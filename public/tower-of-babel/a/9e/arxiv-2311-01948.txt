A comparison theorem for Clark-Aleksandrov measures on the torus
Linus Bergqvist

Abstract
. Given two Clark-Aleksandrov measures σ 1 \sigma^{1} and σ 2 \sigma^{2} on 𝕋 2 \mathbb{T}^{2} , we prove a theorem relating the property that σ 1 ≪ σ 2 \sigma^{1}\ll\sigma^{2} to containment of a concrete function in a certain de Branges-Rovnyak space. We show that our theorem is applicable for all rational inner functions on 𝔻 2 \mathbb{D}^{2} , and we provide several examples of how the theorem can be applied to investigate Clark-Aleksandrov measures related to such functions.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
28A25, 28A35 (primary); 32A10 (secondary)

1.
Background

In Theorem (III-11) of
[
13
]
, Sarason proved a comparison theorem for Clark-Aleksandrov measures on
\mathbb{T}
that relates mutual absolute continuity and
L^{2}
integrability to containment of a concrete function in a de Branges-Rovnyak space. Among other things, this result can be used to show that a Borel measure on
\mathbb{T}
has an atom at a point
z_{0}\in\mathbb{T}
if and only if a related function is contained in a specific de Branges-Rovnyak space.

More recently in
[
2
]
, Aleksandrov and Doubtsov generalized this result to Clark-Aleksandrov measures on the unit ball of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
for
n\geq 2
by using results about the singular parts of pluriharmonic measures on the unit ball.

In this paper, we give a generalization of Sarason’s Theorem (III-11) to Clark-Aleksandrov measures on the torus
\mathbb{T}^{2}
, and we give several examples of how this theorem can be applied. Before proceeding with preliminaries and notation, we give a short description of the proofs and structure of this paper. In the proof of Theorem (III-11) in
[
13
]
, it is important that the measure
\sigma^{1}
(using the notation of Theorem
4.5
below) is a singular measure. But this might be a little misleading when trying to generalize the result, since the key property needed from the measure is not that it is singular with respect to Lebesgue measure, but rather that it is carried by a null set of the annihilators of the disc algebra. However, by the F. and M. Riesz theorem the measures on
\mathbb{T}
with this property are precisely the singular measures, so for measures on
\mathbb{T}
, these properties are equivalent. This is no longer the case for measures on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
, and finding a suitable substitute for the F. and M. Riesz theorem is the main difficulty when trying to generalize this result.

Combining Lemmas
4.1
and
4.3
below gives a generalization of Sarason’s result for measures on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
that have the property that they are carried by a null set for the annihilators of the polydisc algebra. However, due to the product structure of
\mathbb{T}^{n}
, pluriharmonic measures on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
may have degenerate parts, i.e. parts of the form
\sigma_{k}\times m_{n-k}
where
\sigma_{k}
is a pluriharmonic measure on
\mathbb{T}^{k}
, where
k<n
, and
m_{n-k}
denotes Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{T}^{n-k}
(or measures obtained by permuting the variables of such a measure). Since the degenerate parts become notationally cumbersome for large
n
, we restrict our analysis of such measures to the case
n=2
, although it should be pointed out that similar results likely hold for
n>2
as well. We prove that our comparison theorem holds for degenerate pluriharmonic measures as well (see Lemmas
4.1
and
4.4
), and by combining all of these results, we obtain our main result, Theorem
4.5
, which is a generalization of Sarason’s theorem to pluriharmonic measures on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
.

As an application, we show that Theorem
4.5
is always applicable for Clark-Aleksandrov measures corresponding to rational inner functions on
\mathbb{D}^{2}
, and we give a few examples of how the theorem can be applied to compare Clark-Aleksandrov measures on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
corresponding to such functions.

Finally, there are indications that Clark-Aleksandrov measures corresponding to inner functions more generally will have non-degenerate part carried by some set with the desired property (see for example Theorem
5.1
below), but a proof of this statement would require a finer analysis of the non-closed null sets of annihilators of the polydisc algebra. This is illustrated in Example
5.6
.

2.
Preliminaries

We will now give a very brief overview of the concepts and notation that will be used in this paper. For more on Clark-Aleksandrov measures on the
n
-torus we refer the reader to
[
7
]
, in which these measures were originally introduced, as well as
[
5
]
and
[
3
]
, and for more on de Branges-Rovnyak spaces on
\mathbb{D}
we refer the reader to
[
13
]
. For more on polydisc analysis in general, see the textbook
[
12
]
.

Let
C(z,w)
,
z,w\in\mathbb{D}^{n}
, denote the (product)
Cauchy kernel

C(z,w):=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{1-z_{j}\overline{w_{j}}},

for the polydisc
\mathbb{D}^{n}:=\{z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}):z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}\in\mathbb{D}\}
.
For a (complex) Borel measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{T}^{n}:=\{z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}):z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}\in\mathbb{T}\}
, we denote by
\mu_{+}
the
Cauchy transform
of
\mu
, which is defined as

\mu_{+}(z):=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}C(z,w)d\mu(w)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}\hat{\mu}(k)z^{k},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}^{n},

where
\hat{\mu}(k):=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\overline{w}^{k}d\mu(w)
, in standard multi-index notation.

From the definition of
\mu_{+}
it follows that the measures
\mu
for which
\mu_{+}\equiv 0
are precisely the measures such that
\hat{\mu}(k)=0
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
. By continuity of Borel measures as linear functionals on the continuous functions on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
, these measures are precisely the measures that annihilate all functions of the form
\overline{f}
, where
f
is a continuous function on
\overline{\mathbb{D}^{n}}
, the closure of
\mathbb{D}^{n}
, whose restriction to
\mathbb{D}^{n}
is holomorphic, i.e. where
f
is a function in the
polydisc algebra

A(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. We denote by
A(\mathbb{D}^{n})^{\perp}
the class of Borel measures on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
that annihilate
A(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. By the above discussion it follows that all measures in the kernel of the Cauchy transform are of the form
\overline{\mu}
for some
\mu\in A(\mathbb{D}^{n})^{\perp}
.

Now, let
b:\mathbb{D}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
be a non-constant holomorphic function. For
\alpha\in\mathbb{T}
, the quotient

\frac{1-|b(z)|^{2}}{|\alpha-b(z)|^{2}}=\text{Re}\left(\frac{\alpha+b(z)}{\alpha-b(z)}\right),\quad z\in\mathbb{D}^{n}

is positive and
n
-harmonic (i.e. it is harmonic in each variable separately), and thus there exists a (unique) positive Borel measure on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
whose Poisson integral

P[\mu](z):=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1-|z_{j}|^{2}}{|\zeta_{j}-z_{j}|^{2}}d\mu(\zeta_{1},\ldots,z_{n}),\quad z\in\mathbb{D}^{n},

equals the quotient above. We call this measure the
Clark-Aleksandrov measure
corresponding to the function
b
and the parameter
\alpha
, and we denote this measure by
\sigma_{\alpha}[b]
. When it is contextually clear what the underlying function is, we will simply write
\sigma_{\alpha}
instead of
\sigma_{\alpha}[b]
.

A positive measure on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
is called a
pluriharmonic measure
if its Poisson integral is the real part of some holomorphic function on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
. These measures are sometimes called RP-measures, and following the notation in
[
12
]
, we will denote the class of pluriharmonic measures on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
by RP
(\mathbb{T}^{n})
. By Theorem
2.4.1.
in
[
12
]
, a pluriharmonic measures are characterized by a condition on their Fourier coefficients. Namely, a real measure
\mu
is a pluriharmonic measure if and only if all of its mixed Fourier coefficients vanish. That is,
\mu
is an pluriharmonic measure if and only if

(1)

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}z_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots z_{n}^{k_{n}}d\mu(z)=0

unless all
k_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}
satisfy
k_{j}\geq 0
for
j=1,\ldots,n
, or
k_{j}\leq 0
for
j=1,\ldots n
.

By their defining property, all Clark-Aleksandrov measures are pluriharmonic measures. It may seem like Clark-Aleksandrov measures constitute an obscure class of measures, but in fact, they are no more obscure than general pluriharmonic measures. Every (positive) pluriharmonic measure of mass
1
on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
is the Clark-Aleksandrov measure corresponding to
\alpha=1
and some holomorphic function
\phi:\mathbb{D}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
. If the pluriharmonic measure is singular with respect to Lebesgue measure, then the corresponding function
\phi
will be
inner
, where an inner function means a holomorphic function
I:\mathbb{D}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
that has radial limits of modulus
1
Lebesgue almost everywhere on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
. The proof of this correspondence between Clark-Aleksandrov measures and pluriharmonic measures when
n\geq 2
is more or less analogous to the proof of the corresponding statement for
n=1
(see Lemma
2.2.
of
[
3
]
for details).

Direct inspection shows that

k_{w}(z)=k_{b}(z,w):=(1-b(z)\overline{b(w)})C(z,w),\quad z,w\in\mathbb{D}^{n}

is conjugate symmetric and positive definite, and thus it is
the reproducing kernel of some reproducing kernel Hilbert space on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
. We call this Hilbert space the
de Branges-Rovnyak space
corresponding to
b
, and we denote it by
\mathcal{H}(b)
. Note that if
I
is inner, then
\mathcal{H}(I)
is just the
Model space
corresponding to
I
, i.e.
\mathcal{H}(I)=H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\ominus IH^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
.

We can now define

(U_{b,\alpha}k_{w})(\cdot):=(1-\alpha\overline{b(w)})C(\cdot,w).

As in one variable, this operator has a unique extension to a unitary operator from
\mathcal{H}(b)
onto
H^{2}(\sigma_{\alpha})
, where
H^{2}(\sigma_{\alpha})
denotes the closed linear span of the holomorphic polynomials in
L^{2}(\sigma_{\alpha})
(this is a special case of Theorem
3.1.
in
[
2
]
).

Furthermore, we can define
V_{b,\alpha}:L^{2}(\sigma_{\alpha}[b])\rightarrow\mathcal{H}(b)
by

(2)

(V_{b,\alpha}g)(z):=(1-\overline{\alpha}b(z))(g\sigma_{\alpha})_{+}(z),\quad g\in L^{2}(\sigma_{\alpha}),z\in\mathbb{D}^{n}.

When the value of
\alpha
is contextually clear, we will sometimes omit it and simply write
V_{b}
.

We will use the following theorem from
[
2
]
.

Theorem 2.1

(Special case of Theorem
3.2.
in
[
2
]
)
.

For each
\alpha\in\mathbb{T}
, formula (
2
) defines a partial isometry from
L^{2}(\sigma_{\alpha})
into
\mathcal{H}(b)
. The restriction of
V_{b,\alpha}
to
H^{2}(\sigma_{\alpha})
coincides with
U_{b,\alpha}^{*}
, and in particular

(3)

\left(V_{b,\alpha}C(\cdot,w)\right)(z)=\frac{1}{1-\alpha\overline{b(w)}}k_{b}(z,w).

Remark 2.2
.

Note that this implies that the restriction of
V_{b,\alpha}
to
H^{2}(\sigma_{\alpha})
is a unitary operator onto
\mathcal{H}(b)
.

Finally, a Borel set
S\subset\mathbb{T}^{n}
is called a
carrier
of the measure
\mu
if
\mu(A)=\mu(A\cap S)
for all Borel subsets
A\subset\mathbb{T}^{n}
, and
S
is called a
null set for

A(\mathbb{D}^{n})^{\perp}
if
|\mu|(S)=0
for all
\mu\in A(\mathbb{D}^{n})^{\perp}
. For a Borel set
S\subset\mathbb{T}^{n}
, we denote by
\mu|_{S}
the restriction of
\mu
to
S
defined by
\mu|_{S}(A):=\mu(A\cap S)
for all Borel sets
A\subset\mathbb{T}^{n}
.

3.
Degenerate pluriharmonic measures and decompositions of measures on
\mathbb{T}^{2}

Before proving our main theorem, we must first address an issue arising as a consequence of the product structure of the
n
-torus – namely that a pluriharmonic measure on
\mathbb{T}^{n}=\mathbb{T}^{k}\times\mathbb{T}^{l}
, where
k+l=n
, may have a component carried by
E\times\mathbb{T}^{l}
, where
E
is a set of Lebesgue measure zero on
\mathbb{T}^{k}
. In fact, for
\mathbb{T}^{2}=\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T}
, the product of
any
singular measure on
\mathbb{T}
with Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{T}
will be a pluriharmonic measure, and in general, the product of a Borel measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{T}^{k}
with Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{T}^{l}
will be a pluriharmonic measure if and only if
\mu\in
RP
(\mathbb{T}^{k})
. What is less obvious however, is that the converse also holds: all pluriharmonic measures carried by
E\times\mathbb{T}^{l}
are of this form. This is a consequence of a theorem due to Ahern, which will be presented below.

Denote by
\pi\mu
the measure on
\mathbb{T}^{k}
defined by
(\pi\mu)(S)=\mu(S\times\mathbb{T}^{l})
for every Borel set
S\subset\mathbb{T}^{l}
, and let
m_{s}
be Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{T}^{s}
for
s\in\mathbb{N}
. Then the following holds.

Theorem 3.1

(Proposition 1 in
[
1
]
)
.

Let
\mu\in RP(\mathbb{T}^{n})
and let
E\subset\mathbb{T}^{k}
be a Borel set such that
m_{k}(E)=0
; then
\mu|_{E\times\mathbb{T}^{l}}=(\pi\mu)|_{E}\times m_{l}
.

In general, a pluriharmonic measure will not be entirely degenerate, but may have
parts
that are degenerate. In order to analyze the degenerate parts of pluriharmonic measures, we will need the following (unpublished) decomposition of annihilators of the bidisc algebra due to Brian Cole.

Theorem 3.2

(Theorem
29.
in
[
9
]
)
.

Let
\nu\in A(\mathbb{D}^{2})^{\perp}
. Then

\nu=\nu_{0}+\nu_{1}+\nu_{2},

where
\nu_{0},\nu_{1},\nu_{2}
are mutually singular measures in
A(\mathbb{D}^{2})^{\perp}
; the measure
\nu_{0}
is absolutely continuous with respect to some representing measure for the origin;
\nu_{1}=h(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2})d\tau(\zeta_{1})dm(z_{2})
, where
\tau
is a measure on
\mathbb{T}
carried by a set of Lebesgue measure zero,
h\in L^{1}(\tau\times dm)
and
h(\zeta_{1},\cdot)
belongs to the Hardy space
H^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{T})
– consisting of functions in
H^{1}
that vanish at the origin – for each fixed
\zeta_{1}\in\mathbb{T}
; and
\nu_{2}
has a description similar to that of
\nu_{1}
except that the roles of
\zeta_{1}
and
\zeta_{2}
are interchanged.

By applying a change of variables and the fact that pluriharmonic measures are characterized by satisfying (
1
), Cole’s decomposition can be used to obtain a similar decomposition for measures in RP
(\mathbb{T}^{2})
.

Now denote by
T
the change of variables
T:(z_{1},z_{2})\mapsto(z_{1}\overline{z_{2}})
, and denote by
T_{*}(\mu)
the pushforward measure, defined by
T_{*}(\mu)(S):=\mu(T^{-1}(S))
for all Borel sets
S
. If
\mu\in
RP
(\mathbb{T}^{2})
, then
\nu:=z_{1}z_{2}T_{*}(\mu)\in A(\mathbb{D}^{2})^{\perp}
, and conversely, for any real measure
\nu\in A(\mathbb{D}^{2})^{\perp}
,
\mu:=T_{*}(\overline{z_{1}z_{2}}\nu)\in
RP
(\mathbb{T}^{2})
(note that
T=T^{-1}
is a bijection). By applying Cole’s decomposition to
\nu
, we see that any measure
\mu\in
RP
(\mathbb{T}^{2})
can be decomposed as

(4)

\mu=\mu_{0}+\mu_{1}+\mu_{2},

where
\mu_{0},\mu_{1},\mu_{2}
are mutually singular measures in RP
(\mathbb{T}^{2})
; the measure
\mu_{0}=T_{*}(\overline{z_{1}z_{2}}\nu_{0})=\overline{z_{1}}z_{2}T_{*}(\nu_{0})
for some
\nu_{0}\in A(\mathbb{D}^{2})^{\perp}
that is absolutely continuous with respect to some representing measure for
(0,0)
;
\mu_{1}=\tau_{1}\times dm
, where
\tau_{1}
is a real measure on
\mathbb{T}
carried by some set
E_{1}\subset\mathbb{T}
of Lebesgue measure zero;
\mu_{2}=dm\times\tau_{2}
where
\tau_{2}
is a real measure on
\mathbb{T}
carried by some set
E_{2}\subset\mathbb{T}
of Lebesgue measure zero.

For more details on how
T
relates measures in RP
(\mathbb{T}^{2})
to measures in
A(\mathbb{D}^{2})^{\perp}
, see pages
4
and
5
in
[
4
]
and pages
145
and
146
in
[
9
]
.

4.
Main result

We are now ready to prove our main result, which establishes a connection between absolute continuity of two pluriharmonic measures and containment of a concrete function in a certain de Branges-Rovnyak space. We begin by proving the first implication: namely that mutual absolute continuity implies containment of the function in a certain de Branges-Rovnyak space.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
b_{1},b_{2}:\mathbb{D}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
be non-constant holomorphic functions, and let
\sigma^{1}=\sigma_{\alpha}[b_{1}]
and
\sigma^{2}=\sigma_{\alpha}[b_{2}]
, where
\alpha\in\mathbb{T}
, be the corresponding Clark-Aleksandrov measures. As before, let
k_{b_{1}}(\cdot,w)
denote the reproducing kernel of
\mathcal{H}(b_{1})
.

Then the following implication holds:

If
\sigma^{1}\ll\sigma^{2}\text{ and }\frac{d\sigma^{1}}{d\sigma^{2}}\in L^{2}(\sigma^{2})
, then
\frac{\alpha-b_{2}}{\alpha-b_{1}}k_{b_{1}}(\cdot,w)\in\mathcal{H}(b_{2})

for all
w\in\mathbb{D}^{n}
.

Proof.

We can assume that
\alpha=1
without loss of generality.

By applying the formulas (
2
) and (
3
), we get that

(5)

(1-b_{2}(z))(C(\cdot,w)\sigma^{1})_{+}(z)=(1-b_{2}(z))\frac{1-b_{1}(z)}{1-b_{1}(z)}(C(\cdot,w)\sigma^{1})_{+}(z)\\
=\frac{1-b_{2}(z)}{1-b_{1}(z)}(V_{b_{1},1}C(\cdot,w))(z)=\frac{1-b_{2}(z)}{1-b_{1}(z)}\frac{1}{1-\overline{b_{1}(w)}}k_{b_{1}}(z,w).

Equation (
5
) will be used several times in this paper.

By the definition of
V_{b_{2}}
we have

V_{b_{2}}\left(\frac{d\sigma^{1}}{d\sigma^{2}}C(\cdot,w)\right)=(1-b_{2})\left(\frac{d\sigma^{1}}{d\sigma^{2}}C(\cdot,w)\sigma^{2}\right)_{+}=(1-b_{2})(C(\cdot,w)\sigma^{1})_{+}.

By Theorem
2.1

V_{b_{2}}
maps
L^{2}(\sigma^{2})
into
\mathcal{H}(b_{2})
, and thus combining the above expression with (
5
) finishes the proof.

∎

We will now prove the reverse implication. For this we will use the following lemma, which establishes an important connection between containment of
\frac{\alpha-b_{2}}{\alpha-b_{1}}k_{b_{1}}(\cdot,w)
in
\mathcal{H}(b_{2})
, and existence of a combination of
\sigma^{1}
and
\sigma^{2}
that annihilates the polydisc algebra. This will be used to determine when
\sigma^{1}\ll\sigma^{2}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
w\in\mathbb{D}^{n}
be fixed, let
b_{1},b_{2}:\mathbb{D}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
be non-constant holomorphic functions, and let
\sigma^{1}=\sigma_{1}[b_{1}]
and
\sigma^{2}=\sigma_{1}[b_{2}]
be the corresponding Clark-Aleksandrov measures. As before, let
k_{b_{1}}(\cdot,w)
denote the reproducing kernel of
\mathcal{H}(b_{1})
.

Then
\frac{1-b_{2}}{1-b_{1}}k_{b_{1}}(\cdot,w)\in\mathcal{H}(b_{2})
if and only if
C(\cdot,w)\sigma^{1}-q_{w}\sigma^{2}\in A(\mathbb{D}^{n})^{\perp}
for some function
q_{w}\in H^{2}(\sigma^{2})
.

Proof.

Since
V_{b_{2}}:H^{2}(\sigma^{2})\mapsto\mathcal{H}(b_{2})
is unitary (see the remark following Theorem
2.1
), there is a function
q_{w}\in H^{2}(\sigma^{2})
such that

(1-\overline{b_{1}(w)})^{-1}\frac{1-b_{2}(z)}{1-b_{1}(z)}k_{b_{1}}(\cdot,w)=(V_{b_{2}}q_{w})(z)=(1-b_{2}(z))(q_{w}\sigma^{2})_{+}(z),

if and only if
\frac{1-b_{2}}{1-b_{1}}k_{b_{1}}(\cdot,w)\in\mathcal{H}(b_{2})
. By combining this with Equation (
5
), we see that this is equivalent to the statement that

(6)

(1-b_{2}(z))(C(\cdot,w)\sigma^{1}-q_{w}\sigma^{2})_{+}(z)=0,\quad z\in\mathbb{D}^{n}.

Since both
(1-b_{2}(z))
and
(C(\cdot,w)\sigma^{1}-q_{w}\sigma^{2})_{+}(z)
are analytic functions on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
and
1-b_{2}\not\equiv 0
by assumption, (
6
) holds if and only if

(7)

(C(\cdot,w)\sigma^{1}-q_{w}\sigma^{2})_{+}(z)\equiv 0.

Finally, (
7
) holds if and only if
(C(\cdot,w)\sigma^{1}-q_{w}\sigma^{2})\in A(\mathbb{D}^{n})^{\perp}
, which finishes the proof. ∎

We are now ready to prove the second implication for parts of pluriharmonic measures that are carried by some null set for
A(\mathbb{D}^{n})^{\perp}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
b_{1},b_{2}:\mathbb{D}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
be non-constant holomorphic functions, and let
\sigma^{1}=\sigma_{\alpha}[b_{1}]
and
\sigma^{2}=\sigma_{\alpha}[b_{2}]
, where
\alpha\in\mathbb{T}
, be the corresponding Clark-Aleksandrov measures. As before, let
k_{b_{1}}(\cdot,w)
denote the reproducing kernel of
\mathcal{H}(b_{1})
.

Let
N
be a null set of
A(\mathbb{D}^{n})^{\perp}
. Then the following implication holds:

If
\frac{\alpha-b_{2}}{\alpha-b_{1}}k_{b_{1}}(\cdot,w)\in\mathcal{H}(b_{2})
for some
w\in\mathbb{D}^{n}
, then
\sigma^{1}|_{N}\ll\sigma^{2}\text{ and }\frac{d\sigma^{1}|_{N}}{d\sigma^{2}}\in L^{2}(\sigma^{2})
.

Proof.

We can assume that
\alpha=1
without loss of generality.

By Lemma
4.2
, there is a function
q_{w}\in H^{2}(\sigma^{2})
such that

(1-b_{2}(z))(C(\cdot,w)\sigma^{1}-q_{w}\sigma^{2})_{+}(z)=0,\quad z\in\mathbb{D}^{n}.

Since
b_{2}(z)\neq 1
on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
, this means that

(C(\cdot,w)\sigma^{1}-q_{w}\sigma^{2})_{+}(z)\equiv 0\Rightarrow(C(\cdot,w)\sigma^{1}-q_{w}\sigma^{2})\in A(\mathbb{D}^{n})^{\perp}.

By assumption
N
is a null set for
A(\mathbb{D}^{n})^{\perp}
, so

C(\cdot,w)\sigma^{1}|_{N}-q_{w}\sigma^{2}_{N}\equiv 0.

Thus

\sigma^{1}|_{N}=\frac{q_{w}}{C(\cdot,w)}\sigma^{2}|_{N}\ll\frac{q_{w}}{C(\cdot,w)}\sigma^{2},

and in particular
\sigma^{1}|_{N}\ll\sigma^{2}
. Since
q_{w}\in H^{2}(\sigma^{2})
and
C(\cdot,w)^{-1}
is bounded, we have that
d\sigma^{1}|_{N}/d\sigma^{2}\in L^{2}(\sigma^{2})
, which finishes the proof. ∎

Finally, we will prove the second implication for the degenerate parts of pluriharmonic measures on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
. The proof relies on the decompositions from Section
3
, and thus we restrict our attention to pluriharmonic measures on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
w\in\mathbb{D}^{2}
be fixed, let
\sigma^{1},\sigma^{2}\in
RP(
\mathbb{T}^{2})
and let
E\subset\mathbb{T}
be a set of Lebesgue measure zero.

Then if
(C(\cdot,w)\sigma^{1}-q_{w}\sigma^{2})=\nu\in A(\mathbb{D}^{n})^{\perp}
for some
q_{w}\in H^{2}(\sigma^{2})
, we have that
\sigma^{1}|_{E\times\mathbb{T}}\ll\sigma^{2}|_{E\times\mathbb{T}}
and
d\sigma^{2}|_{E\times\mathbb{T}}/d\sigma^{1}|_{E\times\mathbb{T}}\in L^{2}(\sigma^{2})
. The same conclusion holds if
E\times\mathbb{T}
is replaced by
\mathbb{T}\times E
.

Proof.

To begin with, by Ahern’s theorem (Theorem
3.1
),
\sigma^{1}|_{E\times\mathbb{T}}=\tau_{1}\times dm
and
\sigma^{2}|_{E\times\mathbb{T}}=\tau_{2}\times dm
for some singular measures
\tau_{1}
and
\tau_{2}
on
\mathbb{T}
. We are done if we can show that
\tau_{1}\ll\tau_{2}
and
d\tau_{2}/d\tau_{2}\in L^{2}(\tau_{2})
.

We begin by showing that
\tau_{1}\ll\tau_{2}
. If this was not the case, there would, by definition, be some set
S\subset\mathbb{T}
such that
\tau_{2}(S)=0
, but
\tau_{1}(S)\neq 0
. By restricting to
S\times\mathbb{T}
and using Cole’s decomposition (Theorem
3.2
) on
\nu
, we see that

C(\cdot,w)\tau_{1}|_{S}\times dm=h(\cdot)\tau_{3}|_{S}\times dm,

where
\tau_{3}
is some singular measure on
\mathbb{T}
and
h(\zeta_{1},\cdot)
belongs to
H^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{T})
for every fixed value of
\zeta_{1}\in\mathbb{T}
.

But this is impossible, since for each fixed
\zeta_{1}\in\mathbb{T}
, the function
h(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2})(1-\zeta_{2}\overline{w_{2}})\in H^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{D})
has to be constant as a function of
\zeta_{2}\in\mathbb{T}
, but it also has to vanish for
\zeta_{2}=0
. This can only happen if the function (and thus
\tau_{1}|_{S}
) is identically equal to
0
, which contradicts our assumption on
S
.

Now denote the Radon-Nikodym derivative
d\tau_{2}/d\tau_{1}
by
f
. It remains to show that
f\in L^{2}(\tau_{2})
.

We have that

(C((\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}),w)f(\zeta_{1})-q_{w}(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}))\tau_{1}\times dm\in A(\mathbb{D}^{2})^{\perp},

so

(8)

C((\zeta_{1},0),w)f(\zeta_{1})-q_{w}(\zeta_{1},0))=0.

Note that
q_{w}(\zeta_{1},0)
is well-defined, at least as a formal power series, since
q_{w}\in H^{2}(\sigma^{2})
. Furthermore

\int_{\mathbb{T}}|q_{w}(\zeta_{1},0)|^{2}d\tau_{2}(\zeta_{1})=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{T}}q_{w}(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2})dm(\zeta_{2})\right|^{2}d\tau_{2}(\zeta_{1})\leq\sigma^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})\|q_{w}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\sigma^{2})}<\infty,

and thus (
8
) implies that
f\in L^{2}(\tau_{2})
, as was to be shown.

The proof for
\mathbb{T}\times E
is done in the same way. ∎

By combining the above Lemmas, we obtain the main theorem of this paper.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
b_{1},b_{2}:\mathbb{D}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
be non-constant holomorphic functions, and let

(2)

(3)

Proof.

Applying Lemma

5.
Applications

In order to apply Theorem
4.5
, we must determine what functions
b_{1}
have Clark-Aleksandrov measures (corresponding to some parameter
\alpha
) whose non-degenerate part is carried by some null set of

. The following theorem relates compact null sets to zero sets of the polydisc algebra, which will be used to conclude that Clark-Aleksandrov measures corresponding to a large class of inner functions are indeed carried by such sets. Here, a compact subset

Theorem 5.1

(Part of Theorem
6.1.2.
in
[
12
]
)
.

Let

\mathbb{T}^{n}
. Then

is a zero set of

if and only if
|\mu|(K)=0
for every
\mu\in A(\mathbb{D}^{n})^{\perp}
.

An interesting consequence of this theorem is that every compact subset of a zero set of

is also a zero set of
A(\mathbb{D}^{n})
.

An obvious candidate for the carrier
S_{\sigma^{1}}
is the entire support of the measure
\sigma^{1}
. By Theorem
5.1
, the support satisfies the assumptions on

if and only if it is a level set for some function in

.

If
I
is an inner function, then the corresponding Clark-Aleksandrov measure
\sigma_{\alpha}[I]
has support contained in the set

\overline{\{\zeta\in\mathbb{T}^{n}:\lim_{r\rightarrow 1^{-}}I(r\zeta)=\alpha\}}

(see Lemma
2.1.
in
[
3
]
or Proposition
2.1.
in
[
10

4.5
is satisfied whenever

is an inner function in

(i.e. when

is a rational inner function with no singularities on the boundary, see Theorem
5.2.5.

(b)

[

b_{1}
is a rational inner function on
\mathbb{D}^{2}
.

By Theorem
5.2.5.
of
[
12
]
, every rational inner function is of the form

e^{iv}z^{d}\frac{\tilde{p}(z)}{p(z)},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}^{n},

where
v\in[0,2\pi)

,d\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
,
p(z)
is a polynomial of polydegree
(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})
with no zeros in

\tilde{p}(z):=z_{1}^{d_{1}}\cdots z_{n}^{d_{n}}\overline{p\left(\frac{1}{\overline{z_{1}}},\ldots,\frac{1}{\overline{z_{n}}}\right)}.

5.3

For most purposes it suffices to analyze rational inner functions of the form
\tilde{p}/p
.
If
b_{1}
is a rational inner function of the form

, then

(10)

\overline{\{\zeta\in\mathbb{T}^{n}:\lim_{r\rightarrow 1^{-}}b_{1}(r\zeta)=\alpha\}}=\{\zeta\in\mathbb{T}^{n}:\tilde{p}(\zeta)-\alpha p(\zeta)=0\},

(see Theorem

of

). If

does not vanish inside

Furthermore if

for details). Clearly there can only be finitely many such factors. Thus the set (

(something similar likely holds for RIFs on

Thus the assumptions of Theorem

.

Theorem

Example 5.3
.

Let
b_{2}=z_{1}z_{2}

be another holomorphic function with corresponding Clark-Aleksandrov measure
\sigma^{1}:=\sigma_{1}[b_{1}]

4.5
for

For example, if

c+f(z_{1})+g(z_{2}),
where

It may be interesting to note that another application of Theorem

shows that

\frac{1-z_{1}z_{2}}{1-\phi(z_{1},z_{2})}\frac{(1-\phi(z)\overline{\phi(w)})}{(1-z_{1}\overline{w_{1}})(1-z_{2}\overline{w_{2}})}=\frac{2-z_{1}-z_{2}}{2}\frac{(1-\frac{2z_{1}z_{2}-z_{1}-z_{2}}{2-z_{1}-z_{2}}\overline{\phi(w)})}{(1-z_{1}\overline{w_{1}})(1-z_{2}\overline{w_{2}})}\\

for

, which is not as obvious.

That

and one can in fact calculate the density with respect to

in

However,

does

\frac{2}{2-z_{1}-z_{2}}\not\in H^{2}\ominus\phi H^{2}.

We can also go back and try to determine for what holomorphic functions

\mathcal{H}(z_{1}z_{2})
consists of all holomorphic functions of the form

. Thus, we are looking for holomorphic functions on
\mathbb{D}^{2}
that map into
\mathbb{D}
that are of the form

b_{1}(z_{1},z_{2})=1-\frac{1-z_{1}z_{2}}{c+f(z_{1})+g(z_{2})}=\frac{(c-1)+f(z_{1})+g(z_{2})+z_{1}z_{2}}{c+f(z_{1})+g(z_{2})}.

Note that

since we want

to be holomorphic in
\mathbb{D}^{2}
.

If we limit our attention to finding rational inner functions
b_{1}=\tilde{p}/p
, where

(m,n)

\mathbb{D}^{2}
, and
\tilde{p}
is given by (

.

The numerator on the right hand side of (

.

This means that

\tilde{p}=cz_{1}z_{2}+az_{2}+bz_{1}=(c-1)+az_{1}+bz_{2}+z_{1}z_{2},

b_{1}(z)=(z_{1}z_{2}+az_{1}+az_{2})/(1+az_{1}+az_{2})
.
Finally, since

is holomorphic on
\mathbb{D}^{2}

-1/a
is not contained in the open disc with radius
2

is of the form

(13)

b_{1}(z)=\frac{dz_{1}z_{2}-z_{1}-z_{2}}{d-z_{1}-z_{2}},

As we saw earlier

\frac{1-b_{1}(z_{1},z_{2})}{1-z_{1}z_{2}}\not\in H^{2}\ominus b_{1}H^{2}

for

as in (

where the last equality holds since

The calculations showing that the orthogonality requirement automatically holds whenever

seem to be an example of a more general phenomenon that appears with RIFs. More concretely, if
c\tilde{p_{1}}-\alpha p_{1}=\tilde{p_{2}}-\alpha p_{2}
(which in particular means that the corresponding Clark measures are supported on the same curve), then
(\alpha-\phi_{2})/(\alpha-\phi_{1})=cp_{1}/p_{2}

\tilde{p}

denotes the polydegree of

(\alpha-\phi_{2})(\alpha-\phi_{1})^{-1}k_{\phi_{1}}(\cdot,0)
automatically lies in the model space
H^{2}\ominus\phi_{2}H^{2}
whenever

lies in
H^{2}
. We clarify this in the following corollary.

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
\phi_{1}=\tilde{p}_{1}/p_{1}
and
\phi_{2}=\tilde{p}_{2}/p_{2}
be two non-constant rational inner functions on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
, let
n_{j}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
denote the polydegree of
p_{j}
for
j=1,2
and let
\sigma^{1}:=\sigma_{\alpha}[\phi_{1}]
and
\sigma^{2}:=\sigma_{\alpha}[\phi_{2}]
. Assume that
n_{2}\geq n_{1}
, and that

\frac{\alpha-\phi_{2}}{\alpha-\phi_{1}}=c\frac{p_{1}}{p_{2}}

for some constant
c\neq 0
.

Then
p_{1}/p_{2}\in H^{2}
, if and only if
\sigma^{1}\ll\sigma^{2}
and
d\sigma^{1}/d\sigma^{2}\in L^{2}(d\sigma^{2})
.

Note that if
c=0
then
\phi_{2}
is constant.

Proof.

If
p_{1}/p_{2}\in H^{2}
, then for all
f\in H^{2}

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\frac{\overline{\alpha-\phi_{2}(z)}}{\overline{\alpha-\phi_{1}(z)}}(1-\phi_{1}(0)\overline{\phi_{1}(z)}))\phi_{2}(z)f(z)|dz|\\
=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}(1-\phi_{1}(0)\overline{\phi_{1}(z)})\frac{\overline{p_{1}(z)}}{\overline{p_{2}(z)}}\frac{\tilde{p}_{2}(z)}{p_{2}(z)}f(z)|dz|\\
=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}(1-\phi_{1}(0)\overline{\phi_{1}(z)}))\frac{\overline{p_{1}}(z)}{\overline{p_{2}}(z)}\frac{z^{n_{2}}\overline{p}_{2}(z)}{p_{2}(z)}f(z)|dz|\\
=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}(1-\phi_{1}(0)\overline{\phi_{1}(z)}))z^{n_{2}-n_{1}}\frac{\tilde{p}_{1}(z)}{p_{2}(z)}f(z)|dz|\\
=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}(1-\phi_{1}(0)\overline{\phi_{1}(z)}))\frac{p_{1}}{p_{2}}z^{n_{2}-n_{1}}\phi_{1}(z)f(z)|dz|\\
=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}(\phi_{1}(z)-\phi_{1}(0)))\frac{p_{1}(z)}{p_{2}(z)}z^{n_{2}-n_{1}}f(z)|dz|=0,

where the last equality holds since the integrand lies in
H^{2}
and vanishes at the origin. Applying Theorem
4.5
for
w=0
finishes the proof of the first implication.

If instead
\sigma^{1}\ll\sigma^{2}
and
d\sigma^{1}/d\sigma^{2}\in L^{2}(d\sigma^{2})
, then applying
4.5
for
w=0
shows that
(1-\phi_{1}(z)\overline{\phi_{1}(0)})p_{1}/p_{2}
lies in
H^{2}\ominus\phi_{2}H^{2}
, which in particular means that it lies in
H^{2}
. Since
\phi_{1}
is inner, it follows that
p_{1}/p_{2}\in H^{2}
, which finishes the proof. ∎

\sigma^{1}\ll\sigma^{2}
and
d\sigma^{1}/d\sigma^{2}\in L^{2}(d\sigma^{2})
if and only if
p_{1}/p_{2}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})
.

Thus, we are asking whether
p_{1}
is contained in the ideal – which we will denote by
\mathcal{I}_{L^{2}}(p_{2})
– which consists of all polynomials
q
such that
q/p_{2}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
. Ideals of this sort were extensively studied in
[
11
]
.

The ideal
\mathcal{I}_{L^{2}}(p_{2})
contains the ideal
\langle p_{2},\tilde{p}_{2}\rangle
, since all polynomials
q\in\langle p_{2},\tilde{p}_{2}\rangle
have the property that
q/p_{2}
is bounded on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
.

A Gröbner basis for
\langle p_{2},\tilde{p_{2}}\rangle
is given by

\displaystyle v_{1}

\displaystyle:=(z_{1}-1)^{2}=-2xp_{2}-(1+x)\tilde{p}_{2},

\displaystyle v_{2}

\displaystyle:=(3z_{1}+2z_{2}-5)=-4(1+xy)p_{2}-(3+2y(1+x))\tilde{p}_{2},

but

p_{1}:=4-z_{2}-3z_{1}-z_{1}z_{2}+z_{1}^{2}=\frac{5}{2}(z_{1}-1)^{2}-\frac{z_{1}+1}{2}(3z_{1}+2z_{2}-5)+(z_{1}-1)\\
=\frac{5}{2}v_{1}-\frac{z_{1}+1}{2}v_{2}+(z_{1}-1),

so
p_{1}
is not in the ideal generated by
p_{2}
and
\tilde{p}_{2}
as it has remainder
z_{1}-1
relative to the Gröbner basis
v_{1},v_{2}
(see
[
8
]
for more on Gröbner bases).

However, from the above calculations it follows that
p_{1}\in\mathcal{I}_{L^{2}}(p_{2})
if (and only if)
(1-z_{1})\in\mathcal{I}_{L^{2}}(p_{2})
. By using Cauchy’s integral formula we see that

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\frac{|z_{1}-1|^{2}}{|2-z_{1}z_{2}-z_{1}^{2}z_{2}|^{2}}|dz|=\int_{\mathbb{T}}|z_{1}-1|^{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{1}{|2-z_{1}z_{2}-z_{1}^{2}z_{2}|^{2}}|dz_{2}||dz_{1}|\\
=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|z_{1}-1|^{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{1}{2-z_{1}z_{2}-z_{1}^{2}z_{2}}\frac{1}{1-\overline{z_{2}\frac{(z_{1}+z_{1}^{2})}{2}}}|dz_{2}||dz_{1}|\\
=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|z_{1}-1|^{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{1}{(2-z_{1}z_{2}-z_{1}^{2}z_{2})}\frac{1}{\left(1-\overline{z_{2}}\frac{\overline{z_{1}+z_{1}^{2}}}{2}\right)}|dz_{2}||dz_{1}|\\
=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|z_{1}-1|^{2}\frac{1}{2-\frac{|z_{1}+z_{1}^{2}|^{2}}{2}}|dz_{1}|=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{|z_{1}-1|^{2}}{4-|1+z_{1}|^{2}}|dz_{1}|\\
=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{|z_{1}-1|^{2}}{(2-|1+z_{1}|)(2+|1+z_{1}|)}|dz_{1}|<\infty,

where the last inequality follows since
|z_{1}-1|^{2}/(2-|1+z_{1}|)\leq 4
for
z_{1}\in\mathbb{T}
.
It follows that
p_{1}\in\mathcal{I}_{L^{2}}(p_{2})
, and thus
\sigma^{1}\ll\sigma^{2}
and
d\sigma^{1}/d\sigma^{2}\in L^{2}(d\sigma^{2})
.

Conversely,
\sigma^{2}\ll\sigma^{1}
and
d\sigma^{2}/d\sigma^{1}\in L^{2}(d\sigma^{1})
if and only if
p_{2}/p_{1}\in H^{2}
, i.e. if and only if
p_{2}\in\mathcal{I}_{L^{2}}(p_{1})
.

In Example
15.1
of
[
11
]
, Knese shows that the ideal
\mathcal{I}_{L^{2}}(p_{1})
is generated by the polynomials
v_{1}=(1-z_{2})^{2},v_{2}=(1-z_{1})(1-z_{2}),
and
v_{3}=1-z_{1}z_{2}
. A Gröbner basis for this ideal is given by
g_{1}=(1-z_{2})^{2},g_{2}=z_{1}+z_{2}-2
. That it is indeed a basis is verified by noting that
v_{1}=g_{1}
,
v_{3}=g_{1}-z_{2}g_{2}
, and
v_{2}=-(g_{2}+v_{3})=-(g_{2}(1-z_{2})+g_{1})
.

It can now be checked directly that
p_{2}
is
not
contained in the ideal generated by these polynomials (its remainder with respect to
g_{1},g_{2}
is
r(z):=z_{2}-1\neq 0
).

Thus
p_{2}/p_{1}\not\in H^{2}
, and so it is not the case that
\sigma^{2}\ll\sigma^{1}
and
d\sigma^{2}/\sigma^{1}\in L^{2}(\sigma^{1})
.

We end this note with an example where Theorem
4.5

is
applicable, i.e. where the non-degenerate part of
\sigma^{1}
is carried by a null set of
A(\mathbb{D}^{2})^{\perp}
, but where one can not use Theorem
5.1
to deduce this.

Example 5.6
.

For
\alpha\in\mathbb{T}
, consider the Borel measures on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
given by
\sigma_{\alpha}:=\sigma_{\alpha}[z_{1}z_{2}]
. As we saw in Example
5.3
,
\sigma_{\alpha}
is normalized Lebesgue measure on the straight line
\{(e^{iv},\alpha e^{-iv})\in\mathbb{T}^{2}:v\in[0,2\pi)\}
, and for every
\alpha
, this straight line will be a null set of
A(\mathbb{D}^{n})^{\perp}
by Theorem
5.1
. Now let
\{\alpha_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
with
\alpha_{j}\in\mathbb{T}
be a dense sequence in
\mathbb{T}
, and consider the measure

\sigma:=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{j}}\sigma_{\alpha_{j}}.

This measure is a singular Borel probability measure on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
, so as we noted in the introduction it will be the Clark-Aleksandrov measure corresponding to
\alpha=1
and some inner function
\phi:\mathbb{D}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
(again, see Lemma
2.2.
of
[
3
]
for details). The set

\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}\{(e^{iv},\alpha_{j}e^{-iv})\in\mathbb{T}^{2}:v\in[0,2\pi)\}\subset\mathbb{T}^{2}

is a carrier of the measure
\sigma
, and furthermore it is a null set for
A(\mathbb{D}^{2})^{\perp}
since countable unions of sets of measure zero have measure zero. It follows that Theorem
4.5
is applicable for
b_{1}=\phi
and
\sigma_{1}[b_{1}]=\sigma
.

However, there is no closed null set for
A(\mathbb{D}^{2})^{\perp}
that carries
\sigma
, since the support of
\sigma
is all of
\mathbb{T}^{2}
.

Thus it would be desirable to have a way of determining whether or not a given set is a null set for
A(\mathbb{D}^{2})^{\perp}
, which does not require that the set in question is closed.

In general, it would be interesting to know for which inner functions
b_{1}
Theorem
4.5
is applicable. Will all Clark-Aleksandrov measures corresponding to inner functions (i.e. all singular pluriharmonic measures of mass
1
) have non-degenerate part carried by some null-set for
A(\mathbb{D}^{2})^{\perp}
, or is this only the case for certain inner functions?

Weak Cosmic Censorship Conjecture Cannot be Violated in Gedanken Experiments


Abstract
We innovate a systematic investigation of the Weak Cosmic Censorship Conjecture (WCCC) using gedanken experiments involving black hole perturbations by test particles. We classify various WCCC violation scenarios proposed in recent decades, including Hubeny, mixed, and the latest Sorce-Wald (SW). We provide general formulae in each case, and resolve contradictions in numerous studies. Following SW type, our analysis reveals that WCCC depends on the sign choice of the parameter W ≡ ( ∂ S ∂ T ) Q i ; T = 0 W\equiv\big(\frac{\partial S}{\partial T}\big)_{Q_{i};T=0} of the extremal black holes. W > 0 W>0 preserves WCCC and W < 0 W<0 would indicate potential violation. We show explicitly that W > 0 W>0 for spherically-symmetric and static black holes, and for general case, we argue that it is protected by the black hole no-hair theorem. We also consider asymptotically-(A)dS black holes and argue that there can be no violation either.

1
Introduction

The Weak Cosmic Censorship Conjecture (WCCC), proposed by Penrose
[
1
]
, states that for a geodesically complete and asymptotically flat spacetime, the evolution of matter fields satisfying the null energy condition (NEC) cannot lead to a naked singularity. The importance of this conjecture cannot be overestimated since it preserves the predictive power of physical laws by preventing a visible singularity from being observed at infinity through physical processes
[
2
]
. The general proof or demonstration of WCCC is challenging, one approach involves perturbing a black hole to determine whether there exists a critical situation that can lead to a visible singularity. According to the gedanken experiments proposed by Wald
[
3
]
, by throwing or dropping matter, carrying energy
E>\delta M
and charges
q_{i}=\delta Q_{i}
, into a black hole of
(M,Q_{i})
, the final state will have mass
M+\delta M
and charges
Q_{i}+\delta Q_{i}
, where the matter obeys the null energy condition (NEC). Note that
Q_{i}
denote conserved quantities such as electric charges and/or angular momentum,
etc.
Applying the thermodynamic second law
\delta S\geq 0
, the linear variation of black hole mechanics leads to

\delta M\geq\phi_{i}\delta Q_{i}\,.

(1)

Note that the extremal case
(T=0)
always saturates the inequality. For asymptotically Minkowski spacetimes, the null energy condition (NEC) implies that the flux of energy and charges must be positive, leading to inequality (
1
)
[
4
,
5
]
. This inequality can also be derived for an infalling particle in geodesic calculations, e.g., in the case of a Kerr-Newman (KN) black hole
[
3
,
6
]
. Focusing on the KN solution, Wald proposed that if the horizon condition, characterized by

M^{2}\geq Q^{2}+\left(J/M\right)^{2},

were not satisfied after perturbation, the spacetime would contain a naked singularity
[
3
]
. He showed that no violation of WCCC could occurred by throwing particle matter into an extremal KN black hole. However, Hubeny and Jacobson
[
7
,
8
]
suggested that the WCCC could be violated for a slightly non-extremal black hole. (We call it as Hubney’s type.) Following these works, it has been shown
[
6
]
that WCCC can be violated for an extremal KN black hole by expanding the horizon condition up to the second order in
\delta M
and
\delta Q_{i}
. (We call it as mixed type.) See
[
5
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
]
for some followup works on the Hubeny’s type and
[
13
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
11
,
12
]
on the mixed type.

To resolve these contradictions with Hubeny or others, Sorce and Wald (SW)
[
19
,
20
]
realized that the analysis in Hubeny’s type of gedanken experiments
[
7
]
is insufficient at the linear order of
\delta Q_{i}
and
\delta M
, while the near-extremal expansion is second order
[
21
,
22
]
. We also find that there is confusion in mixing different orders in the mixed type analysis
[
6
]
. We shall clarify these points in this paper by investigating the problems and providing general formulae.

SW proposed a method to examine the WCCC of the near extremal KN black hole for up to the second-order variation
[
19
]
. (We refer to this as the SW type.) At the second order, they suggested the following inequality

\delta^{2}M-\Omega_{H}\delta^{2}J-\phi_{H}\delta^{2}Q\geq-\frac{k}{8\pi}\delta^{2}A^{KN},

(2)

which is the second-order generalization of black hole dynamics, (where the first-order variation is given by

\delta M\geq\phi_{i}\delta Q_{i},
)
and
\delta^{2}A^{KN}
, as expressed in Eq. (113) in
[
19
]
, is in fact the Hessian metric of
A^{KN}
. For the KN black hole, the existence horizon condition is
f(\lambda)\geq 0
, where

f(\lambda)=M(\lambda)^{2}-Q(\lambda)^{2}-\frac{J(\lambda)^{2}}{M(\lambda)^{2}}\,.

(3)

Using the inequality (
2
) and
\delta M=\phi_{i}\delta Q_{i}
, expanding the above equation up to the second order in
\lambda
and
\epsilon
leads to

\displaystyle f(\lambda)\geq\left(-\frac{(M^{4}-J^{2})Q\delta Q+2JM^{2}\delta J}{M(M^{4}+J^{2})}\lambda+M\epsilon\right)^{2}+O(\lambda^{3},\lambda^{2}\epsilon,\ldots)\,,

(4)

in which
\epsilon=\frac{r_{+}}{M}-1\geq 0
is a dimensionless blackening parameter. Note that at the above order, the mass
M
should be taken to be the extremal value
M_{\rm ext}=\sqrt{{\frac{1}{2}}(Q^{2}+\sqrt{Q^{4}+4J^{2}})}
.

In the gedanken experiment, we need to deal with two independent inequalities: one is the
energy condition
(it is equivalent to Eqs. (
1
) and (
2
), up to the first and second orders, respectively), and the other is the existence of horizon condition, or
horizon condition
for short. In other words,
M>M_{\text{ext}}
must be satisfied before and after perturbation to protect WCCC. In the perturbation process, we therefore need to be careful about how to expand the two inequality equations in terms of small quantities up to the appropriate orders, and we need to avoid mixing different orders in the near-extremal limit. We shall clarify these points with great care.

We first need to carefully define the meaning of
\delta
and
\delta^{2}
terms in the SW method
[
19
,
20
]
. Considering a function
F[Q(\lambda)]
, the Taylor expansion up to the second order at
\lambda=0
is

F[Q(\lambda)]=F[Q(0)]+F^{\prime}[Q(0)]Q^{\prime}(0)\lambda+\frac{1}{2}\left(F^{\prime\prime}[Q(0)]Q^{\prime}(0)^{2}+F^{\prime}[Q(0)]Q^{\prime\prime}(0)\right)\lambda^{2}+\cdots.

Alternatively, expanding

F[Q+\lambda\delta Q+\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}\delta^{2}Q],

up to the second order in
\lambda
gives the same result, where
Q=Q(0)
,
\delta Q=Q^{\prime}(0)
and
\delta^{2}Q=Q^{\prime\prime}(0)
. It should be noted that expanding
F[Q+\lambda\delta Q]
is different from
F[Q(\lambda)]
in the second order and does not include the
Q^{\prime\prime}[0]=\delta^{2}Q
term. Thus, SW’s second-order perturbation is based on this: if a particle with energy
E>\Delta M
and charge
q_{i}=\Delta Q_{i}
falls into a black hole, then the black hole’s parameters will change to

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle M+\Delta M=M+\lambda\delta M+\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}\delta^{2}M+\cdots,

(5)

\displaystyle Q_{i}

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle Q_{i}+\Delta Q_{i}=Q_{i}+\lambda\delta Q_{i}+\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}\delta^{2}Q_{i}+\cdots.

(6)

The main focus of this paper is to generalize the
horizon condition
after an infalling test particle perturbs a black hole. To achieve this, for the near-extremal case
M(T_{\epsilon},Q_{i})
, we introduce the quantity
X_{\epsilon}
, defined as

X_{\epsilon}\equiv M(T_{\epsilon},Q_{i})+\Delta M-M_{\text{ext}}(Q_{i}+\Delta Q_{i})=\Big(M(T_{\epsilon},Q_{i})-M_{\text{ext}}(Q_{i})\Big)+\Delta M-\Delta M_{\text{ext}}\,.

(7)

The sign of this quantity indicates whether the black hole’s mass after perturbation, denoted as
M_{\text{new}}=M(T_{\epsilon},Q_{i})+\Delta M
, remains larger than the extremal mass
M_{\text{ext}}(Q_{i}+\Delta Q_{i})
, where
\Delta M
is restricted by the
energy condition
. In our analysis,
X_{\epsilon}
is directly related to the stability of the black hole’s horizon. If the mass of the black hole is less than the extremal mass after perturbation (
X_{\epsilon}<0
), it means the black hole is destroyed, or the WCCC can be violated. In the extreme case, we start from an extremal black hole where
M_{\text{ext}}(Q_{i})=M(T=0,Q_{i})
, so that one can write

X_{\text{ext}}\equiv\Delta M-\Delta M_{\text{ext}}\,.

(8)

Our conclusion is that WCCC cannot be violated in this gedanken experiment at the second order, owing to the fact that extremal black holes satisfy
W\geq 0
, with
W
defined by

W=\lim_{T\rightarrow 0}\Big({\frac{\partial S}{\partial T}}\Big)_{Q_{i}}\equiv\Big({\frac{\partial S}{\partial T}}\Big)_{Q_{i};T=0}\,.

(9)

In other words, for fixed charges, the extremal black hole has the minimum entropy.

Categorizing different types of WCCC

In this section, we use the proposed formulae in Eqs. (
7
) and (
8
) to calculate the general formulae, critiquing the different well-known types of WCCC, including Hubeny’s type, mixed type, and SW’s type. In the last case, we first prove the Wald inequality (
2
) by using a new approach and considering
\delta S=0
and
\delta^{2}S>0

[
23
]
in the second order of expansion. Then we show that for examining WCCC, we only need to look at the sign of the quantity
W
for both the extreme and near-extreme cases.

2.1
Hubeny’s type

The Hubeny’s test of WCCC is effectively replacing
\Delta
in (
7
) by
\delta
, namely

\displaystyle X_{\rm H}=\Big(M(T_{\epsilon},Q_{i})-M_{\text{ext}}(Q_{i})\Big)+\delta M-\delta M_{\text{ext}}\,.

(10)

We perform the expansion near the extremal case up to the first order of
\lambda
, where
\delta^{2}M=\delta^{2}Q_{i}=0
. Making use of the fact that

\delta M=T_{\epsilon}\delta S+\phi_{\epsilon,i}\delta Q_{i}
and

\delta M_{\rm ext}=\phi_{\text{ext},i}\delta Q_{i}
, we find that near the extreme limit
T=T_{\epsilon}
, we have

\displaystyle X_{\text{H}}=T_{\epsilon}\delta S+{\textstyle{\frac{\scriptstyle 1}{\scriptstyle 2}}}W\,T_{\epsilon}^{2}+\lambda(\phi_{\epsilon,i}-\phi_{\text{ext},i})\delta Q_{i}\geq{\textstyle{\frac{\scriptstyle 1}{\scriptstyle 2}}}W\,T_{\epsilon}^{2}-\lambda{\frac{\partial S_{\rm ext}}{\partial Q_{i}}}\delta Q_{i}\,T_{\epsilon}\,,

(11)

where the inequality arises from
\delta S\geq 0
and
W
is define by (
9
). The last term above can also be simply expressed as

. In the above derivation, we used the cyclic differential identity

\Big({\frac{\partial x}{\partial y}}\Big)_{z}\,\Big({\frac{\partial y}{\partial z}}\Big)_{x}\,\Big({\frac{\partial z}{\partial x}}\Big)_{y}=-1\,.

(12)

The bracket term in (
10

\displaystyle M(T_{\epsilon},Q_{i})-M_{\text{ext}}(Q_{i})=M(T_{\epsilon},Q_{i})-M(0,Q_{i})={\textstyle{\frac{\scriptstyle 1}{\scriptstyle 2}}}W\,T_{\epsilon}^{2}+{\cal O}(T_{\epsilon}^{3})\,.

(13)

Note that in this paper,
\epsilon
denotes some non-extremal (dimensionless) parameter with
T_{\epsilon}\propto\epsilon+{\cal O}(\epsilon^{2})
as
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
. In the extremal limit,
T_{\epsilon}=0
, we simply have
X_{\rm H}=0
. In fact, our general formula (
11
) summarizes all results of previous papers that adopted Hubeny’s method, as in
[
5
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
]
. For example, for the RN black hole we have
T_{\epsilon}=\frac{\epsilon}{2\pi M}
and
S_{\rm ext}=\pi Q^{2}
, then one can obtain
X_{\rm H}\geq\frac{M\epsilon^{2}}{2}-\lambda\epsilon\delta Q

[
7
]
. (For the more complicated KN black hole
[
10
]
see Appendix A.1.) As is obvious, the first term has the
\epsilon^{2}
order, and the second term has
\lambda\epsilon
order (since
\delta Q
carries
\lambda

\lambda^{2}
term; therefore, Hubeny’s method is incomplete.

2.2
Mixed type

Another type of investigating WCCC is related to the mixing of orders in the extremal case, where
\epsilon=0
and we have Eq. (

where we used

2.3
SW type

Before we derive the general formula for

in the appropriate order that includes all the

\displaystyle\!\frac{\partial^{2}M}{\partial Q_{i}\partial Q_{j}}\delta Q_{i}\delta Q_{j}+\frac{2\,\partial^{2}M}{\partial Q_{i}\partial S}\delta Q_{i}\delta S+\frac{\partial^{2}M}{\partial S^{2}}\delta S^{2}.

From (

\delta^{2}M\geq\phi_{i}\delta^{2}Q_{i}+\delta\phi_{i}\delta Q_{i}\,.

The extremal limit always saturates this inequality, owing to the fact that

\displaystyle\Delta M_{\text{ext}}(Q_{i})=\lambda\phi_{\text{ext},i}\delta Q_{i}+{\frac{1}{2}}\lambda^{2}\left(\phi_{\text{ext},i}\delta^{2}Q_{i}+\frac{\partial^{2}M_{\text{ext}}}{\partial Q_{i}\partial Q_{j}}\delta Q_{i}\delta Q_{j}\right).

\displaystyle X_{\epsilon}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\textstyle{\frac{\scriptstyle 1}{\scriptstyle 2}}}W\,T_{\epsilon}^{2}+\lambda(\phi_{i}-\phi_{\text{ext},i})\delta Q_{i}+\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}\left(\delta^{2}M-\phi_{\text{ext},i}\delta^{2}Q_{i}-\frac{\partial^{2}M_{\text{ext}}}{\partial Q_{i}\partial Q_{j}}\delta Q_{i}\delta Q_{j}\right)

3
On the possibility of WCCC violation

Using the gedanken experiment and expanding the horizon condition and energy conditions, given by
\delta M
and
\delta^{2}M
in Eqs. (
1
) and (
2
), we calculated
X_{\epsilon}
and
X_{\rm ext}
in Eq. (
24
) for the general near-extreme and extreme black holes, up to the appropriate orders. We see that the sign of
W
plays a crucial rule in WCCC. It is important to clarify that the sign is an intrinsic property of an extremal black hole, whereas in the testing of the WCCC (
24
), we made no assumption of the final outcome in Eq. (
7
) after perturbing the black hole, except for applying (
19
).

In Appendix C, we provide an independent proof of the positivity of
W
for spherically-symmetric and static black holes. In Appendix D, we considered two interesting examples: charged Born-Infeld (BI)
[
27
,
28
]
and Myers-Perry (MP)
[
29
]
black holes in more detail. While the MP black hole is not spherically-symmetric, it respects the WCCC. For general black holes that are specified by conserved mass
M
and charges
Q_{i}
, (which include,
e.g.
electromagnetic charges and/or angular momenta,) and have extremal limit, we can deduce from (
13
) that
W
is always positive, provided that the extremal mass of the black hole is a local minimum for fixed the charges
Q_{i}

i.e.

M(T_{\epsilon},Q_{i})-M_{\text{ext}}(Q_{i})>0\,,\quad\leftrightarrow\quad W>0\,.

(26)

Therefore,
W>0
is closely related to the black hole no-hair theorem
[
25
,
26
]
, which states that a black hole of given mass, charge and angular momentum is uniquely defined. We must have
M>M_{\rm ext}
; otherwise, two black holes with the same mass and charges could have different temperatures. This is because, for fixed charges, the mass is a function of temperature with
M(T=0)=M_{\rm ext}
. If
M(T)<M_{\rm ext}
as
T\rightarrow 0
, there should be a minimum mass
M_{\rm min}<M_{\rm ext}
at certain non-zero temperature, since it is reasonable to assume that black hole mass is unbound above. In this scenario, there would then exist at least two black holes of the same mass slightly above the minimum mass
M_{\rm min}
, but with different temperatures, violating the uniqueness property of black holes. It is also worth pointing out that
W=\lim_{T\rightarrow 0}T^{-1}C_{Q_{i}}
. Thus the sign of
W
, of an extremal black hole, is the same as the sign of the specific heat capacity
C_{Q_{i}}
of the corresponding near-extremal black hole.

Having established that
W>0
, we thus conclude from (
24
) and (
25
) that WCCC cannot be violated by the gedanken experiment at the second order. On the other hand, the no-hair theorem is relatively weak, and we might entertain the possibility of
W<0
, even though no such an exotic example is known to exist. In this case,
M_{\rm ext}
becomes a local maximum and it follows that
X_{\epsilon}
or
X_{\rm ext}
should be
negative
in order to protect WCCC. Thus WCCC is still protected for extremal black hole, while it could be violated for the near-extremal cases, since (
24
) is now insufficient to ensure that
X_{\epsilon}
is negative. The fact that WCCC is always protected in the extremal case, regardless the sign of
W
, is intriguing.

Our discussion has been focused on the asymptotically-flat spacetimes, since
by geodesic calculation, one can verify that NEC for an infalling test particle to reach the horizon is equivalent to imposing the second law of thermodynamics
\delta S>0

[
6
,
16
]
. (See Appendix A for the KN black hole example). However, such a connection is lacking when the black holes are asymptotic to (A)dS spacetimes, where the energy and angular momenta of a geodesic particle are less well-defined. Nevertheless, we may still discuss the implication of
\delta S>0
, or more precisely the second-order condition (
19
), on WCCC for asymptotic (A)dS black holes.

Since our test on WCCC (
24
) depends on the sign of
W
, which is a local quantity on the horizon, our conclusion remains the same for asymptotic AdS black holes. However, subtleties arise for asymptotically-dS black holes, since there exists a cosmic horizon
r_{c}
. In general such a charged and/or rotating black hole has inner, outer and cosmic horizons
(r_{-},r_{+},r_{c})
. We shall not be concerned with the situation of having a double root
r_{+}\sim r_{c}
, even though it has
W<0
, since it indicates the collapse of the static regions of universe; it is a case beyond the consideration of WCCC. For extremal or near extremal black holes with
r_{+}-r_{-}\sim 0\ll r_{c}-r_{+}
, the conclusion is the same regarding the WCCC. However, it was suggested in
[
23
]
that when a cosmic horizon is involved, one should consider “total” entropy
\widetilde{S}=S_{+}+S_{c}
. As we can see in the Appendix E, the corresponding temperature
\widetilde{T}
and electric potential
\widetilde{\Phi}
must be

\widetilde{T}={\frac{T_{+}T_{c}}{T_{+}+T_{c}}}\,,\qquad\widetilde{\Phi}_{i}={\frac{T_{c}}{T_{+}+T_{c}}}\Phi_{+,i}+{\frac{T_{+}}{T_{+}+T_{c}}}\Phi_{c,i}\,.

(27)

Consequently, the first law
dM=\widetilde{T}d\widetilde{S}+\widetilde{\Phi}_{i}dQ_{i}
is satisfied with the same mass and charges. In this case, the condition (
19
) should be replaced with
\delta\widetilde{S}=0
and
\delta^{2}\widetilde{S}>0
. Then the conclusion (
24
) takes the same form, but with
S
and
T
replaced by the corresponding tilded values. In particular, we expect that there is no WCCC violation owing to the fact that
\widetilde{W}
is positive. This ensures that our main conclusion in this paper does not change, whichever entropy definition one takes. It is worth pointing out that in the extremal limit, both definitions of the temperature vanish, namely
T_{+}=0=\widetilde{T}
. In Appendix E.2, we illustrate with the KN-dS black hole that

\Big({\frac{\partial S_{+}}{\partial T_{+}}}\Big)_{Q;T_{+}=0}=\Big({\frac{\partial\widetilde{S}}{\partial\widetilde{T}}}\Big)_{Q;\widetilde{T}=0}\,.

(28)

However, whether this is generally true or not does not affect our conclusion on the dS black holes.

Conclusion and discussion

In this paper, we have proposed a novel approach to examine the WCCC in the framework of gedanken experiments, using Eqs. (
7
) and (
8
) for near-extremal and extremal black holes, respectively. This approach allows us to systematically examine the WCCC by providing general formulae for different types discussed in recent decades and enables us to efficiently and clearly solve the contradictions presented in many studies, such as
[
3
,
19
,
20
,
21
,
22
,
7
,
5
,
9
,

,

,
12
,
6
,

,

,

,

,
17
,
18
,
23
]
. We categorized well-known types of WCCC violations as Hubeny’s type
[
7
]
, mixed type
[
6
]
, and SW type
[
19
]
, and derived general formulae in Eqs. (
11
), (
14
), and (
24
), respectively for each case, emphasizing the importance of appropriate order expansions and avoiding mixing orders in the near-extremal and extremal limits.

We demonstrated that our general formula for each case provided a concise summary of the results obtained in various papers that claimed to have counterexamples. Focusing on the SW type, we first verified the Wald inequality (
2
) by adopting a new approach of applying the condition (
19
)
[
23
]
. We found that the general testing of WCCC depends on the sign of
W
(
9
) for both near-extremal and extremal black holes, where if
W>0
, WCCC is protected, while for
W<0
, it indicates potential violation near the extreme case. For the extreme case, WCCC is protected regardless the sign of
W
.

The condition
W>0
is in fact an intrinsic property of an extremal black hole, whereas it also appears in the testing of WCCC (
24
) after perturbing the black hole. We illustrated that the
W>0
condition is equivalent to
M(T_{\epsilon},Q_{i})>M_{\text{ext}}(Q_{i})
. This condition is also closely related to the black hole no-hair theorem. We also independently proved
W>0
for the general spherically-symmetric and static extremal black holes.

For asymptotically flat spacetimes, the NEC for test particles reaching the event horizon is linked to
\delta S>0
or can be obtained by geodesic calculations, while this connection is absent for asymptotically (A)dS spacetimes,
where the energy and angular momenta of a geodesic test particle is not well defined. If we assume that the condition (
19
) continues to hold when cosmological constant is involved, we showed, for the same reason of
W>0
, there is no violation of WCCC for (A)dS black holes. However, in the case of dS spacetime, one might consider new thermodynamic system involving both outer and cosmic horizons, with the entropy given by
\widetilde{S}=S_{+}+S_{c}
. We showed that there could be no violation of WCCC again, for
\widetilde{W}>0
.

Our results provide a clear and general framework for analyzing the WCCC in various black hole solutions, reducing the testing of WCCC to only examining the sign of
W
. This approach helps to avoid misunderstandings of the conceptual and technical issues, ensuring the validity of the calculations. Future work could extend these general formulas to more complex spacetimes and higher-order gravities to examine the possibility of WCCC violation in asymptotically flat or AdS/dS spacetimes. Furthermore, quantum and back-reaction effects could be included in the calculation process. Additionally, exploring the role of different energy conditions and their implications on WCCC in various black hole scenarios or theories could provide deeper insights into understanding WCCC in gedanken experiments.

Hubeny and mixed types

According to

\Delta=r^{2}+a^{2}+Q^{2}-2Mr=0

\partial_{r}\Delta=0
simultaneously leads to

M_{\text{ext}}=r_{\text{ext}}=\frac{\sqrt{Q^{2}+\sqrt{4J^{2}+Q^{4}}}}{\sqrt{2}}.

(29)

The thermodynamic properties of the KN BH are as

\phi_{a}=\frac{a}{r_{+}^{2}+a^{2}},\qquad T=\frac{r_{+}-M}{2\pi(r_{+}^{2}+a^{2})},\qquad\phi_{Q}=\frac{r_{+}Q}{r_{+}^{2}+a^{2}},\qquad S=\pi(r_{+}^{2}+a^{2}).

The first law reads
\delta M=T\delta S+\phi_{a}\delta J+\phi_{Q}\delta Q
,
where
\delta
denotes variations in the KN BH’s parameters. For an infalling particle with energy
E
, angular momentum
L
, and charge
q
, the conserved energy and angular momentum are

E=-(mu_{\mu}+qA_{\mu})\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\right)^{\mu},\quad L=(mu_{\mu}+qA_{\mu})\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi}\right)^{\mu},

where

is its four-velocity. Given

\dot{t}=\frac{g_{\phi\phi}(E+qA_{t})+g_{t\phi}(L-qA_{\phi})}{m(g_{t\phi}^{2}-g_{\phi\phi}g_{tt})}.

To ensure a future-pointed velocity,
\dot{t}>0
is necessary, which implies that the numerator of the above relation must be positive. This provides a lower bound for
E
, which can be evaluated at the outer horizon of the BH, yielding

E\geq\frac{aL+qQr_{+}}{a^{2}+r_{+}^{2}}.

(30)

If the particle crosses through the outer horizon, then the mass, charge, and angular momentum of the BH change. Meanwhile, some energy of the particle is lost by radiation. Thus, according to the conservation laws,

\displaystyle\delta M<E,\quad\delta J=L,\quad\delta Q=q.

(31)

Here we assume that the initial spin of the particle aligns with the axis of symmetry of the BH. By symmetry, it remains parallel to the BH’s axis after absorption, ensuring that the BH remains axisymmetric and that any radiation from the particle does not carry away any angular momentum. Considering
\delta S>0
and
\delta M<E
, this equation is equivalent to the inequality obtained from the first law as

E>\delta M\geq\phi_{i}\delta Q_{i}=\frac{a\delta J+Qr_{+}\delta Q}{a^{2}+r_{+}^{2}}.

(32)

Hence, if the black hole is slightly perturbed by a probe, the change in the BH’s parameters should satisfy (
32
), or equivalently (
30

Since we are interested in destroying the horizon (
M^{2}<Q^{2}+a^{2}
) by throwing a particle into it, we must have

(\delta M+M)^{2}-(Q+q)^{2}-\left(\frac{aM+L}{M+\delta M}\right)^{2}<0\,,

(33)

or equivalently

(E+M)^{2}-(Q+q)^{2}-\left(\frac{aM+L}{M+E}\right)^{2}<0\,.

(34)

W>0
for spherically-symmetric and static black holes

Here, we provide an independent proof of the positivity of
W
, defined as (

), for spherically-symmetric and static black holes

ds^{2}=-e^{2\chi}fdt^{2}+{\frac{dr^{2}}{f}}+r^{2}d\Omega_{2}^{2}\,,

(49)

The
f
and
\chi
are functions of

f=f(r,M,Q_{i})\,,\qquad\chi=\chi(r,M,Q_{i})\,.

(50)

The horizon radius
r_{+}
satisfies
f(r_{+},M,Q_{i})=0
, which implies that
M=M(r_{+},Q_{i})
. The temperature is then given by

T={\frac{e^{\chi}f^{\prime}(r,M(r_{+}),Q_{i})}{4\pi}}\Big|_{r=r_{+}}\,.

(51)

A prime denotes a derivative with respect to

\Big({\frac{\partial S}{\partial T}}\Big)_{Q_{i}}=\frac{8\pi^{2}r_{+}\,e^{-\chi}}{f^{\prime\prime}+2\pi T(2e^{-\chi}\chi^{\prime}+r_{+}\partial_{M}f^{\prime})-8\pi^{2}r_{+}T^{2}\partial_{M}e^{-\chi}}\bigg|_{r=r_{+},\,M=M(r_{+})}\,.

Here we used the thermodynamic identity that
\partial_{r_{+}}M=2\pi r_{+}T

T\sim f^{\prime}(r_{+})=0\,,\qquad f^{\prime\prime}(r_{+})>0\,.

(53)

W=\Big({\frac{\partial S}{\partial T}}\Big)_{Q_{i};T=0}=\frac{8\pi^{2}r_{+}e^{-\chi(r_{+})}}{f^{\prime\prime}(r_{+})}>0\,.

(54)

Appendix E
Asymptotically-dS black holes

In the following, we study the thermodynamic properties of asymptotically-dS black holes. In particular, we consider the thermodynamics with the entropy as the total sum of those associated the outer and cosmic horizons
r_{+}<r_{c}
, each of which satisfies the respective first law:

dM=T_{+}dS_{+}+\Phi_{+,i}dQ_{i}\,,\qquad dM=T_{c}dS_{c}+\Phi_{c,i}dQ_{i}\,.

(66)

It is clear that
T_{c}<0
and
T_{+}>0
, with both
S_{+}
and

being positive. Now one can write the two differential equations as

{\frac{1}{T_{+}}}dM=dS_{+}+{\frac{\Phi_{+,i}}{T_{+}}}dQ_{i}\,,\qquad{\frac{1}{T_{c}}}dM=dS_{c}+{\frac{\Phi_{c,i}}{T_{c}}}dQ_{i}\,,

(67)

which gives

\Big({\frac{1}{T_{+}}}+{\frac{1}{T_{c}}}\Big)dM=d(S_{+}+S_{-})+\Big({\frac{\Phi_{+}}{T_{+}}}+{\frac{\Phi_{c}}{T_{c,i}}}\Big)dQ_{i}\,.

(68)

This leads to

{\frac{1}{\widetilde{T}}}={\frac{1}{T_{+}}}+{\frac{1}{T_{c}}}\,,\qquad\widetilde{S}=S_{+}+S_{c}\,,\qquad\widetilde{\Phi}_{i}=\Big({\frac{\Phi_{+,i}}{T_{+}}}+{\frac{\Phi_{c,i}}{T_{c}}}\Big)\widetilde{T}\,,

(69)

which satisfy the first law of the same mass and charge, namely

dM=\widetilde{T}d\widetilde{S}+\widetilde{\Phi}_{i}dQ_{i}\,.

(70)

It is important to note that in the extremal
T_{+}=0
limit, we have
\widetilde{T}=0
. We can thus re-derive the inequality (
24
) based on the above first law and the inequality condition (
19
), all with
(T,S,\Phi)
replaced by the corresponding tilded values. The outcome then depends on the sign of
\widetilde{W}

\widetilde{W}=\Big({\frac{\partial\widetilde{S}}{\partial\widetilde{T}}}\Big)_{Q_{i};\widetilde{T}=0}\,.

(71)

It is clear that analogous argument leads to
\widetilde{W}>0
. In what follows, we consider RN-dS and KN-dS black holes where
\widetilde{W}=W
.

E.1
RN-dS BH

Here we present the RN-dS black hole as a concrete example. The metric profile function
f

f=-{\textstyle{\frac{\scriptstyle 1}{\scriptstyle 3}}}\Lambda r^{2}+1-{\frac{2M}{r^{2}}}+{\frac{Q^{2}}{r^{2}}}\,.

(72)

For appropriate mass
M
and charge
Q
, there are three positive roots, and we label them as
r_{-}\leq r_{+}<r_{c}
. Then the fourth root must be negative, namely
-(r_{-}+r_{+}+r_{c})
, and one can write

f=-\frac{\left(r-r_{-}\right)\left(r-r_{+}\right)\left(r-r_{c}\right)\left(r+r_{c}+r_{-}+r_{+}\right)}{r^{2}\left(r_{-}r_{c}+r_{c}^{2}+r_{+}r_{c}+r_{-}^{2}+r_{+}r_{-}+r_{+}^{2}\right)}\,.

(73)

The mass, charge, and the cosmological constant can be expressed in terms of the three horizon radii as

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(r_{-}+r_{+}\right)\left(r_{c}+r_{-}\right)\left(r_{c}+r_{+}\right)}{2\left(r_{c}^{2}+\left(r_{-}+r_{+}\right)r_{c}+r_{-}^{2}+r_{+}r_{-}+r_{+}^{2}\right)}\,,

\displaystyle Q^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{r_{-}r_{+}r_{c}\left(r_{c}+r_{-}+r_{+}\right)}{r_{-}\left(r_{c}+r_{+}\right)+r_{c}^{2}+r_{+}r_{c}+r_{-}^{2}+r_{+}^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle\Lambda

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{3}{r_{c}^{2}+\left(r_{-}+r_{+}\right)r_{c}+r_{-}^{2}+r_{+}r_{-}+r_{+}^{2}}\,.

(74)

It is encouraging to see that all the above three quantities are positive. We further have

\displaystyle T_{-}=\frac{\left(r_{-}-r_{+}\right)\left(r_{c}-r_{-}\right)\left(r_{c}+2r_{-}+r_{+}\right)}{4\pi r_{-}^{2}\left(r_{c}^{2}+\left(r_{-}+r_{+}\right)r_{c}+r_{-}^{2}+r_{+}r_{-}+r_{+}^{2}\right)}<0\,,

\displaystyle T_{+}=\frac{\left(r_{+}-r_{-}\right)\left(r_{c}-r_{+}\right)\left(r_{c}+r_{-}+2r_{+}\right)}{4\pi r_{+}^{2}\left(r_{c}^{2}+\left(r_{-}+r_{+}\right)r_{c}+r_{-}^{2}+r_{+}r_{-}+r_{+}^{2}\right)}>0\,,

\displaystyle T_{c}=-\frac{\left(r_{c}-r_{-}\right)\left(r_{c}-r_{+}\right)\left(2r_{c}+r_{-}+r_{+}\right)}{4\pi r_{c}^{2}\left(r_{c}^{2}+\left(r_{-}+r_{+}\right)r_{c}+r_{-}^{2}+r_{+}r_{-}+r_{+}^{2}\right)}<0\,,

(75)

\displaystyle\widetilde{T}=\frac{\left(r_{+}-r_{-}\right)\left(r_{c}-r_{-}\right)\left(r_{c}+r_{-}+2r_{+}\right)\left(2r_{c}+r_{-}+r_{+}\right)}{4\pi\left(r_{c}+r_{+}\right)\left(\left(r_{-}-r_{+}\right)r_{c}+r_{-}\left(r_{-}+r_{+}\right)\right)\left(r_{c}^{2}+\left(r_{-}+r_{+}\right)r_{c}+r_{-}^{2}+r_{+}r_{-}+r_{+}^{2}\right)}>0\,.

The requirement that
\Lambda
and
Q
be constants implies

\displaystyle dr_{-}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{r_{-}\left(r_{+}-r_{c}\right)\left(r_{c}+r_{-}+2r_{+}\right)}{r_{+}\left(r_{-}-r_{c}\right)\left(r_{c}+2r_{-}+r_{+}\right)}\,dr_{+}\,,

\displaystyle dr_{c}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(r_{-}-r_{+}\right)r_{c}\left(r_{c}+r_{-}+2r_{+}\right)}{r_{+}\left(r_{c}-r_{-}\right)\left(2r_{c}+r_{-}+r_{+}\right)}\,dr_{+}\,.

(76)

We can now calculate

\Big({\frac{\partial S_{+}}{\partial T_{+}}}\Big)_{Q;T_{+}=0}=\frac{4\pi^{2}r_{+}^{3}\left(2r_{+}r_{c}+r_{c}^{2}+3r_{+}^{2}\right)}{\left(r_{c}-r_{+}\right)\left(r_{c}+3r_{+}\right)}=\Big({\frac{\partial\widetilde{S}}{\partial\widetilde{T}}}\Big)_{Q;\widetilde{T}=0}\,.

(77)

Note that
T_{+}=0\leftrightarrow\widetilde{T}=0
, achieved by setting
r_{-}=r_{+}
.
For
r_{c}>r_{+}
, the above two (same) quantities are both positive, indicating that the RN-dS black holes cannot violate WCCC.

E.2
KN-dS BH

We now consider the more general KN-dS black hole, where the horizon is determined by
[
33
]

\Delta=(r^{2}+a^{2})\left(1-{\textstyle{\frac{\scriptstyle 1}{\scriptstyle 3}}}\Lambda r^{2}\right)-2mr+q^{2}\,.

(78)

The root of
\Delta
is denoted as
r_{i}
, with
i=-,+,c
, associated with inner, outer, and cosmic horizons. The temperature and entropies are then given by

T_{i}=\frac{r_{i}^{2}-a^{2}-q^{2}}{4\pi r_{i}\left(a^{2}+r_{i}^{2}\right)}-\frac{\Lambda r_{i}\left(a^{2}+3r_{i}^{2}\right)}{12\pi\left(a^{2}+r_{i}^{2}\right)}\,,\qquad S_{i}={\frac{\pi(r_{i}^{2}+a^{2})}{\Xi}}\,,

(79)

where

\Xi=1+{\textstyle{\frac{\scriptstyle 1}{\scriptstyle 3}}}\Lambda a^{2}\,.

(80)

We can express the three parameters
(m,q,\Lambda)
in terms of
(r_{-},r_{+},r_{c})
, given by

\displaystyle m

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(r_{-}+r_{+}\right)\left(r_{c}+r_{-}\right)\left(r_{c}+r_{+}\right)}{2\left(a^{2}+r_{c}^{2}+\left(r_{-}+r_{+}\right)r_{c}+r_{-}^{2}+r_{+}^{2}+r_{-}r_{+}\right)}\,,

\displaystyle q^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{r_{-}r_{+}r_{c}\left(r_{c}+r_{-}+r_{+}\right)}{a^{2}+r_{c}^{2}+\left(r_{-}+r_{+}\right)r_{c}+r_{-}^{2}+r_{+}^{2}+r_{-}r_{+}}-a^{2}\,,

\displaystyle\Lambda

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{3}{a^{2}+r_{c}^{2}+\left(r_{-}+r_{+}\right)r_{c}+r_{-}^{2}+r_{+}^{2}+r_{-}r_{+}}\,.

(81)

Now
\Delta
is simply given by

\Delta=-\frac{\left(r-r_{-}\right)\left(r-r_{+}\right)\left(r-r_{c}\right)\left(r_{c}+r+r_{-}+r_{+}\right)}{a^{2}+r_{c}^{2}+\left(r_{-}+r_{+}\right)r_{c}+r_{-}^{2}+r_{+}^{2}+r_{-}r_{+}}\,.

(82)

The mass, charge, and angular momentum are

M={\frac{m}{\Xi^{2}}}\,,\qquad J={\frac{ma}{\Xi^{2}}}\,,\qquad Q={\frac{q}{\Xi}}\,.

(83)

Requiring
\delta J=\delta Q=\delta\Lambda=0
allows us to solve for
\delta r_{-},\delta r_{c}
and
\delta a
in terms of
\delta r_{+}
. In particular, we find that

{\frac{\partial r_{-}}{\partial r_{+}}}\Big|_{Q,J;T=0}=-1\,,\qquad{\frac{\partial r_{c}}{\partial r_{+}}}\Big|_{Q,J;T=0}=0\,,\qquad{\frac{\partial a}{\partial r_{+}}}\Big|_{Q,J;T=0}=0\,.

(84)

We therefore again have

\Big({\frac{\partial S_{+}}{\partial T_{+}}}\Big)_{Q,J;T_{+}=0}=\frac{4\pi^{2}r_{+}\left(a^{2}+r_{+}^{2}\right)\left(a^{2}+r_{c}^{2}+2r_{+}r_{c}+3r_{+}^{2}\right)^{2}}{\left(r_{c}-r_{+}\right)\left(r_{c}+3r_{+}\right)\left(2a^{2}+r_{c}^{2}+2r_{+}r_{c}+3r_{+}^{2}\right)}=\Big({\frac{\partial\widetilde{S}}{\partial\widetilde{T}}}\Big)_{Q,J;\widetilde{T}=0}\,.

(85)

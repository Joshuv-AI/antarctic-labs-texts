Non-integrability of the rational Sasano system of type A 4 ( 2 ) A_{4}^{(2)}
Tsvetana Stoyanova

Abstract
. In this paper we study the integrability the Sasano system of type A 4 ( 2 ) A^{(2)}_{4} from the point of view of the Hamiltonian dynamics. We prove rigorously that for all values of the parameters for which the Sasano system of type A 4 ( 2 ) A^{(2)}_{4} has a particular rational solution it is not integrable by rational first integrals. By an explicit computation we show that the connected component G 0 G^{0} of the unit element of the differential Galois group of the normal variational equations along a simple particular rational solution is a direct product of two groups S ​ L 2 ​ ( ℂ ) SL_{2}({\mathbb{C}}) . Thus the Morales-Ramis theory leads to a non-integrable result. Moreover, using Bäcklund transformations we extend the obtained particular non-integrable result to the entire orbit of the parameters.

One of the essential properties of the Sasano systems is that all of them are time dependent Hamiltonian systems. The question about their integrability from the point of view of

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we briefly recall the Morales-Ramis theory on non-integrability of Hamiltonian systems following
[

,

Consider a complex analytical Hamiltonian system

\displaystyle\dot{x}=X_{H}(x)

(2.5)

with a Hamiltonian
H:M\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
on a complex symplectic manifold

of dimension

. Recall that by the theorem of Liouville-Arnold
[
1
]

the Hamiltonian system (

) is completely integrable if there exist

functionally independent first integrals

and in involution.
Let

be a non-equilibrium particular solution of (
2.5
).
denote by
\Gamma
the phase curve corresponding to this solution. The variational equations
(\textrm{VE})
along
\Gamma
have the form

\,\dot{\xi}=\frac{\partial X_{H}(x(t))}{\partial x}\,\xi,\quad\xi\in T_{\Gamma}M\,.\,

We always can reduce the variational equations using the Hamiltonian in the following sense. Consider the normal bundle of
\Gamma
on the level variety

M_{h}=\{x\,|\,H(x)=h\}
. The projection of the variational equations on this bundle is called the normal variational equations
(\textrm{NVE})
. The dimension of the
(\textrm{NVE})

is
2n-2
. The solutions of the
(\textrm{NVE})
define a Picard-Vessiot extension
L
of the basic differential field
K
of coefficients of the
(\textrm{NVE})
. This in its turn defines
a differential Galois group
G=\textrm{Gal}(L/K)
. Then the main theorem of the Morales-Ramis theory states
[
6
,
7
]

Theorem 2.1
.

(Morales-Ruiz and Ramis)
If the Hamiltonian system (
2.5
) is completely integrable by meromorphic first integrals in a neighborhood of
\Gamma
, not necessarily independent on
\Gamma

itself, then the connected component
G^{0}
of the unit element of the differential Galois group
G=\textrm{Gal}(L/K)
is Abelian.

Theorem
2.1
provides a necessary condition for completely integrability and a sufficient condition for non-integrability of a given Hamiltonian system. That is, if the connected component of the unit
element of the Galois group
G
is not Abelian, then the corresponding Hamiltonian system (
2.5
) is not integrable by meromorphic first integrals.

(3.34)

The point
\eta=0
is a regular singularity for the equation (
3.34
). The characteristic equation
for
\eta=0
is given by

\,\rho\,(\rho-1)+\frac{2}{9}=0\,.\,

The corresponding characteristic exponents are
\rho_{1}=\frac{2}{3}
and
\rho_{2}=\frac{1}{3}
. Since

\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}\notin{\mathbb{Z}}
the local theory of regular singularities ensures that the equation (
3.34
)
has two independent local solutions
u_{1}(\eta)
and
\widetilde{u}_{1}(\eta)
near the origin in the form

\,u_{1}(\eta)=\eta^{2/3}\,f_{1}(\eta)\quad\textrm{and}\quad\widetilde{u}_{1}(\eta)=\eta^{1/3}\,f_{2}(\eta)\,.\,

The functions
f_{1}(\eta)
and
f_{2}(\eta)
are holomorphic functions near the origin.
Then the system (
3
) admits a local fundamental matrix solution
\Psi(\eta)
near the origin
\eta=0

in the form

\,\Psi(\eta)=U(\eta)\,\eta^{J}\,,\,

where
U(\eta)
is a holomorphic matrix-function near the origin, and

\,J={\mathrm{diag}}\left(\frac{2}{3},\frac{1}{3},\frac{2}{3},\frac{1}{3}\right)\,.\,

Therefore the system (
3
) admits a local fundamental matrix solution near the origin in the form

\,\Psi(\tau)=U(\tau^{6})\,\tau^{J_{1}}\,,\,

where
J_{1}=6\,J={\mathrm{diag}}(4,2,4,2)
. Thus the origin
\tau=0
is an apparent singularity for the system
(
3
).

This ends the proof.
∎

Proposition 3.4
.

The connected component
(G_{1})^{0}
of the unit element of the differential Galois group
G_{1}
of the system (
3
) is a direct product of two groups
SL_{2}({\mathbb{C}})
,
i.e. it has the form
(G_{1})^{0}=SL_{2}({\mathbb{C}})\times SL_{2}({\mathbb{C}})
.

Proof.

We will show that the local differential Galois group at
\eta=\infty
of each systems (
3
) coincides with
\textrm{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{C}})
.
The change of the variable

\displaystyle\eta=\frac{3}{\sqrt{-\lambda_{i}\,\lambda_{j}}}\,\zeta

(3.35)

takes the equation (
3.34
) into the equation

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}u_{1}}{d\zeta^{2}}-\left[\frac{1}{4}-\frac{\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{i}}{4\,\sqrt{-\lambda_{i}\,\lambda_{j}}}\,\frac{1}{\zeta}-\frac{2}{9\,\zeta^{2}}\right]\,u_{1}=0.

(3.36)

The equation (
3.36
) is the Whittaker equation
[
21
]

\,\frac{d^{2}u_{1}}{d\zeta^{2}}-\left[\frac{1}{4}-\frac{\kappa}{\zeta}+\frac{4\,\mu^{2}-1}{4\zeta^{2}}\right]\,u_{1}=0\,

with

\,\kappa=\frac{\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{i}}{4\,\sqrt{-\lambda_{i}\,\lambda_{j}}}=\frac{1}{2},\qquad\mu=\frac{1}{6}\,.\,

In
[
4
]
Martinet and Ramis show that the Whittaker equation has two Stokes matrices at
\zeta=\infty

\,St_{\pi}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
\mu_{1}&1\end{array}\right),\quad St_{0}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&\mu_{2}\\
0&1\end{array}\right)\,,\,

which correspond to the singular directions
\theta_{1}=\pi
and
\theta_{2}=0
. In Proposition 3.3.1 in
[
4
]
they
prove that

(1)

\mu_{1}=0
if and only if
\kappa-\mu\in\frac{1}{2}+{\mathbb{N}}
or
\kappa+\mu\in\frac{1}{2}+{\mathbb{N}}

(2)

\mu_{2}=0
if and only if
-\kappa-\mu\in\frac{1}{2}+{\mathbb{N}}
or
-\kappa+\mu\in\frac{1}{2}+{\mathbb{N}}
.

Since
\kappa\pm\mu=\frac{1}{2}\pm\frac{1}{6}\notin\frac{1}{2}+{\mathbb{N}}
and

-\kappa\pm\mu=-\frac{1}{2}\pm\frac{1}{6}\notin\frac{1}{2}+{\mathbb{N}}
we have that the equation
(
3.36
) has two non-trivial Stokes matrices. Since the exponential torus
{\mathcal{T}}
are given by
[
4
]

\,{\mathcal{T}}=\left\{\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&0\\
0&a^{-1}\end{array}\right)\,|\,a\in{\mathbb{C}}^{*}\right\}\,

we conclude that the local differential Galois group of the equation (
3.36
) at
\zeta=\infty
is the group
SL_{2}({\mathbb{C}})
,
[
4
]
.
The substitution (
3.35
) changes the local Galois group at
\zeta=\infty
of the equation (
3.34
) but remains its connected component of the unit element.
Thus the connected component of the unit element of the local differential Galois group at
\eta=\infty
of the system (
3

SL_{2}({\mathbb{C}})
.
Therefore connected component of the unit element of the local differential Galois group at
\eta=\infty
of the systems (
3

SL_{2}({\mathbb{C}})
,
i.e. it has the form

SL_{2}({\mathbb{C}})\times SL_{2}({\mathbb{C}})
.
From Proposition

3.3
it follows that the differential Galois group of the system (

The transformation

This ends the proof.

4.
Bäcklund transformations and generalization

The Sasano systems (
1
) admits an affine Weyl group of symmetry of type
A^{(2)}_{4}
which acts on it as a group of Bäcklund transformations. In this section using the
Bäcklund transformations of the system (
1
) we extend the non-integrable result of Theorem
3.6
to the entire orbit of the parameters
\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha_{2}
.

The Bäcklund transformations of the Sasano system (
1
) are defined by

\displaystyle s_{0}\,:\,(x,y,z,w,t;\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle\left(x,y,z+\frac{\alpha_{0}}{w},w,t;-\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{0},\alpha_{2}\right),

(4.38)

\displaystyle s_{1}\,:\,(x,y,z,w,t;\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle\left(x,y-\frac{\alpha_{1}}{x+z^{2}},z,w-\frac{2\alpha_{1}\,z}{x+z^{2}},t;\alpha_{0}+2\alpha_{1},-\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{1}\right),

\displaystyle s_{2}\,:\,(x,y,z,w,t;\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle\left(x+\frac{2\alpha_{2}\,y}{f_{2}}-\frac{\alpha_{2}^{2}}{f^{2}_{2}},y-\frac{\alpha_{2}}{f_{2}},z+\frac{\alpha_{2}}{f_{2}},w,t;\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1}+2\alpha_{2},-\alpha_{2}\right)\,,

where
f_{2}:=x+y^{2}+w+t
.

Remark 4.1
.

When one of the invariant divisors
f_{0}=w,f_{1}=x+z^{2}
or
f_{2}=x+y^{2}+w+t
vanishes the parameters become
\alpha_{0}=0,\alpha_{1}=0
or

\alpha_{2}=0
, respectively. So, when one of the divisors
f_{i},i=0,1,2
vanishes the corresponding transformation
s_{i},i=0,1,2
acts as an identity transformation.

The transformations (
4.38
) satisfy the relations

\,s_{i}^{2}=1,\quad(s_{0}\,s_{1})^{4}=(s_{1}\,s_{0})^{4}=1,\quad(s_{0}\,s_{2})^{2}=(s_{2}\,s_{0})^{2}=1,\quad(s_{1}\,s_{2})^{4}=(s_{2}\,s_{1})^{4}=1.\,

Hence the transformations
s_{i},i=0,1,2
generate the affine Weyl group
W(A^{(2)}_{4})
.

In
[
5
]
Matsuda proves that acting on the solution (
3.6
) by Bäcklund transformations (
4.38
) we obtain a rational solution of the Sasano system
(
1
) with parameters satisfying one of the following condition:

\displaystyle 5\alpha_{2}-1/2\equiv 0\,\textrm{ mod}\,5,

\displaystyle 5\alpha_{1}-1\equiv 0,2\,\textrm{mod}\,5,

\displaystyle 5\alpha_{2}-1/2\equiv 1\,\textrm{ mod}\,5,

\displaystyle 5\alpha_{1}-1\equiv 2,3\,\textrm{mod}\,5,

(4.39)

\displaystyle 5\alpha_{2}-1/2\equiv 3\,\textrm{ mod}\,5,

\displaystyle 5\alpha_{1}-1\equiv 0,1\,\textrm{mod}\,5,

\displaystyle 5\alpha_{2}-1/2\equiv 4\,\textrm{ mod}\,5,

\displaystyle 5\alpha_{1}-1\equiv 1,3\,\textrm{mod}\,5\,.

Then we have the main result of this paper

Theorem 4.2
.

Assume that the parameters
\alpha_{i},i=0,1,2
satisfy one of the conditions (
4
). Then the Sasano system of type
A^{(2)}_{4}
(
1

Proof.

The proof follows from the fact that the Bäcklund transformations (

[

]
.
∎

5.
Conclusion

In this paper we strictly prove that when the Sasano system of type
A^{(2)}_{4}
admits a particular rational solution it is not integrable by rational first integrals.
The main approach for finding an obstruction to integrability is the Morales-Ramis theory which reduces the problem of integrability of a given Hamiltonian system
to the problem of the computation of the differential Galois group of the normal variational equations along a non-equilibrium particular solution.
We explicitly show that the connected component
G^{0}
of the unit element of the differential
Galois group of the normal variational equations along a simple rational solution is of the form
SL_{2}({\mathbb{C}})\times SL_{2}({\mathbb{C}})
which is not Abelian.
The obtained precise particular non-integrable result is extended to the entire orbit of the parameters by Bäcklund transformations.

It is tempting to utilize the Galois approach of non-integrability to other Sasano systems which have rational or algebraic particular solutions.
We believe that the study the integrability of the Sasano systems
of dimension four and higher will have influence on the development of the algebraic groups and Lie groups theory (classification of the algebraic subgroups of the group
SL_{4}({\mathbb{C}})
),
of the asymptotic and summability theory through the Stokes phenomenon and their applications to the mathematical physics.

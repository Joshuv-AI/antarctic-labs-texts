WEIGHTED RIESZ BASES IN G-FUSION FRAMES AND THEIR PERTURBATION
GHOLAMREZA RAHIMLOU, Vahid Sadri, Reza Ahmadi

Abstract
. In this paper, we introduce orthonoramal and Riesz bases for g-fusion frames and will show that the weights have basic roles. Next, we prove an effective theorem between frames and g-fusion frames by using an operator. Finally, perturbations of g-fusion frames will be presented.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 42C15; Secondary 46C99, 41A58

1.
Introduction

Bases play a prominent role in discrete frames and their studying can extract interesting properties from the frames. One of the most important types of bases are orthonormal bases and also, as their special case, the Riesz basis. The Riesz basis has been defined in
[
6
]
by the image of orthonormal bases with a bounded bijective operator. Afterwards, this basis has been equalized in a property with complete sequences and inequalities for the synthesis operator. Sun in
[
17
]
could introduce a Riesz basis for g-frames by using that property and we will continue his method in Section 3 for g-fusion frames. In Section 4, we present a useful operator for characterizations of these frames and finally in Section 5, a perturbation of these frames will be studied.

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout this paper,
H
and
K
are separable Hilbert spaces and
\mathcal{B}(H,K)
is the collection of all bounded linear operators of
H
into
K
. If
K=H
, then
\mathcal{B}(H,H)
will be denoted by
\mathcal{B}(H)
. Also,
\pi_{V}
is the orthogonal projection from
H
onto a closed subspace
V\subset H
and
\{H_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}}
is a sequence of Hilbert spaces, where
\mathbb{J}
is a subset of
\mathbb{Z}
. It is easy to check that if
u\in\mathcal{B}(H)
is an invertible operator, then

\pi_{uV}u\pi_{V}=u\pi_{V}.

We define the space
\mathscr{H}_{2}:=(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}\oplus H_{j})_{\ell_{2}}
by

(1)

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}_{2}=\big\{\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}}\ :\ f_{j}\in H_{j},\ \sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}\|f_{j}\|^{2}<\infty\big\},

with the inner product defined by

\langle\{f_{j}\},\{g_{j}\}\rangle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}\langle f_{j},g_{j}\rangle.

It is clear that
\mathscr{H}_{2}
is a Hilbert space with pointwise operations.

Definition 1
.

Let
W=\{W_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}}
be a collection of closed subspaces of
H
,
\{v_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}}
be a family of weights, i.e.
v_{j}>0
and
\Lambda_{j}\in\mathcal{B}(H,H_{j})
for each
j\in\mathbb{J}
. We say
\Lambda:=(W_{j},\Lambda_{j},v_{j})
is a generalized fusion frame (or g-fusion frame) for
H
if there exists
0<A\leq B<\infty
such that for each
f\in H

(2)

\displaystyle A\|f\|^{2}\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}^{2}\|\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f\|^{2}\leq B\|f\|^{2}.

We call
\Lambda
a Parseval g-fusion frame if
A=B=1
. When the right hand side of (
2
) holds,
\Lambda
is called a g-fusion Bessel sequence for
H
with bound
B
. Throughout this paper,
\Lambda
will be a triple
(W_{j},\Lambda_{j},v_{j})
with
j\in\mathbb{J}
unless otherwise noted.

The synthesis and analysis operators in the g-fusion frames are defined by

\displaystyle T_{\Lambda}

\displaystyle:\mathscr{H}_{2}\longrightarrow H\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ ,\ \ \ \ \ \ T_{\Lambda}^{*}:H\longrightarrow\mathscr{H}_{2}

\displaystyle T_{\Lambda}(\{f_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda_{j}^{*}f_{j}\ \ \ ,\ \ \ \ T_{\Lambda}^{*}(f)=\{v_{j}\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}}.

Thus, the g-fusion frame operator is given by

S_{\Lambda}f=T_{\Lambda}T^{*}_{\Lambda}f=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}^{2}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f

and

(3)

\langle S_{\Lambda}f,f\rangle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}^{2}\|\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f\|^{2}.

Therefore

AI\leq S_{\Lambda}\leq BI.

This means that
S_{\Lambda}
is a bounded, positive and invertible operator (with adjoint inverse). So, we have the reconstruction formula for any
f\in H
:

(4)

f=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}^{2}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}S^{-1}_{\Lambda}f=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}^{2}S^{-1}_{\Lambda}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f.

For the proof of followings, see
[
16
]
.

\Lambda
is a g-fusion Bessel sequence for

T_{\Lambda}

\tilde{\Lambda}:=(S^{-1}_{\Lambda}W_{j},\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}S_{\Lambda}^{-1},v_{j})
with g-fusion frame operator
S_{\tilde{\Lambda}}=T_{\tilde{\Lambda}}T^{*}_{\tilde{\Lambda}}
is called the (canonical) dual g-fusion frame of
\Lambda
.

Now, we can obtain

(5)

\displaystyle f=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}^{2}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}\tilde{\Lambda_{j}}\pi_{\tilde{W_{j}}}f=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}^{2}\pi_{\tilde{W_{j}}}\tilde{\Lambda_{j}}^{*}\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f,

where
\tilde{W_{j}}:=S^{-1}_{\Lambda}W_{j}\ ,\ \tilde{\Lambda_{j}}:=\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}S_{\Lambda}^{-1}.

.

It is easy to check that
\Lambda
is gf-complete if and only if

\{f:\ \Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f=0,\ j\in\mathbb{J}\}=\{0\}.

, then

3.
gf-Riesz and Orthonormal Bases

Definition 4
.

Let
W=\{W_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}}
be a collection of closed subspaces of
H
and
j\in\mathbb{J}
.
We say that
(W_{j},\Lambda_{j})
is a gf-orthonormal bases for
H
with respect to
\{v_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}}
, if

(6)

\displaystyle\langle v_{i}\pi_{W_{i}}\Lambda^{*}_{i}g_{i},v_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}g_{j}\rangle=\delta_{i,j}\langle g_{i},g_{j}\rangle\ ,\ \ \ i,j\in\mathbb{J}\ ,\ \ \ g_{i}\in H_{i}\ ,\ \ \ g_{j}\in H_{j}

(7)

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}^{2}\|\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f\|^{2}=\|f\|^{2}\ ,\ \ \ \ f\in H.

Definition 5
.

\Lambda=(W_{j},\Lambda_{j},v_{j})
is called a gf-Riesz basis for
H
if

(1)

\Lambda
is gf-complete,

(2)

There exist
0<A\leq B<\infty
such that for each finite subset
\mathbb{I}\subseteq\mathbb{J}
and
g_{j}\in H_{j}
,
j\in\mathbb{I}
,

(8)

A\sum_{j\in\mathbb{I}}\|g_{j}\|^{2}\leq\big\|\sum_{j\in\mathbb{I}}v_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}g_{j}\big\|^{2}\leq B\sum_{j\in\mathbb{I}}\|g_{j}\|^{2}.

It is easy to check that if
\Lambda
is a gf-Riesz bases for
H
, then the operator
T_{\Lambda}
which is defined by

\displaystyle T_{\Lambda}

\displaystyle:\mathscr{H}_{2}\longrightarrow H

\displaystyle T_{\Lambda}(\{g_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda_{j}^{*}g_{j},

is injetive.

Proposition 2
.

Let
\Lambda=(W_{j},\Lambda_{j},v_{j})
be a g-fusion frame for
H
and suppose that (
6
) holds. Then
\Lambda
is a gf-orthonormal basis for
H
.

Proof.

Assume that
S_{\Lambda}
is the g-fusion frame operator of
\Lambda
and

M:=\{f\in H\ :\ S_{\Lambda}f=f\}.

It is clear that
M
is a non-empty closed subspace of
H
. Let
f\in H
and
k\in\mathbb{J}
. Since

v_{j}v_{k}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}\pi_{W_{k}}\Lambda^{*}_{k}\Lambda_{k}\pi_{W_{k}}f=\delta_{j,k}\pi_{W_{k}}\Lambda^{*}_{k}\Lambda_{k}\pi_{W_{k}}f,

then
\pi_{W_{k}}\Lambda^{*}_{k}\Lambda_{k}\pi_{W_{k}}f\in M
. So, for any
h\in M^{\perp}
and
g\in M
we have, for all
j\in\mathbb{J}

\langle\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}h,g\rangle=\langle h,\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}g\rangle=0.

Thus
\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}h=0
for each
j\in\mathbb{J}
, and so
\|\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}h\|=0
. By definition of g-fusion frame, we obtain
h=0
. Therefore,
M^{\perp}=\{0\}
and we conclude
H=M
. So,
S_{\Lambda}=id_{H}
and the proof is completed.
∎

Theorem 3.1
.

\Lambda
is a gf-orthonormal bases for
H
if and only if

(I)

v_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}
is isometric for any
j\in\mathbb{J}
;

(II)

\bigoplus_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}(H_{j})=H
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\Lambda
is a gf-orthonormal basis for
H
. If
j\in\mathbb{J}
and
g\in H_{j}
, we have

\langle v_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}g,v_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}g\rangle=\delta_{j,j}\langle g,g\rangle=\langle g,g\rangle.

Then,
v_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}
is isometric for any
j\in\mathbb{J}
. So,
v_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}(H_{j})
is a closed subspace of
H
. Therefore, for each
j\in\mathbb{J}
and
f\in H
, we have from (
7
)

\displaystyle\langle f,f\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}^{2}\langle\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f,\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}^{2}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f,f\rangle.

Thus, for each
f\in H
,

f=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}^{2}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f.

By letting
g_{j}:=v_{j}\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f
, we obtain

f=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}g_{j}
and

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}^{2}\|\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}g_{j}\|^{2}=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}\|g_{j}\|^{2}=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}^{2}\|\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f\|^{2}=\|f\|^{2}.

So,
\bigoplus_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}(H_{j})=H
. Conversely, if (I) , (II) are satisfied, then (
6
) is clear. Indeed, for each
i\neq j
,
v_{i}\pi_{W_{i}}\Lambda^{*}_{i}\perp v_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda_{j}
and
v_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}
is isometric. Let
f\in H
, we get from (II) for any
j\in\mathbb{J}
and some
g_{j}\in H_{j}

f=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}g_{j}

and

\|f\|^{2}=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}^{2}\|\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}g_{j}\|^{2}=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}\|g_{j}\|^{2}.

Now, let
i\in\mathbb{J}
, then for each
f,h\in H_{j}
,

\displaystyle\langle v_{j}\Lambda_{i}\pi_{W_{i}}f,h\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}^{2}\Lambda_{i}\pi_{W_{i}}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}g_{j},h\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}^{2}\langle\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}g_{j},\pi_{W_{i}}\Lambda^{*}_{i}h\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle v_{i}\pi_{W_{i}}\Lambda^{*}_{i}g_{i},v_{i}\pi_{W_{i}}\Lambda^{*}_{i}h\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle g_{i},h\rangle.

Hence
g_{i}=v_{i}\Lambda_{i}\pi_{W_{i}}f
for each
i\in\mathbb{J}
. So,
f=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}^{2}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f
for all
f\in H
and (
7
) is proved.
∎

Corollary 1
.

Every gf-orthonormal basss for
H
is a gf-Riesz bases for
H
with bounds
A=B=1
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\Theta=(W_{j},\Theta_{j})
be a gf-orthonormal bases with respect to
\{v_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}}
and
\Lambda=(W_{j},\Lambda_{j},v_{j})
be a g-fusion frame for
H
with same weights. Then, there exists a operator
V\in\mathcal{B}(H)
and surjective such that
\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}=\Theta_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}V^{*}
for all
j\in\mathbb{J}
.

Proof.

Let

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle:H\longrightarrow H,

\displaystyle Vf

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}^{2}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}\Theta_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f.

Then,
V
is well-defined and bounded. Indeed, for each finite subset
\mathbb{I}\subseteq\mathbb{J}
and
f\in H
,

\displaystyle\|Vf\|

\displaystyle=\sup_{\|h\|=1}\big|\big\langle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}^{2}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}\Theta_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f,h\big\rangle\big|

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{B}\big(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}^{2}\|\Theta_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f\|^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{B}\|f\|,

where
B
is an upper g-fusion frame bound for
\Lambda
. Therefore, the series is weakly unconditionally Cauchy and so unconditionally convergent in
H
(see
[
7
]
page 58) and also
\|V\|\leq\sqrt{B}
. Since
\Theta
is a gf-orthonormal bases, then

\Theta_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}\pi_{W_{i}}\Theta^{*}_{i}g=(v_{i}v_{j})^{-1}\delta_{i,j}g

and

\displaystyle V\pi_{W_{i}}\Theta^{*}_{i}g

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}^{2}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}\Theta_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}\pi_{W_{i}}\Theta^{*}_{i}g

\displaystyle=\pi_{W_{i}}\Lambda^{*}_{i}g,

for all
g\in H_{j}
and
i\in\mathbb{J}
. Thus
\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}=\Theta_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}V^{*}
. Now, we show that
V
is surjective. Assume that
f\in H
. By Theorem
2.2
, there is
\{g_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}}\in\mathscr{H}_{2}
such that
\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}g_{j}=f
. Let
g:=T_{\Theta}(\{g_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}})
, thus

Vg=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}Vv_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}\Theta^{*}_{j}g_{j}=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}g_{j}=f

and
V
is surjective.
∎

Corollary 2
.

If
\Lambda
is a Parseval g-fusion frame for
H
, then
V^{*}
is isometric.

Corollary 3
.

If
\Lambda
is a gf-Riesz bases for
H
, then
V
is invertible.

Proof.

Let
Vf=0
and
f\in H
. Since
T_{\Lambda}
is injective and

Vf=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}^{2}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}\Theta_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f=T_{\Lambda}T^{*}_{\Theta}f,

therefore,
T^{*}_{\Theta}f=0
. So,
\|f\|^{2}=\|T_{\Theta}f\|^{2}=0
, hence,
f=0
.
∎

Corollary 4
.

If
(W_{j},\Lambda_{j})
is a gf-orthonormal bases for
H
with respect to
\{v_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}}
, then
V
is unitary.

Proof.

By Corollaries
1
and
3
, the operator
V
is invertible. Let
f\in H
, we obtain

\|f\|^{2}=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}v_{j}^{2}\|\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f\|^{2}=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{J}}\|\Theta_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}V^{*}f\|^{2}=\|V^{*}f\|^{2}.

Thus,
VV^{*}=id_{H}
and this means that
V
is unitary.
∎

4.
Characterizations of g-fusion frames, gf-Riesz and gf-orthonormal bases

Sun in
[
17
]
showed that each g-frame for
H
induces a sequence in
H
dependent on the g-frame and he proved a useful theorem about them. In this section, we are going to express Sun’s method for g-fusion frames.

Let
W=\{W_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}}
be a family of closed subspaces of
H
,
\{v_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}}
be a family of weights,
\Lambda_{j}\in\mathcal{B}(H,H_{j})
for each
j\in\mathbb{J}
and
\{e_{j,k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{K}_{j}}
be an orthonormal basis for
H_{j}
, where
\mathbb{K}_{j}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
and
j\in\mathbb{J}
. Suppose that

\displaystyle\varphi:

\displaystyle h\longrightarrow\mathbb{C},

\displaystyle\varphi(f)

\displaystyle=\langle v_{j}\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f,e_{j,k}\rangle.

We have

\|\varphi f\|\leq v_{j}\|\Lambda_{j}\|\|f\|,

therefore,
\varphi
is a bounded linear functional on
H
. Now, we can write

\langle v_{j}\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f,e_{j,k}\rangle=\langle f,v_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}e_{j,k}\rangle.

So, if

(9)

u_{j,k}:=v_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}e_{j,k},\ \ \ j\in\mathbb{J},\ k\in\mathbb{K}_{j}

then
\langle f,u_{j,k}\rangle=\langle v_{j}\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f,e_{j,k}\rangle
for all
f\in H
.

Remark 1
.

Using (
9
), we get for each
f\in H

(10)

v_{j}\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{K}_{j}}\langle f,u_{j,k}\rangle e_{j,k}.

But

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{K}_{j}}|\langle f,u_{j,k}\rangle|^{2}\leq v_{j}^{2}\|\Lambda_{j}\|^{2}\|f\|^{2}.

Thus,
\{u_{j,k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{K}_{j}}
is a Bessel sequence for
H
.
It follows that for each
f\in H
and
g\in H_{j}

\displaystyle\langle f,v_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}g\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle v_{j}\Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f,g\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{K}_{j}}\langle v_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda_{j}f,e_{j,k}\rangle\langle e_{j,k},g\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{K}_{j}}\langle f,u_{j,k}\rangle\langle e_{j,k},g\rangle

\displaystyle=\Big\langle f,\sum_{k\in\mathbb{K}_{j}}\langle g,e_{j,k}\rangle u_{j,k}\Big\rangle.

Therefore,

(11)

\displaystyle v_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}g=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{K}_{j}}\langle g,e_{j,k}\rangle u_{j,k}

for all
g\in H_{j}
.

We say
\{u_{j,k}\ :\ j\in\mathbb{J},\ k\in\mathbb{K}_{j}\}
the sequence induced by
\Lambda
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\Lambda=(W_{j},\Lambda_{j},v_{j})
and
u_{j,k}
be defined by (
9
). Then we get the followings.

(I)

\Lambda
is a g-fusion frame (resp. g-fusion Bessel sequence, Parseval g-fusion frame, gf-Riesz basis, gf-orthonormal basis) for
H
if and only if
\{u_{j,k}\ :\ j\in\mathbb{J},\ k\in\mathbb{K}_{j}\}
is a frame (resp. Bessel sequence, Parseval frame, Riesz basis, orthonormal basis) for
H
.

(II)

The g-fusion operator for
\Lambda
coincides with the frame operator for
\{u_{j,k}\ :\ j\in\mathbb{J},\ k\in\mathbb{K}_{j}\}
.

Proof.

(I). By (
10
),
\Lambda
is a g-fusion frame (resp. g-fusion Bessel sequence, Parseval g-fusion frame) for
H
if and only if
\{u_{j,k}\ :\ j\in\mathbb{J},\ k\in\mathbb{K}_{j}\}
is a frame (resp. Bessel sequence, Parseval frame) for
H
.

Assume that
\Lambda
is a gf-Riesz basis for
H
and
g_{j}\in H_{j}
. Thus,

g_{j}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{K}_{j}}c_{j,k}e_{j,k},

where
c_{j,k}\in\ell(\mathbb{K}_{j})
. Note that, by (
10
),

\{f\ :\ \Lambda_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}f=0,\ \ j\in\mathbb{J}\}=\{f\ :\ \langle f,u_{j,k}\rangle=0,\ \ j\in\mathbb{J},\ k\in\mathbb{K}_{j}\rangle\}

and

A\sum_{j\in\mathbb{I}}\|g_{j}\|^{2}\leq\big\|\sum_{j\in\mathbb{I}}v_{j}\pi_{W_{j}}\Lambda^{*}_{j}g_{j}\big\|^{2}\leq B\sum_{j\in\mathbb{I}}\|g_{j}\|^{2}

is equivalent to

A\sum_{j\in\mathbb{I}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{K}_{j}}|c_{j,k}|^{2}\leq\big\|\sum_{j\in\mathbb{I}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{K}_{j}}c_{j,k}u_{j,k}\big\|^{2}\leq B\sum_{j\in\mathbb{I}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{K}_{j}}|c_{j,k}|^{2}

for any finite
\mathbb{I}\subseteq\mathbb{J}
.
Thus,
\Lambda
is a gf-Riesz basis if and only if
\{u_{j,k}\ :\ j\in\mathbb{J},\ k\in\mathbb{K}_{j}\}
is a Riesz basis.

Now, let
(W_{j},\Lambda_{j})
be a gf-orthonormal basis for
H
with respect to
\{v_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{J}}
. We get for any
j_{1},j_{2}\in\mathbb{J}
,
k_{1}\in\mathbb{K}_{j_{1}}
and
k_{2}\in\mathbb{K}_{j_{2}}

\displaystyle\langle u_{j_{1},k_{1}},u_{j_{2},k_{2}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle v_{j_{1}}\pi_{W_{j_{1}}}\Lambda^{*}_{j_{1}}e_{j_{1},k_{1}},v_{j_{2}}\pi_{W_{j_{2}}}\Lambda^{*}_{j_{2}}e_{j_{2},k_{2}}\rangle

Limit theorems for the coefficients of random walks on the general linear group
Hui Xiao, Ion Grama, Quansheng Liu

Abstract
. Let ( g n ) n ⩾ 1 (g_{n})_{n\geqslant 1} be a sequence of independent and identically distributed random elements with law μ \mu on the general linear group GL ​ ( V ) \textup{GL}(V) , where V = ℝ d V=\mathbb{R}^{d} . Consider the random walk G n := g n ​ … ​ g 1 G_{n}:=g_{n}\ldots g_{1} , n ⩾ 1 n\geqslant 1 , and the coefficients ⟨ f , G n ​ v ⟩ \langle f,G_{n}v\rangle , where v ∈ V v\in V and f ∈ V ∗ f\in V^{*} . Under suitable moment assumptions on μ \mu , we prove the strong and weak laws of large numbers and the central limit theorem for ⟨ f , G n ​ v ⟩ \langle f,G_{n}v\rangle , which improve the previous results established under the exponential moment condition on μ \mu . We further demonstrate the Berry-Esseen bound, the Edgeworth expansion, the Cramér type moderate deviation expansion and the local limit theorem with moderate deviations for ⟨ f , G n ​ v ⟩ \langle f,G_{n}v\rangle under the exponential moment condition. Under a subexponential moment condition on μ \mu , we also show a Berry-Esseen type bound and the moderate deviation principle for ⟨ f , G n ​ v ⟩ \langle f,G_{n}v\rangle . Our approach is based on various versions of the Hölder regularity of the invariant measure of the Markov chain G n ⋅ x = ℝ ​ G n ​ v G_{n}\!\cdot\!x=\mathbb{R}G_{n}v on the projective space of V V with the starting point x = ℝ ​ v x=\mathbb{R}v .

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background and main objectives

Since the pioneering work of Furstenberg and Kesten
[
20
]
,
the theory of random walks on linear groups (also called products of random matrices)
has attracted a great deal of attention of many mathematicians for several decades,
see for instance the influential work of Le Page
[
40
]
,
Guivarc’h and Raugi
[
32
]
, Bougerol and Lacroix
[
7
]
, Goldsheid and Margulis
[
21
]
,
Benoist and Quint
[
6
]
, and the references therein.
This theory has important applications in a number of research areas such as
spectral theory
[
7
,
10
,
9
]
, geometric measure theory
[
43
,
35
,
22
]
,
statistical physics
[
17
]
,
homogeneous dynamics
[
8
,
4
]
,
stochastic recursions and smoothing transforms
[
38
,
31
,
42
]
,
and branching processes in random environment
[
41
,
24
]
.
Of particular interest is the study of asymptotic properties of the random walk

\displaystyle G_{n}:=g_{n}\ldots g_{1},\quad n\geqslant 1,

where
(g_{n})_{n\geqslant 1}
is a sequence of independent and identically distributed
(i.i.d.) random elements with law
\mu
on the general linear group
\textup{GL}(V)
with
V=\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
One natural and important way to describe the random walk
(G_{n})_{n\geqslant 1}
is to
investigate the growth rate
of the coefficients
\langle f,G_{n}v\rangle
,
where
v\in V
and
f\in V^{*}
,
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is the duality bracket:
\langle f,v\rangle=f(v)
.
Bellman
[
3
]
conjectured that the classical central limit theorem
should hold true for
\langle f,G_{n}v\rangle

in the case when the matrices
(g_{n})
are positive.
This conjecture was proved by Furstenberg and Kesten
[
20
]
,
who established the strong law of large numbers and central limit theorem
under the condition that the matrices
g_{n}
are strictly positive and that all the coefficients of
g_{n}
are comparable.
This condition was then relaxed
by Kingman
[
39
]
,
Cohn, Nerman and Peligrad
[
12
]
and Hennion
[
33
]
.

As noticed by Furstenberg
[
19
]
,
the analysis developed in
[
20
]
for positive matrices breaks down for invertible matrices.
It turns out that the situation of invertible matrices is much more complicated and delicate.
Guivarc’h and Raugi
[
32
]
established the strong law of large numbers for the coefficients of products
of invertible matrices
under an exponential moment condition:
for any
v\in V\setminus\{0\}
and
f\in V^{*}\setminus\{0\}
, a.s.

(1.1)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log|\langle f,G_{n}v\rangle|=\lambda_{1},

where
\lambda_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
is a constant (independent of
f
and
v
) called the first Lyapunov exponent of
\mu
.
It is worth mentioning that the result (
1.1
) does not follow from
the classical subadditive ergodic theorem of Kingman
[
39
]
, nor from the recent version by Gouëzel and Karlsson
[
28
]
.
The central limit theorem for the coefficients has also been established in
[
32
]
under the exponential moment condition:
for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

(1.2)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{\log|\langle f,G_{n}v\rangle|-n\lambda_{1}}{\sigma\sqrt{n}}\leqslant t\right)=\Phi(t),

where
\Phi
is the standard normal distribution function on
\mathbb{R}

and
\sigma^{2}>0
is the asymptotic variance of
\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\log|\langle f,G_{n}v\rangle|.

Recently, Benoist and Quint
[
6
]
have extended (
1.1
) and (
1.2
)
to the framework of the general linear group
\textup{GL}(V)
with
V=\mathbb{K}^{d}
,
where
\mathbb{K}
is a local field. Moreover, they also established the law of iterated logarithm and the large deviations bounds.

The first objective of this paper is to prove the weak law of large numbers
under the first moment condition: we prove that, if

\int_{\textup{GL}(V)}\log N(g)\mu(dg)<\infty
with

N(g)=\max\{\|g\|,\|g^{-1}\|\}
, then (
1.1
)
holds in probability.
Moreover, under the second moment condition that

\int_{\textup{GL}(V)}\log^{2}N(g)\mu(dg)<\infty
,
we prove the strong law of large numbers, namely that (
1.1
) holds a.s.
Under the same second moment condition we also prove
the central limit theorem: we show that the Gaussian approximation (
1.2
) holds.

Our second objective is to investigate the rate of convergence in the central limit theorem
(
1.2
).
To this end, we first establish a Berry-Esseen bound
under the exponential moment condition: we prove that if

\int_{\textup{GL}(V)}N(g)^{\varepsilon}\mu(dg)<\infty
for some
\varepsilon>0
,
then there exists a constant
c>0
such that for all
n\geqslant 1
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

v\in V
and
f\in V^{*}
with
\|v\|=\|f\|=1
, and any
\gamma
-Hölder continuous function
\varphi
on
\mathbb{P}(V)
,

(1.3)

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}\left[\varphi(G_{n}\!\cdot\!x)\mathds{1}_{\big\{\frac{\log|\langle f,G_{n}v\rangle|-n\lambda_{1}}{\sigma\sqrt{n}}\leqslant t\big\}}\right]-\nu(\varphi)\Phi(t)\right|\leqslant\frac{c}{\sqrt{n}}\|\varphi\|_{\gamma},

where
\|\varphi\|_{\gamma}
is the
\gamma
-Hölder norm of the function
\varphi
.
Our result (
1.3
) improves a very recent Berry-Esseen bound with
\varphi=1
in
[
18
]
,
where a different approach is applied.
In fact we will establish a much stronger result, that is, the first-order Edgeworth expansion
(cf. Theorem
2.3
).
Moreover, under a subexponential moment condition, we prove that a weaker version of the Berry-Esseen bound
holds true. Namely, the bound (
1.3
) holds with the rate
\frac{c\log^{\beta}n}{\sqrt{n}}

(for some
\beta>0
)
instead of
\frac{c}{\sqrt{n}}
.

We next establish the moderate deviation principle under the subexponential moment condition
that
\int_{\textup{GL}(V)}e^{\log^{\alpha}N(g)}\,\mu(dg)<\infty

for some constant
\alpha\in(0,1)
:
for any Borel set
B\subseteq\mathbb{R}
and any sequence
(b_{n})_{n\geqslant 1}
of positive real numbers satisfying

\frac{b_{n}}{n}\to 0
and
b_{n}=o(n^{\frac{1}{2-\alpha}})
as
n\to\infty
,
we have

\displaystyle-\inf_{t\in B^{\circ}}\frac{t^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{n}{b_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{\log|\langle f,G_{n}v\rangle|-n\lambda_{1}}{b_{n}}\in B\right)

(1.4)

\displaystyle\leqslant\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{n}{b_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{\log|\langle f,G_{n}v\rangle|-n\lambda_{1}}{b_{n}}\in B\right)\leqslant-\inf_{t\in\bar{B}}\frac{t^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}},

where
B^{\circ}
and
\bar{B}
are respectively the interior and the closure of
B
.

We then reinforce the moderate deviation principle (
1.1
) to the Cramér type moderate deviation expansion
under the exponential moment condition: we prove that if

\int_{\textup{GL}(V)}N(g)^{\varepsilon}\mu(dg)<\infty
for some
\varepsilon>0
, then
uniformly in
t\in[0,o(\sqrt{n})]
,
v\in V
and
f\in V^{*}
with
\|v\|=\|f\|=1
, as
n\to\infty
,

(1.5)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{P}\Big(\frac{\log|\langle f,G_{n}v\rangle|-n\lambda_{1}}{\sigma\sqrt{n}}\geqslant t\Big)}{1-\Phi(t)}

\displaystyle=e^{\frac{t^{3}}{\sqrt{n}}\zeta(\frac{t}{\sqrt{n}})}\big[1+o(1)\big],

where
\zeta
is the Cramér series (cf. (
2.4
)). A similar expansion for the lower tail is also obtained.
More generally, we prove the Cramér type moderate deviation expansion for the couple

(G_{n}\!\cdot\!x,\log|\langle f,G_{n}v\rangle|)
with a target function
\varphi
on the Markov chain
(G_{n}\!\cdot\!x)

on the projective space
\mathbb{P}(V)
; see Theorem
2.8
.
We mention that Bahadur-Rao type and Petrov type large deviation asymptotics
for the coefficients
\langle f,G_{n}v\rangle
have been recently established in
[
48
]
,
which give precise estimation of the rate of convergence
in the weak law of large numbers (
1.1
).

Our third objective is to establish the
local limit theorem with moderate deviations for the coefficients
\langle f,G_{n}v\rangle
:
for any real numbers
a_{1}<a_{2}
,
we have, as
n\to\infty
, uniformly in
|t|=o(\sqrt{n})
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(\log|\langle f,G_{n}v\rangle|-n\lambda_{1}\in[a_{1},a_{2}]+\sqrt{n}\sigma t\Big)

(1.6)

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{2}-a_{1}}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi n}}e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{2}+\frac{t^{3}}{\sqrt{n}}\zeta(\frac{t}{\sqrt{n}})}[1+o(1)].

See Theorem
2.9
for a more general statement
where a local limit theorem with moderate deviations for the couple
(G_{n}\!\cdot\!x,\log|\langle f,G_{n}v\rangle|)

with target functions is given.
We would like to mention that using the approach developed in this paper,
it is possible to establish some new and interesting limit theorems
for the Gromov product of random walks on hyperbolic groups;
we refer to Gouëzel
[
26
,
27
]
on this topic.

Finally we would like to mention that all the results of the paper remain valid when
V
is
\mathbb{C}^{d}
or
\mathbb{K}^{d}
, where

\mathbb{K}
is any local field.

1.2.
Proof strategy

Our starting point is the following decomposition
which relates the coefficients
\langle f,G_{n}v\rangle
to the cocycles
\sigma(G_{n},x)
:
for any
x=\mathbb{R}v\in\mathbb{P}(V)
and
y=\mathbb{R}f\in\mathbb{P}(V^{*})
with
\|f\|=1
,

(1.7)

\displaystyle\log|\langle f,G_{n}v\rangle|=\sigma(G_{n},x)+\log\delta(y,G_{n}\!\cdot\!x),

where
\delta(y,x)=\frac{|\langle f,v\rangle|}{\|f\|\|v\|}
.
The proof of (
1.1
) given in
[
32
,
6
]

is based on the
exponential Hölder regularity of the invariant measure
\nu
of the Markov chain
(G_{n}\kern-0.50003pt\cdot\kern-0.50003ptx)

on the projective space
\mathbb{P}(V)
, which requires the exponential moment condition.
We refer to Bougerol and Lacroix
[
7
]
for a comprehensive account of the
proof strategy developed in
[
32
]
.
In order to relax the exponential moment condition,
we will establish a weaker form of the Hölder regularity of
\nu
:
we prove that for any
\varepsilon>0
,

(1.8)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\delta(y,G_{n}\!\cdot\!x)\leqslant e^{-\varepsilon n}\right)=0,

provided that the first moment condition holds. See Proposition
3.4
.
The weak law of large numbers for the coefficients follows from this and the law of large numbers of Furstenberg
[
19
]

for the norm cocycle
\sigma(G_{n},x)
.

To prove the strong law of large numbers, we show the following stronger regularity of
\nu
: if the second moment condition holds,
then for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists a sequence of positive numbers
(a_{k})
such that
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{k}<\infty

and that for all
n\geqslant k\geqslant 1
,

(1.9)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\delta(y,G_{n}\!\cdot\!x)\leqslant e^{-\varepsilon k}\right)\leqslant a_{k}.

See Lemma
3.5
.
This permits us to conclude the strong law of large numbers for the coefficients, using that of Furstenberg
[
19
]
for the norm cocycle.

Using again (
1.9
),
together with the central limit theorem for the norm cocycle
\sigma(G_{n},x)
due to Benoist and Quint
[
5
]
,
allows us to prove the Gaussian approximation (
1.2
) under the optimal second moment condition.

For the proof of the Edgeworth expansion and the Berry-Esseen bound (
1.3
),
we first use a partition
(\chi_{n,k}^{y})_{k\geqslant 1}
of the unity to discretise the component
\log\delta(y,G_{n}\!\cdot\!x)
in (
1.7
).
This allows to reduce the study of the coefficients to that of the norm cocycle
\sigma(G_{n},x)

jointly with the target function
\chi_{n,k}^{y}(G_{n}\!\cdot\!x)
.
Then our strategy is to
make use of the Edgeworth expansion for the couple
(G_{n}\!\cdot\!x,\sigma(G_{n},x))

with target functions on the Markov chain
(G_{n}\!\cdot\!x)
.
Finally, the result (
1.3
) is obtained by patching up these expansions
by means of the exponential Hölder regularity of the invariant measure
\nu
.
For the proof of the Berry-Esseen type bound (Theorem
2.5
) under a subexponential moment condition,
we establish a subexponential regularity of the invariant measure
\nu
(Theorem
2.6
)
and make use of the previous result in
[
15
]

on Berry-Esseen bounds for the norm cocycle
\sigma(G_{n},x)
.

To prove the moderate deviation principle (
1.1
), we use
again the subexponential regularity of the invariant measure
\nu
together with
the moderate deviation principle for the norm cocycle
\sigma(G_{n},x)
established by in
[
14
]
.

To establish the Cramér type moderate deviation expansion (
1.5
), our approach is different from the standard one which is based on
performing a change of measure and proving
a Berry-Esseen bound under the changed measure; see for example Cramér
[
13
]
and Petrov
[
44
]
.
However, even with a Berry-Esseen bound under the changed measure at hands,
we do not know how to obtain (
1.5
) using this strategy.
Our approach is to use again a partition of the unity
and for each piece to pass to the Fourier transforms under the changed measure,
and then to establish exact asymptotic expansions.
We then patch up these expansions by using the exponential Hölder regularity of the invariant measure
\nu
.

The proof of the local limit theorem with moderate deviations (
1.1
)
follows the same lines as that of (
1.5
), together with the uniform version of the exponential Hölder regularity of the invariant measure of the Markov chain
(G_{n}\!\cdot\!x)

under the changed measure.

2.
Main results

2.1.
Notation and conditions

For any integer
d\geqslant 1
, denote by
V=\mathbb{R}^{d}
the
d
-dimensional Euclidean space.
We fix a basis
e_{1},\ldots,e_{d}
of
V
and the associated norm on
V
is defined by
\|v\|^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}|v_{i}|^{2}
for
v=\sum_{i=1}^{d}v_{i}e_{i}\in V
.
Let
V^{*}
be the dual vector space of
V
and its dual basis is denoted by
e_{1}^{*},\ldots,e_{d}^{*}

so that
e_{i}^{*}(e_{j})=1
if
i=j
and
e_{i}^{*}(e_{j})=0
if
i\neq j
.
For any integer
2\leqslant p\leqslant d
, let
\wedge^{p}V
be the
p
-th exterior product of
V

and we use the same symbol
\|\cdot\|
for the norms induced on
\wedge^{p}V
and
V^{*}
.
We equip
\mathbb{P}(V)
with the angular distance

(2.1)

\displaystyle d(x,x^{\prime})=\frac{\|v\wedge v^{\prime}\|}{\|v\|\|v^{\prime}\|}\quad\mbox{for}\ x=\mathbb{R}v\in\mathbb{P}(V),\ x^{\prime}=\mathbb{R}v^{\prime}\in\mathbb{P}(V).

We use the symbol
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
to denote the dual bracket defined by
\langle f,v\rangle=f(v)
for any
f\in V^{*}
and
v\in V
.
Set

\displaystyle\delta(x,y)=\frac{|\langle f,v\rangle|}{\|f\|\|v\|}\quad\mbox{for}\ x=\mathbb{R}v\in\mathbb{P}(V),\ y=\mathbb{R}f\in\mathbb{P}(V^{*}).

Denote by
\mathscr{C}(\mathbb{P}(V))
the space of complex-valued continuous functions on
\mathbb{P}(V)
,
equipped with the norm
\|\varphi\|_{\infty}:=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{P}(V)}|\varphi(x)|
for
\varphi\in\mathscr{C}(\mathbb{P}(V))
.
Let
\gamma>0
be a fixed small enough constant and set

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{\gamma}:=\|\varphi\|_{\infty}+\sup_{x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{P}(V):x\neq x^{\prime}}\frac{|\varphi(x)-\varphi(x^{\prime})|}{d(x,x^{\prime})^{\gamma}}.

Consider the Banach space

\displaystyle\mathscr{B}_{\gamma}:=\left\{\varphi\in\mathscr{C}(\mathbb{P}(V)):\|\varphi\|_{\gamma}<\infty\right\},

which consists of complex-valued
\gamma
-Hölder continuous functions on
\mathbb{P}(V)
.
Denote by
\mathscr{L(B_{\gamma},B_{\gamma})}

the set of all bounded linear operators from
\mathscr{B}_{\gamma}
to
\mathscr{B}_{\gamma}

equipped with the operator norm

\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathscr{B}_{\gamma}\to\mathscr{B}_{\gamma}}
.
The topological dual of
\mathscr{B}_{\gamma}
endowed with the induced norm
is denoted by
\mathscr{B}^{\prime}_{\gamma}
.
Let
\mathscr{B}_{\gamma}^{*}
be the Banach space of
\gamma
-Hölder continuous functions
on
\mathbb{P}(V^{*})
endowed with the norm

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{\mathscr{B}_{\gamma}^{*}}=\sup_{y\in\mathbb{P}(V^{*})}|\varphi(x)|+\sup_{y,y^{\prime}\in\mathbb{P}(V^{*}):\,y\neq y^{\prime}}\frac{|\varphi(y)-\varphi(y^{\prime})|}{d(y,y^{\prime})^{\gamma}},

where
d(y,y^{\prime})=\frac{\|f\wedge f^{\prime}\|}{\|f\|\|f^{\prime}\|}
for
y=\mathbb{R}f\in\mathbb{P}(V^{*})
and
y^{\prime}=\mathbb{R}f^{\prime}\in\mathbb{P}(V^{*})
.

Let
\textup{GL}(V)
be the general linear group of the vector space
V
.
The action of
g\in\textup{GL}(V)
on a vector
v\in V
is denoted by
gv
,
and the action of
g\in\textup{GL}(V)
on a projective line
x=\mathbb{R}v\in\mathbb{P}(V)
is denoted by
g\cdot x=\mathbb{R}gv
.
For any
g\in\textup{GL}(V)
, let
\|g\|=\sup_{v\in V\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|gv\|}{\|v\|}

and denote
N(g)=\max\{\|g\|,\|g^{-1}\|\}
.
Let
\mu
be a Borel probability measure on
\textup{GL}(V)
.
We shall use the following exponential moment condition.

A1

(Exponential moment condition)
.

There exists a constant
\varepsilon>0
such that
\int_{\textup{GL}(V)}N(g)^{\varepsilon}\mu(dg)<\infty
.

Let
\Gamma_{\mu}
be the smallest closed subsemigroup
generated by the support of the measure
\mu
.
An endomorphism
g
of
V
is said to be proximal
if it has an eigenvalue
\lambda
with multiplicity one and all other eigenvalues of
g
have modulus strcitly less than
|\lambda|
.
We shall need the following strong irreducibility and proximality condition.

A2
.

(i)(Strong irreducibility)

No finite union of proper subspaces of
V
is
\Gamma_{\mu}
-invariant.

(ii)(Proximality)

\Gamma_{\mu}
contains a proximal endomorphism.

Define the norm cocycle
\sigma:\textup{GL}(V)\times\mathbb{P}(V)\to\mathbb{R}
as follows:

\displaystyle\sigma(g,x)=\log\frac{\|gv\|}{\|v\|},\quad\mbox{for any}\ g\in\textup{GL}(V)\ \mbox{and}\ x=\mathbb{R}v\in\mathbb{P}(V).

By
[
45
, Proposition 3.15]
, under
1
and
2
,
the following limit exists and is independent of
x\in\mathbb{P}(V)
:

(2.2)

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}\left[(\sigma(G_{n},x)-n\lambda_{1})^{2}\right]\in(0,\infty).

For any
s\in(-s_{0},s_{0})
with
s_{0}>0
small enough,
we define the transfer operator
P_{s}
as follows: for any bounded measurable function
\varphi
on
\mathbb{P}(V)
,

(2.3)

\displaystyle P_{s}\varphi(x)=\int_{\textup{GL}(V)}e^{s\sigma(g,x)}\varphi(g\!\cdot\!x)\mu(dg),\quad x\in\mathbb{P}(V).

It will be shown in Lemma
4.1
that
there exists a constant
s_{0}>0
such that for any
s\in(-s_{0},s_{0})
,
the operator
P_{s}\in\mathscr{L(B_{\gamma},B_{\gamma})}
has
a unique dominant eigenvalue
\kappa(s)

with
\kappa(0)=1
and the mapping
s\mapsto\kappa(s)
being analytic.

We denote
\Lambda=\log\kappa
.
Set
\gamma_{m}=\Lambda^{(m)}(0)
for any
m\geqslant 1
.
In particular,
\gamma_{1}=\lambda_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}=\sigma^{2}
.
Throughout the paper, we write
\zeta
for the Cramér series
[
44
]
:

(2.4)

\displaystyle\zeta(t)=\frac{\gamma_{3}}{6\gamma_{2}^{3/2}}+\frac{\gamma_{4}\gamma_{2}-3\gamma_{3}^{2}}{24\gamma_{2}^{3}}t+\frac{\gamma_{5}\gamma_{2}^{2}-10\gamma_{4}\gamma_{3}\gamma_{2}+15\gamma_{3}^{3}}{120\gamma_{2}^{9/2}}t^{2}+\cdots,

which converges for
|t|
small enough.

Under
1
and
2
,
the Markov chain
(G_{n}\!\cdot\!x)_{n\geqslant 0}
has a unique invariant probability measure

\nu
on
\mathbb{P}(V)
such that for any bounded measurable function
\varphi
on
\mathbb{P}(V)
,

(2.5)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{P}(V)}\int_{\textup{GL}(V)}\varphi(g\!\cdot\!x)\mu(dg)\nu(dx)=\int_{\mathbb{P}(V)}\varphi(x)\nu(dx)=:\nu(\varphi).

2.2.
Law of large numbers and central limit theorem

In this subsection we present the law of large numbers and the central limit theorem
for the coefficients
\langle f,G_{n}v\rangle
.

We first present the law of large numbers for
\langle f,G_{n}v\rangle
: namely
we state a weak law of large numbers under the existence of the first moment and a
strong law of large numbers under the second moment assumption.
Denote by
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
the first and second Lyapunov exponents of
\mu
:

(2.6)

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}\log\|G_{n}\|,\quad\lambda_{2}:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\mathbb{E}\log\frac{\|\wedge^{2}G_{n}\|}{\|G_{n}\|}.

We say that
\Gamma_{\mu}
is irreducible if no proper subspace of
V
is
\Gamma_{\mu}
-invariant.

Theorem 2.1
.

Assume that
\int_{\textup{GL}(V)}\log N(g)\mu(dg)<\infty.

Assume also that
\lambda_{1}>\lambda_{2}
and that
\Gamma_{\mu}
is irreducible.
Then, we have, as
n\to\infty
, uniformly in
v\in V
and
f\in V^{*}
with
\|v\|=\|f\|=1
,

(2.7)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\log|\langle f,G_{n}v\rangle|\to\lambda_{1}\quad\mbox{in probability and in }L^{1}.

Moreover, if
\int_{\textup{GL}(V)}\log^{2}N(g)\,\mu(dg)<\infty

and condition
2
holds, then
the convergence in probability in (
2.7
) can be improved to the a.s. convergence.

Notice that, by a theorem of Guivarc’h
[
29
]
,
condition
2
implies that
\lambda_{1}>\lambda_{2}
and that
\Gamma_{\mu}
is irreducible.
Under the exponential moment condition
1

together with the strong irreducibility and proximality condition
2
,
Guivarc’h and Raugi
[
32
]
proved the a.s. convergence in (
2.7
)
for the special linear group
\textrm{SL}(d,\mathbb{R})
(the set of
d\times d
matrices with determinant
1
);
Benoist and Quint
[
6
]
extended it to the general linear group
\textup{GL}(V)
.
Our Theorem
2.1
states the weak law of large numbers
for
\textup{GL}(V)
under the first moment condition, and the strong
law of large numbers under the second moment condition.
The question remains open whether the strong law of large numbers holds true under the first moment condition.

It is worth mentioning that Aoun and Sert
[
2
]
proved the weak law of large numbers for the spectral radius of
G_{n}

under the first moment condition, and the strong law of large numbers
under the second moment condition.

In the next theorem we formulate the central limit theorem for the coefficients
\langle f,G_{n}v\rangle
under the second moment condition.
Recall that

\Phi(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{t}e^{-u^{2}/2}du,\quad t\in\mathbb{R}

is the standard normal distribution function, and
\sigma^{2}
is the asymptotic variation given by (
2.2
).

Theorem 2.2
.

If
\int_{\textup{GL}(V)}\log^{2}N(g)\,\mu(dg)<\infty

and condition
2
holds,
then for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
, uniformly in
v\in V
and
f\in V^{*}
with
\|v\|=\|f\|=1
,

(2.8)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{\log|\langle f,G_{n}v\rangle|-n\lambda_{1}}{\sigma\sqrt{n}}\leqslant t\right)=\Phi(t).

Theorem
2.2
improves the results of Guivarc’h and Raugi
[
32
]
on
\textrm{SL}(d,\mathbb{R})

and those of Benoist and Quint
[
6
]
on
\textup{GL}(V)

by relaxing the exponential moment condition
1

to the optimal second moment condition.

We mention that the central limit theorem for the spectral radius of
G_{n}
has been obtained by Aoun
[
1
]
.

2.3.
Edgeworth expansion and Berry-Esseen bound

In many applications it is of primary interest to give an estimation of the rate of convergence in the Gaussian approximation (
2.8
).
In this direction we prove a first-order Edgeworth expansion.
For any
\varphi\in\mathscr{B}_{\gamma}
, define the functions

(2.9)

\displaystyle b_{\varphi}(x):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\big[(\sigma(G_{n},x)-n\Lambda^{\prime}(s))\varphi(G_{n}\!\cdot\!x)\big],\quad x\in\mathbb{P}(V)

and

(2.10)

\displaystyle d_{\varphi}(y):=\int_{\mathbb{P}(V)}\varphi(x)\log\delta(y,x)\nu(dx),\quad y\in\mathbb{P}(V^{*}).

It is shown in Lemmas
4.7
and
4.8
that
both functions
b_{\varphi}
and
d_{\varphi}
are well-defined and
b_{\varphi}\in\mathscr{B}_{\gamma}
,

d_{\varphi}\in\mathscr{B}_{\gamma}^{*}
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Assume
1
and
2
.
Then, for any
\varepsilon>0
, uniformly in

t\in\mathbb{R}
,
x=\mathbb{R}v\in\mathbb{P}(V)
,
y=\mathbb{R}f\in\mathbb{P}(V^{*})
with
\|v\|=\|f\|=1
, and

\varphi\in\mathscr{B}_{\gamma}
, as
n\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\varphi(G_{n}\!\cdot\!x)\mathds{1}_{\big\{\frac{\log|\langle f,G_{n}v\rangle|-n\lambda_{1}}{\sigma\sqrt{n}}\leqslant t\big\}}\Big]

\displaystyle\qquad=\nu(\varphi)\Big[\Phi(t)+\frac{\Lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}(0)}{6\sigma^{3}\sqrt{n}}(1-t^{2})\phi(t)\Big]-\frac{b_{\varphi}(x)+d_{\varphi}(y)}{\sigma\sqrt{n}}\phi(t)

(2.11)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\nu(\varphi)o\Big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\Big)+\lVert\varphi\rVert_{\gamma}O\Big(\frac{1}{n^{1-\varepsilon}}\Big).

As a consequence of Theorem
2.3
we get the following Berry-Esseen bound
with the optimal convergence rate, under the exponential moment condition.

Theorem 2.4
.

Under
1
and
2
,
there exist constants
\gamma>0
and
c>0
such that for any

n\geqslant 1
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
x=\mathbb{R}v\in\mathbb{P}(V)
and
f\in V^{*}
with
\|v\|=\|f\|=1
,
and
\varphi\in\mathscr{B}_{\gamma}
,

(2.12)

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}\left[\varphi(G_{n}\!\cdot\!x)\mathds{1}_{\big\{\frac{\log|\langle f,G_{n}v\rangle|-n\lambda_{1}}{\sigma\sqrt{n}}\leqslant t\big\}}\right]-\nu(\varphi)\Phi(t)\right|\leqslant\frac{c}{\sqrt{n}}\|\varphi\|_{\gamma}.

Under the same conditions, our result (
2.12
)
improves on two very recent results in
[
18
]
and
[
16
]
:
it is proved in
[
18
]
that (
2.12
) holds for the particular case when
\varphi=1
,
and in
[
16
]
the authors also consider the case where
\varphi=1
but the convergence rate
\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}
in (
2.12
) is replaced by
\frac{\log n}{\sqrt{n}}
.

It is an open question how to relax the exponential moment condition
1

in the Edgeworth expansion and in the Berry-Esseen bound.
For positive matrices, the Edgeworth expansion (
2.3
)
and the Berry-Esseen bound (
2.12
) have been recently obtained
using a different approach in a forthcoming paper
[
49
]

under optimal moment conditions.
In the following theorem we get a Berry-Esseen type bound for invertible matrices under the sub-exponential moment condition,
with an extra
\log^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}n
factor.

Theorem 2.5
.

Assume
2
and that there exists a constant
\alpha\in(0,1)

such that
\mu(\log N(g)>u)\leqslant\exp\{-u^{\alpha}a(u)\}
for any
u>0

and for some function
a(u)>0
satisfying
a(u)\to\infty
as
u\to\infty
.
Then, there exists a constant
c>0
such that for any
n\geqslant 2
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

v\in V
and
f\in V^{*}
with
\|v\|=\|f\|=1
,

(2.13)

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{\log|\langle f,G_{n}v\rangle|-n\lambda_{1}}{\sigma\sqrt{n}}\leqslant t\right)-\Phi(t)\right|\leqslant\frac{c\log^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}n}{\sqrt{n}}.

Note that the condition
\mu(\log N(g)>u)\leqslant\exp\{-u^{\alpha}a(u)\}

holds true
if
\int_{\textup{GL}(V)}e^{\log^{(\alpha+\varepsilon)}N(g)}\,\mu(dg)<\infty
for some
\varepsilon>0
.

Recently in
[
16
]
,
under the polynomial moment condition of order
p\geqslant 3
, that is,
\int_{\textup{GL}(V)}\log^{p}N(g)\,\mu(dg)<\infty
,
a Berry-Esseen type bound is obtained with the convergence rate
n^{-\frac{p-1}{2p}}
.

The proof of Theorem
2.5
is based on the Berry-Esseen type bound recently established in
[
15
]

and on the subexponential Hölder regularity of the invariant measure
\nu
which is the subject of Section
2.4
.

2.4.
Subexponential Hölder regularity

We shall establish the following subexponential Hölder regularity of the invariant measure
\nu

under only subexponential moments condition.
This turns out to be an important step for proving the Berry-Esseen type bound (
2.13
)
as well as the moderate deviation principle for the coefficients
\langle f,G_{n}v\rangle
(see Theorem
2.7
below).

Theorem 2.6
.

Assume
2
and that there exists a constant
\alpha\in(0,1)

such that
\mu(\log N(g)>u)\leqslant\exp\{-u^{\alpha}a(u)\}
for any
u>0

and for some function
a(u)>0
satisfying
a(u)\to\infty
as
u\to\infty
.
Then, there exist constants
c>0
and
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
n\geqslant k\geqslant k_{0}
,

x\in\mathbb{P}(V)
and
y\in\mathbb{P}(V^{*})
,

(2.14)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\delta(G_{n}\!\cdot\!x,y)\leqslant e^{-k}\right)\leqslant\exp\left(-ck^{\alpha}\right).

Moreover, there exists a constant
\eta>0
such that

(2.15)

\displaystyle\sup_{y\in\mathbb{P}(V^{*})}\int_{\mathbb{P}(V)}\exp\left(\eta|\log\delta(x,y)|^{\alpha}\right)\nu(dx)<+\infty.

In particular, there exist constants
c>0
and
\eta>0
such that for any
y\in\mathbb{P}(V^{*})
and
r>0
,

(2.16)

\displaystyle\nu(B(y,r))\leqslant c\exp\left(\eta|\log r|^{\alpha}\right),

where
B(y,r)=\{x\in\mathbb{P}(V):\delta(y,x)\leqslant r\}
.

Notice that if
2
holds and that subexponential moment condition in Theorem
2.6

is strengthed to the exponential moment condition
1
,
then by a theorem due to Guivarc’h
[
30
]
,
the invariant measure
\nu
satisfies a stronger regularity property:
there exists a constant
\eta>0
such that

(2.17)

\displaystyle\sup_{y\in\mathbb{P}(V^{*})}\int_{\mathbb{P}(V)}\frac{1}{\delta(x,y)^{\eta}}\nu(dx)<+\infty.

In this case, we say that the invariant measure
\nu
is exponentially Hölder regular.

Under the
p
-th moment condition that
\int_{\textup{GL}(V)}\log^{p}N(g)\,\mu(dg)<\infty
for some
p>1
,
Benoist and Quint
[
5
, Proposition 4.5]
have recently established the log-regularity of
\nu
:
under
2
,

(2.18)

\displaystyle\sup_{y\in\mathbb{P}(V^{*})}\int_{\mathbb{P}(V)}|\log\delta(x,y)|^{p-1}\nu(dx)<+\infty.

This result is one of the crucial points for establishing the central limit theorem for the norm cocycle
\sigma(G_{n},x)

under the optimal second moment condition
\int_{\textup{GL}(V)}\log^{2}N(g)\,\mu(dg)<\infty
, see
[
5
]
.

2.5.
Moderate deviation principles and expansions

In this subsection we state a moderate deviation principle and Cramér type moderate deviation expansions for
the coefficients
\langle f,G_{n}v\rangle
.

We first present the moderate deviation principle under a subexponential moment condition,
whose proof is based upon the regularity of the invariant measure
\nu
shown in Theorem
2.6
.

Theorem 2.7
.

Assume
2
and that
\int_{\textup{GL}(V)}e^{\log^{\alpha}N(g)}\,\mu(dg)<\infty

for some constant
\alpha\in(0,1)
.
Then, for any Borel set
B\subseteq\mathbb{R}

and any sequence
(b_{n})_{n\geqslant 1}
of positive numbers satisfying

\frac{b_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\to\infty
and
b_{n}=o(n^{\frac{1}{2-\alpha}})
as
n\to\infty
,
we have, uniformly in
v\in V
and
f\in V^{*}
with
\|v\|=\|f\|=1
,

\displaystyle-\inf_{y\in B^{\circ}}\frac{y^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{n}{b_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{\log|\langle f,G_{n}v\rangle|-n\lambda_{1}}{b_{n}}\in B\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{n}{b_{n}^{2}}\log\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{\log|\langle f,G_{n}v\rangle|-n\lambda_{1}}{b_{n}}\in B\right)\leqslant-\inf_{y\in\bar{B}}\frac{y^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}},

where
B^{\circ}
and
\bar{B}
are respectively the interior and the closure of
B
.

When the sub-exponential moment condition is strengthened to the exponential moment condition
1
,
we are able to establish the following Cramér type moderate deviation expansions for
the coefficients
\langle f,G_{n}v\rangle
, and more generally,
for the couple
(G_{n}\!\cdot\!x,\log|\langle f,G_{n}v\rangle|)
with a target function
\varphi

on the Markov chain
(G_{n}\!\cdot\!x)_{n\geqslant 0}
.

Theorem 2.8
.

Assume
1
and
2
. Then,
we have, as
n\to\infty
, uniformly in
v\in V
and
f\in V^{*}
with
\|v\|=\|f\|=1
,
and
t\in[0,o(\sqrt{n})]
,

(2.19)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{\log|\langle f,G_{n}v\rangle|-n\lambda_{1}}{\sigma\sqrt{n}}\geqslant t\right)}{1-\Phi(t)}

\displaystyle=e^{\frac{t^{3}}{\sqrt{n}}\zeta(\frac{t}{\sqrt{n}})}\big[1+o(1)\big],

(2.20)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{\log|\langle f,G_{n}v\rangle|-n\lambda_{1}}{\sigma\sqrt{n}}\leqslant-t\right)}{\Phi(-t)}

\displaystyle=e^{-\frac{t^{3}}{\sqrt{n}}\zeta(-\frac{t}{\sqrt{n}})}\big[1+o(1)\big].

More generally, for any
\varphi\in\mathscr{B}_{\gamma}
with
\gamma>0
sufficiently small,
we have, as

,
uniformly in

and

, and

(2.21)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[\varphi(G_{n}\!\cdot\!x)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\log|\langle f,G_{n}v\rangle|-n\lambda_{1}\geqslant\sqrt{n}\sigma t\right\}}\right]}{1-\Phi(t)}

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[\varphi(G_{n}\!\cdot\!x)\mathds{1}_{\left\{\log|\langle f,G_{n}v\rangle|-n\lambda_{1}\leqslant-\sqrt{n}\sigma t\right\}}\right]}{\Phi(-t)}

\displaystyle=e^{-\frac{t^{3}}{\sqrt{n}}\zeta(-\frac{t}{\sqrt{n}})}\big[\nu(\varphi)+o(1)\big].

Note that the rate
o(1)
in (
2.19
), (
2.20
),
(
2.21
) and (

) depends on
the rate
o(\sqrt{n})
in

.

2.8
clearly implies the following moderate deviation principle
for the couple
(G_{n}\!\cdot\!x,\log|\langle f,G_{n}v\rangle|)

with a target function
\varphi
on the Markov chain
(G_{n}\!\cdot\!x)
:
under
1
and
2
,
for any sequence of positive numbers
(b_{n})_{n\geqslant 1}

\frac{b_{n}}{n}\to 0
and

,

Local limit theorem with moderate deviations

.

.

3.
Proofs of the laws of large numbers

The following strong law of large numbers for the norm cocycle
\sigma(G_{n},x)
is due to Furstenberg
[
19
]
.
Recall that
\Gamma_{\mu}
is irreducible if no proper subspace of
V
is
\Gamma_{\mu}
-invariant.

Lemma 3.1

(
[
19
]
)
.

Assume
\int_{\textup{GL}(V)}\log N(g)\mu(dg)<\infty

and that
\Gamma_{\mu}
is irreducible.
Then, uniformly in
x\in\mathbb{P}(V)
,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sigma(G_{n},x)}{n}=\lambda_{1}\quad a.s.,

where
\lambda_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
is the first Lyapunov exponent of
\mu
.

In the
next lemma, we state two laws of large numbers for
\|G_{n}\|
and
\|\wedge^{2}G_{n}\|
. The first law is due to
Furstenberg-Kesten
[
20
]
, which can be proved by Kingman’s subadditive ergodic theorem
[
39
]
;
the second one is also an easy consequence of Kingman’s ergodic theorem
[
39
]
using
the definition of
\lambda_{2}
given in (
2.6
).

Lemma 3.2

(
[
20
,
39
]
)
.

Assume
\int_{\textup{GL}(V)}\log N(g)\mu(dg)<\infty
.
Then,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\|G_{n}\|=\lambda_{1}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\|\wedge^{2}G_{n}\|=\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}\quad a.s.

Denote by
K
the group of isometries of
(V,\|\cdot\|)

and by
A^{+}
the semigroup
A^{+}=\{\textrm{diag}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{d}):a_{1}\geqslant\ldots\geqslant a_{d}>0\}
,
where
\textrm{diag}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{d})
is a diagonal endomorphism under the basis
e_{1},\ldots,e_{d}
of
V
.
The well known Cartan decomposition states that
\textup{GL}(V)=KA^{+}K
.
For every
g\in\textup{GL}(V)
, we choose a decomposition (which is not necessarily unique)

\displaystyle g=k_{g}a_{g}l_{g},

where
k_{g},l_{g}\in K
and
a_{g}\in A^{+}
.
Recall that
(e_{i}^{*})_{1\leqslant i\leqslant d}
is the dual basis in
V^{*}
. Set

\displaystyle x_{g}^{M}=\mathbb{R}k_{g}e_{1}\quad\mbox{and}\quad y_{g}^{m}=\mathbb{R}l_{g}e_{1}^{*}.

Let
g^{*}
denote the adjoint automorphism of
g\in\textup{GL}(V)
.
Following
[
5
,
6
]
,

x_{g}^{M}\in\mathbb{P}(V)
and
y_{g}^{m}\in\mathbb{P}(V^{*})
are called respectively the density point of
g
and
g^{*}
.

The next result is taken from
[
5
, Lemma 4.7]
.

Lemma 3.3

(
[
5
]
)
.

For any
g\in\textup{GL}(V)
,
x=\mathbb{R}v
and
y=\mathbb{R}f

with
v\in V\setminus\{0\}
and
f\in V^{*}\setminus\{0\}
, we have

(1)

\delta(x,y_{g}^{m})\leqslant\frac{\|gv\|}{\|g\|\|v\|}\leqslant\delta(x,y_{g}^{m})+\frac{\|\wedge^{2}g\|}{\|g\|^{2}}
,

(2)

\delta(x_{g}^{M},y)\leqslant\frac{\|g^{*}f\|}{\|g\|\|f\|}\leqslant\delta(x_{g}^{M},y)+\frac{\|\wedge^{2}g\|}{\|g\|^{2}}
,

(3)

d(g\!\cdot\!x,x_{g}^{M})\delta(x,y_{g}^{m})\leqslant\frac{\|\wedge^{2}g\|}{\|g\|^{2}}
.

Using Lemmas
3.1
-
3.3
, we get the following:

Proposition 3.4
.

Assume that
\int_{\textup{GL}(V)}\log N(g)\mu(dg)<\infty
.
Assume also that
\lambda_{1}>\lambda_{2}
and that
\Gamma_{\mu}
is irreducible.
Then, for any
\varepsilon>0
,
we have that uniformly in
x\in\mathbb{P}(V)
and
y\in\mathbb{P}(V^{*})
,

(3.1)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(d(G_{n}\!\cdot\!x,x_{G_{n}}^{M})\geqslant e^{-(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}-\varepsilon)n}\right)=0,

(3.2)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\delta(x_{G_{n}}^{M},y)\leqslant e^{-\varepsilon n}\right)=0,

(3.3)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\delta(G_{n}\!\cdot\!x,y)\leqslant e^{-\varepsilon n}\right)=0.

Proof.

We first prove (
3.1
).
For every
g\in\textup{GL}(V)
, using the Cartan decomposition
g=k_{g}a_{g}l_{g}
and the fact that
k_{g}
is an isometry of
(V,\|\cdot\|)
,
we get that for any
x=\mathbb{R}v\in\mathbb{P}(V)
,

\displaystyle d(g\!\cdot\!x,x_{g}^{M})=\frac{\|gv\wedge k_{g}e_{1}\|}{\|gv\|\|k_{g}e_{1}\|}=\frac{\|k_{g}a_{g}l_{g}v\wedge k_{g}e_{1}\|}{\|gv\|}=\frac{\|a_{g}l_{g}v\wedge e_{1}\|}{\|gv\|}.

Since
a_{g}=\textrm{diag}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{d})
, we have
\|a_{g}l_{g}v\wedge e_{1}\|\leqslant a_{2}\|v\|
.
This, together with the fact that
a_{2}=\frac{\|\wedge^{2}g\|}{\|g\|}

Hence, for any

and

\displaystyle d(G_{n}\!\cdot\!x,x_{G_{n}}^{M})\leqslant\frac{\|\wedge^{2}G_{n}\|\|v\|}{\|G_{n}\|\|G_{n}v\|}.

By Lemmas
3.1
and
3.2

,

Hence, using (
3.4
), we obtain (
3.1

We next prove (

).
By Lemma

(2),
we have that for any
\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
and any

with

(3.5)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\delta(x_{G_{n}}^{M},y)\leqslant e^{-\varepsilon^{\prime}n}\right)\leqslant\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{\|G_{n}^{*}f\|}{\|G_{n}^{*}\|\|f\|}-\frac{\|\wedge^{2}G_{n}\|}{\|G_{n}\|^{2}}\leqslant e^{-\varepsilon^{\prime}n}\right).

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{\|\wedge^{2}G_{n}\|}{\|G_{n}\|^{2}}\geqslant e^{-(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}-\varepsilon)n}\right)=0,

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{\|G_{n}^{*}f\|}{\|G_{n}^{*}\|\|f\|}-\frac{\|\wedge^{2}G_{n}\|}{\|G_{n}\|^{2}}\leqslant e^{-\varepsilon^{\prime}n},\frac{\|\wedge^{2}G_{n}\|}{\|G_{n}\|^{2}}<e^{-(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}-\varepsilon)n}\right)+o(1)

6.
Proof of Cramér type moderate deviation expansion

6.1.
Smoothing inequality

For any integrable function
h:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
,
denote its Fourier transform by

\widehat{h}(u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-iuw}h(w)dw,

u\in\mathbb{R}
.
If
\widehat{h}
is integrable on
\mathbb{R}
, then by the Fourier inversion formula we have

h(w)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{iuw}\widehat{h}(u)du,
for almost all
w\in\mathbb{R}

with respect to the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}
.

Now we fix a non-negative density function
\rho
on
\mathbb{R}
with compact support
[-1,1]
,
whose Fourier transform
\widehat{\rho}
is integrable on
\mathbb{R}
.
For any
0<\varepsilon<1
, define the scaled density function

\rho_{\varepsilon}(w)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\rho(\frac{w}{\varepsilon})
,
w\in\mathbb{R},

which has a compact support on
[-\varepsilon^{-1},\varepsilon^{-1}]
.

For any
\varepsilon>0
and non-negative integrable function
\psi
on
\mathbb{R}
, set

(6.1)

\displaystyle\psi^{+}_{\varepsilon}(w)=\sup_{|w-w^{\prime}|\leqslant\varepsilon}\psi(w^{\prime})\quad\mbox{and}\quad\psi^{-}_{\varepsilon}(w)=\inf_{|w-w^{\prime}|\leqslant\varepsilon}\psi(w^{\prime}),\quad w\in\mathbb{R}.

We need the following smoothing inequality shown in
[
23
]
.
Denote by
h_{1}*h_{2}
the convolution of functions
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
on the real line.

Lemma 6.1

(
[
23
]
)
.

Assume that
\psi
is a non-negative integrable function on
\mathbb{R}
and that

{\psi}^{+}_{\varepsilon}
and
{\psi}^{-}_{\varepsilon}
are measurable for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
.
Then, there exists a positive constant
C_{\rho}(\varepsilon)
with
C_{\rho}(\varepsilon)\to 0
as
\varepsilon\to 0
,
such that for any
w\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\psi^{-}_{\varepsilon}*\rho_{\varepsilon^{2}}(w)-\int_{|u|\geqslant\varepsilon}\psi^{-}_{\varepsilon}(w-u)\rho_{\varepsilon^{2}}(u)du\leqslant\psi(w)\leqslant(1+C_{\rho}(\varepsilon))\psi^{+}_{\varepsilon}*\rho_{\varepsilon^{2}}(w).

For sufficiently small constant
s_{0}>0
and
s\in(0,s_{0})
, let

\displaystyle\psi_{s}(w)=e^{-sw}\mathds{1}_{\{w\geqslant 0\}},\quad w\in\mathbb{R}.

With the notation
\psi_{s,\varepsilon}^{-}(w)=\inf_{|w-w^{\prime}|\leqslant\varepsilon}\psi_{s}(w^{\prime})
(cf. (
6.1
)),
we have that for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
,

(6.2)

\displaystyle\psi_{s,\varepsilon}^{-}(w)=e^{-s(w+\varepsilon)}\mathds{1}_{\{w\geqslant\varepsilon\}},\quad w\in\mathbb{R}.

By elementary calculations, for any
s\in(0,s_{0})
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
,
the Fourier transform of
\psi_{s,\varepsilon}^{-}
is given by

(6.3)

\displaystyle\widehat{\psi}^{-}_{s,\varepsilon}(u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-iuw}\psi_{s,\varepsilon}^{-}(w)dw=e^{-2\varepsilon s}\frac{e^{-2i\varepsilon u}}{s+iu},\quad u\in\mathbb{R}.

6.2.
An asymptotic expansion of the perturbed operator

The goal of this section is to
establish the precise asymptotic behaviors of the perturbed operator
R_{s,it}
. This asymptotic result will play an important role for establishing
the Cramér type moderate deviation expansion for the coefficients
\langle f,G_{n}v\rangle

in Theorem
2.8
.
In the sequel, for any fixed
t>1
,
we shall choose
s>0
satisfying the following equation:

(6.4)

\displaystyle\Lambda^{\prime}(s)-\Lambda^{\prime}(0)=\frac{\sigma t}{\sqrt{n}}.

For brevity we denote
\sigma_{s}=\sqrt{\Lambda^{\prime\prime}(s)}
.
By
[
45
]
, the function
\Lambda
is strictly convex in a small neighborhood of
0
,
so that
\sigma_{s}>0
uniformly in
s\in(0,s_{0})
.

Proposition 6.2
.

Assume conditions
1
and
2
.
Let
s>0
be such that (
6.4
) holds.
Then, for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and any positive sequence
(t_{n})_{n\geqslant 1}
satisfying

t_{n}\to\infty
and
t_{n}/\sqrt{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
,
we have, uniformly in
s\in(0,s_{0})
,
x\in\mathbb{P}(V)
,
t\in[t_{n},o(\sqrt{n})]
,

\varphi\in\mathscr{B}_{\gamma}
and
|l|=O(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}})
,

\displaystyle\left|s\sigma_{s}\sqrt{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-iuln}R_{s,iu}^{n}(\varphi)(x)\widehat{\psi}^{-}_{s,\varepsilon}(u)\widehat{\rho}_{\varepsilon^{2}}(u)du-\sqrt{2\pi}\pi_{s}(\varphi)\right|

(6.5)

\displaystyle\leqslant c\left(\frac{t}{\sqrt{n}}+\frac{1}{t^{2}}\right)\|\varphi\|_{\infty}+c\left(|l|\sqrt{n}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\|\varphi\|_{\gamma}.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume that the target function
\varphi
is non-negative on
\mathbb{P}(V)
.
By Lemma
4.2
, we have the following decomposition:
with
\delta>0
small enough,

(6.6)

\displaystyle s\sigma_{s}\sqrt{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-iuln}R_{s,iu}^{n}(\varphi)(x)\widehat{\psi}^{-}_{s,\varepsilon}(u)\widehat{\rho}_{\varepsilon^{2}}(u)du=:I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3},

where

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=s\sigma_{s}\sqrt{n}\int_{|u|\geqslant\delta}e^{-iuln}R^{n}_{s,iu}(\varphi)(x)\widehat{\psi}^{-}_{s,\varepsilon}(u)\widehat{\rho}_{\varepsilon^{2}}(u)du,

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=s\sigma_{s}\sqrt{n}\int_{|u|<\delta}e^{-itln}N^{n}_{s,iu}(\varphi)(x)\widehat{\psi}^{-}_{s,\varepsilon}(u)\widehat{\rho}_{\varepsilon^{2}}(u)du,

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle=s\sigma_{s}\sqrt{n}\int_{|t|<\delta}e^{-iuln}\lambda^{n}_{s,iu}\Pi_{s,iu}(\varphi)(x)\widehat{\psi}^{-}_{s,\varepsilon}(u)\widehat{\rho}_{\varepsilon^{2}}(u)du.

For simplicity, we denote
K_{s}(iu)=\log\lambda_{s,iu}
and choose the branch where
K_{s}(0)=0
.
Using the formula (
4.7
), we have that for
u\in(-\delta,\delta)
,

\displaystyle K_{s}(iu)=\Lambda^{\prime}(s+iu)-\Lambda^{\prime}(s)-iu\Lambda^{\prime}(s).

Since the function
\Lambda
is analytic in a small neighborhood of

,
using Taylor’s formula gives

(6.7)

\displaystyle K_{s}(iu)=\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{\Lambda^{(k)}(s)}{k!}(iu)^{k},\quad\mbox{where}\ \Lambda(s)=\log\kappa(s)

and

(6.8)

\displaystyle\Lambda^{\prime}(s)-\Lambda^{\prime}(0)=\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{\gamma_{k}}{(k-1)!}s^{k-1},\quad\mbox{where}\ \gamma_{k}=\Lambda^{(k)}(0).

Combining
(
6.4
)
and
(
6.8
)
, we get

(6.9)

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma t}{\sqrt{n}}=\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{\gamma_{k}}{(k-1)!}s^{k-1}.

Since
\gamma_{2}=\sigma^{2}>0
, from (

)
we deduce that for any

and sufficiently large

,
the equation (

) has a unique solution given by

(6.10)

\displaystyle s=\frac{1}{\gamma_{2}^{1/2}}\frac{t}{\sqrt{n}}-\frac{\gamma_{3}}{2\gamma_{2}^{2}}\left(\frac{t}{\sqrt{n}}\right)^{2}-\frac{\gamma_{4}\gamma_{2}-3\gamma_{3}^{2}}{6\gamma_{2}^{7/2}}\left(\frac{t}{\sqrt{n}}\right)^{3}+\cdots.

For sufficiently large

, the series on the right-hand side of (
6.10
) is absolutely convergent
according to the theorem on the inversion of analytic functions.
Besides, from (
6.4
) and
t=o(\sqrt{n})
we see that
s\to 0^{+}
as
n\to\infty
,
so that we can assume
s\in(0,s_{0})
for sufficiently small constant

.

Estimate of
I_{1}
.

By Lemma
4.3
, for fixed
\delta>0
, there exist constants
c,C>0
such that

(6.11)

\displaystyle\sup_{s\in(0,s_{0})}\sup_{|u|\geqslant\delta}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{P}(V)}|R^{n}_{s,iu}\varphi(x)|\leqslant Ce^{-cn}\|\varphi\|_{\gamma}.

From (
6.3
) and the fact that
\rho_{\varepsilon^{2}}
is a density function on
\mathbb{R}
, we see that

(6.12)

\displaystyle\sup_{u\in\mathbb{R}}|\widehat{\psi}^{-}_{s,\varepsilon}(u)|\leqslant\widehat{\psi}^{-}_{s,\varepsilon}(0)=\frac{1}{s}e^{-2\varepsilon s},\quad\sup_{u\in\mathbb{R}}|\widehat{\rho}_{\varepsilon^{2}}(u)|\leqslant\widehat{\rho}_{\varepsilon^{2}}(0)=1.

Using (
6.11
) and the first inequality in (

),
and taking into account that the function
\widehat{\rho}_{\varepsilon^{2}}
is integrable on
\mathbb{R}
,
we obtain the desired bound for

:

(6.13)

\displaystyle|I_{1}|\leqslant Ce^{-cn}\|\varphi\|_{\gamma}.

Estimate of
I_{2}
.

Using the bound (
4.8
), we have
that uniformly in

\displaystyle\quad\times\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}_{s}^{x}}\left[\varphi_{s,k}^{y}(G_{n}\!\cdot\!x)({\Psi}^{+}_{s,\varepsilon}*\rho_{\varepsilon^{2}})(T_{n}^{x}-ak)\right],

\varepsilon\to 0
.
Since the function

\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle(\Psi^{+}_{s,\eta,\varepsilon}*\rho_{\varepsilon^{2}})(T_{n}^{x}-ak)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{iu(T_{n}^{x}-ak)}\widehat{\Psi}^{+}_{s,\varepsilon}(u)\widehat{\rho}_{\varepsilon^{2}}(u)du.

By the definition of the perturbed operator

(cf. (
4.5

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant(1+C_{\rho}(\varepsilon))\sigma\sqrt{\frac{n}{2\pi}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathds{1}_{\{k\leqslant M_{n}-1\}}e^{-sa(k-1)}

(7.8)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-iuak}R_{s,iu}^{n}(\varphi_{s,k}^{y})(x)\widehat{\Psi}^{+}_{s,\varepsilon}(u)\widehat{\rho}_{\varepsilon^{2}}(u)du.

To deal with the integral in (
7
), we shall use Lemma
7.1
.
Note that
e^{\frac{Ck^{2}}{n}}\to 1
as
n\to\infty
, uniformly in
0\leqslant k\leqslant M_{n}-1
.
Since the function
\widehat{\Psi}^{+}_{s,\varepsilon}\widehat{\rho}_{\varepsilon^{2}}
is compactly supported on
\mathbb{R}
,
applying Lemma
7.1
with
\varphi=\varphi_{s,k}^{y}
,

\psi=\widehat{\Psi}^{+}_{s,\varepsilon}\widehat{\rho}_{\varepsilon^{2}}

and
l=\frac{ak}{n\sigma}
,
we obtain that there exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
s\in(-s_{0},s_{0})
,

,

,
0\leqslant k\leqslant M_{n}-1
,
\varphi\in\mathscr{B}_{\gamma}
and

,

\displaystyle\left|\sigma\sqrt{\frac{n}{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-itak}R_{s,iu}^{n}(\varphi_{s,k}^{y})(x)\widehat{\Psi}^{+}_{s,\varepsilon}(u)\widehat{\rho}_{\varepsilon^{2}}(u)du-\widehat{\Psi}^{+}_{s,\varepsilon}(0)\widehat{\rho}_{\varepsilon^{2}}(0)\pi_{s}(\varphi_{s,k}^{y})\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant(1+C_{\rho}(\varepsilon))\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sup_{w^{\prime}\in\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon}(w)}\Psi_{0}(w^{\prime})dw\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\nu\left(\varphi_{0,k}^{y}\right)

:

(7.10)

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}J_{2}\leqslant\nu(\varphi)\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi(u)du.

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}J_{2}\geqslant\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\liminf_{n\to\infty}\sigma\sqrt{2\pi n}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}_{s}^{x}}\Big[\varphi_{s,k}^{y}(G_{n}\!\cdot\!x)(\Psi^{-}_{s,\varepsilon}*\rho_{\varepsilon^{2}})(T_{n}^{x}-ak)\Big]

\displaystyle\ -\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sigma\sqrt{2\pi n}\int_{|w|\geqslant\varepsilon}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}_{s}^{x}}\left[\varphi_{s,k}^{y}(G_{n}\!\cdot\!x)\Psi^{-}_{s,\varepsilon}(T_{n}^{x}-ak-w)\right]\rho_{\varepsilon^{2}}(w)dw

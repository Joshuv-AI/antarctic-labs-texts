Introduction to Lorentzian and Flat Affine Geometry of 𝖦𝖫 ⁡ ( 2 , ℝ ) \mathsf{GL}(2,\mathbb{R})
Alberto Medina

Abstract
The goal of this paper is to study the geometry of the connected unit component of the real general linear Lie group 4 4 dimensional G 0 G_{0} as a Lorentzian and flat affine manifold. As the group G 0 G_{0} is naturally equipped with a bi-invariant Hessian metric k + k^{+} , relative to a bi-invariant flat affine structure ∇ \nabla , we examine these structures and the relationships between them. Both structures are defined using the Lie algebra 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} , the first one through the trace k ⁡ ( u , v ) := trace ⁡ ( u ∘ v ) k(u,v):=\mathrm{trace}(u\circ v) and the second by the composition ∇ u + v + := ( u ∘ v ) + \nabla_{u^{+}}v^{+}:=(u\circ v)^{+} , where u , v ∈ 𝔤 u,v\in\mathfrak{g} . The curvatures, tidal force, and Jacobi vector fields of ( G 0 , k + ) (G_{0},k^{+}) are determined in Section 1. Section 2 discusses the causal structure of ( G 0 , k + ) (G_{0},k^{+}) , while Section 3 focuses on the developed map relative to ∇ \nabla in the sense of C. Ehresmann.



Keywords: Lorentzian metric, flat affine structure, quadratic Lie groups, curvatures, Weyl tensor.

1
Lorentzian Structure

Let
G_{0}
be the connected unit component of the Lie group of isomorphisms of the real vector space
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
\mathfrak{g}
its Lie algebra. We will identify
\mathfrak{g}
with
T_{\varepsilon}(G_{0})
, the tangent space of
G_{0}
in the identity element
\varepsilon

G_{0}
.

Consider the left-invariant metric
k^{+}
on
G_{0}
given by the inner product
k
on
\mathfrak{g}
defined by

k(u,v):=\mathrm{trace}(u\circ v),\text{ where }u,v\in\mathfrak{g}.

If
u^{+}
is the left-invariant vector field on
G_{0}
determined by
u\in\mathfrak{g}
, since the operators
ad_{u^{+}}
are
k^{+}
-antisymmetric, the right translations on
G_{0}
are also isometries. Then
k^{+}
is a bi-invariant metric, i.e.
(G_{0},k^{+})
is a quadratic Lie group in the Medina-Revoy’s sense.

Consider the following
k
-orthonormal basis
\mathcal{B}
of
\mathfrak{g}
given by

\displaystyle e_{1}

\displaystyle:=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\

\displaystyle e_{2}

\displaystyle:=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\

\displaystyle e_{3}

\displaystyle:=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&-1\end{array}\right),

\displaystyle e_{4}

\displaystyle:=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\

The Lie brackets, except antisymmetries, relatives to
\mathcal{B}
become

[e_{1},e_{2}]=\sqrt{2}e_{3},\;[e_{1},e_{3}]=-\sqrt{2}e_{2},\;[e_{2},e_{3}]=-\sqrt{2}e_{1}.

As

is given by the table

\begin{array}[]{r|c|c|c|c}k&e_{1}&e_{2}&e_{3}&e_{4}\\
\hline\cr e_{1}&-1&0&0&0\\
\hline\cr e_{2}&0&1&0&0\\
\hline\cr e_{3}&0&0&1&0\\
\hline\cr e_{4}&0&0&0&1\end{array}

k^{+}
is Lorentzian.

Recall that, a tangent vector
u

G_{0}
is called timelike (respectively lightlike, spacelike) if

(respectively

k(u,u)>0

e_{1}

We will show that every left-invariant semi-Riemannian metric on
G_{0}
is non-flat.

The following assertion is clear.

Lemma 1
.

Any left invariant semi-Riemannian metric on a quadratic Lie group
(H,g)
is given by a linear isomorphism
g

\varphi

\mathfrak{h}
by the formula

\langle u,v\rangle_{\varphi}:=g(\varphi(u),v)),\;u,v\in\mathfrak{h}

From Lemma
1

Proposition 1
.

Every left-invariant (respectively right-invariant) semi-Riemannian metric on
G_{0}
is not flat.

The proposition is a consequence of the following lemma

Lemma 2
.

The left-invariant semi-Riemannian metrics on
G_{0}
are given by the following
k
-symmetric linear isomorphisms of
\mathfrak{g}

\displaystyle\varphi_{0}(e_{1})

\displaystyle=-e_{1},\;\varphi_{0}(e_{2})=e_{2},\;\varphi_{0}(e_{3})=e_{3},\;\varphi_{0}(e_{4})=e_{4}

\displaystyle\varphi_{1}(e_{1})

\displaystyle=e_{1},\;\varphi_{1}(e_{2})=e_{2},\;\varphi_{1}(e_{3})=e_{3},\;\varphi_{1}(e_{4})=e_{4}

\displaystyle\varphi_{2}(e_{1})

\displaystyle=e_{1},\;\varphi_{2}(e_{2})=-e_{2},\;\varphi_{2}(e_{3})=e_{3},\;\varphi_{2}(e_{4})=e_{4}

and their index are

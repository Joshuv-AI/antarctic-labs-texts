A foundational result of Stasheff

In this section we recall the basics for complete differential graded Lie algebras, from the homotopy point of view. For it, original references are

For any category
{\mathcal{C}}
, we denote by
\operatorname{\text{\rm Hom}}_{\mathcal{C}}

All considered chain (or cochain) complexes, with possibly extra structures, are unbounded and have

(sV)_{n}=V_{n-1}
,

and
dsv=-sdv
. The
desuspension

{s^{-1}}V

We denote by
\operatorname{{\bf sset}}
the category of simplicial sets. For any
n\geq 0
, we denote by
\underline{\Delta}^{n}=\{\underline{\Delta}^{n}_{p}\}_{p\geq 0}
the simplicial set in which
\underline{\Delta}^{n}_{p}=\{(j_{0},\dots,j_{p})\mid 0\leq j_{0}\leq\dots\leq j_{p}\leq n\}

X
and
Y

\operatorname{{\rm map}}(X,Y)
the
simplicial mapping space
, that is,

\operatorname{{\rm map}}(X,Y)_{\bullet}=\operatorname{\text{\rm Hom}}_{\operatorname{{\bf sset}}}(X\times\underline{\Delta}^{\bullet},Y).

Analogously, we denote by
\operatorname{{\rm map}}^{*}(X,Y)
the
pointed mapping space

\operatorname{\text{\rm Hom}}_{\operatorname{{\bf sset}}^{*}}(X\times\underline{\Delta}^{\bullet},Y)
in the category
\operatorname{{\bf sset}}^{*}

Let
\operatorname{{\bf dgl}}
denote the category of differential graded Lie algebras (dgl’s henceforth). A dgl
L
, or
(L,d)
if we want to specify the differential, is
n
-connected
, for
n\geq 0
, if
L=L_{\geq n}
. As usual, connected means
0
-connected. The
n
-connected cover of a dgl

is the
n
-connected sub dgl
{L}^{(n)}
given by

L^{(n)}_{p}=\begin{cases}\ker d&\text{if $p=n$},\\
\,\,\,L_{p}&\text{if $p>n$}.\end{cases}

We denote by
\widetilde{L}=L^{(1)}
the
1
-connected cover.

Given a dgl
L
we denote by
{\rm Der}L
the dgl of its
derivations
in which, for each
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
{\rm Der}_{n}L
are linear maps
\theta\colon L\to L
of degree
n
such that

\theta[a,b]=[\theta(a),b]+(-1)^{|a|n}[a,\theta(b)],{\quad\text{for}\quad}a,b\in L.

The Lie bracket and differential are given by,

[\theta,\eta]=\theta\circ\eta-(-1)^{|\theta||\eta|}\eta\circ\theta,\quad D\theta=d\circ\theta-(-1)^{|\theta|}\theta\circ d.

On the other hand, given a dgl morphism
f\colon L\to M
we denote by
{\rm Der}_{f}(L,M)
the chain complex of
f
-derivations
in which
{\rm Der}_{f}(L,M)_{n}
are linear maps
\theta\colon L\to M
of degree
n
such that

\theta[a,b]=[\theta(a),f(b)]+(-1)^{n|a|}[f(a),\theta(b)],\quad a,b\in L.

The differential is given as before.

Following
[
36
, §7.2]
, or more generally
[
2
, §3.5]
, a
twisted product

L\widetilde{\times}M
of the dgl’s
L
and
M
is a dgl structure on the underlying vector space
L\times M
so that

0\to L\longrightarrow L\widetilde{\times}M{\longrightarrow}M\to 0

is an exact dgl sequence. In particular

is a sub dgl of the twisted product.

A
filtration
of a dgl
L
is a decreasing sequence of differential Lie ideals,

L=F^{1}\supset\dots\supset F^{n}\supset F^{n+1}\supset\dots

such that
[F^{p},F^{q}]\subset F^{p+q}
for
p,q\geq 1
. In particular, if

L^{1}\supset\dots\supset L^{n}\supset L^{n+1}\supset\dots

denote the lower central series of
L
, that is,
L^{1}=L
and
L^{n}=[L,L^{n-1}]
for
n>1

L^{n}\subset F^{n}
for all
n
.

A
complete differential graded Lie algebra
, cdgl henceforth, is a dgl

\{F^{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
for which the natural map

L\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\varprojlim_{n}L/F^{n}

is a dgl isomorphism. A
morphism

f\colon L\to L^{\prime}
between cdgl’s, associated to filtrations
\{F^{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
and
\{{G}^{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
respectively, is a dgl morphism which preserves the filtrations, that is,
f(F^{n})\subset G^{n}
for each
n\geq 1
.
We denote by
\operatorname{{\bf cdgl}}
the corresponding category which is complete and cocomplete
[
11

.

Given a dgl
L
filtered by
\{F^{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
, its

is the dgl

\widehat{L}=\varprojlim_{n}L/F^{n}.

which is always complete with respect to the filtration

\widehat{F}^{n}=\ker(\widehat{L}\to L/F^{n})

\widehat{L}/\widehat{F}^{n}=L/F^{n}

for an exhaustive treatment of this cdgl. In this instance,

\widehat{F}^{n}=\widehat{\mathbb{L}}^{\geq n}(V)=\prod_{q\geq n}\mathbb{L}^{q}(V),{\quad\text{for}\quad}n\geq 1.

By an abuse of notation, we write

If

2
Free and pointed homotopy classes in
\operatorname{{\bf cdgl}}

Throughout the next sections it will be essential to make a clear distinction between the sets of free and pointed homotopy classes of maps, from the cdgl point of view. This is a subtle but crucial point that we settle here. Note that this discussion is meaningless in the classical Quillen theory as, in the simply connected category, both sets coincide.

Recall from
[
11
, §8.3]
that two cdgl morphisms
\varphi,\psi\colon M\to L
are homotopic if there is a cdgl morphism
\Phi\colon M\to L\widehat{\otimes}\land(t,dt)
such that
\varepsilon_{0}\circ\Phi=\varphi
and
\varepsilon_{1}\circ\Phi=\psi
.
Here,

is the free commutative graded algebra generated by the element

of degree
0
and its differential

. Also, for

,
\varepsilon_{i}\colon L\widehat{\otimes}\land(t,dt)\to L
is the cdgl morphism obtained by sending

to

. We denote by

the set of homotopy classes of cdgl morphisms from

to
L
.

For the rest of this section we fix a connected cdgl
L
and a connected (non necessarily reduced) simplicial set

, pointed by the
0
-simplex

. Analogously, we denote respectively by
\{X,\langle L\rangle\}
and

the set of free and pointed homotopy classes of simplicial maps. Note that, since
\langle L\rangle
is reduced, being

the only

-simplex, every map

is pointed. Then:

Proposition 2.1
.

\{X,\langle L\rangle\}\cong\{{\mathfrak{L}}_{X},L\}

\{X,\langle L\rangle\}^{*}\cong\{{\mathfrak{L}}_{X}/(b),L\}
.

Here

Proof.

As the model and realization functors constitute a Quillen pair, the first identity is obvious

, Corollary 8.2(iv)]

The adjunction (

3
Complete subgroups of complete Lie algebras

In this section all complete Lie algebras will be ungraded, or equivalently, concentrated in degree
0
, and with no differential. We denote by
\operatorname{{\bf cl}}
the corresponding category and, to stress this restriction, we will denote by
M
(instead of

We first recall the category isomorphism between complete Lie algebras and complete groups in the Malcev sense.

Given a group
G
we denote its commutator by curved brackets:

(x,y)=xyx^{-1}y^{-1}

and consider its
lower central series,

G=G^{1}\supset G^{2}\supset\dots\supset G^{i}\supset G^{i+1}\supset\dots

where
G^{i}=(G^{i-1},G)
for
i\geq 2
. A group

is
pronilpotent
if the natural map
G\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\varprojlim_{n}G/G^{n}
is an isomorphism. On the other hand recall that
G
is said to be
0
-local, uniquely divisible or rational if for any natural
n\geq 1
the map
G\to G
,
g\mapsto g^{n}
,
is a bijection.

Definition 3.1
.

A group

is
Malcev
\mathbb{Q}
-complete
(or simply complete) if it is pronilpotent and for each

the abelian group

G^{n}/G^{n+1}
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space. With the obvious notion of morphism we denote by
\operatorname{{\bf cgrp}}
the corresponding category of complete groups.

From the original work of Malcev
[
24
]
several reformulations and generalization of the so called
Malcev equivalence
can be found in the literature. Following
[
29
, §A.3]
and its modern reformulation in
[
16
, §8.2.8]
, a suitable form for our goals of this statement reads:

There is an isomorphism of categories

\textstyle{\operatorname{{\bf cl}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Phi}
\textstyle{\mathbf{dgl}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Psi}

with the following properties:

•

The underlying set is not altered by any of the functors.

•

Given
M
a complete Lie algebra
M
,
\Psi(M)
is the group
(M,\ast)
where
\ast
is the BCH product. There are also explicit formulas for the Lie algebra structure

for any given complete group

(see for instance
[

).

•

If
\Phi(M)=G
then for each

, there is an equality of sets

(see
[
15
, Theorem 2.2]
).

•

When restricted to nilpotent Lie algebras and

-local nilpotent groups, this is the original equivalence given in
[
23
]
and
[
24
]
.

In this context, we will need the following, slightly different, characterization of Malcev complete groups.

Theorem 3.2
.

A group

is Malcev complete if and only if it is pronilpotent and
0
-local.

Proof.

The Malcev equivalence implies that a Malcev complete group is equivalent to a Lie algebra. In particular, the multiplication by scalars in the Lie algebra implies that the group is
0
-local.

For the other implication note first that,
given a complete Lie algebra
M
, the Lie algebras
M^{n}
and
M/M^{n}
are also complete for any
n\geq 1
. Via the Malcev isomorphism we deduce that
G^{n}
and
G/G^{n}
are complete groups whenever

is a complete group.

We only have to prove that
G^{n}/G^{n+1}
is
0
-local for all
n\geq 1
. For it, we
first show that the group
G/G^{n}
is torsion-free for

. Suppose that there is

such that

x^{k}\equiv 0\pmod{G^{n}},

that is,
x^{k}\in G^{n}
. We then may write

x^{k}=\prod_{i=1}^{m}(a_{i},b_{i})

where
a_{i}\in G
and
b_{i}\in G^{n-1}
.
Since
G
is
0
-local, for each

we can find
c_{i}\in G
such that
c_{i}^{k}=a_{i}
. Note that in
G/G^{n+1}
the class of the elements of
G^{n}=(G,G^{n-1})
are in the center and thus one easily checks that

(a_{i},b_{i})=(c_{i}^{k},b_{i})\equiv(c_{i},b_{i})^{k}\pmod{G^{n+1}}.

Then, write

y=\prod_{i=1}^{m}(c_{i},b_{i})

which is a product of elements of
G^{n}
and thus, modulo
G^{n+1}
, we have:

y^{k}=\biggl(\prod_{i=1}^{m}(c_{i},b_{i})\biggr)^{k}\equiv\prod_{i=1}^{m}(c_{i},b_{i})^{k}\equiv\prod_{i=1}^{m}(a_{i},b_{i})=x^{k}\pmod{G^{n+1}}.

In particular, since

,

(xy^{-1})^{k}\equiv 0\pmod{G^{n+1}}.

Write
x_{n}=x
,
x_{n+1}=xy^{-1}
and repeat this process to obtain a sequence of elements

in

such that

x_{j+1}\equiv x_{j}\pmod{G^{j}}

and

for all

.
However, since

and, denoting

, we may then identify the sequence

with an element of

In particular,

, which implies that

. Since

. Therefore, the sequence

is identically zero. In particular,

, and therefore

is torsion-free.

Next, since

is nilpotent and torsion-free, by

Finally, consider the short exact sequence

∎

6
Complete Lie algebras of derivations and twisted products

Given
L
a dgl (non necessarily complete in principle), the following twisted products are key tools for our main results:

Given a cdgc
C
consider the twisted product

\operatorname{\text{\rm Hom}}(C,L)\longrightarrow\operatorname{\text{\rm Hom}}(C,L)\,\widetilde{\times}\,{\rm Der}L\longrightarrow{\rm Der}L

(20)

in which both terms are sub dgl’s and

[\theta,f]=\theta\circ f,\quad\theta\in{\rm Der}L,\quad f\in\operatorname{\text{\rm Hom}}(C,L).

This dgl sequence, and its “dual”
{\rm Coder}(C)\widetilde{\times}\operatorname{\text{\rm Hom}}(C,L)
, have already proven to be useful in the modeling of certain simply connected spaces of homotopy automorphisms, see
[
2
, §3]
and
[
3
, §4]
.

Also, consider the twisted product

{\rm Der}L\longrightarrow{\rm Der}L\widetilde{\times}sL\longrightarrow sL

(21)

where
sL
is an abelian Lie algebra and

Dsx=-sdx+\operatorname{{\rm ad}}_{x},\quad[\theta,sx]=(-1)^{|\theta|}s\theta(x),\quad x\in L,\quad\theta\in{\rm Der}L.

Finally, let

L\longrightarrow L\widetilde{\times}{\rm Der}L\longrightarrow{\rm Der}L

(22)

be the twisted product in which both terms are sub dgl’s and
[\theta,x]=\theta(x)
for any
\theta\in{\rm Der}L
and
x\in L
.

If
L
is a cdgl we observed in Remark
4.1
that
\operatorname{\text{\rm Hom}}(C,L)
and any of its isomorphic forms, see for instance (
10
) or (
16
), are complete dgl’s. However,
{\rm Der}L
is not complete in general and, even if it is,
\operatorname{\text{\rm Hom}}(C,L)\widetilde{\times}{\rm Der}L
may fail to be so. The same applies to the twisted products
{\rm Der}L\widetilde{\times}sL
and
L\widetilde{\times}{\rm Der}L
just defined.

The following illustrates this situation.

Example 6.1
.

(1) Consider the cdgl
L=({\widehat{\mathbb{L}}}(x,y),0)
where
|x|=|y|=0
. Observe that
{\rm Der}L={\rm Der}_{0}L
and define
\theta,\gamma\in{\rm Der}(L)
by

\theta(x)=y,\quad\theta(y)=0,\qquad\gamma(x)=-\frac{1}{2}x,\quad\gamma(y)=\frac{1}{2}y.

A short computation shows that
[\gamma,\theta]=\theta
and thus
\operatorname{{\rm ad}}^{n}_{\gamma}(\theta)=\theta
for any
n\geq 1
. In particular
\theta
lives in the kernel of the canonical map
{\rm Der}L\to\varprojlim_{n\geq 1}{\rm Der}L/({\rm Der}L)^{n}
which, therefore, prevents
{\rm Der}L
to be complete.

(2) On the other hand, consider an odd dimensional sphere
S^{n}
whose model is the abelian Lie algebra
L=(\mathbb{L}(x),0)
with
x
of degree
n-1
. In this case
{\mathscr{C}}(L)=(\land sx,0)
. Then,
\operatorname{{\rm map}}(S^{n}_{\mathbb{Q}},S^{n}_{\mathbb{Q}})
is modeled by
\operatorname{\text{\rm Hom}}({\mathscr{C}}(L),L)
which is an abelian Lie algebra generated by
x
, corresponding to
1\mapsto x
, and an element
z
of degree
-1
, corresponding to
sx\mapsto x
. On the other hand
{\rm Der}L
is an abelian lie algebra generated by
\operatorname{{\rm id}}_{L}
, a degree
0
derivation which we denote by
\theta
. Hence, in this case, (
20
) takes the form

\operatorname{{Span}}\{x,z\}\longrightarrow\operatorname{{Span}}\{x,z,\theta\}\longrightarrow\operatorname{{Span}}\{\theta\}

where, in the middle,
[\theta,x]=x
and
[\theta,z]=z
. Observe then that the bracket does not respect the usual filtration in the non twisted product. Note also that, even though
\operatorname{\text{\rm Hom}}({\mathscr{C}}(L),L)
and
{\rm Der}L
are obviously complete as they are abelian,
\operatorname{\text{\rm Hom}}({\mathscr{C}}(L),L)\,\widetilde{\times}\,{\rm Der}L
is not (with respect to the usual bracket filtration) as its completion is easily seen to be just
\operatorname{{Span}}\{\theta\}
. That is, the completion of (

) becomes

\operatorname{\text{\rm Hom}}({\mathscr{C}}(L),L)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 0}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\rm Der}L\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\operatorname{{\rm id}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\rm Der}L.

This also illustrates that the completion functor is not left exact in general.

(3) Finally, with
L
as in (2) above, observe that the twisted dgl

becomes the dgl

\operatorname{{Span}}\{\theta,sx\}
, with zero differential and

, whose completion is
{\rm Der}L=\operatorname{{Span}}\{\theta\}
. An analogous argument shows that
L\widetilde{\times}{\rm Der}L
fails also to be complete with respect to the usual filtration.

The purpose of this section is to overcome these obstacles by imposing some restrictions which are sufficiently mild for our goals.

From now on we fix
L=({\widehat{\mathbb{L}}}(V),d)
a connected, minimal, free cdgl and a finite filtration of
V
by graded subspaces:

V=V^{0}\supset\dots\supset V^{i}\supset V^{i+1}\supset\dots\supset V^{q}=0,

(23)

We first refine the usual filtration in
L
by considering,
for
n\geq 1
and
p\geq 0
,

{\widehat{\mathbb{L}}}^{n,p}(V)=\operatorname{{Span}}\{\bigl[a_{1},[a_{2},[\dots,[a_{n-1},a_{n}]\bigr]\dots\bigr]\in{\widehat{\mathbb{L}}}^{n}(V),\,a_{i}\in V^{\alpha_{i}}\,\text{and}\,\,{\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}}\alpha_{i}=p\}.

Observe that
{\widehat{\mathbb{L}}}^{n,p}(V)=0
for
p\geq nq
. Then, for
n\geq 1
and
0\leq p\leq nq-1
, define

F^{n,p}={\widehat{\mathbb{L}}}^{n,p}(V)\oplus{\widehat{\mathbb{L}}}^{\geq n+1}(V)

and note that,

\displaystyle{\widehat{\mathbb{L}}}(V)

\displaystyle=F^{1,0}\supset F^{1,1}\supset\dots\supset F^{1,q-1}

\displaystyle\supset F^{2,0}\supset F^{2,1}\supset\dots\supset F^{2,2q-1}

\displaystyle...............................................

\displaystyle\supset F^{n,0}\supset F^{n,1}\supset\dots\supset F^{n,nq-1}

\displaystyle...............................................

Remark that
F^{n,p}

ranks

m=q+\dots+(n-1)q+p+1=\frac{(n-1)nq}{2}+p+1

(24)

in the order given by this chain of inclusions.

Definition 6.2
.

For
n,p
and
m
as above define

F^{m}=F^{n,p}.

Proposition 6.3
.

The sequence
\{F^{m}\}_{m\geq 1}
is a filtration of
L
. Furthermore,
L
is complete with respect to this filtration.

Proof.

Observe that
[F^{n,p},F^{r,s}]\subset F^{n+r,p+s}
. Now,
F^{n,p}
and
F^{r,s}
rank respectively

\frac{(n-1)nq}{2}+p+1
and
\frac{(r-1)rq}{2}+s+1

in the filtration order. The sum of these integers is smaller than

\frac{(n+r-1)(n+r)q}{2}+p+s+1
which is the position of
F^{n+r,p+s}
in that order. This shows that the Lie bracket is filtration preserving.

On the other hand, since the differential of
L
is decomposable each
F^{m}
is a differential ideal. Moreover, we have

dF^{m}\subset F^{m+1}

We now check that
L
is complete respect to this filtration. Since
\cap_{n,p}F^{n,p}=0
the natural map

{\widehat{\mathbb{L}}}(V)\longrightarrow\varprojlim_{m}{\widehat{\mathbb{L}}}(V)/F^{m}

is injective. Finally, write a given element in
\varprojlim_{n,p}{\widehat{\mathbb{L}}}(V)/F^{n,p}
as a series,

\sum_{n,p}x_{n,p}{\quad\text{with}\quad}x_{n,p}\in F^{n,p},

and note that this is a well defined element in
{\widehat{\mathbb{L}}}(V)
since, for each
m\geq 1
,
\sum_{n,p}x_{n,p}
contains only a finite sum in
{\widehat{\mathbb{L}}}^{m}(V)
. This implies the surjectivity of the map above.
∎

Note that considering in
L
the usual filtration
\{{\widehat{\mathbb{L}}}^{\geq m}(V)\}_{m\geq 1}
for which it is complete, the identity
(L,\{{\widehat{\mathbb{L}}}^{\geq m}(V)\}_{m\geq 1})\to(L,\{F^{m}\}_{m\geq 1})
is a cdgl morphism.

Definition 6.4
.

Denote by
{\mathcal{D}er}L\subset{\rm Der}L
the connected sub dgl in which,

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\mathcal{D}er}_{\geq 1}L={\rm Der}_{\geq 1}L,

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\mathcal{D}er}_{0}L=\{\theta\in{\rm Der}_{0}L\cap\ker D,\,\,\theta(V^{i})\subset V^{i+1}\oplus{\widehat{\mathbb{L}}}^{\geq 2}(V)\,\text{for all $i$}\,\}.

Note that this is a well defined Lie algebra and, since the differential in
L
is decomposable, it is a differential sub Lie algebra.

Using the new filtration on
L
we first construct a filtration of
{\mathcal{D}er}_{0}L
for which it becomes a cdgl. For it, note that

{\mathcal{D}er}_{0}L=\{\theta\in{\rm Der}_{0}L\cap\ker D,\,\,\theta(F^{m})\subset F^{m+1}\,\,\text{for all $m$}\,\}

Hence, we filter
{\mathcal{D}er}_{0}L
by,

\operatorname{{\mathcal{F}}}^{n}=\{\theta\in{\mathcal{D}er}_{0}L,\,\,\theta(F^{m})\subset F^{m+n},\,\,\text{for all $m$}\,\},\quad n\geq 1,

(25)

and a straightforward computation proves:

Lemma 6.5
.

{\mathcal{D}er}_{0}L
is complete with respect to
\{\operatorname{{\mathcal{F}}}^{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
.
\square

To finish, we “extend” this filtration on
{\mathcal{D}er}_{0}L
to
{\mathcal{D}er}L
by means of the following procedure:

Consider first the subspaces of
{\rm Der}_{\geq 1}L
,

M^{n}=\{\theta\in{\rm Der}_{\geq 1}L,\,\theta(L)\subset L^{\geq n+1}\},\qquad n\geq 1,

and choose a refinement of this sequence by constant subspaces

\displaystyle M^{1}=

\displaystyle\cal{\displaystyle J}^{1,0}={\cal J}^{1,1}=\dots={\cal J}^{1,q-1}\supset

\displaystyle M^{2}=

\displaystyle\cal{\displaystyle J}^{2,0}={\cal J}^{2,1}=\dots={\cal J}^{2,2q-1}\supset

\displaystyle..................................................

\displaystyle M^{n}=

\displaystyle\cal{\displaystyle J}^{n,0}={\cal J}^{n,1}=\dots={\cal J}^{n,nq-1}\supset

\displaystyle..................................................

As in (
24
),
{\cal J}^{n,p}

ranks

m=\frac{(n-1)nq}{2}+p+1
in the order given by this chain, and define

{\cal J}^{m}={\cal J}^{n,p}.

The only purpose of extracting this refinement is simply to assure that,

[\operatorname{{\mathcal{F}}}^{n},{\cal J}^{m}]\subset{\cal J}^{m+n},\quad n,m\geq 1.

Finally, for any
n\geq 1
define the graded vector space
E^{n}
as follows:

E^{n}_{p}=\begin{cases}\operatorname{{\mathcal{F}}}^{n}&\text{if $p=0$,}\\
{\cal J}^{n-p}_{p}&\text{if $1\leq p\leq n-1$,}\\
{\rm Der}_{p}L&\text{if $p\geq n$.}\end{cases}

(26)

Proposition 6.6
.

{\mathcal{D}er}L
is a cdgl with respect to the filtration
\{E^{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
.

Proof.

Note that,
E^{1}=\operatorname{{\mathcal{F}}}^{1}\oplus{\rm Der}_{\geq 1}L={\mathcal{D}er}L
. Also, since the differential in
L
is decomposable,
D{\cal J}^{m}_{p}\subset{\cal J}^{m+1}_{p-1}
for
p\geq 2
, and
D{\cal J}^{m}_{1}\subset\operatorname{{\mathcal{F}}}^{m+1}
, for all
m\geq 1
. Finally
[{\cal J}^{n},{\cal J}^{m}]\subset{\cal J}^{n+m}
while, as noted above,
[\operatorname{{\mathcal{F}}}^{n},{\cal J}^{m}]\subset{\cal J}^{m+n}
for
n,m\geq 1
. With this data one easily checks that
\{E^{n}\}_{\geq 1}
is a filtration of
{\mathcal{D}er}L
.

Since, for
p\geq 1
and
n\geq 0
, we have

E^{n}_{p}=\begin{cases}{\rm Der}_{p}L&\text{if $n\leq p$,}\\
{\cal J}^{n-p}_{p}&\text{if $n>p$},\end{cases}

it follows that, for
p\geq 1
,

\varprojlim_{n}({\mathcal{D}er}L/E^{n})_{p}=\varprojlim_{n}{\rm Der}_{p}L/{\cal J}^{n}_{p}={\rm Der}_{p}L.

On the other hand, for
p=0
, and in view of Lemma
6.5
,

\varprojlim_{n}({\mathcal{D}er}L/E^{n})_{0}=\varprojlim_{n}{\mathcal{D}er}_{0}L/\operatorname{{\mathcal{F}}}^{n}={\mathcal{D}er}_{0}L,

∎

Next, given
f\colon({\widehat{\mathbb{L}}}(V),d)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\to}}({\widehat{\mathbb{L}}}(V),d)
an element of
\operatorname{{\rm aut}}L
, denote by

f_{*}\colon V\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}V

the induced automorphism on the indecomposables. Then, we prove:

Proposition 6.7
.

\exp\bigl({\mathcal{D}er}_{0}L\bigr)=\{f\in\operatorname{{\rm aut}}(L),\,\,(f_{*}-\operatorname{{\rm id}}_{V})(V^{i})\subset V^{i+1}\,\,\text{for all $i$}\,\}.

Proof.

Let
\theta\in{\mathcal{D}er}_{0}L
and call
f=e^{\theta}
. Then
f-\operatorname{{\rm id}}_{L}=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\theta^{n}}{n!}
which clearly satisfies
(f-\operatorname{{\rm id}}_{L})(V^{i})\subset V^{i+1}\oplus{\widehat{\mathbb{L}}}^{\geq 2}(V)
for all
i
. Hence
(f_{*}-\operatorname{{\rm id}}_{V})(V^{i})\subset V^{i+1}
also for all
i
.

Conversely, let
f\in\operatorname{{\rm aut}}(L)
be such that
f(v)-v\in V^{i+1}\oplus{\widehat{\mathbb{L}}}^{\geq 2}(V)
for all
v\in V^{i}
and all
i
. Define a linear morphism,

\theta\colon V\longrightarrow L,\qquad\theta(v)=\sum_{n\geq 1}(-1)^{n}\frac{(f-\operatorname{{\rm id}}_{L})^{n}(v)}{n}

Recursively, one sees that
the component of
(f-\operatorname{{\rm id}}_{L})^{n}(v)
in
{\widehat{\mathbb{L}}}^{m,r}(V)
is zero for
n
large enough. Thus,
\theta
is well defined and can be extended to a derivation
\theta\in{\mathcal{D}er}_{0}(L)
. Finally, since the formula that defines
\theta
is that of
\log f
, computing
e^{\theta}
recovers
f
.
∎

Next, recall from Remark
4.1
that, for any cdgc
C
and any cdgl

,
\operatorname{\text{\rm Hom}}(C,L)
is complete with respect to the natural filtration given by that of

. However, we will need a new filtration which is “compatible” with the one we just constructed for

.

Definition 6.8
.

Let

be a minimal cdgl filtered by

of Definition

. For

by,

{I}^{m}_{k}=\operatorname{\text{\rm Hom}}(C,F_{k}^{m-k}),\qquad\text{for $k\geq 0$},

where

for

.

Proposition 6.9
.

The sequence
\{{I}^{m}\}_{m\geq 1}
is a filtration of
\operatorname{\text{\rm Hom}}(C,L)
for which it becomes a cdgl.

Proof.

Clearly
I^{1}=\operatorname{\text{\rm Hom}}(C,L)
,
I^{m+1}\subset I^{m}
and
D{I}^{m}\subset{I}^{m}
for all

. On the other hand, given
\varphi\in\operatorname{\text{\rm Hom}}(C,F^{n-i}_{i})\subset I^{n}
,

and

This implies that
[I^{n},I^{m}]\subset I^{n+m}
and therefore

is a filtration. A degree-wise analogous argument to that in Remark

shows that

is complete with respect to this new filtration.
∎

Next, in the restriction of the twisted product in (

\operatorname{\text{\rm Hom}}(C,L)\,\widetilde{\times}\,{\mathcal{D}er}L,

\{J^{n}\}_{n\geq 1}=\{I^{n}\times E^{n}\}_{n\geq 1}

.

The sequence
\{{J}^{n}\}_{n\geq 1}

\square

Furthermore, from Propositions

The maps

7
Nilpotent monoids of homotopy automorphisms and classifying fibrations

Whenever
X
is a simply connected finite complex with minimal model
L
, it is well known (see
[
32
,
36
]
,
[
2
]
in the fiberwise context or
[
3
]
for the relative case) that the fibration,

X_{\mathbb{Q}}\longrightarrow B\operatorname{{\rm aut}}^{*}_{1}(X_{\mathbb{Q}})\longrightarrow B\operatorname{{\rm aut}}_{1}(X_{\mathbb{Q}})

is modeled by a dgl fibration sequence of the form

L\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\operatorname{{\rm ad}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\widetilde{\rm Der}L\longrightarrow\widetilde{\rm Der}L\widetilde{\times}sL.

where
\widetilde{\rm Der}L\widetilde{\times}sL
is the restriction of the twisted product (
21
).
Again for
X
simply connected, the above fibration is the simply connected cover of
the universal classifying fibration

X_{\mathbb{Q}}\longrightarrow B\operatorname{{\rm aut}}^{*}(X_{\mathbb{Q}})\longrightarrow B\operatorname{{\rm aut}}(X_{\mathbb{Q}}).

However, in general and even if
X
is simply connected, this classifying fibration cannot be modeled. Indeed, as the following result shows,
B\operatorname{{\rm aut}}^{*}(X_{\mathbb{Q}})
and
B\operatorname{{\rm aut}}(X_{\mathbb{Q}})
do not lie, in general, in the image of the realization functor. In particular, they are not nilpotent spaces.

Proposition 7.1
.

For any
n\geq 1
, neither
B\operatorname{{\rm aut}}^{*}(S^{n}_{\mathbb{Q}})
nor
B\operatorname{{\rm aut}}(S^{n}_{\mathbb{Q}})
have the homotopy type of the realization of any connected cdgl.

Proof.

Suppose
B\operatorname{{\rm aut}}^{*}(S^{n}_{\mathbb{Q}})\simeq\langle L\rangle
for a given connected cdgl
L
. Then there is a group isomorphism

\pi_{0}\operatorname{{\rm aut}}^{*}(S^{n}_{\mathbb{Q}})\cong\pi_{1}B\operatorname{{\rm aut}}^{*}(S^{n}_{\mathbb{Q}})\cong H_{0}(L)

where in the latter, the group structure is given by the BCH product. However, by Remark
4.5
,
\pi_{0}\operatorname{{\rm map}}^{*}(S^{n}_{\mathbb{Q}})=\{\mathbb{L}(x),\mathbb{L}(x)\}*=\{\mathbb{L}(x),\mathbb{L}(x)\}
. Hence,
\pi_{0}\operatorname{{\rm aut}}^{*}(S^{n}_{\mathbb{Q}})\cong\{\mathbb{L}(x),\mathbb{L}(x)\}-\{0\}
are just the automorphisms of a one dimensional vector space which is identified with the multiplicative group
\mathbb{Q}^{*}=\mathbb{Q}-\{0\}
.

Now, assume there is an isomorphism
\psi\colon\mathbb{Q}^{*}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\to}}H_{0}(L)
and let
a=\psi(2)
. Recall that, for the BCH product,
\mu a*\nu a=(\lambda+\mu)a
for
\mu,\nu\in\mathbb{Q}
. Thus, given
\lambda\in\mathbb{Q}^{*}
with
\psi(\lambda)=\frac{1}{2}a
we have

\psi(\lambda^{2})=\frac{1}{2}a*\frac{1}{2}a=a=\psi(2)
. Then
\lambda^{2}=2
which is a contradiction. The same argument also works for the non-pointed case.

∎

Despite this result we will be able to realize distinguished classifying fibrations, also by means of cdgl’s of derivations.

Let
X
be a connected finite complex and let
\pi_{0}\operatorname{{\rm aut}}(X)
be the group of homotopy classes of self homotopy equivalences of
X
, often denoted by
{\cal E}(X)
. In the same way,
\pi_{0}\operatorname{{\rm aut}}^{*}(X)
is the group
{\cal E}^{*}(X)
of based homotopy classes of pointed self homotopy equivalences of
X
. Denote by

\zeta\colon{\mathcal{E}}^{*}(X)\longrightarrow{\mathcal{E}}(X)

(27)

the natural surjection which induces the bijection
{\mathcal{E}}^{*}(X)/\pi_{1}(X)\cong{\mathcal{E}}(X)
.

Definition 7.2
.

For a given subgroup
G\subset{\mathcal{E}}(X)
we consider the sub monoids
\operatorname{{\rm aut}}_{G}(X)\subset\operatorname{{\rm aut}}(X)
and
\operatorname{{\rm aut}}_{G}^{*}(X)\subset\operatorname{{\rm aut}}^{*}(X)
defined by

\operatorname{{\rm aut}}_{G}(X)=\{f\in\operatorname{{\rm aut}}(X),\,\,[f]\in G\},\quad\operatorname{{\rm aut}}_{G}^{*}(X)=\{f\in\operatorname{{\rm aut}}^{*}(X),\,\,[f]\in G\}

Note that
\pi_{0}\operatorname{{\rm aut}}_{G}(X)=G
while
\pi_{0}\operatorname{{\rm aut}}_{G}^{*}(X)
is the subgroup
G^{*}\subset{\mathcal{E}}^{*}(X)
consisting of pointed homotopy classes of pointed homotopy automorphisms in
\operatorname{{\rm aut}}_{G}^{*}(X)
. In other terms,

G^{*}=\zeta^{-1}(G)=\{[f]^{*}\in{\mathcal{E}}^{*}(X),\,\,\text{with $[f]\in G$}\}.

Thus, note that
G^{*}
is preserved by the action of
\pi_{1}(X)
and

G^{*}/\pi_{1}(X)\cong G.

Then, the evaluation fibration
\operatorname{{\rm map}}^{*}(X,X)\longrightarrow\operatorname{{\rm map}}(X,X)\longrightarrow X
restricts to a fibration
\operatorname{{\rm aut}}_{G}^{*}(X)\to\operatorname{{\rm aut}}_{G}(X)\to X
which is extended on the right to provide a fibration sequence

X\longrightarrow B\operatorname{{\rm aut}}^{*}_{G}(X)\longrightarrow B\operatorname{{\rm aut}}_{G}(X).

This fibration sequence, see
[
17
]
, is universal with respect to fibrations with fiber
X
and whose image of the holonomy action is contained in
G
. In other terms, the set

\operatorname{{\cal F}\text{\rm ib}}_{G}(X,B)

(28)

of equivalence classes of fibration sequences
X\to E\to B
over
B
for which the image of the natural map
\pi_{1}(B)\to{\mathcal{E}}(X)
lies in
G
, is in bijective correspondence with the set of (free) homotopy classes
\{B,B\operatorname{{\rm aut}}_{G}(X)\}
.

The counterpart of the above in the pointed setting is the following:

Definition 7.3
.

Given
\pi\subset{\mathcal{E}}^{*}(X)
a subgroup of pointed homotopy classes of pointed equivalences, define

\operatorname{{\rm aut}}_{\pi}^{*}(X)=\{f\in\operatorname{{\rm aut}}^{*}(X),\,\,[f]^{*}\in\pi\}.

Remark 7.4
.

Note that in general, given
G\subset{\mathcal{E}}(X)
and
\pi\subset{\mathcal{E}}^{*}(X)
,
\operatorname{{\rm aut}}_{G}^{*}(X)
and
\operatorname{{\rm aut}}_{\pi}^{*}(X)
are different submonoids of
\operatorname{{\rm aut}}^{*}(X)
. In particular, for the trivial subgroup
G=\{1\}\subset{\mathcal{E}}(X)
,
\operatorname{{\rm aut}}_{1}^{*}(X)
is the non connected monoid of pointed maps freely homotopic to
\operatorname{{\rm id}}_{X}
. However, for
\pi=\{1\}\subset{\mathcal{E}}^{*}(X)
,
\operatorname{{\rm aut}}_{1}^{*}(X)
is the connected monoid of pointed maps based homotopic to
\operatorname{{\rm id}}_{X}
. In what follows, and to avoid confusion, we always make clear whether the considered subgroup is taken from
{\mathcal{E}}(X)
or
{\mathcal{E}}^{*}(X)
.

Recall that a fibration of pointed spaces is
pointed
if it has a section. Then, see for instance
[
17
, Theorem 2.2]
, there exists a “universal” pointed fibration,

X\longrightarrow Z\longrightarrow B\operatorname{{\rm aut}}_{\pi}^{*}(X)

(29)

such that: the set

\operatorname{{\cal F}\text{\rm ib}}_{\pi}^{*}(X,B)

of equivalence classes of pointed fibrations
F\to E\to B
over
B
, with fiber homotopically equivalent to
X
, and for which the image of the natural map
\pi_{1}(B)\to{\mathcal{E}}^{*}(F)
lies in
\pi
, is in bijective correspondence with the set of free homotopy classes
\{B,B\operatorname{{\rm aut}}_{\pi}^{*}(X)\}
.

From this point on, we fix a finite nilpotent complex
X
. We also recall that an action of a group
G
on an abelian group
H
is
nilpotent
if the lower central series of the action,

\Gamma^{0}\supset\dots\supset\Gamma^{i-1}\supset\Gamma^{i}\supset\dots

where
\Gamma^{0}=H
and
\Gamma^{i}
is generated by
\{gh-h,\,g\in G,\,h\in\Gamma^{i-1}\}
, is finite. That is,
\Gamma^{q}=0
for some
q
.

Let first
G
be a subgroup of
{\cal E}(X)
which acts nilpotently on
H_{*}(X)
. Then, see
[
13
, Theorem D]
, both
B\operatorname{{\rm aut}}_{G}(X)
and
B\operatorname{{\rm aut}}_{G}^{*}(X)
are nilpotent spaces and thus
G\cong\pi_{1}B\operatorname{{\rm aut}}_{G}(X)
and
G^{*}\cong\pi_{1}B\operatorname{{\rm aut}}_{G}^{*}(X)
are nilpotent groups. Let

\Gamma^{0}=H_{*}(X)\supset\dots\supset\Gamma^{i-1}\supset\Gamma^{i}\supset\dots\supset\Gamma^{q}=0

be the central series of the
G
-action. That is,
\Gamma^{i}
is generated by
f_{*}(\alpha)-\alpha
, with
[f]\in G
and
\alpha\in\Gamma^{i-1}
.

Definition 7.5
.

Define
K\subset{\mathcal{E}}(X)
as the subgroup of self homotopy equivalences which “stabilize” the above series. That is,

K=\{[f]\in{\mathcal{E}}(X),\,\,\text{$f_{*}$ induce the identity on $\Gamma^{i}/\Gamma^{i+1}$, for all $i$}\,\}.

Accordingly, define

K^{*}=\zeta^{-1}(K)=\{[f]^{*}\in{\mathcal{E}}^{*}(X),\,\,\text{with $[f]\in K$}\}

and note that
G\subset K\subset{\mathcal{E}}(X)
and
G^{*}\subset K^{*}\subset{\mathcal{E}}^{*}(X)
. Observe that, by
[
13
, Theorem D]
, both
K
and
K^{*}
are nilpotent groups.

On the one hand the following straightforward homological reformulation of
[
25
, Theorem 3.3]
characterizes the rationalization of
K
:

Theorem 7.6
.

The rationalization
K_{\mathbb{Q}}
of
K
is the subgroup of
{\cal E}(X_{\mathbb{Q}})
which stabilize the following series,

\Gamma^{0}_{\mathbb{Q}}=H_{*}(X_{\mathbb{Q}})=H_{*}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}\supset\dots\supset\Gamma^{i-1}_{\mathbb{Q}}\supset\Gamma^{i}_{\mathbb{Q}}\supset\dots\supset\Gamma^{q}_{\mathbb{Q}}=0.

(30)

In other terms, a class
[f]
of
{\cal E}(X_{\mathbb{Q}})
is in
K_{\mathbb{Q}}
if it acts trivially on each
\Gamma^{i-1}_{\mathbb{Q}}/\Gamma^{i}_{\mathbb{Q}}
for all
i\geq 0
. Moreover, the map

K\to K_{\mathbb{Q}}
,
[f]\mapsto[f_{\mathbb{Q}}]
,
is the rationalization.
\square

As
G
is a subgroup of
K
, it follows that
G_{\mathbb{Q}}
is a subgroup of
K_{\mathbb{Q}}
and the map
G\to G_{\mathbb{Q}}
,
[f]\mapsto[f_{\mathbb{Q}}]
,
is also the rationalization.

On the other hand, a completely analogous procedure exhibits
K^{*}_{\mathbb{Q}}
containing
G^{*}_{\mathbb{Q}}
and establishes that the map
G^{*}\to G^{*}_{\mathbb{Q}}
,
[f]^{*}\mapsto[f_{\mathbb{Q}}]^{*}
is the rationalization.

Remark 7.7
.

This defines maps of monoids,

\operatorname{{\rm aut}}_{G}(X)\longrightarrow\operatorname{{\rm aut}}_{G_{\mathbb{Q}}}(X_{\mathbb{Q}}),\qquad\operatorname{{\rm aut}}_{G}^{*}(X)\longrightarrow\operatorname{{\rm aut}}_{G_{\mathbb{Q}}}^{*}(X_{\mathbb{Q}}),

both sending a map to its rationalization. Moreover, and this is crucial in the next result, remark that, componentwise, these maps are also the rationalizations as, for each map
f\colon X\to X
, pointed in the second case,

\operatorname{{\rm map}}_{f_{\mathbb{Q}}}(X_{\mathbb{Q}},X_{\mathbb{Q}})\simeq\operatorname{{\rm map}}_{f}(X,X)_{\mathbb{Q}},\quad\operatorname{{\rm map}}_{f_{\mathbb{Q}}}^{*}(X_{\mathbb{Q}},X_{\mathbb{Q}})\simeq\operatorname{{\rm map}}_{f}^{*}(X,X)_{\mathbb{Q}}.

From all of the above, we may easily deduce:

Proposition 7.8
.

Let
X
be a finite nilpotent complex and

Remark 7.19

Although all spaces involved in Theorems

and not in the model category defined in

Lemma 8.1

We begin by proving that

We finish with two examples which cover a wide spectrum. We see how any finitely generated rational group of nilpotency index
n
can be realized as a subgroup of self homotopy equivalences of the rationalization of a suitable finite complex, acting nilpotently on the homology of the complex with the same nilpotency index. The corresponding classifying group is explicitly described in terms of derivations. On the other hand, any such group can also be realized as a subgroup of self homotopy equivalences acting nilpotently on the homotopy groups of the complex with the same nilpotency index.

For that we first recall how to describe in simple terms the exponential and the logarithm in the finitely generated nilpotent case. Denote by
U(n)
the group of
n\times n

strictly triangular matrices
with rational entries, i.e., matrices
(m_{ij})
with
m_{ij}=0
if
i\leq j
. In
U(n)
we consider the Lie bracket given by commutators. On the other hand, denote by
T(n)
the group of
n\times n

unitriangular matrices
also over
\mathbb{Q}
, that is, lower triangular matrices where all entries in the diagonal are 1. Then, any finitely generated nilpotent
0
-local group and any finitely generated nilpotent Lie algebra can be respectively embedded in
U(n)
and
T(n)
so that their logarithm and exponential maps are just the restriction of the classical bijections

\textstyle{U(n)\,\,\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\scriptscriptstyle\cong}
\scriptstyle{\exp}
\textstyle{{\,T(n).}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\log}

Example 9.12
.

Let
M
be a finitely generated nilpotent Lie algebra, with nilpotency index less than or equal to
n
, and concentrated in degree
0
. By the above observation,
M
can be embedded in
T(n)
. Consider the finite nilpotent complex
X=\vee_{j=1}^{n}S^{m}
with
m>1
, whose minimal model is
L=(\mathbb{L}(y_{1},\dots,y_{n}),0)
where
|y_{j}|=m-1
for all
j
. Note that, for degree reasons, any derivation
\theta\in{\rm Der}_{0}L
is necessarily of the form

\theta(y_{j})=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{jk}\,y_{k},\qquad\lambda_{jk}\in\mathbb{Q}.

That is,
\theta
is identified with the matrix
(\lambda_{jk})
considering

\begin{pmatrix}\theta(y_{1})\\
\vdots\\
\theta(y_{n})\end{pmatrix}=\theta\begin{pmatrix}y_{1}\\
\vdots\\
y_{n}\end{pmatrix}.

(48)

Hence,
M
is a sub Lie algebra of
{\rm Der}_{0}(L)
which, being in
T(n)
, it determines a decreasing filtration of length at most
n
of

,

V=V^{0}\supset\dots\supset V^{i}\supset V^{i+1}\supset\dots\supset V^{q}=0,

(49)

where
V^{i}=\theta(V^{i-1})
,
i\geq 1
.

On the other hand, if we denote by
{\mathcal{G}}=\exp(M)
, we may also identify any matrix
\varphi=e^{\theta}\in{\mathcal{G}}
with an automorphism of
L
as in (
48
):

\varphi\colon L\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}L,\qquad\begin{pmatrix}\varphi(y_{1})\\
\vdots\\
\varphi(y_{n})\end{pmatrix}=\varphi\begin{pmatrix}y_{1}\\
\vdots\\
y_{n}\end{pmatrix}.

Note that
\operatorname{{\rm aut}}(L)=\operatorname{{\rm aut}}(L)/\sim
so that
{\mathcal{G}}=\operatorname{{\rm aut}}_{\mathcal{G}}(L)
is identified with the rational subgroup
G
of
{\mathcal{E}}(X_{\mathbb{Q}})
whose action in

produces, as central series, the suspension of the filtration (
49
) of

(see Definitions
7.5
and
7.9
). That is,

acts nilpotently on

.

In other terms,
M={\rm Der}^{\mathcal{G}}_{0}L
and, by Theorem
7.13
, the sequence

L\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\operatorname{{\rm ad}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\rm Der}^{\mathcal{G}}L\longrightarrow{\rm Der}^{\mathcal{G}}L\widetilde{\times}sL

is a Lie model of the
classifying fibration

X_{\mathbb{Q}}\longrightarrow B\operatorname{{\rm aut}}^{*}_{G}(X_{\mathbb{Q}})\longrightarrow B\operatorname{{\rm aut}}_{G}(X_{\mathbb{Q}}).

Proceeding exactly in the converse direction, any nilpotent rational group
G
of
{\mathcal{E}}(X_{\mathbb{Q}})
can be identified with a rational subgroup of
U(n)
and thus, with a subgroup
\mathcal{G}=\operatorname{{\rm aut}}_{\mathcal{G}}(L)
of automorphisms of
L
. In turn, this determines the nilpotent Lie algebra

so that
{\rm Der}^{\mathcal{G}}_{0}L=M
.

Just to illustrate how to proceed in a particular instance, choose for example

M=T(3)=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
\alpha&0&0\\
\beta&\gamma&0\end{pmatrix},\,\,\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{Q}\right\}

and let

, with

. Every

Optimal entanglement enhancing via conditional measurements
Jiru Liu, Yusef Maleki, M. Suhail Zubairy

Abstract
Enhancing quantum entanglement is important for many quantum information processing applications. In this paper, we consider a protocol for entanglement enhancing in a two-mode squeezed vacuum state (TMSVS), attained based on photon subtraction, photon catalysis, and photon addition. Central to such an operation is the task of mixing and detecting number states with each mode of TMSVS. We analyze various settings and find an optimal setup for improving the entanglement of the state.



I. Introduction

Among various entangled states, continuous variable (CV) systems have attracted considerable attention for their remarkable characteristics, and their usefulness in quantum information tasks
Braunstein and Van Loock 2005
;
Lloyd and Braunstein 1999
;
Adesso et al. 2014
;
Lund et al. 2014
;
Duan et al. 2000
. The prominent examples of CV systems include both Gaussian and non-Gaussian states. It is well-known that Gaussian states, such as coherent and squeezed states, offer a considerable platform for quantum applications. In a parallel line of research studies, it has been shown that non-Gaussian states, as well as non-Gaussian operations, can play a significant role in quantum information processing. For example, two-mode non-Gaussian states present an advantage over Gaussian states in enhancing entanglement
Lee et al. 2011
;
Navarrete-Benlloch et al. 2012
;
Walschaers et al. 2017
. This is an interesting property due to the fact that highly entangled states are of particular importance for both practical applications
Horodecki et al. 2009
;
Renner 2008
;
Pirandola et al. 2015
;
Zubairy 2020
;
Maleki 2021
;
Ge et al. 2020
;
Pirandola et al. 2015
;
Zubairy 2020
;
Maleki 2021
;
Ge et al. 2020
;
Barnett 2009
and fundamental investigations in quantum discipline
Barnett 2009
;
Maleki 2021
;
Genovese 2005
. Moreover, non-Gaussian sources are necessary for entanglement distillation since distilling Gaussian states via Gaussian operations is not possible
Giedke and Cirac 2002
;
Fiurášek 2002
.

Non-Gaussian states can be obtained by simply adding or subtracting photons to Gaussian states. Photon addition and photon subtraction are important tools for improving quantum correlations. The physical properties of photon-added and photon-subtracted non-Gaussian states are studied both theoretically and experimentally, in recent years
Agarwal and Tara 1991
;
Ourjoumtsev et al. 2009
;
Walschaers et al. 2017
;
Zavatta et al. 2004
;
Ourjoumtsev et al. 2007
. Photon addition and photon subtraction on squeezing states and coherent state are used for entanglement distillation
Lee et al. 2011
;
Zhang et al. 2013
, quantum commutation applications
Zavatta et al. 2009
;
Parigi et al. 2007
, entanglement and the teleportation fidelity enhancements
Opatrnỳ et al. 2000
;
Walschaers et al. 2017
;
Hu et al. 2017
;
Ourjoumtsev et al. 2007
;
Yang and Li 2009
;
Lee et al. 2011
;
Navarrete-Benlloch et al. 2012
;
Zhang and van

;
Fiurášek 2011
;
Dell′Anno et al. 2010
;
Dell′Anno et al. 2007
.

Photon subtraction is shown to be realized by taking a small fraction out of the light beam
Wenger et al. 2004
;
Opatrnỳ et al. 2000
. Also, photon addition is demonstrated to be attained in parametric down-conversion processes in BBO crystal
Zavatta et al. 2004
. Both experimental methods include conditional measurements. Considering these approaches, a similar non-Gaussian operation, photon catalysis, was also studied by A. I. Lvovsky and J. Mlynek
Lvovsky and Mlynek 2002
. Photon catalysis has been demonstrated to enhance the entanglement of a two-mode squeezed vacuum state
Hu et al. 2017
.

The importance of the non-Gaussian entanglement enhancement recipes that were mentioned earlier becomes more transparent by considering the fact that generating highly entangled states is not an easy task in general and requires a delicate control and design of quantum system
Zubairy 2020
;
Barnett 2009
;
Scully and Zubairy 1997
.
For example, two-mode squeezed vacuum state (TMSVS), can be attained using non-linear crystals
Taylor and Bowen 2016
. However, due to the weak interaction of non-linear processes, the squeezing factor is usually small. To be more precise, the entanglement of a TMSVS is determined by
E_{\mathcal{N}}=\log_{2}e^{2r}
where
E_{\mathcal{N}}
is the logarithmic negativity
Agarwal 2012
, and
r
is the squeezing factor.

In this study, we consider the problem of enhancing the entanglement in TMSVS based on schemes that take advantage of the photon addition, subtraction, and catalysis phenomena.
We first analyze and compare the performance of all three operations
Hu et al. 2017
;
Xu 2015
. As demonstrated in Fig.
1
, an auxiliary Fock state
\ket{m}_{A}
is mixed with one of the modes of a TMSVS via a BS (see Fig.
1
). Using time-multiplexed photon number resolving detector
Mičuda et al. 2008
;
Fitch et al. 2003
, conditional measurement can be performed on the auxiliary mode of the output states, projecting it to
\ket{m^{\prime}}_{A}
. If
m^{\prime}<m
, this process adds photons to the states, while
m^{\prime}>m
subtracts photons from the state. For
m^{\prime}=m
it serves as a catalyst.
The operation can be performed on both modes of the TMSVS as depicted in Fig.
2
, where each mode gets mixed with an independent Fock state.
We find that photon catalysis provides better results for both success probability and entanglement enhancement compared to the photon addition and subtraction processes. However, even for the photon catalysis, the success probability of the states does not exceed 20%.

To overcome this limitation and to attain even considerably higher entanglement enhancement, we introduce a different method compared to previous studies
Lee et al. 2011
;
Navarrete-Benlloch et al. 2012
;
Opatrnỳ et al. 2000
;
Zhang and van
Loock 2011
;
Hu et al. 2017
;
Xu 2015
. We show that by injecting the auxiliary Fock states into a BS before mixing them with the modes of the TMSVS, the entanglement can be drastically improved. With the new setup, we demonstrate that under photon addition, the success probability for a large entanglement enhancement can even exceed 70%. Our investigations show that adding a single photon to the TMSVS, based on this protocol, is, in fact, optimal for entanglement enhancement.

II.
Photon subtraction, addition, and catalysis

Figure 1:
Quantum state
\ket{\psi}_{out}
is generated by mixing the Fock state

Now, we consider a general setting of non-Gaussian operations, where photons can be added, subtracted, or catalyzed in the process.
The protocol for enhancing entanglement is depicted in Fig.
1
, where an input state
\ket{\psi}_{in}
is injected into one port of a BS and the Fock state
\ket{m}
into the other port. The BS has the transmittance
T=\cos^{2}\theta
. The detector in the output mode detects
{m^{\prime}}
photons, which can be realized using a time-multiplexed photon number resolving detector
Mičuda et al. 2008
;
Fitch et al. 2003
.

Based on the configurations in Fig.
1
, there are three possible scenarios: Photon subtraction for
m^{\prime}>m
, photon addition for
m^{\prime}>m
, and photon catalysis
m^{\prime}=m
.
The BS operation can be described as
\hat{B}(\theta)=\exp\{\theta(a^{\dagger}a_{A}-{a_{A}}^{\dagger}a)\}
, where for convenience we take
\theta
to be real and the phase shift of BS is set to be zero. Together with the photon number measurement operation, the output state
\ket{\psi}_{out}
is given by

\ket{\psi}_{out}=_{A}\bra{m^{\prime}}\hat{B}(\theta)\ket{m}_{A}\ket{\psi}_{in},

(1)

where index
A
denotes the auxiliary mode. Note that BS operator consists of two modes and hence
{}_{A}\bra{m^{\prime}}\hat{B}(\theta)\ket{m}_{A}
is an operator acting on the mode that is not measured.
Thus, the transformation from
\ket{\psi}_{in}
to
\ket{\psi}_{out}
is expressed by

\displaystyle\ket{\psi}_{out}

\displaystyle=\hat{B}_{m,m^{\prime}}\ket{\psi}_{in},

(2)

\displaystyle\hat{B}_{m,m^{\prime}}

\displaystyle={}_{A}\bra{m^{\prime}}\hat{B}(\theta)\ket{m}_{A}.

Considering Eq. (
2
), in order to determine the output state, we need to determine
\hat{B}_{m,m^{\prime}}
. As is shown in the Appendix, the operator
\hat{B}_{m,m^{\prime}}
can be expressed in the Fock basis through

\displaystyle\hat{B}_{m,m^{\prime}}=_{A}\bra{m^{\prime}}\hat{B}(\theta)\ket{m}_{A}=\sum_{k=0}B_{m,m^{\prime},k}\ket{k+m-m^{\prime}}\bra{k},

(3)

where the explicit expression for the coefficient
B_{m,m^{\prime},k}
is presented in Eq. (
A3
), in the Appendix.
This provides a rather general framework for non-Gaussian operations on a quantum state.

To consider a particular setting, for the input state
\ket{\psi}_{in}=\sum_{k}c_{k}\ket{k}
, the output state
\ket{\psi}_{out}
can be obtained to be

\ket{\psi}_{out}\propto\hat{B}_{m,m^{\prime}}\ket{\psi}_{in}=\sum_{k=0}c_{k}B_{m,m^{\prime},k}\ket{k+m-m^{\prime}}.

(4)

Note that
\hat{B}_{m,m^{\prime}}\ket{\psi}_{in}
is not normalized in general. Therefore, considering the normalized factor
N_{m,m^{\prime}}
, determined by
N^{-2}_{m,m^{\prime}}=\sum_{k}|c_{k}B_{m,m^{\prime},k}|^{2}
, the output state can be expressed as

\ket{\psi}_{out}=N_{m,m^{\prime}}\sum_{k=0}c_{k}B_{m,m^{\prime},k}\ket{k+m-m^{\prime}},

(5)

As a particular example, for the photon catalysis with
m=m^{\prime}=1
, we find for the output state

\displaystyle\ket{\psi}_{out}

\displaystyle=N_{1,1}\sum_{k=0}c_{k}(\cos\theta)^{k-1}[\cos^{2}\theta-k\sin^{2}\theta]\ket{k},

(6)

which agrees with Refs.
Hu et al. 2017
;
Sanaka et al. 2006
;
Resch et al. 2007
.
Taking into account the fact that
T=\cos^{2}\theta
and
R=\sin^{2}\theta
, the equation above can be expressed as
\ket{\psi}_{out}=N_{1,1}\sum_{k=0}c_{k}\sqrt{T}^{k-1}[T-kR]\ket{k}
. Therefore, for
k=T/R
, the contribution from the Fock state
\ket{k}
becomes eliminated in the output state.
As an specific case, for
\ket{\psi}_{in}=\ket{k}
, we obtain
\ket{\psi}_{out}=0
.
For a given state, if we take
T=R
, for instance, there will be no contribution from the number state
\ket{1}
, in the output. While, for
T=2R
there will be no contribution from the number state
\ket{2}

in the output state, for instance.

III. Entanglement enhancement of the TMSVS

Now that we have developed a platform for desired non-Gaussian operations, we can
implement specific setups for enhancing entanglement in a continuous variable system. Even though the input state introduced in Fig.
1
, is quite general, we apply the quantum enhancement protocols to the two-mode squeezed vacuum state (TMSVS).
The two-mode squeezed vacuum state is defined by applying two-mode squeezing operator
S(r)=\exp\{r(a_{1}^{\dagger}a_{2}^{\dagger}-a_{1}a_{2})\}
to the two-mode vacuum state
\ket{0,0}
as
Hu et al. 2017
;
Sanaka et al. 2006
;
Resch et al. 2007

\displaystyle\ket{\text{TMSVS}}=

\displaystyle S(r)\ket{0,0}=\text{sech}r\sum_{k=0}\lambda^{k}\ket{k,k},

(7)

where
\lambda=\tanh{r}
(for convinence,
r
is set to be real). The operators
a_{1}^{\dagger}(a_{1})
and
a_{2}^{\dagger}(a_{2})
are the creation (annihilation) operators for the two modes.

In particular, we consider two different setups for entanglement enhancements and determine the optimal scenario to achieve the goal.

A. First setup

Figure 2:
The two modes of TMSVS are injected into two BSs separately, generating a two-mode output state
\ket{out}
.

Now, we introduce the first scheme for the enhancement of the entanglement in TMSVS. The setup is presented in Fig.
2
, where each mode goes though the operation that is explained in Fig.
1
. Considering the formalism developed in the previous section, the output state is given by

\displaystyle\ket{out}

\displaystyle=N_{m,m^{\prime}:n,n^{\prime}}\hat{B}_{m,m^{\prime}}(\theta)\hat{B}_{n,n^{\prime}}(\varphi)\ket{\text{TMSVS}}

(8)

\displaystyle=N_{m,m^{\prime}:n,n^{\prime}}\sum_{k=0}C_{m,m^{\prime}:n,n^{\prime},k}\ket{k+m-m^{\prime},k+n-n^{\prime}},

where
C_{m,m^{\prime}:n,n^{\prime},k}
is the coefficient of
\ket{k+m-m^{\prime},k+n-n^{\prime}}
. Note that
N_{m,m^{\prime}:n,n^{\prime}}
is the normalization factor such that
\bra{out}out\rangle=1
.
C_{m,m^{\prime}:n,n^{\prime},k}
, which we denote as
C_{k}
for convenience, can be obtained using the two separate non-Gaussian operations operated on each mode. In this setting, we have
C_{k}=\text{sech}r\ \lambda^{k}\ B_{m,m^{\prime},k}(\theta)\ B_{n,n^{\prime},k}(\varphi)
.

In this operation, the success probability and the entanglement enhancement are the two quantities that determine how useful the operation outcome is.
N^{-2}_{m,m^{\prime}:n,n^{\prime}}
quantifies the success probability of the conditional measurement
m\rightarrow m^{\prime}\ \text{and}\ \ n\rightarrow n^{\prime}
, which is determined by
N^{-2}_{m,m^{\prime}:n,n^{\prime}}=\sum_{k=0}|C_{k}|^{2}
. For quantifying entanglement, we use the logarithmic negativity
E_{\mathcal{N}}
as a measure of entanglement which is given by
Agarwal 2012

E_{\mathcal{N}}(\rho)=\log_{2}||\rho^{T_{A}}||_{1},

(9)

where
||R||_{1}
denotes the trace norm
\text{Tr}\sqrt{R^{\dagger}R}
and
\rho^{T_{A}}
is the partial transpose of the state
\rho
. In Eq. (
8
),
\ket{out}
is in a Schmidt form, for which the logarithmic negativity is given by

E_{\mathcal{N}}=\log_{2}\left[N^{2}_{m,m^{\prime}:n,n^{\prime}}(\sum_{k=0}|C_{k}|)^{2}\right].

(10)

This formulation clearly shows that the entanglement is determined by the coefficient norms
|C_{k}|
. Considering the explicit form of the coefficients as
C_{k}=\text{sech}r\ \lambda^{k}\ B_{m,m^{\prime},k}(\theta)\ B_{n,n^{\prime},k}(\varphi)

it is evident that the characteristics of the beam-splitters and also
m,m^{\prime},n,
and
n^{\prime}
determine the entanglement. In general, the entanglement can be increased or decreased for some specific parameters. However, the interesting scenario is to find the parameter space where the entanglement increases.

Figure 3:
The three operations for
m=1
. (a) and (b) for photon addition. (c) and (d) for photon catalysis. (e) and (f) for photon subtraction. (a),(c) and (e) present the success probability versus the squeezing factor
r
and the transmittance
T=\cos^{2}\theta

The initial entanglement for TMSVS, whose Schmidt form is shown by Eq. (
7
), can be calculated from Eq. (
10
) that results in
E_{\mathcal{N}}(\text{TMSVS})=2r\log_{2}e.
Therefore, the entanglement change is determined by

\Delta E_{\mathcal{N}}=\log_{2}\left[N^{2}_{m,m^{\prime}:n,n^{\prime}}(\sum_{k=0}|C_{k}|)^{2}\right]-2r\log_{2}e.

(11)

This relation is central to the investigation of the entanglement improvement by the protocol. In fact, if
E_{\mathcal{N}}
is positive, then we can conclude that the entanglement has increased. To find the parameter space in which entanglement can be enhanced, we consider various settings and identify the cases that entanglement can be enhanced more efficiently. To be more specific, we compare the enhancement for the photon addition, subtraction and catalysis
and determine which operation can increase the entanglement better.

In some of the recent studies, photon subtraction (addition) are implemented by directly applying an annihilation (creation) operator to the input state, the theoretical analysis are based on the normalized state
\hat{a}\ket{\psi}_{in}
,
\hat{a}^{\dagger}\ket{\psi}_{in}
and the coherent superposition
(t\hat{a}+r\hat{a}^{\dagger})\ket{\psi}_{in}

Lee et al. 2011
;
Lee and Nha 2010
;
Agarwal and Tara 1991

Biswas and Agarwal 2007

\ket{\psi}_{out}
given by Eq. (
5
).

We plot the outcome for the three operations for the case with
m=1
in Fig.

Photon catalysis with

Therefore, our study unifies the previous consideration in the literature in a comparative setting and shows how various proposals can be compared with each other. B. Second setup

Now, we introduce a method for increasing entanglement that is different from the one depicted in Fig.
2
. We already considered the three non-Gaussian operations on the TMSVS where each mode undergoes a separate operation, using the product Fock states
\ket{m}\otimes\ket{n}
as auxiliary states. Among these schemes, we found that photon catalysis has the best success probability as well as entanglement improvement compared to photon addition and subtraction. However, according to Figs.
3
(c)
and
3
(d)
, for a suitable
r
and
T
, the region which corresponds to a considerable entanglement enhancement has a moderately low success probability that is approximately less than 20%.

Figure 4:
A similar setting to Fig.
2
, but the auxiliary states are first mixed via
BS_{A}
, before the operations are carried out on TMSVS.

We show that a slight adjustment in the setup can drastically enhance both the entanglement and the success probability. This adjustment requires an extra BS (
BS_{A}
), as is shown in Fig.
4
. In this setting, the auxiliary input states for each modes first undergo
BS_{A}
before the operation of Fig.
2
is performed. This process can entangle the two auxiliary modes, and finally apply the non-Gaussian operations to the modes of TMSVS
Biagi et al. 2020
;
Ourjoumtsev et al. 2007
. Since this extra step is unitary, no photon is lost in this process. This extra step is not challenging to implement in the experiment due to the simplicity of BS operations in general. Therefore, any improvement attained in the process can be useful in a practical setting.

In Fig.
4
, we simplify the auxiliary input source to one single photon state (
m=1,n=0
). Once
\ket{\psi}_{A}
is mixed with the TMSVS via
BS(\theta)
, the conditional detection of
\ket{00}_{A}
(
m^{\prime}=0,n^{\prime}=0
) adds one photon to the TMSVS.

For
m=1,n=0
, before
BS_{A}
, the entire state is

\displaystyle\ket{\psi}_{i}

\displaystyle=\text{sech}r\sum_{k=0}\lambda^{k}\ket{k}_{U}\ket{k}_{L}\ket{1}_{UA}\ket{0}_{LA}

(12)

\displaystyle=\text{sech}r\sum_{k=0}\lambda^{k}\ a_{UA}^{\dagger}\ \ket{kk}\ket{00}_{A}

\displaystyle=\text{sech}r\sum_{k=0}\frac{\lambda^{k}}{k!}\ (a_{U}^{\dagger})^{k}\ (a_{UA}^{\dagger})\ (a_{L}^{\dagger})^{k}\ \ket{00}\ket{00}_{A},

where the creation operator of the upper TMSVS mode is denoted as
a^{\dagger}_{U}
while the lower one is denoted as
a^{\dagger}_{L}
. Moreover, the creation operator for the upper (shown in green) path of the ancillary state in Fig.
4
is denoted as
a^{\dagger}_{UA}
while the lower (shown in red) one is
a^{\dagger}_{LA}
. In the second step of above equations,
\ket{ij}_{A}
stands for
\ket{i}_{UA}\ket{j}_{LA}
, and
\ket{ij}
is simply the basis for the two modes of TMSVS,
\ket{i}_{U}\ket{j}_{L}
.

After passing through BS
A
, we attain
\ket{\psi}_{1}
. This state, when it passes through BS
U
and BS
L
, degenerates to
\ket{\psi}_{2}
, where,
\ket{\psi}_{1}
and
\ket{\psi}_{2}
are given by

\displaystyle\ket{\psi}_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\text{sech}r\sum_{k=0}\frac{\lambda^{k}}{k!}\ (a_{U}^{\dagger})^{k}\ (a_{UA}^{\dagger}+a_{LA}^{\dagger})\ (a_{L}^{\dagger})^{k}\ \ket{00}\ket{00}_{A},

(13)

\displaystyle\ket{\psi}_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\text{sech}r\sum_{k=0}\frac{\lambda^{k}}{k!}\ (b_{U}^{\dagger})^{k}\ (b_{UA}^{\dagger}+b_{LA}^{\dagger})\ (b_{L}^{\dagger})^{k}\ \ket{00}\ket{00}_{A}.

Here,
b^{\dagger}=\cos\theta\ a^{\dagger}+\sin\theta\ a_{A}^{\dagger}
and
\ b_{A}^{\dagger}=-\sin\theta\ a^{\dagger}+\cos\theta\ a_{A}^{\dagger}
. Note that
b^{\dagger}
denotes
b^{\dagger}_{U}
or
b^{\dagger}_{L}
while
b^{\dagger}_{A}
denotes
b^{\dagger}_{UA}
or
b^{\dagger}_{LA}
.

In principle, detecting
m^{\prime}=0,n^{\prime}=0
leads to one photon addition to the TMSVS. Substituting
b_{L}^{\dagger},\ b^{\dagger}_{U}
and
b^{\dagger}_{UA},\ b^{\dagger}_{LA}
into the expression of
\ket{\psi}_{2}
, it follows that

\displaystyle\ket{\psi}_{f}=N\ _{A}\braket{00|\psi}_{2}

(14)

\displaystyle=N\frac{\text{sech}r}{\sqrt{2}}\sum_{k=0}\frac{\lambda^{k}}{k!}\ (\cos\theta a^{\dagger}_{U})^{k}\ (\cos\theta a^{\dagger}_{L})^{k}\ (-\sin\theta a^{\dagger}_{U}-\sin\theta a^{\dagger}_{L})\ket{00}

\displaystyle=N\frac{\text{sech}r}{\sqrt{2}}\sum_{k=0}(\lambda\cos^{2}\theta)^{k}\ (-\sin\theta a^{\dagger}_{U}-\sin\theta a^{\dagger}_{L})\ket{kk}

\displaystyle=-N\frac{\text{sech}r}{\sqrt{2}}\sum_{k=0}(\lambda\cos^{2}\theta)^{k}\sin\theta\sqrt{k+1}\ \bigg[\ket{k+1,k}+\ket{k,k+1}\bigg],

where
N
is the normalized factor.

Up to an unimportant global phase, and for
\theta\neq 0
, the state at the output can be expressed as

\displaystyle\ket{\psi}_{f}=(1-\lambda^{2}\cos^{4}\theta)\sum_{k=0}\big[(\lambda\cos^{2}\theta)^{k}\sqrt{k+1}\big]\ket{\phi}_{k},

(15)

where
\ket{\phi}_{k}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\ket{k+1,k}+\ket{k,k+1})
. Alternatively, we can write the state as
\ket{\psi}_{f}=\sum_{k=0}\sqrt{p_{k}}\ket{\phi}_{k}
, in which
p_{k}
is the probability of having the state
\ket{\phi}_{k}
given by

p_{k}=({1-\lambda^{2}\cos^{4}\theta})^{2}(\lambda\cos^{2}\theta)^{2k}({k+1})
. The state
\ket{\phi}_{k}
is a maximally entangled state with any
k
. Therefore, the output state
\ket{\psi}_{f}
reduces to superposition of different maximally entangled states with the probability determined by
p_{k}
. An interesting observation is to find which entangled state (
\ket{\phi}_{k}
) is most probable given the probability distribution
p_{k}
. To this end, one can easily find
p_{k+1}/p_{k}=(\lambda^{2}\cos^{4}\theta)(k+2)/(k+1)
. When the squeezing factor
r
is small enough (
\lambda=\text{tanh}r
), we have
p_{k+1}/p_{k}<1
. Therefore, the most probable state can be attained for
k=0
, i.e.,
\ket{\phi}_{0}=(\ket{1,0}+\ket{0,1})/\sqrt{2}
. However, for the case when
2\lambda^{2}\cos^{4}\theta>1
, one can determine the peak in the probability distribution by setting
p_{k+1}=p_{k}
. This gives
k=[1/(1-\lambda^{2}\cos^{4}\theta)]-2
.

The output state for photon catalysis and photon subtraction can be obtained by applying
{}_{A}\bra{10}
(or
{}_{A}\bra{01}
) and
{}_{A}\bra{11}
to
\ket{\psi}_{2}
, respectively.

The success probability as well as the entanglement enhancement can be calculated from Eq. (
14
). It is worth to mention that, for
\ket{\psi}_{2}
in the last step of Eq. (
14
), we apply Schmit decomposition to
\ket{\psi}_{f}
to get the singular eigenvalue
C_{k}
, then use Eqs. (
10
)-(
11
) to obtain the entanglement enhancement.

Beside the photon detection error, the other imperfection may be accumulated from the BSs. In this work, there is no need for high number of BSs, and thus, the imperfections cannot impair the performance of the protocols.

IV. Summary and conclusion

In this work, we considered a protocol for entanglement enhancing in a TMSVS based on photon subtraction, photon catalysis, and photon addition. Central to such an operation is the task of mixing and detecting number states with each mode of TMSVS and non-deterministic detection. We analyzed various settings for the improvement of quantum entanglement and found an optimal setup for enhancing the entanglement of the state.
To be more specific, we considered two different schemes for enhancing entanglement. In the first scheme each mode interacts with a Fock state in a beam-splitter. While, in the second scheme Fock states undergo a beam-splitter before interacting with the modes of TMSVS.

In the first scheme, photon catalysis outperforms both photon addition and subtraction operations. Nonetheless, the improvement and the success probability remain rather low even for photon catalysis. Of course, entanglement, in this case, can be improved more by using higher-number Fock states for resources. However, generating as well as detecting a high-number Fock state
\ket{m}
is quite challenging.

In contrary to the first scheme, it turns out that the optimal performance of the second setup can be achieved with simply using single photon sources. This, in fact, simplifies both photon generation and detection processes from the practical point of view. The optimal scenario presented in this work can drastically outperform all the settings of the first scheme, with more than three fold improvement of the entanglement.

Quantum entanglement improvement based on non-Gaussian operations are investigated from various perspectives, in recent years
Agarwal and Tara 1991
;
Ourjoumtsev et al. 2009
;
Walschaers et al. 2017
;
Zavatta et al. 2004
;
Ourjoumtsev et al. 2007
;
Lvovsky and Mlynek 2002
. Such operations are shown to be useful for entanglement distillation
Lee et al. 2011
;
Zhang et al. 2013
, quantum commutation
Zavatta et al. 2009
;
Parigi et al. 2007
and quantum teleportation
Opatrnỳ et al. 2000
;
Walschaers et al. 2017
;
Hu et al. 2017
;
Ourjoumtsev et al. 2007
.
Therefore, the optimal entanglement enhancement proposed here can provide an important tool for such applications.

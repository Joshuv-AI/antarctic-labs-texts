The tight bound for the strong chromatic indices of claw-free subcubic graphs Thanks: Supported by NSFC 11771080.
Yuquan Lin, Wensong Lin

Abstract
Let G G be a graph and k k a positive integer. A strong k k -edge-coloring of G G is a mapping ϕ : E ⁡ ( G ) → { 1 , 2 , … , k } \phi:E(G)\to\{1,2,\dots,k\} such that for any two edges e e and e ′ e^{\prime} that are either adjacent to each other or adjacent to a common edge, ϕ ⁡ ( e ) ≠ ϕ ⁡ ( e ′ ) \phi(e)\neq\phi(e^{\prime}) . The strong chromatic index of G G , denoted as χ s ′ ​ ( G ) \chi^{\prime}_{s}(G) , is the minimum integer k k such that G G has a strong k k -edge-coloring. Lv, Li and Zhang [Graphs and Combinatorics 38 (3) (2022) 63] proved that if G G is a claw-free subcubic graph other than the triangular prism then χ s ′ ​ ( G ) ≤ 8 \chi_{s}^{\prime}(G)\leq 8 . In addition, they asked if the upper bound 8 8 can be improved to 7 7 . In this paper, we answer this question in the affirmative. Our proof implies a linear-time algorithm for finding strong 7 7 -edge-colorings of such graphs. We also construct infinitely many claw-free subcubic graphs with their strong chromatic indices attaining the bound 7 7 . Keywords: strong edge coloring; strong chromatic index; claw-free; subcubic graph.

Introduction

Let
G=(V(G),E(G))
be a finite undirected simple graph.
For
v\in V(G)
, let
N(v)=\{u\in V(G):uv\in E(G)\}
denote the open neighborhood of
v
and
d(v)=|N(v)|
be the degree of
v
. Let
\Delta(G)=\max\limits_{v\in V(G)}d(v)
denote the maximum degree of
G
.
For convenience, we use the abbreviation
[1,n]
for
\{1,2,\dots,n\}
, where
n
is any positive integer.

Let
e
and
e^{\prime}
be two edges of
G
. If
e
and
e^{\prime}
are adjacent to each other, we say that the distance between
e
and
e^{\prime}
is
1
, and if they are not adjacent but both of them are adjacent to a common edge, we say they are at distance
2
. Given a positive integer
k
, a
strong
k
-edge-coloring
of
G
is a mapping
\phi:E(G)\to[1,k]
such that for any two edges
e
and
e^{\prime}
that are at distance
1
or
2
,
\phi(e)\neq\phi(e^{\prime})
. The
strong chromatic index
of
G
, denoted by
\chi^{\prime}_{s}(G)
, is the minimum integer
k
such that
G
has a strong
k
-edge-coloring.

The concept of strong edge coloring, first introduced by Fouquet and Jolivet
[
10
]
, can be used to model the conflict-free channel assignment in radio networks
[
21
,
20
]
. In 1985, Erdős and Nešetřil
[
7
,
8
]
proposed the following conjecture about the upper bound of
\chi^{\prime}_{s}(G)
in term of
\Delta(G)
, which if true, is the best possible.

Conjecture 1.1

(Erdős and Nešetřil
[
7
,
8
]
)
If
G
is a graph with maximum degree
\Delta(G)
, then

\chi^{\prime}_{s}(G)\leq\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{cl}\dfrac{5}{4}\Delta(G)^{2},&\text{if}\ \Delta(G)\ \text{is even,}\\
\dfrac{5}{4}\Delta(G)^{2}-\dfrac{1}{2}\Delta(G)+\dfrac{1}{4},&\text{if}\ \Delta(G)\ \text{is odd.}\end{array}\end{cases}

The conjecture is clearly true for
\Delta(G)\leq 2
. The case
\Delta(G)=3
was verified by Andersen
[
1
]
in 1992, and independently by Horák, Qing, and Trotter
[
13
]
in 1993. Furthermore, if
G
is a subcubic planar graph then
\chi^{\prime}_{s}(G)\leq 9

[
16
]
. For
\Delta(G)=4
, an upper bound of
23
was proved by Horák
[
12
]
in 1990. It was improved to
22
by Cranston
[
5
]
in 2006 and more recently Huang, Santana and Yu
[
14
]
obtained the upper bound
21
. Recall that the conjectured bound is
20
for
\Delta(G)=4
. Although the general case when
\Delta(G)=4
has not been solved yet, the bound
20
holds for some special cases. In 1990, Faudree, Schelp, Gyárfás and Tuza
[
9

proved that
\chi^{\prime}_{s}(G)\leq 4\Delta(G)+4
for any planar graph

, which implies that Conjecture
1.1
holds for planar graphs with maximum degree
4
. And this bound for planar graphs with maximum degree
4
was further improved to
19
by Wang, Shiu, Wang and Chen
[
22
]
in 2018.

In 2015, Bensmail, Bonamy and Hocquard
[
2

studied the upper bound on
\chi^{\prime}_{s}(G)
in terms of maximum average degree for the class of graphs with maximum degree
4

[

strengthened Bensmail, Bonamy and Hocquard’s results. And they proved that for any graph

\Delta(G)=4

3

4
, then
\chi^{\prime}_{s}(G)\leq 20
.

For graphs with maximum degree
5
, Zang
[
24
]
gave the upper bound 37 (recall that the conjectured bound is 29), which is the only progress as we know. And for larger
\Delta(G)
, the problem is widely open. In 1997, Molloy and Reed
[
19

used probabilistic techniques to prove that
\chi^{\prime}_{s}(G)\leq 1.998\Delta(G)^{2}
for sufficiently large
\Delta(G)
. And an improvement to

[
4

in 2015. Recently, Bonamy, Perrett and Postle
[

which was showed by Hurley, de Joannis de Verclos and Kang
[

A graph is called

if it has no induced subgraph isomorphic to the complete bipartite graph

. In 2020, Debski, Junosza-Szaniawski and Śleszyńska-Nowak
[

Theorem 1.2

(Debski, Junosza-Szaniawski and Śleszyńska-Nowak
[
6
]

For any claw-free graph
G

\chi^{\prime}_{s}(G)\leq\frac{9}{8}\Delta(G)^{2}+\Delta(G)
.

A graph with maximum degree less than or equal to

Theorem 1.3

Let
G
be a claw-free subcubic graph. If each component of

\chi^{\prime}_{s}(G)\leq 7
.

The remainder of this paper is dedicated to the proof of Theorem

which is organized as follows. In Section 2, after stating some definitions and notations, we explain how to order the edges of a connected claw-free subcubic graph and apply the greedy algorithm to obtain a partial strong

2
Preliminaries and notations

Let
G
be a connected claw-free subcubic graph not isomorphic to the
3
-prism. To prove Theorem
1.3
, we shall first find a subgraph of
G
and construct a strong
7
-edge-coloring of this subgraph, and then try to extend the coloring to the whole graph
G
. The subgraph we find should have the property that any two of its edges at distance greater than
2
must also be at distance greater than
2
in
G
. We next introduce some notations and preliminary facts that we will use in our proofs.

We use
\alpha
,
\beta
,
\gamma
to denote colors and
\phi
,
\psi
,
\sigma
to denote edge colorings. Given two distinct edges
e
and
e^{\prime}
of
G
, we say that
e

sees

e^{\prime}
in
G
if they are distance
1
or
2
apart. An edge coloring of a graph
G
is
good
, if it is a strong edge coloring of
G
using at most
7
colors. A
good partial coloring
of a graph
G
is a good coloring
\phi
of some subgraph
H
of
G
such that
\phi(e)\neq\phi(e^{\prime})
if
e
and
e^{\prime}
see each other in
G
.

Let
\phi
be a good partial coloring of
G
. We say that
e

sees
a color
\alpha
in
\phi
, if
e
sees an edge
e^{\prime}
for which
\phi(e)=\alpha
. For
e\in E(G)
, let
F_{\phi}(e)
denote the set of colors that
e
sees in
\phi
. In addition, let
\bar{E}_{\phi}
denote the set of edges in
G
not already assigned colors by
\phi
and
\bar{G}_{\phi}
denote the subgraph of
G
induced by
\bar{E}_{\phi}
. For
e\in\bar{E}_{\phi}
, let
A_{\phi}(e)
denote the set of colors that
e
does not see in
\phi
. It is clear that
A_{\phi}(e)=[1,7]\setminus F_{\phi}(e)
for any
e\in\bar{E}_{\phi}
.

In order to construct a good partial coloring of a claw-free subcubic graph
G
, we first apply the similar method in
[
23
]
to order the edges of
G
, and then use
the greedy algorithm
to color them in the order, left only a few particular edges uncolored.

Suppose
S
is a nonempty subset of
V(G)
. For a vertex
v\in V(G)
, the distance from
v
to
S
, denoted by
d_{S}(v)
, is equal to
\min\limits_{w\in S}\{d(v,w)\}
, where
d(v,w)
is the distance between
v
and
w
in
G
. Let
I
be the maximum distance from a vertex of
G
to
S
. For
i=0,1,\dots,I
, let
D_{i}=\{v\in V(G):\ d(v,S)=i\}
. A mapping
d_{S}
from
E(G)
to nonnegative real numbers is defined as: for any edge
e
with two end vertices
u
and
v
,
d_{S}(e)=\frac{1}{2}(d_{S}(u)+d_{S}(v))
. Suppose
R=(e_{k_{1}},e_{k_{2}},\dots,e_{k_{m}})
is an ordering of the edges of
G
. For any two integers
i
and
j
in
[1,m]
, if
i<j
implies
d_{S}(e_{k_{i}})\geq d_{S}(e_{k_{j}})
, then we say that the edge ordering
R
of
G
is
compatible
with the mapping
d_{S}
. It is clear that any edge joins two vertices that are either in the same
D_{i}
, or one in
D_{i}
and the other in
D_{i+1}
for some
i
, and that each vertex in
D_{i}
with
i\geq 1
is adjacent to at least one vertex in
D_{i-1}
. Let
e=xy
be an edge with
d_{S}(x)\leq d_{S}(y)
. If
d_{S}(e)\geq 1
, then
e
is adjacent to an edge
e^{\prime}=xz
with
d_{S}(z)=d_{S}(x)-1
.

We are now ready to produce a good partial coloring of a claw-free subcubic graph.

Lemma 2.1

Let
G
be a connected claw-free subcubic graph other than the
3
-prism and
S
a nonempty subset of
V(G)
. The greedy algorithm, coloring the edges of
G
in an order
R
that is compatible with the mapping
d_{S}
, will produce a good partial coloring of
G
with only edges in
\{e\in E(G):d_{S}(e)<1\}
being left uncolored.

Proof.

Let
e=xy
be an edge with
d_{S}(e)\geq 1
. Suppose
d_{S}(x)\leq d_{S}(y)
. Let
e^{\prime}=xz
be a neighbor of
e
with
d_{S}(z)=d_{S}(x)-1
. It is clear that all edges incident to the vertex
z
are behind the edge
e
in the order
R
. And so, when
e
is going to be colored by the greedy algorithm, all edges incident to
z
are not colored.

If
d(x)=2
or
d(x)=3
and the third neighbor
w
of
x
satisfies
d_{S}(w)=d_{S}(x)-1
, then
e
sees at most
5
colors at that moment, implying that
e
can be colored properly. Thus we assume that
d(x)=3
and the third neighbor
w
of
x
satisfies
d_{S}(w)\geq d_{S}(x)
.

If
d_{S}(y)=d_{S}(x)\geq 1
, then
y
has a neighbor
y^{\prime}
with
d_{S}(y^{\prime})=d_{S}(z)
(It is possible that
z=y^{\prime}
). As
G
is a claw-free subcubic graph, it is easy to check that
e
sees at most
6
colors (please refer to Figure
4
(a) and (b)). Thus
e
can be colored properly.

Figure 4:
The illustrations of Lemma
2.1

If
d_{S}(y)=d_{S}(x)+1\geq 2
, then
d_{S}(w)=d_{S}(x)
or
d_{S}(w)=d_{S}(x)+1
. Again, because
G
is a claw-free subcubic graph,
e
sees at most
6
colors (please refer to Figure
4
(c) and (d)) and so
e
can be colored properly. Therefore, the lemma holds.

In the proof of Theorem
1.3
, we will often choose some subset
S
of
V(G)
. With this subset
S
, by Lemma
2.1
, we can obtain a good partial coloring
\phi
of
G
with only edges

with

being uncolored. And then our task is to extend
\phi
to a good coloring of the whole graph

There are two main techniques in our proofs, one is using Hall’s theorem
[
11
]
, the other is the modified caterpillar tree method which is slightly different from the caterpillar tree method used by Horák, Qing and Trotter
[
13
]
in proving the upper bound
10
for the strong chromatic indices of cubic graphs.

By Hall’s theorem
[
11
]
,
\{A_{\phi}(e):e\in\bar{E}_{\phi}\}
has a system of distinct representatives (abbreviated SDR) if and only if
|\cup_{e\in M}A_{\phi}(e)|\geq|M|
, for every
M\subseteq\bar{E}_{\phi}
. Whenever
\{A_{\phi}(e):e\in\bar{E}_{\phi}\}
has a SDR,
\phi
can be easily extended to a good coloring of
G
. In this situation, we will say that we can obtain a good coloring of
G
by SDR.

If

also sees
\alpha
in

4
Final Remarks

Let
G
be any connected claw-free subcubic graph not isomorphic to the triangular prism.
This paper proves that
\chi^{\prime}_{s}(G)\leq 7
. And this upper bound
7
is sharp.
In addition, our proof implies a linear-time
algorithm for finding a strong
7
-edge-coloring for such a graph.

If
G
is a connected claw-free cubic graph not isomorphic to the triangular prism, then it is easy to see that
\chi^{\prime}_{s}(G)\geq 6
. Therefore, for such graph
G
,
\chi^{\prime}_{s}(G)\in\{6,7\}
. Let
H
be a connected cubic graph. Denote by
H^{\Delta}
the graph obtained from
H
by replacing each vertex with a
3
-cycle. It is clear that
H^{\Delta}
is a connected claw-free cubic graph.

We end this paper by asking the following three questions.

Question 1
: Let
k\geq 3
be an integer and
H
the
k
-prism. Is it true that
\chi^{\prime}_{s}(H^{\Delta})=6
?

Question 2
: Is it possible to characterize all connected claw-free cubic graphs
G
with
\chi^{\prime}_{s}(G)=6
?

Question 3
: Is the strong list-chromatic index of any claw-free subcubic graph other than the triangular prism at most 7?

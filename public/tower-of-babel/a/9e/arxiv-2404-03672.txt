On self-similar solutions of a multi-phase Stefan problem in the half-line
E.Yu. Panov

Abstract
We study self-similar solutions of a multi-phase Stefan problem for a heat equation on the half-line x > 0 x>0 with a constant initial data and with Dirichlet or Neumann boundary conditions. In the case of Dirichlet boundary condition we prove that a nonlinear algebraic system for determination of the free boundaries is gradient one and the corresponding potential is an explicitly written strictly convex and coercive function. Therefore, there exists a unique minimum point of the potential, coordinates of this point determine free boundaries and provide the desired solution. This result is also extended to the case of infinitely many phase transitions. In the case of Neumann boundary condition we demonstrate that the problem may have solutions with different numbers (called types) of phase transitions. For each fixed type n n the system for determination of the free boundaries is again gradient and the corresponding potential is proved to be strictly convex and coercive, but in some wider non-physical domain. On the base of these properties it is proved that there exists a unique solution of Stefan-Neumann problem, and we also provide precise conditions to specify the type of the solution. Stefan problem with Dirichlet boundary condition

In a quarter-plane
\Pi_{+}=\{\ (t,x)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\ |\ t,x>0\ \}
we consider the multi-phase Stefan problem for the heat equation

u_{t}=a_{i}^{2}u_{xx},\quad u_{i}<u<u_{i+1},\ i=0,\ldots,m,

(1.1)

where
u_{0}\leq u_{1}<\cdots<u_{m}<u_{m+1}=u_{D}
,
u_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,m
, being the temperatures of phase transitions,
a_{i}>0
,

i=0,\ldots,m
, are the diffusivity constants. We will study continuous piecewise smooth solutions
u=u(t,x)
in
\Pi_{+}
satisfying (
1.1
) in the classical sense in the domains
u_{i}<u(t,x)<u_{i+1}
,
i=0,\ldots,m
, filled with the phases. On the unknown lines
x=x_{i}(t)
of phase transitions where
u=u_{i}
the following Stefan condition

d_{i}x_{i}^{\prime}(t)+k_{i}u_{x}(t,x_{i}(t)+)-k_{i-1}u_{x}(t,x_{i}(t)-)=0

(1.2)

is postulated, where
k_{i}>0
is the thermal conductivity of the
i
-th phase, while
d_{i}\geq 0
is the Stefan number (the latent specific heat) for the
i
-th phase transition. In (
1.2
) the unilateral limits
u_{x}(t,x_{i}(t)+)
,
u_{x}(t,x_{i}(t)-)
on the line
x=x_{i}(t)
are taken from the domain corresponding to the warmer/colder phase, respectively. For physical reasons,
the Stefan numbers
d_{i}
should be positive. We will study an even more general case
d_{i}\geq 0
, assuming that
d_{1}>0
if
u_{0}=u_{1}
.

In this case the problem (
1.1
), (
1.2
) is well-posed for
u_{0}\leq u\leq u_{D}
and reduces to a degenerate nonlinear diffusion equation (see
[
2
]
,
[
3
, Chapter 5]
)

\beta(u)_{t}-\alpha(u)_{xx}=0,

(1.3)

where
\alpha(u)
,
\beta(u)
are strictly increasing functions on
[u_{0},u_{D}]
linear on each interval
(u_{i},u_{i+1})
,

i=0,\ldots,m
, with slopes
\alpha^{\prime}(u)=k_{i}
,
\beta^{\prime}(u)=k_{i}/a_{i}^{2}
, and such that

\alpha(u_{i}+)-\alpha(u_{i}-)=0,\quad\beta(u_{i}+)-\beta(u_{i}-)=d_{i},\ i=1,\ldots,m

(here we agree that
\alpha(u_{1}-)=\alpha(u_{0})
,
\beta(u_{1}-)=\beta(u_{0})
in the case
u_{1}=u_{0}
).

We will study the initial-boundary value problem with constant initial and Dirichlet boundary data

u(0,x)=u_{0}\ \forall x>0,\quad u(t,0)=u_{D}\ \forall t>0.

(1.4)

By the invariance of our problem under the transformation group

(t,x)\to(\lambda^{2}t,\lambda x)
,
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
\lambda>0
, it is natural to seek a self-similar solution of problem (
1.1
), (
1.2
), (
1.4
), which has the form
u(t,x)=u(\xi)
,
\xi=x/\sqrt{t}
. In view of (
1.4
),

u(0)=u_{D},\quad u(+\infty)\doteq\lim_{\xi\to+\infty}u(\xi)=u_{0}<u_{D}.

Thus, it is natural to suppose that the function
u(\xi)
decreases. The case when
u_{D}\leq u_{0}
can be treated similarly.
Certainly, in this case the function
u(\xi)
should be increasing.

For the heat equation

u_{t}=a^{2}u_{xx}
a self-similar solution must satisfy the linear ODE
a^{2}u^{\prime\prime}=-\xi u^{\prime}/2
, the general solution of which is

u=C_{1}F(\xi/a)+C_{2},\ C_{1},C_{2}=\mathrm{const},\mbox{ where }F(\xi)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{\xi}e^{-s^{2}/4}ds.

This allows to write our solution in the form

\displaystyle u(\xi)=u_{i}+\frac{u_{i+1}-u_{i}}{F(\xi_{i+1}/a_{i})-F(\xi_{i}/a_{i})}(F(\xi/a_{i})-F(\xi_{i}/a_{i})),

(1.5)

\displaystyle\xi_{i+1}<\xi<\xi_{i},\ i=0,\ldots,m,

where
+\infty=\xi_{0}>\xi_{1}>\cdots>\xi_{m}>\xi_{m+1}=0
and we agree that
\displaystyle F(+\infty)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-s^{2}/4}ds=1
.
Notice that the function
u(\xi)
is constant on the interval
(\xi_{1},+\infty)
if
u_{0}=u_{1}
.
The parabolas
\xi=\xi_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,m
, where
u=u_{i}
, are free boundaries, which must be determined by conditions (
1.2
).
In the variable
\xi
these conditions have the form (cf.
[
1
, Chapter XI]
)

d_{i}\xi_{i}/2+\frac{k_{i}(u_{i+1}-u_{i})F^{\prime}(\xi_{i}/a_{i})}{a_{i}(F(\xi_{i+1}/a_{i})-F(\xi_{i}/a_{i}))}-\frac{k_{i-1}(u_{i}-u_{i-1})F^{\prime}(\xi_{i}/a_{i-1})}{a_{i-1}(F(\xi_{i}/a_{i-1})-F(\xi_{i-1}/a_{i-1}))}=0,

(1.6)

i=1,\ldots,m
. Formally, we can consider solution (
1.5
) also in the case
u_{m}=u_{m+1}
when
u(\xi)\equiv u_{D}
for

0<\xi<\xi_{m}
but in this case condition (
1.6
) with
i=m
reduces to the relation

d_{m}\xi_{m}/2-\frac{k_{m-1}(u_{m}-u_{m-1})F^{\prime}(\xi_{m}/a_{m-1})}{a_{m-1}(F(\xi_{m}/a_{m-1})-F(\xi_{m-1}/a_{m-1}))}=0,

which is impossible since the left-hand side of this relation is strictly positive. This is the reason why we exclude the case
u_{m}=u_{m+1}
in our setting. On the contrary, the case
u_{1}=u_{0}
is correct and does not cause any problems if
d_{1}>0
because in this case (
1.6
) with
i=1
provides the relation

d_{1}\xi_{1}/2+\frac{k_{1}(u_{2}-u_{1})F^{\prime}(\xi_{1}/a_{1})}{a_{1}(F(\xi_{2}/a_{1})-F(\xi_{1}/a_{1}))}=0,

containing the terms of opposite signs.

In the case
m=1
system (
1.6
) reduces to a single equation, which can be easily analysed. As a result, we obtain the classical Neumann solution of the Stefan problem.
To investigate the nonlinear system (
1.6
) in general case of arbitrary number
m
of phase transitions, we notice that it is a gradient one and coincides with the equality

\nabla E(\bar{\xi})=0
, where the function

\displaystyle E(\bar{\xi})=-\sum_{i=0}^{m}k_{i}(u_{i+1}-u_{i})\ln(F(\xi_{i}/a_{i})-F(\xi_{i+1}/a_{i}))+\sum_{i=1}^{m}d_{i}\xi_{i}^{2}/4,

(1.7)

\displaystyle\bar{\xi}=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{m})\in\Omega,

the open convex domain
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
is given by the inequalities
\xi_{1}>\cdots>\xi_{m}>0
.
Observe that
E(\bar{\xi})\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Since the function
F(\xi)
takes values in the interval
(0,1)
, all the terms in expression (
1.7
) are nonnegative while some of them are strictly positive. Therefore,
E(\bar{\xi})>0
.

1.1
Coercivity of the function
E
and existence of a solution

Let us introduce the sub-level sets

\Omega(c)=\{\ \bar{\xi}\in\Omega\ |\ E(\bar{\xi})\leq c\ \},\quad c>0.

Proposition 1.1

(coercivity)
.

The sets
\Omega(c)
are compact for each
c>0
.
In particular, the function
E(\bar{\xi})
reaches its minimal value.

Proof.

If
\bar{\xi}=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{m})\in\Omega(c)
then

\displaystyle-k_{i}(u_{i+1}-u_{i})\ln(F(\xi_{i}/a_{i})-F(\xi_{i+1}/a_{i}))\leq E(\bar{\xi})\leq c,\quad i=0,\ldots,m;

(1.8)

\displaystyle d_{i}\xi_{i}^{2}/4\leq E(\bar{\xi})\leq c,\quad i=1,\ldots,m.

(1.9)

It follows from (
1.8
) with
i=m
that
F(\xi_{m}/a_{m})\geq e^{-c/(k_{m}(u_{m+1}-u_{m}))}
, which implies
the low bound

\xi_{m}\geq r_{1}=a_{m}F^{-1}(e^{-c/(k_{m}(u_{m+1}-u_{m}))}).

Similarly, we derive from (
1.8
) with
i=0
that in the case
u_{1}>u_{0}

1-F(\xi_{1}/a_{0})\geq e^{-c/(k_{0}(u_{1}-u_{0}))}

(notice that
F(\xi_{0}/a_{0})=F(+\infty)=1
). Therefore,

F(\xi_{1}/a_{0})\leq 1-e^{-c/(k_{0}(u_{1}-u_{0}))}
. This implies the upper bound
\xi_{1}\leq a_{0}F^{-1}(1-e^{-c/(k_{0}(u_{1}-u_{0}))})
.
On the other hand, if
u_{0}=u_{1}
then
d_{1}>0
and it follows from (
1.9
) with
i=1
that
\xi_{1}\leq(4c/d_{1})^{1/2}
. We summarize that in any case

\xi_{1}\leq r_{2}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcr}a_{0}F^{-1}(1-e^{-c/(k_{0}(u_{1}-u_{0}))})&,&u_{0}<u_{1},\\
(4c/d_{1})^{1/2}&,&u_{0}=u_{1}.\end{array}\right.

Further, it follows from (
1.8
) that for all
i=1,\ldots,m-1

F(\xi_{i}/a_{i})-F(\xi_{i+1}/a_{i})\geq\delta_{1}\doteq\exp(-c/\alpha)>0,

(1.10)

where
\displaystyle\alpha=\min_{i=1,\ldots,m-1}k_{i}(u_{i+1}-u_{i})>0
. Since
F^{\prime}(\xi)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}e^{-\xi^{2}/4}<1
, the function
F(\xi)
is Lipschitz with constant
1
, and it follows from (
1.10
) that

(\xi_{i}-\xi_{i+1})/a_{i}\geq F(\xi_{i}/a_{i})-F(\xi_{i+1}/a_{i})\geq\delta_{1},\quad i=1,\ldots,m-1,

and we obtain the estimates
\xi_{i}-\xi_{i+1}\geq\delta=\delta_{1}\min a_{i}
. Thus, the set
\Omega(c)
is contained in a compact

K=\{\ \bar{\xi}=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{m})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\ |\ r_{2}\geq\xi_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\xi_{m}\geq r_{1},\ \xi_{i}-\xi_{i+1}\geq\delta\ \forall i=1,\ldots,m-1\ \}.

Since
E(\bar{\xi})
is continuous on
K
, the set
\Omega(c)
is a closed subset of
K
and therefore is compact. For
c>N\doteq\inf E(\bar{\xi})
, this set is not empty and the function
E(\bar{\xi})
reaches on it a minimal value, which is evidently equal to
N
.
∎

We have established the existence of minimal value
E(\bar{\xi}_{0})=\min E(\bar{\xi})
. At the point
\bar{\xi}_{0}
the required condition
\nabla E(\bar{\xi}_{0})=0
is satisfied, and
\bar{\xi}_{0}
is a solution of the system (
1.6
). The coordinates
of
\bar{\xi}_{0}
determine the solution (
1.5
) of our Stefan problem. Thus, we have established the following existence result.

Theorem 1.1
.

There exists a self-similar solution (
1.5
) of the problem (
1.1
), (
1.2
), (
1.4
).

1.2
Convexity of the function
E
and uniqueness of a solution

In this section we prove that the function
E(\bar{\xi})
is strictly convex. Since a strictly convex function can have at most one critical point (and it is necessarily a global minimum), the system (
1.6
) has at most one solution, that is, a self-similar solution (
1.5
) of the problem (
1.1
), (
1.2
), (
1.4
) is unique. We will need the following simple lemma proven in
[
5
]
(see also
[
4
]
). For the sake of completeness we provide it with the proof.

Lemma 1.1
.

The function
P(x,y)=-\ln(F(x)-F(y))
is strictly convex in the half-plane
x>y
.

Proof.

The function
P(x,y)
is infinitely differentiable in the domain
x>y
. To prove the lemma, we need to establish that the Hessian
D^{2}P
is positive definite at every point. By the direct computation we find

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}P(x,y)=\frac{(F^{\prime}(x))^{2}-F^{\prime\prime}(x)(F(x)-F(y))}{(F(x)-F(y))^{2}},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial y^{2}}P(x,y)=\frac{(F^{\prime}(y))^{2}-F^{\prime\prime}(y)(F(y)-F(x))}{(F(x)-F(y))^{2}},\ \frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x\partial y}P(x,y)=-\frac{F^{\prime}(x)F^{\prime}(y)}{(F(x)-F(y))^{2}}.

We have to prove positive definiteness of the matrix
Q=(F(x)-F(y))^{2}D^{2}P(x,y)
with the components

\displaystyle Q_{11}=(F^{\prime}(x))^{2}-F^{\prime\prime}(x)(F(x)-F(y)),

\displaystyle Q_{22}=(F^{\prime}(y))^{2}-F^{\prime\prime}(y)(F(y)-F(x)),\ Q_{12}=Q_{21}=-F^{\prime}(x)F^{\prime}(y).

Since
F^{\prime}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}e^{-x^{2}/4}
, then
F^{\prime\prime}(x)=-\frac{x}{2}F^{\prime}(x)
and the diagonal elements of this matrix can be written in the form

\displaystyle Q_{11}=F^{\prime}(x)(\frac{x}{2}(F(x)-F(y))+F^{\prime}(x))=

\displaystyle F^{\prime}(x)(\frac{x}{2}(F(x)-F(y))+(F^{\prime}(x)-F^{\prime}(y)))+F^{\prime}(x)F^{\prime}(y),

\displaystyle Q_{22}=F^{\prime}(y)(\frac{y}{2}(F(y)-F(x))+(F^{\prime}(y)-F^{\prime}(x)))+F^{\prime}(x)F^{\prime}(y).

By Cauchy mean value theorem there exists such a value
z\in(y,x)
that

\frac{F^{\prime}(x)-F^{\prime}(y)}{F(x)-F(y)}=\frac{F^{\prime\prime}(z)}{F^{\prime}(z)}=-z/2.

Therefore,

\displaystyle Q_{11}=F^{\prime}(x)(F(x)-F(y))(x-z)/2+F^{\prime}(x)F^{\prime}(y),

\displaystyle Q_{22}=F^{\prime}(y)(F(x)-F(y))(z-y)/2+F^{\prime}(x)F^{\prime}(y),

and it follows that
Q=R_{1}+F^{\prime}(x)F^{\prime}(y)R_{2}
, where
R_{1}
is a diagonal matrix with the positive diagonal elements

F^{\prime}(x)(F(x)-F(y))(x-z)/2
,
F^{\prime}(y)(F(x)-F(y))(z-y)/2
while
R_{2}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&-1\\
-1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
. Since
R_{1}>0
,
R_{2}\geq 0
, then the matrix
Q>0
, as was to be proved.
∎

Remark 1.1
.

In addition to Lemma
1.1
we observe that the functions
P(x,0)=-\ln F(x)
,
P(+\infty,x)=-\ln(1-F(x))

of single variable
x
are strictly convex on
(0,+\infty)
. In fact, it follows from Lemma
1.1
in the limit as
y\to 0
that the function
P(x,0)
is convex on
(0,+\infty)
, moreover,

(F(x))^{2}\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}P(x,0)=F^{\prime}(x)(\frac{x}{2}F(x)+F^{\prime}(x))=\lim_{y\to 0}Q_{11}\geq 0.

Since
F^{\prime}(x)>0
, we find, in particular, that
\frac{x}{2}F(x)+F^{\prime}(x)\geq 0
.
If
\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}P(x,0)=0
at some point
x=x_{0}
then
0=\frac{x_{0}}{2}F(x_{0})+F^{\prime}(x_{0})
is the minimum of the nonnegative function
\frac{x}{2}F(x)+F^{\prime}(x)
. Therefore, its derivative
(\frac{x}{2}F+F^{\prime})^{\prime}(x_{0})=0
. Since
F^{\prime\prime}(x)=-\frac{x}{2}F^{\prime}(x)
, this derivative

(\frac{x}{2}F+F^{\prime})^{\prime}(x_{0})=F(x_{0})/2+\frac{x_{0}}{2}F^{\prime}(x_{0})+F^{\prime\prime}(x_{0})=F(x_{0})/2>0.

But this contradicts our assumption. We conclude that
\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}P(x,0)>0
and the function
P(x,0)
is strictly convex.

The strong convexity of the function
P(+\infty,x)=-\ln(1-F(x))
is proved similarly. For the sake of completeness, we
provide the details.
In the limit as
x<y\to+\infty
we derive from Lemma
1.1
that the function
P(+\infty,x)=\lim\limits_{y\to+\infty}P(y,x)
is convex on
{\mathbb{R}}
and

(1-F(x))^{2}\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}P(+\infty,x)=F^{\prime}(x)(\frac{x}{2}(F(x)-1)+F^{\prime}(x))\geq 0.

If
\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}P(+\infty,x)=0
at some point
x=x_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}
then
x_{0}
is a minimum point of the nonnegative function

\frac{x}{2}(F(x)-1)+F^{\prime}(x)
. Therefore,

0=(\frac{x}{2}(F(x)-1)+F^{\prime}(x))^{\prime}(x_{0})=(F(x_{0})-1)/2+F^{\prime\prime}(x_{0})+F^{\prime}(x_{0})x_{0}/2=(F(x_{0})-1)/2<0.

This contradiction implies that
\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}P(+\infty,x)>0
for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
and, therefore, the function
P(+\infty,x)
is strictly convex (even on the whole line
{\mathbb{R}}
).

Now we are ready to prove the expected convexity of
E(\bar{\xi})
.

Proposition 1.2
.

The function
E(\bar{\xi})
is strictly convex on
\Omega
.

Proof.

We introduce the functions

E_{i}(\bar{\xi})=-k_{i}(u_{i+1}-u_{i})\ln(F(\xi_{i}/a_{i})-F(\xi_{i+1}/a_{i})),\quad i=0,\ldots,m.

By Lemma
1.1
and Remark
1.1
all these functions are convex.
Since

E(\bar{\xi})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}E_{i}(\bar{\xi})+E_{0}(\bar{\xi})+\sum_{i=1}^{m}d_{i}\xi_{i}^{2}/4

and all functions in this sum are convex, it is sufficient to prove strong convexity of the sum

\tilde{E}(\bar{\xi})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}E_{i}(\bar{\xi}).

By Lemma
1.1
and Remark
1.1
all terms in this sum are convex functions. Therefore, the function
\tilde{E}
is convex as well. To prove the strict convexity, we assume that for some vector
\zeta=(\zeta_{1},\ldots,\zeta_{m})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
.

D^{2}\tilde{E}(\bar{\xi})\zeta\cdot\zeta=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\frac{\partial^{2}\tilde{E}(\bar{\xi})}{\partial\xi_{i}\partial\xi_{j}}\zeta_{i}\zeta_{j}=0

(1.11)

Since

0=D^{2}\tilde{E}(\bar{\xi})\zeta\cdot\zeta=\sum_{i=1}^{m}D^{2}E_{i}(\bar{\xi})\zeta\cdot\zeta

while all the terms are nonnegative, we conclude that

D^{2}E_{i}(\bar{\xi})\zeta\cdot\zeta=0,\quad i=1,\ldots,m.

(1.12)

By Lemma
1.1
for
i=1,\ldots,m-1
the function
E_{i}(\bar{\xi})
is strictly convex as a function of two variables
\xi_{i},\xi_{i+1}
and it follows from (
1.12
) that
\zeta_{i}=\zeta_{i+1}=0
,
i=1,\ldots,m-1
. Observe that in the case
m=1
there are no such
i
. In this case we apply (
1.12
) for
i=m
. Taking into account Remark
1.1
, we find that
E_{m}(\bar{\xi})
is a strictly convex function of the single variable
\xi_{m}
, and it follows from (
1.12
) that
\zeta_{m}=0
. In any case we obtain that the vector
\zeta=0
. Thus, relation (
1.11
) can hold only for zero
\zeta
, that is, the matrix
D^{2}\tilde{E}(\bar{\xi})
is (strictly) positive definite, and the function
\tilde{E}(\bar{\xi})
is strictly convex. This completes the proof.
∎

2
The case of infinitely many phase transitions

In this small section we consider the exotic case when the number
m
of phase transitions is infinite. More precisely, we suppose that the phase transitions temperatures
u_{i}\geq u_{0}
form a strictly increasing sequence,
u_{i+1}>u_{i}
,
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
. In (
1.1
) there are infinitely many phases, the
i
-th phase corresponds to the temperature
u\in(u_{i},u_{i+1})
,
i=\{0\}\cup{\mathbb{N}}
. The parameters
a_{i},k_{i}>0
are, respectively, the diffusivity constant and the thermal conductivity of the
i
-th phase,
i=0,1,\ldots
;
d_{i}\geq 0
is the latent specific heat of the
i
-th phase transition,

i=1,2,\ldots
. As in the previous section, we assume that
d_{1}>0
whenever
u_{1}=u_{0}
. We will study the problem (
1.1
), (
1.2
), (
1.4
) with possibly infinite Dirichlet data
u_{D}=\lim\limits_{i\to\infty}u_{i}\leq+\infty
. A self-similar solution
u=u(\xi)
,
\xi=x/\sqrt{t}
, of this problem is given by expression (
1.5
), where now
i
runs over all nonnegative integers. The Stefan conditions (
1.2
) reduce again to (now infinite) system (
1.6
), which can be written in the form
\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi_{i}}E(\bar{\xi})=0
, where the functional

\displaystyle E(\bar{\xi})=-\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}k_{i}(u_{i+1}-u_{i})\ln(F(\xi_{i}/a_{i})-F(\xi_{i+1}/a_{i}))+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}d_{i}\xi_{i}^{2}/4,

(2.1)

\displaystyle\bar{\xi}=(\xi_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\in\Omega,

\Omega=\{\ \bar{\xi}=(\xi_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\in l_{\infty}\ |\ \xi_{i}>\xi_{i+1}>0\ \forall i\in{\mathbb{N}}\ \}.

Here, as usual,

is the space of bounded sequences equipped with the norm
\|\bar{\xi}\|_{\infty}=\sup|\xi_{i}|
.
As is well-known, this space is dual to the space of summable sequences

. Observe also that there is only finite number of terms in (

\xi_{i}
. Therefore, the partial derivatives
\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi_{i}}E(\bar{\xi})
are well defined whenever the value
E(\bar{\xi})
is finite.

We do not include the natural requirement
\lim\limits_{i\to\infty}\xi_{i}=0
in the definition of

E
in the weak-
*
topology. Observe that the functional
E
may take the value

, moreover, it can happen that
E\equiv+\infty
. Assuming that the latter does not however happen, i.e. the functional

is proper,
E\not\equiv+\infty
, we will show that this functional admits a unique global minimum point. For that we need some nice properties of

collected below.

Proposition 2.1
.

(i) The functional
E(\bar{\xi})
is low semi-continuous in weak-
*
topology;

(ii) It is coercive, that is, the sets
\Omega(c)=\{\bar{\xi}\in\Omega\ |\ E(\bar{\xi})\leq c\ \}
are weakly-
*
compact;

(iii) The functional
E(\bar{\xi})
is strictly convex on
\Omega
.

Proof.

It is known that weak-
*
convergence
\bar{\xi}_{n}\rightharpoonup\bar{\xi}
of a sequence
\bar{\xi}_{n}=(\xi_{i}^{n})_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\in l_{\infty}
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, is equivalent to uniform boundedness
|\xi_{ni}|\leq\mathrm{const}
and elementwise convergence
\xi_{i}^{n}\mathop{\to}\limits_{n\to\infty}\xi_{i}
of this sequence. Assume that
\bar{\xi}_{n}\in\Omega
,
\bar{\xi}\in\Omega
, and
\bar{\xi}_{n}\rightharpoonup\bar{\xi}
weakly-
*
in
l_{\infty}
. Then
\xi_{i}^{n}\to\xi_{i}
as
n\to\infty
for each
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Since all terms in formula (
2.1
) are nonnegative, we can apply Fatou’s lemma for sums and conclude that

\displaystyle E(\bar{\xi})=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}-k_{i}(u_{i+1}-u_{i})\ln(F(\xi_{i}/a_{i})-F(\xi_{i+1}/a_{i}))+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}d_{i}\xi_{i}^{2}/4\leq

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\{\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}-k_{i}(u_{i+1}-u_{i})\ln(F(\xi_{i}^{n}/a_{i})-F(\xi_{i+1}^{n}/a_{i}))+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}d_{i}(\xi_{i}^{n})^{2}/4\}=\liminf_{n\to\infty}E(\bar{\xi}_{n}).

Hence, the functional
E
is weakly-
*
low semi-continuous, and (i) is proven.

To prove (ii) we first notice that
\Omega(c)=\emptyset
if
c\leq 0
and we can suppose that
c>0
. If
\bar{\xi}=(\xi_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\in\Omega(c)
then relations (
1.8
), (
1.9
) hold for all
i\in\{0\}\cup{\mathbb{N}}
. As in the proof of Proposition
1.1
, we derive from these relations that

\xi_{1}\leq r_{2}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcr}a_{0}F^{-1}(1-e^{-c/(k_{0}(u_{1}-u_{0}))})&,&u_{0}<u_{1},\\
(4c/d_{1})^{1/2}&,&u_{0}=u_{1}.\end{array}\right.

Further, it follows from (
1.8
) that for every
i\in{\mathbb{N}}

F(\xi_{i}/a_{i})-F(\xi_{i+1}/a_{i})\geq\delta_{i}\doteq\exp(-c/\alpha_{i})>0,

where
\alpha_{i}=k_{i}(u_{i+1}-u_{i})>0
. Since
F(\xi)
is Lipschitz with constant
1
, this implies the inequalities

\xi_{i}-\xi_{i+1}\geq a_{i}\delta_{i}>0,\quad i\in{\mathbb{N}}.

Hence,
\Omega(c)
is contained in the set

K=\{\bar{\xi}\in l_{\infty}\ |\ 0\leq\xi_{i}\leq r_{2},\ \xi_{i}-\xi_{i+1}\geq a_{i}\delta_{i}\ \forall i\in{\mathbb{N}}\ \}.

Obviously, this set is bounded and weakly-
*
closed in
l_{\infty}
. By the Banach-Alaoglu theorem
the set
K
is weakly-
*
compact. Since
E
is weakly-
*
semi-continuous, the set
\Omega(c)
is a closed subset of
K
and, therefore, is compact. Coercivity of
E
is proved.

To prove (iii), we observe that the functional
E(\bar{\xi})
is convex as a sum of the convex functionals
-k_{i}(u_{i+1}-u_{i})\ln(F(\xi_{i}/a_{i})-F(\xi_{i+1}/a_{i}))
and
d_{i}\xi_{i}^{2}/4
. By the same reason for each
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
the functional

R_{m}(\bar{\xi})=-\sum_{i=m}^{\infty}k_{i}(u_{i+1}-u_{i})\ln(F(\xi_{i}/a_{i})-F(\xi_{i+1}/a_{i}))+\sum_{i=m+1}^{\infty}d_{i}\xi_{i}^{2}/4

is convex while the function

E_{m}(\bar{\xi}_{m})=-\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}k_{i}(u_{i+1}-u_{i})\ln(F(\xi_{i}/a_{i})-F(\xi_{i+1}/a_{i}))+\sum_{i=1}^{m}d_{i}\xi_{i}^{2}/4,\quad\bar{\xi}_{m}=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{m})

is strictly convex on
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
, this can be established on the base of Lemma
1.1
in the same way as in the proof of Proposition
1.2
. It is clear that
E(\bar{\xi})=E_{m}(\bar{\xi})+R_{m}(\bar{\xi}_{m})
. Now we take different points
\bar{\xi}^{1},\bar{\xi}^{2}\in\Omega
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Since
\bar{\xi}^{1}\not=\bar{\xi}^{2}
then we can find such
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
that

\bar{\xi}^{1}_{m}\not=\bar{\xi}^{2}_{m}
, where
\bar{\xi}^{n}_{m}
,
n=1,2
, are the vectors in
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
formed by the first
m
elements of
\bar{\xi}^{n}
. Since the function

is strictly convex then

E_{m}((1-\alpha)\bar{\xi}^{1}_{m}+\alpha\bar{\xi}^{2}_{m})<(1-\alpha)E_{m}(\bar{\xi}^{1}_{m})+\alpha E_{m}(\bar{\xi}^{2}_{m})

while

R_{m}((1-\alpha)\bar{\xi}^{1}+\alpha\bar{\xi}^{2})\leq(1-\alpha)R_{m}(\bar{\xi}^{1})+\alpha R_{m}(\bar{\xi}^{2})

by the convexity of
R_{m}
. Therefore,

E((1-\alpha)\bar{\xi}^{1}+\alpha\bar{\xi}^{2})<(1-\alpha)E(\bar{\xi}^{1})+\alpha E(\bar{\xi}^{2}).

This proves the strict convexity of
E(\bar{\xi})
.
∎

In the case

Theorem 2.1
.

Assume that the potential
E(\bar{\xi})
is a proper functional, i.e.

. Then there exists a unique
minimum point
\bar{\xi}^{0}=(\xi^{0}_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\in\Omega
of

E(\bar{\xi}^{0})=N
. Moreover,
\lim\limits_{i\to\infty}\xi^{0}_{i}=0
, and function (

)

\displaystyle u(\xi)=u_{i}+\frac{u_{i+1}-u_{i}}{F(\xi^{0}_{i+1}/a_{i})-F(\xi^{0}_{i}/a_{i})}(F(\xi/a_{i})-F(\xi^{0}_{i}/a_{i})),

(2.2)

\displaystyle\xi^{0}_{i+1}<\xi<\xi^{0}_{i},\ i=0,1,\ldots

is a solution of (
1.1
), (
1.2
), (
1.4
).

Proof.

We define for
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
the sub-level sets
K_{n}=\Omega(N+1/n)
where
E\leq N+1/n
. Then these sets are nonempty. By Proposition
2.1
they are weakly-
*
compact. Obviously,
K_{n+1}\subset K_{n}

\forall n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. By Cantor’s intersection theorem there exists a point
\bar{\xi}^{0}\in\bigcap\limits_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}K_{n}
. Then,
N\leq E(\bar{\xi}^{0})\leq N+1/n
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, which implies that
E(\bar{\xi}^{0})=N
, and
\bar{\xi}^{0}
is a point of global minimum of
E
. Uniqueness of this point directly follows from the strict convexity of
E
stated in Proposition
2.1
(iii). It only remains to prove that the sequence

\bar{\xi}^{0}=(\xi^{0}_{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
vanishes. Assuming the contrary, we will have
\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\xi^{0}_{i}=\inf_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\xi^{0}_{i}=r>0
. Then the sequence
\bar{\xi}^{r}=\bar{\xi}^{0}-r
with components
\xi^{0}_{i}-r
lies in
\Omega
.
Since
1-F((\xi^{0}_{1}-r)/a_{0})>1-F(\xi^{0}_{1})/a_{0}
and for all
i\in{\mathbb{N}}

(\xi^{0}_{i}-r)^{2}<(\xi^{0}_{i})^{2}
,

\displaystyle F((\xi^{0}_{i}-r)/a_{i})-F((\xi^{0}_{i+1}-r)/a_{i})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{(\xi^{0}_{i+1}-r)/a_{i}}^{(\xi^{0}_{i}-r)/a_{i}}e^{-s^{2}/4}ds>

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{\xi^{0}_{i+1}/a_{i}}^{\xi^{0}_{i}/a_{i}}e^{-s^{2}/4}ds=F(\xi^{0}_{i}/a_{i})-F(\xi^{0}_{i+1}/a_{i}),

all the terms in expression (
2.1
) became smaller if we replace
\bar{\xi}^{0}
by
\bar{\xi}^{r}
. Therefore,

E(\bar{\xi}^{r})<E(\bar{\xi}^{0})=N
, which is impossible. Hence,
\lim\limits_{i\to\infty}\xi^{0}_{i}=0
. Since
\bar{\xi}^{0}
is a minimum point of
E
then
\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi_{i}}E(\bar{\xi}^{0})=0
for all

and Stefan conditions (

) are satisfied. We conclude that (

) is a solution of our problem (
1.1
), (
1.2
), (
1.4
).
∎

\displaystyle d_{i}\xi_{i}/2+k_{i}\frac{(u_{i+1}-u_{i})F^{\prime}(\xi_{i}/a_{i})}{a_{i}(F(\xi_{i+1}/a_{i})-F(\xi_{i}/a_{i}))}-

\displaystyle k_{i-1}\frac{(u_{i}-u_{i-1})F^{\prime}(\xi_{i}/a_{i-1})}{a_{i-1}(F(\xi_{i}/a_{i-1})-F(\xi_{i-1}/a_{i-1}))}=0,\quad i=1,\ldots,n-1,

(3.15)

\displaystyle d_{n}\xi_{n}/2+b_{N}\sqrt{\pi}F^{\prime}(\xi_{n}/a_{n})-k_{n-1}\frac{(u_{n}-u_{n-1})F^{\prime}(\xi_{n}/a_{n-1})}{a_{n-1}(F(\xi_{n}/a_{n-1})-F(\xi_{n-1}/a_{n-1}))}=0,\quad i=n.

(3.16)

Like in the case of Dirichlet boundary condition, this system turns out to be gradient one, it coincides with the equality
\nabla E_{n}=0
, where the function

\displaystyle E_{n}(\bar{\xi})=-\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}k_{i}(u_{i+1}-u_{i})\ln(F(\xi_{i}/a_{i})-F(\xi_{i+1}/a_{i}))

\displaystyle+a_{n}b_{N}\sqrt{\pi}F(\xi_{n}/a_{n})+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}\xi_{i}^{2},\quad\bar{\xi}=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})\in\Omega_{n},

(3.17)

\Omega_{n}
is an open convex cone in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
consisting of vectors with strictly decreasing positive coordinates.
Remark that
F^{\prime\prime}(s)=-s/2F^{\prime}(s)<0
for all
s>0
. Since
b_{N}<0
, this implies that the term
a_{n}b_{N}\sqrt{\pi}F(\xi_{n}/a_{n})
is strictly convex function of single variable
\xi_{n}
on the interval

. In the same way as in the proof of Proposition
1.2

we find that the function

\tilde{E}_{n}(\bar{\xi})=-\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}k_{i}(u_{i+1}-u_{i})\ln(F(\xi_{i}/a_{i})-F(\xi_{i+1}/a_{i}))+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}\xi_{i}^{2}

is strictly convex on a cone

\bar{\Omega}_{n}=\{\ \bar{\xi}=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\ |\ \xi_{1}>\cdots>\xi_{n}\geq 0\ \}\supset\Omega_{n},

consisting of points with strictly decreasing nonnegative coordinates. Since

E_{n}(\bar{\xi})=\tilde{E}_{n}(\bar{\xi})+a_{n}b_{N}\sqrt{\pi}F(\xi_{n}/a_{n}),

the function
E_{n}(\bar{\xi})
is strictly convex on
\bar{\Omega}_{n}
as well.
Let us demonstrate that this function is coercive on
\bar{\Omega}_{n}
.

Proposition 3.1
.

For all
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
the set
\bar{\Omega}_{n}(c)=\{\ \bar{\xi}\in\bar{\Omega}_{n}\ |\ E_{n}(\bar{\xi})\leq c\ \}
is compact.

Proof.

Suppose that
\bar{\xi}\in\bar{\Omega}_{n}
,
E_{n}(\bar{\xi})\leq c
. Then

\displaystyle\tilde{E}_{n}(\bar{\xi})\doteq-\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}k_{i}(u_{i+1}-u_{i})\ln(F(\xi_{i}/a_{i})-F(\xi_{i+1}/a_{i}))+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}\xi_{i}^{2}=E(\bar{\xi})-a_{n}b_{N}\sqrt{\pi}F(\xi_{n}/a_{n})\leq c_{1}\doteq c-a_{n}b_{N}\sqrt{\pi}.

Since all the terms of the left-hand side of this inequality are nonnegative, we obtain the relations

\displaystyle-k_{i}(u_{i+1}-u_{i})\ln(F(\xi_{i}/a_{i})-F(\xi_{i+1}/a_{i}))\leq c_{1},\ i=0,\ldots,n-1,

(3.18)

\displaystyle d_{i}\xi_{i}^{2}/4\leq c_{1},\ i=1,\ldots,n,

(3.19)

the same as inequalities (
1.8
), (
1.9
). As follows from (
3.18
), (
3.19
), the set
\bar{\Omega}_{n}(c)=\emptyset
if
c_{1}<0
. Therefore, we may (and will) suppose that
c_{1}\geq 0
. Arguing as in the proof of Proposition
1.1
, we derive from (
3.18
), (
3.19
) the bounds

\displaystyle\xi_{1}\leq r_{2}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcr}a_{0}F^{-1}(1-e^{-c_{1}/(k_{0}(u_{1}-u_{0}))})&,&u_{0}<u_{1},\\
(4c_{1}/d_{1})^{1/2}&,&u_{0}=u_{1},\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle(\xi_{i}-\xi_{i+1})/a_{i}\geq\delta=\exp(-c_{1}/\alpha)>0,\quad i=1,\ldots,n-1,

where
\displaystyle\alpha=\min_{i=1,\ldots,m-1}k_{i}(u_{i+1}-u_{i})>0
. Thus, the set
\bar{\Omega}_{n}(c)
is contained in a compact

K=\{\ \bar{\xi}=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\ |\ r_{2}\geq\xi_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\xi_{n}\geq 0,\ \xi_{i}-\xi_{i+1}\geq\delta a_{i}\ \forall i=1,\ldots,n-1\ \}.

Since

is a closed subset of

and therefore is compact. This completes the proof.
∎

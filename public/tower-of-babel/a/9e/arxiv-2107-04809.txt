Generating functions of the Hurwitz class numbers associated with certain mock theta functions
Dandan Chen, Rong Chen

Abstract
. We find Hecke-Rogers type series representations of generating functions of the Hurwitz class numbers which are similar to certain mock theta functions. We also prove two combinatorial interpretations of Hurwitz class numbers which appeared on OEIS (see A238872 and A321440).



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
11E41, 11P84, 11F37

1.
Introduction

Following
[
11
, Section 5.3]
, we define the Hurwitz class number
H(N)
, where
N
is a non-negative integer, as follows:

(1)If
N\equiv 1,2\pmod{4}
then
H(N)=0
.

(2)If
N=0
then
H(0)=-1/12
.

(3)If
N>0
,
N\equiv 0,3\pmod{4}
, then
H(N)
is the class number of positive definite binary quadratic forms of discriminant
-N
, with those classes that contain a multiple of
x^{2}+y^{2}
or
x^{2}+xy+y^{2}
counted with weight
1/2
or
1/3
, respectively.

For the modular behaviors, we use Kronecker’s notation
[
19
]

F(n)
satisfying
F(8n+7)=H(8n+7)
and
F(8n+3)=3H(8n+3)
, see also
[
23
]
where denote
H(n)
as
F_{1}(n)
. The generating functions of
F(an+b)
are defined as

\mathscr{F}_{a,b}(q):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}F(an+b)q^{n}.

In 1935 Watson
[
23
]
studied the transformations of such generating functions of
H(n)
by using Mordell integrals. Hirzebruch and Zagier
[
17
, Section 2.2]
discussed the relation between the generating functions of
H(n)
and certain weight
3/2
modular forms. On the other hand, Zwegers
[
26
]
found that mock theta functions are related to weight
1/2
modular forms. In this paper, we find some similar results between the generating functions of Hurwitz class numbers and certain mock theta functions.

Mock theta functions were first mentioned by Ramanujan in his last letter to Hardy in 1920 as Eulerian forms. To study the modular behaviors, many authors such as Watson
[
24
,
25
]
, Andrews
[
2
]
, Andrews and Hickerson
[
3
]
, Berndt and Chan
[
4
]
, Choi
[
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
]
, Garvan
[
13
]
, Gordon and McIntosh
[
14
]
, Hickerson
[
15
]
and Zwegers
[
27
]
find the Appell-Lerch series and Hecke-Rogers series of mock theta functions. In a recent work, Hickerson and Mortenson
[
16
]
used the “m-block” to list the Appell-Lerch series of all the mock theta functions.

Recently, the second author and Garvan
[
6
]
provided some of modulo 4 congruences between the Hurwitz class numbers and the coefficients of certain mock theta functions. For example
[
6
, Lemma 3.1]

N_{A}(n)\equiv(-1)^{n+1}H(8n-1)\pmod{4},

where
N_{A}(n)
are coefficients of the second order mock theta function
A(q)
(see
[
20
]
)

A(q):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{(n+1)^{2}}(-q;q^{2})_{n}}{(q;q^{2})^{2}_{n+1}}.

We always assume that
|q|<1
and use the standard
q
-series notations:

\displaystyle(a;q)_{0}:=1,\quad(a;q)_{n}:=\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}(1-aq^{k}),\quad(a;q)_{\infty}:=\prod_{k=0}^{\infty}(1-aq^{k}),

\displaystyle(a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{m};q)_{n}=(a_{1};q)_{n}(a_{2};q)_{n}\cdots(a_{m};q)_{n},\quad n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}.

To find further relation, we find the Hecke-Rogers series of some of
\mathscr{F}_{a,b}(q)
by following one of the methods of studying mock theta functions. Something amazing is that the Hecke-Rogers series we find are quite similar to certain mock theta functions.

Cui, Gu and Hao
[
12
, Eq. (2.24)]
showed the Hecke-Rogers series of
A(q)
and we rewrite it as

(1.1)

\frac{J_{1}^{2}}{J_{2}}A(q)=\sum_{1\leq j\leq|n|}sg(n)(-1)^{n-1}q^{2n^{2}-n-j^{2}+j},

where, as usual,
sg(n)=1
if
n\geq 0
and
sg(n)=-1
if

and

J_{k}:=(q^{k};q^{k})_{\infty}.

For
\mathscr{F}_{8,-1}(q)
, there is a similar identity

(1.2)

\frac{J_{1}^{2}}{J_{2}}\mathscr{F}_{8,-1}(q)=\sum_{1\leq j\leq|n|}sg(n)(-1)^{j-1}(2j-1)q^{2n^{2}-n-j^{2}+j},

which appeared in
[
18
, P.376]
. (
1.2
) is quite similar to (
1.1
) and we find a

-analog of these two identities.

Theorem 1.1
.

(1.3)

(zq,q/z,q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}F_{8}(z,q)=\sum_{1\leq j\leq|n|}sg(n)(-1)^{j-1}q^{2n^{2}-n-j^{2}+j}\cdot\frac{z^{1-j}-z^{j}}{1-z},

where

(1.4)

F_{8}(z,q)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}(q;q^{2})_{n}q^{(n+1)^{2}}}{(zq,q/z;q^{2})_{n+1}}.

Our proof is based on the formulas of generating functions of Hurwitz class numbers by Alfes, Bringmann and Lovejoy

]
, then we use the formulas of Appell-Lerch series by Hickerson and Mortenson

We obtain a similar result for the eighth order mock theta function
V_{1}(q)

\mathscr{F}_{4,-1}(q)

V_{1}(q):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{(n+1)^{2}}(-q;q^{2})_{n}}{(q;q^{2})_{n+1}}.

Theorem 1.2

For

We also find and prove Hecke-Rogers series for

2.1.
Appell-Lerch series

Following
[
16
]
, we define

j(x;q):=(x,q/x,q;q)_{\infty}=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}q^{n(n-1)/2}x^{n},

and the Appell-Lerch series

m(x,q,z):=\frac{1}{j(z;q)}\sum_{r=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{r}q^{r(r-1)/2}z^{r}}{1-q^{r-1}xz},

and ’building block’ of mock theta functions

f_{a,b,c}(x,y,q):=\sum_{sg(r)=sg(s)}sg(r)(-1)^{r+s}x^{r}y^{s}q^{ar(r-1)/2+brs+cs(s-1)/2},

and

\displaystyle g_{a,b,c}(x,y,q,z_{1},z_{0})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{a-1}(-y)^{t}q^{ct(t-1)/2}j(q^{bt}x;q^{a})m(-q^{ab(b+1)/2-ca(a+1)/2-t(b^{2}-ac)}\frac{(-y)^{a}}{(-x)^{b}},q^{a(b^{2}-ac)},z_{0})

\displaystyle+\sum_{t=0}^{c-1}(-x)^{t}q^{at(t-1)/2}j(q^{bt}y;q^{c})m(-q^{cb(b+1)/2-ac(c+1)/2-t(b^{2}-ac)}\frac{(-x)^{c}}{(-y)^{b}},q^{a(b^{2}-ac)},z_{1}).

We have

(2.2)

\frac{d}{dz}j(\pm zq;q^{2})\big|_{z=1}=0.

3.1.
Hecke-Rogers series of
\mathscr{F}_{4,-1}(q)

Following Alfes, Bringmann and Lovejoy
[
1
]
, we define

F_{4}(z,q):=-N^{o}(1,-1/q,-z,-q)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}(q;-q)_{2n}q^{n+1}}{(zq,q/z;q^{2})_{n+1}}.

It showed that
F_{4}(1,q)=\mathscr{F}_{4,-1}(q)
and
F_{4}(i,-q)=-V_{1}(q)
in
[
1
]
. Mortenson
[
21
, Eq. (2.12)]
also studied this function and showed the Appell-Lerch series

(3.1)

\left(1-\frac{1}{z}\right)F_{4}(z,q)=m(-z,q^{2},-q).

The following theorem is equivalent to (
3.1
).

Theorem 3.1
.

For
z\in\mathbb{C^{*}}
and
z\neq 1
, we have

(3.2)

\displaystyle(zq,q/z,q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}F_{4}(z,q)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-z}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}q^{k^{2}}}{1+q^{2k-1}}z^{1-k}=-\frac{1}{1-z}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}q^{k^{2}}}{1+q^{2k-1}}z^{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}q^{k^{2}}}{1+q^{2k-1}}\cdot\frac{z^{1-k}-z^{k}}{1-z}

Proof.

By Theorem
2.4

m(-z,q^{2},-q)=m(-z,q^{2},q/z).

In addition by Proposition
2.3

m(-z,q^{2},q/z)=-\frac{1}{z}m(-1/z,q^{2},z/q)=-\frac{1}{z}m(-1/z,q^{2},qz).

Then (
3.2
) holds obviously.
∎

Letting
z\rightarrow 1
in (
3.2
), we have the Appell-Lerch series of
\mathscr{F}_{4,-1}(q)
.

Corollary 3.2
.

(3.3)

\frac{J_{1}^{2}}{J_{2}}\mathscr{F}_{4,-1}(q)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}(2k-1)q^{k^{2}}}{1+q^{2k-1}}.

Then we prove the Hecke-Rogers series of
F_{4}(z,q)
.

Theorem 3.3
.

For
z\in\mathbb{C^{*}}
and
z\neq 1
, we have

(3.4)

(-zq,-1/z,q;q)_{\infty}F_{4}(z,-q)=\sum_{1\leq j\leq n}q^{n^{2}-j(j-1)/2}\cdot\frac{z^{n}-z^{-n}}{1-z}.

Proof.

Replacing
q
by
-q
in (
3.2
), we have

(-zq,-q/z,q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}F_{4}(z,-q)=\frac{1}{1-z}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{q^{k^{2}}}{1-q^{2k-1}}z^{k}.

Hence (
3.4
) is equivalent to

(3.5)

(-zq,-1/z,q;q)_{\infty}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{q^{k^{2}}}{1-q^{2k-1}}z^{k}=(-zq,-q/z,q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}\sum_{1\leq j\leq|n|}sg(n)q^{n^{2}-j(j-1)/2}z^{n}.

Noting that

\frac{(-zq,-1/z,q;q)_{\infty}}{(-zq,-q/z,q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}}=\frac{J_{1}}{J_{2}^{2}}(-zq^{2},-1/z;q^{2})_{\infty},

(
3.5
) can be simplified as

\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}q^{m(m+1)}z^{m}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{q^{k^{2}}}{1-q^{2k-1}}z^{k}=\frac{J_{2}^{2}}{J_{1}}\sum_{1\leq j\leq|n|}sg(n)q^{n^{2}-j(j-1)/2}z^{n}.

Denote
A_{n}
and
B_{n}
to be the coefficients of
z^{n}
on both sides, respectively. Then

A_{n}=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{q^{k^{2}+(n-k)(n-k+1)}}{1-q^{2k-1}},

and

B_{n}=\frac{J_{2}^{2}}{J_{1}}\sum_{j=1}^{|n|}sg(n)q^{n^{2}-j(j-1)/2}.

It is easy to verify that
B_{n}
satisfy

(1)

For all
n\in\mathbb{Z}

B_{n}+B_{-n}=0,

(2)

For all

\frac{B_{n+1}}{q^{(n+1)^{2}}}-\frac{B_{n}}{q^{n^{2}}}=q^{-\frac{n(n+1)}{2}}\frac{J_{2}^{2}}{J_{1}}.

4.
Hurwitz class numbers and partitions

In this section, we start with a formula by Humbert
[
18
, P. 346]

(4.1)

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}_{4,-1}(q)=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{u=-m}^{m}\frac{q^{(2m+1)^{2}/4+(2m+1)/2+1/4-u^{2}}}{1-q^{2m+1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{u=-m}^{m}\frac{q^{(m+1)^{2}-u^{2}}}{1-q^{2m+1}}.

Then we will prove the combinatorial interpretations of
F(4n-1)
and
H(8n-1)
which appeared on OEIS (see A238872 and A321440).

Denote
P(n)
to be the number of strongly unimodal compositions of
n
with absolute difference of successive parts equal to 1(see A238872 on OEIS). It is easy to see that

n=x+(x+1)+\cdots+(y-1)+y+(y-1)+\cdots+(z+1)+z=y^{2}-\frac{x(x-1)}{2}-\frac{z(z-1)}{2}.

We find that

(4.2)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}P(n)q^{n}=\sum_{y=1}^{\infty}\sum_{x=1}^{y}\sum_{z=1}^{y}q^{y^{2}-x(x-1)/2-z(z-1)/2}.

Theorem 4.1
.

For all
n\in\mathbb{N^{*}}
, we have

P(n)=F(4n-1).

Proof.

Let

A(m,u):=\frac{q^{(m+1)^{2}-u^{2}}}{1-q^{2m+1}},

and

B(y,x,z):=q^{y^{2}-x(x-1)/2-z(z-1)/2}.

Then

\displaystyle A(m,u)=

\displaystyle\frac{q^{(m+1)^{2}-u^{2}}}{1-q^{2m+1}}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}q^{m^{2}-u^{2}+k(2m+1)}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}q^{(k+m)^{2}-(k+u)(k+u-1)/2-(k-u)(k-u-1)/2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}B(k+m,k+u,k-u).

Hence by (
4.1
) and (
4.2
) and noting that

B(y,x,z)=B(y,x,1-z)=B(y,1-x,z),

we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}F(4n-1)q^{n}=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{u=-m}^{m}A(m,u)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{u=-m}^{m}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}B(k+m,k+u,k-u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{y=1}^{\infty}\sum_{(x,z)\in D_{y}}B(y,x,z)=\sum_{y=1}^{\infty}\sum_{x=1}^{y}\sum_{z=1}^{y}B(y,x,z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}P(n)q^{n},

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\sum_{m=1}^{l}\frac{q^{l(l+1)/2-m(m-1)/2}}{1-q^{l}}.

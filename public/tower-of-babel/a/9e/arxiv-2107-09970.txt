Simplicial affine semigroups with monomial minimal reduction ideals
Marco D’Anna, Raheleh Jafari, Francesco Strazzanti

Abstract
. We characterize when the monomial maximal ideal of a simplicial affine semigroup ring has a monomial minimal reduction. When this is the case, we study the Cohen-Macaulay and Gorenstein properties of the associated graded ring and provide several bounds for the reduction number with respect to the monomial minimal reduction.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
13H15, 13H10, 05E40, 20M25

Introduction

In this paper we study affine semigroup rings
{\mathbb{K}}[S]
, with
S
fully embedded in
\mathbb{N}^{d}
, i.e. subalgebras of the polynomial ring
{\mathbb{K}}[x_{1},\dots,x_{d}]
generated by the monomials with exponents in
S
, where
{\mathbb{K}}
is a field. The semigroup ring
R={\mathbb{K}}[S]
is of Krull dimension
d
with a unique monomial maximal ideal
{\mathfrak{m}}
.
The study of affine semigroups and corresponding semigroup rings has many applications in different areas of mathematics; for instance, it gives the combinatorical background for the theory of toric varieties.
Many properties of affine semigroup rings have been studied in connection to the properties of
the associated semigroups (see, e.g.,
[
4
]
and
[
5
]
).
A case of particular interest is when
S
is a simplicial affine semigroup, i.e., when the cone over
S
,
\operatorname{cone}(S)
, has
d
extremal rays. Under this hypothesis, denoting by
{\mathbf{a}}_{1},\dots,{\mathbf{a}}_{d}
the componentwise smallest nonzero vectors of
S
situated respectively, on each extremal ray of
\operatorname{cone}(S)
(which are called the extremal rays of
S
), many properties of

{\mathbb{K}}[S]
(like, e.g., Cohen-Macaulayness or Gorensteinness) can be studied via the Apéry set of
S
with respect to
\{{\mathbf{a}}_{1},\dots,{\mathbf{a}}_{d}\}
(see
[
15
]
).

Affine simplicial semigroups provide also a natural generalization of numerical semigroups, whose theory has been developed mainly in connection to the study of curve singularities. In the case of numerical semigroup rings, a
remarkable property is that the monomial maximal ideal always has a monomial minimal reduction, which is the principal ideal
generated by the monomial of minimal positive degree.
This is a key fact to explore the connection between the associated graded ring
\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)=\oplus_{i=0}^{\infty}\,{\mathfrak{m}}^{i}/{\mathfrak{m}}^{i+1}
, the ring
R
, and the associated semigroup.
However, in the case of affine simplicial semigroup rings, a monomial minimal reduction for
{\mathfrak{m}}
in general does not exist; so it is natural to study when it exists and, if this is the case, to explore which information can be deduced from it.

We firstly observe that, if such a minimal reduction exists, it has to be generated by the monomials
{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{1}},\dots,{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{d}}
corresponding to the extremal rays, provided that
{\mathbb{K}}
is infinite. Moreover, setting

I=({\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{1}},\dots,{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{d}})
and denoting the multiplicity of
I
by
e(I)
, we show that
{\mathbb{K}}[S]
is Cohen-Macaulay if and only if
e(I)
equals the cardinality of the Apéry set of
S
with respect to the set
\{{\mathbf{a}}_{1},\dots,{\mathbf{a}}_{d}\}
. Since
e(I)=e({\mathfrak{m}})
precisely when
I
is a reduction ideal of
{\mathfrak{m}}
, if
I
is a (minimal) reduction ideal of
{\mathfrak{m}}
, it is possible to interpret the multiplicity of
R
in the context of the associated semigroup.
We characterize the existence of the monomial minimal reduction of
{\mathfrak{m}}
by looking at the degrees of those generators of the semigroup that are not among the extremal rays of
S
, in Theorem
2.2
. Meanwhile, we also provide a bound for the reduction number in terms of rational coordinates of the generators of
S
.
We deepen the Cohen-Macaulay case, showing that, when
R
has minimal multiplicity, the monomial minimal reduction exists precisely when the set of non-zero Apéry elements of
S
with respect to the extremal rays coincide with the minimal generating set, in Proposition
2.5
. Applying these results to the case of affine semigroups in dimension 2, we provide a characterization of the existence of a monomial minimal reduction of
{\mathfrak{m}}
(Proposition
3.1
), an explicit formula for
R
to be Cohen-Macaulay (Proposition
3.4
),
and a formula for the multiplicity of
R
in terms of some Apéry sets of
S
(Proposition
3.7
).

In the case that
I=({\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{1}},\dots,{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{d}})
is a minimal reduction for
{\mathfrak{m}}
, two problems naturally arise: if it is possible to characterize the Cohen-Macaulay and Gorenstein properties for the associated graded ring, and if it is possible to determine a bound for
r_{I}({\mathfrak{m}})
, the reduction number of
{\mathfrak{m}}
with respect to
I
. As for the first problem, we give a complete answer in Theorem
4.4
and Proposition
4.11
. We also show that
r_{I}({\mathfrak{m}})
equals the maximum order of Apéry elements with respect to
\{{\mathbf{a}}_{1},\dots,{\mathbf{a}}_{d}\}
, when the associated graded ring is Cohen-Macaulay. The second problem is also motivated by the bound given in
[
11
]
, in the particular case of homogeneous affine simplicial semigroups. It is known that
r_{I}({\mathfrak{m}})\leq e(R)
,
[
19
]
. If
{\mathbb{K}}
has zero characteristic, in
[
21
]
Vasconcelos shows that
r_{I}({\mathfrak{m}})\leq e(R)-1
. For homogeneous affine simplicial semigroups,
I
is a reduction ideal of
{\mathfrak{m}}
and in
[
11
]
it is proved that
r_{I}({\mathfrak{m}})\leq e(R)-\operatorname{codim}(R)
. We confirm the same bound for simplicial affine semigroups with monomial minimal reduction and Cohen-Macaulay associated graded ring, in Proposition
4.6
. As Example
4.7
shows, the Cohen-Macaulay condition of
\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
can not be removed in this result. Finally we provide a formula for the Castelnouvo-Mumford regularity of
\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
, when
{\mathfrak{m}}
has monomial minimal reduction and
\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
is Cohen-Macaulay, in Proposition
4.13
.

1.
Preliminaries

We start by recalling some definitions and known results that we will use in the paper. For more details see
[
16
,
4
]
.
Let
S
be an affine semigroup fully embedded in
{\mathbb{N}}^{d}
, where
{\mathbb{N}}
denotes the set of non-negative integers and
d\in{\mathbb{N}}\setminus\{0\}
. Then,
S
has a unique minimal generating set
\{{\mathbf{a}}_{1},\dots,{\mathbf{a}}_{n}\}
. Let

\operatorname{cone}(S)=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}{\mathbf{a}}_{i}:\lambda_{i}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0},\text{ for }i=1,\dots,n\right\},

denote the rational polyhedral cone generated by
S
.
The
dimension
(or rank) of
S
is defined as the dimension of the affine subspace it generates, which is the same as the dimension of the subspace generated by
\operatorname{cone}(S)
. As
S
is fully embedded in
{\mathbb{N}}^{d}
, its dimension is
d
.
The
\operatorname{cone}(S)
is the intersection of finitely many closed linear half-spaces in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, each of whose bounding hyperplanes contains the origin. These half-spaces are called
support hyperplanes
.
For
d=1
,
\operatorname{cone}(S)={\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
, and for
d=2
the support hyperplanes are one-dimensional vector spaces which are called the
extremal rays
of
\operatorname{cone}(S)
. When
d>2
,
the intersection of any two adjacent support hyperplane is a one-dimensional vector space, which is said to be an
extremal ray
of
\operatorname{cone}(S)
. An element of
S
is called
extremal ray of
S
if it is the smallest non-zero vector of
S
in an extremal ray of
\operatorname{cone}(S)
. The
\operatorname{cone}(S)
has at least
d
extremal rays.
S
is called
simplicial
when
\operatorname{cone}(S)
has exactly
d
extremal rays. Throughout the paper,
S
is a simplicial affine semigroup and
E=\{{\mathbf{a}}_{1},\dots,{\mathbf{a}}_{d}\}
denotes the set of extremal rays of
S
. Then,
{\mathbf{a}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{a}}_{d}
are
{\mathbb{Q}}
-linearly independent and

S\subseteq\sum^{d}_{i=1}{\mathbb{Q}}_{\geq 0}{\mathbf{a}}_{i}.

Throughout the paper
S
is a simplicial affine semigroup minimally generated by
{\mathbf{a}}_{1},\dots,{\mathbf{a}}_{d+s}
, where
{\mathbf{a}}_{1},\dots,{\mathbf{a}}_{d}
are the extremal rays of
S
.
Given
{\mathbf{a}}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{d}\cap\operatorname{cone}(S)
, there exist unique rational numbers
l_{i}\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{\geq 0}
such that
{\mathbf{a}}=\sum^{d}_{i=1}l_{i}{\mathbf{a}}_{i}
. We denote
\deg({\mathbf{a}})=\sum^{d}_{i=1}l_{i}
.
Note that
\deg({\mathbf{a}})+\deg({\mathbf{b}})=\deg({\mathbf{a}}+{\mathbf{b}})
for all
{\mathbf{a}},{\mathbf{b}}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{d}\cap\operatorname{cone}(S)
. The hyperplane defined by
{\mathbf{a}}_{1},\dots,{\mathbf{a}}_{d}
contains an integral vector
{\mathbf{a}}\in\operatorname{cone}(S)
precisely when
\deg({\mathbf{a}})=1
. The set of integral vectors of the convex hull of the extremal rays together with the origin of coordinates consists of the integral vectors
{\mathbf{a}}\in\operatorname{cone}(S)
with
\deg({\mathbf{a}})\leq 1
.

For a field
{\mathbb{K}}
, the
semigroup ring

{\mathbb{K}}[S]
is the
{\mathbb{K}}
-subalgebra of the polynomial ring
{\mathbb{K}}[x_{1},\dots,x_{d}]
generated by the monomials with exponents in
S
.
Given
{\mathbf{a}}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{d})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{d}
, we set
{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}}=x_{1}^{a_{1}}x_{2}^{a_{2}}\cdots x_{d}^{a_{d}}
.

All over the paper,
R={\mathbb{K}}[S]
denote the semigroup ring
{\mathbb{K}}[{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{1}},\dots,{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{d+s}}]
. Consider the standard grading on the polynomial ring, where the degree of
x_{i}
is the vector
(0,0,\dots,0,1,0,\dots,0)
with size
d
and a unique
1
in the
i
-th entry. Then,
R
is a graded ring with the graded maximal ideal
{\mathfrak{m}}=({\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{1}},\dots,{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{d+s}})
.

A subset
H\subseteq S
is called an ideal of
S
, if
S+H\subseteq H
. The largest non-trivial ideal of
S
is the maximal ideal
M=S\setminus\{0\}
.
For any subset
H
of
S
, let
{\mathbb{K}}\{H\}
denote the
{\mathbb{K}}
-linear span of the monomials
{\mathbf{x}}^{\mathbf{a}}
with
{\mathbf{a}}\in H
. Then,
I
is a monomial ideal of
{\mathbb{K}}[S]
if and only if
I={\mathbb{K}}\{H\}
for some ideal
H
of
S
. Note that
{\mathfrak{m}}={\mathbb{K}}\{M\}
.

Let
H\subseteq F
be ideals of
S
.
By
[
8
, Lemma 1.9]
,
F\setminus H
is a finite set if and only if
\lambda_{R}({\mathbb{K}}\{F\}/{\mathbb{K}}\{H\})<\infty
, where
\lambda_{R}(-)
denotes the length of an
R
-module.
In this case,

(1)

|F\setminus H|=\dim_{\mathbb{K}}({\mathbb{K}}\{F\}/{\mathbb{K}}\{H\})=\lambda_{R}({\mathbb{K}}\{F\}/{\mathbb{K}}\{H\}).

Note that if
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
are ideals of
S
, then
H_{1}+H_{2}=\{h_{1}+h_{2}\,;\,h_{1}\in H_{1},h_{2}\in H_{2}\}
is
also an ideal of
S
. For a positive integer
n
and an ideal
H
, by
nH
we mean
H+(n-1)H
and
2H=H+H
.

For
{\mathbf{a}}\in S
,
the maximum integer
n
such that
nM
contains
{\mathbf{a}}
is called the order of
{\mathbf{a}}
in
S
and it is denoted by
\operatorname{ord}_{S}({\mathbf{a}})
. In other words,
{\mathbf{a}}\in nM\setminus(n+1)M
if
and only if
n=\operatorname{ord}_{S}({\mathbf{a}})
. So that
{\mathbf{a}}
may be written as
{\mathbf{a}}=\sum^{d+s}_{i=1}r_{i}{\mathbf{a}}_{i}
for some non-negative integers
r_{i}
such that
\sum^{d+s}_{i=1}r_{i}=n
. We call this representation a
maximal expression
of
{\mathbf{a}}
.

Let
I_{S}
denote the kernel of the
{\mathbb{K}}
-algebra homomorphism
\varphi:{\mathbb{K}}[z_{1},\dots,z_{d+s}]\mapsto{\mathbb{K}}[S]
, defined by
z_{i}\mapsto{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{i}}
, for
i=1,\dots,d+s
. Then, the
defining ideal

I_{S}
is a binomial prime ideal,
[
10
, Proposition 1.4]
.

Remark 1.1
.

It is easy to see that
\deg({\mathbf{a}}_{d+j})=1
for
j=1,\dots,s
if and only if all expressions of any element of
S
are maximal. In this case
I_{S}
is a homogeneous ideal and
R\cong\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
.

The
Apéry set
of
S
with respect to an element
{\mathbf{b}}\in S
is defined as
\operatorname{Ap}(S,{\mathbf{b}})=\{{\mathbf{a}}\in S\,;\,{\mathbf{a}}-{\mathbf{b}}\notin S\}
. Since
S\subseteq{\mathbb{N}}^{d}
, for
{\mathbf{b}}\neq{\mathbf{0}}
we have
{\mathbf{0}}\in\operatorname{Ap}(S,{\mathbf{b}})
, where
{\mathbf{0}}
denotes the zero vector of
{\mathbb{N}}^{d}
. We set

\operatorname{Ap}(S,E)=\{{\mathbf{a}}\in S\,;\,{\mathbf{a}}-{\mathbf{a}}_{i}\notin S,\text{ for }i=1,\dots,d\}=\cap^{d}_{i=1}\operatorname{Ap}(S,{\mathbf{a}}_{i}).

Moreover, let
\operatorname{Gr}(S)=\{{\mathbf{a}}-{\mathbf{b}}\,;\,{\mathbf{a}},{\mathbf{b}}\in S\}
be the
group of differences
of
S
, and let

P_{S}=\left\{{\mathbf{z}}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{d}\,;\,{\mathbf{z}}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}{\mathbf{a}}_{i}\text{ with }0\leq\lambda_{i}<1\text{ for }i=1,\dots,d\right\}.

Any element
{\mathbf{a}}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{d}\cap\operatorname{cone}(S)
is uniquely written as
{\mathbf{a}}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}n_{i}{\mathbf{a}}_{i}+\operatorname{rem}({\mathbf{a}})
, where
\operatorname{rem}({\mathbf{a}})\in P_{S}\cap{\mathbb{N}}^{d}
and
n_{1},\dots,n_{d}\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

The following lemma is an easy consequence of
[
15
, Lemma 1.4 and Corollary 1.6]
. For the convenience of the reader, we include this fact here.

Lemma 1.2
.

The following statements hold:

(a)

\operatorname{rem}(\operatorname{Ap}(S,E))=P_{S}\cap\operatorname{Gr}(S)
;

(b)

R
is Cohen-Macaulay if and only if the restriction of
\operatorname{rem}(-)
to
\operatorname{Ap}(S,E)
is an injective map.

Proof.

(a) If
{\mathbf{z}}=\operatorname{rem}({\mathbf{a}})
for some
{\mathbf{a}}\in\operatorname{Ap}(S,E)
, then
{\mathbf{z}}={\mathbf{a}}-\sum^{d}_{i=1}n_{i}{\mathbf{a}}_{i}
for some
n_{i}\geq 0
, which implies
{\mathbf{z}}\in\operatorname{Gr}(S)
. Now, let
{\mathbf{z}}\in P_{S}\cap\operatorname{Gr}(S)
. Then,
{\mathbf{z}}+{\mathbf{b}}\in S
for some
{\mathbf{b}}\in S
. Since
S
is simplicial,
n{\mathbf{b}}=\sum^{d}_{i=1}r_{i}{\mathbf{a}}_{i}
for some
r_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
and
n\gg 0
. Consequently,
{\mathbf{z}}+n{\mathbf{b}}={\mathbf{z}}+\sum^{d}_{i=1}r_{i}{\mathbf{a}}_{i}\in S
.
Thus
{\mathbf{z}}+\sum^{d}_{i=1}r_{i}{\mathbf{a}}_{i}={\mathbf{w}}+\sum^{d}_{i=1}s_{i}{\mathbf{a}}_{i}
for some
{\mathbf{w}}\in\operatorname{Ap}(S,E)
and
s_{1},\dots,s_{d}\in{\mathbb{N}}
. As
{\mathbf{z}}\in P_{S}
, we derive
{\mathbf{z}}=\operatorname{rem}({\mathbf{w}})
.

(b) Let
{\mathbf{w}}_{1},{\mathbf{w}}_{2}\in\operatorname{Ap}(S,E)
and
{\mathbf{w}}_{j}=\sum^{d}_{i=1}k_{i_{j}}{\mathbf{a}}_{i}+\operatorname{rem}({\mathbf{w}}_{j})
for
k_{i_{j}}\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
j=1,2
. Then,

{\mathbf{w}}_{1}-{\mathbf{w}}_{2}=\operatorname{rem}({\mathbf{w}}_{1})-\operatorname{rem}({\mathbf{w}}_{2})+\sum^{d}_{i=1}(k_{i_{1}}-k_{i_{2}}){\mathbf{a}}_{i}.

Note that
\sum^{d}_{i=1}(k_{i_{1}}-k_{i_{2}}){\mathbf{a}}_{i}\in\operatorname{Gr}({\mathbf{a}}_{1},\dots,{\mathbf{a}}_{d})
and
\operatorname{rem}({\mathbf{w}}_{1}),\operatorname{rem}({\mathbf{w}}_{2})\in P_{S}
. Thus,
{\mathbf{w}}_{1}-{\mathbf{w}}_{2}\in\operatorname{Gr}({\mathbf{a}}_{1},\dots,{\mathbf{a}}_{d})
if and only if
\operatorname{rem}({\mathbf{w}}_{1})=\operatorname{rem}({\mathbf{w}}_{2})
. The result follows by
[
15
, Corollary 1.6]
.
∎

Definition 1.3
.

An ideal
J\subseteq{\mathfrak{m}}
is called a
reduction ideal
of
{\mathfrak{m}}
if
{\mathfrak{m}}^{n+1}=J{\mathfrak{m}}^{n}
for
n\gg 0
.
A reduction ideal is said to be a
minimal reduction ideal
if it is minimal with respect to set inclusion.
If
J
is a minimal reduction ideal of
{\mathfrak{m}}
, the smallest positive integer
r
such that
{\mathfrak{m}}^{n+1}=J{\mathfrak{m}}^{n}
for
n\geq r
is called
reduction number
of
{\mathfrak{m}}
with respect to
J
and it is denoted by
r_{J}(R)
.

Lemma 1.4
.

The following statements hold.

(1)

If
({\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{1}},\dots,{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{d}})
is a reduction ideal of
{\mathfrak{m}}
, then it is a minimal reduction.

(2)

If
{\mathbb{K}}
is an infinite field, then the unique possible monomial minimal reduction ideal of
{\mathfrak{m}}
is the ideal
({\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{1}},\dots,{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{d}})
.

Proof.

Let
I
be a monomial reduction ideal of
{\mathfrak{m}}
. Since
I
is a monomial ideal and
{\mathfrak{m}}
is the unique monomial maximal ideal, it is easy to verify that
I
and
IR_{\mathfrak{m}}
have the same number of generators, and

IR_{\mathfrak{m}}
is also a reduction ideal of
{\mathfrak{m}}R_{\mathfrak{m}}
.
Thus, (1) follows by
[
18
, Corollary 8.3.6]
.
As
{\mathbf{a}}_{1},\dots,{\mathbf{a}}_{d}
are linearly independent,
{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{1}},\dots,{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{d}}
should belong to the generating set of
I
. If
{\mathbb{K}}
is infinite, then
[
18
, Proposition 8.3.7]
implies that
IR_{\mathfrak{m}}
is generated by
d
elements. Therefore,
I=({\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{1}},\dots,{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{d}})
.
∎

For an
{\mathfrak{m}}
-primary ideal
{\mathfrak{q}}
, let
e({\mathfrak{q}})
denote the

of
{\mathfrak{q}}
, i.e.

e({\mathfrak{q}})=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{d!}{n^{d}}\lambda_{R}(R/{\mathfrak{q}}^{n}).

We refer to

as the

and write

for it.

Lemma 1.5
.

Let
{\mathfrak{q}}
be a parameter ideal of
{\mathfrak{m}}
.

(a)

e({\mathfrak{q}})=e(R)
if and only if
{\mathfrak{q}}
is a reduction ideal of
{\mathfrak{m}}
.

(b)

R
is Cohen-Macaulay if and only if
e(({\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{1}},\dots,{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{d}}))=|\operatorname{Ap}(S,E)|
.

(c)

e(R)\leq|\operatorname{Ap}(S,E)|
.

Proof.

(a) is a consequence of
[
18
, Theorem 11.3.1]
and
[
18
, Corollary 1.2.5]
.
Note that
\lambda_{R}(R/({\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{1}},\dots,{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{d}}))=|S\setminus H|=|\operatorname{Ap}(S,E)|
, where
H=\{{\mathbf{a}}_{1},\dots,{\mathbf{a}}_{d}\}+S
. Thus, (b) follows by
[
13
, Theorem 17.11]
.

Let now
{\mathfrak{q}}=({\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{1}},\dots,{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{d}})
. Since
S
is simplicial,
{\mathfrak{q}}
provides a parameter ideal for
R
. As
\lambda_{R}(R/{\mathfrak{q}})=|\operatorname{Ap}(S,E)|
, the result follows by
[
13
, Theorem 14.10]
.
∎

.

The argument in the proof of

Proposition 1.7
.

If two of the following conditions hold, then the third one is also satisfied.

(a)

e(R)=|\operatorname{Ap}(S,E)|
.

(b)

({\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{1}},\dots,{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{d}})
is a minimal reduction ideal of
{\mathfrak{m}}
.

(c)

Let

If

2.
Reduction ideals of the maximal ideal

Let
c_{j}
denote the smallest positive integer with
c_{j}{\mathbf{a}}_{d+j}\in\sum^{d}_{i=1}{\mathbb{N}}{\mathbf{a}}_{i}
for
j=1,\dots,s
.
Then, for every such
j
, there are unique integers
l_{j_{1}},\dots,l_{j_{d}}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that

c_{j}{\mathbf{a}}_{d+j}=\sum^{d}_{i=1}l_{j_{i}}{\mathbf{a}}_{i}.

Note that
\deg({\mathbf{a}}_{d+j})=e_{j}/c_{j}
, where
e_{j}=l_{j_{1}}+\dots+l_{j_{d}}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

If
\deg({\mathbf{a}}_{d+j})\geq 1
for some
1\leq j\leq s
, then

{\mathbf{x}}^{n{\mathbf{a}}_{d+j}}\in({\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{1}},\dots,{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{d}}){\mathfrak{m}}^{n-1}

for
n\geq e_{j}
.

Proof.

Let
I=({\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{1}},\dots,{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{d}})
. For every
n\geq e_{j}\geq c_{j}
,

{\mathbf{x}}^{n{\mathbf{a}}_{d+j}}={\mathbf{x}}^{c_{j}{\mathbf{a}}_{d+j}}{\mathbf{x}}^{(n-c_{j}){\mathbf{a}}_{d+j}}=({\mathbf{x}}^{l_{j_{1}}{\mathbf{a}}_{1}}\cdots{\mathbf{x}}^{l_{j_{d}}{\mathbf{a}}_{d}}){\mathbf{x}}^{(n-c_{j}){\mathbf{a}}_{d+j}}\in I{\mathfrak{m}}^{t},

where
t=\sum^{d}_{i=1}l_{i_{j}}-1+n-c_{j}\geq n-1
. It follows that
{\mathbf{x}}^{n{\mathbf{a}}_{d+j}}\in I{\mathfrak{m}}^{n-1}
.
∎

In the following theorem we characterize when there exists a monomial minimal reduction ideal of
{\mathfrak{m}}
.

Theorem 2.2
.

The following statements are equivalent:

(a)

\deg({\mathbf{a}}_{d+j})\geq 1
, for every
j=1,\dots,s
;

(b)

The ideal
I=({\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{1}},\dots,{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{d}})
is a reduction of
{\mathfrak{m}}
;

If the above statements hold, the reduction number of
{\mathfrak{m}}
with respect to
I
is at most
sl-1
, where
l=\max\{e_{1},\dots,e_{s}\}
.

Proof.

(a)
\Rightarrow
(b) We may assume that
s\geq 1
, otherwise the claim is trivial. Let
n\geq\max\{e_{1},\dots,e_{s}\}
. Then,
{\mathbf{x}}^{n{\mathbf{a}}_{d+j}}\in I{\mathfrak{m}}^{n-1}
by Lemma
2.1
.
For every monomial
{\mathbf{u}}={\mathbf{x}}^{n_{1}{\mathbf{a}}_{d+1}}\cdots{\mathbf{x}}^{n_{s}{\mathbf{a}}_{d+s}}\in{\mathfrak{m}}^{sn}
there is at least one index
j
such that
n_{j}\geq n
. Therefore,
{\mathbf{u}}\in{\mathbf{x}}^{n{\mathbf{a}}_{d+j}}{\mathfrak{m}}^{(s-1)n}\subseteq I{\mathfrak{m}}^{n-1}{\mathfrak{m}}^{(s-1)n}\subseteq I{\mathfrak{m}}^{sn-1}
and this is enough to show that
{\mathfrak{m}}^{rn}\subseteq I{\mathfrak{m}}^{rn-1}
.
Hence,
I
is a reduction ideal of
{\mathfrak{m}}
.
This also proves the last statement, when (a) holds.

(b)
\Rightarrow
(a)
Fix
t
such that
\deg({\mathbf{a}}_{d+t})=\min\{\deg({\mathbf{a}}_{d+j})\,;\,j=1,\dots,s\}
. If
n
is large enough, (b) implies that

{\mathbf{x}}^{(n+1){\mathbf{a}}_{d+t}}={\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{j}}{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{u}}_{1}+\dots+{\mathbf{u}}_{n}},

for some
1\leq j\leq d
and
{\mathbf{u}}_{1},\dots,{\mathbf{u}}_{n}\in S
. Consequently,

(n+1)\deg({\mathbf{a}}_{d+t})=1+\sum^{n}_{i=1}\deg({\mathbf{u}}_{i})\geq 1+n\min\{1,\deg({\mathbf{a}}_{d+t})\},

which implies
\deg({\mathbf{a}}_{d+t})\geq 1
.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

Denote by
{\mathbb{D}}
the hyperplane passing through the extremal rays
{\mathbf{a}}_{1},\dots,{\mathbf{a}}_{d}
, and by
{\mathbb{P}}
the convex hull of the set
\{{\mathbf{0}},{\mathbf{a}}_{1},\dots,{\mathbf{a}}_{d}\}
. As a geometrical interpretation, Theorem
2.2
states that
({\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{1}},\dots,{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{d}})
is a monomial reduction ideal of
{\mathfrak{m}}
precisely when
{\mathbb{P}}\cap\{{\mathbf{a}}_{d+1},\dots,{\mathbf{a}}_{d+s}\}={\mathbb{D}}\cap\{{\mathbf{a}}_{d+1},\dots,{\mathbf{a}}_{d+s}\}
. In other words,
{\mathbf{a}}_{i}
is located on or above
{\mathbb{D}}
, for
i=d+1,\dots,d+s
.

The following example shows that the bound for the reduction number found in Theorem
2.2
is sharp.

Example 2.4
.

Let
S\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{2}
be the affine semigroup generated by
{\mathbf{a}}_{1}=(3,1),{\mathbf{a}}_{2}=(0,4),{\mathbf{a}}_{3}=(2,2)
, where
{\mathbf{a}}_{1}
and
{\mathbf{a}}_{2}
are the extremal rays. Then,
{\mathbf{a}}_{3}=2/3{\mathbf{a}}_{1}+1/3{\mathbf{a}}_{2}
and
\deg({\mathbf{a}}_{3})=1
.
Therefore,
({\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{1}},{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{2}})
is a minimal reduction ideal of
{\mathfrak{m}}=({\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{1}},{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{2}},{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{3}})
.
Moreover, in this case
e_{3}=3
and the reduction number is
2
, which confirms the sharpness of the bound given in Theorem
2.2
.

If
R
is Cohen-Macaulay, then
e(R)\geq{\rm edim}(R)-\dim(R)+1=d+\operatorname{codim}(R)-d+1=\operatorname{codim}(R)+1
, where
{\rm edim}(R)
is the embedding dimension of
R
and
\operatorname{codim}(R)=d-{\rm edim}(R)=s
, see
[
1
]
. If the equality holds, then
R
is said to be a Cohen-Macaulay ring of minimal multiplicity.

Proposition 2.5
.

If
R
is Cohen-Macaulay, the following statements are equivalent:

(a)

\operatorname{Ap}(S,E)
has
1+\operatorname{codim}(R)
elements;

(b)

({\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{1}},\dots,{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{d}})
is a reduction ideal of
{\mathfrak{m}}
and
R
is of minimal multiplicity, i.e.,
e(R)=1+\operatorname{codim}(R)
;

(c)

({\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{1}},\dots,{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{d}}){\mathfrak{m}}={\mathfrak{m}}^{2}
.

Proof.

(a)
\Rightarrow
(b) By assumption, we have
\operatorname{Ap}(S,E)=\{{\mathbf{0}},{\mathbf{a}}_{d+1},\dots,{\mathbf{a}}_{d+s}\}
. Let
{\mathbf{a}}_{d+t}
be such that
\deg({\mathbf{a}}_{d+t})=\min\{\deg({\mathbf{a}}_{d+j})\,;\,1\leq j\leq s\}
. Since
2{\mathbf{a}}_{d+t}\notin\cap_{i=1}^{d}\operatorname{Ap}(S,{\mathbf{a}}_{i})
, we have
2{\mathbf{a}}_{d+t}={\mathbf{a}}_{i}+{\mathbf{b}}
, for some
{\mathbf{b}}\in S
and some
i
. Then,
2\deg({\mathbf{a}}_{d+t})=1+\deg({\mathbf{b}})\geq 1+\min\{1,\deg({\mathbf{a}}_{d+t})\}
, which implies
\deg({\mathbf{a}}_{d+t})\geq 1
. Therefore,
({\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{1}},\dots,{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{d}})
is a minimal reduction ideal of
{\mathfrak{m}}
, by Proposition
2.2
. The equality
e(R)=1+\operatorname{codim}(R)
follows by Proposition
1.7
.

(b)
\Rightarrow
(c) By Lemma
1.4
, the ideal
({\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{1}},\dots,{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{d}})
is a minimal reduction ideal of
{\mathfrak{m}}
. Since
e(R)=|\operatorname{Ap}(S,E)|
by Proposition
1.7
, we have
|\operatorname{Ap}(S,E)|=1+\operatorname{codim}(R)
, which is
\operatorname{Ap}(S,E)=\{{\mathbf{0}},{\mathbf{a}}_{d+1},\dots,{\mathbf{a}}_{d+s}\}
. In particular, for any
{\mathbf{x}}^{\mathbf{a}}\in{\mathfrak{m}}^{2}
, we have
{\mathbf{a}}\notin\operatorname{Ap}(S,E)
, which means
{\mathbf{a}}={\mathbf{a}}_{i}+{\mathbf{b}}
for
1\geq i\geq d
and some
{\mathbf{b}}\in S\setminus\{0\}
. Therefore,
{\mathbf{x}}^{\mathbf{a}}\in({\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{1}},\dots,{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{d}})\mathfrak{m}
.

(c)
\Rightarrow
(a) Clearly,
\operatorname{Ap}(S,E)=\{{\mathbf{0}},{\mathbf{a}}_{d+1},\dots,{\mathbf{a}}_{d+s}\}
.
∎

In Theorem
2.2
we have seen that if
\deg({\mathbf{a}}_{d+j})\geq 1
for every
j=1,\dots,s
, then
(x^{{\mathbf{a}}_{1}},\dots,{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{d}})
is a minimal reduction ideal of
{\mathfrak{m}}
. On the other hand, if
\deg({\mathbf{a}}_{d+j})<1
for

and
\deg({\mathbf{a}}_{d+j})\geq 1
for

, the same proof shows that

is a reduction ideal of

, but it is never minimal if

. We conclude this section by providing a better reduction ideal in this case, indeed the ideal that we find is always contained in

Lemma 2.6
.

If
1\leq j\leq s
, then
{\mathbf{x}}^{e_{j}{\mathbf{a}}_{i}}\in({\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{1}}-{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{2}},\dots,{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{1}}-{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{d}},{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{d+j}}){\mathfrak{m}}^{r_{j}-1}
for every
1\leq i\leq d
, where
r_{j}=\min\{e_{j},c_{j}\}
.

Proof.

Let
I=({\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{1}}-{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{2}},\dots,{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{1}}-{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{d}},{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{d+j}})
. Since
{\mathbf{x}}^{e_{j}{\mathbf{a}}_{1}}-{\mathbf{x}}^{e_{j}{\mathbf{a}}_{i}}\in I{\mathfrak{m}}^{e_{j}-1}
for every
i
, it is enough to show that
{\mathbf{x}}^{e_{j}{\mathbf{a}}_{1}}\in I{\mathfrak{m}}^{r_{j}-1}
.
We recall that
c_{j}{\mathbf{a}}_{d+j}=\sum^{d}_{i=1}l_{j_{i}}{\mathbf{a}}_{i}
and
e_{j}=\sum^{d}_{i=1}l_{j_{i}}
. We may write

\displaystyle{\mathbf{x}}^{e_{j}{\mathbf{a}}_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathbf{x}}^{(e_{j}-l_{j_{2}}){\mathbf{a}}_{1}}({\mathbf{x}}^{l_{j_{2}}{\mathbf{a}}_{1}}-{\mathbf{x}}^{l_{j_{2}}{\mathbf{a}}_{2}})+{\mathbf{x}}^{(e_{j}-l_{j_{2}}){\mathbf{a}}_{1}}{\mathbf{x}}^{l_{j_{2}}{\mathbf{a}}_{2}}=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathbf{x}}^{(e_{j}-l_{j_{2}}){\mathbf{a}}_{1}}({\mathbf{x}}^{l_{j_{2}}{\mathbf{a}}_{1}}-{\mathbf{x}}^{l_{j_{2}}{\mathbf{a}}_{2}})+{\mathbf{x}}^{(e_{j}-l_{j_{2}}-l_{j_{3}}){\mathbf{a}}_{1}}{\mathbf{x}}^{l_{j_{2}}{\mathbf{a}}_{2}}({\mathbf{x}}^{l_{j_{3}}{\mathbf{a}}_{1}}-{\mathbf{x}}^{l_{j_{3}}{\mathbf{a}}_{3}})+

\displaystyle+\ {\mathbf{x}}^{(e_{j}-l_{j_{2}}-l_{j_{3}}){\mathbf{a}}_{1}}{\mathbf{x}}^{l_{j_{2}}{\mathbf{a}}_{2}}{\mathbf{x}}^{l_{j_{3}}{\mathbf{a}}_{3}}=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=2}^{d}{\mathbf{x}}^{(e_{j}-\sum_{t=2}^{i}l_{j_{t}}){\mathbf{a}}_{1}}{\mathbf{x}}^{l_{j_{2}}{\mathbf{a}}_{2}}\cdots{\mathbf{x}}^{l_{j_{i-1}}{\mathbf{a}}_{i-1}}({\mathbf{x}}^{l_{j_{i}}{\mathbf{a}}_{1}}-{\mathbf{x}}^{l_{j_{i}}{\mathbf{a}}_{i}})+{\mathbf{x}}^{l_{j_{1}}{\mathbf{a}}_{1}}\cdots{\mathbf{x}}^{l_{j_{d}}{\mathbf{a}}_{d}},

which easily implies the thesis.
∎

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
T=\{{\mathbf{a}}_{d+j_{1}},\dots,{\mathbf{a}}_{d+j_{l}}\}
be a non-empty set such that

\{{\mathbf{a}}_{d+j}\,;\,\deg({\mathbf{a}}_{d+j})<1\}\subseteq T\subseteq\{{\mathbf{a}}_{d+j}\,;\,\deg({\mathbf{a}}_{d+j})\leq 1\}.

Then,
J=({\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{1}}-{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{2}},\dots,{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{1}}-{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{d}},{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{d+j_{1}}},\dots,{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{d+j_{l}}})
is a reduction ideal for
{\mathfrak{m}}
.

Proof.

Let
n=\max\{e_{1},\dots,e_{s}\}
. For any
l
such that
{\mathbf{a}}_{d+l}\in T
and any
i=1,\ldots,d
, Lemma
2.6
implies that

{\mathbf{x}}^{n{\mathbf{a}}_{i}}={\mathbf{x}}^{(n-e_{s}){\mathbf{a}}_{i}}{\mathbf{x}}^{e_{s}{\mathbf{a}}_{i}}\in{\mathbf{x}}^{(n-e_{s}){\mathbf{a}}_{i}}J{\mathfrak{m}}^{e_{s}-1}\subseteq J{\mathfrak{m}}^{n-1}.

Fix
j
such that
{\mathbf{a}}_{d+j}\notin T
. By Lemma
2.1
, it follows
{\mathbf{x}}^{n{\mathbf{a}}_{d+j}}\in({\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{1}},\dots,{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{d}}){\mathfrak{m}}^{n-1}
and, then,
{\mathbf{x}}^{n{\mathbf{a}}_{d+j}}={\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{i}}{\mathbf{x}}^{\mathbf{u}}
, for some
1\leq i\leq d
and
{\mathbf{x}}^{\mathbf{u}}\in{\mathfrak{m}}^{n-1}
.
Therefore,

{\mathbf{x}}^{n^{2}{\mathbf{a}}_{d+j}}={\mathbf{x}}^{n{\mathbf{a}}_{i}}{\mathbf{x}}^{n{\mathbf{u}}}\in J{\mathfrak{m}}^{n-1}{\mathfrak{m}}^{n^{2}-n}\subseteq J{\mathfrak{m}}^{n^{2}-1}.

Consequently, it is straightforward to see that every monomial in
{\mathfrak{m}}^{p}
is in

, for

3.
Monomial reduction and multiplicity in dimension two

The multiplicity of a projective toric variety can be identified by the Euclidean volume of its convex hull, see for instance
[
17
, Theorem 4.16]
.
In this section, for affine semigroups
S\subset{\mathbb{N}}^{2}
, we use a similar idea computing the volume of
P_{S}
, to provide an explicit formula for the multiplicity of
R
in terms of some Apéry sets and an upper bound in terms of the coordinates of the extremal rays. Firstly, the criteria for having monomial minimal reduction ideal are made more concrete for affine semigroups of dimension two.
Throughout the section,
S
denotes an affine semigroup contained in
{\mathbb{N}}^{2}
minimally generated by
{\mathbf{a}}_{i}=(c_{i},d_{i})
for
i=1,\dots,s+2
, where
{\mathbf{a}}_{1}
and
{\mathbf{a}}_{2}
are the extremal rays. We recall that every 2-dimensional affine semigroup is simplicial.

The following result gives another criterion to see whether
{\mathfrak{m}}
has a monomial reduction.

Proposition 3.1
.

The following statements are equivalent, where
\lambda=\frac{d_{1}-d_{2}}{c_{1}-c_{2}}
.

(a)

({\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{1}},{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{2}})
is a reduction ideal of
{\mathfrak{m}}
.

(b)

d_{i}\geq\lambda(c_{i}-c_{1})+d_{1}
, for
i=3,\dots,s+2
.

(c)

d_{i}\geq\lambda(c_{i}-c_{2})+d_{2}
, for
i=3,\dots,s+2
.

Proof.

Denote by
{\mathbb{D}}
the line passing through the extremal rays
{\mathbf{a}}_{1},{\mathbf{a}}_{2}
. By Remark
2.3
, the statement (a) is equivalent to have all
{\mathbf{a}}_{i}
located on or above the line
{\mathbb{D}}
for
i=3,\dots,s+2
. The result follows by the fact that
{\mathbb{D}}
has the equation

\begin{split}y&=\frac{d_{1}-d_{2}}{c_{1}-c_{2}}(x-c_{1})+d_{1}\\
&=\frac{d_{1}-d_{2}}{c_{1}-c_{2}}(x-c_{2})+d_{2}.\qed\end{split}

Proposition 3.2
.

If
({\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{1}},{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{2}})
is a reduction ideal of
{\mathfrak{m}}
, then
c_{i}\geq\min\{c_{1},c_{2}\}
and

d_{i}\geq\min\{d_{1},d_{2}\}
, for
i=3,\dots,s+2
.

Proof.

Let
{\mathbf{a}}_{i}=l_{i_{1}}{\mathbf{a}}_{1}+l_{i_{2}}{\mathbf{a}}_{2}
, where
l_{i_{1}},l_{i_{2}}\in{\mathbb{Q}}
, for
i=3,\dots,s+2
. By
Theorem
2.2
, we have
l_{i_{1}}+l_{i_{2}}\geq 1
, for
i=3,\dots,s+2
.
Thus, the result follows from the inequalities

\min\{c_{1},c_{2}\}(l_{i_{1}}+l_{i_{2}})\leq l_{i_{1}}c_{1}+l_{i_{2}}c_{2}=c_{i}

and

\min\{d_{1},d_{2}\}(l_{i_{1}}+l_{i_{2}})\leq l_{i_{1}}d_{1}+l_{i_{2}}d_{2}=d_{i}.\qed

As the following example shows, the converse of Proposition
3.2
is not true.

Example 3.3
.

Let
S
be generated by
{\mathbf{a}}_{1}=(4,1)
,
{\mathbf{a}}_{2}=(1,3)
and
{\mathbf{a}}_{3}=(2,2)
. Then,
c_{3}=d_{3}>\min\{c_{1},c_{2}\}=\min\{d_{1},d_{2}\}=1
. On the other hand,
{\mathbf{a}}_{3}=\frac{4}{11}{\mathbf{a}}_{1}+\frac{6}{11}{\mathbf{a}}_{2}
and
\deg({\mathbf{a}}_{3})=\frac{10}{11}<1
, thus,
({\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{1}},{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{2}})
is not a reduction of
{\mathfrak{m}}
by Theorem
2.2
.

In order to give a concrete upper bound for the multiplicity of
R
in the Cohen-Macaulay case, we prove a more general result.

Proposition 3.4
.

The ring
R
is Cohen-Macaulay if and only if

|\operatorname{Ap}(S,E)|+|P_{S}\setminus\operatorname{Gr}(S)|=|c_{1}d_{2}-c_{2}d_{1}|.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
c_{1}d_{2}>c_{2}d_{1}
. The area of the parallelogram spanned by
{\mathbf{a}}_{1}
and
{\mathbf{a}}_{2}
equals

\det\begin{pmatrix}c_{1}&c_{2}\\
d_{1}&d_{2}\end{pmatrix}=c_{1}d_{2}-c_{2}d_{1}
.
If we define

e_{i}=\begin{cases}d_{i}\hskip 35.0pt&\text{if }c_{i}=0\\
c_{i}&\text{if }d_{i}=0\\
\gcd(c_{i},d_{i})&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

for
i=1,2
, the boundary of that parallelogram contains precisely
2(e_{1}+e_{2})
lattice points. Then, Pick’s theorem (
[
2
, Theorem 2.8]
) implies that
P_{S}
has
c_{1}d_{2}-c_{2}d_{1}-(e_{1}+e_{2})+1
inner lattice points.
Given
{\mathbf{a}}\in P_{S}
, then
{\mathbf{a}}
never equals any of the points

\{{\mathbf{a}}_{1}+{\mathbf{a}}_{2},{\mathbf{a}}_{1}+\frac{b_{2}}{e_{2}}{\mathbf{a}}_{2},\frac{b_{1}}{e_{1}}{\mathbf{a}}_{1}+{\mathbf{a}}_{2}\text{, where }0\leq b_{i}<e_{i}\}

on the boundary of the mentioned parallelogram. Consequently, the points of
P_{S}
belong to the union of inner lattice points and the boundary ones except
e_{1}+e_{2}+1
points.
Therefore, on the one hand Lemma
1.2
(a) implies that
|P_{S}|=|P_{S}\cap{\rm Gr}(S)|+|P_{S}\setminus{\rm Gr}(S)|=|r(\operatorname{Ap}(S,E))|+|P_{S}\setminus{\rm Gr}(S)|
, but on the other hand we have

\displaystyle|P_{S}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(c_{1}d_{2}-c_{2}d_{1}-(e_{1}+e_{2})+1)+2(e_{1}+e_{2})-(e_{1}+e_{2}+1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{1}d_{2}-c_{2}d_{1}.

It is now enough to recall that
R
is Cohen-Macaulay if and only if
|r(\operatorname{Ap}(S,E))|=|\operatorname{Ap}(S,E)|
, by Lemma
1.2
(b).
∎

1.5

Proposition 3.7

1.9
. Let

m_{j}=\min\{m\,;\,\operatorname{ord}_{S}({\mathbf{w}}_{j}+m{\mathbf{a}}_{2})\geq r\},

for
j=1,\dots,t.

If
\operatorname{ord}_{S}({\mathbf{w}}_{j}+m_{j}{\mathbf{a}}_{2})=r^{\prime}>r
, then
m_{j}>0
,
{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{w}}_{j}+m_{j}{\mathbf{a}}_{2}}\in{\mathfrak{m}}^{r^{\prime}}=({\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{1}},{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{2}}){\mathfrak{m}}^{r^{\prime}-1}
and, thus,

{\mathbf{w}}_{j}+m_{j}{\mathbf{a}}_{2}={\mathbf{w}}+{\mathbf{a}}_{i}
for some
{\mathbf{w}}\in S
with
\operatorname{ord}_{S}({\mathbf{w}})=r^{\prime}-1
and
1\leq i\leq 2
. Since
\operatorname{ord}_{S}({\mathbf{w}}_{j}+(m_{j}-1){\mathbf{a}}_{2})<r
, we get
i=1
. Consequently
{\mathbf{w}}_{j}+m_{j}{\mathbf{a}}_{2}\notin\operatorname{Ap}(S,{\mathbf{a}}_{1})
. Note that any element in
\operatorname{Ap}(S,{\mathbf{a}}_{1})
is uniquely written as
{\mathbf{w}}+m{\mathbf{a}}_{2}
, for some
{\mathbf{w}}\in\operatorname{Ap}(S,E)
and
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Therefore,

\{{\mathbf{w}}_{j}+m_{j}{\mathbf{a}}_{2}\,;\,j=1,\dots,t\}\cap\operatorname{Ap}(S,{\mathbf{a}}_{1})=\{{\mathbf{w}}\in\operatorname{Ap}(S,{\mathbf{a}}_{1})\,;\,\operatorname{ord}_{S}({\mathbf{w}})=r\}.

Let
R^{\prime}=R/({\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{1}})R
and let
\mathfrak{n}
be its maximal ideal. Then,
e(R)=e(R^{\prime})
, by
[
18
, Proposition 11.1.9]
.
Since
({\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{1}},{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{2}})
is a minimal reduction ideal of
{\mathfrak{m}}
, we derive that
({\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{2}})R^{\prime}
is a minimal reduction ideal of
\mathfrak{n}
with reduction number smaller than or equal to
r
.
As
\dim(R^{\prime})=1
, its multiplicity is equal to
|{\mathfrak{n}}^{k}/{\mathfrak{n}}^{k-1}|
for all

. In other words,

e(R)=|\{{\mathbf{w}}\in\operatorname{Ap}(S,{\mathbf{a}}_{1})\,;\,\operatorname{ord}_{S}({\mathbf{w}})=k\}|,

for all
k\geq r
.
It follows that

\{{\mathbf{w}}\in\operatorname{Ap}(S,{\mathbf{a}}_{1})\,;\,\operatorname{ord}_{S}({\mathbf{w}})=k\}=\{{\mathbf{w}}_{j}+(m_{j}+k-r){\mathbf{a}}_{2}\,;\,j=1,\dots,t\}\cap\operatorname{Ap}(S,{\mathbf{a}}_{1}),

has
e(R)
elements. Implicitly, this means that
{\mathbf{w}}_{j}+m_{j}{\mathbf{a}}_{2}\in\operatorname{Ap}(S,{\mathbf{a}}_{1})
if and only if
{\mathbf{w}}_{j}+k{\mathbf{a}}_{2}\in\operatorname{Ap}(S,{\mathbf{a}}_{1})
for

. Thus,
e(R)=|\operatorname{Ap}(S,E)|-|A_{k}|=|\operatorname{Ap}(S,E)|-|A_{r}|
.
The last statement follows by Proposition
1.7
. ∎

4.
The associated graded ring

In this section we explore the properties of the associated graded ring
\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)=\oplus_{i=0}^{\infty}\,{\mathfrak{m}}^{i}/{\mathfrak{m}}^{i+1}
of
R
assuming that
{\mathfrak{m}}
has a monomial minimal reduction ideal.
We first recall a result which will be useful in what follows. There, given
f\in R
, we denote its image in
\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
by
f^{*}
, whereas if
J
is an ideal of
R
, we set
\operatorname{in}(J)=\{f^{*}\,;\,f\in J\}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

[
22
, Proposition 3.8]

Let
f_{1},\dots,f_{d}\in{\mathfrak{m}}
. Then,
(f_{1},\dots,f_{d})
is a minimal reduction ideal of
{\mathfrak{m}}
if and only if
(f_{1}^{*},\dots,f_{d}^{*})
is a homogeneous system of parameters of
\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
.

Our first aim is to characterize the Cohen-Macaulay property of
\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
. This is a generalization of
[
7
, Theorem 7]
, where the case
d=1
is considered.

Proposition 4.2
.

If
({\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{1}},\dots,{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{d}})
is a reduction ideal of
{\mathfrak{m}}
, then the following statements are equivalent:

(a)

\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
is a Cohen-Macaulay ring;

(b)

({\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{1}})^{*},\dots,({\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{d}})^{*}
provide a regular sequence in
\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
;

(c)

R
is Cohen-Macaulay and
({\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{1}},\dots,{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{d}})\cap{\mathfrak{m}}^{n}=({\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{1}},\dots,{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{d}}){\mathfrak{m}}^{n-1}
, for all
n\geq 1
;

(d)

R
is Cohen-Macaulay and
({\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{i}})^{*}
is a non-zero-divisor in
\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
, for
i=1,\dots,d
.

Proof.

By Lemma
1.4
, the monomial ideal
({\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{1}},\dots,{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{d}})
is a minimal reduction ideal of
{\mathfrak{m}}
.
If
R
is Cohen-Macaulay, then the system of parameters
{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{1}},\dots,{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{d}}
is a regular sequence in
R
.

(a)
\Leftrightarrow
(b) Proposition
4.1
implies that the image of
{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{1}},\dots,{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{d}}
in
\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
is a system of parameters. Therefore,
\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
is Cohen-Macaulay if and only if
({\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{1}})^{*},\dots,({\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{d}})^{*}
provide a regular sequence in
\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
.

(b)
\Leftrightarrow
(c)
Since
0pt(\operatorname{gr}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}(R))\leq 0pt(R)
, (b) implies that
R
is Cohen-Macaulay. Thus, the system of parameters
{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{1}},\dots,{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{d}}
is a regular sequence in
R
. Now, the equivalence between (b) and (c) follows by
Valabrega-Valla criterion
[
20
]
(see also
[
22
, Theorem 5.16]
).

(d)
\Rightarrow
(c) By (d) we have

(2)

{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{i}}({\mathfrak{m}}^{t-1}\setminus{\mathfrak{m}}^{t})\subseteq{\mathfrak{m}}^{t}\setminus{\mathfrak{m}}^{t+1},

for all
t\geq 1
and
1\leq i\leq d
. Let
{\mathbf{x}}^{\mathbf{c}}\in({\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{1}},\dots,{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{d}})\cap{\mathfrak{m}}^{n}
, for some
n\geq 1
. Then,
{\mathbf{c}}={\mathbf{a}}_{i}+{\mathbf{b}}
where
{\mathbf{b}}\in S
and
1\leq i\leq d
. Let
\operatorname{ord}_{S}({\mathbf{b}})=t-1
. Then,
{\mathbf{x}}^{\mathbf{c}}={\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{i}+{\mathbf{b}}}\in{\mathfrak{m}}^{t}\setminus{\mathfrak{m}}^{t+1}
by (
2
). As
{\mathbf{x}}^{c}\in{\mathfrak{m}}^{n}
, it follows that
n\leq t
and, equivalently,
{\mathbf{x}}^{\mathbf{b}}\in{\mathfrak{m}}^{n-1}
. Thus,
{\mathbf{x}}^{c}\in({\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{1}},\dots,{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{d}}){\mathfrak{m}}^{n-1}
and we get (c).

(b)
\Rightarrow
(d) It is clear.
∎

We now prove a lemma that will be useful to show other characterizations of the Cohen-Macaulay property of
\operatorname{gr}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}(R)
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
r=\max\{\operatorname{ord}_{S}({\mathbf{w}})\,;\,{\mathbf{w}}\in\operatorname{Ap}(S,E)\}
. If
\operatorname{ord}_{S}({\mathbf{a}}_{i}+{\mathbf{b}})=\operatorname{ord}_{S}({\mathbf{b}})+1
, for all and
i=1,\dots,d
and
{\mathbf{b}}\in S
with
\operatorname{ord}_{S}({\mathbf{b}})<r
, then
r=r_{I}(R)
.

Proof.

Given
{\mathbf{x}}^{\mathbf{a}}\in{\mathfrak{m}}^{r+1}
, we have
\operatorname{ord}_{S}({\mathbf{a}})\geq r+1
and, then,
{\mathbf{a}}={\mathbf{a}}_{i}+{\mathbf{b}}
for some
1\leq i\leq d
and
{\mathbf{b}}\in S
. By assumption, it is not possible that
\operatorname{ord}_{S}({\mathbf{b}})<r
. Therefore,
{\mathbf{x}}^{\mathbf{b}}\in{\mathfrak{m}}^{r}
and, consequently,
{\mathbf{x}}^{\mathbf{a}}\in I{\mathfrak{m}}^{r}
. This shows that
r_{I}(R)\leq r
and the equality follows by Remark
1.9
.
∎

The following theorem generalizes some results proved in
[
7
]
about numerical semigroup rings.

Theorem 4.4
.

The following statements are equivalent:

(a)

\operatorname{gr}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}(R)
is Cohen-Macaulay and
({\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{1}},\dots,{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{d}})
is a reduction ideal of
{\mathfrak{m}}
;

(b)

R
is Cohen-Macaulay and
\operatorname{ord}_{S}({\mathbf{b}}+{\mathbf{a}}_{i})=\operatorname{ord}_{S}({\mathbf{b}})+1
, for all
{\mathbf{b}}\in S
and
i=1,\dots,d
;

(c)

R
is Cohen-Macaulay and
\operatorname{ord}_{S}({\mathbf{b}}+\sum^{d}_{i=1}n_{i}{\mathbf{a}}_{i})=\operatorname{ord}_{S}({\mathbf{b}})+\sum^{d}_{i=1}n_{i}
, for all
{\mathbf{b}}\in\operatorname{Ap}(S,E)
and
n_{1},\dots,n_{d}\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

In this case, the reduction number is equal to
\max\{\operatorname{ord}_{S}({\mathbf{b}})\,;\,{\mathbf{b}}\in\operatorname{Ap}(S,E)\}
.

Proof.

(a)
\Rightarrow
(b) It follows by Proposition
4.2
.

(b)
\Rightarrow
(c) This is clear.

(c)
\Rightarrow
(b) Any element
{\mathbf{b}}\in S
may be written as
{\mathbf{b}}=\sum^{d}_{j=1}n_{j}{\mathbf{a}}_{j}+{\mathbf{b}}^{\prime}
for some
{\mathbf{b}}^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Ap}(S,E)
and
n_{1},\dots,n_{d}\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Let
1\leq i\leq d
. Then,

{\mathbf{b}}+{\mathbf{a}}_{i}=\sum^{i-1}_{j=1}n_{j}{\mathbf{a}}_{j}+(1+n_{i}){\mathbf{a}}_{i}+\sum^{d}_{j=i+1}n_{j}{\mathbf{a}}_{j}+{\mathbf{b}}^{\prime}.

Therefore,
\operatorname{ord}_{S}({\mathbf{b}}+{\mathbf{a}}_{i})=1+\sum^{d}_{j=1}n_{j}+\operatorname{ord}_{S}({\mathbf{b}}^{\prime})=1+\operatorname{ord}_{S}({\mathbf{b}})
.

(b)
\Rightarrow
(a)
Let
c_{j}
denote the smallest non-zero integer such that

c_{j}{\mathbf{a}}_{d+j}\in\sum^{d}_{i=1}{\mathbb{N}}{\mathbf{a}}_{i}

for
j=1,\dots,r
.
Then, there are unique integers
l_{j_{1}},\dots,l_{j_{d}}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that

c_{j}{\mathbf{a}}_{d+j}=\sum^{d}_{i=1}l_{j_{i}}{\mathbf{a}}_{i}.

Then,
c_{j}\leq\operatorname{ord}_{S}(c_{j}{\mathbf{a}}_{d+j})=\operatorname{ord}_{S}(\sum^{d}_{i=1}l_{j_{i}}{\mathbf{a}}_{i})=\sum^{d}_{i=1}l_{j_{i}},

where the last equality holds by (b). Now,
\deg({\mathbf{a}}_{d+j})=(\sum^{d}_{i=1}l_{j_{i}})/c_{j}\geq 1
, which means that
({\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{1}},\dots,{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{d}})
is a reduction ideal of
{\mathfrak{m}}
by Theorem
2.2
. Moreover,
\operatorname{gr}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}(R)
is Cohen-Macaulay by the previous lemma.
The last statement follows by Lemma
4.3
.
∎

Example 4.5
.

Let
S
be generated by
{\mathbf{a}}_{1}=(6,0),{\mathbf{a}}_{2}=(0,4),{\mathbf{a}}_{3}=(3,3)
, and
{\mathbf{a}}_{4}=(3,9)
. Then,
\deg({\mathbf{a}}_{i})\geq 1
for
i=3,4
, which shows that
{\mathfrak{m}}
has a monomial minimal reduction ideal, by Theorem
4.4
.
As
\operatorname{Ap}(S,E)=\{(0,0),(3,3),(3,9),(6,6)\}
,
{\mathbb{K}}[S]
is Cohen-Macaulay by Lemma
1.2
. However, since the order of
{\mathbf{a}}_{4}+{\mathbf{a}}_{1}=3{\mathbf{a}}_{3}
is greater than
2
,
\operatorname{gr}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}(R)
is not Cohen-Macaulay.

Let
I
be a minimal reduction ideal of
{\mathfrak{m}}
. Then, the reduction number of
{\mathfrak{m}}
with respect to
I
is at most
e(R)
, see
[
19
]
. If
{\mathbb{K}}
has zero characteristic, Vasconcelos showed in
[
21
]
, that
r_{I}(R)\leq e(R)-1
. For homogeneous affine simplicial semigroups,
I
is a reduction ideal of
{\mathfrak{m}}
and in
[
11
]
it is proved that
r_{I}(R)\leq e(R)-\operatorname{codim}(R)
.
In the following we find another class of simplicial affine semigroups satisfying this property.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
I=({\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{1}},\dots,{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{d}})
be a minimal reduction ideal of
{\mathfrak{m}}
. If
\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
is Cohen-Macaulay, then
r_{I}(R)\leq e(R)-\operatorname{codim}(R)
.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.4
, we have
\operatorname{ord}_{S}({\mathbf{b}}+{\mathbf{a}}_{i})=\operatorname{ord}_{S}({\mathbf{b}})+1
for all
{\mathbf{b}}\in S
and
1\leq i\leq d
. Thus,
r_{I}(R)=\max\{\operatorname{ord}_{S}({\mathbf{w}})\,;\,{\mathbf{w}}\in\operatorname{Ap}(S,E)\}
, by Lemma
4.3
. If
r_{I}(R)=1
, then
R
is a ring of minimal multiplicity and the claim follows by Proposition
2.5
. Thus, we assume that
r_{I}(R)\geq 2
.
Let
{\mathbf{w}}\in\operatorname{Ap}(S,E)
be such that
r_{I}(R)=\operatorname{ord}_{S}({\mathbf{w}})
. Then, there are some elements
{\mathbf{w}}_{i}\in S
such that
{\mathbf{w}}_{i}\preceq_{S}{\mathbf{w}}
and
\operatorname{ord}_{S}({\mathbf{w}}_{i})=i
for
i=2,\ldots,r_{I}(R)
. Since
\{{\mathbf{0}},{\mathbf{a}}_{d+1},\dots,{\mathbf{a}}_{d+s},{\mathbf{w}}_{2},\dots,{\mathbf{w}}_{r_{I}(R)}\}\subseteq\operatorname{Ap}(S,E)
and the elements of the first set are pairwise distinct, we derive
|\operatorname{Ap}(S,E)|\geq r_{I}(R)+\operatorname{codim}(R)
. Now, the result follows by Proposition
1.7
.
∎

The Cohen-Macaulay condition of
\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
in Proposition
4.6
can not be removed, even if

is Cohen-Macaulay.

Example 4.7
.

Let
S
be the affine semigroup generated by

{\mathbf{a}}_{1}=(6,0),{\mathbf{a}}_{2}=(0,4),{\mathbf{a}}_{3}=(3,3),{\mathbf{a}}_{4}=(3,9)
. As we have seen in Example
4.5
,

is Cohen-Macaulay but
\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
is not. By Proposition

,
e(R)=|\operatorname{Ap}(S,E)|=4
. Moreover, since
{\mathbf{x}}^{3{\mathbf{a}}_{3}}\in{\mathfrak{m}}^{3}\setminus({\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{1}},{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{2}}){\mathfrak{m}}^{2}
, we have
r_{I}(R)>2=e(R)-2
.

Remark 4.8
.

In Proposition
2.2
we found another bound for the reduction number of
R
without assuming that
\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
is Cohen-Macaulay. These two bounds are not comparable in general. For instance, when
R
is a Cohen-Macaulay ring of minimal multiplicity, we have
r_{I}(R)=e(R)-\operatorname{codim}(R)=|\operatorname{Ap}(S,E)|-\operatorname{codim}(R)=1
by Proposition
2.5
, which can be strictly smaller than

obtained in Proposition

. Now, consider the semigroup generated by
{\mathbf{a}}_{1}=(5,0),{\mathbf{a}}_{2}=(0,5),{\mathbf{a}}_{3}=(6,0),{\mathbf{a}}_{4}=(0,6)
. Then,

is Cohen-Macaulay and
{\mathfrak{m}}
has a monomial reduction. Thus,

, and

.

We conclude this section by investigating the Gorenstein property of
\operatorname{gr}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}(R)
. Define the order
\preceq
in
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
by
{\mathbf{a}}\preceq{\mathbf{b}}
, if
{\mathbf{b}}-{\mathbf{a}}\in S
.
Then,
{\mathbb{K}}[S]
is a Gorenstein ring if and only if it is Cohen-Macaulay and
\operatorname{Ap}(S,E)
has a single maximal element with respect to

, see
[
15
, Theorems 4.6 and 4.8]
.

Remark 4.9
.

Note that
\{{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{1}},\ldots,{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{d}}\}
is a system of parameters for
R
. Consider the zero-dimensional ring
\bar{R}=R/J
, where
J=({\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{1}},\ldots,{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{d}})
and let
\bar{{\mathfrak{m}}}={\mathfrak{m}}/J
. Then,
\bar{R}
has length
\lambda(\bar{R})=|\operatorname{Ap}(S,E)|
.
Since
\operatorname{in}(J)=J
, we have

\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)/J=\operatorname{gr}_{\bar{{\mathfrak{m}}}}(\bar{R})=\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}\bar{G}_{i},

where

\bar{G}_{i}=\frac{({\mathfrak{m}}/J)^{i}}{({\mathfrak{m}}/J)^{i+1}}
and
\lambda_{R}(\bar{G}_{i})=|\{{\mathbf{w}}\in\operatorname{Ap}(S,E)\,;\,\operatorname{ord}_{S}({\mathbf{w}})=i\}|
.

In particular,
\bar{G}_{i}=0
for all

. We will
use the following notations to refer to these numbers:

\beta_{i}(S):=\lambda_{R}(\bar{G}_{i})=|\{{\mathbf{w}}\in\operatorname{Ap}(S,E)\,;\,\operatorname{ord}_{S}({\mathbf{w}})=i\}|,

d(S):=\max\{i\in{\mathbb{N}}\,;\,\bar{G}_{i}\neq 0\}=\max\{\operatorname{ord}_{S}({\mathbf{w}})\,;\,{\mathbf{w}}\in\operatorname{Ap}(S,E)\}.

For elements
{\mathbf{a}},{\mathbf{b}}\in S
, we write
{\mathbf{a}}\preceq_{M}{\mathbf{b}}
if
{\mathbf{a}}\preceq{\mathbf{b}}
and
\operatorname{ord}_{S}({\mathbf{b}})=\operatorname{ord}_{S}({\mathbf{b}}-{\mathbf{a}})+\operatorname{ord}_{S}({\mathbf{a}})
.
The following lemma is obtained from
[
12
, Lemma 2.7]
, replacing the Apéry set with respect to an element
x
, by
\operatorname{Ap}(S,E)
. This extends the result proved by Bryant in
[
6
]
for numerical semigroups.

Lemma 4.10
.

If
R
is Gorenstein, then the following statements
are equivalent:

(a)

\operatorname{gr}_{\bar{{\mathfrak{m}}}}(\bar{R})
is Gorenstein;

(b)

\beta_{i}(S)=\beta_{d(S)-i}(S)
for
0\leq i\leq\lfloor\frac{d(S)+1}{2}\rfloor
;

(c)

\operatorname{Max}_{\preceq_{M}}\operatorname{Ap}(S,E)
is a singleton.

Proposition 4.11
.

Assume that
({\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{1}},\dots,{\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{d}})
is a reduction ideal of
{\mathfrak{m}}
. The following statements are equivalent:

(a)

\operatorname{gr}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}(R)
is Gorenstein;

(b)

\operatorname{gr}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}(R)
is Cohen-Macaulay and
\operatorname{Max}_{\preceq_{M}}\operatorname{Ap}(S,E)
has a single element.

Proof.

In both statements
\operatorname{gr}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}(R)
is Cohen-Macaulay. Thus,
({\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{1}})^{*},\dots,({\mathbf{x}}^{{\mathbf{a}}_{d}})^{*}
is a regular sequence in
\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
, by Proposition

.
Therefore,
\operatorname{gr}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}(R)
is Gorenstein if and only if
\operatorname{gr}_{\bar{{\mathfrak{m}}}}(\bar{R})
is Gorenstein and, hence, the result follows by Lemma
4.10
.
∎

, when

has monomial minimal reduction and

.

If
{\mathfrak{m}}

\operatorname{reg}(\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R))=\max\{\operatorname{ord}_{S}({\mathbf{w}})\,;\,{\mathbf{w}}\in\operatorname{Ap}(S,E)\}.

, and the result follows by Lemma

Corollary 4.14
.

If

is Cohen-Macaulay and the defining ideal

Proof.

Since

Intersections of vertical lines with non-vertical lines
Mateusz Kula

Abstract
We answer negatively a question: if ℱ \mathcal{F} is a family of n ⩾ 3 n\geqslant 3 non-vertical, pairwise non-parallel lines on the plane and ⋂ ℱ = ∅ \bigcap\mathcal{F}=\emptyset , is there a vertical line L L such that L ∩ ⋃ ℱ L\cap\bigcup\mathcal{F} has exactly n − 1 n-1 or n − 2 n-2 points?



In
[
1
]
P. Leonetti posed the following problem.
Let
\mathcal{F}
be a family of
n\geqslant 3
non-vertical, pairwise non-parallel lines on the plane such that they are not concurrent.
Is there a vertical line
L
such that
L\cap\bigcup\mathcal{F}
has exactly
n-1
or
n-2
points?
We answer this question negatively, giving appropriate examples for
n\geqslant 7
.

For our purposes, consider a bijection
f
between the plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and the family of all non-vertical lines such that

f(A,B):=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\colon y+Ax+B=0\}.

Lemma
.

(P,Q)\in f(A,B)
if and only if
(A,B)\in f(P,Q)
.

Proof.

Both conditions are equivalent to the equality
Q+AP+B=0
.
∎

Let
q_{1},\ldots,q_{7}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
be the vertices of a regular heptagon such that no two of them lie on one vertical line.
If vertices
q_{i},q_{j}
lie on the line
K
, then there exist exactly
4
lines parallel to
K
such that each of these lines contains at least one vertex
q
.
The family

\mathcal{F}:=\{f(q_{i})\colon 1\leqslant i\leqslant 7\}

consists of
n=7
pairwise non-parallel lines, which are not concurrent. Indeed, if there was a point
p
such that
p\in f(q_{i})
for each
i
, then by the Lemma points
q_{1},\ldots,q_{7}
would all lie on one line
f(p)
, which contradicts the fact that
q_{1},\ldots,q_{7}
are vertices of the heptagon.

Suppose a vertical line
L
intersects the union
\bigcup\mathcal{F}
at
k
points
(A,D_{1}),\ldots,(A,D_{k})
, where
1\leqslant k\leqslant 7
.
Observe that
p\in L\cap\bigcup\mathcal{F}
if and only if the line
f(p)
is parallel to
f(A,D_{1})
and contains at least one vertex
q_{i}
. Since there are exactly
4
or
7
such lines and
f
is a bijection, we have
k=4
or
k=7
.
Hence no vertical line
L
intersects the union
\bigcup\mathcal{F}
in exactly
n-1=6
or
n-2=5
points. This answers negatively Leonetti’s question.

In our opinion one should consider the following problem.

Problem
.

Assume that
Q\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a finite subset and
\mathcal{G}
is a family of parallel lines which covers
Q
, and such that each line
K\in\mathcal{G}
intersects
Q
.
What are possible values of
|\mathcal{G}|
?

Let
I(Q)
denote the set of all values
|\mathcal{G}|
, where
\mathcal{G}
is as above.
If
q_{1},\ldots,q_{n}
are vertices of a regular polygon, then one readily verifies that:

(1)

If
n=2k
, then
I(q_{1},\ldots,q_{n})=\{k,k+1,2k\}
;

(2)

If
n=2k+1
, then
I(q_{1},\ldots,q_{n})=\{k,2k+1\}
;

(3)

If
n=4k+2
and
q
is the centre of the circle on which points
q_{i}
lie, then
I(q,q_{1},\ldots,q_{n})=\{2k+1,2k+3,4k+3\}
;

(4)

If
n=4k
and
q
is the centre of the circle on which points
q_{i}
lie, then
I(q,q_{1},\ldots,q_{n})=\{2k+1,4k+1\}
.

Theorem
.

For any
n\geqslant 7
there exists a family
\mathcal{F}
of
n
non-vertical, pairwise non-parallel lines, which are not concurrent, such that no vertical line
L
contains exactly
n-1
or
n-2
points of
\bigcup\mathcal{F}
.

Proof.

If
Q\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a set of points as in (1)–(4) and no two points of
Q
lie on one vertical line, then
f[Q]
is a desired family of lines. Indeed, with the Lemma one can check that
k\in I(Q)
if and only if there exists a vertical line, which has exactly
k
intersections with the union of the family of lines
f[Q]
.
∎

In the paper
[
2
, Theorem 2.1]
the set
I(Q)
was examined, too. For example, R. Pinchasi shows that for any finite subset
Q\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
of
n
non-collinear points

\max\left(I(Q)\setminus\{n\}\right)\geqslant\left\lfloor\frac{n+1}{2}\right\rfloor.

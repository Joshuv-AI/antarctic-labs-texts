Abstract

We study the powers of Hamiltonian cycles in randomly augmented Dirac graphs, that is, n n -vertex graphs G G with minimum degree at least ( 1 / 2 + ε ) ​ n (1/2+\varepsilon)n to which some random edges are added. For any Dirac graph and every integer m ≥ 2 m\geq 2 , we accurately estimate the threshold probability p = p ⁡ ( n ) p=p(n) for the event that the random augmentation G ∪ G ⁡ ( n , p ) G\cup G(n,p) contains the m m -th power of a Hamiltonian cycle.



1.
Introduction

In this paper we continue the study of powers of Hamiltonian cycles in randomly augmented Dirac graphs initiated by Bohman, Frieze, and Martin in
[
2
]
and developed in
[
5
,
1
,
8
]
and
[
3
]
.
For
m\in{\mathds{N}}
the
m
-th power
F^{m}
of a graph
F
is defined
as the graph on the same vertex set whose edges join distinct vertices at distance at most
m
in
F
.
The
m
-th power of a path or a cycle will be often called an
m
-path
or, respectively, an
m
-cycle
. Note that the
m
-cycle on
v
vertices has
vm
edges, while the
v
-vertex
m
-path has
vm-\binom{m+1}{2}
edges.

A Hamiltonian cycle in a graph
G
is a cycle which passes through all vertices of
G
. Extending the celebrated result of Dirac
[
4
]
, Komlós, Sárközy, and Szemerédi in
[
6
,
7
]
proved that for large
n
every graph
G
with
n
vertices and minimum degree
\delta(G)\geq\tfrac{k}{k+1}n
contains the
k
-th power of a Hamiltonian cycle.

1.1.
Thresholds and over-thresholds

For an integer
k\geq 0
, a sequence of
n
-vertex graphs
G=G(n)
is
k
-Dirac
if
\delta(G)\geq\left(\tfrac{k}{k+1}+\varepsilon\right)n
for a constant
\varepsilon>0
and all
n\geq n_{0}
, for some
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
. For integers
m>k\geq 1
, a
(k,m)
-Dirac threshold
was defined in
[
1
, Def. 1.1]
with
\alpha=k/(k+1)+\varepsilon
as, roughly, the smallest function
d(n)
such that with probability approaching 1, for every
k
-Dirac sequence of graphs
G(n)
and every
p=p(n)\geq Cd(n)
, where
C=C(\varepsilon,k)>0
, the union
G(n)\cup G(n,p)
contains the
m
-th power of a Hamiltonian cycle. If existed, such a threshold function was denoted by
d_{k,m}(n)
. The main feature of that definition was that it was independent of the constant
\varepsilon
(or
\alpha
) – only the multiplicative constants depended on
\varepsilon
.

For
k=0
, the threshold
d_{0}=n^{-1}
has been established in
[
2
]
.
In
[
5
]
it was proved that
d_{k,k+1}(n)=n^{-1}
for all
k\geq 1
. This was substantially extended in
[
1
]
to cover many other pairs
(k,m)
. In particular, it turned out that
d_{k,m}(n)=n^{-1}
for all
m\leq 2k+1
. (This result was independently obtained by Nenadov and Trujić in
[
8
]
.) For
m=2
, Böttcher, Parczyk, Sgueglia, and Skokan in
[
3
]
extended this result to a more general setting when
\delta(G)\geq\alpha n
, for all values of
\alpha\in(0,1)
.

However, a vast majority of pairs
(k,m)
were still unaccounted for. In this paper we consider the case
k=1
only and solve the problem
for all
values of
m
. Along the way it turned out, however, that we had to come up with a new notion of threshold.

Looking back at the results in
[
1
]
for just
k=1
, it is perhaps surprising that only the values of
m\in\{2,3,4,5,8\}
are covered. Indeed, Theorem 1.2 in
[
1
]
established the bound
d_{1,m}(n)\leq n^{-2/\ell}
where
\ell=\min\{\ell^{\prime}\leq m:\ell^{\prime}\geq(m-\ell^{\prime})(m-\ell^{\prime}+1)\}
. On the other hand, Theorem 1.3 therein yielded the lower bound
d_{1,m}(n)\geq n^{-2/\left(\lfloor m/2\rfloor+1\right)}
, and these bounds coincide only for
m=2,3,4
. For
m=8
they leave a gap
n^{-2/5}\leq d_{1,8}(n)\leq n^{-1/3}
which was closed by showing that
d_{1,8}(n)=n^{-1/3}
(see
[
1
, Theorem 1.5]
).

The case
m=5
, treated only as a concluding remark in
[
1
]
, was exceptional in that it escaped the notion of threshold as defined there.
Indeed, in Claims 9.1 and 9.2 in
[
1
]
the exponent of
n
depends on
\varepsilon
. More precisely, for each
\varepsilon>0
there exist two constants
c^{\prime}=c^{\prime}(\varepsilon)
and
c^{\prime\prime}=c^{\prime\prime}(\varepsilon)
such that the transition from limiting probability zero to one takes place somewhere between
n^{-1/2-c^{\prime}}
and
n^{-1/2-c^{\prime\prime}}
. Moreover, both
c^{\prime}
and
c^{\prime\prime}
converge to 0 with
\varepsilon\to 0
. (For more discussion on thresholds for up to
m=10
, see Section
6
II.)

Back then viewed rather as an eccentricity, the case
m=5
has now become an enlightening indication of the truth in full generality.
Indeed, it turns out that for all
m\not\in\{2,3,4,8\}
, the threshold behavior is similar to that for
m=5
. As our goal has been to determine a threshold function independent of
\varepsilon
, we are in need of a new notion of a threshold.
So, one might view the four “typical” cases solved in
[
5
,
1
]
as the tip of an iceberg which, however, in its depths looks quite different than what the tip might have suggested.

Inspired by the case
m=5
, we now define a new type of threshold. For future references, we do it for all
k\geq 1
, although this paper is exclusively devoted to the classical Dirac case of
k=1
. For integers
m\geq 1
and
n\geq m+2
, the family (or property)
\mathcal{C}_{n}^{m}

consists of all
n
-vertex graphs
G
that contain the
m
-th power of a Hamiltonian cycle.

We say that a function
d(n)
is a
(k,m)
-Dirac over-threshold
if

(i)

for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\mu>0
such that for all
n
-vertex graphs
G
with
\delta(G)\geq(\tfrac{k}{k+1}+\varepsilon)n
and all
p=p(n)\geq d(n)n^{-\mu}

\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathds{P}}(G\cup G(n,p)\in{\mathcal{C}}^{m}_{n})=1,

and

(ii)

for every real
\mu>0
, there exists
\varepsilon>0
and a sequence of
n
-vertex graphs
G_{\varepsilon}=G_{\varepsilon}(n)
with
\delta(G_{\varepsilon})\geq\left(\tfrac{k}{k+1}+\varepsilon\right)n
such that for every
p=p(n)\leq d(n)n^{-\mu}

\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathds{P}}(G_{\varepsilon}\cup G(n,p)\in{\mathcal{C}}^{m}_{n})=0.

If exists, the
(k,m)
-Dirac over-threshold is denoted by
\bar{d}_{k,m}(n)
. Obviously, the existence of the over-threshold
\bar{d}_{k,m}(n)
excludes the existence of the
(k,m)
-Dirac threshold
d_{k,n}(n)
.
The prefix “over” is meant to remind us that the abrupt change in behavior of the probability in question happens just “below” the function
d(n)
. The main difference between the
(k,m)
-Dirac threshold as defined in
[
1
]
and the new notion above is that now the dependence on
\varepsilon
is much more substantial. As a drawback, however, for any given
\varepsilon
we do not obtain a threshold in the classical sense, but a pair of functions bounding it from both sides.

1.2.
Main results

The thresholds obtained in
[
1
]
were related to the density of the so called braid graphs (see Definition
4.2
and Fig.
4.2
). Given a graph
G
, let
v_{G}=|V(G)|
and
e_{G}=|E(G)|
. For a graph
G
with
v_{G}>1
, by
density
and, respectively,
maximum density
, we mean

d_{G}=\frac{e_{G}}{v_{G}-1}\ \ \text{ and }\ \ m_{G}=\max_{H\subseteq G,v_{H}>1}d_{G}.

The parameter
d_{G}
is often called 1-density.

Roughly speaking, for
0\leq r\leq\ell
, a braid graph consists of an ordered collection of
\ell
-cliques such that any two consecutive cliques are joined by a structure called an
r
-bridge having exactly
{r+1\choose 2}
edges (see Definition
4.1
and Fig.
4.1
). It was crucial for the construction used in the proof in
[
1
]
that

(1.1)

m=k\ell+r,

so that when
k+1
braids are intertwined and all
\ell
-cliques at distance at most
k
, one from each braid, are fully connected to each other, each vertex has exactly
k\ell+r=m
neighbors ahead of itself (and, by symmetry, also behind itself). That is, such a structure forms an
m
-path.

The cases of
k
and
m
covered in
[
1
]
were, with the exception of
(1,8)
and
(2,14)
, exactly those for which one could find integers
\ell\geq 2
and
r\geq 0
with
r(r+1)\leq\ell
and such that
(k+1)(\ell-1)\leq m\leq k\ell+r
(the R-H-S inequality is due to the monotonicity of
m
-path containment with respect to
m
). The lower bound on
\ell
implied that the densest subgraph of a braid was an
\ell
-clique and, consequently, the exponent in the
(k,m)
-Dirac threshold equalled the negated reciprocal of the density
d_{K_{\ell}}
of the
\ell
-clique, that is,
-2/\ell
. The reason was that for
p\geq n^{-2/\ell}
there are in
G(n,p)
plenty of copies of the braid (c.f.,
[
1
, Proposition 5.8]
, an immediate corollary of
[
1
, Theorem 2.2]
), a fact which was a crucial building block in the proof therein.

In this paper we allow the opposite case, namely
\ell<r(r+1)
(for
k=1
). It turns out that now the densest subgraph of a braid is the braid itself (see Proposition
4.3
). Moreover, another corollary of
[
1
, Theorem 2.2]
, namely Proposition
4.4
below, implies that, again, there are plenty of copies of braids in
G(n,p)
with appropriately adjusted
p
(the exponent of
n
is now the negated reciprocal of the density of the braid).

We aim at finding an optimal value of
\ell
for which the (asymptotic) density of the braid determines the
(1,m)
-Dirac over-threshold.
For this sake we introduce a pivotal quantity
f_{m}(\ell)
.
For integers
m\geq 2
and
\ell\in[m]
, let

f_{m}(\ell)=\frac{1}{\ell}\left(\binom{\ell}{2}+\binom{m-\ell+1}{2}\right)=\ell+\frac{m^{2}+m}{2\ell}-m-1.

Note that for
k=1
, (
1.1
) becomes
m=\ell+r
. Thus, for
\ell\geq m/2
,
f_{m}(\ell)
equals the average number of edges adjacent to the vertices of the first
\ell
-clique in the braid and, consequently, it equals the asymptotic density of the braid as the number of cliques tends to infinity (see Section
4
for details).

Observe that for
\ell>0

f_{m}(\ell)=\left(\sqrt{\ell}-\frac{\sqrt{2m^{2}+2m}}{2\sqrt{\ell}}\right)^{2}+\sqrt{2m^{2}+2m}-m-1\geq\sqrt{2m^{2}+2m}-m-1.

Thus, the function
f=f_{m}
has a unique global minimum on the
real
interval
(0,m]
at

\lambda_{m}=\frac{\sqrt{2m^{2}+2m}}{2}.

Hence, for each integer
\ell\in[m]
we have
f(\ell)\geq\min\left\{f(\lfloor\lambda_{m}\rfloor),f(\lceil\lambda_{m}\rceil)\right\}
. Let

(1.2)

\ell_{m}\in\{\lfloor\lambda_{m}\rfloor,\lceil\lambda_{m}\rceil\}\quad\mbox{be such that}\quad f(\ell_{m})=\min\left\{f(\lfloor\lambda_{m}\rfloor),f(\lceil\lambda_{m}\rceil)\right\}.

As a convention, when
\lfloor\lambda_{m}\rfloor\neq\lceil\lambda_{m}\rceil
but
f(\lfloor\lambda_{m}\rfloor)=f(\lceil\lambda_{m}\rceil)
, we always set
\ell_{m}=\lfloor\lambda_{m}\rfloor
. For example,
\lambda_{20}=\sqrt{210}
,
\lfloor\lambda_{20}\rfloor=14
,
\lceil\lambda_{20}\rceil=15
, and
f(14)=8=f(15)
, so we set
\ell_{20}=14
. In general,
\lambda_{m}\geq m/2
for all
m
, and
\ell_{m}\geq\lambda_{m}-1\geq m/2
for
m\geq 4
.

We are ready to state the main results of this paper. From now on we abbreviate
(1,m)
-Dirac (over-)threshold to just
m
-Dirac (over-)threshold and use
\bar{d}_{m}(n)
for
\bar{d}_{1,m}(n)
.

Theorem 1.1
.

For every integer
m\geq 2
and a real
\mu>0
, there exists
\varepsilon>0
and a sequence of
n
-vertex graphs
G_{\varepsilon}=G_{\varepsilon}(n)
with
\delta(G_{\varepsilon})\geq(\tfrac{1}{2}+\varepsilon)n
such that for every
p=p(n)\leq n^{-1/f(\ell_{m})-\mu}

\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathds{P}}(G_{\varepsilon}\cup G(n,p)\in{\mathcal{C}}^{m}_{n})=0.

Theorem
1.1
implies that, if an over-threshold exists,
\bar{d}_{m}(n)\geq n^{-1/f(\ell_{m})}
. Below we provide an upper bound.

Theorem 1.2
.

For all integers
m
and
\ell
such that
m/2\leq\ell\leq m-1
and
\ell<(m-\ell)(m-\ell+1)
and for all
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\mu>0
such that for all
n
-vertex graphs
G
with
\delta(G)\geq(1/2+\varepsilon)n
and all
p=p(n)\geq n^{-1/f(\ell)-\mu}

\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathds{P}}(G\cup G(n,p)\in{\mathcal{C}}^{m}_{n})=1.

Theorem
1.2
implies that, if exists,
\bar{d}_{m}(n)\leq n^{-1/f(\ell)}
.

Remember that in
[
5
,
1
]
the
m
-Dirac thresholds for
m\in\{2,3,4,8\}
, as well as the

\ell_{m}
from Theorem

satisfies the restriction on

from Theorem
1.2
.

Proposition 1.3
.

For
m=7
and any integer

we have

\ell_{m}<(m-\ell_{m})(m-\ell_{m}+1).

We defer the simple proof of Proposition

Theorems

For

The remaining cases (

2.
Lower bound – The proof of Theorem
1.1

The proof of Theorem
1.1
relies on two lemmas. The first one is a simple observation about random graphs.

Lemma 2.1
.

For each integer
m\geq 2
, let
\ell_{m}
be as defined in (
1.2
). For every
\mu>0
, if

p\leq n^{-1/f(\ell_{m})-\mu},

then a.a.s.
G(n,p)
contains no copy of
K_{m+1}
.

Proof.

Clearly,
{\mathds{P}}(G(n,p)\supset K_{m+1})=O\left(n^{m+1}p^{\binom{m+1}{2}}\right)=o(1)
, whenever
p=o(n^{-2/m})
. Thus, all we need to show is the inequality
f(\ell_{m})\leq m/2
.
For
m\in\{2,3\}
this can be checked by computing directly
f(\lfloor\lambda_{m}\rfloor)
and
f(\lceil\lambda_{m}\rceil)
. Indeed, we have
\lambda_{2}=\sqrt{3}
and
\lambda_{3}=\sqrt{6}
, so
\ell_{2}=\ell_{3}=2
and
f(\ell_{2})=1/2<1
, while
f(\ell_{3})=1<3/2
.
Assume now that
m\geq 4
. It can be easily verified (by solving a quadratic inequality) that
f(\ell_{m})\leq m/2
is equivalent to
\ell_{m}\geq m/2
. Finally, recall that
\ell_{m}\geq\frac{\sqrt{2m^{2}+2m}}{2}-1\geq m/2
for
m\geq 4
.
∎

The proof of the second lemma, which is fully deterministic, will be given in Section
3
. Recall that by an
m
-path we mean the
m
-th power of a path.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
m\geq 2
and let
P
be an
m
-path with
V(P)=A\cup B
,
A\cap B=\varnothing
, such that there are no
m+1
consecutive vertices in
P
either all belonging to
A
or all belonging to
B
. Then,

|E(P[A])|+|E(P[B])|\geq f(\ell_{m})|V(P)|-2m^{2},

where
\ell_{m}
is defined in (
1.2
).

Lemma
2.2
is the key novelty compared to
[
1
]
and might be of independent interest, as it provides structural information about a graph commonly arising in the study of randomly augmented graphs.

Let us now define this crucial graph. It has been first used in
[
2
]
to prove a lower bound on
d_{0}
, and then in many other papers in the same context, e.g.,
[
5
,
1
,
8
,
3
]
.

Definition 2.3
.

For
\varepsilon>0
and even
n
, let
G
be an
n
-vertex complete bipartite graph with bipartition classes
X\cup Y
, where
|X|=|Y|=n/2
. Fix two subsets
U\subset X
and
W\subset Y
with
|U|=|W|=\lfloor\varepsilon n\rfloor
. The graph
G_{\varepsilon}
is obtained from
G
by adding two complete bipartite graphs with bipartitions
(U,X\smallsetminus U)
and
(W,Y\smallsetminus W)
.

Now we are ready to give a short proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Given
m
and
\mu
, let
\varepsilon>0
be any constant such that
\varepsilon\leq 1/6
and

(2.1)

\frac{1}{\varepsilon}>\frac{6m^{2}}{(f(\ell_{m}))^{2}\mu}+\frac{6m^{2}}{f(\ell_{m})}.

(Note that
\varepsilon
decreases when
\mu
does.)

Let
G_{\varepsilon}
be as in Definition
2.3
. Let
p\leq n^{-1/f(\ell_{m})-\mu}
and suppose that
G_{\varepsilon}\cup G(n,p)
contains the
m
-th power of a Hamiltonian cycle
C
. After removing the vertices of
U\cup W
from
G_{\varepsilon}
(and thus from
C
), we obtain a collection of at most
2\lfloor\varepsilon n\rfloor

m
-paths in
(X\smallsetminus U)\cup(Y\smallsetminus W)
, hence at least one of them must be of length at least

\frac{n-2\lfloor\varepsilon n\rfloor}{2\lfloor\varepsilon n\rfloor}\geq\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}-1\geq\frac{1}{3\varepsilon}.

Fix such a path
P
of length
L=\lceil\tfrac{1}{3\varepsilon}\rceil
and consider its edgewise intersection with
G(n,p)
. Setting
A=V(P)\cap X
and
B=V(P)\cap Y
, by the construction of
G_{\varepsilon}
and the removal of
U\cup W
,

G(n,p)\cap P=P[A]\cup P[B].

Let
M=\lceil f(\ell_{m})L\rceil-2m^{2}
. By Lemma
2.2
, either
G(n,p)\supset K_{m+1}
or there are no
m+1
consecutive vertices in
P
either all belonging to
A
or all belonging to
B
, and thus

|E(P[A])|+|E(P[B])|\geq M.

The former event, by Lemma
2.1
, does not hold a.a.s..
The latter, in turn, implies the existence in
G(n,p)
of a subgraph with
L
vertices and
M
edges. The expected number of such subgraphs, abbreviating
f=f(\ell_{m})
and using our bound on
p
and (
2.1
), can be crudely bounded from above by

\binom{\binom{L}{2}}{M}n^{L}p^{M}\leq O\left(n^{L-(1/f+\mu)M}\right)=O\left(n^{L-(1/f+\mu)(fL-2m^{2})}\right)=O\left(n^{2m^{2}/f+2m^{2}\mu-\mu f/(3\varepsilon)}\right)=o(1).

Hence, a.a.s.
G_{\varepsilon}\cup G(n,p)
cannot contain such a path
P
, and thus a.a.s.

G_{\varepsilon}\cup G(n,p)\notin\mathcal{C}_{n}^{m}.

∎

3.
Proof of Lemma
2.2

This section is entirely devoted to the proof of our main lemma, namely Lemma
2.2
.

Proof of Lemma
2.2
.

The ordering of the vertices on
P
defines a partition of
A\cup B
into segments
S_{1},S_{2},\ldots,S_{t}
, which consist of maximal sets of consecutive vertices of
P
lying entirely in
A
or entirely in
B
. Thus,
A=S_{1}\cup S_{3}\cup\dots
and
B=S_{2}\cup S_{4}\cup\dots
. We will first show that in order to bound the number of edges in
P[A]\cup P[B]
we can consider a different
m
-path
P^{\prime}
on the same set of vertices, but with a new partition
A^{\prime}\cup B^{\prime}=A\cup B
and new segments
S^{\prime}_{1},S^{\prime}_{2},\ldots,S^{\prime}_{t^{\prime}}
which alternate between
A^{\prime}
and
B^{\prime}
, and such that

(a)

for all
i=1,\dots,t^{\prime}
,
|S^{\prime}_{i}|\leq m
, and

(b)

for all
i=1,\dots,t^{\prime}-1
,
|S^{\prime}_{i}|+|S^{\prime}_{i+1}|\geq m
.

Condition (a) implies that neither
P^{\prime}[A^{\prime}]
, nor
P^{\prime}[B^{\prime}]
, contains a copy of
K_{m+1}
, while condition (b) guarantees that all edges in
E(P^{\prime}[A^{\prime}])\cup E(P^{\prime}[B^{\prime}])
connect only vertices lying in consecutive segments of
A^{\prime}
or consecutive segments of
B^{\prime}
. The second condition simplifies the computation of the number of relevant edges as, given (b), the number of edges between segment
S_{i}^{\prime}
and all segments lying to the right will depend only on the size of
S_{i+1}^{\prime}
. In the end, we will bound the size of
E(P[A])\cup E(P[B])
by the size of
E(P^{\prime}[A^{\prime}])\cup E(P^{\prime}[B^{\prime}])
minus a correcting additive term, cf. (
3.1
). Then the latter, owing to properties (a) and (b), will be minimized by
f(\ell_{m})|V(P^{\prime})|+O(1)
, a quantity corresponding to the case when
P^{\prime}[A^{\prime}]
and

The proof of Theorem

, Theorem 1.2]

In our proof all four pillars of the absorbing method: the Connecting Lemma, the Reservoir Lemma, the Absorbing Lemma, and the Covering Lemma, are exactly the same as, respectively, Lemmas 6.2, 4.2, 4.3, 4.4 in
[

]
, except for the assumption on

.

To describe this fundamental change with respect to the proof in
[
1
]
, we begin by recalling the notions of a bridge and a braid graph which played a crucial role therein.

Definition 4.1
.

For

, two sequences of vertices
\accentset{\rightharpoonup}{v}=(v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{r})
and
\accentset{\rightharpoonup}{u}=(u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{r})

(or just

is adjacent in

to all

.

Definition 4.2
.

For

,

, and
1\leq r\leq\ell
, let

be
the braid graph
consisting of

-cliques

, with vertices ordered arbitrarily, where for each

and the first

form an

and for any

, the union of

.

As already mentioned above, the proof in
[

is based on the standard absorbing method whose four main ingredients, Connecting, Reservoir, Absorbing, and Covering Lemmas, all claim the existence of certain

, where

(see

, a.a.s., complementing at least one of them with a suitable copy of

We now describe this change and its consequences. Recall the definitions of the density parameters

and

from Section
1.2
. Clearly, as

is a disjoint union of two copies of

.
In the course of the proof of

it was shown that if

,

, then for all

, the maximum density in a braid is obtained by an

. As a result, the assumption

Now, under the opposite assumption

Proposition 4.3
.

For all

and

satisfying

\ell<r(r+1),

we have

.

We defer the proof of Proposition
4.3
to the end of this section.

In view of Proposition
4.3
, setting

, our assumption on

Indeed, recall that

On the other hand,

In order to obtain the upper bounds on

with, respectively,

,

, and

,

.

Let us then focus on the lower bounds. We are going to use a similar strategy as in the proof of Theorem

, where the existence of the

The following terminology will be useful.

.

Case

m=6
: Given
\mu
, let
\varepsilon>0
be any constant such that
\varepsilon\leq 1/12
and

(5.1)

\frac{9}{16\varepsilon}>\frac{32}{9\mu}+8.

Let

be as in Definition

,

, and

.
We define three events:

–

\mathcal{K}

– the number of copies of

in

is no more than

,

– there is in

a subgraph

with

vertices and

edges.

Clearly,

(5.2)

{\mathds{P}}(\mathcal{C})\leq{\mathds{P}}({\mathcal{C}}\cap{\mathcal{K}})+{\mathds{P}}(\neg{\mathcal{K}}).

The main step of the proof will be to show that
{\mathcal{C}}\cap{\mathcal{K}}
implies
\mathcal{S}
. Taking this for granted, one can quickly complete the proof. Indeed, as
p=o(n^{-4/9})
, the expected number of copies of

in

is less than
n^{5}p^{10}=o(n^{5/9})
, and so, by Markov’s inequality,
{\mathds{P}}(\neg{\mathcal{K}})=o(1)
. Similarly, using (

), the expected number of subgraphs of

with

\binom{\binom{L}{2}}{M}n^{L}p^{M}=O\left(n^{L}n^{\left(-\frac{4}{9}-\mu\right)\left(\frac{9L}{4}-8\right)}\right)=O\left(n^{\frac{32}{9}+8\mu-\frac{9}{4}L\mu}\right)\leq O\left(n^{\frac{32}{9}+8\mu-\frac{9}{16\varepsilon}\mu}\right)=o(1).

Thus, another application of Markov’s inequality yields
{\mathds{P}}({\mathcal{S}})=o(1)
.

It remains to show the inclusion
{\mathcal{C}}\cap{\mathcal{K}}\subset\mathcal{S}
. Let

be the 6th power of a Hamilton cycle given by the event

.
After removing from

all vertices from

, as well as at least one vertex from each copy of at most

in

, we obtain a subgraph of

of order at least

, which is a collection of at most

.

and

, and notice that

-free.

First, observe that both

,

, be a

. As

6.
Concluding remarks

I

Recall that in order to determine the
m
-Dirac over-threshold
\bar{d}_{m}
, we established Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
. For a fixed
\varepsilon>0
and a given
n
-vertex graph
G
with
\delta(G)\geq(1/2+\varepsilon)n
, these theorems yield bounds on the ordinary threshold probability
\hat{p}
for the property
G\cup G(n,p)\in\mathcal{C}^{m}_{n}
(assuming it exists) namely

n^{-1/f(\ell)-\mu_{1}}\leq\hat{p}\leq n^{-1/f(\ell)-\mu_{2}},

where
\mu_{1}=\Theta(\varepsilon)
, while
\mu_{2}=\mu_{2}(\varepsilon)
is an implicit function of
\varepsilon
with
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mu_{2}(\varepsilon)=0
– cf. (
2.1
) and, respectively, the proof of Theorem
1.2
.
It would be nice, but probably extremely difficult, to close the gap.

Problem 6.1
.

For all integers
2\leq\ell\leq m-1
such that
\ell<(m-\ell)(m-\ell+1)
and for all
\varepsilon>0

find a function
\mu=\mu(\varepsilon)>0
such that for all
n
-vertex graphs
G
with
\delta(G)\geq(1/2+\varepsilon)n

\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathds{P}}(G\cup G(n,p)\in{\mathcal{C}}^{m}_{n})=\begin{cases}1\quad\mbox{if}\quad p\gg n^{-1/f(\ell)-\mu},\\
0\quad\mbox{if}\quad p\ll n^{-1/f(\ell)-\mu}.\end{cases}

(Here
a_{n}\ll b_{n}
means
a_{n}=o(b_{n})
, while
a_{n}\gg b_{n}
means
b_{n}=o(a_{n})
.)

II

One may wonder what is so special about the cases
m=2,3,4,8
that just for them we have obtained the standard Dirac-threshold
d_{m}(n)
, while for all other values of
m
we ended up with the Dirac over-threshold
\bar{d}_{m}(n)
. A humorous answer could be that it is yet another instance of the
Law of Small Numbers
: whatever seems to be true for small instances of a parameter, fails to hold in general. Still, in this subsection we attempt to provide a more intelligent answer.

Altogether, we have had at our disposal four statements and one ad hoc technique to deduce upper and lower bounds on the order of magnitude of
p=p(n)
which guarantees the presence of a Hamiltonian
m
-cycle in
G\cup G(n,p)
, where
G
is a Dirac graph: Theorems
[
1
, Theorem 1.2]
and
1.2
for upper bounds, Theorems
[
1
, Theorem 1.3]
and
1.1
for lower bounds, the latter sometimes strengthened by forcing some extra edges in
G(n,p)
.

For
m=2,3,4
, the pair of theorems from
[
1
]
suffices to pinpoint the threshold
d_{m}
. In particular, the lower bounds follow by observing that a Hamiltonian
m
-cycle in
G_{\varepsilon}\cup G(n,p)
must have a linear number of edges (
m=2,3
) or triangles (
m=4
) in
G(n,p)
alone which is unlikely as
p\leq cn^{-1}
(
m=2,3
) or
p\leq cn^{-2/3}
(
m=4
). For
m=8
,
[
1
, Theorem 1.3]
does not apply, yet we have an ad hoc remedy: for
p\leq cn^{-1/3}
there are a.a.s. no copies of
K_{6}
in
G(n,p)
which implies that there have to be long 3-paths with linear number of extra 4-edges which, again, is very unlikely.

With three exceptions, for all other
m
we have
\ell_{m}<(m-\ell_{m})(m-\ell_{m}+1)
(c.f. Proposition
1.3
) and the pair of Theorems
1.2
and
1.1
“squeezes out” an over-threshold
\bar{d}_{m}
. Why not
d_{m}
? On one hand, because for such
\ell_{m}
the corresponding braids are balanced (c.f. Proposition
1.3
) and, on the other hand, because
p
has to be small enough to “eliminate” dense paths from
G(n,p)
(c.f. Lemma
2.2
).

The three remaining cases (
m=5,6,9
), at least in principle, could go either way. Let us focus on
m=5
as the other two are quite similar (though a bit more involved).
We have
\ell_{5}=4
and
f(\ell_{5})=7/4
, while
f(3)=2
is the runner-up. Thus, Theorems
1.2
and
1.1
imply, respectively, that
\bar{d}_{5}(n)\leq n^{-1/2}
and
\bar{d}_{5}(n)\geq n^{-4/7}
. At this point it is still possible that for some
4/7<\tau<1/2
we have a Dirac threshold
d_{5}(n)=n^{-\tau}
. However, it turned out that the lower bound can be dramatically improved to match the upper bound and thus to establish
\bar{d}_{5}(n)=n^{-1/2}
. Again, this improvement was possible by analyzing the structure of
G_{\varepsilon}\cup G(n,p)
and showing that in
G(n,p)
alone either there are linearly many copies of
K_{4}
or a long 2-path, both quite unlikely when
p\leq n^{1/2-\mu}
. Finally, note that the over-thresholds for
m=5,6,9
are
not
of the form
n^{-1/f(\ell_{m})}
.

Appendix B
Proof of Claim
4.5

We can assume that
r<\ell-1
, as the case
\ell\in\{r,r+1\}
was already discussed at the beginning of the proof of Proposition
4.3
. Recall that
t\geq 2
. Suppose that
s=t
, that is
V_{i}\subset H
for
i=1,2,\dots,t-1
, and set
x=|V(H)\cap V_{t}|
. Notice that the maximum number of edges with at least one endpoint in
V(H)\cap V_{t}
is achieved when all
x
vertices form the leftmost subset of
V_{t}
. Therefore, we assume this throughout.

Case
0\leq x\leq r
:
Our aim is to show that

d_{t}=\frac{t\binom{\ell}{2}+(t-1)\binom{r+1}{2}}{t\ell-1}>\frac{(t-1){\binom{\ell}{2}}+(t-2){\binom{r+1}{2}}+rx}{(t-1)\ell+x-1}=d_{H},

where the second equality uses the fact that by our assumptions
H
consists of
t-1
copies of
K_{\ell}
, such that each consecutive two of them are joined by an
r
-bridge and there is an additional
x
-tuple of vertices in
V_{t}
in which each vertex has exactly
r
neighbours to the left of it in
H
.

Consider the function

\displaystyle f(\ell,r,t,x)=\left(t\binom{\ell}{2}+(t-1)\binom{r+1}{2}\right)\left((t-1)\ell+x-1\right)

\displaystyle-\left((t-1){\binom{\ell}{2}}+(t-2){\binom{r+1}{2}}+rx\right)\left(t\ell-1\right).

If we show that for
\ell,r,t,x
satisfying our assumptions
f(\ell,r,t,x)>0
, we will be done. Notice that
f(\ell,r,t,x)
is a linear function with respect to
x
, hence it is enough to verify that
f(\ell,r,t,0)>0
and
f(\ell,r,t,r)>0
. We have

\displaystyle f(\ell,r,t,0)

\displaystyle=\left(t\binom{\ell}{2}+(t-1)\binom{r+1}{2}\right)\left(\ell t-\ell-1\right)

\displaystyle-\left((t-1){\binom{\ell}{2}}+(t-2){\binom{r+1}{2}}\right)\left(\ell t-1\right)

\displaystyle=-\binom{\ell}{2}+\binom{r+1}{2}\left(\ell-1\right)=\frac{\ell-1}{2}\left(r(r+1)-\ell\right)>0,

since
r(r+1)>\ell
. Similarly, since
1-t<0
and
1\leq r<\ell-1
, we get

\displaystyle f(\ell,r,t,r)

\displaystyle=\left(t\binom{\ell}{2}+(t-1)\binom{r+1}{2}\right)\left(\ell t-\ell+r-1\right)

\displaystyle-\left((t-1){\binom{\ell}{2}}+(t-2){\binom{r+1}{2}}+r^{2}\right)\left(\ell t-1\right)

\displaystyle=\binom{\ell}{2}\left(rt-1\right)+\binom{r+1}{2}\left(rt+\ell-r-1\right)-\ell r^{2}t+r^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\ell^{2}rt-\ell^{2}-\ell rt+\ell+r^{3}t+\ell r^{2}-r^{3}-r^{2}+r^{2}t+\ell r-r^{2}-r-2\ell r^{2}t+2r^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left((\ell r^{2}-r^{3})+(-\ell r^{2}t+r^{3}t)+(\ell^{2}rt-\ell r^{2}t)+(-\ell rt+r^{2}t)+(-\ell^{2}+\ell r)+(\ell-r)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\ell-r}{2}\left(r^{2}-r^{2}t+\ell rt-rt-\ell+1\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\ell-r}{2}\left(r^{2}(1-t)+(\ell-1)rt-\ell+1\right)

\displaystyle>\frac{\ell-r}{2}\left((\ell-1)r(1-t)+(\ell-1)rt-\ell+1\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\ell-r}{2}(\ell-1)(r-1)\geq 0.

Case
r+1\leq x\leq\ell-1
:
This time we would like to show that

d_{t}=\frac{t\binom{\ell}{2}+(t-1)\binom{r+1}{2}}{t\ell-1}>\frac{(t-1){\binom{\ell}{2}}+(t-1){\binom{r+1}{2}}+\binom{x}{2}}{(t-1)\ell+x-1}=d_{H},

where the second equality uses the fact that by our assumptions
H
consists of
t-1
copies of
K_{\ell}
and one copy of
K_{x}
, and since
x\geq r+1
each two consecutive cliques in
H
are joined by an
r
bridge.

Consider the function

\displaystyle g(\ell,r,t,x)=\left(t\binom{\ell}{2}+(t-1)\binom{r+1}{2}\right)\left((t-1)\ell+x-1\right)

\displaystyle-\left((t-1){\binom{\ell}{2}}+(t-1){\binom{r+1}{2}}+\binom{x}{2}\right)\left(t\ell-1\right).

Once again we will show that
g(\ell,r,t,x)>0
for
\ell,r,t,x
satisfying our assumptions. Before we proceed, notice that

\displaystyle g(\ell,r,t,x)

\displaystyle=\binom{\ell}{2}(tx-1)+\binom{r+1}{2}(t-1)(x-\ell)-\binom{x}{2}(\ell t-1)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left((\ell^{2}tx-\ell^{2}-\ell tx+\ell)\right.

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ +(r^{2}tx-\ell r^{2}t-r^{2}x+\ell r^{2}+rtx-\ell rt-rx+\ell r)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ +\left.(-\ell tx^{2}+x^{2}+\ell tx-x)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left((\ell^{2}tx-\ell tx^{2})+(-\ell r^{2}t+r^{2}tx)+(\ell r^{2}-r^{2}x)+(-\ell rt+rtx)\right.

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ +\left.(-\ell^{2}+\ell x)+(\ell r-rx)+(-\ell x+x^{2})+(\ell-x)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\ell-x}{2}\left(\ell tx-r^{2}t+r^{2}-rt-\ell+r-x+1\right).

Hence, it is enough to show that

\displaystyle h(\ell,r,t,x)=\ell tx-r^{2}t+r^{2}-rt-\ell+r-x+1>0.

As before, the function
h
is linear with respect to
x
.
Furthermore, since
\ell t-1>0
this function is increasing with respect to
x
and so
h(\ell,r,t,x)\geq h(\ell,r,t,r+1)
. Thus, one only needs to check whether

h(\ell,r,t,r+1)>0
. Since
r\leq\ell-2
and
1-t<0
, we have

\displaystyle h(\ell,r,t,r+1)

\displaystyle=\ell t(r+1)-r^{2}t+r^{2}-rt-\ell

\displaystyle=(\ell-1)rt+r^{2}(1-t)+\ell(t-1)

\displaystyle\geq(\ell-1)rt+(\ell-2)r(1-t)+\ell(t-1)

\displaystyle=(r+t-1)\ell+r(t-2)>0.

This completes the proof of Proposition
4.3
. ∎

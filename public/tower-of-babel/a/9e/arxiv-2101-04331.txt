Equifocal submanifolds with non-flat section and topological Tits buildings
Naoyuki Koike

Abstract
From the Lytchak’s result for polar foliations on an irreducible simply connected symmetric space G / K G/K of compact type and rank greater than one, we can derive that there exists no equifocal submanifold with non-flat section whose codimension is greater than two in the symmetric space G / K G/K . In the first-half part of this paper, we give a new proof of this non-existence theorem. The recipi of our new proof is as follows. Suppose that there exists an equifocal submanifold M M with non-flat section whose codimension is greater than two in an irreducible symmertric space G / K G/K of compact type and rank greater than one. We introduce the notion of a slice topology of G / K G/K associated to M M . We consider the universal covering π : G / K ^ → G / K \pi:\widehat{G/K}\to G/K of the slice topological space G / K G/K and give G / K ^ \widehat{G/K} the manifold structure and the Riemannian metric such that π \pi is a Riemannian submersion onto the symmetric space G / K G/K . First we show that a simplicial decomposition of the Riemannian manifold G / K ^ \widehat{G/K} gives an irreducible topological Tits building of spherical type and rank greater than two. By applying Burns-Spatzier’s theorem to this topological Tits building, we show that the Riemannian manifold G / K ^ \widehat{G/K} is homothetic to the unit sphere. Furthermore, from this fact, we show that G / K G/K is isometric to a sphere, a complex projective space or a quaternionic projective space. This contradicts that G / K G/K is of rank greater than one. This is the recipi of our proof. In the second-half part, we estimate the codimension of M M from above by using the multiplicities of the roots of the root system of G / K G/K . As its result, we can show that there exists no equifocal submanifold with non-flat section in some irreducible simply connected symmetric spaces of compact type.

For equifocal submanifolds with non-flat section, some open problems remain, for example the following.

Open Problem.

Does there exist no equifocal submanifold with non-flat section in an irreducible simply connected
symmetric space of compact type and rank greater than one?

To solve this open problem, it is important to analyze the structure of the sections of the equifocal submanifold with non-flat section.
In 2008, we
[
Ko4
]
proved the following fact for the sections of an equifocal submanifold with non-flat section.

Fact 1.

The sections of an equifocal submanifold with non-flat section in an irreducible symmetric space of compact type are isometric to
a sphere or a real projective space of constant curvature.

In 2014, A. Lytchak (
[
L
]
) proved that polar foliations of codimension greater than two on an irreducible symmetric space of compact type and
rank greater than one are hyperpolar, where we note that he treated also the case where the symmetric space is reducible.
Since parallel submanifolds and focal submanifolds of an equifocal submanifold give a polar foliation, we can derive
the following non-existence theorem of equifocal submanifolds with non-flat section from Lytchak’s result.

Theorem A.

There eixsts no equifocal submanifold with non-flat section whose codimension is greater than two in an irreducible simply connected
symmetric space
G/K
of compact type and rank greater than one.

The recipi of his proof is as follows.
Let
\mathfrak{F}
be a polar foliation of codimension greater than two on an irreducible symmetric space
G/K
of compact type and rank greater than one.
He introduced a new metric called a
horizontal metric
on
G/K
associated to
\mathfrak{F}
, where we note that the topology on the leaves of

\mathfrak{F}
induced from the new metric are discrete. Denote by
d^{\rm hor}
this metric.
Suppose that
\mathfrak{F}
is not hyperpolar. Let
\pi:\widehat{G/K}\to G/K
be the universal covering of
(G/K,d^{\rm hor})
.
He showed that a simplicial decomposition of
\widehat{G/K}
gives an irreducible topological Tits building of spehrical type and rank greater than two
by using the result for metric characterizations of spherical buildings in
[
CL
]
.
By applying Burns-Spatzier’s theorem (see the next paragraph) to this topological Tits building, he showed that
\widehat{G/K}
is homeomorphic to a sphere.
Furthermore, from this fact, he showed that the fundamental group of
(G/K,d^{\rm hor})
is a trivial group, the circle group
U(1)
or the special unitary
group
SU(2)
and hence
G/K
is homeomorphic to a sphere, a complex projective space or a quaternionic projective space. This contradicts that
G/K
is
of rank greater than one. Therefore,
\mathfrak{F}
is hyperpolar. This is the recipi of his proof.

In 1987, K. Burns and R. Spatzier (
[

) classified irreducible topological Tits buildings of spherical type and rank greater than two
such that the topology of the set of all vertices is metric topology. In more detail, they showed that each of such irreducible topological
Tits buildings of spherical type is isomorphic to the classical topological Tits building

of spherical type associated to a simple non-compact (real) Lie group

In 1991, G. Thorbergsson (
[
Th
]
) constructed the topological Tits building
(\mathcal{B}_{M},\mathcal{O}_{M})
of spherical type associated to
a full irreducible isoparametric submanifold

of codimension greater than two in a Euclidean space. As above, according to the classification theorem of
Burns-Spatzier, the building
(\mathcal{B}_{M},\mathcal{O}_{M})
is isomorphic to the classical topological Tits building
(\mathcal{B}_{G^{\prime}},\mathcal{O}_{G^{\prime}})

associated to a simple non-compact (real) Lie group

onto the tangent space of the symmetric space

of non-compact type, where

By showing that this homeomorphism is an isometry and the image of the isoparametric submanifold by this homeomorphism is a principal orbit of
the isotropy representation of

We give a new proof of Theorem A by using Burns-Spatzier’s theorem and the discussion by Thorbergsson (
[
Th

\mathfrak{a}\ominus{\rm Span}\{{\bm{v}}_{0}\}
means
\mathfrak{a}\cap({\rm Span}\{{\bm{v}}_{0}\})^{\perp}
.
Let
H_{\alpha}
be the vector of
\mathfrak{a}
with
\alpha(\cdot)=\langle H_{\alpha},\cdot\rangle
and
set
\displaystyle{e_{\alpha}:=\frac{H_{\alpha}}{||H_{\alpha}||}}
, where

\langle\,\,,\,\,\rangle
is the inner product of
\mathfrak{p}
.
The shape tensor
A^{{\bm{v}}_{0}}
satisfies

A^{{\bm{v}}_{0}}_{\xi}|_{\mathfrak{p}_{\alpha}}=-\frac{\alpha(\xi)}{\alpha({\bm{v}}_{0})}{\rm id}\quad(\alpha\in\triangle_{+}\setminus\triangle_{+}^{{\bm{v}}_{0}},\,\,\,\xi\in T^{\perp}_{{\bm{v}}_{0}}M^{{\bm{v}}_{0}}).

[formula]

By using Lemma 5.1 and
(5.1)
, we can derive the following fact.

Proposition 5.3.

(i) If a vector subspace
\mathfrak{s}
of
\mathfrak{p}
is included by the cone
C(\rho(K)({\bm{v}}_{0}))
, then

{\rm dim}\,\mathfrak{s}\leq\mathop{\max}_{\alpha\in\triangle_{+}}(m_{\alpha}+m_{2\alpha})+1.

(ii)
\displaystyle{\frac{H_{\alpha}}{\|H_{\alpha}\|}}
(
\alpha\in\triangle_{+}
) only can be chracteristic directions of rank one
Lie triple systems.

Proof.
Let
k_{0}
be an element of
K
with
\rho(k_{0})({\bm{v}}_{0})\in\mathfrak{s}\cap S(1)
.
The set
\rho(k_{0})^{-1}(\mathfrak{s}\cap S(1))
is included by
M^{{\bm{v}}_{0}}:=C(\rho(K)({\bm{v}}_{0}))
by the assumption and
it is totally geodesic in
S(1)
. By using these facts and
(5.1)
, we obtain

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{T_{{\bm{v}}_{0}}(\rho(k_{0})^{-1}(\mathfrak{s}\cap S(1)))\subset\left\{{\bm{w}}\in T_{{\bm{v}}_{0}}M^{{\bm{v}}_{0}}\,|\,A^{{\bm{v}}_{0}}_{\xi}{\bm{w}}=0\,\,(\forall\,\xi\in T^{\perp}_{{\bm{v}}_{0}}M^{{\bm{v}}_{0}})\right\}}\\
\displaystyle{=\left\{\left.{\bm{w}}=\sum_{\alpha\in\triangle_{+}\setminus\triangle_{+}^{{\bm{v}}_{0}}}{\bm{w}}_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{p}\,\right|\,\sum_{\alpha\in\triangle_{+}\setminus\triangle_{+}^{{\bm{v}}_{0}}}\frac{\alpha(\xi)}{\alpha({\bm{v}}_{0})}{\bm{w}}_{\alpha}\in{\rm Span}\{{\bm{v}}_{0}\}\,\,(\forall\,\xi\in T^{\perp}_{{\bm{v}}_{0}}M^{{\bm{v}}_{0}})\right\}}\\
\displaystyle{=\left\{\left.{\bm{w}}=\sum_{\alpha\in\triangle_{+}\setminus\triangle_{+}^{{\bm{v}}_{0}}}{\bm{w}}_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{p}\,\right|\,\alpha(\xi){\bm{w}}_{\alpha}=0\,\,(\forall\,\xi\in\mathfrak{a}\ominus{\rm Span}\{{\bm{v}}_{0}\},\,\,\forall\,\alpha\in\triangle_{+}\setminus\triangle_{+}^{{\bm{v}}_{0}})\right\}}\\
\displaystyle{\subset\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\triangle_{+}\setminus\triangle_{+}^{{\bm{v}}_{0}}\,\,{\rm s.t.}\,\,H_{\alpha}\in{\rm Span}\{{\bm{v}}_{0}\}}\mathfrak{p}_{\alpha},}\end{array}

(
{\bm{w}}_{\alpha}:
the
\mathfrak{p}_{\alpha}
-component of
{\bm{w}}
). This implies that

\rho(k_{0})^{-1}(\mathfrak{s})\subset{\rm Span}\{H_{\alpha}\}\oplus\mathfrak{p}_{\alpha}\oplus\mathfrak{p}_{2\alpha}

[formula]

holds for some
\alpha\in\triangle_{+}
.
Therefore we can derive the statement (i).

Let
\mathfrak{t}
be a rank one Lie triple system and
{\bm{v}}_{0}
its characteristic direction.
Since
\mathfrak{t}
is included by the cone
C(\rho(K)({\bm{v}}_{0}))
by Lemma 5.1, it follows from the above proof of the statement (i) that

\rho(k_{0})^{-1}(\mathfrak{t})\subset{\rm Span}\{H_{\alpha}\}\oplus\mathfrak{p}_{\alpha}\oplus\mathfrak{p}_{2\alpha}

[formula]

holds for some
\alpha\in\triangle_{+}
. This implies that the characteristic direction of
\mathfrak{t}
equals to

\displaystyle{\frac{H_{\alpha}}{\|H_{\alpha}\|}}
. Therefore, the statement (ii) is derived. ∎

Now we shall prove Theorem B by using these lemmas and Fact 1 stated in Introduction.

Proof of Theorem B.
Let
M
be as in the statement of Theorem C. Denote by
r
the codimension of
M
.
According to Fact 1 stated in Introduction, the section
\Sigma_{x}
of
M
through
x
is a totally geodesic sphere (or real projective space)
of constant curvature in
G/K
. Take
p\in|(\mathcal{S}_{M})_{1}|\setminus|\mathcal{V}_{M}|
and the focal normal vector field
\widetilde{{\bm{v}}}
of
M

such that
p:=\eta_{\widetilde{{\bm{v}}}}(x)
.
Denote by
\mathcal{D}_{\widetilde{{\bm{v}}}}^{\perp}
the orthogonal complementary distribution of the focal distribution

\mathcal{D}_{\widetilde{{\bm{v}}}}
. Take any
{\bm{w}}\in(\mathcal{D}_{\widetilde{{\bm{v}}}}^{\perp})_{x}
. Let
\alpha:(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\to M
be
a
C^{\infty}
-curve satisfying
\alpha(0)=x
and
\alpha^{\prime}(s)\in(\mathcal{D}_{\widetilde{{\bm{v}}}}^{\perp})_{\alpha(s)}

(
s\in(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)
). Define a geodesic variation
\delta:[0,1]\times(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\to G/K
by

\delta(t,s):=\gamma_{\widetilde{{\bm{v}}}_{\alpha(s)}}(t)\quad\,\,((t,s)\in[0,1]\times(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)),

where
\gamma_{\widetilde{{\bm{v}}}_{\alpha(s)}}
is the normal geodesic of the direction
\widetilde{{\bm{v}}}_{\alpha(s)}
.
Set
\displaystyle{J:=\left.\frac{\partial\delta}{\partial s}\right|_{s=0}}
, which is the Jacobi field along the normal geodesic

\gamma_{\widetilde{{\bm{v}}}_{x}}
with
J(0)={\bm{w}}
and
J^{\prime}(0)=-A_{\widetilde{{\bm{v}}}_{x}}{\bm{w}}
.
Hence we have

J(s)=P_{\gamma_{\widetilde{{\bm{v}}}_{x}}|_{[0,s]}}\left(\cos\left(s\sqrt{\widetilde{R}(\widetilde{{\bm{v}}}_{x})}\right)({\bm{w}})-\frac{\sin\left(s\sqrt{\widetilde{R}(\widetilde{{\bm{v}}}_{x})}\right)}{\sqrt{\widetilde{R}(\widetilde{{\bm{v}}}_{x})}}(A_{\widetilde{{\bm{v}}}_{x}}{\bm{w}})\right),

where
P_{\gamma_{\widetilde{{\bm{v}}}_{x}}|_{[0,s]}}
is the parallel translation along
\gamma_{\widetilde{{\bm{v}}}_{x}}|_{[0,s]}
,

\widetilde{R}(\widetilde{{\bm{v}}}_{x})
is the normal Jacobi operator
\widetilde{R}(\cdot,\widetilde{{\bm{v}}}_{x})\widetilde{{\bm{v}}}_{x}
for
\widetilde{{\bm{v}}}_{x}

(
\widetilde{R}:
the cuvature tensor of
G/K
) and
\displaystyle{\cos\left(s\sqrt{\widetilde{R}(\widetilde{{\bm{v}}}_{x})}\right)}

(resp.
\displaystyle{\frac{\sin\left(s\sqrt{\widetilde{R}(\widetilde{{\bm{v}}}_{x})}\right)}{\sqrt{\widetilde{R}(\widetilde{{\bm{v}}}_{x})}}}
) is
defined by

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{\cos\left(s\sqrt{\widetilde{R}(\widetilde{{\bm{v}}}_{x})}\right):=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(2k)!}\,\widetilde{R}(\widetilde{{\bm{v}}}_{x})^{k}}\\
\displaystyle{\left({\rm resp.}\,\,\,\,\frac{\sin\left(s\sqrt{\widetilde{R}(\widetilde{{\bm{v}}}_{x})}\right)}{\sqrt{\widetilde{R}(\widetilde{{\bm{v}}}_{x})}}:=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(2k+1)!}\,\widetilde{R}(\widetilde{{\bm{v}}}_{x})^{k}\,\,\right).}\end{array}

[formula]

Here we used a general description of Jacobi fields in a symmetric space (see Section 3 of [TT] or (1.2) of [K1]).
Since
\Sigma_{x}
is totally geodesic in
G/K
,
\widetilde{R}({\bm{v}}_{x})
preserves
T_{x}\Sigma_{x}
invariantly.
Hence it preserves
T_{x}M
invariantly. From this fact, we can derive
J(1)\in P_{\gamma_{\widetilde{{\bm{v}}}_{x}}|_{[0,1]}}(T_{x}M)
.
Also, we have
J(1)=(d\eta_{\widetilde{{\bm{v}}}})_{x}({\bm{w}})
. Therefore, from the arbitrariness of
{\bm{w}}
, we obtain

T_{p}F_{\widetilde{{\bm{v}}}}=(d\eta_{\widetilde{{\bm{v}}}})_{x}(T_{x}M)\subset P_{\gamma_{\widetilde{{\bm{v}}}_{x}}|_{[0,1]}}(T_{x}M).

On the other hand, since
\Sigma_{x}
is totally geodesic in
G/K
, we have

P_{\gamma_{\widetilde{{\bm{v}}}_{x}}|_{[0,1]}}(T_{x}^{\perp}M)=P_{\gamma_{\widetilde{{\bm{v}}}_{x}}|_{[0,1]}}(T_{x}\Sigma_{x})=T_{p}\Sigma_{x}.

From these facts, it follows that
\Sigma_{x}
meets
F_{\widetilde{{\bm{v}}}}
orthogonally at
p
.
Similarly, we can show that, for any
y\in L^{\widetilde{{\bm{v}}}}_{x}
,
\Sigma_{y}
meets
F_{\widetilde{{\bm{v}}}}
orthogonally at
p
.
Hence we can derive
\sum\limits_{y\in L^{\widetilde{{\bm{v}}}}_{x}}T_{p}\Sigma_{y}\subset T_{p}^{\perp}F_{\widetilde{{\bm{v}}}}
.
Furthermore, since
L^{\widetilde{{\bm{v}}}}_{x}
is a fibre of the focal map
\eta_{\widetilde{{\bm{v}}}}:M\to F_{\widetilde{{\bm{v}}}}
, we can show that

\sum\limits_{y\in L^{\widetilde{{\bm{v}}}}_{x}}T_{p}\Sigma_{y}=T_{p}^{\perp}F_{\widetilde{{\bm{v}}}}
.
This implies that the normal umbrella
S_{p}:=\exp^{\perp}(T_{p}^{\perp}F_{\widetilde{{\bm{v}}}})
is equal to

\displaystyle{\mathop{\cup}_{y\in L^{\widetilde{{\bm{v}}}}_{x}}\Sigma_{y}}
.
Without loss of generality, we may assume that
p=eK
.
Since
\Sigma_{y}
’s
(y\in L^{\widetilde{{\bm{v}}}}_{x}
) are totally geodesic rank one symmetric spaces,
T_{p}\Sigma_{y}
’s
(y\in L^{\widetilde{{\bm{v}}}}_{x}
)
are rank one Lie triple systems of
\mathfrak{p}=T_{eK}(G/K)
. Also, since
p\in|(\mathcal{S}_{M})_{1}|\setminus|\mathcal{V}_{M}|
,
we can show
\displaystyle{{\rm dim}\left(\mathop{\cap}_{y\in L^{\widetilde{{\bm{v}}}}_{x}}\Sigma_{y}\right)=1}
.
According to Lemma 5.2, it follows from these facts that the Lie triple systems
T_{p}\Sigma_{y}
’s
(y\in L^{\widetilde{{\bm{v}}}}_{x}
) have the same
characteristic direction. Denote by
{\bm{v}}_{0}
this characteristic direction. According to Lemma 5.1,
T_{p}\Sigma_{y}
’s

(y\in L^{\widetilde{{\bm{v}}}}_{x}
) are included by the cone
C(\rho(K)({\bm{v}}_{0}))
and hence
T_{p}^{\perp}F_{\widetilde{{\bm{v}}}}
is also included by
the cone
C(\rho(K)({\bm{v}}_{0}))
. Therefore, by (i) of Proposition 5.3, we have

{\rm dim}\,T_{p}^{\perp}F_{\widetilde{{\bm{v}}}}\leq\mathop{\max}_{\alpha\in\triangle_{+}}(m_{\alpha}+m_{2\alpha})+1.

On the other hand, since
p\in|(\mathcal{S}_{M})_{1}|\setminus|\mathcal{V}_{M}|
again, we have
{\rm dim}\,L^{\widetilde{{\bm{v}}}}_{x}\geq r-1
.
From these facts, we can derive

r\leq\left(\mathop{\max}_{\alpha\in\triangle_{+}}(m_{\alpha}+m_{2\alpha})+1\right)-(r-1),

that is,

r\leq\left[\frac{1}{2}\,\mathop{\max}_{\alpha\in\triangle_{+}}(m_{\alpha}+m_{2\alpha})\right]+1.

∎

Sharp Local L p L^{p} estimates for the Hermite eigenfunctions
Xing Wang and Cheng Zhang

Abstract
. We investigate the concentration of eigenfunctions for the Hermite operator H = − Δ + | x | 2 H=-\Delta+|x|^{2} in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} by establishing local L p L^{p} bounds over the compact sets with arbitrary dilations and translations. These new results extend the local estimates by Thangavelu [ 62 ] and improve those derived from Koch-Tataru [ 45 ] , and explain the special phenomenon that the global L p L^{p} bounds decrease in p p when 2 ≤ p ≤ 2 ​ n + 6 n + 1 2\leq p\leq\frac{2n+6}{n+1} . The key L 2 L^{2} -estimates show that the local probabilities decrease away from the boundary { | x | = λ } \{|x|=\lambda\} , and then they satisfy Bohr’s correspondence principle in any dimension. The proof uses the Hermite spectral projection operator represented by Mehler’s formula for the Hermite-Schrödinger propagator e − i ​ t ​ H e^{-itH} , and the strategy developed by Thangavelu [ 62 ] and Jeong-Lee-Ryu [ 40 ] . We also exploit an explicit version of the stationary phase lemma and Hörmander’s L 2 L^{2} oscillatory integral theorem. Using Koch-Tataru’s strategy, we construct appropriate examples to illustrate the possible concentrations and show the optimality of our local estimates.

The Hermite functions are eigenfunctions of the one dimensional Hermite operator

-h^{\prime\prime}_{k}+x^{2}h_{k}=(2k+1)h_{k},\ k\in\mathbb{N}.

They are

h_{k}(x)=e^{x^{2}/2}(-1)^{k}\frac{d^{k}}{dx^{k}}e^{-x^{2}},\ k\in\mathbb{N}.

The Hermite functions form an
orthonormal basis in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
after normalization.

In dimension

satisfy

(-\Delta+|x|^{2})e_{\lambda}(x)=\lambda^{2}e_{\lambda}(x),\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

and a complete set of eigenfunctions is given by

(1.3)

h_{\alpha}(x)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}h_{\alpha_{j}}(x_{j})

where the corresponding eigenvalue is
\lambda^{2}=2|\alpha|+n
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
,
|\alpha|=\sum\alpha_{j}
. The multiplicity of the eigenvalue

\frac{(k+n-1)!}{k!(n-1)!}

The Hermite eigenfunctions are essentially concentrated in the ball
\{|x|\leq\lambda\}
and have an exponential Airy type decay beyond this threshold. As was observed in
[
45
]
, the behavior of eigenfunctions inside the ball
\{|x|\leq\lambda\}
is not very different from (a rescaling of) what happens in a bounded domain. But considerable care is required near the boundary
\{|x|=\lambda\}
, where the concentration scales are different. Consequently, Koch-Tataru
[
45
]
split the space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
into overlapping dyadic parts with respect to the distance to the boundary

D_{j}^{int}=\{\lambda-|x|\approx\lambda 2^{-2j}\},\ 1\leq 2^{j}\leq\lambda^{\frac{2}{3}}

D^{bd}=\{||x-\lambda|\leq\lambda^{-\frac{1}{3}}\}

D^{ext}=\{|x|>\lambda+\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{-\frac{1}{3}}\}.

Note that the thickness of the annulus
D_{j}^{int}
is comparable to its distance to the boundary.

To state the main theorem in
[
45
]
, we define the spaces
l_{\lambda}^{q}L^{p}
of functions in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with norm

\|f\|_{l_{\lambda}^{q}L^{p}}^{q}=\|f\|_{L^{p}\left(D^{ext}\right)}^{q}+\|f\|_{L^{p}\left(D^{bd}\right)}^{q}+\sum_{1\leq 2^{j}\leq\lambda^{2/3}}\|f\|_{L^{p}\left(D_{j}^{int}\right)}^{q}

with the usual modification when
q=\infty
.
For
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we let

y=\lambda^{-\frac{2}{3}}\left(\lambda^{2}-|x|^{2}\right),\quad\langle y\rangle_{-}=1+y_{-},\quad\langle y\rangle_{+}=1+y_{+}

Koch-Tataru
[
45
]
proved that for
2\leq p\leq\frac{2n+2}{n-1}
,

(1.8)

\left\|\lambda^{\frac{1}{3}-\frac{n}{3}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right)}\langle y\rangle_{+}^{-\frac{1}{4}+\frac{n+3}{4}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right)}\langle y\rangle_{-}^{1-\frac{n}{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right)}e_{\lambda}\right\|_{l_{\lambda}^{\infty}L^{p}}\lesssim\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}},

and for
\frac{2n+2}{n-1}\leq p\leq\infty
,

(1.9)

\left\|\lambda^{\frac{1}{3}-\frac{n}{3}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right)}\langle y\rangle_{+}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{n}{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right)}\langle y\rangle_{-}^{N}e_{\lambda}\right\|_{l_{\lambda}^{\infty}L^{p}}\lesssim\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}},\ \forall N.

As a corollary, one can obtain
L^{p}
bounds over these dyadic annuli. For
2\leq p\leq\frac{2n+2}{n-1}

(1.10)

\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{p}(D_{j}^{int})}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}}2^{j(\frac{n+1}{4}-\frac{n+3}{2p})}\|e_{\lambda}\|_{2},\ \ 1\leq 2^{j}\leq\lambda^{\frac{2}{3}},

(1.11)

\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{p}(D^{bd})}+\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{p}(D^{ext})}\lesssim\lambda^{-\frac{1}{3}+\frac{n}{3}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})}\|e_{\lambda}\|_{2}

and for
\frac{2n+2}{n-1}\leq p\leq\infty

(1.12)

\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{p}(D_{j}^{int})}\lesssim(\lambda 2^{-j})^{\frac{n-2}{2}-\frac{n}{p}}\|e_{\lambda}\|_{2},\ \ 1\leq 2^{j}\leq\lambda^{\frac{2}{3}},

(1.13)

\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{p}(D^{bd})}+\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{p}(D^{ext})}\lesssim\lambda^{-\frac{1}{3}+\frac{n}{3}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})}\|e_{\lambda}\|_{2}.

One may observe that the local
L^{2}
estimate over the dilated ball
D_{0}^{int}=\{|x|<\lambda/4\}
has no improvement on the trivial bound. Similarly, the local
L^{\frac{2n+6}{n+1}}
bounds in (
1.10
) cannot essentially improve the global estimate (
1.7
) over the whole space. Moreover, the local
L^{p}
bounds (
1.12
) over
D_{0}^{int}
strengthen Thangavelu’s estimates
[
62

(1.14)

\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{p}(B)}\leq C\lambda^{\frac{n-2}{2}-\frac{n}{p}}\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},\ \ \tfrac{2n+2}{n-1}\leq p\leq\infty.

where
B
is any fixed compact set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. This means that replacing

by a much larger dilated ball

does not affect the bounds. This interesting phenomenon can be explained by the existence of the Hermite eigenfunctions with point concentration near the origin. See Section 5 and
[
45

.

1.2.
Bohr’s correspondence principle

Now let’s go back to the starting point of this study. Bohr’s correspondence principle demands that classical physics and quantum physics give the same answer when the systems become large, see
[
11
,
63
]
. For the classical harmonic oscillator, when a particle’s potential energy is equal to its energy level, it moves at its slowest speed and has the highest probability of being detected around those points. We call these points turning points, which actually correspond to the boundary
\{|x|=\lambda\}
. By the correspondence principle, we would expect that for a quantum harmonic oscillator, for eigenstates with large energy, their probability density should also peak near these turning points in some sense. For the one-dimensional case, let

s^{-}(x)=\int_{0}^{x}\sqrt{|t^{2}-\lambda^{2}|}dt,\ \ \ \ s^{+}(x)=\int_{\lambda}^{x}\sqrt{|t^{2}-\lambda^{2}|}dt.

As in Szegö
[
58
, p. 201]
and Koch-Tataru
[
45
, Lemma 5.1]
, the normalized eigenfunctions
\tilde{h}_{k}=h_{k}/\|h_{k}\|_{2}
satisfy

\displaystyle\tilde{h}_{2k}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}a_{2k}^{-}\left(\lambda^{2}-x^{2}\right)^{-\frac{1}{4}}\left(\cos s^{-}(x)+\text{ error }\right)&|x|<\lambda-\lambda^{-\frac{1}{3}}\\
O(\lambda^{-\frac{1}{6}})&\lambda-\lambda^{-\frac{1}{3}}\leq x\leq\lambda+\lambda^{-\frac{1}{3}}\\
a_{2k}^{+}e^{-s^{+}(x)}\left(\lambda^{2}-x^{2}\right)^{-\frac{1}{4}}(1+\text{ error })&|x|>\lambda+\lambda^{-\frac{1}{3}}\end{cases}

\displaystyle\tilde{h}_{2k+1}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}a_{2k+1}^{-}\left(\lambda^{2}-x^{2}\right)^{-\frac{1}{4}}\left(\sin s^{-}(x)+\text{ error }\right)&|x|<\lambda-\lambda^{-\frac{1}{3}}\\
O(\lambda^{-\frac{1}{6}})&\lambda-\lambda^{-\frac{1}{3}}\leq x\leq\lambda+\lambda^{-\frac{1}{3}}\\
a_{2k+1}^{+}e^{-s^{+}(x)}\left(\lambda^{2}-x^{2}\right)^{-\frac{1}{4}}(1+\text{ error })&x>\lambda+\lambda^{-\frac{1}{3}}\end{cases}

where
|a_{k}^{\pm}|\sim 1
,
\text{ error }=O((\left|x^{2}-\lambda^{2}\right|^{-\frac{1}{2}}||x|-\lambda|^{-1})
.
Obviously the maximum values of Hermite eigenfunctions occur near the turning points
x=\pm\lambda
, see Figure
2
by Wolfram Mathematica.

Figure 2.
Hermite function
h_{100}

However, the eigenfunctions become more complicated in higher dimensions. Koch-Tataru
[
45
]
showed that there are eigenfunctions that attain maximal
L^{\infty}
growth near the origin. This phenomenon seems to violate Bohr’s correspondence principle, and suggests that it may be not suitable to measure only by the local
L^{\infty}
norm. A more reliable choice should be the local
L^{2}
norm, i.e. local probability, since the square of the amplitude is interpreted as a probability density. As we will see in Theorem
1
, the local probability over a compact set, such as the unit ball with arbitrary center, decreases as it moves away from the boundary
\{|x|=\lambda\}
. This satisfies Bohr’s correspondence principle.

To our best knowledge, compared to the Laplace eigenfunctions on compact manifolds, the picture of the Hermite eigenfunction estimates are still far from complete. See Section 6 for a list of related open problems. So we aim to investigate these problems in a series of works. In this paper, we first establish sharp estimates over the compact sets

1.4.
Paper structure and proof sketch

The paper is structured as follows. In Section 2, we presents the proof of the stationary phase lemma with a precise remainder term. In Section 3, we review the representation of the kernel of the Hermite spectral projection operator. In Section 4, we prove Theorem
1
. In Section 5, we show the sharpness of local
L^{p}
bounds. In Section 6, we discuss some related questions for the Hermite eigenfunctions.

Proof sketch of Theorem
1
.
We handle the Hermite spectral projection operator represented by Mehler’s formula for the kernel of the Hermite-Schrödinger propagator
e^{-itH}
. By the strategy developed by Thangavelu
[
61
,
60
,
62
]
and Jeong-Lee-Ryu
[
41
,
40
,
39
,
42
]
, we explicitly analyze the associated oscillatory integrals by the stationary phase lemma and Hörmander’s
L^{2}
oscillatory integral theorem
[
35
]
. The main difficulties lie in the discussions concerning the critical points of the phase functions in these oscillatory integrals, which require new insights. We find that in our local problem, the Hermite spectral projection operator essentially consists of two oscillatory integral operators with different phase functions, modulo some remainder terms. One phase function behaves like the Euclidean distance function, while the other one has no obvious geometric meaning. Nevertheless, both of the phase functions satisfy the mixed Hessian condition (with rank
=n-1
) in Hörmander’s
L^{2}
oscillatory integral theorem.

Proof sketch of Theorem
3
.
When
r\lesssim\lambda^{-1}\mu^{-\frac{1}{2}}
, we may apply Koch-Tataru’s
L^{\infty}
estimates (
1.12
), (
1.13
) and then the local

bounds follow from Hölder’s inequality. When
\lambda\mu\lesssim r\leq\lambda
, we may essentially cover the set

by the dyadic annuli

with
\lambda\tilde{\mu}\lesssim\lambda 2^{-2j}\lesssim r
(and the boundary annuli, if
\tilde{\mu}=\lambda^{-\frac{4}{3}}
), so the local

bounds are implied by Koch-Tataru’s

bounds over these annuli. When

, the set

is essentially covered by the annulus

2.
Stationary phase lemmas

In this section, we review the one dimensional stationary phase lemma, which is important to analyze the kernel of the spectral projection operator. It is classical and there are many excellent references, e.g. Hörmander
[
36
]
, Stein
[
57
]
and Sogge
[
54
]
. Using the idea in Hörmander
[
36
, Theorem 7.7.5]
, we prove the explicit stationary phase Lemma
4
with a more precise remainder term than the one presented in
[
36
]
.

2.1.
Explicit non-stationary phase lemma

Let
a\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
. Let
\phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
be real-valued. We estimate

I_{\lambda}=\int e^{i\lambda\phi(t)}a(t)dt.

Let
N\geq 1
,
b\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
and
\partial=\frac{d}{dt}
. We define

(\partial\circ b)(a)=\partial(ab)=a\partial b+b\partial a.

Note that

(\partial\circ b)^{N}(a)=\sum_{|\alpha|=N}c_{\alpha}\partial^{\alpha_{0}}a\partial^{\alpha_{1}}b\cdot\cdot\cdot\partial^{\alpha_{N}}b,

where
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{N})\in\mathbb{N}^{N}
, and
|\alpha|=\alpha_{0}+...+\alpha_{N}
.
For example,

(\partial\circ b)^{2}(a)=(\partial\circ b)(a\partial b+b\partial a)=b^{2}\partial^{2}a+3b\partial a\partial b+a\partial b\partial b+ab\partial^{2}b.

Moreover, for
j=1,...,N
,

\partial^{\alpha_{j}}(f^{-1})=f^{-\alpha_{j}-1}\sum_{|\gamma^{j}|=\alpha_{j}}c_{\gamma^{j}}\partial^{\gamma^{j}_{1}}f\cdot\cdot\cdot\partial^{\gamma^{j}_{\alpha_{j}}}f,

where
\gamma^{j}=(\gamma_{1}^{j},...,\gamma^{j}_{\alpha_{j}})
.
Here we use the convention that the sum is 1 if
\alpha_{j}=0
.

Suppose that
\phi^{\prime}\neq 0
on
\text{supp }a
. Integrating by parts
N
times, we have

\displaystyle I_{\lambda}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{-N}|\text{supp }a|\|(\partial\circ(\phi^{\prime-1}))^{N}(a)\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{-N}|\text{supp }a|\sum_{|\alpha|=N}\|(\partial^{\alpha_{0}}a)(\phi^{\prime})^{\alpha_{0}-2N}\prod_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{|\gamma^{j}|=\alpha_{j}}|\partial^{\gamma_{1}^{j}}(\phi^{\prime})\cdot\cdot\cdot\partial^{\gamma_{\alpha_{j}}^{j}}(\phi^{\prime})|\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{-N}|\text{supp }a|\sum_{\alpha_{0}=0}^{N}\sum_{|\beta|=N-\alpha_{0}}\|(\partial^{\alpha_{0}}a)(\phi^{\prime})^{\alpha_{0}-2N}\partial^{\beta_{1}}(\phi^{\prime})\cdot\cdot\cdot\partial^{\beta_{N-\alpha_{0}}}(\phi^{\prime})\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{-N}|\text{supp }a|\sum_{\alpha_{0}=0}^{N}\sum_{\sigma}\|(\partial^{\alpha_{0}}a)(\phi^{\prime})^{\alpha_{0}-2N+\sigma_{0}}(\phi^{\prime\prime})^{\sigma_{1}}\cdot\cdot\cdot(\phi^{(N-\alpha_{0}+1)})^{\sigma_{N-\alpha_{0}}}\|_{\infty}

where
\beta=(\beta_{1},...,\beta_{N-\alpha_{0}})
, and
\sigma=(\sigma_{0},...,\sigma_{N-\alpha_{0}})
satisfies
\sum k\sigma_{k}=N-\alpha_{0}=\sum\sigma_{k}
.

2.2.
Explicit stationary phase lemma

Suppose that
\phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
is real-valued and satisfies
\phi(0)=\phi^{\prime}(0)=0,\phi^{\prime\prime}(0)\neq 0
. Let

(2.1)

g(t)=\phi(t)-\frac{1}{2}\phi^{\prime\prime}(0)t^{2},

(2.2)

\phi_{\theta}(t)=\frac{1}{2}\phi^{\prime\prime}(0)t^{2}+\theta g(t),\ 0\leq\theta\leq 1.

Then
\phi_{1}=\phi
. Suppose that

|t|\leq|\phi^{\prime\prime}(0)|/\|\phi^{\prime\prime\prime}\|_{\infty},\ \ \forall t\in\text{supp }a.

Then

|g(t)/t^{3}|\leq\frac{1}{6}\|\phi^{\prime\prime\prime}\|_{\infty},

|g^{\prime}(t)/t^{2}|\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\phi^{\prime\prime\prime}\|_{\infty},

|g^{\prime\prime}(t)/t|\leq\|\phi^{\prime\prime\prime}\|_{\infty},

|g^{(k)}(t)|\leq\|\phi^{(k)}\|_{\infty},\ k\geq 3.

|t/\phi_{\theta}^{\prime}(t)|=\frac{1}{|\phi^{\prime\prime}(0)+\theta g^{\prime}(t)/t|}\leq\frac{1}{|\phi^{\prime\prime}(0)|-\frac{1}{2}|t|\|\phi^{\prime\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}}\leq\frac{2}{|\phi^{\prime\prime}(0)|},

|t/\phi_{\theta}^{\prime}(t)|=\frac{1}{|\phi^{\prime\prime}(0)+\theta g^{\prime}(t)/t|}\geq\frac{1}{|\phi^{\prime\prime}(0)|+\frac{1}{2}|t|\|\phi^{\prime\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}}\geq\frac{2}{3|\phi^{\prime\prime}(0)|}.

Thus

|\phi_{\theta}^{\prime}(t)|\approx|t||\phi^{\prime\prime}(0)|,

|\phi_{\theta}^{\prime\prime}(t)|=|\phi^{\prime\prime}(0)+\theta g^{\prime\prime}(t)|\leq 2|\phi^{\prime\prime}(0)|,

|\phi_{\theta}^{(k)}(t)|\leq\|\phi^{(k)}\|_{\infty},\ k\geq 3.

Let

I_{\lambda}(\theta)=\int e^{i\lambda\phi_{\theta}(t)}a(t)dt,\ \ a\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}).

For
m\geq 1
, we need to estimate

I_{\lambda}(1)=\sum_{k=0}^{2m-1}I^{(k)}_{\lambda}(0)/k!+\frac{1}{(2m-1)!}\int_{0}^{1}I_{\lambda}^{(2m)}(\theta)(1-\theta)^{2m-1}d\theta.

For the last term,

\displaystyle|I_{\lambda}^{(2m)}(\theta)|\lesssim\lambda^{2m}\Big|\int e^{i\lambda\phi_{\theta}(t)}g(t)^{2m}a(t)dt\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{2m}\lambda^{-3m}|\text{supp }a|\sum_{\alpha_{0}=0}^{3m}\sum_{\sigma}\|(\partial^{\alpha_{0}}(g^{2m}a))(\phi_{\theta}^{\prime})^{\alpha_{0}-6m+\sigma_{0}}(\phi_{\theta}^{\prime\prime})^{\sigma_{1}}\cdot\cdot\cdot(\phi_{\theta}^{(3m-\alpha_{0}+1)})^{\sigma_{3m-\alpha_{0}}}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{-m}|\text{supp }a|\sum_{\alpha_{0}=0}^{3m}\sum_{\gamma,\sigma}\|(\partial^{\gamma^{\prime}}a)g^{\gamma_{0}}(g^{\prime})^{\gamma_{1}}\cdot\cdot\cdot(g^{(\alpha_{0})})^{\gamma_{\alpha_{0}}}(\phi_{\theta}^{\prime})^{\alpha_{0}-6m+\sigma_{0}}(\phi_{\theta}^{\prime\prime})^{\sigma_{1}}\cdot\cdot\cdot(\phi_{\theta}^{(3m-\alpha_{0}+1)})^{\sigma_{3m-\alpha_{0}}}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{-m}|\text{supp }a|\sum_{\alpha_{0}=0}^{3m}\sum_{\gamma,\sigma}\|a^{(\gamma^{\prime})}\|_{\infty}|\phi^{\prime\prime}(0)|^{2\alpha_{0}-12m+3\gamma_{0}+2\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}+2\sigma_{0}+\sigma_{1}}\|\phi^{\prime\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}^{6m-\alpha_{0}-2\gamma_{0}-\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{3}-\sigma_{0}+\sigma_{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\times\prod_{k\geq 4}\|\phi^{(k)}\|_{\infty}^{\gamma_{k}+\sigma_{k-1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{-m}|\text{supp }a|(\|a\|_{\infty}|\phi^{\prime\prime}(0)|^{-3m}\|\phi^{\prime\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}^{2m}+......)

where
\gamma=(\gamma_{0},...,\gamma_{\alpha_{0}})
satisfies
\sum\gamma_{k}=2m
and
\gamma^{\prime}+\sum k\gamma_{k}=\alpha_{0}
, and
\sigma=(\sigma_{0},...,\sigma_{3m-\alpha_{0}})
satisfies
\sum k\sigma_{k}=3m-\alpha_{0}=\sum\sigma_{k}
.

In particular, if
|\phi^{\prime\prime}(0)|\approx B^{-1}
,
\|\phi^{\prime\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}\approx B^{-2}
, and
\|\phi^{(k)}\|_{\infty}\lesssim B^{1-k}
for
k\geq 4
, then we have an elegant estimate

(2.3)

|I_{\lambda}^{(2m)}(\theta)|\lesssim\lambda^{-m}|\text{supp }a|\sum_{k=0}^{3m}\|a^{(k)}\|_{\infty}B^{k-m},

by observing that

\displaystyle-(2\alpha_{0}-12m+3\gamma_{0}+2\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}+2\sigma_{0}+\sigma_{1})-2(6m-\alpha_{0}-2\gamma_{0}-\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{3}-\sigma_{0}+\sigma_{2})

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad-\sum_{k\geq 4}(k-1)(\gamma_{k}+\sigma_{k-1})

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k\geq 0}(k-1)\gamma_{k}-\sum_{k\geq 0}k\sigma_{k}

\displaystyle=2m-\alpha_{0}+\gamma^{\prime}-(3m-\alpha_{0})

\displaystyle=\gamma^{\prime}-m.

Fix
M\geq 3m-1
. For
k=0,1,...,2m-1
, let

(2.4)

a_{k}(t)=g(t)^{k}a(t),

where
g(t)
is defined in (
2.1
). Then
a_{k}^{(j)}(0)=0
for
j=0,1,...,3k-1
.

Recall the Fourier transform formula for Gaussian functions (e.g.
[
36
, Theorem 7.6.1]
) for
\omega,t\in\mathbb{R}

e^{i\frac{1}{2}\omega t^{2}}=\frac{e^{\frac{i\pi}{4}{\text{sgn}}\omega}}{\sqrt{2\pi|\omega|}}\int e^{-itx}e^{-i\frac{x^{2}}{2\omega}}dx.

Then we obtain

\displaystyle I_{\lambda}^{(k)}(0)

\displaystyle=(i\lambda)^{k}\int e^{i\lambda\frac{1}{2}\phi^{\prime\prime}(0)t^{2}}a_{k}(t)dt

\displaystyle=(i\lambda)^{k}\frac{e^{\frac{i\pi}{4}{\text{sgn}}\phi^{\prime\prime}(0)}}{\sqrt{2\pi|\phi^{\prime\prime}(0)|\lambda}}\int e^{-\frac{i}{2\phi^{\prime\prime}(0)\lambda}\xi^{2}}\hat{a}_{k}(\xi)d\xi

\displaystyle=(i\lambda)^{k}\frac{e^{\frac{i\pi}{4}{\text{sgn}}\phi^{\prime\prime}(0)}}{\sqrt{2\pi|\phi^{\prime\prime}(0)|\lambda}}\int\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{M-1}(\frac{-i}{2\phi^{\prime\prime}(0)\lambda})^{j}\frac{\xi^{2j}}{j!}+(\frac{-i}{2\phi^{\prime\prime}(0)\lambda})^{M}\frac{\xi^{2M}}{M!}e^{i\eta}\Big)\hat{a}_{k}(\xi)d\xi,

\displaystyle=(i\lambda)^{k}\frac{e^{\frac{i\pi}{4}{\text{sgn}}\phi^{\prime\prime}(0)}}{\sqrt{2\pi|\phi^{\prime\prime}(0)|\lambda}}\cdot(2\pi)\Big(\sum_{j=\lceil\frac{3k}{2}\rceil}^{M-1}(\frac{i}{2\phi^{\prime\prime}(0)\lambda})^{j}\frac{a^{(2j)}_{k}(0)}{j!}\Big)+R_{k}

where
\eta
is between
0
and
\frac{-\xi^{2}}{2\phi^{\prime\prime}(0)\lambda}
. The remainder term
R_{k}
satisfies

\displaystyle|R_{k}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{k-\frac{1}{2}-M}|\phi^{\prime\prime}(0)|^{-\frac{1}{2}-M}\int|\xi^{2M}\hat{a}_{k}(\xi)|d\xi.

In summary, we state the explicit stationary phase lemma.

Lemma 4
.

Let
\phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
be real-valued and satisfy
\phi(0)=\phi^{\prime}(0)=0,\ \phi^{\prime\prime}(0)\neq 0
. Suppose that
a\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
satisfies

\text{supp }a\subset[-B,B]
for some
B>0
. If
|\phi^{\prime\prime}(0)|\approx B^{-1}
,
\|\phi^{\prime\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}\approx B^{-2}
, and
\|\phi^{(k)}\|_{\infty}\lesssim B^{1-k}
for
k\geq 4
, then for
m\geq 1
and
M\geq 3m+1
,
we have

\displaystyle\Big|\int e^{i\lambda\phi(t)}a(t)dt-\sum_{k=0}^{2m-1}\sum_{j=\lceil\frac{3k}{2}\rceil}^{M-1}c_{kj}\lambda^{k-j-\frac{1}{2}}|\phi^{\prime\prime}(0)|^{-j-\frac{1}{2}}a_{k}^{(2j)}(0)\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{-m}|\text{supp }a|\sum_{k=0}^{3m}\|a^{(k)}\|_{\infty}B^{k-m}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+(\lambda B^{-1})^{-\frac{1}{2}-M}\sum_{k=0}^{2m-1}\lambda^{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\xi^{2M}\hat{a}_{k}(\xi)|d\xi,

where

a_{k}(t)=(\phi(t)-\tfrac{1}{2}\phi^{\prime\prime}(0)t^{2})^{k}a(t),

c_{kj}=\frac{i^{k+j}\sqrt{2\pi}}{j!k!2^{j}}({\text{sgn}}\phi^{\prime\prime}(0))^{j}e^{\frac{i\pi}{4}{\text{sgn}}\phi^{\prime\prime}(0)}.

Here
L^{\infty}
norms are taken over
\text{supp }a
, and
\hat{a}_{k}(\xi)
is the Fourier transform of
a_{k}(t)
on
\mathbb{R}
. The implicit constants are independent of
\lambda,\phi,a,B
, and only depend on
m
and
M
.

The remainder term in Lemma
4
is more precise than the one presented in
[
36
, Theorem 7.7.5]
. Indeed, the proof in
[
36
]
uses Sobolev inequalities to simplify the integrals involving
\hat{a}_{k}(\xi)
and estimates them by the sup norms of the derivatives of
a
. It does not exploit the rapid decay of the Fourier transform
\hat{a}_{k}(\xi)
, which is crucial in our applications.

3.
The spectral projection operator

In this section, we introduce the representation formula of the Hermite spectral projection operator. It is known that the kernel of the Hermite spectral projection operator
P_{\lambda}
can be represented by Mehler’s formula for the kernel of the Hermite-Schrödinger propagator
e^{-itH}
. One may refer to Jeong-Lee-Ryu
[
40
, Section 2.1]
for a detailed introduction. Let

P_{\lambda}f=\sum_{2|\alpha|+n=\lambda^{2}}\langle f,h_{\alpha}\rangle h_{\alpha}

where
\{h_{\alpha}\}
is an orthonormal basis in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
. Each
h_{\alpha}
is an eigenfunction of
H
with eigenvalue
\lambda^{2}=2|\alpha|+n
, and is a tensor product of the Hermite functions on
\mathbb{R}
.

Since
\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}e^{i\frac{t}{2}s}dt=0
for
s\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
, formally we have

P_{\lambda}f=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}e^{i\frac{t}{2}(\lambda^{2}-H)}fdt.

See
[
40
, Section 2.1]
for a detailed proof. Recall the formula (see
[
50
]
,
[
60
, p. 11]
)

e^{-i\frac{t}{2}H}f=c_{n}(\sin t)^{-\frac{n}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{ip(t,x,y)}f(y)dy

where
c_{n}=(2\pi i)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{in\pi/4}
,
p(t,x,y)=\frac{a^{2}\cos t-2b}{2\sin t}
with
a^{2}=|x|^{2}+|y|^{2}
and
b=x\cdot y
.
Then the kernel of
P_{\lambda}
can be written as

(3.1)

K(x,y)=c_{n}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}(\sin t)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{i\phi_{\lambda}(t,x,y)}dt

where

\phi_{\lambda}(t,x,y)=\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}t+\frac{a^{2}\cos t-2b}{2\sin t}.

In applications, it is more convenient to deal with the rescaled kernel with
R=\lambda^{2}

(3.2)

K_{R}(x,y)=K(\lambda x,\lambda y)=2c_{n}\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}(\sin 2t)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{iR\psi(t,x,y)}dt

where

\psi(t,x,y)=t+\frac{a^{2}\cos 2t-2b}{2\sin 2t}.

Here the square root
z^{\frac{1}{2}}=\sqrt{|z|}e^{i\frac{1}{2}\arg(z)},\ \arg(z)\in(-\pi,\pi]
.
By inserting smooth cutoff functions, we split the integral (
3.2
) into four parts

(3.3)

\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}=\int_{0}^{3\pi/8}+\int_{-3\pi/8}^{0}+\int_{-\pi/2}^{-\pi/8}+\int_{\pi/8}^{\pi/2}=I_{0}+I_{0}^{-}+I_{1}+I_{1}^{-}.

By changing variables, we obtain

I_{0}=\int_{0}^{3\pi/8}(\sin 2t)^{-\frac{n}{2}}\rho_{0}(t)e^{iR\psi(t,x,y)}dt,

I_{0}^{-}=e^{-i\frac{n\pi}{2}}\int_{0}^{3\pi/8}(\sin 2t)^{-\frac{n}{2}}\rho_{0}(t)e^{-iR\psi(t,x,y)}dt,

I_{1}=e^{-i\frac{\pi}{2}(n+R)}\int_{0}^{3\pi/8}(\sin 2t)^{-\frac{n}{2}}\rho_{0}(t)e^{iR\psi(t,x,-y)}dt,

I_{1}^{-}=e^{i\frac{\pi}{2}R}\int_{0}^{3\pi/8}(\sin 2t)^{-\frac{n}{2}}\rho_{0}(t)e^{-iR\psi(t,x,-y)}dt,

where
\rho_{0}
is a smooth even function supported in
[-\frac{3\pi}{8},\frac{3\pi}{8}]
satisfying
\rho_{0}\equiv 1
near 0 and
\rho_{0}\equiv 0
near
\pm\frac{3\pi}{8}
, and
\rho_{0}(t)+\rho_{0}(\frac{\pi}{2}-t)\equiv 1
for
t\in[0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
. In the following, we mainly deal with
I_{0}
, and the other terms can be handled similarly.

4.
Proof of Theorem
1

In this section, we prove Theorem
1
. The proof uses the strategy developed by Thangavelu
[
61
,
60
,
62
]
and Jeong-Lee-Ryu
[
41
,
40
,
39
,
42
]
. They handle the oscillatory integrals by an explicit analysis on the critical points of the phase functions. By a standard
TT^{*}
argument, we only need to prove the operator bound (
4.3
) associated with the rescaled kernel
K_{R}(x,y)
. Since the kernel
K_{R}(x,y)
is represented as an oscillatory integral in Section 3, we analyze the critical points of the phase function, and then split the integral properly into several parts with respect to the critical points. For the parts away from the critical points, we can handle them using integration by parts and Young’s inequality. The crucial parts are those around the critical points. We need to use the stationary phase lemma in Section 2 to calculate the kernel explicitly and then apply Hörmander’s
L^{2}
oscillatory integral theorem (see e.g.
[
35
]
,
[
54
, Theorem 2.1.1]
).

Let
n\geq 1
,
w=\lambda^{-1}\nu
,
\mu=\max\{\lambda^{-\frac{4}{3}},1-|w|\}
and
R=\lambda^{2}
.

First, we only need to prove

(4.1)

\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(B(\nu,r))}\leq C\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}r^{\frac{1}{2}}\mu^{-\frac{1}{4}}\|e_{\lambda}\|_{2},\ \ \text{for}\ \lambda^{-1}\mu^{-\frac{1}{2}}\ll r\ll\lambda\mu,

since the remaining two cases
r\lesssim\lambda^{-1}\mu^{-\frac{1}{2}}
and
\lambda\mu\lesssim r\leq\lambda
can be handled easily. Indeed, when
r\lesssim\lambda^{-1}\mu^{-\frac{1}{2}}
, we use the
L^{\infty}
bounds in (
1.12
) and (
1.13
) to obtain the desired local
L^{2}
bound
(\lambda\mu^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\frac{n-2}{2}}r^{\frac{n}{2}}
. When

Note that

Let

Note that

It suffices to prove

\|T\|_{L^{2}(B(w,R^{-\frac{1}{2}}r))\to L^{2}(B(w,R^{-\frac{1}{2}}r))}\lesssim R^{-\frac{n}{2}}(R\mu)^{-\frac{1}{2}}r

under the assumption that

4.1.
Proof of the operator bound (
4.3
)

Let

. Let
D(x,y)=b^{2}-a^{2}+1
. Since
\mu=1-|w|\gg R^{-\frac{1}{2}}r

1-b=2\mu-\mu^{2}+O(R^{-\frac{1}{2}}r)

\sqrt{D}=\sqrt{(1-b)^{2}-|x-y|^{2}}=2\mu-\mu^{2}+O(R^{-\frac{1}{2}}r).

Thus,

1-|x|\approx 1-|y|\approx 1-b\approx\sqrt{D}\approx\mu

By (

After rescaling, we still have the same

This operator bound implies the uniform bound of the kernel for

|K_{R}(x,y)|\lesssim(R\mu)^{\frac{n-2}{2}}.

Consider the truncated kernel

K_{R}(x,y)(1-\eta(|x-y|/r_{0}))

is supported in

(2,\infty)

By Young’s inequality, (
4.4
) and

, the operator associated with the kernel (
4.5
) has

norm bounded by

which is desired. So in the following we only need to handle the truncated kernel

where
|x-y|\geq r_{0}
on the support. Recall that (

We only handle

To analyze the oscillatory integral

, we calculate the derivative of the phase function

By solving

A simple calculation gives

A crucial observation is that

and

. So

, which contradicts to the assumption above. Later we will use this observation in (

.

We can write

\displaystyle\psi^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\sin^{2}2t}(\cos 2t-\cos 2t_{1})(\cos 2t-\cos 2t_{2})

(4.9)

\displaystyle=-\frac{4}{\sin^{2}2t}\sin(t+t_{1})\sin(t-t_{1})\sin(t+t_{2})\sin(t-t_{2})

Next, we split the integral
I_{0}
into the following five parts.

(1)

0<t\ll t_{1}

(2)

t\approx t_{1}

(3)

t_{1}\ll t\ll t_{2}

(4)

t\approx t_{2}

(5)

t_{2}\ll t

Let

be a Littlewood-Paley bump function, we will use it for smooth cutoff, and for dyadic decompositions several times later.

We first handle the three parts

using integration by parts and Young’s inequality.

Part (1):
For
t\ll t_{1}
, by (
4.1
) we have

(4.10)

|\psi^{(k)}(t)|\approx t^{-1-k}t_{1}^{2}t_{2}^{2}\approx t^{-1-k}|x-y|^{2},\ k=1,2,....

We split the integral

(4.11)

\eta(|x-y|/r_{0})\int\eta_{0}(t/t_{1})\rho_{0}(t)(\sin 2t)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{iR\psi}dt=\sum_{2^{-\ell}>r_{0}}\sum_{2^{-j}\ll t_{1}}K_{j\ell}(x,y)

where
\eta_{0}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
is supported in
(-\infty,\frac{3}{4})
and equal to 1 in
(-\infty,\frac{1}{2})
.

By (
4.10
), integration by parts gives

|K_{j\ell}(x,y)|\lesssim 2^{\frac{n-2}{2}j}(R2^{j}2^{-2\ell})^{-N},\ \forall N.

Then by Young’s inequality the operator
T_{j\ell}
associated with the kernel
K_{j\ell}
has
L^{2}-L^{2}
norm bounded by

2^{\frac{n-2}{2}j}(R2^{j}2^{-2\ell})^{-N}\cdot 2^{-n\ell}.

Since

,
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{2^{-\ell}>r_{0}}\sum_{2^{-j}\ll t_{1}}\|T_{j\ell}\|_{2\to 2}

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{-\frac{n}{2}}(R\mu)^{\frac{n-2}{2}-\frac{n}{2}}\cdot(R\mu^{\frac{1}{2}}r_{0})^{-N-\frac{n-2}{2}+n}

\displaystyle=R^{-\frac{n}{2}}(R\mu)^{-\frac{1}{2}}r\cdot(R\mu r^{2})^{-N_{1}-\frac{1}{2}},

which is better than desired if
N
is large enough, since
R\mu r^{2}\gtrsim 1
. Here
N_{1}=\frac{1}{2n}(N+\tfrac{n-2}{2}-n)>0.

Part (3):
For
t_{1}\ll t\ll t_{2}
, by (
4.1
) we get

(4.12)

|\psi^{(k)}(t)|\approx t^{-1-k}t^{2}t_{2}^{2}\approx t^{1-k}\mu,\ k=1,2,....

We dyadically decompose the integral as

(4.13)

\eta(|x-y|/r_{0})\int\eta_{1}(t)\rho_{0}(t)(\sin 2t)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{iR\psi}dt=\sum_{t_{1}\ll 2^{-j}\ll t_{2}}K_{j}(x,y)

K_{j}(x,y)=\eta(|x-y|/r_{0})\int\beta(2^{j}t)\eta_{1}(t)\rho_{0}(t)(\sin 2t)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{iR\psi}dt,

where
\eta_{1}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\frac{3}{2}t_{1},\frac{3}{4}t_{2})
satisfies
\eta_{1}\equiv 1
in
(2t_{1},\frac{1}{2}t_{2})
.

By (
4.12
), integration by parts gives

|K_{j}(x,y)|\lesssim 2^{\frac{n-2}{2}j}(1+R\mu 2^{-j})^{-N},\ \forall N.

Since
t_{1}\approx|x-y|\mu^{-\frac{1}{2}}\geq r_{0}\mu^{-\frac{1}{2}}\gg R^{-1}\mu^{-1}
, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{t_{1}\ll 2^{-j}\ll t_{2}}|K_{j}(x,y)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{t_{1}\ll 2^{-j}\ll t_{2}}2^{\frac{n-2}{2}j}(R\mu 2^{-j})^{-N}

\displaystyle\lesssim t_{1}^{-\frac{n-2}{2}}(R\mu t_{1})^{-N}

\displaystyle\lesssim(|x-y|\mu^{-\frac{1}{2}})^{-\frac{n-2}{2}}(1+R\mu^{\frac{1}{2}}|x-y|)^{-N}.

By Young’s inequality, the operator associated with the kernel given by (
4.13
) has
L^{2}-L^{2}
norm bounded by

\displaystyle\mu^{\frac{n-2}{4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x|^{-\frac{n-2}{2}}(1+R\mu^{\frac{1}{2}}|x|)^{-N}dx

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{-\frac{n}{2}}(R\mu)^{\frac{n-2}{2}-\frac{n}{2}}

\displaystyle=R^{-\frac{n}{2}}(R\mu)^{-\frac{1}{2}}r\cdot(R\mu r^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}},

this is better than desired, since
R\mu r^{2}\gtrsim 1
.

Part (5):
For
t\gg t_{2}
, by (
4.1
) we have

(4.14)

|\psi^{(k)}(t)|\approx t^{3-k},\ k=1,2,....

We need to estimate the integral

(4.15)

\eta(|x-y|/r_{0})\int\eta_{2}(t/t_{2})\rho_{0}(t)(\sin 2t)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{iR\psi}dt

where
\eta_{2}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
is supported in
(\frac{3}{2},\infty)
and equal to 1 in
(2,\infty)
. By (
4.14
) and

, integration by parts gives the bound for the integral

Then by Young’s inequality, the operator associated with the kernel given by (
4.15
) has
L^{2}-L^{2}
norm bounded by

\displaystyle\mu^{-\frac{n-2}{4}}(R\mu^{\frac{3}{2}})^{-N}(R^{-\frac{1}{2}}r)^{n}

\displaystyle=R^{-\frac{n}{2}}(R\mu)^{-\frac{1}{2}}r\cdot(R\mu^{\frac{3}{2}})^{-N+\frac{1}{2}}(\mu r^{-4})^{-\frac{n-1}{4}}

(4.16)

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{-\frac{n}{2}}(R\mu)^{-\frac{1}{2}}r

if

is large enough. The last inequality uses

when

, and uses

.

Next, we handle the remaining two parts (2) and (4) by the explicit stationary phase lemma and Hörmander’s

Part (2): Contribution of the first critical point.

For
t\approx t_{1}
, by (
4.1
) we obtain

(4.17)

\psi^{\prime\prime}(t_{1})=\frac{4\sqrt{D}}{\sin 2t_{1}}\approx\mu t_{1}^{-1}

(4.18)

|\psi^{\prime\prime\prime}(t)|\approx\mu t_{1}^{-2}

(4.19)

|\psi^{(k)}(t)|\lesssim\mu t_{1}^{1-k},\ k=2,3,....

We need to estimate the kernel given by the oscillatory integral

(4.20)

\eta(|x-y|/r_{0})\int\beta(t/t_{1})\rho_{0}(t)(\sin 2t)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{iR\psi}dt.

Let

a(t)=\beta(t/t_{1})\rho_{0}(t)(\sin 2t)^{-\frac{n}{2}}

g(t)=\psi(t)-\psi(t_{1})-\tfrac{1}{2}\psi^{\prime\prime}(t_{1})(t-t_{1})^{2}

a_{k}(t)=g(t)^{k}a(t),\ k=0,1,2,....

Thus, by (
4.17
), (
4.18
), (
4.19
) we have

|a_{k}^{(j)}(t)|\lesssim\mu^{k}t_{1}^{-\frac{n}{2}+k-j},\ j=0,1,2,...

|\hat{a}_{k}(\xi)|\lesssim\mu^{k}t_{1}^{-\frac{n}{2}+k+1}(1+t_{1}|\xi|)^{-N},\ \forall N.

Then by the stationary phase Lemma
4
, we have the following expansion with a remainder term estimate

\displaystyle\Big|\int e^{iR\psi(t)}a(t)dt-\sum_{k=0}^{2m-1}\sum_{j=\lceil\frac{3k}{2}\rceil}^{M-1}c_{kj}R^{k-j-\frac{1}{2}}e^{iR\psi(t_{1})}|\psi^{\prime\prime}(t_{1})|^{-\frac{1}{2}-j}a_{k}^{(2j)}(t_{1})\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim(R\mu t_{1})^{-m}t_{1}^{1-\frac{n}{2}}+\sum_{k=0}^{2m-1}(R\mu t_{1})^{k-\frac{1}{2}-M}t_{1}^{1-\frac{n}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim t_{1}^{1-\frac{n}{2}}((R\mu t_{1})^{-m}+(R\mu t_{1})^{2m-M-\frac{3}{2}})

\displaystyle\lesssim t_{1}^{1-\frac{n}{2}}(R\mu t_{1})^{-m}

where

are constant coefficients.

Since
t_{1}\approx|x-y|\mu^{-\frac{1}{2}}
, the kernel corresponding to the remainder term is bounded by

(4.21)

\eta(|x-y|/r_{0})(R\mu)^{\frac{n-2}{2}}(R\mu^{\frac{1}{2}}|x-y|)^{-m-\frac{n}{2}+1}.

Then by Young’s inequality, the operator associated with (
4.21
) has
L^{2}-L^{2}
norm bounded by

\displaystyle(R\mu)^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\int_{|x|>r_{0}}(R\mu^{\frac{1}{2}}|x|)^{-m-\frac{n}{2}+1}dx

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{-\frac{n}{2}}(R\mu)^{\frac{n-2}{2}-\frac{n}{2}}\cdot(R\mu^{\frac{1}{2}}r_{0})^{-m-\frac{n-2}{2}+n}

\displaystyle=R^{-\frac{n}{2}}(R\mu)^{-\frac{1}{2}}r\cdot(R\mu r^{2})^{-N-\frac{1}{2}},

which is better than desired if
m
is large enough, since
R\mu r^{2}\gtrsim 1
. Here
N=\frac{1}{2n}(m+\tfrac{n-2}{2}-n)>0.

Since all the terms in the expansion share the same oscillatory factor, it suffices to handle the leading term

(4.22)

\eta(|x-y|/r_{0})R^{-\frac{1}{2}}D^{-\frac{1}{4}}(\sin 2t_{1})^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}e^{iR\psi(t_{1})},

where we use (
4.17

Recall that
\mu\gg R^{-\frac{1}{2}}r
,

|\partial_{x,y}^{\alpha}\sqrt{D}|\leq C_{\alpha}\mu^{1-\alpha}.

Step 1 of Part (2): Analyze the first critical point.

Recall that

\psi(t,x,y)=t+\frac{a^{2}\cos 2t-2b}{2\sin 2t},

\cos 2t_{1}=b+\sqrt{D},

(4.24)

\psi_{t}^{\prime}(t_{1}(x,y),x,y)\equiv 0.

We have

(4.25)

\sin^{2}2t_{1}=a^{2}-2b^{2}-2b\sqrt{D}=|x-y|^{2}\Big(1+\frac{2b}{1-b+\sqrt{D}}\Big).

Let

\xi(x,y)=\sqrt{1+\frac{2b}{1-b+\sqrt{D}}}.

Then

(4.26)

\sin 2t_{1}=|x-y|\xi(x,y),

where
\xi\in C^{\infty}
and

\xi(x,y)\approx\mu^{-\frac{1}{2}}

(4.27)

|\partial_{x,y}^{\alpha}\xi(x,y)|\lesssim\mu^{-\frac{1}{2}-\alpha}.

Thus, we obtain

t_{1}(x,y)\approx|x-y|\mu^{-\frac{1}{2}}

(4.28)

|\partial_{x,y}^{\alpha}t_{1}(x,y)|\lesssim|x-y|^{1-\alpha}\mu^{\frac{1}{2}},\ x\neq y.

Moreover,

(4.29)

\psi(t_{1},x,y)=|x-y|\Big(\frac{(2-a^{2})t_{1}}{2|x-y|}+\frac{a^{2}(t_{1}-\tan t_{1})}{2|x-y|}+\frac{|x-y|}{2\sin 2t_{1}}\Big)=|x-y|\zeta(x,y),

where
\zeta\in C^{\infty}
and

\zeta(x,x)=\sqrt{1-|x|^{2}}\approx\sqrt{\mu}

(4.30)

|\partial^{\alpha}_{x,y}\zeta(x,y)|\lesssim\mu^{\frac{1}{2}-\alpha}

by (
4.26
) and (
4.27
).

Taking partial derivatives on (
4.24
) we get

\partial_{x}[\psi_{t}^{\prime}(t_{1}(x,y),x,y)]=\psi_{tt}^{\prime\prime}\partial_{x}t_{1}+\psi_{tx}^{\prime\prime}\equiv 0,

\partial_{x}[\psi(t_{1}(x,y),x,y)]=\psi_{t}^{\prime}\partial_{x}t_{1}+\psi^{\prime}_{x}=\psi^{\prime}_{x}.

Then

(4.31)

\partial_{y}\partial_{x}[\psi(t_{1},x,y)]=\psi_{tx}^{\prime\prime}\partial_{y}t_{1}+\psi_{xy}^{\prime\prime}=-\psi_{tt}^{\prime\prime}\partial_{x}t_{1}\partial_{y}t_{1}+\psi_{xy}^{\prime\prime}.

Let
I_{n}
be the

identity matrix. Note that

\psi_{xy}^{\prime\prime}(t_{1},x,y)=-\frac{1}{\sin 2t_{1}}I_{n},

(4.32)

\psi_{tt}^{\prime\prime}(t_{1},x,y)=\frac{4}{\sin^{3}2t_{1}}(a^{2}\cos 2t_{1}-b(1+\cos^{2}2t_{1}))=\frac{4\sqrt{D}}{\sin 2t_{1}},

\partial_{x}t_{1}=\frac{1}{2\sin 2t_{1}}(\frac{x-by}{\sqrt{D}}-y)=\frac{x-y\cos 2t_{1}}{2\sin 2t_{1}\sqrt{D}}

\partial_{y}t_{1}=\frac{1}{2\sin 2t_{1}}(\frac{y-bx}{\sqrt{D}}-x)=\frac{y-x\cos 2t_{1}}{2\sin 2t_{1}\sqrt{D}}

\partial_{x}t_{1}\partial_{y}t_{1}=\frac{(x-y\cos 2t_{1})(y-x\cos 2t_{1})^{T}}{4D\sin^{2}2t_{1}}

Then

(4.33)

\partial_{y}\partial_{x}[\psi(t_{1},x,y)]=\frac{(x-y\cos 2t_{1})(x\cos 2t_{1}-y)^{T}}{\sqrt{D}\sin^{3}2t_{1}}-\frac{1}{\sin 2t_{1}}I_{n}.

Note that

(x\cos 2t_{1}-y)^{T}(x-y\cos 2t_{1})=\sqrt{D}\sin^{2}2t_{1}.

Then by (
4.33
) the eigenvalues of the matrix
\partial_{y}\partial_{x}[\psi(t_{1},x,y)]
are
-\frac{1}{\sin 2t_{1}}
(multiplicity
=n-1
) and 0.

Step 2 of Part (2): Apply oscillatory integral theorem.

Since
x,y\in B(w,R^{-\frac{1}{2}}r)
, by (
4.22
) we need to estimate the
L^{2}-L^{2}
norm of the operator
T
associated with the kernel

\displaystyle K(x,y)=R^{-\frac{1}{2}}D^{-\frac{1}{4}}(\sin 2t_{1})^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}e^{iR\psi(t_{1},x,y)}\chi(R^{\frac{1}{2}}r^{-1}(x-w))\chi(R^{\frac{1}{2}}r^{-1}(y-w))\eta(|x-y|/r_{0}),

where
\chi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Since the phase function
\psi(t_{1},x,y)
behaves like the rescaled distance function, we may use an argument similar to Burq-Gérard-Tzvetkov
[
17
, Section 6]
.

We split the kernel
K
and consider the operators
T_{j}
associated with the kernel

K_{j}(x,y)=K(x,y)\beta(2^{j}|x-y|),\ \ r_{0}<2^{-j}<R^{-\frac{1}{2}}r

where

is given by (
4.6
).

Next, we introduce a partition of unity locally finite (uniformly with respect to

):

1=\sum_{p\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\chi(2^{j}(x-w)-p),

where
\chi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Write

K_{j}(x,y)=\sum_{q,\tilde{q}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\chi(2^{j}(x-w)-q)K_{j}(x,y)\chi(2^{j}(y-w)-\tilde{q}):=\sum_{q,\tilde{q}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}K_{j,q,\tilde{q}}(x,y).

Note that on the support of
\chi(2^{j}(x-w)-p)
we have
|2^{j}(x-w)-p|\lesssim 1
. So

|q-\tilde{q}|\leq|2^{j}(x-w)-q|+|2^{j}(x-w)-2^{j}(y-w)|+|2^{j}(y-w)-\tilde{q}|\lesssim 1

on the support of
K_{j,q,\tilde{q}}(x,y)
. Let
T_{j,q,\tilde{q}}
be the operator with kernel
K_{j,q,\tilde{q}}(x,y)
. Then we need to handle

T_{j}=\sum_{q,\tilde{q}}T_{j,q,\tilde{q}}.

Note that the kernel of

\int\chi(2^{j}(x-w)-p)\overline{K_{j}(x,z)}\chi(2^{j}(z-w)-\tilde{p})\chi(2^{j}(z-w)-q)K_{j}(z,y)\chi(2^{j}(y-w)-\tilde{q})dz

vanishes when
|(p,\tilde{p})-(q,\tilde{q})|\gtrsim 1
. Thus, Cotlar–Stein lemma (
[
57

) implies

\|T_{j}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\leq\sup_{p,\tilde{p}}\sum_{q,\tilde{q}}\sqrt{\|T_{j,p,\tilde{p}}^{*}T_{j,q,\tilde{q}}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}}\lesssim\sup_{q,\tilde{q}}\|T_{j,q,\tilde{q}}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}.

To estimate
\|T_{j,q,\tilde{q}}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}
, we consider the kernel of
T_{j,q,\tilde{q}}

K_{j,q,\tilde{q}}(x,y)=\chi(2^{j}(x-w)-q)K_{j}(x,y)\chi(2^{j}(y-w)-\tilde{q}).

Let
X=2^{j}(x-w)
,
Y=2^{j}(y-w)
. Let
\tilde{T}_{j,q,\tilde{q}}
be the operator associated with the rescaled kernel

\displaystyle\tilde{K}_{j,q,\tilde{q}}(X,Y)=\chi(X-q)K_{j}(2^{-j}X+w,2^{-j}Y+w)\chi(Y-\tilde{q})

\displaystyle=\chi(X-q)\chi(Y-\tilde{q})\beta(|X-Y|)K(2^{-j}X+w,2^{-j}Y+w)

\displaystyle=\chi(X-q)\chi(Y-\tilde{q})\chi(R^{\frac{1}{2}}r^{-1}2^{-j}X)\chi(R^{\frac{1}{2}}r^{-1}2^{-j}Y)\beta(|X-Y|)e^{iR\psi_{j}(X,Y)}(\sin 2\tau_{j})^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}R^{-\frac{1}{2}}D_{j}^{-\frac{1}{4}}

where

D_{j}(X,Y)=D(2^{-j}X+w,2^{-j}Y+w)

\tau_{j}(X,Y)=t_{1}(2^{-j}X+w,2^{-j}Y+w)

\psi_{j}(X,Y)=\psi(\tau_{j},2^{-j}X+w,2^{-j}Y+w).

Since
|X-Y|\approx 1
on the support of
\beta(|X-Y|)
, by (
4.26
), (
4.27
), (
4.29
) and (
4.30
) we have

(4.35)

\sqrt{D_{j}(X,Y)}\to 1-|w|^{2}\approx\mu

(4.36)

2^{j}\sin(2\tau_{j}(X,Y))\to|X-Y|\sqrt{1+\frac{|w|^{2}}{2(1-|w|^{2})}}\approx|X-Y|\mu^{-\frac{1}{2}}

(4.37)

2^{j}\psi_{j}(X,Y)\to|X-Y|\sqrt{1-|w|^{2}}\approx|X-Y|\mu^{\frac{1}{2}}

in
C^{\infty}
topology as
j\to\infty
.
So for large
j
we can write the kernel as

\displaystyle\tilde{K}_{j,q,\tilde{q}}(X,Y)=2^{\frac{n-1}{2}j}\mu^{\frac{n-3}{4}}R^{-\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle e^{iR\psi_{j}(X,Y)}A_{j}(w,X,Y)

\displaystyle\cdot\chi(X-q)\chi(Y-\tilde{q})\chi(R^{\frac{1}{2}}r^{-1}2^{-j}X)\chi(R^{\frac{1}{2}}r^{-1}2^{-j}Y)\beta(|X-Y|)

where
A_{j}(w,X,Y)
is a smooth function on the support of the kernel and satisfies

(4.38)

|\partial^{\alpha}_{X,Y}A_{j}(w,X,Y)|\lesssim 1.

The implicit constant is independent of
j,\mu,w,q,\tilde{q},R,X,Y
.

By Hörmander’s oscillatory integral theorem, we have

\|\tilde{T}_{j,q,\tilde{q}}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\lesssim 2^{\frac{n-1}{2}j}R^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mu^{\frac{n-3}{4}}(R2^{-j}\mu^{\frac{1}{2}})^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}.

By rescaling,

\|T_{j,q,\tilde{q}}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}=2^{-nj}\|\tilde{T}_{j,q,\tilde{q}}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\lesssim R^{-\frac{n}{2}}\mu^{-\frac{1}{2}}2^{-j}.

By (
4.34
), we get

\|T_{j}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\lesssim R^{-\frac{n}{2}}\mu^{-\frac{1}{2}}2^{-j}.

Then

\sum_{2^{-j}<R^{-1/2}r}\|T_{j}\|_{2\to 2}\lesssim R^{-\frac{n}{2}}(R\mu)^{-\frac{1}{2}}r,

which is expected.

Part (4): Contribution of the second critical point.

For
t\approx t_{2}
, by (
4.1
) we obtain

(4.39)

|\psi^{\prime\prime}(t_{2})|=\frac{4\sqrt{D}}{\sin 2t_{2}}\approx\mu^{\frac{1}{2}}

(4.40)

|\psi^{\prime\prime\prime}(t)|\approx 1

(4.41)

|\psi^{(k)}(t)|\lesssim\mu t_{2}^{1-k}\lesssim\mu^{\frac{3-k}{2}},\ k=2,3,....

We need to estimate the kernel given by the oscillatory integral

(4.42)

\eta(|x-y|/r_{0})\int\beta(t/t_{2})\rho_{0}(t)(\sin 2t)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{iR\psi}dt.

Let

a(t)=\beta(t/t_{2})\rho_{0}(t)(\sin 2t)^{-\frac{n}{2}}

g(t)=\psi(t)-\psi(t_{2})-\tfrac{1}{2}\psi^{\prime\prime}(t_{2})(t-t_{2})^{2}

a_{k}(t)=g(t)^{k}a(t),\ k=0,1,2,....

Thus, by (
4.39
), (
4.40
), (
4.41
) we have

|a_{k}^{(j)}(t)|\lesssim\mu^{k}t_{2}^{-\frac{n}{2}+k-j},\ j=0,1,2,...

|\hat{a}_{k}(\xi)|\lesssim\mu^{k}t_{2}^{-\frac{n}{2}+k+1}(1+t_{2}|\xi|)^{-N},\ \forall N.

Then by the stationary phase Lemma
4
, we have the following expansion with a remainder term estimate

\displaystyle\Big|\int e^{iR\psi(t)}a(t)dt-\sum_{k=0}^{2m-1}\sum_{j=\lceil\frac{3k}{2}\rceil}^{M-1}c_{kj}R^{k-j-\frac{1}{2}}e^{iR\psi(t_{2})}|\psi^{\prime\prime}(t_{2})|^{-\frac{1}{2}-j}a_{k}^{(2j)}(t_{2})\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim(R\mu t_{2})^{-m}t_{2}^{1-\frac{n}{2}}+\sum_{k=0}^{2m-1}(R\mu t_{2})^{k-\frac{1}{2}-M}t_{2}^{1-\frac{n}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim t_{2}^{1-\frac{n}{2}}((R\mu t_{2})^{-m}+(R\mu t_{2})^{2m-M-\frac{3}{2}})

\displaystyle\lesssim t_{2}^{1-\frac{n}{2}}(R\mu t_{2})^{-m}

where
c_{kj}
are constant coefficients.

Since
t_{2}\approx\mu^{\frac{1}{2}}
, the kernel corresponding to the remainder term is bounded by

(4.43)

\mu^{\frac{2-n}{4}}(R\mu^{\frac{3}{2}})^{-m}.

Then by Young’s inequality, the operator associated with (
4.43
) has
L^{2}-L^{2}
norm bounded by

\displaystyle\mu^{\frac{2-n}{4}}(R\mu^{\frac{3}{2}})^{-m}\int_{|x|\leq R^{-1/2}r}dx

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{-\frac{n}{2}}(R\mu^{\frac{3}{2}})^{-m}\mu^{\frac{2-n}{4}}r^{n}

\displaystyle=R^{-\frac{n}{2}}(R\mu)^{-\frac{1}{2}}r\cdot(R\mu^{\frac{3}{2}})^{-m+\frac{1}{2}}(\mu r^{-4})^{-\frac{n-1}{4}}

(4.44)

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{-\frac{n}{2}}(R\mu)^{-\frac{1}{2}}r

if
m
is large enough. The last inequality uses
\mu\gg R^{-\frac{1}{2}}r
when
Rr^{6}\gtrsim 1
, and uses
\mu r^{-4}\gg 1
when
Rr^{6}\lesssim 1
.

Since all the terms in the expansion share the same oscillatory factor, it suffices to handle the leading term

(4.45)

\eta(|x-y|/r_{0})R^{-\frac{1}{2}}D^{-\frac{1}{4}}(\sin 2t_{2})^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}e^{iR\psi(t_{2})},

where we use (
4.39
). We split (
4.45
) into two parts

K_{1}(x,y)=(1-\eta(|x-y|/r_{0}))R^{-\frac{1}{2}}D^{-\frac{1}{4}}(\sin 2t_{2})^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}e^{iR\psi(t_{2})}

K(x,y)=R^{-\frac{1}{2}}D^{-\frac{1}{4}}(\sin 2t_{2})^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}e^{iR\psi(t_{2})}.

We can handle
K_{1}
by Young’s inequality, since it is bounded and has small support. Indeed, the operator associated with
K_{1}
has
L^{2}-L^{2}
norm bounded by

\displaystyle R^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mu^{-\frac{n+1}{4}}r_{0}^{2n/p}

\displaystyle=(R\mu)^{\frac{n-2}{2}}(R\mu^{\frac{3}{2}})^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}r_{0}^{n}

(4.46)

\displaystyle\lesssim(R\mu)^{\frac{n-2}{2}}r_{0}^{n}

\displaystyle=R^{-\frac{n}{2}}(R\mu)^{-\frac{1}{2}}r.

Here we use (
4.7
) and
R\mu^{\frac{3}{2}}\gtrsim 1
. So it remains to handle
K
.

Step 1 of Part (4): Analyze the second critical point.

The calculation is similar to Step 1 of Part (2), but the behavior of the phase function associated with the second critical point are very different. So we still provide all the details. Recall that
\mu\gg R^{-\frac{1}{2}}r
,
1-|x|\approx 1-|y|\approx 1-b\approx\sqrt{D}\approx\mu
and

(4.47)

|\partial_{x,y}^{\alpha}\sqrt{D}|\leq C_{\alpha}\mu^{1-\alpha}.

Moreover,

\psi(t,x,y)=t+\frac{a^{2}\cos 2t-2b}{2\sin 2t},

\cos 2t_{2}=b-\sqrt{D},

(4.48)

\psi_{t}^{\prime}(t_{2}(x,y),x,y)\equiv 0.

Note that

(4.49)

\sin^{2}2t_{2}=a^{2}-2b^{2}+2b\sqrt{D}=|x-y|^{2}+2b(1-b+\sqrt{D}).

Thus, we get

t_{2}(x,y)\approx\mu^{\frac{1}{2}}

(4.50)

|\partial_{x,y}^{\alpha}t_{2}(x,y)|\lesssim\mu^{\frac{1}{2}-\alpha}.

We may write

(4.51)

\psi(t_{2},x,y)=\frac{1}{2}(2-a^{2})t_{2}+\frac{a^{2}}{2}(t_{2}-\tan t_{2})+\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{2\sin 2t_{2}},

then we obtain

(4.52)

|\partial_{x,y}^{\alpha}\psi(t_{2}(x,y),x,y)|\lesssim\mu^{\frac{3}{2}-\alpha}.

Taking partial derivatives on (
4.48
) we get

\partial_{x}[\psi_{t}^{\prime}(t_{2}(x,y),x,y)]=\psi_{tt}^{\prime\prime}\partial_{x}t_{2}+\psi_{tx}^{\prime\prime}\equiv 0,

\partial_{x}[\psi(t_{2}(x,y),x,y)]=\psi_{t}^{\prime}\partial_{x}t_{2}+\psi^{\prime}_{x}=\psi^{\prime}_{x}.

Then

(4.53)

\partial_{y}\partial_{x}[\psi(t_{2},x,y)]=\psi_{tx}^{\prime\prime}\partial_{y}t_{2}+\psi_{xy}^{\prime\prime}=-\psi_{tt}^{\prime\prime}\partial_{x}t_{2}\partial_{y}t_{2}+\psi_{xy}^{\prime\prime}.

Note that

\psi_{xy}^{\prime\prime}(t_{2},x,y)=-\frac{1}{\sin 2t_{2}}I_{n},

(4.54)

\psi_{tt}^{\prime\prime}(t_{2},x,y)=\frac{4}{\sin^{3}2t_{2}}(a^{2}\cos 2t_{2}-b(1+\cos^{2}2t_{2}))=-\frac{4\sqrt{D}}{\sin 2t_{2}},

\partial_{x}t_{2}=\frac{1}{2\sin 2t_{2}}(\frac{by-x}{\sqrt{D}}-y)=\frac{y\cos 2t_{2}-x}{2\sin 2t_{2}\sqrt{D}}

\partial_{y}t_{2}=\frac{1}{2\sin 2t_{2}}(\frac{bx-y}{\sqrt{D}}-x)=\frac{x\cos 2t_{2}-y}{2\sin 2t_{2}\sqrt{D}}

\partial_{x}t_{2}\partial_{y}t_{2}=\frac{(y\cos 2t_{2}-x)(x\cos 2t_{2}-y)^{T}}{4D\sin^{2}2t_{2}}.

Then

(4.55)

\partial_{y}\partial_{x}[\psi(t_{2},x,y)]=\frac{(y\cos 2t_{2}-x)(x\cos 2t_{2}-y)^{T}}{\sqrt{D}\sin^{3}2t_{2}}-\frac{1}{\sin 2t_{2}}I_{n}.

Note that

(x\cos 2t_{2}-y)^{T}(y\cos 2t_{2}-x)=\sqrt{D}\sin^{2}2t_{2}.

Then by (
4.55
) the eigenvalues of the matrix
\partial_{y}\partial_{x}[\psi(t_{2},x,y)]
are
-\frac{1}{\sin 2t_{2}}
(multiplicity
=n-1
) and 0.

Step 2 of Part (4): Apply oscillatory integral theorem.

By rotational symmetry, we may assume that
w=(1-\mu,0,...,0)
. Since
x,y\in B(w,R^{-\frac{1}{2}}r)\subset B(w,\mu)
, we consider the operator
T
with the kernel

(4.56)

K(x,y)=R^{-\frac{1}{2}}D^{-\frac{1}{4}}(\sin 2t_{2})^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}e^{iR\psi(t_{2},x,y)}\chi((x-w)/\mu)\chi((y-w)/\mu).

Here
\chi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Unlike the first critical point, the phase function
\psi(t_{2},x,y)
does not behave like the distance function
|x-y|
, so we need to use a different argument to handle the associated oscillatory integral operator.

We first simplify the matrix in (
4.55
). Note that
x=w+O(R^{-\frac{1}{2}}r)
,
y=w+O(R^{-\frac{1}{2}}r)
. The big oh notations are in the sense of some matrix norm. We have

(y\cos 2t_{2}-x)(x\cos 2t_{2}-y)^{T}=ww^{T}(1-\cos 2t_{2})^{2}+O(R^{-\frac{1}{2}}r),

where
ww^{T}=diag((1-\mu)^{2},0,...,0)
. Moreover,

1-b=2\mu-\mu^{2}+O(R^{-\frac{1}{2}}r)

\sqrt{D}=\sqrt{(1-b)^{2}-|x-y|^{2}}=2\mu-\mu^{2}+O(R^{-\frac{1}{2}}r)

\displaystyle\frac{(1-\cos 2t_{2})^{2}}{\sqrt{D}\sin^{2}2t_{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(1-b+\sqrt{D})^{2}}{\sqrt{D}(1-(b-\sqrt{D})^{2})}=\frac{1-b+\sqrt{D}}{\sqrt{D}(2-(1-b)-\sqrt{D})}

\displaystyle=\frac{2(2\mu-\mu^{2})+O(R^{-\frac{1}{2}}r)}{(2\mu-\mu^{2}+O(R^{-\frac{1}{2}}r))(2-2(2\mu-\mu^{2})+O(R^{-\frac{1}{2}}r))}

(4.57)

\displaystyle=(1-\mu)^{-2}+O(\mu^{-1}R^{-\frac{1}{2}}r).

Here we use the fact that
1-\mu\approx 1
. See the observation before (
4.1
).

Thus,

\displaystyle\partial_{y}\partial_{x}[\psi(t_{2}(x,y),x,y)]

\displaystyle=\frac{(y\cos 2t_{2}-x)(x\cos 2t_{2}-y)^{T}}{\sqrt{D}\sin^{3}2t_{2}}-\frac{1}{\sin 2t_{2}}I_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sin 2t_{2}}\Big(ww^{T}((1-\mu)^{-2}+O(\mu^{-1}R^{-\frac{1}{2}}r))-I_{n}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sin 2t_{2}}((1-\mu)^{-2}ww^{T}-I_{n})+O(\mu^{-\frac{3}{2}}R^{-\frac{1}{2}}r)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\sin 2t_{2}}diag(0,1,...,1)+O(\mu^{-\frac{3}{2}}R^{-\frac{1}{2}}r).

Let
x=(x_{1},x^{\prime}),\ y=(y_{1},y^{\prime})
. Then

\partial_{y^{\prime}}\partial_{x^{\prime}}[\psi(t_{2}(x,y),x,y)]=-\frac{1}{\sin 2t_{2}}I_{n-1}+O(\mu^{-\frac{3}{2}}R^{-\frac{1}{2}}r).

This implies

|\det\partial_{y^{\prime}}\partial_{x^{\prime}}[\psi(t_{2}(x,y),x,y)]|\approx(\mu^{-\frac{1}{2}})^{n-1},

as
\mu\gg R^{-\frac{1}{2}}r
.

Recall the derivatives estimates in (
4.23
), (
4.50
), (
4.52
)

|\partial^{\alpha}_{x,y}\sqrt{D(x,y)}|\lesssim\mu^{1-\alpha}

|\partial_{x,y}^{\alpha}t_{2}(x,y)|\lesssim\mu^{\frac{1}{2}-\alpha}

|\partial_{x,y}^{\alpha}\psi(t_{2}(x,y),x,y)|\lesssim\mu^{\frac{3}{2}-\alpha}.

Consider the operator
T_{\mu}
with the rescaled kernel

\displaystyle K(\mu X,\mu Y)

\displaystyle=R^{-\frac{1}{2}}D(\mu X,\mu Y)^{-\frac{1}{4}}e^{iR\psi(t_{2}(\mu X,\mu Y),\mu X,\mu Y)}(\sin 2t_{2}(\mu X,\mu Y))^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}

\displaystyle=R^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mu^{-\frac{n+1}{4}}e^{iR\mu^{\frac{3}{2}}\Phi(\mu,X,Y)}A(\mu,X,Y)\chi(X-w/\mu)\chi(Y-w/\mu)

where

|\det\partial_{X^{\prime}}\partial_{Y^{\prime}}\Phi(\mu,X,Y)|\approx 1,

|\partial_{X,Y}^{\alpha}\Phi(\mu,X,Y)|\lesssim 1

|\partial_{X,Y}^{\alpha}A(\mu,X,Y)|\lesssim 1.

These bounds follow from rescaling the derivatives estimates above, and the implicit constants are independent of
\mu,w,R,X,Y
.

For fixed
X_{1},Y_{1}
, we define

T_{\mu,X_{1},Y_{1}}g(X)=\int K(\mu X,\mu Y)g(Y^{\prime})dY^{\prime}.

Suppose that supp
f\subset B(w/\mu,R^{-\frac{1}{2}}r/\mu)\subset B(w/\mu,1)
. Then by Minkovski and Hölder inequalities,

\displaystyle\|T_{\mu}f\|_{L^{2}(B(w/\mu,R^{-\frac{1}{2}}r/\mu)}^{2}=\int_{|X_{1}-1+\mu|<R^{-\frac{1}{2}}r}\|\int_{|Y_{1}-1+\mu|<R^{-\frac{1}{2}}r}T_{\mu,X_{1},Y_{1}}(f(Y_{1},\cdot))dY_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}^{2}dX_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{|X_{1}-1+\mu|<R^{-\frac{1}{2}}r}\Big(\int_{|Y_{1}-1+\mu|<R^{-\frac{1}{2}}r}\|T_{\mu,X_{1},Y_{1}}(f(Y_{1},\cdot))\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}dY_{1}\Big)^{2}dX_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{-\frac{1}{2}}r\mu^{-1}\int_{|X_{1}-1+\mu|<R^{-\frac{1}{2}}r}\int_{|Y_{1}-1+\mu|<R^{-\frac{1}{2}}r}\|T_{\mu,X_{1},Y_{1}}(f(Y_{1},\cdot))\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}^{2}dY_{1}dX_{1}

\displaystyle\leq R^{-\frac{1}{2}}r\mu^{-1}\int_{|X_{1}-1+\mu|<R^{-\frac{1}{2}}r}\int\|T_{\mu,X_{1},Y_{1}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}^{2}\|f(Y_{1},\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}^{2}dY_{1}dX_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{-1}r^{2}\mu^{-2}\sup_{X_{1},Y_{1}}\|T_{\mu,X_{1},Y_{1}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}^{2}\|f\|_{L^{2}(B(w/\mu,R^{-\frac{1}{2}}r/\mu))}^{2}.

Thus, by Hörmander’s
L^{2}
oscillatory integral theorem we have

\displaystyle\|T_{\mu}\|_{L^{2}(B(w/\mu,R^{-\frac{1}{2}}r/\mu))\to L^{2}(B(w/\mu,R^{-\frac{1}{2}}r/\mu))}

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{-\frac{1}{2}}r\mu^{-1}\sup_{X_{1},Y_{1}}\|T_{\mu,X_{1},Y_{1}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{-\frac{1}{2}}r\mu^{-1}\cdot(R\mu^{\frac{3}{2}})^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}\cdot R^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mu^{-\frac{n+1}{4}}

\displaystyle=R^{-\frac{n+1}{2}}\mu^{-n-\frac{1}{2}}r.

By rescaling,

\displaystyle\|T\|_{L^{2}(B(w,R^{-\frac{1}{2}}r))\to L^{2}(B(w,R^{-\frac{1}{2}}r))}

\displaystyle=\mu^{n}\|T_{\mu}\|_{L^{2}(B(w/\mu,R^{-\frac{1}{2}}/\mu))\to L^{2}(B(w/\mu,R^{-\frac{1}{2}}/\mu))}

\displaystyle\lesssim R^{-\frac{n}{2}}(R\mu)^{-\frac{1}{2}}r

which is exactly the desired bound. So we complete the proof.

5.
Proof of the sharpness

In this section, we construct new examples to prove the sharpness of local
L^{p}
bounds.
The sharpness means that for each pair of
\nu,r
, there exist eigenfunctions saturating the bound (
1.19
). We shall use the strategy by Koch-Tataru
[
45
]
.

5.1.
Construction of eigenfunctions in
D_{j}^{int}
.

For
1\leq 2^{j}\leq\lambda^{\frac{2}{3}}
,
\lambda^{-1}2^{j}\ll\delta\lesssim 2^{-j/2}
, and fixed
x_{1}^{*}>0
with
\lambda-x_{1}^{*}\approx\lambda 2^{-2j}
, let

\mathcal{T}_{j,\delta}=\{x=(x_{1},x^{\prime})\in D_{j}^{int}:|x_{1}-x_{1}^{*}|\ll\lambda 2^{-j}\delta^{2},\ |x^{\prime}|\ll\delta\}.

Then we can construct the normalized eigenfunctions (dependent on
\lambda,\ j,\ \delta
) so that for most
x\in\mathcal{T}_{j,\delta}
we have

(5.1)

\frac{e_{\lambda}(x)}{\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}\approx\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}2^{j/2}\delta^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}.

To see this, we shall modify the construction of Koch-Tataru’s Example 5.4
[
45
]
. Fix a positive integer
N
and we consider the eigenfunctions with eigenvalue
\lambda^{2}=n+2N
. We consider the set of indices

I=\{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}:|\alpha|=N,\ \alpha_{k}\ \text{even}\ \text{and}\ \alpha_{k}\approx\delta^{-2}\ \text{for}\ 2\leq k\leq n\}

which has size
|I|\approx\delta^{-2(n-1)}
. For some subset
J
of
I
of comparable size, let

e_{\lambda}(x)=\sum_{\alpha\in J}\prod_{k=1}^{n}\tilde{h}_{\alpha_{k}}(x_{k})

where
\tilde{h}_{\alpha_{k}}(x_{k})
are one dimensional normalized Hermite functions. By orthogonality,

(5.2)

\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\approx|J|^{\frac{1}{2}}\approx\delta^{-(n-1)}.

To determine the subset
J
such that (
5.1
) holds, we need to avoid the cancellations in the summation. We shall use the asymptotic formulas in Section 1.2. Note that for any
\alpha\in I
, we have

(5.3)

\tilde{h}_{\alpha_{k}}(x_{k})\approx\delta^{\frac{1}{2}},\ \text{for}\ \ 2\leq k\leq n,

whenever
|x_{k}|\ll\delta
, since

|s^{-}(x_{k})|=\int_{0}^{|x_{k}|}\sqrt{|t^{2}-(2\alpha_{k}+1)|}dt\ll 1.

Moreover, let
u=u(\alpha_{1})=\sqrt{2\alpha_{1}+1}
and

s_{u}^{-}(x_{1})=\int_{0}^{x_{1}}\sqrt{|t^{2}-u^{2}|}dt.

We have for each
u
, the value of
s_{u}^{-}(x_{1})
varies in an interval of size
\approx 1
as
x_{1}
varies in an interval of size
\approx\lambda 2^{-j}\delta^{2}
, since
\delta\gg\lambda^{-1}2^{j}
. So
|\cos(s_{u}^{-}(x_{1}))|\approx 1
for most
x_{1}
.

Moreover, we have

(5.4)

|\tilde{h}_{\alpha_{1}}(x_{1})|\approx\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}2^{j/2},

since

\lambda^{2}-x_{1}^{2}\approx\lambda^{2}2^{-2j},\ \ \lambda^{2}-u^{2}\approx\delta^{-2}\ll\lambda^{2}2^{-2j}.

It remains to insure that the functions
\tilde{h}_{\alpha_{1}}(x_{1})
have the same sign for all
\alpha\in J
.
Since

\frac{d}{du}\frac{d}{dx_{1}}s_{u}^{-}(x_{1})=\frac{u}{\sqrt{|x_{1}^{2}-u^{2}|}}\approx 2^{j},

we may integrate this with respect to
x_{1}
(from
x_{1}^{*}
to
x_{1}
) and then with respect to
u
(from
u_{2}
to
u_{1}
):

(5.5)

s_{u_{1}}^{-}(x_{1})-s_{u_{2}}^{-}(x_{1})=s_{u_{1}}(x_{1}^{*})-s_{u_{2}}(x_{1}^{*})+O(2^{j}(u_{1}-u_{2})(x_{1}-x_{1}^{*})).

The remainder term is
\ll 1
since
|x_{1}-x_{1}^{*}|\ll\lambda 2^{-j}\delta^{2}
and
|u_{1}-u_{2}|\lesssim\lambda^{-1}\delta^{2}
.

Fix a large constant
M\gg 1
. For
k=1,...,M
, let

J_{k}=\{\alpha\in I:s_{u}^{-}(x_{1}^{*})\ \text{mod}\ 2\pi\in[\tfrac{k-1}{M}2\pi,\tfrac{k}{M}2\pi]\}.

Since
\cup_{k=1}^{M}J_{k}=I
, by the pigeonhole principle there is some
J_{k_{0}}
with

|J_{k_{0}}|\geq\frac{1}{M}|I|.

So
|J_{k_{0}}|\approx|I|\approx\delta^{-2(n-1)}
.
Then we have for any two indices in
J_{k_{0}}
, the corresponding two phases are close modulo
2\pi
by (
5.5
), namely

\sup_{x_{1}:|x_{1}-x_{1}^{*}|\ll\lambda 2^{-j}\delta^{2}}|s_{u_{1}}^{-}(x_{1})-s_{u_{2}}^{-}(x_{1})|\ll 1\mod 2\pi.

Together with (
5.2
), (
5.3
) and (
5.4
), this implies (
5.1
).

The examples constructed above can be viewed as intermediate cases between the two kinds of extreme examples in Koch-Tataru
[
45
]
, which correspond to
\delta\approx\lambda^{-1}2^{j}
(point concentration) and
\delta\approx 2^{-j/2}
(tube concentration) respectively. Furthermore, the construction of eigenfunctions in
D^{bd}
is essentially the same as the construction above with
2^{j}=\lambda^{\frac{2}{3}}
.

5.2.
Proof of the sharpness

Fix
\nu\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\mu=\max\{\lambda^{-\frac{4}{3}},1-\lambda^{-1}|\nu|\}\approx 2^{-2j}
and
r>0
. Let
B(\nu,r)
be the ball
\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x-\nu|<r\}
.

Case 1:
When
r\lesssim\lambda^{-1}\mu^{-\frac{1}{2}}
, we choose
\delta=\lambda^{-1}\mu^{-\frac{1}{2}}
and then
B(\nu,r)
is essentially contained in the tube
\mathcal{T}_{j,\delta}
. So we get

\displaystyle\frac{\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{p}(B(\nu,r))}}{\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}

\displaystyle\gtrsim\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}2^{j/2}\delta^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}\cdot r^{\frac{n}{p}}

\displaystyle\approx(\lambda\mu^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\frac{n-2}{2}}r^{\frac{n}{p}},

which saturates (
1.19
).

Case 2:
When
\lambda^{-1}\mu^{-\frac{1}{2}}\ll r\ll\lambda\mu
, we may choose
\delta=(\lambda\mu^{\frac{1}{2}}/r)^{-\frac{1}{2}}
. So we have
\delta\ll r=\lambda\mu^{\frac{1}{2}}\delta^{2}
and then
|B(\nu,r)\cap\mathcal{T}_{j,\delta}|\approx r\delta^{n-1}
. Thus,

\displaystyle\frac{\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{p}(B(\nu,r))}}{\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}

\displaystyle\gtrsim\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}2^{j/2}\delta^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}\cdot|B(\nu,r)\cap\mathcal{T}_{j,\delta}|^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\approx\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mu^{-\frac{1}{4}}r^{\frac{1}{p}}\delta^{(n-1)(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})}

\displaystyle=(\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}r^{\frac{1}{2}}\mu^{-\frac{1}{4}})^{\frac{n+1}{p}-\frac{n-1}{2}}(\lambda\mu^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}}.

Moreover, we can choose
\delta=\lambda^{-1}\mu^{-\frac{1}{2}}
, and then the tube
\mathcal{T}_{j,\delta}
is essentially a ball covered by
B(\nu,r)
. Since
|\mathcal{T}_{j,\delta}|\approx\delta^{n}
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{p}(B(\nu,r))}}{\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}

\displaystyle\gtrsim\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}2^{j/2}\delta^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}\cdot|\mathcal{T}_{j,\delta}|^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\approx(\lambda\mu^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\frac{n-2}{2}-\frac{n}{p}}.

These saturate (
1.19
).

Case 3:
When
\lambda\mu\lesssim r\leq\lambda
, we fix some integer
k
with
\lambda\tilde{\mu}\lesssim\lambda 2^{-2k}\lesssim r
. The ball
B(\nu,r)
may intersect the annuli
D_{k}^{int}
for these
k
. If we choose
\delta=2^{-k/2}
, then the tube
\mathcal{T}_{k,\delta}
is essentially contained in
B(\nu,r)
and
|\mathcal{T}_{k,\delta}|\approx\lambda 2^{-k}\delta^{n+1}
. So we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{p}(B(\nu,r))}}{\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}

\displaystyle\gtrsim\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}2^{k/2}\delta^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}\cdot|\mathcal{T}_{k,\delta}|^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\approx\lambda^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}}2^{k(\frac{n+1}{4}-\frac{n+3}{2p})}.

Moreover, we may choose
\delta=\lambda^{-1}2^{k}
. Then the tube
\mathcal{T}_{k,\delta}
is essentially a ball covered by
B(\nu,r)
. Since
|\mathcal{T}_{j,\delta}|\approx\delta^{n}
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{p}(B(\nu,r))}}{\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}

\displaystyle\gtrsim\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}2^{k/2}\delta^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}\cdot|\mathcal{T}_{k,\delta}|^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\approx(\lambda 2^{-k})^{\frac{n-2}{2}-\frac{n}{p}}.

These saturate (
1.19
) since
\tilde{\mu}^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim 2^{-k}\lesssim(r/\lambda)^{\frac{1}{2}}
. We complete the proof of the sharpness.

(ii)

Kakeya-Nikodym type estimates.
Let
B
be any fixed compact set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
\nu\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For real numbers
r_{1}\geq r_{2}\geq...\geq r_{n}>0
, let
\textbf{r}=diag(r_{1},...,r_{n})
. Let

B(\nu,\textbf{r})=\{\nu+\textbf{r}x:x\in B\}.

The question is to establish sharp local
L^{p}\ (p\geq 2)
estimates over
B(\nu,\textbf{r})
for the Hermite eigenfunctions. The model case
r_{1}=...=r_{n}=r
has been handled in this paper. In the case
r_{1}\gg r_{2}=...=r_{n}
, the set
B(\nu,\textbf{r})
is roughly a tube, and the local

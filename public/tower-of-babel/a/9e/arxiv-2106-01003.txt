Abstract
. The Cucker-Smale(CS) model is a velocity alignment model, and this model also has been generalized on general manifolds. We modify the CS model on manifolds to get rid of a-priori condition on particles’ positions and conditions on communication functions. Since the shortest geodesic is used to define an interaction between two particles, if there exist two or more than two shortest geodesics, then the system is not well-defined. In this paper, instead of using the shortest geodesic to define an interaction between two particles, we use all geodesics to define an interaction. From this assumption, we can relax the a-priori condition and conditions on communication functions. We also explain the relationship between the suggested model and previous models. Finally, we provide some emergent behaviors on some specific manifolds(e.g. flat torus, flat Möbius strip, and flat Klein bottle). From these results, we can discuss the effect of the topology of the domain.

(x_{i}(0),v_{i}(0))=(x_{i}^{0},v_{i}^{0})\in TM\quad\forall i\in\{1,2,\cdots,N\},\end{cases}

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}x_{i}=v_{i},\vskip 5.69046pt\\
\displaystyle\frac{D}{dt}v_{i}=\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{x_{k}}^{x_{i}}}\varphi(|\gamma|)(P_{ik}^{\gamma}v_{k}-v_{i}),\quad t>0,\\

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\tilde{x}_{i}=\tilde{v}_{i},\vskip 5.69046pt\\
\displaystyle\frac{D}{dt}\tilde{v}_{i}=\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sum_{\tilde{y}_{k}\in p^{-1}(p(\tilde{x}_{k}))}\varphi(\mathrm{dist}(\tilde{x}_{i},\tilde{y}_{k}))(P_{\tilde{x}_{i}\tilde{y}_{k}}\tilde{u}^{\tilde{y}_{k}}_{k}-\tilde{v}_{i}),\quad t>0,\\
\tilde{x}_{i}(0)=\tilde{x}_{i}^{0}\in\tilde{M}^{d},\quad\tilde{v}_{i}(0)=\tilde{v}_{i}^{0}\in T_{\tilde{x}_{i}^{0}}\tilde{M},\quad\forall~i\in\mathcal{N},\end{cases}

(

Now, we define the following energy functional

Preliminaries

In this section, we provide some preparatory concepts. Since the goal of this paper is to construct a velocity alignment model using universal covering spaces, we provide a simple review of universal covering spaces and prior studies on velocity alignment models.

2.1.
Universal covering space of spaces of constant curvature

In this subsection, we provide a review on universal covering spaces of constant curvature space, since we will consider quotient spaces of the Euclidean space.

Theorem 2.1

(Theorem 4.1 of
[

]
)
.

Let

\tilde{M}

Here, covering metric means that the covering map

Corollary 2.1

From Theorem

(1) The universal covering space of the flat torus

(2) The universal covering spaces of the flat Möbius strip

2.2.
The Cucker-Smale model on manifolds

In this subsection, we introduce the Cucker-Smale(CS) model on manifolds and its emergent behaviors. System (
1.1
) is a velocity alignment model on the Euclidean space introduced in
[
9
]
. It is natural that generalization of this system onto general manifolds. The authors of
[
15
]
suggested the CS model on general manifolds as system (
1.2
). Now, we present the previous results of system (
1.2
) on various spaces.

2.2.1.
On the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{d}

The CS model (
1.2
) on sphere was first studied in
[
15
]
. In this paper, the authors assumed the following a-priori:

(2.5)

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t<\infty}\max_{i,j\in\mathcal{N}}\mathrm{dist}(x_{i}(t),x_{k}(t))<\pi.

This a priori assumption allows the well-posedness of the system on the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{d}
. The authors of
[
2
]
improved these result. They assumed the following condition on the communication function
\psi
:

(2.6)

\displaystyle\mathrm{dist}(x,y)=\pi\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\psi(x,y)=0.

The well-posedness of system (
1.2
) on the sphere can be broken when the parallel transport
P_{ik}
is not well defined, and this situation occurs when
x_{i}
and
x_{k}
are the antipodal points of each others for some
i,k\in\mathcal{N}
. However, if we impose condition (
2.6
), we do not have to calculate
P_{ik}v_{k}-v_{i}
since
\psi(x_{i},x_{k})
is zero. One of the useful lemma to show a velocity alignment is Barbalat’s lemma. We provide the lemma without the proof.

Lemma 2.1

(Barbalat’s lemma
[
6
]
)
.

Suppose that a real-valued function
f:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is uniformly continuous and it satisfies

\lim_{t\to\infty}\int_{0}^{t}f(s)ds\quad\text{exists}.

Then,
f
tends to zero as
t\to\infty
. i.e.
\lim_{t\to\infty}f(t)=0
.

Theorem 2.2

(Emergent behavior on the sphere
[
2
]
)
.

Let
\{(x_{i},v_{i})\}_{i=1}^{N}
be a global smooth solution to (
1.2
) on the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{d}
and assume that
\psi:\mathbb{S}^{d}\times\mathbb{S}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is a positive smooth function satisfying (
2.6
). Then, we have the following dichotomy for the asymptotic dynamics of
\{(x_{i},v_{i})\}_{i=1}^{N}
:

(1) either the energy converges to zero:

\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathcal{E}(t)=0,

where
\mathcal{E}(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|v_{i}\|^{2}
.

(2) or the energy converges to a nonzero positive value and all positions approach to a common great circle asymptotically: for every
i,j,k\in\mathcal{N}
and
a,b,c\in\{1,\cdots,d+1\}
we have

\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathcal{E}(t)>0\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathrm{det}\begin{pmatrix}x_{i}^{a}(t)&x_{j}^{a}(t)&x_{k}^{a}(t)\\
x_{i}^{b}(t)&x_{j}^{b}(t)&x_{k}^{b}(t)\\
x_{i}^{c}(t)&x_{j}^{c}(t)&x_{k}^{c}(t)\end{pmatrix}=0.

2.2.2.
On the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{2}

The CS model on the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{2}
was studied in
[
1
]
. For any
x,y\in\mathbb{H}^{2}
, there exists the unique geodesic
\gamma
which connects
x
and
y
. This means, the parallel transport
P_{ik}
between
x_{i}
and
x_{k}
is always well-defined. So, we do not need a condition which is similar to (
2.6
) in this case.

Theorem 2.3

(Emergent behavior on the hyperbolic space
[
1
]
)
.

Let
\{(x_{i},v_{i})\}_{i=1}^{N}
be a global smooth solution to (
1.2
) on the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{2}
and assume that
\psi:\mathbb{H}^{2}\times\mathbb{H}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
is a strictly positive smooth function. Then, we have the following dichotomy for the asymptotic dynamics of
\{(x_{i},v_{i})\}_{i=1}^{N}
:

(1) either the energy tends to zero:

\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathcal{E}(t)=0.

(2) or the energy converges to a positive value, and position configuration becomes coplanar asymptotically:

\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathcal{E}(t)=\mathcal{E}^{\infty}>0\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathrm{det}\Big(x_{i}(t)~\Big|~x_{j}(t)~\Big|x_{k}(t)\Big)=0\quad\forall~i,j,k\in\mathcal{N}

where
x_{i}(t)\in\mathbb{H}^{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
is considered as a three-dimensional vector for all
i\in\mathcal{N}
.

2.2.3.
On the special orthogonal group
SO(3)

The CS model on
SO(3)
was studied in
[
12
]
. Instead of using
\{(x_{i},v_{i})\}_{i=1}^{N}
, we use the pair
\{(R_{i},A_{i})\}_{i=1}^{N}
which satisfies

x_{i}=R_{i},\quad v_{i}=R_{i}A_{i}\quad~\forall~i\in\mathcal{N}.

Here,
R_{i}
is an element of
SO(3)
and
A_{i}
is a skew symmetric matrix of size
3\times 3
. Then, we can express system (
1.2
) on
SO(3)
as follows:

(2.7)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}R_{i}=R_{i}A_{i},\\
\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathbf{a}_{i}=\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\varphi(R_{i},R_{k})\left[\left(1-\cos\frac{\theta_{ki}}{2}\right)(\mathbf{n}_{ki}\cdot\mathbf{a}_{k})\mathbf{n}_{ki}+\sin\frac{\theta_{ki}}{2}\mathbf{a}_{k}\times\mathbf{n}_{ki}+\cos\frac{\theta_{ki}}{2}\mathbf{a}_{k}-\mathbf{a}_{i},\right]\end{cases}

where

\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{ki}=\frac{\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{ki}}{\theta_{ki}},\quad\mathbf{a}_{i}=\check{A}_{i}

and

\theta_{ki}=\arccos\left(\frac{\mathrm{tr}(R_{k}^{\top}R_{i})-1}{2}\right),\quad\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{ki}=\frac{\theta_{ki}}{2\sin\theta_{ki}}(R_{k}^{\top}R_{i}-R_{i}^{\top}R_{k}).

Here,
\hat{\cdot}
and
\check{\cdot}
operators are defined as follows:

\hat{x}:=A,\quad\check{A}:=x,

where

x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\quad A=\begin{pmatrix}0&-x_{3}&x_{2}\\
x_{3}&0&-x_{1}\\
-x_{2}&x_{1}&0\end{pmatrix}\in\mathfrak{so}(3).

Theorem 2.4

(Emergent behavior on the special orthogonal group
[
12
]
)
.

Let
\{(R_{i},A_{i})\}_{i=1}^{N}
be a solution to system (
2.7
). Then, we have the following dichotomy for the asymptotic dynamics of
\{(R_{i},A_{i})\}_{i=1}^{N}
:

(1) either the kinetic energy tends to zero:

\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathcal{E}(t)=0.

(2) or the energy converges to positive value
\mathcal{E}^{\infty}
, and

\lim_{t\to\infty}(A_{i}(t)-A_{k}(t))=0,\quad\forall~i,k\in\mathcal{N}.

Also, particles approach and rotate with constant speed along a common geodesic.

Theorems
2.2
,
2.3
, and
2.4
are results on the different manifolds, however, the common result is that the particles are aligned on to a common geodesic.

3.
Velocity alignment models on the flat torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}

A velocity alignment model on general manifolds was suggested in
[
15
]
and given as (
1.2
). If the following a-priori condition is guaranteed in system (
1.2
):

“A shortest geodesic between two points
x_{i}(t)
and
x_{j}(t)
is unique for all
i\neq j
,
t\geq 0
,”

then the solution of the system is well-defined and unique. Since the system contains the parallel transport on the manifold, the a-priori condition should be assumed for the well-posedness. However, if the domain
M
has a cut locus(i.e. there exist at least two shortest geodesic between two points
x,y\in M
), then the system defined on
M
is not well-defined in general. Since we choose the shortest geodesic, we guess that singularities come from the
speciality of the shortest geodesic
. Regarding this reason, in this paper, we suggest a modified system which uses all geodesics to define the interactions. In this section, we suggest a modified velocity alignment model on the flat torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}
.

3.1.
Geodesics on the flat torus

We are planning to consider all geodesics between
x_{i},x_{j}\in M
to define the interaction between
i^{th}
and
j^{th}
particles. So, in this subsection, we study the geodesics on the flat torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}
. We consider the flat torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}
as a quotient space of the Euclidean space

\mathbb{T}^{d}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{d}/_{\sim},

where the equivalence relation
\sim
given as

[x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{d}]\sim[x_{1}+n_{1},x_{2}+n_{2},\cdots,x_{d}+n_{d}]\quad\forall~(n_{1},\cdots,n_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}.

Note that the metric of the torus was induced from
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

3.1.1.
The case when
d=1

Before we study general dimension, we consider the simplest case. Since the universal covering space of
\mathbb{T}^{1}
is
\mathbb{R}
, we can define a covering map
p
as follows:

p:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{T}^{1},\quad x\mapsto x+\mathbb{Z}.

Let
x,y\in\mathbb{T}^{1}
be two points on the one dimensional flat torus and
\gamma
be a one of geodesic which connects two points
x
and
y
. Then there exists two points
\tilde{x},\tilde{y}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\gamma=p([\tilde{x},\tilde{y}])
. Now we will express the following set:

\Gamma_{x}^{y}:=\{\text{geodesics connecting $x$ and $y$}\}.

From the previous argument, we can express the set of geodesics which connects
x
and
y
as follows:

\tilde{\gamma}:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{1},\quad\tilde{\gamma}(t)=\tilde{x}+t(\tilde{y}-\tilde{x}),\quad\gamma=p\circ\tilde{\gamma}.

Since
\tilde{x}
and
\tilde{y}
are not unique, we can express all geodesics
\gamma_{n}
with
n\in\mathbb{Z}
as follows:

\displaystyle\tilde{\gamma}_{n}:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{1},\quad\tilde{\gamma}_{n}(t)=\tilde{x}+t(\tilde{y}-\tilde{x}+n),

\displaystyle\gamma_{n}:[0,1]\to\mathbb{T}^{1},\quad\gamma_{n}=p\circ\tilde{\gamma}_{n}.

Then
\Gamma_{x}^{y}=\{\gamma_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is the set of geodesic from
x
to
y
.

Remark 3.1
.

From the above definition, we have the following properties.

(1)

The length of
\gamma_{n}
is given as

|\gamma_{n}|=|\tilde{y}-\tilde{x}+n|.

(2)

Since
\mathbb{R}^{1}/\mathbb{T}^{1}\simeq\mathbb{Z}
, we can find a correspondence between
\Gamma_{x}^{y}
and
\mathbb{Z}
.

. Now, we are ready to construct a modified model on the flat torus.

3.2.
Construction of the modified model on the flat torus

Before we define a system on the flat torus, we define a system on its covering space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Since we expressed the set of geodesics between two points
x,y
as (
3.9
), we can construct a system which considers all geodesics between two particles as follows:

(3.10)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\tilde{x}_{i}=\tilde{v}_{i},\vskip 5.69046pt\\
\displaystyle\frac{D}{dt}\tilde{v}_{i}=\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sum_{\tilde{y}_{k}\in p^{-1}(p(\tilde{x}_{k}))}\psi(\tilde{x}_{i},\tilde{y}_{k})(P_{\tilde{x}_{i}\tilde{y}_{k}}\tilde{v}_{k}-\tilde{v}_{i}),\quad t>0,\\
\tilde{x}_{i}(0)=\tilde{x}_{i}^{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\quad\tilde{v}_{i}(0)=\tilde{v}_{i}^{0}\in T_{\tilde{x}_{i}^{0}}\mathbb{R}^{d},\quad\forall~i\in\mathcal{N},\end{cases}

where
P_{\tilde{x}_{i}\tilde{y}_{k}}
is a parallel transport from a tangent vector at
\tilde{y}_{k}
to a tangent vector at
\tilde{x}_{k}
. Here, we assume that the sum
\sum_{\tilde{y}_{k}\in p^{-1}(p(\tilde{x}_{k}))}
is well-defined. We will discuss conditions for
\psi
in the last of this subsection. Since the flat torus has zero curvatures, the parallel transport
P_{\tilde{x}_{i}\tilde{y}_{k}}
is not necessary in this case. However, for a future generalization on general manifolds, we leave
P_{\tilde{x}_{i}\tilde{y}_{k}}
. Now, we assume that
\psi(x,y)
is a function of
\mathrm{dist}_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(x,y)
and define a function
\varphi:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
as follows:

\varphi(\mathrm{dist}_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(x,y))=\psi(x,y)\quad\forall~x,y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}.

If we put
x_{i}=p(\tilde{x}_{i})
and
v_{i}=Dp(\tilde{v}_{i})
for all
i\in\mathcal{N}
, we can reduce system (
3.10
) to
M
as follows:

(3.11)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}x_{i}=v_{i},\vskip 5.69046pt\\
\displaystyle\frac{D}{dt}v_{i}=\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{x_{k}}^{x_{i}}}\varphi(|\gamma|)(P^{\gamma}_{ik}v_{k}-v_{i}),\quad t>0,\\
x_{i}(0)=x_{i}^{0}\in\mathbb{T}^{d},\quad v_{i}(0)=v_{i}^{0}\in T_{x_{i}^{0}}\mathbb{T}^{d},\quad\forall~i\in\mathcal{N},\end{cases}

where
P^{\gamma}_{ik}
is a parallel transport from a tangent vector at
x_{k}
to a tangent vector at
x_{i}
along a curve
\gamma
and
|\gamma|
is the length of
\gamma
. Recall that
P_{ik}^{\gamma_{1}}=P_{ik}^{\gamma_{2}}
for any
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\Gamma_{x_{k}}^{x_{i}}
, since the domain of this system is the flat torus. So, we denote that
P_{ik}:=P_{ik}^{\gamma}
for all
\gamma\in\Gamma_{x_{k}}^{x_{i}}
. If we define

(3.12)

\displaystyle\Phi(x_{i},x_{k}):=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{x_{k}}^{x_{i}}}\varphi(|\gamma|)

for all
i,k\in\mathcal{N}
, then system (
3.11
) can be expressed as follows:

(3.13)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}x_{i}=v_{i},\vskip 5.69046pt\\
\displaystyle\frac{D}{dt}v_{i}=\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\Phi(x_{i},x_{k})(P_{ik}v_{k}-v_{i}),\quad t>0,\\
x_{i}(0)=x_{i}^{0}\in\mathbb{T}^{d},\quad v_{i}(0)=v_{i}^{0}\in T_{x_{i}^{0}}\mathbb{T}^{d},\quad\forall~i\in\mathcal{N}.\end{cases}

Now, we discuss conditions for the communication function
\psi
to guarantee the well-posedness of system (
3.10
). Actually, it is equivalent to find a condition for the convergence of
\Phi
in (
3.12
). From the definition of
\Phi
and Remark
3.2
(1), we have the following calculation:

\displaystyle\Phi(x,y)=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{y}^{x}}\varphi(|\gamma|)=\sum_{\vec{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\varphi(\|\tilde{y}-\tilde{x}+\vec{n}\|).

Now we assume the following two conditions for
\varphi
:

(
\mathcal{A}1
) A function
\varphi:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is a continuous decreasing function.

(
\mathcal{A}2
) A sum
\displaystyle\sum_{\vec{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\varphi(\|\tilde{y}-\tilde{x}+\vec{n}\|)
converges.

Here, we assumed
(\mathcal{A}1)
, since
\psi
is a communication function.
(\mathcal{A}2)
is assumed for the well-definedness of system (
3.10
) as we mentioned before. For a further argument, we provide the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\tilde{x},\tilde{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, then we have the following inequality:

\min_{\vec{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\|\tilde{y}-\tilde{x}+\vec{n}\|\leq\frac{\sqrt{d}}{2}.

Proof.

From a simple calculation, we get

\min_{\vec{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\|\tilde{y}-\tilde{x}+\vec{n}\|^{2}=((\tilde{y})_{1}-(\tilde{x})_{1}+(\vec{n})_{1})^{2}+\cdots+((\tilde{y})_{d}-(\tilde{x})_{d}+(\vec{n})_{d})^{2}.

Now, we pick
\vec{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
as follows:

(3.14)

\displaystyle(\vec{m})_{i}=\begin{cases}[(\tilde{x})_{i}-(\tilde{y})_{i}]\qquad&\text{if }\quad 0\leq((\tilde{x})_{i}-(\tilde{y})_{i})-[(\tilde{x})_{i}-(\tilde{y})_{i}]<\frac{1}{2},\\
[(\tilde{x})_{i}-(\tilde{y})_{i}]+1\qquad&\text{if }\quad\frac{1}{2}\leq((\tilde{x})_{i}-(\tilde{y})_{i})-[(\tilde{x})_{i}-(\tilde{y})_{i}]<1\end{cases}

where
[x]
is the greatest integer which is not greater than
x
. Then we know that
-\frac{1}{2}\leq(\tilde{y})_{i}-(\tilde{x})_{i}+(\vec{m})_{i}\leq\frac{1}{2}
for all
1\leq i\leq N
and this yields

\min_{\vec{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\|\tilde{y}-\tilde{x}+\vec{n}\|^{2}\leq\|\tilde{y}-\tilde{x}+\vec{m}\|^{2}\leq d\times\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}.

Finally we can conclude that

\min_{\vec{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\|\tilde{y}-\tilde{x}+\vec{n}\|\leq\frac{\sqrt{d}}{2}.

This is the desired result.
∎

3.3.
Condition of the communication functions
\varphi

In this subsection, we study the condition for
\varphi
which satisfies

\Phi(x,y)=\sum_{\vec{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\varphi(\|\tilde{y}-\tilde{x}+\vec{n}\|)<\infty\quad\forall~x,y\in M.

Since
\varphi
is continuous, non-negative and decreasing, we can apply the integral test on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
to get

\sum_{\vec{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\varphi(\|\tilde{y}-\tilde{x}+\vec{n}\|)\quad\text{converges}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(|x|)dx<\infty.

From the radial symmetry, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(|x|)dx=|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{d-1}\varphi(r)dr,

where
|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|
is a
(d-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure of the unit
(d-1)
-dimensional sphere. Actually,
|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|
can be expressed as

(3.15)

\displaystyle|\mathbb{S}^{d-1}|=\frac{d\pi^{d/2}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{2}+1\right)},

where
\Gamma
is the gamma function. Since (
3.15
) only depends on
d
, we have

\sum_{\vec{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\varphi(\|\tilde{y}-\tilde{x}+\vec{n}\|)\quad\text{converges}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{d-1}\varphi(r)dr<\infty.

So we can conclude that the condition (
\mathcal{A}1
) and (
\mathcal{A}2
) is equivalent to the following condition.

(
\mathcal{A}
): A function
\varphi:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is a continuous decreasing function and satisfies
\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{d-1}\varphi(r)dr
exists.

Now, we study some examples of communication functions
\varphi
which satisfy (
\mathcal{A}
).

Example 3.1
.

(1) When
\varphi
has a compact support:
Let
\mathrm{supp}\varphi\subset[0,A]
. Then of course,
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}r^{d-1}\varphi(r)dr<\infty
.

(2) When
\varphi(r)=e^{-r}
:
From a simple calculation, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{d-1}e^{-r}dr

\displaystyle=[-r^{d-1}e^{-r}]_{0}^{\infty}+(d-1)\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{d-2}e^{-r}dr

\displaystyle=\cdots

\displaystyle=(d-1)!\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-r}dr=(d-1)!<\infty.

If
d=1
, we can calculate the explicit form of
\Phi(x,y)
. From a similar argument that we used in (
3.14
), we can choose
\tilde{x}\in p^{-1}(x)
and
\tilde{y}\in p^{-1}(y)
which satisfy
0\leq\delta:=\tilde{x}-\tilde{y}<1
. Then we have

\displaystyle\Psi(x,y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{-|\tilde{x}-\tilde{y}+n|}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{-|\delta+n|}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-(\delta+n)}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{(\delta-n)}

\displaystyle=e^{-\delta}\frac{1}{1-e^{-1}}+e^{\delta}\frac{e^{-1}}{1-e^{-1}}=\frac{e^{1-\delta}+e^{\delta}}{e-1}=\frac{\sinh(\delta-1/2)}{\sinh(1/2)}.

(3) When
\varphi(r)=\frac{1}{(1+r^{2})^{\alpha}}
with
\alpha>\frac{d}{2}
:
From a simple calculation, we have

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{r^{d-1}}{(1+r^{2})^{\alpha}}dr<\int_{1}^{\infty}r^{d-1-2\alpha}dr<\infty.

In this section, we generalize the result of Section

4.1.
Construction of the modified model on general manifold

We can simply generalize system (
3.11

(4.16)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}x_{i}=v_{i},\vskip 5.69046pt\\
\displaystyle\frac{D}{dt}v_{i}=\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{x_{k}}^{x_{i}}}\varphi(|\gamma|)(P_{ik}^{\gamma}v_{k}-v_{i}),\quad t>0,\\

We only have to concern about the well-definedness and the convergence of the sum

4.16

\tilde{x}_{i}(0)=\tilde{x}_{i}^{0}\in\tilde{M}^{d},\quad\tilde{v}_{i}(0)=\tilde{v}_{i}^{0}\in T_{\tilde{x}_{i}^{0}}\tilde{M},\quad\forall~i\in\mathcal{N},\end{cases}

where
p^{-1}_{\tilde{x}}
is a inverse map of

defined on neighborhood of
\tilde{x}
and
\tilde{u}_{k}^{\tilde{y}_{k}}=Dp^{-1}_{\tilde{y}_{k}}(v_{k})=Dp^{-1}_{\tilde{y}_{k}}\circ Dp_{\tilde{x}_{k}}(\tilde{v}_{k})
and

is a parallel transport from a tangent vector at

to a tangent vector at
\tilde{x}

should be well-defined, we assume the follows:

(

is at most countable set for any

.

When

, we have already showed that there exists a one-to-one correspondence between

and
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
in Section
3.1
. So, in this case, obviously

is countable for any

. On the other hand, we know that

only depends on two points
\tilde{x}_{i}
and
\tilde{y}_{k}

(

If

4.2.
Reduction to the original CS model on manifold

In this section, we introduce the relationship between system (

4.16
) is defined on the Euclidean space(i.e.

Now, we compare two systems (

and

determine the interaction between

and

4.3.
Emergent behaviors of the modified model

In this subsection, we study the emergent behaviors of system (
4.16
). We define the following energy functional:

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{V})=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|v_{i}\|^{2},

where
\mathcal{V}=\{v_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})
be a solution of system (
4.16
). Then we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{V})=-\frac{\kappa}{2N}\sum_{i,k=1}^{N}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{x_{k}}^{x_{i}}}\psi(|\gamma|)\|P_{ik}^{\gamma}v_{k}-v_{i}\|^{2}.

i.e. the energy of the system is a non-increasing function.

Proof.

From the simple calculation, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{V})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\langle\frac{D}{dt}v_{i},v_{i}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{i,k=1}^{N}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{x_{k}}^{x_{i}}}\psi(|\gamma|)\langle P_{ik}^{\gamma}v_{k}-v_{i},v_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{\kappa}{2N}\sum_{i,k=1}^{N}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{x_{k}}^{x_{i}}}\psi(|\gamma|)\langle P_{ik}^{\gamma}v_{k}-v_{i},v_{i}\rangle+\frac{\kappa}{2N}\sum_{i,k=1}^{N}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{x_{i}}^{x_{k}}}\psi(|\gamma|)\langle P_{ki}^{\gamma}v_{i}-v_{k},v_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{\kappa}{2N}\sum_{i,k=1}^{N}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{x_{k}}^{x_{i}}}\psi(|\gamma|)\langle P_{ik}^{\gamma}v_{k}-v_{i},v_{i}\rangle+\frac{\kappa}{2N}\sum_{i,k=1}^{N}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{x_{k}}^{x_{i}}}\psi(|\gamma|)\langle P_{ki}^{-\gamma}v_{i}-v_{k},v_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{\kappa}{2N}\sum_{i,k=1}^{N}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{x_{k}}^{x_{i}}}\psi(|\gamma|)\Big(\langle P_{ik}^{\gamma}v_{k}-v_{i},v_{i}\rangle+\langle P_{ki}^{-\gamma}v_{i}-v_{k},v_{k}\rangle\Big)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\kappa}{2N}\sum_{i,k=1}^{N}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{x_{k}}^{x_{i}}}\psi(|\gamma|)\|P_{ik}^{\gamma}v_{k}-v_{i}\|^{2},

where
-\gamma
stands for the inverse path of
\gamma
. We have the desired result.
∎

Remark 4.1
.

It is easy to show that the second temporal derivative of
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{V})
is bounded.

We combine Lemma
4.1
and Barbalat’s lemma(Lemma ) to obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})
be a solution of system (
4.16
). Then we have

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{x_{k}}^{x_{i}}}\psi(|\gamma|)\|P_{ik}^{\gamma}v_{k}-v_{i}\|^{2}=0

for any
i,k\in\mathcal{N}
.

From this theorem, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 4.1
.

Let
(\tilde{\mathcal{X}},\tilde{\mathcal{V}})
be a solution of system (
4.17
). Then we have

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\sum_{\tilde{y}_{k}\in p^{-1}(p(\tilde{x}_{k}))}\varphi(\mathrm{dist}(\tilde{x}_{i},\tilde{y}_{k}))\|P_{\tilde{x}_{i}\tilde{y}_{k}}\tilde{u}_{k}^{\tilde{y}_{k}}-\tilde{v}_{i}\|^{2}=0,

where
P_{\tilde{x}_{i}\tilde{y}_{k}}\tilde{u}_{k}^{\tilde{y}_{k}}
is defined in (
4.17
).

Since given models (
4.16
) and (
4.17
) are defined on too general manifold
M
, we can not provide more specific emergent behaviors. In Section
5
, we provide more specific emergent behaviors of system (
4.16
) and (
4.17
) on the specific manifold
M
.

4.4.
Self-interaction effect

In a previous velocity alignment model on manifold (
1.2
),
i^{th}
particle does not interact with itself, since
P_{ii}
is the identity map and this yields
P_{ii}v_{i}-v_{i}=0
. However, system (
4.16
) is different. Since we considered all geodesics which is connecting
x_{i}
and
x_{j}
to define the interaction between
i^{th}
and
j^{th}
particles, if there exists non-trivial geodesic starts from
x_{i}
and finish at
x_{i}
then
i^{th}
particle interacts with itself. In this subsection, we focus on this effect. We put
N=1
and
(x,v):=(x_{1},v_{1})
to system (
4.16
). Then we get

(4.18)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}x=v,\vskip 5.69046pt\\
\displaystyle\frac{D}{dt}v=\kappa\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{x}^{x}}\varphi(|\gamma|)(P^{\gamma}v-v),\quad t>0,\\
x(0)=x^{0}\in M,\quad v(0)=v^{0}\in T_{x^{0}}M,\end{cases}

where
P^{\gamma}
is a parallel transport along
\gamma
. From Lemma
4.1
, we know that

(4.19)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{1}{2}\|v\|^{2}\right)=-\frac{\kappa}{2}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{x}^{x}}\varphi(|\gamma|)\|P^{\gamma}v-v\|^{2}.

This yields

\frac{d}{dt}\|v\|^{2}=-\kappa\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{x}^{x}}\varphi(|\gamma|)\|P^{\gamma}v-v\|^{2}\geq-4\kappa\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{x}^{x}}\varphi(|\gamma|)\|v\|^{2},

and from a simple fact of ODE, we know that if
v^{0}\neq 0
, then
v(t)\neq 0
for all
t\geq 0
. So we can define
u(t)
for all
t\geq 0
as follows:

(4.20)

\displaystyle u(t):=\frac{v(t)}{\|v(t)\|}.

Now we substitute (
4.20
) into (
4.19
) to get

\frac{d}{dt}\|v\|^{2}=-\kappa\|v\|^{2}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{x}^{x}}\varphi(|\gamma|)\|P^{\gamma}u-u\|^{2}

or equivalently,

\frac{d}{dt}\ln\|v\|=-\frac{\kappa}{2}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{x}^{x}}\varphi(|\gamma|)\|P^{\gamma}u-u\|^{2}.

From this, we can conclude that the self-interaction effect reduces the speed of particle, and the ratio is at most exponential. If we apply Theorem
4.1
to system (
4.18
), then we get

(4.21)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\|v\|^{2}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{x}^{x}}\varphi(|\gamma|)\|P^{\gamma}u-u\|^{2}=0.

Finally, we can obtain a dichotomy for the long-time behaviors of system (
4.18
):

(1) The speed converges to zero. i.e.

\lim_{t\to\infty}\|v\|=0.

(2) The direction of the velocity
u
satisfies

\lim_{t\to\infty}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{x}^{x}}\varphi(|\gamma|)\|P^{\gamma}u-u\|^{2}=0.

This effect comes from the topology of the domain. This argument has not been argued before.

for
\tilde{x},\tilde{y}\in p^{-1}(x)
. In this section, we provide some specific spaces which are quotient spaces of the Euclidean space(e.g. The flat torus, Möbius strip, and the flat Klein bottle), and calculate the explicit form of
Dp_{\tilde{y}}^{-1}\circ Dp_{\tilde{x}}
to obtain more specific emergent behaviors of the modified velocity alignment system.

5.1.
Example 1: The flat torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}

We consider the flat torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}
in this subsection. We have already studied the covering map
p
between
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\mathbb{T}^{d}
in Section
3.1
as follows:

p(\tilde{x})=((\tilde{x})_{1}-[(\tilde{x})_{1}],(\tilde{x})_{2}-[(\tilde{x})_{2}],\cdots,(\tilde{x})_{d}-[(\tilde{x})_{d}])\quad\forall~\tilde{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

This yields that
Dp^{-1}_{\tilde{y}}\circ Dp_{\tilde{x}}
is the identity map. Also, we have already express the set of geodesics between
x,y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
as (
3.8
). From the above results, we can reduce system (
5.22
) as follows:

(5.23)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\tilde{x}_{i}=\tilde{v}_{i},\vskip 5.69046pt\\
\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\tilde{v}_{i}=\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\sum_{\vec{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\varphi(\mathrm{dist}(\tilde{x}_{i},\tilde{x}_{k}+\vec{n}))\right)(\tilde{v}_{k}-\tilde{v}_{i}),\quad t>0,\vskip 5.69046pt\\
\tilde{x}_{i}(0)=\tilde{x}_{i}^{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\quad\tilde{v}_{i}(0)=\tilde{v}_{i}^{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\quad\forall~i\in\mathcal{N}.\end{cases}

Now, we apply Corollary
4.1
, we can obtain the following theorem.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})
be a solution of system (
4.16
) defined on the flat torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}
. Let
\varphi
satisfies
(\mathcal{A})
and

\varphi\left(\frac{\sqrt{d}}{2}\right)>0.

Then, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}(\tilde{v}_{k}-\tilde{v}_{i})=0\quad\forall~i,k\in\mathcal{N}.

Proof.

We apply Corollary
4.1
to system (
5.23
) to get

(5.24)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\sum_{\vec{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\varphi(\mathrm{dist}(\tilde{x}_{i},\tilde{x}_{k}+\vec{n})\|\tilde{v}_{k}-\tilde{v}_{i}\|^{2}=0.

Since
\varphi\geq 0
, we also have

\displaystyle\sum_{\vec{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\varphi(\mathrm{dist}(\tilde{x}_{i},\tilde{x}_{k}+\vec{n})\geq\inf_{\vec{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\varphi(\mathrm{dist}(\tilde{x}_{i},\tilde{x}_{k}+\vec{n})).

Now we apply Lemma
3.1
to get

\sum_{\vec{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\varphi(\mathrm{dist}(\tilde{x}_{i},\tilde{x}_{k}+\vec{n})\geq\varphi\left(\frac{\sqrt{d}}{2}\right).

Now, we substitute the above relation into (
5.24
) to get

\varphi\left(\frac{\sqrt{d}}{2}\right)\lim_{t\to\infty}\|\tilde{v}_{k}-\tilde{v}_{i}\|^{2}=0.

Since
\varphi\left(\frac{\sqrt{d}}{2}\right)>0
, we get

\lim_{t\to\infty}\|\tilde{v}_{k}-\tilde{v}_{i}\|=0.

∎

Remark 5.1

(Self-interaction effect of the flat torus)
.

Since
P^{\gamma}
is identity map for any closed curve
\gamma
, we have
P^{\gamma}v-v=0
for any tangent vector
v
. So, relation (
4.21
) implies nothing in this case. So we can conclude that there is no self-interaction effect on the flat torus.

5.2.
Example 2: The flat Möbius strip
\mathbb{M}

We consider the flat Möbius strip in this subsection and denote it as
\mathbb{M}
. We consider that
\mathbb{M}
is given on
[0,1]\times\mathbb{R}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
with the identification
(0,t)\sim(1,-t)
where
t\in\mathbb{R}
. We can express the universal covering
\tilde{\mathbb{M}}=\mathbb{R}^{2}
with the following covering map
p:\tilde{\mathbb{M}}\to[0,1)\times\mathbb{R}\simeq\mathbb{M}
:

\displaystyle p(\tilde{x})=\begin{cases}((\tilde{x})_{1}-[(\tilde{x})_{1}],(\tilde{x})_{2}),\quad&\text{if $[(\tilde{x})_{1}]$ is even},\\
((\tilde{x})_{1}-[(\tilde{x})_{1}],-(\tilde{x})_{2}),\quad&\text{if $[(\tilde{x})_{1}]$ is odd},\end{cases}\quad\forall~\tilde{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2},

where
[x]
is the largest integer not greater than
x\in\mathbb{R}
. From the above covering map
p
, we get

Dp^{-1}_{\tilde{y}}\circ Dp_{\tilde{x}}(\tilde{v})=\begin{cases}((\tilde{v})_{1},(\tilde{v})_{2}),\quad&\text{if }[(\tilde{y})_{1}]-[(\tilde{x})_{1}]\text{ is even},\\
((\tilde{v})_{1},-(\tilde{v})_{2}),\quad&\text{if }[(\tilde{y})_{1}]-[(\tilde{x})_{1}]\text{ is odd}.\end{cases}

For the notation simplicity, we define the operator
J
as follows:

J(\tilde{x})=((\tilde{x})_{1},-(\tilde{x})_{2})\quad\forall~\tilde{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

We also denote
J^{n}
be the
n
-th power of the operator
J
. Then, we can express
p^{-1}(p(\tilde{x}))
as follows:

p^{-1}(p(\tilde{x}))=\{J^{n}(\tilde{x})+(n,0):n\in\mathbb{Z}\}

and

Dp^{-1}_{\tilde{y}}\circ Dp_{\tilde{x}}(\tilde{v})=J^{n}(\tilde{v})

if
\tilde{y}=J^{n}(\tilde{x})+(n,0)
for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Using these notations, we can reduce system (
5.22
) into

(5.25)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\tilde{x}_{i}=\tilde{v}_{i},\vskip 5.69046pt\\
\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\tilde{v}_{i}=\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\varphi(\mathrm{dist}(\tilde{x}_{i},J^{n}(\tilde{x}_{k})+(n,0))(J^{n}(\tilde{v}_{k})-\tilde{v}_{i}),\quad t>0,\\
\tilde{x}_{i}(0)=\tilde{x}_{i}^{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\quad\tilde{v}_{i}(0)=\tilde{v}_{i}^{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\quad\forall~i\in\mathcal{N}.\end{cases}

From a similar argument that we made in Section
3.3
, we can prove

(5.26)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\varphi\big(\mathrm{dist}(\tilde{x},J^{n}(\tilde{y})+(n,0))\big)<\infty\quad\forall~\tilde{x},\tilde{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi(r)dr<\infty

from the integral test. If we apply Corollary
4.1
to system (
5.25
), we get

(5.27)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\varphi(\mathrm{dist}(\tilde{x}_{i},J^{n}(\tilde{x}_{k})+(n,0))\|J^{n}(\tilde{v}_{k})-\tilde{v}_{i}\|=0.

Since
J^{2}=\mathrm{Id}
, we have
J^{2m+1}=J
and
J^{2m}=\mathrm{Id}
for all integer
m
. From this fact, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\varphi(\mathrm{dist}(\tilde{x}_{i},J^{n}(\tilde{x}_{k})+(n,0))\|J^{n}(\tilde{v}_{k})-\tilde{v}_{i}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\varphi(\mathrm{dist}(\tilde{x}_{i},\tilde{x}_{k}+(2m,0))\right)\|\tilde{v}_{k}-\tilde{v}_{i}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt+\left(\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\varphi(\mathrm{dist}(\tilde{x}_{i},J(\tilde{x}_{k})+(2m+1,0))\right)\|J(\tilde{v}_{k})-\tilde{v}_{i}\|^{2}.

We combine the above result and (
5.27
) to obtain

(5.28)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\left(\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\varphi(\mathrm{dist}(\tilde{x}_{i},\tilde{x}_{k}+(2m,0))\right)\|\tilde{v}_{k}-\tilde{v}_{i}\|=0,\\
\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\left(\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\varphi(\mathrm{dist}(\tilde{x}_{i},J(\tilde{x}_{k})+(2m+1,0))\right)\|J(\tilde{v}_{k})-\tilde{v}_{i}\|=0,\end{cases}

for all
i,k\in\mathcal{N}
. Now we have the following lemma.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
p
be a covering map between two dimensional the Euclidean space and the flat Möbius strip.
Let
x,y\in[0,1]\times\mathbb{R}/_{\sim}\simeq\mathbb{M}
and
|x_{2}|,|y_{2}|<L
for some positive number
L
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\inf_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{dist}(\tilde{x},\tilde{y}+(2m,0))<\sqrt{1+4L^{2}},\quad\inf_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{dist}(\tilde{x},J(\tilde{y})+(2m+1,0))<\sqrt{1+4L^{2}},

where
\tilde{x}\in p^{-1}(x),\tilde{y}\in p^{-1}(y)
.

Proof.

First, we fix two points
x,y\in\mathbb{M}
and we choose
\tilde{x},\tilde{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that
\tilde{x}\in p^{-1}(x)
and
\tilde{y}\in p^{-1}(y)
:

\tilde{x}=(\tilde{x}_{1},\tilde{x}_{2}),\quad\tilde{y}=(\tilde{y}_{1},\tilde{y}_{2}).

Without loss of generality, we can assume that
-1\leq\tilde{x}_{1}-\tilde{y}_{1}<1
. Then we have

\mathrm{dist}(\tilde{x},\tilde{y}+(2m,0))^{2}=(\tilde{x}_{1}-\tilde{y}_{1}-2m)^{2}+(\tilde{x}_{2}-\tilde{y}_{2})^{2}\leq(\tilde{x}_{1}-\tilde{y}_{1})^{2}+(\tilde{x}_{2}-\tilde{y}_{2})^{2}.

Here, the last equality only holds for
m=0
. Since
-1\leq\tilde{x}_{1}-\tilde{y}_{1}<1
and
-2L<\tilde{x}_{2}-\tilde{y}_{2}<2L
, we have

\inf_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{dist}(\tilde{x},\tilde{y}+(2m,0))^{2}<1+4L^{2}.

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\inf_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{dist}(\tilde{x},J(\tilde{y})+(2m+1,0))^{2}

\displaystyle=\inf_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\left((\tilde{x}_{1}-\tilde{y}_{1}-2m-1)^{2}+(\tilde{x}_{2}+\tilde{y}_{2})^{2}\right)<1+4L^{2}.

∎

From this lemma and (
5.28
), we get the following proposition.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{V})
be a solution of system (
4.16
) defined on
M=\mathbb{M}
. If we assume the following a-priori assumption: “the second component of
x_{i}\in[0,1)\times\mathbb{R}
lies in
(-L,L)
for all
t\geq 0
and
i\in\mathcal{N}
.” We also assume that
\varphi
satisfies (
5.26
) and
\varphi(\sqrt{1+4L^{2}})>0
. Then, we have

\lim_{t\to\infty}(\tilde{v}_{i}-\tilde{v}_{k})=0\quad\forall~i,k\in\mathcal{N},

and the second components of all velocities converges to zero. i.e.

\lim_{t\to\infty}(\tilde{v}_{i})_{2}=0\quad\forall i\in\mathcal{N}.

Proof.

From Lemma
5.1
, we have

(5.29)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\varphi(\mathrm{dist}(\tilde{x}_{i},\tilde{x}_{k}+(2m,0))&\geq\inf_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\varphi(\mathrm{dist}(\tilde{x}_{i},\tilde{x}_{k}+(2m,0))\geq\varphi(\sqrt{1+4L^{2}}),\\
\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\varphi(\mathrm{dist}(\tilde{x}_{i},J(\tilde{x}_{k})+(2m+1,0))&\geq\inf_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\varphi(\mathrm{dist}(\tilde{x}_{i},J(\tilde{x}_{k})+(2m+1,0))\geq\varphi(\sqrt{1+4L^{2}}).\end{aligned}

Here, we used the given a-priori assumption. We substitute (
5.29
) into (
5.28
) to obtain

\varphi(\sqrt{1+4L^{2}})\lim_{t\to\infty}\|\tilde{v}_{k}-\tilde{v}_{i}\|=0,\quad\varphi(\sqrt{1+4L^{2}})\lim_{t\to\infty}\|J(\tilde{v}_{k})-\tilde{v}_{i}\|=0.

Since
\varphi(\sqrt{1+4L^{2}})>0
, we have

(5.30)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\|\tilde{v}_{k}-\tilde{v}_{i}\|=0,\quad\lim_{t\to\infty}\|J(\tilde{v}_{k})-\tilde{v}_{i}\|=0.

We consider the case when
k=i
of second equality for (
5.30
) to get

\lim_{t\to\infty}\|J(\tilde{v}_{i})-\tilde{v}_{i}\|=0\quad\forall i\in\mathcal{N}.

From the definition of the operator
J
, we could obtain that the second component of
\tilde{v}_{i}
converges to zero.
∎

Remark 5.2

(Self-interaction effect of the flat Möbius strip)
.

If we put
x_{i}=x_{k}=x
into (
5.27
), we get (
4.21
) on the Möbius strip as follows:

\lim_{t\to\infty}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\varphi(\mathrm{dist}(\tilde{x},J^{n}(\tilde{x})+(n,0)))\|J^{n}(\tilde{v})-\tilde{v}\|=0.

From the definition of
J
, we can simplify the above relation as follows:

\lim_{t\to\infty}\left(\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\varphi(\mathrm{dist}(\tilde{x},J(\tilde{x})+(2m+1,0)))\right)\|J(\tilde{v})-\tilde{v}\|=0.

The simple calculation yields

1\leq\inf_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{dist}(\tilde{x},J(\tilde{x})+(2m+1,0))=\inf_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\sqrt{(2m+1)^{2}+4(\tilde{x})_{2}^{2}}\leq\sqrt{1+4L^{2}}.

If
\varphi(1)=0
, then
\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\varphi(\mathrm{dist}(\tilde{x},J(\tilde{x})+(2m+1,0)))=0
and there is no self-interaction effect. However, if we assume
\varphi(\sqrt{1+4L^{2}})>0
and a-priori condition:
|x^{2}|<L
, then we have

\lim_{t\to\infty}\|J(\tilde{v})-\tilde{v}\|=0.

This also implies

\lim_{t\to\infty}(\tilde{v})_{2}=0.

So, in this case, the self-interaction effect of the flat Möbius strip yields that the second coordinate of the velocity converges to zero.

(5.31)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\tilde{x}_{i}=\tilde{v}_{i},\vskip 5.69046pt\\

Remark 5.3

\lim_{t\to\infty}\sum_{(l,m)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\varphi(\mathrm{dist}(\tilde{x},J(\tilde{x})+(2l+1,m)))\|J(\tilde{v})-\tilde{v}\|^{2}=0

6.
Conclusion

Throughout this paper, we constructed a velocity alignment model on manifolds and studied the emergent dynamics in various spaces. Previously in
[
15
]
, the CS model on manifold uses the shortest geodesic which connects two particles to define an interaction between them. One disadvantage of this model is that if there exist two or more than two shortest geodesics then the system can not be well-defined. We tried to remove the speciality of the shortest geodesic. So, we consider all of geodesics which is connecting
i^{th}
particle and
j^{th}
particle to define an interaction between
i^{th}
and
j^{th}
particles. In this way, we could obtain a modified velocity alignment system. One of the interesting properties of a modified system is the self-interaction effect. This effect depends on the structure of the domain of the system. In the last section, we provided the emergent behaviors of the modified system defined on some specific spaces(flat torus, flat Möbius strip, flat Klein bottle). However, our model can not be applied on the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{d}
with
d\geq 2
. Since there are uncountably many geodesics that connecting antipodal pair points, we can not define the sum of interactions in this case. We need another setup to cover this case, and it can be nice future work.

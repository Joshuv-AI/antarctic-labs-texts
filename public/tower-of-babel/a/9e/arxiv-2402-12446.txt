Information-processing in theories constrained by no superluminal causation vs no superluminal signalling
V. Vilasini, Roger Colbeck

Abstract
Relativistic causality principles constrain information processing possibilities in space-time. No superluminal causation (NSC) and no superluminal signaling (NSS) are two such principles which, although related, are distinct. In this work we study the consequence of these principles by considering the tasks of generating non-classical correlations within two space-time configurations. Considering theories constrained by NSC, we show that the first task is impossible in any classical theory and the second is impossible in any (possibly non-classical) theory. However, we construct a protocol enabling non-classical correlations to be generated in both configurations in a theory restricted by the weaker NSS principle. To do so we exploit theories that allow an effect called jamming. In our realisation, non-communicating agents sharing classical resources and assisted by jamming, can generate PR-box correlations. Using this protocol the violation of NSC without violating NSS would be verifiable. We discuss the implications of these findings for the speed of generation of non-classical correlations. Our work offers insights into the differences in information processing power of theories constrained by NSC, NSS and other relativistic causality principles.

I
Introduction

Relativistic causality principles shape the landscape of information processing in space and time. One such fundamental principle is no superluminal causation (NSC), which constrains information flow between different space-time events. The principle of no superluminal signalling (NSS) is a distinct, yet related causality principle, constraining the ability of agents at different space-time locations to communicate through interventions.

In the context of relativistic causality in tripartite Bell scenarios, so-called jamming non-local theories were proposed in
Grunhaus1996
. Jamming theories enable a party (say Bob) to influence the correlations between the measurement outcomes of space-like separated parties (Alice and Charlie) without influencing the individual outcomes. It has been suggested
Grunhaus1996
;
Horodecki2019
that such theories do not allow for superluminal signalling in Minkowski space-time. The role of other relativistic causality principles in these theories, such as NSC have remained little understood.

In recent works
VilasiniColbeckPRA
;
VilasiniColbeckPRL
, we have developed a general framework in which distinct relativistic causality principles can be fully formalised within a broad class of (possibly non-classical) theories. Applying this to jamming theories, we showed in
VilasiniColbeck_Jamming
that these theories necessarily violate the NSC principle. Moreover, we found that under certain circumstances, jamming theories may also violate NSS (contrary to previous claims), and characterised the necessary and sufficient conditions under which jamming would adhere to the NSS principle. These conditions enable an identification of a concrete set of scenarios that violate NSC but respect NSS.

Building on this foundation, we investigate the information processing consequences of the NSC and NSS principles in different theories, highlighting their distinctions. In particular, we consider the generation of non-classical correlations in two space-time configurations. In the first, the task is impossible to achieve in any
classical
theory constrained by NSC, and in the second it is impossible to achieve in
any
theory (possibly non-classical) constrained by NSC. Although impossible under the NSC principle, we explore the possibility of these tasks in a theory constrained by the weaker principle of NSS.

We then investigate these tasks in jamming theories, finding that both tasks can be perfectly achieved in such theories without violating NSS. Specifically, we provide a protocol by which space-like separated agents Alice and Charlie sharing classical resources can generate maximally non-classical PR box correlations, in theories that permit a third agent Bob to jam Alice and Charlie’s correlations. Crucially this only requires Alice and Charlie to communicate to Bob (with no other communication involved) and surprisingly, remains possible in a space-time configuration where the measurement outcomes can be arbitrarily earlier in time with respect to the corresponding measurement settings, in some reference frame. In light of these results, we discuss the ability to operationally certify the violation of NSC in a theory, without violating NSS and also discuss the speed of generating non-classical correlations in different scenarios.

We begin by providing an overview of jamming and of our causality framework for formalising NSC and NSS principles, before presenting the tasks and results.

Jamming non-local correlations

Consider a tri-partite Bell scenario where Alice, Bob and Charlie measure some shared system
\Lambda

A

B

X

Causal structures and their compatibility with space-time

Following the framework of
VilasiniColbeckPRA
, we describe information-theoretic causal structures (henceforth simply referred to as causal structures) through an approach based on causal modelling and inference.

Causal modelling and causal inference was originally developed in classical statistics
Pearl2009
and recently generalised to quantum and other non-classical theories (e.g.,
Henson2014
;
Barrett2020A
).
Here,
causal structures
are modelled as directed graphs with directed edges
, and each node can either be an observed node (which corresponds to a classical variable such as a measurement setting or outcome) or an unobserved node (which can be a classical, quantum or a post-quantum system depending on the underlying theory).

In a classical causal model over a causal structure
\mathcal{G}
, all nodes correspond to classical variables and the model is obtained by specifying, for each node

that maps the values of the random variables that are parents of

Causal models defined on acyclic causal structures are known to satisfy the
d-separation property

Pearl2009
;
Henson2014

\mathcal{G}
this property allows one to read off conditional independences of any observed probability distribution,
P_{\mathcal{G}}(N_{obs})

from the graph (see Appendix

As well as accounting for correlations, the framework we have developed in
VilasiniColbeckPRA

of observed nodes in a causal structure
\mathcal{G}
and associates them with a new causal structure
\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{do}(X)}

by removing all incoming edges to

X
is freely chosen, i.e., independent of prior causes of
X
. For two disjoint subsets

and
Y

X

affects

Y
if there exists a value
x
of
X
such that

P_{\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{do}(X)}}(Y|X=x)\neq P_{\mathcal{G}}(Y).

(2)

This encapsulates the idea that the probability of
Y

Causal models can be embedded in space-time by assigning to each observed node

Ref.
VilasiniColbeckPRA

V
Achieving the tasks in NSS theories

The following theorem shows that the above tasks which are impossible in NSC theories become possible in NSS theories.

Theorem 2
.

Tasks
1
and
2
can both be achieved in a classical theory that allows for jamming, without violating NSS and using the assistance of one additional agent Bob. Alice and Charlie only send classical communication to Bob, and exchange no communication otherwise. In particular, the maximally non-classical, PR box correlations can be generated in this manner.

We now describe a protocol that achieves the above.

Protocol 1
.

Consider an additional agent Bob (associated with a variable
B
) who can jam the correlations between Alice and Charlie’s output variables
X
and
Z
, through his choice of
B
. All variables are taken to be binary. Alice and Charlie communicate their classical inputs
A
and
C
to Bob, who sets
B=A.C
. Then through jamming, Bob can ensure that
B
determines the parity of
X
and
Z
without being correlated individually with
X
or
Z
, i.e.,
B=X\oplus Z
. The result is
X\oplus Z=A.C
which correspond to PR-box correlations
P(XZ|AC)
.

By modifying the associated causal model other non-classical correlations are also possible as explained in Appendix
C
.

The jamming required in this protocol can be achieved through a classical common cause
\Lambda
influencing
X
and
Z
as well as a superluminal causal influence from
B
to
X
or
Z

VilasiniColbeck_Jamming
. The common cause
\Lambda
must remain inaccessible to avoid the possibility of superluminal signalling using this influence. In Appendix
C

1a
and
1b
. This proves that the protocol satisfies NSS. Moreover, there is also a natural connection between our protocol with jamming and non-local hidden variable theories, see Appendix
D

VI
Certifying violation of NSC without violation of NSS

We have seen that NSS theories can achieve strictly more informational tasks than NSC theories (as NSC implies NSS, see Appendix
B
for details). We now ask whether this can be used to certify the presence of a superluminal causal influence in NSS theories that achieve the tasks, as this would operationally establish that the violation of NSC is the relevant resource involve.

We observe that in Task
1
, there is no theory-independent way to verify the superluminal causal influence. This is because the task is possible without superluminal causal influence in quantum and post-quantum non-classical theories. In particular, in box-world (a post-quantum non-classical theory), Alice and Charlie can share a bipartite (non-classical) resource of the theory, measure it using their settings
A
and
C
to obtain the outcomes
X
and
Z
respectively which would yield the non-classical PR box correlations
X\oplus Z=A.C
without invoking any superluminal influences even when the measurements take place in space-like separation.

By contrast, in Task
2
, we can certify the existence of the superluminal causal influence in a theory-independent manner. In any theory without superluminal causation, we have shown that
P(XZ|AC)=P(XZ)
holds in Task
2
. Therefore repeating this experiment several times and collecting statistics, if we find that with access to
A
and
C
we can predict any function of
X
and
Z
better than without
A
and
C
, then we certify the existence of a superluminal causal influence in a theory-independent manner. Our model with jamming described in Protocol
1
allows the parity function
f(X,Z)=X\oplus Z
to be predicted perfectly given
A
and
C
, even when the parity cannot be predicted better than a random guess when
A
and
C
are not given. Notice that in the space-time embedding of Figure
1b
,
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{C}
can be jointly accessed outside the joint future of
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Z}
, and thus, in a theory that allows jamming, an agent with access to
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{C}
can perfectly predict the parity of
X
and
Z
even outside the joint future of
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Z}
.

VII
Speed of creation of non-classical correlations

Consider again the task of generating non-classical correlations
P(XZ|AC)
between Alice and Charlie. For simplicity, we discuss the case of
(1+1)
-dimensional Minkowski space-time, though the idea generalises. Suppose that Alice and Charlie remain stationary at the spatial locations
x_{A}
and
x_{C}
respectively (let
x_{C}>x_{A}
wlog) while carrying out their measurements. In an NSC theory, any information travelling between the locations
x_{A}
and
x_{C}
that would correlate the variables produced there can at best travel at the speed of light
c
(even if the communication of this information was mediated by a third party, Bob). Therefore the minimum time required to establish any non-classical correlations between Alice and Charlie if they do not have any pre-shared non-classical resources would be
t_{{\rm NSC}}=\frac{x_{C}-x_{A}}{c}
.
3
3

3

In the common rest frame of the parties, since we have taken them to be stationary at their respective locations.

On the other hand, consider the causal model of Protocol
1
embedded in space-time as per Figure
1a
where Alice’s setting and outcome are associated with the same spatial location and similarly for Charlie, but the timing is such that the measurements are space-like separated. Our results imply that in a theory that allows jamming of space-like separated correlations,
Alice and Charlie can establish non-classical correlations (such as the PR-box correlations) in half this time i.e.,
t_{{\rm jam}}=\frac{x_{C}-x_{A}}{2c}=\frac{t_{{\rm NSC}}}{2}
. They can do so with the help of a third party Bob situated half way between Alice and Charlie, who can receive information from them (which takes
t_{{\rm jam}}
amount of time) and immediately jam their outputs to produce the required PR box correlations. Thus non-classical correlations can be created at double the speed in such theories.

If we allow the outputs
X
and
Z
to be produced at different spatial locations
x_{X}\leq x_{A}
and
x_{Z}\geq x_{C}
, then in theories without superluminal causation, more time would be required for information to travel from the input of one party to the output of the other and we would have
t_{{\rm NSC}}\geq\frac{x_{C}-x_{A}}{c}
. However, a surprising situation arises in jamming theories. It is possible to establish non-classical correlations even with a negative
t_{{\rm jam}}
i.e., where the outputs are produced at a much earlier time than the inputs (in some frame of reference). Referring to Figure
1b
, we can move the RVs
X
and
Z
down along a light-like surface such that they end up arbitrarily far apart in space and time but their joint future (blue region) remains unaltered and continues to be contained in the future of the ORV
\mathcal{B}
, enabling Bob to jam them. Thus we can always find a reference frame in which
t_{{\rm jam}}
can be arbitrarily negative.

Thus a theory constrained by no superluminal signalling can in general create non-classical correlations in a shorter time than in a theory constrained by the stronger principle of no superluminal causation. Jamming theories are an example of the former but can lead to different times for creating non-classical correlations, depending on the space-time configuration under consideration.

VIII
Conclusions and outlook

Our work highlights fundamental and practical distinctions between the relativistic causality principles of no superluminal causation (NSC) and no superluminal signaling (NSS). We demonstrated that theories constrained by the weaker NSS principle can offer larger information processing possibilities over those restricted by NSC, by considering concrete tasks. Specifically, we link a previously introduced class of theories, jamming non-local theories to these questions, showing the existence of scenarios with jamming that violate NSC but satisfy NSS, which are capable of perfectly achieving the aforementioned tasks.

Recent work
VilasiniColbeck_Jamming
shows that jamming can lead to a violation of NSS in certain scenarios (contrary to previous claims
Grunhaus1996
;
Horodecki2019
). Our results highlight that even in scenarios where jamming obeys NSS, it can lead to counter-intuitive effects due to the (certifiable) violation of NSC. The space-time configuration of our second task allows measurement outcomes to occur at earlier times than the corresponding settings, yet jamming would enable the generation of PR box correlations between these setting-outcome variables. This also provides a connection between jamming non-local correlations with other post-quantum correlations such as PR boxes.

There is scope to further investigate the information processing power and limitations of theories with these principles, as well as related principles such as
no causal loops (NCL)
which has been formalised in our framework
VilasiniColbeckPRA
;
VilasiniColbeckPRL
. For example, in the context of comparing NSC and NSS, it would be interesting to understand the implications of being able to generate PR box correlations in the space-time configuration of Task
2
for tasks such as cryptography or communication complexity. Furthermore, Ref.
VilasiniColbeckPRL
shows, through a constructive example that NSS does not imply NCL even in acyclic space-times such as Minkowski space-time. Formally comparing information processing in theories constrained by NCL to those constrained by NSS would allow us to identify whether one can surpass the other.

The relationship between relativistic causality principles has bearing on our understanding of fundamental physics. Considering the differences in information processing power that arise from such principles facilitates a better understanding of why the world works in the way it does and may point to new axioms for physical laws.

Appendix B
Details of how we define relativistic causality principles

The two relativistic causality principles considered in this paper are: no superluminal causation (NSC) and no superluminal signalling (NSS). More precisely, NSC (/NSS) imposes that when we embed a informational protocol (which can be equivalently described as a causal model) in space-time by promoting the relevant variables of the protocols to space-time variables, then there is no causal influence (/signalling) from one space-time event to another event outside its future lightcone. In our framework, space-time is modelled as a partially ordered set
\mathcal{T}
where
P\prec Q
,
P\succ Q
and
P\not\prec\not\succ Q
for
P,Q\in\mathcal{T}
represent
P
being in the future light-cone of, past light-cone of, and space-like separated to
Q
respectively. An embedding
\mathcal{E}
of a causal model with observed nodes
S_{{\rm obs}}
(or a set
\mathscr{A}
of affects relations over a set
S_{{\rm obs}}
of variables) simply assigns a location in
\mathcal{T}
to each random variable
X\in S_{{\rm obs}}
, thereby generating an ordered random variable (ORVs)
\mathcal{X}:=(X,O(X))
where
O(X)\in\mathcal{T}
. ORVs are simply an abstraction of space-time random variables, to general partially ordered sets. Moreover, the inclusive future of an ORV is then given as

\overline{\mathcal{F}}(\mathcal{X}):=\{P\in\mathcal{T}|P\succeq O(X)\}.

(6)

Intrinsically harmonic forms and flat circle bundles
Elizeu França, Francesco Mercuri

Abstract
. We establish a criterion for the flatness of a principal circle bundle in terms of the intrinsically harmonic form problem. It states that the flatness is equivalent to the intrinsic harmonicity of a certain natural associated form.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 57R22; Secondary 53C65, 53C05, 53C15.

Introduction and statement of results

A classical theorem due to Hodge states that in each De Rham’s cohomology class of a compact Riemannian manifold, there is one, and only one, harmonic form. A natural question is the following.

Given a closed-form
\omega
on a compact manifold
M
, is there a Riemannian metric
g
on
M
such that
\omega
is harmonic concerning
g
?

If such a metric exists,
\omega
is called an
intrinsically harmonic form
(I.H.). The problem of obtaining an intrinsic characterization of harmonic forms was first placed by Calabi
[
2
]
.
He showed that an
1
-form
\omega
is I.H., under suitable conditions on its zero-set, if and only if it is
transitive
.
Transitivity means that for every point
x
that is not a zero of
\omega
, there is an embedded circle containing
x
, such that the restriction of
\omega
to it never vanishes. Volkov
[
25
]
made the most notable advance in this subject. He generalized Calabi’s characterization of I.H.
1
-forms by showing that a closed 1-form is I.H. if, and only if, it is harmonic in a neighborhood of its zero set and transitive. The condition of transitivity can be generalized for higher degree forms. Roughly speaking, a
k
-form
\omega
is transitive if any “regular point” is contained in a closed submanifold, to which
\omega
restricts to a volume form. In
[
9
]
Honda proved a “dual” version of Calabi-Volkov’s result by showing that a transitive closed (
n-1
)-form is I.H., under suitable conditions on its zero-set.

By a standard foliation’s argument, one can prove that nowhere-vanishing closed 1-form on a closed manifold is transitive, hence I.H. Naturally, the following issue comes up. Is a nowhere-vanishing closed
(n-1)
-form I.H.? As we will see, there is a relation between this issue and the one of obtaining conditions so that a nondegenerate volume-preserving flow admits a global cross-section.

Given a volume-form
\Omega
on an orientable manifold, every closed
(n-1)
-form corresponds to a unique vector field which induces a flow preserving
\Omega
. Conversely, any volume-preserving flow induces a closed
(n-1)
-form. Furthermore, the fixed points of the flow are exactly the singular points of the associate form (Lemma
2
). In Honda’s thesis, the following criterion is implicitly given

Theorem 1
.

Let
M
be a closed orientable manifold. A nowhere-vanishing volume-preserving flow defined on
M
admits a global cross-section if, and only if, the induced closed nowhere-vanishing
(n-1)
-form is I.H.

Using this Theorem and a standard foliation’s argument, we can prove that the existence of a transversal
C^{r}
-foliation (
r\geq 2
) is equivalent to the existence of a global cross-section (Theorem
10
).

We look for examples of
(n-1)
-forms that are
not
I.H. among the nowhere-vanishing given in circle bundles. Since a closed nowhere-vanishing
(n-1)
-form is I.H. if, and only if, the induced flow admits a complementary foliation, the pull-back of the volume form on a circle bundle is an I.H. form if, and only if, the bundle is smoothly foliated (or admits a global cross-section).

The first criterion to decide if a flow admits a global cross-section is due to Schwartzman
[
22
]
. He introduced the notion of asymptotic cycles. Such a cycle is a real homology class defined for each invariant measure. A global cross-section is determined by an integral 1-dimensional cohomology class that is positive on all asymptotic cycle. With this result in the hands, we provide a class of I.H. forms, and by using such a class, we characterize flat circle bundles. Our main results are the following theorems.

Theorem 2
.

Let
M
be a closed smooth manifold with a nowhere-vanishing closed
(n-1)
-form
\omega
inducing a pointwise periodic flow. If the orbits of the induced flow are homologous to each other and
[\omega]\neq 0
in
H^{n-1}_{DR}(M)
, then
\omega
is I.H. Furthermore, there exists a smooth
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-action on
M
with the same orbits as the induced flow.

Theorem 3
.

A differentiable principal circle bundle
\mathcal{B}=\{B,p,M,\mathbb{S}^{1}\}
over a closed orientable base admits a flat connection, if and only if, one (and consequently all) of the four conditions below holds:

(1)

p^{*}(\Omega_{M})
is I.H.;

(2)

p^{*}(\Omega_{M})
determines a nonzero cohomology class;

(3)

the fiber represents a nonzero class in
H_{1}(B;\mathbb{R})
;

(4)

\chi(\mathcal{B})
, the Euler’s class of
\mathcal{B}
, is a torsion element.

Also, we presented similar results for the non-orientable cases. In degrees different from
0,1,n-1,n
, the question of obtaining an intrinsic characterization of harmonic forms is still open. A notable aspect of the problem, illustrating its difficulty, is the fact that Calabi’s argument does not apply to intermediate degrees (see Remark
4
).

Throughout this work, all manifolds, differential forms (forms for short), flows and vector fields are considered to be
C^{\infty}
, unless explicitly otherwise stated.

Let

Definition 1

Let

be a Riemannian manifold. The Laplace-Beltrami operator concerning to

Lemma 1

Let
(M,g)

If

It follows that

∎

Theorem 4
.

(Hodge) In each cohomology class of a Riemannian closed manifold

there exists a unique harmonic representation.

It follows from Hodge’s Theorem that if

Definition 2
.

Let

be a manifold. A form

Hear some simple observations.

An exact form is intrinsically harmonic if and only if it is the zero form.

2)

A 0-form is intrinsically harmonic if and only if is locally constant.

Associated vector field

Let

(n-1)
-form on

Lemma 2

There exists a unique vector field

\omega=\iota_{X}\Omega
. The form is closed if, and only if, the flow
\Phi
generated by

is volume preserving. Furthermore, the fixed points of
\Phi
are the same as the zeros of
\omega

Proof.

An
(n-1)
-form
\omega
on
M
defines a unique vector field
X
such that
\iota_{X}\Omega=\omega
, in the following way. Let
\{U_{i}\}
be an atlas for

, and
\{\lambda_{i}\}
a partition of the unity subordinated to it. On each open
U_{i}
, one can choose a local frame
\{X_{1}^{i},\cdots X_{n}^{i}\}
so that

\begin{cases}\iota_{X^{i}_{1}}\omega\equiv 0,\\
\Omega(X_{1}^{i},\cdots X_{n}^{i})\equiv 1.\end{cases}

Once such choice is made, one can see that in
U_{i}
the following equality holds:

(1)

\omega=\omega(X_{2}^{i},\cdots X_{n}^{i})\iota_{X^{i}_{1}}\Omega.

We define a field
X
on the entire

by setting

X:=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}\omega(X_{2}^{i},\cdots X_{n}^{i})X_{1}^{i}.

It then follows from Equation
1

\omega=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}\omega=\sum_{i}\lambda_{\omega}(X_{2}^{i},\cdots X_{n}^{i})\iota_{X_{1}^{i}}\Omega=\iota_{X}\Omega.

Suppose now that

so that

is indeed volume preserving. Conversely, given any volume preserving field

is closed, since by Cartan’s Formula

\mathrm{d}\omega=\mathrm{d}(\iota_{X}\Omega)=\mathcal{L}_{X}\Omega-\iota_{X}\mathrm{d}\Omega=0.

Finally,
\omega_{x}=0
if and only if

\omega_{x}=0

∎

4.
Characterization of flat circle bundles

It is well-known that every orientable smooth circle bundle
\mathcal{B}=\{B,p,M,\mathbb{S}^{1}\}
admits principal bundle structure. On other hand, a principal bundle is trivial if, and only if, it admits a global cross-section, i.e., a smooth function
s:M\longrightarrow B
satisfying
ps=1
. The attempt to construct a global cross-section concerning a circle bundle, furnish an obstruction, to known, a element

\chi(\mathcal{B})\in H^{2}(M;\mathbb{Z})

named
Euler characteristic
of the bundle
\mathcal{B}
(
[
16
, page 346]
). Hence, an orientable circle bundle
\mathcal{B}
has a global cross-section if and only if
\chi(\mathcal{B})=0
. This cohomology class carries not only information about the triviality of the bundle. As we will see, it is possible to characterize when is a circle bundle smooth foliated in terms of this class.

There is a natural bijection between the set of all smooth foliated orientable circle bundles over
M
modulo leaf preserving bundle isomorphism and the set of all homomorphisms
\varphi:\pi_{1}(M)\longrightarrow\text{Diff}^{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
modulo conjugacy. Many papers were concerned about the existence of a codimension-one foliation transverse to the fibers of a given circle bundle, i.e., when is foliated a circle bundle.
About circle bundles over surfaces, a necessary and sufficient condition for the existence of a transverse foliation was obtained by Milnor
[
12
]
and Wood
[
26
]
. Circle bundles over a three-manifold were studied by Miyoshi
[
13
]
. An answer to when is smooth foliated a circle bundle was given in
[
14
,
17
]
.

In this section, the main result is to characterize a flat circle bundle over an orientable base by proving Theorem
3

are known in the literature. However, we furnish alternative proof based on our techniques. We began with a useful lemma.

\mathbb{S}^{1}

\mathcal{B}
is differentiable isomorphic to
\mathcal{B}^{{}^{\prime}}
as
\text{Diff}^{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
-bundles then the same is true as
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-bundles. To finish, a flat connection in
\mathcal{B}^{{}^{\prime}}
can be transferred to a flat connection for
\mathcal{B}

[
10
, pag. 79]
. The converse follows easily from Theorem
14
.
∎

To finish the proof of Theorem
3
we need the following observation.

Remark 7
.

(Plante,
[
20
]
)
Given a smooth orientable sphere bundle
\mathcal{B}=\{B,p,M,\mathbb{S}^{k}\}
we have the Gysin cohomology
sequence (real coefficients)

...\longrightarrow H^{k}(M)\longrightarrow^{p^{*}}H^{k}(B)\longrightarrow H^{0}(M)\longrightarrow^{\Psi}H^{k+1}(M)\longrightarrow...

where
\Psi(1)\in H^{k+1}(M)
is just the Euler’s class of the bundle. Hence, the fiber is null-homologous in
H_{k}(E)
if and only if
p*
is surjective. This happens if, and only if, the Euler’s class is nonzero.

We are now in a position to prove Theorem
3
.

Theorem 16
.

A differentiable principal circle bundle
\mathcal{B}=\{B,p,M,\mathbb{S}^{1}\}
over a closed orientable base admits a flat connection, if and only if, one (and consequently all) of the four conditions below holds:

(1)

p^{*}(\Omega_{M})
is I.H.;

(2)

p^{*}(\Omega_{M})
determines a nonzero cohomology class;

(3)

the fiber represents a nonzero class in
H_{1}(B;\mathbb{R})
;

(4)

\chi(\mathcal{B})
, the Euler’s class of
\mathcal{B}
, is a torsion element.

Proof.

Note we have the equivalence between item 2 and item 3, by Lemma
7
, and between item 3 and item 4, by Plante’s observation
7
(considering that the real Euler’s class
\chi_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathcal{B})
of
\mathcal{B}
is equal to
\chi(\mathcal{B})\otimes\mathbb{R}
). Since item 2 follows from item 1 by Hodge’s theorem and 1 from 2 by Theorem
13
, the proof is completed by using Theorem
14
.
∎

For a general smooth circle bundle, we have the following theorem.

Theorem 17
.

Let
\mathcal{B}=\{B,p,M,\mathbb{S}^{1}\}
be a circle bundle over a closed manifold. If fiber determines a nonzero real homological class then
\mathcal{B}
is smooth foliated.

Proof.

Let
\pi:\tilde{B}\longrightarrow B
the double cover of
B
. Since
p
is a submersion, by Eheresmann’s lemma
[
6
]
,
\tilde{\mathcal{B}}=\{\tilde{B},p\circ\pi,M\}
determines a fiber bundle with fibers given by the connected components of
\pi^{-1}(p^{-1}(x))
(that are at most two). Let
B_{x}
the fiber of
\mathcal{B}
over
x
. Suppose that
[B_{x}]\neq 0
in
H_{1}(B;\mathbb{R})
. Then, given fiber
\tilde{B}_{x}
of
\tilde{\mathcal{B}}
satisfying
\pi(\tilde{B}_{x})=B_{x}
, we have
\pi_{*}([\tilde{B}_{x}])=[B_{x}]\neq 0
, and
[\tilde{B}_{x}]\neq 0
. It follows by Theorem
3
that
\tilde{\mathcal{B}}
is smooth foliated. Let
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
be a transversal foliation to
\tilde{\mathcal{B}}
. Then,
\pi_{*}(T\tilde{\mathcal{F}})
is an involutive constant rank distribution transversal to the fibers of
\mathcal{B}
, since
p_{*}[X,Y]=[p_{*}X,p_{*}Y]
. Hence, it give a transversal foliation
\mathcal{F}
on
B
, concluding that
\mathcal{B}
is smooth foliated.
∎

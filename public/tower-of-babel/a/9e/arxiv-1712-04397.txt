Dispersion of particles in an infinite-horizon Lorentz gas
Lior Zarfaty, Alexander Peletskyi, Itzhak Fouxon, Sergey Denisov, Eli Barkai

Abstract
We consider a two-dimensional Lorentz gas with infinite horizon. This paradigmatic model consists of pointlike particles undergoing elastic collisions with fixed scatterers arranged on a periodic lattice. It was rigorously shown that when t → ∞ t\to\infty , the distribution of particles is Gaussian. However, the convergence to this limit is ultraslow, hence it is practically unattainable. Here we obtain an analytical solution for the Lorentz gas’ kinetics on physically relevant timescales, and find that the density in its far tails decays as a universal power law of exponent − 3 -3 . We also show that the arrangement of scatterers is imprinted in the shape of the distribution.

Discussion and summary.

In Ref.
Carl
, an interesting doubling effect was pointed out. While for familiar diffusion processes, e.g., Brownian motion, the Gaussian packet’s variance is equal to the mean-square displacement (MSD), in this case there exists a factor of
2
between them. It arises from the fat-tail behavior of the packet of particles, as the MSD has two contributing elements, the far tail
\sim|\boldsymbol{r}|^{-3}
found here and the Gaussian bulk. As only half of the MSD can be explained using the Gaussian approximation, one needs to go beyond it. In this sense, the power-law tail is needed for a correct description of the MSD, which is the standard quantifier of diffusive processes. Calculation of the MSD demands the introduction of a far-tail cutoff, namely the density is zero beyond
|\boldsymbol{r}|=t
(see Figs.
2
and
3
). In order to receive a full description of the problem, one must construct a theory moving from that end point
|\boldsymbol{r}|=t
inward, e.g., to introduce the infinite covariant density
ICD
.

Our theory provides a description for the dynamics of the two-dimensional infinite-horizon LG based on the LW approach. With a correct choice of
F(\boldsymbol{v})
and an appropriate calculation of
\psi(\tau)
, this theory can be extended to arbitrary lattice geometries, as well for other models and systems which exhibit similar features to the infinite horizon LG model
kin
;
obs
;
mushroom
;
bac
;
emp
. Importantly, since the power-law behavior Eq. (
1
) is valid for any spatial dimension
d<6
of the LG, so do our findings. While for the intermediate times we found smooth behavior of the particle’s PDF, for short enough times one finds oscillations in
P(\boldsymbol{r},t)
(see Fig. 1 of Ref.
Carl
). These clearly originate from the stairlike structure of
\text{CDF}(\tau)
[see Fig.
1
(c)].

Finally, we have carried out numerical simulations of a one-dimensional chain of stadium billiards
channel
. We found that the PDF produced by this model perfectly fits a one-dimensional variant of Eq. (
5
). This is further evidence that our findings are universal and irrespective of the system’s spatial dimension, assuming the infinite horizon and chaotic (renewal) conditions are met.

\tau_{0}^{2}=\lim_{M\to\infty}\frac{16}{M}\sum_{n=0}^{M}\int_{\beta_{n,1}^{\rm min}(R)}^{\beta_{n,1}^{\rm max}(R)}\frac{\text{d}\beta}{2\pi}\int_{b_{n,1}^{\rm min}(\beta,R)}^{\phi_{n,1}^{\rm max}(\beta,R)}\frac{\text{d}\phi}{2R}\;\tau^{*3}_{n,1}(\beta,b,R).

(SM9)

Eq. (
SM9
) converges rather slowly. Since its summands are all positive, it can be changed into:

\tau_{0}^{2}=\lim_{M\to\infty}16\int_{\beta_{M,1}^{\rm min}(R)}^{\beta_{M,1}^{\rm max}(R)}\frac{\text{d}\beta}{2\pi}\int_{b_{M,1}^{\rm min}(\beta,R)}^{\phi_{M,1}^{\rm max}(\beta,R)}\frac{\text{d}\phi}{2R}\;\tau^{*3}_{M,1}(\beta,b,R).

(SM10)

For
M=5000
, we obtained
\tau_{0}\approx 0.25238
, with a relative error of
0.028\%
to our analytical result Eq. (10). Finally, for
C_{\psi}
we obtain the following formula out of Eq. (4):

C_{\psi}=\lim_{T\to\infty}\exp\left[\gamma-\frac{3}{2}-\int_{0}^{T}\text{d}\tau\,\psi(\tau)\left(\frac{\tau}{\tau_{0}}\right)^{2}+\ln\left(\frac{\tau}{\tau_{0}}\right)\right].

(SM11)

Combining it with Eq. (
SM4
) yields:

C_{\psi}=\lim_{M\to\infty}\exp\left[\gamma-\frac{3}{2}+\ln\left(\frac{M}{\tau_{0}}\right)-16\sum_{n=0}^{M}\int_{\beta_{n,1}^{\rm min}(R)}^{\beta_{n,1}^{\rm max}(R)}\frac{\text{d}\beta}{2\pi}\int_{b_{n,1}^{\rm min}(\beta,R)}^{\phi_{n,1}^{\rm max}(\beta,R)}\frac{\text{d}\phi}{2R}\frac{\tau^{*2}_{n,1}(\beta,b,R)}{\tau_{0}^{2}}\right],

(SM12)

and we receive for
M=5000
the value
C_{\psi}\approx 4.4816\cdot 10^{-4}
.

I.2
Leading behavior of
\hat{\psi}(u\rightarrow 0)

Here we derive Eq. (3), obtaining Eq. (4) during the process. We assume that
\psi(\tau)
behaves asymptotically as

\lim_{\tau\rightarrow\infty}\psi(\tau)\tau^{3}=\tau_{0}^{2},\quad\tau_{0}>0,

(SM13)

with its Laplace transform being defined by

\hat{\psi}(u)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\text{d}\tau\,\psi(\tau)e^{-u\tau}.

(SM14)

We will now show that using Eq. (
SM13
), Eq. (
SM14
) reduces to Eq. (3) for small
u
. To do so, we rewrite Eq. (
SM14
) as:

\hat{\psi}(u)=1-u\left<\tau\right>+\int_{0}^{\infty}\text{d}\tau\,\psi(\tau)\left[e^{-u\tau}-1+u\tau\vphantom{\frac{1}{2}}\right].

(SM15)

In order to find the leading behavior of the last term of Eq. (
SM15
), the following limit is considered:

l_{1}=\lim_{u\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{u^{2}\ln(u)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\text{d}\tau\,\psi(\tau)\left[e^{-u\tau}-1+u\tau\vphantom{\frac{1}{2}}\right].

(SM16)

Using L’Hospital’s rule three times, followed by a change of variable to
\eta=u\tau
, Eq. (
SM16
) takes the form:

l_{1}=-\frac{1}{2}\lim_{u\rightarrow 0}\int_{0}^{\infty}\text{d}\eta\,\psi\left(\frac{\eta}{u}\right)\left(\frac{\eta}{u}\right)^{3}e^{-\eta}.

(SM17)

For any finite
u
, the integrand vanishes for
\eta=0
, as
\psi(\tau)
is normalized. Since a finite set of bounded points does not contribute to an integral, we may discard this point, such that the integration is carried over the domain
(0,\infty)
. This allows us to employ the dominated convergence theorem, and switch the order of limit and integration in Eq. (
SM17
). Together with Eq. (
SM13
), we have:

l_{1}=-\frac{\tau_{0}^{2}}{2}\int_{0^{+}}^{\infty}\text{d}\eta\,e^{-\eta}=-\frac{\tau_{0}^{2}}{2},

(SM18)

which shows that the limit
l_{1}=-\tau_{0}^{2}/2
is finite, yielding:

\hat{\psi}(u)\simeq 1-\langle\tau\rangle u+l_{1}u^{2}\ln(u)+\mathcal{O}\left(u^{2}\right).

(SM19)

To complete the derivation, we calculate the next order correction
\sim u^{2}
. For that cause, the following limit is considered:

l_{2}=\lim_{u\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{u^{2}}\left\{\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi(\tau)\left[e^{-u\tau}-1+u\left<\tau\right>\vphantom{\frac{1}{2}}\right]\text{d}\tau+\frac{\tau_{0}^{2}}{2}u^{2}\ln(u)\right\}.

(SM20)

Using L’Hospital’s rule two times, followed by a split of the integral at
\tau=\tau_{0}
, Eq. (
SM20
) becomes:

l_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\lim_{u\rightarrow 0}\int_{0}^{\tau_{0}}\text{d}\tau\,\psi(\tau)\tau^{2}e^{-u\tau}+\frac{1}{2}\lim_{u\rightarrow 0}\left\{\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\infty}\text{d}\tau\psi(\tau)\tau^{2}e^{-u\tau}+\tau_{0}^{2}\left[\ln(u)+\frac{3}{2}\right]\right\}.

(SM21)

The first integral of Eq. (
SM21
) is carried over a finite region, so one can exchange the order of limit and integration. Moving to the second integral, we add and subtract the term
\tau_{0}^{2}/\tau^{3}
from
\psi(\tau)
, which yields:

l_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\tau_{0}}\text{d}\tau\,\psi(\tau)\tau^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\lim_{u\rightarrow 0}\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\infty}\text{d}\tau\left[\psi(\tau)-\frac{\tau_{0}^{2}}{\tau^{3}}\right]\tau^{2}e^{-u\tau}+\frac{1}{2}\lim_{u\rightarrow 0}\left\{\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\infty}\text{d}\tau\frac{\tau_{0}^{2}}{\tau}e^{-u\tau}+\tau_{0}^{2}\left[\ln(u)+\frac{3}{2}\right]\right\}.

(SM22)

Note that due to the asymptotics Eq. (
SM13
), the second integral in Eq. (
SM22
) converges when
u\rightarrow 0
. Evaluating the middle row limit and the bottom row integral, we obtain:

l_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\tau_{0}}\text{d}\tau\,\psi(\tau)\tau^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\infty}\text{d}\tau\left[\psi(\tau)-\frac{\tau_{0}^{2}}{\tau^{3}}\right]\tau^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\lim_{u\rightarrow 0}\tau_{0}^{2}\left[\Gamma\left(0,\tau_{0}u\right)+\ln(u)+\frac{3}{2}\right],

(SM23)

where
\Gamma(\cdot,\cdot)
is the incomplete Gamma function, which has the small
\eta
behavior
\Gamma(0,\eta)\simeq-\ln(\eta)-\gamma+\eta
. Thus, after some algebra one finds for the limit
l_{2}
:

l_{2}=-\frac{\tau_{0}^{2}}{2}\ln\left(C_{\psi}\tau_{0}\right),

(SM24)

where the constant
C_{\psi}
is defined by Eq. (4). Equation (3) then follows from:

\hat{\psi}(u)\simeq 1-\langle\tau\rangle u+l_{1}u^{2}\ln(u)+l_{2}u^{2}+o\left(u^{2}\right).

(SM25)

I.3
Approximation of the Lévy walk model with Lambert scaling

The two-dimensional Lévy walk model is defined as follows: A random walker is placed at
\boldsymbol{r}(0)
on time
t=0
. Its movement consists of segments of ballistic motion with constant velocity, separated by collision-like events which induce a change in the velocity’s magnitude and/or direction. The process lasts for a predetermined time duration (the measurement time
t
). The model employs two PDFs in order to determine the displacement during each of the ballistic motion epochs. The velocity of each segment is drawn from a PDF
F(\boldsymbol{v})
, whose moments are all finite, and is further assumed to be symmetric with respect to each of the components
v_{j}
, with
j=1,2
, such that its odd moments vanish. The time duration of each ballistic section is drawn from a PDF
\psi(\tau)
. The movement continues until the allotted measurement time is met, thus the number of collisions
N
in
(0,t)
is random. This yields the total displacement as:

\boldsymbol{r}(t)-\boldsymbol{r}(0)=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\boldsymbol{v}_{n-1}\tau_{n}+\boldsymbol{v}_{N}\tau^{*},

(SM26)

with
0\leq\tau^{*}=t-\sum_{n=1}^{N}\tau_{n}
. The traveling times and velocities
\{\tau_{n},\boldsymbol{v}_{n}\}
, for
1\leq n\leq N
, are independent identically distributed random variables, and the initial conditions
\boldsymbol{r}(0)
and
\boldsymbol{v}_{0}
are drawn from equilibrium. Let us denote the probability to find the walker at a position
\boldsymbol{r}
on time
t
as
P(\boldsymbol{r},t)
. Applying Fourier and Laplace transforms to the spatial and temporal coordinates of
P(\boldsymbol{r},t)
respectively, an exact expression of the probability in Fourier-Laplace space, denoted
\Pi(\boldsymbol{k},u)
, is given by the Montroll-Weiss Eq. (2), where

\left<\cdots\right>=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\text{d}^{2}vF(\boldsymbol{v})\cdots

(SM27)

(not to be confused with
\langle\tau\rangle
, which is simply the mean time between collisions). As mentioned in the main text, in order to find the density of particles while accounting for the problem’s spatial structure, we take for the Lorentz gas a “cross” velocity PDF, which represents the underlying square lattice:

F\left(\boldsymbol{v}\right)=\frac{1}{4}\left\{\left[\delta\left(v_{x}-V\right)+\delta\left(v_{x}+V\right)\vphantom{\frac{v_{\times}}{\sqrt{2}}}\right]\delta\left(v_{y}\right)+\delta\left(v_{x}\right)\left[\delta\left(v_{y}-V\right)+\delta\left(v_{y}+V\right)\vphantom{\frac{v_{\times}}{\sqrt{2}}}\right]\right\},

(SM28)

where
V>0
is a constant (in the main text
V=1
). This yields for the denominator of Eq. (2):

1-\left<\hat{\psi}(u-i\boldsymbol{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{v})\right>=1-\frac{1}{4}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left[\hat{\psi}\left(u-iVk_{j}\right)+\hat{\psi}\left(u+iVk_{j}\right)\right].

(SM29)

In order to use the asymptotic form Eq. (3), we assume a scaling of
u\sim k^{2}L(|\boldsymbol{k}|)
, where
L(\cdot)
is some logarithmic-behaving function. This suggests that
u\ll V|\boldsymbol{k}|
when
|\boldsymbol{k}|\rightarrow 0
. Using the identity

\ln\left(a\pm ib\right)=\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(a^{2}+b^{2}\right)\pm i\arctan\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)

(SM30)

together with Eq. (3), while neglecting the appropriate terms according to the above scaling assumption, one has

\hat{\psi}\left(u\pm iVk_{j}\right)\simeq 1-\left<\tau\right>\left(u\pm iVk_{j}\right)-\frac{1}{4}\left(\tau_{0}Vk_{j}\right)^{2}\ln\left[\left(C_{\psi}\tau_{0}Vk_{j}\right)^{2}\right]\pm i\frac{\pi}{4}\left(\tau_{0}Vk_{j}\right)^{2}.

(SM31)

Plugging this into Eq. (
SM29
) while discarding irrelevant terms with respect to the above scaling assumption, we get for the denominator of Eq. (2):

1-\left<\hat{\psi}(u-i\boldsymbol{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{v})\right>\simeq u\left<\tau\right>-\frac{1}{8}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left(\tau_{0}Vk_{j}\right)^{2}\ln\left[\left(C_{\psi}\tau_{0}Vk_{j}\right)^{2}\right],

(SM32)

while for the numerator we have:

\left<\frac{1-\hat{\psi}(u-i\boldsymbol{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{v})}{u-i\boldsymbol{k}\cdot\boldsymbol{v}}\right>\simeq\left<\tau\right>.

(SM33)

Therefore we find, using Eq. (2):

\Pi(\boldsymbol{k},u)\simeq\left[u-\frac{\tau_{0}^{2}V^{2}}{8\left<\tau\right>}\sum_{j=1}^{2}k_{j}^{2}\ln\left(C_{\psi}^{2}\tau_{0}^{2}V^{2}k_{j}^{2}\right)\right]^{-1}.

(SM34)

Returning to the time domain yields for the Fourier transform of
P(\boldsymbol{r},t)
:

\tilde{P}(\boldsymbol{k},t)\simeq\exp\left[t\frac{\tau_{0}^{2}V^{2}}{8\left<\tau\right>}\sum_{j=1}^{2}k_{j}^{2}\ln\left(C_{\psi}^{2}\tau_{0}^{2}V^{2}k_{j}^{2}\right)\right].

(SM35)

Defining
\boldsymbol{\kappa}=\boldsymbol{k}\sqrt{\tau_{0}^{2}V^{2}t\Omega(t)/8\left<\tau\right>}
, where
\Omega(t)
is a yet unknown scaling function, leads to:

\tilde{P}(\boldsymbol{\kappa},t)\simeq\frac{8\left<\tau\right>}{\tau_{0}^{2}V^{2}t\Omega(t)}\exp\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{2}\frac{\kappa_{j}^{2}}{\Omega(t)}\ln\left[\frac{8C_{\psi}^{2}\left<\tau\right>}{t\Omega(t)}\kappa_{j}^{2}\right]\right\}.

(SM36)

Demanding that
\ln[t\Omega(t)/(8C_{\psi}^{2}\langle\tau\rangle)]=\Omega(t)
yields:

\Omega(t)=\left|\text{W}_{-1}\left(-8C_{\psi}^{2}\frac{\left<\tau\right>}{t}\right)\right|,

(SM37)

thus we obtain the following form for (
SM36
):

\tilde{P}(\boldsymbol{\kappa},t)\simeq\frac{8\left<\tau\right>e^{-\kappa^{2}}}{\tau_{0}^{2}V^{2}t\Omega(t)}\exp\left[\sum_{j=1}^{2}\frac{\kappa_{j}^{2}}{\Omega(t)}\ln\left(\kappa_{j}^{2}\right)\right]\simeq\frac{8\left<\tau\right>e^{-\kappa^{2}}}{\tau_{0}^{2}V^{2}t\Omega(t)}\left[1+\sum_{j=1}^{2}\frac{\kappa_{j}^{2}}{\Omega(t)}\ln\left(\kappa_{j}^{2}\right)\right].

(SM38)

The assumption of large
t
constricts us to large
\Omega(t)
, and thus the second exponential term of Eq. (
SM38
) can be expanded to sub-leading order. The inverse Fourier transform is:

P(\boldsymbol{r},t)=\int\frac{\text{d}^{2}\kappa}{(2\pi)^{2}}\tilde{P}\left(\boldsymbol{\kappa},t\right)\cos\left[\frac{\boldsymbol{\kappa}\cdot\boldsymbol{r}}{\tau_{0}V}\sqrt{\frac{8\left<\tau\right>}{t\Omega(t)}}\right].

(SM39)

Evaluating these integrals, the leading and sub-leading orders of Eq. (
SM39
) result in Eqs. (5) and (6). The mean square displacement (MSD) can be calculated from differentiating the Montroll-Weiss Eq. (2). We find that in the long time limit:

\left<r^{2}(t)\right>\simeq\tau_{0}^{2}V^{2}\frac{t}{\left<\tau\right>}\left[\ln\left(\frac{t}{C_{\psi}\tau_{0}}\right)-2+\gamma\right].

(SM40)

Using Eq. (6), in the extremely long time limit we have
\text{MSD}\simeq 2\sigma^{2}t\ln(t)
, with
\sigma^{2}=\tau_{0}^{2}/2\langle\tau\rangle
, and thus the doubling effect can be seen.

Abstract
. For a henselian valued field ( K , v ) (K,v) we establish a complete parallelism between the arithmetic properties of irreducible polynomials F ∈ K ⁡ [ x ] F\in K[x] , encoded by their Okutsu frames, and the valuation-theoretic properties of their induced valuations v F v_{F} on K ⁡ [ x ] K[x] , encoded by their Mac Lane-Vaquié chains. This parallelism was only known for defectless irreducible polynomials.

Introduction

The pioneering work of Mac Lane on valuations on a polynomial ring
[
13
]
, was inspired in a question of Ore about the design of an algorithm to compute prime ideal decomposition in number fields
[
22
]
. To solve this question, Mac Lane used the methods of
[
13
]
to develop a polynomial factorization algorithm over the completion
K_{v}
of any discrete rank-one valued field
(K,v)

[
14
]
. The algorithm finds
key polynomials
of certain valuations on
K[x]
, as approximations to the irreducible factors in
K_{v}[x]
of any given separable polynomial in
K[x]
.

Motivated by the computation of integral bases in finite extensions of local fields, Okutsu constructed similar approximations without using valuations on
K[x]
, nor key polynomials
[
21
]
. Each irreducible polynomial
F\in K_{v}[x]
admits an
Okutsu frame
, which is a finite list of polynomials which are best possible approximations to
F
among all polynomials with degree smaller than a certain bound.

Fernández, Guàrdia, Montes and Nart showed that the approaches of Ore-Mac Lane and Okutsu are essentially equivalent in the discrete rank-one case
[
5
,
6
]
.

The techniques of Mac Lane in
[
13
]

were extended to arbitrary valued fields independently by Vaquié
[
23
]
and Herrera-Mahboub-Olalla-Spivakovsky
[
7
,
8
]
. The extension of
[
14
]
to a polynomial factorization algorithm over arbitrary henselian fields is still an open problem.

Let
(K,v)
be a henselian valued field of an arbitrary rank. Let

\operatorname{Irr}(K)
be the set of all monic irreducible polynomials in
K[x]
.
Every
F\in\operatorname{Irr}(K)
determines a valuation
v_{F}
on
K[x]
given by

v_{F}(f)=v(f(\theta)),\quad\mbox{for all }f\in K[x],

where
\theta
is any root of
F
in an algebraic closure of
K
.

This valuation
v_{F}
is the end node of some finite
Mac Lane-Vaquié (MLV) chain
of valuations on
K(x)

\mu_{0}\,\,\longrightarrow\,\,\mu_{1}\,\,\longrightarrow\,\,\cdots\,\,\longrightarrow\,\,\mu_{r}\,\,\longrightarrow\,\,v_{F}

where
\mu_{0}
admits key polynomials of degree one, and every step
\mu_{i}\to\mu_{i+1}
may be either an ordinary or a limit augmentation.

We say that
F\in\operatorname{Irr}(K)
is
defectless
if the unique extension
w
of
v
to
K[x]/(F)
satisfies
\deg(F)=e(w/v)f(w/v)
; or, equivalently, if the MLV chain of
v_{F}
contains only ordinary augmentations
[
24
,
15
]
.

The results of
[
5
,
6
]
were generalized in
[
15
]
to approximate defectless polynomials over arbitrary henselian fields.
A certain
Okutsu equivalence relation

\sim_{\operatorname{ok}}
was defined on the set
\operatorname{Dless}
of all defectless polynomials, so that the quotient set
\operatorname{Dless}/\!\!\sim_{\operatorname{ok}}
admits a parametrization by a
Mac Lane space

\mathbb{M}
described in terms of valuations on
K[x]

[
15
, Thm. 5.14]
. On the other hand, a complete parallelism was established between the arithmetic properties of any
F\in\operatorname{Dless}
, encoded by their Okutsu frames, and the valuation-theoretic properties of the valuation
v_{F}
, encoded by their MLV chains
[
15
, Thms. 5.5, 5.6]
.

In this paper, we apply the methods of
[
2
, Secs. 6,7]
on valuative trees to generalize all these results
to arbitrary irreducible polynomials in
K[x]
. The main obstacle is the presence of limit augmentations in the MLV chains of
v_{F}
. This implies that there are no longer “best possible” approximations to
F
of a given bounded degree, so that Okutsu frames need to be reformulated.

The structure of the paper is as follows. Section
1
collects preliminary results on key polynomials, valuative trees and their tangent spaces. Let
\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{Q}}
be the tree whose nodes are extensions of
v
to valuations on
K[x]
taking values in the divisible hull of the group
v(K^{*})
. This tree admits a certain
small-extensions closure

\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{Q}}\subset\mathcal{T}_{\mbox{\tiny$\operatorname{sme}$}}
whose tangent space plays a key role.

In Section
2
, we generalize
[
15
, Thm. 5.14]
. We introduce an Okutsu equivalence relation
\sim_{\operatorname{ok}}
on the set
\mathcal{L}_{\operatorname{fin}}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{Q}})
of finite leaves of the tree
\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{Q}}
. After a natural identification of
\mathcal{L}_{\operatorname{fin}}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{Q}})
with
\operatorname{Irr}(K)
, the quotient set
\operatorname{Irr}(K)/\!\!\sim_{\operatorname{ok}}
may be parametrized by the set
\mathbb{T}^{\operatorname{prim}}
of primitive tangent vectors in the tangent space of
\mathcal{T}_{\mbox{\tiny$\operatorname{sme}$}}
.

In Section
3
we show that two irreducible polynomials are Okutsu equivalent if and only if they are “sufficiently close” with respect to the classical ultrametric topology induced by
v
. Also, we see that the subset of
\mathbb{T}^{\operatorname{prim}}
corresponding to defectless polynomials may be identified with the Mac Lane space
\mathbb{M}
.

In Section
4
, an
Okutsu frame
of any
F\in\operatorname{Irr}(K)
is defined as a list of sets of polynomials:
\left[\Phi_{0},\dots,\Phi_{r},\Phi_{r+1}=\{F\}\right]
.
Each
\Phi_{i}
contains monic polynomials
f\in K[x]
of constant degree
m_{i}
, whose weighted values
v_{F}(f)/\deg(f)
are cofinal in the set of all weighted
v_{F}
-values of polynomials of degree less than
m_{i+1}
. These degrees satisfy

1=m_{0}\,\mid\,m_{1}\,\mid\,\cdots\,\mid\,m_{r}\,\mid\,m_{r+1}=\deg(F).

Theorems
4.4
and
4.5
show that the Okutsu frames of
F
and the MLV chains of
v_{F}
are essentially equivalent objects.
As a consequence, we obtain still another interpretation of Okutsu frames:
the set
\Phi_{0}\cup\cdots\cup\Phi_{r}\cup\{F\}
is a complete set of
abstract
key polynomials of
v_{F}
, in the terminology of
[
3
,
19
]
.

1.
Valuative trees

In this section we recall some results on valuations on a polynomial ring, valuative trees and their tangent spaces.

Let
(K,v)
be a valued field. Let
k
be the residue class field,
\Gamma=v(K^{*})
the value group and
\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}}=\Gamma\otimes\mathbb{Q}
the divisible hull of
\Gamma
. The
rational rank
of
\Gamma
is defined as
\operatorname{rr}(\Gamma)=\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}}
.

Let
\Gamma\hookrightarrow\Lambda
be an extension of ordered abelian groups. We write simply
\Lambda\infty
instead of
\Lambda\cup\{\infty\}
.
Consider a valuation on the polynomial ring
K[x]

\mu\colon K[x]\,\longrightarrow\,\Lambda\infty

whose restriction to
K
is
v
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}=\operatorname{supp}(\mu):=\mu^{-1}(\infty)\in\operatorname{Spec}(K[x])
be the support of
\mu
.
The valuation
\mu
induces in a natural way a valuation
\overline{\mu}
on the field of fractions of
K[x]/\mathfrak{p}
, which is
K(x)
if
\mathfrak{p}=0
, or
K[x]/\mathfrak{p}
if
\mathfrak{p}=fK[x]
for some
f\in\operatorname{Irr}(K)
.

The residue field
k_{\mu}
of
\mu
is, by definition, the residue field of
\overline{\mu}
. The field
\kappa(\mu)
of
algebraic residues
of
\mu
is the relative algebraic closure of
k
in
k_{\mu}
.

We say that
\mu
is
commensurable
(over
v
) if
\Gamma_{\mu}/\Gamma
is a torsion group; or equivalently,
\operatorname{rr}(\Gamma_{\mu}/\Gamma)=0
. In this case, there is a canonical embedding
\Gamma_{\mu}\hookrightarrow\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}}
.

All valuations on
K[x]
satisfy
Abhyankar’s inequality

\operatorname{rr}(\Gamma_{\mu}/\Gamma)+\operatorname{trdeg}(k_{\mu}/k)\leq 1.

This yields a basic classification of these valuations in three families:

•

\mu
is
valuation-algebraic
if
\operatorname{rr}(\Gamma_{\mu}/\Gamma)=\operatorname{trdeg}(k_{\mu}/k)=0
.

•

\mu
is incommensurable if
\operatorname{rr}(\Gamma_{\mu}/\Gamma)=1
.

•

\mu
is
residue transcendental
if
\operatorname{trdeg}(k_{\mu}/k)=1
.

The valuations for which equality holds in Abhyankar’s inequality, corresponding to the two latter families, are said to be
valuation-transcendental
.

All valuations with nontrivial support are valuation-algebraic, but the converse statement is false.

1.1.
Key polynomials

For any
\alpha\in\Gamma_{\mu}
, consider the abelian groups:

\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}=\{g\in K[x]\mid\mu(g)\geq\alpha\}\supset\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}^{+}=\{g\in K[x]\mid\mu(g)>\alpha\}.

The
graded algebra of
\mu
is the integral domain:

\mathcal{G}_{\mu}:=\operatorname{gr}_{\mu}(K[x])=\bigoplus\nolimits_{\alpha\in\Gamma_{\mu}}\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}/\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}^{+}.

There is an
initial coefficient
mapping
\operatorname{in}_{\mu}\colon K[x]\to\mathcal{G}_{\mu}
, given by
\operatorname{in}_{\mu}\mathfrak{p}=0
and

\operatorname{in}_{\mu}g=g+\mathcal{P}_{\mu(g)}^{+}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mu(g)}/\mathcal{P}_{\mu(g)}^{+},\qquad\mbox{if }\ g\in K[x]\setminus\mathfrak{p}.

The following definitions translate properties of the action of
\mu
on
K[x]
into algebraic relationships in the graded algebra
\mathcal{G}_{\mu}
.

Definition.
Let
g,\,h\in K[x]
.

We say that
g,h
are
\mu
-equivalent
, and we write
g\sim_{\mu}h
, if
\operatorname{in}_{\mu}g=\operatorname{in}_{\mu}h
.

We say that
g
is
\mu
-divisible
by
h
, and we write
h\mid_{\mu}g
, if
\operatorname{in}_{\mu}h\mid\operatorname{in}_{\mu}g
in
\mathcal{G}_{\mu}
.

We say that
g
is
\mu
-irreducible if
\operatorname{in}_{\mu}g
is a prime element; that is, it generates a nonzero homogeneus prime ideal.

We say that
g
is
\mu
-minimal if
g\nmid_{\mu}f
for all nonzero
f\in K[x]
with
\deg(f)<\deg(g)
.

For all
g\in K[x]\setminus K
we define the
truncation

\mu_{g}
as follows:

f=\sum\nolimits_{0\leq s}a_{s}g^{s},\quad\deg(a_{s})<\deg(g)\ \ \Longrightarrow\ \ \mu_{g}(f)=\min\left\{\mu\left(a_{s}g^{s}\right)\mid 0\leq s\right\}.

This function
\mu_{g}
is not necessarily a valuation, but it is useful to characterize the
\mu
-minimality of
g
. Let us recall
[
16
, Prop. 2.3]
.

Lemma 1.1
.

A polynomial
g\in K[x]\setminus K
is
\mu
-minimal if and only if
\mu_{g}=\mu
.

Definition.
A
(Mac Lane-Vaquié) key polynomial
for
\mu
is a monic polynomial in
K[x]
which is simultaneously
\mu
-minimal and
\mu
-irreducible.

The set of key polynomials for
\mu
is denoted
\operatorname{KP}(\mu)
.

All
\phi\in\operatorname{KP}(\mu)
are irreducible in
K[x]
.
A
tangent direction
of
\mu
is a
\mu
-equivalence class
\left[\,\phi\,\right]_{\mu}\subset\operatorname{KP}(\mu)
determined by all key polynomials having the same initial coefficient in
\mathcal{G}_{\mu}
. We denote the set of all tangent directions of
\mu
by:

\mbox{\rm\bf td}(\mu)=\operatorname{KP}(\mu)/\!\sim_{\mu}.

Since all polynomials in
\left[\,\phi\,\right]_{\mu}
have the same degree
[
16
, Prop. 6.6]
, it makes sense to consider the degree
\deg\,[\phi]_{\mu}
of a tangent direction.

The basic families of valuations can be characterized as follows by their tangent directions
[
2
, Thms. 1.2, 1.4]
.

•

If
\mu
is valuation-algebraic, then
\mbox{\rm\bf td}(\mu)=\emptyset
. That is,
\operatorname{KP}(\mu)=\emptyset
.

•

If
\mu
is incommensurable, then
\mbox{\rm\bf td}(\mu)
is a one-element set.

•

If
\mu
is residue transcendental, then
\mbox{\rm\bf td}(\mu)
is in bijection with
\operatorname{Irr}(\kappa(\mu))
.

In the latter case, the bijection is determined by the choice of a key polynomial of minimal degree and a certain homogeneous unit in
\mathcal{G}_{\mu}

[
16
, Sec. 6]
.

Definition.
Suppose that
\operatorname{KP}(\mu)\neq\emptyset
and take
\phi\in\operatorname{KP}(\mu)
of minimal degree. The following data are independent of the choice of
\phi
:

\deg(\mu)=\deg(\phi),\qquad\operatorname{sv}(\mu)=\mu(\phi),\qquad\operatorname{wt}(\mu)=\operatorname{sv}(\mu)/\deg(\mu).

They are called the
degree
, the
singular value
and the
weight
of
\mu
, respectively.

Theorem 1.2
.

[
16
, Thm. 3.9]

If
\operatorname{KP}(\mu)\neq\emptyset
, then for all monic
f\in K[x]\setminus K
, we have
\,\mu(f)/\deg(f)\leq\operatorname{wt}(\mu)
. Equality holds if and only if
f
is
\mu
-minimal.

1.2.
Tangent space of a valuative tree

Let
\mathcal{T}=\mathcal{T}(\Lambda)
be the set of all valuations

\mu\colon K[x]\,\longrightarrow\,\Lambda\infty,

whose restriction to
K
is
v
.
This set admits a partial ordering.
For
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{T}
we define

\mu\leq\nu\ \ \Longleftrightarrow\ \ \mu(f)\leq\nu(f),\quad\forall\,f\in K[x].

As usual, we write
\mu<\nu
to indicate that
\mu\leq\nu
and
\mu\neq\nu
.

This poset
\mathcal{T}
has the structure of a tree. By this, we simply mean that the intervals

(-\infty,\mu\,]:=\left\{\rho\in\mathcal{T}\mid\rho\leq\mu\right\}

are totally ordered for all
\mu\in\mathcal{T}

[
17
, Thm. 2.4]
.

A node
\mu\in\mathcal{T}
is a
leaf
if it is a maximal element with respect to the ordering
\leq
. Otherwise, we say that
\mu
is an
inner node
.

Theorem 1.3
.

[
17
, Thm. 2.3]

A node
\mu\in\mathcal{T}
is a leaf if and only it
\operatorname{KP}(\mu)=\emptyset
.

All valuations with nontrivial support are leaves of
\mathcal{T}
. We call them
finite leaves
.
The leaves of
\mathcal{T}
having trivial support are called
infinite leaves
.

A finite leaf
\nu\in\mathcal{T}
has
\operatorname{supp}(\nu)=FK[x]
for some
F\in\operatorname{Irr}(K)
. We define

\deg(\nu)=\deg(F),\qquad\operatorname{sv}(\nu)=\operatorname{wt}(\nu)=\infty.

The following result recalls some fundamental properties of tangent directions. It follows from
[
23
, Thm. 1.15]
,
[
17
, Prop. 2.2]
and
[
2
, Prop. 2.5]
.

Lemma 1.4
.

Let
\mu<\nu
be two nodes in
\mathcal{T}
. Let
\mathbf{t}(\mu,\nu)
be the set of all monic polynomials
\phi\in K[x]
of minimal degree satisfying
\mu(\phi)<\nu(\phi)
.

(i) If
\nu
is an inner node or a finite leaf, then
\,\deg(\mu)\leq\deg(\nu)
and
\,\operatorname{wt}(\mu)<\operatorname{wt}(\nu)
.

(ii)  The set
\mathbf{t}(\mu,\nu)
is a tangent direction of
\mu
. Moreover, for all
\phi\in\mathbf{t}(\mu,\nu)
,
f\in K[x]
, the equality
\mu(f)=\nu(f)
holds if and only if
\phi\nmid_{\mu}f
.

(iii)  If
\mu<\nu^{\prime}
for some
\nu^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}
, then

\mathbf{t}(\mu,\nu)=\mathbf{t}(\mu,\nu^{\prime})\ \Longleftrightarrow\ (\mu,\nu\,]\cap(\mu,\nu^{\prime}\,]\neq\emptyset
.

Definition.
The
tangent space
of
\mathcal{T}
is the set

\mathbb{T}(\mathcal{T})=\left\{(\mu,t)\mid\mu\mbox{ inner node in }\mathcal{T},\ t\in\mbox{\rm\bf td}(\mu)\right\}.

1.3.
Mac Lane–Vaquié chains

For any
\phi\in\operatorname{KP}(\mu)
and any
\gamma\in\Lambda\infty
such that
\mu(\phi)<\gamma
, we can construct the
ordinary augmented valuation

\nu=[\mu;\,\phi,\gamma]\in\mathcal{T}
,
defined in terms of
\phi
-expansions as

f=\sum\nolimits_{0\leq s}a_{s}\phi^{s},\quad\deg(a_{s})<\deg(\phi)\ \ \Longrightarrow\ \ \nu(f)=\min\nolimits_{0\leq s}\{\mu(a_{s})+s\gamma\}.

Note that
\nu(\phi)=\gamma
,
\mu<\nu
and
\mathbf{t}(\mu,\nu)=\left[\,\phi\,\right]_{\mu}
.

If
\gamma<\infty
, then
\phi
is a key polynomial for
\nu
of minimal degree
[
16
, Cor. 7.3]
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\left(\rho_{i}\right)_{i\in A}\subset\mathcal{T}
be a totally ordered family
not admitting a maximal element. Assume that
\mathcal{A}
is
parametrized by a totally ordered set
A
of indices such that the mapping

A\to\mathcal{A}
determined by
i\mapsto\rho_{i}

is an isomorphism of ordered sets.

If
\deg(\rho_{i})
is stable for all sufficiently large
i\in A
, we say that
\mathcal{A}
has
stable degree
, and we denote this stable degree by
\operatorname{deg}(\mathcal{A})
.

We say that
f\in K[x]
is
\mathcal{A}
-stable
if for some index
i\in A
, it satisfies

\rho_{i}(f)=\rho_{j}(f),\quad\mbox{ for all }\ j>i.

We obtain a
stability function

\rho_{\mathcal{A}}
, defined
on the set of all
\mathcal{A}
-stable polynomials by
\rho_{\mathcal{A}}(f)=\max\{\rho_{i}(f)\mid i\in A\}
.

A
limit key polynomial
for
\mathcal{A}
is a monic
\mathcal{A}
-unstable polynomial of minimal degree. Let
\operatorname{KP}_{\infty}(\mathcal{A})
be the set of all these limit key polynomials. Since the product of stable polynomials is stable, all limit key polynomials are irreducible in
K[x]
.

The
limit degree
of
\mathcal{A}
, denoted
\operatorname{deg}_{\infty}(\mathcal{A})
, is the degree of any limit key polynomial. If
\operatorname{KP}_{\infty}(\mathcal{A})=\emptyset
, we agree that
\operatorname{deg}_{\infty}(\mathcal{A})=\infty
.

Definition.
We say that
\mathcal{A}
is an
essential continuous family
of valuations in
\mathcal{T}
if it has stable degree and
\operatorname{deg}(\mathcal{A})<\operatorname{deg}_{\infty}(\mathcal{A})<\infty
.

Take any limit key polynomial
\phi\in\operatorname{KP}_{\infty}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
, and any
\gamma\in\Lambda\infty
such that
\rho_{i}(\phi)<\gamma
for all
i\in A
.
We define the
limit augmentation

\nu=[\mathcal{A};\,\phi,\gamma]\in\mathcal{T}
as the following mapping, defined in terms of
\phi
-expansions:

f=\sum\nolimits_{0\leq s}a_{s}\phi^{s}\quad\deg(a_{s})<\deg(\phi)\ \ \Longrightarrow\ \ \nu(f)=\min\nolimits_{0\leq s}\{\rho_{\mathcal{A}}(a_{s})+s\gamma\}.

Since
\deg(a_{s})<\operatorname{deg}_{\infty}(\mathcal{A})
, all coefficients
a_{s}
are
\mathcal{A}
-stable.

Note that
\nu(\phi)=\gamma
and
\rho_{i}<\nu
for all
i\in A
.
If
\gamma<\infty
, then
\phi
is a key polynomial for
\nu
of minimal degree
[
16
, Cor. 7.13]
.

Consider a finite chain of valuations in
\mathcal{T}

\mu_{0}\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi_{1},\gamma_{1}}}{{\,\longrightarrow\,}}\ \mu_{1}\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi_{2},\gamma_{2}}}{{\,\longrightarrow\,}}\ \cdots\ \,\longrightarrow\,\ \mu_{r-1}\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi_{r},\gamma_{r}}}{{\,\longrightarrow\,}}\ \mu_{r}=\nu

in which every node is an augmentation of the previous node, of one of the two types:

Ordinary augmentation
:
\mu_{n+1}=[\mu_{n};\,\phi_{n+1},\gamma_{n+1}]
, for some
\phi_{n+1}\in\operatorname{KP}(\mu_{n})
.

Limit augmentation
:
\mu_{n+1}=[\mathcal{A}_{n};\,\phi_{n+1},\gamma_{n+1}]
, for some
\phi_{n+1}\in\operatorname{KP}_{\infty}(\mathcal{A}_{n})
, where
\mathcal{A}_{n}
is an essential continuous family containing
\mu_{n}
as its first valuation.

We always consider an implicit choice of a key polynomial
\phi_{0}\in\operatorname{KP}(\mu_{0})
of minimal degree, and we denote
\gamma_{0}=\mu_{0}(\phi_{0})
.

Therefore, for all
n
such that
\gamma_{n}<\infty
, the polynomial
\phi_{n}
is a key polynomial for
\mu_{n}
of minimal degree, and we have

m_{n}:=\deg(\mu_{n})=\deg(\phi_{n}),\qquad\operatorname{sv}(\mu_{n})=\mu_{n}(\phi_{n})=\gamma_{n}.

Definition.
A chain of mixed augmentations as above is said to be a
Mac Lane–Vaquié (MLV) chain
if
\deg(\mu_{0})=1
and every augmentation step satisfies:

•

If
\,\mu_{n}\to\mu_{n+1}\,
is ordinary, then
\ \deg(\mu_{n})<\deg(\mu_{n+1})
.

•

If
\,\mu_{n}\to\mu_{n+1}\,
is limit, then
\ \deg(\mu_{n})=\deg(\mathcal{A}_{n})
and
\ \phi_{n}\not\in\mathbf{t}(\mu_{n},\mu_{n+1})
.

In an MLV chain, all nodes
\mu_{n}
, with
n<r
, are residue transcendental valuations and satisfy
\phi_{n}\not\in\mathbf{t}(\mu_{n},\mu_{n+1})
. In particular,
\nu(\phi_{n})=\gamma_{n}
for all
n
, by Lemma
1.4
.

Theorem 1.5
.

[
17
, Thm. 4.3]

Let
\nu\in\mathcal{T}
be an inner node or a finite leaf. Then,
\nu
is the end node of a finite MLV chain.

These valuations are “bien-specifiées” in Vaquié’s terminology.

The main advantage of imposing the technical condition of MLV chain is that the nodes of the chain are essentially unique
[
17
, Thm. 4.7]
. Thus, we may read in the chain several data intrinsically associated to the valuation
\nu
.

Definition.
The
depth
of
\nu
is the length of any MLV chain with end node
\nu
. The
limit-depth
of
\nu
is the number of limit augmentations in this MLV chain.

An inner node or a finite leaf
\nu\in\mathcal{T}
is said to be an
inductive
valuation if it has limit-depth equal to zero.

The depth-zero valuations take the form
\nu=\omega_{a,\delta}
, for some
a\in K
and
\delta\in\Lambda\infty
. They act as follows on
(x-a)
-expansions:

\nu\left(\sum\nolimits_{0\leq s}a_{s}(x-a)^{s}\right)=\min\left\{v(a_{s})+s\delta\mid 0\leq s\right\}.

Clearly,
\omega_{a,\infty}
is a finite leave of
\mathcal{T}
with support
(x-a)K[x]
, while for
\delta<\infty
the valuation
\omega_{a,\delta}
is an inner node admitting
x-a
as a key polynomial.
The valuation
\omega_{a,\delta}
is commensurable if and only if
\delta\in\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}}\infty
. We clearly have

(1)

\omega_{a,\delta}\leq\omega_{b,\epsilon}\ \ \Longleftrightarrow\ \ \delta\leq\min\{v(b-a),\epsilon\}.

1.4.
Parametrization of equivalence classes of extensions of
v
to
K[x]

Let
\Gamma\hookrightarrow\Lambda
be an extension of ordered abelian groups, and let
\Delta\subset\Lambda
be the relative divisible hull of
\Gamma
in
\Lambda
. We say that the extension
\Gamma\hookrightarrow\Lambda
is
small
if
\Lambda/\Delta
is a cyclic group.
For instance, for every valuation
\mu
extending
v
to
K[x]
, the extension
\Gamma\hookrightarrow\Gamma_{\mu}
is small
[
10
, Theorem 1.5]
.

In
[
11
]
, a totally ordered real vector space
\mathbb{R}_{\mbox{\tiny$\operatorname{sme}$}}
was constructed, which contains all small extensions of
\Gamma
up to
\Gamma
-equivalence. This object is canonical, depending only on the set of nonzero principal convex subgroups of
\Gamma
. However, the order-preserving embeddings
\Gamma\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\mbox{\tiny$\operatorname{sme}$}}
are non-canonical, because they are obtained as an application of Hahn’s embedding theorem
[
11
, Section 4]
.

From now on, we fix any such embedding
\Gamma\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\mbox{\tiny$\operatorname{sme}$}}
and we identify
\Gamma
with its image in
\mathbb{R}_{\mbox{\tiny$\operatorname{sme}$}}
. By the universal property of
\mathbb{R}_{\mbox{\tiny$\operatorname{sme}$}}
, for any small extension
\Gamma\hookrightarrow\Lambda
, there exists an embedding
\Lambda\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\mbox{\tiny$\operatorname{sme}$}}
fitting into a commutative diagram

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\Lambda&&\\
\uparrow&\searrow&\\
\Gamma&\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow&\mathbb{R}_{\mbox{\tiny$\operatorname{sme}$}}.\end{array}

In particular, every extension of
v
to
K[x]
is equivalent to some extension of
v
taking values in
\mathbb{R}_{\mbox{\tiny$\operatorname{sme}$}}
.

Consider two elements
x,y\in\mathbb{R}_{\mbox{\tiny$\operatorname{sme}$}}
to be equivalent if there exists an isomorphism of ordered groups between the subgroups
\big\langle\,{\Gamma,x}\,\big\rangle
and
\big\langle\,{\Gamma,y}\,\big\rangle
, which maps
x
to
y
and acts as the identity on
\Gamma
. There is a canonical set of representatives
\Gamma_{\operatorname{sme}}\subset\mathbb{R}_{\mbox{\tiny$\operatorname{sme}$}}
of this equivalence relation. We have

\Gamma\subset\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}}\subset\Gamma_{\operatorname{sme}}\subset\mathbb{R}_{\mbox{\tiny$\operatorname{sme}$}}
.

For each
x\in\Gamma_{\operatorname{sme}}
let us denote

\left(\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}}\right)_{\leq x}=\left\{\gamma\in\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}}\mid\gamma\leq x\right\},\qquad\left(\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}}\right)_{\geq x}=\left\{\gamma\in\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}}\mid\gamma\geq x\right\}

The set
\Gamma_{\operatorname{sme}}
satisfies the following property, easily deduced from
[
11
, Lemma 5.4]
.

Proposition 1.6
.

The mapping
x\mapsto\left(\left(\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}}\right)_{\leq x},\left(\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}}\right)_{\geq x}\right)
establishes an order-preserving isomorphism between
\Gamma_{\operatorname{sme}}
and the set of all quasicuts in
\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}}
.

A
quasicut
in
\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}}
is a pair of subsets
D=(D^{L},D^{R})
such that
D^{L}\leq D^{R}
(every element in
D^{L}
is less than or equal to every element in
D^{R}
),
D^{L}\cup D^{R}=\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}}
and
D^{L}\cap D^{R}
contains at most one element. The set of quasicuts admits a total ordering:

\left(D^{L},D^{R}\right)\leq\left(E^{L},E^{R}\right)\ \ \Longleftrightarrow\ \ D^{L}\subset E^{L}\quad\mbox{and}\quad D^{R}\supset E^{R}.

The following result follows immediately from Proposition
1.6
.

Corollary 1.7
.

Every subset
S\subset\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}}
admits a supremum in
\Gamma_{\operatorname{sme}}
, which we simply denote by
\,\sup(S)
. If
S
does not contain a maximal element, then
\sup(S)\not\in\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}}
.

Indeed, if
S
contains a maximal element
\gamma
, then
\sup(S)=\gamma\in\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}}
. Otherwise,
\sup(S)
is the cut
(I,\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}}\setminus I)
, where
I
is the initial segment of
\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}}
generated by
S
.
Since
I
contains no maximal element, necessarily
I\neq\left(\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}}\right)_{\leq\gamma}
for all
\gamma\in\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}}
.

Denote
\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{Q}}=\mathcal{T}(\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}})
. Consider the intermediate tree
\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{Q}}\subset\mathcal{T}_{\mbox{\tiny$\operatorname{sme}$}}\subset\mathcal{T}(\mathbb{R}_{\mbox{\tiny$\operatorname{sme}$}})
, defined as follows

\mathcal{T}_{\mbox{\tiny$\operatorname{sme}$}}=\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{Q}}\cup\left\{\rho\in\mathcal{T}(\mathbb{R}_{\mbox{\tiny$\operatorname{sme}$}})\mid\rho\mbox{ inner node with }\operatorname{sv}(\rho)\in\Gamma_{\operatorname{sme}}\right\}.

Note that
\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{Q}}
and
\mathcal{T}_{\mbox{\tiny$\operatorname{sme}$}}
have the same finite leaves.

The nodes of
\mathcal{T}_{\mbox{\tiny$\operatorname{sme}$}}
parametrize the equivalence classes of valuations on
K[x]
whose restriction to
K
is equivalent to
v

[
2
, Thm. 7.1]
. We need to recall some particular properties of the tree
\mathcal{T}_{\mbox{\tiny$\operatorname{sme}$}}
which will be useful in the sequel.

1.4.1.
Inner depth-zero nodes

The inner depth-zero nodes in
\mathcal{T}_{\mbox{\tiny$\operatorname{sme}$}}
are of the form
\omega_{a,\gamma}
for
a\in K
and
\gamma\in\Gamma_{\operatorname{sme}}
.

Let
-\infty=\min(\Gamma_{\operatorname{sme}})
be the absolute minimal element in
\Gamma_{\operatorname{sme}}
, corresponding to the quasicut
(\emptyset,\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}})
.
By (
1
), we have

(2)

\omega_{a,-\infty}=\omega_{b,-\infty}\leq\omega_{c,\gamma}\quad\mbox{ for all }\ a,b,c\in K,\ \gamma\in\Gamma_{\operatorname{sme}}.

Definition.
Let us denote by
\omega_{-\infty}=\omega_{a,-\infty}
this minimal depth-zero valuation, which is independent of
a\in K
.
By Theorem
1.5
and (
2
),
\omega_{-\infty}
is an absolute minimal node of
\mathcal{T}_{\mbox{\tiny$\operatorname{sme}$}}
. We say that
\omega_{-\infty}
is the
root node
of
\mathcal{T}_{\mbox{\tiny$\operatorname{sme}$}}
.

Since
\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{Q}}
has no minimal node, the root node
\omega_{-\infty}
must be incommensurable. Hence, it has a unique tangent direction; actually,

\operatorname{KP}(\omega_{-\infty})=\{x-a\mid a\in K\}=[x]_{\omega_{-\infty}}.

All inner depth-zero nodes in
\mathcal{T}_{\mbox{\tiny$\operatorname{sme}$}}
are obtained as a single ordinary augmentation of the root node:

\omega_{a,\gamma}=[\omega_{-\infty};\,x-a,\gamma]\quad\mbox{ for all }a\in K,\ \gamma\in\Gamma_{\operatorname{sme}},\ \gamma>-\infty.

1.4.2.
Limit augmentations

Let
\mathcal{A}=(\rho_{i})_{i\in A}
be an essential continuous family in
\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{Q}}
, and let
\phi\in\operatorname{KP}_{\infty}(\mathcal{A})
be a limit key polynomial. By Corollary
1.7
, there exists a minimal limit augmentation of
\mathcal{A}
in
\mathcal{T}_{\mbox{\tiny$\operatorname{sme}$}}
with respect to
\phi
; namely

\mu_{\mathcal{A}}:=[\mathcal{A};\,\phi,\gamma_{\mathcal{A}}],\qquad\gamma_{\mathcal{A}}:=\sup\left\{\rho_{i}(\phi)\mid i\in A\right\}\in\Gamma_{\operatorname{sme}}.

Since
\mathcal{A}
has no maximal element, the family
\{\rho_{i}(\phi)\mid i\in A\}
has no maximal element either. By Corollary
1.7
,
\gamma_{\mathcal{A}}
does not belong to
\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}}
.

Lemma 1.8
.

[
2
, Lem. 7.2]

The value
\gamma_{\mathcal{A}}\in\Gamma_{\operatorname{sme}}\setminus\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}}
and the valuation
\mu_{\mathcal{A}}\in\mathcal{T}_{\mbox{\tiny$\operatorname{sme}$}}
are independent of the choice of the limit key polynomial
\phi
in
\operatorname{KP}_{\infty}(\mathcal{A})
.

Since
\mu_{\mathcal{A}}
is incommensurable, it has a unique tangent direction. Actually,

\operatorname{KP}(\mu_{\mathcal{A}})=[\phi]_{\mu_{\mathcal{A}}}=\left\{\phi+a\mid a\in K[x],\ \deg(a)<\deg(\phi),\ \rho_{\mathcal{A}}(a)>\gamma_{\mathcal{A}}\right\}=\operatorname{KP}_{\infty}(\mathcal{A}).

Also, all limit augmentations
[\mathcal{A};\,\phi,\gamma]
are ordinary augmentations of
\mu_{\mathcal{A}}
:

[\mathcal{A};\,\phi,\gamma]=[\mu_{\mathcal{A}};\,\phi,\gamma]\quad\mbox{ for all }\gamma\in\Gamma_{\operatorname{sme}},\ \gamma>\gamma_{\mathcal{A}}.

1.4.3.
Primitive nodes of
\mathcal{T}_{\mbox{\tiny$\operatorname{sme}$}}

For the ease of the reader we shall consider the depth-zero valuations as a special case of limit augmentations.

Convention.
We admit the empty set
\mathcal{A}=\emptyset
as an essential continuous family in
\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{Q}}
. We agree that this family has

\gamma_{\mathcal{A}}=-\infty,\quad\mu_{\mathcal{A}}=\omega_{-\infty},\quad\operatorname{KP}_{\infty}(\mathcal{A})=\operatorname{KP}(\mu_{\mathcal{A}})=\left\{x-a\mid a\in K\right\}.

Consider the subset
\operatorname{KP}_{\operatorname{str}}(\mu)\subset\operatorname{KP}(\mu)
of
strong
key polynomials, defined as

\operatorname{KP}_{\operatorname{str}}(\mu)=\left\{\phi\in\operatorname{KP}(\mu)\mid\deg(\phi)>\deg(\mu)\right\}.

If
\operatorname{KP}_{\operatorname{str}}(\mu)\neq\emptyset
, then
\mu
admits more than one tangent direction; thus, it is necessarily a residue transcendental valuation. In particular,
\operatorname{sv}(\mu)\in\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}}
and
\mu\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{Q}}
.

Definition.
A
limit-primitive
node in
\mathcal{T}_{\mbox{\tiny$\operatorname{sme}$}}
is the inner limit node
\mu_{\mathcal{A}}
associated to an essential continuous family
\mathcal{A}
in
\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{Q}}
.

An
ordinary-primitive
node in
\mathcal{T}_{\mbox{\tiny$\operatorname{sme}$}}
is an inner node
\mu\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{Q}}
such that
\operatorname{KP}_{\operatorname{str}}(\mu)\neq\emptyset
.

A
primitive
node in
\mathcal{T}_{\mbox{\tiny$\operatorname{sme}$}}
is a node which is either limit-primitive or ordinary-primitive. Let us denote by
\operatorname{Prim}\left(\mathcal{T}_{\mbox{\tiny$\operatorname{sme}$}}\right)
the set of all primitive nodes.

For any inner node
\mu\in\mathcal{T}_{\mbox{\tiny$\operatorname{sme}$}}
and any
\phi\in\operatorname{KP}(\mu)
, consider the set of all ordinary augmentations of
\mu
with respect to
\phi
:

\mathcal{P}_{\mu}(\phi)=\left\{[\mu;\,\phi,\gamma]\mid\gamma\in\Gamma_{\operatorname{sme}}\infty,\ \ \mu(\phi)<\gamma\leq\infty\right\}\subset\mathcal{T}_{\mbox{\tiny$\operatorname{sme}$}}.

Definition.
Let
\rho\in\mathcal{T}_{\mbox{\tiny$\operatorname{sme}$}}
be a primitive node. Then, we define

\mathcal{P}(\rho)=\begin{cases}\bigcup_{\phi\in\operatorname{KP}_{\operatorname{str}}(\rho)}\mathcal{P}_{\rho}(\phi),&\mbox{ if $\rho$ is ordinary-primitive},\\
\{\rho\}\cup\bigcup_{\phi\in\operatorname{KP}(\rho)}\mathcal{P}_{\rho}(\phi),&\mbox{ if $\rho$ is limit-primitive}.\end{cases}

Theorem 1.9
.

[
2
, Thm. 7.3]

Let
\nu\in\mathcal{T}_{\mbox{\tiny$\operatorname{sme}$}}
be either an inner node, or a finite leaf. There exists a unique primitive node
\rho\in\operatorname{Prim}(\mathcal{T}_{\mbox{\tiny$\operatorname{sme}$}})
such that
\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\rho)
.

2.
Okutsu equivalence of finite leaves

Let
(K,v)
be a valued field. Consider an algebraic closure of
K
and the corresponding separable closure:
K\subset K^{\operatorname{sep}}\subset\overline{K}
.
Let
\overline{v}
be an extension of
v
to
\overline{K}
, and let
(K^{h},\overline{v})
be the henselization of
(K,v)
determined by
\overline{v}
. Thus,
K^{h}\subset K^{\operatorname{sep}}
is the fixed field of the decomposition group of
\overline{v}
in the Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}(K^{\operatorname{sep}}/K)
.

In this section, we introduce a certain
Okutsu equivalence

\sim_{\operatorname{ok}}
on the set
\mathcal{L}_{\operatorname{fin}}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{Q}})
of finite leaves of
\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{Q}}
and we obtain a natural parametrization of the quotient set
\mathcal{L}_{\operatorname{fin}}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{Q}})/\!\sim_{\operatorname{ok}}
by a certain subset of the tangent space of
\mathcal{T}_{\mbox{\tiny$\operatorname{sme}$}}
.
Also, we use the tree structure of
\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{Q}}
to define an ultrametric topology on the set
\mathcal{L}_{\operatorname{fin}}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{Q}})
.

For any
\phi\in\operatorname{Irr}(K)
, we denote by
K_{\phi}
the simple field extension
K[x]/\phi K[x]
.

2.1.
Finite leaves of
\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{Q}}

Any
F\in\operatorname{Irr}(K^{h})
determines a valuation
v_{F}
on
K^{h}[x]
defined as:

v_{F}(f)=v\left(f(\theta)\right)\ \mbox{ for all }\ f\in K^{h}[x],

where
\theta\in\overline{K}
is a root of
F
. By the henselian property, the value
v\left(f(\theta)\right)
does not depend on the choice of
\theta
. The support of
v_{F}
is the prime ideal
FK^{h}[x]
.

Let us denote by
w_{F}
the restriction of
v_{F}
to
K[x]
. The support of
w_{F}
is the prime ideal
\phi K[x]
, where the “norm” polynomial
\phi=N(F)\in\operatorname{Irr}(K)
is uniquely determined by the equality
\left(FK^{h}[x]\right)\cap K[x]=\phi K[x]
. Let
\overline{w}_{F}
be the valuation on
K_{\phi}
naturally induced by
w_{F}
.

As a consequence of the results in
[
4
, Sec. 17]
, the following mapping is bijective:

(3)

\operatorname{Irr}(K^{h})\,\longrightarrow\,\mathcal{L}_{\operatorname{fin}}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{Q}}),\qquad F\longmapsto w_{F}.

Let us now recall how to describe the extensions of
v
to the field
K_{\phi}
, for an arbitrary
\phi\in\operatorname{Irr}(K)
.
Since
K^{h}/K
is a separable extension, the factorization of
\phi
into a product of monic irreducible polynomials in
K^{h}[x]
takes the form

\phi=F_{1}\cdots F_{r},

with pairwise different
F_{1},\dots,F_{r}\in\operatorname{Irr}(K^{h})
, whose norm is
N(F_{i})=\phi
for all
i
.

Theorem 2.1
.

[
18
, Cor. 3.2]

The extensions of
v
to
K_{\phi}
are precisely
\overline{w}_{F_{1}},\dots,\overline{w}_{F_{r}}
.

2.2.
Primitive tangent space of
\mathcal{T}_{\mbox{\tiny$\operatorname{sme}$}}
and finite leaves

By Theorem
1.9
, each finite leaf
\mathfrak{f}\in\mathcal{L}_{\operatorname{fin}}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{Q}})
belongs to
\mathcal{P}(\rho)
for a unique primitive node
\rho\in\mathcal{T}_{\mbox{\tiny$\operatorname{sme}$}}
.
We say that
\rho
is the
previous primitive node
of
\mathfrak{f}
, and we denote it by
\rho=\rho(\mathfrak{f})\in\mathcal{T}_{\mbox{\tiny$\operatorname{sme}$}}
.

Since
\mathfrak{f}
is commensurable and has nontrivial support, we have
\rho(\mathfrak{f})\neq\mathfrak{f}
. Indeed, the ordinary-primitive nodes have trivial support, while the limit-primitive nodes are incommensurable.
Consider a finite MLV chain whose end node is
\mathfrak{f}
:

\mu_{0}\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi_{1},\gamma_{1}}}{{\,\longrightarrow\,}}\ \mu_{1}\ \,\longrightarrow\,\ \cdots\ \,\longrightarrow\,\ \mu_{r-1}\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi_{r},\infty}}{{\,\longrightarrow\,}}\ \mu_{r}=\mathfrak{f}.

If
\mathfrak{f}
has depth zero, then
\mathfrak{f}=\mu_{0}=\omega_{a,\infty}
for some
a\in K
. Then,

\rho(\mathfrak{f})=\omega_{-\infty},\qquad\deg(\mathfrak{f})=\deg(\rho(\mathfrak{f}))=1.

Suppose that
\mathfrak{f}
has a positive depth and the last augmentation
\mu_{r-1}\to\mathfrak{f}
is ordinary; that is,
\mathfrak{f}=[\mu_{r-1};\,\phi_{r},\infty]
.
By the definition of a MLV chain,
\phi_{r}\in\operatorname{KP}(\mu_{r-1})
and
\deg(\mathfrak{f})=\deg(\phi_{r})>\deg(\mu_{r-1})
; thus,
\mu_{r-1}
is a ordinary-primitive node and

(4)

\rho(\mathfrak{f})=\mu_{r-1},\qquad\deg(\mathfrak{f})=\deg(\phi_{r})>\deg(\rho(\mathfrak{f})).

Suppose that
\mathfrak{f}
has a positive depth and
\mu_{r-1}\to\mathfrak{f}
is a limit augmentation; that is,
\mathfrak{f}=[\mathcal{A};\,\phi_{r},\infty]
for some essential continuous family
\mathcal{A}
in
\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{Q}}
whose first valuation is
\mu_{r-1}
. Then,

\rho(\mathfrak{f})=\mu_{\mathcal{A}},\qquad\deg(\mathfrak{f})=\deg(\phi_{r})=\deg(\rho(\mathfrak{f})).

Definition.
On the set
\mathcal{L}_{\operatorname{fin}}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{Q}})
, we define the equivalence relation

\mathfrak{f}\sim_{\operatorname{ok}}\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}\ \ \Longleftrightarrow\ \ \rho(\mathfrak{f})=\rho(\mathfrak{f}^{\prime})\ \mbox{ and }\ \mathbf{t}(\rho(\mathfrak{f}),\mathfrak{f})=\mathbf{t}(\rho(\mathfrak{f}),\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}).

In this case, we say that the finite leaves
\mathfrak{f}
and
\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}
are
Okutsu equivalent
.

We denote by
\left[\,\mathfrak{f}\,\right]_{\operatorname{ok}}
the Okutsu equivalence class of
\mathfrak{f}
.

Definition.
Let
\mathbb{T}^{\operatorname{prim}}
be the subset of the tangent space of
\mathcal{T}_{\mbox{\tiny$\operatorname{sme}$}}
consisting of all tangent vectors based on primitive nodes:

\mathbb{T}^{\operatorname{prim}}=\left\{(\rho,t)\mid\rho\in\operatorname{Prim}(\mathcal{T}_{\mbox{\tiny$\operatorname{sme}$}}),\ t\in\mbox{\rm\bf td}(\rho)\right\}.

The next result follows immediately from Theorem
1.9
and the definition of
\sim_{\operatorname{ok}}
.

Theorem 2.2
.

There is a canonical bijective mapping

\mathbb{T}^{\operatorname{prim}}\,\longrightarrow\,\mathcal{L}_{\operatorname{fin}}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{Q}})/\!\sim_{\operatorname{ok}},\qquad\left(\rho,[\phi]_{\rho}\right)\longmapsto\left[\,[\rho;\,\phi,\infty]\,\right]_{\operatorname{ok}},

whose inverse mapping is:
\left[\,\mathfrak{f}\,\right]_{\operatorname{ok}}\mapsto\left(\rho(\mathfrak{f}),\mathbf{t}(\rho(\mathfrak{f}),\mathfrak{f})\right)
.

Combined with the identification
\mathcal{L}_{\operatorname{fin}}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{Q}})=\operatorname{Irr}(K)
given by (
3
), this result generalizes
[
15
, Thm. 5.14]
to the most general case, where no assumption at all is made on the base valued field
(K,v)
.

Let us quote some basic properties of the equivalence relation
\sim_{\operatorname{ok}}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{f}
,
\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}
be two finite leaves in
\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{Q}}
, and let
\operatorname{supp}(\mathfrak{f})=\phi K[x]
.

(i)

The polynomial
\phi
belongs to
\operatorname{KP}(\rho(\mathfrak{f}))
and
\mathbf{t}(\rho(\mathfrak{f}),\mathfrak{f})=[\phi]_{\rho(\mathfrak{f})}
.

(ii)

Let
\rho
be a primitive node. Then,
\rho=\rho(\mathfrak{f})
if and only if
\rho<\mathfrak{f}
,
\phi\in\operatorname{KP}(\rho)
and

(5)

\deg(\phi)>\deg(\rho),\quad\mbox{if $\rho$ is ordinary-primitive}.

(iii)

If
\rho(\mathfrak{f})
is a limit-primitive node, then
\mathfrak{f}\sim_{\operatorname{ok}}\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}
if and only if
\rho(\mathfrak{f})=\rho(\mathfrak{f}^{\prime})
.

(iv)

If
\mathfrak{f}\sim_{\operatorname{ok}}\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}
, then
\deg(\mathfrak{f})=\deg(\mathfrak{f}^{\prime})
.

Proof.

By the definition of
\rho(\mathfrak{f})
, the valuation
\mathfrak{f}=[\rho(\mathfrak{f});\,\varphi,\gamma]
is an ordinary augmentation of
\rho(\mathfrak{f})
, for some
\varphi\in\operatorname{KP}(\rho(\mathfrak{f}))
,
\gamma\in\Gamma_{\operatorname{sme}}\infty
. Since
\mathfrak{f}
has nontrivial support, we have necessarily
\gamma=\infty
. Then,
\varphi\in K[x]
is a monic irreducible polynomial such that
\operatorname{supp}(\mathfrak{f})=\varphi K[x]
. This implies
\varphi=\phi
, and this proves (i).

Let
\rho
be a primitive node. If
\rho=\rho(\mathfrak{f})
, then
\rho<\mathfrak{f}
and
\phi\in\operatorname{KP}(\rho)
by (i). Also, (
5
) follows from (
4
).

Conversely, suppose that
\rho<\mathfrak{f}
and
\phi\in\operatorname{KP}(\rho)
. Since
\rho(\phi)<\infty=\mathfrak{f}(\phi)
, Lemma
1.4
shows that
\mathbf{t}(\rho,\mathfrak{f})=[\varphi]_{\rho}
for some
\varphi\in\operatorname{KP}(\rho)
such that
\varphi\mid_{\rho}\phi
. By
[
16
, Prop. 6.6]
, we have
\varphi\sim_{\rho}\phi
, so that
\mathbf{t}(\rho,\mathfrak{f})=[\phi]_{\rho}
.
This implies,
[\rho;\,\phi,\infty]=\mathfrak{f}
because both valuations coincide on
\phi
-expansions, again by Lemma
1.4
.

If
\rho
is limit-primitive, or
\rho
is ordinary-primitive and (
5
) holds, then
\mathfrak{f}
belongs to
\mathcal{P}(\rho)
and this implies
\rho=\rho(\mathfrak{f})
by Theorem
1.9
. This ends the proof of (ii).

If
\rho(\mathfrak{f})
is a limit-primitive node, then it has a unique tangent direction; thus, for all
\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}
such that
\rho(\mathfrak{f})<\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}
we have necessarily
\mathbf{t}(\rho(\mathfrak{f}),\mathfrak{f})=\mathbf{t}(\rho(\mathfrak{f}),\mathfrak{f}^{\prime})
. This proves (iii).

Finally, if
\mathfrak{f}\sim_{\operatorname{ok}}\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}
, then (iv) follows from (i), because

\deg(\mathfrak{f})=\deg\,\mathbf{t}(\rho(\mathfrak{f}),\mathfrak{f})=\deg\,\mathbf{t}(\rho(\mathfrak{f}),\mathfrak{f}^{\prime})=\deg(\mathfrak{f}^{\prime})
. ∎

2.3.
Ultrametric topology on the set of finite leaves

Let us introduce an ultrametric topology on the set
\mathcal{L}_{\operatorname{fin}}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{Q}})
.

Definition.
For all
\mathfrak{f},\mathfrak{g}\in\mathcal{L}_{\operatorname{fin}}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{Q}})
, we define

u(\mathfrak{f},\mathfrak{g})=\begin{cases}\operatorname{wt}\left(\mathfrak{f}\wedge\mathfrak{g}\right),&\mbox{ if }\mathfrak{f}\neq\mathfrak{g},\\
\infty,&\mbox{ if }\mathfrak{f}=\mathfrak{g},\end{cases}

where
\mathfrak{f}\wedge\mathfrak{g}
is the greatest common lower node in the tree
\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{Q}}

[
2
, Sec. 5.6]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

The function
u\colon\mathcal{L}_{\operatorname{fin}}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{Q}})\times\mathcal{L}_{\operatorname{fin}}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{Q}})\to\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}}\infty
has the following properties:

(i)

u(\mathfrak{f},\mathfrak{g})=\infty\ \ \Longleftrightarrow\ \ \mathfrak{f}=\mathfrak{g}
.

(ii)

u(\mathfrak{f},\mathfrak{g})\geq\min\{u(\mathfrak{f},\mathfrak{h}),u(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g})\}\quad\mbox{for all}\quad\mathfrak{h}\in\mathcal{L}_{\operatorname{fin}}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{Q}})
.

(iii)

u(\mathfrak{f},\mathfrak{g})=u(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{f})
.

Proof.

Conditions (i) and (iii) follow immediately from the definition of
u
.

Let us prove (ii). If there is any coincidence between the three leaves
\mathfrak{f},\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h}
, then the statement of (ii) is obvious. Let us suppose that these leaves are pairwise different.

Let
\rho=\mathfrak{h}\wedge\mathfrak{f}
,
\mu=\mathfrak{h}\wedge\mathfrak{g}
. Since
u(\mathfrak{f},\mathfrak{g})=u(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{f})
, we may assume that
\rho\leq\mu
. The relative position of all these nodes in the tree
\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{Q}}
is the following:

We have
u(\mathfrak{f},\mathfrak{g})=u(\mathfrak{f},\mathfrak{h})=\operatorname{wt}(\rho)
and
u(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})=\operatorname{wt}(\mu)
. On the other hand, Lemma
1.4
shows that
\operatorname{wt}(\rho)<\operatorname{wt}(\mu)
, if
\rho<\mu
. Thus, condition (ii) holds.
∎

If
u(\mathfrak{f},\mathfrak{h})\neq u(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g})
, then
\rho<\mu
and
u(\mathfrak{f},\mathfrak{g})=\operatorname{wt}(\rho)=\min\{u(\mathfrak{f},\mathfrak{h}),u(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g})\}
. The inequality
u(\mathfrak{f},\mathfrak{g})>\min\{u(\mathfrak{f},\mathfrak{h}),u(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g})\}
holds in the following situation:

As a consequence, the function
u
provides an structure of
ultrametric space
on the set
\mathcal{L}_{\operatorname{fin}}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{Q}})
, and a corresponding topology
[
9
, Sec. 1.3]
. A basis for the topology is formed by the balls

B_{\gamma}(\mathfrak{f}):=\left\{\mathfrak{g}\in\mathcal{L}_{\operatorname{fin}}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{Q}})\mid u(\mathfrak{f},\mathfrak{g})>\gamma\right\},\quad\mathfrak{f}\in\mathcal{L}_{\operatorname{fin}}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{Q}}),\quad\gamma\in\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}}.

In this topology, two finite leaves
\mathfrak{f},\mathfrak{g}
are “close” if the value
u(\mathfrak{f},\mathfrak{g})
is “large”.

Finally, let us remark that two finite leaves in
\mathcal{L}_{\operatorname{fin}}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{Q}})
are Okutsu equivalent if and only if they are “close enough” with respect to the ultrametric topology.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\mathfrak{f},\mathfrak{g}\in\mathcal{L}_{\operatorname{fin}}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{Q}})
. Then, the following conditions are equivalent.

(i)

\mathfrak{f}\sim_{\operatorname{ok}}\mathfrak{g}
,

(ii)

\rho(\mathfrak{f}),\rho(\mathfrak{g})<\mathfrak{f}\wedge\mathfrak{g}
,

(iii)

u(\mathfrak{f},\mathfrak{g})>\max\{\operatorname{wt}(\rho(\mathfrak{f})),\operatorname{wt}(\rho(\mathfrak{g}))\}
.

Proof.

The equivalence between (i) and (ii) follows from Theorem
1.9
. The equivalence between (ii) and (iii) follows from Lemma
1.4
.
∎

3.
Okutsu equivalence in the henselian case

In this section, we suppose that
(K,v)
is henselian. We fix an algebraic closure
\overline{K}
of
K
and we still denote by
v
the unique extension of
v
to
\overline{K}
.

Recall the canonical bijection between
\operatorname{Irr}(K)
and
\mathcal{L}_{\operatorname{fin}}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{Q}})
given in (
3
):

(6)

\operatorname{Irr}(K)\,\longrightarrow\,\mathcal{L}_{\operatorname{fin}}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{Q}}),\qquad F\longmapsto v_{F}.

Our first aim is to show that, under the identification
\mathcal{L}_{\operatorname{fin}}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{Q}})=\operatorname{Irr}(K)
, the ultrametric topology on the set
\mathcal{L}_{\operatorname{fin}}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{Q}})
introduced in Section
2
coincides with the classical topology induced by
v
on
\operatorname{Irr}(K)
.

The following result was proved in
[
15
]
for defectless polynomials and extended to arbitrary irreducible polynimials in
[
18
, Thm. 4.4]
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
F\in\operatorname{Irr}(K)
and
\phi\in\operatorname{KP}(\mu)
for some valuation
\mu
on
K[x]
. Then,

\phi\mid_{\mu}F\ \ \Longleftrightarrow\ \ \mu<v_{F}\ \mbox{ and }\ \,\mathbf{t}(\mu,v_{F})=\left[\,\phi\,\right]_{\mu}.

In this case,

F\sim_{\mu}\phi^{\ell}
with
\ell=\deg(F)/\deg(\phi)
.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
F\in\operatorname{Irr}(K)
. Then,
F
is
\mu
-minimal for all valuations
\mu<v_{F}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{t}(\mu,v_{F})=[\phi]_{\mu}
. Since
\mu(F)<\infty=v_{F}(F)
, Lemma
1.4
shows that
\phi\mid_{\mu}F
.
By Theorem
3.1
,
F\sim_{\mu}\phi^{\ell}
with
\ell=\deg(F)/\deg(\phi)
. Since
\phi
is
\mu
-minimal, Theorem
1.2
shows that

\dfrac{\mu(F)}{\deg(F)}=\dfrac{\mu(\phi)}{\deg(\phi)}=\operatorname{wt}(\mu).

Hence,
F
is
\mu
-minimal, again by Theorem
1.2
.
∎

As another consequence of Theorem
3.1
, the ultrametric distance
u
on
\operatorname{Irr}(K)=\mathcal{L}_{\operatorname{fin}}(\mathcal{T}_{\mbox{\tiny$\operatorname{sme}$}})
is given by a classical formula.

Corollary 3.3
.

For all
F,G\in\operatorname{Irr}(K)
we have

u(F,G):=u(v_{F},v_{G})=v_{F}(G)/\deg(G)=v\left(\operatorname{Res}(F,G)\right),

where
\operatorname{Res}(F,G)
is the resultant of the two polynomials.

Proof.

If
F=G
, we have
v_{F}(F)=\infty=u(F,F)
. If
F\neq G
, then
\mu=v_{F}\wedge v_{G}
is an inner node of
\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{Q}}
satisfying

\mu<v_{F}
,
\mu<v_{G}
and
\mathbf{t}(\mu,v_{F})\neq\mathbf{t}(\mu,v_{G})
.

Let
\mathbf{t}(\mu,v_{F})=[\phi]_{\mu}
,
\mathbf{t}(\mu,v_{G})=[\varphi]_{\mu}
, so that
\phi\not\sim_{\mu}\varphi
. By Theorem
3.1
,

G\sim_{\mu}\varphi^{\ell},\quad\ell=\deg(G)/\deg(\varphi).

Hence,
\phi\nmid_{\mu}G
and this implies
\mu(G)=v_{F}(G)
by Lemma
1.4
. On the other hand,
G
is
\mu
-minimal by Corollary
3.2
. By Theorem
1.2
,

\dfrac{v_{F}(G)}{\deg(G)}=\dfrac{\mu(G)}{\deg(G)}=\operatorname{wt}(\mu)=u(F,G).

The equality
v_{F}(G)/\deg(G)=v\left(\operatorname{Res}(F,G)\right)
is well-known.∎

In particular, the ultrametric topology on
\operatorname{Irr}(K)
coincides with the classical topology induced by the valuation
v
.

Definition.
We denote by
\rho_{F}:=\rho(\mathfrak{f})
the previous primitive node of the leaf
\,\mathfrak{f}=v_{F}
.

Corollary 3.4
.

If
F,\,G\in\operatorname{Irr}(K)
have the same degree, then the following conditions are equivalent.

(a)

v_{F}\sim_{\operatorname{ok}}v_{G}
.

(b)

u(F,G)>\max\{\operatorname{wt}(\rho_{F}),\operatorname{wt}(\rho_{G})\}
.

(c)

F\sim_{\rho_{F}}G
.

Proof.

Lemma
2.5
shows that conditions (a) and (b) are equivalent.

Since
\deg(F)=\deg(G)
, we have
\deg(F-G)<\deg(F)
, so that

\rho_{F}(F-G)=v_{F}(F-G)=v_{F}(G).

Thus, condition (c) is equivalent to
v_{F}(G)>\rho_{F}(F)=\deg(F)\operatorname{wt}(\rho_{F})
, which is equivalent to (b) by Corollary
3.3
and the symmetry of the argument.
∎

An obvious comparison of Corollary
3.4
with
[
15
, Lem. 5.13]
shows that the restriction of the equivalence relation
\sim_{\operatorname{ok}}
to the subset
\operatorname{Dless}(K)\subset\operatorname{Irr}(K)
of defectless polyomials, coincides with the Okutsu equivalence defined in
[
15
]
.

Definition.
An
F\in\operatorname{Irr}(K)
is said to be
defectless
if

\deg(F)=e(\overline{v}_{F}/v)f(\overline{v}_{F}/v)
,
where
\overline{v}_{F}
is the valuation on
K_{F}
induced by
v_{F}
.

Vaquié characterized this property as follows
[
24
]
,
[
17
, Cor. 6.1]
.

Theorem 3.5
.

An
F\in\operatorname{Irr}(K)
is defectless if and only if
v_{F}
is inductive.

Since
v_{F}
is an ordinary augmentation of its previous primitive node
\rho_{F}
, we see that
F
is defectless if and only if
\rho_{F}
is inductive.

Therefore, after identifying
\operatorname{Irr}(K)=\mathcal{L}_{\operatorname{fin}}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{Q}})
through (
6
), Theorem
2.2
yields a bijection between
\operatorname{Dless}(K)/\!\sim_{\operatorname{ok}}
and the following subset of
\mathbb{T}^{\operatorname{prim}}
:

\mathbb{T}^{\operatorname{ind}}:=\left\{(\rho,t)\in\mathbb{T}^{\operatorname{prim}}\mid\rho\ \mbox{ is inductive}\right\}.

This subset
\mathbb{T}^{\operatorname{ind}}
may be easily identified with the Mac Lane space
\mathbb{M}
of
[
15
]
. Therefore, even in the henselian case, Theorem
2.2
extends
[
15
, Thm. 5.14]
to a parametrization of Okutsu equivalence classes of arbitrary irreducible polynomials by a certain subset of the tangent space of
\mathcal{T}_{\mbox{\tiny$\operatorname{sme}$}}
.

4.
Okutsu frames over henselian fields

We keep assuming that our valued field
(K,v)
is henselian. Let us fix some
F\in\operatorname{Irr}(K)
of degree
n>1
. We define the
weight
of a non-constant
g\in K[x]
as

\operatorname{wt}(g):=v_{F}(g)/\deg(g)\in\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}}.

4.1.
Definition of Okutsu frames

For every integer
1<m\leq n
, consider the set

W_{m}(F)=\left\{\operatorname{wt}(g)\mid g\in K[x]\mbox{ monic},\ 0<\deg(g)<m\right\}\subset\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}}.

The polynomial
F
is defectless if and only if all these subsets
W_{m}(F)
contain a maximal value
[
24
]
,
[
15
, Thm. 5.7]
.

In Section
1.4
, we defined a certain set
\Gamma_{\operatorname{sme}}
containing
\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}}
, and we showed the existence of the following supremums:

w_{m}(F):=\sup\left(W_{m}(F)\right)\in\Gamma_{\operatorname{sme}},\qquad 1<m\leq n.

By Corollary
1.7
, if
W_{m}(F)
does not contain a maximal element, then
w_{m}(F)\not\in\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\theta\in\overline{K}
be a root of
F
in
\overline{K}
and consider Krasner’s constant

\Omega(F)=\max\left\{v(\theta-\theta^{\prime})\mid\theta^{\prime}\mbox{ root of }F,\ \theta^{\prime}\neq\theta\right\}\in\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}}.

If
F
is separable, then
w_{n}(F)\leq\Omega(F)
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\operatorname{wt}(g)>\Omega(F)
for some monic
g\in K[x]
such that
0<\deg(g)<n
.
Clearly,
\operatorname{wt}(g)=v(g(\theta))/\deg(g)
is the average of all
v(\theta-\alpha)
for
\alpha
running in the multiset
Z(g)
of roots of
g
in
\overline{K}
, counting multiplicities. Hence, we must have
v(\theta-\alpha)>\Omega(F)
for some
\alpha\in Z(g)
.

By Krasner’s lemma,
\theta
is purely inseparable over
K(\alpha)
. Since
\theta
is separable over
K
, we must have
\theta\in K(\alpha)
and this contradicts the fact that
\deg(g)<n
.
∎

If
F
is inseparable and has defect, then the set
W_{n}(F)
may be unbounded in
\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}}
in which case,
w_{n}(F)=\max(\Gamma_{\operatorname{sme}})
corresponds to the quasicut
(\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}},\emptyset)
.

Lemma 4.2
.

If there exists a maximal element in
W_{n}(F)
, then any monic
\phi\in K[x]
of minimal degree satisfying
\operatorname{wt}(\phi)=w_{n}(F)

is irreducible in
K[x]
.

Proof.

Suppose
\phi=ab
for some monic
a,b\in K[x]
with
\deg(a),\deg(b)<\deg(\phi)
. By the minimality of
\deg(\phi)
, we have
\operatorname{wt}(a),\ \operatorname{wt}(b)<w_{n}(F)
; thus,

w_{n}(F)=\operatorname{wt}(\phi)=\dfrac{v_{F}(a)+v_{F}(b)}{\deg(\phi)}<\dfrac{\deg(a)w_{n}(F)+\deg(b)w_{n}(F)}{\deg(\phi)}=w_{n}(F),

which is a contradiction.
∎

Suppose that
\max(W_{n}(F))
does not exist. For all weighted values
\beta\in W_{n}
, let
\deg(\beta)
be the minimal
\ell\in\mathbb{N}\cap[1,n)
such that there exists a monic
g\in K[x]
of degree
\ell
such that
\beta=\operatorname{wt}(g)
.
Consider the minimal
m\in\mathbb{N}\cap[1,n)
such that there exists a totally ordered cofinal family of constant degree
m
:

{\mathcal{B}}=\left(\beta_{i}\right)_{i\in B}\subset W_{n}(F),\quad\deg(\beta_{i})=m,\ \forall\,i\in B.

We may assume that the set of indices
B
is well-ordered and the mapping
i\mapsto\beta_{i}
is an isomorphism of ordered sets between
B
and our family
\left(\beta_{i}\right)_{i\in B}
.

For all
i\in B
, choose a monic
\chi_{i}\in K[x]
of degree
m
such that
\beta_{i}=\operatorname{wt}(\chi_{i})
.

Let
A\subset B
be the subset of all indices
i\in B
such that
\chi_{i}
is irreducible. Then the subfamily
\left(\beta_{i}\right)_{i\in A}
is a final segment of
{\mathcal{B}}
.

Indeed, by the minimality of
m
, there exists
\beta_{i}\in{\mathcal{B}}
such that
\operatorname{wt}(g)<\beta_{i}
for all monic

g\in K[x]
of degree less than
m
. Then, similar arguments to those used in the proof of Lemma
4.2
show that
\chi_{j}
is irreducible for all
j\geq i
.

In particular, the family
\left(\beta_{i}\right)_{i\in A}
is still cofinal in
W_{n}(F)
.

Consider the following set of monic irreducible polynomials of constant degree:

\Phi=\begin{cases}\left\{\chi_{i}\mid i\in A\right\},&\mbox{if }\,\nexists\max\left(W_{n}(F)\right),\\
\left\{\phi\right\},&\operatorname{wt}(\phi)=\max\left(W_{n}(F)\right),\ \mbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Let
m
be the constant degree of the polynomials in the set
\Phi
. We may apply this construction to find a set
\Phi^{\prime}
of monic irreducible polynomials of constant degree optimizing the weighted values in
W_{m}(F)
.
An iteration of this procedure leads to a finite sequence of such sets of polynomials

(7)

\left[\Phi_{0},\Phi_{1},\dots,\Phi_{r},\Phi_{r+1}=\{F\}\right],

whose degrees grow strictly:

(8)

1=m_{0}<m_{1}<\cdots<m_{r+1}=n,\qquad m_{\ell}=\deg\left(\Phi_{\ell}\right),\ \ 0\leq\ell\leq r+1,

and the following property is satisfied: for any index
0\leq\ell\leq r
and any monic polynomial
g\in K[x]
with
0<\deg(g)<m_{\ell+1}
, there exists
\phi\in\Phi_{\ell}
such that

(9)

\operatorname{wt}(g)\leq\operatorname{wt}(\phi).

Definition.
An
Okutsu frame
of
F
is a list of monic irreducible polynomials as in (
7
), having degrees as in (
8
), and satisfying the fundamental property (
9
).

Clearly, we may replace each
\Phi_{\ell}
with a suitable subset so that the following additional properties are satisfied, for all
0\leq\ell\leq r
:

(OF1)

\#\Phi_{\ell}=1
whenever
\max\left(W_{m_{\ell+1}}\right)
exists.

(OF2)

If
\max\left(W_{m_{\ell+1}}\right)
does not exist, we may consider a total ordering on
\Phi_{\ell}
determined by the action of
v_{F}
:

\phi<\phi^{\prime}\ \ \Longleftrightarrow\ \ v_{F}(\phi)<v_{F}(\phi^{\prime}).

(OF3)

For all
\phi\in\Phi_{\ell}
,
\varphi\in\Phi_{\ell+1}
, we have
\operatorname{wt}(\phi)<\operatorname{wt}(\varphi)
.

From now on, we shall assume that our Okutsu frames satisfy these additional properties. Note that
w_{m_{1}}(F)<\cdots<w_{m_{r+1}}(F)=w_{n}(F)
and

w_{m_{\ell+1}}(F)=\begin{cases}\operatorname{wt}(\phi),&\mbox{ if }\Phi_{\ell}=\{\phi\},\\
\sup\left\{\operatorname{wt}(\chi_{i})\mid i\in A\right\},&\mbox{ if }\Phi_{\ell}=\{\chi_{i}\mid i\in A\}.\end{cases}

4.2.
Okutsu frames and Mac Lane–Vaquié chains

In this section, we show that any MLV chain of
v_{F}
determines an Okutsu frame of
F
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\left[\Phi_{0},\Phi_{1},\dots,\Phi_{r},\{F\}\right]
be an Okutsu frame of
F
. For
0\leq\ell\leq r
, let

V_{m_{\ell}}=\left\{v_{F}(f)\mid f\in K[x]\mbox{ monic of degree }m_{\ell}\right\}.

Then, there exists
\max\left(W_{m_{\ell+1}}\right)
if and only if there exists
\max\left(V_{m_{\ell}}\right)
.

Proof.

If
\max\left(W_{m_{\ell+1}}\right)
exists, then
\Phi_{\ell}=\{\phi\}
with
\max\left(W_{m_{\ell+1}}\right)=\operatorname{wt}(\phi)
, by the property (
9
). Obviously,
\max\left(V_{m_{\ell}}\right)=v_{F}(\phi)
.

Suppose now that
\max\left(V_{m_{\ell}}\right)=v_{F}(\varphi)
for some monic
\varphi\in K[x]
of degree
m_{\ell}
. By (
9
), for all monic
f\in K[x]
with
\deg(f)<m_{\ell+1}
we have

\operatorname{wt}(f)\leq\operatorname{wt}(\phi)
,
for some
\phi\in\Phi_{\ell}
. Since
v_{F}(\phi)\leq v_{F}(\varphi)
, we deduce that
\operatorname{wt}(\varphi)=\max\left(W_{m_{\ell+1}}\right)
.
∎

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
F\in\operatorname{Irr}(K)
and consider a MLV chain of
v_{F}
:

\mu_{0}\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi_{1},\gamma_{1}}}{{\,\longrightarrow\,}}\ \mu_{1}\ \,\longrightarrow\,\ \cdots\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi_{r-1},\gamma_{r-1}}}{{\,\longrightarrow\,}}\ \mu_{r-1}\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi_{r},\gamma_{r}}}{{\,\longrightarrow\,}}\ \mu_{r}\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle F,\infty}}{{\,\longrightarrow\,}}\ \mu_{r+1}=v_{F}.

Take
\Phi_{r+1}=\{F\}
. For each
0\leq\ell\leq r
, consider the following set of monic irreducible polynomials of constant degree:

\Phi_{\ell}=\begin{cases}\left\{\phi_{\ell}\right\},&\mbox{ if }\ \mu_{\ell+1}=[\mu_{\ell};\,\phi_{\ell+1},\gamma_{\ell+1}]\mbox{ is an ordinary augmentation},\\
\left\{\chi_{i}\mid i\in A_{\ell}\right\},&\mbox{ if }\ \mu_{\ell+1}=[\mathcal{A}_{\ell};\,\phi_{\ell+1},\gamma_{\ell+1}]\mbox{ is a limit augmentation},\end{cases}

where
\mathcal{A}_{\ell}=\left(\rho_{i}\right)_{i\in A_{\ell}}
with
\rho_{i}=[\mu_{\ell};\,\chi_{i},v_{F}(\chi_{i})]
for all
i\in A_{\ell}
.
Then,
\left[\Phi_{0},\dots,\Phi_{r},\Phi_{r+1}\right]
is an Okutsu frame of
F
.

Proof.

Let us first prove that the fundamental property (
9
) holds for all
0\leq\ell\leq r
.

Suppose that
\mu_{\ell}\to\mu_{\ell+1}
is an ordinary augmentation.
By the definition of a MLV chain, we have
m_{\ell}=\deg(\phi_{\ell})<m_{\ell+1}=\deg(\phi_{\ell+1})
. Hence, for all monic
g\in K[x]
of degree
0<\deg(g)<m_{\ell+1}
, we have simultaneously
\phi_{\ell+1}\nmid_{\mu_{\ell}}\phi_{\ell}
and
\phi_{\ell+1}\nmid_{\mu_{\ell}}g
. Since
\mathbf{t}(\mu_{\ell},v_{F})=\left[\phi_{\ell+1}\right]_{\mu_{\ell}}
, Lemma
1.4
shows that

\mu_{\ell}(\phi_{\ell})=v_{F}(\phi_{\ell})
and
\mu_{\ell}(g)=v_{F}(g)
.

Now, since
\phi_{\ell}
is a key polynomial for
\mu_{\ell}
, Theorem
1.2
implies

\operatorname{wt}(g)=\dfrac{\mu_{\ell}(g)}{\deg(g)}\leq\dfrac{\mu_{\ell}(\phi_{\ell})}{m_{\ell}}=\operatorname{wt}(\phi_{\ell}).

This ends the proof concerning all ordinary augmentation steps.

Suppose that
\mu_{\ell}\to\mu_{\ell+1}
is a limit augmentation. We mimic the above arguments, just by replacing the pair
\mu_{\ell},\phi_{\ell}
with the pair
\rho_{i},\chi_{i}
for a sufficiently large
i\in A_{\ell}
.

Let
\mu_{\mathcal{A}_{\ell}}=[\mathcal{A}_{\ell};\,\phi_{\ell+1},\gamma_{\mathcal{A}_{\ell}}]
be the minimal limit augmentation of the essential continuous family
\mathcal{A}_{\ell}
.
By the definition of a MLV chain, we have

m_{\ell}=\deg(\chi_{i})<m_{\ell+1}=\deg(\phi_{\ell+1})\ \mbox{ for all }i\in A_{\ell}.

Hence, for all monic
g\in K[x]
of degree
0<\deg(g)<m_{\ell+1}
, we have simultaneously
\phi_{\ell+1}\nmid_{\mu_{\ell}}\chi_{i}
and
\phi_{\ell+1}\nmid_{\mu_{\ell}}g
. Since
\mathbf{t}(\mu_{\mathcal{A}_{\ell}},v_{F})=\left[\phi_{\ell+1}\right]_{\mu_{\mathcal{A}_{\ell}}}
, Lemma
1.4
shows that

\mu_{\mathcal{A}_{\ell}}(g)=v_{F}(g),\qquad\mu_{\mathcal{A}_{\ell}}(\chi_{i})=v_{F}(\chi_{i})\ \mbox{ for all }i\in A_{\ell}.

On the other hand,
m_{\ell+1}=\operatorname{deg}_{\infty}(\mathcal{A}_{\ell})
is the unstable degree of
\mathcal{A}_{\ell}
. Hence,
g
and
\chi_{i}
are
\mathcal{A}_{\ell}
-stable. Take a sufficiently large
i
such that

\rho_{i}(g)=\rho_{\mathcal{A}_{\ell}}(g)=\mu_{\mathcal{A}_{\ell}}(g)=v_{F}(g).

By
[
2
, Lem. 4.12]
, we may assume that
\chi_{j}\nmid_{\rho_{i}}\chi_{i}
for all
j>i
. This implies

\rho_{i}(\chi_{i})=\rho_{j}(\chi_{i})=\rho_{\mathcal{A}_{\ell}}(\chi_{i})=\mu_{\mathcal{A}_{\ell}}(\chi_{i})=v_{F}(\chi_{i}),

as well. Since
\chi_{i}
is a key polynomial for
\rho_{i}
, Theorem
1.2
implies

\operatorname{wt}(g)=\dfrac{\rho_{i}(g)}{\deg(g)}\leq\dfrac{\rho_{i}(\chi_{i})}{m_{\ell}}=\operatorname{wt}(\chi_{i}).

This ends the proof of (
9
).

Finally, by
[
17
, Thm. 4.7]
, the augmentation step
\mu_{\ell}\to\mu_{\ell+1}
is ordinary if and only if the set
V_{m_{\ell}}
contains a maximal element. By Lemma
4.3
,
\#\Phi_{\ell}=1
if and only if
W_{m_{\ell+1}}
contains a maximal element.
∎

In particular, the length
r+1
of this Okutsu frame of
F
is equal to the Mac Lane–Vaquié depth of
v_{F}
.

Conversely, all Okutsu frames of
F
arise in this way.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
\left[\Phi_{0},\dots,\Phi_{r},\Phi_{r+1}=\{F\}\right]
be an Okutsu frame of
F\in\operatorname{Irr}(K)
. For all
0\leq\ell\leq r+1
, choose an arbitrary
\,\phi_{\ell}\in\Phi_{\ell}
and denote
\gamma_{\ell}=v_{F}(\phi_{\ell})
. Then, the truncation of
v_{F}
by
\phi_{\ell}
is a valuation
\mu_{\ell}
fitting into a MLV chain of
v_{F}
:

\mu_{0}\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi_{1},\gamma_{1}}}{{\,\longrightarrow\,}}\ \mu_{1}\ \,\longrightarrow\,\ \cdots\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi_{r-1},\gamma_{r-1}}}{{\,\longrightarrow\,}}\ \mu_{r-1}\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi_{r},\gamma_{r}}}{{\,\longrightarrow\,}}\ \mu_{r}\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle F,\infty}}{{\,\longrightarrow\,}}\ \mu_{r+1}=v_{F}.

If
\Phi_{\ell}=\left\{\phi_{\ell}\right\}
, then
\mu_{\ell+1}=[\mu_{\ell};\,\phi_{\ell+1},\gamma_{\ell+1}]
is an ordinary augmentation.

If
\Phi_{\ell}=\left\{\chi_{i}\mid i\in I_{\ell}\right\}
, then
\mu_{\ell+1}=[\mathcal{A}_{\ell};\,\phi_{\ell+1},\gamma_{\ell+1}]
is a limit augmentation with respect to the essential continuous family
\mathcal{A}_{\ell}=\{\mu_{\ell}\}\cup\left(\rho_{i}\right)_{i\in A_{\ell}}
, where
\rho_{i}=[\mu_{\ell};\,\chi_{i},v_{F}(\chi_{i})]
and
A_{\ell}\subset I_{\ell}
contains the indices
i
such that
v_{F}(\chi_{i})>\gamma_{\ell}
.

Proof.

Let
\phi_{0}=x-a
for some
a\in K
. Since
\operatorname{supp}(v_{F})=FK[x]
and
\deg(F)>1
, we have
\gamma_{0}:=v_{F}(\phi_{0})<\infty
. Thus,
the truncation of
v_{F}
by
\phi_{0}
is the depth-zero valuation
\mu_{0}=\omega_{a,\gamma_{0}}\leq v_{F}
. Since
\mu_{0}
has trivial support, we have
\mu_{0}<v_{F}
and
\phi_{0}
is a key polynomial for
\mu_{0}
of minimal degree.
Also, the equality
\mu_{0}(\phi_{0})=\gamma_{0}=v_{F}(\phi_{0})
shows that
\phi_{0}\not\in\mathbf{t}(\mu_{0},v_{F})
.

Now, suppose that for some
0\leq\ell\leq r
we have constructed a MLV chain of the valuation
\mu_{\ell}
:

\mu_{0}\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi_{1},\gamma_{1}}}{{\,\longrightarrow\,}}\ \mu_{1}\ \,\longrightarrow\,\ \cdots\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi_{\ell},\gamma_{\ell}}}{{\,\longrightarrow\,}}\ \mu_{\ell}<v_{F},

satisfying all conditions of the theorem, for all indices
0,\dots,\ell
. Since
\mu_{\ell}\left(\phi_{\ell}\right)=\gamma_{\ell}=v_{F}\left(\phi_{\ell}\right)
, we have
\phi_{\ell}\not\in\mathbf{t}(\mu_{\ell},v_{F})
.

Let us construct a further step of the MLV chain, satisfying the conditions of the theorem for the index
\ell+1
as well.

Denote
m_{\ell}=\deg(\phi_{\ell})
for all
0\leq\ell\leq r
.
Suppose that
\Phi_{\ell}=\left\{\phi_{\ell}\right\}
. Since
\phi_{\ell}
is a key polynomial for
\mu_{\ell}
of minimal degree, Theorem
1.2
shows that

\dfrac{\mu_{\ell}(\phi_{\ell+1})}{m_{\ell+1}}\leq\dfrac{\mu_{\ell}(\phi_{\ell})}{m_{\ell}}=\operatorname{wt}(\phi_{\ell})<\operatorname{wt}(\phi_{\ell+1}).

Hence,
\mu_{\ell}(\phi_{\ell+1})<v_{F}(\phi_{\ell+1})
.
We claim that
m_{\ell+1}
is the least degree of a monic polynomial in
K[x]
satisfying this inequality.
Indeed, suppose that
g\in K[x]
is a monic polynomial of minimal degree such that
\mu_{\ell}(g)<v_{F}(g)
. By Lemma
1.4
,
g
is a key polynomial for
\mu_{\ell}
; hence, it is
\mu_{\ell}
-minimal and Theorem
1.2
shows that

\dfrac{\mu_{\ell}(g)}{\deg(g)}=\dfrac{\mu_{\ell}(\phi_{\ell})}{m_{\ell}}=\operatorname{wt}(\mu_{\ell}).

If
\deg(g)<m_{\ell+1}
, the fundamental property (
9
) would imply:

\operatorname{wt}(g)\leq\operatorname{wt}(\phi_{\ell})=\dfrac{\mu_{\ell}(\phi_{\ell})}{m_{\ell}}=\dfrac{\mu_{\ell}(g)}{\deg(g)},

contradicting our initial assumption.

Thus,
\phi_{\ell+1}\in K[x]
is a monic polynomial of minimal degree satisfying
\mu_{\ell}(\phi_{\ell+1})<v_{F}(\phi_{\ell+1})
. By Lemma
1.4
,
\phi_{\ell+1}
is a key polynomial for
\mu_{\ell}
. The ordinary augmentation

\mu_{\ell+1}:=[\mu_{\ell};\phi_{\ell+1},\gamma_{\ell+1}],\quad\gamma_{\ell+1}=v_{F}\left(\phi_{\ell+1}\right),

satisfies
\mu_{\ell}<\mu_{\ell+1}\leq v_{F}
. If we add the augmentation step
\mu_{\ell}\to\mu_{\ell+1}
to the previous MLV chain, we obtain a MLV chain of
\mu_{\ell+1}
, because

\deg(\mu_{\ell})=m_{\ell}<m_{\ell+1}=\deg(\mu_{\ell+1}).

If
\ell=r
, then
\phi_{\ell+1}=F
and
\gamma_{\ell+1}=\infty
, so that
\mu_{\ell+1}=v_{F}
and the theorem is proven.

If
\ell<r
, then
m_{\ell+1}<\deg(F)
and this implies
\gamma_{\ell+1}<\infty
. In this case,
\mu_{\ell+1}
has trivial support and
\mu_{\ell+1}<v_{F}
. Also,
\phi_{\ell+1}
is a key polynomial for
\mu_{\ell+1}
of minimal degree. By Lemma
1.1
, the truncation of
v_{F}
by
\phi_{\ell+1}
is equal to:

\left(v_{F}\right)_{\phi_{\ell+1}}=\left(\mu_{\ell+1}\right)_{\phi_{\ell+1}}=\mu_{\ell+1}.

Finally, since
\mu_{\ell+1}\left(\phi_{\ell+1}\right)=\gamma_{\ell+1}=v_{F}\left(\phi_{\ell+1}\right)
, we have necessarily
\phi_{\ell+1}\not\in\mathbf{t}(\mu_{\ell+1},v_{F})
. This ends the recurrence step in the case
\Phi_{\ell}=\left\{\phi_{\ell}\right\}
.

Now, suppose that
\Phi_{\ell}=\left\{\chi_{i}\mid i\in I\right\}
and let
i_{0}\in I
be the index for which
\phi_{\ell}=\chi_{i_{0}}
.

By our recurrence assumption,
\mu_{\ell}
is a valuation and
\phi_{\ell}
is a key polynomial for
\mu_{\ell}
of minimal degree. Since
\deg\,\mathbf{t}(\mu_{\ell},v_{F})\geq\deg(\mu_{\ell})=m_{\ell}
, Lemma
1.4
shows that

\mu_{\ell}(a)=v_{F}(a)\quad\mbox{ for all }a\in K[x],\ \mbox{ with }\deg(a)<m_{\ell},

because
\phi\nmid_{\mu_{\ell}}a
for any
\phi\in\mathbf{t}(\mu_{\ell},v_{F})
.

Denote
\beta_{i}=v_{F}(\chi_{i})
for all
i\in I
. By the additional property (i) of the Okutsu frame, we have
\beta_{i_{0}}<\beta_{i}
for all
i_{0}<i
in
I
. Since all
\chi_{i}\in K[x]
are monic polynomials of degree
m_{\ell}
, we deduce that

\mu_{\ell}\left(\chi_{i_{0}}-\chi_{i}\right)=v_{F}\left(\chi_{i_{0}}-\chi_{i}\right)=\beta_{i_{0}}=\operatorname{sv}(\mu_{\ell})\quad\mbox{ for all }i_{0}<i.

By
[
16
, Prop. 6.3]
, all these
\chi_{i}\in\Phi_{\ell}
are key polynomials for
\mu_{\ell}
of degree
m_{\ell}
. Hence, we may consider the family of ordinary augmentations

\rho_{i}=[\mu_{\ell};\,\chi_{i},\beta_{i}]\quad\mbox{ for all }i>i_{0}.

By comparing their action of
\chi_{j}
-expansions, we clearly have

\mu_{\ell}<\rho_{i}<\rho_{j}<v_{F}\quad\mbox{ for all }\,i_{0}<i<j\,\mbox{ in }I.

Denote
\rho_{i_{0}}:=\mu_{\ell}
.
For the totally ordered set of indices
A=I_{\geq i_{0}}
, we may consider a continuous family of valuations
\mathcal{A}=\left(\rho_{i}\right)_{i\in A}
of stable degree
m_{\ell}
.

Let us show that
\phi_{\ell+1}
is
\mathcal{A}
-unstable. For all
i\in A
, Theorem
1.2
shows that

\dfrac{\rho_{i}\left(\phi_{\ell+1}\right)}{m_{\ell+1}}\leq\dfrac{\operatorname{sv}(\rho_{i})}{m_{\ell}}=\dfrac{\beta_{i}}{m_{\ell}}<\operatorname{wt}(\phi_{\ell+1}),

the last inequality by the additional property (OF3) of the Okutsu frame.
Hence,
\rho_{i}\left(\phi_{\ell+1}\right)<v_{F}(\phi_{\ell+1})
. By
[
17
, Cor. 2.5]
, this implies

\rho_{i}\left(\phi_{\ell+1}\right)<\rho_{j}\left(\phi_{\ell+1}\right)\quad\mbox{ for all }\,i<j,

so that
\phi_{\ell+1}
is
\mathcal{A}
-unstable.

Let us now show that
m_{\ell+1}
is the minimal degree of
\mathcal{A}
-unstability; that is, all monic
g\in K[x]
with
\deg(g)<m_{\ell+1}
are
\mathcal{A}
-stable. Since the product of
\mathcal{A}
-stable polynomials is
\mathcal{A}
-stable, we may assume that
g
is irreducible. By the fundamental property (
9
), there exists
\chi_{i}\in\Phi_{\ell}
such that

(10)

\operatorname{wt}(g)\leq\operatorname{wt}(\chi_{i})=\dfrac{\beta_{i}}{m_{\ell}}=\operatorname{wt}(\rho_{i}).

We claim that
\rho_{i}(g)=\rho_{j}(g)
for all
j>i
. Indeed, suppose that
\rho_{i}(g)<\rho_{j}(g)\leq v_{F}(g)
for some
j>i
. On one hand, from (
10
) we deduce

\dfrac{\rho_{i}(g)}{\deg(g)}<\operatorname{wt}(g)\leq\operatorname{wt}(\rho_{i}),

so that
g
is not
\rho_{i}
-minimal, by Theorem
1.2
. This contradicts Corollary
3.2
.

Therefore,
\phi_{\ell+1}
is a monic
\mathcal{A}
-unstable polynomial of minimal degree. In other words,
\phi_{\ell+1}\in\operatorname{KP}_{\infty}(\mathcal{A})
.
The limit augmentation

\mu_{\ell+1}:=[\mathcal{A};\phi_{\ell+1},\gamma_{\ell+1}],\quad\gamma_{\ell+1}=v_{F}\left(\phi_{\ell+1}\right),

satisfies
\mu_{\ell+1}\leq v_{F}
. Since
\phi_{\ell}\not\in\mathbf{t}(\mu_{\ell},\mu_{\ell+1})=\mathbf{t}(\mu_{\ell},v_{F})
, if we add the augmentation step
\mu_{\ell}\to\mu_{\ell+1}
to the previous MLV chain, we obtain a MLV chain of
\mu_{\ell+1}
.

If
\ell=r
, we have
\phi_{\ell+1}=F
and
\gamma_{\ell+1}=\infty
, so that
\mu_{\ell+1}=v_{F}
and the theorem would be proven.

If
\ell<r
, then
m_{\ell+1}<\deg(F)
and this implies
\gamma_{\ell+1}<\infty
. In this case,
\mu_{\ell+1}
has trivial support and
\mu_{\ell+1}<v_{F}
. Also,
\phi_{\ell+1}
is a key polynomial for
\mu_{\ell+1}
of minimal degree. By Lemma
1.1
, the truncation of
v_{F}
by
\phi_{\ell+1}
is equal to:

\left(v_{F}\right)_{\phi_{\ell+1}}=\left(\mu_{\ell+1}\right)_{\phi_{\ell+1}}=\mu_{\ell+1}.

Finally, since
\mu_{\ell+1}\left(\phi_{\ell+1}\right)=\gamma_{\ell+1}=v_{F}\left(\phi_{\ell+1}\right)
, we have necessarily
\phi_{\ell+1}\not\in\mathbf{t}(\mu_{\ell+1},v_{F})
. This ends the recurrence step in the case
\Phi_{\ell}=\left\{\chi_{i}\mid i\in I\right\}
. ∎

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
\left[\Phi_{0},\dots,\Phi_{r},\Phi_{r+1}=\{F\}\right]
be an Okutsu frame of some
F\in\operatorname{Irr}(K)
. Then,
1=m_{0}\mid m_{1}\mid\cdots\mid m_{r}\mid\deg(F)
.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.5
,
1=m_{0},\dots,m_{r+1}=\deg(F)
are the degrees of a MLV chain of
v_{F}
. Since
(K,v)
is henselian, all jumps
m_{\ell+1}/m_{\ell}
take integer values.
Indeed, this follows from the results of Vaquié
[
24
]
, described in
[
17
, Sec. 6]
as well.
∎

4.3.
Computation of ramification indices, residual degrees and defect

Since
(K,v)
is henselian, the valuation
\overline{v}_{F}
is the only extension of
v
to the field extension
K_{F}=K[x]/(F)
.
By Ostrowski’s lemma, we have

\deg(F)=e(F)f(F)d(F),

where
e(F)=e(\overline{v}_{F}/v)
is the ramification index,
f(F)=f(\overline{v}_{F}/v)
the residual degree and
d(F)=d(\overline{v}_{F}/v)
the defect of
\overline{v}_{F}/v
. Also, Ostrowski showed that
d(F)
is a power of the exponent characeristic, defined as

p=\begin{cases}\operatorname{char}(k),&\mbox{ if }\operatorname{char}(k)>0,\\
1,&\mbox{ if }\operatorname{char}(k)=0.\end{cases}

In
[
17
, Sec.6]
, it is shown how to compute these invariants
e(F)
,
f(F)
,
d(F)
in terms of a MLV chain of
v_{F}
. Hence, as a consequence of Theorem
4.5
, we can compute them in terms of an Okutsu frame.

Let
\left[\Phi_{0},\dots,\Phi_{r},\Phi_{r+1}=\{F\}\right]
be an Okutsu frame of
F\in\operatorname{Irr}(K)
. Consider the MLV chain of
v_{F}
described in Theorem
4.5
:

\mu_{0}\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi_{1},\gamma_{1}}}{{\,\longrightarrow\,}}\ \mu_{1}\ \,\longrightarrow\,\ \cdots\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi_{r-1},\gamma_{r-1}}}{{\,\longrightarrow\,}}\ \mu_{r-1}\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi_{r},\gamma_{r}}}{{\,\longrightarrow\,}}\ \mu_{r}\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle F,\infty}}{{\,\longrightarrow\,}}\ v_{F}.

Recall that
\gamma_{\ell}=v_{F}(\phi_{\ell})
for all
\ell\geq 0
.

By the MLV condition, this chain induces a chain of abelian groups:

\Gamma_{\mu_{-1}}:=\Gamma\,\subset\,\Gamma_{\mu_{0}}\,\subset\,\Gamma_{\mu_{1}}\,\subset\,\cdots\,\Gamma_{\mu_{r}}=\Gamma_{v_{F}}=\Gamma_{\overline{v}_{F}},

with
\Gamma_{\mu_{\ell}}=\big\langle\,{\Gamma,\gamma_{0},\dots,\gamma_{\ell}}\,\big\rangle
for all
0\leq\ell\leq r

[
17
, Sec. 4.1]
. Hence,

e(F)=\left(\Gamma_{\overline{v}_{F}}\colon\Gamma\right)=\left(\Gamma_{\mu_{r}}\colon\Gamma\right)=e_{0}\cdots e_{r},

where
e_{\ell}=\left(\Gamma_{\mu_{\ell}}\colon\Gamma_{\mu_{\ell-1}}\right)
for all
0\leq\ell\leq r
.

Thus, each index
e_{\ell}
can be computed as the least positive integer
e
such that

e\,v_{F}(\phi_{\ell})\in\big\langle\,{\Gamma,v_{F}(\phi_{0}),\dots,v_{F}(\phi_{\ell-1})}\,\big\rangle.

On the other hand, Vaquié proved in
[
24
]
that
d(F)=d_{1}\cdots d_{r}
, where

d_{\ell}=\begin{cases}1,&\mbox{ if }\mu_{\ell}\to\mu_{\ell+1}\mbox{ is an ordinary augmentation},\\
m_{\ell+1}/m_{\ell},&\mbox{ if }\mu_{\ell}\to\mu_{\ell+1}\mbox{ is a limit augmentation},\end{cases}

for all
0\leq\ell\leq r
. Equivalently,

d_{\ell}=1\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \#\Phi_{\ell}=1\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \exists\max(W_{m_{\ell+1}}).

Thus,
d(F)
may be computed solely in terms of the Okutsu frame too.

Finally,
f(F)=\deg(F)/e(F)d(F)
is determined by
e(F)
and
d(F)
.

4.4.
Okutsu frames and abstract key polynomials

Abstract key polynomials were introduced by Herrera-Olalla-Spivakovsky as an alternative approach to the methods of Mac Lane and Vaquié, aiming at a thorough comprehension of the extensions to
K[x]
of arbitrary (not necessarily henselian) valuations on a field
K

[
7
,
8
]
.

These polynomials were further studied by several authors and the comparison with Mac Lane-Vaquié key polynomials is by now fully understood
[
12
,
3
,
19
,
1
,
20
]
.

Although they were classically defined only for valuations with trivial support, the paper
[
1
]
develops their properties for arbitrary valuations on
K[x]
too.
Let us recall a concrete comparison result between the two sorts of “key polynomials”.

Theorem 4.7
.

[
1
, Thm. 2.21]

Let
\nu
be a valuation on
K[x]
with nontrivial support. A monic polynomial
Q\in K[x]\setminus K
is an abstract key polynomial for
\nu
if and only if either
\operatorname{supp}(\nu)=QK[x]
, or the truncation
\nu_{Q}
is a valuation and
Q
is a (MLV) key polynomial of minimal degree for
\nu_{Q}
.

A set
\Psi
of abstract key polynomials for
\nu
is said to be
complete
if for all non-constant
g\in K[x]
there exists
Q\in\Psi
such that

(11)

\deg(Q)\leq\deg(g)\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\nu_{Q}(g)=\nu(g).

As a consequence of Theorem
4.5
, we derive another interpretation of abstract key polynomials.

Theorem 4.8
.

Suppose that
(K,v)
is henselian and let
\left[\Phi_{0},\dots,\Phi_{r},\Phi_{r+1}=\{F\}\right]
be an Okutsu frame of some
F\in\operatorname{Irr}(K)
. Then, the set
\,\Phi_{0}\cup\cdots\cup\Phi_{r}\cup\{F\}
is a complete set of abstract key polynomials for
v_{F}
.

Proof.

By Theorems
4.5
and
4.7
, all polynomials in the set
\Phi_{0}\cup\cdots\cup\Phi_{r}\cup\{F\}
are abstract key polynomials for
v_{F}
.

Take a monic
g\in K[x]\setminus K
. If
\deg(g)\geq\deg(F)
, then (
11
) is satisfied for
Q=F
.

If
\deg(g)<\deg(F)
, then there exists
0\leq\ell\leq r
such that
m_{\ell}\leq\deg(g)<m_{\ell+1}
.

With the notation in Theorem
4.5
, consider the MLV chain of
v_{F}
:

\mu_{0}\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi_{1},\gamma_{1}}}{{\,\longrightarrow\,}}\ \mu_{1}\ \,\longrightarrow\,\ \cdots\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi_{r-1},\gamma_{r-1}}}{{\,\longrightarrow\,}}\ \mu_{r-1}\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi_{r},\gamma_{r}}}{{\,\longrightarrow\,}}\ \mu_{r}\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle F,\infty}}{{\,\longrightarrow\,}}\ \mu_{r+1}=v_{F}.

If
\Phi_{\ell}=\{\phi_{\ell}\}
, then
\mu_{\ell+1}=[\mu_{\ell};\phi_{\ell+1},\gamma_{\ell+1}]
is an ordinary augmentation and
\mathbf{t}(\mu_{\ell},v_{F})=\mathbf{t}(\mu_{\ell},\mu_{\ell+1})=[\phi_{\ell+1}]_{\mu_{\ell}}
. Since
\deg(g)<\deg(\phi_{\ell+1})
, necessarily
\phi_{\ell+1}\nmid_{\mu_{\ell}}g
and
\mu_{\ell}(g)=v_{F}(g)
by Lemma
1.4
. Thus, (
11
) is satisfied for
Q=\phi_{\ell}
.

If
\Phi_{\ell}=\left\{\chi_{i}\mid i\in I_{\ell}\right\}
, then
\mu_{\ell+1}=[\mathcal{A}_{\ell};\,\phi_{\ell+1},\gamma_{\ell+1}]
is a limit augmentation and
\phi_{\ell+1}
is a limit key polynomial for
\mathcal{A}_{\ell}
. Hence,
g
is
\mathcal{A}_{\ell}
-stable and (
11
) is satisfied for
Q=\chi_{i}
, for a sufficiently large
i\in I_{\ell}
. ∎

The converse statement follows easily from the definitions.
Consider a complete set of abstract key polynomials for
\nu:=v_{F}
,

\Psi=\Psi_{m_{0}}\cup\cdots\Psi_{m_{r}}\cup\Psi_{m_{r+1}}=\{F\},\quad m_{0}=1<m_{1}<\cdots<m_{r}<\deg(F),

where all polynomials in
\Psi_{m_{\ell}}
have degree
m_{\ell}
.

In order to show that the list
[\Psi_{1},\dots,\Psi_{r},\{F\}]
is an Okutsu frame of
F
, we need only to check that the property (
9
) is satisfied.

Take a monic
g\in K[x]
such that
0<\deg(g)<m_{\ell+1}
for some
0\leq\ell\leq r
. By the completeness of
\Psi
, there exists
P\in\Psi
such that
\deg(P)\leq\deg(g)
and
\nu_{P}(g)=\nu(g)
.
Since
\deg(P)\leq m_{\ell}
, there exists
Q\in\Psi_{m_{\ell}}
such that
\nu_{P}\leq\nu_{Q}\leq\nu
. Thus,
\nu_{Q}(g)=\nu(g)
as well. By Theorem
4.7
,
Q
is a MLV key polynomial of minimal degree for
\nu_{Q}
. Thus, Theorem
1.2
shows that

\operatorname{wt}(g)=\dfrac{\nu(g)}{\deg(g)}=\dfrac{\nu_{Q}(g)}{\deg(g)}\leq\dfrac{\nu_{Q}(Q)}{m_{\ell}}=\dfrac{\nu(Q)}{m_{\ell}}=\operatorname{wt}(Q).

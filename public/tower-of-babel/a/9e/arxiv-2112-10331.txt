Relative Brauer Relations of Abelian p-groups
Marian F. Anton

Abstract
. The Brauer relations of a finite group G G are virtual differences of non-isomorphic G G -sets X − Y X-Y which induce isomorphic permutation G G -representations ℚ ⁡ [ X ] ≃ ℚ ⁡ [ Y ] \mathbb{Q}[X]\simeq\mathbb{Q}[Y] over the rationals. These relations have been classified by Tornehave-Bouc and Bartel-Dokchitser. Motivated by stable homotopy theory, a relative version of Brauer relations for ( G , C p ) (G,C_{p}) -bisets which are C p C_{p} -free have been classified by Kahn in case G G is an elementary Abelian p p -group. In this paper we extend Kahn’s classification to the case when G G is a finite Abelian p p -group.

3.

We denote by

(
[

]
)

Cyclic groups have no Brauer relations except zero. Products

of cyclic groups of prime order have one independent Brauer relation given below where

runs over all non-trivial cyclic subgroups of

\Theta_{P}=1-\sum_{C=\text{ cyclic }}C+pP.

Recall that an element

\displaystyle\sum n_{i}\text{ Ind}^{G}(1_{H_{i}})=0,

\displaystyle 1_{H_{i}}=\text{ trivial }H_{i}\text{-module }\mathbb{Q}.

According to Proposition

from subgroups

(or restricted by

) and can be lifted by

from quotients

(or projected by

)

\displaystyle P=K/N\text{ sub-quotient of }G,\;n_{P}\in\mathbb{Z}

of relations

LEMMA 4.2

LEMMA 4.3

(Goursat
[
9
]
)
.

The subgroups of a direct product of two finite groups

are in bijection with the quintuples
(K,N,A,B,\theta)
of subgroups

and

and isomorphisms

.

The correspondence in Goursat Lemma is given by the following map

\displaystyle(K,N,A,B,\theta)\mapsto S=\{(kn,\theta(kN)b)|n\in N,\;k\in K,\;b\in B\}\leq\Gamma\times\Omega

Let
\mathcal{S}_{G}
be the graph with a vertex
K
for each subgroup
K\leq G
and an edge
(K,N)
for each pair of subgroups

having index
[K:N]=p
.

LEMMA 4.4
.

\text{\rm rank }B(G\times C_{p})-\text{\rm rank }B(G,C_{p})=\text{\rm rank }B(G).

Proof.

By Proposition
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle\text{\rm rank }B(G)

\displaystyle=\#\text{ vertices in }\mathcal{S}_{G},

\displaystyle\text{\rm rank }B(G\times C_{p})

\displaystyle=\#\text{ vertices in }\mathcal{S}_{G\times C_{p}}.

By Proposition
2.3
, a basis for

is given by graphs of homomorphisms
\rho:K\to C_{p}
from subgroups
K\leq G
. Each such
\rho
is either trivial

or factors through a canonical map

and an automorphism of
C_{p}
where

is a subgroup of index
[K:N]=p
. The number of automorphisms of
C_{p}
is
p-1
. Hence, the number of graphs
K\times\rho
equals the number of subgroups
K\leq G
(
\rho=1
) plus

times the number of pairs

with

having index

(

):

\text{\rm rank }B(G,C_{p})=\#\text{ vertices in }\mathcal{S}_{G}+(p-1)\cdot\#\text{ edges in }\mathcal{S}_{G}.

By Goursat’s Lemma
4.3
, the vertices of
\mathcal{S}_{G\times C_{p}}
are in bijection with quintuples
(K,N,A,B,\theta)
of subgroups

and

and isomorphisms
\theta:K/N\approx A/B
. We distinguish two cases:

,
K=N
,

and

∎

Abstract
. We continue the study of the Drinfeld double of the Jordan plane, denoted by 𝒟 \mathcal{D} and introduced in [ AP ] . The simple finite-dimensional modules were computed in [ ADP ] ; it turns out that they factorize through U ⁡ ( 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 2 ​ ( 𝕜 ) ) U(\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\Bbbk)) . Here we introduce the Verma modules and the category 𝔒 \mathfrak{O} for 𝒟 \mathcal{D} , which have a resemblance to the similar ones in Lie theory but induced from indecomposable modules of the 0-part of the triangular decomposition. Accordingly, there is the notion of highest weight rank (hw-rk). We classify the indecomposable modules of hw-rk one and find families of hw-rk two. The Gabriel quiver of 𝒟 \mathcal{D} is computed implying that it has a wild representation type.



1.
Introduction

Let
\Bbbk
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
0
.
There were substantial advances in the ongoing classification of Nichols algebras over abelian groups with finite Gelfand-Kirillov dimension (abbreviated as
\operatorname{GKdim}
), an important step towards the classification of Hopf algebras with finite
\operatorname{GKdim}
, see
[
AAH
,
AAM
,
AGI
]
. Recall that Nichols algebras of diagonal type correspond to
infinitesimal braidings that are semisimple as Yetter-Drinfeld modules (over abelian groups).
In the context of finite
\operatorname{GKdim}
, Nichols algebras
with non-semisimple infinitesimal braidings appear naturally.
The well-known Jordan plane, denoted by
J
, is a paradigmatic example.

Quantized enveloping algebras can be defined via Drinfeld doubles
of (bosonizations of) suitable Nichols algebras of diagonal type.
A natural question is to investigate the behaviour of the Drinfeld doubles
of other Nichols algebras, specifically those with non-semisimple infinitesimal braidings.
In this direction, we studied in
[
AP
,
ADP
]
a Hopf algebra
\mathcal{D}

thought of as the Drinfeld double of the bosonization
J\#\Bbbk\mathbb{Z}
, which differs from quantized enveloping algebras
for some distinctive features:

\circ

[
AP
]
The Hopf algebra paired with
J\#\Bbbk\mathbb{Z}
in order to build the double
has the form
J^{d}\#U(\mathfrak{h})

where
J^{d}
is the graded dual of
J
and
\dim\mathfrak{h}=1
.

\circ

[
AP
]

The Hopf algebra
\mathcal{D}
is not simple and, in fact, it
fits into an
abelian
exact sequence of Hopf algebras

\textstyle{\mathcal{O}(\mathbf{G})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathcal{D}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi\hskip 16.38895pt}
\textstyle{U(\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\Bbbk))}

where
\mathcal{O}(\mathbf{G})
is the algebra of regular functions on
\mathbf{G}=(\mathbf{G}_{a}\times\mathbf{G}_{a})\rtimes\mathbf{G}_{m}
.

\circ

[
AP
]

The analogue of the Cartan subalgebra of
\mathcal{D}
splits as
\mathcal{D}^{0}\simeq U(\mathfrak{h})\otimes\Bbbk\Gamma
.

\circ

[
ADP
]

The finite-dimensional simple modules over
\mathcal{D}
are in bijective correspondence with those of

via
\pi
.

The next step is to understand the indecomposable modules.
We observe that the action of
\mathcal{D}^{0}
is not semisimple, what leads us to introduce
Verma modules inducing from indecomposable
\mathcal{D}^{0}
-modules; highest weight modules
and the category
\mathfrak{O}
they belong to.
The notions of
\operatorname{hw-rk}
and hw-series arise naturally.
See Section
3
.
In Sections
4
and
5

we study the indecomposable modules with

In the case of

In Section

There are convincing reasons to believe that the representation theory of the Drinfeld doubles of many of the Nichols algebras discovered in

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
The double of the Jordan plane

The well-known Jordan plane, the quadratic algebra
J=\Bbbk\langle x,y|xy-yx-\frac{1}{2}x^{2}\rangle
,
is a braided Hopf algebra where
x
and
y
are primitive.
The Hopf algebra
\mathcal{D}
was introduced in
[
AP
, Proposition 2.3]
, where it is denoted
\widetilde{D}
.
See
[
AP
,
ADP
]
for properties of
\mathcal{D}
, some of which are listed below.
The algebra
\mathcal{D}

is presented by generators
u
,
v
,
\xi
,
g^{\pm 1}
,
x
,
y
and relations

(1)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} g^{\pm 1}g^{\mp 1}&=1,&\xi g&=g\xi,\end{aligned}

(2)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} gx&=xg,&gy&=yg+xg,&\xi y&=y\xi-2y,&\xi x&=x\xi-2x,\\
ug&=gu,&vg&=gv+gu,&v\xi&=\xi v-2v,&u\xi&=\xi u-2u,\end{aligned}

(3)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} yx&=xy-\frac{1}{2}x^{2},&vu&=uv-\frac{1}{2}u^{2},\\
ux&=xu,&vx&=xv+(1-g)+xu,\\
uy&=yu+(1-g),&vy&=yv+\frac{1}{2}g\xi+yu.\end{aligned}

Notice that we have replaced the generator
\zeta
by
\xi=-2\zeta
in the presentation of
[
AP
]
and adjusted the relations accordingly.
The Hopf algebra structure is determined by

g\in G(\mathcal{D})
,
u,\xi\in{\mathcal{P}}(\mathcal{D})
,
x,y\in{\mathcal{P}}_{g,1}(\mathcal{D})
and

\displaystyle\Delta(v)

\displaystyle=v\otimes 1+1\otimes v-\frac{1}{2}\xi\otimes u.

The following set is a PBW-basis of
\mathcal{D}
:

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=\{x^{n}\,y^{r}\,g^{m}\,\xi^{k}\,u^{i}\,v^{j}:i,j,k,n,r\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\quad m\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

We shall consider the following subalgebras of
\mathcal{D}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{<0}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\Bbbk\langle x,y\rangle,

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{0}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\Bbbk\langle g^{\pm 1},\xi\rangle,

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\Bbbk\langle g^{\pm 1},\xi,x,y\rangle,

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{>0}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\Bbbk\langle u,v\rangle

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\Bbbk\langle g^{\pm 1},\xi,u,v\rangle.

The algebra
\mathcal{D}
has a
\mathbb{Z}
-grading
\mathcal{D}=\oplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{D}^{[n]}
given by

(4)

\displaystyle\deg x=\deg y=-2,

\displaystyle\deg u=\deg v=2,

\displaystyle\deg g=\deg\xi=0.

The algebra
\mathcal{D}
has a triangular decomposition

\mathcal{D}\simeq\mathcal{D}^{<0}\otimes\mathcal{D}^{0}\otimes\mathcal{D}^{>0}
.

2.2.
An exact sequence of Hopf algebras

Let
\mathcal{O}\coloneqq\Bbbk\langle x,u,g^{\pm 1}\rangle
; this is a commutative Hopf subalgebra of
\mathcal{D}
,
hence
\mathcal{O}\simeq\mathcal{O}(\mathbf{G})
,
where
\mathbf{G}
is the algebraic group as in the Introduction.
Let
e,f,h
be the Chevalley generators of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\Bbbk)
, i. e.

[e,f]=h
,
[h,e]=2e
,
[h,f]=-2f
.
The Hopf algebra map
\pi\colon\mathcal{D}\to U(\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\Bbbk))
determined by

(5)

\displaystyle\pi(v)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}e,

\displaystyle\pi(y)

\displaystyle=f,

\displaystyle\pi(\xi)

\displaystyle=h,

\displaystyle\pi(u)

\displaystyle=\pi(x)=\pi(g-1)=0,

induces an
isomorphism of Hopf algebras
\mathcal{D}/\mathcal{D}\mathcal{O}^{+}\simeq U(\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\Bbbk))
.
Thus we have an exact sequence as mentioned in the Introduction.

3.
Highest weight modules

3.1.
Weight decompositions

Proposition
2.1

and Remark
2.3
lead us to the following considerations.
Given
P\in{}_{\mathcal{D}^{0}\hskip-2.0pt}\operatorname{Mod}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, we set

\displaystyle P^{(n)}

\displaystyle=\{m\in P:(\xi-n)^{a}\cdot m=0,\ (g-1)^{b}\cdot m=0\text{ for some }a,b\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}.

By a standard argument, see e. g.
[
D
, 1.2.13]
, the sum
\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}P^{(n)}
is direct.
Let
{}_{\mathcal{D}^{0}}\mathcal{SG}
be the full subcategory of
{}_{\mathcal{D}^{0}\hskip-2.0pt}\operatorname{Mod}
consisting of those

P\in{}_{\mathcal{D}^{0}\hskip-2.0pt}\operatorname{Mod}
such that
P=\oplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}P^{(n)}
.
Notice that
{}_{\mathcal{D}_{0}\hskip-2.0pt}\operatorname{mod}
is a subcategory of
{}_{\mathcal{D}^{0}}\mathcal{SG}
.

Given
M\in{}_{\mathcal{D}\hskip-2.0pt}\operatorname{Mod}
, we set by abuse of notation

\displaystyle M^{(n)}=\big(\operatorname{Res}^{\mathcal{D}^{0}}_{\mathcal{D}}(M)\big)^{(n)}.

The weights of
M\in{}_{\mathcal{D}\hskip-2.0pt}\operatorname{Mod}
are the elements of

\varPi(M)\coloneqq\{n\in\mathbb{Z}:M^{(n)}\neq 0\}
;
the
M^{(n)}
’s are called weight subspaces even when they are 0.

Definition 3.1
.

A module
M\in{}_{\mathcal{D}\hskip-2.0pt}\operatorname{Mod}
is
suitably graded
if

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle=\oplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}M^{(n)}

and

\displaystyle\dim M^{(n)}

\displaystyle<\infty

\displaystyle\text{for all }n

\displaystyle\in\mathbb{Z}.

Let
{}_{\mathcal{D}}\mathcal{SG}
be the full subcategory of
{}_{\mathcal{D}\hskip-2.0pt}\operatorname{Mod}
of suitably graded modules.

Remark 3.2
.

(i)

From the defining relations we get that

(7)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} x\cdot M^{(n)}&\subset M^{(n-2)}\supset y\cdot M^{(n)},&g\cdot M^{(n)}&=M^{(n)}\supset\xi\cdot M^{(n)},\\
u\cdot M^{(n)}&\subset M^{(n+2)}\supset v\cdot M^{(n)},&n&\in\mathbb{Z}.\end{aligned}

(ii)

Morphisms of
\mathcal{D}
-modules preserve the weight subspaces.
Thus submodules and quotients of suitably graded modules are suitably graded.
Given an exact sequence of
\mathcal{D}
-modules
\textstyle{N\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{M\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{S}
,
the sequence
\textstyle{N^{(n)}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{M^{(n)}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{S^{(n)}}
of
\mathcal{D}^{0}
-modules
is exact for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

(iii)

Any
M\in{}_{\mathcal{D}\hskip-2.0pt}\operatorname{mod}
is suitable graded by Remark
2.3
.
If
n\in\varPi(M)
, then

\displaystyle\dim M^{(n)}

\displaystyle=\dim M^{(-n)}

and

\displaystyle\{n,n-2,n-4,\dots,2-n,-n\}

\displaystyle\subseteq\varPi(M).

Proof.

Let
\lambda\in\Bbbk
. Then
(\xi-\lambda+2)y=y(\xi-\lambda)
by (
2
); hence by induction

(\xi-\lambda+2)^{a}y=y(\xi-\lambda)^{a}
for any
a\in\mathbb{N}
.
Also,
(g-1)y=y(g-1)+gx
, hence by induction

(g-1)^{a}y=y(g-1)^{a}+axg(g-1)^{a-1}
for any
a\in\mathbb{N}
.
Therefore

y\cdot M^{(n)}\subset M^{(n-2)}
; the rest is similar.
The proof of
(iii)
follows from the representation theory of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
.
∎

Let
M\in{}_{\mathcal{D}}\mathcal{SG}
. Given
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, one identifies the dual
\big(M^{(n)}\big)^{*}

with the subspace
\big(\oplus_{n\neq a\in\mathbb{Z}}M^{(a)}\big)^{\perp}=\{f\in M^{*}:f_{|M(a)}=0,a\neq n\}

of
M^{*}
. Clearly the sum of the various duals
\big(M^{(n)}\big)^{*}
is direct. The graded dual of
M
is

\displaystyle M^{\vee}

\displaystyle=\oplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\big(M^{(n)}\big)^{*}\hookrightarrow M^{*}.

We need the formula for the antipode:

(8)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} {\mathcal{S}}(g)&=g^{-1},&{\mathcal{S}}(x)&=-g^{-1}x,&{\mathcal{S}}(y)&=-g^{-1}y,\\
{\mathcal{S}}(\xi)&=-\xi,&{\mathcal{S}}(u)&=-u,&{\mathcal{S}}(v)&=-v-\frac{1}{2}\xi u.\end{aligned}

Lemma 3.3
.

If
M\in{}_{\mathcal{D}}\mathcal{SG}
, then
M^{\vee}\in{}_{\mathcal{D}}\mathcal{SG}

and

(9)

\displaystyle(M^{\vee})^{(n)}

\displaystyle=\big(M^{(-n)}\big)^{*},

\displaystyle n

\displaystyle\in\mathbb{Z}.

Proof.

Fix
q\in\mathbb{Z}
. Notice that
\big(M^{(q)}\big)^{*}
is stable under the action of
g
and
\xi
.
We claim that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} x\cdot\big(M^{(q)}\big)^{*}&\subset\big(M^{(q+2)}\big)^{*}\supset y\cdot\big(M^{(q)}\big)^{*},\\
u\cdot\big(M^{(q)}\big)^{*}&\subset\big(M^{(q-2)}\big)^{*}\supset v\cdot\big(M^{(q)}\big)^{*}.\end{aligned}

Indeed, if
f\in\big(M^{(q)}\big)^{*}
,
p\in\mathbb{Z}
and
m\in M^{(p)}
, then

\displaystyle\langle x\cdot f,m\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle f,x\cdot m\rangle=0\text{ unless }x\cdot m\in M^{(q)}\text{ that is }p-2=q.

The other inclusions are similar. Thus
M^{\vee}
is a submodule of
M^{*}
.
We next claim that
\big(M^{(q)}\big)^{*}\subset\big(M^{*}\big)^{(-q)}
.
Since
\dim M^{(q)}<\infty
,
there exist
a,b\in\mathbb{N}
such that
(\xi-q)^{a}
and
(g-1)^{b}
act by 0 on
M^{(q)}
.
Pick
f\in\big(M^{(q)}\big)^{*}
. By (
8
) we have, for any
p\in\mathbb{Z}
and
m\in M^{(p)}
, that

\displaystyle\langle(\xi+q)\cdot f,m\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle f,(-\xi+q)\cdot m\rangle

\displaystyle\implies\langle(\xi+q)^{a}\cdot f,m\rangle

\displaystyle=(-1)^{a}\langle f,(\xi-q)^{a}\cdot m\rangle=0;

\displaystyle\langle(g-1)\cdot f,m\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle f,(g^{-1}-1)\cdot m\rangle

\displaystyle\implies\langle(g-1)^{b}\cdot f,m\rangle

\displaystyle=(-1)^{b}\langle f,(g-1)^{b}\cdot(g^{-b}\cdot m)\rangle=0

The claim follows. Together with the first claim, this implies (
9
).
∎

Remark 3.4
.

If
M,N\in{}_{\mathcal{D}}\mathcal{SG}
, then
M\otimes N=\oplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(M\otimes N)^{(n)}
and

\displaystyle(M\otimes N)^{(n)}

\displaystyle=\oplus_{p+q=n}M^{(p)}\otimes N^{(q)}.

Notice however that

It suffices to show that
M^{(p)}\otimes N^{(q)}\subset(M\otimes N)^{(p+q)}
for any

; this follows from
the equalities

\displaystyle\Delta(\xi-\lambda)^{a}

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq c\leq a}\binom{a}{c}(\xi-\mu)^{c}\otimes(\xi-\nu)^{a-c},

\displaystyle\mu+\nu=\lambda;

\displaystyle\Delta(g-1)^{b}

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq d\leq b}\binom{b}{d}(g-1)^{d}\otimes g^{d}(g-1)^{b-d},

\displaystyle a,b

\displaystyle\in\mathbb{N}.

Remark 3.8

(i)

If

has highest weight

, then there is a morphism of

-modules

(ii)

with

.

(iii)

The category

is a subcategory of

.
Thus any

is a highest weigth module,

i\in\mathbb{I}_{r}
, and

\operatorname{hw-rk}M_{i}/M_{i-1}>\operatorname{hw-rk}M_{i+1}/M_{i}
,
i\in\mathbb{I}_{r-1}
.

(iv)

If
M,N\in\mathfrak{O}
, then
M\otimes N\in\mathfrak{O}
. This follows from Remark
3.4
; an
elementary argument shows that the set of pairs
(p,q)\in\varPi(M)\times\varPi(N)
such that
p+q=n
for a given
n
,
is finite when both
\varPi(M)
and
\varPi(N)
are bounded above (or below).

3.3.
Lowest weight modules

We also have the full subcategory
\mathfrak{o}
of
{}_{\mathcal{D}}\mathcal{SG}
consisting of
those
M
such that
\varPi(M)
is bounded below; for
M\in\mathfrak{o}
, we set
\operatorname{lw}M\coloneqq\inf\varPi(M)
and

\operatorname{lw-rank}M\coloneqq\dim M^{(\operatorname{lw}M)}
.
If
M\in\mathfrak{O}
, then
M^{\vee}\in\mathfrak{o}
. This gives a contravariant equivalence of categories between

\mathfrak{O}
and
\mathfrak{o}
.

Given
P\in{}_{\mathcal{D}^{0}\hskip-2.0pt}\operatorname{mod}
such that
P=P^{(n)}
for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,

P
becomes a
\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}
-module by
x\cdot m=0
,
y\cdot m=0
,
m\in P
.
The opposite Verma module is

\displaystyle\overline{\mathtt{M}}(P)\coloneqq\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}}^{\mathcal{D}}(P)\simeq\mathcal{D}\otimes_{\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}}P.

Clearly
\overline{\mathtt{M}}(P)\simeq\mathcal{D}^{>0}\otimes_{\Bbbk}P
as vector spaces.
Then
M\in\mathfrak{o}
is a
lowest weight
module if it is generated by
M^{(\operatorname{lw}M)}
.
We have the following properties.

(i)

If
M\in\mathfrak{o}
and
P:=M^{(\operatorname{lw}M)}
, then there is a morphism of
\mathcal{D}
-modules

\varphi:\overline{\mathtt{M}}(P)\to M
,
\varphi_{|P}=\operatorname{id}_{P}
.
Moreover,
\operatorname{lw}(M/\operatorname{Im}\varphi)>\operatorname{lw}M
.

(ii)

The opposite Verma modules are lowest weight modules and
any lowest weight module is the quotient of one of them.

(iii)

The category
{}_{\mathcal{D}\hskip-2.0pt}\operatorname{mod}
is a subcategory of
\mathfrak{o}
and any object belonging to
\mathfrak{o}
and
\mathfrak{O}
is in
{}_{\mathcal{D}\hskip-2.0pt}\operatorname{mod}
.
Thus any
M\in{}_{\mathcal{D}\hskip-2.0pt}\operatorname{mod}
has a unique series of submodules

0=M_{0}\subsetneq M_{1}\subsetneq M_{2}\dots\subsetneq M_{t}=M

(that we call the
lw-series
)
such that
M_{i}/M_{i-1}
is a lowest weigth module,

i\in\mathbb{I}_{t}
, and

\operatorname{lw-rank}M_{i}/M_{i-1}<\operatorname{lw-rank}M_{i+1}/M_{i}
,
i\in\mathbb{I}_{t-1}
.

(iv)

If
M,N\in\mathfrak{o}
, then
M\otimes N\in\mathfrak{o}
.

(v)

Let
P\in{}_{\mathcal{D}^{0}\hskip-2.0pt}\operatorname{mod}
such that
P=P^{(n)}
for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then
P^{*}
does not generate

\mathtt{M}(P)^{\vee}
.

(vi)

Let
P\in{}_{\mathcal{D}^{0}\hskip-2.0pt}\operatorname{mod}
such that
P=P^{(n)}
for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then
the natural map of
\mathcal{D}
-modules
\varphi:\overline{\mathtt{M}}(P)\to\mathtt{M}(P^{*})^{\vee}
is not an isomorphism.

Proof of
(v)
. Let
p\in P
,
f\in P^{*}
and
a,b\in\Bbbk
. Because of (
3
), we have

\displaystyle\langle(au+bv)\cdot f,x\cdot p\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle\cdot f,(au+bv)x\cdot p\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle f,

\displaystyle\left(x(au+b(u+v))+b(1-g)\right)\cdot p\rangle=-\langle f,b(1-g)\cdot p\rangle;

thus,
\langle(au+bv)\cdot f,x\cdot p\rangle=0
if
p\in\ker(1-g)
. Since
\ker(1-g)\neq 0
, we conclude that

(x\cdot P)^{*}
is not contained in the submodule generated by
P^{*}
.
∎

Proof of
(vi)
.
The natural isomorphism
P\simeq P^{**}
is of
\mathcal{D}_{0}
-modules because
{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathcal{D}_{0}}^{2}=\operatorname{id}
; thus

\varphi
exists. Now
\operatorname{Im}\varphi
is generated by
P^{**}
, thus
(v)
applies.
∎

4.
Modules of highest weight rank one

We aim to describe indecomposable finite-dimensional highest weight modules; we start in this section with those having
highest weight 1. We shall define an indecomposable module
\mathtt{T}(n,m)\in{}_{\mathcal{D}\hskip-2.0pt}\operatorname{mod}
for any
(n,m)\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}
.

The only Verma modules of
\operatorname{hw-rk}=1
, with the conventions of this article, are

\mathtt{M}(n)\coloneqq\mathtt{M}(\mathbf{\lambda}_{n})
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
where
\mathbf{\lambda}_{n}\in{}_{\mathcal{D}^{\geq 0}\hskip-2.0pt}\operatorname{mod}
has dimension
1
, with basis
\{z_{n}\}
and action

\displaystyle g\cdot z_{n}

\displaystyle=z_{n},

\displaystyle\xi\cdot z_{n}

\displaystyle=nz_{n},

\displaystyle u\cdot z_{n}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle v\cdot z_{n}

\displaystyle=0.

The elements
z_{n,(i,j)}\coloneqq y^{i}x^{j}\cdot z_{n}
,
i,j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, form a basis of
\mathtt{M}(n)
.
Recall that
[t]^{[k]}
denotes the raising factorial
[t]^{[k]}\coloneqq\prod_{i=1}^{k}(t+i-1)
for
t\in\Bbbk
and
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
By
[
AP
, Lemma 2.5]
the action of
\mathcal{D}
is explicitly given by

(10)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} x\cdot z_{n,(i,j)}&=\sum_{k=0}^{i}\tbinom{i}{k}\tfrac{k!}{2^{k}}z_{n,(i-k,j+k+1)},\\
y\cdot z_{n,(i,j)}&=z_{n,(i+1,j)},\\
g\cdot z_{n,(i,j)}&=\sum_{k=0}^{i}\tbinom{i}{k}\tfrac{[2]^{[k]}}{2^{k}}z_{n,(i-k,j+k)},\\
\xi\cdot z_{n,(i,j)}&=\big(n-2(i+j)\big)z_{n,(i,j)},\\
\end{aligned}

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} u\cdot z_{n,(i,j)}&=(-1)\sum_{k=1}^{i-1}\tbinom{i}{k+1}\tfrac{(k+1)!}{2^{k}}z_{n,(i-1-k,j+k)},\\
v\cdot z_{n,(i,j)}&=\tfrac{i(n-2j-i+1)}{2}z_{n,(i-1,j)}+\sum_{k=1}^{i-1}\tfrac{i!(n-j-i+1)}{(n-k-1)!2^{k+1}}z_{n,(i-1-k,j+k)}.\end{aligned}

It was shown in
[
ADP
]
that the Verma module

\mathtt{M}(n)
has a unique simple quotient
\mathtt{M}(n)/\langle z_{n,(0,1)},z_{n,(n+1,0)}\rangle
which is isomorphic
to
\mathtt{L}(n)
, cf. Theorem
2.2
.
Now
\mathtt{M}(n)
is presented by the generator
z_{n}
with defining relations

(11)

\displaystyle g\cdot z_{n}

\displaystyle=z_{n},

\displaystyle\xi\cdot z_{n}

\displaystyle=nz_{n},

\displaystyle u\cdot z_{n}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle v\cdot z_{n}

\displaystyle=0.

Similarly
\mathtt{L}(n)
is presented by
z_{n}
with defining relations (
11
) and

(12)

\displaystyle x\cdot z_{n}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle y^{n+1}\cdot z_{n}

\displaystyle=0.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
(n,m)\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}
. We define
\mathtt{T}(n,m)\coloneqq\mathtt{M}(n+2m)/\mathtt{N}(n,m)
where
\mathtt{N}(n,m)

is the submodule of
\mathtt{M}(n+2m)
generated by

(13)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} z_{n+2m,(0,m+1)}&=x^{m+1}\cdot z_{n+2m},&&\\
z_{n+2m,(n+2(m-j)+1,j)}&=y^{n+2(m-j)+1}x^{j}\cdot z_{n},&j&\in\mathbb{I}_{0,m}.\end{aligned}

Thus
\mathtt{T}(n,m)
is presented by
z=z_{n+2m}
with defining relations

(14)

\displaystyle g\cdot z_{n+2m}=z_{n+2m},

\displaystyle\xi\cdot z_{n+2m}=(n+2m)z_{n+2m},

(15)

\displaystyle u\cdot z_{n+2m}=0,

\displaystyle v\cdot z_{n+2m}=0,

(16)

\displaystyle x^{m+1}\cdot z_{n+2m}=0,

\displaystyle y^{n+2(m-j)+1}x^{j}\cdot z_{n+2m}=0,

\displaystyle j

\displaystyle\in\mathbb{I}_{0,m}.

Let
\mathtt{z}_{n,m,(i,j)}
be the image of
z_{n+2m,(i,j)}
in
\mathtt{T}(n,m)
.
For simplicity, we denote
z_{n+2m,(i,j)}
by
z_{(i,j)}
and
\mathtt{z}_{n,m,(i,j)}
by
\mathtt{z}_{(i,j)}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

The set
B=\{\mathtt{z}_{(i,j)}\colon j\in\mathbb{I}_{0,m},i\in\mathbb{I}_{0,n+2(m-j)}\}
is a basis of
\mathtt{T}(n,m)
,
thus

\dim\mathtt{T}(n,m)=(m+1)(n+m+1).

Proof.

Let
\mathtt{N}
be the vector subspace of
\mathtt{M}(n+2m)
generated by the elements

(17)

\displaystyle z_{(i,j)}

with either

\displaystyle\begin{cases}j\geq m+1&\text{ and }i\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\text{ or else}\\
0\leq j\leq m&\text{ and }i\geq n+2(m-j)+1.\end{cases}

We claim that
\mathtt{N}(n,m)=\mathtt{N}
; clearly this equality implies the Lemma.
For this claim, since the generators (
13
) belong to
\mathtt{N}
,
we are reduced to prove

(i)

\mathtt{N}(n,m)\supset\mathtt{N}
.

(ii)

\mathtt{N}
is a submodule of
\mathtt{M}(n+2m)
,

(i)
is clear: if
j\geq m+1
, then
z_{(i,j)}=y^{i}x^{j-m-1}\cdot z_{(0,m+1)}
; if

j\in\mathbb{I}_{0,m}
and
i\geq n+2(m-j)+1
, then
z_{(i,j)}=y^{i-n-2(m-j)-1}\cdot z_{(n+2(m-j)+1,j)}
.

(ii)
:
We use the formulas (
10
) to show that the generators leave
\mathtt{N}
invariant.
Fix
z_{(i_{0},j_{0})}
as in (
17
). If
j_{0}\geq m+1
, then by
(
10
) the actions of the generators involve linear combinations of
z_{(i,j)}
with
j\geq j_{0}
hence they are in
\mathtt{N}
.
So we assume that
0\leq j_{0}\leq m
and
i_{0}\geq n+2(m-j_{0})+1
.

Action of
x
on
z_{(i_{0},j_{0})}

Here
x\cdot z_{(i_{0},j_{0})}
is a linear combination of elements of the form
z_{(i_{0}-k,j_{0}+k+1)}
with
k\in\mathbb{I}_{0,i_{0}}
. If
j_{0}+k+1\geq m+1
, then we are done. Otherwise,

\displaystyle i_{0}-k

\displaystyle\geq n+2m-2j_{0}+1-k\geq n+2m-2(j_{0}+k+1)+1.

Action of
y
or
\xi
on
z_{(i_{0},j_{0})}

This is clear.

Action of
g
on
z_{(i_{0},j_{0})}

Here
g\cdot z_{(i_{0},j_{0})}
is a linear combination of elements of the form
z_{(i_{0}-k,j_{0}+k)}
with
k\in\mathbb{I}_{0,i_{0}}
. If
j_{0}+k\geq m+1
, then we are done. Otherwise,

\displaystyle i_{0}-k

\displaystyle\geq n+2m-2j_{0}+1-k\geq n+2m-2(j_{0}+k)+1.

Action of
u
on
z_{(i_{0},j_{0})}

Here
u\cdot z_{(i_{0},j_{0})}
is a linear combination of elements of the form

z_{(i_{0}-k-1,j_{0}+k)}
with
k\in\mathbb{I}_{i_{0}-1}
. If
j_{0}+k\geq m+1
, then we are done. Otherwise,

\displaystyle i_{0}-k-1

\displaystyle\geq n+2m-2j_{0}+1-k-1\geq n+2m-2(j_{0}+k)+1.

Action of
v
on
z_{(i_{0},j_{0})}

Here
v\cdot z_{(i_{0},j_{0})}
is a linear combination of

z_{(i_{0}-1,j_{0})})
and elements of the form
z_{(i_{0}-k-1,j_{0}+k)}
with
k\in\mathbb{I}_{i_{0}-1}
.
We begin with the latter case. If
j_{0}+k\geq m+1
, then we are done. Otherwise,

\displaystyle i_{0}-1-k

\displaystyle\geq n+2m-2j_{0}+1-1-k\geq n+2m-2(j_{0}+k)+1.

Now for
z(i_{0}-1,j_{0})
, if
i_{0}=n+2(m-j_{0})+1
then the coefficient that goes with
z(i_{0}-1,j_{0})
is zero. So we can assume
i_{0}>n+2(m-j_{0})+1
. Then
i_{0}-1\geq n+2(m-j_{0})+1
and we are done.
∎

Clearly,
\mathtt{T}(n,0)\simeq\mathtt{L}(n)
.

Lemma 4.3
.

The linear map
\psi:\mathtt{L}(n)\hookrightarrow\mathtt{T}(n,m)
given by

z_{n,i}\mapsto\mathtt{z}_{(i,m)}
for
i\in\mathbb{I}_{0,n}
, is a monomorphism of
\mathcal{D}
-modules.
Let
\mathtt{L}\coloneqq\operatorname{Im}\psi
. If
m\geq 1
, then

\mathtt{T}(n,m)/\mathtt{L}\simeq\mathtt{T}(n+2,m-1).

Proof.

Using the formulas (
10
) we see first that
\mathtt{z}_{(0,m)}
satisfies the defining relations
(
11
) and (
12
) of
\mathtt{L}(n)
, implying the existence of
\psi

with the desired properties. Second, we see that the class of
\mathtt{z}_{(0,0)}
in
\mathtt{T}(n,m)/\mathtt{L}

satisfies the defining relations
(
14
), (
15
) and (
16
) of
\mathtt{T}(n+2,m-1)
.
Thus we have an epimorphism
\mathtt{T}(n+2,m-1)\twoheadrightarrow\mathtt{T}(n,m)/\mathtt{L}
which is an isomorphism
because
\dim\mathtt{T}(n+2,m-1)=m(n+m+2)=\dim\mathtt{T}(n,m)/\mathtt{L}
.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
N
be a non-zero submodule of
\mathtt{T}(n,m)
. Then
\mathtt{L}\subseteq N
.

Proof.

We first show that for every
j\in\mathbb{I}_{m}
and
i\in\mathbb{I}_{0,n+2(m-j)}
we have

(18)

\displaystyle v^{i}\cdot\mathtt{z}_{(i,j)}=\frac{(i!)^{2}}{2^{i}}\binom{n+2(m-j)}{i}\mathtt{z}_{(0,j)}.

We argue recursively on
i
. For
i=0
the equality is clear. Suppose that

\displaystyle v^{\ell}\cdot\mathtt{z}_{(\ell,j)}=\frac{(\ell!)^{2}}{2^{\ell}}\binom{n+2(m-j)}{\ell}\mathtt{z}_{(0,j)},\qquad\text{ for }\ell\in\mathbb{I}_{0,i}.

Using (
10
) we see that
v^{i+1}\cdot\mathtt{z}_{(i+1,j)}
is equal to

\displaystyle\tfrac{(i+1)(n+2(m-j)-i)}{2}v^{i}\mathtt{z}_{(i,j)}+\sum_{k=1}^{i}\tbinom{i+1}{k+1}\tfrac{(n+2m-j-i)(k+1)!}{2^{k+1}}v^{k}v^{i-k}\cdot\mathtt{z}_{(i-k,j+k)}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{(i+1)(n+2(m-j)-i)(i!)^{2}}{2^{i+1}}\tbinom{n+2(m-j)}{i}\mathtt{z}_{(0,j)}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{i}\tbinom{i+1}{k+1}\tfrac{(n+2m-j-i)(k+1)!((i-k)!)^{2}}{2^{i+1}}\tbinom{n+2(m-j-k)}{i-k}v^{k}\mathtt{z}_{(0,j+k)}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{((i+1)!)^{2}}{2^{i+1}}\tbinom{n+2(m-j)}{i+1}\mathtt{z}_{(0,j)}.

Clearly (
18
) implies that
v^{k}\cdot\mathtt{z}_{(i,j)}=0
for
k>i
.
Now let
z\in N-0
. Then
z=\sum_{i,j}c_{i,j}\mathtt{z}_{(i,j)}
for some
c_{i,j}\in\Bbbk
. Let
i_{0}=\max\{i\colon c_{i,j}\neq 0\}
. Then

\displaystyle v^{i_{0}}\cdot z=\sum_{j}c_{i_{0},j}\frac{(i_{0}!)^{2}}{2^{i_{0}}}\binom{n+2(m-j)}{i_{0}}\mathtt{z}_{(0,j)}\in N.

Taking
j_{0}=\max\{j\colon c_{i_{0},j}\neq 0\}
we get that

\displaystyle x^{m-j_{0}}v^{i_{0}}\cdot z

\displaystyle=c_{i_{0},j_{0}}\frac{(i_{0}!)^{2}}{2^{i_{0}}}\binom{n+2(m-j_{0})}{i_{0}}\mathtt{z}_{(0,m)}\in N.

Hence
\mathtt{z}_{(0,m)}\in N
and since
\mathtt{L}
is simple, this shows that
\mathtt{L}\subseteq N
.
∎

Proposition 4.5
.

The module
\mathtt{T}(n,m)
is uniserial and indecomposable.

Proof.

If
N
is a simple submodule of
\mathtt{T}(n,m)
, then
N=\mathtt{L}
by Lemma
4.4
, thus
\mathtt{L}
is the socle of
\mathtt{T}(n,m)
.
We conclude from Lemma
4.3
that the socle series is a composition series, hence

\mathtt{T}(n,m)
is uniserial and indecomposable.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

The dual module
\mathtt{T}(n,m)^{*}
is also uniserial and indecomposable but it is not a highest weight module;
the subfactors of its hw-series are the simple modules
\mathtt{L}(n)
etc.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
\mathtt{T}
be an indecomposable finite-dimensional highest weight module with
\operatorname{hw-rk}\mathtt{T}=1
. Then
\mathtt{T}\simeq\mathtt{T}(n,m)

for some
(n,m)\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

Proof.

Assume that
\operatorname{hw}\mathtt{T}=p\in\mathbb{Z}
. Since
\mathtt{T}

is generated by
\mathtt{T}^{(p)}
,
\mathtt{T}
is a quotient of
\mathtt{M}(p)
.
Fix
\mathtt{z}\in\mathtt{T}^{(p)}-0
. Since
\operatorname{hw-rk}\mathtt{T}=1
,
\mathtt{z}

generates
\mathtt{T}
,
\xi\cdot\mathtt{z}=p\mathtt{z}
and
g\cdot\mathtt{z}=\mathtt{z}
.
Given a simple quotient
L
of
\mathtt{T}
,
L
is then a (finite-dimensional) simple quotient of
\mathtt{M}(p)
. Then
p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
L\simeq L(p)
as in
[
ADP
, 3.11]
.

By Proposition
2.1
,
x^{m}\cdot\mathtt{z}\neq 0
and
x^{m+1}\cdot\mathtt{z}=0

for some
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
Let
n\coloneqq p-2m
. Applying the relations (
10
), we see that
the submodule generated by
x^{m}\cdot\mathtt{z}
is a quotient of
M(n)
; hence
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
arguing as above.

Let
j\in\mathbb{I}_{0,m}
. By Remark
2.3
there exists
a_{j}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that

y^{a_{j}}x^{j}\cdot\mathtt{z}\neq 0
,
y^{a_{j}+1}x^{j}\cdot\mathtt{z}=0
.
Then the last commutation relation in (
10
) says that

(19)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} 0=vy^{a_{j}+1}x^{j}\cdot\mathtt{z}&=\tfrac{(a_{j}+1)(p-2j-a_{j})}{2}y^{a_{j}}x^{j}\cdot\mathtt{z}\\
&\qquad+\sum_{k=1}^{a_{j}}\tfrac{(a_{j}+1)!(p-j-a_{j})}{(p-k-1)!2^{k+1}}y^{a_{j}-k}x^{j+k}\cdot\mathtt{z}.\end{aligned}

Let
I=\{k\in\mathbb{I}_{0,a_{j}}:y^{a_{j}-k}x^{j+k}\cdot\mathtt{z}\neq 0\}
. Then
\{y^{a_{j}-k}x^{j+k}\cdot\mathtt{z}:k\in I\}
is linearly independent. Indeed if
\sum_{k\in I}c_{k}y^{a_{j}-k}x^{j+k}\cdot\mathtt{z}=0
, then applying
y
enough times one can show that each
c_{k}
should be zero. Then equation (
19
) tell us that
p-2j-a_{j}=0
since
0\in I
. Then
a_{j}=p-2j=n-2(m-j)
by definition of
n
. Hence
\mathtt{T}
is a quotient of
\mathtt{T}(n,m)
. Let
\mathtt{z}_{i,j}\coloneqq y^{i}x^{j}\cdot\mathtt{z}
. By an argument similar to the one given in Lemma
4.2
, one showa that the elements
\{\mathtt{z}_{i,j}\}
form a basis of
\mathtt{T}
, hence
\mathtt{T}\simeq\mathtt{T}(n,m)
.
∎

Remark 4.8
.

The previous result shows that the requirement in Definition
3.7
that highest weight modules
are generated by their highest weight subspaces is necessary.
Indeed, otherwise there would be many more
indecomposables than in Proposition
4.7
. For instance
let
\widetilde{\mathtt{T}}=\mathtt{T}(n,m)\oplus S_{\gamma}(n)
,
where
m\geq 1
and
S_{\gamma}(n)
is the indecomposable module defined in
Proposition
5.2
, let
N
be the submodule generated by the
elements
s_{i}-\mathtt{z}_{i,m}
, and let
\mathtt{T}=\widetilde{\mathtt{T}}/N
.
Then
\mathtt{T}
is easily seen to be indecomposable since it satisfies a
property similar to the one proven in Lemma
4.4
.
It satisfy
\operatorname{hw-rk}\mathtt{T}=1
, but it is not isomorphic to any
\mathtt{T}(n^{\prime},m^{\prime})

since it has two copies of
\mathtt{L}(n)
as composition factors.

5.
Modules of highest weight rank two

In this Section we introduce a family
\mathtt{S}_{\gamma}(n)
of highest weight modules of
\operatorname{hw-rk}2
and use it to classify self extensions of simple modules.

5.1.
Highest weight rank 2

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
(\lambda,\mu)\in\Bbbk^{2}
.
We consider the
\mathcal{D}^{0}
-module
\mathtt{P}_{\lambda,\mu}(n)
with basis
\mathtt{s},\mathtt{w}
and action

(20)

\displaystyle g\cdot\mathtt{s}

\displaystyle=\mathtt{s},

\displaystyle g\cdot\mathtt{w}

\displaystyle=\mathtt{w}+\lambda\mathtt{s},

\displaystyle\xi\cdot\mathtt{s}

\displaystyle=n\mathtt{s},

\displaystyle\xi\cdot\mathtt{w}

\displaystyle=n\mathtt{w}+\mu\mathtt{s}.

The module
\mathtt{P}_{\lambda,\mu}(n)
is isomorphic to

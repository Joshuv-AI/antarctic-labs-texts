Distribution of 3-regular and 5-regular partitions
QI-YANG ZHENG

Abstract
. In this paper we study the function b 3 ​ ( n ) b_{3}(n) and b 5 ​ ( n ) b_{5}(n) , which denote the number of 3 3 -regular partitions and 5 5 -regular partitions of n n respectively. Using the theory of modular forms, we prove several arithmetic properties of b 3 ​ ( n ) b_{3}(n) and b 5 ​ ( n ) b_{5}(n) modulo primes greater than 3 3 .



1.
Introduction

The number of partitions of
n
in which no parts are multiples of
k
is denoted by
b_{k}(n)
, the
k
-regular partitions.
b_{k}(n)
is also the number of partitions of
n
into at most
k-1
copies of each part.

We agree that
b_{3}(0)=b_{5}(0)=1
for convenience. Moreover, let
b_{3}(n)=b_{5}(n)=0
if
n\not\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. The
k
-regular partitions has generating function as follows:

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{k}(n)q^{n}=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1-q^{kn}}{1-q^{n}}.

In 1919, Ramanujan found three remarkable congruences of
p(n)
as follows

\displaystyle p(5n+4)

\displaystyle\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 5),

\displaystyle p(7n+5)

\displaystyle\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 7),

\displaystyle p(11n+6)

\displaystyle\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 11).

In 2000, Ono
[
9
]
proved that for each prime number
m\geq 5
, there exists infinitely many arithmetic sequences
An+B
such that

p(An+B)\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ m).

We call such congruences Ramanujan-type congruences. Subsequently Lovejoy
[
6
]
gave similar results for the function
Q(n)
, the number of partitions of
n
into distinct parts. Following strategies of Ono and Lovejoy, we prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

For each prime
m\geq 5
, there are infinitely many Ramanujan-type congruences of
b_{3}(n)
and
b_{5}(n)
modulo
m
.

Lovejoy and Penniston
[
7
]
study the distribution of
b_{3}(n)
modulo
3
. Recently, Keith and Zanello
[
5
]
study the parity of
b_{3}(n)
. As for the
5
-regular partitions, Calkin et al.
[
1
]
, Hirschhorn and Sellers
[
4
]
study the parity of
b_{5}(n)
. Gordon and Ono
[
3
]
study the distribution of
b_{5}(n)
modulo
5
. Moreover, they prove that

(1.1)

b_{5}(5n+4)\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 5).

Up to now, we only know the distribution of
b_{3}(n)
and
b_{5}(n)
modulo primes mentioned above. In this paper, we study the distribution of
b_{3}(n)
and
b_{5}(n)
modulo primes
m\geq 5
. It is noteworthy that we still do not know anything about
b_{5}(n)
modulo
3
.

As a natural corollary of Theorem
1.1
, we have

Corollary 1.2
.

If
m\geq 5
is a prime and
k=3,5
, then there are infinitely many positive integers
n
for which

b_{k}(n)\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ m).

More precisely, we have

\#\{0\leq n\leq X\ :\ b_{k}(n)\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ m)\}\gg X.

For other residue classes
i\not\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ m)
, we provide a useful criterion to verify whether there are infinitely many
n
such that
b_{k}(n)\equiv i\ (\mathrm{mod}\ m)
.

Proposition 1.3
.

If
m\geq 5
is a prime and there is one
k\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

b_{3}\left(mk+\frac{m^{2}-1}{12}\right)\equiv e\not\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ m),

then for each
i=1,2,\cdots,m-1
, we have

\#\{0\leq n\leq X\ :\ b_{3}(n)\equiv i\ (\mathrm{mod}\ m)\}\gg\frac{X}{\log X}.

Moreover, if such
k
exists, then
k<18(m-1)
.

We obtain similar results for
b_{5}(n)
.

Proposition 1.4
.

Let
m\geq 5
be a prime. If there exists one
k\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

b_{5}\left(mk+\frac{m^{2}-1}{6}\right)\equiv e\not\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ m),

then for each
i=1,2,\cdots,m-1
, we have

\#\{0\leq n\leq X\ |\ b_{5}(n)\equiv i\ (\mathrm{mod}\ m)\}\gg\frac{X}{\log X}.

Moreover, if such
k
exists, then
k<10(m-1)
.

Remark
.

The congruence (
1.1
) show that our criterion is inapplicable for the case
m=5
. However the case
m=5
is studied in
[
3
]
.

2.
Preliminaries on modular forms

First we introduce the
U
operator. If
j
is a positive integer, then

\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a(n)q^{n}\right)\ |\ U(j):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a(jn)q^{n}.

Recalling that Dedekind’s eta function is defined by

\eta(z)=q^{\frac{1}{24}}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{n}),

where
q=e^{2\pi iz}
.

If
m
is a prime, then let
M_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)_{m}
(resp.
S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)_{m}
) denote the
\mathbb{F}_{m}
-vector space of the reductions mod
m
of the
q
-expansions of modular forms(resp. cusp forms) in
M_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
(resp.
S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
) with integer coefficients.

Sometimes, we will use the notation
a\equiv_{m}b
in the place of
a\equiv b\ (\mathrm{mod}\ m)
for convenience.

We need the following theorem to construct modular forms
[
2
, Theorem 3]
:

Theorem 2.1

(B. Gordon, K. Hughes)
.

Let

f(z)=\prod_{\delta|N}\eta^{r_{\delta}}(\delta z)

be a
\eta
-quotient provided

\mathrm{(i)}

\sum_{\delta|N}\delta r_{\delta}\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 24);

\mathrm{(ii)}

\sum_{\delta|N}\frac{Nr_{\delta}}{\delta}\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 24);

\mathrm{(iii)}

k:=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\delta|N}r_{\delta}\in\mathbb{Z},

then

f\left(\frac{az+b}{cz+d}\right)=\chi(d)(cz+d)^{k}f(z),

for each
\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma_{0}(N)
and
\chi
is a Dirichlet character
(\mathrm{mod}\ N)
defined by

\chi(n):=\left(\frac{(-1)^{k}\prod_{\delta|N}\delta^{r_{\delta}}}{n}\right),\ if\ n>0\ and\ (n,6)=1.

If
f(z)
is holomorphic (resp. vanishes) at all cusps of
\Gamma_{0}(N)
, then
f(z)\in M_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
(resp.
S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
), since
\eta(z)
is never vanishes on
\mathcal{H}
. The following theorem (c.f.
[
8
]
) provide a useful criterion for compute the orders of an
\eta
-quotient at all cusps of
\Gamma_{0}(N)
.

Theorem 2.2

(Y. Martin)
.

Let
c
,
d
and
N
be positive integers with
d\,|\,N
and
(c,d)=1
. If
f(z)
is an
\eta
-quotient satisfying the conditions of Theorem
2.1
, then the order of vanishing of
f(z)
at the cusp
c/d
is

\frac{N}{24}\sum_{\delta|N}\frac{r_{\delta}(d^{2},\delta^{2})}{\delta(d^{2},N)}.

3.
Ramanujan-type congruences

In this section, we will prove Theorem
1.1
via theory of modular forms. However, the generating function of regular partition function is not a modular form. But for primes
m\geq 5
, it turns out that for a properly chosen function
h_{m}(n)
, then

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{k}(h_{m}(n))q^{n}

is the Fourier expansion of a cusp form modulo
m
. In fact, we have

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
m\geq 5
be a prime, then

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{3}\left(\frac{mn-1}{12}\right)q^{n}\in S_{3m-3}(\Gamma_{0}(432),\chi_{12})_{m},

where
\chi_{12}(n)=\left(\frac{n}{3}\right)\left(\frac{-4}{n}\right)
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
m\geq 5
be a prime, then

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{5}\left(\frac{mn-1}{6}\right)q^{n}\in S_{2m-2}(\Gamma_{0}(180),\chi_{5})_{m},

where
\chi_{5}(n)=\left(\frac{n}{5}\right)
.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

We begin with an
\eta
-quotient

f(m;z):=\frac{\eta(3z)}{\eta(z)}\eta^{a}(3mz)\eta^{b}(mz),

where
m^{\prime}:=(m\ \mathrm{mod}\ 12)
,
a:=9-m^{\prime}
and
b:=m^{\prime}-3
.

It is easy to verify that
f(m;z)\equiv_{m}\eta^{am+1}(3z)\eta^{bm-1}(z)
satisfies the conditions of Theorem
2.1
. Moreover, one can compute via Theorem
2.2
that
\eta^{am+1}(3z)\eta^{bm-1}(z)
has the minimal order of vanishing of
(m(3a+b)+2)/24
at the cusp
\infty
and
(m(a+3b)-2)/24
at the cusp
0
.

Since
(m(3a+b)+2)/24=(m(12-m^{\prime})+1)/12>0
and
(m(a+3b)-2)/24=(mm^{\prime}-1)/12>0
,
\eta^{am+1}(3z)\eta^{bm-1}(z)\in S_{3m}(\Gamma_{0}(3),\chi_{3})
, where
\chi_{3}(n)=\left(\frac{n}{3}\right)
. On the other hand,

f(m;z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{3}(n)q^{n+\frac{m(3a+b)+2}{24}}\cdot\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{3mn})^{a}(1-q^{mn})^{b}.

Thus,

(3.1)

\displaystyle\eta^{am+1}(3z)\eta^{bm-1}(z)\ |\ U(m)

\displaystyle\equiv_{m}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{3}(n)q^{n+\frac{m(3a+b)+2}{24}}\ |\ U(m)\right)\cdot\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{3n})^{a}(1-q^{n})^{b}.

As for LHS of (
3.1
),

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{3}(n)q^{n+\frac{m(3a+b)+2}{24}}\ |\ U(m)={\sum_{n\geq 0}}^{*}b_{3}(n)q^{\frac{24n+m(3a+b)+2}{24m}},

where
{\sum}^{*}
means take integral power coefficients of
q
, i.e.

24n+m(3a+b)+2\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 24m).

It is easy to check that
24\,|\,24n+m(3a+b)+2
. Thus the condition becomes
m\,|\,12n+1
.

As for RHS of (
3.1
), we have

\eta^{am+1}(3z)\eta^{bm-1}(z)\ |\ U(m)\equiv_{m}\eta^{am+1}(3z)\eta^{bm-1}(z)\ |\ T(m),

where
T(m)
denotes usual Hecke operator acting on
S_{3m}(\Gamma_{0}(3),\chi_{3})
.

Now we analyze the
\eta
-product
\eta^{6}(z)\eta^{6}(3z)
. By Theorem
2.1
and Theorem
2.2
,
\eta^{6}(z)\eta^{6}(3z)
is a cusp form of weight
6
and level
3
and has the minimal order of vanishing of
1
at the two cusps of
\Gamma_{0}(3)
. Since
\eta(z)
never vanishes on
\mathcal{H}
, we can write
\eta^{am+1}(3z)\eta^{bm-1}(z)\ |\ T(m)=\eta^{6}(z)\eta^{6}(3z)g(m;z)
, where
g(m;z)\in M_{3m-6}(\Gamma_{0}(3),\chi_{3})
.

In summary, we have

(3.2)

\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n\geq 0}{m|12n+1}}b_{3}(n)q^{\frac{24n+m(3a+b)+2}{24m}}\equiv_{m}\frac{\eta^{6}(z)\eta^{6}(3z)g(m;z)}{\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{3n})^{a}(1-q^{n})^{b}}.

Replacing
q
by
q^{12}
and then multiplying by
q^{-(3a+b)/2}
on both sides of (
3.2
), obtaining

\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n\geq 0}{m|12n+1}}b_{3}(n)q^{\frac{12n+1}{m}}\equiv_{m}\eta^{6-a}(36z)\eta^{6-b}(12z)g(m;12z),

namely,

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{3}\left(\frac{mn-1}{12}\right)q^{n}\equiv_{m}\eta^{6-a}(36z)\eta^{6-b}(12z)g(m;12z).

Using Theorem
2.1
and Theorem
2.2
again, one can verity that
\eta^{6-a}(36z)\eta^{6-b}(12z)
is a cusp form of weight
3
and level
432
and has the minimal order of vanishing of
m^{\prime}
at the cusps
c/d
if
d=1,2,3,4,6,8,12,16,24,48
and
12-m^{\prime}
if
d
is other divisor of
432
.

Therefore we obtain

\eta^{6-a}(36z)\eta^{6-b}(12z)\in S_{3}\left(\Gamma_{0}(432),\chi_{4}\right),

where
\chi_{4}(n)=\left(\frac{-3}{n}\right)
. Together with
g(m;12z)\in M_{3m-6}(\Gamma_{0}(36),\chi_{3})
, we have

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{3}\left(\frac{mn-1}{12}\right)q^{n}\in S_{3m-3}(\Gamma_{0}(432),\chi_{12})_{m}.

∎

Proof of Theorem
3.2
.

For a fixed prime
m
, let

f(m;z):=\frac{\eta(5z)}{\eta(z)}\eta^{a}(5mz)\eta^{b}(mz),

where
m^{\prime}:=(m\ \mathrm{mod}\ 6)
and
a:=5-m^{\prime},\ b:=m^{\prime}-1
. It is easy to show that
f(m;z)\equiv\eta^{am+1}(5z)\eta^{bm-1}(z)\ \left(\mathrm{mod}\ m\right)
and

\eta^{am+1}(5z)\eta^{bm-1}(z)\in S_{2m}(\Gamma_{0}(5),\chi_{5}),

where
\chi_{5}(n)=\left(\frac{n}{5}\right)
. On the other hand,

f(m;z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{5}(n)q^{\frac{24n+m(5a+b)+4}{24}}\cdot\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{5mn})^{a}(1-q^{mn})^{b}.

Acting the
U(m)
operator on
f(z)
and since
U(m)\equiv T(m)\ \left(\mathrm{mod}\ m\right)
, obtaining

(3.3)

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{5}(n)q^{\frac{24n+m(5a+b)+4}{24}}\ |\ U(m)\equiv\frac{\eta^{am+1}(5z)\eta^{bm-1}(z)\ |\ T(m)}{\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{5n})^{a}(1-q^{n})^{b}}\ \left(\mathrm{mod}\ m\right),

where
T(m)
denotes usual Hecke operator acting on
S_{2m}(\Gamma_{0}(5),\chi_{5})
. As for the LHS of (
3.3
), we have

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{5}(n)q^{\frac{24n+m(5a+b)+4}{24}}\ |\ U(m)=\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n=0}{m|6n+1}}^{\infty}b_{5}(n)q^{\frac{24n+m(5a+b)+4}{24m}}.

Using Theorem
2.1
and
2.2
, one can verify that
\eta^{4}(5z)\eta^{4}(z)\in S_{4}(\Gamma_{0}(5))
and have the order of
1
at all cusps. Thus we can write
\eta^{am+1}(5z)\eta^{bm-1}(z)\ |\ T(m)=\eta^{4}(5z)\eta^{4}(z)g(m;z)
, where
g(m;z)\in M_{2m-4}(\Gamma_{0}(5),\chi_{5})
. Hence

\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n=0}{m|6n+1}}^{\infty}b_{5}(n)q^{\frac{6n+1}{6m}}\equiv\eta^{4-a}(5z)\eta^{4-b}(z)g(m;z)\ \left(\mathrm{mod}\ m\right).

Replacing
q
by
q^{6}
shows that

\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n=0}{m|6n+1}}^{\infty}b_{5}(n)q^{\frac{6n+1}{m}}\equiv\eta^{4-a}(30z)\eta^{4-b}(6z)g(m;6z)\ \left(\mathrm{mod}\ m\right).

Since
b_{5}(n)
vanishes for non-integer
n
, so

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{5}\left(\frac{mn-1}{6}\right)q^{n}\equiv\eta^{4-a}(30z)\eta^{4-b}(6z)g(m;6z)\ \left(\mathrm{mod}\ m\right).

Moreover, one can verify that
\eta^{4-a}(30z)\eta^{4-b}(6z)\in S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(180))
. Together with
g(m;6z)\in M_{2m-4}(\Gamma_{0}(30),\chi_{5})
, we have

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{5}\left(\frac{mn-1}{6}\right)q^{n}\in S_{2m-2}(\Gamma_{0}(180),\chi_{5})_{m}.

∎

We need some important results due to Serre (c.f.
[
10
, (6.4)]
), which are the critical factors of the existence of Ramanujan-type congruences.

Theorem 3.3

(J.-P. Serre)
.

The set of primes
l\equiv-1\ (\mathrm{mod}\ Nm)
such that

f\ |\ T(l)\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ m)

for each
f(z)\in S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\psi)_{m}
has positive density, where
T(l)
denotes the usual Hecke operator acting on
S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\psi)
.

Now Theorem
1.1
is an immediately corollary of the next two theorems.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
m\geq 5
be a prime. A positive density of the primes
l
have the property that

b_{3}\left(\frac{mln-1}{12}\right)\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ m)

for each nonnegative integer
n
coprime to
l
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
m\geq 5
be a prime. Then a positive density of primes
l
have the property that

b_{5}\left(\frac{mln-1}{6}\right)\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ m)

satisfied for each integer
n
coprime to
l
.

Proof of Theorem
3.4
.

Let

F(m;z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{3}\left(\frac{mn-1}{12}\right)q^{n},

then
F(m;z)\in S_{3m-3}(\Gamma_{0}(432),\chi_{12})_{m}
.

For a fix prime
m\geq 5
, let
S(m)
denote the of primes
l
such that

f\ |\ T(l)\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ m)

for each
f\in S_{3m-3}(\Gamma_{0}(432),\chi_{12})
. By Theorem
3.3
,
S(m)
contains a positive density of primes. So if
l\in S(m)
, we have

F(m;z)\ |\ T(l)\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ m).

Then by the theory of Hecke operator we have

F(m;z)\ |\ T(l)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(b_{3}\left(\frac{mln-1}{12}\right)+\left(\frac{3}{l}\right)l^{3m-4}b_{3}\left(\frac{mn-l}{12l}\right)\right)q^{n}\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ m).

Since
b_{3}(n)
vanishes when
n
is not an integer,
b_{3}\left((mn-l)/12l\right)=0
for each
n
coprime to
l
and
l\neq m
. Thus

b_{3}\left(\frac{mln-1}{12}\right)\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ m)

satisfied for each integer
n
coprime to
l
with
l\neq m
. Moreover, the set of such primes
l
has a positive density of primes.

∎

Proof of Theorem
3.5
.

Let

F(m;z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{5}\left(\frac{mn-1}{6}\right)q^{n}\in S_{2m-2}(\Gamma_{0}(180),\chi_{5})_{m}.

By Theorem
3.3
, the set of primes
l
such that

F(m;z)\ |\ T(l)\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ m)

has positive density, where
T(l)
denotes Hecke operator acting on
S_{2m-2}(\Gamma_{0}(180),\chi_{5})
. Moreover, by the theory of Hecke operator, we have

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}F(m;z)\ |\ T(l)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(b_{5}\left(\frac{mln-1}{6}\right)+\left(\frac{l}{5}\right)l^{2m-3}b_{5}\left(\frac{mn-l}{6l}\right)\right)q^{n}.

Since
b_{5}(n)
vanishes for non-integer
n
,
b_{5}((mn-l)/6l)=0
when
(n,l)=1
and
l\neq m
. Thus we obtain

b_{5}\left(\frac{mln-1}{6}\right)\equiv 0\ \left(\mathrm{mod}\ m\right)

satisfied for each integer
n
with
(n,l)=1
and
l\neq m
. Moreover, the set of such primes
l
has a positive density of primes.

∎

Since the number of selections of
l
is infinite, choose
l>3
. Replacing
n
by
12nl+ml+12
, then we have
b_{3}(ml^{2}n+ml+(m^{2}l^{2}-1)/12)\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ m)
satisfied for each nonnegative integer

. Similar way can be applied to

. Hence we obtain Theorem
1.1
. Moreover, since the choices of

is infinite, together with the Chinese Remainder Theorem and previous results, we obtain

Corollary 3.6
.

If

is a squarefree integer, then there are infinitely many Ramanujan-type congruences of

modulo
m
; if
k
is a squarefree integer coprime to

, then there are infinitely many Ramanujan-type congruences of

modulo

.

4.
Distribution on nonzero residues

Following Lovejoy
[
6
]
, we need the following theorem due to Serre
[
10
]
.

Theorem 4.1

(J.-P. Serre)
.

The set of primes
l\equiv 1\ (\mathrm{mod}\ Nm)
such that

a(nl^{r})\equiv(r+1)a(n)\ (\mathrm{mod}\ m)

for each
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a(n)q^{n}\in S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\psi)_{m}
has positive density, where
r
is a positive integer and
n
is coprime to
l
.

Here we introduce a theorem of Sturm(Theorem 1 of
[
11
]
), which provide a useful criterion for deciding when modular forms with integer coefficients are congruent to zero modulo a prime via finite computation.

Theorem 4.2

(J. Sturm)
.

Suppose
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a(n)q^{n}\in M_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)_{m}
such that

a(n)\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ m)

for all
n\leq\frac{kN}{12}\prod_{p|N}\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right)
. Then
a(n)\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ m)
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

If there is one
k\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

b_{3}\left(mk+\frac{m^{2}-1}{12}\right)\equiv e\not\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ m),

let
s=12k+m
. Since
b_{3}(n)
vanishes for negative
n
, we have
mk+\frac{m^{2}-1}{12}\geq 0
. Hence
s=12k+m>0
and

b_{3}\left(\frac{ms-1}{12}\right)=b_{3}\left(mk+\frac{m^{2}-1}{12}\right)\equiv e\ (\mathrm{mod}\ m).

For a fix prime
m\geq 5
, let
R(m)
denote the set of primes
l
such that

a(nl^{r})\equiv(r+1)a(n)\ (\mathrm{mod}\ m)

for each
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a(n)q^{n}\in S_{3m-3}(\Gamma_{0}(432),\chi_{12})_{m}
, where
r
is a positive integer and
n
is coprime to
l
. By the proof of Theorem
3.4
we have
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{3}\left(\frac{mn-1}{12}\right)q^{n}\in S_{3m-3}(\Gamma_{0}(432),\chi_{12})_{m}
. Since
R(m)
is infinite by Theorem
4.1
, choose
l\in R(m)
such that
l>s
, then

b_{3}\left(\frac{ml^{r}s-1}{12}\right)\equiv(r+1)b_{3}\left(\frac{ms-1}{12}\right)\equiv(r+1)e\ (\mathrm{mod}\ m).

Now we fix

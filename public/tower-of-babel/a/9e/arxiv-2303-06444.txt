Complete Description of Invariant, Associative Pseudo-Euclidean Metrics on Left Leibniz Algebras via Quadratic Lie Algebras
Fatima-Ezzahrae Abid, Mohamed Boucetta

Abstract
A pseudo-Euclidean non-associative algebra ( 𝔤 , ∙ ) (\mathfrak{g},\bullet) is a real algebra of finite dimension that has a metric, i.e., a bilinear, symmetric, and non-degenerate form ⟨ , ⟩ \langle\;,\;\rangle . The metric is considered L {\mathrm{L}} -invariant (resp. R {\mathrm{R}} -invariant) if all left multiplications (resp. right multiplications) are skew-symmetric. The metric is called associative if ⟨ u ∙ v , w ⟩ = ⟨ u , v ∙ w ⟩ \langle u\bullet v,w\rangle=\langle u,v\bullet w\rangle for all u , v , w ∈ 𝔤 u,v,w\in\mathfrak{g} . These three notions coincide when 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} is a Lie algebra and in this case 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} endowed with the metric is known as a quadratic Lie algebra. This paper provides a complete description of L {\mathrm{L}} -invariant, R {\mathrm{R}} -invariant, or associative pseudo-Euclidean metrics on left Leibniz algebras. It shows that a left Leibniz algebra with an associative metric is also right Leibniz and can be obtained easily from its underlying Lie algebra, which is a quadratic Lie algebra. Additionally, it shows that at the core of a left Leibniz algebra endowed with a L {\mathrm{L}} -invariant or R {\mathrm{R}} -invariant metric, there are two Lie algebras with one quadratic and the left Leibniz algebra can be built from these Lie algebras. We derive many important results from these complete description. Finally, the paper provides a list of left Leibniz algebras with an associative metric up to dimension 6, as well as a list of left Leibniz algebras with an L {\mathrm{L}} -invariant metric, up to dimension 4, and R {\mathrm{R}} -invariant metric up to dimension 5.



Keywords: Leibniz algebras, invariant metrics, associative metrics, double extension,
T^{*}
-extension.

Introduction

Lie groups having a bi-invariant pseudo-Riemannian metric is a very large class which contains all semi-simple Lie groups. They became relevant some years ago when they were useful in the formulation of some physical problems, for instance in the so-known Adler-Kostant-Symes scheme. More recently they appeared in conformal field theory
[
20
]
. The Lie algebra of a Lie group having a bi-invariant pseudo-Riemannian is called quadratic. It is a real Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;])
endowed with a bilinear non-degenerate form
\langle\;,\;\rangle
such that, for any
u\in\mathfrak{g}
,
{\mathrm{ad}}_{u}:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
,
v\mapsto[u,v]
is skew-symmetric with respect to
\langle\;,\;\rangle
. This class of pseudo-Euclidean Lie algebras has been the focus of intensive study by numerous authors (refer to
[
28
]
and the bibliography therein).

There are three ways of generalizing the notion of bi-invariant pseudo-Euclidean metric on Lie algebras. Let
(\mathfrak{g},\bullet)
be a nonassociative algebra and
\langle\;,\;\rangle
a pseudo-Euclidean metric on
\mathfrak{g}
. For any
u\in\mathfrak{g}
, we denote by
{\mathrm{L}}_{u},{\mathrm{R}}_{u}:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
, respectively, the left and the right multiplication by
u
, i.e.,
{\mathrm{L}}_{u}v={\mathrm{R}}_{v}u=u\bullet v
. The metric is called
{\mathrm{L}}
-invariant (resp.
{\mathrm{R}}
-invariant) if, for any
u\in\mathfrak{g}
,
{\mathrm{L}}_{u}
(resp.
{\mathrm{R}}_{u}
) is skew-symmetric. It is called associative if, for any
u\in\mathfrak{g}
,
{\mathrm{R}}_{u}^{*}={\mathrm{L}}_{u}
, i.e.,
\langle u\bullet v,w\rangle=\langle u,v\bullet w\rangle
.
In the first case (resp. the second case), we call
(\mathfrak{g},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)

{\mathrm{L}}
-quadratic (resp.
{\mathrm{R}}
-quadratic) and, in the last case, we call
(\mathfrak{g},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
metrised. All these notions coincide when the product
\bullet
is anti-commutative.
In the previous decade, various nonassociative algebras endowed with an associative pseudo-Euclidean metric have been studied, for example, general nonassociative algebras (
[
17
]
), quadratic Lie algebras (see
[
28
]
for a large bibliography), quadratic super-Lie algebras (
[
2
,
15
]
) or quadratic Malcev super-algebras (
[
3
]
), Leibniz algebras (
[
25
,
12
]
). However,
{\mathrm{L}}
-quadratic or
{\mathrm{R}}
-quadratic nonassociative algebras have attracted little attention. There are some results on
{\mathrm{L}}
-quadratic or
{\mathrm{R}}
-quadratic Leibniz algebras in
[
14
,
25
]
.

Leibniz algebras are a non-commutative generalization of Lie algebras and were first introduced and investigated in the papers of Bloh
[
18
,
16
]
under the name of D-algebras.
Then they were rediscovered by Loday
[
26
]
who called them Leibniz algebras. A left Leibniz algebra (resp. right Leibniz algebra) is an algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\bullet)
over a field
\mathbb{K}
such that, for any
u\in\mathfrak{g}
,
{\mathrm{L}}_{u}
(resp.
{\mathrm{R}}_{u}
) is a derivation of
(\mathfrak{g},\bullet)
. An algebra which is both left and right Leibniz is called symmetric Leibniz algebra. A Lie algebra is obviously a symmetric Leibniz algebra.
Many results of the theory of Lie algebras can be extended to left Leibniz algebras (see
[
6
,
7
,
8
]
). Moreover, real left Leibniz algebras are the infinitesimal version of Lie racks. In 2004, Kinyon
[
22
]
proved that if
(X,e)
is a pointed Lie rack,
T_{e}X
carries a structure of left Leibniz algebra.

In this paper, we undertake a complete study of
{\mathrm{L}}
-quadratic,
{\mathrm{R}}
-quadratic or metrised left Leibniz algebras.
Our results can be translated to right Leibniz algebras since there is a correspondence between left and right Leibniz algebras.

Let us explain the two main ideas at the heart of this paper. First, it is known that a metrised left Leibniz algebra is also a right Leibniz algebra and hence it is Lie admissible. We show that the underlying Lie algebra is a quadratic Lie algebra which can be used to build the metrised left Leibniz algebra straightforwardly.

Secondly, let
(\mathfrak{g},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
be a
{\mathrm{L}}
-quadratic or
{\mathrm{R}}
-quadratic left Leibniz algebra. The vector space
\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})={\mathrm{span}}\{u\bullet v+v\bullet u\}
is an ideal known as the Leibniz ideal. Denote by
\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})^{\perp}
its orthogonal. When
\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})\cap\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})^{\perp}=\{0\}
,
\mathfrak{g}
is a Lie algebra when the metric is
{\mathrm{R}}
-invariant and, when the metric is
{\mathrm{L}}
-invariant,

\mathfrak{g}=\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})\oplus\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})^{\perp}
and
\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})^{\perp}
is a quadratic Lie algebra.

When
I=\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})\cap\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})^{\perp}\not=\{0\}
,
we show that
{\mathfrak{h}}=\mathfrak{g}/I^{\perp}
has a structure of a Lie algebra and
A=I^{\perp}/I\cap I^{\perp}
has a structure of a
{\mathrm{L}}
-quadratic left Leibniz algebra with nondegenerate Leibniz ideal when the metric is
{\mathrm{L}}
-invariant, and a structure of a quadratic Lie algebra when the metric is
{\mathrm{R}}
-invariant . We call
A
and
{\mathfrak{h}}
endowed with their structures the core of
(\mathfrak{g},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
. Our main result is that
(\mathfrak{g},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
can be reconstructed from its core in a way which combine the double extension process and the
T^{*}
-extension. The double extension is a well-known process which appeared first in
[
27
]
in the study of quadratic Lie algebras while the
T^{*}
-extension was introduced in
[
17
]
in the study of metrised nonassociative algebras.

Notations

For a non associative algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\bullet)
, we denote by
{\mathrm{L}}_{u}
and
{\mathrm{R}}_{u}
, respectively, the left and the right multiplication by
u
, i.e.,
{\mathrm{L}}_{u}(v)=u\bullet v
and
{\mathrm{R}}_{u}(v)=v\bullet u
. If
F,G
are two vector subspaces of
\mathfrak{g}
, we denote by
F\bullet G
the subspace generated by
u\bullet v
for any
u\in G
and
v\in G
. A vector subspace
I
is a left ideal (resp. right ideal) if
\mathfrak{g}\bullet I\subset I
(resp.
I\bullet\mathfrak{g}\subset I
) and
I
is an ideal if it is both a left ideal and a right ideal. Finally, we denote

Z^{\ell}(\mathfrak{g})=\{u\in\mathfrak{g},{\mathrm{L}}_{u}=0\},\;Z^{r}(\mathfrak{g})=\{u\in\mathfrak{g},{\mathrm{R}}_{u}=0\}\quad\mbox{and}\quad Z(\mathfrak{g})=Z^{\ell}(\mathfrak{g})\cap Z^{r}(\mathfrak{g}).

A metric on
\mathfrak{g}
is a nondegenerate bilinear symmetric form
\langle\;,\;\rangle
. For any endomorphism
F:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
and a vector subspace
I
, we denote by
F^{*}
the adjoint with respect to
\langle\;,\;\rangle
and by
I^{\perp}
the orthogonal with respect to
\langle\;,\;\rangle
. The vector subspace
I
is called non-degenerate if
I\cap I^{\perp}=\{0\}
and it is called totally isotropic if
I\subset I^{\perp}
. We call a metric Euclidean if it is definite positive and Lorentzian if it has the signature
(n-1,1)
.

2
Invariant symmetric bilinear forms and the Killing form of left Leibniz algebras

In the literature, there are three notions of invariant symmetric bilinear forms on a nonassociative algebra. We recall these notions and give some of their properties.
The Killing form of a Lie algebra is an invariant bilinear form which plays an important role and can be used to give a criterion for a Lie algebra to be semi-simple or solvable. Left Leibniz algebras generalize Lie algebras but, as we will show in this section, lack a good notion of Killing form.
We introduce a bilinear symmetric form on any Leibniz algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and we show that it is the accurate notion of the Killing form of
\mathfrak{g}
since, in particular, it can give an analogue of Cartan’s criterions for semi-simple and solvable Leibniz algebras.

Let
(A,\bullet)
be a nonassociative algebra and
S
a symmetric bilinear form on
A
.
S
is called

1.

{\mathrm{L}}
-invariant if for any
u,v,w\in A
,
S(u\bullet v,w)+S(v,u\bullet w)=0
,

2.

{\mathrm{R}}
-invariant if for any
u,v,w\in A
,
S(u\bullet v,w)+S(u,w\bullet v)=0
,

3.

associative if for any
u,v,w\in A
,
S(u\bullet v,w)=S(u,v\bullet w)
.

It is clear that these three notions coincide if
\bullet
is anti-commutative. However, when
\bullet
is commutative and
S
is
{\mathrm{L}}
-invariant or
{\mathrm{R}}
-invariant then
S(u\bullet v,w)=0
for any
u,v\in A
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
(A,\bullet)
be a nonassociative algebra and
S
a symmetric bilinear form on
A
. If
S
satisfies two of the properties above then it satisfies the third one and, for any
u,v\in A
,
u\bullet v+v\bullet u\in\ker S
. In this case, we call
S
bi-invariant.

Proof.

Suppose, for instance, that
S
is
{\mathrm{L}}
-invariant and
{\mathrm{R}}
-invariant. Then, for any
u,v,w\in A
,

S(u\bullet v,w)=-S(v,u\bullet w)=S(v\bullet w,u)

and hence
S
is associative. Moreover,

S(u\bullet v,w)=-S(v,u\bullet w)=S(v\bullet w,u)=-S(w,v\bullet u)

which shows that
u\bullet v+v\bullet u\in\ker S
. The other cases can be treated in a similar way.
∎

Recall that
(\mathfrak{g},\bullet)
is a left Leibniz algebra if for any
u,v,w\in\mathfrak{g}
,

u\bullet(v\bullet w)=(u\bullet v)\bullet w+v\bullet(u\bullet w)\quad\mbox{or}\quad\mathrm{L}_{u\bullet v}=[\mathrm{L}_{u},\mathrm{L}_{v}].

(1)

(\mathfrak{g},\bullet)
is a right Leibniz algebra if for any
u,v,w\in\mathfrak{g}
,

(v\bullet w)\bullet u=(v\bullet u)\bullet w+v\bullet(w\bullet u)\quad\mbox{or}\quad\mathrm{R}_{w\bullet u}=-[\mathrm{R}_{w},\mathrm{R}_{u}].

(2)

It is obvious that if
(\mathfrak{g},\bullet)
is a left Leibniz algebra then
(\mathfrak{g},\circ)
is a right Leibniz algebra when
u\circ v=u\bullet v
.

(\mathfrak{g},\bullet)
is called a symmetric Leibniz algebra if it is both a left and right Leibniz algebra.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\bullet)
be a left Leibniz algebra. We denote
\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})=\mathrm{span}\left\{u\bullet v+v\bullet u,u,v\in\mathfrak{g}\right\}
.
It is well-known
[
9
]
that
\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})
is an ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
, for any
u\in\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})
,
\mathrm{L}_{u}=0
,
\mathrm{Lie}(\mathfrak{g})=\mathfrak{g}/\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})
is a Lie algebra and
\mathfrak{g}
is a Lie algebra if and only if
\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})=\{0\}
. We call
\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})
the Leibniz ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
.

We consider
B
and
K
the two bilinear symmetric forms on
\mathfrak{g}
given by

B(u,v)={\mathrm{tr}}(\mathrm{L}_{u}\circ\mathrm{L}_{v})\quad\mbox{and}\quad K(u,v)=-\frac{1}{2}\left({\mathrm{tr}}(\mathrm{L}_{u}\circ\mathrm{R}_{v})+{\mathrm{tr}}(\mathrm{L}_{v}\circ\mathrm{R}_{u})\right).

The following proposition is our first main result.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\bullet)
be a left Leibniz algebra. Then the 2-forms
B
and
K
are bi-invariant and hence
\ker B
and
\ker K
contain
\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})
. Moreover, for any
u,v\in\mathfrak{g}
,

K(u,v)=\widehat{K}(\pi(u),\pi(v))

(3)

where
\pi:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}/\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})
and
\widehat{K}
is the Killing form of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}/\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})
. Furthermore,
\ker K
is a solvable ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
and hence its contained in the radical of
\mathfrak{g}
(the largest solvable ideal in
\mathfrak{g}
).

Proof.

The fact that
B
is bi-invariant is an immediate consequence of (
1
). To show that
K
is bi-invariant, it suffices to show (
3
).
Consider a complement
V
of
\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})
, i.e.,
\mathfrak{g}=\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})\oplus V
and put, for any
u,v\in\mathfrak{g}
,

u\bullet v=[u,v]_{V}+\omega(u,v)

where
[u,v]_{V}\in V
and
\omega(u,v)\in\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})
. Since, for any
u,v\in\mathfrak{g}
,
u\bullet v+v\bullet u\in\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})
then
[\;,\;]_{V}
is skew-symmetric and, by using the Leibniz identity,
one can check easily that
(V,[\;,\;]_{V})
is a Lie algebra. For any
u\in V
, denote by
{\mathrm{ad}}_{u}^{V}:V\longrightarrow V
the adjoint. For any
u\in\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})
and
v\in V
,

\mathrm{L}_{u}=0,\;\mathrm{R}_{u}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\omega(.,u)\\
0&0\end{array}\right),\;\mathrm{L}_{v}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}D_{v}&\omega(v,.)\\
0&{\mathrm{ad}}_{v}^{V}\end{array}\right)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathrm{R}_{v}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\omega(.,v)\\
0&-{\mathrm{ad}}_{v}^{V}\end{array}\right).

So

K(u,v)=\begin{cases}0\quad\mbox{if}\quad u\in\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})\;\mbox{or}\;v\in\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g}),\\
{\mathrm{tr}}_{V}({\mathrm{ad}}_{u}^{V}\circ{\mathrm{ad}}_{v}^{V})=K_{V}(u,v)\quad\mbox{if}\quad u,v\in V,\end{cases}

where
K_{V}
is the Killing form of
(V,[\;,\;]_{V})
. From this formula, we can deduce that

\pi:V\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}/\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})
is an isomorphism of Lie algebras which sends
K_{V}
to the quotient of
K
.

Since
K
is bi-invariant then
\ker K
is obviously an ideal. We have
\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})
is solvable and, according to (
3
),
\ker K/\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})=\ker\widehat{K}
. It is a well-known fact
[
24
]
that the kernel of the Killing form of a Lie algebra is solvable, hence
\ker K
is solvable.
∎

The 2-form
B
was presented by many authors as the Killing form of the Leibniz algebra (see
[
9
,
5
]
). However, it presents a serious inconvenient, namely, the quotient
\widetilde{B}
of
B
on
\mathfrak{g}/\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})
is not, in general, the Killing form of
\mathfrak{g}/\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})
as the following example shows.

Example 1
.

Consider the 2-dimensional Leibniz algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\hbox{\bb R}^{2}
endowed with the bracket

[e_{2},e_{1}]=e_{1}\quad\mbox{and}\quad[e_{2},e_{2}]=e_{1}.

Then
B(e_{1},e_{1})=B(e_{1},e_{2})=0
and
B(e_{2},e_{2})=1
. The quotient
\mathfrak{g}/\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})=\hbox{\bb R}e_{2}
is abelian but the quotient of
\widetilde{B}
of
B
satisfies
\widetilde{B}(e_{2},e_{2})=1
. However,
K=0
.

Remark 1
.

From the proof of the last proposition, one can see that, for any
u,v\in V
,

B(u,v)={\mathrm{tr}}_{\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})}(D_{u}\circ D_{v})+{\mathrm{tr}}_{V}({\mathrm{ad}}_{u}^{V}\circ{\mathrm{ad}}_{v}^{V})

which explains the difference between
B
and
K
.

It is known
[
9
]
that a left Leibniz algebra is semi-simple (resp. solvable) if and only if
\mathfrak{g}/\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})
is semi-simple (resp. solvable). Thus we can deduce the following result which generalize Cartan’s criterions and shows that
K
is the good notion of Killing form.

Proposition 2.3
.

1.

A left Leibniz algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is semi-simple if and only if
\ker K=\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})
.

2.

A left Leibniz algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is solvable if and only if
\mathfrak{g}\bullet\mathfrak{g}\subset\ker K
.

3
Metrised left Leibniz algebras

Associative metrics on left Leibniz algebra were studied in
[
12
,
14
]
. It was shown that if a left Leibniz algebra has an associative metric then it is also right Leibniz. In this section, we complete the results obtained in
[
12
,
14
]
by giving a simple and complete description of symmetric Leibniz algebras having an associative metric. We call such pseudo-Euclidean symmetric Leibniz algebras metrised.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\bullet)
be a nonassociative algebra. Denote, respectively, by
[\;,\;]
and
\circ
the skew-symmetric and symmetric parts of
\bullet
. That is

[u,v]=\frac{1}{2}(u\bullet v-v\bullet u),\;u\circ v=\frac{1}{2}(u\bullet v+v\bullet u),\quad\mbox{and}\quad u\bullet v=[u,v]+u\circ v.\;\forall u,v\in\mathfrak{g}.

(4)

Put,
{\mathrm{ad}}_{u}:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
,
v\mapsto[u,v]
and
{\mathrm{sd}}:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
,
v\mapsto u\circ v
for any
u\in\mathfrak{g}
. Hence,

{\mathrm{L}}_{u}={\mathrm{ad}}_{u}+{\mathrm{sd}}_{u}\quad\mbox{and}\quad{\mathrm{R}}_{u}=-{\mathrm{ad}}_{u}+{\mathrm{sd}}_{u}.

The following result, even if it is easy to establish, will have an important consequence and to our knowledge doesn’t appear in the literature.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
be an algebra endowed with a metric
\langle\;,\;\rangle
. Then
\langle\;,\;\rangle
is associative if and only if
\langle\;,\;\rangle
is bi-invariant on

(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;])
and associative on
(\mathfrak{g},\circ)
.

Proof.

The metric is associative if and only if
{\mathrm{L}}_{u}={\mathrm{R}}_{u}^{*}
for any
u\in g
. That is, for any
u\in\mathfrak{g}
,

{\mathrm{ad}}_{u}+\mathrm{sd}_{u}=-{\mathrm{ad}}_{u}^{*}+\mathrm{sd}_{u}^{*}.

Thus,
{\mathrm{ad}}_{u}+{\mathrm{ad}}_{u}^{*}={\mathrm{sd}}_{u}^{*}-{\mathrm{sd}}_{u}
. Since
{\mathrm{ad}}_{u}+{\mathrm{ad}}_{u}^{*}
is symmetric and
{\mathrm{sd}}_{u}^{*}-{\mathrm{sd}}_{u}
skew-symmetric then
{\mathrm{ad}}_{u}+{\mathrm{ad}}_{u}^{*}={\mathrm{sd}}_{u}^{*}-{\mathrm{sd}}_{u}=0
, for any
u\in\mathfrak{g}
which completes the proof.
∎

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
be a left Leibniz algebra endowed with an associative metric. Then, for any
u\in\mathfrak{g}
,
{\mathrm{L}}_{u}^{*}={\mathrm{R}}_{u}
and by using (
1
) and
(2)
, we deduce
that
(\mathfrak{g},\bullet)
is also right Leibniz algebra. This remark appeared in
[
12
, Theorem 2.4]
.
We conclude that if a left Leibniz algebra has an associative metric then it is a symmetric Leibniz algebra.

On the other hand, according to
[
11
, Proposition 2.11]
, any symmetric Leibniz algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\bullet)
is given by

u\bullet b=[a,b]+\omega(u,v),

where
[\;,\;]
is a Lie bracket on
\mathfrak{g}
and
\omega
is a bilinear symmetric map

\omega\,:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow Z(\mathfrak{g})
(
Z(\mathfrak{g})
is the center of
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;])
),
(x,y)\mapsto\omega(x,y)=x\circ y
such that, for any
x,\,y,\,z\in\mathfrak{g}
,

\omega([x,y],z)=\omega(\omega(x,y),z)=0.

(5)

We call
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;]+\omega)
the symmetric Leibniz algebra obtained from the Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;])
by means of

.

By using this characterization of symmetric Leibniz algebras an Proposition

, we get a new and complete description of symmetric Leibniz algebras endowed with an associative metric. But before, we can give a complete description of metrised symmetric Leibniz algebras whose underlying Lie algebra is abelian.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)

be a metrised commutative symmetric Leibniz algebra. Then there exists a pseudo-Euclidean vector space
(A,\langle\;,\;\rangle_{A})
, a vector space
{\mathfrak{h}}
and a symmetric 3-linear form

such that

is isomorphic to
({\mathfrak{h}}\oplus A\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}^{*},\circ,\langle\;,\;\rangle_{0})
where
{\mathfrak{h}}\circ{\mathfrak{h}}\subset{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
and for any
X,Y,Z\in{\mathfrak{h}}
,

,
\alpha\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
,

\langle X+a+\alpha,X+a+\alpha\rangle_{0}=2\prec\alpha,X\succ+\langle a,a\rangle_{A},\;{\mathrm{L}}_{a}={\mathrm{L}}_{\alpha}=0\quad\mbox{and}\quad\prec X\circ Y,Z\succ=T(X,Y,Z),

Proof.

According to what above, a commutative symmetric Leibniz algebra is a commutative algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\bullet)
such that for any
u,v\in\mathfrak{g}
,
{\mathrm{L}}_{u}\circ{\mathrm{L}}_{v}=0
. Suppose that
\langle\;,\;\rangle
is an associative metric on
\mathfrak{g}
. Then, for any
u\in\mathfrak{g}
,
{\mathrm{L}}_{u}
is symmetric with respect to
\langle\;,\;\rangle
and, if
I=\sum_{u\in\mathfrak{g}}\mathrm{Im}{\mathrm{L}}_{u}

I^{\perp}=\bigcap_{u\in\mathfrak{g}}\ker{\mathrm{L}}_{u}
and
I\subset I^{\perp}
. Write

for a complement
A

\langle\;,\;\rangle_{A}
of the metric to

such that

and
\mathfrak{g}=I\oplus A\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}

to the dual

to
({\mathfrak{h}}^{*}\oplus A\oplus{\mathfrak{h}},\langle\;,\;\rangle_{0})

With this identification in mind, the bracket
\bullet
satisfies
{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}\oplus A\subset Z({\mathfrak{h}}^{*}\oplus A\oplus{\mathfrak{h}})
and
{\mathfrak{h}}\bullet{\mathfrak{h}}\subset{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
and the 3-linear form given by

for any

We state now an important result.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
be a pseudo-Euclidean symmetric Leibniz algebra obtained from
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;])
by means of
\omega
. We denote by

the center of
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;])

(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;],\langle\;,\;\rangle)

T:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\hbox{\bb R}
and a totally isotropic vector subspace
I\subset Z(\mathfrak{g})
such that
T(I^{\perp},.,.)=0
and

\langle\omega(x,y),z\rangle=T(x,y,z),\quad x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

(6)

Proof.

According to Proposition
3.1
and the description of symmetric Leibniz algebras given above, if
\langle\;,\;\rangle
is associative then
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;],\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is a quadratic Lie algebra and
\omega
satisfies
I=\mathrm{span}\{\omega(x,y),x,y\in\mathfrak{g}\}\subset Z(\mathfrak{g})
,

\omega([x,y],z)=\omega(\omega(x,y),z)=0\quad\mbox{and}\quad\langle\omega(x,y),z\rangle=\langle\omega(x,z),y\rangle,\quad x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

For any
x,y,z,t\in\mathfrak{g}
,

0=\langle\omega(\omega(x,y),z),t\rangle=\langle\omega(z,t),\omega(x,y)\rangle=0

and hence
I
is totally isotropic.
Put
T(x,y,z)=\langle\omega(x,y),z\rangle

T
is symmetric and for any

and

T(x,y,z)=\langle\omega(x,y),z\rangle=0.

Conversely, let
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;],\langle\;,\;\rangle)
be a quadratic Lie algebra,
I\subset Z(\mathfrak{g})
a totally isotropic vector subspace and

a
3-linear symmetric form such that

It is obvious that

and, for any

\langle\omega(\omega(x,y),z),t\rangle=T(\omega(x,y),z,t)=0\quad\mbox{and}\quad\langle\omega([x,y],z),t\rangle=\langle\omega(z,t),[x,y]\rangle=-\langle[x,\omega(z,t)],y\rangle=0.

So
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;]+\omega,\langle\;,\;\rangle)

4.1
General properties of
{\mathrm{L}}
-quadratic and
{\mathrm{R}}
-quadratic left Leibniz algebras

A
{\mathrm{L}}
-quadratic (resp.
{\mathrm{R}}
-quadratic) left Leibniz algebra is a pseudo-Euclidean left Leibniz algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
such that the metric is
{\mathrm{L}}
-invariant (resp.
{\mathrm{R}}
-invariant). This means that for any
u\in\mathfrak{g}
,
{\mathrm{L}}_{u}^{*}=-{\mathrm{L}}_{u}
(resp.
{\mathrm{R}}_{u}^{*}=-{\mathrm{R}}_{u}
).

According to Proposition
2.1
, if a pseudo-Euclidean left Leibniz algebra is both
{\mathrm{L}}
-quadratic and
{\mathrm{R}}
-quadratic then it is a Lie algebra.

The following example shows that the classes of
{\mathrm{L}}
-quadratic or
{\mathrm{R}}
-quadratic left Leibniz algebras are large.

Example 2
.

1.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;])
be a Lie algebra. We denote by
\mathrm{ad}^{*}:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathrm{End}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
its co-adjoint representation, i.e.,
\prec\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{u}\alpha,v\succ=-\prec\alpha,[u,v]\succ
. We endow
T^{*}\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*}
by the bracket

(u+\alpha)\bullet(v+\beta)=[u,v]+\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{u}\beta,\quad u,v\in\mathfrak{g},\alpha,\beta\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}

and its canonical neutral metric given by

\langle u+\alpha,v+\beta\rangle=\prec\alpha,v\succ+\prec\beta,u\succ.

Then
(T^{*}\mathfrak{g},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is a
{\mathrm{L}}
-quadratic left Leibniz algebra.

2.

Let,
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;])
be a 2-step nilpotent Lie algebra. . We endow
T^{*}\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*}
by the bracket

(u+\alpha)\bullet(v+\beta)=\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{u}\beta,\quad u,v\in\mathfrak{g},\alpha,\beta\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}

and its canonical neutral metric
\langle\;,\;\rangle
. Then
(T^{*}\mathfrak{g},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is a
{\mathrm{R}}
-quadratic left Leibniz algebra.

The following proposition can be easily established.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
be a pseudo-Euclidean left Leibniz algebra and
I
an ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
. Then the following assertions hold.

1.

If
\langle\;,\;\rangle
is
{\mathrm{L}}
-invariant
(
resp.
{\mathrm{R}}
-invariant) then
Z^{r}(\mathfrak{g})=(\mathfrak{g}\bullet\mathfrak{g})^{\perp}
(resp.

Z^{l}(\mathfrak{g})=(\mathfrak{g}\bullet\mathfrak{g})^{\perp})
.

2.

If
\langle\;,\;\rangle
is
{\mathrm{L}}
-invariant, then
I^{\perp}
is a left ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
and
I\bullet I^{\perp}=0
. Moreover,
I^{\perp}
is an ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
if and only if
I^{\bot}\bullet I=0
.

3.

If
\langle\;,\;\rangle
is
{\mathrm{R}}
-invariant, then
I^{\perp}
is a right ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
and
I^{\perp}\bullet I=0
. Moreover,
I^{\perp}
is an ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
if and only if
I\bullet I^{\perp}=0
.

The following proposition will be useful later.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
be a pseudo-Euclidean left Leibniz algebra. Then the following assertions hold.

1.

If
\langle\;,\;\rangle
is
{\mathrm{L}}
-invariant then

\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})^{\perp}=\{u\in\mathfrak{g},\mathrm{R}_{u}+\mathrm{R}_{u}^{*}=0\}.
Moreover,
\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})^{\perp}
is a left ideal,
I=\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})+\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})^{\perp}
is an ideal and
I\bullet I^{\perp}=0

2.

If
\langle\;,\;\rangle
is
{\mathrm{R}}
-invariant then

\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})^{\perp}=\{u\in\mathfrak{g},\mathrm{L}_{u}+\mathrm{L}_{u}^{*}=0\}.

Moreover,
\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})\subset\mathfrak{g}\bullet\mathfrak{g}\subset\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})^{\perp}
. Thus,
\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})
is a totally isotropic ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
,
\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})^{\perp}
is an ideal and
\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})^{\perp}\bullet\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})=0
.

Proof.

1.

Let
u\in\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})^{\perp}
. We have, for any
v,w\in\mathfrak{g}
,

0=\langle u,v\bullet w+w\bullet v\rangle\\
=-\langle v\bullet u,w\rangle-\langle w\bullet u,v\rangle\\
=-\langle{\mathrm{R}}_{u}v,w\rangle-\langle{\mathrm{R}}_{u}w,v\rangle

and hence
{\mathrm{R}}_{u}^{*}=-{\mathrm{R}}_{u}
. According to Proposition
4.1
,
\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})^{\perp}
is a left ideal. Put
I=\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})+\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})^{\perp}
. Then
I
is a left ideal,
I^{\perp}=\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})\cap\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})^{\perp}
and, for any
u\in\mathfrak{g}
,
v\in I
and
w\in I^{\perp}
,

\langle v\bullet u,w\rangle=-\langle u,v\bullet w\rangle=\langle u\bullet w,v\rangle=0

since
I^{\perp}
is a left ideal and
{\mathrm{R}}_{w}^{*}=-{\mathrm{R}}_{w}
. Hence
I
is an ideal and
I\bullet I^{\perp}=0
, according to Proposition
4.1
.

2.

The first part of the second assertion can be obtained similarly. Since
\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})
is an ideal,
\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})^{\perp}\bullet\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})=0
by virtue of Proposition
4.1
.

On the other hand, recall that for any
u\in\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})
,
{\mathrm{L}}_{u}=0
.
Then for
v,w\in\mathfrak{g}
and
u\in\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})

\langle u,v\bullet w\rangle=-\langle u\bullet w,v\rangle=0

so
\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})\subset(\mathfrak{g}\bullet\mathfrak{g})^{\perp}
which completes the proof since obviously

.

A
{\mathrm{R}}
-quadratic Euclidean left Leibniz algebra is necessarily a Lie algebra.

4.2

{\mathrm{L}}

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
be a
{\mathrm{L}}
-quadratic left Leibniz algebra such that
\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})
is nondegenerate. According to Proposition

\mathfrak{g}=\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})\oplus\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})^{\perp}

Conversely, let
({\mathfrak{h}},\langle\;,\;\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}})
be a quadratic Lie algebra,
(N,\langle\;,\;\rangle_{N})

\rho:{\mathfrak{h}}\longrightarrow\mathrm{so}(N,\langle\;,\;\rangle_{N})

[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}\;\mbox{if}\;u,v\in{\mathfrak{h}},\\

and the metric
\langle\;,\;\rangle=\langle\;,\;\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}}\oplus\langle\;,\;\rangle_{N}

{\mathrm{L}}
-quadratic left Leibniz algebra.

u,v,w\in\mathfrak{g}
,

\langle{\mathrm{L}}_{u}^{*}v,w\rangle=\langle v,u\bullet w\rangle=-\langle v\bullet w,u\rangle=-\langle{\mathrm{L}}_{v}^{*}u,w\rangle

and hence
{\mathrm{L}}_{u}^{*}v=-{\mathrm{L}}_{v}^{*}u
. Moreover, for any
u\in I^{\perp}
,
v\in\mathfrak{g}
and
w\in I
,

\langle{\mathrm{L}}_{u}^{*}v,w\rangle=\langle v,u\bullet w\rangle=-\langle v\bullet w,u\rangle=0

since
I=\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})
is an ideal. So we have shown that
[\;,\;]_{\mathfrak{h}}
is well-defined and skew-symmetric.

Let
X=\pi_{\mathfrak{h}}(u),Y=\pi_{\mathfrak{h}}(v),Z=\pi_{\mathfrak{h}}(w)
. We have

[X,[Y,Z]_{\mathfrak{h}}]_{\mathfrak{h}}=\pi_{\mathfrak{h}}({\mathrm{L}}_{u}^{*}\circ{\mathrm{L}}_{v}^{*}w).

By virtue of (
1
),

{\mathrm{L}}_{u}^{*}\circ{\mathrm{L}}_{v}^{*}w={\mathrm{L}}_{v}^{*}\circ{\mathrm{L}}_{u}^{*}w-{\mathrm{L}}_{v\bullet u}^{*}w.

But for any
t\in I=\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})
, we have

\langle{\mathrm{L}}_{v\bullet u}^{*}w,t\rangle=\langle w,(v\bullet u)\bullet t\rangle=-\langle w\bullet t,v\bullet u\rangle=0

since we have seen in Proposition
4.2
that
I
is an ideal and
I\subset(\mathfrak{g}\bullet\mathfrak{g})^{\perp}
. So we get that for any
X,Y,Z\in{\mathfrak{h}}
,

[X,[Y,Z]_{\mathfrak{h}}]_{\mathfrak{h}}=[Y,[X,Z]_{\mathfrak{h}}]_{\mathfrak{h}}.

This relation combined to the skew-symmetry of
[\;,\;]_{\mathfrak{h}}
will show that
[X,[Y,Z]_{\mathfrak{h}}]_{\mathfrak{h}}=0
. Indeed,

\displaystyle[X,[Y,Z]_{\mathfrak{h}}]_{\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle=[Y,[X,Z]_{\mathfrak{h}}]_{\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle=-[Y,[Z,X]_{\mathfrak{h}}]_{\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle=-[Z,[Y,X]_{\mathfrak{h}}]_{\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle=[Z,[X,Y]_{\mathfrak{h}}]_{\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle=[X,[Z,Y]_{\mathfrak{h}}]_{\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle=-[X,[Y,Z]_{\mathfrak{h}}]_{\mathfrak{h}}

which completes the proof.∎

Definition 4.1
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
be a
{\mathrm{L}}
-quadratic (resp.
{\mathrm{R}}
-quadratic) left Leibniz algebras. We call the
{\mathrm{L}}
-quadratic left Leibniz algebra
(A,\bullet_{A},\langle\;,\;\rangle_{A})
and the Lie algebra
({\mathfrak{h}},[\;,\;]_{\mathfrak{h}})
(resp. the quadratic Lie algebra
(A,[\;,\;]_{A},\langle\;,\;\rangle_{A})
and the Lie algebra
({\mathfrak{h}},[\;,\;]_{\mathfrak{h}})
) defined in Proposition
4.4
the core of
(\mathfrak{g},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
.

Actually, we can go further and give more information about the core of a
{\mathrm{L}}
-quadratic or
{\mathrm{R}}
-quadratic left Leibniz algebra.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
be a pseudo-Euclidean left Leibniz algebra. Denote by
\mathrm{Lie}(\mathfrak{g})=\mathfrak{g}/\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})
the quotient Lie algebra and
\pi:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Lie}(\mathfrak{g})
the canonical projection.

1.

If
(\mathfrak{g},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is
{\mathrm{L}}
-quadratic then
the map
\kappa:A\longrightarrow\mathrm{Lie}(\mathfrak{g})
is a well-defined morphism of left algebras,
\ker\kappa=\pi_{A}(\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g}))
and
\pi_{A}(\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})^{\perp})
with an ideal of
\mathrm{Lie}(\mathfrak{g})
and the Lie algebra quotient
\mathrm{Lie}(\mathfrak{g})/\pi_{A}(\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})^{\perp})
is isomorphic to
({\mathfrak{h}},[\;,\;]_{\mathfrak{h}})

2.

If
(\mathfrak{g},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is
{\mathrm{R}}
-quadratic then the map
\kappa:A\longrightarrow\mathrm{Lie}(\mathfrak{g})
is a well-defined into morphism of Lie algebras and
\kappa(A)
is an ideal of
\mathrm{Lie}(\mathfrak{g})
which contains
[\mathrm{Lie}(\mathfrak{g}),\mathrm{Lie}(\mathfrak{g})]
.

Proof.

1.

The first part of statement is obvious and the second part is a consequence of the fact that the map
m:\mathrm{Lie}(\mathfrak{g})\longrightarrow{\mathfrak{h}}
,
\pi(u)\mapsto\pi_{\mathfrak{h}}(u)
is an onto morphism of Lie algebras and
\ker m=\pi_{A}(\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})^{\perp})
.

2.

It is clear if one has in mind the fact
\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})\subset\mathfrak{g}\bullet\mathfrak{g}\subset\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})^{\perp}
established in Proposition
4.2
.∎

Recall that a left Leibniz algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is called simple if, by definition, the only ideals of
\mathfrak{g}
are
\{0\}
,
\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})
and
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{g}\bullet\mathfrak{g}\not=\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})
. It is called semi-simple if its radical is equal to
\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})
which is equivalent to
\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})
is semi-simple. One can see
[
5
]
for details.

From what above we can derive the following important results.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
be a pseudo-Euclidean left Leibniz algebra. Then the following assertions hold.

1.

If
(\mathfrak{g},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is
{\mathrm{L}}
-quadratic and simple then either
\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})=\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})^{\perp}
,
A=\{0\}
and
{\mathfrak{h}}=\mathrm{Lie}(\mathfrak{g})
or
\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})
is nondegenerate,
\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})^{\perp}
is a simple quadratic Lie algebra and
\mathfrak{g}=\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})\oplus\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})^{\perp}
.

2.

If
(\mathfrak{g},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is
{\mathrm{R}}
-quadratic and semi-simple then
(\mathfrak{g},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is a quadratic Lie algebra.

Proof.

1.

According to Proposition
4.2
,
\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})+\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})^{\perp}
is an ideal and hence
\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})+\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})^{\perp}=\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})
or
\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})+\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})^{\perp}=\mathfrak{g}
. In the first case
\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})^{\perp}\subset\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})
and
\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})^{\perp}
becomes an ideal and
\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})^{\perp}=\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})
. Thus
A=\{0\}
and
{\mathfrak{h}}=\mathrm{Lie}(\mathfrak{g})
. In the second case
\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})
is nondegenerate,
\mathfrak{g}=\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})\oplus\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})^{\perp}
and
\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})^{\perp}
is a simple quadratic Lie algebra.

2.

If
\mathfrak{g}
is semi-simple then
\mathfrak{g}\bullet\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}
but, according to Proposition
4.2
,
\mathfrak{g}\bullet\mathfrak{g}\subset\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})^{\perp}
and hence
\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})=\{0\}
which completes the proof.∎

5
Description of
{\mathrm{L}}
-quadratic left Leibniz algebras with degenerate Leibniz ideal

In this section, we utilize the core of a
{\mathrm{L}}
-quadratic left Leibniz algebra, as defined in Definition
4.1
, to reconstruct the original algebra using a process that resembles a combination of double extension and
T^{*}
-extension. This reconstruction method enables us to derive several significant results.
The double extension is a well-known process which appeared first in
[
27
]
in the study of quadratic Lie algebras while the
T^{*}
-extension was introduced in
[
17
]
in the study of metrised nonassociative algebras.

For an algebra
(A,\bullet)
,

\mathrm{Der}(A)
is the Lie algebra of derivations of
A
, and if
A
carries a pseudo-Euclidean metric
\langle\;,\;\rangle_{A}
,
\mathrm{so}(A)
is the Lie algebra of skew-symmetric endomorphisms of
A
and, for any vector space
{\mathfrak{h}}
, we call natural the metric
\langle\;,\;\rangle_{n}
on
{\mathfrak{h}}\oplus A\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
given by

\langle X+a+\alpha,X+a+\alpha\rangle_{n}=2\prec\alpha,X\rangle+\langle a,a\rangle_{A},\quad X\in{\mathfrak{h}},a\in A,\alpha\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}.

(8)

For a Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;])
,
{\mathrm{ad}}:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathrm{End}(\mathfrak{g})
is the adjoint representation,
{\mathrm{ad}}^{*}:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathrm{End}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
the coadjoint representation give by
\prec{\mathrm{ad}}_{X}^{*}\alpha,Y\succ=-\prec\alpha,[X,Y]\succ.
The coadjoint representation extends to
\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
by the formula

{\mathrm{ad}}_{X}^{*}\omega(Y,Z)=-\omega([X,Y],Z)-\omega(Y,[X,Z]),\quad\omega\in\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}^{*},X,Y,Z\in\mathfrak{g}.

For
\Omega:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, the differential of
\Omega
is the skew-symmetric bilinear map
\Delta\Omega:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
given by

\Delta(\Omega)(X,Y)={\mathrm{ad}}_{X}^{*}\Omega(Y)-{\mathrm{ad}}_{Y}^{*}\Omega(X)-\Omega([X,Y])

(9)

Finally, for any
\omega\in\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, we denote by
\omega^{\flat}:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{*}
the linear map given by
\prec\omega^{\flat}(X),Y\succ=\omega(X,Y)
.

Let us state one of our main result.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\bullet_{\mathfrak{g}},\langle\;,\;\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a
{\mathrm{L}}
-quadratic left Leibniz algebra such that
\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})
is degenerate and let
({\mathfrak{h}},[\;,\;]_{\mathfrak{h}})
and
(A,\bullet_{A},\langle\;,\;\rangle_{A})
be its core. Then

(\mathfrak{g},\bullet_{\mathfrak{g}},\langle\;,\;\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}})
is isomorphic to
({\mathfrak{h}}\oplus A\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}^{*},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle_{n})
where, for any
X,Y\in{\mathfrak{h}}
,
a,b\in A
,
\alpha\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
, the non vanishing Leibniz products are given by

\begin{cases}X\bullet Y=[X,Y]_{\mathfrak{h}}+\theta(X,Y)+\Omega(X)^{\flat}(Y),\;a\bullet b=a\bullet_{A}b+\mu(a,b),,\\
X\bullet a=F(X)a+\nu(X,a),\;X\bullet\alpha=(\mathrm{ad}^{{\mathfrak{h}}})_{X}^{*}\alpha,\;a\bullet X=G(X)a+\rho(a,X),\end{cases}

(10)

and

F:{\mathfrak{h}}\longrightarrow\mathrm{so}(A)\cap\mathrm{Der}(A)
,
G:{\mathfrak{h}}\longrightarrow\mathrm{End}(A)
,
\Omega:{\mathfrak{h}}\longrightarrow\wedge^{2}{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
are linear maps,

\theta,\omega:{\mathfrak{h}}\times{\mathfrak{h}}\longrightarrow A
are bilinear maps such that
\omega
is skew-symmetric and
\mu,\rho,\nu
are defined by the following relations:

\prec\rho(a,X),Y\succ=-\langle\omega(X,Y),a\rangle_{A},\;\prec\mu(a,b),X\succ=-\langle G(X)a,b\rangle_{A},\;\prec\nu(X,a),Y\succ=-\langle\theta(X,Y),a\rangle_{A}.

(11)

Moreover,
(F,G,\omega,\theta,\Omega)
satisfy the following equations, for any
X,Y,Z,T\in{\mathfrak{h}}
and
a\in A
,

\displaystyle\begin{cases}[{\mathrm{L}}_{a}^{A},F(X)]={\mathrm{L}}_{G(X)a}^{A},\;[{\mathrm{L}}_{a}^{A},G(X)]={\mathrm{R}}^{A}_{G(X)a}\\
{\mathrm{L}}_{\omega(X,Y)}^{A}=G^{*}(X)G(Y)-G^{*}(Y)G(X),\\
{\mathrm{R}}_{\theta(X,Y)}=[F(X),G(Y)]-G([X,Y]_{\mathfrak{h}})=F(X)G(Y)+G(Y)G(X)-G([X,Y]_{{\mathfrak{h}}}),\\
{\mathrm{L}}^{A}_{\theta(X,Y)}=[F(X),F(Y)]-F([X,Y]_{\mathfrak{h}}),\\
\theta(X,[Y,Z]_{\mathfrak{h}})-\theta([X,Y]_{\mathfrak{h}},Z)-\theta(Y,[X,Z]_{\mathfrak{h}})+F(X)\theta(Y,Z)-F(Y)\theta(X,Z)-G(Z)\theta(X,Y)=0,\\
G(Z)^{*}\theta(X,Y)-G(Y)^{*}\theta(X,Z)-F(X)\omega(Y,Z)+\omega(Y,[X,Z]_{\mathfrak{h}})+\omega([X,Y]_{\mathfrak{h}},Z)=0,\\
(G(X)^{*}+F(X))\omega(Y,Z)=0,\\
\Delta(\Omega)(X,Y)(Z,T)=\langle\theta(X,T),\theta(Y,Z)\rangle_{A}-\langle\theta(Y,T),\theta(X,Z)\rangle_{A}-\langle\omega(Z,T),\theta(X,Y)\rangle_{A},\end{cases}

(12)

where
\Delta
is given by (
9
).

Conversely, if
(A,\bullet_{A},\langle\;,\;\rangle_{A})
is a
{\mathrm{L}}
-quadratic left Leibniz algebra,
({\mathfrak{h}},[\;,\;]_{\mathfrak{h}})
is a Lie algebra and
(F,G,\omega,\theta,\Omega)
a list as above satisfying (
11
) and (
12
) then
({\mathfrak{h}}\oplus A\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}^{*},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle_{n})
where
\bullet
is given by (
10
) is a
{\mathrm{L}}
-quadratic left Leibniz algebra. We call it the
{\mathrm{L}}
-quadratic left algebra obtained form
(A,\bullet_{A},\langle\;,\;\rangle_{A})
and
({\mathfrak{h}},[\;,\;]_{\mathfrak{h}})
by means of
(F,G,\omega,\theta,\Omega)
.

Proof.

We prove the theorem in two steps.

First step.
In this step we build all the data given in the theorem.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
be a degenerate
{\mathrm{L}}
-invariant Leibniz algebra. Let
({\mathfrak{h}},[\;,\;]_{\mathfrak{h}})
and
(A,\bullet_{A},\langle\;,\;\rangle_{A})
be its core defined in Definition
4.1
. We denote by
I=\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})\cap\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})^{\perp}
. Choose a vector subspace
B
such that
I^{\perp}=B\oplus I
.

B^{\perp}
contains
I
and
\dim B^{\perp}=2\dim I
. So there exists a totally isotropic vector subspace
{\mathfrak{h}}_{0}
such that
B^{\perp}=I\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}_{0}
. Now, the metric on
B^{\perp}
permits the identification of
I
to
{\mathfrak{h}}_{0}^{*}
and hence
(B^{\perp},\langle\;,\;\rangle)
is isometric
{\mathfrak{h}}_{0}\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}_{0}^{*}
endowed with its canonical neutral metric. We obtain that

\mathfrak{g}={\mathfrak{h}}_{0}\oplus B\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}_{0}^{*}
and the metric is
\langle\;,\;\rangle_{n}
.

Now,
I
is a totally isotropic ideal such that for any
u\in I
,
\mathrm{L}_{u}=0
,
I^{\perp}=\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})+\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})^{\perp}
and, by virtue of Proposition
4.2
,
I^{\perp}
is an ideal and
I^{\perp}\bullet I=0
. So the Leibniz product can be written, for any
X,Y\in{\mathfrak{h}}
,
a,b\in B
,
\alpha\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
,

\begin{cases}X\bullet Y=[X,Y]_{{\mathfrak{h}}_{0}}+\theta(X,Y)+\theta_{2}(X,Y),\;a\bullet b=a\bullet_{B}b+\mu(a,b),\;\alpha\bullet X=\alpha\bullet a=0,\\
X\bullet a=F(X)a+\nu(X,a),\;X\bullet\alpha=H(X)\alpha,\;a\bullet X=G(X)a+\rho(a,X),\;a\bullet\alpha=0,\end{cases}

where
\theta(X,Y)\in B
,
\theta_{2}(X,Y)\in{\mathfrak{h}}_{0}^{*}
,
a\bullet_{B}b\in B
,
\mu(X,a),\nu(X,a),\rho(a,X)\in{\mathfrak{h}}_{0}^{*}
,
F(X),G(X)\in\mathrm{End}(A)
and
H(X)\in\mathrm{End}({\mathfrak{h}}_{0}^{*})
.

Let
\pi_{\mathfrak{h}}:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow{\mathfrak{h}}=\mathfrak{g}/I^{\perp}
and
\pi_{A}:I^{\perp}\longrightarrow A=I^{\perp}/I
. It is clear that
\pi_{\mathfrak{h}}
identifies
({\mathfrak{h}}_{0},[\;,\;]_{{\mathfrak{h}}_{0}})
with
({\mathfrak{h}},[\;,\;]_{\mathfrak{h}})
and
\pi_{A}
identifies
(B,\bullet_{B},\langle\;,\;\rangle_{B})
with
(A,\bullet_{A},\langle\;,\;\rangle_{A})
. This shows that
[\;,\;]_{{\mathfrak{h}}_{0}}
is a Lie bracket and
\bullet_{B}
is a left Leibniz bracket. We identify
{\mathfrak{h}}_{0}
to
{\mathfrak{h}}
and
B
to
A
. Furthermore, the relation
\langle X\bullet\alpha,Y\rangle=-\langle\alpha,X\bullet Y\rangle
gives that
H(X)=({\mathrm{ad}}_{X}^{\mathfrak{h}})^{*}
.
For any
X,Y,Z\in{\mathfrak{h}}

\langle X\bullet Y,Z\rangle_{n}=\prec\theta_{2}(X,Y),Z\succ=-\langle X\bullet Z,Y\rangle_{n}=-\prec\theta_{2}(X,Z),Y\succ.

Define
\Omega:{\mathfrak{h}}\longrightarrow\wedge^{2}{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
by the relation
\Omega(X)(Y,Z)=\prec\theta_{2}(X,Y),Z\rangle
and hence
\theta_{2}(X,Y)=\Omega(X)^{\flat}(Y)
.
We introduce
\omega\in\wedge^{2}{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
by the relation
\prec\rho(a,X),Y\succ=-\langle\omega(X,Y),a\rangle_{A}
and the other relation in (
11
) are a direct consequence of the fact that the metric is
{\mathrm{L}}
-quadratic.

Second step.
In this step we show that the product given by (
10
) is left Leibniz and the metric is
{\mathrm{L}}
-quadratic if and only if (
12
) holds and
F
takes its values in
\mathrm{Der}(A)\cap\mathrm{so}(A)
. This is a long and tedious computation we defer to the appendix.∎

Corollary 5.1
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\bullet_{\mathfrak{g}},\langle\;,\;\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a
{\mathrm{L}}
-quadratic left Leibniz algebra,
({\mathfrak{h}},[\;,\;]_{\mathfrak{h}})
and
(A,\bullet_{A},\langle\;,\;\rangle_{A})
its core. If
\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})^{\perp}=\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})
then
A=\{0\}
,
{\mathfrak{h}}=\mathrm{Lie}(\mathfrak{g})
and
(\mathfrak{g},\bullet_{\mathfrak{g}},\langle\;,\;\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}})
is isomorphic to
({\mathfrak{h}}\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}^{*},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle_{n})
where, for any
X,Y\in{\mathfrak{h}}
,
\alpha,\beta\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}

(X+\alpha)\bullet(Y+\beta)=[X,Y]_{\mathfrak{h}}+({\mathrm{ad}}^{{\mathfrak{h}}})_{X}^{*}\beta+\Omega(X)^{\flat}(Y),\;

(13)

and
\Omega:{\mathfrak{h}}\longrightarrow\wedge^{2}{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
is a 1-cocycle, i.e.,
\Delta(\Omega)=0
.
Moreover, two such structures of
{\mathrm{L}}
-quadratic left Leibniz algebras on
{\mathfrak{h}}\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
associated to two 1-cocycles
\Omega_{1}
and
\Omega_{2}
are isomorphic if
\Omega_{1}
and
\Omega_{2}
define the same cohomolgy class in
H^{2}({\mathfrak{h}},\wedge^{2}{\mathfrak{h}}^{*})
.

Proof.

The first part is a consequence of Theorem
5.1
. Let
({\mathfrak{h}},[\;,\;])
be a Lie algebra and
\Omega_{1}
and
\Omega_{2}
two 1-cocycles on
{\mathfrak{h}}
with values in
\wedge^{2}{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
. Denote by
\bullet_{i}
the left Leibniz product on
{\mathfrak{h}}\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
associated to
\Omega_{i}
and given by (
13
). If
\Omega_{1}
and
\Omega_{2}
define the same cohomolgy class in
H^{2}({\mathfrak{h}},\wedge^{2}{\mathfrak{h}}^{*})
then there exists
\omega\in\wedge^{2}{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
such that for any
X\in{\mathfrak{h}}
,
\Omega_{2}(X)=\Omega_{1}(X)+{\mathrm{ad}}_{X}^{*}\omega
. On can check easily that the isomorphism
\phi:{\mathfrak{h}}\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}\longrightarrow{\mathfrak{h}}\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
,
X+\alpha\mapsto X+\alpha-\omega^{\flat}(X)
is an isomorphism of
{\mathrm{L}}
-quadratic left Leibniz algebras between
({\mathfrak{h}}\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}^{*},\bullet_{1},\langle\;,\;\rangle_{n})
and
({\mathfrak{h}}\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}^{*},\bullet_{2},\langle\;,\;\rangle_{n})
.
∎

As a consequence we can give a complete description of
{\mathrm{L}}
-quadratic simple left Leibniz algebras.

Corollary 5.2
.

(\mathfrak{g},\bullet_{\mathfrak{g}},\langle\;,\;\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a
{\mathrm{L}}
-quadratic simple left Leibniz algebra. Then
(\mathfrak{g},\bullet_{\mathfrak{g}},\langle\;,\;\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}})
is isomorphic to
({\mathfrak{h}}\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}^{*},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle_{n})
where
{\mathfrak{h}}=\mathrm{Lie}(\mathfrak{g})
and, for any
X,Y\in{\mathfrak{h}}
,
\alpha,\beta\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}

(X+\alpha)\bullet(Y+\beta)=[X,Y]_{\mathfrak{h}}+({\mathrm{ad}}_{X}^{{\mathfrak{h}}})^{*}\beta.

Proof.

According to Proposition
4.6
,
A=0
and
{\mathfrak{h}}=\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})
which is a simple Lie algebra. We can apply Corollary
5.1
and in addition, since
{\mathfrak{h}}
is simple,
\Omega
is a co-boundary which completes the proof.
∎

Let us recall a property of quadratic Lie algebras we will use in the following Theorem.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;],\langle\;,\;\rangle)
be a quadratic Lie algebra and
\omega\in\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. Then there exists a skew-symmetric endomorphism
D:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
such that, for any
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
,

\omega(x,y)=\langle Dx,y\rangle.

It is a well-known fact which is easy to establish that
\omega
is a 2-cocycle of
\mathfrak{g}
if and only if
D
is a derivation. Hence
H^{2}(\mathfrak{g})=(\mathrm{so}(\mathfrak{g})\cap\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{g}))/\mathrm{Inner}(\mathfrak{g})
where
\mathrm{Inner}(\mathfrak{g})=\{{\mathrm{ad}}_{u},u\in\mathfrak{g}\}
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
({\mathfrak{h}}\oplus A\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}^{*},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle_{n})
be the
{\mathrm{L}}
-quadratic left algebra obtained form
(A,\bullet_{A},\langle\;,\;\rangle_{A})
and
({\mathfrak{h}},[\;,\;]_{\mathfrak{h}})
by means of
(F,G,\omega,\theta,\Omega)
.

If
A
is a Lie algebra,
Z(A)=0
and
H^{2}(A)=0
then there exists a 1-cocycle
\Omega_{0}
of
{\mathfrak{h}}
with values in
\wedge^{2}{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
and an endomorphism
U:{\mathfrak{h}}\longrightarrow A
such that
({\mathfrak{h}}\oplus A\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}^{*},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle_{n})
is isomorphic to
({\mathfrak{h}}\oplus A\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}^{*},\circ,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
where, for any
X,Y\in{\mathfrak{h}}
,
a,b\in A
,
\alpha,\beta\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
, the non vanishing Leibniz products and the metric are given by

\displaystyle X\circ Y=[X,Y]_{\mathfrak{h}}+\Omega_{0}(X)^{\flat}(Y),\;a\circ b=[a,b]_{A}+U^{*}([a,b]_{A}),\;X\circ\alpha=({\mathrm{ad}}_{X}^{\mathfrak{h}})^{*}\alpha,

\displaystyle\langle X+a+\alpha,Y+b+\beta\rangle=\langle X+a+\alpha,Y+b+\beta\rangle_{n}+\langle U(X),U(Y)\rangle_{A}-\langle a,U(Y)\rangle_{A}-\langle b,U(X)\rangle_{A},

\displaystyle\prec U^{*}(a),X\succ=\langle a,U(X)\rangle_{A}.

Proof.

The structure of
{\mathrm{L}}
-quadratic left Leibniz algebra of
{\mathfrak{h}}\oplus A\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
is given in Theorem
5.1
with the conditions satisfied by
(F,G,\omega,\theta,\Omega)
. Suppose that
A
is a Lie algebra,
Z(A)=0
and
H^{2}(A)=0
.
Then
[A,A]_{A}=Z(A)^{\perp}=A
and any skew-symmetric derivation is inner. From the first equation in (
12
), the fact that
F
is a derivation and
Z(A)=0
we deduce that
G=-F
.
So exists a unique endomorphism
U:{\mathfrak{h}}\longrightarrow A
such that

F(X)=-G(X)={\mathrm{ad}}_{U(X)}
and from the third equation in (
12
), we must have

\theta(X,Y)=[U(X),U(Y)]_{A}-U([X,Y]_{\mathfrak{h}}).

We consider now the isomorphism
\phi:{\mathfrak{h}}\oplus A\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}\longrightarrow{\mathfrak{h}}\oplus A\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
given by
\phi(X+a+\alpha)=X+a-U(X)+\alpha
. Under this isomorphism,
({\mathfrak{h}}\oplus A\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}^{*},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle_{n})
is isomorphic to
({\mathfrak{h}}\oplus A\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}^{*},\circ,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
where, for any
u,v\in{\mathfrak{h}}\oplus A\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
,

u\circ v=\phi^{-1}(\phi(u)\bullet\phi(v))\quad\mbox{and}\quad\langle u,v\rangle=\langle\phi(u),\phi(v)\rangle_{n}.

So

\langle X+a+\alpha,Y+b+\beta\rangle=\langle X+a+\alpha,X+b+\alpha\rangle_{n}+\langle U(X),U(Y)\rangle_{A}-\langle a,U(Y)\rangle_{A}-\langle b,U(X)\rangle_{A}.

For any
X,Y\in{\mathfrak{h}}
,

\displaystyle\phi(X\circ Y)

\displaystyle=(X-U(X))\bullet(Y-U(Y))

\displaystyle=[X,Y]_{\mathfrak{h}}+\theta(X,Y)+\Omega(X)^{\flat}(Y)-[U(X),U(Y)]_{A}-\nu(X,U(Y))+[U(Y),U(X)]_{A}

\displaystyle-\rho(U(X),Y)+[U(X),U(Y)]_{A}+\mu(U(X),U(Y))

\displaystyle=[X,Y]_{\mathfrak{h}}-U([X,Y]_{\mathfrak{h}})+\Omega(X)^{\flat}(Y)-\rho(U(X),Y)+\mu(U(X),U(Y)).

So there exist
\theta_{2}
such that

X\circ Y=[X,Y]_{\mathfrak{h}}+\theta_{2}(X,Y).

From the relation

\langle X\circ Y,Z\rangle=\prec\theta_{2}(X,Y),Z\succ=-\langle X\circ Z,Y\rangle=-\prec\theta_{2}(X,Z),Y\succ

we can define

\Omega_{0}:{\mathfrak{h}}\longrightarrow\wedge^{2}{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
by the relation
\Omega_{0}(X)(Y,Z)=\prec\theta_{2}(X,Y),Z\succ
and write

X\circ Y=[X,Y]_{\mathfrak{h}}+\Omega_{0}(X)^{\flat}(Y).

In the same way, we can show that there exists,
\nu^{0},\rho^{0}
such that for any
X\in{\mathfrak{h}}
,
a,b\in A
,
\alpha\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}
,

a\circ b=[a,b]_{a}+\mu(a,b),\;X\circ a=\nu^{0}(X,a),\;X\circ\alpha=({\mathrm{ad}}_{X}^{\mathfrak{h}})^{*}\alpha\quad\mbox{and}\quad a\circ X=\rho^{0}(a,X).

From the relations

\langle a\circ b,X\rangle=-\langle b,a\circ X\rangle\quad\mbox{and}\quad\langle X\circ a,Y\rangle=-\langle a,X\circ Y\rangle

we get

\nu^{0}=0\quad\mbox{and}\quad\prec\mu(a,b),X\succ=\langle[a,b]_{A},U(X)\rangle_{A}.

The bracket
\circ
has the same form as (
10
) with
F=G=0
,
\theta=0
and
\nu=0
and (
12
) are the conditions for this bracket to be left Leibniz. So we get from the
equation (2) in (
12
) that
\omega_{0}=0
and the last equation in (
12
) gives that
\Omega_{0}
is a 1-cocycle. This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3
.

The class of quadratic Lie algebras
\mathfrak{g}
satisfying
Z(\mathfrak{g})=\{0\}
and
H^{2}(\mathfrak{g})=\{0\}
is large. It contains the class of semi-simple Lie algebras. It contains also the cotangents of simple Lie algebras. Indeed, if
(\mathfrak{g},[\;,\;]_{\mathfrak{g}})
is a Lie algebra its cotangent
T^{*}\mathfrak{g}
is the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{*}
with the bracket

[X+\alpha,Y+\beta]=[X,Y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+{\mathrm{ad}}_{X}^{*}\alpha-{\mathrm{ad}}_{Y}^{*}\beta,\quad X,Y\in\mathfrak{g},\alpha,\beta\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}.

The natural metric on
T^{*}\mathfrak{g}
is quadratic. Moreover, if
\mathfrak{g}
is simple then
Z(T^{*}\mathfrak{g})=\{0\}
and
H^{2}(T^{*}\mathfrak{g})=\{0\}
(see
[
1
, Theorem 2.2]
).

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
be a
{\mathrm{L}}
-quadratic left Leibniz algebra which is not a Lie algebra. Then
\dim\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})\geq 2
.

Proof.

Suppose that

. If
\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})
is nondegenerate then
\mathfrak{g}=\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})\oplus\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})^{\perp}
and the action of
\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})^{\perp}
on
\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})
is made by skew-symmetric endomorphisms so it is trivial and hence
\mathfrak{g}
is a Lie algebra.

Suppose tha
\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})
is degenerate then
\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})\subset\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})^{\perp}
and hence
\dim{\mathfrak{h}}=1
. So

is isomorphic to the
{\mathrm{L}}
-quadratic left Leibniz algebra

described in Theorem
5.1
with the data
(F,G,\omega,\theta,\Omega)
. Since
\dim{\mathfrak{h}}=1
, we have
\Omega=0
and
\omega=0
.
Moreover,
{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}=\mathrm{Leib}(\mathfrak{g})
and hence for any
X,Y\in{\mathfrak{h}}
and

X\bullet Y+Y\bullet X=\theta(X,Y)+\theta(Y,X)\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}\quad\mbox{and}\quad X\bullet a+a\bullet X=F(X)a+G(X)a+\nu(X,a)\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}

this implies that

,
\theta=0
and
\nu=0
. The product
\bullet
becomes skew-symmetric and hence

is a Lie algebra which is a contradiction.
∎

{\mathrm{L}}

Theorem 6.1

(14)

-quadratic left algebra obtained form

and
({\mathfrak{h}},[\;,\;]_{\mathfrak{h}})
by means of

.

Proof.

We follow the same steps as in the proof of Theorem
5.1
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\bullet,\langle\;,\;\rangle)
be a degenerate
{\mathrm{R}}
-invariant Leibniz algebra. Let

Abstract
. We develop the basic model theory of local positive logic, a new logic that mixes positive logic (where negation is not allowed) and local logic (where models omit types of infinite distant pairs). We study several basic model theoretic notions such as compactness, positive closedness (existential closedness) and completeness (irreducibility).

Introduction

It is common practice in mathematics to modify basic objects with more and more structure for the sake of generality. In model theory, well-known examples are continuous logic
[
BYBHU08
]
and positive logic
[
BYP07
,
PY18
,
BY03
]
as generalisations of first-order logic. In this paper we present the basic framework of a new logic, specially underlining the differences and similarities between this new logic and usual positive logic.

Local positive logic was first introduced by Hrushovski in
[
Hru22a
]
and applied in his study of approximate subgroups. Local positive logic is the combination of positive logic and local logic. While positive logic is well-known, local logic was also first introduced in
[
Hru22a
]
.

Local logic is a variation of first-order logic that aims to relax compactness. Compactness is a fundamental tool in model theory, but its full power is sometimes a hindrance. Local logic attempts to replace the role of compactness in first-order logic with “local compactness”. It does this by restricting quantifiers to
local quantifiers
, i.e. each quantifier imposes that the variable is located in some “ball”. Although local logic has several features that distinguish it from first-order logic, at the same time this extension is very lightweight in the sense that, by adding constants, local logic can be mostly reduced to sorted first-order logic by adding a new sort for each ball centred at the constants.

Positive logic was born to generalise Robinson’s work on existential closedness. Existential closedness is a fundamental notion in model theory capturing the idea of a “generic” or “limit” model. In positive logic this notion is modified by allowing arbitrary homomorphisms in place of just embeddings, obtaining what we here call
positive closedness
. Formally, this is achieved by restricting the first-order syntax exclusively to
positive formulas
(i.e. formulas without negations). As a consequence, positive logic also eliminates the total disconnectedness nature of first-order logic.

As already observed in
[
BY03
]
, there is no substantial difference between definable and type-definable sets in positive logic, which makes it a suitable syntax for hyperdefinable sets (i.e. quotients by type-definable equivalence relations). In turn, the original motivation for the development of local logic in
[
Hru22a
]
was to introduce a natural syntax for the study of locally definable sets, more specifically of definably generated groups. It is therefore natural for us to study the combination of both logics, our original motivation being the development of a syntax for locally hyperdefinable sets, more precisely hyperdefinably generated groups.

We now briefly explain our intended application. We aim to study a group
G
generated by a symmetric hyperdefinable set
X
in some first-order structure. We can study
G
as a local structure in positive logic where the locality relations
\{{\mathrm{d}}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
are given by
{\mathrm{d}}_{n}(x,y)\Leftrightarrow x^{-1}y\in X^{n}
and we have a relation symbol for every hyperdefinable subset. Alternatively, we can instead consider
G
as a torsor, rather than a group: in this case, for each
n
-ary hyperdefinable subset
V
we add a
2n
-ary relation symbol with intended interpretation
R_{V}(\bar{x},\bar{y})\Leftrightarrow(x^{-1}_{i}y_{i})_{i<n}\in V
— considering
G
as a torsor has the advantage that
G
becomes a subgroup of the group of automorphisms of the structure. The use of local logic ensures that the structure as group is generated by
X
. On the other hand, the use of positive logic captures the hyperdefinable nature of
G
.

To further motivate the introduction of this new logic, we present now a typical case where we think that the introduction of local positive logic might be of interest. This is the case of the study of connected graphs with extra structure. As positive logic was developed to generalise Robinson’s work on existentially closed models, the study of graphs with extra structure is a typical example where positive logic is applied in order to obtain universal models analogous to the random graph. Now, these analogues are not always suitable if one is focused on connected graphs.

For instance, let us take
\mathbb{Z}
as a graph (i.e. with a binary relation for graph distance
\leq 1
) with unary predicates for the intervals; the associated universal positively closed model turns out to be
\mathbb{Z}\cup\{\pm\infty\}
which can easily be understood as the “limit” obtained from the substructures
(-\infty,-n^{2}]\cup[-n,n]\cup[n^{2},\infty)
when
n\rightarrow\infty
. We can immediately see that this structure has three critically different parts (namely,
\mathbb{Z}
,
\infty
and
-\infty
). More precisely,
\mathbb{Z}
as graph can be visualised as a line and, while
\mathbb{Z}
is connected (as graph), the resulting universal positively closed model
\mathbb{Z}\cup\{\pm\infty\}
is disconnected with three different connected components. This is a natural consequence of the fact that positive logic takes into account all substructures of
\mathbb{Z}
, regardless of their connectedness. Local positive logic restricts our attention to just “connected” structures, allowing us to obtain only the “connected limits”. In this example, instead of
\mathbb{Z}\cup\{\pm\infty\}
, we separately obtain the three different connected components as locally positively closed models.

Remark 1.2
.

Let us make some remarks about
Definition

1.1

(i)

We only study relational languages with constants. This significantly simplifies the definition of the bound function
\mathtt{B}

This is common practice in positive logic, since those relations are forced to define graphs of functions in positively closed models. We leave as a task to develop local positive logic for languages with function symbols. The key issue is to find a good definition of
\mathtt{B}
for function symbols that is not too restrictive.

(ii)

We only consider locality relations between elements of the same sort. For locality relations between different sorts, it might be more natural to merge all these sorts in a new one and replace them by disjoint unary predicates. For positively closed models, these predicates would give a partition of the new sort.

(iii)

Locality relations are binary. We leave as an open task the development of a higher-arity local logic.

Remark 2.16
.

In the case of weakly complete theories, it is easy to identify approximations and complements. If

, concluding that
{\mathrm{Th}}_{-}(M)\models_{\mathtt{L}}\theta\perp\varphi
.

. Indeed, if
{\mathrm{Th}}_{-}(M)\models_{\mathtt{L}}\theta\perp\psi
, then

, so

\exists zw\mathrel{}z=w\in{\mathtt{T}}_{+}
for

, so
\exists xyzw\mathrel{}(\psi(x,y)\wedge{\mathrm{d}}_{s}(x,z)\wedge{\mathrm{d}}_{s}(y,w)\wedge z=w)\in{\mathtt{T}}_{+}
by
{\mathrm{d}}
-irreducibility, concluding
\exists xy\mathrel{}(\psi(x,y)\wedge{\mathrm{d}}_{s}\ast{\mathrm{d}}_{s}(x,y))\in{\mathtt{T}}_{+}
by

and
A3
, which contradicts

Conversely, suppose
{\mathtt{T}}
is weakly complete and every locally positively closed model of
{\mathtt{T}}
is a ball. Pick

with
{\mathtt{T}}={\mathrm{Th}}_{-}(M)
. Let

be a choice of elements for each sort of

and

a choice of locality relations such that

for each

. Say
\exists x\mathrel{}\varphi(x)\in{\mathtt{T}}_{+}
and
\exists y\mathrel{}\psi(y)\in{\mathtt{T}}_{+}
with

positive formulas and

and

disjoint finite variables on the same sort. Then,

and

for some

and

is
{\mathrm{d}}\ast{\mathrm{d}}
-irreducible, where

∎

Remark 3.10
.

When
x
is a finite variable, the bounds of

are quantifier free positive formulas on

constructed using only locality predicates and constants.

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
\varphi(x)
be a positive formula such that
\exists x\mathrel{}\varphi(x)\in{\mathtt{T}}_{+}
. Then, there is a bound
\mathrm{B}(x)
of

such that
\exists x\mathrel{}(\mathrm{B}(x)\wedge\varphi(x))\in{\mathtt{T}}_{+}
.

Proof.

Suppose
\exists x\mathrel{}\varphi(x)\in{\mathtt{T}}_{+}
. Take
M
witnessing it and

such that
M\models_{\mathtt{L}}\varphi(a)
. As
M
is local, there is a bound
\mathrm{B}(x)
such that
M\models_{\mathtt{L}}\mathrm{B}(a)
. Therefore,
M\models_{\mathtt{L}}\exists x\mathrel{}(\mathrm{B}(x)\wedge\varphi(x))
, concluding
\exists x\mathrel{}(\mathrm{B}(x)\wedge\varphi(x))\in{\mathtt{T}}_{+}
.

Theorem 3.12
.

Assume
{\mathtt{L}}
is countable. Then,
{\mathtt{T}}
is weakly complete if and only if it is irreducible.

Proof.

Let
I
be the family of quantifier free primitive positive formulas
\varphi
(up to renaming variables) such that
\exists x\mathrel{}\varphi(x)\in{\mathtt{T}}_{+}
. Without loss of generality, we pick disjoint variables
x_{\varphi}
for each
\varphi\in I
. Since
{\mathtt{L}}
is countable, fix an enumeration
\{\varphi_{i}(x_{i})\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of
I
.

By irreducibility, recursively define bounds
\mathrm{B}_{i}
of
x_{0}\ldots x_{i}
for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\exists x_{0}\ldots x_{i}\mathrel{}\psi_{i}\in{\mathtt{T}}_{+}
where
\psi_{i}\coloneqq\bigwedge_{j\leq i}\varphi_{j}(x_{j})\wedge\mathrm{B}_{j}(x_{0}\ldots x_{j})
. Indeed, by
Lemma

3.11
, we can find
\mathrm{B}_{0}
. Now, given
\mathrm{B}_{0},\ldots,\mathrm{B}_{i}
, as
\exists x_{0}\ldots x_{i}\mathrel{}\psi_{i}\in{\mathtt{T}}_{+}
and
\exists x_{i+1}\mathrel{}\varphi_{i+1}(x_{i+1})\in{\mathtt{T}}_{+}
, by irreducibility and
Lemma

3.11
, there is a bound
\mathrm{B}_{i+1}(x_{0}\ldots x_{i+1})
such that
\exists x_{0}\ldots x_{i+1}\mathrel{}\psi_{i+1}=\exists x_{0}\ldots x_{i+1}\mathrel{}(\psi_{i}\wedge\varphi_{i+1}\wedge\mathrm{B}_{i+1})\in{\mathtt{T}}_{+}
.

Consider the partial type
\Sigma(x_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}=\{\psi_{i}(x_{0},\ldots,x_{i})\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
. Then, it is finitely satisfiable with
{\mathtt{T}}\wedge{\mathtt{T}}_{\mathrm{loc}}
by construction, so there is some structure
M^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathord{\models}_{\star}}{\mathtt{T}}\wedge{\mathtt{T}}_{\mathrm{loc}}
and
a\coloneqq(a_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
in
M^{\prime}
realising
\Sigma
. Pick a subtuple
b
of
a
containing one element for each sort and consider
M=\mathtt{D}(b)
, which is certainly a local
{\mathtt{L}}
-structure and contains
a
. By
Lemma

1.8
,
M\models_{\mathtt{L}}{\mathtt{T}}
and
M\models_{\mathtt{L}}\Sigma(a)
. Thus,
{\mathrm{Th}}_{-}(M^{\prime})={\mathtt{T}}
as required.
∎

A
(local) weak completion
of
{\mathtt{T}}
is a negative local theory that is minimal among the weakly complete negative local theories containing
{\mathtt{T}}
. A
(local) completion
of
{\mathtt{T}}
is a negative local theory that is minimal among the complete negative local theories containing
{\mathtt{T}}
.

Lemma 3.13
.

Suppose
\widetilde{{\mathtt{T}}}
is a weak completion of
{\mathtt{T}}
. Then,
\widetilde{{\mathtt{T}}}={\mathrm{Th}}_{-}(M)
for some
M\models^{\mathrm{pc}}_{\mathtt{L}}{\mathtt{T}}
.

Proof.

Take a local model
N\models_{\mathtt{L}}\widetilde{{\mathtt{T}}}
with
{\mathrm{Th}}_{-}(N)=\widetilde{{\mathtt{T}}}
. Obviously,
N\models_{\mathtt{L}}{\mathtt{T}}
. By
Theorem

2.9
, there is a homomorphism
f\colon N\to M
to
M\models^{\mathrm{pc}}_{\mathtt{L}}{\mathtt{T}}
. By
Remark

1.11
,
N
is a local model of
\widetilde{{\mathtt{T}}}_{\pm}
. As
f
is a homomorphism, by
Remark

2.2
, we have that
M\models_{\mathtt{L}}\varphi
for any
\varphi\in\widetilde{{\mathtt{T}}}_{+}
. Hence,
\widetilde{{\mathtt{T}}}_{+}\subseteq{\mathrm{Th}}_{+}(M)
, concluding that
{\mathtt{T}}\subseteq{\mathrm{Th}}_{-}(M)\subseteq\widetilde{{\mathtt{T}}}
. By minimality of
\widetilde{{\mathtt{T}}}
, it follows that
\widetilde{{\mathtt{T}}}={\mathrm{Th}}_{-}(M)
.
∎

Remark 3.14
.

By
Lemma

3.13
, in particular, the weak completions of
{\mathtt{T}}
are exactly the minimal elements of
\{{\mathrm{Th}}_{-}(M):M\models^{\mathrm{pc}}_{\mathtt{L}}{\mathtt{T}}\}
.

Lemma 3.15
.

Assume
{\mathtt{L}}
is countable. Every locally satisfiable negative local theory has a weak completion.

Proof.

Let
{\mathtt{T}}_{0}
be a locally satisfiable negative local theory. Consider the family of weakly complete negative local theories containing
{\mathtt{T}}_{0}
. Note that it is not empty as
{\mathtt{T}}_{0}
is locally satisfiable and
{\mathrm{Th}}_{-}(M)
for any
M\models_{\mathtt{L}}{\mathtt{T}}
is a weakly complete extension of it. By Zorn’s Lemma, it is sufficient to show that if
\mathcal{T}
is a (non-empty) family of weakly complete negative local theories containing
{\mathtt{T}}_{0}
linearly ordered under inclusion, then
{\mathtt{T}}=\bigcap\mathcal{T}
is also a weakly complete negative local theory containing
{\mathtt{T}}_{0}
. Trivially,
{\mathtt{T}}_{0}\subseteq{\mathtt{T}}
. Certainly,
{\mathtt{T}}
is locally satisfiable, since any local model of any
{\mathtt{T}}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}
is also a local model of
{\mathtt{T}}
. It is also obviously closed under local implication. Indeed, if
{\mathtt{T}}\models_{\mathtt{L}}\varphi
for some negative sentence
\varphi
, then
{\mathtt{T}}^{\prime}\models_{\mathtt{L}}\varphi
for any
{\mathtt{T}}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}
, so
\varphi\in{\mathtt{T}}^{\prime}
for all
{\mathtt{T}}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}
, concluding
\varphi\in{\mathtt{T}}
.

Finally, by
Theorem

3.12
, it is enough to show that
{\mathtt{T}}
is irreducible. Suppose
\exists x\mathrel{}\varphi(x)
and
\exists y\mathrel{}\psi(y)
are in
{\mathtt{T}}_{+}
, with
\varphi(x)
and
\psi(y)
positive formulas and
x
and
y
disjoint variables on the same sort. Then, there are
{\mathtt{T}}^{\prime},{\mathtt{T}}^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{T}
such that
\exists x\mathrel{}\varphi(x)\in{\mathtt{T}}^{\prime}_{+}
and
\exists y\mathrel{}\psi(y)\in{\mathtt{T}}^{\prime\prime}_{+}
. Since
\mathcal{T}
is linearly ordered, we may assume
{\mathtt{T}}^{\prime}={\mathtt{T}}^{\prime\prime}
. Since
{\mathtt{T}}^{\prime}
is irreducible by
Theorem

3.12
, we conclude
\exists xy\mathrel{}(\varphi(x)\wedge\psi(y))\in{\mathtt{T}}^{\prime}_{+}
. Therefore, since
{\mathtt{T}}\subseteq{\mathtt{T}}^{\prime}
, we get
{\mathtt{T}}^{\prime}_{+}\subseteq{\mathtt{T}}_{+}
, so
\exists xy\mathrel{}(\varphi(x)\wedge\psi(y))\in{\mathtt{T}}_{+}
as required.
∎

Remark 3.16
.

Note that in

3.15
the countability assumption on
{\mathtt{L}}
is only used to apply

3.12
. Without this assumption, the previous lemma shows that every negative local theory has an

, i.e. a minimal irreducible negative local theory extending it.

Example 3.17
.

In uncountable languages, weak completions may not exist:

Consider the theory
{\mathtt{T}}
from

(

, the theory

extending
{\mathtt{T}}
but only allowing

. Then each

is irreducible and, since it is effectively a theory of a countable language, it is weakly complete by

is by definition the theory of a model

of

Lemma 3.18
.

Suppose

satisfies that
{\mathrm{Th}}_{-}(N)
is complete. Then,
{\mathrm{Th}}_{-}(N)
is a completion of
{\mathtt{T}}
and maximal in
\{{\mathrm{Th}}_{-}(M):M\models^{\mathrm{pc}}_{\mathtt{L}}{\mathtt{T}}\}

Proof.

Let
{\mathtt{T}}\subseteq\widetilde{{\mathtt{T}}}\subseteq{\mathrm{Th}}_{-}(N)

Let

4.
Inherent locality

Whenever one modifies a concept, it is useful to find the conditions under which the new concept behaves like the old one. The following definition seems to be the correct one for negative local theories and general negative theories. Recall that, unless otherwise stated, we have fixed a local language
{\mathtt{L}}
and a locally satisfiable negative local theory
{\mathtt{T}}
.

Definition 4.1

(Inherently local theories)
.

We say that

is

if every

To understand this definition, we note the following fact:

,

By

and

can be specified in the form

for

positive.

(

\forall xy\mathrel{}({\mathrm{d}}(x,y)\rightarrow{\mathrm{d}}(y,x))
for

an arbitrary locality relation.

\forall xy\mathrel{}({\mathrm{d}}_{1}(x,y)\rightarrow{\mathrm{d}}_{2}(x,y))

Remark 4.6
.

To get an inherently local theory

which is not irreducible, thus certainly without the local joint continuation property, just take any negative theory which is not irreducible and add a locality relation which holds between any two elements. For example, consider the theory

where

are unary relation symbols, which satisfies

. If one now adds
\mathtt{D}=\{=,\top\}
(with the trivial ordered monoid structure), then
{\mathtt{T}}
becomes inherently local, since all positively closed models are singletons.

Every uniformly irreducible negative local theory is inherently local.

Assume

for each sort for every local positively closed model. Let

Example 4.9
.

The converse of
Corollary

4.8
does not hold, i.e. inherently local does not imply uniform irreducibility.

Consider the one-sorted local language
{\mathtt{L}}
with locality relations
\mathtt{D}=\{{\mathrm{d}}_{m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
(with the usual ordered monoid structure induced by the natural numbers) and binary predicates
\{e_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. Consider the local
{\mathtt{L}}
-structure in
\mathbb{Z}
given by
{\mathrm{d}}_{m}(x,y)\Leftrightarrow|x-y|\leq m
and
e_{n}(x,y)\Leftrightarrow|x-y|=n
. Let
{\mathtt{T}}
be the negative local theory of
\mathbb{Z}
. Clearly, a map
f\colon\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}
is an automorphism if and only if it is distance preserving, if and only if it is of the form
x\mapsto x+a
or
x\mapsto-x+a
.

We claim that every type is isolated by a formula like
\bigwedge_{i<j<n}e_{n_{i,j}}(x_{i},x_{j})
; call those formulas
full distance formulas
. Indeed, assume
a
and
b
are tuples such that
\left|a_{i}-a_{j}\right|=\left|b_{i}-b_{j}\right|
for all
i,j<n
. Without loss of generality,
a_{0}<...<a_{n-1}
(if
a_{i}=a_{j}
for
i\neq j
, we can safely omit
a_{j}
). Denote
c_{i}\coloneqq a_{i+1}-a_{i}
. We have
\left|b_{0}-b_{1}\right|=\left|a_{0}-a_{1}\right|\neq 0
, so we have two options:

(1)

If
b_{0}<b_{1}
, then we have that

\left|b_{2}-b_{0}\right|=\left|a_{2}-a_{0}\right|=c_{0}+c_{1}=\left|b_{2}-b_{1}\right|+\left|b_{1}-b_{0}\right|.

Obviously, for all
x,y,z
, we have
\left|x-z\right|=\left|y-x\right|+\left|z-y\right|
if and only if
x\leq y\leq z
or
z\leq y\leq x
. Therefore,
b_{0}<b_{1}<b_{2}
(since
b_{2}\leq b_{1}\leq b_{0}
is impossible). Likewise, we get
b_{0}<b_{1}<\cdots<b_{n-1}
. Thus,

b_{i}=b_{0}+\sum_{j<i}(b_{j+1}-b_{j})=b_{0}+\sum_{j<i}(a_{j+1}-a_{j})=b_{0}-a_{0}+a_{i}.

Therefore,
x\mapsto x+b_{0}-a_{0}
maps
a
to
b
.

(2)

If
b_{1}<b_{0}
, we likewise conclude
b_{n-1}<\cdots<b_{0}
. Thus,

b_{i}=b_{0}-\sum_{j<i}(b_{j}-b_{j+1})=b_{0}-\sum_{j<i}(a_{j}-a_{j+1})=b_{0}-a_{0}+a_{i}.

Therefore,
x\mapsto b_{0}-a_{0}-x
maps
a
to
b
.

Now, we claim that every positive formula is equivalent in
\mathbb{Z}
to a disjunction of full distance formulas (i.e. a positive boolean combination of
e
relations).

Since
{\mathrm{d}}_{m}
is equivalent to
\bigvee_{n\leq m}e_{n}
, by induction on formula complexity we only need to show that, if
\theta
is equivalent to a finite disjunction of full distance formulas, then so is
\varphi=\exists y\mathrel{}\theta
. By distributivity of
\exists
over disjunctions, elimination of
\exists
over dummy variables, symmetry of
e_{m}
for each
m
and the fact that
e_{n}(x,y)\wedge e_{m}(x,y)
is inconsistent for
m\neq n
, it suffices to show that every formula of the form
\varphi(x)=\exists y\mathrel{}\bigwedge_{i<n}e_{m_{i}}(x_{i},y)
is equivalent in
\mathbb{Z}
to a finite disjunction of full distance formulas.

We prove this by induction on
n
. If
n=1
, then
\exists y\,e_{m_{0}}(x_{0},y)
is just equivalent to
e_{0}(x_{0},x_{0})
. Suppose it holds for
n-1>1
. Then, noting that
e_{m_{i}}(x_{i},y)\wedge e_{m_{j}}(x_{j},y)
implies
e_{m_{i}+m_{j}}(x_{i},x_{j})\vee e_{|m_{i}-m_{j}|}(x_{i},x_{j})
, we get that
\varphi
implies

\bigwedge_{i<j<n}(e_{m_{i}+m_{j}}(x_{i},x_{j})\vee e_{|m_{i}-m_{j}|}(x_{i},x_{j})),

which is equivalent by distributivity to a finite disjunction of full distance formulas.

Let
\Sigma(x)
be the set of all full distance formulas in this disjunction that imply
\varphi
. Since each full distance formula isolates a type, if
\psi
is a full distance formula that does not imply
\varphi
, then
\mathbb{Z}\models_{\mathtt{L}}\neg\exists x\mathrel{}(\varphi\wedge\psi)
. If
\psi
is a full distance formula that does not appear in the disjunction, it is inconsistent with it in
\mathbb{Z}
(since different full distance formulas are inconsistent). Therefore,
\mathbb{Z}\models_{\mathtt{L}}\forall x\mathrel{}(\varphi\leftrightarrow\bigvee\Sigma)
, as required.

We can deduce from this that
{\mathtt{T}}
is inherently local. Indeed, by
Theorem

4.4
, we need to show that every finitely locally satisfiable set
\Gamma(x,y)
of positive formulas on two single variables is locally satisfiable.

Assume
\Gamma
is finitely locally satisfiable in
{\mathtt{T}}
. Then, for any finite subset
\Gamma_{0}\subseteq\Gamma
we have
\neg\exists xy\mathrel{}\bigwedge\Gamma_{0}\notin{\mathtt{T}}
. That means there exist
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
realising
\Gamma_{0}
. If every
\varphi\in\Gamma
is equivalent in
\mathbb{Z}
to
\top
(i.e.
e_{0}(x,x)
), there is nothing to show. Thus, fix some
\varphi_{0}(x,y)\in\Gamma
not equivalent to
\top
.

By the previous discussion,
\varphi_{0}(x,y)
is equivalent to a disjunction
\bigvee_{i<k}\psi_{i}(x,y)
of full distance formulas. At least one of the
\psi_{i}
is realised in
\mathbb{Z}
since
\varphi
is realised. Since
e_{n}\wedge e_{m}
for
n\neq m
is inconsistent (and
\varphi_{0}
is consistent), we may assume that every
\psi_{i}
is just
e_{n_{i}}(x,y)
, so
\varphi_{0}
is equivalent to
\bigvee_{i<k}e_{n_{i}}(x,y)
. Denote by
A_{\varphi_{0}}
the set
\{n_{i}:i<k\}
.

Since the same holds for any
\varphi\in\Gamma
, if
\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{m}\in\Gamma
, then
\mathbb{Z}\models_{\mathtt{L}}\exists xy\mathrel{}\bigwedge_{i=1}^{m}\varphi_{i}
, so
\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}A_{\varphi_{i}}\neq\emptyset
. Since
A_{\varphi_{0}}
is finite, sets of the form
A_{\varphi_{0}}\cap\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}A_{\varphi_{i}}
have a minimum (which is not empty), thus, for any
n
in that minimum,
e_{n}
implies
\varphi
for any
\varphi\in\Gamma
, so
\mathbb{Z}\models_{\mathtt{L}}\Gamma(0,n)
, as required.

On the other hand,
{\mathtt{T}}
is not uniformly irreducible, since for any fixed

, we have that
\exists x_{0}x_{1}\mathrel{}x_{0}=x_{1}
and
\exists y_{0}y_{1}\mathrel{}e_{n_{0}+n_{1}+1}(y_{0},y_{1})
are both true in

, but

\neg\exists x_{0}x_{1}y_{0}y_{1}\mathrel{}(x_{0}=x_{1}\wedge e_{n_{0}+n_{1}+1}(y_{0},y_{1})\wedge{\mathrm{d}}_{n_{0}}(x_{0},y_{0})\wedge{\mathrm{d}}_{n_{1}}(x_{1},y_{1}))\in{\mathtt{T}}.

In the context of one-sorted languages, the reasoning in
Example

4.9
is somehow optimal, in the sense that it can be generalised to get a sufficient condition under which the local joint continuation property implies inherent locality.

Lemma 4.10
.

Assume
{\mathtt{T}}
is a negative local theory with the local joint continuation property in a one-sorted language
{\mathtt{L}}
. Assume further that for any atomic formula
\varphi(x,y,z)
with
x
and
y
single variables, there is some
{\mathrm{d}}\in\mathtt{D}
such that

(\exists z\mathrel{}\varphi(x,y,z))\leq{\mathrm{d}}(x,y).

Then,
{\mathtt{T}}
is inherently local.

Proof.

By
Theorem

4.4
, it suffices to show that for any
N\mathrel{\mathord{\models}^{\mathrm{pc}}_{\star}}{\mathtt{T}}
and any
a_{0},a_{1}\in N
there is some
{\mathrm{d}}\in\mathtt{D}
such that
N\models_{\mathtt{L}}{\mathrm{d}}(a_{0},a_{1})
. In other words, it suffices to show that
\mathtt{D}(a)=N
for any
a\in N
, where
\mathtt{D}(a)
denotes the local component at
a
. By
Lemma

2.15
, if
(\exists z\mathrel{}\varphi(x,y,z))\leq{\mathrm{d}}(x,y)
, then
N\mathrel{\mathord{\models}_{\star}}\forall xy\mathrel{}((\exists z\mathrel{}\varphi(x,y,z))\rightarrow{\mathrm{d}}(x,y))
.

Let
\kappa>|N|^{+}
, and take some
local homomorphism
\kappa
-universal model

M
of
{\mathtt{T}}
, i.e. a local model
M
of
{\mathtt{T}}
such that every local model of
{\mathtt{T}}
of size at most
\kappa
admits a homomorphism to
M
— such a model exists by the local joint continuation property and
Lemmas

2.8
and
2.9
.

Assume towards a contradiction that, for some
a_{0}\in N
, we have
\mathtt{D}(a_{0})\neq N
, and pick
a_{1}\in N\setminus\mathtt{D}(a_{0})
. For any
{\mathrm{d}}\in\mathtt{D}
there exists, by
[
Hru22b
, Lemma 2.3(1)]
(or, for more original references,
[
BY03
, Lemma 1.26]
and
[
BYP07
, Lemma 14]

\theta_{\mathrm{d}}(x)
(with variable

) such that

{\mathtt{T}}\mathrel{\mathord{\models}_{\star}}\neg\exists x_{0}x_{1}\mathrel{}((\exists x_{2}\ldots x_{n-1}\mathrel{}\theta_{\mathrm{d}}(x))\wedge{\mathrm{d}}(x_{0},x_{1}))

Fix

Define the equivalence relation

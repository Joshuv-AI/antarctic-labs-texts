Topology of Lagrangian fibrations and Hodge theory of hyper-Kähler manifolds
Junliang Shen, Qizheng Yin

Abstract
. We establish a compact analogue of the P = W P=W conjecture. For a projective irreducible holomorphic symplectic variety with a Lagrangian fibration, we show that the perverse numbers associated with the fibration match perfectly with the Hodge numbers of the total space. This builds a new connection between the topology of Lagrangian fibrations and the Hodge theory of hyper-Kähler manifolds. We present two applications of our result, one on the cohomology of the base and fibers of a Lagrangian fibration, the other on the refined Gopakumar–Vafa invariants of a K ​ 3 K3 surface. Furthermore, we show that the perverse filtration associated with a Lagrangian fibration is multiplicative under cup product.

0.5.
Idea of the proof

The proof of Theorem
0.2
compares two structures, one on each end of the equality (
4
). It manifests some form of symmetry between holomorphic symplectic geometry and hyper-Kähler geometry.

On the perverse side, there is an action of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}\times\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
on
the graded pieces

\bigoplus_{i}\mathrm{Gr}_{i}^{P}H^{*}(M,\mathbb{Q}),

which arises from the decomposition theorem package and keeps track of the perverse grading. An important observation is that for
M
holomorphic symplectic, this action admits a natural lifting to
H^{*}(M,\mathbb{Q})
via the Beauville–Bogomolov form of
M
; see Proposition
1.1
.

On the Hodge side, the key ingredient is the action of a special orthogonal Lie algebra on the cohomology
H^{*}(M,\mathbb{C})
of a compact hyper-Kähler manifold
M
. An action of
\mathfrak{so}(5)
was first discovered by Verbitsky
[
48
]
, and was used to keep track of the Hodge grading. It was later generalized in
[
49
,
50
]
to an action of

\mathfrak{so}(b_{2}(M)+2),

where
b_{2}(M)
is the second Betti number of
M
. The same result was also obtained by Looijenga and Lunts
[
31
, Section 4]
.

Finally, we construct a variation of
\mathfrak{so}(5)
-actions over the Zariski closure of the space of twistor lines. This provides a “continuous deformation” from the decomposition theorem package associated with
\pi:M\to B
to the Hodge decomposition of
M
; see Theorem
3.1
. The dimensions of the weight spaces are conserved under this variation, proving Theorem
0.2
.

A similar argument also yields the multiplicativity of the perverse filtration on
H^{*}(M,\mathbb{Q})
. This is of independent interest and is included in Appendix
A
. It is worth mentioning that multiplicativity turns out to be the only remaining step in proving the original
P=W
conjecture following
[
10
]
.

2.
Hyper-Kähler manifolds

This section concerns the Hodge side. We first review Verbitsky’s construction of an
\mathfrak{so}
-action on the cohomology of a compact hyper-Kähler manifold following
[
31
,
48
,
49
,
50
]
. Then we study a certain variation of
\mathfrak{so}(5)
-actions over the Zariski closure of the space of twistor lines. We shall see in Section
3
that variations of
\mathfrak{so}(5)
-actions play the same role as hyper-Kähler rotations in the conjectural
P=W
picture. Throughout this section, let
M
be a compact hyper-Kähler manifold of complex dimension
2n
.

2.1.
The
\mathfrak{so}(5)
-action

A hyper-Kähler manifold is a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
which is Kähler with respect to three complex structures
I,J
, and
K
satisfying the quaternion relations

I^{2}=J^{2}=K^{2}=IJK=-1.

We view
(M,I)
as a complex manifold which admits a hyper-Kähler structure, and we use
\omega_{I},\omega_{J}
, and
\omega_{K}
to denote the corresponding Kähler classes in
H^{2}(M,{\mathbb{C}})
.

By the hard Lefschetz theorem, each Kähler class
\omega_{\ast}
(
\ast\in\{I,J,K\}
) gives rise to a Lefschetz triple
(L_{\ast},H,\Lambda_{\ast})
acting on the cohomology
H^{\ast}(M,{\mathbb{C}})
,

\rho_{\ast}:\mathfrak{sl}_{2}\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(H^{\ast}(M,{\mathbb{C}})).

Here

\displaystyle L_{\ast}

\displaystyle:H^{i}(M,{\mathbb{C}})\to H^{i+2}(M,{\mathbb{C}}),\quad L_{\ast}(v)=\omega_{*}\cup v,

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle:H^{i}(M,{\mathbb{C}})\to H^{i}(M,{\mathbb{C}}),\quad H(v)=(i-2n)v,

and the action

\Lambda_{\ast}:H^{i}(M,{\mathbb{C}})\to H^{i-2}(M,{\mathbb{C}})

is uniquely determined by
L_{\ast}
and
H
. We recall the main result of
[
48
]
.

Theorem 2.1

(Verbitsky
[
48
]
)
.

The three
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-actions
\rho_{I},\rho_{J}
, and
\rho_{K}
generate an
\mathfrak{so}(5)
-action on the cohomology
H^{\ast}(M,{\mathbb{C}})
, with

\mathfrak{h}=\left\langle H,H^{\prime}=-\sqrt{-1}[L_{J},\Lambda_{K}]\right\rangle\subset\mathfrak{so}(5)

a rank
2
Cartan subalgebra. Moreover, the corresponding weight decomposition of
H^{\ast}(M,{\mathbb{C}})
coincides with the Hodge decomposition of the Kähler manifold
(M,I)
.

For the second statement, let

H^{*}(M,{\mathbb{C}})=\bigoplus_{i,j}H^{i,j}(M),

be the Hodge decomposition of
M
. Then for any
v\in H^{i,j}(M)
we have

H(v)=(i+j-2n)v,\quad H^{\prime}(v)=(i-j)v.

2.2.
Larger
\mathfrak{so}
-actions

The cohomology algebra
H^{*}(M,{\mathbb{C}})
is a
Frobenius–Lefschetz algebra
in the sense of
[
31
]
. Recall the scaling

H:H^{i}(M,{\mathbb{C}})\to H^{i}(M,{\mathbb{C}}),\quad H(v)=(i-2n)v.

An element
\omega\in H^{2}(M,{\mathbb{C}})
is called of Lefschetz type if there is an associated Lefschetz triple
(L_{\omega},H,\Lambda_{\omega})
acting on
H^{\ast}(M,{\mathbb{C}})
,

\rho_{\omega}:\mathfrak{sl}_{2}\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(H^{\ast}(M,{\mathbb{C}})).

The structure Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}(M)\subset\mathrm{End}(H^{*}(M,{\mathbb{C}}))
is defined to be the Lie subalgebra generated by
\rho_{\omega}
for all
\omega\in H^{2}(M,{\mathbb{C}})
of Lefschetz type.

The following theorem extends the
\mathfrak{so}(5)
-action to a larger
\mathfrak{so}
-action on the cohomology
H^{\ast}(M,{\mathbb{C}})
.

Theorem 2.2

(Looijenga–Lunts
[
31
, Section 4]
, Verbitsky
[
49
,
50
]
)
.

The structure Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}(M)
is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{so}(b_{2}(M)+2)
, where
b_{2}(M)
is the second Betti number of
M
.

2.3.
Twistor lines

As is explained in
[
49
, Introduction]
, the key to the proof of Theorem
2.2
is the following observation.

Let
q_{M}(-)
denote the Beauville–Bogomolov quadratic form on the second cohomology
H^{2}(M,{\mathbb{C}})
, and let
(-,-)_{M}
be the corresponding bilinear form
[
1
]
. Given a hyper-Kähler structure
(g,I,J,K)
on
M
, the three Kähler classes

\omega_{I},\omega_{J}
, and
\omega_{K}
satisfy

(18)

\begin{gathered}q_{M}(\omega_{I})=q_{M}(\omega_{J})=q_{M}(\omega_{K}),\\
(\omega_{I},\omega_{J})_{M}=(\omega_{J},\omega_{K})_{M}=(\omega_{K},\omega_{I})_{M}=0.\end{gathered}

Let
\mathrm{Hyp}
be the classifying space of the hyper-Kähler structures on
M
. We consider the map

(19)

\mathrm{Hyp}\to H^{2}(M,{\mathbb{C}})\times H^{2}(M,{\mathbb{C}})\times H^{2}(M,{\mathbb{C}})

which sends a hyper-Kähler structure
(g,I,J,K)
to the triple
(\omega_{I},\omega_{J},\omega_{K})
. By (
18
), the image of (
19
) lies in the quasi-affine variety

(20)

D^{\circ}\subset H^{2}(M,{\mathbb{C}})\times H^{2}(M,{\mathbb{C}})\times H^{2}(M,{\mathbb{C}})

defined by

\Big\{(x,y,z):q_{M}(x)=q_{M}(y)=q_{M}(z)\neq 0,\,(x,y)_{M}=(y,z)_{M}=(z,x)_{M}=0\Big\}.

The key observation is a density statement, which is a consequence of the Calabi–Yau theorem and local Torelli.

Proposition 2.3
.

The image of (
19
) is Zariski-dense in
D^{\circ}
. In particular, all algebraic relations which hold for triples
(\omega_{I},\omega_{J},\omega_{K})
still hold for all
(x,y,z)\in D^{\circ}
where the relations are defined.

Proof.

It was shown in
[
49
, Theorem 1.5]
that the image of (
19
) is Zariski-dense in the real points
D^{\circ}({\mathbb{R}})
of the complex algebraic variety
D^{\circ}
. Hence it suffices to verify that
D^{\circ}({\mathbb{R}})
is Zariski-dense in
D^{\circ}
.

We consider the dominant map

f:D^{\circ}\rightarrow\mathrm{Gr}(3,b_{2}(M))

sending a triple
(x,y,z)\in D^{\circ}
to the linear subspace
\langle x,y,z\rangle\subset H^{2}(M,{\mathbb{C}})
. If

\langle x,y,z\rangle\in\mathrm{Gr}(3,b_{2}(M))(\mathbb{R})

and the
restriction of
q_{M}(-)
to
\langle x,y,z\rangle
is positive definite, then the fiber of
f
over
\langle x,y,z\rangle
contains a real point and admits a transitive action of
O(3)\times\mathbb{G}_{m}
defined over
\mathbb{R}
. In particular, the real points are Zariski-dense in the fiber of
f
over a positive definite
3
-space. On the other hand, the positive definite
3
-spaces are Zariski-dense in
\mathrm{Gr}(3,b_{2}(M))(\mathbb{R})
, hence in
\mathrm{Gr}(3,b_{2}(M))
.

Therefore, the Zariski closure
\overline{D^{\circ}({\mathbb{R}})}
of
D^{\circ}({\mathbb{R}})
contains the fibers of
f
over a Zariski-dense subset of the base
\mathrm{Gr}(3,b_{2}(M))
. This shows that
\overline{D^{\circ}({\mathbb{R}})}
dominates
\mathrm{Gr}(3,b_{2}(M))
with general fibers of the same dimension as the fiber of
f
over a positive definite
3
-space. Since
D^{\circ}
is irreducible, a dimension count yields
\overline{D^{\circ}({\mathbb{R}})}=D^{\circ}
.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

We shall see in the proof of Theorem
3.1
that the complex points of

play an essential role in capturing the perverse filtration associated with a Lagrangian fibration. It is therefore crucial to work over the total complex variety
D^{\circ}
rather than the twistor lines lying in

.

2.4.

\mathfrak{so}

We construct a family of

-actions on the cohomology

Let

For

Then, given a point

For every

Proof.

This is a direct consequence of Theorem

A Cartan subalgebra of the

We first view

We now construct an

for any

Note that if

Let
(L_{\ast},H,\Lambda_{\ast})
be the Lefschetz triple associated with

Then

L_{\sigma_{p}}=\frac{1}{2}(L_{y}+\sqrt{-1}L_{z}),\quad\Lambda_{\sigma_{p}}=\frac{1}{2}(\Lambda_{y}-\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{z}),\quad H_{\sigma_{p}}=[L_{\sigma_{p}},\Lambda_{\sigma_{p}}]

-triple
(L_{\sigma_{p}},H_{\sigma_{p}},\Lambda_{\sigma_{p}})\subset\mathfrak{so}(5)

\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple associated with
\bar{\sigma}_{p}
by

L_{\bar{\sigma}_{p}}=\frac{1}{2}(L_{y}-\sqrt{-1}L_{z}),\quad\Lambda_{\bar{\sigma}_{p}}=\frac{1}{2}(\Lambda_{y}+\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{z}),\quad H_{\bar{\sigma}_{p}}=[L_{\bar{\sigma}_{p}},\Lambda_{\bar{\sigma}_{p}}].

Then
(L_{\sigma_{p}},H_{\sigma_{p}},\Lambda_{\sigma_{p}})
and
(L_{\bar{\sigma}_{p}},H_{\bar{\sigma}_{p}},\Lambda_{\bar{\sigma}_{p}})
induce an
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}\times\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-action associated with the
\mathfrak{so}(5)
-action
\iota_{p}
. The connection between the
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}\times\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-action and the
\mathfrak{so}(5)
-action is the following: a vector
v\in H^{\ast}(M,{\mathbb{C}})
has weight

H(v)=(i+j-2n)v,\quad H^{\prime}(v)=(i-j)v

with respect to the Cartan subalgebra

\mathfrak{h}=\left\langle H,H^{\prime}=-\sqrt{-1}[L_{y},\Lambda_{z}]\right\rangle\subset\mathfrak{so}(5)

if and only if it has weight

H_{\sigma_{p}}(v)=(i-n)v,\quad H_{\bar{\sigma}_{p}}(v)=(j-n)v

under the
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}\times\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-action. In particular, the weight decompositions of these two actions coincide.

By
[
4
, Lemma 1.8]
, if an element
w\in H^{2}(M,{\mathbb{C}})
satisfies
q_{M}(w)=0
, then

w^{n+1}=0\in H^{2n+2}(M,{\mathbb{C}}).

Hence the weight decomposition of the
\mathfrak{so}(5)
-action
\iota_{p}
yields a primitive decomposition

(21)

\bigoplus_{0\leq i,j\leq n}{\mathbb{C}}[\sigma_{p}]/(\sigma_{p}^{n-i+1})\otimes{\mathbb{C}}[\bar{\sigma}_{p}]/(\bar{\sigma}_{p}^{n-j+1})\otimes W_{p}^{i,j}\xrightarrow{\sim}H^{\ast}(M,{\mathbb{C}})

for the induced
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}\times\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-action.

The following theorem connects the representation-theoretic description of
H^{\ast}(M,{\mathbb{C}})
to the perverse filtration associated with the Lagrangian fibration
\pi:M\to B
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
M
be a projective irreducible holomorphic symplectic variety equipped with a Lagrangian fibration
\pi:M\to B
, and let
D^{\circ}
be as in (
20
). Then there exist two points
p,p^{\prime}\in D^{\circ}
such that the weight decomposition of
\iota_{p}
splits the perverse filtration associated with
\pi
, and the weight decomposition of
\iota_{p^{\prime}}
coincides with the Hodge decomposition of
M
.

Proof.

We take
p^{\prime}
to be the triple
(\omega_{I},\omega_{J},\omega_{K})\in D^{\circ}
given by the hyper-Kähler structure
(g,I,J,K)
of
M
. To prove the theorem, it suffices to find
p\in D^{\circ}
splitting the perverse filtration associated with
\pi:M\to B
.

Let
\beta\in H^{2}(M,{\mathbb{Q}})
be the pullback of an ample class on
B
. For any ample class
\eta^{\prime}
on
M
, the signature of the Beauville–Bogomolov form implies that there is a unique
\lambda\in{\mathbb{Q}}
satisfying

q_{M}(\eta^{\prime}+\lambda\beta)=0.

We use
\eta
to denote this
\pi
-relative ample class
\eta^{\prime}+\lambda\beta\in H^{2}(M,{\mathbb{Q}})
. In particular, we have

(22)

q_{M}(\eta)=q_{M}(\beta)=0,\quad\eta^{n+1}=\beta^{n+1}=0.

We claim that there exists a point
{p}=(x,y,z)\in D^{\circ}
such that

\sigma_{p}=\eta,\quad\bar{\sigma}_{p}=\beta.

In fact, we take

y=\eta+\beta,\quad z=-\sqrt{-1}(\eta-\beta).

Then (
22
) implies that

q_{M}(y)=q_{M}(z)=2(\eta,\beta)\neq 0,\quad(y,z)_{M}=\sqrt{-1}\left(q_{M}(\eta)-q_{M}(\beta)\right)=0.

By scaling a nonzero vector of
H^{2}(M,{\mathbb{C}})
perpendicular to
y
and
z
with respect to
q_{M}(-)
, we obtain
x\in H^{2}(M,{\mathbb{C}})
with

q_{M}(x)=q_{M}(y)=q_{M}(z)\neq 0,\quad(x,y)_{M}=(y,z)_{M}=(z,x)_{M}=0,

which gives the point
p=(x,y,z)
as desired.

Lastly, we check that
\iota_{p}
splits the perverse filtration. From (
21
), we see that the cohomology
H^{\ast}(M,{\mathbb{Q}})
(viewed as a
{\mathbb{Q}}[\eta,\beta]
-module) admits a primitive decomposition of the form (
12
) with
r=n
. Here the
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}\times\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-action is with
{\mathbb{Q}}
-coefficients. Proposition
1.1
then implies (after tensoring with
{\mathbb{C}}
) that the perverse filtration on
H^{\ast}(M,{\mathbb{C}})
associated with
\pi:M\to B
has a canonical splitting given by the weight decomposition of the
\mathfrak{so}(5)
-action
\iota_{p}
. This completes the proof.
∎

The action

\iota_{p}:\mathfrak{so}(5)\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(H^{\ast}(M,{\mathbb{C}}))

attached to a point
p\in D^{\circ}
can be viewed as a “generalized” hyper-Kähler rotation from the original hyper-Kähler structure
p^{\prime}=(\omega_{I},\omega_{J},\omega_{K})\in D^{\circ}
. By Corollary
2.7
, Theorem
0.2
is a direct consequence of Theorem
3.1
.

In Appendix

, we shall see that the weight decomposition of
\iota_{p}
is compatible with cup product for all

.

Proof of Theorem
0.4

We apply the decomposition theorem
[

]
to the Lagrangian fibration
\pi:M\to B
,

For (a), let

be an ample class. Since

. This gives a nontrivial class

Appendix A
Multiplicativity of perverse filtrations

This appendix is devoted to the multiplicativity of the perverse filtration associated with a Lagrangian fibration.
Recall the moduli spaces
{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{Dol}}
and
{\mathcal{M}}_{B}
in Section
0.2
. On one hand, the
P=W
conjecture implies that the perverse filtration associated with the Hitchin fibration (
2
) is multiplicative under cup product,
i.e.
,

(23)

P_{k}H^{d}({\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{Dol}},{\mathbb{Q}})\times P_{k^{\prime}}H^{d^{\prime}}({\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{Dol}},{\mathbb{Q}})\xrightarrow{\cup}P_{k+k^{\prime}}H^{d+d^{\prime}}({\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{Dol}},{\mathbb{Q}}).

On the other hand, the multiplicativity (
23
) is also expected to play an essential role in the proof of the
P=W
conjecture. This is because the tautological classes on
{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{Dol}}
, whose weights are calculated explicitly on
{\mathcal{M}}_{B}
in
[
45
]
, are ring generators of the cohomology

H^{\ast}({\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{Dol}},{\mathbb{Q}})=H^{\ast}({\mathcal{M}}_{B},{\mathbb{Q}}).

We refer to
[
7
,
32
,
44
,
53
]
for more discussions on tautological classes, multiplicativity, and
P=W
.

We now prove a compact version of (
23
). In the spirit of Theorem
0.4
, we shall see that the multiplicativity of the perverse filtration is controlled effectively by Hodge theory.

Theorem A.1
.

For a projective irreducible holomorphic symplectic variety
M
equipped with a Lagrangian fibration
\pi:M\to B
, the perverse filtration on
H^{*}(M,{\mathbb{Q}})
is multiplicative under cup product,
i.e.
,

P_{k}H^{d}(M,{\mathbb{Q}})\times P_{k^{\prime}}H^{d^{\prime}}(M,{\mathbb{Q}})\xrightarrow{\cup}P_{k+k^{\prime}}H^{d+d^{\prime}}(M,{\mathbb{Q}}).

Recall the variety
D^{\circ}
introduced in (
20
). By Corollary
2.6
, each point
p=(x,y,z)\in D^{\circ}
gives rise to an
\mathfrak{so}(5)
-action

\iota_{p}:\mathfrak{so}(5)\to\mathrm{End}(H^{*}(M,{\mathbb{C}})).

There is a weight decomposition

H^{\ast}(M,{\mathbb{C}})=\bigoplus_{i,j}H^{i,j}(\iota_{p})

with respect to the Cartan subalgebra

\mathfrak{h}=\left\langle H,H^{\prime}=-\sqrt{-1}[L_{y},\Lambda_{z}]\right\rangle\subset\mathfrak{so}(5).

Here for any
v\in H^{i,j}(\iota_{p})
, we have

H(v)=(i+j-2n)v,\quad H^{\prime}(v)=(i-j)v.

In view of Theorem
3.1
and the canonical splitting of the perverse filtration on
H^{*}(M,{\mathbb{C}})
, Theorem
A.1
is implied by the following more general statement.

Proposition A.2
.

For every
p\in D^{\circ}
, we have

(24)

H^{i,j}(\iota_{p})\times H^{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}(\iota_{p})\xrightarrow{\cup}H^{i+i^{\prime},j+j^{\prime}}(\iota_{p}).

Proof.

We first show that Poincaré duality satisfies

(25)

H^{i,j}(\iota_{p})^{*}=H^{2n-i,2n-j}(\iota_{p}).

For this we look at the class of the diagonal

\Delta\in H^{4n}(M\times M,{\mathbb{C}}).

The duality
H^{d}(M,{\mathbb{C}})^{*}=H^{4n-d}(M,{\mathbb{C}})
corresponds to the Künneth component

\Delta^{4n-d,d}\in H^{4n-d}(M,{\mathbb{C}})\otimes H^{d}(M,{\mathbb{C}}).

We consider the induced
\mathfrak{so}(5)
-action on the tensor product

H^{*}(M\times M,{\mathbb{C}})=H^{*}(M,{\mathbb{C}})\otimes H^{*}(M,{\mathbb{C}}).

Here for any
v\in H^{i,j}(\iota_{p})\otimes H^{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}(\iota_{p})
, we have

H(v)=(i+i^{\prime}+j+j^{\prime}-4n)v,\quad H^{\prime}(v)=(i+i^{\prime}-j-j^{\prime})v.

The duality (
25
) is equivalent to

\Delta^{4n-d,d}\in\bigoplus_{i+j=d}H^{2n-i,2n-j}(\iota_{p})\otimes H^{i,j}(\iota_{p}).

Then it is enough to show that
H^{\prime}(\Delta)=0
.

If
p=(\omega_{I},\omega_{J},\omega_{K})\in D^{\circ}
for some hyper-Kähler structure
(g,I,J,K)
, then (
25
) holds by Hodge theory, and hence
H^{\prime}(\Delta)=0
. Now since
H^{\prime}
varies algebraically over
D^{\circ}
, the density statement of Proposition
2.3
implies
H^{\prime}(\Delta)=0
for all
p\in D^{\circ}
.

We use exactly the same argument to prove (
24
). The cup product on
H^{*}(M,{\mathbb{C}})
is governed by the class of the small diagonal

\Delta_{3}\in H^{8n}(M\times M\times M,{\mathbb{C}}).

More precisely, the product

H^{d}(M,{\mathbb{C}})\times H^{d^{\prime}}(M,{\mathbb{C}})\xrightarrow{\cup}H^{d+d^{\prime}}(M,{\mathbb{C}})

corresponds via Poincaré duality to the Künneth component

\Delta_{3}^{4n-d,4n-d^{\prime},d+d^{\prime}}\in H^{4n-d}(M,{\mathbb{C}})\otimes H^{4n-d^{\prime}}(M,{\mathbb{C}})\otimes H^{d+d^{\prime}}(M,{\mathbb{C}}).

This time we consider the induced
\mathfrak{so}(5)
-action on the tensor product

H^{*}(M\times M\times M,{\mathbb{C}})=H^{*}(M,{\mathbb{C}})\otimes H^{*}(M,{\mathbb{C}})\otimes H^{*}(M,{\mathbb{C}}).

Then for any
v\in H^{i,j}(\iota_{p})\otimes H^{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}(\iota_{p})\otimes H^{i^{\prime\prime},j^{\prime\prime}}(\iota_{p})
, we have

H(v)=(i+i^{\prime}+i^{\prime\prime}+j+j^{\prime}+j^{\prime\prime}-6n)v,\quad H^{\prime}(v)=(i+i^{\prime}+i^{\prime\prime}-j-j^{\prime}-j^{\prime\prime})v.

Using (
25
), we see that (
24
) is equivalent to

\Delta_{3}^{4n-d,4n-d^{\prime},d+d^{\prime}}\in\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=d\\
i^{\prime}+j^{\prime}=d^{\prime}\end{subarray}}H^{2n-i,2n-j}(\iota_{p})\otimes H^{2n-i^{\prime},2n-j^{\prime}}(\iota_{p})\otimes H^{i+i^{\prime},j+j^{\prime}}(\iota_{p}).

Again, it is enough to show that
H^{\prime}(\Delta_{3})=0
. For
p=(\omega_{I},\omega_{J},\omega_{K})\in D^{\circ}
, this holds by Hodge theory, and the general case follows from Proposition
2.3
. This completes the proof of Proposition
A.2
.
∎

Appendix B
A note on the topology of fibrations

by Claire Voisin

We give in this appendix an alternative proof of Theorem
0.4
(b), that is, the following statement which in degree
k=2
had been proved by
Matsushita
(see
[
36
, Lemma 2.2]
).

Theorem B.1
.

Let
X
be compact hyper-Kähler manifold of dimension
2n
, and
f:X\rightarrow B
a Lagrangian fibration with general fiber
F
. Then the restriction map
H^{k}(X,\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow H^{k}(F,\mathbb{Q})
is
0
for
k
odd and has rank
1
for
k
even,
k\leq 2n
.

The proof given below is in fact possibly equivalent to the one given by the authors, but it is written in a different language. Our presentation avoids any representation theory and rests on the following Proposition
B.2
that seems to be of independent interest. Let
X
be a smooth connected projective or compact Kähler manifold and
\phi:X\rightarrow B
be a surjective holomorphic map, where
B
is an analytic space. Let
F\subset X
be the general fiber
of
\phi
. It is thus a smooth submanifold of
X
, that we can assume to be connected after Stein factorization. We denote by
[F]
its cohomology class.

Proposition B.2
.

(i)

The kernel of the map

{j}^{*}:H^{*}(X,\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow H^{*}({F},\mathbb{Q})
is equal to the kernel
of the cup-product map

[F]\cup{}:H^{*}(X,\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow H^{*+2r}(X,\mathbb{Q})
.

(ii)

Equivalently, the intersection pairing
\langle\,,\,\rangle_{{F}}
is nondegenerate on
{\rm Im}\,{j}^{*}
.

Remark B.3
.

It is proved in
[
51
]
that for any closed complex subvariety
F
of
X
, the statement above holds for the restriction map

{j}^{*}:H^{k}(X,\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow H^{k}({F},\mathbb{Q})
when
k\leq 2
. However this is not true without assumptions on
F
starting from degree
\geq 3
. For example,
start from an elliptic curve
D\subset\mathbb{P}^{3}
, and let
X
be the blow-up of
\mathbb{P}^{3}
along
D
, with exceptional divisor
E\subset X
.
Then the restriction map
H^{3}(X,\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow H^{3}(E,\mathbb{Q})
is nonzero, while the cup-product map

[E]\cup{}:H^{3}(X,\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow H^{5}(X,\mathbb{Q})=0
is zero.

Proof of Proposition
B.2
.

The equivalence of the two statements is proved as follows. If
\alpha\in H^{k}(X,\mathbb{R})
satisfies
\alpha_{\mid F}\not=0
, then (ii) says that there is a class
\beta\in H^{2d-k}(X,\mathbb{R})
, where
d={\rm dim}\,F
, such that
\langle\alpha_{\mid F},\beta_{\mid F}\rangle_{F}\not=0
. As this is equal to
\langle[F]\cup\alpha,\beta\rangle_{X}
we conclude that
[F]\cup\alpha\not=0
, so (ii) implies (i).
Conversely, let
\alpha\in H^{*}(X,\mathbb{R})
such that

\alpha_{\mid F}\not=0
. Then (i) implies that
[F]\cup\alpha\not=0
in
H^{k+2r}(X,\mathbb{R})
, where
r={\rm codim}\,F
, and by Poincaré duality on
X
, there exists a class
\beta\in H^{2d-k}(X,\mathbb{R})
such that

\langle[F]\cup\alpha,\beta\rangle_{X}\not=0
, that is
\langle\alpha_{\mid F},\beta_{\mid F}\rangle_{F}\not=0
. Thus the intersection pairing
\langle\,,\,\rangle_{{F}}
is nondegenerate on
{\rm Im}\,{j}^{*}
and (i) implies (ii).

We now prove (ii). Let
h\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{R})
be a Kähler class and let
h_{F}
be its restriction to
F
.
The Lefschetz decomposition of
H^{*}(F,\mathbb{R})
relative to
h_{F}
provides for any

\alpha_{F}\in H^{k}(F,\mathbb{R})
a unique decomposition

\alpha_{F}=\bigoplus_{0\leq k-2l\leq d}h_{F}^{l}\,\alpha_{F,l},

where
\alpha_{F,l}\in H^{k-2l}(F,\mathbb{R})_{\rm prim}
is primitive with respect to
h_{F}
, that is, annihilated by
h_{F}^{d-k+2l+1}\cup{}
.
We have

Lemma B.4
.

The image
I^{*}:={\rm Im}\,(j^{*}:H^{*}(X,\mathbb{R})\rightarrow H^{*}(F,\mathbb{R}))
is stable under the Lefschetz decomposition of
H^{*}(F,\mathbb{R})
relative to
h_{F}
.

Proof.

Let
U\subset B
be the Zariski (or Zariski analytic) open subset over which
\phi_{U}:X_{U}:=\phi^{-1}(U)\rightarrow U
is smooth. Then
the relative Lefschetz decomposition associated to
h
decomposes the local system
R\phi_{U*}\mathbb{R}
. If

\alpha\in H^{k}(X,\mathbb{R})
, the restriction of
\alpha
to the fibers of
\phi_{U}
provides a constant global section

\tilde{\alpha}
of
R^{k}\phi_{U*}\mathbb{R}
. The components
\tilde{\alpha}_{l}
of the relative
Lefschetz decomposition of
\tilde{\alpha}
are also global constant sections of

R^{k-2l}\phi_{U*}\mathbb{R}
on
U
. By Deligne’s global invariant cycle theorem
[
15
]
, there exist
classes
\alpha_{l}\in H^{k-2l}(X,\mathbb{R})
inducing the sections
\tilde{\alpha}_{l}
, which means that

{\alpha_{F,l}}={\alpha_{l}}_{\mid F}
. This proves the lemma.
∎

Lemma
B.4
implies now Proposition
B.2
(ii) using the Hodge–Riemann bilinear relations.
The Lefschetz decomposition on
I^{*}
is a decomposition into Hodge substructures, and it
satisfies the Lefschetz isomorphism property saying that

h_{F}^{d-k}\cup{}:I^{k}\rightarrow I^{2d-k}

is an isomorphism.
In order to prove (ii), it thus suffices to prove that
the Lefschetz intersection pairing

\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle_{F,h}=\langle h_{F}^{d-k}\cup\alpha\cup\beta\rangle_{F}

is nondegenerate on
I^{k}
for
k\leq d
.
However we know that each
I^{k}
is a real Hodge structure stable under
the Lefschetz decomposition

I^{k}=\bigoplus_{2l\leq k}h_{F}^{l}\,I^{k-2l}_{{\rm prim}}

which is orthogonal for
\langle\,,\,\rangle_{F,h}
, so each term
I^{k-2l}_{{\rm prim}}
is a real Hodge substructure
of
H^{k-2l}(F,\mathbb{R})_{\rm prim}
with its induced Lefschetz intersection pairing. It is finally well-known that the restriction of
\langle\,,\,\rangle_{F,h}
on any real Hodge substructure of
H^{k-2l}(F,\mathbb{R})_{\rm prim}
is nondegenerate
because
\langle\,,\,\rangle_{F,h}
polarizes it, so the corresponding Hermitian pairing has a definite sign on each
(p,q)
-component.
∎

We now turn to the case of a Lagrangian fibration
X\rightarrow B
on a compact hyper-Kähler
2n
-fold. The class of the fiber

is proportional to

, where

and

generates the Picard group of the base. Using the Beauville–Fujiki relations, the class

satisfies

, where

is the Beauville–Bogomolov form, because

.

Proof of Theorem
B.1
.

By Proposition

, we have to show that the map

when

is odd and has rank

when

is even. Note that this rank is at least

in the second case, because the

Let
Q\subset\mathbb{P}(H^{2}(X,\mathbb{C}))
be the quadric defined by

an (Euclidean) open set of

consists of classes
\sigma_{t}
of holomorphic forms on a deformation

of

By semicontinuity of the rank, it suffices to show that for

\sigma_{t}^{n}\cup{}:H^{k}(X,\mathbb{C})\rightarrow H^{k+2n}(X,\mathbb{C})

, hence the image of
\sigma_{t}^{n}\cup{}
is contained

when

.
∎

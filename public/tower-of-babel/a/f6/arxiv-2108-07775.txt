Global gradient estimate for a divergence problem and its application to the homogenization of a magnetic suspension
Thuyen Dang, Yuliya Gorb, and Silvia Jiménez Bolaños

Abstract
This paper generalizes the results obtained by the authors in dangHomogenizationNondiluteSuspension2021 concerning the homogenization of a non-dilute suspension of magnetic particles in a viscous flow. More specifically, in this paper, a restrictive assumption on the coefficients of the coupled equation, made in dangHomogenizationNondiluteSuspension2021 , that significantly narrowed the applicability of the homogenization results obtained, is relaxed and a new regularity of the solution of the fine-scale problem is proven. In particular, we obtain a global L ∞ L^{\infty} -bound for the gradient of the solution of the scalar equation − div [ 𝐚 ( x / ε ) ∇ φ ε ( x ) ] = f ( x ) -\Div\left[\mathbf{a}\left(x/\varepsilon\right)\nabla\varphi^{\varepsilon}(x)\right]=f(x) , uniform with respect to microstructure scale parameter ε ≪ 1 \varepsilon\ll 1 in a small interval ( 0 , ε 0 ) (0,\varepsilon_{0}) , where the coefficient 𝐚 \mathbf{a} is only piecewise Hölder continuous. Thenceforth, this regularity is used in the derivation of the effective response of the given suspension discussed in dangHomogenizationNondiluteSuspension2021 .

3
Statement and discussion of the main result

The main result of this paper is summarized in the following theorem.

Theorem 3.1
(Global Lipschitz Estimate)

Let
\Omega
be a bounded
C^{1,\alpha}
domain,
g\in C^{1,\alpha^{\prime}}(\partial\Omega)

and
f\in L^{\infty}({\Omega})
, where
0<\alpha^{\prime}<\alpha<1
. Suppose that

\mathbf{a}\in\mathfrak{M}_{\mathrm{per}}(\lambda,\Lambda)
is piecewise

C^{\alpha}
-continuous. There exists
C=C(\alpha,\alpha^{\prime},\lambda,\Lambda,d,\Omega)>0
such that, for all
\varepsilon>0
, the (unique) solution
\varphi^{\varepsilon}
of:

\displaystyle-\Div\left[\mathbf{a}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\nabla\varphi^{\varepsilon}\right]

\displaystyle=f,\quad

\displaystyle\text{ in }\Omega

(4a)

\displaystyle\varphi^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=g,\quad

\displaystyle\text{ on }\partial\Omega,

(4b)

satisfies:

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla\varphi^{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{C^{1,\alpha^{\prime}}(\partial\Omega)}+\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\Omega})}\right).

(5)

Remark 1

For each
\varepsilon>0
, let

N_{\varepsilon}
be the number of subdomains inside
\Omega
such
that in each of them the function
\mathbf{a}
is

C^{\alpha}
-continuous. Denote those subdomains by
D_{m},~1\leq m\leq N_{\varepsilon}
. Then, for

0<\alpha^{\prime}<\min\left\{\alpha,\frac{\alpha}{(\alpha+1)d}\right\}
,
by
(
liGradientEstimatesSolutions2000
, Corollary 1.3)
, one has:

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla\varphi^{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C\left(\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{C^{1,\alpha^{\prime}}(\partial\Omega)}+\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right),

(6)

where
C
depends on
\Omega,d,\alpha,\alpha^{\prime},\lambda,\Lambda,\left\lVert\mathbf{a}\right\rVert_{C^{\alpha^{\prime}}(\overline{D_{m}},{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times d})}
and the

C^{1,\alpha}
-modulus of
\cup_{m=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}}\partial D_{m}
(defined in page 92

liGradientEstimatesSolutions2000
). As
\varepsilon\to 0,
the number

increases, while the sizes of the subdomains decrease, which leads to the blow-up of the
C^{1,\alpha}
-modulus. Therefore, estimate (

uniform in
\varepsilon
.

However, if
\varepsilon_{0}\leq\varepsilon\leq 1
for some constant
\varepsilon_{0}>0
,
then one can control the number, size, distance and

-modulus of the subdomains in
\Omega

\varepsilon

\varepsilon

(

\varepsilon

\varepsilon_{0}\leq\varepsilon\leq 1
. Therefore,
Theorem

3.1
will be proven, if one can specify a constant
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that (
5
) holds for

Our proof of

Step (i) is obtained via the

Interior and boundary Hölder estimates, see

2

3

•

Estimates for the Green’s function, which are obtained using the Hölder bounds above, see

6
Application to magnetic suspensions

In this section, we apply the regularity results obtained above to the rigorous homogenization procedure discussed in
dangHomogenizationNondiluteSuspension2021
. For that, we first recap the formulation of the fine-scale problem and the homogenization result itself. We begin by introducing the definition of two-scale convergence, which will be used below.

Definition 1

A sequence
\{v^{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
is said to
two-scale converge
to
v=v({x},{y})
, with
v\in L^{2}(\Omega\times Y)
, if and only if:

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\Omega}v^{\varepsilon}(x)\psi\left(x,\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\di x=\frac{1}{\left\lvert Y\right\rvert}\int_{\Omega}\int_{Y}v(x,y)\psi(x,y)\di y\di x,

for any test function
\psi=\psi({x},{y})
, with
\psi\in\mathcal{D}(\Omega,C_{\mathrm{per}}^{\infty}(Y))
,
see
allaireHomogenizationTwoscaleConvergence1992
;
cioranescuIntroductionHomogenization1999
;
nguetsengGeneralConvergenceResult1989
. In this case, we write
v^{\varepsilon}\xrightharpoonup{~2~}v
.

Let the kinematic viscosity be denoted by
\nu=\frac{\eta}{\rho_{f}}
, where
\eta>0
and
\rho_{f}>0
are the fluid viscosity and the fluid density, respectively. The dimensionless quantities that appear in this problem are the (hydrodynamic)
Reynolds number

{R}_{{e}}=UL/\nu
, the
Froude number

{F}_{{r}}=U/\sqrt{FL}
and the
coupling parameter

S=\frac{B^{2}}{\rho_{f}\Lambda U^{2}},

where
L,U,B
and
F
are the characteristic scales
corresponding to length, fluid velocity,
magnetic field and body density force, respectively. Moreover,
\Lambda>0
is defined in
(A2)
.

From now on, we suppose
\Omega
is
C^{3,\alpha}
, which is
needed for the corrector result below. Suppose further that

\mathbf{g}\in H^{1}({\Omega},{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,
k\in C^{1,\alpha}(\partial{\Omega})

and
f\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Let
\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}
and

p^{\varepsilon}
be the fluid velocity and the fluid pressure,
respectively. Also, in a space free of current, the magnetic field
strength is given by

\mathbf{H}^{\varepsilon}=\nabla\varphi^{\varepsilon}
, for some
magnetic potential
\varphi^{\varepsilon}(x)
. Let

\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,

p^{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}(\Omega)/{\mathbb{R}}
, and

\varphi^{\varepsilon}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
be the solution of the
following boundary value problem:

\displaystyle-\Div\left[\boldsymbol{\sigma}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon},p^{\varepsilon})+\boldsymbol{\tau}(\varphi^{\varepsilon})\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{{F}_{{r}}^{2}}\mathbf{g},

\displaystyle\text{ in }\Omega_{f}^{\varepsilon}

(41a)

\displaystyle\Div\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\text{ in }\Omega_{f}^{\varepsilon}

(41b)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{D}}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\text{ in }\Omega_{s}^{\varepsilon}

(41c)

\displaystyle-\Div\left[\mathbf{a}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\nabla\varphi^{\varepsilon}\right]

\displaystyle=f

\displaystyle\text{ in }\Omega,

(41d)

together with the balance equations:

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{i}^{\varepsilon}}\left[\boldsymbol{\sigma}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon},p^{\varepsilon})+\boldsymbol{\tau}(\varphi^{\varepsilon})\right]\mathbf{n}_{i}\di\mathcal{H}^{d-1}

\displaystyle=0,

(42a)

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{i}^{\varepsilon}}\left(\left[\boldsymbol{\sigma}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon},p^{\varepsilon})+\boldsymbol{\tau}(\varphi^{\varepsilon})\right]\mathbf{n}_{i}\right)\times\mathbf{n}_{i}\di\mathcal{H}^{d-1}

\displaystyle=0,

(42b)

and boundary conditions:

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=0,\text{ on }\partial\Omega,

(43a)

\displaystyle\varphi^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=k,\text{ on }\partial\Omega,

(43b)

(44a)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{D}}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})

(44c)

are the

7
Conclusions

This paper concerns a homogenized description of a non-dilute
suspension of magnetic particles in a viscous flow. The results demonstrated
in this paper generalize the ones obtained by the authors in

dangHomogenizationNondiluteSuspension2021
, where a more
restrictive assumption on the magnetic permeability (
1
) was used and a
Neumann boundary condition (
3
) was imposed instead of the Dirichlet condition
(
4b
).

Theorem

6.1
above demonstrates the

effective response
of a viscous fluid with a locally periodic
array of paramagnetic/diamagnetic particles suspended in it, given by
the system of equations (
41
). The effective equations are
described by (
50
), with the effective coefficients given
in (
49
). These effective quantities depend only on the
instantaneous position of the particles, their geometry, and the
magnetic and flow properties of the original suspension described by
(
41
).
Using the tools introduced in

liGradientEstimatesSolutions2000
and the compactness method, an
improved regularity estimate for the gradient of the magnetic potential of the
original fine-scale problem (
41
) was obtained, see

Theorem

3.1
. This theorem allows us to drop the restrictive assumption (
1
) mentioned above. Comparing to the classical results on regularity of this type, we do require the coefficient matrix
belongs to a VMO-space, see e.g.
avellanedaCompactnessMethodsTheory1987
;
prangeUniformEstimatesHomogenization2014
;
shenPeriodicHomogenizationElliptic2018
. Recently, in
(
francfortEnhancementElastodielectricsHomogenization2021
, Proposition
3.1)
, the authors obtained an
L^{q}
-bound of the
gradient of the solution of the scalar divergence equation, uniform with respect to
\varepsilon
, for
q<\infty
. Our result, in
Theorem

3.1
, shows that the gradient bound actually holds for the case

q=\infty
.

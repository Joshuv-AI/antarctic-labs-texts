General Gauge–Yukawa–Quartic
β

Abstract
We determine the full set of coefficients for the completely general 4-loop gauge and 3-loop Yukawa β \beta -functions for the most general renormalizable four-dimensional theories. Using a complete parametrization of the β \beta -functions, we compare the general form to the specific β \beta -functions of known theories to constrain the unknown coefficients. The Weyl consistency conditions provide additional constraints, completing the determination. Preprint: FERMILAB-PUB-21-471-T

1  Introduction

The Renormalization Group (RG) is a key concept in quantum field theory, which determines the change in physics due to changing energy scales. Among numerous applications, its role in improving perturbative computations makes it indispensable for precision computations both in and beyond the Standard Model (SM).
Completely general results for the 2-loop
\beta
-functions of gauge-Yukawa theories have served the community well for almost four decades
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
]
, with the noteworthy addition of the 3-loop contribution for simple gauge groups
[
5
]
.

In the last decade, the discovery of the Higgs and a new drive for precision physics prompted the calculation of the SM
\beta
-functions to 4-loop order for gauge and 3-loop order for Yukawa and quartic couplings
[
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
]
.
Recent years have also seen new developments in hitherto neglected aspects of
\beta
-functions, namely, the
\beta
-functions for the relevant operators
[
13
,
14
,
15
]
, the running of the kinetic-mixing parameters between Abelian gauge fields
[
16
,
17
,
18
]
, and the extension of the 3-loop gauge
\beta
-function to semi-simple gauge groups
[
19
,
18
]
.
All these developments have been collated in a number of accessible computer tools—
SARAH 4

[
20
,
21
]
,
PyR@TE 3

[
22
,
23
]
,
ARGES

[
24
]
, and
RGBeta

[
25
]
—for the application of the general formulas to specific theories, which have opened the doors for the wider application of higher-loop general results in the community.
The bespoke tool
RGE++

[
26
]
has also been made available for fast evaluation of the resulting RG equations.

New insights into the underlying structure of the RG have been obtained in recent years on the basis of the powerful machinery of the
local RG
(LRG)
[
27
,
28
,
29
,
30
,
31
,
32
]
.
Crucially, as it pertains to the present matter, it was pointed out that Osborn’s equation implies nontrivial consistency conditions on the
\beta
-function, now known as
Weyl consistency conditions
(WCC).
The WCCs have since been leveraged to check the self-consistency of already-known
\beta
-functions and to make predictions for as-of-yet undetermined
\beta
-function coefficients
[

,

,

.
These endeavors have emphasized the usefulness of parametrizing the unknown
\beta
-functions in terms of a basis of tensor structures (TS) constructed from the couplings of the theory in order to attempt to fix the coefficients of the parametrization.
By comparing the generic expression to known results in specific theories, the 3-loop
\beta

[
37
,
38

.

This paper represents the culmination of several years of research by finally fixing the full gauge-Yukawa-quartic
\beta

To this end, we use the TS basis of Ref.
[
18
]

We fix the remaining coefficients by matching the generic expressions to the known
\beta

[
12

[
39

While we were finalizing our computations, another paper with overlapping scope appeared
[
40

.
The two computations agree and serve as valuable crosschecks.
However, both computations rely on the
\beta

[
18

and the tensor implementation of
RGBeta

[
25

That said, such a failure appears highly unlikely due to the required self-consistency of both computations.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows: In the next section, we review the necessary aspects of the LRG and introduce the
\beta

In section
3

4

\beta

2  Setup and Methods

2.1   Notation and conventions

In the mass-independent
\overline{\text{MS}}
scheme, the
\beta
-functions of marginal couplings are independent of the relevant couplings. Accordingly, we restrict our attention to the most general four-dimensional theory allowing only for marginal couplings:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=

\displaystyle-\tfrac{1}{4}a^{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.6}[1]{$-$}}}1}_{AB}F^{A}_{\mu\nu}F^{B\mu\nu}+\tfrac{1}{2}(D_{\mu}\phi)_{a}(D^{\mu}\phi)_{a}+i\psi_{i}^{\dagger}\bar{\sigma}^{\mu}(D_{\mu}\psi)^{i}

\displaystyle-\tfrac{1}{2}\left(Y_{aij}\psi^{i}\psi^{j}\;+\;\mathrm{H.c.}\;\right)\phi_{a}-\tfrac{1}{24}\lambda_{abcd}\phi_{a}\phi_{b}\phi_{c}\phi_{d}.

(2.1)

To be clear, when we name this the ‘‘most general’’ theory, it is because any other renormalizable theory can be cast in this exact form.
1
1

1

In practice recasting theories in this form can be a rather grueling task. Methods to do it systematically have been developed, see e.g. Ref.
[
41
]
.

In this construction, all scalar fields are collected into the real scalar multiplet
\phi_{a}
, while the fermions, allowing for chiral theories, are collected in the multiplet
\psi^{i}
using Weyl spinor notation.
Thus, we avoid having to distinguish between left- and right-handed fields.

In this framework
\phi_{a}
and
\psi^{i}
are generically in reducible representations of the gauge group
G
.
The covariant derivatives of these fields are given by

\begin{split}D_{\mu}\phi_{a}&=\partial_{\mu}\phi_{a}-iA_{\mu}^{A}(T^{A}_{\phi})_{ab}\phi_{b}\,,\\
D_{\mu}\psi^{i}&=\partial_{\mu}\psi^{i}-iA_{\mu}^{A}(T^{A}_{\psi})\phantom{}^{i}\phantom{}_{j}\psi^{j}\,.\end{split}

(2.2)

As we allow for any gauge group, it is convenient to move the gauge couplings of all the simple product groups and Abelian factors to the kinetic term, where they are incorporated in the gauge coupling matrix
a_{AB}

[
18
]
.

It is convenient to arrange the fermion index space in a Majorana-like manner to incorporate both left and right chiralities simultaneously
[
34
,
18
]
. The Yukawa couplings and gauge generators are then given by

y_{a}=\begin{pmatrix}Y_{a}&0\\
0&Y_{a}^{\ast}\end{pmatrix}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad T^{A}=\begin{pmatrix}T_{\psi}^{A}&0\\
0&-T_{\psi}^{A\,\ast}\end{pmatrix},

(2.3)

and we define the chirality-flipped derivative quantities by

\tilde{y}_{a}=\sigma_{1}y_{a}\sigma_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}Y_{a}^{\ast}&0\\
0&Y_{a}\end{pmatrix}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\widetilde{T}^{A}=\sigma_{1}T^{A}\sigma_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}-T_{\psi}^{A,\,\ast}&0\\
0&T_{\psi}^{A}\end{pmatrix},

(2.4)

with a similar construction for all other ‘‘tilde’’ quantities.
2
2

2

In the context of the fermion indices, the
\sigma
matrices are to be understood as a tensor product of Pauli matrices with the identity matrix.

In this notation, the
\gamma_{5}
-odd contribution to the 4-loop gauge and 3-loop Yukawa are characterized by the appearance of
\sigma_{3}
on the fermion lines:
the underlying diagrams carry opposite signs for the different chiralities.

The perturbative
\beta
-functions of the tensor couplings are expanded by loop orders and parametrized as

\displaystyle\beta_{AB}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}a_{AB}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}a_{AC}\dfrac{\beta^{(\ell)}_{CD}}{(4\pi)^{2\ell}}a_{DB}\,,

(2.5)

\displaystyle\beta_{aij}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y_{aij}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{\beta^{(\ell)}_{aij}}{(4\pi)^{2\ell}}\,,

(2.6)

\displaystyle\beta_{abcd}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda_{abcd}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{\beta^{(\ell)}_{abcd}}{(4\pi)^{2\ell}}\,,

(2.7)

where
t=\ln\mu
is the RG time.
These are the main objects of our study.

2.2   Tensor parametrization

As all other perturbative quantities, the
\beta
-functions are polynomials in the couplings, of an order determined by the loop order.
It follows that the
\ell
-loop contributions to the
\beta
-functions can be parametrized by

\displaystyle\beta^{(\ell)}_{AB}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n}\mathbf{g}^{(\ell)}_{n}\,[G^{(\ell)}_{n}(a,\,y,\,\lambda,\,T,\,T_{\phi},F)]_{AB}\,,

(2.8)

\displaystyle\beta^{(\ell)}_{aij}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2}\,\!\!\sum_{2\,\mathrm{perm}}\!\!\sum_{n}\mathbf{y}^{(\ell)}_{n}\,[Y^{(\ell)}_{n}(a,\,y,\,\lambda,\,T,\,T_{\phi},F)]_{aij}\,,

(2.9)

\displaystyle\beta^{(\ell)}_{abcd}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{24}\,\!\!\sum_{24\,\mathrm{perm}}\!\!\sum_{n}\mathbf{q}^{(\ell)}_{n}\,[Q^{(\ell)}_{n}(a,\,y,\,\lambda,\,T,\,T_{\phi},F)]_{abcd}\,.

(2.10)

The coefficients
\mathbf{g}^{(\ell)}_{n}
,
\mathbf{y}^{(\ell)}_{n}
, and
\mathbf{q}^{(\ell)}_{n}
are real numbers, while
[G^{(\ell)}_{n}]_{AB}
,
[Y^{(\ell)}_{n}]_{aij}
, and
[Q^{(\ell)}_{n}]_{abcd}
are tensors constructed from a combination of couplings, gauge generators, and structure constants (
F
), referred to as
tensor structures
(TSs).
By explicitly symmetrizing the Yukawa and quartic
\beta
-functions—hence the sums over all index permutations—the TSs are kept as simple as possible; they are monomials rather than polynomials.

Whereas a determination of
\beta^{(\ell)}
typically requires a full perturbative loop computation, it is possible to find a suitable basis of TSs from general considerations, thereby leaving only the coefficients unknown.
The main obstacle to finding such a basis is the sheer number of tensors at higher orders and the need to eliminate the redundancy in the parametrization due to the gauge identities stemming from all of the couplings being singlets under the symmetry.
One suitable basis was determined in Ref.
[
18
]
, and we will employ it for our computation here too.
We have taken the liberty of presenting the full basis in regular tensor notation in the Appendix for posterity, as they have only been made available graphically in Ref.
[
18
]
.

We implemented the TSs of the 4-loop gauge and 3-loop Yukawa
\beta
-functions in
RGBeta
, which allows us to evaluate Eqs. (
2.8
–
2.10
) in specific models and match the generic expressions to the
\beta
-functions determined with traditional loop computations.

2.3   Flavor-improved
\beta
-function

The
\overline{\text{MS}}

\beta
-functions are generally not unique beyond the second loop order. The flow in the direction of flavor symmetry transformations (the symmetry of the kinetic term of Lagrangian (
2.1
)) is fixed by the choice of renormalization constants, which are themselves ambiguous
[
42
]
.
It can even be divergent in the dimensional expansion, although physics is insensitive to any such flavor rotations along the flow due to the symmetry of the action under combined field and coupling transformations.
In one instance, a perturbative limit cycle was found but soon ascribed to a non-physical flow along the flavor group transformation, the physics being that of a regular fixed point
[
43
,
44
]
.

The flavor-improved
\beta
-function
B_{I}
is a particular construction of the
\beta
-function engineered to be invariant under the ambiguity
[
44
,
31
,
32
]
and has been shown to be independent of the choice of renormalization constants
[
42
]
.
It factors out the flow along the flavor-symmetric direction, thereby avoiding the appearance of non-physical limit cycles in the RG flow. It is given by

B_{I}=\beta_{I}-(\upsilon\,g)_{I},

(2.11)

where
I,J,\ldots
are coupling indices taking values of all the couplings of the theory
g_{I}=\left\{a_{AB},\,y_{aij},\,\lambda_{abcd}\right\}
.
\upsilon
is another RG function of the LRG associated with the current of the flavor symmetry group.
3
3

3

Inconveniently,
\upsilon
(lower-case upsilon) is referred to as
S
in parts of the literature.

A conformal field theory is characterized by
B_{I}=0
and not, as is often said,
\beta_{I}=0
.
That being said, in simple/simplified theories in which the couplings are trivial w.r.t. the flavor group, the two functions coincide.

In Ref
[
42
]
, we found that there always exists a choice of renormalization constants for which
\beta_{I}
is identical to
B_{I}
(meaning that
\upsilon=0
).
Thus it is perfectly valid to use
B_{I}
in place of
\beta_{I}
for the RG, a choice that also carries the benefits discussed above.
Unfortunately, there is no direct prescription for the renormalization constants for which this identification is valid, and
B_{I}
will have to be determined directly from
\beta_{I}
and
\upsilon
.

\upsilon
is, potentially, nontrivial starting from
3
-loop order in gauge-Yukawa theories, but only when the flavor structure is nontrivial.
The flavor symmetry acts trivially on the gauge group, so
(\upsilon\,a)_{AB}=0
. Thus, it is only the 3-loop Yukawa
\beta
-function that is different from its flavor-improved cousin at 4–3–2-loop order.
The action of
\upsilon
on the Yukawa coupling is given by

(\upsilon\,y)_{a}=\upsilon_{ab}\,y_{b}+\upsilon\,y_{a}-y_{a}\tilde{\upsilon}\,.

(2.12)

\upsilon
is technically a single element of the Lie algebra of the flavor group.
The scalar and fermion indices on
\upsilon_{ab}
and
\upsilon\phantom{}_{i}\phantom{}^{j}
(as per usual we use implicit fermion indices in Eq. (
2.12
)) are implicitly taken to refer to different representations of
\upsilon
as determined by the transformation of the scalar and fermion fields.
We parametrize
\upsilon
at
\ell
-loop order by

\displaystyle\upsilon^{(\ell)}\phantom{}_{i}\phantom{}{}^{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n}\mathbf{f}^{(\ell)}_{n}[F^{(\ell)}_{n}-\widetilde{F}^{(\ell)\,\mathrm{T}}_{n}]\phantom{}_{i}\phantom{}^{j}\,,

(2.13)

\displaystyle\upsilon^{(\ell)}_{ab}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n}\mathbf{s}^{(\ell)}_{n}\big([S^{(\ell)}_{n}]_{ab}-[S^{(\ell)}_{n}]_{ba}\big)\,,

(2.14)

with the TSs
F_{n}^{(\ell)}
and
S_{n}^{(\ell)}
. A basis for this parametrization was provided in
[
18
]
.

2.4   Weyl consistency conditions

Using the LRG, Jack and Osborn
[
28
,
29
,
30
,
31
,
32
]
demonstrated a perturbative
A
-theorem via Osborn’s equation

\partial^{I}\!A=T^{IJ}B_{J}\,,

(2.15)

where
\partial^{I}\equiv\partial/\partial g_{I}
.
Osborn’s equation relates the function
A
, which coincides with the Weyl anomaly coefficient of the Euler density at fixed points, with the flavor-improved
\beta
-function via
T^{IJ}
, which itself is a combination of various coefficients of the Weyl anomaly.
Since
T^{IJ}
is positive-definite at leading order in the loop expansion, this establishes a perturbative
A
-theorem.

As it transpires, Osborn’s equation implies nontrivial self-consistency conditions on the
\beta
-functions, known as Weyl consistency conditions (WCCs)
[
33
,
31
,
34
,
35
]
.

A
and
T^{IJ}
can be parametrized in terms of the couplings of the theory and the unknown coefficients eliminated from Eq. (
2.15
), leaving a number of nontrivial consistency conditions between the coefficients of the
\beta
-functions. Ref.
[
18
]
extended this program to the generic gauge-Yukawa theory (
2.1
) and up to order 4–3–2 in the
\beta
-functions.
With full knowledge of the known 3–2–2
\beta
-functions, the resulting WCCs provide 266 independent conditions on the 510 coefficients
\mathbf{g}^{(4)}_{n}
and
\mathbf{y}^{(3)}_{n}
parametrizing the 4-loop gauge and 3-loop Yukawa
\beta
-functions, respectively.
In particular, the conditions unambiguously fix the
\gamma_{5}
ambiguity in
\beta^{(4)}_{AB}
on basis of the known
\gamma_{5}
-odd contribution to
\beta^{(3)}_{aij}

[
36
]
, also confirming the prescription in Ref.
[
10
]
.

The coefficients of
\upsilon
can be eliminated from Osborn’s equations, leaving a set of WCCs exclusively relating
\beta
-function coefficients. This elimination fixes all 3
\mathbf{s}^{(3)}_{n}
coefficients in terms of the
\beta
-function coefficients.
The 6
\mathbf{f}^{(3)}_{n}
coefficients are fixed up to one free parameter.
Meanwhile, the explicit SM evaluation of the pure Yukawa part of
\upsilon
in Ref.
[
42
]
fixes
\mathbf{f}^{(3)}_{4,5}
; this is enough to fix the remaining parameter and provide an additional consistency check.
Thus,
\upsilon
can be fixed once the
\beta
-function coefficients are known, allowing us to fix the 3-loop Yukawa
B_{aij}
.

2.5   Loop Computations

To fix the remaining coefficients, gauge and Yukawa
\beta
-functions need to be computed in various models. To this end, we follow methods outlined in Refs.
[
39
,
12
,
42
]
. In the following,
we present our computational setup and discuss issues arising in such calculations.

Gauge and Yukawa
\beta
-functions are related to the counterterms of the couplings. Coupling counterterms can be related to renormalization constants of vertices that are proportional to the respective coupling at tree-level, as well as the renormalization constants of the fields involved in the vertex.
As a consequence, 3-loop Yukawa counterterms can be computed from 3-loop renormalization constants of fermion and scalar fields
as well as fermion-scalar vertices, and 4-loop gauge coupling counterterms can be obtained from 4-loop renormalization constants of gauge and ghost fields, and ghost-gauge vertices.

Our well-tested computational system has been introduced in Ref.
[
12
]
and subsequently improved for the computations in Ref.
[
42
]
.
It relies on
QGRAF

[
45
]
for diagram generation, followed by
q2e
and
exp

[
46
,
47
]
for translating Feynman diagrams
into
FORM

[
48
]
expressions and mapping them onto integral families, as well as
COLOR

[
49
]
to compute
\mathrm{SU}(N)
color factors.
Diagrams sharing the same color factors, flavor structures, and integral families are grouped into so-called “superdiagrams”, which allows for the efficient computation
of
\mathcal{O}(10^{6})
4-loop Feynman diagrams; cancellations between different diagrams occur in early stages of the evaluation of superdiagrams, rather than in the final summation of all diagrams.
The superdiagrams are processed using
FORM
and the Feynman integrals are evaluated by
FORCER

[
50
]
.
Aside from the computational complexity involved with 4-loop computations, there are two main issues: the treatment of
\gamma_{5}
in
D
dimensions and the ambiguities in Yukawa
\beta
-functions.

Starting from 3-loop order, nontrivial contributions from
\gamma_{5}
-odd traces arise in the computation of Yukawa
\beta
-functions. These contributions can be computed by evaluating such
traces in 4 dimensions, as has been done for both the SM and the various 2HDMs
[
51
,
52
,
39
]
. Matching Eq. (
2.9
) to these models allows us to fix the coefficients
of all 5
\gamma_{5}
-odd tensors structures. As discussed in Sec.
2.4
, the WCCs then allow us to fix the coefficients of the
\gamma_{5}
-odd tensor structures in the gauge
\beta
-function.
Therefore, we ignore all
\gamma_{5}
-odd contributions in our calculations here and focus on the missing coefficients in the
\gamma_{5}
-even sector.

The second issue relates to the ambiguity of the 3-loop Yukawa
\beta
-function.
As observed in
[
51
,
39
]
, the presence of global symmetries of the kinetic terms of fermion and scalar fields leads to ambiguities in the wave-function renormalization constants, which in turn lead to an ambiguity in the Yukawa
\beta
-function.
As well as being ambiguous, the Yukawa
\beta
-function is, in general, not finite.
As we noted in Section
2.3
, this can ultimately be resolved by determining the flavor-improved
\beta
-function
B_{I}
.
Here we proceed by choosing Hermitian square roots of the wave-function renormalization constants, consistent with the one-particle-irreducible
\beta
-function parametrization of Ref.
[
18
]
.
We extract only the finite part of the RG functions from this and rely on it being possible to modify the renormalization constants in such a way as to cancel the divergences without changing the finite part
[
42
]
.

3  Fixing the
\beta
-Functions

3.1   The models we use as input

While the
\beta
-functions of the SM and 2HDMs in combination with the WCCs provide a large number of constraints on the 510 free coefficients in the 4-loop gauge and 3-loop Yukawa
\beta
-functions, a number of
coefficients of TSs involving scalar fields remain unconstrained. To constrain these, we consider two types of models: one with scalars charged under two non-Abelian gauge groups
and another in which the scalar field is in a nontrivial representation of the gauge group different from that of the fermions.
In the following, we describe two models that possess the required properties. First, we use a leptoquark model, i.e., an extension of the SM with a scalar field charged under
\mathrm{SU}(3)
that couples quarks to leptons.
The one remaining coefficient that can not be fully constrained by combining the leptoquark model, the SM, 2HDMs, and the WCCs can be fixed by a simple toy model; an
\mathrm{SU}(2)
gauge theory with a vector-like fermion in the fundamental representation
and a scalar in the adjoint representation.

3.1.1  Leptoquark model

Let
\phi\sim(\mathbf{3},\mathbf{2},7/6)
be a scalar leptoquark added to the SM field content. The Yukawa interactions of the model are then given by

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{yuk}}=\mathcal{L}_{\text{SM,yuk}}-y_{1}^{ij}\varepsilon^{\alpha\beta}\phi^{\ast}_{c\beta}\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{L},\alpha}^{i}u_{\mathrm{R}}^{j,c}-y_{2}^{ij}\phi^{c\alpha}\overline{q}_{\mathrm{L},c\alpha}^{i}e_{\mathrm{R}}^{j}\,,

(3.1)

where
c,d,\ldots
are color,
\alpha,\beta,\ldots
isospin, and
i,j
generation indices.
\varepsilon^{\alpha\beta}=i\sigma_{2}
is the invariant of
\mathrm{SU}(2)_{\mathrm{L}}
.
For comparison, in this notation the SM Yukawas are given by

\mathcal{L}_{\text{SM,yuk}}=-y_{u}^{ij}\varepsilon^{\alpha\beta}H^{\ast}_{\beta}\overline{q}_{\mathrm{L},c\alpha}^{i}u_{\mathrm{R}}^{j,c}-y_{d}^{ij}H^{\alpha}\overline{q}_{\mathrm{L},c\alpha}^{i}d_{\mathrm{R}}^{j,c}-y_{e}^{ij}H^{\alpha}\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{L},\alpha}^{i}e_{\mathrm{R}}^{j}\,.

(3.2)

The quartic couplings are given by

V=\dfrac{\lambda_{1}}{2}(H^{\dagger}H)^{2}+\lambda_{2}(H^{\dagger}H)\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\!\big[\phi^{\dagger}\phi\big]\!+\dfrac{\lambda_{3}}{2}\mathrm{Tr}^{2}\big[\phi^{\dagger}\phi\big]+\kappa_{1}H^{\mathrm{T}}\phi^{\dagger}\phi H^{\ast}+\dfrac{\kappa_{2}}{2}\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\!\big[\phi^{\dagger}\phi\,\phi^{\dagger}\phi\big]\!\,,

(3.3)

where
\phi^{c\alpha}
is thought of as a matrix with indices in color and isospin respectively. In index notation, the
\kappa_{1}
coupling is given by
\kappa_{1}H^{\alpha}\phi^{\ast}_{c\alpha}\phi^{c\beta}H^{\ast}_{\beta}
.

3.1.2
\mathrm{SU}(2)
toy model

Next, we consider a toy model with a gauge group
G=\mathrm{SU}(2)
, a real scalar triplet
\phi_{a}
, and a vector-like fermion doublet
\psi^{\alpha}
.
This theory is described by the Lagrangian

\mathcal{L}_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{TM}}=-\dfrac{1}{4g^{2}}(F^{A}_{\mu\nu})^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}(D_{\mu}\phi_{a})^{2}+i\overline{\psi}_{\alpha}\not{D}\psi^{\alpha}-\dfrac{y}{2}\phi_{a}\overline{\psi}_{\alpha}\sigma\phantom{}^{a\alpha}\phantom{}_{\beta}\psi^{\beta}-\tfrac{1}{8}\lambda(\phi_{a}\phi_{a})^{2}\,,

(3.4)

where
\alpha,\beta
are doublet indices and
a
is a triplet index of
G
.
\sigma^{a}/2
are the generators of the fundamental representation of
\mathrm{SU}(2)
, where
\sigma^{a}
are the Pauli matrices.
This toy model, with the scalar in a non-fundamental representation, is rather different from the SM-like models we otherwise consider.
This helps us to resolve the last 1-parameter degeneracy in
\mathbf{g}^{(4)}_{n}
in terms with both gauge and Yukawa couplings.

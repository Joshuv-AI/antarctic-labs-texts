Pure Differential Privacy for Functional Summaries via a Laplace-like Process
Haotian Lin, Matthew Reimherr

Abstract
Many existing mechanisms to achieve differential privacy (DP) on infinite-dimensional functional summaries often involve embedding these summaries into finite-dimensional subspaces and applying traditional DP techniques. Such mechanisms generally treat each dimension uniformly and struggle with complex, structured summaries. This work introduces a novel mechanism for DP functional summary release: the Independent Component Laplace Process (ICLP) mechanism. This mechanism treats the summaries of interest as truly infinite-dimensional objects, thereby addressing several limitations of existing mechanisms. We establish the feasibility of the proposed mechanism in multiple function spaces. Several statistical estimation problems are considered, and we demonstrate one can enhance the utility of sanitized summaries by oversmoothing their non-private counterpart. Numerical experiments on synthetic and real datasets demonstrate the efficacy of the proposed mechanism.



1
Introduction

Data security has garnered critical attention in the last decade as substantial individualized data are collected. The most widely used paradigm in formal data privacy is
differential privacy
(DP), introduced by
Dwork et al. 2006
. DP provides a rigorous and interpretable definition for data privacy as it bounds the amount of information that attackers can infer from publicly released database queries. Numerous mechanisms have been developed under conventional data settings like scalar or vector-valued data. However, advances in technologies enable us to collect and process densely observed data over some temporal or spatial domains, which are coined
functional data
to differentiate them from classic multivariate data
[
Ramsay et al. 2005
;
Kokoszka and Reimherr 2017
;
Ferraty and Romain 2011
]
. Even though functional data analysis (FDA), has been proven useful in various fields like economics, finance, genetics, etc., and has been researched widely in the statistical community, there are only a few works concerning privacy preservation within the realm of functional data.

When the statistical summaries are finite-dimensional, additive noise mechanisms are one of the most commonly used mechanisms to achieve DP, which sanitizes statistical summaries by adding calibrated noise from predetermined distributions, e.g. Laplace and Gaussian mechanism
[
Dwork et al. 2006
;
Dwork et al. 2014
]
.

In this paper, we are concerned about establishing an additive noise mechanism for functional summaries, namely infinite dimensional summaries, to achieve
\epsilon
-DP. With the challenge that functional summaries are typically infinite-dimensional, most existing mechanisms embed the non-private summaries into a finite-dimensional subspace by using finite basis expansions to approximate summaries and apply classical multivariate privacy tools, like perturb expansion coefficients with i.i.d. noise
[
Zhang et al. 2012
;
Wang et al. 2013
;
Chandrasekaran et al. 2014
;
Alda and Rubinstein 2017
]
. The finite-dimensional embedding process is typically unavoidable as when the summaries are infinite-dimensional, adding i.i.d. noise to each dimension is not even feasible if one wants the sanitized summaries to remain in a specific function space.

However, these mechanisms have potential weaknesses. First, determining the dimension of the subspace is crucial as it plays a trade-off role between utility and privacy. While data-driven approaches might cause potential privacy leakage, a pre-determined dimension will lack adaption to the data, potentially failing to capture the shape of the functions or injecting excess noise. Second, in multivariate settings,
classical privacy tools treat all components equally and allocate the privacy budget over each component uniformly, failing to recognize the different importance of different components and thus injecting excess noise for “more important" components. This greatly degrades the utility and robustness of sanitized functional summaries substantially. Conversely, some previous work has shown that capturing the covariance structure in the data can substantially reduce the amount of noise injected
[
Hardt and Talwar 2010
;
Awan and Slavković 2021
]
.

Contribution:

To overcome the downsides inherent in finite-dimensional subspaces embedding approaches, we treat the functional summary and privacy noise as truly infinite-dimensional. Concretely, our contributions can be summarized as follows:

1.

We propose a
\epsilon
-DP mechanism by perturbing functional summaries with a stochastic process called the
Independent Component Laplace Process
(ICLP), and name this mechanism as
the ICLP mechanism
. It ensures a full path-level release rather than a finite grid of evaluating points. We establish the feasibility of the ICLP mechanism in a general separable Hilbert space,
\mathbb{H}
, by characterizing a subspace of
\mathbb{H}
and show that the feasibility holds if and only if the difference of two functional summaries based on adjacent datasets reside in this subspace. We also show how the proposed mechanism applies to the space of the continuous functions, even though this space is not a Hilbert space.

2.

We provide strategies based on regularization empirical risk minimization to obtain feasible functional summaries for the ICLP mechanism. We undercover the trade-off between regularization and privacy by showing that one can achieve
\epsilon
-DP with substantially less privacy error than the non-private error by slightly over-smoothing the summaries. We demonstrate the proposed mechanism for the mean function protection problem and show it can go beyond classic functional data settings, as it is also applicable to the realm of more classic non-parametric smoothing problems like kernel density estimation.

3.

To obtain a privacy-safe regularization parameter, we propose a privacy-safe selection approach such that choosing regularization parameters is tied to the covariance structure of the ICLP while achieving end-to-end privacy with theoretical utility guarantees. This approach overcomes the potential privacy leakage in conventional data-driven approaches.

The proposed mechanism differentiates itself from current mechanisms in the following senses. First, the ICLP mechanism avoids finite-dimensional subspace embeddings and frees the assumption that every dataset in the database shares the same finite-dimensional subspace. Second, the privacy budgets allocated to each component are not uniform but proportional to the global sensitivity of that component. Compared to uniform allocation in current mechanisms, like the functional mechanism
[
Zhang et al. 2012
]
and Bernstein mechanism
[
Alda and Rubinstein 2017
]
, such allocation injects
less noise
into the
more important
components, which enhances the utility of the released summaries.

Related Work:

In the overlap of functional summaries and differential privacy, the landmark paper is
Hall et al. 2013
which provided a framework for achieving
(\epsilon,\delta)
-DP on infinite dimensional functional objects, but focussed on a finite grid of evaluation points. The follow-up work in
Mirshani et al. 2019
pushed
Hall et al. 2013
’s result forward, established
(\epsilon,\delta)
-DP over the full functional path for objects in Banach spaces. In more general spaces,
Reimherr and Awan 2019a
considered elliptical perturbations to achieve
(\epsilon,\delta)
-DP in locally convex vector spaces, including all Hilbert spaces, Banach spaces, and Frechet spaces. They also showed the impossibility of achieving
\epsilon
-DP in infinite dimensional objects with elliptical distributions.

Turning to
\epsilon
-DP, a series of works have been proposed by resorting to finite-dimensional representations, like polynomial bases, trigonometric bases, or Bernstein polynomial bases to approximate target functional summaries
[
Wang et al. 2013
;
Chandrasekaran et al. 2014
;
Alda and Rubinstein 2017
]
and loss functions
[
Zhang et al. 2012
]
. One then perturbs the expansion coefficients via the Laplace mechanism independently. In addition to additive noise mechanisms,
Awan et al. 2019
extended the exponential mechanism
[
McSherry and Talwar 2007
]
to any arbitrary Hilbert spaces and showed its application to functional principal component analysis. From the perspective of the robust noise injection, a heterogeneous noise injection scheme
[
Phan et al. 2019
]
is proposed by assigning different weighted privacy budgets on each coordinate to further improve the robustness of private summaries.

2.1
Functional Summaries and Spaces

We first define the functional summary. Let
\mathbb{H}
be an infinite-dimensional real separable Hilbert space with inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}

\mathcal{D}

n

D
be an element of
\mathcal{D}
. In this paper, we consider the functional summary of interest as an element of
\mathbb{H}
, i.e.
f:\mathcal{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{H}

A random element

is said to have mean
\mu\in\mathbb{H}
and (linear) covariance operator

\E[\langle X,h\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}]=\langle\mu,h\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Cov(\langle X,h_{1}\rangle_{\mathbb{H}},\langle X,h_{2}\rangle_{\mathbb{H}})=\langle Ch_{1},h_{2}\rangle_{\mathbb{H}},

for any
h,h_{1},h_{2}
in

, is a covariance operator if and only if it is symmetric, positive semidefinite, and trace class

]

\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
and
\{\phi_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}

\left\|h\right\|_{C}=\sqrt{\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{\langle h,\phi_{j}\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}{\lambda_{j}}}\quad\textit{and}\quad\left\|h\right\|_{1,C}=\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{|\langle h,\phi_{j}\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}|}{\sqrt{\lambda_{j}}}.

We also denote the two corresponding subspaces of
\mathbb{H}
as
\mathcal{H}_{C}=\{h\in\mathbb{H}:\|h\|_{C}<\infty\}
and
\mathcal{H}_{1,C}=\{h\in\mathbb{H}:\|h\|_{1,C}<\infty\}
.
Note
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{C}
is the classic Cameron-Martin norm induced by
C

[
Bogachev 1998
]
and
\mathcal{H}_{C}
is called the Cameron-Martin space of
C
. Here
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{1,C}
is analogous to a weighted
\ell^{1}
-norm, and
h\in\mathcal{H}_{1,C}
leads to
h\in\mathcal{H}_{C}
.

We also define the power operator of
C
. For any
s\geq 0
, the power operator
C^{s}
is defined as

C^{s}(h)=\sum_{j\geq 1}\lambda_{j}^{s}\langle h,\phi_{j}\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}\phi_{j},\quad\forall h\in\mathbb{H},

i.e.
C^{s}
shares the same eigenfunctions as
C
while the eigenvalues are raised to power of
s
. We define its corresponding power space as

\mathcal{H}_{C^{s}}=\left\{h\in\mathbb{H}:\left\|h\right\|_{C^{s}}:=\sqrt{\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{\langle h,\phi_{j}\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}}{\lambda_{j}^{s}}}<\infty\right\}.

The
\mathcal{H}_{1,C^{\eta}}
and its associated norm
\|\cdot\|_{1,C^{\eta}}
follow a similar definition.

Finite-Dimensional Representation-wise Laplace

We introduce the
F
inite-Dimensional
R
epresentation-wise
L
aplace
(FRL) mechanism. FRL generalizes almost all current subspace embedding mechanisms, which rely on finite-dimensional representations
[
Wang et al. 2013
;
Chandrasekaran et al. 2014
;
Alda and Rubinstein 2017
;
Zhang et al. 2012
]
. Let
\{e_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
be an orthonormal basis in
\mathbb{H}
. Then we approximate the summary using
M
bases, i.e.

\hat{f}_{D}=\sum_{j=1}^{M}f_{Dj}e_{j}\quad\textit{with}\quad f_{Dj}=\langle f_{D},e_{j}\rangle.

Expanding the functional summaries via a finite basis facilitates dimension reduction so that the classic multivariate Laplace mechanism can be implemented to sanitize coefficient vector
(f_{D1},\cdots,f_{DM})
. Specifically, the sanitized private summary can be expressed

\tilde{f}_{D}=\sum_{j=1}^{M}(f_{Dj}+Z_{j})e_{j},\quad\text{with}\quad Z_{j}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i.i.d.}}{{\sim}}Lap(0,\frac{\Delta}{\epsilon})

where
\Delta
is the global sensitivity in
\ell^{1}
-distance. FRL operates without regard to the varying importance of components. Although some components are more crucial for estimation, FRL’s equal treatment of all components during the sanitizing process leads to a reduced utility in the private summary. Additionally, the truncation level
M
controls the trade-off between variance, bias, and privacy; this approach would force one to either introduce more noise or accept higher bias when more components are required to deal with complex sample trajectories.

3
The ICLP Mechanism and its Feasibility

In this section, we first define the Independent Component Laplace Process, and then formally propose the
the ICLP mechanism
. To achieve
\epsilon
-DP, we will release the private summary that takes the form of
\tilde{f}_{D}=f_{D}+\sigma Z
, where
\sigma
is a positive scalar and
Z
as an ICLP noise. Initially, we assume
f_{D}
lies in a real separable Hilbert space
\mathbb{H}
. Then we also show that privacy protection can also hold for the space of continuous functions (which is not a Hilbert space) as well under certain assumptions on the covariance operator. The proofs of all the Lemmas and Theorems can be found in Appendix
A
.

3.1
Independent Component Laplace Process

The proposed stochastic process is motivated by
Mirshani et al. 2019
, who achieved
(\epsilon,\delta)
-DP on functional summaries in Banach spaces. Formally, their mechanism can be written as
\tilde{f}_{D}=f_{D}+\sigma Z
where
Z\sim GP(0,C)
is a centered Gaussian process with covariance operator
C
. There is a dual perspective of this mechanism. By applying the Karhunen-Loéve Theorem
[
Kosambi 2016
]
, the mechanism is equivalent to

\tilde{f}_{D}=f_{D}+\sigma Z=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(\left\langle f_{D},\phi_{j}\right\rangle+\sigma\left\langle Z,\phi_{j}\right\rangle\right)\phi_{j},

(1)

where
\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
and
\{\phi_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
are the eigenvalues and eigenfunctions of
C
and
\langle Z,\phi_{j}\rangle
independently follows
N(0,\lambda_{j})
, meaning that the mechanism perturbs each component’s coefficient with independent normal random variables. Unfortunately, the existing Laplace process cannot play a role analogous to the Gaussian process under such an expansion since it is an elliptical distribution and it has been proved that no elliptical distribution can be used to achieve
\epsilon
-DP in infinite-dimensional spaces.
Specifically, in an infinite dimensional space, adding any elliptical distribution is equivalent to adding noise from a Gaussian process, which only satisfies
(\epsilon,\delta)
-DP, the weaker notion of
\epsilon
-DP. We refer readers to Theorem 4 of
Reimherr and Awan 2019a
for more detail.
Motivated by the dual perspective from the Karhunen-Loéve expansion and the Laplace mechanism in the scalar setting, we consider adding independent Laplace noises with heterogeneous variances in the expansion (
1
), which is equivalent to perturbing the functional summary with a particular stochastic process. The desired stochastic process is defined as follows.

Definition 2
.

Let
X
be a random element in
\mathbb{H}
with
\E\|X\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}<\infty
. Given a non-negative decreasing real sequence,
\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
, satisfying
\sum_{j\geq 1}\lambda_{j}<\infty
, and an orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{H}
,
\{\phi_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
,
we say
X
is an Independent Component Laplace Process (ICLP) with mean
\mu
if it admits the decomposition

X=\mu+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sqrt{\lambda_{j}}Z_{j}\phi_{j},

where
Z_{j}
are i.i.d. Laplace random variables with zero-mean and variance
1
.

Remark 1
.

If the objective is to achieve
\epsilon
-DP, alternative i.i.d. sequences of random variables
\{Z_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
with zero mean and unit variance, characterized by a well-constructed density, are technically viable. However, this requires that the density tail of these random variables be calibrated “just right” to comply with
\epsilon
-DP. Typically, their density tails should be as heavy, or closely similar, to the Laplace distribution. Additionally, practical considerations arise regarding the sampling of these variables, as theoretically, one would need to sample an infinite number of them.

The collection of square integrable random elements of
\mathbb{H}
is itself a Hilbert space with inner product
\E\langle X,Y\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}
. Thus, while the ICLP is given as an infinite sum, it is still well-defined, see Chapter 1 in
Bosq 2000
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
C
represent the covariance operator of the ICLP, with
\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
and
\{\phi_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
denoting its eigenvalues and eigenfunctions, respectively. If
C
belongs to the trace class, i.e. its non-negative decreasing real eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
satisfy
\sum_{j\geq 1}\lambda_{j}<\infty
, and the basis
\{\phi_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
is orthonormal in
\mathbb{H}
, then the stochastic process as defined in Definition
2
is well-defined within
\mathbb{H}
.

3.2
Feasibility in Separable Hilbert Spaces

To investigate the feasibility of a randomized mechanism for
\epsilon
-DP, one can start with the equivalence/orthogonality of probability measures. As discussed in
Awan et al. 2019
and
Reimherr and Awan 2019a
, the probability measures induced by an
\epsilon
-DP mechanism are necessarily equivalent (though this is not sufficient for DP) in a probabilistic sense; otherwise, it is impossible to be DP if the measures are orthogonal. More specifically, if the mechanism produces a private summary
\tilde{f}_{D}
that is probabilistically orthogonal to
\tilde{f}_{D^{\prime}}
, i.e. there exists a
A\in\mathcal{F}
so that
P_{D}(A)=0
and
P_{D^{\prime}}(A)=1
, then the mechanism cannot be DP since
f_{D}
and
f_{D^{\prime}}
can be distinguished with probability one on
A
. In the following, we use this perspective to develop the feasibility of the ICLP mechanism. Denote the probability measure family induced by the ICLP mechanism as
\{P_{D}:D\in\mathcal{D}\}
, in the following theorem, we provide necessary and sufficient conditions for pairwise equivalence in
\{P_{D}:D\in\mathcal{D}\}
.

Theorem 2

(
Equivalence of ICLP measure
)
.

Let
D,D^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}
be two adjacent datasets,
\tilde{f}_{D},\tilde{f}_{D^{\prime}}
be the private summaries based on the ICLP mechanism. Denote the corresponding probability measures over
\mathbb{H}
as
P_{D}
and
P_{D^{\prime}}
, and the covariance operator of ICLP as

. Then
P_{D}
and
P_{D^{\prime}}
are equivalent if and only if

f_{D}-f_{D^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{H}_{C}=\left\{h\in\mathbb{H}:\|h\|_{C}<\infty\right\}.

(2)

Theorem
2
shows that if the difference of
f_{D}
and
f_{D^{\prime}}
resides in the Cameron-Martin space of
C
then the probability family will be pairwise equivalent. An analogous result for the equivalence of elliptical distributions appears in Theorem 2 of
Reimherr and Awan 2019a
even though the ICLP is not an elliptical distribution. However, it turns out that, unlike elliptical distributions, pairwise equivalence is not enough for the ICLP mechanism to achieve DP. To see the reason behind this, one needs to consider the density of

in
\mathbb{H}

\mathbb{R}^{d}

\mathbb{H}
.
Fortunately, we are adding the same type of noise to functional summaries, and therefore we only need the density as the Radon-Nikodym derivative of
P_{D}
w.r.t.
P_{0}

P_{0}
is the probability measure induced by
\sigma Z
.

Lemma 1

(
Density of ICLP
)
.

Let

and

be the probability measures induced by

and

respectively. Suppose

w.r.t.

is given by

\frac{dP_{h}}{dP_{0}}(z)=exp\left\{-\frac{1}{\sigma}\left(\left\|z-h\right\|_{1,C}-\left\|z\right\|_{1,C}\right)\right\},

(3)

almost everywhere and is unique.

Now we are ready to show why Condition (
2
) is not enough for
\epsilon

guarantees the pairwise equivalence between

and

-DP however.
Meanwhile,

is enough for

-DP with Gaussian process with covariance

[
Mirshani et al. 2019
]
since it allows densities to be unbounded up to a set with

. In the next theorem, we will show the appropriate space that

should reside in is actually the subspace of
\mathcal{H}_{1,C}
.

Theorem 3

(
Impossibility of The ICLP Mechanism
)
.

Under the same conditions of Theorem
2
, let

\mathcal{H}_{1,C}=\left\{f\in\mathbb{H}:\|f\|_{1,C}<\infty\right\}

be a subspace of
\mathcal{H}_{C}
and if

f_{D_{1}}-f_{D_{2}}\in\mathcal{H}_{C}\setminus\mathcal{H}_{1,C},

then there is no
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that the ICLP mechanism,

satisfies

-DP.

Indeed, if
f_{D}
resides in the gap between
\mathcal{H}_{C}
and
\mathcal{H}_{1,C}
, the sensitivity of

will be infinite and there is no possibility to calibrate the ICLP noise with any

to achieve

-DP. Now, with the proper space in Theorem

and the feasible density in Lemma

, we can establish the ICLP mechanism formally.

Theorem 4

(
The ICLP Mechanism
)
.

Let

be the functional summary and

. Define the

\Delta=\sup_{D\sim D^{\prime}}\|f_{D}-f_{D^{\prime}}\|_{1,C}\quad\textit{and}\quad\sigma=\frac{\Delta}{\epsilon}.

3.3
Example of
\mathcal{H}_{C}
and
\mathcal{H}_{1,C}
for Smooth Functions

In this section, we provide a concrete example of the space
\mathcal{H}_{C}
and
\mathcal{H}_{1,C}
in which the smoothness of the element in the spaces is of primary interest. Consider the Hilbert space
\mathbb{H}
as
L^{2}(\mathcal{X})
where
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the input space. For the ICLP covariance function

are an orthonormal basis of

and

thus can be expressed as

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{C}=\left\{f=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}f_{j}\phi_{j}\in L^{2}(\mathcal{X}):\sum_{j=1}j^{2\beta}f_{j}^{2}<\infty\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{1,C}=\left\{f=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}f_{j}\phi_{j}\in L^{2}(\mathcal{X}):\sum_{j=1}j^{\beta}|f_{j}|<\infty\right\}.

If
\{\phi_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
are the Fourier basis,
\mathcal{H}_{C}
corresponds to a Sobolev space of order
\beta
, containing functions with weak derivatives up to order
\beta
are
L^{2}
-integrable.
\mathcal{H}_{1,C}
corresponds to a Hölder space of order

, consists of those functions with continuous derivatives up to order

and
\lfloor\beta\rfloor
-th derivative is

Hölder continuous. For further details, see

.

3.4
Extensions To Space of Continuous Functions

Theorem
4
implies the ICLP mechanism provides privacy protection for a wide range of infinite-dimensional objects in separable Hilbert spaces. The DP

]

,
\tilde{Z}_{\omega}(t)
is continuous w.r.t.
t\in T
;

2.

For any
t\in T
,
P(\tilde{Z}(t)=Z(t))=1
.

Meaning that there exists a stochastic process in
\mathcal{C}(T)
equally distributed as the ICLP except on a zero-measure set.

All of the results of the ICLP mechanism for
\mathbb{H}
in Section
3.2
are now applicable to summaries in
\mathcal{C}(T)
. Furthermore, the point-wise evaluation is now a measurable operation and thus is protected. We also note that the proof of Theorem
5
is not just a standard theorem from stochastic processes and relies heavily on the structure of the ICLP.

4
Methodology

Based on Theorem
3
and
4
, to achieve
\epsilon
-DP one would need to guarantee the difference of functional summaries,
f_{D}-f_{D^{\prime}}
, lies in
\mathcal{H}_{1,C}
for any neighboring databases
D
and
D^{\prime}
. We call a functional summary that satisfies such conditions a qualified summary. This is a challenging task to do directly and it is easier to address the problem by restricting the individual functional summary residing in
\mathcal{H}_{1,C}
, which automatically leads to
f_{D}-f_{D^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{H}_{1,C}
. In the rest of this section, we will provide different approaches to constructing qualified summaries. We also apply the ICLP mechanism to achieve privacy protection over several statistical estimation problems.

4.1
Generalized Obtainment of Qualified Summaries

To derive the qualified summaries, we leverage Regularized Empirical Risk Minimization (RERM) to obtain qualified summaries
f_{D}
such that
f_{D}\in\mathcal{H}_{1,C}
.

To satisfy
f_{D}\in\mathcal{H}_{1,C}
while avoiding a finite-dimensional representation, we consider obtaining
f_{D}
via RERM. Formally, let
L(f,D):\mathbb{H}\times\mathcal{D}\to\mathbb{R}
be a loss function, the
ICLP with Absolute Regularization (ICLP-AR)
estimator is defined as follows.

(\text{{ICLP-AR}}):\quad\hat{f}_{D}=\underset{f\in\mathbb{H}}{\operatorname{argmin}}\left\{L(f,D)+\psi\left\|f\right\|_{1,C^{\eta}}\right\}\quad\textit{for}\ \eta\geq 1,

(5)

where
C^{\eta}
is the power kernel of
C
that shares the same eigenfunctions as
C
while the eigenvalues are raised to
\lambda_{j}^{\eta}
and
\psi
is the regularization parameter.

The benefits of using a power of the kernel,
C^{\eta}
, are twofold. First, the space corresponding to
\|\cdot\|_{1,C^{\eta}}
is a subspace of
\mathcal{H}_{1,C}
, guaranteeing that
\hat{f}_{D}\in\mathcal{H}_{1,C}
. Second, it allows more flexibility to control the smoothness of the constructed functional summaries. Later on, we will see even though
\eta=1
is a natural setting, a slightly over-smoothing summary can be helpful for utility and even make privacy error negligible compared to statistical error. However, we will see in Section
4.2
that there are some serious drawbacks to using the
\|\cdot\|_{1,C}
-norm as the regularization.

We, therefore, consider restricting functional summaries in the power space of
\mathcal{H}_{C}
, i.e.
\mathcal{H}_{C^{\eta}}
and using
\|\cdot\|_{C^{\eta}}
as the regularization term in RERM as our final strategy, which turns out to work quite well in our applications. Formally, for a given
\eta>1
, by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

\|h\|_{1,C}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{|h_{j}|}{\sqrt{\lambda_{j}}}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{|h_{j}|}{\lambda_{j}^{\frac{\eta}{2}}}\lambda_{j}^{\frac{\eta-1}{2}}\leq\left\|h\right\|_{C^{\eta}}\sqrt{\operatorname{trace}(C^{\eta-1})}.

Therefore, by taking
\eta>1
such that
C^{\eta-1}
is a trace-class operator, we obtain
f_{D}
via the following
ICLP with Quadratic Regularization (ICLP-QR)
strategy.

\text{({ICLP-QR})}:\quad\hat{f}_{D}=\underset{f\in\mathbb{H}}{\operatorname{argmin}}\left\{L(f,\mathcal{D})+\psi\left\|f\right\|_{C^{\eta}}^{2}\right\}\quad\textit{for}\ \eta>1.

(6)

Since the power space
\mathcal{H}_{C^{\eta}}
is an RKHS, and
\|\cdot\|_{C^{\eta}}
takes quadratic form, we name this approach as ICLP-QR.
Here, the
\eta
is strictly greater than
1
leads to
\mathcal{H}_{C^{\eta}}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{1,C}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{C}
, which guarantees the feasibility of the ICLP mechanism. In general, this approach allows us to trade off the regularization and the utility, i.e. we restrict
f_{D}
in a slightly smoother space but gain good properties.

4.2
Protection for Mean Functions

In this part, we consider the problem of protecting the privacy of the mean function summary. Assume
X_{1},X_{2},\cdots,X_{n}
are i.i.d. elements of an arbitrary real separable Hilbert space
\mathbb{H}
with
\E X_{i}=\mu_{0}\in\mathbb{H}
. Our goal is to release a differentially private estimator of the true mean function
\mu_{0}
that satisfies
\epsilon
-DP. When using FRL, one can start with
\hat{\mu}_{D}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
, which is an unbiased estimator of the mean function
\mu_{0}
. For the ICLP mechanism, we use RERM with square loss to obtain qualified non-private summaries, i.e.,

\hat{\mu}_{D}=\underset{\theta\in\mathbb{H}}{\operatorname{argmin}}\left\{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|X_{i}-\theta\right\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}+\psi P(\theta)\right\},

(7)

with
P(\theta)=\left\|\theta\right\|_{1,C^{\eta}}
or
\left\|\theta\right\|_{C^{\eta}}^{2}
corresponding to ICLP-AR and ICLP-QR respectively.

To obtain the global sensitivity and utility analysis for the proposed strategies, we first state some standard assumptions in DP literature on the norm of the observed data and the eigenvalue decay rate of
C
.

Assumption 1

(Boundness)
.

Assume for any sample path
X
, its
L^{2}
-norm is bounded by
\tau
, i.e.
\|X\|_{\mathbb{H}}\leq\tau
.

The bounded norm assumption is commonly used in DP literature, primarily to ensure a finite global sensitivity. This assumption is often adapted to align with specific DP paradigms and mechanisms. For instance, in employing the Gaussian mechanism to attain
(\epsilon,\delta)
-DP, it is customary to assume that the
\ell^{2}
-norm of the data is bounded, in line with the global sensitivity being assessed via
\|\cdot\|_{\ell^{2}}
. Conversely, for the Laplace mechanism, which aims for
\epsilon
-DP, the focus shifts to the
\ell^{1}
-norm, with global sensitivity gauged through
\|\cdot\|_{\ell^{1}}
. In cases where a more general norm in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is used, such as in the exponential mechanism, the data is presumed to have such a finite general norm, see mean estimation example in
Reimherr and Awan 2019b
.

In the context of the ICLP mechanism, where global sensitivity is evaluated under
\|\cdot\|_{1,C}
, it seems logical to posit that
\|X\|_{1,C}\leq\tau
for some finite
\tau
. However, the
\|\cdot\|_{1,C}
-norm is intrinsically linked to
C
, dependent on the eigenvalues and eigenfunctions of the covariance used in ICLP. This reliance implies that assuming
|X|_{1,C}\leq\tau
could narrow the ICLP mechanism’s applicability. Practically, it would mean that feasible
X_{i}
should be drawn from stochastic processes
X
whose covariance eigenfunctions align with those of ICLP’s covariance
C
, a condition often unverifiable in practice. Therefore, we consider a more general and relaxed boundness assumption by assuming
\|X\|_{\mathbb{H}}\leq\tau
which is more likely to be met in most practical scenarios.

Assumption 2

(Eigenvalue Decay Rate (EDR))
.

Suppose the eigenvalue decay rate of
C
is
\beta>1
, i.e. there exist constant
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
such that

c_{1}j^{-\beta}\leq\lambda_{j}\leq c_{2}j^{-\beta},\quad\forall i=1,2,\cdots.

Note that the eigenvalues
\lambda_{j}
and EDR are only determined by the ICLP covariance
C
. The polynomial eigenvalue decay rate assumption is standard in nonparametric literature. For example, if
C
satisfies the Sacks–Ylvisaker conditions
[
Sacks and Ylvisaker 1966
;
Sacks and Ylvisaker 1968
;
Sacks and Ylvisaker 1970
]
of order
s
, then
\lambda_{j}\asymp j^{-2(s+1)}
. If setting
C
equals to the reproducing kernel of the univariate Sobolev space
\mathcal{W}_{2}^{m}([0,1])
results in
\lambda_{j}\asymp j^{-2m}
, see
Micchelli and Wahba 1979
;
Yuan and Cai 2010
for more instances.

In the following theorems, we derive the close form of the estimators and provide their global sensitivity analysis for FRL, ICLP-AR, and ICLP-QR.

Theorem 6

(Global Sensitivity Analysis)
.

Suppose Assumption
1
holds, then

1.

(FRL) Suppose the functional summary used in FRL is a truncated sample mean function, i.e.
\hat{\mu}_{D}=\sum_{j=1}^{M}\langle\bar{X},\phi_{j}\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}\phi_{j}
, then

\Delta=\underset{D,D^{\prime}}{\operatorname{max}}\|\hat{\mu}_{D}-\hat{\mu}_{D^{\prime}}\|_{\ell^{1}}\leq\frac{2M\tau}{n}.

2.

(ICLP-AR) The solution of regularized ERM is

\hat{\mu}_{D}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}s_{\psi,2\lambda_{j}^{\eta/2}}\left(\left\langle\bar{X},\phi_{j}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}\right)\phi_{j},

(8)

for all
\eta\geq 1
and
s_{a,b}(x)=\operatorname{sgn}(|x|)\left(|x|-a/b\right)^{+}
is the soft thresholding function with threshold
a/b
. Then there exists an integer
J^{*}
such that

\sup_{D\sim D^{\prime}}\|\hat{\mu}_{D}-\hat{\mu}_{D^{\prime}}\|_{1,C}\leq\frac{2\tau}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{J^{*}}\lambda_{j}^{-\frac{\eta}{2}}.

3.

(ICLP-QR) The solution of regularized ERM is

\hat{\mu}_{D}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{j}^{\eta}}{\lambda_{j}^{\eta}+\psi}\left\langle\bar{X},\phi_{j}\right\rangle\phi_{j},

(9)

for all
\eta>1+\beta^{-1}
.
Then the global sensitivity of
\hat{\mu}_{D}
satisfies

\sup_{D\sim D^{\prime}}\|\hat{\mu}_{D}-\hat{\mu}_{D^{\prime}}\|_{1,C}\leq\frac{2\tau}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\lambda_{j}^{\eta-\frac{1}{2}}}{\lambda_{j}^{\eta}+\psi}\right).

Remark 2
.

The integer
J^{*}:=\operatorname{min}\{j\geq 1:\tau\leq\psi/(2\lambda_{j})^{\eta/2}\}
in ICLP-AR indeed can be viewed as a truncation number as the coefficients after
J^{*}
will be shrunk to
0
, i.e. the summation in (
8
) is indeed finite. The upper bound for global sensitivity is based on the fact that, in the worst-case scenario, the coefficients are not shrunk to zero and thus the soft thresholding adjustments cancel out. Therefore, unfortunately, the ICLP-AR type estimator doesn’t produce a better sensitivity than the FRL approach while the soft thresholding introduces extra bias into the summary. On the other hand, the coefficients of the ICLP-QR estimator (
9
) will not be shrunk exactly to zero, and hence one is able to perturb the functional summary with the truly infinite-dimensional ICLP.

In the next Theorem, we provide a guarantee of the utility of the ICLP mechanism, which is measured by the mean square error (MSE) of private summarised
\tilde{\mu}_{D}
.

Theorem 7
.

Assume
X_{i}
are i.i.d. observation drawn from population
X
with mean function
\mu_{0}
, and the EDR assumption holds. We also assume
\mu_{0}\in\mathcal{H}_{C^{\eta}}
for some
\eta>1+\beta^{-1}
. Let
\hat{\mu}_{D}
be non-private estimators under different approaches and
\tilde{\mu}_{D}
as their sanitized version. Then

\E\left\|\tilde{\mu}_{D}-\mu_{0}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &O\left(\frac{8M^{3}\tau^{2}}{n^{2}\epsilon^{2}}+\frac{1}{n}+M^{-\eta\beta}\right),&\text{(FRL)}\\
&O\left(\frac{4\tau^{2}}{n^{2}\epsilon^{2}}(J^{*})^{\eta\beta+2}+\frac{1}{n}+\psi\right),&\text{(ICLP-AR)}\\
&O\left(\frac{8\tau^{2}}{n^{2}\epsilon^{2}}\text{tr}(C^{\eta-1})\psi^{-\frac{2}{\eta}(\frac{\beta+2}{2\beta})}+\frac{1}{n}+\psi\right).&\text{(ICLP-QR)}\end{aligned}\right.

We refer
\E\|\tilde{\mu}_{D}-\hat{\mu}_{D}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}
as the privacy error and
\E\|\hat{\mu}_{D}-\mu_{0}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}
the statistical error of non-private summaries
\hat{\mu}_{D}
. Choosing the tuning parameters optimally in different strategies leads to the following explicit bounds.

Corollary 1
.

Suppose the condition on the above Theorem
7
holds and Assumption
1
holds.

1.

(FWCL) Setting
n^{\frac{1}{\eta\beta}}\lesssim M\lesssim n^{\frac{1}{3}}
leads to

\E\left\|\tilde{\mu}_{D}-\mu_{0}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}=O(n^{-1}).

2.

(ICLP-AR) There is no
\psi
such that

\E\left\|\tilde{\mu}_{D}-\mu_{0}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}=O(n^{-1}).

3.

(ICLP-QR) Setting
(n\epsilon^{2})^{-\frac{\eta\beta}{\beta+2}}\lesssim\psi\lesssim n^{-1}
with
\eta\geq 1+2\beta^{-1}
leads to

\E\left\|\tilde{\mu}_{D}-\mu_{0}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}=O(n^{-1}).

In particular, setting
\psi\asymp n^{-1}
and
\eta>1+2\beta^{-1}
,

\E\left\|\tilde{\mu}_{D}-\hat{\mu}_{D}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}=o(n^{-1}).

Corollary
1
implies that by optimally choosing the finite-dimensional number
M
in FRL or the regularization parameter
\psi
in ICLP-QR, the privacy error will not dominate the total error, making the MSE of private summaries match the optimal MSE for non-private summaries, which is
O(n^{-1})
. Besides, in ICLP-QR, a slight oversmoothing (choosing
\eta>1+2\beta^{-1}
) makes the privacy error to be a lower order of the statistical error and thus asymptotically negligible. This phenomenon is referred to as “free privacy” since sanitizing the summaries will not affect their statistical performance. Conversely, for ICLP-AR, achieving the same order of MSE optimality with private summaries as with non-private ones appears unattainable, regardless of the
\psi
chosen. This could be attributed to our method of analyzing the MSE using the largest integer
J^{*}
(beyond which all coefficients are zero), representing the worst scenario. In real-world applications, the actual value of
J^{*}
is usually smaller than what we use in our analysis, leading to a performance that might be slightly worse than that of FRL, see in Section
6
. However, it’s important to note that this true value of
J^{*}
is not analyzable within our current framework.

Finally, while this section primarily considers a relatively simple problem of mean functional protection in this section, the approaches and analysis can be methodologically extended to other functions whose estimations can be boiled down to estimate the average statistic. For example, the covariance functional estimation is essentially the average of sample
(X_{i}(t)-\bar{X}(t))(X_{i}(s)-\bar{X}(s))
; estimating the coefficient in the function-on-scalar linear regression model is essentially the average of sample
X_{i}Y_{i}(t)
. Due to the inherently repetitive nature of these problems, we only provide analysis on mean functional protection and refer readers to Appendix
C
for more detailed discussions on the parallels between these problems.

4.3
Beyond Functional Data

In this part, we demonstrate how the ICLP mechanism is not only feasible on functional summaries but also can be applied to more general learning problems where the summary of interest is a function.

Kernel Density Estimation:

Let
D=\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\}\subseteq T
, where
T
is a compact set over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, be an i.i.d. sample from a distribution with density
f_{0}
. For any given ICLP kernel
K
, we adopt the ICLP-QR by picking the density estimation kernel as
K^{\eta}
with
\eta>1
. For a given
d\times d
symmetric and positive definite bandwidth matrix
\mathbf{H}
, the kernel density estimator under ICLP-QR takes the form of

\hat{K}_{D}(x)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}K_{\mathbf{H}}^{\eta}\left(x-x_{i}\right)=\frac{1}{n\sqrt{det(\mathbf{H})}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}K^{\eta}\left(\mathbf{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(x-x_{i})\right).

(10)

We now provide the global sensitivity and utility analysis of
\hat{K}_{D}(x)
in the following theorem.

Theorem 8
.

Suppose
K^{\eta}(\cdot,\cdot)
is pointwise bounded by constant
M_{K}
, then the global sensitivity
\Delta
of
\hat{K}_{D}(x)
in (
10
) satisfies

\Delta=\sup_{D\sim D^{\prime}}\left\|\hat{K}_{D}-\hat{K}_{D^{\prime}}\right\|_{1,K}\leq\frac{2M_{K}}{n\sqrt{det(\mathbf{H})}}\sqrt{tr(K^{\eta-1})}.

Furthermore, taking
\mathbf{H}
to be a diagonal matrix with same entry, i.e.
\mathbf{H}=h\mathbf{I}
, and assume
f_{0}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}
is absolutely continuous,
\int_{T}(f_{0}^{{}^{\prime\prime\prime}}(x))^{2}dx<\infty
and
\int_{T}K^{\eta}(x)dx=1
, the risk
R
satisfies

R=\E\int_{T}\left(\tilde{f}_{D}(x)-f_{0}(x)\right)^{2}dx\leq O\left(\frac{c_{1}}{n^{2}h^{2d}}+h^{4}+\frac{c_{2}}{nh^{d}}\right),

for some constants
c_{2}
and
c_{2}
.

Remark 3
.

If
h
is taken to be
h\asymp n^{\frac{1}{4+d}}
, then
R=O(n^{-\frac{4}{4+d}})
, which matches the optimal kernel density estimation risk
[
Wasserman 2006
]
.

The connection between estimating kernel and the noise kernel also appeared in
Hall et al. 2013
where they stated that one can achieve
(\epsilon,\delta)
-DP by adding a Gaussian process with its covariance function equal to the kernel used in estimation. For privacy-safe bandwidth,
h
, we can pick
h\asymp n^{\frac{1}{4+d}}
to ensure privacy is gained for free. But a private version of “rule of thumb", see
Rao and Scott 1992
and
Hall et al. 2013
, is also feasible.

Functionals via Regularized Empirical Risk Minimization:

The functional summaries one desires to release may come from learning algorithms like regularization-based algorithms. In section
4.1
, we have proposed using such algorithms to obtain qualified functional summaries. Here, we generalized the approach to broader scenarios like non-parametric regression and classification. Let
\mathcal{D}=\{d_{1},\cdots,d_{n}\}
be the collection of
n
samples, where
d_{i}
is a tuple with finite size. Given a loss function
L
, we consider the following RERM problem,

\hat{f}_{D}=\underset{f\in\mathcal{H}_{C^{\eta}}}{\operatorname{argmin}}\left\{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}L(d_{i},f)+\psi\|f\|_{C^{\eta}}\right\}\quad\text{for some}\quad\eta>1.

(11)

When
d_{i}
’s are couples i.e.
d_{i}=(y_{i},x_{i})
, (
11
) can be viewed as non-parametric classification (
y_{i}
’s take discrete value) or regression (
y_{i}
’s take continuous value) problem. The solution of (
11
) can be expressed as
\hat{f}_{D}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}C^{\eta}(\cdot,d_{i})
by the Representer Theorem
[
Kimeldorf and Wahba 1971
]
. However, although the theorem provides an elegant solution for (
11
), it is ill for calculating the global sensitivity as all the elements in the vector
(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n})
change once we swap one individual in the dataset. In the following theorem, we provide sensitivity analysis for
\hat{f}_{D}
under certain regularized conditions.

Theorem 9
.

Suppose
\hat{f}_{D}
is the solution of (
11
) and the loss function
L
in (
11
) is an
M
-admissible loss function
[
Bousquet and Elisseeff 2002
]
, then the global sensitivity for
\hat{f}_{D}
satisfies

\Delta=\sup_{D\sim D^{\prime}}\left\|\hat{f}_{D}-\hat{f}_{D^{\prime}}\right\|_{1,C}\leq\frac{M}{\psi n}\sqrt{\sup_{x}C^{\eta}(x,x)}\sqrt{tr(C^{\eta-1})}.

Remark 4
.

One can also prove the privacy loss
\E\|\tilde{f}_{D}-\hat{f}_{D}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
is bounded by
c_{1}(\psi n)^{-2}
. We don’t provide utility analysis for this case study as the statistical loss can vary based on different regularization conditions on
f_{0}
and is out of the scope of this paper.

The application scenario is wide since the upper bound holds for any convex and locally
M
-admissible loss function and bounded kernel with finite trace. For example, support vector mechanism with hinge-loss, non-parametric regression with square-loss, and logistic regression with
log(1+x)
-loss are potentially applicable learning models.

4.4
Privacy-Safe Regularization Parameter Selection

Determining the regularization parameters in the mechanisms, like the finite-dimensional number
M
(for subspace embedding mechanisms) and
\psi
(for the ICLP mechanism), is crucial to guarantee a reasonable performance of the private releases. Tuning regularization parameter in statistical modeling has been well studied and
Cross Validation
(CV), or one of its many variants, is a widely used approach. However, CV focuses on balancing variance and bias in the statistical error, which ignores the trade-off between the privacy error and the statistical error. To fit CV into the DP framework,
Private Cross Validation
(PCV) is proposed in
Mirshani et al. 2019
, which aims to find out the “sweet spot" between privacy error and statistical error. However, as data-driven approaches, both CV and PCV are not truly privacy-safe since the regularization parameters may contain information about the data. There are some approaches that one can get end-to-end privacy-guaranteed regularization parameters. For example, one can use out-sample public datasets
[
Zhang et al. 2012
]
or one can spend extra privacy budget on the tuning process
[
Chaudhuri et al. 2011
;
Chaudhuri and Vinterbo 2013
]
.

Since the ICLP mechanism is tied to a kernel, one can obtain privacy-safe regularization parameters by picking kernels whose eigenvalues decay at a polynomial rate and therefore satisfy the conditions in Theorem
7
, then the optimal values for
M
and
\psi
in Theorem
7
can be directly used as regularization parameter inputs. We name this approach as
“Privacy Safe Selection” (PSS)
. The PSS doesn’t degrade the privacy guarantee since the employed regularization parameters only rely on sample size, privacy budget, and the noise’s covariance function, without further information about the dataset. We would like to remark that the PSS is not a data-driven approach since its acquisition never depends on the data. In practice, the constant for employed PSS values can affect the performance of the ICLP mechanism. In our experiments, we observe that by appropriately normalizing the data trajectories and the trace of the covariance kernel, setting the constant to
1
usually leads to satisfactory performance.

6
Experiments

In this section, we numerically evaluate the effectiveness of the ICLP mechanism and some other comparable mechanisms like the FRL with fixed basis and the Bernstein mechanism.

6.1
Simulation for Mean Function Protection

In this section, we conduct the simulation for the mean function privacy protection problem discussed in Section
4.2
. We use the isotropic Matérn kernel
[
Cressie and Huang 1999
]
as the covariance kernel for the ICLP since its resulting RKHS ties to a particular Sobolev space, allowing us to control the smoothness directly.
Specifically, the RKHS associated with Matérn kernel
C_{\alpha}
over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is norm-equivalent to Sobolev space
H^{\alpha+\frac{d}{2}}
and thus the eigenvalue
\lambda_{j}
decays polynomially as
\lambda_{j}\asymp j^{-2(\alpha+\frac{d}{2})}
.
It takes the form of

C_{\alpha}(s,t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\nu)2^{\alpha-1}}\left(\frac{\sqrt{2\alpha}|t-s|}{\rho}\right)^{\alpha}K_{\alpha}\left(\frac{\sqrt{2\alpha}|t-s|}{\rho}\right)

where
K_{\alpha}
is the modified Bessel function. Denote
\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
and
\{\phi_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
as its eigenvalues and eigenfunctions respectively. Specifically, we set
\rho=0.1
and
\alpha=1.5
such that
\lambda_{j}\asymp j^{-4}
.

For sample curves, we consider
\mathbb{H}=L^{2}([0,1])
and generate curves as

X_{i}(t)=\mu_{0}(t)+e_{i}(t)

where
e_{i}(t)
are Gaussian processes with zero mean and Radial basis function kernel as covariance functions.

For the true mean function, we consider four true mean function scenarios:

S-1:

\mu_{0}(t)=10t*exp(-t)
.

S-2:

\mu_{0}(t)=0.3f_{0.3,0.05}(t)+0.7*f_{0.8,0.05}(t)
.

S-3:

\mu_{0}(t)=0.2*\left(f_{0,0.03}(t)+f_{0.2,0.05}(t)+f_{0.5,0.05}(t)-f_{0.75,0.03}(t)+f_{1,0.03}(t)\right)
.

S-4:

\mu_{0}(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{25}R_{ij}\phi_{j}(t)
, where
R_{ij}\overset{i.i.d.}{\sim}U[-1,1]
.

where
f_{a,b}
is the probability density function of normal distribution with mean
a
and variance
b^{2}
. The shape complexity of the curves rises sequentially, for example, S-1 is just a monotonically increasing function, S-2 is a bimodal function, and S-3 and S-4 are many fluctuating multimodal functions. For the Bernstein mechanism, the implementation is based on r package
diffpriv
and we use the sample mean
\bar{X}(t)
as the non-private summary by setting the cover size parameter as
20
.

The efficacy of the sanitized mean estimators
\tilde{\mu}
is quantified using the
mean square error
(MSE), defined as
\E\|\tilde{\mu}-\mu_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
. Given that each DP strategy yields distinct non-private mean estimators, the corresponding non-private statistical errors also vary. Consequently, we employ the MSE of
\tilde{\mu}
as a comprehensive metric to assess the overall performance of various DP strategies. The MSE is approximated via Monte-Carlo by generating
1000
sanitized mean estimators.

We also conduct the experiments in different settings. For example, we consider generated error stochastic process
e_{i}
from Gaussian processes with different covariance functions, or
e_{i}
is generated from basis expansion with heavy tail distributions. We also consider the ICLP covariance kernel as Matérn kernel with
\alpha=2.5
. We refer to Appendix
D
for these additional experiment results.

reaches optimal rate is a collection of integers, i.e.
\mathcal{M}=\{M_{1},\cdots,M_{K}\}
. We calculate MSE for each
M\in\mathcal{M}
and take the smallest MSE as the PSS result. While for PCV, we consider a wider range of
\mathcal{M}
by adding and subtracting
3

8
Conclusion

In this paper, we proposed a new mechanism, the ICLP mechanism, to achieve
\epsilon
-DP for statistical summaries in infinite-dimensional space. It provides a wide range of output privacy protections with more flexible data assumptions and a more robust noise injection process than current mechanisms. We establish its feasibility in separable Hilbert spaces and spaces of continuous functions. Different approaches are proposed to construct qualified summaries compatible with the ICLP mechanism, along with parameter selection via PSS to guarantee end-to-end protection. In the example of mean function privacy protection, we also show that slightly over-smoothing the summary promotes the trade-off between utility and privacy, and can match the known optimal rate for mean function estimation.

There are some limitations of the proposed mechanism and interesting future works. As we show in Section
5
, the implementation of the ICLP mechanism relies on the Karhunen-Loéve expansion and thus will be computationally expensive. Therefore, a computation approach that doesn’t rely on the Karhunen-Loéve expansion is an important future direction. Additionally, even though various experiment results show that by appropriately processing the sample trajectories and the ICLP covariance kernel, omitting the constant for PSS values has led to satisfactory performance, we believe a more careful investigation of the constant can further enhance performance.

Proof of Theorem

Proof.

To prove the existence of the ICLP in
\mathbb{H}
, we only need to prove
\E\langle X,X\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}<\infty
, then the proof can be done by Fubini’s theorem. Notice

\E\langle X,X\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}=\E\sum_{j\geq 1}\sum_{k\geq 1}\sqrt{\lambda_{j}}\sqrt{\lambda_{k}}Z_{j}Z_{k}\langle\phi_{j},\phi_{k}\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}=\E\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}Z_{j}^{2}

Since
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\E\lambda_{j}Z_{j}^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}<\infty
, by Fubini’s theorem,
\E\langle X,X\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}<\infty
, which proves the existence of
X
.

Proof.

To show the explicit form of the Randon-Nikodym derivative of
P_{D}
w.r.t.

\mathbb{H}

\ell^{2}

\mathbb{H}

\mathcal{T}:\mathbb{H}\rightarrow\ell^{2}

\mathcal{T}(f)=\{\langle f,\phi_{j}\rangle\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
, and its inverse is
\mathcal{T}^{-1}(\{\langle f,\phi_{j}\rangle\})=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\langle f,\phi_{j}\rangle\phi_{j}
. This mapping is an isometry between
\mathbb{H}
and
\ell^{2}
and we can consider the probability measure over
\ell^{2}
rather than over
\mathbb{H}
.

For a Laplace raodam variable
X\sim\Lap(\mu,b)
over
\mathbb{R}
, it induces a probability measure over
(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{B})
, where
\mathcal{B}
is the borel set over
\mathbb{R}
, as

\gamma_{\mu,b}(dx)=\frac{1}{2b}exp\left(-\frac{|x-\mu|}{b}\right)dx.

Let
\{(\lambda_{j},\phi_{j})\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be the eigencomponents (eigenfunctions and eigenvalues) of
C
and let
\lambda_{j}=2b_{j}^{2}
. By Existence of Product Measures Theorem
[
Tao 2011
]
and isomorphism mapping
\mathcal{T}
,

is a unique probability measure defined as
\gamma(f_{D},C):=\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{f_{Dj},b_{j}}
over
(\mathbb{R}^{\infty},\mathcal{B}^{\infty}):=(\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{R}_{j},\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{B}_{j})
. We further restrict
\gamma(f_{D},C)
on
(\ell^{2},\sigma(\ell^{2}))
and keep denoting it by
\gamma(f_{D},C)
.

By the fact that
\mathcal{T}
is an isomorphism mapping between
\mathcal{H}
and
\ell^{2}
, to prove
P_{D}
and
P_{D^{\prime}}
are equivalent, it’s sufficient to prove

and
\gamma(f_{D^{\prime}},C)
are equivalent. We now formally prove
\gamma(h,C)
and
\gamma(0,C)
iff
h\in\mathcal{H}_{C}
. For the “if” part, for two infinite product measures, we can apply Kakutani’s theorem
[
Kakutani 1948
]
. Then the two measures are equivalent if the following sum converge

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\log\int_{R}\sqrt{\frac{\gamma_{j}(h_{j},b_{j})}{\gamma_{j}(0,b_{j})}}\gamma_{j}(0,b_{j})(dx).

A few calculation leads to the target space is

\mathcal{H}_{C}^{*}=\left\{h\in\mathbb{H}:\sum_{j\geq 1}\left[\frac{|h_{j}|}{2b_{j}}-log(1+\frac{|h_{j}|}{2b_{j}})\right]<\infty\right\},

and we now prove that,
\mathcal{H}_{C}^{*}=\mathcal{H}_{C}
. We only need to prove that for a non-negative sequence
\{a_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
, the series
\sum_{j\geq 1}[a_{n}-log(1+a_{n})]
converges if and only if
\sum_{j\geq 1}a_{n}^{2}
converges. Let
f(x)
be the Taylor expansion of
x-log(1+x)
and
g(x)=x^{2}
. Besides, note that
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}f(a_{n})=0
if and only if
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}a_{n}=0
. Thus,

•

If
\sum_{j\geq 1}[a_{n}-log(1+a_{n})]<\infty
, then
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}f(a_{n})=0
and so
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}a_{n}=0
. Then,

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f(a_{n})}{g(a_{n})}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{a_{n}-log(1+a_{n})}{a_{n}^{2}}=\frac{1}{2}

therefore by limit comparison test
\sum_{j\geq 1}a_{n}^{2}
converges too.

•

If
\sum_{j\geq 1}a_{n}^{2}<\infty
, by the same statement as above also holds and therefore
\sum_{j\geq 1}[a_{n}-log(1+a_{n})]<\infty
.

For the “only if" part, the argument is the same as proof of Theorem 2 in
Reimherr and Awan 2019a
.

\displaystyle\E\left\|\hat{\mu}-\mu_{0}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{M}\E\langle X_{1}-\mu_{0},\phi_{j}\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}+\sum_{j=M+1}^{\infty}\langle\mu_{0},\phi_{j}\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{n}+\sum_{j=M+1}^{\infty}\langle\mu_{0},\phi_{j}\rangle_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{n}+\lambda_{M}^{\eta}\|\mu_{0}\|_{C^{\eta}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{n}+M^{-\eta\beta}.

The first inequality is based on Assumption
1
, while the second one is based on the fact that
\mu_{0}\in\mathcal{H}_{C^{\eta}}
.

ICLP-AR:

For the privacy error,

\displaystyle\E\left\|\tilde{\mu}-\hat{\mu}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Delta^{2}}{\epsilon^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{J^{*}}\lambda_{j}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{4\tau^{2}}{\epsilon^{2}n^{2}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{J^{*}}j^{\frac{\eta\beta}{2}}\right)\left(\sum_{j=1}^{J^{*}}j^{-\beta}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}n^{2}}(J^{*})^{\eta\beta+2}.

Next, we turn to Statistical Error. Define
\mu_{0,\psi}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}f_{\psi,2\lambda_{j}^{\frac{\eta_{l}}{2}}}\left(\left\langle\mu_{0},\phi_{j}\right\rangle\right)\phi_{j}
, by triangular inequality

\E\left\|\hat{\mu}-\mu_{0}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}\leq\E\left\|\hat{\mu}-\mu_{0,\psi}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}+\left\|\mu_{0,\psi}-\mu_{0}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}.

For the bias term, let
A=\left\{j:|\mu_{j}|\geq\frac{\psi}{2\lambda_{j}^{\eta_{l}/2}}\right\}
, then

\left\|\mu_{0,\psi}-\mu_{0}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}=\sum_{A}\left(\mu_{0j}-f_{\psi,2\lambda_{j}^{\frac{\eta_{l}}{2}}}(\mu_{0j})\right)^{2}+\sum_{A^{c}}\left(\mu_{0j}-f_{\psi,2\lambda_{j}^{\frac{\eta_{l}}{2}}}(\mu_{0j})\right)^{2}.

Starting with the summation over
A
, since
\frac{\lambda_{j}^{-\frac{\eta_{l}}{2}}}{2}<\frac{|\mu_{0j}|}{\psi}
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{A}\left(\mu_{0j}-f_{\psi,2\lambda_{j}^{\frac{\eta_{l}}{2}}}(\mu_{0j})\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{A}\frac{\psi^{2}}{4\lambda_{j}^{\eta_{l}}}\leq\psi\sum_{A}\frac{\left|\mu_{0j}\right|}{2\lambda_{j}^{\frac{\eta_{l}}{2}}}\leq\frac{\psi}{2}\left\|\mu_{0}\right\|_{1,C^{\eta_{l}}}.

Turning to summation over
A^{c}
, since
|\mu_{0j}|\leq\frac{\psi}{2\lambda_{j}^{\frac{\eta_{l}}{2}}}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{A^{c}}\left(\mu_{0j}-f_{\psi,2\lambda_{j}^{\frac{\eta_{l}}{2}}}(\mu_{0j})\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{A^{c}}\mu_{0j}^{2}\leq\psi\sum_{A^{c}}\frac{|\mu_{0j}|}{2\lambda_{j}^{\frac{\eta_{l}}{2}}}\leq\frac{\psi}{2}\left\|\mu_{0}\right\|_{1,C^{\eta_{l}}}.

Therefore, the overall bias is bounded by

\left\|\mu_{0,\psi}-\mu_{0}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}\leq\frac{\psi}{2}\left\|\mu_{0}\right\|_{1,C^{\eta_{l}}}.

Now consider variance term
\E\left\|\hat{\mu}-\mu_{0,\psi}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}
,

\left\|\hat{\mu}-\mu_{0}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(f_{\psi,2\lambda_{j}^{\frac{\eta_{l}}{2}}}\left(\bar{X}_{j}\right)-f_{\psi,2\lambda_{j}^{\frac{\eta_{l}}{2}}}\left(\mu_{0j}\right)\right)^{2}.

Similar to the bias part, the summation can be decomposed into sum of four disjoint pieces

\displaystyle A_{0,0}

\displaystyle=\{|\bar{X}_{j}|\leq\psi/2\lambda_{j}^{\frac{\eta_{l}}{2}},|\mu_{j}|\leq\psi/2\lambda_{j}^{\frac{\eta_{l}}{2}}\},

\displaystyle A_{0,1}

\displaystyle=\{|\bar{X}_{j}|\leq\psi/2\lambda_{j}^{\frac{\eta_{l}}{2}},|\mu_{j}|>\psi/2\lambda_{j}^{\frac{\eta_{l}}{2}}\},

\displaystyle A_{1,0}

\displaystyle=\{|\bar{X}_{j}|>\psi/2\lambda_{j}^{\frac{\eta_{l}}{2}},|\mu_{j}|\leq\psi/2\lambda_{j}^{\frac{\eta_{l}}{2}}\},

\displaystyle A_{1,1}

\displaystyle=\{|\bar{X}_{j}|>\psi/2\lambda_{j}^{\frac{\eta_{l}}{2}},|\mu_{j}|>\psi/2\lambda_{j}^{\frac{\eta_{l}}{2}}\}.

When
j\in A_{0,0}
, the summation is zero. Consider
j\in A_{0,1}
, since
|\bar{X}_{j}|\leq\frac{\psi}{2\lambda_{j}^{\frac{\eta_{l}}{2}}}
,

\displaystyle\left(f_{\psi,2\lambda_{j}^{\frac{\eta_{l}}{2}}}\left(\bar{X}_{j}\right)-f_{\psi,2\lambda_{j}^{\frac{\eta_{l}}{2}}}\left(\mu_{0j}\right)\right)^{2}=\left(f_{\psi,2\lambda_{j}^{\frac{\eta_{l}}{2}}}\left(\mu_{0j}\right)\right)^{2}=\left(\mu_{0j}-\operatorname{sgn}(\mu_{0j})\frac{\psi}{2\lambda_{j}^{\frac{\eta_{l}}{2}}}\right)^{2}\leq\left(\mu_{0j}-\bar{X}_{j}\right)^{2}.

By symmetry, we get the same bound over
A_{1,0}
. So lastly we consider summation over
A_{1,1}
For
j\in A_{1,1}
we have

\displaystyle\left(f_{\psi,2\lambda_{j}^{\frac{\eta_{l}}{2}}}\left(\bar{X}_{j}\right)-f_{\psi,2\lambda_{j}^{\frac{\eta_{l}}{2}}}\left(\mu_{0j}\right)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\mu_{0j}-\bar{X}_{j}-\left(\operatorname{sgn}(\mu_{0j})-\operatorname{sgn}(\bar{X}_{j})\right)\frac{\psi}{2\lambda_{j}^{\frac{\eta_{l}}{2}}}\right)^{2}.

If both
\mu_{0j}
and
\bar{X}_{j}
have the same sign, then this is just
(\mu_{0j}-\bar{X}_{j})^{2}
. If they have opposite signs, then we have

\left|\left(\operatorname{sgn}(\mu_{0j})-\operatorname{sgn}(\bar{X}_{j})\right)\frac{\psi}{2\lambda_{j}^{\frac{\eta_{l}}{2}}}\right|\leq\left|\mu_{0j}-\bar{X}_{j}\right|.

Therefore,

\left(f_{\psi,2\lambda_{j}^{\frac{\eta_{l}}{2}}}\left(\bar{X}_{j}\right)-f_{\psi,2\lambda_{j}^{\frac{\eta_{l}}{2}}}\left(\mu_{0j}\right)\right)^{2}\leq 4\left(\mu_{0j}-\bar{X}_{j}\right)^{2},\quad\textit{for}\quad j\in A_{1,1}.

Finally, the overall variance term is bounded by

\E\left\|\hat{\mu}-\mu_{0}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}\leq 4\E\left\|\bar{X}-\mu_{0}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}\leq\frac{4}{n}\E\left\|X_{1}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}\lesssim n^{-1}.

ICLP-QR:

Recall the global sensitivity,

\sup_{D\sim D^{\prime}}\|\hat{\mu}_{D}-\hat{\mu}_{D^{\prime}}\|_{1,C}\leq\frac{2\tau}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{j}^{\eta_{r}-\frac{1}{2}}}{\lambda_{j}^{\eta_{r}}+\psi}.

For the summation term, observe that given
\eta>\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{\beta}

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{j}^{\eta_{r}-\frac{1}{2}}}{\lambda_{j}^{\eta_{r}}+\psi}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{C_{1}x^{\frac{\beta}{2}}}{C_{2}+x^{\eta\beta}}dx\cdot\psi^{-\frac{1}{\eta}\left(\frac{1}{\beta}+\frac{1}{2}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\left\{\int_{0}^{M}\frac{C_{1}x^{\frac{\beta}{2}}}{C_{2}+x^{\eta\beta}}dx+\int_{M}^{\infty}x^{\frac{\beta}{2}-\eta\beta}dx\right\}\cdot\psi^{-\frac{1}{\eta}\left(\frac{1}{\beta}+\frac{1}{2}\right)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\psi^{-\frac{1}{\eta}\left(\frac{1}{\beta}+\frac{1}{2}\right)}

where the first inequality is based on the Assumption
2
and change of variable. Therefore, for privacy error:

\displaystyle\E\|\tilde{\mu}-\hat{\mu}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Delta^{2}}{\epsilon^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}

\displaystyle\lesssim(n\epsilon)^{-2}\cdot\psi^{-\frac{2}{\eta}\left(\frac{1}{\beta}+\frac{1}{2}\right)}

For Statistical Error: The
n^{-1}
part comes from variance while for bias,

\|\E\hat{\mu}-\mu_{0}\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\psi}{\lambda_{j}^{\eta_{r}}+\psi}\right)^{2}\left\langle\mu_{0},\phi_{j}\right\rangle^{2}\leq\psi\|\mu_{0}\|_{C^{\eta_{r}}}^{2}\lesssim\psi,

where the last inequality is by assuming
\|\mu_{0}\|_{C^{\eta_{r}}}<\infty
. Combining privacy error and statistical error, one gets the desired results.
∎

B.3
Proof of Theorem
8

Proof.

Recall that the exact form of the kernel density estimator is

\hat{K}_{D}(x)=\frac{1}{n\sqrt{det(\mathbf{H})}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}K^{\eta}\left(\mathbf{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(x-x_{i})\right).

Then by the definition of global sensitivity,

\displaystyle\Delta=\sup_{D\sim D^{\prime}}\left\|\hat{K}_{D}-\hat{K}_{D^{\prime}}\right\|_{1,K}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n\sqrt{det(\mathbf{H})}}\left\|K^{\eta}(\mathbf{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}x_{n})-K^{\eta}(\mathbf{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}x_{n}^{{}^{\prime}})\right\|_{K^{\eta}}\sqrt{tr(K^{\eta-1})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n\sqrt{det(\mathbf{H})}}\sqrt{tr(K^{\eta-1})}\sqrt{2\left(K^{\eta}(0)-K^{\eta}(\mathbf{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(x_{n}-x_{n}^{{}^{\prime}}))\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2M_{K}}{n\sqrt{det(\mathbf{H})}}\sqrt{tr(K^{\eta-1})}.

The first inequality is based on the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, which is also used in deriving the ICLP-QR approach. The last inequality holds by the assumption that
K^{\eta}(\cdot,\cdot)
is pointwise bounded.

Turning to the utility, taking
\mathbf{H}
to be a diagonal matrix with same entry, then assumptions stated in the Theorem
8
and by the Theorem 6.28 in
Wasserman 2006
, the risk
R
satisfies

\displaystyle R

\displaystyle=\E\int_{T}\left(\tilde{f}_{D}(x)-f_{0}(x)\right)^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq 2*\left(\E\int_{T}\left(\tilde{f}_{D}(x)-\hat{f}_{D}(x)\right)^{2}dx+\E\int_{T}\left(\hat{f}_{D}(x)-f_{0}(x)\right)^{2}dx\right)

\displaystyle\leq O\left(\frac{c_{1}}{n^{2}h^{2d}}+h^{4}+\frac{c_{2}}{nh^{d}}\right).

for some constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
.
∎

B.4
Proof of Theorem
9

Proof.

Recall while deriving the ICLP-QR approach, for a given
\eta>1
s.t.
tr(C^{\eta-1})
is finite, we have

\|h\|_{1,C}\leq\left\|h\right\|_{C^{\eta}}\sqrt{\operatorname{trace}(C^{\eta-1})},

substituting
h
by
\hat{f}_{D}-\hat{f}_{D^{\prime}}
leads to

\|\hat{f}_{D}-\hat{f}_{D^{\prime}}\|_{1,C}\leq\left\|\hat{f}_{D}-\hat{f}_{D^{\prime}}\right\|_{C^{\eta}}\sqrt{\operatorname{trace}(C^{\eta-1})},

meaning that we need to found the upper bound for
\|\hat{f}_{D}-\hat{f}_{D^{\prime}}\|_{C^{\eta}}
. First, let
t\in[0,1]
,
\delta_{D^{\prime},D}=\hat{f}_{D^{\prime}}-\hat{f}_{D}
and
L_{D}(f)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}L_{d_{i},f}
. Notice that
\hat{f}_{D^{\prime}}
and
\hat{f}_{D}
are the minimizers of (
7
), we have

L_{D}\left(\hat{f}_{D}\right)+\psi\left\|\hat{f}_{D}\right\|_{C^{\eta}}^{2}\leq L_{D}\left(\hat{f}_{D}+t\delta_{D^{\prime},D}\right)+\psi\left\|\hat{f}_{D}+t\delta_{D^{\prime},D}\right\|_{C^{\eta}}^{2},

and

L_{D}\left(\hat{f}_{D^{\prime}}\right)+\psi\left\|\hat{f}_{D^{\prime}}\right\|_{C^{\eta}}^{2}\leq L_{D}\left(\hat{f}_{D^{\prime}}-t\delta_{D^{\prime},D}\right)+\psi\left\|\hat{f}_{D^{\prime}}-t\delta_{D^{\prime},D}\right\|_{C^{\eta}}^{2}.

Combining the two inequalities above,

\displaystyle L_{D}\left(\hat{f}_{D}\right)-

\displaystyle L_{D}\left(\hat{f}_{D}+t\delta_{D^{\prime},D}\right)+L_{D}\left(\hat{f}_{D^{\prime}}\right)-L_{D}\left(\hat{f}_{D^{\prime}}-t\delta_{D^{\prime},D}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\psi\left(\left\|\hat{f}_{D}+t\delta_{D^{\prime},D}\right\|_{C^{\eta}}^{2}-\left\|\hat{f}_{D}\right\|_{C^{\eta}}^{2}+\left\|\hat{f}_{D^{\prime}}-t\delta_{D^{\prime},D}\right\|_{C^{\eta}}^{2}-\left\|\hat{f}_{D^{\prime}}\right\|_{C^{\eta}}^{2}\right)

Then using the same proof techniques in Section 4.3 of
Hall et al. 2013
, we have

\|\hat{f}_{D}-\hat{f}_{D^{\prime}}\|_{C^{\eta}}\leq\frac{M}{\psi n}\sqrt{\sup_{x}C^{\eta}(x,x)},

which completes the proof.
∎

Appendix C
Appendix: Extension of Mean Function

In Section
4.2
, we only consider the mean function protection which looks rather limited. However, in this section, we will show that a lot of estimation problems can be boiled down to mean function estimation. Therefore, the mean function protection technique, analysis we derive in Section
4.2
can be applied to these problems too. In general, we consider the estimation and protection of (1) the covariance function and (2) the coefficient function in Function-on-Scalar linear regression.

C.1
Covariance Function

For a given sample
X_{1},\cdots,X_{n}\in\mathbb{H}
, our goal is to estimate its covariance function, i.e.

C(s,t)=\E\left(X(s)-\mu(s)\right)\left(X(t)-\mu(t)\right)

where
\mu
is the mean function and the empirical sample covariance function is

\bar{C}(s,t)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(X_{i}(s)-\bar{X}(s)\right)\left(X_{i}(t)-\bar{X}(t)\right).

Thus, estimating the covariance function can be also viewed as estimating the mean function of
(X_{i}(s)-\mu(s))(X_{i}(t)-\mu(t))
.

To obtain the qualified summary, one can also apply quadratic regularization. For the ICLP covariance function
K
, denote the tensor product space

\mathcal{H}_{K^{\eta}\otimes K^{\eta}}:=\mathcal{H}_{K^{\eta}}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{K^{\eta}},

and RKHS associated with reproducing kernel

K^{\eta}\otimes K^{\eta}((s_{1},t_{1}),(s_{2},t_{2}))=K^{\eta}(s_{1},s_{2})K^{\eta}(t_{1},t_{2}).

With slight abuse of notation, we let
\otimes
also denote the tensor product of elements in
\mathbb{H}
, and then ICLP with quadratic regularization can be expressed as

\hat{C}=\underset{C\in\mathcal{H}_{K^{\eta}\otimes K^{\eta}}}{\operatorname{argmin}}\left\{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|(X_{i}-\bar{X})\otimes(X_{i}-\bar{X})-C\right\|_{\mathbb{H}\otimes\mathbb{H}}^{2}+\psi\|C\|_{\mathcal{H}_{K^{\eta}\otimes K^{\eta}}}^{2}\right\}

which leads to

\hat{C}=\left(K^{\eta}\otimes K^{\eta}+\psi\mathbf{I}\right)^{-1}K^{\eta}\otimes K^{\eta}(\bar{C}).

Further assuming
\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
and
\{\phi_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
as the eigenvalue and eigenfunction of
K
, then

\hat{C}(s,t)=\sum_{j,l\geq 1}\frac{\lambda_{l}^{\eta}\lambda_{l}^{\eta}}{\lambda_{l}^{\eta}\lambda_{l}^{\eta}+\psi}\left\langle\bar{C},\phi_{j}\phi_{l}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{H}\otimes\mathbb{H}}\phi_{j}(s)\phi_{l}(t).

The expression of
\hat{C}
is analogous to the expression of
\hat{\mu}
under the quadratic regularization in Section
B.1
, therefore, similar global sensitivity and utility analysis can be applied to
\hat{C}
as well since sanitizing the covariance function is using the same ICLP via tensor basis, i.e.

X(s,t)=\sum_{j,l\geq 1}\sqrt{\lambda_{k}\lambda_{l}}Z_{kl}\phi_{k}(s)\phi_{l}(t)

where
Z_{k,l}
are i.i.d. Laplace random variables with mean
0
and variance
1
.

C.2
Function-on-Scalar Linear Regression

We consider the following Function-on-Scalar linear regression, i.e.

Y_{i}(t)=X_{i}^{T}\beta(t)+\epsilon_{i}(t),\quad\text{for}\quad i=1,\cdots,n

where
Y_{i}(t)
and
e_{i}(t)
are functional response and error that lies in Hilbert space
\mathbb{H}
, covariates
X_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
and coefficient function
\beta(t)\in\mathbb{H}^{\otimes p}
when
\mathbb{H}^{\otimes p}
denotes
p
-fold Cartesian product of
\mathbb{H}
. Estimating and sanitizing the coefficient functions
\beta(t)
is of primary interest.

One can obtain the estimation via the classical ordinary least square (OLS) technique, i.e.

\hat{\beta}=\underset{\beta\in\mathbb{H}^{\otimes p}}{\operatorname{argmin}}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|Y_{i}-X_{i}^{T}\beta\right\|_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}.

The OLS estimator is then

\hat{\beta}(t)=\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}X_{i}^{T}\right)^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}Y_{i}(t)X_{i}\right).

Noticing the functional components involved in
\hat{\beta}(t)
are
Y_{i}(t)
, thus estimating the coefficient function can be also viewed as estimating the mean function of
Y_{i}(t)X_{i}
and the applied the inverse matrix with scalar elements.

In classical simple linear regression, where both response and covariate are scalars, i.e.

y_{i}=x_{i}\beta+\epsilon_{i},\quad\text{for}\quad i=1,\cdots,n.

Let
\mathbf{x}:=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})
and
\mathbf{y}:=(y_{1},\cdots,y_{n})

To achieve
\epsilon
-DP for the OLS estimator, one typically sanitizes the empirical variance of
\mathbf{x}
and the empirical covariance between
\mathbf{x}
and
\mathbf{y}
, rather than sanitized the
\hat{\beta}
directly, see
Alabi et al. 2020
. Specifically, let
\bar{x}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}
,
\bar{y}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}
,
n\text{cov}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=\langle\mathbf{x}-\bar{x}\mathbf{1},\mathbf{y}-\bar{y}\mathbf{1}\rangle_{\ell^{2}}
, and
n\text{var}(\mathbf{x})=\langle\mathbf{x}-\bar{x}\mathbf{1},\mathbf{x}-\bar{x}\mathbf{1}\rangle_{\ell^{2}}
. Assuming the sensitivity of
n\text{cov}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
and
n\text{var}(\mathbf{x})
are both
1
without losing generality. Then the sanitized OLS estimator that achieves
\epsilon
-DP is

\tilde{\beta}=\frac{n\text{cov}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})+Z_{1}}{n\text{var}(\mathbf{x})+Z_{2}}

where
Z_{1}
and
Z_{2}
are independent random variables generated from
Lap(0,\frac{1}{\gamma\epsilon})
and
Lap(0,\frac{1}{(1-\gamma)\epsilon})
respectively with
\gamma\in(0,1)
.

Turning back to the Function-on-Scalar regression case, one can sanitize the statistic
T_{1}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}X_{i}^{T}
and
T_{2}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}Y_{i}(t)X_{i}
separately by spliting the privacy budget
\epsilon
in to
\gamma\epsilon
(for
T_{1}
) and
(1-\gamma)\epsilon
(for
T_{2}
). While
T_{1}
is a
p\times p
matrix with scalar elements, one can sanitize it with a classical multivariate privacy tool with budget
\gamma\epsilon
. For the statistic
T_{2}
, it is the mean function of
Y_{i}(t)X_{i}
and thus we can directly apply the mean function protection we develop in Section
4.2
.

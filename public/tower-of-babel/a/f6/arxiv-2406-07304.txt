Alexandrov-Fenchel inequalities for capillary hypersurfaces in hyperbolic space
Xinqun Mei, Liangjun Weng

Abstract
. In this article, we first introduce the quermassintegrals for compact hypersurfaces with capillary boundaries in hyperbolic space from a variational viewpoint, and then we solve an isoperimetric type problem in hyperbolic space. By constructing a new locally constrained inverse curvature flow, we obtain the Alexandrov-Fenchel inequalities for convex capillary hypersurfaces in hyperbolic space. This generalizes a theorem of Brendle-Guan-Li [ 11 ] for convex closed hypersurfaces in hyperbolic space.

Introduction

Let
\Omega
be a convex body in hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
with boundary
\partial\Omega
. The
k
-th quermassintegral of
\Omega
, denoted as
\mathcal{W}_{k}(\Omega)
, is defined as the volume of the set of totally geodesic
k
-dimensional subspaces that intersect
\Omega
(see e.g.
[
42
, Part IV]
or
[
46
]
). In particular,

(1.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{0}(\Omega)=|\Omega|,\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \mathcal{W}_{1}(\Omega)=\frac{1}{n+1}|\partial\Omega|.

If further assume that
\partial\Omega
is smooth (say at least
C^{2}
), then the quermassintegrals
\mathcal{W}_{k}(\Omega)
and the curvature integrals are related
(see e.g.
[
46
, Proposition 7]
) by

(1.2)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathcal{W}_{k+1}(\Omega)=\frac{1}{n+1}\int_{\partial\Omega}H_{k}dA-\frac{k}{n+2-k}\mathcal{W}_{k-1}(\Omega),\penalty\ 1\leq k\leq n-1.\end{aligned}

Here
H_{k}
is the normalized
k
-th mean curvature of
\partial\Omega\subset\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
, see Section
2.1
for precise definition. The above quermassintegrals possess a nice variational structure (see e.g.
[
50
, Proposition 3.1]
or
[
7
, Section 4]
):

(1.3)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{W}_{k}(\Omega_{t})=\frac{n+1-k}{n+1}\int_{\partial\Omega_{t}}fH_{k}dA_{t},\quad 0\leq k\leq n+1,

for any normal variation along
\partial\Omega_{t}
with the speed function
f
. Furthermore, the Alexandrov-Fenchel inequalities involving the quermassintegrals
\mathcal{W}_{k}(\Omega)
in hyperbolic space have attracted wide attention in recent decades, it states

(1.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{k}(\Omega)\geq f_{k}\circ f^{-1}_{l}\left(\mathcal{W}_{l}(\Omega)\right),\penalty\ 0\leq l<k\leq n,

where
f_{k}:[0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
is the monotone function defined by
f_{k}(\rho):=\mathcal{W}_{k}(B_{\rho})
, with
B_{\rho}
being the geodesic ball of radius
\rho
in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
, and
f_{l}^{-1}
being the inverse function of
f_{l}
. Moreover, the equality holds in (
1.4
) if and only if
\partial\Omega
is a geodesic sphere. In
[
50
]
, Wang-Xia studied a globally constrained quermassintegral preserving flow, given by one parameter family of embedded hypersurface
x(\cdot,t):M^{n}\times[0,T)\to\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
satisfying

(1.5)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}x=\left(\frac{\int_{\partial\Omega_{t}}H_{k}^{\frac{1}{k-l}}H_{l}^{1-\frac{1}{k-l}}dA_{t}}{\int_{\partial\Omega_{t}}H_{l}dA_{t}}-\left(\frac{H_{k}}{H_{l}}\right)^{\frac{1}{k-l}}\right)\nu,\quad 0\leq l<k\leq n.

where
\nu=\nu(\cdot,t)
is the unit outward normal of
x(\cdot,t)
. The flow (
1.5
) preserves
\mathcal{W}_{l}(\Omega_{t})
while decreases
\mathcal{W}_{k}(\Omega_{t})
, then they established the Alexandrov-Fenchel inequalities (
1.4
) for
h
-convex domain
\Omega
in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
(cf.
[
50
, Theorem 1.1]
).
Here a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
is referred to as
h
-convex if all the principal curvatures of its boundary
\partial\Omega
are greater or equal to
1
. In other words, the minimum value of
\mathcal{W}_{k}(\Omega)
among all the
h
-convex closed hypersurfaces
\partial\Omega
in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
with a fixed value
\mathcal{W}_{l}(\Omega)
is achieved by the geodesic sphere. This solves a natural isoperimetric type problem for closed hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
. In particular, when
k=1
and
l=0
, (
1.4
) reduces to the classical isoperimetric inequality in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
relating the area and volume, which was established by Schmidt in
[
45
]
.

On the other hand, the hyperbolic space can be viewed as a warped product space
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}=[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{S}^{n}
, equipped with the metric

\bar{g}=d\rho^{2}+\phi^{2}(\rho)\sigma,

where
\phi(\rho)=\sinh\rho
and
\sigma
is the standard spherical metric on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. Based on the Minkowski formula (see e.g. Guan-Li
[
23
, Proposition 2.5]
) for the closed hypersurface
M:=\partial\Omega\subset\mathbb{H}^{n+1}

(1.6)

\displaystyle\int_{M}(\phi^{\prime}H_{k-1}-vH_{k})dA=0,\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ 1\leq k\leq n,

where
\phi^{\prime}=\cosh\rho
and
v
is the support function of
M
as

v=\bar{g}(\phi(\rho)\partial_{\rho},\nu).

In
[
11
]
, Brendle-Guan-Li designed a locally constrained inverse curvature flow as

(1.7)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}x=\left(\frac{\phi^{\prime}}{F}-v\right)\nu,

where
F=\frac{H_{k}}{H_{k-1}}
. Along the flow (
1.7
), when the evolving hypersurfaces are
k
-convex, then
the
k
-th quermassintegral
\mathcal{W}_{k}(\Omega_{t})
is preserved and the
(k+1)
-th quermassintegral
\mathcal{W}_{k+1}(\Omega_{t})
is non-increasing with respect to the time
t\geq 0
. Here a smooth hypersurface
M\subset\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
is
k
-convex for some
1\leq k\leq n
means that its principal
curvatures
\kappa:=(\kappa_{1},\cdots,\kappa_{n})\in\Gamma_{k}
, see (
2.1
). A smooth hypersurface
M\subset\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
is called star-shaped if its support function
v
is positive everywhere on
M
. In
[
11
, Theorem 1.3]
, Brendle-Guan-Li established the long-time existence and convergence of flow (
1.7
) under two cases: either the initial closed hypersurface

is strictly convex and

or

is star-shaped,
k
-convex and satisfying a gradient bound condition. As a consequence, the inequalities (
1.4
) holds for

and

provided that
\partial\Omega
is convex. Recently, Hu-Li-Wei
[

obtained the long-time existence and convergence of flow (

is a

-convexity for all

, which also provided an alternative proof of the inequalities (

-convex domain

) hold for a domain under the weak geometric assumption, say for instance assuming

Based on the aforementioned results, a natural question arises regarding the corresponding geometric variational problem, specifically the
isoperimetric type problems
, for compact hypersurface with non-empty boundaries in hyperbolic space. This paper’s primary objective is to first introduce the quermassintegrals for capillary hypersurfaces supported on totally geodesic hyperplanes in hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
. Subsequently, we establish the Alexandrov-Fenchel inequalities (a higher-order isoperimetric type inequality) for these quantities in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
. To describe our results, we introduce some notations and definitions. We employ the Poincaré ball model to represent the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
, denoted by
\left(\mathbb{B}^{n+1},\bar{g}\right)
with

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}^{n+1}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}:|x|<1\},\quad\bar{g}=e^{2u}\delta,\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ e^{2u}:=\frac{4}{(1-|x|^{2})^{2}},

where
\delta
is the standard Euclidean metric. The supported totally geodesic hyperplane is given by

\mathcal{H}:=\{x\in\mathbb{B}^{n+1}:\delta(x,E_{n+1})=0\},

where
E_{n+1}=(0,\cdots,0,1)
is the
(n+1)
-th coordinate basis in
\mathbb{B}^{n+1}
. We further denote

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{+}:=\{x\in\mathbb{B}^{n+1}:\delta(x,E_{n+1})\geq 0\}.

Let
\Sigma
be an embedded hypersurface in
{\mathcal{H}^{+}}
satisfying

(1.8)

\displaystyle{\rm{int}}(\Sigma)\subset{\rm int}(\mathcal{H}^{+})\quad\text{and}\quad\partial\Sigma\subset\mathcal{H}.

We denote
\widehat{\Sigma}
as the bounded domain enclosed by
\Sigma
and the totally geodesic hyperplane
\mathcal{H}
in
\mathcal{H}^{+}

and
\widehat{\partial\Sigma}
as the bounded domain enclosed by
\partial\Sigma
inside
\mathcal{H}
, see Figure
1
. Without loss of generality, throughout this paper, we assume that the origin point
O\in\text{int}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma})
.

Definition 1.1
.

A compact hypersurface
\Sigma\subset{{\mathcal{H}}^{+}}
is called a capillary hypersurface if
it satisfies (
1.8
) and intersects with
\mathcal{H}
at a constant contact angle
\theta\in(0,\pi)
along
\partial\Sigma\cap\mathcal{H}
.

The simplest example of capillary hypersurface in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
is a family of geodesic spherical caps lying entirely in
{\mathcal{H}^{+}}
and intersecting with
\mathcal{H}
at a constant contact angle
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
, which is given by

(1.9)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\theta,r_{0}}:=\left\{x\in{\mathcal{H}^{+}}:|x+r_{0}\cos\theta E_{n+1}|=r_{0}\right\},\penalty\ \text{for}\penalty\ 0<r_{0}<\frac{1}{\sin\theta}.

It is clear that the constraint
0<r_{0}<\frac{1}{\sin\theta}
is a necessary and sufficient condition for
\mathcal{C}_{\theta,r_{0}}
lying in the unit ball.

Inspired by the recent progress about the capillary hypersurface in Euclidean space as
[
43
,
54
]
and
[
48
]
, we first introduce a family of new geometric quantities (Quermassintegrals)
\mathcal{A}_{k,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})
for capillary hypersurface
\Sigma
in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
in this paper.
Before that, we fix some notations for the
(n+1)
-dimensional convex body
\widehat{\Sigma}\subset\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
. Parallel to (
1.1
) and (
1.2
), we denote

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\mathcal{W}_{0}(\widehat{\Sigma}):=|\widehat{\Sigma}|,\quad\penalty\ \penalty\ \mathcal{W}_{1}(\widehat{\Sigma}):=\frac{1}{n+1}|\Sigma|,\\
&\mathcal{W}_{k+1}(\widehat{\Sigma})=\frac{1}{n+1}\int_{\Sigma}H_{k}dA-\frac{k}{n+2-k}\mathcal{W}_{k-1}(\widehat{\Sigma}),\penalty\ 1\leq k\leq n-1,\end{aligned}

where
H_{k}
is the normalized
k
-th mean curvature of
\Sigma\subset\mathcal{H}^{+}
. Similarly, we have the quermassintegrals for the
n
-dimensional convex body
\widehat{\partial\Sigma}\subset\mathcal{H}\subset\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
, which are defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{0}^{\mathcal{H}}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma}):=|\widehat{\partial\Sigma}|,\quad\quad\mathcal{W}_{1}^{\mathcal{H}}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma}):=\frac{1}{n}|\partial\Sigma|,

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{k+1}^{\mathcal{H}}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma})=\frac{1}{n}\int_{\partial\Sigma}H_{k}^{\partial\Sigma}ds-\frac{k}{n+1-k}\mathcal{W}^{\mathcal{H}}_{k-1}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma}),\penalty\ 1\leq k\leq n-2,

where
H_{k}^{\partial\Sigma}:=\frac{1}{\binom{n-1}{k}}\sigma^{\partial\Sigma}_{k}
is the normalized
k
-th mean curvature of
\partial\Sigma\subset\mathcal{H}
.

Now we are ready to introduce the quermassintegrals
\mathcal{A}_{k,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})
for capillary hypersurface
\Sigma
in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{0,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma}):=\mathcal{W}_{0}(\widehat{\Sigma}),\penalty\ \penalty\ \mathcal{A}_{1,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma}):=\mathcal{W}_{1}(\widehat{\Sigma})-\frac{\cos\theta}{n+1}\mathcal{W}_{0}^{\mathcal{H}}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma}),

and for
1\leq k\leq n-1
,

(1.10)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{k+1,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma}):=\mathcal{W}_{k+1}(\widehat{\Sigma})+\frac{\cos\theta}{n+1}\sum\limits_{l=0}^{[\frac{k}{2}]}(-1)^{l-1}(\sin\theta)^{k-2l}\cdot\mathcal{W}_{k-2l}^{\mathcal{H}}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma})\prod_{s=0}^{l-1}\frac{k-2s}{n-k+2(s+1)},\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\

where we have used the convention that
\prod\limits_{s=0}^{-1}\cdot=1
.

When
\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}
, it is easy to see that
\mathcal{A}_{k+1,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})=\mathcal{W}_{k+1}(\widehat{\Sigma})
for all
-1\leq k\leq n
, which make us to expect that
\mathcal{A}_{k+1,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})
would be the correct capillary counterpart of the quermassintegrals for the closed hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
.
Besides, the following first variational formula is the motivation for us to define
\mathcal{A}_{k,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})
as the quermassintegrals for capillary hypersurface in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Sigma_{t}\subset{{\mathcal{H}}^{+}}
be a family of smooth capillary hypersurfaces, given by embeddings
x(\cdot,t):M\rightarrow{{\mathcal{H}}^{+}}
and satisfying

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}x)^{\perp}=f\nu,

for some smooth function
f
. Then for
0\leq k\leq n
,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{A}_{k,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma_{t}})=\frac{n+1-k}{n+1}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}fH_{k}dA_{t}.

Furthermore, we establish the Alexandrov-Fenchel inequalities for the quermassintegrals
\mathcal{A}_{k,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})
, when
\Sigma
is a convex capillary hypersurface in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
and
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
.

Theorem 1.3
.

For
n\geq 2
, let
\Sigma\subset{{\mathcal{H}}^{+}}
be a convex capillary hypersurface with contact angle
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
. Assume that

(1.11)

there exists a geodesic spherical cap

\mathcal{C}_{\theta,r_{0}}
such that
\Sigma\subset\widehat{\mathcal{C}_{\theta,r_{0}}}
.

Then there holds

(1.12)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{n,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})\geq(f_{n,\theta}\circ f_{k,\theta}^{-1})\left(\mathcal{A}_{k,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})\right),\quad\forall\penalty\ 1\penalty\ \leq k\leq n-1,

where

is a strictly monotone function defined by

where
\mathcal{C}_{\theta,r}
is the geodesic spherical cap given by (

\Sigma
is a geodesic spherical cap.

In other words, the maximum value of

among all the convex capillary hypersurfaces

with a fixed value

\mathbb{H}^{n+1}

Furthermore, when

\Sigma
in
\mathbb{H}^{3}

2.
Quermassintegrals and first variational formula

2.1.
Elementary symmetric polynomial functions

In this subsection, we recall some well-known properties
of the
k
-th elementary symmetric functions.
Let
A=\{A_{ij}\}
be an
n\times n
symmetric matrix, and
k\in\{1,2,\cdots,n\}
, define

\displaystyle\sigma_{k}(A):=\sigma_{k}(\lambda(A))=\sum\limits_{1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}\cdots<i_{k}\leq n}\lambda_{i_{1}}\lambda_{i_{2}}\cdots\lambda_{i_{k}},

where
\lambda:=\lambda(A)=(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})
is the eigenvalues of
A
.
We use the convention that
\sigma_{0}=1
and
\sigma_{k}=0
for
k>n
. Let
H_{k}(A):=H_{k}(\lambda(A))
be the normalization of
\sigma_{k}(\lambda)
given by

H_{k}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{\binom{n}{k}}\sigma_{k}(\lambda).

Denote
\sigma_{k}(\lambda\left|i\right.)
the symmetric polynomial function of
\sigma_{k}(\lambda)
with
\lambda_{i}=0
and
\sigma_{k}(\lambda\left|ij\right.)
the symmetric polynomial function of
\sigma_{k}(\lambda)
with
\lambda_{i}=\lambda_{j}=0
. Recall that Gårding’s cone is defined as

(2.1)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{k}=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\sigma_{i}(\lambda)>0,\penalty\ \penalty\ \forall 1\leq i\leq k\}.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

(3)

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}{\lambda_{i}\sigma_{k-1}(\lambda|i)}=k\sigma_{k}(\lambda)

(4)

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}{\lambda_{i}^{2}\sigma_{k-1}(\lambda|i)}=\sigma_{1}(\lambda)\sigma_{k}(\lambda)-(k+1)\sigma_{k+1}(\lambda)

We denote

symmetric polynomial function of the matrix

3.1.
Scalar equation

In this subsection, we reduce the flow (
3.4
) to a scalar parabolic equation with oblique boundary value condition on
\bar{\mathbb{S}}^{n}_{+}
, if the evolving hypersurfaces are star-shaped.
Under the polar coordinate
(r,\zeta,\theta)\in[0,1)\times[0,\frac{\pi}{2}]\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, the standard Euclidean metric in
\mathbb{B}^{n+1}_{+}
has the following form

\displaystyle|dz|^{2}=dr^{2}+r^{2}\sigma=dr^{2}+r^{2}\left(d\zeta^{2}+\sin^{2}\zeta g_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\right),

where
\sigma
is the standard spherical metric on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
,
then the constant vector field
E_{n+1}
is given by

\displaystyle E_{n+1}=\cos\zeta\partial_{r}-\frac{\sin\zeta}{r}\partial_{\zeta}.

On the other hand, one can also view the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
as a warped product manifold
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{S}^{n}
equipped with the metric

\bar{g}=d\rho^{2}+\phi^{2}(\rho)\sigma,

where
\phi(\rho):=\sinh\rho
. We denote
r(x)
and
\rho(x)
as the geodesic distance from
x
to the origin in Euclidean space and hyperbolic space respectively. Then it follows (see e.g.
[
51
, Section 4.1]
)

\displaystyle V_{0}=\cosh\rho=\frac{1+r^{2}}{1-r^{2}},\quad\sinh\rho=\frac{2r}{1-r^{2}},

and

(3.7)

\displaystyle r=\frac{e^{\rho}-1}{e^{\rho}+1}.

The position vector
x
in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
(in polar coordinate) can be represented as

\displaystyle x=\sinh\rho\partial_{\rho}.

Using (
3.7
), the constant vector field
E_{n+1}
(in polar coordinate) is

(3.8)

\displaystyle E_{n+1}=\cos\zeta\partial_{r}-\frac{\sin\zeta}{r}\partial_{\zeta}=\frac{\cos\zeta(e^{\rho}+1)^{2}}{2e^{\rho}}\partial_{\rho}-\frac{\sin\zeta(e^{\rho}+1)}{e^{\rho}-1}\partial_{\zeta}.

Assuming that capillary hypersurface
\Sigma
is star-shaped to the origin in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
, then we can reparametrize
\Sigma
as a graph over
\bar{\mathbb{S}}^{n}_{+}
. Namely, there exists a positive function
\rho
defined on
\bar{\mathbb{S}}^{n}_{+}
, such that

\displaystyle\Sigma=\{\rho(\eta)\eta|\eta\in\bar{\mathbb{S}}^{n}_{+}\}.

Define a new function
\varphi:\bar{\mathbb{S}}^{n}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
by

\varphi(\eta):=\Phi(\rho(\eta)),

where

\displaystyle\frac{d\Phi(\rho)}{d\rho}=\frac{1}{\phi(\rho)}.

Let
\eta:=(\eta^{1},\cdots,\eta^{n})
be a local coordinate system of
\bar{\mathbb{S}}^{n}_{+}
, we write
\partial_{i}:=\partial_{\eta^{i}}
,

\varphi_{i}=\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{i}}\varphi
and
\varphi_{ij}=\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{i}}\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{j}}\varphi
, where
\overline{\nabla}
is the Levi-Civita connection on
\bar{\mathbb{S}}^{n}_{+}
. We denote
\sigma_{ij}=\sigma(\partial_{i},\partial_{j}),\varphi^{i}=\sigma^{ij}\varphi_{j}
, then the unit outward normal vector of
\Sigma
is given by

(3.9)

\displaystyle\nu=\frac{1}{\omega}\left(\partial_{\rho}-\frac{\varphi^{j}}{\phi}\partial_{j}\right),

where
\omega:=\sqrt{1+|\overline{\nabla}\varphi|^{2}}
.

Let
X_{i}
denote the vector
\partial_{i}+\rho_{i}\partial_{\rho}
, then
\{X_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
forms a basis of the tangent space of
\Sigma
. The induced metric of
\Sigma
can be represented as

\displaystyle g_{ij}=\bar{g}(X_{i},X_{j})=\phi^{2}\sigma_{ij}+\rho_{i}\rho_{j}=\phi^{2}(\sigma_{ij}+\varphi_{i}\varphi_{j}),

and its inverse is given by

g^{ij}=\frac{1}{\phi^{2}}\left(\sigma^{ij}-\frac{\varphi^{i}\varphi^{j}}{\omega^{2}}\right).

The second fundamental form of
\Sigma
is given by

\displaystyle h_{ij}=-\bar{g}(D_{X_{i}}X_{j},\nu)=-\frac{\phi}{\omega}\left(\varphi_{ij}-\phi^{{}^{\prime}}(\sigma_{ij}+\varphi_{i}\varphi_{j})\right),

and

\displaystyle h_{j}^{i}=g^{ik}h_{kj}=-\frac{1}{\omega\phi}\left(\widehat{\sigma}^{ik}\varphi_{kj}-\phi^{{}^{\prime}}(\rho)\delta_{ij}\right),

where
\widehat{\sigma}^{ik}:=\sigma^{ik}-\frac{\varphi^{i}\varphi^{k}}{\omega^{2}}
.
The support function of
\Sigma
is

\displaystyle\bar{g}(x,\nu)=\frac{\phi}{\omega}.

Combining (
3.7
), (
3.8
) and (
3.9
), we have

\displaystyle\bar{g}(E_{n+1},\nu)=\frac{\cos\zeta(e^{\rho}+1)^{2}}{2e^{\rho}\omega}+\frac{\sin\zeta(e^{\rho}+1)\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\zeta}}\rho}{\omega(e^{\rho}-1)},

and

\displaystyle\delta(x,E_{n+1})=r\cos\zeta=\frac{\cos\zeta(e^{\rho}-1)}{e^{\rho}+1}.

Therefore we obtain

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{V_{0}-\cos\theta\bar{g}(Y_{n+1},\nu)}{F}-\bar{g}(x,\nu)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{H_{n-1}}{H_{n}}\Bigg[\phi^{{}^{\prime}}-\frac{\cos\theta}{2}\left(1+\left(\frac{e^{\rho}-1}{e^{\rho}+1}\right)^{2}\right)\left(\frac{\cos\zeta(e^{\rho}+1)^{2}}{2e^{\rho}\omega}+\frac{\sin\zeta(e^{\rho}+1)\phi\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\zeta}}\varphi}{\omega(e^{\rho}-1)}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{\cos\theta\cos\zeta(e^{\rho}-1)}{e^{\rho}+1}\frac{\phi}{\omega}\Bigg]-\frac{\phi}{\omega}.

Along
\partial\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}
, i.e.
\zeta=\frac{\pi}{2}

\displaystyle\overline{N}\circ x=\frac{1}{\phi}\partial_{\zeta},

it follows that

\displaystyle-\cos\theta=\bar{g}(\nu,\overline{N}\circ x)=\bar{g}\left(\frac{1}{\omega}\left(\partial_{\rho}-\frac{\varphi^{j}}{\phi}\partial_{j}\right),\frac{1}{\phi}\partial_{\zeta}\right)=-\frac{\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\zeta}}\varphi}{\omega},

is equivalent to

\displaystyle\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\zeta}}\varphi=\cos\theta\sqrt{1+|\overline{\nabla}\varphi|^{2}}.

In summary, we can transform the flow (
3.4

into the following scalar parabolic flow on

with an oblique boundary value condition

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}&\partial_{t}\varphi=\frac{\omega}{\phi}f:=G(\overline{\nabla}^{2}\varphi,\overline{\nabla}\varphi,\rho,\zeta),&\hbox{ in }\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}\times[0,T),\\

&\varphi(\cdot,0)=\varphi_{0}(\cdot),&\text{ on }\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n}.\end{array}\right.

Note that

(3.11)

\displaystyle\bar{g}(Y_{n+1},Y_{n+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(1+|x|^{2})^{2}-4[\delta(x,E_{n+1})]^{2}}{(1-|x|^{2})^{2}},

Therefore, the scalar flow (

) follows from the standard parabolic theory.

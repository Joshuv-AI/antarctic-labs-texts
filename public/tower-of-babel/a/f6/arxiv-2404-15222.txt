Local well-posedness for a novel nonlocal model for cell-cell adhesion via receptor binding
Mabel Lizzy Rajendran, Anna Zhigun

Abstract
Local well-posedness is established for a highly nonlocal nonlinear diffusion-adhesion system for bounded initial values with small support. Macroscopic systems of this kind were previously obtained by the authors through upscaling in [ 33 ] and can account for the effect of microscopic receptor binding dynamics in cell-cell adhesion. The system analysed here couples an integro-PDE featuring degenerate diffusion of the porous media type and nonlocal adhesion with a novel nonlinear integral equation. The approach is based on decoupling the system and using Banach’s fixed point theorem to solve each of the two equations individually and subsequently the entire system. The main challenge of the implementation lies in selecting a suitable framework. One of the key results is the local well-posedness for the integral equation with a Radon measure as a parameter. The analysis of this equation utilizes the Kantorovich-Rubinstein norm, marking the first application of this norm in handling a nonlinear integral equation. Keywords : aggregation, degenerate diffusion, integro-PDE, Kantorovich-Rubinstein norm, nonlinear integral equation, nonlocal cell-cell adhesion, Wasserstein metric MSC 2020: 35K65 35Q92 45G10 45K05 47G10 92C17

Radon measures and the Kantorovich-Rubinstein norm

Let

We denote by

is a non-separable Banach space. In particular, singular measures, such as point masses, cannot be obtained as limits of Lebesgue integrable functions in this norm. This issue is relevant in our case, see the discussion in
Section

2
(IE2)
. To counteract it, we also use the Kantorovich-Rubinstein (KR) norm associated with the Euclidean metric on

. Since

is compact, this norm is well-defined on the subspace

{\mathcal{M}}_{KR}(\overline{\Omega}):=\{\mu\in{\mathcal{M}}(\overline{\Omega}):\ \mu(\overline{\Omega})=0\}.

By the KR duality theorem
[
22
, Chapter VIII, §4.5, Theorem 1]
, this norm is given by

\displaystyle\|\mu\|_{{\mathcal{M}}_{KR}(\overline{\Omega})}:=\sup\left\{\int_{\overline{\Omega}}\varphi\,d\mu(y):\quad\varphi\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)\text{ and }\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq 1\right\}\quad\text{for all }\mu\in{\mathcal{M}}_{KR}(\overline{\Omega}).

(3.1)

It is strictly weaker than the total variation and satisfies

\displaystyle\|\mu\|_{{\mathcal{M}}_{KR}(\overline{\Omega})}\leq\frac{\operatorname{diam}(\Omega)}{2}|\mu|\qquad\text{for all }\mu\in{\mathcal{M}}_{KR}(\overline{\Omega}).

(3.2)

This norm gives rise to the Wasserstein
1
-distance on the set of probability measures
[
30
]
or, more generally, on the set of positive measures that have the same total variation. Moreover, when restricted to an arbitrary closed ball in the variational norm, the convergence with respect to the Wasserstein metric is equivalent to the weak-
*

[
22
, Chapter VIII, §4.6, Theorem 3]
. As a result, this metric turns such a ball into a metric space which is compact and, hence, separable.
We refer, e.g. to
[
22
, Chapter VIII, §4]
for further details.

For

Borel and bounded and

\displaystyle w\mu(B):=\int_{B}w\,d\mu\quad\text{for any Borel set }B\subset\overline{\Omega}.

It is well-known that

, and for any bounded Borel

it holds that

\displaystyle\int_{\overline{\Omega}}f\,d(w\mu)=\int_{\overline{\Omega}}fw\,d\mu.

\displaystyle(\partial_{w}{\mathcal{Y}}(\mu_{0},w_{0})(\cdot))h\qquad{\text{in }\overline{B_{\rho}}}\qquad\text{for }h\in W^{1,\infty}(B_{\rho})

\displaystyle\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in({\mathcal{M}}^{+}(\overline{B_{\rho}}))\backslash\{0\}\qquad\text{such that }|\mu_{1}|=|\mu_{2}|=|\mu_{0}|,

\displaystyle h\in W^{1,\infty}(B_{\rho})

\displaystyle\left\|(\partial_{w}{\mathcal{Y}}(\mu_{1},w_{1})-\partial_{w}{\mathcal{Y}}(\mu_{2},w_{2}))h\right\|_{{W^{1,\infty}(B_{\rho})}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Cr{CCro}|\mu_{0}|^{-1}\left(1+\|\nabla w_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\rho})}\right)\|h\|_{W^{1,\infty}(B_{\rho})}\|\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}\|_{{\mathcal{M}}_{KR}(\overline{B_{\rho}})}+\Cr{CCro}\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\rho})}\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{W^{1,\infty}(B_{\rho})}.

(5.19)

Proof.

Step 1
.

Let

be such that
\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\rho})}<\min\{\min w_{0},1-\max w_{0}\}
. Thanks to

, function

is two times continuously differentiable on

.
By Taylor’s theorem for real functions, we then have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Y}}(\mu_{0},w_{0}+h)(x)-{\mathcal{Y}}(\mu_{0},w_{0})(x)-(\partial_{w}{\mathcal{Y}}(\mu_{0},w_{0})(x))h

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Y}}(\mu_{0},w_{0}+h)(x)-{\mathcal{Y}}(\mu_{0},w_{0})(x)-\frac{d}{ds}{\mathcal{Y}}(\mu_{0},w_{0}+sh)(x)|_{s=0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)\frac{d^{2}}{ds^{2}}{\mathcal{Y}}(\mu_{0},w_{0}+sh)(x)\,ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)(\partial_{w}(\partial_{w}{\mathcal{Y}}(\mu_{0},w_{0}+sh)(x))h)h\,ds.

(5.20)

Using
5.6c
, we compute the integrand on the right-hand side of
5.20

\displaystyle(1-s)(\partial_{w}(\partial_{w}{\mathcal{Y}}(\mu_{0},w_{0}+sh)(x))h)h

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-s)D^{2}\psi({\mathcal{G}}^{+}((1-{(w_{0}+sh)})\mu_{0}),{\mathcal{G}}^{-}((w_{0}+sh)\mu_{0}))(-{\mathcal{G}}^{+}(h\mu_{0}),{\mathcal{G}}^{-}(h\mu_{0}))^{T}

\displaystyle\cdot(-{\mathcal{G}}^{+}(h\mu_{0}),{\mathcal{G}}^{-}(h\mu_{0}))^{T}(x).

(5.21)

Exploiting the regularity of
\psi
and the linearity and regularity of
{\mathcal{G}}^{\pm}
, we conclude that

5.21
and its spatial gradient are continuous functions of

and

5.20
is the integral of the gradient of the integrand. Together with
5.15c
and
5.20
this implies

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathcal{Y}}(\mu_{0},w_{0}+h)-{\mathcal{Y}}(\mu_{0},w_{0})-(\partial_{w}{\mathcal{Y}}(\mu_{0},w_{0})(\cdot))h\right\|_{{W^{1,\infty}(B_{\rho})}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)(\partial_{w}(\partial_{w}{\mathcal{Y}}(\mu_{0},w_{0}+sh)(\cdot))h)h\,ds\right\|_{{W^{1,\infty}(B_{\rho})}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)\left\|(\partial_{w}(\partial_{w}{\mathcal{Y}}(\mu_{0},w_{0}+sh)(\cdot))h)h\right\|_{{W^{1,\infty}(B_{\rho})}}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)\Cr{CCro}\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\rho})}^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Cr{CCro}\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\rho})}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle o\left(\|h\|_{W^{1,\infty}(B_{\rho})}\right).

(5.22)

From
5.6a

with respect to

in

shows that

and

and

Step 2
.

Finally, we derive estimate

as provided by

and once again Taylor’s theorem for real functions, we compute for every

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\partial_{w}{\mathcal{Y}}(\mu_{1},w_{1}){(x)}-\partial_{w}{\mathcal{Y}}(\mu_{2},w_{1}){(x)})h+(\partial_{w}{\mathcal{Y}}(\mu_{2},w_{1}){(x)}-\partial_{w}{\mathcal{Y}}(\mu_{2},w_{2}){(x)})h

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}(\partial_{\mu}(\partial_{w}{\mathcal{Y}}(\mu(s),w_{1}){(x)})h){k_{\mu}}\,ds+\int_{0}^{1}(\partial_{w}(\partial_{w}{\mathcal{Y}}(\mu_{2},w(s)){(x)})h)h_{w}\,ds,

(5.23)

where

\displaystyle h_{w}=w_{1}-w_{2},

\displaystyle\qquad{k_{\mu}}=\mu_{1}-\mu_{2},

\displaystyle w(s)=(1-s)w_{1}+sw_{2},

\displaystyle\qquad\mu(s)=(1-s)\mu_{1}+s\mu_{2}.

Combining

with
5.15c
and

\displaystyle\left\|(\partial_{w}{\mathcal{Y}}(\mu_{1},w_{1})(\cdot)-\partial_{w}{\mathcal{Y}}(\mu_{2},w_{2})(\cdot))h\right\|_{{W^{1,\infty}(B_{\rho})}}

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{1}(\partial_{\mu}(\partial_{w}{\mathcal{Y}}(\mu(s),w_{1})(\cdot))h){k_{\mu}}\,ds\right\|_{{W^{1,\infty}(B_{\rho})}}+\left\|\int_{0}^{1}(\partial_{w}(\partial_{w}{\mathcal{Y}}(\mu_{2},w(s))(\cdot))h)h_{w}\,ds\right\|_{{W^{1,\infty}(B_{\rho})}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\Cr{CCro}|\mu(s)|^{-1}\left(1+\|\nabla w_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\rho})}\right)\|h\|_{W^{1,\infty}(B_{\rho})}\|{k_{\mu}}\|_{{\mathcal{M}}_{KR}(\overline{B_{\rho}})}{\,ds}+\Cr{CCro}\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\rho})}\|h_{w}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\rho})}

Length minima for an infinite family of filling closed curves on a one-holed torus
Zhongzi Wang, Ying Zhang

Abstract
. We explicitly find the minima as well as the minimum points of the geodesic length functions for the family of filling (hence non-simple) closed curves, a 2 ​ b n a^{2}b^{n} ( n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 ), on a complete one-holed hyperbolic torus in its relative Teichmüller space, where a , b a,b are simple closed curves on the one-holed torus which intersect exactly once transversely. This provides concrete examples for the problem to minimize the geodesic length of a fixed filling closed curve on a complete hyperbolic surface of finite type in its relative Teichmüller space.



1.
Introduction

The length of any non-simple closed geodesic on an orientable, complete hyperbolic surface is known to have a positive lower bound (see Yamada
[
11
]
, also Hempel
[
7
]
). In
[
1
]
and
[
2
]
Basmajian obtained lower bounds for the length of a non-simple closed geodesic in terms of its self-intersection numbers.
In
[
9
]
Shen–Wang optimized the asymptotic multiplier in Basmajian’s lower bound. In a slightly different direction,
Erlandsson–Parlier
[
8
]
initiated the study of the self-intersection number of the shortest non-simple closed geodesics with at least
k
self-intersections. Advancing further from
[
10
]
, Basmajian–Parlier–Vo
[
3
]
showed that, for
k\geq 10^{125}
, the shortest among all non-simple closed geodesics with at least
k
self-intersections on any orientable, complete hyperbolic surface
S
is uniquely realized by a corkscrew geodesic with exactly
k
self-intersections on an ideal pair of pants (a corkscrew geodesics is that represented by word
ab^{k}
in the fundamental group of the pair of pants where
a
and
b
are simple closed curves each surrounding a different cusp).

Let
S
be an orientable surface of finite type equipped with a complete hyperbolic structure.
Then
S
has a finite number (possible none) of holes and cusps.
Surrounding each hole of
S
there is a unique simple closed geodesic which we call the neck geodesic of the hole.
Though there is no simple closed geodesics surrounding a cusp, we shall still say that a cusp has a null neck geodesic of length zero at infinity.

It is a well-known fact that a homotopically non-trivial, non-peripheral closed curve
c
on a complete hyperbolic surface
S
can be realized as a unique closed geodesic which might not be primitive (see, for example, Buser
[
4
, Theorem 1.6.6]
); we denote its geodesic length by
L_{c}
.
By definition, the relative Teichmüller space of
S
consists of all the marked complete hyperbolic structures on the same topological surface with all the lengths of the neck geodesics fixed.

We address the problem to minimize the geodesic length of a fixed filling (hence non-simple) closed curve on a finite type surface in the relative Teichmüller space of the surface. It will be shown in a future paper that such a geodesic length function has exactly one minimum point in the relative Teichmüller space.

In this paper, we solve the problem for a concrete infinite family of filling closed curves,
a^{2}b^{n}
(
n\geq 3
), on a complete one-holed hyperbolic torus
S_{1,1}
, where
a,b
are simple closed curves on
S_{1,1}
which intersect once transversely.
Precisely, we find the minima and the corresponding minimum points for the geodesic lengths of the filling closed curves,
a^{2}b^{n}
(
n\geq 3
), on
S_{1,1}
in its relative Teichmüller space (see Theorems
4.1
and
4.2
).

Note that the closed curve
a^{2}b^{n}
(
n\geq 3
) on
S_{1,1}
has self-intersection number
n-1
(see
[
5
, Proposition 6.1]
). Figure
1
shows
a^{2}b^{4}
drawn on a cut-open torus.

) gives

(11)

\displaystyle x_{m}^{2}+y^{2}+x_{m+1}^{2}-x_{m}yx_{m+1}=\mu.

By (
8
), we have
(x_{m}y-2x_{m+1})^{2}=x_{m}^{2}(y^{2}-4)-4(y^{2}-\mu)
, and hence

(12)

\displaystyle x_{m}^{2}=\frac{(x_{m-1}-x_{m+1})^{2}+4(y^{2}-\mu)}{y^{2}-4}.

By (
9
), we have
x_{m}x_{m+1}(y-2)=(x_{m}-x_{m+1})^{2}+y^{2}-\mu
,
and hence

(13)

x_{m}x_{m+1}=\frac{(x_{m}-x_{m+1})^{2}+y^{2}-\mu}{y-2}.

Combining
x_{m-2}+x_{m}=yx_{m-1}
and
x_{m-1}^{2}+y^{2}+x_{m}^{2}-x_{m-1}yx_{m}=\mu
gives

(14)

x_{m-2}x_{m}-x_{m-1}^{2}=y^{2}-\mu.

Definition 2.2
.

For each
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, we define a polynomial
P_{n}
in
x,y,z
as follows:

1) If
n=2m
is even, define

(15)

\displaystyle P_{2m}=-\,{\rm tr}(XY^{m}X^{-1}Y^{-m});

2) If
n=2m+1
is odd, define

(16)

\displaystyle P_{2m+1}=-\,{\rm tr}(XY^{m}X^{-1}Y^{-m-1})=-\,{\rm tr}(XY^{m+1}X^{-1}Y^{-m}).

In particular, we have
P_{0}=-2
and
P_{1}=-y
.

The polynomials
P_{n}
can be characterized by recursive relations as follows.

Proposition 2.3
.

The polynomials
P_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
in
x,y,z
satisfy recursive relations

(17)

\displaystyle P_{2m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle yP_{2m-1}-P_{2m-2}+y^{2}-\mu,

(18)

\displaystyle P_{2m+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle yP_{2m}-P_{2m-1},

with
P_{0}=-2
and
P_{1}=-y
.

Proof.

It is easy to see that

(19)

\displaystyle P_{0}=-\,{\rm tr}(XY^{0}X^{-1}Y^{0})=-2,

(20)

\displaystyle P_{1}=-\,{\rm tr}(XY^{0}X^{-1}Y^{-1})=-y.

We first prove (
17
). As

(21)

\displaystyle P_{2m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,{\rm tr}(XY^{m}X^{-1}Y^{-m+1}Y^{-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,{\rm tr}(XY^{m}X^{-1}Y^{-m+1})\,{\rm tr}(Y^{-1})+{\rm tr}(XY^{m}X^{-1}Y^{-m+2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle yP_{2m-1}+{\rm tr}(XY^{m}X^{-1}Y^{-m+2}),

it remains to show that

(22)

\displaystyle{\rm tr}(XY^{m}X^{-1}Y^{-m+2})-{\rm tr}(XY^{m-1}X^{-1}Y^{-m+1})=y^{2}-\mu.

By Fricke–Klein trace identity (
1
), we have

(23)

\displaystyle{\rm tr}(XY^{m}X^{-1}Y^{-m+2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm tr}(XY^{m})\,{\rm tr}(X^{-1}Y^{-m+2})-{\rm tr}(XY^{2m-2}X),

(24)

\displaystyle{\rm tr}(XY^{m-1}X^{-1}Y^{-m+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm tr}(XY^{m-1})\,{\rm tr}(X^{-1}Y^{-m+1})-{\rm tr}(XY^{2m-2}X).

Hence

(25)

\displaystyle{\rm tr}(XY^{m}X^{-1}Y^{-m+2})-{\rm tr}(XY^{m-1}X^{-1}Y^{-m+1})=x_{m}x_{m-2}-x_{m-1}^{2}=y^{2}-\mu.

This proves (
17
). For (
18
), we have

(26)

\displaystyle P_{2m+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,{\rm tr}(XY^{m}X^{-1}Y^{-m}Y^{-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,{\rm tr}(XY^{m}X^{-1}Y^{-m})\,{\rm tr}(Y^{-1})+{\rm tr}(XY^{m}X^{-1}Y^{-m+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle yP_{2m}-P_{2m-1}.

This finishes the proof of Proposition
2.3
.
∎

It follows from Proposition
2.3
that
P_{n}
can be rewritten as a polynomial in
y
and
\mu
.

Proposition 2.4
.

There exist univariate polynomials
q_{n}
,
n\geq 2
such that

(27)

\displaystyle P_{2m}-P_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(4-\mu)\,q_{2m}(y-2),

(28)

\displaystyle P_{2m+1}-P_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(4-\mu)\,q_{2m+1}(y-2).

Precisely,
q_{2}(t)=1
,
q_{3}(t)=t+2
, and
q_{n}
satisfies the following recursive relations:

(29)

\displaystyle q_{2m}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(t+2)\,q_{2m-1}(t)-q_{2m-2}(t)+1,

(30)

\displaystyle q_{2m+1}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(t+2)\,q_{2m}(t)-q_{2m-1}(t).

Furthermore, the coefficients of
q_{n}
for
n\geq 2
are all positive integers.

Proof.

Applying Proposition
2.3
and by induction, we have

\displaystyle P_{2m}-P_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle y(P_{2m-1}-P_{1})-(P_{2m-2}-P_{0})+4-\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle y(4-\mu)q_{2m-1}(y-2)-(4-\mu)q_{2m-2}(y-2)+4-\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(4-\mu)\big[yq_{2m-1}(y-2)-q_{2m-2}(y-2)+1\big],

and

\displaystyle P_{2m+1}-P_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle y(P_{2m}-P_{0})-(P_{2m-1}-P_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle y(4-\mu)q_{2m}(y-2)-(4-\mu)q_{2m-1}(y-2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(4-\mu)\big[yq_{2m}(y-2)-q_{2m-1}(y-2)\big],

This proves all the formulas in Proposition
2.4
.

It remains to prove that the coefficients of
q_{n}
for
n\geq 2
are positive integers.
Actually, it is easily shown by induction that, for
n\geq 2
, the coefficients of
q_{n}-q_{n-1}
are all positive integers.
This finishes the proof of Proposition
2.4
.
∎

Now we are in a position to obtain expressions for
{\rm tr}(X^{2}Y^{n})
,
n\geq 3
,
which will be used in §
4
to minimize the geodesic length of
a^{2}b^{n}
.

Proposition 2.5
.

For
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

(31)

\displaystyle{\rm tr}(X^{2}Y^{2m})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(x_{m-1}-x_{m+1})^{2}}{y^{2}-4}+2+(4-\mu)\big[4(y^{2}-4)^{-1}+q_{2m}(y-2)],

(32)

\displaystyle{\rm tr}(X^{2}Y^{2m+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(x_{m}-x_{m+1})^{2}}{y-2}+2+(4-\mu)\big[(y-2)^{-1}+q_{2m+1}(y-2)].

Proof.

We apply (
1
), (
12
), (
13
), (
27
) and (
28
)
to obtain (
31
) and (
32
) as follows:

\displaystyle{\rm tr}(X^{2}Y^{2m})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm tr}(XY^{m}Y^{m}X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm tr}(XY^{m})\,{\rm tr}(Y^{m}X)-{\rm tr}(XY^{m}X^{-1}Y^{-m})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x_{m}^{2}+P_{2m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(x_{m-1}-x_{m+1})^{2}}{y^{2}-4}+2+(4-\mu)\big[4(y^{2}-4)^{-1}+q_{2m}(y-2)],

\displaystyle{\rm tr}(X^{2}Y^{2m+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm tr}(XY^{m}Y^{m+1}X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm tr}(XY^{m})\,{\rm tr}(Y^{m+1}X)-{\rm tr}(XY^{m}X^{-1}Y^{-m-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x_{m}x_{m+1}+P_{2m+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(x_{m}-x_{m+1})^{2}}{y-2}+2+(4-\mu)\big[(y-2)^{-1}+q_{2m+1}(y-2)].

This proves Proposition
2.5
.
∎

3.
Geometry of a complete one-holed hyperbolic torus

Let
S_{1,1}
be a one-holed torus, and let
a,b
be two simple closed curves on
S_{1,1}
which intersect once transversely.
The commutator
aba^{-1}b^{-1}
then represents the simple closed curve
\partial
surrounding the hole.

We shall use the upper-half complex plane model
H^{2}
of the hyperbolic plane.
An orientation-preserving isometry of
H^{2}
is then given by a fractional linear transformation mapping the upper-half complex plane onto itself.
As a consequence, the group of orientation-preserving isometries of
H^{2}
coincides with
{\rm PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
.

A complete hyperbolic structure on
S_{1,1}
gives rise to a holonomy representation

(33)

\eta:\pi_{1}(S_{1,1})\rightarrow{\rm PSL}(2,\mathbb{R}),

which is unique up to simultaneous conjugation by elements in
{\rm PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
.

As the axes of
\eta(a)
and
\eta(b)
cross each other, a lifted representation

(34)

\rho:\pi_{1}(S_{1,1})\rightarrow{\rm SL}(2,\mathbb{R})

of
\eta
can be chosen so that
{\rm tr}\,\rho(a)>2
,
{\rm tr}\,\rho(b)>2
,
{\rm tr}\,\rho(ab)>2
.
We write

(35)

x={\rm tr}\,\rho(a),\quad y={\rm tr}\,\rho(b),\quad z={\rm tr}\,\rho(ab).

Then

(36)

x=2\cosh\frac{L_{a}}{2},\quad y=2\cosh\frac{L_{b}}{2},\quad z=2\cosh\frac{L_{ab}}{2}.

Using the fact that the axes of
\eta(a)
and
\eta(b)
cross each other, direct calculations show that

{\rm tr}\,\rho(aba^{-1}b^{-1})<2
, and hence
{\rm tr}\,\rho(aba^{-1}b^{-1})\leq-2
.
It follows that

(37)

{\rm tr}\,\rho(aba^{-1}b^{-1})=-2\cosh\frac{L_{\partial}}{2}.

On the other hand, by (
2
), we have
{\rm tr}\,\rho(aba^{-1}b^{-1})=x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}-xyz-2
.
Write
\mu=2+{\rm tr}\,\rho(aba^{-1}b^{-1})
. Then
\mu=2-2\cosh\frac{L_{\partial}}{2}\leq 0
and

(38)

x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}-xyz=\mu.

The relative Teichmüller space
{\rm Teich}_{1,1}(L_{\partial})
of all the marked complete hyperbolic structures on
S_{1,1}
with fixed length
L_{\partial}\geq 0
of neck geodesic is then parametrized as

(39)

\displaystyle{\rm Teich}^{\rm length}_{1,1}(L_{\partial})=\{(L_{a},L_{b},L_{ab})\in(\mathbb{R}_{>0})^{3}\mid x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}-xyz=\mu\},

where
x={\rm tr}\,\rho(a)
,
y={\rm tr}\,\rho(b)
,
z={\rm tr}\,\rho(ab)
and
\mu=2-2\cosh\frac{L_{\partial}}{2}\leq 0
.

The simple closed geodesics representing the simple closed curves
a
,
b
and
ab
intersect pairwise at the so-called
Weierstrass points
and thus form a hyperbolic triangle (called the
Weierstrass triangle
) of side-lengths
\frac{1}{2}L_{a}
,
\frac{1}{2}L_{b}
and
\frac{1}{2}L_{ab}
.

The following proposition concerns the geometric nature of
L_{\partial}
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
S_{1,1}
be a complete one-holed hyperbolic torus and let
a,b
be simple closed geodesics on
S_{1,1}
which intersect once transversely. Then

(40)

\sinh\frac{L_{a}}{2}\sinh\frac{L_{b}}{2}\sin\theta=\cosh\frac{L_{\partial}}{4},

where
\theta\in(0,\pi)
is the interior angle of the associated Weierstrass triangle contained by the two sides corresponding to
a
and
b
.
The constraint (
40
) is equivalent to

(41)

\sinh H_{b}\sinh\frac{L_{b}}{2}=\cosh\frac{L_{\partial}}{4},

where
H_{b}
is the length of the altitude perpendicular to the side of length
\frac{1}{2}L_{b}
.

Proof.

By the Laws of Cosines for the Weierstrass triangle, we have

(42)

\cosh\frac{L_{ab}}{2}=\cosh\frac{L_{a}}{2}\cosh\frac{L_{b}}{2}-\sinh\frac{L_{a}}{2}\sinh\frac{L_{b}}{2}\cos\theta.

The desired identity (
40
) follows by direct calculations as follows:

\displaystyle\hskip-12.0pt\sinh\frac{L_{a}}{2}\sinh\frac{L_{b}}{2}\sin\theta

\displaystyle\!=\!

\displaystyle\sqrt{\left(\sinh\frac{L_{a}}{2}\sinh\frac{L_{b}}{2}\right)^{2}-\left(\cosh\frac{L_{a}}{2}\cosh\frac{L_{b}}{2}-\cosh\frac{L_{ab}}{2}\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle\!=\!

\displaystyle\sqrt{1+2\cosh\frac{L_{a}}{2}\cosh\frac{L_{a}}{2}\cosh\frac{L_{ab}}{2}-\cosh^{2}\frac{L_{a}}{2}-\cosh^{2}\frac{L_{b}}{2}-\cosh^{2}\frac{L_{ab}}{2}}

\displaystyle\!=\!

\displaystyle\sqrt{1-\frac{\mu}{4}}

\displaystyle\!=\!

\displaystyle\cosh\frac{L_{\partial}}{4}.

By the Law of Sines for a right-angled triangle,

(43)

\sinh H_{b}=\sinh\frac{L_{a}}{2}\sin\theta.

Hence the constraint (
40
) can be replaced by (
41
).
This proves Proposition
3.1
.
∎

4.
Length minima of closed curves
a^{2}b^{n}
on one-holed tori

In this section we solve the problem of minimizing the geodesic length of the filling closed curve
a^{2}b^{n}
(
n\geq 3
) in the relative Teichmüller space
{\rm Teich}_{1,1}(L_{\partial})
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
a,b
be simple closed curves on a one-holed torus
S_{1,1}
which intersect once transversely.
Given integer
n\geq 3
and length
L_{\partial}\geq 0
of the neck geodesic of
S_{1,1}
, the geodesic length function
L_{a^{2}b^{n}}
has exactly one minimum point
(L^{*}_{a},L^{*}_{b},L^{*}_{ab})\in{\rm Teich}^{\rm length}_{1,1}(L_{\partial})
, where
L^{*}_{b}
is the unique positive solution to the equation

(44)

\frac{n}{2}\tanh\frac{nL^{*}_{b}}{4}\tanh\frac{L^{*}_{b}}{2}=1

(hence
L^{*}_{b}
is independent of
L_{\partial}
), and the minimum value
L^{\rm min}_{a^{2}b^{n}}
is determined by

(45)

\sinh\frac{L^{\rm min}_{a^{2}b^{n}}}{4}=\frac{n}{2}\cosh\frac{L_{\partial}}{4}\sinh\frac{nL^{*}_{b}}{4}\Big/\cosh\frac{L^{*}_{b}}{2}.

Proof.

Given a complete hyperbolic structure on
S_{1,1}
, we may choose a lifted holonomy representation

\rho:\pi_{1}(S_{1,1})\rightarrow{\rm SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
so that

{\rm tr}\,\rho(a)>2
,
{\rm tr}\,\rho(b)>2
and
{\rm tr}\,\rho(ab)>2
.

We write
X=\rho(a)
and
Y=\rho(b)
so that the calculations done in §
2
can used directly with the notations therein.

By formulas (
31
) and (
32
) for
{\rm tr}(X^{2}Y^{n})
in §
2
, if
(L_{a},L_{b},L_{ab})\in{\rm Teich}^{\rm length}_{1,1}(L_{\partial})
is the minimum point of the length function
L_{a^{2}b^{n}}
,
n\geq 3
, there must hold that

{\rm tr}(XY^{m})={\rm tr}(XY^{m+1})
if
n=2m+1
, or
{\rm tr}(XY^{m-1})={\rm tr}(XY^{m+1})
if
n=2m
.

Thus we may assume
L_{ab^{m}}=L_{ab^{m+1}}
if
n=2m+1
, and
L_{ab^{m-1}}=L_{ab^{m+1}}
if
n=2m
, and proceed to minimizing
L_{a^{2}b^{n}}
.

First, we find a formula, (
53
) below, for
L_{a^{2}b^{n}}
.
We set

(46)

Z:=XY^{\frac{n}{2}}.

Then
X=ZY^{-\frac{n}{2}}
and

(47)

X^{2}Y^{n}=ZY^{-\frac{n}{2}}ZY^{\frac{n}{2}}.

It follows from the symmetry
L_{ab^{m}}=L_{ab^{m+1}}
(if
n=2m+1
) or
L_{ab^{m-1}}=L_{ab^{m+1}}
(if
n=2m
) that
the axes of the fractional linear transformations
Y
and
Z
intersect perpendicularly.
Thus we may assume that

(48)

Y=\begin{pmatrix}e^{\frac{L_{b}}{2}}&0\\
0&e^{-\frac{L_{b}}{2}}\end{pmatrix},\quad Z=\begin{pmatrix}\cosh H_{b}&\sinh H_{b}\\
\sinh H_{b}&\cosh H_{b}\end{pmatrix},

where
H_{b}
is the length of the altitude perpendicular to the side of length
\frac{1}{2}L_{b}
in the Weierstrass triangle associated to the triple
a,b,ab
.
By Proposition
3.1
, we have

(49)

\sinh H_{b}\sinh\frac{L_{b}}{2}=\cosh\frac{L_{\partial}}{4}.

Using (
47
) and (
48
), a direct calculation gives

(50)

X^{2}Y^{n}=\begin{pmatrix}\cosh^{2}H_{b}+(\sinh^{2}H_{b})e^{\frac{nL_{b}}{2}}&(\cosh H_{b}\sinh H_{b})(e^{-\frac{nL_{b}}{2}}+1)\\
(\cosh H_{b}\sinh H_{b})(1+e^{\frac{nL_{b}}{2}})&(\sinh^{2}H_{b})e^{-\frac{nL_{b}}{2}}+\cosh^{2}H_{b}\end{pmatrix}.

Taking traces gives

(51)

2\cosh\frac{L_{a^{2}b^{n}}}{2}=2\cosh^{2}H_{b}+2\sinh^{2}H_{b}\cosh\frac{nL_{b}}{2}.

Consequently,

(52)

\sinh\frac{L_{a^{2}b^{n}}}{4}=\sinh H_{b}\cosh\frac{nL_{b}}{4}.

Combining with (
49
), we have

(53)

\sinh\frac{L_{a^{2}b^{n}}}{4}=\cosh\frac{L_{\partial}}{4}\cosh\frac{nL_{b}}{4}\Big/\sinh\frac{L_{b}}{2}.

Next, we find the minimum point for
L_{a^{2}b^{n}}
.
Differentiating the two sides of (
53
) with respect to
L_{b}
, we see that

(54)

\frac{{\rm d}L_{a^{2}b^{n}}}{{\rm d}L_{b}}=0\Longleftrightarrow\frac{n}{4}\sinh\frac{nL_{b}}{4}\sinh\frac{L_{b}}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\cosh\frac{nL_{b}}{4}\cosh\frac{L_{b}}{2}=0.

Therefore at the minimum point
(L^{*}_{a},L^{*}_{b},L^{*}_{ab})
for
L_{a^{2}b^{n}}
, the value of
L^{*}_{b}
is determined by

(55)

\frac{n}{2}\tanh\frac{L^{*}_{b}}{2}\tanh\frac{nL^{*}_{b}}{4}=1,

which is equality (
44
) as desired.

It is easy to know that, the solution
L^{*}_{b}>0
to equation (
55
) exists and is unique,
since the left side of (
55
) is a strictly increasing function in
L^{*}_{b}\in(0,+\infty)
which
has limit
0
as
L^{*}_{b}\rightarrow 0
and limit
n/2>1
as
L^{*}_{b}\rightarrow+\infty
.

We conclude that, given
n\geq 3
and
L_{\partial}\geq 0
, the minimum
L^{\rm min}_{a^{2}b^{n}}
of
L_{a^{2}b^{n}}
is given by

(56)

\sinh\frac{L^{\rm min}_{a^{2}b^{n}}}{4}=\cosh\frac{L_{\partial}}{4}\cosh\frac{nL^{*}_{b}}{4}\Big/\sinh\frac{L^{*}_{b}}{2},

where
L^{*}_{b}>0
is determined by equation (
55
). It follows from (
55
) that

(57)

\cosh\frac{nL^{*}_{b}}{4}\Big/\sinh\frac{L^{*}_{b}}{2}=\sinh\frac{nL^{*}_{b}}{4}\Big/\cosh\frac{L^{*}_{b}}{2}.

Substituting this into (
56
) gives

(58)

\sinh\frac{L^{\rm min}_{a^{2}b^{n}}}{4}=\frac{n}{2}\cosh\frac{L_{\partial}}{4}\sinh\frac{nL^{*}_{b}}{4}\Big/\cosh\frac{L^{*}_{b}}{2},

which is equality (
45
) as desired. This finishes the proof of Theorem
4.1
.
∎

The solution to (
55
) defines a function

(59)

L^{*}_{b}=L^{*}_{b}(L_{\partial},n),

and consequently a function

(60)

L^{\rm min}_{a^{2}b^{n}}=L^{\rm min}_{a^{2}b^{n}}(L_{\partial},n).

In the following addendum we obtain monotonicity and asymptotic properties of
L^{*}_{b}
and
L^{\rm min}_{a^{2}b^{n}}

as one of
n
and
L_{\partial}
tends to infinity while the other remains fixed.

Theorem 4.2
.

We have the following addenda to Theorem
4.1
.

(a) For
L_{\partial}\geq 0
fixed, both
L^{*}_{b}
and
(nL^{*}_{b})/4
, where
n\geq 3
are strictly decreasing in
n
.
Furthermore, for
L_{\partial}\geq 0
fixed and
n\rightarrow+\infty
, there hold

(61)

L^{*}_{b}\rightarrow 0,

(62)

\frac{nL^{*}_{b}}{4}\rightarrow t^{*},

(63)

L^{\rm min}_{a^{2}b^{n}}\sim 4\log n,

where
t^{*}=1.199678640...
is the unique positive solution to the equation

(64)

t^{*}\tanh(t^{*})=1.

(b) As
n\geq 3
fixed,
L^{\rm min}_{a^{2}b^{n}}
is strictly increasing in
L_{\partial}\in[0,+\infty)
. Furthermore, as
n\geq 3
fixed and

L_{\partial}\rightarrow+\infty
, there hold

(65)

L^{\rm min}_{a^{2}b^{n}}\rightarrow+\infty,

(66)

L^{\rm min}_{a^{2}b^{n}}\sim L_{\partial}.

Proof.

For (a), let
L_{\partial}\geq 0
be fixed.
Note that the left side of (
55
) is an expression which is strictly increasing in
n
and in
L^{*}_{b}
.
It follows that
L^{*}_{b}
is strictly decreasing in
n
. For the limit as
n\rightarrow+\infty
, there must hold

(67)

\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}L^{*}_{b}=0,

otherwise there would be a contradiction by taking limit in equality (
55
).

Rewriting equality (
55
) as

(68)

\frac{nL^{*}_{b}}{4}\tanh\frac{nL^{*}_{b}}{4}=\frac{L^{*}_{b}}{2}\left(\tanh\frac{L^{*}_{b}}{2}\right)^{-1},

one concludes that
\frac{nL^{*}_{b}}{4}
is strictly decreasing in
n
.

Let us write

(69)

t^{*}=\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{nL^{*}_{b}}{4}.

Then
t^{*}>0
. Taking limits in (
68
) as
n\rightarrow+\infty
gives

t^{*}\tanh t^{*}=1,

which is (
64
) as desired.
Numerically solving this equation using Maple
®
gives

(70)

t^{*}=1.199678640257733833916369848641141944261458788418607...

It is easy to derive from (
45
) that, as
L_{\partial}\geq 0
fixed and
n\rightarrow+\infty
,

\exp\Big(\frac{1}{4}L^{\rm min}_{a^{2}b^{n}}\Big)\sim n\cosh\frac{L_{\partial}}{4}\sinh t^{*},

and hence

L^{\rm min}_{a^{2}b^{n}}\sim 4\log n,

which is (
63
) as desired.

For (b), let
n\geq 3
be fixed. Then
L^{*}_{b}
is fixed since it depends on
n
only.
Thus it is easy to see from (
45
) that
L^{\rm min}_{a^{2}b^{n}}
is strictly increasing in
L_{\partial}\in[0,+\infty)
and

\lim_{L_{\partial}\rightarrow+\infty}L^{\rm min}_{a^{2}b^{n}}=+\infty.

Furthermore, one has, as
n\geq 3
fixed and
L_{\partial}\rightarrow+\infty
,

\exp\Big(\frac{1}{4}L^{\rm min}_{a^{2}b^{n}}\Big)\sim\exp\Big(\frac{L_{\partial}}{4}\Big)\,\frac{n}{2}\sinh\frac{nL^{*}_{b}}{4}\Big/\cosh\frac{L^{*}_{b}}{2},

and hence

L^{\rm min}_{a^{2}b^{n}}\sim L_{\partial},

which is (
66
) as desired. This finishes the proof of Theorem
4.2
.
∎

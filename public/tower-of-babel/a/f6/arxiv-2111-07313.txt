An upwind DG scheme preserving the maximum principle for the convective Cahn-Hilliard model
Daniel Acosta-Soba , Francisco Guillén-González , J. Rafael Rodríguez-Galván

Abstract
The design of numerical approximations of the Cahn-Hilliard model preserving the maximum principle is a challenging problem, even more if considering additional transport terms. In this work we present a new upwind Discontinuous Galerkin scheme for the convective Cahn-Hilliard model with degenerate mobility which preserves the maximum principle and prevents non-physical spurious oscillations. Furthermore, we show some numerical experiments in agreement with the previous theoretical results. Finally, numerical comparisons with other schemes found in the literature are also carried out.



Keywords:

Discontinuous Galerkin, upwind scheme, diffuse interface, convection, degenerate mobility.

1
Introduction

This paper is concerned with the development of Discontinuous Galerkin (DG)
numerical schemes for the following convective Cahn-Hilliard (CCH) problem (written as a second order system): Given an incompressible velocity field

\bm{v}\in C(\overline{\Omega})^{d}
with
\nabla\cdot\bm{v}=0
in
\Omega
,
such that
\bm{v}\cdot\bm{n}=0
on
\partial\Omega
, find two real valued functions, the phase
u
and the chemical potential
\mu
, defined in

\Omega\times[0,T]
such that:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathsf{Pe}}\nabla\cdot\left(M(u)\nabla\mu\right)-\nabla\cdot(u\bm{v})\quad

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega\times(0,T),

(1a)

\displaystyle\mu

\displaystyle=F^{\prime}(u)-\varepsilon^{2}\Delta u\quad

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega\times(0,T),

(1b)

\displaystyle\nabla u\cdot\bm{n}

\displaystyle=\left(M(u)\nabla\mu-u\bm{v}\right)\cdot\bm{n}=0\quad

\displaystyle\text{on }\partial\Omega\times(0,T),

(1c)

\displaystyle u(0)

\displaystyle=u_{0}\quad

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega.

(1d)

The phase field variable
u
localizes the two different phases at the values
u=0
and
u=1
, while the interface occurs when
0<u<1
.
Here
\Omega
is a bounded
polygonal domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}

(
d=2
or
3
in practice), with boundary
\partial\Omega
whose
outward-pointing unit normal is denoted
\bm{n}
. Parameters are
\mathsf{Pe}>0
the
Péclet number of the flow and
\varepsilon>0
related to the width interface. For simplicity, we take
\mathsf{Pe}=1
. The given initial phase is denoted by
u_{0}
.
The classical Cahn–Hilliard equation (CH) corresponds to the case without convection, i.e.

\bm{v}=0
.

We consider the double-well Ginzburg-Landau potential

F(u)=\frac{1}{4}u^{2}(1-u)^{2}

and the
degenerate mobility

M(u)=u(1-u).

(2)

This type of degenerate mobility, vanishing at the pure phases
u=0
and
u=1
, implies the following maximum principle property (see
[
16
]
for
the CH equation): if
0\leq u_{0}\leq 1
in
\overline{\Omega}
then
0\leq u(\cdot,t)\leq 1
in
\overline{\Omega}
for
t\in(0,T)
. This property does not hold for constant mobility, and in general for fourth-order parabolic equations.
We are concerned in numerical schemes maintaining this property at the discrete level.

Cahn-Hilliard equation was originally
introduced in
[
9
,
8
]
as a phenomenological
model of phase separation in a binary alloy and it has been
successfully applied in different contexts as a model which
characterizes phase segregation and interface dynamics. Applications
include tumor
tissues
[
35
,
34
]
,
image processing
[
6
]
and multi-phase fluid
systems, see e.g. the review
[
26
]
.
The dynamics of this equation comprises a first stage in which a rapid
separation process takes place, leading to the creation of interfaces,
followed by a
second stage where aggregation and development of bulk phases separated by thin diffuse interfaces take place.
These two phenomena
(separation and aggregation) are characterized by different temporal
and spatial scales which, together with the non-linear and
fourth-order nature of CH, makes efficiently solving this equation an
interesting computational challenge.

The interface is represented as a layer of small thickness of order

\varepsilon
and the auxiliary function
u
(the phase-field
function) is used to localize the (pure) phases
u=0
and
u=1
. The
chemical potential
\mu
is the variational derivative
of the Helmholtz free energy
E(u)
with respect to
u
,

\mu=\partial_{u}E(u)
, where

\displaystyle E(u)=\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+F(u)\right)dx.

(3)

Then the dynamics of CH correspond to the physical energy dissipation
of
u_{t}=-\nabla\cdot J(u)
, where

J(u)=-M(u)\nabla\mu=-M(u)\nabla\partial_{u}E(u)
. For existence of
solution for CH equation with a degenerate mobility of the
type (
2
), besides bounds for the phase over time, we
can refer to
[
16
]
. A review and
many variants can be read in
[
30
]
.

Numerical approximation of CH is a research topic of great
interest due to the wide spectrum of its applications, as well as a
source of interesting mathematical challenges due to the computational
issues referred above. Time approximations include both linear
schemes
[
21
]
and nonlinear ones, where
a Newton method is usually employed. Although some authors (see
e.g.
[
15
]
) consider the
fourth-order equation obtained by substitution in the equation
(
1a
) of the expression of
\mu
given
in (
1b
), the spatial discretization
is usually based on the mixed phase field/chemical potential
formulation presented in (
1
).
These equations can be approximated by classical numerical methods,
including: (a) Finite differences, see
e.g.
[
20
,
12
]
for
constant mobility
M(u)\equiv 1

and
[
25
]
for degenerate mobility
similar to (
2
), see
also
[
32
]
for applications in
crystallography. (b) Finite volumes, see
e.g.
[
13
,
4
]

for degenerate mobility schemes. (c) And, above all, finite elements see
the precursory papers
[
15
]

(constant mobility) and
[
5
]

(degenerate mobility).

In recent years, an increasing number of advances has been published exploring
the use of discontinuous Galerkin (DG) methods both for
constant
[
24
,
2
]

and degenerate
mobility
[
33
,
36
,
18
,
28
]
. Although DG methods lead in general to more complex
algorithms and to bigger amounts of degrees of freedom, they exhibit
some benefits of which one can take advantage also in CH
equations, for instance doing mesh refining and adaptivity
[
2
]
.
In this paper we are interested in the following relevant point: the possibility of designing conservative schemes preserving the maximum-principle
0\leq u(t,x)\leq 1
at the discrete level, even for the CCH variant of CH equations,
namely where a convective term models the convection or transport of the phase-field.

The idea of introducing a convection term in the CH equations modeling
a phase-field driven by a flow, arriving at
the CCH problem (
1
), arouses
great interest. Specially, in the case where the phase field is
coupled with fluid equations.

To design adequate numerical approximations of these CCH equations is an extremely challenging problem because it adds the hyperbolic nature of convective terms to the inherent difficulties of the CH equation. There are not many numerical methods in the literature on this topic, although some interesting contributions can be found. The first of them
[
3
]
is worth mentioning for the application of high-resolution spectral Fourier schemes. We also underline the papers
[
7
,
27
]
, based on finite volume approximations, and also
[
11
,
22
]
, where finite difference techniques are applied for Navier-Stokes CH equations.

Some authors have recently worked in DG methods
for spatial discretization of the CCH problem. Using DG schemes
in this context is natural because they are well-suited in
convection-dominated problems, even when the Péclet number is
substantially large. For instance, the work of
[
24
]
is focused on construction and
convergence analysis of a DG method
for the CCH equations with constant mobility,
applying an interior penalty technique to the second order terms and
an upwind operator for discretization of the convection term. Authors
of
[
18
]
consider CCH with degenerate mobility applying also an interior penalty to second order terms in the mixed
form (
1
) and a more elaborated
upwinding technique, based on a sigmoid function, to the convective
term.

These previous works show that DG methods are well suited for the approximation
of CCH equations, obtaining optimal convergence
order and maintaining most of the properties of the continuous
model.
But they have room for improvement in one specific question: getting a maximum
principle for the phase in the discrete case. Although there are some works in which the maximum principle for the CH model is preserved at the discrete level using finite volumes
[
4
]
and flux limiting for DG
[
19
]
, to the best knowledge of the authors, no scheme has been published in which an upwind DG technique is used to obtain a discrete maximum principle property.

Our main contribution in this paper is made in this regard: the
development of numerical schemes which guarantee punctual estimates of the phase
at the discrete level, in addition to maintaining the rest of
continuous properties of (
1
). The
main idea is to introduce an upwind DG discretization
associated to the transport of the phase by the convective velocity

\bm{v}
, but also associated to the degenerate mobility
M(u)
. The main result in
this work is Theorem
3.11
,
where we show that, for a piecewise constant approximation of the
phase, our DG scheme preserves the maximum principle, that is the discrete phase
is also bounded in
[0,1]
.

Our scheme is specifically designed for the CH equation with non-singular (polynomial) chemical potential and degenerate mobility. For the CH equation with the logarithmic Flory-Huggins potential, a strict maximum principle is satisfied without the need of degenerate mobility. In this case, designing maximum principle-preserving numerical schemes is a different issue. See, for instance, the paper
[
10
]
where a finite difference numerical is proposed in this regard.

The paper is organized as follows:
In Section
2
, we fix notation and review DG
techniques for discretization of conservative laws.
We consider the linear convection given by a
velocity field and show that the usual linear upwind DG numerical scheme with
P_{0}
approximation preserves positivity in general, and the maximum principle in the divergence-free case.
In Section
3
, we consider the CCH problem (
1
). Using
a truncated potential and a convex-splitting time discretization
we obtain a scheme satisfying an energy law, which is decreasing for the CH case (
\bm{v}=0
). Then we introduce our fully discrete
scheme (
29
) providing a
maximum principle for the discrete phase variable.
Finally, in Section
4
we present several numerical tests, comparing our DG scheme with two different space discretizations found in the literature: classical continuous
P_{1}
finite elements and the SIP+upwind sigmoid DG approximation proposed in
[
18
]
. We show error order tests and also we present qualitative comparisons where the maximum principle of our scheme is confirmed (and it is not conserved by the other ones).

DG discretization of conservative laws

2.2
Conservative laws

We consider the following non-linear conservative problem for
v
:

v_{t}+\nabla\cdot\bm{F}(v)=0\quad\text{en }\Omega\times(0,T),

(4)

where the flux
\bm{F}(\cdot)
is a vectorial continuous function.

Let
S_{h}=\mathbb{P}^{\text{disc}}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
.
Multiplying by any
\overline{v}\in\mathcal{S}_{h}
and using the Green Formula in each element
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
:

\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}v\;\overline{v}-\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{K}\bm{F}(v)\cdot\nabla\overline{v}+\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{\partial K}\big(\bm{F}(v)\cdot\bm{n}_{K}\big)\,\overline{v}=0,

where
\bm{n}_{K}
is the normal vector to
\partial K
pointing outwards
K
.
If
v
is a strong solution of (
4
), such that
v
is
continuous over
e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}
,
we get:

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}v\;\overline{v}-\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{K}\bm{F}(v)\cdot\nabla\overline{v}+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}\int_{e}\big(\bm{F}(v)\cdot\bm{n}_{e}\big)\,\left[\!\left[\overline{v}\right]\!\right]

\displaystyle=0.

(5)

But, if we look for functions
v\in\mathcal{S}_{h}
which are discontinuous over
e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}
, then the term

\bm{F}(v)\cdot\bm{n}_{e}
in the last integral of (
5
) is not well defined and we need to approach it. Hence, the concept of numerical flux is used, taking
expressions like

\bm{\Phi}(v_{K},v_{L},\bm{n}_{e})
such that:

\int_{e}\big(\bm{F}(v)\cdot\bm{n}_{e}\big)\,\left[\!\left[\overline{v}\right]\!\right]\approx\int_{e}\bm{\Phi}(v_{K},v_{L},\bm{n}_{e})\,\left[\!\left[\overline{v}\right]\!\right].

From now on we will consider fluxes of the form

\bm{F}(v)=M(v)\bm{\beta},

with
M=M(v)\in\mathbb{R}
and
\bm{\beta}=\bm{\beta}(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Since in this paper we are interested in studying conservative
problems defined over isolated domains, we impose from now on that the
transport vector
\bm{\beta}
satisfies the slip condition

\bm{\beta}\cdot\bm{n}=0\quad\text{on}\ \partial\Omega.

(6)

Remark 2.1
.

We will have to take into consideration the sign of
M^{\prime}(v)
, i.e. if
M(v)
is nonincreasing or nondecreasing, in order to work out a well-suited upwinding scheme. This is due to

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\bm{F}(v)=\nabla\cdot(M(v)\bm{\beta})=M^{\prime}(v)\bm{\beta}\cdot\nabla v+M(v)\nabla\cdot\bm{\beta}.

(7)

where

•

The first term
M^{\prime}(v)\bm{\beta}\cdot\nabla v
is a transport of
v
in the direction of
M^{\prime}(v)\bm{\beta}
.

•

The second term
M(v)\nabla\cdot\bm{\beta}
can be seen as a reaction term with coefficient
\nabla\cdot\bm{\beta}
.

It may be specially interesting the case
\bm{\beta}=-\nabla v
, where the whole term
\nabla\cdot(M(v)\bm{\beta})=-\nabla\cdot(M(v)\nabla v)
is a nonlinear diffusion term.

2.3
Linear convection and positivity

Taking
M(v)=v
, we arrive at the linear conservative problem:

\displaystyle v_{t}+\nabla\cdot(v\bm{\beta})

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega\times(0,T),

(8a)

\displaystyle v(0)

\displaystyle=v_{0}

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega,

(8b)

where
\bm{\beta}\colon\overline{\Omega}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is continuous.

Remark 2.2
.

The problem (
8
) is well-posed without imposing
boundary conditions due to the slip
condition (
6
) (it can be derived writing an integral expression of solution in terms of the characteristic curves, see for example
[
31

if

\overline{\Omega}
,

in
\overline{\Omega}\times(0,T)
.

For the linear problem (
8a
), we introduce
the upwind numerical flux

\bm{\Phi}(v_{K},v_{L},\bm{n}_{e})\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=(\bm{\beta}\cdot\bm{n}_{e})_{\oplus}v_{K}-(\bm{\beta}\cdot\bm{n}_{e})_{\ominus}v_{L},

(9)

which leads to the following discrete scheme for
(
8a

, find

v^{m+1}\in\mathcal{S}_{h}
solving

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\delta_{t}v^{m+1}\overline{v}+a_{h}^{\text{upw}}(\bm{\beta};v^{m+1}_{\oplus},\overline{v})

\displaystyle=0,\quad\forall\overline{v}\in\mathcal{S}_{h},

(10)

where

\displaystyle a_{h}^{\text{upw}}(\bm{\beta};v,\overline{v})\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=

\displaystyle-\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{K}v(\bm{\beta}\cdot\nabla\overline{v})

\displaystyle+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{i},e=K\cap L}\int_{e}\left((\bm{\beta}\cdot\bm{n}_{e})_{\oplus}v_{K}-(\bm{\beta}\cdot\bm{n}_{e})_{\ominus}v_{L}\right)\left[\!\left[\overline{v}\right]\!\right]

(11)

and

Notice that we have truncated

by its positive part

, taking into account that the solution of the continuous model (

Remark 2.3
.

The numerical flux
\bm{\Phi}(v_{K},v_{L},\bm{n}_{e})
giving in (

\bm{\Phi}(v_{K},v_{L},\bm{n}_{e})=(\bm{\beta}\cdot\bm{n}_{e})\left\{\!\!\left\{v\right\}\!\!\right\}+\frac{1}{2}|\bm{\beta}\cdot\bm{n}_{e}|\left[\!\left[v\right]\!\right],

where

Now we will prove that if we set

Theorem 2.4

(DG scheme (

.

If

Proof.

Taking the following test function

\displaystyle\overline{v}=\begin{cases}(v_{K^{*}}^{m+1})_{\ominus}&\text{in }K^{*}\\
0&\text{out of }K^{*}\end{cases},\quad\overline{v}\in\mathcal{S}_{h},

where

By applying for all

\displaystyle a_{h}^{\text{upw}}(\bm{\beta}

\displaystyle;v^{m+1}_{\oplus},(v_{K^{*}}^{m+1})_{\ominus})=

\displaystyle=\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{i},e=K^{*}\cap L}\int_{e}\left((\bm{\beta}\cdot\bm{n}_{e})_{\oplus}(v_{K^{*}}^{m+1})_{\oplus}-(\bm{\beta}\cdot\bm{n}_{e})_{\ominus}(v_{L}^{m+1})_{\oplus}\right)(v_{K^{*}}^{m+1})_{\ominus}

On the other hand,

hence

Theorem 2.5

(Existence of DG scheme (

))

Assume that
\mathcal{S}_{h}=\mathbb{P}^{\text{disc}}_{0}(\mathcal{T}_{h})

Proof.

Consider the following well-known theorem:

Theorem 2.6

(Leray-Schauder fixed point theorem)

Let
\mathcal{X}

\{x\in\mathcal{X}\colon x=\alpha\,T(x)\quad\text{for some }0\leq\alpha\leq 1\}

Given a function

with

T\colon\mathbb{P}^{\text{disc}}_{0}(\mathcal{T}_{h})\longrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{\text{disc}}_{0}(\mathcal{T}_{h})

where

is the unique solution of the linear scheme:

\displaystyle v\in\mathbb{P}^{\text{disc}}_{0}(\mathcal{T}_{h}),\quad\int_{\Omega}\frac{v-w}{\Delta t}\overline{v}

\displaystyle=-a_{h}^{\text{upw}}(\bm{\beta};\widehat{v}_{\oplus},\overline{v}),\quad\forall\overline{v}\in\mathbb{P}^{\text{disc}}_{0}(\mathcal{T}_{h}).

(14)

The idea is to use the Leray-Schauder fixed point theorem
2.6
to the operator
T
. First of all,
T
is well defined.

Secondly, we will check that
T
is continuous. Let
\{\widehat{v}_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{P}^{\text{disc}}_{0}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
be a sequence such that
\lim_{j\to\infty}\widehat{v}_{j}=\widehat{v}
. Taking into account that all norms are equivalent in
\mathbb{P}^{\text{disc}}_{0}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
since it is a finite-dimensional space, the convergence
\widehat{v}_{j}\to\widehat{v}
is equivalent to the convergence elementwise
(\widehat{v}_{j})_{K}\to\widehat{v}_{K}
for every
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
(this may be seen, for instance, by using the norm
\|\cdot\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
). Taking limits when
j\to\infty
in the scheme (
14
) (with
\widehat{v}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\widehat{v}_{j}
and
v\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=T(\widehat{v}_{j})
),
and using the notion of convergence elementwise, we get that

\lim_{j\to\infty}T(\widehat{v}_{j})=T(\widehat{v})=T\left(\lim_{j\to\infty}\widehat{v}_{j}\right),

hence
T
is continuous.

In particular, as
T
is a continuous operator defined over
\mathbb{P}^{\text{disc}}_{0}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
, which is finite-dimensional,
T
is also compact.

Finally, let us prove that the set

B=\{v\in\mathbb{P}^{\text{disc}}_{0}(\mathcal{T}_{h})\colon v=\alpha\,T(v)\quad\text{for some }0\leq\alpha\leq 1\}

is bounded. The case
\alpha=0
is trivial so we will assume that
\alpha\in(0,1]
. If
v\in B
, then
v
is the solution of the scheme

\displaystyle v\in\mathbb{P}^{\text{disc}}_{0}(\mathcal{T}_{h}),\quad\int_{\Omega}\frac{v-\alpha w}{\Delta t}\overline{v}

\displaystyle=-\alpha\,a_{h}^{\text{upw}}(\bm{\beta};v_{\oplus},\overline{v}),\quad\forall\overline{v}\in\mathbb{P}^{\text{disc}}_{0}(\mathcal{T}_{h}).

(15)

Now, testing (
15
) by
\overline{v}=1
, we get that

\int_{\Omega}v=\alpha\int_{\Omega}w,

and, since
w\geq 0
, and since it can be proved that
v\geq 0
using the same arguments than in Theorem
2.4
, we get that

\|v\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leq\|w\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}.

Hence, since
\mathbb{P}^{\text{disc}}_{0}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
is a finite-dimensional space where the norms are equivalent, we have proved that
B
is bounded.
Thus, using the Leray-Schauder fixed point theorem
2.6
, there is a solution of the scheme (
10
).
∎

Let us focus on the the following linear scheme (without
truncating
v
by its positive part,
v_{\oplus}
): Given
v^{m}\in\mathbb{P}^{\text{disc}}_{0}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
, find
v^{m+1}\in\mathbb{P}^{\text{disc}}_{0}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
such that, for every
\overline{v}\in\mathbb{P}^{\text{disc}}_{0}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
:

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\delta_{t}v^{m+1}\overline{v}+a_{h}^{\text{upw}}(\bm{\beta};v^{m+1},\overline{v})

\displaystyle=0.

(16)

It is well-known (see e.g.
[
14
]
and references
therein) that (
16
) has a unique solution.
Since we have shown positivity of the nonlinear truncated scheme
(
10
), then any solution
of (
10
) is the unique
solution of (
16
). This argument implies uniqueness of (
10
) and
positivity of (
16
).

Corollary 2.7

(Linear DG scheme (
16
) preserves positivity)
.

For any
v^{m}\in\mathbb{P}^{\text{disc}}_{0}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
with
v^{m}\geq 0
in
\overline{\Omega}
, the unique solution
v^{m+1}
of the linear scheme (
16
) is positive, i.e.
v^{m+1}\geq 0
in
\overline{\Omega}
.

2.4
Linear convection with incompressible velocity and maximum principle

Now let us focus on the particular case of the conservation problem
(
8
)
where

\bm{\beta}\colon\overline{\Omega}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is continuous and
incompressible
,
i.e.
\nabla\cdot\bm{\beta}=0
in
\Omega
. In this case, the
solution of the problem
(
8
),
satisfies the
following maximum principle (it can be proved, for instance, using the characteristics method as in Remark
2.2
):

\min_{\overline{\Omega}}{v_{0}}\leq v\leq\max_{\overline{\Omega}}{v_{0}}\quad\hbox{in $\overline{\Omega}\times(0,T).$}

We are going to show that
the solution of the linear scheme
16
(without truncating
v
) preserves this maximum principle. The proof is based on the fact that, as consequence of the divergence theorem, one has

\displaystyle\int_{\partial K}\bm{\beta}\cdot\bm{n}_{K}=0,\quad\forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}.

(17)

Proposition 2.8

(Linear DG (
16
) preserves the maximum principle)
.

For any
v^{m}\in\mathbb{P}^{\text{disc}}_{0}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
, the solution
v^{m+1}\in\mathbb{P}^{\text{disc}}_{0}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
of the scheme (
16
) satisfies the maximum principle, that is

\min_{\overline{\Omega}}{v^{m}}\leq v^{m+1}\leq\max_{\overline{\Omega}}{v^{m}}\quad\text{in }{\overline{\Omega}}.

Proof.

First, we will prove that
v^{m+1}\geq\min_{\overline{\Omega}}v^{m}
. Let us denote
v_{min}=\min_{\overline{\Omega}}v^{m}
.
Taking the following test function

\displaystyle\overline{v}=\begin{cases}(v_{K^{*}}^{m+1}-v_{min})_{\ominus}&\text{in }K^{*}\\
0&\text{out of }K^{*}\end{cases},

where
K^{*}
is an element of
\mathcal{T}_{h}
such that the value
v_{K^{*}}^{m+1}=\min_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}v_{K}^{m+1}
, the scheme (
16
) becomes

\displaystyle|K^{*}|\delta_{t}v_{K^{*}}^{m+1}(v_{K^{*}}^{m+1}-v_{min})_{\ominus}=-a_{h}^{\text{upw}}(\bm{\beta};v^{m+1},(v_{K^{*}}^{m+1}-v_{min})_{\ominus}).

Now, as we have chosen
K^{*}
we can assure that
v_{L}^{m+1}\geq v_{K^{*}}^{m+1}
for all
L\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
. Hence, using (
17
), one has

\displaystyle a_{h}^{\text{upw}}(\bm{\beta}

\displaystyle;v^{m+1},(v_{K^{*}}^{m+1}-v_{min})_{\ominus})=

\displaystyle=\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{i},e=K^{*}\cap L}\int_{e}\left((\bm{\beta}\cdot\bm{n}_{e})_{\oplus}v_{K^{*}}^{m+1}-(\bm{\beta}\cdot\bm{n}_{e})_{\ominus}v_{L}^{m+1}\right)(v_{K^{*}}^{m+1}-v_{min})_{\ominus}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{i},e=K^{*}\cap L}\int_{e}\left((\bm{\beta}\cdot\bm{n}_{e})_{\oplus}v_{K^{*}}^{m+1}-(\bm{\beta}\cdot\bm{n}_{e})_{\ominus}v_{K^{*}}^{m+1}\right)(v_{K^{*}}^{m+1}-v_{min})_{\ominus}

\displaystyle=v_{K^{*}}^{m+1}(v_{K^{*}}^{m+1}-v_{min})_{\ominus}\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{i},e=K^{*}\cap L}\int_{e}(\bm{\beta}\cdot\bm{n}_{e})=0.

Therefore,

|K^{*}|\delta_{t}v_{K^{*}}^{m+1}(v_{K^{*}}^{m+1}-v_{min})_{\ominus}\geq 0.

Moreover,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq|K^{*}|(\delta_{t}v_{K^{*}}^{m+1})(v_{K^{*}}^{m+1}-v_{min})_{\ominus}

\displaystyle=\frac{|K^{*}|}{\Delta t}\left((v_{K^{*}}^{m+1}-v_{min})+(v_{min}-v_{K^{*}}^{m})\right)(v_{K^{*}}^{m+1}-v_{min})_{\ominus}

\displaystyle=\frac{|K^{*}|}{\Delta t}\left(-(v_{K^{*}}^{m+1}-v_{min})_{\ominus}^{2}+(v_{min}-v_{K^{*}}^{m})(v_{K^{*}}^{m+1}-v_{min})_{\ominus}\right)\leq 0,

then we have proved that
(v_{K^{*}}^{m+1}-v_{min})_{\ominus}=0
. Hence, from the choice of
K^{*}
, we can assure
v^{m+1}\geq\min_{\overline{\Omega}}v^{m}
.

Now, we will prove that
v^{m+1}\leq\max_{\overline{\Omega}}v^{m}
.
Let us denote
v_{max}=\max_{\overline{\Omega}}v^{m}
. Taking the following test function

\displaystyle\overline{v}=\begin{cases}(v_{K^{*}}^{m+1}-v_{max})_{\oplus}&\text{in }K^{*}\\
0&\text{out of }K^{*}\end{cases},

where
K^{*}
is an element of
\mathcal{T}_{h}
such that the value
v_{K^{*}}^{m+1}=\max_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}v_{K}^{m+1}
and using similar arguments to those above, we arrive at

|K^{*}|\delta_{t}v_{K^{*}}^{m+1}(v_{K^{*}}^{m+1}-v_{max})_{\oplus}\leq 0.

Moreover,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|K^{*}|}{\Delta t}\left((v_{K^{*}}^{m+1}-v_{max})^{2}_{\oplus}+(v_{max}-v_{K^{*}}^{m})(v_{K^{*}}^{m+1}-v_{max})_{\oplus}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{|K^{*}|}{\Delta t}\left((v_{K^{*}}^{m+1}-v_{max})+(v_{max}-v_{K^{*}}^{m})\right)(v_{K^{*}}^{m+1}-v_{max})_{\oplus}

\displaystyle=|K^{*}|\delta_{t}v_{K^{*}}^{m+1}(v_{K^{*}}^{m+1}-v_{max})_{\oplus}\leq 0,

then we have proved that
(v_{K^{*}}^{m+1}-v_{max})_{\oplus}=0
. From the choice of
K^{*}
, we can assure that
v^{m+1}\leq\max_{\overline{\Omega}}v^{m}
.
∎

3
Cahn-Hilliard with degenerate mobility and incompressible convection

At this point, given
\bm{v}\colon\Omega\times(0,T)\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}

a continuous incompressible velocity field satisfying the slip condition (
6
), we are in position to consider the CCH problem (
1
).

Remark 3.1
.

Any smooth enough solution
(u,\mu)
of the CCH model (
1
)
satisfies the maximum principle
0\leq u\leq 1
in
\overline{\Omega}\times(0,T)
whenever
0\leq u_{0}\leq 1
in
\overline{\Omega}
. The proof of this statement is a straightforward consequence of Remark
2.2
.

•

To prove that
u\geq 0
, it is enough to notice that
u
is the solution of
(
8
)
with
\bm{\beta}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=-(1-u)\nabla\mu+\bm{v}
and to use Remark
2.2
.

•

To check that
u\leq 1
we make the change of variables
w\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=1-u
and, using that
\nabla\cdot\bm{v}=0
, notice that
w
is the solution of
(
8
)
with
\bm{\beta}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=(1-w)(\nabla\mu+\bm{v})
. Then, we just use Remark
2.2
.

Owing to this maximum principle, the following
C^{2}(\mathbb{R})
truncated potential is considered

F(u)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\frac{1}{4}\begin{cases}u^{2}&u<0,\\
u^{2}(1-u)^{2}&u\in[0,1],\\
(u-1)^{2}&u>1.\end{cases}

(18)

This truncated potential will allow us to define a linear time discrete convex-splitting scheme satisfying an energy law (where the energy is decreasing in the case
\bm{v}=0
), see Subsection
3.1
.

Assume
0\leq u_{0}\leq 1
in
\overline{\Omega}
. The weak formulation of problem (
1
) consists of finding
(u,\mu)
such that,
u(t)\in H^{1}(\Omega)
,
\mu(t)\in H^{1}(\Omega)
with
\partial_{t}u(t)\in H^{1}(\Omega)^{\prime}
,
M(u(t))\nabla\mu(t)\in L^{2}(\Omega)
a.e.
t\in(0,T)
, and
satisfying the following variational problem a.e.
t\in(0,T)
for every
\overline{\mu},\overline{u}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
:

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{t}u(t),\overline{\mu}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\left(M(u(t))\nabla\mu(t)-u(t)\bm{v}(t),\nabla\overline{\mu}\right)_{\left(L^{2}(\Omega)\right)^{d}},

(19a)

\displaystyle\left(\mu(t),\overline{u}\right)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{2}\left(\nabla u(t),\nabla\overline{u}\right)_{\left(L^{2}(\Omega)\right)^{d}}+\left(F^{\prime}(u(t)),\overline{u}\right)_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

(19b)

with the initial condition
u(0)=u_{0}
in
\Omega
.

Remark 3.2
.

By taking
\overline{\mu}=1
in (
19a
), any solution
u

of (
19
)
conserves the mass, because

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}u(x,t)dx=0.

Remark 3.3
.

By taking
\overline{\mu}=\mu(t)
and
\overline{u}=\partial_{t}u(t)
in (
19
), and adding the resulting expressions, one has that any solution
(u,\mu)

of (
19
)
satisfies the following energy law

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}E(u(t))

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}M(u(x,t))|\nabla\mu(x,t)|^{2}dx=\int_{\Omega}u(x,t)\,\bm{v}(x,t)\cdot\nabla\mu(x,t)dx,

(20)

where
E\colon H^{1}(\Omega)\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
is the Helmholtz free energy

\displaystyle E(u)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+F(u)\right)dx.

(21)

Indeed, by applying the chain rule we get

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}E(u(t))

\displaystyle=\left\langle\frac{\delta E}{\delta u}(u(t)),\partial_{t}u(t)\right\rangle=\int_{\Omega}\mu(x,t)\partial_{t}u(x,t)dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}M(u(x,t))|\nabla\mu(x,t)|^{2}dx+\int_{\Omega}u(x,t)\,\bm{v}(x,t)\cdot\nabla\mu(x,t)dx.

In particular, in the CH case (
\bm{v}=0
), the energy
E(u(t))
is dissipative. Contrarily, to the best knowledge of the authors, for the CCH problem with
\bm{v}\neq 0
, there is no evidence of the existence of a dissipative energy.

3.1
Convex splitting time discretization

Now we are ready to focus on a convex splitting time discretization (of Eyre’s type
[
17
]
) of (
19
). Specially, we decompose the truncated potential (
18
) as follows:

F(u)=F_{\text{i}}(u)+F_{\text{e}}(u),

where

F_{\text{i}}(u)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\frac{3}{8}u^{2},\qquad F_{\text{e}}(u)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\frac{1}{4}\begin{cases}-\frac{1}{2}u^{2}&u<0,\\
u^{4}-2u^{3}-\frac{1}{2}u^{2}&u\in[0,1],\\
1-2u-\frac{1}{2}u^{2}&u>1.\end{cases}

It can be easily proved that
F_{\text{i}}(u)
is a convex operator, which will be
treated implicitly whereas
F_{\text{e}}(u)
is a concave operator that will be
treated explicitly. Then we consider the following convex

E_{\text{i}}(u(t))
and concave
E_{\text{e}}(u(t))
energy terms:

\displaystyle E_{\text{i}}(u)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{2}dx+\int_{\Omega}F_{\text{i}}(u(x))dx,

\displaystyle E_{\text{e}}(u)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\int_{\Omega}F_{\text{e}}(u(x))dx,

such that the free energy (
21
) is split as
E(u)=E_{\text{i}}(u)+E_{\text{e}}(u)
.

Finally we define the following time discretization of (
19
):
find
u^{m+1}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
and
\mu^{m+1}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
such that, for every
\overline{\mu},\overline{u}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
:

\displaystyle\left(\delta_{t}u^{m+1},\overline{\mu}\right)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=-\left(M(u^{m+1})_{\oplus}\nabla\mu^{m+1}-u^{m+1}\bm{v}(t_{m+1}),\nabla\overline{\mu}\right)_{\left(L^{2}(\Omega)\right)^{d}},

(22a)

\displaystyle\left(\mu^{m+1},\overline{u}\right)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{2}\left(\nabla u^{m+1},\nabla\overline{u}\right)_{\left(L^{2}(\Omega)\right)^{d}}+\left(f(u^{m+1},u^{m}),\overline{u}\right)_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

(22b)

where we denote the convex-implicit and concave-explicit linear approximation of the potential as follows

\displaystyle f(u^{m+1},u^{m})

\displaystyle\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=F_{\text{i}}^{\prime}(u^{m+1})+F_{\text{e}}^{\prime}(u^{m})

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{4}u^{m+1}+\frac{1}{4}\begin{cases}-u^{m},&u^{m}\in(-\infty,0),\\
4(u^{m})^{3}-6(u^{m})^{2}-u^{m},&u^{m}\in[0,1],\\
-\left(u^{m}+2\right),&u^{m}\in(1,+\infty).\end{cases}

(23)

Notice that the positive part of the mobilitity has been taken in (
22a
), regarding the Remark
3.1
, in order to prevent possible overshoots of the solution
u^{m+1}
beyond the interval
[0,1]
.

3.1.1
Discrete energy law

By adding
(
22a
) and
(
22b
) for

\overline{\mu}=\mu^{m+1}
and
\overline{u}=\delta_{t}u^{m+1}
in (
22
), we get:

\int_{\Omega}M(u^{m+1})_{\oplus}|\nabla\mu^{m+1}|^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\left(\nabla u^{m+1},\delta_{t}\nabla u^{m+1}\right)_{\left(L^{2}(\Omega)\right)^{d}}\\
+\left(f(u^{m+1},u^{m}),\delta_{t}u^{m+1}\right)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\int_{\Omega}u^{m+1}\bm{v}(\cdot,t_{m+1})\cdot\nabla\mu^{m+1}.

(24)

Taking into account that

\varepsilon^{2}\left(\nabla u^{m+1},\delta_{t}\nabla u^{m+1}\right)_{\left(L^{2}(\Omega)\right)^{d}}=\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\delta_{t}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u^{m+1}|^{2}+\frac{k\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\delta_{t}\nabla u^{m+1}|^{2},

and by adding and substracting
\delta_{t}\int_{\Omega}F(u^{m+1})
,
we get the following equality

\delta_{t}E(u^{m+1})+\int_{\Omega}M(u^{m+1})_{\oplus}|\nabla\mu^{m+1}|^{2}+\frac{k\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\delta_{t}\nabla u^{m+1}|^{2}\\
+\left(f(u^{m+1},u^{m}),\delta_{t}u^{m+1}\right)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-\delta_{t}\int_{\Omega}F(u^{m+1})=\int_{\Omega}u^{m+1}\bm{v}(\cdot,t_{m+1})\cdot\nabla\mu^{m+1},

where
E(u)
is defined in (
21
).

Then, using the Taylor theorem we get

\displaystyle F_{\text{i}}(u^{m})

\displaystyle=F_{\text{i}}(u^{m+1})+F_{\text{i}}^{\prime}(u^{m+1})(u^{m}-u^{m+1})+\frac{F_{\text{i}}^{\prime\prime}(u^{m+\xi})}{2}(u^{m}-u^{m+1})^{2},

\displaystyle F_{\text{e}}(u^{m+1})

\displaystyle=F_{\text{e}}(u^{m})+F_{\text{e}}^{\prime}(u^{m})(u^{m+1}-u^{m})+\frac{F_{\text{e}}^{\prime\prime}(u^{m+\eta})}{2}(u^{m+1}-u^{m})^{2},

for certain
\xi,\eta\in(0,1)
with
u^{m+\xi}=\xi u^{m+1}+(1-\xi)u^{m}
,
u^{m+\eta}=\eta u^{m+1}+(1-\eta)u^{m}
. Hence, adding these expressions and taking into consideration that
F(u)=F_{\text{i}}(u)+F_{\text{e}}(u)
for every
u\in\mathbb{R}
, we arrive at

\displaystyle F(u^{m+1})-F(u^{m})=

\displaystyle=f(u^{m+1},u^{m})(u^{m+1}-u^{m})-\frac{F^{\prime\prime}_{i}(u^{m+\xi})-F^{\prime\prime}_{e}(u^{m+\eta})}{2}(u^{m+1}-u^{m})^{2}.

Furthermore, as

\displaystyle\frac{F^{\prime\prime}_{i}(u^{m+\xi})-F^{\prime\prime}_{e}(u^{m+\eta})}{2k}=

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2k},&u^{m+\eta}\in(-\infty,0)\cup(1,+\infty),\\[3.30002pt]
\displaystyle\frac{3-12(u^{m+\eta})^{2}+12(u^{m+\eta})+1}{8k},&u^{m+\eta}\in[0,1],\end{cases}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2k}\geq 0,&u^{m+\eta}\in(-\infty,0)\cup(1,+\infty),\\[3.30002pt]
\frac{1}{2k}\left(1-3u^{m+\eta}(u^{m+\eta}-1)\right)\geq 0,&u^{m+\eta}\in[0,1],\end{cases}

we have

\frac{F^{\prime\prime}_{i}(u^{m+\xi})-F^{\prime\prime}_{e}(u^{m+\eta})}{2k}\geq 0,

and finally

\left(f(u^{m+1},u^{m}),\delta_{t}u^{m+1}\right)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-\int_{\Omega}\delta_{t}F(u^{m+1})\geq 0.

Therefore, we arrive at the following result:

Theorem 3.4
.

Any solution
of the scheme (
22
) satisfies the following
discrete energy law

\delta_{t}E(u^{m+1})+\int_{\Omega}M(u^{m+1})_{\oplus}|\nabla\mu^{m+1}|^{2}+\frac{k\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\delta_{t}\nabla u^{m+1}|^{2}\\
\leq\int_{\Omega}u^{m+1}\bm{v}(\cdot,t_{m+1})\cdot\nabla\mu^{m+1}.

(25)

In particular, if
\bm{v}=0
the time-discrete scheme is unconditionally energy stable, because
E(u^{m+1})\leq E(u^{m})
.

3.2
Fully discrete scheme

At this point we are going to introduce the key idea for the spatial approximation: to treat equation (
1a
) as a conservative problem where there are two different fluxes (linear and nonlinear). In this sense, we propose an upwind DG scheme approximating the nonlinear flux
\bm{F}(u)=M(u)\nabla\mu
properly.

To this aim, we take for values
v\in\mathbb{R}
the increasing and decreasing part of
M(v)_{\oplus}
, denoted respectively by
M^{\uparrow}(v)
and
M^{\downarrow}(v)
, as follows:

\displaystyle M^{\uparrow}(v)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{v}\left(\partial_{s}\left(M(s)_{\oplus}\right)\right)_{\oplus}ds=\int_{0}^{\min(v,1)}M^{\prime}(s)_{\oplus}ds=\int_{0}^{\min(v,1)}(1-2s)_{\oplus}ds,

\displaystyle M^{\downarrow}(v)

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{v}\left(\partial_{s}\left(M(s)_{\oplus}\right)\right)_{\ominus}ds=-\int_{0}^{\min(v,1)}M^{\prime}(s)_{\ominus}ds

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{\min(v,1)}(1-2s)_{\ominus}ds.

Therefore,

\displaystyle M^{\uparrow}(v)=\begin{cases}M(v)_{\oplus}&\text{if }v\leq\frac{1}{2}\\[1.99997pt]
M\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)&\text{if }v>\frac{1}{2}\end{cases},~~M^{\downarrow}(v)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }v\leq\frac{1}{2}\\
M(v)_{\oplus}-M\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)&\text{if }v>\frac{1}{2}\end{cases}.

(27)

Notice that
M^{\uparrow}(v)+M^{\downarrow}(v)=M(v)_{\oplus}
.
We define the followig generalized upwind bilinear form to be applied for the nonlinear flux
\bm{F}(u)=M(u)\bm{\beta}
where now
\bm{\beta}
can be discontinuous over
\mathcal{E}_{h}^{i}
(in fact we will take
\bm{\beta}=\nabla\mu
):

a_{h}^{\text{upw}}(\bm{\beta};M(v)_{\oplus},\overline{v})\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=-\int_{\Omega}(\bm{\beta}\cdot\nabla\overline{v})M(v)_{\oplus}\\
+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{i},e=K\cap L}\int_{e}\left((\left\{\!\!\left\{\bm{\beta}\right\}\!\!\right\}\cdot\bm{n}_{e})_{\oplus}(M^{\uparrow}(v_{K})+M^{\downarrow}(v_{L}))\right.\\
\left.-(\left\{\!\!\left\{\bm{\beta}\right\}\!\!\right\}\cdot\bm{n}_{e})_{\ominus}(M^{\uparrow}(v_{L})+M^{\downarrow}(v_{K}))\right)\left[\!\left[\overline{v}\right]\!\right].

Then, we propose the following fully discrete DG+Eyre scheme (named

Find

, with

\displaystyle\left(\delta_{t}u^{m+1},\overline{u}\right)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\hskip-60.00009pt+a_{h}^{\text{upw}}(-\nabla\mu^{m+1};M(u^{m+1})_{\oplus},\overline{u})+a_{h}^{\text{upw}}(\bm{v}(t_{m+1});u^{m+1},\overline{u})=0,

(29a)

\displaystyle\left(\mu^{m+1},\overline{\mu}\right)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{2}\left(\nabla w^{m+1},\nabla\overline{\mu}\right)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\left(f(u^{m+1},u^{m}),\overline{\mu}\right)_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

(29b)

\displaystyle\left(w^{m+1},\overline{w}\right)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\left(u^{m+1},\overline{w}\right)_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

(29c)

for all

\overline{\mu},\overline{w}\in\mathbb{P}^{\text{cont}}_{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})

Following the notation of the section

\displaystyle a_{h}^{\text{upw}}(\bm{v};u,\overline{u})

\displaystyle=\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{i},e=K\cap L}\int_{e}\left((\bm{v}\cdot\bm{n}_{e})_{\oplus}u_{K}-(\bm{v}\cdot\bm{n}_{e})_{\ominus}u_{L}\right)\left[\!\left[\overline{u}\right]\!\right].

In this scheme we have introduced a truncation of the function

taking its positive part

(

Notice that we have introduced a new continuous variable

, which is used in the diffusion term in (

) (which corresponds to the philic term in the energy of the model (
21
)). In fact, both variables
w^{m+1}
and

are approximations of

.

Remark 3.6
.

We are using the same notation in the fully discrete scheme (
29
) than the one we have used in the time-discrete scheme (
22
)
given in the section

, satisfying an energy law.

Nevertheless, in this case we are changing the meaning of the equations since we are treating (
29
) as the
u
-equation and (
29b
) as the
\mu
-equation, contrary to computations done to reach the energy law.
This has been done for the purpose of preserving the maximum principle in the equation (
29
) and adequately approximating the laplacian term of the equation (

).

Remark 3.7
.

The boundary condition
\nabla w^{m+1}\cdot\bm{n}=0
on
\partial\Omega\times(0,T)
is imposed implicitly by the term
\left(\nabla w^{m+1},\nabla\overline{\mu}\right)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
in (

).

Remark 3.8
.

Since
f(\cdot,u^{m})
is linear, we have the following equality of the potential term of (

\left(f(w^{m+1},u^{m}),\overline{\mu}\right)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\left(f(u^{m+1},u^{m}),\overline{\mu}\right)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Remark 3.9
.

The scheme (
29
) is nonlinear, hence we will have to use an iterative procedure, the Newton’s method, to approach its solution.

Theorem 3.11

(DG (
29
) preserves the maximum principle)
.

For any
u^{m}\in\mathbb{P}^{\text{disc}}_{0}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
with

in
\overline{\Omega}
, then any solution
u^{m+1}
of (

0\leq u^{m+1}\leq 1
in
\overline{\Omega}

Proof.

Firstly, we prove
that
u^{m+1}\geq 0
. Taking the following
\mathbb{P}^{\text{disc}}_{0}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
test
function

\displaystyle\overline{u}=\begin{cases}(u_{K^{*}}^{m+1})_{\ominus}&\text{in }K^{*}\\
0&\text{out of }K^{*}\end{cases},

where
K^{*}
is an element of
\mathcal{T}_{h}
such that
u_{K^{*}}^{m+1}=\min_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}u_{K}^{m+1}
, equation (

) becomes

|K^{*}|\delta_{t}u_{K^{*}}^{m+1}(u_{K^{*}}^{m+1})_{\ominus}=\\
=-a_{h}^{\text{upw}}(-\nabla\mu^{m+1};M(u^{m+1})_{\oplus},\overline{u})-a_{h}^{\text{upw}}(\bm{v}(t_{m+1});u^{m+1},\overline{u}).

(30)

Now, since
u_{L}^{m+1}\geq u_{K^{*}}^{m+1}
for all
L\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, we can assure that

M^{\uparrow}(u_{L}^{m+1})\geq M^{\uparrow}(u_{K^{*}}^{m+1})\quad\hbox{and}\quad M^{\downarrow}(u_{L}^{m+1})\leq M^{\downarrow}(u_{K^{*}}^{m+1}).

Then, we can bound as follows:

\displaystyle a_{h}^{\text{upw}}(

\displaystyle-\nabla\mu^{m+1};M(u^{m+1})_{\oplus},\overline{u})=

\displaystyle=\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{i},e=K^{*}\cap L}\int_{e}\left((\left\{\!\!\left\{-\nabla\mu^{m+1}\right\}\!\!\right\}\cdot\bm{n}_{e})_{\oplus}(M^{\uparrow}(u_{K^{*}}^{m+1})+M^{\downarrow}(u_{L}^{m+1}))\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\left.-(\left\{\!\!\left\{-\nabla\mu^{m+1}\right\}\!\!\right\}\cdot\bm{n}_{e})_{\ominus}(M^{\uparrow}(u_{L}^{m+1})+M^{\downarrow}(u_{K^{*}}^{m+1}))\right)(u_{K^{*}}^{m+1})_{\ominus}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{i},e=K^{*}\cap L}\int_{e}\left((\left\{\!\!\left\{-\nabla\mu^{m+1}\right\}\!\!\right\}\cdot\bm{n}_{e})_{\oplus}(M^{\uparrow}(u_{K^{*}}^{m+1})+M^{\downarrow}(u_{K^{*}}^{m+1}))\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\left.-(\left\{\!\!\left\{-\nabla\mu^{m+1}\right\}\!\!\right\}\cdot\bm{n}_{e})_{\ominus}(M^{\uparrow}(u_{K^{*}}^{m+1})+M^{\downarrow}(u_{K^{*}}^{m+1}))\right)(u_{K^{*}}^{m+1})_{\ominus}

\displaystyle=\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{i},e=K^{*}\cap L}\int_{e}(\left\{\!\!\left\{-\nabla\mu^{m+1}\right\}\!\!\right\}\cdot\bm{n}_{e})\left(M(u_{K^{*}}^{m+1})\right)_{\oplus}(u_{K^{*}}^{m+1})_{\ominus}=0.

On the other hand, applying the incompressibility of
\bm{v}
and proceeding as in
Section
2.4
, one has that

a_{h}^{\text{upw}}(\bm{v}(t_{m+1});u^{m+1},\overline{u})\leq 0.

Therefore, from (
30
)

|K^{*}|\delta_{t}u_{K^{*}}^{m+1}(u_{K^{*}}^{m+1})_{\ominus}\geq 0.

Consequently, it is satisfied that

0\leq|K^{*}|(\delta_{t}u_{K^{*}}^{m+1})(u_{K^{*}}^{m+1})_{\ominus}=-\frac{|K^{*}|}{\Delta t}\left((u_{K^{*}}^{m+1})_{\ominus}^{2}+u_{K^{*}}^{m}(u_{K^{*}}^{m+1})_{\ominus}\right)\leq 0,

hence, since
u_{K^{*}}^{m}\geq 0
, we prove that
(u_{K^{*}}^{m+1})_{\ominus}=0
. Hence
u^{m+1}\geq 0
.

Secondly, we prove that
u^{m+1}\leq 1
. Taking the following test function

\displaystyle\overline{u}=\begin{cases}(u_{K^{*}}^{m+1}-1)_{\oplus}&\text{in }K^{*}\\
0&\text{out of }K^{*}\end{cases},

where
K^{*}
is an element of
\mathcal{T}_{h}
such that
u_{K^{*}}^{m+1}=\max_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}u_{K}^{m+1}
and using similar arguments than above, we arrive at

|K^{*}|\delta_{t}u_{K^{*}}^{m+1}(u_{K^{*}}^{m+1}-1)_{\oplus}\leq 0.

Besides, it is satisfied that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\geq|K^{*}|\delta_{t}u_{K^{*}}^{m+1}(u_{K^{*}}^{m+1}-1)_{\oplus}=\frac{|K^{*}|}{\Delta t}\left((u_{K^{*}}^{m+1}-1)+(1-u_{K^{*}}^{m})\right)(u_{K^{*}}^{m+1}-1)_{\oplus}

\displaystyle=\frac{|K^{*}|}{\Delta t}\left((u_{K^{*}}^{m+1}-1)^{2}_{\oplus}+(1-u_{K^{*}}^{m})(u_{K^{*}}^{m+1}-1)_{\oplus}\right)\geq 0,

hence we deduce that
(u_{K^{*}}^{m+1}-1)_{\oplus}=0
and, therefore,

.

Theorem 3.13
.

There is at least one solution of the scheme
(
29
).

Proof.

Given a function
z\in\mathbb{P}^{\text{disc}}_{0}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
with
0\leq z\leq 1
, we define the map

T\colon\mathbb{P}^{\text{disc}}_{0}(\mathcal{T}_{h})\times\mathbb{P}^{\text{cont}}_{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})\times\mathbb{P}^{\text{cont}}_{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})\longrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{\text{disc}}_{0}(\mathcal{T}_{h})\times\mathbb{P}^{\text{cont}}_{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})\times\mathbb{P}^{\text{cont}}_{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})

such that
T(\widehat{u},\widehat{\mu},\widehat{w})=(u,\mu,w)\in\mathbb{P}^{\text{disc}}_{0}(\mathcal{T}_{h})\times\mathbb{P}^{\text{cont}}_{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})\times\mathbb{P}^{\text{cont}}_{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
is the unique solution, for every
\overline{u}\in\mathbb{P}^{\text{disc}}_{0}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
,
\overline{\mu},\overline{w}\in\mathbb{P}^{\text{cont}}_{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
, of the linear (and decoupled) scheme:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Delta t}\left(u-z,\overline{u}\right)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle+a_{h}^{\text{upw}}(\bm{v};u,\overline{u})=-a_{h}^{\text{upw}}(-\nabla\widehat{\mu};M(\widehat{u})_{\oplus},\overline{u}),

(31a)

\displaystyle\left(\mu,\overline{\mu}\right)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{2}\left(\nabla w,\nabla\overline{\mu}\right)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\left(f(u,z),\overline{\mu}\right)_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

(31b)

\displaystyle\left(w,\overline{w}\right)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\left(u,\overline{w}\right)_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

(31c)

To check that
T
is well defined, one may use the following steps. First, it is easy to prove that there is a unique solution
u
of (
31a
) which implies that there is a unique solution
w
of (
31c
) using, for instance, the Lax-Milgram theorem. Then, it is straightforward to see that the solution
\mu
of (
31b
) is unique, which implies its existence as
\mathbb{P}^{\text{cont}}_{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
is a finite-dimensional space.

It can be proved, using the notion of convergence elementwise, as it was done in Theorem
2.5
and taking into consideration that
\nabla\widehat{\mu}\in\left(\mathbb{P}^{\text{disc}}_{0}(\mathcal{T}_{h})\right)^{d}
, that the operator
T
is continuous, and, therefore, it is compact since
\mathbb{P}^{\text{disc}}_{0}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
and
\mathbb{P}^{\text{cont}}_{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
have finite dimension.

Finally, let us prove that the set

B=\{(u,\mu,w)\in\mathbb{P}^{\text{disc}}_{0}(\mathcal{T}_{h})\times\mathbb{P}^{\text{cont}}_{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})\times\mathbb{P}^{\text{cont}}_{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})\colon\\
(u,\mu,w)=\alpha T(u,\mu,w)\text{ for some }0\leq\alpha\leq 1\}

is bounded (independent of
\alpha
). The case
\alpha=0
is trivial so we will assume that
\alpha\in(0,1]
.

If
(u,\mu,w)\in B
, then
u\in\mathbb{P}^{\text{disc}}_{0}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
is the solution, for every
\overline{u}\in\mathbb{P}^{\text{disc}}_{0}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
, of

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Delta t}\left(u-\alpha z,\overline{u}\right)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle+a_{h}^{\text{upw}}(\bm{v};u,\overline{u})=-\alpha\,a_{h}^{\text{upw}}(-\nabla\mu;M({u})_{\oplus},\overline{u}).

(32)

Now, testing (
32
) by
\overline{u}=1
, we get that

\int_{\Omega}u=\alpha\int_{\Omega}z,

and, since
0\leq z\leq 1
, and since it can be proved that
0\leq u\leq 1
using the same arguments than in Theorem
3.11
, we get that

\|u\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leq\|z\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}.

Moreover,
w\in\mathbb{P}^{\text{cont}}_{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
is the solution of the equation

\displaystyle\left(w,\overline{w}\right)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\left(u,\overline{w}\right)_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

\displaystyle\forall\overline{w}\in\mathbb{P}^{\text{cont}}_{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h}).

(33)

Testing with
\overline{w}=w
and using that
u\leq 1
and that the norms are equivalent in
\mathbb{P}^{\text{cont}}_{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
, we obtain

\|w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=\left(u,w\right)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\|w\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leq|\Omega|^{1/2}\|w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

hence
\|w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq|\Omega|^{1/2}
holds.

Finally, we will check that
\mu
is bounded.
Regarding that,
\mu\in\mathbb{P}^{\text{cont}}_{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
is the solution of

\displaystyle\left(\mu,\overline{\mu}\right)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{2}\left(\nabla w,\nabla\overline{\mu}\right)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\left(f(u,z),\overline{\mu}\right)_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

\displaystyle\forall\overline{\mu}\in\mathbb{P}^{\text{cont}}_{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h}),

(34)

by testing by
\overline{\mu}=\mu
we get that

\displaystyle\|\mu\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{2}\|\nabla w\|_{\left(L^{2}(\Omega)\right)^{d}}\|\nabla\mu\|_{\left(L^{2}(\Omega)\right)^{d}}+\|f(u,z)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\mu\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{2}\|w\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\|\mu\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\|f(u,z)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\mu\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

The norms are equivalent in the finite-dimensional space
\mathbb{P}^{\text{cont}}_{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
, therefore, there is
C_{\text{cont}}\geq 0
such that

\|\mu\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\varepsilon^{2}C_{\text{cont}}\|w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|f(u,z)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Hence, as
0\leq u,z\leq 1
, we know that
\|f(u,z)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
is bounded, and therefore
\|\mu\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
is bounded.

Since
\mathbb{P}^{\text{disc}}_{0}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
and
\mathbb{P}^{\text{cont}}_{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
are finite-dimensional spaces where all the norms are equivalent, we have proved that
B
is bounded.

Thus, using the Leray-Schauder fixed point theorem
2.6
, there is a solution
(u,\mu,w)
of the scheme
(
29
).
∎

4.2
Error order test

We now introduce the results of a numerical test in which we
study the convergence order of our numerical scheme DG-UPW, verifying experimentally that the expected order is obtained.
For the sake of completeness, we also compare the convergence orders of the two others aforementioned space semidiscretizations: FEM, DG-SIP.
We consider again the same initial conditions than in Subsection
4.1.1
with
\varepsilon=0.01
, see Figure
2
.

First, in the nonconvective case, errors and convergence order
are compared with respect to an approximate solution which is computed using the FEM scheme in a very fine mesh of size

h=1.414\cdot 10^{-3}
and a time step
\Delta t=10^{-6}
(which is taken as the “exact solution”). In this case we have used conforming structured meshes for the space discretization.
The results for the DG-UPW scheme, which are shown in the first row of Table
1
, confirm order 1 (in fact, slightly over 1) in norm
\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}}
.
These results match our expectations for the
P_{0}
approximation of
u^{m+1}
, with the upwind discretization of the nonlinear second-order term.
It is interesting to emphasize that, unexpectedly, the scheme produces kind results in
\|\cdot\|_{H^{1}}
, reaching order 1.
On the other hand, the FEM and DG-SIP schemes reach order 2 in
L^{2}
and order 1 in
H^{1}
norms, as expected (see also Table
1
).

Next, we focus on the case with convection, where we take
\bm{v}=(y,-x)
in the unit ball.

.

h=4\cdot 10^{-3}

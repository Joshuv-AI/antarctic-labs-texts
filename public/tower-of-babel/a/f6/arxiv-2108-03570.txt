Robust 1-bit Compressive Sensing with Partial Gaussian Circulant Matrices and Generative Priors
Zhaoqiang Liu, Subhroshekhar Ghosh, Jun Han, Jonathan Scarlett

Abstract
In 1-bit compressive sensing, each measurement is quantized to a single bit, namely the sign of a linear function of an unknown vector, and the goal is to accurately recover the vector. While it is most popular to assume a standard Gaussian sensing matrix for 1-bit compressive sensing, using structured sensing matrices such as partial Gaussian circulant matrices is of significant practical importance due to their faster matrix operations. In this paper, we provide recovery guarantees for a correlation-based optimization algorithm for robust 1-bit compressive sensing with randomly signed partial Gaussian circulant matrices and generative models. Under suitable assumptions, we match guarantees that were previously only known to hold for i.i.d. Gaussian matrices that require significantly more computation. We make use of a practical iterative algorithm, and perform numerical experiments on image datasets to corroborate our theoretical results.

Problem Setup

Except where stated otherwise, we consider the following setup and assumptions:

•

Let
\mathbf{x}^{*}=[x_{0}^{*},x_{1}^{*},\ldots,x_{n-1}^{*}]^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

•

Fixing any (deterministic) index set

\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{R}_{\Omega}\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{g}}\mathbf{D}_{\bm{\xi}},

(1)

n
-dimensional vector to coordinates indexed by
\Omega

is a partial Gaussian circulant matrix with random column sign flips. For concreteness, we fix the index set

as

, but the same analysis holds for any other fixed set. We also write
\tilde{\mathbf{A}}
as
\tilde{\mathbf{A}}=\mathbf{R}_{\Omega}\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{g}}

•

We assume that the measurements, or response variables,

, are generated according to

b_{i}=\theta\left(\left\langle\mathbf{a}_{i},\mathbf{x}^{*}\right\rangle\right),

(2)

where
\mathbf{a}_{i}^{T}
is the
i
-th row of the sensing matrix

is a (possibly random) function with

–

Random bit flips:

\theta(x)=\tau\cdot\mathrm{sign}{(}x)
, where
\tau
is a
\pm 1
-valued random variable with
\mathbb{P}(\tau=-1)=p<\frac{1}{2}
, independent of
\mathbf{A}
.

–

Random noise before quantization
:
\theta(x)=\mathrm{sign}{(}x+e)
, where

represents a random noise term added before quantization, independent of

. For example, when

•

We assume that

Then, we have

is the probability density function (pdf) of

\displaystyle\lambda=\mathbb{E}[\theta(g)g]=\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{E}[\theta(g)g|g]]=\mathbb{E}[\phi(g)g]=\mathbb{E}[\phi^{\prime}(g)]=2\mathbb{E}[f_{e}(-g)]>0.

In particular, when

•

For the case of random noise added before quantization, we additionally make the following assumptions:

III
Main Results

Recall that the
i
-th observation is
b_{i}=\theta(\langle\mathbf{a}_{i},\mathbf{x}^{*}\rangle)
,
\tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{*}=\mathbf{D}_{\bm{\xi}}\mathbf{x}^{*}
, and
\tilde{\mathbf{A}}=\mathbf{R}_{\Omega}\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{g}}
, i.e., a partial Gaussian circulant matrix without random column sign flips. We have the following important lemma.

Lemma 1
.

For any
t>0
satisfying
m=\Omega\left(t+\log n\right)
, suppose that the unit vector
\mathbf{x}^{*}
satisfies
\|\mathbf{x}^{*}\|_{\infty}\leq\rho
with
\rho=O\big(\frac{1}{m(t+\log m)}\big)
. Suppose that
\mathbf{b}=\theta(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}^{*})
with
\theta(x)=\tau\cdot\mathrm{sign}{(}x)
or
\theta(x)=\mathrm{sign}{(}x+e)
(cf. Sec.
II
), and
\mathbf{A}
is a partial Gaussian circulant matrix with random column sign flips. Then, with probability at least
1-e^{-\Omega(t)}-e^{-\Omega(\sqrt{m})}
,

\left\|\frac{1}{m}\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^{T}\mathbf{b}-\lambda\tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{*}\right\|_{\infty}\leq O\left(\sqrt{\frac{t+\log n}{m}}\right).

(8)

Based on Lemma
1
, and following the proof techniques used in
[
46
]
(see Section
IV-C
for the details), we have the following theorem concerning the recovery guarantee for noisy 1-bit CS with partial Gaussian circulant matrices and generative priors.

Theorem 1
.

Consider any
\mathbf{x}^{*}\in\mathcal{K}
with
\mathcal{K}=G(B_{2}^{k}(r))
for some
L
-Lipschitz generative model
G\,:\,B_{2}^{k}(r)\to\mathcal{S}^{n-1}
. Fix
\delta>0
satisfying
\delta=O\big(\frac{\sqrt{k\log\frac{Lr}{\delta}}}{\lambda\sqrt{m}}+\frac{\varsigma}{\lambda}\big)
, and suppose that
Lr=\Omega(\delta n)
,
n=\Omega\big(m\big)
, and the signal
\mathbf{x}^{*}
satisfies
\|\mathbf{x}^{*}\|_{\infty}\leq\rho=O\big(\frac{1}{mk\log\frac{Lr}{\delta}}\big)
. Suppose that all other conditions are the same as those in Lemma
1
. Let
\hat{\mathbf{x}}
be a solution to the correlation-based optimization problem (
7
). Then, if

m=\Omega\left(k\log\frac{Lr}{\delta}\cdot\left(1+\mathbbm{1}(\varsigma>0)S_{\varsigma}\right)\right),

(9)

where
S_{\varsigma}=\big(\log^{2}n\big)\cdot\big(\log^{2}\big(k\log\frac{Lr}{\delta}\big)\big)
is a sample complexity term concerning the adversarial noise, with probability at least
1-e^{-\Omega(k\log\frac{Lr}{\delta})}-e^{-\Omega(\sqrt{m})}-\mathbbm{1}(\varsigma>0)\cdot e^{-\Omega((\log^{2}(k\log\frac{Lr}{\delta}))(\log^{2}n))}
, it holds that

\|\hat{\mathbf{x}}-\mathbf{x}^{*}\|_{2}\leq O\left(\frac{\sqrt{k\log\frac{Lr}{\delta}}}{\lambda\sqrt{m}}+\frac{\varsigma}{\lambda}\right).

(10)

Note that the extra logarithmic terms
\log^{2}(k\log\frac{Lr}{\delta})
and
\log^{2}n
in (
9
) are only required for deriving an upper bound corresponding to the adversarial noise. If there is no adversarial noise, i.e.,
\varsigma=0
, the sample complexity in (
9
) becomes
m=\Omega\big(k\log\frac{Lr}{\delta}\big)
, and it follows from (
10
) that
O\big(\frac{k\log\frac{Lr}{\delta}}{\lambda^{2}\epsilon^{2}}\big)
samples suffice for an
\epsilon
-accurate recovery. This matches the scaling derived for i.i.d. Gaussian measurements in
[
44
]
.

Remark 1
.

As mentioned in the remark in
[
24
, Page 8]
, for “typical” unit vectors, we have
\|\mathbf{x}^{*}\|_{\infty}=O\big(\frac{\log n}{\sqrt{n}}\big)
. Thus, assuming
m=\Theta\big(k\log\frac{Lr}{\delta}\big)
, we find that the condition
\rho=O\big(\frac{1}{mk\log\frac{Lr}{\delta}}\big)
holds for typical signals when
\frac{1}{(k\log\frac{Lr}{\delta})^{2}}\gg\frac{\log n}{\sqrt{n}}
. Assuming
\frac{Lr}{\delta}={\rm poly}(n)

[
3
]
, it in turn suffices that
k=O(n^{\theta})
for some
\theta\in\big(0,\frac{1}{4}\big)
. In addition, for an arbitrary unit vector
\mathbf{x}^{*}
, we can use a simple trick to ensure the
\ell_{\infty}
bound with high probability: Instead of using a randomly signed partial Gaussian circulant sensing matrix
\mathbf{A}
, we can use
\mathbf{A}\mathbf{H}
as the sensing matrix, where
\mathbf{H}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
is a randomly signed Hadamard matrix that also allows fast matrix-vector multiplications. Then, the observation vector becomes
\mathbf{b}=\theta(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{x}^{*})
. Since
\mathbf{H}\mathbf{x}^{*}
satisfies the desired
\ell_{\infty}
bound with high probability
[
50
, Lemma 3.1]
, for any
\hat{\mathbf{x}}
that solves (
7
),
\|\hat{\mathbf{x}}-\mathbf{H}\mathbf{x}^{*}\|_{2}
satisfies the upper bound in (
10
). Because
\mathbf{H}
is symmetric and orthogonal, we have the same upper bound for
\|\mathbf{H}\hat{\mathbf{x}}-\mathbf{x}^{*}\|_{2}
.

Remark 2
.

The use of random column sign flips and a randomly signed Hadamard matrix is most suited to applications where one has the freedom to choose any measurement matrix, but is limited by computation. In this sense, the design is more practical than the widespread i.i.d. Gaussian choice, though it may remain unsuitable in applications such as medical imaging where one must use subsampled Fourier measurements without sign flips or the Hadamard operator.

IV-A

General Auxiliary Results

Definition 1
.

A random variable
X
is said to be sub-Gaussian if there exists a positive constant
C
such that
\left(\mathbb{E}\left[|X|^{p}\right]\right)^{1/p}\leq C\sqrt{p}
for all

is defined as
\|X\|_{\psi_{2}}:=\sup_{p\geq 1}p^{-1/2}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[|X|^{p}\right]\right)^{1/p}
.

We have the following concentration inequality for sub-Gaussian random variables.

Lemma 2
.

(Hoeffding-type inequality
[
51
, Proposition 5.10]
)
Let
X_{1},\ldots,X_{N}
be independent zero-mean sub-Gaussian random variables, and let
K=\max_{i}\|X_{i}\|_{\psi_{2}}
. Then, for any
\bm{\alpha}=[\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\ldots,\alpha_{N}]^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and any
t\geq 0
, it holds that

\mathbb{P}\left(\Big|\sum_{i=1}^{N}\alpha_{i}X_{i}\Big|\geq t\right)\leq\exp\left(1-\frac{ct^{2}}{K^{2}\|\bm{\alpha}\|_{2}^{2}}\right).

(11)

In particular, we have the following lemma concerning the Hoeffding’s inequality for (real) Rademacher sums.

Lemma 3
.

(
[
52
, Proposition 6.11]
)

Let
\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
\bm{\epsilon}=(\epsilon_{j})_{j\in[N]}
be a Rademacher sequence. Then, for
u>0
,

\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\sum_{j=1}^{N}\epsilon_{j}b_{j}\right|\geq\|\mathbf{b}\|_{2}u\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{u^{2}}{2}\right).

(12)

We also use the following well-established fact based on the Riesz-Thorin interpolation theorem.

Lemma 4
.

(
[
53
]
)

For any matrix
\mathbf{A}
,

\|\mathbf{A}\|_{2\to 2}\leq\max\left\{\|\mathbf{A}\|_{1\to 1},\|\mathbf{A}\|_{\infty\to\infty}\right\}.

(13)

Next, we present the Hanson-Wright inequality, which gives a tail bound for quadratic forms in independent Gaussian variables.

Lemma 5
.

(Hanson-Wright inequality
[
54
]
)
If
\mathbf{g}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a standard Gaussian vector and
\mathbf{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
, then for any

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\mathbf{g}^{T}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{g}-\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbf{g}^{T}\mathbf{B}\mathbf{g}\right]\right|\geq t\right)\leq\exp\left(-c\cdot\min\left\{\frac{t^{2}}{\|\mathbf{B}\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}},\frac{t}{\|\mathbf{B}\|_{2\to 2}}\right\}\right).

\left\langle\frac{1}{m}\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^{T}\tilde{\mathbf{b}}-\lambda\tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{*},\mathbf{D}_{\bm{\xi}}\hat{\mathbf{x}}-\tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{*}\right\rangle+\langle\lambda\tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{*},\mathbf{D}_{\bm{\xi}}\hat{\mathbf{x}}-\tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{*}\rangle\geq 0.

(109)

By the assumption that
G(B_{2}^{k}(r))\subseteq\mathcal{S}^{n-1}
, we obtain that
\|\mathbf{D}_{\bm{\xi}}\hat{\mathbf{x}}\|_{2}=\|\hat{\mathbf{x}}\|_{2}=1
, and

\langle\lambda\tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{*},\tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{*}-\mathbf{D}_{\bm{\xi}}\hat{\mathbf{x}}\rangle=\lambda\langle\mathbf{x}^{*},\mathbf{x}^{*}-\hat{\mathbf{x}}\rangle=\frac{\lambda}{2}\|\mathbf{x}^{*}-\hat{\mathbf{x}}\|_{2}^{2}.

(110)

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda}{2}\|\mathbf{x}^{*}-\hat{\mathbf{x}}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left|\left\langle\frac{1}{m}\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^{T}(\tilde{\mathbf{b}}-\mathbf{b}),\mathbf{D}_{\bm{\xi}}\hat{\mathbf{x}}-\tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{*}\right\rangle\right|+\left|\left\langle\frac{1}{m}\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^{T}\mathbf{b}-\lambda\tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{*},\mathbf{D}_{\bm{\xi}}\hat{\mathbf{x}}-\tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{*}\right\rangle\right|

(111)

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}(\tilde{\mathbf{b}}-\mathbf{b})\right\|_{2}\cdot\left\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}\mathbf{A}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}-\mathbf{x}^{*})\right\|_{2}+\left|\left\langle\frac{1}{m}\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^{T}\mathbf{b}-\lambda\tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{*},\mathbf{D}_{\bm{\xi}}\hat{\mathbf{x}}-\tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{*}\right\rangle\right|.

(112)

Setting
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}
in Lemma
14
, we have that if
m=\Omega(k\log\frac{Lr}{\delta}(\log^{2}(k\log\frac{Lr}{\delta}))(\log^{2}n))
, with probability at least
1-e^{-\Omega(k\log\frac{Lr}{\delta})}-e^{-\Omega((\log^{2}(k\log\frac{Lr}{\delta}))(\log^{2}n))}
,

\left\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}\mathbf{A}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}-\mathbf{x}^{*})\right\|_{2}\leq O(\|\hat{\mathbf{x}}-\mathbf{x}^{*}\|_{2}+\delta).

(113)

Moreover, by Lemma
11
, with probability
1-e^{-\Omega(k\log\frac{Lr}{\delta})}-e^{-\Omega(\sqrt{m})}
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle\frac{1}{m}\tilde{\mathbf{A}}^{T}\mathbf{b}-\lambda\tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{*},\mathbf{D}_{\bm{\xi}}\hat{\mathbf{x}}-\tilde{\mathbf{x}}^{*}\right\rangle\right|\leq O\left(\sqrt{\frac{k\log\frac{Lr}{\delta}}{m}}\right)(\|\hat{\mathbf{x}}-\mathbf{x}^{*}\|_{2}+\delta).

(114)

Recall that we assume
\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}\|\tilde{\mathbf{b}}-\mathbf{b}\|_{2}\leq\varsigma
. Combining (
112
), (
113
) and (
114
), we obtain with probability at least
1-e^{-\Omega(k\log\frac{Lr}{\delta})}-e^{-\Omega((\log^{2}(k\log\frac{Lr}{\delta}))(\log^{2}n))}-e^{-\Omega(\sqrt{m})}
that

\frac{\lambda}{2}\|\mathbf{x}^{*}-\hat{\mathbf{x}}\|_{2}^{2}\leq O\left(\sqrt{\frac{k\log\frac{Lr}{\delta}}{m}}+\varsigma\right)(\|\hat{\mathbf{x}}-\mathbf{x}^{*}\|_{2}+\delta).

(115)

Then, by considering both possible cases of which of the two terms in (
115
) is larger, we find that the desired result holds when
\delta=O\big(\frac{\sqrt{k\log\frac{Lr}{\delta}}}{\lambda\sqrt{m}}+\frac{\varsigma}{\lambda}\big)
.
∎

[

We follow
[

300

\mu=0.2

\mathbf{x}^{(t)}

\mathbf{x}^{(t)}

20

30

VI
Conclusion and Future Work

We have established a sample complexity upper bound for noisy 1-bit compressed sensing for the case of using a randomly signed partial Gaussian circulant sensing matrix and a generative prior. The sample complexity upper bound matches that obtained previously for i.i.d. Gaussian sensing matrices, and our scheme is robust to adversarial noise.

We focused only on non-uniform recovery guarantees, and uniform recovery is an immediate direction for further research. Relaxing the
\ell_{\infty}
assumption and allowing for general scalings of
k
(not only
k\ll n^{1/4}
) would also be of significant interest. Finally, handling Fourier measurements without column sign flips would be of significant interest for applications where the measurement matrix is constrained as such.

Appendix A
Proof of Lemma
7

For any
\varepsilon>0
, from Lemma
6
and the union bound, with probability at least
1-m^{2}e^{-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{8\rho^{2}}}
, it holds that

|\langle\mathbf{s}_{i},\mathbf{s}_{j}\rangle|\leq\varepsilon

(117)

for all
0\leq i\neq j<m
. Setting
m^{2}e^{-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{8\rho^{2}}}=\nu
, we obtain

\varepsilon=2\sqrt{2}\rho\sqrt{\log\frac{m^{2}}{\nu}}.

(118)

Let
\mathbf{B}=[\mathbf{s}_{0},\mathbf{s}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{s}_{m-1}]\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
. Then, all the diagonal entries of
\mathbf{B}^{T}\mathbf{B}
are
1
, and the magnitude of all the off-diagonal entries are upper bounded by
\varepsilon
. By the Gershgorin circle theorem
[
62
]
, we have

\sigma_{m}(\mathbf{B}^{T}\mathbf{B})\geq 1-(m-1)\varepsilon>1-m\varepsilon,

(119)

where
\sigma_{m}(\cdot)
denotes the
m
-th largest singular value of a matrix. For any
i\in\{1,\ldots,m-2\}
, and any unit vector
\mathbf{t}
in the span of
\mathbf{s}_{0},\ldots,\mathbf{s}_{i-1}
, we write
\mathbf{t}
as
\mathbf{t}=\sum_{j<i}\alpha_{j}\mathbf{s}_{j}
. Let
\bm{\alpha}_{i}=[\alpha_{0},\ldots,\alpha_{i-1}]^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{i}
and
\mathbf{B}_{i}=[\mathbf{s}_{0},\mathbf{s}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{s}_{i-1}]\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times i}
. Similarly to (
119
), we have

\sigma_{i}(\mathbf{B}_{i}^{T}\mathbf{B}_{i})\geq 1-(i-1)\varepsilon>1-m\varepsilon.

(120)

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=\|\mathbf{B}_{i}\bm{\alpha}_{i}\|_{2}^{2}\geq\sigma_{i}^{2}(\mathbf{B}_{i})\|\bm{\alpha}_{i}\|_{2}^{2}=\sigma_{i}(\mathbf{B}_{i}^{T}\mathbf{B}_{i})\|\bm{\alpha}_{i}\|_{2}^{2}\geq(1-m\varepsilon)\|\bm{\alpha}_{i}\|_{2}^{2},

(121)

and hence

\|\bm{\alpha}_{i}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{1-m\varepsilon}.

(122)

In addition,

\displaystyle\langle\mathbf{s}_{i},\mathbf{t}\rangle^{2}=\left(\sum_{j<i}\alpha_{j}\langle\mathbf{s}_{i},\mathbf{s}_{j}\rangle\right)^{2}\leq\|\bm{\alpha}_{i}\|_{2}^{2}\left(\sum_{j<i}\langle\mathbf{s}_{i},\mathbf{s}_{j}\rangle^{2}\right)

(123)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{1-m\varepsilon}\cdot i\varepsilon^{2}<\frac{m\varepsilon^{2}}{1-m\varepsilon}.

(124)

By definition, the squared length of the projection of
\mathbf{s}_{i}
onto the span of
\mathbf{s}_{0},\ldots,\mathbf{s}_{i-1}
is equal to
\max\{\langle\mathbf{t},\mathbf{s}_{i}\rangle^{2}\,:\,\mathbf{t}\in\mathrm{span}\{\mathbf{s}_{0},\ldots,\mathbf{s}_{i-1}\},\|\mathbf{t}\|_{2}=1\}
. Hence, the projection of
\mathbf{s}_{i}
onto the span of
\mathbf{s}_{0},\ldots,\mathbf{s}_{i-1}
has squared length at most
\frac{m\varepsilon^{2}}{1-m\varepsilon}
. Then, if

2\sqrt{2}m\rho\log\frac{m^{2}}{\nu}<\frac{1}{2},

(125)

recalling that
\varepsilon=2\sqrt{2}\rho\sqrt{\log\frac{m^{2}}{\nu}}
, we also have
m\varepsilon<\frac{1}{2}
, and

\displaystyle\frac{m\varepsilon^{2}}{1-m\varepsilon}<2m\varepsilon^{2}=16m\rho^{2}\log\frac{m^{2}}{\nu}

(126)

\displaystyle=(2\sqrt{2}\rho)\times(4\sqrt{2}m\rho\log\frac{m^{2}}{\nu})\leq 2\sqrt{2}\rho<4\rho.

(127)

We can now establish Lemma
7
by performing the following procedure on the vectors (essentially Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization):

1.

Initialize:
\mathbf{u}_{0}=\mathbf{s}_{0}
,
\mathbf{r}_{0}=\mathbf{0}
.

2.

For
i=2,3,\ldots,m-1
, we set
\mathbf{r}_{i}
to be the projection of
\mathbf{s}_{i}
onto the span of
\mathbf{s}_{0},\ldots,\mathbf{s}_{i-1}
, and
\mathbf{u}_{i}=\mathbf{s}_{i}-\mathbf{r}_{i}
.

From (
118
) and (
127
), we obtain the desired result.

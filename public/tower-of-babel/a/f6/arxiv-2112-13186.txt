I
Introduction

Neutrino oscillations have been well established by experimental data over the last three decades. Oscillation physics is now in the precision era, where the values of the various parameters that affect the oscillation probabilities are being measured to an unprecedented level of precision. Neutrino mixing is described by the unitary
3\times 3
PMNS matrix:

U=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}c_{13}c_{12}&s_{12}c_{13}&s_{13}e^{-i\delta_{\mathrm{CP}}}\\
-s_{12}c_{23}-c_{12}s_{23}s_{13}e^{i\delta_{\mathrm{CP}}}&c_{12}c_{23}-s_{12}s_{23}s_{13}e^{i\delta_{\mathrm{CP}}}&s_{23}c_{13}\\
s_{12}s_{23}-s_{13}c_{12}c_{23}e^{i\delta_{\mathrm{CP}}}&-c_{12}s_{23}-s_{13}c_{23}s_{12}e^{i\delta_{\mathrm{CP}}}&c_{23}c_{13}\\
\end{array}\right],

(1)

where
c_{ij}=\cos\theta_{ij}
and
s_{ij}=\sin\theta_{ij}
. Neutrino oscillation probabilities depend on three mixing angles:
{\theta_{12}}
,
{\theta_{13}}
, and
{\theta_{23}}
; two independent mass squared differences
{\Delta_{21}}=m_{2}^{2}-m_{1}^{2}
and
{\Delta_{31}}=m_{3}^{2}-m_{1}^{2}
, where
m_{i}
is the mass of the neutrino mass eigenstate
\nu_{i}
; and a CP-violating phase,
\delta_{\mathrm{CP}}
. Among these parameters,
{\theta_{12}}
and
{\Delta_{21}}
have been measured in solar neutrino experiments
Bahcall et al. 2004
;
Ahmad et al. 2002
;
Fukuda et al. 1998
, and in long baseline reactor neutrino experiment KamLAND
Abe et al. 2008
. The long-baseline accelerator neutrino experiment MINOS
Nichol 2012a
measured
\sin^{2}2{\theta_{23}}
and
|{\Delta_{31}}|
by measuring
\nu_{\mu}
survival probabilities
1
1

1

To be precise, MINOS used the
\nu_{\mu}
disappearance channel to measure the effective atmospheric mixing parameters
\Delta_{\mu\mu}
and
\theta_{\mu\mu}

Nunokawa et al. 2005
;
Raut 2012
.
{\theta_{13}}
has been measured in reactor neutrino experiments
An et al. 2012
;
Ahn et al. 2012
;
Abe et al. 2012
. The current unknowns are the sign of
{\Delta_{31}}
, the octant of
{\theta_{23}}
and the CP-violating phase
\delta_{\mathrm{CP}}
. Depending on the sign of
{\Delta_{31}}
, there can be two possible mass orderings of the neutrino mass eigenstates: normal hierarchy (NH) which implies
m_{3}>>m_{2}>m_{1}
, and inverted hierarchy (IH) which implies
m_{2}>m_{1}>>m_{3}

De Salas et al. 2018
. Similarly, for
\sin^{2}2{\theta_{23}}
, there can be two possibilities: lower octant (LO), i.e.
\sin^{2}{\theta_{23}}<0.5
and higher octant (HO), i.e.
\sin^{2}{\theta_{23}}>0.5
.
The values of the mixing parameters are important as discriminators between new physics models that may be invoked to explain the tiny non-zero masses of the neutrinos
Albright and Chen 2006
. For models involving neutrinos of a Majorana nature, the mass ordering can have implications on the observability of processes like neutrinoless double beta-decay
Dolinski et al. 2019
. Further, the neutrino masses have significance for the energy density, and hence the expansion rate of the Universe. Constraints on (the sum of) neutrino masses also arise from cosmological observations
Hannestad 2010
, though these are model-dependent.

The currently running long-baseline accelerator neutrino oscillation experiments NO
\nu
A
Ayres et al. 2004
and T2K
Itow et al. 2001
are expected to measure these unknown quantities. In Neutrino 2020 conference, both the experiments released their latest data
Himmel 2020
;
Acero et al. 2021
;
Dunne 2020
;
Abe et al. 2021
. Each experiment has four data sets:

1.

\nu_{\mu}
disappearance data:
\nu_{\mu}\to\nu_{\mu}
,

2.

\bar{\nu}_{\mu}
disappearance data:
\bar{\nu}_{\mu}\to\bar{\nu}_{\mu}
,

3.

\nu_{e}
appearance data:
\nu_{\mu}\to\nu_{e}
, and

4.

\bar{\nu}_{e}
appearance data:
\bar{\nu}_{\mu}\to\bar{\nu}_{e}
.

In table
1
, we have listed the best-fit values of the oscillation parameters measured by both the experiments. It is obvious that both the experiments agree on the best-fit value of
|\Delta_{32}|
which essentially comes from the
\nu_{\mu}
disappearance data. They also nearly agree on the best-fit value of
\sin^{2}{\theta_{23}}
at
1\,\sigma
. However there is a strong disagreement between the best-fit values of
\delta_{\mathrm{CP}}
. Moreover, there is no overlap between the
1\,\sigma
allowed regions on the
\sin^{2}{\theta_{23}}-\delta_{\mathrm{CP}}
plane, as shown in Ref.
Himmel 2020
. However, the
3\,\sigma
allowed regions are quite similar. Moreover, if IH is assumed to be the correct hierarchy, the allowed regions of the two experiments essentially coincide. Although both the experiments prefer NH over IH, the best-fit point for IH has a
\Delta\chi^{2}\simeq 1
compared to the best-fit point at NH. In Ref.
Kelly et al. 2021
, it has been shown that the combined analysis of NO
\nu
A and T2K has a minimum for IH instead of NH. The question of the physical origin of this discrepancy is still open. Joint analyses are also planned between the NO
\nu
A and T2K collaborations with the aim of obtaining better constraints on oscillation parameters due to resolved degeneracy and to understand the non-trivial systematic correlations between them
Berns 2021
.

There have been studies attempting to explain this discrepancy with beyond standard model (BSM) physics. Ref.
Miranda et al. 2021
has tried to resolve the tension with non-unitary neutrino mixing while Ref.
Chatterjee and Palazzo 2021
;
Denton et al. 2021
have done the same with non-standard neutrino-matter NC interaction during propagation. In Ref.
Rahaman 2021
an effort has been made to resolve the tension with CPT violating Lorentz invariance violation. All these papers give a hint towards the presence of BSM physics in the present NO
\nu
A and T2K data. The information obtained from these two experiments comes from the four oscillation channels listed above. If the tension between the data from NO
\nu
A and T2K is indeed a signature of new physics, it is crucial to understand which channel or combination of channels is responsible for this difference. Alternately, if the difference is purely a statistical or systematic artifact, it is important to identify the channel that is the source of this effect. Thus it is important for both model-building and understanding the experimental systematics, to be certain about the origin of the tension. Previously a similar study was done with the 2018 data from NO
\nu
A and T2K
Nizam et al. 2019
.

In this paper, we try to identify the disagreement between the best-fit values of
\delta_{\mathrm{CP}}
. Details of our analysis are discussed in section
II
. We present our result after analysing NO
\nu
A and T2K data in section
III
. An explanation of the tension based on parameter degeneracy has been given in section
IV
. In section
V
, future prospects of the long-baseline experiments are discussed and we have tried to determine if the tension can be established with greater statistical significance in future. The conclusions are presented in section
VI
.

0.084

.
\Delta_{\mu\mu}

\Delta_{\mu\mu}=\sin^{2}{\theta_{23}}{\Delta_{31}}+\cos^{2}{\theta_{12}}\Delta_{32}+\cos\delta_{\mathrm{CP}}\sin 2{\theta_{12}}\sin{\theta_{13}}\tan{\theta_{12}}{\Delta_{21}}.

(2)

We calculated the theoretical event rates and the
\chi^{2}
between data and theoretical event rates using GLoBES
Huber et al. 2005
;
Huber et al. 2007
. The experimental data have been taken from
Himmel 2020
;
Acero et al. 2021
;
Dunne 2020
;
Abe et al. 2021
. To calculate the theoretical event rates, we fixed the signal and background efficiencies by matching with the Monte-Carlo simulations given by the collaborations
Himmel 2020
;
Acero et al. 2021
;
Dunne 2020
;
Abe et al. 2021
. Automatic bin-based energy smearing for generated theoretical events has been implemented within GLoBES
Huber et al. 2005
;
Huber et al. 2007
using a Gaussian smearing function

R^{c}(E,E^{\prime})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-\frac{(E-E^{\prime})^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}(E)}},

(3)

where
E^{\prime}
is the reconstructed energy. The energy resolution function is given by

\sigma(E)=\alpha E+\beta\sqrt{E}+\gamma.

(4)

For NO
\nu
A, we used
\alpha=0.11\,(0.09)
,
\beta=\gamma=0
for electron (muon) like events
Acero et al. 2018
;
Acero et al. 2019
. For T2K, we used
\alpha=0
,
\beta=0.075
,
\gamma=0.05
for both electron and muon like events.

After the calculation of theoretical event rates,
\chi^{2}
s were calculated using the expression:

\displaystyle\chi^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sum_{i}\left\{(1+z)N_{i}^{\rm th}-N_{i}^{\rm exp}+N_{i}^{\rm exp}\ln\left[\frac{N_{i}^{\rm exp}}{(1+z)N_{i}^{\rm th}}\right]\right\}+2\sum_{j}(1+z)N_{j}^{\rm th}+z^{2}\,\,

where the index
i
represents the bins for which
N_{i}^{\rm exp}\neq 0
, and
j
represents the bins for which
N_{j}^{\rm exp}=0
. The parameter
z
defines the additional systematic uncertainties. For both NO
\nu
A and T2K, the relevant systematic uncertainties are

•

5\%
normalisation and
5\%
energy callibration systematics uncertainty for
e
-like events, and

•

5\%
normalisation and
0.01\%
energy callibration systematics uncertainty for
\mu
-like events.

In calculating
\chi^{2}
we added (the older, pre-2020) priors on
\sin^{2}2{\theta_{13}}
,
\sin^{2}2{\theta_{23}}
, and
|\Delta_{\mu\mu}|
, in cases where we have not included muon disappearance data. This is done to prevent ‘overcounting’ of information obtained from the disappearance channels of the NO
\nu
A and T2K dataset. In case of
\sin^{2}2{\theta_{13}}
, and
\sin^{2}{\theta_{23}}
, we used the priors from 2019 global analysis
nuf
;
Esteban et al. 2019
, while for
|\Delta_{\mu\mu}|
we have used the priors from the 2012 MINOS result
Nichol 2012b
. In all other cases, priors have been added on
\sin^{2}2{\theta_{13}}
only (to account for electron disappearance data from reactor neutrino experiments). After calculating
\chi^{2}
, we have marginalised it over all the oscillation parameters including the hierarchy, and subtracted it from the
\chi^{2}
s to calculate
\Delta\chi^{2}
.

While doing the analysis of future experiments, we calculated the true experimental event rates with NO
\nu
A and T2K current best-fit points and calculated test event rates and
\Delta\chi^{2}
as described above. In case of simulations,
\chi^{2}
and
\Delta\chi^{2}
are essentially equivalent.

III.4
Analysing the data with vacuum oscillation hypothesis

The matter effect is included in the neutrino oscillation probabilities through the Wolfenstein matter term
Wolfenstein 1978

A=2\sqrt{2}G_{F}N_{e}E=0.76\times 10^{-4}\rho({\rm g/cc})E(GeV)\,\,({\rm in\,\,eV^{2}}),

(6)

where
E
is the neutrino beam energy,
N_{e}
is the electron density in matter, and
\rho
is the earth matter density. Matter effect plays a crucial role in solving the solar neutrino problem
Mikheyev and Smirnov 1985
;
Mikheev and Smirnov 1986
;
Bahcall et al. 2004
. Existence of matter effect has been established at more than
5\,\sigma
C.L. for oscillations driven by
{\Delta_{21}}

Fogli et al. 2006
. However, there is still no significant evidence of matter effect in the case of oscillations driven by
{\Delta_{31}}

Bharti et al. 2021
. It is expected that in case of atmospheric neutrinos, the survival probabilities
P(\nu_{\mu}\rightarrow\nu_{\mu})
and
P(\bar{\nu}_{\mu}\rightarrow\bar{\nu}_{\mu})
will undergo significant changes due to matter effect
Abe et al. 2018
. However, at present Super-Kamiokande can only distinguish between vacuum oscillation and matter effect at
2\,\sigma

Abe et al. 2018
. For baselines of the order of 1000 km, changes in survival probabilities due to matter effect are negligible
Gandhi et al. 2007
. However, the oscillation probabilities
P(\nu_{\mu}\rightarrow\nu_{e})
and
P(\bar{\nu}_{\mu}\rightarrow\bar{\nu}_{e})
undergo large changes due to matter effect. In cases of experiments like NO
\nu
A and T2K, the sensitivities to mass hierarchy,
\delta_{\mathrm{CP}}
and octant of
{\theta_{23}}
come from measuring these two oscillation probabilities
Lipari 2000
;
Narayan and Sankar 2000
. Since T2K has a smaller baseline (and correspondingly, lower energy and matter density), the change in oscillation probabilities due to matter effect is also smaller. Therefore, the measured values of the unknown oscillation parameters in T2K do not depend significantly on whether matter effect is considered or not
Bharti et al. 2021
. However, for NO
\nu
A , the changes in oscillation probabilities due to matter effect are comparable to the changes due to the shift of
\delta_{\mathrm{CP}}
value by
90^{\circ}

Bharti et al. 2018
. That is why the
\delta_{\mathrm{CP}}
values measured in NO
\nu
A are highly dependent on whether matter effect is considered or not. Just like the oscillations driven by two different mass-squared differences have been established, it is extremely important to establish the matter effects in the case of oscillations driven by
{\Delta_{31}}
at the same level of significance as those driven by
{\Delta_{21}}
.

In Ref.
Bharti et al. 2021
, it was shown that the 2019 data of NO
\nu
A and T2K could not disfavour vacuum oscillation even at
1\,\sigma
.
We have done an analysis of the full 2020 data from each of the two experiments with vacuum oscillation hypothesis and the results are shown in Fig.
8
. As expected, if the data are analysed with vacuum oscillation hypothesis, a distinction between NH and IH cannot be made. The minimum
\chi^{2}
for NO
\nu
A (T2K) with 50 (88) bins is 48.94 (95.85). The minimum
\chi^{2}
for the combined analysis with 138 bins is 147.02. Therefore, vacuum oscillation gives as good fit to the data as oscillation with matter effect. From Fig.
8
, it can be observed that if we use vacuum oscillation hypothesis, there is a compatibility between the two experiments at
1\,\sigma
.

IV
Explaining the tension with parameter degeneracy

We will try to explain the tension between the two experiments in terms of the effects of oscillation parameters degeneracy on the (anti-) electron neutrino appearance events. To do so, let us first look at the
\nu_{\mu}\to\nu_{e}
oscillation probability for a neutrino of energy
E
traversing a distance
L

Cervera et al. 2000
:

\displaystyle P_{\mu e}

\displaystyle\simeq\sin^{2}2{\theta_{13}}\sin^{2}{\theta_{23}}\frac{\sin^{2}\hat{\Delta}(1-\hat{A})}{(1-\hat{A})^{2}}

(7)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\alpha\cos{\theta_{13}}\sin 2{\theta_{12}}\sin 2{\theta_{13}}\sin 2{\theta_{23}}\cos(\hat{\Delta}+\delta_{\mathrm{CP}})

\displaystyle\frac{\sin\hat{\Delta}\hat{A}}{\hat{A}}\frac{\sin\hat{\Delta}(1-\hat{A})}{1-\hat{A}},

where
\alpha=\frac{{\Delta_{21}}}{{\Delta_{31}}}
,
\hat{\Delta}=\frac{{\Delta_{31}}L}{4E}

and
\hat{A}=\frac{A}{{\Delta_{31}}}
. The antineutrino oscillation probability
P_{\bar{\mu}\bar{e}}
can be obtained by changing the sign of
A
and
\delta_{\mathrm{CP}}
in eq.
7
. The oscillation probability formula shows the dependence on mass hierarchy, octant of
{\theta_{23}}
and
\delta_{\mathrm{CP}}
.

From eq.
7
, it can be easily seen that the dominant term in
P_{\mu e}
is proportional to
\sin^{2}2{\theta_{13}}
, and thus this probability is rather small. Matter effect can enhance (suppress) the probability if
{\Delta_{31}}
is positive (negative). The situation is reversed for
P_{\bar{\mu}\bar{e}}
. The dominant term is also proportional to
\sin^{2}{\theta_{23}}
. When
{\theta_{23}}
is in LO (HO), i.e.
\sin^{2}{\theta_{23}}<0.5
(
\sin^{2}{\theta_{23}}>0.5
), both
P_{\mu e}
and
P_{\bar{\mu}\bar{e}}
is suppressed (enhanced) relative to the maximal mising, i.e.
\sin^{2}{\theta_{23}}=0.5
.
\delta_{\mathrm{CP}}
sensitivity of the experiments comes from the second term which is proportional to
\alpha\approx 0.03
. When
\delta_{\mathrm{CP}}
is in LHP,
P_{\mu e}
(
P_{\bar{\mu}\bar{e}}
) is larger (smaller), compared to the CP conserving case. However, when
\delta_{\mathrm{CP}}
is in UHP,
P_{\mu e}
(
P_{\bar{\mu}\bar{e}}

{\theta_{13}}

Fogli and Lisi 1996
;
Burguet-Castell
et al. 2001
;
Minakata and Nunokawa 2001
;
Mena and Parke 2004
;
Prakash et al. 2012

Meloni 2008

Agarwalla et al. 2013

Nath et al. 2016
;
Bora et al. 2016

Barger et al. 2002
;
Kajita et al. 2007
in the expression of
P_{\mu e}
and
P_{\bar{\mu}\bar{e}}
if
\sin^{2}2{\theta_{13}}
is not known precisely. The recent precision measurement of
{\theta_{13}}
breaks this eight-fold degeneracy into
(1+3+3+1)

Bharti et al. 2018

VI
Conclusion

From the analysis of the four present data sets from each of the two experiments NO
\nu
A and T2K, we can conclude that there is not much discrepancy between the
\nu_{\mu}
and
\bar{\nu}_{\mu}
disappearance data of the two experiments. However, the
\bar{\nu}_{\mu}
disappearance data of NO
\nu
A rule out
\sin^{2}{\theta_{23}}\approx 0.5
at
1\,\sigma
, whereas the same data from T2K allow the whole range of
\sin^{2}{\theta_{23}}
at
1\,\sigma
. Therefore NO
\nu
A
\bar{\nu}_{\mu}
disappearance data have a mild conflict with all other disappearance data. The tension arises primarily from the
\nu_{e}
appearance data of the two experiments, and is compounded by their
\nu_{\mu}
disappearance data. Because of the very large number of observed
\nu_{e}
appearance events in the T2K experiment,
\delta_{\mathrm{CP}}
anchors around
\delta_{\mathrm{CP}}=-90^{\circ}
. All other appearance data sets, namely
\nu_{e}
appearance from NO
\nu
A,
\bar{\nu}_{e}
appearance from both NO
\nu
A and T2K allow other values of
\delta_{\mathrm{CP}}
values different from
\delta_{\mathrm{CP}}\approx-90^{\circ}
. The observed
\nu_{e}
appearance event number of NO
\nu
A is moderately large such that it can be accommodated by either NH-
{\theta_{23}}
in HO-
\delta_{\mathrm{CP}}
in UHP or IH-
{\theta_{23}}
in HO-
\delta_{\mathrm{CP}}
in LHP. The moderately large electron appearance events in NO
\nu
A and very large electron appearance events in T2K are the biggest sources of tension between the two experiments and the addition of
\bar{\nu}_{\mu}
disappearance data makes this tension even stronger.

Vacuum oscillation gives as good fit to the data from both the experiments as matter effect. There is a large overlap between the allowed regions at
1\,\sigma
C.L. in the
\sin^{2}{\theta_{23}}-\delta_{\mathrm{CP}}
plane.

If the current trend continues, then after 5 years of neutrino and anti-neutrino run each, NO
\nu
A (T2K) can exclude the T2K (NO
\nu
A) NH best-fit point at
3\,\sigma
for both NH and IH (only NH) being the true hierarchy. Thus after 5 years of equal neutrino and anti-neutrino run, the tension at NH test can be established at
3\,\sigma
by NO
\nu
A irrespective of the true hierarchy. On the other hand, after the same run time, T2K can establish the tension at NH at
3\,\sigma
only when NH is the true hierarchy. For IH being the true hierarchy, T2K can establish the tension at NH test only at
1\,\sigma
after 5 years of equal neutrino and anti-neutrino runs. For both the true hierarchies, the IH best-fit point of one experiment can be ruled out by the other only at
1\,\sigma
. When IH is the true hierarchy, T2K can rule out the NO
\nu
A NH best-fit point only at
1\,\sigma
.

If the present tension continues, then DUNE after its 5 years each of neutrino and anti-neutrino run, can rule out the T2K (NO
\nu
A) NH and IH best-fit points at
3\,\sigma
for both NH and IH being the true hierarchy. In such a case, we will have to look at the possibility of systematic effects or non-standard physics as the source of this tension.

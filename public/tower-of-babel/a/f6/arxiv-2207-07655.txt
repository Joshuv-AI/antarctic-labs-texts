Closed Graph Theorem for Linear Random Operators


Abstract
. In this work, which was inspired by the article [ 2 ] by M. V. Velasco and A. R. Villena, we obtain a characterization for probably continuous operators and show that the probability of a linear random operator being continuous coincides with the probability of having a closed graph. Furthermore, we laid the foundations to start a new branch of mathematics, namely, Random Analysis.

1.
Introdução

The study of random variables that take values in Banach spaces is very important in the theory of random equations
[
8
,
9
]
, where stochastic continuity is an essential property. Stochastically continuous random linear operators behave like continuous in some sense that can be quantified with some precision. We will see that the "stochastic size" of the separation subspace of a linear random operator designates its continuity.

Classical probability theory introduces the concept of random variables as being measurable functions that take on values in the set
\mathbb{R}
of the real numbers. Generally speaking, random variables that take on values in a Banach space are measurable functions; Throughout this work, we will consider a fixed probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{A},\mathbb{P}),
where
\Omega
is an arbitrary set,
\mathcal{A}
a
\sigma
–algebra of subsets of
\Omega
and
\mathbb{P}
a measure of probability.

Let
(Y,\mathfrak{B})
be a measurable space where
Y
is a Banach space and
\mathfrak{B}
is the
\sigma
–algebra of all Borel subsets of
Y.

Definition 2.1
.

A map
x:\Omega\to Y
is said to be a random variable with values in
Y
if the inverse image of every Borel set
B,
under the map
x,
belongs to
\mathcal{A},
i.e. if
x^{-1}(B)\in\mathcal{A}
for all
B\in\mathfrak{B}.

In other words, a random variable valued in
Y
is a measurable Borel function valued in the Banach space
Y
; When
Y=\mathbb{R},
the above definition matches the definition of ordinary random variable.

A randomization
\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}(Y)
(
\mathcal{R}(Y)
if there is no confusion) is the linear space of all random variables
Y-
valued over
\left(\Omega,\mathcal{A},\mathbb{P}\right)
(where matching elements are almost certainly equivalent). As in
[
1

3.
Characterizing the probable continuity of linear random operators

Given
y\in\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}(Y)
and given
\tau\in\mathbb{R},
we use the notation

\left[y\geq\tau\right]=\{\omega\in\Omega;y(\omega)\geq\tau\},

as well as

\left[\|y\|\geq\tau\right]=\{\omega\in\Omega;\|y(\omega)\|\geq\tau\}.

Analogous notations are used when replacing the aforementioned
\geq
with
\leq,=,>
or
<.
Also, given
A,B\in\mathcal{A},
we use the notation

A,B=A\cap B.

Definition 3.1
.

A sequence
(y_{n})
in
\mathcal{R}(Y)
is said to converge in probability to an element
y
in
\mathcal{R}(Y)
when

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left[\|y_{n}-y\|\geq\tau\right]=0,\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\tau>0

or, more specifically, when for all
\tau>0
and
0<\varepsilon\leq 1,
there is
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\mathbb{P}\left[\|y_{n}-y\|\geq\tau\right]<\varepsilon

whenever it is
n>N.

It is important to note that, due to the equality

\mathbb{P}\left[\|y_{n}-y\|<\tau\right]+\mathbb{P}\left[\|y_{n}-y\|\geq\tau\right]=1,

it follows that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left[\|y_{n}-y\|\geq\tau\right]=0,\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\tau>0,
equivalent to

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left[\|y_{n}-y\|<\tau\right]=1,\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\tau>0;

In other words, for all
\tau>0
and
0<\varepsilon\leq 1,
there is
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\mathbb{P}\left[\|y_{n}-y\|<\tau\right]\geq\varepsilon

for all
n>N.

Definition 3.2
.

An operator
T:X\to\mathcal{R}(Y),
called the random operator of
X
on
Y,
is said to be linear if, for all constant
\alpha,\beta,

\mathbb{P}\left[T(\alpha x+\beta y)=\alpha T(x)+\beta T(y)\right]=1,\hskip 10.00002pt\forall x,y\in X.

In particular, note that
\mathbb{P}\left[T(0)=0\right]=1,
since
\mathbb{P}\left[T(0x+0y)=0T(x)+0T(y)\right]=1
for all
x,y\in X.
Also note that the inclusion

\left[T(\alpha x+\beta y)=\alpha T(x)+\beta T(y)\right]\subset\left[\|T(\alpha x+\beta y)\|=\|\alpha T(x)+\beta T(y)\|\right]

will give us
\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(\alpha x+\beta y)\|=\|\alpha T(x)+\beta T(y)\|\right]=1
always that the random operator
T
is linear. Finally, it’s important to remember that given
A,B\in\mathcal{A}
with, say,
\mathbb{P}(B)=1
and using the notation
A,B=A\cap B,
we have

\mathbb{P}\left(A\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(A,B\right),

since

1=\mathbb{P}\left(B\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(A\cup B\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(A\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(B\right)-\mathbb{P}\left(A,B\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(A\right)+1-\mathbb{P}\left(A,B\right).

Definition 3.4
.

We will say that the operator

In item

Thus,

for all

Also note that item
(1)
of the above definition can be reformulated due to the equality

\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x)-T(x_{0})\|<\tau\right]+\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x)-T(x_{0})\|\geq\tau\right]=1,

for which

\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x)-T(x_{0})\|<\tau\right]>\varepsilon\Leftrightarrow\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x)-T(x_{0})\|\geq\tau\right]<1-\varepsilon

and consequently

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lim_{x\to x_{0}}\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x)-T(x_{0})\|<\tau\right]\geq\alpha\Leftrightarrow\displaystyle\lim_{x\to x_{0}}\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x)-T(x_{0})\|\geq\tau\right]\leq 1-\alpha;

thus,

is probably continuous in

with probability

if and only if

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to x_{0}}\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x)-T(x_{0})\|\geq\tau\right]\leq 1-\alpha.

It is important to note that the equivalence (
3.1
) holds, in particular, for the sequential limit, that is,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x_{n})-T(x_{0})\|<\tau\right]\geq\alpha

equals

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x_{n})-T(x_{0})\|\geq\tau\right]\leq 1-\alpha

for every sequence
\left(x_{n}\right)_{n=1}^{\infty}
in

converging to

This last limit, in turn, means that for all
\tau>0
and
0<\varepsilon<\alpha,

\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x_{n})-T(x_{0})\|\geq\tau\right]<1-\varepsilon
whenever
x_{n}\in X
and
\|x_{n}-x_{0}\|<\delta.

Remark 3.6
.

Let
T:X\to\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}(Y)
be a linear random operator. Then
T
is probably continuous at the origin with probability
\geq\alpha
, that is,

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0}\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x)-T(0)\|<\tau\right]\geq\alpha,\textrm{ for all }\tau>0,

if, and only if,

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0}\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x)\|<\tau\right]\geq\alpha,\textrm{ for all }\tau>0.

In fact, it suffices to show that

\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x)-T(0)\|<\tau\right]=\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x)\|<\tau\right].

Since
\|T(x)\|-\|T(0)\|\leq\|T(x)-T(0)\|,
for all
x\in X,
and
\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(0)\|=0\right]=1,
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x)-T(0)\|<\tau\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x)\|-\|T(0)\|<\tau\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x)\|-\|T(0)\|<\tau,\|T(0)\|=0\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x)\|-0<\tau,\|T(0)\|=0\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x)\|<\tau\right],

that is,
\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x)-T(0)\|<\tau\right]\leq\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x)\|<\tau\right].

Conversely, in view of the inequality
\|T(x)-T(0)\|-\|T(0)\|\leq\|T(x)\|,
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x)\|<\tau\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x)-T(0)\|+\|T(0)\|<\tau\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x)-T(0)\|<\tau\right]

i.e.
\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x)\|<\tau\right]\leq\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x)-T(0)\|<\tau\right]
and consequently
\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x)-T(0)\|<\tau\right]=\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x)\|<\tau\right].

It is important to note that when there is
\alpha=1
satisfying the theorem above, we conclude that
T
is stochastically continuous at the origin, that is,

\lim_{x\to 0}\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x)-T(0)\|<\tau\right]=1,\textrm{ for all }\tau>0,

if, and only if,

\lim_{x\to 0}\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x)\|<\tau\right]=1,\textrm{ for all }\tau>0.

Note that, taking into account the equivalences of type (
3.1
), we have, for all
\tau>0,

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0}\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x)-T(0)\|\geq\tau\right]\leq 1-\alpha

if, and only if,

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0}\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x)\|\geq\tau\right]\leq 1-\alpha,

and when
\alpha=1,
remains the conclusion that
T
is stochastically continuous.

.

.

4.
Randomized version of the Closed Graph Theorem

4.1.
Conditionally continuous linear random operators

Given a linear random operator
T:X\to\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}(Y)
and a measurable subset
\Omega^{\prime}
with
\mathbb{P}\left[\Omega^{\prime}\right]>0
, we can consider
\Omega^{\prime}
as a new probability space with the structure inherited from
\Omega
. In this case, the probability induced on
\Omega^{\prime}
is the conditional probability
\mathbb{P}^{\prime}
given by

\mathbb{P}^{\prime}(\hat{\Omega})=\frac{\mathbb{P}(\hat{\Omega})}{\mathbb{P}(\Omega^{\prime})},\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\hat{\Omega}\in\mathcal{A^{\prime}},

where
\mathcal{A^{\prime}}
is the
\sigma
–algebra induced in
\mathcal{A}
by
\Omega^{\prime}.
Thus, for each random variable
y
in
\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}(Y)
, we will consider its restriction
y/\Omega^{\prime}
and, in these terms, we present the following definition.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
T(x)/\Omega^{\prime}
be the restriction of the random variable
T(x)
to the subspace
\Omega^{\prime}
, with
\mathbb{P}(\Omega^{\prime})>0
. The operator
T/\Omega^{\prime}:X\to\mathcal{R}_{\Omega^{\prime}}(Y)
, given by

T/\Omega^{\prime}(x)=T(x)/\Omega^{\prime},

is named conditional operator of
T.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
0<\alpha\leq 1.
A linear random operator
T
is probably continuous with probability
\geq\alpha
if and only if there is a measurable subset
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\Omega
with
\mathbb{P}\left(\Omega^{\prime}\right)\geq\alpha
such that the conditional operator
T/\Omega^{\prime}
is stochastically continuous (on probability
\mathbb{P}^{\prime}
).

Proof.

Assuming that
T
is probably continuous with probability
\geq\alpha,
that is, that

\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x)\|\leq M_{\varepsilon}\|x\|\right]>\varepsilon,

for all
0<\varepsilon<\alpha
and
x\in X,
let us consider a sequence
(\varepsilon_{n}),~0<\varepsilon_{n}<\alpha
such that
\varepsilon_{n}\to\alpha
and, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
x\in X,
let’s define

\Omega_{x}^{\varepsilon_{n}}=\left[\|T(x)\|\leq M_{\varepsilon_{n}}\|x\|\right],

that is,
\Omega_{x}^{\varepsilon_{n}}=\{\omega\in\Omega;\|T(x)(\omega)\|\leq M_{\varepsilon_{n}}\|x\|\}.
So, by hypothesis, we have
\mathbb{P}\left(\Omega_{x}^{\varepsilon_{n}}\right)>\varepsilon_{n}
for all
x\in X.
Also writing

\Omega_{x}^{n}=\bigcup_{k=1}^{n}\Omega_{x}^{\varepsilon_{k}}

we get, for all
x\in X
and
n\in\mathbb{N},

\mathbb{P}\left(\Omega_{x}^{n}\right)>\varepsilon_{n}\text{ and }\Omega_{x}^{n}\subset\Omega_{x}^{n+1}

so, for each
x\in X,
a new event is defined, denoted by
\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\Omega_{x}^{n}
, to know

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\Omega_{x}^{n}=\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}\Omega_{x}^{k}

who has the property

\mathbb{P}\left(\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}\Omega_{x}^{k}\right):=\mathbb{P}\left(\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\Omega_{x}^{n}\right)=\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\Omega_{x}^{n}\right)\geq\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\varepsilon_{n}=\alpha.

So, fixed
x\in X,
just take
\Omega^{\prime}=\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}\Omega_{x}^{k}
and we will have

\mathbb{P}\left[\|T/\Omega^{\prime}(x)\|\leq M_{\varepsilon}\|x\|\right]=\mathbb{P}\left[\Omega^{\prime}\right]>\mathbb{P}\left[\Omega^{\prime}\right]\varepsilon,\hskip 10.00002pt\forall~0<\varepsilon<1,

or yet,

\mathbb{P}^{\prime}\left[T/\Omega^{\prime}\right]:=\frac{\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x)\|\leq M_{\varepsilon}\|x\|\right]}{\mathbb{P}\left[\Omega^{\prime}\right]}>\varepsilon,\hskip 10.00002pt\forall~0<\varepsilon<1,

i.e. there exists a measurable subset
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\Omega
with
\mathbb{P}\left(\Omega^{\prime}\right)\geq\alpha
such that the conditional operator
T/\Omega^{\prime}
is stochastically continuous in the conditional probability
\mathbb{P}^{\prime}
.

Conversely, in view of item
(vii)
of Theorem
3.7
, to say that
T/\Omega^{\prime}
is stochastically continuous in the conditional probability
\mathbb{P}^{\prime},
means to say that for every
0<\varepsilon<1
there is a constant
M_{\varepsilon}\geq 0
such that,
\text{for all }x\text{ in }X,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{\prime}\left[\|T/\Omega^{\prime}(x)\|\leq M_{\varepsilon}\|x\|\right]>\varepsilon

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x)/\Omega^{\prime}\|\leq M_{\varepsilon}\|x\|\right]}{\mathbb{P}(\Omega^{\prime})}>\varepsilon

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x)/\Omega^{\prime}\|\leq M_{\varepsilon}\|x\|\right]>\mathbb{P}(\Omega^{\prime})\varepsilon.

Since

\left[\|T(x)/\Omega^{\prime}\|\leq M_{\varepsilon}\|x\|\right]\subset\left[\|T(x)\|\leq M_{\varepsilon}\|x\|\right],

we have

\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x)/\Omega^{\prime}\|\leq M_{\varepsilon}\|x\|\right]>\mathbb{P}(\Omega^{\prime})\varepsilon\Rightarrow\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x)\|\leq M_{\varepsilon}\|x\|\right]>\mathbb{P}(\Omega^{\prime})\varepsilon,

hence

\mathbb{P}^{\prime}\left[\|T/\Omega^{\prime}(x)\|\leq M_{\varepsilon}\|x\|\right]>\varepsilon\Rightarrow\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x)\|\leq M_{\varepsilon}\|x\|\right]>\mathbb{P}(\Omega^{\prime})\varepsilon\geq\alpha\varepsilon.

As
0<\alpha\varepsilon<\alpha
for all
0<\varepsilon<1,
it follows that
T
is probably continuous with probability
\geq\alpha.

∎

Definition 4.3
.

For a probably continuous linear random operator
T:X\to\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}(Y),
we define the real number

\alpha_{T}=\sup\{\alpha;\text{ if }0<\varepsilon<\alpha,\exists~M_{\varepsilon}\geq 0\text{ such that }\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x)\|\leq M_{\varepsilon}\|x\|\right]>\varepsilon~\forall~x\in X\}.

When
T
is probably not continuous, we define
\alpha_{T}=0.
We call
\alpha_{T}
probability of
T
being continuous.

Note that when T is stochastically continuous, we have
\alpha_{T}=1.
On the other hand, Corollary
4.4
below shows us, among other things, that if
\alpha_{T}=1,

T
is stochastically continuous. This same result also justifies the fact that we call
\alpha_{T}

probability of
T
being continuous
.

Next, the sets
\Omega^{\prime}
and
\Omega^{\prime}_{0}
represent certain measurable subsets of
\Omega,
as explained in the result below:

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
T:X\to\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}(Y)
be a linear probably continuous random operator. Then

\displaystyle\alpha_{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\{\alpha;\text{ if }0<\varepsilon<\alpha,\exists~M_{\varepsilon}\geq 0\text{ such that }\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x)\|\leq M_{\varepsilon}\|x\|\right]>\varepsilon~\forall~x\in X\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\{\mathbb{P}\left[\Omega^{\prime}\right]:T/\Omega^{\prime}\text{ is stochastically continuous in probability }\mathbb{P^{\prime}}\}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\Omega_{0}^{\prime}\right].

In particular,
T
is probably continuous with probability equal to
\alpha_{T},

T
has a conditional operator
T/\Omega_{0}^{\prime}
stochastically continuous (in conditional probability) with
\mathbb{P}\left[\Omega_{0}^{\prime}\right]=\alpha_{T},
and it is not possible to find a conditional operator
T/\Omega^{\prime}
stochastically continuous with
\mathbb{P}\left[\Omega^{\prime}\right]>\alpha_{T}.

Proof.

To show that

\alpha_{T}=\max\{\alpha;\text{ if }0<\varepsilon<\alpha,\exists~M_{\varepsilon}\geq 0\text{ such that }\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x)\|\leq M_{\varepsilon}\|x\|\right]>\varepsilon~\forall~x\in X\},

we need to show that
\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x)\|\leq M_{\varepsilon}\|x\|\right]>\varepsilon
for all
0<\varepsilon<\alpha_{T}
and
x\in X.
In fact, assuming the opposite, there is
\varepsilon_{0},~0<\varepsilon_{0}<\alpha_{T}
such that, for every constant
M\geq 0,
we can find
x=x(M)
so that

(4.1)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x)\|\leq M\|x\|\right]\leq\varepsilon_{0}.

On the other hand,
\alpha_{T}
being the supreme of the set

\{\alpha;\text{ if }0<\varepsilon<\alpha,\exists~M_{\varepsilon}\geq 0\text{ such that }\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x)\|\leq M_{\varepsilon}\|x\|\right]>\varepsilon~\forall~x\in X\},

there is
\alpha_{0},~\varepsilon_{0}<\alpha_{0}<\alpha_{T},
such that, for all
x\in X
and
0<\varepsilon<\alpha_{0},
is valid

\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x)\|\leq M_{\varepsilon}\|x\|\right]>\varepsilon,

which contradicts (
4.1
) when
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{0}.
It follows in particular that
T
is probably continuous with probability
\geq\alpha_{T}
and
\alpha_{T}
being the largest (the supreme) of the
\alpha
for which
\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x)\|\leq M_{\varepsilon}\|x\|\right]>\varepsilon
for all
x\in X
and
0<\varepsilon<\alpha,
it follows that T has probability equal to
\alpha_{T}.

Let us now prove tha

\alpha_{T}=\max\{\mathbb{P}\left[\Omega^{\prime}\right]:T/\Omega^{\prime}\text{ is stochastically continuous in probability }\mathbb{P^{\prime}}\}.

Since
T
is probably continuous with probability
=\alpha_{T},
it follows from Proposition
4.2
that there exists a measurable set
\Omega^{\prime}_{0}\subset\Omega
such that
\mathbb{P}\left[\Omega^{\prime}_{0}\right]\geq\alpha_{T}.
However, if it were
\mathbb{P}\left[\Omega^{\prime}_{0}\right]>\alpha_{T},
there would be
\beta
such that
\mathbb{P}\left[\Omega^{\prime}_{0}\right]\geq\beta>\alpha_{T}
and, by the same proposition, we would conclude that
T
is probably continuous with probability
\geq\beta>\alpha_{T},
contradicting the first part of this corollary.
∎

Corollary 4.5
.

For a linear random operator
T:X\to\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}(Y)Y
, the following statements are equivalent.

i)

T
is probably Lipschitzian with probability
\alpha_{T}
.

ii)

T
is probably uniformly continuous with probability
\alpha_{T}
.

iii)

T
is probably continuous with probability
\alpha_{T}
.

iv)

T
is probably continuous at some point in
X
with probability
\alpha_{T}
.

v)

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0}\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x)\|<\tau\right]=\alpha_{T},\text{ for all }\tau>0,
i.e.
T
is probably continuous at the origin with probability
\alpha_{T}
.

vi)

T
is probably bound with probability
\alpha_{T}
.

vii)

For every
0<\varepsilon<\alpha_{T}
there is a constant
M_{\varepsilon}\geq 0
such that

\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x)\|\leq M_{\varepsilon}\|x\|\right]>\varepsilon\text{ for all }x\text{ in }X.

With the characterization given by the Theorem
3.7
, as well as the Corollary
4.5
, we next prove that the notion of probable continuity (in particular, stochastic continuity) of a linear random operator coincides with the notion of probable continuity (respectively, stochastic continuity) sequential.

Definition 4.6
.

We will say that a linear random operator
T:X\to\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}(Y)
is probably sequentially continuous, with probability
\geq\alpha,
when

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x_{n})\|<\tau\right]\geq\alpha,

for any
\tau>0
and every sequence
(x_{n})
in
X
such that
x_{n}\to 0.
Precisely, this means that, given arbitrarily
\tau>0,0<\varepsilon<\alpha
and
(x_{n})
in
X
such that
x_{n}\to 0
when
n\to\infty,
exists
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x_{n})\|<\tau\right]>\varepsilon\text{ for all }n>n_{0}.

If
\alpha
is the largest number for which inequality in the above limit holds, that is, if given any
\gamma>\alpha
and
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N},
there are
\tau>0,n>n_{0}
and
\alpha\leq\varepsilon<\gamma
such that

\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x_{n})\|<\tau\right]\leq\varepsilon,

we will say that
T
is probably sequentially continuous with probability
\alpha.
In particular, when
\alpha=1,
we will say that
T
is stochastically sequentially continuous, or probably sequentially continuous with probability
1.

The following corollary shows us that a linear random operator is probably continuous, with probability
\geq\alpha
if and only if it is probably sequentially continuous, with probability
\geq\alpha
and that, in addition, equality holds when
\alpha=\alpha_{T}
.

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
T:X\to\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}(Y)
be a linear random operator. Then

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0}\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x)\|<\tau\right]\geq\alpha

for all
\tau>0

if and only if

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x_{n})\|<\tau\right]\geq\alpha

for every
\tau>0
and every sequence
(x_{n})
in
X
such that
x_{n}\to 0.
Furthermore, if
\alpha=\alpha_{T},
holds equality in both limits of the equivalence.

Proof.

Suppose
\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0}\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x)\|<\tau\right]\geq\alpha
for all
\tau>0,
that is, that given
\tau>0
and
0<\varepsilon<\alpha,
exists
\delta>0
such that
\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x)\|<\tau\right]>\varepsilon
whenever
\|x\|<\delta.
Let
(x_{n})
be a sequence in
X
such that
x_{n}\to 0
when
n\to\infty.
So there is
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\|x_{n}\|<\delta
for all
n>n_{0},
hence
\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x_{n})\|<\tau\right]>\varepsilon
for all
n>n_{0},
i.e.
T
is probably sequentially continuous, with probability
\geq\alpha
.

To prove the converse, suppose that
T
is probably not continuous with probability
\geq\alpha
, that is, that there are
\tau>0
and
0<\varepsilon<\alpha
such that, for all
\delta>0,
exists
x\in X
such that
\|x\|<\delta
and
\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x)\|<\tau\right]\leq\varepsilon.
Thus, for each
\delta=\frac{1}{n},
we can choose
x_{n}\in X
such that
\|x_{n}\|<\frac{1}{n}
and
\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x_{n})\|<\tau\right]\leq\varepsilon,
so
T
is probably not sequentially continuous, with probability
\geq\alpha
.

In particular, if
\alpha=\alpha_{T},
follows from item
(v)
of Corollary
4.5
that

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0}\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x)\|<\tau\right]=\alpha_{T}

for all
\tau>0.
In this case, it remains to show that
\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x_{n})\|<\tau\right]>\alpha_{T}
for some
\tau>0
and some sequence
(x_{n})
in
X
such that
x_{n}\to 0.
In fact, if that were possible, we would take
\beta
such that
\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x_{n})\|<\tau\right]\geq\beta>\alpha_{T},
and the first part of the theorem would give us
\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0}\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x)\|<\tau\right]\geq\beta>\alpha_{T}
for all
\tau>0,
a contradiction.
∎

Corollary 4.8
.

Let
T:X\to\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}(Y)
be a linear random operator. If there is a sequence
(x_{n})
in
X
with
x_{n}\to 0
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x_{n})\|<\tau\right]\geq\alpha

for all
\tau>0,
then

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0}\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x)\|<\tau\right]\geq\alpha

for all
\tau>0.
Furthermore, if
\alpha=\alpha_{T},
holds equality on both limits.

Proof.

Analogously to the proof of the reciprocal of the Corollary
4.7
, suppose that
T
is probably not continuous with probability
\geq\alpha
, that is, that there are
\tau>0
and
0<\varepsilon<\alpha
such that, for every
\delta>0,
there is
x\in X
such that
\|x\|<\delta
and
\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x)\|<\tau\right]\leq\varepsilon.
As
x_{n}\to 0
, for each
\delta>0,
we can choose
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\|x_{n}\|<\delta
for all
n>n_{0}
and consequently
\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x_{n})\|<\tau\right]\leq\varepsilon,
for all
n>n_{0},
so
T
is probably not sequentially continuous, with probability
\geq\alpha
.
∎

The above corollary tells us that for a linear random operator to be probably continuous with probability
\geq\alpha
, it is sufficient to show that there is a sequence
(x_{n})
in
X,x_{n}\to 0,
satisfying the limit
\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x_{n})\|<\tau\right]\geq\alpha

for all
\tau>0.

4.2.
Random and Stochastic Closed Graph Theorem

Definition 4.9
.

Let
T:X\to\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}(Y)
be a linear random operator and let
S(T)
be the subspace separating
T,
that is,

S(T)=\{y\in\mathcal{R}(Y):\exists x_{n}\to 0\text{ with }T(x_{n})\to y\text{ in probability}\}.

We will say that
T
has a probably closed graph, with probability
\geq\alpha,
if
\mathbb{P}\left[y=0\right]\geq\alpha
for all
y\in S(T).
When
\alpha=1,
we say that
T
has a stochastically closed graph.

The classical closed graph theorem (see, for example,
[
4
, Theorem 5.3.1]
) has a stochastic version that was presented without proof in
[
2
]
. Next, we reformulate the statement presented in
[
2
]
in order to obtain a demonstration of its veracity.

Theorem 4.10

(Random/stochastic version of the Closed Graph Theorem)
.

Let
T:X\to\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}(Y)
be a linear random operator. Then
T
is probably continuous with probability
\geq\alpha
if and only if
T
has probably closed graph, with probability
\geq\alpha
.

Proof.

Suppose
T
is probably continuous with probability
\geq\alpha
. If
y\in S(T),
then there is a sequence
(x_{n})
in
X
zuch that
x_{n}\to 0
and
T(x_{n})
converges to
y
in probability, that is,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x_{n})-y\|<\frac{\tau}{2}\right]=1\text{ for all }\tau>0.

On the other hand, since
T
is probably continuous with probability
\geq\alpha
, the Corollary
4.7
gives us

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x_{n})\|<\frac{\tau}{2}\right]\geq\alpha\text{ for all }\tau>0.

As
\|y\|\leq\|T(x_{n})-y\|+\|T(x_{n})\|
and

\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x_{n})-y\|<\frac{\tau}{2},\|T(x_{n})\|<\frac{\tau}{2}\right]\geq\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x_{n})-y\|<\frac{\tau}{2}\right]+\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x_{n})\|<\frac{\tau}{2}\right]-1,

we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\|y\|<\tau\right]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x_{n})-y\|+\|T(x_{n})\|<\tau\right]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x_{n})-y\|<\frac{\tau}{2},\|T(x_{n})\|<\frac{\tau}{2}\right]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x_{n})-y\|<\frac{\tau}{2}\right]+\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x_{n})\|<\frac{\tau}{2}\right]-1.

Doing
n\to\infty,
we get
\mathbb{P}\left[\|y\|<\tau\right]\geq 1+\alpha-1=\alpha,
for all
\tau>0,
so we conclude that
\mathbb{P}\left[y=0\right]\geq\alpha.

Now consider a sequence
(x_{n})
in
X
such that
x_{n}\to 0,
and
y\in\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}(Y)
such that
(T(x_{n}))
converges to
y
in probability, that is, such that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x_{n})-y\|<\tau\right]=1

for all
\tau>0.
Thus,
y\in S(T)
and, by hypothesis,
\mathbb{P}\left[y=0\right]\geq\alpha.
Hence, in view from the inequality
\|T(x_{n})\|\leq\|T(x_{n})-y\|+\|y\|,
we can write

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x_{n})\|<\tau\right]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x_{n})-y\|+\|y\|<\tau\right]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x_{n})-y\|<\frac{\tau}{2},\|y\|\leq\frac{\tau}{2}\right]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x_{n})-y\|<\frac{\tau}{2}\right]+\left[\|y\|\leq\frac{\tau}{2}\right]-1,

soon

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left[\|T(x_{n})\|<\tau\right]\geq 1+\alpha-1=\alpha,

and the Corollary
4.8
guarantees that
T
is probably continuous with probability
\geq\alpha
.
∎

Of course, in the Theorem above, by virtue of the Corollary
4.5
, we can exchange
``\geq"
for
``="
and
``\alpha"
for
``\alpha_{T}":

Corollary 4.11
.

Let
T:X\to\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}(Y)
be a linear random operator. Then
T
is probably continuous with probability
\alpha_{T}
if and only if
T
has a probably closed graph, with probability
\alpha_{T}
.

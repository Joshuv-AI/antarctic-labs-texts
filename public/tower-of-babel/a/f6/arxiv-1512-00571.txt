Abstract
. Given a sequence ( 𝔛 i , 𝒦 i ) i = 1 ∞ (\mathfrak{X}_{i},{\mathscr{K}}_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty} of Markov chains, the cut-off phenomenon describes a period of transition to stationarity which is asymptotically lower order than the mixing time. We study mixing times and the cut-off phenomenon in the total variation metric in the case of random walk on the groups ℤ / p ​ ℤ \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z} , p p prime, with driving measure uniform on a symmetric generating set A ⊂ ℤ / p ​ ℤ A\subset\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z} .

Introduction

The mixing analysis of random walk on a finite abelian group is a classical
problem of probability
theory, with widespread applications; the
Ehrnfest urn and sandpile models of statistical mechanics are motivating
examples
[
8
,
17
,
26
]
. Among the early results in this area is
a theorem of
Greenhalgh
[
15
]
, which shows that for generating set of
size
k
contained in
\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
, the mixing time of the corresponding
random walk satisfies
t^{\operatorname{mix}}\gg_{k}n^{\frac{2}{k-1}}
. A
set of size
k
with mixing time bounded by
\ll_{k}n^{\frac{2}{k-1}}\log n
is
also exhibited. Dou, Hildebrand and Wilson
[
13
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
28
]

consider the mixing of measures driven by typical generating sets on cyclic and
more general groups. Among the results of
[
16
]
is that typical generating
sets of size
k=(\log n)^{a}
,
a>1
produce a random walk satisfying the
cut-off phenomenon. We confine our attention to cyclic groups and symmetric
generating sets
which are smaller than logarithmic size in the order of the group, and prove
a number of refined results on the mixing behavior. Our results are in a
similar spirit to those of Diaconis and Saloff-Coste
[
5
]
proven in the
more general context of random walk on groups of polynomial growth, but in
narrowing our focus we emphasize strong uniformity in the number of generators
of the random walk. Note that in the context of random walk on nilpotent groups, the mixing of the walk projected to the abelianization often controls the mixing in the group as a whole, see
[
14
]
,
[
9
]
.

To briefly summarize the results,
Theorem

1
gives spectral upper and lower bounds for the
mixing time in a sharper form than previous results which have appeared in the
literature.
A natural
conjecture regarding random walk on a connected graph is that the total
variation mixing time is bounded by the maximum degree times the diameter
squared.
A highlight of our work is
Theorem
2
, which verifies the conjecture for the
mixing time
of random walk on the Cayley graph of
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}

with a small symmetric generating set. Theorem
3

gives a lower bound for the period of transition to uniformity relative to the
mixing time – a lower bound on the cut-off window. Theorem
4

determines the generic and worst case mixing behavior for a sequence of typical
symmetric random walks. We conclude by analyzing the mixing time of a
walk which may be considered an approximate embedding of the hypercube

(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{d}

into the cycle, demonstrating a cut-off phenomenon.

1.1.
Precise statement of results

Let
{\mathscr{P}}
be the set of primes. Given
p\in{\mathscr{P}}

be symmetric (

be those sets of size

denote the uniform measure on
A

\mu_{A}=\frac{1}{|A|}\sum_{x\in A}\delta_{x}.

The distribution at step

is given by the convolution power

\mu_{A}^{*1}=\mu_{A},\qquad\mu_{A}^{*n}=\mu_{A}^{*(n-1)}*\mu_{A},\;n>1.

As

\mu_{A}^{*n}
converges to the uniform measure

\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}}
on
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
and we consider asymptotic behavior of this

. In particular, the behavior of these walks
as

, and as

Given measure space
(\mathfrak{X},{\mathscr{B}})

\|\cdot\|
on
the space
{\mathscr{M}}(\mathfrak{X})

\mathfrak{X}

and the standard mixing time

In the cases considered

is a (finite, compact, locally compact)
abelian group, and, due to the symmetry of the walk, it is sufficient to
take for

the point mass at 0.
Of primary interest is the total variation norm, which for

\mu,\nu\in{\mathscr{M}}(\mathfrak{X})
is given by

\|\mu-\nu\|_{\operatorname{TV}(\mathfrak{X})}=\sup_{S\in{\mathscr{B}}}|\mu(S)-\nu(S)|.

The mixing time with respect to this norm is indicated

t^{\operatorname{mix}}_{1}
. Two further important parameters in
considering reversible Markov chains are the spectral gap of the transition
kernel

\operatorname{gap}=1-\sup\left\{|\lambda|:\lambda\in\operatorname{spec}(P)\setminus\{\pm 1\}\right\}

and the relaxation time

t^{\operatorname{rel}}=\frac{1}{-\log(1-\operatorname{gap})}\approx\frac{1}{\operatorname{gap}}.

In
stating our results we let
\tau_{0}

denote the ratio
\frac{t^{\operatorname{mix}}_{1}}{t^{\operatorname{rel}}}
of the
one dimensional Gaussian diffusion

(1.1)

\theta(x,t)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\exp(-2\pi^{2}tj^{2})e^{2\pi ijx}

on
(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z},dx)

solves the equation

\int_{0}^{1}|\theta(x,t)-1|dt=\frac{2}{e}

and has numerical value
1

We use parentheses to indicate the last significant digit of numerical constants.

(1.2)

Let

Also, uniformly in

\frac{2k+1}{16\pi\Gamma\left(\frac{k}{2}+1\right)^{\frac{2}{k}}}p^{\frac{2}{k}}\lesssim_{p}t^{\operatorname{rel}}.

The relationship

Theorem 2
.

Let
p
be an odd prime and let
A\in{\mathscr{A}}(p)
with
|A|=2k+1

The mixing time

t^{\operatorname{mix}}_{1}\ll k\cdot\operatorname{diam}_{\operatorname{geom}}({\mathscr{C}}(A,p))^{2}.

Remark
.

In the context of random walk on a cycle, Theorem
2

refines in two ways the much more general Theorem 1.2 of
[
5

applies in the context of groups of moderate growth. The dependence on the

Given a sequence of triples
(\mathfrak{X}_{i},P_{i},\nu_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}

\mathfrak{X}_{i}

is a Markov kernel on
\mathfrak{X}_{i}

\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{t^{\operatorname{mix}}_{1,i}(\epsilon)}{t^{\operatorname{mix}}_{1,i}(1-\epsilon)}=1.

The cut-off phenomenon is frequently observed in natural families of Markov
chains including the hypercube walk of
[
8
]
and
riffle
shuffling viewed as a random walk on the symmetric group
[
1
]
.
Especially in total variation, the cut-off phenomenon is still imperfectly
understood, so that there is significant interest in deciding its occurrence
in specific examples, see for instance
[
10
]
,
[
12
]
,

[
4
]
,
[

]

[

One necessary condition for cut-off in total variation to occur is

\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{t^{\operatorname{mix}}_{1,i}}{t^{\operatorname{rel}}_{i}}=\infty,

see Chapter 18.3 of
[

In particular, by Theorem
1
any sequence of
walks generated by
\{A_{p}\bmod p\subset\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}\}_{p\in{\mathscr{P}}}

remains bounded does not have cut-off, a result first obtained in

[

We give a different proof of this result found independently by the

uniformity.

Theorem 3
.

Let
p\geq 3
be prime, let
1\leq k\leq\frac{\log p}{\log\log p}
and let
A\in{\mathscr{A}}(p,k)
.
For any
0<\epsilon<\frac{1}{e}
the
total variation mixing times of
\mu_{A}
on
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
satisfy

t^{\operatorname{mix}}_{1}(\epsilon)-t^{\operatorname{mix}}_{1}(1-\epsilon)\gg_{\epsilon}\frac{t^{\operatorname{mix}}_{1}}{k}.

In contrast to Theorem
3
, our next theorem shows
that the generic behavior when
|A_{p}|
grows slowly is for
there to be a sharp transition to uniformity with infrequent exceptions.

Theorem 4
.

Let
k:{\mathscr{P}}\to\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
tend to
\infty
with
p
in such a way that

k(p)\leq\frac{\log p}{\log\log p}
. Let sets
\{A_{p}\bmod p\}_{p\in{\mathscr{P}}}

be
chosen independently with
A_{p}
chosen uniformly from
{\mathscr{A}}(p,k(p))
.
The following hold with
probability 1.

(1)

Let
\rho:{\mathscr{P}}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
satisfy
\sum_{p}\frac{1}{\rho(p)^{k}}=\infty
. There is an infinite subsequence
{\mathscr{P}}_{0}\subset{\mathscr{P}}
such that for

p
increasing through
{\mathscr{P}}_{0}
,

t^{\operatorname{rel}}(p)\gtrsim\frac{e}{\pi}\rho(p)^{2}p^{\frac{2}{k(p)}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad t^{\operatorname{mix}}_{1}(p)\sim\tau_{0}t^{\operatorname{rel}}(p).

In particular, the cut-off phenomenon does not occur for

(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z},\mu_{A_{p}},\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}})_{p\in{\mathscr{P}}}
.

(2)

Let
\rho:{\mathscr{P}}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
satisfy
\sum_{p}\frac{1}{\rho(p)^{k}}<\infty
.
Then

t^{\operatorname{mix}}_{1}(p)\lesssim\frac{\tau_{0}e}{\pi}\rho(p)^{2}p^{\frac{2}{k(p)}}.

(3)

For any sequence
\{\epsilon(p)\}_{p\in{\mathscr{P}}}\subset\mathbb{R}_{>0}

satisfying
\epsilon(p)\sqrt{k(p)}\to\infty
there is a density 1 subset

{\mathscr{P}}_{0}\subset{\mathscr{P}}
such that in the family
(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z},\mu_{A_{p}},\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}})_{p\in{\mathscr{P}}_{0}}
we have

t^{\operatorname{mix}}_{1}(p)\sim\frac{k(p)}{2\pi e}p^{\frac{2}{k(p)}},

and as
p
increases through
{\mathscr{P}}_{0}

\lim\left\|\mu_{A_{p}}^{(1-\epsilon)t^{\operatorname{mix}}_{1}(p)}-\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}}\right\|_{\operatorname{TV}(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})}=1,\qquad\lim\left\|\mu_{A_{p}}^{(1+\epsilon)t^{\operatorname{mix}}_{1}(p)}-\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}}\right\|_{\operatorname{TV}(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})}=0.

In particular, the cut-off phenomenon occurs.

Remark
.

Since
\sum_{p}\frac{1}{p}=\infty
, items (1) and (3) of Theorem
4
demonstrate that almost surely among a sequence of walks, infinitely often there are slowly mixing walks which are slower than the typical behavior by a factor of
\gg\frac{p^{\frac{2}{k(p)}}}{k(p)}
.

Remark
.

(3) of Theorem
4
gives a cut-off
sequence with, for
0<\epsilon<\frac{1}{2}
,
period of transition between
t_{1}^{\operatorname{mix}}(1-\epsilon)
and

of length

O_{\epsilon}\left(\frac{t^{\operatorname{mix}}_{1}}{\sqrt{k}}\right)

While this is

given in

transition period for many known examples giving cut-off. For instance, the
transition period of random walk on the hypercube is faster than the mixing
time by a factor which is logarithmic in the number of generators.

Lemma 5
.

Let

Let

For

the standard Gaussian density.
As

\left\|\nu_{k}^{*n}\ast\mathbf{1}_{\left[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right)^{k}}-\eta_{k}\left(\sqrt{\frac{2n}{2k+1}},\cdot\right)\right\|_{\operatorname{TV}(\mathbb{R}^{k})}=o(1).

After transition to the diffusion model, the measure on lattices induced from
the random choice in Theorem

Theorem 6
.

For
p\in{\mathscr{P}}

1.2.
Discussion of method

Our arguments view random walk on the cycle
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
with symmetric generating set
A
,
|A|=2k+1
as random walk on an index
p
quotient of
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
, in which a standard basis vector is assigned to each non-zero symmetric pair
\{x,-x\}
of generators. The index
p
lattice is the set
\Lambda=\{\underline{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}:\sum_{x}n_{x}x\equiv 0\bmod p\}
. In the case of Theorem
6
, the corresponding lattice is approximately cubic, and the argument is a perturbation of the Fourier analytic analysis of the hypercube walk in
[
11
]
. In particular, the mixing time and cut-off are the same in total variation and in
L^{2}
.

For
k\leq\frac{\log p}{\log\log p}
, a random index
p
lattice gives a mixing time in total variation which is less than the
L^{2}
mixing time by a constant, and thus the
L^{2}
methods of proving cut-off are not immediately suitable. Thus in our first four Theorems the arguments are made initially in time domain by first applying Lemma
5
to replace the discrete random walk with a diffusion on
\mathbb{R}^{k}/\Lambda
. This initial step is the reason for the restriction on the size of
k
since the corresponding approximation fails for
k>(1+\epsilon)\frac{\log p}{\log\log p}
. For larger
k
there is a standard method of correcting the approximation using the saddle point method, but we have not made an attempt to do so.

After having made the Gaussian approximation, Theorem
1
combines standard spectral estimates with bounds for the shortest vector in a lattice (the lower bound) and for sphere packing (the upper bound). Theorem
2
goes through in time domain, using convexity. Theorem
3
goes through in time domain, and uses an estimate for the derivative of the density in time.

Parts (1) and (2) of Theorem 4 study rare events in which the random lattice is essentially one dimensional due to the presense of many short vectors. We study these cases in frequency space. The dual lattice of an index
p
lattice of
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
is
\Lambda^{\vee}=\mathbb{Z}^{k}+\ell
where

\ell=\ell_{v}=\{av:0<a<p\},\qquad v\in\frac{1}{p}\mathbb{Z}^{k}\setminus\mathbb{Z}^{k}

is a line. We are able to show that with high probability the large Fourier coefficients arise from frequencies which are small multiples of a single vector. The analysis restricts attention to primitive vectors, and their multiples by
Farey fractions modulo
p
, which are residues
bq^{-1}\bmod p
in which
b
and
q
are bounded.

Part (3) of Theorem 4 is proven in time domain again. After removing a small
L^{1}
error, the modified density may be estimated using a variance bound. In particular, our argument requires averages concerning pairs of short vectors in a random lattice which are discrete analogues of the averages performed by Siegel and Rogers
[
23
]
,
[
22
]
regarding the
distribution of vectors in a random lattice.

1.3.
Possible extensions

From the point of view of mixing of Markov chains, an attractive open problem is to decide the Peres conjecture

\text{cut-off}\;\Leftrightarrow\;t^{\operatorname{mix}}/t^{\operatorname{rel}}\to\infty

for random walk on a cycle.

Abelian groups are prevalent in arithmetic, and there would be interest in extending the results to random walks on more general abelian groups. The class group of an imaginary quadratic field grows like the discriminant to the power
\frac{1}{2}+o(1)
, so a reweighting of Theorem
4
with roughly
d
groups of order
d
would be of interest. The techniques presented should translate without any great difficulty to studying random walk on cycles of composite order. The general case has not been considered, but see
[
28
]
for a study of random random walk on the hypercube.

To model abelian sandpiles, asymmetric generating sets should be considered.

2.1.
Classical probability

See

We have already introduced the total variation distance between two probability
measures
\mu,\nu
on a measure space
(\mathfrak{X},{\mathscr{B}})

\left\|\mu-\nu\right\|_{\operatorname{TV}(\mathfrak{X})}=\sup_{S\in{\mathscr{B}}}\left|\mu(S)-\nu(S)\right|.

In the case when
\mu
has a density with respect to
\nu
, equivalent
characterizations are

\left\|\mu-\nu\right\|_{\operatorname{TV}(\mathfrak{X})}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathfrak{X}}\left|\frac{d\mu}{d\nu}-1\right|d\nu=\int_{\mathfrak{X}}\left(\frac{d\mu}{d\nu}-1\right)\mathbf{1}\left(\frac{d\mu}{d\nu}>1\right)d\nu.

When
\mu
is the distribution of a Markov chain with stationary measure
\nu

define the
L^{2}(d\nu)
distance to stationarity by

\|\mu-\nu\|_{L^{2}(d\nu)}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\int\left(\frac{d\mu}{d\nu}-1\right)^{2}d\nu\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

with the convention that the norm is infinite if
\frac{d\mu}{d\nu}
is not in

L^{2}(d\nu)
. The factor of
\frac{1}{2}
is for consistency with the
interpretation of total variation distance as half the
L^{1}(d\nu)
norm.
For
\epsilon>0
denote
t^{\operatorname{mix}}_{2}(\epsilon)
the
\epsilon
-mixing time
of the
L^{2}(d\nu)
norm.

Lemma 7
.

Convolution with a probability measure is a contraction in the total variation
norm. Also, given symmetric probability measure
\mu
on
finite or compact
abelian group
G
, for any
0<\epsilon<1
the total variation mixing time of
random walk driven by

\mu
satisfies

t^{\operatorname{rel}}\log\frac{1}{2\epsilon}\leq t^{\operatorname{mix}}_{1}(\epsilon)\leq t^{\operatorname{mix}}_{2}(\epsilon)
and

\frac{2\pi^{2}}{27}\epsilon^{3}t^{\operatorname{rel}}\lesssim t^{\operatorname{mix}}_{1}(1-\epsilon)
as

\epsilon\downarrow 0
.

Proof.

The contraction property follows from the triangle inequality.

To prove
t^{\operatorname{mix}}_{1}(\epsilon)\leq t^{\operatorname{mix}}_{2}(\epsilon)
, use the
L^{1}

characterization of the total
variation metric and Cauchy-Schwarz

\|\mu^{*n}-\mathbb{U}_{G}\|_{\operatorname{TV}(G)}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{G}\left|\frac{d\mu^{*n}}{d\mathbb{U}_{G}}-1\right|d\mathbb{U}_{G}\leq\|\mu^{*n}-\mathbb{U}_{G}\|_{L^{2}(d\mathbb{U}_{G})}.

To prove the lower bounds regarding
t^{\operatorname{rel}}
,
observe that the eigenvalues of the
transition kernel for the
random walk are given by

\operatorname{spec}(\mu)=\left\{\mathbf{E}_{\mu}[\chi]:\chi\in\widehat{G}\right\},

where
\widehat{G}
denotes the set of characters of
G
.
Let
\chi_{1}
generate the spectral gap.
Since
\|\chi_{1}\|_{\infty}\leq 1
, we have, for any
n\geq 1
,

\|\mu^{*n}-\mathbb{U}_{G}\|_{\operatorname{TV}(G)}\geq\frac{1}{2}\left|\mathbf{E}_{\mu^{*n}}[\chi_{1}]\right|=\frac{1}{2}\left|\left(\mathbf{E}_{\mu}[\chi_{1}]\right)^{n}\right|,

so that the first mixing time bound follows by taking logarithms.

To obtain the
bound for
t^{\operatorname{mix}}_{1}(1-\epsilon)
, let
\epsilon_{0}>\epsilon_{1}
be small
parameters, satisfying, for some
A,B>0
,
\epsilon_{0}=A\epsilon^{2}
,
\epsilon_{1}=B\epsilon^{3}
. Let
n

be maximal such that
\mathbf{E}_{\mu^{*n}}[\chi_{1}]\geq 1-\epsilon_{1}
.
Set
S=\{g\in G:\operatorname{Re}(\chi_{1}(g))\geq 1-\epsilon_{0}\}
and
\alpha=\mu^{*n}(S)
. Bounding
\operatorname{Re}\left(\chi|_{S}\right)\leq 1
and

\operatorname{Re}\left(\chi|_{S^{c}}\right)\leq 1-\epsilon_{0}
,

(1-\epsilon_{1})\leq\mathbf{E}_{\mu^{*n}}[\chi_{1}]\leq\alpha+(1-\epsilon_{0})(1-\alpha)

whence
\alpha\geq 1-\frac{\epsilon_{1}}{\epsilon_{0}}
. According to uniform
measure,
\operatorname{Re}(\chi)
has the same distribution as
\cos(2\pi x)
on
(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z},dx)
, so that

\mathbb{U}_{G}(S)=\frac{\cos^{-1}(1-\epsilon_{0})}{\pi}=\frac{\sqrt{2\epsilon_{0}}}{\pi}(1+O(\epsilon_{0})).

It follows that

\displaystyle\left\|\mu^{*n}-\mathbb{U}_{G}\right\|_{\operatorname{TV}(G)}

\displaystyle\geq\mu^{*n}(S)-\mathbb{U}_{G}(S)

\displaystyle\geq 1-\frac{\epsilon_{1}}{\epsilon_{0}}-\frac{\cos^{-1}(1-\epsilon_{0})}{\pi}=1-\left(\frac{B}{A}+\frac{\sqrt{2A}}{\pi}+O(\epsilon^{2})\right)\epsilon.

Imposing the constraint
\mu^{*n}(S)-\mathbb{U}_{G}(S)\geq 1-\epsilon
gives

.
As
\epsilon\downarrow 0
, one obtains the constraint
\left(\frac{B}{A}+\frac{\sqrt{2A}}{\pi}+O(\epsilon^{2})\right)<1
, which gives the asymptotic
claimed with

,

.

∎

Define the standard symmetric centered normal distribution on

For

We use several results regarding concentration of the Gaussian measure.

Lemma 8
.

Let

and
\sigma>0
. There are positive constants

such
that,

and, for all

, for all
2\leq p<\infty
,

\displaystyle\int_{\underline{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}}\eta_{k}(\sigma,\underline{x})\mathbf{1}\left(\|\underline{x}\|_{p}>C_{p}\sigma k^{\frac{1}{p}}+t\sigma\right)d\underline{x}

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(-\frac{t^{2}}{2}\right).

Proof.

All quantities scale with
\sigma
so we may assume
\sigma=1
. Let

\gamma_{k}
denote the measure on
\mathbb{R}^{k}
with density

Let

. Since

[

∎

2.2.
Lattices

Siegel’s
Lectures on the Geometry of Numbers

[
24
]
are a recommended reference.

A lattice
\Lambda<\mathbb{R}^{k}
is a discrete finite co-volume subgroup of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
.
Write

\operatorname{vol}(\Lambda)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}/\Lambda}d\underline{x}

for its co-volume.
Fixing the usual inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
on
\mathbb{R}^{k}
,
the dual lattice of lattice
\Lambda
is

\Lambda^{\vee}=\left\{\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}:\forall\,\lambda\in\Lambda,\langle\lambda^{\prime},\lambda\rangle\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}.

This satisfies
\operatorname{vol}(\Lambda)\cdot\operatorname{vol}(\Lambda^{\vee})=1
. For instance, the dual lattice to
\Lambda=2\mathbb{Z}
is
\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
. More generally, if
\Lambda=Q\mathbb{Z}^{k}
for some
Q\in\operatorname{GL}_{k}(\mathbb{R})
, then
\Lambda^{\vee}=(Q^{-1})^{t}\mathbb{Z}^{k}
. We reserve

\lambda^{*}

for the shortest non-zero vector of
\Lambda^{\vee}
.

Given lattice
\Lambda<\mathbb{R}^{k}
, its norm-minimal fundamental domain (Voronoi cell) is

{\mathscr{F}}(\Lambda)=\{\underline{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}:\forall\;\lambda\in\Lambda\setminus\{0\},\|\underline{x}\|<\|\underline{x}-\lambda\|\}.

One may choose a set
{\mathscr{F}}^{0}(\Lambda)

{\mathscr{F}}(\Lambda)\subset{\mathscr{F}}^{0}(\Lambda)\subset\overline{{\mathscr{F}}(\Lambda)}

such that every
\underline{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}/\Lambda
has a unique representative in

{\mathscr{F}}^{0}(\Lambda)
.

Minkowski’s geometry of numbers gives an upper bound for the shortest non-zero
vector in a lattice.

Theorem 9

(Minkowski’s Theorem)
.

Let
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
be a
lattice and let
C
be a convex symmetric body, i.e.
\underline{x}\in C\Leftrightarrow-\underline{x}\in C
. If

\mathrm{vol}(C)>2^{k}\mathrm{vol}(\Lambda)

then
C
contains a non-zero vector in

\Lambda
. In particular

\min_{\lambda\in\Lambda\setminus\{0\}}\|\lambda\|_{2}\leq\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\left(\Gamma\left(\frac{k}{2}+1\right)\operatorname{vol}(\Lambda)\right)^{\frac{1}{k}}\sim\sqrt{\frac{2k}{\pi e}}\operatorname{vol}(\Lambda)^{\frac{1}{k}}.

with the asymptotic holding as
k\to\infty
.

For lattice
\Lambda
, the diameter of the norm-minimal fundamental domain and
the shortest non-zero vector in the dual lattice are related as follows.

Lemma 10
.

Let
\Lambda
be a lattice with norm-minimal fundamental domain
{\mathscr{F}}
and dual
lattice
\Lambda^{\vee}
. Let
\lambda^{*}
be the shortest non-zero vector in

\Lambda^{\vee}
. We have

\|\lambda^{*}\|_{2}\cdot\operatorname{diam}({\mathscr{F}})\geq 1.

Proof.

Let
{\underline{v}}=\frac{\lambda^{*}}{\|\lambda^{*}\|_{2}}
and choose
\underline{x}
the point on
the boundary of
{\mathscr{F}}
on the ray determined by
{\underline{v}}
. Write
\underline{x}=x_{0}{\underline{v}}
. Since
\underline{x}\in\partial({\mathscr{F}})
we may find
\underline{y}\in\Lambda\setminus\{0\}

with
\left|\langle\underline{x},\underline{y}\rangle\right|=\frac{1}{2}\|\underline{y}\|_{2}^{2}.
Set
\underline{y}=y_{0}{\underline{v}}+{\underline{v}}^{\prime}
where
\langle{\underline{v}},{\underline{v}}^{\prime}\rangle=0
. In particular,

y_{0}\neq 0
so
\left|\langle\underline{y},\lambda^{*}\rangle\right|=\|\lambda^{*}\|_{2}|y_{0}|\geq 1
. Since
|x_{0}y_{0}|\geq\frac{1}{2}y_{0}^{2}
it follows that

\|\underline{x}\|_{2}\cdot\|\lambda^{*}\|_{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}.
The diameter is at
least as large as
2\|\underline{x}\|_{2}
.
∎

Given
\underline{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
and
R>0
, let
B_{2}(\underline{x},R)
denote the ball

B_{2}(\underline{x},R)=\{\underline{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}:\|\underline{x}-\underline{y}\|_{2}\leq R\}.

The following is an easy estimate for the number of lattice points
contained in a ball.

Lemma 11
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, let
\underline{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
and let

\left|\mathbb{Z}^{k}\cap B_{2}(\underline{x},R)\right|=\left(1+O\left(\frac{k^{\frac{3}{2}}}{R}\right)\right)\operatorname{vol}(B_{2}(\underline{x},R)).

Proof.

Let
\mu_{\underline{x},R}=\sum_{\underline{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}\cap B_{2}(\underline{x},R)}\delta_{\underline{n}}
.
Since the hypercube
\left[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right)^{k}
has diameter
\sqrt{k}
,

\mathbf{1}_{B_{2}(\underline{x},R-\sqrt{k})}\leq\mu_{\underline{x},R}\ast\mathbf{1}_{\left[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right)^{k}}\leq\mathbf{1}_{B_{2}(\underline{x},R+\sqrt{k})}

and thus

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{Z}^{k}\cap B_{2}(\underline{x},R)\right|

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}}\mu_{\underline{x},R}\ast\mathbf{1}_{\left[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right)^{k}}

\displaystyle=\left(1+O\left(\frac{\sqrt{k}}{R}\right)\right)^{k}\operatorname{vol}(B_{2}(\underline{x},R))

\displaystyle=\left(1+O\left(\frac{k^{\frac{3}{2}}}{R}\right)\right)\operatorname{vol}(B_{2}(\underline{x},R)).

∎

We also use the following estimate counting lattice points of a more general

This follows from
[

Write

for a

\mathbb{R}^{j}

\mathbb{R}^{k}
as a hyperplane
through zero in
\mathbb{R}^{k+1}

Given a probability measure
\mu\in{\mathscr{M}}(G)
,
G=\mathbb{Z}^{k}
or
G=\mathbb{R}^{k}
, and
a lattice
\Lambda<G
the quotient measure
\mu_{\Lambda}
is defined for

by

\langle f,\mu_{\Lambda}\rangle_{G/\Lambda}=\langle f,\mu\rangle_{G}.

Quotienting commutes with convolution and contracts the total variation norm.
For lattice
\Lambda<\mathbb{R}^{k}
,

and
\underline{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
, the quotient measure of Gaussian
\eta_{k}\left(\sqrt{t},\cdot\right)

is the theta function

\Theta(\underline{x},t;\Lambda)=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\eta_{k}\left(\sqrt{t},\underline{x}+\lambda\right).

This has a representation in frequency space as

\Theta(\underline{x},t;\Lambda)=\frac{1}{\operatorname{vol}(\Lambda)}\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda^{\vee}}\exp\left(-2\pi^{2}t\|\lambda\|_{2}^{2}\right)e(\lambda\cdot\underline{x}).

To check the expansion, Fourier expand
\Theta
in the orthonormal basis
\left\{\frac{e(\lambda\cdot\underline{x})}{\sqrt{\operatorname{vol}(\Lambda)}}\right\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda^{\vee}}
for

(this is the usual proof of the Poisson summation formula).
In the case of a cubic lattice, where for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
,
\Lambda=\alpha\mathbb{Z}^{k}
, the theta function is particularly pleasant.

Lemma 13
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
,
\alpha,t\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and
\underline{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
. We
have

\Theta\left(\underline{x},t;\alpha\mathbb{Z}^{k}\right)=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\Theta\left(x_{i},t;\alpha\mathbb{Z}\right).

The one dimensional theta function

satisfies

\displaystyle\Theta(x,t;\alpha\mathbb{Z})

\displaystyle=\frac{\exp\left(-\frac{\alpha^{2}\left\|\frac{x}{\alpha}\right\|_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}^{2}}{2t}\right)}{\sqrt{2\pi t}}+O\left(\frac{\exp\left(-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{8t}\right)}{\sqrt{2\pi t}\left(1-\exp\left(-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{8t}\right)\right)}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\alpha}+O\left(\frac{\exp\left(-\frac{2\pi^{2}t}{\alpha^{2}}\right)}{\alpha\left(1-\exp\left(-\frac{2\pi^{2}t}{\alpha^{2}}\right)\right)}\right).

Proof.

The factorization is immediate from the definition of
\Theta
. The first
estimate for

is the result of pulling out the largest
term and bounding the remaining terms by a geometric progression. For the
second, apply the Poisson summation formula,

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\eta_{1}\left(\sqrt{t},x+\alpha n\right)=\frac{1}{\alpha}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\exp\left(-\frac{2\pi^{2}tn^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}\right)e\left(\frac{xn}{\alpha}\right)

and bound the

\mathbb{U}_{L^{0}(p,k)}

Combining this discussion with Minkowski’s theorem has the following
consequence.

Lemma 14
.

Let
p
be a large prime, let
1\leq k<\frac{2\log p}{\log\log p}
and let
A\in{\mathscr{A}}(p,k)
. Let

\Lambda<\mathbb{Z}^{k}
be any lattice in the class of
\Lambda(A)\in{\mathscr{L}}
, and let

\ell(A)=\min\{\|\lambda\|_{2}:0\neq\lambda\in\Lambda^{\vee}\}.

The relaxation time of random walk driven by

\mu_{A}
on
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
satisfies

t^{\operatorname{rel}}\sim\frac{2k+1}{4\pi^{2}\ell(A)^{2}}.

Proof.

The characters of
\mathbb{Z}^{k}/\Lambda
are given by the dual group,

\Lambda^{\vee}/\mathbb{Z}^{k}
.
Let
\lambda^{*}=(\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{k})
be a vector of minimal length in

\Lambda^{\vee}\setminus\{0\}
. The claim follows on noting that the spectral gap is given by

1-\hat{\nu}_{\Lambda}(\lambda^{*})=\frac{1}{2k+1}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(2-2\cos\left(2\pi\lambda_{j}\right)\right)=\frac{4\pi^{2}}{2k+1}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(\lambda_{j}^{2}+O(\lambda_{j}^{4})\right).

The error is of lower order since
\|\lambda^{*}\|_{\infty}\ll\sqrt{k}p^{-\frac{1}{k}}=o(1)
by Minkowski’s
Theorem.
∎

Lemma
5
from the introduction has the following
consequence.

Lemma 15
.

Let
p\geq 3
be a prime, let
1\leq k\leq\frac{\log p}{\log\log p}
and let
A\in{\mathscr{A}}(p,k)
with

\Lambda<\mathbb{Z}^{k}
any representative of
\Lambda(A)\in{\mathscr{L}}^{0}(p,k)
.
There is a function
\epsilon:\mathbb{R}_{>0}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
with
\lim_{x\to\infty}\epsilon(x)=0
, such that, for
n\geq 1

\left\|\mu_{A}^{*n}-\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}}\right\|_{\operatorname{TV}(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})}=\left\|\Theta\left(\cdot,\frac{2n}{2k+1};\Lambda\right)-\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{R}^{k}/\Lambda}\right\|_{\operatorname{TV}(\mathbb{R}^{k}/\Lambda)}+O\left(\epsilon\left(\frac{n}{k^{2}}\right)\right).

Proof.

Write
\Lambda=\Lambda(A)
and
\mathbf{1}_{\left[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right)^{k}}
for the
indicator function of the cube
\left[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right)^{k}\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
.
We have

\left\|\mu_{A}^{*n}-\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}}\right\|_{\operatorname{TV}(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})}=\left\|\nu_{\Lambda}^{*n}-\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{Z}^{k}/\Lambda}\right\|_{\operatorname{TV}(\mathbb{Z}^{k}/\Lambda)}=\left\|\nu_{\Lambda}^{*n}\ast\mathbf{1}_{\left[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right)^{k}}-\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{R}^{k}/\Lambda}\right\|_{\operatorname{TV}(\mathbb{R}^{k}/\Lambda)}

and

\displaystyle\Biggl|\left\|\nu_{\Lambda}^{*n}\ast\mathbf{1}_{\left[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right)^{k}}-\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{R}^{k}/\Lambda}\right\|_{\operatorname{TV}(\mathbb{R}^{k}/\Lambda)}

\displaystyle-\left\|\Theta\left(\underline{x},\frac{2n}{2k+1};\Lambda\right)-\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{R}^{k}/\Lambda}\right\|_{\operatorname{TV}(\mathbb{R}^{k}/\Lambda)}\Biggr|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\nu_{\Lambda}^{*n}\ast\mathbf{1}_{\left[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right)^{k}}-\Theta\left(\underline{x},\frac{2n}{2k+1};\Lambda\right)\right\|_{\operatorname{TV}(\mathbb{R}^{k}/\Lambda)}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\nu^{*n}\ast\mathbf{1}_{\left[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right)^{k}}-\eta_{k}\left(\sqrt{\frac{2n}{2k+1}},\cdot\right)\right\|_{\operatorname{TV}(\mathbb{R}^{k})}

by two applications of the triangle inequality. The bound now follows from
Lemma
5
.

∎

Combining the pieces above we prove the following lemma which is the main
reduction in this section.

Lemma 16
.

Let
0<\epsilon<1
, and let
k=k(p)
satisfy
1\leq k\leq\frac{\log p}{\log\log p}
. For
any set
A\in{\mathscr{A}}(p,k)
with uniform measure
\mu_{A}
of total variation
mixing time
t^{\operatorname{mix}}_{1}(\epsilon)
, we have, as

, for all

\left\|\mu_{A}^{*n}(x)-\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}}\right\|_{\operatorname{TV}(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})}=\left\|\Theta\left(\underline{x},\frac{2n}{2k+1};\Lambda(A)\right)-\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{R}^{k}/\Lambda(A)}\right\|_{\operatorname{TV}(\mathbb{R}^{k}/\Lambda(A))}+o_{\epsilon}(1).

Proof.

By Minkowski’s geometry of numbers, the shortest non-zero vector in the dual

\Lambda(A)^{\vee}
has length

so that Lemmas

and

give
for the discrete walk
t^{\operatorname{mix}}_{1}(\epsilon)\gg t^{\operatorname{rel}}\gg p^{\frac{2}{k}}

∎

3.
Mixing time estimates

Let
p
be prime,
A\in{\mathscr{A}}(p)
with
|A|=2k+1
and
1\leq k\leq\frac{\log p}{\log\log p}
. Let
\Lambda=\Lambda(A)
be any
lattice associated to
A
in
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
, as above.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Theorem
1
is contained in the set of estimates

\tau_{0}\frac{2k+1}{16\pi\Gamma\left(\frac{k}{2}+1\right)^{\frac{2}{k}}}p^{\frac{2}{k}}\lesssim_{p}\tau_{0}t^{\operatorname{rel}}\lesssim_{p}t^{\operatorname{mix}}_{1}\lesssim_{k}0.163kt^{\operatorname{rel}}.

since

\frac{2k+1}{16\pi\Gamma\left(\frac{k}{2}+1\right)^{\frac{2}{k}}}\to\frac{e}{4\pi},\qquad k\to\infty.

Combining Lemma
14
and Minkowski’s theorem gives

t^{\operatorname{rel}}\sim\frac{2k+1}{4\pi^{2}\ell(A)^{2}}\geq\frac{2k+1}{16\pi\Gamma\left(\frac{k}{2}+1\right)^{\frac{2}{k}}}p^{\frac{2}{k}}.

The estimate
t^{\operatorname{rel}}(1-\log 2)\leq t^{\operatorname{mix}}_{1}
is given in Lemma

7
. To replace
(1-\log 2)
with the larger constant
\tau_{0}
, consider the theta function
\Theta\left(\underline{x},\frac{2t}{2k+1};\Lambda\right)
, which has asymptotically the same relaxation time as
\mu_{A}
by Lemma
14
. Let
\lambda^{*}
be a shortest non-zero vector in the dual space, and consider

\Theta_{0}\left(\underline{x},\frac{2t}{2k+1};\Lambda\right)=\frac{1}{p}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\exp\left(\frac{-4\pi^{2}t}{2k+1}\|\lambda^{*}\|_{2}^{2}j^{2}\right)e(j\lambda^{*}\cdot\underline{x}),

which is found by projecting
\Theta
in frequency space onto the line determined by
\lambda^{*}
.
Equivalently, identify
\mathbb{R}^{k-1}
with
\mathbb{R}^{k}\cap(\lambda^{*})^{\perp}
and let
\eta_{k-1}(T,\cdot)
denote a Gaussian of covariance matrix
T^{2}I
on this space. Write
\lambda\in\Lambda^{\vee}
as
\lambda=\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}
where
\lambda_{1}
is the projection to the span of
\lambda^{*}
and
\lambda_{2}
is orthogonal to
\lambda^{*}
. One has, for
T>0
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}\cap(\lambda^{*})^{\perp}}\eta_{k-1}(T,\underline{y})\Theta\left(\underline{x}+\underline{y},\frac{2t}{2k+1};\Lambda\right)d\underline{y}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda^{\vee}}\exp\left(-\frac{4\pi^{2}t}{2k+1}\|\lambda\|_{2}^{2}-2\pi^{2}T^{2}\|\lambda_{2}\|_{2}^{2}\right)e(\lambda_{1}\cdot x)

and thus

\Theta_{0}\left(\underline{x},\frac{2t}{2k+1};\Lambda\right)=\lim_{T\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}\cap(\lambda^{*})^{\perp}}\eta_{k-1}(T,\underline{y})\Theta\left(\underline{x}+\underline{y},\frac{2t}{2k+1};\Lambda\right)d\underline{y}.

The convergence is uniform in
\underline{x}
as the error at
T
is dominated by the case in which
\underline{x}
is orthogonal to
\lambda^{*}
so that all the terms are positive. This justifies exchanging the limit and integral in the following calculation. Let
{\mathscr{F}}
be a fundamental domain for
\mathbb{R}^{k}/\Lambda
.

\displaystyle\left\|\Theta_{0}\left(\underline{x},\frac{2t}{2k+1};\Lambda\right)-\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{R}^{k}/\Lambda}\right\|_{\operatorname{TV}(\mathbb{R}^{k}/\Lambda)}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathscr{F}}}\left|\Theta_{0}\left(\underline{x},\frac{2t}{2k+1};\Lambda\right)-\frac{1}{p}\right|d\underline{x}

\displaystyle=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathscr{F}}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}\cap(\lambda^{*})^{\perp}}\eta_{k-1}(T,\underline{y})\left(\Theta\left(\underline{x}+\underline{y},\frac{2t}{2k+1};\Lambda\right)-\frac{1}{p}\right)d\underline{y}\right|d\underline{x}

Applying the triangle inequality,

\displaystyle\left\|\Theta_{0}-\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{R}^{k}/\Lambda}\right\|_{\operatorname{TV}(\mathbb{R}^{k}/\Lambda)}

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathscr{F}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}\cap(\lambda^{*})^{\perp}}\eta_{k-1}(T,\underline{y})\left|\Theta\left(\underline{x}+\underline{y},\frac{2t}{2k+1};\Lambda\right)-\frac{1}{p}\right|d\underline{y}d\underline{x}

\displaystyle=\lim_{T\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k}\cap(\lambda^{*})^{\perp}}\eta_{k-1}(T,\underline{y})\left\|\Theta-\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{R}^{k}/\Lambda}\right\|_{\operatorname{TV}(\mathbb{R}^{k}/\Lambda)}d\underline{y}

\displaystyle=\left\|\Theta-\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{R}^{k}/\Lambda}\right\|_{\operatorname{TV}(\mathbb{R}^{k}/\Lambda)}.

Let

\theta(x,t)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\exp(-2\pi^{2}tj^{2})e(jx)

denote the time
t
Gaussian diffusion on
\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
. For
t>0
,

\left\|\Theta_{0}\left(\cdot,\frac{t}{\|\lambda^{*}\|_{2}^{2}};\Lambda\right)-\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{R}^{k}/\Lambda}\right\|_{\operatorname{TV}(\mathbb{R}^{k}/\Lambda)}=\|\theta(\cdot,t)-\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\|_{\operatorname{TV}(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z})}.

Since the latter distance is monotonically decreasing and smooth, and since for
n\geq t_{1}^{\operatorname{mix}}
,

\left\|\mu_{A}^{*n}-\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{Z}^{k}/\Lambda}\right\|_{\operatorname{TV}(\mathbb{Z}^{k}/\Lambda)}=\left\|\Theta\left(\cdot,\frac{2n}{2k+1};\Lambda\right)-\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{R}^{k}/\Lambda}\right\|_{\operatorname{TV}(\mathbb{R}^{k}/\Lambda)}+o(1)

by Lemma
16
, it follows that
t_{1}^{\operatorname{mix}}\gtrsim\tau_{0}t^{\operatorname{rel}}
.

To give the spectral upper bound for
t^{\operatorname{mix}}_{1}
, again consider instead the
distance from uniformity of
\Theta\left(\cdot,\frac{2n}{2k+1};\Lambda\right)

on
\mathbb{R}^{k}/\Lambda
.
For
t>0
,

\displaystyle\left\|\Theta\left(\cdot,\frac{2t}{2k+1};\Lambda\right)-\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{R}^{k}/\Lambda}\right\|_{\operatorname{TV}(\mathbb{R}^{k}/\Lambda)}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{4}\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda^{\vee}\setminus\{0\}}\exp\left(-\frac{8\pi^{2}t\|\lambda\|_{2}^{2}}{2k+1}\right)

Writing the sum as a Stieltjes integral, then integrating by parts, the right hand side becomes

(3.1)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\int_{s=1^{-}}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{8\pi^{2}t\|\lambda^{*}\|_{2}^{2}s^{2}}{2k+1}\right)d\left(\left|\Lambda^{\vee}\cap B_{2}(0,s\|\lambda^{*}\|)\right|\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{4\pi^{2}t\|\lambda^{*}\|_{2}^{2}}{2k+1}\int_{1-}^{\infty}s\exp\left(-\frac{8\pi^{2}t\|\lambda^{*}\|_{2}^{2}s^{2}}{2k+1}\right)\left|\Lambda^{\vee}\cap B_{2}(0,s\|\lambda^{*}\|)\right|ds.

Set
t=\tau\frac{2k+1}{4\pi^{2}\|\lambda^{*}\|_{2}^{2}}
so that
\tau\sim\frac{t}{t^{\operatorname{rel}}}.
Thus (
3.1
) simplifies to

\displaystyle(\ref{L_2_estimate})

\displaystyle=\tau\int_{1^{-}}^{\infty}s\exp\left(-2\tau s^{2}\right)\left|\Lambda^{\vee}\cap B_{2}(0,s\|\lambda^{*}\|)\right|ds

\displaystyle\leq\tau\int_{1^{-}}^{\infty}s\exp\left(-2\tau s^{2}+(1+\varepsilon(k))kF(s)\right)ds

where
\varepsilon(k)\to 0
as
k\to\infty
, and

F(s)=\left[\frac{1+\sin\theta}{2\sin\theta}\log\frac{1+\sin\theta}{2\sin\theta}-\frac{1-\sin\theta}{2\sin\theta}\log\frac{1-\sin\theta}{2\sin\theta}\right],\qquad\theta(s)=2\sin^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{2s}\right)

see Lemma

12
. The maximum of
\frac{F(s)}{s^{2}}
in
s\geq 1
occurs at
s=1.260816271(1)
with maximum
<0.324908241
and

\frac{F(s)}{s^{2}}\to 0
as
s\to\infty
. Thus, choosing
2\tau=(0.325+\tilde{\varepsilon}(k))k

for an appropriate function
\tilde{\varepsilon}(k)
tending to 0 as
k\to\infty
the
L^{2}
distance is negligible so that
\tau t^{\operatorname{rel}}
is an upper
bound for
t^{\operatorname{mix}}_{2}\geq t^{\operatorname{mix}}_{1}
.
∎

3.1.
Geometric mixing time bound, proof of Theorem

2

Let
p
,
A
and

\Lambda
as above, and let
{\mathscr{F}}
be the Voronoi cell for
\mathbb{R}^{k}/\Lambda
. Note that
\mathbb{Z}^{k}\cap\overline{{\mathscr{F}}}

contains a system of representatives for
\mathbb{Z}^{k}/\Lambda
, and that the Cayley
graph
{\mathscr{C}}(A,p)
is isomorphic to
{\mathscr{C}}(\{0,\pm e_{i}:1\leq i\leq k\},\mathbb{Z}^{k}/\Lambda)
. Thus

\operatorname{rad}({\mathscr{F}}):=\sup\left\{\|\underline{x}\|_{2}:\underline{x}\in{\mathscr{F}}\right\}=\operatorname{diam}_{\operatorname{geom}}({\mathscr{C}}(A,p)).

Proof of Theorem
2
.

Write
D=\operatorname{diam}_{\operatorname{geom}}({\mathscr{C}}(A,p))
and assume, as we may, that
t>kD^{2}
.
In view of Lemma
10
, which proves
D\geq\frac{1}{\ell(A)}
, we have
t\gg t^{\operatorname{rel}}
, and thus as in Lemma
16

\left\|\mu_{A}^{*t}-\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}}\right\|_{\operatorname{TV}(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})}+o(1)=\left\|\Theta\left(\cdot,\frac{2t}{2k+1};\Lambda\right)-\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{R}^{k}/\Lambda}\right\|_{\operatorname{TV}(\mathbb{R}^{k}/\Lambda)},

so we will estimate the right hand side.

Since, for any
\underline{x}
,
t
,
\mathbf{E}_{\underline{y}\in{\mathscr{F}}}\left[\Theta\left(\underline{x}+\underline{y},\frac{2t}{2k+1};\Lambda\right)\right]=\frac{1}{p}
, we may estimate using the triangle inequality

\displaystyle\left\|\Theta-\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{R}^{k}/\Lambda}\right\|_{\operatorname{TV}(\mathbb{R}^{k}/\Lambda)}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\underline{x}\in{\mathscr{F}}}\left|\Theta\left(\underline{x},\frac{2t}{2k+1};\Lambda\right)-\mathbf{E}_{\underline{y}\in{\mathscr{F}}}\left[\Theta\left(\underline{x}+\underline{y},\frac{2t}{2k+1};\Lambda\right)\right]\right|d\underline{x}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\underline{x}\in{\mathscr{F}}}\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\left|\eta_{k}\left(\sqrt{\frac{2t}{2k+1}},\underline{x}-\lambda\right)-\mathbf{E}_{\underline{y}\in{\mathscr{F}}}\left[\eta_{k}\left(\sqrt{\frac{2t}{2k+1}},\underline{x}+\underline{y}-\lambda\right)\right]\right|d\underline{x}.

Now use the inequality
|1-e^{x}|\leq e^{|x|}-1

to obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\Theta-\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{R}^{k}/\Lambda}\right\|_{\operatorname{TV}(\mathbb{R}^{k}/\Lambda)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\underline{x}\in{\mathscr{F}}}\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\eta_{k}\left(\sqrt{\frac{2t}{2k+1}},\underline{x}-\lambda\right)\mathbf{E}_{\underline{y}\in{\mathscr{F}}}\left[\exp\left(\frac{2k+1}{4t}\left(\|\underline{y}\|_{2}^{2}+2|\langle\underline{x}-\lambda,\underline{y}\rangle|\right)\right)-1\right]d\underline{x}.

Fold together the sum over
\lambda
and the integral over
\underline{x}
, then integrate away all directions in
\underline{x}
orthogonal to
\underline{y}
to obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\Theta\left(\cdot,\frac{2t}{2k+1};\Lambda\right)-\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{R}^{k}/\Lambda}\right\|_{\operatorname{TV}(\mathbb{R}^{k}/\Lambda)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\eta_{1}\left(\sqrt{\frac{2t}{2k+1}},x\right)\mathbf{E}_{\underline{y}\in{\mathscr{F}}}\left[\exp\left(\frac{2k+1}{4t}\left(\|\underline{y}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\underline{y}\|_{2}|x|\right)\right)-1\right]dx

\displaystyle\ll D\sqrt{\frac{k}{t}}.

The last estimate follows on using
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}}e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{2}+\delta|x|}dx=1+O(\delta)
as
\delta\downarrow 0
.
∎

4.
Transition window bound, proof of Theorem

3

We prove the following somewhat more general theorem.

Theorem 17
.

Let
p
be a large prime and let
k\leq\frac{\log p}{\log\log p}
. Let
A\subset\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
be a lazy symmetric
generating set of size
|A|=2k+1

Proof.

Let
\Lambda<\mathbb{Z}^{k}
be any lattice representing the class of
\Lambda(A)\in{\mathscr{L}}
. By Lemma
16
we may replace

\left\|\mu_{A}^{*n}-\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}}\right\|_{\operatorname{TV}(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})}

\left\|\Theta\left(\underline{x},\frac{2n}{2k+1};\Lambda\right)-\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{R}^{k}/\Lambda}\right\|_{\operatorname{TV}(\mathbb{R}^{k}/\Lambda)}
making error
o(1)
.

Write
n=\sigma t^{\operatorname{mix}}_{1}(\epsilon_{1})
. Differentiating under the sum in the
\theta

(4.1)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\sigma}\Theta\left(\underline{x},\frac{2\sigma t^{\operatorname{mix}}_{1}(\epsilon_{1})}{2k+1};\Lambda\right)\Bigg|_{\sigma=\sigma^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{k}{2\sigma^{\prime}}\Theta\left(\underline{x},\frac{2\sigma^{\prime}t^{\operatorname{mix}}_{1}(\epsilon_{1})}{2k+1};\Lambda\right).

Also,
\left\|\Theta\left(\underline{x},\frac{2\sigma t^{\operatorname{mix}}_{1}(\epsilon_{1})}{2k+1};\Lambda\right)-\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{R}^{k}/\Lambda}\right\|_{\operatorname{TV}(\mathbb{R}^{k}/\Lambda)}
is a
decreasing function of

. Define

Now for any

\displaystyle\left\|\Theta\left(\cdot,\frac{2\sigma t^{\operatorname{mix}}_{1}(\epsilon_{1})}{2k+1};\Lambda\right)-\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{R}^{k}/\Lambda}\right\|_{\operatorname{TV}(\mathbb{R}^{k}/\Lambda)}

\displaystyle=\int_{P(\sigma)}\Theta\left(\underline{x},\frac{2\sigma t^{\operatorname{mix}}_{1}(\epsilon_{1})}{2k+1};\Lambda\right)-\frac{1}{p}d\underline{x}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{P(\sigma_{0})}\Theta\left(\underline{x},\frac{2\sigma t^{\operatorname{mix}}_{1}(\epsilon_{1})}{2k+1};\Lambda\right)-\frac{1}{p}d\underline{x}.

Thus for
\sigma>\sigma_{0}
,

\displaystyle\left\|\Theta\left(\cdot,\frac{2\sigma t^{\operatorname{mix}}_{1}(\epsilon_{1})}{2k+1};\Lambda\right)-\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{R}^{k}/\Lambda}\right\|_{\operatorname{TV}(\mathbb{R}^{k}/\Lambda)}-\left\|\Theta\left(\cdot,\frac{2\sigma_{0}t^{\operatorname{mix}}_{1}(\epsilon_{1})}{2k+1};\Lambda\right)-\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{R}^{k}/\Lambda}\right\|_{\operatorname{TV}(\mathbb{R}^{k}/\Lambda)}

(4.2)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\geq\int_{P(\sigma_{0})}\Theta\left(\underline{x},\frac{2\sigma t^{\operatorname{mix}}_{1}(\epsilon_{1})}{2k+1};\Lambda\right)-\Theta\left(\underline{x},\frac{2\sigma_{0}t^{\operatorname{mix}}_{1}(\epsilon_{1})}{2k+1};\Lambda\right)d\underline{x}

Differentiate under the integral, then apply (
4.1
) and finally drop the restriction to
P(\sigma_{0})
to obtain the estimate

\displaystyle(\ref{P_sigma})

\displaystyle=\int_{P(\sigma_{0})}\int_{\sigma_{0}}^{\sigma}\frac{d}{ds}\Theta\left(\underline{x},\frac{2st^{\operatorname{mix}}_{1}(\epsilon_{1})}{2k+1};\Lambda\right)\Bigg|_{s=\sigma^{\prime}}d\sigma^{\prime}d\underline{x}

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{k}{2}\int_{\sigma_{0}}^{\sigma}\frac{1}{\sigma^{\prime}}\int_{P(\sigma_{0})}\Theta\left(\underline{x},\frac{2\sigma^{\prime}t^{\operatorname{mix}}_{1}(\epsilon_{1})}{2k+1};\Lambda\right)d\underline{x}d\sigma^{\prime}

(4.3)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{-k}{2}\log\frac{\sigma}{\sigma_{0}}.

Note that
k=o\left(t^{\operatorname{mix}}_{1}(\epsilon_{1})\right)
.
Applying (
4.3
) with
\sigma_{0}=1-\frac{1}{t^{\operatorname{mix}}_{1}(\epsilon_{1})}
and

, which corresponds to the random walk at the mixing time and the step
before, we deduce

\left\|\Theta\left(\cdot,\frac{2t^{\operatorname{mix}}_{1}(\epsilon_{1})}{2k+1};\Lambda\right)-\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{R}^{k}/\Lambda}\right\|_{\operatorname{TV}(\mathbb{R}^{k}/\Lambda)}=\epsilon_{1}+o_{\epsilon_{1}}(1).

Applying (
4.3
) again, but

, we obtain
in the range
t^{\operatorname{mix}}_{1}(\epsilon_{1})<n<\exp(\frac{2\epsilon_{2}}{k})\cdot t^{\operatorname{mix}}_{1}(\epsilon_{1})
,

\displaystyle\left\|\mu_{A}^{(n)}-\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}}\right\|_{\operatorname{TV}(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})}\geq\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{2}+o_{\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2}}(1).

∎

5.
Random random walk, proof of Theorem
4

We record several facts regarding the uniform measure
\mathbb{U}_{L}
on the set

p

\mathbb{Z}^{k}

Lemma 18
.

When
\Lambda

\Lambda^{\vee}
has the distribution of

\{0,1,\cdots,p-1\}\frac{{\underline{v}}}{p}+\mathbb{Z}^{k}

.

When
\Lambda
is chosen uniformly from

{\mathscr{D}}=\{{\underline{v}}\in(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\})^{k}:\forall 1\leq i<j\leq k,v_{i}\neq\pm v_{j}\}.

In the case of

[\Lambda:\mathbb{Z}^{k}]=p

\frac{1}{p}\mathbb{Z}^{k}<\Lambda

\operatorname{SL}_{k}(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})

The further conditions imposed in the case of

are those necessary to

\Lambda
does not contain a vector
\lambda
with
\|\lambda\|_{2}^{2}\in\{1,2\}.

∎

Lemma 19
.

Let
p
be prime, let
k\geq 2
and let
{\underline{v}}\neq\underline{w}\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}

5.1.
Summary of argument

As the calculations in the remainder of this section are somewhat involved, we
pause to sketch the main ideas.

Theorem
4
has three claims, the first two of which
consider the worst case mixing time behavior, with the third considering
typical behavior. When considering the walk as a diffusion on

\mathbb{R}^{k}/\Lambda

where
\Lambda
is a lattice, the spectrum of the transition kernel is
determined by the dual lattice
\Lambda^{\vee}
. In general, it is
difficult to work on the spectral side due to the high concentration of
eigenvalues near the spectral gap, but in the worst case regime we are able to
show that for all behavior that persists, the dual lattice is essentially one
dimensional. When this occurs the mixing and relaxation times are
proportional and we obtain a slow transition.

In typical behavior the walk has a sharp transition to uniformity. The analysis
in this regime consists of separate arguments estimating the
distance to uniformity at times
(1\pm\epsilon)t^{\operatorname{mix}}_{1}
. When considering
the walk at time
(1-\epsilon)t^{\operatorname{mix}}_{1}
we study the diffusion

\Theta\left(\underline{x},\frac{2t}{2k+1};\Lambda\right)
on the norm-minimal
fundamental domain
{\mathscr{F}}(\Lambda)
for
\mathbb{R}^{k}/\Lambda
. For a particular lattice

\Lambda
,
{\mathscr{F}}(\Lambda)
is a highly complex convex body determined by a number
of hyperplanes, but in a statistical sense, for the purpose of the lower bound,

{\mathscr{F}}(\Lambda)
behaves very much like the volume
p
ball of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
centered
at the origin. A Gaussian in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
centered at the origin is
concentrated on a thin spherical shell (see Lemma

8
), and the mixing time is essentially the
time needed for this spherical shell to expand to the boundary of the volume

p
ball. At time
(1-\epsilon)t^{\operatorname{mix}}_{1}
we are then
able to show that the diffusion is typically concentrated on a small measure
part of
{\mathscr{F}}(\Lambda)
.

For the upper bound at time
(1+\epsilon)t^{\operatorname{mix}}_{1}
, we note that
p\mathbb{Z}^{k}<\Lambda
, and we show that the distribution of values of

\Theta\left(\underline{x},\frac{2t}{2k+1};\Lambda\right)
is concentrated near 1
when
\underline{x}
is chosen uniformly from
\mathbb{R}^{k}/p\mathbb{Z}^{k}
and
\Lambda
is chosen at
random from
L(p,k)
. This is the most delicate part of the argument. For
instance, it is not sufficient to consider the expectation of

\left(\Theta\left(\underline{x},\frac{2t}{2k+1};\Lambda\right)-1\right)^{2}
as
this gives an upper bound which is too weak, so we split
\Theta
into an

L^{2}
-concentrated piece
\Theta_{M}
plus a small
L^{1}
error
\Theta_{E}
.

We prove
Theorem

in two parts. In this section we prove parts (1) and (2) which concern rare slow mixing walks. In Section

we prove part (3) regarding the typical behavior. The main estimate regarding slow mixing behavior is the following theorem.

Let

, parts (1) and (2).

Before giving the proof of the Theorem we prove an auxiliary claim.

Let

6.

6.1.
A Chebyshev cut-off criterion

We begin by describing a commonly used second moment method for proving cut-off,
which we apply in analyzing the power-of-2 random walk. The following is a variant of the lower bound method from
[
11
]
, see also Wilson’s lemma in
[

Given a probability measure
\mu

\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
and frequency

Define, as before, the

t^{\operatorname{mix}}_{2}=\inf\left\{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}:\sum_{0\neq\xi\in\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}}\left|\hat{\mu}(\xi)\right|^{2n}\leq\frac{4}{e^{2}}\right\}

and the spectral gap

\operatorname{gap}=1-\max_{0\neq\xi\in\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}}\left|\hat{\mu}(\xi)\right|.

.

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{\xi\in B_{p}}\hat{\mu}_{A_{p}}^{n}(\xi)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in B_{p}}\hat{\mu}_{A_{p}}^{n}(\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}).

To prove the lower bound, let

Meanwhile

Let

6.3.
Proof of Theorem

, upper bound

We prove the following somewhat more precise estimate.

Proposition 32
.

For all
0<\beta<\log\ell
, for all
n\geq\frac{\ell}{2c_{0}}(\log\ell+\beta)

we have

\|\mu_{A_{2,p}}^{*n}-\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}}\|_{\operatorname{TV}(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})}^{2}\ll e^{-\beta}+\frac{e^{-\frac{\beta}{c_{0}}}\log\ell}{\ell^{\frac{1}{c_{0}}}}.

Remark
.

The second term results from a discrepancy between the eigenvalue generating
the spectral gap and the bulk of the large spectrum which determines the
mixing time. With more effort, the factor of
\log\ell
could be removed.

The proof uses the following frequently used application of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, see
[
6
]
for an introduction to these types of estimates, also
[
7
]
.

Lemma 33
.

Let
\mu
be a probability measure on
finite abelian group
G
. We have the upper bound

\left\|\mu-\mathbb{U}_{G}\right\|_{\operatorname{TV}(G)}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{0\neq\chi\in\widehat{G}}\left|\hat{\mu}(\chi)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

In particular,

(6.13)

\|\mu_{A_{2,p}}^{*n}-\mathbb{U}_{\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}}\|_{\operatorname{TV}(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{0\not\equiv\xi\bmod p}\left|\hat{\mu}_{A_{2,p}}(\xi)\right|^{2n}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Proof.

We have

\left\|\mu-\mathbb{U}_{G}\right\|_{\operatorname{TV}(G)}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in G}\left|\mu(x)-\mathbb{U}_{G}(x)\right|.

Hence, by
Cauchy-Schwarz,

\displaystyle\left\|\mu-\mathbb{U}(G)\right\|_{\operatorname{TV}(G)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(|G|\sum_{x\in G}\left|\mu(x)-\mathbb{U}_{G}(x)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{0\neq\chi\in\widehat{G}}\left|\hat{\mu}(\chi)\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

∎

The above lemma reduces to estimation of the size of the Fourier
coefficients
\hat{\mu}_{A_{2,p}}(\xi)
. In estimating these coefficients it
will
be convenient to use the following modified binary expansion of
\frac{\xi}{p}
.

Lemma 34
.

Let
p\geq 3
be prime. For each

there is an
increasing sequence
{\mathscr{I}}=\{i_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, and

\epsilon=\pm 1
such that

\frac{\xi}{p}\equiv\epsilon\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{j}2^{-i_{j}}\bmod 1.

This representation is unique.

Proof.

Write
-\frac{\xi}{p}
in binary as
*.s_{1}s_{2}s_{3}...
with each
s_{i}\in\{0,1\}
, then write
\frac{\xi}{p}=-\frac{\xi}{p}-\left(-\frac{2\xi}{p}\right)

where
\left(-\frac{2\xi}{p}\right)
is obtained by a left shift, and then the
subtraction is performed bitwise.

The uniqueness follows because any two distinct such representations

(\epsilon,\{i_{j}\}),(\epsilon^{\prime},\{i^{\prime}_{j}\})
differ by
\gg 2^{-J}
, where

is

\min(i_{1},i^{\prime}_{1})

\{i_{j}\}\Delta\{i^{\prime}_{j}\}

6.3.1.
Index sequences

We introduce several notions which will
be useful in the remainder of the argument.

Given a real parameter

-sequence

-sequence is called

We denote

the set of

-sequences,
{\mathscr{J}}_{0}

-sequences and

J
-sequences.

A

-sequence

-subsequence. We say that

-subsequence

-subsequence

Given parameter

-subsequences.
For instance, in the first sequence above,

We write

-subsequences of

-sequences in

-separated

.

In the remainder of the argument we think of the non-zero bits in the expansion of

above as partitioned into maximal

-sequences. These

Let
J\geq\log_{2}\ell
be a parameter.
Given
\xi\bmod p

\xi
as
({\mathscr{I}}(\xi),\epsilon(\xi))
as above.
Truncate

We call

the set of

-sequence. If there exists

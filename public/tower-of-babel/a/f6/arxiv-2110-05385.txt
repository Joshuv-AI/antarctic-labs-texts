Restricted Gröbner Fans and Re-embeddings of Affine Algebras
Martin Kreuzer, Le Ngoc Long, Lorenzo Robbiano

Abstract
. In this paper we continue the study of good re-embeddings of affine K K -algebras started in [ 5 ] . The idea is to use special linear projections to find isomorphisms between a given affine K K -algebra K ⁡ [ X ] / I K[X]/I , where X = ( x 1 , … , x n ) X=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}) , and K K -algebras having fewer generators. These projections are induced by particular tuples of indeterminates Z Z and by term orderings σ \sigma which realize Z Z as leading terms of a tuple F F of polynomials in I I . In order to efficiently find such tuples, we provide two major new tools: an algorithm which reduces the check whether a given tuple F F is Z Z -separating to an LP feasibility problem, and an isomorphism between the part of the Gröbner fan of I I consisting of marked reduced Gröbner bases which contain a Z Z -separating tuple and the Gröbner fan of I ∩ K ⁡ [ X ∖ Z ] I\cap K[X\setminus Z] . We also indicate a possible generalization to tuples Z Z which consist of terms. All results are illustrated by explicit examples.

1.
Introduction

This paper is a natural continuation of
[
5
]
. The topic treated here and there
is the search for good re-embeddings of affine algebras over a field
K
,
or equivalently, of affine schemes. What do we mean by that? It is a classical research topic
in algebraic geometry to find embeddings of a given scheme into low-dimensional spaces.
For affine varieties, the main result of
[
11
]
has been generalized in
several directions (see for instance
[
10
]
and
[
9
]
, Section 10.2).
Here we follow a more computational approach which tries to avoid the frequently costly
calculation of Gröbner bases as much as possible.

Let
K
be a field and
I
an ideal in a polynomial ring
P=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
.
We are looking for a
polynomial ring
P^{\prime}=K[y_{1},\dots,y_{m}]
and an ideal
I^{\prime}
in
P^{\prime}
such
that
m<n
and such that there is a
K
-algebra isomorphism
P/I\cong P^{\prime}/I^{\prime}
.
In other words, we are looking for a smaller number of
K
-algebra generators of
P/I
.
In general, this problem is very hard, but there are chances to get
good solutions using the following type of linear projections.

Assume that
I\subseteq\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\rangle
.
Let
Z=(z_{1},\dots,z_{s})
be a set of distinct indeterminates in
X=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
,
and suppose that there exist a term ordering
\sigma
and polynomials

f_{1},\dots,f_{s}\in I
with
\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(f_{i})=z_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,s

and such that this is the only appearance of
z_{i}
in a term of one
of the polynomials
f_{1},\dots,f_{s}
. Then we say that
I
is
Z
-separating
and
\sigma
is a
Z
-separating term ordering for
I
. In this setting,
the reduced
\sigma
-Gröbner basis of
I
allows us to define
a
K
-algebra isomorphism
P/I\cong\widehat{P}/(I\cap\widehat{P})
,
where
\widehat{P}=K[X\setminus Z]
, called a
Z
-separating re-embedding of
I
.
Notice that the search for such
f_{1},\dots,f_{s}\in I
is in general non-trivial.
In particular, they can be hidden and far away from a given set of generators of
I

(see for instance
[
2
]
and
[
5
]
, Example 3.7).

As explained in
[
5
]
, the discovery of
Z
-separating
re-embeddings for non-trivial ideals
I
relies on the study of the Gröbner
fan
\mathop{\rm GFan}\nolimits(I)
which was introduced first in
[
8
]
.
As mentioned above, it may
not be possible to use a
Z
-separating re-embedding to get an optimal
re-embedding
P/I\cong P^{\prime}/I^{\prime}
in the sense that
\dim(P^{\prime})
is the embedding
dimension of
P/I
, i.e., the smallest possible number. Moreover,
the usage of Gröbner fans has the added disadvantage that their
computation is prohibitively expensive in all but the smallest examples.

This brings us to the main topic of this paper, namely the task to
improve the search for
Z
-separating re-embeddings and to use a smaller portion
of the Gröbner fan which is easier to compute. To achieve this goal, we take
a markedly different point of view than in
[
5
]
. Here we concentrate
not on specific
Z
-separating tuples, but on finding suitable tuples
Z

and the corresponding
Z
-separating tuples for a given ideal
I
.
As a consequence, we have to study the intimate relationships between
the given ideal
I
, possible tuples
Z
, and possible
Z
-separating
tuples of polynomials
F=(f_{1},\dots,f_{s})
with
f_{i}\in I
very carefully,
and in fact the entire Section
2

is devoted to this clarification task. In particular, Proposition
2.2

explains the relationship between
Z
-separating tuples and elimination orderings for
Z
.

Another novelty relates to the search for suitable
Z
-separating tuples.
Suppose we have a reasonable candidate for
Z=(z_{1},\dots,z_{s})

and for a
Z
-separating tuple
F=(f_{1},\dots,f_{s})
of polynomials in
I
.
How can we check if
F
is indeed a
Z
-separating tuple for
I
?
Recall that this means that we need to find a term ordering
\sigma
such
that
\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(f_{i})=z_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,s
.
In Section
3
we show how to convert this problem
to a Linear Programming (LP) feasibility problem (see Proposition
3.3
).
The usage of LP solvers for this task is explained in detail in Corollary
3.5
.
Several examples illustrate the efficiency and power of this approach.

In Section
4
, we characterize
Z
-separating term
orderings for
I
by the shape of their reduced Gröbner basis
(see Proposition
4.2
). This Gröbner basis allows us then to construct
the desired re-embeddings of
I
which we called
Z
-separating
re-embeddings in
[
5
]
. Altogether, we arrive at an efficient strategy for
finding good re-embeddings of
I
which does not require
the (possibly expensive) pre-calculation of a reduced Gröbner basis of
I
:

(1)

Find a (large) tuple of indeterminates
Z=(z_{1},\dots,z_{s})

and a tuple of polynomials
F=(f_{1},\dots,f_{s})
such that
f_{i}
is

z_{i}
-separating for
i=1,\dots,s
.

(2)

Using an LP solver, verify that
F
is
Z
-separating
and obtain a
Z
-separating term ordering for
I
.

(3)

With the help of some inexpensive interreduction steps,
create polynomials
z_{1}-h_{1},\dots,z_{s}-h_{s}

in
I
with
h_{i}\in\widehat{P}=K[X\setminus Z]
.

(4)

Using the polynomials
h_{1},\dots,h_{s}
, define the
Z
-separating re-embedding

\varPhi_{Z}:\;P/I\longrightarrow\widehat{P}/(I\cap\widehat{P})

of
I
.

The viability and efficiency of this strategy is then demonstrated
using some concrete examples.

The theoretical main result of this paper is contained in
Section
5
. Recall that the Gröbner fan

\mathop{\rm GFan}\nolimits(I)
of
I
consists of all marked reduced Gröbner bases of
I
.
In non-trivial cases, it tends to be huge and very demanding to compute.
In Definition
5.1
we introduce the notion
of the
Z
-restricted Gröbner fan of
I
,
denoted by
\mathop{\rm GFan}\nolimits_{Z}(I)
, which is the set of all marked reduced Gröbner bases
containing a
Z
-separating tuple of polynomials.
Then, in Theorem
5.5
, we prove that there is a bijective map

\Gamma_{Z}:\;\mathop{\rm GFan}\nolimits_{Z}(I)\longrightarrow\mathop{\rm GFan}\nolimits(I\cap\widehat{P})
.
Since
\widehat{P}=K[X\setminus Z]
may have considerably fewer indeterminates
than
P
, this turns out to be a nice tool which can
simplify the search for a good, and possibly optimal, re-embeddings of
I
.
Some examples illustrate this phenomenon.

Section
6
provides several heuristics and approaches
for actually finding good re-embeddings of
I
, and it explains how
to overcome some difficulties that may arise.
In particular, Example
6.5
deals with the problem that
the hypothesis
I\subseteq\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\rangle
, which we were
using throughout the paper, may not be satisfied. Therefore we may need
to perform a linear change of coordinates such that the origin is contained
in
\mathcal{Z}(I)
. Which point should we move to the origin?
By
[
5
]
, Theorem 4.1, the dimension of the cotangent space
at the origin is a lower bound for the embedding dimension of
P/I
.
Therefore we should move the
worst
singularity of
P/I

to the origin. Since there is a unique
K
-rational singular point
in this example, we know what we have to do, but the general situation
may be more challenging.
On the positive side, at the end of this section we also
provide a criterion which allows us to show that some re-embeddings are
actually isomorphisms between the given scheme and an affine space
(see Proposition
6.7
).

Finally, in Section
7
we generalize the approach
from using
Z
-separating tuples of polynomials to
T
-separating tuples,
where
T=(t_{1},\dots,t_{s})
denotes a tuple of terms that we try to
realize as leading terms of polynomials in
I
. To adapt the definitions
to this more general setting, we let
Z
be the tuple of indeterminates
dividing one of the terms in
T
and
Y=X\setminus Z
.
Using a suitable definition of a
T
-separating
Gröbner fan
\mathop{\rm GFan}\nolimits_{T}(I)
, we show that there is a free module
M

over
K[Y]
such that the elements of
\mathop{\rm GFan}\nolimits_{T}(I)
are related to

K[Y]
-module Gröbner bases of
I\cap M
and such that
a
T
-separating module re-embedding
P/I\cong M/(I\cap M)
of
I
results.
However, we were not able to find an analogue of Theorem
5.5

in this setting and leave this task for future research.

True to our preferred style, we have sprinkled this paper generously
with many illustrative examples. The calculations underlying these examples
were performed using the computer algebra system
CoCoA
(see
[
1
]
)
and with the help of the several
CoCoA
-packages written by the second
and third authors. For the notation and definitions used throughout the paper,
we follow
[
6
]
and
[
7
]
.

2.
Z
-Separating Polynomials, Tuples, and Ideals

In this section we use the notation introduced in
[
5
]
with some
appropriate changes and extensions. Specifically, we let
K
be a field,
let
P=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
, and let
{\mathfrak{M}}=\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\rangle
.
The tuple formed by the indeterminates of
P
is denoted by
X=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
.
Furthermore, we let
1\leq s\leq n
, let
z_{1},\dots,z_{s}
be pairwise
distinct indeterminates in
X
, and let
Z=(z_{1},\dots,z_{s})
.
The remaining indeterminates are denoted by

\{y_{1},\dots,y_{n-s}\}=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}\setminus\{z_{1},\dots,z_{s}\}
,
and we let
Y=(y_{1},\dots,y_{n-s})
.
Finally, given a term ordering
\sigma
on
P
,
its restriction to
K[Y]=K[y_{1},\dots,y_{n-s}]
is denoted by
\sigma_{Y}
.

The following definition extends
[
5
]
, Definitions 2.1 and 2.5.

Definition 2.1
.

In the above setting, let
f_{1},\dots,f_{s}\in\mathfrak{M}\setminus\{0\}
,
let
F=(f_{1},\dots,f_{s})
, and let
I_{F}=\langle f_{1},\dots,f_{s}\rangle

be the ideal generated by
\{f_{1},\dots,f_{s}\}
.

(a)

Given
i\in\{1,\dots,s\}
, we say that the polynomial
f_{i}
is

z_{i}
-separating
if
z_{i}\in\mathop{\rm Supp}\nolimits(f_{i})
and
z_{i}
does not divide
any other term in
\mathop{\rm Supp}\nolimits(f_{i})
.

(b)

The tuple
F
is called
Z
-separating
if there exists
a term ordering
\sigma
such that
\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(f_{i})=z_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,s
.
In this case
\sigma
is called a
Z
-separating term ordering
for
F
.

(c)

The tuple
F
is called
coherently
Z
-separating
if it is

Z
-separating, i.e., there exists a term ordering
\sigma
such that

\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(f_{i})=z_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,s
,
and if, additionally, the reduced
\sigma
-Gröbner basis of
I_{F}
is

\{\frac{1}{c_{1}}\,f_{1},\dots,\frac{1}{c_{s}}\,f_{s}\}
,
where
c_{i}=\mathop{\rm LC}\nolimits_{\sigma}(f_{i})
for
i=1,\dots,s
.

(d)

The ideal
I_{F}
is called
Z
-separating
if there exists a
term ordering
\sigma
such that
\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(I_{F})=\langle Z\rangle
.
In this case
\sigma
is called a
Z
-separating term ordering
for
I_{F}
.

(e)

The set of all
Z
-separating term orderings for
I_{F}
is
denoted by
\mathop{\rm Sep}\nolimits_{Z}(I_{F})
.

Let us collect some basic observations about these notions.

Proposition 2.2
.

In the above setting, let
f_{1},\dots,f_{s}\in\mathfrak{M}\setminus\{0\}
,
let
F=(f_{1},\dots,f_{s})
, and let
I_{F}=\langle f_{1},\dots,f_{s}\rangle
.

(a)

If
F
is a
Z
-separating tuple then
f_{i}
is
z_{i}
-separating
for
i=1,\dots,s
.

(b)

The tuple
F
is coherently
Z
-separating if and only
if
f_{i}
is
z_{i}
-separating for
i=1,\dots,s
and
z_{i}
does not divide any term
in
\mathop{\rm Supp}\nolimits(f_{j})
for
i,j\in\{1,\dots,s\}
such that
j\neq i
.

(c)

If the ideal
I_{F}
is
Z
-separating and
\sigma
is a

Z
-separating term ordering for
I_{F}
then the reduced
\sigma
-Gröbner
basis of
I_{F}
is coherently
Z
-separating.

(d)

If
F
is a
Z
-separating tuple and
\sigma
is a
Z
-separating
term ordering for
F
then the ideal
I_{F}
is
Z
-separating and
\sigma

is a
Z
-separating term ordering for
I_{F}
. Moreover, the reduced
\sigma
-Gröbner
basis of
I_{F}
is obtained by making the polynomials in
F
monic and interreducing them.

(e)

If the ideal
I_{F}
is
Z
-separating and
\sigma
is an elimination
ordering for
Z
then
\sigma\in\mathop{\rm Sep}\nolimits_{Z}(I_{F})
.

Proof.

To prove (a), we let
i\in\{1,\dots,s\}
. Then
z_{i}
does not divide
any term in
\mathop{\rm Supp}\nolimits(f_{i})
besides itself, since in this case that term would
be larger than
z_{i}
w.r.t.
\sigma
in contradiction to
z_{i}=\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(f_{i})
.

Claim (b) was proven in
[
5
]
, Proposition 2.6.
Next we show (c). Using Definition
2.1
.d, we know that there
exists polynomials
g_{1},\dots,g_{s}\in I_{F}
such that
\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(g_{i})=z_{i}

for
i=1,\dots,s
. In particular, this implies that
g_{i}
is
z_{i}
-separated.
Therefore the reduced
\sigma
-Gröbner basis of
I_{F}
is necessarily of the
form
\{z_{1}-h_{1},\dots,z_{s}-h_{s}\}
with
h_{1},\dots,h_{s}\in K[Y]
.
Hence the claim follows from Definition
2.1
.c.

In order to prove (d), we note that the leading terms
z_{i}=\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(f_{i})

are pairwise coprime. Hence
[
6
]
, Corollary 2.5.10,
implies that
F
is a
\sigma
-Gröbner basis of
I_{F}
and
\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(I_{F})=\langle\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(f_{1}),\dots,\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(f_{s})\rangle=\langle Z\rangle
.
The additional claim follows from the observation that
F
is actually a
minimal
\sigma
-Gröbner basis of
I_{F}
, i.e., that
\{z_{1},\dots,z_{s}\}

is the minimal monomial system of generators of
\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(I_{F})
.

Finally, we show (e). By (d), there exists a term ordering
\tau
such that
the reduced
\tau
-Gröbner basis of
I_{F}
has the form
\{z_{1}-h_{1},\dots,z_{s}-h_{s}\}
with
h_{1},\dots,h_{s}\in K[Y]
.
Since
\sigma
is an elimination ordering for
Z
, it follows that

\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(z_{i}-h_{i})=z_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,s
, and hence that this is the
reduced
\sigma
-Gröbner basis of
I_{F}
, as well.
∎

The next examples illustrate the preceding definition and proposition.
In particular, they show that a tuple of
Z
-separating polynomials is not necessarily
a
Z
-separating tuple, and that a tuple generating a
Z
-separating
ideal is in general not a
Z
-separating tuple.

Example 2.3
.

Let
P=\mathbb{Q}[x,y,z]
, let
Z=(x,y)
, and let
F=(f_{1},f_{2})
, where

f_{1}=x-y^{2}
and
f_{2}=y-xz
. Then
f_{1}
is
x
-separated and
f_{2}
is

y
-separated, but
F
is not
Z
-separated. To see why this is so,
assume that
\sigma
is a term ordering such that
x>_{\sigma}y^{2}
and
y>_{\sigma}xz
.
Then
x>_{\sigma}y^{2}>_{\sigma}x^{2}z^{2}
yields a contradiction to
xz^{2}>_{\sigma}1
.
Hence the implication in part (a) of the proposition is strict.

Example 2.4
.

Let
P=\mathbb{Q}[x,y,z]
, let
Z=(x,y)
, and let
F=(f_{1},f_{2})
, where

f_{1}=x-z^{2}
and
f_{2}=y-xz
. Then
I_{F}=\langle f_{1},f_{2}\rangle
is a

Z
-separated ideal for any term ordering
\sigma
satisfying

x>_{\sigma}z^{2}
and
y>_{\sigma}xz
, since then we have
\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(f_{1})=x

and
\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(f_{2})=y
. Such term ordering are easy to construct, for instance
by taking
\sigma={\rm ord}(V)
with a matrix
V\in\mathop{\rm Mat}\nolimits_{3}(\mathbb{Z})

whose first row is
(3,5,1)
.
Thus
\{f_{1},f_{2}\}
is a
\sigma
-Gröbner basis
of
I_{F}
and
\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(I_{F})=\langle Z\rangle
.

Note that the tuple
F
is not coherently
Z
-separating, because
x=\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(f_{1})

divides a term in
\mathop{\rm Supp}\nolimits(f_{2})
. To create a coherently
Z
-separating tuple,
we have to calculate the reduced
\sigma
-Gröbner basis of
I_{F}
.
Interreducing
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
yields
\{x-z^{2},\,y-z^{3}\}
, a coherently
Z
-separating
tuple which generates
I_{F}
.

Example 2.5
.

Let
P=\mathbb{Q}[x,y,z]
, let
Z=(x,y)
, and let
F=(f_{1},f_{2})
, where

f_{1}=x+y-z^{2}
and
f_{2}=x-y+z^{2}
. Here
F
is not a
Z
-separating
tuple, because
x=\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(f_{1})
implies
x>_{\sigma}y
and
y=\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(f_{2})

implies
y>_{\sigma}x
, a contradiction.

However, the ideal
I_{F}
is
Z
-separating. Let us replace the generating
tuple
F
by
G=(f_{1}+f_{2},\;f_{1}-f_{2})=(2x,\;2y-2z^{2})
. Then
G
is
a
\sigma
-Gröbner basis of
I_{F}
for any term ordering
\sigma
such that

y>_{\sigma}z^{2}
. Notice that, in order to pass from the given tuple of
generators of
I_{F}
to a coherently separating one, we had to perform
linear combinations of the given generators.

Furthermore, let us reconsider Example 2.8 of
[
5
]
.

Example 2.6
.

Let
P=\mathbb{Q}[x,y,z]
, let
Z=(y,z)
, and let
F=(f_{1},f_{2})
, where

f_{1}=x^{2}-x-y
and
f_{2}=y^{2}-z
.
Then the tuple
F
is not coherently
Z
-separating.
However, if we reduce
f_{2}
using
f_{1}
, i.e., if we replace
y^{2}
in
f_{2}

with
(x^{2}-x)^{2}
, we get
\tilde{f}_{2}=(x^{2}-x)^{2}-z
.

Then
(f_{1},\tilde{f}_{2})=(y-x^{2}+x,\ z-(x^{2}-x)^{2})
is the reduced
\sigma
-Gröbner
basis of

for every elimination ordering

for

, and therefore

.

The process of finding and verifying
Z
-separating tuples is, in general,
very time consuming. The following remark contains some suggestions
for speeding it up.

Remark 2.7
.

Suppose we are given polynomials
f_{1},\dots,f_{s}\in{\mathfrak{M}}\setminus\{0\}

which generate an ideal
I_{F}
, and we are looking for a
Z
-separating
generating tuple of
I_{F}
.

(a)

The first thing to do is, of course, to check whether
F=(f_{1},\dots,f_{s})

is a
Z
-separating tuple already. It is easy to inspect
f_{1},\dots,f_{s}

and see whether they are
Z
-separating polynomials. But for a
Z
-separating tuple,
we also need a term ordering
\sigma
such that
\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(f_{i})=z_{i}

for
i=1,\dots,s
. In the next section we shall examine this problem
more closely and show that it amounts to the feasibility problem of a
Linear Programming Feasibility problem which may frequently be solved quickly.

(b)

Suppose that our initial test says that
F
is not
Z
-separated
for any term ordering. This does not exclude the possibility that there exists
another tuple which generates
I_{F}
and is
Z
-separating.
According to Proposition
2.2
.c, to find such a tuple
we should, in principle, choose a candidate
term ordering
\sigma
and compute a
\sigma
-Gröbner basis of
I_{F}
.
For larger examples, this may take a long time. However, if the given polynomials
yield a
\sigma
-Gröbner basis after some interreduction steps,
the Buchberger algorithm may terminate quickly enough. Hence we can try to
choose a promising candidate term ordering
\sigma
and start to calculate
the
\sigma
-Gröbner basis of
I_{F}
with a suitable temporal or spacial timeout.
If we are lucky and the process stops, we can usually move from the resulting
Gröbner basis to the reduced Gröbner basis with comparatively little
effort and end up with a coherently
Z
-separated tuple generating
I_{F}
.

3.
Finding
Z
-Separating Tuples

In the setting of the preceding section, let
f_{1},\dots,f_{s}\in{\mathfrak{M}}\setminus\{0\}
,
let
F=(f_{1},\dots,f_{s})
, and let
I_{F}=\langle f_{1},\dots,f_{s}\rangle
.
In the following we let
\mathbb{N}_{+}
be the set of positive integers.
Recall that, for a term
t=x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}\in\mathbb{T}^{n}
,
the number
\log(t)=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})
is called the
logarithm
of
t
.

Definition 3.1
.

As above, let
f_{1},\dots,f_{s}\in{\mathfrak{M}}\setminus\{0\}
, let
F=(f_{1},\dots,f_{s})
,
and assume that
z_{i}\in\mathop{\rm Supp}\nolimits(f_{i})
for
i=1,\dots,s
.

(1)

For
i=1,\dots,s
, the set

\{\log(z_{i})-\log(t)\mid t\in\mathop{\rm Supp}\nolimits(f_{i})\setminus\{z_{i}\}\}
is
called the
set of logarithmic differences
of
f_{i}
with respect to
z_{i}

and is denoted by
\mathop{\rm DLog}\nolimits_{z_{i}}(f_{i})
.

(2)

The set
\bigcup_{i=1}^{s}\mathop{\rm DLog}\nolimits_{z_{i}}(f_{i})
is called the

set of logarithmic differences

of
F
with respect to
Z
and is denoted by
\mathop{\rm DLog}\nolimits_{Z}(F)
.

In the setting of Example
2.6
, we can evaluate these sets as follows.

Example 3.2
.

As in Example
2.6
, let
P=\mathbb{Q}[x,y,z]
, let
Z=(y,z)
, and let

F=(f_{1},f_{2})
, where
f_{1}=x^{2}-x-y
and
f_{2}=y^{2}-z
.
Then we have
\mathop{\rm DLog}\nolimits_{y}(f_{1})=\mathop{\rm DLog}\nolimits_{y}(x^{2}-x)=\{(-2,1,0),(-1,1,0)\}
and

\mathop{\rm DLog}\nolimits_{z}(f_{2})=\mathop{\rm DLog}\nolimits_{z}(y^{2})=\{(0,-2,1)\}
. Altogether, we obtain

\mathop{\rm DLog}\nolimits_{Z}(F)=\{(-2,1,0),(-1,1,0),(0,-2,1)\}
.

Recall that we may identify a term ordering on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
with
the corresponding term ordering on
\mathbb{N}^{n}
and its unique
extension to
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
as explained in
[
6
]
, p. 54.
Using this terminology, we can characterize
Z
-separating tuples as follows.

Proposition 3.3
.

(Characterizing
Z
-Separating Tuples)

Let
f_{1},\dots,f_{s}\in{\mathfrak{M}}\setminus\{0\}
, let
F=(f_{1},\dots,f_{s})
,
and assume that
z_{i}\in\mathop{\rm Supp}\nolimits(f_{i})
for
i=1,\dots,s
.
Then the following conditions are equivalent.

(a)

The tuple
F
is
Z
-separating.

(b)

There exists a term ordering
\sigma
on
\mathbb{N}^{n}
such that
its unique extension to
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
satisfies
v>_{\sigma}0

for every
v\in\mathop{\rm DLog}\nolimits_{Z}(F)
.

(c)

There exists a vector
u\in\mathbb{N}_{+}^{n}

such that
u\cdot v>0
for every
v\in\mathop{\rm DLog}\nolimits_{Z}(F)
.
(Here
u\cdot v
denotes the dot product of two elements of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.)

Proof.

First we prove that (a) implies (b). By the definition of a
Z
-separating set,
we know that there exists a term ordering
\sigma
on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
such
that
z_{i}=\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(f_{i})
for
i=1,\dots,s
. This implies
\mathop{\rm DLog}\nolimits_{z_{i}}(t)>_{\sigma}0
for every
t\in\mathop{\rm Supp}\nolimits(f_{i})\setminus\{z_{i}\}
, and therefore

v>_{\sigma}0
for every
v\in\mathop{\rm DLog}\nolimits_{Z}(F)
.

Next we note that (b)
\Rightarrow
(c) is shown in the theory of Gröbner fans
(see
[
8
]
, Corollary 2.2).
Finally, to prove that (c) implies (a), we observe that the vector
u

can be taken as the first row of a matrix
V\in\mathop{\rm Mat}\nolimits_{n}(\mathbb{Z})

which defines a term ordering
\sigma
on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
.
Then we have
v>_{\sigma}0
for all
v\in\mathop{\rm DLog}\nolimits_{Z}(F)
, and therefore

z_{i}>_{\sigma}t
for all
t\in\mathop{\rm Supp}\nolimits(f_{i})\setminus\{z_{i}\}
and
i=1,\dots,s
.
Hence we conclude that
z_{i}=\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(f_{i})
for
i=1,\dots,s
.
∎

In the next step, we transform the problem of finding the vector
u

in part (c) of the preceding proposition into a Linear Programming
Feasibility (LPF) problem over the field
\mathbb{Q}
. In this way we will
get an algorithm which can detect efficiently whether a tuple
F
is
Z
-separating.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
A=(a_{ij})\in\mathop{\rm Mat}\nolimits_{k,n}(\mathbb{Q})
and let
b=(b_{1},\dots,b_{k})\in\mathbb{Q}^{k}
. Consider the following system of inequalities:

\displaystyle a_{i1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{in}x_{n}\leq b_{i}

\displaystyle\qquad\hbox{\rm for\ }i=1,\dots,k

\displaystyle x_{j}\geq 0

\displaystyle\qquad\hbox{\rm for\ }j=1,\dots,n

Then the task of deciding whether this system of linear inequalities has a solution

x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{Q}^{n}
is called a

Linear Programming Feasibility (LPF)
problem in standard form over the rationals.
It is usually written as
Ax\leq b
and
x\geq 0
.

It is known that LPF instances can be decided in polynomial time, including
the computation of a solution instance in the positive case (see
[
3
]
,

[
4
]
). The following corollary allows us to view the task of checking whether
the tuple
F
is
Z
-separating as an LPF instance.

Corollary 3.5
.

Given
Z
and
F
as above, we let
\mathop{\rm DLog}\nolimits_{Z}(F)=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\}

and write
v_{i}=(v_{i1},\dots,v_{in})
for
i=1,\dots,k
,
where
v_{ij}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Let
A=(a_{ij})
, where
a_{ij}=-v_{ij}

and
b=(b_{1},\dots,b_{k})
, where
b_{i}=v_{i1}+\cdots+v_{in}-1
for

i=1,\dots,k
. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(a)

The tuple
F
is
Z
-separating.

(b)

The LPF in standard form given by
Ax\leq b
and
x\geq 0
has
a solution
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
in
\mathbb{Q}^{n}
.

If these conditions are satisfied, we let
\tilde{u}_{i}=x_{i}+1
for
i=1,\dots,n
,
multiply the numbers
\tilde{u}_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}_{+}
with their common denominator
to get
u_{i}\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
,
and obtain a vector
u=(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})\in\mathbb{N}_{+}^{n}
such that

u\cdot v>0
for all
v\in\mathop{\rm DLog}\nolimits_{Z}(F)
.

Proof.

By the proposition, condition (a) is equivalent to the existence of
integers
u_{i}\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
for
i=1,\dots,n
such that

u_{1}v_{i1}+\cdots+u_{n}v_{in}\geq 1
for
i=1,\dots,k
.
Clearly, this is equivalent to the existence of rational numbers
u_{i}\geq 1

satisfying these inequalities, as we can multiply those numbers by their
common denominator and still get a solution.

Now we let
A=(a_{ij})
with
a_{ij}=-v_{ij}
and
b_{i}=v_{i1}+\cdots+v_{in}-1
and
x_{j}=u_{j}-1
for
i=1,\dots,k
and
j=1,\dots,n
.
Then
u_{1}v_{i1}+\cdots+u_{n}v_{in}\geq 1
is equivalent to

x_{1}(-v_{i1})+\cdots+x_{n}(-v_{in})\leq v_{i1}+\cdots+v_{in}-1=b_{i}

for
i=1,\dots,k
. Hence the task is equivalent to finding
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{Q}^{n}
such that
Ax\leq b
and
x\geq 0
.

The additional claim follows immediately from these equivalences.
∎

4.
Z
-Separating Re-Embeddings

In this section we apply the material developed in the previous sections
in order to find good re-embeddings of affine schemes, extending and building on
the investigation of this topic in
[
5
]
.

Let
P=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be a polynomial ring
over a field
K
, and let
I
be an ideal in
P
.
Our goal is to find a tuple
Z
of indeterminates in
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}

which can be eliminated easily, without resorting to potentially very heavy
Gröbner basis computations. In other words, we look for an isomorphism
of
K
-algebras
P/I\cong\widehat{P}/(I\cap\widehat{P})
,
where
\widehat{P}=K[Y]
is the polynomial ring in the remaining indeterminates.

As in the previous sections, we let
{\mathfrak{M}}=\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\rangle
,
we denote the tuple
(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
by
X
, we let
Z=(z_{1},\dots,z_{s})

be a tuple of distinct indeterminates in
P
, and we assume that
I
is
an ideal in
P
which is contained in
{\mathfrak{M}}
.
Recall that, given
f_{1},\dots,f_{s}\in{\mathfrak{M}}\setminus\{0\}
,
a term ordering
\sigma
on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
is called a
Z
-separating term ordering
for the ideal
I_{F}=\langle f_{1},\dots,f_{s}\rangle
if we have

\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(I_{F})=\langle z_{1},\dots,z_{s}\rangle
.
Now we generalize this definition to arbitrary ideals
I
contained in
{\mathfrak{M}}

as follows.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
I
be an ideal in
P
which is contained in
{\mathfrak{M}}
.

(a)

A term ordering
\sigma

on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
is called a
Z
-separating term ordering for
I

if there exist polynomials
f_{1},\dots,f_{s}\in I\setminus\{0\}
such that
\sigma

is a
Z
-separating term ordering for
I_{F}=\langle f_{1},\dots,f_{s}\rangle
.

(b)

The ideal
I
is called
Z
-separating
if there exists a
Z
-separating
term ordering
\sigma
for
I
.

Let us characterize this notion in several ways and show that it is
equivalent to the one defined in
[
5
]
, Definition 2.9.a.

Proposition 4.2
.

(Characterization of
Z
-Separating Term
Orderings)

As above, let
I\subseteq{\mathfrak{M}}
be an ideal in
P
, and let
\sigma
be a term
ordering on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
. Then the following conditions are equivalent.

(a)

The term ordering
\sigma
is a
Z
-separating term ordering for
I
.

(b)

There exist polynomials
f_{1},\dots,f_{s}\in I\setminus\{0\}
such
that
\sigma
is a
Z
-separating term ordering for
F=(f_{1},\dots,f_{s})
, i.e.,
such that
\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(f_{i})=z_{i}
for

.

(c)

There exist polynomials
f_{1},\dots,f_{s}\in I\setminus\{0\}
such
that
\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(f_{i})=z_{i}
for

and

is

-separating.

(d)

The reduced
\sigma
-Gröbner basis of

has the shape

\{f_{1},\dots,f_{s},\,g_{1},\dots,g_{t}\}
where
F=(f_{1},\dots,f_{s})

is a coherently
Z
-separating tuple and
\{g_{1},\dots,g_{t}\}

is the reduced
\sigma_{Y}
-Gröbner basis of

.

Proof.

To prove that (a) implies (c), we let
f_{1},\dots,f_{s}\in I\setminus\{0\}

such that the ideal
I_{F}=\langle f_{1},\dots,f_{s}\rangle
satisfies

\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(I_{F})=\langle Z\rangle
. Now we let
\{\tilde{f}_{1},\dots,\tilde{f}_{s}\}
be the reduced
\sigma
-Gröbner basis of
I_{F}
, and
let
\widetilde{F}=(\tilde{f}_{1},\dots,\tilde{f}_{s})
.
By Proposition
2.2
.c, the tuple
\widetilde{F}
is coherently

Z
-separating and
\sigma
is a
Z
-separating term ordering for
\widetilde{F}
.

As (c)
\Rightarrow
(b) is obvious, we prove (b)
\Rightarrow
(d) next.
Let
\{\tilde{f}_{1},\dots,\tilde{f}_{s}\}
be the reduced
\sigma
-Gröbner
basis of
I_{F}=\langle f_{1},\dots,f_{s}\rangle
. By Proposition
2.2
.d,
the polynomials in
\widetilde{F}=(\tilde{f}_{1},\dots,\tilde{f}_{s})

are obtained by making the polynomials in
F
monic and interreducing them.
Letting
\{g_{1},\dots,g_{t}\}
be the reduced
\sigma_{Y}
-Gröbner basis of

, it follows that
\{\tilde{f}_{1},\dots,\tilde{f}_{s},g_{1},\dots,g_{t}\}

is a

-separating Gröbner basis of

in the sense of
[

]

[

\{z_{1}-h_{1},\dots,z_{s}-h_{s},\,g_{1},\dots,g_{t}\}
,

.
Since
(z_{1}-h_{1},\dots,z_{s}-h_{s})
is coherently

-separating,
the claim follows.

Finally, we note that (d)
\Rightarrow
(a) is obviously true.
∎

The following remark provides us with a plenty of

-separating term orderings
for

.

Remark 4.3
.

Let
I\subseteq{\mathfrak{M}}
be an ideal in
P
, and assume that there
exists a

-separating term ordering
\sigma
for

Using the reduced Gröbner basis of

with respect to a
Z
-separating

Definition 4.4
.

Let

be an ideal in
P
, and assume that there exists
a
Z
-separating term ordering
\sigma
for
I
. Let

\sigma
-Gröbner basis of

,
where

-algebra isomorphism

\varPhi:\;P/I\;\longrightarrow\;\widehat{P}/(I\cap\widehat{P})

given by
\varPhi(\bar{x}_{i})=\bar{x}_{i}

\varPhi(\bar{x}_{i})=\bar{h}_{j}

.

Remark 4.5
.

In
[
5
]
, Proposition 2.14, it is shown that the isomorphism

is, in fact, independent of the choice of a
Z
-separating term ordering for

,
as long as one exists. It is important to note that the tuple

,
whose existence is required in the definition of a

-separating term

, may change during the construction of the isomorphism
\varPhi
.

In actual fact, the computation of a reduced Gröbner basis will be out of
reach in non-trivial cases, and the true way to construct

will be:

(1)

Find a (hopefully large) tuple
Z
and a tuple of polynomials

F=(f_{1},\dots,f_{s})
in

for

(2)

Using the methods explained in Remark

is

-separating

-separating term ordering
\sigma
.

(3)

Interreduce the polynomials in

-separating system of generators
\{\tilde{f}_{1},\dots,\tilde{f}_{s}\}

of

such that
\tilde{f}_{i}=z_{i}-h_{i}

with

for

(4)

Define

using

of the reduced
\sigma
-Gröbner basis of

by
[
5
]
,
Theorem 2.13.c.

5.
Z
-Restricted Gröbner Fans

In the preceding section we saw that in order to find good re-embeddings
of a polynomial ideal, we may try to find a tuple of indeterminates
Z

and a
Z
-separating term ordering for
I
. The possible choices of term
orderings leading to distinct reduced Gröbner bases of
I
are
classified by the Gröbner fan of
I
, introduced and studied first in
[
8
]
.
Here we want to classify the possible choices of
Z
-separating term orderings
leading to distinct reduced Gröbner bases of
I
.

First things first, let us recall the setting and the general definitions.
Let
P=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be a polynomial ring over a field
K
, let

{\mathfrak{M}}=\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\rangle
, let
I\subseteq{\mathfrak{M}}
be a non-zero ideal
of
P
, and let
Z=(z_{1},\dots,z_{s})
be a tuple of distinct indeterminates

z_{i}\in\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
for
i=1,\dots,s
.

Moreover, recall that, given a term ordering
\sigma
and the reduced

\sigma
-Gröbner basis
G=\{g_{1},\dots,g_{r}\}
of
I
, the set of pairs

\overline{G}\;=\;\{\,(\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(g_{1}),\,g_{1}),\dots,(\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(g_{r}),\,g_{r})\,\}

is called the
marked reduced
\sigma
-Gröbner basis
of
I
. The set
of all distinct marked reduced
\sigma
-Gröbner bases of
I
is called
the
Gröbner fan
of
I
and is denoted by
\mathop{\rm GFan}\nolimits(I)
.
Next we restrict our attention to those marked reduced
\sigma
-Gröbner bases
of
I
which are
Z
-separating.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
Z=(z_{1},\dots,z_{s})
be a tuple of distinct indeterminates such that

z_{i}\in\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
for
i=1,\dots,s
. The set of all marked
reduced
\sigma
-Gröbner bases

\overline{G}=\{(\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(g_{1}),g_{1}),\dots,(\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(g_{r}),g_{r})\}

of
I
such that for
j=1,\dots,s
there are indices
i_{j}\in\{1,\dots,r\}
with

z_{j}=\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(g_{i_{j}})
is called the
Z
-restricted
Gröbner fan
of
I
and is denoted by
\mathop{\rm GFan}\nolimits_{Z}(I)
.

Notice that, by Proposition
2.2
.d, every choice of a

Z
-separating term ordering
\sigma
for
I

corresponds to a reduced
\sigma
-Gröbner basis of
I

of the type described in the definition,
but in general not in a unique way. It is also possible to
require that some the leading terms of a reduced Gröbner basis
are predescribed terms given by a tuple
T=(t_{1},\dots,t_{s})
with
t_{i}\in\mathbb{T}^{n}

rather than predescribed indeterminates given by a tuple
Z=(z_{1},\dots,z_{s})

with
z_{i}\in\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
. This more general type of restriction
leads to the
T
-restricted Gröbner fan introduced in Section
7
.

For now, let us have a look at a concrete example of a
Z
-restricted
Gröbner fan.

Example 5.2
.

Extending Example
2.5
, we let
P=\mathbb{Q}[x,y,z]
, let
Z=(x,y)
, and
let
I=\langle f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}\rangle
, where
f_{1}=x+y-z^{2}
and
f_{2}=x-y+z^{2}

and
f_{3}=z-y^{2}
.
If we let
g_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\,(f_{1}+f_{2})=x
and
g_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\,(f_{1}-f_{2})=y-z^{2}
then the tuple
(g_{1},g_{2})
is
Z
-separated and the reduced

\sigma
-Gröbner basis of
I
for any term ordering
\sigma

satisfying
y>_{\sigma}z^{2}
is
G=\{g_{1},\,g_{2},\,z^{4}-z\}
.
Here
\mathop{\rm GFan}\nolimits_{Z}(I)
consists of a single marked reduced Gröbner basis, namely

\overline{G}\;=\;\{\,(x,x),\,(y,y-z^{2}),\,(z^{4},z^{4}-z)\,\}
.

To clarify the structure of
\mathop{\rm GFan}\nolimits_{Z}(I)
in general, we introduce
the following map.

Definition 5.3
.

Let
Z=(z_{1},\dots,z_{s})
be a tuple of distinct indeterminates
in
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
, and let
Y=(y_{1},\dots,y_{n-s})
, where

\{y_{1},\dots,y_{n-s}\}=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}\setminus\{z_{1},\dots,z_{s}\}
.
Let
I\subseteq{\mathfrak{M}}
be an ideal in
P
such that
\mathop{\rm GFan}\nolimits_{Z}(I)

is not empty. Consider the map

\Gamma_{Z}:\;\mathop{\rm GFan}\nolimits_{Z}(I)\;\longrightarrow\mathop{\rm GFan}\nolimits(I\cap K[Y])

given as follows: for a
Z
-separating reduced
\sigma
-Gröbner
basis
G=\{g_{1},\dots,g_{r}\}
of
I
, we have
r\geq s
and we may assume

\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(g_{i})=z_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,s
. Next, we let

\Gamma_{Z}(\overline{G})\;=\;\{\,(\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(g_{s+1}),\,g_{s+1}),\;\dots,\;(\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(g_{r}),\,g_{r})\,\}

Then the map
\Gamma_{Z}
is called the
Z
-restricted GFan projection
.

Notice that, in the setting of this definition, the set
\{g_{s+1},\dots,g_{r}\}
is the reduced
\sigma
-Gröbner basis of
I\cap K[Y]

by Proposition
4.2
.d. Thus the map
\Gamma_{Z}
is well-defined.
Our main result in this section is that the map
\Gamma_{Z}
is bijective.
For its proof we need the following result which is part of the
theory of Gröbner fans (see
[
8
]
). For the convenience of the readers,
we include it here together with a proof.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
I
be an ideal in
P=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
, let
\sigma
and
\tau
be two term
orderings on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
, let
G=\{g_{1},\dots,g_{r}\}
be the reduced

\sigma
-Gröbner basis of
I
, and let
G^{\prime}=\{g^{\prime}_{1},\dots,g^{\prime}_{r^{\prime}}\}

be the reduced
\tau
-Gröbner bases of
I
.
If we have
r=r^{\prime}
and
\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(g_{i})=\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\tau}(g^{\prime}_{i})
for
i=1,\dots,r
, then
G=G^{\prime}
.

Proof.

For a contradiction, we may assume that there exists an index
i\in\{1,\dots,r\}

such that
g_{i}\neq g^{\prime}_{i}
. Then, if we let
f=g_{i}-g^{\prime}_{i}
, we have
f\in I\setminus\{0\}

and no term of
\mathop{\rm Supp}\nolimits(f)
is divisible by any leading term
\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(g_{k})=\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(g^{\prime}_{k})

with
k\in\{1,\dots,r\}
, because
G
and
G^{\prime}
are fully interreduced.
Therefore
\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(f)\notin\{\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(g_{1}),\dots,\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(g_{r})\}

contradicts the hypothesis that
G
is a Gröbner basis of
I
.
∎

Now we are ready to formulate and prove our main result.

Theorem 5.5
.

(Structure of Restricted Gröbner Fans)

Let
Z=(z_{1},\dots,z_{s})
be a tuple of distinct indeterminates
in
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
, and let
Y=(y_{1},\dots,y_{n-s})
be such that

\{y_{1},\dots,y_{n-s}\}=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}\setminus\{z_{1},\dots,z_{s}\}
.
Let
I\subseteq{\mathfrak{M}}
be an ideal of
P
such that the set
\mathop{\rm GFan}\nolimits_{Z}(I)
is not empty.
Then the map
\Gamma_{Z}:\;\mathop{\rm GFan}\nolimits_{Z}(I)\longrightarrow\mathop{\rm GFan}\nolimits(I\cap K[Y])
is bijective.

Proof.

First we prove injectivity. Let
\sigma
and
\tau
be

Z
-separating term orderings for
I
, write
G=\{g_{1},\dots,g_{r}\}

for the reduced
\sigma
-Gröbner basis of
I
, and write

H=\{h_{1},\dots,h_{r^{\prime}}\}
for the reduced
\tau
-Gröbner basis of
I
.
Here we may assume that
r\geq s
and
r^{\prime}\geq s
and

\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(g_{i})=\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\tau}(h_{i})=z_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,s
.
Suppose that the map
\Gamma_{Z}
satisfies

\displaystyle\Gamma_{Z}(\overline{G})

\displaystyle\;=\;\{\,(\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(g_{s+1}),g_{s+1}),\;\dots,\;(\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(g_{r}),g_{r})\,\}

\displaystyle\;=\;\{\,\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\tau}(h_{s+1}),h_{s+1}),\;\dots,\;(\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\tau}(h_{r^{\prime}}),h_{r^{\prime}})\,\}\;=\;\Gamma_{Z}(\overline{H})

Then we see that
r=r^{\prime}
and
\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(g_{i})=\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\tau}(h_{i})

for
i=s+1,\dots,r
, after possibly renumbering
h_{s+1},\dots,h_{r}

suitably. By the lemma, we get
G=H
, and hence
\overline{G}=\overline{H}
.

Next we prove surjectivity. Let
\tau
be a term ordering on
K[Y]
,
and let
H=\{h_{1},\dots,h_{t}\}
be the reduced
\tau
-Gröbner basis of
I\cap K[Y]
.
Using the theory of Gröbner fans (cf.
[
8
]
), we may assume that

\tau={\rm ord}(W)
with a matrix
W\in\mathop{\rm Mat}\nolimits_{n-s}(\mathbb{Z})
.

By assumption, the set
\mathop{\rm GFan}\nolimits_{Z}(I)
is not empty.
So, let
F=(f_{1},\dots,f_{s})
be a coherently
Z
-separating tuple of polynomials in
I
.
Now we construct a matrix
V\in\mathop{\rm Mat}\nolimits_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
which defines a
term ordering
\sigma={\rm ord}(V)
in
\mathbb{T}^{n}
as follows.
First we put the matrix of size
s\times s
defining the ordering

{\tt DegRevLex}
on
K[Z]
into the columns of
V
corresponding to
Z

(see
[
6
]
, Definition 1.4.7 and Proposition 1.4.12)
and zeroes into the other columns. This fills the first
s
rows of
V
.
In the lower
n-s
rows, we put
W
into the columns of
V
corresponding to
Y

and zeroes into the columns corresponding to
Z
.

The resulting term ordering
\sigma
satisfies
\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(f_{i})=z_{i}

for
i=1,\dots,s
and
\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(h_{j})=\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\tau}(h_{j})
for
j=1,\dots,t
.
Therefore the reduced
\sigma
-Gröbner basis of
I
is of the form

G=\{z_{1}-g_{1},\dots,z_{s}-g_{s},\,h_{1},\dots,h_{t}\}
with
g_{1},\dots,g_{s}\in K[Y]

and we get
\Gamma_{Z}(\overline{G})=\overline{H}
, as desired.
∎

The proof of this theorem yields the following observation.

Corollary 5.6
.

In the setting of the theorem,
let
\sigma
be a
Z
-separating term ordering for
I
,
and let
G
be the reduced
\sigma
-Gröbner basis of
I
.
Then there exists a
Z
-elimination term ordering
\tau
such
that
G
is also a reduced
\tau
-Gröbner basis of
I
.

Proof.

Let
G=\{g_{1},\dots,g_{r}\}
and
\Gamma_{Z}(\overline{G})=\{(\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(g_{s+1}),\,g_{s+1}),\dots,(\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(g_{r}),\,g_{r})\}
.
As in the proof of the theorem, we now construct a term ordering
\tau

of the form
\tau={\rm ord}(V)
where
V
is a block matrix
consisting of a block of size
s\times s
representing
DegRevLex

and a block of size
(r-s)\times(r-s)
representing
\sigma_{Y}
.
Then
\tau
is an elimination ordering for
Z
and the reduced
\tau
-Gröbner
basis
H
of
I
satisfies
\Gamma_{Z}(\overline{H})=\Gamma_{Z}(\overline{G})
.
This yields
H=G
by the theorem.
∎

The theorem can simplify the search for an optimal re-embedding
considerably. To demonstrate how this works, we consider the setting
of Example 4.3 in
[
5
]
.

Example 5.7
.

Let
P=\mathbb{Q}[x,y,z,u,v]
, and let
I=\langle f_{1},f_{2},f_{3},f_{4}\rangle
,
where
f_{1}=x^{2}+x-z+v
,
f_{2}=z^{2}-u^{2}-u
,
f_{3}=u^{2}-y+u-v
, and

f_{4}=x^{2}+u^{2}-u
.
Recall that using
CoCoA
we may calculate the Gröbner fan of
I
. It
comprises 462 marked reduced Gröbner bases. Moreover, if we choose
Z^{\prime}=(y,u,v)
,
then an optimal re-embedding of
I
is given by the
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra isomorphism

\varPhi_{Z^{\prime}}:\;P/I\longrightarrow\mathbb{Q}[x,z]/\langle x^{4}+2x^{2}z^{2}+z^{4}+2x^{2}-2z^{2}\rangle
.
The computation of the Gröbner fan of
I
is quite demanding,
so let us see if we can get the same result using a better strategy
which follows from the theorem.

If we let
Z=(y,v)
and use the technique explained in
Section
3

(f_{3},f_{1})
is

Z
-separating and every term ordering
\sigma

represented by a matrix whose first row is
(1,4,1,1,3)

yields
\{y-x+z-2u,\;v-u^{2}+x-z+u\}
as the reduced
\sigma
-Gröbner
basis of
\langle f_{1},f_{3}\rangle
.
Here we have
K[Y]=\mathbb{Q}[x,z,u]
and the bijective map

\Gamma_{Z}:\;\mathop{\rm GFan}\nolimits_{Z}(I)\cong\mathop{\rm GFan}\nolimits(I\cap K[Y])
.
The computation of
\mathop{\rm GFan}\nolimits(I\cap K[Y])
, which takes almost no time,
returns five elements. They are

\begin{array}[]{lll}\overline{H}_{1}&=&\{(z^{2},\ z^{2}-u^{2}-u),(x^{2},\ x^{2}+u^{2}-u)\}\cr\overline{H}_{2}&=&\{(u^{2},\ u^{2}-z^{2}+u),(x^{2},\ x^{2}+z^{2}-2u)\}\cr\overline{H}_{3}&=&\{(u,\ u-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}x^{2}-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}z^{2}),(x^{4},\ x^{4}+2x^{2}z^{2}+z^{4}+2x^{2}-2z^{2})\}\cr\overline{H}_{4}&=&\{(u^{2},\ u^{2}+x^{2}-u),(z^{2},\ z^{2}+u^{2}-2u)\}\cr\overline{H}_{5}&=&\{(u,\ u-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}z^{2}-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}x^{2}),(z^{4},\ z^{4}+2x^{2}z^{2}+x^{4}-2z^{2}+2x^{2})\}\end{array}

Let us consider
H_{3}=\{u-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}x^{2}-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}z^{2},\ x^{4}+2x^{2}z^{2}+z^{4}+2x^{2}-2z^{2}\}
and let us find
\Gamma_{Z}^{-1}(\overline{H}_{3})
.
Any term ordering
\tau
on
\mathbb{Q}[x,z,u]
represented by a matrix whose first row is
(2,1,5)

yields
H_{3}
as the reduced
\tau
-Gröbner basis of
I\cap\mathbb{Q}[x,z,u]
.
According to the proof of the surjectivity in Theorem
5.5
, we consider
the term ordering
\sigma
on
P
represented by the matrix

\left(\begin{array}[]{llrrr}0&1&0&0&1\cr 0&0&0&0&-1\cr 2&0&1&5&0\cr 0&0&0&-1&0\cr 0&0&-1&0&0\end{array}\right)

and get the following reduced
\sigma
-Gröbner basis of
I
:

G_{3}=\{y-x^{2}-x-z^{2}+z,\ v+x^{2}+x-z,\ u-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}x^{2}-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}z^{2},\ x^{4}+2x^{2}z^{2}+2x^{2}+z^{4}-2z^{2}\}

Notice that
\Gamma_{Z}(\overline{G}_{3})=\overline{H}_{3}
.
The
Z
-separating reduced Gröbner basis
G_{3}
yields a
re-embedding
\varPhi:\;P/I\cong\mathbb{Q}[x,z,u]/\langle u-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}x^{2}-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}z^{2},\;x^{4}+2x^{2}z^{2}+z^{4}+2x^{2}-2z^{2}\rangle

given by
\varPhi(\bar{x})=\bar{x}
,
\varPhi(\bar{z})=\bar{z}
,
\varPhi(\bar{u})=\bar{u}
,

\varPhi(\bar{y})=\bar{x}^{2}+\bar{x}+\bar{z}^{2}-\bar{z}
,
and
\varPhi(\bar{v})=-\bar{x}^{2}-\bar{x}+\bar{z}
.

With similar computations we get

\Gamma_{Z}(\overline{G}_{1})=\overline{H}_{1}
,
\Gamma_{Z}(\overline{G}_{2})=\overline{H}_{2}
,

\Gamma_{Z}(\overline{G}_{3})=\overline{H}_{3}
,
\Gamma_{Z}(\overline{G}_{4})=\overline{H}_{4}
,
and
\Gamma_{Z}(\overline{G}_{5})=\overline{H}_{5}

where

\displaystyle G_{1}=\{

\displaystyle v+x-z-u^{2}+u,\;y-x+z-2u,\;z^{2}-u^{2}-u,\;x^{2}+u^{2}-u\}

\displaystyle G_{2}=\{

\displaystyle v+x-z^{2}+2u-z,\;y-x-2u+z,\;u^{2}-z^{2}+u,\;x^{2}+z^{2}-2u\}

\displaystyle G_{3}=\{

\displaystyle y-x^{2}-x-z^{2}+z,\;v+x^{2}+x-z,\;u-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}x^{2}-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}z^{2},

\displaystyle x^{4}+2x^{2}z^{2}+2x^{2}+z^{4}-2z^{2}\}

\displaystyle G_{4}=\{

\displaystyle v+x^{2}-z+x,\;y+z-2u-x,\;u^{2}+x^{2}-u,\;z^{2}+x^{2}-2u\}

\displaystyle G_{5}=\{

\displaystyle v+x^{2}-z+x,\;y-z^{2}-x^{2}+z-x,

\displaystyle u-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}z^{2}-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}x^{2},\;z^{4}+2x^{2}z^{2}+x^{4}-2z^{2}+2x^{2}\}

are the five reduced Gröbner bases corresponding to the elements of
\mathop{\rm GFan}\nolimits_{Z}(I)
.

Next we note that among the five marked Gröbner bases of
\mathop{\rm GFan}\nolimits(I\cap\mathbb{Q}[x,z,u])

there are two which can be used to enlarge
Z
. They are
H_{3}
and
H_{5}
.
Using

, for instance, we get
Z^{\prime}=(y,u,v)
,

F^{\prime}=(f_{3},\ u-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}x^{2}-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}z^{2},\ f_{1})

and the
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra isomorphism
\varPhi^{\prime}:\;P/I\longrightarrow\mathbb{Q}[x,z]/\langle x^{4}+2x^{2}z^{2}+z^{4}+2x^{2}-2z^{2}\rangle
.
This is exactly the isomorphism we found using the computation of the huge

\mathop{\rm GFan}\nolimits(I)
at the beginning of this example.

6.
Computing Some Good Re-Embeddings

As before, we let
P=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be a polynomial ring over a field
K
, let

{\mathfrak{M}}=\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\rangle
, and let
I\subseteq{\mathfrak{M}}
be an ideal of
P
.
In view of the results of the preceding sections, the task to find
a good re-embedding of
I
can be solved if we find a large tuple
Z

of distinct indeterminates such that there exists a
Z
-separating
term ordering for
I
. Unfortunately, this task does not appear to have
a systematic and uniform solution. In this section we collect a number
of heuristics and approaches which we found useful for constructing
good re-embeddings. In the final part of the section we describe
a criterium which allows us to detect re-embeddings which show that
P/I

is isomorphic to a polynomial ring, i.e., which show that
\mathop{\rm Spec}\nolimits(P/I)

is isomorphic to an affine space.

Once a candidate tuple
Z=(z_{1},\dots,z_{s})
and a tuple
F=(f_{1},\dots,f_{s})

have been found such that
f_{i}\in I
is
z_{i}
-separating for
i=1,\dots,s
,
the question whether
I
is
Z
-separating can be decided efficiently
using the methods of Section
3
.
To find such a candidate tuple, the following strategy is sometimes useful.

Remark 6.1
.

Let
I=\langle g_{1},\dots,g_{r}\rangle\subseteq{\mathfrak{M}}
be an ideal in
P
.

(1)

Recall that the
linear part
of
I

can be computed via

\mathop{\rm Lin}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{M}}(I)\;=\;\langle(g_{1})_{1},\dots,\langle(g_{r})_{1}\rangle_{K}

where
(g_{i})_{1}
is the homogeneous component of degree one of
g_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,r

(cf.
[
5
]
, Proposition 1.6).

(2)

Bring the coefficient matrix of
(g_{1})_{1},\dots,(g_{r})_{1}
into
reduced row echelon form. Let
g_{1}^{\prime},\dots,g_{r}^{\prime}
be the polynomials
obtained by applying the same reduction steps to
g_{1},\dots,g_{r}
.

(3)

For each
i\in\{1,\dots,r\}
such that
(g^{\prime}_{i})_{1}\neq 0
, choose one
indeterminate in the support of this linear form. Let
Z^{\prime}=(z_{1},\dots,z_{s})
be the tuple
of indeterminates gathered in this way, where
z_{j}\in\mathop{\rm Supp}\nolimits((f_{j})_{1})
and

f_{j}=g^{\prime}_{i_{j}}
for
j=1,\dots,s
and
i_{j}\in\{1,\dots,r\}
.

(4)

Use the subtuple
Z
of
Z^{\prime}
consisting of all
z_{j}
such that

f_{j}
is
z_{j}
-separating and check whether the ideal generated by these
polynomials is
Z
-separating.

There are several potential drawbacks related to this strategy.
Let us illustrate them with examples.

Example 6.2
.

Let
P=\mathbb{Q}[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4},x_{5}]
, and let
I=\langle g_{1},g_{2},g_{3}\rangle
,
where
g_{1}=x_{1}-x_{2}^{2}
,
g_{2}=x_{1}+x_{3}^{2}+x_{4}^{2}+x_{5}^{2}
, and
g_{3}=x_{2}-x_{1}^{3}+x_{3}^{4}
.
Let us apply the above steps.

(1)

We have
\mathop{\rm Lin}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{M}}(I)=\langle x_{1},\,x_{1},\,x_{2}\rangle_{K}
.

(2)

Here we get
g_{1},\,g^{\prime}_{2},g_{3}
, where
g^{\prime}_{2}=g_{2}-g_{1}
.

(3)

This yields
Z^{\prime}=(x_{1},x_{2})
and
f_{1}=g_{1}
as well as
f_{3}=g_{3}
.

(4)

We have
Z=Z^{\prime}
, but unfortunately the ideal
\langle f_{1},f_{3}\rangle=\langle x_{1}-x_{2}^{2},\;x_{2}-x_{1}^{3}+x_{3}^{4}\rangle
is not
Z
-separating,
since we would need a term ordering
\sigma
such that

x_{1}>_{\sigma}x_{2}^{2}>_{\sigma}(x_{1}^{3})^{2}=x_{1}^{6}
.

Instead, if we perform the Gauß-Jordan reduction in Step (2) differently,
we may calculate
g^{\prime}_{1},g_{2},g_{3}
where
g^{\prime}_{1}=g_{1}-g_{2}
.
Then Step (3) yields
Z^{\prime}=(x_{1},x_{2})
and
(f_{1},f_{2})=(g_{2},g_{3})
.
Thus we have
Z=Z^{\prime}
in Step (4), and
\langle f_{1},f_{2}\rangle

turns out to be
Z
-separating.
Altogether, we see that the success of the above strategy may depend
in a subtle way on how the reduction steps are performed in the Gauß-Jordan
algorithm.

In the following example we encounter a different problem.

Example 6.3
.

Let
P=\mathbb{Q}[x,y,z]
and
I=\langle g_{1},\,g_{2}\rangle
, where

g_{1}=y+z-x^{2}
and
g_{2}=x-y^{2}
.

(a)

Following the above strategy, we have
g^{\prime}_{1}=g_{1}
and
g^{\prime}_{2}=g_{2}
.
If we now choose
Z=Z^{\prime}=(y,x)
then the ideal
\langle g^{\prime}_{1},\,g^{\prime}_{2}\rangle=I

is not
Z
-separating, since we would need
y>_{\sigma}x^{2}
and
x>_{\sigma}y^{2}
.

(b)

However, if we choose
Z=Z^{\prime}=(z,x)
then
I
is
Z
-separating
and
(f_{1},\,f_{2})=(z-(y^{4}-y),\,x-y^{2})
is a coherently
Z
-separated
system of generators of
I
.

By choosing
Z=(z,x)
, we get the good re-embedding
P/I\cong\mathbb{Q}[y]
.
How do we arrive at this choice? One way would be to try each indeterminate
in
\mathop{\rm Supp}\nolimits(g^{\prime}_{i})_{1}
in Step (3) above. Unfortunately, this may result
in too many cases for non-trivial examples.

To overcome the difficulty presented by the preceding example,
one method is to
shuffle
the indeterminates which show up in the supports
of the linear forms. We found that, after a few shuffles, the correct choice was detected
quite frequently. The next example is a case in point.

Example 6.4
.

Let
P=\mathbb{Q}[x,y,z,w]
, and let
I=\langle g_{1},\,g_{2},\,g_{3}\rangle
, where

g_{1}=x+z+w-y^{2}
,
g_{2}=y+w-x^{2}
, and
g_{3}=y-xw
.

(a)

Following Remark
6.1
, we find
that
g^{\prime}_{1}=x+z+x^{2}-y^{2}-xw
,
g_{2}^{\prime}=w-x^{2}+xw
, and
g_{3}^{\prime}=y-xw

have linear form which correspond to a reduced row echelon form of their coefficient
matrix. This suggests to use
Z=(x,w,y)
or
Z=(z,w,y)
, but none of these
choices work.

(b)

After shuffling the indeterminates in the supports of
(g^{\prime}_{1})_{1}
,

(g^{\prime}_{2})_{1}
, and
(g^{\prime}_{3})_{1}
, we find that
Z=(z,y)
leads to the
Z
-separating ideal

\langle g^{\prime}_{1},\,g^{\prime}_{3}\rangle
and the coherently
Z
-separating tuple

(z-(x^{2}w^{2}-x^{2}+xw-x),\,y-xw)
. This allows us to construct the re-embedding

P/I\cong\mathbb{Q}[x,w]/\langle x^{2}-xw-w\rangle
.

In the following example we combine several techniques to find an optimal
re-embedding of an ideal in
P
. Recall that a re-embedding

\varPhi_{Z}:\;P/I\cong\widehat{P}/(I\cap\widehat{P})
was called

optimal
in
[
5
]
, Definition 3.3, if any other
re-embedding
P/I\cong P^{\prime}/I^{\prime}
satisfies
\dim(P^{\prime})\geq\dim(\widehat{P})
.

Example 6.5
.

Let
P=\mathbb{Q}[x,y,z]
, and let
I=\langle f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}\rangle
,
where
f_{1}=x^{2}-y^{2}-x+2y-z+1
,
f_{2}=y^{3}-3y^{2}+3y-z+1
, and

f_{3}=y^{2}+x-2y+z-2
. Our goal is to find an optimal re-embedding
of
I
.

Note that the ideal
I
is not contained in
{\mathfrak{M}}
. Therefore, as explained
in the introduction, we have to perform a linear change of coordinates
such that the origin is in
\mathcal{Z}(I)
, and the most useful
way to do this is to move a singularity of
\mathcal{Z}(I)
to the origin.
In the current example, the ring
P/I
has a unique singular
point at
p=(1,1,2)
. Therefore we perform the linear change of coordinates

x\mapsto x+1
,
y\mapsto y+1
,
z\mapsto z+2
and get the new
ideal
I^{\prime}=\langle f^{\prime}_{1},f^{\prime}_{2},f^{\prime}_{3}\rangle
, where
f^{\prime}_{1}=x^{2}-y^{2}+x-z
,

f^{\prime}_{2}=y^{3}-z
, and
f^{\prime}_{3}=y^{2}+x+z
.

The linear part of this ideal is
\mathop{\rm Lin}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{M}}(I^{\prime})=\langle x-z,z,x+z\rangle
.
As discussed above, for choosing good candidates for the tuple
Z
,
we have to bring these linear parts into reduced row echelon form.
Here it turns out that one of the three linear forms reduces to zero,
and that it is best to reduce the first one to zero via
f^{\prime}_{1}\mapsto f^{\prime}_{1}-f^{\prime}_{3}-2f^{\prime}_{2}
.
Then we reduce
f^{\prime}_{3}
to
f^{\prime}_{3}+f^{\prime}_{2}
and get

I^{\prime}=\langle g_{1},g_{2},g_{3}\rangle
where

g_{1}=f^{\prime}_{2}=y^{3}-z
,
g_{2}=f^{\prime}_{3}+f^{\prime}_{2}=y^{3}+y^{2}+x
, and

g_{3}=f^{\prime}_{1}-f^{\prime}_{3}-2f^{\prime}_{2}=-2y^{3}-2y^{2}+x^{2}
.

Here
(g_{1},g_{2})
is coherently
Z
-separating with respect to

Z=(z,x)
. This leads to the re-embedding
\varPhi_{Z}:\;P/I^{\prime}\cong\mathbb{Q}[y]/\langle y^{6}+2y^{5}+y^{4}-2y^{3}-2y^{2}\rangle
.
Using Corollary 4.2 of
[
5
]
, we deduce that this is an
optimal re-embedding.

Going back to the original coordinate system, we see that the map given by

z\mapsto y^{3}-3y^{2}+3y+1,\ x\mapsto-y^{3}+2y^{2}-y+1
provides a

\mathbb{Q}
-algebra isomorphism

P/I\cong K[y]/\langle y^{6}-4y^{5}+6y^{4}-6y^{3}+5y^{2}-2y\rangle

which is an optimal re-embedding.

As promised, the final part of the section provides a positive result
related to the search of good re-embeddings.
According to Remark
4.5
,
if there exists a tuple of
s
distinct indeterminates
Z=(z_{1},\dots,z_{s})

in
X=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
such that
I
is
Z
-separating,
then every
Z
-separating term ordering
\sigma
for
I
produces the same isomorphism

\varPhi:\;P/I\;\longrightarrow\;\widehat{P}/(I\cap\widehat{P})
, as
described in Definition
4.4
.
Additionally, recall that Corollary 4.2 of
[
5
]
says that, if
\dim_{K}(\mathop{\rm Lin}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{M}}(I))=s
,
then the isomorphism
\varPhi
is an optimal embedding. Unfortunately,
the following example shows that we cannot reverse this implication.

Example 6.6
.

Let
P=\mathbb{Q}[x,y]
, and let
I=\langle x-y^{2},\,y-x\rangle
. In this case
we have
{\mathfrak{M}}=\langle x,y\rangle
and
\mathop{\rm Lin}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{M}}(I)=\langle x,y\rangle
.
For the tuple
Z=(x)
, we have
s=1
and the inequality

\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathop{\rm Lin}\nolimits_{\mathfrak{M}}(I))=2>s=1
. Nevertheless, the isomorphism

\varPhi:P/I\longrightarrow\mathbb{Q}[y]/\langle y-y^{2}\rangle=:R
is an optimal re-embedding,
since
P/I
cannot be isomorphic to
\mathbb{Q}
, as
R
is not an integral domain.

However, the next result says that we can sometimes find good re-embeddings
which show that our original scheme is isomorphic to an affine space.

Proposition 6.7
.

(Affine Space Criterion)

Let
Z=(z_{1},\dots,z_{s})
be a tuple of distinct indeterminate in
X=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
,
and let
I\subseteq{\mathfrak{M}}
be an ideal such that the following conditions are
satisfied.

(1)

The ideal
I
is
Z
-separating.

(2)

The ring
P_{{\mathfrak{M}}}/I_{{\mathfrak{M}}}
is a regular local ring.

(3)

The
K
-vector space
\mathop{\rm Lin}\nolimits_{{\mathfrak{M}}}(I)
is
s
-dimensional.

Then the following claims hold.

(a)

We have
s=n-\dim(P_{{\mathfrak{M}}}/I_{{\mathfrak{M}}})
.

(b)

We have
I\cap\widehat{P}=\{0\}
.

In particular, the map
\varPhi:\;P/I\longrightarrow\widehat{P}=K[X\setminus Z]

is an isomorphism with a polynomial ring and the scheme
\mathop{\rm Spec}\nolimits(P/I)
is isomorphic
to an
(n-s)
-dimensional affine space over
K
.

Proof.

To prove (a), we let
d=\dim(P_{{\mathfrak{M}}}/I_{{\mathfrak{M}}})
.
Since
P_{{\mathfrak{M}}}/I_{{\mathfrak{M}}}
is a regular local ring, the cotangent space at its
maximal ideal has dimension
d
. Hence
[
5
]
, Proposition 1.5.b
and hypothesis (3) imply
s=n-d
.

Now we show (b). Using (a), we deduce that
the cardinality of
X\setminus Z
is
n-(n-d)=d
.
We know that the map
\varPhi
is an isomorphism between
P/I
and

\widehat{P}/(I\cap\widehat{P})
, and that
\widehat{P}

is a polynomial ring having
d
indeterminates.
On the other hand, the dimension of the localization
\dim(P_{\mathfrak{M}}/I_{\mathfrak{M}})
of
P/I

is invariant under this isomorphism, and hence we have

\dim(\widehat{P}/(I\cap\widehat{P}))\geq d
.
It follows that
I\cap\widehat{P}
has to be the zero ideal.
∎

7.
T
-Restricted Gröbner Fans

The definition of the
Z
-restricted Gröbner fan of an ideal can be generalized.
To introduce the idea, let us look at a simple example.

Example 7.1
.

Let
P=\mathbb{Q}[x,y,z,w]
, let
F=(f_{1},f_{2})
, where
f_{1}=x^{2}-yz-xw

and
f_{2}=y^{3}-w^{3}-x
, and let
I=\langle f_{1},\,f_{2}\rangle
.
Then the Gröbner fan of
I
consists of 24 marked reduced Gröbner bases.
The only way to choose
Z
to get a non-trivial restricted Gröbner fan
is
Z=(x)
, and in this case we have a 3-element set
\mathop{\rm GFan}\nolimits_{Z}(I)\cong\mathop{\rm GFan}\nolimits(I\cap\mathbb{Q}[x,y,w])
. Its three elements are

\displaystyle\{(x,\ x-y^{3}+w^{3}),(y^{6},\ y^{6}-2y^{3}w^{3}-y^{3}w+w^{6}+w^{4}-yz)\}

\displaystyle\{(x,\ x+w^{3}-y^{3}),(yz,\ yz-w^{6}+2y^{3}w^{3}-w^{4}-y^{6}+y^{3}w)\}

\displaystyle\{(x,\ x+w^{3}-y^{3}),(w^{6},\ w^{6}-2y^{3}w^{3}+w^{4}+y^{6}-y^{3}w-yz)\}

However, if we use
T=(yz,x)
and require that a marked reduced

\sigma
-Gröbner basis of
I
contains polynomials
g_{1},g_{2}
such that

\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(g_{1})=yz
and
\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(g_{2})=x
, then there exists only
one such Gröbner basis, namely

\{(yz,\;yz-w^{6}+2y^{3}w^{3}-w^{4}-y^{6}+y^{3}w),\,(x,\;x+w^{3}-y^{3})\}
.
For instance, it can be obtained using any term ordering represented by
a matrix whose first row is
(4,1,8,1)
.

This example shows that it makes sense to use
T
to define a restricted
Gröbner fan of
I
. The general definition is given as follows.

Definition 7.2
.

Let
I\subseteq{\mathfrak{M}}
be an ideal in
P
, and let

T=(t_{1},...,t_{s})
be a tuple of distinct terms in
\mathbb{T}^{n}\setminus\{1\}
.

(a)

A tuple of polynomials
F=(f_{1},\dots,f_{s})
with

f_{i}\in{\mathfrak{M}}\setminus\{0\}
for
i=1,\dots,s

is called
coherently
T
-separating
if the following conditions
are satisfied.

(1)

There exists a term ordering
\sigma
such that we have

\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(f_{i})=t_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,s
.

(2)

For
i,j\in\{1,\dots,s\}
such that
i\neq j
, no term
in the support of
f_{j}
is divisible by
t_{i}
.

In this case
\sigma
is called a
T
-separating term ordering
for
F
.

(b)

A term ordering
\sigma
is called a
T
-separating
term ordering
for
I
if there
exists a coherently
T
-separating tuple
F=(f_{1},\dots,f_{s})

with
f_{1},\dots,f_{s}\in I\setminus\{0\}
such that
\sigma
is a
T
-separating
term ordering for
F
.

(c)

The set of all marked reduced Gröbner bases of
I
of the form

\overline{G}\;=\;\{\,(\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(g_{1}),\,g_{1}),\;\dots,\;(\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(g_{r}),\,g_{r})\,\}

such that for
i=1,\dots,s
there exist
j_{i}\in\{1,\dots,r\}

with
\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(g_{j_{i}})=t_{i}
is called the
T
-restricted Gröbner fan
of
I

and is denoted by
\mathop{\rm GFan}\nolimits_{T}(I)
.

Notice that, for the case of a tuple of distinct indeterminates
T
,
this definition agrees with Definitions
2.1
.c,
4.1
,
and
5.1
if we keep Proposition
4.2

in mind. Moreover, for a marked reduced Gröbner basis
\overline{G}

in
\mathop{\rm GFan}\nolimits_{T}(I)
as in part (c) of the definition, the tuple

(g_{j_{1}},\dots,g_{j_{s}})
is coherently
T
-separating.

The following example illustrates the
T
-restricted Gröbner fan of an ideal.

Example 7.3
.

Let
P=\mathbb{Q}[x,y,z,w]
, and let
I=\langle f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}\rangle
,
where
f_{1}=x^{2}+yz+xw
,
f_{2}=y^{2}-w^{2}-x
, and

f_{3}=w^{2}-y+w
. Then the Gröbner fan of
I
consists of 162 marked reduced Gröbner
bases.

(a)

For
Z=(x,w)
, the
Z
-restricted Gröbner fan of
I
is empty,
but if we let
T=(x,w^{2})
then
\mathop{\rm GFan}\nolimits_{T}(I)
consists of 9 marked
reduced Gröbner bases.

(b)

Using
T=(x,yz)
, we get that
\mathop{\rm GFan}\nolimits_{T}(I)
contains only
one element, namely
\overline{G}=\{(x,\,g_{1}),(w^{2},\,g_{2}),(yz,\,g_{3})\}
,
where
g_{1}=x-y^{2}-w+y
,
g_{2}=w^{2}+w-y
, and
g_{3}=yz+y^{4}+3y^{2}w-2y^{3}-3yw+y^{2}-2w+2y
.
This Gröbner basis
G
is also the reduced Gröbner basis of
I

with respect to the elimination term ordering
{\tt Elim}(x,z)
.
Therefore we get
I\cap\mathbb{Q}[y,w]=\langle w^{2}+w-y\rangle
.
Notice that this does not yield a re-embedding of
I
,
since there is no natural
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra isomorphism
between
P/I
and
\mathbb{Q}[y,w]/\langle w^{2}+w-y\rangle
, as we do not know
how to map
\bar{z}
.

This example shows that the elements of
\mathop{\rm GFan}\nolimits_{T}(I)
do not yield re-embeddings
of
I
in the sense of Section
4
. However,
they allow us to perform certain eliminations and correspond to the following
type of isomorphisms.

Proposition 7.4
.

Let
I\subseteq{\mathfrak{M}}
be an ideal in
P
, and let

T=(t_{1},...,t_{s})
be a tuple of distinct terms in
\mathbb{T}^{n}
such that

\mathop{\rm GFan}\nolimits_{T}(I)\neq\emptyset
. Let
Z
be the set of all indeterminates
in
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
dividing one of the terms in
T
, and let
Y

be the complement of
Z
in
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
.

(a)

The set of all terms
u\in\mathbb{T}(Z)
such that

u\notin\langle t_{1},\dots,t_{s}\rangle
forms an order ideal
in
\mathbb{T}(Z)
which we denote by
\mathcal{O}_{T}
.

(b)

Let
\overline{G}=\{(t_{1},t_{1}-h_{1}),\dots,(t_{s},t_{s}-h_{s}),(\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(g_{s+1}),\,g_{s+1}),\dots,(\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(g_{r}),\,g_{r})\}

be an element of
\mathop{\rm GFan}\nolimits_{T}(I)
, where
h_{1},\dots,h_{s},g_{s+1},\dots,g_{r}\in P
.
Consider the free
K[Y]
-module
M=\bigoplus_{t\in{\mathcal{O}}_{T}}\,t\cdot K[Y]

and form the set

H\;=\;\{u\,g_{i}\mid u\in\mathbb{T}(Z),\;i\in\{s+1,\dots,r\},\;\mathop{\rm Supp}\nolimits(u\,g_{i})\subseteq M\}

Then
H
is a (possibly infinite) Gröbner basis of the
K[Y]
-submodule

I\cap M
of
M
with respect to the
restriction
\sigma_{M}
of
\sigma
to
M
.

(c)

In the setting of (b), let
F=(t_{1}-h_{1},\dots,t_{s}-h_{s})
.
Then the normal form map

\mathop{\rm NF}\nolimits_{F}:\;P\longrightarrow M
induces an isomorphism of
K[Y]
-modules

\varPhi_{T}:\;P/I\cong M/(I\cap M)

which is called the
T
-separating module re-embedding
of
I
.

Proof.

To prove (a), it suffices to note that the complement of the
monoideal generates by
\{t_{1},\dots,t_{s}\}
in
\mathbb{T}(Z)

is an order ideal, i.e., for
u\in{\mathcal{O}}_{T}
and
v\in\mathbb{T}(Z)

such that
v\mid u
we have
v\notin\langle t_{1},\dots,t_{s}\rangle

and hence
v\in{\mathcal{O}}_{T}
.

To show (b), we first note that
\sigma_{M}
is clearly a
K[Y]
-module
term ordering on
M
. Since the polynomials
g_{s+1},\dots,g_{r}

are fully reduced against the polynomials
t_{1}-h_{1},\dots,t_{s}-h_{s}
,
no term in their supports is divisible by a term in
T
.
Hence they are contained in
I\cap M
.
It remains to show that their leading terms generate the leading term
module of
I\cap M
. For
f\in I\cap M
, we have
\mathop{\rm NF}\nolimits_{G}(f)=0
.
However, since the support of
f
is contained in
M
, only

g_{s+1},\dots,g_{r}
can be involved in the reduction steps
f\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits^{G}0
.
We may assume that in each reduction step a polynomial of the
form
ug_{i}\in M
with
u\in\mathbb{T}^{n}
and
i\in\{s+1,\dots,r\}

is subtracted. We write
u=u^{\prime}\,u^{\prime\prime}
with
u^{\prime}\in\mathbb{T}(Z)

and
u^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{T}(Y)
. Then the reduction step subtracts a
K[Y]
-multiple
of
u^{\prime}g_{i}\in M
. Hence
f
can be reduced to zero in the
K[Y]
-module

I\cap M
via the elements of
H
, and the claim is proved.

Finally, we note that the map
\mathop{\rm NF}\nolimits_{F}
in part (c) is well-defined, because

{\mathcal{O}}_{T}\cdot\mathbb{T}(Y)
are the terms in the complement of the
monoideal generated by
t_{1},\dots,t_{s}
. The map
\mathop{\rm NF}\nolimits_{F}
is

K[Y]
-linear, as the indeterminates in
Y
do not divide any term in
T
.
Letting
\varepsilon:\;M\longrightarrow M/(I\cap M)
be
the canonical surjection, it is clear that the composed
map
\varepsilon\circ\mathop{\rm NF}\nolimits_{F}:\;P\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow M/(I\cap M)
is surjective. Hence it suffices to show
that the kernel of
\varepsilon\circ\mathop{\rm NF}\nolimits_{F}
is
I
. This follows from
the fact that a polynomial
f\in P
satisfies
\mathop{\rm NF}\nolimits_{F}(f)\in I
if and
only if
f
reduces to zero via
g_{s+1},\dots,g_{r}
, and this is equivalent
to
f\in I\cap M
.
∎

Notice that the
K[Y]
-module
M
is not necessarily finitely generated.
To get a full analogue to Theorem
5.5
in the

T
-separating case, one would have to develop a theory of
Gröbner fans for modules and then examine which Gröbner bases
of
I\cap M
result from the restriction given in part (b) of the proposition.
We leave this task to the interested readers and end this paper
by applying the proposition to an easy example and to the
setting of Example
7.3
.b.

Example 7.5
.

Let
P=\mathbb{Q}[x,y]
, let
T=(x^{3})
, and let
G=\{g_{1},g_{2}\}
,
where
g_{1}=x^{3}-x
and
g_{2}=xy
.
Let us look at the various statements of the proposition.

(a)

We have
X=(x,y)
and
Z=(x)
and
Y=(y)
.
The order ideal
{\mathcal{O}}_{T}\subseteq\mathbb{T}(Z)
is given by

{\mathcal{O}}_{T}=\{1,\,x,\,x^{2}\}
.

(b)

The module
M
is the free
\mathbb{Q}[y]
-module

M\;=\;\mathbb{Q}[y]\oplus x\,\mathbb{Q}[y]\oplus x^{2}\,\mathbb{Q}[y]
.

The set
H=\{g_{2},\,xg_{2}\}=\{xy,\,x^{2}y\}
is a
\sigma_{M}
-Gröbner
basis of the
\mathbb{Q}[y]
-module
I\cap M
, and
hence
I\cap M=\mathbb{Q}[y]\cdot xy\oplus\mathbb{Q}[y]\cdot x^{2}y
.

(c)

The
T
-separating module re-embedding of
I
is given
by the
\mathbb{Q}[y]
-isomorphism

P/I\cong(\mathbb{Q}[y]\oplus x\,\mathbb{Q}[y]\oplus x^{2}\,\mathbb{Q}[y])/(\mathbb{Q}[y]\cdot xy\oplus\mathbb{Q}[y]\cdot x^{2}y)
.

Example 7.6
.

Let
P=\mathbb{Q}[x,y,z,w]
, let
T=(x,yz)
, and let
G=\{g_{1},g_{2},g_{3}\}
,

,

\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(g_{1})=x

\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(g_{2})=w^{2}

\mathop{\rm LT}\nolimits_{\sigma}(g_{3})=yz

Let us look at the various statements of the proposition.

(a)

The order ideal
{\mathcal{O}}_{T}

{\mathcal{O}}_{T}=\{1,\,y,\,z,\,y^{2},\,z^{2},\,\dots\}

(b)

The module
M

\mathbb{Q}[w]

H=\{g_{2},\,yg_{2},\,y^{2}g_{2},\dots\}

\sigma_{M}
-Gröbner

\mathbb{Q}[w]

(c)

The
T
-separating module re-embedding of

\mathbb{Q}[w]
-isomorphism
P/I\cong M/\mathbb{Q}[y,w]\cdot g_{2}\cong\mathbb{Q}[y,w]/\langle g_{2}\rangle\oplus\bigoplus_{i\geq 1}\mathbb{Q}[w]\,z^{i}

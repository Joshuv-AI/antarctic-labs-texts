Abstract
. A polynomial-in-time growth bound is established for global Sobolev H s ​ ( 𝕋 ) H^{s}(\mathbb{T}) solutions to the derivative nonlinear Schrödinger equation on the circle with s > 1 s>1 . These bounds are derived as a consequence of a nonlinear smoothing effect for an appropriate gauge-transformed version of the periodic Cauchy problem, according to which a solution with its linear part removed possesses higher spatial regularity than the initial datum associated with that solution.

1.
Introduction and Results

We consider the Cauchy problem for the derivative nonlinear Schrödinger (dNLS) equation on the circle

(1.1a)

\displaystyle u_{t}-iu_{xx}=\partial_{x}(|u|^{2}u),\penalty\ \penalty\ x\in\mathbb{T},\ t\in\mathbb{R},

(1.1b)

\displaystyle u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T}),

where
u=u(x,t)
is a complex-valued function,
\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}
is the one-dimensional torus (circle), and
H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
is the
L^{2}
-based Sobolev space on the circle.

The dNLS equation was derived as a model in plasma physics in the 1970s, see
[
MMOT
]
. As shown in
[
KN
]
, it is a completely integrable system, possessing a Lax pair formulation and an infinite number of conserved quantities, including the following:

(1.2)

\displaystyle M(u)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}}|u|^{2}\penalty\ dx,\quad P(u)=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left[\text{Im}(u\overline{u_{x}})+\tfrac{1}{2}|u|^{4}\right]dx,

\displaystyle E(u)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left[|u_{x}|^{2}+\tfrac{3}{2}|u|^{2}\text{Im}(u\overline{u_{x}})+\tfrac{1}{2}|u|^{6}\right]dx,

where
M(u)
,
P(u)
and
E(u)
correspond to the mass, momentum and energy, respectively, of the solution. Note that
P(u)
is the Hamiltonian for (
1.1
).

Concerning the well-posedness of (
1.1
), Fukuda and Tsutsumi
[
FT1
]
showed local well-posedness in
H^{s}
,
s>3/2
, on both the line and the circle using the method of parabolic regularization. Furthermore, in
[
FT2
]
they demonstrated global well-posedness of solutions in
H^{2}
with sufficiently small norm
\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{H^{1}}
. Hayashi and Ozawa
[
Ha
,
HO1
,
HO2
]
improved upon this result in the Euclidean setting by showing global well-posedness of solutions in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
with sufficiently small norm
\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}
. In particular, their result was obtained by first performing a gauge transformation of (
1.1a
), which removed the term
|u|^{2}u_{x}
from the nonlinearity. Takaoka
[
Tak
]
combined the gauge transformation of Hayashi and Ozawa and the Fourier restriction norm method introduced by Bourgain in the breakthrough paper
[
B1
]
to establish local well-posedness in
H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R})
. This result was shown to be sharp by Biagoni and Linares
[
BL
]
in the sense that the data-to-solution map fails to be uniformly continuous for
s<1/2
. Thus,
s=1/2
is the optimal result attainable for the well-posedness of (
1.1
) using a fixed point argument on the gauge equation, although the critical regularity for scaling in the Euclidean setting is at the level of
s=0
. Under the assumption of a sufficiently small
\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}
norm, Colliander, Keel, Staffilani, Takaoka and Tao
[
CKSTT1
]
obtained global well-posedness for
s>1/2
. Finally, global well-posedness for
s=1/2
was demonstrated by Miao, Wu and Xu
[
MWX
]
and later by Guo and Wu
[
GW
]
, with the latter work improving the restriction on the initial data from
\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}<\sqrt{2\pi}
to
\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}<\sqrt{4\pi}
. Such mass restrictions come from the sharp Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequalities. Finally, it is worth mentioning that Jenkins, Liu, Perry and Sulem
[
JLPS
]
and, more recently, Bahouri and Perelman
[
BP
]
demonstrate global well-posedness of dNLS with initial data
u_{0}
in the weighted Sobolev space
H^{2,2}(\mathbb{R})
and in
H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R})
, respectively, without a mass restriction.

The majority of the above results concern the Cauchy problem on the line. Regarding the periodic problem (
1.1
), local well-posedness in
H^{1/2}(\mathbb{T})
was established by Herr
[
He
]
by adapting the gauge transformation of Hayashi and Ozawa to the periodic setting. The same article gives global well-posedness for
u_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T})
such that
\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}<2/3
. This mass threshold was improved by Mosincat and Oh in
[
MO
]
, where they show global well-posedness in
H^{1}(\mathbb{T})
for
\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}<\sqrt{4\pi}
. Using the
I
-method, Win
[
W
]
obtained global well-posedness in
H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
for
s>1/2
under the assumption of a sufficiently small
\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}
norm. Finally, Mosincat
[
M
]
established global well-posedness in
H^{1/2}(\mathbb{T})
provided that
\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}<\sqrt{4\pi}
. At the time of writing of the present article, the mass restriction in the periodic case had not been removed.

(1.4)

f(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{i\xi x}\widehat{f}(\xi).

\mathcal{F}_{t}(f)(\tau):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-it\tau}f(t)\penalty\ dt,\penalty\ \penalty\ \tau\in\mathbb{R}.

Finally, for

(1.6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{t}\mathcal{F}_{x}(f)(\xi,\tau)=\widetilde{f}(\xi,\tau).

\bullet

We define the Bessel potential

(1.7a)

\widehat{J_{x}^{s}f}(\xi):=\langle{\xi\rangle}^{s}\widehat{f}(\xi),\quad\langle{\cdot\rangle}:=\left(1+|\cdot|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Then, for any

(1.7b)

H^{s,p}(\mathbb{T}):=\left\{f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{T}):\left\|f\right\|_{H^{s,p}(\mathbb{T})}:=\left\|J_{x}^{s}f\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T})}<\infty\right\},

In the special case
p=2
, the above space reduces to the

H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
.

\bullet

For any
s,b\in\mathbb{R}
, we define the Bourgain space
X^{s,b}
as the closure of
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}_{t};C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}_{x}))
under the norm

(1.8)

\left\|f\right\|_{X^{s,b}}:=\left\|\left\langle\xi\right\rangle^{s}\left\langle\tau+\xi^{2}\right\rangle^{b}\widetilde{f}(\xi,\tau)\right\|_{\ell^{2}_{\xi}L^{2}_{\tau}}.

Similarly, the space
Y^{s,b}
is defined via the norm

(1.9)

\left\|f\right\|_{Y^{s,b}}:=\left\|\langle{\xi\rangle}^{s}\langle{\tau+\xi^{2}\rangle}^{b}\widetilde{f}(\xi,\tau)\right\|_{\ell^{2}_{\xi}L^{1}_{\tau}}.

In addition, we define the Banach space
Z^{s}:=X^{s,\frac{1}{2}}\cap Y^{s,0}
with norm

(1.10)

\left\|f\right\|_{Z^{s}}:=\left\|f\right\|_{X^{s,\frac{1}{2}}}+\left\|f\right\|_{Y^{s,0}}.

Finally, the restriction of
Z^{s}
on
\mathbb{T}\times[0,T]
with
T>0
is denoted by
Z^{s}_{T}
and is defined via the norm

(1.11)

\left\|f\right\|_{Z^{s}_{T}}:=\inf\left\{\left\|g\right\|_{Z^{s}}:g|_{[0,T]}=f\right\}.

\bullet

We define the Littlewood-Paley-type projection operator
P_{k}
by

(1.12)

\widehat{P_{k}(f)}(\xi):=\begin{cases}\chi_{\{\xi=0\}}\widehat{f}(0),\penalty\ k=0\\
\chi_{\left\{2^{k-1}\leqslant|\xi|<2^{k}\right\}}\widehat{f}(\xi),\penalty\ k\in\mathbb{N},\end{cases}

where
\chi_{A}
is the characteristic function of the set
A
. We will often denote
P_{k}(f)
simply by
f_{k}
. By this definition, it follows that

(1.13)

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\widehat{f_{k}}(\xi)=\widehat{f}(\xi),\quad\xi\in\mathbb{Z}.

\bullet

We let
\eta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(-2,2)
with
0\leq\eta\leq 1
and
\eta(t)=1
for all
t\in[-1,1]
. For
T>0
, we define
\eta_{T}(t):=\eta(t/T)
.

\bullet

Following
[
He
]
, we introduce the periodic gauge transformation of a solution
u
to (
1.1
) by

(1.14)

\displaystyle v(x,t)=\mathcal{G}(u)(x,t):=e^{-i\mathcal{I}(u)(x,t)}u(x,t),

where
\mathcal{I}(u)(x,t)
is the mean-zero spatial primitive of
|u(x,t)|^{2}-\frac{1}{2\pi}\left\|u(t)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}
given by

\mathcal{I}(u)(x,t):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{\theta}^{x}\left[|u(y,t)|^{2}-\frac{1}{2\pi}\left\|u(t)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}\right]dyd\theta.

Let

(1.15)

\mu:=\frac{1}{2\pi}\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\left\|u(t)\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})},\quad t\in\mathbb{R},

where the second equality is due to the conservation of mass in (
1.2
). In fact, we further have
\mu=\frac{1}{2\pi}\left\|v(t)\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}
. A straightforward computation then shows that
v
satisfies the equation

(1.16)

v_{t}-iv_{xx}-2\mu v_{x}=-v^{2}\overline{v}_{x}+\frac{i}{2}|v|^{4}v-i\mu|v|^{2}v+i\psi(v)v,

where

\psi(v)(t):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left[2\text{Im}(\overline{v}_{x}v)(\theta,t)-\frac{1}{2}|v|^{4}(\theta,t)\right]d\theta+\mu^{2}.

The term
2\mu v_{x}
can be removed from (
1.16
) by means of the transformation

(1.17)

w(x,t)=\tau_{-\mu}v(x,t):=v(x-2\mu t,t).

Indeed, since
\tau_{-\mu}
commutes with
\psi
and is an isometry on
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
, we find that
w
satisfies

(1.18)

\displaystyle w_{t}-iw_{xx}=-w^{2}\overline{w}_{x}+\frac{i}{2}|w|^{4}w-i\mu|w|^{2}w+i\psi(w)w.

Finally, we introduce a second gauge transformation,

(1.19)

z(x,t):=e^{-ig(t)}w(x,t),

where

g(t):=\frac{8\pi^{3}-1}{16\pi^{4}}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|w(t^{\prime})\right\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{T})}^{4}dt^{\prime}-\mu^{2}t.

We note that
z
is related to
u
as follows:

z(x,t)=e^{-ig(t)}e^{-i\mathcal{I}(u)(x-2\mu t,t)}u(x-2\mu t,t)=e^{-ig(t)}\tau_{-\mu}\mathcal{G}(u)(x,t).

Also, it will be shown in Section
2
that
z
satisfies the Cauchy problem (
2.7
) as well as the integral equation (
2.21
).

With the above notation in place, we now state some essential previous results and then introduce the main results of this work. We begin with the well-posedness of the gauge-equivalent Cauchy problem (
2.7
), which follows from Theorem 5.1 of

Theorem 1.1

(
Well-posedness of the gauge equation –
[
He
]
, Theorem 5.1
)
.

Suppose
z_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
with
s\geq 1/2
. Then, there exists a non-increasing function
T:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)

T=T(\left\|z_{0}\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T})})

satisfying the gauge-equivalent Cauchy problem (

) in the Duhamel sense with the estimate

(1.20)

\displaystyle\left\|z\right\|_{Z_{T}^{s}}

\displaystyle\leq c\left\|z_{0}\right\|_{H^{s}}.

Furthermore, the data-to-solution map is Lipschitz from bounded subsets of
H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
to bounded subsets of
Z_{T}^{s}
.

Remark 1.1
.

In
[
He
]
, it is stated that the local time of existence for the solution
z
can be taken to depend only on
\left\|z_{0}\right\|_{H^{1/2}(\mathbb{T})}
instead of
\left\|z_{0}\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T})}
, namely,
T=T(\left\|z_{0}\right\|_{H^{1/2}(\mathbb{T})})
.

Next, we recall the well-posedness of the dNLS Cauchy problem (
1.1
) as guaranteed by Theorem 1.1 of
[
He
]
.

Theorem 1.2

(
Well-posedness of dNLS on
\mathbb{T}
–
[
He
]
, Theorem 1.1
)
.

Suppose
u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
with
s\geq 1/2
. If
z\in Z_{T}^{s}
is the solution to the gauge-equivalent Cauchy problem (

) as guaranteed by Theorem

, then
u=e^{ig(t)}\mathcal{G}^{-1}(\tau_{\mu}z)\in C([0,T];H^{s}(\mathbb{T}))
is the unique solution satisfying the dNLS Cauchy problem (
1.1
) in the sense of Duhamel. Furthermore,

is a limit of smooth solutions.

Remark 1.2
.

Lemma
5.1
implies that there exists a non-increasing function
\tilde{T}:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
such that
\tilde{T}=\tilde{T}(\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T})})
and
\tilde{T}(\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T})})\leq T(\left\|z_{0}\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T})})
. Therefore, for
s\geq 1/2
the time of existence in Theorem
1.2
may be taken to depend on
\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{H^{1/2}(\mathbb{T})}
instead of
\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T})}
.

Finally, we state the following global existence result from
[
He
]
.

Corollary 1.1

(
Global existence –
[
He
]
, Corollary 1.2
)
.

For
s\geq 1
let
u\in C([0,T];H^{s}(\mathbb{T}))
be the solution to the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) from Theorem

. Then, for
\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}
sufficiently small,

(1.21)

\displaystyle\left\|u(t)\right\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle\leq C(\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T})}),\quad t\in[0,T].

Consequently, the time of existence for the solution

can be taken arbitrarily large.

Remark 1.3
.

The above global result follows from the observation that the local time of existence
T
is bounded below by a function of
\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T})}
. While global well-posedness in
H^{1/2}(\mathbb{T})
has been obtained in
[
M
]
, an estimate of the form (
1.21
) does not seem to hold with
H^{1}(\mathbb{T})
replaced by
H^{1/2}(\mathbb{T})
.

The main goal of this paper is to establish a polynomial-in-time bound on the growth of global solutions to the periodic dNLS Cauchy problem (
1.1
). Key to demonstrating this bound is the discovery of a local nonlinear smoothing effect for the gauge problem (
2.7
), according to which the solution
z
of (
2.7
) with the linear part removed possesses higher spatial regularity than the initial data
z_{0}
. The effect is more readily seen by first recasting (
2.7
) into the Duhamel form (
2.21
) via a method known as differentiation by parts (see Section
2
). The precise statement of this first result is as follows.

Theorem 1.3

(
Nonlinear smoothing
)
.

Suppose
s>1/2+\varepsilon
with
0<\varepsilon\ll 1/2
and let
z_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
. Then, for
T=T(\left\|z_{0}\right\|_{H^{1/2+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{T})})
, the solution
z\in Z^{s}_{T}
of the Cauchy problem (
2.7
) from Theorem
1.1
satisfies the integral equation (
2.21
).

Moreover, for
0<a<\min\{s-1/2-\varepsilon,1/2-\varepsilon\}
and
\sigma=\min\{s,1\}
we have
z-e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}z_{0}\in C([0,T];H^{s+a}(\mathbb{T}))
with

(1.22)

\big\|z-e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}z_{0}\big\|_{C([0,T];H^{s+a}(\mathbb{T}))}\leq C(s,\left\|z_{0}\right\|_{H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{T})},T)\left\|z_{0}\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T})}.

Remark 1.4
.

Corollary
1.1
and Lemma
5.1
imply that
z
satisfies (
2.21
) globally.

We note that the dispersion on the circle is weaker than on the line in the sense that no Kato smoothing or maximal inequalities are available on the circle. Thus, proving (
1.22
) requires a careful treatment of resonant frequencies in addition to the differentiation by parts mentioned above.

The nonlinear smoothing estimate (
1.22
) allows us to demonstrate the following polynomial-in-time bound, which is the main result of this work.

Theorem 1.4

(
Polynomial bound
)
.

Let
s\geq 1
. Then, the global solution
u
to the periodic dNLS Cauchy problem (
1.1
) given by Corollary
1.1
satisfies

(1.23)

\left\|u(t)\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T})}\leq C(\varepsilon,s,\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T})},T)\ \langle{t\rangle}^{2(s-1)+\varepsilon},

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
0<\varepsilon\ll 1/2
.

Bourgain
[
B2
,
B3
]
was the first to demonstrate the connection between nonlinear smoothing and polynomial bounds for Hamiltonian equations. By employing Fourier truncation operators in conjunction with smoothing estimates, he obtained the following local-in-time inequality for solutions of various dispersive equations:

(1.24)

\left\|u(t+\delta)\right\|_{H^{s}}\leqslant\left\|u(t)\right\|_{H^{s}}+C\left\|u(t)\right\|_{H^{s}}^{1-\delta}

for some
\delta\in(0,1)
. Local time iterations using the above inequality resulted in the polynomial growth bound
\left\|u(t)\right\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\left\langle t\right\rangle^{1/\delta}
. Staffilani
[
St1
,
St2
]
used further multilinear smoothing estimates to obtain (
1.24
), which led to polynomial bounds of
H^{s}
solutions,
s>1
, for Korteweg-de Vries (KdV) and nonlinear Schrödinger (NLS) equations. Colliander, Keel, Staffilani, Takaoka and Tao
[
CKSTT2
]
developed a new method using modified energy called the “upside-down
I
-method” to produce polynomial bounds in low Sobolev norms,
s\in(0,1)
, for the NLS equation. Sohinger
[
So1
,
So2
]
further developed the upside-down
I
-method to obtain polynomial bounds for high Sobolev norms for NLS. We also refer the reader to
[
CKO
]
and the references therein for further developments in this direction. In addition, Oh and Stefanov
[
OS2
]
determined a nonlinear smoothing effect for periodic, generalized KdV equations that gave rise to a polynomial bound in
H^{s}
,
s>1
. Finally, in
[
IMOS
]
, Oh and the authors of the present work identified a nonlinear smoothing effect for a periodic, gauge-transformed Benjamin-Ono equation which led to a polynomial bound on solutions to the periodic Benjamin-Ono equation for
1/2<s\leq 1
.

Several recent works have established uniform-in-time bounds for a number of completely integrable dispersive equations using inverse scattering techniques. In particular, Killip, Visan and Zhang
[
KVZ
]
showed that the
H^{s}
-norm of solutions to the KdV and NLS equations is uniformly bounded in time for
-1\leqslant s<1
and
-1/2<s<1
, respectively, both on the line and on the circle. Similarly, Koch and Tataru
[
KTat
]
showed that there exists a conserved energy equivalent to the
H^{s}
-norm for
s>-1/2
in the case of the NLS and mKdV equations and for
s\geqslant-1
in the case of the KdV equation. For the Benjamin-Ono equation, Talbut
[
Tal
]
proved a uniform-in-time bound in
H^{s}
for
-1/2<s<0
on the line and the circle. Gérard, Kappeler and Topalov
[
GKT
]
then established this uniform bound for the periodic Benjamin-Ono equation with
s>0
. In the case of dNLS, uniform-in-time bounds were obtained on the line and the circle by Klaus and Schippa
[
KS
]
for
0<s<1/2
. Furthermore, Bahouri and Perelman
[
BP
]
showed boundedness of
H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R})
solutions.

Nonlinear smoothing properties analogous to the one of Theorem
1.3
have been previously established for several important dispersive equations. Indicatively, we mention the works of Erdogan and Tzirakis on the periodic KdV equation
[
ET1
]
as well as on the fractional NLS equation on the circle and line
[
EGT2
]
, the NLS equation on the half-line
[
ET3
]
, the dNLS equation on the line and half-line
[
EGT1
]
, and the Zakharov system on the circle
[
ET2
]
.
The main technique used in the proof of these results is known as the normal form method or, as previously mentioned, the differentiation by parts method. It was first introduced by Shatah
[
Sh
]
in the context of the Klein-Gordon equation with a quadratic nonlinearity and was further developed by Germain, Masmoudi and Shatah for two-dimensional quadratic Schrödinger equations
[
GMS1
]
and for the gravity water waves equation
[
GMS2
]
. Babin, Ilyin and Titi
[
BIT
]
applied this method to obtain unconditional well-posedness results for the periodic KdV equation. Chung, Guo, Kwon and Oh
[
CGKO
]
also obtained unconditional well-posedness of the quadratic dNLS using normal form reductions. An alternative formulation of the normal form method, which involves a multilinear, pseudo-differential operator in place of differentiation by parts, was provided by Oh and Stefanov
[
OS1
,
Oh
]
for establishing smoothing estimates and well-posedness.

Structure of the paper
. In Section
2
, we employ the gauge transformation (

) to remove the resonant frequencies from the

.

2.
Removal of Resonances and Differentiation by Parts

We begin with equation (
1.18
) for
w

\displaystyle\left\|z\right\|_{C(\mathbb{R};H^{s}(\mathbb{T}))}\lesssim\left\|z\right\|_{Z^{s}}.

Moreover, for
b_{2}>b_{1}+\frac{1}{2}
,

(4.4)

\displaystyle\left\|z\right\|_{Y^{s,b_{1}}}\lesssim\left\|z\right\|_{X^{s,b_{2}}}.

In particular, for all
b>\frac{1}{2}
,
X^{s,b}\hookrightarrow Z^{s}
.

Combining Lemmas
3.3
-
3.10
with Lemmas
4.1
-
4.3
, we deduce that any solution
z\in Z^{s}_{T}
,
s>\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon
,
T>0
, of the Duhamel equation (
2.21
) on
[0,T]
with
z_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
enjoys the nonlinear smoothing effect
z-e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}z_{0}\in C([0,T];H^{s+a}(\mathbb{T}))
with the estimate (
1.22
). Next, we transition to the solution of the Cauchy problem (
2.7
).

Let
z_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
with
s>\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon
and take
z_{0}^{(n)}\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
such that
z_{0}^{(n)}\rightarrow z_{0}
in
H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
. From Theorem
1.1
, there exist
T=T(\left\|z_{0}\right\|_{H^{1/2+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{T})})>0
and functions
z\in Z^{1/2+\varepsilon}_{T}
and, for
n
sufficiently large,
z^{(n)}\in Z^{\infty}_{T}
that are solutions of the Cauchy problem (
2.7
) with initial data
z_{0}
and
z_{0}^{(n)}
, respectively.
Furthermore, from the computations of Section
2
, the smooth solution
z^{(n)}
satisfies the Duhamel equation (
2.21
) on
[0,T]
. In addition, thanks to the Lipschitz continuity of the data-to-solution map, we have
z^{(n)}\rightarrow z
in
Z^{1/2+\varepsilon}_{T}
. Therefore, using Lemmas
3.3
-
3.10
and the fact that
z^{(n)}
is Cauchy in
Z^{1/2+\varepsilon}_{T}
, we conclude that
z\in Z^{1/2+\varepsilon}_{T}
satisfies (
2.21
) on
[0,T]
.
In turn, as noted below Lemma
4.3
, we can employ the nonlinear smoothing estimate (
1.22
) for an appropriate value of
a
to infer

\displaystyle\left\|z\right\|_{Z^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon+a}_{T}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|z_{0}\right\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon+a}(\mathbb{T})}+\big\|z-e^{it\partial_{x}^{2}}z_{0}\big\|_{Z^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon+a}_{T}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|z_{0}\right\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon+a}(\mathbb{T})}+C(\left\|z\right\|_{Z^{\sigma}_{T}},T)\left\|z\right\|_{Z^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}_{T}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|z_{0}\right\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon+a}(\mathbb{T})}+C(\left\|z_{0}\right\|_{H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{T})},T)\left\|z_{0}\right\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{T})}

(4.5)

\displaystyle\lesssim C(\left\|z_{0}\right\|_{H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{T})},T)\left\|z_{0}\right\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon+a}(\mathbb{T})},

which shows that
z\in Z^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon+a}_{T}
with the estimate (
4.5
).
Iterating this process, we eventually obtain

\left\|z\right\|_{Z^{s}_{T}}\leq C(s,\left\|z_{0}\right\|_{H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{T})},T)\left\|z_{0}\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T})}.

This estimate shows that
z\in Z^{1/2+\varepsilon}_{T}
actually belongs to
Z^{s}_{T}
. Therefore, since
z
satisfies the Duhamel equation (
2.21
), it admits the nonlinear smoothing estimate (
1.22
), completing the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

5.
Polynomial Bound: Proof of Theorem
1.4

We shall now exploit the nonlinear smoothing effect of Theorem
1.3
in order to establish the polynomial bound of Theorem
1.4
.
We begin by proving such a bound for the solution
z
of the gauged Cauchy problem (
2.7
).

First, we suppose that
1\leq s\leq\frac{3}{2}-\varepsilon
for
\varepsilon
as in Theorem
1.3
.
Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
t\in[nT,(n+1)T]
, where
T=T(\left\|z_{0}\right\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T})})
is the local time of existence from Theorem
1.1
. Then, write
z
in the form

z(t)=Q_{\leq n^{2}}z(t)+Q_{>n^{2}}z(t),

where
\widehat{Q_{\leq N}z}(\xi)=\chi_{|\xi|\leq N}\widehat{z}(\xi)
and
Q_{>N}z
is defined similarly.
The term
Q_{\leq n^{2}}z(t)
satisfies

(5.1)

\left\|Q_{\leq n^{2}}z(t)\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T})}\leq c\,\langle{n\rangle}^{2(s-1)}\left\|z(t)\right\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T})}\leq C(\left\|z_{0}\right\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T})},T)\,\langle{t\rangle}^{2(s-1)}.

The term
Q_{>n^{2}}z(t)
can be handled by taking advantage of the nonlinear smoothing effect (
1.22
). Indeed,

(5.2)

Q_{>n^{2}}z(t)=Q_{>n^{2}}\big(z(t)-e^{i(t-nT)\partial_{x}^{2}}z(nT)\big)+Q_{>n^{2}}e^{i(t-nT)\partial_{x}^{2}}z(nT).

Thus, since
t\in[nT,(n+1)T]
and
s\leq\frac{3}{2}-\varepsilon
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|Q_{>n^{2}}\big(z(t)-e^{i(t-nT)\partial_{x}^{2}}z(nT)\big)\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle=\Big\|J_{x}^{s-(\frac{3}{2}-\varepsilon)}J^{\frac{3}{2}-\varepsilon}_{x}Q_{>n^{2}}\big(z(t)-e^{i(t-nT)\partial_{x}^{2}}z(nT)\big)\Big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle\leq c\,\langle{n\rangle}^{2(s-(\frac{3}{2}-\varepsilon))}\left\|Q_{>n^{2}}\big(z(t)-e^{i(t-nT)\partial_{x}^{2}}z(nT)\big)\right\|_{H^{\frac{3}{2}-\varepsilon}(\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle\leq\langle{n\rangle}^{2(s-(\frac{3}{2}-\varepsilon))}C(s,\left\|z(nT)\right\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T})},T)\left\|z(nT)\right\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T})}

(5.3)

\displaystyle\leq\langle{n\rangle}^{2(s-(\frac{3}{2}-\varepsilon))}\tilde{C}(s,\left\|z_{0}\right\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T})},T).

In order to estimate
Q_{>n^{2}}e^{i(t-nT)\partial_{x}^{2}}z(nT)
in (
5.2
), we first use the strict inequality

(5.4)

\left\|Q_{>n^{2}}e^{i(t-nT)\partial_{x}^{2}}z(nT)\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T})}\leq\left\|Q_{>(n-1)^{2}}z(nT)\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T})}.

Then, writing

Q_{>(n-1)^{2}}z(nT)=Q_{>(n-1)^{2}}\big(z(nT)-e^{i(nT-(n-1)T)\partial_{x}^{2}}z((n-1)T)\big)+Q_{>(n-1)^{2}}e^{iT\partial_{x}^{2}}z((n-1)T)

and proceeding similarly to (
5.3
) and (
5.4
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|Q_{>(n-1)^{2}}z(nT)\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle\leq\langle{n-1\rangle}^{2(s-(\frac{3}{2}-\varepsilon))}\tilde{C}(s,\left\|z_{0}\right\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T})},T)+\left\|Q_{>(n-2)^{2}}z((n-1)T)\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T})}.

We may inductively continue this process to arrive at

\displaystyle\left\|Q_{>n^{2}}z(t)\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle{k\rangle}^{2(s-(\frac{3}{2}-\varepsilon))}\tilde{C}(s,\left\|z_{0}\right\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T})},T)+\left\|z_{0}\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T})}.

Then, noting that

\sum_{k=1}^{n}k^{\alpha}\leq c_{\alpha}n^{\alpha+1},\quad\alpha>-1,

and observing that
2(s-(\frac{3}{2}-\varepsilon))>-1
since
s\geq 1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|Q_{>n^{2}}z(t)\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle{k\rangle}^{2(s-(\frac{3}{2}-\varepsilon))}\tilde{C}(s,\left\|z_{0}\right\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T})},T)+\left\|z_{0}\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle\leq\langle{n\rangle}^{2(s-1+\varepsilon)}\widetilde{C}(s,\left\|z_{0}\right\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T})},T)+\left\|z_{0}\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T})}

(5.5)

\displaystyle\leq\langle{t\rangle}^{2(s-1+\varepsilon)}\widetilde{\widetilde{C}}(\varepsilon,s,\left\|z_{0}\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T})},T).

Combining (
5.1
) and (
5.5
) yields

(5.6)

\left\|z(t)\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T})}\leq\langle{t\rangle}^{2(s-1+\varepsilon)}C(\varepsilon,s,\left\|z_{0}\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T})},T),\quad 1\leq s\leq\frac{3}{2}-\varepsilon.

Next, we consider the range
\frac{3}{2}-\varepsilon\leq s\leq 2-2\varepsilon
. We remark that the argument outlined for this range also extends to the range
s>2-2\varepsilon
. Once again, let
t\in[nT,(n+1)T]
and split
z(t)
as before. For
Q_{\leq n^{2}}z(t)
, we still have estimate (
5.1
). Also, as before, we write

Q_{>n^{2}}z(t)=Q_{>n^{2}}\big(z(t)-e^{i(t-nT)\partial_{x}^{2}}z(nT)\big)+Q_{>n^{2}}e^{i(t-nT)\partial_{x}^{2}}z(nT).

Then,

\displaystyle\left\|Q_{>n^{2}}\big(z(t)-e^{i(t-nT)\partial_{x}^{2}}z(nT)\big)\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle=\left\|J^{s-(2-2\varepsilon)}_{x}J^{2-2\varepsilon}_{x}Q_{>n^{2}}\big(z(t)-e^{i(t-nT)\partial_{x}^{2}}z(nT)\big)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle\leq c\,\langle{n\rangle}^{2(s-(2-2\varepsilon))}\left\|Q_{>n^{2}}\big(z(t)-e^{i(t-nT)\partial_{x}^{2}}z(nT)\big)\right\|_{H^{2-2\varepsilon}(\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle\leq\langle{n\rangle}^{2(s-(2-2\varepsilon))}C(s,\left\|z(nT)\right\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T})},T)\left\|z(nT)\right\|_{H^{\frac{3}{2}-\varepsilon}(\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle\leq\langle{n\rangle}^{2(s-(2-2\varepsilon))}\tilde{C}(s,\left\|z_{0}\right\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T})},T)\left\|z(nT)\right\|_{H^{\frac{3}{2}-\varepsilon}(\mathbb{T})}.

In addition, the bound (
5.6
) gives

\left\|z(nT)\right\|_{H^{\frac{3}{2}-\varepsilon}(\mathbb{T})}\leq\langle{nT\rangle}\,C(\left\|z_{0}\right\|_{H^{\frac{3}{2}-\varepsilon}(\mathbb{T})},T).

Consequently,

\left\|Q_{>n^{2}}\big(z(t)-e^{i(t-nT)\partial_{x}^{2}}z(nT)\big)\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T})}\leq\langle{n\rangle}^{2(s-(2-2\varepsilon))+1}\tilde{C}(\varepsilon,s,\left\|z_{0}\right\|_{H^{\frac{3}{2}-\varepsilon}(\mathbb{T})},T).

Repeating the procedure as before yields

\displaystyle\left\|Q_{>n^{2}}z(t)\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle{k\rangle}^{2(s-(2-2\varepsilon))+1}\tilde{C}(\varepsilon,s,\left\|z_{0}\right\|_{H^{\frac{3}{2}-\varepsilon}(\mathbb{T})},T)+\left\|z_{0}\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle\leq\langle{n\rangle}^{2(s-(2-2\varepsilon))+2}\tilde{C}(\varepsilon,s,\left\|z_{0}\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T})},T)+\left\|z_{0}\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle\leq\langle{t\rangle}^{2(s-(1-2\varepsilon))}\tilde{\tilde{C}}(\varepsilon,s,\left\|z_{0}\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T})},T).

Overall, recalling also (
5.6
), we have established the bound

\left\|z(t)\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T})}\leq\langle{t\rangle}^{2(s-(1-2\varepsilon))}C(\varepsilon,s,\left\|z_{0}\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T})},T),\quad 1\leq s\leq 2-2\varepsilon.

We may then repeat the above procedure to establish the bound (
5
) for all
s\geq 1
.

In order to extend the result to the solution
u
of the dNLS Cauchy problem (
1.1
), we begin by establishing the following product estimate.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
s_{1},s_{2}\geq s\geq 0
and
s_{1}+s_{2}>s+\frac{1}{2}
. Then

(5.7)

\displaystyle\left\|fg\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{H^{s_{1}}(\mathbb{T})}\left\|g\right\|_{H^{s_{2}}(\mathbb{T})}.

Proof.

The argument is standard and resembles the proof of the algebra property for Sobolev spaces.
For
r,r_{1},r_{2}\geq 0
, we claim that there exists a constant
C>0
so that

(5.8)

\displaystyle(1+|x|+|y|)^{r}\leq C\left[(1+|x|)^{r+r_{1}}(1+|y|)^{-r_{1}}+(1+|y|)^{r+r_{2}}(1+|x|)^{-r_{2}}\right].

Indeed, first note that

\displaystyle(1+|x|+|y|)^{r}(1+|x|)^{r_{2}}(1+|y|)^{r_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq(1+|x|+|y|)^{r+r_{1}+r_{2}}.

Thus, to establish (
5.8
), it suffices to show that there exists
C=C(t)>0
so that

(5.9)

\displaystyle(1+|x|+|y|)^{t}\leq C\left[(1+|x|)^{t}+(1+|y|)^{t}\right]

for all
t\geq 0
. Without loss of generality, assume that
(1+|y|)\leq(1+|x|)
. Then

(1+|x|+|y|)^{t}\leq(1+|x|+1+|y|)^{t}\leq 2^{t}(1+|x|)^{t}\leq 2^{t}\left[(1+|x|)^{t}+(1+|y|)^{t}\right].

Therefore, (
5.9
) holds with
C=2^{t}
, for all
t\geq 0
. Consequently, we have (
5.8
).

Next, we apply (
5.8
) with
r=s
,
r_{1}=s_{1}-s
, and
r_{2}=s_{2}-s
to see that

\displaystyle\langle{\xi\rangle}^{s}\leq(1+|\xi|)^{s}\lesssim(1+|\xi-\eta|)^{s_{1}}(1+|\eta|)^{s-s_{1}}+(1+|\xi-\eta|)^{s-s_{2}}(1+|\eta|)^{s_{2}}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\left\|fg\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\xi}(1+|\xi|)^{2s}\bigg(\sum_{\eta}|\widehat{f}(\xi-\eta)||\widehat{g}(\eta)|\bigg)^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\xi}\bigg(\sum_{\eta}(1+|\xi-\eta|)^{s_{1}}|\widehat{f}(\xi-\eta)|(1+|\eta|)^{s-s_{1}}|\widehat{g}(\eta)|\bigg)^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\sum_{\xi}\bigg(\sum_{\eta}(1+|\xi-\eta|)^{s-s_{2}}|\widehat{f}(\xi-\eta)|(1+|\eta|)^{s_{2}}|\widehat{g}(\eta)|\bigg)^{2},

where we have applied (
5.9
) for
t=2
in the previous inequality. It suffices to bound the first sum above, as the second sum is treated the same after a change of variables. We apply Minkowski’s inequality to obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\xi}\bigg(\sum_{\eta}(1+|\xi-\eta|)^{s_{1}}|\widehat{f}(\xi-\eta)|(1+|\eta|)^{s-s_{1}}|\widehat{g}(\eta)|\bigg)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|f\right\|_{H^{s_{1}}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}\bigg(\sum_{\eta}(1+|\eta|)^{s-s_{1}}|\widehat{g}(\eta)|\bigg)^{2}.

Finally, due to the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

\displaystyle\sum_{\eta}(1+|\eta|)^{s-s_{1}}|\widehat{g}(\eta)|

\displaystyle=\sum_{\eta}(1+|\eta|)^{s-s_{1}-s_{2}}(1+|\eta|)^{s_{2}}|\widehat{g}(\eta)|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|g\right\|_{H^{s_{2}}(\mathbb{T})}\left\|(1+|\eta|)^{-(s_{1}+s_{2}-s)}\right\|_{\ell^{2}_{\eta}},

where the above norm is finite due to the fact that
s_{1}+s_{2}-s>\frac{1}{2}
.
∎

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
s\geq 0
. Then, there exists
C>0
such that

(5.10)

\displaystyle\left\|u(t)\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T})}\leq C\Big(1+\left\|z(t)\right\|^{2}_{H^{\frac{1}{4}}(\mathbb{T})}\Big)\left\|z(t)\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T})}.

Proof.

Clearly,
\left\|z(t)\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T})}=\left\|w(t)\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T})}
, for
w
given by (
1.17
) . Furthermore, since
\widehat{w}(\xi,t)=e^{-2i\xi\mu t}\widehat{v}(\xi,t)
, for
v
given by (
1.14
), we have that
\left\|w(t)\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T})}=\left\|v(t)\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T})}
. Thus, it suffices to establish (
5.10
) for
z(t)
replaced by
v(t)
.

Recall that
u(x,t)=e^{i\mathcal{I}(v)(x,t)}v(x,t)
. For
\delta>0
, we apply Proposition
5.1
with
s_{1}=s
and
s_{2}=\frac{1}{2}+\delta
to obtain

\left\|u(t)\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T})}=\left\|e^{i\mathcal{I}(v)(t)}v(t)\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T})}\leq C\left\|e^{i\mathcal{I}(v)(t)}\right\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}+\delta}(\mathbb{T})}\left\|v(t)\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T})}.

Now, observe that

\displaystyle\left\|e^{i\mathcal{I}(v)(t)}\right\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}+\delta}(\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|e^{i\mathcal{I}(v)(t)}\right\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T})}=\left\|e^{i\mathcal{I}(v)(t)}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}+\left\|\partial_{x}\mathcal{I}(v)(t)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2\pi}+\left\||v(t)|^{2}-\mu\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}\leq C\left(1+\left\|v(t)\right\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}\right)\leq C\Big(1+\left\|v(t)\right\|^{2}_{H^{\frac{1}{4}}(\mathbb{T})}\Big),

where the last inequality follows from Sobolev embedding.
∎

Remark 5.1
.

It is clear from the proof of Lemma
5.1
that the estimate (
5.10
) holds with the roles of
u
and
z
switched.

Returning to the case that
s\geq 1
and applying Lemma
5.1
and the bound (
5.6
) yields

\displaystyle\left\|u(t)\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(1+\left\|z(t)\right\|_{H^{\frac{1}{4}}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}\Big)\left\|z(t)\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle\leq C(\left\|z_{0}\right\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T})})\left\|z(t)\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle\leq C(\left\|z_{0}\right\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T})})\,\tilde{C}(\varepsilon,s,\left\|z_{0}\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T})},T)\,\langle{t\rangle}^{2(s-1)+\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\leq C(\varepsilon,s,\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T})},T)\,\langle{t\rangle}^{2(s-1)+\varepsilon},

which concludes the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

The Hankel determinants for the generalized derangement polynomials of order r r
GHANIA GUETTAI, DIFFALAH LAISSAOUI, MOURAD RAHMANI

Abstract
. This paper sets out to introduce the generalized derangement polynomials of order r r . It then proceeds to establish various identities associated with these polynomials, along with providing recurrence relations for derangement polynomials of order r r . Additionally, the paper offers a probabilistic approach for the generalized derangement polynomials of order r r . Furthermore, it furnishes a clear expression for the Hankel determinants pertaining to these generalized derangement polynomials of order r r , and subsequently infers the Hankel determinants for derangement polynomials of the same order, as well as for the count of cyclic derangements.

1.
Introduction

A derangement refers to a permutation where none of the elements occupy their original positions. The derangement number, denoted as
D_{n}
, represents the count of such permutations for a set of
n
elements (also known as the number of fixed point-free permutations). The task of enumerating derangements was first tackled by Pierre Rémond de Montmort (1678-1719) and was referred to as ”le problème de rencontres” in French. The first few terms of the derangement number are
D_{0}=1
,
D_{1}=0
,
D_{2}=1
,
D_{3}=2
,
D_{4}=9,\ldots
.

A simple explicit expression for
D_{n}
is (see
[
2
,
3
]
)

(1.1)

D_{n}=n!\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{k}}{k!}.

From (
1.1
), we note that the exponential generating function of derangement numbers is given by

(1.2)

\frac{e^{-z}}{1-z}=\sum_{n\geq 0}D_{n}\frac{z^{n}}{n!}.

A fixed point-free permutations on
n+r
letters will be called fixed point-free
r
-permutation if in its cycle decomposition the first
r
letters appear to be in distinct cycles. The number of fixed point-free
r
-permutations denote by
D_{n}^{(r)}
and call
r
-derangement number
(r\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast})
. The first
r
elements, as well as the cycles they are contained in will be called distinguished. For any
r\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast}
, the exponential generating function of the sequence of
r
-derangements numbers is given by (see
[
7
]
)

(1.3)

\frac{z{{}^{r}}e^{-z}}{\left(1-z\right)^{r+1}}=\sum_{n\geq 0}D_{n}^{(r)}\frac{z^{n}}{n!}.

The derangement numbers
d_{n}^{(r)}
of order
r

(n\geq 0)
, are defined by the exponential generating function (see
[
4
]
)

(1.4)

\frac{e^{-z}}{\left(1-z\right)^{r}}=\sum_{n\geq 0}d_{n}^{(r)}\frac{z^{n}}{n!}.

Many generalizations and extensions have been published in recent years. Firstly, the
r
-derangement polynomials
D_{n}^{(r)}(x)
, are defined by the exponential generating function (see
[
7
]
)

(1.5)

\frac{z{{}^{r}}e^{xz}}{\left(1-z\right)^{r+1}}=\sum_{n\geq 0}D_{n}^{(r)}(x)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}.

Secondly, the derangement polynomials
d_{n}^{(r)}(x)
of order
r
, are defined by the exponential generating function (see
[
4
]
)

(1.6)

\frac{e^{xz}}{\left(1-z\right)^{r}}=\sum_{n\geq 0}d_{n}^{(r)}(x)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}.

Another generalization of the derangement number is the number of cyclic derangements
d_{n,r}
, are defined by the exponential generating function (see
[
1
]
)

(1.7)

\frac{e^{-z}}{1-rz}=\sum_{n\geq 0}d_{n,r}\frac{z^{n}}{n!}.

2.
The generalized derangement polynomials of order
r

This section introduces a novel set of polynomials termed as the ”generalized derangement polynomials of order
r
”. We will outline their characteristics and elucidate their connection with derangement polynomials of the same order. Following this, we will provide the recurrence relations governing these derangement polynomials of degree
r
.

Definition 2.1
.

We define the generalized derangement polynomials
\mathbb{D}_{n}^{(r)}\left(x\right)
of order
r
by

(2.1)

\frac{e^{z}}{\left(1-xz\right)^{r}}=\sum_{n\geq 0}\mathbb{D}_{n}^{(r)}\left(x\right)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}.

Theorem 2.2
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have the explicit formula of the generalized derangement polynomials of order
r

(2.2)

\mathbb{D}_{n}^{(r)}\left(x\right)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}r^{\overline{k}}x^{k}.

where
r^{\overline{k}}
is the rising factorial, defined by
r^{\overline{k}}=r(r+1)...(r+k-1)
, with
r^{\overline{0}}=1
.

Proof.

By using the exponential generating function (
2.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\mathbb{D}_{n}^{(r)}\left(x\right)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{z}}{\left(1-xz\right)^{r}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{r^{\overline{k}}(xz)^{k}}{k!}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{z^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 0}r^{\overline{k}}x^{k}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{z^{k}z^{n}}{k!n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 0}r^{\overline{k}}x^{k}\sum_{n\geq 0}\binom{n+k}{k}\frac{z^{n+k}}{(n+k)!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 0}r^{\overline{k}}x^{k}\sum_{n\geq k}\binom{n}{k}\frac{z^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}r^{\overline{k}}x^{k}\right)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}.

Equating the coefficients of
\frac{z^{n}}{n!}
in both sides of the above expression, we get the desired result.
∎

The recurrence relation of the generalized derangement polynomials
\mathbb{D}_{n}^{(r)}\left(x\right)
of order
r
is given by

Theorem 2.3
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

(2.3)

\mathbb{D}_{n+1}^{(r)}\left(x\right)=\mathbb{D}_{n}^{(r)}\left(x\right)+rx\mathbb{D}_{n}^{(r+1)}\left(x\right).

Proof.

By derivation the exponential generating function (
2.1
) with respect to
z
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dz}\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}\mathbb{D}_{n}^{(r)}\left(x\right)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dz}\left(\frac{e^{z}}{\left(1-xz\right)^{r}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{z}}{\left(1-xz\right)^{r}}+\frac{rxe^{z}}{\left(1-xz\right)^{r+1}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}\mathbb{D}_{n}^{(r)}\left(x\right)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}+rx\sum_{n\geq 0}\mathbb{D}_{n}^{(r+1)}\left(x\right)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}\left(\mathbb{D}_{n}^{(r)}\left(x\right)+rx\mathbb{D}_{n}^{(r+1)}\left(x\right)\right)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}.

On the other hand, we have

\frac{d}{dz}\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}\mathbb{D}_{n}^{(r)}\left(x\right)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}\right)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\mathbb{D}_{n+1}^{(r)}\left(x\right)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}.

Equating the coefficients of
\frac{z^{n}}{n!}
in both sides of the above expression, we get the desired result.
∎

Theorem 2.4
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

(2.4)

\mathbb{D}_{n+1}^{(r)}\left(x\right)=\mathbb{D}_{n}^{(r)}\left(x\right)+rx\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\mathbb{D}_{k}^{(r)}\left(x\right)x^{n-k}\left(n-k\right)!.

Proof.

Using the derivative of the generating function (
2.1
) with respect to
z
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dz}\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}\mathbb{D}_{n}^{(r)}\left(x\right)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dz}\left(\frac{e^{z}}{\left(1-xz\right)^{r}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{z}}{\left(1-xz\right)^{r}}+\frac{rxe^{z}}{\left(1-xz\right)^{r+1}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}\mathbb{D}_{n}^{(r)}\left(x\right)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}+rx\sum_{n\geq 0}\mathbb{D}_{n}^{(r)}\left(x\right)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}\sum_{n\geq 0}n!x^{n}\frac{z^{n}}{n!}.

Thus,

\sum_{n\geq 0}\mathbb{D}_{n+1}^{(r)}\left(x\right)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}=\sum_{n\geq 0}\left(\mathbb{D}_{n}^{(r)}\left(x\right)+rx\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\mathbb{D}_{k}^{(r)}\left(x\right)x^{n-k}\left(n-k\right)!\right)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}.

Equating the coefficients of
\frac{z^{n}}{n!}
in both sides of the above expression, we get the desired result. ∎

Here we present some properties of the derangement polynomials
d_{n}^{(r)}(x)
of order
r
given by the formula (
1.6
). We start with the explicit form and then provide two recurrence relations.

Theorem 2.5
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have the explicit formula of the derangement polynomials
d_{n}^{(r)}(x)
of order
r

(2.5)

{d}_{n}^{(r)}\left(x\right)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}r^{\overline{k}}x^{n-k}.

where
r^{\overline{k}}
is the rising factorial.

Proof.

By using the exponential generating function (
1.6
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}{d}_{n}^{(r)}\left(x\right)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{xz}}{\left(1-z\right)^{r}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{r^{\overline{k}}z^{k}}{k!}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{(xz)^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 0}r^{\overline{k}}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{x^{n}z^{n+k}}{k!n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 0}r^{\overline{k}}\sum_{n\geq 0}\binom{n+k}{k}x^{n}\frac{z^{n+k}}{(n+k)!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 0}r^{\overline{k}}\sum_{n\geq k}\binom{n}{k}x^{n-k}\frac{z^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}r^{\overline{k}}x^{n-k}\right)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}.

Equating the coefficients of
\frac{z^{n}}{n!}
in both sides of the above expression, we get the desired result.
∎

Now, it is easy to show the relationship between the polynomials
\mathbb{D}_{n}^{(r)}\left(x\right)
and the derangement polynomials
d_{n}^{\left(r\right)}\left(x\right)
of order
r
. For
n\geq 0
and
x\neq 0
, we have

(2.6)

x^{n}\mathbb{D}_{n}^{(r)}\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)=d_{n}^{\left(r\right)}\left(x\right).

Then, we obtain the following corollaries

Corollary 2.6
.

For
n\geq 0
and
x\neq 0
, we have

(2.7)

d_{n+1}^{(r)}\left(x\right)=xd_{n}^{(r)}\left(x\right)+rd_{n}^{(r+1)}\left(x\right)\text{.}

Proof.

Using the formula (
2.3
) and replacing
x
by
\frac{1}{x}
, after that multiplying the both sides of the new expression by
x^{n+1}
. Finally, by using the formula (
2.6
), we obtain the desired formula (
2.7
).
∎

Corollary 2.7
.

For
n\geq 0
and
x\neq 0
, we have

(2.8)

d_{n+1}^{(r)}\left(x\right)=xd_{n}^{(r)}\left(x\right)+r\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}d_{k}^{(r)}\left(x\right)\left(n-k\right)!.

3.
Probabilistic approach

In this section, we will present a probabilistic representation of the generalized derangement polynomials of order
r
. Let
f_{X}(x)
be the probability density function of the continuous random variable
X
, and let
h(x)
be a real valued function. Then the expectation
of
h(X)
, is defined by (see
[
6
]
)

E(h(X))={\displaystyle\int\limits_{-\infty}^{+\infty}}h(x)f_{X}(x)dx.

A continuous random variable
X
, is said to be the gamma random variable with parameters
\alpha,\beta
; for some
\alpha>0
and
\beta>0
, if its density function is given by

f_{X}(x)=\genfrac{}{.}{0.0pt}{}{\dfrac{\beta^{\alpha}x^{\alpha-1}e^{-\beta x}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\text{ \ \ if }x\geq 0,}{0\text{ \ }else}

where
\Gamma(\alpha)
is the gamma function, is denoted
X\sim\Gamma(\alpha,\beta)
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
Y\sim\Gamma(1,1).
Then, for all
x<1
, we have

e^{z}\phi_{Y}(z)=\sum_{n\geq 0}D_{n}\frac{z^{n}}{n!},

where
\phi_{Y}
is the moment generating function of random variable
Y
.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{r}
be independent and identically distributed gamma random variables with shape
\alpha=1
and rate
\beta=1
, we assume

Y_{r}={\displaystyle\sum\limits_{l=1}^{r}}X_{l}
, then

e^{z}\phi_{Y_{r}}(xz)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\mathbb{D}_{n}^{(r)}\left(x\right)\frac{z^{n}}{n!},

where
\phi_{Y_{r}}
is the moment generating function of random variable

Y_{r}.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{r}
be independent and identically distributed
random variables with
X\sim\Gamma(1,1)
, we assume
Y_{r}={\displaystyle\sum\limits_{l=1}^{r}}X_{l}
, then

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}_{n}^{(r)}\left(x\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}x^{k}E(Y_{r}^{k})

and

d_{n}^{\left(r\right)}\left(x\right)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}x^{n-k}E(Y_{r}^{k}).

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{r}
be independent and identically distributed random variables with
X\sim\Gamma(1,1)
, then

\displaystyle E\left(X_{1}+X_{2}+\cdots+X_{r}\right)^{k}

\displaystyle=r^{\overline{k}}.

4.
On the Hankel determinant of
r-
derangement polynomials

The Hankel matrix of order
n+1
of a sequence
a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,
is the
n+1
by
n+1
matrix whose
(i,j)
entry is
a_{i+j},
where the indices range between
0
and
n
. The Hankel determinant of order
n+1
is the determinant of the corresponding Hankel matrix, that is,

\det(a_{i+j})_{0\leq i,j\leq n}=\det\left(\begin{array}[c]{cccc}a_{0}&a_{1}&\cdots&a_{n}\\
a_{1}&a_{2}&\cdots&a_{n+1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
a_{n}&a_{n+1}&\cdots&a_{2n}\end{array}\right).

In this section, employing a methodology akin to that in
[
5
]
, we construct a comprehensive formulation for the Hankel determinant of the generalized derangement polynomials in terms of order
r
. Subsequently, we derive the Hankel determinant for the derangement polynomials of order
r
, as well as for the count of cyclic derangements.

Lemma 4.1
.

The following formula holds true

\mathbb{D}_{n}^{(r)}\left(\frac{1}{1-z}\right)=\left(1-z\right)^{r}e^{-z}\frac{d^{n}}{dz^{n}}\left(\frac{e^{z}}{\left(1-z\right)^{r}}\right).

Proof.

According to Leibniz’s formula, we have the
n
-th derivative of the generating function of
\mathbb{D}_{n}^{(r)}\left(1\right)

\displaystyle\frac{d^{n}}{dz^{n}}\left(\frac{e^{z}}{\left(1-z\right)^{r}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\frac{d^{n-k}}{dz^{n-k}}\left(e^{z}\right)\frac{d^{k}}{dz^{k}}\left(\frac{1}{\left(1-z\right)^{r}}\right)

\displaystyle=e^{z}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}r^{\overline{k}}\frac{1}{\left(1-z\right)^{r+k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{z}}{\left(1-z\right)^{r}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}r^{\overline{k}}\frac{1}{\left(1-z\right)^{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{z}}{\left(1-z\right)^{r}}\mathbb{D}_{n}^{(r)}\left(\frac{1}{1-z}\right).

∎

Theorem 4.2
.

For
n\geq 1
and
z\neq 1
, we have

(4.1)

\displaystyle\det\left(\frac{\partial^{i+j-2}}{\partial z^{i+j-2}}\left(\frac{e^{z}}{\left(1-z\right)^{r}}\right)\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq n}=\left(\frac{e^{z}}{\left(1-z\right)^{r}}\right)^{n}\frac{r^{\overline{n-1}}{\displaystyle\prod\limits_{k=1}^{n-1}}r^{\overline{k-1}}k!}{\left(z-1\right)^{\left(n-1\right)n}}.

Proof.

By induction, using Sylvestre’s formula, for
x=1
, we get the numbers

\mathbb{D}_{n}^{(r)}\left(1\right)=\mathbb{D}_{n}^{(r)}
, according to lemma
4.1
, the generating function of
\mathbb{D}_{n}^{(r)}
is

G\left(z\right)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\mathbb{D}_{n}^{(r)}\frac{z^{n}}{n!}=\frac{e^{z}}{\left(1-z\right)^{r}}.

We denote that the Hankel determinant

\left(\det\left(\frac{\partial^{i+j-2}}{\partial z^{i+j-2}}G\left(z\right)\right)_{i,j=1,2,\ldots,n}\right)

of the
i+j-2
-th derivative of the generating function
G\left(z\right)
by
\Delta_{n}\left(G\left(z\right)\right)
.

For
n=1
, we have

\Delta_{1}\left(G\left(z\right)\right)=\frac{e^{z}}{\left(1-z\right)^{r}}.

For
n=2
, we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{2}\left(G\left(z\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\left|\begin{array}[c]{cc}G\left(z\right)&\frac{\partial}{\partial z}G\left(z\right)\\
\frac{\partial}{\partial z}G\left(z\right)&\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z^{2}}G\left(z\right)\end{array}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\begin{array}[c]{cc}\frac{e^{z}}{\left(1-z\right)^{r}}&\frac{e^{z}}{\left(1-z\right)^{r}}\left[1+\frac{r}{\left(1-z\right)}\right]\\
\frac{e^{z}}{\left(1-z\right)^{r}}\left[1+\frac{r}{\left(1-z\right)}\right]&\frac{e^{z}}{\left(1-z\right)^{r}}\left[1+\frac{2r}{\left(1-z\right)}+\frac{r\left(r+1\right)}{\left(1-z\right)^{2}}\right]\end{array}\right|

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{e^{z}}{\left(1-z\right)^{r}}\right)^{2}\frac{r}{\left(z-1\right)^{2}}.

Suppose that, the formula (
4.1
) is true for
n=1,2,\ldots,m
and we calculate
\Delta_{m+1}\left(G\left(z\right)\right)
by Sylvester’s formula

\Delta_{m+1}\left(G\left(z\right)\right)=\frac{\Delta_{2}\left(\Delta_{m}\left(G\left(z\right)\right)\right)}{\Delta_{m-1}\left(G\left(z\right)\right)}.

By induction hypothesis, we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{m}\left(G\left(z\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{e^{z}}{\left(1-z\right)^{r}}\right)^{m}\frac{r^{\overline{m-1}}{\displaystyle\prod\limits_{k=1}^{m-1}}r^{\overline{k-1}}k!}{\left(z-1\right)^{\left(m-1\right)m}}

\displaystyle=\left(r^{\overline{m-1}}{\displaystyle\prod\limits_{k=1}^{m-1}}r^{\overline{k-1}}k!\right)\frac{e^{mz}}{\left(1-z\right)^{mr+\left(m-1\right)m}}.

To calculate
\Delta_{2}\left(\Delta_{m}\left(G\left(z\right)\right)\right)
, we need to calculate the
1
st and the
2
nd derivative of
\Delta_{m}\left(G\left(z\right)\right)
, for that we have the
1
st derivative

\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\left(\Delta_{m}\left(G\left(z\right)\right)\right)=\left(r^{\overline{m-1}}{\displaystyle\prod\limits_{k=1}^{m-1}}r^{\overline{k-1}}k!\right)e^{mz}\\
\left(\frac{m\left(1-z\right)^{mr+\left(m-1\right)m}+\left(mr+m\left(m-1\right)\right)\left(1-z\right)^{mr+m\left(m-1\right)-1}}{\left(1-z\right)^{2(mr+m\left(m-1\right))}}\right)\\
=\left(r^{\overline{m-1}}{\displaystyle\prod\limits_{k=1}^{m-1}}r^{\overline{k-1}}k!\right)e^{mz}\times\alpha_{m}\left(z\right),

with

\displaystyle\alpha_{m}\left(z\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{m}{\left(1-z\right)^{m\left(r+m-1\right)}}+\frac{m\left(r+m-1\right)}{\left(1-z\right)^{m\left(r+m-1\right)+1}}.

The 2nd derivative of
\Delta_{m}\left(G\left(z\right)\right)
is

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z^{2}}\left(\Delta_{m}\left(G\left(z\right)\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\left(r^{\overline{m-1}}{\displaystyle\prod\limits_{k=1}^{m-1}}r^{\overline{k-1}}k!\right)e^{mz}\left[m\times\alpha_{m}\left(z\right)+\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\alpha_{m}\left(z\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\left(r^{\overline{m-1}}{\displaystyle\prod\limits_{k=1}^{m-1}}r^{\overline{k-1}}k!\right)e^{mz}\times\beta_{m}\left(z\right),

with

\displaystyle\beta_{m}\left(z\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{m^{2}}{\left(1-z\right)^{m\left(r+m-1\right)}}+\frac{2m^{2}\left(r+m-1\right)}{\left(1-z\right)^{m\left(r+m-1\right)+1}}

\displaystyle+\frac{m\left(r+m-1\right)\left(m\left(r+m-1\right)+1\right)}{\left(1-z\right)^{m\left(r+m-1\right)+2}}.

Therefore

\Delta_{2}\left(\Delta_{m}\left(G\left(z\right)\right)\right)=\left(r^{\overline{m-1}}{\displaystyle\prod\limits_{k=1}^{m-1}}r^{\overline{k-1}}k!\right)^{2}e^{2mz}\\
\times\left|\begin{array}[c]{c}\frac{1}{\left(1-z\right)^{m\left(r+m-1\right)}}\\
\frac{m}{\left(1-z\right)^{m\left(r+m-1\right)}}+\frac{m\left(r+m-1\right)}{\left(1-z\right)^{m\left(r+m-1\right)+1}}\end{array}\right.\\
\left.\begin{array}[c]{c}\frac{m}{\left(1-z\right)^{m\left(r+m-1\right)}}+\frac{m\left(r+m-1\right)}{\left(1-z\right)^{m\left(r+m-1\right)+1}}\\
\frac{m^{2}}{\left(1-z\right)^{m\left(r+m-1\right)}}+\frac{2m^{2}\left(r+m-1\right)}{\left(1-z\right)^{m\left(r+m-1\right)+1}}+\frac{m\left(r+m-1\right)\left(m\left(r+m-1\right)+1\right)}{\left(1-z\right)^{m\left(r+m-1\right)+2}}\end{array}\right|.

Thus,

\displaystyle\Delta_{2}\left(\Delta_{m}\left(G\left(z\right)\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\left(r^{\overline{m-1}}{\displaystyle\prod\limits_{k=1}^{m-1}}r^{\overline{k-1}}k!\right)^{2}e^{2mz}\frac{1}{\left(1-z\right)^{2m\left(r+m-1\right)}}

\displaystyle{\small\times\left|\begin{array}[c]{cc}1&m+\frac{m\left(r+m-1\right)}{1-z}\\
m+\frac{m\left(r+m-1\right)}{1-z}&m^{2}+\frac{2m^{2}\left(r+m-1\right)}{1-z}+\frac{m\left(r+m-1\right)\left(m\left(r+m-1\right)+1\right)}{\left(1-z\right)^{2}}\end{array}\right|}

\displaystyle=\left(r^{\overline{m-1}}{\displaystyle\prod\limits_{k=1}^{m-1}}r^{\overline{k-1}}k!\right)^{2}e^{2mz}\frac{m\left(r+m-1\right)}{\left(1-z\right)^{2m\left(r+m-1\right)+2}}.

According to Sylvester’s formula, we find

\displaystyle\Delta_{m+1}\left(G\left(z\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Delta_{2}\left(\Delta_{m}\left(G\left(z\right)\right)\right)}{\Delta_{m-1}\left(G\left(z\right)\right)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(r^{\overline{m-1}}{\displaystyle\prod\limits_{k=1}^{m-1}}r^{\overline{k-1}}k!\right)^{2}e^{2mz}\frac{m\left(r+m-1\right)}{\left(1-z\right)^{2m\left(r+m-1\right)+2}}}{\left(\left(r\right)^{\overline{m-2}}{\displaystyle\prod\limits_{k=1}^{m-2}}r^{\overline{k-1}}k!\right)\frac{e^{(m-1)z}}{\left(1-z\right)^{\left(r+m-2\right)\left(m-1\right)}}}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\Delta_{m+1}\left(G\left(z\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\left(r^{\overline{m-1}}{\displaystyle\prod\limits_{k=1}^{m-1}}r^{\overline{k-1}}k!\right)r^{\overline{m-1}}m!e^{mz+z}\frac{\left(r+m-1\right)}{\left(1-z\right)^{mr+m^{2}+r+2m}}

\displaystyle=\left(r^{\overline{m}}{\displaystyle\prod\limits_{k=1}^{m}}r^{\overline{k-1}}k!\right)e^{\left(m+1\right)z}\frac{1}{\left(1-z\right)^{\left(m+1\right)r+\left(m+1\right)m}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{e^{z}}{\left(1-z\right)^{r}}\right)^{m+1}\frac{r^{\overline{m}}{\displaystyle\prod\limits_{k=1}^{m}}r^{\overline{k-1}}k!}{\left(z-1\right)^{\left(m+1\right)m}}.

∎

Lemma 4.3
.

For
n\geq 1
and
z\neq 1
, we have

\det\left(\frac{e^{z}}{\left(1-z\right)^{r}}\mathbb{D}_{i+j-2}^{(r)}\left(\frac{1}{1-z}\right)\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq n+1}=\\
\left(\frac{e^{z}}{\left(1-z\right)^{r}}\right)^{n+1}\det\left(\mathbb{D}_{i+j-2}^{(r)}\left(\frac{1}{1-z}\right)\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq n+1}.

Theorem 4.4
.

The Hankel determinant of order
n+1
of the generalized derangement polynomials
\mathbb{D}_{n}^{(r)}\left(z\right)
of order
r
, is given by

\det\left(\mathbb{D}_{i+j-2}^{(r)}\left(z\right)\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq n+1}=z^{n\left(n+1\right)}r^{\overline{n}}{\displaystyle\prod\limits_{k=1}^{n}}r^{\overline{k-1}}k!.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.3
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\frac{e^{z}}{\left(1-z\right)^{r}}\right)^{n+1}\det\left(\mathbb{D}_{i+j-2}^{(r)}\left(\frac{1}{1-z}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\det\left(\frac{\partial^{i+j-2}}{\partial z^{i+j-2}}\left(\frac{e^{z}}{\left(1-z\right)^{r}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{e^{z}}{\left(1-z\right)^{r}}\right)^{n+1}\frac{r^{\overline{n}}{\displaystyle\prod\limits_{k=1}^{n}}r^{\overline{k-1}}k!}{\left(z-1\right)^{\left(n+1\right)n}},

from where

\displaystyle\det\left(\mathbb{D}_{i+j-2}^{(r)}\left(\frac{1}{1-z}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{r^{\overline{n}}{\displaystyle\prod\limits_{k=1}^{n}}r^{\overline{k-1}}k!}{\left(z-1\right)^{\left(n+1\right)n}}

\displaystyle=\left(-1\right)^{n(n+1)}\left(\frac{1}{1-z}\right)^{{}^{\left(n+1\right)n}}r^{\overline{n}}{\displaystyle\prod\limits_{k=1}^{n}}r^{\overline{k-1}}k!

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{1-z}\right)^{{}^{\left(n+1\right)n}}r^{\overline{n}}{\displaystyle\prod\limits_{k=1}^{n}}r^{\overline{k-1}}k!.

Now let’s pose
T=\frac{1}{1-z}
, we obtain

\det\left(\mathbb{D}_{i+j-2}^{(r)}\left(T\right)\right)=T^{n\left(n+1\right)}r^{\overline{n}}{\displaystyle\prod\limits_{k=1}^{n}}r^{\overline{k-1}}k!.

∎

Remark 4.5

When
r=1

n+1

[(i+j)!]

Theorem 4.6
.

The Hankel determinant of order
n+1
of the derangement polynomials
d_{n}^{(r)}\left(z\right)
of order
r
, is given by

\det\left(d_{i+j-2}^{(r)}\left(z\right)\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq n+1}=r^{\overline{n}}{\displaystyle\prod\limits_{k=1}^{n}}r^{\overline{k-1}}k!.

Proof.

We have,

\displaystyle\det\left(d_{i+j-2}^{(r)}\left(z\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\left|\begin{array}[c]{cccc}z^{0}\mathbb{D}_{0}^{(r)}\left(\frac{1}{z}\right)&z^{1}\mathbb{D}_{1}^{(r)}\left(\frac{1}{z}\right)&\ldots&z^{n}\mathbb{D}_{n}^{(r)}\left(\frac{1}{z}\right)\\
z^{1}\mathbb{D}_{1}^{(r)}\left(\frac{1}{z}\right)&z^{2}\mathbb{D}_{2}^{(r)}\left(\frac{1}{z}\right)&\ldots&z^{n+1}\mathbb{D}_{n+1}^{(r)}\left(\frac{1}{z}\right)\\
\ldotp&\ldotp&\ldotp&\ldotp\\
z^{n}\mathbb{D}_{n}^{(r)}\left(\frac{1}{z}\right)&z^{n+1}\mathbb{D}_{n+1}^{(r)}\left(\frac{1}{z}\right)&\ldots&z^{2n}\mathbb{D}_{2n}^{(r)}\left(\frac{1}{z}\right)\end{array}\right|

\displaystyle=\left(z^{0}z^{1}z^{2}\cdots z^{n}\right)^{2}\left|\begin{array}[c]{cccc}\mathbb{D}_{0}^{(r)}\left(\frac{1}{z}\right)&\mathbb{D}_{1}^{(r)}\left(\frac{1}{z}\right)&\ldots&\mathbb{D}_{n}^{(r)}\left(\frac{1}{z}\right)\\
\mathbb{D}_{1}^{(r)}\left(\frac{1}{z}\right)&\mathbb{D}_{2}^{(r)}\left(\frac{1}{z}\right)&\ldots&\mathbb{D}_{n+1}^{(r)}\left(\frac{1}{z}\right)\\
\ldotp&\ldotp&\ldotp&\ldotp\\
\mathbb{D}_{n}^{(r)}\left(\frac{1}{z}\right)&\mathbb{D}_{n+1}^{(r)}\left(\frac{1}{z}\right)&\ldots&\mathbb{D}_{2n}^{(r)}\left(\frac{1}{z}\right)\end{array}\right|

\displaystyle=\left(z^{0}z^{1}z^{2}\cdots z^{n}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{1}{z}\right)^{n\left(n+1\right)}r^{\overline{n}}{\displaystyle\prod\limits_{k=1}^{n}}r^{\overline{k-1}}k!

\displaystyle=z^{n\left(n+1\right)-n\left(n+1\right)}r^{\overline{n}}{\displaystyle\prod\limits_{k=1}^{n}}r^{\overline{k-1}}k!

\displaystyle=r^{\overline{n}}{\displaystyle\prod\limits_{k=1}^{n}}r^{\overline{k-1}}k!.

∎

The relationship between the generalized derangement polynomials

(4.2)

Now, using the Theorem
4.4

∎

Relativistic Exchange Bounds
Long Meng, Heinz Siedentop, Matthias Tiefenbeck

Abstract
. We collect estimates of the exchange energy of the relativistic no-pair Hartree-Fock and Müller functional and use them to show the existence of a minimizer and stability of matter of the relativistic Müller functional in the free picture.

The no-pair relativistic molecular Hartree-Fock functional is

(1.3)

\begin{split}{\mathcal{E}}^{\mathrm{HF}}_{c,Z}:&G\to\mathbb{R}\\
\gamma\mapsto&{\rm tr\,}[(D_{c,\varphi}\gamma)+{\mathcal{D}}[\rho_{\gamma}]-\mathcal{X}[\gamma]+\underbrace{\sum_{1\leq k<l\leq K}{Z_{k}Z_{l}\over|{\mathbb{R}}_{k}-{\mathbb{R}}_{l}|}}_{=:R}\end{split}

where

(1.4)

{\mathcal{D}}(\rho,\sigma):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathrm{d}{\mathbb{x}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathrm{d}{\mathbb{y}}{\overline{\rho({\mathbb{x}})}\sigma({\mathbb{y}})\over|{\mathbb{x}}-{\mathbb{y}}|},\ \mathcal{X}(\gamma,\delta):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Gamma}\mathrm{d}{\mathbb{x}}\int_{\Gamma}\mathrm{d}{\mathbb{y}}{\overline{\gamma(x,y)}\delta(x,y)\over|{\mathbb{x}}-{\mathbb{y}}|}.

The associated quadratic forms
{\mathcal{D}}[\rho]
and
\mathcal{X}[\gamma]
are
known as classical interaction energy of the charge distribution

\rho
and the exchange energy of the reduced one-particle density
matrix
\gamma
. Note that we write
\gamma(x,y)
for the integral
kernel of
\gamma
and

\rho_{\gamma}({\mathbb{x}}):=\sum_{\sigma=1}^{4}\lambda_{n}|\xi_{n}({\mathbb{x}},\sigma)|^{2}

where the
\xi_{n}

\gamma

with associated eigenvalues
\lambda_{n}
, i.e.,

(1.5)

\gamma=\sum_{n}\lambda_{n}|\xi_{n}\rangle\langle\xi_{n}|.

In the language of quantum chemistry the
\xi_{n}

\lambda_{n}
occupation numbers of the
\xi_{n}
.

We note that in the atomic case, i.e.,

(
1.3

At first sight

G_{V}:=\{\gamma\in G|\gamma\leq P_{V}\}

or

(1.7)

\ G_{V,N}:=\{\gamma\in G_{V}|{\rm tr\,}\gamma\leq N\}

(1)

(D_{c,V}+\mathrm{i})^{-1}-(D_{c,0}+\mathrm{i})^{-1}\in\mathfrak{S}^{\infty}(\mathfrak{H})

(2)

The interval

is in the resolvent set of

\epsilon\in[0,1]

(3)

\mathfrak{Q}_{V}:=P_{V}\mathfrak{Q}\subset\mathfrak{Q}
.

The Euler equations of the functional

are the no-pair Hartree-Fock
equations, namely

(1.8)

[P_{V}{D^{\mathrm{HF}}_{\gamma}}P_{V},\gamma]=0,\ 0\leq\gamma\leq P_{V},\ {\rm tr\,}\gamma=Z,\ T_{c}^{1/2}\gamma T_{c}^{1/2}\in\mathfrak{S}^{1}(\mathfrak{H})

(1.9)

{D^{\mathrm{HF}}_{\gamma}}:=D_{c,\varphi}+\phi_{\gamma}-X_{\gamma}

\gamma\in G

\phi_{\gamma}

(1.10)

\phi_{\gamma}:=\rho_{\gamma}*|\cdot|^{-1}\ \mathrm{and}\ X_{\gamma}(x,y):={\gamma(x,y)\over|{\mathbb{x}}-{\mathbb{y}}|}.

We note in passing that for
N\in\mathbb{N}
, the minimizer can be picked as

, by a minor modification of

A solution of the non-uniqueness problem induced by

(1.11)

[{D^{\mathrm{HF}}_{\gamma}},\gamma]=0

\gamma^{2}=\gamma\leq\chi_{(-c^{2},\infty)}({D^{\mathrm{HF}}_{\gamma}})

2.
Estimates on exchange terms in relativistic Hartree-Fock theory

We start with a well known observation (see, e.g., Lieb and Yau

[
33
, Eq. (7.4)]
):

Lemma 1
.

We have the pointwise bound

(2.1)

\sum_{\sigma,\tau=1}^{4}|\gamma(x,y)|\leq\sqrt{\rho_{\gamma}({\mathbb{x}})}\sqrt{\rho_{\gamma}({\mathbb{y}})}.

Proof.

The claim follows immediately from the eigenfunction expansion
(
1.5
) of
\gamma
using the Schwarz inequality in
the summation index
n
.
∎

Next, we address the relative form compactness of

W_{\gamma}
.

Lemma 2
.

Suppose
\gamma\in G
. Then

(2.2)

\sqrt{\phi_{\gamma}}({\mathbb{p}}^{2}+1)^{-\frac{1}{4}}\in\mathfrak{S}^{8}(\mathfrak{H}).

In particular,
\phi_{\gamma}
is relatively form compact with respect to
({\mathbb{p}}^{2}+1)^{1/2}
.

Proof.

We recall Hoffmann-Ostenhofs’ inequality for
|{\mathbb{p}}|

[
31
, Theorem 7.13]

(2.3)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}||{\mathbb{p}}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{\rho_{\gamma}}(x)|^{2}\leq{\rm tr\,}(|{\mathbb{p}}|\gamma)

Thus, using first the Sobolev inequality and then (
2.3
) we get

(2.4)

\left(\int\rho_{\gamma}^{\frac{3}{2}}\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}\leq C(\sqrt{\rho_{\gamma}},|{\mathbb{p}}|\sqrt{\rho_{\gamma}})\leq{\rm tr\,}(|{\mathbb{p}}|\gamma)<\infty.

Therefore
\rho_{\gamma}\in L^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
if
\gamma\in G
.

From the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality, it follows (see Lieb and
Loss
[
31
, Section 4.3, Eq. (9)]
)

(2.5)

\|\phi_{\gamma}\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq C\|\rho_{\gamma}\|_{p}

for

\frac{1}{p}=\frac{2}{3}+\frac{1}{r},\ p\in(1,\tfrac{3}{2}).

Thus
\phi_{\gamma}\in L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
for all
r\in(3,\infty)
. Using
Seiler’s and Simon’s inequality
[
45
]
we get

\sqrt{\phi_{\gamma}(x)}({\mathbb{p}}^{2}+1)^{-\frac{1}{4}}\in\mathfrak{S}^{8}(\mathfrak{H})
and thus
relative compactness.
∎

Next we show that the exchange operator is a Hilbert-Schmidt operator
and estimate its norm:

Lemma 3
.

Assume
\gamma\in G
. Then
X_{\gamma}\in\mathfrak{S}^{2}(\mathfrak{H})
and

(2.6)

\|X_{\gamma}\|_{2}\lesssim{\rm tr\,}(|{\mathbb{p}}|\gamma).

Proof.

Pick
\xi\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}:C^{4})
. Then, using the
Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality followed by the Sobolev inequality we get

(2.7)

\int_{\Gamma}\mathrm{d}x\int_{\Gamma}\mathrm{d}y\left|{\xi(x)^{*}\xi(y)\over|{\mathbb{x}}-{\mathbb{y}}|}\right|^{2}\lesssim\||\xi|^{2}\|_{\frac{3}{2}}^{2}\lesssim(\xi,|{\mathbb{p}}|\xi)^{2}.

Taking the root, replacing
\xi
by
\xi_{n}
, multiplying by

\lambda_{n}
and summing over
n
we get by the eigenfunction expansion
(
1.5
) of
\gamma

(2.8)

\|X_{\gamma}\|_{2}\lesssim\sum_{n}\lambda_{n}(\xi_{n},|{\mathbb{p}}|\xi_{n})\lesssim{\rm tr\,}(|{\mathbb{p}}|\gamma)<\infty.

Thus
X_{\gamma}
is a Hilbert-Schmidt operator and therefore trivially
relatively form compact with respect to
|D_{c}|
.
∎

Next we turn to some quantitative operator bounds. The minimum of the
no-pair Hartree-Fock functional is supposed to approximate the atomic
ground-state energy which – as long as
N\geq CZ
and
Z/c<1

– is expected to be
O(Z^{\frac{7}{3}})
, the same as the Thomas-Fermi
energy, and also the energy of the test functions proving the upper
bound of the Scott correction in Frank
et al.

[
16
,
17
]
and

[
19
]
. Thus, it is reasonable to consider only
those
\gamma\in G_{Z}
that fulfill

(2.9)

{\rm tr\,}\left(T_{c}\gamma\right)=\mathcal{O}(Z^{\frac{7}{3}}).

Lemma 4
.

Assume
\gamma\in G^{N}
with
N=O(Z)
and

{\rm tr\,}(T_{c}\gamma)=O(Z^{\frac{7}{3}})
. Then

\|X_{\gamma}\|,\|\phi_{\gamma}\|=O(Z^{\frac{5}{3}})
.

Proof.

By (operator) positivity of the Coulomb kernel we have for all

f\in\mathfrak{H}
using Schwarz again

(2.10)

0\leq(f,X_{\gamma}f)\leq\int_{\Gamma}\mathrm{d}x|f(x)|^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathrm{d}{\mathbb{y}}{\rho_{\gamma}({\mathbb{y}})\over|{\mathbb{x}}-{\mathbb{y}}|}=(f,\phi_{\gamma}f).

Thus it suffices to show the bound for
\phi_{\gamma}
.

Now, for every
{\mathbb{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}

\displaystyle\phi_{\gamma}({\mathbb{x}})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n}{\lambda_{n}\over dc}\int_{\Gamma}\mathrm{d}{\mathbb{y}}{|\xi_{n}(y)|^{2}\over|{\mathbb{x}}-{\mathbb{y}}|}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n}{\lambda_{n}\over dc}(\xi_{n},(|D_{c}|-c^{2}+{\pi^{2}\over 4}d^{2}c^{2})\xi_{n})

(2.11)

\displaystyle\leq{1\over dc}{\rm tr\,}\left((|D_{c}|-c^{2}+\tfrac{\pi^{2}}{8}d^{2}c^{2})\gamma\right)\lesssim Z^{\frac{5}{3}}

where we use
\sqrt{1-t}\leq 1-t/2
for
t\leq 1
and the translational invariance of the differential operator and Herbst’s massive version of Kato’s inequality
[
20
, Eq. (2.1)]

(2.12)

{d\over|\cdot|}\leq\sqrt{{\mathbb{p}}^{2}+1}-\sqrt{1-\left(\tfrac{\pi}{2}d\right)^{2}}

for all
d\in(0,2/\pi)
. We use it after rescaling
x\to cx
,

p\to\bf{p}/c
and setting
d:=c^{-\frac{1}{3}}
. (In passing we note
that the bound can be improved for
d
close to
2/\pi
: it stays
uniformly positive (see Raynal
et al.

[
43
]
and Tix
[
57
,
58
]
). However, this is not needed here.)
∎

Lemmata
2
,
3
, and
4
have an immediate
consequence:

Corollary 5
.

Suppose
\gamma\in G_{Z}
and
{\rm tr\,}(T_{c}\gamma)=O(Z^{\frac{7}{3}})
. Then the
total Hartree-Fock mean-field potential

W_{\gamma}:=\phi_{\gamma}-X_{\gamma}\in{\mathcal{W}}_{\frac{1}{3}}
is relatively form
compact with respect to
\sqrt{{\mathbb{p}}^{2}+1}
and

\|W_{\gamma}\|=O(Z^{\frac{5}{3}})
.

To further discuss the exchange energy the following bound of the
massless kinetic energy in terms of the massive one is useful.

Lemma 6
.

For
\lambda\in(0,1)
and
c\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
we have

(2.13)

|{\mathbb{p}}|\leq{1\over c\lambda}\left(\sqrt{c^{2}|{\mathbb{p}}|^{2}+c^{4}}-c^{2}+c^{2}\lambda^{2}\right).

Proof.

It suffices to show (
2.13
) for
c=1
, since the general case
is obtained by scaling
{\mathbb{p}}\to c{\mathbb{p}}
.

We compute the infimum of quotient of the right and left side:

(2.14)

f_{\lambda}(t):={\sqrt{t^{2}+1}-\sqrt{1-\lambda^{2}}\over t}

which is assumed at
t_{m}=\lambda/\sqrt{1-\lambda^{2}}
with

f_{\lambda}(t_{m})=\lambda
which shows

(2.15)

|{\mathbb{p}}|\leq\lambda^{-1}(\sqrt{|{\mathbb{p}}|^{2}+1}-\sqrt{1-\lambda^{2}})\leq\lambda^{-1}(\sqrt{|{\mathbb{p}}|^{2}+1}-1+\lambda^{2})

where we use
\lambda\in(0,1)
and therefore
1-\lambda\in(0,1)
.
∎

Eventually we mention that the exchange energy of the Hartree-Fock
minimizer is of lower order:

Lemma 7
.

Assume
\gamma\in\mathfrak{S}^{1}(\mathfrak{H})
with
{\rm tr\,}\gamma=O(Z)
,

\gamma^{2}=\gamma
, and
{\rm tr\,}(T_{c}\gamma)=O(Z^{\frac{7}{3}})
. Then

(2.16)

\mathcal{X}[\gamma]=O(Z^{\frac{5}{3}}).

Proof.

By the correlation inequality of Mancas
et al.

[
34
, Theorem
1]
(setting
N=2
) and by the estimate

(2.17)

\int_{|{\mathbb{x}}-{\mathbb{y}}|<R({\mathbb{x}})}\mathrm{d}{\mathbb{y}}{\sigma({\mathbb{y}})\over|{\mathbb{x}}-{\mathbb{y}}|}\geq{\sqrt[3]{9\pi}\over 2}(M\sigma)({\mathbb{x}})^{\frac{1}{3}}\ \mathrm{with}\ \int_{|{\mathbb{x}}-{\mathbb{y}}|<R({\mathbb{x}})}\mathrm{d}{\mathbb{y}}\;\sigma({\mathbb{y}})=R({\mathbb{y}})

(see
[
34
, Lemma 2]
) on the exchange hole we have

(2.18)

{1\over|{\mathbb{x}}-{\mathbb{y}}|}\geq\sigma*|\cdot|^{-1}({\mathbb{x}})+\sigma*|\cdot|^{-1}({\mathbb{y}})-{\sqrt[3]{9\pi}\over 2}(M\sigma)({\mathbb{x}})^{\frac{1}{3}}-{\sqrt[3]{9\pi}\over 2}(M\sigma)({\mathbb{y}})^{\frac{1}{3}}-D[\sigma]

where
\sigma
is any non-negative function with

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sigma>1/2
and
D[\sigma]<\infty
and
M(\sigma)
is
the maximal function of
\sigma
. We integrate the inequality
against
(\rho_{\gamma}({\mathbb{x}})\rho_{\gamma}({\mathbb{y}})-|\gamma(x,y)|^{2})/2
and get

(2.19)

D[\rho_{\gamma}]-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Gamma\times\Gamma}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y{|\gamma(x,y)|^{2}\over|{\mathbb{x}}-{\mathbb{y}}|}\\
\geq 2D(\rho_{\gamma},\sigma)N-2D(\rho_{\gamma},\sigma)-\sqrt[3]{9\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathrm{d}{\mathbb{x}}(M\sigma)(x)^{\frac{1}{3}}\rho({\mathbb{x}})-\frac{1}{2}D[\sigma](N^{2}-N).

Picking

\sigma:={2+\sqrt{2}\over N}\rho_{\gamma}

yields

(2.20)

\int_{\Gamma}\mathrm{d}x\int_{\Gamma}\mathrm{d}y{|\gamma(x,y)|^{2}\over|{\mathbb{x}}-{\mathbb{y}}|}\lesssim{D[\rho_{\gamma}]\over N}+\|M\rho_{\gamma}\|^{\frac{1}{4}}_{\frac{4}{3}}\|\rho\|_{\frac{4}{3}}\\
\lesssim{{\rm tr\,}(|{\mathbb{p}}|\gamma)N\over N}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\rho_{\gamma}^{\frac{4}{3}}\lesssim{\rm tr\,}(|{\mathbb{p}}|\gamma)\leq{{\rm tr\,}T_{c}\gamma\over\lambda c}+\lambda cN=O(Z^{\frac{5}{3}})

where we use Kato’s inequality, the maximal inequality, Daubechies’
inequality
[
6
, Inequality (3.4)]
, and (
2.13
)
with
\lambda=Z^{-\frac{1}{3}}
.
∎

Using the same argument as in the last line of (
2.20
) on
(
2.8
) yields

Corollary 8
.

Assume
\gamma\in G
with
{\rm tr\,}(T_{c}\gamma)={\mathcal{O}}(Z^{\frac{7}{3}})
. Then

(2.21)

\mathcal{X}[\gamma]={\mathcal{O}}(Z^{\frac{5}{3}}).

3.
Estimate on exchange energy in relativistic Müller theory

We will consider the Müller functional. The basic inequality is the
bound on the ground state energy of of an hydrogen atom by Tix

[
58
, Eq. (2.1)]
. Inserting it in the Ritz variational
principle gives the inequality

(3.1)

{1\over c\kappa_{k}}T_{c}+c\geq{1\over|{\mathbb{x}}|}

in
\mathfrak{H}_{0}
with
\kappa_{k}:=2/(\pi/2+2/\pi)
.
Next we,
seemingly unmotivated, introduce the no-pair operator of two
gravitating particles in the free picture. Set

(3.2)

\begin{split}\mathcal{C}_{\varphi,\lambda_{0}}[\psi]&:\mathfrak{Q}_{0}\otimes\mathfrak{Q}_{0}\to\mathbb{R}\\
\psi&\mapsto\tfrac{1}{2}\langle\psi|[(T_{c}-\varphi)\otimes 1+1\otimes(T_{c}-\varphi)-|{\mathbb{x}}-{\mathbb{y}}|^{-1}-\lambda_{0}]\psi\rangle\end{split}

with
\lambda_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
. The form
\mathcal{C}_{\varphi,\lambda_{0}}
is
bounded from below for
0\leq Z+1/2\leq c\kappa_{k}
defining the
operator
C_{{}_{\varphi},\lambda_{0}}
according to Friedrichs. (See the first step of
Theorem
11
for more details on the boundedness). Moreover,

(3.3)

\sigma_{\mathrm{ess}}(C_{\varphi,\lambda_{0}})=[0,\infty)

if we pick

(3.4)

\lambda_{0}:=\inf\sigma(C_{0,0}),

and assume
Z\geq 1/2
. This is a special case of Morozov’s HVZ theorem

[
38
, Theorem 3.1.1]
or
[
37
, Theorem
6]
. (A slightly more restricted version of the HVZ
theorem not covering the case of attracting particles was already
given by Jakubaßa-Amundsen
[
26
]
.) We will
fix this choice (
3.4
) of
\lambda_{0}
and write simply
C_{\varphi}

instead of
C_{\varphi,\lambda_{0}}
.

The relativistic Müller functional is the same as the Hartree-Fock
functional except
\gamma
is replaced by the operator square root

\gamma^{\frac{1}{2}}
in the exchange term and the renormalizing term

-\lambda_{0}{\rm tr\,}\gamma
is added, i.e., in the molecular case it reads

(3.5)

\begin{split}{\mathcal{E}}^{\mathrm{M}}_{c,\varphi}:&G\to\mathbb{R}\\
\gamma\mapsto&{\rm tr\,}\left[(D_{c,\varphi}-\lambda_{0})\gamma\right]+{\mathcal{D}}[\rho_{\gamma}]-\mathcal{X}[\gamma^{\frac{1}{2}}]+\sum_{1\leq k<l\leq K}{Z_{k}Z_{l}\over|{\mathbb{R}}_{k}-{\mathbb{R}}_{l}|}\end{split}

restricted to
G_{\varphi}
. The renormalizing term makes the infimum
of the Müller functional for
\varphi=0
equal to zero. This
renormalization of the energy is not needed for the Hartree-Fock
functional but is similar to the non-relativistic setting (Frank et
al.
[
15
]
). The same phenomena happens already for the
simple Thomas-Fermi-Dirac functional (Lieb
[
29
, Section
VI.a]
).

We have the following estimate on the Müller exchange energy in
terms of the relativistic kinetic energy:

Theorem 9
.

Assume
\gamma\in G
. Then for all
d\in(0,2/\pi)
and
c>0

(3.6)

\mathcal{X}[\gamma^{\frac{1}{2}}]={1\over dc}{\rm tr\,}\left[\left(T_{c}+{\pi^{2}\over 4}d^{2}c^{2}\right)\gamma\right].

Furthermore, if
{\rm tr\,}(T_{c}\gamma)={\mathcal{O}}(Z^{\frac{7}{3}})
, then

(3.7)

\mathcal{X}[\gamma^{\frac{1}{2}}]=O(Z^{\frac{5}{3}}).

Proof.

For fixed
{\mathbb{y}}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
we set
g_{y}(x):=\gamma^{\frac{1}{2}}(x+y,y)
. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}[\gamma^{\frac{1}{2}}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Gamma}\mathrm{d}y\int_{\Gamma}\mathrm{d}x{|g_{y}(x)|^{2}\over|{\mathbb{x}}|}

(3.8)

\displaystyle\leq{1\over 2dc}\int_{\Gamma}\mathrm{d}y(g_{y},\left(T_{c}+\left({\pi^{2}d^{2}c^{2}\over 4}\right)g_{y}\right)={1\over dc}{\rm tr\,}\left[\left(T_{c}+{\pi^{2}d^{2}c^{2}\over 4}\right)\gamma\right]

where use again Herbst’s massive Kato inequality similar to the
second estimate in (
2.11
). This proves the first claim.

Picking
d:=Z^{-\frac{1}{3}}
shows the second claim.
∎

Theorem 10
.

Assume
\gamma\in G_{0}
. Then

(3.9)

\mathcal{X}[\gamma^{\frac{1}{2}}]=\frac{1}{2}{\rm tr\,}\left[\left({1\over c\kappa_{K}}T_{c}+c\right)\gamma\right].

Proof.

We start as in the proof of Theorem
9
: For fixed

{\mathbb{y}}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
we set
g_{y}(x):=\gamma^{\frac{1}{2}}(x+y,y)
. (Note that
although
\mathfrak{H}_{0}
is not invariant under dilations it is
invariant under translations.) Then by (
3.1
) dividing by

2
and using the second assumption in the last step, we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}[\gamma^{\frac{1}{2}}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Gamma}\mathrm{d}y\int_{\Gamma}\mathrm{d}x{|g_{y}(x)|^{2}\over|{\mathbb{x}}|}

(3.10)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Gamma}\mathrm{d}y\left(g_{y},\left({1\over\kappa_{k}c}T_{c}+c\right)g_{y}\right)=\frac{1}{2}{\rm tr\,}\left[\left({1\over c\kappa_{k}}T_{c}+c\right)\gamma\right].

This ends the proof.
∎

4.
Existence of a groundstate of the atomic Müller functional in the free picture

The existence of a ground-state for the Chandrasekhar kinetic energy
was proven by
[
56
]
and also addressed by Chen

[
4
]
. We will follow the strategy of
[
56
]

but will extend it to the free picture in the diction of Sucher and to
the molecular case.

Since
\gamma\in\mathfrak{S}^{1}(\mathfrak{H}_{0})
implies that

\gamma^{\frac{1}{2}}\in\mathfrak{S}^{2}(\mathfrak{H}_{0})
its integral kernel

u(x,y):=\gamma^{\frac{1}{2}}(x,y)
is in
\mathfrak{Q}_{0}\otimes\mathfrak{Q}_{0}
. This
allows to recast the Müller functional as

(4.1)

{\mathcal{E}}^{\rm M}_{c,\varphi}(\gamma)=\frac{1}{2}\left(u,C_{\varphi}-\lambda_{0})u\right)_{\mathfrak{H}_{0}\otimes\mathfrak{H}_{0}}+{\mathcal{D}}[\rho_{\gamma}]+R.

Theorem 11
.

Let
(Z+\tfrac{1}{2})<\kappa_{k}c
and
Z\geq 1/2
. Then

{\mathcal{E}}^{\mathrm{M}}_{c,\varphi}
has a minimizer
\gamma_{*}\in G_{0,N}
.

Proof.

We split the proof into the following steps.

Step 1. Some properties of minimizing sequences.
To study
minimizing sequences, we first bound the Müller functional from
below.

Note that for any
\gamma\in G_{\varphi,N}
,

D_{c,0}\gamma=T_{c}\gamma
. Since
{\mathcal{D}}
is a positive quadratic form
(Onsager’s lemma), we know that

(4.2)

\begin{split}{\mathcal{E}}^{\rm M}_{c,\varphi}(\gamma)&\geq{\rm tr\,}(T_{c}\gamma)-\left(\frac{\kappa}{\kappa_{c}}+\frac{1}{2\kappa_{k}c}\right){\rm tr\,}(T_{c}\gamma)-[\lambda_{0}+c(Z+\tfrac{1}{2})]{\rm tr\,}\gamma\\
&=\left[1-{1\over\kappa_{k}c}\left(Z+\tfrac{1}{2}\right)\right]{\rm tr\,}T_{c}\gamma-(\lambda_{0}+cZ+\tfrac{1}{2})N.\end{split}

As
0\in G_{0,N}
, we know
E_{c,\varphi}^{\rm M}(G_{0,N})\leq 0
. Thus, there exists a minimizing sequence
\gamma_{n}
in
G_{0,N}

such that
{\mathcal{E}}^{\rm M}_{c,\varphi}[\gamma_{n}]\leq 0
. From
(
4.2
), we infer

(4.3)

{\rm tr\,}(T_{c}\gamma_{n})\leq{(\lambda_{0}+cZ+\tfrac{1}{2})N\over 1-{Z+\frac{1}{2}\over\kappa_{k}c}}.

This implies that
T_{c}^{\frac{1}{2}}\gamma_{n}T_{c}^{\frac{1}{2}}
is uniformly
bounded in
\mathfrak{S}^{1}(\mathfrak{H}_{0})
. Thus, up to subsequences, there
exists a density matrix
\gamma_{*}
such that

T_{c}^{\frac{1}{2}}\gamma_{*}T_{c}^{\frac{1}{2}}\in\mathfrak{S}^{1}(\mathfrak{H}_{0})
and

(4.4)

\displaystyle T_{c}^{\frac{1}{2}}\gamma_{n}T_{c}^{\frac{1}{2}}\rightharpoonup T_{c}^{\frac{1}{2}}\gamma_{*}T_{c}^{\frac{1}{2}}\qquad\textrm{in }\mathfrak{S}^{1}(\mathfrak{H}_{0}).

Obviously
\gamma*
is a candidate for a minimizer. To show that this
is true, we are going to use (
4.1
) and the kernel of the
operator
\gamma_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Next, we are going to study the properties of

\gamma_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Since

(4.5)

\|T_{c}^{\frac{1}{2}}\gamma_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|^{2}_{\mathfrak{S}^{2}(\mathfrak{H}_{0})}={\rm tr\,}(T_{c}^{\frac{1}{2}}\gamma_{n}T_{c}^{\frac{1}{2}})={\rm tr\,}(T_{c}\gamma_{n})

the sequence
T_{c}^{\frac{1}{2}}\gamma_{N}^{\frac{1}{2}}
is bounded in

\mathfrak{S}^{2}(\mathfrak{H}_{0})
and therefore – possibly extracting a subsequence
which in abuse of notation is written again as
\gamma_{n}
– is also
weakly convergent to some
T_{c}^{\frac{1}{2}}\gamma_{**}T_{c}^{\frac{1}{2}}
for
some
\gamma_{**}
which, however, again by (
4.5
), equals

\gamma_{*}
since
T_{c}>0
, i.e.,

(4.6)

T_{c}^{\frac{1}{2}}\gamma_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}\rightharpoonup T_{c}^{\frac{1}{2}}\gamma_{*}^{\frac{1}{2}}\qquad\textrm{in }\mathfrak{S}^{2}(\mathfrak{H}_{0}).

We write
u_{n}
and
u_{*}
, both in
\mathfrak{H}_{0}\otimes\mathfrak{H}_{0}
, for the kernels of
\gamma_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}
and

\gamma_{*}^{\frac{1}{2}}
respectively. Then

(T_{c}^{\frac{1}{2}}\otimes 1)u_{n}
and
(T_{c}^{\frac{1}{2}}\otimes 1)u_{*}
are the integral kernels of
T_{c}^{\frac{1}{2}}\gamma_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}
and
T_{c}^{\frac{1}{2}}\gamma_{*}^{\frac{1}{2}}
respectively, and

(4.7)

\|(T_{c}^{\frac{1}{2}}\otimes 1)u_{n}\|_{\mathfrak{H}_{0}\otimes\mathfrak{H}_{0}}=\|T_{c}^{\frac{1}{2}}\gamma_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{2}(\mathfrak{H}_{0})}<\infty.

For any operator
G\in\mathfrak{S}^{2}(\mathfrak{H}_{0})
the associated
integra. kernel
g
is in
\mathfrak{H}_{0}\otimes\mathfrak{H}_{0}
and we have

(4.8)

\left((T_{c}^{\frac{1}{2}}\otimes 1)u_{n},g\right)={\rm tr\,}[(T_{c}^{\frac{1}{2}}\otimes 1)\gamma_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}G]\to{\rm tr\,}[(T_{c}^{\frac{1}{2}}\otimes 1)\gamma_{*}^{\frac{1}{2}}G]=\left((T_{c}^{\frac{1}{2}}\otimes 1)u_{*},g\right).

This implies

(4.9)

(T_{c}^{\frac{1}{2}}\otimes 1)u_{n}\rightharpoonup(T_{c}^{\frac{1}{2}}\otimes 1)u_{*}\qquad\textrm{in }\mathfrak{H}_{0}\otimes\mathfrak{H}_{0}.

Next we note that
{\mathcal{D}}[\rho_{\gamma_{n}}]
is bounded. Since
{\mathcal{D}}
is a
scalar product there is a
\gamma_{***}
such that – possibly by
extracting again a subsequence –

(4.10)

D(\sigma,\rho_{\gamma_{n}})\to D(\sigma,\rho_{***})

for all
\sigma
with
D[\sigma]<\infty
. That
\gamma_{***}=\gamma_{*}

follows from

D(\sigma,\rho_{\gamma_{*}})=C{\rm tr\,}(|{\mathbb{p}}|^{-2}(\sigma)\circ\gamma_{*})
.

Step 2:
G_{\varphi,N}
is weakly closed.
From
(
4.4
), it is easy to see that

\displaystyle{\rm tr\,}\gamma_{*}=\|\gamma_{*}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{1}(\mathfrak{H}_{0})}\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}{\rm tr\,}\gamma_{n}\leq N.

On the other hand, for any function
f\in\mathfrak{H}_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle\left(f,\gamma_{*}f\right)=\lim_{n\to\infty}(f,\gamma_{n}f).

This and
0\leq\gamma_{n}\leq P_{0}
for any
n\geq 1
show that

0\leq\gamma_{*}\leq P_{0}
. Thus

\displaystyle\gamma_{*}\in G_{0,N}.

Step 3. Passing to the limit in the energy.
Now, we are
going to show that for the minimizing sequence
\gamma_{n}
constructed
above, we have

(4.11)

\inf E_{c,\varphi}^{\rm M}(G_{0,N})=\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathcal{E}}^{\rm M}_{c,\varphi}(\gamma_{n})\geq{\mathcal{E}}^{\rm M}_{c,\varphi}(\gamma_{*})

which would prove the theorem. To this end we will make use of the
recasting (
4.1
) of the Müller functional treating it term by
term.

Step 3.1. Passing the limit for the term

\left(u_{n},C_{\varphi}u_{n}\right)_{\mathfrak{H}_{0}\otimes\mathfrak{H}_{0}}
.
We split

C_{\varphi}
into its positive and negative parts
C^{+}\geq 0
and

C^{-}\leq 0
, i.e.,

(4.12)

C_{\varphi}=C^{+}+C^{-}

Since
(u,C^{+}u)
is a nonnegative quadratic form the Schwarz inequality implies

(4.13)

\lim\inf_{n\to\infty}(u_{n},C^{+}u_{n})\geq(u_{*},C^{+}u_{*}).

By the HVZ theorem
C^{-}\in\mathfrak{S}^{\infty}(\mathfrak{H}_{0})
, which turns the weak convergence into strong convergence, i.e.,

(4.14)

\liminf_{n\to\infty}(u_{n},C^{-}u_{n})=(u_{*},C^{-}u_{*}).

Combining (
4.13
) and (
4.14
) yields the wanted inequality for the
first term:

(4.15)

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\left(u_{n},C_{\varphi}u_{n}\right)_{P_{0}\mathfrak{H}_{0}\otimes P_{0}\mathfrak{H}_{0}}\geq\left(u_{*},C_{\varphi}u_{*}\right)_{\mathfrak{H}_{0}\otimes\mathfrak{H}_{0}}.

Step 3.2. Passing to the limit for the term

{\mathcal{D}}(\rho_{\gamma_{n}})
.
We finally consider the term

{\mathcal{D}}(\rho_{\gamma_{n}})
. Since it is a scalar product, the Schwarz
inequality implies

\liminf_{n\to\infty}D[\rho_{n}]\geq D[\rho_{\gamma_{*}}]
.

Step 4. Conclusion.

Since
R
does not depend on
\gamma
, we merely have to sum the
inequalities of the the second and third step to obtain the wanted
result.
∎

We conclude this section by remarking that we expect the

{\rm tr\,}\gamma_{*}=N
, if
N\leq Z
and possibly beyond although we have to
leave this question open.

5.
Stability of the second kind for the relativistic Müller functional

Theorem
9
allows us to show stability of the
second kind, also known as stability of matter, for the Müller
functional in the free picture (
W=\varphi
):

Theorem 12
.

Assume that
0<Z_{1}/c,...,Z_{K}/c\leq\kappa<2/\pi
,

{\mathbb{R}}_{1},...,{\mathbb{R}}_{K}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
pairwise different, and

\gamma\in G_{\varphi,N}
with
N\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

(5.1)

{\mathcal{E}}^{\mathrm{M}}_{c,\varphi}[\gamma]\geq-\left(c^{2}+{1\over 2-\kappa\pi}-\lambda_{0}\right)N.

Proof.

We start by reminding of the inequality

(5.2)

\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left(c|{\mathbb{p}}_{n}|-\sum_{k=1}^{K}{Z_{k}\over|{\mathbb{x}}_{n}-{\mathbb{R}}_{k}|}\right)+\sum_{1\leq m<n\leq N}{1\over|{\mathbb{x}}_{m}-{\mathbb{x}}_{n}|}+\sum_{1\leq k<l\leq K}{Z_{k}Z_{l}\over|{\mathbb{R}}_{k}-{\mathbb{R}}_{l}|}\geq 0

for
0\leq Z_{1},...,Z_{K}\leq\frac{2}{\pi}c
on
\mathfrak{Q}^{N}
(Lieb and Yau

[
33
]
).

Thus rescaling (
5.2
) by
{\mathbb{x}}\to\frac{\kappa\pi}{2}{\mathbb{x}}
with a
Slater determinant with orbitals
\xi_{1},...,\xi_{n}
we obtain for

\gamma=\sum_{n=1}^{N}|\xi_{n}\rangle\langle\xi_{n}|
and therefore
according to Lieb
[
30
,
28
]
or Bach
[
1
]
–
adapted to the relativistic case – for general

\gamma\in G_{0,N}
that

(5.3)

{\rm tr\,}[(\frac{\kappa\pi}{2}c|{\mathbb{p}}|-\varphi({\mathbb{x}}))\gamma)+{\mathcal{D}}[\rho_{\gamma}]-\mathcal{X}[\gamma]+\sum_{1\leq k<l\leq K}{Z_{k}Z_{l}\over|{\mathbb{R}}_{k}-{\mathbb{R}}_{l}|}\geq 0.

Using (
5.3
) and (
3.8
) we get

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}_{c,\varphi}^{\mathrm{M}}[\gamma]

\displaystyle={\mathcal{E}}_{c,\varphi}^{\mathrm{HF}}[\gamma]+\mathcal{X}[\gamma]-\mathcal{X}[\gamma^{\frac{1}{2}}]-\lambda_{0}{\rm tr\,}\gamma

\displaystyle\geq(1-\frac{\kappa\pi}{2\lambda}-\frac{1}{2dc}){\rm tr\,}(T_{c}\gamma)-(c^{2}\lambda+cd+\lambda_{0}){\rm tr\,}\gamma

\displaystyle=-\left(c^{2}+{1\over 2-\kappa\pi}-\lambda_{0}\right){\rm tr\,}\gamma

where we picked
\lambda=1
and

(5.4)

d:={1\over 2c\left(1-{\kappa\pi\over 2}\right)}

to obtain the last inequality.
∎

Abstract
. We consider Hardy operators, i.e., homogeneous Schrödinger operators consisting of the ordinary or fractional Laplacian in a half-space plus a potential, which only depends on the appropriate power of the distance to the boundary of the half-space. We compare the scales of homogeneous L p L^{p} -Sobolev spaces generated by these Hardy operators with and without potential with each other. To that end, we prove and use new square function estimates for operators with slowly decaying heat kernels. Our results hold for all admissible coupling constants in the local case and for repulsive potentials in the fractional case, and extend those obtained recently in L 2 L^{2} . They also cover attractive potentials in the fractional case, once expected heat kernel estimates are available.

1.1.
Statement of the problem and main result

For
d\in\mathbb{N}=\{1,2,...\}
, we consider the following generalized Schrödinger operators in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
,

(1)

L_{\lambda}^{(\alpha)}:=(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}+\lambda x_{d}^{-\alpha},

where
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}:=\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
\mathbb{R}_{+}:=(0,\infty)
,
x=(x^{\prime},x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
with
x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
and
x_{d}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
\alpha\in(0,2]
,

(-\Delta)_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}}
is the Laplacian with a Dirichlet boundary condition on
(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d})^{c}
,
and, when
\alpha\in(0,2)
,
(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}
denotes the regional fractional Laplacian, introduced by Bogdan, Budzy, and Chen
[
BBC03
]
: It is the self-adjoint Friedrichs extension of the quadratic form

(2)

\displaystyle\langle u,(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}}u\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d})}\!=\!\frac{\alpha}{2^{2-\alpha}\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}}\ \frac{\Gamma(\frac{d+\alpha}{2})}{\Gamma(1-\frac{\alpha}{2})}\iint_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{d+\alpha}}\,dx\,dy,\ u\in C_{c}^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d})

and its pointwise action is given by

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}}u(x)=\frac{\alpha}{2^{2-\alpha}\pi^{d/2}}\ \frac{\Gamma(\frac{d+\alpha}{2})}{\Gamma(1-\frac{\alpha}{2})}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{d+\alpha}}\,dy,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}.

By the decomposition
(-\Delta)_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}}=(-\Delta)_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}+(-\Delta)_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}
and the classical Hardy inequality
[
Har19
,
Har20
,
OK90
,
KMP06
]
,
L_{\lambda}^{(2)}
is bounded from below if and only if
\lambda\geq-1/4
. Moreover, if
\lambda\geq-1/4
, then
L_{\lambda}^{(2)}
is bounded from below by zero. Bogdan and Dyda
[
BD11
]
showed the corresponding Hardy inequality for
\alpha\in(0,2)
. That is,

(3)

\displaystyle L_{\lambda}^{(\alpha)}\geq 0

if and only if
\lambda\geq\lambda_{*}
with

\lambda_{*}(\alpha):=-\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1+\alpha}{2})}{\pi}\Big(\Gamma(\frac{1+\alpha}{2})-\frac{2^{\alpha-1}\sqrt{\pi}}{\Gamma(1-\frac{\alpha}{2})}\Big).

Since
\lambda_{*}(2)=-1/4
and we regard
\alpha\in(0,2]
as fixed, we write
\lambda_{*}
instead of
\lambda_{*}(\alpha)
and
L_{\lambda}
instead of
L_{\lambda}^{(\alpha)}
from now on. Throughout the paper, we assume
\lambda\geq\lambda_{*}
, and we will call
L_{\lambda}
a Hardy operator.

The homogeneity of
L_{\lambda}
motivates to ask in how far
L_{\lambda}
and
L_{0}
are comparable with each other. Our main result in this paper, Theorem
1.1
below, says that the scales of Sobolev norms
\|(L_{\lambda})^{s/2}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d})}
are equivalent to the norms
\|(L_{0})^{s/2}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d})}
for
s>0
belonging to some interval depending on all parameters
d,\alpha,\lambda
.
This extends
[
FM23
]
, where Frank and the second-named author proved this equivalence of Sobolev norms for
p=2
.
To give a convenient presentation of our result, we introduce the following parameterization of the coupling constant
\lambda
of the Hardy potential.
For
\alpha\in(0,2]
, let
M:=\alpha
if
\alpha<2
and
M:=\infty
if
\alpha=2
, and

(4)

(-1,M)\ni\sigma\mapsto C(\sigma):=\frac{1}{\pi}\Big(\Gamma(\alpha)\sin\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}+\Gamma(1+\sigma)\Gamma(\alpha-\sigma)\sin\frac{\pi(2\sigma-\alpha)}{2}\Big).

As noted in
[
FM23
]
and the references therein, the function
\sigma\mapsto C(\sigma)
is continuous and symmetric with respect to
\sigma=\frac{\alpha-1}{2}
, strictly increasing on
[\frac{\alpha-1}{2},M)
and
C(\frac{\alpha-1}{2})=\lambda_{*}
. Moreover,
\lim_{\sigma\to M}C(\sigma)=+\infty
. Therefore, for any
\lambda\in[\lambda_{*},\infty)
there is a unique

(5)

\sigma\in\left[\frac{\alpha-1}{2},M\right)\ \text{with}\ \ C(\sigma)=\lambda.

In particular,
C(\alpha-1)=C(0)=0
. Hence, the case
\lambda=0
corresponds to
\sigma=\max\{0,\alpha-1\}
and the case
\lambda>0
corresponds to
\sigma>(\alpha-1)_{+}
. For
\alpha=2

.

In the following, we always write
C

We write

The following is our main result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
,
\alpha\in(0,2]
, and let
\lambda\geq 0
when
\alpha<2
and
\lambda\geq-1/4
when

\sigma
be defined by (

(1)

If
\frac{1}{1+\sigma\wedge 0}<p<\frac{1}{(\alpha s/2-\sigma)_{+}}
, then

(6)

\displaystyle\|(L_{0})^{s/2}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d})}\lesssim_{d,\alpha,\lambda,s,p}\|(L_{\lambda})^{s/2}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d})}\quad\text{for all}\ u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}).

(2)

If
\frac{1}{1+\sigma\wedge 0}<p<\frac{1}{\max\{\alpha s/2-(\alpha-1)_{+},-\sigma\}}
, then

(7)

\displaystyle\|(L_{\lambda})^{s/2}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d})}\lesssim_{d,\alpha,\lambda,s,p}\|(L_{0})^{s/2}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d})}\quad\text{for all}\ u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}).

In particular, if
\frac{1}{1+\sigma\wedge 0}<p<\min\left\{\frac{1}{\max\{\alpha s/2-(\alpha-1)_{+},-\sigma\}},\frac{1}{(\alpha s/2-\sigma)_{+}}\right\}
, then we have the equivalence

\|(L_{\lambda})^{s/2}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d})}\simeq_{d,\alpha,\lambda,s,p}\|(L_{0})^{s/2}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d})}\quad\text{for all}\ u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}).

Theorem
1.1
and a density argument (Theorem

), yield the following result for the Riesz transforms associated to

and

with

.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,2]
and let
\lambda\geq 0
when
\alpha<2
and
\lambda\geq-1/4
when

. Let
\sigma

\displaystyle\|(L_{0})^{s/2}(L_{\lambda})^{-s/2}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d})}\lesssim_{d,\alpha,\lambda,s,p}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d})}.

Compared with the case

in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d})

, understood as an operator in
L^{p}
, is. A classical way to tackle this question is to try to express
(L_{\lambda})^{s/2}
using Littlewood–Paley square functions, i.e., to prove square function estimates such as
\|(L_{\lambda})^{s/2}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d})}\simeq\|\left(\sum_{N\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}}|N^{s/2}\Psi(L_{\lambda}/N)f|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d})}
for a bump function
\Psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}([1/2,3/2]:[0,1])
, say. As in the earlier works
[
KMV+18
,
Mer21
,
BD23
]
, we will work with square functions, where the Littlewood–Paley projection
\Psi(L_{\lambda}/N)
is replaced with the well-studied heat kernel
\mathrm{e}^{-tL_{\lambda}}
. See Sections
2
–
2.2
below for estimates involving
\mathrm{e}^{-tL_{\lambda}}
that are most relevant for us.

One standard approach to prove Littlewood–Paley square function estimates relies on a Mikhlin–Hörmander spectral multiplier theorem, i.e., the
L^{p}
-boundedness of sufficiently smooth functions of
L_{\lambda}
. This approach was used in the works
[
KMV+18
,
Mer21
]
, which considered

\alpha=2
and
\lambda\geq-(d-2)^{2}/4
or

and
\lambda\geq 0
, and relies heavily on the

off-diagonal estimates with exponential decay or the Poisson upper bounds for the heat kernels. Unfortunately, neither of these two crucial ingredients is available when
\lambda<0
due to the emergence of singular weights in the sharp heat kernel bound. To address this challenge, D’Ancona and the first author
[
BD23
]
introduced the “continuous square functions” approach, which is based on the boundedness of the square function associated to the main operator
(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}+\lambda/|x|^{\alpha}
. Since the heat kernel of
(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}+\lambda/|x|^{\alpha}
lacks regularity conditions, the renowned classical Calderón-Zygmund theory can not be applied in this context. To circumvent this limitation,
[
BD23
]
employed the theory of singular integrals beyond the Calderón-Zygmund theory as outlined in
[
Aus07
, Chapter 6]

\lambda\leq 0
, and resolving the previously open case in
[

]
. Encouraged by
[

f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
there is a sequence
(\phi_{n})\subset C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
such that

L_{\lambda}^{s/2}\phi_{n}\to f\ \text{in}\ L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})\,.

Remark 1.11
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,2)
,
\lambda\in[\lambda_{*},0)
and assume that
\mathrm{e}^{-tL_{\lambda}}(x,y)
satisfies the upper bound in (
17
) with
\sigma
defined by (
5
). Then Theorem
1.10
remains valid for this value of
\lambda
. This follows by the same arguments as in the proof below, since Lemma
7.1
remains valid for this value of
\lambda
.

We can now give the

Proof of Corollary
1.2
.

It suffices to show (
8
) for a dense set of
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d})
. By Theorem
1.10
, we may choose
f=(L_{\lambda})^{s/2}g
with
g\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d})
. Then, by Theorem
1.1
,

(16)

\displaystyle\|(L_{0})^{s/2}g\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d})}\lesssim_{d,\alpha,\lambda,s,p}\|(L_{\lambda})^{s/2}g\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d})}=\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d})},

which concludes the proof of (
8
).
∎

Organization

In Section

, we recall kernel bounds for
\mathrm{e}^{-tL_{\lambda}}
and use them, together with a Phragmén–Lindelöf argument, to prove bounds for complex-time heat kernels. We use these bounds to prove new bounds for the kernels of

and prove corresponding

-estimates. We use these estimates in Section

to prove the square function estimates for

in Theorem

.
In Section

, we extend bounds for the kernel of the difference
e^{-tL_{\lambda}}-e^{-tL_{0}}
considered in
[
FM23
]
and prove bounds for the kernel of the difference
tL_{0}e^{-tL_{0}}-tL_{\lambda}e^{-tL_{\lambda}}
.
These bounds are crucial to prove the reversed Hardy inequality in Section
5
.
In Section
6
, we prove the generalized Hardy inequality (Theorem

).
Finally, in Section

in

2.
Estimates involving the heat kernel of
L_{\lambda}

In this section, we collect known pointwise estimates for the heat kernel
\mathrm{e}^{-tL_{\lambda}}
and derive novel bounds for the complex-time heat kernel and
(tL_{\lambda})^{k}\mathrm{e}^{-tL_{\lambda}}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
. In the first subsection, we discuss pointwise and in the following subsection
L^{p}\to L^{q}
-estimates.

2.1.
Pointwise estimates

We recall pointwise estimates for the kernel
p_{t}(x,y)
of
\mathrm{e}^{-tL_{\lambda}}
.

Theorem 2.1
.

(a)

Let
\alpha\in(0,2)
,
\lambda\geq 0
, and let
\sigma
be defined by (
5
). Then for all
t>0
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
,

(17)

\displaystyle p_{t}(x,y)\ {\simeq}\ \Big(1\wedge\frac{x_{d}}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{\sigma}\Big(1\wedge\frac{y_{d}}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{\sigma}t^{-d/\alpha}\Big(\frac{t^{1/\alpha}+|x-y|}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{-d-\alpha}.

(b)

Let
\alpha=2
,
\lambda\geq-\frac{1}{4}
, and let
\sigma
be defined by (
5
). Then for all
t>0
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
,

(18)

\displaystyle p_{t}(x,y)\ {\simeq}\ \Big(1\wedge\frac{x_{d}}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{\sigma}\Big(1\wedge\frac{y_{d}}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{\sigma}t^{-d/2}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big).

For
\lambda\!=\!0
and
\alpha\!\leq\!1
the bounds were proved in
[
CK03
]
, while
[
CKS10
]
proved the estimates for
\alpha\!\in\!(1,2)
.
The bounds for
\alpha\in(0,2)
and
\lambda\geq 0
were recently proved in
[
CKSV20
]
and extended in
[
SWW22
]
.
For
\alpha=2
, the bounds follow from the factorization of the Gaussian heat kernel with respect to
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}\!=\!\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\!\times\!\mathbb{R}_{+}
and bounds for the one-dimensional heat kernel, i.e., the Bessel heat kernel. Bounds for Bessel heat kernels can be found, e.g., in
[
BS02
, p. 75]
. The bound stated in
[
BS02
]
does not have the product structure as stated in (
18
), but can be obtained from it; see, e.g.,
[
FM23
, Appendix B]
and
[
BM23
,
GT21
]
for bounds for the related
\tfrac{\alpha}{2}
-stable subordinated Bessel heat kernels.

Next, by a Phragmén–Lindelöf argument, we extend the upper bound for
p_{t}(x,y)
to complex times
z\in\mathbb{C}_{\theta}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|\arg z|<\theta\}
,
\theta\in[0,\pi/2]
. These bounds are the key to estimate the kernels of
(tL_{\lambda})^{k}\mathrm{e}^{-tL_{\lambda}}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proposition 2.2
.

(a)

Let
\alpha\in(0,2)
,
\lambda\geq 0
, and let
\sigma
be defined by (
5
). Let
p_{z}(x,y)
be the kernels associated to the semigroups
e^{-zL_{\lambda}}
with
z\in\mathbb{C}_{\pi/2}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|\arg z|<\pi/2\}
. Then for any
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, there exists a constant
C=C_{\epsilon}
such that

(19)

|p_{z}(x,y)|\leq C\Big(1\wedge\frac{x_{d}}{|z|^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{\sigma}\Big(1\wedge\frac{y_{d}}{|z|^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{\sigma}|z|^{-d/\alpha}\Big(\frac{|z|^{1/\alpha}}{|z|^{1/\alpha}+|x-y|}\Big)^{(d+\alpha)(1-\epsilon)}

for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
and
z\in\mathbb{C}_{\epsilon\pi/4}
.

(b)

Let
\alpha=2
,
\lambda\geq-1/4
, and let
\sigma
be defined by (
5
). Let
p_{z}(x,y)
be the kernels associated to the semigroups
e^{-zL_{\lambda}}
with
z\in\mathbb{C}_{\pi/2}
. Then there exist constants
C,c>0
such that

(20)

|p_{z}(x,y)|\leq C\Big(1\wedge\frac{x_{d}}{\sqrt{|z|}}\Big)^{\sigma}\Big(1\wedge\frac{y_{d}}{\sqrt{|z|}}\Big)^{\sigma}|z|^{-d/2}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{c|z|}\Big)

for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
and
z\in\mathbb{C}_{\pi/4}
.

Remark 2.3
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,2)
,
\lambda\in[\lambda_{*},0)
and assume that
\mathrm{e}^{-tL_{\lambda}}(x,y)
satisfies the bound in (
17
) with
\sigma
defined by (
5
). Then (
19
) remains valid. This follows by the same arguments as in the proof below.

Proof.

We only to give the proof for (
19
) since the proof of (
20
) can be done similarly. To that end, we use a Phragmén–Lindelöf argument as in
[
Dav90
, Section 3.4]
. We set

w_{z}(x)=\Big(1+\frac{{|z|^{1/\alpha}}}{x_{d}}\Big)^{\sigma},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+},\ z\in\mathbb{C}

and first claim that there exists
C>0
such that

(21)

\left|w_{z}(x)p_{z}(x,y)w_{z}(y)\right|\leq\frac{C}{|z|^{d/\alpha}}

holds for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
and
z\in\mathbb{C}_{\pi/4}
.
To prove this, we define, for
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, the norms

|f|_{L_{w}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}\left|f(x)w_{z}(x)\right|

and

|f|_{L_{w}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}|f(x)|w_{z}(x)dx.

Hence, the inequality (
21
) is equivalent to

\|e^{-zL_{\lambda}}\|_{L^{1}_{w_{z}^{-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})\rightarrow L^{\infty}_{w_{z}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}\leq\frac{C}{|z|^{d/\alpha}}.

For
z\in\mathbb{C}_{\pi/4}
we can write
z=2t+is
for some
t\geq 0
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
such that
t\simeq|z|
. Then,

\displaystyle\|e^{-zL_{\lambda}}\|_{L^{1}_{w_{z}^{-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})\rightarrow L^{\infty}_{w_{z}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\|e^{-tL_{\lambda}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})\rightarrow L^{\infty}_{w_{z}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}\|e^{-isL_{\lambda}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}\|e^{-tL_{\lambda}}\|_{L^{1}_{{w_{z}}^{-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}.

We first estimate
\|e^{-tL_{\lambda}}\|_{L^{1}_{{w_{z}}^{-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}
. For
f\in L^{1}_{{w_{z}}^{-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
we have, by Theorem
2.1
and
\displaystyle\Big(1\wedge\frac{y_{d}}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{\sigma}\simeq\Big(1\wedge\frac{y_{d}}{|z|^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{\sigma}\simeq w_{z}(y)^{-1}
,

\displaystyle\|e^{-tL_{\lambda}}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}{t^{-\frac{d}{\alpha}}\Big(\frac{t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}+|x-y|}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{-d-\alpha}w_{z}(y)^{-1}|f(y)|}dy\Big|^{2}\left(1\wedge\frac{x_{d}}{t^{1/\alpha}}\right)^{2\sigma}\,dx\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}.

By Minkowski’s inequality,

\displaystyle\|e^{-tL_{\lambda}}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}\Big|t^{-\frac{d}{\alpha}}\Big(\frac{t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}+|x-y|}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{-d-\alpha}\Big|^{2}\left(1\wedge\frac{x_{d}}{t^{1/\alpha}}\right)^{2\sigma}\,dx\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}w_{z}(y)^{-1}|f(y)|dy

\displaystyle\lesssim{\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}}\Big[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big|t^{-\frac{d}{\alpha}}\Big(\frac{t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}+|x-y|}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{-d-\alpha}\Big|^{2}\left(1\wedge\frac{x_{d}}{t^{1/\alpha}}\right)^{2\sigma}\,dx\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{L^{1}_{w_{z}^{-1}}}.

This, along with the fact that

(22)

{\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}}\Big[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big|t^{-d/\alpha}\Big(\frac{t^{1/\alpha}+|x-y|}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{-d-\alpha}\Big|^{2}\left(1\wedge\frac{x_{d}}{t^{1/\alpha}}\right)^{2\sigma}\,dx\Big]^{1/2}\lesssim t^{-\frac{d}{2\alpha}},

which can be seen by distinguishing between
x_{d}\lessgtr t^{1/\alpha}
and using
\sigma>-1/2
, implies

\|e^{-tL_{\lambda}}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}\lesssim t^{-\frac{d}{2\alpha}}\|f\|_{L^{1}_{w_{z}^{-1}}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d})}.

It follows that

\|e^{-tL_{\lambda}}\|_{L^{1}_{{w_{z}}^{-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}\lesssim t^{-\frac{d}{2\alpha}}.

Similarly,

\|e^{-tL_{\lambda}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})\to L^{\infty}_{{w_{z}}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}\lesssim t^{-\frac{d}{2\alpha}}.

Moreover, since
L_{\lambda}
is a (non-negative) self-adjoint operator,
\|e^{-isL_{\lambda}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}\leq 1
. Taking these three estimates into account, we get

\|e^{-zL_{\lambda}}\|_{L^{1}_{w_{z}^{-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})\rightarrow L^{\infty}_{w_{z}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}\leq t^{-\frac{d}{\alpha}}\simeq\frac{1}{|z|^{d/\alpha}},

which implies (
21
), i.e.,

|p_{z}(x,y)|\lesssim\frac{1}{|z|^{d/\alpha}}\Big(1\wedge\frac{x_{d}}{|z|^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{\sigma}\Big(1\wedge\frac{y_{d}}{|z|^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{\sigma}

for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
and
z\in\mathbb{C}_{\pi/4}
. By the Phragmén–Lindelöf argument in
[
DR96
, Proposition 3.3]
, we get (
19
).
∎

For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote by
p_{t,k}(x,y)
the kernel of
(tL_{\lambda})^{k}e^{-tL_{\lambda}}
. As a direct consequence of Proposition
2.2
and Cauchy’s formula, we obtain the following result.

Proposition 2.4
.

(a)

Let
\alpha\in(0,2)
,
\lambda\geq 0
, and let
\sigma
be defined by (
5
). Then for any
\epsilon>0
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a constant
C=C(k,\epsilon)
such that

(23)

|p_{t,k}(x,y)|\leq C\Big(1\wedge\frac{x_{d}}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{\sigma}\Big(1\wedge\frac{y_{d}}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{\sigma}t^{-(k+d/\alpha)}\Big(\frac{t^{1/\alpha}+|x-y|}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{-d-\alpha+\epsilon}

for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
and
t>0
.

(b)

Let
\alpha=2
and let
\sigma
be defined by (
5
). Then for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
there exist constants
C,c>0
such that

(24)

|p_{t,k}(x,y)|\leq C\Big(1\wedge\frac{x_{d}}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{\sigma}\Big(1\wedge\frac{y_{d}}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)^{\sigma}t^{-(k+d/2)}\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{ct}\Big)

for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
and
t>0
.

Remark 2.5
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,2)
,
\lambda\in[\lambda_{*},0)
and assume that
\mathrm{e}^{-tL_{\lambda}}(x,y)
satisfies the bound in (
17
) with
\sigma
defined by (
5
). Then (
23
) remains valid. This follows by the same arguments as in the proof below, taking into account Remark
2.3
.

Proof.

Applying Cauchy’s formula, for every
t>0
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

L_{\lambda}^{k}e^{-tL_{\lambda}}=\frac{(-1)^{k}k!}{2\pi i}\int_{|\xi-t|=\eta t}e^{-\xi L_{\lambda}}\frac{d\xi}{(\xi-t)^{k}},

where
\eta>0
is small enough so that
\{\xi:|\xi-t|=\eta t\}\subset\mathbb{C}_{\tilde{\epsilon}\pi/4}
with
\tilde{\epsilon}=\frac{\epsilon}{d+\alpha}
, and the integral does not depend on the choice of
\eta
. Thus, by Proposition
2.2
and
|\xi|\simeq|\xi-t|\simeq t
,

\displaystyle|p_{t,k}(x,y)|

\displaystyle\leq C_{k,\tilde{\epsilon}}\Big(1\wedge\frac{x_{d}}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{\sigma}\Big(1\wedge\frac{y_{d}}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{\sigma}t^{-(k+d/\alpha)}\Big(\frac{t^{1/\alpha}+|x-y|}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{-(d+\alpha)(1-\tilde{\epsilon})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{k,\epsilon}\Big(1\wedge\frac{x_{d}}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{\sigma}\Big(1\wedge\frac{y_{d}}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{\sigma}t^{-(k+d/\alpha)}\Big(\frac{t^{1/\alpha}+|x-y|}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{-(d+\alpha-\epsilon)}

holds for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
t>0
.
∎

2.2.
Spatially averaged estimates

In this section, we use the pointwise estimates from the previous section and tools from singular integral theory to prove spatially weighted estimates involving the heat kernel bounds of
L_{\lambda}
. We will use them to prove the square function estimates in Section
3
below. To do so, we introduce further notation.
We write
\|f\|_{p}:=\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d})}
and denote, for
\theta<1/2
,

p_{\theta}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\theta},\ \ &\text{if $\theta>0$},\\
\infty,\ \ &\text{if $\theta\leq 0$}.\end{cases}

We denote the average of a measurable function
f
over a measurable set
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
N
-dimensional Lebesgue measure
|E|\in(0,\infty)
by

\fint_{E}f(x)dx=\frac{1}{|E|}\int_{E}f(x)dx.

For any ball
B\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we associate annuli
S_{j}(B):=2^{j}B\backslash 2^{j-1}B
,
j\in\mathbb{N}
, and write
S_{0}(B)=B
.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
\{T_{t}\}_{t>0}
be a family of linear integral operators on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
with their associated kernels
T_{t}(x,y)
. Assume that there exist
0<\beta<\alpha
and
\theta<1/2
such that for all
t>0
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
,

(25)

|T_{t}(x,y)|\leq t^{-d/\alpha}\Big(\frac{t^{1/\alpha}+|x-y|}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{-d-\beta}\Big(1\wedge\frac{x_{d}}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{-\theta}\Big(1\wedge\frac{y_{d}}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{-\theta}.

Assume that
(p_{\theta})^{\prime}<p\leq q<p_{\theta}
. Then for any ball
B
with radius
r_{B}
, for every
t>0
and
j\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

(26)

\Big(\fint_{S_{j}(B)}|T_{t}f|^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq\max\Big\{\Big(\frac{r_{B}}{t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}}\Big)^{d-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}},\Big(\frac{r_{B}}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{d}\Big\}\Big(1+\frac{t^{1/\alpha}}{2^{j}r_{B}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\Big(1+\frac{2^{j}r_{B}}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{-d-\beta}\Big(\fint_{B}|f|^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}

for all
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
supported in
B
, and

(27)

\Big(\fint_{B}|T_{t}f|^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq\max\Big\{\Big(\frac{2^{j}r_{B}}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{d-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}},\Big(\frac{2^{j}r_{B}}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{d}\Big\}\Big(1+\frac{t^{1/\alpha}}{r_{B}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\Big(1+\frac{2^{j}r_{B}}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{-d-\beta}\Big(\fint_{S_{j}(B)}|f|^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}

for all
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d})
supported in
S_{j}(B)
.

Proof.

For
\theta\leq 0
, the proof is immediate. The following more elaborate arguments cover this case nevertheless. In the following, we only prove (
26
); the proof of (
27
) is analogous.
Let
B=B(x_{B},r_{B})
be a ball in
\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
with
x_{B}=(x_{B}^{\prime},x_{B}^{d})
with
x_{B}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
and
x_{B}^{d}\in(0,\infty)
. We write

B^{\prime}:=\{y^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}:\|y^{\prime}-x_{B}^{\prime}\|<r_{B}\}.

With the abbreviation

D_{\theta}(x_{d},t):=\Big(1\wedge\frac{x_{d}}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{-\theta}\quad\text{for all}\ x_{d},t>0,\ \theta\in\mathbb{R},

we have

\displaystyle\|T_{t}f\|_{L^{q}(S_{j}(B))}

\displaystyle\leq\left\{\int_{S_{j}(B)}\Big[\int_{B}t^{-\frac{d}{\alpha}}\Big(\frac{t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}+|x-y|}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{-d-\alpha}D_{\theta}(x_{d},t)D_{\theta}(y_{d},t)|f(y)|dy\Big]^{q}dx\right\}^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}+I_{4},

where

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\left\{\int_{S_{j}(B)\cap\{x_{d}\leq t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\}}\Big[\int_{B\cap\{y_{d}\leq t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\}}t^{-\frac{d}{\alpha}}\Big(\frac{t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}+|x-y|}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{-d-\beta}D_{\theta}(x_{d},t)D_{\theta}(y_{d},t)|f(y)|dy\Big]^{q}dx\right\}^{\frac{1}{q}},

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\left\{\int_{S_{j}(B)\cap\{x_{d}\leq t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\}}\Big[\int_{B\cap\{y_{d}>t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\}}t^{-\frac{d}{\alpha}}\Big(\frac{t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}+|x-y|}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{-d-\beta}D_{\theta}(x_{d},t)D_{\theta}(y_{d},t)|f(y)|dy\Big]^{q}dx\right\}^{\frac{1}{q}},

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle=\left\{\int_{S_{j}(B)\cap\{x_{d}>t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\}}\Big[\int_{B\cap\{y_{d}\leq t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\}}t^{-\frac{d}{\alpha}}\Big(\frac{t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}+|x-y|}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{-d-\beta}D_{\theta}(x_{d},t)D_{\theta}(y_{d},t)|f(y)|dy\Big]^{q}dx\right\}^{\frac{1}{q}},

\displaystyle\text{and}

\displaystyle I_{4}

\displaystyle=\left\{\int_{S_{j}(B)\cap\{x_{d}>t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\}}\Big[\int_{B\cap\{y_{d}>t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\}}t^{-\frac{d}{\alpha}}\Big(\frac{t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}+|x-y|}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{-d-\beta}D_{\theta}(x_{d},t)D_{\theta}(y_{d},t)|f(y)|dy\Big]^{q}dx\right\}^{\frac{1}{q}}.

We first consider
I_{1}
. Here, we have

D_{\theta}(x_{d},t)=\Big(\frac{x_{d}}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{-\theta},\qquad D_{\theta}(y_{d},t)=\Big(\frac{y_{d}}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{-\theta}.

By Hölder’s inequality,

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\leq t^{-d/\alpha}\Big(1+\frac{d(B,S_{j}(B))}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{-d-\beta}\|f\|_{p}

\displaystyle\times\Big[\int_{S_{j}(B)\cap\{x_{d}\leq t^{1/\alpha}\}}\Big(\frac{x_{d}}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{-\theta q}dx\Big]^{1/q}\Big[\int_{B\cap\{y_{d}\leq t^{1/\alpha}\}}\Big(\frac{y_{d}}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{-\theta p^{\prime}}dy\Big]^{1/p^{\prime}}.

Using
d(B,S_{j}(B))\simeq 2^{j}r_{B}
,

(28)

\displaystyle\Big[\int_{S_{j}(B)\cap\{x_{d}\leq t^{1/\alpha}\}}\Big(\frac{x_{d}}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{-\theta q}dx\Big]^{1/q}

\displaystyle\leq\Big[\int_{2^{j}B^{\prime}}\int_{0}^{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big(\frac{x_{d}}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{-\theta q}dx_{d}dx^{\prime}\Big]^{1/q}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{\frac{1}{\alpha q}}|2^{j}B^{\prime}|^{1/q}.

since
\theta q<1
, and

(29)

\displaystyle\Big[\int_{B\cap\{y_{d}\leq t^{1/\alpha}\}}\Big(\frac{y_{d}}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{-\theta p^{\prime}}dy\Big]^{1/p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{\frac{1}{\alpha p^{\prime}}}|B^{\prime}|^{1/p^{\prime}},

we get, for some
c>0
,

(30)

\displaystyle|S_{j}(B)|^{-1/q}\times I_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\frac{d}{\alpha}+\frac{1}{\alpha q}+\frac{1}{\alpha p^{\prime}}}\Big(1+\frac{2^{j}r_{B}}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{-d-\beta}|2^{j}B|^{-1/q}|2^{j}B^{\prime}|^{1/q}|B^{\prime}|^{1/p^{\prime}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(B)}

\displaystyle=ct^{-\frac{d}{\alpha}+\frac{1}{\alpha q}+\frac{1}{\alpha p^{\prime}}}(2^{j}r_{B})^{-1/q}r_{B}^{d-1/p^{\prime}}\Big(1+\frac{2^{j}r_{B}}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{-d-\beta}\Big(\fint_{B}|f|^{p}\Big)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim{\Big(\frac{r_{B}}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{d-1/p^{\prime}}\Big(1+\frac{t^{1/\alpha}}{2^{j}r_{B}}\Big)^{1/q}\Big(1+\frac{2^{j}r_{B}}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{-d-\beta}\Big(\fint_{B}|f|^{p}\Big)^{1/p}}.

This concludes the estimate for
I_{1}
. We now bound
I_{2}
. Here, we have

D_{\theta}(x_{d},t)=\Big(\frac{x_{d}}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{-\theta},\quad D_{\theta}(y_{d},t)=1.

Hence, using Hölder’s inequality and (
28
), we have

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\{\int_{S_{j}(B)\cap\{x_{d}\leq t^{1/\alpha}\}}\Big[\int_{B\cap\{y_{d}>t^{1/\alpha}\}}\Big(\frac{x_{d}}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{-\theta}t^{-d/\alpha}\Big(\frac{t^{1/\alpha}+|x-y|}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{-d-\beta}|f(y)|dy\Big]^{q}dx\right\}^{1/q}

\displaystyle\lesssim{t^{-\frac{d}{\alpha}}}\Big(1+\frac{d(B,S_{j}(B))}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{-d-\beta}\|f\|_{L^{1}(B)}\Big(\int_{S_{j}(B)\cap\{x_{d}\leq t^{1/\alpha}\}}\Big(\frac{x_{d}}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{-\theta q}dx\Big)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\frac{d}{\alpha}+\frac{1}{\alpha q}}\Big(1+\frac{2^{j}r_{B}}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{-d-\beta}|2^{j}B^{\prime}|^{1/q}|B|\Big(\fint_{B}|f|^{p}\Big)^{1/p}.

It follows that

(31)

\displaystyle|S_{j}(B)|^{-1/q}\times I_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\frac{d}{\alpha}+\frac{1}{\alpha q}}\Big(1+\frac{2^{j}r_{B}}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{-d-\beta}|2^{j}B|^{-1/q}|2^{j}B^{\prime}|^{1/q}|B|\Big(\fint_{B}|f|^{p}\Big)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim{\Big(\frac{r_{B}}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{d}\Big(1+\frac{t^{1/\alpha}}{2^{j}r_{B}}\Big)^{1/q}\Big(1+\frac{2^{j}r_{B}}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{-d-\beta}\Big(\fint_{B}|f|^{p}\Big)^{1/p}}.

Considering the term
I_{3}
, we have

D_{\theta}(x_{d},t)=1,\ \ \ D_{\theta}(y_{d},t)=\Big(\frac{y_{d}}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{-\theta}.

Hence,

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\{\int_{S_{j}(B)\cap\{x_{d}>t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\}}\Big[\int_{B\cap\{y_{d}\leq t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\}}t^{-\frac{d}{\alpha}}\Big(\frac{t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}+|x-y|}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{-d-\beta}\Big(\frac{y_{d}}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{-\theta}|f(y)|dy\Big]^{q}dx\right\}^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\frac{d}{\alpha}}\Big(1+\frac{d(B,S_{j}(B))}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{-d-\beta}|2^{j}B|^{1/q}\int_{B\cap\{y_{d}\leq t^{1/\alpha}\}}\Big(\frac{y_{d}}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{-\theta}|f(y)|dy.

By Hölder’s inequality and (
29
),

\displaystyle\int_{B\cap\{y_{d}\leq t^{1/\alpha}\}}\Big(\frac{y_{d}}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{-\theta}|f(y)|dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\int_{B\cap\{y_{d}\leq t^{1/\alpha}\}}\Big(\frac{y_{d}}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{-\theta p^{\prime}}dy\Big)^{1/p^{\prime}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(B)}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}\alpha}}|B^{\prime}|^{1/p^{\prime}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(B)}.

Consequently,

(32)

\displaystyle|S_{j}(B)|^{-1/q}\times I_{3}

\displaystyle\lesssim\ t^{-\frac{d}{\alpha}+\frac{1}{\alpha p^{\prime}}}|B^{\prime}|^{1/p^{\prime}}|B|^{1/p}\Big(1+\frac{2^{j}r_{B}}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{-d-\beta}\Big(\fint_{B}|f|^{p}\Big)^{1/p}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\simeq}\ \Big(\frac{r_{B}}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{d-1/p^{\prime}}\Big(1+\frac{2^{j}r_{B}}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{-d-\beta}\Big(\fint_{B}|f|^{p}\Big)^{1/p}{.}

It remains to estimate the term
I_{4}
. Since in this case

D_{\theta}(x_{d},t)=D_{\theta}(y_{d},t)=1,

we have,

\displaystyle I_{4}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\{\int_{S_{j}(B)}\Big[\int_{B}t^{-d/\alpha}\Big(\frac{t^{1/\alpha}+|x-y|}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{-d-\alpha}|f(y)|dy\Big]^{q}dx\right\}^{1/q}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-d/\alpha}\Big(1+\frac{d(B,S_{j}(B))}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{-d-\beta}|S_{j}(B)|^{1/q}\|f\|_{L^{1}(B)}.

Thus, by Hölder’s inequality, we obtain

(33)

\displaystyle|S_{j}(B)|^{-1/q}\times I_{4}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\frac{d}{\alpha}}|B|\Big(1+\frac{2^{j}r_{B}}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{-d-\beta}\Big(\fint_{B}|f|^{p}\Big)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{r_{B}}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{d}\Big(1+\frac{2^{j}r_{B}}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{-d-\beta}\Big(\fint_{B}|f|^{p}\Big)^{1/p}.

This completes the proof of (
26
).

Lemma 2.7
.

\displaystyle\|T_{t}f\|_{q}

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle=\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}\cap\{x_{d}>t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\}}\Big[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}\cap\{y_{d}\leq t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\}}t^{-\frac{d}{\alpha}}\Big(\frac{t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}+|x-y|}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{-d-\beta}D_{\theta}(x_{d},t)D_{\theta}(y_{d},t)|f(y)|dy\Big]^{q}dx\right\}^{\frac{1}{q}},

\displaystyle I_{4}

I_{1}
and
I_{2}

I_{3}
and
I_{4}
can be treated similarly as the corresponding terms in the proof of Theorem
2.6
with minor modifications. To estimate

Then, applying (

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\frac{d-1}{\alpha}(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})}\left\{\int_{0}^{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big[\int_{0}^{t^{1/\alpha}}t^{-1/\alpha}\Big(\frac{x_{d}}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{-\theta}\Big(\frac{y_{d}}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{-\theta}\|f(\cdot,y_{d})\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d-1})}dy_{d}\Big]^{q}dx_{d}\right\}^{1/q}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}}\Big(\frac{y_{d}}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{-\theta}\|f(\cdot,y_{d})\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d-1})}dy_{d}

\displaystyle I_{1}\lesssim t^{-\frac{d}{\alpha}(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}.

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big(\frac{t^{1/\alpha}+|x_{d}-y_{d}|}{t^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{-1-\beta^{\prime}}\|f(\cdot,y_{d})\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d-1})}dy_{d}

Remark 2.9

Let

Remark 2.11

3.
Proof of the square function estimates (Theorem
1.4
)

For
\gamma>0
, recall the square function

\displaystyle(S_{L_{\lambda},\gamma}f)(x):=\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}|(tL_{\lambda})^{\gamma}e^{-tL_{\lambda}}f|^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\Big)^{1/2}.

We recall two criteria for singular integrals to be bounded on Lebesgue spaces. These will play an important role in the proof of the boundedness of the square functions. The first theorem gives a criterion on the boundedness on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
spaces with
p\in(1,2)
, while the second one covers the range
p>2
. The latter also involves, for
r>0
, the Hardy–Littlewood maximal operator
\mathcal{M}_{r}
, which acts as

(\mathcal{M}_{r}f)(x):=\sup_{B\ni x}\Big(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}|f(y)|^{r}\,dy\Big)^{1/r},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Here the supremum is taken over all balls
B\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
containing
x
.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
Aus07
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

Let
1\leq p_{0}<2
and let
T
be a sublinear operator which is bounded on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Assume that there exists a family of operators
\{\mathcal{A}_{t}\}_{t>0}
satisfying that for
j\geq 2
and every ball
B\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}

(37)

\Big(\fint_{S_{j}(B)}|T(I-\mathcal{A}_{r_{B}})f|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\leq\alpha(j)\Big(\fint_{B}|f|^{p_{0}}\Big)^{1/p_{0}},

and

(38)

\Big(\fint_{S_{j}(B)}|\mathcal{A}_{r_{B}}f|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\leq\alpha(j)\Big(\fint_{B}|f|^{p_{0}}\Big)^{1/p_{0}},

for all
f
supported in
B
. If
\sum_{j\geq 2}\alpha(j)2^{jd}<\infty
, then
T
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for all
p\in(p_{0},2)
.

Theorem 3.2

(
[
Aus07
, Theorem 1.2]
)
.

Let
2<q_{0}\leq\infty.
Let
T
be a bounded sublinear operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Assume that there exists a family of operators
\{\mathcal{A}_{t}\}_{t>0}
satisfying that

(39)

\displaystyle\Big(\fint_{B}\big|T(I-\mathcal{A}_{r_{B}})f\big|^{2}dx\Big)^{1/2}\leq C\mathcal{M}_{2}(f)(x),

The reverse inequality follows from this and duality. We provide the details. By functional calculus, for any

, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}f(x)g(x)dx

\displaystyle=c(\gamma)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}\int_{0}^{\infty}(tL_{\lambda})^{2\gamma}e^{-2tL_{\lambda}}f(x)g(x)\frac{dt}{t}dx,

where
c(\gamma)=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{2\gamma}e^{-2t}\frac{dt}{t}
. By Hölder’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}f(x)g(x)dx\right|

\displaystyle=c(\gamma)\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}\int_{0}^{\infty}(tL_{\lambda})^{\gamma}e^{-tL_{\lambda}}f(x)(tL_{\lambda})^{\gamma}e^{-tL_{\lambda}}g(x)\frac{dt}{t}dx\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}S_{L_{\lambda},\gamma}f(x)S_{L_{\lambda},\gamma}g(x)dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\|S_{L_{\lambda},\gamma}f\|_{p}\|S_{L_{\lambda},\gamma}g\|_{p^{\prime}}.

By the upper bound
\|S_{L_{\lambda},\gamma}g\|_{p^{\prime}}\lesssim\|g\|_{p^{\prime}}
,

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}f(x)g(x)dx\right|\lesssim\|S_{L_{\lambda},\gamma}f\|_{p}\|g\|_{p^{\prime}},

and consequently,

\|f\|_{p}\lesssim\|S_{L_{\lambda},\gamma}f\|_{p}.

This completes the proof of Theorem
1.4
. ∎

4.

In this section, we prepare the proof of the reversed Hardy inequality (Theorem

Let

\sigma

\displaystyle\times t^{-d/\alpha}\Big[\Big(\frac{t^{1/\alpha}}{t^{1/\alpha}+|x-y|}\Big)^{d+\beta}\mathbf{1}_{\alpha<2}+\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{t}\Big)\mathbf{1}_{\alpha=2}\Big]

\displaystyle\mathbf{1}_{x_{d}\vee y_{d}\geq t^{1/\alpha}}\mathbf{1}_{|x-y|\leq(x_{d}\wedge y_{d})/2}

\displaystyle\times\frac{t^{1-d/\alpha}}{(x_{d}\vee y_{d})^{\alpha}}\Big[\Big(\frac{t^{1/\alpha}}{t^{1/\alpha}+|x-y|}\Big)^{d+\beta}\mathbf{1}_{\alpha<2}+\exp\Big(-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{t}\Big)\mathbf{1}_{\alpha=2}\Big].

These two functions were used in

in the proof of the upper bound for the kernel of the difference

tL_{0}e^{-tL_{0}}-tL_{\lambda}e^{-tL_{\lambda}}
, see Proposition
4.4
below. We will often use the following auxiliary bound.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
. Then for all
\beta\in(0,2]
and all
s,t>0
we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{(st)^{\beta}}{(s+|x-z|)^{N+\beta}(s+|z-y|)^{N+\beta}}dz\simeq\frac{(s+t)^{\beta}}{[(s+t)+|x-y|]^{N+\beta}}

for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

Proof.

The proof of the inequality “
\lesssim
” was given in
[
FM23
, Lemma 22]
. Here, we prove “
\simeq
” directly. It is well-known that

e^{-t(-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{N}})^{\beta/2}}(x,y)\simeq\frac{t}{(t^{1/\beta}+|x-y|)^{N+\beta}}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{(st)^{\beta}}{(s+|x-z|)^{N+\beta}(s+|z-y|)^{N+\beta}}dz

\displaystyle\simeq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{-s^{\beta}(-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{N}})^{\beta/2}}(x,z)e^{-t^{\beta}(-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{N}})^{\beta/2}}(z,y)dz

\displaystyle=e^{-(s^{\beta}+t^{\beta})(-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{N}})^{\beta/2}}(x,z)

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{(s+t)^{\beta}}{[(s+t)+|x-y|]^{N+\beta}}

for all
s,t>0
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
∎

The following technical lemma is inspired from
[
FM23
]
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,2)

\min\{0,\alpha-1\}<\sigma<\alpha

Then there exists

such that

(49)

\displaystyle\begin{split}U^{\alpha,\beta}_{t}(x,y)&:=\mathbf{1}_{x_{d}\vee y_{d}\geq t^{1/\alpha}}\mathbf{1}_{|x-y|\leq(x_{d}\wedge y_{d})/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}\int_{0}^{t}H^{\alpha,\beta}_{t-s}(x,z)z_{d}^{-\alpha}T^{\alpha,\beta}_{s}(z,y)dsdz\\
&\leq CM^{\alpha,\beta}_{t}(x,y)\end{split}

for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
and
t>0
.

Note that
\sigma\geq(\alpha-1)/2
implies
\sigma>\min\{0,\alpha-1\}
when
\alpha\neq 1
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume
y_{d}\geq x_{d}
and
t=1
. Set

S:=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}:x_{d}\vee y_{d}\geq 1,|x-y|\leq(x_{d}\wedge y_{d})/2\}.

Since
|x_{d}-y_{d}|\leq|x-y|\leq(x_{d}\wedge y_{d})/2
, we have
x_{d}\simeq y_{d}
. Hence,
x_{d}\simeq y_{d}\gtrsim 1
for
(x,y)\in S
. We now write

\displaystyle U^{\alpha,\beta}_{t}(x,y)=

\displaystyle\mathbf{1}_{S}(x,y)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}\int_{0}^{x_{d}/2}\int_{0}^{1}H^{\alpha,\beta}_{1-s}(x,z)z_{d}^{-\alpha}T^{\alpha,\beta}_{s}(z,y)dsdz

\displaystyle+\mathbf{1}_{S}(x,y)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}\int_{x_{d}/2}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}H^{\alpha,\beta}_{1-s}(x,z)z_{d}^{-\alpha}T^{\alpha,\beta}_{s}(z,y)dsdz

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle E_{1}+E_{2}.

For the term
E_{2}
, since
z_{d}\geq x_{d}/2
and
x_{d}\simeq y_{d}\gtrsim 1
, by Lemma
4.1
, we have

\displaystyle E_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{x_{d}^{\alpha}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}(1-s)^{-d/\alpha}\Big(\frac{(1-s)^{1/\alpha}}{(1-s)^{1/\alpha}+|x-z|}\Big)^{d+\beta}s^{-d/\alpha}\Big(\frac{s^{1/\alpha}}{s^{1/\alpha}+|z-y|}\Big)^{d+\beta}dzds

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{x_{d}^{\alpha}}\int_{0}^{1}\Big(\frac{1}{1+|x-y|}\Big)^{d+\beta}ds\lesssim\frac{1}{x_{d}^{\alpha}}\Big(\frac{1}{1+|x-y|}\Big)^{d+\beta}\lesssim M_{1}^{\alpha,\beta}(x,y).

For the term
E_{1}
, we note that since
y_{d}\geq x_{d}\geq 2z_{d}
, we have
|y_{d}-z_{d}|\simeq y_{d}\simeq x_{d}\simeq|x_{d}-z_{d}|
. Therefore, in this case for
s\in(0,1)
,

(1-s)^{1/\alpha}+|x-z|\simeq x_{d}+|x^{\prime}-z^{\prime}|\ \ \text{and}\ \ s^{1/\alpha}+|y-z|\simeq x_{d}+|y^{\prime}-z^{\prime}|.

This and Lemma
4.1
imply

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}\frac{(1-s)^{\beta/\alpha}}{((1-s)^{1/\alpha}+|x-z|)^{d+\beta}}\frac{s^{\beta/\alpha}}{(s^{1/\alpha}+|x-z|)^{d+\beta}}dz^{\prime}

\displaystyle\simeq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}\frac{(1-s)^{\beta/\alpha}}{(x_{d}+|x^{\prime}-z^{\prime}|)^{d+\beta}}\frac{s^{\beta/\alpha}}{(x_{d}+|y^{\prime}-z^{\prime}|)^{d+\beta}}dz^{\prime}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{x_{d}^{-\beta-1}s^{\beta/\alpha}(1-s)^{\beta/\alpha}}{(x_{d}+|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|)^{d+\beta}}\simeq\frac{x_{d}^{-\beta-1}(1-s)^{\beta/\alpha}s^{\beta/\alpha}}{(1+|x-y|)^{d+\beta}}.

It follows that

\displaystyle E_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{x_{d}^{-\beta-1}}{(1+|x-y|)^{d+\beta}}\int_{0}^{x_{d}/2}\int_{0}^{1}z_{d}^{-\alpha}(1-s)^{\beta/\alpha}s^{\frac{\beta}{\alpha}}\Big(1\wedge\frac{z_{d}}{(1-s)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}}\Big)^{(\alpha-1)_{+}}\Big(1\wedge\frac{z_{d}}{s^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{\sigma}dsdz_{d}.

By a simple calculation,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)^{\beta/\alpha}s^{\beta/\alpha}\Big(1\wedge\frac{z_{d}}{(1-s)^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{(\alpha-1)_{+}}\Big(1\wedge\frac{z_{d}}{s^{1/\alpha}}\Big)^{\sigma}ds=\int_{0}^{1/2}\ldots+\int_{1/2}^{1}\ldots

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(1\wedge z_{d}\Big)^{\sigma+(\alpha-1)_{+}}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle E_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{x_{d}^{-\beta-1}}{(1+|x-y|)^{d+\beta}}\int_{0}^{x_{d}/2}z_{d}^{-\alpha}\Big(1\wedge z_{d}\Big)^{\sigma+(\alpha-1)_{+}}dz_{d}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{x_{d}^{\beta+1}}\frac{1}{(1+|x-y|)^{d+\beta}}\int_{0}^{x_{d}/2}z_{d}^{-\alpha}\Big(1\wedge z_{d}\Big)^{\sigma+(\alpha-1)_{+}}dz_{d}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{x_{d}^{\beta+1}}\frac{1}{(1+|x-y|)^{d+\beta}}\Big[x_{d}^{-\alpha+1}\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\alpha<1}+\ln(x_{d})\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\alpha=1}+\mathbf{1}_{\alpha>1}\Big].

Since
x_{d}\gtrsim 1
, we use the fact
\ln(x_{d})\leq c_{\epsilon}x_{d}^{\epsilon}
for every
\epsilon>0
to further obtain

\displaystyle E_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{(1+|x-y|)^{d+\beta}}\Big[\Big(\frac{1}{x_{d}}\Big)^{\beta+\alpha}\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\alpha<1}+\Big(\frac{1}{x_{d}}\Big)^{\beta+\alpha-\epsilon}\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\alpha=1}+\Big(\frac{1}{x_{d}}\Big)^{\beta+1}\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\alpha>1}\Big].

This completes our proof.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\alpha=2
and
\sigma
as in (

Then there exist

such that

\displaystyle U_{t}(x,y)

\displaystyle:=\mathbf{1}_{x_{d}\vee y_{d}\geq\sqrt{t}}\mathbf{1}_{|x-y|\leq(x_{d}\wedge y_{d})/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}\int_{0}^{t}H_{t-s}(x,z)z_{d}^{-\alpha}T_{s}(z,y)dsdz

\displaystyle\leq CM^{\alpha,\beta}_{ct}(x,y)

for all

.

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of Lemma
4.2

Hence, we omit the details.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,2)
,
\lambda\in[\lambda_{*},0)
and assume that
\mathrm{e}^{-tL_{\lambda}}(x,y)
satisfies the bound in (
17
) with
\sigma
defined by (
5
). Then (
50
) remains valid. This follows by the same arguments as in the proof below.

Proof.

We only give the proof for
\alpha\in(0,2)
since the case
\alpha=2
can be treated similarly.
Let
T^{\alpha,\beta}_{t}(x,y)
and
{H}^{\alpha,\beta}_{t}(x,y)
be the two functions defined in Lemma
4.2
. Let
\widetilde{p}_{t}(x,y)
and
\widetilde{p}_{t,1}(x,y)
denote the kernels of
e^{-tL_{0}}
and
-L_{0}e^{-tL_{0}}
, respectively. By Proposition
2.4
,

|\widetilde{p}_{t}(x,y)|+t|\widetilde{p}_{t,1}(x,y)|\lesssim H^{\alpha,\beta}_{t}(x,y)

and

|p_{t}(x,y)|+t|p_{t,1}(x,y)|\lesssim T^{\alpha,\beta}_{t}(x,y)

for all
t>0
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
. We now consider two cases.

Case 1:

x_{d}\vee y_{d}\leq t^{1/\alpha}
OR
x_{d}\vee y_{d}\geq t^{1/\alpha}
and
|x-y|\geq(x_{d}\wedge y_{d})/2
.

Since in this case

H^{\alpha,\beta}_{t}(x,y)+T^{\alpha,\beta}_{t}(x,y)\lesssim L^{\alpha,\beta}_{t}(x,y),

we get

|Q_{t}(x,y)|\lesssim L^{\alpha}_{t}(x,y).

Case 2:

x_{d}\vee y_{d}\geq t^{1/\alpha}
and
|x-y|<(x_{d}\wedge y_{d})/2
.

By Duhamel’s formula,

\displaystyle\tilde{p}_{t}(x,y)

\displaystyle-{p}_{t}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\lambda\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}\tilde{p}_{t-s}(x,z){z_{d}^{-\alpha}}p_{s}(z,y)dzds

\displaystyle=\lambda\int_{0}^{t/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}\tilde{p}_{t-s}(x,z){z_{d}^{-\alpha}}p_{s}(z,y)dzds+\lambda\int_{0}^{t/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}\tilde{p}_{s}(x,z){z_{d}^{-\alpha}}p_{t-s}(z,y)dzds.

Differentiating both sides with respect to
t
and multiplying by
t
, we get

(51)

\displaystyle{Q}_{t}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\lambda t\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}\tilde{p}_{t/2}(x,z){z_{d}^{-\alpha}}p_{t/2}(z,y)dz+\lambda t\int_{0}^{t/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}\tilde{p}_{t-s,1}(x,z){z_{d}^{-\alpha}}p_{s}(z,y)dzds

\displaystyle+\lambda t\int_{t/2}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}\tilde{p}_{t-s}(x,z){z_{d}^{-\alpha}}p_{s,1}(z,y)dzds

\displaystyle=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}.

The term
I_{1}
can be written as

\displaystyle\lambda t\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}\tilde{p}_{t/2}(x,z){z_{d}^{-\alpha}}p_{t/2}(z,y)dz

\displaystyle=6\lambda\int_{t/3}^{t/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}\tilde{p}_{t/2}(x,z){z_{d}^{-\alpha}}p_{t/2}(z,y)dzds.

It is easy to see that
H^{\alpha,\beta}_{t-s}(\cdot,\cdot)\simeq H^{\alpha,\beta}_{t/2}(\cdot,\cdot)\gtrsim\tilde{p}_{t/2}(\cdot,\cdot)
and
T^{\alpha,\beta}_{s}(\cdot,\cdot)\simeq T^{\alpha,\beta}_{t/2}(\cdot,\cdot)\gtrsim p_{t/2}(\cdot,\cdot)
for
s\in[t/3,t/2]
. This, along with Lemma
4.2
, implies

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{t/3}^{t/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}H^{\alpha,\beta}_{t-s}(x,z){z_{d}^{-\alpha}}T^{\alpha,\beta}_{s}(z,y)dzds\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}H^{\alpha,\beta}_{t-s}(x,z){z_{d}^{-\alpha}}T^{\alpha,\beta}_{s}(z,y)dzds

\displaystyle\lesssim L^{\alpha,\beta}_{t}(x,y)+M^{\alpha,\beta}_{t}(x,y).

For the second term, since
t-s\simeq t
for
{s}\in(0,t/2)
, we have

t|\tilde{p}_{t-s,1}(x,z)|\simeq(t-s)|\tilde{p}_{t-s,1}(x,z)|\lesssim H^{\alpha,\beta}_{t-s}(x,z).

Therefore, by Lemma
4.2
,

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}H^{\alpha,\beta}_{t-s}(x,z){z_{d}^{-\alpha}}T^{\alpha,\beta}_{s}(z,y)dzds\lesssim L^{\alpha,\beta}_{t}(x,y)+M^{\alpha,\beta}_{t}(x,y).

Similarly,

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{t/2}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}H^{\alpha,\beta}_{t-s}(x,z)z_{d}^{-\alpha}T^{\alpha,\beta}_{s}(z,y)dzds\lesssim L^{\alpha,\beta}_{t}(x,y)+M^{\alpha,\beta}_{t}(x,y).

This completes our proof.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

In
[
FM23
]
, similar upper bounds were obtained for the kernel of the difference
e^{-tL_{0}}-e^{-tL_{\lambda}}
. However, these are not suitable for our purpose since the square functions used here are different.

5.
Proof of the reversed Hardy inequality (Theorem
1.6
)

We now use the previous bounds for the difference of kernels to prove Theorem
1.6
, i.e., the reversed Hardy inequality, expressed in our new square functions.

Step 1.

By Proposition
4.4
,

(52)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}&t^{-s}\left|\left(tL_{\lambda}e^{-tL_{\lambda}}-tL_{0}e^{-tL_{0}}\right)f(x)\right|^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\Big)^{1/2}\\
&=\Big[\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{2^{\alpha j}}^{2^{\alpha(j+1)}}t^{-s}\left|\left(tL_{\lambda}e^{-tL_{\lambda}}-tL_{0}e^{-tL_{0}}\right)f(x)\right|^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\Big]^{1/2}\\
&\leq\left[\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{2^{\alpha j}}^{2^{\alpha(j+1)}}t^{-s}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}[L^{\alpha,\beta}_{t}(x,y)+M^{\alpha,\beta}_{t}(x,y)]|f(y)|dy\right)^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\right]^{1/2}\\
&\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-js\alpha/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}\left[L^{\alpha,\beta}_{2^{\alpha(j+1)}}(x,y)+M^{\alpha,\beta}_{2^{j\alpha}}(x,y)\right]y_{d}^{\alpha s/2}\,\frac{|f(y)|}{y_{d}^{\alpha s/2}}dy\end{split}

where in the last inequality we used the embedding
\ell_{1}\hookrightarrow\ell_{2}
. Thus, it suffices to show the
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d})
-boundedness of the operator with kernel

(53)

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-j\alpha s/2}\left[L_{2^{\alpha(j+1)}}^{\alpha,\beta}(x,y)+M_{2^{j\alpha}}^{\alpha,\beta}(x,y)\right]y_{d}^{\alpha s/2}.

To that end, we use Schur tests. In the following, we will always replace the Gaussian decay for
\alpha=2
by a polynomial decay of order
d+2
.

Step 2.

We begin with the kernel coming from the
M_{2^{j\alpha}}^{\alpha,\beta}(x,y)
-part in (
54
). As discussed in the proof of Lemma
4.2
, we have
x_{d}\simeq y_{d}
on the support of
M_{2^{j\alpha}}^{\alpha,\beta}(x,y)
. Thus, the kernel

(54)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-j\alpha s/2}M_{2^{j\alpha}}^{\alpha,\beta}(x,y)\,y_{d}^{\alpha s/2}\simeq\sum_{N\in 2^{\alpha\mathbb{Z}}}N^{-s/2}M_{N}^{\alpha,\beta}(x,y)\,(x_{d}y_{d})^{\alpha s/4}\end{split}

in question is replaced by a symmetric one and we only have to perform a single Schur test instead of two. The Schur test is analogous to that in the proof of Step 2 in
[
FM23
, Theorem 5]
, where the summation over dyadic numbers
2^{\alpha\mathbb{Z}}
is replaced with an integral over
t
with respect to the Haar measure
dt/t
. Indeed, the bound follows from

(55)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int\limits_{y_{d}\simeq x_{d}}dy\sum_{N\in 2^{\alpha\mathbb{Z}}}N^{-\frac{s}{2}-\frac{d}{\alpha}}\,\frac{N}{x_{d}^{\alpha}}\left(\frac{N^{1/\alpha}}{N^{1/\alpha}+|x-y|}\right)^{d+\beta}\mathbf{1}_{N<x_{d}^{\alpha}}x_{d}^{\alpha s/2}\\
&\quad\lesssim x_{d}^{\alpha s/2-\alpha}\sum_{2^{\alpha\mathbb{Z}}\ni N<x_{d}^{\alpha}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}dy\,\left(1\wedge\frac{N^{(d+\beta)/\alpha}}{(N^{1/\alpha}+|x-y|)^{d+\beta}}\right)\lesssim 1.\end{split}

Step 3.

We now consider the kernel coming from the
L_{2^{\alpha(j+1)}}^{\alpha,\beta}(x,y)
-part in (
54
). As in the proof of
[
FM23
, Theorem 5]
, we will prove the stronger statement that the operator with kernel

(56)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\tilde{L}_{t}^{\alpha,\beta}(x,y)&:=\left(\mathbf{1}_{x_{d}\vee y_{d}<t^{1/\alpha}}+\mathbf{1}_{x_{d}\vee y_{d}>t^{1/\alpha}}\mathbf{1}_{|x-y|>(x_{d}\wedge y_{d})/2}\right)\,\left(1\wedge\frac{x_{d}}{t^{1/\alpha}}\right)^{-r}\left(1\wedge\frac{y_{d}}{t^{1/\alpha}}\right)^{-r}\\
&\qquad\times t^{-d/\alpha}\left(\frac{t^{1/\alpha}}{t^{1/\alpha}+|x-y|}\right)^{d+\beta}\end{split}

is
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d})
-bounded, where
-r:=(q\wedge 0)\leq 0
and
q=\min\{\sigma,(\alpha-1)_{+}\}
. As in the proof of
[
FM23
, Theorem 5]
, we will show that

(57)

\displaystyle\sum_{N\in 2^{\alpha\mathbb{Z}}}N^{-s/2}\tilde{L}_{N}^{\alpha,\beta}(x,y)y_{d}^{\alpha s/2}\lesssim\left(\frac{|x-y|\vee x_{d}\vee y_{d}}{\sqrt{x_{d}y_{d}}}\right)^{2r}\frac{(|x-y|\vee x_{d}\vee y_{d})^{\alpha}}{(|x-y|\vee(x_{d}\wedge y_{d}))^{d+\alpha}}.

As with the
M_{t}^{\alpha,\beta}
-part, the proof is similar to that in
[
FM23
]
. Let us give the details. We distinguish between
N\lessgtr(x_{d}\vee y_{d})^{\alpha}
. On the one hand,

(58)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\sum_{2^{\alpha\mathbb{Z}}\ni N\geq(x_{d}\vee y_{d})^{\alpha}}N^{-\frac{s}{2}}\tilde{L}_{N}^{\alpha,\beta}(x,y)y_{d}^{\alpha s/2}\\
&\quad\simeq y_{d}^{\frac{\alpha s}{2}}(x_{d}y_{d})^{-r}\sum_{2^{\alpha\mathbb{Z}}\ni N\geq(x_{d}\vee y_{d})^{\alpha}}N^{-\frac{s}{2}+\frac{2r}{\alpha}-\frac{d}{\alpha}}\left(1\wedge\frac{N^{(\beta+d)/\alpha}}{|x-y|^{d+\beta}}\right)\\

The first term here comes from the
N

to

|x-y|^{\alpha}

from

. We have

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\sum_{2^{\alpha\mathbb{Z}}\ni N\leq(x_{d}\vee y_{d})^{\alpha}}N^{-\frac{s}{2}}\tilde{L}_{N}^{\alpha,\beta}(x,y)\,y_{d}^{\frac{\alpha s}{2}}\\
&\simeq y_{d}^{\frac{\alpha s}{2}}\sum_{2^{\alpha\mathbb{Z}}\ni N\leq(x_{d}\vee y_{d})^{\alpha}}N^{-\frac{s}{2}-\frac{d}{\alpha}}\left(1\wedge\frac{x_{d}\wedge y_{d}}{N^{1/\alpha}}\right)^{-r}\left(1\wedge\frac{N^{(\beta+d)/\alpha}}{|x-y|^{d+\beta}}\right)\mathbf{1}_{|x-y|\geq(x_{d}\wedge y_{d})/2}\\
&\leq y_{d}^{\frac{\alpha s}{2}}|x-y|^{-d-\alpha}\sum_{2^{\alpha\mathbb{Z}}\ni N\leq(x_{d}\vee y_{d})^{\alpha}}N^{1-\frac{s}{2}}\left(1\wedge\frac{x_{d}\wedge y_{d}}{N^{1/\alpha}}\right)^{-r}\mathbf{1}_{|x-y|\geq(x_{d}\wedge y_{d})/2}\\
&\lesssim y_{d}^{\frac{\alpha s}{2}}|x-y|^{-d-\alpha}\left[(x_{d}\wedge y_{d})^{\alpha-\frac{\alpha s}{2}}+(x_{d}\wedge y_{d})^{-r}(x_{d}\vee y_{d})^{r+\alpha-\frac{\alpha s}{2}}\right]\mathbf{1}_{|x-y|\geq(x_{d}\wedge y_{d})/2}\,.\end{split}

The first term here comes from the sum from
0
to
(x_{d}\wedge y_{d})^{\alpha}
. This converges since
s<2
. The second term comes from an upper bound on the sum from
(x_{d}\wedge y_{d})^{\alpha}
to
(x_{d}\vee y_{d})^{\alpha}
, in fact, from an upper bound on the sum between
0
and
(x_{d}\vee y_{d})^{\alpha}
. This sum converges since
-\frac{s}{2}+\frac{r}{\alpha}>-1
. Combining (
58
)–(
59
) as in the proof of
[
FM23
, Theorem 5]
eventually yields (

). Thus, the

-boundedness of
L_{2^{\alpha(j+1)}}^{\alpha,\beta}(x,y)
-part in (

) follows from Proposition
5.1
below. This concludes the proof of Theorem
1.6
. ∎

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\alpha>0
and
0\leq r<\frac{1}{2}
. Then the integral operator with integral kernel

\displaystyle\left(\frac{|x-y|\vee x_{d}\vee y_{d}}{\sqrt{x_{d}y_{d}}}\right)^{2r}\frac{(|x-y|\vee x_{d}\vee y_{d})^{\alpha}}{(|x-y|\vee(x_{d}\wedge y_{d}))^{d+\alpha}}

is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d})
for all
p\in(\frac{1}{1-r},\frac{1}{r})
.

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of
[
FM23
, Proposition 19]
.
We denote the kernel in the proposition by
k(x,y)
. By symmetry of
k(x,y)
and a duality argument, it suffices to consider
p\leq 2
.

Step 1.

As a preliminary step to the main argument, we integrate over the
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
-variables. By
[
FM23
, (20)]
,

(60)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}dy^{\prime}\,k(x,y)\lesssim\left(\frac{x_{d}\vee y_{d}}{\sqrt{x_{d}y_{d}}}\right)^{2r}\frac{(x_{d}\vee y_{d})^{\alpha}}{(|x_{d}-y_{d}|\vee(x_{d}\wedge y_{d}))^{1+\alpha}}.

Step 2.

We perform weighted Schur tests for the operator with kernel given by the right side of (
60
). We use the weight

w(x,y)=\left(\frac{x_{d}}{y_{d}}\right)^{\beta}\qquad\text{with}\ \beta\in(pr,p(1-r))\cap(p^{\prime}r,p^{\prime}(1-r))\,.

Since
r<1/2
and
p\in(1/(1-r),1/r)
, it is possible to find such a
\beta
.

For the first part of the Schur test, we use (
60
) to bound

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}dy\,w(x,y)^{1/p}k(x,y)

\displaystyle\simeq\int_{0}^{\infty}dy_{d}\,\left(\frac{x_{d}}{y_{d}}\right)^{\beta/p}\left(\frac{x_{d}\vee y_{d}}{\sqrt{x_{d}y_{d}}}\right)^{2r}\frac{(x_{d}\vee y_{d})^{\alpha}}{(|x_{d}-y_{d}|\vee(x_{d}\wedge y_{d}))^{1+\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\,t^{-\beta/p-r}\frac{(1\vee t)^{\alpha+2r}}{(|1-t|\vee(1\wedge t))^{1+\alpha}}

\displaystyle\simeq\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\,t^{-\beta/p-r}(1\wedge t^{-1+2r})<\infty\,.

The finiteness of the last integral uses the assumptions
pr<\beta<p(1-r)
.

For the second part of the Schur test, we note that, by symmetry, (
60
) remains valid with
dy^{\prime}
replaced by
dx^{\prime}
. Thus, and by
p^{\prime}r<\beta<p^{\prime}(1-r)
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}dx\,w(x,y)^{-1/p^{\prime}}k(x,y)

\displaystyle\simeq\int_{0}^{\infty}dx_{d}\,\left(\frac{y_{d}}{x_{d}}\right)^{\beta/p^{\prime}}\left(\frac{x_{d}\vee y_{d}}{\sqrt{x_{d}y_{d}}}\right)^{2r}\frac{(x_{d}\vee y_{d})^{\alpha}}{(|x_{d}-y_{d}|\vee(x_{d}\wedge y_{d}))^{1+\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\,t^{-\beta/p^{\prime}-r}\frac{(1\vee t)^{\alpha+2r}}{(|1-t|\vee(1\wedge t))^{1+\alpha}}<\infty\,,

as before. The
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
-boundedness therefore follows from the Schur test.
∎

Lemma 6.3

Let

Proof.

We first consider

Let
\hat{f}(\xi)=(2\pi)^{-d/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathrm{e}^{2\pi ix\cdot\xi}f(x)\,dx

for all

Now let

\displaystyle L_{\lambda}^{s/2}f

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-s/2)}\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{1-s/2}L_{\lambda}e^{-uL_{\lambda}}f\frac{du}{u}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-s/2)}\int_{0}^{1}u^{1-s/2}e^{-uL_{\lambda}}L_{\lambda}f\frac{du}{u}+\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-s/2)}\int_{1}^{\infty}uL_{\lambda}e^{-uL_{\lambda}}f\frac{du}{u^{1+s/2}},

which implies

\displaystyle\|L_{\lambda}^{s/2}f\|_{p}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-s/2)}\int_{0}^{1}u^{1-s/2}\|e^{-uL_{\lambda}}L_{\lambda}f\|_{p}\frac{du}{u}+\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-s/2)}\int_{1}^{\infty}\|uL_{\lambda}e^{-uL_{\lambda}}f\|_{p}\frac{du}{u^{1+s/2}}.

From Lemma
2.7
and Proposition
2.4
, we have

\|e^{-uL_{\lambda}}\|_{p\to p}+\|uL_{\lambda}e^{-uL_{\lambda}}\|_{p\to p}\lesssim 1

uniformly in

Therefore,

\displaystyle\|L_{\lambda}^{s/2}f\|_{p}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-s/2)}\int_{0}^{1}u^{1-s/2}\|L_{\lambda}f\|_{p}\frac{du}{u}+\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-s/2)}\int_{1}^{\infty}\|f\|_{p}\frac{du}{u^{1+s/2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|L_{\lambda}f\|_{p}+\|f\|_{p}.

Since
f,L_{\lambda}f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
,
L_{\lambda}^{s/2}f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
.
∎

We can now give the

Proof of Theorem

From Lemma

For the integral with respect to

\displaystyle\int_{|x-y|\leq 4(x_{d}\wedge y_{d})}K(x,y)dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{|x-y|\leq 4y_{d}}\frac{y_{d}^{-\alpha s/2}}{|x-y|^{d-\frac{\alpha s}{2}}}dx\lesssim 1.

Case 2:

From Lemma

,

Since

\displaystyle\int_{4y_{d}\leq|x-y|\leq 4x_{d}}w(x,y)^{1/p}K(x,y)dy

\displaystyle\simeq\int_{4y_{d}\leq|x-y|\leq 4x_{d}}\Big(\frac{|x-y|}{y_{d}}\Big)^{\gamma/p}\frac{x_{d}^{-\frac{\alpha s}{2}}y_{d}^{\sigma}}{|x-y|^{d+\sigma-\frac{\alpha s}{2}}}dy

\displaystyle\int_{4y_{d}\leq|x-y|\leq 4x_{d}}w(x,y)^{1/p}K(x,y)dy

\displaystyle K

This, along with (

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j\geq 1}2^{j(\alpha s/2-1-2\sigma+(\gamma-\beta)/p)}2^{j(1+\sigma-\gamma/p)}

\displaystyle\times\Big[\mathbf{1}_{\alpha=2}+\Big(\mathbf{1}_{\sigma\leq\frac{\alpha}{2}(1+\frac{s}{2})}+\ln(2^{j}){\mathbf{1}_{\sigma=\frac{\alpha}{2}(1+\frac{s}{2})}}+2^{j(2\sigma-\alpha(1+\frac{s}{2}))}\mathbf{1}_{\sigma>\frac{\alpha}{2}(1+\frac{s}{2})}\Big)\mathbf{1}_{\alpha<2}\Big]

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j\geq 1}2^{j(\alpha s/2-\sigma-\beta/p)}

\displaystyle\times\Big[\mathbf{1}_{\alpha=2}+\Big(\mathbf{1}_{\sigma\leq\frac{\alpha}{2}(1+\frac{s}{2})}+\ln(2^{j}){\mathbf{1}_{\sigma=\frac{\alpha}{2}(1+\frac{s}{2})}}+2^{j(2\sigma-\alpha(1+\frac{s}{2}))}\mathbf{1}_{\sigma>\frac{\alpha}{2}(1+\frac{s}{2})}\Big)\mathbf{1}_{\alpha<2}\Big].

At this stage, arguing similarly to the first inequality we also come up with

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}w(x,y)^{1/p}K(x,y)dx\lesssim 1.

This completes the proof of (
13
) under the assumption
(\frac{\alpha s}{2}-\sigma)_{+}<\frac{1}{p}<1+\sigma\wedge 0
.
∎

7.
Density of
(L_{\lambda})^{s/2}C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d})

The strategy to prove Theorem
1.10
is the same as in
[
FM23
, Theorem 25]
.
We first prove Theorem
1.10
for
f
of a special form, namely,
f\in L_{\lambda}^{s/2}\mathrm{e}^{-tL_{\lambda}}C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
for some
0<t<\infty
. To do so, we use the following pointwise bounds on functions in
\mathrm{e}^{-tL_{\lambda}}C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
. For a function
u
on a set
\Omega
and
0<\beta\leq 2
, we define its
\beta
-th Hölder seminorm as

(69)

[u]_{C^{\beta}(\Omega)}:=\begin{cases}\sup_{x,y\in\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{\beta}}&\text{if}\ 0<\beta\leq 1\,,\\
\sup_{x,y\in\Omega}\frac{|\nabla u(x)-\nabla u(y)|}{|x-y|^{\beta-1}}&\text{if}\ 1<\beta\leq 2\,.\end{cases}

Lemma 7.1

(
[
FM23
, Lemma 27]
)
.

Let
\alpha
,
\lambda
and
\sigma
be as in Theorem
1.10
. Let
0<t<\infty
and
\psi\in\mathrm{e}^{-tL_{\lambda}}C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
. Then, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
,

(70)

\displaystyle|\psi(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim(1\wedge x_{d})^{\sigma}(1\wedge|x|^{-d-\alpha})\,,

(71)

\displaystyle|L_{\lambda}\psi(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim(1\wedge x_{d})^{\sigma}(1\wedge|x|^{-d-\alpha})\,,

(72)

\displaystyle|(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}\psi(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim(1\wedge x_{d})^{\sigma-\alpha}(1\wedge|x|^{-d-\alpha})\,,

(73)

\displaystyle[\psi]_{C^{\beta}(B_{\ell_{x}}(x))}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1\wedge x_{d})^{\sigma-\beta}(1\wedge|x|^{-d-\alpha})\quad\text{with}\ \ell_{x}:=1\wedge\tfrac{x_{d}}{2}\,,\ 0<\beta<\alpha.

As noted in
[
FM23
]
the decay in the bounds for
\alpha=2
can be vastly improved, but it is convenient for us to have a unified statement.
To give the proof of Theorem
1.10
, we use, for two parameters
0<r\leq 1\leq R<\infty
, the commutator bounds in Corollary
7.2
below. These bounds involve functions satisfying

(74)

0\leq\chi\leq 1\,,\qquad\chi(x)=1\ \text{if}\ |x|\leq R\,,\qquad\chi(x)=0\ \text{if}\ |x|\geq 2R\,,\qquad|\nabla\chi|\lesssim R^{-1}\,,

and

(75)

0\leq\theta\leq 1\,,\qquad\theta(x)=0\ \text{if}\ x_{d}\leq r\,,\qquad\theta(x)=1\ \text{if}\ x_{d}\geq 2r\,,\qquad|\nabla\theta|\lesssim r^{-1}\,,

and, if
\alpha\geq 1
, also the following bounds on their Hessians, namely

(76)

|D^{2}\chi|\lesssim R^{-2},

and

(77)

|D^{2}\theta|\lesssim r^{-2}.

Corollary 7.2
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and
0<\alpha<2
. Let
0<r\leq 1\leq R<\infty
, assume that
\chi
and
\theta
satisfy (
74
) and (
75
) and, if
\alpha\geq 1
, also (
76
) and (
77
). Let
\frac{\alpha-1}{2}\leq\sigma<\alpha
, assume that
v
satisfies

(78)

|v(x)|\leq(1\wedge|x|^{-d-\alpha})(1\wedge x_{d})^{\sigma}\qquad\text{for all}\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}\,

and, if
\alpha\geq 1
, also

(79)

[v]_{C^{\beta}(B_{\ell_{x}}(x))}\leq(1\wedge|x|^{-d-\alpha})\,(1\wedge x_{d})^{\sigma-\beta}\qquad\text{for all}\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}\ \text{with}\ \ell_{x}:=1\wedge\tfrac{x_{d}}{2}

with some
\beta>\alpha-1
. Then

\|[(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2},\chi\theta]v\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}\lesssim r^{\sigma-\alpha+1/p}+R^{-\alpha-d/p^{\prime}}\,.

Proof.

The proof is exactly as in
[
FM23
, Corollary 23]
. For an explicit constant
c_{d,\alpha}>0
, we estimate the
L^{p}
-norm of the right-hand side of

(80)

\displaystyle c_{d,\alpha}[(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2},\chi\theta]v(x)=\theta(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\chi(x)-\chi(y)}{|x-y|^{d+\alpha}}v(y)\,dy+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\theta(x)-\theta(y)}{|x-y|^{d+\alpha}}\chi(y)v(y)\,dy.

For the first summand on the right-hand side, we estimate
|\theta(x)|\leq 1
and use the pointwise bounds in
[
FM23
, Lemmas 20]
, i.e.,

(81)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\chi(x)-\chi(y)}{|x-y|^{d+\alpha}}v(y)\,dy\right|\lesssim\mathbf{1}_{|x|\leq R}R^{-d-2\alpha}+\mathbf{1}_{|x|>R}|x|^{-d-\alpha}\\
&\quad+\mathbf{1}_{\alpha\geq 1}\mathbf{1}_{|x|\simeq R}R^{-d-\alpha-1}\left((1\wedge x_{d})^{-(\sigma-\alpha+1)_{-}}+\mathbf{1}_{\sigma=\alpha-1}\ln\tfrac{1}{1\wedge x_{d}}+\mathbf{1}_{\alpha=1}\ln R\right)\\
&\quad\text{for all}\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}\end{split}

and all
v
satisfying the assumptions of the present corollary. The
L^{p}
-norm of this right-hand side is
\mathcal{O}(R^{-\alpha-d/p^{\prime}})
for
R>1
. For the second summand on the right-hand side of (
80
), we use
[
FM23
, Lemmas 21]
, i.e.,

(82)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\theta(x)-\theta(y)}{|x-y|^{d+\alpha}}(\chi v)(y)\,dy\right|\lesssim\left(r^{\sigma-\alpha}\wedge\frac{r^{\sigma+1}}{x_{d}^{1+\alpha}}\right)(1+x_{d})^{1+\alpha}(1\wedge|x|^{-d-\alpha})\quad\text{for all}\ x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}\end{split}

whenever
\chi v
satisfies the assumptions of the present corollary. By
[
FM23
, Corollary 23]
the function
\chi v
indeed satisfies these assumptions when
v
does. Since the
L^{p}
-norm of the right-hand side of (
82
) is
\mathcal{O}(r^{\sigma-\alpha+1/p})
for
r<1
, the proof is concluded.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.10
.

Step 1.
We first prove this theorem for
f\in(L_{\lambda})^{s/2}\mathrm{e}^{-tL_{\lambda}}C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
for some
0<t<\infty
. Let
0<t<\infty
and let
\psi\in\mathrm{e}^{-tL_{\lambda}}C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. For parameters
0<r\leq 1\leq R<\infty
to be determined, let
\chi
and
\theta
be as in Corollary
7.2
and

\phi:=\chi\theta\psi.

Then, by (
70
),

\|\phi-\psi\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}\leq\|\mathbf{1}_{x_{d}\leq 2r}\psi\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}+\|\mathbf{1}_{|x|>R}\psi\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}\lesssim r^{\sigma+1/p}+R^{-\alpha-d/p^{\prime}}\,.

Moreover,

\|L_{\lambda}(\phi-\psi)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}\leq\|(1-\chi\theta)L_{\lambda}\psi\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}+\|[(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2},\chi\theta]\psi\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}

and, by (
71
),

\|(1-\chi\theta)L_{\lambda}\psi\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}\lesssim r^{\sigma+1/p}+R^{-\alpha-d/p^{\prime}}\,.

For
\alpha<2
we apply Corollary
7.2
and find

\|[(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2},\chi\theta]\psi\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}\lesssim r^{\sigma-\alpha+1/p}+R^{-\alpha-d/p^{\prime}}\,.

The same bound holds for
\alpha=2
as well, as follows by writing

[-\Delta,\chi\theta]\psi=-2\nabla(\chi\theta)\cdot\nabla\psi-\Delta(\chi\theta)\psi

and using the pointwise bounds (
70
) and (
73
). Thus, for all
\alpha\leq 2
,

\|L_{\lambda}(\phi-\psi)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}\lesssim r^{\sigma-\alpha+1/p}+R^{-\alpha-d/p^{\prime}}\,.

Since
0<s\leq 2
we have, by interpolation,

\|L_{\lambda}^{s/2}(\phi-\psi)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}\leq\|\phi-\psi\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}^{1-s/2}\|L_{\lambda}(\phi-\psi)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}^{s/2}\,.

Inserting the above bounds, we get

\|L_{\lambda}^{s/2}(\phi-\psi)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}\lesssim r^{\sigma+1/p-\alpha s/2}+R^{-\alpha-d/p^{\prime}}.

Since, by assumption
s<2(1/p+\sigma)/\alpha
, this tends to zero as
r\to 0
and
R\to\infty
.

Step 2.
We now prove Theorem
1.10
in the general case.
Let
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
and
\epsilon>0
. By Lemma
2.10
, there exist
t:=t_{1}/2
and
T:=t_{2}/2
such that

(83)

\|(\mathrm{e}^{-2tL_{\lambda}}-\mathrm{e}^{-2TL_{\lambda}})f-f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}\leq\epsilon\,.

On the other hand,

\mathrm{e}^{-2tL_{\lambda}}-\mathrm{e}^{-2TL_{\lambda}}f=L_{\lambda}^{s/2}(\mathrm{e}^{-tL_{\lambda}}+\mathrm{e}^{-TL_{\lambda}})L_{\lambda}^{-s/2}(\mathrm{e}^{-tL_{\lambda}}-\mathrm{e}^{-TL_{\lambda}})f.

By Lemma
2.8
,

\|L_{\lambda}^{-s/2}(\mathrm{e}^{-tL_{\lambda}}-\mathrm{e}^{-TL_{\lambda}})f\|\lesssim(T^{s/2}-t^{s/2})\|f\|_{p}.

Since
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
is dense in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
, there is a
k\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
such that

(84)

\|k-L_{\lambda}^{-s/2}(\mathrm{e}^{-tL_{\lambda}}-\mathrm{e}^{-TL_{\lambda}})f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}\leq\frac{\epsilon}{\|L_{\lambda}^{s/2}(\mathrm{e}^{-tL_{\lambda}}+\mathrm{e}^{-TL_{\lambda}})\|_{p\to p}}.

We define
\psi:=L_{\lambda}^{s/2}(\mathrm{e}^{-tL_{\lambda}}+\mathrm{e}^{-TL_{\lambda}})k
. Then, according to Step 1 (applied both to
L_{\lambda}^{s/2}\mathrm{e}^{-tL_{\lambda}}k
and to
L_{\lambda}^{s/2}\mathrm{e}^{-TL_{\lambda}}k
) there is a
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}
such that

(85)

\|L_{\lambda}^{s/2}\phi-\psi\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}\leq\epsilon\,.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\|L_{\lambda}^{s/2}\phi-f\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\|L_{\lambda}^{s/2}\phi-\psi\|_{p}+\|\psi-L_{\lambda}^{-s/2}(\mathrm{e}^{-tL_{\lambda}}+\mathrm{e}^{-TL_{\lambda}})L_{\lambda}^{-s/2}(\mathrm{e}^{-tL_{\lambda}}-\mathrm{e}^{-TL_{\lambda}})\mathrm{e}^{-TL_{\lambda}})f\|_{p}

\displaystyle+\|L_{\lambda}^{-s/2}(\mathrm{e}^{-tL_{\lambda}}-\mathrm{e}^{-TL_{\lambda}})L_{\lambda}^{-s/2}(\mathrm{e}^{-tL_{\lambda}}-\mathrm{e}^{-TL_{\lambda}})f-f\|_{p}.

By (
85
),

\|L_{\lambda}^{s/2}\phi-\psi\|_{p}\leq\epsilon.

Using (
84
) and Lemma
2.8
,

\displaystyle\|\psi-L_{\lambda}^{-s/2}(\mathrm{e}^{-tL_{\lambda}}-L_{\lambda}^{-s/2}(\mathrm{e}^{-tL_{\lambda}}+\mathrm{e}^{-TL_{\lambda}})\mathrm{e}^{-TL_{\lambda}})\mathrm{e}^{-TL_{\lambda}})f\|_{p}

\displaystyle=\|L_{\lambda}^{s/2}(\mathrm{e}^{-tL_{\lambda}}+\mathrm{e}^{-TL_{\lambda}})k-L_{\lambda}^{-s/2}(\mathrm{e}^{-tL_{\lambda}}-L_{\lambda}^{-s/2}(\mathrm{e}^{-tL_{\lambda}}-\mathrm{e}^{-TL_{\lambda}})\mathrm{e}^{-TL_{\lambda}})f\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\|L_{\lambda}^{s/2}(\mathrm{e}^{-tL_{\lambda}}+\mathrm{e}^{-TL_{\lambda}})\|_{p\to p}\|k-L_{\lambda}^{-s/2}(\mathrm{e}^{-tL_{\lambda}}-\mathrm{e}^{-TL_{\lambda}})f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}\leq\epsilon.

Finally, by (
83
),

\displaystyle\|L_{\lambda}^{-s/2}(\mathrm{e}^{-tL_{\lambda}}-\mathrm{e}^{-TL_{\lambda}})L_{\lambda}^{-s/2}(\mathrm{e}^{-tL_{\lambda}}-\mathrm{e}^{-TL_{\lambda}})f-f\|_{p}

\displaystyle=\|(\mathrm{e}^{-2tL_{\lambda}}-\mathrm{e}^{-2TL_{\lambda}})f-f\|_{p}\leq\epsilon.

Consequently, for any
\epsilon>0
there is a
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}
such that

\|L_{\lambda}^{-s/2}\phi-f\|_{p}\leq 3\epsilon.

This concludes the proof.
∎

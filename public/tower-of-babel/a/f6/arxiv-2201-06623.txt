Extremal clustering and cluster counting for spatial random fields
ARAnders Rønn–Nielsenabel=e1, mark]aro.fi@cbs.dk, MSMads Stehrabel=e2, mark]mast.fi@cbs.dk

Abstract
We consider a stationary random field indexed by an increasing sequence of subsets of ℤ d {\mathbb{Z}^{d}} obeying a very broad geometrical assumption on how the sequence expands. Under certain mixing and local conditions, we show how the tail distribution of the individual variables relates to the tail behavior of the maximum of the field over the index sets in the limit as the index sets expand. Furthermore, in a framework where we let the increasing index sets be scalar multiplications of a fixed set C C , potentially with different scalars in different directions, we use two cluster definitions to define associated cluster counting point processes on the rescaled index set C C ; one cluster definition divides the index set into more and more boxes and counts a box as a cluster if it contains an extremal observation. The other cluster definition that is more intuitive considers extremal points to be in the same cluster, if they are close in distance. We show that both cluster point processes converge to a Poisson point process on C C . Additionally, we find a limit of the mean cluster size. Finally, we pay special attention to the case without clusters.

Introduction

This paper will provide a multitude of results within the realm of spatial extreme value theory, some of which will be generalizations of one-dimensional results or results from a simpler spatial framework. More precisely, we consider a stationary field
(\xi_{v})_{v\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}

[

]

[

In the literature, results for spatial objects, comparable to some of the present results, are to the best of the authors’ knowledge only formulated under the assumption of

for similar
however slightly less general

Our requirement will simply be that each

is the lattice points of a sufficiently nice full-dimensional subset of
{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
.
To be precise, we assume that
D_{n}
is given as

, where

is asymptotically bounded by the cubic root of its volume, and that the surface area is asymptotically bounded by the cubic root of the squared volume.

The first part of the paper concerns the asymptotic distribution function of
\max_{v\in D_{n}}\xi_{v}

from the representation (

The remainder of the paper is devoted to deriving asymptotic representations of various forms of cluster and exceedance counting processes for spatial random fields satisfying mixing and local conditions as above. Specifically, we work under the additional assumption that

D_{n}=(\boldsymbol{c_{n}}C)\cap{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}

\mathcal{D}(x_{n};K_{n})

\lvert D_{n}\rvert\mathbb{P}(\xi_{0}>x_{n})\to\tau\in(0,\infty)

Now, we define two different cluster counting processes of exceedances over the threshold
x_{n}
. The first is a spatial version of the classical one-dimensional definition, e.g. seen in
[
4
]
and
[
7
]
:
{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
is divided into disjoint (increasing)

each potentially being counted as a cluster if there is an

-exceedance within the
box. For
d=1
such a counting process is usually defined on
(0,1]
via a rescaling of the indices, whereas our geometric
construction in (
3
) allows us to define it on the general set
C
. The second cluster counting process is to the best of our knowledge previously unseen in the literature in relation to extreme value theory. This counting process is also defined on
C
but is based on the more intuitive definition that a cluster is formed by the indices of
x_{n}
-exceedances which are within a given distance of other exceedances. Here, the relevant distance corresponds to the length of the diagonal of a rescaled version of the boxes from the first type of counting process.
We show that both counting processes in fact converges in distribution towards a homogeneous Poisson process on
C
with intensity
\theta\tau
. A one-dimensional counterpart of the result for the first of the two cluster processes only is found in
[
8
]
. A related spatial result under different and much stronger conditions is found in
[
3
]
in the simpler spatial scenario, where the index sets are increasing boxes: It is shown that the original exceedance point process (before collecting them into clusters) converges to a compound Poisson point process.

Moreover, we show that the expected size of such clusters is asymptotically equal to
1/\theta
. This is in accordance with the typical interpretation of the extremal index as the reciprocal of the mean number of exceedances in a cluster, but it is indeed not a triviality for the second cluster counting process. That the cluster counting processes converge to a Poisson process in particular means independence between cluster positions in the limit. The extremal independence between clusters is further underlined in the additional result, where we demonstrate that the limiting mean cluster size is in fact independent of the total number of clusters in
C
.

Related to the two cluster counting processes defined on
C
, we also define two associated cluster counting processes defined on the original scale in
{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
. Using the distributional convergence results above, we show that both of these original-scale counting processes satisfy the following: If
(B_{n}^{1}),\dots,(B_{n}^{G})
are disjoint sequences of subsets of

each satisfying the geometric Assumption
2.2
, then the joint distribution of cluster counts of either type converges in distribution to
(L^{1},\dots,L^{G})
, which are independent random variables with each

being Poisson distributed with parameter
\theta\tau\lim_{n\to\infty}\lvert B_{n}^{g}\rvert/\lvert D_{n}\rvert
(assuming that the limit exists). This result is far from being a trivial consequence of the convergence of the cluster point processes defined above, as the asymptotic behavior of the
B_{n}^{g}
sets can be considerably more complex than what is obtained by rescaling to original scale of subsets of the set
C
introduced in (
3

In the last part of the paper, we consider the non-clustering case of
\theta=1
and the usual point process of exceedances over the threshold

, now defined on the general set
C
. We show that this process and its associated original-scale process converges exactly as the cluster counting processes but with
\theta=1
; see e.g.
[
8
, Chapter 5]
for the classical one-dimensional case.

Geometric assumption and preliminaries

As mentioned in the introduction, we consider a stationary random field
(\xi_{v})_{v\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}

First, we define a type of continuous set which will be the cornerstone of the index set assumption. In the following definition, a convex body in

Definition 2.1
.

A set

where

The geometric requirement on the discrete index sets

Essentially we require that the so-called intrinsic volumes of a down-scaled version of the convex bodies defining the

, and additionally, the collection of intrinsic volumes constitutes an essential part in the famous Steiner formula from convex geometry. As indicated in the introduction, the intrinsic volumes have a direct geometrical meaning, among which we mention a few:

is proportional to the mean width,

is half the surface area, and

V_{j}(C)\geq 0

V_{j}(\gamma C)=\gamma^{j}V_{j}(C)

for

\displaystyle K_{n}

\displaystyle={\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\cap\boldsymbol{c_{n}}[-c,c]^{d}

\displaystyle={\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\cap\bigtimes_{\ell=1}^{d}\bigl[-c\cdot c_{n,\ell}\>,\>c\cdot c_{n,\ell}\bigr]

for some
0<c<\infty
, such that
D_{n,k_{n}}^{+}\subseteq K_{n}

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

If the sets
(C_{n})
are all connected and satisfy (
5
) instead of (
4
), then Assumption
2.2
still holds and in particular
(i)
–
(iii)
does so too.

3
Behavior of the maximum

In this paper we consider a stationary random field
(\xi_{v})_{v\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}
and a sequence of index sets
(D_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
with
D_{n}\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
satisfying Assumption
2.2
above. Under the assumption of certain mixing and local conditions, we present results relating the distribution of the tail of the individual variables to the distribution of the maximum of the field over
D_{n}
.
As mentioned in the previous section, the geometric approximation used in this paper is to some extend a generalization of that of
[
13
]
in that the approximation is constructed with
n
-dependent
k_{n}
. Moreover, the conditions and associated results of this section naturally generalize those of
[
13
]
by allowing index sets to increase with a different pace in different directions and, perhaps more importantly, a certain degree of clustering of extremes.

Before presenting conditions and results, we introduce some relevant notation used throughout the paper. For a
d
-dimensional vector
\boldsymbol{\gamma}
, we say that two subsets
A,B
of
{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
are
\boldsymbol{\gamma}
-separated if
B\subseteq(A\oplus\boldsymbol{\gamma}B(1))^{c}
. If
\boldsymbol{\gamma}=(\gamma,\dots,\gamma)
for some
0<\gamma<\infty
, we say that the sets are
\gamma
-separated.
Moreover, for two vectors, as for instance
\boldsymbol{\gamma_{n}}=(\gamma_{n,1},\dots,\gamma_{n,d})
and
\boldsymbol{c_{n}}=(c_{n,1},\dots,c_{n,d})
used below, we write
\boldsymbol{\gamma_{n}}=o(\boldsymbol{c_{n}})
if
\gamma_{n,\ell}/c_{n,\ell}\to 0
for each
\ell=1,\dots,d
. Lastly, for any
A\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
we shorten
\max_{v\in A}\xi_{v}
by
M_{\xi}(A)
.

In the remainder of the paper we consider index sets
(D_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfying Assumption
2.2
. In particular, Theorem
2.4
holds. In fact, the mixing condition
\mathcal{D}(x_{n};K_{n})
given shortly is described in terms of the surrounding box
K_{n}\supseteq D_{n,k_{n}}^{+}
that exists due to Theorem
2.4
(iii)
.

Condition
\mathcal{D}(x_{n};K_{n})
.

The condition
\mathcal{D}(x_{n};K_{n})
is satisfied for the stationary field
(\xi_{v})_{v\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}
if there exists an increasing sequence
(\boldsymbol{\gamma_{n}})
of
d
-dimensional vectors such that: 1)
\boldsymbol{\gamma_{n}}=o(\boldsymbol{c_{n}})
as
n\to\infty
and 2) for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and all
\boldsymbol{\gamma_{n}}
-separated sets
A,B\subseteq K_{n}

where at least one is a box, it holds that

\big\lvert\mathbb{P}(M_{\xi}(A\cup B)\leq x_{n})-\mathbb{P}(M_{\xi}(A)\leq x_{n})\mathbb{P}(M_{\xi}(B)\leq x_{n})\big\rvert\leq\alpha_{n},

(8)

where
\alpha_{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.

Example 2
.

Suppose
(\xi_{v})_{v\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}
is an
m
-dependent random field for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
: For all
m
-separated sets
A,B\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
it holds that
(\xi_{v})_{v\in A}
and
(\xi_{v})_{v\in B}
are independent. If furthermore
(D_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of sets satisfying Assumption
2.2
, then for any sequence
(x_{n})
the condition
\mathcal{D}(x_{n};K_{n})
is satisfied. The sequence
(\boldsymbol{\gamma_{n}})
should satisfy
\boldsymbol{\gamma_{n}}=o(\boldsymbol{c_{n}})
as
n\to\infty
and
\gamma_{n,\ell}\geq m
eventually for each
\ell=1,\ldots,d
. Note that
(\alpha_{n})
can be chosen to be 0 eventually.

If
(\xi_{v})_{v\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}
is an i.i.d. random field, it is well known that

\lvert D_{n}\rvert\mathbb{P}(\xi_{0}>x_{n})\to\tau\quad\text{if and only if}\quad\mathbb{P}(M_{\xi}(D_{n})\leq x_{n})\to\exp(-\tau)

(9)

as
n\to\infty
; see e.g.
[
8
, Theorem 1.5.1.]
.
However, as the following example illustrates, the approximate independence implied by the mixing condition
\mathcal{D}
is not enough to guarantee the equivalence (
9
) to hold true for dependent fields. The example is a spatial generalization of the classical example of a one-dimensional process with local maximum occurring in clusters of a given size; see e.g.
[
2
, Example 4.4.2]
.

Example 3
.

Let
(D_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of sets satisfying Assumption
2.2
, and let
F
be a distribution function chosen such that

\lvert D_{n}\rvert\overline{F}(x_{n})=\lvert D_{n}\rvert(1-F(x_{n}))\to\tau

as
n\to\infty
for some sequence
(x_{n})
and some
\tau\in(0,\infty)
. Let
B\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
be a finite set with
\lvert B\rvert\geq 1
, and let
(Y_{z})_{z\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}
be a field of i.i.d. random variables with common distribution function
F^{1/\lvert B\rvert}
. Define the stationary field
(\xi_{v})_{v\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}
by

\xi_{v}=\max_{z\in v+B}Y_{z},

and note that
\xi_{v}
has distribution function
F
and that
(\xi_{v})_{v\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}
is
m
-dependent for
m
large relative to the span of
B
. Thus condition
\mathcal{D}(x_{n};K_{n})
is satisfied, cf. Example
2
. From the convergence of the tail of
F
it is seen that

\lvert D_{n}\rvert\mathbb{P}(Y_{0}>x_{n})\to\tau/\lvert B\rvert,

(10)

which will be used shortly.
Appealing to Lemma
A.1
in Appendix
A
we have that the number of points
\lvert D_{n}\oplus B\rvert
in the Minkowski sum
D_{n}\oplus B
is asymptotically equivalent to
\lvert D_{n}\rvert
. For
n\to\infty
, we therefore conclude that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigl(\max_{v\in D_{n}}\xi_{v}\leq x_{n}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\bigl(\max_{z\in D_{n}\oplus B}Y_{v}\leq x_{n}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\bigl(Y_{0}\leq x_{n}\bigr)^{\lvert D_{n}\oplus B\rvert}

\displaystyle\sim\mathbb{P}\bigl(Y_{0}\leq x_{n}\bigr)^{\lvert D_{n}\rvert}

\displaystyle\to\exp\bigl(-\tau/\lvert B\rvert\bigr),

where the asymptotic equivalence and the convergence follow from (
10
) by standard arguments.

In fact, under the assumption of the mixing condition above, the distribution function of the maximum
M_{\xi}(D_{n})
is close to the
k_{n}
th power of the distribution function of the maximum taken over certain
\boldsymbol{\gamma_{n}}
-separated boxes.
More precisely, the lemma below holds with subsets
H_{z}^{n,k_{n}}
of
J_{z}^{n,k_{n}}
defined by

H_{z}^{n,k_{n}}=\bigl\{u\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\>:\>z_{\ell}t_{n,k_{n}}^{\ell}\leq u_{\ell}\leq(z_{\ell}+1)t_{n,k_{n}}^{\ell}-1-\gamma_{n,\ell},\ \text{for all }\ell=1,\dots,d\bigr\}.

Note that they are indeed
\boldsymbol{\gamma_{n}}
-separated for varying
z\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(D_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of sets satisfying Assumption
2.2
, and let
(\xi_{v})_{v\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}
be a stationary field satisfying
\mathcal{D}(x_{n};K_{n})
for some sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. If
k_{n}
is a sequence of integers satisfying
k_{n}\to\infty
,
k_{n}\alpha_{n}\to 0
and
k_{n}^{1/d}\boldsymbol{\gamma_{n}}=o(\boldsymbol{c_{n}})
as
n\to\infty
, then

\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{\xi}(D_{n})\leq x_{n}\bigr)\leq\mathbb{P}^{k_{n}}\bigl(M_{\xi}(H_{0}^{n,k_{n}})\leq x_{n}\bigr)+o(1)

(11)

as
n\to\infty
. If furthermore

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\lvert D_{n}\rvert\mathbb{P}(\xi_{0}>x_{n})<\infty,

(12)

then

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{\xi}(D_{n})\leq x_{n}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}^{k_{n}}\bigl(M_{\xi}(H_{0}^{n,k_{n}})\leq x_{n}\bigr)+o(1)

(13)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}^{k_{n}}\bigl(M_{\xi}(J_{0}^{n,k_{n}})\leq x_{n}\bigr)+o(1)

as
n\to\infty
.

Before proving the lemma, we give a brief remark on the equality (
13
). In the one-dimensional case, it is stated in
[
7
]
that the equality holds even without the tail assumption (
12
). This is however not the case unless stronger assumptions on the increase rate of
k_{n}
are made.

Using the set
D_{n,k_{n}}^{-}
introduced in (
6
) and the fact that
H_{z}^{n,k_{n}}\subseteq J_{z}^{n,k_{n}}
, we find that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{\xi}(D_{n,k_{n}}^{-})\leq x_{n}\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\Bigl(\bigcap_{z\in P_{n,k_{n}}}\{M_{\xi}(H_{z}^{n,k_{n}})\leq x_{n}\}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}^{p_{n,k_{n}}}\bigl(M_{\xi}(H_{0}^{n,k_{n}})\leq x_{n}\bigr)+(p_{n,k_{n}}-1)\alpha_{n},

where the last inequality follows from the mixing condition (
8
) by induction.
Since
p_{n,k_{n}}\sim k_{n}
as shown in Theorem
2.4
(ii)
, and
k_{n}\alpha_{n}\to 0
by assumption, the inequality (
11
) follows as

\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{\xi}(D_{n})\leq x_{n}\bigr)\leq\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{\xi}(D_{n,k_{n}}^{-})\leq x_{n}\bigr).

Now assume that (
12
) is satisfied, and let
H_{0}^{*}=J_{0}^{n,k_{n}}\setminus H_{0}^{n,k_{n}}

(omitting the
n,k_{n}
dependence of
H_{0}^{*}
).
Note that, up to a constant,

\lvert H_{0}^{*}\rvert\sim\Bigl(\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}\frac{\gamma_{n,\ell}}{t_{n,k_{n}}^{\ell}}\Bigr)\prod_{\ell=1}^{d}t_{n,k_{n}}^{\ell}.

Following the lines of
[
13
, Lemma 3]
(which is under the set expansion given in Example
1
though) and utilizing that
D_{n,k_{n}}^{-}\subseteq D_{n}\subseteq D_{n,k_{n}}^{+}
,
p_{n,k_{n}}\sim q_{n,k_{n}}\sim k_{n}
and
k_{n}\alpha_{n}\to 0
yield

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{\xi}(D_{n})\leq x_{n}\bigr)=\mathbb{P}^{k_{n}}\bigl(M_{\xi}(J_{0}^{n,k_{n}})\leq x_{n}\bigr)

\displaystyle+k_{n}\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{\xi}(H_{0}^{n,k_{n}})\leq x_{n}<M_{\xi}(H_{0}^{*})\bigr)+o(1)

as
n\to\infty
.
Since each
t_{n,k_{n}}^{\ell}\sim c_{n,\ell}/k_{n}^{1/d}
giving in particular that
\prod_{\ell}t_{n,k_{n}}^{\ell}\sim\lvert D_{n}\rvert/k_{n}
, we obtain

\displaystyle k_{n}\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{\xi}(H_{0}^{n,k_{n}})\leq x_{n}<M_{\xi}(H_{0}^{*})\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq k_{n}\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{\xi}(H_{0}^{*})>x_{n}\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq k_{n}\lvert H_{0}^{*}\rvert\mathbb{P}(\xi_{0}>x_{n})

\displaystyle\sim\Bigl(\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}\frac{k_{n}^{1/d}\gamma_{n,\ell}}{c_{n,\ell}}\Bigr)\lvert D_{n}\rvert\mathbb{P}(\xi_{0}>x_{n})

as
n\to\infty
. By the tail assumption (
12
) and the assumption that
k_{n}^{1/d}\boldsymbol{\gamma_{n}}=o(\boldsymbol{c_{n}})
we conclude that

k_{n}\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{\xi}(H_{0}^{n,k_{n}})\leq x_{n}<M_{\xi}(H_{0}^{*})\bigr)\to 0

(14)

as
n\to\infty
. This gives one of the equalities in (
13
). The fact that
J_{0}^{n,k_{n}}
can be substituted by
H_{0}^{n,k_{n}}
in (
13
) is a consequence of (
14
) since

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}^{k_{n}}\bigl(M_{\xi}(H_{0}^{n,k_{n}})\leq x_{n}\bigr)-\mathbb{P}^{k_{n}}\bigl(M_{\xi}(J_{0}^{n,k_{n}})\leq x_{n}\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq k_{n}\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{\xi}(H_{0}^{n,k_{n}})\leq x_{n}<M_{\xi}(H_{0}^{*})\bigr).

This concludes the proof.
∎

Let
\preceq
be an arbitrary translation invariant total order on
{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
and define

A_{v}^{(m)}=\{z\in v+[-m,m]^{d}\cap{\mathbb{Z}^{d}})\>:\>v\prec z\}

for an integer
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
{v\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}
. Similarly, we define

A_{v}^{n,k_{n}}=\{z\in v+\boldsymbol{t_{n,k_{n}}}\,[-1,1]^{d}\cap{\mathbb{Z}^{d}})\>:\>v\prec z\},

which has varying side-length given by the
d
-dimensional vector
\boldsymbol{t_{n,k_{n}}}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
(D_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of sets satisfying Assumption
2.2
, and let
(\xi_{v})_{v\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}
be a stationary field satisfying
\mathcal{D}(x_{n};K_{n})
for some sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. If
k_{n}
is a sequence of integers satisfying
k_{n}\to\infty
,
k_{n}\alpha_{n}\to 0
and
k_{n}^{1/d}\boldsymbol{\gamma_{n}}=o(\boldsymbol{c_{n}})
as
n\to\infty
, then

\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{\xi}(D_{n})\leq x_{n}\bigr)\leq\exp\Bigl(-\lvert D_{n}\rvert\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{\xi}(A_{0}^{n,k_{n}})\leq x_{n}<\xi_{0}\bigr)\Bigr)+o(1)

(15)

as
n\to\infty
. If furthermore (
12
) is satisfied then

\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{\xi}(D_{n})\leq x_{n}\bigr)=\exp\Bigl(-\lvert D_{n}\rvert\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{\xi}(A_{0}^{n,k_{n}})\leq x_{n}<\xi_{0}\bigr)\Bigr)+o(1)

(16)

as
n\to\infty
.

Using the fact that

a_{n}^{n}-\exp(-n(1-a_{n}))\to 0,\qquad a_{n}\in[0,1],

(17)

see
[
9
, Formula (2.8)]
, it follows from (
11
) that

\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{\xi}(D_{n})\leq x_{n}\bigr)\leq\exp\Bigl(-k_{n}\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{\xi}(H_{0}^{n,k_{n}})>x_{n}\bigr)\Bigr)+o(1).

(18)

As the order
\preceq
is translation invariant we obtain the inequality

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{\xi}(H_{0}^{n,k_{n}})>x_{n}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in H_{0}^{n,k_{n}}}\mathbb{P}\bigl(\,\max_{v\prec z\in H_{0}^{n,k_{n}}}\xi_{z}\leq x_{n}<\xi_{v}\bigr)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{v\in H_{0}^{n,k_{n}}}\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{\xi}(A_{v}^{n,k_{n}})\leq x_{n}<\xi_{v}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\lvert H_{0}^{n,k_{n}}\rvert\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{\xi}(A_{0}^{n,k_{n}})\leq x_{n}<\xi_{0}\bigr).

Since
k_{n}^{1/d}\boldsymbol{\gamma_{n}}=o(\boldsymbol{c_{n}})
, which gives that
\lvert H_{0}^{n,k_{n}}\rvert\sim\lvert D_{n}\rvert/k_{n}
, formula (
18
) now implies (
15
).

For the opposite inequality assume that (
12
) is satisfied. Then, due to (
13
) and (
17
),

\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{\xi}(D_{n})\leq x_{n}\bigr)=\exp\Bigl(-k_{n}\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{\xi}(J_{0}^{n,k_{n}})>x_{n}\bigr)\Bigr)+o(1).

(19)

Since also
\lvert J_{0}^{n,k_{n}}\rvert\sim\lvert D_{n}\rvert/k_{n}
we obtain that

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{\xi}(D_{n})\leq x_{n}\bigr)>0.

Utilizing this, the same arguments used in the latter part of the proof of
[
12
, Theorem 3.1.]
(more precisely those leading to the reverse of their inequality (
3.4
)) show that the reverse inequality to (
15
) also holds, and thus we obtain equality as in (
16
).
∎

With the lemma above in mind, it is natural to consider the local condition
\mathcal{D}^{\ell}
below. The condition, that introduces potential clustering of exceedances of
x_{n}
, is defined for
\theta\in[0,1]
.

Condition
\mathcal{D}^{\ell}(x_{n};k_{n};\theta)
.

The condition
\mathcal{D}^{\ell}(x_{n};k_{n};\theta)
is satisfied for the stationary field
(\xi_{v})_{v\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}
if

\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{\xi}(A_{0}^{n,k_{n}})\leq x_{n}\mid\xi_{0}>x_{n}\bigr)\to\theta

(20)

as
n\to\infty
.

The following result is a generalization of
[
13
, Theorem 5]
allowing clustering of extremes in the sense of (
20
), and allowing the more general expansion of
(D_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
as assumed in Assumption
2.2
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
(D_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of sets satisfying Assumption
2.2
, and let
(\xi_{v})_{v\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}
be a stationary field satisfying
\mathcal{D}(x_{n};K_{n})
for some sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. Moreover, let
k_{n}
be a sequence of integers satisfying
k_{n}\to\infty
,
k_{n}\alpha_{n}\to 0
and
k_{n}^{1/d}\boldsymbol{\gamma_{n}}=o(\boldsymbol{c_{n}})
as
n\to\infty
, such that
\mathcal{D}^{\ell}(x_{n};k_{n};\theta)
is satisfied for some
\theta\in(0,1]
. Then, for all
0\leq\tau<\infty
,

\lvert D_{n}\rvert\mathbb{P}(\xi_{0}>x_{n})\to\tau

if and only if

\mathbb{P}\bigl(\max_{v\in D_{n}}\xi_{v}\leq x_{n}\bigr)\to\exp(-\theta\tau)

as
n\to\infty
.

Assume first that
\lvert D_{n}\rvert\mathbb{P}(\xi_{0}>x_{n})\to\tau
as
n\to\infty
for some finite
\tau
. In particular, condition (
12
) is satisfied and hence also (
16
) is so. Using (
20
) it now follows easily that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{\xi}(D_{n})\leq x_{n}\bigr)=\exp(-\theta\tau).

Assume conversely that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(M_{\xi}(D_{n})\leq x_{n})=\exp(-\theta\tau)

for some finite
\tau
. By an application of (
15
) this in particular implies that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\lvert D_{n}\rvert\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{\xi}(A_{0}^{n,k_{n}})\leq x_{n}<\xi_{0}\bigr)<\infty.

Since
\theta>0
we obtain that (
12
) is satisfied, and thus the equality (
16
) holds. Hence

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\lvert D_{n}\rvert\mathbb{P}(\xi_{0}>x_{n})

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log\exp\Bigl(-\lvert D_{n}\rvert\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{\xi}(A_{0}^{n,k_{n}})\leq x_{n}<\xi_{0}\bigr)\Bigr)}{\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{\xi}(A_{0}^{n,k_{n}})\leq x_{n}\mid\xi_{0}>x_{n}\bigr)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\log\exp(-\theta\tau)}{\theta}=\tau

completing the proof.
∎

We note that a range of anti-clustering conditions are found in the literature. Below we include a condition from
[
12
]
which is a multi-dimensional counterpart of the condition
D^{(m+1)}(x_{n})
from
[
1
]
and which is satisfied by e.g.

-dependent fields under the assumption of (

); see
[

, Section 5.1]
.

Condition
\mathcal{D}^{(m)}(x_{n};k_{n})
.

The condition
\mathcal{D}^{(m)}(x_{n};k_{n})
is satisfied for the stationary field
(\xi_{v})_{v\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}
if

\lvert D_{n}\rvert\,\mathbb{P}\Bigl(M_{\xi}(A_{0}^{(m)})\leq x_{n}<\xi_{0}\,,\,M_{\xi}(A_{0}^{n,k_{n}}\setminus A_{0}^{(m)})>x_{n}\Bigr)\to 0

(21)

as
n\to\infty
, with the convention that
M_{\xi}(A_{0}^{(0)})=M_{\xi}(\emptyset)=-\infty
.

Obviously, the condition (

) is satisfied if

\lvert D_{n}\rvert\sum_{v\in A_{0}^{n,k_{n}}\setminus A_{0}^{(m)}}\mathbb{P}\Bigl(M_{\xi}(A_{0}^{(m)})\leq x_{n}<\xi_{0}\,,\,\xi_{v}>x_{n}\Bigr)\to 0.

(22)

In fact, setting

in (

) corresponds to the anti-clustering condition

of

]
(which however, was formulated slightly incorrect) now with

.

Clearly, under the assumption of the anti-clustering condition

is equivalent to the much simpler convergence

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\bigl(M_{\xi}(A_{0}^{(m)})\leq x_{n}\mid\xi_{0}>x_{n}\bigr)=\theta,

at least if

. The following lemma, which follows simply by rearranging the probabilities, gives this and another relation between the conditions which are used throughout the paper.

.

Assume that
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\lvert D_{n}\rvert\mathbb{P}(\xi_{0}>x_{n})>0

, which is satisfied

.

given below does in fact not follow directly by an application of Lemma

.

Corollary 3.5
.

(D_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

, and let
(\xi_{v})_{v\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}

\mathcal{D}(x_{n};K_{n})

(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

\mathcal{D}^{(m)}(x_{n};k_{n})

Then, for all

,

\lvert D_{n}\rvert\mathbb{P}(\xi_{0}>x_{n})\to\tau

as

.

4
Cluster counting processes

In the remainder of the paper we focus on sets
D_{n}
of the specific structure defined below. Moreover, we only consider choices of the corresponding sequence
x_{n}
such that
\lvert D_{n}\rvert\mathbb{P}(\xi_{0}>x_{n})
converges to a finite, non-zero limit. In particular, this means that the local condition
\mathcal{D}^{\ell}(x_{n};k_{n};\theta)
is implied by the anti-clustering condition
\mathcal{D}^{(m)}(x_{n};k_{n})
in combination with (
23
) for some
\theta\in[0,1]
; see Lemma
3.4
(i)
. Though only formulated under the assumption of
\mathcal{D}^{\ell}
, all results from Section
4
therefore remain true under the combined assumption of
\mathcal{D}^{(m)}
and (
23
).

Now assume for some
p\in\mathbb{N}
that
C
is a
p
-convex set. Then define the sequence
(C_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
as

C_{n}=\boldsymbol{c_{n}}C,

where
(\boldsymbol{c_{n}})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of
d
-dimensional vectors
\boldsymbol{c_{n}}=(c_{n,1},\dots,c_{n,d})
such that
0<c_{n,\ell}\to\infty
, and where we without loss of generality will assume that
\lvert C\rvert=1
.
We now construct the index sets

D_{n}=C_{n}\cap{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and note in particular that
(D_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies Assumption
2.2

with
(\boldsymbol{c_{n}})
playing the role of the scaling vector from the assumption. Furthermore,
(x_{n})
and
(k_{n})
will be sequences, such that
(\xi_{v})
,
(D_{n})
,
(x_{n})
and
(k_{n})
jointly satisfy conditions specified in the relevant theorems.

As mentioned in the introduction we introduce two definitions of cluster counting processes,
N_{n}
and
\tilde{N}_{n}
, respectively, counting the number of clusters (of the relevant type) of indices, where
\xi_{v}
is above the level
x_{n}
within
D_{n}
. First we define the measure
N_{n}
, based on the grid formed by the
J_{z}^{n,k_{n}}
-sets, and show Theorem
4.1
. Afterwards, we introduce the more intuitive cluster measure
\tilde{N}_{n}
and show a similar convergence theorem for that.

In the definition of the measure
N_{n}
, we count the set
J_{z}^{n,k_{n}}
as a cluster, if
M_{\xi}(J_{z}^{n,k_{n}})>x_{n}
. For each
n
we define the random measure
N_{n}
on
C
by counting the number of clusters in a scaled version of the index set as follows

N_{n}(A)=\sum_{z\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}\boldsymbol{1}_{A}\Big(z\frac{\boldsymbol{t_{n,k_{n}}}}{\boldsymbol{c_{n}}}\Big)\boldsymbol{1}_{\{M_{\xi}(J_{z}^{n,k_{n}})>x_{n}\}}

for all
A\in\mathcal{B}(C)
. Note that the cluster is counted as placed in the set
A
, if the down-scaled corner-point
z\,\boldsymbol{t_{n,k_{n}}}/\boldsymbol{c_{n}}
of the set
J_{z}^{n,k_{n}}
is in
A
. We will later refer to such points, each representing a cluster, as
cluster points
. It should be emphasized that
N_{n}
depends crucially on the choice of the
k_{n}
-sequence.

In Theorem
4.1
below we show that the sequence of random measures
(N_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

converges in distribution with respect to the vague topology
towards a homogeneous Poisson measure
N
, denoted
N_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle vd}}{{\to}}N
. Details on this type of convergence can be found in
[
6
, Chapter 4]
, and it is defined as

\int_{C}f\mathrm{d}N_{n}\to\int_{C}f\mathrm{d}N\qquad\text{for all }f\in\hat{C}_{C},

where
\hat{C}_{C}
denotes all bounded, continuous functions
f:C\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
with compact support. In the present case, where the limiting measure is a homogeneous Poisson measure, the convergence can equivalently be formulated as

(N_{n}(A_{1}),\ldots,N_{n}(A_{K}))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{D}}}{{\to}}(N(A_{1}),\ldots,N(A_{K}))

as
n\to\infty
for all
K\in\mathbb{N}
and all
A_{1},\ldots,A_{K}\in\mathcal{B}(C)
with
\lvert\partial A_{k}\rvert=0
for
k=1,\ldots,K
. See
[
6
, Theorem 4.11]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
(D_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be defined as above, and let
(\xi_{v})_{v\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}
be a stationary field satisfying
\mathcal{D}(x_{n};K_{n})
for some sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. Moreover, let
k_{n}
be a sequence of integers satisfying
k_{n}\to\infty
,
k_{n}\alpha_{n}\to 0
and
k_{n}^{1/d}\boldsymbol{\gamma_{n}}=o(\boldsymbol{c_{n}})
as
n\to\infty
, such that
\mathcal{D}^{\ell}(x_{n};k_{n};\theta)
is satisfied for some
\theta\in(0,1]
. If for some
0<\tau<\infty
,

\lvert D_{n}\rvert\mathbb{P}(\xi_{0}>x_{n})\to\tau

then
N_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle vd}}{{\to}}N
, where
N
is a homogeneous Poisson measure on
C
with intensity
\theta\tau
.

According to
[
6
, Theorem 4.18]
it suffices to show that
\mathbb{E}N_{n}(A)\to\mathbb{E}N(A)
, where
A\subseteq C
is a box on the form
A=\bigtimes_{j=1}^{d}(a_{j},b_{j}]
, and that
\mathbb{P}(N_{n}(B)=0)\to\mathbb{P}(N(B)=0)
for all
B
, where
B\subseteq C
is a finite union of boxes each on the form
\bigtimes_{j=1}^{d}(a_{j},b_{j}]
.

First we find that

\mathbb{E}N_{n}(A)=\mathbb{P}(M_{\xi}(J_{0}^{n,k_{n}})>x_{n})\sum_{z\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}\boldsymbol{1}_{A}\Big(z\frac{\boldsymbol{t_{n,k_{n}}}}{\boldsymbol{c_{n}}}\Big).

Recalling that

t_{n,k_{n}}^{\ell}=\lfloor c_{n,\ell}/k_{n}^{1/d}\rfloor,

it is easily seen that

\sum_{z\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}\boldsymbol{1}_{A}\Big(z\frac{\boldsymbol{t_{n,k_{n}}}}{\boldsymbol{c_{n}}}\Big)\sim k_{n}\lvert A\rvert

as
n\to\infty
. Combining this with (
16
), (
19
) and (
20
) gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}N_{n}(A)

\displaystyle\sim\lvert A\rvert k_{n}\mathbb{P}(M_{\xi}(J_{0}^{n,k_{n}})>x_{n})

\displaystyle\sim\lvert A\rvert\lvert D_{n}\rvert\mathbb{P}(\xi_{0}>x_{n})\mathbb{P}(M_{\xi}(A_{0}^{n,k_{n}})\leq x_{n}\mid\xi_{0}>x_{n})

\displaystyle\sim\lvert A\rvert\theta\tau

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}N(A).

Now let
B\subseteq C
be a finite union of boxes. Define
B_{n}=\boldsymbol{c_{n}}B
and furthermore

B_{n}^{-}=\bigcup_{z:I_{z}\subseteq B_{n}}I_{z}^{n,k_{n}},\qquad B_{n}^{+}=\bigcup_{z:I_{z}\cap B_{n}\neq\emptyset}I_{z}^{n,k_{n}},

such that
B_{n}^{-}\subseteq B_{n}\subseteq B_{n}^{+}
.
By arguments as in Theorem
2.4
,
\lvert B_{n}^{-}\rvert/\lvert B_{n}\rvert\to 1
and
\lvert B_{n}^{+}\rvert/\lvert B_{n}\rvert\to 1
, respectively.
Also note that both
B_{n}^{-}
and
B_{n}^{+}
themselves will be unions of (at most) the same number of boxes as
B
. Then the sequences
(B_{n}^{-}\cap{\mathbb{Z}^{d}})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(B_{n}^{+}\cap{\mathbb{Z}^{d}})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
together with
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(\xi_{v})_{v\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}
satisfy the conditions of Theorem
3.3
with

\lvert B_{n}^{-}\cap{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\rvert\,\mathbb{P}(\xi_{0}>x_{n})\to\lvert B\rvert\tau\quad\text{and}\quad\lvert B_{n}^{+}\cap{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\rvert\,\mathbb{P}(\xi_{0}>x_{n})\to\lvert B\rvert\tau,

since
\lvert B_{n}\cap{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\rvert/\lvert D_{n}\rvert\to\lvert B\rvert
. Then, by Theorem
3.3
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(M_{\xi}(B_{n}^{-}\cap{\mathbb{Z}^{d}})\leq x_{n})

\displaystyle\to\exp(-\lvert B\rvert\theta\tau)=\mathbb{P}(N(B)=0),

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(M_{\xi}(B_{n}^{+}\cap{\mathbb{Z}^{d}})\leq x_{n})

\displaystyle\to\exp(-\lvert B\rvert\theta\tau)=\mathbb{P}(N(B)=0).

The desired limit follows, since

\mathbb{P}(M_{\xi}(B_{n}^{+}\cap{\mathbb{Z}^{d}})\leq x_{n})\leq\mathbb{P}(N_{n}(B)=0)\leq\mathbb{P}(M_{\xi}(B_{n}^{-}\cap{\mathbb{Z}^{d}})\leq x_{n}).

This concludes the proof.
∎

Next we define the alternative cluster counting process
\tilde{N}_{n}
based on what intuitively is interpreted as clusters. For this we define the set

Q_{n,k_{n}}^{-}=\bigl\{z\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\>:\>z\frac{\boldsymbol{t_{n,k_{n}}}}{\boldsymbol{c_{n}}}\in C\bigr\}.

That is all indices
z
where
J_{z}^{n,k_{n}}
can be counted by
N_{n}
. Note that
P_{n,k_{n}}\subseteq Q_{n,k_{n}}^{-}\subseteq Q_{n,k_{n}}
. Furthermore, let
\tilde{D}_{n,k_{n}}
be the union of the corresponding
J_{z}^{n,k_{n}}
-sets

\tilde{D}_{n,k_{n}}=\bigcup_{z\in Q_{n,k_{n}}^{-}}J_{z}^{n,k_{n}}.

Now we consider the rescaled set of indices

\Phi_{n}=\{v/\boldsymbol{c_{n}}\>:\>v\in\tilde{D}_{n,k_{n}}\>,\>\xi_{v}>x_{n}\},

where the field is above
x_{n}
. Note that both of the measures
N_{n}
and
\tilde{N}_{n}
are based on variables
\xi_{v}
in the same extended index set
\tilde{D}_{n,k_{n}}
. To define
\tilde{N}_{n}
, we divide
\Phi_{n}
into a number of disjoint clusters of points such that different clusters are separated by a certain distance. More precisely, we say that
u,u^{\prime}\in\Phi_{n}
are in the same cluster, if there is a sequence of distinct elements
u=u_{0},u_{1}\ldots,u_{R}=u^{\prime}
in that cluster such that

\lvert u_{i}-u_{i-1}\rvert\leq\frac{\sqrt{d}}{k_{n}^{1/d}}

for all
i=1,\ldots,R
. The distance
\sqrt{d}/k_{n}^{1/d}
is asymptotically equivalent to the largest distance within the rescaled boxes
\boldsymbol{c_{n}^{-1}}J_{z}^{n,k_{n}}
, and therefore the clusters produced this way is somewhat comparable to those counted by
N_{n}
. The procedure uniquely divides
\Phi_{n}
into
X_{n,k_{n}}
disjoint clusters, where
0\leq X_{n,k_{n}}\leq\lvert\Phi_{n}\rvert
. If
X_{n,k_{n}}\geq 1
, let these clusters be denoted
\mathcal{C}_{i}^{n,k_{n}}
for
i=1,\ldots,X_{n,k_{n}}
. The ordering is arbitrary and will not be relevant subsequently. For each cluster, we define the cluster point
x_{i}^{n,k_{n}}
, meaning the point in
{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
that represents the cluster, as the point in
\mathcal{C}_{i}^{n,k_{n}}
closest to the mean of the points in the cluster, i.e.

x_{i}^{n,k_{n}}=\argmin_{x\in\mathcal{C}_{i}^{n,k_{n}}}\sum_{x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}_{i}^{n,k_{n}}}\lvert x-x^{\prime}\rvert^{2}.

In principle, we could have used any systematically chosen point from
\mathcal{C}_{i}^{n,k_{n}}
as the cluster point representing the cluster — the above is just an intuitively natural choice. Alternatively, we could have used the actual mean of the points in
\mathcal{C}_{i}^{n,k_{n}}
, but it will be helpful in the subsequent arguments that the cluster point itself corresponds to (the rescaling of) an extremal observation.

Based on the cluster points
x_{1}^{n,k_{n}},\ldots,x_{X_{n,k_{n}}}^{n,k_{n}}
we define a random point measure on
C
as

\tilde{N}_{n}(A)=\sum_{i=1}^{X_{n,k_{n}}}\boldsymbol{1}_{A}(x_{i}^{n,k_{n}})

for all
A\in\mathcal{B}(C)
with the convention that
\tilde{N}_{n}(A)=0
if
X_{n,k_{n}}=0
. Note that similar to
N_{n}
, the measure
\tilde{N}_{n}
will also depend on the sequence
(k_{n})
.

In Theorem
4.2
below we see that
(\tilde{N}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges in exactly the same way as
(N_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. The proof relies on finding a set with sufficiently large probability, where the two measures
N_{n}
and
\tilde{N}_{n}
are identical on the sets
A
and
B
under study in the proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
(D_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be defined as above, and let
(\xi_{v})_{v\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}
be a stationary field satisfying
\mathcal{D}(x_{n};K_{n})
for some sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. Moreover, let
k_{n}
be a sequence of integers satisfying
k_{n}\to\infty
,
k_{n}\alpha_{n}\to 0
and
k_{n}^{1/d}\boldsymbol{\gamma_{n}}=o(\boldsymbol{c_{n}})
as
n\to\infty
, such that
\mathcal{D}^{\ell}(x_{n};k_{n};\theta)
is satisfied for some
\theta\in(0,1]
. If for some
0<\tau<\infty
,

\lvert D_{n}\rvert\mathbb{P}(\xi_{0}>x_{n})\to\tau

then
\tilde{N}_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle vd}}{{\to}}N
, where
N
is a homogeneous Poisson measure on
C
with intensity
\theta\tau
.

Similarly to the proof of Theorem
4.1
it suffices to show that
\mathbb{E}\tilde{N}_{n}(A)\to\mathbb{E}N(A)
, where
A\subseteq C
is a box on the form
A=\bigtimes_{j=1}^{d}(a_{j},b_{j}]
and that
\mathbb{P}(\tilde{N}_{n}(B)=0)\to\mathbb{P}(N(B)=0)
for all
B
, where
B\subseteq C
is a finite union of boxes on the form
\bigtimes_{j=1}^{d}(a_{j},b_{j}]
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
and define the events

\displaystyle A_{m}

\displaystyle=\{N(\partial A\oplus[-1/m,1/m]^{d})=0\},

\displaystyle A_{m,n}

\displaystyle=\{N_{n}(\partial A\oplus[-1/m,1/m]^{d})=0\},

\displaystyle B_{m}

\displaystyle=\{N(\partial B\oplus[-1/m,1/m]^{d})=0\},

\displaystyle B_{m,n}

\displaystyle=\{N_{n}(\partial B\oplus[-1/m,1/m]^{d})=0\},

with the convention that
N_{n}
and
N
are 0 outside of
C
.
These sets represent that there is no activity close (with
m
large) to the boundaries of the sets
A
and
B
, and they will help to ensure that clusters counted inside
A
and
B
by
N_{n}
will also be counted as inside
A
and
B
by
\tilde{N}_{n}
. Note that
\mathbb{P}(A_{m}^{c})\to 0
and
\mathbb{P}(B_{m}^{c})\to 0
as
m\to\infty
.

Furthermore consider the finite collection
\mathcal{E}_{m}
of overlapping boxes with side lengths
2/m
, indexed by the same
m\in\mathbb{N}
as above,

\mathcal{E}_{m}=\Bigl\{\frac{z}{m}+[-1/m,1/m]^{d}\>:\>\mathrm{dist}\Big(\frac{z}{m},C\Big)\leq\frac{\sqrt{d}}{m},\,z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\Bigr\}.

We define the events

\displaystyle E_{m}

\displaystyle=\{N(E)\leq 1\text{ for all }E\in\mathcal{E}_{m}\},

\displaystyle E_{m,n}

\displaystyle=\{N_{n}(E)\leq 1\text{ for all }E\in\mathcal{E}_{m}\},

and notice that the overlapping nature of the sets in
\mathcal{E}_{m}
ensures that no sufficiently small neighborhood in
C
experiences more than one of the clusters counted by
N_{n}
.

Using the facts that the number of sets in
\mathcal{E}_{m}
is of order
m^{d}
and that
\mathbb{P}(N(E)\geq 2)=O(1/m^{2d})
together with Boole’s inequality, it is seen that

\mathbb{P}(E_{m}^{c})=O(1/m^{d})

as
m\to\infty
.

From Theorem
4.1
we have
\mathbb{P}(E_{m,n})\to\mathbb{P}(E_{m})
and
\mathbb{P}(B_{m,n})\to\mathbb{P}(B_{m})
as
n\to\infty
, so for given
\delta>0
we can choose
m,n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\mathbb{P}(B_{m,n}^{c})<\delta\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{P}(E_{m,n}^{c})<\delta

for all
n\geq n_{0}
.

Note that any distance between two points within a set
\boldsymbol{c_{n}^{-1}}J_{z}^{n,k_{n}}
is smaller than
\sqrt{d}/k_{n}^{1/d}
. Hence, choosing
n
large enough ensures that for any set
\boldsymbol{c_{n}^{-1}}J_{z}^{n,k_{n}}
the collection of all sets
\boldsymbol{c_{n}^{-1}}J_{z^{\prime}}^{n,k_{n}}
within a distance of at most
\sqrt{d}/k_{n}^{1/d}
from
\boldsymbol{c_{n}^{-1}}J_{z}^{n,k_{n}}
is fully contained in at least one set from
\mathcal{E}_{n}
.
For instance, choosing
n
such that
(2\sqrt{d}+3)/k_{n}^{1/d}<1/m
suffices, and this choice will also be used in the proof of Theorem
5.1
.

With this choice of
n
we have
N_{n}(A)=\tilde{N}_{n}(A)
on
A_{m,n}\cap E_{m,n}
and that
N_{n}(B)=\tilde{N}_{n}(B)
on
B_{m,n}\cap E_{m,n}
: By definition of the two types of clusters any cluster point for
\tilde{N}_{n}
will be counted as at least one cluster point for
N_{n}
. The set
E_{m,n}
ensures that a cluster for
\tilde{N}_{n}
is also at most one cluster for
N_{n}
. Furthermore, assuming the sets
A_{m,n}
and
B_{m,n}
gives that there is the same amount of clusters inside
A
and
B
, respectively.

Now we easily see that

\mathbb{P}(\tilde{N}_{n}(B)=0)\in\bigl(\mathbb{P}(N_{n}(B)=0)-2\delta,\,\mathbb{P}(N_{n}(B)=0)+2\delta\bigr)

for
n
large, which together with Theorem
4.1
gives the desired convergence of
\mathbb{P}(\tilde{N}_{n}(B)=0)
by letting
n\to\infty
and then
\delta\to 0
.

Next we turn to showing the convergence
\mathbb{E}\tilde{N}_{n}(A)\to\mathbb{E}N(A)
. First we find, using that
N_{n}(A)
and
\tilde{N}_{n}(A)
are equal on
A_{m,n}\cap E_{m,n}
for
n
large,

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\tilde{N}_{n}(A)

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}(\tilde{N}_{n}(A);A_{m,n}\cap E_{m,n})

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}(N_{n}(A);A_{m,n}\cap E_{m,n})=\mathbb{E}(N(A);A_{m}\cap E_{m}).

The last equality follows, since
\{N_{n}(A),N_{n}(E)\>:\>E\in\mathcal{E}_{m}\}
converges in distribution to
\{N(A),N(E)\>:\>E\in\mathcal{E}_{m}\}
, such that also
(N_{n}(A),1_{A_{m,n}\cap E_{m,n}})
converges in distribution to
(N(A),1_{A_{m}\cap E_{m}})
, and in addition
N_{n}(A)1_{A_{m,n}\cap E_{m,n}}\leq N_{n}(A)
, where
\mathbb{E}N_{n}(A)\to\mathbb{E}N(A)
. Letting
m\to\infty
shows that
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\tilde{N}_{n}(A)\geq\mathbb{E}N(A)
.

For the upper bound we utilize the inequality
\tilde{N}_{n}(A)\leq N_{n}(A\oplus[-1/m,1/m]^{d})
for
n
large. Then, with similar considerations,

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\tilde{N}_{n}(A)

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}(N_{n}(A);A_{m,n}\cap E_{m,n})

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}(N_{n}(A\oplus(-1/m,1/m]^{d});A_{m,n}^{c}\cup E_{m,n}^{c})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}(N(A);A_{m}\cap E_{m})+\mathbb{E}(N(A\oplus(-1/m,1/m]^{d});A_{m}^{c}\cup E_{m}^{c}).

Letting
m\to\infty
together with integrability of
N(A)
and
N(A\oplus(-1/m,1/m]^{d})
gives
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\tilde{N}_{n}(A)\leq\mathbb{E}N(A)
as desired.
∎

Mean number of points in clusters

We continue considering the framework used to introduce the cluster processes
N_{n}
and
\tilde{N}_{n}
in Section
4
. In particular,
we define
(D_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
as

D_{n}=(\boldsymbol{c_{n}}C)\cap{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}

for a
p
-convex set
C\subseteq{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
with
\lvert C\rvert=1
.
In this section we study the mean cluster size with respect to both of the cluster definitions. It should be noted that under the assumptions of Theorem
4.1
and
4.2
the limit of the mean number of clusters within
C
with respect to either definition is
\theta\tau
. The expected number of extremal points above
x_{n}
within
D_{n}
is (before rescaling)

\sum_{v\in D_{n}}\mathbb{P}(\xi_{0}>x_{n}),

which by assumption converges to
\tau
as

. Thus one would expect that the mean number of extremal points within each cluster in the limit will be

below, we show that this is indeed true. However, for the clusters counted by
\tilde{N}_{n}
we need to impose a stronger mixing condition than
\mathcal{D}(x_{n};K_{n})
. Furthermore we demonstrate via conditioning that this limit is still valid independently of how many clusters there are in
C
in total.

In relation to the cluster counting process
N_{n}
, where a set
J_{z}^{n,k_{n}}
is counted as a cluster if
M_{\xi}(J_{z}^{n,k_{n}})>x_{n}
, we define the number of points in this potential cluster as

Y^{n,k_{n}}_{z}=\big\lvert\{v\in J_{z}^{n,k_{n}}\>:\>\xi_{v}>x_{n}\}\big\rvert.

Clearly,
J_{z}^{n,k_{n}}
represents a cluster if
Y_{z}^{n,k_{n}}>0
. We will be interested in the mean number of points in such a cluster, i.e.

\mathbb{E}(Y_{0}^{n,k_{n}}\mid Y_{0}^{n,k_{n}}>0).

Similarly, for the cluster point measure
\tilde{N}_{n}
, there are clusters if
X_{n,k_{n}}=\tilde{N}_{n}(C)>0
. In that case let
\mathcal{C}
be a cluster chosen uniformly among the
\tilde{N}_{n}(C)
clusters. More specifically, we assume that
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}^{n,k_{n}}_{S}
, where conditioned on
(\tilde{N}_{n}(C)=\ell)
for each
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, the variable
S
is uniform on
\{1,\ldots,\ell\}
and independent of everything else.
Then we consider the mean cluster size defined as

\mathbb{E}(\lvert\mathcal{C}\rvert\mid\tilde{N}_{n}(C)>0).

In particular, we will focus on the limit of these conditional means as
n\to\infty
, and results for this are found in Theorem
5.1
below. We almost immediately have a result for the first of the two, while the second result requires the following stronger version of the mixing condition
\mathcal{D}(x_{n};K_{n})
.

Condition
\overline{\mathcal{D}}(x_{n};k_{n};K_{n})
.

The condition
\overline{\mathcal{D}}(x_{n};k_{n};K_{n})
is satisfied for the stationary field
(\xi_{v})_{v\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}
if there exists an increasing sequence
(\boldsymbol{\gamma_{n}})
of
d
-dimensional vectors with

such that the following holds: First, for all
\boldsymbol{\gamma_{n}}
-separated sets

\big\lvert\mathbb{P}((M_{\xi}(A)\leq x_{n})\cap\mathcal{B})-\mathbb{P}(M_{\xi}(A)\leq x_{n})\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{B})\big\rvert\leq\alpha_{n},

(24)

and

, where

\boldsymbol{\gamma_{n}}
-separated,

\big\lvert\mathbb{P}((\xi_{v}>x_{n})\cap\mathcal{B})-\mathbb{P}(\xi_{v}>x_{n})\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{B})\big\rvert\leq\tilde{\alpha}_{n},

(25)

\mathcal{B}\in\sigma((\xi_{v^{\prime}}>x_{n})\>:\>v^{\prime}\in B\})

Cluster counting measure on original scale

The results from Section

in the rescaled set
\boldsymbol{c_{n}}A

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
(\xi_{v})_{v\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}

Let

We demonstrate the proof of (

In addition to the usual set constructions, the proof applies the set construction from Section

Theorem 7.1

This result immediately gives the asymptotic behavior of upper order statistics of the field

\displaystyle=\max_{z\in Q_{n,k_{n}}^{-}}\Big\lvert\mathbb{P}(M_{\xi}(J_{z}^{n,k_{n}})>x_{n},N_{n}(C\setminus\boldsymbol{c_{n}^{-1}}J_{z}^{n,k_{n}})\in\mathcal{L}-1)-\frac{\theta\tau}{k_{n}}\mathbb{P}(N(C)\in\mathcal{L}-1)\Big\rvert

\displaystyle=\max_{z\in Q_{n,k_{n}}^{-}}\Big\lvert\mathbb{P}(M_{\xi}(\tilde{H}_{z}^{n,k_{n}})>x_{n},N_{n}(C\setminus\boldsymbol{c_{n}^{-1}}J_{z}^{n,k_{n}})\in\mathcal{L}-1)-\frac{\theta\tau}{k_{n}}\mathbb{P}(N(C)\in\mathcal{L}-1)\Big\rvert

\displaystyle+o(1/k_{n})

\displaystyle=\max_{z\in Q_{n,k_{n}}^{-}}\Big\lvert\mathbb{P}(M_{\xi}(\tilde{H}_{z}^{n,k_{n}})>x_{n})\mathbb{P}(N_{n}(C\setminus\boldsymbol{c_{n}^{-1}}J_{z}^{n,k_{n}})\in\mathcal{L}-1)-\frac{\theta\tau}{k_{n}}\mathbb{P}(N(C)\in\mathcal{L}-1)\Big\rvert

\displaystyle+o(1/k_{n})

\displaystyle=\max_{z\in Q_{n,k_{n}}^{-}}\Big\lvert\mathbb{P}(M_{\xi}(J_{z}^{n,k_{n}})>x_{n})\mathbb{P}(N_{n}(C\setminus\boldsymbol{c_{n}^{-1}}J_{z}^{n,k_{n}})\in\mathcal{L}-1)-\frac{\theta\tau}{k_{n}}\mathbb{P}(N(C)\in\mathcal{L}-1)\Big\rvert

\displaystyle+o(1/k_{n})

\displaystyle=\max_{z\in Q_{n,k_{n}}^{-}}\Big\lvert\mathbb{P}(M_{\xi}(J_{z}^{n,k_{n}})>x_{n})\mathbb{P}(N(C)\in\mathcal{L}-1)-\frac{\theta\tau}{k_{n}}\mathbb{P}(N(C)\in\mathcal{L}-1)\Big\rvert+o(1/k_{n})

\displaystyle=o(1/k_{n}),

where the
second and fourth equality follow by arguments similar to those leading to (
39
),
the fifth equality is a result of (
40
) and the last equality is due to (
37
) and the assumption
\lvert D_{n}\rvert\mathbb{P}(\xi_{0}>x_{n})\to\tau
. Combining (
41
) and (
42
) gives (
38
).

To show the results (
27
) and (
29
) for the second cluster measure, we first demonstrate that

\limsup_{m\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\max_{z\in Q_{n,k_{n}}^{-}}\mathbb{E}(Y_{z}^{n,k_{n}}\boldsymbol{1}_{E_{m,n}^{c}}\mid Y_{z}^{n,k_{n}}>0,N_{n}(C)\in\mathcal{L})=0,

(43)

where
\mathcal{L}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
is again a fixed subset. Now let
z\in Q_{n,k_{n}}^{-}
. For each
m\in\mathbb{N}
we divide
\mathcal{E}_{m}
into two sub-collections

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{m,z,n}

\displaystyle=\{E\in\mathcal{E}_{m}\>:\>\boldsymbol{c_{n}^{-1}}\boldsymbol{t_{n,k_{n}}}z\in E\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{m,z,n}

\displaystyle=\{E\in\mathcal{E}_{m}\>:\>\boldsymbol{c_{n}^{-1}}\boldsymbol{t_{n,k_{n}}}z\notin E\}.

That is the
E
-sets wherein the potential cluster
J_{z}^{n,k_{n}}
will be counted by
N_{n}
and the
E
-sets, wherein it is not counted, respectively. We note that the number of sets in
\mathcal{E}_{m,z,n}
is bounded by some finite constant
R
independent of
m,z,n
. Obviously,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(Y_{z}^{n,k_{n}}\boldsymbol{1}_{E_{m,n}^{c}}\mid Y_{z}^{n,k_{n}}>0,N_{n}(C)\in\mathcal{L})

(44)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{m,n,z}}\mathbb{E}(Y_{z}^{n,k_{n}}\boldsymbol{1}_{\{N_{n}(E)\geq 2\}}\mid Y_{z}^{n,k_{n}}>0,N_{n}(C)\in\mathcal{L})

\displaystyle+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{m,n,z}}\mathbb{E}(Y_{z}^{n,k_{n}}\boldsymbol{1}_{\{N_{n}(F)\geq 2\}}\mid Y_{z}^{n,k_{n}}>0,N_{n}(C)\in\mathcal{L}).

For any
F\in\mathcal{F}_{m,z,n}
it is obtained with similar arguments to the above that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(Y_{z}^{n,k_{n}}\boldsymbol{1}_{\{N_{n}(C)\in\mathcal{L},N_{n}(F)\geq 2\}})

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}(Y_{z}^{n,k_{n}}\boldsymbol{1}_{\{N_{n}(F)\geq 2\}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k_{n}}\tau\mathbb{P}(N(F)\geq 2)+o(1/k_{n})

uniformly in
F
and
z
. Using (
42
) we then find

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\max_{z\in Q_{n,k_{n}}^{-}}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{m,z,n}}\mathbb{E}(Y_{z}^{n,k_{n}}\boldsymbol{1}_{\{N_{n}(F)\geq 2\}}\mid Y_{z}^{n,k_{n}}>0,N_{n}(C)\in\mathcal{L})

(45)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{m}}\frac{\mathbb{P}(N(E)\geq 2)}{\theta\,\mathbb{P}(N(C)\in\mathcal{L}-1)},

which converges to 0 as
m\to\infty
, using that
\mathbb{P}(N(E)\geq 2)
is of order
1/m^{2d}
and
\lvert\mathcal{E}_{m}\rvert
is of order
m^{d}
.
We similarly have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(Y_{z}^{n,k_{n}}\boldsymbol{1}_{\{N_{n}(C)\in\mathcal{L},N_{n}(E)\geq 2\}})

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}(Y_{z}^{n,k_{n}}\boldsymbol{1}_{\{N_{n}(E)\geq 2\}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k_{n}}\tau\mathbb{P}(N(E)\geq 1)+o(1/k_{n})

uniformly in
F
and
z
. Thus,

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\max_{z\in Q_{n,k_{n}}^{-}}\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{m,z,n}}\mathbb{E}(Y_{z}^{n,k_{n}}\boldsymbol{1}_{\{N_{n}(E)\geq 2\}}\mid Y_{z}^{n,k_{n}}>0,N_{n}(C)\in\mathcal{L})

(46)

\displaystyle\leq R\max_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{m}}\frac{\mathbb{P}(N(E)\geq 1)}{\theta\,\mathbb{P}(N(C)\in\mathcal{L}-1)}.

Since
\mathbb{P}(N(E)\geq 1)
is of order
1/m^{d}
, this converges to 0 as
m\to\infty
. Combining (
44
), (
45
) and (
46
) gives (
43
).

Using that the number of extremal points in
\mathcal{C}
are bounded by the total number of extremal points in
\bigcup_{z\in Q_{n,k_{n}}^{-}}J_{z}^{n,k_{n}}
, we find that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(\lvert\mathcal{C}\rvert\boldsymbol{1}_{E_{m,n}^{c}}\boldsymbol{1}_{\{\tilde{N}_{n}(C)=\ell\}})

(47)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}(\lvert\mathcal{C}\rvert\boldsymbol{1}_{E_{m,n}^{c}}\boldsymbol{1}_{\{N_{n}(C)>0\}})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{z\in Q_{n,k_{n}}^{-}}\mathbb{E}(Y_{z}^{n,k_{n}}\boldsymbol{1}_{E_{m,n}^{c}}\boldsymbol{1}_{\{N_{n}(C)>0\}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{z\in Q_{n,k_{n}}^{-}}\mathbb{E}(Y_{z}^{n,k_{n}}\boldsymbol{1}_{E_{m,n}^{c}}\mid Y_{z}^{n,k_{n}}>0,N_{n}(C)>0)\mathbb{P}(Y_{z}^{n,k_{n}}>0,N_{n}(C)>0)

\displaystyle\leq\lvert Q_{n,k_{n}}^{-}\rvert\mathbb{P}(Y_{0}^{n,k_{n}}>0)\max_{z\in Q_{n,k_{n}}^{-}}\mathbb{E}(Y_{z}^{n,k_{n}}\boldsymbol{1}_{E_{m,n}^{c}}\mid Y_{z}^{n,k_{n}}>0,N_{n}(C)>0).

From
P_{n,k_{n}}\subseteq Q_{n,k_{n}}^{-}\subseteq Q_{n,k_{n}}
and Theorem
2.4
we have
\lvert Q_{n,k_{n}}^{-}\rvert\sim k_{n}
, so due to (
37
), (
43
) and the fact that
\mathbb{P}(\tilde{N}_{n}(C)=\ell)
has a non-vanishing limit, we also have

\limsup_{m\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}(\lvert\mathcal{C}\rvert\boldsymbol{1}_{E_{m,n}^{c}}\mid\tilde{N}_{n}(C)=\ell)=0.

Now let
\delta>0
and choose
m,n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(\lvert\mathcal{C}\rvert\boldsymbol{1}_{E_{m,n}^{c}}\mid\tilde{N}_{n}(C)=\ell)

\displaystyle<\delta

\displaystyle\max_{z\in Q_{n,k_{n}}^{-}}\mathbb{E}(Y_{z}^{n,k_{n}}\boldsymbol{1}_{E_{m,n}^{c}}\mid Y_{z}^{n,k_{n}}>0,N_{n}(C)=\ell)

\displaystyle<\delta

\displaystyle\frac{2\sqrt{d}+3}{k_{n}^{1/d}}

\displaystyle<1/m

for
n\geq n_{0}
. We write

\mathbb{E}(\lvert\mathcal{C}\rvert\mid\tilde{N}_{n}(C)=\ell)=\mathbb{E}(\lvert\mathcal{C}\rvert\boldsymbol{1}_{E_{m,n}}\mid\tilde{N}_{n}(C)=\ell)+\mathbb{E}(\lvert\mathcal{C}\rvert\boldsymbol{1}_{E_{m,n}^{c}}\mid\tilde{N}_{n}(C)=\ell),

where the second term is bounded from above by
\delta
for
n\geq n_{0}
. On
E_{m,n}
and due to the assumption
(2\sqrt{d}+3)/k_{n}^{1/d}<1/m
we have with the same arguments as in the proof of Theorem
4.2
that each of the clusters, as counted by
\tilde{N}_{n}
, are contained in a
J_{z}^{n,k_{n}}
-set. In particular, on the set
E_{m,n}\cap(N_{n}(C)=\ell)
we have that

\lvert\mathcal{C}\rvert=Y_{z_{S}}^{n,k_{n}},

where
z_{S}
is chosen uniformly on
\{z\in Q_{n,k_{n}}^{-}\>:\>Y_{z}^{n,k_{n}}>0\}
independently of everything else. Technically, this means that we choose a uniform integer number from
\{1,\ldots,\ell\}
and use an order on
{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
to translate the integer back to a
z
-value. Thus, using that
\tilde{N}_{n}(C)=N_{n}(C)
on
E_{m,n}
and
\mathbb{P}(\tilde{N}_{n}(C)=\ell)\sim\mathbb{P}(N_{n}(C)=\ell)
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(\lvert\mathcal{C}\rvert\boldsymbol{1}_{E_{m,n}}\mid\tilde{N}_{n}(C)=\ell)

\displaystyle\sim\mathbb{E}(\lvert\mathcal{C}\rvert\boldsymbol{1}_{E_{m,n}}\mid N_{n}(C)=\ell)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}(Y_{z_{S}}^{n,k_{n}}\boldsymbol{1}_{E_{m,n}}\mid N_{n}(C)=\ell)+o(1)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}(Y_{z_{S}}^{n,k_{n}}\mid N_{n}(C)=\ell)-\mathbb{E}(Y_{z_{S}}^{n,k_{n}}\boldsymbol{1}_{E_{m,n}^{c}}\mid N_{n}(C)=\ell)+o(1).

Applying similar arguments to (
47
) gives that also

\mathbb{E}(Y_{z_{S}}^{n,k_{n}}\boldsymbol{1}_{E_{m,n}^{c}}\mid N_{n}(C)=\ell)<\delta

for
n\geq\tilde{n}_{0}
by choosing
\tilde{n}_{0}
large enough. We now find that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(Y_{z_{S}}^{n,k_{n}}\boldsymbol{1}_{\{N_{n}(C)=\ell\}})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}(\mathbb{E}(Y_{z_{S}}^{n,k_{n}}\boldsymbol{1}_{\{N_{n}(C)=\ell\}}\mid(\xi_{v})_{v\in{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}))

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Bigl(\frac{1}{\ell}\sum_{z\in Q_{n,k_{n}}^{-}}Y_{z}^{n,k_{n}}\boldsymbol{1}_{\{Y_{z}^{n,k_{n}}>0,N_{n}(C)=\ell\}}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\ell}\sum_{z\in Q_{n,k_{n}}^{-}}\mathbb{E}(Y_{z}^{n,k_{n}}\mid Y_{z}^{n,k_{n}}>0,N_{n}(C)=\ell)\mathbb{P}(Y_{z}^{n,k_{n}}>0,N_{n}(C)=\ell),

where the second equality is due to the independence between
z_{S}
and everything else on
(N_{n}(C)=\ell)
. Using the uniform convergences in (
28
) and (
42
) and that
\lvert Q_{n,k_{n}}^{-}\rvert\sim k_{n}
, we find that

\mathbb{E}(Y_{z_{S}}^{n,k_{n}}\boldsymbol{1}_{\{N_{n}(C)=\ell\}})=\frac{\tau}{\ell}\mathbb{P}(N(C)=\ell-1)+o(1).

Using that

\frac{\mathbb{P}(N(C)=\ell-1)}{\mathbb{P}(N(C)=\ell)}=\frac{\ell}{\theta\tau}

and letting
\delta\to 0
gives (
29
).

Finally, we turn to demonstrating (
27
). For this, recalling that always
\tilde{N}_{n}(C)\leq N_{n}(C)
, we follow the lines from (
47
) and find for all
\ell_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(\lvert\mathcal{C}\rvert\boldsymbol{1}_{\{\tilde{N}_{n}(C)>\ell_{0}\}})

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}(\lvert\mathcal{C}\rvert\boldsymbol{1}_{\{N_{n}(C)>\ell_{0}\}})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{z\in Q_{n,k_{n}}^{-}}\mathbb{E}(Y_{z}^{n,k_{n}}\boldsymbol{1}_{\{N_{n}(C)>\ell_{0}\}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{z\in Q_{n,k_{n}}^{-}}\mathbb{E}(Y_{z}^{n,k_{n}}\mid Y_{z}^{n,k_{n}}>0,N_{n}(C)>\ell_{0})\mathbb{P}(Y_{z}^{n,k_{n}}>0,N_{n}(C)>\ell_{0}).

Again using the uniform convergences in (
28
) and (
42
), we find that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}(\lvert\mathcal{C}\rvert\boldsymbol{1}_{\{\tilde{N}_{n}(C)>\ell_{0}\}})\leq\tau\mathbb{P}(N(C)>\ell_{0}-1),

which decreases to 0 as
\ell_{0}\to\infty
. Note that (
29
) equivalently writes that for every fixed
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
,

\mathbb{E}(\lvert\mathcal{C}\rvert\boldsymbol{1}_{\{\tilde{N}_{n}(C)=\ell\}})\to\mathbb{P}(N(C)=\ell)/\theta,

(48)

since
\mathbb{P}(\tilde{N}_{n}(C)=\ell)\to\mathbb{P}(N(C)=\ell)
. Therefore,

\mathbb{E}(\lvert\mathcal{C}\rvert\boldsymbol{1}_{\{\tilde{N}_{n}(C)>0\}})=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\ell_{0}}\mathbb{E}(\lvert\mathcal{C}\rvert\boldsymbol{1}_{\{\tilde{N}_{n}(C)=\ell\}})+\mathbb{E}(\lvert\mathcal{C}\rvert\boldsymbol{1}_{\{\tilde{N}_{n}(C)>\ell_{0}\}}),

where the first term converges to
\mathbb{P}(0<N(C)\leq\ell_{0})/\theta
as
n\to\infty
due to (
48
), and the second in the limit is bounded by
\tau\mathbb{P}(N(C)>\ell_{0}-1)
. Letting
\ell_{0}\to\infty
shows

\mathbb{E}(\lvert\mathcal{C}\rvert\boldsymbol{1}_{\{\tilde{N}_{n}(C)>0\}})\to\mathbb{P}(N(C)>0)/\theta,

from which the desired property (
27
) follows.
∎

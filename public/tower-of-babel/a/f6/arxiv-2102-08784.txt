Peeking into the θ \theta vacuum
Ryuichiro Kitano, Ryutaro Matsudo, Norikazu Yamada, Masahito Yamazaki

Abstract
We propose a subvolume method to study the θ \theta dependence of the free energy density of the four-dimensional SU \mathrm{SU} ( N N ) Yang-Mills theory on the lattice. As an attempt, the method is first applied to SU \mathrm{SU} (2) Yang-Mills theory at T = 1.2 ​ T c T=1.2\,T_{c} to understand the systematics of the method. We then proceed to the calculation of the vacuum energy density and obtain the θ \theta dependence qualitatively different from the high temperature case. The numerical results combined with the theoretical requirements provide the evidence for the spontaneous CP violation at θ = π \theta=\pi , which is in accordance with the large N N prediction and indicates that the similarity between 4d SU( N N ) and 2d CP N − 1 {}^{N\!-\!1} theories does not hold for N N =2.

II
Subvolume Method and Lattice Set Up

In what follows, the subvolume method is described.
After generating a number of gauge configurations at
\theta=0
and
implementing the
n_{\rm APE}
steps of APE
smearing
Albanese:1987ds
, the topological charge density
q(x)

is calculated with the five-loop improved topological charge
operator
deForcrand:1997esx
on the lattice.
Defining the subvolume topological charge
Q_{\rm sub}
,

\displaystyle Q_{\rm sub}=\displaystyle\sum_{x\in V_{\rm sub}}q(x)\ ,

(1)

we calculate the free energy density for the subvolume
as
Luscher:1978rn
;
KeithHynes:2008rw
,

\displaystyle f_{\rm sub}(\theta)=\frac{-1}{V_{\rm sub}}\ln\frac{Z(\theta)}{Z(0)}=\frac{-1}{V_{\rm sub}}\ln\,\langle\,\cos\left(\theta\,Q_{\rm sub}\right)\,\rangle\ ,

(2)

\displaystyle Z(\theta)=\int\!\!{\cal D}U\,e^{-S_{g}+i\theta Q_{\rm sub}}\ ,

(3)

where
U
denotes the link variable and
S_{g}
the gauge action.
Note that the expectation value
\langle\cdots\rangle
is estimated on
the
\theta=0
configurations.
The free energy density is then obtained as the infinite volume
limit of
f_{\rm sub}(\theta)
:

\displaystyle f(\theta)=\lim_{V_{\rm sub}\to\infty}f_{\rm sub}(\theta)\ .

(4)

The goal of this study is to answer whether the spontaneous CP
violation does occur at
\theta=\pi
.
Thus, the order parameter should be useful and is calculated through

\displaystyle\frac{d\,f(\theta)}{d\,\theta}=\lim_{V_{\rm sub}\rightarrow\infty}\frac{d\,f_{\rm sub}(\theta)}{d\,\theta}\ ,

(5)

\displaystyle\frac{d\,f_{\rm sub}(\theta)}{d\,\theta}=\frac{1}{V_{\rm sub}}\frac{\left\langle Q_{\rm sub}\,\sin(\theta Q_{\rm sub})\right\rangle}{\langle\,\cos(\theta Q_{\rm sub})\,\rangle}\,.

(6)

f
and
df/d\theta
are evaluated separately and later used to make a
consistency check.

Some cautions are in order.
First, the size of the subvolume
V_{\rm sub}
cannot be arbitrary.
Obviously it has to be large enough to cover the typical correlation
length of the system, which is considered to be around

1/(aT_{c})=9.50

Giudice:2017dor
in the lattice unit.
We thus restrict the size of the subvolume to
V_{\rm sub}\geq 10^{4}
.
Suppose that
V_{\rm sub}
is large enough but
V_{\rm sub}\ll V_{\rm full}
, where

V_{\rm full}
denotes the full volume.
Then, the resulting
f(\theta)
is expected to be independent of

V_{\rm full}
, and
f_{\rm sub}
would shows a scaling behavior as a function
of
V_{\rm sub}
.
As
V_{\rm sub}
grows and approaches
V_{\rm full}
,
Q_{\rm sub}
becomes close to
an integer global
Q
, and
f_{\rm sub}
starts to converge a full volume
result, in which we are not interested as it becomes clear later.
The critical goal in this method is to identify the scaling behavior
from which the infinite volume limit is extracted.

Secondly, the observables (
2
) and (
6
) contain
the expectation value
\langle\cos(\theta Q_{\rm sub})\rangle
.
Either observable becomes incalculable when

\langle\cos(\theta Q_{\rm sub})\rangle
fluctuates across zero.
This is the sign problem in this method and sets the
\theta
dependent
upper limit on the size of
V_{\rm sub}
.
Thus, another crucial point is whether the scaling behavior is
realized before
V_{\rm sub}
reaches the upper limit.
We will return to this point in the discussion section.

\mathrm{SU}
(2) Yang-Mills theory is calculated at

V
Discussion

The symmetry of
\mathrm{SU}
(
N
) gauge theories indicates
f(\theta)=f(-\theta)
and

f(\theta)=f(\theta+2\pi)
.
In the subvolume method,
f(\theta)=f(-\theta)
is automatic from
(
2
) but the
2\pi
-periodicity is not seen in
f(\theta)
shown
in Fig.
6
.

The subvolume method is equivalent to modifying the value of
\theta

inside the subvolume.
If the difference of
\theta
is a multiple of
2\pi
and the calculation
respects the
2\pi
-periodicity, the free energy would scale as the
surface area of the subvolume when the subvolume is large enough.
The lack of
2\pi
periodicity in the free energy density should thus be
interpreted as the presence of a meta-stable vacuum for a fixed value of

\theta
(except for
\theta=\pi
where two vacua interchanged by CP
are degenerate and stable).
Thus, we expect that the meta-stable vacuum should eventually decay into
the stable one by the creation of a dynamical domain wall that attaches
to the interface.

The absence of the decay of the domain into the domain-wall in the
lattice calculation has an analog in the calculation of the static
potential
6
6

6

The similar reasoning is found for 2d CP
{}^{N\!-\!1}
-model in

KeithHynes:2008rw
.
.
The static potential is calculated by inserting a Wilson loop, and
should show the string breaking for configurations with light dynamical
quarks when the two test charges are distant enough.
But it does not occur, at least, within naive methods, and the resulting
potential sticks to the original branch even after passing the
transition point.
The probable reason is that the overlap between the original state with two
static charges and another lower energy state with two mesons are
extremely small.
We infer that the same happens to the calculation of
f(\theta)
for

\theta>\pi
and the first order phase transition is missing
7
7

7

We
expect that the subvolume method can capture second order transitions in
principle because meta-stable states do not exist.
.
It is clearly interesting to directly see the formation of the domain
wall on the lattice though it would not be straightforward.

We have mentioned the
\theta
dependent upper limit on the size of
V_{\rm sub}

in sec.
II
.
We examine the relation between the limit and

\theta\langle|Q_{\rm sub}|\rangle
at
T=0
.

and

.

VI
Summary

We developed the subvolume method, which enables us to extract the
\theta

dependence of the free energy density in 4d Yang-Mills theory not
restricted to
\theta\sim 0
.
At high temperature, the method yields
\theta
dependence consistent with
the instanton prediction, as expected, within the large uncertainty due
to the ambiguity of the scaling region.
To fix this ambiguity, it is necessary to go to larger lattices.
On the other hand, at zero temperature the sign problem arises instead,
but still
f(\theta)
could be calculated to
\theta\sim\pi
with the
systematic uncertainty under control.
Combining the numerical result with the theoretical requirement leads to
the conclusion that the vacuum of 4d
\mathrm{SU}
(2) Yang-Mills theory undergoes
spontaneous CP violation at
\theta=\pi
as large
N
theory does.
Although the overlap problem prohibits the domain-wall from being
formed, it is interesting to learn that such a object actually exists in
the Yang-Mills theory
Luscher:1978rn
.

We have tested the stability of the results by exchanging the order of
extrapolations and obtained the consistent results with enlarged
uncertainties.

In order to promote this study to the quantitative level, it is
necessary to perform lattice simulations with larger volumes and
finer lattice spacings.
Further studies will be presented in the forthcoming
paper
InProgress
.
It is a fascinating question to ask if our method is applicable for
other questions with sign problems, such as gauge theories with finite
values of the chemical potential.

While numerical results are not accurate past
\theta\sim 3\pi/2
,
there are indications that the derivative
df(\theta)/d\theta
decreases
past
\theta=\pi
, and becomes smaller near
\theta\sim 2\pi
.
This is consistent with the expectation
Yamazaki:2017ulc
that
there are two meta-stable branches of the
\mathrm{SU}
(2) theory, each of which
has
4\pi
periodicity.

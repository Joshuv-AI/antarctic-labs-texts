Torsors and Gerbes
Ashwin Deopurkar

Abstract




1.
Introduction

Let
X_{\tau}
be a site with a final object
X
. Let
A
be an abelian sheaf on
X_{\tau}
on which we have an action of a finite group
G
. Let
A^{G}
denote the sheaf of invariants. Then given an
A\mbox{-torsor}
(respectively a gerbe banded by
A
), we would like to know under what conditions it is induced from an
A^{G}\mbox{-torsor}
(respectively a gerbe banded by
A^{G}
) (Definition
4.3
). It is easy to see that it is necessary for the torsor or the gerbe to admit a lift of the group action (Definition
4.4
, Definition
4.14
). Given such a
G\mbox{-action}
, we give explicit construction of an obstruction class which is a cohomology class in
{\text{H}^{2}(G,A(X))}
for torsors and in
{\text{H}^{3}(G,A(X))}
for gerbes. We prove that if a torsor or a gerbe is induced from the invariants then the obstruction class is zero (Proposition
4.34
). Moreover, the vanishing of the obstruction class is sufficient for the reverse conclusion for torsors when the first group cohomology classes locally vanish (Definition
4.2
). Similarly for gerbes, the vanishing of the obstruction class in
{\text{H}^{3}(G,A(X))}
is sufficient if we assume that the first two group cohomology classes locally vanish (Theorem
4.35
). The first two sheaf cohomology groups
\text{H}^{i}(X,A)
can be realized concretely as the group of
A\mbox{-torsors}
and the group of abelian gerbes banded by
A
. Using this realization and the notion of obstruction classes, we describe morphisms of cohomologies in low degrees which arise from a variant of the Hoschild-Serre spectral sequence.

As an application we obtain the following result (Proposition
7.1
). Let
X
be a scheme such that
\text{H}^{2}_{\acute{e}t}(X,\mathbf{G}_{m})
is zero. Suppose
{\widetilde{X}\xrightarrow{\pi}X}
is a finite Galois étale cover with Galois group
G
. Then for any homomorphism
{\Phi:G\to\text{Pic}(X)}
, the group cohomology class of
{\{\pi^{*}\Phi(g)\}_{g\in G}}
in
{\text{H}^{1}(G,\text{Pic}(\widetilde{X}))}
is zero. In the case of a double cover of a curve over an algebraically closed field, this was observed by Mumford in the context of theta characteristics (See Lemma 1,
[
3
]
). Since our proof uses purely cohomological methods, it also applies when
X
is a stacky curve (for example a twisted curve) as long as
{\text{H}^{2}_{\acute{e}t}(X,G_{m})}
is zero. Although we do not delve into it in this article, these considerations are related to cup products in sheaf cohomology (See Section 2,
[
1
]
). We use this to study Weil pairing on twisted curves in our upcoming paper.

2.
Preliminaries

We use notations and conventions as in Stacks Project (
[
5
,
Tag 0013
]
) for categories and sites (
[
5
,
Tag 00VG
]
). Let
X_{\tau}
be a site with a final object
X
. Let
A
be an abelian sheaf on
X_{\tau}
. By
\text{H}^{i}(X,-)
we mean the sheaf cohomology on the site
X_{\tau}
.

Definition 2.1
.

An
A\mbox{-torsor}

y
on
X
is a sheaf of sets
y
on
X_{\tau}
together with a group action
{A\times y\to y}
such that

•

For every object
U
in
X_{\tau}
, the map
A(U)\times y(U)\to y(U)
is simply transitive whenever
y(U)
is non-empty.

•

For every object
U
in
X_{\tau}
, there exists a cover
\{U_{i}\to U\}_{i\in I}
such that
y(U_{i})
is non-empty for every
i\in I
.

We say
y
is a pseudo
A\mbox{-torsor}
, if it satisfies the above conditions except possibly the last. The sheaf
A
itself is an
A\mbox{-torsor}
with the action given by the group law. We refer to
A
as the trivial torsor.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
be abelian sheaves on
X
. Let
y_{1}
,
y_{2}
be an
A_{1}\mbox{-torsor}
and an
A_{2}\mbox{-torsor}
, respectively. Suppose we have a map
{f:A_{1}\to A_{2}}
of abelian sheaves. Then an
f\mbox{-equivariant}
morphism
{\lambda:y_{1}\to y_{2}}
is a morphism of sheaves such that the following diagram commutes.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A_{1}\times y_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces y_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A_{2}\times y_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces y_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f\times\lambda}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lambda}

An
A\mbox{-equivariant}
morphism or a morphism of
A\mbox{-torsors}
is a morphism of sheaves which is equivariant with respect to the identity map from
A
to itself.

The category
\underline{A\mbox{-torsors}}
is defined as follows (See
[
5
,
Tag 036Z
]
). The objects of
\underline{A\mbox{-torsors}}
are pairs
(U,y)
where
U
is an object of
X_{\tau}
and
y
is an
A|_{U}
-torsor on
X_{\tau}/U
. A morphism
{(U,x)\to(V,y)}
is a pair
(f,\lambda)
where
{f:U\to V}
is a morphism in
X_{\tau}
and
\lambda:f^{-1}y\to x
is a morphism of
A|_{U}
-torsors on
X_{\tau}/U
. The category
\underline{A\mbox{-torsors}}
is a stack in groupoids over
X_{\tau}
(See
[
5
,
Tag 04UJ
]
).

Definition 2.3
.

An abelian gerbe
\mathscr{Y}
over
X
is a fibered category
\mathscr{Y}
over
X_{\tau}
such that the following holds:

•

\mathscr{Y}
is a stack in groupoids over
X_{\tau}
.

•

For any object
U
of
X_{\tau}
, and
x,y\in\text{Ob}(\mathscr{Y}_{U})
, there exists a cover
\{U_{i}\to U\}
of
U
such that
x|_{U_{i}},y|_{U_{i}}
are isomorphic in
\mathscr{Y}_{U_{i}}
.

•

For any object
U
of
X_{\tau}
, and
x\in\text{Ob}(\mathscr{Y}_{U})
, the sheaf of groups
\text{Aut}(x)
on
X_{\tau}/U
is abelian.

•

For any object
U
of
X_{\tau}
, there exists a cover
\{U_{i}\to U\}
of
U
such that the fiber categories
\mathscr{Y}_{U_{i}}
are non-empty.

A morphism of abelian gerbes over
X
is a morphism of fibered categories over
X_{\tau}
. We say
\mathscr{Y}
is a pseudo abelian gerbe over
X
if it satisfies all the above conditions except possibly the last. Since we only consider gerbes which are abelian, we sometimes drop the adjective.

Let
\mathscr{Y}
be a gerbe on
X
. Then there is an associated sheaf of abelian groups denoted by
\text{Band}(\mathscr{Y})
on
X_{\tau}
with the following property. For every object
U
of
X_{\tau}
, and
x\in\text{Ob}(\mathscr{Y}_{U})
, there is an isomorphism
{\text{Band}(\mathscr{Y})|_{U}\to\text{Aut}(x)}
such that for every morphism
{\psi:x\to y}
in
\mathscr{Y}_{U}
, the following diagram commutes:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{Band}(\mathscr{Y})(U)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{Band}(\mathscr{Y})(U)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{Aut}(x)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{Aut}(y)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces=}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha\to\psi\circ\alpha\circ\psi^{-1}}

A morphism of abelian gerbes over
X
induces a group homomorphism of the associated bands. A gerbe banded by
A
is an abelian gerbe
\mathscr{Y}
over
X
together with an isomorphism of sheaves
{\text{Band}(\mathscr{Y})\to A}
. Let
\mathscr{Y}_{1},\mathscr{Y}_{2}
be two gerbes over
X
banded by
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
respectively. Suppose
{f:A_{1}\to A_{2}}
is a group homomorphism of sheaves. An
f\mbox{-equivariant}
morphism
{\mathscr{Y}_{1}\to\mathscr{Y}_{2}}
is a morphism of gerbes
\mathscr{Y}_{1}\to\mathscr{Y}_{2}
such that the associated morphism of their bands fits in the following commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{Band}(\mathscr{Y}_{1})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{Band}(\mathscr{Y}_{2})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

An
A\mbox{-equivariant}
(or just equivariant) morphism of gerbes banded by
A
is a morphism which is equivariant with respect to the identity map of
A
. As in the case of torsors, equivariant morphisms of gerbes are isomorphisms.

Definition 2.4
.

If
Y_{1},Y_{2}
are categories fibered over a category
C
, then
{\text{Hom}_{C}(Y_{1},Y_{2})}
is a category whose objects are morphisms
Y_{1}\to Y_{2}
of fibered categories and the morphisms are base preserving natural transformations. If
Y_{1}
and
Y_{2}
are fibered in groupoids over
C
, then the category
\text{Hom}_{C}(Y_{1},Y_{2})
is a groupoid (See Chapter 3,
[
4
]
).

Remark 2.5
.

Suppose
\mathscr{Y}_{1}
and
\mathscr{Y}_{2}
are abelian gerbes over
X
banded by
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
, respectively. If
F,G:\mathscr{Y}_{1}\to\mathscr{Y}_{2}
are morphisms of gerbes which are isomorphic in the category
\text{Hom}_{X}(\mathscr{Y}_{1},\mathscr{Y}_{2})
, then they induce identical map between their bands.

Torsors and gerbes have functorial constructions in the sense of the following proposition:

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
{\psi:A_{1}\to A_{2}}
be a group homomorphism of abelian sheaves on
X
. Suppose we are given an
A_{1}\mbox{-torsor}

y_{1}
on
X
and an abelian gerbe
\mathscr{Y}_{1}
on
X
which is banded by
A_{1}
. Then we have the following:

(1)

There exists an
A_{2}\mbox{-torsor}

y_{2}
and a
\psi\mbox{-equivariant}
morphism
{f:y_{1}\to y_{2}}
. Moreover, if
y_{2}^{\prime}
is another
\operatorname{A_{2}-torsor}
with a
\psi\mbox{-equivariant}
map
{f^{\prime}:y_{1}\to y_{2}}
, then there is a unique
A_{2}\mbox{-equivariant}
map
{h:y_{2}\to y_{2}^{\prime}}
such that
{h\circ f=f^{\prime}}
.

(2)

There exists an abelian gerbe
\mathscr{Y}_{2}
banded by
A_{2}
and a
\psi\mbox{-equivariant}
morphism of gerbes
F:\mathscr{Y}_{1}\to\mathscr{Y}_{2}
.

Suppose
\mathscr{Y}_{2}^{\prime}
is another gerbe banded by
A_{2}
and admits a
\psi\mbox{-equivariant}
morphism
{F^{\prime}:\mathscr{Y}_{1}\to\mathscr{Y}_{2}^{\prime}}
. Then there exists an
A_{2}\mbox{-equivariant}
morphism
{H:\mathscr{Y}_{2}\to\mathscr{Y}_{2}^{\prime}}
such that
H\circ F
is isomorphic
F^{\prime}
in the category
\text{Hom}_{X_{\tau}}(\mathscr{Y}_{1},\mathscr{Y}_{2}^{\prime})
. Furthermore,
H
is unique up to an isomorphism in
\text{Hom}_{X_{\tau}}(\mathscr{Y}_{2},\mathscr{Y}_{2}^{\prime})
.

The above proposition also allows to define an abelian group structure on the equivariant isomorphism classes of
A\mbox{-torsors}
and gerbes banded by
A
. Suppose
y
and
y^{\prime}
are
A\mbox{-torsor}
on
X
. Then the product sheaf
y\times y^{\prime}
is an
(A\times A)\mbox{-torsors}
on
X
. We have the map
{A\times A\to A}
given by the group law. By the above proposition, there exists a unique (up to an isomorphism)
A\mbox{-torsor}

y\otimes y^{\prime}
with a morphism
{y\times y^{\prime}\to y\otimes y^{\prime}}
that is equivariant with respect to the group law. It is easy to verify that the operation thus constructed defines an abelian group structure on the isomorphism classes of
A\mbox{-torsors}
. A similar construction can be made for gerbes banded by
A
.

Theorem 2.7
.

The sheaf cohomology group
\text{H}^{1}(X,A)
is isomorphic to the group of
A\mbox{-equivariant}
isomorphism classes of
A\mbox{-torsors}
. Similarly, the sheaf cohomology group
\text{H}^{2}(X,A)
is isomorphic to the group of
A\mbox{-equivariant}
isomorphism classes of abelian gerbes banded by
A
.

3.
More on Gerbes

The category of gerbes over
X
is a
2\mbox{-category}
where
2\mbox{-morphisms}
are as in Definition
2.4
. Suppose
{F,H:\mathscr{Y}_{1}\to\mathscr{Y}_{2}}
are morphisms of gerbes over
X
. We say
F
and
H
are
2\mbox{-isomorphic}
if they are isomorphic objects in the category
\text{Hom}_{X_{\tau}}(\mathscr{Y}_{1},\mathscr{Y}_{2})
.

The category
\underline{A\mbox{-torsors}}
is an abelian gerbe over
X
whose band is isomorphic to
A
. We refer to it as the trivial gerbe banded by
A
. We fix a choice of pullbacks for the fibered category
\underline{A\mbox{-torsors}}
over
X_{\tau}
. Suppose
y
is an
A\mbox{-torsor}
on
X
and
(U,z)
is an object of
\underline{A\mbox{-torsors}}
. Then the sheaf
\text{Isom}(y|_{U},z)
is a torsor over the group sheaf
{\text{Isom}(y|_{U},y|_{U})\cong A|_{U}}
. The assignment
{(U,z)\to(U,\text{Isom}(y|_{U},z))}
defines an
A\mbox{-equivariant}
morphism of gerbes banded by
A
. We denote this morphism by
\text{Isom}(y,-)
. More generally, if
\mathscr{Y}
is a gerbe banded by
A
that admits a global section
y
, then the isom functor as defined above gives an
A\mbox{-equivariant}
isomorphism of gerbes
{\mathscr{Y}\to\underline{A\mbox{-torsors}}}
. Thus, as in the case of torsors, a gerbe which admits a global section is isomorphic to the trivial gerbe.

Remark 3.1
.

Any
A\mbox{-equivariant}
morphism of gerbes
{\mathscr{Y}\to\underline{A\mbox{-torsors}}}
is
2\mbox{-isomorphic}
to
\text{Isom}(y,-)
for some global section
y
of
\mathscr{Y}
.

Remark 3.2
.

If
y_{1}
and
y_{2}
are two
A\mbox{-torsors}
on
X
then the composite morphism

\text{Isom}(y_{1},-)\circ\text{Isom}(y_{2},-):\underline{A\mbox{-torsors}}\to\underline{A\mbox{-torsors}}

is
2\mbox{-isomorphic}
to the morphism
{\text{Isom}(y_{1}\otimes y_{2},-)}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\mathscr{Y}_{1}
and
\mathscr{Y}_{2}
be gerbes over
X
banded by
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
, respectively. Suppose we have a morphism
F:\mathscr{Y}_{1}\to\mathscr{Y}_{2}

of gerbes. Then the automorphism group of the object
F
in the category
\text{Hom}_{X_{\tau}}(\mathscr{Y}_{1},\mathscr{Y}_{2})
is isomorphic to the global sections
A_{2}(X)
of the band of
\mathscr{Y}_{2}
. Suppose we use overline to denote this isomorphism. Then for any
2\mbox{-morphisms}

{\alpha:F\to F}
and
{\gamma:G\to F}
, we have
\overline{\alpha}=\overline{\gamma\circ\alpha\circ\gamma^{-1}}
.

Proof.

Suppose
{\{U_{i}\to X\}_{i\in I}}
is a cover of
X
such that the fiber categories
{{\mathscr{Y}_{1}}_{U_{i}}}
are non-empty. Choose
{x_{i}\in\text{Ob}({\mathscr{Y}_{1}}_{U_{i}})}
. A base preserving natural transformation from
F
to itself gives an automorphism
F(x_{i})\to F(x_{i})
in the fiber category
{\mathscr{Y}_{2}}_{U_{i}}
for each
i\in I
. Since
\text{Aut}(F(x_{i}))|_{U_{i}}
is isomorphic to
{A_{2}}|_{U_{i}}
, we get a section
a_{i}
of the sheaf
A_{2}
over
U_{i}
. For a pair of indices
i,j\in I
, there exists a cover
{\{U_{k}\to U_{i}\times_{X}U_{J}\}_{k\in I_{i,j}}}
of
U_{i}\times_{X}U_{j}
where the objects
x_{i},x_{j}
are isomorphic. For
k\in I_{i,j}
, after choosing an isomorphism
\beta:x_{i}|_{U_{k}}\to x_{j}|_{U_{k}}
in the category
{\mathscr{Y}_{1}}_{U_{k}}
, we get the following commutative diagram of arrows in the category
{\mathscr{Y}_{2}}_{U_{k}}
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F(x_{i}|_{U_{k}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F(x_{i}|_{U_{k}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F(x_{j}|_{U_{k}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F(x_{j}|_{U_{k}})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces a_{i}|_{U_{k}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F(\beta)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F(\beta)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces a_{j}|_{U_{k}}}

Therefore we conclude that
a_{i}|_{U_{k}}=a_{j}|_{U_{k}}
for each
k\in I_{i,j}
. By the sheaf condition for the cover
{\{U_{k}\to U_{i}\times_{X}U_{J}\}_{k\in I_{i,j}}}
we see that
{a_{i}|_{U_{i}\times_{X}U_{J}}=a_{j}|_{U_{i}\times_{X}U_{J}}}
for every
i,j\in I
. Using the sheaf condition again but for the cover
{\{U_{i}\to X\}_{i\in I}}
we conclude that there exists a global section
a\in A_{2}(X)
whose restriction to
U_{i}
is
a_{i}
for each
i\in I
. In other words, a base preserving transformation from
F
to itself is given by the action of a global section of
A_{2}
. The second part of the lemma follows easily from this observation.

∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\mathscr{Y}_{1},\mathscr{Y}_{2},
and
\mathscr{Y}_{3}
be abelian gerbes on
X
banded by
A_{1},A_{2},
and
A_{3}
respectively. Let
{G:\mathscr{Y}_{1}\to\mathscr{Y}_{2}}
and
{F:\mathscr{Y}_{2}\to\mathscr{Y}_{3}}
be morphisms of gerbes. Let
{\delta_{F}:A_{2}\to A_{3}}
denote the map induced by
F
on the bands of the gerbes. Let

{\alpha:F\to F}
and
{\beta:G\to G}

be 2-morphisms. Then with the notation as in Lemma
3.3
, we have

{\overline{\alpha\star\beta}=\delta_{F}(\overline{\beta})\cdot\overline{\alpha}}
.

Proof.

For
U\in\text{Ob}(X_{\tau})
and
x\in\text{Ob}({\mathscr{Y}_{1}}_{U_{i}})
, the
2\mbox{-morphism}

{\alpha\star\beta:F\circ G\to F\circ G}
is locally given by the commutative diagram below:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F(G(x))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F(G(x))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F(G(x))}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F(\beta)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha*\beta}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha}

It follows from the proof of Lemma
3.3
that this corresponds to the element
\delta_{F}(\overline{\beta})\cdot\overline{\alpha}
.
∎

4.
Group actions

We use additive notation for the group structure of
A
. Let
G
be a finite group. By a (left) action of the group
G
on
A
we mean a group homomorphism
{G^{opp}\to\text{Isom}_{Ab(X_{\tau})}(A,A)}
.

Definition 4.1
.

Suppose the group
G
acts on the abelian sheaf
A
by automorphisms
{\{\rho_{g}\}_{g\in G}}
. The invariants of
A
is the sheaf defined by the assignment
{U\to A(U)^{G}}
. We denote this sheaf by
A^{G}
. We have a map
{i:A^{G}\to A}
such that for every
g\in G
, we have
{\rho_{g}\circ i=i}
.

Definition 4.2
.

Suppose we have an action of the group
G
on
A
. For an index
j>0
we say that “the
j^{th}
group cohomology locally vanishes” if the following holds: For every object
U
of
X_{\tau}
, there exists a cover
{\{U_{i}\to U\}_{i\in I}}
such that for all
i\in I
, the group cohomology
{\text{H}^{j}(G,A(U_{i}))}
is zero.

We say that “the
j^{th}
group cohomology classes locally vanish” if the following holds: For every object
U
of
X_{\tau}
, and a group cohomology class
\alpha\in\text{H}^{j}(G,A(U))
, there exists a cover
{\{U_{i}\to U\}_{i\in I}}
such that for all
i\in I
, the restriction of
\alpha
to
{\text{H}^{j}(G,A(U_{i}))}
is zero. It is clear the latter condition is weaker than the former.

Definition 4.3
.

Suppose we have a group action by
G
on the abelian sheaf
A
. Let
A^{G}\xrightarrow{i}A
denote the sheaf of invariants. We say an
A\mbox{-torsor}

y
is induced from the invariants if there exists an
A^{G}\mbox{-torsor}

\overline{y}
and an
i\mbox{-equivariant}
morphism
\overline{y}\to y
.

Suppose
\mathscr{Y}
is an abelian gerbe banded by
A
. We say
\mathscr{Y}
is induced from the invariants if there exists a gerbe
\overline{\mathscr{Y}}
banded by
A^{G}
and an
i\mbox{-equivariant}
morphism of gerbes

{\overline{\mathscr{Y}}\to\mathscr{Y}}
.

Definition 4.4
.

Let
y
be an
A\mbox{-torsor}
on
X
. By an action of
G
on
y
we mean an assignment of a morphism
{\lambda_{g}:y\to y}
for every
g\in G

which is equivariant with respect to an automorphism
{\phi_{g}:A\to A}
of
A
. Moreover, we require that the automorphisms
\{\phi_{g}\}_{g\in G}
define a (left)
G\mbox{-action}
on
A
. We refer to the action on
A
as the underlying action. Given the underlying action on
A
, a lift of the action on
y
is a
G\mbox{-action}
on
y
whose underlying action on
A
is the given action.

Definition 4.5
.

Let
{f:A_{1}\to A_{2}}
be a morphism of abelian sheaves on
X
. Let
y_{1}
(resp.
y_{2}
) be an
A_{1}\mbox{-torsor}
(resp.
A_{2}\mbox{-torsor}
) on
X
. Suppose we have an action of
G
on
y_{1}
given by morphisms
{\{\lambda_{g}^{1}\}_{g\in G}}
and also on
y_{2}
given by morphisms
{\{\lambda_{g}^{2}\}_{g\in G}}
. By a
G\mbox{-equivariant}
morphism
{\lambda:y_{1}\to y_{2}}
we mean a map of torsors
{\lambda:y_{1}\to y_{2}}
such that it is
f\mbox{-equivariant}
and for
g\in G
, the following diagram commutes:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces y_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces y_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces y_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces y_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lambda_{g}^{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lambda}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lambda_{g}^{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lambda}

It is easy to see that if such a
G\mbox{-equivariant}
morphisms exists, then
f
is a
G\mbox{-equivariant}
map of sheaves with respect to the underlying actions on
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
.

Definition 4.6
.

Suppose
y
is an
A\mbox{-torsor}
with an action of
G
given by morphisms
\{\lambda_{g}\}_{g\in G}
as in Definition
4.4
. Then for
g,h\in G
, we denote by
\chi_{\lambda}(g,h)\in A(X)
the scalar that corresponds to the unique
A\mbox{-equivariant}
map which makes the diagram below commute.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces y}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lambda_{h}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lambda_{gh}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\chi_{\lambda}(g,h)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lambda_{g}}

We regard

as a

with values in

.

We want to prove that in the situation of Definition

, the cochain
\chi_{\lambda}
is a cocycle with respect to the underlying action on

. Moreover, its cohomology class in

is independent of the chosen lift of the

on

.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
y
be an
A\mbox{-torsor}
. Suppose we have two
G\mbox{-actions}
on
y
given by morphisms
{\{\lambda_{g}^{1}\}_{g\in G}}
and
{\{\lambda_{g}^{2}\}_{g\in G}}
such that their underlying action on

is identical. Then the two cochains
{\chi_{\lambda^{1}}}
and
{\chi_{\lambda^{2}}}
differ by a coboundary. Conversely, given any
2\mbox{-cochain}

\chi
which is cohomologous to
\chi_{\lambda^{1}}
, there exists a lift of the action of
G
such that the corresponding cochain is
\chi
.

Proof.

It follows from Proposition
2.6
that for
g\in G
, there exists an
A\mbox{-equivariant}
map given by

such that
\lambda^{2}_{g}=a_{g}\circ\lambda^{1}_{g}
. Then for

, we see that the
{\chi_{\lambda^{1}}(g,h)}
and
{\chi_{\lambda^{2}}(g,h)}
differ by
{(g\cdot a_{h})-a_{gh}+a_{g}}
which is the coboundary of the
1\mbox{-cochain}

\{a_{g}\}_{g\in G}
. The second part of the lemma follows easily by suitably altering the action on

.

∎

Lemma 4.8
.

Suppose we have a
G\mbox{-equivariant}
morphism of torsors
{\lambda:y_{1}\to y_{2}}
as in Definition
4.5
. Then we have
\chi_{\lambda^{2}}=f\circ\chi_{\lambda^{1}}
.

Proof.

For
g,h\in G
, we have

\displaystyle\lambda\circ\left(\chi_{\lambda^{1}}(g,h)\circ\lambda^{1}_{gh}\right)

\displaystyle=\lambda\circ\left(\lambda^{1}_{g}\circ\lambda^{1}_{h}\right)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2}_{g}\circ\lambda^{2}_{h}\circ\lambda

\displaystyle=\left(\chi_{\lambda^{2}}(g,h)\circ\lambda^{2}_{gh}\right)\circ\lambda

\displaystyle=\chi_{\lambda^{2}}(g,h)\circ\lambda\circ\lambda^{1}_{gh}

Since
\lambda^{1}_{gh}
is an isomorphism, we conclude that the diagram below commutes.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces y_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces y_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces y_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces y_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\chi_{\lambda^{1}}(g,h)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lambda}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\chi_{\lambda^{2}}(g,h)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lambda}

Therefore, it follows that we have
f(\chi_{\lambda^{1}}(g,h))=\chi_{\lambda^{2}}(g,h)
.
∎

Situation 4.9
.

Suppose we have an action of
G
on an
A\mbox{-torsor}

y
as in Definition
4.4
. Suppose
U\in\text{Ob}(X_{\tau})
is such that
y
admits a section
x\in y(U)
. For
g\in G
, we set

to be the unique scalar such that
{\lambda_{g}(x)=c_{g}\cdot x}
.

Lemma 4.10
.

In the above Situation
4.9
, we have the following:

(1)

The
1\mbox{-cochain}

\{c_{g}\}_{g\in G}
with values in
A(U)
is independent of the chosen section

up to a coboundary.

(2)

The restriction of
\chi_{\lambda}
to
U
is the coboundary of the
1\mbox{-cochain}

\{c_{g}\}_{g\in G}
.

Proof.

For
c\in A(U)
, we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{g}(c\cdot x)

\displaystyle=(g\cdot c)\cdot(\lambda_{g}(x))

\displaystyle=\left(g\cdot c+c_{g}\right)\cdot x

\displaystyle=\left(g\cdot c+c_{g}-c\right)\cdot(c\cdot x)

This proves first part of the lemma.
For
g,h\in G
, we see that

\displaystyle\left(\lambda_{g}\circ\lambda_{h}\right)(x)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{g}\left(c_{h}\cdot x\right)

\displaystyle=\left(c_{g}+(g\cdot c_{h})\right)\cdot x

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\left(\lambda_{g}\circ\lambda_{h}\right)(x)

\displaystyle=\left(\chi_{\lambda}(g,h)\circ\lambda_{gh}\right)(x)

\displaystyle=(\chi_{\lambda}(g,h)+c_{gh})\cdot x

Thus we see that

\chi_{\lambda}(g,h)+c_{gh}=c_{g}+(g\cdot c_{h}).

In other words,
\chi_{\lambda}(g,h)
restricted to
U
is the
coboundary of
\{c_{g}\}_{g\in G}
.
∎

Proposition 4.11
.

Suppose we have an action of
G
on an
A\mbox{-torsor}

y
as in Definition
4.4
. Then
\chi_{\lambda}
is a

.

Proof.

There exists a cover
{\{U_{i}\to X\}_{i\in I}}
of

such that

admits section on each

. By Lemma

, we see that
the restriction of
\chi_{\lambda}
on each

is a coboundary and therefore its coboundary vanishes on each
U_{i}
. Thus we see that
\chi_{\lambda}
is a cocycle.
∎

Suppose

is an

and we have an action

on

given by morphisms
{\{\lambda_{g}\}_{g\in G}}
. For an object

of

.

.

Now we shift our attention to gerbes. We use the following terminology. Suppose

are morphisms of gerbes. We say
F
and

2-commute if they are isomorphic in the category
{\text{Hom}_{X}(\mathscr{Y}_{1},\mathscr{Y}_{2})}
. Similarly, we say a diagram of 1-morphisms 2-commutes, if the arrows with the same source and the target are 2-isomorphic.

Let
\mathscr{Y}
be an abelian gerbe banded by
A
.

Definition 4.14
.

An action of
G
on
\mathscr{Y}
is an assignment of a 1-morphism
{F_{g}:\mathscr{Y}\to\mathscr{Y}}
for every

such that the following holds:

•

A\mbox{-equivariant}
.

•

For every
g,h\in G
, the morphisms
F_{g\cdot h}
and
F_{g}\circ F_{h}
2-commute.

By “connecting 2-morphisms” we mean a choice of 2-morphisms
{\alpha_{g,h}:F_{g\cdot h}\to F_{g}\circ F_{h}}
for every
g,h\in G
. If we have a
G\mbox{-action}
and connecting 2-morphisms as above, then we denote this data by the pair
(F,\alpha)
. Given the action on
\mathscr{Y}
, we have an action of
G
on
A
such that for all
g\in G
, the morphism
F_{g}
is equivariant with respect to the action of
g
on
A
. We refer to this action as the underlying action of

.

Definition 4.15
.

Suppose
(F,\alpha)
is the data of
G

together with connecting

. Then for

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{g_{1}\cdot g_{2}\cdot g_{3}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{g_{1}}\circ F_{g_{2}\cdot g_{3}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{g_{1}}\circ F_{g_{2}}\circ F_{g_{3}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{g_{1}\cdot g_{2}\cdot g_{3}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{g_{1}\cdot g_{2}}\circ F_{g_{3}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{g_{1}}\circ F_{g_{2}}\circ F_{g_{3}}}

For

, there exists an automorphism of

corresponding to an element
{c(g,h)\in A(X)}
such that the following diagram of arrows in

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{g\circ h}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{g}\circ F_{h}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{g\circ h}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha^{1}_{g,h}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces c(g,h)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha^{2}_{g,h}}

We write

and see that the difference between

This is the coboundary of the 2-cochain

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\omega(g_{1},g_{2},g_{3})}

Proof.

We have two morphisms

given by

{F_{g_{1}}(\gamma(g_{2},g_{3}))\circ\,\gamma(g_{1},g_{2}g_{3})\circ\,\phi_{g_{1}g_{2}g_{3}}}\text{ and }{\omega(g_{1},g_{2},g_{3})\circ\,\gamma(g_{1}g_{2},g_{3})\circ\,\phi_{g_{1}g_{2}g_{3}}}.

It is straightforward to verify that both of these morphisms are equal to the morphism below:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{g_{1}}(y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(F_{g_{1}}\circ F_{g_{2}})(y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(F_{g_{1}}\circ F_{g_{2}}\circ F_{g_{3}})(y)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{g_{1}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{g_{1}}(\phi_{g_{2}})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(F_{g_{1}}\circ F_{g_{2}})(\phi_{g_{3}})}

Since
\phi_{g_{1}g_{2}g_{3}}
is an isomorphism, the conclusion follows.
∎

Lemma 4.22
.

In Situation
4.20
we have the following:

(1)

The
2\mbox{-cochain}

\{c(g,h)\}_{g,h\in G}
is independent of the isomorphisms
\{\phi_{g}\}_{g\in G}
up to a coboundary.

(2)

If
y^{\prime}\in\text{Ob}(\mathscr{Y}_{U})
is an object isomorphic to
y
, then there exist isomorphisms
{\{\phi^{\prime}_{g}:y^{\prime}\to F_{g}(y^{\prime})\}}
for which the resulting
2\mbox{-cochain}
is identical to
{\{c(g,h)\}_{g,h\in G}}
.

(3)

The restriction of
\kappa_{(F,\alpha)}
to
U
is the
coboundary of the
2\mbox{-cochain}

\{c(g,h)\}_{g,h\in G}
.

Proof.

A different choice of isomorphisms is given by
\{a_{g}\cdot\phi_{g}\}_{g\in G}
, where
\{a_{g}\}_{g\in G}
is a
1\mbox{-cochain}
with values in
A(U)
. It is straightforward to see that this alters the cochain
\{c(g,h)\}_{g,h}
by the coboundary of
\{a_{g}\}_{g\in G}
.

Suppose
y^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\lambda}y
is an isomorphism in
\mathscr{Y}_{U}
. Then we can choose
{\phi^{\prime}_{g}:y^{\prime}\to\phi_{g}(y^{\prime})}
such following diagram commutes:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{g}(y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces y^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{g}(y^{\prime})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lambda}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi_{g}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{g}(\lambda)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi^{\prime}_{g}}

Then the resulting cochain for
y^{\prime}
is identical to
\{c(g,h)\}_{g,h\in G}
.

Let
g_{1},g_{2},g_{3}\in G
be three group elements. Since
{\alpha_{g_{1},g_{2}}:F_{g_{1}g_{2}}\to F_{g_{1}}\circ F_{g_{2}}}
is a natural transformation, we see that the following diagram commutes:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{g_{1}g_{2}}(y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{g_{1}g_{2}}\circ F_{g_{3}}(y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{g_{1}}\circ F_{g_{2}}(y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{g_{1}}\circ F_{g_{2}}\circ F_{g_{3}}(y)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha_{g_{1},g_{2}}(y)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{g_{1}g_{2}}(\phi_{g_{3}})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha_{g_{1},g_{2}}(F_{g_{3}}(y))}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{g_{1}}\circ F_{g_{2}}(\phi_{g_{3}})}

We note that
{\alpha_{g_{1},g_{2}}(F_{g_{3}}(y))=(\alpha_{g_{1},g_{2}}\star id_{F_{g_{3}}})(y)}
so that with

4.21
, we have
{\omega(g_{1},g_{2},g_{3})=a(g_{1},g_{2})\cdot\left(\alpha_{g_{1},g_{2}}\star id_{F_{g_{3}}}\right)(y)}
. Also, we have
{F_{g_{1}}(\alpha_{g_{2},g_{3}}(y))=\left(id_{F_{g_{1}}}\star\alpha_{g_{2},g_{3}}\right)(y)}
. By Lemma
4.21
we have

F_{g_{1}}(\gamma(g_{2},g_{3}))\circ\,\gamma(g_{1},g_{2}g_{3})=\omega(g_{1},g_{2},g_{3})\circ\,\gamma(g_{1}g_{2},g_{3})

where we make the following substitutions:

\displaystyle\gamma(g_{1},g_{2}g_{3})

\displaystyle=c(g_{1},g_{2}g_{3})\cdot\left(\alpha_{g_{1},g_{2}}(y)\right)

\displaystyle F_{g_{1}}(\gamma(g_{2},g_{3}))

\displaystyle=F_{g_{1}}(c(g_{2},g_{3})\cdot\alpha(g_{2},g_{3})(y))

\displaystyle\gamma(g_{1}g_{2},g_{3})

\displaystyle=c(g_{1}g_{2},g_{3})\cdot\left(\alpha_{g_{1}g_{2},g_{3}}(y)\right)

\displaystyle\omega(g_{1},g_{2},g_{3})

\displaystyle=a(g_{1},g_{2})\cdot\left(\alpha_{g_{1},g_{2}}\star id_{F_{g_{3}}}\right)(y)

Then, we see that (See Lemma

)

\kappa_{F,\alpha}(g_{1},g_{2},g_{3})|_{U}=c(g_{1},g_{2}g_{3})+\left(g_{1}\cdot c(g_{2},g_{3})\right)-c(g_{1}g_{2},g_{3})-c(g_{1},g_{2}).

This proves the last part of the lemma.

Definition 4.24
.

Let
y
be an
A\mbox{-torsor}
on
X
and let
\mathscr{Y}
be a gerbe on
X
which is banded by
A
. Given an action of the group
G
on
y
we refer to the associated cohomology class in
{\text{H}^{2}(G,A(X))}
as the obstruction cohomology class. Similarly, given an action of
G
on
\mathscr{Y}
we refer the associated cohomology class in
{\text{H}^{3}(G,A(X))}
as the obstruction cohomology class.

Previously, given an
A\mbox{-torsor}
together with a
G\mbox{-action}
, we defined a pseudo torsor over the invariants. Following is the analogous construction for gerbes. Let
\mathscr{Y}
be a gerbe banded by
A
. Suppose
(F,\alpha)
is the data of an action of
G
on
\mathscr{Y}
together with connecting
2\mbox{-morphisms}
. We define a category
\mathscr{Y}_{(F,\alpha)}
as follows. An object of
\mathscr{Y}_{(F,\alpha)}
is a tuple
{(U,y,\{\phi_{g}\}_{g\in G})}
where

•

U\in\text{Ob}(X_{\tau})
is an object of
X_{\tau}
.

•

y\in\text{Ob}(\mathscr{Y}_{U})
is an object of
\mathscr{Y}
over
U
.

•

For
g\in G
,
{\phi_{g}:y\to F_{g}(y)}
is a morphism in
\mathscr{Y}_{U}
such that for
g,h\in G
, the following diagram commutes:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{g\cdot h}(y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{g}(y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left(F_{g}\circ F_{h}\right)(y)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi_{g}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi_{g\cdot h}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha_{g,h}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{g}(\phi_{h})}

A morphism
{(U_{1},y_{1},\{\phi^{1}_{g}\}_{g\in G})\to(U_{2},y_{2},\{\phi^{2}_{g}\}_{g\in G})}
in
\mathscr{Y}_{(F,\alpha)}
is a pair
(f,\lambda)
, where
{f:U_{1}\to U_{2}}
is a morphism in
X_{\tau}
, and
{\lambda:y_{1}\to y_{2}}
is a morphism in
\mathscr{Y}
over
f
such that the following diagram commutes:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces y_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces y_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{g}(y_{1})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{g}(y_{2})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi^{1}_{g}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lambda}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi^{2}_{g}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{g}(\lambda)}

We can pretend that the functors
\{F_{g}\}_{g\in G}
commute with the pull back functors (See Appendix I, Section
8
). Therefore, it is easy to see that the objects of
\mathscr{Y}_{(F,\alpha)}
can be pulled back, making it a fibered category in groupoids over
X_{\tau}
. Furthermore, we see that
\mathscr{Y}_{(F,\alpha)}
is a stack in groupoids. We have a morphism

over

which is given by
{(U,y,\{\phi_{g}\}_{g\in G})\to y}
.

Lemma 4.25
.

Suppose
{Y_{1}=\left(U,y_{1},\{\phi^{1}_{g}\}_{g\in G}\right)}
and
{Y_{2}=\left(U,y_{2},\{\phi^{2}_{g}\}_{g\in G}\right)}
are objects of
\mathscr{Y}_{(F,\alpha)}
such that there exists an isomorphism
{\lambda:y_{1}\to y_{2}}
in
\mathscr{Y}_{U}
. For
g\in G
, we let
a_{g}\in A(U)
denote the scalar such that the following diagram commutes:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces y_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{g}(y_{1})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces y_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{g}(y_{2})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lambda}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi^{1}_{g}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces a_{g}\cdot F_{g}(\lambda)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi^{2}_{g}}

Then
\{a_{g}\}_{g\in G}
is a
1\mbox{-cocycles}
with values in
A(U)
. Moreover, its cohomology class in
\text{H}^{1}(G,A(U))
is zero if and only if
Y_{1}
and
Y_{2}
are isomorphic objects of
\mathscr{Y}_{(F,\alpha)}
over
U
.

Proof.

Let
g,h\in G
be two elements of the group. We know that
a_{h}
is so that the following diagram commutes:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces y_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{h}(y_{1})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces y_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{h}(y_{2})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lambda}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi^{1}_{h}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces a_{h}\cdot F_{h}(\lambda)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi^{2}_{h}}

By applying
F_{g}
, we conclude that the following diagram commutes as well:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{g}(y_{1})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(F_{g}\circ F_{h})(y_{1})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{g}(y_{2})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(F_{g}\circ F_{h})(y_{2})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{g}(\lambda)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{g}(\phi^{1}_{h})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(g\cdot a_{h})\cdot(F_{g}\circ F_{h})(\lambda)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{g}(\phi^{2}_{h})}

Now we can verify that the following diagram commutes:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces y_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{g}(y_{1})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(F_{g}\circ F_{h})(y_{1})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces y_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{g}(y_{2})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(F_{g}\circ F_{h})(y_{2})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lambda}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi^{1}_{g}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces a_{g}\cdot F_{g}(\lambda)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{g}(\phi^{1}_{h})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(a_{g}+g\cdot a_{h})\cdot(F_{g}\circ F_{h})(\lambda)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi^{2}_{g}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{g}(\phi^{2}_{h})}

Since
{\alpha_{g,h}:F_{gh}\to F_{g}\circ F_{h}}
is a base preserving natural transformation, we see that the following diagram commutes:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces y_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{g\circ h}(y_{1})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(F_{g}\circ F_{h})(y_{1})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces y_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{g\circ h}(y_{2})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(F_{g}\circ F_{h})(y_{2})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lambda}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi^{1}_{g\circ h}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces a_{g\circ h}\cdot F_{g\circ h}(\lambda)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha_{g,h}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces a_{gh}\cdot(F_{g}\circ F_{h})(\lambda)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi^{2}_{g\circ h}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha_{g,h}}

For

, the two composite morphisms
{y_{j}\to(F_{g}\circ F_{h})(y_{j})}
, namely
{F_{g}(\phi^{j}_{h})\circ\phi^{j}_{g}}
and
{\alpha_{g,h}(y_{j})\circ\phi^{j}_{gh}}
, which appear in the above commutative diagrams are equal. Therefore, we conclude that
{a_{gh}=a_{g}+(g\cdot a_{h})}
. Thus, we see that
\{a_{g}\}_{g\in G}
is a
1\mbox{-cocycle}
.

If
\{a_{g}\}_{g\in G}
is a coboundary, then there exists
{a\in A(U)}
such that for
g\in G
, we have
{a_{g}=(g\cdot a)-a}
. Then we see that

{a\cdot\lambda:y_{1}\to y_{2}}
gives an isomorphism of
Y_{1}
and
Y_{2}
in the fiber category of
\mathscr{Y}_{(F,\alpha)}
over
U
.

∎

5.

Let
G
be a finite group and

be an abelian sheaf on

as before. In this section, we consider group actions on the trivial gerbe banded by

. Our first goal is to prove that if

acts equivariantly on the trivial gerbe (i.e. the underlying action on

is trivial), then the obstruction class is zero.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let

be an
A\mbox{-torsor}
. Suppose we are given an action of
G
on

. Let

denote the normal subgroup of
G
of those elements whose underlying action on

is trivial. Then we have an action of

on

such that the obstruction class for the

is given by inflating the obstruction class for

via the inflation map
{\text{H}^{2}(G/N,A(X))\to\text{H}^{2}(G,A(X))}
.

Proof.

Suppose the action of

on

is given by the morphisms
\{\lambda_{g}\}_{g\in G}
. Since

acts trivially on

and the obstruction class for

does not depend on the choice of the lift (Lemma

), we can assume that whenever

and

are congruent modulo

, we have
{\lambda_{g}=\lambda_{g^{\prime}}}
. Thus we get an action of
G/N
on

. Moreover, it is clear that the cochain that defines the obstruction class factors through

. Therefore it follows that the obstruction class is given by the inflation map as claimed.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\mathscr{Y}
be a gerbe banded by
A
over

. Suppose we have an action of
G
on
\mathscr{Y}
. Let
N

to the identity morphism. Then we have an action of

on
\mathscr{Y}

via the inflation map
{\text{H}^{3}(G/N,A(X))\to\text{H}^{3}(G,A(X))}
.

Let

denote the automorphisms of
\mathscr{Y}
through which

acts on
\mathscr{Y}
. Since the obstruction class only depends on the

(Corollary

{F_{g}=F_{g^{\prime}}}
whenever

and the cochain that defines the obstruction class factors through

∎

Proposition 5.3
.

Suppose
G
is a cyclic group that acts on a gerbe banded by
A
such that the underlying action on
A
is trivial. Then the associated obstruction cohomology class in
{\text{H}^{3}(G,A(X))}
is trivial.

Proof.

Let

and let

be an odd index. Since

is a cyclic group acting trivially, for

in
\text{Ob}(X_{\tau})

{\text{H}^{j}(G,A(U))\cong\{a\in A(U)|n\cdot a=0\}}

. We know that the restriction of the obstruction class is locally zero (See proof of Proposition

∎

6.
Hoschild-Serre spectral sequence

A typical situation where we have a sheaf
A
with a group action occurs in the following scenario. Suppose
X
is a scheme and we consider the small étale site of
X
. Let
{\widetilde{X}\xrightarrow{\pi}X}
be a Galois cover with the Galois group
G
. Then for any sheaf
E
on
\widetilde{X}
, the sheaf
\pi_{*}(E)
carries a natural
G
action. Moreover, the higher group cohomology locally vanishes for all indices for this action. In this section, we mimic this scenario in a more general setting of an arbitrary site.

We denote by
\underline{G}
the constant group sheaf with value
G
on
X_{\tau}
. We denote by
{G}\mbox{-{sh}}(X_{\tau})
the category whose objects are sheaves with an action of
G
and the morphisms are
{G\mbox{-equivariant}}
morphisms. Similarly, we denote by
\textit{Mod}(\mathbb{Z}[\underline{G}])
the abelian category whose objects are abelian sheaves with an action of
G
and the morphisms are
{G\mbox{-equivariant}}
morphisms of abelian sheaves. Let
M
be a left
{\underline{G}\mbox{-torsor}}
(Definition
2.1
). We have an action of
G
on
M
which is given via the sheafification map
{G\to\underline{G}}
.

Remark 6.1
.

Suppose
B
is a sheaf with an action of
G
. Thus we have a map of presheaves

{G\times B\to B}
. Since sheafification is exact, we have a bijection:

\textit{Mor}_{\textit{PSh}(X_{\tau})}(G\times B,B)\cong\textit{Mor}_{\mathit{Sh}(X_{\tau})}(\underline{G}\times B,B).

Thus we see that an action of
G
induces an action of
\underline{G}
. We can define a category whose objects are sheaves with an action of
\underline{G}
and morphisms are
\underline{G}\mbox{-equivariant}
morphisms. This category is equivalent to
{G}\mbox{-{sh}}(X_{\tau})
.

Definition 6.2
.

Suppose
E
is a sheaf on
X_{\tau}
. We define
E[M]
as the internal hom sheaf (See
[
5
,
Tag 04TP
]
) which assigns to every
U\in\text{Ob}(X_{\tau})
, the set
{\textit{Mor}_{Sh(X_{\tau}/U)}(M|_{U},E|_{U})}
. We have a left action of
G
on
E[M]
which is locally defined by
{(g\cdot f)(m)=f(g^{-1}\cdot m)}
. When
E
is an abelian sheaf, so is
E[M]
. The abelian group structure on
E[M]
is given via the evaluation map to
E
. It is clear that in this case the group
G
acts by group homomorphisms.

Lemma 6.3
.

Suppose
M\cong\underline{G}
is the trivial torsor. Then we have an isomorphism of sheaves
{E[M]\cong\prod_{g\in G}E}
. Moreover, if we let
G
act on the right hand side by permuting the indices, then the isomorphism is
G\mbox{-equivariant}
.

Proof.

By the universal property of sheafification, for
U\in\text{Ob}(X_{\tau})
, we have

\textit{Mor}_{\mathit{Psh}(X_{et})}(G|_{U},E|_{U})\cong\textit{Mor}_{\mathit{Sh}(X_{et})}(\underline{G}|_{U},E|_{U})

where by
G|_{U}
we mean the constant presheaf with value
G
on
X_{\tau}/U
. Clearly, the left hand side is equal to
{\prod_{g\in G}E|_{U}}
. These identifications glue to give the desired isomorphism as claimed.

∎

Corollary 6.4
.

Let
E
be an abelian sheaf on
X_{\tau}
. Then the group cohomology locally vanishes for all indices
j>0
for the action of
G
on
E[M]
.

Proof.

By the above Lemma
6.3
, for
U\in\text{Ob}(X_{\tau})
, we have a cover
\{U_{i}\to U\}_{i\in I}
such that
E[M]|_{U_{i}}
is isomorphic to
{\prod_{g\in G}{E}|_{U_{i}}}
where
G
acts by permuting the indices. Therefore for all
j>0
, the cohomology groups
\text{H}^{j}(G,E[M](U_{i}))
vanish.
∎

Lemma 6.5
.

The functor
{\textit{Ab}(X_{\tau})\to\textit{Mod}(\mathbb{Z}[\underline{G}])}
defined by
{E\to E[M]}
is an exact functor.

Proof.

We note that a complex in
\textit{Mod}(\mathbb{Z}[\underline{G}])
is exact if and only if the underlying complex of abelian sheaves is exact. If
M
is a trivial torsor then we have an isomorphism
{E[M]\cong\prod_{g\in G}E}
which is functorial in
E
. Since
M
is locally trivial, we conclude that the functor
E\to E[M]
is locally exact and therefore exact.

∎

Lemma 6.6
.

We have an injective morphism of sheaves
E\to E[M]
that identifies
E
as the sheaf of invariants of
E[M]
under the action of
G
.

Proof.

For an object
U
of
X_{\tau}
, we can define a map
{E(U)\to\textit{Mor}_{Sh(X_{\tau}/U)}(M|_{U},E|_{U})}
which sends an element
e
to the morphism which on each object is a constant map with the value equal to the restriction of
e
. Thus we have a morphism
{E\to E[M]}
. We know
E[M]
is locally isomorphic to
\prod_{g\in G}E
on which
G
acts by permuting the indices. Thus we see that this map is locally injective and isomorphic onto the fixed points of
E[M]
.

∎

The functor “internal morphisms to M” is right adjoint to the functor
{-\times M}
(See
[
5
,
Tag 0BWQ
]
). That is, for a sheaf
B
on
X_{\tau}
, we have a bijection

\textit{Mor}_{\mathit{Sh}(X_{\tau})}(B,E[M])\cong\textit{Mor}_{\mathit{Sh}(X_{\tau})}(B\times M,E)

which is functorial in all three entrees. Suppose
B
is a sheaf on which we have a left action of the group
G
. As in Remark
6.1
, we consider the associated action of
\underline{G}
on
B
. For
U\in\text{Ob}(X_{\tau})
, we define an equivalence relation on
B(U)\times M(U)
by declaring
{(b,m)\sim\left(g\cdot b,g\cdot m\right)}
for all
{g\in\underline{G}(U)}
.
It is clear that the equivalence classes modulo this relation define a presheaf on
X_{\tau}
. We denote this presheaf by
\nicefrac{{B\times M}}{{\sim}}
and we define
B\times_{G}M
as the sheaffication of this presheaf.

Lemma 6.7
.

The functor

\displaystyle-\times_{G}M:{G}\mbox{-{sh}}(X_{\tau})

\displaystyle\to\mathit{Sh}(X_{\tau})

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle\to B\times_{G}M

is left adjoint to the functor

\displaystyle-[M]:\mathit{Sh}(X_{\tau})

\displaystyle\to{G}\mbox{-{sh}}(X_{\tau})

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle\to E[M]

Proof.

In other words, we want to prove that there is a bijection

\textit{Mor}_{{G}\mbox{-{sh}}(X_{\tau})}(B,E[M])\cong\textit{Mor}_{\mathit{Sh}(X_{\tau})}(B\times_{G}M,E)

which is functorial with respect to
B
and
E
. We have a bijection

\displaystyle\textit{Mor}_{\mathit{Sh}}(B,E[M])

\displaystyle\cong\textit{Mor}_{\mathit{Sh}}(B\times M,E)

which we denote by

. For
U\in\text{Ob}(X_{\tau})
, and
{b\in B(U)}
, we have the morphism
{f(b):M|_{U}\to E|_{U}}
of sheaves over
X_{\tau}/U
. The morphism
\widetilde{f}
on
U
is given by

\displaystyle\widetilde{f}:B(U)\times M(U)

\displaystyle\to E(U)

\displaystyle(b,m)

\displaystyle\to(f(b))(m)

By Remark
6.1
, we note that
f
is
G\mbox{-equivariant}
if and only if it is equivariant with respect to the action of
\underline{G}
. If this is the case, then we see that for all
{(b,m)\in B(U)\times M(U)}
and
g\in\underline{G}(U)
, we have
{\widetilde{f}(b,m)=\widetilde{f}(g\cdot b,g\cdot m)}
. Since
{B\times M\to\nicefrac{{B\times M}}{{\sim}}}
is an epimorphism of presheaves, we see that
\widetilde{f}
uniquely factors through
{\nicefrac{{B\times M}}{{\sim}}}
. By the universal property of sheafification, it factors uniquely through the sheaf
B\times_{G}M
. Thus we get an injective map

\textit{Mor}_{{G}\mbox{-{sh}}(X_{\tau})}(B,E[M])\to\textit{Mor}_{\mathit{Sh}(X_{\tau})}(B\times_{G}M,E)

By following the same argument backward, we see that it is surjective as well.

∎

Suppose
B
is an abelian sheaf on
X_{\tau}
on which
G
acts by group homomorphisms. Then the sheaf
B\times_{G}M
also has a structure of an abelian sheaf. This can be seen as follows. Suppose
U
is an object of
X_{\tau}
and we have
{(b_{1},m_{1}),(b_{2},m_{2})\in B(U)\times M(U)}
. For any
m_{3}\in M(U)
, there are unique elements
g_{1},g_{2}\in\underline{G}(U)
such that
{g_{1}\cdot m_{1}=m_{3}}
and
{g_{2}\cdot m_{2}=m_{3}}
. Then we define

(b_{1},m_{1})+(b_{2},m_{2})=(g_{1}\cdot b_{1}+g_{2}\cdot b_{2},m_{3}).

Thus we see that
\nicefrac{{B\times M}}{{\sim}}
is an abelian presheaf. After sheafification, we get a structure of an abelian sheaf on
B\times_{G}M.

Remark 6.8
.

We can interpret the construction of
B\times_{G}M
as follows. A trivialization for
M
is a cover of
X
where
M
is trivial together with gluing morphisms which are elements of the group
G
(such that the cocycle condition is met). The sheaf
B\times_{G}M
is locally identical to
B
wherever
M
is trivial and the gluing data is borrowed from the gluing data of
M
via the homomorphism of
G
to
\text{Aut}(B)
.

Lemma 6.9
.

The functor

\displaystyle\textit{Mod}(\mathbb{Z}[\underline{G}])

\displaystyle\to\textit{Ab}(X_{\tau})

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle\to B\times_{G}M

is left adjoint to the functor

\displaystyle\textit{Ab}(X_{\tau})

\displaystyle\to\textit{Mod}(\mathbb{Z}[\underline{G}])

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle\to E[M]

Proof.

By Lemma
6.7
we have a bijection

\textit{Mor}_{{G}\mbox{-{sh}}(X_{\tau})}(B,E[M])\cong\textit{Mor}_{\mathit{Sh}(X_{\tau})}(B\times_{G}M,E).

It is clear from the proof of Lemma
6.9
that when
B
and
E
are abelian sheaves the above correspondence gives a bijection

\textit{Mor}_{\textit{Mod}(\mathbb{Z}[\underline{G}])}(B,E[M])\cong\textit{Mor}_{\textit{Ab}(X_{\tau})}(B\times_{G}M,E).

∎

Lemma 6.10
.

Suppose

is the trivial torsor. Then for any module

in

When

{E[M]\cong\prod_{g\in G}E}
. We see that we have a functorial bijection:

\textit{Mor}_{\textit{Mod}(\mathbb{Z}[\underline{G}])}(B,\prod_{g\in G}E)\cong\textit{Mor}_{\textit{Ab}(X_{\tau})}(B,E).

Therefore the conclusion follows from Yoneda’s lemma and the above Lemma

(See Remark

).

∎

.

The functor

Hochschild-Serre spectral sequence

Suppose
X
is a scheme and
X_{\text{ét}}
is the small étale site. Then any

M
on
X_{\text{ét}}
is representable (See
[
5
,
Tag 09Y8
]
). That is, there is a finite étale morphism
\widetilde{X}\xrightarrow{\pi}X
together with an isomorphism
G\cong\text{Aut}(\widetilde{X}/X)
such that
{M\cong\textit{Mor}_{X_{\text{ét}}}(-,\widetilde{X})}

{E_{2}^{p,q}=\text{H}^{q}(G,\text{H}^{p}(\widetilde{X},\pi^{-1}E))}
that converges to
\text{H}^{p+q}(X,E)
. We can realize this as another instance of the Grothendieck spectral sequence given by writing the global sections functor as a composition of two functors:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\textit{Ab}(X_{\tau})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\textit{Mod}(\mathbb{Z}[G])}
Ab
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{H}^{0}(\widetilde{X},\pi^{-1}E)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\text{H}^{0}(\widetilde{X},\pi^{-1}E))^{G}.}

When the torsor
M
is representable by the object
\widetilde{X}\xrightarrow{\pi}X
, the internal sheaf hom

is isomorphic to
\pi_{*}(E|_{\widetilde{X}})
(See
[
5
,
Tag 0D7X

Appendix I: Fibered categories and pullbacks

Suppose
\mathscr{Y}
is a fibered category over a category
\mathscr{C}
. A choice of pullbacks (See
[
5
,
Tag 02XN
]
) for the category
\mathscr{Y}
is a choice of a strongly cartesian morphism
f^{*}x\to x
lying over
f
, for every morphism
f:V\to U
in
\mathscr{C}
, and an object
x\in\text{Ob}(\mathscr{Y}_{U})
over
U
. After making a choice of pullbacks, we have a pullback functor
{f^{*}:\mathscr{Y}_{U}\to\mathscr{Y}_{V}}
. Suppose we have a morphism
{{F:\mathscr{Y}^{1}\to\mathscr{Y}^{2}}}
of fibered categories over
\mathscr{C}
. Then for

, we have the functor
{F_{U}:\mathscr{Y}^{1}_{U}\to\mathscr{Y}^{2}_{U}}

and
\mathscr{Y}^{2}
. Then for a morphism
{f:V\to U}
in

, we have two functors:

For
{y\in\text{Ob}(\mathscr{Y}^{1}_{U})}
, we have the following two strongly cartesian morphisms:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(f_{2}^{*}\circ F)y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Fy}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(F\circ f_{1}^{*})y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Fy}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

In this section, we construct examples to demonstrate that in the main Theorem

\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
which we denote by

.

Remark 9.1
.

In the situation of Definition
4.2
, if

group cohomology classes locally vanish, then for any stalk

of

, the group cohomology
{\text{H}^{j}(G,A_{p})}
is zero.

Example 9.2
.

Let
{\pi:\mathds{P}^{1}\to\mathds{P}^{1}}
be the squaring map

from the projective line over
\mathbb{C}
to itself. Let
\mathbf{G}_{m}
denote the sheaf of invertible functions on the small étale site of
\mathds{P}^{1}
and let

denote the sheaf
\pi_{*}(\mathbf{G}_{m})
. Let

act on

via the map

. This gives us an action of

on

. Let

denote the sheaf of invariants.

The sheaf

is isomorphic to
\mathbf{G}_{m}
on the compliment of

where

is étale. The stalk

at zero is
{(\mathbf{G}_{m})_{0}\oplus(\mathbf{G}_{m})_{0}}

is non-zero. Thus the first cohomology classes do not locally vanish near

or

Lemma 9.3

We have
{\text{H}^{1}_{\acute{e}t}(\mathds{P}^{1},A)\cong\mathbb{Z}}
and
{\text{H}^{1}_{\acute{e}t}(\mathds{P}^{1},A^{G})\cong\mathbb{Z}}

{\text{H}^{1}_{\acute{e}t}(\mathds{P}^{1},A^{G})\to\text{H}^{1}_{\acute{e}t}(\mathds{P}^{1},A)}

Since
\pi
is a finite morphism, the higher direct images along

are zero (See
[
5
,
Tag 03QP
]
). Therefore we have
{\text{H}^{1}(\mathds{P}^{1},A)\cong\text{H}^{1}(\mathds{P}^{1},\mathbf{G}_{m})}
which is isomorphic to

. We have the following exact sequence of sheaves:

The sheaf

is a pushforward along the closed immersion
\{0,\infty\}\to\mathds{P}^{1}
. Since a closed immersion has no higher direct images, and since we are working over an algebraically closed field, we see that
A^{G}/\mathbf{G}_{m}
has no higher cohomology. Therefore from the long exact sequence of cohomologies arising from above exact sequence, we see that
{\text{H}^{1}(\mathds{P}^{1},A^{G})\cong\text{H}^{1}(\mathds{P}^{1},\mathbf{G}_{m})\cong\mathbb{Z}}

Thus, both the cohomology groups
\text{H}^{1}(\mathds{P}^{1},A^{G})

∎

The action of

on
\text{H}^{1}(\mathds{P}^{1},A)
corresponds to pulling back a line bundle along the map

and therefore it is trivial. The cohomology group

is isomorphic to

which is zero. Let

be an

corresponding to a degree one line bundle. Then

is stable under

and therefore admits a lift of the

which is zero. However, we see from the above lemma that

is not induced from the invariants as the first group cohomology classes fail to vanish locally.

\text{H}^{1}(X,A)
. It is evident that we have
A^{G}\cong\overline{{\mathbb{Z}}/{2\mathbb{Z}}}_{X}
and therefore
\text{H}^{1}(X,A^{G})
is zero. The group cohomology
\text{H}^{1}(G,A_{P})
for the stalk
A_{P}
is
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
. Therefore the first group cohomology classes fail to vanish locally.

Lemma 9.5
.

Let
y
be the non-trivial
A\mbox{-torsor}
on
S^{1}
. Then it admits a lift of the underlying action such that the obstruction class is zero. However, it is not induced from the invariants.

Proof.

Since
y
represents a sheaf cohomology class in
{\text{H}^{1}(X,A)}^{G}
, it admits a lift of the
G\mbox{-action}
. For a connected open set
U\subseteq X
that contains either of the endpoints
P
or
Q
, we have a
G\mbox{-equivariant}
isomorphism
A(U)\cong A(X)
. Therefore we have an isomorphism
{\text{H}^{2}(G,A(U))\cong\text{H}^{2}(G,A(X))}
. Since the obstruction class locally vanishes everywhere, it is locally zero near
P
or
Q
and therefore is globally zero. There are no non-trivial
A^{G}\mbox{-torsors}
and therefore we conclude that
y
is not induced from the invariants.
∎

Example 9.6
.

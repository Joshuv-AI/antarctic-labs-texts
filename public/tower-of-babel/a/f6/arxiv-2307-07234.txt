Polynomially knotted 2 2 Spheres
Tumpa Mahato, Rama Mishra

Abstract
We review the polynomial parameterization of classical knots and prove the analogous results for long 2 2 knots. We also construct polynomial parameterization for certain classes of knotted spheres (such as spun and twist spun of the classical knots).



1
Introduction

Higher dimensional knot theory has attracted a lot of interest among knot theorists recently. By an
n
-knot, one means a smooth, proper, locally flat embedding of
S^{n}
in
S^{n+2}
. Moving from classical knots, the simplest situation is to understand knotting of
S^{2}
in
S^{4}
or in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
. In fact, studies are being done on any surface getting knotted inside four-dimensional space, and
2
-knots are just a special case. The notion of ambient isotopy can be defined
as in the case of classical knots. The central problem remains the same: classify all
2
-knots up to ambient isotopy.

Similar to the classical knots, a diagrammatic theory (
[
5
]
) and a braid theory (
[
15
]
) have been developed for
2
-knots. Both theories have provided many interesting invariants for
2
-knots. In the case of classical knots, we have encountered many numerical invariants whose computations become easy using a suitable parameterization. In knot theory, parameterization of knots is a way to present a knot, defining its coordinates using elementary functions, such as trigonometric or polynomial functions. They are referred to as trigonometric knots or polynomial knots.
Fourier knots
[
8
]
and Lissajous knots
[
4
]
are examples of trigonometric knots. Trigonometric parameterization provides a compact classical knot embedding, whereas polynomial parameterization presents long knots, i.e., embeddings of
\mathbb{R}
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Polynomial knots are important because they bring an algebraic flavour into knot theory. One point compactification of a polynomial knot is a classical knot, and the projective closure of a polynomial knot is a knot in
\mathbb{R}P^{3}.
Polynomial knots are extensively studied by Shastri
[
13
]
, Mishra-Prabhakar
[
12
]
, Durfee-Oshea
[
6
]
.
Explicit polynomial representation for all knots up to
8
crossings can be found in
[
12
]
.

In this paper, we discuss parameterization of
2
-knots using polynomial functions. Since a
2
-knot is a proper, smooth, locally flat embedding of
S^{2}
in
S^{4}
, removing a point from
S^{2}
and its corresponding image under the embedding in
S^{4}
we get an embedding of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
to
\mathbb{R}^{4}
. We will call this embedding a
long
2
-knot
. We prove that
every long
2
-knot is ambient isotopic to a polynomial embedding of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
. We also show that any two polynomial embeddings of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
, which are ambient isotopic, are, in fact, polynomially isotopic. The idea of our proof is similar to the classical case
[
14
]
.

We also address the problem of explicitly constructing a polynomial parameterization for a given
2
-knot. We begin by taking the simplest
2
-knots, which are constructed from classical knots, such as
spun knots
, introduced by E. Artin
[
2
]
. Then we move to
d-twist spun 2-knots
which is a generalization of Artin’s construction, introduced by E. C. Zeeman
[
19
]
. Obtaining a polynomial parameterization for
these classes of knots is relatively simple because we can use the existing polynomial representation of classical knots
[
12
]

which can be brought into the form suitable for spun or twist spun construction. In this paper, we provide the steps to parameterize spun knots and twist spun knots using polynomial functions and present their
3
dimensional projections using Mathematica.

This paper is organized as follows: Section
2
is divided into three subsections. Section
2.1
includes the basic definitions required to study
2
-knots. Section
2.2
describes the two constructions, spinning and twist spinning, for obtaining 2-knots from classical knots. In Section
2.3
, we provide a brief exposition of the polynomial parameterization of classical knots. In Section
3
, we prove the existence of polynomial parameterization for all long
2
-knots and show that it is unique up to a polynomial isotopy. In Sections
4.1
and
4.2
, we provide steps to find a polynomial representation for spun knots and twist spun knots, respectively. We write down explicit parameterizations for the spun trefoil and the spun figure eight knot and also the
d
twist spun trefoil for
d=1,2,5,10
and
20
. Finally, in Section
, the
Mathmatica
notebooks for all the examples are provided.

Definition 2.1

Let

In other words, the manifold
f(N)

\partial f(N)=f(N)\cap\partial M
.

Let

\mathbb{R}^{k}

Definition 2.4
.

A

is a proper, locally flat embedding of a closed surface

in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
or

.

•

If

.

•

When

Definition 2.5
.

A proper, locally flat embedding of

-knot
.

Note that every

and the image of that point from

Definition 2.6
.

Two surface links
F
and

are

if and only if they are ambient isotopic, i.e. there exists an ambient isotopy

When

are oriented, then

It is hard to visualize a 2-dimensional knot in the 4-dimensional space. So, we consider its projection to a 3-dimensional subspace.
But it is sometimes hard to draw the whole surface diagram as some parts are behind
other parts. To avoid that we can use movies or motion pictures where we cut the surface diagram by parallel hyperplanes. Then each intersection gives a 1-dimensional manifold.
Let
F\subset\mathbb{R}^{4}
be a given knotted surface and take a vector
v\in\mathbb{R}^{4}
. Let
\{\mathbb{R}^{3}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
be family of parallel hyperplanes, each of which is orthogonal to the vector
v
, containing the point
tv
. Now,
F_{t}=F\cap\mathbb{R}^{3}_{t}
ia always a 1-dimenisonal manifold i.e a classical link or empty. Since,
F
is compact, there exists an interval
[a,b]
such that
F\subset\cup_{t\in[a,b]}\,\mathbb{R}^{3}_{t}
.

Let
p_{v}:\mathbb{R}^{4}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{v}
be the projection map onto the line
\mathbb{R}_{v}=\{tv\in\mathbb{R}^{4}\,|\,t\in\mathbb{R}\}
. Consider the restriction map
p_{v}|_{F}:F\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{v}
.
By Morse theory, non-degenrate critical points of
p_{v}|_{F}
are isolated and the intersections at those points are of our interest as the knot type does not change at any point other than the critical points.

Definition 2.9
.

For the Morse function on
F
defined above by restricting the projection of
\mathbb{R}^{4}
onto
\mathbb{R}_{v}
, the one parameter family
\{F_{t}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}_{t}\}_{t\in[a,b]}
is called the
motion picture
or a
movie
of the surface link
F
. And each
F_{t}
for a
t\in\mathbb{R}
is called a
still
of the movie.

Remark 2.10
.

Since the knot type changes at only at finitely many critical points, a finite number of stills are sufficient to describe the knotted surface. Therefore, although the whole famliy
\{F_{t}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}_{t}\}_{t\in[a,b]}
is called the movie, we can take only a finite set of stills as the motion picture or a movie of the surface link.

-knots can be found in

2.2
Construction of knotted spheres:

In this section, we discuss the constructions to obtain knotted surfaces in 4-space such as spinning and twist spinning.

2.2.1
Spinning construction

E. Artin
[
2
]
introduced a way to construct
2
-knots by spinning a knotted arc embedded in the half-space
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+}
around
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. The resulting
2
-knots are called
spun 2-knots
. Spun knots represent the simplest class of
2
-knots. Their construction is described as follows. In
\mathbb{R}^{4}
, consider the upper half space

\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+}=\left\{(x,y,z,0):z\geq 0\right\}

with the boundary

\partial\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+}=\mathbb{R}^{2}=\left\{(x,y,0,0)\right\}.

Now, locus of a point
x=(x,y,z,0)
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+}
, rotated in 4-space about
\mathbb{R}^{2}
is given by

\left\{(x,y,z\,\cos\theta,z\,\sin\theta)\,\middle|\,0\leq\theta\leq 2\pi\right\}.

To construct a spun 2-knot, we choose a properly, locally flatly embedded arc
k
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+}
i.e
k
is embedded in
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+}
locally flatly and intersects
\partial\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+}
transversely only at the endpoints (Figure
5(a)
). Then we spin
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+}
along
\mathbb{R}^{2}
by
360^{o}
and the continuous locus of the arc
k
produces the spun 2-knot given by

\left\{(x,y,z\,\cos\theta,z\,\sin\theta)\,\middle|\,(x,y,z)\in k,0\leq\theta\leq 2\pi\right\}.

2.2.2
Twist spinning construction

E. C. Zeeman generalized Artin’s spinning construction to
twist spinning
in 1965
[
19
]
. In this case, we imagine the knotted part of
k
inside a 3-ball as in Figure
5(b)
. Now, to include twisting in the spinning construction we rotate the ball
d
times around its own axis while rotating
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+}
around
\mathbb{R}^{2}
once. Position of the knotted arc after
d
twists should match its initial position after completing the rotation around
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. This way we get a 2-sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
, called
d-twist spun 2-knot
. By definition, for
d=0
we get a spun knot.

2.3
Classical polynomial knots

It is already known that the ambient isotopy classes of classical knots (
S^{1}\subset S^{3}
) are in bijective correspondence with the ambient isotopy classes of long knots, i.e., the smooth embeddings of
\mathbb{R}
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
which are proper and have asymptotic behaviour outside a closed interval. In 1994, the following theorems were proved regarding parameterization of long knots.

Theorem 2.11

(
[
13
]
,
[
14
]
)
.

For every long knot
K
there exists a polynomial embedding
t\to(f(t),g(t),h(t))
from
\mathbb{R}
to
\mathbb{R}^{3}
which is isotopic to
K
.

Theorem 2.12

(
[
14
]
)
.

If two polynomial embeddings
\phi_{0}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
\phi_{1}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
represent isotopic knots then there exists a one-parameter family of polynomial embeddings
\phi_{t}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
for each
t\in[0,1]
. We say that
\phi_{0}
and
\phi_{1}
are polynomially isotopic.

Both of these theorems stated above were proved using Weierstrass’ Approximation
[
17
]
. Later, concrete polynomial embeddings representing a few classes of knots were constructed, and their degrees were estimated (
[
12
]
,
[
11
]
). In all these constructions the main idea was to find a concrete embedding to fix a suitable knot diagram and find real polynomials
f(t)
and
g(t)
such that the plane curve

(x(t),y(t))=(f(t),g(t))
provides the projection of the diagram. For this curve, one can explicitly find the parameter pairs
(s_{i},t_{i})
for which
f(s_{i})=f(t_{i})
and
g(s_{i})=g(t_{i})
for each
i
. Then, depending upon the over/under crossing information, the authors construct a polynomial
h(t)
which satisfies
h(s_{i})<h(t_{i})
, at an undercrossing and
h(s_{i})>h(t_{i})
, at an overcrossing. Note that, in these embeddings, the knot is always projected
on the
XY
plane, and the
Z
co-ordinate provides the height function. All the crossings of the knot lie in the image of a closed interval
[a,b]
.

Here, we prove the following lemma, which will be useful in Section
2.2
.

Lemma 2.13
.

Given a long knot
K
, there exists a polynomial embedding from
\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
defined by
t\to(x(t),y(t),z(t))
such that for some interval
[a,b]
,
z(a)=z(b)=0
,
z(t)>0
for
t\in(a,b)
and there are no crossings outside
[a,b]
.

Proof.

We construct polynomials
f(t)
and
g(t)
such that the curve, defined by
(x,y)=(f(t),g(t))
represents a projection of
K
on the
XY
plane. We find the parameters
(t_{i},s_{i})
such that
f(s_{i})=f(t_{i})
and
g(s_{i})=g(t_{i})
. We arrange
t_{i}
and
s_{i}
in ascending order. We choose
\mu_{j}
between any two parameters which are giving rise to crossing points of the same kind (all over or all under) and consider
\tilde{h}(t)=\pi_{j}(t-\mu_{j})
. This polynomial
\tilde{h}(t)
provides the over/under crossing information. Since our crossings are finite, they lie within a closed interval say
[c,d]
. If the degree of
\tilde{h}(t)
is odd, we can choose a real number
\mu
outside
[c,d]
in such a way that the polynomial
h_{0}(t)=-\tilde{h}(t)(t-\mu)
still provides the over/under crossing information. Note that
h_{0}(t)
is an even degree polynomial. Now by adding a sufficiently large real number
R
, we can show that
h(t)=h_{0}(t)+R
has exactly two real roots
a
and
b
outside
[c,d]
,
h(t)>0
for
t\in(a,b)
and all the double points of the projection lie inside
[a,b]
(Figure
6
). This completes the proof.

In this section, we generalize the results in
[
14
]
in the context of
2
-knots.

Theorem 3.1
.

Every long
2
-knot given by a smooth embedding
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{4}
, has a polynomial representation.

Proof.

Let
F
be long 2-knot in

\mathbb{R}^{4}
given by a smooth embedding
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{4}

defined by
\phi(s,t)=\left(\alpha(s,t),\beta(s,t),\gamma(s,t),\delta(s,t)\right)
such that
\tilde{\phi}\equiv\left(\alpha,\beta,\gamma\right):\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}
is a generic immersion (having finite number of double point sets and triple points as the only singularities).
Up to equivalence, we can assume that the Jacobian matrix

J=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\partial\alpha}{\partial s}&\frac{\partial\alpha}{\partial t}\\[5.69054pt]
\frac{\partial\beta}{\partial s}&\frac{\partial\beta}{\partial t}\\[5.69054pt]
\frac{\partial\gamma}{\partial s}&\frac{\partial\gamma}{\partial t}\end{pmatrix}

has rank
2
outside some closed region
\mathcal{I}_{1}=[a,b]\times[a,b]
.

Since we have finitely many double point sets or triple points in the image of
\tilde{\phi}
, we can choose another closed region
\mathcal{I}_{2}=[c,d]\times[c,d]
such that
\tilde{\phi}\left(\mathcal{I}_{2}\right)
contains all the singularities. Let,

\mathcal{M}=[m_{1},m_{2}]\times[m_{1},m_{2}]
such that
\mathcal{M}
contains both the rectangles
\mathcal{I}_{1}

and
\mathcal{I}_{2}
. Let
\phi(\mathcal{M})
be contained inside a
4
-ball of radius
r
with

\|\phi(m_{i},m_{j})\|=r\quad\forall\,i,j=1,2.

Let
\mathcal{N}=[n_{1},n_{2}]\times[n_{1},n_{2}]
be such that
\phi(\mathcal{N})
is contained inside a
4
-ball of radius
2r
with

\|\phi(n_{i},n_{j})\|=2r\quad\forall\,i,j=1,2.

By a smooth reparameterization, we can assume that

[m_{1},m_{2}]=\left[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right]\quad\text{and }\quad[n_{1},n_{2}]=[-1,1].

Thus,
\phi\left(\mathcal{M}\right)
and
\phi(\mathcal{N})
are contained inside balls of radius
r
and
2r
respectively with

\Big\|\phi\left(\pm\frac{1}{2},\pm\frac{1}{2}\right)\Big\|=r\quad\text{and}\quad\|\phi(\pm 1,\pm 1)\|=2r

and the Jacobian matrix has rank
2
outside
\mathcal{M}
.
Now, consider the restriction of
\phi
to
\mathcal{I}_{1}
i.e

\phi|_{\mathcal{I}_{1}}:\mathcal{I}_{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{4}.

Since the set of embeddings from a compact Hausdorff manifold to any manifold forms an open set in the set of all smooth maps with
C^{1}
-topology, there exists an
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that

\psi\in N(\phi,\varepsilon_{0})
i.e.
\psi
is an embedding of
\mathcal{I}_{1}
in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
,
where

N(\phi,\varepsilon_{0})=\left\{\psi:\underset{(s,t)\in\mathcal{I}_{1}}{\sup}\{\|\psi(s,t)-\phi(s,t)\|,\|\psi^{\prime}(s,t)-\phi^{\prime}(s,t)\|\}<\varepsilon_{0}\right\}.

Let
\varepsilon<\min\{R/2,\varepsilon_{0}\}.

For this
\varepsilon
, we can take an
\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
-approximation
\psi
using the Bernstein polynomial
[
18
]

or the Weierstrass’ Approximation
of two variables inside the square
\mathcal{N}

[
18
]
. Let
\psi_{1}\equiv(x_{1},y_{1},z_{1},w_{1})
be the approximation,where
x_{1},y_{1},z_{1}
and
w_{1}
are polynomials in two variables
s,t
.
Then all the partial derivatives of the polynomials
x_{1},y_{1},z_{1}
and
w_{1}
are greater than
(1-\frac{\varepsilon}{2})
in the region
\mathcal{N}\setminus\mathcal{M}
.

Now, for
\delta_{i}\in(0,\varepsilon/2),i=1,2
, we can choose
\mathrm{N}\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
large enough so that

\displaystyle\psi

\displaystyle\equiv\Big(x_{1}+\frac{\delta_{1}}{2\mathrm{N}+1}s^{2\mathrm{N}+1}+\frac{\delta_{2}}{2\mathrm{N}+1}t^{2\mathrm{N}+1},\,y_{1}+\frac{\delta_{1}}{2\mathrm{N}+1}s^{2\mathrm{N}+1}+\frac{\delta_{2}}{2\mathrm{N}+1}t^{2\mathrm{N}+1},

\displaystyle\qquad z_{1}+\frac{\delta_{1}}{2\mathrm{N}+1}s^{2\mathrm{N}+1}+\frac{\delta_{2}}{2\mathrm{N}+1}t^{2\mathrm{N}+1},\,w_{1}+\frac{\delta_{1}}{2\mathrm{N}+1}s^{2\mathrm{N}+1}+\frac{\delta_{2}}{2\mathrm{N}+1}t^{2\mathrm{N}+1}\Big)

\displaystyle\equiv(x,y,z,w).

Then
\psi
is an
\varepsilon
-approximation of
\phi
inside
\mathcal{N}
such that the derivative of
\psi
is positive outside the square
\mathcal{N}
as each partial derivative

\frac{\partial x}{\partial s},\frac{\partial x}{\partial t},\frac{\partial y}{\partial s},\frac{\partial y}{\partial t},\frac{\partial z}{\partial s},\frac{\partial z}{\partial t},\frac{\partial w}{\partial s},\frac{\partial w}{\partial t}

are positive outside
\mathcal{N}
. Now, inside a compact region, this polynomial map
\psi
approximates the knot. Also the presence of large odd degree term with a very small coefficient ensures that the long
2
-knot becomes asymptotically flat outside the compact region.

It remains to show that
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{4}
is an embedding.

\phi:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{4}
being an embedding implies it is an injective immersion. That means the Jacobian
J(\phi;s,t)
has rank

Since the Jacobian matrix for

This completes the proof.
∎

Definition 3.2
.

Notation 3.3

For

Lemma 3.4
.

Let

Proof.

To prove the lemma we show that the maps

are embeddings for each

.

The Jacobian for each

\dfrac{\partial y}{\partial s}&\dfrac{\partial y}{\partial t}\\[5.69054pt]
\dfrac{\partial z}{\partial s}+(2\mathrm{N}+1)u\varepsilon s^{2N}&\dfrac{\partial z}{\partial t}\\[5.69054pt]
\dfrac{\partial w}{\partial s}&\dfrac{\partial w}{\partial t}+(2\mathrm{N}+1)u\varepsilon t^{2N}\\
\end{pmatrix}

2

Now, consider the set

0

. First, consider the function

F(t)=\begin{cases}e^{-\frac{1}{t}}\,,&t>0\\
0\,,&t\leq 0.\end{cases}

Then define the bump function,

as follows:

B(t)=\frac{F(d_{1}-t^{2})}{F(t^{2}-d_{2})+F(d_{1}-t^{2})},

where
d_{1},d_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
are chosen in such a way that

B(t)=\begin{cases}1\,,&t\in[a^{\prime},b^{\prime}]\\
0\,,&t\in[a,t_{1}]\cup[t_{2},b]\\

See Figure

\displaystyle\tilde{f}(t,\phi)=

\displaystyle\,B(t)\,f^{\prime}(t,\phi)+(1-B(t))\,f(t),

\displaystyle\tilde{g}(t,\phi)=

\displaystyle\,B(t)\,g^{\prime}(t,\phi)+(1-B(t))\,g(t),

\displaystyle\tilde{h}(t,\phi)=

\displaystyle\,B(t)\,h^{\prime}(t,\phi)+(1-B(t))\,h(t).

Now, the knotted arc is rotating

\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+}

PQ

d

XY

\phi=d\,\theta.

Hence, the parameterization for d-twist spun knot is given by

(5)

(t,\theta)\longrightarrow\left\{\left(\tilde{f}(t,d\theta)\,,\,\tilde{g}(t,d\theta)\,,\,\tilde{h}(t,d\theta)\,\cos\,\theta\,,\,\tilde{h}(t,d\theta)\,\sin\,\theta\right)\,\middle|\,\begin{aligned} a\leq&t\leq b\\
0\leq&\theta<2\pi\end{aligned}\right\}.

Here,
\tilde{f},\tilde{g},\tilde{h}
are linear combinations of the functions
f,g,h
, cosine, sine and the bump function
B(t)

(6)

\left\{\left(\bar{f}(t,\theta)\,,\,\bar{g}(t,\theta)\,,\,\bar{h}(t,\theta)\,,\,\bar{p}(t,\theta)\right)\,\middle|\,a\leq t\leq b,\,0\leq\theta<2\pi\right\}.

This completes the proof.

Deformation of rigid conjugate self-dual Galois representations
Yifeng Liu, Yichao Tian, Liang Xiao, Wei Zhang, Xinwen Zhu

Abstract
. In this article, we study deformations of conjugate self-dual Galois representations. The study has two folds. First, we prove an R=T type theorem for a conjugate self-dual Galois representation with coefficients in a finite field, satisfying a certain property called rigid. Second, we study the rigidity property for the family of residue Galois representations attached to a symmetric power of an elliptic curve, as well as to a regular algebraic conjugate self-dual cuspidal representation.

1.
Introduction

Let
F/F^{+}
be a CM extension of number fields with
\mathtt{c}\in\Gal(\overline{F}{}/F^{+})
the complex conjugation. In this article, we study deformations of conjugate self-dual Galois representations of
F
. The study has two folds. First, we prove an R=T type theorem (Theorem
3.6.3
) for a conjugate self-dual Galois representation
\bar{r}
of
F
with coefficients in a finite field, satisfying a certain property called
rigid
(Definition
3.6.1
). It is worth mentioning that unlike many other references in the field, we neither assume that the characteristic of the coefficient field is relatively split in
F/F^{+}
nor assume that
\bar{r}
only ramifies at places that are split in
F
. Second, we study the rigidity property for the family of residue Galois representations attached to a symmetric power of an elliptic curve, as well as to a regular algebraic conjugate self-dual cuspidal representation
\Pi
. In particular, we show in Theorem
4.2.6
that if
\Pi
has a supercuspidal component, then the residue Galois representation is rigid whenever the characteristic is large enough.

The main purpose of this article is to make preparation for our work
[
21
]
in which we prove major cases toward the Beilinson–Bloch–Kato conjecture on the relation between Selmer groups and
L
-functions. To see how Galois deformation is used in the study of Selmer groups, we refer to that article.

The two main theorems are both technical to state. In order to give some flavor of what we can prove in this article, we now state a result (Theorem
1.0.1
below) that follows from the combination of the two main theorems and is relatively straightforward to formulate. Also, it seems to us this kind of result is new in literature.

In this subsection, we introduce the notion of unitary Satake parameters and unitary Hecke algebras.

(Abstract Satake parameter)
.

Let

(1)

of cardinality

is

P_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}(T)=(-T)^{N}\cdot P_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}(T^{-1})
.

(2)

Suppose that

splits in
F
. We define an
(abstract) Satake parameter
in
L
at
v
of rank

to be a pair
\boldsymbol{\alpha}\coloneqq(\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{1};\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{2})

.

not in

Lemma 3.3.7

Let

be an unramified representation of

are mutually coprime for

be a lifting of

to an object

(1)

There is a unique decomposition

of free

is stable under the action of

and it is a lifting of

(2)

is not an eigenvalue for the canonical action of

Part (1) is elementary, which we leave to the readers as an exercise.

For (2), we choose a basis of

over

in (1). We identify

and

with their matrices under this basis. We have

\displaystyle\rho(t)^{q}=(1+(\rho(t)-1))^{q}=1+q(\rho(t)-1)+\sum_{a\geqslant 2}\binom{q}{a}(\rho(t)-1)^{a},

.

Let

above

in

Lemma 4.2.5
.

Consider a field tower
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}\subseteq L\subseteq L^{\prime}\subseteq\overline{\mathbb{Q}}{}_{\ell}
in which

is finite unramified and

is finite Galois. Let
\bar{\psi}\colon\Gamma_{L^{\prime}}\to\GL_{m}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}{}_{\ell})

\bar{\rho}\coloneqq\Ind_{\Gamma_{L^{\prime}}}^{\Gamma_{L}}\bar{\psi}

(a)

\bar{\rho}
is crystalline with regular Fontaine–Laffaille weights in

(Definition

) for some integers

and

; and

(b)

(b-a)[L^{\prime}:L]<\ell-1
.

Then

is unramified over

.

Proof.

We may assume

. We assume on the contrary that
L^{\prime}/L
is ramified. Up to replacing
L
by the maximal subfield of

that is unramified over

, we may assume that
L^{\prime}/L
is totally ramified. Up to replacing
\bar{\psi}
by its semisimplification, we may assume that
\bar{\psi}
is semisimple. Since
[L^{\prime}:L]
is coprime to
\ell
,
\bar{\rho}=\Ind_{\Gamma_{L^{\prime}}}^{\Gamma_{L}}(\bar{\psi})
is also semisimple. As
\mathrm{P}_{L}
is a pro-
\ell
normal subgroup of
\Gamma_{L}
, we have
\bar{\rho}(\mathrm{P}_{L})=\{1\}
by
[
25
]
*Proposition 4. By Lemma
4.2.4
, there exists a finite unramified extension

inside

such that
\bar{\rho}\mathbin{|}_{\Gamma_{L^{\prime\prime}}}
is a direct sum of characters. Up to replacing

by
L^{\prime\prime}
and

by

, we may assume that
\bar{\rho}
itself is a direct sum of characters of

, say
\chi_{1},\dots,\chi_{N}\colon\Gamma_{L}\to\overline{\mathbb{F}}{}_{\ell}^{\times}
. On the other hand, since

. For a prime p p we construct a subset of 𝔽 p ( k 2 − k ) / 2 \mathbb{F}_{p}^{(k^{2}-k)/2} of size p ( k 2 − k ) / 2 − 1 p^{(k^{2}-k)/2-1} that does not contain progressions of length k k . 1.
Introduction

For a prime
p
and a prime power
q
we denote by
r_{k}(\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n})
the maximal cardinality of a subset
S\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
, such that
S
does not contain
k
points in arithmetic progression and by
\bar{r}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n})
the maximal cardinality of a subset
T\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
, such that
T
does not contain
k
points on a line. Since every
k
-term progression
\{a+ib\;|\;i\in[0,k-1]\}
in
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
is contained in the line
\{a+ib\;|\;i\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\}
, it folllows that
r_{k}(\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n})\geq\bar{r}_{k}(\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n})
.

A hypercube
[0,k-2]^{n}
of side length
k-1
gives a trivial bound
r_{k}(\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n})\geq(k-1)^{n}.

Lin and Wolf
[
4
]
gave the bounds
\bar{r}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2k})\geq q^{2(k-1)}+q^{k-1}-1
and
\bar{r}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k})\geq q^{k-1}
that beat the trivial bound with increasing
q
when
k
is constant. For
k=p
, Frankl, Graham and Rödl
[
3
]
gave the bound
\bar{r}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2q})\geq(q-1)^{2q-1}q.

We will show the following new bound:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
q
be a prime power and
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
k\leq q
then

\bar{r}_{k}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{(k^{2}-k)/2})\geq q^{(k^{2}-k)/2-1}.

In particular, for a prime
p
and
k\leq p
,

r_{k}(\mathbb{F}_{p}^{(k^{2}-k)/2})\geq p^{(k^{2}-k)/2-1}.

Every
k
-progression-free set
S\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n_{1}}
provides an asymptotic lower bound
(|S|^{1/{n_{1}}}-o(1))^{n}
for
r_{k}(\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n})
using the product construction
S\times S\times...\times S
(see e.g.
[
5
]
). The previously best asymptotic bound for sets containing no full line in
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
is derived from the result of Frankl, Graham and Rödl
[
3
]
. Theorem
1.1
gives an improved bound.

Lemma 1.2
.

Let
F_{1}(p):=p^{1-\frac{1}{p}}
,
F_{2}(p):=\big(p(p-1)^{2p-1}\big)^{\frac{1}{2p}}
,
F_{3}(p):=p^{1-\frac{2}{p^{2}-p}}
be the bases of the asymptotic lower bounds derived from Lin-Wolf
[
4
]
, Frankl-Graham-Rödl
[
3
]
and Theorem
1.1
, respectively. Then

\begin{split}&F_{1}(p)-(p-\log(p))=\frac{\log(p)^{2}}{2p}+\mathcal{O}\bigg(\frac{\log(p)^{3}}{p^{2}}\bigg),\\
&F_{2}(p)-(p-1)=\frac{1}{2p}+\mathcal{O}\bigg(\frac{1}{p^{2}}\bigg),\\
&F_{3}(p)-p=-\frac{2\log(p)}{p}+\mathcal{O}\bigg(\frac{\log(p)}{p^{2}}\bigg).\end{split}

In particular,
F_{1}(p)\sim p-\log(p)
,
F_{2}(p)\sim p-1
,
F_{3}(p)\sim p
.

Corollary 1.3
.

r_{p}(\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n})=(p+o_{p}(1)+o_{n}(1))^{n},

where
o_{p}(1)=o(1)
as a function in
p
and
o_{n}(1)=o(1)
as a function in
n
.

The construction used to proof Theorem
1.1
can be shifted to partition the whole space, which leads to the following coloring variant of the theorem:

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
q
be a prime power and
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
can be colored by

c\left(q^{\frac{2}{q^{2}-q}}\right)^{n}

colors such that there is no monochromatic line, where
c<q
is a constant.

For earlier results and further references, see
[
2
]
.

2.
Notation

We write
\mathbb{F}_{q}
for the field with
q
elements whenever
q
is a prime power and we use
p
instead of
q
if it is a prime.

We write
[k,\ell]
for the set
\{k,k+1,...,\ell\}
either as a subset of
\mathbb{Z}
or of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

We use the Landau natation where
f(n)=\mathcal{O}(g(n))
denotes that there is a positive constant
C
such that
Cf(n)\leq g(n)
for all
n
large enough and
f(n)=o(g(n))
denotes that
cf(n)\leq g(n)
for all positive constants
c
and large enough
n
. We write
f(n)\sim g(n)
whenever
f(n)=\mathcal{O}(g(n))
and
g(n)=\mathcal{O}(f(n))
.

3.
proofs

The following proof is inspired by Lin and Wolf
[
4
, Proposition 3]
. As in their proof we use the existence of homogenious polynomials of degree
d
in
d
variables over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with only the trivial root. For this purpose see e.g.
[
1
, page 6]
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

For each
i\in[1,k-1]
let
P_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X_{(i^{2}-i)/2+1},...,X_{(i^{2}+i)/2}]
be a homogenious polynomial of degree
i
in
i
variables, such that
P(x)=0
implies
x=0
for
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{i}
. Let
P:=\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}P_{i}
and
S:=\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{(k^{2}-k)/2}\;|\;P(x)=0\}
. We claim that there is no line in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{(k^{2}-k)/2}
containing at least
k
points of
S
.
Assume that
a=(a_{1},a_{2},...,a_{k-1})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{(k^{2}-k)/2}
and
b=(b_{1},b_{2},...,b_{k-1})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{(k^{2}-k)/2}
with
a_{i},b_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{i}
for each
i\in[1,k-1]
, are such that
L:=\{a+bj|j\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\}
contains at least
k
points of
S
. Consider
Q:=P(a+bY)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[Y]
.

Q=P(a+bY)=\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}P_{i}(a_{i}+b_{i}Y)=\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\big(R_{i}(Y)+Y^{i}P_{i}(b_{i})\big)

where
R_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[Y]
are polynomials of degree at most
i-1
.
Since
Q
has at least
k
roots but
\deg(Q)\leq k-1
it follows that
Q=0
.

Let
l\in[1,k-1]
and assume that
b_{i}=0
for all
i\geq l
. Then the coefficient of
Y^{l}
in
Q
is
P_{l}(b_{l})
and therefore
P_{l}(b_{l})=0
, which implies
b_{l}=0
. By induction,
b_{i}=0
for all
i\in[1,k-1]
and
L
is a single point, a contradiction. Consequently, there is no line in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{(k^{2}-k)/2}
containing at least
k
points of
S
.

As
P_{1}
is linear, for each choice of
x_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{(k^{2}-k)/2-1}
there is exactly one
x_{1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
such that
P(x_{1},x_{2})=0
and therefore
|S|=q^{(k^{2}-k)/2-1}
.

∎

Proof of Lemma
1.2
.

\begin{split}F_{1}(p)=&pe^{-\frac{\log(p)}{p}}\\
=&p\left(1-\frac{\log(p)}{p}+\frac{\log(p)^{2}}{2p^{2}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\log(p)^{3}}{p^{3}}\right)\right),\\
=&p-\log(p)+\frac{\log(p)^{2}}{2p}+\mathcal{O}\bigg(\frac{\log(p)^{2}}{p}\bigg).\\
F_{2}(p)=&(p-1)\left(\frac{p}{p-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{2p}}\\
=&(p-1)\left(e^{\frac{1}{2p}\log\left(1+\frac{1}{p-1}\right)}\right),\\
=&(p-1)\left(e^{\frac{1}{2p}\left(\frac{1}{p-1}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{p^{2}}\right)\right)}\right),\\
=&(p-1)\left(1+\frac{1}{2p(p-1)}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{p^{3}}\right)\right),\\
=&p-1+\frac{1}{2p}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{p^{2}}\right).\\
F_{3}(p)=&pe^{-\frac{2\log(p)}{p^{2}-p}}\\
=&p\left(1-\frac{2\log(p)}{p^{2}-p}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\log(p)^{2}}{p^{4}}\right)\right),\\
=&p-\frac{2\log(p)}{p}\left(1+\frac{1}{p-1}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\log(p)^{2}}{p^{3}}\right),\\
=&p-\frac{2\log(p)}{p}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\log(p)}{p^{2}}\right).\end{split}

∎

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Let
\bar{c}:=\lceil 2n/(q^{2}-q)\rceil-1
and
r:=n-\bar{c}(q^{2}-q)/2\leq(q^{2}-q)/2
. The set
S
in the proof of Theorem
1.1
can be shifted into
q
copies
S_{i}:=S+(i,0,0,...,0)
for
i\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
such that the
S_{i}
partition
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{(q^{2}-q)/2}
. Note that
S
can be sliced down to a line-free set
S^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{r}
of size
q^{r-1}
by taking only points with zero entries in the last
(q^{2}-q)/2-r
coordinates. Again,
S^{\prime}
can be shifted into
q
copies
S^{\prime}_{i}:=S^{\prime}+(i,0,0,...,0)
for
i\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
such that the
S^{\prime}_{i}
partition
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{r}.
Now the sets

\mathcal{S}:=\{S_{i_{1}}\times...\times S_{i_{\bar{c}}}\times S^{\prime}_{j}\;|\;(i_{1},...,i_{bar{c}},j)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\bar{c}+1}\}

partitions
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
into
|\mathcal{S}|=q^{\bar{c}+1}
line-free sets and

q^{\bar{c}+1}=\left(q^{\frac{2}{q^{2}-q}}\right)^{n}q^{\epsilon},

where
\epsilon:=\lceil 2n/(q^{2}-q)\rceil-2n/(q^{2}-q)<1.

∎

Yi-Huang Shen, Guangjun Zhu ∗

Abstract
. Connected bipartite graphs whose binomial edge ideals are Cohen–Macaulay have been classified by Bolognini et al. In this paper, we compute the depth, Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity, and dimension of the generalized binomial edge ideals of these graphs.



1.
Introduction

Let
m
and
n
be two positive integers. Following the convention, the
notation
[m]
denotes the set
\{1,2,\ldots,m\}
.
Let
S={\mathbb{K}}[\bm{X}]\coloneqq{\mathbb{K}}[x_{ij}:i\in[m],j\in[n]]
be the polynomial ring over the field
{\mathbb{K}}
in
m\times n
variables.
In
[
9
]
, Ene et al. introduced the binomial edge ideal of a pair of graphs. Specifically, let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be simple graphs on vertex sets
[m]
and
[n]
respectively. Suppose that
e=\{i,j\}\in E(G_{1})
and
f=\{t,l\}\in E(G_{2})
are two edges with
i<j
and
k<l
. Then, one can assign a
2
-minor
p_{(e,f)}=[i,j\,|\,t,l]\coloneqq x_{it}x_{jl}-x_{il}x_{jt}
to the pair
(e,f)
. The
binomial edge ideal of the pair
(G_{1},G_{2})
is defined to be

\mathcal{J}_{G_{1},G_{2}}\coloneqq(p_{(e,f)}:e\in E(G_{1}),f\in E(G_{2}))

in
S
. This is a generalization of the classical
binomial edge ideals

in
[
10
,
18
]
when one of
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
is the complete
graph
K_{2}
. Meanwhile, the
ideals generated by adjacent minors
in

[
8
]
turn out to be the binomial edge ideals of a pair of line
graphs.

Much research has been done to understand
the connection between the
algebraic properties of
\mathcal{J}_{G_{1},G_{2}}
and the combinatorial properties of

G_{1}
and
G_{2}
.
In particular, the combinatorial invariants of
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
can be related to homological invariants such as depth, Betti numbers, and Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity (regularity for short) of
\mathcal{J}_{G_{1},G_{2}}
, see
[
9
,
20
]
. In general, however, the algebraic properties of these ideals are still widely open. For
S/\mathcal{J}_{G_{1},G_{2}}
possessing a decent algebraic property, a good rule of thumb is that one of
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
is a complete graph. For example, it was proved in
[
9
, Theorem 1.2, Corollary 2.2 and Proposition 4.1]
that this is an equivalent condition for
\mathcal{J}_{G_{1},G_{2}}
to be radical ideal. Therefore,
\mathcal{J}_{G_{1},G_{2}}
is a prime ideal if and only if
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are complete. Furthermore, the condition for
\mathcal{J}_{G_{1},G_{2}}
to be unmixed is that one of the graphs is complete and the other graph satisfies certain numerical conditions for all its subsets with the cut-point property.
The need to include complete graphs can also be justified by
[
2
, Theorem 3.1]
and
[
20
, Theorems 1 and 10]
.

Let
K_{m}
be a complete graph on
m
vertices and
G
be a simple graph.
Then,
\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}
is the
generalized binomial edge ideal
associated with
G
, which was previously introduced by Rauh in
[
19
]
for studying conditional independence ideals.
Inspired by the progress regarding binomial edge ideals, researchers turn to the study of the generalized binomial edge ideals. For example,
in
[
7
]
, Chaudhry and Irfan considered the depth and regularity of the generalized binomial edge ideals of connected generalized block graphs. In
[
17
]
, Kumar proved that when
G
is a
connected graph with
n
vertices, the regularity of
S/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}
is bounded above by
n-1
. Moreover, he showed that if
m\geq n
, then the regularity of
S/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}
is exactly
n-1
, and if
m<n
, then the regularity of
S/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}
is bounded below by
\max\{m-1,\ell(G)\}
, where
\ell(G)
is the length of the longest induced path in
G
. In
[
16
]
, Katsabekis studied the cohomological dimension of
\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}
. For a connected graph
G
, he found out that
\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}
is a cohomologically complete intersection for
m\geq 3
if and only if
G
is the complete graph and
\operatorname{char}({\mathbb{K}})>0
. In this case,
\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}
is Cohen–Macaulay. In
[
1
]
, Amata et al. characterized the unmixedness of the generalized binomial edge ideals associated with non–complete power cycles. Nevertheless, so far, not much is known about the algebraic properties of the generalized binomial edge ideals.

Recall that in
[
3
]
Bolognini et al. classified the
Cohen–Macaulay binomial edge ideals of bipartite graphs, giving an explicit
and recursive construction in graph-theoretic terms. As a basic block, they
introduced a family of bipartite graphs
F_{p}
whose binomial edge ideals are
Cohen–Macaulay. They also introduced an auxiliary family of non-bipartite
graphs
F_{k}^{W}(K_{n})
, called
k
-fan graphs
, whose binomial edge
ideals are also Cohen–Macaulay. In one of the most important results, they
proved that if
G
is a connected bipartite graph, then
J_{G}
is
Cohen–Macaulay if and only if
G=G_{1}*\cdots*G_{s}
, where
G_{i}=F_{p}
or

G_{i}=F_{p_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ F_{p_{t}}
for some
p\geq 1
and
p_{j}\geq 3
; see

Section

2
for the definition of the operations
*
and
\circ
.
Later, in
[
14
]
, Jayanthan et al. studied the regularity of
binomial edge ideals of these graphs by understanding the behavior of the
regularity under the operation
\circ
. In this paper, we are mainly
interested in the depth and Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity of the
generalized

binomial edge ideal associated with these graphs. Unfortunately, the
*

operation does not behave as nicely as in
[
14
]
. We compensate for this
by including computations of local cohomology modules and repetitive
glue-and-delete operations on related graphs.

The article is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we recall some essential definitions and terminology that we will need later. In Sections
3
and
4
, we study the dimension, depth, and regularity of the generalized binomial edge ideal of the fan graph
F_{k}^{W}(K_{n})
and the bipartite graph
F_{p}
respectively. We then introduce a class of simple graphs by gluing these two types of graphs together, via the
*
and
\circ
operations.
Some formulas for the depth, regularity, and dimension of the generalized binomial edge ideals within this class are given
in Sections
5
,
6
, and
7
, respectively.

2.1.
Regularity and depth

In the sequel, let
S_{+}
be the unique graded maximal ideal of the standard
graded algebra
S
. The local cohomology modules of a finitely generated
graded
S
-module
M
with respect to
S_{+}
are denoted by
H_{S_{+}}^{i}(M)

for
i\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
M
be a finitely generated graded
S
-module.

(1)

The
depth
of
M
is defined as

\operatorname{depth}\,(M)\coloneqq\min\{i:H_{S_{+}}^{i}(M)\neq 0\}.

(2)

For
i=0,\dots,\operatorname{dim}(M)
, the
i
th

a
-invariant
of
M
is defined as

a_{i}(M)\coloneqq\max\{t:(H_{S_{+}}^{i}(M))_{t}\neq 0\}

with the convention that
\max\emptyset=-\infty
.

(3)

The
Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity
of
M
is defined as

\operatorname{reg}(M)\coloneqq\max\{a_{i}(M)+i:0\leq i\leq\operatorname{dim}(M)\}.

The following lemmas are often used to compute the depth and regularity of a module.
In particular, since the facts in
2.2
are well-known, they will be used implicitly in this paper.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
M,N
be two finitely generated graded
S
-modules. Then,

\operatorname{depth}(M\oplus N)=\min\{\operatorname{depth}(M),\operatorname{depth}(N)\}
and

\operatorname{reg}(M\oplus N)=\max\{\operatorname{reg}(M),\operatorname{reg}(N)\}
.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
11
, Lemmas 2.1 and 3.1]
)
.

Let
0\rightarrow M\rightarrow N\rightarrow P\rightarrow 0
be a short
exact sequence of finitely generated graded
S
-modules.

(1)

One has
\operatorname{depth}(M)\geq\min\{\operatorname{depth}(N),\operatorname{depth}(P)+1\}
. The equality holds if
\operatorname{depth}(N)\neq\operatorname{depth}(P)
.

(2)

One also has

\operatorname{reg}(M)\leq\max\{\operatorname{reg}(N),\operatorname{reg}(P)+1\}
. The equality holds if

\operatorname{reg}(N)\neq\operatorname{reg}(P)
.

2.2.
Notions of simple graphs

Let
G
be a simple graph with the vertex set
V(G)
and the edge set
E(G)
. Let
\widetilde{G}
denote the complete graph on
V(G)
.
For any subset
A
of
V(G)
, let
G[A]
denote the
induced subgraph
of
G
on
the vertex set
A
, i.e., for
i,j\in A
,
\{i,j\}\in E(G[A])
if and only
if
\{i,j\}\in E(G)
. At the same time, we denote the induced subgraph of
G

on
V(G)\setminus A
by
G\setminus A
.

If
v
is a vertex in
G
, its
neighborhood
is defined as
N_{G}(v):=\{u\in V(G):\{u,v\}\in E(G)\}
.
Let
N_{G}[v]\coloneqq N_{G}(v)\cup\{v\}
. The vertex
v
is called a
free vertex
or
leaf
if the cardinality
|N_{G}(v)|=1
;
otherwise, it is called an
internal vertex
. We will write
G\setminus v
instead of
G\setminus\{v\}
for simplicity. Meanwhile, for this vertex,
let
G_{v}
denote the graph on the vertex set
V(G)
with edge set

E(G_{v})=E(G)\cup\{\{u,w\}:u,w\in N_{G}(v)\}
.

Let
c(G)
denote the number of connected components of
G
. A vertex
v
is
called a
cut vertex
of
G
if
c(G)<c(G\setminus v)
. Let
T
be a
subset of
V(G)
.
As an abuse of notation, we also let
c(T)
denote the number of connected
components of
G\setminus T
.
If
v
is a cut vertex of the induced subgraph
G\setminus(T\setminus\{v\})

for any
v\in T
, then we say that
T
has the
cut point property
. Set

\mathcal{C}(G)\coloneqq\{\emptyset\}\cup\Set{T:T\text{\ has the cut point
property}}
.

Finally, the graph
G
is said to be
bipartite
if there exists a decomposition
of
V(G)=V_{1}\sqcup V_{2}
into two disjoint subsets, such
that no two vertices of the same subset are adjacent in
G
.

2.3.
Basic facts regarding generalized binomial ideals

Throughout what follows, we always assume that
m,n\geq 2
are two positive
integers. The following result is well-known.

Lemma 2.4

(
[
12
, Corollary 4]
)
.

Let
\bm{X}=(x_{ij})_{1\leq i\leq m,1\leq j\leq n}
be the matrix of
indeterminates over
{\mathbb{K}}
. Then the ideal
I_{t}(\bm{X})
in
S={\mathbb{K}}[x_{ij}:i\in[m]\text{ and }j\in[n]]
, generated by all
t\times t
minors of
\bm{X}
,
is perfect of height
(m-t+1)(n-t+1)
. In particular,

S/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},K_{n}}=S/I_{2}(\bm{X})
is Cohen–Macaulay of dimension
m+n-1
.

Suppose that
G
is a simple graph on
[n]
. From now on, we will study its
generalized binomial edge ideal
\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}
in
S={\mathbb{K}}[x_{ij}:i\in[m]\text{ and }j\in[n]]
. We also write this ring as
S_{G}
when we want to emphasize
its relation to the graph
G
.

For each subset
T
of
[n]
, we can introduce the ideal

P_{T}(K_{m},G)\coloneqq(x_{ij}:(i,j)\in[m]\times T)+\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},\widetilde{G_{1}}}+\cdots+\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},\widetilde{G_{c(T)}}}

in
S
, where
G_{1},\ldots,G_{c(T)}
are the connected components of

G\setminus T
. It is clear from
2.4
that

\displaystyle\operatorname{dim}(S/P_{T}(K_{m},G))

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{c(T)}(m+|V(\widetilde{G_{i}})|-1)=c(T)(m-1)+\sum_{t=1}^{c(T)}|V(\widetilde{G}_{i})|

(1)

\displaystyle=c(T)(m-1)+|V(G)|-|T|.

The minimal primes of
\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}
can be described explicitly by these
ideals.

Lemma 2.5

(
[
19
, Theorem 7]
)
.

Let
G
be a finite simple graph. Then,

\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}=\bigcap_{T\in\mathcal{C}(G)}P_{T}(K_{m},G)

is the minimal primary decomposition of the radical ideal
\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}
.

The theoretical argument of this paper depends essentially on the study of the
following short exact sequence.

Lemma 2.6

(
[
17
, Theorem 3.2]
)
.

Let
G
be a finite simple graph and
v
be an internal vertex in
G
.
Then

\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}=\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G_{v}}\cap((x_{iv}:i\in[m])+\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G\setminus v}).

Note that

\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G_{v}}+((x_{iv}:i\in[m])+\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G\setminus v})=(x_{iv}:i\in[m])+\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G_{v}\setminus v}.

Hence, we have the following short exact sequence:

0\to\frac{S}{\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}}\to\frac{S}{\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G_{v}}}\oplus\frac{S}{(x_{iv}:i\in[m])+\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G\setminus v}}\to\frac{S}{(x_{iv}:i\in[m])+\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G_{v}\setminus v}}\to 0,

namely,

(2)

0\to\frac{S_{G}}{\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}}\to\frac{S_{G_{v}}}{\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G_{v}}}\oplus\frac{S_{G\setminus v}}{\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G\setminus v}}\to\frac{S_{G_{v}\setminus v}}{\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G_{v}\setminus v}}\to 0.

We end this section by recalling several key results for understanding the
regularity of generalized binomial edge ideals.

Lemma 2.7

(
[
20
, Proposition 8]
)
.

Let
G
be a graph over
[n]
and
H
be an induced subgraph of
G
. Then,
we have
\operatorname{reg}(S/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},H})\leq\operatorname{reg}(S/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G})
.

Corollary 2.8
.

Let
G
be a simple graph and
v
be an internal vertex of
G
. Then,

\operatorname{reg}(S/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G})\leq\max\{\operatorname{reg}(S/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G_{v}})+1,\operatorname{reg}(S/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G\setminus v})\}.

Proof.

Note that
G_{v}\setminus v
is an induced subgraph of
G_{v}
. So

\operatorname{reg}(S/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G_{v}\setminus v})\leq\operatorname{reg}(S/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G_{v}})
by

2.7
. Now, it remains to apply

2.3
2
to the exact sequence
(
2
) in
2.6
.
∎

Lemma 2.9

(
[
17
, Theorems 3.6 and 3.7]
)
.

Let
G
be a connected graph.
Then
\operatorname{reg}(S/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G})\leq|V(G)|-1
.
If additionally
m\geq|V(G)|\geq 2
, then

\operatorname{reg}(S/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G})=|V(G)|-1
.
Moreover,
\operatorname{reg}(S/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},K_{n}})=\min\{m-1,n-1\}
.

3.
Study of fan graphs

In order to study bipartite graphs whose binomial edge ideals are Cohen–Macaulay, Bolognini et al. in
[
3
]
introduced the fan graphs of complete graphs, which is a family of chordal graphs. In this section, we will study the dimension, depth, and regularity of the quotient ring of the generalized binomial edge ideal of these graphs. Let us start with the definition reformulated in
[
14
]
.

Definition 3.1

(
[
14
, Definition 3.1]
)
.

Let
K_{n}
be a complete graph on the vertex set
[n]
.

(1)

Let
U=\{u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{r}\}
be a subset of
[n]
. Suppose that for each
i\in[r]
, a new complete graph
K_{a_{i}}
with
a_{i}>i
is attached to
K_{n}
in such a way that
V(K_{n})\cap V(K_{a_{i}})=\{u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{i}\}
. We say that the resulting graph is obtained by
adding a fan
to
K_{n}
on the set
U
and
\{K_{a_{1}},\ldots,K_{a_{r}}\}
is the
branch
of that fan on
U
.

(2)

Let
W
be a subset of
[n]
and suppose that
W=W_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup W_{k}
is a partition of
W
. Let
F^{W}_{k}(K_{n})
be a graph obtained from
K_{n}
by adding a fan on each
W_{i}
. The resulting graph
F^{W}_{k}(K_{n})
is called a
k
-fan graph
of
K_{n}
on the set
W
. For future reference, for each
i\in[k]
, we assume that
\{K_{a_{i,1}},\ldots,K_{a_{i,r_{i}}}\}
is the branch of the fan on
W_{i}=\{w_{i,1},\ldots,w_{i,r_{i}}\}
. For notational convenience, we also set
h_{i,j}\coloneqq a_{i,j}-j
.

(3)

A branch
\{K_{a_{i,1}},\ldots,K_{a_{i,r_{i}}}\}
of the fan on
W_{i}
is called a
pure branch
if
h_{i,j}=1
for every
j\in[r_{i}]
. Furthermore, if each branch is pure, then
F^{W}_{k}(K_{n})
is said to be a
k
-pure fan graph
of
K_{n}
on
W
.

(4)

The complete graph
K_{n}
can be considered as a degenerate fan without branches, i.e., with
k=0
.

The following result generalizes the dimensional formula contained in the proof of
[
3
, Lemma 3.2]
for the case
m=2
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
G=F_{k}^{W}(K_{n})
be a
k
-fan graph with
n\geq 2
.
Then, we have

\operatorname{dim}(S/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G})=m+|V(G)|-1+s(m-2)

where
s=\min\{|W|,n-1\}
.

Proof.

When
k=0
, the set
W=\emptyset
and
G=K_{n}
is a complete graph. The
claimed equality is clear in this case by
2.4
.

In the following, we assume that
k\geq 1
. For each
i\in[k]
, with respect
to
W_{i}=\{w_{i,1},\ldots,w_{i,r_{i}}\}
, we set
\mathcal{C}_{i}\coloneqq\{\emptyset\}\cup\left\{\{w_{i,1},\ldots,w_{i,j}\}:j\in[r_{i}]\right\}
.
From the proof of
[
3
, Lemma 3.2]
, we know that the collection
of sets with cut point property is

\mathcal{C}(G)=\{T_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup T_{k}:T_{i}\in\mathcal{C}_{i}\text{\ and\ }T_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup T_{k}\subsetneq[n]\}.

For any
T\in\mathcal{C}(G)
, we have the corresponding decomposition
T=T_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup T_{k}
with
T_{i}\in\mathcal{C}_{i}
. It is clear that

G\setminus T
consists of the following
|T|+1
components:

(1)

the complete graphs
K_{h_{i,j}}
for each
i\in[k]
and
j\in[|T_{i}|]
, and

(2)

the fan graph
G^{\prime}
of
K_{n}\setminus T
on the set
\bigsqcup_{i}(W_{i}\setminus T_{i})
.

It follows from equation (
1
) that

\displaystyle\operatorname{dim}\left(S/P_{T}(K_{m},G)\right)

\displaystyle=(|T|+1)(m-1)+|V(G)|-|T|=m+|V(G)|-1+|T|(m-2).

Hence by
2.5
we get

\displaystyle\operatorname{dim}(S/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G})

\displaystyle=\max\{m+|V(G)|-1+|T|(m-2):T\in\mathcal{C}(G)\}

\displaystyle=m+|V(G)|-1+s(m-2)

where
s=\min\{|W|,n-1\}
.
∎

Next, we study the depth and regularity of the generalized binomial edge ideal of fan graphs in a fairly general situation.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
G=F_{k}^{W}(K_{n})
be a
k
-fan graph with
n\geq 2
. Let

\delta\coloneqq|\{(i,j):h_{i,j}\geq 2\}|
. Then, we have the following.

(1)

The depth of
S/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G}
is given by
|V(G)|+m-1
.

(2)

If
m\geq|V(G)|
, then
\operatorname{reg}(S/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G})=|V(G)|-1
.

(3)

If
m<|V(G)|
, then

\operatorname{reg}(S/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G})\leq k+(\delta+1)(m-1)
.

(4)

We have
\operatorname{reg}(S/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G})\leq\operatorname{cl}(G)(m-1)
, where

\operatorname{cl}(G)
is the number of maximal cliques of
G
. Unless
G=F_{1}^{W}(K_{n})
with
W=[n]
, this number of cliques is exactly
|W|+1
.

(5)

Suppose further that

n>1+|W|
and
h_{i,j}\geq m
for all
i\in[k]
and
j\in[r_{i}]
.
Then

\operatorname{reg}(S/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G})=(\operatorname{cl}(G)-1)(m-1)+\min\{m-1,n-|W|-1\}
.

Proof.

(1)

We prove by induction on
N:=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{r_{i}}h_{i,j}
. The base case is when
N=0
. Whence,
G=K_{n}
and

k=\delta=0
. It follows from
2.4
that

\operatorname{depth}(S/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G})=m+n-1
in this case.

In the following, we assume that
N\geq 1
. Whence,
k\geq 1
as well.
With respect to
v=w_{k,1}\in W_{k}
, we note that
G_{v}
(resp.

G_{v}\setminus v
) is a
(k-1)
-fan of

K_{n+h_{k,1}+\cdots+h_{k,r_{k}}}
(resp.

K_{n+h_{k,1}+\cdots+h_{k,r_{k}}-1}
) on the set
W\setminus W_{k}
.
Thus, by induction, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{depth}(S_{G_{v}}/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G_{v}})

\displaystyle=m+|V(G)|-1,

and

\displaystyle\operatorname{depth}\left(S_{G_{v}\setminus v}/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G_{v}\setminus v}\right)

\displaystyle=m+|V(G)|-2.

Meanwhile,
G\setminus v
is the disjoint union of
K_{h_{k,1}}

and
G^{\prime}
, where
G^{\prime}
is a
k
-fan graph of
K_{n-1}
on the set

W\setminus v
if
r_{k}\geq 2
, or a
(k-1)
-fan of
K_{n-1}
on
the set
W\setminus v
if
r_{k}=1
. Thus, by induction and

,

7.
Dimension

In this section, we are interested in the dimension of the generalized binomial edge ideals of graphs in
\mathbf{FFan}
. Unlike the depth or the Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity, the dimension does not behave very well on the short exact sequence of modules. Therefore, we can only deal with a very special case in
7.4
. Our result depends on the following simple observations.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
0\rightarrow M\rightarrow N\rightarrow P\rightarrow 0
be a short exact sequence of finitely generated graded
S
-modules. If
\operatorname{dim}(N)>\operatorname{dim}(P)
, then
\operatorname{dim}(M)=\operatorname{dim}(N)
.

Proof.

This follows easily from the fact that
\operatorname{dim}(N)=\max\{\operatorname{dim}(M),\operatorname{dim}(P)\}
.
∎

We start with a simple case.

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
G=F_{k}^{W}(K_{n})
be a
k
-fan graph. Suppose that

. Then,
\operatorname{dim}(S_{G}/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G})>\operatorname{dim}(S_{G\setminus v}/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G\setminus v})
.

Proof.

Notice that
|V(G\setminus{v})|<|V(G)|
and
\min\{|W|,n-1\}\leq\min\{|W|,(n-1)-1\}
. Thus, the desired inequality follows from
3.2
.
∎

.

Suppose that

where

with

.

(1)

, or

p\geq 2m
. At the same time, since
G\setminus v=F_{p_{1}-1}\sqcup F_{q}^{W\setminus\{v\}}(K_{n-1})
for suitable
q
, we have

\operatorname{dim}(S_{G\setminus v}/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G\setminus v})=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\operatorname{dim}(S_{G_{i}}/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G_{i}})-2m.

Meanwhile, it follows from
7.2
that

\operatorname{dim}(S_{G_{v}\setminus v}/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G_{v}\setminus v})<\operatorname{dim}(S_{G_{v}}/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G_{v}}).

In conclusion, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{dim}(S_{G\setminus v}/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G\setminus v})

\displaystyle=\max\{\operatorname{dim}(S_{G_{v}}/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G_{v}}),\operatorname{dim}(S_{G\setminus v}/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G\setminus v})\}

\displaystyle>\operatorname{dim}(S_{G_{v}\setminus v}/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G_{v}\setminus v}).

Thus, the expected result follows from applying
7.1
to the short exact sequence (
2
) in
2.6
.
∎

We can generalize the result in
7.3
.

Theorem 7.4
.

Let
G=G_{1}\circ_{v_{1}}\cdots\circ_{v_{t-1}}G_{t}\circ_{v_{t}}G_{t+1}
, where
t\geq 2
and
G_{i}=F_{p_{i}}
with
p_{i}\geq 3
for
1\leq i\leq t
. Suppose further that

(1)

G_{t+1}=F_{p_{t+1}}
with
p_{t+1}\geq 2
, or

(2)

G_{t+1}=F_{k}^{W}(K_{n})
is a
k
-fan graph with respect to the decomposition
W=W_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup W_{r}
, such that either
|W|<n
, or
|W|=n
,
|W_{1}|\geq 2
, and
v\in W_{1}
.

Then,
\operatorname{dim}(S_{G}/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G})=\sum_{i=1}^{t+1}\operatorname{dim}(S_{G_{i}}/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G_{i}})-s
, where

s=\begin{cases}2m\left\lfloor\frac{t}{2}\right\rfloor+(m+2)\left\lceil\frac{t}{2}\right\rceil,&\text{if $G_{t+1}=F_{p_{t+1}}$,}\\
2m\left\lceil\frac{t}{2}\right\rceil+(m+2)\left\lfloor\frac{t}{2}\right\rfloor,&\text{if $G_{t+1}=F_{k}^{W}(K_{n})$.}\end{cases}

As usual,
\left\lfloor\frac{t}{2}\right\rfloor
here is the largest integer
\leq\frac{t}{2}
and
\left\lceil\frac{t}{2}\right\rceil
is the smallest integer
\geq\frac{t}{2}
.

Proof.

Let
\widetilde{G}=G_{t}\circ_{v_{t}}G_{t+1}
and
H
be the complete graph on the set
N_{\widetilde{G}}[v_{t}]
. Then, by
5.5
we see that
\widetilde{G}_{v_{t}}=F_{q}^{W^{\prime}}(H)
and
\widetilde{G}_{v_{t}}\setminus v_{t}=F_{q}^{W^{\prime}}(H\setminus v_{t})
are two fan graphs, where
q=2
if
G_{t+1}=F_{p_{t+1}}
, and
q=k
if
G_{t+1}=F_{k}^{W}(K_{n})
. Furthermore, since
G_{t}=F_{p_{t}}
with
p_{t}\geq 3
, these two fans are with respect to some partition
W^{\prime}=W_{1}^{\prime}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup W_{r^{\prime}}^{\prime}
such that
|W_{1}^{\prime}|=p_{t}-1\geq 2
and
v_{t-1}\in W_{1}^{\prime}
.
We have seen in the proof of
7.3
that

(25)

\operatorname{dim}(S_{\widetilde{G}_{v_{t}}\setminus v_{t}}/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},\widetilde{G}_{v_{t}}\setminus v_{t}})<\operatorname{dim}(S_{\widetilde{G}_{v_{t}}}/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},\widetilde{G}_{v_{t}}}).

We prove the claimed formula by induction
t
. The base case
t=1
has been verified in
7.3
. Next, we assume that
t\geq 2
.

(1)

Suppose that
G_{t+1}=F_{p_{t+1}}
with
p_{t+1}\geq 2
. It follows from
7.3
1
and its proof that
|W^{\prime}|<|V(H)|
and

(26)

\operatorname{dim}(S_{\widetilde{G}_{v_{t}}}/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},\widetilde{G}_{v_{t}}})=\operatorname{dim}(S_{G_{t}}/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G_{t}})+\operatorname{dim}(S_{G_{t+1}}/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G_{t+1}})-(m+2).

Since
G_{v_{t}}=G_{1}\circ_{v_{1}}\cdots\circ_{v_{t-2}}G_{{t-1}}\circ_{v_{t-1}}\widetilde{G}_{v_{t}}
, by induction hypothesis and
Equation

26
, we get that

\displaystyle\operatorname{dim}(S_{G_{v_{t}}}/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G_{v_{t}}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}\operatorname{dim}(S_{G_{i}}/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G_{i}})+\operatorname{dim}(S_{\widetilde{G}_{v_{t}}}/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},\widetilde{G}_{v_{t}}})-s

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{t+1}\operatorname{dim}(S_{G_{i}}/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G_{i}})-s-(m+2),

for
s\coloneqq 2m\left\lceil\frac{t-1}{2}\right\rceil+(m+2)\left\lfloor\frac{t-1}{2}\right\rfloor
.
Since
G_{v_{t}}\setminus v_{t}=G_{1}\circ_{v_{1}}\cdots\circ_{v_{t-2}}G_{{t-1}}\circ_{v_{t-1}}(\widetilde{G}_{v_{t}}\setminus v_{t})
, it also follows from
Equation

25
and the induction hypothesis that

\displaystyle\operatorname{dim}(S_{G_{v_{t}}\setminus v_{t}}/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G_{v_{t}}\setminus v_{t}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}\operatorname{dim}(S_{G_{i}}/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G_{i}})+\operatorname{dim}(S_{\widetilde{G}_{v_{t}}\setminus v_{t}}/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},\widetilde{G}_{v_{t}}\setminus v_{t}})-s

\displaystyle<\operatorname{dim}(S_{G_{v_{t}}}/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G_{v_{t}}}).

In the end,
G\setminus v_{t}=G_{1}\circ_{v_{1}}\cdots\circ_{v_{t-2}}G_{{t-1}}\circ_{v_{t-1}}(G_{t}\setminus\{v_{t},f_{t}\})\sqcup(G_{t+1}\setminus\{v_{t},f_{t+1}\})
, where
f_{j}
is the leaf in
G_{j}
whose neighbor point is
v_{t}
for
j\in\{t,t+1\}
. By induction and
4.3
1
, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{dim}(S_{G\setminus v_{t}}/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G\setminus v_{t}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}\operatorname{dim}(S_{G_{i}}/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G_{i}})+\operatorname{dim}(S_{G_{t}\setminus\{v_{t},f_{t}\}/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G_{t}\setminus\{v_{t},f_{t}\}}})-s^{\prime}

\displaystyle\qquad+\operatorname{dim}(S_{G_{t+1}\setminus\{v_{t},f_{t+1}\}}/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G_{t+1}\setminus\{v_{t},f_{t+1}\}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{t+1}\operatorname{dim}(S_{G_{i}}/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G_{i}})-s^{\prime}-2m,

where
s^{\prime}\coloneqq 2m\left\lfloor\frac{t-1}{2}\right\rfloor+(m+2)\left\lceil\frac{t-1}{2}\right\rceil
. Since
m\geq 2
, we have
s+m+2\leq s^{\prime}+2m
.
In conclusion, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{dim}(S_{G_{v_{t}}}/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G_{v_{t}}})

\displaystyle=\max\{\operatorname{dim}(S_{G\setminus v_{t}}/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G\setminus v_{t}}),\operatorname{dim}(S_{G_{v_{t}}}/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G_{v_{t}}})\}

\displaystyle>\operatorname{dim}(S_{G_{v_{t}}\setminus v_{t}}/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G_{v_{t}}\setminus v_{t}}).

Therefore, after applying
7.1
to the short exact sequence (
2
) in
2.6
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\operatorname{dim}(S_{G}/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{dim}(S_{G_{v_{t}}}/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G_{v_{t}}})=\sum_{i=1}^{t+1}\operatorname{dim}(S_{G_{i}}/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G_{i}})-s-(m+2)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{t+1}\operatorname{dim}(S_{G_{i}}/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G_{i}})-2m\left\lfloor\frac{t}{2}\right\rfloor-(m+2)\left\lceil\frac{t}{2}\right\rceil.

(2)

Suppose that
G_{t+1}=F_{k}^{W}(K_{n})
. It follows from the proof of
7.3
2
that

(27)

\operatorname{dim}(S_{\widetilde{G}_{v_{t}}}/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},\widetilde{G}_{v_{t}}})=\sum_{j=t}^{t+1}\operatorname{dim}(S_{G_{j}}/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G_{j}})-p,

for some
p\geq 2m
.
Since
G_{v_{t}}=G_{1}\circ_{v_{1}}\cdots\circ_{v_{t-2}}G_{{t-1}}\circ_{v_{t-1}}\widetilde{G}_{v_{t}}
, by induction hypothesis and
Equation

27
, we get that

\displaystyle\operatorname{dim}(S_{G_{v_{t}}}/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G_{v_{t}}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}\operatorname{dim}(S_{G_{i}}/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G_{i}})+\operatorname{dim}(S_{\widetilde{G}_{v_{t}}}/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},\widetilde{G}_{v_{t}}})-s

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{t+1}\operatorname{dim}(S_{G_{i}}/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G_{i}})-s-2m,

where
s\coloneqq 2m\left\lceil\frac{t-1}{2}\right\rceil+(m+2)\left\lfloor\frac{t-1}{2}\right\rfloor
.
Since
G_{v_{t}}\setminus v_{t}=G_{1}\circ_{v_{1}}\cdots\circ_{v_{t-2}}G_{{t-1}}\circ_{v_{t-1}}(\widetilde{G}_{v_{t}}\setminus v_{t})
, it also follows from
Equation

25
and the induction hypothesis that

\displaystyle\operatorname{dim}(S_{G_{v_{t}}\setminus v_{t}}/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G_{v_{t}}\setminus v_{t}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}\operatorname{dim}(S_{G_{i}}/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G_{i}})+\operatorname{dim}(S_{\widetilde{G}\setminus v_{t}}/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},\widetilde{G}\setminus v_{t}})-s

\displaystyle<\operatorname{dim}(S_{G_{v_{t}}}/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G_{v_{t}}}).

In the end,
G\setminus v_{t}=G_{1}\circ_{v_{1}}\cdots\circ_{v_{t-2}}G_{{t-1}}\circ_{v_{t-1}}(G_{t}\setminus\{v_{t},f_{t}\})\sqcup(G_{t+1}\setminus\{v_{t},f_{t+1}\})
, where
f_{j}
is the leaf in
G_{j}
whose neighbor point is
v_{t}
for
j\in\{t,t+1\}
. By the induction and
4.3
1
, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{dim}(S_{G\setminus v_{t}}/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G\setminus v_{t}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}\operatorname{dim}(S_{G_{i}}/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G_{i}})+\operatorname{dim}(S_{G_{t}\setminus\{v_{t},f_{t}\}/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G_{t}\setminus\{v_{t},f_{t}\}}})-s^{\prime}

\displaystyle\qquad+\operatorname{dim}(S_{G_{t+1}\setminus\{v_{t},f_{t+1}\}}/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G_{t+1}\setminus\{v_{t},f_{t+1}\}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{t+1}\operatorname{dim}(S_{G_{i}}/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G_{i}})-s^{\prime}-2m,

where
s^{\prime}\coloneqq 2m\left\lfloor\frac{t-1}{2}\right\rfloor+(m+2)\left\lceil\frac{t-1}{2}\right\rceil
. Since
s^{\prime}\leq s
, it is clear that

\displaystyle\operatorname{dim}(S_{G\setminus{v_{t}}}/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G\setminus{v_{t}}})

\displaystyle=\max\{\operatorname{dim}(S_{G\setminus v_{t}}/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G\setminus v_{t}}),\operatorname{dim}(S_{G_{v_{t}}}/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G_{v_{t}}})\}

\displaystyle>\operatorname{dim}(S_{G_{v_{t}}\setminus v_{t}}/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G_{v_{t}}\setminus v_{t}}).

Therefore, it follows from the short exact sequence (
2
) in
2.6
that

\displaystyle\operatorname{dim}(S_{G}/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{dim}(S_{G\setminus{v_{t}}}/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G\setminus{v_{t}}})=\sum_{i=1}^{t+1}\operatorname{dim}(S_{G_{i}}/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G_{i}})-s^{\prime}-2m

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{t+1}\operatorname{dim}(S_{G_{i}}/\mathcal{J}_{K_{m},G_{i}})-2m\left\lceil\frac{t}{2}\right\rceil-(m+2)\left\lfloor\frac{t}{2}\right\rfloor.\qed

Non-abelian models in sQFT
Karl-Henning Rehren

Abstract
It is shown how string-localized QFT predicts interactions of the Standard Model. Detailed computations are presented for various non-abelian models: Yang-Mills, QCD, Higgs-Kibble. They serve as backup for [ 11 , 12 ] and as “proof of concept” for the treatment of the full Standard Model.



1
Purpose of these notes

These notes are not (yet) intended for journal publication. They serve as
documentation of model-specific computations backing up claims made in the more conceptual but non-technical paper

[
12
]
on string-localized QFT (sQFT) and in the paper
[
11
]

that is concerned with the
higher-order systematics of perturbation theory in sQFT rather than the actual computations.

Updates of these notes may be found once in a while at
https://www.theorie.physik.uni-goettingen.de/papers/
rehren/24/nab-models.pdf.

String-localized quantum field theory (sQFT) allows to determine
interactions by the constraints imposed by fundamental quantum
principles, and to assess the localization of interacting quantum
fields that in a Hilbert space must be – by physical necessity – at variance with the usual
“axiom of locality”. For more background information, see

[
12
,
11
]
.

The general terminology of sQFT is used without detailed
explanations. General formulas from
[
11
]
are displayed when used.

Much effort is devoted to the appearance of obstructions involving
“string-integrated delta functions” – a characteristic feature in
non-abelian models. These obstructions decouple from the determination
of higher-order interactions, but they are essential for the
localization of interacting fields.

Sect.
2
(QED) is mainly a streamlined reminder of what has been done in

[
7
,
8
]
, adapted to the needs of Sect.
3
(QCD).
Sect.
4
(Higgs-Kibble) is meant as a “proof of concept” for the more
involved ongoing analysis of the electroweak interactions
[
5
]
.

2
Quantum Electrodynamics

We review sQFT methods developped for QED in
[
8
]
. Several of
them are also useful in the non-abelian cases.

2.1
The autonomous method:
L
-
Q
pair

Define
F_{\mu\nu}
on the Wigner Fock space, and

\displaystyle A_{\mu}(x,c):=\int d\sigma(e)\,c(e)\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\,F_{\mu\nu}(x+se)e^{\nu},

(2.1)

where
d\sigma(e)
is the Lorentz invariant measure on the spacelike
hyperboloid
H_{1}
.
Because
c
must have unit weight, a variation must be of
the form
\delta c(e)=\partial^{e}_{\kappa}b^{\kappa}(e)
. It follows

\displaystyle\delta_{c}A_{\mu}(x,c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d\sigma(e)\,b^{\kappa}(e)\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\,\partial^{e}_{\kappa}(F_{\mu\nu}(x+se)e^{\nu})

(2.2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d\sigma(e)\,b^{\kappa}(e)\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\,(s\partial^{x}_{\kappa}F_{\mu\nu}(x+se)e^{\nu}+F_{\mu\kappa}(x+se))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d\sigma(e)\,b^{\kappa}(e)\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\,(s(\partial^{x}_{\mu}F_{\kappa\nu}(x+se)+\partial^{x}_{\nu}F_{\mu\kappa}(x+se))e^{\nu}+F_{\mu\kappa}(x+se))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d\sigma(e)\,b^{\kappa}(e)\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\,\big(s\partial^{x}_{\mu}F_{\kappa\nu}(x+se)e^{\nu}+\partial_{s}(sF_{\mu\kappa}(x+se))\big).

The
s
-integral over the second term vanishes, and the first term
gives

\displaystyle\delta_{c}A_{\mu}(x,c)=\partial_{\mu}w(x,\delta c)\quad\hbox{with}\quad w(x,\delta c)=\int d\sigma(e)\,b^{\kappa}(e)\int_{0}^{\infty}s\,ds\,F_{\kappa\nu}(x+se)e^{\nu}.

(2.3)

The interaction density is

\displaystyle L(c,x)=q\,L_{1}(c,x)=q\,A_{\mu}(x,c)j^{\mu}(x).

(2.4)

The
L
-
Q
pair is

\displaystyle L_{1,\rm QED}(c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{\mu}(c)j^{\mu}

(2.5)

\displaystyle Q_{1,\rm QED}^{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle wj^{\mu}.

The second-order obstruction requires to compute

\displaystyle[T,\partial_{\mu}]j^{\mu}(x)j^{\kappa}(x^{\prime})\quad\hbox{and}\quad[T,\partial_{\mu}]w(x,\delta c),A_{\kappa}(x^{\prime},c).

(2.6)

The first vanishes by the standard neutral Ward identity for the Dirac
current. For the second, we use the kinematic propagator (time-ordered
vacuum expectation value)
\langle\!\langle TF(x)F(x^{\prime})\rangle\!\rangle
and perform the string integrations to define the
kinematic propagators
\langle\!\langle TwX^{\prime}\rangle\!\rangle
and
\langle\!\langle T\partial wX^{\prime}\rangle\!\rangle
for
X=F
or

A
. With these
definitions, one has
\langle\!\langle T\partial wX^{\prime}\rangle\!\rangle=\partial\langle\!\langle TwX^{\prime}\rangle\!\rangle
because no
field equations are used under the
T
-product which could produce a
delta function when the derivative acts outside, see also Sect.
3
. Consequently, also
the second term in (
2.6
) vanishes. It follows that

\displaystyle O^{(2)}(x,x^{\prime}):=2\mathfrak{S}_{xx^{\prime}}[T,\partial_{\mu}]Q_{1}^{\mu}(x,c,\delta c)L_{1}(x^{\prime},c)=2\mathfrak{S}_{xx^{\prime}}j^{\mu}(x)\cdot[T,\partial_{\mu}]w(x,\delta c)A_{\kappa}(x^{\prime},c)\cdot j^{\kappa}(x^{\prime})

(2.7)

vanishes, and the S-matrix is string-independent without induced
interactions.

As discussed in
[
8
]
, this is true for the S-matrix on the
vacuum Hilbert space, without the extension to superselection sectors
with non-trivial “photon clouds”. The latter arise due to the
infrared singularity of QED that was not considered here.

2.2
L
-
V
pairs allow comparison with gauge theory

From now on, we use short-hand
notations for string-integrations. For
e
spacelike,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\,f(x+se)\equiv(I_{e}f)(x)\quad\hbox{and}\quad\int d\sigma(e)\,c(e)e^{\nu}(I_{e}f)(x)\equiv\big\langle e^{\nu}I_{e}f\big\rangle_{c}(x)\equiv(I_{c}^{\nu}f)(x),

(2.8)

where
c(e)
has total weight 1. Similarly
I_{u}^{\nu}=u^{\nu}I_{u}
and
I_{\ell}^{\nu}=\ell^{\nu}I_{\ell}
for

e=u
timelike or
e=\ell
lightlike, in which cases a
smearing (averaging) with
c(e)
is not necessary. For
f
vanishing
at infinity it holds

\displaystyle(\partial_{\mu}I_{v}^{\mu}f)(x)=(I_{v}^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}f)(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\,v^{\nu}\partial_{\mu}f(x+sv)=\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\,\hbox{\large{$\frac{d}{ds}$}}f(x+se)=-f(x),

(2.9)

where
v=e,u,\ell
. Because
c(e)
has total weight 1, the same
identity holds for
I_{c}
.

To establish that the string-independent S-matrix coincides with that
of gauge theory (in the vacuum sector, and at tree level), we embed
the Maxwell tensor into the indefinite state space (Krein space) of the gauge potential

A^{K}_{\mu}
in the Feynman gauge, via

\displaystyle F^{K}_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}A^{K}_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}A^{K}_{\mu}.

(2.10)

This also embeds
A_{\mu}(c)
via (
2.1
) (with
F
replaced by

F^{K}
) into the Krein space, and it holds
one has

\displaystyle A^{K}_{\mu}(x,c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I^{\nu}_{c}(\partial_{\mu}A^{K}_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}A^{K}_{\mu})=A^{K}_{\mu}(x)+\partial_{\mu}\phi(x,c)\quad\hbox{where}\quad\phi(x,c):=(I_{c}^{\nu}A^{K}_{\nu}).

(2.11)

This yields the
L
-
V
pair

\displaystyle L^{K}_{1,\rm QED}(c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{\mu}(c)j^{\mu},

(2.12)

\displaystyle K_{1,\rm QED}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A^{K}_{\mu}j^{\mu},

\displaystyle V_{1,\rm QED}^{\mu}(c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi(c)j^{\mu}.

We define kinematic propagators involving
\phi(c)
and
A^{K}_{\mu}(c)
by
performing the string integrations over the
Feynman gauge propagator
\langle\!\langle TA^{K}A^{\prime K}\rangle\!\rangle
, see Sect.
3
. Again, this makes all
obstructions of interest vanish. There are no induced interactions,
and the S-matrices computed with
L(c)=q\,A(c)j
and with
q\,A^{K}j

coincide.

However, there is an issue with Gauss’ Law. The free Maxwell field

F^{K}
satisfies

\displaystyle\partial^{\mu}F^{K}_{\mu\nu}=-\partial_{\nu}N\quad\hbox{with}\quad N=\partial^{\mu}A^{K}_{\mu},

(2.13)

known as the “fictitious current”.
N
is a null field: its
two-point function vanishes, and so do all
correlations with
F^{K}
. But its correlations with
A^{K}
do not
vanish. This produces a non-vanishing source term of “fictitious
provenience” in charged states created by the Dirac field when
F^{\mu\nu}

and
\psi
are perturbed with the local gauge interaction
q\,A^{K}j
. The
fictitious source term is cancelled in first order when
the interaction
q\,\partial_{\mu}V_{1}
is added, see

[
8
, Table 1]
.

Indeed, the perturbation theory of the fields
F^{K}
and
\psi
with
interaction
L^{K}(c)
is the same as with interaction that of
F
and
\psi
with
interaction
L(c)
, because the gauge potential
A^{K}
does not
appear in the expansion, and therefore all correlations and
propagators involving the null
field
N
vanish. By the “magic formula”
[
8
,
12
]
, one also gets the same
perturbation theory when one subjects
F^{K}
(which is its own dressed
field because all bosonic obstructions vanish) and the dressed field

\psi_{[q]}
to the local gauge interaction
q\,A^{K}j
. Schematically,

\displaystyle\{\psi,F\}|_{q\,A(c)j}=\{\psi,F^{K}\}|_{q\,A^{K}(c)j}=\{\psi_{[q]},F\}|_{q\,A^{K}j}.

(2.14)

The first version is defined on the Wigner Fock space of the electron,
positron and photon, while the locality of the interacting fields
cannot be assessed. The second version is identical with the first, but with
its fields embedded into the Krein space. The third version is defined on the
Krein space, and the perturbatively defined dressed Dirac field is string-localized.
Consequently, also the interacting Dirac field in the first two versions is
string-localized.

2.3
The dressed Dirac field (formal construction)

The “magic formula”

[
8
,
12
]

\displaystyle\psi|_{q\,A(c)j}=\psi_{[q]}|_{q\,A^{K}j}

(2.15)

characterizes properties of the interacting Dirac
field in terms of the “dressed Dirac field” as an
intermediate “free infrafield”.
The perturbative expansion of the latter is
[
11
]

\displaystyle\psi_{[q]}(x)=\exp\big[iq\int dy\,O_{V_{1}(y)}\big](\psi(x)),

(2.16)

where

\displaystyle O_{V_{1}(y)}(\psi(x)):=\phi(y)\cdot[T,\partial_{\mu}]j^{\mu}(y)\psi(x)=\phi(y)\psi(x)\cdot\delta(y-x)

(2.17)

by the standard charged Ward identity for the Dirac current, see also Sect.
3
. Iterating

O_{V_{1}}
and resumming the exponential series, this results in the
formal expression

\displaystyle\psi_{[q]}(x)=\,\colon\!e^{iq\phi(x,c)}\!\colon\cdot\psi(x).

(2.18)

However, the field
\phi(c)
is infrared divergent, so that the
all its Wick powers and the exponential series are ill-defined. In contrast, the exponential
understood as a limit of a Weyl operator can be regularized in the IR, which
provides a non-perturbative definition of (
2.18
) on an
extended Hilbert space with a rich superselection structure

[
8
, Sect. 3]
. The construction requires the support of
admissible smearing functions
c(e)
to be orthogonal to an arbitrary timelike unit
vector
u
, which ensures that two-point functions among
\phi(c)
and

\phi(c^{\prime})
define a positive definite inner product.

The magic formula requires to define
\psi_{[q]}
and the interaction

q\,A^{K}j
as operators on the same space. This requires an artifice
because
\psi_{[q]}
is defined on the superselected photon cloud
extension of the Wigner Fock space, while
q\,A^{K}j
is defined on the
Feynman gauge Krein space, and the former cannot be embedded into the
latter. While there is a formal extension
of the superselected Hilbert space to an indefinite space that also
supports
A^{K}
, the control of positivity remains unclear.

2.4
The dressed Dirac field (Hilbert space construction)

This motivates yet another version to define the perturbation theory.
One redefines the Feynman gauge potential by adding a term involving
the null field
N=(\partial A^{K})
, so
as to remove the fictitious current for the corresponding Maxwell
field:

\displaystyle\,{}^{u}\!\!A_{\mu}(x):=A^{K}_{\mu}(x)+I_{u}^{\mu}(\partial A^{K})(x)\quad\hbox{and}\quad\,{}^{u}\!F_{\mu\nu}:=\partial_{\mu}\,{}^{u}\!\!A_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}\,{}^{u}\!\!A_{\mu},

(2.19)

where
u
is an arbitrary timelike unit vector. Then
it holds
\partial\,{}^{u}\!\!A=0
and
\partial\,{}^{u}\!F=0
. Moreover, because
\langle\!\langle FN\rangle\!\rangle=0
, all correlations
of
\,{}^{u}\!F
are the same as those of
F
.

The field
\,{}^{u}\!\!A
creates from the vacuum only states for which

(uA^{K})^{-}|\Phi\rangle=0
. This follows from

\displaystyle\langle\!\langle A^{K}_{\mu}(x)\,{}^{u}\!\!A_{\nu}(x^{\prime})\rangle\!\rangle=-(\delta_{\nu}^{\kappa}+u_{\nu}I^{\prime}_{u}\partial^{\prime\kappa})\eta_{\mu\kappa}W(x-x^{\prime})=-(\eta_{\mu\nu}+u_{\nu}I^{\prime}_{u}\partial^{\prime}_{\mu})W(x-x^{\prime}),

(2.20)

and
I^{\prime}_{u}(u\partial^{\prime})=-1
. Such states
|\Phi\rangle
generate a positive-definite Fock subspace
with three spacelike massless modes (e.g.,
a_{i}^{+}(k)|0\rangle
if

u=(1,\vec{0})
with CCR
[a_{i}(k),a_{j}^{+}(k^{\prime})]=\delta_{ij}\cdot(2\pi)^{3}2k^{0}\delta(k-k^{\prime})
).

Choosing the support of
c(e)
orthogonal to
u
and defining

I_{c}^{\nu}
as in (
2.8
) with the
SO(3)
-invariant measure on the
sphere
H_{1}\cap u^{\perp}
,
one defines the associated
string-localized potential

\displaystyle\,{}^{u}\!\!A_{\mu}(x,c):=I_{c}^{\nu}(\,{}^{u}\!F_{\mu\nu})(x)=\,{}^{u}\!\!A_{\mu}(x)+\partial_{\mu}\phi(x,c),

(2.21)

where
\phi(c)
the same as in
(
2.11
) because
e_{\mu}I_{u}^{\mu}=0
.
Then one has another
L
-
V
pair

\displaystyle L^{u}_{1,\rm QED}(c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,{}^{u}\!\!A_{\mu}(c)j^{\mu},

(2.22)

\displaystyle K^{u}_{1,\rm QED}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,{}^{u}\!\!A_{\mu}j^{\mu},

\displaystyle V_{1,\rm QED}^{\mu}(c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi(c)j^{\mu},

and one may write instead of
(
2.14
)

\displaystyle\{\psi,F\}|_{q\,A(c)j}=\{\psi,\,{}^{u}\!F\}|_{q\,\,{}^{u}\!\!A(c)j}=\{\psi_{[q]},\,{}^{u}\!F\}|_{q\,\,{}^{u}\!\!Aj}.

(2.23)

In particular, because
\phi
does not depend on
u
, the dressed field
constructed with the
L
-
V
pair (
2.22
) is the same as with
the pair (
2.12
).

The advantage of the equivalences (
2.23
) is that all three
versions are manifestly positive. The disadvantage is that, unlike

\,{}^{u}\!F
,
\,{}^{u}\!\!A
is a non-local field (because

I_{u}
is a kind of inverse square root of the Laplacian
\Delta_{u}
in
the plane orthogonal to
u
, namely on the massless mass-shell it
holds
(u\partial)^{2}-\Delta_{u}=\square=0
, hence

\Delta_{u}I_{u}^{2}=(u\partial)^{2}I_{u}^{2}=1
). Then the interaction
q\,\,{}^{u}\!\!Aj

is non-local but positive, while the interaction
q\,A^{K}j
in (
2.14
)
was local but indefinite. The equivalence of the resulting
perturbative expansions has been tested in a nontrivial instance
[
8
, Sect. 4]
,
where all non-local terms involving
I_{u}
cancel out identically.

3
Yang-Mills and Quantum Chromodynamics

We want to compute the induced interactions for YM and QCD (= YM with
massless quarks), and in particular confirm that there are no induced
interactions at third order. This can be done with an
L
-
Q
pair.

We also want to compute the dressed quark field, for which we also
need an
L
-
V
pair on a Hilbert space. The latter requires the
artifice previously used in QED.

L
-
Q
pairs involving several massless fields of helicity 1 (“free gluons”),
described by Maxwell tensors
F^{a}_{\mu\nu}
(
a=1,\dots,N
) as well as
their string-localized potentials
A^{a}_{\mu}(c)
,
necessarily involve coefficients
f_{abc}
which are the completely
anti-symmetric structure constants of some Lie algebra
[
3
]
, see
below.

We assume a simple non-abelian Lie
algebra with generators satisfying

\displaystyle[\xi_{a},\xi_{b}]=if_{abc}\xi_{c},

(3.1)

and we define the symmetric inner product of the Lie algebra

\displaystyle\big\langle\xi_{a}\big|\xi_{b}\big\rangle=\delta_{ab}.

(3.2)

It enjoys the invariance property
\langle gXg^{-1}|gZg^{-1}\rangle=\langle X|Y\rangle
, implying

\displaystyle\big\langle i[X,Z]\big|Y\big\rangle=\big\langle X\big|i[Z,Y]\big\rangle,\quad\hbox{in particular}\quad\big\langle i[X,Y]\big|Y\big\rangle=0.

(3.3)

We shall write interactions in terms of Lie-algebra valued
fields
F_{\mu\nu}=F_{\mu\nu}^{a}\xi_{a}
and
A_{\mu}(c)=A_{\mu}^{a}(c)\xi_{a}
, and extend the
notations (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) to Lie-algebra valued
fields, where Wick ordering of the operators is always understood.
E.g.,
i[F,A]=-f_{abc}\,\colon\!F^{a}A^{b}\!\colon\xi_{c}
.

3.1
L
-
Q
and
L
-
V
pairs

The
L
-
Q
pair for YM is

\displaystyle L_{1,\rm YM}(c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hbox{\large{$\frac{1}{2}$}}\big\langle F^{\mu\nu}\big|i[A_{\mu}(c),A_{\nu}(c)]\big\rangle

(3.4)

\displaystyle Q_{1,\rm YM}^{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big\langle F^{\mu\nu}\big|i[w,A_{\nu}]\big\rangle\equiv-\big\langle i[F^{\mu\nu},A_{\nu}(c)]\big|w\big\rangle.

The cubic Yang-Mills interaction in the Feynman
gauge is

\displaystyle K_{1,\rm YM}=\hbox{\large{$\frac{1}{2}$}}\big\langle F^{K,\mu\nu}\big|i[A^{K}_{\mu},A^{K}_{\nu}]\big\rangle.

(3.5)

However, embedding
F
in the Krein space as in (
2.10
), there is no

L
-
V
relating the resulting
L^{K}_{1,\rm YM}(c)
to the cubic
gauge theory interaction (
3.5
) in the Feynman gauge, because
the difference is not a derivative (suppressing
irrelevant common factors and the superscript
K
for the sake of this computation):

\displaystyle L_{1}(c)-K_{1}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\big\langle F^{\mu\nu}\big|[A_{\mu}(c)-A_{\mu},A_{\nu}(c)+A_{\nu}]\big\rangle=\big\langle F^{\mu\nu}\big|[\partial_{\mu}\phi(c),A_{\nu}(c)+A_{\nu}]\big\rangle

(3.6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}\big\langle F^{\mu\nu}\big|[\phi(c),A_{\nu}(c)+A_{\nu}]\big\rangle-\big\langle\partial_{\mu}F^{\mu\nu}\big|[\phi(c),A_{\nu}(c)+A_{\nu}]\big\rangle-\big\langle F^{\mu\nu}\big|[\phi(c),\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}(c)+\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}]\big\rangle.

The last term equals
-\langle F^{\mu\nu}|[\phi(c),F_{\mu\nu}\rangle=0
by (
3.3
), but the second term
exhibits the
“fictitious current” (
2.13
):

\displaystyle\big\langle\partial^{\nu}N\big|[\phi,A_{\nu}(c)+A_{\nu}]\big\rangle=\partial^{\nu}\big\langle N\big|[\phi,A_{\nu}(c)+A_{\nu}]\big\rangle-\big\langle N\big|[\partial^{\nu}\phi,A_{\nu}(c)+A_{\nu}]\big\rangle-\big\langle N\big|[\phi,\partial^{\nu}A_{\nu}(c)+\partial^{\nu}A_{\nu}]\big\rangle,\quad

(3.7)

where the last term equals
-\langle N|[\phi,N]\rangle=0
by (
3.7
) and
(
3.3
), and the second term equals

\displaystyle-2\big\langle N\big|[\partial^{\nu}\phi,A_{\nu}]\big\rangle=-2\partial^{\nu}\big\langle N\big|[\phi,A_{\nu}]\big\rangle+2\big\langle\partial^{\nu}N\big|[\phi,A_{\nu}]\big\rangle+2\big\langle N\big|[\phi,N]\big\rangle.

(3.8)

Again, the last term vanishes but the second term does not. Thus,

L_{1}(c)-K_{1}
is not a derivative.

We shall therefore proceed with vector potentials
\,{}^{u}\!\!A^{a}

(
a=1,\dots,N
) as introduced in QED, so that
\partial\,{}^{u}\!F^{a}=0
. Then,
redoing (
3.6
) with
\,{}^{u}\!\!A
, we have the
L
-
V
pair for YM

\displaystyle L_{1,\rm YM}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hbox{\large{$\frac{1}{2}$}}\big\langle\,{}^{u}\!F^{\mu\nu}\big|i[\,{}^{u}\!\!A_{\mu}(c),\,{}^{u}\!\!A_{\nu}(c)]\big\rangle,

(3.9)

\displaystyle K_{1,\rm YM}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hbox{\large{$\frac{1}{2}$}}\big\langle\,{}^{u}\!F^{\mu\nu}\big|i[\,{}^{u}\!\!A_{\mu},\,{}^{u}\!\!A_{\nu}]\big\rangle,

\displaystyle V^{\mu}_{1,\rm YM}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big\langle\,{}^{u}\!F^{\mu\nu}\big|i[\phi,\,{}^{u}\!\!A_{\nu}(c)+\,{}^{u}\!\!A_{\nu}]\big\rangle.

The
L
-
Q
and
L
-
V
pairs for QCD are obtained by adding

\displaystyle L_{1,\rm QCD,min}=\big\langle\,{}^{u}\!\!A_{\mu}(c)\big|j^{\mu}\big\rangle,

(3.10)

\displaystyle Q^{\mu}_{1,\rm QCD,min}=\big\langle w\big|j^{\mu}\big\rangle,

\displaystyle K_{1,\rm QCD,min}=\big\langle\,{}^{u}\!\!A_{\mu}\big|j^{\mu}\big\rangle,

\displaystyle V^{\mu}_{1,\rm QCD,min}=\big\langle\phi(c)\big|j^{\mu}\big\rangle

to (
3.4
) and (
3.9
), where
j^{\mu}_{a}=\overline{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\pi(\xi_{a})\psi
.

3.2
Obstructions

All obstructions of the S-matrix can be
computed in terms of “obstruction maps”

\displaystyle O_{Y(x)}(X(x^{\prime})):=T[\partial^{x}_{\mu}Y^{\mu}(x)X(x^{\prime})]-\partial^{x}_{\mu}T[Y^{\mu}(x)X(x^{\prime})],\quad\hbox{in short}\quad O_{Y}(X^{\prime})=[T,\partial_{\mu}]Y^{\mu}X^{\prime},

(3.11)

where
X
and
Y^{\mu}
are Wick polynomials. For our purposes, tree
level is sufficient, and is always understood. Then by Wick’s theorem,

\displaystyle O_{Y}(X^{\prime})=\sum_{\varphi,\chi}\hbox{\large{$\frac{\partial Y^{\mu}}{\partial\varphi}$}}(x)O_{\mu}(\varphi(x),\chi(x^{\prime}))\hbox{\large{$\frac{\partial X}{\partial\chi}$}}(x^{\prime})\equiv\sum_{\varphi,\chi}\hbox{\large{$\frac{\partial Y^{\mu}}{\partial\varphi}$}}O_{\mu}(\varphi,\chi^{\prime})\hbox{\large{$\frac{\partial X^{\prime}}{\partial\chi^{\prime}}$}}

(3.12)

where the sum runs over all linear free fields appearing in
Y
and

X
, and the “two-point obstructions” are defined in terms of the
the propagators = time-ordered two-point functions

\displaystyle O_{\mu}(\varphi(x),\chi(x^{\prime}))=\langle\!\langle T[\partial^{x}_{\mu}\varphi(x)\chi(x^{\prime})]\rangle\!\rangle-\partial^{x}_{\mu}\langle\!\langle T[\varphi(x)\chi(x^{\prime})]\rangle\!\rangle\equiv\langle\!\langle[T,\partial_{\mu}]\varphi\chi^{\prime}\rangle\!\rangle.

(3.13)

They may be non-vanishing whenever in the first term some equation of
motion is used under the
T
-product, or when non-kinematic
propagators are used, as will be necessary in Sect.
4
.

3.3
Propagators and two-point obstructions

All obstruction maps
are computed in terms of the propagators of free fields.
We use the shorthand notation
T_{m}\equiv T_{m}(x-x^{\prime})
for the time-ordered
two-point function of a scalar field of mass

3.10
“Kinematic confinement?”

B. Schroer has speculated whether the infrared behaviour of the dressed
quark field might already show signals of confinement, e.g., by one of
the criteria prevailing in the literature.

Recall that the IR superselection structure of QED arises by using the Weyl algebra:
correlation functions of dressing factors are exponentials of the
two-point function of the escort field. The IR structure cannot be seen in any
finite order of the exponential series.

We expect the same also in the non-abelian case. Unfortunately, our
methods (both the obstruction formalism to compute the dressed field
in
L
-
V
, and the computation of the classical “smeared Wilson
operator” by imposing (
3.104
)) are perturbative (recursive
order by order), and therefore not suitable for any control of
“kinematic confinement”.

Some all-order results have been obtained by M. Pabst

[
10
]
for the field

\pi(e^{ig\phi})\psi

with the ordinary exponential in the representation
\pi
in which the
quarks transform. This field arises as the dressed field when the
initial
L
-
V
pair is given by
K_{1}=0

and
L_{1}=\partial_{\mu}U_{1}^{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}\langle\phi\big|j^{\mu}\rangle
(notes KHR
2022/23: one finds
K_{n}=0
in all orders and
L_{n}=\langle\mathrm{ad}_{i\phi}^{n-1}(\partial\phi)\big|j^{\mu}\rangle
which amounts to a non-abelian pure-gauge vector potential).
The computation of its two-point function requires the knowledge of
the values of infinitely many Casimir operators

C^{\pi}_{n}:=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}C^{\pi}_{n}(\sigma),\qquad C^{\pi}_{n}(\sigma):=\pi\big(\tau_{a_{1}}\dots\tau_{a_{n}}\tau_{a_{\sigma(n)}}\dots\tau_{a_{\sigma(1)}}\big)

(
n\in\mathbb{N}
) in the representation
\pi
. These are not know (to me)
in closed form.

Pabst succeeded to compute the two-point function by a different
method, for the
spin
\frac{1}{2}
and spin
1
representations of
su(2)
. In both cases, he found
essentially the same IR behaviour that leads to an IR superselection
structure, as in QED.

Thus, if there is any “kinematic confinement”, then it cannot be
seen in this simple model. Starting from
K_{1}=\langle A_{\mu}\big|j^{\mu}\rangle

and
L_{1}=\langle A_{\mu}(c)\big|j^{\mu}\rangle
(“colored QCD”) turns out to
be inconsistent (the SI condition for the S-matrix cannot be
fulfilled), unless one adds the
L
-
V
pair of YM
(“lock-key”). Thus, one is forced to study the dressed field of QCD, as in
Sect.
3.9
, with a non-perturbative closed expression for
W(c)

lacking.

Trying to compute the two-point function in lowest orders, one notices
two things. (i): Rather than the Casimir operators
C_{n}^{\pi}
, one needs all
Casimir operators
C_{n}^{\pi}(\sigma)
separately, and (ii): Without
smearing with
c(e)
, the resulting momentum integrals are not only
divergent (as in the abelian case) but ill-defined. In the
abelian case, the “non-perturbative” Weyl-algebra
methods allowed to get a finite result.

Abstract

We begin with some useful identities whose proofs can be shown algebraically.

Lemma 1
.

Suppose that
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}
are such that all quantities below are well-defined. Then

(a)

[\alpha,1,\beta,\gamma]=[\alpha+1,-(\beta+1),-\gamma],

(b)

[\alpha,\beta,\gamma]=[\alpha+1,-1,-(\beta-1),-\gamma],\text{ and}

(c)

[\alpha,0,\gamma]=[\alpha+\gamma].

\displaystyle=[\alpha+1,\underbrace{-2,2,-2,\dots,\pm 2}_{\beta-1\text{ terms}},(-1)^{\beta}(\gamma+1)].

\displaystyle E(\llbracket q_{0}-1,1\rrbracket\oplus g(-\llbracket q_{1}+1,q_{2},\dots,q_{t+1}\rrbracket)\text{ by the definition of $g$}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q_{0}-1+\cfrac{1}{1+\cfrac{1}{(E\circ g)(-\llbracket q_{1}+1,q_{2},\dots,q_{t+1}\rrbracket)}}\text{ by Lemma~\ref{Eid}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[q_{0}-1,1,-(q_{1}+1),-q_{2}\dots,-q_{t+1}]\text{ by the induction hypothesis}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[q_{0},q_{1},\dots,q_{t+1}]\text{ by Lemma~\ref{firstid}(a)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E(\llbracket q_{0},q_{1},\dots,q_{t+1}\rrbracket).

Case 2:
Suppose
q_{1}=-1
and
q_{2}=1
. Then

\displaystyle(E\circ g)(\llbracket q_{0},q_{1},\dots,q_{t+1}\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(E\circ g)(\llbracket q_{0}-1\rrbracket\oplus-\llbracket q_{2}-1,q_{3},\dots,q_{t+1}\rrbracket)\text{ by the definition of $g$}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(E\circ g)(\llbracket q_{0}-q_{3}-1,-q_{4},\dots,-q_{t+1}\rrbracket)\text{ by the definition of $\oplus$}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[q_{0}-q_{3}-1,-q_{4},\dots,-q_{t+1}]\text{ by the induction hypothesis}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[q_{0}-1,0,-q_{3},-q_{4},\dots,-q_{t+1}]\text{ by Lemma~\ref{firstid}(c)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[q_{0},-1,1,q_{3},\dots,q_{t+1}]\text{ by Lemma~\ref{firstid}(b)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E(\llbracket q_{0},q_{1},\dots,q_{t+1}\rrbracket).

Case 3:
Suppose
q_{1}=-1
and
q_{2}\neq 1
. Then

\displaystyle(E\circ g)(\llbracket q_{0},q_{1},\dots,q_{t+1}\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(E\circ g)(\llbracket q_{0}-1\rrbracket\oplus-\llbracket q_{2}-1,q_{3},\dots,q_{t+1}\rrbracket)\text{ by the definition of $g$}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(E\circ g)(\llbracket q_{0}-1,-(q_{2}-1),-q_{3},\dots,-q_{t+1}\rrbracket)\text{ by the definition of $\oplus$}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[q_{0}-1,-(q_{2}-1),-q_{3},\dots,-q_{t+1}]\text{ by the induction hypothesis}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[q_{0},-1,q_{2},q_{3},\dots,q_{t+1}]\text{ by Lemma~\ref{firstid}(b)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E(\llbracket q_{0},q_{1},\dots,q_{t+1}\rrbracket).

For the remaining case,
q_{1}>0
, if
r=1
, then the result is trivial and if
r>1
, then it is similar to case 1. Having exhausted all possibilities, the result follows by induction.
∎

We have shown thus far that
f_{u,v}
and
g
are well-behaved with respect to continued fraction representations, much like the original
f
and
g
were in
[
4
]
. The functions
f_{u,v}
attempt to find equivalent continued fraction representations satisfying the
(u,v)
-divisibility property while
g
computes the short continued fraction representation or a rational number.

Before we can proceed to the main results, we need to show that the new definition of
g
continues to hold similar properties to those of the function of the same name as it was originally defined in
[
4
]
.

Lemma 4
.

For all
\llbracket q_{0},q_{1},\dots,q_{r}\rrbracket\in A_{3}
, if

g(\llbracket q_{0},q_{1},\dots,q_{r}\rrbracket)=\llbracket q^{\prime}_{0},q^{\prime}_{1},\dots,q^{\prime}_{s}\rrbracket,

then for any integer
n
,

g(\llbracket q_{0}+n,q_{1},\dots,q_{r}\rrbracket)=\llbracket q^{\prime}_{0}+n,q^{\prime}_{1},\dots,q^{\prime}_{s}\rrbracket.

The proof of Lemma
4
is trivial and therefore omitted.

Lemma 5
.

For all
\llbracket q_{0},q_{1},\dots,q_{r}\rrbracket\in A_{3}
, if

g(\llbracket q_{0},q_{1},\dots,q_{r}\rrbracket)=\llbracket q^{\prime}_{0},q^{\prime}_{1},\dots,q^{\prime}_{s}\rrbracket

is such that
\llbracket q_{0},q_{1},\dots,q_{r}\rrbracket
satisfies the
(u,v)
-divisibility property with
u+v>4
and
q_{0}>0
, then
q^{\prime}_{0}\geq 0
. In particular,
q^{\prime}_{0}
is equal to either
q_{0}+1
,
q_{0}
,
q_{0}-1
or
q_{0}-2
, with the latter only possible when
u=1
and
v\geq 4
.

Proof.

We can prove the lemma by examining three cases. Note that by the definition of
g
, we can assume that
r>1
.

Case 1:
Suppose
q_{1}>-1
. Then
q^{\prime}_{0}=q_{0}\geq 0
.

Case 2:
Suppose
q_{1}<-1
. Then
q^{\prime}_{0}=q_{0}-1\geq 0
.

Case 3:
Suppose
q_{1}=-1
. Then
q_{0}\geq 4
, so

\displaystyle g(\llbracket q_{0},q_{1},q_{2},\dots,q_{r}\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=g(\llbracket q_{0}-1\rrbracket\oplus-\llbracket q_{2}-1,\dots,q_{r}\rrbracket).

Since it cannot be the case that
q_{2}=2
, it follows that this case resolves to a previous case with
q_{0}-1
in place of
q_{0}
. From this we see that the desired result holds.

Having exhausted all possible cases, the statement of the lemma holds.
∎

Proposition 1
.

If
\llbracket q_{0},q_{1},\dots,q_{r}\rrbracket\in A_{3}
satisfies the
(u,v)
-divisibility property with
u+v>4
, then

g(\llbracket q_{0},q_{1},\dots,q_{r}\rrbracket)\in A_{1}
.

Proof.

We will prove the desired result by strong induction on
r
. Note that by Lemma
4
, we may assume that
q_{0}
is a positive multiple of
v
.

Suppose that
r=0
. Then the result follows immediately since
g(\llbracket q_{0}\rrbracket)=q_{0}
.

Suppose that
r=1
. If
q_{1}=\pm 1
, then
g(\llbracket q_{0},\pm 1\rrbracket)=\llbracket q_{0}\pm 1\rrbracket\in A_{1}
. If
q_{1}=-2
, then
g(\llbracket q_{0},-2\rrbracket)=\llbracket q_{0}-1,2\rrbracket\in A_{1}
. If
q_{1}<-2
, then
g(\llbracket q_{0},q_{1}\rrbracket)=\llbracket q_{0}-1,1,-(q_{1}+1)\rrbracket\in A_{1}
. If
q_{1}>1
, then
g(\llbracket q_{0},q_{1}\rrbracket)=\llbracket q_{0},q_{1}\rrbracket\in A_{1}
.

Suppose that the result holds for
0\leq r\leq t
for some
t\geq 1
and all pairs
u
and
v
with
u+v>4
.

Case 1:
Suppose
q_{1}>-1
. Then

\displaystyle g(\llbracket q_{0},q_{1},q_{2},\dots,q_{t+1}\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=\llbracket q_{0}\rrbracket\oplus g(\llbracket q_{1},\dots,q_{t+1}\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=\llbracket q_{0}\rrbracket\oplus\llbracket p_{0},\dots,p_{s}\rrbracket.

In this case we have that
\llbracket q_{1},\dots,q_{t+1}\rrbracket
satisfies the
(v,u)
-divisibility property, so
\llbracket p_{0},\dots,p_{s}\rrbracket\in A_{1}
by the induction hypothesis and
p_{0}\geq 0
by Lemma
5
. In particular,
p_{i}>0
for
0<i\leq s
. If
p_{0}>0
, then

\llbracket q_{0}\rrbracket\oplus\llbracket p_{0},\dots,p_{s}\rrbracket=\llbracket q_{0},p_{0},\dots,p_{s}\rrbracket.

Whereas if
p_{0}=0
, then

\llbracket q_{0}\rrbracket\oplus\llbracket p_{0},\dots,p_{s}\rrbracket=\llbracket q_{0}+p_{1},\dots,p_{s}\rrbracket.

In either situation we get the desired result.

Case 2:
Suppose
q_{1}<-1
. Then

\displaystyle g(\llbracket q_{0},q_{1},q_{2},\dots,q_{t+1}\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=\llbracket q_{0}-1,1\rrbracket\oplus g(-\llbracket q_{1}-1,q_{2},\dots,q_{t+1}\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=\llbracket q_{0}-1,1\rrbracket\oplus g(-\llbracket q_{1}-1,q_{2},\dots,q_{t+1}\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=\llbracket q_{0}-1,1\rrbracket\oplus\llbracket p_{0}+1,p_{1},\dots,p_{s}\rrbracket

by Lemma
4
, where

g(-\llbracket q_{1},q_{2},\dots,q_{t+1}\rrbracket)=\llbracket p_{0},p_{1},\dots,p_{s}\rrbracket.

Since
-\llbracket q_{1},q_{2},\dots,q_{t+1}\rrbracket
satisfies the
(v,u)
-divisibility property and
-q_{1}>1
, then
\llbracket p_{0},p_{1},\dots,p_{s}\rrbracket\in A_{1}
by the induction hypothesis, so
\llbracket p_{0}+1,p_{1}\dots,p_{s}\rrbracket\in A_{1}
. The remaining portion of this case follows similarly to case 1.

Case 3:
Suppose
q_{1}=-1
. Then

\displaystyle g(\llbracket q_{0},q_{1},q_{2},\dots,q_{t+1}\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=g(\llbracket q_{0}-1\rrbracket\oplus-\llbracket q_{2}-1,\dots,q_{t+1}\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=g(\llbracket q_{0}-1,1-q_{2},-q_{3},\dots,-q_{t+1}\rrbracket).

If
q_{2}<0
, then

g(\llbracket q_{0},q_{1},q_{2},\dots,q_{t+1}\rrbracket)=\llbracket q_{0}-1\rrbracket\oplus g(\llbracket 1-q_{2},-q_{3},\dots,-q_{t+1}\rrbracket)

and we can use an argument similar to case 2. Alternatively, if
q_{2}>0
, then

g(\llbracket q_{0},q_{1},q_{2},\dots,q_{t+1}\rrbracket)=\llbracket q_{0}-2,1\rrbracket\oplus g(\llbracket q_{2}-2,q_{3},\dots,q_{t+1}\rrbracket)

and we can, again, use an argument similar to case 2 since it must be the case that
q_{2}\geq 4
.

Having exhausted all possibilities, the result follows by strong induction.
∎

Corollary 3
.

If
\llbracket q_{0},q_{1},\dots,q_{r}\rrbracket\in A_{3}
satisfies the
(u,v)
-divisibility property with
u+v>4
, then

g(\llbracket q_{0},q_{1},\dots,q_{r}\rrbracket)=(C\circ E)(\llbracket q_{0},q_{1},\dots,q_{r}\rrbracket).

Proof.

Let
\llbracket q^{\prime}_{0},q^{\prime}_{1},\dots,q^{\prime}_{s}\rrbracket=g(\llbracket q_{0},q_{1},\dots,q_{r}\rrbracket)
. By Proposition
1
,
\llbracket q^{\prime}_{0},q^{\prime}_{1},\dots,q^{\prime}_{s}\rrbracket\in A_{1}
. Furthermore,
q^{\prime}_{s}>1
, so
[q^{\prime}_{0},q^{\prime}_{1},\dots,q^{\prime}_{s}]
is the short continued fraction representation of a rational number. By Corollary
2
and the uniqueness of short continued fraction representations, it must be the case that

\displaystyle(C\circ E)(\llbracket q_{0},q_{1},\dots,q_{r}\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=(C\circ E\circ g)(\llbracket q_{0},q_{1},\dots,q_{r}\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=(C\circ E)(\llbracket q^{\prime}_{0},q^{\prime}_{1},\dots,q^{\prime}_{s}\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=C([q^{\prime}_{0},q^{\prime}_{1},\dots,q^{\prime}_{s}])

\displaystyle=\llbracket q^{\prime}_{0},q^{\prime}_{1},\dots,q^{\prime}_{s}\rrbracket

\displaystyle=g(\llbracket q_{0},q_{1},\dots,q_{r}\rrbracket).

∎

We are now in a position to prove the analogue of Proposition 2.9 in
[
4
]
, albeit under a more restricted hypothesis.

Proposition 2
.

If
\llbracket q_{0},q_{1},\dots,q_{r}\rrbracket\in A_{3}
satisfies the
(u,v)
-divisibility property with
u+v>4
, then

\displaystyle(f_{u,v}\circ g)(\llbracket q_{0},q_{1},\dots,q_{r}\rrbracket)=\llbracket q_{0},q_{1},\dots,q_{r}\rrbracket.

Proof.

We will prove the desired result by strong induction on
r
.

Suppose that
r=0
. Then the result follows immediately for all possible pairs
u
and
v
since
(f_{u,v}\circ g)(\llbracket q_{0}\rrbracket)=f_{u,v}(\llbracket q_{0}\rrbracket)=\llbracket q_{0}\rrbracket
.

Suppose that
r=1
. If
q_{1}=\pm 1
, then

\displaystyle(f_{u,v}\circ g)(\llbracket q_{0},\pm 1\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=f_{u,v}(\llbracket q_{0}\pm 1\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=\llbracket q_{0},\pm 1\rrbracket.

If
q_{1}=-2
, then it must be the case that
u=1
or
2
. If
u=1
, then

\displaystyle(f_{1,v}\circ g)(\llbracket q_{0},-2\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=f_{1,v}(\llbracket q_{0}-1,2\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=\llbracket q_{0},-1\rrbracket\oplus-f_{1,v}(\llbracket 1\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=\llbracket q_{0},-1\rrbracket\oplus-\llbracket 0,1\rrbracket

\displaystyle=\llbracket q_{0},-2\rrbracket.

Otherwise,

\displaystyle(f_{2,v}\circ g)(\llbracket q_{0},-2\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=f_{2,v}(\llbracket q_{0}-1,2\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=\llbracket q_{0}\rrbracket\oplus-f_{v,2}(\llbracket 1,1\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=\llbracket q_{0}\rrbracket\oplus-(\llbracket 2\rrbracket\oplus-f_{2,v}(\lambda))

\displaystyle=\llbracket q_{0}\rrbracket\oplus-(\llbracket 2\rrbracket\oplus\lambda)

\displaystyle=\llbracket q_{0},-2\rrbracket.

If
q_{1}<-2
, then

\displaystyle(f_{u,v}\circ g)(\llbracket q_{0},q_{1}\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=f_{u,v}(\llbracket q_{0}-1,1\rrbracket\oplus g(-\llbracket q_{1}+1\rrbracket))

\displaystyle=f_{u,v}(\llbracket q_{0}-1,1,-(q_{1}+1)\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=\llbracket q_{0}\rrbracket\oplus-f_{v,u}(\llbracket-q_{1}\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=\llbracket q_{0},q_{1}\rrbracket

If
q_{1}>1
, then

\displaystyle(f_{u,v}\circ g)(\llbracket q_{0},q_{1}\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=f_{u,v}(\llbracket q_{0}\rrbracket\oplus g(\llbracket q_{1}\rrbracket))

\displaystyle=f_{u,v}(\llbracket q_{0},q_{1}\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=\llbracket q_{0}\rrbracket\oplus f_{v,u}(\llbracket q_{1}\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=\llbracket q_{0},q_{1}\rrbracket.

Having exhausted all possibilities, we have that the result holds for
r=1
.

Suppose that the result holds for
0\leq r\leq t
for some
t\geq 1
and all pairs
u
and
v
with
u+v>4
.

Case 1:
Suppose
q_{1}>0
. Then

\displaystyle(f_{u,v}\circ g)(\llbracket q_{0},q_{1},q_{2},\dots,q_{t+1}\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=f_{u,v}(\llbracket q_{0}\rrbracket\oplus g(\llbracket q_{1},q_{2},\dots,q_{t+1}\rrbracket))

\displaystyle=\llbracket q_{0}\rrbracket\oplus(f_{v,u}\circ g)(\llbracket q_{1},q_{2},\dots,q_{t+1}\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=\llbracket q_{0},q_{1},q_{2},\dots,q_{t+1}\rrbracket{\text{ by the induction hypothesis,}}

as desired.

Case 2:
Suppose
q_{1}<-1
. Let

(1)

\llbracket q^{\prime}_{0},\dots,q^{\prime}_{s}\rrbracket=g(-\llbracket q_{1}+1,q_{2},\dots,q_{t+1}\rrbracket).

Then

\displaystyle(f_{u,v}\circ g)(\llbracket q_{0},q_{1},q_{2},\dots,q_{t+1}\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=f_{u,v}(\llbracket q_{0}-1,1\rrbracket\oplus g(-\llbracket q_{1}+1,q_{2},\dots,q_{t+1}\rrbracket))

\displaystyle=f_{u,v}(\llbracket q_{0}-1,1\rrbracket\oplus\llbracket q^{\prime}_{0},\dots,q^{\prime}_{s}\rrbracket),

where
q^{\prime}_{0}\geq 0
by Lemma
5
. If
q^{\prime}_{0}>0
, then, using Lemma
4
and the induction hypothesis,

\displaystyle(f_{u,v}\circ g)(\llbracket q_{0},q_{1},q_{2},\dots,q_{t+1}\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=f_{u,v}(\llbracket q_{0}-1,1,q^{\prime}_{0},\dots,q^{\prime}_{s}\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=\llbracket q_{0}\rrbracket\oplus-f_{v,u}(\llbracket q^{\prime}_{0}+1,q^{\prime}_{1},\dots,q^{\prime}_{s}\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=\llbracket q_{0}\rrbracket\oplus-(f_{v,u}\circ g)(-\llbracket q_{1},q_{2},\dots,q_{t+1}\rrbracket){\text{ by Lemma~\ref{firstEntry}}}

\displaystyle=\llbracket q_{0}\rrbracket\oplus\llbracket q_{1},q_{2},\dots,q_{t+1}\rrbracket

\displaystyle=\llbracket q_{0},q_{1},q_{2},\dots,q_{t+1}\rrbracket.

Otherwise, if
q^{\prime}_{0}=0
, then using (
1
) and Lemma
4
,

(2)

g(\llbracket-q_{1},-q_{2},\dots,-q_{t+1}\rrbracket)=\llbracket 1,q^{\prime}_{1},q^{\prime}_{2},\dots,q^{\prime}_{s}\rrbracket.

In view of Lemma
5
, for (
2
) to hold, it must be the case that
u\leq 2
,
q_{1}=-2
, and
q_{2}\geq 3
. In particular,

(3)

\displaystyle(f_{u,v}\circ g)(\llbracket q_{0},q_{1},q_{2},\dots,q_{t+1}\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=(f_{u,v}\circ g)(\llbracket q_{0},-2,q_{2},\dots,q_{t+1}\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=f_{u,v}(\llbracket q_{0}-1,1\rrbracket\oplus g(-\llbracket-1,q_{2},\dots,q_{t+1}\rrbracket))

\displaystyle=f_{u,v}(\llbracket q_{0}-1,1\rrbracket\oplus(\llbracket 0,1\rrbracket\oplus g(\llbracket q_{2}-1,q_{3},\dots,q_{t+1}\rrbracket)))

\displaystyle=f_{u,v}(\llbracket q_{0}-1,2\rrbracket\oplus g(\llbracket q_{2}-1,q_{3},\dots,q_{t+1}\rrbracket)).

Let

(4)

\llbracket q^{\ast}_{0},q^{\ast}_{1},\dots,q^{\ast}_{w}\rrbracket=g(\llbracket q_{2},q_{3},\dots,q_{t+1}\rrbracket).

If
u=1
, then using (
3
), (
4
), and Lemma
4
,

(5)

\displaystyle(f_{u,v}\circ g)(\llbracket q_{0},q_{1},q_{2},\dots,q_{t+1}\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=\llbracket q_{0},-1\rrbracket\oplus-f_{u,v}(\llbracket 1\rrbracket\oplus g(\llbracket q_{2}-1,q_{3},\dots,q_{t+1}\rrbracket))

(6)

\displaystyle=\llbracket q_{0},-1\rrbracket\oplus-f_{u,v}(\llbracket 1\rrbracket\oplus\llbracket q^{\ast}_{0}-1,q^{\ast}_{1},\dots,q^{\ast}_{w}\rrbracket){\text{ by Lemma~\ref{firstEntry}}}.

Note that since
u=1
, we must have
q_{2}\geq 4
, so by Lemma
5
,
q^{\ast}_{0}\geq 2
. It follows using (
4
) and (
5
) that

\displaystyle(f_{u,v}\circ g)(\llbracket q_{0},q_{1},q_{2},\dots,q_{t+1}\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=\llbracket q_{0},-1\rrbracket\oplus-f_{u,v}(\llbracket 1,q^{\ast}_{0}-1,q^{\ast}_{1},\dots,q^{\ast}_{w}\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=\llbracket q_{0},-1\rrbracket\oplus-(\llbracket 0,1\rrbracket\oplus-f_{u,v}(\llbracket q^{\ast}_{0},q^{\ast}_{1},\dots,q^{\ast}_{w}\rrbracket))

\displaystyle=\llbracket q_{0},-2\rrbracket\oplus(f_{u,v}\circ g)(\llbracket q_{2},q_{3},\dots,q_{t+1}\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=\llbracket q_{0},-2,q_{2},q_{3},\dots,q_{t+1}\rrbracket{\text{ by the induction hypothesis.}}

Alternatively, suppose that
u=2
. It again follows from Lemma
5
that
q^{\ast}_{0}\geq 2
. Using (
3
), (
4
), and Lemma
4
,

\displaystyle(f_{u,v}\circ g)(\llbracket q_{0},q_{1},q_{2},\dots,q_{t+1}\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=\llbracket q_{0}\rrbracket\oplus-f_{v,u}(\llbracket 1,1\rrbracket\oplus g(\llbracket q_{2}-1,q_{3},\dots,q_{t+1}\rrbracket))

\displaystyle=\llbracket q_{0}\rrbracket\oplus-f_{v,u}(\llbracket 1,1\rrbracket\oplus\llbracket q^{\ast}_{0}-1,q^{\ast}_{1},\dots,q^{\ast}_{w}\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=\llbracket q_{0}\rrbracket\oplus-(\llbracket 2\rrbracket\oplus-f_{u,v}(\llbracket q^{\ast}_{0},q^{\ast}_{1},\dots,q^{\ast}_{w}\rrbracket))

\displaystyle=\llbracket q_{0}\rrbracket\oplus-(\llbracket 2\rrbracket\oplus-(f_{u,v}\circ g)(\llbracket q_{2},q_{3},\dots,q_{t+1}\rrbracket))

\displaystyle=\llbracket q_{0},-2,q_{2},q_{3},\dots,q_{t+1}\rrbracket{\text{ by the induction hypothesis.}}

So the result holds in this case.

Case 3:
Suppose
q_{1}=-1
. Let

(7)

\llbracket q^{\dagger}_{0},q^{\dagger}_{1},\dots,q^{\dagger}_{y}\rrbracket=g(\llbracket-q_{2},-q_{3},\dots,-q_{t+1}\rrbracket).

By assumption, we must have
u=1
and
v\geq 4
. If
q_{2}\leq-4
, then, using (
7
) and Lemma
4
,

\displaystyle(f_{u,v}\circ g)(\llbracket q_{0},-1,q_{2},\dots,q_{t+1}\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=(f_{u,v}\circ g)(\llbracket q_{0}-1,1-q_{2},-q_{3},\dots,-q_{t+1}\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=f_{u,v}(\llbracket q_{0}-1\rrbracket\oplus g(\llbracket 1-q_{2},-q_{3},\dots,-q_{t+1}\rrbracket))

\displaystyle=f_{u,v}(\llbracket q_{0}-1\rrbracket\oplus\llbracket q^{\dagger}_{0}+1,q^{\dagger}_{1},\dots,q^{\dagger}_{y}\rrbracket){\text{ by Lemma~\ref{firstEntry}}}

\displaystyle=f_{u,v}(\llbracket q_{0}-1,q^{\dagger}_{0}+1,q^{\dagger}_{1},\dots,q^{\dagger}_{y}\rrbracket).

It follows that
q^{\dagger}_{0}\geq 2
by Lemma
5
. Now using (
7
),

\displaystyle(f_{u,v}\circ g)(\llbracket q_{0},-1,q_{2},\dots,q_{t+1}\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=\llbracket q_{0},-1\rrbracket\oplus-f_{u,v}(\llbracket q^{\dagger}_{0},q^{\dagger}_{1},\dots,q^{\dagger}_{y}\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=\llbracket q_{0},-1\rrbracket\oplus-(f_{u,v}\circ g)(\llbracket-q_{2},-q_{3},\dots,-q_{t+1}\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=\llbracket q_{0},-1,q_{2},\dots,q_{t+1}\rrbracket{\text{ by the induction hypothesis.}}

If, instead,
q_{2}\geq 4
, then, using (
4
) and Lemma
4
,

(8)

\displaystyle(f_{u,v}\circ g)(\llbracket q_{0},-1,q_{2},\dots,q_{t+1}\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=(f_{u,v}\circ g)(\llbracket q_{0}-1,1-q_{2},-q_{3},\dots,-q_{t+1}\rrbracket)

(9)

\displaystyle=f_{u,v}(\llbracket q_{0}-2,1\rrbracket\oplus g(-\llbracket 2-q_{2},-q_{3},\dots,-q_{t+1}\rrbracket))

\displaystyle=f_{u,v}(\llbracket q_{0}-2,1\rrbracket\oplus\llbracket q^{\ast}_{0}-2,q^{\ast}_{1},\dots,q^{\ast}_{w}\rrbracket).

Note that
q^{\ast}_{0}\geq 2
by Lemma
5
. If
q^{\ast}_{0}\geq 3
, then using (
4
) and (
8
),

\displaystyle(f_{u,v}\circ g)(\llbracket q_{0},-1,q_{2},\dots,q_{t+1}\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=f_{u,v}(\llbracket q_{0}-2,1,q^{\ast}_{0}-2,q^{\ast}_{1},\dots,q^{\ast}_{w}\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=\llbracket q_{0},-1\rrbracket\oplus f_{u,v}(\llbracket q^{\ast}_{0},q^{\ast}_{1},\dots,q^{\ast}_{w}\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=\llbracket q_{0},-1\rrbracket\oplus(f_{u,v}\circ g)(\llbracket q_{2},q_{3},\dots,q_{t+1}\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=\llbracket q_{0},-1,q_{2},\dots,q_{t+1}\rrbracket{\text{ by the induction hypothesis.}}

Alternatively, if
q^{\ast}_{0}=2
, then by Lemma
5
, we must have that
t\geq 3
with
v=4
,
q_{2}=4
,
q_{3}=-1
, and
q_{4}\geq 4
. Let

(10)

\llbracket q^{\diamond}_{0},q^{\diamond}_{1},\dots,q^{\diamond}_{z}\rrbracket=g(\llbracket q_{4},q_{5},\dots,q_{t+1}\rrbracket).

Now, using (

\displaystyle(f_{u,v}\circ g)(\llbracket q_{0},-1,4,-1,q_{4},\dots,q_{t+1}\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=(f_{u,v}\circ g)(\llbracket q_{0}-1\rrbracket\oplus-\llbracket 3,-1,q_{4},\dots,q_{t+1}\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=(f_{u,v}\circ g)(\llbracket q_{0}-1,-3,1,-q_{4},\dots,-q_{t+1}\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=f_{u,v}(\llbracket q_{0}-2,1\rrbracket\oplus g(-\llbracket-2,1,-q_{4},\dots,-q_{t+1}\rrbracket))

\displaystyle=f_{u,v}(\llbracket q_{0}-2,1\rrbracket\oplus g(\llbracket 2,-1,q_{4},\dots,q_{t+1}\rrbracket))

\displaystyle=f_{u,v}(\llbracket q_{0}-2,1\rrbracket\oplus g(\llbracket 1\rrbracket\oplus-\llbracket q_{4}-1,q_{5},\dots,q_{t+1}\rrbracket))

\displaystyle=f_{u,v}(\llbracket q_{0}-2,1\rrbracket\oplus g(\llbracket 1,1-q_{4},-q_{5},\dots,-q_{t+1}\rrbracket))

\displaystyle=f_{u,v}(\llbracket q_{0}-2,1\rrbracket\oplus(\llbracket 0,1\rrbracket\oplus g(-\llbracket 2-q_{4},-q_{5},\dots,-q_{t+1}\rrbracket)))

\displaystyle=f_{u,v}(\llbracket q_{0}-2,2\rrbracket\oplus g(\llbracket q_{4}-2,q_{5},\dots,q_{t+1}\rrbracket))

\displaystyle=f_{u,v}(\llbracket q_{0}-2,2,q^{\diamond}_{0}-2,q^{\diamond}_{1},\dots,q^{\diamond}_{z}\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=\llbracket q_{0},-1\rrbracket\oplus f_{u,v}(\llbracket 2,1,q^{\diamond}_{0}-2,q^{\diamond}_{1},\dots,q^{\diamond}_{z}\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=\llbracket q_{0},-1\rrbracket\oplus(\llbracket 4,-1\rrbracket\oplus f_{u,v}(\llbracket q^{\diamond}_{0},q^{\diamond}_{1},\dots,q^{\diamond}_{z}\rrbracket))

\displaystyle=\llbracket q_{0},-1,4,-1\rrbracket\oplus(f_{u,v}\circ g)(\llbracket q_{4},q_{5},\dots,q_{t+1}\rrbracket))

\displaystyle=\llbracket q_{0},-1,4,-1,q_{4},q_{5},\dots,q_{t+1}\rrbracket\text{ by the induction hypothesis.}

So the result also holds in this case.

Having exhausted all possibilities, the result follows by strong induction.
∎

Lemma 7
.

We have that

Proof.

(
\Rightarrow
) Suppose that

(
\Leftarrow

∎

Proposition 4
.

If

, then

Proof.

Let

c&d\end{bmatrix}\in\mathscr{G}_{1,2}
and let

be the short continued fraction representation of

Following the ideas in the proof of Theorem

, we must show that this continued fraction representation can be manipulated to an equivalent form so that all coefficients with an even index are even.

If

, we have

n_{3}&1\end{bmatrix}
with

. In this case,

, so

. If

, we apply the previous argument to

, which gives that

Suppose

. By repeatedly applying Lemma

from left to right with

representing the leftmost odd partial quotient in the continued fraction representation of

we obtain a continued fraction representation

\frac{b}{d}=[\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{s-1},\alpha_{s}],

and

If

[\alpha_{s-1}+1,-1,-(\alpha_{s}-1)]&\text{ if $\alpha_{s-1}$ is odd and $\alpha_{s}$ is odd},\\
[\alpha_{s-1}+1,-1,-\alpha_{s},1]&\text{ if $\alpha_{s-1}$ is odd and $\alpha_{s}$ is even}.\\
\end{cases}

If

[\alpha_{s-1},\alpha_{s}]=\begin{cases}[\alpha_{s-1},\alpha_{s}]&\text{ if $\alpha_{s}$ is even},\\

The cases follow directly from the short and long continued fraction representations

Proposition 5

If

Let

If

d=1

. If

b/d=[q_{0},q_{1}]
with

and

\displaystyle[q_{0},q_{1}]

\displaystyle=[3\alpha_{0},1,-(q_{1}+1)]

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}[3\alpha_{0},1,-q_{1},-1]&\text{ if $q_{1}\equiv 0\bmod{3}$,}\\

A similar argument works if

for some

. Then
b/d=[q_{0},q_{1},\dots,q_{r}]

(
\Rightarrow

and

Theorem 5
.

We have that

\displaystyle[\mathscr{G}_{u,v}\colon G_{u,v}]=\begin{cases}1&u+v\leq 4,\\
\infty&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Proof.

From Theorem
4
we get the desired result when
u+v\leq 4
, so we may assume otherwise.

For
v\geq u\geq 2
we use the fact that
M_{u,v}(k)=\left(M_{u,v}(1)\right)^{k}
. Suppose that
k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
are such that

and

are representatives of the same coset. That is, assume

M_{u,v}(k_{1})G_{u,v}=M_{u,v}(k_{2})G_{u,v}.

Without loss of generality, suppose
k_{1}\geq k_{2}
. The above equality occurs if and only if
M_{u,v}(k_{1})(M_{u,v}(k_{2}))^{-1}\in G_{u,v}
. But

\displaystyle M_{u,v}(k_{1})(M_{u,v}(k_{2}))^{-1}

\displaystyle=(M_{u,v}(1))^{k_{1}}(M_{u,v}(1))^{-k_{2}}

\displaystyle=(M_{u,v}(1))^{k_{1}-k_{2}}

\displaystyle=M_{u,v}(k_{1}-k_{2}).

For
M_{u,v}(k_{1}-k_{2})\in G_{u,v}
to hold, it must be that
k_{1}=k_{2}
. This means that
\{M_{u,v}(k)G_{u,v}:k\geq 0\}
represents an infinite family of distinct left cosets.

For
v\geq 4
and
u=1
, we use the fact that if
N_{v}(k_{1})\left(N_{v}(k_{2})\right)^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}
, then

. Suppose that
k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
are such that
N_{v}(k_{1})
and
N_{v}(k_{2})
are representatives of the same coset. That is, assume

N_{v}(k_{1})G_{u,v}=N_{v}(k_{2})G_{u,v}.

Without loss of generality, suppose

This means that

3.
Example

The following example shows our motivation for defining
f_{u,v}
in order to extend our previous results.

Consider the matrix
M=\begin{bmatrix}2401&12975\\
250&1351\end{bmatrix}=R_{3}^{3}L_{2}R_{3}^{-1}L_{2}^{5}R_{3}L_{2}^{-1}R_{3}^{2}.
Applying
f\circ C
to
\frac{12975}{1351}
as was done in
[
4
]
gives that

\displaystyle(f\circ C)\left(\frac{12975}{1351}\right)

\displaystyle=\llbracket 10,-3,2,9,2,2,5\rrbracket,

which clearly does not satisfy the
(2,3)
-divisibility property despite the fact that
M\in G_{2,3}.
The main issue here is the way that the 1’s were eliminated. Using
f_{2,3}
in place of
f
gives that

\displaystyle(f_{2,3}\circ C)\left(\frac{12975}{1351}\right)

\displaystyle=f_{2,3}(\llbracket 9,1,1,1,1,9,2,2,5\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=\llbracket 9\rrbracket\oplus f_{3,2}(\llbracket 1,1,1,1,9,2,2,5\rrbracket)

\displaystyle=\llbracket 9\rrbracket\oplus(\llbracket 2\rrbracket\oplus-f_{2,3}(\llbracket 2,1,9,2,2,5\rrbracket))

\displaystyle=\llbracket 9\rrbracket\oplus(\llbracket 2\rrbracket\oplus-(\llbracket 3\rrbracket\oplus-f_{3,2}(\llbracket 10,2,2,5\rrbracket)))

\displaystyle=\llbracket 9\rrbracket\oplus(\llbracket 2\rrbracket\oplus-(\llbracket 3\rrbracket\oplus-(\llbracket 10\rrbracket\oplus f_{2,3}(\llbracket 2,2,5\rrbracket))))

\displaystyle=\llbracket 9\rrbracket\oplus(\llbracket 2\rrbracket\oplus-(\llbracket 3\rrbracket\oplus-(\llbracket 10\rrbracket\oplus(\llbracket 3\rrbracket\oplus f_{3,2}(\llbracket-2,6\rrbracket)))))

\displaystyle=\llbracket 9\rrbracket\oplus(\llbracket 2\rrbracket\oplus-(\llbracket 3\rrbracket\oplus-(\llbracket 10\rrbracket\oplus(\llbracket 3,-2\rrbracket\oplus f_{2,3}(\llbracket 6\rrbracket)))))

\displaystyle=\llbracket 9\rrbracket\oplus(\llbracket 2\rrbracket\oplus-(\llbracket 3\rrbracket\oplus-(\llbracket 10\rrbracket\oplus(\llbracket 3,-2\rrbracket\oplus\llbracket 6\rrbracket))))

\displaystyle=\llbracket 9,2,-3,10,3,-2,6\rrbracket,

which does satisfy the
(2,3)
-divisibility property, as desired, and encodes the exponents in the product representation of
M
.

4.
Concluding Remarks

The definition of
f_{u,v}
shows that the function always changes the value of the partial quotients by no more than 2 in absolute value. In effect, the function
f_{u,v}
takes in a vector and attempts to output an equivalent vector satisfying the
(u,v)
-divisibility property by adjusting the components according to Lemmas
1
and
2
. Two questions may come to mind at this stage:

•

Why are Lemmas
1
and
2
enough for our purposes?

•

Is there some version of Lemmas
1
and
2
that can adjust the components by more?

Both questions can be addressed by noting that

(11)

[\alpha,\beta,\gamma]=[\alpha+k,1,-1,k+1,-\beta,-\gamma]

for
k\in\mathbb{Z}.
This identity shows that attempting to adjust a component by a ‘large’ value of
k
may introduce new components that need to be adjusted as well. In some cases, this is problematic. For example, consider
\llbracket 2,3\rrbracket
and suppose that we are attempting to find some equivalent vector that satisfies the
(8,1)
-divisibility property. Using (
11
), we see that we can indeed raise the 2 to a multiple of 8, giving us the equivalent vector
\llbracket 8,1,-1,7,-3\rrbracket.
Unfortunately this creates an endless set of problems each time that we attempt to fix every other component.

By applying Lemma
1
(b) repeatedly, we get

\displaystyle[\alpha,\beta,\gamma]

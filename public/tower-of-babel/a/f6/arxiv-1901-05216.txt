Abstract

In this paper, we investigate the existence of weak solution for a Kirchhoff type problem driven by a nonlocal operator of elliptic type in a fractional Orlicz-Sobolev space, with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions

(D_{K,A})\hskip 14.22636pt\left\{\begin{array}[]{clclc}M\left(\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}A\left([u(x)-u(y)]K(x,y)\right)dxdy\right)\mathcal{L}^{K}_{A}u&=&f(x,u)&\text{ in }&\Omega,\\
\\
\hskip 199.16928ptu&=&0&\text{ in }&\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega.\end{array}\right.

Where
\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{K}_{A}
is a nonlocal operator with singular kernel
\displaystyle K
and
\displaystyle A
is an
\displaystyle N
-function,
\displaystyle\Omega
is an open bounded subset in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{N}
with Lipschitz boundary
\displaystyle\partial\Omega
.

1.
Introduction

The equation,

(1)

\rho\dfrac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial t^{2}}-\left(\dfrac{P_{0}}{h}+\dfrac{E}{2L}\int_{0}^{L}|\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial x}|^{2}dx\right)\dfrac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x^{2}}

[
25

\displaystyle(\ref{1.})

\displaystyle L
is the length of string,
\displaystyle h
is the area of the cross section,
\displaystyle E
is the Young modulus of the material,
\displaystyle\rho
is the mass density, and
\displaystyle P_{0}
is the initial tension. The Kirchhoff’s model takes into account the length changes of the string produced by transverse vibrations. Some interesting results can be found, for example in
[
13
]
. On the other hand, Kirchhoff-type boundary value problems model several physical and biological systems where
\displaystyle u
describes a process which depend on the average of itself, as for example, the population density. We refer the reader to
[
5
,
22
,
33
]
for some related works. In
[
17
]
, the authors showed the existence and multiplicity of solutions to a class of
\displaystyle p(x
)-Kirchhoff type equations via variational methods. In
[
25
]
, the author obtained the existence of infinite solutions to the
\displaystyle p
-Kirchhoff type quasilinear elliptic equations via the fountain theorem. In
[
15
]
, the authors investigated
higher order
\displaystyle p(x)
-Kirchhoff type equations via symmetric mountain pass Theorem, even in the degenerate case. However, they did not consider the existence of solutions for Kirchhoff type problems in the fractional setting. In the very recent paper
[
21
]
, the authors first provided a detailed discussion about the physical meaning underlying the fractional Kirchhoff models and their applications, see
[
21
]
for further details.

Problems of this type have been intensively studied in the last few years, due to numerous and relevant applications in many fields of mathematics, such as approximation theory, mathematical physics (electrorheological fluids), calculus of variations, nonlinear potential theory, the theory of quasiconformalmappings, differential geometry, geometric function theory, probability theory and image processing (see for instance
[

The study of nonlinear elliptic equations involving quasilinear homogeneous type operators is based on the theory of Sobolev spaces and fractional Sobolov spaces
\displaystyle W^{s,p}(\Omega)
in order to find weak solutions. In certain equations, precisely in the case of nonhomogeneous differential operators, when trying to relax some conditions on these operators (as growth conditions), the problem can not be formulated with classical Lebesgue and Sobolev spaces. Hence, the adequate functional spaces is the so-called Orlicz spaces. These spaces consists of functions that have weak derivatives and satisfy certain integrability conditions. Many properties of Orlicz-Sobolev spaces and fractional Orlicz-Sobolev spaces come in
[

,
32

For this, many researchers have studied the existence of solutions for the eigenvalue problems involving nonhomogeneous operators in the divergence form through Orlicz-Sobolev spaces by using variational methods and critical point theory, monotone operator methods, fixed point theory and degree theory (see for instance

\displaystyle N>sp

\displaystyle s\in(0,1)

\displaystyle\Omega
is an open bounded subset in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{N}
with Lipschitz boundary
\displaystyle\partial\Omega
,
\displaystyle M:[0,\infty)\longrightarrow(0,\infty)
is a continuous function,
\displaystyle f:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a Carathéodory function and
\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{p}_{K}u

In this paper, our purpose is to generalize the previous works in the setting of fractional Orlicz-Sobolev spaces. That is we are interested to study the existence of weak solution for a Kirchhof type problem driven by a nonlocal operator of elliptic type, with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions as follows:

(D_{K,A})\hskip 14.22636pt\left\{\begin{array}[]{clclc}M\left(\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}A\left([u(x)-u(y)]K(x,y)\right)dxdy\right)\mathcal{L}^{K}_{A}u&=&f(x,u)&\text{ in }&\Omega\\

where
\displaystyle N>1
,
\displaystyle\Omega
is an open bounded subset in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{N}
with Lipschitz boundary
\displaystyle\partial\Omega
,
\displaystyle M:[0,\infty)\longrightarrow(0,\infty)
is a continuous function
\displaystyle f:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a Carathéodory function satisfying the Ambrosetti-Rabinowitz type condition and
\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{K}_{A}u

\displaystyle=2P.V\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}A^{\prime}\left((u(x)-u(y))K(x,y)\right)K(x,y)dy,\hskip 5.69046ptx\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

\displaystyle=2\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{\varepsilon}(x)}a\left((u(x)-u(y))K(x,y)\right)K(x,y)dy,\hskip 5.69046ptx\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

with
\displaystyle A

\displaystyle N

A typical example for
\displaystyle K

\displaystyle K(x,y)=\frac{1}{|x-y|^{s}A^{-1}(|x-y|^{N})}

(D_{s,A})\left\{\begin{array}[]{clclc}M\left(\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}A\left(\dfrac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{s}A^{-1}(|x-y|^{N})}\right)dxdy\right)(-\Delta)^{s}_{A}u&=&f(x,u)&\text{ in }&\Omega\\

where
\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s}_{A}

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s}_{A}u(x)

\displaystyle=2P.V\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}A^{\prime}\left(\dfrac{(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{s}A^{-1}(|x-y|^{N})}\right)\dfrac{dy}{|x-y|^{s}A^{-1}(|x-y|^{N})}

\displaystyle=2\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{\varepsilon}(x)}a\left(\dfrac{(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{s}A^{-1}(|x-y|^{N})}\right)\dfrac{dy}{|x-y|^{s}A^{-1}(|x-y|^{N})}

Note that for

\displaystyle(D_{K,A})

\displaystyle(D_{K,p})

This paper is organized as follows : in the second section, we recall some properties of Orlicz-Sobolev and fractional Orlicz-Sobolev spaces into Lebesgue spaces. In the third section, we will introduce some necessary definitions and properties of space

3.
Variational framework

One of the aims of this paper is to study nonlocal problems driven by
\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s}_{A}

(or its generalization
\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{K}_{A}
) with Dirichlet boundary data via variational methods. For this purpose, we need to work in a suitable fractional Orlicz-Sobolev space: for
this, we consider a functional analytical setting that is inspired by (but not equivalent to) the fractional Orlicz-Sobolev spaces in order to correctly encode the Dirichlet
boundary datum in the variational formulation.

In this section, we show some basic results that will be used in the next section.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\displaystyle\Omega
be an open subset of
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{N}

\displaystyle Q=\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\mathcal{O}

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}=(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)\times(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega)

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle N

\displaystyle(K_{0})

\displaystyle W^{K}L_{A}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{N}

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle u_{|_{\Omega}}

\displaystyle L_{A}(\Omega)

(u(x)-u(y))K(x,y)\in L_{A}(Q).

The space
\displaystyle W^{K}L_{A}
is equipped with the norm

||u||_{K,A}=||u||_{A}+[u]_{K,A}

\displaystyle u=0

Proof

By definition of
\displaystyle A_{1}
and estimation

\displaystyle I_{1}=\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega\cap\left\{|x-y|^{N}\leqslant\beta\right\}}\dfrac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p_{0}}}{|x-y|^{p_{0}s+N}}

and

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega\cap\left\{|x-y|^{N}>\beta\right\}}\dfrac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p_{0}}}{|x-y|^{sp_{0}+N}}dxdy

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega\cap\left\{|x-y|^{N}>\beta\right\}}\dfrac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p_{0}}}{|x-y|^{sp_{0}}(A_{1}^{-1}(|x-y|^{N}))^{p_{0}}}\dfrac{(A_{1}^{-1}(|x-y|^{N}))^{p_{0}}}{|x-y|^{N}}dxdy

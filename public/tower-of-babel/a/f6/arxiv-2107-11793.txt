On The Enhanced Power Graph of a Semigroup
Sandeep Dalal, Jitender Kumar, Siddharth Singh

Abstract
. The enhanced power graph 𝒫 e ​ ( S ) \mathcal{P}_{e}(S) of a semigroup S S is a simple graph whose vertex set is S S and two vertices x , y ∈ S x,y\in S are adjacent if and only if x , y ∈ ⟨ z ⟩ x,y\in\langle z\rangle for some z ∈ S z\in S , where ⟨ z ⟩ \langle z\rangle is the subsemigroup generated by z z . In this paper, first we described the structure of 𝒫 e ​ ( S ) \mathcal{P}_{e}(S) for an arbitrary semigroup S S . Consequently, we discussed the connectedness of 𝒫 e ​ ( S ) \mathcal{P}_{e}(S) . Further, we characterized the semigroup S S such that 𝒫 e ​ ( S ) \mathcal{P}_{e}(S) is complete, bipartite, regular, tree and null graph, respectively. Also, we have investigated the planarity together with the minimum degree and independence number of 𝒫 e ​ ( S ) \mathcal{P}_{e}(S) . The chromatic number of a spanning subgraph, viz. the cyclic graph, of 𝒫 e ​ ( S ) \mathcal{P}_{e}(S) is proved to be countable. At the final part of this paper, we construct an example of a semigroup S S such that the chromatic number of 𝒫 e ​ ( S ) \mathcal{P}_{e}(S) need not be countable.

3.
Graph invariants of
\mathcal{P}_{e}(S)

In this section, we first describes the structure of the enhanced power graph of a semigroup
S
. Further, we characterized the semigroup
S
such that
\mathcal{P}_{e}(S)
is complete, connected, bipartite, tree and regular, respectively. Finally, we characterize the semigroup
S
such that
\mathcal{P}_{e}(S)
is planar. For
x\in S
and
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
, we define

S(x,m,n)=\{y\in S:\;x^{m}=y^{n}\}

and we write
C(x)=\bigcup\limits_{m,n\in\mathbb{N}}S(x,m,n)
. The
following proposition describes the structure of
\mathcal{P}_{e}(S)
.

Proposition 3.1
.

The set
C(x)
is a connected component of
\mathcal{P}_{e}(S)
. Moreover, the components of the graph
\mathcal{P}_{e}(S)
are precisely
\{C(x)\;|\;x\in S\}
.

Proof.

Let
y,z\in C(x)
. Then
y\in S(x,m,n)
and
z\in S(x,p,q)
. It follows that
x^{m}=y^{n}
and
x^{p}=z^{q}
. Note that
y\sim x^{mp}\sim z
. Thus,
C(x)
is connected in
\mathcal{P}_{e}(S)
. Now suppose that the element
z
of
S
is adjacent to a vertex
y
in
C(x)
. Since
y\sim z
implies
y,z\in\langle t\rangle
for some
t\in S
. For
y\in C(x)
, we have
x^{m}=y^{n}
for some
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
. It follows that
y=t^{\alpha},\;z=t^{\beta}
and
z^{n\alpha}=x^{m\beta}
. Thus,
z\in C(x)
. Hence,
C(x)
is a connected component of
\mathcal{P}_{e}(S)
.
∎

Corollary 3.2

Let
S
be a semigroup. Then
\mathcal{P}_{e}(S)
is connected if and only if
\langle x\rangle\cap\langle y\rangle\neq\varnothing
for all

. In this case,

(\mathcal{P}_{e}(S))\leq 2
.

The following lemma is useful in the sequel.

Lemma 3.3

Let
a
be an element of a finite semigroup
S
. Then the subsemigroup
\langle a\rangle
contains exactly one idempotent.

Proof.

Let
m
and

be the index and period of

, respectively. Then,
\langle a\rangle=\{a,a^{2},\ldots,a^{m+r-1}\}
. The subgroup
\mathcal{K}_{a}=\{a^{m},a^{m+1},\ldots,a^{m+r-1}\}
of
\langle a\rangle
contains exactly one idempotent. If for
1\leq i<m
,
a^{i}
is an idempotent, then we have
a^{2i}=a^{i}
. Consequently,

; a contradiction. Hence, the idempotent element of
\mathcal{K}_{a}
is the only idempotent in
\langle a\rangle
.
∎

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
S
be a semigroup of bounded exponent. Then
S_{f}
is a connected component of
\mathcal{P}_{e}(S)
with unique idempotent

. Moreover, the connected components of
\mathcal{P}_{e}(S)

\{S_{f}\;:\;f\in E(S)\}

.

Proof.

Suppose

. If any one of them is

a\sim b
in
\mathcal{P}_{e}(S)
. If
a,b\in S_{f}\backslash\{f\}
, then there is a path

\mathcal{P}_{e}(S)
. Let if possible

such that
x\sim b
for some
b\in S_{f}
so that
x,b\in\langle c\rangle
for some

. Since
b^{n}=f
for some

so that
f\in\langle c\rangle
. Consequently we get

; a contradiction. Hence,

is a connected component of
\mathcal{P}_{e}(S)
and by Remark

Unless stated otherwise, hereafter

Lemma 3.6

Let

(ii)

Proof.

(i) Since

(ii) If

. Now we define another relation

R^{\infty}=\bigcup\{R^{n}:\;n\geq 1\},

where
R^{n}
is the

R^{e}
is the smallest equivalence relation containing

Proposition 4.1

(
[

)
.

For every relation
R

.

Proposition 4.2

(
[
14
, Proposition 1.4.10]
)
.

If
R
is a relation on a set
X
and
R^{e}
is the smallest equivalence relation on

R
, then
(x,y)\in R^{e}
if and only if either
x=y
or, for some

x=z_{1}\rightarrow z_{2}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow z_{n}=y

(z_{i},z_{i+1})\in R
or
(z_{i+1},z_{i})\in R
.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
S
be a semigroup. A relation
R
on
S
is
left compatible
if

(\forall a,s,t\in S)\ \ (s,t)\in R\ \ \Rightarrow(as,at)\in R,

and
right compatible
if

(\forall a,s,t\in S)\ \ (s,t)\in R\ \ \Rightarrow(sa,ta)\in R.

It is called
compatible
if

(\forall s,s^{\prime},t,t^{\prime}\in S)\ \ (s,s^{\prime})\in R\ \ and\ \ (t,t^{\prime})\in R\ \ \Rightarrow(ss^{\prime},tt^{\prime})\in R.

A left [right] compatible equivalence relation is called
left [right] congruence
. A compatible equivalence relation is called
congruence
.

Proposition 4.4

(
[
14
, Proposition 1.5.1]
)
.

A relation
\rho
on a semigroup

is a congruence if and only if it is both left and right congruence.

Let

Example 4.8
.

Consider the sets
B=\{(i,j)\in[1,2]\times[1,2]:\;j<i\}

on

(i,j)^{2}\rho\;i\;{\rm and}\;(i,j)^{3}\rho\;j\;{\rm for\;all}\;(i,j)\in B.

We prove that
\chi(\mathcal{P}_{e}(A^{*}/\rho^{\#}))
is uncountable. First we claim that:
i\rho^{\#}\neq j\rho^{\#}
for

. On contrary, we assume that
i\rho^{\#}=j\rho^{\#}
for some
i,j\in[1,2]
. Then
i\;\rho^{\#}j
. Since
\rho^{\#}=(\rho^{c})^{e}
(see Proposition
4.7
) so that there exist
z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n}\in A^{*}
such that
z_{1}=i
and
z_{n}=j
with
z_{k}\;\rho^{c}\;z_{k+1}
for all
k
, where
1\leq k\leq n-1
. Consequently, we get
z_{k}=x_{k}a_{k}y_{k}
and
z_{k+1}=x_{k}b_{k}y_{k}
for some

and
a_{k}\;\rho\;b_{k}

a_{k},b_{k}\in\{u,(v,w)^{2},(v^{\prime},w^{\prime})^{3}\}

. For

x_{1}=y_{1}=\epsilon

z_{2}=b_{1}

and so

Let

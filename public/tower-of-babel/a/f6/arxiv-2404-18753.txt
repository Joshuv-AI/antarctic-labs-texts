1.
Introduction

Let
G\leqslant\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)
be a non-trivial finite transitive permutation group with point stabiliser
H
. Recall that a
derangement
for
G
is a fixed-point-free permutation. This concept has been widely studied for many decades (for example, see the books
[
6
,
17
]
). A classical theorem of Jordan
[
27
]
shows that
G
contains a derangement. Of course, subgroups of
G
may not contain such elements and this leads us naturally to the following definition.

Definition
.

A subgroup
K
of
G
is a
fixer
if every element in
K
has fixed points on
\Omega
.

There are two main sources of motivation for studying fixers, which arise in finite group theory on the one hand and extremal combinatorics on the other. Let us briefly explain these connections.

As defined in
[
30
]
, a subset
R
of
G
is called
semiregular
if
xy^{-1}
is a derangement for any
x,y\in R
(in addition, if
R
is transitive, then we say that it is
regular
). The concept of a fixer is complementary to the definition of a semiregular subset. More precisely,
[
30
, Proposition 1.1]
states that if
R
is a semiregular subset and
K
is a fixer, then
|R||K|\leqslant|G|
, with equality if and only if
G=RK
. From this factorisation, one can obtain a lower bound on the proportion of derangements in
G
by determining the orders of fixers. This is an intensively studied problem, and we refer the reader to
[
8
,
15
,
20
]
for various results on the proportion of derangements.

In a different direction, recall that
S\subseteq G
is an
intersecting subset
if for any pair of elements
g,h\in S
, the product
gh^{-1}
is not a derangement. Note that every fixer of
G
is an intersecting subset. A well-known problem is to determine whether or not the size of an intersecting subset of
G
is bounded above by the order of
H
, which is motivated by the famous Erdős-Ko-Rado (EKR) theorem
[
13
]
in extremal set theory. More specifically, there has been an intense focus on studying the finite transitive permutation groups for which every intersecting subset has size at most
|H|
(a group with this property is said to have the
EKR property
). This problem dates back to work of Frankl and Deza
[
14
]
in 1977, and there have been several major advances in this direction in recent years, see
[
1
,
12
,
31
,
35
,
36
]
for more details.

Given this connection, we focus our attention on the fixers of large order.

Definition
.

Let
K\leqslant G
be a fixer.

(i)

K
is
stable
if
K\leqslant H^{g}
for some
g\in G
, otherwise
K
is
non-stable
;

(ii)

K
is
large
if it is non-stable and
|K|\geqslant|H|
.

We say that
G
has the
weak-EKR property
if every fixer has order at most
|H|
, and
G
has the
strict-weak-EKR property
if it has no large fixer. As noted in
[
30
]
, most permutation groups do not satisfy the weak-EKR property; indeed, the largest order of a fixer is often significantly larger than
|H|
.
To measure the deficit,
the following invariant

\rho_{0}(G):=\max\left\{\frac{|K|}{|H|\sqrt{|\Omega|}}:\mbox{$K\leqslant G$ is a fixer}\right\}.

is defined in
[
30
]
(where it is denoted
\rho_{0}(G/\Omega)
). Note that
\rho_{0}(G)\geqslant 1/\sqrt{|\Omega|}
, with equality if and only if
G
has the weak-EKR property.

At the other end of this spectrum, we make the following conjecture (recall that a transitive permutation group is
primitive
if a point stabiliser is a maximal subgroup).

Conjecture 1
.

There exists an absolute constant
c
such that
\rho_{0}(G)<c
for all finite primitive permutation groups
G
.

It is shown in
[
30
, Theorem 1.6]
that for any number
M
, there exist infinitely many imprimitive permutation groups
G\leqslant\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)
with
\rho_{0}(G)>M
, so the primitivity condition is necessary.

In Theorem
2.10
, we will show that if
G
is primitive, then
G
does not have the weak-EKR property (and hence
\rho_{0}(G)>1/\sqrt{|\Omega|}
) only if it is almost simple or of product action type (recall that a group
G
is
almost simple
if
G_{0}\trianglelefteqslant G\leqslant\mathrm{Aut}(G_{0})
for some non-abelian simple group
G_{0}
; here
G_{0}=\mathrm{soc}(G)
is the
socle
of
G
). In fact, by constructing a suitable product action as in
[
30
, Construction 3.2]
, one can see that Conjecture
1
is equivalent to the following conjecture.

Conjecture 2
.

We have
\rho_{0}(G)\leqslant 1
for all almost simple primitive groups
G
.

Our main theorem takes the first step towards a proof of Conjecture

by determining the large fixers when
G
is an almost simple primitive group with socle
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(q)
. The relevant table is presented at the end of the paper in Section

Suppose

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout, let
G\leqslant\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)
be a finite transitive permutation group with point stabiliser
H
.

2.1.
First observations

We first give some basic observations of the fixers of permutation groups.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
K
be a subgroup of
G
. Then the following statements are equivalent.

(i)

K
is a fixer.

(ii)

K^{g}
is a fixer for some
g\in G
.

(iii)

K\subseteq\bigcup_{g\in G}H^{g}
.

(iv)

\bigcup_{g\in G}K^{g}\subseteq\bigcup_{g\in G}H^{g}
.

(v)

Every element of
K
is
G
-conjugate to an element in
H
.

(vi)

For any
G
-conjugacy class
C
,
C\cap K\neq\emptyset
implies that
C\cap H\neq\emptyset
.

The
spectrum
of a finite group
X
, denoted
{\sf Spec}(X)
, is the set of the orders of the elements of
X
. We also write
\pi(X)
for the set of prime divisors of
|X|
, so
\pi(X)\subseteq{\sf Spec}(X)
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
K
be a fixer of
G
. Then

(i)

{\sf Spec}(K)\subseteq{\sf Spec}(H)
;

(ii)

\pi(K)\subseteq\pi(H)
;

(iii)

K
is intransitive on
\Omega
; and

(iv)

KH\neq G
.

Proof.

Parts (i) and (ii) are clear from Lemma
2.1
. For parts (iii) and (iv), recall that any finite transitive group has a derangement
[
27
]
.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
K
be a fixer of
G
and let
G_{0}\leqslant G
. Then
K\cap G_{0}
is a fixer of
G_{0}
.

Let
\Delta
be a finite set and suppose
X\leqslant\mathrm{Sym}(\Delta)
. Then

\mbox{{\rm fpr}}_{\Delta}(x):=\frac{|\mathrm{fix}_{\Delta}(x)|}{|\Delta|}

is the
fixed point ratio
of
x\in X
, where
\mathrm{fix}_{\Delta}(x)
is the set of fixed points of
x
on
\Delta
. If
X
is transitive on
\Delta
, then we have

\mbox{{\rm fpr}}_{\Delta}(x)=\frac{|x^{X}\cap X_{\delta}|}{|x^{X}|},

where
\delta\in\Delta
(see, for example,
[
5
, Lemma 1.2(iii)]
).

Lemma 2.4
.

Suppose there exists a set
\Delta
such that
G\leqslant\mathrm{Sym}(\Delta)
and there exists
x\in K
such that
\mbox{{\rm fpr}}_{\Delta}(x)\neq\mbox{{\rm fpr}}_{\Delta}(y)
for any
y\in H
. Then
K
is not a fixer of
G
.

Proof.

This is given by the fact that
\mbox{{\rm fpr}}_{\Delta}(x)=\mbox{{\rm fpr}}_{\Delta}(x^{g})
for any
g\in\mathrm{Sym}(\Delta)
.
∎

Recall that the
minimal degree

\mu_{\Delta}(X)
of
X
is defined to be the smallest number of points moved by any non-identity element. That is,

\mu_{\Delta}(X)=\min_{1\neq x\in X}\left(|\Delta|-|\mathrm{fix}_{\Delta}(x)|\right)=|\Delta|\cdot\left(1-\max_{1\neq x\in X}\mbox{{\rm fpr}}_{\Delta}(x)\right).

Corollary 2.5
.

Suppose
K
is a fixer of
G
. Then for any set
\Delta
with
G\leqslant\mathrm{Sym}(\Delta)
, we have
\mu_{\Delta}(K)\geqslant\mu_{\Delta}(H)
.

Proof.

If
\mu_{\Delta}(K)<\mu_{\Delta}(H)
, then there exists
x\in K
such that
\mbox{{\rm fpr}}_{\Delta}(x)>\mbox{{\rm fpr}}_{\Delta}(y)
for any
y\in H
. Now apply Lemma
2.4
.
∎

2.2.
Large fixers

Recall that a non-stable fixer
K
is
large
if
|K|\geqslant|H|
, and we say
K
is
strictly large
if
|K|>|H|
.

Example 2.6
.

Let
G=\mathrm{PGL}_{n}(q)
and suppose the point stabiliser
H=\mathrm{PGL}_{n}(q_{0})
comprises the images of all the invertible matrices over
\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}}
, where
q_{0}^{r}=q
for some integer
r\geqslant 3
. Let
B=U{:}D
be a Borel subgroup of
G
consisting of the images of all upper-triangular in
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(q)
, where
U
is a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
and
D
is a maximal split torus. Then
K:=U{:}(D\cap H)
is a large fixer of
G
, and so

does not have the EKR property. To see this, first note that

|K|=q^{n(n-1)/2}(q_{0}-1)^{n-1}\geqslant q_{0}^{n^{2}-1}>|H|.

Let
x\in K
and let
\widehat{x}\in B
be a pre-image of
x
. Then
\widehat{x}
is an upper-triangular matrix whose diagonal entries are all in
\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}}
. Hence, the elementary divisors of
\widehat{x}
are polynomials in
\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}}
, and
[
23
, Theorem 6.7.3]
implies that

is
G
-conjugate to an element in
H
.

As introduced in Section
1
, we say
G
has the
weak-EKR property
if
G
has no strictly large fixer, while
G
is said to have the
strict-weak-EKR property
if
G
has no large fixer. Now we list some results in the literature in the study of the EKR properties of permutation groups, which will be helpful in the study of weak-EKR properties, noting that the EKR property implies the weak-EKR property. The first result is
[
36
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Theorem 2.7
.

Any finite
2
-transitive permutation group has the EKR property.

The following is
[
17
, Lemma 14.6.1]
.

Lemma 2.8
.

If
G
has a regular subgroup, then
G
has the EKR property.

Corollary 2.9
.

If
G
has a regular subgroup, then
G
does not have a strictly large fixer.

Recall that
G
is called
primitive
if
H
is a maximal subgroup of
G
.

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
G\leqslant\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)
be a primitive group without the weak-EKR property. Then

can be embedded in a wreath product

, where

,

Proof.

If

The following elementary lemma will be useful to study the fixers of almost simple primitive groups with large sizes.

Suppose

is a primitive group with socle

. Then

3.
Conjugacy classes of
\mathrm{A\Gamma L}_{1}(q)

To study the fixers of two-dimensional linear groups in Sections
4
and
5
, we will use the information of conjugacy classes of
\mathrm{A\Gamma L}_{1}(q)
, noting that it is isomorphic to a maximal subgroup of
\mathrm{P\Gamma L}_{2}(q)
of type
P_{1}
. Here we write

(1)

\Gamma=(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{+}{:}\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}){:}\langle\phi\rangle\cong\mathrm{A\Gamma L}_{1}(q),

where
q=p^{f}
for some prime
p
, and
\langle\phi\rangle=\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{p})
is the Galois group, with the multiplication

(a,\lambda)\phi^{i}(b,\mu)\phi^{j}=(a+\lambda^{-1}b^{\phi^{-i}},\lambda\mu^{\phi^{-i}})\phi^{i+j}.

Recall that for a subfield
\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}}
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, the trace map
\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}}}:\mathbb{F}_{q}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}}
is given by

\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}}}(x)=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}})}\sigma(x).

It is well-known that the trace map
\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}}}
is surjective since
\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}}
is a separable extension. That is,
\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})=\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}}
.

The following lemma combines
[
41
, Lemmas 2.14 and 2.15]
, which describes the conjugacy classes of
\Gamma
.

Lemma 3.1
.

The elements
(a,1)\phi
and
(b,1)\phi
of
\Gamma
are conjugate in
\Gamma
if and only if
\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{p}}(a)\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{p}}(b)\neq 0
or
\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{p}}(a)=\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{p}}(b)=0
.

In this section, we extend Lemma
3.1
by considering conjugacy classes in
\Gamma
with some specific elements. Suppose
q=q_{1}^{s}=q_{0}^{r}
for some positive integers
s
and
r
, and assume
r
is prime. We first record some observations on the trace maps.

Lemma 3.2
.

The following properties hold.

(i)

If
\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}}
and
r=p
, then
\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}}(\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}})=\{0\}
.

(ii)

If
\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}\not\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}}
or
r\neq p
, then
\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}}(\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}})=\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}\cap\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}}
.

Proof.

First note that if
r=p
then
\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}}}(\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}})=\{0\}
. Thus, if
\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}}
and
r=p
then

\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}}(x)=\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}}/\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}}(\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}}}(x))=\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}}/\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}}(0)=0

for any
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}}
. This gives part (i).

Next, we consider the case where
\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}}
and
r\neq p
. Assume
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}}
, noting that
\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}}}(x)=rx
. Thus,

\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}}(x)=\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}}/\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}}(\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}}}(x))=\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}}/\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}}(rx)=r\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}}/\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}}(x).

This implies that
\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}}(\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}})=\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}
since
(r,p)=1
and
\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}}/\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}}(\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}})=\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}
.

Finally, let us assume
\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}\not\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}}
, so
\mathbb{F}_{q}=\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}}
since
\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}}
is a maximal subfield of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Hence, if
\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}}(\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}})=\{0\}
, then

\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})=\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}}(\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}})=\{0\},

a contradiction to the surjectivity of
\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}}
. It follows that there exists
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}}
such that
\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}}(x)\neq 0
, noting that
\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}}(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}\cap\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}}
. For any
y\in\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}\cap\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}}
, we have
y(\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}}(x))^{-1}x\in\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}}
and

\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}}(y(\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}}(x))^{-1}x)=y.

This shows that
\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}\cap\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}}\subseteq\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}}(\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}})
, which completes the proof as
\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}}(\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}})\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}\cap\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}}
is obvious.
∎

Now let
i
be an integer such that
|\phi^{i}|=s
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then the following properties hold.

(i)

\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}}(a)=\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}}(b),
then
(a,1)\phi^{i}
and
(b,1)\phi^{i}
are
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{+}
-conjugate.

(ii)

If
\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}}(a)\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}}(b)\neq 0
, then
(a,1)\phi^{i}
and
(b,1)\phi^{i}
are
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{+}{:}\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}^{\times}
-conjugate.

(iii)

If
\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}}(a)=0
and
\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}}(b)\neq 0
, then
|(a,1)\phi^{i}|\neq|(b,1)\phi^{i}|
.

Proof.

First assume
\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}}(a)=\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}}(b)
, so
\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}}(b-a)=0
.
Note that
\langle\phi^{i}\rangle=\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}).

Thus, by the additive form of Hilbert 90, there exists
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
such that
b-a=c^{\phi^{-i}}-c
.
This implies that

((a,1)\phi^{i})^{(c,1)}=(-c,1)(a,1)\phi^{i}(c,1)=(-c+a+c^{\phi^{-i}},1)\phi^{i}=(b,1)\phi^{i}\\
,

which gives (i).

Next, assume both
\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}}(a)
and
\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}}(b)
are in
\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}^{\times}
.
Then there exists
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}^{\times}
such that
\lambda\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}}(a)=\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}}(b)
, and hence
\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}}(\lambda a)=\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}}(b)
.
In view of (i), we see that
(\lambda a,1)\phi^{i}
and
(b,1)\phi^{i}
are conjugate in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{+}
.
Since
\lambda^{\phi^{i}}=\lambda
, we have

((a,1)\phi^{i})^{(0,\lambda)}=(a,1)^{(0,\lambda)}\phi^{i}=(\lambda a,1)\phi^{i}
, which implies (ii).

Now assume
\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}}(a)=0
and
\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}}(b)\neq 0
. Then

((a,1)\phi^{i})^{s}=(a+a^{\phi^{-i}}+\cdots+a^{\phi^{-(s-1)i}},1)=(\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}}(a),1)=(0,1)

is the identity of
\Gamma
, whereas

((b,1)\phi^{i})^{s}=(\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}}(b),1)\neq(0,1).

This shows (iii).
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Suppose
r\nmid s
or
r\neq p
. Then for any
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, there exists
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}}
such that
(a,1)\phi^{i}
is conjugate to
(b,1)\phi^{i}
by an element in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{+}{:}\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
.

Proof.

If
\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}}(a)=0,
then Lemma
3.3
(i) implies
(a,1)\phi^{i}
is
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{+}
-conjugate to
(0,1)\phi^{i}.

If
\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}}(a)\neq 0,
then by Lemma
3.2
(ii), there exists
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}}
such that
\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}}(b)\neq 0,
and hence
(a,1)\phi^{i}
is
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{+}{:}\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
-conjugate to
(b,1)\phi^{i}
by Lemma
3.3
(ii).
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
X\leqslant\Gamma
be a subgroup such that
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{+}{:}\langle\phi\rangle_{p}\leqslant X
. Suppose
x\in X
and
\langle x\rangle\cap\mathbb{F}_{q}^{+}=1
. Then
x
is
X
-conjugate to an element in
X\cap(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}{:}\langle\phi\rangle)
.

Proof.

First note that
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{+}{:}\langle\phi\rangle_{p}
is a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
\Gamma
. Thus, conjugating by a suitable element in
X
, we may assume
\langle x\rangle_{p}\leqslant\mathbb{F}_{q}^{+}{:}\langle\phi\rangle_{p}
. Let
y
be a generator of
\langle x\rangle_{p}
. Then
y=(a,\phi^{i})
for some
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
and some integer
i
such that
\phi^{i}\in\langle\phi\rangle_{p}
. Let
|\phi^{i}|=s
and
q=q_{1}^{s}
. Then we have

y^{s}=(a+a^{\phi^{-i}}+\dots+a^{\phi^{-(s-1)i}},1)\phi^{i}=(\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}}(a),1)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{+}\cap\langle y\rangle=\{(0,1)\}.

Thus, by Lemma
3.3
(i), there exists
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{+}
such that
y^{(b,1)}=\phi^{i}
, and hence

x^{(b,1)}\in C_{\Gamma}(y^{(b,1)})=C_{\Gamma}(\phi^{i})=(\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}^{+}{:}\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}^{\times}){:}\langle\phi\rangle.

Note that
\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}^{+}\leqslant X
, so
C_{X}(\phi^{i})=\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}^{+}{:}(X\cap(\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}^{\times}{:}\langle\phi\rangle))
, and
X\cap(\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}^{\times}{:}\langle\phi\rangle_{p^{\prime}})
is a Hall-
p^{\prime}
subgroup of
C_{X}(\phi^{i})
.
Thus,

\langle x\rangle_{p^{\prime}}^{(b,1)z}\leqslant X\cap(\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}^{\times}{:}\langle\phi\rangle_{p^{\prime}})
for some
z\in C_{X}(\phi^{i})
. Therefore,

\langle x^{(b,1)z}\rangle=\langle x\rangle_{p^{\prime}}^{(b,1)z}\langle y\rangle^{(b,1)z}=\langle x\rangle_{p^{\prime}}^{(b,1)z}\langle\phi^{i}\rangle\leqslant\mathbb{F}_{q_{1}}^{\times}{:}\langle\phi\rangle

and the proof is complete by noting that
(b,1)z\in X
.
∎

Lemma 4.15
.

Suppose

is even. Let

be of even order, and suppose the order of

in

is odd. Then
\langle x^{2}\rangle
is

-conjugate to a subgroup of
\langle\psi\rangle_{2^{\prime}}
and
\langle x\rangle
is
G
-conjugate to a subgroup of
\langle z\rangle\times\langle\psi\rangle_{2^{\prime}}

Let

. Then

and

is an involution in

. Note that any element in

of even order is an involution, and all involutions in

such that

and so

, where

is a Sylow

containing

is an element of odd order

such that

is contained in
\langle\psi\rangle_{2^{\prime}}
since
\langle\psi\rangle_{2^{\prime}}
is a Hall

-subgroup of the soluble group

. Therefore,

\langle x\rangle^{gg_{1}}=\langle x^{t}\rangle^{gg_{1}}\times\langle x^{2}\rangle^{gg_{1}}\leqslant\langle z\rangle\times\langle\psi\rangle_{2^{\prime}},

which concludes the proof.

5.1.

Lemma 5.1
.

Suppose

5.2.
Case (b)

Next, we turn to case (b) of Proposition

.

Lemma 5.2
.

Assume Proposition
4.14
(b). Then
\widehat{K}
is a large fixer of

.

Proof.

In view of Lemma

Now let

Observe that

does not fix a partition of
[n]
into
2
parts, then

(14)

|X|\leqslant 6\cdot\left(\left\lceil\frac{n}{3}\right\rceil!\right)^{3}.

Proof.

First note that

\frac{1}{2}\cdot(a!)^{b}\cdot b!=\frac{1}{2}\cdot|S_{a}\wr S_{b}|\leqslant|X|\leqslant|S_{a}\wr S_{b}|=(a!)^{b}\cdot b!,

where
ab=n
.

Now we consider the first inequality of (
13
). By Lemma
8.3
, we have

\frac{1}{2}\cdot(a!)^{b}\cdot b!\geqslant\frac{1}{2}\cdot\left(\frac{a^{a}}{e^{a-1}}\right)^{b}\cdot\frac{b^{b}}{e^{b-1}}=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{n^{n}}{e^{n-1}}\cdot b^{b-n}.

Hence, to prove that
|X|\geqslant 2^{n}
, it suffices to show that

\left(\frac{n}{2e}\right)^{n}>b^{n-b},

which is equivalent to

(15)

\frac{n}{2e}>\left(\frac{n}{a}\right)^{1-1/a}

since
ab=n
. Note that this clearly holds for
a>2e>4
, and so we only need to consider the cases where
a\in\{2,3,4,5\}
. One can check that (
15
) holds for each case in turn.

Now we establish the second inequality in (
13
). Using the bounds in Lemma
8.3
, we see that

(a!)^{b}\cdot b!\leqslant\left(\frac{a^{a+1}}{e^{a-1}}\right)^{b}\cdot\frac{b^{b+1}}{e^{b-1}}=\frac{a^{n}\cdot a^{b}\cdot b^{b}\cdot b}{e^{n-1}}=\frac{n^{n}}{e^{n-1}}\cdot\frac{n^{b}}{b^{n-1}}

and

2\cdot\left(\left\lceil\frac{n}{2}\right\rceil!\right)^{2}\geqslant 2\cdot\left(\frac{(n/2)^{n/2}}{e^{n/2-1}}\right)^{2}=\frac{n^{n}}{e^{n-2}}\cdot\frac{1}{2^{n-1}}.

Thus, we only need to show that

(16)

2^{n-1}\cdot e^{-1}\leqslant\frac{b^{n-1}}{n^{b}}=:f(b).

for
3\leqslant b\leqslant n/2
. To see this, note that

f^{\prime}(b)=\frac{b^{n-2}((n-1)-b\ln n)}{n^{b}},

which implies that
f(b)\geqslant\min\{f(3),f(n/2)\}
. Now it is easy to show that (
16
) holds for
n\geqslant 25
.

To complete the proof, assume
X
does not fix a partition of
[n]
into
2
parts, and we show that (
14
) holds. We first note that (
14
) holds for
n\leqslant 44
, so we may assume
n\geqslant 45
. Once again, we have
|X|\leqslant(a!)^{b}\cdot b!
with
ab=n
and
b\geqslant 3
. By arguing as above, it suffices to show that

f(b)\geqslant\frac{3^{n}}{6e^{2}}

for
4\leqslant b\leqslant n/2
. This is easy to obtain for all
n\geqslant 45
by noting that
f(b)\geqslant\min\{f(4),f(n/2)\}
with the same reason as above.
∎

Now we are able to verify the statement of Theorem
8
when
H
is intransitive on
[n]
.

Proposition 8.5
.

Suppose
n\geqslant 25
and
H
is a setwise stabiliser of a
k
-subset of
[n]
with
1\leqslant k<n/2
. Then for any maximal subgroup
K
of
G
,
K
is not a large fixer of
G
if
K\not\cong H
.

Proof.

For convenience, we assume
G=S_{n}
, as the proof for
G=A_{n}
is the same. First note that

|H|\geqslant k!(n-k)!\geqslant\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor!\cdot\left\lceil\frac{n}{2}\right\rceil!.

Now we use the bounds given in Lemma
8.3
, so

\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor!\cdot\left\lceil\frac{n}{2}\right\rceil!\geqslant\left(\frac{(n/2)^{n/2}}{e^{n/2-1}}\right)^{2}=\frac{(n/2)^{n}}{e^{n-2}}

and

6\cdot\left(\left\lceil\frac{n}{3}\right\rceil!\right)^{3}\leqslant 6n^{3}\cdot\left(\frac{(n/3)^{n/3+1}}{e^{n/3-1}}\right)^{3}=6n^{3}\cdot\frac{(n/3)^{n+3}}{e^{n-3}}.

With these bounds, it is routine to check that

\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor!\cdot\left\lceil\frac{n}{2}\right\rceil!>6\cdot\left(\left\lceil\frac{n}{3}\right\rceil!\right)^{3}

for all
n\geqslant 60
by arguing as in the proof of Lemma
8.4
, so if
K
is transitive on
[n]
then
|K|\geqslant|H|
only if
K=S_{n/2}\wr S_{2}
(one can check that this also holds if
25\leqslant n\leqslant 59
). Now if
K=S_{n/2}\wr S_{2}
then there exists an element of
K
of cycle type
[n/2,n/2]
, which does not fix any
k
-subset. Hence,
K=S_{n/2}\wr S_{2}
is not a fixer of
G
.

This implies that
K
is intransitive on
[n]
, and thus
K
fixes an
\ell
-subset for some
1\leqslant\ell<n/2
. Note that

|K|=\ell!(n-\ell)!\geqslant k!(n-k)!=|H|

if and only if
\ell\leqslant k
. Since
K\cong H
if
\ell=k
, we may assume
\ell<k
and

K=\mathrm{Sym}(\{1,\dots,\ell\})\times\mathrm{Sym}(\{\ell+1,\dots,n\}).

If
\ell\geqslant 2
, then
k>2
and
x=(1,2)^{\delta}(\ell+1,\dots,n)
does not fix a
k
-subset of
[n]
, where
\delta=1
if
n-\ell
is even, and
\delta=0
otherwise (so
x
is an even permutation), and thus
K
is not a fixer of
G
.

Finally, assume
\ell=1
. If
k\geqslant 3
then either
(2,\dots,n)
or
(3,\dots,n)
is an even permutation, and we see that each of these elements does not fix a
k
-subset. Now assume
k=2
. If
n
is odd, then we take
x=(2,3,4)(5,\dots,n)\in K
, while if
n
is even, then take
x=(2,3,4)(5,6,7,8)(9,\dots,n)\in K
. In either case,
x
is an even permutation that does not fix any
2
-subset. This completes the proof.
∎

To complete the proof of Theorem
8
, we turn to the case where
H
is imprimitive on
[n]
.

Lemma 8.6
.

Suppose
H
is imprimitive on
[n]
, and let
K
be a maximal subgroup of
G
which is intransitive on
[n]
. Then
K
is not a fixer of
G
.

Proof.

Suppose
H\cong(S_{a}\wr S_{b})\cap G
and
K\cong(S_{k}\times S_{n-k})\cap G
is a fixer of
G
, where
a,b\geqslant 2
and
k<n/2
. Let
m\in\{n-k,n-k-1\}
be an odd number, noting that
K
has an
m
-cycle and
a\leqslant m
. Then
H
has an
m
-cycle since
K
is a fixer of
G
, so we have
a\mid m
. If
a\leqslant n/3
, then
a
divides
m-2
as
K
has an
(m-2)
-cycle (note that
a\leqslant m-2
), which yields
a=1
since
(m,m-2)=1
, a contradiction. Now if
a=n/2
, then
m=n/2
,
k=n/2-1
and

. In this setting,
K
has an element of cycle type
[n/2-1,n/2+1]
, which is not
G
-conjugate to any element in
H
.
∎

Lemma 8.7
.

Suppose
H
is imprimitive on
[n]
, and let
K
be a maximal subgroup of
G
which is imprimitive on
[n]
. If
K\not\cong H
, then
K
is not a fixer of
G
.

Proof.

Suppose
H\cong(S_{a}\wr S_{b})\cap G
and
K\cong(S_{a^{\prime}}\wr S_{b^{\prime}})\cap G
, where
n=ab=a^{\prime}b^{\prime}
and
a\neq a^{\prime}
. We will prove that
K
is not a fixer of
G
, and we divide the proof into several cases.

Case 1.
a<a^{\prime}
and
a
is even.

In this case,
K
has an
(a+1)
-cycle. By arguing as in the proof of Lemma
8.6
, we see that
a\mid a+1
, which is impossible.

Case 2.
a+1<a^{\prime}
and
a
is odd.

Note that
K
has an
(a+2)
-cycle. Thus, with the same reason as above, we have
a\mid a+2
. But this yields
a=2
, which is incompatible with our assumption.

Case 3.
a+1=a^{\prime}
and
a
is odd.

Here we see that
K
has an element of cycle type
[(a^{\prime})^{2},1^{n-2a^{\prime}}]
, which is not
G
-conjugate to any element in
H
.

Case 4.
a>a^{\prime}
and
a^{\prime}
is odd.

We have
3\leqslant a^{\prime}<a\leqslant n/2
in this setting. First assume
n-a^{\prime}
is odd. Then we have
a\mid n-a^{\prime}
as
K
contains an
(n-a^{\prime})
-cycle, which yields
a\mid a^{\prime}
as
a\mid n
. However, this is incompatible to our assumption
a>a^{\prime}
. Now assume
n-a^{\prime}
is even, so both
n
and
n-2a^{\prime}
are odd. Note that
K
has an
(n-2a^{\prime})
-cycle and
n-2a^{\prime}>a
, and thus
a\mid n-2a^{\prime}
. But this forces
a\mid 2a^{\prime}
and hence
a=2a^{\prime}
is the only possibility, which gives a contradiction because
n
is odd and
a
is even.

Case 5.
a>a^{\prime}
and
a^{\prime}
is even.

In this final case, we note that
K
has an element of cycle type
[(n-a^{\prime})^{1},2^{1},1^{a-2}]
, and it is routine to check that this element is not
G
-conjugate to any element in
H
.
∎

Lemma 8.12
.

Suppose
H
is a primitive group on
[n]
and
K\not\cong H
is a maximal subgroup of
G
of type HA. Then
K
is not a large fixer of
G
.

Proof.

Suppose
K=\mathrm{AGL}_{d}(r)\cap G
is a large fixer of
G
. First note that
K
has an element
g
of cycle type

[(r^{d}-1)/2,(r^{d}-1)/2,1],\

which is
G
-conjugate to an element in
H
. This implies that
H
is of type HA or AS by
[
19
, Theorem 1.1]
. If
H
is of type HA, then we have
K\cong H
.

It suffices to consider the case where
H
is of type AS. Note that
H
contains a
G
-conjugate of
g
. Thus, by inspecting the groups listed in
[
19
, Theorem 1.1]
, which classifies the finite primitive permutation groups containing a permutation having at most four cycles, we see that either
\mathrm{soc}(H)=\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(p)
and

for some prime

, or
\mathrm{soc}(H)=\mathrm{PSL}_{m}(2)
and

.

In the former case,

is even, so by Theorem
8.11
, we have

\mu_{[n]}(K)=n/2<2n/3\leqslant\mu_{[n]}(H),

which is incompatible with Corollary

. Finally, in the latter case, we have

. Now Catalan’s conjecture, which is proved true in
[

, implies that

∎

Lemma 8.13
.

Suppose
H
is a primitive group on
[n]
and
K\not\cong H
is a maximal subgroup of
G
of type PA. Then
K
is not a large fixer of
G
.

Proof.

We may write

[n]=\Delta^{b}=\Delta_{1}\times\cdots\times\Delta_{b}

with
|\Delta_{i}|=a
, and

K=((\mathrm{Sym}(\Delta_{1})\times\cdots\times\mathrm{Sym}(\Delta_{b})){:}\mathrm{Sym}([b]))\cap G.

The action of
K
on
[n]
is given by

(17)

\left(\delta_{1},\dots,\delta_{b}\right)^{\left(x_{1},\dots,x_{b}\right)\sigma}=\left(\delta_{1^{\sigma^{-1}}}^{x_{1^{\sigma{-1}}}},\dots,\delta_{b^{\sigma^{-1}}}^{x_{b^{\sigma-1}}}\right)

for any
\delta_{i}\in\Delta_{i}
,
x_{i}\in\mathrm{Sym}(\Delta_{i})
and
\sigma\in\mathrm{Sym}([b])
.

Let
x=(x_{1},1,\dots,1)
, where
x_{1}
is a
3
-cycle in
\mathrm{Sym}(\Delta_{1})
. Obviously,
x
is an even permutation in
\mathrm{Sym}([n])
, so

. Note that

\left(\delta_{1},\dots,\delta_{b}\right)^{x}=(\delta_{1}^{x_{1}},\delta_{2},\dots,\delta_{b})

for any
(\delta_{1},\dots,\delta_{b})\in[n]
. This implies that

\mbox{{\rm fpr}}_{[n]}(x)=\frac{(a-3)a^{b-1}}{a^{b}}=\frac{a-3}{a}

and so
\mu_{[n]}(K)\leqslant 3a^{b-1}=3n/a
(in fact, if

then
\mu_{[n]}(K)=2n/a

[
21
, p. 130]
). In particular, we have
\mu_{[n]}(K)<2n/3
, and by Corollary
2.5
we have
\mu_{[n]}(K)\geqslant\mu_{[n]}(H)
. Therefore,

is one of the primitive groups listed in Theorem
8.11
.

First assume case (ii) of Theorem
8.11
, so

\mu_{[n]}(H)=\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\frac{1}{m-1}\right)n.

\mu_{[n]}(K)\geqslant\mu_{[n]}(H)

. However,

is clearly not a
5
-power, so no case with

8.11
since

is assumed. By inspecting
[
7
, Table 2]
and using Catalan’s conjecture
[
37
]
, one can see that either

does not have an integer solution with

and

, or
\mu_{[n]}(H)>3n/5
, so case (iv) of Theorem
8.11
does not arise. For example, if
\mathrm{soc}(H)=\mathrm{PSL}_{d}(2)
with its action on

-subspaces of
\mathbb{F}_{d}^{2}
, then

, and as noted above, there is no integer solution to

with

. If
H
is of case (v) of Theorem
8.11
, then
\mu_{[n]}(H)=n/2
and
a
is a
2
-power, which implies that
a\geqslant 8
and
\mu_{[n]}(K)\leqslant 3n/8
, so this is incompatible to Corollary
2.5
.

Now let us consider case (i) of Theorem
8.11
. Here we have
\mathrm{soc}(H)=A_{m}
and

(18)

a^{b}={m\choose\ell},

where
2\leqslant\ell\leqslant m/2
. By
[
22
, Theorem 2]
, we see that apart from the case

, the only integer solution to (
18
) is
(a,b,m,\ell)=(140,2,50,3)
. For the latter case, we have

, which is incompatible with Lemma
2.2
(ii). Thus, we may now assume

, so

. Recall that
x=(x_{1},1,\dots,1)
, where
x_{1}
is a
3
-cycle in
\mathrm{Sym}(\Delta_{1})
. As noted above, we have
\mbox{{\rm fpr}}_{[n]}(x)=1-3/a
. Moreover,
[
7
, Proposition 3.4]
shows that

\mbox{{\rm fpr}}_{[n]}(y)\leqslant 1-\frac{6(m-2)}{m(m-1)}

.
In view of Lemma

, it suffices to show that

This is equivalent to

.

For the remained of the proof, let us turn to case (vi) of Theorem

, where

is also a maximal subgroup of

of type PA, which requires a more detailed analysis. First note that there exists an integer

Case 1.
m<\ell

In this case, we have

as discussed above, which is incompatible with Corollary

.

Case 2.
\ell=2
,

is odd and

is a
2
-power

First assume
c\neq 3
, noting that
8
divides

Let

be the greatest
2

\frac{d}{2}

(\frac{d}{m})_{2}=2k
as

\sigma=\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\dots\sigma_{\frac{d}{2k}}\in\mathrm{Sym}([d/2])

disjoint

-cycles. We claim that

is not
G
-conjugate to any element in
H
.

To see this, first note that
x
is the product of
\frac{c^{d}-1}{k(c+1)}
disjoint
k(c+1)
-cycles. Suppose

is
G
-conjugate to

. Then by
[

h=(h_{1},\underbrace{1,\dots,1}_{s_{1}-1},h_{2},\underbrace{1,\dots,1}_{s_{2}-1},,\dots,h_{t},\underbrace{1,\dots,1}_{s_{t}-1})\sigma^{\prime}\in H

, where

\sigma_{i}=(s_{1}+\cdots+s_{i-1}+1,\dots,s_{1}+\cdots+s_{i}).

As

is a power of

and

are

|\mathrm{fix}_{[n]}(h)|=|\mathrm{fix}_{[n]}(x)|=1

|\mathrm{fix}_{[n]}(h)|=|\mathrm{fix}_{\Gamma}(h_{1})|^{s_{1}}\cdots|\mathrm{fix}_{\Gamma}(h_{t})|^{s_{t}}.

This implies that
|\mathrm{fix}_{\Gamma}(h_{i})|=1
.
One can also observe that there exists
1\leqslant i\leqslant t
such that
|\sigma_{i}|
divides
2k
, otherwise
4k
divides
|\sigma_{i}|
for each
1\leqslant i\leqslant t
and hence

|\sigma_{1}|+|\sigma_{2}|+\dots+|\sigma_{t}|=\frac{d}{m}

is divisible by
4k,
which is incompatible to
(\frac{d}{m})_{2}=2k
.
Note that
h
contains a cycle of length
2ok
for some integer
o
if
h_{i}
contains a cycle of length
o
.
Thus,
h_{i}
is a product of
\frac{2(c^{m}-1)}{c+1}
disjoint
\frac{c+1}{2}
-cycles. This is impossible, since
c^{m}-1
is indivisible by
4
.

We next assume
c=3
. It follows from the main theorem of
[
38
]
that either
d=6
or there exists a prime
p
such that
\frac{d}{m}<p<\frac{d}{2}
. For the former, it is easy to check that
|H|>|K|.

Now assume the latter case and let
\sigma=(1,2,\dots,p)\in S_{\frac{d}{2}}\leqslant K
. We claim that
\sigma
is not
G
-conjugate to any element of
H.

To verify the claim, we first observe that
\sigma
fixes exactly
3^{d-2p+2}
points of
[n]
. Assume
\sigma
is
G
-conjugate to an element
h\in H
. Then
h\in\mathrm{Sym}(\Gamma_{1})\times\cdots\times\mathrm{Sym}(\Gamma_{d/m})
since
p>d/m
. Thus, we may write

h=(h_{1},h_{2},\dots,h_{d/m}),

where

h_{1}^{p}=h_{2}^{p}=\dots h_{d/m}^{p}=1.

Since
|h|=|\sigma|=p
, there exists
1\leqslant j\leqslant\frac{d}{m}
such that
|h_{j}|=p.

Thus,
|\mathrm{fix}_{\Gamma}(h_{j})|
is coprime to
3,
a contradiction to
|\mathrm{fix}_{\Gamma}(h_{j})|
dividing
|\mathrm{fix}_{[n]}(h)|=3^{d-2p+2}.

Case 3.
m>\ell
,
\ell
does not divide
m
, and
(c,m,\ell)
does not satisfy Case 2

By Zsigmondy’s theorem
[
45
]
, there exists a prime divisor
r
of
c^{\ell}-1
that does not divide
c^{m}-1
. Let
\tau
be an element in
\mathrm{Sym}(\Delta)
of order
r
that fixes a unique point of
\Delta
, and let
x=(\tau,\dots,\tau)\in K
, noting that
x
fixes a unique point in
[n]
. We claim that
x
is not
G
-conjugate to any element in
H
.

Suppose
h\in H
is
G
-conjugate to
x
. Again, by
[
9
, Proposition 2.1]
, we may assume

h=(h_{1},\underbrace{1,\dots,1}_{r-1},h_{2},\underbrace{1,\dots,1}_{r-1},\dots,h_{t^{\prime}},\underbrace{1,\dots,1}_{r-1},h_{t^{\prime}+1},h_{t^{\prime}+2},\dots,h_{t})\sigma\in H

for some
t
and
t^{\prime}
, where
\sigma_{i}=(ri-r+1,\dots,ri)
and
\sigma=\sigma_{1}\cdots\sigma_{t^{\prime}}
. Since
h^{r}=1
, we have

h_{1}=\cdots=h_{t^{\prime}}=h_{t^{\prime}+1}^{r}=\cdots=h_{t}^{r}=1.

Hence,
t^{\prime}=0
since
h
has a unique fixed point in
[n]
, and moreover,
h_{i}
fixes a unique point in
\Delta
if
t^{\prime}+1\leqslant i\leqslant t
, which is impossible since
r
does not divide
c^{m}-1
.

Now it suffices to consider the case where
\ell
divides
m
, and thus from now we write
m=\ell t
for some
t\geqslant 2
(so
c^{m}=a^{t}
and
d/m=b/t
).

Case 4.
\ell
divides
m
and
b\geqslant 2a

Let
r
be a prime such that
b/2<r<b
(the existence follows from Bertrand postulate). Note that
r>b/2\geqslant a
. Let
x
be an
r
-cycle in
\mathrm{Sym}([b])
. Then
x
fixes exactly
a^{b-r+1}
points in
[n]
. Suppose
h\in H
is of order
r
, which fixes
a^{b-r+1}
points in
[n]
. The assumption
b/2<r
yields
h\in S_{a^{t}}^{b/t}
, so we may write
h=(h_{1},\dots,h_{b/t})
with
h_{i}\in\mathrm{Sym}(\Gamma_{i})
, noting that there exists
j
such that
|h_{j}|=r
. Now
|\mathrm{fix}_{\Gamma_{j}}(h_{j})|=a^{t}-kr
for some positive integer
k
. However,
|\mathrm{fix}_{\Gamma_{j}}(h_{j})|
divides
|\mathrm{fix}_{[n]}(x)|=a^{b-r+1}
, whereas

(a^{t},a^{t}-kr)=(a^{t},r)=1

implies that
|\mathrm{fix}_{\Gamma_{j}}(h_{j})|
is coprime to
a
. This gives a contradiction, so
x
is not
G
-conjugate to any element in
H
.

Case 5.
\ell
divides
m
and
b<2a

In this final case, we will prove that
|H|>|K|
. To do this, first note that

(19)

\frac{|H|}{|K|}=\frac{(a^{t})!^{b/t}(\frac{b}{t})!}{(a!)^{b}\cdot b!}=\frac{\prod_{j=a+1}^{a^{t}}j^{b/t}}{(a!)^{b-b/t}\cdot\prod_{k=b/t+1}^{b}k}>\frac{\prod_{j=a+1}^{a^{t}}j^{b/t-1}\cdot\prod_{i=a+1}^{a^{t}-b+b/t}i}{(a!)^{b-b/t}}

since
b<2a<a^{t}
. Observe that the numerator of the last term of (
19
) is a product of
(a^{t}-a)b/t+b/t-b
integers strictly larger than
a
, so it suffices to show that

(a^{t}-a)b/t+b/t-b\geqslant a(b-b/t).

This is given by the inequality
a^{t}+1\geqslant t(a+1)
.

We conclude the proof since all cases have been discussed.
∎

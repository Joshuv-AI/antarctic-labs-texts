On the Study of Sample Complexity for Polynomial Neural Networks
Chao Pan, Chuanyi Zhang

Abstract
As a general type of machine learning approach, artificial neural networks have established state-of-art benchmarks in many pattern recognition and data analysis tasks. Among various kinds of neural networks architectures, polynomial neural networks (PNNs) have been recently shown to be analyzable by spectrum analysis via neural tangent kernel, and particularly effective at image generation and face recognition. However, acquiring theoretical insight into the computation and sample complexity of PNNs remains an open problem. In this paper, we extend the analysis in previous literature to PNNs and obtain novel results on sample complexity of PNNs, which provides some insights in explaining the generalization ability of PNNs.



1
Introduction

Neural networks are powerful tools in the area of machine learning, especially deep learning. And it is observed that we can normally get better performance when we increase the number of parameters in neural networks, such as the width (the dimensions of parameter matrices each layer) and the depth (the number of layers). By intuition, when the number of parameters grows neural networks will have the potential to overfit the training data, resulting in a larger generalization error. However, it is surprising that neural networks as deep as
1,000
layers can still possess remarkable generalization ability
[
Li et al., 2021
]
. This inspires researchers to study the representation power of neural networks.

It is well-known that generalization error is related to sample complexity of the function class of neural networks
[
Bousquet et al., 2003
]
. Therefore, most works start from the analysis of sample complexity in neural networks. In
[
Neyshabur et al., 2015
]
, by inductively applying contraction principle on single layer of a neural network, it gives a bound on the network generalization error which has exponential dependence on the network depth
d
. With further investigation,
[
Bartlett et al., 2017
]
shows that this bound can be tightened to polynomial dependence on
d
, and
[
Zhu et al., 2022
]
studies the sample complexities of the Coupled CP-decomposition model (CCP) and Nested Coupled CP-decomposition model (NCP) of polynomial neural networks (PNNs), which also have polynomial dependence on the number of layers. However, in practice, people have observed that the number of training samples required for convergence does not necessarily goes up when a network becomes deeper.
[
Golowich et al., 2018
]
proves that, under certain conditions on norms of weight matrices and assumptions on the activation functions, we can achieve a depth-independent bound on the sample complexity.

In this paper, we mainly focus on developing a similar depth-independent bound on sample complexity of a more general class of PNNs, which is quite different from the architectures that previous works have considered. Specifically, we use
\sigma(x)=x^{k}\ (k\geq 2)
as our activation function. There are two main reasons why PNNs are of particular interest in this paper. First, PNNs are easy to analyze because of the simple structure.
[
Chrysos et al., 2020
,
Choraria et al., 2022
]
study the representation power of one instance of PNNs called
\Pi
-Nets.
[
Livni et al., 2014
,
Soltani and Hegde, 2018
,
Du and Lee, 2018
]
show that PNNs can efficiently approximate other networks. Specifically, it only requires
\mathcal{O}(\log\log(1/\epsilon))
polynomial activation layers to approximate a 2-layer sigmoid networks with
l_{1}
regularization, where
\epsilon
indicates the approximation error. Beside this,
[
Livni et al., 2014
]
shows that the training technique for such networks is very similar to SGD, and can be computed efficiently for each iteration. The other reason is that we are still lacking an understanding of the generalization ability of PNNs. Therefore, our contribution in this paper is an in-depth study on the sample complexity of PNNs, and we show that the complexity can be depth-independent under certain conditions, which may shed some light in understanding the recent success of deep PNNs in image generation
[
Karras et al., 2019
]
and face recognition
[
Chrysos et al., 2020
]
. Note that most previous works on PNNs only focus on one specific choice of
k
(i.e.,
k=2
), but our conclusion in this paper is applicable to any
k\geq 2
.

2
Preliminaries

This section introduces the notations used in later context. We use bold-faced lower case letters for vectors, non-bold lower case letters for scalars, and capital letters for matrices or fixed parameters.

For a vector
\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, its
L_{p}
norm is denoted as
\|\mathbf{w}\|_{p}=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|w_{i}|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}
, and
\|\mathbf{w}\|=\|\mathbf{w}\|_{2}
. For a matrix
W\in\mathbb{R}^{h\times n}
,

\left\lVert W\right\rVert_{q,p}=\left(\sum_{j}\left(\sum_{k}|W_{j,k}|^{q}\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}=\left(\sum_{j}\left\lVert\mathbf{w}_{j}\right\rVert_{q}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}
, where
\mathbf{w}_{j}
is the
j
-th row of matrix
W
. Note that our definition of
\left\lVert W\right\rVert_{q,p}
is a bit different from the
L_{p,q}
norm of a matrix, and it is for simplicity of analysis.

\left\lVert W\right\rVert_{F}
refers to Frobenius norm and
\|W\|
refers to spectral norm.
\left\lVert W\right\rVert_{p}
denotes Schatten
p
-norm of the spectrum of
W
with
p\geq 1
, which is defined by
\left\lVert W\right\rVert_{p}=\left(\sum_{i}\sigma_{i}^{p}(W)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}
and
\sigma_{i}(W)
’s are singular values of
W
.

A cascade neural network with depth
d
is defined as follows:

\mathsf{N}^{(d)}:\mathbf{x}\rightarrow W_{d}\sigma_{d-1}(W_{d-1}\sigma_{d-2}(\dots\sigma_{1}(W_{1}\mathbf{x}))).

And to simplify the notation, we denote
W_{b}^{r}
as the shorthand for the matrix tuple
\{W_{b},W_{b+1},\dots,W_{r}\}
, and
\mathsf{N}_{W_{b}^{r}}
denotes the function computed by the sub-network composed of layers
b
through
r
:

\mathsf{N}_{W_{b}^{r}}:\mathbf{x}\rightarrow W_{r}\sigma_{r-1}(W_{r-1}\sigma_{r-2}(\dots\sigma_{b}(W_{b}\mathbf{x}))).

Given a real-valued function class
\mathcal{H}
and some set of data points
\mathbf{x}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{m}\in\mathcal{X}
, the (empirical) Rademacher complexity
\hat{\mathcal{R}}_{m}(\mathcal{H})
is

\hat{\mathcal{R}}_{m}(\mathcal{H})=\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon^{m}}\sup_{h\in\mathcal{H}}\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{i}h(\mathbf{x}_{i}),

where
\varepsilon_{i}
is the Rademacher random variable, which takes value
+1
and
-1
with equal probability. Throughout this paper,
\mathbb{E}
is a shorthand for
\mathbb{E}_{\varepsilon^{m}}
.

Our main results provide upper bounds on the Rademacher complexity with respect to PNNs under mild assumptions on input data.

3
Related Works

Classical results on the sample complexity of neural networks normally have strong dependency on the dimensions of parameter matrices. The upper bound shown in
[
Anthony et al., 1999
]
is based on VC dimension and strongly rely on both the depth and the width of the network. In
[
Neyshabur et al., 2015
]
, the authors make use of contraction principle and show an upper bound that have an exponential dependence on the network depth
d
, even if the norm of each parameter matrix is strictly upper bounded. Specifically, if input data
\mathbf{x}_{i},i\in[m]
satisfies
||\mathbf{x}_{i}||\leq B
and each parameter matrix
W_{j},j\in[d]
satisfies
||W_{j}||_{F}\leq M_{F}(j)
, then under some assumptions on the activations, the results in
[
Neyshabur et al., 2015
]
show that

\hat{\mathcal{R}}_{m}\left(\mathcal{F}\left(X^{m}\right)\right)\sim\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{B2^{d}\prod_{j=1}^{d}M_{F}(j)}{\sqrt{m}}\right),

where
X^{m}
is a matrix consisting of all input data points. Although this bound has no explicit dependence on the network width (the dimensions of
W_{j},j\in[d]
), it has an exponential dependence on the network depth
d
, even if
M_{F}(j)\leq 1,\forall j\in[d]
, which is obviously not desirable.
[
Bartlett et al., 2017
]
improves this exponential dependency to polynomial dependency based on a covering numbers argument. The authors show that

\hat{\mathcal{R}}_{m}\left(\mathcal{F}\left(X^{m}\right)\right)\sim\tilde{\mathcal{O}}\left(B\prod_{j=1}^{d}\left\lVert W_{j}\right\rVert\sqrt{\frac{d^{3}}{m}}\right),

where some lower-order logarithmic factors are ignored. Although the dependency of
d
becomes polynomial (i.e.,
d^{3/2}
) in the new bound, the bound becomes trivial when
d\geq\Omega(m^{1/3})
, since the bound will not decrease with the growth of sample size
m
in this case, indicating that it is not possible to reduce generalization error by increasing the size of training data. This is obviously not consistent with our observations during the training process of deep neural networks
[
Li et al., 2021
]
.

In
[
Golowich et al., 2018
]
the authors show another line of proof techniques that gives rise to the first
depth-independent
upper bound on sample complexity of neural networks. Their main idea follows these two observations:

•

Each single layer of a neural network is equivalent to a generalized linear classifier of the form
\left\{\mathbf{x}\mapsto\sigma\left(\mathbf{w}^{\top}\mathbf{x}\right):\|\mathbf{w}\|\leq M,\|\mathbf{x}\|\leq B\right\}
, where
\sigma(\cdot)
is some common activation function such as ReLU. And the generalization error of this function class is known to be
\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{MB}{\sqrt{m}}\right)
.

•

We can also view the classifier stated above as a class of “ultra-thin” network with following form:

\mathcal{F}:=\left\{\sigma_{d}(w_{d}\sigma_{d-1}(w_{d-1}\sigma_{d-2}(\dots\sigma_{1}(\mathbf{w}_{1}^{\top}\mathbf{x})))),\|\mathbf{w}_{1}\|\cdot\prod_{j=2}^{d}|w_{j}|\leq M\right\}.

Note that here
\mathbf{w}_{1}
is a vector and
w_{2},\dots,w_{d}
are scalars. Therefore, for this ultra-thin network, we should still have
\hat{\mathcal{R}}_{m}\left(\mathcal{F}\left(X^{m}\right)\right)\sim\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{MB}{\sqrt{m}}\right)
. The only difference between real neural networks and this “ultra-thin” network is that
W_{2},\dots,W_{d}
are matrices in real neural networks. Therefore, one could image that the change would take place in the constraint
\prod_{j=1}^{d}\left\lVert W_{j}\right\rVert\leq M
, but the upper bound should remain independent of depth
d
.

With these two observations,
[
Golowich et al., 2018
]
proposes the following two theorems:

Theorem 3.1

(
[
Golowich et al., 2018
]
)
.

For any
p\geq 1
and any net
\mathsf{N}^{(d)}
such that
\prod_{j=1}^{d}\lVert W_{j}\rVert\geq\Gamma
and
\prod_{j=1}^{d}\lVert W_{j}\rVert_{p}\leq M
, for
\forall r\in[d]
, there exists an alternative net
\widetilde{\mathsf{N}}^{(d)}
such that they are identical except for weights of the
r
-th layer:
\widetilde{W}_{r}
.

\widetilde{W}_{r}=s\mathbf{u}\mathbf{v}^{T}
, where
s,\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}
are the most significant singular value and corresponding left and right singular vectors of
W_{r}
.

\sup_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\left\lVert\mathsf{N}^{(d)}(\mathbf{x})-\widetilde{\mathsf{N}}^{(d)}(\mathbf{x})\right\rVert\leq B\prod_{j=1}^{d}\lVert W_{j}\rVert\left(\frac{2p\log(M/\Gamma)}{r}\right)^{1/p}.

Theorem 3.2

(
[
Golowich et al., 2018
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{H}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[-R,R]
,
\mathcal{F}_{L,a}:[-R,R]\to\mathbb{R}
are
L
-Lipschitz functions and
f(0)=a
for some fixed
a
. Denote
\mathcal{F}_{L,a}\circ\mathcal{H}:=\{f(h(\cdot)):f\in\mathcal{F}_{L,a},h\in\mathcal{H}\}
. The Rademacher complexity satisfies

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{R}}_{m}\left(\mathcal{F}_{L,a}\circ\mathcal{H}\right)

\displaystyle\leq cL\left(\frac{R}{\sqrt{m}}+\log^{3/2}(m)\hat{\mathcal{R}}_{m}(\mathcal{H})\right),

where
c>0
is some universal constant.

With Theorem
3.1
, we can substitute the original network to a
r
-layer net followed by a univariate net. And Theorem
3.2
shows that we achieve a bound on the sample complexity of neural networks by complexity of the first
r
layers net. Note that after Theorem
3.2
the complexity is independent of the depth
d
of the original network. Combining these two we arrive at the depth-independent bound.

Theorem 3.3

(
[
Golowich et al., 2018
]
)
.

Consider the following hypothesis class of networks on
\mathcal{X}=\{\mathbf{x}:\lVert\mathbf{x}\rVert\leq B\}
:

\mathcal{H}=\left\{\mathsf{N}_{W_{1}^{d}}:\begin{array}[]{c}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\left\|W_{j}\right\|\geq\Gamma\\
\forall j\in\{1,\ldots,d\},W_{j}\in\mathcal{W}_{j},\max\left\{\frac{\left\|W_{j}\right\|}{M(j)},\frac{\left\|W_{j}\right\|_{p}}{M_{p}(j)}\right\}\leq 1\end{array}\right\},

for some parameters
p,\Gamma\geq 1,\{M(j),M_{p}(j),\mathcal{W}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{d}
. Also for any
r\in\{1,\dots,d\}
, define

\mathcal{H}_{r}=\left\{\mathsf{N}_{W_{1}^{r}}:\begin{array}[]{c}\mathsf{N}_{W_{1}^{r}}\text{ maps to }\mathbb{R}\\
\forall j\in\{1,\ldots,r-1\},W_{j}\in\mathcal{W}_{j}\\
\forall j\in\{1,\ldots,r\},\max\left\{\frac{\left\|W_{j}\right\|}{M(j)},\frac{\left\|W_{j}\right\|_{p}}{M_{p}(j)}\right\}\leq 1\end{array}\right\}.

Finally, for
m>1
, let
\ell\circ\mathcal{H}:=\{(\ell_{1}(h(\mathbf{x}_{1})),\ldots,\ell_{m}(h(\mathbf{x}_{m}))):h\in\mathcal{H}\}
, where
\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{m}
are real-valued loss functions which are
\frac{1}{\gamma}
-Lipschitz and satisfy
\ell_{1}(\mathbf{0})=\cdots=\ell_{m}(\mathbf{0})=a
for some
a\in\mathbb{R}
. Assume that
|a|\leq\frac{B\Pi_{j=1}^{d}M(j)}{\gamma}
. Then the Rademacher complexity
\hat{\mathcal{R}}_{m}\left(\ell\circ\mathcal{H}\right)
is upper bounded by

\displaystyle\frac{cB\prod_{j=1}^{d}M(j)}{\gamma}\left(\min_{r\in\{1,\ldots,d\}}\left\{\frac{\log^{3/2}(m)}{B}\cdot\max_{r^{\prime}\in\{1,\ldots,r\}}\frac{\hat{\mathcal{R}}_{m}\left(\mathcal{H}_{r^{\prime}}\right)}{\prod_{j=1}^{r^{\prime}}M(j)}+\left(\frac{\log\left(\frac{1}{\Gamma}\prod_{j=1}^{d}M_{p}(j)\right)}{r}\right)^{1/p}+\frac{1+\sqrt{\log r}}{\sqrt{m}}\right\}\right),

where
c>0
is a universal constant.

It is worth pointing out that the results show in
[
Golowich et al., 2018
]
are not directly applicable to PNNs, due to their assumptions on activations. Therefore, we extend this line of analysis to PNNs in this paper.

4
Improved Sample Complexity for Polynomial Neural Networks

Both depth-dependent and depth-independent results are provided in this section. Some proofs are relegated to a longer version of this paper for brevity.

4.1
Depth-Dependent Sample Complexity for Polynomial Neural Networks

We will need the following Lemma
4.1
and Lemma
4.2
to get the bound for polynomial networks with dependence on only
\sqrt{d}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let activations be
\sigma(x)=x^{k}(k\geq 2)
which is applied element-wise. Then for
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, function class
\mathcal{F}:\forall f\in\mathcal{F},\|f(\mathbf{x})\|_{p}\leq B,\|W\|_{q,p}\leq R
, where
\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{p}=1
, and any convex and monotonically increasing function
g:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
, we can have that with condition
BR\leq\left(\frac{1}{k}\right)^{\frac{1}{k-1}}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F};\|W\|_{q,p}\leq R}g\left(\left\lVert\sum_{i=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{i}\sigma(Wf(\mathbf{x}_{i}))\right\rVert_{p}\right)\leq 2\mathbb{E}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}g\left(R\cdot\left\lVert\sum_{i=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{i}f(\mathbf{x}_{i})\right\rVert_{p}\right).

(1)

Proof.

Denote
\mathbf{w}_{1},\mathbf{w}_{2},\dots,\mathbf{w}_{h}
as the rows of the matrix
W\in\mathbb{R}^{h\times n}
. Since the activation is
\sigma(x)=x^{k}
, we have for any
\mathbf{w}_{j}
,

\varepsilon_{i}\sigma(\mathbf{w}_{j}^{\mathrm{T}}f(\mathbf{x}_{i}))=\varepsilon_{i}\left\lVert\mathbf{w}_{j}\right\rVert_{q}^{k}\sigma\left(\frac{\mathbf{w}_{j}^{\mathrm{T}}}{\left\lVert\mathbf{w}_{j}\right\rVert_{q}}f(\mathbf{x}_{i})\right).

Then for the whole matrix
W
we have

\left\lVert\sum_{i=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{i}\sigma(Wf(\mathbf{x}_{i}))\right\rVert_{p}^{p}=\sum_{j=1}^{h}\left\lVert\mathbf{w}_{j}\right\rVert_{q}^{kp}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{i}\sigma\left(\frac{\mathbf{w}_{j}^{\mathrm{T}}}{\left\lVert\mathbf{w}_{j}\right\rVert_{q}}f(\mathbf{x}_{i})\right)\right)^{p}

According to definition of
\left\lVert W\right\rVert_{q,kp}
, we have

\left\lVert W\right\rVert_{q,kp}=\left(\sum_{j}\left(\sum_{k}|W_{j,k}|^{q}\right)^{\frac{kp}{q}}\right)^{\frac{1}{kp}}=\left(\sum_{j}\left\lVert\mathbf{w}_{j}\right\rVert_{q}^{kp}\right)^{\frac{1}{kp}}\leq\left(\sum_{j}\left\lVert\mathbf{w}_{j}\right\rVert_{q}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}=\left\lVert W\right\rVert_{q,p}\leq R,

here we use the fact that
\|\mathbf{w}\|_{p}\leq\|\mathbf{w}\|_{q}
if
p\geq q
. So the supremum over
\mathbf{w}_{1},\mathbf{w}_{2},\dots,\mathbf{w}_{h}
such that
\left\lVert W\right\rVert_{q,kp}^{kp}=\sum_{j}\left\lVert\mathbf{w}_{j}\right\rVert_{q}^{kp}\leq\left\lVert W\right\rVert_{q,p}^{kp}\leq R^{kp}
must be attained when
\left\lVert\mathbf{w}_{j}\right\rVert_{q}=R
for some
j
, and
\left\lVert\mathbf{w}_{i}\right\rVert_{q}=0
for all
i\neq j
, as we can consider
\frac{\mathbf{w}_{j}^{\mathrm{T}}}{\left\lVert\mathbf{w}_{j}\right\rVert_{q}}
as a unit vector in the sense of
L_{q}
norm. Therefore, we have

\mathbb{E}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F},\left\lVert W\right\rVert_{q,p}\leq R}g\left(\left\lVert\sum_{i=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{i}\sigma\left(Wf(\mathbf{x}_{i})\right)\right\rVert_{p}\right)=\mathbb{E}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F},\|\mathbf{w}\|_{q}=R}g\left(\left\lvert\sum_{i=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{i}\sigma\left(\mathbf{w}^{\mathrm{T}}f(\mathbf{x}_{i})\right)\right\rvert\right).

Since
g(|z|)\leq g(z)+g(-z)
, the above quantity can be upper bounded by

2\mathbb{E}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F},\|\mathbf{w}\|_{q}=R}g\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{i}\sigma\left(\mathbf{w}^{\mathrm{T}}f(\mathbf{x}_{i})\right)\right),

where we use the symmetry property of Rademacher variables
\varepsilon_{i}
. By Eq. (4.20) in
[
Ledoux and Talagrand, 1991
]
, we can further bound this term by

\displaystyle 2\mathbb{E}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F},\|\mathbf{w}\|_{q}=R}g\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{i}\sigma\left(\mathbf{w}^{\mathrm{T}}f(\mathbf{x}_{i})\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbb{E}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F},\|\mathbf{w}\|_{q}=R}g\left(L\left\lVert\mathbf{w}\right\rVert_{q}\left\lVert\sum_{i=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{i}f(\mathbf{x}_{i})\right\rVert_{p}\right)

\displaystyle=2\mathbb{E}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}g\left(LR\left\lVert\sum_{i=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{i}f(\mathbf{x}_{i})\right\rVert_{p}\right),

where
L
represents the Lipschitz constant for
\sigma\left(\cdot\right)
. Since
\sigma(x)
is continuous, it is equivalent to make sure that
|\sigma^{\prime}\left(\mathbf{w}^{\mathrm{T}}f(\mathbf{x}_{i})\right)|\leq L
. Let us consider
L=1
in the rest of this paper for simplicity,

\displaystyle kx^{(k-1)}

\displaystyle\leq 1

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{k}\right)^{\frac{1}{k-1}}.

So if
\|f(\mathbf{x})\|_{p}\leq B
and
\|\mathbf{w}\|_{q}\leq R
, we have

\displaystyle\left\lvert\mathbf{w}^{\mathrm{T}}f(\mathbf{x}_{i})\right\rvert\leq\|\mathbf{w}\|_{q}\cdot\|f(\mathbf{x})\|_{p}\leq BR\leq\left(\frac{1}{k}\right)^{\frac{1}{k-1}}.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{d}
be the class of real-valued networks of depth
d
over the domain
\mathcal{X}
, and
\lVert W_{i}\rVert_{q,p}\leq M(i),\,\forall i\in[d]
, where
\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{p}=1
, and with activations satisfying Lemma
4.1
for each layer. Then,

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{R}}_{m}\left(\mathcal{H}_{d}\right)\leq\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{d}M(i)}{m}\left(\sqrt{2d\log 2}\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lVert\mathbf{x}_{i}\rVert_{p}^{2}}+\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left\lVert\mathbf{x}_{i}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\right).

Proof.

For some
\lambda>0
, we can upper bound the empirical Rademacher complexity by

\displaystyle m\hat{\mathcal{R}}_{m}\left(\mathcal{H}_{d}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\sup_{\mathsf{N}^{(d-1)},W_{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{i}W_{d}\sigma_{d-1}\left(\mathsf{N}^{(d-1)}(\mathbf{x}_{i})\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{\leq}}\frac{1}{\lambda}\log\mathbb{E}\sup_{\mathsf{N}^{(d-1)},W_{d}}\exp\left(\lambda\sum_{i=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{i}W_{d}\sigma_{d-1}\left(\mathsf{N}^{(d-1)}(\mathbf{x}_{i})\right)\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(b)}}{{\leq}}\frac{1}{\lambda}\log\mathbb{E}\sup_{\mathsf{N}^{(d-1)}}\exp\left(\lambda M(d)\left\lVert\sum_{i=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{i}\sigma_{d-1}\left(\mathsf{N}^{(d-1)}(\mathbf{x}_{i})\right)\right\rVert_{p}\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(c)}}{{\leq}}\frac{1}{\lambda}\log\left(2^{d}\cdot\mathbb{E}\exp\left(\lambda\prod_{i=1}^{d}M(i)\left\lVert\sum_{i=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{i}\mathbf{x}_{i}\right\rVert_{p}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda}\log\left(2^{d}\cdot\mathbb{E}\exp{\lambda Z}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{d\log 2}{\lambda}+\frac{1}{\lambda}\log\left(\mathbb{E}\exp\left(\lambda\left(Z-\mathbb{E}Z\right)\right)\right)+\mathbb{E}Z,

(3)

where
M=\prod_{i=1}^{d}M(i),Z=M\left\lVert\sum_{i=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{i}\mathbf{x}_{i}\right\rVert_{p}
. Here,
(a)
follows from Jensen’s inequality,
(b)
follows from Lemma
4.1
with
g(x)=\exp\left(M(d)\cdot\lambda x\right)
, and
(c)
follows from repeating this process
d
times.

For the second term
\frac{1}{\lambda}\log\left(\mathbb{E}\exp\left(\lambda\left(Z-\mathbb{E}Z\right)\right)\right)
, we have that

|Z\left(\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{i},\ldots,\varepsilon_{m}\right)-Z\left(\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,-\varepsilon_{i},\ldots,\varepsilon_{m}\right)|\leq 2M\left\|\mathbf{x}_{i}\right\|_{p}.

This implies that
Z
is a sub-Gaussian random variable and we can have the concentration inequality

\frac{1}{\lambda}\log\mathbb{E}\exp\left(\lambda\left(Z-\mathbb{E}Z\right)\right)\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}\frac{\lambda^{2}\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(2M\lVert\mathbf{x}_{i}\rVert_{p}\right)^{2}}{2}=\frac{\lambda M^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lVert\mathbf{x}_{i}\rVert_{p}^{2}}{2}.

Also by Jensen’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}Z

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(M\left\lVert\sum_{i=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{i}\mathbf{x}_{i}\right\rVert_{p}\right)=M\cdot\mathbb{E}\left(\left\lVert\sum_{i=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{i}\mathbf{x}_{i}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{\leq}}M\cdot\left(\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\sum_{i=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{i}\mathbf{x}_{i}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}=M\cdot\left(\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\lvert\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{ij}\varepsilon_{i}\right\rvert^{p}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=M\cdot\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left(\left\lvert\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{ij}\varepsilon_{i}\right\rvert^{p}\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(b)}}{{\leq}}M\cdot\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left\lVert\mathbf{x}_{i}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Here
(a)
follows from the fact that
f(x)=x^{1/p}
is a concave function for
p\geq 1
. Note that
(b)
is a pretty loose general bound for all
p\geq 1
. A much tighter bound can be obtained if we know the specific choice of
p
(i.e.,
p=2
).

Combining the results we have that Eq. (
3
) can be further upper bounded as follows

\displaystyle m\hat{\mathcal{R}}_{m}\left(\mathcal{H}_{d}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{d\log 2}{\lambda}+\frac{\lambda M^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lVert\mathbf{x}_{i}\rVert_{p}^{2}}{2}+M\cdot\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left\lVert\mathbf{x}_{i}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{\leq}}\sqrt{2d\log(2)}M\cdot\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lVert\mathbf{x}_{i}\rVert_{p}^{2}}+M\cdot\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left\lVert\mathbf{x}_{i}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where we choose
\lambda=\frac{\sqrt{2d\log(2)}}{M\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left\|\mathbf{x}_{i}\right\|_{p}^{2}}}
to optimize the upper bound in
(a)
.
∎

Combining Lemma
4.1
and Lemma
4.2
, we can have a depth-dependent upper bound on the sample complexity. But we need to take care of the norm constraints of all
W_{i}
to make sure that the condition in Lemma
4.1
holds for each layer.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{d}
be the class of real-valued networks of depth
d
over the domain
\mathcal{X}
. For
j\in\{2,\cdots,d\}
,
W_{j}
has
\left\lVert W_{j}\right\rVert_{q,p}\leq M(j)
,
M(j)\leq 2^{k-1}
;
\lVert W_{1}\rVert_{q,p}\leq\frac{1}{2B}
,
\lVert\mathbf{x}_{i}\rVert_{p}\leq B
; and activation function
\sigma(x)=x^{k}(k\geq 2)
which is applied element-wise. Then,

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{R}}_{m}\left(\mathcal{H}_{d}\right)\leq\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{d}M(i)}{m}\left(\sqrt{2d\log 2}\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lVert\mathbf{x}_{i}\rVert_{p}^{2}}+\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left\lVert\mathbf{x}_{i}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\right).

(4)

Proof.

First for function
f(x)=\left(\frac{1}{k}\right)^{\frac{1}{k-1}}
, it is monotonically increasing on
[2,\infty)
. So if
k\geq 2
, the condition in Lemma
4.1
becomes
BR\leq\frac{1}{2}
, meaning that
R\leq\frac{1}{2B}
. Let us consider the first layer. Since
\|\mathbf{x}_{i}\|_{p}\leq B
, we have for sure that

\lVert W_{1}\rVert_{q,p}\leq\frac{1}{2B}.

Then for second layer, the input becomes
f(\mathbf{x}_{i})=\sigma_{1}(W_{1}\mathbf{x}_{i})
. By definition we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert f(\mathbf{x}_{i})\right\rVert_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\sigma_{1}(W_{1}\mathbf{x}_{i})\right\rVert_{p}^{p}=\sum_{j=1}^{h}\left(\sigma(\mathbf{w}_{j}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{x}_{i})\right)^{p}=\sum_{j=1}^{h}\left(\mathbf{w}_{j}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{x}_{i}\right)^{kp}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{\leq}}\sum_{j=1}^{h}\left(\left\lVert\mathbf{w}_{j}\right\rVert_{q}\left\lVert\mathbf{x}_{i}\right\rVert_{p}\right)^{kp}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(b)}}{{\leq}}B^{kp}\sum_{j=1}^{h}\left\lVert\mathbf{w}_{j}\right\rVert_{q}^{kp}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(c)}}{{\leq}}B^{kp}\left\lVert W_{1}\right\rVert_{q,p}^{kp}\leq\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{kp}=B^{\prime p},

where
B^{\prime}=\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{k}
, which is independent of
B
. Here
(a)
follows from Hölder’s inequality,
(b)
follows from
\left\lVert W_{1}\right\rVert_{q,kp}^{kp}=\sum_{j=1}^{h}\left\lVert\mathbf{w}_{j}\right\rVert_{q}^{kp}
, and
(c)
follows from
\left\lVert W_{1}\right\rVert_{q,p}^{kp}\geq\left\lVert W_{1}\right\rVert_{q,kp}^{kp}
. This requires
\left\lVert W_{2}\right\rVert_{q,p}\leq\frac{1}{2B^{\prime}}=2^{k-1}
. The conclusion holds for all
W_{i}
when
i\geq 2
. It shows that as long as the weight matrix of the first layer satisfies certain conditions related with original input, the constraints on other layers only depends on the activation of each layer. So we can get the overall condition:
\left\lVert W_{j}\right\rVert_{q,p}\leq 2^{k-1}
for
j\geq 2
;
\lVert W_{1}\rVert_{q,p}\leq\frac{1}{2B}
,
\lVert\mathbf{x}_{i}\rVert_{p}\leq B
. This allows us to use Lemma
4.1
and Lemma
4.2
and the proof is completed.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

For simplicity, we consider the activations of each layer to be the same in Theorem
4.3
as
\sigma(x)=x^{k}
. We can easily extend it to the case where activations are different in each layer. It will change nothing but the norm restriction of each weight matrix with respect to the value of
k
. And a similar conclusion would also hold for all activations satisfying property
\sigma(x)\leq x^{k}
.

4.2
Depth-Independent Sample Complexity for Polynomial Neural Networks

We next show how to get a depth-independent bound for polynomial networks following a similar argument in
[
Golowich et al., 2018
]
.

Lemma 4.4
.

For any matrix
W\in\mathbb{R}^{h\times n}
,
\forall p,q\geq 1
and
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
, there exists a rank-1 matrix
\widetilde{W}
of the same size as
W
such that

\displaystyle\lVert\widetilde{W}\rVert_{q,p}\leq\lVert W\rVert_{q,p},\;\lVert W-\widetilde{W}\rVert_{q,p}=\left(\lVert W\rVert_{q,p}^{p}-\lVert\widetilde{W}\rVert_{q,\infty}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(5)

Proof.

Let
\widetilde{W}
be a rank-1 matrix of the same size as
W
containing only one non-zero row
\mathbf{w}_{i}
, which is the row with largest
L_{q}
norm in
W
, meaning that
i=\argmax_{j\in[h]}\left\lVert\textbf{w}_{j}\right\rVert_{q}
. Then by definition, we know that

\displaystyle\lVert W-\widetilde{W}\rVert_{q,p}^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\neq i}\left\lVert\mathbf{w}_{j}\right\rVert_{q}^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{h}\left\lVert\mathbf{w}_{j}\right\rVert_{q}^{p}-\left\lVert\mathbf{w}_{i}\right\rVert_{q}^{p}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert W\right\rVert_{q,p}^{p}-\left\lVert W\right\rVert_{q,\infty}^{p}.

So we can have
\lVert W-\widetilde{W}\rVert_{q,p}=\left(\lVert W\rVert_{q,p}^{p}-\lVert\widetilde{W}\rVert_{q,\infty}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}
. And obviously, we also have
\lVert\widetilde{W}\rVert_{q,p}\leq\lVert W\rVert_{q,p}
.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

The original proof in
[
Golowich et al., 2018
]
uses SVD decomposition to construct the rank-1 matrix. We change the analysis to the row with largest
L_{q}
norm in our case.

Lemma 4.5
.

For two polynomial networks
\mathsf{N}^{(d)}:\left\{W_{1},\ldots,W_{r},\ldots,W_{d}\right\}
and
\widetilde{\mathsf{N}}^{(d)}:\left\{W_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{W_{r}},\ldots,W_{d}\right\}
, they only differ in the
r
-th layer. If all conditions in Theorem
4.3
hold for both
\mathsf{N}^{(d)}
and
\widetilde{\mathsf{N}}^{(d)}
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\left\lVert\mathsf{N}^{(d)}(\mathbf{x})-\widetilde{\mathsf{N}}^{(d)}(\mathbf{x})\right\rVert_{p}\leq B\prod_{j=1}^{d}\lVert W_{j}\rVert_{q,p}\cdot\frac{\lVert W_{r}-\widetilde{W}_{r}\rVert_{q,p}}{\lVert W_{r}\rVert_{q,\infty}}

(6)

Proof.

Since input
\mathbf{x}
and weight matrices satisfy the constraints, every activation function
\sigma_{i}
is 1-Lipschitz in
L_{p}
norm sense (
p\geq 1
), thus the Lipschitz constant of function
\mathsf{N}_{W_{r+1}^{d}}
is at most
\prod_{i=r+1}^{d}\left\lVert W_{i}\right\rVert_{q,p}
.

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathsf{N}^{(d)}(\mathbf{x})-\widetilde{\mathsf{N}}^{(d)}(\mathbf{x})\right\rVert_{p}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\mathsf{N}_{W_{r+1}^{d}}\left(\sigma_{r}\left(W_{r}\sigma_{r-1}\left(\mathsf{N}_{W_{1}^{r-1}}(\mathbf{x})\right)\right)\right)-\mathsf{N}_{W_{r+1}^{d}}\left(\sigma_{r}\left(\widetilde{W}_{r}\sigma_{r-1}\left(\mathsf{N}_{W_{1}^{r-1}}(\mathbf{x})\right)\right)\right)\right\rVert_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{i=r+1}^{d}\left\lVert W_{i}\right\rVert_{q,p}\left\lVert\sigma_{r}\left(W_{r}\sigma_{r-1}\left(\mathsf{N}_{W_{1}^{r-1}}(\mathbf{x})\right)\right)-\sigma_{r}\left(\widetilde{W}_{r}\sigma_{r-1}\left(\mathsf{N}_{W_{1}^{r-1}}(\mathbf{x})\right)\right)\right\rVert_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{i=r+1}^{d}\left\lVert W_{i}\right\rVert_{q,p}\left\lVert W_{r}\sigma_{r-1}\left(\mathsf{N}_{W_{1}^{r-1}}(\mathbf{x})\right)-\widetilde{W}_{r}\sigma_{r-1}\left(\mathsf{N}_{W_{1}^{r-1}}(\mathbf{x})\right)\right\rVert_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{i=r+1}^{d}\left\lVert W_{i}\right\rVert_{q,p}\cdot\left\lVert W_{r}-\widetilde{W}_{r}\right\rVert_{q,p}\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{r-1}\left\lVert W_{j}\right\rVert_{q,p}\cdot\left\lVert\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}

\displaystyle\leq B\prod_{j=1}^{d}\lVert W_{j}\rVert_{q,p}\cdot\frac{\lVert W_{r}-\widetilde{W}_{r}\rVert_{q,p}}{\lVert W_{r}\rVert_{q,p}}

\displaystyle\leq B\prod_{j=1}^{d}\lVert W_{j}\rVert_{q,p}\cdot\frac{\lVert W_{r}-\widetilde{W}_{r}\rVert_{q,p}}{\lVert W_{r}\rVert_{q,\infty}}\qed

Lemma 4.6
.

If
\mathsf{N}^{(d)}
satisfy
\prod_{j=1}^{d}\lVert W_{j}\rVert_{q,\infty}\geq\Gamma
,
\prod_{j=1}^{d}\lVert W_{j}\rVert_{q,p}\leq M
. Then for any
r\in[d]
, we have

\displaystyle\min_{j\in\{1,\cdots,r\}}\frac{\lVert W_{j}\rVert_{q,p}}{\lVert W_{j}\rVert_{q,\infty}}\leq\left(\frac{M}{\Gamma}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}.

(7)

Proof.

The proof is similar to the proof of Lemma 6 in
[
Golowich et al., 2018
]
. Note that
\left\lVert W_{j}\right\rVert_{q,p}\geq\left\lVert W_{j}\right\rVert_{q,\infty}\geq 0
for
p\geq 1
. So we have

\displaystyle\frac{M}{\Gamma}\geq\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{d}\left\|W_{j}\right\|_{q,p}}{\prod_{j=1}^{d}\left\|W_{j}\right\|_{q,\infty}}=\prod_{j=1}^{d}\frac{\left\|W_{j}\right\|_{q,p}}{\left\|W_{j}\right\|_{q,\infty}}\geq\prod_{j=1}^{r}\frac{\left\|W_{j}\right\|_{q,p}}{\left\|W_{j}\right\|_{q,\infty}}\geq\left(\min_{j\in\{1,\ldots,r\}}\frac{\left\|W_{j}\right\|_{q,p}}{\left\|W_{j}\right\|_{q,\infty}}\right)^{r},

which completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 4.7
.

For any polynomial network
\mathsf{N}^{(d)}
such that
\prod_{j=1}^{d}\lVert W_{j}\rVert_{q,\infty}\geq\Gamma
and
\prod_{j=1}^{d}\lVert W_{j}\rVert_{q,p}\leq\prod_{j=1}^{d}M(j)=M
, and for
j\in\{2,\cdots,d\}
,
M(j)\leq 2^{k-1}
;
\lVert W_{1}\rVert_{q,p}\leq\frac{1}{2B}
,
\lVert\mathbf{x}_{i}\rVert_{p}\leq B
, there exists an alternative net
\widetilde{\mathsf{N}}^{(d)}
such that they are identical except for weights of the
r
-th layer for
\forall r\in[d]
:
\widetilde{W}_{r}
, where
\widetilde{W}_{r}=\mathbf{a}\mathbf{b}^{\mathrm{T}}
, where
\mathbf{b}
is the
m
-th row of
W_{r}
with the largest
L_{q}
norm, and
\mathbf{a}
is a one-hot vector
\mathbf{a}=\delta_{m}
. And we have

\displaystyle\sup_{\mathbf{x}}\left\lVert\mathsf{N}^{(d)}(\mathbf{x})-\widetilde{\mathsf{N}}^{(d)}(\mathbf{x})\right\rVert_{p}\leq B\prod_{j=1}^{d}\lVert W_{j}\rVert_{q,p}\left(\frac{2p\log(M/\Gamma)}{r}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(8)

Proof.

Combine Lemma
4.5
,
4.4
and
4.6
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\left\lVert\mathsf{N}^{(d)}(\mathbf{x})-\widetilde{\mathsf{N}}^{(d)}(\mathbf{x})\right\rVert_{p}

\displaystyle\leq B\prod_{j=1}^{d}\lVert W_{j}\rVert_{q,p}\cdot\frac{\lVert W_{r}-\widetilde{W}_{r}\rVert_{q,p}}{\lVert W_{r}\rVert_{q,\infty}}

(Lemma
4.5
)

\displaystyle\leq B\prod_{j=1}^{d}\lVert W_{j}\rVert_{q,p}\cdot\left(\frac{\lVert W_{r}\rVert_{q,p}^{p}-\lVert\widetilde{W}_{r}\rVert_{q,\infty}^{p}}{\lVert W_{r}\rVert_{q,\infty}^{p}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

(Lemma
4.4
)

\displaystyle\leq B\prod_{j=1}^{d}\lVert W_{j}\rVert_{q,p}\cdot\left(\frac{\lVert W_{r}\rVert_{q,p}^{p}}{\lVert W_{r}\rVert_{q,\infty}^{p}}-1\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq B\prod_{j=1}^{d}\lVert W_{j}\rVert_{q,p}\cdot\left(\left(\frac{M}{\Gamma}\right)^{p/r}-1\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

(Lemma
4.6
)

\displaystyle\leq B\prod_{j=1}^{d}\lVert W_{j}\rVert_{q,p}\cdot\left(\exp\left(\frac{p}{r}\log(M/\Gamma)\right)-1\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{\leq}}B\prod_{j=1}^{d}\lVert W_{j}\rVert_{q,p}\cdot\left(\frac{2p\log(M/\Gamma)}{r}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.\qed

Here
(a)
follows from the conclusion shown in
[
Golowich et al., 2018
]
.

Remark 4.4
.

Theorem
4.7
shows that the original neural network can be approximated with composition of two networks,

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle\mapsto W_{d}\sigma_{d-1}(W_{d-1}\cdots\sigma_{r}(\lVert\mathbf{b}\rVert_{q}\mathbf{a}\left(\frac{\mathbf{b}}{\lVert\mathbf{b}\rVert_{q}}\right)^{\mathrm{T}}\cdots\sigma_{1}(W_{1}\mathbf{x})\cdots)\cdots)

\displaystyle\Downarrow

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle\mapsto\left(\frac{\mathbf{b}}{\lVert\mathbf{b}\rVert_{q}}\right)^{\mathrm{T}}\sigma_{r-1}(W_{r-1}(\cdots\sigma_{1}(W_{1}\mathbf{x})\cdots))

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle\mapsto W_{d}\sigma_{d-1}(W_{d-1}(\cdots\sigma_{r}\left(\lVert\mathbf{b}\rVert_{q}\mathbf{a}x\right)\cdots)),

where
\mathbf{b}
is the
m
-th row of
W_{r}
with the largest
L_{q}
norm, and
\mathbf{a}
is a one-hot vector
\mathbf{a}=\delta_{m}
.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
\mathcal{H}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[-R,R]
,
\mathcal{F}_{L,a}:[-R,R]\to\mathbb{R}
which is
L
-Lipschitz and
f(0)=a
for some fixed
a
, then we have

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{R}}_{m}\left(\mathcal{F}_{L,a}\circ\mathcal{H}\right)\leq cL\left(\frac{R}{\sqrt{m}}+\log^{3/2}(m)\hat{\mathcal{R}}_{m}(\mathcal{H})\right),

(9)

where
c>0
is a universal constant.

Proof.

This is a general result which is independent of any specify activation functions. So the proof is the same as Theorem 4 in
[
Golowich et al., 2018
]
and is omitted here for brevity.
∎

Theorem 4.9
.

Considering the following hypothesis class of networks on
\mathcal{X}=\{\mathbf{x}:\lVert\mathbf{x}\rVert\leq B\}

\mathcal{H}=\left\{\mathsf{N}^{(d)}:\begin{aligned} &\prod_{j=1}^{d}\lVert W_{j}\rVert_{q,\infty}\geq\Gamma\\
&\forall j\in\{1,\ldots,d\},W_{j}\in\mathcal{W}_{j},\lVert W_{j}\rVert_{q,p}\leq M(j)\end{aligned}\right\},

for some
\Gamma\geq 1
,
\left\lVert W_{1}\right\rVert_{q,p}\leq\frac{1}{2B}
and
\left\lVert W_{i}\right\rVert_{q,p}\leq 2^{k-1},i\in\{2,\cdots,d\}
. For any
r\in\{1,\cdots,d\}
, define

\mathcal{H}_{r}=\left\{\mathsf{N}^{(r)}:\begin{aligned} &\mathsf{N}^{(r)}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}\\
&\forall j\in\{1,\cdots,r-1\},W_{j}\in\mathcal{W}_{j};\\
&\forall j\in\{1,\cdots,r\},\lVert W_{j}\rVert_{q,p}\leq M(j)\end{aligned}\right\}.

Define
\ell\circ\mathcal{H}=\{(\ell_{1}(h(\mathbf{x}_{1})),\cdots,\ell_{m}(h(\mathbf{x}_{m}))):h\in\mathcal{H}\}
, where
\ell_{1},\cdots,\ell_{m}
are real-valued loss functions which are
\frac{1}{\gamma}
-Lipschitz and satisfy
\ell_{1}(0)=\ell_{2}(0)=\ldots=\ell_{m}(0)=a
for some
a
such that
\lvert a\rvert\leq\frac{B\prod_{j=1}^{d}M(j)}{\gamma}
. Then the sample complexity
\hat{\mathcal{R}}_{m}(\ell\circ\mathcal{H})
is upper bounded by

\displaystyle\frac{cB\prod_{j=1}^{d}M(j)}{\gamma}\left(\min_{r\in[d]}\left\{\frac{\log^{\frac{3}{2}}(m)\cdot\hat{\mathcal{R}}_{m}(\mathcal{H}_{r})}{B\prod_{j=1}^{r}M(j)}+\left(\frac{\log\left(\prod_{j=1}^{d}M(j)/\Gamma\right)}{r}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}+\frac{1+\sqrt{\log r}}{\sqrt{m}}\right\}\right).

Proof.

Combining Theorem
4.7
and
4.8
and following the same line of argument in the proof of Theorem 5 in
[
Golowich et al., 2018
]
, we can arrive the similar results shown above. Note that the difference here is that we are considering a more general form of norm constraints for network parameters
\left\lVert W_{j}\right\rVert_{q,p}
which depends on the choice of activation function
\sigma(x)=x^{k}\;(k\geq 2)
.
∎

Using the above result and some other tricks, it’s easy to show that polynomial networks can also have depth-independent bound, which holds under such constraints of arbitrary
q,p
-norm of the weight matrices.

Corollary 4.10
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a class of depth-
d
polynomial network, with weight matrices satisfying
\lVert W_{j}\rVert_{q,p}\leq M(j),j\in[d]
, and loss function and
\mathcal{H}
satisfying the conditions of
4.9
, and
\left\lVert\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}\leq B
,
\left\lVert W_{1}\right\rVert_{q,p}\leq\frac{1}{2B}
and
\left\lVert W_{i}\right\rVert_{q,p}\leq 2^{k-1},i\in\{2,\cdots,d\}
. It holds that

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{R}}_{m}(\ell\circ\mathcal{H})\leq\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{B\prod_{j=1}^{d}M(j)}{\gamma}\cdot\min\left\{\frac{\bar{\log}^{\frac{3}{4}}(m)\sqrt{\bar{\log}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{d}M(j)/\Gamma\right)}}{m^{\frac{1}{4}}},\sqrt{\frac{d}{m}}\right\}\right),

(10)

where
\bar{\log}(z):=\max\{1,\log(z)\}
.

Proof.

From Theorem
4.3
we know that
\hat{\mathcal{R}}_{m}(\ell\circ\mathcal{H})\leq\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{B\prod_{j=1}^{d}M(j)}{\gamma}\sqrt{\frac{d}{m}}\right)
. Combine this with Lemma 3 in
[
Golowich et al., 2018
]
we can have the above result.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

If we assume that
\prod_{j=1}^{d}M(j)
is upper bounded by a constant, the first term in the
\min
argument is independent of the depth
d
. Although this may be a very strong assumption in practice
[
Golowich et al., 2018
]
, it leads to a depth-independent upper bound for sample complexity of polynomial neural networks.

5
Conclusion and Future Work

In this paper, we study the sample complexity of one special type of neural networks: polynomial neural networks (PNNs), which uses polynomial functions as activations. We discuss both depth-dependent and depth-independent upper bound for sample complexity of PNNs, following a similar line of analysis in
[
Golowich et al., 2018
]
. There are many other interesting applications of this depth-independent bound shown in
[
Golowich et al., 2018
]
, which can be possible future directions for this paper.

.

Remark 6.1
.

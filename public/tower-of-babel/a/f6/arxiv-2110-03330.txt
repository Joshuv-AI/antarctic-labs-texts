1.
Introduction

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a complete Riemannian manifold. We shall consider the Brownian motion
X_{t}
in
M
and, given
x\in M
, its associated family of probability measures
\mathbb{P}^{x}
in the space of Brownian paths emanating from a point
x\in M
.
Given a smoothly bounded precompact domain
D\subseteq M
, the first exit time from
D
is given by the quantity

\tau_{D}:=\inf\{t\geq 0:X_{t}\notin D\}\,.

Given
x\in D
, the function
E_{D}:D\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
that assigns to the point
x
the expectation of the value of the first exit time
\tau_{D}
with respect
\mathbb{P}^{x}
is the
mean exit time function from x
,
E_{D}(x)
. We have the following characterization, (see
[
Dy
]
), of this function as a solution of the second order PDE, with Dirichlet boundary data:

(1.1)

\begin{split}\Delta^{M}E_{D}+1&=0,\,\text{ in }D,\\
\left.E_{D}\right|_{\partial D}&=0,\end{split}

where
\Delta^{M}
denotes the Laplace-Betrami operator on
(M^{n},g)
.

The mean exit time function is the first in a sequence of functions
\{E=u_{1,D}\;,u_{2,D}\;,....\}
defined in
D\subseteq M
inductively as follows

(1.2)

\begin{split}\Delta^{M}u_{1,D}+1&=0,\,\text{ on }D,\\
u_{1,D}\lvert_{{}_{\partial D}}&=0,\end{split}

and, for
k\geq 2
,

(1.3)

\begin{split}\Delta^{M}u_{k,D}+ku_{k-1,D}&=0,\,\,\text{on }\,\,D,\\
u_{k,D}\lvert_{{}_{\partial D}}&=0.\end{split}

This sequence is the so-called, (see
[
DLD
]
),
Poisson hierarchy for

D
.

The Poisson hierarchy of the domain
D
determines the
L^{p}
-moment spectrum of
D
, which can be defined as the following sequence of integrals, (see e.g.
[
Mc
]
and references therein for a more detailed exposition about these concepts):

\mathcal{A}_{p,k}(D):=\bigg(\int_{D}(u_{k,D}(x))^{p}dV\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,,\,\,k=1,2,...,\infty.

We are going to focus our study in the
L^{1}
-moment spectrum of
D
,
\{\mathcal{A}_{1,k}(D)\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
which we denote as
\{\mathcal{A}_{k}(D)\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
and, in particular, in its first value,
\mathcal{A}_{1}(D)
, called the
torsional rigidity of
D
which is defined as the integral

(1.4)

\mathcal{A}_{1}(D)=\int_{D}E_{D}(x)\,d\sigma,

where
E_{D}
is the smooth solution of the Dirichlet-Poisson equation (
1.1
).

The name “torsional rigidity” comes from the fact that, when
D\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a plane domain, the quantity

\mathcal{A}_{1}(D)
represents the torque required when twisting an elastic beam of uniform cross section
D
, (see
[
PS
]
). A natural question consists in to opimize this quantity among all the domains having the same given area/volume in a fixed space or under some other geometrical setting. This problem is known as a
Saint-Venant type problem
.

The study of this variational problem in the general context of Riemannian manifolds involves the stablishment of bounds on the torsional rigidity of a given domain
D\subseteq M
, together the determination of the domains and the spaces that shelter them where these bounds are attained, in an analogous way that the study of the Rayleigh conjecture for the fundamental tone, and the techniques involved in this analysis encompasses the use of the notion of Schwarz symmetrization as well as the isoperimetric inequalities satisfied by the domains in question.

1.2.
Example

We remark that, under the bounds on the sectional curvatures of the manifold as hypothesis and if we have the equality with the corresponding bound in the model space of any of our invariants defined on the geodesic ball
B^{M}_{R}
, (namely, the Poisson hierarchy, the averaged
L^{1}
-moment spectrum, or the torsional rigidity), then
B^{M}_{R}
is isometric to the geodesic balls in the model space,
B^{w}_{R}\subseteq M^{n}_{w}
. However, the equality of the mean curvature of distance spheres in the Riemannian manifold
M
, with its radial bound given by the mean curvature of distance spheres in the model space
M_{\omega}
does not imply the isometry among the geodesic balls, as in the previous case.

This observation is coherent with the fact that bounds on the sectional curvatures of the manifold implies bounds for the mean curvature of its geodesic spheres, namely, if (M,g) is a Riemannian manifold with radial sectional curvatures

K_{sec,g}(\frac{\partial}{\partial r},\,)\leq\,(\geq)\,K_{sec,g_{w}}(\frac{\partial}{\partial r},\,)=-\frac{w^{\prime\prime}(r)}{w(r)}

then we have that

H_{S^{M}_{r}}\geq\,(\leq)\,H_{S^{w}_{r}}=\frac{w^{\prime}(r)}{w(r)}\,.

These implications follows from the observation that the mean curvature of geodesics spheres is the Laplacian of the distance from its center in the manifold, (see Proposition
2.3
), together the Hessian comparison analysis of the distance function as it can be found in
[
GreW
]
,
[
MM
]
or
[
Pa3
]
.

However, in the paper
[
BM
]
, the authors exhibit in Example 3.1 in Section 3, smooth complete and rotationally symmetric metrics
g
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with radial sectional curvatures bounded from below,
K_{sec,g}(\frac{\partial}{\partial r},\,)\geq b
outside a compact set and such that the distance spheres
S^{(\mathbb{R}^{n},g)}_{t}
have mean curvature
H_{S^{(\mathbb{R}^{n},g)}_{t}}\geq H_{S^{w_{b}}_{t}}
.

In the following, we are going to present a new example which shows that bounds on the mean curvature of geodesic spheres of the manifold does not imply that the sectional curvatures of the manifold are controlled.

Let
(\mathbb{R}^{2},g)
be a Riemannian manifold such that its metric tensor expressed in polar coordinates is given by
g=dr^{2}+\omega^{2}(r,\theta)d\theta^{2}
, where
\omega:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
is a positive smooth function given by

(1.15)

\omega(r,\theta)=r\left(1+\dfrac{r^{2}}{1+r^{2}\cos^{2}\theta}\right).

On the other hand, we consider as a model space the simply connected space form
(\mathbb{R}^{2},g_{{\rm can}})
of constant sectional curvature
b=0
.

We are going to see that the mean curvatures of the geodesic spheres
{\rm S}^{(\mathbb{R}^{2},g)}_{t}(\vec{0})
of
(\mathbb{R}^{2},g)
centered at
\vec{0}
with radius
t
, are bounded from below by the mean curvatures of the geodesic spheres
{\rm S}_{t}^{\omega_{0}}(\vec{0})
of
(\mathbb{R}^{2},g_{\rm can})
centered at
\vec{0}
with the same radius, namely, that

{\rm H}_{{\rm S}^{(\mathbb{R}^{2},g)}_{t}}\geq{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{t}^{\omega_{0}}}

As
{\rm H}_{{\rm S}^{(\mathbb{R}^{2},g)}_{t}}(t,\theta)=\dfrac{\frac{\partial\omega}{\partial t}(t,\theta)}{\omega(t,\theta)}
and we have

\begin{split}\dfrac{\partial\omega}{\partial t}(t,\theta)&=1+\dfrac{t^{2}}{1+t^{2}\cos^{2}\theta}+\dfrac{2t^{2}}{(1+t^{2}\cos^{2}\theta)^{2}}\end{split}

we obtain

{\rm H}_{{\rm S}^{(\mathbb{R}^{2},g)}_{t}}(t,\theta)=\dfrac{\frac{\partial\omega}{\partial t}(t,\theta)}{\omega(t,\theta)}=\dfrac{1}{t}+\dfrac{2t}{(1+\frac{t^{2}}{1+t^{2}\cos^{2}\theta})(1+t^{2}\cos^{2}\theta)^{2}}

But

\dfrac{2t}{(1+\frac{t^{2}}{1+t^{2}\cos^{2}\theta})(1+t^{2}\cos^{2}\theta)^{2}}\geq 0\,\,\text{for all}\,\,(t,\theta)\in(0,+\infty)\times[0,2\pi)

Hence we have that

{\rm H}_{{\rm S}^{(\mathbb{R}^{2},g)}_{t}}(t,\theta)\geq\dfrac{1}{t}={\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{t}^{\omega_{b}}}(t)\,\,\text{for all}\,\,(t,\theta)\in(0,+\infty)\times[0,2\pi)

Now, let us consider the unique
2
-plane tangent to a point
(t,\theta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
generated by the coordinate vector fields
\left\{\frac{\partial}{\partial r},\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\right\}
. We are going to compute the sectional curvature of
(\mathbb{R}^{2},g)
at this point and we will see that it is not bounded by the corresponding sectional curvature of
(\mathbb{R}^{2},g_{\rm can})
, i.e., we will show that
K_{sec,g}(t,\theta)
is not bounded from below by
0
.

As
K_{sec,g}(t,\theta)=-\dfrac{\frac{\partial^{2}\omega}{\partial t^{2}}(t,\theta)}{\omega(t,\theta)}
then, it is straightforward to check that

K_{sec,g}(t,\theta)=-\dfrac{\frac{\partial^{2}\omega}{\partial t^{2}}(t,\theta)}{\omega(t,\theta)}=\dfrac{2(t^{2}\cos^{2}\theta-3)}{(1+t^{2}\cos^{2}\theta)^{2}(1+t^{2}+t^{2}\cos^{2}\theta)}

Thus, for
\theta=0
, we have that

K_{sec,g}(t,0)=\dfrac{2(t^{2}-3)}{(1+t^{2})^{2}(1+2t^{2})}

This shows that there are points
(t,\theta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
where the sectional curvature of
(\mathbb{R}^{2},g)
is bounded either below or above by
0
which is the sectional curvature of
(\mathbb{R}^{2},g_{{\rm can}})
.

Definition 2.1

2.6
), it is straightforward to check that
E_{R}(r)=\int_{r}^{R}q_{\omega}(t)\,dt
satisfies the
equation

\Delta^{M^{n}_{w}}E_{R}=-1

with boundary condition
E_{R}(R)=0
.
∎

2.3.
Balance conditions

We present now a purely intrinsic condition on the general model spaces
M_{\omega}^{n}
, (see
[
MP4
]
), which will play a key role in the last section of the paper:

Definition 2.8
.

A given
\omega
-model space
M_{\omega}^{n}
is
balanced from above
if we have the inequality

(2.8)

q_{\omega}(r)\eta_{\omega}(r)\leq\dfrac{1}{n-1},\quad\text{for all }r\geq 0.

In
[
MP4
]
it was proved the following characterization of the balance condition defined previously:

Proposition 2.9
.

Let us consider the
\omega
-model space
M_{\omega}^{n}
. Then
M_{\omega}^{n}
is
balanced from above
if and only if the following equivalent conditions hold:

(2.9)

\displaystyle\dfrac{d}{dr}\left(q_{\omega}(r)\right)

\displaystyle\geq 0,

(2.10)

\displaystyle\omega^{n}(r)

\displaystyle\geq(n-1)\,\omega^{\prime}(r)\int_{0}^{r}\omega^{n-1}(t)\,dt.

Also in
[
MP4
]
it were listed several examples of balanced from above
\omega
-model spaces
M_{\omega}^{n}
. Here we enumerate some of them:

Examples 2.10
.

(1)

Every
\omega_{b}
-model space
M^{n}_{\omega_{b}}=[0,R[\times_{\omega_{b}}\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{1}
of constant positive sectional curvature
b>0
and
R<\frac{\pi}{2\sqrt{b}}
is balanced from above. In fact, when
b>0
and for
r>0
we have that (
2.10
) is a strict inequality, (see Lemma 2.4 in
[
MP3
]
).

(2)

On the other hand, the
\omega_{b}
-model spaces
M^{n}_{\omega_{b}}
of constant non-positive sectional curvature
b\leq 0
are balanced from above too. In fact, when
b<0
, we have that inequality (
2.10
) is equivalent to inequality

\int_{0}^{r}\sinh^{n-1}(\sqrt{-b}t)\leq\frac{\sinh^{n}(\sqrt{-b}r)}{\sqrt{-b}(n-1)\cosh(\sqrt{-b}r)}

which holds for all
r>0
because
\tanh^{2}(\sqrt{-b}r)\leq 1\,\,\forall r>0
. The case
b=0
is trivial.

(3)

Let us consider the
\omega
-model space
M^{n}_{\omega}
, being
\omega(t):=t+t^{3},t\in[0,\infty)
. This model space is balanced from above.

2.4.
Symmetrization into Model Spaces

As in
[
MP4
]
we use the concept of
Schwarz-symmetrization
as considered in e.g.,
[
Ba
]
,
[
Po
]
, or, more recently, in
[
Mc
]
and
[
Ch2
]
. For the sake of completeness, we review and show some facts about this instrumental concept, in the context of Riemannian manifolds.

Definition 2.11
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a complete Riemannian manifold. Suppose that
{\rm D}\subseteq M
is a precompact open connected domain in
M^{n}
. Let
(M_{\omega}^{n},g_{\omega})
be a rotationally symmetric model space, with pole
o_{\omega}\in M_{\omega}^{n}
. Then the
\omega
-model space symmetrization of
{\rm D}
is denoted by
{\rm D}^{*_{\omega}}
and is defined to be the unique
L(D)
-ball in
M_{\omega}^{n}
, centered at
o_{\omega}

D^{*_{\omega}}:=B^{\omega}_{L(D)}(o_{\omega})

satisfying

\operatorname{Vol}(D)=\operatorname{Vol}\left(B^{\omega}_{L(D)}(o_{\omega})\right).

In the particular case that
D
is a geodesic
R
-ball
{\rm B}_{R}^{M}(o)
in
M
centered at
o\in M
, then the radius
L({\rm B}_{R}^{M}(o))
is some increasing function
s(R)=L({\rm B}_{R}^{M}(o))
which depends on the geometry of
M
, so we can write

{\rm B}_{R}^{M}(o)^{*_{\omega}}=B^{\omega}_{s(R)}(o_{\omega})

and this symmetrization
B^{\omega}_{s(R)}(o_{\omega})
satisfies

(2.11)

\operatorname{Vol}({\rm B}_{R}^{M}(o))=\operatorname{Vol}(B^{\omega}_{s(R)}(o_{\omega})).

Remark 2.12
.

When it is clear from the context, we write
{\rm D}^{*}
instead of
{\rm D}^{*_{\omega}}
.

Along the rest of the paper, and if there is not confusion, we shall omit the centers
o\in M
and
o_{\omega}\in M^{n}_{\omega}
when we refer to the balls
{\rm B}_{r}^{M}(o)
and
{\rm B}_{r}^{\omega}(o_{\omega})
and the spheres
{\rm S}_{r}^{M}(o)
and
{\rm S}_{r}^{\omega}(o_{\omega})
.

Given
f:D\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
a smooth non-negative function on
{\rm D}
, we are going to introduce the notion of
\omega
- symmetrization

f^{*_{\omega}}:{\rm D}^{*_{\omega}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
. But first, we will show some useful facts.

Definition 2.13
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a complete Riemannian manifold,
D\subseteq M
a precompact domain in
M
and
f:{\rm D}\subseteq M\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
a smooth non-negative function on
{\rm D}
. For
t\geq 0
we define the sets

{\rm D}(t):=\{x\in{\rm D}\,|\,f(x)\geq t\}\subseteq M

and

\Gamma(t):=\{x\in{\rm D}\,|\,f(x)=t\}.

Remark 2.14
.

(1)

The set
D(t)
is precompact for all
t\geq 0
and moreover,
\partial{\rm D}(t)=\Gamma(t)\subseteq D(t)
.

(2)

Note too that
D(0)=D
and that if
t_{1}\leq t_{2}
then
{\rm D}(t_{2})\subseteq{\rm D}(t_{1})
.

(3)

If
T:=\sup_{x\in D}f(x)
, then
D(t)=\emptyset\,\,\forall t>T
, and hence,
\operatorname{Vol}(D(t))=0\,\,\forall t\geq T
.

(4)

Therefore, we have a family of nested sets
\{D(t)\}_{t\in[0,T]}
that covers
D
.

Now, we define the
symmetrization
of a function:

Definition 2.15
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a complete Riemannian manifold,
D\subseteq M
a precompact domain in
M
and
f:{\rm D}\subseteq M\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
a smooth non-negative function on
{\rm D}
. Let
(M_{\omega}^{n},g_{\omega})
be a rotationally symmetric model space. Then the
\omega
-symmetrization of
f
is the function
f^{*_{\omega}}:{\rm D}^{*_{\omega}}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
defined, for all
x^{*}\in{\rm D}^{*_{\omega}}
, by

f^{*_{\omega}}(x^{*})=\sup\{t\geq 0\,|\,x^{*}\in{\rm D}(t)^{*_{\omega}}\}.

Note that the symmetrization
f^{*_{\omega}}
ranges on
[0,T]
, namely,
f^{*_{\omega}}:D^{*_{\omega}}\rightarrow[0,T]
, where
T:=\sup_{x\in D}f(x)
.

Remark 2.16
.

(1)

When it is clear from the context, we write
f^{*}
instead of
f^{*_{\omega}}
and
D^{*}
instead of
D^{*_{\omega}}
.

(2)

By Sard’s theorem, if
D_{f}\subseteq D
denotes the set of critical points of
f
, the set
S_{f}=f(D_{f})\subseteq[0,T]
of critical values of
f
has null measure, and the set of regular values of
f
,
R_{f}=[0,T]\sim S_{f}
is open and dense in
[0,T]
. In particular, for any
t\in R_{f}
, the set
\Gamma(t)=\{x\in{\rm D}\,|\,f(x)=t\}
is a smooth embedded hypersurface in
D
and
\|\nabla^{M}f\|
does not vanish along
\Gamma(t)
.

With these observations in hand, we have the following

Definition 2.17
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a complete Riemannian manifold and let
(M_{\omega}^{n},g_{\omega})
be a rotationally symmetric model space. Given the precompact domain
D\subseteq M
and
f:D\subseteq M\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
a smooth non-negative function on
D
, let us define the function

\widetilde{r}:[0,T]\rightarrow[0,L(D)]

such that, for all
t\in[0,T]
,
\widetilde{r}(t)
is defined as the radius of the symmetrization

D(t)^{*}={\rm B}_{\widetilde{r}(t)}^{\omega}(o_{\omega})

satisfying

\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm D}(t)\right)=\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm B}_{\widetilde{r}(t)}^{\omega}(o_{\omega})\right).

Remark 2.18
.

Note that, as
D(0)=D
, then
D(0)^{*}=D^{*}
, i.e.,
\widetilde{r}(0)=L(D)
, the radius defined in Definition
2.11
, and
D^{*}={\rm B}_{\widetilde{r}(0)}^{\omega}(o_{\omega})
. On the other hand, as
\operatorname{Vol}(D(t))=0\,\,\forall t\geq T
, then
\widetilde{r}(t)=0\,\,\forall t\geq T
.

Concerning this last definition, we have the following result, which will play an important rôle in the proof of Proposition
4.6
:

Lemma 2.19
.

The function
\widetilde{r}:[0,T]\rightarrow[0,L(D)]
is non-increasing. In particular, for all regular values
t\in R_{f}
, the function
\widetilde{r}|_{R_{f}}:R_{f}\subseteq[0,T]\rightarrow[0,L(D)]
satisfies

\widetilde{r}^{\prime}(t)=-\dfrac{\int_{\partial D(t)}\left\lVert\nabla^{M}f\right\rVert^{-1}\,d\mu_{t}}{\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm S}_{\widetilde{r}(t)}^{\omega}\right)}<0

so it is strictly decreasing in
R_{f}
, and hence, injective (and bijective onto its image).

Remark 2.20
.

Note that when
R_{f}=[0,T]
, then
\widetilde{r}:[0,T]\rightarrow[0,L(D)]
is bijective.

Proof.

When
t_{1}\leq t_{2}
, then
{\rm D}(t_{2})\subseteq{\rm D}(t_{1})
and hence
\operatorname{Vol}({\rm D}(t_{2}))\leq\operatorname{Vol}({\rm D}(t_{1}))
, so
\operatorname{Vol}({\rm B}_{\widetilde{r}(t_{2})}^{\omega}(o_{\omega}))\leq\operatorname{Vol}({\rm B}_{\widetilde{r}(t_{1})}^{\omega}(o_{\omega}))
and hence
\widetilde{r}(t_{2})\leq\widetilde{r}(t_{1})
.

On the other hand, given
t\in R_{f}
, let us denote as:

{\rm V}(t)=\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm D}(t)\right)=\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm B}_{\widetilde{r}(t)}^{\omega}\right).

Then,

{\rm V}^{\prime}(t)=\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm S}_{\widetilde{r}(t)}^{\omega}\right)\widetilde{r}^{\prime}(t)

and as
\partial{\rm D}(t)=\Gamma(t)=\{x\in{\rm D}\,|\,f(x)=t\}
, by the co-area formula (see
[
Ch1
]
,
[
Sa
]
), and as
t\in R_{f}
, we have

\widetilde{r}^{\prime}(t)=-\dfrac{\int_{\partial D(t)}\left\lVert\nabla^{M}f\right\rVert^{-1}\,d\mu_{t}}{\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm S}_{\widetilde{r}(t)}^{\omega}\right)}<0

for all
t\in R_{f}
. Therefore,
\widetilde{r}|_{R_{f}}
is strictly decreasing.
∎

To finish this subsection, we are going to prove in Theorem
2.21
that, given
f:{\rm D}\subseteq M\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
a non-negative function defined on the precompact domain
D
, the symmetrized function
f^{*}:D^{*_{\omega}}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a radial function, and that
f
and
f^{*}
are both equimeasurable.

Theorem 2.21
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a complete Riemannian manifold,
D\subseteq M
a precompact domain in
M
and
f:{\rm D}\subseteq M\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
a non-negative and smooth function on
{\rm D}
. Let
(M_{\omega}^{n},g_{\omega})
a rotationally symmetric model space such that its center
o_{\omega}
is a pole. The symmetrized objects
f^{*}
and
{\rm D}^{*}
satisfy the following properties:

(1)

The function
f^{*}
depends only on the geodesic distance to the center
o_{\omega}
of the ball
{\rm D}^{*}
in
M_{\omega}^{n}
and is non-increasing.

(2)

The functions
f
and
f^{*}
are equimeasurable in the sense that

(2.12)

\operatorname{Vol}_{M}\left(\{x\in{\rm D}\,|\,f(x)\geq t\}\right)=\operatorname{Vol}_{M_{\omega}^{n}}\left(\{x^{*}\in{\rm D}^{*}\,|\,f^{*}(x^{*})\geq t\}\right)

for all
t\geq 0
.

Proof.

We are going to prove first statement. Let us consider
x_{1}^{*},x_{2}^{*}\in{\rm D}^{*}={\rm B}_{\widetilde{r}(0)}^{\omega}(o_{\omega})
such that
r_{o_{\omega}}(x_{1}^{*})=r_{o_{\omega}}(x_{2}^{*})
. Then it is evident that
x_{1}^{*}\in{\rm B}_{\widetilde{r}(t)}^{\omega}(o_{\omega})
if and only if
x_{2}^{*}\in{\rm B}_{\widetilde{r}(t)}^{\omega}(o_{\omega})
for all
t\in[0,T]
. Hence,

f^{*}(x_{1}^{*})=\sup\left\{t\geq 0\,|\,x_{1}^{*}\in{\rm B}_{\widetilde{r}(t)}^{\omega}(o_{\omega})\right\}=\sup\left\{t\geq 0\,|\,x_{2}^{*}\in{\rm B}_{\widetilde{r}(t)}^{\omega}(o_{\omega})\right\}=f^{*}(x_{2}^{*})

which means that
f^{*}
is a radial function. Namely,
f^{*}
depends only on the geodesic distance to the center
o_{\omega}
,
f^{*}(x^{*})=f^{*}(r_{o_{\omega}}(x^{*}))
.

To see that
f^{*}
is non-increasing, let us consider
x_{1}^{*},x_{2}^{*}\in{\rm D}^{*}
such that
r_{o_{\omega}}(x_{1}^{*})\leq r_{o_{\omega}}(x_{2}^{*})
. We are going to see that
t_{1}:=f^{*}(x_{1}^{*})\geq t_{2}:=f^{*}(x_{2}^{*})
.

As

f^{*}(x_{2}^{*})=\sup\left\{t\geq 0\,|\,x_{2}^{*}\in{\rm B}_{\widetilde{r}(t)}^{\omega}(o_{\omega})\right\}=\sup\left\{t\geq 0\,|\,r_{o_{\omega}}(x_{2}^{*})\leq\widetilde{r}(t)\right\}=t_{2}

then, if
t\leq t_{2}
, we have that
x_{2}^{*}\in{\rm B}_{\widetilde{r}(t)}^{\omega}(o_{\omega})
, so
r_{o_{\omega}}(x_{2}^{*})\leq\widetilde{r}(t)\,\,\forall t\leq t_{2}
. In particular,
r_{o_{\omega}}(x_{1}^{*})\leq r_{o_{\omega}}(x_{2}^{*})\leq\widetilde{r}(t_{2})
, so
x_{1}^{*}\in{\rm B}_{\widetilde{r}(t_{2})}^{\omega}(o_{\omega})
and therefore,
t_{1}=f^{*}(x_{1}^{*})=\sup\left\{t\geq 0\,|\,x_{1}^{*}\in{\rm B}_{\widetilde{r}(t)}^{\omega}(o_{\omega})\right\}\geq t_{2}=f^{*}(x_{2}^{*})
.

To prove second statement, note that, for all
t>0
, we have, by Definitions
2.15
and
2.17
,

\begin{split}{\rm D}(t)^{*}&={\rm B}_{\widetilde{r}(t)}^{\omega}(o_{\omega})=\left\{x^{*}\in{\rm D}^{*}\,|\,f^{*}(x^{*})\geq t\right\}.\end{split}

In fact, if
x^{*}\in{\rm B}_{\widetilde{r}(t)}^{\omega}(o_{\omega})
, then
f^{*}(x^{*})=\sup\left\{t\geq 0\,|\,x^{*}\in{\rm B}_{\widetilde{r}(t)}^{\omega}(o_{\omega})\right\}\geq t
and, conversely, if
f^{*}(x^{*})=\sup\left\{t\geq 0\,|\,x^{*}\in{\rm B}_{\widetilde{r}(t)}^{\omega}(o_{\omega})\right\}\geq t
, then
x^{*}\in{\rm B}_{\widetilde{r}(t)}^{\omega}(o_{\omega})
.

Therefore, since
{\rm D}(t)=\left\{x\in{\rm D}\,|\,f(x)\geq t\right\}
, we obtain that

\operatorname{Vol}\left(\left\{x\in{\rm D}\,|\,f(x)\geq t\right\}\right)=\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm D}(t)\right)=\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm D}(t)^{*}\right)=\operatorname{Vol}\left(\left\{x^{*}\in{\rm D}^{*}\,|\,f^{*}(x^{*})\geq t\right\}\right).

∎

3.
Mean Exit Time Comparison

We start this section with the notion of
transplanted mean exit time
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
(M,g)
a complete Riemannian manifold and
(M_{\omega}^{n},g_{\omega})
a model space with center
o_{\omega}
. Given
o\in M
, let us consider a geodesic
R
-ball
B^{M}_{R}(o)
, with
0<R<\,inj(o)
and the geodesic
R
-ball in
M_{\omega}^{n}
, centered at the center
o_{\omega}
,
B^{\omega}_{R}(o_{\omega})
. Let
E_{R}^{M}
and
E_{R}^{\omega}
be the mean exit time functions defined on
B^{M}_{R}(o)
and
B^{\omega}_{R}(o_{\omega})
, respectively.

Now, we
transplant
the radial mean exit time function of
M_{\omega}^{n}
to
M
by defining the function
\mathbb{E}_{R}^{\omega}:\,{\rm B}_{R}^{M}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
as
\mathbb{E}_{R}^{\omega}(x):=E_{R}^{\omega}\left(r_{o}(x)\right)\,\,\forall x\in{\rm B}_{R}^{M}
where
r_{o}
is the distance function to
o
, the center of the ball
{\rm B}_{R}^{M}(o)
.

The function
\mathbb{E}_{R}^{\omega}
is a radial function called the
transplanted mean exit time
function in
{\rm B}_{R}^{M}
.

We can compare the transplanted mean exit time function
\mathbb{E}_{R}^{\omega}
defined in a geodesic ball
B^{M}_{R}
with the mean exit time function
E^{M}_{R}
corresponding with this ball. Remember that, along the text, we can omit the centers
o\in M
and
o_{\omega}\in M^{n}_{\omega}
when we refer to the balls
{\rm B}_{r}^{M}(o)
and
{\rm B}_{r}^{\omega}(o_{\omega})
and the spheres
{\rm S}_{r}^{M}(o)
and
{\rm S}_{r}^{\omega}(o_{\omega})
.

Our first result in this regard is following:

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a complete Riemannian manifold and let
(M_{\omega}^{n},g_{\omega})
be a rotationally symmetric model space with center
o_{\omega}\in M_{\omega}^{n}
. Let
o\in M
be a point in
M
and let us suppose that
inj(o)\leq inj(o_{\omega})
. Let us consider a geodesic ball
B^{M}_{R}(o)
, with
R<inj(o)\leq inj(o_{w})
. Then the following assertions are equivalent:

(1)

E^{M}_{R}=\mathbb{E}_{R}^{\omega}\,\,\text{on}\,\,\,B^{M}_{R}(o)
.

(2)

H_{S^{w}_{r}(o_{\omega})}=H_{S^{M}_{r}(o)}\,\,\,\forall r\in]0,R]
.

where
{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{M}(o)}
denotes the mean curvature of the geodesic
r
- sphere
{\rm S}_{r}^{M}(o)\subseteq M
and
{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{\omega}(o_{\omega})}
is the corresponding mean curvature of the geodesic
r
-sphere
{\rm S}_{r}^{\omega}(o_{\omega})\subseteq M_{\omega}^{n}
.

Proof.

Using polar coordinates
(r,\overline{\theta})
in
M-(Cut(o)\cup\{o\})
, equations (
2.1
) and (
2.6
) and applying Maximum Principle, equality
E^{M}_{R}=\mathbb{E}_{R}^{\omega}\,\,\text{on}\,\,\,B^{M}_{R}(o)
is equivalent to equality

\Delta^{M}\mathbb{E}_{R}^{\omega}(r,\overline{\theta})=\Delta^{M}E^{M}_{R}(r,\overline{\theta})=-1=\Delta^{M^{n}_{\omega}}E^{\omega}_{R}\,\,\,\,\forall(r,\overline{\theta})

which, in its turn, applying Proposition
2.3
and equations (
2.4
) and (
2.6
), is equivalent to equality, for all
(r,\overline{\theta})\in[0,R]\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{1}
:

\mathbb{E}_{R}^{\omega}{{}^{\prime\prime}}(r)+(n-1)H_{S^{M}_{r}(o)}\,\mathbb{E}_{R}^{\omega}{{}^{\prime}}(r)=E_{R}^{\omega}{{}^{\prime\prime}}(r)+(n-1)H_{S^{w}_{r}(o_{\omega})}\,E_{R}^{\omega}{{}^{\prime}}(r)

and, as for all
r\in]0,R]
,
\mathbb{E}_{R}^{\omega}{{}^{\prime\prime}}(r)=E_{R}^{\omega}{{}^{\prime\prime}}(r)
and
\mathbb{E}_{R}^{\omega}{{}^{\prime}}(r)=E_{R}^{\omega}{{}^{\prime}}(r)<0\,\forall r\in]0,R]
, this last equality is equivalent to equality

H_{S^{M}_{r}(o)}=H_{S^{w}_{r}(o_{\omega})}\,\,\forall r\in]0,R]

.
∎

Now, we can state the following comparison theorem:

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a complete Riemannian manifold and let
(M_{\omega}^{n},g_{\omega})
be a rotationally symmetric model space with center
o_{\omega}\in M_{\omega}^{n}
. Let
o\in M
be a point in
M
and let us suppose that
inj(o)\leq inj(o_{\omega})
. Let us consider a metric ball
B^{M}_{R}(o)
, with
R<inj(o)\leq inj(o_{w})
. Let us suppose moreover that

(3.1)

{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{\omega}(o_{\omega})}\leq\,(\geq)\,{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{M}(o)}\quad\text{for all}\quad 0<r\leq R

where
{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{M}}
denotes the mean curvature of the metric
r
- sphere
{\rm S}_{r}^{M}(o)\subseteq M
and
{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{\omega}}
is the corresponding mean curvature of the metric
r
-sphere
{\rm S}_{r}^{\omega}(o_{\omega})\subseteq M_{\omega}^{n}
.

Then, we have the inequality

(3.2)

\mathbb{E}_{R}^{\omega}\geq\,(\leq)\,E_{R}^{M}\quad\text{in}\quad{\rm B}_{R}^{M}(o)\,,

where
\mathbb{E}_{R}^{\omega}(x):=E_{R}^{\omega}\left(r_{o}(x)\right)
is the transplanted mean exit time function in
{\rm B}_{R}^{M}(o)
.

Moreover, if there exists
p\in B^{M}_{R}(o)
such that
\mathbb{E}_{R}^{\omega}(p)=E^{M}_{R}(p)
, then

\mathbb{E}_{R}^{\omega}=E_{R}^{M}\,\quad\text{in}\,\,\,{\rm B}_{R}^{M}(o)

and hence,

H_{S^{w}_{r}(o_{\omega})}=H_{S^{M}_{r}(o)}\,\,\,\forall r\in]0,R].

Proof.

To prove first assertion, let us consider polar coordinates
(r,\overline{\theta})\in[0,inj(o))\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{1}
centered at the center
o\in M
of the geodesic ball
B^{M}_{R}
, with
R<inj(o)
, (as before and along the rest of the paper, we shall omit the center point of the ball
o
if there is not confusion). By definition of
\mathbb{E}_{R}^{\omega}
, and using equation (
2.7
) we have that this radial function satisfies

(3.3)

\mathbb{E}_{R}^{\omega}{{}^{\prime}}(r)=E_{R}^{\omega}{{}^{\prime}}(r)<0,\quad\text{for all }r\in]0,R],

and, since
\Delta^{M_{\omega}^{n}}E_{R}^{\omega}=-1\,\text{on}\,B^{\omega}_{R}
,

\mathbb{E}_{R}^{\omega}{{}^{\prime\prime}}(r)=E_{R}^{\omega}{{}^{\prime\prime}}(r)=-1-(n-1)\,\dfrac{\omega^{\prime}(r)}{\omega(r)}\,E_{R}^{\omega}{{}^{\prime}}(r).

Therefore, using equation (
2.1
) and applying Proposition
2.3
and equations (
2.4
) and (
2.6
), we have, for all
(r,\overline{\theta})\in]0,R]\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{1}
:

(3.4)

\Delta^{M}\mathbb{E}_{R}^{\omega}(r,\overline{\theta})=-1+(n-1)\left({\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{M}}-{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{\omega}}\right)E_{R}^{\omega}{{}^{\prime}}(r)\,.

Then, from equations (
3.4
) and (
3.3
), and assuming inequality
{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{\omega}}\,\leq\,{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{M}}\,\,\text{for all}\,r>0
we obtain that

(3.5)

\Delta^{M}\mathbb{E}_{R}^{\omega}(r,\overline{\theta})\leq-1=\Delta^{M}E_{R}^{M}(r,\overline{\theta}),\quad\text{for all }(r,\overline{\theta})\in]0,R]\,\times\,\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{1}\,.

Thus

\Delta^{M}\left(E_{R}^{M}-\mathbb{E}_{R}^{\omega}\right)(r,\overline{\theta})\geq 0\,\,\text{on}\,\,B^{M}_{R}

and since
\left(E_{R}^{M}-\mathbb{E}_{R}^{\omega}\right)(R)=0
we have, applying the strong maximum principle

\mathbb{E}_{R}^{\omega}\geq E_{R}^{M}\,\,\text{ on}\,\,B^{M}_{R}

as we wanted to prove.
We obtain opposite inequalities with same arguments, assuming that
{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{\omega}}\geq\,{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{M}}\quad\text{for all}\quad r>0
.

We are going to prove second assertion assuming that

{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{\omega}(o_{\omega})}\leq\,{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{M}(o)}\quad\text{for all}\quad 0<r\leq R\,.

For that, let us suppose that there exists
p\in B^{M}_{R}
such that
\mathbb{E}_{R}^{\omega}(p)=E^{M}_{R}(p)
. Therefore, we have that
\Delta^{M}\left(E_{R}^{M}-\mathbb{E}_{R}^{\omega}\right)\geq 0
on
B^{M}_{R}
and that
E^{M}_{R}-\mathbb{E}_{R}^{\omega}\leq 0=(E_{R}^{M}-\mathbb{E}_{R}^{\omega})(p)
on
B^{M}_{R}
. Hence,
E_{R}^{M}-\mathbb{E}_{R}^{\omega}
attains its maximum in
B^{M}_{R}
. Applying the strong maximum principle, the diference function
E_{R}^{M}-\mathbb{E}_{R}^{\omega}=C
is constant on
B^{M}_{R}
and, by continuity, as
E_{R}^{M}-\mathbb{E}_{R}^{\omega}=0
on
\partial B^{M}_{R}=S^{M}_{R}
, then
C=0
.

∎

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a complete Riemannian manifold and let
(M_{\omega}^{n},g_{\omega})
be a rotationally symmetric model space with center
o_{\omega}\in M_{\omega}^{n}
. Let
o\in M
be a point in
M
and let us suppose that
inj(o)\leq inj(o_{\omega})
. Let us consider a metric ball
B^{M}_{R}(o)
, with
R<inj(o)\leq inj(o_{w})
. Let us suppose moreover that

(3.6)

{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{\omega}}\leq\,(\geq)\,{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{M}}\quad\text{for all}\quad 0<r\leq R.

Then we have the isoperimetric inequalities

(3.7)

\dfrac{\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm B}_{r}^{\omega}(o_{\omega})\right)}{\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm S}_{r}^{\omega}(o_{\omega})\right)}\geq\,(\leq)\,\dfrac{\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm B}_{r}^{M}(o)\right)}{\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm S}_{r}^{M}(o)\right)}\quad\text{for all}\quad 0<r\leq R.

Moreover, equality in inequalites (
3.7
) for some radius
r_{0}\in]0,R]
implies that

H_{S^{w}_{r}(o_{\omega})}=H_{S^{M}_{r}(o)}\,\,\,\forall r\in]0,r_{0}].

As a consequence of inequalities (
3.7
), for all
0<r\leq R
, we have

(3.8)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm B}_{r}^{\omega}(o_{\omega})\right)\leq

\displaystyle(\geq)\,\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm B}_{r}^{M}(o)\right),

\displaystyle\operatorname{Vol}(S^{\omega}_{r}(o_{\omega}))\leq

\displaystyle(\geq)\,\operatorname{Vol}(S^{M}_{r}(o)).

Finally, equality

\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm B}_{r_{0}}^{\omega}(o_{\omega})\right)=\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm B}_{r_{0}}^{M}(o)\right)

for some radius
r_{0}\in]0,R]
implies that

H_{S^{w}_{r}(o_{\omega})}=H_{S^{M}_{r}(o)}\,\,\,\forall r\in]0,r_{0}].

Proof.

Let us fix one radius
r\in]0,R]
. The proof follows the lines of the proof of Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 1.2 in
[
Pa2
]
, adapting it to this intrinsic context and using the new hypotheses.

First, let us assume that
{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{\omega}}\leq{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{M}}
, for all
0<r\leq R
. If we fix
r\in]0,R]
, then we have, in particular, that
{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{s}^{\omega}}\leq{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{s}^{M}}
, for all
0<s\leq r
. We can apply Theorem
3.3
to obtain

\Delta^{M}\mathbb{E}_{r}^{\omega}\leq\,(\geq)\,\Delta^{M}E_{r}^{M}=-1\,\,\text{on}\,\,B^{M}_{r}.

Therefore, since
\left\lVert\nabla^{M}r\right\rVert=1
, and using the
Divergence Theorem
, we have

(3.9)

\begin{split}\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm B}_{r}^{M}\right)&\leq\int_{{\rm B}_{r}^{M}}-\Delta^{M}\mathbb{E}_{r}^{\omega}\,d\widetilde{\sigma}=-\int_{{\rm B}_{r}^{M}}\operatorname{div}\left(\nabla^{M}\mathbb{E}_{r}^{\omega}\right)\,d\widetilde{\sigma}\\
&=-\int_{{\rm S}_{r}^{M}}\left<\nabla^{M}\mathbb{E}_{r}^{\omega},\nabla^{M}r\right>\,d\sigma=-\mathbb{E}_{r}^{\omega}{{}^{\prime}}({r})\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm S}_{r}^{M}\right).\end{split}

Thus, we obtain, using Proposition
2.7
,

\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm B}_{r}^{M}\right)\leq-\mathbb{E}_{r}^{\omega}{{}^{\prime}}({r})\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm S}_{r}^{M}\right)=q_{\omega}({r})\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm S}_{r}^{M}\right)=\dfrac{\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm B}_{r}^{\omega}\right)}{\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm S}_{r}^{\omega}\right)}\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm S}_{r}^{M}\right)

and therefore

\dfrac{\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm B}_{r}^{\omega}\right)}{\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm S}_{r}^{\omega}\right)}\geq\dfrac{\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm B}_{r}^{M}\right)}{\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm S}_{r}^{M}\right)}.

We are going to discuss the equality assertion: we are still assuming that
{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{\omega}}\leq\,{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{M}}
, for all
0<r\leq R
. If there exists
r_{0}\in]0,R]
such that we have

\dfrac{\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm B}_{r_{0}}^{\omega}\right)}{\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm S}_{r_{0}}^{\omega}\right)}=\dfrac{\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm B}_{r_{0}}^{M}\right)}{\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm S}_{r_{0}}^{M}\right)}

then all the inequalities in (
3.9
) become equalities with the radius
r_{0}
.

In particular,

\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm B}_{r_{0}}^{M}\right)=\int_{{\rm B}_{r_{0}}^{M}}-\Delta^{M}\mathbb{E}_{r_{0}}^{\omega}\,d\widetilde{\sigma}

and hence, as
1+\Delta^{M}\mathbb{E}_{r_{0}}^{\omega}\leq 0
on
B^{M}_{r_{0}}
, we conclude that
1+\Delta^{M}\mathbb{E}_{r_{0}}^{\omega}=0
on
B^{M}_{r_{0}}
and hence, as
\Delta^{M}\mathbb{E}_{r_{0}}^{\omega}=\Delta^{M}E^{M}_{r_{0}}
on
B^{M}_{r_{0}}
then, applying the maximum principle,
\mathbb{E}_{r_{0}}^{\omega}=E^{M}_{r_{0}}
on
B^{M}_{r_{0}}
and hence, by Proposition
3.2
,
{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{\omega}}={\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{M}}\,\,\forall r\in]0,r_{0}]
.

When we assume that
{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{\omega}}\geq{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{M}}\,\,\forall r\in]0,R]
, we argue as before, inverting all the inequalities to conclude the opposite isoperimetric inequality. The equality discussion is the same, mutatis mutandi.

To prove statement (
3.8
), and as in Corollary 1.2 in
[
Pa2
]
, let us define, given
0<R<inj(o)
the function

G:[0,R]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

as

G(s):=\begin{cases}\ln\left(\dfrac{\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm B}_{s}^{M}\right)}{\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm B}_{s}^{\omega}\right)}\right),\quad\text{if }s>0,\\
0,\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\;\;\,\text{if }s=0.\end{cases}

Then, if
{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{s}^{\omega}}\leq{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{s}^{M}}\,\,\forall s\in]0,R]
, we have, applying inequality (
3.7
), that

G^{\prime}(s)=\dfrac{\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm S}_{s}^{M}\right)}{\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm B}_{s}^{M}\right)}-\dfrac{\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm S}_{s}^{\omega}\right)}{\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm B}_{s}^{\omega}\right)}\geq\,0\,\,\forall s\in]0,R].

Hence,
G
is non-decreasing in
]0,R]
. The rest of the proof follows as in
[
Pa2
]
, using in this case the asymptotic expansion around
s=0
for the volume of a geodesic
s
-ball, (see Theorem 9.12 in
[
Gray1
]
) to conclude with a straightforward computation, that
\lim_{s\to 0}G(s)=0=G(0)
, and hence, that
G(s)
is continuous and
G(s)\geq G(0)\,\,\forall s\in[0,R]
, so, given
s=r\in]0,R]
, we have

\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm B}_{r}^{\omega}\right)\leq\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm B}_{r}^{M}\right)\,\,\forall r\in]0,R].

Moreover, isoperimetric inequality (
3.7
), together above inequality implies that

\operatorname{Vol}(S^{\omega}_{r})\leq\operatorname{Vol}(S^{M}_{r})\,\,\,\forall r\leq R.

We are going to discuss the equality assertion: let us assume that
{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{\omega}}\leq{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{M}}\,\,\forall r\in]0,R]
and that there exists
r_{0}\in]0,R]
such that
\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm B}_{r_{0}}^{\omega}\right)=\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm B}_{r_{0}}^{M}\right)
. Then,
G(0)=G(r_{0})=0
and, as
G
in non-decreasing, for all
r\in[0,r_{0}]
, we have

0=G(0)\leq G(r)\leq G(r_{0})=0

so
G(r)=0\,\,\forall r\in[0,r_{0}]
and therefore,
G^{\prime}(r)=0\,\,\forall r\in[0,r_{0}]
which implies that
{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{\omega}}={\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{M}}\,\,\forall r\in]0,r_{0}]
.

When we assume that
{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{\omega}}\geq{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{M}}\,\,\forall r\in]0,R]
, we argue as before, inverting all the inequalities to conclude that
G
is non-increasing in
]0,R]
and hence

\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm B}_{r}^{\omega}\right)\,\geq\,\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm B}_{r}^{M}\right)\,\,\forall r\in]0,R]

and
\operatorname{Vol}(S^{\omega}_{r})\geq\operatorname{Vol}(S^{M}_{r})\,\,\,\forall r\leq R
.

The equality discussion is the same than above, mutatis mutandi.

∎

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a complete Riemannian manifold and let
(M_{\omega}^{n},g_{\omega})
be a rotationally symmetric model space with center

. Let
o\in M
be a point in

and let us suppose that

(3.10)

{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{\omega}}\leq\,(\geq)\,{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{M}}\quad\text{for all}\quad 0<r\leq R.

Then, if there exists
p\in B^{M}_{R}(o)
such that equality
\mathbb{E}^{\omega}_{R}(p)=E^{M}_{R}(p)
holds, we have, for all

The equalities
\mathbb{E}^{\omega}_{r}=E^{M}_{r}
on

(2)

The isoperimetric equalities

\dfrac{\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm B}_{r}^{\omega}(o_{\omega})\right)}{\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm S}_{r}^{\omega}(o_{\omega})\right)\,}=\,\dfrac{\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm B}_{r}^{M}(o)\right)}{\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm S}_{r}^{M}(o)\right)}.

\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm B}_{r}^{\omega}\right)\,=\,\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm B}_{r}^{M}\right)

\operatorname{Vol}(S^{\omega}_{r})=\operatorname{Vol}(S^{M}_{r})

Proof.

First of all, equality assertion in Theorem
3.3
states that, as we are assuming one of the inequalities in (

), then if there exists
p\in B^{M}_{R}(o)
such that equality
\mathbb{E}^{\omega}_{R}(p)=E^{M}_{R}(p)
holds, we con conclude the equality
\mathbb{E}^{\omega}_{R}=E^{M}_{R}
on

and applying Proposition
3.2
, from this equality, we have equality

on

.

On the other hand, equality

on

\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm B}_{R}^{M}\right)=\int_{{\rm B}_{R}^{M}}-\Delta^{M}\mathbb{E}_{R}^{\omega}\,d\sigma=-\mathbb{E}_{R}^{\omega}{{}^{\prime}}({R})\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm S}_{R}^{M}\right)

and hence, by Proposition
2.7

\dfrac{\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm B}_{R}^{\omega}(o_{\omega})\right)}{\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm S}_{R}^{\omega}(o_{\omega})\right)\,}=\,\dfrac{\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm B}_{R}^{M}(o)\right)}{\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm S}_{R}^{M}(o)\right)}.

\mathbb{E}^{\omega}_{r}=E^{M}_{r}\,\,\text{on}\,\,B^{M}_{r}(o)
applying Proposition

\dfrac{\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm B}_{r}^{\omega}(o_{\omega})\right)}{\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm S}_{r}^{\omega}(o_{\omega})\right)\,}=\,\dfrac{\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm B}_{r}^{M}(o)\right)}{\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm S}_{r}^{M}(o)\right)}

and differentiating with respect the parameter

4.
Moment spectrum comparison

We are going to apply the Mean Exit comparisons obtained in Section 3 to obtain estimates of the moment spectrum, and the torsional rigidity of a geodesic ball in a Riemannian manifold with bounds on the mean curvature of its extrinsic spheres.

4.1.
Estimates for the Poisson hierarchy and the moment spectrum of a geodesic ball

We shall start defining the so called Poisson hierarchy of a domain in a Riemannian manifold, (see
[
DLD
]
).

Definition 4.1
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a complete Riemannian manifold and let
D\in M
be a smooth precompact domain. We define the
Poisson hierarchy for
D
as the sequence
\{u_{k,D}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
of solutions of the following recurrence of boundary value problems

(4.1)

\begin{split}\Delta^{M}u_{k,D}+ku_{k-1,D}&=0,\,\,\text{on }\,\,D,\\
u_{k,D}\lvert_{{}_{\partial D}}&=0,\end{split}

with
u_{0,D}=1\,\,\text{on}\,\,D
.

Let us note that
u_{1,D}=E_{D}^{M}
, i.e. the mean exit time function from
D
.

As we did in Definition
3.1
, we transplant the Poisson hierarchy for the geodesic balls in a model space to the geodesic balls in a Riemanian manifold in the following way:

Definition 4.2
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a complete Riemannian manifold and let
(M_{\omega}^{n},g_{\omega})
be a rotationally symmetric model space with center
o_{\omega}\in M_{\omega}^{n}
. Let
o\in M
be a point in
M
and let us suppose that
inj(o)\leq inj(o_{\omega})
. Let us consider a metric ball
B^{M}_{R}(o)
, with
R<inj(o)\leq inj(o_{w})
.

Let us consider the Poisson hierarchy for
B^{\omega}_{R}(o_{\omega})
, namely, the sequence
\{u_{k,R}^{\omega}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
which, for
k\geq 1
, are the solutions of

\begin{split}\Delta^{M_{\omega}^{n}}u_{k,R}^{\omega}+ku_{k-1,R}^{\omega}&=0,\,\,\text{on }{\rm B}_{R}^{\omega},\\
u_{k,R}^{\omega}\lvert_{{}_{{\rm S}_{R}^{\omega}}}&=0,\end{split}

with
u^{\omega}_{0,R}=1\,\,\text{on}\,\,{\rm B}_{R}^{\omega}
.

It is known, for all
k\geq 1
that
u_{k,R}^{\omega}(x)=u_{k,R}^{\omega}\left(r_{o_{\omega}}(x)\right)
, i.e.
u_{k,R}^{\omega}
is radial, and that
u_{k,R}^{\omega}{{}^{\prime}}\leq 0
(see Proposition 3.1 of
[
HuMP2
]
).

Thus, for all
k\geq 1
, we can transplant these functions to
{\rm B}_{R}^{M}(o)\subseteq M
by defining

\bar{u}_{k,R}^{\omega}:{\rm B}_{R}^{M}(o)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

as
\bar{u}_{k,R}^{\omega}(x):=u_{k,R}^{\omega}\left(r_{o}(x)\right)\,\,\forall x\in{\rm B}_{R}^{M}(o)
, where
r_{o}
is the distance function to the center of
{\rm B}_{R}^{M}(o)
.

The sequence
\{\bar{u}_{k,R}^{\omega}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
is the
transplanted Poisson hierarchy for
B^{M}_{R}(o)
.

Associated to the Poisson hierarchy of a domain
D\subseteq M
it is defined the exit time moment spectrum of this domain in the following way:

Definition 4.3
.

Let
D\subseteq M
a smooth precompact domain. We define the
moment spectrum of
D
as the sequence of integrals
\{\mathcal{A}_{k}(D)\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
given by:

\mathcal{A}_{k}(D):=\int_{D}u_{k,D}\,d\sigma\,,

where
\{u_{k,D}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
is the Poisson hierarchy for
D
.

Let us note that
\mathcal{A}_{1}(D)
is the torsional rigidity of
D
.

We have the following comparison for the Poisson hierarchy of a geodesic ball in a Riemannian manifold:

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a complete Riemannian manifold and let
(M_{\omega}^{n},g_{\omega})
be a rotationally symmetric model space with center
o_{\omega}\in M_{\omega}^{n}
. Let
o\in M
be a point in
M
and let us suppose that
inj(o)\leq inj(o_{\omega})
. Let us consider a metric ball
B^{M}_{R}(o)
, with
R<inj(o)\leq inj(o_{w})
. Let us suppose moreover that the mean curvatures of the geodesic spheres in
M
and
M_{\omega}^{n}
satisfies

(4.2)

{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{\omega}}\leq\,(\geq)\,{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{M}}\quad\text{for all}\quad 0<r\leq R.

Then the Poisson hierarchy for
{\rm B}_{R}^{M}(o)\subseteq M
,
\{u_{k,R}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
, and its transplanted Poisson hierarchy for
B^{M}_{R}(o)
,
\{\bar{u}_{k,R}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
(and, fixed any
r\in]0,R]
, the corresponding Poisson hierarchies for
B^{M}_{r}(o)
), satisfies

(1)

\bar{u}_{1,R}^{\omega}\geq\,(\leq)\,u_{1,R}
on
{\rm B}_{R}^{M}
.

(2)

For all
k\geq 2
,
\bar{u}_{k,R}^{\omega}\geq\,(\leq)\,u_{k,R}
on
{\rm B}_{R}^{M}
.

(3)

If there exists
p\in B^{M}_{R}
and
k_{0}\geq 1
such that
\bar{u}_{k_{0},R}^{\omega}(p)=u_{k_{0},R}(p)
, then

{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{\omega}}={\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{M}}\,\forall r\in]0,R]

and

\bar{u}_{k,R}^{\omega}=u_{k,R}\,\text{ in}\,\,\,B^{M}_{R}\,\,\forall\,k\geq 1.

(4)

If there exists
p\in B^{M}_{R}
and
k_{0}\geq 1
such that
\bar{u}_{k_{0},R}^{\omega}(p)=u_{k_{0},R}(p)
, then

\bar{u}_{k,r}^{\omega}=u_{k,r}\,\text{ in}\,\,\,B^{M}_{r}\,\,\forall\,k\geq 1\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\forall r\in[0,R].

and hence,

\mathcal{A}_{k}(B^{\omega}_{r})=\mathcal{A}(B^{M}_{r})\,\,\forall r\in]0,R]\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,k\geq 1\,.

Proof.

Statement (
1
) is proved in Theorem
3.3
.

The proof of statement (
2
) follows using induction on
k
, as it is done in
[
HuMP2
]
. Indeed, assuming that
{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{\omega}}\leq{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{M}}\,\,\forall r\in]0,R]
and as
\bar{u}_{k,R}^{\omega}{{}^{\prime}}(r)\leq 0\,\,\forall r\in]0,R]
, we have that

\bar{u}_{k,R}^{\omega}{{}^{\prime}}(r){\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{\omega}}\geq\bar{u}_{k,R}^{\omega}{{}^{\prime}}(r){\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{M}}\,\,\forall r\in]0,R]

and then, by equations (
2.4
) and (
2.6
) and Proposition
2.3
, we have, for all
k\geq 2
:

(4.3)

\displaystyle\Delta^{M}\bar{u}_{k,R}^{\omega}

\displaystyle=\bar{u}_{k,R}^{\omega}{{}^{\prime\prime}}(r)+(n-1){\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{M}}\,\bar{u}_{k,R}^{\omega}{{}^{\prime}}(r)\leq\bar{u}_{k,R}^{\omega}{{}^{\prime\prime}}(r)+(n-1){\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{\omega}}\bar{u}_{k,R}^{\omega}{{}^{\prime}}(r)

\displaystyle=\Delta^{M_{\omega}^{n}}u_{k,R}^{\omega}=-ku_{k-1}^{\omega}(r)=-k\bar{u}_{k-1}^{\omega}(r)\,\,\forall r\in]0,R].

Now remember that
\bar{u}_{1}^{\omega}\geq u_{1}
on
{\rm B}_{R}^{M}
and let us suppose that

\bar{u}_{k,R}^{\omega}\geq u_{k,R}\,\,\text{on}\,\,{\rm B}_{R}^{M}.

Then, by induction with
k+1
and using equation (
4.3
), we have that

(4.4)

\Delta^{M}\bar{u}_{k+1,R}^{\omega}\leq-(k+1)\bar{u}_{k,R}^{\omega}\leq-(k+1)u_{k,R}=\Delta^{M}u_{k+1,R}\,\,\text{on}\,\,\,{\rm B}_{R}^{M}.

Thus,
\Delta^{M}\left(u_{k+1,R}-\bar{u}_{k+1,R}^{\omega}\right)\geq 0
on
{\rm B}_{R}^{M}
and, applying the Maximum Principle, we obtain that

\bar{u}_{k+1,R}^{\omega}\geq u_{k+1,R}\,.

When we assume that
{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{\omega}}\geq\,{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{M}}\,\,\forall r\in]0,R]
, the argument is exactly the same, inverting all the inequalities. All this proves (
2
).

We are going to prove assertion (3). Let us suppose that, as hypothesis,
{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{\omega}}\leq{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{M}}\,\,\forall r\in]0,R]
, and that there exists
p\in B^{M}_{R}
and
k_{0}\geq 1
such that

\bar{u}_{k_{0},R}^{\omega}(p)=u_{k_{0},R}(p).

We know that, for all
k\geq 1
,
\bar{u}_{k,R}^{\omega}\geq u_{k,R}\,\,\text{on}\,\,{\rm B}_{R}^{M}
. Then, as, on
{\rm B}^{M}_{R}(o)
,
\Delta^{M}\bar{u}_{k,R}^{\omega}\leq-k\bar{u}_{k-1}^{\omega}\leq-k\ u_{k-1}=\Delta^{M}u_{k,R}
for all
k\geq 1
, we have, in particular,

\Delta^{M}\left(u_{k_{0},R}-\bar{u}_{k_{0},R}^{\omega}\right)\geq 0

on
{\rm B}_{R}^{M}(o)
.

Moreover, as
\bar{u}_{k_{0},R}^{\omega}\geq u_{k_{0},R}
on
{\rm B}_{R}^{M}(o)
, then
u_{k_{0},R}-\bar{u}_{k_{0},R}^{\omega}\leq 0
on
{\rm B}_{R}^{M}(o)
and there exists
p\in B^{M}_{R}
such that
(u_{k_{0},R}-\bar{u}_{k_{0},R}^{\omega})(p)=0
. Then, applying the strong maximum principle,
\bar{u}_{k_{0},R}^{\omega}=u_{k_{0},R}
on
{\rm B}_{R}^{M}
, because
u_{k_{0},R}-\bar{u}_{k_{0},R}^{\omega}
is constant on
{\rm B}^{M}_{R}(o)
, continuous in
\overline{{\rm B}^{M}_{R}(o)}
and
u_{k_{0},R}-\bar{u}_{k_{0},R}^{\omega}=0
on
{\rm S}^{M}_{R}(o)
.

On the other hand, as
\bar{u}_{k_{0}-1}^{\omega}\geq u_{k_{0}-1}
on
{\rm B}_{R}^{M}
, we have, on
B^{M}_{R}(o)
:

(4.5)

\begin{split}\Delta^{M}\bar{u}_{k_{0},R}^{\omega}&=\Delta^{M}u_{k_{0},R}=-k_{0}u_{k_{0}-1,R}\geq\\
&-k_{0}\bar{u}_{k_{0}-1,R}^{\omega}=-k_{0}u_{k_{0}-1,R}^{\omega}=\Delta^{M_{\omega}^{n}}u_{k_{0},R}^{\omega}\end{split}

so, for all
r\in]0,R]
:

(4.6)

\bar{u}_{k_{0},R}^{\omega}{{}^{\prime\prime}}(r)+(n-1){\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{M}}\,\bar{u}_{k_{0},R}^{\omega}{{}^{\prime}}(r)\geq u_{k_{0},R}^{\omega}{{}^{\prime\prime}}(r)+(n-1){\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{\omega}}u_{k_{0},R}^{\omega}{{}^{\prime}}(r).

As
\bar{u}_{k_{0},R}^{\omega}{{}^{\prime\prime}}(r)=u_{k_{0},R}^{\omega}{{}^{\prime\prime}}(r)
and
\bar{u}_{k_{0},R}^{\omega}{{}^{\prime}}(r)=u_{k_{0},R}^{\omega}{{}^{\prime}}(r)
for all
r\in]0,R]
, we conclude that

{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{M}}\bar{u}_{k_{0},R}^{\omega}{{}^{\prime}}(r)\geq{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{\omega}}u_{k_{0},R}^{\omega}{{}^{\prime}}(r)\,\,\forall r\in]0,R]

and hence, as
u_{k_{0},R}^{\omega}{{}^{\prime}}(r)<0\,\,\forall r\in]0,R]
, then

{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{\omega}}\geq{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{M}}\,\,\forall r\in]0,R].

As, by hypothesis,
{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{\omega}}\leq{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{M}}\,\,\forall r\in]0,R]
, we have finally that

{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{\omega}}={\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{M}}\,\,\forall r\in]0,R].

Now, to prove that
\bar{u}_{k,R}^{\omega}=u_{k,R}
on
B^{M}_{R}(o)
, we argue as follows: as we know that
{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{\omega}}={\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{M}}\,\,\forall r\in]0,R]
, let us apply Proposition
3.2
, to have that
\bar{u}_{1,R}^{\omega}=u_{1,R}
on
B^{M}_{R}(o)
, and we procceed by induction: let us suppose that
\bar{u}_{k,R}^{\omega}=u_{k,R}
on
B^{M}_{R}(o)
. Let us see that
\bar{u}_{k+1,R}^{\omega}=u_{k+1,R}
on
B^{M}_{R}(o)
. For that, we compute

(4.7)

\begin{split}\Delta^{M}\bar{u}_{k+1,R}^{\omega}&=\bar{u}_{k+1,R}^{\omega}{{}^{\prime\prime}}(r)+{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{M}}\,\bar{u}_{k+1,R}^{\omega}{{}^{\prime}}(r)=\bar{u}_{k+1,R}^{\omega}{{}^{\prime\prime}}(r)+{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{\omega}}\bar{u}_{k+1,R}^{\omega}{{}^{\prime}}(r)\\
&=\Delta^{M_{\omega}^{n}}u_{k+1,R}^{\omega}=-(k+1)u_{k,R}^{\omega}=-(k+1)\bar{u}_{k,R}^{\omega}\\
&=-(k+1)u_{k,R}=\Delta^{M}u_{k+1,R}\,\,\text{on}\,\,B^{M}_{R}(o).\end{split}

Hence
\Delta^{M}(\bar{u}_{k+1,R}^{\omega}-u_{k+1,R})=0
on
B^{M}_{R}(o)
and as
\bar{u}_{k+1,R}^{\omega}-u_{k+1,R}=0
on
S^{M}_{R}(o)
, then, appliyng Maximum Principle again, we conclude that
\bar{u}_{k+1,R}^{\omega}=u_{k+1,R}=0
on
B^{M}_{R}(o)
.

Finally, to prove assertion (4), let us assume that that
{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{\omega}}\leq{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{M}}\,\,\forall r\in]0,R]
, and that there exists
p\in B^{M}_{R}
and
k_{0}\geq 1
such that

\bar{u}_{k_{0},R}^{\omega}(p)=u_{k_{0},R}(p).

As before, we conclude that

{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{\omega}}={\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{M}}\,\,\forall r\in]0,R],

and hence, fixing
r\in]0,R]
, that

{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{s}^{\omega}}={\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{s}^{M}}\,\,\forall s\in]0,r].

Now, to prove that
\bar{u}_{k,r}^{\omega}=u_{k,r}
on
B^{M}_{r}(o)
, we argue as in the proof of (3): as we know that
{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{s}^{\omega}}={\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{s}^{M}}\,\,\forall s\in]0,r]
, let us apply Proposition
3.2
, to have that
\bar{u}_{1,r}^{\omega}=u_{1,r}
on
B^{M}_{r}(o)
, and we procceed by induction, as in the proof of assertion (3).

∎

As a consequence of the Theorem
4.4
we have the following result, where it is proved that, under our hypotheses, any of the averaged moments of the geodesic balls determines its first Dirichlet eigenvalue:

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a complete Riemannian manifold and let
(M_{\omega}^{n},g_{\omega})
be a rotationally symmetric model space with center
o_{\omega}\in M_{\omega}^{n}
. Let
o\in M
be a point in
M
and let us suppose that
inj(o)\leq inj(o_{\omega})
. Let us consider a metric ball
B^{M}_{R}(o)
, with
R<inj(o)\leq inj(o_{w})
. Let us suppose moreover that the mean curvatures of the geodesic spheres in
M
and
M_{\omega}^{n}
satisfies

(4.8)

{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{\omega}}\leq\,(\geq)\,{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{M}}\quad\text{for all}\quad 0<r\leq R.

Then, for all
k\geq 1
,

(4.9)

\dfrac{\mathcal{A}_{k}\left({\rm B}_{R}^{\omega}\right)}{\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm S}_{R}^{\omega}\right)}\geq\,(\leq)\,\dfrac{\mathcal{A}_{k}\left({\rm B}_{R}^{M}\right)}{\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm S}_{R}^{M}\right)}.

Equality in any of inequalities (
4.9
) for some
k\geq 1
implies that

{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{R}^{\omega}(o_{\omega})}=\,{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{R}^{M}(o)}\,\,\,\text{for all }0<r\leq R

and hence, we have the equalities

(1)

Equality
\bar{u}_{k,R}^{\omega}=u_{k,R}
on
B^{M}_{R}(o)
for all
k\geq 1
, and hence, equality
\bar{u}_{k,r}^{\omega}=u_{k,r}
on
B^{M}_{r}(o)
for all
k\geq 1
and for all
0<r\leq R
.

(2)

Equalities
\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm B}_{r}^{\omega}\right)=\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm B}_{r}^{M}\right)
and
\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm S}_{r}^{\omega}\right)=\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm S}_{r}^{M}\right)\,\,\,\text{for all }\,0<r\leq R
.

(3)

Equalities
\mathcal{A}_{k}\left({\rm B}_{r}^{\omega}\right)=\mathcal{A}_{k}\left({\rm B}_{r}^{M}\right)
, for all
k\geq 1
and for all
0<r\leq R
.

(4)

Equalities

\lambda_{1}(B^{\omega}_{r})=\lambda_{1}(B^{M}_{r})
for all
0<r\leq R
.

Namely, one value of
\dfrac{\mathcal{A}_{k}\left({\rm B}_{R}^{M}(o)\right)}{\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm S}_{R}^{M}(o)\right)}
for some
k\geq 1
determines the Poisson hierarchy, the volume, the
L^{1}
-moment spectrum and the first Dirichlet eigenvalue of the ball
B^{M}_{r}(o)
for all
0<r\leq R
.

Proof.

In the model spaces we have that
\Delta^{M_{\omega}}u_{k+1,R}^{\omega}=-(k+1)u_{k}^{\omega}
on the geodesic ball
{\rm B}^{M_{\omega}}_{R}(o_{\omega})
, so, applying Divergence theorem in this setting , we obtain

\mathcal{A}_{k}\left({\rm B}_{R}^{\omega}\right)=\int_{{\rm B}_{R}^{\omega}}u_{k,R}^{\omega}\,d\widetilde{\sigma}=-\dfrac{1}{k+1}\int_{{\rm B}_{R}^{\omega}}\Delta^{M_{\omega}^{n}}u_{k+1,R}^{\omega}\,d\widetilde{\sigma}=-\dfrac{1}{k+1}\,u_{k+1,R}^{\omega}{{}^{\prime}}(R)\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm S}_{R}^{\omega}\right).

Therefore, for all
k\geq 1
,

(4.10)

-\dfrac{1}{k+1}u_{k+1,R}^{\omega}{{}^{\prime}}(R)=\dfrac{\mathcal{A}_{k}\left({\rm B}_{R}^{\omega}\right)}{\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm S}_{R}^{\omega}\right)}.

Assuming now as hypothesis one of the inequalities in (
4.8
), we obtain correspondingly the inequalities

\Delta^{M}\bar{u}_{k+1,R}^{\omega}\leq\,(\geq)\,\Delta^{M}u_{k+1,R}\,\,\text{on}\,\,\,{\rm B}_{R}^{M}.

Then, using the Divergence theorem and that
\bar{u}_{k+1,R}^{\omega}
is radial in
{\rm B}_{R}^{M}
, we have

(4.11)

\begin{split}\mathcal{A}_{k}\left({\rm B}_{R}^{M}\right)&=\int_{{\rm B}_{R}^{M}}u_{k,R}\,d\sigma=-\dfrac{1}{k+1}\int_{{\rm B}_{R}^{M}}\Delta^{M}u_{k+1,R}d\sigma\\
&\leq\,(\geq)\,-\dfrac{1}{k+1}\int_{{\rm B}_{R}^{M}}\Delta^{M}\bar{u}_{k+1,R}^{\omega}\,d\sigma\\
&=-\dfrac{1}{k+1}\int_{{\rm S}_{R}^{M}}\left<\nabla^{M}\bar{u}_{k+1,R}^{\omega},\nabla^{M}r\right>\,d\sigma_{r}\\
&=-\dfrac{1}{k+1}\,\bar{u}_{k+1,R}^{\omega}{{}^{\prime}}(R)\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm S}_{R}^{M}\right).\end{split}

Then, using equation (
4.10
), and that
\bar{u}_{k+1,R}^{\omega}{{}^{\prime}}(R)=u_{k+1,R}^{\omega}{{}^{\prime}}(R)
, we finally obtain that

\mathcal{A}_{k}\left({\rm B}_{R}^{M}\right)\leq\,(\geq)\,\dfrac{\mathcal{A}_{k}\left({\rm B}_{R}^{\omega}\right)}{\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm S}_{R}^{\omega}\right)}\,\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm S}_{R}^{M}\right).

We are going to discuss the equality case: assuming that
{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{\omega}}\leq\,{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{M}},\,\text{for all }0<r\leq R
, equality

(4.12)

\dfrac{\mathcal{A}_{k_{0}}\left({\rm B}_{R}^{\omega}\right)}{\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm S}_{R}^{\omega}\right)}=\,\dfrac{\mathcal{A}_{k_{0}}\left({\rm B}_{R}^{M}\right)}{\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm S}_{R}^{M}\right)}

for some
k_{0}\geq 1
implies that all inequalities in (
4.11
) are equalities for this fixed
k_{0}
, so
\bar{u}_{k_{0}+1,R}^{\omega}=u_{k_{0}+1,R}
on
B^{M}_{R}(o)
.
Applying assertion (3) in Theorem
4.4
, we have that
{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{\omega}}=\,{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{M}}\,\,\,\text{for all }0<r\leq R
and that
\bar{u}_{k,R}^{\omega}=u_{k,R}
on
B^{M}_{R}(o)
for all
k\geq 1
. In particular,
\bar{u}_{1,R}^{\omega}=u_{1,R}
on
B^{M}_{R}(o)
, so, by Corollary
3.5
,

\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm S}_{r}^{\omega}\right)=\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm S}_{r}^{M}\right)
and
\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm B}_{r}^{\omega}\right)=\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm B}_{r}^{M}\right)
for all
r\in]0,R]
and, hence, for all
k\geq 1
,

(4.13)

\begin{split}\mathcal{A}_{k}\left({\rm B}_{R}^{M}\right)&=\int_{{\rm B}_{R}^{M}}u_{k,R}\,d\sigma=-\dfrac{1}{k+1}\int_{{\rm B}_{R}^{M}}\Delta^{M}u_{k+1,R}d\sigma\\
&=\,-\dfrac{1}{k+1}\int_{{\rm B}_{R}^{M}}\Delta^{M}\bar{u}_{k+1,R}^{\omega}\,d\sigma=-\dfrac{1}{k+1}\,\bar{u}_{k+1,R}^{\omega}{{}^{\prime}}(R)\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm S}_{R}^{M}\right)\\
&=\dfrac{\mathcal{A}_{k}\left({\rm B}_{R}^{\omega}\right)}{\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm S}_{R}^{\omega}\right)}\,\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm S}_{R}^{M}\right)=\mathcal{A}_{k}\left({\rm B}_{R}^{\omega}\right).\end{split}

Moreover, applying assertion (4) in Theorem
4.4
, from equality
\bar{u}_{k_{0}+1,R}^{\omega}=u_{k_{0}+1,R}
on
B^{M}_{R}(o)
we can deduce that
\bar{u}_{k,r}^{\omega}=u_{k,r}
on
B^{M}_{r}(o)
for all
k\geq 1
and for all
r\in]0,R]
, so given
r\in]0,R]
, and for all
k\geq 1
,

(4.14)

\begin{split}\mathcal{A}_{k}\left({\rm B}_{r}^{M}\right)&=\int_{{\rm B}_{r}^{M}}u_{k,r}\,d\sigma=-\dfrac{1}{k+1}\int_{{\rm B}_{r}^{M}}\Delta^{M}u_{k+1,r}d\sigma\\
&=\,-\dfrac{1}{k+1}\int_{{\rm B}_{r}^{M}}\Delta^{M}\bar{u}_{k+1,r}^{\omega}\,d\sigma=-\dfrac{1}{k+1}\,\bar{u}_{k+1,r}^{\omega}{{}^{\prime}}(r)\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm S}_{r}^{M}\right)\\
&=\dfrac{\mathcal{A}_{k}\left({\rm B}_{r}^{\omega}\right)}{\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm S}_{r}^{\omega}\right)}\,\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm S}_{r}^{M}\right)=\mathcal{A}_{k}\left({\rm B}_{r}^{\omega}\right).\end{split}

Finally, to prove the last assertion of the Theorem, we know that, assuming that
{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{\omega}}\leq{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{M}}\,\,\text{for all}\,\,0<r\leq R
, the equality

\dfrac{\mathcal{A}_{k_{0}}\left({\rm B}_{R}^{\omega}\right)}{\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm S}_{R}^{\omega}\right)}=\dfrac{\mathcal{A}_{k_{0}}\left({\rm B}_{R}^{M}\right)}{\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm S}_{R}^{M}\right)}

implies equalities
\mathcal{A}_{k}\left({\rm B}_{r}^{\omega}\right)=\mathcal{A}_{k}\left({\rm B}_{r}^{M}\right)
, for all
k\geq 1
, and for all
r\in]0,R]
.
Then, given
B^{M}_{r}\subseteq M
in a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
, (see
[
HuMP3
]
and
[
BGJ
]
):

(4.15)

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(B^{M}_{r})

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to\infty}\dfrac{k\mathcal{A}_{k-1}\left({\rm B}_{r}^{M}\right)}{\mathcal{A}_{k}\left({\rm B}_{r}^{M}\right)}

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to\infty}\dfrac{k\mathcal{A}_{k-1}\left({\rm B}_{r}^{\omega}\right)}{\mathcal{A}_{k}\left({\rm B}_{r}^{\omega}\right)}=\lambda_{1}(B^{\omega}_{r}).

∎

4.2.
An estimate for the torsional rigidity of a geodesic R-ball

We are going to bound the torsional rigidity of a metric ball
{\rm B}_{R}^{M}
in a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
in Theorem
4.7
, assuming that the mean curvature of the geodesic spheres in this Riemnnian manifold is bounded from above or from below by the corresponding mean curvature of the geodesic spheres in a symmetric model space
(M_{\omega}^{n},g_{\omega})
which is balanced from above.

This result can be considered as a continuation of the intrinsic comparison done in Section 6 of the paper
[
HuMP1
]
. In that paper it were obtained upper and lower bounds for the torsional rigidity of a metric ball
{\rm B}_{R}^{M}(o)
in a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
with a pole
o\in M
under more restrictive conditions, namely, assuming that the radial sectional curvatures were bounded above or below by the corresponding radial sectional curvatures of a suitable model space.

To do that, let us consider a symmetric model space rearrangement of the metric ball
{\rm B}_{R}^{M}
as it has been described in Definition
2.11
and Definition
2.15
, namely, a symmetrization of
{\rm B}_{R}^{M}
which is a geodesic
s(R)
-ball in the model space
M_{\omega}^{n}
such that
\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm B}_{R}^{M}(o)\right)=\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm B}_{s(R)}^{\omega}(o_{\omega})\right)
, together the symmetrization
\mathbb{E}_{R}^{\omega}{{}^{*}}\,:\,{\rm B}_{s(R)}^{\omega}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
of the transplanted mean exit time function
\mathbb{E}_{R}^{\omega}\,:\,{\rm B}_{R}^{M}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
. It is evident that Proposition
4.6
, Theorem
4.7
and Corollary
4.8
make sense for those geodesic balls
B^{M}_{R}(o)
which posses a Schwarz symmerization
{\rm B}_{s(R)}^{\omega}(o_{\omega})
.

Then, we have the following comparison. Its proof follows closely the lines of the proof of Propositions 5.2 and 5.4 in
[
HuMP1
]
, we have included it because the changes due to its intrinsic character, the different assumptions on the curvatures we have assumed here and the new analysis of the equality we present in this case.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a complete Riemannian manifold and let
(M_{\omega}^{n},g_{\omega})
be a rotationally symmetric model space with center
o_{\omega}\in M_{\omega}^{n}
, balanced from above. Let
o\in M
be a point in
M
and let us suppose that
inj(o)\leq inj(o_{\omega})
. Let us consider a metric ball
B^{M}_{R}(o)
, with
R<inj(o)\leq inj(o_{w})
. Let us suppose moreover that the mean curvatures of the geodesic spheres in
M
and
M_{\omega}
satisfies

(4.16)

{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{\omega}}\leq\,(\geq)\,{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{M}}\quad\text{for all}\quad 0<r\leq R.

(4.17)

\mathbb{E}_{R}^{\omega}{{}^{*}}{{}^{\prime}}(\widetilde{r})\geq\,(\leq)\,E_{s(R)}^{\omega}{{}^{\prime}}(\widetilde{r})\quad\text{for all }\,\,\widetilde{r}\in(0,s(R))

and the equality

{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{\omega}}=\,{\rm H}_{{\rm S}_{r}^{M}},\;\,\text{for all }0<r\leq R

and hence, we have the equalities among the volumes

\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm S}_{r}^{\omega}\right)=\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm S}_{r}^{M}\right)\,\,\,\text{for all }\,0<r\leq R

Proof.

We are going to analyze first the symmetrization
\mathbb{E}_{R}^{\omega}{{}^{*}}
. The transplanted function

\mathbb{E}_{R}^{\omega}:B_{R}^{M}(o)\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}

satisfies that
\mathbb{E}_{R}^{\omega}\in C^{\infty}(B_{R}^{M}(o)\sim\{o\})\cap C^{0}(\overline{B}_{R}^{M}(o))
, and, moreover, that
\mathbb{E}_{R}^{\omega}|_{S^{M}_{R}(o)}=0
.

Let us consider the radial function
\psi=E_{R}^{\omega}
defined on the interval
[0,R]
in equation (
2.7
) of Proposition
2.7
. Let us denote by
T=\max_{[0,R]}\psi
. Thus, as
\psi
is monotone, (strictly decreasing, with
\psi(0)=T
and
\psi(R)=0
), we have that
\frac{d}{dr}\psi<0
on
]0,R]
and that
\psi:[0,R]\longrightarrow[0,T]
is bijective.

Now, let us define the function
a:[0,T]\longrightarrow[0,R]
as

, satisfying
a(0)=\psi^{-1}(0)=R
and
a(T)=\psi^{-1}(T)=0
. We know that

a^{\prime}(t)=\frac{1}{\psi^{\prime}(a(t))}<0\,\,\forall t\in(0,T)

so
a(t)
is strictly decreasing in
(0,T)
.

Let us denote, for all
x\in B^{M}_{R}(o)
,

\varphi(x)=\mathbb{E}^{\omega}_{R}(x):=E^{\omega}_{R}(r_{o}(x))=\psi(r_{o}(x)).

We have that
\varphi(B^{M}_{R}(o))=\psi([0,R])=[0,T]

\|\nabla^{M}\varphi\|=|\frac{d}{dr}\psi|\|\nabla^{M}r_{o}\|\neq 0
for all
x\in B^{M}_{R}(o)-\{o\}
. Therefore, the set of regular values of
\varphi
is
R_{\varphi}=(0,T)
.

On the other hand, and given
t\in[0,T]
, let us consider the sets

\displaystyle D(t)

\displaystyle=\left\{x\in{\rm B}_{R}^{M}\,|\,\varphi(x)\geq t\right\}=\left\{x\in{\rm B}_{R}^{M}\,|\,\mathbb{E}^{\omega}_{R}(x)\geq t\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{x\in{\rm B}_{R}^{M}\,|\,r_{o}(x)\leq\psi^{-1}(t)\right\}={\rm B}_{a(t)}^{M}

and

\Gamma(t)=\left\{x\in{\rm B}_{R}^{M}\,|\,\varphi(x)=t\right\}=\left\{x\in{\rm B}_{R}^{M}\,|\,\psi\left(r_{o}(x)\right)=t\right\}={\rm S}_{a(t)}^{M}.

We have too that
D(0)=B_{a(0)}^{M}=B_{R}^{M}
and
D(T)=B_{a(T)}^{M}=\{o\}
, where
o
is the center of the geodesic ball
B_{R}^{M}
.

We consider the symmetrization in
M_{\omega}^{n}
of the sets
{\rm D}(t)=B^{M}_{a(t)}\subseteq{\rm B}_{R}^{M}\subseteq M
, namely, the geodesic balls
{\rm D}(t)^{*}={\rm B}_{\widetilde{r}(t)}^{\omega}(o_{\omega})
in
M_{\omega}^{n}
such that

\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm D}(t)\right)=\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm B}_{\widetilde{r}(t)}^{\omega}(o_{\omega})\right)\,.

For each
t\in[0,T]
, let us consider the function
\widetilde{r}(t)
, defined in Definition
2.17
. Then, in this particular context, we have that
\widetilde{r}\,:\,[0,T]\longrightarrow[0,s(R)]
is strictly decreasing and hence, bijective. In fact, note that if
t_{1},t_{2}\in[0,T]
such that
t_{1}<t_{2}
, then, as
a(t)
is strictly decreasing,
a(t_{1})>a(t_{2})
, so

\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm B}_{\widetilde{r}(t_{1})}^{\omega}(o_{\omega})\right)=\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm B}_{a(t_{1})}^{M}\right)>\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm B}_{a(t_{2})}^{M}\right)=\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm B}_{\widetilde{r}(t_{2})}^{\omega}(o_{\omega})\right)

and hence
\widetilde{r}(t_{1})>\widetilde{r}(t_{2})
.

On the other hand, applying Lemma
2.19
, we have that for all
t\in R_{\varphi}=(0,T)
,

(4.19)

\widetilde{r}{{}^{\prime}}(t)=\dfrac{-1}{\operatorname{Vol}\left({\rm S}_{\widetilde{r}(t)}^{\omega}\right)}\int_{\Gamma(t)}\left\lVert\nabla^{M}\varphi\right\rVert^{-1}\,d\sigma_{t}.

The inverse of
\widetilde{r}
is the decreasing function

\phi\,:\,[0,s(R)]\longrightarrow[0,T];\quad\phi:=\phi(\widetilde{r}),

such that
\phi^{\prime}\left(\widetilde{r}(t)\right)=\frac{1}{\widetilde{r}^{\prime}(t)}
for all
t\in[0,T]
,
\phi(0)=T
and
\phi(s(R))=0
.

With all this background, we can say now that, in accordance with Definition
2.15
and Theorem

, the symmetrization of
\varphi=\mathbb{E}^{\omega}_{R}:\,{\rm B}_{R}^{M}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a radial function
\varphi^{*}=\mathbb{E}_{R}^{\omega^{*}}\,:\,{\rm B}_{s(R)}^{\omega}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
which satisfies the following equality

(4.20)

Theorem 4.7
.

(4.39)

{\rm B}_{R}^{M}

-moment spectrum and the first Dirichlet eigenvalue of the ball

3.3

We are going to study the case of equality, when we assume the hypothesis

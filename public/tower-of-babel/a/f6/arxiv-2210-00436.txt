Inductive Freeness of Ziegler’s Canonical Multiderivations for Restrictions of Reflection Arrangements
Torsten Hoge, Gerhard Röhrle, and Sven Wiesner

Abstract
. Let 𝒜 {\mathscr{A}} be a free hyperplane arrangement. In 1989, Ziegler showed that the restriction 𝒜 ′′ {\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime} of 𝒜 {\mathscr{A}} to any hyperplane endowed with the natural multiplicity κ \kappa is then a free multiarrangement. Recently, in [ HR25 ] , an analogue of Ziegler’s theorem for the stronger notion of inductive freeness was proved: if 𝒜 {\mathscr{A}} is inductively free, then so is the free multiarrangement ( 𝒜 ′′ , κ ) ({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa) . In [ HR18 ] , all reflection arrangements which admit inductively free Ziegler restrictions are classified. The aim of this paper is to extend this classification to all arrangements which are induced by reflection arrangements utilizing the aforementioned fundamental result from [ HR25 ] .

52C35 (14N20, 32S22, 51D20)

1.
Introduction

1.1.

The class of free arrangements, respectively free multiarrangements,
plays a fundamental role in the theory of
hyperplane arrangements, respectively
multiarrangements.
In
[
Z89
]
, Ziegler
introduced the notion of multiarrangements and initiated the study of their
freeness.
We begin by recalling Ziegler’s
fundamental construction from
[
Z89
]
.

Let
{\mathbb{K}}
be a field and let
V

be a finite-dimensional
{\mathbb{K}}
-vector space.
A
hyperplane arrangement
or simply an
arrangement
is a pair

({\mathscr{A}},V)
, where
{\mathscr{A}}
is a finite collection of (central) hyperplanes in
V
.
Usually, we just write
{\mathscr{A}}
in place of
({\mathscr{A}},V)
; see Section
2.1
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}
be an arrangement.
Fix
H_{0}\in{\mathscr{A}}
and consider the restriction

{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}
with respect to
H_{0}
.
Define the
canonical multiplicity

\kappa
on
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}
as follows. For
Y\in{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}
set

\kappa(Y):=|{\mathscr{A}}_{Y}|-1,

i.e.,
\kappa(Y)
is the number of hyperplanes in
{\mathscr{A}}\setminus\{H_{0}\}

lying above
Y
.
Ziegler showed that freeness of
{\mathscr{A}}
implies
freeness of the multiarrangement
({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa)
as follows.

Theorem 1.2

(
[
Z89
, Thm. 11]
)
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}
be a free arrangement with exponents

\exp{\mathscr{A}}=\{1,e_{2},\ldots,e_{\ell}\}
.
Let
H_{0}\in{\mathscr{A}}
and consider the restriction

{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}
with respect to
H_{0}
.
Then the multiarrangement
({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa)
is free with
exponents

\exp({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa)=\{e_{2},\ldots,e_{\ell}\}
.

Because of the relevance of Ziegler’s multiplicity
in the theory of free arrangements,
it is natural to investigate stronger
freeness properties for
({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa)
and
specifically to ask for an analogue of
Theorem
1.2
for
inductive freeness. This was settled recently in
[
HR25
]
:

Theorem 1.3

(
[
HR25
, Thm. 1.3]
)
.

If
{\mathscr{A}}
is inductively free, then so is the Ziegler restriction
({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa)
, for every
H_{0}\in{\mathscr{A}}
.

Even if
{\mathscr{A}}
itself is not inductively free, the following result from
[
HR25
]
still guarantees
inductive freeness of
({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa)
, provided the restriction
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}
itself is inductively free.

Theorem 1.4

(
[
HR25
, Thm. 1.6]
)
.

Let
H_{0}\in{\mathscr{A}}
. Suppose that
{\mathscr{A}}\setminus\{H_{0}\}
is free.
If
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}
is inductively free, then so is
({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa)
.

We note that here the freeness assumption on
{\mathscr{A}}\setminus\{H_{0}\}
is needed, cf.
[
HR25
, Ex. 4.2]
.

Because of the compatibility of
the product constructions for
inductive freeness for simple arrangements,

[
HR15
, Prop. 2.10]
,
as well as for multiarrangements,

[
HRS17
, Thm. 1.4]

(cf. Theorem
2.13
),
the question of inductive freeness
of
({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa)
readily reduces to
the case when
{\mathscr{A}}
is irreducible.

1.2.

In this subsection assume that
{\mathbb{K}}={\mathbb{C}}
, the complex numbers, and let
W
be an irreducible complex reflection group with
V
the
{\mathbb{C}}
-vector space affording the reflection representation of
W
. Denote by
{\mathscr{A}}(W)
the

reflection arrangement
of
W
in
V
which consists of all the complex reflecting hyperplanes of
W
.
In
[
HR18
]
, all reflection arrangements
{\mathscr{A}}(W)
were classified
which admit Ziegler restrictions
({\mathscr{A}}(W)^{\prime\prime},\kappa)
that
are inductively free:

Theorem 1.5

(
[
HR18
, Thm. 1.4]
)
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}={\mathscr{A}}(W)
be the reflection arrangement of
the irreducible complex reflection group
W
.
Let
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}
be the restriction of
{\mathscr{A}}
to
a hyperplane in
{\mathscr{A}}
.
Then
({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa)
is inductively free
if and only if one of the following holds:

(i)

{\mathscr{A}}
is inductively free; or

(ii)

{\mathscr{A}}
is non-inductively free of rank at most
4
.

We record a
consequence of Theorem
1.5

along with the classification of all
inductively free restrictions of
reflection arrangements from

[
AHR14
, Thm. 1.2]
,
(cf. Theorem
3.4
).

Corollary 1.6

(
[
HR18
, Cor. 1.5]
)
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}={\mathscr{A}}(W)
be the reflection arrangement of
the complex reflection group
W
. Let
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}
be the restriction of
{\mathscr{A}}
to
a hyperplane in
{\mathscr{A}}
.
Then
({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa)
is inductively free
if and only if
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}
itself is inductively free.

The goal of this paper is to extend the classification of
Theorem
1.5
and Corollary
1.6
to restrictions
{\mathscr{A}}(W)^{X}
of complex reflection arrangements
{\mathscr{A}}(W)
.
Clearly, the question reduces
readily to the case when
W
is irreducible.
Thanks to work by
Orlik–Terao
[
OT93
]
,

[
OT92
, Prop. 6.73, Prop. 6.77, Cor. 6.86, App. D]

and Hoge–Röhrle
[
HR13
]
,
every restriction

{\mathscr{A}}(W)^{X}
of a reflection arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}(W)

is free for
W
an irreducible complex reflection group.
So in particular, the
Ziegler multiplicity of a restriction of
{\mathscr{A}}(W)^{X}
to
a hyperplane is free, by Theorem
1.2
.
Here is the principal result of our paper.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
W
be a finite, irreducible, complex
reflection group with reflection arrangement

{\mathscr{A}}(W)
. Fix
X\in L({\mathscr{A}}(W))\setminus\{V\}
and
let
{\mathscr{A}}={\mathscr{A}}(W)^{X}
be the restriction of

{\mathscr{A}}(W)
to
X
.
Let
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}
be the restriction of
{\mathscr{A}}
to
a hyperplane in
{\mathscr{A}}
.
Then
({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa)
is inductively free
if and only if one of the following holds:

(i)

{\mathscr{A}}(W)
is inductively free; or

(ii)

W=G(r,r,\ell)
and the restriction
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}
of
{\mathscr{A}}

itself is inductively free; or

(iii)

W
is of exceptional type
G_{33}
or
G_{34}
and
\operatorname{rank}{\mathscr{A}}\leq 4
.

Theorem
1.5
shows that the question of inductive freeness of
({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa)
for
{\mathscr{A}}={\mathscr{A}}(W)
a complex reflection arrangement does not depend on the choice of hyperplane. In contrast for restrictions of complex reflection arrangements this question is sensitive to the choice of hyperplane for the restriction,
according to Theorem
1.7
(ii) in view of Theorem
3.4
(i)(b).

The next observation follows from Theorem
1.7
along with
the classification of all
inductively free restrictions of
reflection arrangements from

[
AHR14
, Thm. 1.2]
,
see Theorem
3.4
, which is the analogue of Corollary
1.6
for the class of restrictions of
reflection arrangements.

Corollary 1.8
.

Let
W
be a finite, irreducible, complex
reflection group with reflection arrangement

{\mathscr{A}}(W)
and let
X\in L({\mathscr{A}}(W))
.
Let
{\mathscr{A}}={\mathscr{A}}(W)^{X}
be the restriction of

{\mathscr{A}}(W)
to
X
.
Let
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}
be the restriction of
{\mathscr{A}}
to
a hyperplane in
{\mathscr{A}}
.
Then
({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa)
is inductively free
if and only if
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}
itself is inductively free.

We mention that the equivalences from both Corollaries
1.6
and
1.8

are false in general
(i.e., if
{\mathscr{A}}
is not a reflection arrangement, and not a restriction of such).
For the failure of the forward implication, see

[
HR25
, Ex. 1.13]
and for the failure of
the reverse implication, see

[
HR18
, Ex. 2.24]
.
In view of these elementary counterexamples,
the equivalences of these two corollaries
are rather striking.

1.3.

In this subsection we allow
{\mathbb{K}}
to be an arbitrary field again.
In a separate part, we also study
multiplicities which are
concentrated at a single hyperplane.
These were introduced by
Abe, Terao and Wakefield,

[
ATW08
, §5]
.
It turns out that they are closely related to Ziegler’s
canonical multiplicity,
see Proposition
1.12
.

Definition 1.9
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}
be a simple arrangement.
Fix
H_{0}\in{\mathscr{A}}
and
m_{0}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
and
define the

multiplicity
\delta
concentrated at
H_{0}

by

\delta(H):=\delta_{H_{0},m_{0}}(H):=\begin{cases}m_{0}&\text{ if }H=H_{0},\\
1&\text{ else}.\end{cases}

In general a multiarrangement
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
need not be free
for a free hyperplane arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}
and
an arbitrary multiplicity
\mu
,
e.g. see
[
Z89
, Ex. 14]
.
However, as opposed to the general case, both
{\mathscr{A}}
and
({\mathscr{A}},\delta)
do
inherit freeness from each other.

Theorem 1.10

(
[
HR18
, Thm. 1.7]
)
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}
be an arrangement.
Fix
H_{0}\in{\mathscr{A}}
,
m_{0}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
and let

\delta=\delta_{H_{0},m_{0}}
be
the multiplicity concentrated at
H_{0}
.
Then
{\mathscr{A}}
is free
with exponents

\exp{\mathscr{A}}=\{1,e_{2},\ldots,e_{\ell}\}

if and only if

({\mathscr{A}},\delta)
is free with exponents

\exp({\mathscr{A}},\delta)=\{m_{0},e_{2},\ldots,e_{\ell}\}
.

Our aim here is to strengthen Theorem
1.10
to inductive freeness.

Theorem 1.11
.

Let
H_{0}\in{\mathscr{A}}
,
m_{0}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
and let

\delta=\delta_{H_{0},m_{0}}
be
the multiplicity concentrated at
H_{0}
.
Then
{\mathscr{A}}
is inductively free
if and only if

({\mathscr{A}},\delta)
is inductively free.

The forward implication of Theorem
1.11

is
[
HR25
, Cor. 5.6]
.
We observe that Theorem
1.11
gives a uniform case free proof of

[
HR18
, Thm. 1.9]
.

The following combines

[
ATW08
, Prop. 5.2]
, parts of its proof
and Theorem
1.2
. While not needed for the proof of
Theorem
1.11
, this result does illustrate the connection with the canonical multiplicity.

Proposition 1.12

(
[
HR18
, Prop. 2.14]
)
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}
be a free arrangement with exponents

\exp{\mathscr{A}}=\{1,e_{2},\ldots,e_{\ell}\}
.
Let
H_{0}\in{\mathscr{A}}
,
m_{0}\geq 1
, and

\delta=\delta_{H_{0},m_{0}}
.
Let
({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\delta^{*})
be the restriction with respect to
H_{0}
.
Then we have

(i)

({\mathscr{A}},\delta)
is free with exponents

\exp({\mathscr{A}},\delta)=\{m_{0},e_{2},\ldots,e_{\ell}\}
;

(ii)

({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\delta^{*})=({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa)
is free with exponents

\exp({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\kappa)=\{e_{2},\ldots,e_{\ell}\}
.

2.
Recollections and Preliminaries

2.1.
Freeness of hyperplane arrangements

Let
V={\mathbb{K}}^{\ell}

be an
\ell
-dimensional
{\mathbb{K}}
-vector space.
A
hyperplane arrangement
is a pair

({\mathscr{A}},V)
, where
{\mathscr{A}}
is a finite collection of hyperplanes in
V
.
Usually, we simply write
{\mathscr{A}}
in place of
({\mathscr{A}},V)
.
We write
|{\mathscr{A}}|
for the number of hyperplanes in
{\mathscr{A}}
.
The empty arrangement in
V
is denoted by
\Phi_{\ell}
.

The
lattice

L({\mathscr{A}})
of
{\mathscr{A}}
is the set of subspaces of
V
of
the form
H_{1}\cap\dotsm\cap H_{i}
where
\{H_{1},\ldots,H_{i}\}
is a subset
of
{\mathscr{A}}
.
For
X\in L({\mathscr{A}})
, we have two associated arrangements,
firstly

{\mathscr{A}}_{X}:=\{H\in{\mathscr{A}}\mid X\subseteq H\}\subseteq{\mathscr{A}}
,
the
localization of
{\mathscr{A}}
at
X
,
and secondly,
the
restriction of
{\mathscr{A}}
to
X
,
({\mathscr{A}}^{X},X)
, where

{\mathscr{A}}^{X}:=\{X\cap H\mid H\in{\mathscr{A}}\setminus{\mathscr{A}}_{X}\}
.
Note that
V
belongs to
L({\mathscr{A}})

as the intersection of the empty
collection of hyperplanes and
{\mathscr{A}}^{V}={\mathscr{A}}
.
The lattice
L({\mathscr{A}})
is a partially ordered set by reverse inclusion:

X\leq Y
provided
Y\subseteq X
for
X,Y\in L({\mathscr{A}})
.

Suppose
{\mathscr{A}}\neq\Phi_{\ell}
. Fix a member
H_{0}
in
{\mathscr{A}}
. Set
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}={\mathscr{A}}\setminus\{H_{0}\}
and
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}={\mathscr{A}}^{H_{0}}
. Then
({\mathscr{A}},{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime},{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime})
is frequently referred to as a
triple of arrangements
.

Let
S=S(V^{*})
be the symmetric algebra of the dual space
V^{*}
of
V
.
If
x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell}
is a basis of
V^{*}
, then we identify
S
with
the polynomial ring
{\mathbb{K}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell}]
.
Letting
S_{p}
denote the
{\mathbb{K}}
-subspace of
S

consisting of the homogeneous polynomials of degree
p
(along with
0
),

S
is naturally
{\mathbb{Z}}
-graded:
S=\oplus_{p\in{\mathbb{Z}}}S_{p}
, where

S_{p}=0
in case
p<0
.

Let
{\operatorname{Der}}(S)
be the
S
-module of algebraic
{\mathbb{K}}
-derivations of
S
.
Using the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-grading on
S
,
{\operatorname{Der}}(S)
becomes a graded
S
-module.
For
i=1,\ldots,\ell
,
let
D_{i}:=\partial/\partial x_{i}
be the usual derivation of
S
.
Then
D_{1},\ldots,D_{\ell}
is an
S
-basis of
{\operatorname{Der}}(S)
.
We say that
\theta\in{\operatorname{Der}}(S)
is

homogeneous of polynomial degree p

provided

\theta=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}f_{i}D_{i}
,
where
f_{i}
is either
0
or homogeneous of degree
p

for each
1\leq i\leq\ell
.
In this case we write
\operatorname{pdeg}\theta=p
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}
be an arrangement in
V
.
Then for
H\in{\mathscr{A}}
we fix
\alpha_{H}\in V^{*}
with

H=\ker(\alpha_{H})
.
The
defining polynomial

Q({\mathscr{A}})
of
{\mathscr{A}}
is given by

Q({\mathscr{A}}):=\prod_{H\in{\mathscr{A}}}\alpha_{H}\in S
.

The
module of
{\mathscr{A}}
-derivations
of
{\mathscr{A}}
is
defined by

D({\mathscr{A}}):=\{\theta\in{\operatorname{Der}}(S)\mid\theta(\alpha_{H})\in\alpha_{H}S\text{ for each }H\in{\mathscr{A}}\}.

We say that
{\mathscr{A}}
is
free
if the module of
{\mathscr{A}}
-derivations

D({\mathscr{A}})
is a free
S
-module.

With the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-grading of
{\operatorname{Der}}(S)
,
also
D({\mathscr{A}})

becomes a graded
S
-module,

[
OT92
, Prop. 4.10]
.
If
{\mathscr{A}}
is a free arrangement, then the
S
-module

D({\mathscr{A}})
admits a basis of
\ell
homogeneous derivations,
say
\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{\ell}
,
[
OT92
, Prop. 4.18]
.
While the
\theta_{i}
’s are not unique, their polynomial
degrees
\operatorname{pdeg}\theta_{i}

are unique (up to ordering). This multiset is the set of

exponents
of the free arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}

and is denoted by
\exp{\mathscr{A}}
.

The fundamental
Addition Deletion Theorem

due to Terao
[
T80a
]
plays a
crucial role in the study of free arrangements,

[
OT92
, Thm. 4.51]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Suppose
{\mathscr{A}}\neq\Phi_{\ell}
and
let
({\mathscr{A}},{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime},{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime})
be a triple of arrangements. Then any
two of the following statements imply the third:

(i)

{\mathscr{A}}
is free with
\exp{\mathscr{A}}=\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{\ell-1},b_{\ell}\}
;

(ii)

{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}
is free with
\exp{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}=\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{\ell-1},b_{\ell}-1\}
;

(iii)

{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}
is free with
\exp{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}=\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{\ell-1}\}
.

Theorem
2.1
motivates the following notion.

Definition 2.2

(
[
OT92
, Def. 4.53]
)
.

The class
{\mathcal{IF}}
of
inductively free
arrangements
is the smallest class of arrangements subject to

(i)

\Phi_{\ell}\in{\mathcal{IF}}
for each
\ell\geq 0
;

(ii)

if there exists a hyperplane
H_{0}\in{\mathscr{A}}
such that both

{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}
and
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}
belong to
{\mathcal{IF}}
, and
\exp{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}\subseteq\exp{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}
,
then
{\mathscr{A}}
also belongs to
{\mathcal{IF}}
.

Definition 2.3

(
[
OT92
, p. 253]
)
.

An arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}
is called
hereditarily inductively free
if
{\mathscr{A}}
and every restriction of
{\mathscr{A}}
is inductively free.

2.2.
Freeness of multiarrangements

A
multiarrangement
is a pair

({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
consisting of a hyperplane arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}
and a

multiplicity
function

\mu:{\mathscr{A}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
associating
to each hyperplane
H
in
{\mathscr{A}}
a non-negative integer
\mu(H)
.
The
order
of the multiarrangement
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)

is defined by

|\mu|:=\sum_{H\in{\mathscr{A}}}\mu(H)
.
For a multiarrangement
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
, the underlying
arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}
is sometimes called the associated

simple
arrangement, and so
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
itself is
simple if and only if
\mu(H)=1
for each
H\in{\mathscr{A}}
. We indicate this case with the notation
\mu=\mathbbm{1}
.
In case
\mu\equiv 0
,
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
is the empty arrangement.

Let
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
be a multiarrangement in
V
and let

X\in L({\mathscr{A}})
. The

localization of
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
at
X
is defined to be
({\mathscr{A}}_{X},\mu_{X})
,
where
\mu_{X}=\mu|_{{\mathscr{A}}_{X}}
.

The
defining polynomial

Q({\mathscr{A}},\mu)

of the multiarrangement
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
is given by

Q({\mathscr{A}},\mu):=\prod_{H\in{\mathscr{A}}}\alpha_{H}^{\mu(H)},

a polynomial of degree
|\mu|
in
S
.

Following Ziegler
[
Z89
]
,
we extend the notion of freeness to multiarrangements as follows.
The
module of
{\mathscr{A}}
-derivations
of
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
is
defined by

D({\mathscr{A}},\mu):=\{\theta\in{\operatorname{Der}}(S)\mid\theta(\alpha_{H})\in\alpha_{H}^{\mu(H)}S\text{ for each }H\in{\mathscr{A}}\}.

We say that
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
is
free
if

D({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
is a free
S
-module,

[
Z89
, Def. 6]
.

As in the case of simple arrangements,

D({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
is a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-graded
S
-module and
thus, if
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
is free, there is a
homogeneous basis
\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{\ell}
of
D({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
.
The multiset of the unique polynomial degrees
\operatorname{pdeg}\theta_{i}

forms the set of
exponents
of the free multiarrangement
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)

and is denoted by
\exp({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
.

We recall the construction from
[
ATW08
]
for the
counterpart of Theorem
2.1
in this more general setting.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)\neq\Phi_{\ell}
be a multiarrangement. Fix
H_{0}
in

(2). Secondly, we checked that in each instance in Table
4
when
{\mathscr{A}}
is of rank
5
, none of the
({\mathscr{A}}^{H},\kappa)
is inductively free. Specifically, we proved a slightly stronger statement, namely that none of the multiarrangements
((G_{34},A_{1}^{2}),\kappa)
and
((G_{34},A_{2}),\kappa)
is additively free, cf. Definition
2.14
.
This was accomplished by computer calculations carried out in SAGE
[
St15
]
as follows:
Let
{\mathscr{A}}
be
(G_{34},A_{1}^{2})
or
(G_{34},A_{2})
.
If
({\mathscr{A}},\kappa)
were additively free with a chain of free multiplicities
({\mathscr{A}},\mu_{i})
starting at the empty
4
-arrangement, ending at
({\mathscr{A}},\kappa)
, then according to Theorem
2.9
, for each Euler restriction with respect to
H_{i}
say, in the chain
({\mathscr{A}},\mu_{i})
, we get

\exp({\mathscr{A}}^{H_{i}},\mu_{i}^{*})\subset\exp({\mathscr{A}},\mu_{i})
at every step.
In order to show that
({\mathscr{A}},\kappa)
does not admit such a chain, we start with the free arrangement
({\mathscr{A}},\kappa)
along with its set of exponents and successively eliminate hyperplanes. Starting with the exponents of

({\mathscr{A}},\kappa)
, we assign
“virtual exponents” to a deletion along such a chain recursively as follows.
We determine the number of hyperplanes in the corresponding Euler restriction
({\mathscr{A}}^{H_{i}},\mu_{i}^{*})
for any
H_{i}\in{\mathscr{A}}
. Theorem
2.9
can only be employed if
|\mu_{i}^{*}|
equals the sum of
3
out of
4
“virtual exponents” of
({\mathscr{A}},\mu_{i})
. So if we do have such an equality, we save the corresponding deletion
({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime},\mu_{i}^{\prime})=({\mathscr{A}},\mu_{i-1})

in a list, lower the “virtual exponent” that was not part of the sum by
1
and assign this new set of “virtual exponents” in the next round of our algorithm to
({\mathscr{A}},\mu_{i-1})
. We do stress
the “virtual exponents” that are being determined along the way need not be actual exponents of
({\mathscr{A}},\mu_{i-1})
, as the latter may not be free. Once every hyperplane
H_{i}\in{\mathscr{A}}_{i}
has been tested, we repeat the algorithm with
each of the deletions
({\mathscr{A}},\mu_{i-1})
along with its set of “virtual exponents”
that was incurred in the previous round.

It turns out that,
as a result of this algorithm,
every chain of subarrangements
({\mathscr{A}},\mu_{i})
admitting a correct number of hyperplanes in each restriction such that Theorem
2.9
could in principal be applied does terminate well before reaching the empty arrangement.
Consequently, there is no free chain of subarrangements for
({\mathscr{A}},\kappa)
, so in particular,

({\mathscr{A}},\kappa)
fails to be
additively free, so it is not
inductively free.

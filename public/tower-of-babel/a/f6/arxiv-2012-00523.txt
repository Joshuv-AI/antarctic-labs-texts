Hyperspheres in Euclidean and Minkowski 4-spaces as almost paracontact almost paracomplex Riemannian manifolds
Mancho Manev, Veselina Tavkova

Abstract
. Almost paracontact almost paracomplex Riemannian manifolds of the lowest dimension are studied. Such structures are constructed on hyperspheres in 4-dimensional spaces, Euclidean and pseudo-Euclidean, respectively. The obtained manifolds are studied and characterised in terms of the classification used and their geometric properties.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 53C15, 53C45; Secondary 53D15

1.
Introduction

In
[
12
]
, I. Sato introduced the notion of
almost paracontact Riemannian structure

on a differentiable manifold of arbitrary dimension so that this structure is compatible with a Riemannian metric such that the metric preserves the structure endomorphism on the paracontact distribution.
Later, other geometers (K. Matsumoto, T. Adati, T. Miyazawa, S. Sasaki) joined Sato in the initial development of the differential geometry of almost paracontact Riemannian manifolds (e.g.
[
1
,
11
]
).

Another type of structure-metric compatibility is known in addition to the above.
If the structure endomorphism induces an anti-isometry with respect to the metric on the paracontact distribution of each tangent fibre,
then it is said that the manifold has an
almost paracontact metric structure
(see, e.g.,
[
3
,
13
]
).

The restriction of the almost paracontact structure on the paracontact distribution is an almost product structure.
In
[
10
]
, A.M. Naveira gives a classification of Riemannian almost product
manifolds with respect to the covariant derivative of the
almost product structure regarding the Levi-Civita connection of the Riemannian metric.

Almost paracontact Riemannian manifolds of Sasaki type
(n,n)

are classified in
[
6
]
.
For them, the induced almost product structure
on the paracontact distribution is traceless and it is called an
almost paracomplex structure
(
[
4
]
).
These manifolds are necessarily odd-dimensional and
are called

almost paracontact almost paracomplex Riemannian manifolds
in
[
7
]
.

An object of particular interest in our research is the case of the lowest dimension (which is three) of
almost paracontact almost paracomplex Riemannian manifolds. In this regard,
we study their properties in
[
7
,
8
,
9
]
.

In the present work, we use two different approaches to construct
an almost paracontact almost paracomplex Riemannian manifold
on a hypersphere.
The first case is of a hypersphere in Euclidean space
\mathbb{E}^{4}

and the second is of a time-like hypersphere in pseudo-Euclidean space

\mathbb{E}^{4}_{1}
(i.e. Minkowski space).
Similar research is made for almost contact B-metric hyperspheres in
[
2
,
5
]
.

The purpose of this paper
is to study the basic geometric characteristics of the considered manifolds.
The obtained results will provide
explicit examples of the lowest dimension of the manifolds
under study and will contribute to the understanding of
their geometry.

The paper is organized as follows.
In Sect.
2
, we recall some necessary basic definitions and properties for the studied manifolds.
In Sect.
3
and Sect.
4
, we construct and characterize such manifolds on hyperspheres in
\mathbb{E}^{4}
and

\mathbb{E}^{4}_{1}
, respectively.

2.
Almost paracontact almost paracomplex Riemannian manifolds

Let us consider an
almost paracontact almost paracomplex Riemannian manifold

(\mathcal{M},\phi,\allowbreak{}\xi,\eta,g)
,
i.e.
\mathcal{M}
is a real differentiable manifold of dimension
(2n+1)
equipped with an
almost
paracontact almost paracomplex structure

(\phi,\xi,\eta)
and a Riemannian metric
g
.
Namely,

\phi
is a tensor field of type
(1,1)
(known as a paracontact endomorphism) of the tangent bundle
T\mathcal{M}
of
\mathcal{M}
,
\xi
is a Reeb vector field and
\eta
is its dual 1-form, which together with
g
satisfy the following conditions:

[
12
]
,
[
6
]

\begin{array}[]{c}\phi^{2}=\mathcal{I}-\eta\otimes\xi,\quad\eta(\xi)=1,\quad\eta\circ\phi=0,\quad\phi\xi=0,\quad{\rm tr}\phi=0,\\[4.0pt]
g(\phi x,\phi y)=g(x,y)-\eta(x)\eta(y),\end{array}

where
\mathcal{I}
denotes the identity on
T\mathcal{M}
.

Here and further
x
,
y
,
z
,
w
will stand for arbitrary
elements of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M})
of tangent vector fields on
\mathcal{M}
or vectors in the tangent space
T_{p}\mathcal{M}
at
p\in\mathcal{M}
.

Let us denote the Levi-Civita connection of
g
by
\nabla
.
The fundamental tensor
F
of type (0,3) on
(\mathcal{M},\phi,\xi,\eta,g)
is defined by

F(x,y,z)=g\bigl(\left(\nabla_{x}\phi\right)y,z\bigr).

It has the following basic properties with respect to the structure

\begin{array}[]{l}F(x,y,z)=F(x,z,y)\\[4.0pt]
\phantom{F(x,y,z)}=-F(x,\phi y,\phi z)+\eta(y)F(x,\xi,z)+\eta(z)F(x,y,\xi).\end{array}

The relations of
\nabla\xi
and
\nabla\eta
with
F
are as follows:

(2.1)

(\nabla_{x}\eta)(y)=g\left(\nabla_{x}\xi,y\right)=-F(x,\phi y,\xi).

Let
\left\{\xi;e_{i}\right\}

(i=1,2,\dots,2n)
be a basis of

T_{p}\mathcal{M}
at an arbitrary point
p\in\mathcal{M}
and
g^{ij}
are the components of the inverse
matrix of
g
. Using this basis, the structure
(\phi,\xi,\eta)
and the metric
g
, the following 1-forms (known as Lee forms)
are associated with
F
:

\theta(z)=g^{ij}F(e_{i},e_{j},z),\quad\theta^{*}(z)=g^{ij}F(e_{i},\phi e_{j},z),\quad\omega(z)=F(\xi,\xi,z).

In
[
6
]
, a classification of almost paracontact almost paracomplex
Riemannian manifolds is made with respect to basic properties of
F
with respect to the tensor structure of the studied manifold. This classification consists of 11 basic
classes
\mathcal{F}_{1}
,
\mathcal{F}_{2}
,
\dots
,
\mathcal{F}_{11}
.
Furthermore,
the components
F^{s}

(s\in\{1,2,\dots,11\})
of
F
, which correspond to the classes
\mathcal{F}_{s}
, are determined in
[
7
]
.
The latter approach provides an alternative way to determine the basic classes of the considered classification.
Namely, the manifold
(\mathcal{M},\allowbreak{}\phi,\allowbreak{}\xi,\allowbreak{}\eta,g)
belongs to
\mathcal{F}_{s}

if and only if the equality
F=F^{s}
is valid.
As a corollary we have the following. A manifold of the studied type
belongs to a direct sum of two or more basic classes, i.e.

(\mathcal{M},\allowbreak{}\phi,\allowbreak{}\xi,\allowbreak{}\eta,g)\in\mathcal{F}_{i}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{j}\oplus\cdots
, if and only if the
tensor
F
on
(\mathcal{M},\allowbreak{}\phi,\allowbreak{}\xi,\allowbreak{}\eta,g)
is the sum of the corresponding components

F^{i}
,
F^{j}
,
\ldots
of
F
, i.e. the following condition is
satisfied
F=F^{i}+F^{j}+\cdots
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},\phi,\xi,\eta,g)
have the lowest dimension
(i.e.
\dim{\mathcal{M}}=3
) and let the set of vectors

\{e_{0},e_{1},e_{2}\}
be a
\phi
-basis of
T_{p}\mathcal{M}
which satisfies the following
conditions:

(2.2)

\begin{array}[]{l}\phi e_{0}=0,\quad\phi e_{1}=e_{2},\quad\phi e_{2}=e_{1},\quad\xi=e_{0},\\[4.0pt]
\eta(e_{0})=1,\quad\eta(e_{1})=\eta(e_{2})=0,\end{array}

(2.3)

g(e_{i},e_{j})=\delta_{ij},\qquad i,j\in\{0,1,2\}.

According to
[
7
]
, the components
{F_{ijk}=F(e_{i},e_{j},e_{k})}
,
{\theta_{k}=\theta(e_{k})}
,
{\theta^{*}_{k}=\theta^{*}(e_{k})}
and
{\omega_{k}=\omega(e_{k})}
of
F
,
\theta
,
\theta^{*}
and
\omega
, respectively, with respect to the
\phi
-basis
\left\{e_{0},e_{1},e_{2}\right\}
are determined as follows:

\begin{array}[]{c}\begin{array}[]{ll}\theta_{0}=F_{110}+F_{220},&\theta_{1}=F_{111}=-F_{122}=-\theta^{*}_{2},\\[4.0pt]
\theta^{*}_{0}=F_{120}+F_{210},&\theta_{2}=F_{222}=-F_{211}=-\theta^{*}_{1},\\[4.0pt]
\end{array}\\
\begin{array}[]{lll}\omega_{0}=0,&\omega_{1}=F_{001},&\omega_{2}=F_{002}.\end{array}\end{array}

Hence, the components
F^{s}
,
s\in\{1,2,\dots,11\}
, of
F
on
(\mathcal{M},\phi,\xi,\eta,g)
in the corresponding basic classes
\mathcal{F}_{s}
have the following form:
[
7
]

(2.4)

\begin{array}[]{l}F^{1}(x,y,z)=\left(x^{1}\theta_{1}-x^{2}\theta_{2}\right)\left(y^{1}z^{1}-y^{2}z^{2}\right);\\[4.0pt]
F^{2}(x,y,z)=F^{3}(x,y,z)=0;\\
F^{4}(x,y,z)=\frac{\theta_{0}}{2}\Bigl\{x^{1}\left(y^{0}z^{1}+y^{1}z^{0}\right)+x^{2}\left(y^{0}z^{2}+y^{2}z^{0}\right)\bigr\};\\[4.0pt]
F^{5}(x,y,z)=\frac{\theta^{*}_{0}}{2}\bigl\{x^{1}\left(y^{0}z^{2}+y^{2}z^{0}\right)+x^{2}\left(y^{0}z^{1}+y^{1}z^{0}\right)\bigr\};\\[4.0pt]
F^{6}(x,y,z)=F^{7}(x,y,z)=0;\\[4.0pt]
F^{8}(x,y,z)=\lambda\bigl\{x^{1}\left(y^{0}z^{1}+y^{1}z^{0}\right)-x^{2}\left(y^{0}z^{2}+y^{2}z^{0}\right)\bigr\},\\[4.0pt]
\hskip 38.0pt\lambda=F_{110}=-F_{220};\\[4.0pt]
F^{9}(x,y,z)=\mu\bigl\{x^{1}\left(y^{0}z^{2}+y^{2}z^{0}\right)-x^{2}\left(y^{0}z^{1}+y^{1}z^{0}\right)\bigr\},\\[4.0pt]
\hskip 38.0pt\mu=F_{120}=-F_{210};\\[4.0pt]
F^{10}(x,y,z)=\nu x^{0}\left(y^{1}z^{1}-y^{2}z^{2}\right),\quad\nu=F_{011}=-F_{022};\\[4.0pt]
F_{11}(x,y,z)=x^{0}\bigl\{\omega_{1}\left(y^{0}z^{1}+y^{1}z^{0}\right)+\omega_{2}\left(y^{0}z^{2}+y^{2}z^{0}\right)\bigr\},\end{array}

where the decompositions
x=x^{i}e_{i}
,
y=y^{i}e_{i}
,
z=z^{i}e_{i}

with respect to
\left\{e_{0},e_{1},e_{2}\right\}
are used.

By virtue of (
2.4
), it is determined in
[
7
]
that the studied 3-dimensional manifolds can belong only to
the basic classes

\mathcal{F}_{1}
,
\mathcal{F}_{4}
,
\mathcal{F}_{5}
,
\mathcal{F}_{8}
,
\mathcal{F}_{9}
,
\mathcal{F}_{10}
,
\mathcal{F}_{11}
and their direct sums.

The Nijenhuis tensor
N
of the structure
(\phi,\xi,\eta)
is defined by the equality

N(x,y)=[\phi,\phi](x,y)-\mathrm{d}\eta(x,y)\xi
,
where the Nijenhuis torsion of
\phi
is determined by

[\phi,\phi](x,y)=[\phi x,\phi y]+\phi^{2}[x,y]-\phi[\phi x,y]-\phi[x,\phi y]

and
\mathrm{d}\eta
is the exterior derivative of
\eta
given by

\mathrm{d}\eta(x,y)=(\nabla_{x}\eta)y-(\nabla_{y}\eta)x
.
The corresponding tensor of type (0,3) of the Nijenhuis tensor on
(\mathcal{M},\allowbreak{}\phi,\allowbreak{}\xi,\allowbreak{}\eta,g)
is defined by the
equality
N(x,y,z)=g\left(N(x,y),z\right)
.
According to
[
7
]
, we express
N
in terms of
F
as follows:

(2.5)

\begin{array}[]{ll}N(x,y,z)=F(\phi x,y,z)-F(\phi y,x,z)-F(x,y,\phi z)+F(y,x,\phi z)\\[4.0pt]
\phantom{N(x,y,z)=}+\eta(z)\left\{F(x,\phi y,\xi)-F(y,\phi x,\xi)\right\}.\end{array}

The associated Nijenhuis tensor
\widehat{N}
of the structure

(\phi,\xi,\eta,g)
is defined by the following way

\widehat{N}(x,y)=\{\phi,\phi\}(x,y){-}(\mathcal{\mathfrak{L}}_{\xi}g)(x,y)\xi
.
In the latter equality,
\{\phi,\phi\}
is the symmetric tensor of type
(1,2)
determined by

\{\phi,\phi\}(x,y)=\{\phi x,\phi y\}+\phi^{2}\{x,y\}-\phi\{\phi x,y\}-\phi\{x,\phi y\}

for
\{x,y\}=\nabla_{x}y+\nabla_{y}x
and
\mathfrak{L}_{\xi}g
is the Lie derivative of
g
along
\xi

expressed by
\left(\mathfrak{L}_{\xi}g\right)(x,y)=(\nabla_{x}\eta)y+(\nabla_{y}\eta)x
.
The corresponding tensor of type (0,3) of the associated Nijenhuis tensor is defined by

\widehat{N}(x,y,z)=g\left(\widehat{N}(x,y),z\right)
. In
[
7
]
, we express
\widehat{N}
by
F
as
follows:

(2.6)

\begin{array}[]{ll}\widehat{N}(x,y,z)=F(\phi x,y,z)+F(\phi y,x,z)-F(x,y,\phi z)-F(y,x,\phi z)\\[4.0pt]
\phantom{\widehat{N}(x,y,z)=}+\eta(z)\left\{F(x,\phi y,\xi)+F(y,\phi x,\xi)\right\}.\end{array}

The curvature tensor
R
of type
(1,3)
for
\nabla
is defined as usually by
R=\left[\nabla,\nabla\right]-\nabla_{[\ ,\ ]}
.
The corresponding
(0,4)
-tensor is denoted by the same letter and it is given by
R(x,y,z,w)=g(R(x,y)z,w)
.

The Ricci tensor
\rho
and the scalar curvature
\tau
for
R
as well as
their associated quantities are determined respectively by:

(2.7)

\begin{array}[]{ll}\rho(y,z)=g^{ij}R(e_{i},y,z,e_{j}),&\tau=g^{ij}\rho(e_{i},e_{j}),\\[4.0pt]
\rho^{*}(y,z)=g^{ij}R(e_{i},y,z,\phi e_{j}),&\tau^{*}=g^{ij}\rho^{*}(e_{i},e_{j}).\end{array}

Moreover, we use the Kulkarni-Nomizu
product
g\owedge h
of two
(0,2)
-tensors
g
and
h
defined by

\begin{array}[]{l}\left(g\owedge h\right)(x,y,z,w)=g(x,z)h(y,w)-g(y,z)h(x,w)\\[4.0pt]
\phantom{\left(g\owedge h\right)(x,y,z,w)}+g(y,w)h(x,z)-g(x,w)h(y,z).\end{array}

Obviously,
g\owedge h
has the basic properties of
R

if and only if
g
and
h
are symmetric.

Let
\alpha
be a non-degenerate 2-plane in
T_{p}\mathcal{M}
,
p\in\mathcal{M}
, having a basis
\{x,y\}
.
The sectional curvature
k(\alpha;p)
is determined by

(2.8)

k(\alpha;p)=-\frac{2R(x,y,y,x)}{(g\owedge g)(x,y,y,x)}.

3.
A hypersphere with the studied structure in Euclidean 4-space

Let
\mathbb{E}^{4}
be the Euclidean space
\left(\mathbb{R}^{4},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\right)
, where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is the usual Euclidean inner product determined by

\langle x,y\rangle=x^{1}y^{1}+x^{2}y^{2}+x^{3}y^{3}+x^{4}y^{4}

for
x(x^{1},x^{2},x^{3},x^{4})
,
y(y^{1},y^{2},y^{3},y^{4})
from
\mathbb{R}^{4}
.

Then, we consider a hypersphere
S_{1}
in
\mathbb{E}^{4}
at the origin with a real radius
r
identifying an arbitrary point
p
in
\mathbb{E}^{4}
with its position vector
z
, i.e.

(3.1)

S_{1}:\quad\langle z,z\rangle=r^{2}.

It has the following parametrization

{z}(r\cos u^{1}\cos u^{2},r\cos u^{1}\sin u^{2},r\sin u^{1}\cos u^{0},r\sin u^{1}\sin u^{0}),

where
u^{0},u^{1},u^{2}
are real parameters such as
u^{0},u^{1},u^{2}\in[0;2\pi)
,
u^{1}\neq\frac{k\pi}{2}
for
k\in\{0,1,2,3\}
.
Consequently, the local basic vectors
\partial_{i}=\frac{\partial z}{\partial{u^{i}}}
,
i\in\{0,1,2\}

have the following inner products:

\begin{array}[]{l}\langle\partial_{0},\partial_{0}\rangle=r^{2}\sin^{2}u^{1},\quad\langle\partial_{1},\partial_{1}\rangle=r^{2},\quad\langle\partial_{2},\partial_{2}\rangle=r^{2}\cos^{2}u^{1},\\[4.0pt]
\langle\partial_{i},\partial_{j}\rangle=0,\;i\neq j.\end{array}

Substituting
e_{i}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\langle\partial_{i},\partial_{i}\rangle}}\partial_{i}
,
i\in\{0,1,2\}
, we obtain an orthonormal basis
\{e_{i}\}
,
i\in\{0,1,2\}
as follows

(3.2)

\begin{array}[]{l}{e_{0}}=\frac{\varepsilon_{2}}{r\sin u^{1}}\partial_{0},\qquad{e_{1}}=\frac{1}{r}\partial_{1},\qquad{e_{2}}=\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{r\cos u^{1}}\partial_{2},\end{array}

where
\varepsilon_{1}=\mathrm{sgn}(\cos u^{1})
,
\varepsilon_{2}=\mathrm{sgn}(\sin u^{1})
.

Next, we introduce an almost paracontact almost paracomplex structure
(\phi,\xi,\eta)
on

S_{1}
determined as shown in (
2.2
).
The metric
g
on the hypersurface is the
restriction of
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
on
S_{1}
.
Therefore,
\{e_{i}\}
,

is an orthonormal
\phi
-basis with respect to

on

at

, i.e. (
2.3
) is satisfied.
Thus, we obtain for
(S_{1},\phi,\xi,\eta,g)
the following

Proposition 3.1
.

The manifold
(S_{1},\phi,\xi,\eta,g)
is a 3-dimensional almost paracontact almost paracomplex Riemannian manifold.

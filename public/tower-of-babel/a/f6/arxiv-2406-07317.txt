The lower semi-continuity of the Morse index is a rather general and “robust” inequality. It holds in various frameworks like for instance in the viscosity method for minimal surfaces,

The upper-semi-continuity of the extended Morse index has been established in two remarkable works on minimal surface theory respectively by O. Chodosh and C. Mantoulidis for sequences of critical points to the Allen-Cahn functional (see

Compared to the lower semi-continuity Morse index estimates, upper semi-continuity results are

Let

{E}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Sigma}|du|^{2}_{h}\,dvol_{h}\,,

\frac{d}{dt}{E}\bigg(\frac{u+t\varphi}{|u+t\varphi|}\bigg)\bigg|_{t=0}=0\quad\text{for all}\quad\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma)\,.

The Euler-Lagrange equation associated to such critical points is

-\Delta_{h}u=|du|_{h}^{2}\,u\quad\text{in}\quad\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Sigma),

where

We recall that the negative Laplace Beltrami operator associated to the metric

\Delta_{h}f=\frac{1}{\sqrt{{\rm det}(h_{ij})}}\partial_{x_{i}}(h^{ij}{\rm det}(h_{ij})\partial_{x_{j}}f)

where we use the Einstein summation convention. We also have in local coordinates
|df|_{h}=h^{ij}\partial_{x_{i}}f\partial_{x_{j}}f

The equation is critical in the sense that for solutions

the right hand side

is only in

and one cannot directly apply Calderon-Zygmund theory to get a better regularity of the solutions

To prove the continuity of harmonic maps taking values into the sphere, F. Hélein

identified a Jacobian structure in the Euler-Lagrange equation (

-\Delta u=\nabla^{\perp}B\cdot\nabla u,\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \mbox{in $\mathbb{D}^{2}$}

where for simplicity we consider the unit ball

centered at the origin and
\nabla^{\perp}B=(\nabla^{\perp}B_{ij})
with
\nabla^{\perp}B_{ij}=u_{i}\nabla u_{j}-u_{j}\nabla u_{i},
(for every
vector field

,

\nabla^{\perp}v=(-\partial_{y}v,\partial_{x}v))\,.

The r.h.s of (

\nabla^{\perp}B_{ij}\nabla u_{j}=\partial_{x}u_{j}\partial_{y}B_{ij}-\partial_{y}u_{j}\partial_{x}B_{ij}\,.

This particular structure allows one to apply Wente’s Inequality (

One of the most interesting questions related to the study of the energy (
1
) is the existence of non-trivial critical points. Unfortunately the energy (
1

In order to overcome this difficulty one considers suitable relaxations of the energy satisfying the Palais-Smale condition.
We mention that J. Sacks and K. Uhlenbeck in the ground-breaking paper
[
23
]
considered the following regularization of the Dirichlet Energy

(9)

E_{p}(u):=\int_{\Sigma}(1+|\nabla u|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}\ dx^{2}

where

Since the energy (
9

-harmonic maps, which

and as an application of their blow-up analysis they were able to show the existence of a minimal
two-sphere under suitable topological conditions on the target manifold. T. Lamm
[
8
]
later proved that there is no energy loss during the blow-up process under an ad-hoc entropy condition. In a joint recent paper
[
3
]
of the first author with T. Rivière, the authors proved an
L^{2,1}
estimate result in neck-regions for a sequence of critical points of the energy

(10)

E_{p}(u):=\int_{\Sigma}(1+|\nabla u|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}\ dx^{2}+\int_{\Sigma}u^{*}\alpha

under an entropy condition which is a bit stronger than the one used by T. Lamm in
[
8
]
, and which links the exponent
p
and the concentration radii appearing in the neck region analysis. The energy identity and the ”necklessness” property for
p
-harmonic maps into spheres have been obtained in
[
14
]
by J. Li and X. Zhu, by combining the existence of conservation laws with the use of Lorentz spaces, using an approach similar to the one introduced twenty years earlier by F.-H. Lin and T. Rivière in
[

and further developed by P. Laurain and T. Rivière in
[
9
]

Contrary to the case of Ginzburg-Landau type perturbations that we will present below, energy can be lost and necks can arise during the bubbling process for

-harmonic maps when the target manifold is not a sphere. An example of this phenomenon has been found by Y. Li and Y. Wang in

In this paper we consider a perturbation of (

Let

Discussion.
The strategy of the proof of theorem
4.2
is strongly inspired by the authors’ and T. Rivière’s recent work on the Morse index upper continuity for conformally invariant Lagrangians
[
2
]
. The main idea consists in showing that the neck regions of the given sequence
positively
contribute to the second derivative of the Lagrangian
E_{\varepsilon_{k}}(u_{k})
:

(19)

D^{2}{E_{\varepsilon_{k}}}(u_{k})[v]=\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla v|^{2}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{k}^{2}}\langle u_{k},v\rangle^{2}-\frac{1}{2\varepsilon_{k}^{2}}(1-|u_{k}|^{2})|v|^{2}

The key step towards obtaining quantitative estimates for the contribution of the index in the neck regions in our previous work
[
2
]
was a precise point-wise estimate of the gradients in these regions (Proposition III.1 in
[
2
]
). Similarly in the current paper point-wise estimates in the neck regions for the energy densities

(20)

e_{\varepsilon}(u)=\frac{1}{2}|du|_{h}^{2}+\frac{1}{4\varepsilon^{2}}(1-|u|^{2})^{2}

play a fundamental role.
The proof of the point-wise estimates in Theorem
4
requires a subtle analysis of the conservation laws hidden in the Euler-Lagrange equations (
6
). The approach differs substantially from the one taken in
[
2
]
in this part of the proof. More precisely, we first prove that the part of the gradient given by
u_{k}\wedge\nabla u_{k}
satisfies
L^{2,1}
estimates thanks to the conservation law
{\rm div}(u_{k}\wedge\nabla u_{k})=0
and then use these estimates to get an improved bound for the entire energy density
e_{\varepsilon_{k}}(u_{k})
in the neck regions. Unlike in the joint paper
[
2
]
,
we do not need an a-priori
L^{2,1}
estimate of the gradient to get
3.1
. This is due to the fact that in the case of equation (
12
) the conservation law
{\rm div}(u_{k}\wedge\nabla u_{k})=0
holds on the entire space and we do not need a
gauge change
on the neck regions under small assumption of the
L^{2}
norm of gradients of the given sequence. As explained in the paper
[
2
]
such a change of the Gauge produces a conservation law up to a harmonic error that one needs to control by means of
L^{2,1}
quantization of the gradient. We would like to mention that the
L^{2}
-energy identity and the no-neck property for a sequence of critical points of the energy (
11
) with uniformly bounded energy have been already obtained by different methods by F. H. Lin and C. Wang in
[
13
]
. Our analysis yields a precise
quantitative
version of the no-neck property, which is necessary for the proof of Theorem
5
.

We believe that our approach will be robust enough to treat the case of Ginzburg - Landau type approximation of conformally invariant variational problems in
2
-D of the form

(21)

\mathfrak{L}_{\varepsilon}(u):=\int_{\Sigma}\frac{1}{2}|du|^{2}_{h}\ dvol_{h}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}F(u)dvol_{h}+u^{*}\alpha

where
F
is some smoothing of the square of the distance to a
C^{2}
closed Riemannian manifold
{\mathcal{N}}^{n}
of arbitrary dimension
n
, and
\alpha
is an arbitrary
2
-form on
{\mathcal{N}}^{n}
.

The analysis of the extended index stability for the Lagrangians (
21
) is the aim of our future investigation.

Further directions.
We finally mention that M. Karpukhin and D. Stern in
[
7
]
characterized maximal conformal eigenvalues of surfaces using the energy of harmonic maps coming from a converging sequence of Ginzburg-Landau critical points taking values in
S^{n}
, with an explicit upper bound for the indices of the critical points along the sequence. The bubbling of (some of) the min-max sequences constructed in
[
7
]
could be ruled out by the authors using index considerations. It would be interesting to understand which consequences and/or applications the upper-semi-continuity of the index for this sequences as proven in Theorem
4.2
might have.

Preliminaries and notation

We consider an arbitrary smooth closed and oriented 2-dimensional Riemann manifold
(\Sigma,h)
and
the Ginzburg-Landau perturbation of the Dirichlet energy given by

(22)

E_{\varepsilon}(u)=\int_{\Sigma}e_{\varepsilon}(u)\,dvol_{h}

where
u\in W^{1,2}(\Sigma,\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
and

(23)

e_{\varepsilon}(u)=\frac{1}{2}|du|_{h}^{2}+\frac{1}{4\varepsilon^{2}}(1-|u|^{2})^{2}\,.

Critical points of the functional (
22

(24)

-\Delta_{h}u=u\ \frac{1-|u|^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\penalty\ \penalty\ \mbox{in $\Sigma$}.

By standard elliptic regularity arguments such critical points are smooth, and the following uniform
L^{\infty}
bounds hold:

Lemma 1.1
.

Let
u\colon(\Sigma,h)\to\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a smooth solution of

(25)

-\Delta_{h}u=u\ \frac{1-|u|^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}

for some
\varepsilon>0
. Then

(26)

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma)}\leq 1.

Proof of Lemma
1.1
.

We recall the following identity

(27)

\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{h}|u|^{2}={\rm div}_{h}(u\cdot du)=u\Delta_{h}u+|du|_{h}^{2}\,.

Multiplying (
25
) by
u(1-|u|^{2})^{-}
, where
f^{-}=\min(0,f)\leq 0
and integrating over
\Sigma
we get

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}-(1-|u|^{2})^{-}u\Delta_{h}u=\int_{\Sigma}-(1-|u|^{2})^{-}\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{h}|u|^{2}-|du|_{h}^{2}\bigg)=\int_{\Sigma}\frac{|u|^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\bigg((1-|u|^{2})^{-}\bigg)^{2}.

This implies

(28)

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}-d(1-|u|^{2})^{-}\cdot_{h}\bigg(\frac{d|u|^{2}}{2}\bigg)\,dvol_{h}=\int_{\Sigma}(1-|u|^{2})^{-}\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{h}|u|^{2}

\displaystyle\noindent=

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}(1-|u|^{2})^{-}|du|_{h}^{2}-\int_{\Sigma}\frac{|u|^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\bigg((1-|u|^{2})^{-}\bigg)^{2}\,dvol_{h}\leq 0\,.

On the other hand we have

(29)

-\frac{1}{2}d(1-|u|^{2})^{-}\cdot_{h}(-d(1-|u|^{2}))=\frac{1}{2}|d(1-|u|^{2})^{-}|^{2}\,dvol_{h}\geq 0.

Hence
|u|\leq 1
a.e in
\Sigma
and we conclude the proof.
∎

Let
\varepsilon_{k}
be a monotone sequence converging to zero and consider an associated family of critical points
u_{k}
of
E_{\varepsilon_{k}}

(30)

-\Delta_{h}u_{k}=u_{k}\frac{1-|u_{k}|^{2}}{\varepsilon_{k}^{2}}

with uniformly bounded energy in the sense

(31)

Let us denote by

the weak limit

(32)

u_{k}\rightharpoonup u_{\infty}\text{ weakly in }W^{1,2}(\Sigma,\mathbb{R}^{n+1}).

While each

is smooth, no uniform estimates for higher derivatives a priori hold, so that the weak convergence to
u_{\infty}
cannot be expected to be strong in general. Thanks to the so-called small-energy regularity, one can prove that the convergence is strong away from a finite number of points, and that around these points, the convergence is strong after a re-scaling. This kind of convergence is known as bubble-tree convergence and is common for conformally invariant problems.
By following the approach of R. Schoen in
[
24
]
for the case of harmonic maps we get the following the small-energy regularity result whose proof is postponed in the appendix.

for some

independent of
\varepsilon.

]
(Lemma 2.2), one can obtain second order point-wise estimates.

satisfying

Appendix A
proof of small-energy regularity

In this appendix we prove the small-energy regularity property for the Ginzburg-Landau perturbation, that is Proposition
1.2
.

An important underlying tool is

Bochner’s identity
that we first present in the following lemma.

Lemma A.1

(Bochner’s identity)
.

Let
u\in W^{1,2}(\Sigma,\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
be a critical point of
E_{\varepsilon}
. Then the energy density
e_{\varepsilon}(u)
satisfies

(209)

\displaystyle-\Delta_{h}(e_{\varepsilon}(u))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(e_{\varepsilon}^{2}(u)+e_{\varepsilon}(u))\penalty\ \penalty\ \mbox{in $\Sigma$}

for some positive constant
C
which is independent of
u
and
\varepsilon
.

Proof of Lemma
A.1
.

To simplify the computation we take isothermal coordinates so that we can assume that
h
is conformal to the flat metric, namely

h_{ij}=e^{2\lambda}\delta_{ij}
for some smooth function
\lambda.

We set

(210)

e_{\varepsilon}(u)=\frac{1}{2}|du|_{h}^{2}+\frac{1}{4\varepsilon^{2}}(1-|u|^{2})^{2}

We recall that

(211)

\Delta_{h}e_{\varepsilon}(u)=-e^{-2\lambda}\Delta e_{\varepsilon}(u)

and

(212)

|du|_{h}^{2}=e^{-4\lambda}|\nabla u|^{2}.

1.
We have

(213)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Delta(e^{-4\lambda}|\nabla u|^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\Delta e^{-4\lambda})|\nabla u|^{2}+\nabla e^{-4\lambda}\cdot\nabla|\nabla u|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}e^{-4\lambda}\Delta|\nabla u|^{2}.

Now we have

(214)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Delta|\nabla u|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{ii}^{2}\big(\frac{1}{2}\partial_{k}u\partial_{k}u\big)=\partial_{i}(\partial_{ik}^{2}u\partial_{k}u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}+\nabla\Delta u\cdot\nabla u.

Using the fact that
u
is a critical point and therefore solves the Euler-Lagrange equation

(215)

-\Delta u=u\ \frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}(1-|u|^{2})

we can compute
\nabla\Delta u\cdot\nabla u
and we obtain

(216)

\displaystyle-\nabla\Delta u\cdot\nabla u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla\big(\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}(1-|u|^{2})u\big)\cdot\nabla u=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\partial_{i}(1-|u|^{2})u^{j}\partial_{i}u^{j}=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}(-2u^{k}\partial_{i}u^{k}u^{j}\partial_{i}u^{j})+(1-|u|^{2})\partial_{i}u^{j}\partial_{i}u^{j})=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{\varepsilon^{2}}(u^{j}\partial_{i}u^{j})^{2}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}(1-|u|^{2})|\nabla u|^{2}.

2.
We compute

\Delta(\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\varepsilon^{2}}(1-|u|^{2})^{2})
.

(217)

\displaystyle\Delta(\frac{1}{4\varepsilon^{2}}(1-|u|^{2})^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\varepsilon^{2}}\partial_{ii}^{2}\big((1-u^{j}u^{j})^{2}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\varepsilon^{2}}(-u^{j}\partial_{i}u^{j})^{2}-\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}(1-|u|^{2})(\partial_{i}u^{j}\partial_{i}u^{j}+u^{j}\partial^{2}_{ii}u^{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\varepsilon^{2}}(u^{j}\partial_{i}u^{j})^{2}-\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}(1-|u|^{2})(|\nabla u|^{2}+u\cdot\Delta u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\varepsilon^{2}}(u^{j}\partial_{i}u^{j})^{2}-\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}(1-|u|^{2})(|\nabla u|^{2}-\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}(1-|u|^{2})|u|^{2}),

where in the last step in (
217
) we use the fact that
u
solves the Euler-Lagrange equation:

(218)

u\cdot\Delta u=-\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}(1-|u|^{2})|u|^{2}.

The combination of the estimates
(
213
)-(
217
) yields

(219)

\displaystyle-\Delta(e_{\varepsilon}(u))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-e^{-4\lambda}|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}-e^{-4\lambda}\frac{4}{\varepsilon^{2}}(u^{j}\partial_{i}u^{j})^{2}+e^{-4\lambda}\frac{2}{\varepsilon^{2}}(1-|u|^{2})|\nabla u|^{2}-e^{-4\lambda}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{4}}(1-|u|^{2})^{2}|u|^{2}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\Delta e^{-4\lambda})|\nabla u|^{2}+\nabla e^{-4\lambda}\cdot\nabla|\nabla u|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-e^{-4\lambda}|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}+e^{-4\lambda}\frac{2}{\varepsilon^{2}}(1-|u|^{2})|\nabla u|^{2}+C_{\lambda}\nabla|\nabla u|^{2}+C_{\lambda}|\nabla u|^{2}.

To conclude the proof we show that it is possible to bound (point-wise in
x
) the last term in (
219
) in terms of the energy density
e_{\varepsilon}(u)
and of
e_{\varepsilon}(u)^{2}
.

(220)

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\varepsilon^{2}}(1-|u|^{2})|\nabla u|^{2}-|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\varepsilon^{2}}(1-|u|^{2})|u||\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{2}{\varepsilon^{2}}(1-|u|^{2})(1-|u|)|\nabla u|^{2}-|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}

\displaystyle{\leq}

\displaystyle 2|\Delta u||\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{2}{\varepsilon^{2}}(1-|u|^{2})^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}-|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}

\displaystyle{\leq}

\displaystyle\delta_{1}|\Delta u|^{2}+2C_{\delta_{1}}|\nabla u|^{4}+\bigg(\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\big(1-|u|^{2}\big)^{2}\bigg)^{2}-|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ce_{\varepsilon}(u)^{2},

where we used in (
220
) that
u
solves the Euler Lagrange equation and
(1-|u|)\leq(1-|u|^{2})
by Lemma
1.1
.

We also have

(221)

\nabla|\nabla u|^{2}=2\nabla^{2}u\cdot\nabla u\leq\delta_{2}|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}+C_{\delta_{2}}|\nabla u|^{2}.

Since
|\Delta u|^{2}\leq|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}
, we can choose
\delta_{1}
and
\delta_{2}
in such a way that

e^{-4\lambda}\delta_{1}|\Delta u|^{2}-e^{-4\lambda}|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}+C_{\lambda}\delta_{2}|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}<0.

Hence from (
211
), (
219
) and (
220
) it follows

(222)

\displaystyle-\Delta_{h}(e_{\varepsilon}(u))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ce^{-2\lambda}e_{\varepsilon}^{2}(u)+C|\nabla u|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{\lambda}(e_{\varepsilon}^{2}(u)+e_{\varepsilon}(u))

where
C_{\lambda}
is a positive constant depending on
\lambda,\nabla\lambda
and
D^{2}\lambda.

This concludes the proof of the lemma.

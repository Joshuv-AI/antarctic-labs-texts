Complete quantum coherent control of ultracold molecular collisions
Adrien Devolder 1 , Paul Brumer 1 , and Timur V. Tscherbul 2

Abstract
We show that quantum interference-based coherent control is a highly efficient tool for tuning ultracold molecular collision dynamics, and is free from the limitations of commonly used methods that rely on external electromagnetic fields. By varying the relative populations and phases of an initial coherent superpositions of degenerate molecular states, we demonstrate complete coherent control over integral scattering cross sections in the ultracold s s -wave regime of both the initial and final collision channels. The proposed control methodology is applied to ultracold O 2 + O 2 collisions, showing extensive control over s s -wave spin-exchange cross sections and product branching ratios over many orders of magnitude.

First, dc fields control cannot be applied to control molecular systems that lack magnetic (or electric)

Quantum coherent control is a well-established approach free of these limitations, whereby quantum interference of transition pathways from an initially prepared coherent superposition of molecular states is used to maximize or minimize the transition amplitudes, as in the classic Young double-slit experiment

While coherent control has enjoyed great success when applied to unimolecular processes (such as photodissociation), its application to bimolecular collision dynamics has been limited by the need to entangle the internal and external degrees of freedom of collision partners, a significant experimental challenge

As the general conditions for efficient coherent control of ultracold collisions are unexplored, it remains unclear whether quantum interference-based control methods can be used to tune ultracold collision dynamics with an efficiency exceeding that of their traditional dc counterparts.

Here, we recognize quantum coherent control as an important approach to manipulating ultracold molecular collisions. We show that by forming coherent superpositions of initial molecular states, it is possible to achieve complete control over integral scattering cross sections and branching ratios in the
s

Our coherent control scenario does not require external electromagnetic fields and can be applied to a wide range of atomic and molecular collisions that are not amenable to external dc field control, such as those involving H

Theory.

As a first step to achieving coherent control of cold collisions, we prepare an initial coherent superposition of
N_{s}
two-molecule internal states
\ket{a_{i}b_{i}}
, where
a_{i}
and
b_{i}
denote internal states of each of the two colliding molecules:

\ket{\psi_{s}}=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{s}}c_{i}\ket{a_{i}b_{i}}.

(1)

Using the standard expression for the state-to-state integral cross section (ICS)
\sigma_{ab\rightarrow a^{\prime}b^{\prime}}=\frac{\pi}{k^{2}}\sum_{\ell,m_{\ell}}\sum_{\ell^{\prime},m_{\ell}^{\prime}}|T_{ab\ell,m_{\ell}\rightarrow a^{\prime}b^{\prime}\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}_{\ell}}|^{2}
, where
\ell
and
\ell^{\prime}
are the initial and final orbital angular momenta of the collision,
m_{\ell}
and
m^{\prime}_{\ell}
are the projections of
\ell
and
\ell^{\prime}
on the space-fixed quantization axis
Z
,
k
is the initial relative momentum, and
T_{ab\ell m_{\ell}\rightarrow a^{\prime}b^{\prime}\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}_{\ell}}
are the
T
-matrix elements, we obtain
the cross-section for scattering from the initial superposition (
1
) to the final two-molecule internal state
\ket{a^{\prime}b^{\prime}}
as

\sigma_{s\rightarrow a^{\prime}b^{\prime}}=\frac{\pi}{k^{2}}\sum_{\ell,m_{\ell}}\sum_{\ell^{\prime},m^{\prime}_{\ell}}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{N_{s}}c_{i}T_{a_{i}b_{i}\ell m_{\ell}\rightarrow a^{\prime}b^{\prime}\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}_{\ell}}\right|^{2}.

(2)

For a superposition of two states (
N_{s}
=2), this expression is analogous to that describing a set of double slit experiments for each partial wave pair
\{\ell m_{\ell},\ell^{\prime}m_{\ell}^{\prime}\}
, where the interfering paths are defined by the initial collision channels,
\ket{a_{1}b_{1}\ell m_{\ell}}
and
\ket{a_{2}b_{2}\ell m_{\ell}}
leading to the final channel
\ket{a^{\prime}b^{\prime}\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}_{\ell}}
. The amplitudes of each path are
|c_{1}T_{a_{1}b_{1}\ell,m_{\ell}\rightarrow a^{\prime}b^{\prime}\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}_{\ell}}|
and
|c_{2}T_{a_{2}b_{2}\ell m_{\ell}\rightarrow a^{\prime}b^{\prime}\ell^{\prime}m^{\prime}_{\ell}}|
, and the relative phase between the paths is given by the sum of the relative phase of superposition coefficients
\beta
(see below) and that of the
T
-matrix elements (
\delta_{1}^{\{\ell m_{\ell},\ell^{\prime}m_{\ell}^{\prime}\}}-\delta_{2}^{\{\ell m_{\ell},\ell^{\prime}m_{\ell}^{\prime}\}}
). Because there is no interference between the terms with different
\ell
,
m_{\ell}
,
\ell^{\prime}
, and
m_{\ell}^{\prime}
in Eq. (
2
), the efficiency of coherent control of the ICS depends on how well we can control the individual partial wave contributions. Thus, we expect the control efficiency to be strongly enhanced at low temperatures, when only a limited number of initial and final partial wave terms are present in Eq. (
2
). In particular, in the limit of zero collision energy, only
s
-wave terms with
\ell=0,m_{\ell}=0
contribute to the ICS due to the Wigner threshold law
Sadeghpour et al. 2000
;
Krems 2005
, and the number of partial waves in the final channel is often strongly limited by angular momentum conservation
Volpi and Bohn 2002
;
Krems and Dalgarno 2003
.

Below we consider the case of nearly thermoneutral spin-exchange
collisions dominated by
s
-waves in both the incident and final scattering channels (
\ell=\ell^{\prime}=0)
, where the above arguments lead us to expect a large extent of coherent control.

Consider then a coherent superposition of two incident
s
-wave channels
\ket{a_{1}b_{1}00}
and
\ket{a_{2}b_{2}00}
, which allows for coherent control of the ICS to the final
s
-wave channel
\ket{a^{\prime}b^{\prime}00}
. Equation (
2
) then reduces to

\sigma_{s\rightarrow a^{\prime}b^{\prime}}=\frac{\pi}{k^{2}}\left|\cos\eta T_{1}+\sin\eta e^{i\beta}T_{2}\right|^{2},

(3)

where we define
c_{1}=\cos\eta
,
c_{2}=\sin\eta e^{i\beta}
,
T_{1}=T_{a_{1}b_{1}00\rightarrow a^{\prime}b^{\prime}00}
and
T_{2}=T_{a_{2}b_{2}00\rightarrow a^{\prime}b^{\prime}00}
. Note that
\eta
defines the relative population of each state in the superposition while
\beta
gives the relative phase between the states.

The values of
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
that extremize the ICS can be found by diagonalizing the matrix
\mathcal{T}_{ij}=T_{i}T^{*}_{j}

Frishman et al. 1999
.
The lowest eigenvalue corresponds to
\sigma_{s\rightarrow a^{\prime}b^{\prime}}^{\text{min}}=0
, which shows that
it is always possible to coherently suppress collision-induced transitions to any given final channel
\ket{a^{\prime}b^{\prime}00}
regardless of the values of
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
.
The optimal values of the superposition parameters
\eta
and
\beta
that minimize the ICS are given by

\displaystyle\eta_{\text{min}}

\displaystyle=\cos^{-1}\left[\sqrt{{\sigma_{2}}/(\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2}})\right]=\tan^{-1}(\sqrt{{\sigma_{1}/\sigma_{2}}})

(4)

\displaystyle\beta_{\text{min}}

\displaystyle=(\delta_{2}-\delta_{1})-\pi,

(5)

where
\sigma_{1}=\frac{\pi}{k^{2}}|T_{1}|^{2}
and
\sigma_{2}=\frac{\pi}{k^{2}}|T_{2}|^{2}
are the
s
-wave ICS for the incident channels
\ket{a_{1}b_{1}00}
and
\ket{a_{2}b_{2}00}
.

We observe that the optimal value of
\eta
depends on the ratio of the s-wave ICS while the relative phase
\beta
depends on the difference in phases of the
T
-matrix elements. These results
can be understood using the analogy with the double slit experiment: The angle
\eta
determines the distinguishability between the two paths while the angle
\beta
controls their relative phase. The paths interfere destructively when they are indistinguishable and when the relative phase between them is a multiple of
\pi
.
Indeed, at
\eta=\eta_{\text{min}}
, the two paths are indistinguishable with equal amplitudes, i.e.,
\cos(\eta_{\text{min}})|T_{1}|=\sin(\eta_{\text{min}})|T_{2}|
, and the relative phase between the two paths
\beta+(\delta_{1}-\delta_{2})
reaches
\pi
when
\beta=\beta_{\text{min}}
[Eq. (
5
)].

From the second eigenvalue of
\mathcal{T}_{ij}
, we obtain the maximum value of the ICS, which is given by the sum of the ICSs from the initial channels
\ket{a_{1}b_{1}00}
and
\ket{a_{2}b_{2}00}

\sigma_{s\rightarrow a^{\prime}b^{\prime}}^{\text{max}}=\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2}.

(6)

Using coherent control, it is therefore possible to tune the
s
-wave ICSs between zero and
\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2}
. As
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
can reach very large values near collision thresholds
Sadeghpour et al. 2000
;
Krems 2005
;
Balakrishnan 2016

a very wide control range is possible, as shown below for O
2
+ O
2
collisions. The superposition angles
\eta
and
\beta
that maximize the ICS are given by

\displaystyle\eta_{\text{max}}

\displaystyle=\cos^{-1}\left[\sqrt{{\sigma_{1}}/(\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2}})\right]=\tan^{-1}(\sqrt{{\sigma_{2}/\sigma_{1}}})

(7)

\displaystyle\beta_{\text{max}}

\displaystyle=\delta_{2}-\delta_{1}.

(8)

Using the double-slit experiment analogy developed above, the maximum ICS is obtained when the relative phase between the two paths
\beta_{\text{max}}+(\delta_{1}-\delta_{2})
is zero, leading to Eq. (
8
).
In contrast to the case of
\sigma_{s\to a^{\prime}b^{\prime}}^{\text{min}}
, the best strategy for maximizing the ICS is to increase the distinguishability of the two interfering paths.
Interestingly, the values of the superposition parameters that minimize and maximize the ICS are related by

\eta_{\text{max}}+\eta_{\text{min}}=\pi/2
and

\beta_{\text{max}}-\beta_{\text{min}}=\pi
.

Having demonstrated complete coherent control of the total ICS, we now show that such control can be extended to include the branching ratios
{\sigma_{s\rightarrow 1^{\prime}}}/{\sigma_{s\rightarrow 2^{\prime}}}
for transitions to the final channels
|1^{\prime}\rangle=\ket{a^{\prime}_{1}b^{\prime}_{1}00}
and
|2^{\prime}\rangle=\ket{a^{\prime}_{2}b^{\prime}_{2}00}
.
As shown above, there exists a superposition, defined by the parameters
\eta_{\text{min}}^{1^{\prime}}
and
\beta_{\text{min}}^{1^{\prime}}
, for which the
s
-wave ICS
\sigma_{s\rightarrow 1^{\prime}}
vanishes. Similarly, there is a superposition with the parameters
\eta_{\text{min}}^{2^{\prime}}
and
\beta_{\text{min}}^{2^{\prime}}
, for which the ICS
\sigma_{s\rightarrow 2^{\prime}}
vanishes. Then, the ICS ratio
{\sigma_{s\rightarrow 1^{\prime}}}/{\sigma_{s\rightarrow 2^{\prime}}}
can be varied from zero to infinity by tuning the superposition parameters from (
\eta_{\text{min}}^{1^{\prime}}
,
\beta_{\text{min}}^{1^{\prime}}
) to (
\eta_{\text{min}}^{2^{\prime}}
,
\beta_{\text{min}}^{2^{\prime}}
), thus achieving complete control over the branching ratio.

Application: Coherent control of ultracold molecular collisions.

As an example consider the coherent control of ultracold collisions of O
{}_{2}(X^{3}\Sigma)
molecules in their ground electronic and rovibrational states (
v=N=0
, where
v
is the vibrational quantum number and
N
is the quantum number related to the square of the rotational angular momentum
\hat{N}^{2}
).
Cold and ultracold O
{}_{2}(X^{3}\Sigma)
+ O
{}_{2}(X^{3}\Sigma)
collisions were studied theoretically by several groups
Pérez-Rios et al. 2009
;
Tscherbul et al. 2009
;
Avdeenkov and Bohn 2001

and have recently observed experimentally in a magnetically trapped oxygen gas

Due to their nonzero electron spin
S=1

{}_{2}(X^{3}\Sigma)
molecules
can occupy three different spin states
\ket{M_{S}}
with
M_{S}=-1,0,
and 1 (assuming
S=1
and neglecting the hyperfine structure for simplicity). An inelastic collision can change the spin projection of one or both molecules i.e.,
\ket{{M_{A},M_{B}}}_{p}\rightarrow\ket{{M^{\prime}_{A},M^{\prime}_{B}}}_{p}
,
where

\ket{{M_{A},M_{B}}}_{p}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2(1+\delta_{M_{A}M_{B}})}}\left[\ket{M_{A},M_{B}}+p\ket{M_{B},M_{A}}\right].

(9)

These are internal states of the colliding molecules that have been identical particle symmetrized and that include the parity
p
of the state
Tscherbul et al. 2009
. When
M_{A}=M_{B}
, only the state for
p=1
exists while the both parities are possible otherwise. In this paper, we drop the index
p
, writing
\ket{{M_{A},M_{B}}}
, when the calculated quantity (ICS or branching ratio) includes a sum on partial waves and then on the both parities.

More specifically, consider the nearly thermoneutral spin-exchange collisions
\ket{{0,0}}_{p}\leftrightarrow\ket{{-1,+1}}_{p}
, which can be used to generate entanglement
Duan et al. 2002
and quantum many-body phases in spinor Bose-Einstein condensates
Chang et al. 2005
;
Kawaguchi and Ueda 2012
;
Stamper-Kurn and Ueda 2013
and play an important role in ultracold atom-molecule and atom-ion chemistry

;
Liu et al. 2019
;
Sikorsky et al. 2018
. At ultracold temperature, these flip-flop collisions occur in the
s
-wave regime for both the incident and final channels, thus forming an ideal testing ground for the application of the coherent control theory developed above.

To calculate the
T
-matrix elements for ultracold O
2
+ O
2
collisions, we employ a rigorous time-independent quantum scattering approach

using an uncoupled symmetrized space-fixed basis set composed of three rotational states (
N=0{-}4
) and 6 partial waves (
\ell=0{-}10
).
The coupled-channel equations were integrated on the radial grid from 4.0 to 150
a_{0}
, with a step of 0.04
a_{0}
to obtain the
T
-matrix elements converged to within
5\%
. The calculation includes both parities
p=\pm 1
.
The calculated
T
-matrix elements are then used to construct the ICSs [Eq. (
2
)]. The sum on partial waves is carried out in order to study collisions outside the double
s
-wave regime.

To coherently control the spin-exchange ICS to the final channels
\ket{{0,0}}
and
\ket{{-1,+1}}
, consider three different kinds of coherent superpositions of the initial molecular spin states
\ket{{0,0}}_{p}
and
\ket{{-1,+1}}_{p}
. In particular,

an entangled two-molecule superposition

\ket{\psi_{E}}=\cos\eta\ket{{-1,+1}}_{p=\pm 1}+\sin\eta e^{i\beta}\ket{{0,0}}_{p=+1}

(10)

cannot be represented as a direct product of the individual molecules’s states. While this superposition is the simplest to consider from a theoretical perspective, and provides robust control (see below), it is challenging to prepare experimentally, as it requires entangling the internal states of the colliding molecules. Note that only the positive partity (
p=1
) gives a superposition of the two states. For a negative parity,
\ket{\psi_{E}}
reduces to
\ket{{-1,+1}}_{-1}
and gives uncontrollable odd-partial wave contributions to the ICS.

A non-entangled initial superposition
has the form of a tensor product of two single-molecule superposition states
\ket{\psi_{A}}\ket{\psi_{B}}
, where

\displaystyle\ket{\psi_{A}}

\displaystyle=N_{2}\left(\sqrt{\cos\eta}\ket{-1}+\sqrt{\sin\eta}e^{i\frac{\beta}{2}}\ket{0}\right)

(11)

\displaystyle\ket{\psi_{B}}

\displaystyle=N_{2}\left(\sqrt{\sin\eta}e^{i\frac{\beta}{2}}\ket{0}+\sqrt{\cos\eta}\ket{+1}\right),

(12)

where
N_{2}=(\sin\eta+\cos\eta)^{-1/2}
. For two identical bosonic molecules such as O
2
, this initial state must be symmetrized to account for identical particle permutation symmetry

giving

\ket{\psi_{2}^{S}}=N_{2}^{2}\Big[\cos\eta\ket{{-1,+1}}_{p=\pm 1}+\sin\eta e^{i\beta}\ket{{0,0}}_{p=+1}\\
+\sqrt{\cos\eta\sin\eta}e^{i\frac{\beta}{2}}(\ket{{-1,0}}_{p=\pm 1}+\ket{{0,+1}}_{p=\pm 1})\Big].

(13)

This initial state can be created in, e.g., merged beam experiments

by preparing coherent superpositions of internal states of the individual molecules prior to collision.
In a similar way, we can prepare a

\ket{\psi_{A}}=N_{3}\left[\sqrt{\cos\eta}(\ket{-1}+\ket{+1})+\sqrt{\sin\eta}e^{i\frac{\beta}{2}}\ket{0}\right],

(14)

where
N_{3}=({\sin\eta+2\cos\eta})^{-1/2}

After symmetrization, the initial wavefunction becomes

\begin{split}\ket{\psi_{3}^{S}}=N_{3}^{2}\Big[&\cos\eta\ket{{-1,+1}}_{p=\pm 1}+\sin\eta e^{i\beta}\ket{{0,0}}_{p=+1}\\
+&\sqrt{\cos\eta\sin\eta}e^{i\frac{\beta}{2}}(\ket{{-1,0}}_{p=\pm 1}+\ket{{0,+1}}_{p=\pm 1})\\
+&\cos\eta(\ket{{-1,-1}}_{p=+1}+\ket{{+1,+1}}_{p=+1})\Big].\end{split}

(15)

In conclusion, we have developed a general theory of quantum interference-based coherent control of ultracold collisions, which allowed us to establish the possibility of complete coherent control over quantum scattering in the regime where only a single partial wave is involved in both the incident and final collision channels. We show that ultralow temperatures strongly enhance coherent control by favoring
s
-wave threshold scattering,
and we determine the optimal parameters of the coherent superpositions required to maximize and minimize the ICS.
The theory was applied to control ultracold spin-exchange collisions of oxygen molecules. We demonstrate vast control over both the ICS and their branching ratios in the
s
-wave threshold regime.
Our results demonstrate the possibility of using quantum interference as a powerful tool for controlling ultracold collision dynamics, which can be applied to a much wider range of molecular species (such as H
2
) than dc field control.
We envision examining the possible use of coherent control to tune spin-exchange interactions in spinor Bose-Einstein condensates
Chang et al. 2005
;
Duan et al. 2002
;
Stamper-Kurn and Ueda 2013
and to control cold atom-molecule and atom-ion spin-exchange chemistry
Rui et al. 2017
;
Liu et al. 2019
;
Sikorsky et al. 2018
.
It would also be interesting to explore the possibility of coherently controlling exothermic relaxation processes in ultracold collisions outside the
s
-wave threshold regime for the final collision channel, especially when a single partial wave is implied due to the presence of a resonance
Jambrina et al. 2019
;
Morita et al. 2020
.

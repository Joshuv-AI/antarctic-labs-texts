Abstract
. In this paper, we study the construction of the supersymmetric extensions of vertex algebras. In particular, for N = n ∈ ℤ + N=n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+} , we show the universal enveloping N = n N=n supersymmetric (SUSY) vertex algebra of an N = n N=n SUSY Lie conformal algebra can be extended to an N = n ′ > n N=n^{\prime}>n SUSY vertex algebra.



1.
Introduction

Vertex algebras were introduced by R. Borcherds in the 1980’s (see
[
3
]
) to describe the chiral part of two-dimensional conformal field theory (see
[
4
]
). A vertex algebra consists of a vector superspace
V
,
\text{End}(V)
-valued quantum fields
a(z)
for
a\in V
and a translation operator
\partial
with certain axioms. One of the axiom called locality tells that, for a given pair of fields
(a,b)
in
V
, there is a positive integer
t\in\mathbb{N}
such that
(z-w)^{t}[a(z),b(w)]=0
. Considering the formal delta distribution
\delta(z,w):=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}z^{j}w^{-j-1}
, the elements
a_{(m)}b\in V
for
m=0,1,\cdots,t
are determined by the supercommutator formula:

(1.1)

[a(z),b(w)]=\sum_{m=0}^{t}\frac{1}{m!}a_{(m)}b(w)\ \partial_{w}^{m}\delta(z,w).

The existence of such formula (
1.1
) is guaranteed by so-called the decomposition theorem. Additionally, (
1.1
) and the state-field correspondence yield another well-known definition of a vertex algebra using the
\lambda
-bracket formalism (see Section
2
). Speaking briefly, a vertex algebra
V
is a differential quasi-commutative quasi-associative supereralgebra endowed with a
\lambda
-bracket which is compatible with the differential algebra structure. Here, the derivation on
V
for the differential algebra structure arises from the translation operator
\partial.

To describe two-dimensional conformal field theory with supersymmetries in mathematical language, it is good to use the notion of supersymmetric vertex algebra, which is a generalization of the superconformal vertex algebras (see Section 5.9 of
[
19
]
). A supersymmetric vertex algebra consists of a vector superspace
V
and superfields
a(z,\Theta):=a(z,\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\cdots,\theta_{n})
for
a\in V
and odd variables
\theta_{i}
. Roughly speaking, the superfield

a(z,{\Theta})=\sum_{I}\pm\ \theta^{I}D^{I}a(z),

where the summation is over the ordered subset
I=\{i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{s}\}\in\{1,2,\cdots,n\}
satisfying
i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{s}
,
D^{I}a(z):=D^{i_{1}}D^{i_{2}}\cdots,D^{i_{s}}a(z)
is a field of parity
p(a)+\bar{s}

and
\theta_{I}=\theta_{i_{1}}\theta_{i_{2}}\cdots\theta_{i_{s}}
. Here
D^{i}
’s can be understood as odd endomorphisms of fields such that
D^{i}D^{j}+D^{j}D^{i}=\delta_{i,j}2\partial
for
i,j\in I

and, indeed, the odd endomorphisms determine the structures of
V
coming from the supersymmetry. In particular, in
N=1
(resp.
N=2
) case, a superfield
a(z,\Theta)
consists of a couple (resp. quadruple) of fields
(a(z),Da(z))
(resp.
(a(z),D^{1}a(z),D^{2}a(z),D^{1}D^{2}(z))
). Analogous to the
\lambda
-bracket formalism in vertex algebras theory, an
N=n
supersymmetric vertex algebra can be introduced via so-called
\Lambda=(\lambda,\chi^{1},\chi^{2},\cdots,\chi^{n})
-bracket formalism (see Section
3
).

As one can expect, a SUSY vertex algebra is a vertex algebra by itself but the converse cannot be true. However, it is well-known that if a vertex algebra has a superconformal vector then the vector induces a supersymmetry in the vertex algebra (see, for example
[
13
,
19
]
). Here a superconformal vector
G
in a vertex algebra
V
is a primary of conformal weight
{\frac{3}{2}}
element satisfying

[G\ _{\lambda}\ G]=2{L}+{\frac{c}{3}}{\lambda}^{2}

for a conformal vector
L
of
V
. Indeed, in this case, we can define an odd operator
D
by letting
D(a)
to be the coefficient of
\lambda^{0}
in
[G\ _{\lambda}\ a]
and the vertex algebra
V
together with
D
becomes an
N=1
SUSY vertex algebra. We remark that constructions of
N=1
and
N=2
superconformal structures in vertex algebras have been studied in various contexts (see
[
16
,
18
]
).

In Section
4
, we investigate superconformal vectors of a vertex algebra which are main sources of the
N=1
SUSY vertex algebra structures. Sometimes, there are more than one superconformal vectors which yield the same SUSY structure but different conformal structures. In this case, we call one of the superconformal vectors a shift of the other one (see Proposition
4.3
).
The shift of conformal vectors has a crucial application in the context of
W
–algebras (see
[
1
,
20
]
). More precisely, a conformal vector of the
W
–algebra is induced from a conformal vector of the corresponding BRST complex, which is the tensor product of the affine vertex algebra and the free fermion vertex algebra. Hence the sum of conformal vectors of these two algebras becomes a conformal vector of the BRST complex. Each of the affine vertex algebra and the free fermion vertex algebra has an infinite number of conformal vectors, and any two of these vectors are shifts of each other. As a consequence, the BRST complex has infinitely many conformal vectors which are shifts of each other, and exactly one of the them yields a desired conformal structure of the
W
–algebra. In this paper, we present analogous results on the
N=1
SUSY BRST complexes (see Theorem
4.7
).

If a vertex algebra does not have a superconformal vector, we cannot guarantee the existence of supersymmetries.
For example, the Virasoro vertex algebra and affine vertex algebras do not have neither superconformal vectors nor known supersymmetries. Nevertheless, we can impose supersymmtries on these vertex algebras by embedding them into bigger supersymmetric vertex algebras.
For the Virasoro vertex algebra, consider the

In this section, we review basic notions and properties of vertex algebras used throughout this article. For more details, we refer to
[

In this paper, the base field is
\mathbb{C}
. A vector superspace
V=V_{\bar{0}}\oplus V_{\bar{1}}
is a
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector space. An element

is called even (resp. odd) if

(resp.

is even (resp. odd), the parity of

is given by
p(a)=0
(resp.
p(a)=1

A vector superspace

is called a

if it is
endowed with

for

and

with the properties described below.
The
(-1)
-th product

is usually denoted by
:ab:
and called the

of

and

has an even derivation

such that

{a}_{(-m-1)}{b}={\frac{1}{m!}}:({\partial}^{m}{a}){b}:

for

Therefore the structure of

is determined by (i) the
m
-th product for

, (ii) the normally ordered product
:\ :
, and (iii) the derivation
\partial
. In order to deal with all the information from (i) together, we define a bracket

by

(2.1)

(a,b)\mapsto[a\ _{\lambda}\ b]=\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}{\frac{{\lambda}^{m}}{m!}}{a}_{(m)}{b},

where

is an even indeterminate. The linear bracket (
2.1
) satisfies the following property:

(1)

(Sesquilinearity)

[\partial a\ _{\lambda}\ b]=-\lambda[a\ _{\lambda}\ b],\quad[a\ _{\lambda}\ \partial b]=(\partial+\lambda)[a\ _{\lambda}\ b].

The RHS of the second equality in sesquilinearity can be computed by the relation
\partial\lambda=\lambda\partial
.
More generally, for a
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module

\lambda
-bracket

-bracket on the vertex algebra

Moreover, a vertex algebra is a Lie conformal algebra, which is defined as below.

(

A

\begin{gathered}[L\ _{\lambda}\ L]=({\partial}+2{\lambda})L+{\frac{c}{12}}{\lambda}^{3},{\quad}[L\ _{\lambda}\ G^{\pm}]=(\partial+\frac{3}{2}\lambda){G}^{\pm},{\quad}[G^{\pm}\ _{\lambda}\ G^{\pm}]=0,\\
[G^{+}\ _{\lambda}\ G^{-}]=L+(\frac{1}{2}\partial+\lambda)J+\frac{c}{6}{\lambda}^{2},{\quad}[L\ _{\lambda}\ J]=(\partial+\lambda)J,{\quad}[G^{\pm}\ _{\lambda}\ J]={\mp}{G}^{\pm},{\quad}[J\ _{\lambda}\ J]=\frac{c}{3}\lambda.\end{gathered}

The
N=3
super-Virasoro vertex algebra
is generated by a conformal vector
L
with central charge
c
, three even primary vectors
{J}^{+}
,
{J}^{-}
,
{J}^{0}
of conformal weight
1
, three odd primary vectors
{G}^{+}
,
{G}^{-}
,
{G}^{0}
of conformal weight
\frac{3}{2}
, and an odd primary vector
{\Phi}
of conformal weight
\frac{1}{2}
, satisfying the following
N=3
super-Virasoro relation where the non-zero
{\lambda}
-brackets are:

(2.5)

\begin{gathered}[{G}^{+}\ _{\lambda}\ {G}^{-}]={L}+({\frac{1}{2}}{\partial}+{\lambda}){J}^{0}+{\frac{c}{6}}{\lambda}^{2},{\quad}[{G}^{\pm}\ _{\lambda}\ {G}^{0}]=\pm({\frac{1}{2}}{\partial}+{\lambda}){J}^{\pm},{\quad}[{G}^{0}\ _{\lambda}\ {G}^{0}]=L+{\frac{c}{6}}{\lambda}^{2},\\
[{J}^{+}\ _{\lambda}\ {J}^{-}]={J}^{0}+{\frac{c}{3}}{\lambda},{\quad}[{J}^{\pm}\ _{\lambda}\ {J}^{0}]={\mp}{J}^{\pm},{\quad}[{J}^{0}\ _{\lambda}\ {J}^{0}]={\frac{c}{3}}{\lambda},\\
[{G}^{\pm}\ _{\lambda}\ {J}^{\mp}]=\mp{G}^{0}+({\partial}+{\lambda}){\Phi},{\quad}[{G}^{\pm}\ _{\lambda}\ {J}^{0}]=[{G}^{0}\ _{\lambda}\ {J}^{\pm}]={\mp}{G}^{\pm},{\quad}[{G}^{0}\ _{\lambda}\ {J}^{0}]=-({\partial}+{\lambda}){\Phi},{\quad}\\
[{G}^{\pm}\ _{\lambda}\ {\Phi}]={\frac{1}{2}}{J}^{\pm},{\quad}[{G}^{0}\ _{\lambda}\ {\Phi}]=-{\frac{1}{2}}{J}^{0},{\quad}[{\Phi}\ _{\lambda}\ {\Phi}]={\frac{c}{6}}.\end{gathered}

Note that, for the
N=3
super-Virasoro vertex algebra, there is an automorphism of the form

\displaystyle{G}^{\pm}\mapsto{G}^{\mp},{\quad}{G}^{0}\mapsto-{G}^{0},{\quad}{J}^{\pm}\mapsto{J}^{\mp},{\quad}{J}^{0}\mapsto-{J}^{0}.

For
{\mu}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
, the following map defined by

,
{J}^{\pm}\mapsto{\mu}^{\pm}{J}^{\pm}
is also an automorphism of the

super-Virasoro vertex algebra. For more details, the reader is referred to
[

]
.

3.
Supersymmetric vertex algebras

In this section, we introduce supersymmetric (SUSY) vertex algebras
using so-called
\Lambda
-bracket. It can be understood as an analogous definition to that of vertex algebras in Definition
2.2
. We remark, all the SUSY vertex algebras considered in this paper are the
N_{K}=n
SUSY vertex algebras in
[
13
]
. For the simplicity of notations, let us denote by
N=n
SUSY vertex algebras, instead of
N_{K}=n
SUSY vertex algebras. The main reference of this section is
[
13
]
.

Let
R
be a
\mathbb{C}[\nabla]
-module, where
\nabla=(\partial,D^{1},D^{2},\cdots,D^{n})
consists of an even operator
\partial
and odd operators
D^{i}
such that

(3.1)

[D^{i},D^{j}]=\delta_{i,j}2{\partial}

for
i,j=1,2,\cdots,n
.
Note that the bracket in (
3.1
) is a supercommutator.
In addition, consider a tuple
\Lambda:=(\lambda,\chi^{1},\chi^{2},\cdots,\chi^{n})
of an even formal variable
\lambda
and odd formal variables
\chi^{i}
which are subject to the relations

[\chi^{i},\chi^{j}]=-\delta_{i,j}2\lambda,

where
i,j=1,2,\cdots,n
, and the bracket is again a supercommutator. Now, a
\Lambda
-bracket

R\otimes R\to\mathbb{C}[\Lambda]\otimes R
is a degree
\bar{n}
linear map with the following property:

(1)

(Sesquilinearity)

[{D}^{i}a\ _{\Lambda}\ b]=(-1)^{n+1}{\chi}^{i}[a\ _{\Lambda}\ b],\quad[a\ _{\Lambda}\ {D}^{i}b]=(-1)^{p(a)+n}({D}^{i}+{\chi}^{i})[a\ _{\Lambda}\ b]
.

The RHS of the second equality in sesquilinearity can be computed by the relation

(3.2)

[D^{i},\chi^{j}]=\delta_{i,j}2\lambda,\quad[D^{i},\lambda]=0.

Definition 3.1

(
[
13
]
)
.

An
N=n

supersymmetric
(
SUSY
)
Lie conformal algebra
is a
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-graded
\mathbb{C}[\nabla]
-module
R
with a
\Lambda
-bracket which is a
\mathbb{C}
-linear map of degree
\bar{n}
:

\displaystyle[\ _{\Lambda}\ ]:R\otimes R\rightarrow\mathbb{C}[\Lambda]\otimes R,\quad a\otimes b\mapsto[a\ _{\Lambda}\ b],

satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

(Skew-symmetry)

[b\ _{\Lambda}\ a]=(-1)^{p(a)p(b)+n+1}[a\ _{-\nabla-\Lambda}\ b]
,

(2)

(Jacobi identity)

[a\ _{\Lambda}\ [b\ _{\Gamma}\ c]]=(-1)^{(p(a)+1)n}[[a\ _{\Lambda}\ b]\ _{\Lambda+\Gamma}\ c]+(-1)^{(p(a)+n)(p(b)+n)}[b\ _{\Gamma}\ [a\ _{\Lambda}\ c]]
,

for
a,b,c\in R
. We remark that the Jacobi identity holds in
\mathbb{C}[\Lambda,\Gamma]
, where
\Gamma=(\gamma,{\eta}^{1},{\eta}^{2},\cdots,{\eta}^{n})
consists of an even formal variable
{\gamma}
and odd formal variables
{\eta}^{i}
with the relations
[{\eta}^{i},{\eta}^{j}]=-{\delta}_{i,j}2{\gamma}
and
\Lambda
and
\Gamma
are supercommute. In addition,
\mathbb{C}[\Lambda,\Gamma]\otimes R
is a
\mathbb{C}[\nabla]
-module via the relation (
3.2
) and

\displaystyle[D^{i},\eta^{j}]=\delta_{i,j}2\gamma,\quad[D^{i},\gamma]=0.

In addition, the
\Lambda
-bracket supercommutes with
\mathbb{C}[\Lambda,\Gamma]
. For example,
[a\ {}_{\Lambda}\ \eta^{i}b]=(-1)^{p(a)+n}\eta^{i}[a\ {}_{\Lambda}\ b]
for any
a,b\in R
.

In particular, we denote the
\Lambda
-bracket for
N=1
SUSY Lie conformal algebras by

\displaystyle[a\ _{\Lambda}\ b]=\sum\limits_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\frac{{\lambda}^{m}}{m!}a_{(m|0)}b+\chi\sum\limits_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\frac{{\lambda}^{m}}{m!}a_{(m|1)}b,

and the
\Lambda
-bracket for
N=2
SUSY Lie conformal algebras by

\displaystyle[a\ _{\Lambda}\ b]=\sum\limits_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\frac{\lambda^{m}}{m!}{a}_{(m|00)}b-{\chi}^{1}\sum\limits_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\frac{\lambda^{m}}{m!}{a}_{(m|10)}b-{\chi}^{2}\sum\limits_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\frac{\lambda^{m}}{m!}{a}_{(m|01)}b-{\chi}^{1}{\chi}^{2}\sum\limits_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\frac{\lambda^{m}}{m!}{a}_{(m|11)}b.

Definition 3.2

(
[
13
]
)
.

An
N=n

supersymmetric
(
SUSY
)
vertex algebra
is a tuple
(V,\nabla,[\ _{\Lambda}\ ],\ket{0},:\ :)

\bullet

is an

\bullet

(1)

(Quasi-commutativity)

\displaystyle:ab:-(-1)^{p(a)p(b)}:ba:=\int_{-\nabla}^{0}[a\ _{\Lambda}\ b]\ d\Lambda,

(2)

(Quasi-associativity)

::ab:c:-:a:bc::=:\left(\int_{0}^{\nabla}\ d\Lambda a\right)[b\ _{\Lambda}\ c]:+(-1)^{p(a)p(b)}:\left(\int_{0}^{\nabla}\ d\Lambda b\right)[a\ _{\Lambda}\ c]:,

4.
Various superconformal structures of a vertex algebra

In a vertex algebra
V
, a superconformal vector induces the conformal weight decomposition of
V
. The structure arising from the decomposition is called the superconformal structure of
V
. It is closely related to a SUSY structure in the sense that both of them can be derived from a superconformal vector. In this section, we describe various superconformal structures correlated with the same
N=1
SUSY structure of a vertex algebra by performing so-called ‘shifts’ of a given superconformal vector.

Recall that an odd vector
G
of a vertex algebra
V
is called a
superconformal vector
if it satisfies the super-Virasoro relation (
2.3
) and
L={\frac{1}{2}}{G}_{(0)}{G}
is a conformal vector. In this case, we say that
V
is an
N=1
superconformal vertex algebra
. In the context of
N=1
SUSY vertex algebras, as in Section 1.2 of
[
13
]
, an
N=1
superconformal vertex algebra
V
with a superconformal vector
G\in V
has an
N=1
SUSY vertex algebra structure by taking the odd derivation
D:=G_{(0)}
. The following examples are superconformal vectors of
N=1
SUSY affine vertex algebras and the
bc
–
{\beta}{\gamma}
system.

Example 4.1

(
N=1
superconformal structure of
N=1
SUSY affine vertex algebra)
.

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}
be a basic Lie superalgebra with a non-degenerate invariant even supersymmetric bilinear form
(\,|\,)
, and let
{V}^{k}_{N=1}(\mathfrak{g})
be the
N=1
SUSY affine vertex algebra of level

in Example

[

(4.1)

\displaystyle{\tau}^{\mathfrak{g}}={\frac{1}{k}}\Big(\sum_{i\in I}(-1)^{p({a}^{i})}(D{\bar{a}}^{i}){\bar{b}}^{i}+{\frac{1}{3k}}\sum_{i,j,r\in I}([{a}^{i},{a}^{j}]\,|\,{a}^{r}){\bar{b}}^{i}{\bar{b}}^{j}{\bar{b}}^{r}\Big),

is a superconformal vector of
{V}^{k}_{N=1}(\mathfrak{g})
, where
\{{a}^{i}\}_{i{\in}I}
and
\{{b}^{i}\}_{i{\in}I}
are dual bases of
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to
(\,|\,)

is called the

(see also

\displaystyle{c}^{\mathfrak{g}}=\frac{(k-{h}^{\vee}){\textup{sdim}}{\mathfrak{g}}}{k}+\frac{{\textup{sdim}}{\mathfrak{g}}}{2},

For the

In general, for an
N=1
superconformal vertex algebra, if there is an odd vector satisfying the conditions in the following proposition, we can shift the superconformal vector to another superconformal vector, giving rise to various conformal weights. At the level of vertex algebra structures, the
shifting
of a superconformal vector is a superconformal vector that yields the same
N=1
SUSY structures but a different superconformal structure.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
V
be an
N=1
superconformal vertex algebra with a superconformal vector
G
, where
L
is the conformal vector with central charge
c
. Suppose that
v\in V
is an odd vector satisfying the following relations

(4.2)

\displaystyle[{L}\ _{\lambda}\ {v}]=({\partial}+\frac{\lambda}{2}){v},{\quad}[{G}\ _{\lambda}\ {v}]={G}_{(0)}{v}+{c}_{1}{\lambda},{\quad}[{v}\ _{\lambda}\ {v}]={c}_{2},

for some
{c}_{1},{c}_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
and
({G}_{(0)}{v})_{(0)}
is diagonalizable on
V
. Then, the vector
G+{\partial}v
is also a superconformal vector and the corresponding conformal vector is
L+{\frac{1}{2}}{\partial}{G}_{(0)}{v}
with central charge
c+6{c}_{1}-3{c}_{2}
.

Proof.

It follows from the
\lambda
-bracket calculations using the properties of Lie conformal algebras. For example, we know that the following
\lambda
-bracket relations hold:

\displaystyle[{G}_{(0)}{v}\ _{\lambda}\ {G}]={\lambda}{v},{\quad}[{G}_{(0)}{v}\ _{\lambda}\ {v}]=0,

hence, by the Jacobi identity, we obtain:

\displaystyle[{G}_{(0)}{v}\ _{\lambda}\ {G}_{(0)}{v}]=[{G}_{(0)}{v}\ _{\lambda}\ [{G}\ _{\gamma}\ {v}]]=[[{G}_{(0)}{v}\ _{\lambda}\ {G}]\ _{\lambda+\gamma}\ {v}]+[{G}\ _{\gamma}\ [{G}_{(0)}{v}\ _{\lambda}\ {v}]]=[{\lambda}{v}\ _{\lambda+\gamma}\ {v}]={\lambda}{c}_{2}.

On the other hand, from the Jacobi identity, we have:

\displaystyle[{L}\ _{\lambda}\ {G}_{(0)}{v}]

\displaystyle=[{L}\ _{\lambda}\ [{G}\ _{\gamma}\ {v}]]=[[{L}\ _{\lambda}\ {G}]\ _{\lambda+\gamma}\ {v}]+[{G}\ _{\gamma}\ [{L}\ _{\lambda}\ {v}]]

\displaystyle=[({\partial}+{\frac{3}{2}}{\lambda})G\ _{\lambda+\gamma}\ {v}]+[{G}\ _{\gamma}\ ({\partial}+{\frac{1}{2}}{\lambda}){v}]

\displaystyle=({\frac{1}{2}}{\lambda}-{\gamma})({G}_{(0)}{v}+{c}_{1}({\lambda}+{\gamma}))+({\partial}+{\frac{1}{2}}{\lambda}+{\gamma})({G}_{(0)}{v}+{c}_{1}{\gamma})

\displaystyle=({\partial}+{\lambda}){G}_{(0)}{v}+{\frac{{c}_{1}}{2}}{\lambda}^{2}.

Combining the above
\lambda
-bracket relations, we obtain the following
\lambda
-bracket result:

\displaystyle[{L}+{\frac{1}{2}}{\partial}{G}_{(0)}{v}\ _{\lambda}\ {L}+{\frac{1}{2}}{\partial}{G}_{(0)}{v}]

\displaystyle=[{L}\ _{\lambda}\ {L}]+{\frac{1}{2}}({\partial}+{\lambda})[{L}\ _{\lambda}\ {G}_{(0)}{v}]-{\frac{1}{2}}{\lambda}[{G}_{(0)}{v}\ _{\lambda}\ {L}]-{\frac{1}{4}}{\lambda}({\partial}+{\lambda})[{G}_{(0)}{v}\ _{\lambda}\ {G}_{(0)}{v}]

\displaystyle=({\partial}+2{\lambda}){L}+{\frac{c}{12}}{\lambda}^{3}+{\frac{1}{2}}({\partial}+{\lambda})({\partial}+{\lambda}){G}_{(0)}{v}+{\frac{{c}_{1}}{2}}{\lambda}^{3}-{\frac{1}{2}}{\lambda}^{2}{G}_{(0)}{v}-{\frac{{c}_{2}}{4}}{\lambda}^{3}

\displaystyle=({\partial}+2{\lambda})({L}+{\frac{1}{2}}{\partial}{G}_{(0)}{v})+{\frac{1}{12}}({c}+6{c}_{1}-3{c}_{2}){\lambda}^{3}.

Similarly, the other
\lambda
-brackets of the super-Virasoro relation (
2.3
) can be verified.
∎

in terms of

5.
Supersymmetric extension theorem

Recall that an
N=1
(resp.
N=2
) SUSY vertex algebra
V
is a vertex algebra with the
m
-th product for
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
defined by

a_{(m)}b:=a_{(m|1)}b\quad(\,\text{resp.}\ a_{(m)}b:=a_{(m|11)}b\,)
.
Conversely, in this section, we investigate conditions to embed a given vertex algebra into an
N=1
SUSY vertex algebra.
In Theorem
5.6
, we also present the construction of an
N=1
SUSY extension of
V(R)
for any Lie conformal algebra
R
, by considering some proper ghost vectors which consist of the SUSY counterpart of vectors in the given Lie conformal algebra.

In order to impose an
N=1
SUSY structure on a vertex algebra
V
, we need an odd derivation
D
on
V
which satisfies
[D,D]=2{\partial}
and

(5.1)

\displaystyle D[a\ _{\lambda}\ b]=[Da\ _{\lambda}\ b]+(-1)^{p(a)}[a\ _{\lambda}\ Db],

where
[D,\lambda]=0
(see Section 5.9 of
[
19
]
). In other words, the operator

should also be an odd derivation with respect to the
\lambda
-bracket.
Indeed, by assuming the above properties, the
{\Lambda}
-bracket

(5.2)

\displaystyle[a\ _{\Lambda}\ b]:=[Da\ _{\lambda}\ b]+{\chi}[a\ _{\lambda}\ b],

defined on
V
satisfies all the axioms for
N=1
SUSY vertex algebras. The sesquilinearity, skew-symmetry and Jacobi identity of
N=1
SUSY Lie conformal algebras follow from those properties of non-SUSY Lie conformal algebras, (
5.1
) and the property
[D,D]=2{\partial}
. The quasi-commutativity, quasi-associativity and non-commutative Wick formula of
N=1
SUSY vertex algebras also follow from those properties of vertex algebras. For example, the non-commutative Wick formula of
N=1
SUSY vertex algebras holds since we can check the following computations:

\displaystyle[a\ _{\Lambda}\ bc]

\displaystyle=[Da\ _{\lambda}\ bc]+{\chi}[a\ _{\lambda}\ bc]

\displaystyle=\big(\,[Da\ _{\lambda}\ b]c+(-1)^{(p(a)+1)p(b)}b[Da\ _{\lambda}\ c]+\int_{0}^{\lambda}[[Da\ _{\lambda}\ b]\ _{\gamma}\ c]\ d\gamma\,\big)

\displaystyle\quad+{\chi}\,\big(\,[a\ _{\lambda}\ b]c+(-1)^{p(a)p(b)}b[a\ _{\lambda}\ c]+\int_{0}^{\lambda}[[a\ _{\lambda}\ b]\ _{\gamma}\ c]\ d\gamma\,\big)

\displaystyle=\big(\,[Da\ _{\lambda}\ b]c+{\chi}[a\ _{\lambda}\ b]c\,\big)+(-1)^{(p(a)+1)p(b)}\big(\,b[Da\ _{\lambda}\ c]+(-1)^{p(b)}{\chi}b[a\ _{\lambda}\ c]\,\big)

\displaystyle\quad+{\partial}_{\eta}\int_{0}^{\lambda}[D[Da\ _{\lambda}\ b]\ _{\gamma}\ c]+{\eta}[[Da\ _{\lambda}\ b]\ _{\gamma}\ c]-{\chi}[D[a\ _{\lambda}\ b]\ _{\gamma}\ c]-{\chi}{\eta}[[a\ _{\lambda}\ b]\ _{\gamma}\ c]\ d\gamma

\displaystyle=[a\ _{\Lambda}\ b]c+(-1)^{(p(a)+1)p(b)}b[a\ _{\Lambda}\ c]+\int_{0}^{\Lambda}[[a\ _{\Lambda}\ b]\ _{\Gamma}\ c]\ d\Gamma,

for the
\Lambda
-bracket in (
5.2
) and
a,b,c\in V
. Hence if there is an odd derivation
D
of the normally ordered product satisfying

and (

itself can be considered as an

SUSY vertex algebra.

SUSY vertex algebra. More concretely, the following question is the main topic we will deal with in the rest of Section

\displaystyle=({D}[{u}_{i}\ {}_{\lambda}\ {\bar{u}}_{j}])^{\circ}+{\lambda}[{\bar{u}}_{i}\ {}_{\lambda}\ {\bar{u}}_{j}]^{\circ}=-{D}^{1}([{u}_{i}\ {}_{\lambda}\ {\bar{u}}_{j}]^{\circ})+{\lambda}[{\bar{u}}_{i}\ {}_{\lambda}\ {\bar{u}}_{j}]^{\circ}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{p({u}_{i})+1}(D^{1}[{u}_{i}\ {}_{\lambda}\ {\bar{u}}^{\circ}_{j}]+{\lambda}[{\bar{u}}_{i}\ {}_{\lambda}\ {\bar{u}}^{\circ}_{j}]),

\displaystyle[{\bar{u}}_{i}\ {}_{\lambda}\ {u}^{\circ}_{j}]

\displaystyle=(-1)^{p({u}_{i})+1}[{\bar{u}}_{i}\ {}_{\lambda}\ {u}_{j}]^{\circ}=-([{u}_{i}\ {}_{\lambda}\ {\bar{u}}_{j}]-{D}[{\bar{u}}_{i}\ {}_{\lambda}\ {\bar{u}}_{j}])^{\circ}

\displaystyle=-[{u}_{i}\ {}_{\lambda}\ {\bar{u}}_{j}]^{\circ}+({D}[{\bar{u}}_{i}\ {}_{\lambda}\ {\bar{u}}_{j}])^{\circ}=-[{u}_{i}\ {}_{\lambda}\ {\bar{u}}_{j}]^{\circ}-{D}^{1}([{\bar{u}}_{i}\ {}_{\lambda}\ {\bar{u}}_{j}]^{\circ})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{p({u}_{i})+1}([{u}_{i}\ {}_{\lambda}\ {\bar{u}}^{\circ}_{j}]-D^{1}[{\bar{u}}_{i}\ {}_{\lambda}\ {\bar{u}}^{\circ}_{j}]),

which follow from the fact that
\{{\bar{u}}_{i}\}_{i{\in}I}
is a SUSY generator in the
D
–direction. Therefore, by Proposition

,

is an
N=2
SUSY vertex algebra.
∎

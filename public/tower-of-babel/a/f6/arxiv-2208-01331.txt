On the isolated calmness property of implicitly defined multifunctions
Helmut Gfrerer, Jiří V. Outrata

Abstract
The paper deals with an extension of the available theory of SCD (subspace containing derivatives) mappings to mappings between spaces of different dimensions. This extension enables us to derive workable sufficient conditions for the isolated calmness of implicitly defined multifunctions around given reference points. This stability property differs substantially from isolated calmness at a point and, possibly in conjunction with the Aubin property, offers a new useful stability concept. The application area includes a broad class of parameterized generalized equations, where the respective conditions ensure a rather strong type of Lipschitztan behavior of their solution maps.



Key words.
strong metric subregularity and isolated calmness on a neighborhood, generalized derivatives, semismoothness
∗
, implicit multifunctions.

AMS Subject classification.
65K10, 65K15, 90C33.

Introduction

Analysis of Lipschitzian stability of set-valued mappings is one of the most important parts of modern variational analysis. Above all, the notions of the Aubin and the calmness property play a central role both in parameter-dependent equilibria (especially in presence of unknown parameters) and in qualification conditions of generalized differential calculus. But also the so-called isolated calmness and (the existence of) single-valued Lipschitzian localization have a great importance, e.g., in connection with Newton-type methods for nonsmooth problems.

There are various pointwise characterizations of the above mentioned stability notions in terms of generalized derivatives as, e.g., the Mordukhovich or the Levy-Rockafellar criteria.

Recently, in connection with the so-called SCD (subspace containing derivatives) mappings and the associated SCD semismooth
∗
Newton method in
[
6
]
, the authors derived for such mappings a characterization of the strong metric subregularity on a neighborhood which amounts (
[
3
, Theorem 3I.2]
) to the isolated calmness on a neighborhood of their inverses.

Both these properties differ from their counterparts
at a point
rather substantially and one obtains thus a useful amendment to the available arsenal of regularity and stability properties. In
[
6
]
, one finds both a characterization of the strong metric subregularity on a neighborhood for general mappings and special mappings having SCD and semismooth
∗
properties. These characterizations of strong metric subregularity, however, were presented in
[
6
]
only for mappings between spaces of the same dimension. So, in order to derive workable criteria for the isolated calmness around a reference point for, say, a class of implicitly given multifunctions, the basic framework has to be extended. This extension, along with the corresponding stability results, is the aim of the present paper.

The plan of the paper is as follows. In the next section one finds a necessary background from variational analysis which is used throughout the whole paper. Section 3 contains the basic elements of the theory of SCD mappings between spaces of different dimensions. In this development one uses the corresponding part of
[
6
]
as a template. In Section 4 several calculus rules are derived which are needed in the proofs of the stability results presented in Section 5. The main statement (Theorem
5.1
) provides us with two types of conditions ensuring that the implicit multifunction, defined via the inclusion

0\in H(x,y)

possesses the
isolated calmness property on a neighborhood
of the given
reference point
. One of these conditions is based on the so-called
outer limiting graphical derivative
and works for general mappings
H
, whereas the other one is tailored to semismooth
∗
SCD mappings and is available in a primal and a dual form. To illustrate the nature of these conditions, we use a class of parameterized
generalized equations
(GEs). In case of variational inequalities with polyhedral constraint sets, we work out these conditions to an efficient form expressed in terms of faces of the critical cone to the constraint set. It appears that the specialized condition is much simpler to verify than the general one.
In addition, we present in Section 5 another condition expressed in terms of the limiting (Mordukhovich) coderivative of
H
which ensures that the respective implicit mapping has both the Aubin and the isolated calmness property around the reference point.

Background from variational analysis

Throughout the whole paper, we will frequently use the following basic notions of modern
variational analysis. All the sets under consideration are supposed to be
locally closed
around the points in question without further mentioning.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
A
be a set in
\mathbb{R}^{s}
and let
\bar{x}\in A
. Then

(i)

The tangent (contingent, Bouligand) cone to
A
at
\bar{x}
is given by

T_{A}(\bar{x}):=\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}\limits_{t\downarrow 0}\frac{A-\bar{x}}{t},

the paratingent cone to
A
at
\bar{x}
is given by

T^{P}_{A}(\bar{x}):=\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}\limits_{{\textstyle{x\mathop{\rightarrow}\limits^{A}\bar{x}\atop t\downarrow 0}}}\frac{A-x}{t}

and the outer limiting tangent cone to
A
at
\bar{x}
is defined as

T^{\sharp}_{A}(\bar{x}):=\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\mathop{\rightarrow}\limits^{A}\bar{x}}T_{A}(x)=\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{x\mathop{\rightarrow}\limits^{A}\bar{x}}\Big(\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{t\downarrow 0}\frac{A-x}{t}\Big).

(1)

(ii)

The set

\widehat{N}_{A}(\bar{x}):=\big(T_{A}(\bar{x})\big)^{\circ}

is the regular (Fréchet) normal cone to
A
at
\bar{x}
, and

N_{A}(\bar{x}):=\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}\limits_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle A}}{{x\rightarrow\bar{x}}}}\widehat{N}_{A}(x)

is the limiting (Mordukhovich) normal cone to
A
at
\bar{x}
.

In this definition ”
\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}
” stands for the Painlevé-Kuratowski
outer (upper) set limit
, see, e.g.,
[
1
]
.
The outer limiting tangent cone
T^{\sharp}_{A}(\bar{x})
was very recently defined in
[
6
]
and it is always contained in the paratingent cone
T^{P}_{A}(\bar{x})
. All the other objects from variational geometry are well-known and can be found in standard textbooks, see, e.g.,
[
15
]
.

If
A
is convex, then
\widehat{N}_{A}(\bar{x})=N_{A}(\bar{x})
amounts to the classical normal cone in
the sense of convex analysis and we will write
N_{A}(\bar{x})
.

The above listed cones enable us to describe the local behavior of set-valued maps via various
generalized derivatives. All the set-valued mappings under consideration are supposed to have
locally closed graph
around the points in question.

Definition 2.2
.

Consider a multifunction
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{m}
and let
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\mathrm{gph}\,F
.

(i)

The multifunction
DF(\bar{x},\bar{y}):\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{m}
given by
\mathrm{gph}\,DF(\bar{x},\bar{y})=T_{\mathrm{gph}\,F}(\bar{x},\bar{y})
is called the graphical derivative of
F
at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
.

(ii)

The outer limiting graphical derivative of
F
at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
is the multifunction

D^{\sharp}F(\bar{x},\bar{y}):\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{m}
given by

\mathrm{gph}\,D^{\sharp}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})=T^{\sharp}_{\mathrm{gph}\,F}(\bar{x},\bar{y}).

(iii)

The multifunction
D_{*}F(\bar{x},\bar{y}):\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{m}
given by
\mathrm{gph}\,D_{*}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})=T^{P}_{\mathrm{gph}\,F}(\bar{x},\bar{y})
is called the strict (paratingent) derivative of
F
at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
.

(iv)

The multifunction
\widehat{D}^{\ast}F(\bar{x},\bar{y}):\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{n}
defined by

\mathrm{gph}\,\widehat{D}^{\ast}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})=\{(y^{*},x^{*})\,\big|\,(x^{*},-y^{*})\in\widehat{N}_{\mathrm{gph}\,F}(\bar{x},\bar{y})\}

is called the regular (Fréchet) coderivative of
F
at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
.

(v)

The multifunction
D^{\ast}F(\bar{x},\bar{y}):\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{n}
, defined by

\mathrm{gph}\,D^{\ast}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})=\{(y^{*},x^{*})\,\big|\,(x^{*},-y^{*})\in N_{\mathrm{gph}\,F}(\bar{x},\bar{y})\}

is called the limiting (Mordukhovich) coderivative of
F
at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
.

The outer limiting graphical derivative has been introduced by the authors in
[
6
]
.

If
F
is single-valued, we can omit the second argument and write
DF(x)
,
\widehat{D}^{*}F(x),\ldots
instead of
DF\big(x,F(x)\big)
,
\widehat{D}^{*}F\big(x,F(x)\big),\ldots
. However, be aware that when considering limiting objects at
x
where
F
is not continuous, it is not enough to consider only sequences
x_{k}\to x
but we must work with sequences
\big(x_{k},F(x_{k})\big)\to\big(x,F(x)\big)
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open and consider a mapping
F:U\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
. The B-Jacobian of
F
at
x\in U
is defined as

\overline{\nabla}F(x):=\{A\,\big|\,\exists x_{k}\to x:\mbox{$F$ is Fr\'{e}chet differentiable at $x_{k}$ and }A=\lim_{k\to\infty}\nabla F(x_{k})\}.

(2)

Recall that the Clarke Generalized Jacobian is given by
{\rm conv\,}\overline{\nabla}F(x)
, i.e., the convex hull of the B-Jacobian.

There exists the following relation between the B-Jacobian and the limiting coderivative of a single-valued mapping
F
, which states that every element from the B-Jacobian defines a certain subspace contained in the graph of the coderivative.

Proposition 2.4

(
[
6
, Proposition 2.4]
)
.

Let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open and let
F:U\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a mapping. Let
F
be continuous at
x\in U
and let
A\in\overline{\nabla}F(x)
. Then

(y^{*},A^{T}y^{*})\in\mathrm{gph}\,D^{*}F(x)\ \forall y^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}.

If the mapping
F:U\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
is Lipschitz continuous, then by Rademacher’s Theorem
F
is differentiable almost everywhere in
U
and
\|\nabla F(x)\|
is bounded there by the Lipschitz constant of
F
. Thus
\overline{\nabla}F(\bar{x})\not=\emptyset
for Lipschitz continuous mappings
F
.

Let us now recall the following regularity notions.

Definition 2.5
.

Consider a mapping
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{m}
and let
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\mathrm{gph}\,F
. Then

1.

F
is said to be metrically subregular at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
if there exists
\kappa\geq 0
along with some neighborhood
U
of
\bar{x}
such that

{\rm dist}(x,F^{-1}(\bar{y}))\leq\kappa{\rm dist}(\bar{y},F(x))\ \forall x\in U.

(3)

2.

F
is said to be strongly metrically subregular at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
if there is
\kappa\geq 0
together with some neighborhood
U
of
\bar{x}
such that

\|x-\bar{x}\|\leq\kappa{\rm dist}(\bar{y},F(x))\ \forall x\in U.

(4)

3.

F
is said to be metrically regular around
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
if there is
\kappa\geq 0
together with neighborhoods
U
of
\bar{x}
and
V
of
\bar{y}
such that

{\rm dist}(x,F^{-1}(y))\leq\kappa{\rm dist}(y,F(x))\ \forall(x,y)\in U\times V.

(5)

Note that conditions (
4
) implies that
F^{-1}(\bar{y})\cap U=\{\bar{x}\}
.

Related with these regularity properties are the following Lipschitzian properties.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
S:\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a mapping and let
(\bar{y},\bar{x})\in\mathrm{gph}\,S
. Then

1.

S
is said to be calm at
(\bar{y},\bar{x})
if there exists
\kappa\geq 0
along with a neighborhood
U
of
\bar{x}
such that

S(y)\cap U\subset S(\bar{x})+\kappa\|y-\bar{y}\|{\cal B}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\ \forall y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}.

2.

S
has the isolated calmness property at
(\bar{y},\bar{x})
if
there exists
\kappa\geq 0
along with a neighborhood
U
of
\bar{x}
such that

S(y)\cap U\subset\{\bar{x}\}+\kappa\|y-\bar{y}\|{\cal B}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\ \forall y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}.

(6)

3.

S
has the Aubin property around
(\bar{y},\bar{x})
if there is some constant
\kappa\geq 0
along with neighborhoods
V
of
\bar{y}
and
U
of
\bar{x}
such that

S(y)\cap U\subset S(y^{\prime})+\kappa\|y-y^{\prime}\|{\cal B}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\ \forall y,y^{\prime}\in V.

Now the condition (
6
) defining isolated calmness ensures that
S(\bar{y})\cap U=\{\bar{x}\}
.

It is well-known, see, e.g.,
[
3
]
, that the property of (strong) metric subregularity for
F
at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
with constant
\kappa
is equivalent with the property of
(isolated) calmness
for
F^{-1}
at
(\bar{y},\bar{x})
with constant
\kappa
.
Further,
F
is metrically regular around
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
with constant
\kappa
if and only if the inverse mapping
F^{-1}
has the so-called
Aubin property
around
(\bar{y},\bar{x})
with constant
\kappa
.

The properties of metric regularity and strong metric subregularity are stable under Lipschitzian and calm perturbations, respectively, cf.
[
3
]
. Further note that the property of metric regularity holds around all points belonging to the graph of
F
sufficiently close to the reference point, whereas the property of (strong) metric subregularity is guaranteed to hold only at the reference point. This leads to the following definition.

Definition 2.7
.

1.

We say that the mapping
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{m}
is (strongly) metrically subregular around
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\mathrm{gph}\,F
if there is
\kappa\geq 0
and a neighborhood
W
of
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
such that
F
is (strongly) metrically subregular with constant
\kappa
at every point
(x,y)\in\mathrm{gph}\,F\cap W
.

In this case we will also speak about (strong) metric subregularity on a neighborhood.

2.

We say that the mapping
S:\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called (isolatedly) calm around
(\bar{y},\bar{x})\in\mathrm{gph}\,S
if there is some constant
\kappa\geq 0
along with some neighborhood
W
of
(\bar{y},\bar{x})
such that
S
is isolatedly calm with constant
\kappa
at every point
(y,x)\in\mathrm{gph}\,S\cap W
.

In this case we will also speak about (isolated) calmness on a neighborhood.

The notion of (strong) metric subregularity on a neighborhood was introduced in
[
6
, Definition 2.8]
.
Due to the relation between (strong) metric subregularity of
F
and (isolated) calmness of
F^{-1}
we immediately obtain the following result.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a mapping and let
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\mathrm{gph}\,F
. Then
F
is (strongly) metrically subregular around
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
if and only if
F^{-1}
is isolatedly calm around
(\bar{y},\bar{x})
.

Note that every polyhedral multifunction, i.e., a mapping whose graph is the union of finitely many convex polyhedral sets, is both metrically subregular and calm around every point of its graph by Robinson’s result
[
13
]
. In this paper, we will restrict our investigations to the properties of strong metric subregularity and isolated calmness on a neighborhood. Let us first have a closer look on Definition
2.7
.

The mapping
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{m}
is strongly metrically subregular around
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\mathrm{gph}\,F
if and only if there is some
\kappa\geq 0
together with some neighborhood
W
of
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
such that for every
(x,y)\in\mathrm{gph}\,F\cap W
there is some neighborhood
U_{xy}
of
x
with

{\rm dist}(x^{\prime},F^{-1}(y))\leq\kappa{\rm dist}(y,F(x^{\prime}))\ \forall x^{\prime}\in U_{xy}.

Note that the neighborhoods
U_{xy}
depends both on

amd

and can be arbitrarily small.

Similarly, the mapping
S:\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{n}
is isolatedly calm around
(\bar{y},\bar{x})\in\mathrm{gph}\,S
if and only if there is some
\kappa\geq 0
together with some neighborhood
W
of
(\bar{y},\bar{x})
such that for every
(y,x)\in\mathrm{gph}\,S\cap W
there is some neighborhood
U_{yx}
of
x
with

S(y^{\prime})\cap U_{yx}\subset\{x\}+\kappa\|y^{\prime}-y\|{\cal B}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\ \forall y^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}.

In this paper we will use the following point-based characterizations of the above regularity properties.

Theorem 2.9
.

Consider a mapping
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{m}
and let
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\mathrm{gph}\,F
. Then

(i)

(Levy-Rockafellar criterion, see, e.g.,
[

)

(\bar{x},\bar{y})
if and only if

0\in DF(\bar{x},\bar{y})(u)\ \Rightarrow u=0.

(7)

(ii)

(Mordukhovich criterion, see, e.g.,
[
11

)

is metrically regular around
(\bar{x},\bar{y})

(8)

(iii)

F

(\bar{x},\bar{y})
if and only if

0\in D^{\sharp}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})(u)\ \Rightarrow u=0.

(9)

Next we introduce the semismooth
∗
sets and mappings.

Definition 2.10
.

1.

A set
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{s}
is called semismooth
∗
at a point
\bar{x}\in A
if for every
\epsilon>0
there is some
\delta>0
such that

|\langle x^{*},x-\bar{x}\rangle|\leq\epsilon\|x-\bar{x}\|\|x^{*}\|

holds for all
x\in A\cap{\cal B}_{\delta}(\bar{x})
and all
x^{*}\in\widehat{N}_{A}(x)
.

2.

A set-valued mapping
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{m}
is called semismooth
∗
at a point
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\mathrm{gph}\,F
, if

\mathrm{gph}\,F
is semismooth
∗
at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
, i.e., for every
\epsilon>0
there is some
\delta>0
such that

|\langle x^{*},x-\bar{x}\rangle-\langle y^{*},y-\bar{y}\rangle|\leq\epsilon\|(x,y)-(\bar{x},\bar{y})\|\|(x^{*},y^{*})\|

holds for all
(x,y)\in\mathrm{gph}\,F\cap{\cal B}_{\delta}(\bar{x},\bar{y})
and all
(y^{*},x^{*})\in\mathrm{gph}\,\widehat{D}^{*}F(x,y)
.

Note that the above definitions of semismooth
∗
sets and multifunctions are not the same as the ones introduced in
[

]
, but by
[
5

they are equivalent.

We now state a sufficient condition for the semismooth
∗
property of sets with constraint structure.

Proposition 2.12
.

Let
A=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{s}\,\big|\,\Phi(x)\in D\}
, where
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{s}\to\mathbb{R}^{p}
is continuously differentiable and
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{p}
is a closed set. Given
\bar{x}\in A
, assume that the mapping
x\mapsto F(x):=\Phi(x)-D
is metrically subregular at
(\bar{x},0)
and assume that
D
is semismooth
∗
at
\Phi(\bar{x})
. Then
A
is semismooth
∗
at
\bar{x}
.

Proof.

By metric subregularity of
F
there exists a real
\kappa>0
together with some open neighborhood
U
such that (
3
) holds. It follows that for every
x\in A\cap U
the mapping
F
is metrically subregular with constant
\kappa
at
(x,0)
and thus, by
[
7
, Theorem 3]
there holds

N_{A}(x)\subset\{\nabla\Phi(x)^{T}y^{*}\,\big|\,y^{*}\in N_{D}\big(\Phi(x)\big)\cap\kappa\|x^{*}\|{\cal B}_{R^{p}}\},\ x\in A\cap U.

Since
D
is semismooth
∗
at
\Phi(\bar{x})
, by
[
5
, Proposition 3.2]
there is some radius
\rho>0
such that

|\langle y^{*},d-\Phi(\bar{x})\rangle|\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2L\kappa}\|d-\Phi(\bar{x})\|\|y^{*}\|\ \forall d\in D\cap{\cal B}_{\rho}\big(\Phi(\bar{x})\big)\ \forall y^{*}\in N_{D}(d),

where
L
denotes the Lipschitz constant of
\Phi
on some ball
B_{\delta}(\bar{x})\subset U
. Next choose
0<\bar{\delta}<\min\{\delta,\rho/L\}
such that

\|\Phi(\bar{x})-\Phi(x)-\nabla\Phi(x)(\bar{x}-x)\|\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2\kappa}\|x-\bar{x}\|,\ x\in{\cal B}_{\bar{\delta}}(\bar{x}),

and consider
x\in A\cap{\cal B}_{\bar{\delta}}(\bar{x})
and
x^{*}\in N_{A}(x)
together with
y^{*}\in N_{D}\big(\Phi(x)\big)
satisfying
\|y^{*}\|\leq\kappa\|x^{*}\|
and
x^{*}=\nabla\Phi(x)^{T}y^{*}
.
Then

\displaystyle|\langle x^{*},x-\bar{x}\rangle|

\displaystyle=|\langle y^{*},\nabla\Phi(x)(x-\bar{x})\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq|\langle y^{*},\Phi(x)-\Phi(\bar{x})\rangle|+|\langle y^{*},\Phi(\bar{x})-\Phi(x)-\nabla\Phi(x)(x-\bar{x})\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2L\kappa}\|\Phi(x)-\Phi(\bar{x})\|\|y^{*}\|+\|y^{*}\|\|\Phi(\bar{x})-\Phi(x)-\nabla\Phi(x)(\bar{x}-x)\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2L\kappa}L\|x-\bar{x}\|\kappa\|x^{*}\|+\frac{\epsilon}{2\kappa}\kappa\|x^{*}\|\|x-\bar{x}\|=\epsilon\|x-\bar{x}\|\|x^{*}\|,

verifying that
A
is semismooth
∗
at
\bar{x}
.
∎

In case of single-valued Lipschitzian mappings the semismooth
∗
property is equivalent with the semismooth property introduced by Gowda
[
8
]
, which is weaker than the one in
[
12
]
.

3
Preliminaries

This section is composed from two parts. The first one, Section 3.1, contains a generalization of the basic facts about the SCD mappings from
[
6
, Section 3]
to multifunctions between different finite-dimensional spaces. Section 3.2 is then devoted to the important notion of SCD regularity, playing a crucial role in the subsequent development.

3.1
SCD mappings

Let us denote by
{\cal Z}_{nm}
the metric space of all n-dimensional subspaces of
\mathbb{R}^{n+m}
equipped with the metric

d_{{\cal Z}_{nm}}(L_{1},L_{2}):=\|P_{1}-P_{2}\|,

(10)

where
P_{i}
is the symmetric
(n+m)\times(n+m)
matrix representing the orthogonal projection onto
L_{i},i=1,2
.
Throughout the whole paper we make use of the following relationships.

Lemma 3.1
.

(i)

Let
A_{k}
be a sequence of
(n+m)\times(n+l)
full-column-rank matrices converging to a full-column-rank matrix
A
and let
L_{k}\in{\cal Z}_{nl}
be a sequence of subspaces converging to
L\in{\cal Z}_{nl}
. Then
\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}d_{{\cal Z}_{nm}}(A_{k}L_{k},AL)=0
.

(ii)

The metric space
{\cal Z}_{nm}
is (sequentially) compact.

The above statements can be proved in the same way as their counterparts in
[
6
, Lemma 3.1(iii),(iv)]
and therefore the proofs are omitted.

To be consistent with the notation in
[
6
]
we will write
{\cal Z}_{n}
instead of
{\cal Z}_{nn}
.

With each
L\in{\cal Z}_{nm}
one can associate its
adjoint
subspace
L^{*}
defined by

L^{*}:=\{(-v^{*},u^{*})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\,\big|\,(u^{*},v^{*})\in L^{\perp}\}.

(11)

Since
\dim L^{\perp}=m
, it follows that
L^{*}\in{\cal Z}_{mn}
(i.e., its dimension is
m
). It is easy to see that

L^{*}=S_{nm}L^{\perp},\mbox{ where }S_{nm}=\left(\begin{array}[]{lc}0&-I_{m}\\
I_{n}&0\end{array}\right),

(12)

yielding
(L^{*})^{\perp}=\{z\,\big|\,S_{nm}^{T}z\in(L^{\perp})^{\perp}=L\}
. Hence we obtain

\displaystyle(L^{*})^{*}

\displaystyle=\{(-u,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\,\big|\,(v,u)\in(L^{*})^{\perp}\}=\{(-u,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\,\big|\,S_{nm}^{T}(v,u)=(u,-v)\in L\}

\displaystyle=-L=L.

(13)

Further, if we denote by
P_{L^{*}}
,
P_{L^{\perp}}
and
P_{L}
the symmetric
(n+m)\times(n+m)
matrices representing the orthogonal projections onto
L^{*}
,
L^{\perp}
and
L
, respectively, then we have
P_{L^{\perp}}=I_{n+m}-P_{L}
and, since
S_{nm}
is orthogonal,

P_{L^{*}}=S_{nm}P_{L^{\perp}}S_{nm}^{T}=I_{n+m}-S_{nm}P_{L}S_{nm}^{T}.

We conclude that for any two subspaces
L_{1},L_{2}\in{\cal Z}_{nm}
there holds

d_{{\cal Z}_{mn}}(L_{1}^{*},L_{2}^{*})=\|I_{n+m}-S_{nm}P_{L_{1}}S_{nm}^{T}-(I_{n+m}-S_{nm}P_{L_{2}}S_{nm}^{T})\|=\|P_{L_{1}}-P_{L_{2}}\|=d_{{\cal Z}_{n,m}}(L_{1},L_{2})

and thus the mapping
L\to L^{*}
is an isometry between
{\cal Z}_{nm}
and
{\cal Z}_{mn}
.
In what follows, the symbol
L^{*}
signifies both the adjoint subspace to some
L\in{\cal Z}_{nm}
as well as an arbitrary subspace from
{\cal Z}_{mn}
. This double role, however, cannot lead to a confusion.

Consider now a mapping
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

Definition 3.2
.

We say that
F
is graphically smooth of dimension
n
at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
if
T_{\mathrm{gph}\,F}(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in{\cal Z}_{nm}
. By
\mathcal{O}_{F}

we denote the subset of
\mathrm{gph}\,F
, where
F
is graphically smooth of dimension
n
.

Clearly, for
(x,y)\in\mathcal{O}_{F}
and
L=T_{\mathrm{gph}\,F}(x,y)=\mathrm{gph}\,DF(x,y)
it holds that

L^{\perp}=\hat{N}_{\mathrm{gph}\,F}(x,y)
and
L^{*}=\mathrm{gph}\,\widehat{D}^{*}F(x,y)
.

Next we introduce the four derivative-like mappings
\widehat{\mathcal{S}}F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightrightarrows{\cal Z}_{nm},\widehat{\mathcal{S}^{*}}F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightrightarrows{\cal Z}_{mn},{\mathcal{S}}F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightrightarrows{\cal Z}_{nm}
and
{\mathcal{S}}^{*}F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightrightarrows{\cal Z}_{mn}
defined by

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{S}}F(x,y):=\begin{cases}\mathrm{gph}\,DF(x,y)&\mbox{ if }(x,y)\in\mathcal{O}_{F}\\
\emptyset&\mbox{ otherwise, }\end{cases}

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{S}^{*}}F(x,y):=\begin{cases}\mathrm{gph}\,\widehat{D}^{*}F(x,y)&\mbox{ if }(x,y)\in\mathcal{O}_{F}\\
\emptyset&\mbox{ otherwise, }\end{cases}

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}F(x,y):

\displaystyle=\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}\limits_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{gph}\,F}}{{(u,v)\rightarrow(x,y)}}}\widehat{\mathcal{S}}F(u,v)

\displaystyle=\{L\in{\cal Z}_{nm}\,\big|\,\exists(x_{k},y_{k})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{O}_{F}}}{{\rightarrow}}(x,y)\mbox{ such that }\lim d_{{\cal Z}_{nm}}\big(L,\mathrm{gph}\,DF(x_{k},y_{k})\big)=0\},

and

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}^{*}}F(x,y):

\displaystyle=\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}\limits_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{gph}\,F}}{{(u,v)\rightarrow(x,y)}}}\widehat{\mathcal{S}^{*}}F(u,v)

\displaystyle=\{L^{*}\in{\cal Z}_{mn}\,\big|\,\exists(x_{k},y_{k})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{O}_{F}}}{{\rightarrow}}(x,y)\mbox{ such that }\lim d_{{\cal Z}_{mn}}\big(L^{*},\mathrm{gph}\,\widehat{D}^{*}F(x_{k},y_{k})\big)=0\}.

Both
{\mathcal{S}}F
and
{\mathcal{S}}^{*}F
constitute generalized derivatives of
F
whose elements, by virtue of the above definitions, are subspaces of the graphs of the outer limiting graphical derivative and the limiting coderivative:

\displaystyle L\subset\mathrm{gph}\,D^{\sharp}F(x,y)\subset\mathrm{gph}\,D_{*}F(x,y)\ \forall L\in{\mathcal{S}}F(x,y),

(14)

\displaystyle L^{*}\subset\mathrm{gph}\,D^{*}F(x,y)\ \forall L^{*}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{*}F(x,y).

(15)

In what follows
{\mathcal{S}}F
will be called
SC (subspace containing) limiting graphical derivative
and
{\mathcal{S}^{*}}F
will be termed
SC limiting coderivative at
(x,y)
.

Due to the isometry
L\to L^{*}
we obtain a useful mutual relationship between
\mathcal{S}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})
and
{\mathcal{S}}^{*}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})
.
It holds, namely, that

{\mathcal{S}}^{*}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})=\{L^{*}\,\big|\,L\in\mathcal{S}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})\}~\mbox{ and }~\mathcal{S}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})=\{L\,\big|\,L^{*}\in\mathcal{S}^{*}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})\},

(16)

which enables us together with (
12
) a simple conversion of the statements in terms of
L\in\mathcal{S}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})
to statements in terms of
L^{*}\in\mathcal{S}^{*}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})
and vice versa.

On the basis of
{\mathcal{S}}^{*}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})
we may now introduce the following notion playing a crucial role in the sequel.

Definition 3.3
.

A mapping

F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{m}
is said to have the SCD property at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\mathrm{gph}\,F
, provided
\mathcal{S}^{*}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})\neq\emptyset
.
F
is termed an SCD mapping if it has the SCD property at all points of
\mathrm{gph}\,F
.

By virtue of (
16
), the SCD property at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
is obviously equivalent with the condition
{\mathcal{S}}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})\not=\emptyset
.

Since we consider convergence in the compact metric space
{\cal Z}_{nm}
, we obtain readily the following result.

Lemma 3.4

(cf.
[
6
, Lemma 3.6]
)
.

A mapping
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{m}
has the SCD property at
(x,y)\in\mathrm{gph}\,F
if and only if
(x,y)\in{\rm cl\,}{\cal O}_{F}
. Further,
F
is an SCD mapping if and only if
{\rm cl\,}{\cal O}_{F}={\rm cl\,}\mathrm{gph}\,F
, i.e.,
F
is graphically smooth of dimension
n
at the points of a dense subset of its graph.

The derivatives
{\mathcal{S}}F
and
{\mathcal{S}}^{*}F
can be considered as a generalization of the B-Jacobian to multifunctions. In case of single-valued continuous mappings one has the following relationship.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open and let
f:U\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
be continuous. Then for every
x\in U
there holds

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}f(x):={\mathcal{S}}\big(x,f(x)\big)\supseteq\{{\rm rge\;}(I,A)\,\big|\,A\in\overline{\nabla}f(x)\},

(17)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}^{*}f(x):={\mathcal{S}}^{*}\big(x,f(x)\big)\supseteq\{{\rm rge\;}(I,A^{T})\,\big|\,A\in\overline{\nabla}f(x)\}.

(18)

If
f
is Lipschitz continuous near
x
, these inclusions hold with equality and
f
has the SCD property around
x
.

Proof.

We can carry over the proof of
[
6
, Lemma 3.11]
with marginal modifications.
∎

3.2
SCD regularity

For
m=n
we recall the following weakening of metric regularity tailored to SCD mappings.

Definition 3.6

(
[
6
, Definition 4.1]
)
.

A mapping
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called SCD regular around
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
, provided it has the SCD property on a neighborhood of
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
and for all
L^{*}\in\mathcal{S}^{*}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})
one has the implication

(v^{*},0)\in L^{*}\ \Rightarrow\ v^{*}=0.

(19)

It is easy to see, cf.
[
6
, Lemma 4.5]
, that implication (
19
)
is equivalent with the requirement that

(u,0)\in L\ \Rightarrow\ u=0~~\mbox{ for all }~~L\in\mathcal{S}F(\bar{x},\bar{y}).

(20)

Further we observe that SCD regularity persists on a neighborhood of
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
, cf.
[
6
, Proposition 4.8]
, and, taking into account (
15
) and the Mordukhovich criterion, it is implied by the (classical) metric regularity of
F
around
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
.

The main vehicle in our stability analysis of SCD mappings in the fifth section are the following statements taken over from
[
6
, Theorem 6.2, Corollary 6.4]
.

Theorem 3.7
.

Assume that
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{n}
is SCD regular around a point
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\mathrm{gph}\,F
. Then there is a neighborhood
\mathcal{U}
of
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
such that
F
is strongly metrically subregular at each point of
\mathrm{gph}\,F\cap\mathcal{U}
, where
F
is semismooth
∗
.

Corollary 3.8
.

Assume that
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{n}
is semismooth
∗
and has the SCD property around
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\mathrm{gph}\,F
. Then
F
is strongly metrically subregular around
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
if and only if
F
is SCD regular around
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
.

Conversely, thanks to Theorem
2.9
(iii) and (
14
), strong metric subregularity around
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
implies the SCD regularity at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
even in absence of the semismooth
∗
property. Since by virtue of
[
3
, Theorem 3H.3]

F
is strongly metrically subregular at

(\bar{x},\bar{y})
if and only if
F^{-1}
is isolatedly calm at
(\bar{y},\bar{x})
, Corollary
3.8
thus provides us with a workable characterization of isolated calmness of inverses to SCD mappings having the semismooth
∗
property.

Let us compare Corollary
3.8
with the characterization of strong metric subregularity on a neighborhood provided by Theorem
2.9
(iii). To this aim we write down relation (
9
) equivalently in the form

(u,0)\in\mathrm{gph}\,D^{\sharp}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})\ \Rightarrow\ u=0.

(21)

By taking into account (
14
) and (
20
), we see that we need not to check (
21
) for the whole graph of
D^{\sharp}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})
, but only for the part which is given by the subspaces contained in
{\mathcal{S}}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})
. It seems that for the analysis of strong metric subregularity and isolated calmness on a neighborhood of semismooth
∗
SCD mappings the outer limiting graphical derivative is much too large and contains useless parts. Moreover, it seems that the outer limiting graphical derivative is much harder to compute than the SC limiting graphical derivative.

Because of the mentioned relationship between the metric regularity and SCD regularity and Theorem
3.7
we arrive finally at the following corollary.

Corollary 3.9
.

Assume that an SCD mapping
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{n}
is metrically regular and semismooth
∗
around
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
. Then
F^{-1}
has not only the Aubin property around
(\bar{y},\bar{x})
, but it is also isolatedly calm around
(\bar{y},\bar{x})
.

4
Calculus

In this section we present some calculus rules for SCD mappings which can be useful in various situations.

Consider a mapping
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{m}
defined by

\mathrm{gph}\,F=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\,\big|\,\Phi(x,y)\in\mathrm{gph}\,Q\},

(22)

where
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{l}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a continuously differentiable function and
Q:\mathbb{R}^{l}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a closed-graph mapping.

Theorem 4.1
.

Assume that
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\mathrm{gph}\,F
,
Q
has the SCD property at
\Phi(\bar{x},\bar{y})
and the
(l+m)\times(n+m)
matrix
\nabla\Phi(\bar{x},\bar{y})
has full row rank
l+m
. Then
F
has the SCD property at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
,

{\mathcal{S}}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})=\{L\in{\cal Z}_{nm}\,\big|\,\nabla\Phi(\bar{x},\bar{y})L\in{\mathcal{S}}Q\big(\Phi(\bar{x},\bar{y})\big)\}

(23)

and

{\mathcal{S}}^{*}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})=\{L^{*}\in{\cal Z}_{mn}\,\big|\,L^{*}=S_{nm}\nabla\Phi(\bar{x},\bar{y})^{T}S^{T}_{lm}M^{*}\mbox{ with }M^{*}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{*}Q\big(\Phi(\bar{x},\bar{y})\big)\}.

(24)

Proof.

Since
\nabla\Phi(\bar{x},\bar{y})
is surjective, the mapping
\Phi
is metrically regular around
\big((\bar{x},\bar{y}),\Phi(\bar{x},\bar{y})\big)
, cf.
[
15
, Example 9.44]
. Moreover, there is an open neighborhood
\mathcal{W}
of
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
such that
\nabla\Phi(x,y)
is surjective for all
(x,y)\in\mathcal{W}
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}=\Phi(\mathcal{W})
is open. By virtue of
[
15
, Exercise 6.7]
it holds that

T_{\mathrm{gph}\,F}(x,y)=\{w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\,\big|\,\nabla\Phi(x,y)w\in T_{\mathrm{gph}\,Q}\big(\Phi(x,y)\big)\}

(25)

for all
(x,y)\in\mathrm{gph}\,F\cap\mathcal{W}
. We now claim that

{\cal O}_{Q}\cap\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}=\{\Phi(x,y)\,\big|\,(x,y)\in{\cal O}_{F}\cap\mathcal{W}\}.

(26)

Indeed, consider
(x,y)\in{\cal O}_{F}\cap\mathcal{W}
and take two tangents
q_{1},q_{2}\in T_{\mathrm{gph}\,Q}\big(\Phi(x,y)\big)
. Since
\nabla\Phi(x,y)
is surjective, there exist
w_{i}
,
i=1,2
, with
\nabla\Phi(x,y)w_{i}=q_{i}
implying
w_{i}\in T_{\mathrm{gph}\,F}(x,y)
by (
25
). Since
T_{\mathrm{gph}\,F}(x,y)
is a subspace, we have
\alpha_{1}w_{1}+\alpha_{2}w_{2}\in T_{\mathrm{gph}\,F}(x,y)

\forall\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
and consequently

\nabla\Phi(x,y)(\alpha_{1}w_{1}+\alpha_{2}w_{2})=\alpha_{1}q_{1}+\alpha_{2}q_{2}\in T_{\mathrm{gph}\,Q}\big(\Phi(x,y)\big).

Hence
T_{\mathrm{gph}\,Q}\big(\Phi(x,y)\big)
is a subspace.
From
(x,y)\in{\cal O}_{F}
we deduce that the dimension of the subspace
T_{\mathrm{gph}\,F}(x,y)
is
n
. On the other hand, by (
25
) together with the surjectivity of
\nabla\Phi(x,y)
, the dimension of
T_{\mathrm{gph}\,F}(x,y)
equals to the dimension of the subspace
T_{\mathrm{gph}\,Q}\big(\Phi(x,y)\big)
plus
(n+m)-(k+m)
, the dimension of the nullspace of
\nabla\Phi(x,y)
. Hence, the dimension of
T_{\mathrm{gph}\,Q}\big(\Phi(x,y)\big)
is
k
and
\Phi(x,y)\in{\cal O}_{Q}\cap\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
is verified.

Next, consider
z\in{\cal O}_{Q}\cap\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}
. Then we can find
(x,y)\in\mathcal{W}
such that
z=\Phi(x,y)
and using similar arguments as above, we can show that
T_{\mathrm{gph}\,F}(x,y)
is a subspace of dimension
n
implying
(x,y)\in(x,y)\in{\cal O}_{F}\cap\mathcal{W}
. Hence our claim (
26
) holds true.

Since
Q
has the SCD property at
\Phi(\bar{x},\bar{y})
, there holds
{\mathcal{S}}Q\big(\Phi(\bar{x},\bar{y})\big)\not=\emptyset
. Consider
M\in{\mathcal{S}}Q\big(\Phi(\bar{x},\bar{y})\big)
together with a sequence
z_{k}\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits^{{\cal O}_{Q}}\Phi(\bar{x},\bar{y})
such that
M_{k}:=T_{\mathrm{gph}\,Q}(z_{k})\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits^{{\cal Z}_{lm}}M
. For every
k
sufficiently large we can find
(x_{k},y_{k})\in\mathcal{W}
with
z_{k}=\Phi(x_{k},y_{k})
and, due to the metric regularity of
\Phi
,
(x_{k},y_{k})\to(\bar{x},\bar{y})
. Further,
M_{k}^{\perp}
converges in
{\cal Z}_{ml}
to
M^{\perp}
. Let
L_{k}:=T_{\mathrm{gph}\,F}(x_{k},y_{k})=\nabla\Phi(x_{k},y_{k})^{-1}M_{k}
. Here,
\nabla\Phi(x_{k},y_{k})^{-1}
denotes the inverse of the linear mapping induced by
\nabla\Phi(x_{k},y_{k})
. By our claim (
26
) we have that
L_{k}\in{\cal Z}_{nm}
and, since
L_{k}^{\perp}=\nabla\Phi(x_{k},y_{k})^{T}M_{k}^{\perp}
by
[
14
, Corollary 16.3.2]
,
L_{k}^{*}=S_{nm}\nabla\Phi(x_{k},y_{k})^{T}M_{k}^{\perp}
converges to
L^{*}:=S_{nm}\nabla\Phi(\bar{x},\bar{y})^{T}M^{\perp}=S_{nm}\nabla\Phi(\bar{x},\bar{y})^{T}S_{lm}^{T}M^{*}
by Lemma
3.1
(i). On the other hand, since
\nabla\Phi(\bar{x},\bar{y})^{T}M^{\perp}=\big(\nabla\Phi(\bar{x},\bar{y})^{-1}M)^{\perp}
, we obtain
L=\nabla\Phi(\bar{x},\bar{y})^{-1}M
. These arguments show the inclusion ”
\supset
” in (
23
) and (
24
).

In order to show the reverse inclusion, consider
L\in{\mathcal{S}}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})
together with sequences
(x_{k},y_{k})\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits^{{\cal O}_{F}}(\bar{x},\bar{y})
and
L_{k}:=T_{\mathrm{gph}\,F}(x_{k},y_{k})\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits^{{\cal Z}_{nm}}L
. By (
25
) and (
26
) together with the surjectivity of
\nabla\Phi(x_{k},y_{k})
, we obtain that
M_{k}:=\nabla\Phi(x_{k},y_{k})L_{k}=T_{\mathrm{gph}\,Q}\big(\Phi(x_{k},y_{k})\big)\in{\cal Z}_{lm}
. The metric space
{\cal Z}_{lm}
is compact and thus, after possibly passing to a subsequence, we may assume that
M_{k}
converges in
{\cal Z}_{lm}
to some
M\in{\mathcal{S}}Q\big(\Phi(\bar{x},\bar{y})\big)
. Utilizing the same arguments as before, we obtain that the sequence

L_{k}^{*}=S_{nm}\nabla\Phi(x_{k},y_{k})^{T}S_{lm}^{T}M_{k}^{*}

converges to
L^{*}=S_{nm}\nabla\Phi(\bar{x},\bar{y})^{T}S_{lm}^{T}M^{*}
and
L=\nabla\Phi(\bar{x},\bar{y})^{-1}M
. This completes the proof.
∎

As a first consequence of this theorem we derive that graphically Lipschitzian mappings have the SCD property.

Definition 4.2

(cf.
[
15
, Definition 9.66]
)
.

A mapping
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{m}
is graphically Lipschitzian of dimension
d
at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\mathrm{gph}\,F
if there is an open neighborhood
W
of
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
and a one-to-one mapping
\Phi
from
W
onto an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n+m}
with
\Phi
and
\Phi^{-1}
continuously differentiable, such that
\Phi(\mathrm{gph}\,F\cap W)
is the graph of a Lipschitz continuous mapping
f:U\to\mathbb{R}^{n+m-d}
, where
U
is an open set in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Many mappings
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{n}
, important in applications, are graphically Lischitzian of dimension
n
. As an example we mention the subdifferential mapping of prox-regular and subdifferentially continuous functions
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
, cf.
[
15
, Proposition 13.46]
.

Corollary 4.3
.

Assume that
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{m}
is graphically Lipschitzian of dimension
n
at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\mathrm{gph}\,F
. Then
F
has the SCD property at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
.

Proof.

Let
\Phi
,
W
,
U
and
f
be as in Definition
4.2
and observe that
\mathrm{gph}\,F\cap W=\{(x,y)\,\big|\,\Phi(x,y)\in\mathrm{gph}\,Q\}
, where

Q(u):=\begin{cases}\{f(u)\}&\mbox{if $u\in U$},\\
\emptyset&\mbox{else.}\end{cases}

By Lemma
3.5
,
Q
has the SCD property at
\big(\bar{u},f(\bar{u})\big):=\Phi(\bar{x},\bar{y})
and the statement follows from Theorem
4.1
.
∎

Let us now provide a calculus rule for the outer limiting tangent cone.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
be continuously differentiable, let
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a closed set and consider

C:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,\big|\,\Phi(x)\in A\}.

Then for any
\bar{x}\in C
there holds

T^{\sharp}_{C}(\bar{x})\subset\{u\,\big|\,\nabla\Phi(\bar{x})u\in T^{\sharp}_{A}\big(\Phi(\bar{x})\big)\}

(27)

If
\nabla\Phi(\bar{x})
has full row rank
m
then this inclusion holds with equality.

Proof.

By
[
15
, Theorem 6.31]
, for any
x\in C
there holds the inclusion

T_{C}(x)\subset\{u\,\big|\,\nabla\Phi(x)u\in T_{A}\big(\Phi(x)\big)\}.

(28)

Consider
u\in T^{\sharp}_{C}(\bar{x})
together with sequences
(x_{k},u_{k})\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits^{\mathrm{gph}\,T_{C}}(\bar{x},u)
. Then

(\Phi(x_{k}),\nabla\Phi(x_{k})u_{k})\to(\Phi(\bar{x}),\nabla\Phi(\bar{x})u)

and
\nabla\Phi(x_{k})u_{k}\in T_{A}\big(\Phi(x_{k})\big)
verifying
\nabla\Phi(\bar{x})u\in T^{\sharp}_{A}\big(\Phi(\bar{x})\big)
. This proves (
27
).
Now assume that
\nabla\Phi(\bar{x})
has full row rank. Then
\Phi
is metrically regular with some constant
\kappa
around
\big(\bar{x},\Phi(\bar{x})\big)
, see, e.g.,
[
15
, Example 9.44]
. In addition, we can find a neighborhood
U
of
\bar{x}
such that
\nabla\Phi(x)
has full row rank for every
x\in U
and we conclude from
[
15
, Exercise 6.7]
that inclusion (
28
) holds with equality for every
x\in U
. Consider
v\in T_{A}^{\sharp}\big(\Phi(\bar{x})\big)
together with sequences
(y_{k},v_{k})\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits^{\mathrm{gph}\,T_{A}}(\Phi(\bar{x}),v)
. By metric regularity of
\Phi
, for every
k
sufficiently large we can find
x_{k}\in\Phi^{-1}(y_{k})
with
\|x_{k}-\bar{x}\|\leq\kappa\|y_{k}-\Phi(\bar{x})\|
so that
x_{k}\to\bar{x}
and
x_{k}\in U
. Consider
u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\nabla\Phi(\bar{x})u=v
. For the pseudo-inverse
\nabla\Phi(\bar{x})^{\dagger}:=\nabla\Phi(\bar{x})^{T}\big(\nabla\Phi(\bar{x})\nabla\Phi(\bar{x})^{T}\big)^{-1}
there holds

u=\nabla\Phi(\bar{x})^{\dagger}v+\big(I-\nabla\Phi^{(}\bar{x})^{\dagger}\nabla\Phi(\bar{x})\big)u.

Since the pseudo-inverses
\nabla\Phi(x_{k})^{\dagger}
converge to
\nabla\Phi(\bar{x})^{\dagger}
, we conclude that the sequence

u_{k}:=\nabla\Phi(x_{k})^{\dagger}v_{k}+\big(I-\nabla\Phi(x_{k})^{\dagger}\nabla\Phi(x_{k})\big)u

converges to
u
. Further, since
\nabla\Phi(x_{k})u_{k}=v_{k}\in T_{A}\big(\Phi(x_{k})\big)
, we have
u_{k}\in T_{C}(x_{k})
and
u\in T^{\sharp}_{C}(\bar{x})
follows. This justifies the inclusion
T^{\sharp}_{C}(\bar{x})\supset\{u\,\big|\,\nabla\Phi(\bar{x})u\in T^{\sharp}_{A}\big(\Phi(\bar{x})\big)\}
and the proof of the proposition is complete.
∎

The next calculus rule is essential for the main stability result presented in the fifth section.

Let us consider the situation when
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{l}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
is given via

F(x):=\begin{pmatrix}G(x)\\
H(x)\end{pmatrix}

(29)

where
G:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{l}
is a
C^{1}
function and
H:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{k}
has a closed graph.

Proposition 4.5
.

Consider
(\bar{x},\bar{z})\in\mathrm{gph}\,H
. Then for the mapping
F
given by (
29
) one has:

(i)

\displaystyle T^{\sharp}_{\mathrm{gph}\,F}\big(\bar{x},(G(\bar{x}),\bar{z})\big)=\big\{\big(u,(\nabla G(\bar{x})u,w)\big)\,\big|\,(u,w)\in T^{\sharp}_{\mathrm{gph}\,H}(\bar{x},\bar{z})\}

(30)

(ii)

If
H
is semismooth
∗
at
(\bar{x},\bar{z})
, then
F
is semismooth
∗
at
\big(\bar{x},(G(\bar{x}),\bar{z})\big)
.

(iii)

Assume that
H
has the SCD property at
(\bar{x},\bar{z})
. Then
F
has the SCD property at
\big(\bar{x},(G(\bar{x}),\bar{z})\big)
and one has that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}F\big(\bar{x},(G(\bar{x}),\bar{z})\big)=\Big\{\big\{\big(u,(\nabla G(\bar{x})u,w)\big)\,\big|\,(u,w)\in M\big\}\,\Big|\,M\in{\mathcal{S}}H(\bar{x},\bar{z})\Big\},

(31)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}^{*}F\big(\bar{x},(G(\bar{x}),\bar{z})\big)=\Big\{\{\big((q^{*},w^{*}),\nabla G(\bar{x})^{T}q^{*}+u^{*}\big)\,\big|\,q^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{l},\ (w^{*},u^{*})\in M^{*}\}\,\Big|\,M^{*}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{*}H(\bar{x},\bar{z})\Big\}.

(32)

Proof.

Let
\tilde{H}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{l}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
be given by
\tilde{H}(x)=\{0\}\times H(x)
. Then

\mathrm{gph}\,F=\{(x,p,z)\,\big|\,\Phi(x,p,z)\in\mathrm{gph}\,\tilde{H}\},

where
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{l}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{l}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
is given by
\Phi(x,p,z)=(x,p-G(x),z)^{T}
.
Note that for every triple
(x,p,z)
the Jacobian

\nabla\Phi(x,p,z)=\left(\begin{matrix}I_{n}&0&0\\
-\nabla G(x)&I_{l}&0\\
0&0&I_{k}\end{matrix}\right)

Ad (i): Obviously there holds

. Thus we obtain from Proposition
4.4

that

\displaystyle T^{\sharp}_{\mathrm{gph}\,F}\big(\bar{x},(G(\bar{x}),\bar{z})\big)

\displaystyle=\Big\{\big(u,(q,w)\big)\,\Big|\,\nabla\Phi(\bar{x},G(\bar{x}),\bar{z})(u,q,w)\in T^{\sharp}_{\mathrm{gph}\,\tilde{H}}(\bar{x},0,\bar{z})\Big\}

\displaystyle=\Big\{\big(u,(q,w)\big)\,\big|\,(u,w)\in T^{\sharp}_{\mathrm{gph}\,H}(\bar{x},\bar{z}),\ q-\nabla G(\bar{x})u=0\},

yielding (
30
).

Ad (ii): Since
H
is semismooth
∗
at
(\bar{x},\bar{z})
,
\tilde{H}
is semismooth
∗
at
\big(\bar{x},(0,\bar{z})\big)
. Surjectivity of
\nabla\Phi(\bar{x},G(\bar{x}),\bar{z})
ensures that the mapping
\Phi(\cdot)-\mathrm{gph}\,\tilde{H}
is metrically regular around
\big(\bar{x},(G(\bar{x}),\bar{z})\big)
, cf.
[
15
, Example 9.44]
and therefore metrically subregular as well. Now the claimed statement follows from Proposition
2.12
.

Ad (iii): It is easy to see that
\tilde{H}
has the SCD property at
\big(\bar{x},(0,\bar{z})\big)
with

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}\tilde{H}\big(\bar{x},(0,\bar{z})\big)=\Big\{\{\big(u,(0,w)\big)\,\big|\,(u,w)\in M\}\,\Big|\,M\in{\mathcal{S}}H(\bar{x},\bar{z})\Big\},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}^{*}\tilde{H}\big(\bar{x},(0,\bar{z})\big)=\Big\{\{\big((q^{*},w^{*}),u^{*}\big)\,\big|\,q^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{l},\ (w^{*},u^{*})\in M^{*}\}\,\Big|\,M^{*}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{*}H(\bar{x},\bar{z})\Big\}.

Next we can apply Theorem
4.1
to obtain

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}F\big(\bar{x},(G(\bar{x}),\bar{z})\big)=\{\nabla\Phi(\bar{x},G(\bar{x}),\bar{z})^{-1}\tilde{M}\,\big|\,\tilde{M}\in{\mathcal{S}}\tilde{H}\big(\bar{x},(0,\bar{z})\big)\},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}^{*}F\big(\bar{x},(G(\bar{x}),\bar{z})\big)=\{S_{n(l+k)}\nabla\Phi(\bar{x},G(\bar{x}),\bar{z})^{T}S_{n(l+k)}^{T}\tilde{M}^{*}\,\big|\,\tilde{M}^{*}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{*}\tilde{H}\big(\bar{x},(0,\bar{z})\big)\}.

Straightforward calculations yield that

\nabla\Phi(\bar{x},G(\bar{x}),\bar{z})^{-1}=\left(\begin{matrix}I_{n}&0&0\\
\nabla G(\bar{x})&I_{l}&0\\
0&0&I_{k}\end{matrix}\right),\ S_{n(l+k)}\nabla\Phi(\bar{x},G(\bar{x}),\bar{z})^{T}S_{n(l+k)}^{T}=\left(\begin{matrix}I_{l}&0&0\\
0&I_{k}&0\\
\nabla G(\bar{x})^{T}&0&I_{n}\end{matrix}\right)

and formulas (

), (

5
Isolated calmness on a neighborhood of implicit multifunctions

Given a multifunction
H:\mathbb{R}^{l}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{k}
with closed graph and a point
\big((\bar{x},\bar{y}),\bar{z}\big)\in\mathrm{gph}\,H
, then the relation

\mathrm{gph}\,\Sigma=H^{-1}(\bar{z})

(33)

defines the so-called
implicit multifunction

\Sigma:\mathbb{R}^{l}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{k}
. Our aim is now to ensure a certain stability property of
\Sigma
around
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
by imposing suitable assumptions on
H
around
(\bar{x},\bar{y},\bar{z})
. Usually one puts
\bar{z}=0
so that

\mathrm{gph}\,\Sigma=\{(x,y)\,\big|\,0\in H(x,y)\}.

(34)

It is easy to see that any stability property of
\Sigma
around
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
is inherited by the same stability property of the inverse to the “extended” mapping
F:\mathbb{R}^{l+k}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{l+k}
given by

F(x,y)=\begin{pmatrix}x\\
H(x,y)\end{pmatrix}

(35)

around
\big((\bar{x},\bar{y}),(\bar{x},0)\big)
. In fact, in this way, e.g., the classical Implicit Function Theorem or the Clarke Implicit Function Theorem have been proved. Alternatively, one can combine a suitable characterization of the examined property in terms of a generalized derivative with the available calculus, as shown, e.g., in
[
10
, Section 4.3]
or
[
4
, Section 4]
in case of the Aubin property. In our approach we will use the mapping (
35
) along with Theorem
2.9
(iii) and Corollary
3.8
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Consider the inclusion
0\in H(x,y)
and a point
\big((\bar{x},\bar{y}),0\big)\in\mathrm{gph}\,H
. Then any of the following two conditions ensures the isolated calmness property of the respective implicit solution map
\Sigma
around
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
.

(i)

\displaystyle 0\in D^{\sharp}H\big((\bar{x},\bar{y}),0\big)(0,v)\ \Rightarrow\ v=0.

(36)

(ii)

The mapping
H
has both the SCD property and the semismooth
∗
property around
\big((\bar{x},\bar{y}),0\big)
and either the implication

\big((0,v),0\big)\in L\ \Rightarrow\ v=0

(37)

holds for all
L\in\mathcal{S}H\big((\bar{x},\bar{y}),0\big)
, or, equivalently, the implication

\big(w^{*},(u^{*},0)\big)\in L^{*}\ \Rightarrow\ w^{*}=0,\ u^{*}=0

(38)

holds for all
L^{*}\in\mathcal{S}^{*}H\big((\bar{x},\bar{y}),0\big)
.

Proof.

In the first case we conclude from Proposition
4.5
(i) that the mapping
F
given by (
35
) fulfills

D^{\sharp}F\big((\bar{x},\bar{y}),(\bar{x},0)\big)(u,v)=\{(u,w)\,\big|\,w\in D^{\sharp}H\big((\bar{x},\bar{y}),0\big)(u,v)\}.

Thus it follows from Theorem
2.9
(iii) that condition (
36
) is equivalent with strong metric subregularity of
F
around
\big((\bar{x},\bar{y}),(\bar{x},0)\big)
and the claimed isolated calmness of
\Sigma
around
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
follows.

In the second case, note that by Proposition
4.5
(ii), (iii) the mapping
F
has the SCD property around
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
and is semismooth
∗
around
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
. Further we have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}F\big((\bar{x},\bar{y}),(0,0)\big)=\Big\{\{\big((u,v),(u,w)\big)\,\big|\,\big((u,v),w\big)\in L\}\,\Big|\,L\in{\mathcal{S}}H\big((\bar{x},\bar{y}),0\big)\Big\},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}^{*}F\big((\bar{x},\bar{y}),(0,0)\big)=\Big\{\{\big((q^{*},w^{*}),(q^{*}+u^{*},v^{*})\big)\,\big|\,q^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{l},\ \big(w^{*},(u^{*},v^{*})\big)\in L^{*}\}\,\Big|\,L^{*}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{*}H\big((\bar{x},\bar{y}),0\big)\Big\}.

Implications (
20
) and (
19
) now yield conditions (
37
), (
38
) which, by Corollary
3.8
, are equivalent with the strong metric subregularity of
F
around
\big((\bar{x},\bar{y}),(\bar{x},0)\big)
. The proof is complete.
∎

Theorem
5.1
can well be applied to parameterized GEs. To this aim consider the case when
H:\mathbb{R}^{l}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{k}
is given via

H(x,y):=f(x,y)+Q(x,y),

(39)

where
x\in\mathbb{R}^{l}
is the
perturbation parameter
,
y\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
is the
decision variable
,
f:\mathbb{R}^{l}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{k}
is continuously differentiable and
Q:\mathbb{R}^{l}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{k}
has a closed graph.

Proposition 5.2
.

Consider the reference point
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in H^{-1}(0)
and assume that one of the following conditions hold true:

(i)

\displaystyle 0\in\nabla_{y}f(x,y)v+D^{\sharp}Q\big((\bar{x},\bar{y}),-f(\bar{x},\bar{y})\big)(0,v)\ \Rightarrow\ v=0.

(40)

(ii)

Q
has the SCD property around
\big((\bar{x},\bar{y}),-f(\bar{x},\bar{y})\big)
and is semismooth
∗
on a neighborhood of
\big((\bar{x},\bar{y}),-f(\bar{x},\bar{y})\big)
and either one of the implications

\big((0,v),-\nabla_{y}f(\bar{x},\bar{y})v\big)\in M\ \Rightarrow\ v=0~\mbox{ for all }~M\in{\mathcal{S}}Q\big((\bar{x},\bar{y}),-f(\bar{x},\bar{y})\big)

(41)

and

\big(w^{*},(u^{*},-\nabla_{y}f(\bar{x},\bar{y})^{T}w^{*})\big)\in M^{*}\ \Rightarrow\ w^{*}=0,\ u^{*}=0~\mbox{ for all }~M^{*}\in\mathcal{S}^{*}Q\big((\bar{x},\bar{y}),-f(\bar{x},\bar{y})\big)

(42)

holds true.

Then the respective solution mapping
\Sigma:\mathbb{R}^{l}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{k}
is isolatedly calm around
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
.

Proof.

Clearly

\mathrm{gph}\,H=\{\big((x,y),z\big)\in\mathbb{R}^{l}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}\,\big|\,\Phi\big((x,y),z\big)\in\mathrm{gph}\,Q\}\mbox{ with }\Phi\big((x,y),z\big)=\big((x,y),z-f(x,y)\big)^{T}

so that we can apply Proposition
4.4
and Theorem
4.1
to obtain

\displaystyle T^{\sharp}_{\mathrm{gph}\,H}\big((\bar{x},\bar{y}),0\big)=\nabla\Phi\big((\bar{x},\bar{y}),0\big)^{-1}T^{\sharp}_{\mathrm{gph}\,Q}\big(\Phi\big((\bar{x},\bar{y}),0\big)\big),

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}H\big((\bar{x},\bar{y}),0\big)=\nabla\Phi\big((\bar{x},\bar{y}),0\big)^{-1}{\mathcal{S}}Q\big(\Phi\big((\bar{x},\bar{y}),0\big)\big),

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}^{*}H\big((\bar{x},\bar{y}),0\big)=\{L^{*}\in{\cal Z}_{k(l+k)}\,\big|\,L^{*}=S_{(l+k)k}\nabla\Phi\big((\bar{x},\bar{y}),0\big)^{T}S^{T}_{(l+k)k}M^{*}\mbox{ with }M^{*}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{*}Q\big(\Phi\big((\bar{x},\bar{y}),0\big)\big)\}.

Straightforward calculations yield

\nabla\Phi\big((\bar{x},\bar{y}),0\big)^{-1}=\left(\begin{matrix}I_{l+k}&0\\
\nabla f(\bar{x},\bar{y})&I_{k}\end{matrix}\right),\quad S_{(l+k)k}\nabla\Phi\big((\bar{x},\bar{y}),0\big)^{T}S^{T}_{(l+k)k}=\left(\begin{matrix}I_{k}&0\\
\nabla f(\bar{x},\bar{y})^{T}&I_{l+k}\end{matrix}\right)

and we arrive at the formulas

\displaystyle D^{\sharp}H\big((\bar{x},\bar{y}),0\big)(u,v)=\{\nabla_{x}f(\bar{x},\bar{y})u+\nabla_{y}f(\bar{x},\bar{y})v+w\,\big|\,w\in D^{\sharp}Q\big((\bar{x},\bar{y}),-f(\bar{x},\bar{y})\big)(u,v)\},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}H\big((\bar{x},\bar{y}),0\big)=\Big\{\{\big((u,v),\nabla_{x}f(\bar{x},\bar{y})u+\nabla_{y}f(\bar{x},\bar{y})v+w\big)\,\big|\,\big((u,v),w\big)\in M\}\,\Big|\,M\in{\mathcal{S}}Q\big((\bar{x},\bar{y}),-f(\bar{x},\bar{y})\big)\Big\}

and

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}^{*}H\big((\bar{x},\bar{y}),0\big)

\displaystyle=\Big\{\big\{\big(w^{*},(\nabla_{x}f(\bar{x},\bar{y})^{T}w^{*}+u^{*},\nabla_{y}f(\bar{x},\bar{y})^{T}w^{*}+v^{*})\big)\,\big|\,(w^{*},(u^{*},v^{*}))\in M^{*}\big\}\,\Big|\,M^{*}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{*}Q\big((\bar{x},\bar{y}),-f(\bar{x},\bar{y})\big)\Big\}.

Conditions (
36
), (
37
), (
38
) thus read as

\displaystyle\left.\begin{array}[]{l}u=0,\ \nabla_{x}f(\bar{x},\bar{y})u+\nabla_{y}f(\bar{x},\bar{y})v+w=0\\
w\in D^{\sharp}Q\big((\bar{x},\bar{y}),-f(\bar{x},\bar{y})\big)(u,v)\end{array}\right\}\Rightarrow v=0,

\displaystyle\left.\begin{array}[]{l}u=0,\ \nabla_{x}f(\bar{x},\bar{y})u+\nabla_{y}f(\bar{x},\bar{y})v+w=0\\
\big((u,v),w\big)\in M\end{array}\right\}\Rightarrow v=0,\ M\in{\mathcal{S}}Q\big((\bar{x},\bar{y}),-f(\bar{x},\bar{y})\big),

\displaystyle\left.\begin{array}[]{l}\nabla_{y}f(\bar{x},\bar{y})^{T}w^{*}+v^{*}=0\\
\big(w^{*},(u^{*},v^{*})\big)\in M^{*}\end{array}\right\}\Rightarrow w^{*}=0,\nabla_{x}f(\bar{x},\bar{y})^{T}w^{*}+u^{*}=0,\ M^{*}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{*}Q\big((\bar{x},\bar{y}),-f(\bar{x},\bar{y})\big),

which are equivalent to (
40
), (
41
) and (
42
). This completes the proof.

Recall that for SCD mappings
Q
having the semismooth
∗
property any of the three conditions (
40
), (
41
) and (
42
) is equivalent to the strong metric subregularity on a neighborhood of the mapping
F
given by (
35
) and thus the conditions (
40
), (
41
) and (
42
) are equivalent. Whereas
(
42
) is a dual formulation of (
41
), conditions (
40
) and (
41
) might look quite different. Let us shed light on this issue by the following application of Proposition
5.2
to parameterized variational inequalities with polyhedral constraint sets.

Consider the GE

0\in H(x,y):=f(x,y)+N_{D}\big(g(x,y)\big),

(43)

where
f,g:\mathbb{R}^{l}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
are continuously differentiable and
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
is a convex polyhedral set, and let
0\in H(\bar{x},\bar{y})
. In what follows we denote by

{\cal K}_{D}(d,d^{*}):=T_{D}(d)\cap[d^{*}]^{\perp},\ (d,d^{*})\in\mathrm{gph}\,N_{D}

the critical cone to
D
at
d
for
d^{*}
.

Our further development makes use of the following statements.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
be a convex polyhedral set. Then the normal cone mapping
N_{D}(\cdot)
is an SCD mapping, which is semismooth
∗
at every point of its graph. Further, for every point
(d,d^{*})\in\mathrm{gph}\,N_{D}
there holds

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}N_{D}(d,d^{*})

\displaystyle={\mathcal{S}}^{*}N_{D}(d,d^{*})=\{({\cal F}-{\cal F})\times({\cal F}-{\cal F})^{\perp}\,\big|\,\mbox{${\cal F}$ is face of ${\cal K}_{D}(d,d^{*})$}\},

(44)

and the outer limiting tangent cone
T^{\sharp}_{\mathrm{gph}\,N_{D}}(d,d^{*})
is the union of all sets
\mathrm{gph}\,N_{{\cal F}_{1}-{\cal F}_{2}}
, where
{\cal F}_{1},{\cal F}_{2}
are closed faces of
{\cal K}_{D}(d,d^{*})
with
{\cal F}_{2}\subset{\cal F}_{1}
.

Proof.

Since the normal cone mapping
N_{D}
is the subdifferential mapping of the convex lsc function
\delta_{D}
, it is an SCD mapping by
[
6
, Corollary 3.28]
. Further, since
\mathrm{gph}\,N_{D}
is the union of finitely many convex polyhedral sets,
N_{D}
is semismooth
∗
at every point of its graph by Propsoition
2.11
. Formula (
44
) can be found in
[
6
, Example 3.29]
and there remains to show the representation of
T^{\sharp}_{\mathrm{gph}\,N_{D}}(d,d^{*})
.

For any
(d^{\prime},d^{\prime*})\in\mathrm{gph}\,N_{D}
there holds
T_{\mathrm{gph}\,N_{D}}=\mathrm{gph}\,N_{{\cal K}_{D}(d^{\prime},d^{\prime*})}
, cf.
[
3
, Lemma 2E4]
. Further, by the Critical Superface Lemma
[
3
, Lemma 4H.2]
, for every sufficiently small neighborhood
W
of
(d,d^{*})
the collection of all critical cones
{\cal K}_{D}(d^{\prime},d^{\prime*})
,
(d^{\prime},d^{\prime*})\in\mathrm{gph}\,N_{D}\cap W
coincides with the collection of the so-called critical superfaces
{\cal F}_{1}-{\cal F}_{2}
, where
{\cal F}_{1},{\cal F}_{2}
are faces of the critical cone
{\cal K}_{D}(d,d^{*})
with
{\cal F}_{2}\subset{\cal F}_{1}
. Now consider a quadruple
\big((d,d^{*}),(e,e^{*})\big)
satisfying the relation
(e,e^{*})\in T^{\sharp}_{\mathrm{gph}\,N_{D}}(d,d^{*})
together with sequences
\big((d_{k},d_{k}^{*}),(e_{k},e_{k}^{*})\big)\to\big((d,d^{*}),(e,e^{*})\big)
with
(e_{k},e_{k}^{*})\in T_{\mathrm{gph}\,N_{D}}(d_{k},d_{k}^{*})
. Since the convex polyhedral set
D
has only finitely many faces, after possibly passing to a subsequence we can assume that there are two faces
{\cal F}_{1},{\cal F}_{2}
of
{\cal K}_{D}(d,d^{*})
with
{\cal F}_{2}\subset{\cal F}_{1}
such that
{\cal K}_{D}(d_{k},d_{k}^{*})={\cal F}_{1}-{\cal F}_{2}
for all
k
. Thus,
(e_{k},e_{k}^{*})\in T_{\mathrm{gph}\,N_{D}}(d_{k},d_{k}^{*})=\mathrm{gph}\,N_{{\cal K}_{D}(d_{k},d_{k}^{*})}=\mathrm{gph}\,N_{{\cal F}_{1}-{\cal F}_{2}}
for all
k
and
(e,e^{*})\in\mathrm{gph}\,N_{{\cal F}_{1}-{\cal F}_{2}}
follows. Conversely, let
{\cal F}_{2}\subset{\cal F}_{1}
be two faces of
{\cal K}_{D}(d,d^{*})
and let
(e,e^{*})\in\mathrm{gph}\,N_{{\cal F}_{1}-{\cal F}_{2}}
. Then there exists some sequence
(d_{k},d_{k}^{*})\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits^{\mathrm{gph}\,N_{D}}(d,d^{*})
with

Proposition 5.5
.

In the setting of (
43

\big((\bar{x},\bar{y}),0\big)\in\mathrm{gph}\,H

(i)

The mapping

The implication

{\cal F}

6
Conclusion

As explained in Corolary 4.3, graphically Lipschitzian mappings of dimension
n
are SCD mappings for which both SC limiting derivatives can be computed. For GEs with such multi-valued parts thus the respective conditions (
41
) and (
42
) can be used in a large number of parameterized GEs corresponding, e.g., to variational inequalities of the 2nd kind, hemivariational inequalities or implicit complementarity problems.

In
[
6
]
one finds also a relationship between SCD and strong metric regularity. This indicates that in some cases the SC limiting derivatives could be used also to ensure that an implicitly defined mapping has a single-valued and Lipschitzian localization around the reference point. This task we postpone to a future research.

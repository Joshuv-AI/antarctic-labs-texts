Construction of graphs being determined by their generalized Q Q -spectra
Gui-Xian Tian, Jun-Xing Wu, Shu-Yu Cui, Hui-Lu Sun

Abstract
Given a graph G G on n n vertices, its adjacency matrix and degree diagonal matrix are represented by A ⁡ ( G ) A(G) and D ⁡ ( G ) D(G) , respectively. The Q Q -spectrum of G G consists of all the eigenvalues of its signless Laplacian matrix Q ⁡ ( G ) = A ⁡ ( G ) + D ⁡ ( G ) Q(G)=A(G)+D(G) (including the multiplicities). A graph G G is known as being determined by its generalized Q Q -spectrum ( D ​ G ​ Q ​ S DGQS for short) if, for any graph H H , H H and G G have the same Q Q -spectrum and so do their complements, then H H is isomorphic to G G . In this paper, we present a method to construct D ​ G ​ Q ​ S DGQS graphs. More specifically, let the matrix W Q ​ ( G ) = [ e , Q ​ e , … , Q n − 1 ​ e ] W_{Q}(G)=\left[e,Qe,\dots,Q^{n-1}e\right] ( e e denotes the all-one column vector ) be the Q Q -walk matrix of G G . It is shown that G ∘ P k G\circ P_{k} ( k = 2 , 3 k=2,3 ) is D ​ G ​ Q ​ S DGQS if and only if G G is D ​ G ​ Q ​ S DGQS for some specific graphs. This also provides a way to construct D ​ G ​ Q ​ S DGQS graphs with more vertices by using D ​ G ​ Q ​ S DGQS graphs with fewer vertices. At the same time, we also prove that G ∘ P 2 G\circ P_{2} is still D ​ G ​ Q ​ S DGQS under specific circumstances. In particular, on the basis of the above results, we obtain an infinite sequences of D ​ G ​ Q ​ S DGQS graphs G ∘ P k t G\circ P_{k}^{t} ( k = 2 , 3 ; t ≥ 1 k=2,3;t\geq 1 ) for some specific D ​ G ​ Q ​ S DGQS graph G G . Introduction

All graphs in this paper are simple, that is, finite undirected graphs without multiple edges and loops. For a graph

Given a graph

and copying
n
graphs

i

and the rooted vertex

[
2

of graph

Two graphs are cospectral if they have the same spectrum. A graph
G
is known as being determined by its spectrum (
DS
for short) if, for any graph
H
,
H
and

have the same spectrum, then

is isomorphic to
G
. The generalized spectrum of
G
is the spectrum of
G
and the spectrum of its complement graph
\overline{G}
, where
\overline{G}
is the complement of
G
satisfying
A(\overline{G})=J-I-A(G)
.

“Which graphs are
DS
?” is an interesting question in graph theory. The problem goes back sixty years and is rooted in chemical theory (see
[
3
]
). Another similar question was also raised by Kay in
[
4
]
: “Can one hear the shape of a drum?” This problem simulates the sound of the drum as the eigenvalue of the graph and the shape of the drum as the graph. However, it turns out that determining whether a graph is
DS
is a difficult problem.

It is well-known that
DS
graphs are very few and have special structural properties. Wang and Xu in
[
5
,
6
]
considered the above problem relative to the generalized spectrum determined in the controllable graph, where a graph
G
is controllable if and only if the walk-matrix
W(G)=\left[e,Ae,\dots,A^{n-1}e\right]
is non-singular. A graph
G
is known as being determined by its generalized spectrum (
DGS
for short) if, for any graph
H
,
H
and
G
have the same spectrum and so do their complements, then
H
is isomorphic to
G
. In
2006
, they also proposed a simple arithmetic condition to prove that a class of graphs is
DGS
in which
G
satisfies
\frac{{\rm det}(W(G))}{2^{\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor}}
is odd and square-free. Recently, some families of
DGS
graphs were constructed by using the rooted product operation in
[
1
,
8
]
. More specifically, it was proved that the rooted product
G\circ P_{k}
(
k=2,3,4
) is
DGS
when the graph
G
satisfies
{\rm det}W(G)=\pm 2^{\frac{n}{2}}
(
n
is even) and
a_{0}=\pm 1
(where
a_{0}
is the constant term with respect to the characteristic polynomial of
G
). In
[
7
]
, the authors also presented a similar simple arithmetic condition to prove that a class of graphs is determined by its generalized
Q
-spectrum (
DGQS
for short) in which
G
satisfies
\frac{{\rm det}(W_{Q}(G))}{2^{\left\lfloor\frac{3n-2}{2}\right\rfloor}}
is odd and square-free. In this paper, we follow this research direction and explore how to construct
DGQS
graphs.

The contributions of this article are as follows:

(1)

We first establish an important determinant relationship between
W_{Q}(G)
and
W_{Q}(G\circ P_{k})
(

) as follows

{\rm det}W_{Q}(G\circ P_{2})=\pm a_{0}({\rm det}W_{Q}(G))^{2},~{\rm det}W_{Q}(G\circ P_{3})=\pm a_{0}^{2}({\rm det}W_{Q}(G))^{3},

(1)

where

is the constant term with respect to the characteristic polynomial of the matrix

.

(2)

Based on the results above, we prove that the rooted product graph

(

) is

when
{\rm det}W_{Q}(G)=\pm 2^{\frac{3n-2}{2}}
(

. In particular, we also prove that each graph in

(

(3)

We study a family of graph

is odd and square-free. We show that the rooted product graph

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we present some preliminary results. In Section 3, we give a relationship between

Differentiable approximation of continuous definable maps that preserves the image
Antonio Carbone

Abstract
. Recently Pawłucki showed that compact sets that are definable in some o-minimal structure admit triangulations of class 𝒞 p {\mathcal{C}}^{p} for each integer p ≥ 1 p\geq 1 . In this work, we make use of these new techniques of triangulation to show that all continuous definable maps between compact definable sets can be approximated by differentiable maps without changing their image. The argument is an interplay between o-minimal geometry and PL geometry and makes use of a ‘surjective definable version’ of the finite simplicial approximation theorem that we prove here.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary: 14P10, 57Q55; Secondary: 03C64, 32B20, 57R35

1.
Introduction

Approximation of continuous maps is an important tool in many areas of mathematics. In particular, in geometry the possibility of approximating continuous maps by a dense subclass of maps with a better behaviour often allows a deeper and better understanding of many properties. A famous example is the classical Weierstrass approximation theorem:
Every continuous map
f:X\to{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
, defined on a compact subset
X
of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, can be uniformly approximated on
X
by polynomial maps.
Here, the approximating maps take values in the Euclidean space
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
, but several difficulties arise when one tries to restrict the image of the approximating map to a fixed target space
Y\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
. For instance, there exist no nonconstant polynomial maps from the sphere
{\mathbb{S}}^{2}
to the circle
{\mathbb{S}}^{1}

[
BCR
, Thm.13.1.9]
.

A deep problem in real algebraic geometry regards the possibility of approximating continuous (or smooth) maps between nonsingular real algebraic sets by regular maps. Besides the Weierstrass approximation theorem quoted above, where the target space
Y
is an Euclidean space, determining when a continuous map between real algebraic sets can be approximated by a regular map is a hard problem. For instance, except for the cases
m=1,2,4
studied mainly by Bochnak and Kucharz
[
BK
,
BK2
,
BK3
]
, it is not known whether a continuous map between a sphere
{\mathbb{S}}^{n}
of dimension
n
and a sphere
{\mathbb{S}}^{m}
of dimension
m
could be approximated by a regular map or not. We refer the reader to
[
BCR
, §12, §13.3]
and the survey
[
BK4
]
for further informations about this fascinating problem. It is worthwhile to remark that compact real algebraic sets with at least two points do not have tubular neighbourhoods with regular retractions
[
Gh
, Thm.2]
. If the target space
Y
is ‘generic’ in a suitable sense, then regular maps from a compact nonsingular real algebraic set
X
to
Y
are ‘very few’, see
[
Gh2
,
Gh3
]
. This lack of regular maps seems to be one of the main difficulties for an extension of the Nash-Tognoli algebraization techniques
[
T
]
from smooth manifolds to singular polyhedra, see
[
AKi
,
GhT
]
.

To overcome these difficulties one can consider, instead of polynomials or regular maps, a more flexible class of approximating maps like differentiable maps. Let
X\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
Y\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
be sets and
p\geq 1
a positive integer. A map
f:X\to Y
is
of class
{\mathcal{C}}^{p}
if it admits an extension
\widehat{f}:\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
, defined on an open neighbourhood
\Omega
of
X
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, which is of class
{\mathcal{C}}^{p}
in the usual sense. A classical example of differentiable approximation concerns Whitney’s approximation theorem
[
W
]
for continuous maps whose target space is a
{\mathcal{C}}^{p}
submanifold of
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
. Here, a crucial point is the existence of
{\mathcal{C}}^{p}
tubular neighbourhoods for
{\mathcal{C}}^{p}
submanifolds in
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
. When the target space
Y
has ‘singularities’, the lack of existence of tubular neighbourhoods (of class
{\mathcal{C}}^{p}
for
p\geq 1
), makes the approximation problem difficult to approach in its full generality. One possible way to proceed is to consider domains of definitions and target spaces in some suitable tame category. For instance, Fernando and Ghiloni
[
FG2
]
conducted an extended study on differentiable approximation for continuous maps when the target space
Y
admits suitable triangulations. They showed that differentiable approximation is possible for a wide class of triangulable sets including differentiable manifolds, polyhedra, semialgebraic sets, subanalytic sets and more generally definable sets of an o-minimal structure.

In this work we focus on differentiable approximation for continuous maps that are definable in an o-minimal structure. An
o-minmal structure
(on the ordered field of real numbers
{\mathbb{R}}
) is a collection
\mathfrak{S}:=\{\mathfrak{S}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
of families of subsets of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
satisfying the following properties:

•

\mathfrak{S}_{n}
is a Boolean algebra,

•

\mathfrak{S}_{n}
contains the algebraic subsets of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,

•

if
X\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
and
Y\in\mathfrak{S}_{m}
, then
X\times Y\in\mathfrak{S}_{n+m}
,

•

if
\pi:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is the projection onto the first factor and
X\in\mathfrak{S}_{n+1}
, then
\pi(X)\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
,

•

\mathfrak{S}_{1}
consists exactly of all the finite unions of points and intervals (of any type).

The elements of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
are called
definable subsets of

{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. A map
f:X\to Y
, between a definable subset
X\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and a definable subset
Y\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
, is a
definable map
if its graph
\Gamma_{f}
is a definable subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n+m}
. As a consequence of the Tarski-Seidenberg theorem
[
BCR
, Thm.2.2.1]
, semialgebraic sets constitute an o-minimal structure, which is the ‘smallest’ o-minimal structure, in the sense that it is contained in any other o-minimal structure. In particular, semialgebraic sets and maps are definable in any o-minimal structure. The collection of global subanalytic sets is precisely the collection of definable sets in the o-minimal structure
{\mathbb{R}}_{\text{an}}
(see
[
Wi
]
). We refer the reader to
[
Co
,
vD
,
vDM
]
for further information on the theory of o-minimal structures. For the rest of this article, even if not explicitly mentioned, when we refer to definable sets or definable maps we mean definable in a fixed o-minimal structure.

Let
X\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a compact set and
Y\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
any set. Given a continuous map
f:X\to Y
we denote by
\|f\|
the uniform norm of
f
, that is

\|f\|:=\max_{x\in X}\{|f(x)|_{m}:x\in X\},

where
|\cdot|_{m}
denotes the Euclidean norm of
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
. We denote by
{\mathcal{C}}^{0}(X,Y)
the set of all continuous maps from
X
to
Y
endowed with the compact-open topology. A fundamental system of open neighbourhoods of
f\in{\mathcal{C}}^{0}(X,Y)
is given by the sets

\mathcal{N}(f,\varepsilon):=\{g\in{\mathcal{C}}^{0}(X,Y):\|f-g\|<\varepsilon\},

where
\varepsilon>0
is any strictly positive number. Let
p\geq 1
be an integer and assume that the sets
X\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
Y\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
are definable. A definable map
f:X\to Y
is
of class
{\mathcal{C}}^{p}
if it admits a definable extension
\widehat{f}:\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
of class
{\mathcal{C}}^{p}
defined on an open definable neighbourhood
\Omega
of
X
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.

In
[
FG2
]
the authors investigated differential approximation for continuous maps in a very general context and their results do not take into account whether the involved continuous map
f
is definable or not. Therefore, it is not ensured that the approximating map is definable in case that the map
f
is definable. When one deals with maps that are definable in an o-minimal structure it is natural to wonder if it is possible to preserve this additional structure after the approximation. The general problem is the following:

Problem 1.1

(Definable approximation problem)
.

Let
p\geq 1
be an integer. Determine which definable subsets
Y\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
have the following property: For each
\varepsilon>0
, each compact definable subset
X
of each Euclidean space
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and each continuous definable map
f:X\to Y
, there exists a definable map
g:X\to Y
of class
{\mathcal{C}}^{p}
such that
\|f-g\|<\varepsilon
.

In
[
FG
]
the authors focused on the semialgebraic case. Even if not explicitly mentioned in the paper, the results of
[
FG
]
hold true (with the obvious modifications to the proofs) in every o-minimal structure. In particular, as a consequence of the existence of
{\mathcal{C}}^{1}
-triangulations for definable sets
[
OS
]
, they showed that Problem
1.1
has a positive answer for each definable set
Y
in the case
p=1
, see
[
FG
, Thm.1.3]
. Recently, Pawłucki
[
P1
,
P2
]
improved the results of
[
OS
]
, showing that definable sets admit triangulations of class
{\mathcal{C}}^{p}
for each
p\geq 1
, see §
2.2
. As an application of his remarkable results and
[
FG
, Cor.1.5]
, he provided a complete answer to Problem
1.1
for every integer
p\geq 1
, see also the proof of
[
FG
, Thm.1.4]
.

Theorem 1.2

(
[
P1
, Thm.9.2]
)
.

Let
X
be a compact definable subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
Y
a definable subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
and
f:X\to Y
a continuous definable map. Let
\varepsilon>0
and let
p\geq 1
be an integer. Then, there exists a definable map
g:X\to Y
of class
{\mathcal{C}}^{p}
such that
\|f-g\|<\varepsilon
and
g(X)\subset f(X)
.

For a general target space
Y
, the previous theorem does not hold true for
p=\infty
. Already in the semialgebraic case, the problem of determining for which semialgebraic sets
Y\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
it is possible to approximate a continuous semialgebraic map with values in
Y
by a Nash map (i.e. a
{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}
semialgebraic map) is a very difficult task. Shiota
[
Sh
]
showed that continuous semialgebraic maps can be approximated by Nash maps when the target space
Y
is an affine Nash manifold (i.e. a semialgebraic subset of some
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
that is also a smooth submanifold). As a consequence of Theorem
1.2
and the results of Baro, Fernando and Ruiz
[
BFR
, Thm.1.7]
, it holds that a continuous semialgebraic map between compact Nash sets with only normal crossings (and more generally monomial) singularities and which preserves the irreducible components can be approximated by a Nash map. We refer the reader to
[
BFR
, §1]
for the precise definitions and further information. Recently, we showed
[
CF
, Thm.1.12]
that Nash approximation for continuous semialgebraic maps is possible when the target space is an affine Nash manifold with (divisorial) corners. An
affine Nash manifold with corners
is a semialgebraic subset of some
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
that is also a submanifold with corners (see
[
Jo
]
for further information about manifolds with corners). Besides these cases, very little is known about Nash approximation of continuous semialgebraic maps. The main difficulty lies in the fact that Nash maps are analytic and of algebraic nature
[
BCR
, Prop.8.1.8]
, so one cannot use the standard tools in approximation theory, typical of the smooth category, like partitions of unity or integration of vector fields. The following example shows that, in general, a continuous semialgebraic map cannot be approximated by Nash maps:

Example 1.3
.

Let
X:=[0,1]
and
Y:=\{{\tt x}{\tt y}=0\}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. Consider the continuous semialgebraic map
f:X\to Y
defined as follows:

f(x):=\begin{cases}\big(\tfrac{1}{2}-x,0\big),\text{ if }0\leq x\leq\tfrac{1}{2},\\
\big(0,x-\tfrac{1}{2}\big),\text{ if }\tfrac{1}{2}<x\leq 1.\end{cases}

Let
g:X\to Y
be any Nash map. As Nash maps are analytic, then either
g(X)\subset\{{\tt x}=0\}
or
g(X)\subset\{{\tt y}=0\}
. We deduce,
\|f-g\|\geq\tfrac{1}{2}
. Thus,
f
cannot be approximated by a Nash map.
\sqbullet

Theorem
1.2
, as well as the mentioned results of Fernando and Ghiloni, only takes into account the target space
Y
during the approximation process and do not guarantee that
g(X)=f(X)
. The purpose of this paper is to improve Theorem
1.2
in order to have an approximating map
g
that not only has
Y
as target space, but that has also the same image of
f
.

Our result reads as follows.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
f:X\to{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
be a continuous definable map defined on a compact definable set
X\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Let
\varepsilon>0
and let
p\geq 1
be an integer. Then, there exists a definable map
g:X\to{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
of class
{\mathcal{C}}^{p}
such that
\|f-g\|<\varepsilon
and
g(X)=f(X)
.

The proof of this theorem is an interplay between o-minimal geometry and PL geometry and makes use of the following ‘surjective definable version’ of the finite simplicial approximation theorem, which seems interesting in its own right. In general, the classical finite approximation theorem
[
Mu
, Thm.16.1]
does not guarantee that a simplicial approximation
h
is surjective if the involved continuous map
f
is surjective, see Examples
3.3
below. If
{\mathcal{K}}
is a finite simplicial complex of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, we indicate with
\operatorname{sd}^{\kappa}({\mathcal{K}})
its
\kappa^{\text{th}}
barycentric subdivision, see §
2.1
.

Proposition 1.5

(Surjective simplicial approximation)
.

Let
{\mathcal{K}}
be a finite simplicial complex of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
{\mathcal{L}}
a finite simplicial complex of
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
and
f:|{\mathcal{K}}|\to|{\mathcal{L}}|
a surjective continuous definable map. Then, for each
\varepsilon>0
there exist two integers
\kappa,\ell\geq 0
and a surjective simplicial map
h:|\operatorname{sd}^{\kappa}({\mathcal{K}})|=|{\mathcal{K}}|\to|\operatorname{sd}^{\ell}({\mathcal{L}})|
such that
\|f-h\|<\varepsilon
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we present some preliminary definitions and results that will be freely used throughout this article.

2.1.
Finite simplicial complexes

A
simplex

\sigma\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
of dimension
d
is the convex hull of
d+1
affinely independent points
\nu_{0},\ldots,\nu_{d}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, that is

\sigma=\operatorname{Conv}(\{\nu_{0},\ldots,\nu_{d}\}):=\Big\{\lambda_{0}\nu_{0}+\ldots+\lambda_{d}\nu_{d}:\lambda_{0}\geq 0,\ldots,\lambda_{d}\geq 0,\sum_{i=0}^{d}\lambda_{i}=1\Big\}.

If
0\leq i_{0}<\ldots<i_{k}\leq d
, the simplex
\operatorname{Conv}(\{\nu_{i_{0}},\ldots,\nu_{i_{k}}\})
is called a
face of

\sigma
of dimension
k
. As usual, a face of dimension
d-1
is called a
facet of

\sigma
while a face of dimension zero is called a
vertex of

\sigma
. We denote
\partial\sigma
the
(relative) boundary of

\sigma
defined as the union of the proper faces of
\sigma
and
\overset{\circ}{\sigma}:=\sigma\setminus\partial\sigma
the
(relative) interior of

\sigma
, which is equal to the interior of
\sigma
in the affine space generated by
\sigma
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
(that is, the smallest affine subspace of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
that contains
\sigma
). Observe that if
\sigma
is a simplex of dimension zero, then
\partial\sigma=\varnothing
, so
\overset{\circ}{\sigma}=\sigma
.

A
finite simplicial complex
{\mathcal{K}}
of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is a finite family of simplices of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
such that

•

for each simplex
\sigma\in{\mathcal{K}}
all the faces of
\sigma
belong to
{\mathcal{K}}
,

•

for each
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in{\mathcal{K}}
the intersection
\sigma_{1}\cap\sigma_{2}
is either empty or a common face of both
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
.

The
(underlying) polyhedron
|{\mathcal{K}}|
of

{\mathcal{K}}
is the set
\bigcup_{\sigma\in{\mathcal{K}}}\sigma
equipped with the topology induced by the Euclidean topology of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Observe that
|{\mathcal{K}}|
is always a compact subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. A simplex
\sigma\in{\mathcal{K}}
is a
maximal simplex of

{\mathcal{K}}
if it is not a proper face of another simplex
\sigma^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{K}}
. Equivalently,
\sigma\in{\mathcal{K}}
is a maximal simplex of
{\mathcal{K}}
if
\sigma
satisfies the following property: for each
\sigma^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{K}}
, it holds
\overset{\circ}{\sigma}\cap\sigma^{\prime}\neq\varnothing
if and only if
\sigma=\sigma^{\prime}
. We indicate the set of vertices of
{\mathcal{K}}
with
{\mathcal{K}}_{\bullet}
. Note that if
\sigma\in{\mathcal{K}}_{\bullet}
is a vertex that is also a maximal simplex of
{\mathcal{K}}
, then
\sigma
is an isolated point of
|{\mathcal{K}}|
. A
subcomplex
{\mathcal{T}}
of

{\mathcal{K}}
is a finite simplicial complex such that
{\mathcal{T}}\subset{\mathcal{K}}
. For each vertex
\nu\in{\mathcal{K}}_{\bullet}
, the
(open) star
\operatorname{St}(\nu,{\mathcal{K}})
of
\nu
in
{\mathcal{K}}
is the open neighbourhood
\bigcup_{\sigma\in{\mathcal{K}},\nu\in\sigma}\overset{\circ}{\sigma}
. The
closed star of
\nu
in
{\mathcal{K}}
is defined as
\overline{\operatorname{St}}(\nu,{\mathcal{K}}):=\operatorname{Cl}(\operatorname{St}(\nu,{\mathcal{K}}))
. Observe that the set of simplicies
\sigma\in{\mathcal{K}}
such that
\sigma\in\overline{\operatorname{St}}(\nu,{\mathcal{K}})
is a subcomplex of
{\mathcal{K}}
for each vertex
\nu\in{\mathcal{K}}_{\bullet}
. A
refinement
(also called
subdivision
)
of

{\mathcal{K}}
is a (finite) simplicial complex
{\mathcal{K}}^{*}
such that

•

|{\mathcal{K}}^{*}|=|{\mathcal{K}}|
,

•

each simplex
\sigma^{*}\in{\mathcal{K}}^{*}
is contained in some simplex
\sigma\in{\mathcal{K}}
.

We denote
\operatorname{sd}^{\kappa}({\mathcal{K}}):=\operatorname{sd}(\operatorname{sd}^{\kappa-1}({\mathcal{K}}))
the
k^{\text{th}}
barycentric subdivision of

{\mathcal{K}}
, where
\operatorname{sd}^{0}({\mathcal{K}}):={\mathcal{K}}
.

Let
{\mathcal{K}}
be a finite simplicial complex of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
{\mathcal{L}}
a finite simplicial complex of
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
. A map
h:|{\mathcal{K}}|\to{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
is
piecewise affine
if its restriction to each simplex
\sigma
of
{\mathcal{K}}
is the restriction to
\sigma
of an affine map
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
. Note that this definition depends on the simplicial complex
{\mathcal{K}}
and not only on the polyhedron
|{\mathcal{K}}|
. A map
h:|{\mathcal{K}}_{\bullet}|\to{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
extends uniquely to a piecewise affine map from
|{\mathcal{K}}|
to
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
and we denote this extension again
h:|{\mathcal{K}}|\to{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
. In particular, a piecewise affine map is completely determined by its values on
|{\mathcal{K}}_{\bullet}|
. A piecewise affine map
h:|{\mathcal{K}}|\to|{\mathcal{L}}|
is a
simplicial map
if for each
\nu_{0},\ldots,\nu_{k}\in{\mathcal{K}}_{\bullet}
that span a simplex of
{\mathcal{K}}
, the images
h(\nu_{0}),\ldots,h(\nu_{k})
are vertices of
{\mathcal{L}}
that span a simplex in
{\mathcal{L}}
.

2.2.
Pawłucki’s desingularization

We recall here some definitions and the main result of
[
P1
]
that we need in the following sections. Let
p\geq 0
be an integer and
X\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
a compact definable set. A
definable
{\mathcal{C}}^{p}
-triangulation of
X
is a pair
({\mathcal{K}},\varphi)
, where
{\mathcal{K}}
is a finite simplicial complex of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
\varphi:|{\mathcal{K}}|\to X
is a definable homeomorphism such that for each
\sigma\in{\mathcal{K}}
the restriction
\varphi|_{\overset{\circ}{\sigma}}
is an embedding of class
{\mathcal{C}}^{p}
of
\overset{\circ}{\sigma}
into
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. A definable
{\mathcal{C}}^{0}
-triangulation
({\mathcal{K}},\varphi)
is simply called
definable triangulation
. If
{\mathcal{E}}
is a finite family of definable subsets of
X
, we say that a definable triangulation
({\mathcal{K}},\varphi)
is
compatible with
{\mathcal{E}}
if for each
E\in{\mathcal{E}}
the inverse image
\varphi^{-1}(E)
is a union of some open simplices
\overset{\circ}{\sigma}
, where
\sigma\in{\mathcal{K}}
. A definable
{\mathcal{C}}^{p}
-triangulation
({\mathcal{K}},\varphi)
is a
strict definable
{\mathcal{C}}^{p}
-triangulation
if the definable homeomorphism
\varphi:|{\mathcal{K}}|\to X
is of class
{\mathcal{C}}^{p}
.

In
[
P1
]
Pawłucki showed the following remarkable result about the existence of a strict definable
{\mathcal{C}}^{p}
-triangulation
({\mathcal{K}},\varphi)
of
X
compatible with a finite family of definable subsets
{\mathcal{E}}
of
X
that ‘desingularize’ a given continuous definable map
f:X\to{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
(in the sense that it smooths the map
f
to the class
{\mathcal{C}}^{p}
, that is
f\circ\varphi
is of class
{\mathcal{C}}^{p}
). Moreover, such triangulation can be chosen to be a refinement of a given definable triangulation of
X
compatible with the finite family
{\mathcal{E}}
.

Theorem 2.1

(Strict
{\mathcal{C}}^{p}
-refinement theorem,
[
P1
, §1]
)
.

Let
X\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a compact definable set and
f:X\to{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
a continuous definable map. Let
({\mathcal{T}},\psi)
be a definable triangulation compatible with a finite family
{\mathcal{E}}
of definable subsets of
X
. Then, for each integer
p\geq 1
, there exists a strict
{\mathcal{C}}^{p}
-triangulation
({\mathcal{K}},\varphi)
of
|{\mathcal{T}}|
such that:

•

{\mathcal{K}}
is a refinement of
{\mathcal{T}}
.

•

\varphi(\sigma)=\sigma
for each simplex
\sigma\in{\mathcal{T}}
.

•

({\mathcal{K}},\psi\circ\varphi)
is a strict
{\mathcal{C}}^{p}
-triangulation of
X
compatible with the family
{\mathcal{E}}
.

•

f\circ\psi\circ\varphi
is of class
{\mathcal{C}}^{p}
.

3.
Surjective simplicial approximation for definable maps.

The purpose of this section is to show Proposition
1.5
. Let
{\mathcal{K}}
be a finite simplicial complex of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
{\mathcal{L}}
a finite simplicial complex of
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
and
f:|{\mathcal{K}}|\to|{\mathcal{L}}|
a continuous map. A simplicial map
h:|{\mathcal{K}}|\to|{\mathcal{L}}|
is called a
simplicial approximation of

f
if
f(\operatorname{St}(\nu,{\mathcal{K}}))\subset\operatorname{St}(h(\nu),{\mathcal{L}})
for each vertex
\nu\in{\mathcal{K}}_{\bullet}
. If
h
is a simplicial approximation of
f
, then for each
x\in|{\mathcal{K}}|
there exists a simplex
\tau_{x}\in{\mathcal{L}}
such that
f(x)\in\overset{\circ}{\tau}_{x}
and
h(x)\in\tau_{x}
(see
[
Mu
, Lem.14.2]
), so

|f(x)-h(x)|_{m}<\operatorname{diam}(\tau_{x})\leq\max_{\tau\in{\mathcal{L}}}\{\operatorname{diam}(\tau)\}.

We now recall the finite simplicial approximation theorem. The proof is included here for the reader’s convenience, as we will make use of it in Example
3.3
(ii) and Theorem
3.4
.

Theorem 3.1

(Finite simplicial approximation,
[
Mu
, Thm.16.1]
)
.

Let
f:|{\mathcal{K}}|\to|{\mathcal{L}}|
be a continuous map. Then, there exists an integer
\kappa\geq 0
such that
f
has a simplicial approximation
h:|\operatorname{sd}^{\kappa}({\mathcal{K}})|=|{\mathcal{K}}|\to|{\mathcal{L}}|
.

Proof.

The family
{\mathcal{F}}:=\{f^{-1}(\operatorname{St}(\omega,{\mathcal{L}}))\}_{\omega\in{\mathcal{L}}_{\bullet}}
is an open covering of
|{\mathcal{K}}|
. As
|{\mathcal{K}}|
is compact, there exists a Lebesgue number
\lambda
for the open covering
{\mathcal{F}}
. That is, a strictly positive number
\lambda
with the following property:
If
A\subset|{\mathcal{K}}|
satisfies
\operatorname{diam}(A):=\sup_{x,y\in A}|x-y|_{n}<\lambda
, then there exists
F\in{\mathcal{F}}
such that
A\subset F
.
Let
\kappa\geq 0
be an integer such that

for each

[
Mu
, Thm.15.4]
. Then, for each vertex

it holds

. In particular, there exists a vertex

such that

. Thus, there exists a map
h:\operatorname{sd}^{\kappa}({\mathcal{K}})_{\bullet}\to{\mathcal{L}}_{\bullet}
such that

. By

.
∎

As an immediate consequence:

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. Then, there exist two integers
\kappa,\ell\geq 0
and a simplicial map

.

Proof.

By
[

, there exists an integer
\ell\geq 0

∎

Step 1.

{\mathcal{K}}

. We deduce:
For each vertex

\sigma_{\nu}\in{\mathcal{K}}
of minimal dimension such that
\nu\in\sigma_{\nu}
.

{\mathcal{L}}

{\mathcal{L}}

\sigma_{\nu}

4.

The purpose of this section is to show the following proposition. Together with Proposition
1.5
it will be the key ingredient for the proof of Theorem
1.4
. The
\epsilon

4.1
below. Roughly speaking the

of a finite simplicial complex

is a surjective continuous definable map

that ‘squeezes’ a small neighbourhood of the boundary of each maximal simplex of
{\mathcal{L}}
onto its boundary and that is the identity map on each simplex of
{\mathcal{L}}
that is not maximal.

Proposition 4.1

Let
{\mathcal{K}}
be a finite simplicial complex of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,

and

a surjective simplicial map. Then, there exists
\epsilon>0
with the following property: if
g:|{\mathcal{K}}|\to|{\mathcal{L}}|
is a continuous map such that
\|h-g\|<\epsilon
, then

•

\pi_{\epsilon}\circ g:|{\mathcal{K}}|\to|{\mathcal{L}}|
is surjective, where
\pi_{\epsilon}
is the
\epsilon
-squeezing map of

,

•

\|h-\pi_{\epsilon}\circ g\|<2\displaystyle\max_{\tau\in{\mathcal{L}}}\{\operatorname{diam}(\tau)\}

4.1.
\epsilon
-squeezing map

We start by recalling the following well-known result, that we include here for the sake of completeness. The proof is taken from
[
FU
, Lem.1.1]
and adapted to our situation.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
d\geq 1
be an integer and
\tau\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
a simplex of dimension
d
. Then, for each
z\in\overset{\circ}{\tau}
there exists a continuous definable retraction
\rho_{z}:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\{z\}\to\partial\tau
.

Proof.

Let
z\in\overset{\circ}{\tau}
. For each
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\{z\}
let
\ell_{x}:=\{z+t(x-z):t\in[0,+\infty)\}
be the ray from
z
that passes through
x
. As
\tau\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
has dimension
d
, then
\ell_{x}\cap\partial\tau\neq\varnothing
. Moreover, as
\tau
is convex, by
[
Be
, 11.1.2.3, 11.1.2.7]
,
\ell_{x}\cap\partial\tau
is a single point. Define
\rho:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\{z\}\to\partial\tau
as
\rho(x):=\ell_{x}\cap\partial\tau
. Observe that
\rho(x)=x
for each
x\in\partial\tau
. Let
h_{0},\ldots,h_{d}\in{\mathbb{R}}[{\tt x}_{1},\ldots,{\tt x}_{d}]
be polynomials of degree 1 such that the hyperplanes
H_{i}:=\{h_{i}=0\}
contain the facets of
\tau
. Assume
\tau\subset\{h_{i}\geq 0\}
for each
i=0,\ldots,d
. Note that
\rho(x)=z+\lambda(x-z)
, where
\lambda
is the smallest value
\mu>0
such that
h_{i}(z+\mu(x-z))=0
for some
i=0,\ldots,d
. As
z\in\overset{\circ}{\tau}
, we have
h_{i}(z)>0
for
i=0,\ldots,d
. Thus,

\frac{1}{\lambda}=\max\Big\{\frac{h_{i}(z)-h_{i}(x)}{h_{i}(z)}:\ i=0,\ldots,d\Big\}>0.

Consequently,

\rho(x)=z+\frac{1}{\max\Big\{\frac{h_{i}(z)-h_{i}(x)}{h_{i}(z)}:\ i=0,\ldots,d\Big\}}(x-z),

so
\rho:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\{z\}\to\partial\tau
is a continuous semialgebraic map such that
\rho|_{\partial\tau}=\operatorname{id}_{\partial\tau}
, that is,
\rho
is a semialgebraic retraction. In particular,
\rho
is a retraction that is definable in every o-minimal structure expanding the ordered field of real numbers
{\mathbb{R}}
.
∎

Let
{\mathcal{L}}
be a finite simplicial complex of
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
and
\tau\in{\mathcal{L}}
a simplex of dimension
d\geq 1
. Let
b_{\tau}
be the barycentre of
\tau
and
\delta_{\tau}:=\operatorname{dist}(b_{\tau},\partial\tau)
. Observe that, as
d\geq 1
, then
\delta_{\tau}>0
. Let
H_{\tau}
be the affine space generated by
\tau
, that is, the smallest affine subspace
H_{\tau}
of
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
that contains
\tau
. For each
\epsilon\in(0,\delta_{\tau})
denote

(4.1)

\theta_{\tau,\epsilon}:H_{\tau}\to H_{\tau},\,x\mapsto b_{\tau}+\frac{\epsilon}{\delta_{\tau}}(x-b_{\tau})

the homothety of
H_{\tau}
of centre
b_{\tau}
and radius
\epsilon/\delta_{\tau}
. Observe that
\theta_{\tau,\epsilon}(\tau)\subset\tau
, because
\epsilon/\delta_{\tau}<1
. As
\dim(\tau)=\dim(H_{\tau})
, by Lemma
4.2
, there exists a continuous definable retraction
\rho_{b_{\tau}}:H_{\tau}\setminus\{b_{\tau}\}\to\partial\tau
. Define

(4.2)

\pi_{\tau,\epsilon}:\tau\to\tau,\,x\mapsto\begin{cases}\theta^{-1}_{\tau,\epsilon}(x)=\theta_{\tau,\tfrac{1}{\epsilon}}(x),\text{ if }x\in\theta_{\tau,\epsilon}(\tau),\\
\rho_{b_{\tau}}(x),\text{ if }x\in\tau\setminus\theta_{\tau,\epsilon}(\overset{\circ}{\tau}).\end{cases}

It is clear that
\pi_{\tau,\epsilon}
is a continuous definable map such that
\pi_{\tau,\epsilon}(\tau)=\tau
and the restriction
\pi_{\tau,\epsilon}|_{\partial\tau}
to
\partial\tau
is the identity map
\operatorname{id}_{\partial\tau}
of
\partial\tau
.

Let
\Lambda_{{\mathcal{L}}}\subset{\mathcal{L}}
be the family of maximal simplices of
{\mathcal{L}}
of dimension
\geq 1
. Define

\delta_{{\mathcal{L}}}:=\min_{\tau\in\Lambda_{{\mathcal{L}}}}\{\delta_{\tau}\}=\min_{\tau\in\Lambda_{{\mathcal{L}}}}\{\operatorname{dist}(b_{\tau},\partial\tau)\}.

Then, for each
\tau\in\Lambda_{{\mathcal{L}}}
and
\epsilon\in(0,\delta_{{\mathcal{L}}})
, the map
\pi_{\tau,\epsilon}:\tau\to\tau
extends to a continuous definable map
\widehat{\pi}_{\tau,\epsilon}:|{\mathcal{L}}|\to|{\mathcal{L}}|
that is the identity on
|{\mathcal{L}}|\setminus\overset{\circ}{\tau}
. In fact, as
\tau
is a maximal simplex of
{\mathcal{L}}
, then
\overset{\circ}{\tau}
is open in
|{\mathcal{L}}|
, so
|{\mathcal{L}}|\setminus\overset{\circ}{\tau}
is closed. As
\tau\cap(|{\mathcal{L}}|\setminus\overset{\circ}{\tau})=\partial\tau
and
\pi_{\tau,\epsilon}|_{\partial\tau}=\operatorname{id}_{\partial\tau}
, the map

\widehat{\pi}_{\tau,\epsilon}:|{\mathcal{L}}|\to|{\mathcal{L}}|,\,x\mapsto\begin{cases}\pi_{\tau,\epsilon}(x),\text{ if }x\in\tau\\
x,\text{ if }x\in|{\mathcal{L}}|\setminus\overset{\circ}{\tau},\end{cases}

is a well-defined continuous definable map.

Let
\Lambda_{{\mathcal{L}}}=\{\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{s}\}
and
\epsilon\in(0,\delta_{{\mathcal{L}}})
. We are ready to give the definition of
\epsilon
-squeezing map of a finite simplicial complex
{\mathcal{L}}
.

Definition 4.3
.

The
\epsilon
-squeezing map of
{\mathcal{L}}
is the continuous definable map

\pi_{\epsilon}:=\widehat{\pi}_{\tau_{s},\epsilon}\circ\cdots\circ\widehat{\pi}_{\tau_{1},\epsilon}:|{\mathcal{L}}|\to|{\mathcal{L}}|.

Observe that the map
\pi_{\epsilon}
depends on the simplicial complex
{\mathcal{L}}
and not only on the polyhedron
|{\mathcal{L}}|
. Moreover, as
\widehat{\pi}_{\tau_{i},\epsilon}\circ\widehat{\pi}_{\tau_{j},\epsilon}=\widehat{\pi}_{\tau_{j},\epsilon}\circ\widehat{\pi}_{\tau_{i},\epsilon}
for each
\tau_{i},\tau_{j}\in\Lambda_{{\mathcal{L}}}
, the map
\pi_{\epsilon}
does not depend on how we order the set
\Lambda_{{\mathcal{L}}}
, so it is completely determined by
\epsilon\in(0,\delta_{{\mathcal{L}}})
and the simplicial complex
{\mathcal{L}}
.

We end this section with the following remark.

Remark 4.4
.

We have
\pi_{\epsilon}(\tau)=\tau
for each
\tau\in{\mathcal{L}}
and
\epsilon\in(0,\delta_{{\mathcal{L}}})
.
In fact, let
\tau\in{\mathcal{L}}
and
\tau_{j}\in\Lambda_{{\mathcal{L}}}
. As
\tau_{j}
is a maximal simplex of
{\mathcal{L}}
, then either
\tau=\tau_{j}
or
\tau\subset|{\mathcal{L}}|\setminus\overset{\circ}{\tau}_{j}
. In particular,
\widehat{\pi}_{\tau_{j},\epsilon}(\tau)=\tau
for each
\epsilon\in(0,\delta_{{\mathcal{L}}})
, because
\widehat{\pi}_{\tau_{j},\epsilon}(\tau_{j})=\tau_{j}
and
\widehat{\pi}_{\tau_{j},\epsilon}
is the identity map on
|{\mathcal{L}}|\setminus\overset{\circ}{\tau}_{j}
. As
\pi_{\epsilon}
is the composition of the the maps
\widehat{\pi}_{\tau_{1},\epsilon},\ldots,\widehat{\pi}_{\tau_{s},\epsilon}
, we conclude that
\pi_{\epsilon}(\tau)=\tau
for each
\epsilon\in(0,\delta_{{\mathcal{L}}})
.
\sqbullet

4.2.
Proof of Proposition
4.1

In this section we show Proposition
4.1
. Let
\pi_{\tau,\epsilon}
be the map introduced in (
4.2
), we start with the following lemma.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\tau\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
be a simplex of dimension
d\geq 1
and
\delta_{\tau}:=\operatorname{dist}(b_{\tau},\partial\tau)>0
. Then, there exists
\epsilon^{*}\in(0,\delta_{\tau})
with the following property: if
g:\tau\to\tau
is a continuous map such that

\|\operatorname{id}_{\partial\tau}-g|_{\partial\tau}\|<\frac{1}{2}\delta_{\tau},

then
\pi_{\tau,\epsilon}(g(\tau))=\tau
for each
\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon^{*})
.

Proof.

Let
\epsilon\in(0,\delta_{\tau})
and let
\theta_{\epsilon}:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
be the homothety of centre
b_{\tau}
and radius
\epsilon/\delta_{\tau}
(see (
4.1
)). As
\pi_{\tau,\epsilon}(\theta_{\epsilon}(\tau))=\tau
, it is enough to show that there exists
\epsilon^{*}\in(0,\delta_{\tau})
such that
\theta_{\epsilon}(\tau)\subset g(\tau)
for each
0<\epsilon<\epsilon^{*}
. Suppose, by the way of contradiction, that for all
\epsilon\in(0,\delta_{\tau})
there exists a point
z_{\epsilon}\in\theta_{\epsilon}(\tau)\setminus g(\tau)
. As
z_{\epsilon}\in\theta_{\epsilon}(\tau)\subset\overset{\circ}{\tau}
, by Lemma
4.2
, there exists a (continuous) retraction
\rho_{z_{\epsilon}}:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\{z_{\epsilon}\}\to\partial\tau
. We claim:
There exists
\epsilon^{*}\in(0,\delta_{\tau})
such that the (continuous) map
\widehat{g}_{\epsilon}:=\rho_{z_{\epsilon}}\circ g|_{\partial\tau}:\partial\tau\to\partial\tau
is homotopic to the identity map
\operatorname{id}_{\partial\tau}
of
\partial\tau
for all
0<\epsilon<\epsilon^{*}
.

For each
x\in\partial\tau
let

{\tt s}_{x,\epsilon}:=\{t\widehat{g}_{\epsilon}(x)+(1-t)x:0\leq t\leq 1\}

be the segment between
x
and
\widehat{g}_{\epsilon}(x)
. Let
\rho_{b_{\tau}}:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\{b_{\tau}\}\to\partial\tau
be the (continuous) retraction relative to the barycentre
b_{\tau}
of
\tau
(see Lemma
4.2
). If
b_{\tau}\not\in{\tt s}_{x,\epsilon}
for each
x\in\partial\tau
, then the map

\partial\tau\times[0,1]\to\partial\tau,\,(x,t)\mapsto\rho_{b_{\tau}}(t\widehat{g}_{\epsilon}(x)+(1-t)x)

is a well-defined homotopy between the identity map
\operatorname{id}_{\partial\tau}
and
\widehat{g}_{\epsilon}
. If there exists
x\in\partial\tau
such that
b_{\tau}\in{\tt s}_{x,\epsilon}
, then

\displaystyle\delta_{\tau}+\operatorname{dist}(b_{\tau},\widehat{g}_{\epsilon}(x))

\displaystyle=\min_{y\in\partial\tau}\{\operatorname{dist}(y,b_{\tau})\}+\operatorname{dist}(b_{\tau},\widehat{g}_{\epsilon}(x))

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{dist}(x,b_{\tau})+\operatorname{dist}(b_{\tau},\widehat{g}_{\epsilon}(x))=\operatorname{dist}(x,\widehat{g}_{\epsilon}(x))

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{dist}(x,g(x))+\operatorname{dist}(g(x),\widehat{g}_{\epsilon}(x))<\frac{\delta_{\tau}}{2}+\operatorname{dist}(z_{\epsilon},\widehat{g}_{\epsilon}(x))

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\delta_{\tau}}{2}+\operatorname{dist}(z_{\epsilon},b_{\tau})+\operatorname{dist}(b_{\tau},\widehat{g}_{\epsilon}(x))

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\delta_{\tau}}{2}+\frac{\epsilon}{\delta_{\tau}}\operatorname{diam}(\tau)+\operatorname{dist}(b_{\tau},\widehat{g}_{\epsilon}(x))

The inequality
\operatorname{dist}(g(x),\widehat{g}_{\epsilon}(x))\leq\operatorname{dist}(z_{\epsilon},\widehat{g}_{\epsilon}(x))
holds because
g(x)
belongs to the segment between
z_{\epsilon}
and
\widehat{g}_{\epsilon}(x)
. We deduce

(4.3)

\delta_{\tau}<\frac{\delta_{\tau}}{2}+\frac{\epsilon}{\delta_{\tau}}\operatorname{diam}(\tau)

Define the number

\epsilon^{*}:=\frac{\delta_{\tau}^{2}}{2\operatorname{diam}(\tau)}>0

and observe that, as
\delta_{\tau}<\operatorname{diam}(\tau)
, then
\epsilon^{*}<\delta_{\tau}
. If
\epsilon<\epsilon^{*}
, the inequality (
4.3
) becomes

\delta_{\tau}\leq\frac{\delta_{\tau}}{2}+\frac{\epsilon}{\delta_{\tau}}\operatorname{diam}(\tau)<\delta_{\tau},

which leads to a contradiction. Thus, if
\epsilon<\epsilon^{*}
, then
b_{\tau}\not\in{\tt s}_{x,\epsilon}
for each
x\in\partial\tau
, so
\widehat{g}_{\epsilon}
is homotopic to the identity map
\operatorname{id}_{\partial\tau}
, as claimed.

The map
\widehat{g}_{\epsilon}
is the restriction to
\partial\tau
of the continuous map
\rho_{z_{\epsilon}}\circ g:\tau\to\partial\tau
. Thus, the map induced by
\widehat{g}_{\epsilon}
on homology

(\widehat{g}_{\epsilon})_{*}:H_{d}(\partial\tau,{\mathbb{Z}})\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}\to H_{d}(\partial\tau,{\mathbb{Z}})\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}

is zero, because it factors through
H_{d}(\tau,{\mathbb{Z}})=0
. If
\epsilon<\epsilon^{*}
this leads to a contradiction. In fact,
(\widehat{g}_{\epsilon})_{*}=\operatorname{id}_{H_{d}(\partial\tau,{\mathbb{Z}})}
because
\widehat{g}_{\epsilon}
is homotopic to the identity map
\operatorname{id}_{\partial\tau}
. In particular, if
\epsilon<\epsilon^{*}
, then
\theta_{\epsilon}(\tau)\subset g(\tau)
. We deduce
\tau=\pi_{\tau,\epsilon}(\theta_{\epsilon}(\tau))\subset\pi_{\tau,\epsilon}(g(\tau))\subset\tau
, so
\pi_{\tau,\epsilon}(g(\tau))=\tau
, as required.
∎

We are ready to show Proposition
4.1
.

Proof of Proposition
4.1
.

The proof is conducted in three steps.

Step 1. Initial reduction.
Let
g:|{\mathcal{K}}|\to|{\mathcal{L}}|
be a continuous map such that
\|h-g\|<\epsilon
. As
\pi_{\epsilon}(\tau)=\tau
for each
\tau\in{\mathcal{L}}
(see Remark
4.4
), we have

\|h-\pi_{\epsilon}\circ g\|\leq\|h-g\|+\|g-\pi_{\epsilon}\circ g\|<\epsilon+\max_{\tau\in{\mathcal{L}}}\{\operatorname{diam}(\tau)\}

In particular, if
\epsilon<\max\{\operatorname{diam}(\tau):\tau\in{\mathcal{L}}\}
, then
\|h-\pi_{\epsilon}\circ g\|<2\max\{\operatorname{diam}(\tau):\tau\in{\mathcal{L}}\}
.

As
|{\mathcal{L}}|
has finitely many connected components, we may assume that
|{\mathcal{L}}|
is connected. If
\tau\in{\mathcal{L}}_{\bullet}
is a vertex that is also a maximal simplex of
{\mathcal{L}}
, then
\tau
is an isolated point of
|{\mathcal{L}}|
, so
|{\mathcal{L}}|=\tau
, because
|{\mathcal{L}}|
is connected. In particular, we may also assume that all the maximal simplices of
{\mathcal{L}}
have dimension
\geq 1
.

Let
\tau\in{\mathcal{L}}
be a simplex that is not a maximal simplex of
{\mathcal{L}}
. Then, there exists a maximal simplex
\tau^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{L}}
such that
\tau\subset\tau^{\prime}
. In particular, if
\tau^{\prime}\subset\pi_{\epsilon}(g(|{\mathcal{K}}|))
, then
\tau\subset\tau^{\prime}\subset\pi_{\epsilon}(g(|{\mathcal{K}}|))
. Thus, in order to conclude we are reduced to show:
There exists
\epsilon>0
such that if
\|h-g\|<\epsilon
, then
\tau\subset\pi_{\epsilon}(g(|{\mathcal{K}}|))
for each maximal simplex
\tau\in{\mathcal{L}}
.

Step 2. Choice of
\epsilon
.
Let
\Lambda_{{\mathcal{L}}}
be the family of maximal simplices of
{\mathcal{L}}
and let
\tau\in\Lambda_{{\mathcal{L}}}
. We may assume that
\tau
is not the only maximal simplex of
{\mathcal{L}}
, because in the case where
\tau
is the only maximal simplex of
{\mathcal{L}}
the proof is similar but easier (in what follows it is enough to take
U_{\tau}:=\tau
,
\rho_{\tau}=\operatorname{id}_{\tau}
and
\epsilon_{1}=1
). By
Step 1
, the polyhedron
|{\mathcal{L}}|
is connected, so

\partial\operatorname{St}(\tau,{\mathcal{L}}):=\overline{\operatorname{St}}(\tau,{\mathcal{L}})\setminus\operatorname{St}(\tau,{\mathcal{L}})\neq\varnothing,

where
\operatorname{St}(\tau,{\mathcal{L}})
is the (open) star of
\tau
(see (
3.1
)). By
[
Mu
, Lem.70.1]
, there exists a (continuous) retraction
\rho^{\prime}_{\tau}:\operatorname{St}(\tau,{\mathcal{L}})\to\tau
. As
\tau
and
\partial\operatorname{St}(\tau,{\mathcal{L}})
are compact sets,
\tau\cap\partial\operatorname{St}(\tau,{\mathcal{L}})=\varnothing
and
\partial\operatorname{St}(\tau,{\mathcal{L}})\neq\varnothing
, we have

\epsilon_{1}:=\min_{\tau\in\Lambda_{{\mathcal{L}}}}\{\operatorname{dist}(\tau,\partial\operatorname{St}(\tau,{\mathcal{L}}))\}>0.

For each
\tau\in\Lambda_{{\mathcal{L}}}
the set

U_{\tau}:=\{x\in|{\mathcal{L}}|:\operatorname{dist}(x,\tau)<\epsilon_{1}\}

is an open neighbourhood of
\tau
in
|{\mathcal{L}}|
such that
U_{\tau}\subset\operatorname{St}(\tau,{\mathcal{L}})
. In particular, the restriction
\rho_{\tau}:=\rho^{\prime}_{\tau}|_{U_{\tau}}:U_{\tau}\to\tau
of the retraction
\rho^{\prime}_{\tau}
to
U_{\tau}
is well-defined for each
\tau\in\Lambda_{\tau}
.

Let
\tau\in\Lambda_{{\mathcal{L}}}
. As
h
is a surjective simplicial map, there exists a simplex

such that

. As

\sigma

\sigma_{\tau}

\displaystyle<\frac{1}{2}\delta_{{\mathcal{L}}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{dist}(b_{\tau},\partial\tau).

. Thus,

\pi_{\epsilon}(\rho_{\tau}(g(\sigma_{\tau})))=\pi_{\epsilon}(\rho_{\tau}(g(h|_{\sigma_{\tau}}^{-1}(\tau))))=\pi_{\tau,\epsilon}(\rho_{\tau}(g(h|_{\sigma_{\tau}}^{-1}(\tau))))=\tau,

\displaystyle\begin{split}g(\sigma_{\tau})\cap\overset{\circ}{\tau}&=\rho_{\tau}(g(\sigma_{\tau})\cap\overset{\circ}{\tau})\subset\rho_{\tau}(g(\sigma_{\tau}))\cap\rho_{\tau}(\overset{\circ}{\tau})=\rho_{\tau}(g(\sigma_{\tau}))\cap\overset{\circ}{\tau}\\

, we deduce,

\displaystyle\overset{\circ}{\tau}

\displaystyle=\tau\cap\overset{\circ}{\tau}=\pi_{\epsilon}(\rho_{\tau}(g(\sigma_{\tau})))\cap\overset{\circ}{\tau}=(\pi_{\epsilon}(\rho_{\tau}(g(\sigma_{\tau}))\cap\overset{\circ}{\tau})\cup\pi_{\epsilon}(\rho_{\tau}(g(\sigma_{\tau}))\cap\partial\tau))\cap\overset{\circ}{\tau}

\displaystyle\subset(\pi_{\epsilon}(\rho_{\tau}(g(\sigma_{\tau}))\cap\overset{\circ}{\tau})\cup\partial\tau)\cap\overset{\circ}{\tau}=\pi_{\epsilon}(\rho_{\tau}(g(\sigma_{\tau}))\cap\overset{\circ}{\tau})\cap\overset{\circ}{\tau}

\displaystyle=\pi_{\epsilon}(g(\sigma_{\tau})\cap\overset{\circ}{\tau})\cap\overset{\circ}{\tau}\subset\pi_{\epsilon}(g(\sigma_{\tau}))\cap\overset{\circ}{\tau}\subset\pi_{\epsilon}(g(\sigma_{\tau})).

As
\overset{\circ}{\tau}\subset\pi_{\epsilon}(g(\sigma_{\tau}))
and
\pi_{\epsilon}(g(\sigma_{\tau}))
is compact, we conclude

\tau=\operatorname{Cl}(\overset{\circ}{\tau})\subset\operatorname{Cl}(\pi_{\epsilon}(g(\sigma_{\tau})))=\pi_{\epsilon}(g(\sigma_{\tau})),

as required.
∎

5.
Differentiable approximation of continuous definable maps

We make use of Proposition
1.5
and Proposition
4.1
to deduce Theorem
1.4
as a consequence of Pawłucki’s desingularization (see Theorem
2.1
).

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
and an integer
p\geq 1
. As
X
is compact and
f
a continuous definable map, the set
Y:=f(X)\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
is a compact definable set. By Pawłucki’s desingularization (see Theorem
2.1
), there exists a definable triangulation
({\mathcal{L}},\psi)
of
Y
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
such that
\psi:|{\mathcal{L}}|\to Y
is a definable homeomorphism of class
{\mathcal{C}}^{p}
. The map
\psi_{*}:{\mathcal{C}}^{0}(X,|{\mathcal{L}}|)\to{\mathcal{C}}^{0}(X,Y),G\mapsto\psi\circ G
is continuous with respect to the compact-open topology. Thus, there exists
\delta_{0}>0
such that if
g\in{\mathcal{C}}^{0}(X,|{\mathcal{L}}|)
is a definable map of class
{\mathcal{C}}^{p}
such that
\|\psi^{-1}\circ f-g\|<\delta_{0}
, then the definable map
\psi\circ g:X\to Y
satisfies

\|f-\psi\circ g\|=\|\psi\circ\psi^{-1}\circ f-\psi\circ g\|<\varepsilon.

In particular, up to substituting
f
with
\psi^{-1}\circ f
, we may assume:
Y=|{\mathcal{L}}|
is the (underlying) polyhedron of a finite simplicial complex
{\mathcal{L}}
of
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
.
Moreover, by
[
Mu
, Thm.15.4]
, there exists an integer
\ell\geq 0
such that
\operatorname{diam}(\tau)<\tfrac{\varepsilon}{6}
for each
\tau\in\operatorname{sd}^{\ell}({\mathcal{L}})
. Thus, up to substituting
{\mathcal{L}}
with
\operatorname{sd}^{\ell}({\mathcal{L}})
, we may also assume assume:
\operatorname{diam}(\tau)<\tfrac{\varepsilon}{6}
for each
\tau\in{\mathcal{L}}
.

By
[
vD
, Cor.8.3.9]
, there exists an open definable neighbourhood
V\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
of
|{\mathcal{L}}|
and a definable retraction
\rho:\operatorname{Cl}(V)\to|{\mathcal{L}}|
. As
|{\mathcal{L}}|
is compact, up to shrinking
V
if necessary, we may assume that
V=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}:d(x,|{\mathcal{L}}|)<\mu_{0}\}
for some
\mu_{0}>0
. By the Tietze extension theorem for definable maps
[
vD
, Cor.8.3.10]
, the continuous definable map
f:X\to|{\mathcal{L}}|
can be extended to a continuous definable map
\widehat{f}:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
. We claim:
There exists a compact polyhedron
Q\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
such that
X\subset\operatorname{Int}(Q)
and
\widehat{f}(Q)\subset V
.

As
X
is compact, there exists
R>0
such that
X\subset(-R,R)^{n}
. Let

U:=\widehat{f}^{-1}(V)\cap(-R,R)^{n}.

As
X
is compact,
U
is open and
X\subset U
, then
\nu:=\operatorname{dist}(X,\partial U)>0
. In particular, the set

U^{\prime}:=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:d(x,X)<\nu\}

is an open definable neighbourhood of
X
in
U
. Consider a triangulation
{\mathcal{Q}}_{0}
of
[-R,R]^{n}
such that
\operatorname{diam}(\sigma)<\nu
for each
\sigma\in{\mathcal{Q}}_{0}
. Define the compact polyhedron

Q:=\bigcup\{\sigma\in{\mathcal{Q}}_{0}:\sigma\subset U^{\prime}\}.

Observe that, as
Q\subset\widehat{f}^{-1}(V)
, then
\widehat{f}(Q)\subset V
. Let
x\in X
and
B\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
the open ball of centre
x
and radius
\tfrac{\nu}{2}
. Let
\{\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{k}\}
be the set of simplices of
{\mathcal{Q}}_{0}
such that
B\cap\sigma_{i}\neq\varnothing
. For each
i=1,\ldots,k
and
y\in\sigma_{i}
we have
d(y,X)\leq d(y,x)<\nu
. In particular,
\sigma_{i}\subset Q
for each
i=1,\ldots,k
. As
B\subset[-R,R]^{n}
, then

B=\bigcup_{\sigma\in{\mathcal{Q}}_{0}}(B\cap\sigma)=\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}(B\cap\sigma_{i})\subset Q,

so
x\in\operatorname{Int}(Q)
. We conclude that
X\subset\operatorname{Int}(Q)
, as desired.

In order to lighten the notation, we replace
\widehat{f}
by
\widehat{f}|_{Q}
. As
\widehat{f}(Q)\subset V
, the definable map
F:=\rho\circ\widehat{f}:Q\to|{\mathcal{L}}|
is continuous and well-defined. By
[
vD
, Thm.8.2.9]
, there exists a definable triangulation
({\mathcal{K}},\varphi)
of
Q
compatible with
X
. As the triangulation
({\mathcal{K}},\varphi)
is compatible with
X
and
X
is compact, the set
{\mathcal{T}}:=\{\sigma\in{\mathcal{K}}:\sigma\subset\varphi^{-1}(X)\}
is a subcomplex of
{\mathcal{K}}
. The map

\rho_{*}:{\mathcal{C}}^{0}(|{\mathcal{K}}|,V)\to{\mathcal{C}}^{0}(|{\mathcal{K}}|,|{\mathcal{L}}|),\,G\mapsto\rho\circ G

is continuous with respect to the compact-open topology, thus, there exists
\delta^{\prime}_{1}>0
such that if
h\in{\mathcal{C}}^{0}(|{\mathcal{K}}|,V)
is a continuous map such that
\|\widehat{f}\circ\varphi-h\|<\delta^{\prime}_{1}
, then

\|F\circ\varphi-\rho\circ h\|=\|\rho\circ\widehat{f}\circ\varphi-\rho\circ h\|<\frac{\varepsilon}{3}.

As
|{\mathcal{K}}|
is compact and
\widehat{f}(\varphi(|{\mathcal{K}}|))=\widehat{f}(Q)\subset V
, then
\mu_{1}:=\operatorname{dist}(\widehat{f}(\varphi(|{\mathcal{K}}|),\partial V)>0
. We define
\delta_{1}:=\min\{\delta_{1}^{\prime},\mu_{1}\}
. As

\widehat{f}(\varphi(|{\mathcal{T}}|))=\widehat{f}(X)=f(X)=|{\mathcal{L}}|,

by Proposition
1.5
, there exists two integers
\kappa,\ell\geq 0
and a surjective simplicial map
h:|\operatorname{sd}^{\kappa}({\mathcal{T}})|=|{\mathcal{T}}|\to|\operatorname{sd}^{\ell}({\mathcal{L}})|
such that
\|\widehat{f}\circ\varphi|_{|{\mathcal{T}}|}-h\|<\tfrac{\delta_{1}}{2}
. We claim:
There exists a continuous definable extension
\widehat{h}:|{\mathcal{K}}|\to V
of
h
such that
\|\widehat{f}\circ\varphi-\widehat{h}\|<\delta_{1}
.

If
\widehat{h}:|{\mathcal{K}}|\to{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
is a continuous definable map such that
\|\widehat{f}\circ\varphi-\widehat{h}\|<\delta_{1}
, then

|\widehat{f}(\varphi(x))-\widehat{h}(x)|_{m}<\delta_{1}\leq\mu_{1}

for each
x\in|{\mathcal{K}}|
. In particular
\widehat{h}(|{\mathcal{K}}|)\subset V
. As
|{\mathcal{K}}|
is compact, the map
\widehat{f}\circ\varphi
is uniformly continuous. In particular, there exists
\delta^{*}>0
such that if
x,y\in|{\mathcal{K}}|
are such that
|x-y|_{n}<\delta^{*}
, then

|\widehat{f}(\varphi(x))-\widehat{f}(\varphi(y))|_{m}<\frac{\delta_{1}}{2}.

By
[
vD
, Cor.8.3.9]
, there exists an open definable neighbourhood
W\subset|{\mathcal{K}}|
of
|{\mathcal{T}}|
and a definable retraction
\xi:\operatorname{Cl}(W)\to|{\mathcal{T}}|
. Observe that as
|{\mathcal{K}}|
is compact, also
\operatorname{Cl}(W)
is compact. The set
W^{\prime}:=\{x\in W:|x-\xi(x)|_{n}<\tfrac{\delta^{*}}{2}\}
is an open definable neighbourhood of
|{\mathcal{T}}|
in
|{\mathcal{K}}|
. Thus, up to replacing
W
with
W^{\prime}
, we may assume
|x-\xi(x)|_{n}<\delta^{*}
for each
x\in\operatorname{Cl}(W)
. Let
\{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\}
be a definable continuous partition of unity subordinated to the open covering
\{W,|{\mathcal{K}}|\setminus|{\mathcal{T}}|\}
of
|{\mathcal{K}}|
. For each
x\in|{\mathcal{K}}|
define

\widehat{h}(x):=\theta_{1}(x)h(\xi(x))+\theta_{2}(x)\widehat{f}(\varphi(x)).

Observe first that, as
\theta_{1}\equiv 0
on
|{\mathcal{K}}|\setminus W
, then
\widehat{h}:|{\mathcal{K}}|\to{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
is a well-defined continuous definable map. Let
x\in|{\mathcal{K}}|
. If
x\in|{\mathcal{K}}|\setminus W
, then
\widehat{h}(x)=\widehat{f}(\varphi(x))
, so
|\widehat{f}(\varphi(x))-\widehat{h}(x)|_{m}=0
. If
x\in W
, then

\displaystyle|\widehat{f}(\varphi(x))

\displaystyle-\widehat{h}(x)|_{m}=|\widehat{f}(\varphi(x))-(\theta_{1}(x)h(\xi(x))+\theta_{2}(x)\widehat{f}(\varphi(x)))|_{m}

\displaystyle=|(1-\theta_{2}(x))\widehat{f}(\varphi(x))-\theta_{1}(x)h(\xi(x))|_{m}=|\theta_{1}(x)\widehat{f}(\varphi(x))-\theta_{1}(x)h(\xi(x))|_{m}

\displaystyle\leq\theta_{1}(x)|\widehat{f}(\varphi(\xi(x)))-h(\xi(x))|_{m}+\theta_{1}(x)|\widehat{f}(\varphi(x))-\widehat{f}(\varphi(\xi(x)))|_{m}

\displaystyle<\max_{x\in|{\mathcal{K}}|}\{\theta_{1}(x)\}\|\widehat{f}\circ\varphi|_{|{\mathcal{T}}|}-h\|+\frac{\delta_{1}}{2}\leq\|\widehat{f}\circ\varphi|_{|{\mathcal{T}}|}-h\|+\frac{\delta_{1}}{2}

\displaystyle<\frac{\delta_{1}}{2}+\frac{\delta_{1}}{2}=\delta_{1}.

We conclude that
\|\widehat{f}\circ\varphi-\widehat{h}\|<\delta_{1}
, as desired.

Define

. By Proposition
4.1
, there exists
\epsilon>0

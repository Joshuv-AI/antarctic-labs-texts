Two-step minimization approach to an L ∞ L^{\infty} -constrained variational problem with a generalized potential


Abstract
We study a variational problem on H 1 ​ ( ℝ ) H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}) under an L ∞ L^{\infty} -constraint related to Sobolev-type inequalities for a class of generalized potentials, including L p L^{p} -potentials, non-positive potentials, and signed Radon measures. We establish various essential tools for this variational problem, including the decomposition principle, the comparison principle, and the perturbation theorem, which are the basis of the two-step minimization method. As for their applications, we present precise results for minimizers of minimization problems, such as the study of potentials of Dirac’s delta measure type and the analysis of trapped modes in potential wells.

Introduction

In our previous paper
[
1
]
, we considered the following variational problem
for a positive bounded potential
V\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
with
\essinf_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}}V(x)>0
:

\displaystyle m(V):=\min_{u\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}),\|u\|_{\infty}=1}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}(|u^{\prime}(x)|^{2}+V(x)|u(x)|^{2})\,dx,

(1.1)

where
\|u\|_{\infty}:=\|u\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})}
.
The above variational problem (
1.1
) is closely related
to the problem of finding the best constant for the following Sobolev-type inequality
for the
L^{\infty}
-norm:

\|u\|_{\infty}\leq C\|u\|_{V},

(1.2)

where

\|u\|_{V}^{2}:=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}(|u^{\prime}(x)|^{2}+V(x)|u(x)|^{2})\,dx.

The best constant of the Sobolev-type inequality (
1.2
)
is given by
m(V)^{-1/2}
.
The Sobolev-type inequality has been studied in various settings
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
]
.
We proposed a two-step minimization approach to the variational problem (
1.1
) in
[
1
]
.
This approach enabled us
to evaluate the best constant for inhomogeneous positive bounded potentials precisely.

The aim of this paper is to consider the variational problem (
1.1
)
with a more general class of potentials
V
,
including unbounded potentials, non-positive potentials,
and the Dirac delta measures,
and to extend the two-step minimization approach to them.
We provide various essential tools for this variational problem,
such as the decomposition principle that provides the basis
for the two-step minimization method,
the comparison principle,
the perturbation theorem,
the existence and some properties of the minimizer, the continuity of the minimum value
in the first minimization step, etc.

As applications of those tools, we consider two specific potential cases.
For a potential that contains the Dirac delta measure,
applying our comparison principle, we give an alternative proof
for the best constant of the Sobolev-type inequality

\|u\|_{\infty}\leq C\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\left(|u^{\prime}(x)|^{2}+\alpha|u(x)|^{2}\right)dx+\beta|u(0)|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

by
[
5
]
in Theorem
4.5
.
The other application is devoted to the case for a potential well.
By using the precise property of the minimizer, we will provide
sufficient conditions for the trapped mode in terms of the
depth and width of the potential well.

The structure of this paper is as follows.
Section
2
introduces a class of generalized potentials and
demonstrates the decomposition principle underlying
the two-step minimization approach.
In Section
3
, we survey the results of
[
1
]
for positive bounded potentials,
which are necessary for the discussion of this paper.
Section
4
describes a comparison principle and a perturbation theorem of
m(V)

for generalized potentials.
As their application, we give an alternative proof
for the potential given by a constant plus Dirac delta measure.
In Section
5
,
we study
the existence of the minimizer in the first minimization step
and also the continuity of the minimum value.
We finally provide a sufficient condition for the trapped mode
in the potential well.

Generalized potential and decomposition principle

In this paper, we deal with Sobolev-type inequalities
related to the Schrödinger-type operator
-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+V

with a potential term
V
.
We begin this section by introducing a general class of the potential
V
.
We define

X:=\{V:H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})\times H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})\to{\mathbb{R}};~\mbox{$V$: bounded symmetric bilinear map}\}.

Then, it is known that
X
is a Banach space over
{\mathbb{R}}
with the norm:

\|V\|_{X}:=\sup_{u,v\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}),u,v\not{\equiv}0}\frac{|V(u,v)|}{\|u\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}\|v\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}}.

Without loss of generality,
we suppose that
u\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
(or more generally,
u\in W^{1,p}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}})

for
p\in[1,\infty]
) always satisfies
u\in C^{0}({\mathbb{R}})
, since
an element of the function space
W^{1,p}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}})
has a continuous representation
(Theorem 8.8 of
[
2
]
). We also remark that

u\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
satisfies
u\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
and

\lim_{|x|\to\infty}u(x)=0
(Theorem 8.8 and Corollary 8.9 of
[
2
]
).

We remark that
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}})
(
1\leq p\leq\infty
) is continuously embedded
in
X
by identifying
V\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}})
with the following
\tilde{V}\in X
:

\tilde{V}(u,v):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}V(x)u(x)v(x)\,dx\quad(u,v\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})),

where
Vuv\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
is clear from
uv\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})\cap L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})\subset L^{q}({\mathbb{R}})

for any
q\in[1,\infty]
.

We denote the set of signed Radon measure
V
on
{\mathbb{R}}
with finite total variation
|V|_{TV}:=|V|({\mathbb{R}})<\infty

by
{\cal M}_{1}({\mathbb{R}})
. For example, the Dirac measure
\delta_{a}
belongs to
{\cal M}_{1}({\mathbb{R}})
.
It is defined by
\delta_{a}(A)=1
if
a\in A
,
and
\delta_{a}(A)=0
if
a\not\in A
for
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
A\in{\cal B}({\mathbb{R}})
,
where
{\cal B}({\mathbb{R}})
is the set of Borel sets on
{\mathbb{R}}
.
We also remark that
L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})\subset{\cal M}_{1}({\mathbb{R}})
and

|V|_{TV}=\|V\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}
holds for
V\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
.

Then,
V\in{\cal M}_{1}({\mathbb{R}})

is also considered as
V\in X

by identifying
V
with the following
\tilde{V}\in X
:

\tilde{V}(u,v):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}uv\,dV\quad(u,v\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})).

It is well-defined since
uv\in C^{0}({\mathbb{R}})\cap L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})

and
\|\tilde{V}\|_{X}\leq 2|V|_{TV}
holds since

\displaystyle\left|\tilde{V}(u,v)\right|\leq\|u\|_{\infty}\|v\|_{\infty}|V|_{TV}\leq 2\|u\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}\|v\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}|V|_{TV},

where the last inequality follows from
\|u\|_{\infty}\leq\sqrt{2}\|u\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}

(see p.213 of
[
2
]
). We note that the best constant of this inequality
is
\|u\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\|u\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}
(see
[
1
]
).

For
V\in X
, we define

\displaystyle I(u;V):=\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}^{2}+V(u,u)\quad(u\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})),

and define a Rayleigh-type quotient:

\displaystyle R(u;V):=\frac{I(u;V)}{\|u\|_{\infty}^{2}}\quad(u\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})\setminus\{0\}).

We also define
m(V)\in[-\infty,\infty)
and
M(V)
by

\displaystyle m(V):=\inf_{u\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}),u\not{\equiv}0}R(u;V),

(2.3)

\displaystyle M(V):=\left\{u\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})\setminus\{0\};~m(V)=R(u;V)\right\}.

Then, if and only if
m(V)>0
,
the following Sobolev-type inequality holds:

~{}^{\exists}C>0~~\text{s.t}~~\|u\|_{\infty}\leq C\,I(u;V)^{\frac{1}{2}}\quad(^{\forall}u\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})).

(2.4)

In this case,
C=m(V)^{-1/2}

gives the best constant of Sobolev-type inequality (
2.4
).

In this paper, we often consider the class of the generalized potentials

L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})+{\cal M}_{1}({\mathbb{R}})\subset X
, where

L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})+{\cal M}_{1}({\mathbb{R}})=\{V=V_{0}+V_{1}\in X;~V_{0}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}),~V_{1}\in{\cal M}_{1}({\mathbb{R}})\}.

We remark that
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}})\subset L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})+{\cal M}_{1}({\mathbb{R}})
holds
for any
p\in[1,\infty]
.
Indeed, it is trivial if
p=\infty
, and if
p\in[1,\infty)
,
let
V\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}})
and set

A:=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}};~|V(x)|>1\}
,
V_{0}(x):=(1-\chi_{A}(x))V(x)
, and
V_{1}(x):=\chi_{A}(x)V(x)
,
where
\chi_{A}
is the indicator function of
A
.
Then,
\|V_{0}\|_{\infty}\leq 1
and

\|V_{1}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}=\int_{A}|V(x)|\,dx\leq\int_{A}|V(x)|^{p}\,dx\leq\|V\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}})}^{p}<\infty.

Therefore, we obtain

V=V_{0}+V_{1}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})+L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})\subset L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})+{\cal M}_{1}({\mathbb{R}}),

since
V_{0}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
and
V_{1}\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})\subset{\cal M}_{1}({\mathbb{R}})
.

In
[
1
]
,
the authors proved the following decomposition principle of the minimization problem
(
2.3
) for general non-constant bounded positive potentials
V\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
,
and established the two-step minimization approach to study
the precise properties of the minimizer for the Sobolev-type inequality.
We aim to extend the two-step minimization approach
to the case of the generalized potential
V\in X
in this paper.

For
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
, we set

K_{a}:=\{u\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}});~u(a)=\|u\|_{\infty}=1\},

and define

F(a;V):=\inf_{u\in K_{a}}I(u;V).

We remark that
K_{a}
is a closed convex set in
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
, which implies
that
K_{a}
is weakly closed in
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
.

Theorem 2.1

(decomposition principle)
.

Let
V\in X
and set
m(V)
as (
2.3
). Then, we have

\displaystyle m(V)=\inf_{a\in{\mathbb{R}}}F(a;V).

(2.5)

Proof.

We first remark that
F(a;V)
and
m(V)

can have their values in
[-\infty,\infty)
.
For
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
, there exists
\{u_{a,n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset K_{a}

such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}I(u_{a,n};V)=F(a;V)
. Since

m(V)\leq R(u_{a,n};V)=I(u_{a,n};V),

it follows that
m(V)\leq F(a;V)
.
Let us define
\tilde{m}(V):=\inf_{a\in{\mathbb{R}}}F(a;V)
.
Then, taking the infimum concerning
a
in
m(V)\leq F(a;V)
, we obtain
m(V)\leq\tilde{m}(V)
.

Let
\{u_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a minimizing sequence attaining the infimum of (
2.3
).
Choosing
a_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}
as
|u_{n}(a_{n})|=\|u_{n}\|_{\infty}>0
,
we define
v_{n}:=u_{n}(a_{n})^{-1}u_{n}\in K_{a_{n}}
.
Then we have

m(V)=\lim_{n\to\infty}R(u_{n};V)=\lim_{n\to\infty}I(v_{n};V),

and
\tilde{m}(V)\leq m(V)
follows from
\tilde{m}(V)\leq F(a_{n};V)\leq I(v_{n};V)

as
n\to\infty
. Hence, we obtain
\tilde{m}(V)=m(V)
.
∎

Bounded positive potentials

We briefly summarize the results obtained in

[
1
]
for the case of bounded positive potentials.
In this section, we suppose

\displaystyle V\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}),\quad 0<v_{0}:=\essinf_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}}V(x),\quad v_{1}:=\esssup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}}V(x).

(3.1)

Then, for
u,~v\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
, we define

\displaystyle(u,v)_{V}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(u^{\prime}(x)v^{\prime}(x)+V(x)u(x)v(x)\right)\,dx,\quad\|u\|_{V}:=(u,u)_{V}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We remark that
(u,v)_{V}
defines an inner product on
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
.
The corresponding norm
\|u\|_{V}
is equivalent to the norm
of
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
and it satisfies
I(u;V)=\|u\|_{V}^{2}
.

We consider the first minimization step:

\displaystyle F(a;V)=\inf_{u\in K_{a}}\|u\|_{V}^{2}.

(3.2)

Theorem 3.1

(
[
1
]
)
.

We suppose the condition (
3.1
)
and fix
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
. There exists a unique minimizer
u_{a}\in K_{a}
to (
3.2
), that is,

\displaystyle u_{a}=\mathop{\rm arg~min}\limits\limits_{u\in K_{a}}\|u\|_{V}^{2},

(3.3)

\displaystyle F(a;V)=\min_{u\in K_{a}}\|u\|_{V}^{2}=\|u_{a}\|_{V}^{2},

and it satisfies the following properties:

\displaystyle u_{a}\in W^{2,\infty}({\mathbb{R}}{\hskip-0.3pt\setminus\hskip-0.3pt}\{a\})\quad\mbox{and}\quad u_{a}^{\prime\prime}(x)=V(x)u_{a}(x)~(\mbox{a.e.}~x\in{\mathbb{R}}{\hskip-0.3pt\setminus\hskip-0.3pt}\{a\}).

(3.4)

\displaystyle e^{-\sqrt{v_{1}}|x-a|}\leq u_{a}(x)\leq e^{-\sqrt{v_{0}}|x-a|}\quad(x\in{\mathbb{R}}),

(3.5)

\displaystyle\frac{v_{0}}{\sqrt{v_{1}}}e^{-\sqrt{v_{1}}|x-a|}\leq\mbox{\rm sgn}(a-x)u_{a}^{\prime}(x)\leq\frac{v_{1}}{\sqrt{v_{0}}}e^{-\sqrt{v_{0}}|x-a|}\quad(x\in{\mathbb{R}}{\hskip-0.3pt\setminus\hskip-0.3pt}\{a\}).

(3.6)

Theorem 3.2

(
[
1
]
)
.

We assume (
3.1
) and suppose that
V
is a non-decreasing function.
Then, it holds that

m(V)=\lim_{a\to-\infty}F(a;V)=2\sqrt{v_{0}}
.
Furthermore, if
v_{0}<v_{1}
, then
F
is a strictly increasing function
and
M(V)=\emptyset
. If
V
is constant,
then it holds that

m(V)=2\sqrt{V}
and

M(V)=\{cu_{a};~c\in{\mathbb{R}}{\hskip-0.3pt\setminus\hskip-0.3pt}\{0\},~a\in{\mathbb{R}}\}
,

.

Comparison principle and perturbation theorem

From this section onwards,
we consider the generalized potentials.
We consider the following comparison principle of
m(V)
.

Theorem 4.1

(comparison principle of
m(V)
)
.

We suppose that
V_{1},V_{2}\in X
and that
m(V_{1})\neq-\infty
or
m(V_{2})\neq-\infty
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\inf\limits_{u\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}),u\neq 0}\frac{(V_{1}-V_{2})(u,u)}{\|u\|_{\infty}^{2}}\leq m(V_{1})-m(V_{2})\leq\sup\limits_{u\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}),u\neq 0}\frac{(V_{1}-V_{2})(u,u)}{\|u\|_{\infty}^{2}}.

(4.7)

Proof.

For
V_{1}
, let
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
be a minimizing sequence
to the infimum
m(V_{1})
and suppose
\|u_{n}\|_{\infty}=1
.
Then, it satisfies

\lim_{n\to\infty}I(u_{n};V_{1})=m(V_{1})
and

\displaystyle I(u_{n};V_{1})-m(V_{2})\geq I(u_{n};V_{1})-I(u_{n};V_{2})=(V_{1}-V_{2})(u_{n},u_{n})\geq\inf\limits_{u\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}),u\neq 0}\frac{(V_{1}-V_{2})(u,u)}{\|u\|_{\infty}^{2}}.

Taking the limit as
n\to\infty
, we obtain the first inequality of (
4.7
).
By exchanging
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
, we derive the second inequality as

\displaystyle m(V_{1})-m(V_{2})\leq-\left(\inf\limits_{u\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}),u\neq 0}\frac{(V_{2}-V_{1})(u,u)}{\|u\|_{\infty}^{2}}\right)=\sup\limits_{u\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}),u\neq 0}\frac{(V_{1}-V_{2})(u,u)}{\|u\|_{\infty}^{2}}.

∎

Corollary 4.2
.

Under the condition of Theorem
4.1
, if
(V_{1}-V_{2})(u,u)\geq 0

for
u\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
, then
m(V_{2})\leq m(V_{1})
holds.

Also, from Theorem
4.1
, we immediately have the following theorem.

Theorem 4.3
.

We suppose that
V\in X
and
m(V)\neq-\infty
.
If
\mu\in{\cal M}_{1}({\mathbb{R}})
, then

\displaystyle-|\mu_{-}|_{TV}\leq m(V+\mu)-m(V)\leq|\mu_{+}|_{TV},

(4.8)

where
\mu_{+}
and
\mu_{-}
are the positve and negative parts of the Radon measure
\mu
.
In particular, we have

|m(V+\mu)-m(V)|\leq\max\big(|\mu_{-}|_{TV},|\mu_{+}|_{TV}\big)\leq|\mu|_{TV}.

Proof.

We apply Theorem
4.1
with
V_{1}=V+\mu
and
V_{2}=V
.
Then, we have

\displaystyle\inf\limits_{u\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}),u\neq 0}\frac{\mu(u,u)}{\|u\|_{\infty}^{2}}\leq m(V_{1})-m(V_{2})\leq\sup\limits_{u\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}),u\neq 0}\frac{\mu(u,u)}{\|u\|_{\infty}^{2}}.

Since
\mu(u,u)=\mu_{+}(u,u)-\mu_{-}(u,u)
,
paying attention to the following inequalities

-|\mu_{-}|_{TV}\|u\|_{\infty}^{2}\leq-\mu_{-}(u,u)\leq\mu(u,u)\leq\mu_{+}(u,u)\leq|\mu_{+}|_{TV}\|u\|_{\infty}^{2},

we obtain (
4.8
). The last inequality also follows from

|\mu|_{TV}=|\mu_{+}|_{TV}+|\mu_{-}|_{TV}
.
∎

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
V\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
with
\essinf_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}}V(x)>0
.
We suppose

\displaystyle m(V)=\lim_{a\to\infty}F(a;V),\quad\mbox{or}\quad m(V)=\lim_{a\to-\infty}F(a;V).

(4.9)

Let
p\in[1,\infty)
.
If
\mu\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}})+{\cal M}_{1}({\mathbb{R}})\subset X
is nonnegative,
i.e.,
\mu(u,u)\geq 0
for
u\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
,
then
m(V+\mu)=m(V)
and
M(V+\mu)\subset M(V)
hold.

Proof.

Choosing
V_{1}=V+\mu
and
V_{2}=V
in Theorem
4.1
, we have

\displaystyle m(V+\mu)-m(V)\geq\inf\limits_{u\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}),u\neq 0}\frac{\mu(u,u)}{\|u\|_{\infty}^{2}}\geq 0.

(4.10)

We define
u_{a}(x)
as in Theorem
3.1
. Then, we have

m(V+\mu)\leq I(u_{a};V+\mu)=I(u_{a};V)+\mu(u_{a},u_{a})=F(a;V)+\mu(u_{a},u_{a}).

From the assumption (
4.9
), taking the limit
a\to\infty
or
a\to-\infty
,
we obtain

\displaystyle m(V+\mu)\leq m(V)+\lim_{a\to\pm\infty}\mu(u_{a},u_{a})=m(V),

(4.11)

where the last equality holds as follows.

Let
\mu=\mu_{0}+\mu_{1}
with
\mu_{0}\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}})
and
\mu_{1}\in{\cal M}_{1}({\mathbb{R}})
.
Since
L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})\subset{\cal M}_{1}({\mathbb{R}})
, we assume
p\in(1,\infty)
without loss of generality
and define
q\in(1,\infty)
as
p^{-1}+q^{-1}=1
.
From the estimate (
3.5
), we have
|u_{a}(x)|\leq e^{-\sqrt{v_{0}}|x-a|}
.
For
R>0
, we define
I_{R}:=[-R,R]
and
J_{R}:={\mathbb{R}}\setminus I_{R}
,
and suppose
a\in J_{R}
.
Then,

|u_{a}(x)|\leq e^{-\sqrt{v_{0}}(|a|-R)}
holds for
x\in I_{R}
.
Hence, we have

\displaystyle\mu_{0}(u_{a},u_{a})

\displaystyle=\int_{I_{R}}\mu_{0}(x)|u_{a}(x)|^{2}\,dx+\int_{J_{R}}\mu_{0}(x)|u_{a}(x)|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq e^{-2\sqrt{v_{0}}(|a|-R)}\|\mu_{0}\|_{L^{1}(I_{R})}+\|\mu_{0}\|_{L^{p}(J_{R})}\|u_{a}^{2}\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{R}})}.

Noting that

\displaystyle\|u_{a}^{2}\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{R}})}\leq\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}e^{-2q\sqrt{v_{0}}|x-a|}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}=\left(q\sqrt{v_{0}}\right)^{-\frac{1}{q}},

for an arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
R>0
such that

\|\mu_{0}\|_{L^{p}(J_{R})}\|u_{a}^{2}\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{R}})}\leq\varepsilon
. Then, there exists
\tilde{R}>R
such that

e^{-2\sqrt{v_{0}}(|a|-R)}\|\mu_{0}\|_{L^{1}(I_{R})}\leq\varepsilon
holds for
|a|>\tilde{R}
.
It implies that
\lim_{|a|\to\infty}\mu_{0}(u_{a},u_{a})=0
.

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\mu_{1}(u_{a},u_{a})

\displaystyle=\int_{I_{R}}|u_{a}|^{2}\,d\mu_{1}+\int_{J_{R}}|u_{a}|^{2}\,d\mu_{1}\leq e^{-2\sqrt{v_{0}}(|a|-R)}|\mu_{1}|(I_{R})+|\mu_{1}|(J_{R}).

For an arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
R>0
such that

|\mu_{1}|(J_{R})\leq\varepsilon
. Then, there exists
\tilde{R}>R
such that

e^{-2\sqrt{v_{0}}(|a|-R)}|\mu_{1}|(I_{R})\leq\varepsilon
holds for
|a|>\tilde{R}
.
It implies that
\lim_{|a|\to\infty}\mu_{1}(u_{a},u_{a})=0
.

Hence, we conclude
m(V+\mu)=m(V)
from (
4.10
) and (
4.11
).
Moreover, for
u\in M(V+\mu)
, since we have

I(u;V)\leq I(u;V)+\mu(u,u)=I(u;V+\mu)=m(V+\mu)=m(V)\leq I(u;V),

I(u;V)=m(V)
follows and it implies the inclusion
M(V+\mu)\subset M(V)
.
∎

Using Theorem
4.4
,
we can give an alternative proof for the following result.
We define
\delta_{0}\in{\cal M}_{1}({\mathbb{R}})\subset X
by
\delta_{0}(u,v):=u(0)v(0)
.

Theorem 4.5

(Kametaka et al.
[
5
]
)
.

Let
\alpha>0
and
\beta\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
Then
m(\alpha+\beta\delta_{0})=2\sqrt{\alpha}-\beta_{-}
holds,
where
\beta_{-}=\max(-\beta,0)
.
In particular, if
2\sqrt{\alpha}+\beta>0
, then the Sobolev-type inequality

\|u\|_{\infty}\leq C\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\left(|u^{\prime}(x)|^{2}+\alpha|u(x)|^{2}\right)dx+\beta|u(0)|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

holds and its best constant is given by
C=(2\sqrt{\alpha}-\beta_{-})^{-\frac{1}{2}}
.

Proof.

For the case of
\beta\geq 0
,
m(\alpha+\beta\delta_{0})=m(\alpha)=2\sqrt{\alpha}=2\sqrt{\alpha}-\beta_{-}

holds from Theorems
3.2
and
4.4
, since
V=\alpha
satisfies the condition (
4.9
).

If
\beta<0
, from Theorem
4.1
with
V_{1}=\alpha+\beta\delta_{0}
and
V_{2}=\alpha
,
we have

\displaystyle m(\alpha+\beta\delta_{0})-m(\alpha)\geq\inf\limits_{u\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}),u\neq 0}\frac{\beta|u(0)|^{2}}{\|u\|_{\infty}^{2}}=\beta\left(\sup\limits_{u\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}),u\neq 0}\frac{|u(0)|^{2}}{\|u\|_{\infty}^{2}}\right)=\beta.

(4.12)

On the other hand, setting
u_{0}(x)=e^{-\sqrt{\alpha}|x|}
, we also have

\displaystyle m(\alpha+\beta\delta_{0})\leq I(u_{0};\alpha+\beta\delta_{0})=I(u_{0};\alpha)+\beta|u_{0}(0)|^{2}=m(\alpha)+\beta.

(4.13)

Hence, from (
4.12
) and (
4.13
), we obtain

m(\alpha+\beta\delta_{0})=m(\alpha)+\beta=2\sqrt{\alpha}-\beta_{-}
.
∎

Properties of the minimizers

In the following discussion, we will often
make the following assumption on the generalized potential
V\in X
:

\displaystyle V=V_{0}+V_{1},~~V_{0}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}),~~\essinf\,V_{0}>0,~~V_{1}\in{\cal M}_{1}({\mathbb{R}}).

(5.1)

We note that the next identity holds under the assumption (
5.1
),

\displaystyle I(u;V)=I(u;V_{0})+V_{1}(u,u)\quad(u\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})).

(5.2)

Lemma 5.1
.

We suppose that
V\in X
satisfies (
5.1
).

1.

There exist
C_{1}
,
C_{2}>0
such that
the following inequality holds for
u\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
:

\displaystyle\|u\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}^{2}\leq C_{1}I(u;V)+C_{2}\|u\|_{\infty}^{2}.

(5.3)

2.

I(\cdot;V)
is weakly lower semi-continuous in
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
.

Proof.

We set
\alpha:=\essinf\,V_{0}>0
.
For
u\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
, using (
5.2
), we have

\displaystyle\min(1,\alpha)\|u\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(|u^{\prime}(x)|^{2}+\alpha|u(x)|^{2}\right)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq I(u;V_{0})

\displaystyle=I(u;V)-V_{1}(u,u)

\displaystyle\leq I(u;V)+|V_{1}|_{TV}\|u\|_{\infty}^{2}.

Hence, we have the first assertion (
5.3
) by setting

\displaystyle C_{1}:=\frac{1}{\min(1,\alpha)},\quad C_{2}:=\frac{|V_{1}|_{TV}}{\min(1,\alpha)}.

Let us suppose that
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
and
u\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})

satisfy

u_{n}\to u
weakly in
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
as
n\to\infty
.
Since
\essinf\,V_{0}>0
, the inner product

(\cdot,\cdot)_{V_{0}}
gives
an equivalent topology on
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
and
I(u;V_{0})=(u,u)_{V_{0}}
holds.
Therefore, it follows that

I(\cdot;V_{0})
is weakly lower semi-continuous in
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
, i.e.,
it holds that

\displaystyle I(u;V_{0})\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}I(u_{n};V_{0}).

(5.4)

Paying attention to the identity (
5.2
),
it is sufficient to show that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}V_{1}(u_{n},u_{n})=V_{1}(u,u),

(5.5)

to prove the second assertion of the lemma.

For any
\varepsilon>0
,
from
|V_{1}|_{TV}<\infty
,
there exists
R>0
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\{|x|\geq R\}}\,d|V_{1}|\leq\varepsilon.

(5.6)

Since
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is boudned in
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
,
from the Rellich-Kondrachov theorem for the compact embedding

H^{1}(-R,R)\subset C^{0}([-R,R])
,
there exists a subsequence which uniformly convergent on
[-R,R]
.
However, the limit function of the uniform convergence
coincides with
u
. As a result, the whole sequence
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}

converges uniformly to
u
on
[-R,R]
.
Hence,
there exists
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that

\displaystyle\|u_{n}-u\|_{L^{\infty}(-R,R)}<\varepsilon\quad(n\geq N).

(5.7)

We set

B:=\sup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\|u_{n}\|_{\infty}<\infty
.
It implies
\|u\|_{\infty}\leq B
.
Thus, from (
5.6
) and (
5.7
), we obtain

\displaystyle\big|V_{1}(u_{n},u_{n})-V_{1}(u,u)\big|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(|u_{n}|^{2}-|u|^{2}\right)\,dV_{1}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\{|x|<R\}}\left|u_{n}+u\right|\,\left|u_{n}-u\right|\,d|V_{1}|+\int_{\{|x|\geq R\}}\left(|u_{n}|^{2}+|u|^{2}\right)\,d|V_{1}|

\displaystyle\leq 2B|V_{1}|_{TV}\|u_{n}-u\|_{L^{\infty}(-R,R)}+2B^{2}\int_{\{|x|\geq R\}}\,d|V_{1}|

\displaystyle\leq 2B\left(|V_{1}|_{TV}+B\right)\varepsilon.

From this estimate, we obtain (
5.5
) and

\displaystyle I(u;V)=I(u;V_{0})+V_{1}(u,u)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}I(u_{n};V_{0})+\lim_{n\to\infty}V_{1}(u_{n},u_{n})=\liminf_{n\to\infty}I(u_{n};V).

∎

Theorem 5.2
.

We suppose (
5.1
).
Then, for each
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
, there exists
u_{a}\in K_{a}
such that

\displaystyle I(u_{a};V)=\min_{u\in K_{a}}I(u;V),

(5.8)

i.e.,
F(a;V)
is attained as
F(a;V)=I(u_{a};V)
.

Proof.

For any fixed
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
let
\{u_{a,n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset K_{a}

be a minimizing sequence for
I(\cdot;V)
in
K_{a}
, i.e.,

\lim_{n\to\infty}I(u_{a,n};V)=F(a;V)
.
From (
5.3
),
\{u_{a,n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is bounded in
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
.
Replacing
\{u_{a,n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
by a subsequence if necessary,
there exists
u_{a}\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
such that
u_{a,n}
weakly converges
to
u_{a}
in
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
as
n\to\infty
.
Since
u_{a,n}\in K_{a}
and
K_{a}
is weakly closed,
u_{a}\in K_{a}
holds.
Hence, we obtain (
5.8
) as

I(u_{a};V)\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}I(u_{a,n};V)=F(a;V)

from
the second claim of Lemma
5.1
.
∎

Theorem 5.3
.

We suppose that (
5.1
) holds with
V_{1}\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
,
and that
u_{a}\in K_{a}
satisfies (
5.8
).
Then,
u_{a}(x)>0
holds for
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
, and, setting
J:=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}};~u_{a}(x)<1\}
,

u_{a}\in W^{2,1}(J)
and
u_{a}^{\prime\prime}(x)=V(x)u_{a}(x)
hold for a.e.
x\in J
.

Proof.

For
u_{a}\in K_{a}
which satisfies (
5.8
), we define
J:=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}};~|u_{a}(x)|<1\}
,
which is an open set in
{\mathbb{R}}
since
u_{a}
is continuous.
Then, for any
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(J\,)
, there exists
\tau>0
such that

u_{a}+t\varphi\in K_{a}
for
t\in(-\tau,\tau)
.
Since
I(u_{a}+t\varphi;V)
has a local minimum at
t=0
, we obtain

\displaystyle 0=\frac{d}{dt}I(u_{a}+t\varphi;V)\big|_{t=0}=2\int_{J}\left(u_{a}^{\prime}(x)\varphi^{\prime}(x)+V(x)u_{a}(x)\varphi(x)\right)\,dx,

which implies that
u_{a}\in W^{2,1}(J\,)

and
u_{a}^{\prime\prime}(x)=V(x)u_{a}(x)
holds for a.e.
x\in J
.

We set
\bar{u}_{a}(x):=|u_{a}(x)|
. Then, since
\bar{u}_{a}\in K_{a}
and

I(\bar{u}_{a};V)=I(u_{a};V)=F(a;V)
, we have that

\bar{u}_{a}\in W^{2,1}(J\,)
and

\displaystyle\bar{u}_{a}^{\prime\prime}(x)=V(x)\bar{u}_{a}(x)\quad(\mbox{a.e. }x\in J\,).

(5.9)

Let
J_{0}
be an open component of
J
.
Then,

\displaystyle\bar{u}_{a}(x)=1\quad(x\in\overline{J_{0}}\setminus J_{0}\neq\emptyset),

(5.10)

from the definition of
J
.

We assume that
u_{a}(x_{0})=0
holds at some
x_{0}\in J_{0}
.
Then
\bar{u}_{a}(x_{0})=\bar{u}_{a}^{\prime}(x_{0})=0
holds,
since
\bar{u}_{a}\in C^{1}(J)
and
\bar{u}_{a}(x)\geq 0
.
From (
5.9
), for
x\in J_{0}\cap[x_{0},\infty)
, we have

\displaystyle\bar{u}_{a}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{x_{0}}^{x}\bar{u}_{a}^{\prime}(s)\,ds\leq\int_{x_{0}}^{x}|\bar{u}_{a}^{\prime}(s)|\,ds,

\displaystyle|\bar{u}_{a}^{\prime}(x)|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{x_{0}}^{x}\bar{u}_{a}^{\prime\prime}(s)\,ds\right|\leq\int_{x_{0}}^{x}|V(s)|\bar{u}_{a}(s)\,ds,

Setting
v(x):=\bar{u}_{a}(x)+|\bar{u}_{a}^{\prime}(x)|
,
we have

\displaystyle v(x)\leq\int_{x_{0}}^{x}(1+|V(s)|)v(s)\,ds\quad(x\in J_{0}\cap[x_{0},\infty)).

(5.11)

Applying the Gronwall inequality to (
5.11
),
we obtain that
v(x)\leq 0
for
x\in J_{0}\cap[x_{0},\infty)
.
Since we can similarly obtain
v(x)\leq 0
for
x\in J_{0}\cap(-\infty,x_{0}]
too,

v(x)=0
holds for
x\in J_{0}
.
However, this contradicts (
5.10
).
Hence, we conclude that
u_{a}(x)\neq 0
for
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
It implies that
u_{a}(x)=\bar{u}_{a}(x)>0
for
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
and

J=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}};~u_{a}(x)<1\}
.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

We suppose
V\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})+{\cal M}_{1}({\mathbb{R}})
and
u\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
.
Then, it holds that

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}I(u(\cdot-h);V)=I(u;V).

(5.12)

Proof.

First, for
x,~h\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
u\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
, we remark that

|u(x)-u(x-h)|=\left|\int_{x-h}^{x}u^{\prime}(y)\,dy\right|\leq\left(\left|\int_{x-h}^{x}|u^{\prime}(y)|^{2}\,dy\right|\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}|h|^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\|u\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}|h|^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We use the idea in the proof of
Proposition 4.2.6 of
[
7
]
.
For
h\in{\mathbb{R}}
, we set

f_{h}(x):=|u(x)|+|u(x-h)|-|u(x)-u(x-h)|\geq 0\quad(x\in{\mathbb{R}}).

Since
u
is continuous,
\lim_{h\to 0}f_{h}(x)=2|u(x)|
holds for
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
Applying Fatou’s lemma, we obtain

\displaystyle 2\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u(x)|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{h\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f_{h}(x)\,dx

\displaystyle=\liminf_{h\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big(|u(x)|+|u(x-h)|-|u(x)-u(x-h)|\big)\,dx

\displaystyle=2\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u(x)|\,dx+\liminf_{h\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big(-|u(x)-u(x-h)|\big)\,dx

\displaystyle=2\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u(x)|\,dx-\limsup_{h\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u(x)-u(x-h)|\,dx.

This implies
\limsup_{h\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u(x)-u(x-h)|\,dx\leq 0

and also

\lim_{h\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u(x)-u(x-h)|\,dx=0.

We write
V=V_{0}+V_{1}
, where
V_{0}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
and
V_{1}\in{\cal M}_{1}({\mathbb{R}})
.
Then we have

\displaystyle I(u;V)-I(u(\cdot-h);V)

\displaystyle=V(u,u)-V(u(\cdot-h),u(\cdot-h))

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}V_{0}(x)\left(|u(x)|^{2}-|u(x-h)|^{2}\right)\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(|u|^{2}-|u(\cdot-h)|^{2}\right)\,dV_{1}.

Hence, we obtain (
5.12
) from

\displaystyle\big|I(u;V)-I(u(\cdot-h);V)\big|

\displaystyle\leq 2\|V_{0}\|_{\infty}\|u\|_{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u(x)-u(x-h)|\,dx+2\|u\|_{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u-u(\cdot-h)|\,d|V_{1}|

\displaystyle\leq 2\|V_{0}\|_{\infty}\|u\|_{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u(x)-u(x-h)|\,dx+2\|u\|_{\infty}|V_{1}|_{TV}\|u\|_{H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})}|h|^{\frac{1}{2}}.

∎

Theorem 5.5
.

If
V\in X
satisfies (
5.1
), then
F(\cdot;V)\in C^{0}({\mathbb{R}})
holds.

Proof.

For
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
and any convergent sequence
a_{n}\to a
as
n\to\infty
,
from Theorem
5.2
,
there exists
u_{a}\in K_{a}
and
u_{a_{n}}\in K_{a_{n}}
such that

F(a;V)=I(u_{a};V)
and
F(a_{n};V)=I(u_{a_{n}};V)
.
We set
\tilde{u}_{a_{n}}:=u_{a}(\cdot+a-a_{n})\in K_{a_{n}}
.
Then,
F(a_{n};V)\leq I(\tilde{u}_{a_{n}};V)
holds.
Applying Lemma
5.4
,
we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}F(a_{n};V)\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}I(\tilde{u}_{a_{n}};V)=I(u_{a};V)=F(a;V).

(5.13)

In particular, we obtain

\sup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}I(u_{a_{n}};V)=\sup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}F(a_{n};V)<\infty.

Then, from Lemma
5.1
, it follows that

\{u_{a_{n}}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is bounded in
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
.
Therefore,
replacing
\{a_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
by a subsequence if necessary,
there exists
v_{a}\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
such that

u_{a_{n}}
weakly converges to
v_{a}
in
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
as
n\to\infty
.
It implies
v_{a}\in K_{a}
and

\displaystyle I(v_{a};V)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}I(u_{a_{n}};V).

Thus, it holds that

F(a;V)\leq I(v_{a};V)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}I(u_{a_{n}};V)=\liminf_{n\to\infty}F(a_{n};V)\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}F(a_{n};V)\leq F(a;V).

Hence, we obtain
\lim_{n\to\infty}F(a_{n};V)=F(a;V)

and conclude that
F\in C^{0}({\mathbb{R}})
.
∎

The following theorem gives a sufficient condition for the trapped mode
by a potential well in terms of the width and depth of the potential well.

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
\alpha,\beta>0
and
b<c
.
We suppose that
V\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})\subset X

satisfies
V(x)\geq\alpha
for a.e.
x\in(-\infty,b)\cup(c,\infty)

and
V(x)=-\beta
for
x\in(b,c)
.
If
\sqrt{\beta}(c-b)\geq\pi
, then
there exists
a\in[b,c]
such that
F(a;V)=m(V)
.

Remark 5.7
.

In Theorem
5.6
,
c-b
represents the width of the potential well,
and
\beta
is the depth of the potential well.
The condition
\sqrt{\beta}(c-b)\geq\pi
gives a sufficient condition
for the trapped mode in terms of the width and depth of the potential well.

Proof of Theorem
5.6
.

For
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
, from Theorems
5.2
and
5.3
,
there exists
u_{a}\in K_{a}
such that

I(u_{a};V)=F(a;V)
and
u_{a}(x)>0
for
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
Let
a\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus[b,c]
.
If
u_{a}(x)<1
for
x\in[b,c]
, then,
from Theorem
5.3
,
it has to satisfy
u_{a}^{\prime\prime}(x)+\beta u_{a}(x)=0

and

\displaystyle u_{a}(x)=C\sin(\sqrt{\beta}x+\theta)\quad(x\in(b,c)),

(5.14)

where

C\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}
are some constants. But it is impossible for a function of the
form (
5.14
) to
satisfy the condition
0<u_{a}(x)<1
for
x\in[b,c]

if
c-b\geq\pi/\sqrt{\beta}
.
Hence, there exists
\tilde{a}\in[b,c]
such that

u_{a}\in K_{\tilde{a}}
holds.
Since
F(\tilde{a};V)\leq I(u_{a};V)=F(a;V)
, we conclude that

~{}^{\forall}a\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus[b,c],~^{\exists}\tilde{a}\in[b,c]~~\text{s.t.}~~F(\tilde{a};V)\leq F(a;V).

(5.15)

Since
F(\cdot;V)\in C^{0}({\mathbb{R}})
holds from Theorem
5.5
,
we obtain

\inf_{a\in{\mathbb{R}}}F(a;V)=\inf_{a\in[b,c]}F(a;V)=\min_{a\in[b,c]}F(a;V).

Therefore, from Theorem
2.1
, there exists
a\in[b,c]
such that
F(a;V)=m(V)
.
∎

Small-Gain Theorem for Safety Verification of Interconnected Systems
Ziliang Lyu, Xiangru Xu, and Yiguang Hong

Abstract
A small-gain theorem in the formulation of barrier function is developed in this work for safety verification of interconnected systems. This result is helpful to verify input-to-state safety (ISSf) of the overall system from the safety information encoded in the subsystem’s ISSf-barrier function. Also, it can be used to obtain a safety set in a higher dimensional space from the safety sets in two lower dimensional spaces.



I
Introduction

Safety is crucial in the design of control systems, which requires any trajectories of the control systems initialized in a prescribed safety set to be kept out of the unsafe region. Applications concerned with safety are ubiquitous in our daily life, ranging from the cars on the ground to the aeroplanes in the air. For example, autonomous vehicles are equipped with lane keeping modules
[
1
]
, a robot team is designed to avoid the collision between robots
[
2
]
, and aircrafts must satisfy the safety requirement during takeoff and landing
[
3
]
.

For safety-critical systems, a rigorous verification of safety is the first step towards other control objectives (e.g., stabilization and regulation). In general, the techniques for safety verification can be classified into two sorts: model checking
[
4
]
and deductive verification
[
5
]
. Compared with the former one, deductive verification provides a safety certificate by mathematical inferences rather than exhaustively checking all of the possible system behaviors. The barrier function gives a promising deductive verification approach, with a set of Lyapunov-like criterions for safety verification; e.g., see
[
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
]
for more details. In
[
6
]
, barrier functions were used to formulate the verification tasks as convex programming problems. A framework by combining control Lyapunov functions and barrier functions was proposed in
[
7
]
to balance the objective of stabilization and safety verification. In
[
8
]
, a novel barrier function, called the zeroing barrier function, was proposed for establishing safety and analyzing the robustness of safe sets. Later,
[
10
]
redefined a new notion of input-to-state safety (ISSf) and provided a sufficient condition in the sense of barrier function to check ISSf, which could be regarded as the counterpart of Sontag’s input-to-state stability (ISS)
[
14
]
for safety verification. However, it should be mentioned that most of these results were not given for interconnected systems.

The small gain technique is effective for interconnected systems to keep good properties, which completes the verification tasks of a large-scale system to be completed by analyzing its less complicated subsystems. In the stability analysis, this technique has been investigated
[
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
19
]
. For example, the Lyapunov-based small-gain theorem developed in
[
16
]
has been found useful to establish ISS for interconnected systems. This result has been extended to switched systems
[
17
]
and hybrid systems
[
19
]
in recent years.

Considering that the barrier function provides a Lyapunov-like verification approach, it is natural to ask a question: whether there exists a small-gain condition such that one can establish ISSf for the overall system from the safety information encoded in the subsystem’s ISSf-barrier function. This question is not trivial. First, the existing small-gain theorems for stability rely on some nonnegative assumptions, which cannot be met in safety verification. Therefore, we cannot directly employ the tools developed in
[
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
19
]
to analyze the small-gain condition for safety verification. Second, the ISS plays important roles on the small-gain conditions, but there are no small-gain results of ISSf.

The objective of this paper is to develop a small-gain theorem based on barrier functions for safety verification of interconnected system, in order to establish a higher dimensional safety set from two lower dimensional safety sets. Based on the proposed ISSf-barrier function, we prove that, if the absolute value of the composition of ISSf gains is smaller than that of the identity function, then the overall system is ISSf. This condition is different from traditional small-gain conditions for stability.

II
Preliminaries

Consider the system

\displaystyle\dot{x}=f(x,u),\;\;x(t_{0})=x_{0}

(1)

where
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the state,
u\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
is the external input, and the vector field
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is locally Lipschitz continuous with
f(0,0)=0
.

We first review two definitions related to safety.

Definition 1

(Forward Robust Invariance
[
21
]
)
.

A set
\mathcal{S}
is forward robustly invariant if for every
x_{0}\in\mathcal{S}
, there exists an external input
u
such that the solution
x(t)
to (
1
) satisfies
x(t)\in\mathcal{S}
for all
t\geq{t}_{0}
.

Definition 2

(Input-to-State Safety
[
10
]
)
.

The system (
1
) is input-to-state safe (ISSf) on the set

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\gamma(\|u\|)}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:h(x)+\gamma(\|u\|)\geq 0\}

(2)

with respect to the external input
u
, if
\mathcal{S}_{\gamma(\|u\|)}
is forward robustly invariant. Herein,
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a continuously differentiable function, and
\gamma
is a function of class
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
. In particular, we say that system (
1
) is safe if there is no external input (i.e.,
u=0
), in which case
S_{\gamma(\|u\|)}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:h(x)\geq 0\}
.

Because in the proof of our small-gain theorem we need to handle nonsmooth functions, here we introduce the Dini derivative and corresponding results.

Definition 3

(Dini Derivative
[
22
]
)
.

Consider a function
h:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. The upper and the lower Dini derivatives of
h
at
r\in\mathbb{R}
are, respectively, defined by

\displaystyle D^{+}h(r)=\limsup_{s\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{h(r+s)-h(r)}{s},

\displaystyle D_{+}h(r)=\liminf_{s\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{h(r+s)-h(r)}{s}.

Given a continuous function
h:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, it is known that
h
is not increasing at
r\in\mathbb{R}
if and only if the Dini derivative of
h
at
r
is nonpositive, and is not decreasing if and only if the Dini derivative of
h
at
r
is nonnegative.

Lemma 1
.

For
i=1,2
, suppose that
h_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is locally Lipschitz and let
h(x)=\min\{h_{1}(x_{1}),h_{2}(x_{2})\}
. Then
h(x)
is locally Lipschitz as well, and

\displaystyle D_{+}{h}(x)=\dot{h}_{1}(x_{1}),\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\,\text{if }h_{1}(x_{1})<h_{2}(x_{2});

\displaystyle D_{+}h(x)=\dot{h}_{2}(x_{2}),\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\,\text{if }h_{1}(x_{1})>h_{2}(x_{2});

\displaystyle D_{+}h(x)=\min\{\dot{h}_{1}(x_{1}),\dot{h}_{2}(x_{2})\},\;\;\text{if }h_{1}(x_{1})=h_{2}(x_{2}).

Let
f_{i}(x)=-h_{i}(x)
and
f(x)=-h(x)
. Then Lemma
1
follows directly from
[
23
, Theorem 2.1]
due to
D^{+}f(x)=-D_{+}h(x)
and
\max\{\dot{f}_{1}(x_{1}),\dot{f}_{2}(x_{2})\}=-\min\{\dot{h}_{1}(x_{1}),\dot{h}_{2}(x_{2})\}
.

Lemma 2

(Comparison Principle
[
24
]
)
.

Consider

\displaystyle\dot{x}=f(x),\;\;x(t_{0})=x_{0}

where
f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is locally Lipschitz. Let
[t_{0},T)
be the maximal interval of existence of the solution
x(t)
. Suppose that
y(t)
is a continuous function with

\displaystyle D_{+}y(t)\geq{f}(y(t)),\;\;y(t_{0})\geq{x}_{0}.

(3)

Then
y(t)\geq{x}(t)
for all
t\in[t_{0},T)
.

III
ISSf-Barrier Function

In this section, we present a new notion of ISSf-barrier function, which is suitable to characterize the ISSf gains of our small-gain condition.

Definition 4

(ISSf-Barrier Function)
.

A continuously differentiable function
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is called an ISSf-barrier function for system (
1
), if there exist functions
\gamma
of class
\mathcal{K}
and
\alpha
of extended class
\mathcal{K}
such that for each
u\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,

\displaystyle h(x)\leq-\gamma(|u|)\Rightarrow\nabla{h}(x)f(x,u)\geq-\alpha(h(x)).

(4)

Herein,
\gamma
is referred to as the ISSf gain.

Note that
[
10
]
has presented another definition of ISSf-barrier functions; referring to (
5
) below. The relation between the ISSf-barrier function defined in (
4
) and the one of
[
10
]
is shown by the following result.

Proposition 1
.

A continuously differentiable function
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is an ISSf-barrier function for system (
1
) with
\alpha
of extended class
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
if and only if there exist functions
\chi
of extended class
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
and
\phi
of class
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
such that

\displaystyle\nabla{h}(x)f(x,u)\geq-\chi(h(x))-\phi(|u|).

(5)

Proof.

Select a constant
c\in(0,1)
. Then it follows from (
5
) that

\displaystyle h(x)\leq-\hat{\phi}(|u|)\Rightarrow\nabla{h}(x)f(x,u)\geq-(1-c)\chi(h(x))

where
\hat{\phi}(r)=-\chi^{-1}(-\phi(r)/c)
. The remainder is to verify that
\hat{\phi}
is of class
\mathcal{K}
. To see this, note that
\hat{\chi}(r)=-\chi^{-1}(-r/c)
is continuous and strictly increasing on the interval
[0,\infty)
, which in turn, implies that
\hat{\phi}(r)=\hat{\chi}\circ\phi(r)
is of class
\mathcal{K}
.

Then we show that the converse is also true. Assume that (
4
) holds with some
\alpha
of extended class
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
and
\gamma
of class
\mathcal{K}
. According to (
4
), we have the following two cases.

Case 1.

h(x)\leq-\gamma(|u|)
. In this case, there exists a function
\phi
of class
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
such that,

\displaystyle\nabla{h}(x)f(x,u)\geq-\alpha(h(x))\geq-\alpha(h(x))-\phi(|u|).

(6)

Case 2.

h(x)>-\gamma(|u|)
. Since
\alpha
is of extended class
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
,

\displaystyle\nabla{h}(x)f(x,u)+\alpha(h(x))

\displaystyle\geq\nabla{h}(x)f(x,u)+\alpha(-\gamma(|u|))

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{h(x)\geq-\gamma(|u|)}\nabla{h}(x)f(x,u)+\alpha(-\gamma(|u|))

(7)

Let
\tilde{\phi}(r)=\max\{0,\hat{\phi}(r)\}
with

\displaystyle\hat{\phi}(r)=-\inf_{h(x)\geq-\gamma(|r|)}\nabla{h}(x)f(x,r)+\alpha(-\gamma(|r|)).

This implies that
\tilde{\phi}
is continuous and
\tilde{\phi}(0)=0
(recalling
f(0,0)=0
). Note that
\tilde{\phi}(r)\geq 0
for each
r\geq 0
. By combining this with (
7
), we can select a class
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
function
\phi
with
\phi(r)\geq\tilde{\phi}(r)
for all
{r}\geq 0
such that

\displaystyle\nabla{h}(x)f(x,u)+\alpha(h(x))\geq-\phi(|u|).

By combining the two cases above, we conclude that the converse is true as well.

\Box

Then we are ready to present the main result of this section, which can be used as a tool to establish ISSf.

Theorem 1
.

Consider the system (
1
) and the set
\mathcal{S}_{\gamma(\|u\|)}
defined in (
2
). Suppose that
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is an ISSf-barrier function satisfying (
4
). Then,

(i)
\mathcal{S}_{\gamma(\|u\|)}
is forward robustly invariant, and system (
1
) is ISSf on
\mathcal{S}_{\gamma(\|u\|)}
;

(ii) the safety set
\mathcal{S}_{\gamma(\|u\|)}
is ISS.

Proof.
We show the conclusion of (i) by contradiction. If this is not true, then there exist some
t\geq{t_{0}}
and some
\epsilon>0
such that
h(x(t))<-\gamma(\|u\|)-\epsilon
. Let
\tau=\inf\{t\geq{t_{0}}:h(x(t))\leq-\gamma(\|u\|)-\epsilon\}
. Therefore,
h(x(\tau))\leq-\gamma(\|u\|)\leq-\gamma(|u(\tau)|)
, which in turn, implies that

\displaystyle\dfrac{\text{d}}{\text{d}t}\bigg|_{t=\tau}h(x(t))

\displaystyle=\nabla{h}(x(\tau))f(x(\tau),u(\tau))

\displaystyle\geq-\alpha(h(x(\tau)))>0.

(8)

Thus,
h(x(t))\leq{h}(x(\tau))
for some
t
in
(t_{0},\tau)
. This contradicts the minimality of
\tau
and consequently,
x(t)\in\mathcal{S}_{\gamma(\|u\|)}
for all
t\geq{t_{0}}
.

Then we consider (ii). Construct a Lyapunov-like function as in
[
8
]
:

\displaystyle V(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&\text{if }x\in\mathcal{S}_{\gamma(\|u\|)}\\
-h(x),&\text{if }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\mathcal{S}_{\gamma(\|u\|)}\end{array}\right.

With (
4
), we have

\displaystyle V(x)\geq\gamma(|u|)\Rightarrow\nabla{V}(x)f(x,u)\leq\alpha(-V(x)),\;\;\forall{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\mathcal{S}_{\gamma(\|u\|)}.

Moreover, with
[
14
, Lemma 2.14]
and
[
8
, Proposition 4]
, we conclude that the set
\mathcal{S}_{\gamma(\|u\|)}
is ISS.

\Box

IV
Small-Gain Theorem for Safety Verification

Consider the interconnected system

\displaystyle\dot{x}_{1}=f_{1}(x_{1},x_{2},u_{1})

(11a)

\displaystyle\dot{x}_{2}=f_{2}(x_{1},x_{2},u_{2})

(11b)

where, for
i=1,2
,
x_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}}
,
u_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{i}}
and
f_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}}\times\mathbb{R}^{m_{i}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}}
is locally Lipschitz. Let
x=(x_{1}^{\text{T}},x_{2}^{\text{T}})^{\text{T}}
,
u=(u_{1}^{\text{T}},u_{2}^{\text{T}})^{\text{T}}
and
f(x,u)=(f_{1}(x_{1},x_{2},u_{1})^{\text{T}},f_{2}(x_{1},x_{2},u_{2})^{\text{T}})^{\text{T}}

The small-gain theorem for safety verification of the interconnected system (

) is summarized as follows.

Theorem 2
.

Consider the interconnected system given in (
11
). Assume that there exist functions
\alpha_{i}
of extended class
\mathcal{K}
,
\phi_{i}
of extended class
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
and
\gamma_{i}
of class
\mathcal{K}
such that

\displaystyle h_{1}(x_{1})\leq-\max\{\phi_{1}(|h_{2}(x_{2})|),\gamma_{1}(|u_{1}|)\}

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\Rightarrow\nabla{h}_{1}(x_{1})f_{1}(x_{1},x_{2},u_{1})\geq-\alpha_{1}(h_{1}(x_{1})),

(12)

\displaystyle h_{2}(x_{2})\leq-\max\{\phi_{2}(|h_{1}(x_{1})|),\gamma_{2}(|u_{2}|)\}

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\Rightarrow\nabla{h}_{2}({x}_{2})f_{2}(x_{1},x_{2},u_{2})\geq-\alpha_{2}(h_{2}(x_{2})).

(13)

Then there exist an ISS-barrier function
h:\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, which is dependent on
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
, and a class
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
function
\gamma:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, which is dependent on
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
, such that the interconnected system (
11
) is ISSf on the set

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\gamma(\|u\|)}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:h(x)+\gamma(\|u\|)\geq 0\}

(14)

with respect to the external input
u
, if

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\phi_{1}\circ\phi_{2}(r)>r,&\forall{r}<0;\\
\phi_{1}\circ\phi_{2}(r)<r,&\forall{r}>0.\end{array}\right.

Remark 1
.

Clearly,
h_{i}(x_{i})
is an ISSf-barrier function for
x_{i}
-subsystem. According to Theorem
1
, the barrier-type conditions given in (
12
) and (
13
) imply that the sets

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{1}=\big\{x_{1}:h_{1}(x_{1})+\max\{\phi_{1}(\|h_{2}(x_{2})\|),\gamma_{1}(\|u_{1}\|)\}\geq 0\big\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{2}=\big\{x_{2}:h_{2}(x_{2})+\max\{\phi_{2}(\|h_{1}(x_{1})\|),\gamma_{2}(\|u_{2}\|)\}\geq 0\big\}

are forward robustly invariant. Intuitively, Theorem
2
can be regarded as a tool to establish a robustly invariant set
\mathcal{S}_{\gamma(\|u\|)}
in a higher dimensional space from the robustly invariant sets
\mathcal{S}_{1}
and
\mathcal{S}_{2}
in lower dimensional spaces. This tool is particularly useful for safety verification of large scale systems, since it allows the safety verification to be completed by analyzing its less complicated subsystems rather than analyzing the overall complex system.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

By applying Lemma
4

\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}

\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
function
\phi
, which is continuously differentiable on
\mathbb{R}\backslash 0

\displaystyle\phi_{1}^{-1}(r)<\phi(r)<\phi_{2}(r),\;\;\forall r<0;

(17a)

\displaystyle\phi_{2}(r)<\phi(r)<\phi_{1}^{-1}(r),\;\;\forall r>0.

(17b)

Define a function

\displaystyle h(x)=\min\{\phi(h_{1}(x_{1})),h_{2}(x_{2})\}.

(18)

Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\phi_{1}(\|h_{2}\|)}=\{x_{1}:h_{1}(x_{1})+\phi_{1}(\|h_{2}\|)\geq 0\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\gamma_{1}(\|u_{1}\|)}=\{x_{1}:h_{1}(x_{1})+\gamma_{1}(\|u_{1}\|)\geq 0\},

Clearly,
\mathcal{S}_{1}=\mathcal{S}_{\phi_{1}(\|h_{2}\|)}\cup\mathcal{S}_{\gamma_{1}(\|u_{1}\|)}

\mathcal{S}_{2}=\mathcal{S}_{\phi_{2}(\|h_{1}\|)}\cup\mathcal{S}_{\gamma_{2}(\|u_{2}\|)}

For every

\phi(h_{1}(x_{1}))<h_{2}(x_{2})

\displaystyle\phi_{1}^{-1}(h_{1}(x_{1}))<\phi(h_{1}(x_{1}))<h_{2}(x_{2})

Case 2.

\phi(h_{1}(x_{1}))>h_{2}(x_{2})

\displaystyle h_{2}(x_{2})<\phi(h_{1}(x_{1}))<\phi_{1}(h_{1}(x_{1}))

\displaystyle D_{+}{h}(x)=\nabla{h}_{2}(x_{2})f_{2}(x_{1},x_{2},u_{2})\geq-\alpha_{2}(h_{2}(x_{2}))

(20)

when
h(x)\leq-\gamma_{2}(|u_{2}|)
.

Case 3.

\phi(h_{1}(x_{1}))=h_{2}(x_{2})

\displaystyle D_{+}{h}(x)\geq-\max\{\phi^{\prime}(h_{1}(x_{1}))\alpha_{1}(h_{1}(x_{1})),\alpha_{2}(h_{2}(x_{2}))\}

\alpha(r):=\max\{\phi^{\prime}(r)\alpha_{1}(r),\alpha_{2}(r)\}

\phi^{\prime}(r)>0

\alpha

\displaystyle h(x)\leq-\gamma(|u|){\Rightarrow}D_{+}{h}(x)\geq-\alpha(h(x)).

Without loss of generality, we can assume that
\alpha

[
25

to show that such an assumption is always possible.

Pick an extended class
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
function
\tilde{\alpha}

\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\tilde{\alpha}(s)\geq-{s}\alpha(s),\;\;\forall{s}\in[-1,0);

\displaystyle\tilde{\alpha}(s)\leq{s}\alpha(s),\;\;\;\;\,\forall{s}\in(0,1].

This is possible since
\alpha(r)

r>0

r<0

\eta:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be an extended class
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
function, which is smooth in
\mathbb{R}
, such that

•

\eta(r)\leq{r}
for all
r\in[-1,0)
;

•

\eta(r)<-\tilde{\alpha}(r)/\alpha(r)
for all
r<-1
.

Define

\displaystyle\beta(r)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\int_{0}^{s}\eta(s),&\forall{r}\geq 0;\\
\int_{s}^{0}\eta(s),&\forall{r}<0.\end{array}\right.

Note that
\beta
is of extended class
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
. Let
W(h(x))=\beta(h(x))
and then,

\displaystyle D_{+}W(h(x))=\beta^{\prime}(h(x))D_{+}h(x)\geq\eta(h(x))\alpha(h(x))

(25)

when
h(x)\leq-\gamma(|u|)
. In the following, we show that
D_{+}W(h(x))
is lower bounded by
-\tilde{\alpha}(h(x))
. Since
h(x)\leq-\gamma(|u|)\leq 0
, we have the following two cases.

\bullet
If

,

\displaystyle\eta(h(x))\leq{h}(x)\leq-\frac{\tilde{\alpha}(h(x))}{\alpha(h(x))}.

(26)

\bullet
If
h(x)\in(-\infty,-1)
, with the definition of
\eta

\displaystyle\eta(h(x))\leq-\frac{\tilde{\alpha}(h(x))}{\alpha(h(x))}.

(27)

Therefore,

\displaystyle h(x)\leq-\gamma(|u|)\Rightarrow{D}_{+}W(h(x))\geq-\tilde{\alpha}(h(x)).

(28)

By combining the proof of Theorem
1
and Lemma
2
, we can conclude from (

) that the set
\mathcal{S}_{\gamma(\|u\|)}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:h(x)+\gamma(\|u\|)\geq 0\}
is forward robustly invariant. In other words, the interconnected system (
11
) is ISSf on the set
\mathcal{S}_{\gamma(\|u\|)}
.

\Box

Before the end of this section, we provide a simple example for illustration.

Consider the interconnected system

\displaystyle\dot{x}_{1}=-x_{1}-0.24x_{2}\sin(x_{1}-x_{2})-0.5u_{1}^{3},

\displaystyle\dot{x}_{2}=-x_{2}-0.16x_{1}\sin(x_{2}-x_{1})-0.5u_{2}.

To achieve the control objective of Example 1, we choose

\displaystyle h_{1}(x_{1})\leq-\max\{0.96|h_{2}|,2|u_{1}|^{3}\}\Rightarrow\dot{h}_{1}(x_{1})\geq-0.5h_{1}(x_{1}),

\displaystyle h_{2}(x_{2})\leq-\max\{0.64|h_{1}|,2|u_{2}|\}\Rightarrow\dot{h}_{2}(x_{2})\geq-0.5h_{2}(x_{2}).

V
Conclusion

We have developed a small-gain theorem based on ISSf-barrier functions for safety verification. It is shown that an interconnected system, which consists of two ISSf subsystems, is ISSf if the absolute value of the composition of ISSf gains of two subsystems is smaller than that of the identity function.

Abstract
. Garland introduced a vanishing criterion for characteristic zero cohomology groups of locally finite and locally connected simplicial complexes based on the spectral gaps of the graph Laplacians of face links which has turned out to be effective in a wide range of examples. In this work we provide a homological algebra version of this method and define a class of posets which provide examples including Garland’s original vanishing theorem in the simplicial setting. The cubical case is analyzed in some detail where it provides a cohomological structure rather than vanishing theorem.



1.
Introduction

In
[
5
]
Garland introduced a local to global method to establish characteristic zero cohomological vanishing for locally finite locally connected
simplicial complexes from sufficiently large spectral gaps for the Laplacians of link graphs. This has turned out to be effective in a wide range of examples in fields including geometric group theory (see e.g.,
[
1
,
14
,
12
]
) and geometric combinatorics (see e.g.,
[
6
,
8
]
).

In this note we generalize this approach to an exactness criterion for
3
-term complexes of (real) Hilbert-spaces in
Theorem 2.0
.
In Section
§6
we expand on examples of simplicial and cubical complexes where the global property becomes a geometric restriction on cohomology
classes and the local one involves both (in the cubical case)
geometric link and transversal graphs. We then analyze models for
random simplicial and cubical complexes with different cohomologcal behavior.

Garland structures and Garland posets are introduced in
§2
and

§3
respectively. These give a homological algebra setting for the method and a collection of combinatorial examples (
Proposition 3.0
) including locally finite simplicial
Corollary 4.0
and cubical
Corollary 4.0
complexes which are expanded on in
§4
. Details of proofs are in
§5
and
§6
contains a random cubical example. Garland posets also appear under a different name in the theory of high dimensional expanders (see e.g.,
[
9
]
).

2.
Garland Structures

Write
\mathfrak{b}^{*}
for the adjoint of a map
\mathfrak{b}
of Hilbert spaces and

{\mathfrak{p}}_{X}
for the
orthogonal projection to a subspace
X
.
We call a pair
(A,{\mathfrak{a}})
a
3
-term complex if

A:A^{-1}\overset{{\mathfrak{a}}_{0}}{\rightarrow}A^{0}\overset{{\mathfrak{a}}_{1}}{\rightarrow}A^{1}

with
{\mathfrak{a}}_{0}
and
{\mathfrak{a}}_{1}
bounded linear maps of Hilbert spaces such that
{\mathfrak{a}}_{1}{\mathfrak{a}}_{0}=0
.
The map
\mathcal{L}_{A}^{+}={\mathfrak{a}}_{1}^{*}{\mathfrak{a}}_{1}
is the positive
Laplacian of
(A,{\mathfrak{a}})
,

\mathrm{H}^{0}(A)=\frac{\ker({\mathfrak{a}}_{1})}{\mathrm{im}({\mathfrak{a}}_{0})}\cong\ker({\mathfrak{a}}^{*}_{0})\cap\ker({\mathfrak{a}}_{1})
is its homology and the complex is called
exact
if this homology vanishes.

Definition 2.0
.

A
Garland structure

G=(A,B,{\mathfrak{a}})
is a pair of
3
-term complexes
(A,{\mathfrak{a}})
and
(B,\mathfrak{b})
with
A
exact,
B^{i}\subseteq A^{i}
,
B^{-1}=A^{-1}
and

\mathfrak{b}_{i}={\mathfrak{p}}_{B^{i}}{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}
.

If
A:A^{-1}\xrightarrow{{\mathfrak{a}}_{0}}A^{0}\xrightarrow{{\mathfrak{a}}_{1}}A^{1}
is a three term complex set

\alpha_{A}=\inf_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\phi\in\ker(\fa^*_0)}{\|\phi\|=1}}\|{\mathfrak{a}}_{1}(\phi)\|^{2}.

If
G=(A,B,{\mathfrak{a}})
is a Garland structure set

\alpha_{G}=\alpha_{A}\,\,\,\hbox{ and }\,\,\,\beta_{G}=\sup_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\phi\in\ker(\fb_1)}{\|\phi\|=1}}\|{\mathfrak{a}}_{1}(\phi)\|^{2}.

As usual the infimum and supremum over the empty set are
\pm\infty
.
Note that for a Garland structure
\alpha_{G}
is the spectral gap of
\mathcal{L}_{A}^{+}
.

Theorem 2.0
.

If a Garland structure has
\beta_{G}<\alpha_{G}
then
B
is exact.

Proof.

If
\mathrm{H}^{0}(B)\neq\{0\}
there is
\phi\in\ker(\mathfrak{b}^{*}_{0})\cap\ker(\mathfrak{b}_{1})
with

\|\phi\|=1
. Since
B^{-1}=A^{-1}

we have
\ker(\mathfrak{b}^{*}_{0})\subseteq\ker({\mathfrak{a}}^{*}_{0})
. It then follows that

\alpha_{G}\leq\|{\mathfrak{a}}_{1}(\phi)\|^{2}\leq\beta_{G}<\alpha_{G}
.

\Box

Note that if a group acts by automorphisms on a Garland structure
G
then it induces
decompositions into direct sums of Garland structures and the theorem can be applied to each
summand separately.

3.
Garland Posets

In this section we set define Garland posets to which we associate Garland structures.
In
§4
these are shown to include posets associated to simplicial and cubical complexes for which the above theorem gives cohomological restrictions.
Proofs are sketched here and expanded in
§5
.

For a poset
P
and a subposet
Q
we write
Q_{>x}
(
Q_{<x}
) for the
subposet of elements of
Q
greater (less) than
x\in P
.
Analogously defined are
Q_{\leq p}
and
Q_{\geq p}
.
An order relation
x<y
in
P
is called a cover relation if there is no

z\in P
with
x<z<y
. By
\mathrm{Cov}(P)\subseteq P\times P
we denote the
set of all
(x,y)
such that
x<y
is a cover relation.

Definition 3.0
.

A
Garland poset

{\mathcal{S}}=(S,\leq,w,n,\rho)
is a poset
(S,\leq)

together with a (cover preserving) poset map
\rho:S\rightarrow\{-1<0<1\}
,
an orientation

w:\mathrm{Cov}(S)\rightarrow\{-1,+1\}
,
(x,y)\mapsto w_{xy}
, and
numbers
n_{0}
,
n_{1}
and
n_{010}
so that for
S^{i}=\rho^{-1}(i)

we have:

(P1)

if
b\in S^{0}
then
|S_{<b}|=n_{0}
,

(P2)

if
c\in S^{1}
then
|S_{<c}^{-1}|=n_{1}
,

(P3)

if
S^{0}\ni b<c>b^{\prime}\in S^{0}-\{b\}
then
|S_{<b}\cap S_{<b^{\prime}}|=n_{010}
,

(P4)

if
a\in S^{-1}
then
S_{>a}
is finite and connected,

(P5)

if
S^{-1}\ni a<c\in S^{1}
then
S_{>a}\cap S_{<c}=\{b,b^{\prime}\}
with

(1)

\displaystyle w_{ab}w_{bc}

\displaystyle=-w_{ab^{\prime}}w_{b^{\prime}c}.

In our examples of Garland posets we usually
define the mutually disjoint sets
S^{i}
directly, rendering
\rho
superfluous.
Given a Garland poset
{\mathcal{S}}
and a choice of
i
consider the Hilbert space
with orthonormal basis

\{\,z_{axc}\,|\,S^{-1}\ni a\leq x\leq c\in S^{1},x\in S^{i}\}.

By (P4) the closed subspaces
A^{i}
and
B^{i}
generated
respectively by

\left\{\,z_{ax}=\sum_{c\in S_{\geq x}^{1}}z_{axc}\,|\,S^{-1}\ni a\leq x\in S^{i}\,\right\}\,\,\text{and}\,\,\left\{\,z_{x}=\sum_{a\in S_{\leq x}^{-1}}z_{ax}\,|\,x\in S^{i}\,\right\}

are well defined.
Note that the two generating sets are orthogonal but not normalized bases of the respective spaces.

Lemma 3.1
.

The maps

{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}\big(\,z_{ax}\,\big)=\sum_{y\in S_{>x}^{i}}w_{xy}z_{ay}

extend to complexes

A_{{\mathcal{S}}}~:~A^{-1}\xrightarrow{{\mathfrak{a}}_{0}}A^{0}\xrightarrow{{\mathfrak{a}}_{1}}A^{1}

and

B_{{\mathcal{S}}}~:~B^{-1}\xrightarrow{\mathfrak{b}_{0}={\mathfrak{p}}_{B^{0}}{\mathfrak{a}}_{0}}B^{0}\xrightarrow{\mathfrak{b}_{1}={\mathfrak{p}}_{B^{1}}{\mathfrak{a}}_{1}}B^{1}

of bounded linear maps.

The proof of this is a simple calculation and can be found in
§5
.
For
a\in S^{-1}
we write
A_{a}
for the three term subcomplex of
(A,{\mathfrak{a}})
with

A^{i}_{a}
generated by
\{\,z_{ax}\,|\,x\in S_{\geq a}^{i}\,\}
.
Note that by (P4)
A_{a}
is of finite dimension.
The following lemma is a direct consequence of our construction and again is proved in
§5
.

Lemma 3.2
.

If
{\mathcal{S}}
is a Garland poset then

A_{{\mathcal{S}}}=\overline{\oplus_{a\in S^{-1}}A_{a}}
is the closure of the internal
orthogonal sum of complexes.
Moreover, each
A_{a}
is exact and hence
A_{{\mathcal{S}}}
is exact.

By (P5) for a Garland poset
{\mathcal{S}}
and
a\in S^{-1}
we can consider
S_{>a}
as a graph,
called the link graph at
a
,
with vertices
S^{0}_{>a}
and edges
S^{1}_{>a}
. The disjoint
union
\Gamma_{{\mathcal{S}}}
of the graphs
S_{>a}
for
a\in S^{-1}
is called the link graph of
{\mathcal{S}}
.
The positive Laplacian
\mathcal{L}^{+}_{A_{a}}
of the complex
A_{a}
is then the left normalized graph Laplacian
(in the sense of
[
3
]
)
of the link graph at
a
. The normalization is induced by the fact that
the basis elements
z_{ab}
of
A_{a}^{0}
have norm the degree of
b
in the
link graph at
a
.
Note that if
\alpha_{A_{a}}\not=0
the spectrum of
\mathcal{L}_{a}^{+}
contains

0
with multiplicity one and
\alpha_{A_{a}}
and is contained in
\{0\}\cup[\alpha_{A_{a}},2]
. In particular,
\alpha_{A_{a}}
is the spectral gap of

\mathcal{L}_{a}^{+}
.
In the formulation of the following proposition we call a Garland structure
H=(A_{H},B_{H},{\mathfrak{a}}^{H})
a substructure of the
Garland structure
G=(A,B,{\mathfrak{a}})
if
A_{H}
is a subcomplex of
A
,
B_{H}
a subcomplex of
B
and
{\mathfrak{a}}^{H}
is the restriction of
{\mathfrak{a}}
.

Proposition 3.0
.

If
{\mathcal{S}}
is a Garland poset then

G=(A_{{\mathcal{S}}},B_{{\mathcal{S}}},{\mathfrak{a}})
is a Garland structure with:

(i)

\alpha_{G}=\displaystyle{\inf_{a\in S^{-1}}\alpha_{A_{a}}}
and

(ii)

\beta_{H}=\frac{n_{0}-n_{010}}{n_{0}}

if
H
is any Garland substructure of
G
with
\ker\mathfrak{b}_{1}\cap B_{H}^{0}
nonzero.

In particular,
B_{{\mathcal{S}}}
is acyclic if
\frac{n_{0}-n_{010}}{n_{0}}<\alpha_{A_{a}}
(the spectral gap) for every

.

Sketch of proof (For a full proof see
§5
):

The fact that
A_{{\mathcal{S}}}
and
B_{{\mathcal{S}}}
are complexes is proved in

Lemma 3.1
.
By
Lemma 3.2

A_{{\mathcal{S}}}
is exact.
Assertion (i) again follows from
Lemma 3.2
.

For (ii) note that if
\ker\mathfrak{b}_{1}^{H}\neq 0
then

(2)

\displaystyle\beta_{H}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{0\neq\phi\in B_{H}^{0}}\frac{\|{\mathfrak{a}}_{1}^{H}(\phi)\|^{2}-\kappa\|\mathfrak{b}_{1}^{H}(\phi)\|^{2}}{\|\phi\|^{2}}

for any

\kappa>0
since
\beta_{H}
is a supremum over the kernel of
\mathfrak{b}_{1}^{H}
.
Choose
\kappa=\frac{n_{010}n_{1}}{n_{0}^{2}}
and show that for every choice of
\phi
the expression after the supremum is
\frac{n_{0}-n_{010}}{n_{0}}
.

The final assertion is a direct consequence of (i), (ii),
Theorem 2.0
and the previous paragraph.

\Box

4.
Application to Simplicial and Cubical Complexes

Garland’s original work treated the case of locally finite simplicial
complexes with connected links. Our approach, among other examples, also treats
locally finite cubical complexes and disconnected links.
In the case of simplicial complexes with connected links this is exactly Garland’s vanishing
criterion but in the cases of cubical complexes or simplicial ones with disconnected links
it yields a weaker restriction on the generators of cohomology.

4.1.
Simplicial

Let
P
be a locally finite simplicial poset (that is, every lower interval
is Boolean and every upper interval is finite).
Equivalently,
P
is the face poset of a locally finite
\Delta
-complex

\Delta_{P}
(see
[
7
]
).
We write
P^{k}
for the elements of
P
covering
k+1
elements,

P^{k,k+1}
for
P^{k}\cup P^{k+1}

and
P^{k-1,k,k+1}
for
P^{k-1}\cup P^{k}\cup P^{k+1}
.

For each dimension
k\geq 1
we construct a Garland poset
{\mathcal{S}}_{k}P

with
S^{1}=P^{k+1}
,
S^{0}=P^{k}
and
S^{-1}
a set of representatives of the
connected components of
P_{>p}^{k+1,k}
for all

p\in P^{k-1}
(so
S^{-1}=P^{k-1}
in the case of connected links) with the
partial order on
S^{1}\cup S^{0}
induced from
P
and
S^{-1}\ni a<b\in S^{0}

if
b
is a vertex in the component represented by
a
.
Note that here the link graph
\Gamma_{{\mathcal{S}}_{k}P}
is the disjoint
union of the (geometric) link graphs of the
(k-1)
-faces in
\Delta_{P}
.

If
n_{0}=k+1
,
n_{1}=\binom{k+2}{2}
and
n_{010}=1

then
{\mathcal{S}}_{k}P
clearly satisfies (P1), (P2), (P3) and (P4) in the definition of Garland posets.
The coefficients from the simplicial differential of
\Delta_{P}

yield an orientation
w
on the cover relations of
P
, whose lift
to
S
is easily seen to satisfy (P5).
Thus
{\mathcal{S}}_{k}P=(S,\leq,w,n,\rho)
is a Garland poset.

We write
C^{k-1,k,k+1}
for the degree
k-1
,
k
, and
k+1
three term
simplicial cochain complex for
\Delta_{P}
with real coefficients.
The poset
S
comes with a surjective poset map

\pi:S\rightarrow P^{k-1,k,k+1}
.
The map

\pi
induces an injective map of three term cochain complexes

\pi^{*}:C^{k-1,k,k+1}\rightarrow B_{{\mathcal{S}}_{k}P}

with cokernel
B^{-1}_{{\mathcal{S}}_{k}P}/C^{k-1}
supported only in one degree and isomorphic to
\bigoplus_{p\in P^{k-1}}\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}^{0}(\,\Delta_{P_{\geq p}}\,)
.
For
p\in P^{k-1}
write
\lambda_{p}:\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}^{0}(\,\Delta_{P_{\geq p}}\,)\rightarrow{\mathrm{H}}^{k}(\Delta_{P})

for the connecting homomorphism in the induced long exact sequence

\bigoplus_{p\in P^{k-1}}\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}^{0}(\Delta_{P_{\geq p}})\rightarrow\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}^{k}(\Delta_{P})\rightarrow\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}^{0}(B_{{\mathcal{S}}_{k}P})\rightarrow 0.

Write
L_{k,P}
for the span of the images of the
\lambda_{p}
for all
p
.
Thus by
Proposition 3.0
and
Theorem 2.0
:

Corollary 4.0
.

If
P
is a locally finite simplicial poset and the spectral gap
of every connected component of the link graph
\Gamma_{{\mathcal{S}}_{k}P}
is
greater than
\frac{k}{k+1}
then
\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}^{k}(\Delta_{P})=L_{k,P}
.

If all
P_{\geq p}
are connected and
P
is the face poset of a locally finite simplicial complex then
L=0
and this is the original Garland result.
Note that the gap condition for an
a\in S^{-1}
means that the spectrum of
\mathcal{L}^{+}_{A_{a}}
is contained in
\{0\}\cup(\frac{k}{k+1},2]
.
Also note that if the link graph is regular of degree
d
and has diameter
at least four then the Alon-Boppona bound (see
[
10
,
11
]
)
implies that this spectral bound can not be satisfied unless
d\geq k^{2}
.

4.2.
Cubical

Let
P
be a locally finite cubical poset which means that if
p\in P
then
P_{\leq p}
is
isomorphic to a product of copies of the three element poset
\bigwedge=\{x<z>y\}
and
P_{\geq p}
is finite.
Equivalently,
P
is the face poset (excluding the empty face) of a locally finite complex
\Box_{P}
of cubes, with a restriction analogous
to that for
\Delta
-complexes in the simplicial case. Here we write
P^{k}
for the elements of
P
covering
2k
elements,

P^{k,k+1}
for
P^{k}\cup P^{k+1}
and
P^{k-1,k,k+1}
for
P^{k-1}\cup P^{k}\cup P^{k+1}
.

The construction of a Garland poset
{\mathcal{S}}_{k}P
is similar to the simplicial
case except that
S^{-1}
is more complicated
and along with the geometric link graphs,
\Gamma_{{\mathcal{S}}_{k}P}
also contains a
new class of transversal link graphs.
Again take
S^{0}=P^{k}
and
S^{1}=P^{k+1}
with the order induced from
P
.

S^{-1}
will index the connected components of the graph
\Gamma
which is
constructed next.
Let
\varphi
be an additional element
smaller than any element in
P^{k}=S^{0}
.
Consider the graph
\Gamma
with
vertex set

\big\{\,(p,b)\,\big|\,\{\varphi\}\cup P^{k-1}\ni p<b\in S^{0}\,\big\}

and edge set

\big\{\,(\,\{b,b^{\prime}\},c\,)\in S^{0}\times S^{1}\times S^{0}\,\big|\,b<c>b^{\prime},b\neq b^{\prime}\,\big\}

with
(\{b,b^{\prime}\},c)
connecting the vertices
(b\bowtie b^{\prime},b)
and
(b\bowtie b^{\prime},b^{\prime})
, where
b\bowtie b^{\prime}
is the infimum of
b
and
b^{\prime}
as elements of
P

if there is an infimum in
P^{k-1}
and
\varphi
otherwise.
Let
S^{-1}
index the set of connected components of
\Gamma
.
For
a\in S^{-1}
and
b\in S^{0}
set
a<b
if a vertex
(p,b)
of

\Gamma
lies in the component indexed by
a
.

For
n_{0}=2k+1
,
n_{1}=\binom{2k+2}{2}
and
n_{010}=1
it is now straightforward to check that
(S,\leq,n,\rho)
satisfies
(P1), (P2), (P3) and (P4) in the definition of a Garland poset.
The definitions also imply that
\Gamma
is isomorphic to the
link graph
\Gamma_{{\mathcal{S}}_{k}P}
with an
isomorphism mapping the edge
(\{b,b^{\prime}\},c)
of
\Gamma
to the
copy of
c\in P^{k+1}
in the link graph of the
a\in S^{-1}

representing its connected component.

As in the simplicial case there is a surjective poset map

\pi:S\rightarrow P^{k-1,k,k+1}\cup\{\varphi\}
which is the identity on

S^{0}
and
S^{1}
and for

a\in S^{-1}
then
\pi(a)=b
if there is a vertex
(p,b)
of
\Gamma
in
the component indexed by
a
.
Call a connected component of
\Gamma_{{\mathcal{S}}_{k}P}
associated to

a\in S^{-1}
geometric if
\pi(a)\in P^{k-1}
in which case it is a component of the link graph of
\pi(a)
in
\Box_{P}
and transversal if

\pi(a)=\varphi
(see
§6
for examples). We call

P
transversally finite if all transversal components are finite
and in this case they will turn out to be another source of classes in

\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}^{k}(\Box_{P})
not arising in the simplicial case.

If
a\in S^{-1}
indexes a transversal component of
\Gamma_{{\mathcal{S}}_{k}P}

with edges
E\subseteq P_{>a}^{k+1}
write

P(a)=\{\,p\in P\,|\,\exists c\in E\cap P_{\geq p}\,\}
and we say
P
is

k
-monodromy-free
if
P(a)
is isomorphic to
S_{>a}\times\bigwedge^{k}
for every
a\in\pi^{-1}(\varphi)

(see
§6
for examples).
If
P
is
k
-monodromy-free it is
straightforward to construct an orientation

so that
{\mathcal{S}}_{k}P=(S,\leq,w,n,\rho)
satisfies (P5) and hence is a Garland poset.
For cover relations not involving
a\in\pi^{-1}(\varphi)
, one can for
example use the coefficients from the cellular
differential for
\Box_{P}
as in the simplicial case. Monodromy freeness –
and again using a cellular model –
then allows the extension of this

to all cover relations in
S
.

We adopt the notations for chain complexes and homology from the simplicial case.
Note that
\pi
again induces an injective map of three term cochain complexes
\pi^{*}:C^{k-1,k,k+1}\rightarrow B_{{\mathcal{S}}_{k}P}
with cokernel

B^{-1}_{{\mathcal{S}}_{k}P}/\penalty C^{k-1}
supported only in one degree.
This time the cokernel is generated by both the image
L_{k,P}
of the geometric link terms and the image
T_{k,P}
of classes of transversal components of

\Gamma_{S}
under the connecting homomorphism.
Thus by
Proposition 3.0
and
Theorem 2.0
:

Corollary 4.0
.

If
P
is a locally and transversally finite
k
-monodromy free cubicial
poset and the spectral gap
of every connected component of the link graph
\Gamma_{{\mathcal{S}}_{k}P}
is
greater than
\frac{2k}{2k+1}
then
\widetilde{\mathrm{H}}^{k}(\Box_{P})=L_{k,P}+T_{k,P}
.

If
P
has
k
-monodromy there is a similar statement using the
same Laplacian for geometric links but one on a larger local system
for the transversal links.

The gap condition means that both the connected components of the geometric
link graph and the transversal link graph have positive Laplacian spectra
contained in
\{0\}\cup(\frac{2k}{2k+1},2]
.
Note that if the link graph is regular of degree
d
and has diameter at least four then the Alon-Boppona bound implies that this spectral bound can not be satisfied unless

d\geq 4k^{2}
.

Example 4.0
.

A
2
-dimensional torus gives somewhat misleading but easy to draw examples
of related simplicial and cubical complexes for which one can compare the
above criteria.

Write
T^{\Box}
for the standard cubical subdivision of the
2
-dimensional
torus with
16
vertices and
T^{\Delta}
for its simplicial refinement also
with
16
vertices and with all vertex degrees six. The former is the
cellular product and the latter a simplicial product of two four cycles.
Write
P^{\Box}
and

for their face posets. Take

and note
that the associated Garland poset link graphs
\Gamma_{{\mathcal{S}}_{1}P^{\Box}}

and
\Gamma_{{\mathcal{S}}_{1}P^{\Delta}}
are the disjoint union of
24
squares
and
16
hexagons respectively. The squares include
16
arising as the
links of the
16

4

16

Note, that the vertex links here are dual to cocycles representing classes

Write
\Lambda_{G}=I_{VG}-\frac{1}{deg}Adj_{G}

Laplacian in the case that

is regular and note that

and
in this case
\Lambda_{C_{4}}

\Lambda_{C_{6}}
are both tridiagonal
matrices with entries

,

and
\frac{-1}{2}
and

{\mathrm{Spec}}(\Lambda_{C_{4}})={\mathrm{Spec}}(\Lambda_{\Gamma_{{\mathcal{S}}_{1}P^{\Box}}})=\{0,1,2\}

while
{\mathrm{Spec}}(\Lambda_{C_{6}})={\mathrm{Spec}}(\Lambda_{\Gamma_{{\mathcal{S}}_{1}P^{\Delta}}})=\{0,\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{2},2\}
, so the relevant spectral gaps are

for the simplicial one.

For the cubical case the hypotheses of

hold since

PBW bases of irreducible Ising modules
Diego Salazar

Abstract
. To every h + ℕ h+\mathbb{N} -graded module M M over an ℕ \mathbb{N} -graded conformal vertex algebra V V , we associate an increasing filtration ( G p ​ M ) p ∈ ℤ (G^{p}M)_{p\in\mathbb{Z}} , which is compatible with the filtrations introduced by Haisheng Li. The associated graded vector space gr G ⁡ ( M ) \gr^{G}(M) is naturally a module over the vertex Poisson algebra gr G ⁡ ( V ) \gr^{G}(V) . We study gr G ⁡ ( M ) \gr^{G}(M) for the three irreducible modules over the Ising model Vir 3 , 4 \Vir_{3,4} , namely Vir 3 , 4 = L ⁡ ( 1 / 2 , 0 ) \Vir_{3,4}=L(1/2,0) , L ⁡ ( 1 / 2 , 1 / 2 ) L(1/2,1/2) and L ⁡ ( 1 / 2 , 1 / 16 ) L(1/2,1/16) . We obtain an explicit PBW basis of each of these modules and a formula for their refined characters, which are related to Nahm sums for the matrix ( 8 3 3 2 ) \left(\begin{smallmatrix}8&3\\ 3&2\end{smallmatrix}\right) . Keywords. Vertex algebras, Ising model, Nahm sums, quantum algebra, combinatorics.

Introduction

In
[
Li04
]
, Li introduced an increasing filtration
(G^{p}V)_{p\in\mathbb{Z}}
on an arbitrary
\mathbb{N}
-graded vertex algebra
V
.
The associated graded space
\gr^{G}(V)
with respect to this increasing filtration then carries the structure of an
\mathbb{N}
-graded vertex Poisson algebra.

Then in
[
Li05
]
, Li introduced a decreasing filtration
(F_{p}V)_{p\in\mathbb{Z}}
on an arbitrary vertex algebra
V
, not necessarily
\mathbb{N}
-graded.
The associated graded space
\gr_{F}(V)
with respect to this decreasing filtration again carries the structure of a vertex Poisson algebra.
Li also introduced a decreasing filtration
(F_{p}M)_{p\in\mathbb{Z}}
for modules
M
over a vertex algebra
V
and showed that the associated graded space
\gr_{F}(M)
is a module over the vertex Poisson algebra
\gr_{F}(V)
.

In summary, Li constructed three functors:

\displaystyle\gr^{G}:\{\text{$\mathbb{N}$-graded vertex algebras}\}

\displaystyle\to\{\text{$\mathbb{N}$-graded vertex Poisson algebras}\},

\displaystyle\gr_{F}:\{\text{vertex algebras}\}

\displaystyle\to\{\text{vertex Poisson algebras}\},

\displaystyle\gr_{F}:\{\text{$V$-modules}\}

\displaystyle\to\{\text{$\gr_{F}(V)$-modules}\}.

Then Arakawa showed in
[
Ara12
, Proposition 2.6.1]
that when
V
is
\mathbb{N}
-graded,
\gr_{F}(V)
and
\gr^{G}(V)
are isomorphic as vertex Poisson algebras.

In this article, we define an increasing filtration

for

-graded modules
M
over an
\mathbb{N}
-graded conformal vertex algebra

.

Again, the resulting modules
\gr^{G}(M)
and
\gr_{F}(M)
are isomorphic.
However, for our purposes, the filtration
(G^{p}M)_{p\in\mathbb{Z}}
is better suited.

Preliminaries and notation

The set of natural numbers
\{0,1,\dots\}
is denoted by
\mathbb{N}
, the set of integers is denoted by
\mathbb{Z}
, and the set of positive integers
\{1,2,\dots\}
is denoted by
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.

First, we review the theory of representations of the Virasoro Lie algebra following
[
KRR13
]
.
The
Virasoro Lie algebra
is a Lie algebra given by

\Vir=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}L_{n}\oplus\mathbb{C}C.

These elements satisfy the following commutation relations:

\displaystyle[L_{m},L_{n}]

\displaystyle=(m-n)L_{m+n}+\delta_{m,-n}\frac{m^{3}-m}{12}C\quad\text{for $m,n\in\mathbb{Z}$},

\displaystyle[\Vir,C]

\displaystyle=0.

Let
c,h\in\mathbb{C}
.
We set
\Vir_{\geq 0}=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{C}L_{n}
.
We make the subalgebra
\Vir_{\geq 0}\oplus\mathbb{C}C
of
\Vir
act on
\mathbb{C}
as follows:

\text{$L_{n}1=0$ for $n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}$, $L_{0}1=h$ and $C1=c$}.

The
Verma representation
of
\Vir
with
highest weight

(c,h)
is defined as

M(c,h)=\Ind^{\Vir}_{\Vir_{\geq 0}\oplus\mathbb{C}C}(\mathbb{C})=U(\Vir)\otimes_{U(\Vir_{\geq 0}\oplus\mathbb{C}C)}\mathbb{C},

where
\Vir
acts by left multiplication.
We take
|c,h\rangle=1\otimes 1
as
highest weight vector
.

A
partition (of
n\in\mathbb{N}
)
is a sequence
\lambda=[\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{m}]
such that
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
for
i=1,\dots,m
,
\lambda_{1}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{m}
(and
\lambda_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{m}=n
).
We also consider the
empty partition

\emptyset
, which is the unique partition of
0
.
For a partition
\lambda=[\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{m}]
, we define

L_{\lambda}=L_{-\lambda_{1}}\dots L_{-\lambda_{m}}\in U(\Vir).

By the PBW theorem, the set

\{L_{\lambda}|c,h\rangle\mid\text{$\lambda$ is a partition}\}

is a vector space basis of
M(c,h)
.
The representation
M(c,h)
has a unique maximal proper subrepresentation
J(c,h)
, and the quotient

L(c,h)=M(c,h)/J(c,h)

is the
irreducible highest weight representation
of
\Vir
with highest weight
(c,h)
.
We usually simplify
|c,h\rangle
to just
|h\rangle
when
c
is understood.
In
[
AVEH22
]
,
\Vir_{3,4}=L(1/2,0)
was studied.
In this article, we study

and
L(1/2,1/16)
.
These three representations are the only irreducible modules of
\Vir_{3,4}
as a vertex algebra (see
[

, Theorem 4.2]

-graded vertex algebra, meaning it has a Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\alpha^{p}(a)\alpha^{q}(b)

\displaystyle=\alpha^{p+q}(a_{(-1)}b),

\displaystyle T\alpha^{p}(a)

\displaystyle=\alpha^{p}(Ta),

\displaystyle Y_{-}(\alpha^{p}(a),z)\alpha^{q}(b)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\alpha^{p+q-1}(a_{(n)}b)z^{-n-1},

.

.

Remark 1.3
.

We do not have the property
L_{-1}^{M}(G^{p}M)\subseteq G^{p}M
as in the case of vertex algebras.

Let

\gr^{G}(M)=\bigoplus_{p\in\mathbb{N}}G^{p}M/G^{p-1}M

be the associated graded vector space.
The vector space
\gr^{G}(M)
is a module over
\gr^{G}(V)
with operations given as follows.
For
p,q\in\mathbb{N}
,
a\in G^{p}V
and
u\in G^{q}M
, we set:

\displaystyle\alpha^{p}(a)\alpha_{M}^{q}(u)

\displaystyle=\alpha_{M}^{p+q}(a^{M}_{(-1)}u),

\displaystyle Y^{M}_{-}(\alpha^{p}(a),z)\alpha_{M}^{q}(u)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\alpha_{M}^{p+q-1}(a^{M}_{(n)}u)z^{-n-1},

where
\alpha_{M}^{p}:G^{p}M\to\gr^{G}(M)
is the principal symbol map.
The filtration
(G^{p}M)_{p\in\mathbb{Z}}
is called the standard filtration of
M
.

By
Proposition

1.2
(vii), we can define a diagonalizable operator

by setting

for

and

.

For

, we set
G^{p}M_{h+n}=G^{p}M\cap M_{h+n}
.
Then

(1)

\gr^{G}(M)=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\gr^{G}(M)_{h+n},

where
\gr^{G}(M)_{h+n}=\bigoplus_{p\in\mathbb{N}}\alpha^{p}_{M}(G^{p}M_{h+n})
and
\gr^{G}(M)_{h+n}=\ker(H^{M}-(h+n)\Id_{\gr^{G}(M)})
.
Besides the grading (

), we have the refined grading

(2)

\gr^{G}(M)=\bigoplus_{p,n\in\mathbb{N}}\alpha^{p}_{M}(G^{p}M_{h+n}).

We assume that for

,

is finite dimensional.
We define the

and the

(with respect to grading (

\displaystyle\ch_{\gr^{G}(M)}(q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\dim(\gr^{G}(M)_{h+n})q^{h+n}\in q^{h}\mathbb{C}[[q]],

\displaystyle\ch_{\gr^{G}(M)}(t,q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{p,n\in\mathbb{N}}\dim(\alpha^{p}_{M}(G^{p}M_{h+n}))t^{p}q^{h+n}\in q^{h}\mathbb{C}[[t,q]].

-graded

\displaystyle\gr^{G}(f):\gr^{G}(M_{1})

\displaystyle\to\gr^{G}(M_{2}),

\displaystyle\gr^{G}(f)(\alpha^{p}_{M_{1}}(u))

\displaystyle=\alpha^{p}_{M_{2}}(f(u))\quad\text{for $p\in\mathbb{N}$ and $u\in G^{p}M_{1}$}

-graded

Therefore, we obtain a functor

\gr^{G}:\{\text{$h+\mathbb{N}$-graded $(V,\omega)$-modules}\}\to\{\text{$h+\mathbb{N}$-graded $\gr^{G}(V)$-modules}\}.

Remark 1.5

4.
Proofs of the main theorems

Proof of
Theorem

0.4
.

Since we cannot apply Gröbner basis theory directly on the free module
\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{C}[L_{-2},L_{-3},\dots]L_{-1}^{k}
, we need to truncate somehow.
We have to keep in mind the isomorphisms given in
Proposition

1.8
.
For
N\in\mathbb{N}
, we define:

\displaystyle\gr^{G}(M)_{\leq 1/2+N}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\leq N}\gr^{G}(M)_{1/2+n},

\displaystyle\gr^{G}(L)_{\leq 1/2+N}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\leq N}\gr^{G}(L)_{1/2+n}.

We note that
\gr^{G}(M)_{\leq 1/2+N}
is a vector subspace of the free module

F_{N}=\bigoplus_{n\leq N}\mathbb{C}[L_{-2},L_{-3},\dots,L_{-N}]L_{-1}^{n}

with base ring
\mathbb{C}[L_{-2},L_{-3},\dots,L_{-N}]
because a basis of
\gr^{G}(M)_{\leq 1/2+N}
is given by elements of the form
u_{\lambda}
, with all the elements of
\lambda
being less than
N
, and with
\lambda
having at most
N
ones.
We note that
K\cap F_{N}
is a submodule of
F_{N}
for
N\in\mathbb{N}
.

For
N\in\mathbb{N}
, we have natural vector space isomorphisms

(7)

\frac{F_{N}}{K\cap F_{N}}\supseteq\frac{\gr^{G}(M)_{\leq 1/2+N}}{K\cap F_{N}}\xrightarrow{\sim}\frac{\gr^{G}(M)_{\leq 1/2+N}}{K\cap\gr^{G}(M)_{\leq 1/2+N}}\xrightarrow{\sim}\gr^{G}(L)_{\leq 1/2+N}.

Therefore, if we find a basis of each vector space
\gr^{G}(M)_{\leq 1/2+N}/K\cap F_{N}\subseteq F_{N}/K\cap F_{N}
such that each basis is contained in the next one when considering the isomorphism (
7
), we get a basis of
\gr^{G}(L)
by taking the union of these bases because
\bigcup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\gr^{G}(L)_{\leq 1/2+N}=\gr^{G}(L)
.

Let
G_{N}
be a Gröbner basis of
K\cap F_{N}
.
We define

B_{N}=\{u_{\lambda}\mid\text{$\Delta(\lambda)\leq N$ and for $u\in G_{N}$, $\lm(u)\nmid u_{\lambda}$}\}.

By
[
AL94
, Proposition 3.6.4]
,

(8)

\{u_{\lambda}+K\cap F_{N}\mid u_{\lambda}\in B_{N}\}

is a vector space basis of
\gr^{G}(M)_{\leq 1/2+N}/K\cap F_{N}
.
Therefore, by
Lemma

2.5
, isomorphism (
7
) and the isomorphism between
\gr^{G}(L)_{1/2+n}
and
L_{1/2+n}
of
Proposition

1.8
, we have

|B_{N}|=\sum_{n\leq N}\dim(\gr^{G}(L)_{1/2+n})=\sum_{n\leq N}\dim(L_{1/2+n})=\sum_{n\leq N}|P(n)|.

We define

\overline{B_{N}}=\{u_{\lambda}\mid\text{$\Delta(\lambda)\leq N$ and for $\eta\in\overline{R}$, $u_{\eta}\nmid u_{\lambda}$}\}.

By
Lemma

3.10
, we also have

\overline{B_{N}}=\{u_{\lambda}\mid\text{$\Delta(\lambda)\leq N$ and $\lambda\in P$}\}.

From the definition of
P(n)
, we see that

|\overline{B_{N}}|=\sum_{n\leq N}|P(n)|.

On the other hand,
B_{N}\subseteq\overline{B_{N}}
.
This is because if
u_{\lambda}\in B_{N}
and
\eta\in\overline{R}
, then
u_{\eta}\nmid u_{\lambda}
, as we now show.
Suppose, for the sake of contradiction, that
u_{\eta}\mid u_{\lambda}
.
By
Lemma

3.8
, there would exist
u\in K^{\prime}\cap F_{N}\subseteq K\cap F_{N}
such that
\lm(u)=u_{\eta}
.
However, by the definition of a Gröbner basis, there would then exist

such that
\lm(v)\mid\lm(u)
, implying
\lm(v)\mid u_{\lambda}
, a contradiction.
As both
B_{N}
and
\overline{B_{N}}
are finite sets and
|B_{N}|=|\overline{B_{N}}|
, we get

.
We see that the family of bases given by (

This observation, together with the isomorphism between

and
\gr^{G}(L)
of

1.8
, concludes the proof of

0.4
.
∎

Proof of

Corollary 4.1
.

We have the equality

K^{\prime}=K.

Proof.

This proof is a copy of the proof of
Theorem

0.3
.
We know that
K^{\prime}\subseteq K
.

Let

be a Gröbner basis of

considered as a submodule of

Like in the proof of

.
∎

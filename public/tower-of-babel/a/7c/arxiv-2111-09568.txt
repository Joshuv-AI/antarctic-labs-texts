1.
Introduction

Thue equations are Diophantine equations
F(X,Y)=m
, where
F\in\mathbb{Z}[X,Y]
is an irreducible form of degree at least
3
and
m\neq 0
a fixed integer. A. Thue showed that such equations only have finitely many solutions
[
Thu1909
]
. But the methods he used were ineffective and not suitable to derive bounds on the number of solutions for concrete equations. It was Baker
[
Bak1968
]
that could give such effective bounds using his theory on linear forms in logarithms. These bounds have been used and refined many times since.

E. Thomas studied so-called split Thue equations,

X\prod_{i=2}^{N}\left(X-p_{i}(a)Y\right)-Y^{N}=\pm 1,

that are parametrised by monic polynomials
p_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}[a]
. He conjectured that, given some technical conditions for the
p_{i}
, the equation would only have trivial solutions immediately apparent by its form, provided the parameter
a\geq a_{0}
is sufficiently large. The bound
a_{0}
would also be effectively computable. The conjecture has since been proven, first by Thomas
[
Tho1990
]
in the cubic and Heuberger
[
Heu2001
]
in the general case.

The methods involved rely heavily on Baker’s theory of linear forms in logarithms but not so much on the parameter functions being polynomials. Hence, the authors in
[
Hil2022
]
and
[
Vuk2021
]
made attempts using other parameter functions, namely linear-recurrent sequences. This paper, mimicking Thomas’ work on polynomial-parametrised split Thue equations, strives to proof the cubic case for when the parameter functions are linear-recurrent sequences in as much of a general setting as possible.

Let
\left(A_{n}\right)_{n_{\in}\mathbb{N}},\left(B_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be two integer sequences that satisfy some linear recurrences with some starting conditions. We denote the characteristic polynomials of the recurrence sequences as

\left(X-\alpha\right)^{d_{A}}\prod_{i=1}^{m_{A}}\left(X-\alpha_{i}\right)^{d_{A,i}},\text{ and }\left(X-\beta\right)^{d_{B}}\prod_{i=1}^{m_{B}}\left(X-\beta_{i}\right)^{d_{B,i}}

respectively to give the algebraic multiplicity of each characteristic root. We will consider the sequences almost exclusively via their explicit formulae

A_{n}=c_{A}(n)\alpha^{n}+c_{A,1}(n)\alpha_{1}^{n}+\cdots+c_{A,m_{A}}(n)\alpha_{m_{A}}^{n}\\

(1)

B_{n}=c_{B}(n)\beta^{n}+c_{B,1}(n)\beta_{1}^{n}+\cdots+c_{B,m_{B}}(n)\alpha_{m_{B}}^{n}

(2)

that always exist for linear recurrence sequences.

Both are to admit to a dominant root condition, i.e.
\left|\alpha\right|>\max_{i}\left|\alpha_{i}\right|
and
\left|\beta\right|>\max_{i}\left|\beta_{i}\right|
. We assume without loss of generality that
\left|\alpha\right|\leq\left|\beta\right|
. All characteristic roots must be algebraic over
\mathbb{Q}
.

While we cannot expect the remaining terms, say
c_{A}
, to be constant, they are polynomials in the splitting field of the respective characteristic polynomial. In addition, the degree is bounded by the related multiplicity of the root, e.g.
\deg\left(c_{A}\right)\leq d_{A}-1
. We also define

\displaystyle d_{1}

\displaystyle:=\min\left\{\deg\left(c_{A}\right),\deg\left(c_{A,i}\right),\deg\left(c_{B}\right),\deg\left(c_{B,i}\right)\right\},

\displaystyle d_{2}

\displaystyle:=\max\left\{\deg\left(c_{A}\right),\deg\left(c_{A,i}\right),\deg\left(c_{B}\right),\deg\left(c_{B,i}\right)\right\}.

(3)

In this setting, we prove the following result:

Theorem 1

The split family of Thue equations

\left|X(X-A_{n}Y)(X-B_{n}Y)-Y^{3}\right|=1

(4)

has only the trivial solutions
\left\{\pm\left(0,1\right),\pm\left(A_{n},1\right),\pm\left(B_{n},1\right),\left(\pm 1,0\right)\right\}
for sufficiently large
n
, if
A_{n}
and
B_{n}
meet a dominant root condition, where at least one dominant root has absolute value larger than
1
, and either

•

1\leq A_{n}\leq B_{n}-2
or

•

-1\geq A_{n}\geq B_{n}+3
.

In case the dominant roots have equal absolute value, we also need that for sufficiently large
n
, both
\left|c_{B}(n)\right|\neq\left|c_{A}(n)\right|
and one of the following conditions holds:

•

\left|c_{B}(n)\right|>1
and
\left|c_{B}(n)-c_{A}(n)\right|>\frac{1}{\left|c_{B}(n)\right|}

•

0<\left|c_{B}(n)\right|<1
and
0<\left|c_{B}(n)-c_{A}(n)\right|<1

•

\left|c_{B}(n)\right|=1
and
\left|c_{B}(n)-c_{A}(n)\right|\not\in\left\{0,1\right\}

2.
Preliminaries

Even if the terms
c_{B}(n),c_{A}(n)
are polynomials in
n
in general, we will forego to note the dependency every time for the sake of readability and simply write
c_{B}\;\left(=c_{B}(n)\right)
and
c_{A}\;\left(=c_{A}(n)\right)
.

The conditions
1\leq A_{n}\leq B_{n}-2
or
-1\geq A_{n}\geq B_{n}+3
translate to
1\leq\left|\alpha\right|,\left|\beta\right|
, with
\left|\alpha\right|\leq\left|\beta\right|
we further get that
\left|\beta\right|>1
. We need at least one of the dominant roots to be strictly larger than
1
so that the corresponding sequence has exponential growth. This is not really a restriction, since
\left|\alpha\right|=\left|\beta\right|=1
would mean that both sequences have at most polynomial growth and that we are essentially in the case which Thomas had already studied and solved.

Also note that in the case of
\left|\alpha\right|=\left|\beta\right|
, since
\left|c_{A}\right|\neq\left|c_{B}\right|
at least for sufficiently large
n
, we can conclude that
\left|c_{A}\right|\leq\left|c_{B}\right|
, as neither of the inequalities
1\leq A_{n}\leq B_{n}-2
or
-1\geq A_{n}\geq B_{n}+3
could hold otherwise.

We first consider the case where a solution
(x,y)
has small
\left|y\right|
.

Lemma 1

If a solution
(x,y)
to the Thue-Equation (
4
), for sufficiently large
n
, has
y\in\left\{-1,0,1\right\}
, then it is a trivial solution.

Proof.

For
y=0
, it follows immediately that
x^{3}=\pm 1
, thus the only solutions with
y=0
are trivial. If instead
y=\pm 1
, then either
X\left(X\pm A_{n}\right)\left(X\pm B_{n}\right)=0
or
X\left(X\pm A_{n}\right)\left(X\pm B_{n}\right)=\pm 2
.

The first case immediately gives
x\in\left\{0,\mp A_{n},\mp B_{n}\right\}
and thus that
(x,y)
is a trivial solution. The second case is solvable for
x
only if
\left|B_{n}-A_{n}\right|\leq 2
, which can hold at most for small
n
: Since either
1\leq A_{n}\leq B_{n}-2
or
-1\geq A_{n}\geq B_{n}+3
, we also have
\left|B_{n}-A_{n}\right|\geq 2
and thus equality, which is impossible for
\left|\alpha\right|<\left|\beta\right|
and precluded by
\left|c_{B}\right|>\left|c_{A}\right|
for
\left|\alpha\right|=\left|\beta\right|
.
∎

Lemma 2

Assume
x\in\mathbb{C}
such that
\left|x-1\right|\leq 1/2
, then
\left|\log\left|x\right|\right|\leq 2\left|x-1\right|
.

Proof.

Use the Taylor series expansion of
\log x
at
1
.
∎

Theorem 2

(Thomas,
[
Tho1979
]
)

Let
F_{\lambda}
be the field generated by
\lambda
, where either

\lambda\left(\lambda-r\right)\left(\lambda-s\right)+1=0,\;\;\;1\leq r\leq s-3

or

\lambda\left(\lambda-r\right)\left(\lambda-s\right)-1=0,\;\;\;1\leq r\leq s-2.

Then
\left\{\lambda,\lambda-r\right\}
is a fundamental system of units for the order generated by
\left\{1,\lambda,\lambda^{2}\right\}
.

Theorem 3

(Bugeaud, Győry,
[
BuGy1996
]
)

Let
B\geq\max\left\{\left|m\right|,e\right\}
,
\alpha
be a root of
F(X,1)
,
K:=\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)
,
R:=R_{K}
the regulator of
K
and
r
the unit rank of
K
. Let
H\geq 3
be an upper bound for the absolute values of the coefficients of
F
and

Then all solutions
(x,y)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
of the Thue equation
F(X,Y)=m
satisfy

\max\left\{\log\left|x\right|,\log\left|y\right|\right\}\leq C(r,N)\cdot R\cdot\max\left\{\log R,1\right\}\cdot\left(R+\log(HB)\right),

where
C(r,N)=3^{r+27}(r+1)^{7r+19}N^{2N+6r+14}
.

For any algebraic number
\gamma
of degree
d
with conjugates
\gamma^{(1)},\dots,\gamma^{(d)}
, we define the absolute logarithmic height
h
of
\gamma
as

h\left(\gamma\right)=\frac{1}{d}\left(\log\left|a\right|+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\log\max\left\{\left|\gamma^{(i)}\right|,1\right\}\right),

where

is the leading coefficient of the minimal polynomial of
\gamma
over
\mathbb{Z}

(Baker, Wüstholz,
[
BaWh1993
]
)

Let
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{t}
be algebraic numbers, not
0
or
1
, in

of degree

, let

\log\left|\Lambda\right|\geq-18(t+1)!t^{t+1}(32D)^{t+2}\log(2tD)h_{1}\cdots h_{t}\log B,

3.
Approximating the roots

We start by giving good approximations for the roots of

f_{n}(X)=X(X-A_{n})(X-B_{n})-1=\left(x-\lambda_{1}\right)\left(x-\lambda_{2}\right)\left(x-\lambda_{3}\right).

(5)

The polynomial is irreducible over
\mathbb{Q}
as per the rational root theorem, at least for sufficiently large
n
: The only candidates for a rational root are
1
and
-1
. When we plug them in and look at either
f_{n}(1)=0
or
f_{n}(-1)=0
, we get that
A_{n}\cdot B_{n}=A_{n}+B_{n}
, which can hold at most for small
n
.

To describe the quality of the approximation, we use the
L
-notation, where for two complex functions
f,g
, we write
f=L(g)
if
\left|f(x)\right|\leq\left|g(x)\right|
for all
x\in\mathbb{C}
. This way, we can consider constants that we would lose in the more classical
O
-notation.

Lemma 3

We have, for sufficiently large
n
:

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}

\displaystyle=B_{n}+L\left(B_{n}^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}

\displaystyle=A_{n}+L\left(A_{n}^{-1}\right)=A_{n}+\frac{1}{A_{n}\left(A_{n}-B_{n}\right)}+L\left(\frac{1}{A_{n}^{2}\left(A_{n}-B_{n}\right)^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\lambda_{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{A_{n}B_{n}}+L\left(\frac{1}{A_{n}^{2}B_{n}^{2}}\right)

Proof.

We start with the approximation for
\lambda_{1}
. To that end, we first note that
f_{n}(B_{n})=-1<0
. We then take a look at

f_{n}(B_{n}\pm B_{n}^{-1})=\left(B_{n}\pm B_{n}^{-1}\right)\left(B_{n}-A_{n}\pm B_{n}^{-1}\right)\left(\pm B_{n}^{-1}\right)-1,

where we plug in
B_{n}+B_{n}^{-1}
in case
1\leq A_{n}\leq B_{n}-2
and
B_{n}-B_{n}^{-1}
in the other.

Now, since either
B_{n}-A_{n}\geq 2
or
B_{n}-A_{n}\leq-3
, we have either

f_{n}(B_{n}+B_{n}^{-1})\geq 1+B_{n}^{-1}\left(1+2B_{n}^{-1}+B_{n}^{-2}\right)>0

or

f_{n}(B_{n}-B_{n}^{-1})\geq 2-B_{n}^{-1}\left(1+2B_{n}^{-1}-B_{n}^{-2}\right)>0

for sufficiently large
n
. By the intermediate value theorem, a root
\lambda_{1}
must lie between
B_{n}-B_{n}^{-1}
and
B_{n}+B_{n}^{-1}
, thus
\lambda_{1}=B_{n}+L\left(B_{n}^{-1}\right)
.

We prove the first approximation for
\lambda_{2}
analogously by showing that either
f_{n}(A_{n}-A_{n}^{-1})>0
o r
f_{n}(A_{n}+A_{n}^{-1})>0
, depending on the sign of
A_{n}
.

The second approximation for
\lambda_{2}
follows analogously to the approximation for
\lambda_{3}
: We first write

f_{n}\left(\frac{1}{A_{n}B_{n}}+\kappa\right)=\left(\frac{1}{A_{n}B_{n}}+\kappa\right)\left(\frac{1}{A_{n}B_{n}}-A_{n}+\kappa\right)\left(\frac{1}{A_{n}B_{n}}-B_{n}+\kappa\right)-1

and then expand the product and group by powers of
\kappa
. This then becomes

\displaystyle f_{n}\left(\frac{1}{A_{n}B_{n}}+\kappa\right)=

\displaystyle\kappa\left(A_{n}B_{n}-\frac{2}{A_{n}}-\frac{2}{B_{n}}+\frac{3}{A_{n}^{2}B_{n}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\kappa^{2}\left(-\left(A_{n}+B_{n}\right)+\frac{3}{A_{n}B_{n}}\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\kappa^{3}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{A_{n}^{2}B_{n}}+\frac{1}{A_{n}B_{n}^{2}}\right)+\frac{1}{A_{n}^{3}B_{n}^{3}}.

From this expansion, we see that the highest-order term is
\kappa A_{n}B_{n}
, as long as
\kappa
is of order strictly greater than
\frac{1}{A_{n}^{2}B_{n}^{2}}\max\left\{\frac{1}{A_{n}},\frac{1}{B_{n}}\right\}
. Thus, choosing
\kappa=\pm\frac{1}{A_{n}^{2}B_{n}^{2}}
gives alternating signs for
f_{n}\left(\frac{1}{A_{n}B_{n}}+\kappa\right)
and thus the claimed form for
\lambda_{3}
by the intermediate value theorem.
∎

Their logarithms play as important a role as the roots themselves. We thus explicitly state their approximations too.

Lemma 4

We have, up to an exponentially decreasing term
L\left(C\,n^{d_{2}}\varepsilon^{n}\right)
,

\displaystyle\log\left|\lambda_{1}\right|

\displaystyle=n\log\left|\beta\right|+\log\left|c_{B}\right|

\displaystyle\log\left|\lambda_{1}-A_{n}\right|

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}n\log\left|\beta\right|+\log\left|c_{B}\right|\;\;&\text{ if }\left|\alpha\right|<\left|\beta\right|\\
n\log\left|\beta\right|+\log\left|c_{B}-c_{A}\right|\;\;&\text{ if }\left|\alpha\right|=\left|\beta\right|\end{cases}

\displaystyle\log\left|\lambda_{2}\right|

\displaystyle=n\log\left|\alpha\right|+\log\left|c_{A}\right|

\displaystyle\log\left|\lambda_{2}-A_{n}\right|

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}-n\left(\log\left|\alpha\right|+\log\left|\beta\right|\right)-\log\left|c_{A}\right|-\log\left|c_{B}\right|\;\;&\text{ if }\left|\alpha\right|<\left|\beta\right|\\
-n\left(\log\left|\alpha\right|+\log\left|\beta\right|\right)-\log\left|c_{A}\right|-\log\left|c_{B}-c_{A}\right|\;\;&\text{ if }\left|\alpha\right|=\left|\beta\right|\end{cases}

\displaystyle\log\left|\lambda_{3}\right|

\displaystyle=-n\left(\log\left|\alpha\right|+\log\left|\beta\right|\right)-\log\left|c_{A}\right|-\log\left|c_{B}\right|

\displaystyle\log\left|\lambda_{3}-A_{n}\right|

\displaystyle=n\log\left|\alpha\right|+\log\left|c_{A}\right|

Proof.

The claims follow immediately from the approximations for the roots by Lemma
3
. We demonstrate for
\lambda_{1}
:

\log\left|\lambda_{1}\right|=\log\left|B_{n}+L\left(B_{n}^{-1}\right)\right|=\log\left|B_{n}\right|+\log\left|1+L\left(B_{n}^{-2}\right)\right|.

For sufficiently large
n
, we have
\left|B_{n}\right|^{-2}\leq\frac{1}{2}
and thus, by Lemma
2
,

\log\left|\lambda_{1}\right|=\log\left|B_{n}\right|+L\left(2\left|B_{n}^{-2}\right|\right).

We expand the term
\log\left|B_{n}\right|
next:

\displaystyle\log\left|B_{n}\right|

\displaystyle=\log\left|c_{B}\beta^{n}+c_{B,1}\beta_{1}^{n}+\cdots\right|

\displaystyle=n\log\left|\beta\right|+\log\left|c_{B}\right|+\log\left|1+\sum_{i}\frac{c_{B,i}}{c_{B}}\left(\frac{\beta_{i}}{\beta}\right)^{n}\right|.

The
c_{B},c_{B,i}
are polynomials in
n
of degree at most
d_{2}
, as defined in Equation (
1
). The first terms in the sum are thus of order at most
n^{d_{2}}
. All the while, the second terms decrease exponentially because of
\left|\beta\right|>\max_{i}\left|\beta_{i}\right|
. Thus, the sum will have an absolute value of at most
\frac{1}{2}
for sufficiently large
n
, and we can use Lemma
2
again.

We thus have proved the form of
\log\left|\lambda_{1}\right|
, up to an error of order

L\left(2\sum_{i}\left|\frac{c_{B,i}}{c_{B}}\right|\left|\frac{\beta_{i}}{\beta}\right|^{n}\right)+L\left(2\left|B_{n}^{-2}\right|\right).

The first
L
-term is of strictly higher order. We can thus ignore the second
L
-term if we take the first one
3
times instead. We then bound the rational functions
\left|\frac{c_{B,i}}{c_{B}}\right|\leq c_{1}n^{d_{2}}
, where
c_{1}
depends only on the coefficients of the
c_{B},c_{B,i}
. We also have
\left|\frac{\beta_{i}}{\beta}\right|^{n}\leq\max_{i}\left|\frac{\beta_{i}}{\beta}\right|^{n}
trivially and thus arrive at the
L

L\left(n^{d_{2}}\max_{i}\left|\frac{\beta_{i}}{\beta}\right|^{n}\cdot 3c_{1}\sum_{i}1\right)=L\left(3c_{1}m_{B}\cdot n^{d_{2}}\max_{i}\left|\frac{\beta_{i}}{\beta}\right|^{n}\right),

which decreases exponentially.

We analogously get

\displaystyle\log\left|\lambda_{2}\right|

\displaystyle=\log\left|A_{n}\right|+L\left(2\left|A_{n}\right|^{-2}\right)

\displaystyle=n\log\left|\alpha\right|+\log\left|c_{A}\right|+L\left(3c_{2}m_{A}\cdot n^{d_{2}}\max_{i}\left|\frac{\alpha_{i}}{\alpha}\right|^{n}\right),

where we bound
\left|\frac{c_{A,i}}{c_{A}}\right|\leq c_{2}n^{d_{2}}
.

If we look at
\lambda_{1}-A_{n}
, we get

\log\left|\lambda_{1}-A_{n}\right|=\log\left|B_{n}-A_{n}\right|+L\left(2\left|B_{n}\right|^{-2}\right)

and must now differentiate between the cases
\left|\alpha\right|<\left|\beta\right|
and
\left|\alpha\right|=\left|\beta\right|
. In the first case, we factorise
\left|c_{B}\right|\left|\beta\right|^{n}
and get the subsequent

3c_{3}\left(m_{B}+m_{A}+1\right)n^{d_{2}}\max_{i}\left\{\left|\frac{\beta_{i}}{\beta}\right|,\left|\frac{\alpha_{i}}{\beta}\right|,\left|\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\right|\right\}^{n},

where we bound
\left|\max_{i}\left\{\frac{c_{A}}{c_{B}},\frac{c_{A,i}}{c_{B}},\frac{c_{B_{i}}}{c_{B}}\right\}\right|\leq c_{3}n^{d_{2}}
. With
\left|\alpha\right|<\left|\beta\right|

\left|\alpha\right|
for
\left|\beta\right|
in

and factorise
\left|c_{B}-c_{A}\right|\left|\beta\right|^{n}
instead. This then leads to the

3c_{4}\left(m_{B}+m_{A}\right)n^{d_{2}}\max_{i}\left\{\left|\frac{\beta_{i}}{\beta}\right|,\left|\frac{\alpha_{i}}{\beta}\right|\right\}^{n},

\left|\max_{i}\left\{\frac{c_{A}}{c_{B}-c_{A}},\frac{c_{A,i}}{c_{B}-c_{A}},\frac{c_{B_{i}}}{c_{B}-c_{A}}\right\}\right|\leq c_{4}n^{d_{2}}

The remaining logarithms follow analogously, as we already know the

. For example,

\displaystyle\log\left|\lambda_{2}-A_{n}\right|

and we can simply sum the

and

We can use the same

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=5\max\left\{c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4}\right\}\left(m_{B}+m_{A}+1\right),

\displaystyle\varepsilon

\displaystyle=\max_{i}\left\{\left|\frac{\beta_{i}}{\beta}\right|,\left|\frac{\alpha_{i}}{\beta}\right|,\left|\frac{\alpha_{i}}{\alpha}\right|,\chi_{\mathbb{R}\backslash\left\{\left|\beta\right|\right\}}(\left|\alpha\right|)\left|\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\right|\right\},

Lemma 5

There are effectively computable constants
c_{5},c_{6}>0
such that the difference between two distinct roots
\lambda_{i},\lambda_{i^{\prime}}
is bounded by

\displaystyle c_{5}\left|\beta\right|^{n}\leq\left|\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}\right|\leq c_{6}\,n^{d_{2}}\left|\beta\right|^{n}

\displaystyle c_{5}\,n^{d_{1}}\left|\beta\right|^{n}\leq\left|\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{3}\right|\leq c_{6}\,n^{d_{2}}\left|\beta\right|^{n}

\displaystyle c_{5}\,n^{d_{1}}\left|\alpha\right|^{n}\leq\left|\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{3}\right|\leq c_{6}\,n^{d_{2}}\left|\alpha\right|^{n}

Proof.

By Lemma
3
, the difference between two distinct roots is approximately either

\displaystyle\left|\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}\right|

\displaystyle\approx\left|B_{n}-A_{n}\right|

\displaystyle\approx\left|c_{B}-\chi_{\left\{\beta\right\}}(\alpha)c_{A}\right|\left|\beta\right|^{n}

\displaystyle\left|\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{3}\right|

\displaystyle\approx\left|B_{n}-\frac{1}{A_{n}B_{n}}\right|

\displaystyle\approx\left|c_{B}\right|\left|\beta\right|^{n}

\displaystyle\left|\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{3}\right|

\displaystyle\approx\left|A_{n}-\frac{1}{A_{n}B_{n}}\right|

\displaystyle\approx\left|c_{A}\right|\left|\alpha\right|^{n},

in the sense that we can bound it from below and above by the respective right-hand term, using effectively computable constants. Doing the same again, i.e.
n^{d_{1}}\ll\left|c_{A}\right|,\left|c_{B}\right|\ll n^{d_{2}}
and
1\ll\left|c_{B}-c_{A}\right|\ll n^{d_{2}}
, we can bound the difference between two distinct roots as in the statement.
∎

4.
Regulator and upper bound for
\log\left|y\right|

As per Theorem
2
, the pairs
\lambda_{i},\lambda_{i}-A_{n}
are fundamental units of the order
\mathbb{Z}[\lambda_{i}]
. As they are generators, we can express the regulator
R
up to sign as the determinant

\pm R=\det\begin{pmatrix}\log\left|\lambda_{i}\right|&\log\left|\lambda_{i}-A_{n}\right|\\
\log\left|\lambda_{i^{\prime}}\right|&\log\left|\lambda_{i^{\prime}}-A_{n}\right|\end{pmatrix},

independent of the choice for
i,i^{\prime}
.

Lemma 6

For the regulator of the order
\mathbb{Z}[\lambda_{i}]
, we have
R=\Theta(n^{2})
.

Proof.

We calculate the regulator using
(i,i^{\prime})=(1,2)
, thus

\pm R=\log\left|\lambda_{1}\right|\log\left|\lambda_{2}-A_{n}\right|-\log\left|\lambda_{2}\right|\log\left|\lambda_{1}-A_{n}\right|.

With the approximations from Lemma
4
, where we omit the
L
-terms for readability, this yields

\displaystyle\pm R

\displaystyle\approx\left(n\log\left|\beta\right|+\log\left|c_{B}\right|\right)\left(-n\left(\log\left|\alpha\right|+\log\left|\beta\right|\right)-\log\left|c_{A}\right|-\log\left|c_{B}\right|\right)

\displaystyle\;-\left(n\log\left|\alpha\right|+\log\left|c_{A}\right|\right)\left(n\log\left|\beta\right|+\log\left|c_{B}\right|\right)

\displaystyle=-n^{2}\,\log\left|\beta\right|\left(2\log\left|\alpha\right|+\log\left|\beta\right|\right)+O\left(n\log n\right)

if
\left|\alpha\right|<\left|\beta\right|
. If instead
\left|\alpha\right|=\left|\beta\right|
, we have

\displaystyle\pm

\displaystyle R\approx\left(n\log\left|\alpha\right|+\log\left|c_{A}\right|\right)\left(-n\left(\log\alpha+\log\left|\beta\right|\right)-\log\left|c_{A}\right|-\log\left|c_{B}-c_{A}\right|\right)

\displaystyle\;-\left(n\log\left|\alpha\right|+\log\left|c_{A}\right|\right)\left(n\log\left|\beta\right|+\log\left|c_{B}-c_{A}\right|\right)

\displaystyle=-n^{2}\,\log\left|\beta\right|\left(2\log\left|\alpha\right|+\log\left|\beta\right|\right)+O\left(n\log n\right).

In both cases, the regulator is quadratic in
n
.
∎

If we use the regulator
R
being quadratic, which is then a quadratic upper bound to the regulator of the number field, we immediately get from Theorem
3
:

Lemma 7

We have

\log\left|y\right|\ll n^{4}\log n.

Proof.

Let
K=\mathbb{Q}(\lambda_{1})
. By Lemma
6
, we have
R_{K}\ll n^{2}
. The unit rank is
r=2
. The highest coefficient of the Thue equation is given by
A_{n}B_{n}
, which is at most of order
c_{A}\,c_{B}\,\beta^{2n}
. We plug everything into the upper bound from Theorem
3
, and as the highest-order term is of the form
n^{4}\log n
, the claim follows.
∎

5.
Constructing the linear form in logarithms

We now set out to construct a linear form in logarithms, on which we want to use Baker’s and Wüstholz’s lower bound to then deduce a lower bound for
\log\left|y\right|
. To that end, we start with the expressions

x-\lambda_{i}y
and fix the index
j
, sometimes called the type of the solution
(x,y)
, such that

\left|x-\lambda_{j}y\right|\leq\left|x-\lambda_{i}y\right|\;\;\;\forall i\in\left\{1,2,3\right\},

and denote the other two indices by
k
and
l
, i.e.
\left\{j,k,l\right\}=\left\{1,2,3\right\}
. First, we ascertain that the
x-\lambda_{i}y
give units in
\mathbb{Z}[\alpha_{i}]
.

Lemma 8

For each
i\in\left\{1,2,3\right\}
, we have
x-\lambda_{i}y\in\left(\mathbb{Z}[\lambda_{i}]\right)^{\times}
.

Proof.

We take the Norm
N=N_{\mathbb{Q}(\lambda_{1})/\mathbb{Q}}
, which is the product of the conjugates, i.e.

N(x-\lambda_{i}y)=\left(x-\lambda_{j}y\right)\left(x-\lambda_{k}y\right)\left(x-\lambda_{l}y\right).

Now the right hand side is
y^{3}f_{n}\left(\frac{x}{y}\right)
and thus exactly the left hand side of our Thue equation at
(x,y)
. Thus,
\left|N(x-\lambda_{i}y)\right|=1
, from which the claim follows.
∎

As the
x-\lambda_{i}y
are units in
\mathbb{Z}[\lambda_{i}]
, we can write them in terms of the fundamental units
\lambda_{i},\lambda_{i}-A_{n}
. Let thus

x-\lambda_{i}y=\pm\lambda_{i}^{b_{1}}\left(\lambda_{i}-A_{n}\right)^{b_{2}}

(6)

for each
i\in\left\{1,2,3\right\}
. We then take a look at Siegel’s identity,

\left(x-\lambda_{j}\,y\right)\left(\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{l}\right)+\left(x-\lambda_{l}\,y\right)\left(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{k}\right)+\left(x-\lambda_{k}\,y\right)\left(\lambda_{l}-\lambda_{j}\right)=0,

which we rewrite into

\frac{x-\lambda_{l}y}{x-\lambda_{k}y}\cdot\frac{\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{k}}{\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{l}}=1+\frac{x-\lambda_{j}y}{x-\lambda_{k}y}\cdot\frac{\lambda_{l}-\lambda_{k}}{\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{l}}=:1+\gamma.

We take the absolute value and use our decomposition into fundamental units on the first term of the left hand side, thus defining the linear form in logarithms

\Lambda:=b_{1}\log\left|\frac{\lambda_{l}}{\lambda_{k}}\right|+b_{2}\log\left|\frac{\lambda_{l}-A_{n}}{\lambda_{k}-A_{n}}\right|+\log\left|\frac{\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{k}}{\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{l}}\right|=\log\left|1+\gamma\right|,

which is very small, as
\gamma
is very small. In fact:

Lemma 9

We have

\left|\gamma\right|\leq 2c_{5}^{3}\,n^{-d_{1}}\left|\alpha\right|^{-2n}\left|\beta\right|^{-n}

and, if
2c_{5}^{3}\,n^{-d_{1}}\left|\alpha\right|^{-2n}\left|\beta\right|^{-n}\leq\frac{1}{2}
,

\left|\Lambda\right|\leq 4c_{5}^{3}\,n^{-d_{1}}\left|\alpha\right|^{-2n}\left|\beta\right|^{-n}.

Proof.

As

\gamma=\frac{x-\lambda_{j}y}{x-\lambda_{k}y}\cdot\frac{\lambda_{l}-\lambda_{k}}{\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{l}},

we have to bound all four terms. For
i\neq j
, we have

\left|y\right|\left|\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}\right|\leq\left|x-\lambda_{i}y\right|+\left|x-\lambda_{j}y\right|\leq 2\left|x-\lambda_{i}y\right|.

In combination with
\left|y\right|\geq 2
, we thus have

\left|x-\lambda_{k}y\right|\geq\left|\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{j}\right|

and, as the product
\left(x-\lambda_{j}y\right)\left(x-\lambda_{k}y\right)\left(x-\lambda_{l}y\right)=\pm 1
,

\left|x-\lambda_{j}y\right|=\left|x-\lambda_{k}y\right|^{-1}\left|x-\lambda_{l}y\right|^{-1}\leq\left|\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{j}\right|^{-1}\left|\lambda_{l}-\lambda_{j}\right|^{-1}.

We put everything together and get

\displaystyle\left|\frac{x-\lambda_{j}y}{x-\lambda_{k}y}\right|\cdot\left|\frac{\lambda_{l}-\lambda_{k}}{\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{l}}\right|\leq\frac{\left|\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{j}\right|^{-1}\left|\lambda_{l}-\lambda_{j}\right|^{-1}}{\left|\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{j}\right|}\cdot\frac{\left|\lambda_{l}-\lambda_{j}\right|+\left|\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{j}\right|}{\left|\lambda_{l}-\lambda_{j}\right|},

where we also used the triangle inequality on the numerator of the second fraction. The right hand side is now equal to

\left|\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{j}\right|^{-2}\left|\lambda_{l}-\lambda_{j}\right|^{-1}+\left|\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{j}\right|^{-1}\left|\lambda_{l}-\lambda_{j}\right|^{-2}\leq 2c_{5}^{3}\,n^{-d_{1}}\left|\alpha\right|^{-2n}\left|\beta\right|^{-n}

as per Lemma
5
, and the bound decreases exponentially even if
\left|\alpha\right|=1
.
∎

We’d like to use the lower bound from Theorem
4
directly on
\Lambda

, their logarithmic heights are linear in

, from which we can only deduce

\log\left|y\right|\gg n

(7)

instead of the desired exponential lower bound.

We thus aim to shift the exponential dependency on

from the logarithms into the coefficients, where an additional factor

5.2.
A closer look at the powers

To apply Baker’s and Wüstholz’s lower bound on the linear form
\xi

\xi\neq 0
first. For that, we have to either restrict the logarithms, such as demanding linear independence or gain further information on the coefficients, and thus on
b_{1},b_{2}

To that end, we return to the equations

\left|x-\lambda_{i}y\right|=\left|\lambda_{i}^{b_{1}}\left(\lambda_{i}-A_{n}\right)^{b_{2}}\right|\;\;\forall i\in\left\{1,2,3\right\}.

If we take the logarithm, the equations for

\begin{pmatrix}\log\left|x-\lambda_{k}y\right|\\

to get an explicit formula for

\displaystyle\log\left|x-\lambda_{i}y\right|

\displaystyle=\log\left|x-\lambda_{j}y-\left(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}\right)y\right|

\displaystyle=\log\left|y\right|+\log\left|\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}\right|+\log\left(1-\frac{x-\lambda_{j}y}{\left(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}\right)y}\right)

for

\frac{\left|x-\lambda_{j}\right|}{\left|\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}\right|\left|y\right|}\leq\frac{\left|\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{j}\right|^{-1}\left|\lambda_{l}-\lambda_{j}\right|^{-1}}{2\left|\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}\right|}\leq\frac{1}{2}c_{5}^{3}\,n^{-d_{1}}\left|\alpha\right|^{-2n}\left|\beta\right|^{-n}

\log\left|x-\lambda_{i}y\right|=\log\left|y\right|+\log\left|\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}\right|+L\left(\frac{1}{2}c_{5}^{3}\,n^{-d_{1}}\left|\alpha\right|^{-2n}\left|\beta\right|^{-n}\right).

We use this expression to rewrite Equation (

\begin{pmatrix}b_{1}\\
b_{2}\end{pmatrix}=\frac{1}{R}\begin{pmatrix}\log\left|\lambda_{l}-A_{n}\right|&-\log\left|\lambda_{k}-A_{n}\right|\\

\displaystyle\left(\log\left|y\right|+n\log\left|\beta\right|+\chi_{\mathbb{R}\backslash\left\{\left|\beta\right|\right\}}(\left|\alpha\right|)\log\left|c_{B}\right|+\chi_{\left\{\left|\beta\right|\right\}}(\left|\alpha\right|)\log\left|c_{B}-c_{A}\right|\right)

up to exponentially decreasing errors. Further assume that
\left|\alpha\right|\neq\left|\beta\right|
and we look at the logarithms
\log\left|\alpha\right|,\log\left|\beta\right|
in
\xi_{2}
. The term in question is

n\left(b_{1}-\left(b_{2}-1\right)\right)\log\left|\alpha\right|+n\left(2b_{1}+b_{2}-1\right)\log\left|\beta\right|

and dominates the other terms. Thus, it suffices to show that its highest order terms do not cancel each other out, which is easily verified with the formula derived for
b_{1}
and
b_{2}
.

If
\left|\alpha\right|=\left|\beta\right|
instead, the same term simplifies to
3nb_{1}\log\left|\alpha\right|
and the formula for
b_{1}
itself to
R\,b_{1}=\log\left|y\right|\left(\log\left|c_{B}-c_{A}\right|-\log\left|c_{A}\right|\right)
. We can no longer claim that
3nb_{1}
dominates the other coefficients of
\xi_{2}
and have to take a closer look.

To that end, we first write
R\,b_{2}
explicitly in terms of
n^{2},n
and
\log\left|y\right|
. We have

\displaystyle R\,b_{2}

\displaystyle=n^{2}\left(-3\left(\log\left|\alpha\right|\right)^{2}\right)+n\cdot 2\log\left|\alpha\right|\left(-\log\left|c_{A}\right|-\log\left|c_{B}\right|-\log\left|c_{B}-c_{A}\right|\right)

\displaystyle+\log\left|y\right|\left(-3n\log\left|\alpha\right|-2\log\left|c_{B}\right|-\log\left|c_{A}\right|\right).

We plug everything into the linear form
\xi_{2}
,

3nb_{1}\log\left|\alpha\right|+\left(b_{1}-b_{2}+1\right)\log\left|c_{A}\right|+2b_{1}\log\left|c_{B}\right|+\left(b_{2}-1\right)\log\left|c_{B}-c_{A}\right|,

and group the terms with respect to their order in
n
, differentiating two cases. Either the term with
\frac{1}{R}\log\left|y\right|\,n
, the whole term is

\frac{1}{R}\log\left|y\right|\,n\cdot 3\log\left|\alpha\right|\left(\log\left|c_{A}\right|-\log\left|c_{B}-c_{A}\right|\right),

is at most constant, equivalently
\log\left|y\right|=O(n)
. In that case, since
\log\left|c_{A}\right|-\log\left|c_{B}-c_{A}\right|>0
as per our conditions on
A_{n},B_{n}
, the whole term equals at most some positive constant. While the term
\frac{1}{R}n^{2}\cdot 3\log\left|\alpha\right|^{2}\left(\log\left|c_{A}\right|-\log\left|c_{B}-c_{A}\right|\right)
equals some positive constant, and thus their sum. All the other terms decrease with increasing
n
, thus the linear form
\xi_{2}
does not cancel for sufficiently large

. In the other case, the term with
\frac{1}{R}\log\left|y\right|\cdot n
dominates all others – including the one with
\frac{1}{R}n^{2}
– and we can conclude
\xi_{2}\neq 0
for sufficiently large
n
just by the non-vanishing of the
\frac{1}{R}\log\left|y\right|\,n
term.

Finally, assume
j=3
and take
(k,l)=(2,1)
. We again have, by Equation (
10
) and Lemma
4
,

\displaystyle R\,b_{1}=

\displaystyle\left(n\log\left|\beta\right|+\chi_{\mathbb{R}\backslash\left\{\left|\beta\right|\right\}}(\left|\alpha\right|)\log\left|c_{B}\right|+\chi_{\left\{\left|\beta\right|\right\}}(\left|\alpha\right|)\log\left|c_{B}-c_{A}\right|\right)

\displaystyle\left(\log\left|y\right|+n\log\left|\alpha\right|+\log\left|c_{A}\right|\right)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\big(-n\left(\log\left|\alpha\right|+\log\left|\beta\right|\right)-\log\left|c_{A}\right|-\chi_{\mathbb{R}\backslash\left\{\left|\beta\right|\right\}}(\left|\alpha\right|)\log\left|c_{B}\right|

\displaystyle+\chi_{\left\{\left|\beta\right|\right\}}(\left|\alpha\right|)\log\left|c_{B}-c_{A}\right|\big)\left(\log\left|y\right|+n\log\left|\beta\right|+\log\left|c_{B}\right|\right)

and

\displaystyle R\,b_{2}=-

\displaystyle\left(n\log\left|\beta\right|+\log\left|c_{B}\right|\right)\left(\log\left|y\right|+n\log\left|\alpha\right|+\log\left|c_{A}\right|\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left(n\log\left|\alpha\right|+\log\left|c_{A}\right|\right)\left(\log\left|y\right|+n\log\left|\beta\right|+\log\left|c_{B}\right|\right)

up to exponentially decreasing errors. Again, if
\left|\alpha\right|\neq\left|\beta\right|
, the coefficients of
\log\left|\alpha\right|,\log\left|\beta\right|
dominate the others in
\xi_{3}
, the term in question being

n\left(b_{2}-\left(b_{1}-1\right)\right)\log\left|\alpha\right|+n\left(2b_{2}+\left(b_{1}-1\right)\right)\log\left|\beta\right|.

It follows from the formulas for

\xi_{3}\neq 0
.

If
\left|\alpha\right|=\left|\beta\right|

b_{2}

,

\displaystyle R\,b_{1}

\displaystyle=n^{2}\left(3\left(\log\left|\alpha\right|\right)^{2}\right)+n\cdot 2\log\left|\alpha\right|\left(\log\left|c_{A}\right|+\log\left|c_{B}\right|+\log\left|c_{B}-c_{A}\right|\right)

\displaystyle+\log\left|y\right|\left(3n\log\left|\alpha\right|+\log\left|c_{A}\right|+2\log\left|c_{B}-c_{A}\right|\right).

We then plug everything into the linear form
\xi_{3}

3nb_{2}\log\left|\alpha\right|+\left(b_{2}+b_{1}-1\right)\log\left|c_{A}\right|-\log\left|c_{B}\right|+2b_{2}\log\left|c_{B}-c_{A}\right|,

∎

With

. The coefficients of

Everything put together, Theorem
4

\log\left|\xi_{j}\right|\gg-\left(\log n\right)^{3}\cdot\log\left(n^{4}\log n\right).

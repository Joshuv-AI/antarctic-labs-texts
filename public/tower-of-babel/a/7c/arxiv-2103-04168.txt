Construction of excited multi-solitons for the focusing 4D cubic wave equation
XU YUAN

Abstract
. Consider the focusing 4D cubic wave equation ∂ t ​ t u − Δ ​ u − u 3 = 0 , on ​ ( t , x ) ∈ [ 0 , ∞ ) × ℝ 4 . \partial_{tt}u-\Delta u-u^{3}=0,\quad\mbox{on}\ (t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{4}. The main result states the existence in energy space H ˙ 1 × L 2 \dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2} of multi-solitary waves where each traveling wave is generated by Lorentz transform from a specific excited state, with different but collinear Lorentz speeds. The specific excited state is deduced from the non-degenerate sign-changing state constructed in Musso-Wei [ 34 ] . The proof is inspired by the techniques developed for the 5D energy-critical wave equation and the nonlinear Klein-Gordon equation in a similar context by Martel-Merle [ 30 ] and Côte-Martel [ 6 ] . The main difficulty originates from the strong interactions between solutions in the 4D case compared to other dispersive and wave-type models. To overcome the difficulty, a sharp understanding of the asymptotic behavior of the excited states involved and of the kernel of their linearized operator is needed.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Main result

Consider the focusing 4D cubic wave equation,

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{tt}u-\Delta u-u^{3}=0,\ \ (t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{4},\\
&(u,\partial_{t}u)_{|t=0}=(u_{0},u_{1})\in\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2}.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

Recall that the Cauchy problem for equation (
1.1
) is locally well-posed in the energy space
\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2}
. See
e.g.

[
25
]
and references therein. For any
\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2}
solution, the energy
E
and momentum
P
are conserved, where

E(u,\partial_{t}u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+(\partial_{t}u)^{2}-\frac{1}{4}u^{4}\right){\rm{d}}x,\quad P(u,\partial_{t}u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\partial_{t}u\nabla u{\rm{d}}x.

Denote by
\Sigma
the set of non-zero stationary solutions of (
1.1
),

\Sigma=\left\{q\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{4})\setminus 0:-\Delta q-q^{3}=0\ \ \mbox{on}\ \mathbb{R}^{4}\right\}.

(1.2)

For
q\in\Sigma
and
\boldsymbol{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}^{4}
with
|\boldsymbol{\ell}|<1
, let

q_{\boldsymbol{\ell}}(x)=q\left(\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-|\boldsymbol{\ell}|^{2}}}-1\right)\frac{\boldsymbol{\ell}(\boldsymbol{\ell}\cdot x)}{|\boldsymbol{\ell}|^{2}}+x\right),

then
u(t,x)=q_{\boldsymbol{\ell}}(x-\boldsymbol{\ell}t)
is a global, bounded solution of (
1.1
).

Standard elliptic arguments (see
e.g.

[
21
]
) show that if
q\in\Sigma
, then
q
is of class
C^{\infty}
. It is known by the results developed in
[
2
,
4
,
35
,
36
]
that up to dilation and translation, the only
positive
stationary solution of (
1.1
) is the
ground state

W
, where
W
has the explicit form

W(x)=\left(1+\frac{|x|^{2}}{8}\right)^{-1}\quad\mbox{on}\ \mathbb{R}^{4}.

Functions
q\in\Sigma
with
q\neq W
(up to sign, dilation and translation) are called
excited states
.
The existence of excited states that are nonradial, sign-changing and with arbitrary large energy was first proved by Ding
[
15
]
using variational arguments. Later, more explicit constructions of sign-changing solutions have been obtained by del Pino-Musso-Pacard-Pistoia
[
10
]
. More specifically, they constructed solutions with a centered soliton crowned by negative spikes (rescaled solitons) at the vertices of a regular polygon of radius 1. Then, following a similar general strategy in
[
10
]
, they constructed in
[
11
]
sign changing, non radial solutions to the Yamabe-type equation on the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{d}
(
d\geq 4
) whose energy is concentrated along special submanifolds of
\mathbb{S}^{d}
.

For any
q\in\Sigma
, we denote the linearized operator around
q
by

\mathcal{L}_{q}=-\Delta-3q^{2}.

Set

\mathcal{Z}_{q}=\left\{f\in\dot{H}^{1}\ \mbox{such that}\ \mathcal{L}_{q}f=0\right\},

and

\qquad\widetilde{{\mathcal{Z}}}_{q}={\rm{Span}}\bigg\{q+x\cdot\nabla q;\ (x_{i}\partial_{x_{j}}q-x_{j}\partial_{x_{i}}q),1\leq i<j\leq 4;\\
\partial_{x_{i}}q;\ -2x_{i}q+|x|^{2}\partial_{x_{i}}q-2x_{i}(x\cdot\nabla q),1\leq i\leq 4\bigg\}.\qquad\qquad

The function space
\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}_{q}\subset\mathcal{Z}_{q}
is the null space of
\mathcal{L}_{q}
that is generated by a family of explicit transformations (see
[
20
, Lemma 3.8]
and §
2.1
).
Following Duyckaerts-Kenig-Merle
[
20
]
, we call
q\in\Sigma
a
non-degenerate state
if
\mathcal{Z}_{q}=\widetilde{{\mathcal{Z}}}_{q}
. The first nontrivial example of non-degenerate sign-changing state was obtained in Musso-Wei
[
34
]
who proved that the sign-changing states constructed in
[
10
]
are non-degenerate. Later, following a similar strategy, Medina-Musso-Wei
[
31
]
constructed a non-degenerate state with maximal rank,
i.e.

{\rm{dim}}\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}_{q}=15
.

The main goal of this article is to construct multi-solitons of (
1.1
) based on a specific non-degenerate excited state.

Theorem 1.1

(Existence of excited multi-solitons)
.

There exists a non-degenerate excited state
Q\in\Sigma
such that the following is true. Let
N\geq 2
and
\boldsymbol{\rm{e}}_{1}
be the first vector of the canonical basis of
\mathbb{R}^{4}
. For
n=1,\dots,N
, let
\tau_{n}=\pm 1
and
\boldsymbol{\ell}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{4}
be such that

\boldsymbol{\ell}_{n}=\ell_{n}\boldsymbol{\rm{e}}_{1}\ \mbox{with}\ -1<\ell_{1}<\ell_{2}<\dots<\ell_{N}<1.

Then, there exist
T_{0}>0
and a solution
\vec{u}=(u,\partial_{t}u)
of (
1.1
) on
[T_{0},+\infty)
in the energy space
\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2}
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to+\infty}\left\|u(t)-\sum_{n=1}^{N}\tau_{n}Q_{\boldsymbol{\ell}_{n}}(\cdot-\boldsymbol{\ell}_{n}t)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}=0,

(1.3)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to+\infty}\left\|\partial_{t}u(t)+\sum_{n=1}^{N}\tau_{n}\ell_{n}\partial_{x_{1}}Q_{\boldsymbol{\ell}_{n}}(\cdot-\boldsymbol{\ell}_{n}t)\right\|_{L^{2}}=0.

Remark 1.2
.

The specific non-degenerate state
Q
involved in Theorem
1.1
is obtained from the non-degenerate sign-changing state constructed in
[
10
,
34
]
. Indeed, by rotation, translation and Kelvin transformation on a non-degenerate sign-changing state, we obtain a non-degenerate excited state that decays fast enough at least along
\boldsymbol{\rm{e}}_{1}
(see more details in §
2.1
). The interaction of two such traveling waves with different speeds along
\boldsymbol{\rm{e}}_{1}
, is formally of order
t^{-4}
for large time. This rate allows us to close the energy estimates (see more details in §
4.1
and §
4.2
).
However, the slow algebraic decay of the linearized operator’s kernel leads to another technical difficulty. To overcome it, we need to refine the energy estimates (see more details in §
3.2
and §
4.2
).

Remark 1.3
.

The Theorem
1.1
is also true for the more general case where each traveling wave is based on non-degenerate
q
with the same asymptotic behavior as
Q
(see Lemma
2.2
and Lemma
2.3
). In particular, we do not need each traveling wave to be based on the same non-degenerate state.

Remark 1.4
.

Following
[
30
, §1.2]
, we conjecture that in the 4D case, there exists no multi-soliton based on ground state
W
in the sence of Theorem
1.1
, for any
N\geq 2
. Heuristically, from
W(x)\sim|x|^{-2}
as
|x|\to\infty
, the interaction between two solitons of different speeds is
t^{-2}
. Following the modulation and energy method, we find these interactions are too strong and formally that can create diverging terms of the geometric parameters. However, such nonexistence result is open problem for any space dimension
d\geq 3
.

Historically, the existence of multi-soliton solutions for non-integrable cases was first studied by Merle
[
32
]
for the
L^{2}
critical nonlinear Schrödinger equation (NLS) and Martel
[
28
]
for the subcritical and critical generalized Korteweg-de Vries equations (gKdV). Later, the strategy of these works was extended to other dispersive or wave-type models (see
[
3
,
5
,
8
,
29
,
33
,
37
,
38
]
). In particular, the present work is inspired by the proof of the existence of multi-soliton solutions for the nonlinear Klein-Gordon and 5D energy-critical wave equations in
[
6
,
30
]
. Technically, such constructions of multi-solitons of the energy-critical wave equation are more challenging than for other dispersive and wave-type models, because of the weak algebraic decay of solitons.

To our knowledge, this article proves the first statement of the existence of multi-soliton solutions for the 4D energy-critical wave equation. The construction of such solutions for the energy-critical wave equation is especially motivated by the soliton resolution conjecture, fully solved in the 3D radial case by Duyckaerts-Kenig-Merle
[
19
]
, and for a subsequence of time in 3, 4 and 5D by Duyckaerts-Jia-Kenig-Merle
[
16
]
. Recently, the conjecture in the 4D radial case is proved by Duyckaerts-Kenig-Martel-Merle
[
17
]
. Indeed, the existence of such multi-solitons complements the statements in
[
16
]
by exhibiting examples of solutions containing
N
solitons at
t\to+\infty
for any
N\geq 2
. In the same direction, recall that for the 6D energy-critical wave equation, Jendrej
[
23
]
proved the existence of radial two-bubble solutions. Later, for the 5D energy-critical wave equation, Jendrej-Martel
[
24
]
proved the existence of
N
-bubble solutions for any
N\geq 2
. For the 3 and 5D cases, Krieger-Nakanishi-Schlag
[
26
]
constructed a center-stable manifold of the ground state. Recall also that, for the 4D case, Hillairet-Raphaël
[
22
]
proved the existence of type-II finite time blow-up solutions containing single soliton.

We refer to
[
12
,
13
,
14
]
for results of the existence of type-II blow up solutions containing single or multi-bubble of the energy-critical heat equation. Particularly related to the present article, the blow up profile in
[
14
]
is the non-degenerate sign-changing state constructed in
[
10
,
34
]
rather than the ground state
W
.

The article is organized as follows. Section
2
introduces the technical tools involved in the choice of specific excited state
Q
: the spectral theory of the linearized operator, the asymptotic behavior of
Q
and of the kernel of its linearized operator. Section
3
introduces the technical tools involved in a dynamical approach to the
N
-soliton problem for (
1.1
): estimates of the nonlinear interactions between solitons, decomposition by modulation and parameter estimates. Finally, Theorem
1.1
is proved in Section
4
by energy estimates and a suitable compactness argument.

Preliminaries

2.1.
Transforms and Asymptotic behavior

Following
[
20
]
, we first recall that
\Sigma
is invariant under the following four transformations:

{\rm{(i)}}

translation: If
q\in\Sigma
then
q(x+x_{0})\in\Sigma
for all
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{4}
;

{\rm{(ii)}}

dilation: If
q\in\Sigma
then
\lambda q(\lambda x)\in\Sigma
for all
\lambda\in(0,\infty)
;

{\rm{(iii)}}

orthogonal transformation: If
q\in\Sigma
then
q\left(Ox\right)\in\Sigma
for all
O\in\mathcal{O}_{4}
.

{\rm{(iv)}}

Kelvin transformation: If
q\in\Sigma
then
\mathrm{K}q\in\Sigma
where
\mathrm{K}q(x)=\frac{1}{|x|^{2}}q\left(\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}\right)
.

We denote by
\mathcal{M}
the group of isometries of
\dot{H}^{1}
generated by the preceding transformations.
From
[
20
, Section 3]
, it is known
\mathcal{M}
defines a 15-parameter family of transformations in a
neighborhood of the identity.

For
\theta=(\theta_{ij})_{1\leq i<j\leq 4}\in\mathbb{R}^{6}
, set

A_{\theta}=\left[a_{i,j}^{\theta}\right]_{1\leq i\leq j\leq 4},\quad\mathcal{R}_{\theta}=e^{A_{\theta}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{A_{\theta}^{n}}{n!},

where
a_{i,i}^{\theta}=0
,
a_{i,j}^{\theta}=\theta_{ij}
if
i<j
,
a^{\theta}_{j,i}=-\theta_{ij}
if
i<j
.

For
\mathcal{A}=(\lambda,z,\xi,\theta)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\times\mathbb{R}^{6}
, we introduce the following explicit formula of transform
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{A}}\in\mathcal{M}
,

\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{A}}(f)(x)=\lambda\left|\frac{x}{|x|}-z|x|\right|^{-2}f\left(\xi+\frac{\lambda\mathcal{R}_{\theta}(x-z|x|^{2})}{1-2\langle z,x\rangle+|z|^{2}|x|^{2}}\right)\quad\mbox{for}\ f\in\dot{H}^{1}.

Recall that for all
q\in\Sigma
,
\mathcal{\widetilde{Z}}_{q}
is generated by taking partial derivatives of
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{A}}(q)
with respect to
\mathcal{A}=(\lambda,z,\xi,\theta)

.
Also, following
[

Lemma 2.2
.

There exists non-degenerate excited state

(i)

Asymptotic behavior of
Q
. For all

\left|Q(x)-\frac{x_{4}}{|x|^{4}}\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{|x|^{4}}.

(2.1)

(ii)

\left|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}Q(x)\right|\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-\left(3+|\alpha|\right)}\quad\mbox{on}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{4}.

(2.2)

Proof.

Let
q\in\Sigma
be the non-degenerate sign-changing state in Theorem
2.1
. Without loss of generality, we assume
q(0)>0
. Set

r=\sup\left\{R>0;q(x)>0\ \mbox{holds on}\ \mathcal{B}_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}(R)\right\}.

Since
q
is sign-changing state, we know that

is well-defined finite, and there exists

. Moreover, by the Hopf’s Lemma (see for instance
[

\nabla q(x_{0})\neq 0

Considering translation

satisfying

\tilde{q}(0)=\partial_{x_{1}}\tilde{q}(0)=\partial_{x_{2}}\tilde{q}(0)=\partial_{x_{3}}\tilde{q}(0)=0\quad\mbox{and}\quad\partial_{x_{4}}\tilde{q}(0)=1.

(2.3)

Next, we consider the Kelvin transform
\mathrm{K}
to
\tilde{q}
and denote it by
Q
,
i.e.

Q(x)=\left(\mathrm{K}\tilde{q}\right)(x)=\frac{1}{|x|^{2}}\tilde{q}\left(\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}\right)\quad\mbox{for}\ x\neq 0.

Following
[
20
, Section 3]
, we know that
Q
is also a non-degenerate state,

-\Delta Q-Q^{3}=0\ \ \mbox{with}\ Q\in C^{\infty},\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathcal{Z}_{Q}=\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}_{Q}.

Moreover, from
[
20
, Lemma 3.8]
and
\mathrm{K}^{2}={\rm{Id}}
, we have, for
i=1,\dots,4
,

\widetilde{\Omega}_{i}Q(x)=\left(\mathrm{K}\left(\partial_{x_{i}}\tilde{q}\right)\right)(x)=\frac{1}{|x|^{2}}\left((\partial_{x_{i}}\tilde{q})\left(\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}\right)\right)\quad\mbox{for}\ x\neq 0.

(2.4)

Now we start to prove
Q
satisfying (i) and (ii).

Proof of (i). Applied the Taylor expansion (
1.5
) of
\tilde{q}
at

), we have

\tilde{q}(x)=\tilde{q}(0)+\sum_{i=1}^{4}\partial_{x_{i}}\tilde{q}(0)x_{i}+O(|x|^{2})=x_{4}+O(|x|^{2})\quad\forall\ |x|<1.

Replace

Therefore, from the definition of Kelvin transform in §

, we have

Q(x)=\frac{1}{|x|^{2}}\tilde{q}\left(\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}\right)=\frac{x_{4}}{|x|^{4}}+O\left(\frac{1}{|x|^{4}}\right)\quad\forall\ |x|>1,

which means (

Proof of (ii). On the one hand, from (

\left|\tilde{q}\left(\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}\right)\right|\lesssim\frac{|x_{4}|}{|x|^{2}}+O\left(\frac{1}{|x|^{2}}\right)\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-1},\quad\forall\ |x|>1.

Then, from

\left|\partial_{x_{i}}\tilde{q}\left(\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}\right)\right|=\left|\sum_{j=1}^{4}\left((\partial_{x_{j}}\tilde{q})\left(\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}\right)\right)\partial_{x_{i}}\left(\frac{x_{j}}{|x|^{2}}\right)\right|\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-2}.

Using a similar argument as above for all

\left|\partial^{\gamma}_{x}\tilde{q}\left(\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}\right)\right|\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-(1+|\gamma|)}\quad\forall\ |x|>1.

(2.7)

On the other hand, by direct computation and chain rule,

\left|\partial_{x}^{\beta}\left(\frac{1}{|x|^{2}}\right)\right|\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-(2+|\beta|)}\quad\forall\ \beta\in\mathbb{N}^{4}\ \mbox{and}\ |x|>1.

\displaystyle=\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha}C_{\alpha}^{\beta}\left(\partial^{\gamma}_{x}\tilde{q}\left(\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}\right)\right)\left(\partial_{x}^{\beta}\left(\frac{1}{|x|^{2}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\beta+\gamma=\alpha}O\left(\langle x\rangle^{-(1+|\gamma|)}\langle x\rangle^{-(2+|\beta|)}\right)=O\left(\langle x\rangle^{-(3+|\alpha|)}\right).

Combining above estimates with

Lemma 2.3

(Asymptotic behavior of kernel functions)
.

There exist

\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}_{Q}={\rm{Span}}\left\{\psi,\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{K}\right\}.

Moreover, we have

Lemma 3.9

For

\xi

\sigma

\psi

\psi

which means (

Proof of (
3.34

n=1,\dots,N

\left(\frac{x_{1}-\ell_{n}t}{\sqrt{1-\ell_{n}^{2}}},\bar{x}\right)\mapsto(y_{1},\bar{y}).

\left(\Psi_{n},\Psi_{n}\xi_{n}\right)_{L^{2}}=(1-\ell_{n}^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\psi^{2}(x)\xi\left(\frac{|x|}{\sigma t}\right){\rm{d}}x.

\displaystyle=\int_{|x|<1}\psi^{2}(x){\rm{d}}x,\quad\quad\ H_{4}=\int_{\sigma t<|x|<2\sigma t}\psi^{2}(x)\xi\left(\frac{|x|}{\sigma t}\right){\rm{d}}x,

\displaystyle=\int_{1<|x|<\sigma t}|x|^{-4}{\rm{d}}x,\quad H_{6}=\int_{1<|x|<\sigma t}\left(\psi^{2}(x)-|x|^{-4}\right){\rm{d}}x.

\displaystyle\left(\vec{\Phi}_{n,k},\vec{Z}_{n,j}^{\pm}\right)_{L^{2}}=\left(\vec{\phi}_{k,\ell_{n}},\mathcal{H}_{\ell_{n}}\vec{\Upsilon}_{j,\ell_{n}}^{\pm}\right)_{L^{2}}=\left(\mathcal{H}_{\ell_{n}}\vec{\phi}_{k,\ell_{n}},\vec{\Upsilon}_{j,\ell_{n}}^{\pm}\right)_{L^{2}}=0.

\displaystyle\left(\vec{{\rm{Mod}}},\vec{Z}_{n,j}^{\pm}\right)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n^{\prime}\neq n}\dot{a}_{n^{\prime}}\left(\vec{\Psi}_{n^{\prime}},\vec{Z}_{n,j}^{\pm}\right)_{L^{2}}+\sum_{n^{\prime}\neq n}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\dot{b}_{n^{\prime},k}\left(\vec{\Phi}_{n^{\prime},k},\vec{Z}_{n,j}^{\pm}\right)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=O\left((\|\vec{\varphi}\|_{\mathcal{H}}+|\boldsymbol{a}|^{2}+|\boldsymbol{b}|^{2}+t^{-4})t^{-2}\right)

\displaystyle=O\left(\|\vec{\varphi}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+|\boldsymbol{a}|^{4}+|\boldsymbol{b}|^{4}+t^{-4}\right).

Gathering above estimates, we obtain (

∎

Recall that,

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\hbox{$\chi_{N}(t,x)=\ell_{1}$ for $x_{1}\in(-\infty,\bar{\ell}_{1}t]$},\\

This strictly improves the estimate on
\vec{\varphi}
in (
4.4
) for
C_{0}
large enough.

Step 2.
Closing the estimate in
\boldsymbol{a}
. First, from (
3.31
) and (
4.4
), for all
t\in[T^{*},S_{m}]
,

\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left|c_{n}(t)\right|\lesssim\log^{-1}t\left(\|\varphi_{1}\|_{L^{4}}\|\Psi_{n}\xi_{n}\|_{L^{\frac{4}{3}}}\right)\lesssim C_{0}t^{-2}\log^{-1}t.

(4.40)

Then, from (
3.35
) and (
4.4
), we have

\sum_{n=1}^{N}|\dot{a}_{n}+\dot{c}_{n}|\lesssim C_{0}t^{-3}\log^{-\frac{1}{2}}t+C_{0}^{2}t^{-4}.

Integrating above estimate on
[t,S_{m}]
for any
t\in[T^{*},S_{m}]
and then using (
4.40
),

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N}|a_{n}(t)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left(|c_{n}(t)|+|c_{n}(S_{m})|\right)+C_{0}t^{-2}\log^{-\frac{1}{2}}t+C_{0}^{2}t^{-3}

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{0}t^{-2}\log^{-1}t+C_{0}t^{-2}\log^{-\frac{1}{2}}t+C_{0}^{2}t^{-3}\lesssim C_{0}t^{-2}\log^{-\frac{1}{2}}t,

for
T_{0}
large enough. This strictly improves the estimate on
\boldsymbol{a}
in (
4.4
) for taking
C_{0}
large enough.

Step 3.
Closing the estimate in
\boldsymbol{b}
. From (
3.25
) and (
4.4
), we have

\sum_{(n,k)\in I^{0}}|\dot{b}_{n,k}|\lesssim C_{0}t^{-3}+C_{0}^{4}t^{-4}.

Integrating above estimate on
[t,S_{m}]
for any
t\in[T^{*},S_{m}]
and then using (
4.39
),

\sum_{(n,k)\in I^{0}}|b_{n,k}(t)|\lesssim C_{0}t^{-2}+C_{0}^{4}t^{-3}\lesssim C_{0}t^{-2},

for
T_{0}
large enough. This strictly improves the estimate on
\boldsymbol{b}
in (
4.4
) for taking
C_{0}
large enough.

Step 4.
Closing the estimate in
(z_{n,j}^{-})_{(n,j)\in I}
. By (
3.38
) and (
4.4
), for all
(n,j)\in I
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm{d}}}{{\rm{d}}t}\left(z_{n,j}^{-}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=2\alpha_{n,j}\left(z_{n,j}^{-}\right)^{2}+O\left(\|\vec{\varphi}\|_{\mathcal{H}}\left(\|\vec{\varphi}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+|\boldsymbol{a}|^{2}+|\boldsymbol{b}|^{2}+t^{-4}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=2\alpha_{n,j}\left(z_{n,j}^{-}\right)^{2}+O\left(C_{0}^{3}t^{-9}+C_{0}^{5}t^{-7}\right)=2\alpha_{n,j}\left(z_{n,j}^{-}\right)^{2}+O\left(C_{0}^{5}t^{-7}\right).

Integrating above estimate on
[t,S_{m}]
for any
t\in[T^{*},S_{m}]
and then using (
4.39
)

\displaystyle\left(z_{n,j}^{-}\right)^{2}(t)

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{-2\alpha_{n,j}(S_{m}-t)}\left(z_{n,j}^{-}\right)^{2}(S_{m})+C_{0}^{5}\int_{t}^{S_{m}}e^{2\alpha_{n,j}(t-s)}s^{-7}{\rm{d}}s\lesssim C_{0}^{5}t^{-7}.

This strictly improves the estimate on
(z_{n,j}^{-})_{(n,j)\in I}
in (
4.4
) for taking
T_{0}
and
C_{0}
large enough.

Step 5.
Final argument on the unstable parameters. Let

\mathcal{A}(t)=\sum_{(n,j)\in I}\left(z_{n,j}^{+}\right)^{2}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\bar{\alpha}=\min_{(n,j)\in I}\alpha_{n,j}>0.

We claim, for any time
t\in[T^{*},S_{m}]
where it holds
\mathcal{A}(t)=t^{-7}
, the following transversality property holds,

\frac{{\rm{d}}}{{\rm{d}}t}\left(t^{7}\mathcal{A}(t)\right)\leq-\bar{\alpha}.

(4.41)

Indeed, from (
3.38
) and (
4.4
), for any
t\in[T^{*},S_{m}]
where it holds
\mathcal{A}(t)=t^{-7}
,

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm{d}}}{{\rm{d}}t}\mathcal{A}(t)

\displaystyle=-2\sum_{(n,j)\in I}\alpha_{n,j}\left(z^{+}_{n,j}\right)^{2}+O\bigg(\sum_{(n,j)\in I}|z^{+}_{n,j}|\left(\|\vec{\varphi}\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+|\boldsymbol{a}|^{2}+|\boldsymbol{b}|^{2}+t^{-4}\right)\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq-2\bar{\alpha}t^{-7}+O\left(C_{0}^{2}t^{-\frac{19}{2}}+C_{0}^{4}t^{-\frac{15}{2}}\right)\leq-2\bar{\alpha}t^{-7}+C_{0}^{5}t^{-\frac{15}{2}},

which implies (
4.41
) for taking
T_{0}
large enough. The transversality relation (
4.41
) is enough to justify the existence of at least a couple
\boldsymbol{z}_{m}=(z_{n,j}^{m})_{(n,j)\in I}\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathbb{R}^{|I|}}\left(S_{m}^{-\frac{7}{2}}\right)
such that
T^{*}(\boldsymbol{z}_{m})=T_{0}
.

The proof is by contradiction, we assume that for all
\boldsymbol{z}_{m}=(z_{n,j}^{m})_{(n,j)\in I}\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathbb{R}^{|I|}}\left(S_{m}^{-\frac{7}{2}}\right)
, it holds
T_{0}<T_{*}(\boldsymbol{z}_{m})
. Then, a contradiction follows from the following discussion (see for instance more details in
[
8
, Section 3.1]
and
[
30
, Lemma 4.2]
).

Continuity of
T^{*}(\boldsymbol{z}_{m})
.
The above transversality condition (
4.41
) implies that the map

\boldsymbol{z}_{m}\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathbb{R}^{|I|}}\left(S_{m}^{-\frac{7}{2}}\right)\mapsto T^{*}(\boldsymbol{z}_{m})\in[T_{0},S_{m}]

is continuous and

T^{*}({\boldsymbol{z}}_{m})=S_{m}\quad\mbox{for}\quad\boldsymbol{z}_{m}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{R}^{|I|}}\left(S_{m}^{-\frac{7}{2}}\right).

Construction of a retraction
. We define

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}:\ \bar{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathbb{R}^{|I|}}\left(S_{m}^{-\frac{7}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\mapsto\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{R}^{|I|}}\left(S_{m}^{-\frac{7}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{z}_{m}=(z_{n,j}^{m})_{(n,j)\in I}

\displaystyle\mapsto\left(\frac{T^{*}(\boldsymbol{z}_{m})}{S_{m}}\right)^{\frac{7}{2}}\left(z_{n,j}^{+}\left(T^{*}(\boldsymbol{z}_{m})\right)\right)_{(n,j)\in I}.

From what precedes,
\mathcal{M}
is continuous. Moreover,
\mathcal{M}
restricted to

\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{R}^{|I|}}\left(S_{m}^{-\frac{7}{2}}\right)
is the identity. The
existence of such a map is contradictory with the no retraction theorem for continuous maps from the ball to
the sphere. Therefore, the existence of
\boldsymbol{z}_{m}\in{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathbb{R}^{|I|}}\left(S_{m}^{-\frac{7}{2}}\right)
have proved. Then, the uniform estimate (
4.3
) is a consequence of bootstrap estimates (
4.4
). The proof of Proposition
4.2
is complete.

∎

4.4.
Proof of Theorem
1.1
from Proposition
4.2

The proof is based on a standard compactness argument (see for example
[
6
,
24
,
38
]
).
The main point is the following proposition of weak continuity of the flow near a compact set.

Proposition 4.8
.

There exists a constant
\varepsilon>0
such that the following holds. Let
\mathcal{S}\subset\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2}
be a compact set and let
\vec{u}_{m}:[T_{1},T_{2}]\to\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2}
be a sequence of solutions of (
1.1
) such that

\sup_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\left(\max_{t\in\left[T_{1},T_{2}\right]}{\rm{dist}}\left(\vec{u}_{m}(t),\mathcal{S}\right)\right)\leq\varepsilon.

Suppose that
\vec{u}_{m}(T_{0})\rightharpoonup\vec{u}_{0}
weakly in
\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2}
. Then the solution
\vec{u}(t)
of (
1.1
) with the initial data
\vec{u}(T_{1})=\vec{u}_{0}
is well-defined on
[T_{1},T_{2}]
and

\vec{u}_{m}(t)\rightharpoonup\vec{u}(t),\quad\mbox{weakly in}\ \dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2}\quad\mbox{for all}\ t\in[T_{1},T_{2}].

Proof.

The proof relies on an argument based on the result of profile decomposition stated in
[
18
, Proposition 2.8]
. See more details in
[
23
, Appendix A.2]
and
[
24
, Appendix]
.
∎

We are in a position to complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We consider the sequence of solutions
(\vec{u}_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
given by Proposition
4.2
.
On the time interval
[T_{0},S_{m}]
, the solution
\vec{u}_{m}
is well-defined. Moreover, for
T_{0}
large enough, we have

\max_{t\in\left[T_{0},S_{m}\right]}\|\vec{u}_{m}(t)-\sum_{n=1}^{N}\vec{Q}_{n}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq Ct^{-2}\leq\varepsilon,

where
\varepsilon>0
is the constant of Proposition
4.8
.

Since the sequence
(\vec{u}_{m}(T_{0}))_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded in
\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2}
, up to the extraction of a subsequence, there exists
\vec{u}_{0}\in\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2}
such that
\vec{u}_{m}(T_{0})\rightharpoonup\vec{u}_{0}
weakly in
\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2}
. Fix
S_{M}=M^{2}>T_{0}
for
M\in\mathbb{N}
. Applied the Proposition
4.8
to the following compact set

\mathcal{S}_{M}=\left\{\sum_{n=1}^{N}\vec{Q}_{n}(t)\in\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2},t\in[T_{0},S_{M}]\right\},

we have, the solution
\vec{u}(t)
of (
1.1
) with the initial data
\vec{u}(T_{0})=\vec{u}_{0}
is well-defined on
[T_{0},S_{M}]
and
\vec{u}_{m}(t)\rightharpoonup\vec{u}(t)
weakly in
\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2}
for all
t\in[T_{0},S_{M}]
. From (
4.3
) and the properties of weak convergence, we have

\max_{t\in\left[T_{0},S_{M}\right]}\left(\|\vec{u}(t)-\sum_{n=1}^{N}\vec{Q}_{n}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}}\right)\leq Ct^{-2}.

Since
S_{M}=M^{2}>T_{0}
is arbitrary, the solution
\vec{u}(t)
is well-defined and satisfies the conclusion of Theorem
1.1
on
[T_{0},\infty)
. The proof of Theorem
1.1
is complete.
∎

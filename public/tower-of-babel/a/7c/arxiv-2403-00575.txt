Equivariant Spectral Flow for Families of Dirac-type Operators
Peter Hochs, Aquerman Yanes

Abstract
In the setting of a proper, cocompact action by a locally compact, unimodular group G G on a Riemannian manifold, we construct equivariant spectral flow of paths of Dirac-type operators. This takes values in the K K -theory of the group C ∗ C^{*} -algebra of G G . In the case where G G is the fundamental group of a compact manifold, the summation map maps equivariant spectral flow on the universal cover to classical spectral flow on the base manifold. We obtain “index equals spectral flow” results. In the setting of a smooth path of G G -invariant Riemannian metrics on a G G -spin manifold, we show that the equivariant spectral flow of the corresponding path of spin Dirac operators relates delocalised η \eta -invariants and ρ \rho -invariants for different positive scalar curvature metrics to each other.



1
Introduction

Let
M
be a compact Riemannian manifold and
E\to M
a Hermitian vector bundle equipped with a Dirac-type operator
D\colon\Gamma^{\infty}_{c}(E)\to L^{2}(M,E)
. It is well-known that
D
is an essentially self-adjoint operator and that it satisfies a Lichnerowicz-Weitzenböck formula

D^{2}=\Delta+\kappa,

where
\Delta
is a Laplace-type operator and
\kappa
is a self-adjoint endomorphism of
E
built out of the curvature. Since
D
is an elliptic operator on a compact manifold, it is Fredholm, and hence it has an index

\Index(D)\in\mathbb{Z}.

It is well-known that there exists a topological formula one can use in order to compute this index
[
AS63
]
, and that it vanishes when the dimension of
M
is odd. One may consider, instead, in the odd-dimensional case, a suitably continuous family of Dirac operators
\{D_{t}\colon\Gamma^{\infty}(E)\to L^{2}(M,E)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
, parametrised by the real line. The so-called Dirac–Schrödinger operator
-i\partial_{x}-iD_{\bullet}
on the Hilbert space
L^{2}(M\times\mathbb{R},E\boxtimes\mathbb{C})
, which is an elliptic differential operator on the (even dimensional) manifold
M\times\mathbb{R}
, can then be considered. If the family
\{D_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is suitably invertible outside a compact interval
K\subset\mathbb{R}
, then
-i\partial_{x}-iD_{\bullet}
is Fredholm (see eg.:
[
GL80
,
Ang93
,
RS95
]
).

It is possible to associate an integer, which we call
classical spectral flow
, to the family
\{D_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
, which represents the net number of negative eigenvalues of
D_{t}
changing sign as
t
varies through
\mathbb{R}
(see e.g.:
[
APS76
,
RS95
,
Phi96
,
Wah07
]
). We denote this number by

\Sf(\{D_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}})\in\mathbb{Z}.

It turns out that the Fredholm index of the Dirac–Schrödinger operator
-i\partial_{x}-iD_{\bullet}
on
L^{2}(M\times\mathbb{R},E\boxtimes\mathbb{C})
coincides with the classical spectral flow of the family
\{D_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}

[
RS95
,
Wah07
,
AW11a
]
, that is,

\Index(-i\partial_{x}-iD_{\bullet})=\Sf(\{D_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}})\in\mathbb{Z}.

(1.1)

In the context of
KK
-theory, equation (
1.1
) can be generalised as follows: let
\{D_{x}\}_{x\in X}
be a family of Dirac-type operators parametrised by a connected, complete Riemannian manifold
X
, and let
P_{X}
be a symmetric, elliptic, first-order differential operator on a Hermitian vector bundle
F\to X
. If
P_{X}
has bounded propagation speed, then it is well-known that it defines a class in
K
-homology

[P_{X}]\in KK^{1}(C_{0}(X),\mathbb{C}).

The family
\{D_{x}\}_{x\in X}
defines a regular self-adjoint and Fredholm operator
D_{\bullet}
on
C_{0}(\mathbb{R},L^{2}(M,E))
, which in turn defines a class

\SF(D_{\bullet})\in KK^{1}(\mathbb{C},C_{0}(X)),

called
KK
-theoretic spectral flow

[
KL13
,
vdD19
]
. Under suitable conditions, the operator
P_{X}-iD_{\bullet}
on
L^{2}(M,E)\otimes L^{2}(X,F)
can be proven to be Fredholm, and its index given by

\Index(P_{X}-iD_{\bullet})=\SF(D_{\bullet})\otimes_{C_{0}(X)}[P_{X}]\in KK^{0}(\mathbb{C},\mathbb{C})\simeq\mathbb{Z},

(1.2)

so that the right-hand of (
1.2
) side can be perceived as a
KK
-theoretic approach to defining spectral flow.

We note here that these constructions are dependent on the fact that the manifold
M
is
compact
. In this paper, we consider a possibly non-compact Riemannian manifold
M
equipped with a proper, isometric and cocompact action by a unimodular, locally compact group
G
. This setup allows us to define an equivariant version of spectral flow for a family of
G
-equivariant Dirac-type operators on
M
, living in the
K
-theory of the (maximal or reduced) group
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}(G)
(see Definition
3.1.5
).
In our construction, we build on the
KK
-theoretic framework of spectral flow in the general context of operators on Hilbert
C^{*}
-modules
[
vdD19
,
KL13
]
. We also adapt and apply techniques from
[
Guo21
]
related to Hilbert
C^{*}
-modules associated to proper group actions.

Other notions of equivariant spectral flow were constructed and applied in
[
IJW21
,
LW22
]
for compact groups,
[
LP03
]
for fundamental groups of compact manifolds acting on their universal covers, and
[
Fan05
]
for families of equivariant Toeplitz operators. A higher version of spectral flow for families of operators was constructed in
[
DZ96
,
DZ98
]
. This notion was employed in
[
Liu21
]
in order to define an equivariant notion of spectral flow in the context of actions by compact Lie groups. A von Neumann-algebraic version of spectral flow was studied in
[
AW11b
]
. A general definition of noncommutative spectral flow for paths of operators on Hilbert
C^{*}
-modules was considered in
[
Wah07
]
.

This paper is structured as follows: we start in Section 2 by laying out the preliminaries and basic constructions used throughout the paper. In Section 3, we discuss and present all the main results of the paper, and the following sections are devoted to laying out the proofs of these results. More precisely, we show in Section 4 that equivariant spectral flow for a path of Dirac-type operators is well-defined under Assumptions
1
-
3
(Definition
3.1.5
), and that it refines classical spectral flow under an integration map (Proposition
4.12
). In Section 5, we prove our “index equals spectral flow” results (Theorem
3.3.2
and
3.3.7
). Finally, in Section 6 we prove how our equivariant spectral flow is related to the notion of delocalised eta invariants and higher rho invariants (Theorems
3.4.2
and
3.4.4
).

Preliminaries

In this section we collect the main definitions and constructions concerning the Hilbert
C^{*}(G)
-module counterpart of the
L^{2}
-space of compactly supported sections on a

-manifold

, due to Kasparov
[

]
. We follow the work of

]
, where a generalisation of this Hilbert module was made in order to encompass Sobolev-type Hilbert modules.

We also recall the definitions of secondary invariants that we will prove to be related by spectral flow: delocalised
\eta
-invariants and
\rho
-invariants.

Throughout this paper, we consider a Riemannian manifold

and a unimodular, locally compact group

acting properly and isometrically on

. Let

be the Riemannian density on
M
, and fix a Haar measure

for

. Let

be a

-equivariant Hermitian vector bundle, where

acts via isometries between the fibers. We also fix a
G
-equivariant, symmetric, elliptic, first-order differential operator

.

2.1
G
-Sobolev Modules

We denote the
L^{2}
-inner product of two compactly supported smooth section
s_{1},s_{2}\in\Gamma_{c}^{\infty}(E)
by

\langle s_{1},s_{2}\rangle_{L^{2}(E)}:=\int_{M}(s_{1}(m),s_{2}(m))_{E_{m}}\,d\mu(m).

(2.1)

The vector space completion of
\Gamma_{c}^{\infty}(E)
with respect to the norm defined by (
2.1
) is denoted by
L^{2}(E)
. The completion of
\Gamma_{c}^{\infty}(E)
with respect to the graph norm defined by
D
defines a first Sobolev space
W^{1}(E)
. Similarly, for each
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
the graph norm of
D^{k}
defines a
k
-th Sobolev space, which we denote by
W^{k}(E)
.

We use the symbol
C^{*}(G)
in order to denote either one of the following two group

-algebras, which are completions of the space of compactly supported function
C_{c}(G)
: the
maximal group

-algebra
, denoted by
C^{*}_{\rm{max}}(G)
, and the
reduced group
C^{*}
-algebra
, denoted by
C^{*}_{r}(G)
. Their norm are related by the inequalities

\|f\|_{C^{*}_{r}(G))}\leq\|f\|_{C^{*}_{\text{max}}(G)}\leq\|f\|_{L^{1}(G)},

(2.2)

for every
f\in C_{c}(G)
. We write
\|f\|_{C^{*}(G)}
to denote either the maximal or reduced norm, if no confusion arises.

The following construction, due to Kasparov
[
Kas16
]
, is a Hilbert
C^{*}(G)
-module analogue of the Hilbert space
L^{2}(E)
. The group
G
acts naturally on compactly supported smooth sections
s\in\Gamma_{c}^{\infty}(E)
by

g(s)(m):=g(s(g^{-1}m)),

for each
g\in G
and
m\in M
. Let
s_{1},s_{2}\in\Gamma_{c}^{\infty}(E)
and
f\in C_{c}(G)
a compactly supported function, and define

\displaystyle\langle s_{1},s_{2}\rangle_{\ee^{0}(E)}

\displaystyle:=\left(g\mapsto\langle s_{1},g(s_{2})\rangle_{L^{2}(E)}\right)\in C_{c}(G),

(2.3)

\displaystyle(s_{1}\cdot f)

\displaystyle:=\left(m\mapsto\int_{G}g(s_{1})(m)f(g^{-1}m)\,dg\right)\in\Gamma_{c}^{\infty}(E).

Note that the inner product
\langle s_{1},s_{2}\rangle_{\ee^{0}(E)}
is indeed a compactly supported function of
G
, by properness of the action. Define the norm

\|s\|_{\ee^{0}(E)}:=\|\langle s,s\rangle_{\ee^{0}(E)}\|_{C^{*}(G)}^{1/2}.

(2.4)

The vector space completion of
\Gamma_{c}^{\infty}(E)
with respect to the norm (
2.4
) is denoted by
\ee^{0}(E)
. By taking completions, one produces a
C^{*}(G)
-valued inner product and
C^{*}(G)
-action on the right, which turns
\ee^{0}(E)
into a Hilbert
C^{*}(G)
-module. This construction is generalised in
[
Guo21
]
: define for each
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and
s_{1},s_{2}\in\Gamma_{c}^{\infty}(E)
the
C_{c}(G)
-valued inner product

\langle s_{1},s_{2}\rangle_{\ee^{k}(E)}:=\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{k}\langle D^{l}s_{1},D^{l}s_{2}\rangle_{\ee^{0}(E)},

which is a Sobolev-type generalisation of the
\ee^{0}
-inner product (
2.3
). Then, define the norm

\|s\|_{\ee^{k}(E)}:=\|\langle s,s\rangle_{\ee^{k}(E)}\|_{C^{*}(G)}^{1/2}.

(2.5)

The vector space completion of
\Gamma_{c}^{\infty}(E)
with respect to the norm defined in (
2.5
) is called the
k
-th
G
-Sobolev module
defined with respect to
D
, and is denoted by
\ee^{k}(E)
. Positivity of these inner products is proved in
[
Guo21
, Lemma 3.2]
.

The operator
D^{k}
defines a map
D^{k}\colon\Gamma_{c}^{\infty}(E)\to\ee^{0}(E)
, for each
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. Since
D
is
G
-equivariant, we see that
D^{k}
is symmetric with respect to the
\ee^{0}
-inner product (
2.3
), and thus the map
D^{k}\colon\Gamma_{c}^{\infty}(E)\to\ee^{0}(E)
is closable. We denote the closure operator by
\overline{D^{k}}\colon\Dom(\overline{D^{k}})\to\ee^{0}(E)
and note that the graph norm of
\overline{D^{k}}
on
\Dom(\overline{D^{k}})
is equivalent to the norm (
2.5
), and thus
\Dom(\overline{D^{k}})=\ee^{k}(E)
. Hence
\overline{D^{k}}\colon\ee^{k}(E)\to\ee^{0}(E)
is a bounded operator for each
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. We omit the overline notation for the closure operator in what follows, so that given a
G
-equivariant, symmetric, elliptic, first-order differential operator

on a Hermitian
G
-equivariant vector bundle
E\to M
, we may talk about the bounded operator

D^{k}\colon\ee^{k}(E)\to\ee^{0}(E).

(2.6)

Adjointability of (
2.6
) is proved in
[
Guo21
, Proposition 3.8]
. We remark that the operator
D\colon\ee^{1}(E)\to\ee^{0}(E)
, of particular interest for us, is regular self-adjoint by
[
Guo21
, Proposition 5.5]
(see
[
Kas16
, Theorem 5.8]
for a proof in the cocompact case).

2.2
The Fredholm Index

In this subsection, we lay out the notion of
Fredholm operators
on a Hilbert
C^{*}
-module, and recall how to define their index. More details on
K
-theory and (unbounded)
KK
-theory and its role in index theory can be found in the standard reference
[
Bla86
]
. See also
[
vdD19
, §2]
for a concise summary of the necessary concepts.

Let
B
be a
\sigma
-unital
C^{*}
-algebra, and let
\ee
be a countably generated Hilbert
B
-module. An adjointable operator
P\in\mathcal{L}(\ee)
is called
Fredholm
if there exists
S\in\mathcal{L}(\ee)
such that
PS-1,SP-1\in\mathcal{K}(\ee)
, that is, if
P
is invertible modulo compact operators in
\mathcal{L}(\ee)
. We now recall how to define the index of such operators as an element in the
K
-theory group
K_{0}(B)
. Let
\mathcal{H}_{B}
denote the standard
B
-Hilbert module, and let
\mathcal{F}(\ee)
denote the space of adjointable Fredholm operators on a Hilbert
B
-module
\ee
. By Kasparov stabilisation, we have
\ee\oplus\mathcal{H}_{B}\simeq\mathcal{H}_{B}
, which yields an embedding
\mathcal{F}(\ee)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{H}_{B})
via
P\mapsto P\oplus 1
. Let
\pi\colon\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{B})\to\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{B})/\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H}_{B})
denote the projection to the Calkin algebra. Then, for each
P\in\mathcal{F}(\ee)
we get an invertible element
\pi(P\oplus 1)\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{B})/\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H}_{B})
, which defines an odd
K
-theory class
[\pi(P\oplus 1)]\in K_{1}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{B})/\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H}_{B}))
. The short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H}_{B})\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{B})\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{B})/\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H}_{B})\longrightarrow 0

yields a 6-term exact sequence in
K
-theory. Since
K_{0}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{B}))=K_{1}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{B}))=0
, the boundary map

\partial\colon K_{1}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{B})/\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H}_{B}))\to K_{0}(\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H}_{B}))\simeq K_{0}(B)

defines an isomorphism between the
K
-theory groups. The
Fredholm index
of
P\in\mathcal{F}(\ee)
is then defined as

\Index(P):=\partial[\pi(P\oplus 1)]\in K_{0}(B).

(2.7)

Let now
P\colon\Dom(P)\to\ee
be a regular operator on the Hilbert
B
-module
\ee
. A
right parametrix
for
P
is an operator
S_{R}\in\mathcal{L}(\ee)
such that the operator
PS_{R}
is adjointable, and
PS_{R}-1
is compact. A
left parametrix
for
P
is an operator
S_{L}\in\mathcal{L}(\ee)
such that
S_{L}P
is closable,
\overline{S_{L}P}
is adjointable and
\overline{S_{L}P}-1
is compact. A
parametrix
for
P
is an operator
S
which is both a left and right parametrix. The regular operator
P
is called
Fredholm
if it has both a left and right parametrix. Equivalently,
P
is Fredholm if its bounded transform
P(1+P^{*}P)^{-1/2}\in\mathcal{L}(\ee)
is Fredholm (
[
Joa03
, Lemma 2.2]
). The index of a regular Fredholm operator
P\colon\Dom(P)\to\ee
is then defined to be the index (
2.7
) of its bounded transform.

A
normalising function
for a regular self-adjoint operator
P\colon\Dom(P)\to\ee
on the Hilbert
B
-module
\ee
is an odd, non-decreasing smooth function
\chi\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\chi(0)=0
,
\chi^{\prime}(0)>0
,
\lim_{x\to\infty}\chi(x)=1
and
\chi(P)^{2}-1\in\mathcal{K}(\ee)
. If
P
is regular self-adjoint and Fredholm, then
P
admits a normalising function
\chi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, which can be used to define the regular self-adjoint operator
\chi(P)
under functional calculus
[
Wah07
, §2]
. The operator
\chi(P)
then defines a class in
KK
-theory

[P]:=[(\ee,\chi(P),1_{\mathbb{C}})]\in KK^{*}(\mathbb{C},B).

(2.8)

The class (
2.8
) does not depend on the choice of normalising function, by
[
vdD19
, Proposition 2.14]
. If
P
has compact resolvents, then the function
\chi(x)=x(x^{2}+1)^{-1/2}
is a normalising function for
P
, so that the class (
2.8
) corresponds to the bounded transform of the unbounded
(\mathbb{C},B)
-cycle
(\ee,P,1_{\mathbb{C}})
. Finally, if we consider an odd Fredholm operator

P=\begin{pmatrix}0&P_{-}\\
P_{+}&0\end{pmatrix}

on the standard
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded Hilbert module
\mathcal{H}_{B}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{B}
, then under the isomorphism
KK^{0}(\mathbb{C},B)\simeq K_{0}(B)
the class
[P]\in KK^{0}(\mathbb{C},B)
is mapped to
\Index(P_{+})\in K_{0}(B)
(cf.
[
Wah07
, §2.1]
).

3.1
G
-Spectral Flow

Let
M
,
E
and
G
be as at the start of Section
2
.
From now on,
let
X
be a locally compact, paracompact topological space. We consider a family
\{D_{x}\colon\Gamma_{c}^{\infty}(E)\to L^{2}(E)\}_{x\in X}
of
G

.

We make the following three assumptions:

1.

The action of
G

2.

The map
D\colon X\to\mathcal{B}(W^{1}(E),L^{2}(E))

3.

There exists a compact subset
K\subset X
such that

D_{x}^{2}=L_{x}^{*}L_{x}+\kappa_{x},

(3.1)

for every

, where
L_{x}
is a
G
-equivariant differential operator, and
\kappa_{x}\in\text{End}(E)^{G}
is a

4.1
Adjointability and Positivity

In this subsection, we follow
[
Guo21
, §3]
in order to investigate the conditions under which an operator

defines an adjointable operator in
\mathcal{L}(\ee^{i}(E),\ee^{j}(E))
. We also relate fiberwise positivity of
G
-equivariant endomorphisms with positivity on the module
\ee^{0}(E)
.

We shall need the following lemma:

Lemma 4.1.1

(
[
Guo21
, Lemma 3.7]
)
.

Suppose that
M/G
is compact. Let
A\in\mathcal{B}(W^{i}(E))
be a
L^{2}
-positive operator with compactly supported distributional kernel. Then the element

\left\langle\left(\int_{G}g(A)\,dg\right)(s),s\right\rangle_{\ee^{i}(E)}\in C^{*}(G)_{+}

is positive in
C^{*}(G)
for every
s\in\Gamma_{c}^{\infty}(E)
.

Proposition
4.1.2
below is a slighted modified version of
[
Guo21
, Proposition 3.5]
, which still holds true if the locality assumption is replaced by the weaker condition of
A
having a properly supported Schwartz kernel. In that case, the constant
C_{\mathfrak{c}}
in (
4.1
) depends on the support of
\mathfrak{c}^{2}A^{*}A+A^{*}A\mathfrak{c}^{2}
, instead of only on the choice of cutoff function
\mathfrak{c}
.

Proposition 4.1.2
.

Suppose that
M/G
is compact. Let
A\colon\Gamma_{c}^{\infty}(E)\to\Gamma_{c}^{\infty}(E)
be a
G
-equivariant, local operator which defines a bounded operator in
\mathcal{B}(W^{i}(E),W^{j}(E))
. Then
A
defines an adjointable operator in
\mathcal{L}(\ee^{i}(E),\ee^{j}(E))
, and

\|A\|_{\mathcal{L}(\ee^{i}(E),\ee^{j}(E))}\leq C_{\mathfrak{c}}\cdot\|A\|_{\mathcal{B}(W^{i}(E),W^{j}(E))},

(4.1)

where the constant
C_{\mathfrak{c}}
depends only on the choice of cutoff function
\mathfrak{c}
.

Proof.

Let
A^{*}\in\mathcal{B}(W^{j}(E),W^{i}(E))
denote the adjoint of
A
. The operator
A_{1}:=(\mathfrak{c}^{2}A^{*}A+A^{*}A\mathfrak{c}^{2})/2
is self-adjoint, has compactly supported distributional kernel and is bounded on
W^{i}(E)
by
\|A\|^{2}\cdot\|\mathfrak{c}^{2}\|_{\infty}
, where
\|A\|
denotes the operator norm of
A\colon W^{i}(E)\to W^{j}(E)
. Let now
\mathfrak{c}_{1}
be a non-negative, compactly supported function on
M
which is identically equal to
1
on the support of
\mathfrak{c}
. Then the operator
A_{2}:=\mathfrak{c}_{1}^{2}\|A\|^{2}\|f^{2}\|_{\infty}-A_{1}
is positive, bounded and has compactly supported distributional kernel (this is where we use the locality assumption). The proof then follows just like in
[
Guo21
]
: apply Lemma
4.1.1
to the operator
A_{2}
, so that, for every
s\in\Gamma_{c}^{\infty}(E)
,

\left\langle s,\left(\int_{G}g(A_{2})\,dg\right)s\right\rangle_{\ee^{i}(E)}=\left(\int_{G}g(\mathfrak{c}_{1}^{2})\|A\|^{2}\|\mathfrak{c}^{2}\|_{\infty}\,dg\right)\langle s,s\rangle_{\ee^{i}(E)}-\langle s,A^{*}A(s)\rangle_{\ee^{i}(E)},

is positive in
C^{*}(G)
, where we used the fact that
\int_{G}g(A_{1})\,dg=A^{*}A
. It follows that

\langle A(s),A(s)\rangle_{\ee^{j}(E)}=\langle s,A^{*}A(s)\rangle_{\ee^{i}(E)}\leq C\cdot\|A\|^{2}\cdot\|\mathfrak{c}^{2}\|_{\infty}\cdot\langle s,s\rangle_{\ee^{i}(E)},

in
C^{*}(G)
, where
C:=\int_{G}g(\mathfrak{c}_{1}^{2})\,dg
. Consequently,
A
extends to an operator in
\mathcal{L}(\ee^{i}(E),\ee^{j}(E))
, since by a similar argument one can check that
A^{*}\colon W^{j}(E)\to W^{i}(E)
defines a bounded adjoint for
A
of
\mathcal{L}(\ee^{j}(E),\ee^{i}(E))
.
∎

As a corollary, if
M/G
is compact, then the operator
D^{k}
defines an element of
\mathcal{L}(\ee^{k}(E),\ee^{0}(E))
for every
k\geq 0
. This result can be extended to non-cocompact actions. We have:

Proposition 4.1.3

(
[
Guo21
, Proposition 3.8]
)
.

For all
k\geq 0
, the operator
D^{k}
defines an element of
\mathcal{L}(\ee^{j+k}(E),\ee^{j}(E))
.

We finish this subsection with a proof of the following fact about positivity of
G
-equivariant endomorphisms on
\ee^{0}(E)
:

Lemma 4.1.4
.

Let
A\in\text{End}(E)^{G}
be a
G
-equivariant endomorphism and suppose that
A\geq c>0
fiberwise. Then
A\geq c>0
on
\ee^{0}(E)
, that is,

\langle(A-c)s,s\rangle_{\ee^{0}(E)},

(4.2)

for every
s\in\Gamma_{c}^{\infty}(E)
.

Proof.

Let
B\in End(E)^{G}
be the fiberwise positive square-root of
A-c\in\text{End}(E)^{G}
. It follows that

\begin{split}\langle(A-c)s,s\rangle_{\ee^{0}(E)}(g)&=\int_{M}\langle((A-c)(m))s(m),(g(s))(m)\rangle_{E_{m}}\,d\mu(m)\\
&=\int_{M}\langle B(m)s(m),(g(Bs))(m)\rangle_{E_{m}}\,d\mu(m)\\
&=\langle Bs,Bs\rangle_{\ee^{0}(E)}(g),\end{split}

for each
s\in\Gamma_{c}^{\infty}(E)
and

, where we used

-equivariance of

on

∎

4.2

The analogue of the non-compact Rellich Lemma on Sobolev
G
-modules can be stated as follows:

Proposition 4.2.1
.

[
Guo21
, Theorem 3.12]

Let

be a cocompactly supported

\ee^{s}(E)\to\ee^{t}(E)

Suppose that

\ee^{s}(E)\hookrightarrow\ee^{t}(E)
is compact if

.

One of the consequences of the elliptic estimate (or Gårding’s inequality) on a compact manifold

is that any two elliptic differential operators define equivalent graph norms as maps

. The following results show that a similar statement can be derived on
G
-Sobolev modules, in the cocompact case. In what follows, we write
\widehat{W^{1}}(E)
and
\widehat{\ee^{1}}(E)
to denote, respectively, the first Sobolev space and first

-Sobolev module defined with respect to the operator
\widehat{D}
.

Proposition 4.2.3

(Equivariant Gårding Inequality)
.

Suppose that
M/G
is compact.
Let
\widehat{D}
be another
G
-equivariant, symmetric, elliptic, first-order differential operator. Then there exist numbers
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that

C_{1}\|s\|_{\widehat{\ee^{1}}(E)}\leq\|s\|_{\ee^{1}(E)}\leq C_{2}\|s\|_{\widehat{\ee^{1}}(E)},

for every
s\in\Gamma_{c}^{\infty}(E)
.

Proof.

Since
M/G
is compact, and since
D
and
\widehat{D}
are
G
-equivariant, by the elliptic estimate we know that
W^{1}(E)=\widehat{W^{1}}(E)
. It follows that
\widehat{D}
defines a bounded operator in
\mathcal{B}(W^{1}(E),L^{2}(E))
, and so the operator
\widehat{D}+i
is bounded

. By Proposition
4.1.2
, the operator
\widehat{D}+i
is in
\mathcal{L}(\ee^{1}(E),\ee^{0}(E))
, and there exists a constant
C_{\mathfrak{c}}
such that

\|\widehat{D}+i\|_{\mathcal{L}(\ee^{1}(E),\ee^{0}(E))}\leq C_{\mathfrak{c}}\|\widehat{D}+i\|_{\mathcal{B}(W^{1}(E),L^{2}(E))}.

(4.3)

It thus follows that, for every compactly supported smooth section
s\in\Gamma_{c}^{\infty}(E)
,

\begin{split}\|s\|_{\widehat{\ee^{1}}(E)}&=\|(\widehat{D}+i)s\|_{\ee^{0}(E)}\\
&\leq\|\widehat{D}+i\|_{\mathcal{L}(\ee^{1}(E),\ee^{0}(E))}\|s\|_{\ee^{1}(E)}\\
&\leq C_{\mathfrak{c}}\|\widehat{D}+i\|_{\mathcal{B}(W^{1}(E),L^{2}(E))}\|s\|_{\ee^{1}(E)},\end{split}

(4.4)

which proves the first inequality. The other one follows similarly.
∎

Corollary 4.2.4
.

Suppose that
M/G
is compact.
Let
\widehat{D}
be another
G
-equivariant, symmetric, elliptic, first-order differential operator. Then the
G
-Sobolev modules
\ee^{1}(E)
and
\widehat{\ee^{1}}(E)
, defined respectively by

, are the same.

.

Lemma 4.4.1
.

For every

(4.7)

Proof.

We calculate directly that

\begin{split}\langle\mathcal{F}_{0}(s_{1}),\mathcal{F}_{0}(s_{2})\rangle_{L^{2}_{T}(E)^{G}}&=\int_{M}\mathfrak{c}(m)^{2}\int_{G}\int_{G}\langle g_{1}(s_{1})(m),g_{2}(s_{2})(m)\rangle_{E_{m}}\,dg_{1}\,dg_{2}\,d\mu(m)\\

&=\int_{M}\mathfrak{c}(m)^{2}\int_{G}\int_{G}\langle(s_{1}(g_{1}^{-1}m),h(s_{2})(g_{1}^{-1}m)\rangle_{E_{g_{1}^{-1}m}}\,dg_{1}\,dh\,d\mu(m),\end{split}

(4.8)

where

\begin{split}\int_{M}\int_{G}\langle(s_{1}(m),h(s_{2})(m)\rangle_{E_{m}}\,dh\,d\mu(m)&=\int_{G}\langle s_{1},h(s_{2})\rangle_{L^{2}}\,dh\\
&=\langle s_{1},s_{2}\rangle_{\phi}\end{split}

where we used the fact that

is
G
-invariant and that
G
is unimodular.
∎

4.4.1

Lemma 4.4.2
.

For every

and every

Proof.

We first prove the claim for compactly supported smooth sections

m\in M

is supported in a coordinate chart
(U;x^{1},\ldots,x^{n})

\begin{split}\left(\partial_{i}\int_{G}g(s)\,dg\right)(m)&=\left(\chi\partial_{i}\int_{G}g(s)\,dg\right)(m)\\

By properness of the action we see that the map

is a linear map, it follows from (

) that

(dD(x)(h)s_{1})|_{U}=0,

for every

and

, which proves the locality condition. The fact that

is

-equivariant is similarly obtained by calculating that

\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{\left|\left\langle\left(g(dD(x)(h))-dD(x)(h))s_{1},s_{2}\right)\right\rangle_{L^{2}(E)}\right|}{\|h\|_{T_{x}X}}=0

for every
x\in X
and
s_{1},s_{2}\in\Gamma_{c}^{\infty}(E)
, which follows by
G
-equivariance of
D_{x}
. The fact that
dD(x)(h)
maps smooth sections to smooth sections is a consequence of its dependence on the local coefficients of
D_{x}
, which are smooth.
∎

Lemma 5.2.2
.

For each
x\in X
, the weak derivative

extends to an adjointable operator

. Moreover,

\displaystyle\sup_{y\in X}\|dD(y)\|_{\mathcal{L}(\ee^{1}(E),\ee^{0}(E)\otimes T_{x}^{*}X)}<\infty.

(5.4)

Proof.

By Lemma

we see we can apply Proposition

in order to conclude that

∎

Lemma 5.2.3
.

For each pair of sections
s_{1}
and
s_{2}\in\Gamma_{c}^{\infty}(E)
, the map
x\mapsto\langle D_{x}s_{1},s_{2}\rangle_{\ee^{0}(E)}
is differentiable.

Proof.

Fix
x\in X
and let
dD(x)\colon\Gamma_{c}^{\infty}(E)\to\Gamma_{c}^{\infty}(E)\otimes T_{x}^{*}X
be as in Lemma
5.2.1
. Note that by (
2.2
), it is enough to prove that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{G}\frac{\left|\left\langle\left(D_{x+h/n}-D_{x}-dD(x)(h/n)\right)s_{1},g(s_{2})\right\rangle_{L^{2}(E)}\right|}{\|h/n\|_{T_{x}X}}\,dg=0,

(5.5)

for every
h\in T_{x}^{*}X
. Let
f_{n}\colon G\to\mathbb{R}
be defined as the integrand

f_{n}(g):=\frac{\left|\left\langle\left(D_{x+h/n}-D_{x}-dD(x)(h/n)\right)s_{1},g(s_{2})\right\rangle_{L^{2}(E)}\right|}{\|h/n\|_{T_{x}X}}.

By hypothesis, we have that
f_{n}\to 0
pointwise. The set

K_{0}:=\{g\in G:\text{supp}(s_{1})\cap\text{supp}(g(s_{2}))\neq\emptyset\}

is compact by properness of the action, and the functions
f_{n}
are supported in
K_{0}
, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, since the operator
D_{x}-D_{x+h}-dD(x)(h/n)
is local by Lemma
5.2.1
. We can see from (

) that

\left|\left\langle\left(D_{x+h/n}-D_{x}\right)s_{1},g(s_{2})\right\rangle_{L^{2}(E)}\right|\leq\displaystyle\sup_{y\in X}\|dD(y)\|\cdot\|s_{1}\|_{W^{1}(E)}\cdot\|s_{2}\|_{L^{2}(E)}\cdot\|h/n\|_{T_{x}X},

(5.6)

where we use the fact that
G
acts by isometries, along with the fact that

\begin{split}\text{dist}(x,x+h/n)&=\text{dist}(x,\exp_{x}(h/n))\\
&=\|h/n\|_{T_{x}X}.\end{split}

The right-hand side of (

) is finite, since the number
\sup_{y\in X}\|dD(y)\|
is finite by assumption. We can estimate

\begin{split}f_{n}(g)&\leq\frac{\left|\left\langle\left(D_{x+h/n}-D_{x}\right)s_{1},g(s_{2})\right\rangle_{L^{2}(E)}\right|+\left|\left\langle dD(x)(h/n)s_{1},g(s_{2})\right\rangle_{L^{2}(E)}\right|}{\|h/n\|_{T_{x}X}}\\
&\leq 2\cdot\displaystyle\sup_{y\in X}\|dD(y)\|\cdot\|s_{1}\|_{W^{1}(E)}\cdot\|s_{2}\|_{L^{2}(E)}\end{split}

for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
g\in G
. Set

V:=2\cdot\displaystyle\sup_{y\in X}\|dD(y)\|\cdot\|s_{1}\|_{W^{1}(E)}\cdot\|s_{2}\|_{L^{2}(E)}

and let

be a compactly supported continuous function such that

. It follows that

is an integrable function on
G
such that
|f_{n}(g)|\leq l(g)
, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
g\in G
. Then, (
5.5
) follows from the fact that

pointwise and the Dominated Convergence Theorem.
∎

6.1
The Localised

-Index

The operator

is said to be

-invertible at infinity
if there exists a non-negative, smooth,

-invariant and cocompactly supported function

on

is invertible, and the inverse satisfies

. Following
[
Guo21
, §4]
, given an operator

which is

-invertible at infinity, one may define the operator
F:=P(P^{2}+f)^{-1/2}
on
\ee^{0}(S)
, which is given by the integral formula
[

, Proposition 4.5]

F=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}P(P^{2}+f+\lambda^{2})^{-1}\,d\lambda.

(6.1)

One then shows that

, Proposition 4.13]
. The short exact sequence

-theory, where we denote the relevant boundary maps by

(6.2)

We call (

-index
of

The operator

can be shown to be self-adjoint modulo compacts, so that the triple

-cycle, and hence a class

[(\ee^{0}(S),F,1_{\mathbb{C}})]\in KK^{0}(\mathbb{C},C^{*}(G)).

(6.3)

By
[

, the class (

For the remaining equality, we adapt a proof of the
G
-Callias operator case to our setting, as follows:

Proposition 6.2.3
.

Suppose
P
is
G
-invertible at infinity. Then

\Index_{G}^{\rm{loc}}(P)=\Ind_{G}(P)\in K_{0}(C^{*}_{r}(G)).

Proof.

Following the proof of
[
GHM21a
, Proposition 6.6]
, let
b\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R})
be the function

b(x):=\begin{cases}-1&x\leq 0,\\
x&0\leq x\leq 1,\\
1&x\geq 1,\end{cases}

and, for each
s>0
, let
b_{s}\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R})
be defined by

b_{s}(x):=\frac{x}{(|x|^{1/s}+1)^{s}}.

It is immediate to see that
\lim_{s\to 0}\|b_{s}-b\|_{\infty}=0
. Define the operator

P_{s}:=b_{s/2}(P)

on
\ee^{0}(S)
. We note that

P_{s}=\frac{P}{(P^{2}+f)^{1/2}}(P^{2}+f)^{1/2}\psi_{s/2}(P),

(6.7)

where
f
comes from the definition of
G
-invertibility at infinity, and

\psi_{s}:=\left(x\mapsto\frac{1}{(|x|^{1/s}+1)^{s}}\right)\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}).

The operator
P(P^{2}+f)^{-1/2}
is invertible modulo
\mathcal{K}(\ee^{0}(S))
by
[
Guo21
, Proposition 4.13]
, and thus so is
P_{s}
for each
s>0
. In particular, for
s=1
we see that the operator
P_{1}
defines a class
[P_{1}]\in K_{1}(\mathcal{L}(\ee^{0}(S))/\mathcal{K}(\ee^{0}(S)))
. Since
(P^{2}+f)^{1/2}\psi_{s/2}(P)
is invertible, one can see by (
6.7
) that
\Ind_{G}(P)=\partial[P_{1}]\in K_{0}(C^{*}_{r}(G))
. Now, because
\lim_{s\to 0}\|P_{s}\oplus 1-b(P)\oplus 1\|=0
, it follows that
P_{s}
is a continuous path of operators which are invertible modulo
\mathcal{K}(\ee^{0}(S))
, connecting
b(P)\oplus 1
to
P_{1}
. This homotopy establishes the desired result.
∎

6.3
The Spin Dirac Operator

Proof of Proposition
3.4.1
.

The family (
3.22
) satisfies conditions
1
and
3
and the map

Until the end of this subsection, we consider the setting of Subsection

\ee^{0}(M\times\mathbb{R},(S_{0}\boxtimes\mathbb{C})^{\oplus 2})

with respect to the
\ee^{0}

\widetilde{P}_{D}:=\begin{pmatrix}0&-i\partial_{t}+iD_{\bullet}\\

-i\widetilde{\partial_{t}}:=\begin{pmatrix}0&-i\partial_{t}\\
-i\partial_{t}&0\end{pmatrix},\quad\quad\widetilde{D}_{\bullet}:=\begin{pmatrix}0&iD_{\bullet}\\
-iD_{\bullet}&0\end{pmatrix}.

Because of (
3.18
), we may write

D_{t}^{2}=\Delta_{t}+\kappa_{t},

where
\Delta_{t}:=(\tau_{t}^{-1}\circ(\nabla^{S_{t}})^{*}\nabla^{S_{t}}\circ\tau_{t})

Because the family

\widetilde{P}_{D}^{2}=(-i\widetilde{\partial}_{t})^{2}+\widetilde{D}_{\bullet}^{2}=(-i\widetilde{\partial}_{t})^{2}+\widetilde{\Delta}_{\bullet}+\widetilde{\kappa}_{\bullet},

(6.8)

outside of

0&\Delta_{\bullet}\end{pmatrix},\quad\quad\widetilde{\kappa}_{\bullet}:=\begin{pmatrix}\kappa_{\bullet}&0\\
0&\kappa_{\bullet}\end{pmatrix}.

We note immediately that

is a

\ee^{0}(M\times X,(E\boxtimes F)^{\oplus 2})
, and that
\widetilde{\kappa}_{\bullet}
is a
G
-equivariant endomorphism of
(S_{0}\boxtimes\mathbb{C})^{\oplus 2}
such that there exists a constant
c

\widetilde{\kappa}_{\bullet}\geq c>0
outside of the cocompact set

. This follows since
\widetilde{\kappa}_{t}
is equal to either
\widetilde{\kappa}_{0}
or
\widetilde{\kappa}_{1}
for all

, and by the assumption that
g_{0}
and
g_{1}
have positive scalar curvature. It follows from Proposition
6.1.3
and the discussion above it that

G

\Ind_{G}(\widetilde{P}_{D})\in K_{0}(C^{*}_{r}(G)).

(6.9)

On the level of

[(\ee^{0}(M\times X,(S_{0}\boxtimes\mathbb{C})^{\oplus 2}),\widetilde{P}_{D}(\widetilde{P}_{D}^{2}+f)^{-1/2},1_{\mathbb{C}})]\in KK^{0}(\mathbb{C},C^{*}(G)),

where the function

We would like to compare the Dirac–Schrödinger operator (

. In order to do that, we let

Using the isometries

as in (
3.20
), we can extend them to an isometry

\tau\colon(S_{0}\boxtimes\mathbb{C})^{\oplus 2}\to\widetilde{S}

(6.10)

over
M\times\mathbb{R}
. Indeed, fiberwisely we see that

(S_{0}\boxtimes\mathbb{C})^{\oplus 2}_{(m,t)}=((S_{0})_{m}\times\{t\})^{\oplus 2}\xrightarrow{\tau_{t}\times 1}((S_{t})_{m}\times\{t\})^{\oplus 2}=\widetilde{S}_{(m,t)},

where the first isomorphism is given by (

3.19
). Using the isomorphism
(\ee^{0}(M,S_{0})\otimes L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}))^{\oplus 2}\simeq\ee^{0}(M\times\mathbb{R},(S_{0}\boxtimes\mathbb{C})^{\oplus 2})

\not{D}_{M\times\mathbb{R}}
and
\widetilde{P}_{D}
as operators defined on

\widetilde{P}:=\tau^{-1}\circ\not{D}_{M\times\mathbb{R}}\circ\tau

in what follows.

Lemma 6.3.1
.

The difference

A:=\widetilde{P}-\widetilde{P}_{D}

is a
G
-equivariant, cocompactly supported endomorphism of
(S_{0}\boxtimes\mathbb{C})^{\oplus 2}
.

Proof.

We note that outside of the cocompact set
M\times[0,1]
the two operators coincide, since
\not{D}_{M\times\mathbb{R}}
is of product form therein. In
[
BGM05
, §3]
, an expression for
\not{D}_{M\times\mathbb{R}}
around the submanifold
M_{t}=M\times\{t\}
is computed in terms of the spin Dirac operator
\not{D}_{M_{t}}
on
M_{t}
(see (
3.17
)), and a choice of vector field
\nu
on
M\times\mathbb{R}
such that
\langle\nu,\nu\rangle=1
and
\langle\nu,TM\rangle=0
. Choose
\nu=-\partial_{t}
, and represent the Clifford action by
\nu
on
\widetilde{S}\big|_{M_{t}}=S_{t}\oplus S_{t}
via

c(\nu)=\begin{pmatrix}0&i\\
i&0\end{pmatrix}.

We see by
[
BGM05
, Equation (11)]
that

\not{D}_{M\times\mathbb{R}}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-i\nabla_{\partial_{t}}^{\widetilde{S}}+i\not{D}_{M_{t}}\\
-i\nabla_{\partial_{t}}^{\widetilde{S}}-i\not{D}_{M_{t}}&0\end{pmatrix}+R

around
M_{t}
, where
R\in\text{End}(\widetilde{S})
is an endomorphism depending on the mean curvature. The result follows from noting that the principal symbols of
\widetilde{P}
and
\widetilde{P}_{D}
are the same. Indeed, by picking a smooth function
f\in C^{\infty}(M\times\mathbb{R})
we can immediately calculate that
\tau^{-1}\circ[\not{D}_{M_{t}},f]\circ\tau=[D_{t},f]
and
[\nabla_{\partial_{t}}^{\widetilde{S}},f]=[\partial_{t},f]=f^{\prime}\in C^{\infty}(M\times\mathbb{R})

∎

Lemma 6.3.2
.

The operator
\not{D}_{M\times\mathbb{R}}
is
G
-invertible at infinity and

\Ind_{\Gamma}(\widetilde{P}_{D})=\Ind_{\Gamma}(\not{D}_{M\times\mathbb{R}})\in K_{0}(C^{*}_{r}(G)).

Proof.

By Lichnerowicz’ formula and the methods in Section
6.1
, we see that the operator
\not{D}_{M\times\mathbb{R}}
and, hence,
\widetilde{P}
are
\Gamma
-invertible at infinity. Moreover, we can find a
\Gamma
-invariant, non-negative, smooth, cocompactly supported function
f\in C^{\infty}(M\times\mathbb{R})
as in Lemma
6.1.2
such that both
\Ind_{\Gamma}(\widetilde{P}_{D})
and
\Ind_{\Gamma}(\widetilde{P})
are represented in

, respectively, by the operators
\widetilde{P}_{D}(\widetilde{P}_{D}^{2}+f)^{-1/2}
and
\widetilde{P}(\widetilde{P}^{2}+f)^{-1/2}
. Using Lemma
6.3.1
, we compute

\widetilde{P}_{D}(\widetilde{P}_{D}^{2}+f)^{-1/2}-\widetilde{P}(\widetilde{P}^{2}+f)^{-1/2}=\widetilde{P}_{D}(\widetilde{P}_{D}^{2}+f)^{-1/2}-(\widetilde{P}_{D}+A)((\widetilde{P}_{D}+A)^{2}+f)^{-1/2}\\
=\widetilde{P}_{D}((\widetilde{P}_{D}+f)^{-1/2}-((\widetilde{P}_{D}+A)^{2}+f)^{-1/2})-A((\widetilde{P}_{D}+A)^{2}+f)^{-1/2}.

(6.11)

The term

(\widetilde{P}_{D}+f+\lambda^{2})^{-1}-((\widetilde{P}_{D}+A)^{2}+f+\lambda^{2})^{-1}=\\
(\widetilde{P}_{D}+f+\lambda^{2})^{-1}((\widetilde{P}_{D}+A)^{2}-\widetilde{P}_{D})((\widetilde{P}_{D}+A)^{2}+f+\lambda^{2})^{-1}.

The first term on the right-hand side of (

6.1

6.12

\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\widetilde{P}_{D}(\widetilde{P}_{D}+f+\lambda^{2})^{-1}(\widetilde{P}_{D}A+A\widetilde{P}_{D}+A^{2})((\widetilde{P}_{D}+A)^{2}+f+\lambda^{2})^{-1}\,d\lambda.

(6.13)

The result follows by noting that the three integrands in (

∎

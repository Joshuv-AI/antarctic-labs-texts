Asymptotic stability for a free boundary tumor model with a periodic supply of external nutrients Thanks: Corresponding author: Yaodan Huang Thanks: Keywords: Free boundary problems; Tumor growth; Periodic solution; Nonlinear stability Thanks: 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 35B10, 35B35, 35R35, 92C37
Yaodan Huang

Abstract
. For tumor growth, the morphological instability provides a mechanism for invasion via tumor fingering and fragmentation. This work considers the asymptotic stability of a free boundary tumor model with a periodic supply of external nutrients. The model consists of two elliptic equations describing the concentration of nutrients and the distribution of the internal pressure in the tumor tissue, respectively. The effect of the parameter μ \mu representing a measure of mitosis on the morphological stability are taken into account. It was recently established in [ 25 ] that there exists a critical value μ ∗ \mu_{\ast} such that the unique spherical periodic positive solution is linearly stable for 0 < μ < μ ∗ 0<\mu<\mu_{\ast} and linearly unstable for μ > μ ∗ \mu>\mu_{\ast} . In this paper, we further prove that the spherical periodic positive solution is asymptotically stable for 0 < μ < μ ∗ 0<\mu<\mu_{\ast} for the fully nonlinear problem.

(1.1)

\displaystyle\Delta\sigma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma\ \ \ \ \ \qquad\ \ \ \ \mbox{in}\ \Omega(t),\ \ t>0,

(1.2)

\displaystyle-\Delta p

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu(\sigma-\widetilde{\sigma})\ \ \ \ \ \mbox{in}\ \Omega(t),\ \ t>0,

(1.3)

\displaystyle\sigma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi(t)\ \ \ \qquad\ \ \mbox{on}\ \partial\Omega(t),\ \ t>0,

(1.4)

\displaystyle p

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma\kappa\ \ \ \ \qquad\ \ \ \mbox{on}\ \partial\Omega(t),\ \ t>0,

(1.5)

\displaystyle V_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial p}{\partial\vec{n}}\ \qquad\ \ \ \mbox{on}\ \partial\Omega(t),\ \ t>0.

1.2

\partial\Omega(t)

1.4

c\sigma_{t}=\Delta\sigma-\sigma\qquad\qquad\ \mbox{in}\ \Omega(t),\ \ t>0,

where the positive constant

is the ratio of the nutrient diffusion time scale,
T_{\mbox{diffusion}}\approx 1\mbox{min}
, to the tumor-cell doubling time scale,
T_{\mbox{growth}}\approx 1\mbox{day}
, so that

(see

If the external concentration of nutrients is assumed to be a constant, i.e., the boundary condition (
1.3

\sigma=\overline{\sigma}=\mbox{const.}
, rigorous mathematical analysis including existence and stability theorems have been established. For the fully non-stationary model, Friedman and Reitich
[

(\sigma_{S}(r),p_{S}(r),R_{S})

(\sigma_{S}(r),p_{S}(r),R_{S})
in
[

In the sequel, it was proved in
[

In this paper, we study the quasi-stationary model (

\gamma=1

\sigma_{\ast}(r,t)=\phi(t)\frac{R_{\ast}^{1/2}(t)}{I_{1/2}(R_{\ast}(t))}\frac{I_{1/2}(r)}{r^{1/2}},\ \ \ 0<r<R_{\ast}(t),

(1.7)

and

satisfies

(1.8)

\begin{split}\frac{dR_{\ast}(t)}{dt}=\mu R_{\ast}(t)\Big\{\phi(t)P_{0}(R_{\ast}(t))-\frac{\widetilde{\sigma}}{3}\Big\},\end{split}

where

As was analyzed in
[
34

, for
\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\phi(t)dt>\widetilde{\sigma}
, there exists a unique
T
-periodic positive solution

for the equation (

Let us introduce the concept of linear stability/instability and asymptotic stability of the periodic solution
(\sigma_{\ast}(r,t),p_{\ast}(r,t),R_{\ast}(t))
. By
linear stability/instability
, we mean:

Linearize the system (
1.1
)-(
1.5
) at
(\sigma_{\ast}(r,t),p_{\ast}(r,t),R_{\ast}(t))
by writing

\begin{split}\sigma(r,\theta,\varphi,t)=\sigma_{\ast}(r,t)+\varepsilon w(r,\theta,\varphi,t),\ \ \ \ p(r,\theta,\varphi,t)=p_{\ast}(r,t)+\varepsilon q(r,\theta,\varphi,t),\end{split}

\partial\Omega(t):\ r=R_{\ast}(t)+\varepsilon\rho(\theta,\varphi,t),

and collect only the
\varepsilon
-order terms. Since the original problem is translation invariant, we say that the periodic solution
(\sigma_{\ast}(r,t),p_{\ast}(r,t),R_{\ast}(t))
is linearly stable in the sense that for any initial data
\rho|_{t=0}=\rho_{0}(\theta,\varphi)
and
w|_{t=0}=w_{0}(r,\theta,\varphi)
,

\Big|\rho(\theta,\varphi,t)-\sum_{m=-1}^{1}a_{m}Y_{1,m}(\theta,\varphi)\Big|\leq Ce^{-\delta t},\ \ \ \ t>\overline{t}

for some constants
a_{m}
,
\delta>0
and
\overline{t}>0
. The periodic solution is said to be linearly unstable if it is not linearly stable.

By
asymptotic stability
, we mean the following:

Given any initial data

(1.9)

\begin{split}\partial\Omega(0):\ r=R_{0}(\theta,\varphi)=R_{\ast}(0)+\varepsilon\rho_{0}(\theta,\varphi),\\
\sigma|_{t=0}=\sigma_{0}(r,\theta,\varphi)=\sigma_{\ast}(r,0)+\varepsilon w_{0}(r,\theta,\varphi),\end{split}

if
|\varepsilon|
is sufficiently small, then there exists a solution to (
1.1
)-(
1.5
) for all
t>0
, and

\partial\Omega(t)\ \ \ \mbox{behaves\ like}\ \ \ \partial B_{R_{\ast}(t)}(a)=\big\{x:|x-a|=R_{\ast}(t)\big\},\ \ \ t>\overline{t}

for some center
a
and
\overline{t}>0
.

Set

(1.10)

\mu_{\ast}=\frac{\int_{0}^{T}\frac{6}{R^{3}_{\ast}(t)}dt}{-\frac{\widetilde{\sigma}}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\left[R_{\ast}(t)\frac{I_{3}(R_{\ast}(t))}{I_{2}(R_{\ast}(t))}-R_{\ast}(t)\frac{I_{0}(R_{\ast}(t))}{I_{1}(R_{\ast}(t))}+2\right]dt}>0,\ \ \ (\mbox{two-space\ dimension}),

or,

(1.11)

\mu_{\ast}=\frac{\int_{0}^{T}\frac{4}{R^{3}_{\ast}(t)}dt}{\int_{0}^{T}\frac{\widetilde{\sigma}}{3}R^{2}_{\ast}(t)\left[P_{1}(R_{\ast}(t))-P_{2}(R_{\ast}(t))\right]dt}>0,\ \ \ \ (\mbox{three-space\ dimension}).

It was recently established in
[
25
]
that the unique radially symmetric
T
-periodic positive solution
(\sigma_{\ast}(r,t),p_{\ast}(r,t),R_{\ast}(t))
is linearly stable under non-radial perturbations for
0<\mu<\mu_{\ast}
and linearly unstable for
\mu>\mu_{\ast}
in the two-space dimensional case; see
[
22
]
for the similar result in the three-space dimensional case. In this paper, we further study the asymptotic stability for the fully nonlinear problem. Compared with the linearized problem, the
O(\varepsilon^{2})
terms cannot be dropped. On the one hand, we need to find the necessary PDE estimates to estimate the nonlinear error terms. On the other hand, since this system is translation invariant in the coordinate space, the center of the limiting sphere is not known in advance which depends on the perturbation of mode 1. To be specific, the perturbation of mode 1 results in the translation of the origin with its magnitude the same order as the perturbation, as well as the decay behavior in time
t
. This is a challenge for this type of problems. We employ on a fixed point theorem to find the correct translation of the origin which is technical and carried out by Theorem
4.1
.

Throughout the paper,
\mu_{\ast}
is defined by (
1.11
). The main result of this paper is stated as follows (see Theorem
5.1
for more explicit statement).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mu<\mu_{\ast}
, then the unique radially symmetric
T
-periodic solution
(\sigma_{\ast}(r,t),p_{\ast}(r,t),R_{\ast}(t))
of the system (
1.1
)-(
1.5
) is asymptotically stable modulus translation, i.e., there exists a new center
\varepsilon a^{\ast}(\varepsilon)
, where
a^{\ast}(\varepsilon)
is a bounded function of
\varepsilon
, such that
\partial\Omega(t)
behaves like

\partial B_{R_{\ast}(t)}(\varepsilon a^{\ast}(\varepsilon))=\big\{x:|x-\varepsilon a^{\ast}(\varepsilon)|=R_{\ast}(t)\big\},\ \ \ \ \ t>\overline{t}

for some
\overline{t}>0
.

Remark 1.1
.

Note that our method and result is also applicable to the two-space dimensional case.

The structure of the rest of this paper is arranged as follows. In the next section, we collect some preliminaries which are needed in the sequel. In Section 3, we transform the nonlinear free boundary problem (
1.1
)-(
1.5
) into an initial-boundary value problem defined on a ball, which we then rewrite as a inhomogeneous linear system (
4.1
)-(
4.5
) in Section 4.

The method we shall use is a fixed point argument:

(i) For given inhomogeneous terms
(f^{i},b^{j})
, solve the inhomogeneous linear system (
4.1
)-(
4.5
) by using the spherical harmonic expansion;

(ii) define new inhomogeneous terms
(\widetilde{f}^{i},\widetilde{b}^{j})
(see (
4.18
));

(iii) establish the existence of a fixed point for the mapping:

S:(f^{i},b^{j})\rightarrow(\widetilde{f}^{i},\widetilde{b}^{j}).

Furthermore, in Section 4, we establish decay estimates for each mono-mode system obtained from the spherical harmonic expansion. In order to derive decay estimates for mode 1 terms, we need to translate the origin
x=0
to
x=\varepsilon a^{\ast}(\varepsilon)
, which is carried out by Theorem
4.1
. Section 5 aims at showing the mapping

admits a fixed point, thereby completing the proof of the asymptotic stability for the fully nonlinear problem.

For

(2.6)

It follows from (

3.
Transformation

In this section, we transform the nonlinear free boundary problem into a nonlinear perturbation in a spherical region.

Let us assume the solution of the system (
1.1
)-(
1.5
) is of the form

\begin{split}\partial\Omega(t):\ r&=R_{\ast}(t)+\varepsilon\rho(\theta,\varphi,t),\\
\sigma(r,\theta,\varphi,t)&=\sigma_{\ast}(r,t)+\varepsilon w(r,\theta,\varphi,t),\\
p(r,\theta,\varphi,t)&=p_{\ast}(r,t)+\varepsilon q(r,\theta,\varphi,t).\end{split}

Recall
[
23
]
that

\vec{n}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+|\varepsilon\nabla_{\omega}\rho|^{2}/(R_{\ast}(t)+\varepsilon\rho)^{2}}}\Big(\vec{e}_{r}-\frac{\varepsilon}{R_{\ast}(t)+\varepsilon\rho}\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\theta}\vec{e}_{\theta}-\frac{\varepsilon}{(R_{\ast}(t)+\varepsilon\rho)\sin\theta}\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\varphi}\vec{e}_{\varphi}\Big),

and

\nabla=\vec{e}_{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}+\vec{e}_{\theta}\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}+\vec{e}_{\varphi}\frac{1}{r\sin\theta}\frac{\partial}{\partial\varphi}=\vec{e}_{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}+\frac{1}{r}\nabla_{\omega},

where
\nabla_{\omega}=\vec{e}_{\theta}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}+\vec{e}_{\varphi}\frac{1}{\sin\theta}\frac{\partial}{\partial\varphi}
.

The system (
1.1
)-(
1.5
) can then be written in terms of
(w,q,\rho)
as follows:

(3.1)

\Delta w=w\ \ \ \mbox{in}\ \Omega(t),\ t>0,

(3.2)

-\Delta q=\mu w\ \ \ \mbox{in}\ \partial\Omega(t),\ t>0,

(3.3)

\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}=-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big(\frac{\partial p_{\ast}}{\partial\vec{n}}+\varepsilon\frac{\partial q}{\partial\vec{n}}\Big)\sqrt{1+\frac{|\varepsilon\nabla_{\omega}\rho|^{2}}{(R_{\ast}(t)+\varepsilon\rho)^{2}}}-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\frac{dR_{\ast}(t)}{dt}\ \ \mbox{on}\ \partial\Omega(t),\ t>0,

(3.4)

w=-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big[\sigma_{\ast}(R_{\ast}(t)+\varepsilon\rho,t)-\sigma_{\ast}(R_{\ast}(t),t)\Big]\ \ \ \mbox{on}\ \partial\Omega(t),\ t>0,

(3.5)

q=-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\big[p_{\ast}(R_{\ast}(t)+\varepsilon\rho,t)-\kappa\big]\ \ \ \mbox{on}\ \partial\Omega(t),\ t>0.

By the Taylor expansion, one of the right-hand side terms of (
3.3
) can be written in the following way,

(3.6)

\begin{split}\sqrt{1+\frac{|\varepsilon\nabla_{\omega}\rho|^{2}}{(R_{\ast}(t)+\varepsilon\rho)^{2}}}\frac{\partial p_{\ast}}{\partial\vec{n}}\Big|_{r=R_{\ast}(t)+\varepsilon\rho}&=\sqrt{1+\frac{|\varepsilon\nabla_{\omega}\rho|^{2}}{(R_{\ast}(t)+\varepsilon\rho)^{2}}}\nabla p_{\ast}|_{r=R_{\ast}(t)+\varepsilon\rho}\cdot\vec{n}\\
&=\frac{\partial p_{\ast}(R_{\ast}(t)+\varepsilon\rho,t)}{\partial r}\\
&=\frac{\partial p_{\ast}(R_{\ast}(t),t)}{\partial r}+\frac{\partial^{2}p_{\ast}(R_{\ast}(t),t)}{\partial r^{2}}\varepsilon\rho+\varepsilon^{2}P_{\varepsilon}.\end{split}

In addition,

(3.7)

\begin{split}\sqrt{1+\frac{|\varepsilon\nabla_{\omega}\rho|^{2}}{(R_{\ast}(t)+\varepsilon\rho)^{2}}}\frac{\partial q}{\partial\vec{n}}\Big|_{r=R_{\ast}(t)+\varepsilon\rho}&=\sqrt{1+\frac{|\varepsilon\nabla_{\omega}\rho|^{2}}{(R_{\ast}(t)+\varepsilon\rho)^{2}}}\nabla q|_{r=R_{\ast}(t)+\varepsilon\rho}\cdot\vec{n}\\
&=\frac{\partial q}{\partial r}\Big|_{r=R_{\ast}(t)+\varepsilon\rho}-\frac{\varepsilon}{(R_{\ast}(t)+\varepsilon\rho)^{2}}\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\theta}\frac{\partial q}{\partial\theta}\Big|_{r=R_{\ast}(t)+\varepsilon\rho}\\
&\quad-\frac{\varepsilon}{(R_{\ast}(t)+\varepsilon\rho)^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\varphi}\frac{\partial q}{\partial\varphi}\Big|_{r=R_{\ast}(t)+\varepsilon\rho}.\end{split}

Again by the Taylor expansion, the right-hand side terms of (
3.4
) and (
3.5
) can be expanded into the following format, respectively,

(3.8)

\sigma_{\ast}(R_{\ast}(t)+\varepsilon\rho,t)-\sigma_{\ast}(R_{\ast}(t),t)=\frac{\partial\sigma_{\ast}}{\partial r}\Big|_{r=R_{\ast}(t)}\varepsilon\rho+\varepsilon^{2}S_{\varepsilon},

and

(3.9)

\begin{split}p_{\ast}(R_{\ast}(t)+\varepsilon\rho,t)-\kappa=&p_{\ast}(R_{\ast}(t),t)+\frac{\partial p_{\ast}}{\partial r}\Big|_{r=R_{\ast}(t)}\varepsilon\rho+\varepsilon^{2}K_{1\varepsilon}\\
&-\frac{1}{R_{\ast}(t)}+\frac{\varepsilon}{R^{2}_{\ast}(t)}\Big(\rho+\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{\omega}\rho\Big)+\varepsilon^{2}K_{2\varepsilon}\\
=&\varepsilon\Big[\frac{1}{R_{\ast}^{2}(t)}\Big(\rho+\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{\omega}\rho\Big)+\frac{\partial p_{\ast}}{\partial r}\Big|_{r=R_{\ast}(t)}\rho\Big]+\varepsilon^{2}K_{\varepsilon},\end{split}

where we have used the fact, see
[
17
, Theorem 8.1]
,

\kappa=\frac{1}{R}-\frac{\varepsilon}{R^{2}}\Big(\rho+\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{\omega}\rho\Big)+\varepsilon^{2}K

with

\Delta_{\omega}\rho=\frac{1}{\sin\theta}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\left(\sin\theta\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\theta}\right)+\frac{1}{\sin^{2}\theta}\frac{\partial^{2}\rho}{\partial\varphi^{2}}.

The Hanzawa transformation is defined by

r=r^{\prime}+\chi(R-r^{\prime})\varepsilon\rho(\theta,\varphi,t),\ \ \ t=t^{\prime},\ \ \ \ \theta=\theta^{\prime},\ \ \ \varphi=\varphi^{\prime},

here

\chi(z)\in C^{\infty},\ \ \ \ \chi(z)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &0,\ \ \mbox{if}\ |z|\geq\frac{3}{4}\delta_{0}\\
&1,\ \ \mbox{if}\ |z|<\frac{1}{4}\delta_{0}\end{aligned}\right.,\ \ \ \ \left|\frac{d^{k}\chi}{dz^{k}}\right|\leq\frac{C}{\delta_{0}^{k}},\ \ \ \ (\delta_{0}\ \mbox{positive\ and\ small}).

Under the Hanzawa transformation and (

)-(

(3.11)

-\Delta^{\prime}q^{\prime}-\mu w^{\prime}=\varepsilon A_{\varepsilon}q^{\prime}\ \ \ \mbox{in}\ B_{R_{\ast}(t)},\ t>0,

(3.12)

\frac{\partial\rho^{\prime}}{\partial t^{\prime}}=-\frac{\partial^{2}p_{\ast}}{\partial r^{\prime 2}}\rho^{\prime}-\frac{\partial q^{\prime}}{\partial r^{\prime}}+\varepsilon B^{1}_{\varepsilon}\ \ \ \mbox{on}\ \partial B_{R_{\ast}(t)},\ t>0,

(3.13)

(3.14)

is the ball with radius

given in

&+\frac{1}{(R_{\ast}(t^{\prime})+\varepsilon\rho^{\prime})^{2}\sin^{2}\theta^{\prime}}\frac{\partial\rho^{\prime}}{\partial\varphi^{\prime}}\frac{\partial q^{\prime}}{\partial\varphi^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\frac{dR_{\ast}(t^{\prime})}{dt^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\frac{\partial^{2}p_{\ast}(R_{\ast}(t^{\prime}),t^{\prime})}{\partial r^{\prime 2}}\rho^{\prime},\end{split}

\begin{split}B^{2}_{\varepsilon}=&-\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\big[\sigma_{\ast}(R_{\ast}(t^{\prime})+\varepsilon\rho^{\prime},t^{\prime})-\sigma_{\ast}(R_{\ast}(t^{\prime}),t^{\prime})\big]+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\frac{\partial\sigma_{\ast}(R_{\ast}(t^{\prime}),t^{\prime})}{\partial r^{\prime}}\rho^{\prime},\end{split}

]
that all terms of

4.
The Inhomogeneous Linear System

In this section, we rewrite (

)-(

) as a inhomogeneous linear system (

)-(
4.5
), and use spherical harmonic expansions to solve this inhomogeneous system. Furthermore, we establish decay estimates for each mono-mode system obtained from the spherical harmonic expansion.

Specifically, we consider the system (

)-(
3.14
) where the
\varepsilon

(4.1)

-\Delta w+w=\varepsilon f^{1}(r,\theta,\varphi,t)\ \ \ \mbox{in}\ B_{R_{\ast}(t)},\ t>0,

(4.2)

(4.3)

\begin{split}\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}=&-\frac{\partial^{2}p_{\ast}}{\partial r^{2}}\Big|_{r=R_{\ast}(t)}\rho-\frac{\partial q}{\partial r}\Big|_{r=R_{\ast}(t)}+\varepsilon b^{1}(\theta,\varphi,t)\ \ \ \ \mbox{on}\ \partial B_{R_{\ast}(t)},\ t>0,\end{split}

w=-\frac{\partial\sigma_{\ast}}{\partial r}\Big|_{r=R_{\ast}(t)}\rho+\varepsilon b^{2}(\theta,\varphi,t)\ \ \ \ \mbox{on}\ \partial B_{R_{\ast}(t)},\ t>0,

\begin{split}q=&-\frac{1}{R_{\ast}^{2}(t)}\Big(\rho+\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{\omega}\rho\Big)-\frac{\partial p_{\ast}}{\partial r}\Big|_{r=R_{\ast}(t)}\rho+\varepsilon b^{3}(\theta,\varphi,t)\ \ \ \mbox{on}\ \partial B_{R_{\ast}(t)},\ t>0,\end{split}

and we add initial conditions (cf. (
1.9
))

(4.6)

w|_{t=0}=w_{0}(r,\theta,\varphi),\ \ \ \quad\ \ \ \rho|_{t=0}=\rho_{0}(\theta,\varphi).

Assume that the functions

,
b^{j}
satisfy the following properties

(4.7)

\sqrt{|\varepsilon|}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{2\delta_{1}t}\|f^{1}(\cdot,t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B_{R_{\ast}(t)})}dt\right)^{1/2}\leq 1,

(4.8)

\sqrt{|\varepsilon|}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{2\delta_{1}t}\|f^{2}(\cdot,t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B_{R_{\ast}(t)})}dt\right)^{1/2}\leq 1,

\sqrt{|\varepsilon|}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{2\delta_{1}t}\|b^{1}(\cdot,t)\|^{2}_{H^{1/2}(\partial B_{R_{\ast}(t)})}dt\right)^{1/2}\leq 1,

(4.10)

\sqrt{|\varepsilon|}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{2\delta_{1}t}\|b^{2}(\cdot,t)\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\partial B_{R_{\ast}(t)})}dt\right)^{1/2}\leq 1,

(4.11)

\sqrt{|\varepsilon|}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{2\delta_{1}t}\|b^{3}(\cdot,t)\|^{2}_{H^{3/2}(\partial B_{R_{\ast}(t)})}dt\right)^{1/2}\leq 1,

where

\sqrt{|\varepsilon|}\|f^{1}\|_{C^{2\alpha/3,\alpha/3}(B_{R_{\ast}(t)}\times[0,\infty))}\leq 1,

(4.13)

\sqrt{|\varepsilon|}\|f^{2}\|_{C^{\alpha,\alpha/3}(B_{R_{\ast}(t)}\times[0,\infty))}\leq 1,

(4.14)

\sqrt{|\varepsilon|}\|b^{1}\|_{C^{1+\alpha,\alpha/3}(\partial B_{R_{\ast}(t)}\times[0,\infty))}\leq 1,

(4.15)

\sqrt{|\varepsilon|}\|b^{2}\|_{C^{2+2\alpha/3,1+\alpha/3}(\partial B_{R_{\ast}(t)}\times[0,\infty))}\leq 1,

(4.16)

\sqrt{|\varepsilon|}\|b^{3}\|_{C^{2+\alpha,\alpha/3}(\partial B_{R_{\ast}(t)}\times[0,\infty))}\leq 1.

For simplicity, we also assume that

\rho_{0}\in C^{4+\alpha}(\overline{B}_{R_{\ast}(0)}),\ \ \ \ \ w_{0}\in C^{2+2\alpha/3}(\overline{B}_{R_{\ast}(0)}),

and the compatibility condition of order 2 for

is satisfied.

Remark 4.1
.

For given functions
f^{i}
,
b^{j}
in some subset

of a Banach space

, we solve the inhomogeneous linear system (

)-(

), and derive the estimate of

. Then we define the new functions

,

We shall show that the mapping
S:(f^{i},b^{j})\rightarrow(\widetilde{f}^{i},\widetilde{b}^{j})
admits a fixed point which is carried out in Section 5, leading to the asymptotic stability.

Remark 4.2
.

The initial data are supposed to satisfy the conditions of Theorem
2.1
rather than (

). However, in the process of showing that the mapping

and

, with

and

(cf. (
4.17
)).

)-(

\displaystyle w(r,\theta,\varphi,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\sum^{n}_{m=-n}w_{n,m}(r,t)Y_{n,m}(\theta,\varphi),

\displaystyle q(r,\theta,\varphi,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\sum^{n}_{m=-n}q_{n,m}(r,t)Y_{n,m}(\theta,\varphi),

\displaystyle\rho(\theta,\varphi,t)

\displaystyle=

By the relation

(4.25)

-\Delta q_{n,m}(r,t)+\frac{n(n+1)}{r^{2}}q_{n,m}(r,t)=\mu w_{n,m}(r,t)+\varepsilon f^{2}_{n,m}(r,t)\ \ \ \mbox{in}\ B_{R_{\ast}(t)},\ t>0,

(4.26)

\begin{split}\frac{d\rho_{n,m}(t)}{dt}=&-\frac{\partial^{2}p_{\ast}}{\partial r^{2}}\Big|_{r=R_{\ast}(t)}\rho_{n,m}(t)-\frac{\partial q_{n,m}(r,t)}{\partial r}\Big|_{r=R_{\ast}(t)}+\varepsilon b^{1}_{n,m}(t),\ \ \ t>0,\end{split}

(4.27)

w_{n,m}(R_{\ast}(t),t)=-\frac{\partial\sigma_{\ast}}{\partial r}\Big|_{r=R_{\ast}(t)}\rho_{n,m}(t)+\varepsilon b^{2}_{n,m}(t),\ \ \ \ \ t>0,

(4.28)

\begin{split}q_{n,m}(R_{\ast}(t),t)=&-\frac{1}{R_{\ast}^{2}(t)}\left(1-\frac{n(n+1)}{2}\right)\rho_{n,m}(t)\\
&-\frac{\partial p_{\ast}}{\partial r}\Big|_{r=R_{\ast}(t)}\rho_{n,m}(t)+\varepsilon b^{3}_{n,m}(t),\ \ \ t>0,\end{split}

(4.29)

\rho_{n,m}|_{t=0}=\rho_{0,n,m},\ \ \ \ \ w_{n,m}|_{t=0}=w_{0,n,m}(r)\ \ \ \ \mbox{in}\ \ B_{R_{\ast}(0)}.

&+\varepsilon\xi_{1,n,m}(r,t),\end{split}

\xi_{1,n,m}(R_{\ast}(t),t)&=0.\end{split}

Let

\psi(r,t)=q_{n,m}(r,t)+\mu w_{n,m}(r,t),

&-\varepsilon\mu R_{\ast}(t)P_{n}(R_{\ast}(t))b^{2}_{n,m}(t)-\varepsilon\mu\frac{\partial\xi_{1,n,m}}{\partial r}\Big|_{r=R_{\ast}(t)}+\varepsilon\frac{\partial\psi_{1,n,m}}{\partial r}\Big|_{r=R_{\ast}(t)}.\end{split}

\begin{split}\rho_{n,m}(t)=\rho_{0,n,m}e^{\int_{0}^{t}H_{n}(\tau)d\tau}+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}Q_{n,m}(s)e^{\int_{s}^{t}H_{n}(\tau)d\tau}ds,\end{split}

=&\Big\{R_{\ast}(\tau)\big[P_{1}(R_{\ast}(\tau))-P_{n}(R_{\ast}(\tau))\big]-\frac{n-1}{R_{\ast}(\tau)}\Big\}\frac{dR_{\ast}(\tau)}{d\tau}\\
&-\frac{n}{R_{\ast}^{3}(\tau)}\Big(\frac{n(n+1)}{2}-1\Big)+\frac{\mu\widetilde{\sigma}}{3}R_{\ast}^{2}(\tau)\big[P_{1}(R_{\ast}(\tau))-P_{n}(R_{\ast}(\tau))\big],\end{split}

here we have used the fact, by (

\begin{split}Q_{n,m}(s)=&\mu\frac{\partial\xi_{1,n,m}}{\partial r}\Big|_{r=R_{\ast}(s)}-\frac{\partial\psi_{1,n,m}}{\partial r}\Big|_{r=R_{\ast}(s)}\\
&+b_{n,m}^{1}(s)+\mu R_{\ast}(s)P_{n}(R_{\ast}(s))b_{n,m}^{2}(s)-\frac{n}{R_{\ast}(s)}b_{n,m}^{3}(s).\end{split}

Note that
\rho_{M,n,m}\triangleq\rho_{0,n,m}e^{\int_{0}^{t}H_{n}(\tau)d\tau}
is the function

,
q
and
\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}
.

Lemma 4.8
.

If
\delta_{1}
is sufficiently small then

(4.60)

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{2\delta_{1}t}\|w-w_{M}\|^{2}_{H^{1}(B_{R_{\ast}(t)})}dt\leq C|\varepsilon|,

(4.61)

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{2\delta_{1}t}\|q-q_{M}\|^{2}_{H^{2}(B_{R_{\ast}(t)})}dt\leq C|\varepsilon|,

(4.62)

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{2\delta_{1}t}\Big\|\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\rho-\rho_{M})\Big\|^{2}_{H^{1/2}(\partial B_{R_{\ast}(t)})}dt\leq C|\varepsilon|.

Proof.
Observe that
\widetilde{w}\equiv w_{n,m}-w_{M,n,m}
satisfies (
4.24
) and (
4.27
) with
\rho_{n,m}
replaced by
\widetilde{\rho}\equiv\rho_{n,m}-\rho_{M,n,m}
, i.e.,

\begin{split}-\Delta\widetilde{w}+\left(\frac{n(n+1)}{r^{2}}+1\right)\widetilde{w}&=\varepsilon f_{n,m}^{1}(r,t)\qquad\qquad\quad\ \ \ \ \ \mbox{in}\ B_{R_{\ast}(t)},\ t>0,\\
\widetilde{w}(R_{\ast}(t),t)&=-\lambda\widetilde{\rho}(t)+\varepsilon b_{n,m}^{2}(t),\ \qquad\ \ \ t>0,\end{split}

where
\lambda=\frac{\partial\sigma_{\ast}}{\partial r}\Big|_{r=R_{\ast}(t)}
.

Define
V=\widetilde{w}-\widetilde{w}|_{r=R_{\ast}(t)}
, we obtain

\begin{split}-\Delta V+\left(\frac{n(n+1)}{r^{2}}+1\right)V&=\varepsilon f_{n,m}^{1}+\left(\frac{n(n+1)}{r^{2}}+1\right)(\lambda\widetilde{\rho}-\varepsilon b_{n,m}^{2})\ \ \ \mbox{in}\ B_{R_{\ast}(t)},\ t>0,\\
V(R_{\ast}(t),t)&=0,\qquad\qquad\quad\ t>0.\end{split}

Multiplying both sides with
V
and integrating over
B_{R_{\ast}(t)}
, we get

\begin{split}&\int_{B_{R_{\ast}(t)}}|\nabla V|^{2}dx+\int_{B_{R_{\ast}(t)}}\left(\frac{n(n+1)}{r^{2}}+1\right)|V|^{2}dx\\
&=\int_{B_{R_{\ast}(t)}}\varepsilon f_{n,m}^{1}Vdx+\int_{B_{R_{\ast}(t)}}\left(\frac{n(n+1)}{r^{2}}+1\right)\lambda\widetilde{\rho}Vdx-\int_{B_{R_{\ast}(t)}}\left(\frac{n(n+1)}{r^{2}}+1\right)\varepsilon b_{n,m}^{2}Vdx.\end{split}

By Young’s inequality, we further have

\begin{split}&\int_{B_{R_{\ast}(t)}}|\nabla V|^{2}dx+\int_{B_{R_{\ast}(t)}}\left(\frac{n(n+1)}{r^{2}}+1\right)|V|^{2}dx\\
&\leq C|\varepsilon|^{2}\|f_{n,m}^{1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B_{R_{\ast}(t)})}+C(n^{2}+1)|\widetilde{\rho}(t)|^{2}+C(n^{2}+1)|\varepsilon|^{2}|b^{2}_{n,m}(t)|^{2}.\end{split}

Then integrating with
e^{2\delta_{1}t}dt
and using (
4.7
), (
4.59
), (
4.10
), we obtain

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{2\delta_{1}t}\|w-{w}_{M}\|^{2}_{H^{1}(B_{R_{\ast}(t)})}dt\leq&C|\varepsilon|^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{2\delta_{1}t}\|f^{1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B_{R_{\ast}(t)})}dt\\
&+C\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{2\delta_{1}t}\|\rho-\widetilde{\rho}\|^{2}_{H^{7/2}(\partial B_{R_{\ast}(t)})}dt\\
&+C|\varepsilon|^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{2\delta_{1}t}\|b^{2}\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\partial B_{R_{\ast}(t)})}dt\\
\leq&C|\varepsilon|,\end{split}

which completes (
4.60
).

Using (
4.60
) and the elliptic estimates, we derive (
4.61
). It follows from (
4.3
), (
4.59
), (
4.61
) and (
4.9
) that (
4.62
) holds. Therefore, the proof is complete.

\Box

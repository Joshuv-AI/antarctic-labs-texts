Approximation Theory of Wavelet Frame Based Image Restoration
Jian-Feng Cai, Jae Kyu Choi, Jianbin Yang

Abstract
In this paper, we analyze the error estimate of a wavelet frame based image restoration method from degraded and incomplete measurements. We present the error between the underlying original discrete image and the approximate solution which has the minimal ℓ 1 \ell_{1} -norm of the canonical wavelet frame coefficients among all possible solutions. Then we further connect the error estimate for the discrete model to the approximation to the underlying function from which the underlying image comes.

Introduction

In many practical image restoration tasks, observed images or measurements are often incomplete in the sense that features of interest in an image are missing partially and/or corrupted by noise. The corresponding inverse problem is to restore the underlying image
{\boldsymbol{f}}
from its incomplete measurements given by

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{g}}[{\boldsymbol{k}}]=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}({\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}}{\boldsymbol{f}})[{\boldsymbol{k}}]+{\boldsymbol{\eta}}[{\boldsymbol{k}}],&{\boldsymbol{k}}\in{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}},\\
\text{unknown},&{\boldsymbol{k}}\in\boldsymbol{\Omega}\setminus{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}.\end{array}\right.

In

1

is a measurement error which is assumed to be uncorrelated with

, and
{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}}
is some linear operator describing the process of imaging, acquisition, and communication. As convention, we consider discrete images as real-valued functions on a two dimensional regular grid
\boldsymbol{\Omega}\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
, and
{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}
is a subset of
\boldsymbol{\Omega}
where
{\boldsymbol{g}}
is available or reliable. Nevertheless, the discussions on other dimensions will be almost the same with slight modifications. Note that this paper involves both functions and their discrete counterparts. We shall use regular characters to denote functions and use bold-faced characters to denote their discrete analogies. For example, we use
u
as an element in a function space, while we use
\boldsymbol{u}
to denote its corresponding discretized version (the type of discretization will be made clear later).

The measurements could be part of (degraded) images, transmitted data, and sensor data. The restoration of missing data from a given incomplete measurement is an essential part of imaging science area where the final image is utilized for the visual interpretation or for the automatic analysis

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\boldsymbol{\Omega}|}\left\|\boldsymbol{u}^{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}}-{\boldsymbol{f}}\right\|_{\ell_{2}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})}^{2}\leq\frac{2\sigma_{\min}^{-2}({\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}})}{|\boldsymbol{\Omega}|}\left(\left\|{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}}\boldsymbol{u}^{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}}-{\boldsymbol{g}}\right\|_{\ell_{2}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})}^{2}+\left\|{\boldsymbol{g}}-{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}}{\boldsymbol{f}}\right\|_{\ell_{2}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})}^{2}\right)\leq 4\sigma_{\min}^{-2}({\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}})\eta^{2},

In the literature, there are efficient numerical algorithms developed to solve

1.4

,
47
]
. These numerical algorithms aim to find a sparse approximation of the underlying image using a properly chosen wavelet frame transform. Then a sparsity promoting regularization term (such as the widely used
\ell_{1}

in the sense that

is bounded rather than the explicit sparsity of
{\boldsymbol{f}}
and/or

{\boldsymbol{f}}
. Second and most importantly, the “sensing matrix” of

1

{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{R}}}}_{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}}{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}}

{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}
;
{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{R}}}}_{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}}{\boldsymbol{f}}[{\boldsymbol{k}}]={\boldsymbol{f}}[{\boldsymbol{k}}]
for
{\boldsymbol{k}}\in{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}
, and
{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{R}}}}_{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}}{\boldsymbol{f}}[{\boldsymbol{k}}]=0
for
{\boldsymbol{k}}\in\boldsymbol{\Omega}\setminus{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}
. Since

is randomly chosen from all

\boldsymbol{\Omega}

does not satisfy the concentration inequality in

may not satisfy the restricted isometry property condition in general, all of which mean that there may not be information in the measurement

sufficient for the exact restoration
[
18
]

[
29
]
of the hypothesis space, which are widely used in classical empirical process
[
59
]
and statistical learning theory
[
60

As a byproduct of the approximation analysis, we can further connect the approximation property of

2
Approximation error of wavelet frame based image restoration

2.1
Preliminaries of wavelet frames

We begin with a brief introduction on wavelet tight frames. For details, one may consult
[
30
,
31
,
55
]
for theories of frames and wavelet frames,
[
57
]
for a short survey on the theory and applications of frames, and
[
37
,
38
]
for more detailed surveys.

A countable set
\left\{\varphi_{n}:n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\right\}\subseteq L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
with
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
is called a tight frame of
L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
if

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{2}=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left|\left\langle f,\varphi_{n}\right\rangle\right|^{2}~~~~~\text{for all}~~~f\in L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),

(2.1)

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is the inner product on
L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, and
\langle f,\varphi_{n}\rangle
is called the canonical coefficient of
f
.

For given
{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}=\left\{\psi_{1},\cdots,\psi_{r}\right\}\subseteq L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
J\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the corresponding quasi-affine system
{\mathscr{X}}^{J}({\boldsymbol{\Psi}})
generated by
{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}
is defined by the collection of the dilations and the shifts of the elements in
{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}
:

\displaystyle{\mathscr{X}}^{J}({\boldsymbol{\Psi}})=\left\{\psi_{\alpha,l,{\boldsymbol{k}}}:1\leq\alpha\leq r,~l\in{\mathbb{Z}},~{\boldsymbol{k}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\right\}

(2.2)

with
\psi_{\alpha,l,{\boldsymbol{k}}}
being defined as

\displaystyle\psi_{\alpha,l,{\boldsymbol{k}}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}2^{\frac{ld}{2}}\psi_{\alpha}(2^{l}\cdot-{\boldsymbol{k}})&~~l\geq J;\\
2^{\left(l-\frac{J}{2}\right)d}\psi_{\alpha}(2^{l}\cdot-2^{l-J}{\boldsymbol{k}})&~~l<J.\end{array}\right.

When
{\mathscr{X}}^{J}({\boldsymbol{\Psi}})
forms a tight frame of
L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, each
\psi_{1},\cdots,\psi_{r}
is called a (tight) framelet and the entire system
{\mathscr{X}}^{J}({\boldsymbol{\Psi}})
is called a (tight) wavelet frame. In particular when
J=0
, we simply write
{\mathscr{X}}({\boldsymbol{\Psi}})={\mathscr{X}}^{0}({\boldsymbol{\Psi}})
. Note that in the literature, the affine system is widely used, which corresponds to the decimate wavelet (frame) transform. The quasi-affine system, which corresponds to the undecimated wavelet (frame) transformation, was first introduced and analyzed in
[
55
]
. Throughout this paper, we only discuss the quasi-affine system
Section

2.1
because it generally performs better in image restoration than the widely used affine system, and the connection to the underlying function in continuum is more natural
[
15
,
16
,
39
]
. The interested reader can find further details on the affine wavelet frame systems and its connections to the quasi-affine wavelet frame systems in
[
23
,
37
,
55
]
.

The constructions of framelets
{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}
, which are desirably (anti-)symmetric and compactly supported functions, are usually based on a multiresolution analysis (MRA) generated by some refinable function
\phi
with a refinement mask
{\boldsymbol{a}}_{0}
such that

\displaystyle\phi=2^{d}\sum_{{\boldsymbol{k}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}{\boldsymbol{a}}_{0}[{\boldsymbol{k}}]\phi(2\cdot-{\boldsymbol{k}}).

(2.5)

The idea of an MRA based construction of
{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}=\left\{\psi_{1},\cdots,\psi_{r}\right\}\subseteq L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is to find finitely supported masks
{\boldsymbol{a}}_{l}
such that

\displaystyle\psi_{\alpha}=2^{d}\sum_{{\boldsymbol{k}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}{\boldsymbol{a}}_{\alpha}[{\boldsymbol{k}}]\phi(2\cdot-{\boldsymbol{k}})~~~~~\alpha=1,\cdots,r.

(2.6)

The sequences
{\boldsymbol{a}}_{1},\cdots,{\boldsymbol{a}}_{r}
are called wavelet frame mask or the high pass filters of the system, and the refinement mask
{\boldsymbol{a}}_{0}
is also called the low-pass filter.

The unitary extension principle (UEP) of
[
55
]
provides a general theory of the construction of MRA based tight wavelet frames. Briefly speaking, as long as
\left\{{\boldsymbol{a}}_{0},{\boldsymbol{a}}_{1},\cdots,{\boldsymbol{a}}_{r}\right\}
are compactly supported and their Fourier series

\displaystyle\widehat{{\boldsymbol{a}}}_{\alpha}({\boldsymbol{\xi}})=\sum_{{\boldsymbol{k}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}{\boldsymbol{a}}_{\alpha}[{\boldsymbol{k}}]e^{-i{\boldsymbol{\xi}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{k}}},~~~~~~\alpha=0,\cdots,r,~~~{\boldsymbol{\xi}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

satisfy

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha=0}^{r}\left|\widehat{{\boldsymbol{a}}}_{\alpha}({\boldsymbol{\xi}})\right|^{2}=1~~~~\text{and}~~~~\sum_{\alpha=0}^{r}\widehat{{\boldsymbol{a}}}_{\alpha}({\boldsymbol{\xi}})\overline{\widehat{{\boldsymbol{a}}}}_{\alpha}({\boldsymbol{\xi}}+\boldsymbol{\nu})=0

(2.7)

for all
\boldsymbol{\nu}\in\left\{0,\pi\right\}^{d}\setminus\left\{{\mathbf{0}}\right\}
and
{\boldsymbol{\xi}}\in[-\pi,\pi]^{d}
, the quasi-affine system
{\mathscr{X}}({\boldsymbol{\Psi}})
with
{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}=\left\{\psi_{1},\cdots,\psi_{r}\right\}
defined by
Eq.

2.6
forms a tight frame of
L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, and the filters
\left\{{\boldsymbol{a}}_{0},{\boldsymbol{a}}_{1},\cdots,{\boldsymbol{a}}_{r}\right\}
form a discrete tight frame on
\ell_{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})

[
37
]
.

One of the most widely used examples is the piecewise linear B-spline
[
31
]
for
L_{2}({\mathbb{R}})
, which has one refinable function and two framelets with the associated filters

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{a}}_{0}=\frac{1}{4}\big[\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&2&1\end{array}\big],~~~~{\boldsymbol{a}}_{1}=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{4}\big[\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&0&-1\end{array}\big],~~\text{and}~~{\boldsymbol{a}}_{2}=\frac{1}{4}\big[\begin{array}[]{ccc}-1&2&-1\end{array}\big].

Indeed, it can be shown that the above
\left\{{\boldsymbol{a}}_{0},{\boldsymbol{a}}_{1},{\boldsymbol{a}}_{2}\right\}
satisfies
Eq.

2.7
, so that
{\mathscr{X}}({\boldsymbol{\Psi}})
with
{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}=\left\{\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\right\}
defined by
Eq.

2.6
forms a tight frame on
L_{2}({\mathbb{R}})
.

One possible way to construct a tight frame on
L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
is by taking tensor products of univariate tight framelets
[
15
,
16
,
30
,
37
]
. Given a set of univariate masks
\big\{{\boldsymbol{a}}_{0},{\boldsymbol{a}}_{1},\cdots,{\boldsymbol{a}}_{r}\big\}
, we define two dimensional masks
{\boldsymbol{a}}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}[{\boldsymbol{k}}]
with
\boldsymbol{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})
and
{\boldsymbol{k}}=(k_{1},k_{2})
as

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{a}}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}[{\boldsymbol{k}}]={\boldsymbol{a}}_{\alpha_{1}}[k_{1}]{\boldsymbol{a}}_{\alpha_{2}}[k_{2}],~~~~~~0\leq\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\leq r,~~{\boldsymbol{k}}=(k_{1},k_{2})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}

so that the corresponding two dimensional refinable function and framelets are defined as

\displaystyle\psi_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}(\boldsymbol{x})=\psi_{\alpha_{1}}(x_{1})\psi_{\alpha_{2}}(x_{2}),~~~~~0\leq\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\leq r,~~\boldsymbol{x}=(x_{1},x_{2})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}

with
\psi_{0}=\phi
for convenience. If the univariate masks
\left\{{\boldsymbol{a}}_{l}:l=0,\cdots,r\right\}
are constructed from UEP, then it can be verified that
\left\{{\boldsymbol{a}}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}:\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in\{0,\cdots,r\}^{2}\right\}
satisfies
Eq.

2.7
and thus
{\mathscr{X}}({\boldsymbol{\Psi}})
with

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}=\left\{\psi_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}:\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in\{0,\cdots,r\}^{2}\setminus\{{\mathbf{0}}\}\right\}

forms a tight frame for
L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
.

The advantage of framelet is that we can easily derive the discrete tight frame system by framelet decomposition and reconstruction algorithms of
[
31
]
. Throughout this paper, we only consider the MRA based tensor product wavelet frame system on
\ell_{2}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})
with
\boldsymbol{\Omega}=\left\{0,\cdots,N-1\right\}^{2}
for simplicity. For
l\geq 0
we construct filters
{\boldsymbol{a}}_{l,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
by

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{a}}_{l,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}=\widetilde{{\boldsymbol{a}}}_{l,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\ast\widetilde{{\boldsymbol{a}}}_{l-1,{\mathbf{0}}}\ast\cdots\ast\widetilde{{\boldsymbol{a}}}_{0,{\mathbf{0}}}~~\text{with}~~\widetilde{{\boldsymbol{a}}}_{l,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}[{\boldsymbol{k}}]=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}{\boldsymbol{a}}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}[2^{-l}{\boldsymbol{k}}],&{\boldsymbol{k}}\in 2^{l}{\mathbb{Z}}^{2};\\
0,&{\boldsymbol{k}}\notin 2^{l}{\mathbb{Z}}^{2},\end{array}\right.

where
{\boldsymbol{a}}_{0,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}={\boldsymbol{a}}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
, and
\ast
denotes the standard discrete convolution on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
.

Let
\boldsymbol{u}\in\ell_{2}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})
. To define an appropriate discrete framelet transform, or an analysis operator (see, e.g.,
[
37
]
), we will impose the periodic boundary conditions. Throughout this paper, we identify
\ell_{p}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})
with the space of
\ell_{p}
sequences on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
with fundamental period on each variable to be
N
. We introduce the following periodization operator
{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{P}}}}_{J}:\ell_{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{2})\to\ell_{1}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})
:

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{P}}}}_{N}\left(\boldsymbol{u}\right)[{\boldsymbol{k}}]=\sum_{{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}}\boldsymbol{u}[{\boldsymbol{k}}+N{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\prime}]~~~~~~~{\boldsymbol{k}}\in\boldsymbol{\Omega}.

Then for
{\boldsymbol{a}}\in\ell_{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{2})
and
\boldsymbol{u}\in\ell_{2}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})
, we define the convolution
{\boldsymbol{a}}\circledast\boldsymbol{u}
as

\displaystyle\left({\boldsymbol{a}}\circledast\boldsymbol{u}\right)[{\boldsymbol{k}}]=\left\langle{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{P}}}}_{N}({\boldsymbol{a}})[{\boldsymbol{k}}-\cdot],\boldsymbol{u}\right\rangle=\sum_{\boldsymbol{n}\in\boldsymbol{\Omega}}{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{P}}}}_{N}\left({\boldsymbol{a}}\right)[{\boldsymbol{k}}-\boldsymbol{n}]\boldsymbol{u}[\boldsymbol{n}],~~~~~{\boldsymbol{k}}\in\boldsymbol{\Omega}.

(2.10)

Using the filters defined as
Section

2.1
, we define the two dimensional fast (discrete) framelet transform, or the analysis operator (see, e.g.,
[
37
]
) with
L
levels of decomposition as

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}\boldsymbol{u}=\big\{{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}_{l,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\boldsymbol{u}:(l,\boldsymbol{\alpha})\in\big(\left\{0,\cdots,L-1\right\}\times{\mathbb{B}}\big)\cup\left\{(L-1,{\mathbf{0}})\right\}\big\}.

(2.11)

where
{\mathbb{B}}=\left\{0,\cdots,r\right\}^{2}\setminus\left\{{\mathbf{0}}\right\}
is the framelet band. Then
{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}
is a linear operator with the frame coefficients
{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}_{l,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\boldsymbol{u}\in\ell_{2}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})
of
\boldsymbol{u}
at level
l
and band
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
being defined as

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}_{l,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\boldsymbol{u}={\boldsymbol{a}}_{l,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}[-\cdot]\circledast\boldsymbol{u},

where the discrete convolution
\circledast
is defined as
Eq.

2.10
. The synthesis framelet transform is denoted as
{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}^{T}
, the adjoint of
{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}
. Since we use a tight wavelet frame, we have the following perfect reconstruction formula

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{u}={\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}^{T}{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}\boldsymbol{u}

(2.12)

for all
\boldsymbol{u}\in\ell_{2}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})
.

Finally, using this analysis operator
{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}
, we define
\left\|\boldsymbol{\lambda}\cdot{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}\boldsymbol{u}\right\|_{1}
by

\displaystyle\left\|\boldsymbol{\lambda}\cdot{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}\boldsymbol{u}\right\|_{1}=\sum_{{\boldsymbol{k}}\in\boldsymbol{\Omega}}\sum_{l=0}^{L-1}\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in{\mathbb{B}}}\lambda_{l,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}[{\boldsymbol{k}}]\left|\big({\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}_{l,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\boldsymbol{u}\big)[{\boldsymbol{k}}]\right|.

(2.13)

In particular, if
\lambda_{l,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}[{\boldsymbol{k}}]=1
for all
(l,\boldsymbol{\alpha},{\boldsymbol{k}})
, we simply write it as
\left\|{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}\boldsymbol{u}\right\|_{1}
:

\displaystyle\left\|{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}\boldsymbol{u}\right\|_{1}=\sum_{{\boldsymbol{k}}\in\boldsymbol{\Omega}}\sum_{l=0}^{L-1}\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in{\mathbb{B}}}\left|\big({\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}_{l,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\boldsymbol{u}\big)[{\boldsymbol{k}}]\right|.

(2.14)

As we shall see later, the parameter
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
in
Eq.

2.13
is related to the decay of wavelet frame coefficients, thereby to control the regularity of
\boldsymbol{u}
.

2.2
Error analysis of discrete model
Eq.

1.4

Throughout the rest of this paper, we denote
{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}=\{\psi_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}:\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in{\mathbb{B}}\}
as the set of framelets,
\phi
(or
\psi_{{\mathbf{0}}}
) as the corresponding refinable function, and
\{{\boldsymbol{a}}_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}:\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in{\mathbb{B}}\cup\left\{{\mathbf{0}}\right\}\}
as the associated filters. In this paper, we focus on the tensor product B-spline wavelet frame systems constructed by
[
55
]
, and
\phi
is a tensor product B-spline function. We shall refer to
\psi_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
for
\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in{\mathbb{B}}
as the tensor product framelets. Note, however, that the analysis can be generalized to the generic MRA based wavelet frame systems whenever the corresponding refinable function is compactly supported with
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\phi(\boldsymbol{x}){\mathrm{d}}\boldsymbol{x}=1
, Lipschitz continuous, and forms a Riesz basis on the closed shift invariant space
\bigvee\left\{\phi(\cdot-{\boldsymbol{k}}):{\boldsymbol{k}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\right\}

[
18
]
.

Let
\boldsymbol{u}^{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}}
be a solution to
Eq.

1.4
with the weighted
\ell_{1}
-norm of wavelet frame coefficients defined as
Eq.

2.13
. Recall that
\boldsymbol{\Omega}=\left\{0,1,\cdots,N-1\right\}^{2}
and
{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}
is a data set uniformly randomly drawn from all
m
-subsets of
\boldsymbol{\Omega}
. Denote by
\rho=m/|\boldsymbol{\Omega}|
the density of the pixels available. To draw an error between
\boldsymbol{u}^{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}}
and the underlying image
{\boldsymbol{f}}
, we assume that the linear operator
{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}}
, and the parameter
\boldsymbol{\lambda}:=\left\{\lambda_{l,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}[{\boldsymbol{k}}]:l=0,\cdots,L-1,~\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in{\mathbb{B}},~{\boldsymbol{k}}\in\boldsymbol{\Omega}\right\}
satisfy the followings:

A1.

\sigma_{\min}({\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}})>0
, and both
\sigma_{\min}({\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}})
and
\left\|{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}}\right\|_{\infty}
are independent of
|\boldsymbol{\Omega}|
, i.e. independent of
N
.

A2.

\boldsymbol{\lambda}=\left\{\lambda_{l,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}[{\boldsymbol{k}}]:l=0,\cdots,L-1,~\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in{\mathbb{B}},~{\boldsymbol{k}}\in\boldsymbol{\Omega}\right\}
satisfies
\lambda_{l,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}[{\boldsymbol{k}}]=2^{\beta\Upsilon(l,\boldsymbol{\alpha},{\boldsymbol{k}})}
where
\beta\geq-1
and
\Upsilon:\Gamma\to{\mathbb{N}}
is a positive integer-valued funciton such that

\displaystyle\max\left\{\Upsilon(l,\boldsymbol{\alpha},{\boldsymbol{k}}):(l,\boldsymbol{\alpha},{\boldsymbol{k}})\in\Gamma\right\}\leq\frac{1}{2}\log_{2}|\boldsymbol{\Omega}|

(2.15)

with
\Gamma=\left\{(l,\boldsymbol{\alpha},{\boldsymbol{k}}):l=0,\cdots,L-1,~\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in{\mathbb{B}},~{\boldsymbol{k}}\in\boldsymbol{\Omega}\right\}
.

Example 2.1
.

Before we continue, we present some examples related to A1.

1.

Image inpainting: in this case, we have
{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}}={\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{I}}}}
, i.e.
\sigma_{\min}\left({\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{I}}}}\right)=\left\|{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{I}}}}\right\|_{\infty}=1
.

2.

Simultaneous Gaussian deblurring and image inpainting: let
{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}}\boldsymbol{u}={\boldsymbol{a}}\circledast\boldsymbol{u}
where
{\boldsymbol{a}}
is defined as

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{a}}[{\boldsymbol{k}}]=\frac{C}{2\pi\sigma^{2}}\exp\left\{-\frac{\left|{\boldsymbol{k}}\right|^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right\},~~~~~{\boldsymbol{k}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2},

for
\sigma>0
and a normalizing constant
C>0
is such that
\left\|{\boldsymbol{a}}\right\|_{\ell_{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{2})}=1
. Obviously, we have
\left\|{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}}\right\|_{\infty}=\left\|{\boldsymbol{a}}\right\|_{\ell_{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{2})}=1
. In addition, by Poisson summation formula (e.g.
[
53
]
), we have

\displaystyle\widehat{{\boldsymbol{a}}}(\boldsymbol{\omega})=C\sum_{\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}}\exp\left\{-\frac{\sigma^{2}\left|\boldsymbol{\omega}-2\pi\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\prime}\right|^{2}}{2}\right\}\geq C\exp\left\{-\sigma^{2}\pi^{2}\right\},~~~\boldsymbol{\omega}\in[-\pi,\pi]^{2}.

Hence, we can set
\sigma_{\min}\left({\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}}\right)=C\exp\left\{-\sigma^{2}\pi^{2}\right\}>0
, which is independent of
|\boldsymbol{\Omega}|
.

3.

Wavelet inpainting: let
{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}}
be a two dimensional orthonormal wavelet basis transform (
{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}}^{T}{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}}={\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}}{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}}^{T}={\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{I}}}}
) with filter banks
\left\{{\boldsymbol{h}}_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}:\boldsymbol{\beta}\in\left\{0,1\right\}^{2}\right\}
generated by the tensor product of a real-valued univariate conjugate mirror filter
{\boldsymbol{h}}
:

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{h}}_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}[{\boldsymbol{k}}]={\boldsymbol{h}}_{\beta_{1}}[k_{1}]{\boldsymbol{h}}_{\beta_{2}}[k_{2}],~~~~~{\boldsymbol{k}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2},~~~\boldsymbol{\beta}=(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})\in\left\{0,1\right\}^{2}

with
{\boldsymbol{h}}_{0}={\boldsymbol{h}}
and
{\boldsymbol{h}}_{1}[k]=(-1)^{1-k}{\boldsymbol{h}}[1-k]
. Then
\left\|{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}}\right\|_{\infty}=\left\|{\boldsymbol{h}}\right\|_{\ell_{1}({\mathbb{Z}})}^{2}
and
\sigma_{\min}\left({\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}}\right)=\sigma_{\max}\left({\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}}\right)=1
.

Remark 2.2
.

Together with the assumption on
{\boldsymbol{f}}
that
\left\|\boldsymbol{\lambda}\cdot{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}{\boldsymbol{f}}\right\|_{1}<\infty
, A2 is related to the decay of the canonical wavelet frame coefficients
{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}{\boldsymbol{f}}
. Notice that the decay of
{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}{\boldsymbol{f}}
is further related to the regularity of
{\boldsymbol{f}}
, which will be clearer in the context of asymptotic function approximation. See
Section

2.3
for details.

Under the above assumptions, we note that the underlying true image
{\boldsymbol{f}}
satisfies the constraints in
Eq.

1.4
with
\left\|\boldsymbol{\lambda}\cdot{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}{\boldsymbol{f}}\right\|_{1}<\infty
. In addition, since
\boldsymbol{u}^{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}}
is a solution to
Eq.

1.4
, its weighted
\ell_{1}
-norm
\left\|\boldsymbol{\lambda}\cdot{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}\boldsymbol{u}^{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}}\right\|_{1}
attains the minimum subject to the constraints, and in particular,
\left\|\boldsymbol{\lambda}\cdot{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}\boldsymbol{u}^{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}}\right\|_{1}\leq\left\|\boldsymbol{\lambda}\cdot{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}{\boldsymbol{f}}\right\|_{1}
. This leads us to consider the following set

\displaystyle{\mathscr{M}}=\left\{\boldsymbol{u}\in\ell_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\Omega}):\left\|\boldsymbol{\lambda}\cdot{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}\boldsymbol{u}\right\|_{1}\leq\left\|\boldsymbol{\lambda}\cdot{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}{\boldsymbol{f}}\right\|_{1},~\frac{1}{|{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}|}\sum_{{\boldsymbol{k}}\in{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}}\left|({\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}}\boldsymbol{u})[{\boldsymbol{k}}]-{\boldsymbol{g}}[{\boldsymbol{k}}]\right|^{2}\leq\eta^{2},~\boldsymbol{u}\in[0,M]^{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}\right\}

(2.16)

as an involved hypothesis space. In
Eq.

2.16
,
M>0
is a positive constant related to the boundedness of each pixel value,
\eta>0
is a fixed positive constant related to the bound of measurement error, and the weighted
\ell_{1}
-norm of wavelet frame coefficients
\left\|\boldsymbol{\lambda}\cdot{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}\boldsymbol{u}\right\|_{1}
is defined as
Eq.

2.13
.

Our error estimate is based on the characterization of the capacity of the hypothesis space
[
18
]
. Among such capacities including VC dimension
[
60
]
,
V_{\gamma}
-dimension and
P_{\gamma}
-dimension
[
3
]
, Rademacher complexities
[
7
,
50
]
, and covering number
[
29
]
, we choose the covering number because it is the most convenient for metric spaces
[
18
]
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
{\mathscr{M}}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}
and
r>0
be given. The covering number
{\mathcal{N}}({\mathscr{M}},r)
is defined as

\displaystyle{\mathcal{N}}\left({\mathscr{M}},r\right)=\inf\left\{K\in{\mathbb{N}}:\exists~\boldsymbol{u}_{1},\cdots,\boldsymbol{u}_{K}\in{\mathscr{M}}~~\text{s.t.}~~{\mathscr{M}}\subseteq\bigcup_{j=1}^{K}\left\{\boldsymbol{u}\in{\mathscr{M}}:\left\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{j}\right\|_{\ell_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})}\leq r\right\}\right\},

i.e. the minimal number of
\ell_{\infty}
balls with radius
r
in
{\mathscr{M}}
that covers
{\mathscr{M}}
.

With the above notation, we can present the first relation between the solution
\boldsymbol{u}^{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}}
to
Eq.

1.4
and the underlying image
{\boldsymbol{f}}
. Following the idea of statistical learning theory
[
60
]
, we first establish the probability of the following event

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\boldsymbol{\Omega}|}\left\|\boldsymbol{u}^{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}}-{\boldsymbol{f}}\right\|_{\ell_{2}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})}^{2}\leq\epsilon+\frac{16}{3}\sigma_{\min}^{-2}({\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}})\eta^{2}

for an arbitrary
\epsilon>0
in terms of the covering number with the radius related to
\epsilon
. The proof is postponed to
Section

3.1

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
{\mathscr{M}}
be defined as in
Eq.

2.16
and
\boldsymbol{u}^{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}}
be a solution to
Eq.

1.4
. Then for an arbitrary
\epsilon>0
, the following inequality

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{\frac{1}{|\boldsymbol{\Omega}|}\left\|\boldsymbol{u}^{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}}-{\boldsymbol{f}}\right\|_{\ell_{2}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})}^{2}\leq\epsilon+\frac{16}{3}\sigma_{\min}^{-2}({\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}})\eta^{2}\right\}\geq 1-{\mathcal{N}}\left({\mathscr{M}},\frac{\sigma_{\min}^{2}({\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}})\epsilon}{12\left\|{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}}\right\|_{\infty}^{2}M}\right)\exp\left\{-\frac{3m\sigma_{\min}^{2}({\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}})\epsilon}{256\left\|{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}}\right\|_{\infty}^{2}M^{2}}\right\}

holds for an arbitrary
m
, where
m=|{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}|
denotes the number of samples.

Remark 2.5
.

Notice that the proof of
Theorem

2.4
is completed if we estimate the probability of the event

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\boldsymbol{\Omega}|}\left\|{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}}\boldsymbol{u}^{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}}-{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}}{\boldsymbol{f}}\right\|_{\ell_{2}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})}^{2}\leq\sigma_{\min}^{2}\left({\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}}\right)\epsilon+\frac{16}{3}\eta^{2}.

In this case, we need to use the uniform boundedness constraint (
\boldsymbol{u}[{\boldsymbol{k}}]\in[0,M]
for all
{\boldsymbol{k}}\in\boldsymbol{\Omega}
), from which we need an assumption on
\left\|{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}}\right\|_{\infty}
. See
Section

3.1
for details.

By
Theorem

2.4
, our error estimate will be completed if we estimate the covering number
{\mathcal{N}}\left({\mathscr{M}},r\right)
. Intuitively, since
{\mathscr{M}}
lies in an
\ell_{\infty}
ball
\big\{\boldsymbol{u}\in\ell_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\Omega}):\left\|\boldsymbol{u}\right\|_{\ell_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})}\leq M\big\}
, it is obvious that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{N}}({\mathscr{M}},r)\leq\left(\frac{2M}{r}\right)^{|\boldsymbol{\Omega}|}

(2.17)

as given in
[
29
]
. However, since the above estimation
Eq.

2.17
is not tight enough to derive an error estimate, we need to find a tighter upper bound for
{\mathcal{N}}({\mathscr{M}},r)
to obtain a reasonable error bound. To do this, we adopt the idea given in
[
18
]
; using the discrete total variation, we connect the wavelet frame coefficients
{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}\boldsymbol{u}
with the regularity of
\boldsymbol{u}
. Specifically, we define the discrete gradient of
\boldsymbol{u}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}
as

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\nabla}\boldsymbol{u}=\left\{\boldsymbol{u}[{\boldsymbol{k}}+{\boldsymbol{e}}_{j}]-\boldsymbol{u}[{\boldsymbol{k}}]:j=1,2,~{\boldsymbol{k}}\in\boldsymbol{\Omega},~\&~{\boldsymbol{k}}+{\boldsymbol{e}}_{j}\in\boldsymbol{\Omega}\right\},

(2.18)

where
{\boldsymbol{e}}_{1}=(1,0)
and
{\boldsymbol{e}}_{2}=(0,1)
. The discrete total variation
\left\|\boldsymbol{\nabla}\boldsymbol{u}\right\|_{1}
is defined as

\displaystyle\left\|\boldsymbol{\nabla}\boldsymbol{u}\right\|_{1}:=\sum_{j=1}^{2}\sum_{\mathrel{{\mathop{{\boldsymbol{k}}+{\boldsymbol{e}}_{j}\in\boldsymbol{\Omega}}\limits}}}\left|\boldsymbol{u}[{\boldsymbol{k}}+{\boldsymbol{e}}_{j}]-\boldsymbol{u}[{\boldsymbol{k}}]\right|.

(2.19)

Under this setting, we present
Lemma

2.6
whose proof is postponed to
Section

3.2
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}
be a tensor product B-spline wavelet frame transform defined as
Eq.

2.11
. Then there exists a constant
C_{{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}}\geq 1
which is independent of
|\boldsymbol{\Omega}|
and
L
such that

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla\boldsymbol{u}\right\|_{1}\leq C_{{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}\boldsymbol{u}\right\|_{1}

(2.20)

for all
\boldsymbol{u}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}
. In
Eq.

2.20
,
\left\|\boldsymbol{\nabla}\boldsymbol{u}\right\|_{1}
is defined as
Eq.

2.19
, and
\left\|{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}\boldsymbol{u}\right\|_{1}
is defined as
Eq.

2.14
.

Lemma

2.6
tells us that, given that
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=\left\{\lambda_{l,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}[{\boldsymbol{k}}]\right\}
satisfies A2, for
\boldsymbol{u}\in{\mathscr{M}}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\boldsymbol{\nabla}\boldsymbol{u}\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq C_{{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}\boldsymbol{u}\right\|_{1}=C_{{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}}\sum_{{\boldsymbol{k}}\in\boldsymbol{\Omega}}\sum_{l=0}^{L-1}\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in{\mathbb{B}}}\left|\left({\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}_{l,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\boldsymbol{u}\right)[{\boldsymbol{k}}]\right|

\displaystyle\leq C_{{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}}2^{\max\left\{-\beta,0\right\}\max\left\{\Upsilon(l,\boldsymbol{\alpha},{\boldsymbol{k}}):(l,\boldsymbol{\alpha},{\boldsymbol{k}})\in\Gamma\right\}}\sum_{{\boldsymbol{k}}\in\boldsymbol{\Omega}}\sum_{l=0}^{L-1}\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in{\mathbb{B}}}2^{\beta\Upsilon(l,\boldsymbol{\alpha},{\boldsymbol{k}})}\left|\left({\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}_{l,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\boldsymbol{u}\right)[{\boldsymbol{k}}]\right|

\displaystyle\leq C_{{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}}|\boldsymbol{\Omega}|^{\frac{\max\{1-\beta,0\}}{2}}\left\|\boldsymbol{\lambda}\cdot{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}{\boldsymbol{f}}\right\|_{1}.

Hence, we can further relax the set
{\mathscr{M}}
into

\displaystyle\widetilde{{\mathscr{M}}}=\left\{\boldsymbol{u}\in\ell_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\Omega}):\left\|\nabla\boldsymbol{u}\right\|_{1}\leq C_{{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}}|\boldsymbol{\Omega}|^{\frac{\max\{1-\beta,0\}}{2}}\left\|\boldsymbol{\lambda}\cdot{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}{\boldsymbol{f}}\right\|_{1},~\left\|\boldsymbol{u}\right\|_{\ell_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})}\leq M\right\}.

(2.21)

Then with the set
\widetilde{{\mathscr{M}}}
defined above, we present
Theorem

2.7
for the estimation of covering number. Similar to
[
18
, Theorem 2.4]
, our estimate is also based on the quantized total variation minimization (e.g.
[
1
,
24
]
) and the max-flow min-cut theorem
[

]
to introduce a new upper bound of the covering number.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}
be a tensor product B-spline wavelet frame transform defined as
Eq.

2.11
. Assume that A2 is satisfied with

and
\left\|\boldsymbol{\lambda}\cdot{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}{\boldsymbol{f}}\right\|_{1}\leq C_{{\boldsymbol{f}}}|\boldsymbol{\Omega}|^{1/2}
. Let
{\mathscr{M}}
be defined as
Eq.

2.16
. For
r\geq|\boldsymbol{\Omega}|^{-a}
with

, we have

\displaystyle\ln{\mathcal{N}}({\mathscr{M}},r)\leq\frac{C_{a}|\boldsymbol{\Omega}|^{\frac{\max\{1-\beta,1\}}{2}}}{r}\log_{2}|\boldsymbol{\Omega}|,

where
C_{a}=20M(4a+\max\{1-\beta,1\})C_{{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}}C_{{\boldsymbol{f}}}(1+C_{{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}}C_{{\boldsymbol{f}}})
.

Proof 1
.

See
Section

3.3
.

With the aid of
Theorems

2.4
and
2.7
, we can establish the explicit formulation of
Eq.

1.5
in
Theorem

2.8
. Briefly speaking, for a fixed
\rho
, as long as the resolution of an image is sufficiently high, the wavelet frame based restoration model
Eq.

1.4
has a good chance to restore the original image within the measurement error. In addition, for a fixed

, the error gets smaller as

, which coincides with our common sense. Finally,

2.8
takes the results in
[

]
as a special case because
\left\|{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{I}}}}\right\|_{\infty}=\sigma_{\min}({\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{I}}}})=1
.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}
be a tensor product B-spline wavelet frame transform. Assume that
{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}}
satisfies A1,
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
satisfies A2 with
-1<\beta<1
, and
{\boldsymbol{f}}\in[0,M]^{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}

. Then the following inequality

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\boldsymbol{\Omega}|}\left\|\boldsymbol{u}^{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}}-{\boldsymbol{f}}\right\|_{\ell_{2}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})}^{2}\leq\widetilde{c}\rho^{-\frac{1}{2}}|\boldsymbol{\Omega}|^{-\frac{\min\{1+\beta,1\}}{4}}\left(\log_{2}|\boldsymbol{\Omega}|\right)^{3/2}+\frac{16}{3}\sigma_{\min}^{-2}({\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}})\eta^{2}

(2.22)

holds with probability at least

. In

,

,

,

, and

.

Proof 2
.

First of all, for any
\epsilon>0
, we can choose

such that
\epsilon\geq\frac{12\left\|{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}}\right\|_{\infty}^{2}M}{\sigma_{\min}^{2}({\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}})}|\boldsymbol{\Omega}|^{-a}

2.4
and
2.7
give us that the inequality

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\boldsymbol{\Omega}|}\left\|\boldsymbol{u}^{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}}-{\boldsymbol{f}}\right\|_{\ell_{2}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})}^{2}\leq\epsilon+\frac{16}{3}\sigma_{\min}^{-2}({\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}})\eta^{2}

holds with probability at least

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle-{\mathcal{N}}\left({\mathscr{M}},\frac{\sigma_{\min}^{2}({\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}})\epsilon}{12\left\|{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}}\right\|_{\infty}^{2}M}\right)\exp\left\{-\frac{3m\sigma_{\min}^{2}({\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}})\epsilon}{256\left\|{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}}\right\|_{\infty}^{2}M^{2}}\right\}

\displaystyle\geq 1-\exp\left\{\frac{240\left\|{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}}\right\|_{\infty}^{2}M^{2}(4a+b)C_{{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}}C_{{\boldsymbol{f}}}(1+C_{{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}}C_{{\boldsymbol{f}}})|\Omega|^{b/2}\log_{2}|\boldsymbol{\Omega}|}{\sigma_{\min}^{2}({\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}})\epsilon}-\frac{3m\sigma_{\min}^{2}({\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}})\epsilon}{256\left\|{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}}\right\|_{\infty}^{2}M^{2}}\right\}.

\displaystyle\frac{240\left\|{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}}\right\|_{\infty}^{2}M^{2}(4a+b)C_{{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}}C_{{\boldsymbol{f}}}(1+C_{{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}}C_{{\boldsymbol{f}}})|\Omega|^{b/2}\log_{2}|\boldsymbol{\Omega}|}{\sigma_{\min}^{2}({\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}})\epsilon}-\frac{3m\sigma_{\min}^{2}({\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}})\epsilon}{256\left\|{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}}\right\|_{\infty}^{2}M^{2}}=\ln\frac{1}{|\boldsymbol{\Omega}|},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\boldsymbol{\Omega}|}\left\|\boldsymbol{u}^{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}}-{\boldsymbol{f}}\right\|_{\ell_{2}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})}^{2}\leq\epsilon^{*}+\frac{16}{3}\sigma_{\min}^{-2}({\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}})\eta^{2}

with probability at least

. Indeed, solving

2.23

2.22

\displaystyle\epsilon^{*}

\displaystyle=\frac{128\left\|{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}}\right\|_{\infty}^{2}M^{2}}{3m\sigma_{\min}^{2}({\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}})}\left[\ln|\boldsymbol{\Omega}|+\sqrt{\ln^{2}|\boldsymbol{\Omega}|+\frac{45}{4}m(4a+b)C_{{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}}C_{{\boldsymbol{f}}}(1+C_{{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}}C_{{\boldsymbol{f}}})|\boldsymbol{\Omega}|^{b/2}\log_{2}|\boldsymbol{\Omega}|}\right]

2.3
Asymptotic function approximation via MRA

In this section, we apply
Theorem

2.8
to further connect with the function approximation given that the underlying true image is obtained by the samples of a function. All functions we consider are defined on
\Omega=[0,1)^{2}
, and we consider the
2^{J}\times 2^{J}
Cartesian grid
\boldsymbol{\Omega}
defined as

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\Omega}=\left\{0,1,\cdots,2^{J}-1\right\}^{2}\simeq 2^{-J}{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\cap\Omega.

In other words, we implicitly identify the
2^{J}\times 2^{J}
grid
\boldsymbol{\Omega}
with the
2^{J}\times 2^{J}
mesh of
\Omega
. For the consistency with the discrete image cases, we identify
L_{p}(\Omega)
with the space of
L_{p}
functions on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
with fundamental period on each variable to be
1
. For the wavelet frame decomposition (e.g.
[
31
,
37
]
) of
f\in L_{2}(\Omega)
, we write

\displaystyle f=\sum_{l\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\sum_{{\boldsymbol{k}}\in\boldsymbol{\Omega}}\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in{\mathbb{B}}}\langle f,\psi_{\boldsymbol{\alpha},l,{\boldsymbol{k}}}\rangle\psi_{\boldsymbol{\alpha},l,{\boldsymbol{k}}}

by identifying
\psi_{\boldsymbol{\alpha},l,{\boldsymbol{k}}}
with its periodized version

\displaystyle\psi_{\boldsymbol{\alpha},l,{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{\mathrm{per}}=\sum_{{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}}\psi_{\boldsymbol{\alpha},l,{\boldsymbol{k}}}(\cdot+2^{\max\{l,J\}}{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\prime})

with a slight abuse of notation.

In the literature of wavelet frame, the discrete image
{\boldsymbol{f}}
is interpreted as discrete sampling of an underlying function
f\in L_{2}(\Omega)
via the inner product with the corresponding refinable function
\phi
. More precisely, let
\phi_{J,{\boldsymbol{k}}}=2^{J}\phi(2^{J}\cdot-{\boldsymbol{k}})
. Then we can write
{\boldsymbol{f}}
as

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{f}}[{\boldsymbol{k}}]=2^{J}\left\langle f,\phi_{J,{\boldsymbol{k}}}\right\rangle.

(2.26)

With this
{\boldsymbol{f}}
, we implicitly use the following interpolated function

\displaystyle f_{J}=\sum_{{\boldsymbol{k}}\in\boldsymbol{\Omega}}{\boldsymbol{f}}[{\boldsymbol{k}}]\phi(2^{J}\cdot-{\boldsymbol{k}})=\sum_{{\boldsymbol{k}}\in\boldsymbol{\Omega}}\left\langle f,\phi_{J,{\boldsymbol{k}}}\right\rangle\phi_{J,{\boldsymbol{k}}}

(2.27)

to approximate
f
.

There are extensive studies on the approximation order of
f_{J}
in
Eq.

2.27
to the original
f
, and most of them are based on certain properties of the refinable function
\phi
. Relevant works include the wavelet frame based scattered data restoration
[
63
]
and the wavelet frame based image denoising/inpainting
[
40
]
. Briefly speaking, these works commonly assume the Strang-Fix condition on
\phi
(e.g.
[
31
,
49
]
) to approximate the underlying function in the Sobolev space of a certain order. Meanwhile, the underlying function that we concern in this paper does not satisfy certain order of regularity. Instead, we only impose some decay condition of the wavelet frame coefficients for the analysis of the approximation order. More precisely, we assume that there is
\beta\geq-1
such that

\displaystyle C_{f}:=\sum_{l=J-L}^{\infty}2^{\beta l}\sum_{{\boldsymbol{k}}\in\boldsymbol{\Omega}}\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in{\mathbb{B}}}\big|\langle f,\psi_{\boldsymbol{\alpha},l,{\boldsymbol{k}}}\rangle\big|<\infty.

(2.28)

Note that the above decay condition links to the regularity of the underlying function
f
when the wavelet frame system satisfies some mild conditions
[
10
,
18
,
46
]
.

Under the decay condition given in
Eq.

2.28
, we present the following approximation of underlying function in
Theorem

2.10
. Briefly speaking, as long as the image resolution is sufficiently large, the approximated function constructed from the restored image gives a good approximation of the underlying function where the underlying true image comes from.

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}
be a tensor product B-spline wavelet frame transform. Assume that A1 is satisfied, and
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=\left\{\lambda_{l,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}[{\boldsymbol{k}}]:l=0,\cdots,L-1,~\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in{\mathbb{B}},~{\boldsymbol{k}}\in\boldsymbol{\Omega}\right\}
is defined as

\displaystyle\lambda_{l,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}[{\boldsymbol{k}}]=2^{\beta(J-l-1)},~~~~~l=0,\cdots,L-1,~\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in{\mathbb{B}},~{\boldsymbol{k}}\in\boldsymbol{\Omega},

(2.29)

and the underlying function
f\in L_{2}(\Omega)
satisfies
Eq.

2.28
with
-1<\beta<1
. Let
\boldsymbol{u}^{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}}
be a solution to
Eq.

1.4
with
{\boldsymbol{g}}
in
Section

1
generated by
{\boldsymbol{f}}
in
Eq.

2.26
. If
f(\boldsymbol{x})\in[0,M]
a.e.
\boldsymbol{x}\in\Omega
, we have the followings:

1.

The inequality

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{2J}}\left\|\boldsymbol{u}^{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}}-{\boldsymbol{f}}\right\|_{\ell_{2}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})}^{2}\leq\widetilde{c}\rho^{-\frac{1}{2}}J^{3/2}2^{-J\min\left\{\frac{1+\beta}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right\}}+\frac{16}{3}\sigma_{\min}^{-2}({\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}})\eta^{2}

(2.30)

holds with probability at least
1-2^{-2J}
, where
\widetilde{c}
is a constant independent of
J
(i.e. independent of
|\boldsymbol{\Omega}|
),
L
,
\rho
, and
\eta
.

2.

Let
u_{J}^{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}}
be defined as

\displaystyle u_{J}^{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}}=\sum_{{\boldsymbol{k}}\in\boldsymbol{\Omega}}\boldsymbol{u}^{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}}[{\boldsymbol{k}}]\phi(2^{J}\cdot-{\boldsymbol{k}}).

Then

\displaystyle\left\|u_{J}^{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}}-f\right\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C_{1}\rho^{-\frac{1}{2}}J^{3/2}2^{-J\min\left\{\frac{1+\beta}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right\}}+C_{2}\eta^{2}+C_{3}2^{-(\beta+1)J}

(2.31)

holds with probability at least
1-2^{-2J}
, where
C_{1}
,
C_{2}
, and
C_{3}
are three positive constants independent of
J
,
L
,
\rho
, and
\eta
.

Remark 2.11
.

Before proving
Theorem

2.10
, we would like to mention that, whenever
J\in{\mathbb{N}}
satisfies

\displaystyle J\geq-\frac{2\log_{2}\eta}{\beta+1},

Eq.

2.31
can be further written as

\displaystyle\left\|u_{J}^{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}}-f\right\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C_{1}\rho^{-\frac{1}{2}}J^{3/2}2^{-J\min\left\{\frac{1+\beta}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right\}}+\left(C_{2}+C_{3}\right)\eta^{2}.

In other words, whenever the mesh is sufficiently dense (i.e.
J
is sufficiently large), the
L_{2}
distance between the interpolation of the restored image and the original unknown function is bounded by the restoration error of the discrete image restoration problem
Eq.

1.4
only.

The rest is devoted to the proof of
Theorem

2.10

\left\{\phi(2^{J}\cdot-{\boldsymbol{k}}):{\boldsymbol{k}}\in\boldsymbol{\Omega}\right\}

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
\phi
be a tensor product B-spline of order
n
. For
J\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we have the followings.

1.

For
u\in L_{2}(\Omega)
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{{\boldsymbol{k}}\in\boldsymbol{\Omega}}\left|\left\langle u,\phi(2^{J}\cdot-{\boldsymbol{k}})\right\rangle\right|^{2}\leq\frac{n^{2}}{2^{2J}}\left\|u\right\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

(2.32)

2.

For
\boldsymbol{u}\in\ell_{2}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{{\boldsymbol{k}}\in\boldsymbol{\Omega}}\boldsymbol{u}[{\boldsymbol{k}}]\phi(2^{J}\cdot-{\boldsymbol{k}})\right\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq\frac{n^{2}}{2^{2J}}\left\|\boldsymbol{u}\right\|_{\ell_{2}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})}^{2}.

(2.33)

Proof 3
.

Notice that
0\leq\phi\leq 1
, and the direct computation shows that
\left\|\phi\right\|_{L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}^{2}\leq 1
, and
\left\|\phi(2^{J}\cdot-{\boldsymbol{k}})\right\|_{L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}^{2}\leq 2^{-2J}
. Then by the Schwarz inequality, we have

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle u,\phi(2^{J}\cdot-{\boldsymbol{k}})\right\rangle\right|\leq 2^{-J}\left(\int_{\Omega}\left|u(\boldsymbol{x})\right|^{2}1_{{\mathbf{S}}_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}}(\boldsymbol{x}){\mathrm{d}}\boldsymbol{x}\right)^{1/2}

where
{\mathbf{S}}_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}=\mathrm{supp}\left(\phi(2^{J}\cdot-{\boldsymbol{k}})\right)
. Since
\sum 1_{{\mathbf{S}}_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}}=n^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{{\boldsymbol{k}}\in\boldsymbol{\Omega}}\left|\left\langle u,\phi(2^{J}\cdot-{\boldsymbol{k}})\right\rangle\right|^{2}\leq 2^{-2J}\int_{\Omega}\left|u(\boldsymbol{x})\right|^{2}\left(\sum_{{\boldsymbol{k}}\in\boldsymbol{\Omega}}1_{{\mathbf{S}}_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}}(\boldsymbol{x})\right){\mathrm{d}}\boldsymbol{x}=\frac{n^{2}}{2^{2J}}\left\|u\right\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}^{2},

which proves
Eq.

2.32
.

For
Eq.

2.33
, let
v\in L_{2}(\Omega)
. We have

\displaystyle\left\langle\sum_{{\boldsymbol{k}}\in\boldsymbol{\Omega}}\boldsymbol{u}[{\boldsymbol{k}}]\phi(2^{J}\cdot-{\boldsymbol{k}}),v\right\rangle=\sum_{{\boldsymbol{k}}\in\boldsymbol{\Omega}}\boldsymbol{u}[{\boldsymbol{k}}]\left\langle\phi(2^{J}\cdot-{\boldsymbol{k}}),v\right\rangle.

By the Schwarz inequality, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{{\boldsymbol{k}}\in\boldsymbol{\Omega}}\left|\boldsymbol{u}[{\boldsymbol{k}}]\right|\left|\left\langle\phi(2^{J}\cdot-{\boldsymbol{k}}),v\right\rangle\right|\leq\left(\sum_{{\boldsymbol{k}}\in\boldsymbol{\Omega}}\left|\boldsymbol{u}[{\boldsymbol{k}}]\right|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{{\boldsymbol{k}}\in\boldsymbol{\Omega}}\left|\left\langle\phi(2^{J}\cdot-{\boldsymbol{k}}),v\right\rangle\right|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

By
Eq.

2.32
, we further have

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle\sum_{{\boldsymbol{k}}\in\boldsymbol{\Omega}}\boldsymbol{u}[{\boldsymbol{k}}]\phi(2^{J}\cdot-{\boldsymbol{k}}),v\right\rangle\right|\leq\left(\sum_{{\boldsymbol{k}}\in\boldsymbol{\Omega}}\left|\boldsymbol{u}[{\boldsymbol{k}}]\right|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\frac{n}{2^{J}}\left\|v\right\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}.

Since
v\in L_{2}(\Omega)
is arbitrary, we have
Eq.

2.33
by the converse of Hölder’s inequality with
p=q=2
(e.g.
[
43
]
). This completes the proof.
\square

Lemma 3.13
.

Assume that a random variable
\xi
on a probability space
{\mathscr{X}}
satisfies
\mu:=\mathbb{E}\xi\geq 0
and
\left|\xi-\mu\right|\leq B
almost surely. If
\mathbb{E}\xi^{2}\leq c\mathbb{E}\xi
for some
c>0
, the following inequality

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{{\boldsymbol{z}}\in{\mathscr{X}}^{m}:\frac{\mu-\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\xi(z_{i})}{\sqrt{\mu+\epsilon}}>\gamma\sqrt{\epsilon}\right\}\leq\exp\left\{-\frac{3\gamma^{2}m\epsilon}{6c+2B}\right\}

holds for arbitrary
\epsilon>0
and
0<\gamma\leq 1
.

Let
{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}
be a random variable which is drawn from the uniform distribution on
\boldsymbol{\Omega}
. For
\boldsymbol{u}\in{\mathscr{M}}
, we introduce
\xi=\left|({\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}}\boldsymbol{u})[{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}]-({\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}}{\boldsymbol{f}})[{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}]\right|^{2}
. Then
\xi
is a random variable which is drawn from the uniform distribution on
\boldsymbol{\Omega}
, and satisfies
0\leq\xi\leq\left\|{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}}\right\|_{\infty}^{2}M^{2}
. Define

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}(\boldsymbol{u})=\mathbb{E}\xi=\mathbb{E}\left(\left|({\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}}\boldsymbol{u})[{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}]-({\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}}{\boldsymbol{f}})[{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}]\right|^{2}\right)=\frac{1}{|\boldsymbol{\Omega}|}\sum_{{\boldsymbol{k}}\in\boldsymbol{\Omega}}\left|({\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}}\boldsymbol{u})[{\boldsymbol{k}}]-({\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}}{\boldsymbol{f}})[{\boldsymbol{k}}]\right|^{2}.

(3.1)

We also define
{\mathcal{E}}_{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}}(\boldsymbol{u})
as a conditional expectation of
\xi
given
{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}
:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}_{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}}(\boldsymbol{u})=\mathbb{E}\left(\xi\big|{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}\right)=\frac{1}{|{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}|}\sum_{{\boldsymbol{k}}\in{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}}\left|({\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}}\boldsymbol{u})[{\boldsymbol{k}}]-({\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}}{\boldsymbol{f}})[{\boldsymbol{k}}]\right|^{2}.

(3.2)

Then we have the following lemma.

Lemma 3.14
.

Let
{\mathscr{M}}
be defined as
Eq.

2.16
. For
\boldsymbol{u}_{1},\boldsymbol{u}_{2}\in{\mathscr{M}}
, we have

\displaystyle\left|{\mathcal{E}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{1})-{\mathcal{E}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{2})\right|

\displaystyle\leq 2\left\|{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}}\right\|_{\infty}^{2}M\left\|\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{2}\right\|_{\ell_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})}

(3.3)

\displaystyle\left|{\mathcal{E}}_{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{1})-{\mathcal{E}}_{{\boldsymbol{\Lambda}}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{2})\right|

\displaystyle\leq 2\left\|{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}}\right\|_{\infty}^{2}M\left\|\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{2}\right\|_{\ell_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})}.

(3.4)

Proof 4
.

We have

\displaystyle\left|{\mathcal{E}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{1})-{\mathcal{E}}(\boldsymbol{u}_{2})\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\boldsymbol{\Omega}|}\left|\sum_{{\boldsymbol{k}}\in\boldsymbol{\Omega}}\left|({\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}}\boldsymbol{u}_{1})[{\boldsymbol{k}}]-({\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}}{\boldsymbol{f}})[{\boldsymbol{k}}]\right|^{2}-\sum_{{\boldsymbol{k}}\in\boldsymbol{\Omega}}\left|({\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}}\boldsymbol{u}_{2})[{\boldsymbol{k}}]-({\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}}{\boldsymbol{f}})[{\boldsymbol{k}}]\right|^{2}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|\boldsymbol{\Omega}|}\sum_{{\boldsymbol{k}}\in\boldsymbol{\Omega}}\left|({\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}}\boldsymbol{u}_{1})[{\boldsymbol{k}}]+({\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}}\boldsymbol{u}_{2})[{\boldsymbol{k}}]-2({\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}}{\boldsymbol{f}})[{\boldsymbol{k}}]\right|\left|({\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}}\boldsymbol{u}_{1})[{\boldsymbol{k}}]-({\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}}\boldsymbol{u}_{2})[{\boldsymbol{k}}]\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|\boldsymbol{\Omega}|}\left\|{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}}\left(\boldsymbol{u}_{1}+\boldsymbol{u}_{2}-2{\boldsymbol{f}}\right)\right\|_{\ell_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})}\left\|{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}}\left(\boldsymbol{u}_{1}-\boldsymbol{u}_{2}\right)\right\|_{\ell_{1}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})}

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{a}}_{0}\ast\boldsymbol{q}[k]=r^{-\frac{1}{2}}\boldsymbol{p}\ast{\boldsymbol{a}}_{1}[k-j_{r}].

Then by the discrete Young’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\left\|{\boldsymbol{a}}_{{\mathbf{0}}}[-\cdot]\ast\boldsymbol{q}_{{\boldsymbol{e}}_{j}}[-\cdot]\circledast\boldsymbol{u}\right\|_{\ell_{1}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})}\leq\frac{2}{\sqrt{r}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{a}}_{{\boldsymbol{e}}_{j}}[-\cdot]\circledast\boldsymbol{u}\right\|_{\ell_{1}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})}=\frac{2}{\sqrt{r}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}_{0,{\boldsymbol{e}}_{j}}\boldsymbol{u}\right\|_{\ell_{1}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})}.

Combining these altogether, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\boldsymbol{q}_{{\boldsymbol{e}}_{1}}[-\cdot]\circledast\boldsymbol{u}\right\|_{\ell_{1}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})}

\displaystyle+\left\|\boldsymbol{q}_{{\boldsymbol{e}}_{2}}[-\cdot]\circledast\boldsymbol{u}\right\|_{\ell_{1}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})}

\displaystyle\leq 4\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in{\mathbb{B}}}\sqrt{\binom{{\boldsymbol{r}}}{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}_{0,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\boldsymbol{u}\right\|_{\ell_{1}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})}+\frac{2}{\sqrt{r}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}_{0,{\boldsymbol{e}}_{1}}\boldsymbol{u}\right\|_{\ell_{1}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})}+\frac{2}{\sqrt{r}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}_{0,{\boldsymbol{e}}_{2}}\boldsymbol{u}\right\|_{\ell_{1}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})}

\displaystyle\leq 6\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in{\mathbb{B}}}\sqrt{\binom{{\boldsymbol{r}}}{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}_{0,\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\boldsymbol{u}\right\|_{\ell_{1}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})},

where the last inequality follows from the fact that
r\geq 1
and
\binom{r}{\alpha}\geq 1
. Therefore, letting

\displaystyle C=6\max_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in{\mathbb{B}}}\sqrt{\binom{{\boldsymbol{r}}}{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}},

we obtain
Eq.

3.11
, and this concludes
Lemma

2.6
.

3.3
Proof of
Theorem

2.7

Theorem

2.7
is to estimate the covering number. The proof follows the line similar to
[
18
, Theorem 2.4]
. However, since we improve
[
18
, Theorem 2.4]
by relaxing the constraint of the radius
r
, we include the detailed proof for the sake of completeness. It is obvious that if
\boldsymbol{u}\in[0,M]^{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}
, then
\left\|\boldsymbol{u}\right\|_{\ell_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})}\leq M
. In addition, if A2 is satisfied, together with the definition of
{\mathscr{M}}
in
Eq.

2.16
and
Lemma

2.6
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\boldsymbol{\nabla}\boldsymbol{u}\right\|_{1}\leq C_{{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}\boldsymbol{u}\right\|_{1}\leq C_{{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}}2^{\max\{-\beta,0\}\max\{\Upsilon(l,\boldsymbol{\alpha},{\boldsymbol{k}}):(l,\boldsymbol{\alpha},{\boldsymbol{k}})\in\Gamma\}}\left\|\boldsymbol{\lambda}\cdot{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}\boldsymbol{u}\right\|_{1}.

Since
\Upsilon:\Gamma\to{\mathbb{N}}
satisfies
Eq.

2.15
, for
\boldsymbol{u}\in{\mathscr{M}}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\boldsymbol{\nabla}\boldsymbol{u}\right\|_{1}\leq C_{{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}}|\boldsymbol{\Omega}|^{\frac{\max\{-\beta,0\}}{2}}\left\|\boldsymbol{\lambda}\cdot{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}{\boldsymbol{f}}\right\|_{1}.

In other words,
{\mathscr{M}}\subseteq\widetilde{{\mathscr{M}}}
and
{\mathcal{N}}\left({\mathscr{M}},r\right)\leq{\mathcal{N}}\left(\widetilde{{\mathscr{M}}},r\right)
where
\widetilde{{\mathscr{M}}}
is defined as
Eq.

2.21
. Hence, it suffices to bound the covering number
{\mathcal{N}}\left(\widetilde{{\mathscr{M}}},r\right)
. In addition, it is easy to see that if there exists a finite set
F\subseteq\widetilde{{\mathscr{M}}}
such that

\displaystyle\widetilde{{\mathscr{M}}}\subseteq\bigcup_{\boldsymbol{q}\in F}\left\{\boldsymbol{u}:\left\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{q}\right\|_{\ell_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})}\leq r\right\},

we have
{\mathcal{N}}\left(\widetilde{{\mathscr{M}}},r\right)\leq\left|F\right|
. What we need now is to construct an appropriate set
F
by exploiting the specific structure of
\widetilde{{\mathscr{M}}}
, so that
\left|F\right|
has an appropriate upper bound.

For this purpose, let
\kappa=\lceil 2M/r\rceil
, and we define

\displaystyle{\mathscr{R}}=\left\{-\kappa r/2,(-\kappa+1)r/2,\cdots,\kappa r/2\right\}.

(3.17)

By
[
18
, Lemma 4.4]
, for each
\boldsymbol{u}\in\widetilde{{\mathscr{M}}}
, there exists
Q(\boldsymbol{u})\in{\mathscr{R}}^{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}
such that

\displaystyle\left\|\boldsymbol{u}-Q(\boldsymbol{u})\right\|_{\ell_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})}\leq r/2~~~\text{and}~~~\left\|\boldsymbol{\nabla}\left(Q(\boldsymbol{u})\right)\right\|_{1}\leq\left\|\boldsymbol{\nabla}\boldsymbol{u}\right\|_{1}.

Let

\displaystyle\widetilde{F}=\left\{\boldsymbol{q}\in\ell_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\Omega}):\boldsymbol{q}=Q(\boldsymbol{u})~~\text{for some}~~\boldsymbol{u}\in\widetilde{{\mathscr{M}}}\right\}\subseteq{\mathscr{R}}^{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}.

Notice that for each
\boldsymbol{q}\in\widetilde{F}
, there may be more than one
\boldsymbol{u}\in\widetilde{{\mathscr{M}}}
such that
\boldsymbol{q}=Q(\boldsymbol{u})
.

For each
\boldsymbol{q}\in\widetilde{F}
, choose
\boldsymbol{u}_{\boldsymbol{q}}\in\widetilde{{\mathscr{M}}}
such that
\left\|\boldsymbol{u}_{\boldsymbol{q}}-\boldsymbol{q}\right\|_{\ell_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})}\leq r/2
, and define
F=\left\{\boldsymbol{u}_{\boldsymbol{q}}:\boldsymbol{q}\in\widetilde{F}\right\}
. For an arbitrary
\boldsymbol{u}\in\widetilde{{\mathscr{M}}}
, there exists
\boldsymbol{q}\in\widetilde{F}
such that
\left\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{q}\right\|_{\ell_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})}\leq r/2
. This implies

\displaystyle\left\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{\boldsymbol{q}}\right\|_{\ell_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})}\leq\left\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{q}\right\|_{\ell_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})}+\left\|\boldsymbol{q}-\boldsymbol{u}_{\boldsymbol{q}}\right\|_{\ell_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})}\leq r,

by the definition of
\boldsymbol{u}_{\boldsymbol{q}}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\widetilde{{\mathscr{M}}}\subseteq\bigcup_{\boldsymbol{u}_{\boldsymbol{q}}\in F}\left\{\boldsymbol{u}:\left\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{\boldsymbol{q}}\right\|_{\ell_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\Omega})}\leq r\right\}~~\text{and}~~{\mathcal{N}}\left(\widetilde{{\mathscr{M}}},r\right)\leq\left|F\right|\leq\left|\widetilde{F}\right|.

Thus, the covering number
{\mathcal{N}}\left(\widetilde{{\mathscr{M}}},r\right)
is bounded by any upper bound of
\left|\widetilde{F}\right|
. Notice that each
\boldsymbol{q}\in\widetilde{F}
is uniquely determined by
\boldsymbol{\nabla}\boldsymbol{q}
and
\boldsymbol{q}[1,\cdots,1]
. Since
\widetilde{F}
is a subset of
{\mathscr{R}}^{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}
, there are
2\kappa+1
choices for
\boldsymbol{q}[1,\cdots,1]
. It remains to count the number of choices in
\boldsymbol{\nabla}\boldsymbol{q}
. Define

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\nabla}\widetilde{F}=\left\{\boldsymbol{\nabla}\boldsymbol{q}:\boldsymbol{q}\in\widetilde{F}\right\}.

Then we need to bound
\left|\boldsymbol{\nabla}\widetilde{F}\right|
.

To do this, we first consider the uniform upper bound of
\left\|\boldsymbol{\nabla}\boldsymbol{q}\right\|_{1}
for
\boldsymbol{q}\in\widetilde{F}
. By the definition of
\widetilde{F}
and
[
18
, Lemma 4.4]
, for each
\boldsymbol{q}\in\widetilde{F}
, there exists
\boldsymbol{u}\in\widetilde{{\mathscr{M}}}
such that
\left\|\boldsymbol{\nabla}\boldsymbol{q}\right\|_{1}\leq\left\|\boldsymbol{\nabla}\boldsymbol{u}\right\|_{1}
. Since
\left\|\boldsymbol{\lambda}\cdot{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}{\boldsymbol{f}}\right\|_{1}\leq C_{{\boldsymbol{f}}}|\boldsymbol{\Omega}|^{1/2}
by assumption, we further have

\displaystyle\left\|\boldsymbol{\nabla}\boldsymbol{q}\right\|_{1}\leq\left\|\boldsymbol{\nabla}\boldsymbol{u}\right\|_{1}\leq C_{{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}}C_{{\boldsymbol{f}}}\left|\boldsymbol{\Omega}\right|^{b/2}

with
b=\max\{1-\beta,1\}
for notational simplicity. Hence, for all
\boldsymbol{q}\in\widetilde{F}
, we have
\left\|\boldsymbol{\nabla}\boldsymbol{q}\right\|_{1}\leq Kr/2
, where

\displaystyle K=\left\lceil\frac{2C_{{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}}C_{{\boldsymbol{f}}}\left|\boldsymbol{\Omega}\right|^{b/2}}{r}\right\rceil.

In addition, since
\boldsymbol{q}\in{\mathscr{R}}^{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}
, each element of
\boldsymbol{\nabla}\boldsymbol{q}
has to be a multiple of
r/2
, which means that the range of
\boldsymbol{\nabla}\boldsymbol{q}
is a subset of
\left\{-Kr/2,-(K-1)r/2,\cdots,Kr/2\right\}^{2}
. Recall that there are
R=2\left(\left|\boldsymbol{\Omega}\right|-\left|\boldsymbol{\Omega}\right|^{1/2}\right)
elements in
\boldsymbol{\nabla}\boldsymbol{q}
. Hence,
\left|\boldsymbol{\nabla}\widetilde{F}\right|
can be bounded by the number of all possible integer solutions of the following inequality

\displaystyle\left|x_{1}\right|+\left|x_{2}\right|+\cdots+\left|x_{R-1}\right|+\left|x_{R}\right|\leq K.

That is (e.g.
[
56
]
),

\displaystyle\left|\boldsymbol{\nabla}\widetilde{F}\right|

\displaystyle\leq 1+\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{s=1}^{\min\{k,R\}}2^{s}\binom{R}{R-s}\binom{k-1}{s-1}

\displaystyle\leq 1+\sum_{k=1}^{K}2^{k}\sum_{s=1}^{\min\{k,R\}}\binom{R}{R-s}\binom{k-1}{s-1}=1+\sum_{k=1}^{K}2^{k}\binom{R+k-1}{R-1}

\displaystyle\leq\binom{R+K-1}{R-1}\sum_{k=0}^{K}2^{k}\leq 2^{K+1}\binom{R+K-1}{K}\leq 2\left[2\left(R+K-1\right)\right]^{K}.

Hence, we have

\displaystyle\left|\widetilde{F}\right|\leq\left(4\kappa+2\right)\left[2\left(R+K-1\right)\right]^{K}.

In other words, using
r\geq\left|\boldsymbol{\Omega}\right|^{-a}
with
a\geq 1
and
K-1\leq 2C_{{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}}C_{{\boldsymbol{f}}}|\boldsymbol{\Omega}|^{a+b/2}
, we have

\displaystyle\ln{\mathcal{N}}\left({\mathscr{M}},r\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2C_{{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}}C_{{\boldsymbol{f}}}\left|\boldsymbol{\Omega}\right|^{b/2}}{r}\ln\left(2\left(R+K-1\right)\right)+\ln\left(\frac{8M}{r}+2\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2C_{{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}}C_{{\boldsymbol{f}}}\left|\boldsymbol{\Omega}\right|^{b/2}}{r}\left[\ln\left(4\left|\boldsymbol{\Omega}\right|+\frac{4C_{{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}}C_{{\boldsymbol{f}}}\left|\boldsymbol{\Omega}\right|^{b/2}}{r}\right)+\ln\frac{10M}{r}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2C_{{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}}C_{{\boldsymbol{f}}}\left|\boldsymbol{\Omega}\right|^{b/2}}{r}\ln\left(\left(1+C_{{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}}C_{{\boldsymbol{f}}}\right)40M\left|\boldsymbol{\Omega}\right|^{2a+b/2}\right)

where we use the fact that
a+b/2\geq 1
from the choice of
a
and
b
in the final inequality. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\ln{\mathcal{N}}\left({\mathscr{M}},r\right)\leq\frac{20M(4a+b)C_{{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}}C_{{\boldsymbol{f}}}\left(1+C_{{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{W}}}}}C_{{\boldsymbol{f}}}\right)\left|\boldsymbol{\Omega}\right|^{b/2}}{r}\log_{2}\left|\boldsymbol{\Omega}\right|,

and this completes the proof.
\square

4
Conclusion

In this paper, we present an approximation analysis of wavelet frame based restoration from degraded and incomplete measurements. By the combination of the uniform law of large numbers and the estimation for its involved covering number of a hypothesis space of the solution, we establish unified approximation properties of the wavelet frame based image restoration model, together with the improved estimate of covering number over the previous one in
[
18
]
. In addition, thanks to the underlying multiresolution analysis structure, we further connect the error analysis in the discrete setting to the approximation of underlying function where the discrete data comes from. For the future works, we plan to study an approximation property of wavelet frame based image restoration from partial Fourier samples, where
{\boldsymbol{{\mathcal{A}}}}={\boldsymbol{{\mathscr{F}}}}
(the unitary discrete Fourier transform) satisfies
\left\|{\boldsymbol{{\mathscr{F}}}}\right\|_{\infty}=|\boldsymbol{\Omega}|^{1/2}
and
\sigma_{\min}({\boldsymbol{{\mathscr{F}}}})=\sigma_{\max}({\boldsymbol{{\mathscr{F}}}})=1
in our framework. We may also plan to establish an approximation of tight frame based missing data restoration on the graph
[
33
]
, and the multiresolution approximation of functions on the manifold
[
35
]
.

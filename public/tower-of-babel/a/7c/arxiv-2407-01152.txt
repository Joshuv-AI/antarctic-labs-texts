Invariants of Finite Orthogonal Groups of Plus Type in Odd Characteristic
H.E.A. Campbell, R. James Shank and David L. Wehlau

Abstract
. We describe the rings of invariants for the finite orthogonal groups of plus type in odd characteristic acting on the defining representations. We also describe the invariants of the corresponding Sylow subgroups in the defining characteristic. In both cases we construct minimal algebra generating sets and describe the relations among the generators. Both rings of invariants are shown to be complete intersections and thus are Cohen-Macaulay. We expect the techniques we use will generalise to give a systematic computation for rings of invariants for all of the finite classical groups in odd characteristic.



1991 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 13A50; Secondary 20F55

1.
Introduction

The fundamental problem in the invariant theory of finite groups is to determine the ring of invariants
of a representation of a finite group. The celebrated theorem of Shepherd–Todd and Chevalley proves that
such a ring of invariants in characteristic zero is a polynomial ring if and only if the group action is
generated by pseudo-reflections (group elements which fix pointwise a hyperplane).
An excellent survey of the characteristic zero results and theory in characteristic zero
is the article by Stanley
[
26
]
.

In positive characteristic the situation is much more complex.
In 1911, L.E. Dickson
[
12
]
gave an explicit description of the ring of
invariants of the general linear group over any finite field. In the past few decades
there has been significant progress in determining the rings of invariants of the defining
representations of the finite classical groups.
The rings of invariants for the symplectic groups were computed by David Carlisle and Peter Kropholler in the 1990s, see

[
1
, §8.3]
.
The invariants in the unitary case were computed by
Huah Chu and Shin-Yao Jow
[
9
]
.
For the orthogonal groups, while there are computations in a number of special cases (see

[
7
]
,

[
8
]
,

[
10
]
,

[
19
]
and

[
25
]

)
there is no general result.
Almost all of the defining representations for these groups are generated by pseudo-reflections, but the rings of invariants are rarely polynomial rings.

We work over the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of order
q
and characteristic
p
where
p
is an odd prime.
We compute the ring of invariants for the
finite orthogonal group
{{\text{O}^{+}_{{2m}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}}
of plus type
acting on its defining representation
V
of dimension
n=2m
.

As an intermediate result, we compute the ring of invariants for
a Sylow
p
-subgroup.
For both of these rings we construct a minimal set of algebra generators and describe
generators for the ideal of relations.
Both of these rings are complete intersections, see Theorems
4.8
and
5.11
, and are thus Cohen-Macaulay. We note that for modular
representations, rings of invariants are rarely Cohen-Macaulay, let alone
complete intersections.

For a Sylow
p
-subgroup
P
of
{{\text{O}^{+}_{{2m}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}}
we find a set
\mathcal{H}_{0}
consisting
of
n
orbit products of linear forms such that

\mathcal{H}_{0}
forms a homogeneous system of parameters. Using a family of

{{\text{O}^{+}_{{2m}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}}
invariants
\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\dots,\xi_{n-1}
, we show that

\mathbb{F}_{q}[V]^{P}=\bigoplus_{\gamma\mid\Gamma_{0}}\mathbb{F}_{q}[\mathcal{H}_{0}]\gamma

where the sum is over all monomials
\gamma
dividing

\Gamma_{0}=\prod_{i=1}^{n-2}\xi_{i}^{e_{i}}\text{ where }e_{i}=q^{m-\lceil i/2\rceil}-1

(see Corollary
5.17
).
In particular,
\mathbb{F}_{q}[V]^{P}
is Cohen-Macaulay and this implies that

\mathbb{F}_{q}[V]^{{{\text{O}^{+}_{{2m}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}}}
is Cohen-Macaulay.
We also give a Khovanskii basis for the Sylow invariants
(see the definition at the end of the next section).

We show that there are homogeneous orthogonal invariants

d_{1,m},d_{2,m},\ldots,d_{m,m}
such that

\mathcal{H}=\{\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{m},d_{1,m},\ldots,d_{m,m}\}

is a homogeneous system of parameters.
Furthermore, we show that

\mathbb{F}_{q}[V]^{{{\text{O}^{+}_{{2m}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}}}=\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\mathcal{B}}\mathbb{F}_{q}[\mathcal{H}]\gamma

where
\mathcal{B}
is the set of monomials dividing

\Gamma=\prod_{i=m+1}^{n-1}\xi_{i}^{q^{n-i}-1}

(see Theorem
4.8
).
For the orthogonal invariants, the ring is generated by two elements as
an algebra over the Steenrod algebra (see Section
3.3
and
Theorem
8.1
).

Since the
P
-action is triangular, there exists a finite Khovanskii basis for
\mathbb{F}_{q}[V]^{P}
(see
[
23
]
). Hence we approximate
\mathbb{F}_{q}[V]^{P}
using its lead term algebra. However, in general, rings of invariants do not have finite Khovanskii bases.
For example, for a permutation representation, the ring of invariants has a finite
Khovanskii basis if and only if the group is generated by reflections, see
[
28
]
.
We do not expect
\mathbb{F}_{q}[V]^{{{\text{O}^{+}_{{2m}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}}}
to have a finite Khovanskii basis.
Instead we approximate
\mathbb{F}_{q}[V]^{{{\text{O}^{+}_{{2m}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}}}

by the associated graded algebra arising from a filtration given by a certain valuation.

For both the orthogonal group and its Sylow
p
-subgroup, we express the ring of invariants
as a free module over a homogeneous system of parameters with basis given by all monomial
factors of a single monomial. Such a basis is called a block basis (see
[
4
]
).
Since the rings are Cohen-Macaulay, having block
bases implies that these rings are both complete intersections.

We believe that the methods we use here generalise to give a systematic computation for rings of invariants for all of the finite classical groups, at least for odd characteristic; this is work in progress.
For background on the finite classical groups we suggest

[
27
]
.
For background on invariant theory see

[
1
]
,

[
5
]
,

[
11
]
or

[
22
]
.

2.
The Setting

For a symmetric bilinear form
\beta
on a vector space
V
,
the orthogonal group
{\rm O}(\beta)
is the subgroup of the the general linear group
{\rm GL}(V)
consisting of linear transformations
g

satisfying
\beta(gv,gu)=\beta(v,u)
for all
u,v\in V
.
In this paper we work over the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}

of order
q
and characteristic
p
where
p
is an odd prime.

In this context, if the dimension of
V
is even, there are two equivalence classes of symmetric non-degenerate
bilinear forms, one of plus type and one of minus type
(see Chapter 11 of
[
27
]
).
In this paper we focus on the plus type.
We expect the generalisation of our methods to the minus type to be relatively straightforward.

We denote the dimension of
V
by
n=2m
.
The quadratic form
\xi_{1}
associated to the bilinear form
\beta
is defined by
\xi_{1}(v):=\beta(v,v)/2
for
v\in V
.
We choose an ordered basis for
V
,

[e_{m},e_{m-1}\ldots,e_{1},f_{1},f_{2},\ldots,f_{m}]
,
so that
\beta(e_{i},f_{i})=1
,
\beta(e_{i},e_{j})=\beta(f_{i},f_{j})=0

and, if
i\not=j
,
\beta(e_{i},f_{j})=0
, in other words,
the basis consists of
m
orthogonal hyperbolic pairs
(see pages 56 and 139 of
[
27
]
).
We note that
m
is the Witt index and the basis vectors are isotropic.
We let
[y_{m},y_{m-1},\ldots,y_{1},x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{m}]
denote the dual basis and observe that the quadratic form is given by

\xi_{1}=y_{m}x_{m}+\cdots+y_{1}x_{1}\ .

The left action of
{\rm GL}(V)
on
V
induces a right action on the dual
V^{*}
given by
(\phi\cdot g)(v)=\phi(g\cdot v)
for
\phi\in V^{*}
,

g\in{\rm GL}(V)
and
v\in V
. This action extends to an action by algebra automorphisms on
\mathbb{F}_{q}[V]
, the symmetric algebra on
V^{*}
.
Choosing a basis for
V
allows us to identify
{\rm GL}(V)

with the matrix group
{{\text{GL}_{{n}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}}
and
identify
\mathbb{F}_{q}[V]
with
the polynomial algebra on
n=2m
variables

S_{m}:=\mathbb{F}_{q}[y_{m},\ldots,y_{1},x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}]\ .

The right action of

{{\text{GL}_{{n}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}}
on
S_{m}
is given by identifying
y_{m}
with

[1\,0\,\cdots\,0]
,
y_{m-1}
with
[0\,1\,0\,\cdots\,0]
, etc.
The orthogonal group of plus type is

{{\text{O}^{+}_{{2m}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}}=\{g\in{{\text{GL}_{{n}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}}\mid\xi_{1}\cdot g=\xi_{1}\}.

Its order is given by

|{{\text{O}^{+}_{{2m}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}}|=2q^{m(m-1)}(q^{m}-1)\prod_{i=1}^{m-1}(q^{2i}-1),

see
[
27
, page 141]
.
For notational simplicity, we write
G_{m}:={{\text{O}^{+}_{{2m}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}}
.
The ring of invariants is

S_{m}^{G_{m}}=\{f\in S_{m}\mid f\cdot g=f,\,\forall g\in G_{m}\}.

Define an algebra map
\psi_{[m,j]}:S_{m}\to S_{m}
by

\psi_{[m,j]}(a):=\prod_{u\in U_{j}}(a-u)

for
\deg(a)=1
, where
U_{j}=\Span_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\{x_{m-j+1},\ldots,x_{m}\}
.
Note that
\psi_{[m,1]}(a)=a^{q}-ax_{m}^{q-1}
and
the height of
\ker(\psi_{[m,j]})
is
j
.

Clearly
\xi_{1}\in S_{m}^{G_{m}}
.
For
i\geq 2
, define

\xi_{i}:=y_{m}^{q^{i-1}}x_{m}+y_{m}x_{m}^{q^{i-1}}+\cdots+y_{1}^{q^{i-1}}x_{1}+y_{1}x_{1}^{q^{i-1}}\ .

Since taking the
q^{th}
power is
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear,
\xi_{i}\in S_{m}^{G_{m}}
.
To avoid cluttering the notation we suppress the
m
from the notation for the
\xi_{i}
.

For an integral domain
A
, let
\mathcal{F}(A)
denote the field of fractions. The following is due to Carlisle and Kropholler
[
6
]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

\mathcal{F}(S_{m}^{G_{m}})=\mathbb{F}_{q}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})
.

Throughout, we order the variables taking

y_{m}>y_{m-1}>\cdots>y_{1}>x_{1}>\ldots>x_{m}
.
There are many ways of extending this ordering to a monomial order on
S_{m}
. We will use both the lexicographic and the graded reverse lexicographic (referred to as grevlex) orders.
For
f\in S_{m}
, we denote the lead term of
f
, with respect to whichever order is being considered, by
\lt(f)
.
For a subalgebra
A\subset S_{m}
,
\lt(A)
denotes the algebra generated by the lead terms of the elements of
A
.
A Khovanskii basis for
A
is a subset of
A
, say
\mathcal{B}
, such that

\{\lt(f)\mid f\in\mathcal{B}\}
is a generating set for
\lt(A)
.
A Khovanskii basis is a nice generating set for
A
.
Khovanksii bases were previously known as SAGBI bases.
For more on Khovanskii(SAGBI) bases see

[
5
, §5.1]
.

3.
Missing Invariants

Define
\overline{V}:=V\otimes\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}

where
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
is the algebraic closure of

\mathbb{F}_{q}
. For
X\subset S_{m}
, the variety associated to
X
is given by

\mathcal{V}(X):=\{v\in\overline{V}\mid f(v)=0,\,\forall f\in X\}\subseteq\overline{V}.

Let
(S_{m}^{G_{m}})_{+}
denote the augmentation ideal, i.e., the set of homogeneous invariants of positive degree. Since
G_{m}
is a finite group,

\mathcal{V}((S_{m}^{G_{m}})_{+})=\{\underline{0}\}
.
Recall that
[e_{m},\ldots,e_{1},f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}]
is the ordered basis for
V

dual to the ordered basis for
V^{*}
given by
[y_{m},\ldots,y_{1},x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}]
.
In the following, we write

\mathcal{V}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{i})
for

\mathcal{V}(\{\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{i}\})
.

Theorem 3.1
.

For
s\geq m
,

\mathcal{V}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{s})=\bigcup_{g\in G_{m}}g\cdot\Span_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{m}\}.

Proof.

Observe that the variety
\mathcal{V}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{s})
is closed under scalar multiplication
since it is defined by homogeneous polynomials.
Let
W_{m}
denote

\Span_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{m}\}
.
Since
\xi_{i}(v)=0
for
v\in W_{m}
and
\xi_{i}\in S_{m}^{G_{m}}
,

\bigcup_{g\in G_{m}}g\cdot W_{m}\subseteq\mathcal{V}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{s}).

Therefore, it is sufficient to prove that for

v\in\mathcal{V}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{m})
, there exists
g\in G_{m}
such that

gv\in W_{m}
.

The proof is by induction on
m
. For
m=1
, we have
x_{1}(v)y_{1}(v)=0
. If
x_{1}(v)\not=0
, then take
g
to be the transposition which interchanges
x_{1}
and
y_{1}
.

For
m>1
, define
\bar{v}:=v-y_{m}(v)e_{m}-x_{m}(v)f_{m}
, let

\bar{\xi_{i}}
denote the restriction of
\xi_{i}
to

\Span_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{m-1},f_{m-1},\ldots f_{1}\}
and
identify
G_{m-1}
with the point-wise stabiliser of

{\rm Span}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\{e_{m},f_{m}\}
in
G_{m}
.

If
x_{m}(v)=0
then
\bar{\xi_{i}}(\bar{v})=0
for
i=1,\ldots,m
.
By induction, there is an element
g\in G_{m-1}<G_{m}

with
g\bar{v}\in W_{m-1}
. Observe that

gv\in W_{m}
.

For
u\in\overline{V}
we use
\Fr^{i}(u)
to denote the
i^{th}
iteration of the Frobenius map on
u
.
Recall that
\beta(u,u)=2\xi_{1}(u)
and note that, for
i\geq 2
,

\beta(u,\Fr^{i-1}(u))=\xi_{i}(u)
.

If
x_{m}(v)\not=0
then we scale
v
so that
x_{m}(v)=1
and define
w:=v-\Fr(v)
.
Observe that

\displaystyle\xi_{1}(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta(v-\Fr(v),v-\Fr(v))/2=\left(\beta(v,v)+\beta(\Fr(v),\Fr(v))-2\beta(v,\Fr(v))\right)/2

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\xi_{1}(v)+\xi_{1}(v)^{q}-\xi_{2}(v)=0

and, for
i\geq 2
,

\displaystyle\xi_{i}(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta(v-\Fr(v),\Fr^{i-1}(v-\Fr(v)))=\beta(v-\Fr(v),\Fr^{i-1}(v)-\Fr^{i}(v))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta(v,\Fr^{i-1}(v))-\beta(\Fr(v),\Fr^{i-1}(v))-\beta(v,\Fr^{i}(v))+\beta(\Fr(v),\Fr^{i}(v))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\xi_{i}(v)-\xi_{i-1}(v)^{q}-\xi_{i+1}(v)+\xi_{i}(v)^{q}=0.

Therefore
\xi_{i}(w)=0
for
i=1,\ldots,m-1
.
Since
x_{m}(w)=0
,
we have
\bar{\xi_{i}}(\bar{w})=0
for
i=1,\ldots,m-1
and so by induction
there is an element
g\in G_{m-1}<G_{m}

with
g\bar{w}\in W_{m-1}
and
gw\in W_{m}
.
Hence
(x_{j}-x_{j}^{q})(gv)=x_{j}(gw)=0
for
j=1,\ldots,m
and
x_{j}(gv)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
Since
g
stabilises
{\rm Span}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\{e_{m},f_{m}\}
, we have
x_{m}(gv)=1
.
For convenience, define
c_{j}=x_{j}(gv)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
for
j=1,\ldots,m-1
and
let
h
denote the linear transformation
given by
y_{j}h=y_{j}
for
j=1,\ldots,m-1
,
and
y_{m}h=y_{m}+\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}c_{j}y_{j}
, and
x_{j}h=x_{j}-c_{j}x_{m}
for
j=1,\ldots,m-1

and
x_{m}h=x_{m}
. Observe that
h\in G_{m}

and, since
\xi_{1}(gv)=0
, we have
y_{m}(hgv)=y_{m}(gv)+\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}c_{j}y_{j}(gv)=0
.
Let
\sigma
denote the transposition which exchanges
x_{m}
and
y_{m}
. We then have

x_{m}(\sigma hgv)=0
and we can apply the induction argument as above.
∎

It follows from this theorem that we are missing at least
m
invariants.

Remark 3.2
.

A vector
v\in\overline{V}
is isotropic if
\beta(v,v)=0
.
Since
q
is odd,
\xi_{1}(v)=\beta(v,v)/2
. Therefore the set of isotropic vectors
in
\overline{V}
is
\mathcal{V}(\xi_{1})
. For
m>1
, using Theorem
3.1
,

\mathcal{V}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{m})
is a proper subset of
\mathcal{V}(\xi_{1})
.
The group
G_{m}
acts transitively on the isotropic vectors of
V
;
for
m>1
this follows from
[
27
, Lemma 11.27]
.
Therefore
G_{m}e_{1}=\mathcal{V}(\xi_{1})\cap V
. It then follows from Theorem
3.1

that the set of isotropic vectors in
V
is

\mathcal{V}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{m})\cap V=\bigcup_{g\in G_{m}}g\cdot\Span_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{m}\}.

3.3.
Steenrod Operations

The Steenrod operations are cohomology operations widely used in algebraic topology.
They can be sensibly defined on
S_{m}
and restrict
to operations on
S_{m}^{G_{m}}
, see, for example,
[
24
]
.
Thus applying a Steenrod operation to an invariant produces a potentially new invariant.
The
complete Steenrod operator

\mathcal{P}(t):S_{m}\to S_{m}[t]
is the algebra homomorphism determined by
\mathcal{P}(t)(v)=v+v^{q}t
for
v
homogeneous of degree one. Since the map is linear in degree one,
\mathcal{P}(t)
is well-defined. For
f
homogeneous of degree
d
, the Steenrod operations

\mathcal{P}^{i}(f)
are defined by

\mathcal{P}(t)(f)=\sum_{i=0}^{d}\mathcal{P}^{i}(f)t^{i}.

Note that for
i>d
or
i<0
,
\mathcal{P}^{i}(f)=0
.
It is clear that
\mathcal{P}^{0}(f)=f
and
\mathcal{P}^{d}(f)=f^{q}
,
i.e., the
stability
property is satisfied.
The Steenrod operations satisfy the
Cartan identity
:
for
f_{1},f_{2}\in S_{m}

\mathcal{P}^{i}(f_{1}f_{2})=\sum_{j=0}^{i}\mathcal{P}^{j}(f_{1})\mathcal{P}^{i-j}(f_{2}).

The Steenrod operations also satisfy the
Adem relations
:
for
i<qj

\mathcal{P}^{i}\mathcal{P}^{j}=\sum_{k}(-1)^{i+k}\binom{(q-1)(j-k)-1}{i-qk}\mathcal{P}^{i+j-k}\mathcal{P}^{k}.

We can extend the action of
{{\text{GL}_{{n}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}}
to
S_{m}[t]

by taking
tg=t
for all
g
. Using this action,
since taking a
q^{th}
power is linear in
S_{m}
,
we see that
\mathcal{P}(t)
commutes with the
{{\text{GL}_{{n}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}}
-action
and
\mathcal{P}^{i}g=g\mathcal{P}^{i}
for all
i
.

The following lemma is a consequence of the Cartan identity.

Lemma 3.4
.

For
f\in S_{m}
, we have
\mathcal{P}^{i}(f^{q})=0
unless
q
divides
i
,
in which case
\mathcal{P}^{i}(f^{q})=(\mathcal{P}^{i/q}(f))^{q}
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Suppose
v,f\in S_{m}
with
v
homogeneous of degree one.
Then
v
divides
\mathcal{P}^{i}(vf)
for all
i
. Moreover, if
f
is a product of pairwise relatively prime linear forms, then
f
divides
\mathcal{P}^{i}(f)
.

Proof.

By definition,
v
divides
\mathcal{P}^{j}(v)
. Therefore, using the Cartan identity,
v
divides
\mathcal{P}^{i}(vf)
. The second assertion follows.
∎

Corollary 3.6
.

(a)
\mathcal{P}(t)(\xi_{1})=\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}t+\xi_{1}^{q}t^{2}
.

(b)
\mathcal{P}(t)(\xi_{2})=\xi_{2}+2\xi_{1}^{q}t+\xi_{3}t^{q}+\xi_{2}^{q}t^{q+1}
.

(c) For
i\geq 3
,

\mathcal{P}(t)(\xi_{i})=\xi_{i}+\xi_{i-1}^{q}t+\xi_{i+1}t^{q^{i-1}}+\xi_{i}^{q}t^{q^{i-1}+1}
.

Lemma 3.7
.

Suppose
b\in S_{m}
is homogeneous of degree
j>0
and
a\in S_{m}
is homogeneous of degree
i>0
.

(a)
\mathcal{P}^{i+j-1}(ab)=a^{q}\mathcal{P}^{j-1}(b)+b^{q}\mathcal{P}^{i-1}(a)
.

(b)
\mathcal{P}^{j+1}(\xi_{1}b)=\xi_{2}b^{q}+\xi_{1}^{q}\mathcal{P}^{j-1}(b)
.

(c)
\mathcal{P}^{j+3}(\xi_{1}^{2}b)=2\xi_{1}^{q}\xi_{2}b^{q}+\xi_{1}^{2q}\mathcal{P}^{j-1}(b)
.

(d)
\mathcal{P}^{2q+j-1}(\xi_{1}^{q}b)=\xi_{1}^{q^{2}}\mathcal{P}^{j-1}b
.

Proof.

Part (a) follows from the Cartan identity and the stability condition. Parts (b), (c) and (d) follow from (a) and Corollary
3.6
.
∎

3.8.
New Invariants

Let
P_{m}
denote the intersection of
G_{m}
with the upper triangular unipotent subgroup of
{{\text{GL}_{{n}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}}
.

P_{m}
is a choice of Sylow
p
-subgroup for
G_{m}
.
For
v\in S_{m}
homogenous of degree one, define
N(v)
to be the orbit product of
v
over
P_{m}
. Since
x_{m}\in S_{m}^{P_{m}}
,
N(x_{m})=x_{m}
.
We will see in Section
5
that

\deg(N(x_{i}))=q^{m-i}
and
\deg(N(y_{i}))=q^{m+i-2}
.
Define

u_{m}:=\prod_{i=1}^{m}N(y_{i})N(x_{i})

and observe that
\deg(u_{m})=(q^{m-1}+1)(1+q+\cdots+q^{m-1})
.

Remark 3.9
.

Observe that the linear factors of
u_{m}
lie in a single

G_{m}
-orbit. Furthermore, that orbit consists of the non-zero scalar
multiples of the linear factors of
u_{m}

(see the proof of Theorem
3.10
).

Since
u_{m}
is a product of pairwise relatively prime linear forms, Lemma
3.5

implies that
u_{m}
divides
\mathcal{P}^{j}(u_{m})
in
S_{m}
.
Define
e(i,m):=\sum_{j=1}^{i}q^{n-1-j}
with
n=2m
and, for
1\leq i\leq m
, define
d_{i,m}\in S_{m}
by

u_{m}d_{i,m}=\mathcal{P}^{e(i,m)}(u_{m}).

For convenience, define

Theorem 3.10
.

\psi(u_{m})/u_{m}
is the minimal polynomial of

over the field
\mathcal{F}(S_{m}^{G_{m}})

In particular,

Proof.

If we identify

\frac{|{{\text{O}^{+}_{{n}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}}|}{|{{\text{O}^{+}_{{n-2}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}}||H|}=\frac{(q^{m}-1)(q^{2m-2}-1)}{q^{m-1}-1}=(q^{m}-1)(q^{m-1}+1)=(q-1)\deg(u_{m})

Lemma 3.11

The order of

The field of fractions for the Sylow invariants was calculated in

Let

A direct calculation shows that for

Using the lex order
\lt(\xi_{j}^{q})=x_{m}^{q}y_{m}^{q^{j}}=\lt(\xi_{j+1}x_{m}^{q-1})

To subduct the tête-a-tête

as a polynomial in the generators. Therefore the tête-a-tête

Lemma 5.2

Using Equation

\psi
followed by evaluating

\displaystyle\xi_{2}^{q}-\xi_{3}x_{m}^{q-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\xi_{1}^{q}x_{m}^{q^{2}-q}-\xi_{2}x_{m}^{q^{2}-1}+\psi_{[m,1]}(\xi_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\psi_{[m,1]}(\xi_{2})+\xi_{1}^{q}x_{m}^{q^{2}-q}+x_{m}^{q^{2}-q}(\xi_{1}^{q}-\xi_{2}x_{m}^{q-1}).

Using Equation

\displaystyle\xi_{2}^{q}-\xi_{3}x_{m}^{q-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\psi_{[m,1]}(\xi_{2})+\xi_{1}^{q}x_{m}^{q^{2}-q}+x_{m}^{q^{2}-q}(\psi_{[m,1]}(\xi_{1})-\xi_{1}x_{m}^{2q-2})

Lemma 5.6

Suppose

is a subgroup of
{{\text{GL}_{{n}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}}

\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}\}\subset S_{m}^{G}
.

\mathbb{F}_{q}[y_{m}^{q},\dots,y_{1}^{q},x_{1}^{q},\ldots,x_{m}^{q}]

\{f_{1}^{e_{1}}\cdots f_{n}^{e_{n}}\mid e_{i}<q\}\subset S_{m}

is a free module of rank

.

Proof.

Since the extension
\mathbb{F}_{q}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})=\mathcal{F}(S_{m})^{G}\subset\mathcal{F}(S_{m})
is a Galois extension,
for any intermediate extension
\mathcal{F}(S_{m})^{G}\subset E\subset\mathcal{F}(S_{m})
, we have

for some subgroup
L\leq G
.
Consider

Since the pointwise stabiliser of

{{\text{GL}_{{n}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}}

,
we have
\mathcal{F}(S_{m})^{G}[y_{m}^{q},...,x_{m}^{q}]=\mathcal{F}(S_{m})
.
On the other hand,

5.20.
Computing the Borel Invariants

Let
B
denote the Borel subgroup of upper triangular matrices in
{{\text{O}^{+}_{{2m}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}}
.
The Sylow subgroup
P
is a normal subgroup of
B
and we identify
B/P
with the subgroup of diagonal matrices, which we denote by
T
.
Let
N
denote the normaliser of
T
in
{{\text{O}^{+}_{{2m}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}}
.
The Weyl group,
W:=N/T
, is isomorphic to the wreath product
C_{2}\wr\Sigma_{m}
. We can identify
W
with a subgroup of

{{\text{O}^{+}_{{2m}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}}
: the group
\Sigma_{m}
acts by permuting the hyperbolic pairs
and the additional generator, say
\omega
, interchanges
y_{1}
and
x_{1}

while fixing the other variables.
Note that
B
and
N
are not a
BN
-pair for
{{\text{O}^{+}_{{2m}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}}
.
Most of the axioms are satisfied and
{{\text{O}^{+}_{{2m}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}}=BNB

but
\omega B\omega=B
(see
[
27
, p. 85]

and
[
16
, §1.7.5]
).
The subgroup
{{\text{SO}^{+}_{{2m}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}}
contains
B
and has a
BN
-pair
with Weyl
W^{\prime}
, an index
2
subgroup of
W
. The Dynkin type of
W^{\prime}
is

D_{m}
(see
[
16
, §1.7.9, §1.3.16]
).

Let
\mathcal{H}_{0}
denote the homogeneous system of parameters for
S^{P}
given by the orbit products over
P
of the variables
and let
\mathbb{F}_{q}[\mathcal{H}_{0}]
denote the algebra generated by
\mathcal{H}_{0}
. Define
\mathcal{B}_{H}
and
\mathcal{C}
as in the proof of Theorem
5.11
.
Then

S^{P}=\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\mathcal{B}_{H}\mathcal{C}}\mathbb{F}_{q}[\mathcal{H}_{0}]\gamma.

Lemma 5.21
.

Suppose
w\in S
with
w
homogeneous of degree one and
b\in B
with
w\cdot b=cw
for some scalar
c
.
If the
P
-orbit product

has degree

then
N(w)\cdot b=c^{q^{\ell}}N(w)
.

Proof.

Since
P
is normal in
B
, for every
g\in P
, we have
\overline{g}:=b^{-1}gb\in P
.
Thus

\displaystyle N(w)b

\displaystyle=\left(\prod\{wg\mid g\in P\}\right)b=\prod\{wgb\mid g\in P\}=\prod\{wb\overline{g}\mid g\in P\}

\displaystyle=\prod\{cw\overline{g}\mid g\in P\}=c^{q^{\ell}}\prod\{wg\mid g\in P\}=c^{q^{\ell}}N(w)

as required.
∎

Since the elements of
\mathcal{H}_{0}

Let

Lemma 6.2
.

(-1)^{j}(q-1)\frac{(q-2-j)!}{j!((q-1)-2j)!}\equiv_{(p)}\Cat(j)

\displaystyle(-1)^{j}(q-1)\frac{(q-2-j)!}{j!((q-1)-2j)!}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{j+1}\frac{(q-2-j)!}{j!((q-1)-2j)!}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{j+1}}{j!}(q-2j)(q-2j+1)\cdots(q-2-j)

8.
Generation Over The Steenrod Algebra

Let
\mathcal{A}
denote the algebra of operations on
S_{m}
generated by the Steenrod operations with the product given by composition.

Theorem 8.1
.

S_{m}^{G_{m}}
is generated as an
\mathcal{A}
-algebra by
the two elements
\xi_{1}
and
d_{1,m}
.

Proof.

Let
C
denote the smallest algebra, closed under the action of the
Steenrod algebra and containing
\{\xi_{1},d_{1,m}\}
.
Clearly
C\subseteq S_{m}^{G_{m}}
.
Since
\mathcal{P}^{q^{i-1}}(\xi_{i})=\xi_{i+1}
we see that
\xi_{i+1}\in C
for all
i\geq 1
.
It remains to show that
d_{i,m}\in C
for
i=1,2,3,\dots,m
.
We proceed by induction.
Suppose then that

d_{1,m},d_{2,m},\dots,d_{i-1,m}\in C
and consider
F:=\mathcal{P}^{q^{n-i-1}}(d_{i-1,m})
of degree
q^{n-1}-q^{n-1-i}
.
Recall, from the proof of Theorem
3.12
that

d_{i-1,m}\equiv_{I}\pm d_{i-1}(W_{m})^{q^{m-1}}

where
I
is the ideal

I=\langle x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{m}\rangle
,
W_{m}={\rm Span}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\{y_{m},\ldots,y_{1}\}
,
and
d_{i-1}(W_{m})
is the
(i-1)^{\text{th}}
Dickson invariant in the variables

y_{m},\ldots,y_{1}
. Since
\mathcal{P}^{q^{m-i}}(d_{i-1}(W_{m}))=-d_{i}(W_{m})
(see

[
29
]
) it follows that

F\equiv_{I}\mathcal{P}^{q^{n-i-1}}(d_{i-1}(W_{1})^{q^{m-1}})=-d_{i}(W_{1})^{q^{m-1}}\neq 0
.
But the only invariants of degree
q^{n-1}-q^{n-1-i}
which do not vanish on
W_{m}

are scalar multiples of
d_{i,m}
. Hence
F=\pm d_{i,m}+f
where

f\equiv_{I}0
and

f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{m},d_{1,m},\ldots,d_{i-1,m}]\subseteq C
.
This shows that
d_{i,m}\in C
as required.
∎

A note on degenerate generalized Laguerre polynomials and Lah numbers
Taekyun Kim, Dmitry V. Dolgy, Dae San Kim, Hye Kyung Kim, Seong Ho Park

Abstract
. The aim of this paper is to introduce the degenerate generalized Laguerre polynomials as the degenerate version of the generalized Laguerre polynomials and to derive some properties related to those polynomials and Lah numbers, including an explicit expression, a Rodrigues’ type formula and expressions for the derivatives. The novelty of the present paper is that it is the first paper on degenerate versions of orthogonal polynomials.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
11B83; 42C05; 60E99

1.
Introduction

The generalized Laguerre polynomials are classical orthogonal polynomials which are orthogonal with respect to the gamma distribution
e^{-x}x^{\alpha}dx
on the interval
\left(0,\infty\right)
.
The generalized Laguerre polynomials are widely used in many problems of quantum mechanics, mathematical physics and engineering.
In quantum mechanics, the Schrödinger equation for the hydrogen-like atom is exactly solvable by separation of variables in spherical coordinates. The radial part of the wave function is a generalized Laguerre polynomial [14].
In mathematical physics, vibronic transitions in the Franck-Condon approximation can also be described by using Laguerre polynomials [6].
In engineering, the wave equation is solved for the time domain electric field integral equation for arbitrary shaped conducting structures by expressing the transient behaviors in terms of Laguerre polynomials [4].

The aim of this paper is to introduce the degenerate generalized Laguerre polynomials as the degenerate version of the generalized Laguerre polynomials and to derive some properties related to those polynomials and Lah numbers. In more detail, we obtain an explicit formula and a Rodrigues’ type formula for the degenerate Laguerre polynomials. We also get explicit expressions for the degenerate generalized Laguerre polynomial for
\alpha=-1
, an identity involving Lah numbers, the falling factorial moment of the degenerate Poisson random variable with parameter
\alpha
, and expressions for the derivatives of the degenerate generalized Laguerre polynomials.

We should mention here that degenerate versions of many special numbers and polynomials have been explored and many interesting results have obtained in recent years [8,10,12]. Furthermore, these have been done not only for special numbers and polynomials but also for transcendental functions like gamma functions [11]. The novelty of the present paper is that this is the first paper which treats degenerate versions of orthogonal polynomials. For the rest of this section, we will recall some necessary facts that will be used throughout this paper.

The Lagueree polynomial
L_{n}(x)
satisfies the second-order linear differential equation

xy^{\prime\prime}+(1-x)y^{\prime}+ny=0,\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [16]),

while the generalized Laguerre polynomial (or the associated Laguerre polynomial)
L_{n}^{(\alpha)}(x)
satisfies the second-order linear differential equation

xy^{\prime\prime}+(\alpha+1-x)y^{\prime}+ny=0,\quad(\alpha\in\mathbb{R}).

The Rodrigues’ formula of the Laguerre polynomial
L_{n}(x)
is given by

(1)

L_{n}(x)=\frac{e^{x}}{n!}\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}\big(e^{-x}x^{n}\big)=\frac{1}{n!}\bigg(\frac{d}{dx}-1\bigg)^{n}x^{n},

while that of the generalized Lagurerre polynomial
L_{n}^{(\alpha)}(x)
is given by

(2)

\displaystyle L_{n}^{(\alpha)}(x)\

\displaystyle=\ \frac{1}{n!}x^{-\alpha}e^{x}\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}\big(e^{-x}x^{n+\alpha}\big)

\displaystyle=\ x^{-\alpha}\frac{1}{n!}\bigg(\frac{d}{dx}-1\bigg)^{n}x^{n+\alpha},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [2,9,16,17]).

The generating function of generalized Laguerre polynomials is given by

(3)

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}L_{n}^{(\alpha)}(x)t^{n}=\frac{1}{(1-t)^{\alpha+1}}e^{-x\frac{t}{1-t}},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [9,16,17]).

From (
3
), we get

(4)

L_{n}^{(\alpha)}(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}\binom{n+\alpha}{n-i}\frac{x^{i}}{i!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [9,17]).

Note that

\displaystyle L_{0}^{(\alpha)}(x)

\displaystyle=1

\displaystyle L_{1}^{(\alpha)}(x)

\displaystyle=-x+(\alpha+1)

\displaystyle L_{2}^{(\alpha)}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{x^{2}}{2}-(\alpha+2)x+\frac{(\alpha+1)(\alpha+2)}{2},

\displaystyle L_{3}^{(\alpha)}(x)

\displaystyle=-\frac{x^{3}}{6}+\frac{\alpha+3}{2}x^{2}-\frac{(\alpha+2)(\alpha+3)}{2}x+\frac{(\alpha+1)(\alpha+2)(\alpha+3)}{6},\ \dots.

The rising factorial sequence is defined as

\langle x\rangle_{0}=1,\quad\langle x\rangle_{n}=x(x+1)\cdots(x+n-1),\quad(n\geq 1),

while the falling factorial sequence is defined as

(x)_{0}=1,\quad(x)_{n}=x(x-1)\cdots(x-n+1),\quad(n\geq 1),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [1-3,5,7-13,15-18]).

We note that the Lah numbers are defined by

(5)

\langle x\rangle_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}L(n,k)(x)_{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [3,5,8,13,15]).

From (
5
), we can easily derive the following equation.

(6)

\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(\frac{t}{1-t}\bigg)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}L(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [3,5,13,17]).

For any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, the degenerate exponential function is defined by

(7)

e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(x)_{n,\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [10,11])

where
(x)_{0,\lambda}=1,\ (x)_{n,\lambda}=x(x-\lambda)\cdots(x-(n-1)\lambda)
,
(n\geq 1)
.
For
x=1
, we use the notation by
e_{\lambda}(t)=e_{\lambda}^{1}(t)
.

2.
Degenerate generalized Laguerre polynomials

For any
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, we consider the degenerate generalized Laguerre polynomials given by

(8)

\frac{1}{(1-t)^{\alpha+1}}e_{\lambda}\bigg(-x\frac{t}{1-t}\bigg)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}L_{n,\lambda}^{(\alpha)}(x)t^{n},\quad|t|<1.

From (
7
), we note that

(9)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-t)^{\alpha+1}}e_{\lambda}\bigg(-x\frac{t}{1-t}\bigg)=\frac{1}{(1-t)^{\alpha+1}}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(1)_{m,\lambda}(-1)^{m}x^{m}\frac{1}{m!}\bigg(\frac{t}{1-t}\bigg)^{m}

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(1)_{m,\lambda}(-1)^{m}x^{m}\frac{1}{m!}t^{m}\bigg(\frac{1}{1-t}\bigg)^{m+\alpha+1}

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(1)_{m,\lambda}(-1)^{m}x^{m}\frac{t^{m}}{m!}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\binom{m+\alpha+l}{l}t^{l}

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{m=0}^{n}(1)_{m,\lambda}(-1)^{m}x^{m}\frac{1}{m!}\binom{m+\alpha+n-m}{n-m}\bigg)t^{n}

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{m=0}^{n}(1)_{m,\lambda}(-1)^{m}x^{m}\frac{1}{m!}\binom{n+\alpha}{n-m}\bigg)t^{n}.

Therefore, by (
8
) and (
9
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 1
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

L_{n,\lambda}^{(\alpha)}(x)=\sum_{m=0}^{n}\binom{n+\alpha}{n-m}(-1)^{m}(1)_{m,\lambda}\frac{1}{m!}x^{m}.

Now, by using Theorem1, we observe that

(10)

\displaystyle\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}\bigg[x^{\alpha}e_{\lambda}\bigg(-\frac{a}{x}\bigg)\bigg]=\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}\bigg[\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(1)_{k,\lambda}(-a)^{k}\frac{1}{k!}x^{\alpha-k}\bigg]

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(1)_{k,\lambda}\frac{(-a)^{k}}{k!}\overbrace{(\alpha-k)(\alpha-k-1)\cdots(\alpha-k-n+1)}^{n-times}x^{\alpha-k-n}

\displaystyle\quad=(-1)^{n}x^{\alpha-n}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(1)_{k,\lambda}(-a)^{k}\frac{1}{k!}(k-\alpha)(k-\alpha+1)\cdots(k-\alpha+n-1)x^{-k}

\displaystyle\quad=(-1)^{n}x^{\alpha-n}n!\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(1)_{k,\lambda}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}\bigg(\frac{a}{x}\bigg)^{k}\binom{k+n-\alpha-1}{n}

\displaystyle\quad=(-1)^{n}x^{\alpha-n}n!\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(1)_{k,\lambda}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}\bigg(\frac{a}{x}\bigg)^{k}\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{n-\alpha-1}{n-l}\binom{k}{l}

\displaystyle\quad=(-1)^{n}x^{\alpha-n}n!\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{n-\alpha-1}{n-l}\sum_{k=l}^{\infty}(1)_{k,\lambda}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}\bigg(\frac{a}{x}\bigg)^{k}\frac{k!}{l!(k-l)!}

\displaystyle\quad=(-1)^{n}x^{\alpha-n}n!\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{n-\alpha-1}{n-l}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(1)_{k+l,\lambda}(-1)^{k+l}\bigg(\frac{a}{x}\bigg)^{k+l}\frac{1}{l!k!}

\displaystyle\quad=(-1)^{n}x^{\alpha-n}n!\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{n-\alpha-1}{n-l}(-1)^{l}\bigg(\frac{a}{x}\bigg)^{l}\frac{1}{l!}(1)_{l,\lambda}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(1-l\lambda)_{k,\lambda}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}\bigg(\frac{a}{x}\bigg)^{k}

\displaystyle\quad=(-1)^{n}x^{\alpha-n}n!\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{n-\alpha-1}{n-l}(-1)^{l}(1)_{l,\lambda}\bigg(\frac{a}{x}\bigg)^{l}\frac{1}{l!}e_{\lambda}^{1-l\lambda}\bigg(-\frac{a}{x}\bigg)

\displaystyle\quad=(-1)^{n}x^{\alpha-n}n!e_{\lambda}\bigg(-\frac{a}{x}\bigg)\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{n-\alpha-1}{n-l}(-1)^{l}(1)_{l,\lambda}\bigg(\frac{a}{x-a\lambda}\bigg)^{l}\frac{1}{l!}

\displaystyle\quad=(-1)^{n}x^{\alpha-n}n!e_{\lambda}\bigg(-\frac{a}{x}\bigg)L_{n,\lambda}^{(-\alpha-1)}\bigg(\frac{a}{x-a\lambda}\bigg).

Therefore, by (
10
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}\bigg[x^{\alpha}e_{\lambda}\bigg(-\frac{a}{x}\bigg)\bigg]=(-1)^{n}x^{\alpha-n}n!e_{\lambda}\bigg(-\frac{a}{x}\bigg)L_{n,\lambda}^{(-\alpha-1)}\bigg(\frac{a}{x-a\lambda}\bigg).

By using Leibniz rule and Theorem 1, we have

(11)

\displaystyle\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}\big[e_{\lambda}(-x)x^{n+\alpha}\big]

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{m=0}^{n}\binom{n}{m}\bigg[\frac{d^{m}}{dx^{m}}e_{\lambda}(-x)\bigg]\bigg[\frac{d^{n-m}}{dx^{n-m}}x^{n+\alpha}\bigg]

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{m=0}^{n}\binom{n}{m}(-1)^{m}(1)_{m,\lambda}e_{\lambda}^{1-m\lambda}(-x)\cdot(n+\alpha)_{n-m}x^{n+\alpha-n+m}

\displaystyle\quad=n!e_{\lambda}(-x)x^{\alpha}\sum_{m=0}^{n}\binom{n+\alpha}{n-m}(-1)^{m}(1)_{m,\lambda}e_{\lambda}^{-m\lambda}(-x)x^{m}\frac{1}{m!}

\displaystyle\quad=n!e_{\lambda}(-x)x^{\alpha}\sum_{m=0}^{n}\binom{n+\alpha}{n-m}(-1)^{m}(1)_{m,\lambda}\bigg(\frac{x}{1-\lambda x}\bigg)^{m}\frac{1}{m!}

\displaystyle\quad=n!e_{\lambda}(-x)x^{\alpha}L_{n,\lambda}^{(\alpha)}\bigg(\frac{x}{1-\lambda x}\bigg).

Thus, we obtain Rodrigues’ type formula for the degenerate generalized Laguerre polynomials.

Theorem 3

(Rodrigues’ type formula)
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

\frac{x^{-\alpha}}{n!e_{\lambda}(-x)}\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}\big[e_{\lambda}(-x)x^{n+\alpha}\big]=L_{n,\lambda}^{(\alpha)}\bigg(\frac{x}{1-\lambda x}\bigg).

For
\alpha=-1
, from Theorem 3, we have

(12)

\frac{x}{n!e_{\lambda}(-x)}\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}\big[e_{\lambda}(-x)x^{n-1}\big]=L_{n,\lambda}^{(-1)}\bigg(\frac{x}{1-x}\bigg).

On the other hand, by (
8
), we get

(13)

e_{\lambda}\bigg(-x\frac{t}{1-t}\bigg)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}L_{n,\lambda}^{(-1)}(x)t^{n}.

From (
7
), we can derive the following equation.

(14)

\displaystyle e_{\lambda}\bigg(-x\frac{t}{1-t}\bigg)\

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}(1)_{k,\lambda}x^{k}\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(\frac{t}{1-t}\bigg)^{k}

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}(1)_{k,\lambda}x^{k}\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}L(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}(1)_{k,\lambda}x^{k}L(n,k)\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Thus, by (
13
) and (
14
), we get

(15)

L_{n,\lambda}^{(-1)}(x)=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-x)^{k}(1)_{k,\lambda}L(n,k),

where
L(n,k)=\binom{n-1}{k-1}\frac{n!}{k!}
is the Lah number.

Therefore, we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 4
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

L_{n,\lambda}^{(-1)}(x)=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-x)^{k}(1)_{k,\lambda}L(n,k)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}(1)_{k,\lambda}(-x)^{k}\frac{1}{k!}\binom{n-1}{k-1}.

From Theorem 1, we note that

(16)

\displaystyle L_{n,\lambda}^{(\alpha)}(x)\

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{m=0}^{n}(1)_{m,\lambda}x^{m}(-1)^{m}\frac{1}{m!}\binom{n+\alpha}{n-m}

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{m=0}^{n}(1)_{m,\lambda}(-x)^{m}\frac{(n+\alpha)(n+\alpha-1)\cdots(m+\alpha+1)}{m!(n-m)!}

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{m=0}^{n}(1)_{m,\lambda}(-x)^{m}\frac{(n+\alpha)(n+\alpha-1)\cdots(m+\alpha+1)(m+\alpha)\cdots(\alpha+1)}{m!(n-m)!(m+\alpha)\cdots(\alpha+1)}

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{m=0}^{n}(1)_{m,\lambda}(-x)^{m}\frac{1}{m!(n-m)!}\frac{(n+\alpha)_{n}}{(m+\alpha)_{m}}

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{m=0}^{n}(1)_{m,\lambda}(-x)^{m}\frac{1}{m!(n-m)!}\frac{(n+\alpha)_{n}}{(m+\alpha)_{m}}\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}

\displaystyle=\ \Gamma(n+\alpha+1)\sum_{m=0}^{n}(1)_{m,\lambda}\frac{(-x)^{m}}{m!(n-m)!}\frac{1}{\Gamma(m+\alpha+1)}.

Thus, by (
16
), we get

(17)

L_{n,\lambda}^{(\alpha)}(x)=\frac{\Gamma(n+\alpha+1)}{\Gamma(n+1)}\sum_{m=0}^{n}(1)_{m,\lambda}\binom{n}{m}(-x)^{m}\frac{1}{\Gamma(m+\alpha+1)}.

In particular,
\alpha=-1
, we have

(18)

\displaystyle L_{n,\lambda}^{(-1)}(x)

\displaystyle=\ \frac{1}{n}\sum_{m=0}^{n}(1)_{m,\lambda}\binom{n}{m}(-x)^{m}\frac{1}{\Gamma(m)}

\displaystyle=\ \sum_{m=1}^{n}(1)_{m,\lambda}(-x)^{m}\frac{1}{m!}\binom{n-1}{m-1}.

Now, we observe that

(19)

\displaystyle\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}e_{\lambda}\bigg(\frac{1}{x}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(1)_{k,\lambda}\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(\frac{1}{x}\bigg)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(1)_{k,\lambda}\frac{1}{k!}(-1)^{n}\langle k\rangle_{n}x^{-n-k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(1)_{k,\lambda}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{k!}\sum_{l=0}^{n}L(n,l)(k)_{l}x^{-n-k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{n}(-1)^{n}L(n,l)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(1)_{k,\lambda}\frac{(k)_{l}}{k!}x^{-n-k}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}x^{-n}\sum_{l=0}^{n}L(n,l)\sum_{k=l}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{k,\lambda}}{k!}(k)_{l}x^{-k}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}x^{-n}\sum_{l=0}^{n}L(n,l)\sum_{k=l}^{\infty}\frac{k!}{k!(k-l)!}(1)_{k,\lambda}x^{-k}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}x^{-n}\sum_{l=0}^{n}L(n,l)x^{-l}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{k+l,\lambda}}{k!}x^{-k}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}x^{-n}\sum_{l=0}^{n}L(n,l)x^{-l}(1)_{l,\lambda}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1-l\lambda)_{k,\lambda}}{k!}x^{-k}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}x^{-n}\sum_{l=0}^{n}L(n,l)x^{-l}(1)_{l,\lambda}e_{\lambda}^{1-l\lambda}\bigg(\frac{1}{x}\bigg)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}x^{-n}e_{\lambda}\bigg(\frac{1}{x}\bigg)\sum_{l=0}^{n}L(n,l)(1)_{l,\lambda}x^{-l}\bigg(1+\frac{\lambda}{x}\bigg)^{-l}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}x^{-n}e_{\lambda}\bigg(\frac{1}{x}\bigg)\sum_{l=0}^{n}L(n,l)(1)_{l,\lambda}\bigg(\frac{1}{x+\lambda}\bigg)^{l}.

Therefore, by (
19
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 5
.

For
n\geq 1
, we have

\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}e_{\lambda}\bigg(\frac{1}{x}\bigg)=(-1)^{n}x^{-n}e_{\lambda}\bigg(\frac{1}{x}\bigg)\sum_{l=0}^{n}L(n,l)(1)_{l,\lambda}\bigg(\frac{1}{x+\lambda}\bigg)^{l}.

Since

L(n,k)=\binom{n-1}{k-1}\frac{n!}{k!}=\binom{n-1}{k-1}\frac{n!}{(n-k)!k!}(n-k)!=\binom{n-1}{k-1}\binom{n}{k}(n-k)!.

Thus, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 6
.

For
n\geq 1
, we have

\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}e_{\lambda}\bigg(\frac{1}{x}\bigg)=x^{-n}(-1)^{n}e_{\lambda}\bigg(\frac{1}{x}\bigg)\sum_{l=1}^{n}\binom{n-1}{l-1}\binom{n}{l}(1)_{l,\lambda}(n-l)!\bigg(\frac{1}{x+\lambda}\bigg)^{l}.

3.
Degenerate Poisson random variables

Let
X
be the Poisson random variable with parameter
\alpha(>0)
. Then the probability mass function of
X
is given by

p(i)=P\{X=i\}=\frac{\alpha^{i}}{i!}e^{-\alpha},\quad(i=0,1,2,\dots).

It is easy to show that

E\big[(X)_{n}\big]=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(k)_{n}p(k)=e^{-\alpha}\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{k}}{(k-n)!}=e^{-\alpha}\alpha^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{k}}{k!}=\alpha^{n}.

Thus, we note that

E\bigg[\binom{X}{n}\bigg]=\frac{\alpha^{n}}{n!},\quad(n=0,1,2,\dots).

Let
X_{\lambda}
be the degenerate Poisson random variable with parameter
\alpha(>0)
. Then the probability mass function of
X_{\lambda}
is given by

p(i)=P\{X_{\lambda}=i\}=e_{\lambda}^{-1}(\alpha)\frac{\alpha^{i}}{i!}(1)_{i,\lambda},\quad(i=0,1,2,\dots),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [12]).

Then the following falling factorial moment is given by

(20)

\displaystyle E\Big[\big(X_{\lambda}\big)_{n}\Big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(k)_{n}p(k)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(k)_{n}\frac{e_{\lambda}^{-1}(\alpha)}{k!}\alpha^{k}(1)_{k,\lambda}

\displaystyle=e_{\lambda}^{-1}(\alpha)\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{k(k-1)\cdots(k-n+1)(k-n)!}{k!(k-n)!}\alpha^{k}(1)_{k,\lambda}

\displaystyle=e_{\lambda}^{-1}(\alpha)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\alpha^{k+n}\frac{1}{k!}(1)_{k+n,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\alpha^{n}e_{\lambda}^{-1}(\alpha)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(1)_{n,\lambda}(1-n\lambda)_{k,\lambda}\frac{\alpha^{k}}{k!}

\displaystyle=\alpha^{n}e_{\lambda}^{-1}(\alpha)(1)_{n,\lambda}e_{\lambda}^{1-n\lambda}(\alpha)=\alpha^{n}(1)_{n,\lambda}\bigg(\frac{1}{1+\alpha\lambda}\bigg)^{n}.

Assume that
X_{\lambda}
is the Poisson random variable with parameter
\frac{1}{\alpha}(>0)
. Then, by using (
20
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d^{n}}{d\alpha^{n}}\Bigg(e_{\lambda}\bigg(\frac{1}{\alpha}\bigg)\Bigg)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}\alpha^{-n}\sum_{l=0}^{n}L(n,l)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(k)_{l}}{k!}(1)_{k,\lambda}\alpha^{-k}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}\alpha^{-n}\sum_{l=0}^{n}L(n,l)e_{\lambda}\bigg(\frac{1}{\alpha}\bigg)e_{\lambda}^{-1}\bigg(\frac{1}{\alpha}\bigg)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(k)_{l}}{k!}(1)_{k,\lambda}\alpha^{-k}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}\alpha^{-n}\sum_{l=0}^{n}L(n,l)e_{\lambda}\bigg(\frac{1}{\alpha}\bigg)E\Big[\big(X_{\lambda}\big)_{l}\Big]

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}\alpha^{-n}e_{\lambda}\bigg(\frac{1}{\alpha}\bigg)\sum_{l=0}^{n}L(n,l)(1)_{l,\lambda}\bigg(\frac{1}{\alpha+\lambda}\bigg)^{l}.

4.
Derivatives of degenerate Laguerre polynomials

Let us consider the sequence
y_{n,\lambda}(x)
which are given by

(21)

A(t)e_{\lambda}\bigg(-x\frac{t}{1-t}\bigg)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}y_{n,\lambda}(x)t^{n},

where
A(t)
is an invertible series.

Note that
y_{0,\lambda}(x)=A(0)
is a constant. We now set
\displaystyle F_{\lambda}=F_{\lambda}(x,t)=A(t)e_{\lambda}\bigg(-\frac{x}{1-t}t\bigg)\displaystyle
.

From (
21
), we note that

(22)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial x}F_{\lambda}\

\displaystyle=\ A(t)\bigg(-\frac{t}{1-t}\bigg)e_{\lambda}^{1-\lambda}\bigg(-\frac{xt}{1-t}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\ -\bigg(\frac{t}{(1-t)-x\lambda t}\bigg)A(t)e_{\lambda}\bigg(-\frac{xt}{1-t}\bigg).

By (
22
), we get

(23)

\frac{\partial}{\partial x}F_{\lambda}-(1+x\lambda)t\frac{\partial}{\partial x}F_{\lambda}=-tF_{\lambda}.

From (
21
) and (
23
), we can derive the following equation

(24)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}y_{n,\lambda}^{\prime}(x)t^{n}-(1+x\lambda)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}y_{n-1,\lambda}^{\prime}(x)t^{n}=-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}y_{n-1,\lambda}(x)t^{n}.

By comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
24
), we get

(25)

y_{n,\lambda}^{\prime}(x)-(1+x{\lambda})y_{n-1,\lambda}^{\prime}(x)=-y_{n-1,\lambda}(x),\quad(n\geq 1),

where
y_{n,\lambda}^{\prime}(x)=\frac{d}{dx}y_{n,\lambda}(x)
.

Now, we observe that

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1-t)-x\lambda t}

\displaystyle=-\frac{t}{1-t}\Bigg(\frac{1}{1-\frac{x\lambda}{1-t}t}\Bigg)=-\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}x^{l}\lambda^{l}\bigg(\frac{t}{1-t}\bigg)^{l+1}

(26)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}x^{l-1}\lambda^{l-1}\bigg(\frac{t}{1-t}\bigg)^{l}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}x^{l-1}\lambda^{l-1}t^{l}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\binom{m+l-1}{m}t^{m}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{l=1}^{k}x^{l-1}\lambda^{l-1}\binom{n-1}{n-l}\bigg)t^{k}.

From (
22
) and (
26
), we can derive the following equation (
27
)

(27)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}y_{n,\lambda}^{\prime}(x)t^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial x}F_{\lambda}=-\bigg(\frac{t}{1-t-x\lambda t}\bigg)A(t)e_{\lambda}\bigg(-\frac{xt}{1-t}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\bigg(-\sum_{l=1}^{k}x^{l-1}\lambda^{l-1}\binom{k-1}{k-l}\bigg)t^{k}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}y_{m,\lambda}t^{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigg(-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{l=1}^{k}x^{l-1}\lambda^{l-1}\binom{k-1}{k-l}y_{n-k,\lambda}(x)\bigg)t^{n}.

Thus, by comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
27
), we get

(28)

y_{n,\lambda}^{\prime}(x)=-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{l=1}^{k}x^{l-1}\lambda^{l-1}\binom{k-1}{k-l}y_{n-k,\lambda}(x).

Therefore, we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 7
.

Let

A(t)e_{\lambda}\bigg(-\frac{xt}{1-t}\bigg)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}y_{n,\lambda}(x)t^{n},

where
A(t)
is an invertible series.
For
n\geq 1
, we have

y^{\prime}_{n,\lambda}(x)=(1+x\lambda)y_{n-1,\lambda}^{\prime}(x)-y_{n-1,\lambda}(x)

and

y^{\prime}_{n,\lambda}(x)=-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{l=1}^{k}x^{l-1}\lambda^{l-1}\binom{k-1}{k-l}y_{n-k,\lambda}(x),

where
\displaystyle y_{n}^{\prime}(x)=\frac{d}{dx}y_{n,\lambda}(x)\displaystyle
.

5.
Conclusion

In this paper, we introduced the degenerate generalized Laguerre polynomials, which are the first degenerate versions of the orthogonal polynomials, and derived some results related to those polynomials and Lah numbers. Some of the results are an explicit expression, Rodrigues’ type formula and some expressions for the derivatives of the degenerate generalized Laguerre polynomials.

As one of our future projects, we would like to continue to study degenerate versions of many special numbers and polynomials.

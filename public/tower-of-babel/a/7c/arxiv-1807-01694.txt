Semicontinuity of Structure for Small Sumsets in Compact Abelian Groups
John T. Griesmer

Abstract
We study pairs of subsets A , B A,B of a compact abelian group G G where the sumset A + B := { a + b : a ∈ A , b ∈ B } A+B:=\{a+b:a\in A,b\in B\} is small. Let m m and m ∗ m_{*} be Haar measure and inner Haar measure on G G , respectively. Given ε > 0 \varepsilon>0 , we classify all pairs A , B A,B of Haar measurable subsets of G G satisfying m ⁡ ( A ) , m ⁡ ( B ) > ε m(A),m(B)>\varepsilon and m ∗ ​ ( A + B ) ≤ m ⁡ ( A ) + m ⁡ ( B ) + δ m_{*}(A+B)\leq m(A)+m(B)+\delta where δ = δ ⁡ ( ε ) \delta=\delta(\varepsilon) is small. We also study the case where the δ \delta -popular sumset A + δ B := { t ∈ G : m ( A ∩ ( t − B ) ) > δ } A+_{\delta}B:=\{t\in G:m(A\cap(t-B))>\delta\} is small. We prove that for all ε > 0 \varepsilon>0 , there is a δ > 0 \delta>0 such that if A A and B B are subsets of a compact abelian group G G having m ⁡ ( A ) , m ⁡ ( B ) > ε m(A),m(B)>\varepsilon and m ( A + δ B ) ≤ m ( A ) + m ( B ) + δ m(A+_{\delta}B)\leq m(A)+m(B)+\delta , then there are sets S , T ⊆ G S,T\subseteq G such that m ⁡ ( A ​ △ ​ S ) + m ⁡ ( B ​ △ ​ T ) < ε m(A\triangle S)+m(B\triangle T)<\varepsilon and m ⁡ ( S + T ) ≤ m ⁡ ( S ) + m ⁡ ( T ) m(S+T)\leq m(S)+m(T) . Appealing to known results, the latter inequality yields strong structural information on S S and T T , and therefore on A A and B B .

If

-measurable. The structure of Haar measurable sets

For

Theorem 1.1

For all

Notation 1.2
.

Let

For any given set

If

If

Lemma 1.3
.

Let

be an abelian group and
\mu
a translation invariant probability measure on

. If

preserves

. Furthermore, if

Our results relate subsets of a given compact abelian group

Definition 1.4

(Bohr intervals and cyclic progressions)
.

Let
\mathbb{T}:=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
with the usual topology. The elements of

are
a+\mathbb{Z}
, where

, so every subset of

, where

Let

.

and

-measurable homomorphism
\tau:G\to\mathbb{T}
and intervals

such that

and

.

If

, we say that

is an

-cyclic progression

Remark 1.5

An

Notation 1.6
.

We write
C_{N}
for the subgroup
\{\frac{j}{N}:j\in\mathbb{Z}\}\subseteq\mathbb{T}

is isomorphic to

Lemma 1.7
.

If

is an interval, then
N\lambda(I)-1\leq|C_{N}\cap I|\leq N\lambda(I)+1
. Consequently, if
\tau:G\to C_{N}
is a surjective

-measurable homomorphism, then
\tilde{I}:=\tau^{-1}(I)
is an
N
-cyclic progression satisfying
\lambda(I)-\frac{1}{N}\leq\mu(\tilde{I})\leq\lambda(I)+\frac{1}{N}

Proof.

Haar measure on

is normalized counting measure, so by Lemma
1.3
it suffices to estimate the cardinality of

. To do this we identify
I\cap C_{N}
with a set of the form
[a,b]\cap\{\frac{j}{N}:j\in\mathbb{Z}\}
contained in
\mathbb{R}
, where

[Na,Nb]\cap\mathbb{Z}
, so we use the estimate

, which holds for all

For the following definitions, fix an abelian group
G
with a translation invariant probability measure
\mu
, and let
\mu_{*}
be the associated inner measure.

Definition 1.12

(Periodicity)
.

Given a subgroup
K\leq G
, a set
A\subseteq G
is a union of cosets of
K
if and only if
A=A+K
. The latter equation will abbreviate the assertion “
A
is a union of cosets of
K
”. We may call such a set
A

periodic with respect to

K
.

If
A\sim_{\mu}A+K
, we say that
A
is
essentially periodic with respect to

K

A
is essentially periodic with respect to
K
, then

is
\mu
-similar to a union of cosets of
K
, but the converse does not hold. For example, if
G
is an infinite compact abelian group with Haar probability measure
\mu
,
K\leq G
is a proper open subgroup,
c\notin K
, and
A=K\cup\{c\}
, then
A\sim_{\mu}K
but
A\nsim_{\mu}A+K
.

The following definition describes sets which are close to being periodic. It is stronger than merely insisting that a given set has small symmetric difference with a periodic set.

Definition 1.13
.

If
K\leq G
is a
\mu
-measurable subgroup with
\mu(K)>0
and
\varepsilon\geq 0
, a set
A\subseteq G
is
\varepsilon
-periodic with respect to
K

\mu(A+K)-\mu_{*}(A)\leq\varepsilon\mu(K)
.

The term “quasi-periodic” is introduced in
[

]

[
23
]
to describe pairs of sets satisfying

. Here is the natural generalization of the term to a group with a translation invariant measure.

Definition 1.14

(Quasi-periodicity)

Let
K\leq G
be a
\mu
-measurable subgroup with
\mu(K)>0
. We say that
A\subseteq G
is

with respect to

if
A
can be partitioned into two sets
A_{1}
and
A_{0}
, where
A_{0}\neq\varnothing
,
(A_{1}+K)\cap A_{0}=\varnothing

, and

is contained in a coset of

, the expression

is called a

with respect to

, if the group

If

, we say that

is
\varepsilon
-quasi periodic with respect to

, where

,

is

If we say that “

For a compact abelian group
G

Theorem 1.15
.

For all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
and

such that if
G
is a compact abelian group with Haar probability measure

and

,

, and

(I)

A+B
is
\varepsilon
-periodic with respect to
K

m(A+B+K)\leq m(A+K)+m(B+K)

(II)

is not
\varepsilon
-periodic with respect to

,

and

\varepsilon

,

We prove Theorems

Part 1.
Classify all pairs of
\mu
-measurable sets

To prove Propositions

3
Summary of known inverse theorems

We summarize some known inverse theorems for sumsets in a locally compact abelian (LCA) group
G
. Our proofs proceed by transferring these results to ultraproducts of compact abelian groups.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
G
be an abelian group and
A\subseteq G
. The group
H(A):=\{g\in G:A+g=A\}
is called the
stabilizer
of
A
.

Note that
A+H(A)=A
, and
H(A)
is the largest set
S
such that
A+S=A
. Consequently, if
H(A)
is open, then
A
is open as well.

The next three results are due to M. Kneser.

Theorem 3.2

(
[
29
]
, Satz 1)
.

Let
G
be a locally compact abelian group with Haar measure
m
. If
A,B\subseteq G
are
m
-measurable sets satisfing
m_{*}(A+B)<m(A)+m(B)
then
H:=H(A+B)
is compact and open (so
A+B
is clopen and therefore measurable), and

m(A+B)=m(A+H)+m(B+H)-m(H).

(3.1)

Equation (
3.1
) plays a crucial role in our arguments; some of its consequences are developed in §
9
.

If
G
is a connected LCA group then
G
has no proper open subgroup, so the next corollary follows from Theorem
3.2
.

Corollary 3.3
.

If
G
is a connected LCA group with Haar measure
m
and
A,B\subseteq G
are
m
-measurable sets, then
m_{*}(A+B)\geq\min\{m(G),m(A)+m(B)\}
.

When
G
is compact, the following theorem classifies the pairs of
m
-nonnull sets where equality holds in Corollary
3.3
. We use terminology from Definition
1.4
to state it.

Theorem 3.4

(
[
29
]
, Satz 2)
.

If
G
is a connected compact abelian group with Haar measure
m
and
A,B\subseteq G
are
m
-measurable sets satisfying
m(A),m(B)>0
and

m_{*}(A+B)=m(A)+m(B)<1,

then there are parallel Bohr intervals
A^{\prime},B^{\prime}\subseteq G
with
m(A^{\prime})=m(A)
,
m(B^{\prime})=m(B)
, and
A\subseteq A^{\prime}
,
B\subseteq B^{\prime}
.

The next result extends Theorem
3.4
to disconnected groups. It is a consequence of Theorem 1.3 in
[
19
]
, classifying the pairs of subsets
A,B
of a compact abelian group where
m_{*}(A+B)=m(A)+m(B)
. We could apply those results from
[
19
]
directly, but we find it easier to use the following theorem, which presents the same result in a more convenient organization. Its derivation from the results of
[
19
]
is explained in Remark
3.7
. Here we use terminology and notation from §
1
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
G
be a compact abelian group with Haar probability measure
m
and let
A,B\subseteq G
be
m
-measurable sets with
m(A),m(B)>0
and
m_{*}(A+B)=m(A)+m(B)
. Then there is a compact open subgroup
K\leq G
(possibly
K=G
) such that exactly one of the following holds.

(I)

A+B\sim_{m}A+B+K
,

(II)

A+B\nsim_{m}A+B+K
, and
A
and
B
have quasi-periodic decompositions
A=A_{1}\cup A_{0}
,
B=B_{1}\cup B_{0}
with respect to
K
, where at least one of
A_{1}
or
B_{1}
is nonempty and
m_{*}(A_{0}+B_{0})=m(A_{0})+m(B_{0})
, or

(III)

A+B\nsim_{m}A+B+K
, and there are parallel Bohr intervals
A^{\prime},B^{\prime}\subseteq K
having
m(A^{\prime})=m(A)
,
m(B^{\prime})=m(B)
and
a,b\in G
such that
A\subseteq a+A^{\prime}
and
B\subseteq b+B^{\prime}
.

While the possibilities (I)-(III) are mutually exclusive for a given subgroup
K
, a pair
A,B
may satisfy (I) with a given group
K
and satisfy (II) with a different subgroup
K^{\prime}
in place of
K
.

Remark 3.6
.

The equation
m_{*}(A_{0}+B_{0})=m(A_{0})+m(B_{0})
in conclusion (II) leads to Corollary
10.15
, which seems indispensable to our approach.

Remark 3.7
.

Theorem 1.3 of
[
19
]
has the same hypothesis as Theorem
3.5
here. To see how Theorem
3.5
follows, we list here the four conclusions (P), (E), (K), and (QP) from
[
19
]
and explain how each implies one of (I)-(III) in Theorem
3.5
.

(P)

There is a compact open subgroup
K\leq G
with
A\sim_{m}A+K
and
B\sim_{m}B+K
.

If
A
and
B
satisfy (P) then
A
and
B
satisfy (I) in Theorem

. To see this, note that if

, then for every coset
K^{\prime}
of

or

. Thus
A+B\sim_{m}A+B+K
, meaning (I) is satisfied in Theorem
3.5
.

(E)

There are measurable sets
A^{\prime}\supseteq A
and
B^{\prime}\supseteq B
such that

m(A^{\prime})+m(B^{\prime})>m(A)+m(B),\textit{ and }m_{*}(A^{\prime}+B^{\prime})=m_{*}(A+B).

In this case

, so Theorem
3.2
implies the stabilizer
K:=H(A^{\prime}+B^{\prime})
is compact and open. The containment
A+B\subseteq A^{\prime}+B^{\prime}
then implies
A+B\sim_{m}A+B+K
, meaning
A,B
, and

satisfy (I) in Theorem
3.5
.

(K)

There is a compact open subgroup
K\leq G
, parallel Bohr intervals
\tilde{I},\tilde{J}\subseteq G
, and
a,b\in G
such that
A\subseteq a+\tilde{I}
,
B\subseteq b+\tilde{J}
, while
m(A)=m(\tilde{I})
,
m(B)=m(\tilde{J})
.

If a pair
A,B
satisfies (K) and does not satisfy conclusion (I) of Theorem
3.5
, then it is easy to verify that
A,B
satisfies conclusion (III).

(QP)

There is a compact open subgroup
K\leq G
and partitions
A=A_{1}\cup A_{0}
,
B=B_{1}\cup B_{0}
such that
A_{0}\neq\varnothing
,
B_{0}\neq\varnothing
, at least one of
A_{1}\neq\varnothing
,
B_{1}\neq\varnothing
, and

(QP.1)

A_{1}+K\sim_{m}A_{1}
,
B_{1}+K\sim_{m}B_{1}
, while
A_{0}
and
B_{0}
are each contained in a coset of
K
and
(A_{1}+K)\cap A_{0}=(B_{1}+K)\cap B_{0}=\varnothing
;

(QP.2)

A_{0}+B_{0}+K
is a unique expression element of
A+B+K
in
G/K
;

(QP.3)

m_{*}(A_{0}+B_{0})=m(A_{0})+m(B_{0})
.

If
A,B
satisfies (QP) and
A+B\nsim_{m}A+B+K
, then it is easy to check that
A,B
satisfies conclusion (II) of Theorem
3.5
here.

Remark 3.8
.

The pairs of subsets of a discrete abelian group satisfying
|A+B|<|A|+|B|
are classified in
[
27
]
(see
[
22
]
for exposition). The pairs satisfying

are classified in
[

. These results can be combined with Theorem

to yield additional detail, as the inequality

.

4
Characters of ultraproduct groups

We will use ultraproducts and Loeb measure throughout the remainder of this article; see Appendix
A
for a summary and references. Here we briefly summarize notation.

If
\mathcal{U}
is a nonprincipal ultrafilter on
\mathbb{N}
and
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of sets, we write
\prod_{n\to\mathcal{U}}X_{n}
for the corresponding ultraproduct, which we denote by
\mathbf{X}
. Ultraproducts and internal sets will be denoted with boldface letters. If
Y
is a compact Hausdorff topological space,
(y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of elements of
Y
, and
\mathcal{U}
is an ultrafilter on
\mathbb{N}
, we write
\lim_{n\to\mathcal{U}}y_{n}
for the unique element
y\in Y
such that for every neighborhood
V
of
y
,
\{n\in\mathbb{N}:y_{n}\in V\}\in\mathcal{U}
. For a sequence of functions
f_{n}:X_{n}\to Y
, we denote by
\lim_{n\to\mathcal{U}}f_{n}
the function from
\prod_{n\to\mathcal{U}}X_{n}
to

defined by

, where
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a representative of
\mathbf{x}
.

Note that some authors use “
\lim_{n\to\mathcal{U}}
” for what we call “
\prod_{n\to\mathcal{U}}
”.

For this section, fix a sequence of compact abelian groups

with Haar probability measure
m_{n}
and a nonprincipal ultrafilter
\mathcal{U}
on
\mathbb{N}
. Let
\mathbf{G}
be the ultraproduct
\prod_{n\to\mathcal{U}}G_{n}
and

the Loeb measure corresponding to

. As usual

denotes the circle group

[
12
]
or
[
36
]
. If
G
is a compact abelian group,

denotes its Pontryagin dual, the group of continuous homomorphisms

Corollary 4.8

Proof.

By Lemma
4.6
, a surjective homomorphism
\Phi:\prod_{n\to\mathcal{U}}\widehat{G}_{n}\to\widehat{\mathbf{G}}
may be defined by
\Phi(\bm{\chi}):=\lim_{n\to\mathcal{U}}\chi_{n}
, where
(\chi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a representative of
\bm{\chi}
. Lemma
4.7
implies that the kernel of
\Phi
consists only of a single element, so
\Phi
is one-to-one. Thus
\Phi
is an isomorphism.
∎

Corollary
4.8
allows us to identify the ultraproducts
\mathbf{G}
of compact groups where
\widehat{\mathbf{G}}
is torsion free.

Lemma 4.9
.

For a fixed
k
, the group
\widehat{\mathbf{G}}
has an element of order
k
if and only if for
\mathcal{U}
-many
n
,
\widehat{G}_{n}
has an element of order
k
. Consequently,
\widehat{\mathbf{G}}
is torsion free if and only if for all
k\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\}
and
\mathcal{U}
-many
n
,
\widehat{G}_{n}
does not have an element of order
k
.

Proof.

This follows immediately from the definition of
\prod_{n\to\mathcal{U}}\widehat{G}_{n}
and Corollary
4.8
.
∎

Corollary 4.10
.

Let
H
be a finite group and
\tau:\mathbf{G}\to H
a surjective
\mu
-measurable homomorphism. Then there are continuous homomorphisms
\tau_{n}:G_{n}\to H
such that
\tau=\lim_{n\to\mathcal{U}}\tau_{n}
. If
K_{n}=\ker\tau_{n}
, then for
\mathcal{U}
-many
n
, we have
G_{n}/K_{n}\cong H
. Furthermore
\ker\tau=\prod_{n\to\mathcal{U}}K_{n}
.

Corollary
4.10
says that every
\mu
-measurable finite index subgroup of
\mathbf{G}
is internal.

Proof.

Every finite abelian group is a product of finite cyclic groups, so it suffices to prove the special case of the corollary where
H
is a finite cyclic group. In this case we consider
H
as the subgroup of
\mathcal{S}^{1}
generated by
\exp(2\pi i/N)
, where
N
is the order of
H
. Note that
H
is the unique subgroup of
\mathcal{S}^{1}
having order
N
, and that if
z\in\mathcal{S}^{1}
satisfies
z^{N}=1
, then
z\in H
. We consider
\tau
as a homomorphism into
\mathcal{S}^{1}
with image
H
. Lemma
4.6
then implies
\tau=\lim_{n\to\mathcal{U}}\chi_{n}
for some
\chi_{n}\in\widehat{G}_{n}
. Let
\tau^{N}
be the homomorphism defined by
\tau^{N}(\mathbf{g}):=\tau(\mathbf{g})^{N}
, and similarly define
\chi_{n}^{N}.
Continuity of the map
z\mapsto z^{N}
implies
\tau^{N}=\lim_{n\to\mathcal{U}}\chi_{n}^{N}
, and
\tau^{N}
is the trivial homomorphism, as the image of
\tau
is generated by an element of order
N
. Lemma
4.7
then implies
\chi_{n}^{N}
is trivial for
\mathcal{U}
-many
n
, meaning
\chi_{n}(G_{n})\subseteq H
for
\mathcal{U}
-many
n
. The hypothesis
\tau(\mathbf{G})=H
then implies
|\tau_{n}(G_{n})|=|H|
for
\mathcal{U}
-many
n
, and therefore
\tau_{n}(G_{n})=H
for
\mathcal{U}
-many
n
. Thus
G_{n}/K_{n}\cong H
for
\mathcal{U}
-many
n
.

To see that
\ker\tau=\prod_{n\to\mathcal{U}}K_{n}
, let
\mathbf{g}\in\mathbf{G}
, choose a representative
(g_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of
\mathbf{g}
, and note that the finiteness of
H
implies
\lim_{n\to\mathcal{U}}\tau_{n}(g_{n})=0
if and only if
\tau_{n}(g_{n})=0
for
\mathcal{U}
-many
n
.
∎

The next definition provides a useful splitting of
L^{2}(\mu)
.

Definition 4.11
.

Let
L^{2}(\mu)_{c}
denote the closure of
\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{G})
in
L^{2}(\mu)
. If
f\in L^{2}(\mu)
, write
f^{(c)}
for the orthogonal projection of
f
onto
L^{2}(\mu)_{c}
, and
f^{(wm)}
for
f-f^{(c)}
. Thus
f^{(wm)}\perp\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{G})
. Of course
f^{(c)}
and
f^{(wm)}
are defined only up to equality on
\mu
-null sets, but for our purposes this ambiguity can be resolved by choosing a genuine function to represent
f^{(c)}
.

The “
c
” in the notation above is to indicate that the translates
\{f_{\mathbf{t}}:\mathbf{t}\in\mathbf{G}\}
of a bounded function form a precompact subset of
L^{2}(\mu)
if and only if
f\in L^{2}(\mu)_{c}
.

The following lemma is a collection of common facts about the orthogonal projection of a function onto a space spanned by an algebra of functions, but they are often not stated explicitly for our setting.

Lemma 4.12
.

Let
f\in L^{2}(\mu)
.

(i)

If
f(\mathbf{x})\geq 0
for
\mu
-almost every
\mathbf{x}
, then
f^{(c)}(\mathbf{x})\geq 0
for
\mu
-almost every
\mathbf{x}
.

(ii)

If
f:\mathbf{G}\to[0,1]
, then
f^{(c)}(\mathbf{x})\in[0,1]
for
\mu
-almost every
\mathbf{x}
.

(iii)

\int f^{(c)}\,d\mu=\int f\,d\mu
.

(iv)

If
A\subseteq\mathbf{G}
is
\mu
-measurable, then
A\subset_{\mu}\{\mathbf{x}:1_{A}^{(c)}(\mathbf{x})>0\}
.

(v)

If
f:\mathbf{G}\to[0,1]
then
\{\mathbf{x}:f(\mathbf{x})>0\}\subset_{\mu}\{\mathbf{x}:f^{(c)}(\mathbf{x})>0\}
.

Proof.

(i) Suppose
f
is a nonnegative element of
L^{2}(\mu)
. We will prove that there is a sequence of nonnegative elements of
\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{G})
converging in
L^{2}(\mu)
to
f^{(c)}
, which implies
f^{(c)}
is
\mu
-almost everywhere nonnegative. Let
h_{n}
be any sequence of elements of
\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{G})
converging to
f^{(c)}
in
L^{2}(\mu)
. Since
\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{G})
is a uniformly closed algebra of functions containing the constant functions
0
and
1_{\mathbf{G}}
, we have that for all
h\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{G})
,
h^{+}:=\sup\{h,0\}\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{G})
, by Lemma 8 in Chapter 6 of
[
6
]
. Since
f
is nonnegative we have
\|f-h_{n}^{+}\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}\leq\|f-h_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}
for each
n
, so
h_{n}^{+}
converges to
f^{(c)}
, as
f^{(c)}
is the unique element
h
in
L^{2}(\mu)_{c}
which minimizes
\|f-h\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}
.

Part (ii) follows from Part (i) and the nonnegativity of the functions
1_{\mathbf{G}}-f
and
f
.

Part (iii) is a consequence of the fact that
\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{G})
contains the constant functions on
\mathbf{G}
.

To prove Part (iv), we use the identity
\int f\cdot g^{(c)}\,d\mu=\int f^{(c)}\cdot g^{(c)}\,d\mu
, which follows immediately from the fact that the map
f\mapsto f^{(c)}
is an orthogonal projection. Let
E=A\setminus\{\mathbf{x}:1_{A}^{(c)}(\mathbf{x})>0\}
. Then
1_{E}1_{A}^{(c)}=0
, so that
\int 1_{E}^{(c)}1_{A}^{(c)}\,d\mu=\int 1_{E}1_{A}^{(c)}\,d\mu=0
. Now
0\leq 1_{E}\leq 1_{A}\leq 1_{\mathbf{G}}
, so Part (i) implies
0\leq 1_{E}^{(c)}\leq 1_{A}^{(c)}
. Combining this last inequality with the integral
\int 1_{E}^{(c)}1_{A}^{(c)}\,d\mu=0
, we get that
\int 1_{E}^{(c)}\,d\mu=0
, which by Part (iii) implies
\mu(E)=0
.

Part (v) follows from Part (iv), the linearity of the map
f\mapsto f^{(c)}
, and bounding
f
below by linear combinations of characteristic functions supported on
\{x:f(x)>0\}
.
∎

Lemma 4.13
.

If
f,g:\mathbf{G}\to\mathbb{C}
are bounded
\mu
-measurable functions, then
f*g\equiv_{\mu}f^{(c)}*g^{(c)}
.

Proof.

Write
f
as
f^{(c)}+f^{(wm)}
. Lemma
4.12
implies
f^{(c)}
is bounded, so Lemma
4.5
implies
f^{(wm)}
is a bounded element of
L^{2}(\mu)_{wm}
, and we get that
f^{(wm)}*g\equiv_{\mu}0
by Definition
4.4
. Thus
f*g\equiv_{\mu}f^{(c)}*g
, and by symmetry we get that
f^{(c)}*g\equiv_{\mu}f^{(c)}*g^{(c)}
.
∎

The conclusion of Lemma
4.13
cannot be improved to assert
f*g=f^{(c)}*g^{(c)}
. See Remark
5.2
for elaboration.

The following proposition is part of Theorem 6 of
[
43
]
, cosmetically modified to deal with two functions on
\mathbf{G}
instead of one.

Proposition 4.14
.

If
f,g\in L^{2}(\mu)_{c}
are bounded functions, then there is a compact metrizable abelian group
G
, a surjective measure preserving quotient map
\pi:\mathbf{G}\to G
, and bounded Borel functions
\tilde{f},\tilde{g}
on
G
such that
f\equiv_{\mu}\tilde{f}\circ\pi
and
g\equiv_{\mu}\tilde{g}\circ\pi
.

One can prove Proposition
4.14
by imitating the proof in
[
43
]
, with the following minor modification: where that proof considers the set of characters
\chi
appearing in the Fourier series of
f
, to prove Proposition
4.14
consider instead the set of characters appearing in the Fourier series of
f
or the Fourier series of
g
(or both).

5
Reducing to a compact quotient of
\mathbf{G}

Here we collect some results allowing us to study sumsets in an ultraproduct
\mathbf{G}
of compact groups by studying related sumsets in a compact quotient of
\mathbf{G}
.

For this section, fix a sequence of compact abelian groups
G_{n}
with Haar probability measure
m_{n}
and a nonprincipal ultrafilter
\mathcal{U}
on
\mathbb{N}
. Let
\mathbf{G}
denote the ultraproduct
\prod_{n\to\mathcal{U}}G_{n}
and
\mu
be the Loeb measure corresponding to
(m_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. We continue to use the notation
\widehat{\mathbf{G}}
of Definition
4.1
: the elements of
\widehat{\mathbf{G}}
are the functions
\chi:\mathbf{G}\to\mathcal{S}^{1}
of the form
\chi=\lim_{n\to\mathcal{U}}\chi_{n}
, where
\chi_{n}\in\widehat{G}_{n}
for each
n
; in particular the elements of
\widehat{\mathbf{G}}
are
\mu
-measurable.

The main results of this section are Proposition
5.1
and its consequences, Lemma
5.4
and Corollary
5.5
. Here we abbreviate the set
\{x:f(x)>0\}
as
\{f>0\}
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
f,g:\mathbf{G}\to[0,1]
be
\mu
-measurable. Then there is a compact metrizable quotient
G
of
\mathbf{G}
with Haar measure
m
,
\mu
-measure preserving quotient map
\pi:\mathbf{G}\to G
, and Borel functions
\tilde{f},\tilde{g}:G\to[0,1]
such that

\displaystyle\{f>0\}\subset_{\mu}\{\tilde{f}\circ\pi>0\},\quad\{g>0\}\subset_{\mu}\{\tilde{g}\circ\pi>0\},and

(5.1)

\displaystyle f*_{\mu}g\equiv_{\mu}(\tilde{f}*_{m}\tilde{g})\circ\pi.

(5.2)

Proof.

Let
f,g:\mathbf{G}\to[0,1]
be
\mu
-measurable functions. Lemma
4.12
implies
f^{(c)}
and
g^{(c)}
are bounded, so Proposition
4.14
provides a compact metrizable group
G
, a
\mu
-measure preserving quotient map
\pi:\mathbf{G}\to G
, and Borel functions
\tilde{f},\tilde{g}:G\to\mathbb{C}
such that

f^{(c)}\equiv_{\mu}\tilde{f}\circ\pi,\quad g^{(c)}\equiv_{\mu}\tilde{g}\circ\pi.

(5.3)

We will show that
\tilde{f}
and
\tilde{g}
satisfy the conclusion of Proposition
5.1
.

Lemma
4.12
implies
0\leq f^{(c)}(\mathbf{x}),g^{(c)}(\mathbf{x})\leq 1
for
\mu
-a.e.
\mathbf{x}
, so
0\leq\tilde{f}(x),\tilde{g}(x)\leq 1
for
m
-a.e.
x
. Lemma
4.12
also implies
\{f>0\}\subset_{\mu}\{f^{(c)}>0\}
and
\{g>0\}\subset_{\mu}\{g^{(c)}>0\}
. Since
f^{(c)}\equiv_{\mu}\tilde{f}\circ\pi
, we get the essential containments stated in (
5.1
).

To prove Equation (
5.2
), first note that Lemma
4.13
implies
f*_{\mu}g\equiv_{\mu}f^{(c)}*_{\mu}g^{(c)}
. It therefore suffices to show that
f^{(c)}*_{\mu}g^{(c)}\equiv_{\mu}(\tilde{f}*_{m}\tilde{g})\circ\pi
, and this follows immediately from Lemma
1.3
.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

To be clear, Proposition
5.1
asserts that
(f*g)(\mathbf{x})=(\tilde{f}*\tilde{g})(\pi(\mathbf{x}))
for
\mu
-almost every
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{G}
. This cannot be improved to replace “
\mu
-almost every
\mathbf{x}
” with “every
\mathbf{x}
”. Examples exhibiting the obstruction are discussed in §
13.2.6
.

The next lemma lets us pass between convolutions and sumsets; the main application will take an estimate on the size of a sumset in
\mathbf{G}
and obtain a similar estimate on the size of a related sumset in a compact quotient of
\mathbf{G}
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
G
be a compact metrizable abelian group with Haar probability measure
m
. If
f,g:G\to[0,1]
are
m
-measurable functions let
C_{0}:=\{x\in G:f(x)>0\}
and
D_{0}:=\{x\in G:g(x)>0\}
. Then there are Borel sets
C\subseteq C_{0}
,
D\subseteq D_{0}
such that
m(C)=m(C_{0})
,
m(D)=m(D_{0})
,
C+D
is Borel, and
C+D\subseteq\{x\in G:f*g(x)>0\}
.

For a proof of Lemma
5.3
, see Lemma 2.13 of
[
18
]
. When
G=\mathbb{T}^{d}
for some
d\in\mathbb{N}
, we can take
C
and
D
to be countable unions of compact subsets of the points of Lebesgue density of
\{x:f(x)>0\}
and
\{x:g(x)>0\}
, respectively.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
f,g:\mathbf{G}\to[0,1]
be
\mu
-measurable functions and
E:=\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{G}:f*_{\mu}g(\mathbf{x})>0\}
. If
A:=\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{G}:f(\mathbf{x})>0\}
and
B:=\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{G}:g(\mathbf{x})>0\}
, there is a compact quotient group
G
with Haar probability measure
m
, a
\mu
-measure preserving quotient map
\pi:\mathbf{G}\to G
, and Borel sets
C,D\subseteq G
such that
A\subset_{\mu}A^{\prime}:=\pi^{-1}(C)
,
B\subset_{\mu}B^{\prime}:=\pi^{-1}(D)
and
A^{\prime}+B^{\prime}\subset_{\mu}E
. Furthermore
C+D
is Borel and
m(C+D)\leq\mu(E)
.

Proof.

Let
G
,
\pi:\mathbf{G}\to G
, and
\tilde{f},\tilde{g}:G\to[0,1]
be as in Proposition
5.1
. Consider the level sets
C_{0}:=\{t\in G:\tilde{f}(t)>0\}
,
D_{0}:=\{t\in G:\tilde{g}(t)>0\}
, and apply Lemma
5.3
to choose Borel sets
C\subseteq C_{0}
,
D\subseteq D_{0}
having
C\sim_{m}C_{0}
,
D\sim_{m}D_{0}
such that
C+D
is Borel and

C+D\subseteq V:=\{t\in G:\tilde{f}*_{m}\tilde{g}(t)>0\}.

(5.4)

Let
A^{\prime}:=\pi^{-1}(C)
and
B^{\prime}:=\pi^{-1}(D)
. Now Proposition
5.1
implies
(\tilde{f}*_{m}\tilde{g})\circ\pi\equiv_{\mu}f*_{\mu}g
, so
E\sim_{\mu}\pi^{-1}(V)
, and (
5.4
) implies
A^{\prime}+B^{\prime}\subset_{\mu}E
.

The essential containment
A\subset_{\mu}A^{\prime}
follows from the similarity
A^{\prime}\sim_{\mu}\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{G}:\tilde{f}\circ\pi(\mathbf{x})>0\}
and Proposition
5.1
. Likewise
B\subset_{\mu}B^{\prime}
. The inequality
m(C+D)\leq\mu(E)
follows from the containments
\pi^{-1}(C+D)=A^{\prime}+B^{\prime}\subset_{\mu}E
, since
\pi
preserves
\mu
.
∎

Specializing Lemma
5.4
to the case where
f=1_{A}
and
g=1_{B}
are characteristic functions of sets and observing that
A+_{0}B:=\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{G}:1_{A}*1_{B}(\mathbf{x})>0\}\subseteq A+B
, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 5.5
.

If
A,B\subseteq\mathbf{G}
are
\mu
-measurable sets then there is a compact quotient
G
with Haar measure
m
, a
\mu
-measure preserving quotient map
\pi:\mathbf{G}\to G
, and Borel sets
C,D\subseteq G
such that

(i)

A\subset_{\mu}A^{\prime}:=\pi^{-1}(C)
,
B\subset_{\mu}B^{\prime}:=\pi^{-1}(D)
and
A^{\prime}+B^{\prime}\subset_{\mu}A+_{0}B\subseteq A+B
. Furthermore
C+D
is Borel (so that
A^{\prime}+B^{\prime}
is
\mu
-measurable) and
m(C+D)\leq\mu_{*}(A+B)
.

(ii)

If
A^{\prime\prime}\sim_{\mu}A
and
B^{\prime\prime}\sim_{\mu}B
, then
A^{\prime}+B^{\prime}\subset_{\mu}A^{\prime\prime}+B^{\prime\prime}
.

Proof.

Part (i) follows immediately from Lemma
5.4
. Part (ii) follows from Part (i) and the observation that
A^{\prime\prime}+_{0}B^{\prime\prime}=A+_{0}B
.
∎

6
Bohr intervals and sumsets

For this section fix a sequence of compact abelian groups
G_{n}
with Haar probability measure
m_{n}
and a nonprincipal ultrafilter
\mathcal{U}
on
\mathbb{N}
. Let
\mathbf{G}
be the ultraproduct
\prod_{n\to\mathcal{U}}G_{n}
and
\mu
the corresponding Loeb measure. Write
\widehat{\mathbf{G}}
for the group of strong characters of
\mathbf{G}
, as defined in §
4
. Lemma
6.1
and Corollary
6.2
characterize the internal Bohr intervals in
\mathbf{G}
in terms of their constituent sets. Lemma
6.4
and Corollary
6.5
show that certain sets related to Bohr intervals are themselves Bohr intervals.

Recall (Definition
1.4
) that if
G
is a group and
\mu
is a translation invariant probability measure on
G
, a Bohr interval in
G
is a set of the form
\tau^{-1}(I)
, where
\tau:G\to\mathbb{T}
is a surjective
\mu
-measurable homomorphism and
I\subseteq\mathbb{T}
is an interval. Furthermore, we consider only closed intervals in
\mathbb{T}
.

Here we characterize those sequences of sets
A_{n}\subseteq G_{n}
such that
\prod_{n\to\mathcal{U}}A_{n}
is a Bohr interval in
\mathbf{G}
; we will see that this forces the
A_{n}
to be contained in Bohr intervals or
N
-cyclic progressions (Definition
1.4
) not much larger than
A_{n}
. We use the notation
C_{N}
(Notation
1.6
) and continue to write
\lambda
for Lebesgue measure (
=
normalized Haar measure) on
\mathbb{T}
.

Lemma 6.1
.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\tau_{n}:G_{n}\to\mathbb{T}
be a continuous homomorphism, and define
\tau:\mathbf{G}\to\mathbb{T}
as
\lim_{n\to\mathcal{U}}\tau_{n}
. Assume that
\tau(\mathbf{G})=\mathbb{T}
. Then

(i)

Either

\cdot

\tau_{n}(G_{n})=\mathbb{T}
for
\mathcal{U}
-many
n
, or

\cdot

\tau_{n}(G_{n})=C_{N_{n}}
for
\mathcal{U}
-many
n
, where
\lim_{n\to\mathcal{U}}N_{n}=\infty
.

(ii)

If
I\subseteq\mathbb{T}
is an interval let
\tilde{I}_{n}:=\tau_{n}^{-1}(I)
. Then either
\tilde{I}_{n}
is a Bohr interval of
m_{n}
-measure
\lambda(I)
for
\mathcal{U}
-many
n
, or
\tilde{I}_{n}
is an
N_{n}
-cyclic progression of
m_{n}
-measure at most
\lambda(I)+\frac{1}{N_{n}}
, where
\lim_{n\to\mathcal{U}}N_{n}=\infty
.

(iii)

If
\mathbf{A}=\prod_{n\to\mathcal{U}}A_{n}
is an internal set and
\mathbf{A}\subseteq\tau^{-1}(I)
, then there are intervals
I_{n}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{T}
such that
\lim_{n\to\mathcal{U}}\lambda(I_{n}^{\prime})\leq\lambda(I)
and
A_{n}\subseteq\tau_{n}^{-1}(I_{n}^{\prime})
for
\mathcal{U}
-many
n
.

Proof.

(i) The continuity of
\tau_{n}
implies that
\tau_{n}(G_{n})
is either
\mathbb{T}
or a finite cyclic subgroup of
\mathbb{T}
, as these are the only compact subgroups of
\mathbb{T}
. The hypothesis that
\tau
is surjective implies that for all
x\in\mathbb{T}
, there is a sequence of elements
g_{n}\in G_{n}
such that
\lim_{n\to\mathcal{U}}\tau_{n}(g_{n})=x
. Thus if
\tau_{n}(G_{n})\neq\mathbb{T}
for
\mathcal{U}
-many
n
, then
\tau_{n}(G_{n})=C_{N_{n}}
, where
\lim_{n\to\mathcal{U}}N_{n}=\infty
.

(ii) If
\tau_{n}(G_{n})=\mathbb{T}
, then
\tau_{n}^{-1}(I)
is a Bohr interval of
m_{n}
-measure
\lambda(I)
, by definition. If
\tau_{n}(G_{n})\neq\mathbb{T}
for
\mathcal{U}
-many
n
, then Part (i) implies that
\tau_{n}(G_{n})=C_{N_{n}}
for
\mathcal{U}
-many
n
, and
\lim_{n\to\mathcal{U}}N_{n}=\infty
. For such
n
,
\tilde{I}_{n}
is an
N
-cyclic progression, and the estimate of
m_{n}(\tilde{I}_{n})
then follows from Lemma
1.7
.

(iii) The hypothesis
\mathbf{A}\subseteq\tau^{-1}(I)
is equivalent to the condition
\lim_{n\to\mathcal{U}}\tau_{n}(a_{n})\in I
whenever
a_{n}\in A_{n}
. Let
J\subseteq\mathbb{T}
be an open interval containing
I
and
\bar{J}
its closure. Observe that
\tau_{n}(A_{n})\subseteq\bar{J}
for
\mathcal{U}
-many
n
: assuming otherwise, there is a sequence
a_{n}\in A_{n}
such that
\tau_{n}(a_{n})\notin\bar{J}
for
\mathcal{U}
-many
n
, contradicting the condition
\lim_{n\to\mathcal{U}}\tau_{n}(a_{n})\in I
. If we let
I_{n}^{\prime}
be the smallest closed interval containing
\tau_{n}(A_{n})
, we then have
I_{n}^{\prime}\subseteq\bar{J}
for
\mathcal{U}
-many
n.
Thus
L:=\lim_{n\to\mathcal{U}}\lambda(I_{n}^{\prime})\leq\lambda(\bar{J})
. Since
J
is an arbitrary open interval containing the interval
I
, this implies
L\leq\lambda(I)
, so the
I_{n}^{\prime}
are the desired intervals.
∎

Corollary 6.2
.

If
\mathbf{A}=\prod_{n\to\mathcal{U}}A_{n},\mathbf{B}=\prod_{n\to\mathcal{U}}B_{n}\subseteq\mathbf{G}
are internal sets and
\tilde{I},\tilde{J}\subseteq\mathbf{G}
are parallel Bohr intervals such that
\mathbf{A}\subseteq\tilde{I}
,
\mathbf{B}\subseteq\tilde{J}
, and
\mu(\tilde{I})=\mu(\mathbf{A})
,
\mu(\tilde{J})=\mu(\mathbf{B})
then there are
\tilde{I}_{n},\tilde{J}_{n}\subseteq G_{n}
such that

A_{n}\subseteq\tilde{I}_{n},\quad B_{n}\subseteq\tilde{J}_{n},\quad\lim_{n\to\mathcal{U}}m_{n}(\tilde{I}_{n}\setminus A_{n})+m_{n}(\tilde{J}_{n}\setminus B_{n})=0,

(6.1)

and
\tilde{I}_{n}
and
\tilde{J}_{n}
are either parallel Bohr intervals or parallel
N_{n}
-cyclic progressions, where
\lim_{n\to\mathcal{U}}N_{n}=\infty
.

Proof.

By the definition of “parallel Bohr intervals”, we can write
\tilde{I}
and
\tilde{J}
as
\tau^{-1}(I)
and
\tau^{-1}(J)
for some surjective
\mu
-measurable homomorphism
\tau:\mathbf{G}\to\mathbb{T}
and intervals
I,J
contained in
\mathbb{T}
. Lemma
4.6
implies
\tau=\lim_{n\to\mathcal{U}}\tau_{n}
for some continuous homomorphisms
\tau_{n}:G_{n}\to\mathbb{T}
. Parts (ii) and (iii) of Lemma
6.1
then guarantee the existence of Bohr intervals or
N
-cyclic progressions
\tilde{I}_{n},\tilde{J}_{n}
satisfying (
6.1
).
∎

We often need to infer information about a group element
b
based on the fact that a translate
A+b
is contained in a certain Bohr interval. This possible under strong hypotheses, according to Lemma
6.4
. The following definition helps abbreviate the statement.

Definition 6.3
.

Let
G
be a group and
A,B\subseteq G
. Define

\bar{A}_{B}:=\{g\in G:g+B\subseteq A+B\}.

If
\nu
is a measure on
G
, let
\bar{A}_{B,\nu}:=\{g\in G:g+B\subset_{\nu}A+B\}
.

The following lemma is elementary and well known in the special case where
G
is the torus
\mathbb{T}
.

Lemma 6.4
.

If
G
is an abelian group with translation invariant probability measure
\nu
and
A,B\subseteq G
are parallel Bohr intervals with
\nu(A)+\nu(B)<1
, then
\bar{A}_{B}=\bar{A}_{B,\nu}=A
and
\bar{B}_{A}=\bar{B}_{A,\nu}=B
.

We omit the proof. The general case follows from the case where
G
is
\mathbb{T}
, since the homomorphism
\tau:G\to\mathbb{T}
in the definition of “Bohr interval” is always surjective and measure preserving.

Corollary 6.5
.

With
G
as in Lemma
6.4
, let
A,B\subseteq G
satisfy
\nu_{*}(A+B)\leq\nu(A)+\nu(B)
, and suppose
\tilde{I},\tilde{J}\subseteq G
are parallel Bohr intervals such that
\nu(\tilde{I})+\nu(\tilde{J})<1
,
A\sim_{\nu}\tilde{I}
, and
B\sim_{\nu}\tilde{J}
. Then
A\subseteq\tilde{I}
and
B\subseteq\tilde{J}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
6.4
, to prove
A\subseteq\tilde{I}
it suffices to show that for all
a\in A
,
a+\tilde{J}\subset_{\nu}\tilde{I}+\tilde{J}
. Note that the similarities
A\sim_{\nu}\tilde{I}
and
B\sim_{\nu}\tilde{J}
imply
1_{A}*1_{B}=1_{\tilde{I}}*1_{\tilde{J}}
, and the level set
E:=\{x:1_{\tilde{I}}*1_{\tilde{J}}(x)>0\}
satisfies
E\sim_{\nu}\tilde{I}+\tilde{J}
. Hence
\tilde{I}+\tilde{J}\subset_{\nu}A+B
, so the hypothesis
\nu_{*}(A+B)\leq\nu(A)+\nu(B)
implies
\nu_{*}(A+B)=\nu(\tilde{I}+\tilde{J})
. In particular if
C
is a
\nu
-measurable subset of
A+B
, then
C\subset_{\nu}\tilde{I}+\tilde{J}
. Thus
a+B\subset_{\nu}\tilde{I}+\tilde{J}
, so the similarity
B\sim_{\nu}\tilde{J}
implies
a+\tilde{J}\subset_{\nu}\tilde{I}+\tilde{J}
for all
a\in A
, as desired. This establishes
A\subseteq\tilde{I}
, and the containment
B\subseteq\tilde{J}
follows by symmetry. ∎

Proof of Theorem
1.17

We prove Theorem
1.17
at the end of this section. The main ingredient of the proof is Proposition
7.1
, the natural analogue of Theorem
3.4
in the setting of ultraproducts of connected compact groups. However, we will not assume in Proposition
7.1
that the constituent groups
G_{n}
are connected; we merely assume that
\widehat{\mathbf{G}}
is torsion free. In fact
\widehat{\mathbf{G}}
is torsion free whenever the
G_{n}
are connected, but this is so for other ultraproducts, such as
\prod_{n\to\mathcal{U}}\mathbb{Z}/p_{n}\mathbb{Z}
, where
p_{n}
is the
n^{\text{th}}
prime.

Fix a sequence of compact abelian groups
G_{n}
with Haar probability measure
m_{n}
and a nonprincipal ultrafilter
\mathcal{U}
on
\mathbb{N}
. Let
\mathbf{G}=\prod_{n\to\mathcal{U}}G_{n}
with corresponding Loeb measure
\mu
. Let
\widehat{\mathbf{G}}
denote the group of strong characters of
\mathbf{G}
, as in §
4
. Recall the term “Bohr interval” from Definition
1.4
.

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
\mathbf{G}
be as above and assume
\widehat{\mathbf{G}}
is torsion free. If
A
,
B\subseteq\mathbf{G}
are
\mu
-measurable sets satisfying
\mu(A),\mu(B)>0
and
\mu_{*}(A+B)\leq\mu(A)+\mu(B)<1
, then there are parallel Bohr intervals
\tilde{I},\tilde{J}\subseteq\mathbf{G}
such that
A\subseteq\tilde{I}
,
B\subseteq\tilde{J}
, and
\mu(A)=\mu(\tilde{I})
,
\mu(B)=\mu(\tilde{J})
.

Proof.

By Corollary
6.5
, it suffices to find Bohr intervals
\tilde{I},\tilde{J}\subseteq\mathbf{G}
such that
A\sim_{\mu}\tilde{I}
and
B\sim_{\mu}\tilde{J}
.

First we show that every compact quotient
G
of
\mathbf{G}
with
\mu
-measurable quotient map
\pi:\mathbf{G}\to G
is connected. To see this, note that the map
\pi^{*}:\widehat{G}\to\widehat{\mathbf{G}}
,
\pi^{*}(\chi):=\chi\circ\pi
is an injective homomorphism, so that
\widehat{G}
is isomorphic to a subgroup of
\widehat{\mathbf{G}}
. Hence,
\widehat{G}
is torsion free, and we conclude that
G
is connected by Theorem 2.5.6(c) of
[
36
]
.

Now apply Corollary
5.5
to find a compact metrizable quotient
G
of
\mathbf{G}
and a
\mu
-measurable quotient map
\pi
and Borel sets
C,D\subseteq G
,
A^{\prime}:=\pi^{-1}(C)
,
B^{\prime}:=\pi^{-1}(D)
, such that
C+D
is Borel,
A^{\prime}+B^{\prime}\subset_{\mu}A+B
, and
A\subset_{\mu}A^{\prime}
,
B\subset_{\mu}B^{\prime}
. Then
\mu(A^{\prime})\geq\mu(A)
and
\mu(B^{\prime})\geq\mu(B)
, so

\displaystyle m(C+D)=\mu(A^{\prime}+B^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq\mu_{*}(A+B)

\displaystyle\leq\mu(A)+\mu(B)\leq\mu(A^{\prime})+\mu(B^{\prime})=m(C)+m(D).

Then
m(C+D)\leq m(C)+m(D)
and
m(C+D)<1
, so Corollary
3.3
and the connectedness of
G
imply
m(C+D)=m(C)+m(D)
. The inequalities displayed above are therefore all equalities, and in particular
\mu(A)+\mu(B)=\mu(A^{\prime})+\mu(B^{\prime})
. The containments
A\subset_{\mu}A^{\prime}
and
B\subset_{\mu}B^{\prime}
then imply
\mu(A)=\mu(A^{\prime})
and
\mu(B)=\mu(B^{\prime})
, so

A\sim_{\mu}A^{\prime}\quad\text{and}\quad B\sim_{\mu}B^{\prime}.

(7.1)

Theorem
3.4
implies that there are parallel Bohr intervals
I^{\prime},J^{\prime}\subseteq G
such that
C\subseteq I^{\prime}
,
D\subseteq J^{\prime}
and
m(C)=m(I^{\prime})
,
m(D)=m(J^{\prime})
. Then
\tilde{I}:=\pi^{-1}(I^{\prime})
and
\tilde{J}:=\pi^{-1}(J^{\prime})
are parallel Bohr intervals such that
A^{\prime}\subseteq\tilde{I}
,
B^{\prime}\subseteq\tilde{J}
, and
\mu(A^{\prime})=\mu(\tilde{I})
,
\mu(B^{\prime})=\mu(\tilde{J})
. The similarities in (
7.1
) then imply
A\sim_{\mu}\tilde{I}
and
B\sim_{\mu}\tilde{J}
, as desired.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.17
.

Suppose, to get a contradiction, that Theorem
1.17
fails for a given
\varepsilon>0
. Then for all sufficiently large

, there is a connected compact abelian group
G_{n}
with Haar probability measure
m_{n}
, inner Haar measure
m_{n*}
, and sets

having
m_{n}(A_{n}),m_{n}(B_{n})>\varepsilon
such that

(a.1)

m_{n*}(A_{n}+B_{n})\leq m_{n}(A_{n})+m_{n}(B_{n})+\frac{1}{n}<1-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
, and

(a.2)

for every pair of parallel Bohr intervals
\tilde{I}_{n}
,

having

m_{n}(\tilde{J}_{n})<m(B_{n})+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
, we have

or

.

Let
\mathcal{U}
be a nonprincipal ultrafilter on
\mathbb{N}
, and form the ultraproduct

corresponding to

. Consider the internal sets
\mathbf{A}=\prod_{n\to\mathcal{U}}A_{n}

and

, so
\mu_{*}(\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{B})\leq\mu(\mathbf{A})+\mu(\mathbf{B})~<~1

Observe that

implies that there are parallel Bohr intervals

Periodic and quasi-periodic sets in ultraproducts

Here we study periodic and quasi-periodic subsets of ultraproducts (Definitions
1.12
and
1.14
).
Let
(G_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of compact abelian groups with Haar probability measure
m_{n}
and inner Haar measure
m_{n*}
. Let
\mathcal{U}
be a nonprincipal ultrafilter on
\mathbb{N}
, and fix the ultraproduct
\mathbf{G}=\prod_{n\to\mathcal{U}}G_{n}
with Loeb measure
\mu
corresponding to
m_{n}
. Let
\mu_{*}
denote the inner measure associated to
\mu
. If
K\leq\mathbf{G}
is a
\mu
-measurable finite index subgroup, Corollary
4.10
implies
K
is internal: there is a sequence of compact open subgroups
K_{n}\leq G_{n}
such that
K=\mathbf{K}:=\prod_{n\to\mathcal{U}}K_{n}
. With a view toward the proofs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.15
, we investigate how decompositions of an internal set
\mathbf{A}
by cosets of
\mathbf{K}
are related to coset decompositions of the constituent sets
A_{n}
by cosets of
K_{n}
.

For the remainder of the section, fix coset representatives
\mathbf{x}^{(1)},\dots,\mathbf{x}^{(k)}
for
\mathbf{K}
, so that
\mathbf{G}
is the disjoint union
\bigcup_{j=1}^{k}(\mathbf{x}^{(j)}+\mathbf{K})
. For each
j\leq k
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, choose
x_{n}^{(j)}\in G_{n}
so that
(x_{n}^{(j)})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
represents
\mathbf{x}^{(j)}
(in the sense of §
A.2
). We write
\mathbf{K}^{(j)}
for
\mathbf{x}^{(j)}+\mathbf{K}
and write
K^{(j)}_{n}
for
x^{(j)}_{n}+K_{n}
.

Lemma 8.1
.

Suppose
\mathbf{G}
,
\mathbf{K}
,
\mathbf{K}^{(j)}
, and
K_{n}^{(j)}
are as defined in the preceding paragraph. Let
\mathbf{A}=\prod_{n\to\mathcal{U}}A_{n}\subseteq\mathbf{G}
be an internal set. Then the following hold for
\mathcal{U}
-many
n
. To be clear, this means that there is a
U\in\mathcal{U}
such that for all
n\in U
, the following are true.

(i)

The map
\phi_{n}:G_{n}/K_{n}\to\mathbf{G}/\mathbf{K}
defined by
\phi_{n}(K_{n}^{(j)})=\mathbf{K}^{(j)}
is an isomorphism.

(ii)

A_{n}\cap K_{n}^{(j)}=\varnothing
iff
\mathbf{A}\cap\mathbf{K}^{(j)}=\varnothing
, while
A_{n}\cap K_{n}^{(j)}=K_{n}^{(j)}
iff
\mathbf{A}\cap\mathbf{K}^{(j)}=\mathbf{K}^{(j)}
.

(iii)

\phi_{n}(A_{n}+K_{n})=\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{K}
; consequently
\mu(\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{K})=m_{n}(A_{n}+K_{n})
.

Furthermore,

(iv)

For all
j
,
\lim_{n\to\mathcal{U}}m_{n*}(A_{n}\cap K_{n}^{(j)})=\mu_{*}(\mathbf{A}\cap\mathbf{K}^{(j)})
; if the sets
A_{n}
are
m_{n}
-measurable we have
\lim_{n\to\mathcal{U}}m_{n}(A_{n}\cap K_{n}^{(j)})=\mu(\mathbf{A}\cap\mathbf{K}^{(j)})
.

Proof.

To prove (i) first observe that by Corollary
4.10
we have
|G_{n}/K_{n}|=k
for
\mathcal{U}
-many
n
. Thus it suffices to prove that
\Phi:=\prod_{n\to\mathcal{U}}\phi_{n}
is an isomorphism from
\prod_{n\to\mathcal{U}}(G_{n}/K_{n})
to
\mathbf{G}/\mathbf{K}
. That
\Phi
is a homomorphism follows from the identity
(x_{n}^{(i)}+x_{n}^{(j)})\sim_{\mathcal{U}}(x_{n}^{(i)})+(x_{n}^{(j)})
for each
i,j\leq k
. The injectivity of
\Phi
follows from the fact that if
x_{n}^{(i)}-x_{n}^{(j)}\in K_{n}
for
\mathcal{U}
-many
n
, then
\mathbf{x}^{(i)}-\mathbf{x}^{(j)}\in\mathbf{K}
, and surjectivity then follows from the equality of cardinalities of the domain and codomain of
\Phi
. So
\Phi
is an isomorphism, and hence
\phi_{n}
is an isomorphism for
\mathcal{U}
-many
n
.

Part (ii) follows from the definition of the ultraproduct and our choice of
(x_{n}^{(j)})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. Part (iii) is an immediate consequence of Part (ii). Part (iv) follows from the definition of Loeb measure and Lemma
A.6
. ∎

Before stating the next lemma, we remark that if
\mathbf{A}\subseteq\mathbf{G}
is internal,
\mathbf{K}
is an internal subgroup, and
\mathbf{A}=A_{1}\cup A_{0}
is a quasi-periodic decomposition of
\mathbf{A}
with respect to
\mathbf{K}
, then the sets
A_{1}
and
A_{0}
are internal:
A_{0}
is the intersection of
\mathbf{A}
with a coset of
\mathbf{K}
, while
A_{1}
is the difference
\mathbf{A}\setminus A_{0}
.

Lemma 8.2
.

Suppose
\mathbf{G},\mathbf{K}
,
\mathbf{A}
,
K_{n}
, and
\phi_{n}
are as in Lemma
8.1
, and
\mathbf{A}
has a quasi-periodic decomposition
\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{A}_{1}\cup\mathbf{A}_{0}
with respect to
\mathbf{K}
. If
\varepsilon>0
then for
\mathcal{U}
-many
n
,
A_{n}
has an
\varepsilon
-quasi-periodic decomposition
A_{n}=A_{n,1}\cup A_{n,0}
with respect to

, such that
\phi_{n}(A_{n,l}+K_{n})=\mathbf{A}_{l}+\mathbf{K}
for

.

Proof.

Write
\mathbf{A}_{l}
as
\prod_{n\to\mathcal{U}}A_{n,l}
for

. Parts (i) and (ii) of Lemma

imply that for

-many

\lim_{n\to\mathcal{U}}m_{n}(A_{n,1}+K_{n})-m_{n*}(A_{n,1})=0

-many

.
∎

Lemma 8.3
.

With
\mathbf{K}
and

as in the preceding lemmas, let
\mathbf{A}=\prod_{n\to\mathcal{U}}A_{n}
and
\mathbf{B}=\prod_{n\to\mathcal{U}}B_{n}\subseteq\mathbf{G}
be internal sets such that
\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{B}+\mathbf{K}
. Then

(i)

A_{n}+B_{n}=A_{n}+B_{n}+K_{n}

-many

.

(ii)

∎

9
Reducing to tame critical pairs

Here we collect some technical lemmas for §
10
, where the majority of our proofs are carried out. These are mainly consequences of the
conclusion
of Theorem
3.2
. The results of this section do not require countable additivity of the relevant measures, so we work in a slightly more general setting than in the other sections.

Call a measure
\nu
on a group
G

symmetric
if
\nu(-A)=\nu(A)
for all
\nu
-measurable sets
A
. Note that Haar measure
m
on a compact abelian group is symmetric, so the associated Loeb measure on an ultraproduct of compact abelian groups is also symmetric.

For the remainder of the section, we fix an abelian group
G
and a
finitely
additive (or countably additive), symmetric, translation invariant measure
\nu
on
G
. We do not assume that
\nu(G)
is finite. We continue denote by
\nu_{*}
the inner measure associated to
\nu
:
\nu_{*}(A)=\sup\{\nu(C):C\subseteq A\text{ is }\nu\text{-measurable}\}
.

Lemma 9.1
.

If
A,B\subseteq G
have finite measure and satisfy
\nu(A)+\nu(B)>\nu(G)
, then
A+B=G
. If
K\leq G
is a
\nu
-measurable subgroup such that
0<\nu(K)<\infty
and
\nu((a+K)\cap A)+\nu((b+K)\cap B)>\nu(K)
for some
a\in A,b\in B
, then
a+b+K\subseteq A+B
.

Proof.

If
\nu(A)+\nu(B)>\nu(G)
then for all
t\in G
, we have
\nu(A\cap(t-B))>0
, and in particular
t\in A+B
. Thus
A+B=G
. To prove the second assertion, define the measure
\nu_{K}
as
\nu
restricted to
K
. Then
\nu_{K}((A-a)\cap K)+\nu_{K}((B-b)\cap K)>\nu(K)
, so by the first part of the lemma we have
K\subseteq A+B-a-b
, meaning
a+b+K\subseteq A+B
.
∎

Definition 9.2
.

If
K
is a
\nu
-measurable finite index subgroup of
G
and
A\subseteq G
, we say that
A
is
K
-solid
if for all
g\in G
, either
\nu((g+K)\cap A)>0
or
(g+K)\cap A=\varnothing
.

Lemma 9.3
.

Let
K\leq G
be a
\nu
-measurable finite index subgroup of
G
,
A\subseteq G
, and let
B\subseteq G
be
K
-solid. If
A+B\sim_{\nu}A+B+K
and
g\in G
, then
g+B\subset_{\nu}A+B+K
if and only if
g+B+K\subseteq A+B+K
.

In the notation of Definition
6.3
, the conclusion of the lemma says
\bar{A}_{B,\nu}=\bar{A}_{B+K}
.

Proof.

The inclusion
\bar{A}_{B+K}\subseteq\bar{A}_{B,\nu}
follows from the similarity
A+B\sim_{\nu}A+B+K
. To prove the reverse inclusion, let
g\in\bar{A}_{B,\nu}
, so that
g+B\subset_{\nu}A+B
. If
g+B\nsubseteq A+B+K
, then for some coset
h+K
disjoint from
A+B+K
,
(g+B)\cap(h+K)\neq\varnothing
, so the
K
-solidity of
B
implies
\nu((g+B)\cap(h+K))>0
, and we get that
g+B\not\subset_{\nu}A+B+K
, and therefore
g\notin\bar{A}_{B,\nu}
. So we conclude that
g+B\subseteq A+B+K
, meaning
g\in\bar{A}_{B+K}
.
∎

In the next definition we use the term “stabilizer” from Definition
3.1
.

Definition 9.4
.

We say that two
\nu
-measurable subsets
A,B
of
G
form a
critical pair
if
\nu_{*}(A+B)<\nu(A)+\nu(B)
. We say they form a
tame critical pair
if, in addition,
A+B
is
\nu
-measurable, the stabilizer
H:=H(A+B)
is
\nu
-measurable, and

\nu(A+B)=\nu(A+H)+\nu(B+H)-\nu(H).

(9.1)

In other words,
A,B
is a tame critical pair if it satisfies the conclusion of Theorem
3.2
.

Observe that the criticality of
A,B
and Equation (
9.1
) imply
\nu(H)>0
.

The following lemmas provide useful information for tame pairs and related pairs of subsets of
G
. The first of these uses notation from Definition
6.3
and the following.

Notation 9.5
.

If
H\leq G
is a subgroup and
g\in G
, we write
H_{g}
for the coset
g+H
.

Lemma 9.6
.

Let
A,B\subseteq G
be a tame critical pair and
H:=H(A+B)
the stabilizer of
A+B
. Then

(i)

for all
a\in A
,
b\in B

\begin{split}\nu(A\cap H_{a})+\nu(A+B)\geq\nu(A)+\nu(B),\\
\nu(B\cap H_{b})+\nu(A+B)\geq\nu(A)+\nu(B),\end{split}

(9.2)

In particular,
A
and
B
are
H
-solid.

(ii)

We have

\bar{A}_{B,\nu}=\bar{A}_{B}=A+H,\ \bar{B}_{A,\nu}=\bar{B}_{A}=B+H.

(9.3)

(iii)

Furthermore, if
g\in G
and
A+g\nsubseteq A+B
, then
\nu((A+g)\cup(A+B))\geq\nu(A)+\nu(B).

Proof.

To prove the first inequality in Part (i), fix
a\in A
. Let
A^{\prime}:=A\cap H_{a}
,
A^{\prime\prime}:=A\setminus A^{\prime}
, so that
A^{\prime\prime}\cap H_{a}=\varnothing
and
\nu(A)=\nu(A^{\prime})+\nu(A^{\prime\prime})
. Then
A+H
is the disjoint union
(A^{\prime\prime}+H)\cup H_{a}
, so
\nu(A+H)-\nu(H)=\nu(A^{\prime\prime}+H)
. Replacing
\nu(A+H)-\nu(H)
with
\nu(A^{\prime\prime}+H)
in Equation (
9.1
) and adding
\nu(A^{\prime})
to both sides results in the following:

\nu(A^{\prime})+\nu(A+B)=\nu(A^{\prime})+\nu(A^{\prime\prime}+H)+\nu(B+H)\geq\nu(A^{\prime})+\nu(A^{\prime\prime})+\nu(B)=\nu(A)+\nu(B),

meaning
\nu(A^{\prime})+\nu(A+B)\geq\nu(A)+\nu(B)
. This is the desired inequality. The second inequality in Part (i) follows by symmetry. Note that the criticality of
A,B
and (
9.2
) imply that
A
and
B
are
H
-solid.

To prove Part (ii) we first establish
\bar{A}_{B,\nu}=\bar{A}_{B}
. Note that the tameness of
A+B
implies
A+B
is a union of cosets of
H
, so the
H
-solidity of
B
and Lemma
9.3
imply
\bar{A}_{B,\nu}=\bar{A}_{B}
. To prove that
\bar{A}_{B,\nu}=A+H
, we reduce the problem to the special case where
\nu
is counting measure. Lemma
9.3
implies
\bar{A}_{B,\nu}=\bar{A}_{B+H}
, and the last set is clearly a union of cosets of
H
. So all sets we are currently considering in the proof are unions of cosets of
H
, and we may work in the quotient
G/H
. We consider
G/H
as a discrete group with counting measure, so we may apply Theorem
3.2
in this setting. Replacing
A
with
A+H
and
B
with
B+H
in
G/H
, the problem is to prove that
\bar{A}_{B}=A
, assuming
|A+B|=|A|+|B|-1
and the stabilizer
H
of
A+B
is trivial. We clearly have
A\subseteq\bar{A}_{B}
, so we will prove the reverse inclusion. Assume, to get a contradiction, that there is a
c\notin A
such that
c+B\subseteq A+B
. Setting
\tilde{A}=A\cup\{c\}
, we get that
\tilde{A}+B=A+B
, so
H(\tilde{A}+B)=H(A+B)
. Our choice of
\tilde{A}
then implies
|\tilde{A}+B|=|A+B|=|\tilde{A}|+|B|-2
, which is strictly less than
|\tilde{A}+H|+|B+H|-|H|
, as
|H|=1
. This contradicts Inequality (
3.1
) in Theorem
3.2
applied to
\tilde{A},B
. Thus
\bar{A}_{B}=A
.

Part (iii) is a consequence of Part (i) and the fact that
A+B
is a union of cosets of
H
: under the hypothesis of (iii) we have that
(A+g)\setminus(A+B)
contains a set of the form
(A\cap H_{a})+g
which is disjoint from
A+B
. ∎

Lemma 9.7
.

Suppose
H
is a subgroup of
G
having
0<\nu(H)<\infty
and
A,B\subseteq G
satisfy

\nu(A+H)+\nu(B+H)-\nu(H)<\nu(A)+\nu(B).

(9.4)

Then for all
a\in A
,
b\in B
, we have
\nu(A\cap H_{a})+\nu(B\cap H_{b})>\nu(H)
.

Proof.

Inequality (
9.4
) can be written as
\nu(A+H)-\nu(A)+\nu(B+H)-\nu(B)<\nu(H)
, meaning

\nu((A+H)\setminus A)+\nu((B+H)\setminus B)<\nu(H).

(9.5)

Now
A+H=\bigcup_{a\in A}H_{a}
and
B+H=\bigcup_{b\in B}H_{b}
, so (
9.5
) implies
\nu(H_{a}\setminus A)+\nu(H_{b}\setminus B)<\nu(H)
for all
a,b\in H
. The last inequality can be written as
\nu(H)-\nu(A\cap H_{a})+\nu(H)-\nu(B\cap H_{b})<\nu(H)
, which can be rearranged to yield the desired conclusion.
∎

The next two lemmas form a major portion of the proof of Proposition
10.1
, generalizing Theorem
3.2
to ultraproducts of compact abelian groups.

Lemma 9.8
.

Suppose
A,B\subseteq G
is a critical pair and
A^{\prime},B^{\prime}
is a tame critical pair such that
A\subseteq A^{\prime}
,
B\subseteq B^{\prime}
, and
\nu_{*}(A+B)=\nu(A^{\prime}+B^{\prime})
. Let
H=H(A^{\prime}+B^{\prime})
. Then for all
a\in A^{\prime},b\in B^{\prime}
, we have

\nu(A\cap H_{a})+\nu(B\cap H_{b})>\nu(H).

(9.6)

Consequently,

A+H=A^{\prime}+H,\quad B+H=B^{\prime}+H,\quad A+B=A^{\prime}+B^{\prime}+H,

(9.7)

and
A,B
is a tame critical pair with
H(A+B)=H
.

Proof.

The proof of Inequality (
9.6
) is similar to the proof of Lemma
9.7
. We have

\nu(A)+\nu(B)>\nu_{*}(A+B)=\nu(A^{\prime}+B^{\prime})=\nu(A^{\prime}+H)+\nu(B^{\prime}+H)-\nu(H),

so
\nu(A^{\prime}+H)-\nu(A)+\nu(B^{\prime}+H)-\nu(B)<\nu(H)
. Since
A\subseteq A^{\prime}+H
and
B\subseteq B^{\prime}+H
, this inequality implies
\nu(H_{a}\setminus A)+\nu(H_{b}\setminus B)<\nu(H)
for all
a\in A^{\prime},b\in B^{\prime}
. As in the proof of Lemma
9.7
, the latter inequality can be rearranged to yield (
9.6
).

Now Inequality (
9.6
) implies that
A\cap H_{a}\neq\varnothing
for all
a\in A^{\prime}
. Together with the assumption
A\subseteq A^{\prime}
this implies
A+H=A^{\prime}+H
, and by symmetry we get
B+H=B^{\prime}+H
. The equation
A+B+H=A^{\prime}+B^{\prime}+H
follows from these equations, and the equation
A+B=A^{\prime}+B^{\prime}+H
then follows from (
9.6
) and Lemma
9.1
. We therefore have
H(A+B)=H(A^{\prime}+B^{\prime}+H)
, while the definition of
H
implies
A^{\prime}+B^{\prime}=A^{\prime}+B^{\prime}+H
, and we may conclude
H(A+B)=H(A^{\prime}+B^{\prime})
. The tameness of
A,B
then follows from the tameness of
A^{\prime},B^{\prime}
and the equations in (
9.7
).
∎

Lemma 9.9
.

Suppose
A,B\subseteq G
is a critical pair and
A^{\prime},B^{\prime}
is a tame critical pair with
A^{\prime}\subseteq A,B^{\prime}\subseteq B
, and
\nu(A)=\nu(A^{\prime})
,
\nu(B)=\nu(B^{\prime})
. Then
A,B
is a tame critical pair with
H(A+B)=H(A^{\prime}+B^{\prime})
.

Proof.

We must prove that
H:=H(A^{\prime}+B^{\prime})
is the stabilizer of
A+B
and and that Equation (
9.1
) is satisfied. To prove this, we will show that
A+B=A^{\prime}+B^{\prime}
,
A+H=A^{\prime}+H
, and
B+H=B^{\prime}+H
.

We first show that

A^{\prime}+b\subset_{\nu}A^{\prime}+B^{\prime}\quad\text{ for all }b\in B.

(9.8)

To prove (
9.8
) we assume otherwise and derive a contradiction by proving that
\nu_{*}(A+B)\geq\nu(A)+\nu(B)
, contrary to the hypothesis. So we assume there is a
b\in B
such that
C:=(A^{\prime}+b)\setminus(A^{\prime}+B^{\prime})
satisfies
\nu(C)>0
. Then Part (iii) of Lemma
9.6
implies
\nu((A^{\prime}+b)\cup(A^{\prime}+B^{\prime}))\geq\nu(A^{\prime})+\nu(B^{\prime})
. The containments in the hypothesis imply
(A^{\prime}+b)\cup(A^{\prime}+B^{\prime})\subseteq A+B
, so
\nu_{*}(A+B)\geq\nu(A^{\prime})+\nu(B^{\prime})=\nu(A)+\nu(B)
, which is the desired contradiction.

Having proved (
9.8
), Part (ii) of Lemma
9.6
and (
9.8
) together imply
B\subseteq B^{\prime}+H
. Likewise we have
A\subseteq A^{\prime}+H
. The containments
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
and
B^{\prime}\subseteq B
then imply
A+H=A^{\prime}+H
and
B+H=B^{\prime}+H
, and the equation
A^{\prime}+B^{\prime}=A^{\prime}+B^{\prime}+H
implies
A+B=A^{\prime}+B^{\prime}+H
. Finally, the tameness of
A^{\prime},B^{\prime}
implies
\nu(A+B)=\nu(A^{\prime}+B^{\prime})=\nu(A+H)+\nu(B+H)-\nu(H)
, as desired. ∎

Lemma 9.11
.

Assume
A,B\subseteq G

\nu_{*}(A+B)=\nu(A)+\nu(B)

\nu(B^{\prime})=\nu(B)

,

(iii)

with respect to

If

, and

Throughout this proof we let

To prove Parts (ii) and (iii), choose

\nu(A+H)-\nu(A)+\nu(B^{\prime}+H)-\nu(B^{\prime})=\nu(H)-\nu(A_{0}).

\nu(A+H)-\nu(A)=\nu(H)-\nu(A_{0})

(9.12)

and
\nu(B^{\prime}+H)-\nu(B^{\prime})=0

\nu_{*}(A+B)

\nu(A_{0})=\nu_{*}(A+B)-\nu(A+B^{\prime})

We prove Part (iv) using the following claim.

Claim

The additional hypothesis (iv) implies

and

Now let

Lemma 9.12
.

10
Lifting Theorems
3.2
and
3.5
to ultraproducts

The main results of this section are Propositions
10.1
and
10.2
, the natural generalizations of Theorems
3.2
and
3.5
to ultraproducts of compact abelian groups.

For this section fix a sequence of compact abelian groups
G_{n}
with Haar probability measure
m_{n}
and a nonprincipal ultrafilter
\mathcal{U}
on
\mathbb{N}
. Let
\mathbf{G}
be the ultraproduct
\prod_{n\to\mathcal{U}}G_{n}
and
\mu
the Loeb measure on
\mathbf{G}
corresponding to
(m_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. Let
\mu_{*}
be the inner measure on
\mathbf{G}
corresponding to
\mu
. Recall Definition
3.1
: the stabilizer of a set
C\subseteq\mathbf{G}
is the subgroup

H(C):=\{g\in\mathbf{G}:C+g=C\}.

Proposition 10.1
.

If
A,B\subseteq\mathbf{G}
are
\mu
-measurable sets with
\mu_{*}(A+B)<\mu(A)+\mu(B)
, then the stabilizer
\mathbf{H}:=H(A+B)
is an internal
\mu
-measurable finite index subgroup of
\mathbf{G}
and

\mu(A+B)=\mu(A+\mathbf{H})+\mu(B+\mathbf{H})-\mu(\mathbf{H}).

(10.1)

The proof of Proposition
10.1
is presented after the proof of Lemma
10.6
.

In the next proposition we use the terms “Bohr interval” and “quasi-periodic”, from Definitions
1.4
and
1.14
.

Proposition 10.2
.

Let
A,B\subseteq\mathbf{G}
be
\mu
-measurable sets satisfying
\mu(A)>0
,
\mu(B)>0
, and
\mu_{*}(A+B)=\mu(A)+\mu(B)
. Then there is an internal
\mu
-measurable finite index subgroup
\mathbf{K}\leq\mathbf{G}
such that exactly one of the following holds.

(I)

A+B\sim_{\mu}A+B+\mathbf{K}
.

(II)

A+B\nsim_{\mu}A+B+\mathbf{K}
and
A
and
B
have quasi-periodic decompositions
A=A_{1}\cup A_{0}
,
B=B_{1}\cup B_{0}
with respect to
\mathbf{K}
where at least one of
A_{1}
,
B_{1}
is nonempty and
\mu_{*}(A_{0}+B_{0})=\mu(A_{0})+\mu(B_{0})
.

(III)

A+B\nsim_{\mu}A+B+\mathbf{K}
and there are parallel Bohr intervals
A^{\prime},B^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbf{K}
and
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\in\mathbf{G}
such that
\mu(A)=\mu(A^{\prime})
,
\mu(B)=\mu(B^{\prime})
, and
A\subseteq\mathbf{a}+A^{\prime}
,
B\subseteq\mathbf{b}+B^{\prime}
.

The proof of Proposition
10.2
is presented following the proof of Lemma
10.13
.

Remark 10.3
.

The condition
A+B\nsim_{\mu}A+B+\mathbf{K}
in conclusion (II) above implies that
A_{0}+B_{0}+\mathbf{K}
is a unique expression element of
A+B+\mathbf{K}
in
\mathbf{G}/\mathbf{K}
(Definition
9.10
): assuming otherwise we would have
A+B\sim_{\mu}A+B+\mathbf{K}
. Consequently,

\displaystyle\mu_{*}((A+B)\setminus(A_{0}+B_{0}))

\displaystyle=\mu_{*}(A+B)-\mu_{*}(A_{0}+B_{0})

\displaystyle=\mu(A)+\mu(B)-\mu(A_{0})-\mu(B_{0})

\displaystyle=\mu(A_{1})+\mu(B_{1}).

Thus if
A,B
satisfies conclusion (II) of Proposition
10.2
, then

\mu_{*}((A+B)\setminus(A_{0}+B_{0}))=\mu(A_{1})+\mu(B_{1}).

(10.2)

We thereby deduce the following corollary (cf. Corollary 3.1 of
[
19
]
).

Corollary 10.4
.

If
G
,
A
, and
B
are as in Proposition
10.2
and
A,B
, and
\mathbf{K}
satisfy conclusion (II) therein, then
\mu(A+B+\mathbf{K})=\mu(A+\mathbf{K})+\mu(B+\mathbf{K})-\mu(\mathbf{K}).

Proof.

Note that the quasi-periodic decompositions of
A
and
B
with respect to
\mathbf{K}
yield
\mu(A+\mathbf{K})=\mu(A_{1})+\mu(\mathbf{K})
, and
\mu(B+\mathbf{K})=\mu(B_{1})+\mu(\mathbf{K})
. Adding these and rearranging yields

\mu(A_{1})+\mu(B_{1})+\mu(\mathbf{K})=\mu(A+\mathbf{K})+\mu(B+\mathbf{K})-\mu(\mathbf{K}).

(10.3)

Equation (
10.2
) and the fact that
A_{0}+B_{0}
is a unique expression element of

∎

The remainder of this section is dedicated to the proofs of Propositions

and

. The next definitions introduce some useful terminology for these proofs.

For the following definitions let

(as in §

Definition 10.5

(Tameness and type)
.

A pair of sets

is a

\nu_{*}(A+B)=\nu(A)+\nu(B)
and there is a

-measurable finite index subgroup

When the ambient group is compact, the terms “tame” and “type” can be applied, simply replacing Proposition
10.2
with Theorem
3.5
in the definition.

Lemma 10.6
.

Let
G
be a compact quotient of
\mathbf{G}
with
\mu
-measurable quotient map
\pi:\mathbf{G}\to G
. If
C,D\subseteq G
is a subcritical pair (critical pair) in
G
, then
\pi^{-1}(C)
,
\pi^{-1}(D)
is a tame subcritical pair (tame critical pair) in
\mathbf{G}
.

Proof.

We only prove the assertion for critical pairs. The assertion for subcritical pairs follows from a similar routine analysis, using Theorem
3.5
in place of Theorem
3.2
. Let
m
denote Haar measure on
G
.

Assume
C,D
is a critical pair in
G
. Let
A=\pi^{-1}(C)
,
B=\pi^{-1}(D)
, so the
\mu
-measurability of
\pi
and Lemma
1.3
imply
\mu(A)=m(C)
,
\mu(B)=m(D)
, and

\mu(A+B)=m(C+D)<m(C)+m(D)=\mu(A)+\mu(B),

so
A,B
is a critical pair. Theorem
3.2
guarantees the existence of a compact open (and therefore finite index) subgroup
H\leq G
such that
C+D=C+D+H
and
m(C+D)=m(C+H)+m(D+H)-m(H)
. The quotient
G/H
is finite, so if
\rho:G\to G/H
denotes the quotient map, then
\tau:=\rho\circ\pi
is a
\mu
-measurable homomorphism from
\mathbf{G}
onto the finite group
G/H
. Corollary
4.10
implies the kernel
\mathbf{H}
of

is an internal
\mu
-measurable finite index subgroup of
\mathbf{G}
. Now
\mathbf{H}
is the stabilizer of
A+B
and the equation
\mu(A+B)=\mu(A+\mathbf{H})+\mu(B+\mathbf{H})-\mu(\mathbf{H})
follows as
\pi
preserves
\mu
. Thus
A,B
is a tame critical pair in
\mathbf{G}
. ∎

Proof of Proposition

.

Consider
\mu
-measurable sets

\mu_{*}(A+B)<\mu(A)+\mu(B)

5.5
to find a compact abelian quotient

with Haar probability measure

\pi:\mathbf{G}\to G

,

\displaystyle m(C+D)=\mu(A^{\prime}+B^{\prime})

implies

We have shown that

The next definition introduces some pairs of sets for the proof of Proposition

.

Definition 10.7
.

A subcritical pair of sets
A,B\subseteq\mathbf{G}
is

\bullet

extendible
if there are
A^{\prime}\supseteq A
,
B^{\prime}\supseteq B
such that
\mu(A^{\prime})+\mu(B^{\prime})>\mu(A)+\mu(B)
, while
\mu_{*}(A^{\prime}+B^{\prime})=\mu_{*}(A+B)

\bullet

reducible

A^{\prime\prime}\subseteq A
,

\mu(A^{\prime\prime})=\mu(A)
,
\mu(B^{\prime\prime})=\mu(B)
, and
\mu_{*}(A^{\prime\prime}+B^{\prime\prime})<\mu_{*}(A+B)
. In this case we say
A,B

reduces to

A^{\prime\prime},B^{\prime\prime}
.

If the pair
A,B
is not extendible, we say it is
nonextendible
, and if it is not reducible, we say that it is

.

In subsequent proofs it will be convenient to assume that a pair
A,B
is nonextendible and irreducible. The following three lemmas allow us to do so, by proving that if a subcritical pair is extendible or reducible, then it is a tame subcritical pair of type (I) or type (II) (in the terminology of Definition

).

Lemma 10.8
.

If
A,B\subseteq\mathbf{G}
satisfy
\mu(A),\mu(B)>0
,
\mu_{*}(A+B)=\mu(A)+\mu(B)
, and
A,B
is extendible, then
A,B
has type (I).

Proof.

If
A,B
is extendible, then there exist sets
A^{\prime},B^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbf{G}
such that
A\subseteq A^{\prime}
,
B\subseteq B^{\prime}
,
\mu(A^{\prime})+\mu(B^{\prime})>\mu(A)+\mu(B)
, and
\mu_{*}(A^{\prime}+B^{\prime})=\mu_{*}(A+B)
. Thus
\mu_{*}(A^{\prime}+B^{\prime})<\mu(A^{\prime})+\mu(B^{\prime})
. Then Proposition
10.1
implies that there is an internal
\mu
-measurable finite index subgroup
\mathbf{H}\leq\mathbf{G}
such that
A^{\prime}+B^{\prime}=A^{\prime}+B^{\prime}+\mathbf{H}
. The assumption
\mu_{*}(A^{\prime}+B^{\prime})=\mu_{*}(A+B)
and the containment

now imply
A+B\sim_{\mu}A^{\prime}+B^{\prime}+\mathbf{H}
, so
A+B\sim_{\mu}A+B+\mathbf{H}
, meaning

has type (I).
∎

Lemma 10.9
.

Suppose that
A,B\subseteq G
satisfies
\mu_{*}(A+B)=\mu(A)+\mu(B)
, and

, and assume that

. Then
A
and
B
have quasi-periodic decompositions with respect to
\mathbf{H}
where at least one of
\mu(A_{0})=0
or
\mu(B_{0})=0

\mu_{*}(A_{0}+B_{0})=\mu(A_{0})+\mu(B_{0}).

Proof.

We consider cases based on

and
\mu_{*}(A^{\prime}+B)
.

Case 1:

\mu_{*}(A+B^{\prime})<\mu_{*}(A+B)
or
\mu_{*}(A^{\prime}+B)<\mu_{*}(A+B)
. By symmetry we need only consider the first inequality. In this case Proposition

and Lemma

, so

then implies the desired conclusion.

Case 2:
Neither

, and thus obtain a quasi-periodic decomposition

A

\mu_{*}(A+B_{1})=\mu_{*}(A+B)

\mu_{*}(D)=0

Lemma 10.11
.

Let

The next lemma deals with a case arising in the proof of Proposition

.

Lemma 10.12
.

Suppose
A,B\subseteq\mathbf{G}
have
\mu(A),\mu(B)>0
, and
\mu_{*}(A+B)=\mu(A)+\mu(B)
. Furthermore assume there are sets
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
and
B^{\prime}\subseteq B
and an internal
\mu
-measurable finite index subgroup
\mathbf{H}\leq\mathbf{G}

\bullet

A^{\prime}\sim_{\mu}A,B^{\prime}\sim_{\mu}B

\mu_{*}(A^{\prime}+B^{\prime})=\mu(A^{\prime})+\mu(B^{\prime})

\bullet

A^{\prime}

\mathbf{H}
-solid (Definition

A^{\prime}+B^{\prime}\sim_{\mu}A^{\prime}+B^{\prime}+\mathbf{H}

\mathbf{H}

.

Then

satisfies conclusion (I) or conclusion (II) of Proposition

.

First note that for all

Case 1:

\mu(A^{\prime}+\mathbf{H})+\mu(B^{\prime}+\mathbf{H})>\mu(A^{\prime})+\mu(B^{\prime})

A\subseteq A^{\prime}+\mathbf{H}
and

C=A^{\prime}+\mathbf{H}
,

10.1
to the pair

\mathbf{H}
is the stabilizer of

implies

. In other words: if

. So we have shown that

. The containment
B\subseteq B^{\prime}+\mathbf{H}
follows by symmetry.

Now
A\subseteq C
and
B\subseteq D
, while
\mu_{*}(C+D)=\mu_{*}(A+B)
. The identity

and containment

then imply

. Thus

satisfy conclusion (I) of Proposition
10.2
.

and

Let

13.2.3
The hypothesis

In Theorem

13.2.4
Semicontinuity in general

Given
\varepsilon,\delta>0
, which pairs of subsets

of a compact abelian group

13.2.5

Is there a proof of Theorem

? A simpler proof might generalize more easily to the nonabelian setting.

13.2.6
The exceptional set in Proposition
5.1

As mentioned in Remark
5.2
, the equation
f*g\equiv_{\mu}(\tilde{f}*\tilde{g})\circ\pi
in Proposition
5.1
cannot be improved to assert genuine equality. It may be interesting to characterise exactly which subsets of
\mathbf{G}
can be the set of
\mathbf{x}
where
f*g(\mathbf{x})\neq(\tilde{f}*\tilde{g})\circ\pi(\mathbf{x})
. Here is an example of
f
,
g
where the exceptional set has some interesting structure, based on the sets mentioned in §
13.1.3
. Let
G_{n}=(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{n}
, and let
\mathbf{G}=\prod_{n\to\mathcal{U}}G_{n}
. Let
d_{n}
be a sequence of natural numbers slowly tending to
\infty
, and choose
A_{n}\subseteq G_{n}
so that
|A_{n}|\geq(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{d_{n}})|G_{n}|
while
A_{n}+A_{n}
does not contain a coset of a subgroup of index
d_{n}
. The standard construction of such a set
A_{n}
will also guarantee that
\lim_{n\to\mathcal{U}}\sup_{\chi\in\widehat{G}_{n}\setminus\{1\}}|\widehat{1}_{A_{n}}(\chi)|=0
. Letting
\mathbf{A}=\prod_{n\to\mathcal{U}}A_{n}
and
f=1_{\mathbf{A}}
, we then have that
\mathbf{G}\setminus(\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{A})
has nonempty intersection with every coset of every internal finite index subgroup of
\mathbf{G}
. Furthermore
f*f(\mathbf{x})=0
for all
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{G}\setminus(\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{A})
, while
\tilde{f}\equiv_{m}\frac{1}{2}

(\tilde{f}*\tilde{f})\circ\pi(\mathbf{x})=\frac{1}{4}
for all
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{G}
. This construction guarantees that
\{\mathbf{x}:(f*f)(\mathbf{x})\neq(\tilde{f}*\tilde{f})\circ\pi(\mathbf{x})\}
has nonempty intersection with every coset of every
\mu
-measurable finite index subgroup of
\mathbf{G}
.

13.2.7
Nonabelian groups

Kemperman

partially extended Theorem

to arbitrary locally compact (not necessarily abelian) groups: if

[

.

For compact nonabelian groups with nontrivial abelian identity component, pairs

and an additional hypothesis (called “spread out”) are classified in

(here

[
4
]
to handle the hypothesis

for very small

for open subgroups

.

Appendix A
Ultraproducts and Loeb measure

We summarize the ultraproduct construction and properties of Loeb measure. Our framework is basically identical to the one developed in
[
42
,
43
]
, and similar to that of
[
2
]
and
[
45
]
. The undergraduate thesis
[
46
]
is a very clear introduction to these topics. The presentation here is to fix terminology and to state some important results from the literature. We will assume familiarity with the basic properties of an ultrafilter on
\mathbb{N}
.

A.1
Limits along ultrafilters

If
\mathcal{U}
is an ultrafilter on
\mathbb{N}
and
P
is a statement depending on
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we write “
P(n)
is true for
\mathcal{U}
-many
n
” if
\{n\in\mathbb{N}:P(n)\text{ is true}\}\in\mathcal{U}
.

If
X
is a topological space and
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of elements of
X
, we say that
\lim_{n\to\mathcal{U}}x_{n}=x
if for every neighborhood
V
of
x
,
\{n\in\mathbb{N}:x_{n}\in V\}\in\mathcal{U}
. With the above terminology we may say that
\lim_{n\to\mathcal{U}}x_{n}=x
if for every neighborhood
V
of
x
,
x_{n}\in V
for
\mathcal{U}
-many
n
. When
X
is a compact Hausdorff space, there is always a unique
x\in X
such that
\lim_{n\to\mathcal{U}}x_{n}=x
.

A.2
The ultraproduct construction

Fix a nonprincipal ultrafilter
\mathcal{U}
on
\mathbb{N}
. If
(S_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of sets, we form an equivalence relation
\sim_{\mathcal{U}}
on the set of sequences
(s_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
where
s_{n}\in S_{n}
, defined by
(s_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\sim_{\mathcal{U}}(t_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
iff
\{n:s_{n}=t_{n}\}\in\mathcal{U}
. The
ultraproduct

\prod_{n\to\mathcal{U}}S_{n}
, denoted
\mathbf{S}
, is the set of equivalence classes under the relation
\sim_{\mathcal{U}}
. If
\mathbf{s}\in\mathbf{S}
and
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a member of the equivalence class
\mathbf{s}
, we say that
\mathbf{s}
is
represented by

(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, and by abuse of notation we write
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\sim_{\mathcal{U}}\mathbf{s}
.

Given a constant sequence of sets,
S_{n}=S
for all
n
, we write
{\vphantom{*}}{}^{*}S
for the ultraproduct
\prod_{n\to\mathcal{U}}S
, which is then called an
ultrapower
of the underlying set.

A word of warning: we use the symbols “
\prod_{n\to\mathcal{U}}
” and “
\lim_{n\to\mathcal{U}}
” to denote different operations. Some articles use the symbol “
\lim_{n\to\mathcal{U}}
” to denote what we call “
\prod_{n\to\mathcal{U}}
”.

A.2.1
Internal sets

Given a sequence of sets
S_{n}
and the corresponding ultraproduct
\mathbf{S}=\prod_{n\to\mathcal{U}}S_{n}
, a sequence of subsets
A_{n}\subseteq S_{n}
determines an ultraproduct
\mathbf{A}=\prod_{n\to\mathcal{U}}A_{n}
which is itself a subset of
\mathbf{S}
. We call a set
\mathbf{A}\subseteq\mathbf{S}

internal
if it arises in this way, i.e. if there is a sequence of sets
A_{n}\subseteq S_{n}
such that
\mathbf{A}=\prod_{n\to\mathcal{U}}A_{n}
.

We will always use boldface letters such as
\mathbf{S}
,
\mathbf{A}
, etc., to denote ultraproducts and internal sets.

A.2.2
Standard elements

We call an element of an ultrapower
{\vphantom{*}}{}^{*}S

standard
if it has a constant representative
(s_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, meaning there is an
s
such that
s_{n}=s
for all
n
. The standard elements of
{\vphantom{*}}{}^{*}S
are naturally identified with elements of
S
.

A.2.3
Infinitesimals and bounded elements

The set of complex numbers
\mathbb{C}
may be identified with the set of standard elements of the ultrapower
{\vphantom{*}}{}^{*}\mathbb{C}
. An element
\mathbf{a}
of
{\vphantom{*}}{}^{*}\mathbb{C}
is called

\cdot

infinitesimal
if
\mathbf{a}
has a representative
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
\lim_{n\to\mathcal{U}}a_{n}=0
.

\cdot

bounded
if there is a
c\in\mathbb{R}
and a representative
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of
\mathbf{a}
such that
|a_{n}|\leq c
for
\mathcal{U}
-many
n
.

For every bounded
a\in{\vphantom{*}}^{*}\mathbb{C}
, there is a unique standard element
b\in{\vphantom{*}}^{*}\mathbb{C}
such that
a-b
is infinitesimal. Identifying
b
with an element of
\mathbb{C}
, we write
\operatorname{st}(a)
for
b
and call
\operatorname{st}(a)
the
standard part
of
a
.

A.2.4
Functions from
\mathbf{S}
into compact spaces

Given a compact Hausdorff topological space
Y
and a sequence of functions
f_{n}:S_{n}\to Y
, consider the ultraproduct
\mathbf{S}=\prod_{n\to\mathcal{U}}S_{n}
and let
\lim_{n\to\mathcal{U}}f_{n}
denote the function
f:\mathbf{S}\to Y
given by
f(\mathbf{s})=\lim_{n\to\mathcal{U}}f_{n}(s_{n})
, where
(s_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
represents
\mathbf{s}
.

A.2.5
Internal functions

If
(S_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
,
(T_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
are sequences of sets with corresponding ultraproducts
\mathbf{S}
and
\mathbf{T}
, and
f_{n}:S_{n}\to T_{n}
is a function for each
n
, we may define a function
\mathbf{f}:\mathbf{S}\to\mathbf{T}
as follows:
\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{s})
is the element of
\mathbf{T}
represented by the sequence
(f_{n}(s_{n}))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. Functions from
\mathbf{S}
to
\mathbf{T}
arising this way are called
internal
functions.
4
4

4

It is routine to check that this definition of
\mathbf{f}
agrees with the definition of
\mathbf{f}
as the ultraproduct
\prod_{n\to\mathcal{U}}f_{n}
, where each
f_{n}
is considered as a subset of
S_{n}\times T_{n}
. Thus the terminology “internal” is appropriate.

If
f_{n}:S_{n}\to\mathbb{C}
for each
n
and the
f_{n}
are uniformly bounded, then
\mathbf{f}
is a bounded function from
\mathbf{S}
into
{\vphantom{*}}{}^{*}\mathbb{C}
, and the function
{\vphantom{\circ}}{}^{\circ}\mathbf{f}
defined by
{\vphantom{\circ}}{}^{\circ}\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{s}):=\operatorname{st}(\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{s}))
is a function from
\mathbf{S}
to
\mathbb{C}
.

When
\mathbf{f}:\mathbf{S}\to{\vphantom{*}}^{*}\mathbb{C}
is bounded, there is an another way to define
{\vphantom{\circ}}{}^{\circ}\mathbf{f}
. If
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of uniformly bounded functions from
S_{n}
to
\mathbb{C}
and
\mathbf{f}=\prod_{n\to\mathcal{U}}f_{n}
, then for each
\mathbf{s}\in\mathbf{S}
and each representative
(s_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of
\mathbf{s}
we have
{\vphantom{\circ}}{}^{\circ}\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{s})=\lim_{n\to\mathcal{U}}f_{n}(s_{n})
. Thus
{\vphantom{\circ}}{}^{\circ}\mathbf{f}=\lim_{n\to\mathcal{U}}f_{n}
.

A.3
Loeb measure

We summarize the development of Loeb measure as used in
[
42
]
,
[
43
]
, and
[
2
]
, for example. See Chapter 1 of
[
10
]
for an overview of the construction and references.

For each
n
, let
X_{n}
be a set,
\mathscr{X}_{n}
an algebra of subsets of
X_{n}
, and
\mu_{n}
a finitely additive measure defined on
\mathscr{X}_{n}
. Consider the ultraproduct
\mathbf{X}=\prod_{n\to\mathcal{U}}X_{n}
and the

\sigma
-algebra
\mathscr{X}
of subsets of
\mathbf{X}
generated by the internal sets of the form

\prod_{n\to\mathcal{U}}A_{n}
, where
A_{n}\in\mathscr{X}_{n}
for
\mathcal{U}
-many
n
.
Then there is a unique countably additive measure
\mu
on
(\mathbf{X},\mathscr{X})
such that for every internal set
\mathbf{A}=\prod_{n\to\mathcal{U}}A_{n}
where
A_{n}\in\mathscr{X}_{n}
for
\mathcal{U}
-many
n
, we have

\mu(\mathbf{A})=\lim_{n\to\mathcal{U}}\mu_{n}(A_{n}).

(A.1)

Extending
\mathscr{X}
to its completion
\mathscr{X}^{\prime}
under
\mu
, the measure
\mu
is called the
Loeb measure
associated to
(X_{n},\mathscr{X}_{n},\mu_{n})
, and
(\mathbf{X},\mathscr{X}^{\prime},\mu)
is the
Loeb measure space
associated to
(X_{n},\mathscr{X}_{n},\mu_{n})
. Given such a measure
\mu
and a function
f:\mathbf{X}\to\mathbb{C}
, we say that
f
is
\mu
-measurable
(or
Loeb measurable
, if there is no ambiguity) if
f
is measurable with respect to
\mathscr{X}^{\prime}
. We say that a set
A\subseteq\mathbf{X}
is
\mu
-measurable
if
A\in\mathscr{X}^{\prime}
.

We need some natural approximation results for Loeb measurable functions, summarized in the following proposition. Here
D\subseteq\mathbb{C}
is the closed unit disk.

Proposition A.4
.

Let
(X_{n},\mathscr{X}_{n},\mu_{n})
be a sequence of probability measure spaces and let
(\mathbf{X},\mathscr{X},\mu)
be the associated Loeb measure space.

(i)

[
31
, Theorem 1]
If
A\subseteq\mathbf{X}
is
\mu
-measurable then for all
\varepsilon>0
there are internal sets
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
,
\mathbf{A}^{\prime\prime}
such that
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\subseteq A\subseteq\mathbf{A}^{\prime\prime}
and
\mu(\mathbf{A}^{\prime\prime}\setminus\mathbf{A}^{\prime})<\varepsilon
.

(ii)

[
31
, Theorem 3]
If
f_{n}:X_{n}\to D
is
\mathscr{X}_{n}
-measurable for
\mathcal{U}
-many
n
and
f=\lim_{n\to\mathcal{U}}f_{n}
, then
f
is
\mu
-measurable and
\int f\,d\mu=\lim_{n\to\mathcal{U}}\int f_{n}\,d\mu_{n}
.

A.5
Ultraproducts of compact abelian groups

If
(G_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of abelian groups, the ultraproduct
\mathbf{G}=\prod_{n\to\mathcal{U}}G_{n}
is an abelian group with addition defined by
\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{y}\sim_{\mathcal{U}}(x_{n}+y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, where
\mathbf{x}\sim_{\mathcal{U}}(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\mathbf{y}\sim_{\mathcal{U}}(y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. The inverse and identity are defined similarly. If each
G_{n}
is a compact abelian group with Haar probability measure
m_{n}
, then the Loeb measure
\mu
corresponding to
m_{n}
is translation invariant:
\mu(A+\mathbf{t})=\mu(A)
for all
\mu
-measurable
A\subseteq\mathbf{G}
and all
\mathbf{t}\in\mathbf{G}
. We will consider inner Haar measure
m_{n*}
on
G_{n}
, so we need to relate
m_{n*}
to the inner measure
\mu_{*}
associated to
\mu
. The next lemma says that the expected relationship holds.

Lemma A.6
.

If
\mathbf{G}
and
\mu
are as in the preceding paragraph and
A\subseteq\mathbf{G}
is any set, then
\mu_{*}(A)=\sup\{\mu(\mathbf{C}):\mathbf{C}\in\mathcal{S},\mathbf{C}\subseteq A\}
, where
\mathcal{S}
is the class of internal sets of the form
\prod_{n\to\mathcal{U}}C_{n}\subseteq\mathbf{G}
such that
C_{n}
is compact for all
n
. Consequently, if
\mathbf{A}=\prod_{n\to\mathcal{U}}A_{n}
is an internal (not necessarily
\mu
-measurable) subset of
\mathbf{G}
, then
\mu_{*}(\mathbf{A})=\lim_{n\to\mathcal{U}}m_{n*}(A_{n})
.

Proof.

Let
A\subseteq\mathbf{G}
be an arbitrary set, let
\varepsilon>0
, and choose a
\mu
-measurable set
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
such that
\mu(A^{\prime})>\mu_{*}(A)-\varepsilon
. By Proposition
A.4
choose an internal set
\mathbf{B}=\prod_{n\to\mathcal{U}}B_{n}\subseteq A^{\prime}
such that each
B_{n}
is
m_{n}
-measurable and
\mu(\mathbf{B})>\mu_{*}(A)-\varepsilon
. For each
n
, choose a compact set
C_{n}\subseteq B_{n}
such that
m_{n}(C_{n})>m_{n}(B_{n})-\frac{1}{n}
. Let
\mathbf{C}=\prod_{n\to\mathcal{U}}C_{n}
, so that
\mathbf{C}\in\mathcal{S}
,
\mathbf{C}\subseteq A
, and
\mu(\mathbf{C})=\mu(\mathbf{B})>\mu_{*}(A)-\varepsilon
. This shows that
\mu_{*}(A)\leq\sup\{\mu(\mathbf{C}):\mathbf{C}\in\mathcal{S},\mathbf{C}\subseteq A\}
, and the reverse inequality is obvious.
∎

A.7
Convolutions on ultraproducts

The ultraproduct
\mathbf{G}
is not (in any nontrivial case) a locally compact group, so we need to prove some properties of convolutions on
\mathbf{G}
which are inherited from the constituent groups. We assume familiarity with basic properties of convolution on compact groups, see
[
12
]
or
[
36
]
, for example. Recall the notation
f_{t}
from Definition
4.2
:
f_{t}(x):=f(x-t)
.

Lemma A.8
.

Let
\mathcal{U}
be a nonprincipal ultrafilter on
\mathbb{N}
,
(G_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
a sequence of compact abelian groups with Haar probability measure
m_{n}
, and
\mathbf{G}=\prod_{n\to\mathcal{U}}G_{n}
the ultraproduct with corresponding Loeb measure
\mu
. Let
f_{n},g_{n}:G_{n}\to\mathbb{C}
be uniformly bounded functions, and consider the internal functions
\mathbf{f}=\prod_{n\to\mathcal{U}}f_{n}
,
\mathbf{g}=\prod_{n\to\mathcal{U}}g_{n}
. Then

(i)

({\vphantom{\circ}}^{\circ}\mathbf{f})_{\mathbf{t}}={\vphantom{\circ}}^{\circ}(\mathbf{f}_{\mathbf{t}})
for all
\mathbf{t}\in\mathbf{G}
.

(ii)

{\vphantom{\circ}}{}^{\circ}\mathbf{f}*_{\mu}{\vphantom{\circ}}^{\circ}\mathbf{g}=\lim_{n\to\mathcal{U}}f_{n}*_{m_{n}}g_{n}
.

Proof.

Part (i) follows from the relevant definitions. To prove Part (ii) let
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{G}
and choose
x_{n}\in G_{n}
so that
\mathbf{x}\sim_{\mathcal{U}}(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. Then
{\vphantom{\circ}}{}^{\circ}\mathbf{f}*_{\mu}{\vphantom{\circ}}^{\circ}\mathbf{g}(\mathbf{x})=\int{\vphantom{\circ}}^{\circ}\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{t}){\vphantom{\circ}}^{\circ}\mathbf{g}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{t})d\mu(\mathbf{t})
, which equals
\lim_{n\to\mathcal{U}}\int f_{n}(t)g(x_{n}-t)\,dm_{n}(t)
by Part (ii) of Proposition
A.4
and Part (i) of the present lemma. The last integral can be rewritten as
\lim_{n\to\mathcal{U}}(f_{n}*_{m_{n}}g_{n})(x_{n})
, as desired. ∎

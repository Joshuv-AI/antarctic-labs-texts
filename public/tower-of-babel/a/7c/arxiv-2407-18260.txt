A note on the parity conjecture and base change
Vladimir Dokchitser

Abstract
. The parity conjecture predicts that the parity of the rank of an abelian variety is determined by its global root number, that is by the sign in the conjectural functional equation of its L L -function. Assuming the Shafarevich–Tate conjecture, we show that if a semistable principally polarised abelian variety A / ℚ A/\mathbb{Q} satisfies the parity conjecture over ℚ \mathbb{Q} and over all quadratic fields, then it satisfies it over all number fields. More generally, we establish a criterion for when the parity conjecture for the base change of an abelian variety to a larger number field is already implied by the parity conjecture over the ground field and over other small extensions.

1.
Introduction

The Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture classically predicts that the parity of the rank of the Mordell–Weil group of an abelian variety over a number field
A/K
agrees with the order of vanishing of its
L
-function
L(A,s)
at the central point
s\mskip-3.0mu=\mskip-3.0mu1
. In view of the conjectural functional equation
L(A,s)\leftrightarrow\pm L(A,2-s)
, the parity of the order of vanishing is determined by the sign
\pm
, which is given by the global root number
w(A/K)=\pm 1
. The resulting “parity conjecture” thus predicts

(-1)^{\rk A/K}=w(A/K).

An advantage over the full Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture is that this formulation does not require the knowledge of the analytic continuation or the functional equation of
L(A/K,s)
, since the root number is defined independently of these. Nonetheless, almost all the results on the parity conjecture rely on some form of finiteness of Tate–Shafarevich groups, as the rank remains an impossibly difficult invariant to control otherwise.

In this note we establish the following compatibilty of the parity conjecture with extensions of the base field:

Theorem 1.1

(see Theorem
3.2
, Corollaries
3.3
,
3.4
)
.

Let
A
be a principally polarised abelian variety over a number field
K
. Let
F/K
be a finite Galois extension such that
\text{\tencyr Sh}(A/F)
is finite. Suppose that either

•

A
is an elliptic curve and
K
is totally real, or

•

A
is an elliptic curve that satisfies the parity conjecture over
K
, or

•

A
is semistable and satisfies the parity conjecture over
K
and over all the quadratic extensions of
K
in
F
.

Then the parity conjecture holds for
A
over all extensions of
K
in
F
.

The proof of the above result makes use of known cases of the parity conjecture for “Artin twists” of abelian varieties.
The group of
F
-rational points
A(F)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}
is naturally a
\Gal(F/K)
-representation; we will write
A(F)_{\mathbb{C}}
for its character.
Assuming the finiteness of
Sh
, there is a known supply of characters
\rho
for which one can control the parity of the inner product
\langle\rho,A(F)_{\mathbb{C}}\rangle
and relate it to root numbers. This includes all characters of the form
\rho\mskip-3.0mu=\mskip-3.0mu\tau\mskip-3.0mu+\mskip-3.0mu\bar{\tau}
and, most crucially, characters
\rho\mskip-3.0mu=\mskip-3.0mu\tau\mskip-3.0mu+\mskip-3.0mu\mathds{1}\mskip-3.0mu+\mskip-3.0mu\det\tau
when
\tau
is irreducible of degree 2 and
\Gal(F/K)
is the dihedral group
D_{2p}
for an odd prime
p
(with suitable analogues for
C_{2}\times C_{2}
and
D_{8}
).
Our contribution is the observation that this supply is sufficient to prove the above theorem. The key new input is the following purely representation-theoretic induction theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
\mathcal{P}
a set of real-valued generalised characters of

that satisfies:

(1)

\tau+\bar{\tau}\in\mathcal{P}
for every generalised character
\tau
of degree 0,

(2)

\Ind_{H}^{G}(\tau-\mathds{1}_{H}-\det\tau)\in\mathcal{P}
for every subgroup

of degree 2 of

that factors through a quotient of

\tau_{i}\in\mathcal{P}
and

Here by a

\mathbb{Z}

A

\mathbb{Z}
-linear combination of characters of permutation representations.
We extend the notion of the

In the context of the parity conjecture, it is crucial that the induction theorem only makes use of the most basic dihedral groups. Indeed, to the best of the author’s knowledge, there is yet no way to control the parity of the inner product
\langle\tau\mskip-3.0mu+\mskip-3.0mu\mathds{1}\mskip-3.0mu+\mskip-3.0mu\det\tau,A(F)_{\mathbb{C}}\rangle

[
3
,
4
]
nor of
[
9
]
appear to have been extended to these cases. (The most elementary problematic case is
D_{42}

Lemma 2.3
.

If
\rho\in\mathcal{S}_{G}
is a
\mathbb{Z}

Proof.

The only real-valued linear characters are either trivial or have order 2, so we can write

Lemma 2.4
.

If

\mathcal{S}_{G}\subseteq\mathcal{P}_{G}

Proof.

In a group of odd order, the only real-valued irreducible character is

\tau+\bar{\tau}+m\mathds{1}

Lemma 2.5
.

Every
\rho\in\mathcal{S}_{G}
can be written in the form

\rho=\sum_{H}\rho_{H}+(\text{a sum of linear characters})

3.
Applications to the parity conjecture

We now turn to the behaviour of the parity conjecture under base change.

Let
A/K
be an abelian variety over a number field, and
F/K
a finite Galois extension. To a real character of
\tau
of
\Gal(F/K)
one associates a root number
w(A/K,\tau)\in\{\pm 1\}
, which , conjecturally, determines the sign in the functional equation for the twisted
L
-function
L(A/K,s)
. One also expects the following “parity conjecture for twists” (see e.g.
[
4
]
§1.1 or the introduction to
[
13
]
):

(-1)^{\langle\tau,A(F)_{\mathbb{C}}\rangle}=w(A/K,\tau).

As before,
A(F)_{\mathbb{C}}
is the character of
A(F)_{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes\mathbb{C}
, the
\Gal(F/K)
-representation on the
F
-rational points of
A
. (Here we use the natural extension of the definition of root numbers of twists from characters of representations to generalised characters by
w(A/K,\tau_{1}-\tau_{2})=\frac{w(A/K,\tau_{1})}{w(A/K,\tau_{2})}
.)

The parity conjecture for twists is known in the following cases for dihedral Galois groups.

.

Let
A
be a principally polarised abelian variety over a number field

and

Let

be a finite Galois extension with Galois group

if

, or

if

.

, and if the

Proof.

For

]

) and
[

We now have the necessary tools for the proof of Theorem
1.1

In what follows, we will freely use the standard fact that for an abelian variety over a number field
A/K

F/K
, if
\text{\tencyr Sh}(A/F)[p^{\infty}]

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
A
be a principally polarised abelian variety over a number field
K
.
Let
F/K
be a finite Galois extension such that
\text{\tencyr Sh}(A/F)[p^{\infty}]
is finite for all
p\,|\,[F\mskip-3.0mu:\mskip-3.0muK]
.
Suppose moreover that either
A/K
has semistable reduction at all primes that ramify in
F/K
, or that
A
is an elliptic curve.
If the parity conjecture holds for
A
over
K
and over all the quadratic extensions of
K
in
F
, then it holds over all intermediate fields of
F/K
.

Proof.

First recall the following basic properties of root numbers of Artin twists of abelian varieties (the first and the final one come direct from the definitions, for the others see e.g.
[
4
]
Proposition A.2):

\begin{array}[]{rcl}w(A/K,\rho_{1}+\rho_{2})&=&w(A/K,\rho_{1})w(A/K,\rho_{2}),\cr w(A/K,\bar{\rho}+\rho)&=&1,\cr w(A/K,\Ind_{H}^{G}\rho)&=&w(A/F^{H},\rho),\cr w(A/K,\mathds{1})&=&w(A/K),\end{array}

where
G=\Gal(F/K)
and
H\leq G
is a subgroup.

Write
A(F)_{\mathbb{C}}
for the character of
A(F)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}
as a
G
-representation.

The character
A(F)_{\mathbb{C}}
is clearly
\mathbb{Q}
-valued, so for
\rho=\tau+\bar{\tau}
we have
(-1)^{\langle\rho,A(F)_{\mathbb{C}}\rangle}=1=w(A,\rho)
.
If
\rho=\Ind_{H}^{G}(\tau-\mathds{1}_{H}-\det\tau)
for a character
\tau
of
H
with
\deg\tau=2
that factors through a quotient of
H
isomorphic to
C_{2}\mskip-3.0mu\times\mskip-3.0muC_{2},D_{8}
or
D_{2p}
for an odd prime
p
, then

(-1)^{\langle\rho,A(F)_{\mathbb{C}}\rangle}=(-1)^{\langle\tau-\mathds{1}_{H}-\det\tau,\>A(F)_{\mathbb{C}}\rangle_{H}}=w(A/F^{H},\tau-\mathds{1}_{H}-\det\tau)=w(A/K,\rho)

by Frobenius reciprocity and Theorem
3.1
.
Hence, by Theorem
1.2
,
(-1)^{\langle A(F)_{\mathbb{C}},\rho\rangle}=w(A,\rho)
for every permutation character
\rho
of degree 0 and with trivial determinant.

By hypothesis and the above basic properties of root numbers, the formula
(-1)^{\langle A(F)_{\mathbb{C}},\rho\rangle}=w(A,\rho)
also holds when
\rho
is either the trivial character or a character of order 2. It follows that it holds for by all permutation characters
\rho
.

Thus, by Frobenius reciprocity, for any subgroup
H\leq G
,

(-1)^{\rk A/F^{H}}=(-1)^{\langle\Ind_{H}^{G}\mathds{1},A(F)_{\mathbb{C}}\rangle}=w(A,\Ind_{H}^{G}\mathds{1})=w(A/F^{H}).

∎

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
E/K
be an elliptic curve over a number field satisfying the parity conjecture, and
F/K
a finite Galois extension.
If
\text{\tencyr Sh}(E/F)[p^{\infty}]
is finite for all primes
p\,|\,[F\mskip-3.0mu:\mskip-3.0muK]
, then the parity conjecture holds for
E
over all intermediate fields of
F/K
.

Proof.

The parity conjecture holds for
E
over every quadratic extension of
K
inside
F
by the Kramer–Tunnell theorem (
[
8
]
and
[
5
]
Corollary 1.6) and the assumed finiteness of the 2-primary part of
Sh
over that extension.
∎

∎

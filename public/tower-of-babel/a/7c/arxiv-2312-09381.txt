Crossed Product C ∗ -Algebras Associated with p-Adic Multiplication
Shelley Hebert, Slawomir Klimek, Matt McBride, J. Wilson Peoples

Abstract
. We introduce and investigate some examples of C ∗ -algebras which are related to multiplication maps in the ring of p p -adic integers. We find ideals within these algebras and use the corresponding short exact sequences to compute the K K -Theory.



1.
Introduction

Many interesting examples of C
∗
-algebras with connections to number theory have been studied, see, for instance,
[
2
,
7
]
. Explorations of such algebras lead to intriguing problems and have inspired development of new C
∗
-algebraic techniques.

One basic example of C
∗
-algebras related to number theory are the Bunce-Deddens (BD) algebras, a recent detailed study of which can be found in
[
6
]
. In special cases, BD algebras can be realized as crossed product algebras
C({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})\rtimes{\mathbb{Z}}
, where
C({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
denotes the continuous functions on the
p
-adic integers, and the automorphism implementing the action of
{\mathbb{Z}}
makes use of the additive group structure of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
.

Since the
p
-adic integers also carry a ring structure, the above suggests studying maps
C({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})\to C({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
of the form

f(x)\mapsto f(rx),

for
0\neq r\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
, from a C
∗
-algebra perspective. Such maps are not always automorphisms, and hence the most natural setting for this investigation are the crossed products by endomorphism C
∗
-algebras. Early notions of crossed products by endomorphism were introduced by
[
11
]
and also
[
8
]
. A similar, yet slightly different notion was suggested in
[
3
]
, and further improved upon in
[
4
]
. For the particular algebras considered in the present paper, these notions coincide.

In this paper, we study the structure of C
∗
-algebras which are crossed products of
C({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
by endomorphisms corresponding to
p
-adic multiplication for three interesting cases of
p
-adic integers
r
depending on the
p
-adic norm and the order of
r
. In particular, we relate those algebras with some familiar C
∗
-algebras and compute their
K
-Theory.

We start with a bit of necessary number theoretic preparations. We then discuss a closely related example of the Hensel-Steinitz C
∗
-algebra, a detailed study of which can be found in
[
5
]
. We conclude by providing an in depth exploration of crossed product of
C({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
by endomorphism associated with
p
-adic multiplication by
r
for different special cases of
r\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
.

In particular, when
r\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}
is an element of the
p
-adic unit sphere
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}
, and depending on whether
r
is or is not a root of unity in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}
, we find geometrical representations of the crossed product which can be used to characterize an ideal in it, which can in turn be used to compute the
K
-Theory of the full algebra. On the other hand, when
r\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
is divisible by
p^{N}
for some
N\geq 1
, we construct a C
∗
-algebra isomorphism with the Hensel-Steinitz C
∗
-algebra studied in
[
5
]
.

The discussion separates into cases depending on the dynamics of the map
x\mapsto rx
, and the methods of proof are based on finding faithful representations of the crossed products.

2.
C
∗
-Algebras Associated to p-Adic Multiplication

Here we introduce the main objects of study in this paper. Given a
p
-adic integer
r\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
,
r\neq 1
, consider the endomorphism
\alpha_{r}:C({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})\to C({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
given by

(\alpha_{r}f)(x)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &f\left(\frac{x}{r}\right)&&\textrm{ if }r|x\\
&0&&\textrm{else.}\end{aligned}\right.

Similarly, consider the map
\beta_{r}:C({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})\to C({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
given by

(\beta_{r}f)(x)=f(rx).

We have the following relation between
\alpha_{r}
and
\beta_{r}
:

(\beta_{r}\circ\alpha_{r})f(x)=f(x).

(2.1)

In what follows, we investigate the structure of the crossed product C
∗
-algebra by endomorphism
\alpha_{r}
:

A_{r}:=C({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})\rtimes_{\alpha_{r}}{\mathbb{N}},

utilizing the crossed product definition in
[
11
]
. Recall that the crossed product by endomorphism in our case is defined as the universal unital C
∗
-algebra generated by
C({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
, with generators denoted as
M_{f}
, for
f\in C({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
, and an additional generator
V_{r}
satisfying relations

V_{r}^{*}V_{r}=I,\quad V_{r}M_{f}V_{r}^{*}=M_{\alpha_{r}(f)}.

It is immediate from the appendix in
[
5
]
that
\alpha_{r}
is a monomorphism and that the range of
\alpha_{r}
is hereditary.

We briefly describe how the resulting crossed products differ in three distinct cases. First is the case when
r\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}
and
r
is not a root of unity, second is when
r
is a root of unity, while the third is the case when
r\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
satisfies
|r|_{p}=p^{-N}
,
N>0
. The remainder of the paper is dedicated to a more in depth investigation of each case.

2.1.
Case I:
C({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})\rtimes_{\alpha_{r}}{\mathbb{N}}
when
r\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}
and
r
is not a root of unity

A basic fact of
p
-adic integers is that if
|r|_{p}=1
, then
r
is invertible in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
with inverse satisfying
|r^{-1}|_{p}=1
. It follows that
\alpha_{r}
is an automorphism. Indeed, we have that

(\alpha_{r}\circ\beta_{r}f)(x)=(\beta_{r}f)(r^{-1}x)=f(x),

in addition to equation
2.1
. When this is the case, the crossed product by endomorphism coincides with the standard notion of a crossed product by automorphism. This is summarized in the following proposition.

Proposition 2.1
.

When
|r|_{p}=1
,
\alpha_{r}
is an automorphism and
A_{r}
becomes the standard crossed product:

A_{r}\cong C({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})\rtimes_{\alpha_{r}}{\mathbb{Z}}.

Proof.

Since
I=M_{1}=M_{\alpha_{r}(1)}
, where
1
denotes the constant function in
C({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
, we have that

V_{r}V_{r}^{*}=I

and therefore
V_{r}
is unitary.
Moreover, note that

M_{f}=V_{r}^{*}V_{r}M_{f}V_{r}^{*}V_{r}=V_{r}^{*}M_{\alpha_{r}(f)}V_{r}.

This is precisely
C({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})\rtimes_{\alpha_{r}}{\mathbb{Z}}
. This completes the proof.
∎

We study the structure of this algebra thoroughly in Section
5
.

2.2.
Case II:
C({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})\rtimes_{\alpha_{r}}{\mathbb{N}}
when
r\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}
and
r
is a root of unity

The considerations above depend only on the invertibility of
r
and not on
r
being a root of unity.
Thus, we also have in this case:

A_{r}\cong C({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})\rtimes_{\alpha_{r}}{\mathbb{Z}}.

However, if
r
is a root of unity, a similar object of interest is the crossed product:

A^{\prime}_{r}:=C({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})\rtimes_{\alpha_{r}}{\mathbb{Z}}/\!\ord(r){\mathbb{Z}},

where
\ord(r)
is the order of
r
in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}
and is also the order of
\alpha_{r}
, the smallest positive integer
n
such that
r^{n}=1
.
The algebras
A^{\prime}_{r}
and
A_{r}
are closely related but not in an obvious way.
The structure and
K
-Theory of those crossed product algebras is studied in Section
6
.

2.3.
Case III:
C({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})\rtimes_{\alpha_{r}}{\mathbb{N}}
when
r\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
and
|r|_{p}<1

When
|r|_{p}<1
,
r
is no longer invertible, and the notion of crossed product by endomorphism is needed. Following the definition in
[
11
]
, we have that
A_{r}
is the universal C
∗
-algebra generated by elements of
C({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
, along with an isometry
V_{r}
satisfying the relation
V_{r}M_{f}V_{r}^{*}=M_{\alpha_{r}(f)}
. Again, by the appendix in
[
5
]

\alpha_{r}
is a monomorphism and the range of
\alpha_{r}
is hereditary. We give a complete study of the structure and
K
-Theory of this algebra in Section
7
.

3.
Number Theory Preliminaries

In this section, we review some group theory which is used in the construction and investigation of the algebras introduced in Section
2
.
Specifically, given
r\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}
we consider the (closed) subgroup

G_{r}:=\overline{\{r^{k}:k\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}}
of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}
and the quotient group
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}/G_{r}
. To describe
G_{r}
and the quotient
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}/G_{r}
one could use the well-known structure results on
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}
that are obtained using p-adic logarithmic and exponential functions. Alternatively, and this is the more algebraic approach described below, one can use projective limit techniques.

First, we review the notion of a primitive root, and recall some of their useful properties and related concepts.

3.1.
Primitive Roots

Let
p
be any odd prime, and denote by
U_{N}
the set of elements of the ring
{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{N}{\mathbb{Z}}
which have a multiplicative inverse. It is easy to see that

U_{N}=\{0<k<p^{N}:\textup{gcd}(p,k)=1\},

and that
U_{N}
becomes a group when equipped with multiplication modulo
p^{N}
. The following theorem dates back to Gauss.

Theorem 3.1
.

(Gauss)
U_{N}
is isomorphic to the cyclic group of order
p^{N-1}(p-1)
.

The above result gives rise to the following definition.

Definition:

We say that an element
a\in U_{N}\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{N}{\mathbb{Z}}
is a primitive root of
U_{N}
if
a
generates
U_{N}
.

Primitive roots enjoy the following useful properties:

•

If
a
is a primitive root of
U_{1}
, then either
a
or
a+p
is a primitive root of
U_{2}
.

•

If
a
is a primitive root of
U_{N}
for
N\geq 2
, then
a
is also a primitive root of
U_{M}
for
M\geq N
.

3.2.
Subgroups of
U_{N}

Next we consider subgroups of
U_{N}
generated by a single element
r
. In particular, the order of an element
r
in
U_{N}
, denoted by
\ord_{U_{N}}(r)
, can be compared with its order in
U_{N+1}
provided that
r
satisfies certain properties. This result is summarized in the following proposition.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
r
be an integer not divisible by
p
be such that there exists
M\geq 1
such that
\ord_{U_{M}}(r)
is divisible by
p
. Let
N_{r}
denote the smallest such
M
. Then for all
N\geq N_{r}
, we have

\ord_{U_{N+1}}(r)=p\cdot\ord_{U_{N}}(r).

Proof.

Let
d_{N}
denote the order of
r
in
U_{N}
, and let
a
be a primitive root of
U_{N}
for all
N\geq 2
. Note that

r\equiv a^{\frac{p^{N}(p-1)}{d_{N+1}}}\textup{ mod }p^{N+1},

and hence

r\equiv a^{\frac{p^{N}(p-1)}{d_{N+1}}}\textup{ mod }p^{N},

since
p^{N}|p^{N+1}
. Using a standard order formula for cyclic groups, we have that

d_{N}=\frac{p^{N-1}(p-1)}{\textup{gcd}\left(p^{N-1}(p-1),\frac{p^{N}(p-1)}{d_{N+1}}\right)}.

Since
p|d_{N}
, and hence
p|d_{N+1}
, we see that

\textup{gcd}\left(p^{N-1}(p-1),\frac{p^{N}(p-1)}{d_{N+1}}\right)=\frac{p^{N}(p-1)}{d_{N+1}}.

Hence, we obtain:

d_{N}=\frac{p^{N-1}(p-1)}{\frac{p^{N}(p-1)}{d_{N+1}}}=\frac{d_{N+1}}{p}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Similarly, letting
G_{r,N}
denote the subgroup of
U_{N}
generated by
r
, the elements of
G_{r,M}
can be described in terms of the elements of
G_{r,N}
for
M\geq N
, provided that both
M
and
N
are sufficiently large. This description is summarized in the following proposition.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
r
and
N_{r}
be as in Proposition
3.2
. For all
N\geq N_{r}
, we have that

G_{r,N+1}=\{k\in U_{N+1}:k\textup{ mod }p^{N}\in G_{r,N}\}.

Proof.

If
k\in G_{r,N+1}
, then there is
\ell
such that

r^{\ell}\equiv k\textup{ mod }p^{N+1}.

Clearly
r^{\ell}\equiv k\textup{ mod }p^{N}
, and so

k\textup{ mod }p^{N}\in G_{r,N}.

This shows

G_{r,N+1}\subseteq\{k\in U_{N+1}:k\textup{ mod }p^{N}\in G_{r,N}\}.

However, by the proof of Proposition
3.2
, the cardinality of
G_{r,N+1}
is given by
pd_{N}
, where again
d_{N}
denotes the order of
r
in
U_{N}
. Moreover, elements of

\{k\in U_{N+1}:k\textup{ mod }p^{N}\in G_{r,N}\}

are given by

k=k^{\prime}+p^{N}k^{\prime\prime},

where
k^{\prime}\in G_{r,N}
, and
k^{\prime\prime}\in\{0,\dots,p-1\}
. There are precisely
pd_{N}
such elements. Hence, the sets coincide. This completes the proof.
∎

3.3.
P-Adic Integers

Given an integer
s>1
, and an increasing sequence of divisors
s|s^{2}|s^{3}\dots
, we have homomorphisms

\pi_{n,m}:{\mathbb{Z}}/s^{n}{\mathbb{Z}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}/s^{m}{\mathbb{Z}}

given by reduction mod
s^{m}
for
n\geq m
. This gives rise to a directed system, whose inverse limit we define to be
{\mathbb{Z}}_{s}
:

{\mathbb{Z}}_{s}:=\varprojlim{\mathbb{Z}}/s^{n}{\mathbb{Z}}.

Of particular interest in this paper is the case when
s
is an odd prime
p
and
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
is then the ring of p-adic integers.

As
U_{N}\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{N}{\mathbb{Z}}
is the group of units of
{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{N}{\mathbb{Z}}
, we obtain for the group of units of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
:

{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}:=\varprojlim U_{N}.

This is also termed the p-adic unit sphere, since it consists precisely of those
p
-adic integers with
p
-adic norm
1
, i.e. formal sums of the form
\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i}p^{i}
which are not divisible by
p
.

3.4.
Roots of Unity

A corollary of Hensel’s Lemma and Fermat’s Little Theorem is that within
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}
, there exist
p-1
roots of unity of orders dividing
p-1
. In other words, one can find p-adic integers
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}\dots,\omega_{p-1}
, such that for each
i
,

\omega_{i}^{p-1}=1.

Moreover, simple divisibility arguments show that there are no other p-adic roots of unity. The group of all p-adic roots of unity will be denoted
\mathcal{G}_{p}
.

The above roots are typically arranged such that for every
0\leq i\leq p-1
we have:

\omega_{i}\equiv i\textup{ mod }p.

It follows that any
z\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}
can be written uniquely as the product

z=\omega_{i}y

(3.1)

for some
0\leq i\leq p-1
and
y\in 1+p{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
. One can then write
y
as a p-adic exponential, giving a nice description of the group structure of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}
, though we will not use it in this paper.

3.5.
Groups
G_{r}

We now investigate only those
r\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}
which are not roots of unity. Restricting to such
r
allows for the following result which is a consequence of Proposition
3.2
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
r\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}
, and assume
r
is not a root of unity. Then there exists
N_{r}
such that for all
N\geq N_{r}
,

\ord_{U_{N+1}}(r)=p\cdot\ord_{U_{N}}(r).

Proof.

Let
d_{N}
denote the order of
r
in
U_{N}
. For any
N
,

d_{N}|p^{N}(p-1).

However, since

r^{d_{N+1}}\equiv 1\textup{ mod }p^{N+1}\implies r^{d_{N+1}}\equiv 1\textup{ mod }p^{N},

we see that
d_{N+1}\geq d_{N}
and that the orders are increasing. Since
r
is not a root of unity, and the orders grow as
N
increases, we see that eventually
d_{N}
must gain a power of
p
, i.e. there exists
N_{r}
such that
d_{N_{r}}
is divisible by
p
. Hence, the hypothesis of Proposition
3.2
is satisfied. The exact same argument contained in the proof of Proposition
3.2
shows the final result.
∎

The following proposition follows immediately from Proposition
3.3
.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
r\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}
and assume
r
is not a root of unity. Then for all
N\geq N_{r}
, we have

G_{r,N+1}=\{k\in U_{N+1}:k\textup{ mod }p^{N}\in G_{r,N}\}.

Given
r\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}
we consider the closed subgroup of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}
generated by
r
:

G_{r}:=\overline{\{r^{k}:k\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}}.

We have the following result regarding the quotient group
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}/G_{r}
. This is the main result of this section.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
N_{r}
be as in Proposition
3.4
, and assume
r\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}
is not a root of unity. We have the following isomorphism of groups:

{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}/G_{r}\cong U_{N_{r}}/G_{r,N_{r}}.

In particular,
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}/G_{r}
is a finite group.

Proof.

We first prove that

\varprojlim G_{r,N}=G_{r}.

Note that

\varprojlim G_{r,N}:=\{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}:x\textup{ mod }p^{N}\in G_{r,N}\textup{ }\forall N\geq 1\}.

Notice that
G_{r}\subseteq\varprojlim G_{r,N}
. Indeed, since powers of
r
are in
\varprojlim G_{r,N}
and
\varprojlim G_{r,N}
is closed, we have that inclusion for the closure:

\overline{\{r^{k}\}}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}=G_{r}\subseteq\varprojlim G_{r,N}.

To see the other inclusion, let
x\in\varprojlim G_{r,N}
. For any
M\geq 1
we have:

x\textup{ mod }p^{M}\in G_{r,M}.

Hence, by definition there is
k_{M}
such that

r^{k_{M}}\equiv x\textup{ mod }p^{M}.

But this means that

|r^{k_{M}}-x|_{p}\leq p^{-M}.

Hence,
r^{k_{M}}\to x
in
p
-adic norm as
M\to\infty
. Since
\{r^{k_{M}}\}_{M=1}^{\infty}\subseteq G_{r}
, it follows
x\in G_{r}
. This proves that

\varprojlim G_{r,N}=G_{r}.

Next, we give a more useful interpretation of
\varprojlim G_{r,N}
. If
x\in G_{r,N_{r}}
, then certainly
x\in G_{r,N}
for
N\leq N_{r}
. However, by Proposition
3.5
,
G_{r,N_{r}+1}
is identified with those
k\in U_{N_{r}+1}
satisfying

k\textup{ mod }p^{N_{r}}\in G_{r,N_{r}}.

Applying this proposition
M-N_{r}
consecutive times, we see that
G_{r,M}
consists of elements of the form

k_{0}+p^{N_{r}}k_{N_{r}}+p^{N_{r}+1}k_{N_{r}+1}+\dots+p^{M-1}k_{M-1},

where
k_{0}\in G_{r,N_{r}}
and
k_{i}\in\{0,\dots,p-1\}
for
i\neq 0
. This shows that

\varprojlim G_{r,N}=\{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}:x\textup{ mod }p^{N_{r}}\in G_{r,N_{r}}\}.

Consequently, the map

{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}/G_{r}\to U_{N_{r}}/G_{r,N_{r}}

given by

xG_{r}\mapsto(x\textup{ mod }p^{N_{r}})\,G_{r,N_{r}}

is an isomorphism. Hence, we have that

{\mathbb{Z}}^{\times}_{p}/G_{r}=\varprojlim U_{N}/\varprojlim G_{r,N}\cong U_{N_{r}}/G_{r,N_{r}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

4.
The Hensel-Steinitz Algebras

The Hensel-Steinitz algebras, introduced and studied in
[
5
]
, are closely related to the objects of study in this paper. In fact, we show in Section
6
that for appropriate
r
, the C
∗
-algebra associated with
p
-adic multiplication by
r
is an example of a Hensel-Steinitz algebra. In what follows, we discuss the definition of the Hensel-Steinitz algebras, and briefly review their structure and
K
-theory. For a more detailed discussion of the content of this section, we direct the reader to
[
5
]
.

4.1.
Endomorphisms of
C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s})

Let
s>1
be an integer, not necessarily a prime, and let
{\mathbb{Z}}_{s}
be the
s
-adic ring defined in the previous section. Define a map
\alpha_{s}:C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s})\to C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s})
by

(\alpha_{s}f)(x)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &f\left(\frac{x}{s}\right)&&\textrm{ if }s|x\\
&0&&\textrm{else}\end{aligned}\right.

Similarly, define a map
\beta_{s}:C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s})\to C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s})
via

(\beta_{s}f)(x)=f(sx).

We see that both maps are indeed endomorphisms of
C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s})
. We have the following relation between
\alpha_{s}
and
\beta_{s}
:

(\beta_{s}\circ\alpha_{s})f(x)=\beta_{s}(\alpha_{s}(f))(x)=(\alpha_{s}f)(sx)=f(x)

for any
f\in C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s})
. Thus,
\alpha_{s}
is an injection.

4.2.
The Hensel-Steinitz Algebra as a Crossed Product

We define the Hensel-Steinitz algebra to be the following crossed product by endomorphism
\alpha_{s}
:

HS(s)=C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s})\rtimes_{\alpha_{s}}{\mathbb{N}}\,,

where the above crossed product by endomorphism is in the sense of
[
11
]
: the universal unital C
∗
-algebra generated by the shift
V_{s}
and multiplication operator
M_{f}
satisfying the relations

V_{s}^{*}V_{s}=I,\ \ V_{s}M_{f}V_{s}^{*}=M_{\alpha_{s}(f)}.

This algebra can be represented faithfully on
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0})
as the C
∗
-algebra generated by a shift, denoted below by
V
, and by multiplication operators. Indeed, consider
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0})
and let
\{E_{l}\}_{l\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}}
be its canonical basis. Since
{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
is a dense subset of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{s}
, the mapping

C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s})\to B(\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0})),\ f\mapsto\mu_{f},

with
\mu_{f}
given by

\mu_{f}E_{l}=f(l)E_{l},

is a faithful representation of
C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s})
on
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0})
. Let
V
be the following shift operator defined on
E_{l}
by

VE_{l}=E_{sl}\,.

(4.1)

A simple calculation verifies that

V^{*}E_{l}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &E_{l/s}&&\textrm{ if }s|l\\
&0&&\textrm{else.}\end{aligned}\right.

We state here the following proposition, which is proved in
[
5
]
.

Proposition 4.1
.

We have the following isomorphism between C
∗
-algebras:

HS(s)=C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s})\rtimes_{\alpha}{\mathbb{N}}\cong C^{*}(V,\mu_{f}:f\in C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s}))\,.

4.3.
The Structure and
K
-Theory of
HS(s)

To understand the structure of
HS(s)
we look at the ideal, denoted
J_{s}
, which is introduced in the following way.
Consider the map
C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s})\to B(\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}))
which sends
f\mapsto m_{f}
, where
m_{f}
is given by

m_{f}E_{l}=f(0)E_{l}.

Additionally, let

denote the standard bilateral shift

It is easy to check that sending the universal generators

We define

We now have the following proposition regarding the structure of

. For a detailed proof of the following result, see
[

]
.

Proposition 4.2
.

We have the following isomorphism of C
∗
-algebras:

J_{s}\cong C({\mathbb{Z}}_{s}^{\times})\otimes\mathcal{K}\,,

where
{\mathbb{Z}}_{s}^{\times}
denotes the unit sphere in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{s}

This fact leads to the following short exact sequence for

The above sequence can be used to compute the

The

5.
Structure of the Crossed Product: Case I

We study the structure of

via an ideal, denoted by

We begin this section by introducing the ideal and corresponding short exact sequence. Then, after demonstrating a number of irreducible, infinite dimensional representations of

on the Hilbert space

is as in Corollary
3.4
. Using this faithful representation, we describe the ideal

in terms of familiar C
∗

to be computed via the Künneth formula, which in turn allows the

-Theory of

to be computed via the
6
-term exact sequence in

-Theory.
Throughout this section,

which is not a root of unity.

5.1.
Representations of
A_{r}

Let
\{E_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
denote the canonical basis for
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}})
, and consider the representation
\pi_{0}:A_{r}\to B(\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}))
given by the following:

\displaystyle V_{r}\mapsto v

\displaystyle\textrm{ where }\quad vE_{k}=E_{k+1}

\displaystyle M_{f}\mapsto m_{f}

\displaystyle\textrm{ where }\quad m_{f}E_{k}=f(0)E_{k}.

Denote the kernel of this representation by
I_{r}:=\textup{Ker}\,\pi_{0}
. From the same reasoning as in Section
4
, it is clear that the image of
\pi_{0}
can be identified with
C(S^{1})
, and we have the following proposition.

Proposition 5.1
.

We have the following short exact sequence:

0\to I_{r}\to A_{r}\to C(S^{1})\to 0.

There are a number of important representations which are related to the orbits
\{r^{k}x:k\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}
. We summarize these representations and characterize their images in the following proposition.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
r\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}
and assume
r
is not a root of unity. Let
N_{r}
be as defined in Propositions
3.2
and
3.3
. For any fixed nonzero
x\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
, the map
\pi_{x}:A_{r}\to B(\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}))
defined by

\displaystyle V_{r}\mapsto v

\displaystyle\textrm{ where }\quad vE_{k}=E_{k+1}

\displaystyle M_{f}\mapsto M_{r,x}(f)

\displaystyle\textrm{ where }\quad M_{r,x}(f)E_{k}=f(r^{k}x)E_{k}

defines a representation of
A_{r}
. Moreover, there exists an isomorphism of C
∗
-algebras:

\pi_{x}(A_{r})\cong\textup{BD}(\ord_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{\times}_{p}}(r)),

where
\textup{BD}(S)
denotes the Bunce-Deddens algebra with supernatural number
S
, and
\ord_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{\times}_{p}}(r)
denotes the supernatural number given by
\textup{lcm}\{\ord_{U_{N}}(r):N\geq 1\}
.

Proof.

It is clear that
\pi_{x}
defines a representation of
A_{r}
. To see that the image can be identified with a Bunce-Deddens algebra, consider a locally constant (
i.e.,
periodic) function
f
on
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
of period
p^{N}
. We examine the period of the sequence
k\mapsto f(r^{k}x)
. Note that by definition
|G_{r,N}|
is the smallest number such that
r^{k+|G_{r,N}|}\equiv r^{k}(\textup{mod }p^{N})
for any
k
. Hence
r^{|G_{r,N}|}-1
is divisible by
p^{N}
, and therefore so is
xr^{k}(r^{|G_{r,N}|}-1)
. It follows that for any
k
we have
f(xr^{k})=f(xr^{k+|G_{r,N}|})
and that the sequence
k\mapsto f(r^{k}x)
has period
|G_{r,N}|
.
Since periodic functions of arbitrarily large period are dense in
C({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
, by the Stone-Weierstrass Theorem, it follows that
\pi_{x}(A_{r})
is generated by the unitary shift operator
v
, along with periodic diagonal operators of period
|G_{r,N}|
, for any
N\geq 1
. Hence it follows from the definition of Bunce-Deddens algebras that
\pi_{x}(A_{r})\cong\textup{BD}(\ord_{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}}(r))
.
∎

Remark:

We note that since
|r|_{p}=1
, the orbit
\{xr^{k}:k\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}
lies in the
p
-adic sphere with radius
|x|_{p}
, and hence the representation
\pi_{x}(f)
depends only on the values of
f
on that sphere. More specifically,
\pi_{x}(f)
depends only on
f
restricted to
\overline{\{r^{k}x:k\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}}
.

5.2.
The Structure of
I_{r}
and
A_{r}

Using the decomposition of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
into orbits of
G_{r}
, we have the following theorem describing a faithful representation of
A_{r}
.

Theorem 5.3
.

Suppose
r\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}
and assume that
r
is not a root of unity. Let
\gamma:{\mathbb{Z}}^{\times}_{p}/G_{r}\to{\mathbb{Z}}^{\times}_{p}
denote a section of the quotient map
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}\to{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}/G_{r}
. The following map defines a faithful representation of
A_{r}:

\pi:A_{r}\to B(\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}/G_{r})),

given by

\displaystyle V_{r}\mapsto v_{r}

\displaystyle\textrm{ where }\quad v_{r}\phi(k,L,xG_{r})=\phi(k-1,L,xG_{r})

\displaystyle M_{f}\mapsto\mathcal{M}_{f}

\displaystyle\textrm{ where }\quad\mathcal{M}_{f}\phi(k,L,xG_{r})=f(r^{k}p^{L}\cdot\gamma(xG_{r}))\phi(k,L,xG_{r}).

Proof.

First note that

v_{r}^{*}\mathcal{M}_{f}v_{r}\phi(k,L,xG_{r})=f(r^{k+1}p^{L}\gamma(xG_{r}))\phi(k,L,xG_{r})=\mathcal{M}_{\beta_{r}(f)}\phi(k,L,xG_{r}),

so the relations are preserved. Before proceeding, we remark that since the quotient
{\mathbb{Z}}^{\times}_{p}/G_{r}
is discrete, by Theorem
3.6
, the map
\gamma:{\mathbb{Z}}^{\times}_{p}/G_{r}\to{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}
is automatically continuous. Additionally, the representation is faithful on the subalgebra
C^{*}(\mathcal{M}_{f}:f\in C({\mathbb{Z}}_{p}))
. Indeed, suppose that
f(r^{k}p^{L}\gamma(xG_{r}))=0
for all
L,k,x
. We show that
f
must vanish on each sphere. Fix
\varepsilon>0
. We wish to show that for any
x\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}
,
|f(xp^{L})|\leq\varepsilon
. Since
f
is continuous, choose
\delta
so that
|f(y)-f(p^{L}x)|_{p}<\varepsilon
whenever
|x-y|_{p}<\delta
. Note that for any
x\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}
, there exists
g
in
G_{r}
such that
x=\gamma(xG_{r})\cdot g
. Since
g\in G_{r}
, there is
k
such that
|g-r^{k}|_{p}<\delta
. Note that

|p^{L}x-p^{L}r^{k}\gamma(xG_{r})|=|p^{L}\gamma(xG_{r})\cdot g-p^{L}r^{k}\gamma(xG_{r})|_{p}=p^{-L}|g-r^{k}|_{p}<\delta,

and so

|f(p^{L}x)-f(p^{L}r^{k}\gamma(xG_{r})|_{p}=|f(p^{L}x)|_{p}<\varepsilon.

Hence,
f
vanishes on each sphere and so
f=0
. This shows the representation is faithful on
C^{*}(\mathcal{M}_{f}:f\in C({\mathbb{Z}}_{p}))
.

To see that the representation is faithful, we use the adaptation of the O’Donovan condition to crossed products by endomorphism, as described in
[
1
]
; see also the appendix to
[
5
]
. Define a one-parameter group of unitaries

U_{r}(\theta):\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}/G_{r})\to\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}/G_{r})

by

U_{r}(\theta)\phi(k,L,xG_{r}):=e^{2\pi ik\theta}\phi(k,L,xG_{r}).

Next, for
a\in C^{*}(v_{r},\mathcal{M}_{f})
, we define
\rho_{\theta}(a)
by

\rho_{\theta}(a):=U_{r}(\theta)aU_{r}(-\theta).

Note that on generators of
A_{r}
morphisms
\rho_{\theta}
act as follows:

\rho_{\theta}(v_{r})=e^{2\pi i\theta}v_{r},

and

\rho_{\theta}(\mathcal{M}_{f})=\mathcal{M}_{f},

so
\rho_{\theta}
is a continuous one-parameter group of automorphisms of
A_{r}
with invariant subalgebra given by
C^{*}(\mathcal{M}_{f}:f\in C({\mathbb{Z}}_{p}))
.

Consider an expectation defined by

\mathbb{E}(a):=\int_{0}^{1}\rho_{\theta}(a)d\theta.

Clearly we have:

\|\mathbb{E}(a)\|\leq\int_{0}^{1}\|U_{r}(\theta)\|\|a\|\|U_{r}(-\theta)\|d\theta\leq\|a\|.

Given
a
of the form
a=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}v_{r}^{n}\mathcal{M}_{f_{n}}
, we see that

E(a)=\mathcal{M}_{f_{0}},

and so, combining the two observations we obtain

\|\mathcal{M}_{f_{0}}\|\leq\|a\|.

Hence, the representation satisfies O’Donovan’s conditions and is therefore faithful. This completes the proof.
∎

In light of this representation, we can easily deduce the structure of
I_{r}
.

Proposition 5.4
.

Suppose that
r\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}
is not a root of unity. There exists an isomorphism of C
∗
-algebras:

I_{r}\cong c_{0}({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0})\otimes\textup{BD}(\ord_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{\times}_{p}}(r)).

Proof.

Notice that since
\pi_{0}(I_{r})=0
by definition,
\mathcal{M}_{f}\in I_{r}
if and only if
f(0)=0
for
f\in C({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
. Consequently,
I_{r}
can be described as follows:

I_{r}=C^{*}(v_{r}^{n}\mathcal{M}_{f}:n\in{\mathbb{Z}},f\in C({\mathbb{Z}}_{p}),f(0)=0).

Next, we decompose
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
into orbits of
G_{r}
. Any nonzero
x\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
can be uniquely written as a product:

x=p^{L}\,\gamma(jG_{r})\,g,

where
L\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
,
g\in G_{r}
, and
jG_{r}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}/G_{r}
.

If
f(0)=0
and
f\in C({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
, then by continuity we get:

\lim_{L\to\infty}f(p^{L}\,\gamma(jG_{r})\,g)=0.

This limit is uniform in
j
and
g
because
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}/G_{r}
is finite and
G_{r}
is compact. Finiteness of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}/G_{r}
also implies that
G_{r}
is open. It follows from the Stone-Weierstrass Theorem that we can identify the space of continuous functions on
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
vanishing at zero with the space of sequences indexed by
{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}/G_{r}
and converging to
0
with values in
C(G_{r})
:

c_{0}({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}/G_{r},C(G_{r})).

Notice that in the above identification, the action of
\alpha_{r}
is only on
C(G_{r})
.

Denote by
\widehat{\pi}
the representation of
C(G_{r})\rtimes_{\alpha_{r}}{\mathbb{Z}}
in
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}})
generated
\widehat{M}(f)
,
f\in C(G_{r})
and by
v
, defined by

\displaystyle vE_{k}

\displaystyle=E_{k+1}

\displaystyle\widehat{M}(f)E_{k}

\displaystyle=f(r^{k})E_{k}.

Notice that
\widehat{\pi}
has image
*
-isomorphic with the Bunce-Deddens algebra
\textup{BD}(\ord_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{\times}_{p}}(r))
by Proposition
5.2
and thus
C(G_{r})\rtimes_{\alpha_{r}}{\mathbb{Z}}\cong\textup{BD}(\ord_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{\times}_{p}}(r))
.

It is convenient to identify the Hilbert space of the representation
\pi
as follows:

\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}/G_{r})\cong\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}/G_{r},\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}})).

With this identification the operator
v_{r}
becomes the bilateral shift
v
acting on values of sequences in
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}/G_{r},\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}))
.

Now
I_{r}
can be identified with the algebra of sequences indexed by
{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}/G_{r}
and converging to
0
with values in
\textup{BD}(\ord_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{\times}_{p}}(r))
:

c_{0}({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}/G_{r},\textup{BD}(\ord_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{\times}_{p}}(r))).

(5.1)

Indeed, this algebra acts faithfully in
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}/G_{r},\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}))
by acting pointwise on values of sequences in
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}/G_{r},\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}))
:

\phi(L,jG_{r})\mapsto\widehat{\pi}(F(L,jG_{r}))\phi(L,jG_{r}),

where, for every
L
and
jG_{r}
, we have
\phi(L,jG_{r})\in\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}})
and
F(L,jG_{r})\in\textup{BD}(\ord_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{\times}_{p}}(r))
.
Clearly, the algebra generated by those operators, by above identifications, coincides with the algebra generated by
v_{r}^{n}\mathcal{M}_{f}
where
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
f\in C({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
with
f(0)=0
.

The above algebra, equation (
5.1
), can be identified with
c_{0}({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}/G_{r})\otimes\textup{BD}(\ord_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{\times}_{p}}(r))
. Since
{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}/G_{r}
is countable, it is bijective with
{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
and we get

c_{0}({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}/G_{r})\cong c_{0}({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}).

This completes the proof.
∎

The above results allow the

-Theory of

along with the Künneth formula. We need the following additive subgroup of

H_{S}:=\left\{\frac{k}{l}\in{\mathbb{Q}}:k\in{\mathbb{Z}},l|S\right\}

(5.2)

for a supernatural number

.

Proposition 5.5
.

Suppose

is not a root of unity. The

is given by

K_{0}(I_{r})\cong c_{0}\left({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0},H_{\ord_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{\times}_{p}}(r)}\right)\textrm{ and }K_{1}(I_{r})\cong c_{0}({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0},{\mathbb{Z}})

K_{0}
-group of the Bunce-Deddens algebra with supernatural number
\ord_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{\times}_{p}}(r)
, as defined by equation (

c_{0}({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0},{\mathbb{Z}})

denote sequences that are eventually zero with values in
{\mathbb{Z}}
and

respectively.

Proof.

Note first that
c_{0}({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0})
is an inductive limit of finite dimensional algebras of sequences which are eventually
0
. Hence,
c_{0}({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0})
is AF and

(by Exercise 8.7 in
[

]
), while

K_{0}(c_{0}({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}))\cong c_{0}({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0},{\mathbb{Z}}),

which follows from Exercise 3.4 in
[

. The

-Theory of
\textup{BD}(\ord_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{\times}_{p}}(r))
was computed in
[

]
, and is given by

K_{0}(\textup{BD}(\ord_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{\times}_{p}}(r)))\cong H_{\ord_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{\times}_{p}}(r)},\textup{ and }K_{1}(\textup{BD}(\ord_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{\times}_{p}}(r)))={\mathbb{Z}}.

Since
\textup{BD}(\ord_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{\times}_{p}}(r))
is in the Bootstrap Class, and the

, we have isomorphisms

K_{0}(c_{0}({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0})\otimes\textup{BD}(\ord_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{\times}_{p}}(r)))\cong(c_{0}({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0},{\mathbb{Z}})\otimes H_{\ord_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{\times}_{p}}(r)})\oplus({\mathbb{Z}}\otimes 0)\cong c_{0}({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0},{\mathbb{Z}})\otimes H_{\ord_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{\times}_{p}}(r)},

completes the proof.

-Theory of

can be computed from the

The

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{0}(I_{r})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{0}(A_{r})}

ind

\textup{ind}([v]_{1})=[1-V_{r}^{*}V_{r}]_{0}-[1-V_{r}V_{r}^{*}]_{0}=0,

This is a useful and simple result that is hard to find explicitly stated in the literature.

6.
Structure of the Crossed Product: Case II

We analyze here concurrently both algebras

A^{\prime}_{r}:=C({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})\rtimes_{\alpha_{r}}{\mathbb{Z}}/\ord(r){\mathbb{Z}}\quad\textrm{and}\quad A_{r}\cong C({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})\rtimes_{\alpha_{r}}{\mathbb{Z}}

\displaystyle V_{r}\mapsto v^{\prime}

\displaystyle\textup{where}\quad v^{\prime}E_{k}=E_{k+1\text{ mod }(\ord(r))}

\displaystyle M_{f}\mapsto m_{f}^{\prime}

For any fixed nonzero

of

Similarly, since the orbits are finite, we have the following finite dimensional representations

\pi_{x}^{\prime}:A_{r}^{\prime}\to B(\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}/\ord(r){\mathbb{Z}}))

\displaystyle V_{r}\mapsto V_{r}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\textup{where }

\displaystyle V_{r}^{\prime}E_{k}=E_{k+1\text{ mod }(\ord(r))}

\displaystyle M_{f}\mapsto M_{r,x}^{\prime}

Proposition 6.1
.

Suppose that
r\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times}
is a p-adic root of unity. There exist isomorphisms of C
∗

\pi_{x}^{\prime}(A_{r}^{\prime})\cong M_{\ord(r)}({\mathbb{C}}),

\pi_{x}(A_{r})\cong C(S^{1})\otimes M_{\ord(r)}({\mathbb{C}}).

Proof.

Since the representation
\pi_{x}^{\prime}
is irreducible,
\pi_{x}^{\prime}(A_{r}^{\prime})
must be the full matrix algebra.

To see the claimed isomorphism for

, we observe that the algebra is generated by the following set of generators:

u:=v^{\ord(r)},

and

P_{i,j}:=v^{i}P_{0}v^{-j},

for

is the orthogonal projection onto the span of

with

divisible by
\ord(r)
.

Indeed, it is easy to see that the subalgebra generated by all
M_{r,x}(f)
is just the algebra of all diagonal operators in
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}})
whose diagonal elements are
\ord(r)
periodic. But this is precisely the algebra generated by
P_{i,i}
,
i=0,\ldots,\ord(r)-1

Additionally a simple calculation shows that we have:

v=P_{1,0}+P_{2,1}+\ldots+P_{\ord,(r)-1,\ord(r)-2}+uP_{0,\ord(r)-1}.

Thus, one can express

∎

The following theorem describes faithful representations of

Proposition 6.2

Suppose that

\displaystyle V_{r}\mapsto v_{r}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\textup{where }

\displaystyle\textup{where }

\displaystyle\textup{where }

From the definition of

Then, utilizing equation (

7.
Structure of the Crossed Product: Case III

In this section we find that when
|r|_{p}=p^{-N}
, with
N>0
, the crossed product can be identified with a Hensel-Steinitz algebra. This is done by constructing a dense subset of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
on which multiplication by
r
takes a similar form algebraically as multiplication by
s
for the standard
s
-adic integers. We then use this subset to construct a faithful representation of
A_{r}
which is unitarily equivalent to a faithful representation of the Hensel-Steinitz algebras mentioned in Section
4
.

7.1.
A Faithful Representation of
A_{r}

Consider an integer
r
with
|r|_{p}=p^{-N}
. Hence, there is
r^{\prime}
with
|r^{\prime}|_{p}=1
, such that
r=r^{\prime}p^{N}
. Denote
\mathcal{D}^{(N)}_{r}
to be the following subset of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
:

\mathcal{D}^{(N)}_{r}=\left\{x_{0}+x_{1}r+x_{2}r^{2}+\dots+x_{n}r^{n}:n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0},\,x_{i}\in\{0,\dots,p^{N}-1\}\right\}.

We have the following lemma.

Lemma 7.1
.

\mathcal{D}^{(N)}_{r}
is a dense subset of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
.

Proof.

We can construct an approximation of any
x\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
by elements of
\mathcal{D}^{(N)}_{r}
by repeatedly using the following division algorithm:
there exist
q,c\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
such that:

x=qr+c

(7.1)

with
c\in\{0,\dots,p^{N}-1\}
. Thus, we only need to establish this algorithm.

To construct
q,c
consider the expansion

x=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}x_{i}p^{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p},

with
x_{i}\in\{0,\dots,p-1\}
. We let
c=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}x_{i}p^{i}
, and notice that

|x-c|_{p}\leq p^{-N}=|r|_{p}.

Consequently,
r
divides
x-c
in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
giving the quotient
q
in the formula (
7.1
).
This completes the proof.
∎

The above lemma allows one to construct the following faithful representation of
A_{r}
.

Proposition 7.2
.

The map
\tilde{\pi}:A_{r}\to B(\ell^{2}(\mathcal{D}^{(N)}_{r}))
given by

\displaystyle V_{r}\mapsto v_{r}

\displaystyle\text{ where }

\displaystyle v_{r}E_{x}=E_{rx}

\displaystyle M_{f}\mapsto M_{r,f}

\displaystyle\text{ where }

\displaystyle M_{r,f}E_{x}=f(x)E_{x}

defines a faithful representation of
A_{r}
.

Proof.

It is clear that
\tilde{\pi}
defines a
*
-representation of
A_{r}
. To show the O’Donovan conditions, we proceed similarly to what we described in previous sections. First, we introduce a one-parameter group of unitary operators
U_{r}(\theta):\ell^{2}(\mathcal{D}_{r}^{(N)})\to\ell^{2}(\mathcal{D}_{r}^{(N)})
given by

U_{r}(\theta)E_{x}=e^{2\pi i\theta\kappa(x)}E_{x},

where
\kappa(x)=n
for
x=\sum_{i=0}^{n}x_{i}r^{i}
and
x_{n}\neq 0
. The key property of
\kappa(x)
is:

\kappa(rx)=\kappa(x)+1.

For
a\in C^{*}(v_{r},M_{r,f}:f\in C({\mathbb{Z}}_{p}))
, define also

\rho_{\theta}(a)=U_{r}(\theta)aU_{r}(-\theta),

as well as an expectation

\mathbb{E}(a)=\int_{0}^{1}\rho_{\theta}(a)d\theta.

Clearly, we have:

\|\mathbb{E}(a)\|\leq\int_{0}^{1}\|U_{r}(\theta)\|\|a\|\|U_{r}(-\theta)\|d\theta\leq\|a\|.

Note also that

\rho_{\theta}(v_{r})=e^{2\pi i\theta}v_{r},

while

\rho_{\theta}(M_{r,f})=M_{r,f}.

Let
a
be of the form

a=\sum_{m\geq 0}v^{m}_{r}M_{r,f_{m}}+\sum_{m<0}M_{r,f_{m}}(v^{*}_{r})^{-m},

where the sums are finite. A straightforward calculation shows that all terms for which
m\neq 0
will have an expectation of
0
.
Hence,

\mathbb{E}(a)=M_{r,f_{0}},

and, for
a
as above, we have

\|M_{r,f_{0}}\|\leq\|a\|.

Since the above formula holds, by Proposition 2.1 in
[
1
]
, it suffices to check that the representation is faithful on the invariant part, which is generated by
M_{r,f}
for
f\in C({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
. If
M_{r,f}
vanishes, then
f
must vanish on
\mathcal{D}^{(N)}_{r}\subseteq C({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
, which is dense. It follows by continuity that
f=0
, and the representation is faithful.
∎

7.2.
A_{r}
as a Hensel-Steinitz Algebra with
s=p^{N}

Consider the Hensel-Steinitz algebra associated with
s=p^{N}
. Denote the faithful representation of
HS(s)
on
\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0})
described in Section
4
by
\pi_{V}:HS(s)\to B(\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}))
. In what follows, we construct a unitary equivalence between
\tilde{\pi}
and
\pi_{V}
. Consider the map
\mathcal{U}:\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0})\to\ell^{2}(\mathcal{D}^{(N)}_{r})
defined on basis elements as follows:

\mathcal{U}E_{k(x)}=E_{x},

where
k(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}x_{i}p^{iN}
and
x=\sum_{i=0}^{n}x_{i}r^{i}
, with
x_{i}\in\{0,\dots,p^{N}-1\}
. It is easy to check this map is invertible, and satisfies

\mathcal{U}V\mathcal{U}^{-1}=v_{r},

where again
V
is as defined equation (
4.1
) with
s=p^{N}
. Similarly, we have that

C^{*}(\mathcal{U}M_{f}\mathcal{U}^{-1}:f\in C({\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{N}}))=C^{*}(M_{r,f})\cong C({\mathbb{Z}}_{p^{N}}).

These considerations, along with the facts that
\tilde{\pi}
is a faithful representation of
A_{r}
and
\pi_{V}
is a faithful representation of
HS(s)
, immediately lead to the following proposition.

Proposition 7.3
.

Let
r=r^{\prime}p^{N}
, with
|r^{\prime}|_{p}=1
. We have the following isomorphism of C
∗
-algebras:

A_{r}\cong HS(s),

where
HS(s)
denotes the Hensel-Steinitz algebra corresponding to

.

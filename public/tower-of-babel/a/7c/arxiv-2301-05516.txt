Charlie Dworaczek Guera, Ronan Memin

Abstract
We establish a central limit theorem for the fluctuations of the linear statistics in the β \beta -ensemble of dimension N N at a temperature proportional to N N and with confining smooth potential. In this regime, the particles do not accumulate in a compact set as in the fixed β > 0 \beta>0 case which results in an equilibrium measure supported on the whole real line. The space of test functions for which the CLT holds includes bounded 𝒞 2 \mathcal{C}^{2} functions. The method that we use is based on a change of variables in the partition function introduced in [ Joh98 ] and allows to deduce the convergence of the Laplace transform of the recentred linear statistics towards the Laplace transform of the normal distribution. It is obtained by the inversion of the master operator, which is the main contribution of the present paper, by following the scheme developed in [ HL21 ] in the compact case. In the high-temperature regime, the master operator contains an additional differential term due to entropic effects which makes it an unbounded operator. The techniques used in this article involve Schrödinger operators theory as well as concentration of measure.



1
Introduction and main result

1.1
Setting of the problem

The
\beta
-ensemble of dimension
N\geqslant 1
with parameter
\beta>0
and potential
V
, is the probability measure on
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
given by:

d\mathbf{P}^{\beta,V}_{N}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})=\frac{1}{Z_{N}(V,\beta)}\prod_{i<j}|x_{i}-x_{j}|^{\beta}e^{-\frac{N\beta}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}V(x_{i})}dx_{1}\ldots dx_{N}\,.

(1)

The potential
V
has to be chosen so that the partition function

Z_{N}(V,\beta)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\prod_{i<j}|x_{i}-x_{j}|^{\beta}e^{-\frac{N\beta}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}V(x_{i})}dx_{1}\ldots dx_{N}

is finite. This is the case for example if

\liminf_{|x|\to\infty}\frac{V(x)}{2\ln|x|}>1\,,

(2)

see
[
AGZ10
, equation (2.6.2)]
. The parameter
\beta
, which is allowed to depend on
N
, is the so-called inverse temperature.

Under the special choice of the quadratic potential
V_{G}(x)=x^{2}/2
, the measure (
1
) can be seen as the joint law of the (unordered) eigenvalues of certain matrix models:

•

For
\beta=1
(resp.
\beta=2
), it is the law of the eigenvalues of the Gaussian Orthogonal Ensemble (resp. Gaussian Unitary Ensemble), rescaled by a factor
1/\sqrt{N}
, see
[
AGZ10
, Theorem 2.5.2]
.

•

For general
\beta>0
, potentially depending on
N
, it is the law of the spectrum of certain tri-diagonal random matrices as shown by Dumitriu and Edelman in
[
DE02
]
.

We consider here the
high temperature regime
where
\beta
scales as
1/N
, and write
\beta=2P/N
for some
P>0
. Up to absorbing a factor
2P
in the potential, the corresponding measure is therefore

d\mathbb{P}^{V,P}_{N}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})\overset{(\mathrm{def})}{=}\frac{1}{\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{P}[V]}\prod_{i<j}|x_{i}-x_{j}|^{\frac{2P}{N}}e^{-\sum_{i=1}^{N}V(x_{i})}dx_{1}\ldots dx_{N}\,,

(3)

with partition function

\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{P}[V]\overset{(\mathrm{def})}{=}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\prod_{i<j}|x_{i}-x_{j}|^{\frac{2P}{N}}e^{-\sum_{i=1}^{N}V(x_{i})}dx_{1}\ldots dx_{N}\,.

(4)

It was shown in
[
GZ19
]
that under
\mathbb{P}^{V,P}_{N}
, the sequence of empirical measures

\hat{\mu}_{N}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{x_{i}}

satisfies a large deviation principle at speed
N
with strictly convex, good rate function. This result can be seen as the generalization of Sanov and Varadhan theorems dealing with the singular behavior of the logarithmic interaction. As a consequence,
\hat{\mu}_{N}
converges almost surely in distribution towards a deterministic measure
\mu^{P}_{V}
as
N
goes to infinity, meaning that almost surely, for every bounded continuous
f:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)d\hat{\mu}_{N}(x)\underset{N\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)d\mu^{P}_{V}(x)\,.

The limiting measure
\mu^{P}_{V}
can be seen to have a density
\rho^{P}_{V}
which satisfies for almost every
x\in{\mathbb{R}}

V(x)-2P\int_{\mathbb{R}}\ln|x-y|\rho^{P}_{V}(y)dy+\ln\rho^{P}_{V}(x)=\lambda_{V}^{P}\,,

(5)

where
\lambda_{V}^{P}
is constant (see
[
GM22
, Lemma 3.2]
for example).

1.2
Connection with the literature

The high-temperature regime

The
\beta
-ensemble in the regime
\beta N=2P>0
has drawn a lot of attention from the random matrix and statistical physics communities lately. This regime was first considered by
[
CL97
]
with the study of Dyson Brownian motion with vanishing repulsive coefficient scaled like
N^{-1}
. Gases of vortices were also studied with temperature proportional to
N
in
[
BG99
]
. The limiting density was then described in the case of the quadratic potential in
[
ABG12
]
, as a crossover between the Wigner semicircle law (fixed
\beta>0
case) and the Gaussian density (case
\beta=0
). The local statistics of the system in the bulk were studied for quadratic potentials in
[
BGP15
,
NT18
]
and later generalized to more general potentials in
[
NT20
]
. The edge was also studied in
[
Pak18
]
. These results both in the bulk and at the edge were generalized in
[
Lam21
]
to more general geometric settings and interactions. In both cases the limiting microscopic behavior is shown to be Poissonian which can be seen as the behavior dictated by the Sine-
\beta
and Airy
\beta
process in the fixed
\beta>0
regime when
\beta\rightarrow 0

[
AD14a
]
. Instead, at high temperature we recover the microscopic behavior of independent identically distributed particles,
i.e.
at the microscale we do not see the Wigner-Gauss crossover mentioned above. At the edge, which is, up to corrections, the same as in the iid case
[
Lam21
]
, it was also shown that the fluctuations follow a Gumbel distribution as opposed to the Tracy-Widom distribution in the fixed
\beta
-case which is again in accordance with
[
AD14b
]
. At intermediary temperatures when
\beta\gg N^{-1}
, the large deviation principle was established in
[
Pak20
]
. Finally the fluctuations of the linear statistics were shown to be Gaussian in the case of quadratic potentials in
[
NT18
]
. Their proof relied on the tridiagonal representation and martingale methods. In the circular case with general potential, a quantitative CLT was shown in
[
HL21
]
relying on the inversion of the master operator and Stein’s method. It is the only result with the present article where an explicit expression for the variance is given. In the circular case, the authors were also able to show that the high temperature variance interpolates between the
H^{1/2}
-norm which is the variance in the
\beta
-fixed case and the
L^{2}
-norm as in the iid case.

Coulomb gases

The equilibrium measure characterized by (
5
) is often called the
thermal equilibrium measure
in the context of Coulomb gases due to the study of
[
AS22
]
and later in
[
PG23b
,
GZPG24
]
in the case of more general interactions. Its investigation is motivated by the fact that this measure captures more of the behavior of the system when
N
is large and that this description is better as the temperature grows. The description obtained for this measure were then shown to be useful in this literature
[
AS21
,
Ser23
,
PG23a
,
PGPT24
]
.

Link with integrable systems

Recently, Spohn uncovered in
[
Spo20
]
a link between the study of the
classical Toda chain
– a
N
particles Hamiltonian
integrable
system, in the sense that it has
N
independent conserved quantities (in opposition to generic Hamiltonian systems, that only present a few conservation laws) – and the
\beta
-ensemble in the high temperature regime, showing that the limiting density of states of the
Lax matrix
of the classical Toda chain, distributed according to a
generalized Gibbs ensemble
with polynomial potential, can be computed by means of the limiting empirical measure of the
\beta
-ensemble at high temperature. In
[
Maz22
]
, the author established this relation using the matrix representation of the
\beta
-ensemble and a moment method, and in
[
GM22
]
the authors proved a large deviation principle for the empirical measure of the Toda chain, establishing the previous result for potentials with polynomial growth. See also
[
Spo22
,
GM23
,
MM23b
]
for a similar link between the Ablowitz-Ladik lattice and the circular
\beta
-ensemble at high temperature.

The relation between the statistical properties of the Toda chain and the high temperature
\beta
ensemble can be further pushed to compute the limiting averages of the
currents
of the Toda chain through the central limit theorem for the empirical measure in the
\beta
ensemble. The computation of these currents is a crucial step in the derivation of a hydrodynamic equation for the Toda chain, and to the analysis of the correlations of the locally conserved quantities at equilibrium through
linearized hydrodynamics
. The derivation of the currents via the central limit theorem is justified in the recent paper
[
MM23a
]
, where the authors show that on the one hand, the Lax matrix of the Toda chain, distributed with respect to the generalized Gibbs ensemble with pressure parameter
P
and polynomial potential
V
, and on the other hand the
\beta
-ensemble at inverse temperature
2P/N
, both satisfy a central limit theorem of the form

\sqrt{N}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(x)d\hat{\mu}_{N}(x)-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(x)d\mu_{\infty}(x)\right)\overset{(d)}{\underset{N\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2}(\phi,V,P))\,.

This result holds for polynomial test functions
\phi
, and
\hat{\mu}_{N}
denotes, depending on the model, the empirical measure of eigenvalues of the Toda Lax matrix or the empirical measure of the
\beta
ensemble at high temperature, and
\mu_{\infty}
denotes the limiting measure (depending on
V,P
) of the corresponding model. The authors prove that the limiting variances are linked through the formula

\sigma^{2}_{\text{Toda}}(\phi,V,P)=\partial_{P}\bigg(P\sigma^{2}_{\text{HT}}(\phi,V,P)\bigg)\,,

giving access to the limiting variance
\sigma^{2}_{\text{Toda}}(\phi,V,P)
, directly linked with the average of the currents of the Toda chain. Indeed,
[
MM23a
, Lemma 2.5, Remark 2.7]
show that for integer
n
, the
n
-th limiting average current
J^{[n]}
can be expressed
[
Spo21
, (6.10)]
, denoting
h_{n}(x)=x^{n}
, by the limiting covariance in the
\beta
-ensembles at inverse temperature
\beta=2P/N
and polynomial potential
V

J^{[n]}=P\lim_{N\rightarrow+\infty}\mathrm{Cov}_{V}^{P}\bigg(\sqrt{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}}h_{1}(x)d\hat{\mu}_{N}(x),\sqrt{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}}h_{n}(x)d\hat{\mu}_{N}(x)\bigg)

which can then be recast as

J^{[n]}=\frac{P}{2}\left(\sigma^{2}_{\text{HT}}(h_{1}+h_{n},V,P)-\sigma^{2}_{\text{HT}}(h_{1},V,P)-\sigma^{2}_{\text{HT}}(h_{n},V,P)\right)\,.

(6)

However, an explicit form for
\sigma^{2}_{\text{HT}}(\phi,V,P)
is still lacking and the present paper goes in this direction, by computing it for a family of bounded test functions
\phi
. The interested reader can refer to
[
Spo21
]
, where the author explains the strategy to derive the hydrodynamic equations for the Toda lattice.

CLT’s for
\beta
-ensembles with fixed
\beta

The Central Limit Theorem for the fluctuations of the linear statistics of
\beta
-ensembles was first established by
[
Joh98
]
for polynomial potentials, then generalized and further developed in
[
BG13b
]
in the one-cut case by obtaining the asymptotic expansion of the partition function via the analysis of the so-called loop equations. The same approach was used to obtain the fluctuations in
[
Shc13
,
BG13a
]
for the multi-cut case. Some assumptions have to be made to ensure that the fluctuations are indeed Gaussian due to the additional order 1 effects of the tunneling of the particles between the different cuts. In
[
BLS18
]
, the authors proposed a new proof based on a transport method, with relaxed assumptions on
V
and which allow for criticality behavior in the bulk and at the edge. In
[
LLW19
]
, the authors manage, by Stein’s method arguments, to obtain quantitative bounds between the fluctuations and the normal distribution in Wassertein distance. This approach was also simplified with optimal rates of convergences in
[
AHMP24
]
. Finally, note that the fluctuations were obtained: for irregular test-functions,
i.e.
when the usual
H^{1/2}
-norm variance blows up in
[
BMP22
]
, at mesoscopic scales in
[
BL18
,
Pei24
]
and by determinantal structure based methods in the case
\beta=2

[
Lam15
,
BD17
]
.

In this paper, we adapt part of the arguments of
[
HL21
]
to our setup. More precisely, we show that for a class of regular potentials
V
satisfying a growth condition of the type

\lim_{|x|\to+\infty}V(x)=\lim_{|x|\to+\infty}|V^{\prime}(x)|=+\infty,\quad\lim_{|x|\to\infty}\frac{V^{\prime\prime}(x)}{V^{\prime}(x)^{2}}=0,

denoting
\mathrm{fluct}_{N}=\hat{\mu}_{N}-\mu^{P}_{V}
and considering test functions
f
belonging to the range of a certain integro-differential operator, the rescaled fluctuations of
\hat{\mu}_{N}
, defined by

\sqrt{N}\mathrm{fluct}_{N}(f)\overset{(\mathrm{def})}{=}\sqrt{N}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)d\hat{\mu}_{N}(x)-\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)d\mu^{P}_{V}(x)\right)\,,

(7)

converge in law towards a centered Gaussian law with variance depending on
f
.

It is shown in
[
AGZ10
, Theorem 2.6.1]
that the empirical measure satisfies a large deviation principle, and the limiting measure is characterized in
[
AGZ10
, Lemma 2.6.2]
by an equation similar to (
5
). In fact, the entropic term
\ln\rho^{P}_{V}
in the left-hand side of (
5
) is the only difference in the equation characterizing the limiting measure in the fixed
\beta
case, due to the subdominant role of the entropy. We point out the very similar characterization of the equilibrium measure corresponding to the minimization problem arising in
[
BGK16
]
. There again, the limiting measure is compactly supported. The entropic term is of prime importance because its presence implies that the support of
\rho^{P}_{V}
is the whole real line. This fact implies several technicalities such as dealing with weighted Sobolev spaces of the form

H^{k}(\mu^{P}_{V})\overset{(\mathrm{def})}{=}\Big\{f\in L^{2}(\mu^{P}_{V}),\,f^{(i)}\in L^{2}(\mu^{P}_{V}),\,\forall i\in\llbracket 1,k\rrbracket\Big\}\,.

1.3
Main result

We now present the assumptions imposed on the potential function
V
. First, recall that a probability measure
\mu
supported on
{\mathbb{R}}
satisfies the Poincaré inequality if there exists
C>0
such that for all
f\in\mathcal{C}^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
with compact support:

\mathrm{Var}_{\mu}(f)\overset{(\mathrm{def})}{=}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(f(x)-\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(y)d\mu(y)\right)^{2}d\mu(x)\leqslant C\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f^{\prime}(x)|^{2}d\mu(x)\,.

(8)

To state the main results, we will also need the definition of the so-called
master operator

\Xi
which acts on sufficiently smooth functions
\phi
as:

\Xi[\phi](x)\overset{(\mathrm{def})}{=}2P\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\phi(x)-\phi(y)}{x-y}d\mu^{P}_{V}(y)+\phi^{\prime}(x)-V^{\prime}(x)\phi(x)-2P\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{H}[\phi\rho^{P}_{V}](y)d\mu^{P}_{V}(y)\,.

(9)

Showing that this operator is invertible with regular inverse will be of prime importance in the following. To achieve this task, we will need the following Hilbert-space
\mathsf{H}
:

\mathsf{H}=\left\{u\in L^{2}(\mu^{P}_{V})\ \Big|\ u^{\prime}\in L^{2}(\mu^{P}_{V}),\int_{\mathbb{R}}u(x)d\mu^{P}_{V}(x)=0\right\},\qquad\Braket{u,v}_{\mathsf{H}}=\Braket{u^{\prime},v^{\prime}}_{L^{2}(\mu^{P}_{V})}\,.

(10)

Assumptions 1.1
.

The potential
V
satisfies:

(i)

V\in\mathcal{C}^{3}({\mathbb{R}})
,
V(x)\underset{|x|\rightarrow+\infty}{\longrightarrow}+\infty
,
|V^{\prime}(x)|\underset{|x|\rightarrow+\infty}{\longrightarrow}+\infty
and is such that
\mu^{P}_{V}
satisfies the Poincaré inequality (
8
).

(ii)

For all polynomial
Q\in{\mathbb{R}}[X]
and
\alpha>0
,
Q\bigg(V^{\prime}(x)\bigg)e^{-V(x)}=\underset{|x|\to\infty}{o}(x^{-\alpha})
.

(iii)

Furthermore, for any sequence
(x_{N})_{N}
such that
|x_{N}|
goes to infinity, and for all real
a<b
,

\frac{1}{V^{\prime}(x_{N})^{2}}\sup_{a\leqslant x\leqslant b}|V^{\prime\prime}(x_{N}+x)|\underset{N\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}0\,.

(iv)

The function
x\mapsto V^{\prime}(x)^{-2}
is integrable at infinity,
\dfrac{V^{\prime\prime}(x)}{V^{\prime}(x)}=\underset{|x|\rightarrow\infty}{O}(1)
and
\dfrac{V^{(3)}(x)}{V^{\prime}(x)}=\underset{|x|\rightarrow\infty}{O}(1)
.

Since we need another assumption to state our result, we postpone to Remark
1.3
explicit examples of potentials admissible for our main result. We now discuss the previous assumptions.

•

Because
(i)
implies that
V
goes to infinity faster than linearly, we will see that it ensures exponential decay at infinity of
\rho^{P}_{V}
. Recalling the sufficient condition for
\mathbb{P}^{V,P}_{N}
of equation (
2
) to be defined, this first assumption implies that

\liminf_{|x|\to\infty}\frac{V(x)}{|x|}=+\infty.

This guarantees in particular that the
\beta
-ensemble (
3
) is well-defined for all
N\geqslant 1
and
P\geqslant 0
. Finally, we will use the fact that
\mu^{P}_{V}
satisfies the Poincaré inequality to ensure that the space
\mathsf{H}
endowed with
\braket{\cdot,\cdot}_{\mathsf{H}}
is a Hilbert space.

•

The second assumption ensures that any power of
V^{\prime}
(and of
V^{\prime\prime}
by
(iv)
) is in
L^{2}(\mu^{P}_{V})
and that
\rho^{P}_{V}
, which behaves like
e^{-V}
up to a sub-exponential factor, belongs to the Sobolev space
H^{2}({\mathbb{R}})\subset\mathcal{C}^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
. Indeed, for
k\leqslant 2
, using
(iv)
,
\rho^{P}_{V}\,{}^{(k)}
behaves at infinity like

as shown in Lemma
2.2
which is in

by assumption

•

Assumption
(iii)
will be used to localize the minimum/maximum point of a typical configuration
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})
following the law
\mathbb{P}^{V,P}_{N}
: this will be done in Corollary

, which comes as a consequence of
[

, Theorem 3.4]
. More precisely, Corollary

establishes that for some specific sequences

and
(E_{N}^{-})_{N},(E_{N}^{+})_{N}
, all going to infinity, the random variables

\alpha_{N}^{+}\left(\max_{1\leqslant j\leqslant N}x_{j}-E_{N}^{+}\right)\qquad\text{and }\qquad\alpha_{N}^{-}\left(\max_{1\leqslant j\leqslant N}x_{j}-E_{N}^{-}\right)

converge in distribution. For large

, the scalars

and

can thus be seen as the edges of a typical configuration, and verify

V(E_{N}^{\pm})\underset{N\rightarrow\infty}{\sim}\ln N\,.

(11)

We refer to Section
4

from compactly supported functions to more general functions.

•

Finally, we use assumption
(iv)
to control integral remainders in the proof of Theorem

, ensuring that

is regular enough

that for sufficiently smooth functions

is also smooth.

We will need another technical assumption to ensure that Taylor remainders arising in the proof of Theorem

Assumption 1.2
.

With the notations of Theorem

, we have

\sup_{d(x,I_{N})\leqslant 1}\left|V^{(3)}(x)\right|=\underset{N\rightarrow\infty}{o}(\sqrt{N})\,,

where
I_{N}=\left[E_{N}^{-}-2;E_{N}^{+}+2\right]
.

Remark 1.3
.

Taking
V=V_{\mathrm{conv}}+\phi
with
V_{\mathrm{conv}},\,\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{3}({\mathbb{R}})
such that
\phi^{(k)}
is bounded for
k=0,\dots,3
,
V_{\mathrm{conv}}
is convex with
|V_{\mathrm{conv}}^{\prime}|\to+\infty
at infinity, satisfying hypotheses
(ii)
,
(iii)
,
(iv)
and assumption
1.2
, such that there exists
\varepsilon>0

such that

is convex (see Lemma

), then

satisfies Assumptions
1.1
and

. The main point that needs to be checked is that the measure

satisfies the Poincaré inequality, this will be done in Proposition

is introduced as a function that behaves like

.
We are now able to state the main result,
i.e.

Theorem 1.4
.

Assume that
V
satisfies Assumptions
1.1
and Assumption
1.2
. Then for all
\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
with
\phi
,
\phi^{\prime}
and
\phi^{\prime\prime}
bounded, we have the convergence in law towards a normal distribution

\sqrt{N}\mathrm{fluct}_{N}(\phi)\overset{\mathrm{law}}{\underset{N\rightarrow+\infty}{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{N}\Big(0,(\sigma^{P}_{V})^{2}(\phi)\Big)\,,

(12)

where
\mathrm{fluct}_{N}(\phi)
is defined in (
7
) and where the limiting variance
(\sigma^{P}_{V})^{2}(\phi)
is given by

(\sigma^{P}_{V})^{2}(\phi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi^{\prime}(x)\Xi^{-1}\circ\mathcal{X}[\phi](x)d\mu_{V}^{P}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Bigg(\Xi^{-1}[\phi]^{\prime}(x)^{2}+V^{\prime\prime}(x)\Xi^{-1}[\phi](x)^{2}\Bigg)d\mu^{P}_{V}(x)\\
+P\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\Bigg(\frac{\Xi^{-1}[\phi](x)-\Xi^{-1}[\phi](y)}{x-y}\Bigg)^{2}d\mu^{P}_{V}(x)d\mu^{P}_{V}(y)\,.

(13)

where
\mathcal{X}[\phi]\overset{(\mathrm{def})}{=}\phi-\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(x)d\mu_{V}^{P}(x)
.

Remark 1.5
.

1.

In particular, let
\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{d}\in\mathcal{C}^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
, all satisfying the hypotheses of Theorem
1.4
. Considering for
\mathbf{t}=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{d})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
the function
\phi=\sum_{j=1}^{d}t_{j}\phi_{j}
,
\phi
also satisfies the hypotheses. Then we have

\mathbb{E}\left[e^{\mathrm{i}\sqrt{N}(t_{1}\mathrm{fluct}_{N}(\phi_{1})+\ldots+t_{d}\mathrm{fluct}_{N}(\phi_{d})}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[e^{i\sqrt{N}\mathrm{fluct}_{N}(\phi)}\right]\,,

and the last expectation converges towards the characteristic function of a centered Gaussian variable. Thus, we see that the vector
\sqrt{N}(\mathrm{fluct}_{N}(\phi_{1}),\ldots,\mathrm{fluct}_{N}(\phi_{d}))
converges towards a centered Gaussian vector whose covariance matrix is given by
\left(\braket{\phi_{i}^{\prime},\Xi^{-1}\circ\mathcal{X}[\phi_{j}]}_{L^{2}(\mu_{V}^{P})}\right)_{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant d}
.

2.

Note that the formula of the variance we obtain, namely

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi^{\prime}(x)\cdot\Xi^{-1}\circ\mathcal{X}[\phi](x)d\mu_{V}^{P}(x)\,,

has the same structure as the limiting variance in the
\beta
fixed case
[
BLS18
, Theorem 1]
. Indeed, if one uses instead the equilibrium measure
\mu_{\mathrm{fix}}
and the inverse of the master operator
\Xi_{\mathrm{fix}}
of this regime, where the latter is defined by

\Xi_{\mathrm{fix}}[\phi](x)\overset{(\mathrm{def})}{=}\beta\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\phi(x)-\phi(y)}{x-y}d\mu_{\mathrm{fix}}(y)-V^{\prime}(x)\phi(x)\,,

one recovers the variance in the fixed temperature regime.

3.

The CLT was also established for polynomial test-functions, at least in the case of polynomial potentials
[
NT18
,
MM23a
]
. Since
\sigma_{V}^{P}(h_{k})
makes sense for polynomial test-function
h_{k}(x)=x^{k}
, this defines a conjectured expression for the averages of the limiting currents described in (
6
).

A crucial step in our proof is to control the usual

1.4
Strategy

Our strategy is based on a change of variables in the partition function

(
4

[
Joh98
]

[

,
BFG15
,
BLS18

]
, and used in
[

]

Take

Expanding the different terms in this integral, one gets:

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{P}[V]=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\prod_{i<j}|y_{i}-y_{j}|^{\frac{2P}{N}}e^{-\sum_{i=1}^{N}V(y_{i})}e^{\frac{t}{\sqrt{N}}\left[\frac{2P}{N}\sum_{i<j}\frac{\phi(y_{i})-\phi(y_{j})}{y_{i}-y_{j}}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\phi^{\prime}(y_{i})-V^{\prime}(y_{i})\phi(y_{i})\right)\right]}e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{2}q_{N}(\phi)}d^{N}\mathbf{y}\,,

The error term involves the anisotropy term

Equation (

Following

In this section, we discuss the regularity properties of the equilibrium density

The Hilbert transform, whose definition we recall, plays a central role in the analysis of the equilibrium measure. It is first defined on the Schwartz class through
\forall\phi\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}}),\,\forall x\in{\mathbb{R}},\;

\mathcal{H}[\phi](x)\overset{(\mathrm{def})}{=}\fint_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{\phi(t)}{t-x}dt=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\int_{|t-x|>\varepsilon}\dfrac{\phi(t)}{t-x}dt=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\dfrac{\phi(x+t)-\phi(x-t)}{t}dt,

(19)

where
\displaystyle\fint
denotes the Cauchy principal value integral, and then extended to
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
thanks to property
ii)
of Lemma
2.1
:
\|f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}=\dfrac{1}{\pi}\|\mathcal{H}[f]\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}
. The last expression in (
19
) is a definition where the integral converges in the classical sense. We also recall the definition of the logarithmic potential
U^{f}
of a density of probability
f:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
, given for
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
by

U^{f}(x)\overset{(\mathrm{def})}{=}-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\ln|x-y|f(y)dy\,.

(20)

Because we assume

to be nonnegative,
U^{f}
takes values in
[-\infty,+\infty)
. If
f
integrates the function
\ln
,
i.e

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\ln|x|f(x)dx<+\infty
, then
U^{f}
takes real values. Additionally, one can check that the logarithmic potential and the Hilbert transform of

are linked through the distributional identity
\big(U^{f}\big)^{\prime}=\mathcal{H}[f]
.

We recall in the next lemma some properties of the Hilbert transform that we will use in the rest of the paper.

Lemma 2.1

(Properties of the Hilbert transform)
.

i)

Fourier transform: For all
\phi\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})

\mathcal{F}\Big[\mathcal{H}[\phi]\Big](\omega)=\mathrm{i}\pi\text{sgn}(\omega)\mathcal{F}[\phi](\omega)

ii)

As a consequence,

is an isometry of

, and
\mathcal{H}
satisfies on

\mathcal{H}^{2}=-\pi^{2}\mathsf{id}
.

iii)

Derivative: For any

\mathcal{H}[f]
is also
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
and
\mathcal{H}[f]^{\prime}=\mathcal{H}[f^{\prime}]
.

iv)

For all

, the Hilbert transform can be extended as a bounded operator
\mathcal{H}:L^{p}({\mathbb{R}})\to L^{p}({\mathbb{R}})
.

v)

Skew-self adjointness: For any

,
\Braket{\mathcal{H}[f],g}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}=-\langle f,\mathcal{H}[g]\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}
.

Proof.

We refer to
[
Kin09
]
for the proofs of these properties.

We next show that the Hilbert transform of

is continuous and decays at infinity.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
u\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}}u(t)dt\overset{(\mathrm{def})}{=}\lim_{A\to\infty}\int_{-A}^{A}u(t)dt
exists and
f:t\mapsto tu(t)\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
then

\mathcal{H}[u](x)\underset{|x|\rightarrow\infty}{\sim}\displaystyle\dfrac{-\int_{\mathbb{R}}u(t)dt}{x}.

Moreover if
\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}u(t)dt=0
,
\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(t)dt
exists and
g:t\mapsto t^{2}u(t)\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
, then

\mathcal{H}[u](x)\underset{|x|\rightarrow\infty}{\sim}\displaystyle\dfrac{-\int_{\mathbb{R}}tu(t)dt}{x^{2}}.

As a consequence, we obtain that
\mathcal{H}[\rho_{V}](x)\underset{|x|\rightarrow\infty}{\sim}-x^{-1}
and the logarithmic potential
U^{\rho_{V}}
is Lipschitz bounded, with bounded derivative
\mathcal{H}[\rho_{V}]
.

Proof.

Let
u\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
, such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}}u(t)dt
exists and
f:t\mapsto tu(t)\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
. Then

x\mathcal{H}[u](x)+\int_{\mathbb{R}}u(t)dt=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big[\dfrac{xu(x+t)-xu(x-t)}{2t}+\dfrac{u(x+t)}{2}+\dfrac{u(x-t)}{2}\Big]dt=\mathcal{H}[f](x).

Since
f\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
, so is
\mathcal{H}[f]
, proving that it goes to zero at infinity. Hence

\mathcal{H}[u](x)\underset{|x|\rightarrow\infty}{\sim}\displaystyle\dfrac{-\int_{\mathbb{R}}u(t)dt}{x}

Moreover if
\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}u(t)dt=0
,
\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(t)dt
exists and
g:t\mapsto t^{2}u(t)\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
, then by the same argument:

x^{2}\mathcal{H}[u](x)=x\mathcal{H}[f](x)=\mathcal{H}[g](x)-\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(t)dt

where
g(t)=t^{2}u(t)
. We deduce that
\mathcal{H}[u](x)\underset{|x|\rightarrow\infty}{\sim}\displaystyle\dfrac{-\int_{\mathbb{R}}tu(t)dt}{x^{2}}
since
\mathcal{H}[g]
goes to zero at infinity.
∎

(Asymptotic of the logarithmic potential)

U^{\rho_{V}}(x)=-\ln|x|+\underset{|x|\rightarrow\infty}{O}(x^{-1})

Since

Proposition 2.6
.

Assume that

such that for all

\var_{\mu_{V}}(f)\overset{(\mathrm{def})}{=}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bigg(f(x)-\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(y)d\mu_{V}(y)\bigg)^{2}d\mu_{V}(x)\leqslant C\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f^{\prime}|^{2}(x)d\mu_{V}(x)\,.

We use the fact that if

for some other constant. Indeed, in that case let

\displaystyle\var_{\mu_{2}}(f)=\inf_{a}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(f(x)-a\right)^{2}d\mu_{2}(x)

\displaystyle\leqslant C\var_{\mu_{1}}(f)\leqslant C^{2}C_{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f^{\prime}(x)d\mu_{2}(x).

3.1
Concentration inequality

We prove in this section the concentration Proposition
1.6
. Its proof is a direct adaptation of Theorem 1.4 of
[
HL21
]
, which shows the analogous estimate in the circular setup. It is inspired by
[
MMS14
]
and based on a comparison between a configuration
\mathbf{x}_{N}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})
sampled with
\mathbb{P}_{N}^{V,P}
and a regularized version
\mathbf{y}_{N}=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{N})
, which we describe here.

Definition 3.1
.

y_{1}\overset{(\mathrm{def})}{=}x_{1}
, and for
0\leqslant k\leqslant N-1
,
y_{k+1}\overset{(\mathrm{def})}{=}y_{k}+\max\{x_{k+1}-x_{k},N^{-3}\}.

Note that the configuration
\mathbf{y}_{N}
given by the previous definition satisfies
y_{k+1}-y_{k}\geqslant N^{-3}
, and
\mathbf{y}_{N}
is close to
\mathbf{x}_{N}
in the sense that

\sum_{k=1}^{N}|x_{k}-y_{k}|\leqslant\dfrac{1}{2N}\,.

(28)

Indeed, by construction we have
|x_{k}-y_{k}|=y_{k}-x_{k}\leqslant(k-1)N^{-3}
, and we get the bound by summing these inequalities.

The key point of the proof of Proposition
1.6
is comparing the empirical measure
\hat{\mu}_{N}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{x_{i}},
where
\mathbf{x}_{N}
follows
\mathbb{P}_{N}^{V,P}
, to the regularized measure

\widetilde{\mu}_{N}\overset{(\mathrm{def})}{=}\lambda_{N^{-5}}\ast\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{y_{i}},

(29)

i.e.
the convolution of
\lambda_{N^{-5}}
and the empirical measure, where
\lambda_{N^{-5}}
is the uniform measure on
[0,N^{-5}]
. The interest of introducing the measure
\widetilde{\mu}_{N}
is that it is close to
\hat{\mu}_{N}
, while having a finite energy
\mathcal{E}_{V}^{P}(\widetilde{\mu}_{N})
, given by (
21
). Finally, notice that the empirical measure doesn’t change when reordering
x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}
, and thus we do not lose in generality for our purposes in assuming that
x_{1}\leqslant\ldots\leqslant x_{N}
in definition
3.1
.

We now introduce a distance on
\mathcal{P}({\mathbb{R}})
which is well-suited to our context.

Definition 3.2
.

For
\mu,\mu^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}({\mathbb{R}})
we define the distance (possibly infinite)
D(\mu,\mu^{\prime})
by

\displaystyle D(\mu,\mu^{\prime})

\displaystyle\overset{(\mathrm{def})}{=}\left(-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\ln|x-y|d(\mu-\mu^{\prime})(x)d(\mu-\mu^{\prime})(y)\right)^{1/2}

(30)

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{t}\big|\mathcal{F}[\mu-\mu^{\prime}](t)\big|^{2}dt\right)^{1/2}.

(31)

where the Fourier transform of a signed measure
\nu
is defined by
\mathcal{F}[\nu](x)\overset{(\mathrm{def})}{=}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\mathrm{i}tx}d(\mu-\mu^{\prime})(x)

Let
f:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
with finite
1/2
norm
\|f\|_{1/2}\overset{(\mathrm{def})}{=}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|t|\left|\mathcal{F}[f](t)\right|^{2}dt\right)^{1/2}
. By Plancherel theorem and Hölder inequality, for any
\mu,\mu^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}({\mathbb{R}})
, setting
\nu=\mu-\mu^{\prime}
,

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)d\mu(x)-\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)d\mu^{\prime}(x)\right|^{2}=\left|\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|t|^{1/2}\mathcal{F}[f](t)\frac{\overline{\mathcal{F}[\nu](t)}}{|t|^{1/2}}dt\right|^{2}\leqslant\dfrac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\|f\|_{1/2}^{2}D^{2}(\mu,\mu^{\prime}).

Therefore the metric
d
defined in (
15
) is dominated by
D
:

d(\mu,\mu^{\prime})\leqslant\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}\pi}D(\mu,\mu^{\prime}).

(32)

The following lemma shows how the distance
D
is related to the energy-functional
\mathcal{E}_{V}^{P}
defined in (
21
), we will write
\mathcal{E}_{V}
for simplicity.

Lemma 3.3
.

We have for any absolutely continuous
\mu\in\mathcal{P}({\mathbb{R}})
with finite energy
\mathcal{E}_{V}(\mu)
,

\mathcal{E}_{V}(\mu)-\mathcal{E}_{V}(\mu_{V})=PD^{2}(\mu,\mu_{V})+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\ln\frac{d\mu}{d\mu_{V}}(x)d\mu(x)\,.

(33)

Proof of Lemma
3.3
.

Subtracting
\mathcal{E}_{V}(\mu)-\mathcal{E}_{V}(\mu_{V})
we find

\mathcal{E}_{V}(\mu)-\mathcal{E}_{V}(\mu_{V})=\int_{\mathbb{R}}V(x)d(\mu-\mu_{V})(x)+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\ln\frac{d\mu}{dx}(x)d\mu(x)-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\ln\rho_{V}(x)d\mu_{V}(x)\\
-P\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\ln|x-y|d\mu(x)d\mu(y)+P\iint\ln|x-y|d\mu_{V}(x)d\mu_{V}(y)\,.

(34)

Now, if
\nu
is a signed measure of mass zero, integrating (
22
) we get

\int_{\mathbb{R}}V(x)d\nu(x)-2P\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\ln|x-y|d\nu(x)d\mu_{V}(y)+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\ln\rho_{V}(x)d\nu(x)=0\,.

We take
\nu=\mu-\mu_{V}
, and get

\int_{\mathbb{R}}V(x)d(\mu-\mu_{V})(x)=2P\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\ln|x-y|d\mu(x)d\mu_{V}(y)-2P\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\ln|x-y|d\mu_{V}(x)d\mu_{V}(y)\\
-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\ln\rho_{V}(x)d\mu(x)+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\ln\rho_{V}(x)d\mu_{V}(x)\,.

Plugging this last identity in (
34
), we find

\mathcal{E}_{V}(\mu)-\mathcal{E}_{V}(\mu_{V})=-P\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\ln|x-y|d\nu(x)d\nu(y)+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\ln\frac{d\mu}{d\mu_{V}}(x)d\mu(x)

which establishes the result.
∎

Proof of Proposition
1.6
.

We first give a lower bound for the partition function
\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{P}[V]
(
4
) of
\mathbb{P}_{N}^{V,P}
. We rewrite it as

\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{P}[V]=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\exp\Bigg(\dfrac{2P}{N}\sum_{i<j}\ln|x_{i}-x_{j}|-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Big[V(x_{i})+\ln\rho_{V}(x_{i})\Big]\Bigg)d\mu_{V}(x_{1})\dots d\mu_{V}(x_{N})\,,

and apply Jensen inequality to obtain:

\displaystyle\ln\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{P}[V]

\displaystyle\geqslant\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\Bigg(\dfrac{2P}{N}\sum_{i<j}\ln|x_{i}-x_{j}|-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Big[V(x_{i})+\ln\rho_{V}(x_{i})\Big]\Bigg)d\mu_{V}(x_{1})\dots d\mu_{V}(x_{N})

\displaystyle\geqslant P(N-1)\displaystyle\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\ln|x-y|d\mu_{V}(x)d\mu_{V}(y)-N\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big[V+\ln\rho_{V}\Big](x)d\mu_{V}(x)

\displaystyle\geqslant-N\mathcal{E}_{V}^{P}\big[\mu_{V}\big]-P\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\ln|x-y|d\mu_{V}(x)d\mu_{V}(y).

Using this estimate and the fact that for
1\leqslant i,j\leqslant N
we have
|x_{i}-x_{j}|\leqslant|y_{i}-y_{j}|
, with
\mathbf{y}_{N}=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{N})
of definition
3.1
, we deduce the bound on the density of probability

\frac{d\mathbb{P}^{V,P}_{N}}{d\mathbf{x}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})\leqslant e^{N\mathcal{E}_{V}(\mu_{V})+P\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\ln|x-y|d\mu_{V}(x)d\mu_{V}(y)+\frac{P}{N}\sum_{i\neq j}\ln|y_{i}-y_{j}|-\sum_{i=1}^{N}V(x_{i})}\,.

(35)

Recalling (
29
), we now show the following estimate

\sum_{i\neq j}\ln|y_{i}-y_{j}|\leqslant 2+N^{2}\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\ln|x-y|d\widetilde{\mu}_{N}(x)d\widetilde{\mu}_{N}(y)+5N\ln N+\frac{3}{2}N\,.

(36)

Let
i\neq j
and
u,v\in[0,N^{-5}]
. Since for
x\neq 0
and
|h|\leqslant\frac{|x|}{2}
, we have
\big|\ln|x+h|-\ln|x|\big|\leqslant\frac{2|h|}{|x|}
, we deduce

\big|\ln|y_{i}-y_{j}+u-v|-\ln|y_{i}-y_{j}|\big|\leqslant\frac{2|u-v|}{|y_{i}-y_{j}|}\leqslant\frac{2N^{-5}}{N^{-3}}=\dfrac{2}{N^{2}}.

Thus, summing over
i\neq j
and integrating with respect to
u
and
v
, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{i\neq j}\ln|y_{i}-y_{j}|

\displaystyle\leqslant 2+\sum_{i\neq j}\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\ln|y_{i}-y_{j}+u-v|d\lambda_{N^{-5}}(u)d\lambda_{N^{-5}}(v)

\displaystyle=2+N^{2}\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\ln|x-y|d\widetilde{\mu}_{N}(x)d\widetilde{\mu}_{N}(y)-N\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\ln|u-v|d\lambda_{N^{-5}}(u)d\lambda_{N^{-5}}(v)\,.

The last integral is equal to
-\frac{3}{2}-5\ln N
, so we deduce (
36
). We now combine (
35
) and (
36
). Recall (
21
) and set

c_{N}=P\left(\displaystyle\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\ln|x-y|d\mu_{V}(x)d\mu_{V}(y)+3/2+2/N\right)\,.

Then we get

\displaystyle\frac{d\mathbb{P}^{V,P}_{N}}{d\mathbf{x}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})

\displaystyle\leqslant e^{c_{N}+5P\ln N}\exp{\left[N\left\{\mathcal{E}_{V}(\mu_{V})-\mathcal{E}_{V}(\widetilde{\mu}_{N})+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(V+\ln\frac{d\widetilde{\mu}_{N}}{dx}\right)(x)d\widetilde{\mu}_{N}(x)\right\}-\sum_{i=1}^{N}V(x_{i})\right]}

\displaystyle=e^{c_{N}+5P\ln N}\exp{\left[-NPD^{2}(\widetilde{\mu}_{N},\mu_{V})+N\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(V+\ln\rho_{V}\right)(x)d\widetilde{\mu}_{N}(x)-\sum_{i=1}^{N}V(x_{i})\right]}\,

where we used equation (
33
) in the last equality. Using again equation (
22
) we then see that the density
\dfrac{d\mathbb{P}^{V,P}_{N}}{d\mathbf{x}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})
is bounded by

e^{c_{N}+5P\ln N}\exp{\left[-NPD^{2}(\widetilde{\mu}_{N},\mu_{V})+2PN\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\ln|x-y|d(\widetilde{\mu}_{N}-\hat{\mu}_{N})(x)d\mu_{V}(y)\right]}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\rho_{V}(x_{i})\,.

Recalling (
20
), we used that
\displaystyle\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\ln|x-y|d(\widetilde{\mu}_{N}-\hat{\mu}_{N})(x)d\mu_{V}(y)=-\int U^{\rho_{V}}(x)d(\widetilde{\mu}_{N}-\hat{\mu}_{N})(x)
.
As a consequence of the bound on the density
\dfrac{d\mathbb{P}^{V,P}_{N}}{d\mathbf{x}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})
we established, we have for all
r>0

\mathbb{P}^{V,P}_{N}\left(D^{2}(\widetilde{\mu}_{N},\mu_{V})>r\right)\leqslant e^{-NPr+c_{N}+5P\ln N}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\exp\left\{-2PN\int_{\mathbb{R}}U^{\rho_{V}}(x)d(\widetilde{\mu}_{N}-\hat{\mu}_{N})(x)\right\}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\rho_{V}(x_{i})dx_{i}\,.

(37)

Next, we show that
-N\int_{\mathbb{R}}U^{\rho_{V}}d(\widetilde{\mu}_{N}-\hat{\mu}_{N})
is bounded. By Lemma
2.3
,
U^{\rho_{V}}
is differentiable with bounded derivative
\mathcal{H}[\rho_{V}]
on
{\mathbb{R}}
. As a consequence,

\displaystyle\left|N\int_{\mathbb{R}}U^{\rho_{V}}d(\widetilde{\mu}_{N}-\hat{\mu}_{N})\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|U^{\rho_{V}}(y_{i}+u)-U^{\rho_{V}}(x_{i})\right|d\lambda_{N^{-5}}(u)

\displaystyle\leqslant\|\mathcal{H}[\rho_{V}]\|_{\infty}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}|y_{i}-x_{i}|+N\int_{\mathbb{R}}ud\lambda_{N^{-5}}(u)\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\|\mathcal{H}[\rho_{V}]\|_{\infty}\Big(\dfrac{1}{2N}+N^{-4}/2\Big),

where we used (
28
) in the last inequality. Therefore, we deduce from (
37
)

\mathbb{P}^{V,P}_{N}\left(D^{2}(\widetilde{\mu}_{N},\mu_{V})>r\right)\leqslant e^{-NPr+c_{N}+5P\ln N+\frac{2P}{N}\|\mathcal{H}[\rho_{V}]\|_{\infty}}=e^{-NPr+5P\ln N+K_{N}}

(38)

with
K_{N}\overset{(\mathrm{def})}{=}c_{N}+\dfrac{2P}{N}\|\mathcal{H}\left[\rho_{V}\right]\|_{\infty}
. Since
(c_{N})_{N}
is bounded, so is
(K_{N})_{N}
.

Finally, let
f
be a Lipschitz bounded function with
\|f\|_{\text{Lip}}\leqslant 1
, then, we have (as we did for
U^{\rho_{V}}
)

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)d\hat{\mu}_{N}(x)-\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)d\widetilde{\mu}_{N}(x)\right|\leqslant N^{-2}\,.

Thus by (
32
)

d(\hat{\mu}_{N},\mu_{V})\leqslant d(\hat{\mu}_{N},\widetilde{\mu}_{N})+d(\widetilde{\mu}_{N},\mu_{V})\leqslant N^{-2}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}\pi}D(\widetilde{\mu}_{N},\mu_{V})\,,

and for any
N
such that
r-N^{-2}\geqslant r/2
(in particular
r-N^{-2}>0
) we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{V,P}_{N}\left(d(\hat{\mu}_{N},\mu_{V})>r\right)\leqslant\mathbb{P}^{V,P}_{N}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}D^{2}(\widetilde{\mu}_{N},\mu_{V})>(r-N^{-2})^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{P}^{V,P}_{N}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}D^{2}(\widetilde{\mu}_{N},\mu_{V})>r^{2}/4\right)\,,

and the last term is bounded by
e^{-Nr^{2}\frac{P\pi^{2}}{2}+5P\ln N+K}
for some
K
large enough, which concludes the proof.
∎

3.2
Bound on the anistropy term

As a consequence of Proposition
1.6
, we are able to control the anisotropy term, whose definition we recall here for convenience,

\zeta_{N}(\phi)=\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\frac{\phi(x)-\phi(y)}{x-y}d(\hat{\mu}_{N}-\mu_{V})(x)d(\hat{\mu}_{N}-\mu_{V})(y)

(39)

for a certain class of test functions
\phi
.

Corollary 3.4
.

There exists
C,K>0
such that for all
\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{2}({\mathbb{R}})\cap H^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
with bounded second derivative, we have for
\varepsilon>0
and
N
large enough,

\mathbb{P}_{N}^{V,P}\left(\sqrt{N}|\zeta_{N}(\phi)|\leqslant N^{-1/2+\varepsilon}\right)\geqslant 1-\exp\left\{-\frac{PN^{\varepsilon}}{2C\|\psi\|_{H^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}}+5P\ln N+K\right\}\,

with
N_{2}(\phi)=\|\phi^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}+\|\phi^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}
.

Proof.

We follow the proof given in
[
Gui19
, Cor. 4.16]
and adapt it to our setting. Let us denote by
\widetilde{\zeta_{N}}(\phi)
the quantity

\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\frac{\phi(x)-\phi(y)}{x-y}d(\widetilde{\mu}_{N}-\mu_{V})(x)d(\widetilde{\mu}_{N}-\mu_{V})(y)\,.

We have the almost sure inequality, by a Taylor estimate

|\zeta_{N}(\phi)-\widetilde{\zeta_{N}}(\phi)|\leqslant 2N^{-2}\|\phi^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}\,.

(40)

Thus, for any
\delta>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{N}^{V,P}\left(|\zeta_{N}(\phi)|>\delta\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{P}_{N}^{V,P}\left(|\zeta_{N}(\phi)-\widetilde{\zeta_{N}}(\phi)|>\delta/2\right)+\mathbb{P}_{N}^{V,P}\left(|\widetilde{\zeta_{N}}(\phi)|>\delta/2\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{P}_{N}^{V,P}\left(2N^{-2}\|\phi^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}>\delta/2\right)+\mathbb{P}_{N}^{V,P}\left(|\widetilde{\zeta_{N}}(\phi)|>\delta/2\right)\,,

where the first term of the right-hand side is either
0
or
1
. With
\delta=N^{-1+\varepsilon}
,
\varepsilon>0
, it is zero for
N
large enough. For such a choice of
\delta
, and for
N
large enough,

\mathbb{P}_{N}^{V,P}\left(|\zeta_{N}(\phi)|>N^{-1+\varepsilon}\right)\leqslant\mathbb{P}_{N}^{V,P}\left(|\widetilde{\zeta_{N}}(\phi)|>\frac{1}{2}N^{-1+\varepsilon}\right)\,.

We next show that, for some
C>0
independent of
\phi
, we have

|\widetilde{\zeta_{N}}(\phi)|\leqslant CD^{2}(\widetilde{\mu}_{N},\mu_{V})\|\phi\|_{H^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\,.

(41)

We begin by showing this inequality for
\psi\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}})
.
By using the inverse Fourier transform we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{\zeta}_{N}(\psi)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\dfrac{\int_{\mathbb{R}}dt\mathcal{F}[\psi](t)e^{\mathrm{i}tx}-\int_{\mathbb{R}}dt\mathcal{F}[\psi](t)e^{\mathrm{i}ty}}{x-y}d\big(\widetilde{\mu}_{N}-\mu_{V}\big)(x)d\big(\widetilde{\mu}_{N}-\mu_{V}\big)(y)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}dt\mathrm{i}t\mathcal{F}[\psi](t)\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{\mathrm{i}ty}\dfrac{e^{\mathrm{i}t(x-y)}-1}{\mathrm{i}t(x-y)}d\big(\widetilde{\mu}_{N}-\mu_{V}\big)(x)d\big(\widetilde{\mu}_{N}-\mu_{V}\big)(y)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}dt\mathrm{i}t\mathcal{F}[\psi](t)\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}e^{\mathrm{i}ty}\int_{0}^{1}d\alpha e^{\mathrm{i}\alpha t(x-y)}d\big(\widetilde{\mu}_{N}-\mu_{V}\big)(x)d\big(\widetilde{\mu}_{N}-\mu_{V}\big)(y)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}dt\mathrm{i}t\mathcal{F}[\psi](t)\int_{0}^{1}d\alpha\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{\mathrm{i}\alpha tx}d\big(\widetilde{\mu}_{N}-\mu_{V}\big)(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{\mathrm{i}(1-\alpha)ty}d\big(\widetilde{\mu}_{N}-\mu_{V}\big)(y)

We then apply in order the triangular inequality, Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, a change of variable and the fact that
\left|\mathcal{F}\left[\widetilde{\mu}_{N}-\mu_{V}\right]\right|^{2}
is an even function.

\displaystyle|\widetilde{\zeta}_{N}(\psi)|

\displaystyle\leqslant\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}dt\left|t\mathcal{F}[\psi](t)\right|\int_{0}^{1}d\alpha\left|\mathcal{F}\left[\widetilde{\mu}_{N}-\mu_{V}\right](\alpha t)\right|.\left|\mathcal{F}\left[\widetilde{\mu}_{N}-\mu_{V}\right]\big((1-\alpha)t\big)\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}dt\left|t\mathcal{F}[\psi](t)\right|\Big(\int_{0}^{1}d\alpha\left|\mathcal{F}\left[\widetilde{\mu}_{N}-\mu_{V}\right](\alpha t)\right|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\int_{0}^{1}d\alpha\left|\mathcal{F}\left[\widetilde{\mu}_{N}-\mu_{V}\right]\big((1-\alpha)t\big)\right|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}dt\left|t\mathcal{F}[\psi](t)\right|\int_{0}^{1}d\alpha\left|\mathcal{F}\left[\widetilde{\mu}_{N}-\mu_{V}\right](\alpha t)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{+\infty}dt\left|t\mathcal{F}[\psi](t)\right|\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{td\alpha}{t\alpha}\left|\mathcal{F}\left[\widetilde{\mu}_{N}-\mu_{V}\right](\alpha t)\right|^{2}+\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{0}dt\left|t\mathcal{F}[\phi](t)\right|\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{-td\alpha}{-t\alpha}\left|\mathcal{F}\left[\widetilde{\mu}_{N}-\mu_{V}\right](\alpha t)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}dt\left|t\mathcal{F}[\psi](t)\right|D^{2}(\widetilde{\mu}_{N},\mu_{V})

\displaystyle\leqslant\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}dt\left|t\mathcal{F}[\psi](t)\right|^{2}(1+t^{2})\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{dt}{1+t^{2}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}D^{2}(\widetilde{\mu}_{N},\mu_{V})

\displaystyle\leqslant\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}}D^{2}(\widetilde{\mu}_{N},\mu_{V})N_{2}(\psi)

\displaystyle\leqslant\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}}D^{2}(\widetilde{\mu}_{N},\mu_{V})\|\psi\|_{H^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}

By density of
\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}})
in
H^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
, and since
\widetilde{\zeta}_{N}:\Big(H^{2}({\mathbb{R}}),\|\cdot\|_{H^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\Big)\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
is continuous, the inequality still holds for
\phi
. Thus, using equation (
38
),

\mathbb{P}_{N}^{V,P}\left(|\widetilde{\zeta_{N}}(\phi)|>\frac{1}{2}N^{-1+\varepsilon}\right)\leqslant\mathbb{P}_{N}^{V,P}\left(D^{2}(\widetilde{\mu}_{N},\mu_{V})>\frac{N^{-1+\varepsilon}}{2C\|\phi\|_{H^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}}\right)\leqslant\exp\left\{-P\frac{N^{\varepsilon}}{2C\|\phi\|_{H^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}}+5P\ln N+K\right\}\,,

which concludes the proof.
∎

4
Localization of the edge of a configuration

In
[
Lam21
, Theorem 1.8, Theorem 3.4]
, the author was able to control the edge (
i.e
the minimum and the maximum) of a typical configuration
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})
distributed according to
\mathbb{P}^{V,P}_{N}
, by showing that the random measure

\Xi_{N}\overset{(\mathrm{def})}{=}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\delta_{\varphi_{N}^{-1}(x_{j})}

converges in distribution towards a Poisson point process for a function
\varphi_{N}
which takes the form

\varphi_{N}(x)\overset{(\mathrm{def})}{=}E_{N}+\alpha_{N}^{-1}x\,.

Before being more precise on the construction of
(E_{N})_{N}
and
(\alpha_{N})_{N}
, we explain, following
[
Lam21
]
, how one can use this convergence to localize the edge of a typical configuration
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})
. Let us assume for a moment that
\Xi_{N}
converges towards a Poisson point process with intensity
\theta(x)=e^{-x}
, with
E_{N}\to+\infty
. In particular, the random variable

\Xi_{N}(t,+\infty)

converges in distribution towards a Poisson random variable with mean
\int_{t}^{+\infty}e^{-x}dx
. Combined with the equalities

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{V,P}_{N}\bigg(\Xi_{N}(t,+\infty)=0\bigg)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}^{V,P}_{N}\bigg(\forall\ 1\leqslant j\leqslant N,\ \varphi_{N}^{-1}(x_{j})=\alpha_{N}(x_{j}-E_{N})\leqslant t\bigg)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}^{V,P}_{N}\bigg(\alpha_{N}\left(\max_{1\leqslant j\leqslant N}x_{j}-E_{N}\right)\leqslant t\bigg)\,,

we deduce that for all
t\in{\mathbb{R}}

\mathbb{P}^{V,P}_{N}\left(\alpha_{N}\left(\max_{1\leqslant j\leqslant N}x_{j}-E_{N}\right)\leqslant t\right)\underset{N\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}\exp(-e^{-t})\,.

Therefore, the random variable

\alpha_{N}\left(\max_{1\leqslant j\leqslant N}x_{j}-E_{N}\right)

converges in distribution to the Gumbel law, showing that the maximum of a configuration is of order
E_{N}
. Furthermore, as will be clear from the construction of
\alpha_{N}
and
E_{N}
,
\alpha_{N}
is positive, and goes to infinity as
N
goes to infinity.

Replacing in the previous analysis
\theta(x)=e^{x}
and
E_{N}\to-\infty
, we would have deduced in the same fashion that

\alpha_{N}\left(\min_{1\leqslant j\leqslant N}x_{j}-E_{N}\right)

converges in law.

With the above notations, we can apply
[
Lam21
, Theorem 3.4]
to our context.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
v=\pm
. There exists
(E_{N}^{v})_{N},\,(\alpha_{N}^{v})_{N}
sequences of real numbers with
|E_{N}^{v}|\to+\infty
,
\alpha_{N}^{v}>0
for large enough
N
, satisfying

Proposition 5.1
.

For

\mathbb{E}^{V,P}_{N}\left[\exp\Big(t\sqrt{N}\mathrm{fluct}_{N}(\Xi[\phi])\Big)\right]\underset{N\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}\exp\left\{\frac{t^{2}}{2}q(\phi)\right\},

(43)

where
\Xi[\phi]
is given by equation (
9

q(\phi)
is given by

q(\phi)\overset{(\mathrm{def})}{=}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bigg(\phi^{\prime}(x)^{2}+V^{\prime\prime}(x)\phi(x)^{2}\bigg)d\mu_{V}(x)+P\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\Big(\frac{\phi(x)-\phi(y)}{x-y}\Big)^{2}d\mu_{V}(x)d\mu_{V}(y)\,.

(44)

Proof.

Let
\phi\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{1}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{R}})
, and let
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
. We perform in equation (
4
) the change of variables

x_{i}=y_{i}+\frac{t}{\sqrt{N}}\phi(y_{i})
,
1\leqslant i\leqslant N
, which is a diffeomorphism for
N
big enough. We thus have

\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{P}[V]=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant N}\left|y_{i}-y_{j}+\frac{t}{\sqrt{N}}\big(\phi(y_{i})-\phi(y_{j})\big)\right|^{2P/N}.e^{-\sum_{i=1}^{N}V\left(y_{i}+\frac{t}{\sqrt{N}}\phi(y_{i})\right)}\\
\times\prod_{i=1}^{N}\left(1+\frac{t}{\sqrt{N}}\phi^{\prime}(y_{i})\right)d^{N}\mathbf{y},

(45)

and we develop separately the different terms of this integral. The first term can be written as:

\prod_{i<j}\left|y_{i}-y_{j}\right|^{2P/N}\prod_{i<j}\left|1+\frac{t}{\sqrt{N}}\frac{\phi(y_{i})-\phi(y_{j})}{y_{i}-y_{j}}\right|^{2P/N},

The second product above, setting
\Delta\phi_{i,j}\overset{(\mathrm{def})}{=}\frac{\phi(y_{i})-\phi(y_{j})}{y_{i}-y_{j}}
and using Taylor-Lagrange theorem, equals

\exp\bigg(\frac{2P}{N}\sum_{i<j}\ln\left|1+\frac{t}{\sqrt{N}}\frac{\phi(y_{i})-\phi(y_{j})}{y_{i}-y_{j}}\right|\bigg)=\exp\bigg(\frac{2P}{N}\sum_{i<j}\left(\frac{t}{\sqrt{N}}\Delta\phi_{i,j}-\frac{t^{2}}{2N}(\Delta\phi_{i,j})^{2}+R_{N,1}(i,j)\right)\bigg),

where we noticed that
1+\frac{t}{\sqrt{N}}\Delta\phi_{i,j}\geqslant 1-\frac{t}{\sqrt{N}}\|\phi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}>0
if
N
is big enough, and where

|R_{N,1}(i,j)|\leqslant\frac{|t|^{3}}{3N^{3/2}}\|\phi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}^{3}.

Again by Taylor-Lagrange theorem, the second term in (
45
) equals

\exp\bigg(-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(V(y_{i})+\frac{t}{\sqrt{N}}V^{\prime}(y_{i})\phi(y_{i})+\frac{t^{2}}{2N}V^{\prime\prime}(y_{i})\phi(y_{i})^{2}+R_{N,2}(i)\right)\bigg)

where
R_{N,2}(i)=\frac{t^{3}}{6N^{3/2}}V^{(3)}\left(y_{i}+\frac{t\theta_{i}}{\sqrt{N}}\phi(y_{i})\right)\phi(y_{i})^{3}
for some
\theta_{i}\in[0,1]

large enough

|R_{N,2}(i)|\leqslant\frac{|t|^{3}}{6N^{3/2}}\|\phi\|_{\infty}^{3}\sup_{d(x,\text{supp }\phi)\leqslant 1}|V^{(3)}(x)|.

The last term reads

\prod_{i=1}^{N}\left(1+\dfrac{t}{\sqrt{N}}\phi^{\prime}(y_{i})\right)=\exp\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\frac{t}{\sqrt{N}}\phi^{\prime}(y_{i})-\frac{t^{2}}{2N}\phi^{\prime}(y_{i})^{2}+R_{N,3}(i)\right)\bigg),

with
|R_{N,3}(i)|\leqslant\frac{t^{3}}{3N^{3/2}}\|\phi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}^{3}
. Dividing both sides of equation (
45
) by
\mathcal{Z}_{N}^{P}[V]
we get

\mathbb{E}_{N}^{V,P}\bigg[\exp\left\{t\sqrt{N}\bigg(P\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\frac{\phi(x)-\phi(y)}{x-y}d\hat{\mu}_{N}(x)d\hat{\mu}_{N}(y)+\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\phi^{\prime}-V^{\prime}\phi)(x)d\hat{\mu}_{N}(x)\bigg)\right\}\times\exp\left\{K_{N}(t,\phi)\right\}\\

with

where

is independent of
N
. This bound implies that taking the limit

Using Fubini’s theorem (the function
(x,y)\mapsto\frac{\phi(x)-\phi(y)}{x-y}
being bounded continuous on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
), the first line in the expectation value can be rewritten as

(46)

(49)

where the supremum runs over

where we used the rough bound

By continuity of

which establishes, that

\mathbb{E}^{V,P}_{N}\left[e^{\mathrm{i}t\sqrt{N}\mathrm{fluct}_{N}(\Xi[\phi])}\right]

converges to the characteristic function of a Gaussian variable with appropriate variance. Denoting

\widetilde{\Xi}[\phi](x)\overset{(\mathrm{def})}{=}2P\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\phi(x)-\phi(y)}{x-y}d\mu_{V}(y)+\phi^{\prime}(x)-V^{\prime}(x)\phi(x)=\Xi[\phi](x)+2P\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{H}[\phi\rho_{V}](y)d\mu_{V}(y)\,,

we have

\mathrm{fluct}_{N}(\Xi[\phi])=\mathrm{fluct}_{N}(\widetilde{\Xi}[\phi])

and we aim at establishing that

\mathbb{E}^{V,P}_{N}\left[\exp\Big(\mathrm{i}t\sqrt{N}\mathrm{fluct}_{N}(\widetilde{\Xi}[\phi])\Big)\right]=\mathbb{E}^{V,P}_{N}\left[\exp\Big(\mathrm{i}t\sqrt{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widetilde{\Xi}[\phi](x)d\hat{\mu}_{N}(x)\Big)\right].\exp\Big(-\mathrm{i}t\sqrt{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widetilde{\Xi}[\phi](x)d\mu_{V}(x)\Big)

converges towards the appropriate characteristic function.

By Corollary
4.2
, the probability under
\mathbb{P}^{V,P}_{N}
of the event
\mathcal{E}_{N}=\bigg\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}\in[E_{N}^{-}-1,E_{N}^{+}+1]\bigg\}
converges to
1
. Along with the convergence (
51
), we deduce

e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{2}q(\phi)}=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}^{V,P}_{N}\left[\exp\Big(\mathrm{i}t\sqrt{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widetilde{\Xi}[\phi_{N}](x)d\hat{\mu}_{N}(x)\Big)\right].\exp\Big(-\mathrm{i}t\sqrt{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widetilde{\Xi}[\phi_{N}](x)d\mu_{V}(x)\Big)\\
=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}^{V,P}_{N}\left[\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{E}_{N}}\exp\Big(\mathrm{i}t\sqrt{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widetilde{\Xi}[\phi_{N}](x)d\hat{\mu}_{N}(x)\Big)\right].\exp\Big(-\mathrm{i}t\sqrt{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widetilde{\Xi}[\phi_{N}](x)d\mu_{V}(x)\Big)\,,

Where we used

\left|\mathbb{E}^{V,P}_{N}\left[\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{E}_{N}^{c}}\exp\Big(\mathrm{i}t\sqrt{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widetilde{\Xi}[\phi_{N}](x)d\hat{\mu}_{N}(x)\Big)\right].\exp\Big(-\mathrm{i}t\sqrt{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widetilde{\Xi}[\phi_{N}](x)d\mu_{V}(x)\Big)\right|\leqslant\mathbb{P}^{V,P}_{N}(\mathcal{E}_{N}^{c})\underset{N\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}0\,.

Using that
\phi_{N}=\phi
on
J_{N}={[E_{N}^{-}-1,E_{N}^{+}+1]}
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widetilde{\Xi}[\phi_{N}](x)d\mu_{V}(x)

\displaystyle=2P\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\frac{\phi_{N}(x)-\phi_{N}(y)}{x-y}d\mu_{V}(x)d\mu_{V}(y)+\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\phi_{N}^{\prime}-V^{\prime}\phi_{N})(x)d\mu_{V}(x)

\displaystyle=2P\iint_{J_{N}^{2}}\frac{\phi(x)-\phi(y)}{x-y}d\mu_{V}(x)d\mu_{V}(y)+2P\iint_{(J_{N}^{2})^{c}}\frac{\phi_{N}(x)-\phi_{N}(y)}{x-y}d\mu_{V}(x)d\mu_{V}(y)

\displaystyle+\int_{J_{N}}(\phi^{\prime}-V^{\prime}\phi)(x)d\mu_{V}(x)+\int_{J_{N}^{c}}(\phi\chi_{N}^{\prime}+\phi^{\prime}\chi_{N}-V^{\prime}\phi\chi_{N})(x)d\mu_{V}(x)\,.

By boundedness of
(\|\phi_{N}^{\prime}\|_{\infty})_{N}
, the second term is bounded by

C_{P}\iint_{(J_{N}^{2})^{c}}d\mu_{V}(x)d\mu_{V}(y)\leqslant 2C_{P}\mu_{V}(J_{N}^{c})=o(N^{-1/2})\,,

where we used the union bound and Lemma
4.4
. By the same estimate and the fact that
\chi_{N}
can be chosen so that
(\|\chi_{N}^{\prime}\|_{\infty})_{N}
is bounded, and because
\phi^{\prime}
,
V^{\prime}\phi
are bounded, the last integral term is also
o(N^{-1/2})
.
By the previous arguments, we also conclude that

2P\iint_{(J_{N}^{2})^{c}}\frac{\phi(x)-\phi(y)}{x-y}d\mu_{V}(x)d\mu_{V}(y)+\int_{J_{N}^{c}}(\phi^{\prime}-V^{\prime}\phi)(x)d\mu_{V}(x)=o(N^{-1/2})\,,

thus

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\widetilde{\Xi}[\phi_{N}](x)d\mu_{V}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widetilde{\Xi}[\phi](x)d\mu_{V}(x)+o(N^{-1/2})\,,

and so far we have

\exp\Big(-\frac{t^{2}}{2}q(\phi)\Big)=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}^{V,P}_{N}\left[\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{E}_{N}}\exp\Big(\mathrm{i}t\sqrt{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widetilde{\Xi}[\phi_{N}](x)d\hat{\mu}_{N}(x)\Big)\right].\exp\Big(-\mathrm{i}t\sqrt{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widetilde{\Xi}[\phi](x)d\mu_{V}(x)\Big)\,.

Finally, using that on
\mathcal{E}_{N}
the measure
\hat{\mu}_{N}
is supported on
J_{N}
(because each
x_{i}\in J_{N}
), using
\phi_{N}=\phi
on
J_{N}
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widetilde{\Xi}[\phi_{N}](x)d\hat{\mu}_{N}(x)

\displaystyle=2P\iint_{J_{N}^{2}}\frac{\phi(x)-\phi(y)}{x-y}d\mu_{V}(x)d\hat{\mu}_{N}(y)+2P\iint_{(J_{N}^{2})^{c}}\frac{\phi_{N}(x)-\phi_{N}(y)}{x-y}d\mu_{V}(x)d\hat{\mu}_{N}(y)

\displaystyle+\int_{J_{N}}(\phi^{\prime}-V^{\prime}\phi)(x)d\hat{\mu}_{N}(x)

\displaystyle=2P\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\frac{\phi(x)-\phi(y)}{x-y}d\mu_{V}(x)d\hat{\mu}_{N}(y)+\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\phi^{\prime}-V^{\prime}\phi)(x)d\hat{\mu}_{N}(x)+o(N^{-1/2})\,,

Where in the second line we used, using Lemma
4.4
again, that

\iint_{(J_{N}^{2})^{c}}\frac{\phi_{N}(x)-\phi_{N}(y)}{x-y}d\mu_{V}(x)d\hat{\mu}_{N}(y)=\iint_{J_{N}\times J_{N}^{c}}\frac{\phi_{N}(x)-\phi_{N}(y)}{x-y}d\mu_{V}(x)d\hat{\mu}_{N}(y)=o(N^{-1/2})\,,

and the same estimate holds for
\phi_{N}
replaced by
\phi
.
Therefore,

\exp\Big(-\frac{t^{2}}{2}q(\phi)\Big)=\\
\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}^{V,P}_{N}\left[\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{E}_{N}}\exp\Big(\mathrm{i}t\sqrt{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widetilde{\Xi}[\phi](x)d\hat{\mu}_{N}(x)\Big)\right].\exp\Big(-\mathrm{i}t\sqrt{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widetilde{\Xi}[\phi](x)d\mu_{V}(x)\Big)\,.

This establishes that

\lim_{N\rightarrow+\infty}\mathbb{E}^{V,P}_{N}\left[\exp\Big(\mathrm{i}t\sqrt{N}\mathrm{fluct}_{N}(\Xi[\phi])\Big)\right]=\exp\Big(-\frac{t^{2}}{2}q(\phi)\Big)\,,

which concludes the proof.
∎

6
Inversion of
\mathcal{L}

This section is dedicated to the definition of
\mathcal{L}
given by (
18
) and its domain and then we focus on its inversion. We rely heavily on results of Appendix
A
: the diagonalization of the operator
\mathcal{A}
by the use of the theory of Schrödinger operators.

We reintroduce the operators
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{W}
, acting on sufficiently smooth functions of
L^{2}(\mu_{V})
, by

\mathcal{A}[\phi]=-\dfrac{\Big(\phi^{\prime}\rho_{V}\Big)^{\prime}}{\rho_{V}}=-\left(\phi^{\prime\prime}+\frac{\rho_{V}^{\prime}}{\rho_{V}}\phi^{\prime}\right)\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{W}[\phi]=-\mathcal{H}\big[\phi^{\prime}\rho_{V}\big]+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{H}\big[\phi^{\prime}\rho_{V}\big](x)d\mu_{V}(x)\,.

(56)

One can show that the operator
\mathcal{A}
corresponds to the operator verifying:

\braket{\phi,\psi}_{\mathsf{H}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi^{\prime}(x)\psi^{\prime}(x)d\mu_{V}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(x)\mathcal{A}[\psi](x)d\mu_{V}(x)=\braket{\phi,\mathcal{A}[\psi]}_{L^{2}(\mu_{V})}

We first show the following decomposition of
\mathcal{L}
.

Lemma 6.1
.

For
\phi
twice differentiable we have the following pointwise identity

-\mathcal{L}[\phi]=\mathcal{A}[\phi]+2P\mathcal{W}[\phi]\,.

(57)

Proof.

We write for
x\in{\mathbb{R}}

2P\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\phi^{\prime}(x)-\phi^{\prime}(y)}{x-y}\rho_{V}(y)dy=-2P\phi^{\prime}(x)\mathcal{H}[\rho_{V}](x)+2P\mathcal{H}[\phi^{\prime}\rho_{V}](x)\,.

(58)

Then,

\mathcal{L}[\phi]=\phi^{\prime\prime}-V^{\prime}\phi^{\prime}-2P\phi^{\prime}\mathcal{H}[\rho_{V}]+2P\mathcal{H}\big[\phi^{\prime}\rho_{V}\big]-2P\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{H}[\phi^{\prime}\rho_{V}](y)d\mu_{V}(y)\,.

By (
23
) we have

-V^{\prime}-2P\mathcal{H}[\rho_{V}]=\frac{\rho_{V}^{\prime}}{\rho_{V}}
, which concludes the proof.
∎

In order to state the next theorem, whose proof we detail in the Appendix, we introduce the following Sobolev-type spaces.
Let

H^{1}_{V^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}})\overset{(\mathrm{def})}{=}\Big\{u\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}),\,uV^{\prime}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})\Big\}\,.

As explained in the Appendix,
\mathcal{A}
, defined in (
56
), is linked to a Schrödinger operator
\mathcal{S}\overset{(\mathrm{def})}{=}-\Delta+w_{V}
via
\mathcal{A}=\rho_{V}^{-1/2}\mathcal{S}\Big[.\rho_{V}^{1/2}\Big]
. Here
w_{V}
is defined in (
77
), continuous bounded by below and grows at infinity like
\dfrac{V^{\prime}\,{}^{2}}{4}
. This operator
\mathcal{S}
is defined on the following space:

\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{S})=\Big\{u\in H^{1}_{V^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}),\sqrt{\rho_{V}}\mathcal{A}\Big[\dfrac{u}{\sqrt{\rho_{V}}}\Big]\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})\Big\}.

Now, let

\mathcal{D}_{L^{2}(\mu_{V})}(\mathcal{A})\overset{(\mathrm{def})}{=}\rho_{V}^{-1/2}\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{S})

and its homogeneous counterpart

\mathcal{D}_{L^{2}_{0}(\mu_{V})}(\mathcal{A})\overset{(\mathrm{def})}{=}\Big\{u\in\mathcal{D}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}(\mathcal{A}),\,\int_{\mathbb{R}}u(x)d\mu_{V}(x)=0\Big\}\,.

(59)

Finally, we let
L_{0}^{2}(\mu_{V})
be the subset of
L^{2}(\mu_{V})
of zero mean functions with respect to
\rho_{V}
.

We detail the proof of the following theorem in Appendix
A
which is based on Schrödinger operators theory.

Theorem 6.2

(Diagonalization of
\mathcal{A}
in
L_{0}^{2}(\mu_{V})
)
.

There exists a sequence
0<\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}<\ldots
going to infinity, and a complete orthonormal set
(\phi_{n})_{n\geqslant 1}
of
L_{0}^{2}(\mu_{V})
of associated eigenfunctions for
\mathcal{A}
, meaning that

•

\Span\{\phi_{n},\,n\geqslant 1\}
is dense in
L_{0}^{2}(\mu_{V})
,

•

For all
i,j
,
\Braket{\phi_{i},\phi_{j}}_{L^{2}(\mu_{V})}=\delta_{i,j}
,

•

For all
n\geqslant 1
,
\mathcal{A}[\phi_{n}]=\lambda_{n}\phi_{n}
.

Furthermore, each
\phi_{n}
is in
\mathcal{D}_{L^{2}_{0}(\mu_{V})}(\mathcal{A})
,
\mathcal{A}:\mathcal{D}_{L^{2}_{0}(\mu_{V})}(\mathcal{A})\to L_{0}^{2}(\mu_{V})
is bijective, and we have the writing, for
u\in L_{0}^{2}(\mu_{V})

\mathcal{A}^{-1}[u]=\sum_{n\geqslant 1}\lambda_{n}^{-1}\Braket{u,\phi_{n}}_{L^{2}(\mu_{V})}\phi_{n}\,.

We see the operators
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{W}
as unbounded operators on the space

\mathsf{H}=\Big\{u\in H^{1}(\mu_{V})\,|\,\int_{\mathbb{R}}u(x)d\mu_{V}(x)=0\Big\}

endowed with the inner product
\Braket{u,v}_{\mathsf{H}}=\Braket{u^{\prime},v^{\prime}}_{L^{2}(\mu_{V})}
. This defines an inner product on
\mathsf{H}
and makes it a complete space: it can be seen that
H^{1}(\mu_{V})
is the completion of
\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
with respect to the inner product
\Braket{u,v}_{L^{2}(\mu_{V})}+\Braket{u^{\prime},v^{\prime}}_{L^{2}(\mu_{V})}
, see
[
Zhi98
]
. The space
\mathsf{H}
is then the kernel of the bounded (with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{\mathsf{H}}
) linear form,
\langle\widetilde{1},\cdot\rangle_{L^{2}(\mu_{V})}
on
H^{1}(\mu_{V})
, and both inner products are equivalent on
\mathsf{H}
because of the Poincaré inequality, Proposition
2.6
. The use of
\mathsf{H}
is motivated by the fact that both
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{W}
are self-adjoint positive on this space as we show in Lemma
6.5
.

In the next proposition, we deduce from Theorem
6.2
the diagonalization of
\mathcal{A}
in
\mathsf{H}
.

Proposition 6.3

(Diagonalization of
\mathcal{A}
in
\mathsf{H}
)
.

With the same eigenvalues
0<\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}<\ldots
as in Theorem
6.2
, there exists a complete orthonormal set
(\varphi_{n})_{n\geqslant 1}
of
\mathsf{H}
formed by eigenfunctions of
\mathcal{A}
.

Proof.

With
(\phi_{n})_{n\geqslant 1}
of Theorem
6.2
,

\displaystyle\delta_{i,j}=\langle\phi_{i},\phi_{j}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mu_{V})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda_{j}}\langle\phi_{i},\mathcal{A}[\phi_{j}]\rangle_{L^{2}(\mu_{V})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda_{j}}\langle\phi_{i}^{\prime},\phi_{j}^{\prime}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mu_{V})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda_{j}}\langle\phi_{i},\phi_{j}\rangle_{\mathsf{H}}.

With
\varphi_{n}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_{n}}}\phi_{n}
,
(\varphi_{n})_{n\geqslant 1}
is then orthonormal with respect to the inner product of
\mathsf{H}
.
To show that
\Span\{\varphi_{n},n\geqslant 1\}
is dense in
\mathsf{H}
, let
u\in\mathsf{H}
be such that for all
j\geqslant 1
,
\langle u,\phi_{j}\rangle_{\mathsf{H}}=0
. In the last series of equalities, replace
\phi_{i}
by
u
: we see that
u
is orthogonal to each
\phi_{j}
in
L^{2}(\mu_{V})
, thus
u
is a constant as shown in the proof of Lemma
A.10
, and because
u\in\mathsf{H}
it has zero mean against
\rho_{V}
. This shows that
u=0
.
∎

We set for what follows
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})\overset{(\mathrm{def})}{=}\left\{u\in\mathsf{H}\,|\,\mathcal{A}[u]\in\mathsf{H}\right\}
and
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{W})\overset{(\mathrm{def})}{=}\left\{u\in\mathsf{H}\,|\,\mathcal{W}[u]\in\mathsf{H}\right\}
.

Remark 6.4
.

One can notice that
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})\subset\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{W})
. Indeed, it just suffices to see that whenever
u\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})
, then

\mathcal{W}[u]^{\prime}=\mathcal{H}\big[\rho_{V}\mathcal{A}[u]\big]\in L^{2}(\mu_{V}).

Since for all
u\in\mathsf{H}
,
\mathcal{W}[u]\in L^{2}_{0}(\mu_{V})
, then
\mathcal{W}[u]\in\mathsf{H}
.

Lemma 6.5
.

The following properties hold:

•

The operator
\mathcal{W}:\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{W})\to\mathsf{H}
is positive: for all
\phi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{W})
,

\langle\mathcal{W}[\phi],\phi\rangle_{\mathsf{H}}=\dfrac{1}{2}\|\phi^{\prime}\rho_{V}\|_{1/2}^{2}\geqslant 0\,,

with equality only for
\phi=0
, where the
1/2
-norm of
u
is given by

\|u\|_{1/2}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|x|.\left|\mathcal{F}[u](x)\right|^{2}dx\,.

•

Both
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{W}
are self-adjoint for the inner product of
\mathsf{H}
.

Proof.

To prove the first point, let
\phi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{W})
. Then,

2\pi\Braket{\mathcal{W}[\phi],\phi}_{\mathsf{H}}=-2\pi\Braket{\mathcal{H}[\phi^{\prime}\rho_{V}]^{\prime},\phi^{\prime}\rho_{V}}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}=-\Braket{ix\mathcal{F}\big[\mathcal{H}[\phi^{\prime}\rho_{V}]\big],\mathcal{F}[\phi^{\prime}\rho_{V}]}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\\
=\pi\Braket{|x|\mathcal{F}[\phi^{\prime}\rho_{V}],\mathcal{F}[\phi^{\prime}\rho_{V}]}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}=\pi\|\phi^{\prime}\rho_{V}\|_{1/2}^{2}\geqslant 0\,,

and because
\phi
is in
\mathsf{H}
, this last quantity is zero if and only if
\phi
vanishes.

For the second point, note that both
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})
and
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{W})
contain
\mathrm{span}(\varphi_{n},n\geqslant 1)
, so
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{W}
are densely defined. Let
i,j\in\mathbb{N}
, then by orthonormality

\braket{\mathcal{A}[\varphi_{i}],\varphi_{j}}_{\mathsf{H}}=\delta_{i,j}\lambda_{i}=\braket{\varphi_{i},\mathcal{A}[\varphi_{j}]}_{\mathsf{H}}

hence
\mathcal{A}
is symmetric.
Furthermore, we notice that
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})=\{u\in\mathsf{H},\sum_{n\geqslant 1}\lambda_{n}^{2}|\braket{u,\varphi_{n}}_{\mathsf{H}}|^{2}<+\infty\}
. Indeed, we extend
\mathcal{A}
on the space of the RHS by for all
v\in\{u\in\mathsf{H},\sum_{n\geqslant 1}\lambda_{n}^{2}|\braket{u,\psi_{n}}_{\mathsf{H}}|^{2}<+\infty\}
,

\mathcal{A}[v]\overset{(\mathrm{def})}{=}\sum_{n\geqslant 1}\lambda_{n}\braket{v,\psi_{n}}_{\mathsf{H}}\psi_{n}.

It is then clear that
\mathcal{A}[v]\in\mathsf{H}
. The converse is straightforward. Moreover, it is standard that
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})
is therefore the completion of span
(\varphi_{n},n\geqslant 1)
under the graph norm
\|u\|_{\mathcal{A}}^{2}\overset{(\mathrm{def})}{=}\|u\|^{2}_{\mathsf{H}}+\|\mathcal{A}[u]\|_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}
which makes
\big(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})\big)
a closed operator. Moreover,
\big(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})\big)
is self-adjoint since for
v\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}^{*})
, which is defined by:

\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}^{*})\overset{(\mathrm{def})}{=}\Big\{v\in\mathsf{H},\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})\mapsto\braket{\mathcal{A}[\varphi],v}_{\mathsf{H}}\;\mathrm{can}\;\mathrm{be}\;\mathrm{extended}\;\mathrm{into}\;\mathrm{a}\;\mathrm{continuous}\;\mathrm{linear}\;\mathrm{form}\;\mathrm{on}\;\mathsf{H}\Big\},

there exists, by Riesz theorem, an element
f_{v}\in\mathsf{H}
, such that for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})
,

\braket{\mathcal{A}[\varphi],v}_{\mathsf{H}}=\braket{\varphi,f_{v}}_{\mathsf{H}}\,.

Thus for all
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
,

\lambda_{n}\braket{\varphi_{n},v}_{\mathsf{H}}=\braket{\varphi_{n},f_{v}}_{\mathsf{H}}.

The right hand side defines a sequence in
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N}^{*})
because
f_{v}\in\mathsf{H}
and the fact that
(\varphi_{n})_{n}
is a Hilbertian basis, thus
\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\lambda_{n}^{2}|\braket{v,\psi_{n}}_{\mathsf{H}}|^{2}<+\infty
which is the definition of
v\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})
. This concludes the fact that
\mathcal{A}:\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})\rightarrow\mathsf{H}
is self-adjoint.

Now let
u,v\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{W})
, using Plancherel’s isometry and
i)
of Lemma
2.1
, we see that

\displaystyle\Braket{\mathcal{W}[u],v}_{\mathsf{H}}=\Braket{\mathcal{W}[u]^{\prime},v^{\prime}\rho_{V}}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}=\frac{1}{2}\Braket{|x|\mathcal{F}[u^{\prime}\rho_{V}],\mathcal{F}[v^{\prime}\rho_{V}]}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}\,,

and this last expression is symmetric in
(u,v)
, so
\mathcal{W}
is symmetric. To see that
\mathcal{W}
is self-adjoint, one must show that
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{W}^{*})\subset\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{W})
. Let
v\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{W}^{*})
, then the map
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{W})\mapsto\braket{\mathcal{W}[\varphi],v}_{\mathsf{H}}
can be extended to a continuous linear functional on
\mathsf{H}
, so there exists
f_{v}\in\mathsf{H}
such that for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{W})
,

\braket{-\mathcal{H}[\varphi^{\prime}\rho_{V}]^{\prime},v^{\prime}\rho_{V}}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}=\braket{\varphi^{\prime}\rho_{V},f_{v}^{\prime}}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}.

(60)

Let
w\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
, we define

\varphi_{w}(x)\overset{(\mathrm{def})}{=}\int_{-\infty}^{x}\dfrac{w(t)}{\rho_{V}(t)}dt-\int_{\mathbb{R}}d\mu_{V}(y)\int_{-\infty}^{y}\dfrac{w(t)}{\rho_{V}(t)}dt.

It is easy to check that
\varphi_{w}\in\mathsf{H}
and that

\mathcal{W}[\varphi_{w}]=-\mathcal{H}[w]+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{H}[w](t)d\mu_{V}(t)\in\mathsf{H},

therefore
\varphi_{w}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{W})
.
Finally, by inserting the definition of
\varphi_{w}
in (
60
), we obtain:

\braket{-\mathcal{H}[w^{\prime}],v^{\prime}\rho_{V}}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}=\braket{w,f_{v}^{\prime}}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})}.

By using that
\mathcal{H}
is skew self-adjoint, we obtain that, in the sense of distributions,
\mathcal{W}[v]^{\prime}=-\mathcal{H}[v^{\prime}\rho_{V}]^{\prime}=-f_{v}^{\prime}\in L^{2}(\mu_{V})
. Along with the fact that for all
\widetilde{v}\in\mathsf{H}
,
\mathcal{W}[\widetilde{v}]\in L^{2}_{0}(\mu_{V})
, we deduce that
\mathcal{W}[v]\in\mathsf{H}
, therefore that
v\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{W})
. This allows to conclude that
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{W}^{*})\subset\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{W})
, hence that
\mathcal{W}
is self-adjoint.
This concludes the proof.

∎

Definition 6.6

(Quadratic form associated to
-\mathcal{L}
)
.

We define for all
u,v\in\mathsf{H}\cap\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
the quadratic form associated to
-\mathcal{L}
by

q_{-\mathcal{L}}(u,v)=\braket{\mathcal{A}[u],\mathcal{A}[v]}_{L^{2}(\mu_{V})}+P\braket{\mathcal{F}[u^{\prime}\rho_{V}],\mathcal{F}[v^{\prime}\rho_{V}]}_{L^{2}(|x|dx)}\,.

Remark 6.7
.

Note that for all
u,v\in\mathsf{H}\cap\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
,
q_{-\mathcal{L}}(u,v)=\braket{-\mathcal{L}[u],v}_{\mathsf{H}}
and that whenever
u\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})
,

q_{-\mathcal{L}}(u,u)=\braket{\mathcal{A}[u],u}_{\mathsf{H}}+2P\braket{\mathcal{W}[u],u}_{\mathsf{H}}\geqslant\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{A})\|u\|_{\mathsf{H}}^{2}

(61)

by Proposition
6.3
and Lemma
6.5
. We can now extend
q_{-\mathcal{L}}
to its form domain

Q(\mathcal{L})\overset{(\mathrm{def})}{=}\Big\{u\in\mathsf{H},\mathcal{A}[u]\in L^{2}(\mu_{V}),\,\mathcal{F}[u^{\prime}\rho_{V}]\in L^{2}(|x|dx)\Big\}=\mathcal{D}_{L^{2}_{0}(\mu_{V})}(\mathcal{A}).

We now define
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{L})
the domain of definition of
-\mathcal{L}
by:

\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{L})\overset{(\mathrm{def})}{=}\Big\{v\in Q(\mathcal{L})\ |\ \varphi\in Q(\mathcal{L})\mapsto q_{-\mathcal{L}}(\varphi,v)\text{ can be extended into a continuous linear functional on }\mathsf{H}\Big\}.

Proposition 6.8
.

\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{L})=\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}).

Proof.

Let
v\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{L})
. By Riesz theorem, there exists
f_{v}\in\mathsf{H}
such that for all
\varphi\in Q(\mathcal{L})
,
q_{-\mathcal{L}}(\varphi,v)=\braket{u,f_{v}}_{\mathsf{H}}
. We set
-\mathcal{L}[v]\overset{(\mathrm{def})}{=}f_{v}
, it is called the Friedrichs extension of
-\mathcal{L}
. Then for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})\subset\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{L}),

\braket{\varphi,-\mathcal{L}[v]}_{\mathsf{H}}=q_{-\mathcal{L}}(\varphi,v)=\braket{\mathcal{A}[\varphi],v}_{\mathsf{H}}+2P\braket{\mathcal{W}[\varphi],v}_{\mathsf{H}}.

Since
v\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{L})\subset Q(\mathcal{L})
, then
\mathcal{W}[v]\in\mathsf{H}
, hence
\braket{\mathcal{W}[\varphi],v}_{\mathsf{H}}=\braket{\varphi,\mathcal{W}[v]}_{\mathsf{H}}
. We deduce that,

\braket{\mathcal{A}[\varphi],v}_{\mathsf{H}}=\braket{\varphi,-\mathcal{L}[v]-2P\mathcal{W}[v]}_{\mathsf{H}}

Taking
\varphi=\phi_{n}
, one deduces that:

\lambda_{n}\braket{\phi_{n},v}_{\mathsf{H}}=\braket{\phi_{n},-\mathcal{L}[v]-2P\mathcal{W}[v]}_{\mathsf{H}}.

Since
-\mathcal{L}[v]-2P\mathcal{W}[v]\in\mathsf{H}
, the sequence
\Big(\lambda_{n}\braket{\phi_{n},v}_{\mathsf{H}}\Big)_{n}=\Big(\braket{\phi_{n},-\mathcal{L}[v]-2P\mathcal{W}[v]}_{\mathsf{H}}\Big)_{n}\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N}^{*})
which is the definition of
v\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})
. Hence
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{L})\subset\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})
. Conversely, if
v\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})
, then for all
\varphi\in Q(\mathcal{L})
,

q_{-\mathcal{L}}(\varphi,v)=\braket{\varphi,\mathcal{A}[v]+2P\mathcal{W}[u]}_{\mathsf{H}}

which provides a continuous extension of
\varphi\in Q(\mathcal{L})\mapsto q_{-\mathcal{L}}(\varphi,v)
to
\mathsf{H}
, thus
v\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{L})
.

∎

We are now ready to state the main theorem of this section, that is the inversion of
\mathcal{L}
on
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{L})
.

Theorem 6.9

(Inversion of
\mathcal{L}
)
.

-\mathcal{L}:\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{L})\longrightarrow\mathsf{H}
is bijective. Furthermore,
(-\mathcal{L})^{-1}
is positive, continuous from
(\mathsf{H},\|.\|_{\mathsf{H}})
to
(\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{L}),\|.\|_{\mathsf{H}})
. More precisely, for all
f\in\mathsf{H}
,

\|\mathcal{L}^{-1}[f]\|_{\mathsf{H}}\leqslant\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{A})^{-1}\|f\|_{\mathsf{H}}.

Proof.

Let
f\in\mathsf{H}
, since
\braket{f,.}_{\mathsf{H}}
is a linear form on
Q(\mathcal{L})=\mathcal{D}_{L^{2}_{0}(\mu_{V})}(\mathcal{A})
which is, by (
61
), continuous with respect to
q_{-\mathcal{L}}
, one can applies Riesz theorem so there exists a unique
u_{f}\in\mathcal{D}_{L^{2}_{0}(\mu_{V})}(\mathcal{A})
, such that for all
v\in Q(\mathcal{L})
,
\braket{f,v}_{\mathsf{H}}=q_{-\mathcal{L}}(u_{f},v)
. Since,
u_{f}
is clearly in
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{L})
by definition of the Friedrichs extension of
-\mathcal{L}
, we have
-\mathcal{L}[u_{f}]=f
.
Finally, the continuity of
\mathcal{L}^{-1}
with respect to
\|.\|_{\mathsf{H}}
follows from (
61
).
∎

Remark 6.10

Lemma 6.11

Definition 6.12

(Fredholm determinant)
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a kernel Hilbert-Schmidt operator on
L^{2}(\mu_{V})
, we define the Fredholm 2-determinant of
\mathsf{id}+\mathcal{K}
by

\det_{2}\left(\mathsf{id}+\mathcal{K}\right)\overset{(\mathrm{def})}{=}1+\sum_{n\geqslant 1}\dfrac{1}{n!}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\begin{vmatrix}0&k(t_{1},t_{2})&\dots&k(t_{1},t_{n})\\
k(t_{2},t_{1})&0&\dots&k(t_{2},t_{n})\\
\vdots&&&\vdots\\
k(t_{n},t_{1})&k(t_{n},t_{2})&\ldots&0\end{vmatrix}\prod_{i=1}^{n}d\mu_{V}(t_{i}).

where
|.|
is the usual determinant for matrices.

Theorem 6.13

(Explicit expression for
\mathcal{L}^{-1}
)
.

For all
u\in\mathsf{H}
, such that
x\mapsto\dfrac{1}{\rho_{V}(x)}\displaystyle\int_{x}^{+\infty}u(t)\rho_{V}(t)dt
is integrable at
+\infty
, we have:

-\mathcal{L}^{-1}[u]=\mathcal{A}^{-1}[u]-\mathcal{A}^{-1}\mathcal{R}\big[u\big].

(63)

In this expression
\mathcal{A}^{-1}[u]
is given by

\mathcal{A}^{-1}[u](x)=-\int_{x}^{+\infty}\dfrac{ds}{\rho_{V}(s)}\int_{s}^{+\infty}u(t)\rho_{V}(t)dt+C

where
C=\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho_{V}(y)dy\int_{y}^{+\infty}\dfrac{ds}{\rho_{V}(s)}\int_{s}^{+\infty}u(t)\rho_{V}(t)dt
. The operator
\mathcal{R}
is the kernel operator defined for all
v\in L^{2}(\mu_{V})
by:

\mathcal{R}[v](x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}r(x,y)v(y)d\mu_{V}(y)

where

r(x,y)=\dfrac{1}{\underset{2}{\det}(\mathsf{id}-\mathcal{K})}\sum_{n\geqslant 1}\dfrac{(-1)^{n}}{n!}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\begin{vmatrix}k(x,y)&k(x,t_{1})&\dots&k(x,t_{n})\\
k(t_{1},y)&0&\dots&k(t_{1},t_{n})\\
\vdots&&&\vdots\\
k(t_{n},y)&k(t_{n},t_{1})&\ldots&0\end{vmatrix}\prod_{i=1}^{n}d\mu_{V}(t_{i}),

(64)

where
\mathcal{K}
is the kernel operator defined for all
w\in L^{2}(\mu_{V})
by:

\mathcal{K}[w](x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}k(x,y)w(y)dy

(65)

with

k(x,y)=2P\ln|x-y|-2P\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\ln|z-y|d\mu_{V}(z).

(66)

Finally,
2P\mathcal{W}\circ\mathcal{A}^{-1}=-\mathcal{K}
.

Proof.

Let
f\in\mathsf{H}
, there exists a unique
u\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})
such that
\mathcal{A}[u]=f
. Since
(u^{\prime}\rho_{V})^{\prime}=-\rho_{V}\mathcal{A}[u]\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
, we get
u^{\prime}\rho_{V}\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
, so
u^{\prime}(x)\rho_{V}(x)\underset{|x|\rightarrow+\infty}{\longrightarrow}0
. By definition,
-\dfrac{(u^{\prime}\rho_{V})^{\prime}}{\rho_{V}}=f\text{ hence }

\mathcal{A}^{-1}[f]^{\prime}(x)\rho_{V}(x)=u^{\prime}(x)\rho_{V}(x)=\int_{x}^{+\infty}f(t)\rho_{V}(t)dt.

(67)

Using the fact that
\int_{\mathbb{R}}u(x)\rho_{V}(x)dx=0
, integrating again we get:

u(x)=-\int_{x}^{+\infty}\dfrac{ds}{\rho_{V}(s)}\int_{s}^{+\infty}f(t)\rho_{V}(t)dt+C

where
C=\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho_{V}(x)dx\int_{x}^{+\infty}\dfrac{ds}{\rho_{V}(s)}\int_{s}^{+\infty}f(t)\rho_{V}(t)dt
.
Now using that that for all
g\in\mathsf{H}

-\mathcal{L}^{-1}=\Big(\mathcal{A}+2P\mathcal{W}\Big)^{-1}=\mathcal{A}^{-1}\Big(\mathsf{id}+2P\mathcal{W}\circ\mathcal{A}^{-1}\Big)^{-1}

We only need to compute the expression of
\Big(\mathsf{id}+2P\mathcal{W}\circ\mathcal{A}^{-1}\Big)^{-1}
.
Using (
67
), let
v\in\mathcal{A}\big(\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{L})\big)=\mathsf{H}
. We obtain:

\mathcal{W}\circ\mathcal{A}^{-1}[v](x)=-\fint_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{ds}{s-x}\int_{s}^{+\infty}dtv(t)\rho_{V}(t)+\int_{\mathbb{R}}d\mu_{V}(y)\fint_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{ds}{s-y}\int_{s}^{+\infty}dtv(t)\rho_{V}(t).

By Sokhotski-Plejmel formula, we have:

\fint_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{ds}{s-x}\int_{s}^{+\infty}dtv(t)\rho_{V}(t)=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow{0}^{+}}\underset{M\rightarrow+\infty}{\lim}\int_{-M}^{M}\dfrac{ds}{2}\Big\{\dfrac{1}{s-x+\mathrm{i}\varepsilon}+\dfrac{1}{s-x-\mathrm{i}\varepsilon}\Big\}\int_{s}^{+\infty}dtv(t)\rho_{V}(t).

We then proceed to an integration by part:

\fint_{\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{ds}{s-x}\int_{s}^{+\infty}dtv(t)\rho_{V}(t)=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow{0}^{+}}\underset{M\rightarrow+\infty}{\lim}\Big[\dfrac{\ln\big((s-x)^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\big)}{2}\int_{s}^{+\infty}dtv(t)\rho_{V}(t)\Big]_{-M}^{M}\\
+\int_{\mathbb{R}}ds\ln|x-s|v(s)\rho_{V}(s).

To conclude that
\mathcal{W}\circ\mathcal{A}^{-1}[v](x)=-\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}ds\ln|x-s|v(s)\rho_{V}(s)+\int_{\mathbb{R}}d\mu_{V}(y)\int_{\mathbb{R}}ds\ln|y-s|v(s)\rho_{V}(s)
, we just need to show that

\ln|x|\int_{x}^{+\infty}dtv(t)\rho_{V}(t)\underset{|x|\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}0

which can be seen by Cauchy-Schwarz inequality:

\Big|\ln(x)\int_{x}^{+\infty}dtv(t)\rho_{V}(t)\Big|\leqslant|\ln(x)|\|v\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{V})}.\rho_{V}(x)^{1/4}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho_{V}(t)^{1/2}dt\Big)^{1/2}.

In this inequality, we used that
\rho_{V}
is decreasing exponentially fast in a neighborhood of
+\infty
, hence

\ln(x)\int_{x}^{+\infty}dtv(t)\rho_{V}(t)\underset{x\rightarrow+\infty}{\longrightarrow}0.

Using that
\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}v(t)\rho_{V}(t)dt=0
, we have
\displaystyle\int_{x}^{+\infty}v(t)\rho_{V}(t)dt=-\int_{-\infty}^{x}v(t)\rho_{V}(t)dt
, therefore the exact same argument allows us to conclude when
x
goes to
-\infty
. We obtain the following equality:

(\mathsf{id}+2P\mathcal{W}\circ\mathcal{A}^{-1})[v](x)=v(x)-2P\int_{\mathbb{R}}ds\ln|x-s|v(s)\rho_{V}(s)+2P\int_{\mathbb{R}}d\mu_{V}(y)\int_{\mathbb{R}}ds\ln|y-s|v(s)\rho_{V}(s).

With the operator
\mathcal{K}
given in (
65
), by
[
GGK12
, Section XII.2]
, we can conclude that the inverse of the kernel operator
\mathsf{id}-\mathcal{K}
is
\mathsf{id}-\mathcal{R}
where
\mathcal{R}
is given in (
64
). This concludes the proof.

Proposition A.1

The operator

(77)

We compute directly

\displaystyle\dfrac{\Big(\rho_{V}\big(\rho_{V}^{-1/2}u\big)^{\prime}\Big)^{\prime}}{\rho_{V}}

\displaystyle=\big(\rho_{V}^{-1/2}u\big)^{\prime\prime}+\dfrac{\rho_{V}^{\prime}}{\rho_{V}}\big(\rho_{V}^{-1/2}u\big)^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\big(\rho_{V}^{-1/2}u^{\prime}-\dfrac{1}{2}\rho_{V}^{-3/2}\rho_{V}^{\prime}u\big)^{\prime}+\rho_{V}^{\prime}\rho_{V}^{-3/2}u^{\prime}-\dfrac{1}{2}\rho_{V}^{-5/2}\big(\rho_{V}^{\prime}\big)^{2}u

Now, using Lemma

Remark A.2
.

Note that the function

need not be positive on
{\mathbb{R}}
. In fact, neglecting the terms involving the Hilbert transforms of

and
\rho_{V}^{\prime}

would only be positive outside of a compact set. However, using the positivity of

, which will be shown further in the article, we can show that the operator

is itself positive on

is symmetric on

with respect to the inner product of

We now introduce an extension of
\mathcal{S}
by defining its associated bilinear form.

Definition A.3

(Quadratic form associated to
\mathcal{S}
)
.

Let

such that

. We define the quadratic form associated to
\mathcal{S}+\alpha\mathsf{id}
, defined for all

by

This quadratic form can be extended to a larger domain denoted by

, called the

of the operator
\mathcal{S}+\alpha\mathsf{id}
. By the theory of Schrödinger operators, it is well-known (see

Q(\mathcal{S}+\alpha\mathsf{id})=\left\{u\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}),u(w_{V}+\alpha)^{1/2}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})\right\}=\left\{u\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}),uV^{\prime}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})\right\}=:H^{1}_{V^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}})\,.

The space

can be seen to be the completion of
\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
under the norm

.
Now that the quadratic form associated to

has been extended to its form domain, it is possible to go back to the operator and extend it by its Friedrichs extension.

Theorem A.4

(Friedrichs extension of
\mathcal{S}+\alpha\mathsf{id}
)
.

There exists an extension
(\mathcal{S}+\alpha\mathsf{id})_{F}
of the operator
\mathcal{S}+\alpha\mathsf{id}

\mathcal{S}+\alpha\mathsf{id}
defined on
\mathcal{D}\Big((\mathcal{S}+\alpha\mathsf{id})_{F}\Big)=\Big\{u\in H^{1}_{V^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}),-u^{\prime\prime}+(w_{V}+\alpha)u\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})\Big\}

Proof.

We denote

\mathcal{D}\Big((\mathcal{S}+\alpha\mathsf{id})_{F}\Big)\\
\overset{(\mathrm{def})}{=}\Big\{v\in H^{1}_{V^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}),u\in H^{1}_{V^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}})\longmapsto q_{\alpha}(u,v)\text{ can be extended to a continuous linear functional on }L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})\Big\}

If

, by Riesz theorem, there exists a unique

such that

holds for all
u\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
and we can set
(\mathcal{S}+\alpha\mathsf{id})_{F}[v]\overset{(\mathrm{def})}{=}f_{v}
. Note that it is indeed a way of extending

since for all

We want to show that

and

, for all

\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(x)u(x)dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big[f^{\prime}(x)u^{\prime}(x)+(w_{V}(x)+\alpha)f(x)u(x)\Big]dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big[-f(x)u^{\prime\prime}(x)+(w_{V}(x)+\alpha)f(x)u(x)\Big]dx

, hence

In the following, we will deal only with

Remark A.5
.

Note that in the previous proof, the application of Riesz theorem doesn’t allow to say that

Theorem A.6

(Inversion of

\mathcal{A}
then

0=-\dfrac{\big(\phi^{\prime}\rho_{V}\big)^{\prime}}{\rho_{V}}\Rightarrow\exists c\in{\mathbb{R}},\,\phi^{\prime}=\dfrac{c}{\rho_{V}}

But since
\phi^{\prime}
is in
L^{2}(\mu_{V})
then
c=0
which implies that
\phi
is constant. We must restrict
\mathcal{A}
to the orthogonal of
Ker\mathcal{A}
with respect to the inner product of
L^{2}(\mu_{V})
, i.e.

\mathcal{D}_{L^{2}_{0}(\mu_{V})}(\mathcal{A})\overset{(\mathrm{def})}{=}\left\{u\in\mathcal{D}_{L^{2}(\mu_{V})}(\mathcal{A})\,|\,\int_{\mathbb{R}}u(x)d\mu_{V}(x)=0\right\}\,.

Doing so makes
\mathcal{A}
injective.

Before inverting
\mathcal{A}
, we need the following lemma:

Lemma A.10
.

The following equality holds

(\mathcal{A}+\alpha\mathsf{id})\Big(\mathcal{D}_{L^{2}_{0}(\mu_{V})}(\mathcal{A})\Big)=L_{0}^{2}(\mu_{V})\overset{(\mathrm{def})}{=}\Big\{u\in L^{2}(\mu_{V}),\,\int_{\mathbb{R}}u(x)d\mu_{V}(x)=0\Big\}

Proof.

Let
\phi=\widetilde{c}
for
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
(\mathcal{A}+\alpha\mathsf{id})[\phi]=\widetilde{\alpha c}
then
(\mathcal{A}+\alpha\mathsf{id})({\mathbb{R}}.\widetilde{1})={\mathbb{R}}\widetilde{1}
. Hence since
\mathcal{A}+\alpha\mathsf{id}
is self-adjoint with respect to the inner product of
L^{2}(\mu_{V})
and that
{\mathbb{R}}\widetilde{1}
is stable by
\mathcal{A}+\alpha\mathsf{id}
, then
(\mathcal{A}+\alpha\mathsf{id})\Big(({\mathbb{R}}.\widetilde{1})^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{D}_{L^{2}(\mu_{V})}(\mathcal{A})\Big)\subset({\mathbb{R}}.\widetilde{1})^{\perp}
. For the converse, let
u\in({\mathbb{R}}.\widetilde{1})^{\perp}
, since
\mathcal{A}+\alpha\mathsf{id}
is bijective, there exists
v\in\mathcal{D}_{L^{2}(\mu_{V})}(\mathcal{A})
such that
u=(\mathcal{A}+\alpha\mathsf{id})[v]
. For all
w\in{\mathbb{R}}.\widetilde{1}
,

0=\Braket{u,w}_{L^{2}(\mu_{V})}=\Braket{(\mathcal{A}+\alpha\mathsf{id})[v],w}_{L^{2}(\mu_{V})}=\Braket{v,(\mathcal{A}+\alpha\mathsf{id})[w]}_{L^{2}(\mu_{V})}

Hence
v\in\big[(\mathcal{A}+\alpha\mathsf{id})({\mathbb{R}}\widetilde{1})\big]^{\perp}={\mathbb{R}}\widetilde{1}^{\perp}
and so
({\mathbb{R}}.\widetilde{1})^{\perp}\subset(\mathcal{A}+\alpha\mathsf{id})\Big(({\mathbb{R}}.\widetilde{1})^{\perp}\Big)
.
∎

Remark A.11
.

It is easy to see that
L_{0}^{2}(\mu_{V})
is a closed subset of
L^{2}(\mu_{V})
as it is the kernel of the linear form
\phi\in L^{2}(\mu_{V})\mapsto\Braket{\phi,\widetilde{1}}_{L^{2}(\mu_{V})}
, making it a Hilbert space.

We now are able to diagonalize
\mathcal{A}
and deduce that it is invertible.

Proposition A.12

(Diagonalization and invertibility of
\mathcal{A}
)
.

Let
L_{0}^{2}(\mu_{V})
be given in Lemma (
A.10
).
There exists a complete orthonormal set of
\left(L_{0}^{2}(\mu_{V}),\Braket{.,.}_{L^{2}(\mu_{V})}\right)
,
(\phi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathcal{D}_{L^{2}_{0}(\mu_{V})}(\mathcal{A})^{\mathbb{N}}
(meaning that

\overline{\Span\{\phi_{n},\ n\geqslant 0\}}^{\|.\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{V})}}=L_{0}^{2}(\mu_{V})

and
\langle\phi_{i},\phi_{j}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mu_{V})}=\delta_{i,j}
), and an increasing sequence
(\lambda_{n})_{n\geqslant 1}
such that
\mathcal{A}[\phi_{n}]=\lambda_{n}\phi_{n}
. Furthermore,
\mathcal{A}:\mathcal{D}_{L^{2}_{0}(\mu_{V})}(\mathcal{A})\longrightarrow L_{0}^{2}(\mu_{V})
is bijective,
\mathcal{A}^{-1}
is continuous and verifies for all
v\in L_{0}^{2}(\mu_{V})
,

\|\mathcal{A}^{-1}[v]\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{V})}\leqslant\lambda_{1}^{-1}\|v\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{V})}.

Proof.

Since
(\mathcal{S}+\alpha\mathsf{id})^{-1}
considered as an operator of
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
, is compact so is
(\mathcal{A}+\alpha\mathsf{id})^{-1}
on
L^{2}(\mu_{V})
and since
\mathcal{A}
is self-adjoint, by the spectral theorem,
(\mathcal{A}+\alpha\mathsf{id})^{-1}
is diagonalizable. With the notations of Theorem
A.8
,
(\mathcal{A}+\alpha\mathsf{id})^{-1}
has eigenvalues
\big(\mu_{n}(\alpha)\big)_{n\geqslant 0}
and corresponding eigenfunctions
\phi_{n}=\rho_{V}^{-1/2}\psi_{n}\in\mathcal{D}_{L^{2}(\mu_{V})}(\mathcal{A})
. Hence for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\mathcal{A}[\phi_{n}]=\lambda_{n}\phi_{n}
with
\lambda_{n}\overset{(\mathrm{def})}{=}\big(\dfrac{1}{\mu_{n}(\alpha)}-\alpha\big)
. Now,

\lambda_{n}\|\phi_{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mu_{V})}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{A}[\phi_{n}](x)\phi_{n}(x)d\mu_{V}(x)=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\rho_{V}\phi_{n}^{\prime})^{\prime}(x)\phi_{n}(x)dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi_{n}^{\prime}(x)^{2}d\mu_{V}(x)\geqslant\,0\,.

Furthermore, the kernel of
\mathcal{A}
is
{\mathbb{R}}.\widetilde{1}
, thus
\mathcal{A}
restricted to
\mathcal{D}_{L^{2}_{0}(\mu_{V})}(\mathcal{A})
is positive. Finally,
\mathcal{A}
is surjective in
L^{2}_{0}(\mu_{V})
:
First, notice that since
\mathcal{A}
is self-adjoint

\mathcal{D}_{L^{2}_{0}(\mu_{V})}(\mathcal{A})=\left\{u\in L^{2}_{0}(\mu_{V})\ |\ \sum_{n=1}^{+\infty}\lambda_{n}^{2}\braket{u,\phi_{n}}_{L^{2}(\mu_{V})}^{2}<+\infty\right\}\,.

(78)

Now, for
v\in L^{2}_{0}(\mu_{V})
, we have

\mathcal{A}[u]=v\text{,\quad where \quad}u\overset{(\mathrm{def})}{=}\sum_{n\geqslant 1}\dfrac{\braket{v,\phi_{n}}_{L^{2}(\mu_{V})}}{\lambda_{n}}\phi_{n}.

u
is indeed an element of
\mathcal{D}_{L^{2}_{0}(\mu_{V})}(\mathcal{A})
because of (
78
), the fact that
v\in L^{2}(\mu_{V})
and that
(\lambda_{n})_{n}
is bounded from below. Finally, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, by orthonormality of
(\phi_{n})_{n}
,

\braket{\mathcal{A}[u],\phi_{n}}_{L^{2}(\mu_{V})}=\lambda_{n}\braket{u,\phi_{n}}_{L^{2}(\mu_{V})}=\braket{v,\phi_{n}}_{L^{2}(\mu_{V})}

which allows to conclude on the bijectivity of
\mathcal{A}:\mathcal{D}_{L^{2}_{0}(\mu_{V})}(\mathcal{A})\longrightarrow L_{0}^{2}(\mu_{V})
. Finally, it is easy to see that

\|\mathcal{A}^{-1}[v]\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{V})}^{2}=\sum_{n\geqslant 1}\dfrac{|\braket{v,\phi_{n}}_{L^{2}(\mu_{V})}|^{2}}{\lambda_{n}^{2}}\leqslant\lambda_{1}^{-2}\|v\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{V})}^{2}.

∎

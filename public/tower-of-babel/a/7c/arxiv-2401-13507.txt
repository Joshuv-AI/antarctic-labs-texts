On the GREM approximation of TAP free energies Thanks: We are grateful to Nicola Kistler for leading the way in this line of research.
Giulia Sebastiani, Marius Alexander Schmidt

Abstract
. We establish both a Boltzmann-Gibbs principle and a Parisi formula for the limiting free energy of an abstract GREM (Generalized Random Energy Model) which provides an approximation of the TAP (Thouless-Anderson-Palmer) free energies associated to the Sherrington-Kirkpatrick (SK) model.

1.
Introduction

Introduced by Derrida
[
12
,
13
]
in the 80s, the Generalized Random Energy Models (GREMs) serve as simplified, abstract models that are completely solvable and have significantly contributed to our comprehension of specific facets within the Parisi theory for mean field spin glasses
[
23
]
.

The simplest of Derrida’s models is the Random Energy Model (REM)
[
12
]
, whose Hamiltonian at volume
N\in\mathbb{N}
is given by a vector
\{H_{N}(\sigma)\}_{\sigma\in\{-1,+1\}^{N}}
of independent Gaussian random variables with variance
N
. The GREMs
[
13
]
generalize the REM, featuring hierarchically correlated Gaussian fields that inherently exhibit the ultrametricity predicted by the Parisi theory.

In
[
21
]
we related the REM and the Thouless-Anderson-Palmer (TAP) free energies
[
33
]
for the Sherrington-Kirkpatrick (SK) model
[
30
]
, the prototypical mean field spin glass. The linkage is based on the observation that the free energy of
TAP-solutions
for the SK can be expressed as a
highly nonlinear
functional of the
local fields
(see (
4
) and (
5
) below). Through a
replacement
of these fields we obtain abstract models involving only the alleged geometrical properties of the
relevant
TAP-solutions, that are solvable using Boltzmann formalism through a classical, Sanov-type large deviation analysis.

To see how this unfolds and for the reader’s convenience, we briefly review the setting given in Section 2 of
[
21
]
. The starting point is a non-rigorous result
[
33
]
of Thouless, Anderson, and Palmer, which expresses the free energy associated to the SK Hamiltonian (by means of a diagrammatic expansion of the partition function) as a constrained maximum of a certain disorder-dependent function, the so-called TAP free energy (
1
). This function is defined on the space of possible magnetizations of the spins, given as
[-1,1]^{N}
where
N
is the system size.

With
\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
the inverse temperature parameter,
h\in\mathbb{R}
an external magnetic field and
J_{ij}
i.i.d. standard Gaussians issued on some probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
, namely the quenched disorder, the SK Hamiltonian at volume
N
is given by the Gaussian field
\sigma\in\{-1,+1\}^{N}\mapsto(2N)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\beta\sum_{i\neq j}J_{ij}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}+h\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sigma_{i}
. The TAP free energy in
{\bf m}=(m_{i})_{i=1}^{N}
can be written, shortening
\bar{J}_{ij}:=(J_{ij}+J_{ji})/\sqrt{2}
, as

\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{N}}\sum\limits_{i<j}\bar{J}_{ij}m_{i}m_{j}+h\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}m_{i}-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}I(m_{i})+\frac{\beta^{2}N}{4}(1-\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}m_{i}^{2})^{2}

(1)

where
I
is the classic coin tossing rate function
I(m):=\frac{1+m}{2}\log(1+m)+\frac{1-m}{2}\log(1-m)
, which can also be expressed as

I(m)=m\tanh^{-1}(m)-\log\cosh\tanh^{-1}(m).

(2)

The TAP formulation is notably compelling, providing a physically meaningful representation consisting of three discernible components: internal energy, entropic contribution and Onsager correction. Hence, the construction and study of simplified models incorporating the structural elements of the TAP formulation gives a natural avenue to enhance the framework that ideally bridges the classical Boltzmann-Gibbs theory with the powerful and intricate, yet as of now still not fully understood, Parisi theory for mean field spin glasses.

A quick computation shows that the critical points of the TAP free energy (
1
) must be solutions of the
TAP equations

m_{i}=\tanh[\,h+\beta h_{i}({\bf m})\,],\qquad i=1,\dots,N,

(3)

where

h_{i}({\bf m}):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{j\neq i}\bar{J}_{ij}m_{j}-\beta(1-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}m^{2}_{j})m_{i}.

(4)

.

For an analysis of the TAP equations, see e.g.
[

.
A proof that a constrained maximum of the TAP free energy yields the exact SK free energy can be found in

, see

and references therein.

If we assume to have a solution to (
3
), say
{\bf m}^{\alpha}\in[-1,1]^{N}
, and evaluate the TAP free energy (

) in
{\bf m}^{\alpha}
; we find it to be equal to
\text{TAP}_{N}({\bf m}^{\alpha})
, where

\displaystyle\text{TAP}_{N}({\bf m})

\displaystyle:=\frac{\beta^{2}}{4}N\left[\,1-\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\tanh^{2}(h+\beta h_{j}({\bf m}))\right)^{\,2}\,\right]+

(5)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\,-\frac{\beta}{2}h_{i}({\bf m})\tanh(h+\beta h_{i}({\bf m}))+\log\cosh(h+\beta h_{i}({\bf m}))\,\right).

This is easily seen, as follows. If
{\bf m}^{\alpha}
is a solution to (
3
), then the equality

\beta h_{i}({\bf m}^{\alpha})=\tanh^{-1}(m_{i}^{\alpha})-h

must hold for every
i=1,\dots,N
. Using the definition (
4
), the latter implies

\frac{\beta}{2}\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{j\neq i}\bar{J}_{ij}m_{j}^{\alpha}=\frac{1}{2}\tanh^{-1}(m^{\alpha}_{i})-\frac{h}{2}+\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}(1-q_{N}^{\,\alpha})m^{\alpha}_{i},

(6)

where we shortened

q_{N}^{\,\alpha}:=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}(m^{\alpha}_{j})^{2}
.

Consider now the TAP free energy (
1
) evaluated in
{\bf m}={\bf m}^{\alpha}
, which can be rewritten as

\frac{\beta^{2}N}{4}(1-q_{N}^{\,\alpha})^{2}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\left[\left(\frac{\beta}{2}\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum\limits_{j\neq i}\bar{J}_{ij}m^{\alpha}_{j}+h\right)m^{\alpha}_{i}-I(m^{\alpha}_{i})\right].

Plugging (
6
) into this expression, we get

\displaystyle\frac{\beta^{2}N}{4}(1-q_{N}^{\,\alpha})^{2}+\frac{\beta^{2}N}{2}(1-q_{N}^{\,\alpha})q_{N}^{\,\alpha}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\left[\frac{1}{2}m^{\alpha}_{i}\tanh^{-1}(m^{\alpha}_{i})+\frac{h}{2}m^{\alpha}_{i}-I(m_{i}^{\alpha})\right]

(7)

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta^{2}N}{4}[1-(q_{N}^{\,\alpha})^{2}]+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\left[-\frac{1}{2}m_{i}^{\alpha}\tanh^{-1}(m^{\alpha}_{i})+\frac{h}{2}m_{i}^{\alpha}+\log\cosh\tanh^{-1}(m^{\alpha}_{i})\right],

where we used the representation (
2
) for the function
I
.

Expressing each
m^{\alpha}_{i}
as
\tanh(h+\beta h_{i}({\bf m}^{\alpha}))
, specifically
q_{N}^{\alpha}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\tanh(h+\beta h_{i}({\bf m}^{\alpha}))
, the latter becomes

\displaystyle\frac{\beta^{2}}{4}N\left[\,1-\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\tanh^{2}(h+\beta h_{i}({\bf m}^{\alpha}))\right)^{\,2}\,\right]+

(8)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\,-\frac{\beta}{2}h_{i}({\bf m}^{\alpha})\tanh(h+\beta h_{i}({\bf m}^{\alpha}))+\log\cosh(h+\beta h_{i}({\bf m}^{\alpha}))\,\right),

which is exactly
\text{TAP}_{N}({\bf m}^{\alpha})
as defined in (
5
).

The
REM approximation of TAP free energies

[
21
]
is achieved by replacing the local fields
{h_{i}({\bf m}^{\alpha})}
with a set of REM-like variables, specifically independent standard Gaussians
{X_{\alpha,i}}
, where
i=1,\dots,N
and
\alpha=1\dots 2^{N}
.

Positioned as the second natural step beyond the REM-approximation, this paper explores the
GREM approximation of TAP free energies
, wherein the local fields are substituted with GREM-like variables (see (
10
) below).
For the GREM approximation of TAP free energies, we can establish an orthodox Boltzmann-Gibbs principle and introduce a dual formula (given in Theorem
2.2
below) for the free energy, akin to the Parisi formula, which closely resembles the one given by the Parisi theory for models within the
1-step replica symmetry breaking
approximation. The highlight is that the inherent non-linearities of (
5
) bring the Parisi formulation for our GREM-like models into closer correspondence with that for the free energy of the SK, presenting a departure from conventional GREM formulations (for more on this, see Remark (
2.3
) below).
Our original result, Theorem
2.2
below, suggests that the correction to the Parisi formula, caused by the inherent nonlinearities, remains unchanged if we perform a
GREM-replacement
instead of a
REM-replacement
; that is, if we admit more than one level of hierarchy. Specifically, this correction does not vary for models within the
n
-steps replica symmetry breaking
approximation, where
n\in\mathbb{N}
is the number of observable levels of hierarchy.

In the next section, we provide the necessary definitions and present our results, which we then prove in the subsequent two sections.

\nu_{a}:=a\mu+(1-a)\nu^{*}\in\mathcal{K}\quad\text{for}\quad a\in(0,1).

(32)

One has that
\nu_{a}\to\nu^{*}
weakly as
a\to 0
, and
\boldsymbol{\Phi}[\nu_{a}]\to\boldsymbol{\Phi}[\nu^{*}]
,
H(\nu_{a}\mid\mu)\to H(\nu^{*}\mid\mu)
. Given
\epsilon^{\prime}>0
we choose
a=a(\epsilon^{\prime})>0
, and a neighborhood
U
of
\nu_{a}
:

\boldsymbol{\Phi}[\nu_{a}]-H(\nu_{a}\mid\mu)\geq\boldsymbol{\Phi}[\nu^{*}]-H(\nu^{*}\mid\mu)-\epsilon^{\prime},

(33)

\boldsymbol{\Phi}[\nu]\geq\boldsymbol{\Phi}[\nu_{a}]-\epsilon^{\prime}\quad\forall\nu\in U.

(34)

Then

\displaystyle f_{N}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{N}\log\sum_{\alpha:L_{N,\alpha}\in U}\exp{N\boldsymbol{\Phi}[L_{N,\alpha}]}\geq\boldsymbol{\Phi}[\nu_{a}]-\epsilon^{\prime}+\frac{1}{N}\log M_{N}(U)

(35)

so that

\displaystyle\liminf\limits_{N\to\infty}f_{N}

\displaystyle\geq\boldsymbol{\Phi}[\nu_{a}]-H(\nu_{a}\mid\mu)+\Theta\log 2-\epsilon^{\prime}\geq\boldsymbol{\Phi}[\nu^{*}]-H(\nu^{*}\mid\mu)+\Theta\log 2-2\epsilon^{\prime},

(36)

which gives the lower bound. This, together with (
28
), proves Theorem
2.1
.

4.
The Parisi principle: proof of Theorem
2.2

In this Section we prove Theorem
2.2
. First, we show that the Parisi function (
20
) has a minum point on
[\inf\varphi,\sup\varphi]\times\Delta
. To this aim, notice that applying Jensen’s inequality and Fubini’s theorem
n
times we get

\displaystyle P_{0}^{\,q,{\bf m}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m_{1}}\log\int\exp{m_{1}P^{\,q,{\bf m}}_{1}(x_{1})}\mu_{1}(dx_{1})\geq\int P_{1}^{(q,{\bf m})}(x_{1})\mu_{1}(dx_{1})

\displaystyle=\int\frac{1}{m_{2}}\log\left(\int\exp{m_{2}P_{2}(x_{1},x_{2})}\mu_{2}(dx_{2})\right)\,\mu_{1}(dx_{1})\geq\int P_{2}^{\,q,{\bf m}}(x_{1},x_{2})\mu_{1}\otimes\mu_{2}(dx_{1},dx_{2})

\displaystyle\geq\int P^{\,q,{\bf m}}_{n}({\bf x})\mu(d{\bf x})=\int\,\Phi^{\prime}(q)\varphi({\bf x})+f({\bf x})\,\mu(d{\bf x});

so that

\displaystyle P(q,{\bf m})

\displaystyle=\Phi(q)-q\Phi^{\prime}(q)+P^{\,q,\bf m}_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\gamma_{k}\Theta\log 2}{m_{k}}-\Theta\log 2

\displaystyle\geq\Phi(q)-q\Phi^{\prime}(q)+\sup_{{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mid\Phi^{\prime}(q)\varphi({\bf x})+f({\bf x})\mid+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\gamma_{k}\Theta\log 2}{m_{k}}-\Theta\log 2,

which implies that
P(q,{\bf m})\to+\infty
as
(q,{\bf m})
tends in
[\inf\varphi,\sup\varphi]\times\Delta\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
to any point
(q^{\prime},0,m^{\prime}_{2},\cdots,m^{\prime}_{n})
. The infimum of
P
on
[\inf\varphi,\sup\varphi]\times\Delta
, where we recall
\Delta=\{{\bf m}=(m_{1},\dots,m_{n})\in[0,1]^{n}:0<m_{1}\leq\dots\leq m_{n}\leq 1\}
, must be therefore equal to the infimum of
P
on
[\inf\varphi,\sup\varphi]\times\{{\bf m}\in\Delta:m_{1}\geq\epsilon\}
, for some
\epsilon>0
.

Specifically, as
P
achieves minimum on the compact set
[\inf\varphi,\sup\varphi]\times\{{\bf m}\in\Delta:m_{1}\geq\epsilon\}
, being there continous; it must achieve minimum on
[\inf\varphi,\sup\varphi]\times\Delta
. To wit

\text{argmin}_{[\inf\varphi,\sup\varphi]\times\Delta}P\neq\emptyset,

and we can state the following Lemma, whose simple proof shows how the Parisi functional enables finding the solution to the Boltzmann-Gibbs principle.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\bar{q}\in\mathbb{R},\bar{{\bf m}}=(\bar{m}_{j})_{j=1}^{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be s.t.
(\bar{q},{\bf\bar{m}})\in\text{argmin}_{[\inf\varphi,\sup\varphi]\times\Delta}P
, and consider the probability measure
G=G^{\bar{q},{\bar{\bf m}}}\in\mathcal{M}^{+}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
whose Radon-Nikodym derivative w.r.t.
\mu=\bigotimes_{j=1}^{n}\mu_{j}
is

\frac{dG}{d\mu}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}):=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{e^{\bar{m}_{j}P^{\,\bar{q},{\bf\bar{m}}}_{j}(x_{1},\dots,x_{j})}}{\int e^{\bar{m}_{j}P^{\,\bar{q},{\bf\bar{m}}}_{j}(x_{1},\dots,x_{j-1},x^{\prime}_{j})}\mu_{j}(dx^{\prime}_{j})}.

(37)

Then
G\in\mathcal{K}
and

\boldsymbol{\Phi}[\nu]-H(\nu\mid\mu)\leq\boldsymbol{\Phi}[G]-H(G\mid\mu)

(38)

holds
\forall\nu\in\mathcal{K}
.

Proof.

For
{\bf x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
a generic point in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and every
j\in\{1,\cdots,n\}
, we write
{\bf x}_{j}:=(x_{1},\dots,x_{j})
for its projection on the first
j
coordinates and shorten

P_{j}({\bf x}_{j}):=P_{j}^{\,\bar{q},{\bar{\bf m}}}({\bf x}_{j}).

We also set for every couple
(q,{\bf m})\in[\inf\varphi,\sup\varphi]\times\Delta
and given a
\mu^{(j)}-
measurable function
v:S^{j}\to\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle{\sf{E}}^{\,q,\bf m}_{j}(v)

\displaystyle:=\int v({\bf x}_{j})\frac{e^{m_{1}P^{\,q,\bf m}_{1}(x_{1})}}{\int e^{m_{1}P^{\,q,\bf m}_{1}(x^{\prime}_{1})}\mu_{1}(dx_{1}^{\prime})}\dots\frac{e^{m_{j}P^{\,q,\bf m}_{j}({\bf x}_{j})}}{\int e^{m_{j}P^{\,q,\bf m}_{j}({\bf x}_{j-1},x_{j}^{\prime})}\mu_{j}(dx^{\prime}_{j})}\mu^{(j)}(d{\bf x}_{j})

(39)

and

\displaystyle H^{\,q,\bf m}_{j}({\bf x}_{j-1})

\displaystyle:=\int\log\left(\frac{e^{m_{j}P^{\,q,\bf m}_{j}({\bf x}_{j})}}{\int e^{m_{j}P^{\,q,\bf m}_{j}({\bf x}_{j-1},x^{\prime}_{j})}\mu_{j}(dx^{\prime}_{j})}\right)\,\frac{e^{m_{j}P^{\,q,\bf m}_{j}({\bf x}_{j})}}{\int e^{m_{j}P^{\,q,\bf m}_{j}({\bf x}_{j-1},x^{\prime}_{j})}\mu_{j}(dx^{\prime}_{j})}\mu_{j}(dx_{j}),

(40)

with
H^{\,q,\bf m}_{1}
dependent only on
(q,{\bf m})
. It is readily checked that if
(q,{\bf m})=(\bar{q},{\bf\bar{m}})
then
{\sf{E}}^{\,\bar{q},{\bf\bar{m}}}_{j}
is the expectiation w.r.t the marginal
G^{(j)}
of the measure
G
, and that therefore the relation

{\sf{E}}^{\,\bar{q},{\bf\bar{m}}}_{j}(v)=\int v({\bf x}_{j})\,G^{(j)}(d{\bf x}_{j})

(41)

holds for every
j\in\{1,\cdots,n\}
and every
\mu^{(j)}-
measurable function
v
. Upon closer inspection, it also becomes evident that

H^{\,\bar{q},{\bf\bar{m}}}_{1}=H(G^{(1)}\mid\mu_{1})

(42)

and that if
j\geq 2
and
(X_{1},\dots,X_{j})
is a random vector distributed according to
G^{(j)}
, then
H^{\,q,\bf m}_{j}({\bf x}_{j-1})
is the Kullback-Leibler divergence between
\mu^{(j)}
and the conditional probability distribution of
X_{j}
given
X_{1}=x_{1},\dots,X_{j-1}=x_{j-1}
.

This notation enables us to express partial derivatives of
P
in a compact and convenient manner. Specifically, a straightforward computation shows that, if
\partial_{q}P
is the partial derivative of
P
w.r.t the variable
q
and, for every
j\in\{1,\cdots,n\}
,
\partial_{m_{j}}P
is the one w.r.t to the variable
m_{j}
; then

\begin{cases}\partial_{q}P(q,{\bf m})=\Phi^{\prime\prime}(q)\left[{\sf{E}}^{\,q,\bf m}_{n}(\varphi)-q\right]\\
\\
\partial_{m_{1}}P(q,{\bf m})=\frac{1}{m_{1}^{2}}\left[H^{\,q,\bf m}_{1}-\gamma_{1}\Theta\log 2\right]\\
\\
\partial_{m_{j}}P(q,{\bf m})=\frac{1}{m^{2}_{j}}\left[{\sf{E}}^{\,q,\bf m}_{j-1}(H^{\,q,\bf m}_{j})-\gamma_{j}\Theta\log 2\right]\quad\forall j\in\{2,\cdots,n\}.\end{cases}

(43)

Through the first of these relations, the minimality of
\bar{q}
for the univariate function
q\to P(q,{\bf\bar{m}})
on
[\inf\varphi,\sup\varphi]
, together with the fact that
\Phi^{\prime\prime}
is strictly negative on
[\inf\varphi,\sup\varphi]
; imply

\bar{q}={\sf{E}}^{\,\bar{q},{\bf\bar{m}}}_{n}(\varphi)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{relation_E_Gj}\,\text{with}\,j\equiv n}}{{=}}\,\,\,\int\varphi({\bf x})G(d{\bf x}).

(44)

Similarly, the minimality of
\bar{m}_{1}\neq 0
for the univariate function
m_{1}\to P(\bar{q},m_{1},\bar{m}_{2},\dots,\bar{m}_{n})
on
(0,\bar{m}_{2}]

implies that
\partial_{m_{1}}P(\bar{q},{\bf\bar{m}})\leq 0
, i.e.

\gamma_{1}\Theta\log 2\geq H^{\,\bar{q},{\bf\bar{m}}}_{1}\,\,\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{relation_H_HG}}}{{=}}H(G^{(1)}\mid\mu_{1});

(45)

which amounts to say that the probability measure
G
satisfies the
first
entropic condition appearing in the definition (
16
) of the set
\mathcal{K}
.

Given (
45
) as a base case, the fact that the couple
G
actually satisfy
all
the entropic conditions, i.e. that

H(G^{(j)}\mid\mu^{(j)})\leq\Theta\log 2\sum_{k\in[j]}\gamma_{k}

(46)

for every
j\in\{1,\cdots,n\}
, and therefore
G\in\mathcal{K}
; can be easily proved by induction. To see how this comes about, consider
j\in\{2,\cdots,n\}
and assume that for any
k<j

H(G^{(k)}\mid\mu^{(k)})\leq\Theta\log 2\sum_{l\in[k]}\gamma_{l}

(47)

i.e. the inductive hypothesis.
Then consider

\text{min}_{j}:=\min\{k:\quad 1\leq k\leq j:\quad\bar{m}_{k}=\bar{m}_{j}\},

and, setting
\bar{m}_{0}:=0,\bar{m}_{n+1}:=1
, the interval
(\bar{m}_{\text{min}_{j}-1},\bar{m}_{j+1}]
. Notice that
one has
0\!\!<\!\!\bar{m}_{1}\!\leq\!\bar{m}_{k}\,\,\forall k\leq n
, so that the interval
(\bar{m}_{\text{min}_{j}-1},\bar{m}_{j+1}]
is non-empty and we can therefore define
the real function
m\in(\bar{m}_{\text{min}_{j}-1},\bar{m}_{j+1}]\to g_{j}(m)
given by

g_{j}(m):=\begin{cases}P(\bar{q},m,\dots,m)&\text{if}\,\,\min_{j}=1,\,j=n\,;\\
P(\bar{q},\bar{m}_{1},\dots,\bar{m}_{\text{min}_{j}-1},m,\dots,m)&\text{if}\,\,\min_{j}>1,\,j=n\,;\\
P(\bar{q},m,\dots,m,\bar{m}_{j+1},\dots,\bar{m}_{n})&\text{if}\,\,\min_{j}=1,\,j<n\,;\\
P(\bar{q},\bar{m}_{1},\dots,\bar{m}_{\text{min}_{j}-1},m,\dots,m,\bar{m}_{j+1},\dots,\bar{m}_{n})&\text{if}\,\,\min_{j}>1,\,j<n\,.\\
\end{cases}

(48)

By construction,
g_{j}
must have a minimum point in
m=\bar{m}_{j}\in(\bar{m}_{\text{min}_{j}-1},\bar{m}_{j+1}]
which is a critical point for
g_{j}
if
\bar{m}_{j}\neq\bar{m}_{j+1}
while, possibly, such that
g^{\prime}_{j}(\bar{m}_{j})<0
if
\bar{m}_{j}=\bar{m}_{j+1}
.

As
g^{\prime}_{j}(\bar{m}_{j})=\sum\limits_{k=\text{min}_{j}}^{j}\partial_{m_{k}}P(\bar{q},{\bf{\bar{m}}})
, this implies that it must hold
\sum\limits_{k=\text{min}_{j}}^{j}\partial_{m_{k}}P(\bar{q},{\bf{\bar{m}}})\leq 0
in any case.

In order to include the case
\text{min}_{j}\equiv 1
we extend our notation setting
{\sf{E}}^{\,\bar{q},{\bf\bar{m}}}_{0}(H^{\,\bar{q},{\bf\bar{m}}}_{1})\equiv H^{\,\bar{q},{\bf\bar{m}}}_{1}
and
H(G^{(0)}\mid\mu^{(0)})\equiv 0
. Specifically, through the relations (
43
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=\text{min}_{j}}^{j}\partial_{m_{k}}P(\bar{q},{\bf{\bar{m}}})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=\text{min}_{j}}^{j}\frac{1}{\bar{m}^{2}_{k}}\left[{\sf{E}}^{\,\bar{q},{\bf\bar{m}}}_{k-1}(H^{\,\bar{q},{\bf\bar{m}}}_{k})-\Theta\gamma_{k}\log 2\right]

(49)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\bar{m}^{2}_{j}}\sum\limits_{k=\text{min}_{j}}^{j}\left[{\sf{E}}^{\,\bar{q},{\bf\bar{m}}}_{k-1}(H^{\,\bar{q},{\bf\bar{m}}}_{k})-\Theta\gamma_{k}\log 2\right]\leq 0.

The well known
chain-rule
for Kullback-Leibler divergences is now basically everything we need. With our notation, it is equivalent to the fact that for each
k

H(G^{(k)}\mid\mu^{(k)})=H(G^{(k-1)}\mid\mu^{(k-1)})+{\sf{E}}^{\,\bar{q},{\bf\bar{m}}}_{k-1}(H^{\,\bar{q},{\bf\bar{m}}}_{k}),

(50)

which in turn implies that

H(G^{(j)}\mid\mu^{(j)})=H(G^{(\text{min}_{j}-1)}\mid\mu^{(\text{min}_{j}-1)})+\sum_{k=\text{min}_{j}}^{j}{\sf{E}}^{\,\bar{q},{\bf\bar{m}}}_{k-1}(H^{\,\bar{q},{\bf\bar{m}}}_{k}).

Using the latter together with the last inequality in (
49
), we get

\displaystyle H(G^{(j)}\mid\mu^{(j)})\leq H(G^{(\text{min}_{j}-1)}\mid\mu^{(\text{min}_{j}-1)})+\Theta\log 2\sum\limits_{k=\text{min}_{j}}^{j}\gamma_{k}

(51)

so that the induction step follows using the inductive hypotesis (
47
) for
k\equiv\text{min}_{j}-1<j
. All in all, we proved that (
46
) holds for every
j\in\{1,\cdots n\}
, therefore that
G\in\mathcal{K}
.

We now prove (
38
), i.e. our claim is that for any fixed
\nu\in\mathcal{K}

\boldsymbol{\Phi}[G]-H(G\mid\mu)-\boldsymbol{\Phi}[\nu]+H(\nu\mid\mu)\geq 0.

(52)

In order to lighten notation, we set

\displaystyle D_{n}:=\boldsymbol{\Phi}[G]-H(G\mid\mu)-\boldsymbol{\Phi}[\nu]+H(\nu\mid\mu);

\displaystyle D_{j}:=\int P_{j}\,dG^{(j)}-\frac{1}{\bar{m}_{j+1}}H(G^{(j)}\mid\mu^{(j)})-\int P_{j}\,d\nu^{(j)}+\frac{1}{\bar{m}_{j+1}}H(\nu^{(j)}\mid\mu^{(j)}),\quad j\in\{1,\cdots,n-1\}.

We will prove that
D_{1}\geq 0
and that
D_{k}\geq D_{k-1}
for any
k\in\{2,\cdots,n\}
, which implies
D_{n}\geq 0
, namely the claim (
52
). Both proofs follow a slight extension of the argument we used to show that
G\in\mathcal{K}
.
We start by proving that
D_{1}\geq 0
.

First notice that, considering the two possible cases
\bar{m}_{1}=\bar{m}_{2}
and
\bar{m}_{1}<\bar{m}_{2}
, we have:

(1)

if
\bar{m}_{1}=\bar{m}_{2}
then

\displaystyle D_{1}=

\displaystyle\int P_{1}\,dG^{(1)}-\frac{1}{\bar{m}_{1}}H(G^{(1)}\mid\mu_{1})-\int P_{1}\,d\nu^{(1)}+\frac{1}{\bar{m}_{1}}H\left(\nu^{(1)}\mid\mu_{1}\right)\,;

(53)

(2)

if
\bar{m}_{1}<\bar{m}_{2}
(as
\bar{m}_{1}\neq 0
) the partial derivative
\partial_{m_{1}}P
must vanish when evaluated in
(\bar{q},{\bf{\bar{m}}})
, and this implies
H(G^{(1)}\mid\mu_{1})=\gamma_{1}\Theta\log 2
.

Therefore, since
\nu\in\mathcal{K}\Rightarrow H(\nu^{(1)}\mid\mu_{1})\leq\gamma_{1}\Theta\log 2
, it holds

H(G^{(1)}\mid\mu_{1})-H(\nu^{(1)}\mid\mu_{1})\geq 0,

and hence

\frac{1}{\bar{m}_{1}}\left[H(G^{(1)}\mid\mu_{1})-H(\nu^{(1)}\mid\mu_{1})\right]\geq\frac{1}{\bar{m}_{2}}\left[H(G^{(1)}\mid\mu_{1})-H(\nu^{(1)}\mid\mu_{1})\right].

(54)

It follows from (
53
), (
54
) that in both cases it holds

\displaystyle D_{1}

\displaystyle\geq\int P_{1}\,dG^{(1)}-\frac{1}{\bar{m}_{1}}H(G^{(1)}\mid\mu_{1})-\int P_{1}\,d\nu^{(1)}+\frac{1}{\bar{m}_{1}}H(\nu^{(1)}\mid\mu_{1}).

(55)

Therefore, using the standard definition of relative entropy (
14
) to express
H(G^{(1)}\mid\mu_{1})
, we get

\displaystyle D_{1}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\bar{m}_{1}}\log\int\exp{\left(m_{1}P_{1}\right)}d\mu_{1}-\int P_{1}\,d\nu^{(1)}+\frac{1}{\bar{m}_{1}}H(\nu^{(1)}\mid\mu_{1})

(56)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\bar{m}_{1}}\left\{H\left(\nu^{(1)}\mid\mu_{1}\right)-\left[\int\bar{m}_{1}P_{1}d\nu^{(1)}-\log\int\exp{\left(m_{1}P_{1}\right)}d\mu_{1}\right]\right\}\geq 0

where in the last step we used the Donsker and Varadhan variational formula for relative entropies

H(\nu\mid\mu)=\sup_{f\in C_{b}}\{\int fd\nu-\log\int e^{f}d\mu\}

(see
[
14
,
22
]
for details) and the fact that the hypotesis
\varphi,f\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
imply that
P_{j}\in C_{b}(S^{j})
for every
j\in\{1,\cdots,n\}
.

Now, we only need to prove that
D_{k}\geq D_{k-1}
for any
k\in\{1,\cdots,n\}
.

Notice that if
K\in\{1,\cdots,n\}
is the total number of different values assumed by the (positive and non-decreasing w.r.t the indices) entries of
{\bar{\bf m}}=(\bar{m}_{1},\dots,\bar{m}_{n})
; setting
\text{max}_{0}:=0
, we can define for every
k\in\{1,\cdots,K\}

\text{max}_{k}:=\max\{j:\quad\text{max}_{k-1}+1\leq j\leq n,\,\,\,\bar{m}_{j}=\bar{m}_{\text{max}_{k-1}+1}\}.

(57)

To wit: for any
k\in\{1,\cdots,K\}
the index
\text{max}_{k}
is the last one for which the corresponding entry in
{\bf\bar{m}}
assumes the
k
-th higher value in the whole vector: we will refer to the largest sections of the vector
{\bf\bar{m}}
where all entries are equal as
blocks
. By construction,
j=\text{max}_{k}
for some
k
iff
\bar{m}_{j}<\bar{m}_{j+1}
.

As in the case
1)
above, if
\bar{m}_{n}=1
then

D_{n}\geq\boldsymbol{\Phi}[G]-\frac{1}{\bar{m}_{n}}H(G\mid\mu)-\boldsymbol{\Phi}[\nu]+\frac{1}{\bar{m}_{n}}H(\nu\mid\mu);\\

(58)

holds with equality. If
\bar{m}_{n}<1
then, considering the real function
m\in(0,\bar{m}_{\text{max}_{1}+1}]\to g_{\text{max}_{1}}(m)
defined by (
48
) for
j\equiv\text{max}_{1}
, to wit

g_{\text{max}_{1}}(m)=\begin{cases}P(\bar{q},m,\dots,m)&\text{if}\,\,\max_{1}=n\,,\\
P(\bar{q},m,\dots,m,\bar{m}_{\text{max}_{1}+1},\dots,\bar{m}_{n})&\text{if}\,\,\max_{1}<n\,;\end{cases}

we notice that it must have a minimum point in
m=\bar{m}_{1}\in(0,\bar{m}_{max_{1}+1})
, specifically for
m\neq\bar{m}_{\text{max}_{1}+1}
(recall that we have set
\bar{m}_{n+1}\equiv 1
).
This implies that
\bar{m}_{1}
must be a critical point of
g_{\text{max}_{1}}
, i.e. that
\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\text{max}_{1}}\partial_{m_{k}}P(\bar{q},{\bf\bar{m}})=0
, which implies

H(G^{(\text{max}_{1})}\mid\mu^{(\text{max}_{1})})=\Theta\log 2\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\text{max}_{1}}\gamma_{k}.

(59)

If now
K=1
, i.e. if
{\bf m}
is made of one block and
\text{max}_{1}=n
, the latter is exactly
H(G\mid\mu)=\Theta\log 2
. If instead
K\geq 2
, i.e. if
\bar{\bf m}
is made of at least two blocks and
\text{max}_{1}<n
, we easily get, as above, that
\sum_{k=\text{max}_{1}+1}^{\text{max}_{2}}\partial_{m_{k}}P(\bar{q},\bar{\bf m})=0
, which implies

\sum_{k=\text{max}_{1}+1}^{\text{max}_{2}}{\sf{E}}^{\,\bar{q},{\bf\bar{m}}}_{k-1}(H^{\,\bar{q},{\bf\bar{m}}}_{k})=\Theta\log 2\sum_{k=\text{max}_{1}+1}^{\text{max}_{2}}\gamma_{k}.

(60)

Through the chain-rule, (
59
) and (
60
), we get

\displaystyle H(G\mid\mu)=

\displaystyle H(G^{(\text{max}_{1})}\mid\mu^{(\text{max}_{1})})+\sum_{k=\text{max}_{1}+1}^{n}{\sf{E}}^{\,\bar{q},{\bf\bar{m}}}_{k-1}(H^{\,\bar{q},{\bf\bar{m}}}_{k})

(61)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Theta\log 2\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\text{max}_{1}}\gamma_{k}+\Theta\log 2\sum\limits_{k=\text{max}_{1}+1}^{\text{max}_{2}}\gamma_{k}+\sum_{k=\text{max}_{2}+1}^{n}{\sf{E}}^{\,\bar{q},{\bf\bar{m}}}_{k-1}(H^{\,\bar{q},{\bf\bar{m}}}_{k}).

It should now be clear to the reader that by repeating the same reasoning
K
times, since
\text{max}_{K}\equiv n
, we arrive at

\displaystyle H(G\mid\mu)=

\displaystyle\Theta\log 2\sum\limits_{j=0}^{K}\sum\limits_{k=\text{max}_{j}+1}^{\text{max}_{j+1}}\gamma_{k}=\Theta\log 2.

(62)

Specifically, as before,
the inequality (
58
) holds also if
\bar{m}_{n}<1
. From (
50
) and some elementary manipulations one finds

\displaystyle H(G\mid\mu)

\displaystyle=H(G^{(n-1)}\mid\mu^{(n-1)})+{\sf{E}}^{\,\bar{q},{\bf\bar{m}}}_{n-1}(H^{\,\bar{q},{\bf\bar{m}}}_{n})

(63)

\displaystyle=H(G^{(n-1)}\mid\mu^{(n-1)})+\bar{m}_{n}\int P_{n}\,dG-\bar{m}_{n}\int P_{n-1}\,dG^{(n-1)}

\displaystyle=H(G^{(n-1)}\mid\mu^{(n-1)})+\bar{m}_{n}\Phi^{\prime}(\bar{q})\int\varphi\,dG+\bar{m}_{n}\int f\,dG-\bar{m}_{n}\int P_{n-1}\,dG^{(n-1)}.

Through the latter, we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\bar{m}_{n}}H(G\mid\mu)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\bar{m}_{n}}H(G^{(n-1)}\mid\mu^{(n-1)})+\Phi^{\prime}(\bar{q})\int\varphi dG+\int fdG-\int P_{n-1}dG^{(n-1)}

(64)

Plugging the latter into (
58
), and recalling the explicit expression (
17
) for
\boldsymbol{\Phi}
, we find

\displaystyle D_{n}

\displaystyle\geq\Phi\left(\int\varphi dG\right)-\Phi\left(\int\varphi d\nu\right)-\Phi^{\prime}(\bar{q})\int\varphi\,dG-\int fd\nu\,+

(65)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\bar{m}_{n}}H(\nu\mid\mu)-\frac{1}{\bar{m}_{n}}H(G^{(n-1)}\mid\mu^{(n-1)})+\int P_{n-1}\,dG^{(n-1)}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\bar{m}_{n}}H(\nu\mid\mu)-\Phi^{\prime}(\bar{q})\int\varphi d\nu-\int fd\nu-\frac{1}{\bar{m}_{n}}H(G^{(n-1)}\mid\mu^{(n-1)})+\int P_{n-1}\,dG^{(n-1)}

the second inequality by concavity of
\Phi
, using that

.

Visiting early at prime times
Tony Haddad, Sun-Kai Leung, Cihan Sabuncu

Abstract
. Given an integer m ⩾ 2 m\geqslant 2 and a sufficiently large q q , we prove that there always exists an arithmetic progression with common difference q q for which the m m -th least prime in such progression is ≪ q ​ exp ⁡ ( 3.8075 ​ m ) \ll q\exp(3.8075m) . This is obtained using a minorant for the indicator of primes in the Maynard–Tao sieve, in parallel with Baker–Irving and Stadlmann’s work on bounded gaps between primes. The dependence on q q is best possible. Furthermore, we generalize our result to dynamical systems. The quality of the result depends crucially on the first return time, which we illustrate in the case of Diophantine approximation.

Introduction

Analytic Number Theory has witnessed many breakthroughs on fundamental problems in recent years, in particular the bounded gaps between primes, thanks to Zhang
[
22
]
, Maynard
[
14
]
and Tao (see also
[
17
]
), building upon the celebrated work of Goldston–Pintz–Yıldırım
[
6
]
. Maynard
[
15
]
further proved that any subset of primes which is “well distributed” in arithmetic progressions contains many primes that are close together.

In particular, for primes in a fixed progression, the best known result is due to Baker and Zhao
[
2
]
, who proved the following. Given
m\geqslant 1
, let
x
be a sufficiently large real number,
q=x^{\theta}
a suitably “powerful” modulus with
0<\theta\leqslant 5/12-\eta
for a fixed
\eta>0
and
\prod_{p|q}p\leqslant(\log x)^{C}
for a fixed
C>0
, and
(a,q)=1.
Then there exist primes
p_{1}<\cdots<p_{m}
in
(x/2,x]
such that
p_{i}\equiv a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)
for
1\leqslant i\leqslant m
and

\displaystyle p_{m}-p_{1}\leqslant q\exp(cm)

for some constant
c=c(\theta)>0.

Apart from progressions, there are also infinitely many
bounded gaps between primes in a fixed “Chebotarev set”
[
21
]
, a suitable “Sato–Tate interval”
[
5
]
or a “nil-Bohr set”
[
19
]
.

To motivate our work, we begin by asking: how big is the first prime in a given arithmetic progression? Let
p(q,a)
denote the least prime in the arithmetic progression
a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q).
Then recall Linnik’s celebrated theorem that
p(q,a)\ll q^{L}
for some absolute constant
L>0.
If
p_{m}(q,a)
is now the
m
th
prime in the arithmetic progression
a\pmod{q}
, then a quantitative version of Linnik’s theorem
[
8
, Corollary 18.8]
implies

\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)\\
(a,q)=1\end{subarray}}p_{m}(q,a)\ll_{m}q^{L},

for some
L>0
.

Assuming a uniform variant of the Hardy–Littlewood prime
k
-tuple conjecture, the second author
[
12
]
showed that as soon as
f(q)\to\infty
with
q\to\infty,
the least prime
p(q,a)
is
\leqslant f(q)\varphi(q)\log q
for almost all
a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)
. In fact,
the least prime in arithmetic progressions to a common large modulus has an exponential limiting distribution. More precisely, if a reduced residue
a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)
is chosen uniformly at random, then we have the convergence in distribution

\displaystyle\frac{p(q,a)}{\varphi(q)\log q}\xrightarrow{d}\operatorname{Exp}(1)\qquad\text{as $q\to\infty.$ }

Given
m\geqslant 1
and
(a,q)=1,
we are also interested in the
m
-th least prime
p\equiv a\pmod{q},
which we denote as
p_{m}(q,a).
For any residue class containing fewer than
m
primes, we set
p_{m}(q,a)=\infty
. As
a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)
varies uniformly with
q\to\infty
, the second author
[
11
]
further showed conditionally that the normalized primes in the progression,

\displaystyle\frac{p_{1}(q,a)}{\varphi(q)\log q}<\frac{p_{2}(q,a)}{\varphi(q)\log q}<\cdots,

are located on the positive real number line
(0,\infty)
as if they were randomly positioned points, in the sense of a Poisson point process.

In this paper, we study
\min_{a\pmod{q}}p_{m}(q,a)
. By the pigeonhole principle, one can immediately show that
\min_{a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)}p_{m}(q,a)\ll_{m}\varphi(q)\log q
. Applying a variant of the Maynard–Tao sieve weights, we improve this bound.

Theorem 1.1
.

For
m\geqslant 2
and
q
sufficiently large in terms of
m
, we have

\displaystyle\min_{a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)}p_{m}(q,a)\ll q\cdot\exp(3.8075m).

Remark 1.1
.

A similar result can be obtained from the setting of Maynard
[
15
, Theorem 3.1]
, but this natural statement does not seem to have been observed before in the literature. Moreover, Maynard’s theorem gives the upper bound
O(qe^{cm})
for some large constant
c
. If we go into the proof and optimise the constant for our special case, we may take any
c>4
. As a by-product of our result, we see that we can take the exponent
c=3.8075
like in Stadlmann
[
20
]
.

When keeping
m
fixed and letting
q
grow, the order of magnitude is optimal. Indeed, in any arithmetic progression, the
m
-th prime is at least the
m
-th positive integer in that progression. Thus
\min_{a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ q)}p_{m}(q,a)>(m-1)q
.

The dependence on
m
in Theorem
1.1
could be improved under the following variant of the Hardy–Littlewood conjecture: For all fixed
\varepsilon>0
and fixed admissible
m
-tuple of minimal diameter
\mathcal{H}=\{0=h_{1}<\cdots<h_{m}\}
, we expect that for sufficiently large
q
, we must have

\#\Big\{\varepsilon q<a<2\varepsilon q:a+qh_{i}\text{ is prime for all $i$}\Big\}\sim\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{H},q)\frac{\varepsilon q}{(\log q)^{m}}

with

\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{H},q)=\left(\frac{q}{\varphi(q)}\right)^{m-1}\prod_{p\nmid q}\left(1-\frac{\#\{h_{i}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ p)\}}{p}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-m}.

This variant of the Hardy–Littlewood conjecture is consistent with the Cramér–Granville model (see
[
7
]
). With this conjecture, we directly obtain

\min_{a\pmod{q}}p_{m}(q,a)<(H(m)+o(1))q

as we let
q\to\infty
, with
H(k)
being the diameter of the narrowest admissible
k
-tuple. Since
H(m)\asymp m\log m
, we would have

(1.1)

\min_{a\pmod{q}}p_{m}(q,a)\ll qm\log m,

conditional on the variant of the Hardy-Littlewood conjecture.

The bound (
1.1
) would be sharp: Consider
a,q
satisfying
(a,q)=1
and a prime modulus
q>m
. If
(a+\ell_{i}q)_{i=1}^{m}
are the first
m
primes in the arithmetic progression

, then the set
\{\ell_{2},\ldots,\ell_{m}\}
is admissible. Therefore, taking the minimum over all integers
a
coprime to
q
, we have

\min_{a\pmod{q}}p_{m}(q,a)>(H(m-1)+1)q.

Recurrence is a central theme in ergodic theory, i.e., how points in dynamical systems return close to themselves under iteration. In 1890, Poincaré proved a simple yet far-reaching recurrence theorem in the context of the “three-body” problem of planetary orbits (see
[
4
, p. 21]
). For our purposes, here we state a simple version (see
[
4
, Exercise 2.2.1]
for instance).

Lemma 1.1

(Poincaré recurrence theorem)
.

Given a measure-preserving system
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T)
with
\mu(X)=1
, let
A\in\mathcal{B}

with
\mu(A)>0
. Then there exists an integer
1\leqslant n\leqslant\lfloor\mu(A)^{-1}\rfloor
such that

\displaystyle\mu(A\cap T^{-n}A)>0.

Indeed, if
\mu(A\cap T^{-n}A)=0
for
1\leqslant n\leqslant N:=\lfloor\mu(A)^{-1}\rfloor
, then
A,T^{-1}A,\ldots,T^{-N}A
are pairwise disjoint up to null sets, contradicting
(N+1)\mu(A)>1
. A famous example is Dirichlet’s approximation theorem, corresponding to circle rotation. Inspired by the recurrence theorem, we shall visit early at prime times in
metric-measure-preserving systems
defined as follows.

Definition 1.1

(Metric-measure-preserving system)
.

A
metric-measure-preserving system
is a quintuple
(X,d,\mathcal{B},\mu,T)
where
(X,d)
is a metric space and
(X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T)
is a measure-preserving system satisfying the following compatibility conditions:

•

the Borel
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{B}
is induced by the metric
d;

•

the probability measure
\mu
is
pseudo-doubling
, i.e., there exist constants
\lambda\geqslant 1
and
r_{0}>0
such that

\displaystyle\mu(B(x;2r))\leqslant\lambda\cdot\mu(B(x;r))

for any
x\in X
and
0<r<r_{0}
, where
B(x;r):=\{y\in X\,:\,d(y,x)<r\}
;

•

the measure-preserving map
T
is an isometry.

Let
p_{m}^{T}(x_{0},x;\varepsilon)
denote the
m
-th least prime
p
for which
T^{p}x_{0}\in B(x;\varepsilon).
If fewer than
m
primes satisfy this condition, we set
p_{m}^{T}(x_{0},x;\varepsilon)=\infty
. Then, with the definition in hand, we state the dynamical generalization of Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
m\geqslant 2
and
(X,d,\mathcal{B},\mu,T)
be a metric-measure-preserving system. Then for
0<\varepsilon<r_{0}
sufficiently small (in terms of
m
) and
x_{0}\in\operatorname{supp}\mu
, we have

\displaystyle\min_{x\in X}\,p_{m}^{T}(x_{0},x;\varepsilon)\ll_{m,\lambda}{\mu(B(x_{0};\varepsilon))}^{-1}.

Notation.
Throughout the paper, we use the standard big
O

and the Hardy notation
\asymp,
where the implied constants depend only on the subscripted parameters.

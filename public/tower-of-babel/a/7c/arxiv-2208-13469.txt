Counting crucial permutations with respect to monotone patterns
Yunseo Choi

Abstract
. Recently, Avgustinovich, Kitaev, and Taranenko defined five types of ( k , ℓ ) − (k,\ell)- crucial permutations, which are maximal permutations that do not contain an increasing subsequence of length k k or a decreasing subsequence of length ℓ \ell . Further, Avgustinovich, Kitaev, and Taranenko began the enumeration of the ( k , ℓ ) − (k,\ell)- crucial permutations of the minimal length and the next minimal length and the ( k , 3 ) − (k,3)- crucial permutations of all lengths for each of the five types of ( k , ℓ ) − (k,\ell)- crucial permutations. In this paper, we complete the enumeration that Avgustinovich, Kitaev, and Taranenko began.



1.
Introduction

The classical Erdős-Szekeres theorem
[
3
]
from 1935 states that a permutation of length
(k-1)(\ell-1)+1
must contain an increasing subsequence of length
k
or a decreasing subsequence of length
\ell
. Accordingly, the permutations of length
(k-1)(\ell-1)
that do not contain an increasing subsequence of length
k
or a decreasing subsequence of length
\ell
are called the
(k,\ell)
-Erdős-Szekeres extremal permutations

[
12
]
. The number of
(k,\ell)
-Erdős-Szekeres extremal permutations for
k\geq\ell
, which follows from the Robinson-Schensted-Knuth (RSK) correspondence
[
6
,
7
,
8
]
and the hook-length formula
[
5
]
, is given by

(1.1)

\left(\frac{((k-1)(\ell-1))!}{1^{1}2^{2}\cdots(\ell-1)^{\ell-1}\ell^{\ell-1}\cdots(k-1)^{\ell-1}k^{\ell-2}\cdots(k+\ell-3)^{1}}\right)^{2}.

The formula was first presented for
k=\ell
by Stanley
[
11
]
in 1969 and its generality by Avgustinovich, Kitaev, and Taranenko
[
1
]
in 2022.

In a larger context,
crucial objects

[
2
,
4
]
in combinatorics are objects that satisfy a set of conditions but cease to satisfy the conditions when extended in any allowed way. Recently, Avgustinovich, Kitaev, and Taranenko
[
1
]
defined
(k,\ell)
-right-crucial permutations
to be the permutations that are crucial when the set of conditions is avoid increasing subsequences of length
k
and decreasing subsequences of length
\ell
and the allowed extensions are to the right. More formally, let
\pi=\pi_{1}\pi_{2}\cdots\pi_{n}\in S_{n}
. The
i^{\text{th}}
extension of
\pi
to the right (respectively, left)
is the permutation
f(\pi_{1})f(\pi_{2})\cdots f(\pi_{n})i
(respectively,
if(\pi_{1})f(\pi_{2})\cdots f(\pi_{n})
), where

f(j)=\begin{cases}j&\text{if }j<i\\
j+1&\text{if }j\geq i.\end{cases}

In addition to the
(k,\ell)
-right-crucial permutations, Avgustinovich, Kitaev, and Taranenko
[
1
]
defined four other types of
(k,\ell)
-
crucial permutations. Each type of
(k,\ell)
-crucial permutations shares the set of conditions with the
(k,\ell)
-right-crucial permutations but differ in the extensions that are allowed. The following list showcases the five types of
(k,\ell)
-crucial permutations that Avgustinovich, Kitaev, and Taranenko defined alongside their allowed extensions. The
i^{\text{th}}
extension of
\pi
from above (respectively, below)
is the permutation
\pi_{1}\pi_{2}\cdots\pi_{i-1}(n+1)\pi_{i}\cdots\pi_{n}
(respectively,
(\pi_{1}+1)(\pi_{2}+1)\cdots(\pi_{i-1}+1)1(\pi_{i}+1)\cdots(\pi_{n}+1)
). As noted by Avgustinovich, Kitaev, and Taranenko (see Lemma 11 of
[
1
]
), the list below is the exhaustive list of
(k,\ell)
-crucial permutations up to bijections when the set of extensions is some subset of the extensions to the left, to the right, from above, or from below.

(1)

(k,\ell)
-right-crucial: any extension to the right

(2)

(k,\ell)
-bicrucial: any extension to the right or the left

(3)

(k,\ell)
-top-right-crucial: any extension to the right or from above

(4)

(k,\ell)
-tricrucial: any extension to the right or the left or from above

(5)

(k,\ell)
-quadrocrucial: any extension to the right or the left or from above or below

Now, a rephrasing of the Erdős-Szekeres theorem is that
(k,\ell)
-Erdős-Szekeres extremal permutations are
(k,\ell)
-crucial in any of the five ways. But whereas the
(k,\ell)
-Erdős-Szekeres extremal permutations must have length
(k-1)(\ell-1)
, Avgustinovich, Kitaev, and Taranenko (see Lemma 4 and Theorem 10 of
[
1
]
) proved that the length of a
(k,\ell)
-(right, top-right)-crucial can be as short as
k+\ell-3
. Similarly, they proved that a
(k,\ell)
-(bi, tri, quadro)crucial permutation can be as short as length
k+\ell+\mathrm{min}\{k,\ell\}-5
(see Theorem 9, Theorem 32, and Theorem 36 of
[
1
]
). Following Avgustinovich, Kitaev, and Taranenko, we refer to the
(k,\ell)
-crucial permutations of the shortest (respectively, next shortest) length as the
minimal (respectively, next minimal)
(k,\ell)
-crucial permutations
.

In the spite of
Equation

1.1
, which enumerates the
(k,\ell)
-crucial permutations of the longest length, Avgustinovich, Kitaev, and Taranenko
[
1
]
began the enumeration of special classes of
(k,\ell)
-crucial permutations: namely, the minimal and next minimal
(k,\ell)
-crucial permutations and the
(k,3)
-crucial permutations of all lengths. In this paper, we complete the enumeration that they began for all five types of
(k,\ell)
-crucial permutations. As do Avgustinovich, Kitaev, and Taranenko, we base our enumeration on the RSK correspondence and the hook-length formula.

2.
Preliminaries

A
partition

\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k})
of
n
is a weakly decreasing sequence of non-negative integers such that
\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{k}=n
. A
Young diagram of shape
\lambda
is a left justified diagram of boxes with
\lambda_{i}
boxes in the

row for

. A
skew Young diagram of shape
\lambda
is a diagram of boxes with
\lambda_{i}
boxes in the
i^{\text{th}}
row that is not necessarily left and top justified. We say that
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\cdots+\lambda_{k}=n
is the
size
of a (skew) Young diagram. A (skew) Young diagram is
filled
if each box contains exactly one number, and the numbers that appear in the boxes are distinct. A
(skew) standard Young tableau of shape
\lambda
is a (skew) Young diagram of shape
\lambda
filled with numbers
1,2,\ldots,n
such that every row is increasing from the left to right and every column is increasing from the top to bottom. For a (skew) standard Young tableau
T
, let
t_{i,j}
(respectively,
b_{i,j}
) be the entry (respectively, the box) at the
i^{\text{th}}
row and the
j^{\text{th}}
column of
T
. We define the
complement

T^{c}
of a (skew) standard Young tableau
T
to be the (skew) Young diagram that shares the shape with
T
and satisfies
t^{c}_{i,j}=n+1-t_{i,j}
for all entries
t^{c}_{i,j}
in
T^{c}
. We define the
transpose

T^{t}
of a (skew) standard Young tableau
T
to be the (skew) Young diagram that shares the shape with
T
after a reflection across its main diagonal and satisfies
t^{t}_{i,j}=t_{j,i}
for all entries
t^{t}_{i,j}
in
T^{t}
.

The Robinson-Schensted-Knuth (RSK) correspondence
[
6
,
7
,
8
]
is a bijection between the permutations of length

and the pairs of standard Young tableaux
(P,Q)
that have the same shape
\lambda
. The bijection from

to
(P,Q)
sends
\pi
to
(P_{n}(\pi),Q_{n}(\pi))
. The construction of
P_{n}(\pi)
builds off of
row insertion
T\leftarrow i
for a tableau
T
and an element

. Let
T_{1}
be the first row of

and
T_{2}
be the filled Young diagram of

with
T_{1}
removed. The procedure
T\leftarrow i
is defined recursively as follows.

(1)

If
T
is
\emptyset
or the largest entry in
T_{1}
is less than
i
, then insert
i
at the right end of
T_{1}
.

(2)

Otherwise, let
j
be the smallest element in
T_{1}
that is greater than
i
. Replace
j
with
i
in
T_{1}
, and let the remaining rows of be given by
T_{2}\leftarrow j
.

P_{i}(\pi)
is now defined as follows. For
\pi=\pi_{1}\pi_{2}\cdots\pi_{n}\in S_{n}
, we let

P_{i}(\pi)=(\cdots((\emptyset\leftarrow\pi_{1})\leftarrow\pi_{2})\cdots\leftarrow\pi_{i-1})\leftarrow\pi_{i}.

Next, we define
Q_{n}(\pi)
. Let
Q_{0}=\emptyset
, and recursively let
Q_{i}(\pi)
be the tableau obtained by inserting

to
Q_{i-1}(\pi)
such that
Q_{i}(\pi)
and
P_{i}(\pi)
have the same shape and the position of the entries of
Q_{i-1}(\pi)

to
Q_{i}(\pi)
.

For more details on the RSK correspondence, see, for example,
[

]
. As we proceed, let
p_{i,j}
(respectively,
q_{i,j}
) be the entry at the
i^{\text{th}}
row and the
j^{\text{th}}
column of
P_{n}(\pi)
(respectively,
Q_{n}(\pi)
). Furthermore, as usual, we let
\delta_{a,b}
be the Kronecker delta, where

\delta_{a,b}:=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }a=b\text{ and}\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

In addition, we let
\binom{a}{b_{1},b_{2},\cdots,b_{m}}
denote the multinomial coefficient

\binom{a}{b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{m}}:=\frac{a!}{b_{1}!b_{2}!\ldots b_{m}!}.

Q_{n}(\pi)
are that they are standard Young tableaux of shape
(k-1,k-1)
. Therefore, by the hook-length formula, there are

(5.17)

\frac{1}{k}{2k-2\choose k-1}

possible fillings for each of
P_{n}(\pi)
and
Q_{n}(\pi)
.

By the RSK correspondence, we finish by squaring
Equation

5.17
.
∎

That all
(k,\ell)
-tricrucial permutations for
k\geq\ell
must have length at least
k+2\ell-5
and at most
(k-1)(\ell-1)-1
follows from Theorem 32 of Avgustinovich, Kitaev, and Taranenko
[
1
]
. Therefore,
20
gives a complete enumeration of
(k,3)
-tricrucial permutations.

6.
Quadrocrucial Permutations

Our next result is that we enumerate the next minimal
(k,\ell)
-quadrocrucial permutations. We begin by citing the characterization of
(P_{n}(\pi),Q_{n}(\pi))
when
\pi
is quadrocrucial as presented by Avgustinovich, Kitaev, and Taranenko
[
1
]
.

Theorem 21

(Avgustinovich, Kitaev, and Taranenko
[
1
]
)
.

The permutation
\pi\in S_{n}
is
(k,\ell)
-quadrocrucial if and only if:

(1)

the number of columns in
P_{n}(\pi)
(and
Q_{n}(\pi)
) is
k-1
;

(2)

the number of rows in
P_{n}(\pi)
(and
Q_{n}(\pi)
) is
\ell-1
;

(3)

P_{n}(\pi)
contains an increasing sequence of elements
p_{1,k-1},p_{2,j_{2}},\ldots,p_{\ell-1,j_{\ell-1}}
for some
j_{2},\ldots,

j_{\ell-1}
;

(4)

P_{n}(\pi)
contains an increasing sequence of elements
p_{\ell-1,1},p_{i_{2},2},\ldots,p_{i_{k-1},k-1}
for some
i_{2},\ldots,

i_{k-1}
;

(5)

Q_{n}(\pi)
contains an increasing sequence of elements
q_{1,k-1},q_{2,m_{2}},\ldots,q_{\ell-1,m_{\ell-1}}
for some
m_{2},

\ldots,

m_{\ell-1}
;

(6)

Q_{n}(\pi)
contains an increasing sequence of elements
q_{\ell-1,1},q_{r_{2},2},\ldots,q_{r_{k-1},k-1}
for some
r_{2},\ldots,

r_{k-1}
.

6.1.
Next minimal quadrocrucial permutations

The enumeration of next minimal
(k,\ell)
-quadrocrucial permutations follows from
5
and
7
. As in the proof of
8
, we deal with the cases
(k,\ell)\in\{(4,3),(4,4)\}
separately, because unlike
(k,\ell)
with larger
k
for which
k=\ell
or
k=\ell+1
, the shape of
P_{n}(\pi)
(and
Q_{n}(\pi)
) are fixed when
(k,\ell)\in\{(4,3),(4,4)\}
.

Theorem 22
.

Suppose that
k\geq\ell
. The number of next minimal
(4,3)
-quadrocrucial permutations is
25
, the number of next minimal
(4,4)
-quadrocrucial permutations is
756
, and the number of next minimal
(k,\ell)
-quadrocrucial permutations for

and

is

(1+\delta_{k,\ell})(k+2\ell-7)^{2}+\delta_{k-1,\ell}.

Proof.

By Theorem 36 of Avgustinovich, Kitaev, and Taranenko
[
1
]
, the length of a minimal
(k,\ell)
-quadrocrucial permutation is
k+2\ell-5
. Therefore, the length of a next minimal
(k,\ell)
-quadrocrucial permutations is
k+2\ell-4
. By conditions
1
and
2
of
21
,
P_{n}(\pi)
must have
k-1
columns and
\ell-1
rows.

First, suppose that
p_{\ell-1,1}<p_{1,k-1}
. Then, to satisfy condition 3 of
21
, column
2
of
P_{n}(\pi)
must have
\ell-1
boxes. Thus, for

to have
k-1
boxes in row
1
,
\ell-1
boxes in columns
1
and
2
, and
k+2\ell-4
boxes total,
P_{n}(\pi)
must have the shape
(k-1,3,\underbrace{2,\ldots,2}_{\ell-3})
. Now, by
5
, there are

(6.1)

k+2\ell-7

ways to fill
P_{n}(\pi)
such that it satisfies conditions
3
and
4
of
21
. The same applies to
Q_{n}(\pi)
.

By the RSK correspondence, we finish this case by squaring
Equation

6.1
.

Now, suppose that
p_{1,k-1}<p_{\ell-1,1}
. Then because all entries in row
1
are less than
p_{\ell-1,1}
, for condition
4
of
21
to hold, row
2
must have all
k-1
boxes. Therefore, the minimum number of boxes in
P_{n}(\pi)
is
2k+\ell-5
. Since
\pi
has
k+2\ell-4
, it must be that
k\in\{\ell,\ell+1\}
.

Suppose that
k=\ell
. Then, for
P_{n}(\pi)
to have
k-1
boxes in rows
1
and
2
,
k-1
boxes in column
1

3k-4
boxes total,

must have the shape
(k-1,k-1,2,\underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{k-4})

6

(6.2)

3k-7

ways of filling

.

By the RSK correspondence, we finish this case by squaring
Equation

6.2
.

Now, if

7
, there is exactly one way to fill

. The same applies to
Q_{n}(\pi)
.

By the RSK correspondence, there is one additional

-quadrocrucial permutation in this case.

To complete the enumeration of the next minimal

6.2.
(k,3)
-quadrocrucial permutations

Our final result is that we count the
(k,3)
-quadrocrucial permutations for
k\geq 3
. The enumeration follows from the hook-length formula,
9
, and
23
.

Theorem 23
.

Let
k\geq 3
. The number of
(k,3)
-quadrocrucial permutations of length
n
for
k+1\leq n\leq 2k-3
is

\left(\frac{2k-n}{n}{n\choose k}-\frac{2k-n-1}{n-1}{n-1\choose k-1}\right)^{2}.

For
n=2(k-1)
, it is

\left(\frac{1}{k}{2k-2\choose k-1}\right)^{2}.

Proof.

Since
\ell=3
, the third condition of
21
is equivalent to
p_{1,k-1}<p_{2,n-k+1}
. Since the rows of
P_{n}(\pi)
increase, either
p_{1,k-1}=n
or
p_{2,n-k+1}=n
. Therefore, the third condition of
21
is equivalent to
p_{2,n-k+1}=n
. Similarly, the fifth condition of
21
is equivalent to
q_{2,n-k+1}=n
.

Now, let
k+1\leq n\leq 2k-3
. The number of possible fillings for each of
P_{n}(\pi)
and
Q_{n}(\pi)
is given by
19
to be

(6.3)

\frac{2k-n}{n}{n\choose k}-\frac{2k-n-1}{n-1}{n-1\choose k-1}.

By the RSK correspondence, we finish by squaring
Equation

6.3
.

Now, suppose that
n=2(k-1)
. Then the third condition of
11
is always met, because
p_{1,k-1}<p_{2,k-1}=n
. Likewise, the fifth condition is always met. Similarly, the fourth condition of
11
is also always met, because
p_{2,1}<p_{2,2}<\cdots<p_{2,k-1}
. Likewise, the sixth condition is always met. Thus, the only restrictions on
P_{n}(\pi)
and
Q_{n}(\pi)
are that they are standard Young tableaux of shape
(k-1,k-1)
. Therefore, by the hook-length formula, there are

(6.4)

\frac{1}{k}{2k-2\choose k-1}

possible fillings for each of
P_{n}(\pi)
and
Q_{n}(\pi)
.
By the RSK correspondence, we finish by squaring
Equation

6.4
.
∎

That all
(k,\ell)
-quadrocrucial permutations for
k\geq\ell
must have length at least

and at most

Abstract
. This paper is concerned with the L 1 L_{1} in time B q , 1 s B^{s}_{q,1} in space maximal regularity for the Stokes equations obtained by linearization procedure of the Navier-Stokes equations describing the viscous compressible fluid motion. Our main tool of deriving this maximal regularity is based on the spectral analysis of the corresponding resolvent problem for the Stokes operators. An applications of our theorem is to prove the local well-posedness of the Navier-Stokes equations with non-slip boundary conditions in uniform C 3 C^{3} domains, whose boundary is compact. This is an extension of results due to Danchin-Tolksdorf [ 10 ] , where the boundedness of the domain is assumed. In this paper, we assume that the boundary of the domain is compact, namely, not only bounded domains but also exterior domains are considered. Our approach of this paper is based on the spectral analysis of Lamé equations, while the method in [ 10 ] is an extension of a result due to Da Prato-Grisvard [ 11 ] . Our method developed in this paper has applications to extensive system of parabolic and hyperbolic-parabolic equations with non-homogeneous boundary conditions.

1.4.
Why is the
L_{1}
approach important ?

If we use Lagrange transformation
following Ströhmer
[
47
,
48
]
, then we have to require that
the Jacobian of Lagrange transformation

I+\int_{0}^{t}\nabla{\mathbb{u}}(y,\tau)\,d\tau
is invertible,
where
{\mathbb{u}}(y,\tau)
stands for the velocity field of a fluid particle at time
t

which was located in
y
at initial time
t=0
.
Hence, it is always crucial to get a control of

\int_{0}^{t}\nabla{\mathbb{u}}(y,\tau)\,d\tau
in a suitable norm.
In particular, it is necessary to
find a small constant
c>0
such that (
1.5
) holds,
which ensures that Lagrangian transformation is invertible.

Moreover, in view of time trace, if
the velocity field
{\mathbb{u}}
belongs to the maximal regularity class

L_{p}((0,T),W^{2}_{q}(\Omega)^{N})\cap W^{1}_{p}((0,T),L_{q}(\Omega)^{N})
, then

{\mathbb{u}}|_{t=0}\in B^{2(1-1/p)}_{q,p}(\Omega)^{N}
. Thus,
p=1
gives the minimal
regularity for the initial data.
From these points of view, it is worth while investigating
the
L_{1}
maximal regularity theorem
with
B^{s}_{q,1}(\Omega)
in space with
-1+N/q\leq s<1/q
and

N-1<q<2N
. These constraints for
q
and
s
are unavoidable
and essentially depends on estimates of the product of
functions using Besov norms obtained by Abidi and Paicu
[
1
]
. In fact,
if
\eta_{0}
is a constant, then we can relax the condition that

1<q<\infty
and
-1+1/q<s<1/q
for the linear theory.

2.2.
Definition of Besov spaces and some properties

To define Besov space

Let
\phi\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
with
{\rm supp\,}\phi=\{\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\mid 1/\penalty 2\leq\lvert\xi\rvert\leq 2\}
such that

Then, define

\phi_{k}:={\mathcal{F}}^{-1}_{\xi}[\phi(2^{-k}\xi)]\quad(k\in{\mathbb{Z}}),\qquad\psi=1-\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\phi(2^{-k}\xi).

(2.1)

For
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
and

we denote

\lVert f\rVert_{B^{s}_{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}:=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\lVert\psi*f\rVert_{L_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}+\bigg(\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\Big(2^{sk}\lVert\phi_{k}*f\rVert_{L_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}\Big)^{q}\bigg)^{1/\penalty q}&\quad&\text{if $1\leq q<\infty$},\\
&\lVert\psi*f\rVert_{L_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}+\sup_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\Big(2^{sk}\lVert\phi_{k}*f\rVert_{L_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}\Big)&\quad&\text{if $q=\infty$}.\end{aligned}\right.

(2.2)

Here,
f\,*\,g
means the convolution between
f
and
g
.
Then Besov spaces

are defined as the sets of all

such that
\lVert f\rVert_{B^{s}_{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}<\infty
.
In particular,

B^{s}_{q,\infty-}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})=\{g\in B^{s}_{q,\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})\mid\lim_{k\to\infty}2^{sk}\|\phi_{k}*f\|_{L_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}=0\}.

When

,

For any domain

2.4.
Fourier multiplier theorems in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}

To estimate solution formulas in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
, we use the following
Fourier multiplier theorem of Mihlin - Hörmander type
[
33
,
19
]
.
Let

m(\xi)
be a

function such that for any multi-index
\kappa\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{N}
there exists a constant

such that

|D_{\xi}^{\kappa}m(\xi)|\leq C_{\alpha}|\xi|^{-|\kappa|}.

We call

a multiplier symbol of order
0
. Set

[m]=\max_{|\kappa|\leq N}C_{\kappa}
. For any multiplier of order
0
,
we define an operator
T_{m}
by

T_{m}f={\mathcal{F}}^{-1}[m{\mathcal{F}}[f]].

We call
T_{m}
the Fourier multiplier with symbol
m
. We know the following
Fourier multiplier theorem of
Mihlin-Hörmander type.

Proposition 9
.

Let
1<q<\infty
,
1\leq r\leq\infty
and
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
Let
T_{m}
be a Fourier multiplier with symbol
m
. Then, for any

f\in B^{s}_{q,r}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, there holds

\|T_{m}f\|_{B^{s}_{q,r}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}\leq C_{q}[m]\|f\|_{B^{s}_{q,r}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}

with some constant
C_{q}
depending solely on
q
.

Proof.

Let
\phi_{k}
and
\psi
be functions introduced in (
2.1
) to define the Littlewood-
Paley decomposition.
Let
m(\xi)
be a multiplier symbol of order
0
, and then

\phi_{k}m
and
\psi m
are also multiplier symbols of order
0
.
By the standard Fourier multiplier
theorem of Mihlin-Hörmander type, we have

\displaystyle\|\phi_{k}*(T_{m}f)\|_{L_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}

\displaystyle\leq C[m]\|\phi_{k}*f\|_{L_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})},

\displaystyle\|\psi*(T_{m}f)\|_{L_{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}

\displaystyle\leq C[m]\|\psi*f\|_{L_{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}.

Thus, by the definitions of the Besov norms (

), we have

\|T_{m}f\|_{B^{s}_{q,r}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}\leq C[m]\|f\|_{B^{s}_{q,r}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}.

This completes the proof of Proposition

∎

By Cauchy theorem in the theory of one complex variable, we have

0=\int_{-R}^{R}e^{(\gamma+i\tau)t}{\mathcal{N}}(\gamma+i\tau){\mathbb{g}}\,d\tau+\int_{C_{R}+\gamma}e^{\lambda t}{\mathcal{N}}(\lambda){\mathbb{g}}\,d\lambda.

(3.4)

Using (
3.1
), we know that

\|{\mathcal{N}}(\lambda)g\|_{B^{s}_{q,1}(D)}\leq C|\lambda|^{-1}\|g\|_{B^{s}_{q,1}(D)}.

Thus, for
t<0
we have

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\int_{C_{R}+\gamma}e^{\lambda t}{\mathcal{N}}(\lambda)g\,d\lambda\Bigr\|_{B^{s}_{q,1}(D)}

\displaystyle\quad\leq Ce^{\gamma t}\int^{\pi/2}_{-\pi/2}e^{tR\cos\theta}|\gamma+Re^{i\theta}|^{-1}\,Rd\theta\|g\|_{B^{s}_{q,1}(D)}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\quad\leq Ce^{\gamma t}\int^{\pi/2}_{0}e^{-|t|R\cos\theta}\,d\theta\|g\|_{B^{s}_{q,1}(D)}.

Since
|^{-|t|R\cos\theta}|\leq 1
, by Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem, we have

\lim_{R\to\infty}\int^{\pi/2}_{0}e^{-|t|R\cos\theta}\,d\theta=\int^{\pi/2}_{0}\lim_{R\to\infty}e^{-|t|R\cos\theta}\,d\theta=0.

Therefore, letting
R\to\infty
in (
3.4
), we have

0=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}e^{(\gamma+i\tau)t}{\mathcal{N}}(\gamma+i\tau)g\,d\tau=N(t)g,

which proves (
3.3
).

We next consider the case where
t>0
. Let
\Gamma_{\pm}
be the contours defined by

\Gamma_{\pm}=\{\lambda=re^{\pm\pi(\pi-\epsilon)}\mid r\in(0,\infty)\}.

We shall show that

\displaystyle\|\bar{\nabla}^{2}N(t)g\|_{B^{s}_{q,1}(D)}

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{\gamma t}t^{-(1-\frac{\sigma}{2})}\|g\|_{B^{s+\sigma}_{q,1}(D)},

(3.5)

\displaystyle\|\bar{\nabla}^{2}N(t)g\|_{B^{s}_{q,1}(D)}

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{\gamma t}t^{-(1+\frac{\sigma}{2})}\|g\|_{B^{s-\sigma}_{q,1}(D)}.

(3.6)

Noticing that
|e^{\lambda t}|=e^{t{\rm Re}\,\lambda}=e^{tr\cos(\pi-\epsilon)}=e^{-tr\cos\epsilon}
and
|\gamma+re^{\pm(\pi-\epsilon}|=(\gamma^{2}-2\gamma\cos\epsilon r+r^{2})^{1/2}\geq(1-\cos\epsilon)^{1/2}r

for
\lambda\in\Gamma_{+}\cup\Gamma_{-}+\gamma

and using (
3.1
), we have

\displaystyle\|\bar{\nabla}^{2}N(t)g\|_{B^{s}_{q,1}(D)}

\displaystyle\leq\Bigl\|\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\Gamma_{+}\cup\Gamma_{-}+\gamma}e^{\lambda t}\,\bar{\nabla}^{2}{\mathcal{N}}(\lambda)g\,d\lambda\Bigr\|_{B^{s}_{q,1}}

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{\gamma t}\int^{\infty}_{0}e^{-tr\cos\epsilon}((1-\cos\epsilon)^{1/2}r)^{-\frac{\sigma}{2}}\,dr\|g\|_{B^{s+\sigma}_{q,1}(D)}

\displaystyle=Ce^{\gamma t}t^{-1+\frac{\sigma}{2}}\int^{\infty}_{0}e^{-\tau\cos\epsilon}((1-\cos\epsilon)^{1/2}\tau)^{-\frac{\sigma}{2}}\,d\tau\|g\|_{B^{s+\sigma}_{q,1}(D)}.

Here, we have used the change of variable:
tr=\tau
.

We use integration by parts to represent

\bar{\nabla}^{2}N(t)g

by

\bar{\nabla}^{2}N(t)g=\frac{-1}{2\pi it}\int_{\Gamma_{+}\cup\Gamma_{-}+\gamma}e^{\lambda t}\partial_{\lambda}(\bar{\nabla}^{2}{\mathcal{N}}(\lambda)g)\,d\lambda

and applying (
3.1
)
we have

\displaystyle\|\bar{\nabla}^{2}N(t)g\|_{B^{s}_{q,1}(D)}

\displaystyle\leq\Bigl\|\frac{1}{2\pi it}\int_{\Gamma_{+}\cup\Gamma_{-}+\gamma}e^{\lambda t}\partial_{\lambda}(\bar{\nabla}^{2}{\mathcal{N}}(\lambda)g)\,d\lambda\Bigr\|_{B^{s}_{q,1}}

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{\gamma t}t^{-1}\int^{\infty}_{0}e^{-tr\cos\epsilon}((1-\cos\epsilon)^{1/2}r)^{-(1-\frac{\sigma}{2})},dr\|g\|_{B^{s-\sigma}_{q,1}(D)}

\displaystyle=Ce^{\gamma t}t^{-1-\frac{\sigma}{2}}\int^{\infty}_{0}e^{-\tau\cos\epsilon}((1-\cos\epsilon)^{1/2}\tau)^{-(1-\frac{\sigma}{2})},d\tau\|g\|_{B^{s-\sigma}_{q,1}(D)}.

Therefore, we have (
3.5
) and (
3.6
).

We shall prove (
3.2
) by using (
3.5
) and (
3.6
).
We write

\displaystyle\int^{\infty}_{0}e^{-\gamma t}\|\bar{\nabla}^{2}N(t)g\|_{B^{s}_{q,1}(D)}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\int^{2^{(j+1)}}_{2^{j}}e^{-\gamma t}\|\bar{\nabla}^{2}N(t)g\|_{B^{s}_{q,1}(D)}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\int^{2^{(j+1)}}_{2^{j}}\sup_{t\in(2^{j},2^{j+1})}(e^{-\gamma t}\|\bar{\nabla}^{2}N(t)g\|_{B^{s}_{q,1}(D)})\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}2^{j}\sup_{t\in(2^{j},2^{j+1})}(e^{-\gamma t}\|\bar{\nabla}^{2}N(t)g\|_{B^{s}_{q,1}(D)}).

Setting
a_{j}=\sup_{t\in(2^{j},2^{j+1})}e^{-\gamma t}\|\bar{\nabla}^{2}N(t)g\|_{B^{s}_{q,1}(D)}
,
we have

\int^{\infty}_{0}e^{-\gamma t}\|\bar{\nabla}^{2}N(t)g\|_{B^{s}_{q,1}(D)}\,{\rm d}t\leq 2((2^{j}a_{j}))_{\ell_{1}}=2((a_{j})_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}})_{\ell^{1}_{1}}.

Here and in the following,

\ell^{s}_{q}
denotes the set of all sequences
(2^{js}a_{j})_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
such that

\displaystyle\|((a_{j})_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}})\|_{\ell^{s}_{q}}

\displaystyle=\Bigl\{\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}(2^{js}|a_{j}|)^{q}\Bigr\}^{1/q}<\infty\quad\text{for $1\leq q<\infty$},

\displaystyle\|((a_{j})_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}})\|_{\ell^{s}_{\infty}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}2^{js}|a_{j}|<\infty\quad\text{for $q=\infty$}.

By (
3.5
) and (
3.6
), we have

\sup_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}2^{j(1-\frac{\sigma}{2})}a_{j}\leq C\|g\|_{B^{s+\sigma}_{q,1}(D)},\quad\sup_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}2^{j(1+\frac{\sigma}{2})}a_{j}\leq C\|g\|_{B^{s-\sigma}_{q,1}(D)}

Namely, we have

\|(a_{j})\|_{\ell_{\infty}^{1-\frac{\sigma}{2}}}\leq C\|g\|_{B^{s+\sigma}_{q,1}(D)},\quad\|(a_{j})\|_{\ell_{\infty}^{1+\frac{\sigma}{2}}}\leq C\|g\|_{B^{s-\sigma}_{q,1}(D)}.

According to
[
3
, 5.6.1.Theorem]
,
we know that
\ell^{1}_{1}=(\ell^{1-\frac{\sigma}{2}}_{\infty},\ell^{1+\frac{\sigma}{2}}_{\infty})_{1/2,1}
,
where

(\cdot,\cdot)_{\theta,q}
denotes the real interpolation functor,
and therefore we have

\int^{\infty}_{0}e^{-\gamma t}\|N(t)g\|_{B^{s+2}_{q,1}(D)}\,{\rm d}t\leq C\|g\|_{(B^{s+\sigma}_{q,1}(D),B^{s-\sigma}_{q,1}(D))_{1/2,1}}=C\|g\|_{B^{s}_{q,1}(D)}

for any
g\in C^{\infty}_{0}(D)
. But,
C^{\infty}_{0}(D)
is dense in

B^{s}_{q,1}(D)
, so the estimate (
3.2
) holds for any

g\in B^{s}_{q,1}(D)
. This completes the proof of
Proposition
13
.
∎

To treat the perturbation term, we introduce one more definition.

Definition 15
.

Let
1<q<\infty
,
\lambda_{0}>0
, and
0<\epsilon<\pi/2
.
Let
X
and
Y
be two Banach spaces and
{\mathcal{M}}(\lambda)\in{\rm Hom}\,(\Sigma_{\epsilon,\lambda_{0}},{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y))
. We say that
{\mathcal{M}}(\lambda)
has a generalized
resolvent properties for
(X,Y)
if there hold

\|\partial_{\lambda}^{\ell}{\mathcal{M}}(\lambda)f\|_{Y}\leq C|\lambda|^{-\ell-1}\|f\|_{X}\quad\text{for $f\in X$ and $\ell=0,1$}.

Remark 16
.

If
{\mathcal{M}}(\lambda)
is a usual resolvent operator

{\mathcal{M}}(\lambda)=(\lambda{\mathbb{I}}-A)^{-1}
of closed linear opreator
A
defined in
dense subspace
D(A)
of
X
for
\lambda\in\Sigma_{\epsilon,\lambda_{0}}
,
then
\partial_{\lambda}(\lambda{\mathbb{I}}-A)^{-1}=-(\lambda{\mathbb{I}}-A)^{-2}
, and so

(\lambda{\mathbb{I}}-A)^{-1}
has generalized resolvent properties for
(X,X)
.

Let
M(t)
be the Laplace invese transform of
{\mathcal{M}}(\lambda)
defined by

M(t)f={\mathcal{L}}^{-1}[{\mathcal{M}}(\lambda)f]=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}e^{(\gamma+i\tau)t}{\mathcal{M}}(\gamma+i\tau)f\,d\tau.

Then, we have the following proposition about the
L_{1}
integrability of

M(t)
.

Proposition 17
.

Let
1<q<\infty
,
\lambda_{0}>0
, and
0<\epsilon<\pi/2
.
Let
X
and
Y
be two Banach spaces and
{\mathcal{M}}(\lambda)\in{\rm Hom}\,(\Sigma_{\epsilon,\lambda_{0}},{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y))
. If
{\mathcal{M}}(\lambda)
has generalized resolvent properties
for
(X,Y)
,
then, for
f\in X
and
\gamma>\lambda_{0}
, it holds that

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}e^{-\gamma t}\|M(t)f\|_{Y}\,dt\leq C\|f\|_{X}.

Proof.

For
\lambda\in\Sigma_{\epsilon,\lambda_{0}}
, we have

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{M}}(\lambda)f\|_{Y}\leq C|\lambda|^{-1}\|f\|_{X}

\displaystyle\leq C\lambda_{0}^{-(1-\frac{\sigma}{2})}|\lambda|^{-\frac{\sigma}{2}}\|f\|_{X},

\displaystyle\|\partial_{\lambda}{\mathcal{M}}(\lambda)f\|_{Y}\leq C|\lambda|^{-2}\|f\|_{X}

\displaystyle\leq C\lambda_{0}^{-(1+\frac{\sigma}{2})}|\lambda|^{-(1-\frac{\sigma}{2})}\|f\|_{X}

for any
\lambda\in\Sigma_{\epsilon,\lambda_{0}}
. Thus, employing the
same argument as in the proof of Proposition
13
, we can prove
Proposition
17
. This completes the proof.
∎

4.
On the spectral analysis of Lamé equations

We shall prove that a solution operator of
Lamé equations (
1.9
) has
(s,\sigma,q,r)
properties. Our proof is
divided into the whole space case, the half-space case, the bent half space case,
and the general domain case, which is the standard procedure.
We start with

4.1.
The whole space case.

In this subsection, we consider the Lamé equations:

\lambda{\mathbb{u}}-\alpha\Delta{\mathbb{u}}-\beta\nabla{\rm div}\,{\mathbb{u}}={\mathbb{g}}\quad\text{in ${\mathbb{R}}^{N}$}

(4.1)

for
\lambda\in\Sigma_{\epsilon}
with
\epsilon\in(0,\pi/2)
.
We shall prove

Theorem 20
.

Let
1<q<\infty
,
1\leq r\leq\infty
,
-1+1/q<s<1/q
,

0<\epsilon<\pi/2
, and
\lambda_{0}>0
. Let
\sigma
be a small positive number
such that
-1+1/q<s-\sigma<s+\sigma<1/q
. Let
\nu\in\{s-\sigma,s,s+\sigma\}
.
Then, there exists an operator
{\mathcal{S}}(\lambda)\in{\rm Hol}\,(\Sigma_{\epsilon},{\mathcal{L}}(B^{\nu}_{q,r}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})^{N},B^{\nu+2}_{q,r}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})^{N}))
having

(s,\sigma,q,r)
properties in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
such that
for any
{\mathbb{g}}\in B^{s}_{q,r}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})^{N}
,
{\mathbb{u}}={\mathcal{S}}(\lambda){\mathbb{g}}
is a unique solution of
equations (
4.1
).

Proof.

Applying the divergence to equations (
4.1
) gives

\lambda{\rm div}\,{\mathbb{u}}-(\alpha+\beta)\Delta{\rm div}\,{\mathbb{u}}={\rm div}\,{\mathbb{g}}\quad\text{in ${\mathbb{R}}^{N}$}.

Using the Fourier transform
{\mathcal{F}}
and its inverse transform
{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}
, we have

{\rm div}\,{\mathbb{u}}={\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\Bigl[\frac{i\xi\cdot{\mathcal{F}}[{\mathbb{g}}](\xi)}{\lambda+(\alpha+\beta)|\xi|^{2}}\Bigr].

Inserting this formula into (
4.1
), we have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}(\lambda){\mathbb{g}}

\displaystyle={\mathcal{F}}^{-1}[\frac{{\mathcal{F}}[{\mathbb{g}}](\xi)+\beta{\mathcal{F}}[{\rm div}\,{\mathbb{u}}](\xi)}{\lambda+\alpha|\xi|^{2}}\Bigr]

(4.2)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\Bigl[\frac{{\mathcal{F}}[{\mathbb{g}}](\xi)}{\lambda+\alpha|\xi|^{2}}\Bigr]+\beta{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\Bigl[\frac{i\xi i\xi\cdot{\mathcal{F}}[{\mathbb{g}}](\xi)}{(\lambda+\alpha|\xi|^{2})(\lambda+(\alpha+\beta)|\xi|^{2})}\Bigr].

As we know well, there exist positive constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
depending on
\alpha
,

\beta
and
\epsilon
such that for any
\lambda\in\Sigma_{\epsilon}
there hold:

\displaystyle c_{1}(|\lambda|^{1/2}+|\xi|)^{2}\leq{\rm Re}\,(\lambda+\alpha|\xi|^{2})

\displaystyle\leq|\lambda+\alpha|\xi|^{2}|\leq c_{2}(|\lambda|^{1/2}+|\xi|)^{2},

\displaystyle c_{1}(|\lambda|^{1/2}+|\xi|)^{2}\leq{\rm Re}\,(\lambda+(\alpha+\beta)|\xi|^{2})

\displaystyle\leq|\lambda+(\alpha+\beta)|\xi|^{2}|\leq c_{2}(|\lambda|^{1/2}+|\xi|)^{2}.

Thus, applying the Fourier multiplier theorem of Mikhlin-Hörmander type, we have

\|(\lambda,\lambda^{1/2}\bar{\nabla},\bar{\nabla}^{2}){\mathcal{S}}(\lambda){\mathbb{g}}\|_{B^{\nu}_{q,r}({\mathbb{R}}^{B})}\leq C(1+\lambda_{0}^{-1/2}+\lambda_{0}^{-1})\|{\mathbb{g}}\|_{B^{\nu}_{q,r}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}.

(4.3)

Let
0<\sigma<1
. For
{\mathbb{g}}\in B^{s+\sigma}_{q,r}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
, we write

\displaystyle\lambda^{1/2}\lambda^{\sigma/2}\bar{\nabla}{\mathcal{S}}(\lambda){\mathbb{g}}

\displaystyle={\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\Bigl[\frac{\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\sigma}{2}}(1,i\xi)(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\sigma/2}{\mathcal{F}}[{\mathbb{g}}](\xi)}{(\lambda+\alpha|\xi|^{2})(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\sigma/2}}\Bigr]

\displaystyle+\beta{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\Bigl[\frac{\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\sigma}{2}}(i\xi)i\xi i\xi\cdot((1+|\xi|^{2})^{\sigma/2}{\mathcal{F}}[{\mathbb{g}}](\xi))}{(\lambda+\alpha|\xi|^{2})(\lambda+(\alpha+\beta)|\xi|^{2})(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\sigma/2}}\Bigr],

Applying the Fourier multiplier theorem of Mikhilin-Hörmander type, we have

\|\lambda^{1/2}\lambda^{\sigma/2}\nabla{\mathcal{S}}(\lambda){\mathbb{g}}\|_{B^{s}_{q,r}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}\leq C(1+\lambda_{0}^{-1/2})\|{\mathbb{g}}\|_{B^{s+\sigma}_{q,r}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}.

Analogously, we have

\|\lambda^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}\bar{\nabla}^{2}{\mathbb{u}}\|_{B^{s}_{q,r}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}\leq C(1+\lambda_{0}^{-1/2}+\lambda_{0}^{-1})\|{\mathbb{g}}\|_{B^{s+\sigma}_{q,r}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}.

Moreover, since
{\mathbb{g}}\in B^{s+\sigma}_{q,r}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})\subset B^{s-\sigma}_{q,r}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
,
changing
(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\sigma/2}
by
(1+|\xi|^{2})^{-\sigma/2}
,
we have

\|(1,\lambda^{-1/2}\bar{\nabla}){\mathcal{S}}(\lambda){\mathbb{g}}\|_{B^{s}_{q,r}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}\leq C(1+\lambda_{0}^{-1/2})|\lambda|^{-(1-\frac{\sigma}{2})}\|{\mathbb{g}}\|_{B^{s-\sigma}_{q,r}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}.

(4.4)

Concerning
\partial_{\lambda}{\mathcal{S}}(\lambda){\mathbb{g}}
, differentiating equations (
4.1
)
and using the uniqueness of solutions, we see that

\partial_{\lambda}{\mathcal{S}}(\lambda){\mathbb{g}}=-{\mathcal{S}}(\lambda){\mathcal{S}}(\lambda){\mathbb{g}}
. Using (
4.3
)
and (
4.4
), we immediately have

\displaystyle\|(\lambda,\lambda^{1/2}\bar{\nabla},\bar{\nabla}^{2})\partial_{\lambda}{\mathcal{S}}(\lambda){\mathbb{g}}\|_{B^{s}_{q,r}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}

\displaystyle\leq C|\lambda|^{-1}\|{\mathbb{g}}\|_{B^{s}_{q,r}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})},

\displaystyle\|(\lambda,\lambda^{1/2}\bar{\nabla},\bar{\nabla}^{2})\partial_{\lambda}{\mathcal{S}}(\lambda){\mathbb{g}}\|_{B^{s}_{q,r}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}

\displaystyle\leq C|\lambda|^{-(1-\frac{\sigma}{2})}\|{\mathbb{g}}\|_{B^{s-\sigma}_{q,r}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}.

This completes the proof of Theorem
20
.
∎

4.2.
The half-space case.

In this section, we consider the Lamé equations in the half space, which read as

\lambda{\mathbb{u}}-\alpha\Delta{\mathbb{u}}-\beta\nabla{\rm div}\,{\mathbb{u}}={\mathbb{g}}\quad\text{in ${\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+}$},\quad{\mathbb{u}}=0\quad\text{on $\partial{\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+}$}.

Notice that

.
We shall prove

Theorem 21
.

Let
1<q<\infty
,
1\leq r\leq\infty-
,

-1+1/q<s<1/q
,
\epsilon\in(0,\pi/2)

\sigma
be a small positive number such that

Then, there exists an operator

having

\lambda\in\Sigma_{\epsilon,\lambda_{0}}

and

(

).

In what follows, we shall prove Theorem
21
.
Since we know solution operators
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
, we consider the compensation equations:

\lambda{\mathbb{u}}-\alpha\Delta{\mathbb{u}}-\beta\nabla{\rm div}\,{\mathbb{u}}=0\quad\text{in ${\mathbb{R}}^{N}_{+}$},\quad u_{j}|_{x_{N}=0}=h_{j}|_{x_{N}=0},\quad u_{N}|_{x_{N}=0}=0

(4.6)

{\mathbb{h}}^{\prime}=(h_{1},\ldots,h_{N-1})

To obtain a solution formula of (

partial Fourier transform
{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}
with respect to the tangential variables

x^{\prime}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N-1})
and its inverse transform
{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}_{\xi^{\prime}}
with respect
to the dual variables

, and then we have

\displaystyle(\lambda+\alpha|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}-\alpha D_{N}^{2}){\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}[u_{j}]-\beta i\xi_{j}(i\xi^{\prime}\cdot{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}[{\mathbb{u}}^{\prime}]+D_{N}{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}[u_{N}])=0

\displaystyle(x_{N}>0),

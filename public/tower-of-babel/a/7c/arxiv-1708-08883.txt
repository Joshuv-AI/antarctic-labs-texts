Some applications of Hahn Banach theorem in Locally convex spaces

Let
Y
be a subset of a locally
convex space
X
, we put
q^{*}_{Y}(f)=\sup\{|f(y)|:y\in Y,q(y)\leq 1\}

q^{*}(f)=\sup\{|f(x)|:x\in X,q(x)\leq 1\}

on

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose that
Q
is a family of seminorms on a real locally
convex space

Y

. Then there exists a continuous linear functional
h
on
X
that extends
f
such that
q^{*}_{Y}(f)=q^{*}(h)
.

Proof.

If we define
p:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by
p(x)=q^{*}_{Y}(f)q(x)
for each
x\in X
, then we have
p
is a seminorm on
X
such that
f(x)\leq p(x)
, for each
x\in Y
. Because, if
x=0
, clearly
f(x)=0
and
0\leq p(x)
. On the other hand, if
x\in Y
and
x\neq 0
then from our assumption,
q(x)\neq 0
and
q(\frac{x}{q(x)})=1
. Therefore, we have
f(\frac{x}{q(x)})\leq q^{*}_{Y}(f)
, then
f(x)\leq q^{*}_{Y}(f)q(x)=p(x)
. Since
q
is continuous,
p
is also a continuous seminorm, therefore by the Hahn-Banach theorem (Theorem 3.9 in
[
16
]
), there exists a linear continuous extension
h
of
f
to
X
that
h(x)\leq p(x)
for each
x\in X
. Hence, since
X
is a vector space, we have

(1)

|h(x)|\leq q^{*}_{Y}(f)q(x),(x\in X)

and hence,
q^{*}(h)\leq q^{*}_{Y}(f)
. Moreover, since
q^{*}_{Y}(f)=\sup\{|f(y)|:q(y)\leq 1\}\leq\sup\{|h(x)|:q(x)\leq 1\}=q^{*}(h)
, we have
q^{*}_{Y}(f)=q^{*}(h)
.
∎

5.
The weakly fixed point property for commuting semigroups

Let
Q
be a family of seminorms on a locally convex space

which determines the topology of
E
. The goal of this section is to show If
E
has the weakly fixed point property, then

has the weakly fixed
point property for commuting separable semitopological semigroups.

We will make use of the following two Theorems in this section.

Theorem 5.1
.

Suppose that

is a family of seminorms on a locally convex space

which determines the topology of

. Let

be a nonempty closed convex and bounded subset of
E
. If
\{F_{n}\}

For each

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}=1

(3)

\lim_{n}\frac{\sum_{j=n+1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}}{\sum_{j=n}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}}=0.

[
5

\displaystyle q\big(x-r_{n}(x)\big)=

\displaystyle q\big(r(x)-r_{n}(x)\big)=q\big(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}[r_{j}(x)-r_{n}(x)]\big)

(4)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}q(r_{j}(x)-r_{n}(x)),

\displaystyle q\big(x-r_{n}(x)\big)\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\lambda_{j}q(x-r_{n}(x))+d_{q}\sum_{j=n+1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}.

Because
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}=1
, this in turn concludes that

\displaystyle q\big(x-r_{n}(x)\big)\leq d_{q}\frac{\sum_{j=n+1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}}{\sum_{j=n}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}}.

Then by (
3
),

, in
\tau_{Q}
when

. But from the fact that
\{F_{n}\}
is descending, therefore

for each

is the fixed point set of continuous mapping

. Now since

, therefore
F(r)=\bigcap_{n}F_{n}
.

Quadrupole Moment of a Magnetically Confined Mountain on an Accreting Neutron Star in General Relativity
Pedro H. B. Rossetto, Jörg Frauendiener, Andrew Melatos

Abstract
General relativistic corrections are calculated for the quadrupole moment of a magnetically confined mountain on an accreting neutron star. The hydromagnetic structure of the mountain satisfies the general relativistic Grad-Shafranov equation supplemented by the flux-freezing condition of ideal magnetohydrodynamics, as in previous calculations of the magnetic dipole moment. It is found that the ellipticity and hence the gravitational wave strain are up to 12 % 12\% greater than in the analogous Newtonian system. The direct contribution of the magnetic field to the nonaxisymmetric component of the stress-energy tensor is shown to be negligible in accreting systems such as low-mass X-ray binaries.

Introduction

Fast-spinning deformed neutron stars are prime candidates for the emission of continuous gravitational waves (CWs)

It is important to model from first principles the formation mechanisms of non-axisymmetric stellar deformations, such as ‘mountains’, which are static in the star’s rotating frame, in order to predict the detectability of the associated CW signal. Proposed mechanisms include elastic stresses in the crust

,
17
,

. The latter mechanism is the focus of this paper.

Magnetically confined mountains are formed by the accretion of conducting plasma onto the magnetic polar cap of a neutron star in systems like low-mass X-ray binaries (LMXBs). Several features have been incorporated previously into the theoretical model, including mountain stability
[

,
20
,

,

[

They found that relativistic corrections change the hydromagnetic equilibrium of the accreted matter, diminishing the screening of the magnetic moment three-fold when compared to the Newtonian formulation.

In this paper, we discuss how general relativistic corrections modify the mass quadrupole moment of a magnetically confined mountain and hence affect the emission of gravitational radiation. In Section
II
, we summarize the relativistic model of the system and the numerical method. In Section

II
Hydromagnetic equilibrium of a polar mountain

We employ the same theoretical framework presented by
[
28
]
. The system is modelled as an isothermal magnetically confined mountain on a neutron star produced by magnetic burial. We assume that the neutron star generates a background Schwarzschild metric and that the accreted matter and magnetic field adjust to this curved spacetime. Throughout the paper, we assume the fiducial value of
1.4M_{\odot}
for the gravitational mass
M_{*}
of the neutron star. We assume that the whole system is axisymmetric and, hence, that the magnetic field
B^{a}
of the neutron star is fully determined by a scalar function
\psi
as

B^{a}=\frac{1}{r^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}\epsilon^{abcd}u_{d}\nabla_{b}\phi\nabla_{c}\psi,

(1)

where
\epsilon^{abcd}
is the completely antisymmetric tensor. Under these conditions, the equilibrium of the mountain is described by the relativistic isothermal Grad-Shafranov equation

\Delta^{*}\psi=-F^{\prime}(\psi)\exp[-(1+c_{\rm s}^{-2})(\Phi-\Phi_{0})],

(2)

where
c_{\rm s}
is the speed of sound,
\Phi
is the relativistic gravitational potential,
\Phi_{0}
is a reference potential,
\Delta^{*}
is the relativistic Grad-Shafranov operator

\Delta^{*}\psi=\frac{1}{r^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}\left\{\partialderivative{r}\left[\left(1-\frac{2M_{*}}{r}\right)\partialderivative{\psi}{r}\right]+\frac{\sin\theta}{r^{2}}\partialderivative{\theta}\left(\frac{1}{\sin\theta}\partialderivative{\psi}{\theta}\right)\right\},

(3)

and
F(\psi)
is a function related to the fluid pressure at the altitude of the reference potential
\Phi_{0}
.

[
24
]
developed a recipe to calculate the function
F(\psi)
uniquely in the Newtonian theory, by connecting the pre- and post-accretion states through the flux freezing condition of ideal magnetohydrodynamics.
[
28
]
adapted the recipe to general relativity, obtaining

F(\psi)=\left(\derivative{M}{\psi}\right)^{1+c_{\rm s}^{2}}\left(\frac{2\pi}{c_{\rm s}^{2}}\int_{C}r\sin\theta|\nabla\psi|^{-1}e^{-\left(\Phi-\Phi_{0}\right)/c_{\rm s}^{2}}\differential{s}\right)^{-(1+c_{\rm s}^{2})}.

(4)

In (
4
),
\differential{M}/\differential{\psi}
specifies how accreted mass is distributed along flux tubes labelled uniquely by
\psi
. We use
the same phenomenological prescription for this function as
[
24
,
26
]
, viz.

M(\psi)=\frac{M_{a}}{2}\frac{1-e^{-\psi/\psi_{a}}}{1-e^{-\psi_{*}/\psi_{a}}},

(5)

with
\psi_{*}=\psi(R_{*},0)
, where
R_{*}
is the neutron star’s radius and
\psi_{a}
is the flux surface that touches the inner edge of the accretion disk.

Numerical solutions to equations (
2
)–(
5
) are obtained iteratively
[
28
]
. Figure
1
shows an example of a hydromagnetic equilibrium generated by the solver, for
M_{\rm a}=10^{-5}M_{\odot}
and
b=\psi_{*}/\psi_{\rm a}=10
. Panel
1
(a) shows the general relativistic equilibrium state for the magnetic field lines (
\psi
level curves) as solid curves, and the Newtonian equivalent as dashed curves. The system reaches a deformed equilibrium state for both theories, but the deformation in the relativistic case is smaller. Panel
1
(b) confirms that the numerical
\psi
residuals decrease with iteration number in a controlled fashion; that is, the numerical solution converges.

III
Quadrupole moment and ellipticity

In the linear regime for the generation of gravitational waves
[
16
]
, the mass quadrupole source term is proportional to the second time derivative of the mass quadrupole moment, defined by

Q_{ij}=\int_{V^{\prime}}T^{00}\left({x^{\prime}}_{i}{x^{\prime}}_{j}-\frac{1}{3}{x^{\prime}}^{k}{x^{\prime}}_{k}\delta_{ij}\right)\differential[]x^{\prime},

(6)

where
x^{\prime i}
is a spatial position vector (assuming a small source),
V^{\prime}
is the volume of the source and
\delta_{ij}
is the identity tensor. Note that equation (
6
) is usually termed the mass quadrupole moment to distinguish it from the current quadrupole moment, which is proportional to the fluid velocity and generates current quadrupole gravitational radiation
[
33
]
. However, it is important to recognise that
T^{00}
in equation (
6
) includes contributions from the energy density in the magnetic field as well as the mass density of the fluid. We discuss this in Section
III.3
.
T^{00}
is given by

T^{00}=e+\frac{B^{2}}{2}.

(7)

where
e
is the energy density of the fluid, and we have
B^{2}=B_{a}B^{a}
. Furthermore,
e
can be decomposed as
e=\rho(1+\varepsilon)
where
\rho
is the rest-mass density of the fluid and
\varepsilon
is its specific internal energy. We keep both terms in (
7
) for the sake of generality and compare their magnitudes below.

In the triaxial model of a rigidly rotating neutron star, the gravitational wave amplitude is given by
[
16
]

h_{0}=\frac{16\pi^{2}I_{0}f^{2}\epsilon}{D},

(8)

where
I_{0}=(2/5)M_{*}{R_{*}}^{2}
is the moment of inertia of the star before accretion,
f
is the rotation frequency,
D
is the distance to the source and
\epsilon
is the (mass-energy) ellipticity of the star given by

\epsilon=\frac{\pi}{I_{0}}\int_{V^{\prime}}T^{00}r^{4}\sin\theta(3\cos^{2}\theta-1)\differential{r}\differential{\theta}.

(9)

We also define the material and magnetic ellipticities
\epsilon_{e}
and
\epsilon_{B}
in terms of the same integral as in (
9
) but replacing
T^{00}
with
e
and
B^{2}/2
respectively to give
\epsilon=\epsilon_{e}+\epsilon_{B}
. In equilibrium,
e
and
B^{2}
can be related to the flux-function
\psi
output from the numerical solver, with

\displaystyle e=\frac{F(\psi)}{c_{\rm s}^{2}}\exp[-(1+c_{\rm s}^{-2})(\Phi-\Phi_{0})],

(10)

\displaystyle B^{2}=\frac{1}{r^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}\left[\frac{1}{r^{2}}\left(\partialderivative{\psi}{\theta}\right)^{2}+e^{2\Phi}\left(\partialderivative{\psi}{r}\right)^{2}\right].

(11)

III.2
Physical interpretation

As discussed in Section
II
, general relativity reduces the deformation of the magnetic field lines, when compared to Newtonian theory (cf. Figure
1
(a)). Together with flux-freezing, this implies that the density profile is narrower in the
\theta
direction in the relativistic case. Near the polar surface, the mountain is also more compressed in the radial direction in the relativistic case. Therefore, one can expect the relativistic ellipticity to be greater than the Newtonian one, in accord with Figure
3
(b).

To analyse the angular and radial behaviours of the ellipticity, we plot in Figure
4
the Newtonian and relativistic cumulative ellipticities
\epsilon_{\rm ang}(\theta)
and
\epsilon_{\rm rad}(r)
, defined by

\displaystyle\epsilon_{\rm ang}(\theta)

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{I_{0}}\int_{0}^{\theta}\int_{R_{*}}^{R_{\rm m}}T^{00}r^{4}\sin\theta(3\cos^{2}\theta-1)\differential{r}\differential{\theta}\text{, }

(14)

\displaystyle\epsilon_{\rm rad}(r)

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{I_{0}}\int_{R_{*}}^{r}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}T^{00}r^{4}\sin\theta(3\cos^{2}\theta-1)\differential{\theta}\differential{r},

(15)

where

is the maximum radius of the simulation box. We choose two values of accreted mass,
M_{\rm a}
, specifically
1\times 10^{-5}M_{\odot}

III.3
Purely magnetic ellipticity

The magnetic field contributes directly to the quadrupole moment (
6
) and the ellipticity (
9
) of the star through the
B^{2}
term in equation (
7
). This contribution is distinct from the magnetically induced nonaxisymmetry in the accreted matter, which enters through the term
\rho
in (
7
). In the case of magnetically confined mountains, the accreted matter deforms the magnetic field and pushes it equatorwards
[
24
,
28
]
, increasing the magnetic pressure
B^{2}/2
locally. Figure
5
displays how the magnetic ellipticity increases with
M_{\rm a}
. We find that the ellipticity generated directly by the magnetic field is of the order
10^{-11}
, which is
\sim 10^{6}
times smaller than the ellipticity generated by the accreted mass (cf. Figure
3
).

Recent studies
[
13
,
22
,
7
]
investigate the gravitational radiation emitted by a purely dipolar bar magnet rotating in vacuo about an axis inclined to its magnetic axis. They find that the

contribution is generally low but may be detectable by future-generation gravitational-wave detectors in the case of highly-magnetised fast-spinning neutron stars. In our simulations, we can recover the magnetic dipole solution in vacuo by taking the accreted mass to be low (
M_{\rm a}\sim 10^{-9}M_{\odot}
). In the latter regime, we obtain
\epsilon_{B}\sim 10^{-15}
. This value is consistent with the literature, cf. equation (13) of
[
13
]
, equation (46) of
[
22
]
for

[

IV
Discussion

The present work focuses on the modifications arising from including general relativity in models of the formation of magnetically confined mountains and hence the emission of continuous gravitational waves by accreting neutron stars. The baseline for comparison is the Newtonian model calculated by
[
17
]
for an isothermal equation of state. The modifications brought by general relativity to the hydromagnetic structure of the mountain are discussed in detail by
[
28
]
. The main effect is to increase the ellipticity in the relativistic case, as can be seen in equations (
12
) and (
13
).

The characteristic gravitational wave strain
h_{0}
from a star with a static mountain with ellipticity
\epsilon
can be written as

h_{0}=4.7\times 10^{-26}\left(\frac{\epsilon}{10^{-6}}\right)\left(\frac{I_{0}}{1.1\times 10^{38}\mathrm{kg\,m^{2}}}\right)\left(\frac{f}{100\,\rm{Hz}}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{1\,\rm{kpc}}{r}\right).

(16)

From the ellipticity results shown in Figure
3
we obtain strains between
\sim 10^{-26}
and
\sim 10^{-25}
. The relativistic results are up to
12\%
greater than the Newtonian ones, depending on
M_{\rm a}
. The purely magnetic contribution from
\epsilon_{B}
is negligible, generating a wave strain of the order
10^{-32}
.

Several important effects already modeled in the Newtonian literature must also be incorporated into the relativistic model. A summary of these effects can be found in Section
I
. The unmodeled effect that probably affects the system the most is the choice of the equation of state. In the Newtonian literature
[
26
]
, a polytropic or piecewise-polytropic equation of state reduces the quadrupole between three and four orders of magnitude compared to an isothermal equation of state. Furthermore, dynamical effects need to be included. The Grad-Shafranov equation cannot model these effects, as it is an equilibrium equation. Dynamical effects control the stability of the accreted mountain
[
25
,
35
,
21
,
19
]
.

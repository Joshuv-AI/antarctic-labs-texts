On solutions for some class of integrable difference equations
Andrei K. Svinin

Abstract
. In this paper we show that an arbitrary solution of one ordinary difference equation is also a solution for a hierarchy of integrable difference equations. We also provide an example of such a solution that is related to sequence generated by a second-order linear recursion with 2-periodic coefficients.



1.
Introduction

In this paper we consider an infinite one-parameter class of ordinary difference equations

y_{n+1}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}y_{n+j}-H^{(s)}\right)=y_{n+s}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}y_{n+j+2}-H^{(s)}\right)\;\;s\geq 2.

(1.1)

Notice that each of equations (
1.1
) contains one arbitrary parameter
H^{(s)}
and can be rewritten in the form

y_{n+s+1}=R(y_{n},\ldots,y_{n+s};H^{(s)}),

where
R
is corresponding rational function of its arguments. Equation (
1.1
), for some fixed value of
H^{(s)}
, yields a map
\mathbb{R}^{s+1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{s+1}
for real-valued initial
data
\{y_{n}:n=1,\ldots,s\}
. By analogy with ordinary differential equations, a function
J=J_{n}=J(y_{n},\ldots,y_{n+s})
is called a first integral for
difference equation (
1.1
) if by virtue of this equation one has
J_{n+1}=J_{n}
. There are ordinary difference equations which have some special properties which yield a regular behavior of
their solutions. One such property is Liouville integrability
[
2
]
,
[
17
]
.

Equations of the form (
1.1
) appeared in
[
14
]
. They are interesting in that they share the property of having a Lax pair that indicates their possible Liouville integrability.
In
[
15
]
, we have shown a method for constructing a set of the first integrals for these equations and also for a wider class of integrable difference equation regardless of their Lax representation. Unfortunately, the property of Liouville integrability for these equations has not been proved in our works. We say that these equations are integrable only in the sense that they admit a nontrivial Lax representation that yields a number of the first integrals.

Also note that, as was shown in
[
14
]
that, assuming that
y_{n}
’s depend on evolutionary parameters
(t_{1},t_{2},\ldots)
, equations (
1.1
) play the role of compatible constraints for the Volterra lattice

\frac{\partial y_{n}}{\partial t_{1}}=y_{n}\left(y_{n+1}-y_{n-1}\right)

and its hierarchy of generalized symmetries that can be written in explicit form as
[
14
]

\frac{\partial y_{n}}{\partial t_{s}}=y_{n}\left(S^{s}_{s}(n-s+2)-S^{s}_{s}(n-s)\right)\;\;s\geq 2.

(1.2)

Here discrete polynomials
S_{s}^{k}(n)
are explicitly defined by

S_{s}^{k}(n)=\sum_{0\leq q_{1}\leq\cdots\leq q_{k}\leq s-1}y_{n+q_{1}+k-1}\cdots y_{n+q_{k}}.

(1.3)

It should be noted that the first integrals constructed in
[
15
]
are described in terms of these discrete polynomials.

In this article we do not discuss Liouville integrability for equations (
1.1
). Instead, we show a compatibility of these equations, which also, to a certain extent, reflects the integrability property. In general, it should be said that the integrability property for equations of various nature is often formulated as a compatibility condition of some number of equations. An example is the pairwise compatibility of evolutionary equations (
1.2
).

Specifically, we prove the fact that an arbitrary solution of equation (
1.1
) corresponding to the simplest case
s=2
, that is,

y_{n+1}\left(y_{n}+y_{n+1}-H^{(2)}\right)=y_{n+2}\left(y_{n+2}+y_{n+3}-H^{(2)}\right)

(1.4)

is also a solution of equation (
1.1
) for all
s\geq 3
. In these circumstances, it turns out that parameters
H^{(s)}
are related to each other by rather nontrivial recurrence relations which also include two first integrals
G^{(s)}
and
G_{1}^{(s)}
that will be defined in the following section. Ultimately each
H^{(s)}
, for
s\geq 3
, is unambiguously expressed as a rational function of variables
(y_{0},y_{1},y_{2},H^{(2)})
.

Also in the paper we construct some class of solutions for (
1.1
) that are yielded by a second-order linear recursion with 2-periodic coefficients

T_{n}=P_{n}T_{n-1}+QT_{n-2},\;\;P_{n+2}=P_{n}

(1.5)

with arbitrary initial conditions. Rather important circumstance here is that recursion (
1.5
) defines a great number of interesting integer sequences with applications in various areas of mathematics.

The paper is organized as follows. In the following section, using the two integrals mentioned above and some substitutions, we show some difference equations closely related to (
1.1
) which are of interest in themselves. In particular,
[
10
]
we show that equations (
1.1
) are related to bilinear difference equations with 2-periodic coefficients. It should be noted that presentation of material in Section
2
partially follows the lines of the paper
[
10
]
. At the end of Section
2
, we formulate a theorem concerning the above-mentioned compatibility of equations (
1.1
). In Section
3
, we show how the sequences defined by recursion (
1.5
) yield solutions for equation (
1.4
) and, therefore, for all equations of the form (
1.1
).

2.
Difference equations

To begin with, note that equation (
1.1
) has the first integral

G^{(s)}=\left(\sum_{j=0}^{s}y_{n+j}-H^{(s)}\right)\prod_{j=1}^{s-1}y_{n+j}.

(2.1)

We can consider this relation as a difference equation of order
s
with two parameters
G^{(s)}
and
H^{(s)}
, that is equivalent to (
1.1
). Indeed, it is easy to see that the relation
G^{(s)}(n+1)=G^{(s)}(n)
is nothing but (
1.1
).

Remark 2.1
.

The equation (
2.1
) known as the generalized DTKQ equation was the object of study in the work
[
10
]
. It was shown there that for even
s
the equation (
2.1
) are related to the
(s,-1)
periodic reduction of Hirota’s discrete KdV equation, that turned out to be Liouville integrable
[
7
]
. In turn, for odd
s
, equations (
2.1
) are connected to reductions of a discrete Toda lattice
[
9
]
.
It should be also noticed, that this equation with
H^{(s)}=0
has appeared in
[
3
]
.

More general than (
2.1
) is a two-parameter class of integrable equations

G^{(k,s)}=\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{j}H_{j}^{(k,s)}S^{k-j}_{s-j+1}(n+j)\prod_{j=k}^{s-1}y_{n+j},\;\;k\geq 1,\;\;s\geq k+1,

where
H_{0}^{(k,s)}=1
, appeared in
[
14
]
and
[
15
]
. Here
(k,s)
-th difference equation involves
k+1
parameters
\left(G^{(k,s)},H_{1}^{(k,s)},\ldots,H_{k}^{(k,s)}\right)
.

Let us consider the first integral
[
10
]
,
[
15
]

G_{1}^{(s)}=\prod_{j=0}^{s}y_{n+j}+\sum_{j=1}^{s-1}y_{n+j}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{s}y_{n+j}-H^{(s)}\right)\prod_{j=1}^{s-1}y_{n+j},

which further will be useful for us.
In what follows, we use the fact, that
G_{1}^{(s)}
can be rewritten as

G_{1}^{(s)}=\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}y_{n+j}\left(G^{(s)}-\prod_{j=0}^{s-1}y_{n+j}\right)+H^{(s)}\prod_{j=0}^{s-1}y_{n+j}.

(2.2)

With the substitution
y_{n}=w_{n}w_{n+1}
equation (
2.1
) becomes

\sum_{j=0}^{s}w_{n+j}w_{n+j+1}=\frac{G^{(s)}}{\prod_{j=1}^{s-1}w_{n+j}w_{n+j+1}}+H^{(s)}.

(2.3)

By direct calculation, it can be checked that this equation has
2
-integral
[
10
]

\alpha_{n}^{(s)}=\prod_{j=0}^{s}w_{n+j}-\frac{G^{(s)}}{\prod_{j=1}^{s-1}w_{n+j}}.

(2.4)

Indeed, by (
2.3
), we have

\alpha_{n+1}^{(s)}=\prod_{j=1}^{s-1}w_{n+j}\left(H^{(s)}-\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}w_{n+j}w_{n+j+1}\right)\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\alpha_{n+2}^{(s)}=\alpha_{n}^{(s)}.

(2.5)

Lemma 2.2
.

The relation

\alpha_{n}^{(s)}\alpha_{n+1}^{(s)}=G_{1}^{(s)}-H^{(s)}G^{(s)}

(2.6)

is valid.

Proof.
By (
2.4
) and (
2.5
), we have

\displaystyle\alpha_{n}^{(s)}\alpha_{n+1}^{(s)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\prod_{j=0}^{s}w_{n+j}-\frac{G^{(s)}}{\prod_{j=1}^{s-1}w_{n+j}}\right)\prod_{j=1}^{s-1}w_{n+j}\left(H^{(s)}-\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}w_{n+j}w_{n+j+1}\right)

(2.7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\prod_{j=0}^{s-1}w_{n+j}w_{n+j+1}-G^{(s)}\right)\left(H^{(s)}-\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}w_{n+j}w_{n+j+1}\right).

On the other hand, with the substitution
y_{n}=w_{n}w_{n+1}
, (
2.2
) becomes

G_{1}^{(s)}=\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}w_{n+j}w_{n+j+1}\left(G^{(s)}-\prod_{j=0}^{s-1}w_{n+j}w_{n+j+1}\right)+H^{(s)}\prod_{j=0}^{s-1}w_{n+j}w_{n+j+1}.

Comparing the latter with (
2.7
), in a result, we get relation (
2.6
).

\Box

Let us now rewrite (
2.4
) as

\prod_{j=0}^{s-1}w_{n+j}w_{n+j+1}=\alpha_{n}^{(s)}\prod_{j=1}^{s-1}w_{n+j}+G^{(s)},\;\;\alpha_{n+2}^{(s)}=\alpha_{n}^{(s)}.

(2.8)

This is an ordinary difference equation of order
s
. This equation is known as the modified generalized DTKQ equation
[
10
]
. In particular case
s=2
, it becomes

w_{n}w_{n+1}^{2}w_{n+2}=\alpha_{n}^{(2)}w_{n+1}+G^{(2)},\;\;\alpha^{(2)}_{n+2}=\alpha^{(2)}_{n}.

(2.9)

Equation (
2.8
) is of interest, because, as was pointed out in
[
10
]
, it represents an example of
U
-system
[
8
]
.

Let us notice that here
H^{(s)}
plays the role of the first integral for difference equation (
2.8
).
From (
2.5
), we derive the following expression for this integral:

H^{(s)}=\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}w_{n+j}w_{n+j+1}+\frac{\alpha_{n+1}^{(s)}}{\prod_{j=1}^{s-1}w_{n+j}}.

(2.10)

By direct calculation, it can be checked that, by (
2.8
), the first integral (
2.10
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle H^{(s)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{s-2}w_{n+j}w_{n+j+1}+\frac{\alpha_{n}^{(s)}}{\prod_{j=0}^{s-2}w_{n+j}}+\frac{\alpha_{n+1}^{(s)}}{\prod_{j=1}^{s-1}w_{n+j}}

(2.11)

\displaystyle+\frac{G^{(s)}}{\prod_{j=0}^{s-2}w_{n+j}w_{n+j+1}}.

In particular, in the case
s=2
, (
2.11
) becomes

H^{(2)}=w_{n}w_{n+1}+\frac{\alpha_{n}^{(2)}}{w_{n}}+\frac{\alpha_{n+1}^{(2)}}{w_{n+1}}+\frac{G^{(2)}}{w_{n}w_{n+1}}.

(2.12)

With the substitution
w_{n}=f_{n}f_{n+2}/f_{n+1}^{2}
equation (
2.8
) can be rewritten in bilinear form
[
10
]

f_{n}f_{n+s+2}=\alpha_{n}^{(s)}f_{n+1}f_{n+s+1}+G^{(s)}f_{n+2}f_{n+s},\;\;\alpha^{(s)}_{n+2}=\alpha^{(s)}_{n}.

(2.13)

More exactly, any solution of (
2.13
) yields a solution of (
2.8
) but not vice versa. In particular, if
s=2
, then (
2.13
) becomes

f_{n}f_{n+4}=\alpha_{n}^{(2)}f_{n+1}f_{n+3}+G^{(2)}f_{n+2}^{2},\;\;\alpha^{(2)}_{n+2}=\alpha^{(2)}_{n}.

(2.14)

Remark 2.3
.

In fairness, we note that the substitution
y_{n}=f_{n}f_{n+3}/(f_{n+1}f_{n+2})
was used in
[
16
]
to put evolution equations of the Volterra lattice hierarchy (
1.2
) into bilinear form.

Remark 2.4
.

According to Theorem 1.1 from
[
10
]
, the identities

f_{n}f_{n+2s+1}=-G^{(s)}f_{n+1}f_{n+2s}+K^{(s)}f_{n+s}f_{n+s+1}\;\forall\;\;\mbox{even}\;s\geq 2

and

f_{n}f_{n+2s+2}=\left(G^{(s)}\right)^{2}f_{n+2}f_{n+2s}+K^{(s)}f^{2}_{n+s+1}\;\forall\;\;\mbox{odd}\;s\geq 3

are valid, where

is the first integral of
s
-th equation (
2.1
) defined by
[

K^{(s)}=\left(\prod_{j=0}^{s-1}y_{n+2j}+G^{(s)}\right)\prod_{q=0}^{(s-2)/2}\prod_{j=1}^{s-1}y_{n+2q+j},\;\mbox{if}\;s\;\mbox{even}

K^{(s)}=\left(\prod_{j=0}^{2s-1}y_{n+j}-\left(G^{(s)}\right)^{2}\right)\prod_{q=0}^{(s-3)/2}\prod_{j=2}^{s}y_{n+2q+j},\;\mbox{if}\;s\;\mbox{odd}.

Remark 2.5
.

Equation (
2.14
) represents a slight generalization of the Somos-
4
equation

f_{n}f_{n+4}=f_{n+1}f_{n+3}+f_{n+2}^{2},

(2.15)

that generate, for example, the sequence of positive integers that begins with
(1,1,1,1,2,3,7,23,59,\ldots)
. Despite the fact that, generally speaking, this recurrence relation must generate a sequence of fractional numbers, they all turns out to be integers.
A proof of integrity of this sequence known as the Somos-4 sequence can be found in
[
6
]
. It has now become clear that the reason for integrity of this sequence lies in the fact that equation (
2.15
) has the Laurent property. In this connection, it is appropriate to mention here the relationship of equation (
2.15
) to cluster algebras
[
4
]
,
[
5
]
.
One of the properties of equation (
2.13
) is that it has also the Laurent property (cf.
[
10
]
), that means that
f_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}[\alpha_{0}^{(s)},\alpha_{1}^{(s)},f_{0}^{\pm 1},\ldots,f_{s+1}^{\pm 1}]
for any
n\geq s+2
. The Laurent property for equation (
2.13
), in fact, follows, as a special case, from this property for the Hirota-Miwa equation
[
12
]
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Any solution
\{w_{n}\}
to equation (
2.9

\alpha_{0}^{(3)}=\alpha_{1}^{(3)}=-G^{(2)}\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;G^{(3)}=G_{1}^{(2)}=\alpha_{0}^{(2)}\alpha_{1}^{(2)}+G^{(2)}H^{(2)}.

(2.17)

In addition, in these circumstances, we have

.

Proof.

Provided that
\{w_{n}\}
is any solution of equation (

), for some values of

, we get the following:

\displaystyle w_{n}w_{n+1}^{2}w_{n+2}\cdot w_{n+1}w_{n+2}^{2}w_{n+3}

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;=\left(\alpha_{n}^{(2)}w_{n+1}+G^{(2)}\right)\left(\alpha_{n+1}^{(2)}w_{n+2}+G^{(2)}\right)

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;=\alpha_{n}^{(2)}\alpha_{n+1}^{(2)}w_{n+1}w_{n+2}+G^{(2)}\left(\alpha_{n}^{(2)}w_{n+1}+\alpha_{n+1}^{(2)}w_{n+2}\right)+\left(G^{(2)}\right)^{2}.

Taking into account (

), we get

w_{n}w_{n+1}^{2}w_{n+2}^{2}w_{n+3}=-G^{(2)}w_{n+1}w_{n+2}+\left(\alpha_{n}^{(2)}\alpha_{n+1}^{(2)}+G^{(2)}H^{(2)}\right).

Whence it follows that if

satisfies equation (

) with corresponding coefficients given by (

Moreover, by (

\displaystyle H^{(3)}-H^{(2)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle w_{n+2}w_{n+3}+\frac{\alpha^{(3)}_{n+1}}{w_{n+1}w_{n+2}}-\frac{\alpha^{(2)}_{n+1}}{w_{n+1}}

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\alpha^{(2)}_{n+1}}{w_{n+1}}+\frac{G^{(2)}}{w_{n+1}w_{n+2}}\right)+\frac{\alpha^{(3)}_{n+1}}{w_{n+1}w_{n+2}}-\frac{\alpha^{(2)}_{n+1}}{w_{n+1}}.

\Box

3.
Construction of solution for difference equations

In this section, we give an example of a solution for equation (
2.9
). To this aim, let us define the sequence
\{T_{n}:n\geq 0\}
by linear recurrent relation (
1.5
)
with arbitrary initial condition, where
P_{0},P_{1}
and
Q
are supposed to be arbitrary numbers. It is obvious that
T_{n}
can be written as a linear combination
T_{n}=T_{0}N_{n}+T_{1}D_{n}
, where
D_{n}=D_{n}(P_{0},P_{1},Q)
and
N_{n}=N_{n}(P_{0},P_{1},Q)
represent particular solutions of (
1.5
) with initial conditions
(0,1)
and
(1,0)
, respectively.
For example,

D_{0}=0,\;\;D_{1}=1,\;\;D_{2}=P_{0},\;\;D_{3}=P_{0}P_{1}+Q,\;\;D_{4}=P_{0}\left(P_{0}P_{1}+2Q\right),\ldots

and

N_{0}=1,\;\;N_{1}=0,\;\;N_{2}=Q,\;\;N_{3}=QP_{1},\;\;N_{4}=Q\left(P_{0}P_{1}+Q\right),\ldots

To clarify the meaning of these polynomials, for any
P_{0},P_{1}
and
Q
, let us consider the following 2-periodic “continued fraction”:

C=\frac{Q}{P_{0}+\displaystyle{\frac{Q}{P_{1}+\displaystyle{\frac{Q}{P_{0}+\displaystyle{\frac{Q}{P_{1}+\cdots}}}}}}}

(3.1)

and its convergents

C_{2}=\frac{Q}{P_{0}},\;\;C_{3}=\frac{Q}{P_{0}+\displaystyle\frac{Q}{P_{1}}},\;\;C_{4}=\frac{Q}{P_{0}+\displaystyle\frac{Q}{P_{1}+\displaystyle\frac{Q}{P_{0}}}},\ldots

Then, as is known,
C_{n}=N_{n}/D_{n}\;\;\forall n\geq 2
. Of course,
C
is literally a continued fraction only if
P_{0},P_{1}
and
Q
are integers.

Remark 3.1
.

For the polynomials
D_{n}(P_{0},P_{1},Q)
and
N_{n}(P_{0},P_{1},Q)
, one knows the following (see, for example,
[
1
]
). The polynomial
D_{2n}/P_{0}
and
D_{2n+1}
, for any
n\geq 1
, are in fact homogeneous polynomial in
P_{0}P_{1}
and
Q
. Moreover, the sequence
\{D_{n}:n\geq 0\}
is a divisibility sequence. This fact can be illustrated by the formula

D_{n}=\prod_{d|n}\Phi_{d}(P_{0}P_{1},Q),

where, for example,

\Phi_{1}=1,\;\;\Phi_{2}=P_{0},\;\;\Phi_{3}=P_{0}P_{1}+Q,\;\;\Phi_{4}=P_{0}P_{1}+2Q,

\Phi_{5}=P_{0}^{2}P_{1}^{2}+3P_{0}P_{1}Q+Q^{2},\;\;\Phi_{6}=P_{0}P_{1}+3Q,\ldots

Let us define the sequence of polynomials
U_{n}=U_{n}(L,M)
by recurrence relations

Final remarks

Let us briefly discuss the results presented in this paper. In the end, we find the relationship of integer sequences with integrable equations (
1.1

It would also be interesting to explore these results for investigation Volterra lattice integrable hierarchy (

Abstract
We investigate λ \lambda -Hilbert transform, λ \lambda -Possion integral and conjugate λ \lambda -Poisson integral on the atomic Hardy space in the Dunkl setting and establish a new version of Paley type inequality which extends the results in [ 11 ] and [ 14 ] . 2000 MS Classification: 42B20, 42B25, 42A38. 2000 Chinese Library Classification: O178. Introduction and preliminaries

For
0<p<\infty
,
L_{\lambda}^{p}({\mathbb{R}})
is the set of measurable functions satisfying

\|f\|_{L_{\lambda}^{p}}=\Big(c_{\lambda}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}|f(x)|^{p}|x|^{2\lambda}dx\Big)^{1/p}

<\infty

,

space.
For
\lambda\geq 0
, The Dunkl operator on the line is:

D_{x}f(x)=f^{\prime}(x)+\frac{\lambda}{x}[f(x)-f(-x)]

involving a reflection part. The associated Fourier transfrom for the Dunkl setting for
f\in L_{\lambda}^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
is given by:

\displaystyle({\mathscr{F}}_{\lambda}f)(\xi)=c_{\lambda}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}f(x)E_{\lambda}(-ix\xi)|x|^{2\lambda}dx,\quad\xi\in{\mathbb{R}},\,\,f\in L_{\lambda}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}).

(1)

E_{\lambda}(-ix\xi)
is the Dunkl kernel

E_{\lambda}(iz)=j_{\lambda-1/2}(z)+\frac{iz}{2\lambda+1}j_{\lambda+1/2}(z),\ \ z\in{\mathbb{C}}

where
j_{\alpha}(z)
is the normalized Bessel function

j_{\alpha}(z)=2^{\alpha}\Gamma(\alpha+1)\frac{J_{\alpha}(z)}{z^{\alpha}}=\Gamma(\alpha+1)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}(z/2)^{2n}}{n!\Gamma(n+\alpha+1)}.

Since
j_{\lambda-1/2}(z)=\cos z
,
j_{\lambda+1/2}(z)=z^{-1}\sin z
, it follows that
E_{0}(iz)=e^{iz}
, and
{\mathscr{F}}_{0}
agrees with the usual Fourier transform. We assume
\lambda>0
in
what follows.
And the associated
\lambda
-translation in Dunkl setting is

\displaystyle\tau_{y}f(x)=c_{\lambda}\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}}({\mathscr{F}}_{\lambda}f)(\xi)E(ix\xi)E(iy\xi)|\xi|^{2\lambda}d\xi,\ \ x,y\in{{\mathbb{R}}}.

(2)

The
\lambda
-convolution
(f\ast_{\lambda}g)(x)
of two appropriate functions
f
and
g
on
{\mathbb{R}}
associated to the
\lambda
-translation
\tau_{t}
is defined by

(f\ast_{\lambda}g)(x)=c_{\lambda}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}f(t)\tau_{x}g(-t)|t|^{2\lambda}dt.

The "Laplace Equation" associated with the Dunkl setting is given by:

(\triangle_{\lambda}u)(x,y)=\left(D_{x}^{2}+\partial_{y}^{2}\right)u(x,y)=\left(\partial_{x}^{2}+\partial_{y}^{2}\right)u+\frac{\lambda}{x}\partial_{x}u-\frac{\lambda}{x^{2}}\left(u(x,y)-u(-x,y)\right).

A

\lambda
-harmonic.

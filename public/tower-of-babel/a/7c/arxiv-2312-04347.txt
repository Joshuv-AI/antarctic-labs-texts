Abstract
. Let N N be a closed, connected, and oriented Riemannian manifold, which admits a quasiregular ω \omega -curve ℝ n → N \mathbb{R}^{n}\to N with infinite energy. We prove that, if the de Rham class of ω \omega is non-zero and belongs to a so-called Künneth ideal, then there exists a non-trivial graded algebra homomorphism H dR ∗ ​ ( N ) → ⋀ ∗ ℝ n H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(N)\to\bigwedge^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n} from the de Rham algebra H dR ∗ ​ ( N ) H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(N) of N N to the exterior algebra ⋀ ∗ ℝ n \bigwedge^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n} . As an application, we give examples of pairs ( N , ω ) (N,\omega) , where N N is a closed manifold and ω \omega is a closed n n -form for n < dim N n<\dim N , for which every quasiregular ω \omega -curve ℝ n → N \mathbb{R}^{n}\to N is constant.

1.
Introduction

A continuous map
f\colon M\to N
between oriented Riemannian
n
-manifolds,
n\geqslant 2
, is
K
-quasiregular for
K\geqslant 1
if
f\in W_{\operatorname{loc}}^{1,n}(M,N)
and

\lVert Df\rVert^{n}\leqslant K(\star f^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{N})\text{ a.e.~in }M;

here
\lVert Df\rVert
is the operator norm of the weak differential
Df
and
\star f^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{N}
is the Hodge star dual of the
n
-form
f^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{N}
. An oriented and connected
n
-manifold
N
is
quasiregularly elliptic
, if there exists a non-constant quasiregular map
\mathbb{R}^{n}\to N
.

In 1981, Gromov
[
3
, p. 200]
posed a question, whether all closed simply connected manifolds are quasiregularly elliptic. In 2019, Prywes
[
12
, Corollary 1.2]
answered Gromov’s question by giving examples of closed simply connected
4
-manifolds which are not quasiregularly elliptic. The examples were obtained as an application of Prywes’ cohomology dimension bound for closed quasiregularly elliptic manifolds
[
12
, Theorem 1.1]
:
If
N
is a closed quasiregularly elliptic manifold, then
\dim H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k}(N)\leqslant\dim\bigwedge^{k}\mathbb{R}^{n}=\binom{n}{k}
for each
k
.
In
[
6
]
, Pankka and the author showed that, in fact,
H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(N)
is Euclidean in the following sense:
If
N
is a closed quasiregularly elliptic manifold, then there exists an embedding of graded algebras
H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(N)\to\bigwedge^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
This embedding yields a topological classification of closed simply connected quasiregularly elliptic
4
-manifolds
[
6
, Corollary 1.2]
.

In this paper, we prove a generalization of this cohomology embedding theorem for closed manifolds admitting quasiregular curves.

For the definition of a quasiregular curve, let
M
be an oriented Riemannian
n
-manifold with
n\geqslant 2
. Let
N
be an oriented Riemannian manifold and let
\omega\in\Omega^{n}(N)
be a closed non-vanishing form, i.e.,
d\omega=0
and
\omega(x)\neq 0
for every
x\in N
. A continuous map
F\colon M\to N
is a
K
-quasiregular
\omega
-curve for
K\geqslant 1
if
F\in W_{\operatorname{loc}}^{1,n}(M,N)
and

(\lVert\omega\rVert\circ F)\lVert DF\rVert^{n}\leqslant K(\star F^{*}\omega)\text{ a.e.~in }M;

here the
pointwise comass norm of
\omega
is given by

\lVert\omega(x)\rVert=\max\{\omega_{x}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})\colon v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\text{ unit vectors in }T_{x}N\},

\lVert DF\rVert
is the operator norm of the weak differential
DF
, and
\star F^{*}\omega
is the Hodge star dual of the
n
-form
F^{*}\omega
.

Quasiregular curves are introduced in
[
11
]
and include, for example, quasiregular maps, holomorphic curves, and pseudoholomorphic curves.

Quasiregular curves lead to the following two notions of quasiregular ellipticity: Let
N
be an oriented and connected Riemannian manifold and let
\omega\in\Omega^{n}(N)
be a closed non-vanishing form with
n\geqslant 2
. We say that the pair
(N,\omega)
is
quasiregularly elliptic
if there exists a non-constant quasiregular
\omega
-curve
F\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to N
. We also say that the pair
(N,\omega)
is
infinite energy quasiregularly elliptic
if there exists a quasiregular
\omega
-curve
F\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to N
satisfying
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}F^{*}\omega=\infty
.

Remark 1.1
.

We note that a pair
(N,\mathrm{vol}_{N})
, where
N
is a closed
n
-manifold satisfying
H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(N)\neq H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(\mathbb{S}^{n})
, is infinite energy quasiregularly elliptic if and only if
N
is quasiregularly elliptic. The equivalence follows from the Bonk–Heinonen growth result for quasiregular mappings
[
1
, Theorem 1.11]
:
Let
N
be a closed, connected, and oriented Riemannian
n
-manifold with
n\geqslant 2
and
H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(N)\neq H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(\mathbb{S}^{n})
. Then every non-constant
K
-quasiregular map
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to N
satisfies

\liminf_{r\to\infty}\frac{\int_{B^{n}(0,r)}f^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{N}}{r^{\varepsilon}}>0,

where
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(n,K)>0
.
To our knowledge, a similar growth result is not known for quasiregular curves in full generality; for so-called
signed quasiregular curves
, see
[
5
]
. In particular, the growth theorem in
[
5
]
guarantees infinite energy in all the examples presented in this paper; for details see Theorem
2.3
.

To state our main result, we introduce some terminology stemming from the Künneth theorem:
Let
N=M\times P
be a product of closed smooth manifolds. Then the map

\bigoplus_{\ell=0}^{k}H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{\ell}(M)\otimes H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k-\ell}(P)\to H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k}(N),\;c\otimes c^{\prime}\mapsto\pi_{M}^{*}c\wedge\pi_{P}^{*}c^{\prime},

where
\pi_{M}\colon N\to M
and
\pi_{P}\colon N\to P
are the projections, is an isomorphism for each
k
.

For a smooth manifold
N
, we say that the
Künneth ideal of
N
is the ideal
K^{*}(N)=\oplus_{k=2}^{\dim N}K^{k}(N)
of
H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(N)
, where the
k
th layer
K^{k}(N)
is the vector space generated by products
c\wedge c^{\prime}
of de Rham classes
c\in H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{\ell}(N)
and
c^{\prime}\in H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k-\ell}(N)
with
1\leqslant\ell\leqslant k-1
, i.e.,

K^{k}(N)=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\bigcup_{\ell=1}^{k-1}H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{\ell}(N)\wedge H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k-\ell}(N)\right).

We are now ready to present our main result.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(N,\omega)
be infinite energy quasiregularly elliptic, where
N
is closed and
0\neq[\omega]_{\mathrm{dR}}\in K^{n}(N)
. Then there exists a graded algebra homomorphism
\Phi\colon H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(N)\to\bigwedge^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}
for which
\Phi[\omega]_{\mathrm{dR}}\neq 0
.

By the Poincaré duality and the Bonk–Heinonen growth theorem, Theorem
1.2
yields as a corollary the aforementioned embedding theorem in
[
6
]
.

Before discussing further consequences of Theorem
1.2
, we briefly mention examples of non-trivial Künneth ideals, which are used later in this paper. The first example is given by product manifolds, where the factors are equidimensional.

Example 1.3
.

Let
n\geqslant 2
. Let
N=N_{1}\times\cdots\times N_{m}
and
\omega=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\pi_{i}^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{N_{i}}\in\Omega^{n}(N)
, where each
N_{i}
is a closed, connected, and oriented Riemannian
n
-manifold with
H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(N_{i})\neq H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(\mathbb{S}^{n})
and each
\pi_{i}\colon N\to N_{i}
is the projection. Then, by the Künneth theorem and Poincaré duality,
0\neq[\omega]_{\mathrm{dR}}\in K^{n}(N)
. In particular, if
m=1
, then
0\neq[\mathrm{vol}_{N}]\in K^{n}(N)
.

Kähler manifolds also give examples of non-trivial Künneth ideals.

Example 1.4
.

Let
(N,\omega_{\mathrm{sym}})
be a Kähler manifold. Let
m
be an integer satisfying
4\leqslant 2m\leqslant\dim N
and let
\omega=\omega_{\mathrm{sym}}^{\wedge m}=\omega_{\mathrm{sym}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega_{\mathrm{sym}}\in\Omega^{2m}(N)
. Then
0\neq[\omega]_{\mathrm{dR}}\in K^{2m}(N)
.

Examples of non-ellipticity

We obtain from Theorem
1.2
two families of examples of closed manifolds
N
and closed non-vanishing forms
\omega
for which the pair
(N,\omega)
is not quasiregularly elliptic. Our first family of examples consists of pairs
(N,\omega)
, where
N
is a
6
-manifold and
\omega
is a
4
-form.

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
g\geqslant 1
and
T_{g}=\#^{g}(\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{1})
. Let
\omega_{\mathrm{sym}}\in\Omega^{2}(\mathbb{C}P^{2})
be the standard symplectic form. Then
(T_{g}\times\mathbb{C}P^{2},\pi_{1}^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{T_{g}}\wedge\pi_{2}^{*}\omega_{\mathrm{sym}})
is quasiregularly elliptic if and only if
g=1
.

Corollary
1.5
also shows that the quasiregular ellipticity of a pair
(N,\omega)
depends on both
N
and
\omega
. Indeed,
(T_{g}\times\mathbb{C}P^{2},\pi_{2}^{*}\omega_{\mathrm{sym}})
is quasiregularly elliptic for every
g\geqslant 1
.

Our second family of not quasiregularly elliptic examples are pairs
(N,\omega)
, where
N
is a simply connected
8
-manifold and
\omega
is a
6
-form.

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
\nu\geqslant 8
and
S_{\nu}=\#^{\nu}(\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})
. Let
\omega_{\mathrm{sym}}\in\Omega^{2}(\mathbb{C}P^{2})
be the standard symplectic form. Then
(S_{\nu}\times\mathbb{C}P^{2},\pi_{1}^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{S_{\nu}}\wedge\pi_{2}^{*}\omega_{\mathrm{sym}})
is not quasiregularly elliptic.

It is not clear to us, whether the lower bound
\nu\geqslant 8
is sharp in Corollary
1.6
.

Prywes’ theorem for quasiregular curves

As a byproduct of the proof of Theorem
1.2
, we also obtain the following result, which can be interpreted as a relative of Prywes’ theorem for quasiregular curves.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
(N,\omega)
be infinite energy quasiregularly elliptic, where
N
is a closed manifold,
\{0\}\neq\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{R}}([\omega]_{\mathrm{dR}})=K^{n}(N)
, and
\lVert\omega\rVert>0
. Let
M\subset N
be a closed, connected, and oriented
n
-submanifold satisfying
\iota^{*}(H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(N))=H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(M)
and
\iota^{*}[\omega]_{\mathrm{dR}}\neq 0
, where
\iota\colon M\to N
is the inclusion. Then
\dim H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k}(M)\leqslant\binom{n}{k}
for
k=0,\ldots,n
.

Theorem
1.7
has a topological analogue:
Let
F\colon\Sigma\to N
be a continuous map between smooth manifolds for which
F^{*}(K^{n}(N))\neq\{0\}
for some
n\geqslant 1
. Let
M\subset N
be a closed, connected, and oriented
n
-submanifold satisfying
\iota^{*}(H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(N))=H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(M)
and
\ker\iota^{*}\cap\,K^{n}(N)\subset\ker F^{*}
, where
\iota\colon M\to N
is the inclusion. Then there exists an embedding
H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k}(M)\to H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k}(\Sigma)
for each
k
.
For a formulation of Theorem
1.7
more similar to this topological analogue, see Theorem
6.4
.

Quasiregular curves with respect to closed non-exact forms

Instead of directly proving Theorem
1.2
, we prove a stronger statement for pairs
(N,\omega)
, where
\omega
is a closed non-exact form, possibly vanishing at some points on
N
.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
N
be a closed, connected, and oriented Riemannian manifold. Let
2\leqslant n\leqslant\dim N
and let
\omega\in\Omega^{n}(N)
be a closed form with
0\neq[\omega]_{\mathrm{dR}}\in K^{n}(N)
. Let
F\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to N
be a quasiregular
\omega
-curve satisfying
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}F^{*}\omega=\infty
and
\inf_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\lVert\omega\rVert\circ F)>0
. Then there exists a graded algebra homomorphism
\Phi\colon H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(N)\to\bigwedge^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}
for which
\Phi[\omega]_{\mathrm{dR}}\neq 0
.

Remark 1.9
.

Formally,
F
is a quasiregular
\omega_{+}
-curve
\mathbb{R}^{n}\to N_{+}
, where

N_{+}=\{x\in N\colon\omega(x)\neq 0\}\quad\text{ and }\quad\omega_{+}=\omega|_{N_{+}}.

In Theorem
1.8
, we assume that the form is not exact since, if
N
is a closed, connected, and oriented Riemannian manifold and
\omega\in\Omega^{n}(N)
,
2\leqslant n\leqslant\dim N
, is an exact form, then every quasiregular
\omega
-curve
F\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to N
satisfying
\inf_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\lVert\omega\rVert\circ F)>0
is constant; see
[
11
, Theorem 1.7]
and
[
11
, Remark 3.2]
.

Idea of the proof

The proof of Theorem
1.8
follows the proof of the cohomology embedding theorem for closed quasiregularly elliptic manifolds in
[
6
]
.

The proof consists of two key ideas. We reduce the global problem of Theorem
1.8
to a local setting. For the statements, let
N
be an oriented Riemannian manifold and let
\omega\in\Omega^{n}(N)
be a closed non-exact form, where
2\leqslant n\leqslant\dim N
. For constants
K,D\geqslant 1
, let
\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N,\omega)
be the family of non-constant
K
-quasiregular
\omega
-curves
F\colon B_{2}^{n}\to N
satisfying the additional conditions

\int_{B_{2}^{n}}F^{*}\omega\leqslant D\int_{B^{n}}F^{*}\omega

and
\inf_{B_{2}^{n}}(\lVert\omega\rVert\circ F)\geqslant D^{-1}
. Here and in what follows,
B^{n}
and
B_{2}^{n}
denote the Euclidean balls of radius
1
and
2
centered at the origin, respectively. In what follows, we also denote

A_{\omega}(F)=\int_{B^{n}}F^{*}\omega>0

for
F\in\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N,\omega)
.

We are now ready to state a localized reformulation of Theorem
1.8
; see also
[
6
, Theorem 1.3]
.

Theorem 1.10
.

Let
N
be a closed, connected, and oriented Riemannian manifold. Let
2\leqslant n\leqslant\dim N
and let
\omega\in\Omega^{n}(N)
be a closed form with
0\neq[\omega]_{\mathrm{dR}}\in K^{n}(N)
. Assume also that there exists
K,D\geqslant 1
satisfying

\sup_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N,\omega)}A_{\omega}(F)=\infty.

Then there exists a graded algebra homomorphism
\Phi\colon H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(N)\to\bigwedge^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}
for which
\Phi[\omega]_{\mathrm{dR}}\neq 0
.

The reduction of Theorem
1.8
to Theorem
1.10
follows from Rickman’s Hunting Lemma. After the reduction, we construct the homomorphism in Theorem
1.10
as the evaluation of a weak limit of normalized pull-backs.

Let
N
be a closed, connected, and oriented Riemannian manifold and let

h\colon H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(N)\to\Omega^{*}(N),\quad c\mapsto h_{c},

be the map associating to each de Rham class
c\in H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(N)
its unique harmonic representative
h_{c}\in c
; to see that the map
h
is well-defined see e.g.
[
16
, Theorem 6.11]
. Given
\omega
,
K
and
D
, we define, for each
F\in\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N,\omega)
, a
normalized pull-back

F^{\#}\colon H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(N)\to\mathscr{W}^{*}(B_{2}^{n})
by formula

F^{\#}c=A_{\omega}(F)^{-\frac{k}{n}}F^{*}(h(c))

for
c\in H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k}(N)
and
k=0,\ldots,n
. Here and in what follows,
\mathscr{W}^{*}(B_{2}^{n})
is the graded algebra

\mathscr{W}^{k}(B_{2}^{n})=\begin{cases}W^{d,\frac{n}{k}}(B_{2}^{n};{\bigwedge}^{k}\mathbb{R}^{n}),&\,\text{ for }1\leqslant k\leqslant n,\\
\ker(d\colon\Omega^{0}(B_{2}^{n})\to\Omega^{1}(B_{2}^{n})),&\,\text{ for }k=0,\end{cases}

of Sobolev forms on
B_{2}^{n}
; for more details see Section
3
.

The normalized pull-backs have the following weak limit property: if
[\omega]_{\mathrm{dR}}\in K^{n}(N)
and
(F_{j})
is a sequence in
\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N,\omega)
with the properties that
A_{\omega}(F_{j})\to\infty
and that the sequence

\left(F_{j}^{\#}|_{H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k}(N)}\colon H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k}(N)\to\mathscr{W}^{k}(B_{2}^{n})\right)

has a weak limit for each
k=1,\ldots,n-1
, then the limit operator extends to a graded algebra homomorphism
H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(N)\to\mathscr{W}^{*}(B_{2}^{n})
which maps
[\omega]_{\mathrm{dR}}
non-trivially. More precisely, we prove the following theorem; see also
[
6
, Theorem 1.7]
.

Theorem 1.11
.

Let
N
be a closed, connected, and oriented Riemannian manifold. Let
2\leqslant n\leqslant\dim N
and let
\omega\in\Omega^{n}(N)
be a closed form with
0\neq[\omega]_{\mathrm{dR}}\in K^{n}(N)
. Let
K,D\geqslant 1
. Let
(F_{j})
be a sequence in
\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N,\omega)
for which
A_{\omega}(F_{j})\to\infty
and
F_{j}^{\#}\rightharpoonup L
, where

F_{j}^{\#},L\colon\bigoplus_{k=1}^{n-1}H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k}(N)\to\bigoplus_{k=1}^{n-1}L^{\frac{n}{k}}(B_{2}^{n};{\bigwedge}^{k}\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Then
L
extends to a graded algebra homomorphism
L\colon H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(N)\to\mathscr{W}^{*}(B_{2}^{n})
for which
\int_{B^{n}}L[\omega]_{\mathrm{dR}}=1
.

In general, the choice of harmonic representative
h\colon H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(N)\to\Omega^{*}(N)
is not an algebra homomorphism and hence neither are the normalized pull-backs
F_{j}^{\#}
. However, Theorem
1.11
shows that the limiting operator
L
commutes with the exterior product.

2.
Reduction of Theorem
1.8
to Theorem

In this section, we reduce Theorem
1.8
to Theorem
1.10
with the following proposition; see also
[
6
, Proposition 2.1]
and
[
12
, Section 4]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
N
be a closed, connected, and oriented Riemannian manifold. Let
\omega\in\Omega^{n}(N)
be a closed form with
2\leqslant n\leqslant\dim N
and
0\neq[\omega]_{\mathrm{dR}}\in K^{n}(N)
. Let
K\geqslant 1
and let
F\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to N
be a
K
-quasiregular
\omega
-curve satisfying
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}F^{*}\omega=\infty
and
\inf_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\lVert\omega\rVert\circ F)>0
. Then there exist a constant
D=D(n,\omega,F)>1
and a sequence of pairs
(a_{j},r_{j})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty)
for which

F\circ T_{j}\in\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N,\omega)\quad\text{ and }\quad\lim_{j\to\infty}A_{\omega}(F\circ T_{j})=\infty,

where
T_{j}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the affine map
x\mapsto r_{j}x+a_{j}
. In particular,

\sup_{F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N,\omega)}A_{\omega}(F^{\prime})=\infty.

The proof of Proposition
2.1
is based on Rickman’s Hunting Lemma
[
14
, Lemma 5.1]
. The following formulation is due to Bonk and Poggi-Corradini
[
2
, Lemma 2.1]
.

Lemma 2.2

(Rickman’s Hunting Lemma)
.

Let
\mu
be an atomless Borel measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying
\mu(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\infty
and
\mu(B)<\infty
for every ball
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then there exists a constant
D=D(n)>1
with the property that, for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a ball
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
for which

j\leqslant\mu(2B)\leqslant D\mu(B).

Proposition
2.1
is an immediate consequence.

Proof of Proposition
2.1
.

Since
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}F^{*}\omega=\infty
, the Borel measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
defined by
\mu(E)=\int_{E}F^{*}\omega
satisfies the assumptions of Lemma
2.2
. Hence, there exists a constant
D=D(n)>1
and a sequence of balls
(B^{n}(a_{j},r_{j}))
for which

j\leqslant\int_{B^{n}(a_{j},2r_{j})}F^{*}\omega\leqslant D\int_{B^{n}(a_{j},r_{j})}F^{*}\omega.

Let
D^{\prime}=D^{\prime}(n,\omega,F)\geqslant D
be a constant for which
\inf_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\lVert\omega\rVert\circ F)\geqslant(D^{\prime})^{-1}
. We obtain that the maps
F_{j}\colon B_{2}^{n}\to N
,
x\mapsto F(r_{j}x+a_{j})
, form a sequence in
\mathcal{F}_{K,D^{\prime}}(N,\omega)
satisfying
A_{\omega}(F_{j})\to\infty
. The claim follows.
∎

The following theorem gives a sufficient condition for a quasiregular curve to have infinite energy. The theorem is a special case of the Bonk–Heinonen type growth result for signed quasiregular curves; see
[
5
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Theorem 2.3
.

(
[
5
, Theorem 1.1]
)

Let
N
be a connected and oriented Riemannian manifold. Let
\omega\in\Omega^{n}(N)
be a closed non-vanishing form with
2\leqslant n\leqslant\dim N
and

\omega\in\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\bigcup_{k=1}^{n-1}\mathcal{Z}^{k}(N)\wedge\mathcal{Z}^{n-k}(N)\right),

where
\mathcal{Z}^{k}(N)
denotes the space of all smooth, bounded, and closed
k
-forms on
N
. Then every non-constant
K
-quasiregular
\omega
-curve
F\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to N
satisfies

\liminf_{r\to\infty}\frac{\int_{B^{n}(0,r)}F^{*}\omega}{r^{\varepsilon}}>0,

where
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(n,K,\omega)>0
.

Theorem
2.3
implies trivially that non-constant quasiregular
\omega_{\mathrm{sym}}^{\wedge m}
-curves
\mathbb{R}^{2m}\to N
, where
\omega_{\mathrm{sym}}^{\wedge m}
is as in Example
1.4
, have infinite energy. It also follows straightforwardly that non-constant quasiregular
\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\pi_{i}^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{N_{i}}\right)
-curves
\mathbb{R}^{n}\to N_{1}\times\cdots\times N_{m}
, where
\sum_{i=1}^{m}\pi_{i}^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{N_{i}}
is as in Example
1.3
, have infinite energy.

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
n\geqslant 2
and let
N_{1},\ldots,N_{m}
be closed, connected, and oriented Riemannian
n
-manifolds with
H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(N_{i})\neq H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(\mathbb{S}^{n})
. Let
N=N_{1}\times\cdots\times N_{m}
and
\omega=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\pi_{i}^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{N_{i}}\in\Omega^{n}(N)
. Then every non-constant quasiregular
\omega
-curve
F\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to N
satisfies
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}F^{*}\omega=\infty
.

Proof.

By
[
4
, Proposition 2.13]
, it holds that

\mathrm{vol}_{N_{i}}\in\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\bigcup_{k=1}^{n-1}\mathcal{Z}^{k}(N_{i})\wedge\mathcal{Z}^{n-k}(N_{i})\right)

for each
i
. Thus,

\omega\in\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\bigcup_{k=1}^{n-1}\mathcal{Z}^{k}(N)\wedge\mathcal{Z}^{n-k}(N)\right)

and the claim follows by Theorem
2.3
.
∎

3.
Preliminaries

Sobolev forms

We begin by reviewing the basics of Sobolev spaces of differential forms. A
(k+1)
-form
d\tau\in L_{\operatorname{loc}}^{1}(B_{2}^{n};\bigwedge^{k+1}\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the
weak exterior derivative
of a
k
-form
\tau\in L_{\operatorname{loc}}^{1}(B_{2}^{n};\bigwedge^{k}\mathbb{R}^{n})
if

\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\tau\wedge d\varphi=(-1)^{k+1}\int_{B_{2}^{n}}d\tau\wedge\varphi

for every
\varphi\in\Omega_{0}^{n-k-1}(B_{2}^{n})
. For
k=0,\ldots,n-1
,
W^{d,\frac{n}{k}}(B_{2}^{n};\bigwedge^{k}\mathbb{R}^{n})
denotes the Sobolev space of forms
\tau\in L^{\frac{n}{k}}(B_{2}^{n};\bigwedge^{k}\mathbb{R}^{n})
with a weak exterior derivative
d\tau\in L^{\frac{n}{k}}(B_{2}^{n};\bigwedge^{k+1}\mathbb{R}^{n})
. For
k=n
,
W^{d,\frac{n}{k}}(B_{2}^{n};\bigwedge^{k}\mathbb{R}^{n})
denotes
L^{1}(B_{2}^{n};\bigwedge^{n}\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Next, we recall the compatibility of the weak exterior derivative and the pull-back of a continuous Sobolev map.

Lemma 3.1
.

(
[
13
, II.4.7]
)

Let
N
be a smooth manifold and let
F\in W_{\operatorname{loc}}^{1,n}(B_{2}^{n},N)
be a continuous map. Then
dF^{*}\alpha=F^{*}d\alpha
for every
\alpha\in\Omega^{k}(N)
and
k=0,\ldots,n-1
.

We also have an elementary estimate for the the pull-back of a continuous Sobolev map. Namely, if
F\in W_{\operatorname{loc}}^{1,n}(B_{2}^{n},N)
is a continuous map into a smooth manifold
N
, then

(3.1)

\lVert F^{*}\alpha\rVert\leqslant(\lVert\alpha\rVert\circ F)\lVert DF\rVert^{k}\text{ a.e.~in }B_{2}^{n}

for
\alpha\in\Omega^{k}(N)
and
k=0,\ldots,\dim N
.

Lemma
3.1
and the pointwise estimate (
3.1
) yield as an immediate consequence the following.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
N
be a closed smooth manifold and let
F\in W^{1,n}(B_{2}^{n},N)
be a continuous map. Then
F^{*}
defines a graded linear map
F^{*}\colon\Omega^{*}(N)\to\oplus_{k=0}^{n}L^{\frac{n}{k}}(B_{2}^{n};{\bigwedge}^{k}\mathbb{R}^{n})
. In particular,
F^{*}|_{\mathcal{Z}^{k}(N)}\colon\mathcal{Z}^{k}(N)\to W^{d,\frac{n}{k}}(B_{2}^{n};\bigwedge^{k}\mathbb{R}^{n})
is well-defined for
k=0,\ldots,n
.

Estimates for normalized pull-backs

Next, we define the normalized pull-back of a differential form under a quasiregular curve.

Let
N
be a closed, connected, and oriented Riemannian manifold and let
\omega\in\Omega^{n}(N)
be a closed non-exact form with
2\leqslant n\leqslant\dim N
. Let
K,D\geqslant 1
. For each
F\in\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N,\omega)
, let
F^{!}\colon\Omega^{*}(N)\to\bigoplus_{k=0}^{n}L^{\frac{n}{k}}(B_{2}^{n};{\bigwedge}^{k}\mathbb{R}^{n})
be the linear map

F^{!}\alpha=A_{\omega}(F)^{-\frac{k}{n}}F^{*}\alpha\text{ for }\alpha\in\Omega^{k}(N)\text{ and }k=0,\ldots,n;

recall that

A_{\omega}(F)=\int_{B^{n}}F^{*}\omega.

We note that
F^{!}
commutes with the exterior product and that
F^{!}\circ h=F^{\#}
.

The following elementary result gives a bound for the operator norm of
F^{!}
. The proof is similar to the proof of
[
6
, Lemma 3.1]
, so we omit the details.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
N
be a closed, connected, and oriented Riemannian manifold and let
\omega\in\Omega^{n}(N)
be a closed non-exact form with
2\leqslant n\leqslant\dim N
. Let
K,D\geqslant 1
. Let
F\in\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N,\omega)
. Then

\lVert F^{!}\alpha\rVert_{\frac{n}{k},B_{2}^{n}}\leqslant KD^{2}\lVert\alpha\rVert_{\infty}

for every
\alpha\in\Omega^{k}(N)
and
k=0,\ldots,n
.

Lemma
3.3
implies that exact forms vanish in a weak sense under limits of operators
F_{j}^{!}
for sequences
(F_{j})
in
\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N,\omega)
satisfying
A_{\omega}(F_{j})\to\infty
. Since the proof is similar to the proof of
[
6
, Lemma 3.2]
, we omit the details.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
N
be a closed, connected, and oriented Riemannian manifold and let
\omega\in\Omega^{n}(N)
be a closed non-exact form with
2\leqslant n\leqslant\dim N
. Let
K,D\geqslant 1
. Let
(F_{j})
be a sequence in
\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N,\omega)
satisfying

\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi\wedge F_{j}^{!}d\alpha=0

for

4.
Sobolev–Poincaré limits and their properties

In this section, our end goal is to prove Theorem
1.11
. The proof is based on Sobolev–Poincaré limits.

Motivation and definition

We begin by recalling the Poincaré operator of Iwaniec and Lutoborski
[
8
]
:
There exists a graded compact linear operator

T\colon\bigoplus_{k=1}^{n-1}L^{\frac{n}{k}}(B_{2}^{n};{\bigwedge}^{k}\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\bigoplus_{k=1}^{n-1}L^{\frac{n}{k}}(B_{2}^{n};{\bigwedge}^{k-1}\mathbb{R}^{n})

satisfying
{\operatorname{id}}=dT+Td
. In particular,
dT\omega=\omega
for every weakly closed form
\omega\in\oplus_{k=1}^{n-1}L^{\frac{n}{k}}(B_{2}^{n};\bigwedge^{k}\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Next, we show that, for a sequence
(F_{j})
in
\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N,\omega)
, a weak limit of
(F_{j}^{\#})
is the weak exterior derivative of a limit of
(TF_{j_{i}}^{\#})
for some subsequence. Before stating a precise formulation, we recall for the reader’s convenience that, for
F\in\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N,\omega)
,

F^{\#}\colon H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(N)\to\mathscr{W}^{*}(B_{2}^{n}),\;H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k}(N)\ni c\mapsto A_{\omega}(F)^{-\frac{k}{n}}F^{*}(h(c))=F^{!}(h(c)),

where
h\colon H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(N)\to\Omega^{*}(N)
is the choice of harmonic representative. The proof is similar to the proof of
[
6
, Lemma 4.1]
, so we omit the details.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
N
be a closed, connected, and oriented Riemannian manifold and let
\omega\in\Omega^{n}(N)
be a closed non-exact form with
2\leqslant n\leqslant\dim N
. Let
K,D\geqslant 1
. Let
(F_{j})
be a sequence in
\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N,\omega)
for which
F_{j}^{\#}\rightharpoonup L
, where

F_{j}^{\#},L\colon\bigoplus_{k=1}^{n-1}H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k}(N)\to\bigoplus_{k=1}^{n-1}L^{\frac{n}{k}}(B_{2}^{n};{\bigwedge}^{k}\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Then there exists a subsequence
(F_{j_{i}})
and a graded linear operator

\widehat{L}\colon\bigoplus_{k=1}^{n-1}H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k}(N)\to\bigoplus_{k=1}^{n-1}W^{d,\frac{n}{k}}(B_{2}^{n};{\bigwedge}^{k-1}\mathbb{R}^{n})

satisfying
TF_{j_{i}}^{\#}\to\widehat{L}
. In particular,
d\widehat{L}=L
.

We are now ready to give the definition of a Sobolev–Poincaré limit. Let
N
be a closed, connected, and oriented Riemannian manifold and let
\omega\in\Omega^{n}(N)
be a closed non-exact form with
2\leqslant n\leqslant\dim N
. Let
K,D\geqslant 1
. Let
(F_{j})
be a sequence in
\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N,\omega)
. We say that a graded linear operator

\widehat{L}\colon\bigoplus_{k=1}^{n-1}H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k}(N)\to\bigoplus_{k=1}^{n-1}W^{d,\frac{n}{k}}(B_{2}^{n};{\bigwedge}^{k-1}\mathbb{R}^{n})

is a
Sobolev–Poincaré limit of
(F_{j}^{\#})
if
d\widehat{L}
is the weak limit of the operators
F_{j}^{\#}
and
\widehat{L}
is the limit of the operators
TF_{j}^{\#}
.

Vague limits of products

The weak exterior derivative of a Sobolev–Poincaré limit for a sequence whose normalizations grow without bound commutes with the exterior product. We begin by showing the commutativity in a weak sense without assumptions on the growth of the normalizations; see also
[
6
, Proposition 4.2]
and
[
12
, Lemma 4.2]
. For a precise statement, we recall that a sequence
(\mu_{j})
of Radon measures on
B_{2}^{n}

converges vaguely to a Radon measure
\mu
on
B_{2}^{n}
if

\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{j}=\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\mu

for every
\varphi\in C_{0}(B_{2}^{n})
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
N
be a closed, connected, and oriented Riemannian manifold and let
\omega\in\Omega^{n}(N)
be a closed non-exact form with
2\leqslant n\leqslant\dim N
. Let
K,D\geqslant 1
. Let
(F_{j})
be a sequence in
\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N,\omega)
and suppose that the sequence
(F_{j}^{\#})
has a Sobolev–Poincaré limit
\widehat{L}\colon\oplus_{k=1}^{n-1}H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k}(N)\to\oplus_{k=1}^{n-1}W^{d,\frac{n}{k}}(B_{2}^{n};{\bigwedge}^{k-1}\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Let
k_{1},k_{2}\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
for which
k:=k_{1}+k_{2}\leqslant n
. Then, for every
c_{i}\in H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k_{i}}(N)
, we have

F_{j}^{\#}c_{1}\wedge F_{j}^{\#}c_{2}\rightharpoonup d\widehat{L}(c_{1})\wedge d\widehat{L}(c_{2}).

Here, the weak convergence is the usual weak convergence in
L^{\frac{n}{k}}(B_{2}^{n};\bigwedge^{k}\mathbb{R}^{n})
if
k<n
and the vague convergence of measures if
k=n
.

Proof.

Let
c_{i}\in H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k_{i}}(N)
. By Lemma
3.3
, the sequence
(F_{j}^{\#}c_{1}\wedge F_{j}^{\#}c_{2})=(F_{j}^{!}(h(c_{1})\wedge h(c_{2})))
is bounded in
L^{\frac{n}{k}}(B_{2}^{n};\bigwedge^{k}\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Moreover,
\Omega_{0}^{n-k}(B_{2}^{n})
is dense in
L^{\frac{n}{k}}(B_{2}^{n};\bigwedge^{k}\mathbb{R}^{n})
with respect to the
L^{\frac{n}{k}}
-norm if
k<n
and
C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{2}^{n})
is dense in
C_{0}(B_{2}^{n})
with respect to the
L^{\infty}
-norm. Thus, it suffices to prove that

\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi\wedge F_{j}^{\#}c_{1}\wedge F_{j}^{\#}c_{2}=\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi\wedge d\widehat{L}c_{1}\wedge d\widehat{L}c_{2}

for every
\varphi\in\Omega_{0}^{n-k}(B_{2}^{n})
.

Let
\varphi\in\Omega_{0}^{n-k}(B_{2}^{n})
. By the triangle inequality, it suffices to estimate

\left\lvert\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi\wedge(d\widehat{L}c_{1}-F_{j}^{\#}c_{1})\wedge d\widehat{L}c_{2}\right\rvert\text{ and }\left\lvert\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi\wedge F_{j}^{\#}c_{1}\wedge(d\widehat{L}c_{2}-F_{j}^{\#}c_{2})\right\rvert.

Since

\varphi\wedge d\widehat{L}c_{2}\in L^{\frac{n}{k_{2}}}(B_{2}^{n};{\bigwedge}^{n-k_{1}}\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset L^{\frac{n}{n-k_{1}}}(B_{2}^{n};{\bigwedge}^{n-k_{1}}\mathbb{R}^{n})

and
F_{j}^{\#}c_{1}\rightharpoonup d\widehat{L}c_{1}
in
L^{\frac{n}{k_{1}}}(B_{2}^{n};\bigwedge^{k_{1}}\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the first term converges to zero.

For the second term, observe that
dTF_{j}^{\#}c_{2}=F_{j}^{\#}c_{2}
. Thus, we have the estimate

\displaystyle\left\lvert\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi\wedge F_{j}^{\#}c_{1}\wedge(d\widehat{L}c_{2}-F_{j}^{\#}c_{2})\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\left\lvert\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi\wedge F_{j}^{\#}c_{1}\wedge d(\widehat{L}c_{2}-TF_{j}^{\#}c_{2})\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\left\lvert\int_{B_{2}^{n}}d\varphi\wedge F_{j}^{\#}c_{1}\wedge(\widehat{L}c_{2}-TF_{j}^{\#}c_{2})\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leqslant C(n)\lVert d\varphi\rVert_{\infty}\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\lVert F_{j}^{\#}c_{1}\rVert\lVert\widehat{L}c_{2}-TF_{j}^{\#}c_{2}\rVert.

By Hölder’s inequality and Lemma
3.3
, we may estimate further by

\displaystyle\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\lVert F_{j}^{\#}c_{1}\rVert\lVert\widehat{L}c_{2}-TF_{j}^{\#}c_{2}\rVert

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant\lVert F_{j}^{\#}c_{1}\rVert_{\frac{n}{n-k_{2}},B_{2}^{n}}\lVert\widehat{L}c_{2}-TF_{j}^{\#}c_{2}\rVert_{\frac{n}{k_{2}},B_{2}^{n}}

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant C(n,k_{1},k_{2})\lVert F_{j}^{\#}c_{1}\rVert_{\frac{n}{k_{1}},B_{2}^{n}}\lVert\widehat{L}c_{2}-TF_{j}^{\#}c_{2}\rVert_{\frac{n}{k_{2}},B_{2}^{n}}

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant C(n,k_{1},k_{2})KD^{2}\lVert h(c_{1})\rVert_{\infty}\lVert\widehat{L}c_{2}-TF_{j}^{\#}c_{2}\rVert_{\frac{n}{k_{2}},B_{2}^{n}}.

Since
TF_{j}^{\#}c_{2}\to\widehat{L}c_{2}
in
L^{\frac{n}{k_{2}}}(B_{2}^{n};\bigwedge^{k_{2}}\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the claim follows.
∎

In the next result, we show that the weak commutativity of the weak exterior derivative of a Sobolev–Poincaré limit with the exterior product improves into commutativity, provided that the normalizations grow without bound. The argument in the proof is analogous to the one in the proof of
[
6
, Lemma 4.3]
and we omit the details.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
N
be a closed, connected, and oriented Riemannian manifold and let
\omega\in\Omega^{n}(N)
be a closed non-exact form with
2\leqslant n\leqslant\dim N
. Let
K,D\geqslant 1
. Let
(F_{j})
be a sequence in
\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N,\omega)
for which
A_{\omega}(F_{j})\to\infty
and suppose that the sequence
(F_{j}^{\#})
has a Sobolev–Poincaré limit
\widehat{L}
. Then

d\widehat{L}(c_{1}\wedge c_{2})=d\widehat{L}c_{1}\wedge d\widehat{L}c_{2}

for every
c_{1}\in H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k_{1}}(N)
and
c_{2}\in H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k_{2}}(N)
with
k_{1},k_{2}\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
satisfying
k_{1}+k_{2}<n
.

Extension to
K^{n}(N)

In the setting of quasiregular mappings in
[
6
]
, weak exterior derivatives of Sobolev–Poincare limits are extended to
H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{\dim N}(N)=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{R}}([\mathrm{vol}_{N}]_{\mathrm{dR}})
. Next, we show that, in the setting of this paper, the weak exterior derivative of a Sobolev–Poincare limit has a well-defined extension to
K^{n}(N)
arising from the algebraic structure of
K^{n}(N)
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
N
be a closed, connected, and oriented Riemannian manifold and let
\omega\in\Omega^{n}(N)
be a closed non-exact form with
2\leqslant n\leqslant\dim N
. Let
K,D\geqslant 1
. Let
(F_{j})
be a sequence in
\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N,\omega)
for which
A_{\omega}(F_{j})\to\infty
and suppose that the sequence
(F_{j}^{\#})
has a Sobolev–Poincaré limit
\widehat{L}
. Then the mapping

d\widehat{L}_{n}\colon K^{n}(N)\to L^{1}(B_{2}^{n};{\bigwedge}^{n}\mathbb{R}^{n}),\quad\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}\wedge c_{i}^{\prime}\mapsto\sum_{i=1}^{m}d\widehat{L}c_{i}\wedge d\widehat{L}c_{i}^{\prime},

is a well-defined linear map.

Proof.

Let
k_{1},\ldots,k_{m},\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{\nu}\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
. Let
c_{i}\in H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k_{i}}(N)
,
c_{i}^{\prime}\in H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{n-k_{i}}(N)
,
e_{i}\in H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{\ell_{i}}(N)
, and
e_{i}^{\prime}\in H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{n-\ell_{i}}(N)
be de Rham classes satisfying
\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}\wedge c_{i}^{\prime}=\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}e_{i}\wedge e_{i}^{\prime}
.

Let
\alpha\in\Omega^{n-1}(N)
be a form satisfying

\sum_{i=1}^{m}h(c_{i})\wedge h(c_{i}^{\prime})=\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}h(e_{i})\wedge h(e_{i}^{\prime})+d\alpha

and let
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{2}^{n})
. By Proposition
4.2
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}d\widehat{L}c_{i}\wedge d\widehat{L}c_{i}^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi F_{j}^{\#}c_{i}\wedge F_{j}^{\#}c_{i}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\lim_{j\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi F_{j}^{\#}c_{i}\wedge F_{j}^{\#}c_{i}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi\sum_{i=1}^{m}F_{j}^{!}(h(c_{i})\wedge h(c_{i}^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi F_{j}^{!}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}h(c_{i})\wedge h(c_{i}^{\prime})\right).

A similar computation yields that

\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}d\widehat{L}e_{i}\wedge d\widehat{L}e_{i}^{\prime}\right)=\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi F_{j}^{!}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}h(e_{i})\wedge h(e_{i}^{\prime})\right).

Since, by Lemma
3.4
, we have

\displaystyle\left\lvert\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi F_{j}^{!}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}h(c_{i})\wedge h(c_{i}^{\prime})\right)-\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi F_{j}^{!}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}h(e_{i})\wedge h(e_{i}^{\prime})\right)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\quad=\left\lvert\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi F_{j}^{!}d\alpha\right\rvert\to 0,

it follows that

\sum_{i=1}^{m}d\widehat{L}c_{i}\wedge d\widehat{L}c_{i}^{\prime}=\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}d\widehat{L}e_{i}\wedge d\widehat{L}e_{i}^{\prime}.

Hence,
d\widehat{L}_{n}
is well-defined.
∎

The following proposition states that extending
d\widehat{L}
with
d\widehat{L}_{n}\colon K^{n}(N)\to L^{1}(B_{2}^{n};\bigwedge^{n}\mathbb{R}^{n})
is compatible with vague convergence of measures on
B_{2}^{n}
; see also
[
6
, Theorem 4.4]
.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
N
be a closed, connected, and oriented Riemannian manifold and let
\omega\in\Omega^{n}(N)
be a closed non-exact form with
2\leqslant n\leqslant\dim N
. Let
K,D\geqslant 1
. Let
(F_{j})
be a sequence in
\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N,\omega)
for which
A_{\omega}(F_{j})\to\infty
and suppose that the sequence
(F_{j}^{\#})
has a Sobolev–Poincaré limit
\widehat{L}
. Let
\alpha\in\Omega^{n}(N)
be a closed form satisfying
[\alpha]_{\mathrm{dR}}\in K^{n}(N)
. Then

\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi d\widehat{L}_{n}[\alpha]_{\mathrm{dR}}=\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi F_{j}^{!}\alpha

for every
\varphi\in C_{0}(B_{2}^{n})
. In particular,
F_{j}^{!}\alpha\rightharpoonup d\widehat{L}_{n}[\alpha]_{\mathrm{dR}}
vaguely as measures and, if each
F_{j}^{!}\alpha
is non-negative, then
d\widehat{L}_{n}[\alpha]_{\mathrm{dR}}
is non-negative.

Proof.

Let
[\alpha]_{\mathrm{dR}}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}\wedge c_{i}^{\prime}
be a representation of
[\alpha]_{\mathrm{dR}}
in
K^{n}(N)
and let
\beta\in\Omega^{n-1}(N)
be a form satisfying

\sum_{i=1}^{m}h(c_{i})\wedge h(c_{i}^{\prime})=\alpha+d\beta.

Since
\lVert F_{j}^{!}\alpha\rVert_{1,B_{2}^{n}}\leqslant KD^{2}\lVert\alpha\rVert_{\infty}
by Lemma
3.3
and
C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{2}^{n})
is dense in
C_{0}(B_{2}^{n})
with respect to the
L^{\infty}
-norm, it suffices to prove that

\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi d\widehat{L}_{n}[\alpha]_{\mathrm{dR}}=\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi F_{j}^{!}\alpha

for every
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{2}^{n})
.

By Proposition
4.2
and Lemma
3.4
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi d\widehat{L}_{n}[\alpha]_{\mathrm{dR}}

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}d\widehat{L}c_{i}\wedge d\widehat{L}c_{i}^{\prime}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi F_{j}^{\#}c_{i}\wedge F_{j}^{\#}c_{i}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\quad=\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi\sum_{i=1}^{m}F_{j}^{!}(h(c_{i})\wedge h(c_{i}^{\prime}))=\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi F_{j}^{!}(\alpha+d\beta)

\displaystyle\quad=\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi F_{j}^{!}\alpha

for every
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{2}^{n})
. This concludes the proof.
∎

For
\alpha=\omega
, we get the following improved version of Proposition
4.5
; see also
[
6
, Corollary 4.5]
.

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
N
be a closed, connected, and oriented Riemannian manifold. Let
2\leqslant n\leqslant\dim N
and let
\omega\in\Omega^{n}(N)
be a closed form with
0\neq[\omega]_{\mathrm{dR}}\in K^{n}(N)
. Let
K,D\geqslant 1
. Let
(F_{j})
be a sequence in
\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N,\omega)
for which
A_{\omega}(F_{j})\to\infty
and suppose that the sequence
(F_{j}^{\#})
has a Sobolev–Poincaré limit
\widehat{L}
. Then there exists a non-negative measure
\mu\in L^{1}(B_{2}^{n};\bigwedge^{n}\mathbb{R}^{n})
having following properties:

(1)

\mu=d\widehat{L}_{n}[\omega]_{\mathrm{dR}}
, where
d\widehat{L}_{n}
is the extension of
d\widehat{L}
to
K^{n}(N)
as defined in Lemma
4.4
,

(2)

F_{j}^{!}\omega\rightharpoonup\mu
vaguely as measures on
B_{2}^{n}
,

(3)

F_{j}^{!}\omega\llcorner\bar{B}^{n}\rightharpoonup\mu\llcorner\bar{B}^{n}
weakly as measures on
\bar{B}^{n}
,
and

(4)

\mu(B^{n})=1
.

Remark 4.7
.

The assumption
[\omega]_{\mathrm{dR}}\in K^{n}(N)
is necessary; see e.g. the example in
[
6
, Section 8]
.

Proof of Corollary
4.6
.

By Proposition
4.5
, there exists a non-negative measure
\mu\in L^{1}(B_{2}^{n};\bigwedge^{n}\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfying (1) and (2). Thus, it suffices to show that
\mu
satisfies also (3) and (4).

Let
\varphi\in C(\bar{B}^{n})
be a non-negative function. Let
\tilde{\varphi}\in C_{b}(B_{2}^{n})
be a non-negative bounded function satisfying
\tilde{\varphi}|_{\bar{B}^{n}}=\varphi
. Then
\tilde{\varphi}F_{j}^{!}\omega\rightharpoonup\tilde{\varphi}\mu
vaguely as measures on
B_{2}^{n}
.

Let
0<r<1<R<2
. Let
\psi_{r}\in C_{0}(B^{n})
and
\psi_{R}\in C_{0}(B^{n}(0,R))
satisfy
\chi_{B^{n}(0,r)}\leqslant\psi_{r}\leqslant 1
and
\chi_{B^{n}}\leqslant\psi_{R}\leqslant 1
. Since
\tilde{\varphi}\mu
is non-negative, we obtain

\int_{\bar{B}^{n}(0,r)}\tilde{\varphi}\mu\leqslant\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\psi_{r}\tilde{\varphi}\mu=\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\psi_{r}\tilde{\varphi}F_{j}^{!}\omega\leqslant\liminf_{j\to\infty}\int_{B^{n}}\tilde{\varphi}F_{j}^{!}\omega

and

\int_{B^{n}(0,R)}\tilde{\varphi}\mu\geqslant\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\psi_{R}\tilde{\varphi}\mu=\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\psi_{R}\tilde{\varphi}F_{j}^{!}\omega\geqslant\limsup_{j\to\infty}\int_{B^{n}}\tilde{\varphi}F_{j}^{!}\omega.

Since
\mu\in L^{1}(B_{2}^{n};\bigwedge^{n}\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\liminf_{j\to\infty}\int_{B^{n}}\tilde{\varphi}F_{j}^{!}\omega\geqslant\tilde{\varphi}\mu(B^{n})=\tilde{\varphi}\mu(\bar{B}^{n})\geqslant\limsup_{j\to\infty}\int_{B^{n}}\tilde{\varphi}F_{j}^{!}\omega.

Thus

(4.1)

\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{\bar{B}^{n}}\varphi F_{j}^{!}\omega=\int_{\bar{B}^{n}}\varphi\mu.

Equality (
4.1
) is then obtained for an arbitrary function
\varphi\in C(\bar{B}^{n})
by writing
\varphi=\max(\varphi,0)-\max(-\varphi,0)
.

Especially, since
\mu\in L^{1}(B_{2}^{n};\bigwedge^{n}\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have, for
\varphi=1
, that

\displaystyle\mu(B^{n})

\displaystyle=\int_{\bar{B}^{n}}\mu=\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{\bar{B}^{n}}F_{j}^{!}\omega=\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{B^{n}}F_{j}^{!}\omega

\displaystyle=\lim_{j\to\infty}A_{\omega}(F_{j})^{-1}\int_{B^{n}}F_{j}^{*}\omega=1.

This concludes the proof.
∎

Homomorphism
H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(N)\to\mathscr{W}^{*}(B_{2}^{n})

Next, we show that, if the normalizations grow without bound, then the weak exterior derivative of a Sobolev–Poincare limit extends to a homomorphism
H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(N)\to\mathscr{W}^{*}(B_{2}^{n})
; see also
[
6
, Proposition 5.1]
.

For a precise statement, we introduce the following notation. Let
N
be a closed, connected, and oriented Riemannian manifold and let
\omega\in\Omega^{n}(N)
be a closed non-exact form with
2\leqslant n\leqslant\dim N
. Let
\pi\colon H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{n}(N)\to H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{n}(N)
be a projection onto
K^{n}(N)
, i.e., a linear map satisfying
\pi\circ\pi=\pi
and
\mathrm{im}\,\pi=K^{n}(N)
.

Let
K,D\geqslant 1
. Let
(F_{j})
be a sequence in
\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N,\omega)
for which
A_{\omega}(F_{j})\to\infty
and let
\widehat{L}
be the Sobolev–Poincaré limit of
(F_{j}^{\#})
. Let

\Psi_{d\widehat{L}}^{\pi}\colon H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(N)\to\mathscr{W}^{*}(B_{2}^{n})

be the graded linear map defined by

H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k}(N)\ni c\mapsto\begin{cases}\text{constant function }h(c),&\,\text{if }k=0,\\
d\widehat{L}c,&\,\text{if }1\leqslant k\leqslant n-1,\\
d\widehat{L}_{n}\pi c,&\,\text{if }k=n,\\
0,&\,\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

For the reader’s convenience, we recall that
d\widehat{L}_{n}\colon K^{n}(N)\to L^{1}(B_{2}^{n};\bigwedge^{n}\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the linear map

\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}\wedge c_{i}^{\prime}\mapsto\sum_{i=1}^{m}d\widehat{L}c_{i}\wedge d\widehat{L}c_{i}^{\prime}.

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
N
be a closed, connected, and oriented Riemannian manifold and let
\omega\in\Omega^{n}(N)
be a closed non-exact form with
2\leqslant n\leqslant\dim N
. Let
\pi\colon H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{n}(N)\to H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{n}(N)
be a projection onto
K^{n}(N)
. Let
K,D\geqslant 1
. Let
(F_{j})
be a sequence in
\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N,\omega)
for which
A_{\omega}(F_{j})\to\infty
and suppose that the sequence
(F_{j}^{\#})
has a Sobolev–Poincaré limit
\widehat{L}
. Then
\Psi_{d\widehat{L}}^{\pi}\colon H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(N)\to\mathscr{W}^{*}(B_{2}^{n})
is a homomorphism.

Proof.

Let
k_{1},k_{2}\in\{0,\ldots,\dim N\}
and let
c_{i}\in H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k_{i}}(N)
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
k_{1},k_{2}>0
and that
k_{1}+k_{2}\leqslant n
.

If
k_{1}+k_{2}<n
, then, by Lemma
4.3
, we have

\Psi_{d\widehat{L}}^{\pi}(c_{1}\wedge c_{2})=d\widehat{L}(c_{1}\wedge c_{2})=d\widehat{L}c_{1}\wedge d\widehat{L}c_{2}=\Psi_{d\widehat{L}}^{\pi}c_{1}\wedge\Psi_{d\widehat{L}}^{\pi}c_{2}.

Suppose
k_{1}+k_{2}=n
. Since
\pi|_{K^{n}(N)}={\operatorname{id}}
, we obtain

\Psi_{d\widehat{L}}^{\pi}(c_{1}\wedge c_{2})=d\widehat{L}_{n}\pi(c_{1}\wedge c_{2})=d\widehat{L}_{n}(c_{1}\wedge c_{2})=d\widehat{L}c_{1}\wedge d\widehat{L}c_{2}=\Psi_{d\widehat{L}}^{\pi}c_{1}\wedge\Psi_{d\widehat{L}}^{\pi}c_{2}.

The claim follows by linearity.
∎

We are now ready to prove the following generalization of Theorem
1.11
.

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
N
be a closed, connected, and oriented Riemannian manifold and let
\omega\in\Omega^{n}(N)
be a closed non-exact form with
2\leqslant n\leqslant\dim N
. Let
K,D\geqslant 1
. Let
(F_{j})
be a sequence in
\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N,\omega)
for which
A_{\omega}(F_{j})\to\infty
and
F_{j}^{\#}\rightharpoonup L
, where

F_{j}^{\#},L\colon\bigoplus_{k=1}^{n-1}H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k}(N)\to\bigoplus_{k=1}^{n-1}L^{\frac{n}{k}}(B_{2}^{n};{\bigwedge}^{k}\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Then
L
extends to a graded algebra homomorphism
L\colon H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(N)\to\mathscr{W}^{*}(B_{2}^{n})
for which
F_{j}^{\#}|_{K^{n}(N)}\rightharpoonup L|_{K^{n}(N)}
vaguely as measures. Moreover, if
[\omega]_{\mathrm{dR}}\in K^{n}(N)
, then

\int_{B^{n}}L[\omega]_{\mathrm{dR}}=1\;\text{ and }\;Lc\neq 0\text{ if }c\text{ is a factor of }[\omega]_{\mathrm{dR}},

i.e.,
c\in H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(N)
and there exists
c^{\prime}\in H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{n-*}(N)
for which
c\wedge c^{\prime}=[\omega]_{\mathrm{dR}}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.1
, there exists a subsequence
(F_{j_{i}})
for which the operator
L
is the weak exterior derivative
d\widehat{L}
of a Sobolev–Poincaré limit
\widehat{L}
of
(F_{j_{i}}^{\#})
. Let
\pi\colon H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{n}(N)\to H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{n}(N)
be a projection onto
K^{n}(N)
. Then, by Proposition
4.8
, the map
\Psi_{d\widehat{L}}^{\pi}\colon H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(N)\to\mathscr{W}^{*}(B_{2}^{n})
is a graded algebra homomorphism and
\Psi_{d\widehat{L}}^{\pi}
extends
L
by definition. Furthermore, for each
c\in K^{n}(N)
, we have

F_{j}^{\#}c=F_{j}^{!}(h(c))\rightharpoonup d\widehat{L}_{n}c=d\widehat{L}_{n}\pi c=\Psi_{d\widehat{L}}^{\pi}c

vaguely as measures by Proposition
4.5
.

Suppose that
[\omega]_{\mathrm{dR}}\in K^{n}(N)
. By Corollary
4.6
, we obtain

\int_{B^{n}}\Psi_{d\widehat{L}}^{\pi}[\omega]_{\mathrm{dR}}=\int_{B^{n}}d\widehat{L}_{n}\pi[\omega]_{\mathrm{dR}}=\int_{B^{n}}d\widehat{L}_{n}[\omega]_{\mathrm{dR}}=1.

Hence, it remains to prove that
\Psi_{d\widehat{L}}^{\pi}c\neq 0
if
c
is a factor of
[\omega]_{\mathrm{dR}}
.

Let
c\in H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k}(N)
and
c^{\prime}\in H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{n-k}(N)
satisfy
c\wedge c^{\prime}=[\omega]_{\mathrm{dR}}
, where
k\in\{0,\ldots,n\}
. Then, we have

\Psi_{d\widehat{L}}^{\pi}c\wedge\Psi_{d\widehat{L}}^{\pi}c^{\prime}=\Psi_{d\widehat{L}}^{\pi}(c\wedge c^{\prime})=\Psi_{d\widehat{L}}^{\pi}[\omega]_{\mathrm{dR}}\neq 0

implying
\Psi_{d\widehat{L}}^{\pi}c\neq 0
.
∎

5.
Proof of Theorem
1.10

In this section, we recall the statement of Theorem
1.10
and give its proof.

Theorem
1.10
.

Let
N
be a closed, connected, and oriented Riemannian manifold. Let
2\leqslant n\leqslant\dim N
and let
\omega\in\Omega^{n}(N)
be a closed form with
0\neq[\omega]_{\mathrm{dR}}\in K^{n}(N)
. Assume also that there exists
K,D\geqslant 1
for which

\sup_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N,\omega)}A_{\omega}(F)=\infty.

Then there exists a graded algebra homomorphism
\Phi\colon H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(N)\to\bigwedge^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}
for which
\Phi[\omega]_{\mathrm{dR}}\neq 0
.

Before giving the proof of Theorem
1.10
, we state the following elementary lemma.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
N
be a closed, connected, and oriented Riemannian manifold and let
\omega\in\Omega^{n}(N)
be a closed non-exact form with
2\leqslant n\leqslant\dim N
. Let
K,D\geqslant 1
. Let
(F_{j})
be a sequence in
\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N,\omega)
. Then there exist a subsequence
(F_{j_{i}})
and an operator

L\colon\bigoplus_{k=1}^{n-1}H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k}(N)\to\bigoplus_{k=1}^{n-1}L^{\frac{n}{k}}(B_{2}^{n},{\bigwedge}^{k}\mathbb{R}^{n})

satisfying
F_{j_{i}}^{\#}|_{H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k}(N)}\rightharpoonup L
for
k=1,\ldots,n-1
.

Proof.

Since each
H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k}(N)
is finite-dimensional, the claim follows immediately by Lemma
3.3
and the Banach–Alaoglu theorem.
∎

We are now ready to prove Theorem
1.10
.

Proof of Theorem
1.10
.

There exist a sequence
(F_{j})
in
\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N,\omega)
and an operator

L\colon\bigoplus_{k=1}^{n-1}H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k}(N)\to\bigoplus_{k=1}^{n-1}L^{\frac{n}{k}}(B_{2}^{n},{\bigwedge}^{k}\mathbb{R}^{n})

satisfying
A_{\omega}(F_{j})\to\infty
and
F_{j}^{\#}|_{\oplus_{k=1}^{n-1}H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k}(N)}\rightharpoonup L
by Lemma
5.1
. By Theorem
4.9
, the operator
L
extends to a graded algebra homomorphism
L\colon H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(N)\to\mathscr{W}^{*}(B_{2}^{n})
satisfying
\int_{B^{n}}L[\omega]_{\mathrm{dR}}=1
.

Let
c_{1},\ldots,c_{m},c_{m+1},\ldots,c_{\nu}
be a basis of
H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(N)
, where
c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}
is a basis of
K^{n}(N)
. Since
[\omega]_{\mathrm{dR}}\in K^{n}(N)
, there exist
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m}\in\mathbb{R}
satisfying
[\omega]_{\mathrm{dR}}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}c_{i}
.

We fix point-wise Borel representatives for
Lc_{i}
and
L(c_{i}\wedge c_{\ell})
, where
i,\ell=1,\ldots,\nu
. Let

E=\left\{x\in B^{n}\colon\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}Lc_{i}\right)(x)\neq 0\right\}

and

E_{i,\ell}=\{x\in B^{n}\colon(Lc_{i})(x)\wedge(Lc_{\ell})(x)=(Lc_{i}\wedge c_{\ell})(x)\}

for
i,\ell=1,\ldots,\nu
. Now
m_{n}(E_{i,\ell})=m_{n}(B^{n})
for each pair
(i,\ell)\in\{1,\ldots,\nu\}^{2}
. Furthermore,
m_{n}(E)>0
since

\int_{E}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}Lc_{i}=\int_{B^{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}Lc_{i}=\int_{B^{n}}L[\omega]_{\mathrm{dR}}=1.

Thus there exists
x_{0}\in\cap_{i,\ell=1}^{\nu}E\cap E_{i,\ell}
.

Let
\Phi\colon H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(N)\to\bigwedge^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}
be the linear map defined by
c_{i}\mapsto(Lc_{i})(x_{0})
for
i=1,\ldots,\nu
. By definition, the map
\Phi
is graded. Moreover, we have

\Phi(c_{i}\wedge c_{\ell})=(L(c_{i}\wedge c_{\ell}))(x_{0})=(Lc_{i})(x_{0})\wedge(Lc_{\ell})(x_{0})=\Phi c_{i}\wedge\Phi c_{\ell}

for each pair
(i,\ell)\in\{1,\ldots,\nu\}^{2}
since
x_{0}\in E_{i,\ell}
. Hence, the map
\Phi
is a homomorphism by linearity. Finally,

\Phi[\omega]_{\mathrm{dR}}=\Phi\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}c_{i}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}\Phi c_{i}=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}Lc_{i}\right)(x_{0})\neq 0

since
x_{0}\in E
.
∎

6.
Submanifolds controlled by quasiregular curves

In this section, we present two results, which together yield a stronger version of Theorem
1.7
. The first result is, in fact, a reformulation of Theorem
1.8
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
N
be a closed, connected, and oriented Riemannian manifold. Let
2\leqslant n\leqslant\dim N
and let
\omega\in\Omega^{n}(N)
be a closed form satisfying
0\neq[\omega]_{\mathrm{dR}}\in K^{n}(N)
. Let
K\geqslant 1
and let
F\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to N
be a
K
-quasiregular
\omega
-curve for which
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}F^{*}\omega=\infty
and
\inf_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\lVert\omega\rVert\circ F)>0
. Then there exists a graded algebra homomorphism
\Psi\colon H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(N)\to\mathscr{W}^{*}(B_{2}^{n})
satisfying

\int_{B^{n}}\Psi[\omega]_{\mathrm{dR}}=1\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathrm{core}\,_{\omega}(F)\cap K^{n}(N)\subset\ker\Psi.

Here and in what follows,
\mathrm{core}\,_{\omega}(F)\subset H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{n}(N)
denotes the subspace of de Rham classes
c
satisfying

\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{\int_{B^{n}(a_{j},r_{j})}|F^{*}(h(c))|}{\int_{B^{n}(a_{j},r_{j})}F^{*}\omega}=0

for any sequence
(B^{n}(a_{j},r_{j}))
with
\int_{B^{n}(a_{j},r_{j})}F^{*}\omega\to\infty
.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.1
, there exist
D\geqslant 1
and
(a_{j},r_{j})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty)
for which
F_{j}:=F\circ T_{j}\in\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N,\omega)
and
A_{\omega}(F_{j})\to\infty
, where
T_{j}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the affine map
x\mapsto r_{j}x+a_{j}
. By passing to a subsequence, Lemma
5.1
and Theorem
4.9
together yield that there exists a graded algebra homomorphism
L\colon H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(N)\to\mathscr{W}^{*}(B_{2}^{n})
for which
F_{j}^{\#}|_{K^{n}(N)}\rightharpoonup L|_{K^{n}(N)}
vaguely as measures and

\int_{B^{n}}L[\omega]_{\mathrm{dR}}=1.

It remains to show that
\mathrm{core}\,_{\omega}(F)\cap K^{n}(N)\subset\ker L
.

Let
c\in\mathrm{core}\,_{\omega}(F)\cap K^{n}(N)
and let
\varphi\in C_{0}(B_{2}^{n})
. Then

\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi Lc=\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi F_{j}^{\#}c=\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi F_{j}^{!}(h(c)).

On the other hand, since
F_{j}\in\mathcal{F}_{K,D}(N,\omega)
and
c\in\mathrm{core}\,_{\omega}(F)
, we have

\displaystyle\left\lvert\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi F_{j}^{!}(h(c))\right\rvert

\displaystyle=A_{\omega}(F_{j})^{-1}\left\lvert\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi F_{j}^{*}(h(c))\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leqslant A_{\omega}(F_{j})^{-1}\lVert\varphi\rVert_{\infty}\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\left\lvert F_{j}^{*}(h(c))\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\lVert\varphi\rVert_{\infty}\frac{\int_{B^{n}(a_{j},2r_{j})}\left\lvert F^{*}(h(c))\right\rvert}{\int_{B^{n}(a_{j},r_{j})}F^{*}\omega}

\displaystyle\leqslant D\lVert\varphi\rVert_{\infty}\frac{\int_{B^{n}(a_{j},2r_{j})}\left\lvert F^{*}(h(c))\right\rvert}{\int_{B^{n}(a_{j},2r_{j})}F^{*}\omega}\to 0.

Hence

\int_{B_{2}^{n}}\varphi Lc=0

for an arbitrary function
\varphi\in C_{0}(B_{2}^{n})
, which yields that
Lc=0
. This concludes the proof.
∎

The next result is an elementary algebraic result based on the Poincaré duality on a submanifold.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
N
be a closed, connected, and oriented Riemannian manifold and let
M\subset N
be a closed, connected, and oriented
n
-submanifold with
n\geqslant 2
. Let
c\in K^{n}(N)
and let
\Psi\colon H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(N)\to\mathscr{W}^{*}(B_{2}^{n})
be a graded algebra homomorphism for which

\int_{B^{n}}\Psi c=1\quad\text{ and }\quad\ker\iota^{*}\cap K^{n}(N)\subset\ker\Psi,

where
\iota\colon M\to N
is the inclusion. Let
1\leqslant k\leqslant n-1
be an index for which
\iota^{*}\colon H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{n-k}(N)\to H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{n-k}(M)
is surjective. Then
\dim\iota^{*}H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k}(N)\leqslant\binom{n}{k}
.

As a preparatory step, we recall the following elementary dimension bound for subspaces of
\mathscr{W}^{*}(B_{2}^{n})
; see e.g.
[
12
, Corollary 4.4]
for a proof.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
n\geqslant 2
and
1\leqslant k\leqslant n-1
. Let
u_{1},\ldots,u_{m}\in\mathscr{W}^{k}(B_{2}^{n})
and
v_{1},\ldots,v_{m}\in\mathscr{W}^{n-k}(B_{2}^{n})
satisfy
u_{i}\wedge v_{\ell}=\delta_{i\ell}w
, where
\delta_{i\ell}
is the Kronecker delta and
0\neq w\in L^{1}(B_{2}^{n};\bigwedge\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then
m\leqslant\dim\bigwedge^{k}\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proof of Proposition
6.2
.

Let
\iota^{*}c_{1},\ldots,\iota^{*}c_{m}
be a basis of
\iota^{*}H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k}(N)
. By Poincaré duality, there exist de Rham classes
\iota^{*}c_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,\iota^{*}c_{m}^{\prime}\in H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{n-k}(M)
satisfying
\iota^{*}c_{i}\wedge\iota^{*}c_{\ell}^{\prime}=\delta_{i\ell}[\mathrm{vol}_{M}]_{\mathrm{dR}}
, where
\delta_{i\ell}
is the Kronecker delta.

Since
\iota^{*}c\neq 0
, we may fix
\lambda\neq 0
for which
\lambda\iota^{*}c=[\mathrm{vol}_{M}]_{\mathrm{dR}}
. Then
c_{i}\wedge c_{\ell}^{\prime}-\delta_{i\ell}\lambda c\in\ker\iota^{*}\cap\,K^{n}(N)
. Thus

\Psi c_{i}\wedge\Psi c_{\ell}^{\prime}=\Psi(c_{i}\wedge c_{\ell}^{\prime})=\Psi(\delta_{i\ell}\lambda c)=\delta_{i\ell}\lambda\Psi c,

which yields
m\leqslant\dim\bigwedge^{k}\mathbb{R}^{n}=\binom{n}{k}
by Lemma
6.3
.
∎

Combining Theorem
6.1
and Proposition
6.2
yields immediately the following variation of Theorem
1.7
.

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
N
be a closed, connected, and oriented Riemannian manifold. Let
2\leqslant n\leqslant\dim N
and let
\omega\in\Omega^{n}(N)
be a closed form for which
0\neq[\omega]_{\mathrm{dR}}\in K^{n}(N)
. Let
F\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to N
be a quasiregular
\omega
-curve for which
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}F^{*}\omega=\infty
and
\inf_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\lVert\omega\rVert\circ F)>0
. Let
M\subset N
be a closed, connected, and oriented Riemannian
n
-submanifold satisfying
\iota^{*}(H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(N))=H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(M)
and
\ker\iota^{*}\cap\,K^{n}(N)\subset\mathrm{core}\,_{\omega}(F)
, where
\iota\colon M\to N
is the inclusion. Then
\dim H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k}(M)\leqslant\binom{n}{k}
for
k=0,\ldots,n
.

The following example shows that the assumption
\ker\iota^{*}\cap\,K^{n}(N)\subset\mathrm{core}\,_{\omega}(F)
cannot be replaced by a weaker assumption
\iota^{*}[\omega]_{\mathrm{dR}}\neq 0
in Theorem
6.4
.

Example 6.5
.

Let
n\geqslant 2
. Let
M
and
P
be closed, connected, and oriented Riemannian manifolds with
\dim M=n
and
\dim P\geqslant n
. Let
\xi\in\Omega^{n}(P)
be a closed non-exact form. Let
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to M
be a constant map and let
g\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to P
be a quasiregular
\xi
-curve satisfying
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g^{*}\xi=\infty
.

Let
N=M\times P
,
\omega=\pi_{M}^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{M}+\pi_{P}^{*}\xi
, and
F=(f,g)\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to N
. Then
F
is a quasiregular
\omega
-curve with
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}F^{*}\omega=\infty
.

Let also
\iota\colon M\to N
be an inclusion
m\mapsto(m,p)
for some fixed
p\in P
. Then
\iota^{*}\colon H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(N)\to H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(M)
is surjective and
\iota^{*}[\omega]_{\mathrm{dR}}=[\mathrm{vol}_{M}]_{\mathrm{dR}}\neq 0
.

Compare now Example
6.5
with the choices
M=\#^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{1})
,
P=\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
,
\xi=\mathrm{vol}_{P}
, and
g(x,y)=(e^{2\pi ix},e^{2\pi iy})
to Theorem
6.4
.

Considering further the case, where the ambient manifold is a product manifold and the studied submanifold is one of the factors, we obtain the following result.

Corollary 6.6
.

Let
n\geqslant 2
. Let
M
and
P
be closed, connected, and oriented Riemannian manifolds with
\dim M=n
and
\dim P\geqslant n
. Let
\xi\in\Omega^{n}(P)
be a closed form for which
[\xi]_{\mathrm{dR}}\in K^{n}(P)
. Let
N=M\times P
and let
\omega=\pi_{M}^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{M}+\pi_{P}^{*}\xi
. Let
F\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to N
be a quasiregular
\omega
-curve for which
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}F^{*}\omega=\infty
and
[\pi_{P}^{*}\xi]_{\mathrm{dR}}\in\mathrm{core}\,_{\omega}(F)
. Then there exists a graded algebra homomorphism
\Psi\colon H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(M)\to\mathscr{W}^{*}(B_{2}^{n})
satisfying

\int_{B^{n}}\Psi[\mathrm{vol}_{M}]_{\mathrm{dR}}=1.

Proof.

We may assume that
H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(M)\neq H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(\mathbb{S}^{n})
. Then, by Theorem
6.1
, there exists a graded algebra homomorphism
\Psi\colon H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(N)\to\mathscr{W}^{*}(B_{2}^{n})
satisfying

\int_{B^{n}}\Psi[\omega]_{\mathrm{dR}}=1\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathrm{core}\,_{\omega}(F)\cap K^{n}(N)\subset\ker\Psi.

Since
\Psi[\pi_{P}^{*}\xi]_{\mathrm{dR}}=0
, it follows that
\Psi\circ\pi_{M}^{*}\colon H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(M)\to\mathscr{W}^{*}(B_{2}^{n})
is a graded algebra homomorphism satisfying

\int_{B^{n}}(\Psi\circ\pi_{M}^{*})[\mathrm{vol}_{M}]_{\mathrm{dR}}=\int_{B^{n}}\Psi[\omega]_{\mathrm{dR}}=1.

∎

7.
Examples of pairs
(N,\omega)
which are not quasiregularly elliptic

In this section, we show that Theorem
1.8
yields obstructions for non-constant quasiregular curves from the Euclidean space to product manifolds containing
\mathbb{C}P^{2}
with respect to product forms containing the standard symplectic form on
\mathbb{C}P^{2}
. In particular, for pairs
(S_{\nu}\times\mathbb{C}P^{2},\pi_{1}^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{S_{\nu}}\wedge\pi_{2}^{*}\omega_{\mathrm{sym}})
we obtain an obstruction for
\nu\geqslant 8
.

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
\nu\geqslant 8
and let
S_{\nu}=\#^{\nu}(\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})
. Let
\omega_{\mathrm{sym}}\in\Omega^{2}(\mathbb{C}P^{2})
be the standard symplectic form. Let
F\colon\mathbb{R}^{6}\to S_{\nu}\times\mathbb{C}P^{2}
be a quasiregular curve with respect to
\omega_{S_{\nu}}=\pi_{1}^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{S_{\nu}}\wedge\pi_{2}^{*}\omega_{\mathrm{sym}}
. Then
F
is constant.

Proposition
7.1
yields Corollary
1.6
as an immediate consequence.

On the other hand, for pairs
(T_{g}\times\mathbb{C}P^{2},\pi_{1}^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{T_{g}}\wedge\pi_{2}^{*}\omega_{\mathrm{sym}})
we obtain an obstruction for
g\geqslant 2
.

Proposition 7.2
.

Let
g\geqslant 2
and let
T_{g}=\#^{g}(\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{1})
. Let
\omega_{\mathrm{sym}}\in\Omega^{2}(\mathbb{C}P^{2})
be the standard symplectic form. Let
F\colon\mathbb{R}^{4}\to T_{g}\times\mathbb{C}P^{2}
be a quasiregular curve with respect to
\omega_{T_{g}}=\pi_{1}^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{T_{g}}\wedge\pi_{2}^{*}\omega_{\mathrm{sym}}
. Then
F
is constant.

The obstruction in Proposition
7.2
is sharp in the following sense.

Proposition 7.3
.

Let
T_{1}=\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
and let
\omega_{\mathrm{sym}}\in\Omega^{2}(\mathbb{C}P^{2})
be the standard symplectic form. There exists a non-constant map
F\colon\mathbb{R}^{4}\to T_{1}\times\mathbb{C}P^{2}
which is a quasiregular curve with respect to
\omega_{T_{1}}=\pi_{1}^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{T_{1}}\wedge\pi_{2}^{*}\omega_{\mathrm{sym}}
.

Propositions
7.2
and
7.3
together yield immediately Corollary
1.5
.

Before discussing the proofs of Propositions
7.1
,
7.2
, and
7.3
, we briefly recall the definition of the standard symplectic form on a complex projective space. For
m\geqslant 1
,
the standard symplectic form on
\mathbb{C}P^{m}
is the unique form
\omega_{\mathrm{sym}}\in\Omega^{2}(\mathbb{C}P^{m})
satisfying

U^{*}\omega_{\mathrm{sym}}=\omega_{\mathrm{sym}}

for each unitary
(m+1)\times(m+1)
matrix
U\in U(m+1)
and

\iota^{*}\omega_{\mathrm{sym}}=\mathrm{vol}_{\mathbb{C}P^{1}},

where
\iota\colon\mathbb{C}P^{1}\to\mathbb{C}P^{m}
is the inclusion
[z_{0}:z_{1}]\mapsto[z_{0}:z_{1}:0:\cdots:0]
in homogeneous coordinates; see e.g.
[
15
, Section 3.3.2]
for an explicit formula of
\omega_{\mathrm{sym}}
.

Construction of a quasiregular
\omega_{T_{1}}
-curve
\mathbb{R}^{4}\to T_{1}\times\mathbb{C}P^{2}

In preparation for the proof of Proposition
7.3
, we show that the natural inclusion
\mathbb{C}P^{1}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}P^{2}
is a quasiregular curve with respect to the standard symplectic form.

Lemma 7.4
.

Let
1\leqslant i\leqslant\ell
and let
\iota\colon\mathbb{C}P^{i}\to\mathbb{C}P^{\ell}
be the inclusion
[z_{0}:\cdots:z_{i}]\mapsto[z_{0}:\cdots:z_{i}:0:\cdots:0]
. Let
\omega_{\mathrm{sym}}\in\Omega^{2}(\mathbb{C}P^{\ell})
be the standard symplectic form on
\mathbb{C}P^{\ell}
. Then
\iota
is a
1
-quasiregular curve with respect to
\omega_{\mathrm{sym}}^{\wedge i}=\omega_{\mathrm{sym}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega_{\mathrm{sym}}\in\Omega^{2i}(\mathbb{C}P^{\ell})
.

Proof.

The claim follows immediately since
\iota^{*}\omega_{\mathrm{sym}}\in\Omega^{2}(\mathbb{C}P^{i})
is the standard symplectic form on
\mathbb{C}P^{i}
.
∎

We also show that mappings of bounded length distortion
\mathbb{R}^{n}\to N_{i}
induce quasiregular curves
(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{m}\to N_{1}\times\cdots\times N_{m}
; we refer to
[
10
]
and
[
7
]
for more discussion on BLD-maps.

Lemma 7.5
.

Let
n\geqslant 2
and let
N_{1},\ldots,N_{m}
be connected and oriented Riemannian manifolds with
\dim N_{i}\geqslant n
. Let
\omega_{i}\in\Omega^{n}(N_{i})
be closed forms satisfying
\lVert\pi_{1}^{*}\omega_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\pi_{m}^{*}\omega_{m}\rVert\leqslant 1
. Let
L\geqslant m
and let
\psi_{i}\in W_{\operatorname{loc}}^{1,n}(\mathbb{R}^{n},N_{i})
be maps satisfying
\lVert D\psi_{i}\rVert\leqslant L
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\star\psi_{i}^{*}\omega_{i}\geqslant L^{-n}
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then the map
\psi\colon(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{m}\to N_{1}\times\cdots\times N_{m}
,
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})\mapsto(\psi_{1}(x_{1}),\ldots,\psi_{m}(x_{m}))
, is a
(L^{4n})^{m}
-quasiregular curve with respect to
\pi_{1}^{*}\omega_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\pi_{m}^{*}\omega_{m}
.

Proof.

For a.e.
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})\in(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{m}
, we have

\displaystyle\lVert D\psi(x)\rVert^{nm}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lVert D\psi_{i}(x_{i})\rVert\right)^{nm}\leqslant m^{nm}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lVert D\psi_{i}(x_{i})\rVert^{nm}

\displaystyle\leqslant m^{nm+1}L^{nm}\leqslant L^{3nm}\leqslant L^{4nm}\prod_{i=1}^{m}(\star\psi_{i}^{*}\omega_{i})(x_{i})

\displaystyle=L^{4nm}(\star\psi^{*}(\pi_{1}^{*}\omega_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\pi_{m}^{*}\omega_{m}))(x).

Since
(\pi_{1}^{*}\omega_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\pi_{m}^{*}\omega_{m})(\psi(x))\neq 0
for a.e.
x\in(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{m}
, the claim follows.
∎

We are now ready to prove Proposition
7.3
.

Proof of Proposition
7.3
.

Let
\exp\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\to T_{1}
be the covering map
(x,y)\mapsto(e^{2\pi ix},e^{2\pi iy})
. Let
A\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}P^{1}
be an Alexander map (for the construction see e.g.
[
9
, Section 6.5.4]
) and let
L\geqslant 1
a constant satisfying
\lVert DA\rVert\leqslant L
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
\star A^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{\mathbb{C}P^{1}}\geqslant L^{-2}
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Let
\iota\colon\mathbb{C}P^{1}\to\mathbb{C}P^{2}
be the inclusion
[z_{0}:z_{1}]\mapsto[z_{0}:z_{1}:0]
. Then, by Lemma
7.4
,

\lVert D(\iota\circ A)\rVert\leqslant\lVert D\iota\circ A\rVert\lVert DA\rVert\leqslant L

a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and

\star(\iota\circ A)^{*}\omega_{\mathrm{sym}}=\star A^{*}\iota^{*}\omega_{\mathrm{sym}}=\star A^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{\mathbb{C}P^{1}}\geqslant L^{-2}

a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Thus, by Lemma
7.5
, the map

F\colon\mathbb{R}^{4}\to T_{1}\times\mathbb{C}P^{2},\quad(x,y,z,w)\mapsto(\exp(x,y),\iota(A(z,w))),

is a quasiregular curve with respect to
\pi_{1}^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{T_{1}}\wedge\pi_{2}^{*}\omega_{\mathrm{sym}}
. Since
F
is non-constant, this concludes the proof.
∎

Obstruction for quasiregular
\omega_{T_{g}}
-curves
\mathbb{R}^{4}\to T_{g}\times\mathbb{C}P^{2}

The quasiregular
\omega_{T_{1}}
-curve
\mathbb{R}^{4}\to T_{1}\times\mathbb{C}P^{2}
constructed in the proof of Proposition
7.3
arose from a pair
(\exp,\iota\circ A)
, where
\exp\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\to T_{1}
and
\iota\circ A\colon\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}P^{2}
are quasiregular maps. By the classical quasiconformal theory, there does not exist a quasiregular map
\mathbb{R}^{2}\to T_{g}
if
g\geqslant 2
, so a priori Proposition
7.2
, we know that a quasiregular
\omega_{T_{g}}
-curve
\mathbb{R}^{4}\to T_{g}\times\mathbb{C}P^{2}
cannot be constructed using quasiregular coordinate maps. Proposition
7.2
shows that there does not exist any method of constructing a non-constant quasiregular
\omega_{T_{g}}
-curve
\mathbb{R}^{4}\to T_{g}\times\mathbb{C}P^{2}
.

The proof of Proposition
7.2
is based on combining the following elementary algebraic lemma with Theorem
1.8
.

Lemma 7.6
.

Let
N
be a smooth manifold and let
\Phi\colon H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(N)\to\bigwedge^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a graded algebra homomorphism. Let
k,k^{\prime}>0
. Suppose
c\in H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k}(N)
and
c^{\prime}\in H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k^{\prime}}(N)
are de Rham classes satisfying
\Phi c\wedge\Phi c^{\prime}\neq 0
. Suppose
c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}\in H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{1}(N)
are de Rham classes for which
c\wedge c_{i}=0
for
i=1,\ldots,m
. Suppose
c_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,c_{m}^{\prime}\in H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k-1}(N)
are de Rham classes satisfying
c_{i}\wedge c_{\ell}^{\prime}=\delta_{i\ell}c
, where
\delta_{i\ell}c
is the Kronecker delta. Then
m<n
.

Proof.

For every
(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus\{0\}
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}\Phi c_{i}\right)\wedge\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\lambda_{\ell}\Phi c_{\ell}^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,\ell=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}\lambda_{\ell}\Phi c_{i}\wedge\Phi c_{\ell}^{\prime}=\sum_{i,\ell=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}\lambda_{\ell}\Phi(c_{i}\wedge c_{\ell}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}^{2}\Phi c=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}^{2}\right)\Phi c\neq 0,

so
\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}\Phi c_{i}\neq 0
. We conclude that
\Phi c_{1},\ldots,\Phi c_{m}\in\bigwedge^{1}\mathbb{R}^{n}
are linearly independent, which yields that
m\leqslant\dim\bigwedge^{1}\mathbb{R}^{n}=n
.

Suppose now that
m=n
. Then
\Phi c_{1},\ldots,\Phi c_{m}
is a basis of
\bigwedge^{1}\mathbb{R}^{n}
and hence
\{\Phi c_{i_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\Phi c_{i_{k^{\prime}}}\}_{I}
is a basis of
\bigwedge^{k^{\prime}}\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Since
\Phi c^{\prime}\in\bigwedge^{k^{\prime}}\mathbb{R}^{n}
, there exist coefficients
\mu_{I}\in\mathbb{R}
satisfying

\Phi c^{\prime}=\sum_{I}\mu_{I}\Phi c_{i_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\Phi c_{i_{k^{\prime}}}.

Hence, we arrive at a contradiction

\displaystyle\Phi c\wedge\Phi c^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\Phi c\wedge\left(\sum_{I}\mu_{I}\Phi c_{i_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\Phi c_{i_{k^{\prime}}}\right)=\sum_{I}\mu_{I}\Phi c\wedge\Phi c_{i_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\Phi c_{i_{k^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{I}\mu_{I}\Phi(c\wedge c_{i_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge c_{i_{k^{\prime}}})=0.

∎

We are now ready to prove Proposition
7.2
.

Proof of Proposition
7.2
.

Suppose that
F
is non-constant. By Theorem
2.3
, we have
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}F^{*}\omega_{T_{g}}=\infty
. Thus, by Theorem
1.8
, there exists a graded algebra homomorphism
\Phi\colon H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(T_{g}\times\mathbb{C}P^{2})\to\bigwedge^{*}\mathbb{R}^{4}
for which
\Phi[\omega_{T_{g}}]_{\mathrm{dR}}\neq 0
.

Let
c_{1},\ldots,c_{2g}
be basis of
H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{1}(T_{g})
satisfying
c_{i}\wedge c_{i+1}=[\mathrm{vol}_{T_{g}}]_{\mathrm{dR}}
for
i=1,3,\ldots,2g-1
and
c_{i}\wedge c_{\ell}=0
for every pair
(i,\ell)\in\{1,\ldots,2g\}^{2}
with
|2\ell-2i-1|>1
. Denote
c_{i}^{\prime}=c_{i+1}
for
i=1,3,\ldots,2g-1
and
c_{i}^{\prime}=-c_{i-1}
for
i=2,4,\ldots,2g
. Since

\Phi\pi_{1}^{*}[\mathrm{vol}_{T_{g}}]_{\mathrm{dR}}\wedge\Phi\pi_{2}^{*}[\omega_{\mathrm{sym}}]_{\mathrm{dR}}=\Phi[\omega_{T_{g}}]_{\mathrm{dR}}\neq 0,

\pi_{1}^{*}[\mathrm{vol}_{T_{g}}]_{\mathrm{dR}}\wedge\pi_{1}^{*}c_{i}=\pi_{1}^{*}([\mathrm{vol}_{T_{g}}]_{\mathrm{dR}}\wedge c_{i})=0,

and

\pi_{1}^{*}c_{i}\wedge\pi_{1}^{*}c_{\ell}^{\prime}=\pi_{1}^{*}(c_{i}\wedge c_{\ell}^{\prime})=\pi_{1}^{*}(\delta_{i\ell}[\mathrm{vol}_{T_{g}}]_{\mathrm{dR}})=\delta_{i\ell}\pi_{1}^{*}[\mathrm{vol}_{T_{g}}]_{\mathrm{dR}},

Lemma
7.6
yields that
2g<4
. This is a contradiction since
g\geqslant 2
.
∎

Obstruction for quasiregular
\omega_{S_{\nu}}
-curves
\mathbb{R}^{4}\to S_{\nu}\times\mathbb{C}P^{2}

Proposition
7.1
follows from Theorem
1.8
and the following elementary observation. Since the proof is similar to the proof of Lemma
7.6
, we omit the details.

Lemma 7.7
.

Let
N
be a smooth manifold and let
\Phi\colon H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(N)\to\bigwedge^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a graded algebra homomorphism. Let
k\geqslant 2
and suppose
c\in H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k}(N)
is a de Rham class satisfying
\Phi c\neq 0
. Let
1\leqslant k^{\prime}\leqslant k-1
. Suppose
c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}\in H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k^{\prime}}(N)
and
c_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,c_{m}^{\prime}\in H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{k-k^{\prime}}(N)
are de Rham classes for which
c_{i}\wedge c_{\ell}^{\prime}=\delta_{i\ell}c
, where
\delta_{i\ell}c
is the Kronecker delta. Then
m\leqslant\binom{n}{k^{\prime}}
.

Proof of Proposition
7.1
.

Suppose that
F
is non-constant. By Theorem
2.3
, we have
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}F^{*}\omega_{S_{\nu}}=\infty
. Thus, by Theorem
1.8
, there exists a graded algebra homomorphism
\Phi\colon H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}(S_{\nu}\times\mathbb{C}P^{2})\to\bigwedge^{*}\mathbb{R}^{6}
for which
\Phi[\omega_{S_{\nu}}]_{\mathrm{dR}}\neq 0
.

Let
c_{1},\ldots,c_{2\nu}
be basis of
H_{\mathrm{dR}}^{2}(S_{\nu})
satisfying
c_{i}\wedge c_{i+1}=[\mathrm{vol}_{S_{\nu}}]_{\mathrm{dR}}
for
i=1,3,\ldots,2\nu-1
and
c_{i}\wedge c_{\ell}=0
for every pair
(i,\ell)\in\{1,\ldots,2\nu\}^{2}
with
|2\ell-2i-1|>1
. Denote
c_{i}^{\prime}=c_{i+1}
for
i=1,3,\ldots,2\nu-1
and
c_{i}^{\prime}=c_{i-1}
for
i=2,4,\ldots,2\nu
. Since
\Phi\pi_{1}^{*}[\mathrm{vol}_{S_{\nu}}]_{\mathrm{dR}}\neq 0
and

\pi_{1}^{*}c_{i}\wedge\pi_{1}^{*}c_{\ell}^{\prime}=\pi_{1}^{*}(c_{i}\wedge c_{\ell}^{\prime})=\pi_{1}^{*}(\delta_{i\ell}[\mathrm{vol}_{S_{\nu}}]_{\mathrm{dR}})=\delta_{i\ell}\pi_{1}^{*}[\mathrm{vol}_{S_{\nu}}]_{\mathrm{dR}},

Lemma
7.7
yields that
2\nu\leqslant\binom{6}{2}=15
. This is a contradiction since
\nu\geqslant 8
.
∎

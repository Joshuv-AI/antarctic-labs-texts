Abstract
. Given a finite connected graph Λ \Lambda , the space of S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) SU(2) lattice gauge-fields on Λ \Lambda , modulo gauge transformations, is a Lagrangian submanifold – with mild singularities – of the S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) SU(2) character variety (= phase-space of Chern-Simons theory) of an associated surface. We present evidence that, in the limit of large Λ \Lambda , integration over the character variety with respect to the Liouville measure approximates lattice-theoretic integrals. By the works of W. Goldman, L. Jeffrey and J. Weitsman, the formalism of Duistermaat-Heckman applies to the relevant integrals over the character variety. A continuous version of the Verlinde algebra facilitates computations. In two dimensions we recover standard expressions. For the theory on a 3-dimensional periodic lattice Λ \Lambda with Migdal action we get a very pleasant expression for the symplectic partition function, and with the Wilson action a more elaborate one. Each is a sum of a series with positive terms. One can also write down expressions for plaquette-plaquette correlations and ’t Hooft loops.

3.1.

We recall some basic constructions whose natural context is the hyperbolic geometry of surfaces and three-manifold topology. Here we are concerned mostly with elementary topological aspects.

(which we omit from the notation),

/SU(2)

{\tilde{\gamma}}

to the trace.

3.3.
Poisson brackets of Wilson loops

Poisson brackets between the functions
W_{\tilde{\gamma}}
were computed by W. Goldman. We recall the formulae in the cases of interest. Let
{\tilde{\gamma}}
and
{\tilde{\eta}}
be two closed
oriented
loops.

•

If
{\tilde{\gamma}}
and
{\tilde{\eta}}
are disjoint, or can be isotoped to be so, then
\{W_{\tilde{\gamma}},W_{\tilde{\eta}}\}=0
, and

•

If
{\tilde{\gamma}}
and
{\tilde{\eta}}
intersect transversally, then

(6)

\{W_{\tilde{\gamma}},W_{\tilde{\eta}}\}=\sum_{p\in{\tilde{\gamma}}\cap{\tilde{\eta}}}\epsilon_{p}({\tilde{\gamma}},{\tilde{\eta}})\big(W_{{\tilde{\gamma}}\#_{p}{\tilde{\eta}}}-\frac{1}{2}W_{\tilde{\gamma}}W_{\tilde{\eta}}\big)

Here
\epsilon_{p}({\tilde{\gamma}},{\tilde{\eta}})=\pm 1
is the oriented intersection number of
{\tilde{\gamma}}
and
{\tilde{\eta}}
at
p
, and
{\tilde{\gamma}}\#_{p}{\tilde{\eta}}
is the closed loop obtained by concatenating
{\tilde{\gamma}}
and
{\tilde{\eta}}
at
p
.

Note that the intersection number changes sign if the orientation on
one
of the loops is reversed:
{\tilde{\gamma}}\mapsto{\tilde{\gamma}}^{\prime}
. This is consistent because on the one hand the Wilson loop function does not change if the loop flips orientation, but

W_{{\tilde{\gamma}}\#_{p}{\tilde{\eta}}}+W_{{\tilde{\gamma}}^{\prime}\#_{p}{\tilde{\eta}}}=W_{\tilde{\gamma}}W_{\tilde{\eta}}

because of the identity

trace(\mathtt{g}_{1}\mathtt{g}_{2})+trace(\mathtt{g}^{-1}_{1}\mathtt{g}_{2})=trace(\mathtt{g}_{1})trace(\mathtt{g}_{2})

that holds for
\mathtt{g}_{1},\ \mathtt{g}_{2}
in
SU(2)
.

As a consequence, the Hamiltonian vector fields generated by Wilson loop functions corresponding to disjoint loops commute. Goldman gave a geometric description of these flows, and this has the consequence, beautifully exploited by L. Jeffrey and J. Weitsman, that a pants decomposition of
\Sigma
yields a Hamiltonian action of the compact torus
U(1)^{3({\mathsf{b}}-1)}
on
{\mathcal{M}}
, the map
\mathbf{t}
being essentially the moment map.

Although the hypotheses of Duistermaat-Heckman theorem do quite hold (because of the singularities of
{\mathcal{M}}
), its conclusions (as summarised in item
(f)
of the Introduction) hold. For a clear account, see
[
2
]
.

4.
Comparison of integrals on
\mathbf{M}
and
{\mathcal{M}}

Given a closed, non-self-interesting path
\gamma
on the lattice
\Lambda
, and any real-valued continuous function
f_{1}
on

\begin{split}\int f_{1}(\pi t_{\gamma}(g)){\mathcal{D}}g&=\int f_{1}(\arccos{\frac{1}{2}trace{H_{\gamma}(g)}}){\mathcal{D}}g\\
&\underset{*}{=}\int_{G}f_{1}(\arccos{\frac{1}{2}trace(\mathtt{g})})d\mathtt{g}\\

Distribution of one Wilson loop.

Let us consider a simple closed curve that is neither contractible nor separating. Without loss of generality we can suppose that it is one of the bounding circles (say,
{\tilde{\gamma}}_{1}

{\tilde{\Lambda}}
with associated trinion graph

I_{f_{1}}\equiv\frac{\int_{{\mathcal{P}}_{{\mathcal{T}}_{\tilde{\Lambda}}}}f_{1}(\pi t_{1})d\mathbf{t}}{\int_{{\mathcal{P}}_{{\mathcal{T}}_{\tilde{\Lambda}}}}1d\mathbf{t}}

We use the identity
4
and conclude that

\begin{split}I_{f_{1}}&=\frac{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(\frac{\sqrt{2}}{(n+1)\pi})^{2({\mathsf{b}}-1)}\int_{0}^{1}f_{1}(\pi t_{1})\vec{u}_{n}^{2}(t_{1})dt_{1}}{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(\frac{\sqrt{2}}{(n+1)\pi})^{2({\mathsf{b}}-1)}}\\

\begin{split}I_{f_{1}}&=\frac{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(\frac{\sqrt{2}}{(n+1)\pi})^{2}\int_{0}^{1}f_{1}(\pi t_{1})2\sin^{2}{(n+1)\pi t_{1}}dt_{1}}{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(\frac{\sqrt{2}}{(n+1)\pi})^{2}}\\

\end{split}

\begin{split}I_{f_{1}}&=\frac{\int_{{\mathcal{P}}}f_{1}(\pi t_{1})dt_{1}dt_{2}dt_{3}}{\int_{{\mathcal{P}}}dt_{1}dt_{2}dt_{3}}\\

On the other hand, as

6.3.
Symplectic partition function with the Wilson action

Refer to the cubical lattice in Figure
16
. This is the “contracted trinion graph”,
\Gamma
. The set of vertices of
\Gamma
is denoted
{{\mathbb{V}}}_{\Gamma}
and the set of (unoriented) edges
{{\mathbb{V}}}_{\Gamma}
.

Each vertex has an associated index
n_{v}
. Then the partition function of the symplectic theory is

(9)

\begin{split}Z_{\mathbf{e}}=&\sum_{n_{v}=0}^{\infty}\prod_{v\in{{\mathbb{V}}}_{\Gamma}}(\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\pi(n_{v}+1)})^{4}\\
&\times\prod_{e\in{{\mathbb{E}}}_{\Gamma}}\int_{0}^{1}\vec{u}_{n_{v_{1}(e)}}(t_{e})\vec{u}_{n_{v_{2}(e)}}(t_{e})\exp{\{2(\frac{2}{\mathbf{e}^{2}})\cos{\pi t_{e}}\}}\ dt_{e}\end{split}

where
v_{1}(e)
and
v_{2}(e)
are the two vertices at either end of the (unoriented) edge
e
.

Recall that
\vec{u}_{l}(t)=\sqrt{2}{\hat{\chi}}_{l}(\pi t)\sin{\pi t}
and
2\cos{\pi t}={\hat{\chi}}_{1}(\pi t)
. We will also use the relations:

(a)

{\hat{\chi}}_{p}{\hat{\chi}}_{q}={\hat{\chi}}_{|p-q|}+{\hat{\chi}}_{|p-q|+2}+\dots+{\hat{\chi}}_{p+q}\equiv{\widetilde{\sum}}_{r=|p-q|}^{|p+q|}{\hat{\chi}}_{r}
, where we have introduced the notation
{\widetilde{\sum}}
for the sum that skips every alternate term in the usual sum
\sum
.

(b)

{\hat{\chi}}_{1}^{p}=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor p/2\rfloor}N_{p,k}{\hat{\chi}}_{p-2k}
, where

N_{p,k}={}^{n}C_{n-k}-{}^{n}C_{n-k-1}
are multiplicities, and hence non-negative integers.

With all this, the contribution of an edge
e
evaluates to:

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{1}\vec{u}_{n_{v_{1}(e)}}(t_{e})\vec{u}_{n_{v_{2}(e)}}(t_{e})\exp{\{2(\frac{2}{\mathbf{e}^{2}})\cos{\pi t_{e}}\}}\ dt_{e}\\
&={\widetilde{\sum}}_{l_{e}=|n_{v_{1}(e)}-n_{v_{2}(e)}|}^{n_{v_{1}(e)}+n_{v_{2}(e)}}\ \ \sum_{m_{e}=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\frac{2}{\mathbf{e}^{2}})^{m_{e}}}{m_{e}!}\int_{0}^{1}{\hat{\chi}}_{l_{e}}(\pi t_{e}){\hat{\chi}}_{1}^{m_{e}}(\pi t_{e}){\widetilde{dt}}_{e}\\
&={\widetilde{\sum}}_{l_{e}=|n_{v_{1}(e)}-n_{v_{2}(e)}|}^{n_{v_{1}(e)}+n_{v_{2}(e)}}\ \ \sum_{m_{e}=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\frac{2}{\mathbf{e}^{2}})^{m_{e}}}{m_{e}!}\int_{0}^{1}{\hat{\chi}}_{l_{e}}(\pi t_{e})\sum_{k_{e}=0}^{\lfloor m_{e}/2\rfloor}N_{m_{e},k_{e}}{\hat{\chi}}_{m_{e}-2k_{e}}(\pi t_{e}){\widetilde{dt}}_{e}\\
&={\widetilde{\sum}}_{l_{e}=|n_{v_{1}(e)}-n_{v_{2}(e)}|}^{n_{v_{1}(e)}+n_{v_{2}(e)}}\sum_{m_{e}=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\frac{2}{\mathbf{e}^{2}})^{m_{e}}}{m_{e}!}\sum_{k_{e}=0}^{\lfloor m_{e}/2\rfloor}N_{m_{e},k_{e}}\delta_{l_{e},m_{e}-2k_{e}}\\
&={\widetilde{\sum}}_{l_{e}=|n_{v_{1}(e)}-n_{v_{2}(e)}|}^{n_{v_{1}(e)}+n_{v_{2}(e)}}\sum_{k_{e}=0}^{\infty}\frac{{(\frac{2}{\mathbf{e}^{2}})^{l_{e}+2k_{e}}}}{(l_{e}+2k_{e})!}N_{l_{e}+2k_{e},k_{e}}\\
\end{split}

With this, the partition function evaluates to:

(10)

\begin{split}Z_{\mathbf{e}}=&\sum_{n_{v}=0}^{\infty}\prod_{v\in{{\mathbb{V}}}_{\Gamma}}(\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\pi(n_{v}+1)})^{4}\\
&\times\prod_{e\in{{\mathbb{E}}}_{\Gamma}}\{{\widetilde{\sum}}_{l_{e}=|n_{v_{1}(e)}-n_{v_{2}(e)}|}^{n_{v_{1}(e)}+n_{v_{2}(e)}}\sum_{k_{e}=0}^{\infty}\frac{{(\frac{2}{\mathbf{e}^{2}})^{l_{e}+2k_{e}}}}{(l_{e}+2k_{e})!}N_{l_{e}+2k_{e},k_{e}}\}\\
\end{split}

(Recall that
v_{1}(e)
and
v_{2}(e)
are the two vertices at either end of the (unoriented) edge
e
.) This has the advantage that it is a sum of positive terms.

6.4.
The Migdal action

The weight

\exp{\{{\frac{2}{\mathbf{e}^{2}}\sum_{plaquettes\ P}W_{P}(g)}\}}=\prod_{plaquettes\ P}\exp{\{{\frac{2}{\mathbf{e}^{2}}W_{P}(g)}\}}

in the lattice path integral has the following qualitative behaviour: as
\mathbf{e}\to\infty
the weight function tends to (the constant function) 1, so that the measure tends to
{\mathcal{D}}g
, and as
\mathbf{e}\to 0
, the (normalised probability) measure concentrates around (gauge-equivalence class of) the gauge-field configuration
g_{l}=e
for every oriented link
l
. The Migdal recipe replaces the weight with

\prod_{plaquettes\ P}\{\sum_{l_{P}=0}^{\infty}\exp{\{-\frac{l_{P}(l_{P}+1){\mathbf{e}^{\prime}}^{2}}{2}\}}(l_{P}+1)\chi_{l_{P}}(H_{P}(g))\}

which preserves the qualitative behaviour. The “bare coupling constants”
\mathbf{e},\mathbf{e}^{\prime}
will have a complicated relationship depending on normalisation. In
d=2
, this replacement makes the lattice theory explicitly solvable on an arbitrary surface (
[
13
]
).

6.5.
Symplectic partition function with the Migdal action

With the Migdal action the contribution of an edge
e
is:

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{1}\vec{u}_{n_{v_{1}(e)}}(t_{e})\vec{u}_{n_{v_{2}(e)}}(t_{e})\{\sum_{0}^{\infty}\exp{\{-\frac{l_{e}(l_{e}+1){\mathbf{e}^{\prime}}^{2}}{2}\}}(l_{e}+1){\hat{\chi}}_{l_{e}}(\pi t_{e})\}dt_{e}\\
&=\sum_{l_{e}}\exp{\{-\frac{l_{e}(l_{e}+1)}{2{\mathbf{e}^{\prime}}^{2}}\}}(l_{e}+1)\int_{0}^{1}{\hat{\chi}}_{n_{v_{1}(e)}}(\pi t_{e}){\hat{\chi}}_{n_{v_{2}(e)}}(\pi t_{e}){\hat{\chi}}_{l_{e}}(\pi t_{e}){\widetilde{dt}}_{e}\\
&={\widetilde{\sum}}_{l_{e}=|n_{v_{1}(e)}-n_{v_{2}(e)}|}^{n_{v_{1}(e)}+n_{v_{2}(e)}}\exp{\{-\frac{l_{e}(l_{e}+1){\mathbf{e}^{\prime}}^{2}}{2}\}}(l_{e}+1)\end{split}

This gives the expression for the symplectic partition function with Migdal action:

\begin{split}Z^{\prime}_{\mathbf{e}^{\prime}}=&\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{v}=0\\
v\in{{\mathbb{V}}}_{\Gamma}\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\ \ \prod_{v\in{{\mathbb{V}}}_{\Gamma}}(\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\pi(n_{v}+1)})^{4}\\
&\times\prod_{e\in{{\mathbb{E}}}_{\Gamma}}\{{\widetilde{\sum}}_{l_{e}=|n_{v_{1}(e)}-n_{v_{2}(e)}|}^{n_{v_{1}(e)}+n_{v_{2}(e)}}\exp{\{-\frac{l_{e}(l_{e}+1){\mathbf{e}^{\prime}}^{2}}{2}\}}(l_{e}+1)\}\end{split}

This too is a sum of positive terms.

Unlike the case

7.2.
U(1)

In fact, the above strategy works for
U(1)
. The space of flat
U(1)
connections on
\mathbf{S}
modulo
U(1)
gauge transformations can be identified with the
2{\mathsf{b}}
-dimensional (real) torus

\mathbf{S}

U(1)

\Lambda

Consider briefly the case of

Bernoulli Polynomials

We follow the conventions in Hans Rademacher:
Topics in analytic number theory

:
https://arxiv.org/abs/1309.7560
.

The Bernoulli polynomials
B_{0},B_{1},\dots,
can be defined by the conditions

(1)

B_{0}=1

(2)

B^{\prime}_{q}(x)=qB_{q-1}(x)

(3)

\int_{0}^{1}B_{q}(x)dx=0

The Bernoulli number
b_{k}
is defined by
b_{k}=B_{k}(0)
.

It can be shown that

(1)

B_{1}(x)=x-1/2
,
B_{2}(x)=x^{2}-x+1/6
, and more generally
B_{q}
is a degree
q
polynomial with rational coefficients.

(2)

B_{q+1}(x+1)-B_{q+1}(x)=(q+1)x^{q}
, for
q\geq 0
.

(3)

On the interval
[0,1)
we have:

B_{2{\mathsf{b}}-1}(x)=(-1)^{{\mathsf{b}}}\frac{2(2{\mathsf{b}}-1)!}{(2\pi)^{2{\mathsf{b}}-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\sin(2\pi kx)}{k^{2{\mathsf{b}}-1}}\ \textup{for ${\mathsf{b}}>1$}

and

B_{2{\mathsf{b}}}(x)=(-1)^{{\mathsf{b}}+1}\frac{2(2{\mathsf{b}})!}{(2\pi)^{2{\mathsf{b}}}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\cos(2\pi kx)}{k^{2{\mathsf{b}}}}\ \textup{for ${\mathsf{b}}\geq 1$}\ .

(4)

As a consequence, on
[0,1)
and hence everywhere

\begin{split}B_{2{\mathsf{b}}-1}(1-x)&=(-1)^{\mathsf{b}}\frac{2(2{\mathsf{b}}-1)!}{(2\pi)^{2{\mathsf{b}}-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\sin(2\pi k-2\pi kx)}{k^{2{\mathsf{b}}-1}}\\
&=-(-1)^{\mathsf{b}}\frac{2(2{\mathsf{b}}-1)!}{(2\pi)^{2{\mathsf{b}}-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\sin(2\pi kx)}{k^{2{\mathsf{b}}-1}}\\
&=-B_{2{\mathsf{b}}-1}(x)\end{split}

and similarly

B_{2{\mathsf{b}}}(1-x)=B_{2{\mathsf{b}}}(x)

In the topology and algebraic geometry literature“Bernoulli Polynomials”
P_{q}
are used, which are related to the
B_{k}
by

q!P_{q}=B_{q}

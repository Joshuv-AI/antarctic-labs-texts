A three-field Multiscale Method
Franklin de Barros, Alexandre L. Madureira, Frédéric Valentin

Abstract
. Inspired by the seminal paper A Three-Field Domain Decomposition Method by F. Brezzi and L. D. Marini, we propose the Multiscale-Hybrid-Hybrid Method (MH 2 M) for the Darcy problem. After a sequence of formal manipulations, the resulting multiscale finite element method leads to a symmetric positive definite formulation posed solely in terms of the trace variable. We establish stability and convergence results for a family of finite element spaces and highlight connections with other multiscale finite element methods.



1.
Introduction

Simulation of fluid flow in heterogeneous domains, as found in oil reservoirs, transport of contaminants and water resources issues, is a big challenge since fine meshes are needed to capture the multiscale solution. The Darcy equation is the most representative model in the field of porous media, which in its primary formulation is composed of a Poisson equation with a multiscale coefficient. Precisely, the Poisson problem consists of finding the weak solution
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
of

(1)

\begin{gathered}-\operatorname{div}\mathcal{A}\operatorname{\boldsymbol{\operatorname{\nabla}}}u=f\quad\text{in }\Omega,\\
u=0\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{gathered}

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
for
d=2
or
3
is an open bounded domain with a polyhedral Lipschitz boundary
\partial\Omega
, and
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. The symmetric tensor
\mathcal{A}\in[L^{\infty}(\Omega)]_{\text{sym}}^{d\times d}
is uniformly positive definite and let the positive constants
a_{\text{min}}
and
a_{\text{max}}
be such that

(2)

a_{\text{min}}|{\boldsymbol{v}}|^{2}\leq\mathcal{A}({\boldsymbol{x}})\,{\boldsymbol{v}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{v}}\leq a_{\text{max}}|{\boldsymbol{v}}|^{2}\quad\text{for all }{\boldsymbol{v}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

for almost all
{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\Omega
. The tensor
\mathcal{A}
may include multiple scales.

The flow velocity, represented by the pressure gradient, is usually the quantity of interest. To approximate it, the mixed version of Darcy’s equation is preferable to its elliptic (
1
) version, for which the classical inf-sup stable pairs of finite elements are the Raviart-Thomas element (RT
k
)
[
43
]

or the element Brezzi-Douglas-Marini (BDM

)
[
14
,
11
]
, which prescribes the continuity of the
normal velocity component combined with discontinuous pressure interpolations. In addition to being stable, these finite elements preserve local mass, a fundamental property in practical engineering applications. However, since such choices produce linear systems that represent
saddle point problems, efficient solver are harder to come by
[
15
]
.
A way to circumvent this problem is by using hybridization
[
11
]
techniques, which start with a discontinuous version of a stable element (RT
k
or BDM

), forcing the weak continuity of the normal components through Lagrange multipliers. Velocity and pressure are now fully discontinuous and can be eliminated at the element level, leading to a positive definite symmetrical system where the only unknowns are the Lagrange multipliers
[
4
]

Hybridization has also been used to construct and analyze multiscale methods, which have become an attractive option for dealing with primal or mixed weak forms of (
1
) when the standard Galerkin method based on continuous polynomial interpolation becomes computationally expensive. The multiscale methods share the commonality of having global problems built on the solution of local problems that scale up submesh structures. An attractive feature is that the global problem dimension is multiscale independent, and the basis functions are independent of each other and can be computed in parallel. An hybridization of the primal form of (
1

[

yields the

(MHM for short) family of methods

[

[

[

[

[

In this work, we follow the strategy used to build the MHM method, which starts at the continuous level placed on a coarse partition. It consists of decomposing the exact solution into local and global contributions that, when discretized, dissociate local and global problems: the global formulation is defined on the skeleton of the coarse partition, yielding the degrees of freedom; the local problems provide the multiscale basis functions and their computation is embarrassingly parallel as the local problems are independent of each other. However, the MHM method for (
1
) has a saddle point structure similar to Galerkin’s method for the mixed form of (
1
). Notably, its lower order version generalizes the Galerkin’s method with the element RT
0
in simplexes for the case of discretized multiscale coefficient problems with polytopal meshes
[
6
]
.

This work revisits the three field method of F. Brezzi and D. Marini
[
17
]
in the context of the MHM method. Specifically, we relax the continuity of the flow variable
\lambda
and weakly enforce it through the action of a second Lagrange multiplier
\rho
. Then, we decompose the broken infinite dimensional spaces where
u
and
\lambda
belong in such a way that the discretization results in a new multiscale finite element method that

•

induces a definite positive symmetric global linear system to compute the degrees of freedom. It differs from the original MHM method, which induces a linear saddle point system;

•

approximates the flux variable
\lambda
using a multiscale flux basis obtained from a Dirichlet-to-Neumann operator defined from new local Neumann problems driven by the basis functions of
\rho
. This feature is new when compared to the MHM method strategy where the basis for
\lambda
is chosen in an ad-hoc fashion and “ignores” underlying physical properties;

•

imposes weak continuity of discrete primal (pressure) and dual (flow) variables on the skeleton of the coarse partition, with a discrete flow that is in local equilibrium with external forces.

In addition to the construction of the new multiscale method, called the Multiscale Hybrid-Hybrid Method (MH
2
M for short), the main contributions of this work are:

(i)

demonstrate the well-posedness and the best approximation results for the MH

(ii)

propose families of interpolation spaces that fulfill the conditions in
(i)
and for which the optimal convergence of MH

(iii)

bridge the MH
2

[

26
]
;

(iv)

numerical validation and comparison between MsFEM, MHM, and the proposed method, with particular emphasis on the number of global degrees of freedom required to achieve a given error level.

Hybridization

\sum_{\tau\in{\mathcal{T}_{H}}}\int_{\tau}\mathcal{A}\operatorname{\boldsymbol{\operatorname{\nabla}}}TG\rho\cdot\operatorname{\boldsymbol{\operatorname{\nabla}}}TG\xi=\langle G\rho,TG\xi\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{H}}=\langle G\rho,\xi\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{H}},

for all

Thus, using (

=\sum_{\tau\in{\mathcal{T}_{H}}}\int_{\partial\tau}\lambda^{0}c_{\xi}+\sum_{\tau\in{\mathcal{T}_{H}}}\int_{\tau}fTG\xi=-\sum_{\tau\in{\mathcal{T}_{H}}}\int_{\tau}fc_{\xi}+\sum_{\tau\in{\mathcal{T}_{H}}}\int_{\tau}fTG\xi=\sum_{\tau\in{\mathcal{T}_{H}}}\int_{\tau}f(TG\xi-c_{\xi}),

and formulation (

In the next theorem, we show that

Theorem 3
.

Let
g_{\tau}
,

\displaystyle g_{\tau}({\widetilde{\xi}},{\widetilde{\xi}})

\displaystyle=|{\widetilde{\xi}}|_{H^{1/2}({\partial\tau})}^{2}\quad\text{for all }{\widetilde{\xi}}\in{\widetilde{H}}^{1/2}({\partial\tau}),

\displaystyle g(\xi,\xi)

4.2.

If

5.1.
Well-posedness

Define the bilinear forms
g_{h,\tau}:\color[rgb]{0,0,0}H^{1/2}(\partial\tau)\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\times\color[rgb]{0,0,0}H^{1/2}(\partial\tau)\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\to\mathbb{R}
for

, and
g_{h}:H_{0}^{1/2}({\partial\mathcal{T}_{H}})\times H_{0}^{1/2}({\partial\mathcal{T}_{H}})\to\mathbb{R}
by

(34)

g_{h,\tau}(\xi,\phi)=\langle G_{h}\xi,\phi\rangle_{\partial\tau},\qquad g_{h}(\xi,\phi)=\sum_{\tau\in{\mathcal{T}_{H}}}g_{h,\tau}(\xi,\phi)

for
\xi
,
\phi\in H_{0}^{1/2}({\partial\mathcal{T}_{H}})
. Using those notations, the MH
2
M reads: Find
\rho_{H_{\Gamma}}\in\Gamma_{H_{\Gamma}}
such that

(35)

g_{h}(\rho_{H_{\Gamma}},\xi)=-\langle\lambda^{0},\xi\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{H}}+g_{h}(\widetilde{T}_{h}f,\xi)\quad\text{for all }\xi\in\Gamma_{H_{\Gamma}}.

We first address the existence and uniqueness of solution for (
35
). This result is established under the following conditions:

Assumption A
: there exists a positive constant
\beta_{\tau}
, independent of
H_{\tau}
, such that

|\mu|_{H^{-1/2}({\partial\tau})}\leq\beta_{\tau}\,|T_{h}\mu|_{H^{1}_{\mathcal{A}}(\tau)}\quad\text{for all }\mu\in\widetilde{\Lambda}_{H_{\Lambda}}\text{ and }\tau\in{\mathcal{T}_{H}};

Assumption B
: there exists a positive constant
\alpha_{\tau}
, independent of
H_{\tau}
, such that

|\xi|_{H^{1/2}({\partial\tau})}\leq\alpha_{\tau}\,|G_{h}\xi|_{H^{-1/2}({\partial\tau})}\quad\text{for all }\xi\in\Gamma_{H_{\Gamma}}\cap{\widetilde{H}}^{1/2}({\partial\tau})\text{ and }\tau\in{\mathcal{T}_{H}}.

Let
\alpha_{\max}:=\max\{\alpha_{\tau}:\,\tau\in{\mathcal{T}_{H}}\}
and
\beta_{\max}:=\max\{\beta_{\tau}:\tau\in{\mathcal{T}_{H}}\}
.

Remark 8

(Boundeness of
G_{h}
)
.

A first consequence of Assumption A is that the mapping
G_{h}
is bounded. Indeed, let
\xi\in H^{1/2}_{0}({\partial\mathcal{T}_{H}})
and select
\mu:=G_{h}\xi\in\widetilde{\Lambda}_{H_{\Lambda}}
in Assumption A. Then, from Lemma
24
, item
(iii)

\displaystyle\beta_{\tau}^{-2}|G_{h}\xi|_{H^{-1/2}({\partial\tau})}^{2}\leq|T_{h}G_{h}\xi|_{H_{\mathcal{A}}^{1}(\tau)}^{2}=\langle G_{h}\xi,T_{h}G_{h}\xi\rangle_{\partial\tau}

\displaystyle=(G_{h}\xi,\xi)_{\partial\tau}\leq|G_{h}\xi|_{H^{-1/2}({\partial\tau})}|\xi|_{H^{1/2}({\partial\tau})}.

Then,

(36)

\displaystyle|G_{h}\xi|_{H^{-1/2}({\partial\tau})}\leq\beta_{\tau}^{2}|\xi|_{H^{1/2}({\partial\tau})}\quad\text{and}\quad|G_{h}\xi|_{\Lambda}\leq\beta_{\max}^{2}|\xi|_{H^{1/2}({\partial\mathcal{T}_{H}})}.

The MH
2
M is well-posed under Assumptions A and B. This follows next.

Theorem 9
.

Let
g_{h,\tau}
,
g_{h}
be defined by (
34
) and assume that Assumptions A and B hold. Then, we have the following coercivity results:

\displaystyle g_{h,\tau}({\widetilde{\xi}}_{H_{\Gamma}},{\widetilde{\xi}}_{H_{\Gamma}})\geq(\alpha_{\tau}\beta_{\tau})^{-2}|{\widetilde{\xi}}_{H_{\Gamma}}|_{H^{1/2}({\partial\tau})}^{2}\quad\text{for all }{\widetilde{\xi}}_{H_{\Gamma}}\in\Gamma_{H_{\Gamma}}\cap{\widetilde{H}}^{1/2}({\partial\tau}),

\displaystyle g_{h}(\xi_{H_{\Gamma}},\xi_{H_{\Gamma}})\geq(\alpha_{\max}\beta_{\max})^{-2}|\xi_{H_{\Gamma}}|_{H^{1/2}({\partial\mathcal{T}_{H}})}^{2}\quad\text{for all }\xi_{H_{\Gamma}}\in\Gamma_{H_{\Gamma}}.

Moreover, the following continuity results hold:

\displaystyle g_{h,\tau}(\xi_{H_{\Gamma}},\rho_{H_{\Gamma}})\leq\beta_{\tau}^{2}|\xi_{H_{\Gamma}}|_{H^{1/2}({\partial\tau})}|\rho_{H_{\Gamma}}|_{H^{1/2}({\partial\tau})},

\displaystyle g_{h}(\xi_{H_{\Gamma}},\rho_{H_{\Gamma}})\leq\beta_{\max}^{2}|\xi_{H_{\Gamma}}|_{H^{1/2}({\mathcal{T}_{H}})}|\rho_{H_{\Gamma}}|_{H^{1/2}({\mathcal{T}_{H}})},

for all
\xi_{H_{\Gamma}}
,
\rho_{H_{\Gamma}}\in\Gamma_{H_{\Gamma}}
.
Then, the method (
35
) is well-posed and

|\rho_{H_{\Gamma}}|_{H^{1/2}({\partial\mathcal{T}_{H}})}\leq(\alpha_{\max}\beta_{\max})^{2}\Big(|\lambda^{0}|_{\Lambda}+\beta_{\max}^{2}|\widetilde{T}_{h}f|_{H^{1/2}({\partial\mathcal{T}_{H}})}\Big).

Proof.

Fix
{\widetilde{\xi}}_{H_{\Gamma}}\in\Gamma_{H_{\Gamma}}\cap{\widetilde{H}}^{1/2}({\partial\tau})
. The local coercivity holds since, from Assumptions
A
and
B
, and the definition of mapping
G_{h}(\cdot)
in (
26

\displaystyle|{\widetilde{\xi}}_{H_{\Gamma}}|_{H^{1/2}({\partial\tau})}^{2}\leq\alpha_{\tau}^{2}\beta_{\tau}^{2}|T_{h}G_{h}{\widetilde{\xi}}_{H_{\Gamma}}|_{H_{\mathcal{A}}^{1}(\tau)}^{2}=\alpha_{\tau}^{2}\beta_{\tau}^{2}\langle G_{h}{\widetilde{\xi}}_{H_{\Gamma}},T_{h}G_{h}{\widetilde{\xi}}_{H_{\Gamma}}\rangle_{\partial\tau}=\alpha_{\tau}^{2}\beta_{\tau}^{2}\,g_{h,\tau}({\widetilde{\xi}}_{H_{\Gamma}},{\widetilde{\xi}}_{H_{\Gamma}}).

Next, take
\xi_{H_{\Gamma}}\in\Gamma_{H_{\Gamma}}
. Then
\xi_{H_{\Gamma}}={\widetilde{\xi}}_{H_{\Gamma}}+\xi_{H_{\Gamma}}^{0}
, where
{\widetilde{\xi}}_{H_{\Gamma}}|_{\partial\tau}\in{\widetilde{H}}^{1/2}({\partial\tau})
and
\xi_{H_{\Gamma}}^{0}|_{\partial\tau}
is constant for each
\tau\in{\mathcal{T}_{H}}
. So, since
G_{h}(\cdot)
is symmetric and its image has zero mean value on
{\partial\tau}
, it holds that

\displaystyle g_{h}(\xi_{H_{\Gamma}},\xi_{H_{\Gamma}})=\sum_{\tau\in{\mathcal{T}_{H}}}g_{h,\tau}(\xi_{H_{\Gamma}},\xi_{H_{\Gamma}})=\sum_{\tau\in{\mathcal{T}_{H}}}g_{h,\tau}({\widetilde{\xi}}_{H_{\Gamma}},{\widetilde{\xi}}_{H_{\Gamma}})

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\tau\in{\mathcal{T}_{H}}}(\alpha_{\tau}\beta_{\tau})^{-2}|{\widetilde{\xi}}_{H_{\Gamma}}|_{H^{1/2}(\tau)}^{2}\geq(\alpha_{\max}\beta_{\max})^{-2}|\xi_{H_{\Gamma}}|_{H^{1/2}({\partial\mathcal{T}_{H}})}^{2}.

To show continuity, we use Lemma
24
, item
(iii)
and (
36
) to get

g_{h,\tau}(\xi_{H_{\Gamma}},\rho_{H_{\Gamma}})\leq|G_{h}\xi_{H_{\Gamma}}|_{H^{-1/2}({\partial\tau})}|\rho_{H_{\Gamma}}|_{H^{1/2}({\partial\tau})}\leq\beta_{\tau}^{2}|\xi_{H_{\Gamma}}|_{H^{1/2}({\partial\tau})}|\rho_{H_{\Gamma}}|_{H^{1/2}({\partial\tau})}.

Remark 16

(Relaxing Assumptions
(M1)
and
(M2)
)
.

The mesh conditions
(M1)
and
(M2)
are sufficient to establish the well-posedness of MH

), but are not necessary in general. For example, let

be even and consider the finite element

supported on partitions

consisting of one element per face, and with

consisting of the single simplex
\tau
, for each
\tau\in{\mathcal{T}_{H}}
(see Figure
1
for an illustration). Hence, adapting the arguments of
[
43
, Lemma 10]
to our setting, there exists a positive constant
C_{\tau}
, independent of mesh parameters, such that for all
{\widetilde{\mu}}\in{\widetilde{\Lambda}}_{H_{\Lambda}}

(54)

\sup_{v\in H^{1}(\tau)}\frac{\langle{\widetilde{\mu}},v\rangle_{{\partial\tau}}}{\|v\|_{H^{1}(\tau)}}\leq C_{\tau}\sup_{{\widetilde{v}}_{h}\in{\widetilde{V}_{h_{0}}}(\tau)}\frac{\langle{\widetilde{\mu}},{\widetilde{v}}_{h}\rangle_{{\partial\tau}}}{\|{\widetilde{v}}_{h}\|_{H^{1}(\tau)}}.

Moreover, observe that

\Gamma_{H_{\Gamma}}(\partial\tau)=V_{h_{0}}(\tau)|_{\partial\tau}.

Since the moment matrix corresponding to the pairing

\langle{\widetilde{\mu}},{\widetilde{v}}_{h}\rangle_{\partial\tau},\qquad{\widetilde{\mu}}\in\widetilde{\Lambda}_{H_{\Lambda}}(\partial\tau),\quad{\widetilde{v}}_{h}\in{\widetilde{V}_{h_{0}}}(\tau)|_{\partial\tau},

is square and nonsingular according to (
54
), Lemma
11
guarantees Assumption B. Therefore, all the statements of Theorem
15
hold for this family of elements as well.

In the case where
k
is odd, condition (
54
) fails because the local kernel contains a Legendre polynomial of degree
k+1
. Nevertheless, when homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions are imposed on
\partial\Omega
, the interelement continuity constraints and the prescribed boundary values prevent these local kernel functions from giving rise to a nontrivial global function. Hence, the global kernel is trivial and a global inf-sup condition still holds. As a result, the discrete problem remains well posed despite the failure of the local condition. However, we have not been able to establish the independence of the corresponding inf-sup constant with respect to
H_{\tau}
, although the numerical experiments suggest that this property holds.

Remark 22
.

Some points are worth mentioning:

•

Although the solutions
u_{h}
and
\lambda_{H_{\Lambda}}
belong to the space
V_{h}
and
\Lambda_{H_{\Lambda}}
initially, they belong to the much smaller subspaces of dimension

. In fact, by construction,

and
\widetilde{\lambda}_{H_{\Lambda}}\in\operatorname{span}\left\{\widetilde{\lambda}_{1}^{ms},\dots,\widetilde{\lambda}_{N}^{ms},\widetilde{\lambda}_{f}^{ms}\right\}\subset{\widetilde{\Lambda}}_{H_{\Lambda}}
, respectively. Such a basis accounts for the multiscale characteristics of the problem, and are numerically approximated at the local level through steps

as the approximate formula for the base functions are not generally available. The MH
2
M may be seen then as a member of reduced basis methods
[
12
]
.

•

Steps
(i)-(iii)
can be done in an offline stage. Furthermore, they are “embarrassingly” parallel in the sense that the problems are local and entirely independent of each other. The global formulation
(iv)
is the only global formulation because its degrees of freedom are nodal and associated with the coarse mesh skeleton;

•

All linear systems in steps
(ii)
-
(iv)
are symmetric and positive definite. A closer look shows that two types of local linear systems must be assembled to solve the problems in
(ii)
-
(iv)
. The associated matrices can then be factored once and for all in an offline stage and then applied to the various right sides. This is particularly attractive when it comes to troubleshooting multiple queries as found in time marching process or in varying the source data
f

9.
Conclusion

Brezzi and Marini’s three-field method
[
17
]
provided the background for new families of multiscale finite elements for the Poisson (Darcy) problem. The MH
2
M incorporates a multiscale basis for the Lagrange multiplier associated with the flow variable, obtained from the multiscale basis of the original MHM (multiscale hybrid-mixed) method
[
2
]
. Thanks to a new spatial decomposition, these multiscale bases are computed locally from positive definite algebraic systems and are ready to leverage parallel computational architectures. Unlike the MHM method, the global problem associated with MH
2
M is coercive and the degrees of freedom are associated with the trace of the primal variable. Such similarity led us to establish a connection with MsFEM
[
26
]
.

We proved that the MH
2
M converges optimally with respect to the characteristic mesh lengths under reasonable conditions of compatibility between approximation spaces and under assumptions of local regularity (as usual in hybrid methods). Furthermore, we highlighted the interaction between the face mesh diameters (
H_{\Gamma}
and
H_{\Lambda}
) and the second-level mesh parameter (
h
) on convergence.

As verified by the numerical experiments, the MH
2
M method requires fewer degrees of freedom than the MsFEM and MHM methods to achieve a given error level when using low-degree polynomial approximations. Superconvergence behavior was also observed when refining the face meshes associated with the trace variables, while keeping the coarse mesh fixed. Furthermore, we have observed that MH
2
M is less sensitive to resonance effects when approximating oscillatory solutions, in contrast to the MHM method. A rigorous analysis of the method’s sensitivity to resonance phenomena lies beyond the scope of the present work and may be pursued by adopting the strategy proposed in
[
40
,
41
]
.

\mu_{i}
such that

(66)

\int_{F}\mu_{i}\,\xi_{j}\,d{\boldsymbol{x}}\,=\,\delta_{ij}\qquad i,\,j=1,\ldots,k+2.

Next, based on the local averaging construction underlying the
Scott–Zhang interpolation operator
[
45
]
, we define the
following operators: Given
\xi\in H^{1/2}({\partial\tau})
and

\mu\in H^{-1/2}({\partial\tau})
, consider the moments

\langle\mu,\xi_{i}\rangle_{{\partial\tau}}\quad\text{and}\quad\langle\mu_{i},\xi\rangle_{{\partial\tau}},

to define the Fortin operator
\pi_{\Lambda}^{\tau}:H^{-1/2}({\partial\tau})\rightarrow\Lambda_{H_{\Lambda_{0}}}({\partial\tau})
as follows

(67)

\pi_{\Lambda}^{\tau}(\mu):=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mu_{i}\langle\mu,\xi_{i}\rangle_{{\partial\tau}},

and the mappings
\mathcal{I}_{\Gamma}:H^{1/2}({\partial\tau})\rightarrow\Gamma_{H_{\Gamma}}({\partial\tau})

by

(68)

\mathcal{I}_{\Gamma}(\xi):=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\langle\mu_{i},\xi\rangle_{{\partial\tau}}\xi_{i}

for
\xi\in H^{1/2}({\partial\tau})
.
Hereafter,
N:=\dim\Gamma_{H_{\Gamma}}({\partial\tau})
. Using the definition of
\pi_{\Lambda}^{\tau}(\cdot)
in (
67
) and (
66

\int_{{\partial\tau}}\pi_{\Lambda}^{\tau}(\mu)\,\xi_{j}\,d{\boldsymbol{x}}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{{\partial\tau}}\mu_{i}\xi_{j}d{\boldsymbol{x}}\,\langle\mu,\xi_{i}\rangle_{{\partial\tau}}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{F\subset{\partial\tau}}\int_{F}\mu_{i}\xi_{j}d{\boldsymbol{x}}\,\langle\mu,\xi_{i}\rangle_{{\partial\tau}}=\langle\mu,\xi_{j}\rangle_{{\partial\tau}}.

As a result, decomposing
\xi\in\Gamma_{H_{\Gamma}}({\partial\tau})
as

\xi=\sum_{i=1}^{N}c_{i}\xi_{i}\quad\text{where }c_{i}\in\mathbb{R},

we get, for all
\mu\in H^{-1/2}({\partial\tau})
,

\int_{{\partial\tau}}\pi_{\Lambda}^{\tau}(\mu)\,\xi\,d{\boldsymbol{x}}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}c_{i}\int_{{\partial\tau}}\pi_{\Lambda}^{\tau}(\mu)\,\xi_{i}\,d{\boldsymbol{x}}=\langle\mu,\xi\rangle_{{\partial\tau}},

which implies the first condition in (
64
) replacing
\mu
by
{\widetilde{\mu}}
above. Note that the image of
\pi_{\Lambda}^{\tau}(\cdot)
given in (
67
) is a subset of
{\widetilde{\Lambda}}_{H_{\Lambda_{0}}}
when it acts on space
{\widetilde{\Lambda}}
.

Now, we turn to prove the stability of
\pi_{\Lambda}^{\tau}(\cdot)
in the
{\widetilde{H}}^{-1/2}({\partial\tau})
seminorm. First, observe that
\mathcal{I}_{\Gamma}(\cdot)
is a projection operator onto spaces
\Gamma_{H_{\Gamma}}({\partial\tau})
by (
66
), and
\mathcal{I}_{\Gamma}(\cdot)
corresponds to the adjoint of
\pi_{\Lambda}^{\tau}(\cdot)
. Indeed,

(69)

\langle\mu,\mathcal{I}_{\Gamma}(\xi)\rangle_{{\partial\tau}}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\langle\mu,\xi_{i}\rangle_{{\partial\tau}}\langle\mu_{i},\xi\rangle_{{\partial\tau}}=\langle\pi_{\Lambda}(\mu),\xi\rangle_{{\partial\tau}}.

Hence, assuming the existence of a positive constant
\alpha_{\tau}
, independent of
H_{\tau}
, such that

(70)

|\mathcal{I}_{\Gamma}(\xi)|_{H^{1/2}({\partial\tau})}\leq\alpha_{\tau}\,|\xi|_{H^{1/2}({\partial\tau})}\quad\forall\xi\in H^{1/2}({\partial\tau}),

then, we obtain

\displaystyle|\pi_{\Lambda}^{\tau}({\widetilde{\mu}})|_{H^{-1/2}({\partial\tau})}

\displaystyle=\sup_{{\widetilde{\xi}}\in{\widetilde{H}}^{1/2}({\partial\tau})}\frac{\langle\pi_{\Lambda}^{\tau}({\widetilde{\mu}}),{\widetilde{\xi}}\rangle_{{\partial\tau}}}{|{\widetilde{\xi}}|_{H^{1/2}({\partial\tau})}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{{\widetilde{\xi}}\in{\widetilde{H}}^{1/2}({\partial\tau})}\frac{\langle{\widetilde{\mu}},\mathcal{I}_{\Gamma}({\widetilde{\xi}})\rangle_{{\partial\tau}}}{|{\widetilde{\xi}}|_{H^{1/2}({\partial\tau})}}

\displaystyle\leq|{\widetilde{\mu}}|_{H^{-1/2}({\partial\tau})}\sup_{{\widetilde{\xi}}\in{\widetilde{H}}^{1/2}({\partial\tau})}\frac{|\mathcal{I}_{\Gamma}({\widetilde{\xi}})|_{H^{1/2}({\partial\tau})}}{|{\widetilde{\xi}}|_{H^{1/2}({\partial\tau})}}

\displaystyle\leq\alpha_{\tau}\,|{\widetilde{\mu}}|_{H^{-1/2}({\partial\tau})},

where we used the definition of the seminorm
|\cdot|_{H^{-1/2}({\partial\tau})}
, (
69
) and (
70
).
The result (
64
) follows defining
\pi_{\Lambda}(\cdot)
such that
\pi_{\Lambda}(\cdot)|_{\tau}:=\pi_{\Lambda}^{\tau}(\cdot)
for all
\tau\in{\mathcal{T}_{H}}
restricted to space
{\widetilde{\Lambda}}
.

We observe that (
70
) is a consequence of the standard Scott–Zhang patchwise argument (see
[
45
,
21
]
), together with interpolation theory (see
[
39
]
[Chapter 3] and
[
34
]
). For completeness, we detail the proof in the present setting. For each edge
F\subset{\partial\tau}
, define

I(F):=\left\{i:\operatorname{supp}(\xi_{i})\cap F\neq\emptyset\right\}\quad\text{and}\quad\omega_{F}:=\cup_{i\in I(F)}F_{i},

and note that
\omega_{F}
consists of at most the edge
F
and its two neighboring edges. Moreover, the cardinality of
I(F)
is uniformly bounded since the polynomial degree
k+1
is fixed.

Next, for a fixed node
i
, select an edge
F_{i}
such that
\operatorname{supp}\xi_{i}\cap F_{i}\neq\emptyset
, and let

\bar{\mu}_{i}
be the associated local dual basis function, that is, the function satisfying (
66
) with replaced
F
by
F_{i}
. Then, define
\mu_{i}\in\Lambda_{H_{\Lambda_{0}}}(\partial\tau)
by setting

\mu_{i}:=\bar{\mu}_{i}
on
F_{i}
and
\mu_{i}:=0
on
\partial\tau\setminus F_{i}
.
Observe that
\{\xi_{j}|_{F_{i}}\}_{j\in I(F_{i})}
forms a basis for
\mathbb{P}_{k+1}(F_{i})
and that

\int_{\partial\tau}\mu_{i}\,\xi_{j}\,d{\boldsymbol{x}}=\int_{F_{i}}\bar{\mu}_{i}\,\xi_{j}|_{F_{i}}\,d{\boldsymbol{x}}=\delta_{ij},\quad i,\,j=1,\ldots,N.

From a standard scaling argument together with the condition (
66
), and the fact that the lengths of the sub-edges
\mathfrak{f}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{f}_{2}
are uniformly comparable to that of
F_{i}
, we obtain

(71)

\|\xi_{i}\|_{L^{2}(F)}\leq C\,H_{F}^{1/2}\,\quad\text{and}\quad\|\mu_{i}\|_{L^{2}(F_{i})}\leq C\,H_{F_{i}}^{-1/2},

where
H_{F}
and
H_{F_{i}}
are the diameters of
F
and
F_{i}
, respectively. Assumption
(M2)
ensures that these diameters are uniformly comparable, and constants
C
are independent of the mesh parameters. Using (
68
), it follows that, for every edge
F\subset\partial\tau
,

\mathcal{I}_{\Gamma}(\xi)|_{F}=\sum_{i\in I(F)}\langle\mu_{i},\xi\rangle_{\partial\tau}\,\xi_{i}|_{F}=\sum_{i\in I(F)}\langle\mu_{i},\xi\rangle_{F_{i}}\,\xi_{i}|_{F},

since
\operatorname{supp}\mu_{i}\subset F_{i}
. Then, applying the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality together with (
71
) and the uniform equivalence between the diameters
H_{F}
and
H_{F_{i}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{I}_{\Gamma}(\xi)\|_{{L^{2}}(F)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\in I(F)}\|\xi_{i}\|_{L^{2}(F)}|\langle\mu_{i},\xi\rangle_{F_{i}}|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\in I(F)}\|\xi_{i}\|_{L^{2}(F)}\|\mu_{i}\|_{L^{2}(F_{i})}\|\xi\|_{L^{2}(F_{i})}

\displaystyle\leq C\,\|\xi\|_{L^{2}(\omega_{F})}.

Squaring the previous inequality and summing over the edges gives

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{I}_{\Gamma}(\xi)\|_{{L^{2}}({\partial\tau})}^{2}=\sum_{F\subset{\partial\tau}}\|\mathcal{I}_{\Gamma}(\xi)\|_{{L^{2}}(F)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\,\sum_{F\subset{\partial\tau}}\|\xi\|_{L^{2}(\omega_{F})}^{2}.

Since the patches
\omega_{F}
have uniformly bounded overlap, there exists a constant
C
, independent of
H_{\tau}
and
N
, such that

(72)

\|\mathcal{I}_{\Gamma}(\xi)\|_{L^{2}({\partial\tau})}\leq C\,\|\xi\|_{L^{2}({\partial\tau})}.

In addition, since
\mathcal{I}_{\Gamma}(\cdot)
is a projection, we have

\mathcal{I}_{\Gamma}(\xi_{F})|_{F}=\xi_{F}
, where
\xi_{F}:=\frac{1}{|\omega_{F}|}\int_{\omega_{F}}\xi\,d{\boldsymbol{x}}.
Consequently,

\mathcal{I}_{\Gamma}(\xi)|_{F}=\xi_{F}+\mathcal{I}_{\Gamma}(\xi-\xi_{F})|_{F}
. Then, for every edge
F\subset\omega_{F}
, it follows that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{I}_{\Gamma}(\xi)|_{H^{1}(F)}

\displaystyle=|\mathcal{I}_{\Gamma}(\xi-\xi_{F})|_{H^{1}(F)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{H_{F}}\,\|\mathcal{I}_{\Gamma}(\xi-\xi_{F})\|_{L^{2}(F)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{H_{F}}\,\|\xi-\xi_{F}\|_{L^{2}(\omega_{F})}

\displaystyle\leq C\,|\xi|_{H^{1}(\omega_{F})},

where we used an inverse inequality, the
L^{2}
-stability of
\mathcal{I}_{\Gamma}(\cdot)
established above, and the Poincaré inequality on each edge contained in
\omega_{F}
. Squaring the above inequality and summing over all edges yields

(73)

|\mathcal{I}_{\Gamma}(\xi)|_{H^{1}(\partial\tau)}\leq C\,|\xi|_{H^{1}(\partial\tau)}.

As a result, the operator
\mathcal{I}_{\Gamma}(\cdot)
is stable in both

L^{2}(\partial\tau)
and
H^{1}(\partial\tau)
, with constants independent of the mesh parameters.
By the interpolation identity

H^{1/2}(\partial\tau)=[L^{2}(\partial\tau),H^{1}(\partial\tau)]_{1/2},

and the equivalence between the interpolation norm and the trace norm on
H^{1/2}(\partial\tau)
, estimate (
70
) follows from (
72
) and (
73
)

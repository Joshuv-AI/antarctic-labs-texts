Moments of Margulis functions and indefinite ternary quadratic forms
Wooyeon Kim

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the moments of the Margulis α \alpha -function integrating over expanding translates of a unipotent orbit in SL 3 ⁡ ( ℝ ) / SL 3 ⁡ ( ℤ ) \operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{R})/\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z}) . We show that for some λ > 1 \lambda>1 the λ \lambda -moments of the Margulis α \alpha -function over expanding translates of a unipotent orbit are uniformly bounded, under suitable Diophantine conditions of the initial unipotent orbit. As an application, we prove that for any indefinite irrational ternary quadratic form Q Q with suitable Diophantine conditions and a < b a<b the number of integral vectors of norm at most T T satisfying a < Q ⁡ ( v ) < b a<Q(v)<b is asymptotically equivalent to 𝖢 Q ​ ( b − a ) ​ T \mathsf{C}_{Q}(b-a)T as T T tends to infinity, where the constant 𝖢 Q > 0 \mathsf{C}_{Q}>0 depends only on Q Q .

1.
Introduction

The Oppenheim conjecture, proved by Margulis in 1986, states that for a non-degenerate indefinite irrational quadratic form
Q
in
n\geq 3
variables, the image set
Q(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
of integral vectors is a dense subset of
\mathbb{R}
unless
Q
is proportional to a form with rational coefficients. In this paper, we say that a quadratic form is
rational
if it is proportional to a form with rational coefficients, and
irrational
otherwise.

A quantitative version of the Oppenheim conjecture was established in
[
EMM98
]
and
[
EMM05
]
for quadratic forms in
n\geq 4
variables. For
T>0
let
B(T)
denote the ball of radius
T
centered at zero, and for
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
with
a<b
let
N_{Q}(a,b,T)
be the cardinality of the set

\left\{v\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}:v\in B(T)\textrm{ and }a<Q(v)<b\right\}.

Note that for any such quadratic form
Q
there exists a constant
\mathsf{C}_{Q}
such that for any
a<b

\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{\operatorname{Vol}\big(\left\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:v\in B(T)\textrm{ and }a<Q(v)<b\right\}\big)}{T^{n-2}}=\mathsf{C}_{Q}(b-a),

and furthermore the constant
\mathsf{C}_{Q}
can be explicitly computed as follows:

(1.1)

{\mathsf{C}_{Q}=\int_{\mathcal{H}\cap B(1)}\frac{d\sigma}{\|\nabla Q\|},}

where
\mathcal{H}:=\left\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:Q_{0}(v)=0\right\}
, and
d\sigma
is the area element on
\mathcal{H}
.

Determining the asymptotic of
N_{Q}(a,b,T)
is referred to as quantitative Oppenheim conjecture. In
[
EMM98
]
, Eskin, Margulis, and Mozes showed that if
Q
is an indefinite irrational quadratic form and the signature of
Q
is not
(2,2)
and
(2,1)
, then

(1.2)

{\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{N_{Q}(a,b,T)}{T^{n-2}}=\mathsf{C}_{Q}(b-a).}

If the signature of
Q
is
(2,2)
or
(2,1)
, then the universal formula (
1.2
) is no longer true. In fact, there are irrational forms for which
N_{Q}(a,b,T_{j})\gg T_{j}^{n-2}\log T_{j}
along a subsequence
T_{j}\to\infty
, and one may obtain such quadratic forms by considering irrational forms which are very well approximated by rational quadratic forms. Thus, one needs to assume certain Diophantine conditions on quadratic forms of signature
(2,2)
or
(2,1)
to obtain an asymptotic formula like (
1.2
).

A quadratic form
Q
of signature
(2,2)
with
\operatorname{det}Q=1
is said
extremely well approximable by split rational forms
(EWAS) if for any
c>0
and
M>1
there exists a split integral form
Q^{\prime}
satisfying

\|Q-\rho Q^{\prime}\|\leq c\|Q^{\prime}\|^{-M},

where
\rho=\operatorname{det}(Q^{\prime})^{-\frac{1}{4}}
. Here and hereafter,
\|\cdot\|
for quadratic forms stands for the supremum of the coefficients.
In
[
EMM05
]
it was proved that if
Q
is a quadratic form of signature
(2,2)
, which is not EWAS, then

(1.3)

{\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{\widetilde{N}_{Q}(a,b,T)}{T^{2}}=\mathsf{C}_{Q}(b-a),}

where
\widetilde{N}_{Q}(a,b,T)
counts the points not contained in isotropic subspaces.

The purpose of the present paper is to establish the asymptotic of
N_{Q}(a,b,T)
for quadratic forms of signature
(2,1)
satisfying suitable Diophantine conditions.

Definition 1.1
.

For
M>1
we say that a ternary quadratic form
Q
with
\operatorname{det}Q=1
is of
(Diophantine) type

M
if there exists
c>0
such that

\|Q-\rho Q^{\prime}\|>c\|Q^{\prime}\|^{-M}

for any nonzero integral ternary quadratic form
Q^{\prime}
, where
\rho=(\operatorname{det}Q^{\prime})^{-\frac{1}{3}}
.

A ternary quadratic form
Q
with
\operatorname{det}Q=1
is said
extremely well approximable
(EWA) if
Q
is not of type
M
for any
M>1
.

Definition 1.2
.

For a given indefinite ternary quadratic form
Q
, we say that a line
l\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
through the origin is
isotropic
if
Q(v_{l})=0
for a nonzero vector
v_{l}
on
l
. We also say that a plane
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
through the origin is
degenerate
if the restriction
Q|_{P}
on
P
is the square of a linear form.

For a quadratic form
Q
let
A
be the symmetric matrix associated to
Q
, i.e.
Q(v)=\mskip 1.0mu\prescript{\smash{\mathrm{t}\mkern-1.0mu}}{}{\mathstrut v}Av
. The dual quadratic form
Q^{*}
of
Q
is defined as
Q^{*}(v)=\mskip 1.0mu\prescript{\smash{\mathrm{t}\mkern-1.0mu}}{}{\mathstrut v}A^{-1}v
. We note that
P
is degenerate if and only if the line orthogonal to
P
is isotropic for the dual form
Q^{*}
, i.e.
Q^{*}(v_{P})=0
for a nonzero vector
v_{P}
orthogonal to
P
(see Lemma
7.2
).

The main result of this paper is as follows.

Theorem 1.3

(Modified count)
.

Let
Q
be an indefinite ternary quadratic form with
\operatorname{det}Q=1
, which is not EWA. Then for any
a<b
we have

(1.4)

{\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{\widetilde{N}_{Q}(a,b,T)}{T}=\mathsf{C}_{Q}(b-a),}

where
\widetilde{N}_{Q}(a,b,T)
counts the points not contained in rational isotropic lines and rational degenerate planes through the origin, and the constant
\mathsf{C}_{Q}
is as in (
1.1
).

Including the points contained in rational isotropic lines and rational degenerate planes, we have:

Corollary 1.4

(Complete count)
.

Let
Q
be a quadratic form of signature
(2,1)
with
\operatorname{det}Q=1
, which is not EWA. Then for any
a<b
we have

{\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{N_{Q}(a,b,T)}{T}=\mathsf{C}_{Q}(b-a)+\mathsf{I}_{Q}(a,b),}

where
\mathsf{C}_{Q}
is as in (
1.1
), and the constant
\mathsf{I}_{Q}(a,b)\geq 0
depends only on
a,b
, and
Q
.

Remark 1.5
.

In the asymptotic of
N_{Q}(a,b,T)
, the term
\mathsf{I}_{Q}(a,b)T
corresponds to the number of integral points on isotropic lines and degenerate planes. If there is neither rational isotropic lines nor rational degenerate planes through the origin, i.e. both
Q=0
and
Q^{*}=0
do not admit a nontrivial integral solution, then
\mathsf{I}_{Q}(a,b)=0
for any
a<b
. Moreover, one can calculate the constant
\mathsf{I}_{Q}(a,b)
explicitly in terms of the isotropic vectors (see §7.3).

1.1.
Moments of Margulis functions

For the lower bound of
N_{Q}(a,b,T)
, Dani and Margulis showed in
[
DM93
]
that for any irrational quadratic form of signature
(p,q)
with
p\geq 2
and
q\geq 1

(1.5)

{\liminf_{T\to\infty}\frac{N_{Q}(a,b,T)}{T^{n-2}}\geq\mathsf{C}_{Q}(b-a),}

where
n=p+q
. The proof of the lower bound (
1.5
) is based on Ratner’s equidistribution theorem for the action of
\operatorname{SO}(p,q)
on
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})/\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
, and the linearization method developed in
[
DM93
]
.

The proof of the upper bound of
N_{Q}(a,b,T)
is more subtle since an equidistribution theorem for
unbounded
test functions is required, hence one needs a sharp estimate for the quantitative recurrence to compact sets in the space of lattices. Margulis
\alpha
-function is a height function constructed in
[
EMM98
]
for this purpose, and the moments of the height function integrated over
\operatorname{SO}(p,q)
-orbits are studied in
[
EMM98
]
and
[
EMM05
]
.

The construction of the Margulis
\alpha
-function on the space of lattices is as follows. Let
\Delta
be a lattice in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. A linear subspace
L
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called
\Delta
-rational if
L\cap\Delta
is a lattice in
L
, and for a
\Delta
-rational subspace
L
we denote
d(L)
the volume of
L\cap\Delta
. For each
1\leq i\leq n-1
let

(1.6)

{\alpha_{i}(\Delta):=\sup\left\{\frac{1}{d(L)}:L\textrm{ is a }\Delta\textrm{-rational space of dimension }i\right\},}

and define
\alpha(\Delta):=\displaystyle\max_{1\leq i\leq n-1}\alpha_{i}(\Delta)
.

From now on, let us focus on
n=3
. Let
G=\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{R})
and
\Gamma=\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
. We denote by
X
the space of unimodular lattices in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. The space
X
is identified with
G/\Gamma
, since
\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(\mathbb{Z}^{3})=\Gamma
. Let
Q_{0}
the standard indefinite ternary quadratic form defined by

Q_{0}(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})=v_{2}^{2}-2v_{1}v_{3},

and let
H:=\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(Q_{0})\simeq\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
.

For a ternary quadratic form
Q
and
g\in G
we denote by
Q^{g}
the quadratic form defined by
Q^{g}(v)=Q(gv)
. For any quadratic form
Q
with
\operatorname{det}(Q)=1
of signature
(2,1)
, there exists
g\in G
such that
Q=Q_{0}^{g}
. Then let denote
\Delta_{Q}
the lattice
g\mathbb{Z}^{3}
, so that
Q(\mathbb{Z}^{3})=Q_{0}(\Delta_{Q})
.

For a lattice
\Delta
let
\Delta^{*}
denote the dual lattice of
\Delta
. It holds that

\displaystyle\Delta^{*}

\displaystyle:=\left\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:v\cdot w\in\mathbb{Z}\textrm{ for all }w\in\Delta\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{v\times w\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:v,w\in\Delta\right\}.

Note that we can also view
\Delta^{*}
as a lattice of
\bigwedge^{2}\mathbb{R}^{3}
via the canonical isomorphism
v\wedge w\mapsto v\times w
from
\bigwedge^{2}\mathbb{R}^{3}
to
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. We remark that the dual quadratic form
Q^{*}
of
Q
is the associated quadratic form to
\Delta_{Q}^{*}
. Indeed,
\Delta_{Q}^{*}=g^{*}\mathbb{Z}^{3}
and
Q^{*}=Q_{0}^{g^{*}}
, where
g^{*}:=\mskip 1.0mu\prescript{\smash{\mathrm{t}\mkern-1.0mu}}{}{\mathstrut g^{-1}}
.

We have
\alpha_{1}(\Delta)=\sup\left\{\|v\|^{-1}:v\in\Delta\setminus\left\{0\right\}\right\}
and by duality
\alpha_{2}(\Delta)=\alpha_{1}(\Delta^{*})
. Now the Margulis height function can be written as

(1.7)

\displaystyle\alpha(\Delta)

\displaystyle=\max\left\{\alpha_{1}(\Delta),\alpha_{2}(\Delta)\right\}=\max\left\{\alpha_{1}(\Delta),\alpha_{1}(\Delta^{*})\right\}

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{\|v\|^{-1}:v\in(\Delta\cup\Delta^{*})\setminus\left\{0\right\}\right\}.

For
t,r\in\mathbb{R}
let

a_{t}:=\left(\begin{matrix}e^{t}&&\\
&1&\\
&&e^{-t}\end{matrix}\right),\qquad u_{r}:=\left(\begin{matrix}1&r&\frac{r^{2}}{2}\\
&1&r\\
&&1\end{matrix}\right).

The moments of the function
\alpha
integrated over expanding translates of a unipotent trajectory were studied in
[
EMM98
]
:

(1.8)

{\sup_{t>0}\int_{-1}^{1}\alpha(a_{t}u_{r}\Delta)^{\lambda}dr<\infty,\quad\sup_{t>0}\frac{1}{t}\int_{-1}^{1}\alpha(a_{t}u_{r}\Delta)dr<\infty}

for any
0<\lambda<1
and
\Delta\in X
.

Remark 1.6
.

The exponent
\lambda
here cannot be improved beyond
1
for every
\Delta
. For instance, if
\Delta
contains a nonzero vector
v=(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
such that
Q_{0}(v)=0
and
|v_{2}|\leq|v_{3}|
, then
\int_{-1}^{1}\alpha(a_{t}u_{r}\Delta)^{\lambda}dr
diverges as
t\to\infty
for any
\lambda>1
. Indeed, if
Q_{0}(v)=0
and
|v_{2}|\leq|v_{3}|
then
\|a_{t}u_{r}v\|\leq e^{-t}\|v\|
for any
|r+\frac{v_{2}}{v_{3}}|\leq e^{-t}
, hence for any
t>0
we have

\int_{-1}^{1}\alpha(a_{t}u_{r}\Delta)^{\lambda}dr\geq\int_{|r+\frac{v_{2}}{v_{3}}|\leq e^{-t}}\|a_{t}u_{r}v\|^{-\lambda}dr\geq e^{(\lambda-1)t}\|v\|^{-\lambda}.

In this paper, we improve the exponent
\lambda
in (
1.8
) beyond
1
under suitable Diophantine assumptions on the initial point
\Delta\in X
.

Theorem 1.7

(Moment estimates, simplified version)
.

For
M>1
there exists a constant
\delta=\delta(M)>0
such that the following holds. Let
Q
be an indefinite ternary quadratic form of type
M
with
\operatorname{det}(Q)=1
. Suppose that there exists
\eta>0
such that

(1.9)

{\min\left\{|Q(\mathbf{m})|,|Q^{*}(\mathbf{m})|\right\}\geq\eta\|\mathbf{m}\|^{-M}}

for any
\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
. Then we have

(1.10)

{\sup_{t>0}\int_{-1}^{1}\alpha(a_{t}u_{r}\Delta_{Q})^{1+\delta}dr<\infty.}

In fact, (
1.10
) is equivalent to the following statement:

(1.11)

{\sup_{t>0}\int_{K}\alpha(a_{t}k\Delta_{Q})^{1+\delta}dk<\infty,}

where
K
is the maximal compact group of
H
. Then (
1.11
) implies Theorem
1.4
under the additional assumption (
1.9
), by an argument identical to that used in
[
EMM98
, §3.4, §3.5]
to deduce
[
EMM98
, Theorem 2.1]
from
[
EMM98
, Theorem 2.3]
. A more general version of Theorem
1.7
, which implies Theorem
1.3
and Corollary
1.4
without the assumption (
1.9
), will be stated in §2 (see Theorem
2.7
).

1.2.
Related results

Since Margulis proved the Oppenheim conjecture, various refinements and extensions of the Oppenheim conjecture have been studied. Here we give a (non-exhaustive) list of related results.

Recently there has been increased interest in developing
effective
versions of the Oppenheim conjecture. The basic question in this direction is as follows. Given a quadratic form
Q
in
n
variables and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
, how large is the size of a nontrivial solution
\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
to the Diphantine inequality
|Q(\mathbf{m})-\xi|<\epsilon
, in terms of
\epsilon^{-1}
? We remark that asymptotic formulas like (
1.2
), (
1.3
), and (
1.4
) do not give information on the explicit size of
\mathbf{m}
. For quadratic forms in
n\geq 5
variables, Buterus, Götze, Hille, and Margulis established an effective version with a polynomial rate using Fourier analytic methods in
[
BGHM22
]
(see also
[
BG99
,
Göt04
]
). For ternary quadratic forms, an effective version with a logarithmic rate was proved by Lindenstrauss and Margulis
[
LM14
]
. Recently, an effective result with a polynomial rate was announced in
[
LMWY23
]
, by establishing an effective equidistribution theorem.

Effective versions of the Oppenheim conjecture are also studied for generic (in a measure-theoretic sense) quadratic forms, with an emphasis on the optimal rate. Generic effective results are obtained by Bourgain for generic diagonal ternary forms using analytic number theory
[
Bou16
]
, and by Ghosh and Kelmer for generic ternary forms using spectral methods
[
GK18
]
. More results along this line are proved in
[
GGN20
,
AM18
,
KY20
,
KS21
]
.

For a quadratic form
Q
and a shift vector
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, one may consider an
inhomogeneous
quadratic form
Q_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}
defined by
Q_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}(v):=Q(v+\boldsymbol{\xi})
and study the values of
Q_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}
at integral points. Margulis and Mohammadi established quantitative Oppenheim-type statements for inhomogeneous quadratic forms in
[
MM11
]
. Earlier, a special case
Q_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})=(x_{1}-\alpha)^{2}+(x_{2}-\beta)^{2}-(x_{3}-\alpha)^{2}-(x_{4}-\beta)^{2}
related to the Berry-Tabor conjecture was proved by Marklof in
[
Mrk03
]
(see also
[
Mrk02
]
). For this particular case Strömbergsson and Vishe obtained an effective version in
[
SV20
]
. We also refer the readers to
[
GKY22
,
GKY23
]
for effective generic results for inhomogeneous quadratic forms.

In the
S
-arithmetic setting, the analog of Margulis’s theorem was proved by Borel and Prasad
[
BP92
]
. The
S
-arithmetic generalization of the quantitative Oppenheim conjecture was established by Han, Lim, and Mallahi-Karai for forms in
n\geq 5
variables in
[
HLM17
]
, and was studied by Han for forms in
3
or
4
variables in
[
Han21
]
. See also
[
Han22
,
GH22
]
for effective generic results in the
S
-arithmetic setting.

Dani and Margulis considered pairs of a quadratic form and a linear form in
[
DM90
]
. They showed the density of the joint values of forms in three variables, and Gorodnik extended this result to forms in
n\geq 4
variables in
[
Gor04
]
. Recently Han, Lim, and Mallahi-Karai obtained a quantitative refinement of this problem for forms in
n\geq 5
variables in
[
HLM21
]
. See
[
BGH21
]
for effective generic results in this setting, and
[
BG21
,
GH22
]
for pairs of inhomogeneous forms.

1.3.
Structure of the paper

This paper is organized as follows.

In Section 2, we introduce a notion of quasi-null vectors and define Margulis functions excluding the quasi-null vectors. Then we state Theorem
2.7
which is the main technical theorem of this paper, give an outline of the proof of Theorem
2.7
, and sketch how to deduce Theorem
1.3
from Theorem
2.7
.
Sections 3-6 are devoted to proving Theorem
2.7
. In Section 3 we define height functions on
\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
involving certain additional factors related to the quadric surface
\mathcal{H}
and prove contraction inequalities for such functions. In Section 4 we define modified Margulis functions on the space of lattices and establish subharmonic estimates for such Margulis functions, making use of the contraction inequalities from Section 3. In Section 5 we give avoidance estimates to control the contribution of the points which do not satisfy the above subharmonic estimates. In Section 6 we complete the proof of Theorem
2.7
making use of the ingredients we developed in Sections 3-5. In Section 7 we deduce Theorem
1.3
and Corollary
1.4
from Theorem
2.7
.

2.
Preliminaries and Overview of the proofs

We shall use the standard notation
A\ll B
or
A=O(B)
to mean that
A\leq CB
for some constant
C>0
. In this paper, all the implied constants are absolute, unless mentioned explicitly.

2.1.
Metrics and norms

Let
\mathbf{d}_{G}(\cdot,\cdot)
be a right invariant Riemannian metric on
G
. Then this metric induces a metric
\mathbf{d}_{X}(\cdot,\cdot)
on
X
. We denote by
B^{G}(r)
the open
r
-ball around
\operatorname{id}
with respect to the metric
\mathbf{d}_{G}
.

For
v=(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
\gamma=(\gamma_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq 3}\in\operatorname{Mat}_{3,3}(\mathbb{R})
, we use the supremum norms
\|v\|=\displaystyle\max_{1\leq i\leq 3}|v_{i}|
and
\|\gamma\|=\displaystyle\max_{1\leq i,j\leq 3}\|\gamma_{ij}\|
.

By re-scaling the metric
\mathbf{d}_{G}
if necessary, we may assume that
\mathbf{d}_{G}
satisfies

(2.1)

{\|g-\textrm{id}\|_{\textrm{op}}\leq r}

for any sufficiently small
r>0
and
g\in B^{G}(r)
, where
\|\cdot\|_{\textrm{op}}
stands for the operator norm of
\operatorname{Mat}_{3,3}(\mathbb{R})
with respect to the supremum norm of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

2.2.
Diophantine conditions and quasi-null vectors

For an indefinite ternary quadratic form
Q
, if there are five nonzero integral vectors in
\left\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:Q(v)=0\right\}
for which no three of these vectors lie on the same plane, then
Q
must be a rational quadratic form. The following lemma is a quantitative refinement of this observation.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
Q
be an indefinite ternary quadratic form with
\operatorname{det}Q=1
and
\mathbf{m}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{m}_{5}\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
be integral vectors for which no three of these vectors lie on the same plane through the origin. Let
0\leq\epsilon<1
and
R>10
. Suppose that
|Q(\mathbf{m}_{i})|\leq\epsilon
and
\|\mathbf{m}_{i}\|<R
and all
1\leq i\leq 5
. Then there exists a nonzero integral ternary quadratic form
Q^{\prime}
satisfying
|Q-\rho Q^{\prime}|\ll\epsilon R^{10}
and
\|Q^{\prime}\|\leq 10^{6}R^{14}
, where
\rho=(\operatorname{det}Q^{\prime})^{-\frac{1}{3}}
.

Proof.

Let
\gamma\in\operatorname{Mat}_{3,3}(\mathbb{Z})
be the matrix whose columns are given by
\mathbf{m}_{1},\mathbf{m}_{2},\mathbf{m}_{3}
. Note that
\|\gamma\|<R
and
\|\operatorname{adj}(\gamma)\|<2R^{2}
, where
\operatorname{adj}(\gamma):=(\operatorname{det}\gamma)\gamma^{-1}
. Also,
\operatorname{det}\gamma
is a nonzero integer and
|\operatorname{det}\gamma|\leq 6R^{3}
since
\mathbf{m}_{1},\mathbf{m}_{2},\mathbf{m}_{3}
are linearly independent integral vectors. Let
Q_{1}:=Q\circ\gamma
and write

(2.2)

\displaystyle Q_{1}(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})

\displaystyle=Q(v_{1}\mathbf{m}_{1}+v_{2}\mathbf{m}_{2}+v_{3}\mathbf{m}_{3})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{3}Q(\mathbf{m}_{i})v_{i}^{2}+2\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq 3}Q(\mathbf{m}_{i},\mathbf{m}_{j})v_{i}v_{j},

where
Q(\mathbf{m}_{i},\mathbf{m}_{j}):=\frac{1}{2}\big(Q(\mathbf{m}_{i}+\mathbf{m}_{j})-Q(\mathbf{m}_{i})-Q(\mathbf{m}_{j})\big)
. Note that
|Q(\mathbf{m}_{i})|\leq\epsilon
for all
1\leq i\leq 3
and
|Q(\mathbf{m}_{i},\mathbf{m}_{j})|\leq 10\|Q\|R^{2}
for all
1\leq i<j\leq 3
. Let
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},b_{1},b_{2},b_{3}\in\mathbb{Z}
be the integers given by

\mskip 1.0mu\prescript{\smash{\mathrm{t}\mkern-1.0mu}}{}{\mathstrut(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})}=\operatorname{adj}(\gamma)\mathbf{m}_{4},\quad\mskip 1.0mu\prescript{\smash{\mathrm{t}\mkern-1.0mu}}{}{\mathstrut(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3})}=\operatorname{adj}(\gamma)\mathbf{m}_{5}

Then we may also write

(\operatorname{det}\gamma)\mathbf{m}_{4}=a_{1}\mathbf{m}_{1}+a_{2}\mathbf{m}_{2}+a_{3}\mathbf{m}_{3},

(\operatorname{det}\gamma)\mathbf{m}_{5}=b_{1}\mathbf{m}_{1}+b_{2}\mathbf{m}_{2}+b_{3}\mathbf{m}_{3}.

Moreover,
\displaystyle\max_{1\leq i\leq 3}\max(|a_{i}|,|b_{i}|)\leq 3\|\operatorname{adj}(\gamma)\|\max(\|\mathbf{m}_{4}\|,\|\mathbf{m}_{5}\|)<6R^{3}
. In view of (
2.2
) it gives that

\left|\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq 3}Q(\mathbf{m}_{i},\mathbf{m}_{j})a_{i}a_{j}\right|=\frac{1}{2}\left|(\operatorname{det}\gamma)^{2}Q(\mathbf{m}_{4})-\sum_{i=1}^{3}Q(\mathbf{m}_{i})a_{i}^{2}\right|\ll\epsilon R^{6},

\left|\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq 3}Q(\mathbf{m}_{i},\mathbf{m}_{j})b_{i}b_{j}\right|=\frac{1}{2}\left|(\operatorname{det}\gamma)^{2}Q(\mathbf{m}_{5})-\sum_{i=1}^{3}Q(\mathbf{m}_{i})b_{i}^{2}\right|\ll\epsilon R^{6}.

Roughly speaking, the vector
\big(Q(\mathbf{m}_{2},\mathbf{m}_{3}),Q(\mathbf{m}_{3},\mathbf{m}_{1}),Q(\mathbf{m}_{1},\mathbf{m}_{2})\big)
is almost orthogonal to integral vectors
\mathbf{a}:=(a_{3}a_{1},a_{1}a_{2},a_{2}a_{3})
and
\mathbf{b}:=(b_{3}b_{1},b_{1}b_{2},b_{2}b_{3})
. Here
\mathbf{a}
and
\mathbf{b}
are linearly independent because no three of
\mathbf{m}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{m}_{5}
lie in the same plane, and
\mathbf{m}_{4}
and
\mathbf{m}_{5}
are themselves linearly independent. Let

(c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}):=\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}=\big(a_{1}b_{1}(a_{2}b_{3}-a_{3}b_{2}),a_{2}b_{2}(a_{3}b_{1}-a_{1}b_{3}),a_{3}b_{3}(a_{1}b_{2}-a_{2}b_{1})\big)

and consider an integral ternary quadratic form

Q_{1}^{\prime}(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})=c_{1}v_{2}v_{3}+c_{2}v_{1}v_{3}+c_{3}v_{1}v_{2}.

Note that
Q_{1}^{\prime}
satisfies
\|Q_{1}^{\prime}\|\leq 10^{4}R^{12}
and

\|Q_{1}-(\operatorname{det}(Q_{1}^{\prime}))^{-\frac{1}{3}}Q_{1}^{\prime}\|\ll\epsilon R^{6}
.
Now we set
Q^{\prime}:=Q_{1}^{\prime}\circ\operatorname{adj}(\gamma)
, then it holds that

\|Q^{\prime}\|\leq 3\|\operatorname{adj}(\gamma)\|\|Q_{1}^{\prime}\|\leq 10^{6}R^{14}

and

\|Q-(\operatorname{det}(Q^{\prime}))^{-\frac{1}{3}}Q^{\prime}\|\leq 3\|\operatorname{adj}(\gamma)\|\|Q_{1}-(\operatorname{det}(Q_{1}^{\prime}))^{-\frac{1}{3}}Q_{1}^{\prime}\|\ll\epsilon R^{10},

as desired.
∎

Lemma
2.1
directly implies the following qualitative statement.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
Q
be an indefinite irrational ternary quadratic form with
\operatorname{det}Q=1
. There are at most four isotropic rational lines and degenerate rational planes through the origin, respectively.

Proof.

Since for irrational
Q
the dual quadratic form
Q^{*}
is also irrational, by duality it suffices to show the statement for
\Delta_{Q}
-rational lines. Let
Q=Q_{0}^{g}
for some
g\in G
. Suppose for contradiction that there are five isotropic rational lines through the origin. Then we may find
\mathbf{m}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{m}_{5}\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
for which
Q(\mathbf{m}_{i})=Q_{0}(g\mathbf{m}_{i})=0
for
1\leq i\leq 5
and no pair among these vectors lie on the same line.

Note that
g\mathbf{m}_{i}
’s are on the light cone
\mathcal{H}:=\left\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:Q_{0}(v)=0\right\}
, and the intersection of
\mathcal{H}
and any plane through the origin is the union of at most two lines through the origin. Thus, no three of
g\mathbf{m}_{1},\ldots,g\mathbf{m}_{5}
lie on the same plane through the origin. Then Lemma
2.1
with
\epsilon=0
contradicts the assumption that
Q
is irrational.
∎

Definition 2.3
.

For
\eta>0
and
M>1
we say that a vector
v\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
is
(\eta,M)
-quasi-null if
|Q_{0}(v)|<\eta\|v\|^{-50M}
. We denote by
\mathcal{H}_{\eta,M}
the set of
(\eta,M)
-quasi-null vectors, i.e.

\mathcal{H}_{\eta,M}:=\left\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:|Q_{0}(v)|<\eta\|v\|^{-50M}\right\}.

The following lemma asserts that for a quadratic form
Q
of Diophantine type
M
, there cannot be many
(\eta,M)
-quasi-null vectors in
\Delta_{Q}
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
M>1
. For an indefinite ternary quadratic form
Q
of Diophantine type
M
with
\operatorname{det}Q=1
, there exists
0<\eta<1
such that the following holds. For any
R>10
the set
\left\{v\in\Delta_{Q}\cap\mathcal{H}_{\eta,M}:R\leq\|v\|<R^{2}\right\}
is contained in at most six planes.

Proof.

Suppose for contradiction that for any
0<\eta<1
there exists
R=R(\eta)>10
such that the set

\left\{v\in\Delta_{Q}\cap\mathcal{H}_{\eta,M}:R\leq\|v\|<R^{2}\right\}

is not contained in any union of six planes through the origin. Then we can find five nonzero vectors
\mathbf{v}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{v}_{5}\in\Delta_{Q}\cap\mathcal{H}_{\eta,M}
for which no three of them lie on the same plane through the origin and
R\leq\|\mathbf{v}_{i}\|<R^{2}
for
1\leq i\leq 5
. Let
Q=Q_{0}^{g}
for some
g\in G
. Then there are nonzero integral vectors
\mathbf{m}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{m}_{5}
such that
\mathbf{v}_{i}=g\mathbf{m}_{i}
and for
1\leq i\leq 5
. Since
Q(\mathbf{m}_{i})=Q_{0}(\mathbf{v}_{i})
and
\mathbf{v}_{i}\in\mathcal{H}_{\eta,M}
, we have

|Q(\mathbf{m}_{i})|=|Q_{0}(\mathbf{v}_{i})|\leq\eta\|\mathbf{v}_{i}\|^{-50M}\leq\eta R^{-50M}

and
\|\mathbf{m}_{i}\|\leq\|g^{-1}\|\|\mathbf{v}_{i}\|<\|g^{-1}\|R^{2}
for
1\leq i\leq 5
. By Lemma
2.1
there exists a nonzero integral ternary quadratic form
Q^{\prime}
satisfying

\|Q-\rho Q^{\prime}\|\ll(\eta R^{-50M})(\|g^{-1}\|R^{2})^{10}=\eta\|g^{-1}\|R^{-50M+20}

and
\|Q^{\prime}\|\leq 10^{6}\|g^{-1}\|^{14}R^{28}
, where
\rho=(\operatorname{det}Q^{\prime})^{-\frac{1}{3}}
. For any
c>0
this implies

\|Q-\rho Q^{\prime}\|\leq\eta\|g^{-1}\|R^{-28M}\leq c\|Q^{\prime}\|^{-M},

provided
\eta
is sufficiently small. However, this contradicts that
Q
is of Diophantine type
M
. This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
Q
be an indefinite ternary quadratic form with
\operatorname{det}Q=1
. Let
L\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a plane, and let
\mathbf{m}_{1},\mathbf{m}_{2},\mathbf{m}_{3}\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}\in L
be integral vectors such that
\mathbf{m}_{i}
and
\mathbf{m}_{j}
are not on the same line for all
1\leq i\neq j\leq 3
. For
0\leq\epsilon<1
and
R>1
if
|Q(\mathbf{m}_{i})|\leq\epsilon
and
R<\|\mathbf{m}_{i}\|<R^{2}
for all
1\leq i\leq 3
, then
\epsilon\gg R^{-64}
holds, where the implied constant depends on
Q
.

Proof.

Let us take a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis
\left\{\mathbf{n}_{1},\mathbf{n}_{2}\right\}
of
L\cap\mathbb{Z}^{3}
. As
\|\mathbf{m}_{i}\|\leq R^{2}
for
1\leq i\leq 3
, we may choose
\left\{\mathbf{n}_{1},\mathbf{n}_{2}\right\}
so that
\|\mathbf{n}_{1}\|,\|\mathbf{n}_{2}\|\leq R^{2}
. For
1\leq i\leq 3
we may write
\mathbf{m}_{i}=a_{i1}\mathbf{n}_{1}+a_{i2}\mathbf{n}_{2}
for some
a_{i1},a_{i2}\in\mathbb{Z}
with
|a_{i1}|,|a_{i2}|\ll R^{2}
. Then we have
Q(\mathbf{m}_{i})=a_{i1}^{2}Q(\mathbf{n}_{1})+2a_{i1}a_{i2}Q(\mathbf{n}_{1},\mathbf{n}_{2})+a_{i2}^{2}Q(\mathbf{n}_{2})
for
1\leq i\leq 3
, i.e.

\left(\begin{matrix}a_{11}^{2}&a_{11}a_{12}&a_{12}^{2}\\
a_{21}^{2}&a_{21}a_{22}&a_{22}^{2}\\
a_{31}^{2}&a_{31}a_{32}&a_{32}^{2}\end{matrix}\right)\left(\begin{matrix}Q(\mathbf{n}_{1})\\
2Q(\mathbf{n}_{1},\mathbf{n}_{2})\\
Q(\mathbf{n}_{2})\end{matrix}\right)=\left(\begin{matrix}Q(\mathbf{m}_{1})\\
Q(\mathbf{m}_{2})\\
Q(\mathbf{m}_{3})\end{matrix}\right).

Let
\gamma=\left(\begin{matrix}a_{11}^{2}&a_{11}a_{12}&a_{12}^{2}\\
a_{21}^{2}&a_{21}a_{22}&a_{22}^{2}\\
a_{31}^{2}&a_{31}a_{32}&a_{32}^{2}\end{matrix}\right)
. Note that
\|\gamma\|\ll R^{4}
,
\|\operatorname{adj}(\gamma)\|\ll R^{8}
, and moreover, by a projective version of the Van der Monde matrix we have a formula

\operatorname{det}(\gamma)=\prod_{1\leq i\neq j\leq 3}(a_{i1}a_{j2}-a_{i2}a_{j1})\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\left\{0\right\}.

Here,
\operatorname{det}\gamma\neq 0
is by the assumption that
\mathbf{m}_{i}
and
\mathbf{m}_{j}
are not on the same line for all
1\leq i<j\leq 3
. It follows that
|Q(\mathbf{n}_{1})|,|Q(\mathbf{n}_{1},\mathbf{n}_{2})|,|Q(\mathbf{n}_{2})|
are bounded by

\ll\|\gamma^{-1}\|\max_{1\leq i\leq 3}|Q(\mathbf{m}_{i})|\leq\|\operatorname{adj}(\gamma)\|\max_{1\leq i\leq 3}|Q(\mathbf{m}_{i})|\ll R^{8}\epsilon.

Let
\mathbf{n}_{3}=\mathbf{n}_{1}\times\mathbf{n}_{2}
and let
\gamma^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Mat}_{3,3}(\mathbb{Z})
be the matrix whose columns are
\mathbf{n}_{1},\mathbf{n}_{2},\mathbf{n}_{3}
. For
Q_{1}=Q\circ(\gamma^{\prime})^{-1}
we have
Q_{1}(\mathbf{e}_{i},\mathbf{e}_{j})=Q(\mathbf{n}_{i},\mathbf{n}_{j})
for all
1\leq i,j\leq 3
. Note that
\|\gamma^{\prime}\|\ll R^{4}
,
\|\operatorname{adj}(\gamma^{\prime})\|\ll R^{8}
, hence
\|(\gamma^{\prime})^{-1}\|\leq|\operatorname{det}\gamma^{\prime}|^{-1}\|\operatorname{adj}(\gamma^{\prime})\|\ll R^{8}
. This implies that
|Q_{1}(\mathbf{e}_{i},\mathbf{e}_{j})|\leq\|Q\circ(\gamma^{\prime})^{-1}\|\ll\|(\gamma^{\prime})^{-1}\|^{2}\ll R^{16}
for all
1\leq i,j\leq 3
. We also know that
|Q_{1}(\mathbf{e}_{i},\mathbf{e}_{j})|=|Q(\mathbf{n}_{i},\mathbf{n}_{j})|\ll R^{8}\epsilon
for
1\leq i,j\leq 2
, so
|\operatorname{det}Q_{1}|\ll R^{40}\epsilon
. Therefore we get

1=|\operatorname{det}Q|=|\operatorname{det}Q_{1}||\operatorname{det}\gamma^{\prime}|^{2}\ll(R^{40}\epsilon)(R^{12})^{2}=R^{64}\epsilon.

∎

Combining this with Lemma
2.4
, we obtain the following.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
M>1
. For an indefinite ternary quadratic form
Q
of Diophantine type
M
with
\operatorname{det}Q=1
, there exists
0<\eta<1
such that the following holds. For any
R>10
the set
\left\{v\in\Delta_{Q}\cap\mathcal{H}_{\eta,M}:R\leq\|v\|<R^{2}\right\}

lines.

Proof.

According to Lemma
2.4
the set
\left\{v\in\Delta_{Q}\cap\mathcal{H}_{\eta,M}:R\leq\|v\|<R^{2}\right\}

2.3.
Margulis functions excluding quasi-null vectors

To improve the exponent
\lambda
in the moment of the Margulis function
\int_{-1}^{1}\alpha(a_{t}u_{r}\Delta_{Q})^{\lambda}dr
beyond
1
, one needs to exclude the contribution of quasi-null vectors in
\Delta_{Q}
. We introduce the following modification of
\alpha
excluding isotropic or quasi-null vectors. We define
\widehat{\alpha}_{1},\widehat{\alpha}_{2},\widehat{\alpha}:H\times X\to(0,\infty)
by

\widehat{\alpha}_{1}(g;\Delta):=\sup\left\{\|gv\|^{-1}:v\in\Delta\setminus\mathcal{H}\right\},\quad\widehat{\alpha}_{2}(g;\Delta):=\widehat{\alpha}_{1}(g^{*};\Delta^{*}),

\widehat{\alpha}(g;\Delta)=\max\left\{\widehat{\alpha}_{1}(g;\Delta),\widehat{\alpha}_{2}(g;\Delta)\right\}.

For
\eta>0
and
M>1
we also define
\widehat{\alpha}_{1,\eta,M},\widehat{\alpha}_{2,\eta,M},\widehat{\alpha}_{\eta,M},\widehat{\alpha}_{\eta,M}^{\prime}:H\times X\to(0,\infty)
by

(2.3)

{\widehat{\alpha}_{1,\eta,M}(g;\Delta):=\sup\left\{\|gv\|^{-1}:v\in\Delta\setminus\mathcal{H}_{\eta,M}\right\},\quad\widehat{\alpha}_{2,\eta,M}(g;\Delta):=\widehat{\alpha}_{1,\eta,M}(g^{*};\Delta^{*}),}

(2.4)

{\widehat{\alpha}_{\eta,M}(g;\Delta):=\max\left\{\widehat{\alpha}_{1,\eta,M}(g;\Delta),\widehat{\alpha}_{2,\eta,M}(g;\Delta)\right\},}

(2.5)

{\widehat{\alpha}^{\prime}_{\eta,M}(g;\Delta):=\max\left\{\widehat{\alpha}_{\eta,M}(g;\Delta),\alpha(g\Delta)^{0.9}.\right\}}

By definitions, for any
\eta
,
M
, and
\Delta
we have

\widehat{\alpha}(g;\Delta)\leq\alpha(g\Delta)\leq\widehat{\alpha}(g;\Delta)+\!\!\!\sup_{v\in\Delta\cap\mathcal{H}\setminus\left\{0\right\}}\!\!\!\|gv\|^{-1}+\!\!\!\sup_{v\in\Delta^{*}\cap\mathcal{H}\setminus\left\{0\right\}}\!\!\!\|g^{*}v\|^{-1},

\widehat{\alpha}_{\eta,M}(g;\Delta)\leq\widehat{\alpha}(g;\Delta)\leq\alpha(g\Delta)\leq\widehat{\alpha}_{\eta,M}(g;\Delta)+\!\!\!\!\!\!\sup_{v\in\Delta\cap\mathcal{H}_{\eta,M}\setminus\left\{0\right\}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\|g^{*}v\|^{-1}+\!\!\!\!\!\!\sup_{v\in\Delta^{*}\cap\mathcal{H}_{\eta,M}\setminus\left\{0\right\}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\|gv\|^{-1},

\widehat{\alpha}_{\eta,M}^{\prime}(g;\Delta)\leq\widehat{\alpha}_{\eta,M}(g;\Delta)+\!\!\!\!\!\!\sup_{\-v\in\Delta\cap\mathcal{H}_{\eta,M}\setminus\left\{0\right\}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\|gv\|^{-0.9}+\!\!\!\!\!\!\sup_{\-v\in\Delta^{*}\cap\mathcal{H}_{\eta,M}\setminus\left\{0\right\}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\|g^{*}v\|^{-0.9}.

Let
J:=\left(\begin{matrix}&&1\\
&-1&\\
1&&\end{matrix}\right)\in H
. Then we have
J=J^{-1}=\mskip 1.0mu\prescript{\smash{\mathrm{t}\mkern-1.0mu}}{}{\mathstrut J}=J^{*}
. We observe that

Ja_{t}J^{-1}=a_{t}^{*},\quad Ju_{r}J^{-1}=u_{r}^{*},\quad J\mskip 1.0mu\prescript{\smash{\mathrm{t}\mkern-1.0mu}}{}{\mathstrut u_{r}}J^{-1}=u_{r}^{-1}=(\mskip 1.0mu\prescript{\smash{\mathrm{t}\mkern-1.0mu}}{}{\mathstrut u_{r}})^{*}

for any

, hence
JgJ^{-1}=g^{*}
holds for any
g\in H
. We also observe that
J
preserves
\|\cdot\|
and
Q_{0}(\cdot)
, since
Jv=(v_{3},-v_{2},v_{1})
for
v=(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
. It follows that
J
preserves
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{H}_{\eta,M}
for any
\eta>0
and
M>1
.

We readily see from these properties that for any
g\in H
and
\Delta\in X

\displaystyle\widehat{\alpha}_{1}(g;\Delta)

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{\|gv\|^{-1}:v\in\Delta\setminus\mathcal{H}\right\}

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{\|g^{*}Jv\|^{-1}:v\in\Delta\setminus\mathcal{H}\right\}

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{\|g^{*}v\|^{-1}:v\in J\Delta\setminus\mathcal{H}\right\}

\displaystyle=\widehat{\alpha}_{2}(g;J\Delta^{*}),

hence

\widehat{\alpha}(g;\Delta)=\max\left\{\widehat{\alpha}_{1}(g;\Delta),\widehat{\alpha}_{2}(g;\Delta)\right\}=\max\left\{\widehat{\alpha}_{1}(g;\Delta),\widehat{\alpha}_{1}(g;J\Delta^{*})\right\}=\widehat{\alpha}(g;J\Delta^{*}).

Similarly, we also have

\widehat{\alpha}_{1,\eta,M}(g;\Delta)=\widehat{\alpha}_{2,\eta,M}(g;J\Delta^{*}),\quad\widehat{\alpha}_{\eta,M}(g;\Delta)=\widehat{\alpha}_{\eta,M}(g;J\Delta^{*}),\quad\widehat{\alpha}_{\eta,M}^{\prime}(g;\Delta)=\widehat{\alpha}_{\eta,M}^{\prime}(g;J\Delta^{*})

for any
\eta>0
,

,

and
\Delta\in X
.

Theorem 2.7

(Main moment esitmates)
.

For any
M>1
there exists a constant
\delta=\delta(M)>0
such that the following holds. If

is of type
M
, then for any
\eta>0

(2.6)

{\sup_{t>0}\int_{-1}^{1}\widehat{\alpha}_{\eta,M}(a_{t}u_{r};\Delta_{Q})^{1+\delta}dr<\infty.}

1.7
assuming Theorem

.

. Let

for some

. Since

2.4.
Outline of the proof of Theorem
2.7

Let
0<\delta\leq 0.01
. In Section 2.3 we define a function
\phi_{\delta}:\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\left\{0\right\}\to(0,\infty]
by
\phi_{\delta}(v)=\kappa(v)^{-2\delta}\|v\|^{-1-\delta}
, where roughly speaking
\kappa:\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\left\{0\right\}\to[0,1]
measures the distance to the surface
\mathcal{H}
. Then
\phi_{\delta}
satisfies
\phi_{\delta}(v)\geq\|v\|^{-1-\delta}
for all
v\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
, and the following contraction inequality holds (Proposition
3.8
):

(2.7)

{\int_{-1}^{1}\phi_{\delta}(a_{s}u_{r}v)dr\leq 80\delta^{-1}e^{-\delta s}\phi_{\delta}(v)}

for any
s\geq 1
and
v\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
.

In Section 2.4, using the function
\phi_{\delta}
, for
\eta>0
and
M>1
we define a modified height function
\widetilde{\alpha}_{\delta,\eta,M}:H\times X\to[1,\infty]
satisfying

\widetilde{\alpha}_{\delta,\eta,M}(g;\Delta)\geq\max\left\{\widehat{\alpha}_{\eta,M}(g;\Delta)^{1+\delta},\alpha(g\Delta)^{1-3\delta}\right\}

for any
(g,\Delta)\in H\times X
. We then establish the following subharmonic estimate (Proposition
4.3
), using (
2.7
). If
\delta>0
is a sufficiently small constant depending on
M
, then for any large
s
it holds that

(2.8)

{\int_{-1}^{1}\widetilde{\alpha}_{\delta,\eta,M}(a_{s}u_{r}g;\Delta)dr\leq e^{-\frac{\delta}{2}s}\widetilde{\alpha}_{\delta,\eta,M}(g;\Delta)+e^{9s},}

unless
(g,\Delta)
lies within a certain set
\mathcal{E}_{s,\eta,M}\subset H\times X
, where the fibers over

have small volume.

To derive (
2.8
) from (
2.7
) we follow closely the strategy of
[
EMM98
]
. However, here we further need to control the effect of the factor
\kappa(gv)^{-2\delta}
, which is large when the vector

is close to the surface
\mathcal{H}
. The estimate for such contribution is provided by a supremum-version of the contraction inequality for
\phi_{\delta}
(Proposition
4.5
), where the supremum is taken over the vectors very close to

. The main ingredient of the proof of Proposition

is a quantitative version of the following simple geometric observation: for any plane in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, the intersection of the plane and

is the union of at most two lines in

.

We remark that (

) holds only for

. Namely, in Proposition

we show that

{\int_{-1}^{1}\widehat{\alpha}_{\eta,M}^{\prime}(a_{t}u_{r};\Delta)^{1+\delta}\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{E}_{s,\eta,M}}(a_{t}u_{r};\Delta)dr\ll e^{-10s}}

for any

and

, where

[

, Theorem 2]
(see also
[

, Proposition 4.6]
).

In Section 2.6, we combine the subharmonic estimate (

) from Section 2.3 and the avoidance estimate (

) from Section 4, hence completing the proof of Theorem

.

and
g\in G

\widetilde{f}(\Delta)\leq c\alpha(\Delta),\quad\widehat{f}(g;\Delta)\leq c\widehat{\alpha}(g;\Delta),\quad\widehat{f}_{\eta,M}(g;\Delta)\leq c\widehat{\alpha}_{\eta,M}(g;\Delta).

Proof.

The first inequality
\widetilde{f}(\Delta)\leq c\alpha(\Delta)
is the Lipschitz principle of Schmidt, and the other two inequalities are direct modifications; see
[
Sch68
, Lemma 2]
.
∎

In §7.2 we will prove the following proposition, using Theorem
2.7
.

Proposition 2.9

(Equidistribution of Siegel transform)
.

Let
Q
be an indefinite quadratic form with
\operatorname{det}Q=1
, which is not EWA. For any bounded Riemann integrable function
f
compactly supported on
\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
and
\nu\in C(K)
we have

\lim_{t\to\infty}\int_{K}\widehat{f}(a_{t}k;\Delta_{Q})\nu(k)dk=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f(v)dv\int_{K}\nu(k)dk.

Then Theorem
1.3
is deduced from Proposition
2.9
by an argument identical to that used in
[
EMM98
, §3.4, §3.5]
to deduce
[
EMM98
, Theorem 2.1]
from
[
EMM98
, Theorem 2.3]
. Roughly speaking, for the region
\mathcal{W}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
defined by

\mathcal{W}:=\left\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:a<Q_{0}(v)<b,\;\tfrac{1}{2}\leq\|v\|\leq 1,\;v_{1}>0,\;\tfrac{1}{2}v_{1}\leq|v_{2}|\leq v_{1}\right\},

using the fact that
K
preserves both
\|\cdot\|
and
Q_{0}(\cdot)
one can calculate that

ce^{t}\lim_{t\to\infty}\int_{K}\widehat{\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{W}}}(a_{t}k;\Delta_{Q})dk\approx\begin{cases}1\quad\textrm{ if }\tfrac{e^{t}}{2}\leq\|v\|\leq e^{t}\;,a<Q_{0}(v)<b,\\
0\quad\textrm{ otherwise}\end{cases}

for some constant
c>0
, where
\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{W}}
is the characteristic function on
\mathcal{W}
. This implies that

\#\left\{v\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}:\frac{1}{2}e^{t}\leq\|v\|\leq e^{t}\textrm{ and }a<Q(v)<b\right\}\approx ce^{t}\lim_{t\to\infty}\int_{K}\widehat{\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{W}}}(a_{t}k;\Delta_{Q})dk.

Based on this relation, Theorem
1.3
follows from Proposition
2.9
with
f=\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{W}}
and
\nu=\mathds{1}_{K}
.

3.1.
Known estimates

In this subsection, we review several estimates from
[
EMM98
]
, along with certain variants derived from them. We first recall the following contraction inequality from
[
EMM98
, Lemma 5.1]
.

Lemma 3.1

(Contraction for
\|\cdot\|^{-\lambda}
)
.

Let
\frac{1}{2}\leq\lambda\leq 1
. For any
t>0
and
w\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
we have

\int_{-1}^{1}\|a_{t}u_{r}w\|^{-\lambda}dr\leq 100e^{-\frac{(1-\lambda)t}{3}}\|w\|^{-\lambda}.

Utilising the arguments in
[
EMM98
, Lemma 5.1]
(or the proof of Proposition
3.8
in this section alternatively) one can also obtain:

Lemma 3.2

(Bounded expansion for
\|\cdot\|^{-1-\delta}
)
.

Let
0<\delta<\frac{1}{2}
. For any
t>0
and
w\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
we have

\int_{-1}^{1}\|a_{t}u_{r}w\|^{-1-\delta}dr\leq 40\delta^{-1}e^{\delta t}\|w\|^{-1-\delta}.

We note that the analogous inequalities to those in Lemma
3.1
and Lemma
3.2
also hold when
a_{t}u_{r}w
is replaced by
a_{t}^{*}u_{r}^{*}w
.

As in
[
EMM98
, §5]
, Lemma
3.1
and Lemma
3.2
indeed imply the following estimates, respectively, without any assumption on
\Delta\in X
.

Lemma 3.3

(Subharmonic estimate for
\alpha^{\lambda}
)
.

Let
\frac{1}{2}\leq\lambda<1
. For any
t>0
and
\Delta\in X
we have

\int_{-1}^{1}\alpha(a_{t}u_{r}\Delta)^{\lambda}dr\leq 100e^{-\frac{(1-\lambda)t}{3}}\alpha(\Delta)^{\lambda}+e^{4t}.

Lemma 3.4

(Superharmonic estimate for
\alpha^{1+\delta}
)
.

Let
0<\delta<\frac{1}{2}
. For any
\Delta\in X
and
t>0
we have

\int_{-1}^{1}\alpha(a_{t}u_{r}\Delta)^{1+\delta}dr\leq 400\delta^{-1}e^{\delta t}\alpha(\Delta)^{1+\delta}+e^{4t}.

Note that for any
t>0
,
r\in\mathbb{R}
, and
w\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
the following log-Lipschitz property holds:

(3.1)

{(3e^{t})^{-1}\|w\|\leq\|a_{t}u_{r}w\|\leq 3e^{t}\|w\|.}

We also note expansion bounds for
\widehat{\alpha}_{\eta,M}
and
\widehat{\alpha}_{\eta,M}^{\prime}
, which are deduced from Lemma
3.2
.

Lemma 3.5

(An expansion bound for
\widehat{\alpha}_{\eta,M}^{1+\delta}
)
.

Let
0<\eta<1
,
M>1
, and
0<\delta<0.01
. For any
g\in H
and
\Delta\in X
and
t>0
we have

\int_{-1}^{1}\widehat{\alpha}_{\eta,M}(a_{t}u_{r}g;\Delta)^{1+\delta}dr\leq 80\delta^{-1}e^{\delta t}\widehat{\alpha}_{\eta,M}(g;\Delta)^{1+\delta}+80\delta^{-1}\alpha(g\Delta)^{0.9}+\tfrac{1}{2}e^{6t}.

Proof.

Let
v\in\Delta\setminus\mathcal{H}_{\eta,M}
be a vector such that
\widehat{\alpha}_{1,\eta,M}(g;\Delta)=\|gv\|^{-1}
. Suppose that there exists
w\in\Delta\setminus\mathcal{H}_{\eta,M}
with
v\wedge w\neq 0
and
\|gw\|\leq(3e^{t})^{2}\|gv\|
. Then we have

\alpha_{2}(g\Delta)^{-1}\leq\|gv\wedge gw\|\leq\|gv\|\|gw\|\leq(3e^{t})^{2}\widehat{\alpha}_{1,\eta,M}(g;\Delta)^{-2},

hence
\widehat{\alpha}_{1,\eta,M}(g;\Delta)\leq 3e^{t}\alpha_{2}(g\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}
. It follows that

\widehat{\alpha}_{1,\eta,M}(a_{t}u_{r}g;\Delta)\leq(3e^{t})\widehat{\alpha}_{1,\eta,M}(g;\Delta)\leq(3e^{t})^{2}\alpha_{2}(g\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}

for any
r\in[-1,1]
.

If there is no such
w\in\Delta\setminus\mathcal{H}_{\eta,M}
, i.e.
\|gw\|>(3e^{t})^{2}\|gv\|
for all
w\in\Delta\setminus\mathcal{H}_{\eta,M}
with
v\wedge w\neq 0
, then we see that
\|a_{t}u_{r}gv\|\leq\|a_{t}u_{r}gw\|
for all
w\in\Delta\setminus\mathcal{H}_{\eta,M}
, hence
\widehat{\alpha}_{1,\eta,M}(a_{t}u_{r}g;\Delta)=\|a_{t}u_{r}v\|^{-1}
.

Combining these two cases, we deduce that

\displaystyle\widehat{\alpha}_{1,\eta,M}(a_{t}u_{r}g;\Delta)^{1+\delta}

\displaystyle\leq\|a_{t}u_{r}gv\|^{-1-\delta}+\left(9e^{2t}\alpha_{2}(g\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{1+\delta}

\displaystyle\leq\|a_{t}u_{r}gv\|^{-1-\delta}+20\delta^{-1}\alpha(g\Delta)^{0.9}+\tfrac{1}{8}e^{6t},

using Young’s inequality for products, i.e.
xy\leq\frac{1}{p}x^{p}+\frac{1}{q}y^{q}
for
p,q>1
with
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
and
x,y\geq 0
. Applying Lemma
3.2
we have

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}\widehat{\alpha}_{1,\eta,M}(a_{t}u_{r}g;\Delta)^{1+\delta}dr

\displaystyle\leq\int_{-1}^{1}\|a_{t}u_{r}gv\|^{-1-\delta}dr+40\delta^{-1}\alpha(g\Delta)^{0.9}+\tfrac{1}{4}e^{6t}

\displaystyle\leq 40\delta^{-1}e^{\delta t}\|gv\|^{-1-\delta}+40\delta^{-1}\alpha(g\Delta)^{0.9}+\tfrac{1}{4}e^{6t}.

\displaystyle=40\delta^{-1}e^{\delta t}\widehat{\alpha}_{1,\eta,M}(g;\Delta)^{1+\delta}+40\delta^{-1}\alpha(g\Delta)^{0.9}+\tfrac{1}{4}e^{6t}.

By a similar argument for
\widehat{\alpha}_{2,\eta,M}(g;\Delta)
we also have

\int_{-1}^{1}\widehat{\alpha}_{2,\eta,M}(a_{t}u_{r}g;\Delta)^{1+\delta}dr\leq 40\delta^{-1}e^{\delta t}\widehat{\alpha}_{2,\eta,M}(g;\Delta)^{1+\delta}+40\delta^{-1}\alpha(g\Delta)^{0.9}+\tfrac{1}{4}e^{6t},

hence the desired estimate follows, as
\displaystyle\sup_{i=1,2}\widehat{\alpha}_{i,\eta,M}=\widehat{\alpha}_{\eta,M}\leq\widehat{\alpha}_{1,\eta,M}+\widehat{\alpha}_{2,\eta,M}
.
∎

Lemma 3.6

(Superharmonic estimate for
\widehat{\alpha}_{\eta,M}^{\prime 1+\delta}
)
.

Let
0<\eta<1
,
M>1
, and
0<\delta<0.01
. For any
g\in H
and
\Delta\in X
and
t>10
we have

\int_{-1}^{1}\widehat{\alpha}_{\eta,M}^{\prime}(a_{t}u_{r}g;\Delta)^{1+\delta}dr\leq 400\delta^{-1}e^{\delta t}\widehat{\alpha}^{\prime}_{\eta,M}(g;\Delta)^{1+\delta}+e^{6t}.

Proof.

Using Lemma
3.5
and Lemma
3.3
we deduce

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}\widehat{\alpha}_{\eta,M}^{\prime}(a_{t}u_{r}g;\Delta)^{1+\delta}dr

\displaystyle\leq\int_{-1}^{1}\widehat{\alpha}_{\eta,M}(a_{t}u_{r}g;\Delta)^{1+\delta}dr+\int_{-1}^{1}\alpha(a_{t}u_{r}g\Delta)^{0.9(1+\delta)}dr

\displaystyle\leq\big(80\delta^{-1}e^{\delta t}\widehat{\alpha}_{\eta,M}(g;\Delta)^{1+\delta}+80\delta^{-1}\alpha(g\Delta)^{0.9}+\tfrac{1}{2}e^{6t}\big)

\displaystyle+\big(100e^{-0.01t}\alpha(g\Delta)^{0.9(1+\delta)}+e^{4t}\big)

\displaystyle\leq 400\delta^{-1}e^{\delta t}\widehat{\alpha}^{\prime}_{\eta,M}(g;\Delta)^{1+\delta}+e^{6t}.

∎

3.2.
Auxiliary functions

For
w=(w_{1},w_{2},w_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
we define three quantifiers. For
w
with
w_{2}=w_{3}=0
we set
\rho(w)=\infty
,
\kappa_{0}(w)=\infty
, and
\kappa(w)=1
. Assuming
(w_{2},w_{3})\neq(0,0)
we define

(3.2)

{\rho(w):=-\frac{w_{2}}{w_{3}}\in[-\infty,\infty],}

(3.3)

{\kappa_{0}(w):=\frac{Q_{0}(w)}{w_{3}^{2}}\in[-\infty,\infty],}

(3.4)

{\kappa(w):=\begin{cases}|\kappa_{0}(w)|&\text{ if }|\kappa_{0}(w)|<1\text{ and }|\rho(w)|<2,\\
1&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}}

We first observe that
\rho(a_{t}u_{r}w)=e^{t}(\rho(w)-r)
and
\kappa_{0}(a_{t}u_{r}w)=e^{2t}\kappa_{0}(w)
for any
t,r\in\mathbb{R}
. By a straightforward matrix calculation, we also observe that

(3.5)

{a_{t}u_{r}w=w_{3}\left(\begin{matrix}e^{t}(w_{1}+rw_{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}r^{2}w_{3})\\
w_{2}+rw_{3}\\
e^{-t}w_{3}\end{matrix}\right)=w_{3}\left(\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{2}e^{t}\left\{(r-\rho(w))^{2}-\kappa_{0}(w)\right\}\\
r-\rho(w)\\
e^{-t}\end{matrix}\right)}

for any
t,r\in\mathbb{R}
, and
w\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times(\mathbb{R}\setminus\left\{0\right\})
.

Lemma 3.7

(Growth of
\kappa
)
.

For any
t\geq 1
,
|r|\leq 1
, and
w\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
we have

{\kappa(a_{t}u_{r}w)\geq\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }\kappa(w)\geq e^{-2t},\\
e^{2t}\kappa(w)&\text{ if }\kappa(w)<e^{-2t}.\end{cases}}

Furthermore, if
\kappa(a_{t}u_{r}w)<1
then
\kappa(a_{t}u_{r}w)=e^{2t}\kappa(w)
.

Proof.

Since
a_{t}
and
u_{r}
are in
H
, they stabilize
Q_{0}
.

Case 1
. We suppose
\kappa(w)\geq e^{-2t}
.

In this case we have either
|\rho(w)|\geq 2
or
|\kappa_{0}(w)|\geq e^{-2t}
. If
|\rho(w)|\geq 2
, then
|\rho(a_{t}u_{r}w)|\geq e^{t}|r-\rho(w)|=e^{t}(|\rho(w)|-|r|)>2
, hence
\kappa(a_{t}u_{r}w)=1
. If
|\kappa_{0}(w)|\geq e^{-2t}
, then
|\kappa_{0}(a_{t}u_{r}w)|=e^{2t}|\kappa_{0}(w)|\geq 1
, hence
\kappa(a_{t}u_{r}w)=1
.

Case 2
. We suppose
\kappa(w)<e^{-2t}
.

In this case we have
\kappa(w)=|\kappa_{0}(w)|
, hence

(3.6)

{\kappa(a_{t}u_{r}w)\geq|\kappa_{0}(a_{t}u_{r}w)|=e^{2t}|\kappa_{0}(w)|=e^{2t}\kappa(w).}

From
Case 1
we also see that
\kappa(a_{t}u_{r}w)<1
implies
\kappa(w)<1
, hence
\kappa(a_{t}u_{r}w)=|\kappa_{0}(a_{t}u_{r}w)|
and
\kappa(w)=|\kappa_{0}(w)|
. It follows that

{\kappa(a_{t}u_{r}w)=|\kappa_{0}(a_{t}u_{r}w)|=e^{2t}|\kappa_{0}(w)|=e^{2t}\kappa(w).}

∎

3.3.
Contraction for linear actions on
\mathbb{R}^{3}

In this subsection, we construct a modified local height function
\phi_{\delta}
for
0<\delta<0.01
and establish the corresponding contraction inequality. As noted in Remark
1.6
, the contraction inequality for
\|\cdot\|^{-\lambda}
in Lemma
3.1
is no longer valid when the exponent
\lambda>1
. In particular, we observe that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\int_{-1}^{1}\|a_{t}u_{r}v\|^{-\lambda}dr=\infty

for any
\lambda>1
, if the quadratic equation
w_{1}+rw_{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}r^{2}w_{3}
in (
3.5
) has a double root and satisfies
w_{2}+rw_{3}=0
for some
r\in[-1,1]
, i.e.
\kappa_{0}(v)=0
and
|\rho(w)|\leq 1
. However, if this double-zero scenario is excluded, the range of permissible exponents
\lambda
can be refined. This observation motivates the construction of the following modified local height function
\phi_{\delta}
on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, which satisfies
\phi_{\delta}(w)\geq\|w\|^{-1-\delta}
and exhibits the desired contraction property.

For
0<\delta<0.01
let us define
\phi_{\delta}:\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\left\{0\right\}\to(0,\infty]
by

(3.7)

{\phi_{\delta}(w)=\kappa(w)^{-2\delta}\|w\|^{-1-\delta}.}

For any
0\leq\delta<0.1
,
t\geq 1
,
r\in[-1,1]
, and
w\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
, the following log-Lipschitz property holds by (
3.1
) and Lemma
3.7
:

(3.8)

{\phi_{\delta}(a_{t}u_{r}w)\leq(3e^{t})^{1+\delta}\phi_{\delta}(w).}

Moreover, if
\kappa(a_{t}u_{r}w)<1
then

(3.9)

{\phi_{\delta}(a_{t}u_{r}w)\geq(3e^{t})^{-(1+5\delta)}\phi_{\delta}(w).}

This subsection aims to show the following contraction inequality for
\phi_{\delta}
.

Proposition 3.8

(Contraction for
\phi_{\delta}
)
.

Let
0<\delta\leq 0.01
. Then we have

\int_{-1}^{1}\phi_{\delta}(a_{t}u_{r}w)dr\leq 80\delta^{-1}e^{-\delta t}\phi_{\delta}(w)

for any
t\geq 1
and
w\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
.

Proof.

Let
w=(w_{1},w_{2},w_{3})
. If
w_{2}=w_{3}=0
then
a_{t}u_{r}w=e^{t}w
for any
r\in\mathbb{R}
, hence the statement is trivial. If
w_{2}\neq 0
and
w_{3}=0
, then
\kappa(a_{t}u_{r}w)=\kappa(w)=1
for any
r\in\mathbb{R}
, and it is straightforward to see that

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}\phi_{\delta}(a_{t}u_{r}w)dr

\displaystyle=\int_{-1}^{1}\|a_{t}u_{r}w\|^{-1-\delta}dr

\displaystyle=\int_{-1}^{1}\max\left\{e^{t}|w_{1}+w_{2}r|,|w_{2}|\right\}^{-1-\delta}dr

\displaystyle\leq 10\delta^{-1}e^{-t}\|w\|^{-1-\delta}=10\delta^{-1}e^{-t}\phi_{\delta}(w)

for any
t\geq 1
. We now assume
w_{3}\neq 0
and let

f_{w}(r):=(r-\rho(w))^{2}-\kappa_{0}(w)=\frac{2}{w_{3}}\big(w_{1}+w_{2}r+\tfrac{1}{2}w_{3}r^{2}\big).

Case 1
.
\kappa(w)\leq e^{-t}
.

By Lemma
3.7
we have
\kappa(a_{t}u_{r}w)\geq e^{t}\kappa(w)
for any
t\geq 1
and
|r|<1
. In combination with the expansion bound for
\|\cdot\|^{-1-\delta}
in Lemma
3.2
it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}\phi_{\delta}(a_{t}u_{r}w)dr

\displaystyle=\int_{-1}^{1}\kappa(a_{t}u_{r}w)^{-2\delta}\|a_{t}u_{r}w\|^{-1-\delta}dr

\displaystyle\leq e^{-2\delta t}\kappa(w)^{-2\delta}\int_{-1}^{1}\|a_{t}u_{r}w\|^{-1-\delta}dr

\displaystyle\leq e^{-2\delta t}\kappa(w)^{-2\delta}(40\delta^{-1}e^{\delta t}\|w\|^{-1-\delta})

\displaystyle=40\delta^{-1}e^{-\delta t}\kappa(w)^{-2\delta}\|w\|^{-1-\delta}=40\delta^{-1}e^{-\delta t}\phi_{\delta}(w).

Case 2
.
|\rho(w)|\geq 2
and hence
\kappa(w)=1
.

In this case
\kappa(a_{t}u_{r}w)=1
for any
|r|\leq 1
by Lemma
3.7
. Since
f_{w}^{\prime}(r)=2(r-\rho(w))
, we have
|f_{w}^{\prime}(r)|=2|r-\rho(w)|\geq\frac{1}{2}|\rho(w)|
for any
r\in[-\frac{3}{2},\frac{3}{2}]
. In particular,
f_{w}^{\prime}
does not change the sign on
[-\frac{3}{2},\frac{3}{2}]
, and there is at most one zero of
f_{w}
on
[-\frac{3}{2},\frac{3}{2}]
.

Subcase 2-1
. There is one zero of
f_{w}
on
[-\frac{3}{2},\frac{3}{2}]
.

Let
r_{0}\in[-\frac{3}{2},\frac{3}{2}]
be the zero of
f_{w}
. Then
f_{w}(r_{0})=0
and
|\rho(2)|\geq 2
imply that

|w_{1}|=\left|r_{0}w_{2}+\frac{r_{0}^{2}}{2}w_{3}\right|\leq|r_{0}|\big(|w_{2}|+\frac{|w_{3}|}{2}\big)\leq 2|w_{2}|,

hence
\|w\|=\max(|w_{1}|,|w_{2}|,|w_{3}|)\leq 2|w_{2}|
. We also observe that

|f_{w}(r)|=|(r-\rho(w))^{2}-(r_{0}-\rho(w))^{2}|=|r-r_{0}||r+r_{0}-2\rho(w)|\geq\tfrac{1}{2}|\rho(w)||r-r_{0}|

for any
r\in[-\frac{3}{2},\frac{3}{2}]
. It follows that

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}

\displaystyle\phi_{\delta}(a_{t}u_{r}w)dr=\int_{-1}^{1}\|a_{t}u_{r}w\|^{-1-\delta}dr

\displaystyle\leq\int_{e^{-t}\leq|r-r_{0}|<3}\left|\tfrac{1}{2}w_{3}e^{t}f_{w}(r)\right|^{-1-\delta}dr+\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}|r-r_{0}|<e^{-t},\\
|r|<1\end{subarray}}\left|w_{3}(r-\rho(w))\right|^{-1-\delta}dr.

The first integral is bounded by

\displaystyle\int_{e^{-t}\leq|r-r_{0}|<3}\left|\tfrac{1}{2}w_{3}e^{t}f_{w}(r)\right|^{-1-\delta}dr

\displaystyle\leq 2^{1+\delta}|w_{3}e^{t}\rho(w)|^{-1-\delta}\int_{e^{-t}\leq|r-r_{0}|<3}|r-r_{0}|^{-1-\delta}dr

\displaystyle\leq 2^{2+\delta}\delta^{-1}e^{\delta t}|w_{3}e^{t}\rho(w)|^{-1-\delta}\leq 2^{2+\delta}\delta^{-1}e^{-t}|w_{2}|^{-1-\delta}.

The second integral is bounded by

\displaystyle\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}|r-r_{0}|<e^{-t},\\
|r|<1\end{subarray}}\left|\tfrac{1}{2}w_{3}(r-\rho(w))\right|^{-1-\delta}dr

\displaystyle\leq 8^{1+\delta}\int_{|r-r_{0}|<e^{-t}}|w_{3}\rho(w)|^{-1-\delta}dr

\displaystyle\leq 10e^{-t}|w_{3}\rho(w)|^{-1-\delta}=10e^{-t}|w_{2}|^{-1-\delta}.

since
|r-\rho(w)|\geq\frac{1}{4}|\rho(w)|
for any
r\in[-1,1]
. Hence, for any
t>0
we have

\int_{-1}^{1}\phi_{\delta}(a_{t}u_{r}w)dr\leq(10+2^{2+\delta}\delta^{-1})e^{-t}|w_{2}|^{-1-\delta}\leq 10\delta^{-1}e^{-t}\|w\|^{-1-\delta}=10\delta^{-1}e^{-t}\phi_{\delta}(w).

Subcase 2-2
. There is no zero of
f_{w}
on
[-\frac{3}{2},\frac{3}{2}]
.

Recall that
f_{w}^{\prime}
does not change the sign on
[-\frac{3}{2},\frac{3}{2}]
and
|f_{w}^{\prime}(r)|=2|r-\rho(w)|\geq\frac{1}{2}|\rho(w)|
for any
r\in[-\frac{3}{2},\frac{3}{2}]
. It follows that
|f_{w}(r)|\geq\frac{1}{4}|\rho(w)|
for any
r\in[-1,1]
. On the other hand, we also have

\displaystyle|w_{3}f_{w}(r)|

\displaystyle=2|w_{1}+rw_{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}r^{2}w_{3}|

\displaystyle\geq 2\left(|w_{1}|-|w_{2}|-\frac{|w_{2}|}{2|\rho(w)|}\right)\geq 2|w_{1}|-3|w_{2}|

for any
r\in[-1,1]
. It follows that

|w_{3}f_{w}(r)|\geq\max\left\{\frac{1}{4}|w_{2}|,2|w_{1}|-3|w_{2}|\right\}\geq\frac{1}{8}\max(|w_{1}|,|w_{2}|)\geq\frac{1}{8}\|w\|.

We thus obtain an estimate that

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}\phi_{\delta}(a_{t}u_{r}w)dr

\displaystyle=\int_{-1}^{1}\|a_{t}u_{r}w\|^{-1-\delta}dr

\displaystyle\leq 2^{1+\delta}\int_{-1}^{1}\left|w_{3}e^{t}f_{w}(r)\right|^{-1-\delta}dr

\displaystyle<32^{1+\delta}e^{-(1+\delta)t}\|w\|^{-1}\leq 100e^{-t}\phi_{\delta}(w).

Case 3
.
\kappa(w)\geq e^{-t}
and
|\rho(w)|<2
.

In this case
\kappa(a_{t}u_{r}w)=1
by Lemma
3.7
, and
|w_{2}|\leq 2|w_{3}|
.

Subcase 3-1
.
\left|\kappa_{0}(w)\right|\geq 10
.

We have
|2w_{1}w_{3}|\geq|Q_{0}(w)|-w_{2}^{2}\geq 10w_{3}^{2}-4w_{3}^{2}=6w_{3}^{2}
, hence
|w_{1}|\geq 3|w_{3}|
and
\|w\|=|w_{1}|
. We also see
w_{2}^{2}\leq|\rho(w)|^{2}w_{3}^{2}\leq 4w_{3}^{2}\leq\frac{4}{3}|w_{1}w_{3}|
, hence

|Q_{0}(w)|\geq|2w_{1}w_{3}|-w_{2}^{2}\geq\tfrac{2}{3}|w_{1}w_{3}|.

Since
(r-\rho(w))^{2}\leq 9
, we get

|f_{w}(r)|\geq\left|\kappa_{0}(w)\right|-|r-\rho(w)|^{2}\geq\tfrac{1}{10}|\kappa_{0}(w)|=\frac{|Q_{0}(w)|}{10w_{3}^{2}}\geq\frac{|w_{1}|}{15|w_{3}|}

for any
r\in[-1,1]
. It follows that

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}\phi_{\delta}(a_{t}u_{r}w)dr

\displaystyle=\int_{-1}^{1}\|a_{t}u_{r}w\|^{-1-\delta}dr

\displaystyle\leq 2^{1+\delta}\int_{-1}^{1}|w_{3}e^{t}f_{w}(r)|^{-1-\delta}dr

\displaystyle<60^{1+\delta}e^{-(1+\delta)t}|w_{1}|^{-1-\delta}\leq 100e^{-t}\|w\|^{-1-\delta}=100e^{-t}\phi_{\delta}(w).

Subcase 3-2
.
e^{-t}\leq-\kappa_{0}(w)<10
.

We have
|2w_{1}w_{3}|\leq|Q_{0}(w)|+w_{2}^{2}\leq 14w_{3}^{2}
, hence
|w_{1}|\leq 7|w_{3}|
and
\|w\|\leq 7|w_{3}|
. Since
|f_{w}(r)|\geq(r-\rho(w))^{2}+e^{-t}
, we see that

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}\phi_{\delta}(a_{t}u_{r}w)dr

\displaystyle=\int_{-1}^{1}\|a_{t}u_{r}w\|^{-1-\delta}dr

\displaystyle\leq 2^{1+\delta}\int_{-1}^{1}|w_{3}e^{t}f_{w}(r)|^{-1-\delta}dr

\displaystyle\leq 2^{1+\delta}|w_{3}e^{t}|^{-1-\delta}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{dr}{\{(r-\rho(w))^{2}+e^{-t}\}^{1+\delta}}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{1+\delta}|w_{3}e^{t}|^{-1-\delta}\int_{-3}^{3}\frac{dr}{(r^{2}+e^{-t})^{1+\delta}}

\displaystyle<2^{1+\delta}|w_{3}e^{t}|^{-1-\delta}(\pi e^{(\frac{1}{2}+\delta)t})

\displaystyle\leq 14^{1+\delta}\pi e^{-\frac{t}{2}}\|w\|^{-1-\delta}\leq 100e^{-\frac{t}{2}}\phi_{\delta}(w).

Subcase 3-3
.
e^{-t}\leq\kappa_{0}(w)<10
.

As in
Subcase 3-2
we have
\|w\|\leq 7|w_{3}|
. Note that
f_{w}(r)
has two real roots
r_{1}=\rho(w)-\sqrt{\kappa_{0}(w)}
and
r_{2}=\rho(w)+\sqrt{\kappa_{0}(w)}
. Then we may write
|f_{w}(r)|=|r-r_{1}||r-r_{2}|
. Let
I_{1}=[r_{1}-e^{-\frac{3}{2}t},r_{1}+e^{-\frac{3}{2}t}]
and
I_{2}=[r_{2}-e^{-\frac{3}{2}t},r_{2}+e^{-\frac{3}{2}t}]
. Then we have

\displaystyle\int_{-6}^{\rho(w)}\phi_{\delta}(a_{t}u_{r}w)dr

\displaystyle=\int_{-6}^{\rho(w)}\|a_{t}u_{r}w\|^{-1-\delta}dr

\displaystyle\leq\int_{[-6,\rho(w)]\setminus I_{1}}\left|\tfrac{1}{2}w_{3}e^{t}f_{w}(r)\right|^{-1-\delta}dr+\int_{I_{1}}\left|\tfrac{1}{2}w_{3}e^{-t}\right|^{-1-\delta}dr.

The first integral in the last line is bounded by

\displaystyle\leq 2^{1+\delta}|w_{3}e^{t}|^{-1-\delta}\int_{[-6,\rho(w)]\setminus I_{1}}|r-r_{1}|^{-1-\delta}|r-r_{2}|^{-1-\delta}dr

\displaystyle\leq 2^{1+\delta}|w_{3}e^{t}|^{-1-\delta}\left|\kappa_{0}(w)\right|^{-\frac{1+\delta}{2}}\int_{[-6,\rho(w)]\setminus I_{1}}|r-r_{1}|^{-1-\delta}dr

\displaystyle\leq 2^{1+\delta}|w_{3}|^{-1-\delta}e^{-\frac{1+\delta}{2}t}\int_{e^{-\frac{3}{2}t}<|r|<10}|r|^{-1-\delta}dr

\displaystyle<4^{1+\delta}\delta^{-1}|w_{3}|^{-1-\delta}e^{-(\frac{1}{2}-\delta)t}.

The second integral is bounded as follows:

2^{1+\delta}\int_{I_{1}}|w_{3}e^{-t}|^{-1-\delta}dr<4^{1+\delta}|w_{3}|^{-1-\delta}e^{-(\frac{1}{2}-\delta)t}.

Thus, we get the following estimate:

\int_{-6}^{\rho(w)}\phi_{\delta}(a_{t}u_{r}w)dr\leq 30\delta^{-1}e^{-\frac{t}{3}}\|w\|^{-1-\delta}\leq 40\delta^{-1}e^{-\frac{t}{3}}\phi_{\delta}(w).

Similarly, we also have

\int_{\rho(w)}^{6}\phi_{\delta}(a_{t}u_{r}w)dr\leq 40\delta^{-1}e^{-\frac{t}{3}}\phi_{\delta}(w),

hence

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}\phi_{\delta}(a_{t}u_{r}w)dr

\displaystyle\leq\int_{-6}^{\rho(w)}\phi_{\delta}(a_{t}u_{r}w)dr+\int_{\rho(w)}^{6}\phi_{\delta}(a_{t}u_{r}w)dr

\displaystyle\leq 80\delta^{-1}e^{-\frac{t}{3}}\phi_{\delta}(w).

∎

We define
\kappa^{\star}:\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\left\{0\right\}\to[0,1]
and
\phi_{\delta}^{\star}:\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\left\{0\right\}\to(0,\infty]
by
\kappa^{\star}(w)=\kappa(Jw)
and

(3.10)

{\phi_{\delta}^{\star}(w)=\phi_{\delta}(Jw)=\kappa^{\star}(w)^{-2\delta}\|w\|^{-1-\delta}.}

The contraction inequality for
\phi_{\delta}^{\star}
follows from Proposition
3.8
.

Proposition 3.9

(Contraction for
\phi_{\delta}^{\star}
)
.

Let
0<\delta\leq 0.01
. Then we have

\int_{-1}^{1}\phi_{\delta}^{\star}(a_{t}^{*}u_{r}^{*}w)dr\leq 80\delta^{-1}e^{-\delta t}\phi_{\delta}^{\star}(w)

for any
t\geq 1
and
w\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
.

Proof.

In combination with (
3.10
), Proposition
3.8
implies that

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}\phi_{\delta}^{\star}(a_{t}^{*}u_{r}^{*}w)dr

\displaystyle=\int_{-1}^{1}\phi_{\delta}(a_{t}u_{r}Jw)dr

\displaystyle\leq 80\delta^{-1}e^{-\delta t}\phi_{\delta}(Jw)=80\delta^{-1}e^{-\delta t}\phi_{\delta}^{\star}(w).

∎

4.

4.1.

In this subsection, we modify the original Margulis function

. The construction of the modified height function

For

,

(4.2)

{\widehat{\phi}_{2,\delta,\eta,M}(g;v):=\begin{cases}\phi_{\delta}^{\star}(g^{*}v)&\text{ if }v\notin\mathcal{H}_{\eta,M},\\

where
g\in G
and

. For any

and

(4.3)

{\widehat{\phi}_{1,\delta,\eta,M}(g;v)=\widehat{\phi}_{2,\delta,\eta,M}(g;Jv),}

Q_{0}(v)=Q_{0}(Jv)
.

From now on, we will write

and

Proposition 4.1

(Contraction for
\widehat{\phi}_{i,\delta,\eta,M}
)
.

For any
i=1,2
,
0<\delta\leq 0.01
,

,
r\in[-1,1]
,

, and

{\int_{-1}^{1}\widehat{\phi}_{i,\delta,\eta,M}(a_{s}u_{r}g;v)dr\leq 80\delta^{-1}e^{-\delta s}\widehat{\phi}_{i,\delta,\eta,M}(g;v).}

Moreover, we have

(4.5)

{\widehat{\phi}_{i,\delta,\eta,M}(a_{s}u_{r}g;v)\leq(3e^{s})^{1+\delta}\widehat{\phi}_{i,\delta,\eta,M}(g;v).}

We also have

(4.6)

{\widehat{\phi}_{i,\delta,\eta,M}(a_{s}u_{r}g;v)\geq(3e^{s})^{-(1+5\delta)}\widehat{\phi}_{i,\delta,\eta,M}(g;v)\quad\textrm{ if }\kappa_{i}(a_{s}u_{r}gv)<1.}

Proof.

The log-Lipschitz properties (
4.5
) and (
4.6
) follow from (
3.1
), (
3.8
), and (
3.9
). The contraction inequality (
4.4
) follows from the contraction inequality for
\|\cdot\|^{-(1-3\delta)}
in Lemma
3.1
for

and the contraction inequality for
\phi_{\delta}
in Proposition
3.8
for

.
∎

Let us denote
\Lambda^{\circ}:=\Lambda\cap B(1)
for any discrete subset
\Lambda
of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. For
0<\delta\leq 0.01
,
0<\eta<1
, and
M>1
we define
\widetilde{\alpha}_{1,\delta,\eta,M}:H\times X\to[1,\infty]
and

{\widetilde{\alpha}_{1,\delta,\eta,M}(g;\Delta):=\begin{cases}\sup\left\{\widehat{\phi}_{1,\delta,\eta,M}(g;v):gv\in(g\Delta)^{\circ}\setminus\left\{0\right\}\right\}&\text{ if }(g\Delta)^{\circ}\neq\left\{0\right\},\\

(4.8)

{\widetilde{\alpha}_{2,\delta,\eta,M}(g;\Delta):=\begin{cases}\sup\left\{\widehat{\phi}_{2,\delta,\eta,M}(g;v):g^{*}v\in(g^{*}\Delta^{*})^{\circ}\setminus\left\{0\right\}\right\}&\text{ if }(g^{*}\Delta^{*})^{\circ}\neq\left\{0\right\},\\

where
g^{*}\Delta^{*}
is the dual lattice of
g\Delta
.
Now we define the modified height function
\widetilde{\alpha}_{\delta,\eta,M}:H\times X\to[1,\infty]
by

(4.9)

{\widetilde{\alpha}_{\delta,\eta,M}(g;\Delta):=\max\left\{\widetilde{\alpha}_{1,\delta,\eta,M}(g;\Delta),\widetilde{\alpha}_{2,\delta,\eta,M}(g;\Delta)\right\}}

for any
g\in H
and
\Delta\in X
. Note that we have

(4.10)

{\widetilde{\alpha}_{1,\delta,\eta,M}(g;\Delta)=\widetilde{\alpha}_{2,\delta,\eta,M}(g;J\Delta^{*}),\quad\widetilde{\alpha}_{\delta,\eta,M}(g;\Delta)=\widetilde{\alpha}_{\delta,\eta,M}(g;J\Delta^{*}).}

Recall that

for all

. By Minkowski’s first theorem either

or

for any

and

for any
g\in H
and
\Delta\in X
.

We fix a certain absolute constant

, which will be determined later in §5 (see Proposition

).

For
s\geq 1
,
0<\eta\leq 1
, and

, we define

{\epsilon_{s,\eta,M}(g;\Delta):=\begin{cases}\eta(3e^{s})^{-60M}&\text{ if }\widehat{\alpha}_{\eta,M}(g;\Delta)\leq 10^{4}e^{4s},\\
\eta\widehat{\alpha}_{\eta,M}(g;\Delta)^{-100DM^{2}}&\text{ if }\widehat{\alpha}_{\eta,M}(g;\Delta)>10^{4}e^{4s}.\end{cases}}

For
i=1,2
,

and

{\Xi_{i}(s,\epsilon):=\left\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:1\leq\|v\|\leq 3e^{s},\;\kappa_{i}(v)<\epsilon\right\}.}

We define

by

{\mathcal{E}_{1,s,\eta,M}:=\left\{(g;\Delta)\in H\times X:g(\Delta\setminus\mathcal{H}_{\eta,M})\cap\Xi_{1}(s,\epsilon_{s,\eta,M}(g;\Delta))\neq\emptyset\right\},}

(4.14)

, hence

holds.

We see that for any
s\geq 1
,
0<\eta\leq 1
,

, and
\Delta\in X

(4.15)

{(\operatorname{id},\Delta)\notin\mathcal{E}_{s,\eta,M},}

as
\Xi_{1}(s,\epsilon_{s,\eta,M}(\operatorname{id},\Delta))
and

hence

.

We shall record the following lemma:

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\Delta\in X
,
s\geq 1
,

, and

. If
\widehat{\alpha}_{\eta,M}(a_{t}u_{r};\Delta)>10^{4}e^{\frac{t}{DM}}

.

Proof.

Assume for contradiction that there exist

,

, and

and

. Note that

since
\widehat{\alpha}_{\eta,M}(a_{t}u_{r};\Delta)>10^{4}e^{4s}
.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that

. Then there exists

such that

and

, hence using (

), (

), and (

and

{|Q_{0}(v)|=|Q_{0}(a_{t}u_{r}v)|\leq\kappa(a_{t}u_{r}v)\|a_{t}u_{r}v\|^{2}<10^{-100M}\eta e^{-100Mt+2s}.}

On the other hand, since

it holds that

but this contradicts (

)
.

,

,

, and

. Then for any

and

with

we have

(4.17)

{\int_{-1}^{1}\widetilde{\alpha}_{\delta,\eta,M}(a_{s}u_{r}g;\Delta)dr\leq\delta^{-10}\eta^{-4\delta}e^{-\delta s}\widetilde{\alpha}_{\delta,\eta,M}(g;\Delta)+e^{9s}.}

4.2.
Intersection of surface and plane

We begin with a simple observation. Let
L
be a plane in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Then the intersection of
L
and the light cone
\mathcal{H}=\left\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:Q_{0}(v)=0\right\}
is the union of at most two lines in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. The contents of this subsection are based on a quantitative version of this observation.

For
s\geq 1
,
\Delta\in X
, and a
\Delta
-rational plane
L\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
, denote by
\Omega_{0}(\Delta,L,s)
the set of nonzero vectors
v\in\Delta\cap L
such that
|\kappa_{0}(v)|<e^{-3s}
. The following lemma describes the distribution of
\Omega_{0}(\Delta,L,s)
on the plane
L
.

Lemma 4.4
.

For
s\geq 1
,
\Delta\in X
, and a
\Delta
-rational plane
L\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
, there exists a finite set
\mathcal{R}_{L}\subset[-1,1]
with
|\mathcal{R}_{L}|\leq 2
satisfying
\operatorname{dist}(\mathcal{R}_{L},\rho(b))\leq 5e^{-s}
for any
b\in\Omega_{0}(\Delta,L,s)
.

Proof.

We may assume that there are two distinct vectors
v,w\in\Omega_{0}(\Delta,L,s)
with
|\rho(v)-\rho(w)|>5e^{-s}
. We shall show that
\mathcal{R}_{L}=\left\{\rho(v),\rho(w)\right\}
satisfies
\operatorname{dist}(\mathcal{R}_{L},\rho(b))\leq 5e^{-s}
for any
b\in\Omega_{0}(\Delta,L,s)
.

Let
v=(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
w=(w_{1},w_{2},w_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Then any vector in
\Delta\cap L
can be written in a form of
mv+nw
with
(m,n)\in\mathbb{Q}^{2}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
. For any
mv+nw\in\Omega_{0}(\Delta,L,s)
we may expand
\kappa_{0}(mv+nw)
as follows:

\displaystyle|\kappa_{0}(mv+nw)|

\displaystyle=\left|2\left(\frac{mv_{1}+nw_{1}}{mv_{3}+nw_{3}}\right)-\left(\frac{mv_{2}+nw_{2}}{mv_{3}+nw_{3}}\right)^{2}\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{|mnv_{3}w_{3}(\rho(v)-\rho(w))^{2}-(mv_{3}+nw_{3})(mv_{3}\kappa_{0}(v)+nw_{3}\kappa_{0}(w))|}{(mv_{3}+nw_{3})^{2}}.

For simplicity we denote
\theta_{m,n}=\frac{mnv_{3}w_{3}}{(|mv_{3}|+|nw_{3}|)^{2}}
. Since
|\kappa_{0}(v)|,|\kappa_{0}(w)|<e^{-3s}
,

\displaystyle|\kappa_{0}(mv+nw)|

\displaystyle\geq\left|\theta_{m,n}(\rho(v)-\rho(w))^{2}-\frac{(mv_{3}+nw_{3})(mv_{3}\kappa_{0}(v)+nw_{3}\kappa_{0}(w))}{(|mv_{3}|+|nw_{3}|)^{2}}\right|

\displaystyle\geq|\theta_{m,n}|(\rho(v)-\rho(w))^{2}-\left|\frac{(mv_{3}+nw_{3})(mv_{3}\kappa_{0}(v)+nw_{3}\kappa_{0}(w))}{(|mv_{3}|+|nw_{3}|)^{2}}\right|

\displaystyle\geq|\theta_{m,n}|(\rho(v)-\rho(w))^{2}-e^{-3s}.

Thus,
mv+nw\in\Omega_{0}(\Delta,L,s)
implies
|\theta_{m,n}|<\frac{e^{-s}}{10}
. This in turn gives that either
\left|\frac{nw_{3}}{mv_{3}}\right|<\frac{e^{-s}}{4}
or
\left|\frac{mv_{3}}{nw_{3}}\right|<\frac{e^{-s}}{4}
.

Let us first consider the case
\left|\frac{nw_{3}}{mv_{3}}\right|<\frac{e^{-s}}{4}
. In this case, we have

\displaystyle|\rho(mv+nw)-\rho(v)|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{mv_{2}+nw_{2}}{mv_{3}+nw_{3}}-\frac{v_{2}}{v_{3}}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{nw_{3}}{mv_{3}+nw_{3}}\right||\rho(v)-\rho(w)|<\frac{e^{-s}}{4-e^{-s}}\cdot 2<e^{-s}.

If
\left|\frac{mv_{3}}{nw_{3}}\right|<\frac{e^{-s}}{4}
, then we similarly get
|\rho(mv+nw)-\rho(w)|<e^{-s}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Let us denote by
\Omega(\Delta,L,s)
the set of nonzero vectors
v\in\Delta\cap L
such that
\kappa(v)<e^{-3s}
. The set
\Omega(\Delta,L,s)
is clearly a subset of
\Omega_{0}(\Delta,L,s)
. Now we show the following supremum-version of the contraction inequality for
\phi_{\delta}
using Lemma
4.4
, where the supremum is taken over a subset of
\Omega(\Delta,L,s)
.

Proposition 4.5

(Supremum-version of contraction for
\phi_{\delta}
)
.

Let
0\leq\delta\leq 0.01
and let
s,\Delta,L
be as in Lemma
4.4
. For any
\mathcal{T}\subseteq\Omega(\Delta,L,s)
we have

\int_{-1}^{1}\sup_{v\in\mathcal{T}}\phi_{\delta}(a_{s}u_{r}v)dr\leq 200e^{-\delta s}\sup_{v\in\mathcal{T}}\phi_{\delta}(v).

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{R}_{L}
be as in Lemma
4.4
. We may assume
|\mathcal{R}_{L}|=2
and let
\mathcal{R}_{L}=\left\{r_{1},r_{2}\right\}
. Then
\min\left\{|r_{1}-\rho(v)|,|r_{2}-\rho(v)|\right\}\leq 5e^{-s}
for any
v\in\Omega(\Delta,L,s)
.

By Lemma
3.7
we have
\kappa(a_{s}u_{r}v)\geq e^{2s}\kappa(v)
for all
v\in\Omega(\Delta,L,s)
. It follows that

(4.18)

{\int_{-1}^{1}\sup_{v\in\mathcal{T}}\phi_{\delta}(a_{s}u_{r}v)dr\leq e^{-2\delta s}\int_{-1}^{1}\sup_{v\in\mathcal{T}}\kappa(v)^{-2\delta}\|a_{s}u_{r}v\|^{-1-\delta}dr.}

Let us write
v=(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})
. Note that
\|v\|\leq 3|v_{3}|
for any
v\in\Omega(\Delta,L,s)
, since
|\kappa(v)|<e^{-3s}
.
If
\min\left\{|r-r_{1}|,|r-r_{2}|\right\}\geq 10e^{-s}
, then

\displaystyle|r-\rho(v)|

\displaystyle\geq\min\left\{|r-r_{1}|-|r_{1}-\rho(v)|,|r-r_{2}|-|r_{2}-\rho(v)|\right\}

\displaystyle\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\min\left\{|r-r_{1}|,|r-r_{2}|\right\}\geq 5e^{-s}

for any
v\in\Omega(\Delta,L,s)
. Furthermore, we have

\|a_{s}u_{r}v\|\geq\tfrac{1}{2}e^{s}|v_{3}|((r-\rho(v))^{2}-\kappa(v))\geq\tfrac{1}{4}e^{s}|v_{3}|(r-\rho(v))^{2},

hence

(4.19)

\displaystyle\|a_{s}u_{r}v\|^{-1-\delta}

\displaystyle\leq 4^{1+\delta}|v_{3}|^{-1-\delta}e^{-(1+\delta)s}|r-\rho(v)|^{-2(1+\delta)}

\displaystyle\leq 16^{1+\delta}|v_{3}|^{-1-\delta}e^{-(1+\delta)s}(|r-r_{1}|^{-2(1+\delta)}+|r-r_{2}|^{-2(1+\delta)})

\displaystyle\leq 48^{1+\delta}\|v\|^{-1-\delta}e^{-(1+\delta)s}(|r-r_{1}|^{-2(1+\delta)}+|r-r_{2}|^{-2(1+\delta)})

for any
v\in\Omega(\Delta,L,s)
.

Let
I_{1}=[r_{1}-10e^{-s},r_{1}+10e^{-s}]
and
I_{2}=[r_{2}-10e^{-s},r_{2}+10e^{-s}]
. For
r\in[-1,1]\setminus(I_{1}\cup I_{2})
we shall use (
4.19
) and obtain the following estimate:

(4.20)

\displaystyle\int_{[-1,1]\setminus(I_{1}\cup I_{2})}\sup_{v\in\mathcal{T}}\kappa(v)^{-2\delta}\|a_{s}u_{r}v\|^{-1-\delta}dr

\displaystyle\leq 48^{1+\delta}\left(\sup_{v\in\mathcal{T}}\phi_{\delta}(v)\right)e^{-(1+\delta)s}\int_{[-1,1]\setminus(I_{1}\cup I_{2})}(|r-r_{1}|^{-2(1+\delta)}+|r-r_{2}|^{-2(1+\delta)})dr

\displaystyle\leq 200\left(\sup_{v\in\mathcal{T}}\phi_{\delta}(v)\right)e^{-(1+\delta)s}\int_{10e^{-s}<|r|<2}|r|^{-2(1+\delta)}dr

\displaystyle\leq 400\left(\sup_{v\in\mathcal{T}}\phi_{\delta}(v)\right)e^{-(1+\delta)s}(10e^{-s})^{-(1+2\delta)}\leq 40e^{\delta s}\sup_{v\in\mathcal{T}}\phi_{\delta}(v).

On the other hand, for
r\in I_{1}\cup I_{2}
we shall use (
3.1
) and get

(4.21)

\displaystyle\int_{I_{1}\cup I_{2}}\sup_{v\in\mathcal{T}}\kappa(v)^{-2\delta}\|a_{s}u_{r}v\|^{-1-\delta}dr

\displaystyle\leq(3e^{s})^{1+\delta}|I_{1}\cup I_{2}|\sup_{v\in\mathcal{T}}\phi_{\delta}(v)

\displaystyle\leq 160e^{\delta s}\sup_{v\in\mathcal{T}}\phi_{\delta}(v).

Combining (
4.18
), (
4.20
), and (
4.21
), we obtain the desired inequality.

∎

4.3.
Proof of the subharmonic estimate for
\widetilde{\alpha}_{\delta,\eta,M}

For a lattice
\Lambda
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, denote by
\Lambda_{\operatorname{prim}}
the set of representative primitive vectors in
\Lambda
for scalar multiplication by
\pm 1
. Note that the height functions
\kappa(v),\phi_{\delta}(v),\widehat{\phi}_{\delta,\eta,M}(v)
are all invariant under the scalar multiplication by
-1
. For
0<\delta\leq 0.01
,
s\geq 1
,
0<\eta\leq 1
,
M>1
,
g\in H
, and
\Delta\in X
we denote by
\mathcal{P}_{1,\delta,\eta,M}(g,\Delta,s)
the set of
v\in\Delta_{\operatorname{prim}}\setminus\mathcal{H}_{\eta,M}

satisfying

\|gv\|\leq 3e^{s},\quad\phi_{\delta}(gv)>(10e^{2s})^{-1-5\delta}\widetilde{\alpha}_{1,\delta,\eta,M}(g;\Delta).

Similarly, denote by
\mathcal{P}_{2,\delta,\eta,M}(g,\Delta,s)
the set of
v\in\Delta^{*}_{\operatorname{prim}}\setminus\mathcal{H}_{\eta,M}
satisfying

\|g^{*}v\|\leq 3e^{s},\quad\phi_{\delta}^{*}(g^{*}v)>(10e^{2s})^{-1-5\delta}\widetilde{\alpha}_{2,\delta,\eta,M}(g;\Delta).

For
1\leq i\leq 2
let us also denote

\mathcal{Q}_{i,\delta,\eta,M}(g,\Delta,s):=\left\{v\in\mathcal{P}_{i,\delta,\eta,M}(g,\Delta,s):\kappa_{i}(gv)\geq\eta(3e^{s})^{-60M}\right\},

\mathcal{T}_{i,\delta,\eta,M}(g,\Delta,s):=\left\{v\in\mathcal{P}_{i,\delta,\eta,M}(g,\Delta,s):\kappa_{i}(gv)<\eta(3e^{s})^{-60M}\right\}.

Clearly
\mathcal{P}_{i,\delta,\eta,M}(g,\Delta,s)=\mathcal{Q}_{i,\delta,\eta,M}(g,\Delta,s)\cup\mathcal{T}_{i,\delta,\eta,M}(g,\Delta,s)
for
i=1,2
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
s\geq 1
,
0<\eta\leq 1
,
M>1
, and
(g,\Delta)\notin\mathcal{E}_{s,\eta,M}
. For each
i=1,2
, there exists a plane
L_{i}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
such that
\mathcal{T}_{i,\delta,\eta,M}(g,\Delta,s)
is contained in
L_{i}\cap\Delta
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, it suffices to show the statement for
i=1
.

Suppose that
\widehat{\alpha}(g;\Delta)\leq 10^{4}e^{4s}
. In this case
(g,\Delta)\notin\mathcal{E}_{s,\eta,M}
means that there is no vector
v\in g\big(\Delta\setminus\mathcal{H}_{\eta,M}\big)
with
1\leq\|v\|\leq 3e^{s}
and
\kappa(v)<\eta(3e^{s})^{-60M}
. Hence
\mathcal{T}_{1,\delta,\eta,M}(g,\Delta,s)
is contained in
g\Delta\cap B(1)
. Since the co-volume of
g\Delta
is one, by Minkowski’s first theorem there are at most two independent vectors in
g\Delta\cap B(1)
. Thus, there exists a plane
L_{1}
such that
\mathcal{T}_{1,\delta,\eta,M}(g,\Delta,s)
is contained in
L_{1}
.

Suppose that
\widehat{\alpha}(g;\Delta)>10^{4}e^{4s}
. Then there exist
v\in g\big(\Delta\setminus\mathcal{H}_{\eta,M}\big)
such that
\|v\|\leq\frac{1}{50}e^{-2s}
. If there are
v^{\prime},v^{\prime\prime}\in g\Delta\cap B(3e^{s})
such that
\left\{v,v^{\prime},v^{\prime\prime}\right\}
is linearly independent, then this contradicts that the co-volume of
g\Delta
is one. Hence, there exists a plane
L_{1}
such that
\mathcal{T}_{1,\delta,\eta,M}(g,\Delta,s)
is contained in
L_{1}
.
∎

Now we prove the subharmonic estimate for
\widetilde{\alpha}_{\delta,\eta,M}
in Proposition
4.3
.

Proof of Proposition
4.3
.

We first establish the subharmonic estimate for
\widetilde{\alpha}_{1,\delta,\eta,M}
.

Case 1
.
|\mathcal{Q}_{1,\delta,\eta,M}(g,\Delta,s)|\geq 2
.

Let
v
and
w
be linearly independent vectors in
\mathcal{Q}_{1,\delta,\eta,M}(g,\Delta,s)
. We have

\displaystyle(10e^{2s})^{-1-5\delta}\widetilde{\alpha}_{1,\delta,\eta,M}(g;\Delta)

\displaystyle<\phi_{\delta}(gv)=\kappa(gv)^{-2\delta}\|gv\|^{-1-\delta}

\displaystyle\leq\eta^{-2\delta}e^{2(M+2)\delta s}\|gv\|^{-1-\delta},

hence

\displaystyle\|gv\|

\displaystyle\leq 10^{1+5\delta}\eta^{-2\delta}e^{\{2+2(M+7)\delta\}s}\big(\widetilde{\alpha}_{1,\delta,\eta,M}(g;\Delta)\big)^{-1}

\displaystyle<10^{1+5\delta}\eta^{-2\delta}e^{2.5s}\big(\widetilde{\alpha}_{1,\delta,\eta,M}(g;\Delta)\big)^{-1}.

It holds that
\|gw\|\leq 10^{1+5\delta}\eta^{-2\delta}e^{2.5s}\big(\widetilde{\alpha}_{1,\delta,\eta,M}(g;\Delta)\big)^{-1}
similarly. Thus,

\|gv\wedge gw\|\leq\|gv\|\|gw\|\leq 100^{1+5\delta}\eta^{-4\delta}e^{5s}\big(\widetilde{\alpha}_{1,\delta,\eta,M}(g;\Delta)\big)^{-2}.

Identifying
gv\wedge gw
with
gv\times gw\in g^{*}\Delta^{*}
, we get

\alpha_{2}(g\Delta)^{-1}\leq 100^{1+5\delta}\eta^{-4\delta}e^{5s}\big(\widetilde{\alpha}_{1,\delta,\eta,M}(g;\Delta)\big)^{-2}.

Using (
3.8
), it follows that for any
r\in[-1,1]

\widetilde{\alpha}_{1,\delta,\eta,M}(a_{s}u_{r}g;\Delta)\leq(3e^{s})^{1+\delta}\widetilde{\alpha}_{1,\delta,\eta,M}(g;\Delta)\leq 100\eta^{-2\delta}e^{4s}\alpha_{2}(g\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Case 2
.
|\mathcal{Q}_{1,\delta,\eta,M}(g,\Delta,s)|\leq 1
.

Observe that for any
v\in\Delta_{\operatorname{prim}}\setminus\big(\mathcal{P}_{1,\delta,\eta,M}(g,\Delta,s)\cup\mathcal{H}_{\eta,M}\big)
and
r\in[-1,1]
, we have

(4.22)

{\phi_{\delta}(a_{s}u_{r}gv)<0.9\widetilde{\alpha}_{1,\delta,\eta,M}(a_{s}u_{r}g;\Delta)}

by the log-Lipschitz properties (
3.8
) and (
3.9
). Indeed, if
\kappa(a_{s}u_{r}gv)<1
and
v\notin\mathcal{H}_{\eta,M}
then

\displaystyle\phi_{\delta}(a_{s}u_{r}gv)\leq(3e^{s})^{1+5\delta}\phi_{\delta}(gv)

\displaystyle<\left(\frac{3e^{-s}}{10}\right)^{1+5\delta}\widetilde{\alpha}_{1,\delta,\eta,M}(g;\Delta)

\displaystyle<0.9\widetilde{\alpha}_{1,\delta,\eta,M}(a_{s}u_{r}g;\Delta),

and if
\kappa(a_{s}u_{r}gv)=1
and
v\notin\mathcal{H}_{\eta,M}
then

\displaystyle\phi_{\delta}(a_{s}u_{r}gv)

\displaystyle=\|a_{s}u_{r}gv\|^{-1-\delta}\leq(3e^{s})^{1+\delta}\|gv\|^{-1-\delta}\leq(3e^{s})^{1+\delta}\phi_{\delta}(gv)

\displaystyle<\left(\frac{3e^{-s}}{10}\right)^{1+5\delta}\widetilde{\alpha}_{1,\delta,\eta,M}(g;\Delta)<0.9\widetilde{\alpha}_{1,\delta,\eta,M}(a_{s}u_{r}g;\Delta).

It follows from (
4.22
) that

(4.23)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\alpha}_{1,\delta,\eta,M}(a_{s}u_{r}g;\Delta)

\displaystyle\leq\alpha(a_{s}u_{r}g\Delta)^{1-3\delta}+\!\!\!\sup_{v\in\mathcal{P}_{1,\delta,\eta,M}(g,\Delta,s)}\!\!\!\widehat{\phi}_{\delta,\eta,M}(a_{s}u_{r}g;v)

\displaystyle\leq\alpha(a_{s}u_{r}g\Delta)^{1-3\delta}+\!\!\!\sum_{v\in\mathcal{Q}_{1,\delta,\eta,M}(g,\Delta,s)}\!\!\!\widehat{\phi}_{\delta,\eta,M}(a_{s}u_{r}g;v)

\displaystyle+\!\!\!\sup_{v\in\mathcal{T}_{1,\delta,\eta,M}(g,\Delta,s)}\!\!\!\widehat{\phi}_{\delta,\eta,M}(a_{s}u_{r}g;v).

Recall the subharmonic estimate for
\alpha^{1-3\delta}
from Lemma
3.3
: we have

(4.24)

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}\alpha(a_{s}u_{r}g\Delta)^{1-3\delta}dr

\displaystyle\leq 100e^{-\delta s}\alpha(g\Delta)^{1-3\delta}+e^{4s}

\displaystyle\leq 100e^{-\delta s}\widetilde{\alpha}_{\delta,\eta,M}(g;\Delta)+e^{4s}.

Applying the contraction inequality for
\widehat{\phi}_{\delta,\eta,M}
in Proposition
4.1
, we also have

\sum_{v\in\mathcal{Q}_{1,\delta,\eta,M}(g,\Delta,s)}\int_{-1}^{1}\widehat{\phi}_{\delta,\eta,M}(a_{s}u_{r}g;v)dr\leq 80\delta^{-1}e^{-\delta s}\!\!\!\sup_{v\in\mathcal{Q}_{1,\delta,\eta,M}(g,\Delta,s)}\!\!\!\widehat{\phi}_{\delta,\eta,M}(g;v)

since
|\mathcal{Q}_{1,\delta,\eta,M}(g,\Delta,s)|\leq 1
. For
v\in\mathcal{Q}_{1,\delta,\eta,M}(g,\Delta,s)
, if
1<\|gv\|\leq 3e^{s}
then

\displaystyle\widehat{\phi}_{\delta,\eta,M}(g;v)=\phi_{\delta}(gv)

\displaystyle=\kappa(gv)^{-2\delta}\|gv\|^{-1-\delta}

\displaystyle\leq\kappa(gv)^{-2\delta}\leq\eta^{-2\delta}e^{100\delta Ms}\leq\eta^{-2\delta}e^{s},

and if
\|gv\|\leq 1
then

\widehat{\phi}_{\delta,\eta,M}(g;v)\leq\widetilde{\alpha}_{1,\delta,\eta,M}(g;\Delta)
. Hence,

(4.25)

{\sum_{v\in\mathcal{Q}_{1,\delta,\eta,M}(g,\Delta,s)}\int_{-1}^{1}\widehat{\phi}_{\delta,\eta,M}(a_{s}u_{r}g;v)dr\leq 80\delta^{-1}e^{-\delta s}(\widetilde{\alpha}_{1,\delta,\eta,M}(g;\Delta)+\eta^{-2\delta}e^{s}).}

According to Lemma
4.6
there exists a plane
L_{1}
containing
\mathcal{T}_{1,\delta,\eta,M}(g,\Delta,s)
. This allows us to apply the sup-version of the contraction inequality for
\widehat{\phi}_{\delta,\eta,M}
in Proposition
4.5
and we get

\int_{-1}^{1}\sup_{v\in\mathcal{T}_{1,\delta,\eta,M}(g,\Delta,s)}\phi_{\delta}(a_{s}u_{r}gv)dr\leq 200e^{-\delta s}\sup_{v\in\mathcal{T}_{1,\delta,\eta,M}(g,\Delta,s)}\phi_{\delta}(gv).

Recall that
\phi_{\delta}(gv)\leq\widetilde{\alpha}_{1,\delta,\eta,M}(g;\Delta)
for any

. By our assumption
(g,\Delta)\notin\mathcal{E}_{s,\eta,M}
we get
\kappa(gv)\geq\epsilon_{s,\eta,M}(g;\Delta)
for any
v\in\Delta\setminus\mathcal{H}_{\eta,M}
with

. If
\widehat{\alpha}_{\eta,M}(g;\Delta)\leq 10^{4}e^{4s}
then every
v\in\mathcal{T}_{1,\delta,\eta,M}(g,\Delta,s)
is contained in

(see the first case of the proof of Lemma
4.6
). If
\widehat{\alpha}_{\eta,M}(g;\Delta)>10^{4}e^{4s}
then for any

with

{\int_{-1}^{1}\sup_{v\in\mathcal{T}_{1,\delta,\eta,M}(g,\Delta,s)}\!\!\!\phi_{\delta}(a_{s}u_{r}v)dr\leq 200e^{-\delta s}(\widetilde{\alpha}_{1,\delta,\eta,M}(g;\Delta)+\eta^{-2\delta}\alpha(g\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}).}

Together with (

), (
4.24

\int_{-1}^{1}\widetilde{\alpha}_{1,\delta,\eta,M}(a_{s}u_{r}g;\Delta)dr\leq 100\delta^{-1}e^{-\delta s}(\widetilde{\alpha}_{\delta,\eta,M}(g;\Delta)+\eta^{-2\delta}e^{s}\alpha(g\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}).

From
Case 1
and
Case 2

(4.27)

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}\widetilde{\alpha}_{1,\delta,\eta,M}

\displaystyle(a_{s}u_{r}g;\Delta)dr\leq 100\delta^{-1}e^{-\delta s}\widetilde{\alpha}_{\delta,\eta,M}(g;\Delta)+200\delta^{-1}\eta^{-2\delta}e^{4s}\alpha(g\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}

5.
Avoidance estimates

In the previous section, we established the subharmonic estimate for the modified height function
\widetilde{\alpha}_{\delta,\eta,M}
(Proposition
4.3
). However, it does not hold for every point
(g,\Delta)\in H\times X
: it is valid only if
(g,\Delta)
does not belong to
\mathcal{E}_{s,\eta,M}
. The goal of this section is to control the amount of time that the orbit
(a_{t}u_{r},\Delta)
stays within the set
\mathcal{E}_{s,\eta,M}
by establishing the following estimate.

Proposition 5.1

(Avoidance estimate)
.

There exists an absolute constant
D
such that the following holds. Let
0<\delta\leq 0.01
and let
Q
be an indefinite ternary quadratic form of type
M\geq D
with
\operatorname{det}Q=1
. Then there exists
0<\eta<1
such that

\int_{-1}^{1}\widehat{\alpha}_{\eta,M}^{\prime}(a_{t}u_{r};\Delta_{Q})^{1+\delta}\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{E}_{s,\eta,M}}(a_{t}u_{r};\Delta_{Q})dr\ll e^{-10s}

for
s\geq 1
and
t\geq 4DMs
, where the implied constant depends only on
Q
.

5.1.
Integral quadratic forms and closed orbits

We begin with an elementary lemma.

Lemma 5.2
.

There is an absolute constant
D_{1}>1
such that the following holds. Let
Q
be an indefinite ternary quadratic form with
\operatorname{det}Q=1
. For any nonzero integral ternary quadratic form
Q^{\prime}
the closed orbit
H\Delta^{\prime}
associated to
(\operatorname{det}Q^{\prime})^{-\frac{1}{3}}Q^{\prime}
satisfies
\operatorname{Vol}(H\Delta^{\prime})<\|Q^{\prime}\|^{D_{1}}
and

\mathbf{d}(\Delta_{Q},H\Delta^{\prime})\ll\|Q-(\operatorname{det}Q^{\prime})^{-\frac{1}{3}}Q^{\prime}\|.

Proof.

The space of indefinite ternary quadratic forms with determinant one is identified with
H\backslash G
by the map
\iota:Q^{g}\mapsto Hg
for
g\in H
. Let us choose
g^{\prime}\in G
such that
\iota(Q^{\prime})=Hg^{\prime}
. Since the map
Q^{g}\mapsto Hg
is a
C^{1}
-map,

\mathbf{d}^{X}(\Delta_{Q},H\Delta^{\prime})\ll\|Q-(\operatorname{det}Q^{\prime})^{-\frac{1}{3}}Q^{\prime}\|.

The discriminant of the closed orbit
H\Delta^{\prime}
is bounded above by
\operatorname{det}(Q^{\prime})\leq 6\|Q^{\prime}\|^{3}
, and the volume of
H\Delta^{\prime}
is also bounded above by a polynomial of the discriminant of
H\Delta^{\prime}
(we refer the reader to
[
ELMV09
]
,
[
EMV09
, §17.3]
, and
[
LM14
, §2.6]
for discussions on the discriminant and the volume of a closed orbit). It follows that
\operatorname{Vol}(H\Delta^{\prime})<\|Q^{\prime}\|^{D_{1}}
for some constant
D_{1}>1
.
∎

Let us denote
X_{\leq\mathsf{h}}:=\left\{\Delta\in X:\alpha(\Delta)\leq\mathsf{h}\right\}
for
\mathsf{h}>1
, and

\mathcal{K}(s,\epsilon):=\left\{\Delta\in X:\Delta\cap\Xi_{1}(s,\epsilon)\neq\emptyset\right\}

for
s\geq 1
and
0<\epsilon<1
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\mathsf{h}>1
. For any
\Delta\in X_{\leq\mathsf{h}}
,
s\geq 1
,
0<\epsilon<1
, and
T>100
at least one of the followings holds:

(1)

m_{\mathbb{R}}\big(\left\{r\in[-T,T]:u_{r}\Delta\in\mathcal{K}(s,\epsilon)\right\}\big)\leq 100,

(2)

there exists
\Delta^{\prime}\in X
such that
\operatorname{Vol}(H\Delta^{\prime})\leq(e^{s}\mathsf{h}T^{2})^{20D_{1}}
and

\mathbf{d}^{X}(\Delta,\Delta^{\prime})\ll\epsilon(e^{s}\mathsf{h}T^{2})^{10}.

Proof.

For each
v\in\Delta\setminus\left\{0\right\}
we denote

I(v):=\left\{r\in\mathbb{R}:u_{r}v\in\Xi(s,\epsilon)\right\}
and let

\Phi:=\displaystyle\bigcup_{v\in\Delta\setminus\left\{0\right\}}I(v)\cap[-T,T].

Then we have

(5.1)

{\left\{r\in[-T,T]:u_{r}\Delta\in\mathcal{K}(s,\epsilon)\right\}\subseteq\Phi.}

Recall that
\rho(u_{r}v)=\rho(v)-r
for any
r\in\mathbb{R}
, and
\kappa(u_{r}v)<1
holds only if
|\rho(u_{r}v)|<2
. It follows that
I(v)\cap[-T,T]
for any
v\in\Delta\setminus\left\{0\right\}
is contained in an interval of length
4
.

If the set
\Phi
can be covered by eight intervals of length
10
, then in view of (
5.1
) it is clear that (1) holds.

Otherwise, we may find nine points
r_{1},\cdots,r_{9}\in\Phi
with
|r_{i}-r_{j}|>5
for any
i\neq j
. We shall show that (2) holds in this case. For each
1\leq i\leq 9
there exists
\mathbf{v}_{i}\in(\Delta\cup\Delta^{*})\setminus\left\{0\right\}
satisfying
u_{r_{i}}\mathbf{v}_{i}\in\Xi(s,\epsilon)
. By the pigeonhole principle, without loss of generality, we may assume that
\mathbf{v}_{1},\cdots,\mathbf{v}_{5}\in\Delta\setminus\left\{0\right\}
. Note that
u_{r_{i}}\mathbf{v}_{i}\in\Xi(s,\epsilon)
implies that
\kappa_{0}(\mathbf{v}_{i})=\kappa_{0}(u_{r_{i}}\mathbf{v}_{i})<\epsilon
,
|\rho(\mathbf{v}_{i})-r_{i}|<2
, and
\|\mathbf{v}_{i}\|\leq 3|r_{i}|^{2}\|u_{r_{i}}\mathbf{v}_{i}\|\leq 10e^{s}T^{2}
for all
1\leq i\leq 5
. Since
\rho(\mathbf{v}_{1}),\cdots,\rho(\mathbf{v}_{5})
are
1
-separated, in view of Lemma
4.4
no three of
\mathbf{v}_{1},\cdots,\mathbf{v}_{5}
are on the same plane.

For
1\leq i\leq 5
we have

|Q_{0}(\mathbf{v}_{i})|=|Q_{0}(u_{r_{i}}\mathbf{v}_{i})|\leq\kappa(u_{r_{i}}\mathbf{v}_{i})\|u_{r_{i}}\mathbf{v}_{i}\|^{2}\ll e^{2s}\epsilon.

We may choose
g\in H
with
\Delta=g\mathbb{Z}^{3}
so that
\tfrac{1}{10}\|g^{-1}\|\leq\alpha(\Delta)\leq\mathsf{h}
. Let
Q=Q^{g}
and
\mathbf{m}_{i}=g^{-1}\mathbf{v}_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq 5
. Then
|Q(\mathbf{m}_{i})|\leq\epsilon
and

\|\mathbf{m}_{i}\|\leq 3\|g^{-1}\|\|\mathbf{v}_{i}\|\leq 300e^{s}\mathsf{h}T^{2}

for all
1\leq i\leq 5
. Moreover, no three of
\mathbf{m}_{i},\cdots,\mathbf{m}_{5}
are on the same plane. Therefore, by Lemma
2.1
there exists a nonzero integral ternary quadratic form
Q^{\prime}
satisfying
\|Q^{\prime}\|\leq 10^{6}(300e^{s}\mathsf{h}T^{2})^{14}
and

\|Q-(\operatorname{det}Q^{\prime})^{-\frac{1}{3}}Q^{\prime}\|\ll\epsilon(e^{s}\mathsf{h}T^{2})^{10}.

It follows from Lemma
5.2
that there exists
\Delta^{\prime}\in X
such that
H\Delta^{\prime}
is closed,

\operatorname{Vol}(H\Delta^{\prime})\leq\|Q^{\prime}\|^{D_{1}}\leq(e^{s}\mathsf{h}T^{2})^{20D_{1}},

and
\mathbf{d}(\Delta,H\Delta^{\prime})\ll\epsilon(e^{s}\mathsf{h}T^{2})^{10}

∎

5.2.
Effective avoidance principle

We recall an effective avoidance theorem from
[

]
in order to control the amount of time that the orbit

stays very close to periodic

-orbits.

For positive real numbers

,

, and

we denote

{\Upsilon(V,d):=\bigcup_{\operatorname{Vol}(Hx)<V}\left\{y\in X:\mathbf{d}_{X}(y,Hx)<d\right\},}

{\Upsilon(V,d,\mathsf{h}):=\Upsilon(V,d)\cup(X\setminus X_{\leq\mathsf{h}}).}

The following effective avoidance principle is a slightly simplified reformulation of a special case of
[
SS22
, Theorem 2]
(see also
[
LMW22
, Proposition 4.6]
).

Theorem 5.4
.

There exists an absolute constant
D_{2}>1
such that the following holds. Let

. For an indefinite ternary quadratic form
Q
of type
M\geq D_{2}
with
\operatorname{det}Q=1
we have

m_{\mathbb{R}}(\left\{r\in[-1,1]:a_{t}u_{r}\Delta_{Q}\in\Upsilon(V,CV^{-D_{2}},\mathsf{h})\right\})\ll V^{-1}+\mathsf{h}^{-\frac{1}{D_{2}}}

for any

and

, where the implied constant depend only on
Q
and

.

5.3.
Avoidance estimates

In this subsection, we prove Proposition
5.6

Proposition 5.5
.

There is an absolute constant

Proof.

Let

{|\Omega|Te^{-t}\leq m_{\mathbb{R}}(\left\{r\in[-1,1]:a_{t}u_{r}\Delta_{Q}\in\Upsilon(V,CV^{-D_{2}},\mathsf{h})\right\})\ll V^{-1}+\mathsf{h}^{-\frac{1}{D_{2}}}.}

Since

∎

We now prove an avoidance estimate for

Proposition 5.6
.

There exists an absolute constant

We deduce Proposition

from Proposition

6.
Proof of the moment estimate

In this section, we prove the moment estimate in Theorem
2.7
. We denote by
m_{I}
the uniform Lebesgue probability measure on the interval
I=[-1,1]
. The next lemma allows us to write expanding translates of a unipotent orbit in the form of an iteration of random walks.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
s_{1},\cdots,s_{m}\geq 1
. Then

\int_{-\frac{1}{3}}^{\frac{1}{3}}f(a_{s_{1}+\cdots+s_{m}}u_{r};\Delta)dr\leq 2\int f(a_{s_{m}}u_{r_{m}}\cdots a_{s_{1}}u_{r_{1}};\Delta)dm_{I}^{\otimes m}(r_{1},\cdots,r_{m}),

\int f(a_{s_{m}}u_{r_{m}}\cdots a_{s_{1}}u_{r_{1}};\Delta)dm_{I}^{\otimes m}(r_{1},\cdots,r_{m})\leq\int_{-2}^{2}f(a_{s_{1}+\cdots+s_{m}}u_{r};\Delta)dr

for any non-negative measurable function
f:H\times X\to[0,\infty)
and
\Delta\in X
.

Proof.

Let
t_{i}=s_{1}+\cdots+s_{i-1}
and
\omega_{i}=\frac{e^{t_{i}}}{2}\mathds{1}_{[-e^{-t_{i}},e^{-t_{i}}]}
for
1\leq i\leq m
, and let
\omega=\omega_{1}*\cdots*\omega_{m}
. Notice that

\int f(a_{s_{m}}u_{r_{m}}\cdots a_{s_{1}}u_{r_{1}};\Delta)dm_{I}^{\otimes m}(r_{1},\cdots,r_{m})=\int f(a_{s_{1}+\cdots+s_{m}}u_{r};\Delta)\omega(r)dr.

By induction, one can check that for any
1\leq k\leq m
,

0\leq\omega_{1}*\cdots*\omega_{k}(r)\leq\frac{1}{2}\textrm{ for all }r\in\mathbb{R},

\omega_{1}*\cdots*\omega_{k}(r)=\frac{1}{2}\textrm{ if }|r|<1-\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}e^{-t_{i}},

\omega_{1}*\cdots*\omega_{k}(r)=0\textrm{ if }|r|>1+\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}e^{-t_{i}}.

Thus, we have
0\leq\omega(r)\leq\frac{1}{2}
for all
r\in\mathbb{R}
,
\omega(r)=\frac{1}{2}
if
|r|<\frac{1}{3}
, and
\omega(r)=0
if
|r|>2
. It follows that

{\int f(a_{s_{1}+\cdots+s_{m}}u_{r};\Delta)\omega(r)dr\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{-\frac{1}{3}}^{\frac{1}{3}}f(a_{s_{1}+\cdots+s_{m}}u_{r};\Delta)dr,}

{\int f(a_{s_{1}+\cdots+s_{m}}u_{r};\Delta)\omega(r)dr\leq\int_{-2}^{2}f(a_{s_{1}+\cdots+s_{m}}u_{r};\Delta)dr,}

completing the proof.
∎

We will also need the following elementary lemma.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
B>1
,
0<\delta<\frac{1}{1+B}
, and

be given. For any

, we can find a finite sequence
\left\{s_{i}\right\}_{1\leq i\leq N}
such that

,

for

Proof.

Let us denote

for
k\geq 0
. Since
B>1
and
\delta^{-1}>1+B
, we have
b_{k-1}\leq b_{k}\leq 2b_{k-1}
for any

. Thus, we can find

and

such that

,

∎

\displaystyle\leq 2\delta^{-1}\log s_{m+1}e^{8s_{m+1}}\int_{\overline{\Theta}_{m}}\widehat{\alpha}_{\eta,M}^{\prime}(a_{s_{m}}u_{r_{m}}\cdots a_{s_{1}}u_{r_{1}};\Delta_{Q})^{1+\delta}dm_{I}^{\otimes m}(r_{1},\ldots,r_{m})

\displaystyle\ll\delta^{-1}\log s_{m+1}e^{8s_{m+1}}e^{-10s_{m+1}}\ll e^{-s_{m+1}}

using (

). Observe that for any

and

from the construction of
\Theta
. By (

(\operatorname{id},\Delta_{Q})\notin\mathcal{E}_{s_{1},\eta,M}
.

Applying (

\displaystyle\mathsf{Y}_{t}

\displaystyle<\int_{I^{N}\setminus\Theta}\widehat{\alpha}_{\eta,M}(a_{s_{N}}u_{r_{N}}\cdots a_{s_{1}}u_{r_{1}};\Delta_{Q})^{1+\delta^{\prime}}dm_{I}^{\otimes N}(r_{1},\ldots,r_{N})

∎

7.1.

In this subsection, we show that the contribution of non-isotropic quasi-null vectors is small.

Proposition 7.1

Let

7.3.
Proof of Corollary

To deduce the full count in Corollary
1.4
from the modified count in Theorem
1.3
, it suffices to count the number of points on isotropic rational lines and planes.

Lemma 7.2
.

For a ternary quadratic form
Q
and a plane
0\in P\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
the restriction
Q|_{P}
is the square of a linear form if and only if the orthogonal line to
P
is isotropic for the dual form
Q^{*}
.

Proof.

We first claim that
Q(\mathbf{v}_{1})Q(\mathbf{v}_{2})-Q(\mathbf{v}_{1},\mathbf{v}_{2})^{2}=Q^{*}(\mathbf{v}_{1}\times\mathbf{v}_{2})
holds for any
\mathbf{v}_{1},\mathbf{v}_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
. The case
\mathbf{v}_{1}\times\mathbf{v}_{2}=0
is trivial. Otherwise we may choose
g\in G
so that
g\mathbf{v}_{1}=\mathbf{e}_{1}
and
g\mathbf{v}_{2}=\mathbf{e}_{2}
. Then
g^{*}(\mathbf{v}_{1}\times\mathbf{v}_{2})=(g\mathbf{v}_{1}\times g\mathbf{v}_{2})=\mathbf{e}_{3}
follows. Let
Q^{\prime}=Q\circ g^{-1}
. By a straightforward calculation we can check that
Q^{\prime}(\mathbf{e}_{1})Q^{\prime}(\mathbf{e}_{1})-Q^{\prime}(\mathbf{e}_{1},\mathbf{e}_{2})^{2}=(Q^{\prime})^{*}(\mathbf{e}_{3})
holds for any indefinite ternary quadratic form
Q^{\prime}
with
\operatorname{det}(Q^{\prime})=1
. We thus have

(7.13)

\displaystyle Q(\mathbf{v}_{1})Q(\mathbf{v}_{2})-Q(\mathbf{v}_{1},\mathbf{v}_{2})^{2}

\displaystyle=Q^{\prime}(\mathbf{e}_{1})Q^{\prime}(\mathbf{e}_{2})-Q^{\prime}(\mathbf{e}_{1},\mathbf{e}_{2})^{2}

\displaystyle=(Q^{\prime})^{*}(\mathbf{e}_{3})=(Q^{\prime})^{*}\big(g^{*}(\mathbf{v}_{1}\times\mathbf{v}_{2})\big)=Q^{*}(\mathbf{v}_{1}\times\mathbf{v}_{2}).

Let us choose a basis
\left\{\mathbf{v}_{1},\mathbf{v}_{2}\right\}
of
P
. Then for any
\mathbf{v}=a_{1}\mathbf{v}_{1}+a_{2}\mathbf{v}_{2}\in P
with
a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
we have

Q(\mathbf{v})=Q(a_{1}\mathbf{v}_{1}+a_{2}\mathbf{v}_{2})=Q(\mathbf{v}_{1})a_{1}^{2}+2Q(\mathbf{v}_{1},\mathbf{v}_{2})a_{1}a_{2}+Q(\mathbf{v}_{2})a_{2}^{2},

hence
Q|_{P}
is the square of a linear form if and only if
Q(\mathbf{v}_{1})Q(\mathbf{v}_{2})-Q(\mathbf{v}_{1},\mathbf{v}_{2})^{2}=0
. In view (
7.13
) this completes the proof.
∎

For a given indefinite ternary quadratic form
Q
, let us denote

\mathcal{L}(Q):=\left\{\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}_{\operatorname{prim}}:Q(\mathbf{m})=0\right\},

\mathcal{P}(Q):=\left\{\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}_{\operatorname{prim}}:Q^{*}(\mathbf{m})=0\right\}.

Each
\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{L}(Q)
is on an isotropic rational line through the origin, and each
\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{P}(Q)
is orthogonal to an isotropic rational plane through the origin. Since there are two primitive vectors on a rational line, Lemma
2.2
implies that
|\mathcal{L}(Q)|
and
|\mathcal{P}(Q)|
are at most
8
if
Q
is irrational.

The total number of the points of norm
<T
on the isotropic rational lines is asymptotically
\mathsf{L}_{Q}T
, where

\mathsf{L}_{Q}:=\sum_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{L}(Q)}\frac{1}{\|\mathbf{m}\|}.

Since the value of
Q
is always zero on isotropic rational lines, the contribution of the points on isotropic rational lines is
\mathsf{L}_{Q}\mathds{1}_{(a,b)}(0)
.

Now we estimate the contribution of points on isotropic rational planes. Let
\mathbf{m}\in\mathcal{P}(Q)
. We choose an integral basis
\left\{\mathbf{n}_{1}=\mathbf{n}_{1}(\mathbf{m}),\mathbf{n}_{2}=\mathbf{n}_{2}(\mathbf{m})\right\}
of the plane orthogonal to
\mathbf{m}
. Then
\mathbf{n}_{1}\times\mathbf{n}_{2}
is a scalar multiple of
\mathbf{m}
.

Using the identity (
7.13
), we find that

Q(\mathbf{n}_{1})Q(\mathbf{n}_{2})-Q(\mathbf{n}_{1},\mathbf{n}_{2})^{2}=Q^{*}(\mathbf{n}_{1}\times\mathbf{n}_{2})=0,

hence
\frac{Q(\mathbf{n}_{1},\mathbf{n}_{2})}{Q(\mathbf{n}_{1})}=\frac{Q(\mathbf{n}_{2})}{Q(\mathbf{n}_{1},\mathbf{n}_{2})}
holds. Note that
Q(\mathbf{n}_{1})
and
Q(\mathbf{n}_{2})
are positive as
Q
is of signature
(2,1)
.

We shall count the number of points
v
in this plane that satisfy

\|v\|<T
and
a<Q(v)<b
. Write
v=k_{1}\mathbf{n}_{1}+k_{2}\mathbf{n}_{2}
with
k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then

\displaystyle Q(v)

\displaystyle=Q(k_{1}\mathbf{n}_{1}+k_{2}\mathbf{n}_{2})

\displaystyle=Q(\mathbf{n}_{1})k_{1}^{2}+2Q(\mathbf{n}_{1},\mathbf{n}_{2})k_{1}k_{2}+Q(\mathbf{n}_{2})k_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=Q(\mathbf{n}_{1})\left(k_{1}+\frac{Q(\mathbf{n}_{1},\mathbf{n}_{2})}{Q(\mathbf{n}_{1})}k_{2}\right)^{2}+\frac{Q(\mathbf{n}_{1})Q(\mathbf{n}_{2})-Q(\mathbf{n}_{1},\mathbf{n}_{2})^{2}}{Q(\mathbf{n}_{1})}k_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=Q(\mathbf{n}_{1})\left(k_{1}+\frac{Q(\mathbf{n}_{1},\mathbf{n}_{2})}{Q(\mathbf{n}_{1})}k_{2}\right)^{2}=\left(\sqrt{Q(\mathbf{n}_{1})}k_{1}+\sqrt{Q(\mathbf{n}_{2})}k_{2}\right)^{2}.

Hence, the system of the inequalities
\|v\|<T
and
a<Q(v)<b
is written

(7.14)

\displaystyle\|k_{1}\mathbf{n}_{1}+k_{2}\mathbf{n}_{2}\|<T,

\displaystyle\sqrt{a^{+}}<

\displaystyle\left|\sqrt{Q(\mathbf{n}_{1})}k_{1}+\sqrt{Q(\mathbf{n}_{2})}k_{2}\right|<\sqrt{b^{+}}.

One can calculate that the area of this region is asymptotically

(7.15)

{\frac{2(\sqrt{b^{+}}-\sqrt{a^{+}})}{\|\sqrt{Q(\mathbf{n}_{1})}\mathbf{n}_{2}-\sqrt{Q(\mathbf{n}_{2})}\mathbf{n}_{1}\|}T\quad\textrm{ as }\;T\to\infty,}

where
a^{+}:=\max(a,0)
and
b^{+}:=\max(b,0)
.

If
\frac{\sqrt{Q(\mathbf{n}_{2})}}{\sqrt{Q(\mathbf{n}_{1})}}=\frac{Q(\mathbf{n}_{1},\mathbf{n}_{2})}{Q(\mathbf{n}_{1})}
is irrational, the number of integral points
(k_{1},k_{2})
in the region is asymptotically the same as (
7.15
), hence linear in
T
. If
\frac{\sqrt{Q(\mathbf{n}_{2})}}{\sqrt{Q(\mathbf{n}_{1})}}=\frac{Q(\mathbf{n}_{1},\mathbf{n}_{2})}{Q(\mathbf{n}_{1})}
is rational, then for the map
(k_{1},k_{2})\mapsto\sqrt{Q(\mathbf{n}_{1})}k_{1}+\sqrt{Q(\mathbf{n}_{2})}k_{2}
, the preimage of the interval
(\sqrt{a^{+}},\sqrt{b^{+}})
consists of finitely many parallel rational lines in
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. Consequently, the number of solutions to the system of inequalities (
7.14
) remains linear in
T
in this case as well.

To summarize, we demonstrated that for any
a<b
the number of points
v\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}
on isotropic lines and planes satisfying
\|v\|<T
and
a<Q(v)<b
is asymptotically
\mathsf{I}_{Q}(a,b)T
as
T\to\infty
, where the constant
\mathsf{I}_{Q}(a,b)\geq 0
depends only on
a,b
, and
Q
. In combination with Theorem
1.3
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{N_{Q}(a,b,T)}{T}=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{\widetilde{N}_{Q}(a,b,T)+\mathsf{I}_{Q}(a,b)T}{T}=\mathsf{C}_{Q}(b-a)+\mathsf{I}_{Q}(a,b).

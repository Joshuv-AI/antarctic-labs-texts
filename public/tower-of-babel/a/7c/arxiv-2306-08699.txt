Possibility of Spontaneous Symmetry Breaking in the Nambu-Jona-Lasinio model with torsion


Abstract
We discuss the Nambu-Jona-Lasinio (NJL) model in curved spacetime with torsion in the leading order of the 1 / N 1/N expansion. The effective potential of the scalar-torsion sector is calculated using the new technique based on the nonlocal part of anomaly-induced action which was recently found to produce the effective potential in the low-energy limit. The spontaneous symmetry breaking caused by the change of the torsion term is found, confirming the statements known from the existing literature. Furthermore, the gap equation is calculated as a function of curvature and torsion. Finally, the behavior of the effective four-fermion coupling constant as a function of the torsion is discussed. Keywords: Nambu-Jona-Lasinio, torsion, spontaneous symmetry breaking, gap equation.

One motivation for exploring the ECT coupled to fermions with the Holst term lies in the emergence of the four-fermion interaction term appearing in the action after solving for torsion, as we can show below through the following action

S=\dfrac{1}{\kappa^{2}}\int d^{4}x\,\sqrt{-g}\left[-\tilde{R}+\dfrac{1}{2\beta}\epsilon_{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}\tilde{R}^{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}\right]+\dfrac{i}{2}\int d^{4}x\,\sqrt{-g}\left[\bar{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\tilde{\nabla}_{\mu}\psi-\tilde{\nabla}_{\mu}\bar{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\psi\right],

(1)

where the constant
\kappa^{2}=16\pi G

the Newton constant,
g

g_{\mu\nu}
and

is the scalar curvature with torsion, which is given by

\tilde{R}=R-2\nabla_{\alpha}T^{\alpha}-\dfrac{2}{3}T_{\alpha}T^{\alpha}+\dfrac{1}{24}S_{\alpha}S^{\alpha}+\dfrac{1}{2}q_{\alpha\beta\gamma}q^{\alpha\beta\gamma},

(2)

with
R
being the Ricci scalar in GR,
and the torsion being divided into three irreducible components, which are the trace vector
T_{\beta}=T^{\alpha}{}_{\beta\alpha}
that is antisymetric in the last two indices, the axial vector (or pseudotrace)
S^{\nu}=\epsilon^{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}T_{\alpha\beta\mu}

q^{\alpha}{}_{\beta\gamma}
which satisfies two conditions
q^{\alpha}{}_{\beta\alpha}=0
and
\epsilon^{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}q_{\alpha\beta\mu}=0

The second term of right-hand side (
r.h.s.

\dfrac{1}{2\beta}\epsilon_{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}\tilde{R}^{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}=\dfrac{1}{2\beta}\left[-\nabla_{\mu}S^{\mu}-\dfrac{2}{3}T^{\mu}S_{\mu}+\dfrac{1}{2}\epsilon_{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}q^{\lambda\alpha\beta}q_{\lambda}{}^{\mu\nu}\right],

(3)

The Dirac action with torsion is the last term of
r.h.s.
of equation (

\gamma^{\mu}=e_{a}{}^{\mu}\gamma^{a}
is the Dirac matrix in curved spacetime, the quantities
e_{a}{}^{\mu}
is tetrad field, and the covariant derivative in the curved spacetime with torsion of the fermion field is written as follows

\tilde{\nabla}_{\mu}\psi=\partial_{\mu}\psi+\dfrac{i}{2}\tilde{\omega}_{\mu}{}^{ab}\sigma_{ab}\psi,\quad\textrm{and}\quad\tilde{\nabla}_{\mu}\bar{\psi}=\partial_{\mu}\bar{\psi}-\dfrac{i}{2}\bar{\psi}\,\tilde{\omega}_{\mu}{}^{ab}\sigma_{ab}\,,

(4)

where
\sigma_{ab}=\frac{i}{2}\left(\gamma_{a}\gamma_{b}-\gamma_{b}\gamma_{a}\right)

\displaystyle\tilde{\omega}_{\mu}{}^{ab}=\omega_{\mu}{}^{ab}+\dfrac{1}{4}K^{\alpha}{}_{\lambda\mu}\left(e^{\lambda a}e^{b}{}_{\alpha}-e^{\lambda b}e^{a}{}_{\alpha}\right)\quad\textrm{and}\quad K^{\alpha}{}_{\lambda\mu}=\dfrac{1}{2}\left(T^{\alpha}{}_{\lambda\mu}-T_{\lambda}{}^{\alpha}{}_{\mu}+T_{\mu}{}^{\alpha}{}_{\lambda}\right),

(5)

where
\omega_{\mu}{}^{ab}
is spinor connection without torsion. The second equation in (
5

It is important to highlight that the indices

are internal Lorentz indices while the Greek indices
\mu,\nu,\alpha,\ldots=0,1,2,3
are spacetime indices.

Replacing the previous equations (

\displaystyle S=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{\kappa^{2}}\int d^{4}x\left[-R+\dfrac{2}{3}T_{\alpha}T^{\alpha}-\dfrac{1}{24}S_{\alpha}S^{\alpha}-\dfrac{1}{3\beta}T_{\alpha}S^{\alpha}\right]

\displaystyle+\dfrac{1}{8}\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}S_{\alpha}J^{\alpha}+\dfrac{i}{2}\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\bar{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}\psi-\nabla_{\mu}\bar{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\psi\right],

When considering only the spinor action in (

Description and effective potential for the NJL model

Let us start with the NJL model
in an external gravitational field with torsion in
the
1/N
expansion (see, e.g.,
[

42

S_{\textrm{NJL}}=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left\{i\bar{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\left(\nabla_{\mu}-i\eta\gamma_{5}S_{\mu}\right)\psi+\dfrac{\lambda}{2N}\left[\left(\bar{\psi}\psi\right)^{2}+\left(\bar{\psi}i\gamma_{5}\psi\right)^{2}\right]\right\},

(8)

where
N
is the number of fermions, the coupling constant recast

\lambda

\eta

1/8

The interaction terms are constituted by the scalar channel
\left(\bar{\psi}\psi\right)^{2}

\left(\bar{\psi}i\gamma_{5}\psi\right)^{2}

As discussed in the previous section, it was expected that only the axial component of torsion would couple to the fermions. Due to the absence of the Einstein-Hilbert (EH) term and the Holst term in the action (
8

S_{\mu}
, in this context, will not be proportional to the axial current but will act as a external field.

The bosonization procedure the mean field approach consists of introducing the auxiliary fields
\rho

\pi

S\rightarrow S-\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left\{\dfrac{N}{2\lambda}\left[\rho+\dfrac{\lambda}{N}\bar{\psi}\psi\right]^{2}+\dfrac{N}{2\lambda}\left[\pi+\dfrac{\lambda}{N}\bar{\psi}i\gamma_{5}\psi\right]^{2}\right\}.

(9)

The action classically equivalent to (
8

S_{\textrm{NJL}}=\int d{{}^{4}}x\sqrt{-g}\left[i\bar{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\left(\nabla_{\mu}-i\eta\gamma_{5}S_{\mu}\right)\psi-\dfrac{N}{2\lambda}\left(\rho{{}^{2}}+\pi{{}^{2}}\right)-\bar{\psi}\left(\rho+i\gamma_{5}\pi\right)\psi\right].

(10)

The Euler-Lagrange equation for the auxiliary fields in the previous equation has the following form,

\displaystyle\rho=-\dfrac{\lambda}{N}\bar{\psi}\psi\quad\textrm{and}\quad\pi=-\dfrac{\lambda}{N}\bar{\psi}i\gamma_{5}\psi,

when replaced in (
10

8

4
Anomaly-induced effective action

Briefly, we can say that the anomaly-induced effective action is a simple construction aiming to describe loop corrections in the semiclassical approach.
The anomaly and induced action are almost equivalent to the local version of the usual renormalization group approach
[
27
]
, but provide a more detailed output, as explained, e.g. in
[
40
]
.
Here we follow this approach as it is the most economic one, and gives the same result as other methods.
In this section, we will use the conformal anomaly to find the covariant nonlocal solution of anomaly-induced action and, subsequently, in the next section, extract from it the effective potential.

Let us consider a model with Dirac spinor fields and scalar field that is also invariant under the local conformal transformations
2
2

2

The conformal transformation of the axial vector part of torsion does not follow the conformal transformation for the metric and torsion in the ECT. In this particular theory, there is a proportionality between
S_{\mu}
and the axial fermionic current, which allows the following conformal transformation, as

S^{\mu}=e^{-4\sigma}\bar{S}^{\mu}\quad\textrm{and}\quad S_{\nu}=e^{-2\sigma}\bar{S}_{\nu}.

However, this is not a universal rule. The action of torsion, in our case, has additional terms and the connection is more complicated. In fact, we have the freedom to choose the conformal transformation as we like and it is taken to be such that the classical action possesses conformal invariance and we can use trace anomaly to evaluate quantum corrections to the potential of scalar and torsion, see
[

50

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}=\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}e^{2\sigma},\quad S^{2}=\bar{S}^{2}e^{-2\sigma},\quad S_{\mu\nu}^{2}=\bar{S}_{\mu\nu}^{2}e^{-4\sigma},\quad\textrm{and}\quad\rho=\bar{\rho}e^{-\sigma},

(17)

whose bilinear form is given by

\hat{H}=i\gamma^{\mu}\left(\nabla_{\mu}-i\eta\gamma^{5}S_{\mu}\right)-\rho.

5
Anomaly-induced effective action in the IR

As we already know that the nonlocal form of the effective action was recently shown to admit the description in the IR limit, we must follow the procedure presented in
[
28
,
45
]
(see also
[
53
,
54
]
for other detailed). In this way, let us define some assumptions:

Assuming a relatively weak gravitational field in the early Universe, the torsion terms in (
24
) dominate over the square of the Weyl tensor. Consequently, we have the following hierachy:

\left|\rho^{2}S^{2}\right|\gg\left|C^{2}\right|
and
\left|S_{\mu\nu}^{2}\right|\gg\left|C^{2}\right|
.

In general relativity, the IR limit implies a weak gravitational field. Thus, we can describe the weak gravity through a small metric perturbation, i.e,,
\left|\square{R}\right|\gg\left|R^{2}_{\ldots}\right|
for all curvature tensor. In this way, the Green function in the induced action reduces to

G=\Delta_{4}^{-1}=\left(\square^{2}+2R^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}-\dfrac{2}{3}R\square+\dfrac{1}{3}R^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}\right)^{-1}\approx\dfrac{1}{\square{{}^{2}}}.

(25)

Thus, after a small algebra, the nonlocal part of the effective action, equation (
22
),
becomes

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\textrm{ind, nonloc}}^{\textrm{IR}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{6}\int_{x}\int_{y}\left(\beta_{1}\rho^{2}S^{2}+\dfrac{1}{4}\beta_{2}S_{\mu\nu}^{2}\right)_{x}\left(\dfrac{1}{\square}\right)_{x,y}R\left(y\right).

(26)

Here, we aim to find the low-energy effective action pertaining to the scalar-torsion sector mentioned in equation (
26
). To achieve this, we need to isolate the conformal factor from the metric. Subsequently, we draw upon an analogy with the renormalization group-based derivation of effective action
[
44
]
. Therefore, at one loop,
let us consider conformal transformations involving (
17
)
together with

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{\square}=\dfrac{1}{\bar{\square}}e^{2\sigma}\quad\textrm{and}\quad R=e^{-2\sigma}\left[\bar{R}-6\bar{\square}\sigma+\mathcal{O}\left(\sigma^{2}\right)\right],

(27)

then later assume linear approximation in
\sigma
terms in the previous equation.
After integration with the delta function, the low-energy effective action of the scalar-torsion sector from (

\Gamma_{\textrm{ind, nonloc}}^{\textrm{IR}}=-\int_{x}\left(\beta_{1}\bar{\rho}^{2}\bar{S}^{2}+\dfrac{1}{4}\beta_{2}\bar{S}_{\mu\nu}^{2}\right)\sigma\left(x\right).

(28)

We can see through this result that the anomaly-induced
action is a local version of the renormalization group corrected classical
action of torsion.

At this point, we will provide a concise overview of our classical action of torsion, found
[

\displaystyle-\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left\{\left[a_{1}+\dfrac{\beta_{1}}{2}\,\mbox{ln}\,\left(\dfrac{\rho^{2}+\eta^{2}S^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\right)\right]\rho^{2}S^{2}\right.

7
Conclusions and discussions

The effective action of the Nambu-Jona-Lasinio in an external gravitational field with torsion in the
1/N
expansion was found by introducing the auxiliary fields, i.e., using the technique of bosonization. The effective potential of the torsion is calculated using the new technique based on the nonlocal part of anomaly-induced action which was recently found to produce the effective potential in the low-energy limit.

Also, we were able to identify a Coleman-Weinberg potential for the NJL model with some specific values for the external torsion field, see Fig.
2
and for certain values of
a_{1}
in equation (
36
), see Fig.
5
. Furthermore, we concluded that the scalar-torsion sector has a more significant influence than curvature on the effective potential well, as can be seen in Fig.
1
. Later on, we computed the gap equation (
37
) with the intention of explicitly observing spontaneous symmetry breaking.

We concluded that the first-order phase transition occurs when the torsion value changes, i.e., the coupling constant has its value changed as a function of the torsion, as can be seen in Fig
3
. Looking along the graph in Fig
4
, we conclude that only the lower part (continuous line) holds physical relevance. Our decision is based on the observation that higher values of torsion prevent the effective potential of the NJL model from exhibiting behavior similar to that of the Coleman-Weinberg potential. Additionally, one notices that the chiral symmetry can be restored even if the coupling constant is kept in the broken phase region, but only for values of torsion above the critical curve contained in Fig
4
.

In the recent paper
[
56
]
, chiral symmetry breaking was investigated independently in the bosonized version of the Nambu-Jona-Lasino model in a Riemann-Cartan background. In principle, both studies can agree on the influence of the torsion field on the effective potential of the NJL model when considering
n>1
, as shown in Fig
5
. It is worth noting that the effective potential of the scalar-torsion sector (
33
) may not be suitable for describing dynamical symmetry breaking. This characteristic is attributed to the negative value of
\beta_{1}
, as demonstrated in
[
28
]
for a similar theory. On the other hand, as suggested in
[
28
]
, the function
\beta_{1}
could assume a change of sign at higher loops, thus reconciling the results of both works completely.

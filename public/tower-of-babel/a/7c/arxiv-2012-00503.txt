Radiative 𝜶 𝟕 ​ 𝒎 \bm{\alpha^{7}m} QED contribution to the helium Lamb shift
Vojtěch Patkóš, Vladimir A. Yerokhin, Krzysztof Pachucki

Abstract
We present a derivation of the last unknown part of the α 7 ​ m \alpha^{7}m contribution to the Lamb shift of a two-electron atom, induced by the radiative QED effects beyond the Bethe logarithm. This derivation is performed in the framework of nonrelativistic quantum electrodynamics and is valid for the triplet (spin S = 1 S=1 ) atomic states. The obtained formulas are free from any divergences and are suitable for a numerical evaluation. This opens a way for a complete numerical calculation of the α 7 ​ m \alpha^{7}m QED effects in helium, which will allow an accurate determination of the nuclear charge radius from measurements of helium transition frequencies.



I
Introduction

The helium atom has been extensively studied since the very advent of quantum mechanics as the
prototypical three-body problem and an ideal testing ground for various theoretical approaches
describing many-electron atoms. Modern theoretical calculations of helium spectra

pachucki:17:heSummary
approach the level of accuracy previously achievable only for the
simplest case of the hydrogen atom, with a potential for discovery of new effects beyond the Standard
Model. Among the major topics of current interest in the helium atom are possibilities to obtain an
’atomic physics’ value for the fine structure constant
\alpha=\nicefrac{{e^{2}}}{{(4\pi\epsilon_{0}\hbar c)}}

pachucki:10:hefs
;
kato:18
and to provide a novel
method for the determination of the nuclear charge radius
pachucki:15:jpcrd
;
zheng:17
.

Up to now, theoretical calculations of the helium Lamb shift allowed accurate determinations
only for the
differences
of the nuclear charge radii of two isotopes

patkos:17:singlet
. In order to determine the
absolute
value of the nuclear radius
with a precision comparable to what is expected from muonic helium
pohl:16:leap
, one
needs to perform a complete calculation of the
\alpha^{7}m
QED effects, which is a very
challenging task.

The
\alpha^{7}m
QED effects were first calculated for hydrogen in
Refs.
pachucki:93
;
pachucki:94
;
eides:95:pra
and recently generalized for the two-center
hydrogenic problem
korobov:14
. For helium, the
\alpha^{7}m
QED effects were investigated
in the context of the fine-structure splitting

pachucki:06:prl:he
;
pachucki:09:hefs
;
pachucki:10:hefs
, which offered extensive
simplifications to the underlying theory.

Several years ago we started a project of calculating the
\alpha^{7}m
QED effects in the Lamb
shift of helium. The first aim of the project is the triplet (spin
S=1
) states, whose theory
is simplified by the absence of the so-called contact operators
\propto\delta(\vec{r}_{1}-\vec{r}_{2})
. Intermediate results were published in
Refs.
yerokhin:18:betherel
;
patkos:20
. In the present work, we complete the derivation of
the
\alpha^{7}m
contribution, presenting formulas for the last missing part. More specifically, the

\alpha^{7}m

\displaystyle E^{(7)}=E_{L}^{(7)}+E^{(7)}_{\rm exch}+E^{(7)}_{\rm rad}\,,

(1)

where
E_{L}^{(7)}
is the relativistic correction to the so-called Bethe logarithm,
E^{(7)}_{\rm exch}
is the part induced by the electron-electron and electron-nucleus photon exchange, and

E^{(7)}_{\rm rad}
is induced by the radiative QED effects beyond the Bethe logarithm. The
relativistic correction to the Bethe logarithm was calculated in
Ref.
yerokhin:18:betherel

patkos:20

last missing part in Eq. (
1

\alpha^{7}m
contribution for the triplet states of a two-electron atom. The numerical evaluation
of the obtained formulas is left for the forthcoming investigation.

II
Basic approach

The radiative
{\alpha^{7}\,m}
contribution to the Lamb shift is represented by a sum of the
first-order and the second-order perturbation corrections,

\displaystyle E^{(7)}_{\rm rad}=

\displaystyle\ \langle H^{(7)}_{\rm rad}\rangle+2\,\big<H^{(4)}\,\frac{1}{(E_{0}-H_{0})^{\prime}}\,H^{(5)}_{\rm rad}\big>\,.

(2)

Here,
H^{(7)}_{\rm rad}
and
H^{(5)}_{\rm rad}
are the effective Hamiltonians induced by the
radiative effects of order
\alpha^{7}m
and
\alpha^{5}m
, respectively,
H^{(4)}
is the Breit
Hamiltonian (of order
\alpha^{4}m
), and
H_{0}
and
E_{0}
are the nonrelativistic Hamiltonian and the
corresponding eigenvalue, respectively.

E^{(7)}_{\rm rad}
consists of contributions coming from the one-loop self-energy (SE), the
one-loop vacuum polarization (VP), and the two-loop (rad2) and three-loop QED effects (rad3),

\displaystyle E^{(7)}_{\rm rad}=

\displaystyle\ E^{(7)}_{\rm SE}+E^{(7)}_{\rm VP}+E^{(7)}_{\rm rad2}+E^{(7)}_{\rm rad3}.

(3)

The main part of the present investigation will be devoted to the evaluation of the one-loop
self-energy correction, which is by far the most difficult part. The corresponding derivation is
presented in Secs.
III
-
VII
. The one-loop vacuum polarization is calculated
in Sec.
VIII
, whereas the two- and three-loop radiative effects are obtained in
Sec.
IX
.

One of the major problems encountered in deriving formulas for higher-order QED effects is
connected with numerous divergences appearing on intermediate stages of the calculation. In order
to systematically handle these divergences, we use dimensionally regularized nonrelativistic
quantum electrodynamics (NRQED), with the dimension of the space-time
D=4-2\,\epsilon
and the
dimension of space
d=3-2\,\epsilon
. The parameter
\epsilon
is considered as small, but only
on the level of matrix elements, where the analytic continuation to a noninteger spatial
dimension is allowed. The final results will be expanded in small
\epsilon
, and singular
contributions
\propto 1/\epsilon
will be canceled algebraically in momentum space. Subsequently,
the results will be transformed into the coordinate representation, where they can be
calculated numerically. The foundation of the dimensionally regularized NRQED in the context of
the hydrogen Lamb shift was laid in Ref.
pineda:98
, but our approach differs in many
details.

In our derivation of the radiative
{\alpha^{7}\,m}
correction for helium we will rely on the fact
that the corresponding correction is known for hydrogen-like atoms (see, e.g., a review

yerokhin:18:hydr
). With this in mind, in our derivation we will repeatedly drop the
first-order terms containing purely the electron-nucleus Dirac
\delta
-function. (We still have
to keep terms in which the electron-nucleus
\delta
-function is combined with the potential,
energy or momenta, however.) This procedure will simplify the derivation enormously and the
omitted
\delta
-like terms will be later restored by matching the known hydrogenic results. Our
calculation will be performed in the Coulomb gauge, unless explicitly specified otherwise.

When working in the dimensionally regularized NRQED, we need generalizations of basic operators
into the extended number of dimensions, shortly summarized below. The momentum-space
representation of the photon propagator preserves its form in
d
dimensions, namely,

g_{\mu\nu}/k^{2}
. The surface area of a
d
-dimensional unit sphere is

\Omega_{d}=\frac{2\,\pi^{d/2}}{\Gamma(d/2)}\,.

(4)

The electron-nucleus Coulomb interaction becomes

\displaystyle V_{C}(r)

\displaystyle=-Z\,e^{2}\,\int\frac{d^{d}k}{(2\,\pi)^{d}}\,\frac{e^{{\rm i}\,\vec{k}\cdot\vec{r}}}{k^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{Z\,e^{2}}{4\,\pi\,r^{1-2\,\epsilon}}\,\left[(4\,\pi)^{\epsilon}\,\frac{\Gamma(1-2\,\epsilon)}{\Gamma(1-\epsilon)}\right]\equiv-\left[\frac{Z\alpha}{r}\right]_{\epsilon}\,.

(5)

Here and in what follows,
[X]_{\epsilon}
will denote the
d
-dimensional generalization of
the
d=3
operator
X
.
The
d
-dimensional nonrelativistic Hamiltonian of a two-electron atom is given by

H_{0}=\sum_{a=1,2}\frac{\vec{p}_{a}^{\;2}}{2\,m}+V\,,

(6)

where

V=-\bigg[\frac{Z\,\alpha}{r_{1}}\bigg]_{\epsilon}-\bigg[\frac{Z\,\alpha}{r_{2}}\bigg]_{\epsilon}+\bigg[\frac{\alpha}{r}\bigg]_{\epsilon}\,,

(7)

and
\vec{r}=\vec{r}_{1}-\vec{r}_{2}
. The
d
-dimensional generalization of the Breit
Hamiltonian for a two-electron atom is

\displaystyle H^{(4)}

\displaystyle\ =H^{\prime(4)}+H^{\prime\prime(4)}\,,

(8)

\displaystyle H^{\prime(4)}

\displaystyle\ =-\frac{\pi\,\alpha}{m^{2}}\,\delta^{d}(r)+\sum_{a=1,2}\biggl\{-\frac{p_{a}^{4}}{8\,m^{3}}+\frac{\pi\,Z\alpha}{2\,m^{2}}\,\delta^{d}(r_{a})\biggr\}

\displaystyle\ -\frac{\alpha}{2\,m^{2}}\,p_{1}^{i}\,\biggl[\frac{\delta^{ij}}{r}+\frac{r^{i}\,r^{j}}{r^{3}}\biggr]_{\epsilon}\,p_{2}^{j}-\frac{\pi\,\alpha}{d\,m^{2}}\,\sigma_{1}^{ij}\,\sigma_{2}^{ij}\,\delta^{d}(r)\,,

(9)

\displaystyle H^{\prime\prime(4)}

\displaystyle\ =\sum_{a=1,2}\frac{1}{4\,m^{2}}\,\sigma_{a}^{ij}\,\bigl(\nabla^{i}_{a}V\bigr)p_{a}^{j}

\displaystyle\ +\frac{1}{4\,m^{2}}\,\sigma_{1}^{ik}\,\sigma_{2}^{jk}\,\left(\nabla^{i}\,\nabla^{j}-\frac{\delta^{ij}}{d}\,\nabla^{2}\right)\,\left[\frac{\alpha}{r}\right]_{\epsilon}

\displaystyle\ -\frac{1}{2\,m^{2}}\,\biggl(\sigma_{1}^{ij}\,\nabla^{i}\left[\frac{\alpha}{r}\right]_{\epsilon}\,p^{j}_{2}-\sigma_{2}^{ij}\,\nabla^{i}\left[\frac{\alpha}{r}\right]_{\epsilon}\,p^{j}_{1}\biggr)\,,

(10)

where
\delta^{d}(r)
is the Dirac
\delta
-function in
d
dimensions,
\sigma^{ij}=1/(2{\rm i})[\sigma^{i},\sigma^{j}]
, and
\sigma^{i}
are the Pauli matrices. In
d=3
spatial dimensions, the
matrices
\sigma^{ij}
reduce to
\sigma^{ij}=\epsilon^{ijk}\,\sigma^{k}
. The
d
-dimensional
generalization of the algebra of the Pauli matrices is summarized in Appendix B of
Ref.
patkos:20
.

III
NRQED Hamiltonian

We now turn to the derivation of the NRQED Hamiltonian incorporating relativistic corrections and a
class of radiative corrections that comes from the high photon momentum, in
d
dimensions. This Hamiltonian can be obtained from
the Dirac Hamiltonian by means of the Foldy-Wouthuysen transformation. In order to incorporate a high-energy
part of the radiative corrections, we start with the Dirac Hamiltonian modified by the
electromagnetic form factors
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
,

\displaystyle H_{D}=

\displaystyle\ \vec{\alpha}\cdot\left[\vec{p}-e\,F_{1}(\vec{\nabla}^{2})\,\vec{A}\right]+\beta\,m+e\,F_{1}(\vec{\nabla}^{2})\,A_{0}

\displaystyle+F_{2}(\vec{\nabla}^{2})\,\frac{e}{2\,m}\,\left({\rm i}\,\vec{\gamma}\cdot\vec{E}-\frac{\beta}{2}\,\Sigma^{ij}\,B^{ij}\right)\,,

(11)

where
B^{ij}=\nabla^{i}\,A^{j}-\nabla^{j}\,A^{i}\,
,
\nabla^{i}\equiv\nabla_{i}=\partial/\partial x^{i}
, and
\Sigma^{ij}=\frac{\rm i}{2}\,[\gamma^{i},\gamma^{j}]
.
Formulas for the electromagnetic form factors
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
are summarized in
Appendix
A
. Introducing the Foldy-Wouthuysen transformation defined by the
operator
S
,

\displaystyle S=

\displaystyle-\frac{\rm i}{2\,m}\,\left\{\beta\,\vec{\alpha}\cdot\vec{\pi}-\frac{1}{3\,m^{2}}\,\beta\,(\vec{\alpha}\cdot\vec{\pi})^{3}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{e(1+\kappa)}{2\,m}\,{\rm i}\,\vec{\alpha}\cdot\vec{E}-\frac{e\,\kappa}{8\,m^{2}}\,[\vec{\alpha}\cdot\vec{\pi},\beta\,\Sigma^{ij}\,B^{ij}]\right\}\,,

(12)

where
\kappa\equiv F_{2}(0)\approx\alpha/(2\,\pi)
is the electron anomalous magnetic moment,
the transformed Hamiltonian is obtained as

H_{FW}=e^{{\rm i}\,S}\,(H_{D}-{\rm i}\,\partial_{t})\,e^{-{\rm i}\,S}\,.

(13)

We will split the transformed Hamiltonian into two parts,
\delta H_{1}
and
\delta H_{2}
,
H_{FW}=\delta H_{1}+\delta H_{2}
, where
\delta H_{1}
contains the form factors only in the form of the
anomalous magnetic moment
\kappa
, and
\delta H_{2}
is the remainder.

The result is

\displaystyle\delta H_{1}=

\displaystyle\ \frac{\vec{\pi}^{2}}{2\,m}+eA^{0}-\frac{e}{4\,m}\,(1+\kappa)\,\sigma^{ij}\,B^{ij}-\frac{\vec{\pi}^{\;4}}{8\,m^{3}}-\frac{e}{8\,m^{2}}\,(1+2\,\kappa)\,\left[\vec{\nabla}\cdot\vec{E}+\sigma^{ij}\,\{E^{i},\pi^{j}\}\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{e}{16\,m^{3}}\left[\left(1+\frac{\kappa}{2}\right)\,\{p^{2}\,,\,\sigma^{ij}\,B^{ij}\}+4\,\kappa\,p^{k}\,\sigma^{ki}\,B^{ij}\,p^{j}+\kappa\,\vec{p}\,\,\sigma\cdot B\,\vec{p}\right]+\frac{\vec{p}^{\,6}}{16\,m^{5}}+\frac{(\kappa^{2}+\kappa+1)}{8\,m^{3}}\,e^{2}\,\vec{E}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{3+4\,\kappa}{64\,m^{4}}e\,\bigl\{\vec{p}^{\;2},\,\vec{\nabla}\cdot\vec{E}+\sigma^{ij}\,\{E^{i},p^{j}\}\bigr\}+\frac{5\,{\rm i}\,e}{128m^{4}}\,[p^{2},\{\vec{p}\,,\,\vec{E}\}]-\frac{e\,\kappa}{16m^{3}}\,\{\pi^{k},\partial_{t}E^{k}+\nabla^{i}\,B^{ik}\}+\ldots,

(14)

\displaystyle\delta H_{2}=

\displaystyle\ e\Big[F^{\prime}_{1}(0)\,\Delta+\frac{1}{2}F^{\prime\prime}_{1}(0)\,\Delta^{2}\Big]A^{0}-\frac{e}{m}\Big(F^{\prime}_{1}(0)\vec{p}\cdot\Delta\vec{A}+\frac{1}{4}\,\big[F^{\prime}_{1}(0)+F^{\prime}_{2}(0)\big]\,\sigma^{ij}\,\Delta B^{ij}\Big)

\displaystyle-\frac{e}{8\,m^{4}}\,\big[F^{\prime}_{1}(0)+2\,F^{\prime}_{2}(0)\big]\,\Delta\,\vec{\nabla}\cdot\vec{E}+\ldots,

(15)

where
\Delta=\vec{\nabla}^{2}
,
\{X,Y\}\equiv X\,Y+Y\,X
, and
\vec{\pi}=\vec{p}-e\,\vec{A}
. The
ellipsis denotes higher-order terms and spin-dependent terms that do not contribute to the
centroid energies.

It should be noted that the neglect of
\partial/\partial x^{0}
within the
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
form
factors is not valid for the electron-electron interactions. In this case we will calculate
effective operators from the one-photon exchange scattering amplitude in the Feynman gauge.
Moreover, there is an additional radiative correction to the NRQED Hamiltonian that is
not accounted for by the
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
form factors. It is represented by an effective local
operator quadratic in the field strengths. This operator is derived separately by evaluating a
low-energy limit of the electron scattering amplitude off the Coulomb field. It was calculated in
Ref.
jentschura:05:sese
to be

\delta H_{3}=\frac{e^{2}}{m^{3}}\,\vec{E}^{\;2}\,\chi\,,

(16)

where
E
is an electric field, and the function
\chi
is given
by

\chi=\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\bigg(\frac{1}{6}-\frac{1}{3\epsilon}\bigg)\,,

(17)

where we included only the one-loop part. We follow throughout this work the convention of Ref.
jentschura:05:sese
, that a common factor of
\big[(4\pi)^{\epsilon}\,\Gamma(1+\epsilon)\big]
for each loop integration is pulled out from all matrix elements.

We thus obtain the effective NRQED Hamiltonian as

H_{\rm nrqed}=\delta H_{1}+\delta H_{2}+\delta H_{3}\,.

(18)

Individual operators from
H_{\rm nrqed}
induce numerous contributions to the one-loop electron
self-energy, evaluated term by term in Sec.
VI.3
.

IV
Effective
\bm{\alpha^{5}m}
Hamiltonian

We now derive the self-energy part of the effective
\alpha^{5}\,m
operator
H^{(5)}_{\rm rad}
,
which is required for evaluation of the second-order contribution in Eq. (
2
). Despite
the fact that the
\alpha^{5}\,m
correction is well-known in principle
araki:57
;
sucher:58
,
we here need a
d
-dimensional generalization of the corresponding effective operator. From now
on we will make the simplification of setting the electron mass equal to one,
m=1
.

The leading-order self-energy correction
E^{(5)}_{\rm SE}
can be written as a sum of low-energy
and high-energy contributions,

E^{(5)}_{\rm SE}=E_{L0}+E_{H0}.

(19)

The low-energy part comes from the radiative photon momenta of the order
k\propto\alpha^{2}\,m
.
It is represented by the one-loop self-energy contribution in the leading nonrelativistic-dipole
approximation,

\displaystyle E_{L0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{2}\int\frac{d^{d}k}{(2\pi)^{d}2k}\,\delta_{\perp}^{ij}(k)

(20)

\displaystyle\times\,\Big\langle\phi\Big|p_{1}^{i}\,\frac{1}{E_{0}-H_{0}-k}\,p_{1}^{j}\Big|\phi\Big\rangle+(1\leftrightarrow 2)\,,

where
\delta_{\perp}^{ij}(k)=\delta^{ij}-k^{i}k^{j}/k^{2}
. The integration over
|k|
is
split into two parts,
\int_{0}^{\infty}=\int_{0}^{\Lambda}+\int_{\Lambda}^{\infty}
, leading to the
separation

E_{L0}=E_{L}^{(5)}+E^{\Lambda}_{L0}\,,

(21)

where the first term
E_{L}^{(5)}
is the electron self-energy part of the Bethe logarithm in three dimensions,

\displaystyle E_{L}^{(5)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2e^{2}}{3}\int_{0}^{\Lambda}\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}2k}\,\,\Big\langle\phi\Big|p_{1}^{i}\,\frac{1}{E_{0}-H_{0}-k}\,p_{1}^{i}\Big|\phi\Big\rangle

(22)

\displaystyle+(1\leftrightarrow 2)\,,

while the second terms is the remainder to be calculated in
d
-dimensions.
It is assumed that

\Lambda=\alpha^{2}\lambda\,,

(23)

with arbitrary large
\lambda
. Namely, it is assumed that the limit
\epsilon\rightarrow 0

is performed as the first and
\lambda\rightarrow\infty
as the second one.
So, we obtain

\displaystyle E_{L}^{(5)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{2\alpha}{3\pi}\Big\langle\phi\Big|p_{1}^{i}\,(H_{0}-E_{0})\ln\bigg[\frac{(H_{0}-E_{0})}{\alpha^{2}}\bigg]\,p_{1}^{i}\Big|\phi\Big\rangle

(24)

\displaystyle+\frac{\alpha\ln\lambda}{3\pi}\nabla_{1}^{2}V+(1\leftrightarrow 2)\,.

Here, the second term depends on
\lambda
, but it will cancel out in the sum in Eq. (
21
). The remainder
E^{\Lambda}_{L0}
is evaluated as

\displaystyle E^{\Lambda}_{L0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{2}\int_{\Lambda}^{\infty}\frac{d^{d}k}{(2\pi)^{d}2k}\,\delta_{\perp}^{ij}(k)

(25)

\displaystyle\times\,\bigg\langle\phi\bigg|p_{1}^{i}\,\frac{1}{E_{0}-H_{0}-k}\,p_{1}^{j}\bigg|\phi\bigg\rangle+(1\leftrightarrow 2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{2}}{4}\frac{d-1}{d}\int_{\Lambda}^{\infty}\frac{d^{d}k}{(2\pi)^{d}k^{3}}\,

\displaystyle\times\,\bigg\langle\phi\bigg|\big[p_{1}^{i},\big[H_{0}-E_{0},p_{1}^{i}\big]\big]\bigg|\phi\bigg\rangle+(1\leftrightarrow 2)\,.

Performing the integration with respect to
k
we obtain

\displaystyle E_{L0}^{\Lambda}

\displaystyle\ =\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\mathcal{I}_{\epsilon}\,\big(\nabla_{1}^{2}V+\nabla_{2}^{2}V\big)\,,

(26)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\epsilon}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi^{2}\frac{d-1}{d}\int_{\Lambda}^{\infty}\frac{d^{d}k}{(2\pi)^{d}k^{3}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{2^{-d}(d-1)\pi^{2-\frac{d}{2}}\left[\alpha^{2}\lambda\right]^{d-3}}{(d-3)\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{2}+1\right)}=\frac{5}{18}+\frac{1}{6}\ln\Lambda_{\epsilon}+O(\epsilon)

and

\ln{\Lambda_{\epsilon}}=\frac{1}{\epsilon}+2\ln(\alpha^{-2})-2\ln(2\lambda)\,.

(28)

The high-energy contribution,
E_{H0}
in Eq. (
19
), is induced by the region where the
momentum of the radiative photon is of the order of the electron mass
k\propto m
.
E_{H0}
is
conveniently split into two parts,

E_{H0}=E_{H0}^{A}+E_{H0}^{B}.

(29)

The first part comes from the exchange of a transverse photon with one vertex

-\frac{e}{4}\kappa\,\sigma\cdot B
. It is calculated in the nonretardation approximation, with
the result

\displaystyle E_{H0}^{A}=

\displaystyle\ -e^{2}\kappa\int\frac{d^{d}k}{(2\pi)^{d}\,2k^{2}}\delta_{\perp}^{ij}(k)\langle\phi|\biggl(p_{1}^{i}+\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{1}^{ki}\nabla_{1}^{k}\biggr)e^{{\rm i}\vec{k}\cdot\vec{r}_{1}}\biggl(\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{2}^{lj}\nabla_{2}^{l}\biggr)e^{-{\rm i}\vec{k}\cdot\vec{r}_{2}}|\phi\rangle+\textrm{h.c.}+(1\leftrightarrow 2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \alpha\kappa\,\bigg<\frac{1}{4}\sigma_{1}^{ik}\sigma_{2}^{jk}\bigg(\nabla^{i}\nabla^{j}-\frac{\delta^{ij}}{d}\nabla^{2}\bigg)\bigg[\frac{1}{r}\bigg]_{\epsilon}-\frac{\pi}{d}\sigma_{1}\cdot\sigma_{2}\,\delta^{d}(r)+\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{2}^{ij}\nabla_{1}^{i}\bigg[\frac{1}{r}\bigg]_{\epsilon}\,p_{1}^{j}\bigg>+(1\leftrightarrow 2).

(30)

The remaining high-energy contribution comes from the exchange of a Coulomb photon with the
vertices
eA^{0}
and
-\frac{e}{4}\kappa\vec{\nabla}\cdot\vec{E}-\frac{e}{4}\kappa\,\sigma^{ij}\{E^{i},p^{j}\}+F^{\prime}_{1}(0)\,\vec{\nabla}^{2}eA^{0}
.
The result is

E_{H0}^{B}=\Big<\Big[F^{\prime}_{1}(0)+\frac{1}{4}\kappa\Big]\nabla^{2}_{1}V+\frac{1}{4}\kappa\,\sigma_{1}^{ij}\{\nabla_{1}^{i}V,p_{1}^{j}\}\Big>+(1\leftrightarrow 2)\,.

(31)

Here
F^{\prime}_{1}(0)
contains the singular part
\propto 1/\epsilon
, see Appendix
A
. This
singularity will cancel exactly with that in
\ln\Lambda_{\epsilon}
, so that the final result for

E^{(5)}_{\rm SE}
is finite. We thus obtain

E^{(5)}_{\rm SE}=E_{L}^{(5)}+\langle H^{(5)}_{\rm SE}\rangle\,,

(32)

where the self-energy Hamiltonian
H^{(5)}_{\rm SE}=H^{\prime(5)}_{\rm SE}+H^{\prime\prime(5)}_{\rm SE}
is
given by

\displaystyle H^{\prime(5)}_{\rm SE}=

\displaystyle\ \frac{\alpha}{\pi}\bigg(\mathcal{I}_{\epsilon}-\frac{1}{6\epsilon}\bigg)\big(\nabla_{1}^{2}V+\nabla_{2}^{2}V\big)-\alpha\frac{2\pi\kappa}{d}\sigma_{1}\cdot\sigma_{2}\,\delta^{d}(r)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\ \frac{\alpha}{\pi}\bigg[\frac{5}{18}+\frac{1}{3}\ln(\alpha^{-2})-\frac{1}{3}\ln(2\lambda)\bigg]\big(\nabla_{1}^{2}V+\nabla_{2}^{2}V\big)-\alpha\frac{2\pi\kappa}{3}\sigma_{1}\cdot\sigma_{2}\,\delta^{d}(r)\,,

(33)

\displaystyle H^{\prime\prime(5)}_{\rm SE}=

\displaystyle\ \alpha\frac{\kappa}{2}\bigg\{\bigg[\vec{\sigma}_{2}\cdot\bigg(\vec{\nabla}_{1}\frac{1}{r}\bigg)\times\,\vec{p}_{1}+\vec{\sigma}_{1}\cdot\vec{\nabla}_{1}V\times\vec{p}_{1}+(1\leftrightarrow 2)\bigg]-\sigma_{1}^{i}\sigma_{2}^{j}\bigg(\nabla^{i}\nabla^{j}-\frac{\delta^{ij}}{3}\nabla^{2}\bigg)\frac{1}{r}\bigg\}\,.

(34)

Here we used
d=3
for
H^{\prime\prime(5)}_{\rm SE}
because the corresponding
\alpha^{7}m
second-order
contribution will not contain any singularities. Note that the second line in
Eq. (
IV
) holds only for the
\alpha^{5}m
correction. For the
\alpha^{7}m
contribution
coming from the second-order perturbation correction with
H^{(5)}_{\rm SE}
, we will have to
keep
\mathcal{I}_{\epsilon}
in the closed form because of a contribution from the linear in

\epsilon
terms.
We also note that both
E_{L}^{(5)}
and
H^{(5)}_{\rm SE}
are free of any divergencies but they
depend on the cutoff parameter
\lambda
, which cancels out in their sum.

V
Second-order
\bm{\alpha^{7}m}
self-energy correction

We now turn to the derivation of the second-order
\alpha^{7}m
self-energy contribution
represented by the second term in Eq. (
2
),

\displaystyle E^{(7)}_{\rm sec,SE}=2\,\langle H^{(4)}\,\frac{1}{(E_{0}-H_{0})^{\prime}}\,H^{(5)}_{\rm SE}\rangle\,,

(35)

where
H^{(4)}
is the Breit Hamiltonian and
H^{(5)}_{\rm SE}
was derived in the previous
section. It is convenient to split
E^{(7)}_{\rm sec,SE}
into the spin-dependent and
spin-independent parts,

\displaystyle E^{(7)}_{\rm sec,SE}=

\displaystyle\ 2\,\langle H^{\prime(4)}\,\frac{1}{(E_{0}-H_{0})^{\prime}}\,H^{\prime(5)}_{\rm SE}\rangle

\displaystyle+2\,\langle H^{\prime\prime(4)}\,\frac{1}{(E_{0}-H_{0})^{\prime}}\,H^{\prime\prime(5)}_{\rm SE}\rangle\,,

(36)

where
H^{\prime(4)}
and
H^{\prime\prime(4)}
are given by Eqs. (
II
) and (
10
),
respectively, and
H^{\prime(5)}_{\rm SE}
and
H^{\prime\prime(5)}_{\rm SE}
by Eqs. (
IV
) and
(
34
), respectively. The spin-dependent second-order contribution with
H^{\prime\prime(5)}_{\rm SE}
is finite and can be calculated numerically as it stands. The contribution induced by

H^{\prime(5)}_{\rm SE}
, however, is divergent and needs to be regularized and transformed in order
to move all
1/\epsilon
singularities into the first-order terms where they can be canceled.

Omitting terms
\propto\delta^{d}(r)
vanishing for triplet states, we rewrite the first part of
the
\alpha^{5}
Hamiltonian as

\displaystyle H^{\prime(5)}_{\rm SE}=

\displaystyle\ \frac{\alpha}{\pi}\bigg(\mathcal{I}_{\epsilon}-\frac{1}{6\epsilon}\bigg)\big[\vec{P},\big[V,\vec{P}\big]\big]\,,

(37)

where
\vec{P}=\vec{p}_{1}+\vec{p}_{2}
is the total momentum. The corresponding second-order
contribution is thus of the form

\displaystyle\frac{2\alpha}{\pi}\bigg(\mathcal{I}_{\epsilon}-\frac{1}{6\epsilon}\bigg)\,\langle H^{\prime(4)}\,\frac{1}{(E_{0}-H_{0})^{\prime}}\,\big[\vec{P},\big[V,\vec{P}\big]\big]\rangle\,.

(38)

In order to identify divergencies in this second-order matrix element, we introduce the following
identities

\displaystyle H^{\prime(4)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{H_{0}-E_{0},Q\}+H_{R}\,,

(39)

\displaystyle{}[P^{i},[V,P^{i}]]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{H_{0}-E_{0},Q^{\prime}\}+H_{R}^{\prime}\,,

(40)

where the operators
H_{R}
and
H_{R}^{\prime}
are chosen in such a way that the second-order
corrections with these operators are finite. We have

\displaystyle Q

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}\bigg[\frac{Z\alpha}{r_{1}}+\frac{Z\alpha}{r_{2}}\bigg]_{\epsilon}+\frac{\alpha}{2r}\,,

(41)

\displaystyle Q^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\bigg[\frac{Z\alpha}{r_{1}}+\frac{Z\alpha}{r_{2}}\bigg]_{\epsilon}\,,

(42)

and the regularized operators
H_{R}
and
H_{R}^{\prime}
are defined by their action on ket states as

\displaystyle H_{R}|\phi\rangle=

\displaystyle\ \bigg[-\frac{1}{2}(E_{0}-V)^{2}-\frac{Z\alpha}{4}\frac{\vec{r}_{1}\cdot\vec{\nabla}_{1}}{r_{1}^{3}}-\frac{Z\alpha}{4}\frac{\vec{r}_{2}\cdot\vec{\nabla}_{2}}{r_{2}^{3}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\nabla_{1}^{2}\nabla_{2}^{2}-p_{1}^{i}\frac{\alpha}{2r}\bigg(\delta^{ij}+\frac{r^{i}r^{j}}{r^{2}}\bigg)\,p_{2}^{j}\bigg]|\phi\rangle\,,

(43)

\displaystyle H_{R}^{\prime}|\phi\rangle=

\displaystyle\ -2Z\alpha\bigg(\frac{\vec{r}_{1}\cdot\vec{\nabla}_{1}}{r_{1}^{3}}+\frac{\vec{r}_{2}\cdot\vec{\nabla}_{2}}{r_{2}^{3}}\bigg)|\phi\rangle\,.

(44)

We now rewrite the second-order correction as

\displaystyle\bigg\langle[P^{i},[V,P^{i}]]\frac{1}{(E_{0}-H_{0})^{\prime}}H^{\prime(4)}\bigg\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg\langle[P^{i},[V,P^{i}]]\frac{1}{(E_{0}-H_{0})^{\prime}}H_{R}\bigg\rangle+\langle\big(\langle Q\rangle-Q)[P^{i},[V,P^{i}]]\rangle

(45)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg\langle H_{R}^{\prime}\frac{1}{(E_{0}-H_{0})^{\prime}}H_{R}\bigg\rangle+\langle H_{R}\,\big(\langle Q^{\prime}\rangle-Q^{\prime}\big)\rangle+\langle\big(\langle Q\rangle-Q\big)\,[P^{i},[V,P^{i}]]\rangle\,.

(46)

The first term in the last equation
is finite. The third term is evaluated as

\displaystyle\langle\big(\langle Q\rangle-Q\big)\,[P^{i},[V,P^{i}]]\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg\langle\bigg(\frac{E_{0}}{2}+\bigg\langle\frac{\alpha}{4r}\bigg\rangle+\frac{(Z-2)\alpha}{4r_{2}}\bigg)\,4\pi Z\alpha\,\delta^{3}(r_{1})+(1\leftrightarrow 2)\bigg\rangle\,.

(47)

Here we used the identity
\delta^{d}(r)\,[1/r]_{\epsilon}=0
, which is valid in dimensional regularization.
The evaluation of the second term in Eq. (
46
) is more complicated. We rewrite it as

\displaystyle\langle H_{R}\,\big(\langle Q^{\prime}\rangle-Q^{\prime}\big)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E^{(4)}\langle Q^{\prime}\rangle-\langle\big(H^{\prime(4)}-\{H_{0}-E_{0},Q\}\big)\,Q^{\prime}\rangle\rangle

(48)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg\langle E^{(4)}\bigg(\frac{2\alpha}{r}-4E_{0}\bigg)+\frac{1}{2}\big[(\vec{\nabla}_{1}Q)\cdot(\vec{\nabla}_{1}Q^{\prime})+(\vec{\nabla}_{2}Q)\cdot(\vec{\nabla}_{2}Q^{\prime})\big]-H^{\prime(4)}\,Q^{\prime}\bigg\rangle=X_{1}+X_{2}+X_{3}\,,

where
E^{(4)}=\langle H^{(4)}\rangle=\langle H_{R}\rangle
since
\langle H^{\prime\prime(4)}\rangle=0
.
Term
X_{1}
needs no further simplification,

X_{1}=\bigg\langle E^{(4)}\bigg(\frac{2\alpha}{r}-4E_{0}\bigg)\bigg\rangle\,.

(49)

Term
X_{2}
reduces to

\displaystyle X_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\big[(\vec{\nabla}_{1}Q)\cdot(\vec{\nabla}_{1}Q^{\prime})+(\vec{\nabla}_{2}Q)\cdot(\vec{\nabla}_{2}Q^{\prime})\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}\bigg[\frac{(Z\alpha)^{2}}{r_{1}^{4}}+\frac{(Z\alpha)^{2}}{r_{2}^{4}}\bigg]_{\epsilon}+\frac{1}{2}\bigg(\frac{Z\alpha\,\vec{r}_{1}}{r_{1}^{3}}-\frac{Z\alpha\,\vec{r}_{2}}{r_{2}^{3}}\bigg)\cdot\frac{\alpha\,\vec{r}}{r^{3}}\,,

(50)

where
[(Z\,\alpha)^{2}/r_{1}^{4}]_{\epsilon}=[\nabla(Z\,\alpha/r_{1})]^{2}_{\epsilon}
. Term
X_{3}
for triplet states is

\displaystyle X_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle-H^{\prime(4)}\,Q^{\prime}\rangle=\bigg\langle\bigg[\frac{1}{8}(p_{1}^{4}+p_{2}^{4})-\frac{Z\pi\alpha}{2}\big[\delta^{3}(r_{1})+\delta^{3}(r_{2})\big]+\frac{\alpha}{2}p_{1}^{i}\bigg(\frac{\delta^{ij}}{r}+\frac{r^{i}r^{j}}{r^{3}}\bigg)\,p_{2}^{j}\bigg]\,Q^{\prime}\bigg\rangle

(51)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg\langle\,\frac{1}{8}(p_{1}^{4}+p_{2}^{4})\,Q^{\prime}-(Z\alpha)^{2}\pi\bigg[\frac{\delta^{3}(r_{1})}{r_{2}}+\frac{\delta^{3}(r_{2})}{r_{1}}\bigg]+p_{1}^{i}\bigg(\frac{Z\alpha}{r_{1}}+\frac{Z\alpha}{r_{2}}\bigg)\frac{\alpha}{r}\bigg(\delta^{ij}+\frac{r^{i}r^{j}}{q^{2}}\bigg)\,p_{2}^{j}\bigg\rangle\,.

The first term in the last equation contains singularities. We transform it as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8}(p_{1}^{4}+p_{2}^{4})\,Q^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8}\big[(p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}^{2})^{2}-2\,p_{1}^{2}\,p_{2}^{2}\big]\,Q^{\prime}=\frac{1}{8}\bigg[(p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}^{2})\,Q^{\prime}\,(p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}^{2})+\frac{1}{2}\big[p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}^{2},\big[p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}^{2},Q^{\prime}\big]\big]-2\,p_{1}^{2}\,Q^{\prime}\,p_{2}^{2}\bigg]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\bigg[2(E_{0}-V)^{2}\bigg[\frac{Z\alpha}{r_{1}}+\frac{Z\alpha}{r_{2}}\bigg]_{\epsilon}+\bigg\{\bigg[\frac{(Z\alpha)^{2}}{r^{4}_{1}}\bigg]_{\epsilon}-\frac{Z\alpha\,\vec{r}_{1}}{r_{1}^{3}}\cdot\frac{\alpha\,\vec{r}}{r^{3}}+(1\leftrightarrow 2)\bigg\}-\,p_{1}^{2}\bigg(\frac{Z\alpha}{r_{1}}+\frac{Z\alpha}{r_{2}}\bigg)\,p_{2}^{2}\bigg]\,.

It is convenient to convert
[Z^{3}/r_{a}^{3}]_{\epsilon}
into
[Z^{2}/r_{a}^{4}]_{\epsilon}
, which is
achieved by the following identity

\displaystyle\left\langle\bigg[\frac{(Z\alpha)^{2}}{r_{1}^{4}}\bigg]_{\epsilon}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle-2\bigg[\frac{Z\alpha}{r_{1}}\bigg]^{3}_{\epsilon}-2\,Y_{1}\right\rangle\,,

(53)

where

Y_{1}=\bigg(E_{0}+\frac{Z\alpha}{r_{2}}-\frac{\alpha}{r}-\frac{p_{2}^{2}}{2}\bigg)\frac{(Z\alpha)^{2}}{r_{1}^{2}}-\frac{1}{2}\,\vec{p}_{1}\frac{(Z\alpha)^{2}}{r_{1}^{2}}\,\vec{p}_{1}\,.

(54)

The final result for the second-order contribution is a sum
X_{1}+X_{2}+X_{3}
and takes the form

\displaystyle E^{(7)}_{\rm sec,SE}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\,\langle H^{\prime\prime(4)}\,\frac{1}{(E_{0}-H_{0})^{\prime}}\,H^{\prime\prime(5)}_{\rm SE}\rangle+\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\bigg[\frac{5}{9}+\frac{2}{3}\ln(\alpha^{-2})-\frac{2}{3}\ln(2\lambda)\bigg]\bigg(\bigg\langle H_{R}^{\prime}\frac{1}{(E_{0}-H_{0})^{\prime}}H_{R}\bigg\rangle

\displaystyle+\bigg\langle E^{(4)}\bigg(\bigg\langle\frac{2\alpha}{r}\bigg\rangle-4E_{0}\bigg)+\bigg\{\bigg(2E_{0}+\bigg\langle\frac{\alpha}{r}\bigg\rangle\bigg)\,\pi Z\alpha\,\delta^{3}(r_{1})+\bigg(E_{0}+\frac{Z\alpha}{r_{2}}-\frac{\alpha}{r}\bigg)^{2}\,\frac{Z\alpha}{r_{1}}+2\bigg(E_{0}+\frac{Z\alpha}{r_{2}}-\frac{\alpha}{r}\bigg)\,\frac{(Z\alpha)^{2}}{r^{2}_{1}}

\displaystyle-Y_{1}-\frac{1}{2}p_{1}^{2}\,\frac{Z\alpha}{r_{1}}\,p_{2}^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\bigg[\frac{(Z\alpha)^{2}}{r_{1}^{4}}\bigg]_{\epsilon}-2\pi Z\alpha^{2}\frac{\delta^{3}(r_{1})}{r_{2}}+p_{1}^{i}\,\frac{Z\alpha}{r_{1}}\frac{\alpha}{r}\bigg(\delta^{ij}+\frac{r^{i}r^{j}}{r^{2}}\bigg)\,p_{2}^{j}+(1\leftrightarrow 2)\bigg\}\bigg\rangle\bigg)\,.

Note that the above expression is written completely in the coordinate representation. In the
following derivation of other contributions, we will often find it convenient to keep the
two-body electron-electron terms in the momentum representation. This is advantageous because in
the momentum representation the evaluation of the electron-electron terms is simpler.

VI
Effective
\bm{\alpha^{7}m}
Hamiltonian

We now turn to the derivation of the first-order self-energy
\alpha^{7}m
contribution
represented by the first term in Eq. (
2
). This correction can be conveniently split
into three parts, according to the region of the contributing photon momenta,

\big<H^{(7)}_{\rm SE}\big>=E_{L}^{\Lambda}+E_{M}+E_{H}\,.

(56)

Here, the three contributions
E_{L}^{\Lambda}
,
E_{M}
, and
E_{H}
are induced by momenta
k
of the
radiative photon of the order
m\alpha^{2}
,
m\alpha
, and
m
, respectively. These three
terms will be referred to as the low-energy, middle-energy, and high-energy parts,
correspondingly.

VI.1
Low-energy part

We now turn to the evaluation of relativistic corrections of order
\alpha^{7}m
to the
leading-order one-loop nonrelativistic dipole self-energy contribution,
E_{L0}
, given by
Eq. (
20
). Contributions arising from the small-
k
region,
k<\Lambda
, give rise to
the relativistic corrections to the Bethe logarithm, already computed in
Ref.
yerokhin:18:betherel
. So, in the present work we are only concerned with the
large-
k
region,
k>\Lambda
.

The
\alpha^{7}m
corrections to
E_{L0}^{\Lambda}
arise as: (
i
) perturbations of the
reference-state wave function
\phi
, the zeroth-order energy
E_{0}
and the zeroth-order
Hamiltonian
H_{0}
by the Breit Hamiltonian
H^{(4)}
, (
ii
) a perturbation of the current

\vec{p}\to\delta\vec{j}
, and (
iii
) a retardation (quadrupole) correction. The
corresponding
\alpha^{7}m
corrections will be denoted as
E_{L1}
,
E_{L2}
, and
E_{L3}
,
respectively,

\displaystyle E_{L}^{\Lambda}=E_{L1}^{\Lambda}+E_{L2}^{\Lambda}+E_{L3}^{\Lambda}\,.

(57)

Our calculation of these terms will be similar to that of the low-energy photon-exchange
contributions, described in Sec. III of Ref.
patkos:20
.

VI.1.1
E_{L1}^{\Lambda}

The first term
E_{L1}^{\Lambda}
is due to a perturbation by the Breit Hamiltonian and is written
as

\displaystyle E_{L1}^{\Lambda}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{2}\int_{\Lambda}^{\infty}\frac{d^{d}k}{(2\pi)^{d}2k}\,\delta_{\perp}^{ij}(k)\,\delta\bigg\langle\phi\bigg|p_{1}^{i}\frac{1}{E_{0}-H_{0}-k}p_{1}^{j}\bigg|\phi\bigg\rangle

(58)

\displaystyle+(1\leftrightarrow 2)\,.

Here the symbol
\delta\langle\cdots\rangle
denotes the first-order perturbation of the matrix
element
\langle\cdots\rangle
by the Breit Hamiltonian
H^{(4)}
, which implies perturbations of
the reference-state wave function
\phi
, the energy
E_{0}
, and the zeroth-order Hamiltonian

H_{0}
. Since
k
is much bigger than
H_{0}-E_{0}
, we can expand the integrand of Eq. (
58
)
in large
k
, keeping only the
1/k^{2}
term, while
1/k
contributes at the lower order of

\alpha^{6}\,m
. The result is

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\delta\Big<\phi\Big|p_{1}^{i}\frac{1}{E_{0}-H_{0}-k}p_{1}^{j}\Big|\phi\Big>+(1\leftrightarrow 2)

(59)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\,k^{2}}\,\delta\Big<\phi\Big|\big[p_{1}^{i},\big[H_{0}-E_{0},p_{1}^{j}\big]\big]\Big|\phi\Big>+(1\leftrightarrow 2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k^{2}}\,\Big<\phi\Big|[p_{1}^{i},[V,p_{1}^{j}]]\frac{1}{(E_{0}-H_{0})^{\prime}}H^{(4)}\Big|\phi\Big>

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2\,k^{2}}\,\Big<\phi\Big|[p_{1}^{i},[H^{(4)},p_{1}^{j}]]\Big|\phi\Big>+(1\leftrightarrow 2)\,.

The first term in the last equation is the second-order contribution already accounted for in the
previous section, and thus it will be omitted here. Note that the above expansion is valid up to the
electron-nucleus Dirac
\delta
-function terms, which we are omitting for the present. They will
be restored later by matching our calculation against the hydrogenic result. The same will apply
also for
E^{\Lambda}_{L2}
and
E^{\Lambda}_{L3}
.

After expanding in
\epsilon=(3-d)/2
and then in
\alpha
, we obtain the result for the
corresponding effective Hamiltonian
H_{L1}
,
E_{L1}^{\Lambda}=\langle H_{L1}\rangle
,

\displaystyle H_{L1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha^{2}\bigg(\frac{5}{36}+\frac{1}{12}\ln\Lambda_{\epsilon}\bigg)Z\,\big[\nabla_{1}^{2}\delta^{d}(r_{1})+\nabla_{2}^{2}\delta^{d}(r_{2})\big]-\alpha^{2}\bigg\{\sigma_{1}\cdot\sigma_{2}\bigg(-\frac{7}{27}-\frac{1}{9}\ln{\Lambda_{\epsilon}}\bigg)\,q^{2}

(60)

\displaystyle+\bigg(-\frac{5}{9}-\frac{1}{3}\ln{\Lambda_{\epsilon}}\bigg)\,\bigg[q^{2}+4\vec{P}_{1}\cdot\vec{P}_{2}-4\frac{\big(\vec{P}_{1}\cdot\vec{q}\big)\big(\vec{P}_{2}\cdot\vec{q}\big)}{q^{2}}\bigg]\bigg\}

where
\vec{P}_{1}=\frac{1}{2}(\vec{p}_{1}+\vec{p}_{1}{}^{\prime})
and
\vec{P}_{2}=\frac{1}{2}(\vec{p}_{2}+\vec{p}_{2}{}^{\prime})

are sums of the
in
and
out
momenta of the corresponding electron, and
\vec{q}=\vec{p}_{1}{}^{\prime}-\vec{p}_{1}
. Using the identity
valid for the expectation values of the operators,

\displaystyle\nabla_{1}^{2}\delta^{d}(r_{1})=-2\,p_{1}^{2}\,\delta^{d}(r_{1})+2\,\vec{p}_{1}\,\delta^{d}(r_{1})\,\vec{p}_{1}=-4\,(E_{0}-V-p_{2}^{2}/2)\,\delta^{d}(r_{1})+2\,\vec{p}_{1}\,\delta^{d}(r_{1})\,\vec{p}_{1}\,,

(61)

we simplify the expression further, obtaining the final result

\displaystyle H_{L1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha^{2}\bigg\{Z\delta^{d}(r_{1})\bigg(-\frac{5}{9}-\frac{1}{3}\ln\Lambda_{\epsilon}\bigg)\bigg(E_{0}-V-\frac{p_{2}^{2}}{2}\bigg)+\bigg(\frac{5}{18}+\frac{1}{6}\ln\Lambda_{\epsilon}\bigg)\,\vec{p}_{1}\,Z\delta^{d}(r_{1})\,\vec{p}_{1}+(1\leftrightarrow 2)\bigg\}

(62)

\displaystyle+\alpha^{2}\bigg\{\sigma_{1}\cdot\sigma_{2}\bigg(\frac{7}{27}+\frac{1}{9}\ln{\Lambda_{\epsilon}}\bigg)\,q^{2}+\bigg(\frac{5}{9}+\frac{1}{3}\ln{\Lambda_{\epsilon}}\bigg)\,\bigg[q^{2}+4\vec{P}_{1}\cdot\vec{P}_{2}-4\frac{\big(\vec{P}_{1}\cdot\vec{q}\big)\big(\vec{P}_{2}\cdot\vec{q}\big)}{q^{2}}\bigg]\bigg\}\,.

Note that in the above expression we keep the electron-nucleus terms in the coordinate
representation but the electron-electron terms in the momentum representation.

VI.1.2
E_{L2}^{\Lambda}

The second term in Eq. (
57
),
E_{L2}^{\Lambda}
, comes from a correction to the
current. Specifically,
\vec{p}_{1}
gets a correction
\delta\vec{j}_{1}
, which is

\displaystyle\delta j^{i}_{1}={\rm i}\big[H^{(4)},r_{1}^{i}\big]=-\frac{1}{2}p_{1}^{i}p_{1}^{2}-\frac{\alpha}{2}\bigg[\frac{\delta^{ij}}{r}+\frac{r^{i}r^{j}}{r^{3}}\bigg]_{\epsilon}\,p_{2}^{j}\,,

(63)

and the same for
\vec{p}_{2}
. The contribution

E_{L2}^{\Lambda}
is then

\displaystyle E_{L2}^{\Lambda}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2e^{2}\int_{\Lambda}^{\infty}\frac{d^{d}k}{(2\pi)^{d}2k}\,\delta_{\perp}^{ij}(k)\,\bigg\langle\phi\bigg|\delta j_{1}^{i}\frac{1}{E_{0}-H_{0}-k}p_{1}^{j}\bigg|\phi\bigg\rangle+(1\leftrightarrow 2)\,.

(64)

Expanding this expression in large
k
and performing the angular average, we arrive at

\displaystyle E_{L2}^{\Lambda}=e^{2}\frac{d-1}{d}\int_{\Lambda}^{\infty}\frac{d^{d}k}{(2\pi)^{d}2k^{3}}\big\langle\phi\big|\,[\delta j_{1}^{i},[V,p_{1}^{i}]]\,\big|\phi\big\rangle+(1\leftrightarrow 2)=E_{L2}^{A}+E_{L2}^{B}+E_{L2}^{C}\,.

(65)

This expression consists of three-body and two-body terms.
The three-body contribution
E_{L2}^{A}
is due to
the first term in
\delta\vec{j}_{1}
; it is transformed as

\displaystyle E_{L2}^{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-e^{2}\frac{d-1}{2d}\int_{\Lambda}^{\infty}\frac{d^{d}k}{(2\pi)^{d}2k^{3}}\bigg\langle\phi\bigg|\bigg[p_{1}^{i}\,p_{1}^{2},\bigg[-\bigg[\frac{Z\alpha}{r_{1}}\bigg]_{\epsilon},p_{1}^{i}\bigg]\bigg]\bigg|\phi\bigg\rangle+(1\leftrightarrow 2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\bigg(-\frac{5}{18}-\frac{1}{6}\ln\Lambda_{\epsilon}\bigg)\bigg\langle\phi\bigg|-\bigg[V,\bigg[p_{1}^{2},\bigg[\frac{Z\alpha}{r_{1}}\bigg]_{\epsilon}\bigg]\bigg]+8\pi Z\alpha\,\delta^{d}(r_{1})\,\big(E_{0}-V\big)-\vec{p}_{2}\,4\pi Z\alpha\,\delta^{d}(r_{1})\,\vec{p}_{2}\bigg|\phi\bigg\rangle+(1\leftrightarrow 2)\,.

There are two two-body contributions, the first one coming from the term
[-\frac{1}{2}p_{1}^{i}\,p_{1}^{2},[\alpha/r,p_{1}^{i}]]
. We evaluate it by switching into the momentum representation,

\displaystyle E_{L2}^{B}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{2}\frac{d-1}{d}\int_{\Lambda}^{\infty}\frac{d^{d}k}{(2\pi)^{d}2k^{3}}\bigg\langle\phi\bigg|\bigg[-\frac{1}{2}p_{1}^{i}\,p_{1}^{2},\bigg[\bigg[\frac{\alpha}{r}\bigg]_{\epsilon},p_{1}^{i}\bigg]\bigg]\bigg|\phi\bigg\rangle+(1\leftrightarrow 2)

(67)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha^{2}\bigg(\frac{20}{9}+\frac{4}{3}\ln{\Lambda_{\epsilon}}\bigg)\bigg\langle\phi\bigg|\frac{1}{2}\big(\vec{P}_{1}-\vec{P}_{2}\big)^{2}+\vec{P}_{1}\cdot\vec{P}_{2}+\frac{1}{4}q^{2}+\frac{\big[\big(\vec{P}_{1}-\vec{P}_{2}\big)\cdot\vec{q}\big]^{2}}{q^{2}}+2\frac{\big(\vec{P}_{1}\cdot\vec{q}\big)\big(\vec{P}_{2}\cdot\vec{q}\big)}{q^{2}}\bigg|\phi\bigg\rangle\,.

The two-body contribution induced by the second term in
\delta\vec{j}_{1}
is evaluated with help of
integration formulas from Appendix C of Ref.
patkos:20
as

\displaystyle E_{L2}^{C}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{2}\frac{d-1}{d}\int_{\Lambda}^{\infty}\frac{d^{d}k}{(2\pi)^{d}2k^{3}}\big\langle\phi\big|\,\bigg(-\frac{\alpha}{2}\bigg[\frac{\delta^{ij}}{r}+\frac{r^{i}r^{j}}{r^{3}}\bigg]_{\epsilon}\bigg)\bigg[\,p_{2}^{j},\bigg[\bigg[\frac{\alpha}{r}\bigg]_{\epsilon},p_{1}^{i}\bigg]\bigg]\,\big|\phi\big\rangle+(1\leftrightarrow 2)

(68)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi\alpha^{3}\bigg\langle\phi\bigg|\bigg(-\frac{4}{9}-\frac{2}{3}\ln{\Lambda_{\epsilon}}-\frac{4}{3}\ln 2+\frac{4}{3}\ln q\bigg)q\bigg|\phi\bigg\rangle\,.

Adding together the individual contributions to
E_{L2}^{\Lambda}
and using the expectation value
identity

\alpha\frac{\big[(\vec{P}_{1}-\vec{P}_{2})\cdot\vec{q}\big]^{2}}{q^{2}}=\bigg\{\frac{1}{4\pi}\bigg[\frac{Z\alpha\,\vec{r}_{1}}{r_{1}^{3}}\bigg]_{\epsilon}\cdot\bigg[\frac{\alpha\,\vec{r}}{r^{3}}\bigg]_{\epsilon}+(1\leftrightarrow 2)\bigg\}+\frac{\pi\alpha^{2}}{2}\big\{1+\epsilon\,(2\ln 2-2\ln q)\big\}q\,,

(69)

we write the result in terms of the effective Hamiltonian
H_{L2}
,
E_{L2}^{\Lambda}=\langle H_{L2}\rangle
, which is

\displaystyle H_{L2}=

\displaystyle\ \frac{\alpha}{\pi}\bigg\{\bigg(-\frac{5}{9}-\frac{1}{3}\ln\Lambda_{\epsilon}\bigg)\bigg(\bigg[\frac{(Z\alpha)^{2}}{r_{1}^{4}}\bigg]_{\epsilon}-2\bigg[\frac{Z\alpha\,\vec{r}_{1}}{r_{1}^{3}}\bigg]_{\epsilon}\cdot\bigg[\frac{\alpha\,\vec{r}}{r^{3}}\bigg]_{\epsilon}+4\pi Z\alpha\,\delta^{d}(r_{1})\bigg(E_{0}-V-\frac{p_{2}^{2}}{2m}\bigg)\bigg)+(1\leftrightarrow 2)\bigg\}

\displaystyle\ +\alpha^{2}\bigg(\frac{20}{9}+\frac{4}{3}\ln{\Lambda_{\epsilon}}\bigg)\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\big(\vec{P}_{1}-\vec{P}_{2}\big)^{2}+\vec{P}_{1}\cdot\vec{P}_{2}+\frac{1}{4}q^{2}+2\frac{\big(\vec{P}_{1}\cdot\vec{q}\big)\big(\vec{P}_{2}\cdot\vec{q}\big)}{q^{2}}\bigg)+\frac{2\pi}{3}\alpha^{3}\,q\,.

(70)

VI.1.3
E_{L3}^{\Lambda}

The third term in Eq. (
57
),
E_{L3}^{\Lambda}
, is a retardation correction. It can be
expressed as

\displaystyle E_{L3}^{\Lambda}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{2}\int_{\Lambda}^{\infty}\frac{d^{d}k}{(2\pi)^{d}2k}\,\delta_{\perp}^{ij}(k)\,\delta_{k^{2}}\bigg\langle\phi\bigg|p_{1}^{i}\,e^{{\rm i}\vec{k}\cdot\vec{r}_{1}}\frac{1}{E_{0}-H_{0}-k}p_{1}^{j}\,e^{-{\rm i}\vec{k}\cdot\vec{r}_{1}}\bigg|\phi\bigg\rangle+(1\leftrightarrow 2)\,,

(71)

where the symbol
\delta_{k^{2}}\big<\ldots\big>
means that the exponential factors
e^{{\rm i}\vec{k}\cdot\vec{r}_{1}}
and
e^{-{\rm i}\vec{k}\cdot\vec{r}_{1}}
in the matrix element

\big<\ldots\big>
are expanded in small
k
up to order
k^{2}
. Because
\Lambda
is arbitrarily
large, we perform the large-
k
expansion of the resolvent,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{E_{0}-H_{0}-k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{k}+\frac{H_{0}-E_{0}}{k^{2}}-\frac{(H_{0}-E_{0})^{2}}{k^{3}}+\frac{(H_{0}-E_{0})^{3}}{k^{4}}+\cdots\,.

(72)

The
k
expansion needs to be extended up to order
k^{-4}
because of the additional
k^{2}
from
the expansion of the exponential factors. The resulting correction of order
\alpha^{7}\,m
is

\displaystyle E_{L3}^{\Lambda}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{2}\int_{\Lambda}^{\infty}\frac{d^{d}k}{(2\pi)^{d}2k^{5}}\,\delta_{\perp}^{ij}(k)\,\delta_{k^{2}}\bigg\langle\phi\bigg|p_{1}^{i}\,e^{{\rm i}\vec{k}\cdot\vec{r}_{1}}(H_{0}-E_{0})^{3}\,p_{1}^{j}\,e^{-{\rm i}\vec{k}\cdot\vec{r}_{1}}\bigg|\phi\bigg\rangle+(1\leftrightarrow 2)\,.

(73)

The radial integration
k
can be performed in the same way as in the previous low-energy contributions.
For the angular integration we will use the formulas from Appendix C of Ref.
patkos:20
.
The matrix element in Eq. (
73
) can be simplified by using identity
\exp({\rm i}\vec{k}\cdot\vec{r})\,f(\vec{p})\,\exp(-{\rm i}\vec{k}\cdot\vec{r})=f(\vec{p}-\vec{k})\,

have

\displaystyle\delta_{k^{2}}\bigg[p_{1}^{i}\,e^{{\rm i}\vec{k}\cdot\vec{r}_{1}}(H_{0}-E_{0})^{3}\,p_{1}^{j}\,e^{-{\rm i}\vec{k}\cdot\vec{r}_{1}}\bigg]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{k^{2}}\bigg[p_{1}^{i}\,\bigg(H_{0}-E_{0}-\vec{p}_{1}\cdot\vec{k}+\frac{k^{2}}{2}\bigg)^{3}\,p_{1}^{j}\bigg]

(74)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p_{1}^{i}\bigg[\frac{3}{2}(H_{0}-E_{0})^{2}\,k^{2}+\vec{p}_{1}\cdot\vec{k}\,(H_{0}-E_{0})\,\vec{p}_{1}\cdot\vec{k}+2\,\big(\vec{p}_{1}\cdot\vec{k}\big)^{2}\,(H_{0}-E_{0})\bigg]\,p_{1}^{j}\,.

E_{L3}^{A}
,
E_{L3}^{B}
, and

(75)

Starting with the three-photon contribution

, we obtain

\displaystyle E_{L3}^{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{3e^{2}}{2}\int_{\Lambda}^{\infty}\frac{d^{d}k}{(2\pi)^{d}2k^{3}}\,\delta_{\perp}^{ij}(k)\,\bigg\langle\phi\bigg|p_{1}^{i}\,(H_{0}-E_{0})^{2}\,p_{1}^{j}\bigg|\phi\bigg\rangle+(1\leftrightarrow 2)\,.

(76)

Averaging this expression over the angular variables, commuting
H_{0}-E_{0}
to the left and to the

\displaystyle E_{L3}^{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg\langle\phi\bigg|\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\bigg\{\bigg(\frac{5}{6}+\frac{1}{2}\ln\Lambda_{\epsilon}\bigg)\bigg[\frac{(Z\alpha)^{2}}{r_{1}^{4}}\bigg]_{\epsilon}-\bigg(\frac{5}{3}+\ln\Lambda_{\epsilon}\bigg)\bigg[\frac{Z\alpha\,\vec{r}_{1}}{r_{1}^{3}}\bigg]_{\epsilon}\cdot\bigg[\frac{\alpha\,\vec{r}}{r^{3}}\bigg]_{\epsilon}+(1\leftrightarrow 2)\bigg\}

(77)

\displaystyle+\pi\alpha^{3}\bigg(-\frac{5}{3}-\ln\Lambda_{\epsilon}-2\ln 2+2\ln q\bigg)\,q\bigg|\phi\bigg\rangle\,.

The contribution of the second term is evaluated as

\displaystyle E_{L3}^{B}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{2}\int_{\Lambda}^{\infty}\frac{d^{d}k}{(2\pi)^{d}2k^{5}}\,\delta_{\perp}^{ij}(k)k^{m}k^{n}\,\bigg\langle\phi\bigg|p_{1}^{i}p_{1}^{m}\,(H_{0}-E_{0})\,p_{1}^{n}p_{1}^{j}\bigg|\phi\bigg\rangle+(1\leftrightarrow 2)

(78)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{2}}{d(d+2)}\int_{\Lambda}^{\infty}\frac{d^{d}k}{(2\pi)^{d}2k^{3}}\,\bigg\langle\phi\bigg|d\,p_{1}^{i}p_{1}^{j}\,(H_{0}-E_{0})\,p_{1}^{i}p_{1}^{j}-p_{1}^{2}\,(H_{0}-E_{0})\,p_{1}^{2}\bigg|\phi\bigg\rangle+(1\leftrightarrow 2)\,.

We transform this expression further with the help of the following identities,

\displaystyle p_{1}^{2}\,(H_{0}-E_{0})\,p_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\,\big[p_{1}^{2},\big[H_{0}-E_{0},p_{1}^{2}\big]\big]=\frac{1}{2}\,\big[p_{1}^{2},\big[V,p_{1}^{2}\big]\big]\,,

(79)

\displaystyle p_{1}^{i}\,\big[p_{1}^{i},\big[V,p_{1}^{j}\big]\big]\,p_{1}^{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\,\big[p_{1}^{2},\big[V,p_{1}^{2}\big]\big]-\frac{1}{2}\,p_{1}^{2}\,\big[\,p_{1}^{i},\big[V,p_{1}^{i}\big]\big]+\frac{1}{2}\,p_{1}^{i}\,\big[p_{1}^{j},\big[V,p_{1}^{j}\big]\big]\,p_{1}^{i}\,,

(80)

and

\displaystyle p_{1}^{i}p_{1}^{j}\,(H_{0}-E_{0})\,p_{1}^{i}p_{1}^{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\,p_{1}^{2}\,\big[\,p_{1}^{i},\big[V,p_{1}^{i}\big]\big]+\frac{1}{4}\,\big[p_{1}^{2},\big[V,p_{1}^{2}\big]\big]+\frac{1}{2}\,p_{1}^{i}\,\big[p_{1}^{j},\big[V,p_{1}^{j}\big]\big]\,p_{1}^{i}\,.

(81)

The result for the second term is

\displaystyle E_{L3}^{B}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\,\bigg\langle\phi\bigg|\bigg(\frac{2}{225}+\frac{1}{120}\ln\Lambda_{\epsilon}\bigg)\big[p_{1}^{2},\big[V,p_{1}^{2}\big]\big]+\bigg(\frac{3}{25}+\frac{1}{20}\ln\Lambda_{\epsilon}\bigg)

(82)

\displaystyle\times\bigg(p_{1}^{i}\,\big[p_{1}^{j},\big[V,p_{1}^{j}\big]\big]\,p_{1}^{i}+p_{1}^{2}\,\big[\,p_{1}^{i},\big[V,p_{1}^{i}\big]\big]\bigg)\bigg|\phi\bigg\rangle+(1\leftrightarrow 2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg\langle\phi\bigg|\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\,\bigg\{\bigg(\frac{8}{225}+\frac{1}{30}\ln\Lambda_{\epsilon}\bigg)\bigg(\bigg[\frac{(Z\alpha)^{2}}{r_{1}^{4}}\bigg]_{\epsilon}-\bigg[\frac{Z\alpha\,\vec{r}_{1}}{r_{1}^{3}}\bigg]_{\epsilon}\cdot\bigg[\frac{\alpha\,\vec{r}}{r^{3}}\bigg]_{\epsilon}\bigg)+\bigg(\frac{3}{25}+\frac{1}{20}\ln\Lambda_{\epsilon}\bigg)

\displaystyle\times\bigg(p_{1}^{i}\,4\pi Z\alpha\,\delta^{d}(r_{1})\,p_{1}^{i}+\bigg(E_{0}-V-\frac{p_{2}^{2}}{2}\bigg)\,8\pi Z\alpha\,\delta^{d}(r_{1})\bigg)+(1\leftrightarrow 2)\bigg\}

\displaystyle+\alpha^{2}\bigg\{-\frac{32}{225}\frac{\big[(\vec{P}_{1}-\vec{P}_{2})\cdot\vec{q}\big]^{2}}{q^{2}}-\frac{24}{25}(\vec{P}_{1}-\vec{P}_{2})^{2}-\frac{48}{25}\vec{P}_{1}\cdot\vec{P}_{2}-\frac{64}{225}\frac{\big(\vec{P}_{1}\cdot\vec{q}\big)\big(\vec{P}_{2}\cdot\vec{q}\big)}{q^{2}}

\displaystyle+\ln\Lambda_{\epsilon}\bigg(-\frac{2}{5}(\vec{P}_{1}-\vec{P}_{2})^{2}-\frac{2}{15}\frac{\big[(\vec{P}_{1}-\vec{P}_{2})\cdot\vec{q}\big]^{2}}{q^{2}}-\frac{4}{5}\vec{P}_{1}\cdot\vec{P}_{2}-\frac{4}{15}\frac{\big(\vec{P}_{1}\cdot\vec{q}\big)\big(\vec{P}_{2}\cdot\vec{q}\big)}{q^{2}}\bigg)\bigg\}\bigg|\phi\bigg\rangle\,.

Finally, the term
E_{L3}^{C}
is calculated as

\displaystyle E_{L3}^{C}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2e^{2}\int_{\Lambda}^{\infty}\frac{d^{d}k}{(2\pi)^{d}2k^{5}}\,\delta_{\perp}^{ij}(k)k^{m}k^{n}\,\bigg\langle\phi\bigg|p_{1}^{i}p_{1}^{m}p_{1}^{n}\,(H_{0}-E_{0})\,p_{1}^{j}\bigg|\phi\bigg\rangle+(1\leftrightarrow 2)

(83)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{2}\int_{\Lambda}^{\infty}\frac{d^{d}k}{(2\pi)^{d}2k^{3}}\bigg\langle\phi\bigg|\frac{(d-1)}{d(d+2)}\,\big[p_{1}^{i}p_{1}^{2},\big[V,p_{1}^{i}\big]\big]\bigg|\phi\bigg\rangle+(1\leftrightarrow 2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg\langle\phi\bigg|\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\bigg\{\bigg(\frac{62}{225}+\frac{2}{15}\ln\Lambda_{\epsilon}\bigg)\bigg(4\pi Z\alpha\,\delta^{d}(r_{1})\bigg(E_{0}-V-\frac{p_{2}^{2}}{2}\bigg)+\bigg[\frac{(Z\alpha)^{2}}{r_{1}^{4}}\bigg]_{\epsilon}-\bigg[\frac{Z\alpha\,\vec{r}_{1}}{r_{1}^{3}}\bigg]_{\epsilon}\cdot\bigg[\frac{\alpha\,\vec{r}}{r^{3}}\bigg]_{\epsilon}\bigg)+(1\leftrightarrow 2)\bigg\}

\displaystyle+\alpha^{2}\bigg(\frac{124}{225}+\frac{4}{15}\ln\Lambda_{\epsilon}\bigg)\bigg(-\big(\vec{P}_{1}-\vec{P}_{2}\big)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}q^{2}-2\vec{P}_{1}\cdot\vec{P}_{1}-2\frac{\big[(\vec{P}_{1}-\vec{P}_{2})\cdot\vec{q}\big]^{2}}{q^{2}}-4\frac{\big(\vec{P}_{1}\cdot\vec{q}\big)\big(\vec{P}_{2}\cdot\vec{q}\big)}{q^{2}}\bigg)\bigg|\phi\bigg\rangle\,.

Adding together
E_{L3}^{A}
,
E_{L3}^{B}
, and
E_{L3}^{C}
and transforming the sum with help of
Eq. (
69
), we obtain the retardation correction

as

\displaystyle E_{L3}^{\Lambda}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg\langle\phi\bigg|\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\bigg\{\frac{464}{225}\pi Z\alpha\,\delta^{d}(r_{1})\bigg(E_{0}-V-\frac{p_{2}^{2}}{2}\bigg)+\frac{103}{90}\bigg[\frac{(Z\alpha)^{2}}{r_{1}^{4}}\bigg]_{\epsilon}-\frac{103}{45}\bigg[\frac{Z\alpha\,\vec{r}_{1}}{r_{1}^{3}}\bigg]_{\epsilon}\cdot\bigg[\frac{\alpha\,\vec{r}}{r^{3}}\bigg]_{\epsilon}

(84)

\displaystyle+\frac{3}{25}p_{1}^{i}\,4\pi Z\alpha\,\delta^{d}(r_{1})\,p_{1}^{i}+\ln\Lambda_{\epsilon}\bigg(\frac{14}{15}\pi Z\alpha\,\delta^{d}(r_{1})\bigg(E_{0}-V-\frac{p_{2}^{2}}{2}\bigg)

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{3}\bigg[\frac{(Z\alpha)^{2}}{r_{1}^{4}}\bigg]_{\epsilon}-\frac{4}{3}\bigg[\frac{Z\alpha\,\vec{r}_{1}}{r_{1}^{3}}\bigg]_{\epsilon}\cdot\bigg[\frac{\alpha\,\vec{r}}{r^{3}}\bigg]_{\epsilon}+\frac{1}{20}p_{1}^{i}\,4\pi Z\alpha\,\delta^{d}(r_{1})\,p_{1}^{i}\bigg)+(1\leftrightarrow 2)\bigg\}

\displaystyle+\alpha^{2}\bigg\{-\frac{68}{45}\big(\vec{P}_{1}-\vec{P}_{2}\big)^{2}-\frac{62}{225}q^{2}-\frac{136}{45}\vec{P}_{1}\cdot\vec{P}_{2}-\frac{112}{45}\frac{\big(\vec{P}_{1}\cdot\vec{q}\big)\big(\vec{P}_{2}\cdot\vec{q}\big)}{q^{2}}

\displaystyle+\ln\Lambda_{\epsilon}\bigg(-\frac{2}{3}\big(\vec{P}_{1}-\vec{P}_{2}\big)^{2}-\frac{2}{15}q^{2}-\frac{4}{3}\vec{P}_{1}\cdot\vec{P}_{2}-\frac{4}{3}\frac{\big(\vec{P}_{1}\cdot\vec{q}\big)\big(\vec{P}_{2}\cdot\vec{q}\big)}{q^{2}}\bigg)\bigg\}

\displaystyle+\pi\alpha^{3}\bigg(-\frac{103}{45}-\frac{4}{3}\ln\Lambda_{\epsilon}-\frac{8}{3}\ln 2+\frac{8}{3}\ln q\bigg)\,q\,\bigg|\phi\bigg\rangle\,.

Total low-energy result

Adding together

\displaystyle H_{L}^{\Lambda}=

\displaystyle\ +\frac{341}{450}p_{1}^{i}\pi Z\alpha\,\delta^{d}(r_{1})\,p_{1}^{i}+\ln\Lambda_{\epsilon}\bigg(\frac{1}{3}\bigg[\frac{(Z\alpha)^{2}}{r_{1}^{4}}\bigg]_{\epsilon}-\frac{2}{3}\bigg[\frac{Z\alpha\,\vec{r}_{1}}{r_{1}^{3}}\bigg]_{\epsilon}\cdot\bigg[\frac{\alpha\,\vec{r}}{r^{3}}\bigg]_{\epsilon}+\frac{11}{30}p_{1}^{i}\,\pi Z\alpha\,\delta^{d}(r_{1})\,p_{1}^{i}\bigg)+(1\leftrightarrow 2)\bigg\}

VI.2
Middle-energy part

We now turn to the second term in Eq. (
56
), the middle-energy contribution
E_{M}
. This
part originated from the region where both the radiative and the exchanged photons are of the
order
k\propto m\alpha
. We separate
E_{M}
into two parts,
E_{M}=E_{M1}+E_{M2}
, which are
examined as follows.

VI.2.1
Triple seagull contribution

The first middle-energy part is the triple seagull contribution, which is expressed (with
k_{3}

being the radiative photon) as

\displaystyle E_{M1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{6}\int\frac{d^{d}k_{1}}{(2\pi)^{d}\,2k_{1}}\int\frac{d^{d}k_{2}}{(2\pi)^{d}\,2k_{2}}\int\frac{d^{d}k_{3}}{(2\pi)^{d}\,2k_{3}}\,\delta_{\perp}^{ik}(k_{1})\delta_{\perp}^{jk}(k_{2})\delta_{\perp}^{ij}(k_{3})

(86)

\displaystyle\times\bigg<\phi\bigg|e^{{\rm i}(\vec{k}_{1}+\vec{k}_{2})\cdot\vec{r}_{1}}\,\frac{1}{E_{0}-H_{0}-k_{1}-k_{2}}\,e^{{\rm i}(\vec{k}_{3}-\vec{k}_{1})\cdot\vec{r}_{2}}\,\frac{1}{E_{0}-H_{0}-k_{2}-k_{3}}\,e^{-{\rm i}(\vec{k}_{2}+\vec{k}_{3})\cdot\vec{r}_{2}}

\displaystyle+e^{-{\rm i}(\vec{k}_{1}+\vec{k}_{3})\cdot\vec{r}_{2}}\,\frac{1}{E_{0}-H_{0}-k_{1}-k_{3}}\,e^{-{\rm i}(\vec{k}_{2}-\vec{k}_{3})\cdot\vec{r}_{2}}\,\frac{1}{E_{0}-H_{0}-k_{1}-k_{2}}\,e^{{\rm i}(\vec{k}_{1}+\vec{k}_{2})\cdot\vec{r}_{1}}

\displaystyle+e^{-{\rm i}(\vec{k}_{1}+\vec{k}_{3})\cdot\vec{r}_{2}}\,\frac{1}{E_{0}-H_{0}-k_{1}-k_{3}}\,e^{{\rm i}(\vec{k}_{1}+\vec{k}_{2})\cdot\vec{r}_{1}}\,\frac{1}{E_{0}-H_{0}-k_{2}-k_{3}}\,e^{-{\rm i}(\vec{k}_{2}+\vec{k}_{3})\cdot\vec{r}_{2}}\bigg|\phi\bigg>+(1\leftrightarrow 2)\,.

We now have to expand the resolvents for large
k
. To get the contribution of the order

\alpha^{7}m
it is sufficient to take the nonretardation approximation, thus omitting
H_{0}-E_{0}
.
We arrive at

\displaystyle E_{M1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{6}\int\frac{d^{d}k_{1}}{(2\pi)^{d}\,2k_{1}}\int\frac{d^{d}k_{2}}{(2\pi)^{d}\,2k_{2}}\int\frac{d^{d}k_{3}}{(2\pi)^{d}\,2k_{3}}\,\delta_{\perp}^{ik}(k_{1})\delta_{\perp}^{ik}(k_{2})\frac{(d-1)}{d}

(87)

\displaystyle\times\bigg<\phi\bigg|e^{{\rm i}(\vec{k}_{1}+\vec{k}_{2})\cdot\vec{r}}\bigg[\frac{1}{(k_{1}+k_{2})(k_{2}+k_{3})}+\frac{1}{(k_{1}+k_{3})(k_{1}+k_{2})}+\frac{1}{(k_{1}+k_{3})(k_{2}+k_{3})}\bigg]\bigg|\phi\bigg>+(1\leftrightarrow 2)\,.

Similarly to the case of the low-energy contribution, we will express
E_{M1}
as an expectation
value of some effective operator
H_{M1}
. Because it contains purely two-body electron-electron
terms, we express it in momentum representation. We obtain

\displaystyle H_{M1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(4\pi\alpha)^{3}\frac{(d-1)}{8d}\int\frac{d^{d}k}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int\frac{d^{d}k_{3}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\,\frac{\delta_{\perp}^{ik}(k_{1})\delta_{\perp}^{ik}(k_{2})}{k_{1}\,k_{2}\,k_{3}}

(88)

\displaystyle\times\bigg[\frac{1}{\big(k_{1}+k_{2}\big)\big(k_{2}+k_{3}\big)}+\frac{1}{\big(k_{1}+k_{3}\big)\big(k_{1}+k_{2}\big)}+\frac{1}{\big(k_{1}+k_{3}\big)\big(k_{2}+k_{3}\big)}\bigg]+(1\leftrightarrow 2)\,,

with
k_{1}=\big|\vec{k}-\frac{\vec{q}}{2}\big|
and
k_{2}=\big|\vec{k}+\frac{\vec{q}}{2}\big|
. The integration over
radiative photon
k_{3}
is trivial. The remaining integration is performed in spheroidal
coordinates as explained in Appendix
B
. The result for the triple seagull
contribution is

H_{M1}=\alpha^{3}\pi\bigg(-\frac{1}{3}+\frac{4}{3}\ln 2\bigg)\,q.

(89)

VI.2.2
Single seagull with retardation

The second middle-energy contribution comes from the diagram with a single seagull and
retardation. The diagram contains two photons, one of which is a transverse photon exchanged
between the electrons and another one is a radiative photon.
The corresponding contribution is expressed as

\displaystyle E_{M2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{4}\int\frac{d^{d}k_{1}}{(2\pi)^{d}\,2k_{1}}\int\frac{d^{d}k_{2}}{(2\pi)^{d}\,2k_{2}}\delta_{\perp}^{in}(k_{1})\delta_{\perp}^{im}(k_{2})

(90)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\bigg<\phi\bigg|j_{1}^{n}(k_{1})\,e^{{\rm i}\vec{k}_{1}\cdot\vec{r}_{1}}\,\frac{1}{E_{0}-H_{0}-k_{1}}\,e^{-{\rm i}(\vec{k}_{1}+\vec{k}_{2})\cdot\vec{r}_{2}}\,\frac{1}{E_{0}-H_{0}-k_{2}}\,j_{2}^{m}(k_{2})\,e^{{\rm i}\vec{k}_{2}\cdot\vec{r}_{2}}

\displaystyle+j_{2}^{n}(k_{1})\,e^{{\rm i}\vec{k}_{1}\cdot\vec{r}_{2}}\,\frac{1}{E_{0}-H_{0}-k_{1}}\,e^{-{\rm i}(\vec{k}_{1}+\vec{k}_{2})\cdot\vec{r}_{2}}\,\frac{1}{E_{0}-H_{0}-k_{2}}\,j_{1}^{m}(k_{2})\,e^{{\rm i}\vec{k}_{2}\cdot\vec{r}_{1}}

\displaystyle+j_{1}^{n}(k_{1})\,e^{{\rm i}\vec{k}_{1}\cdot\vec{r}_{1}}\,\frac{1}{E_{0}-H_{0}-k_{1}}\,j_{2}^{m}(k_{2})\,e^{{\rm i}\vec{k}_{2}\cdot\vec{r}_{2}}\,\frac{1}{E_{0}-H_{0}-k_{1}-k_{2}}\,e^{-{\rm i}(\vec{k}_{1}+\vec{k}_{2})\cdot\vec{r}_{2}}

\displaystyle+j_{2}^{n}(k_{1})\,e^{{\rm i}\vec{k}_{1}\cdot\vec{r}_{2}}\,\frac{1}{E_{0}-H_{0}-k_{1}}\,j_{1}^{m}(k_{2})\,e^{{\rm i}\vec{k}_{2}\cdot\vec{r}_{1}}\,\frac{1}{E_{0}-H_{0}-k_{1}-k_{2}}\,e^{-{\rm i}(\vec{k}_{1}+\vec{k}_{2})\cdot\vec{r}_{2}}

\displaystyle+e^{-{\rm i}(\vec{k}_{1}+\vec{k}_{2})\cdot\vec{r}_{2}}\,\frac{1}{E_{0}-H_{0}-k_{1}-k_{2}}\,j_{1}^{n}(k_{1})\,e^{{\rm i}\vec{k}_{1}\cdot\vec{r}_{1}}\,\frac{1}{E_{0}-H_{0}-k_{2}}\,j_{2}^{m}(k_{2})\,e^{{\rm i}\vec{k}_{2}\cdot\vec{r}_{2}}

\displaystyle+e^{-{\rm i}(\vec{k}_{1}+\vec{k}_{2})\cdot\vec{r}_{2}}\,\frac{1}{E_{0}-H_{0}-k_{1}-k_{2}}\,j_{2}^{n}(k_{1})\,e^{{\rm i}\vec{k}_{1}\cdot\vec{r}_{2}}\,\frac{1}{E_{0}-H_{0}-k_{2}}\,j_{1}^{m}(k_{2})\,e^{{\rm i}\vec{k}_{2}\cdot\vec{r}_{1}}\bigg|\phi\bigg>+(1\leftrightarrow 2)\,,

where
j_{i}^{l}(k)
is defined as

j_{i}^{l}(k)=p^{l}_{i}+\frac{{\rm i}}{2}\sigma_{i}^{kl}k^{k}.

(91)

The first two terms in the matrix element vanish after performing the retardation expansion and
carrying out the integration over the momentum of the radiative photon as
\int d^{d}k\,k^{\alpha}=0
, which is true by definition in the dimensional regularization. The remainder of the
expression can be cast in the form

\displaystyle E_{M2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2e^{4}\int\frac{d^{d}k_{1}}{(2\pi)^{d}\,2k_{1}}\int\frac{d^{d}k_{2}}{(2\pi)^{d}\,2k_{2}}\frac{1}{k_{1}^{2}(k_{1}+k_{2})}\,\delta_{\perp}^{in}(k_{1})\delta_{\perp}^{im}(k_{2})

(92)

\displaystyle\times\bigg<\phi\bigg|\bigg[\bigg[j_{1}^{n}(k_{1})\,e^{{\rm i}\vec{k}_{1}\cdot\vec{r}_{1}},H_{0}-E_{0}\bigg],j_{2}(k_{2})\,e^{{\rm i}\vec{k}_{2}\cdot\vec{r}_{2}}\bigg]\,e^{-{\rm i}(\vec{k}_{1}+\vec{k}_{2})\cdot\vec{r}_{2}}\bigg|\phi\bigg>+(1\leftrightarrow 2)\,.

The above expression was rearranged so that only photon
k_{2}
is radiative (thus the additional
factor of 2 in the front). Taking into account that only spin-independent terms survive the
double commutator and performing the angular average for the radiative photon, we arrive at

\displaystyle E_{M2}=

\displaystyle\,-\frac{(4\pi\alpha)^{2}}{2}\frac{(d-1)}{d}\int\frac{d^{d}k_{1}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int\frac{d^{d}k_{2}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\frac{1}{k_{1}^{3}k_{2}(k_{1}+k_{2})}

\displaystyle\times\delta_{\perp}^{mn}(k_{1})\langle\phi|\,e^{{\rm i}\vec{k}_{1}\cdot\vec{r}}\,\partial_{1}^{m}\partial_{2}^{n}V|\phi\rangle+(1\leftrightarrow 2)\,.

(93)

This can be again expressed as an expectation value of an effective operator
H_{M2}
, which is
in momentum space

\displaystyle H_{M2}=

\displaystyle\,-(4\pi\alpha)^{3}\frac{(d-1)}{d}\int\frac{d^{d}k_{1}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int\frac{d^{d}k_{2}}{(2\pi)^{d}}

\displaystyle\times\frac{\delta_{\perp}^{mn}(k_{1})q^{m}q^{n}}{k_{1}^{3}\,|\vec{k}_{1}-\vec{q}|^{2}\,k_{2}(k_{1}+k_{2})}\,.

(94)

Performing the remaining integrations, we obtain the result

H_{M2}=\alpha^{3}\pi\bigg(\frac{4}{9}+\frac{2}{3\epsilon}-\frac{8}{3}\ln q\bigg)\,q\,.

(95)

VI.2.3
Total result for the middle-energy contribution

Adding together
H_{M1}
and
H_{M2}
, we obtain the total result for the effective operator
responsible for the middle-energy contribution,

H_{M}=\pi\alpha^{3}\,\bigg(\frac{1}{9}+\frac{2}{3\epsilon}+\frac{4}{3}\ln 2-\frac{8}{3}\ln q\bigg)\,q\,.

(96)

It is not obvious that
E_{M}=\langle H_{M}\rangle
is the complete middle-energy contribution, so we
have verified this by calculating the corresponding scattering amplitude and obtaining agreement
with the above result.

\displaystyle q^{2}+4\,\vec{p}\cdot\vec{p}\,{}^{\prime}\,,\ \ \ \ \ \vec{P}_{1}\cdot\vec{P}_{2}=\frac{1}{4}\Big(P^{2}-q^{2}-4\,\vec{p}\cdot\vec{p}\,{}^{\prime}\Big)\,,

(154)

\displaystyle\frac{(\vec{P}_{1}\cdot\vec{q})(\vec{P}_{2}\cdot\vec{q})}{q^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{1}{4}P^{i}P^{j}-p^{i}p^{\prime j}\Big)\frac{q^{i}q^{j}-\frac{\delta^{ij}}{3}q^{2}}{q^{2}}-\frac{1}{4}q^{2}+\frac{1}{12}P^{2}-\frac{1}{3}\,\vec{p}\cdot\vec{p}\,{}^{\prime}\,.

(155)

Furthermore, we employ the relation
\sigma_{1}\cdot\sigma_{2}=2
valid in
d=3
and take into
account that the operators
P^{2}\,\delta^{3}(r)
and
p^{2}\,\delta^{3}(r)
vanish for triplet states.
Moreover, we perform the replacement
q^{2}\rightarrow-2\,\vec{p}\cdot\vec{p}\,{}^{\prime}
because there
are no
q^{2}\,\ln q
terms.
Performing these transformations
and using formulas for the Fourier transform from Appendix
G
, we bring the
expression for
E_{\textrm{SE}}^{B}
into the coordinate representation (with the overall factor

\alpha^{7}
pulled out),

\displaystyle E_{\textrm{SE}}^{B}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg\langle-\bigg(\frac{2108}{675}+\frac{196}{45}\ln\frac{\alpha^{-2}}{2}\bigg)\vec{p}\,\delta^{3}(r)\,\vec{p}-\frac{1}{60\pi}P^{i}P^{j}\frac{(\delta^{ij}r^{2}-3r^{i}r^{j})}{r^{5}}+\frac{1}{15\pi}p^{i}\frac{(\delta^{ij}r^{2}-3r^{i}r^{j})}{r^{5}}p^{j}

(156)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{3\pi\,r^{4}}\bigg(\frac{203}{15}+6\ln\frac{\alpha^{-2}}{2}-2\ln 2-4\gamma-4\ln r\bigg)\bigg\rangle\,.

The final result for the
\alpha^{7}\,m
one-loop self-energy contribution beyond the
Bethe-logarithmic part is given by the sum of Eqs. (
152
) and (
156
).

VIII
One-loop vacuum polarization

We now turn to the derivation of the
\alpha^{7}\,m
correction induced by the one-loop vacuum
polarization. Its calculation is much simpler than that of the self-energy. It will be convenient
to split the vacuum-polarization correction into the electron-nucleus (
en
) and the
electron-electron (
ee
) parts,

\displaystyle E_{\rm VP}^{(7)}=E_{\rm VP}^{en}+E_{\rm VP}^{ee}\,.

(157)

The electron vacuum polarization modifies the photon propagator as

\frac{g_{\mu\nu}}{q_{0}^{2}-q^{2}}\to\frac{g_{\mu\nu}}{q_{0}^{2}-q^{2}}\,\big[1-\omega\big(q_{0}^{2}-q^{2}\big)\big]\,.

(158)

Here, the function
\omega\big(q^{2}\big)
is the Uehling correction defined as

\displaystyle\omega\big(q^{2}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\,q^{2}\int_{4}^{\infty}d(k^{2})\frac{1}{k^{2}(k^{2}-q^{2})}u(k^{2})\,,

(159)

where

u(k^{2})=\frac{1}{3}\sqrt{1-\frac{4}{k^{2}}}\bigg(1+\frac{2}{k^{2}}\bigg)\,.

(160)

The low-momentum expansion of Eq. (
159
) is

\omega\big(q^{2}\big)=\frac{\alpha}{15\pi}\,q^{2}+\frac{\alpha}{140\pi}\,q^{4}+\cdots\,.

(161)

In the coordinate representation and for the electron-nucleus interaction, these expansion terms
give rise to the following corrections to the Coulomb potential,

\displaystyle\delta V^{(1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{4\,\alpha^{2}}{15}Z\,\delta^{d}(r_{1})+(1\leftrightarrow 2)\,,

(162)

\displaystyle\delta V^{(2)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{35}\vec{\nabla}^{2}Z\,\delta^{d}(r_{1})+(1\leftrightarrow 2)\,.

(163)

VIII.1
Electron-nucleus vacuum polarization

We start with the electron-nucleus part of the vacuum polarization. The corresponding correction
is represented as a sum of four parts,

E_{\rm VP}^{en}=E_{\rm sec}^{en}+E_{L}^{en}+E_{H}^{en}+E_{\textrm{WK}}^{en}\,,

(164)

where
E_{\rm sec}^{en}
is the second-order Uehling correction,
E_{L}^{en}
and
E_{H}^{en}
are
the low-energy and the high-energy Uehling contributions, respectively; and

E_{\textrm{VP}}^{\textrm{WK}}
is the Wichman-Kroll part. The low- and the high-energy
contributions are induced by the exchanged momentum of the order
\alpha\,m
and
m
,
respectively.

The low-energy part
E_{L}^{en}
is induced by an effective operator
H_{L}^{en}
,
E_{L}^{en}=\langle H_{L}^{en}\rangle
, which is evaluated as

\displaystyle H_{L}^{en}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta V^{(2)}+\frac{1}{8}\vec{\nabla}^{2}\,\delta^{(1)}V

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{13\,\alpha^{2}}{210}\vec{\nabla}^{2}Z\,\delta^{d}(r_{1})+(1\leftrightarrow 2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{13\,\alpha^{2}}{105}\bigg[2\bigg(E_{0}-V-\frac{p_{2}^{2}}{2}\bigg)Z\,\delta^{d}(r_{1})-\vec{p}_{1}\,Z\,\delta^{d}(r_{1})\,\vec{p}_{1}\bigg]\,.

The second-order contribution is

\displaystyle E_{\rm sec}^{en}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\,\Big\langle\delta V^{(1)}\,\frac{1}{(E_{0}-H_{0})^{\prime}}\,H^{\prime(4)}\Big\rangle\,.

(166)

Rewriting it as

\displaystyle E_{\rm sec}^{en}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{2\alpha}{15\pi}\,\Big\langle[\vec{P},[V,\vec{P}]]\,\frac{1}{(E_{0}-H_{0})^{\prime}}\,H^{\prime(4)}\Big\rangle\,,

(167)

we obtain the second-order correction we encountered earlier. The result thus is

\displaystyle E_{\rm sec}^{en}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{2\alpha}{15\pi}\,\bigg(\Big\langle H_{R}^{\prime}\frac{1}{(E_{0}-H_{0})^{\prime}}H_{R}\Big\rangle+\Big\langle E^{(4)}\Big(\Big\langle\frac{2\alpha}{r}\Big\rangle-4E_{0}\Big)+\Big\{\Big(2E_{0}-\frac{2\alpha}{r_{2}}+\Big\langle\frac{\alpha}{r}\Big\rangle+\frac{(Z\alpha)^{2}}{2\epsilon}-2(Z\alpha)^{2}\Big)\,\pi Z\alpha\,\delta^{3}(r_{1})

(168)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}\frac{(Z\alpha)^{2}}{r_{1}^{4}}+\bigg(E_{0}+\frac{Z\alpha}{r_{2}}-\frac{\alpha}{r}\bigg)^{2}\,\frac{Z\alpha}{r_{1}}+2\bigg(E_{0}+\frac{Z\alpha}{r_{2}}-\frac{\alpha}{r}\bigg)\,\frac{(Z\alpha)^{2}}{r^{2}_{1}}-\bigg(E_{0}+\frac{Z\alpha}{r_{2}}-\frac{\alpha}{r}-\frac{p_{2}^{2}}{2}\bigg)\frac{(Z\alpha)^{2}}{r_{1}^{2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\vec{p}_{1}\frac{(Z\alpha)^{2}}{r_{1}^{2}}\,\vec{p}_{1}-\frac{1}{2}p_{1}^{2}\,\frac{Z\alpha}{r_{1}}\,p_{2}^{2}+p_{1}^{i}\,\frac{Z\alpha^{2}}{r_{1}\,r}\Big(\delta^{ij}+\frac{r^{i}r^{j}}{r^{2}}\Big)\,p_{2}^{j}+(1\leftrightarrow 2)\Big\}\Big\rangle\bigg)\,.

The high-energy part is expressed as

\displaystyle E_{H}^{en}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(4\pi Z\,\alpha)^{3}\,\phi^{2}(0)\int\frac{d^{d}q_{1}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int\frac{d^{d}q_{2}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\frac{1}{(\vec{q}_{1})^{4}\,(\vec{q}_{2})^{4}\,(\vec{q}_{12})^{2}}\textrm{Tr}\Big[(\not{p}_{1}+1)\gamma_{0}(\not{p}_{2}+1)\frac{(\gamma_{0}+I)}{4}\Big]\big[\omega(-\vec{q}_{1}{}^{2})+\omega(-\vec{q}_{2}{}^{2})+\omega(-\vec{q}{\,}^{2}_{12})\big]\,,

where
\vec{q}_{12}=\vec{q}_{1}-\vec{q}_{2}
and
p_{i}=(1,\vec{q}_{i})
. We evaluate the trace as

\textrm{Tr}\Big[(\not{p}_{1}+1)\gamma_{0}(\not{p}_{2}+1)\frac{(\gamma_{0}+I)}{4}\Big]=4+\vec{q}_{1}\cdot\vec{q}_{2}\,.

(170)

Denoting the part of
E_{H}
induced by the first term in the right-hand side of Eq. (
170
)
as
E_{H1}
, we obtain

\displaystyle E_{H1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\alpha(4\pi Z\,\alpha)^{3}}{\pi}\,4\phi^{2}(0)\int_{4}^{\infty}d(k^{2})\int\frac{d^{d}q_{1}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int\frac{d^{d}q_{2}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\frac{u(k^{2})}{k^{2}}\frac{1}{q_{1}^{4}\,q_{2}^{4}\,\vec{q}{\,}^{2}_{12}}\bigg(\frac{q_{1}^{2}}{k^{2}+q_{1}^{2}}+\frac{q_{2}^{2}}{k^{2}+q_{2}^{2}}+\frac{\vec{q}{\,}^{2}_{12}}{k^{2}+\vec{q}{\,}^{2}_{12}}\bigg)\,.

(171)

We evaluate this integral with help of the following formula,

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{d}k}{(2\,\pi)^{d}}\,\int\frac{d^{d}q}{(2\,\pi)^{d}}\,

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{[k^{2}]^{n_{1}}}\,\frac{1}{[(k-q)^{2}]^{n_{2}}}\,\frac{1}{[q^{2}+m^{2}]^{n_{3}}}=\frac{m^{2\,(d-n_{1}-n_{2}-n_{3})}}{(4\,\pi)^{d}}\,

\displaystyle\times\frac{\Gamma(d/2-n_{1})\,\Gamma(d/2-n_{2})\,\Gamma(n_{1}+n_{2}-d/2)\,\Gamma(n_{1}+n_{2}+n_{3}-d)}{\Gamma(n_{1})\,\Gamma(n_{2})\,\Gamma(n_{3})\,\Gamma(d/2)}\,.

(172)

In the special case of
m=0
, the integral vanishes because of the dimensional
regularization. We obtain

E_{H1}=-\frac{2}{7}\alpha^{4}\Big<Z^{3}\delta^{3}(r_{1})+Z^{3}\delta^{3}(r_{2})\Big>\,.

(173)

Similarly we evaluate the contribution due to the second term in the right-hand side of
Eq. (
170
),

E_{H2}=\alpha^{4}\bigg(-\frac{32}{225}+\frac{1}{15\epsilon}\bigg)\Big<Z^{3}\delta^{3}(r_{1})+Z^{3}\delta^{3}(r_{2})\Big>\,.

(174)

The final result for the one-loop Uehling electron-nucleus vacuum polarization (in atomic units)
is

\displaystyle E^{en}_{\textrm{Ue}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{\rm sec}^{en}+\langle H_{L}^{en}\rangle+E_{H}^{en}=-\frac{2}{15\pi}\,\bigg\{\bigg\langle H_{R}^{\prime}\frac{1}{(E_{0}-H_{0})^{\prime}}H_{R}\bigg\rangle+\bigg\langle E^{(4)}\bigg(\bigg\langle\frac{2}{r}\bigg\rangle-4E_{0}\bigg)

(175)

\displaystyle+\bigg\{\bigg(\frac{1}{7}E_{0}-\frac{1}{7\,r_{2}}-\frac{13Z}{7\,r_{2}}+\frac{13p_{2}^{2}}{14}+\bigg\langle\frac{1}{r}\bigg\rangle+\frac{127}{105}Z^{2}-2Z^{2}\ln\alpha\bigg)\,\pi Z\delta^{3}(r_{1})-\frac{1}{4}\frac{Z^{2}}{r_{1}^{4}}+\bigg(E_{0}+\frac{Z}{r_{2}}-\frac{1}{r}\bigg)^{2}\,\frac{Z}{r_{1}}

\displaystyle+2\bigg(E_{0}+\frac{Z}{r_{2}}-\frac{1}{r}\bigg)\,\frac{Z^{2}}{r^{2}_{1}}+\frac{13}{14}\vec{p}_{1}\,\pi Z\delta^{3}(r_{1})\,\vec{p}_{1}-\bigg(E_{0}+\frac{Z}{r_{2}}-\frac{1}{r}-\frac{p_{2}^{2}}{2}\bigg)\frac{Z^{2}}{r_{1}^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\vec{p}_{1}\,\frac{Z^{2}}{r_{1}^{2}}\,\vec{p}_{1}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}p_{1}^{2}\,\frac{Z}{r_{1}}\,p_{2}^{2}+p_{1}^{i}\,\frac{Z}{r_{1}\,r}\bigg(\delta^{ij}+\frac{r^{i}r^{j}}{r^{2}}\bigg)\,p_{2}^{j}+(1\leftrightarrow 2)\bigg\}\bigg\rangle\bigg\}\,.

The term with
\ln\alpha
comes from rescaling of the operator
(Z\alpha)^{2}/r_{1}^{4}
into atomic units.
The hydrogenic limit of this expression for
nS
-states is (with
r_{1}\equiv r
)

\displaystyle E^{en}_{\textrm{Ue}}({\rm hydr},nS)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{15\pi}\,\bigg\{\bigg\langle H_{R}^{\prime}\frac{1}{(E_{0}-H_{0})^{\prime}}H_{R}\bigg\rangle+\bigg\langle-4E_{0}E^{(4)}-2E_{0}^{3}+\bigg(\frac{1}{7}E_{0}+\frac{127}{105}Z^{2}+Z^{2}\ln\alpha^{-2}\bigg)\,\pi Z\delta^{3}(r)

(176)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}\frac{Z^{2}}{r^{4}}+E_{0}\frac{Z^{2}}{r^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\vec{p}\,\frac{Z^{2}}{r^{2}}\,\vec{p}\bigg\rangle\bigg\}.

Using the expectation values of operators from Appendix
F
, we get

E^{en}_{\textrm{Ue}}({\rm hydr},nS)=Z^{6}\bigg[\frac{19}{35\,n^{5}}-\frac{4}{15\,n^{4}}-\frac{1724}{1575\,n^{3}}+\frac{4}{15\,n^{3}}\bigg(\gamma+\Psi(n)-\ln\frac{n}{2}\bigg)-\frac{2}{15n^{3}}\ln\big[(Z\alpha)^{-2}\big]\bigg]\,,

(177)

in agreement with the known result
eides:01
.

To complete our treatment of the electron-nucleus vacuum polarization, we have to include the
Wichman-Kroll correction, which is purely a Dirac-
\delta
-like type of contribution. It is given
by

E_{\textrm{WK}}^{en}=\bigg(\frac{19}{45}-\frac{\pi^{2}}{27}\bigg)\Big<Z^{3}\,\delta^{3}(r_{1})+Z^{3}\,\delta^{3}(r_{2})\Big>\,.

(178)

VIII.2
Electron-electron vacuum polarization

The electron-electron part of the vacuum polarization is simpler to evaluate because the
corresponding high-energy and second-order contributions vanish for triplet states. The
low-energy part consists of two parts. The first one is due to the
q^{4}
term of the expansion
(
161
). Denoting the corresponding operator as
H_{L1}^{ee}
, we obtain

\displaystyle H_{L1}^{ee}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta V^{(2)}=\frac{\alpha^{2}}{35}\vec{\nabla}_{1}^{2}\delta^{d}(r)+(1\leftrightarrow 2)

(179)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4\alpha^{2}}{35}\vec{p}\,\delta^{d}(r)\,\vec{p}\,.

The remaining part of the electron-electron contribution is induced by the
q_{0}^{2}-q^{2}
term of
the expansion (
161
). For its calculation we again use the scattering amplitude approach in
the Feynman gauge, like in the derivation of
E_{9}
in the one-loop self-energy calculation. In
this case the perturbed vertex is

\Gamma^{\mu}=-\frac{\alpha}{15\,\pi}\big(q_{0}^{2}-q^{2}\big)\,\gamma^{\mu}\equiv\alpha_{\textrm{VP}}\big(q_{0}^{2}-q^{2}\big)\,\gamma^{\mu}.

(180)

Again, we pull out the factor
q_{0}^{2}-q^{2}
and cancel it with the same factor in the denominator
of the photon propagator. The derivation then proceeds in the same way as in the self-energy
calculation, leading to the result

\displaystyle U(\vec{p}_{1},\vec{p}_{2},\vec{q})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha_{\textrm{VP}}e^{2}\bigg[-1+\frac{q^{2}}{4}+\vec{P}_{1}\cdot\vec{P}_{2}+\frac{\sigma_{1}\cdot\sigma_{2}}{4\,d}\,q^{2}\bigg]

(181)

\displaystyle+(1\leftrightarrow 2)\,.

The first term here corresponds to the leading vacuum polarization correction, whereas the
remaining terms are the
\alpha^{7}m
corrections. We now transform them into the coordinate
representation with help of the following transformations,

\displaystyle\vec{P}_{1}\cdot\vec{P}_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\bigg(\vec{P}{}^{2}-\vec{q}{\,}^{2}-4\,\vec{p}\cdot\vec{p}\,{}^{\prime}\bigg)\,,

(182)

\displaystyle\vec{q}{\,}^{2}

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle-2\,\vec{p}\cdot\vec{p}\,{}^{\prime}\,,

(183)

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}\cdot\sigma_{2}

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle 2\,,

(184)

where the second and third equations are valid for triplet states. We then get

H_{L2}^{ee}=-\alpha_{\textrm{VP}}e^{2}\,\frac{8}{3}\vec{p}\,\delta^{3}(r)\,\vec{p}=\bigg(\frac{4\alpha^{2}}{15}\bigg)\,\frac{8}{3}\vec{p}\,\delta^{3}(r)\,\vec{p}\,.

(185)

The total electron-electron part of the
\alpha^{7}\,m
vacuum polarization is the sum of

H_{L1}^{ee}
and
H_{L2}^{ee}
, with the result in atomic units

H_{\textrm{VP}}^{ee}=\frac{52}{63}\,\vec{p}\,\delta^{3}(r)\,\vec{p}\,.

(186)

VIII.3
Total vacuum polarization

Adding together the electron-nucleus and the electron-electron parts, we get the final result for
the one-loop vacuum polarization to the order
\alpha^{7}\,m
in atomic units,

\displaystyle E^{(7)}_{\textrm{VP}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{15\pi}\,\bigg\{\bigg\langle H_{R}^{\prime}\frac{1}{(E_{0}-H_{0})^{\prime}}H_{R}\bigg\rangle+\bigg\langle E^{(4)}\bigg(\bigg\langle\frac{2}{r}\bigg\rangle-4E_{0}\bigg)

(187)

\displaystyle+\bigg\{\bigg(\frac{1}{7}E_{0}-\frac{1}{7\,r_{2}}-\frac{13Z}{7\,r_{2}}+\frac{13p_{2}^{2}}{14}+\Big\langle\frac{1}{r}\Big\rangle+\frac{127}{105}Z^{2}+Z^{2}\ln\alpha^{-2}\bigg)\,\pi Z\delta^{3}(r_{1})-\frac{1}{4}\frac{Z^{2}}{r_{1}^{4}}+\bigg(E_{0}+\frac{Z}{r_{2}}-\frac{1}{r}\bigg)^{2}\,\frac{Z}{r_{1}}

\displaystyle+2\bigg(E_{0}+\frac{Z}{r_{2}}-\frac{1}{r}\bigg)\,\frac{Z^{2}}{r^{2}_{1}}+\frac{13}{14}\vec{p}_{1}\,\pi Z\delta^{3}(r_{1})\,\vec{p}_{1}-\bigg(E_{0}+\frac{Z}{r_{2}}-\frac{1}{r}-\frac{p_{2}^{2}}{2}\bigg)\frac{Z^{2}}{r_{1}^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\vec{p}_{1}\,\frac{Z^{2}}{r_{1}^{2}}\,\vec{p}_{1}-\frac{1}{2}p_{1}^{2}\,\frac{Z}{r_{1}}\,p_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle+p_{1}^{i}\,\frac{Z}{r_{1}\,r}\Big(\delta^{ij}+\frac{r^{i}r^{j}}{r^{2}}\Big)\,p_{2}^{j}+(1\leftrightarrow 2)\bigg\}\bigg\rangle\bigg\}+\bigg\langle\frac{52}{63}\vec{p}\,\delta^{3}(r)\,\vec{p}+\bigg(\frac{19}{45}-\frac{\pi^{2}}{27}\bigg)\big[Z^{3}\,\delta^{3}(r_{1})+Z^{3}\,\delta^{3}(r_{2})\big]\bigg\rangle\,.

with

,
E_{L2}^{(7)}
, and

QED

\xi\in(1,\infty)
.

We now consider the integral of the form

\displaystyle\ \int d^{d}k\,f\biggl(\left|\vec{k}+\frac{\vec{a}}{2}\right|,\left|\vec{k}-\frac{\vec{a}}{2}\right|\biggr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \int d^{d}k\,f(r_{1},r_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \Bigl(\frac{a}{2}\Bigr)^{d}\,\Omega_{d-1}\int d\xi\,d\eta\,\big[(\xi^{2}-1)(1-\eta^{2})\big]^{\frac{d-3}{2}}

In spheroidal coordinates, it is just a two-dimensional integral over
\xi
and
\eta

The particular case of such an integral with integer powers

and

can be transformed to the spheroidal coordinates as

\displaystyle J_{ij}=

\displaystyle\ q^{d-i-j-1}\,2^{i+j-d}\Omega_{d-1}\int_{1}^{\infty}d\xi\,\int_{-1}^{1}d\eta

(205)

\displaystyle\times\,\big[(\xi^{2}-1)(1-\eta^{2})\big]^{\frac{d-3}{2}}\,(\xi-\eta)^{1-j}(\xi+\eta)^{1-i}\,\frac{1}{\xi}\,.

The integrations over
\xi
and
\eta
can now be performed for each particular
i
and

, yielding results in agreement with
those from Appendix C of Ref.
patkos:20
. The advantage of using the spheroidal
coordinates, however, is that this approach can be applied also to non-integer values of
i
and

j

d
, which is needed in the evaluation of
the middle-energy contribution.

Fano manifolds with Lefschetz defect 3
C. Casagrande, E. A. Romano, S. A. Secci

Abstract
. Let X X be a smooth, complex Fano variety, and δ X \delta_{X} its Lefschetz defect. By [ Cas12 ] , if δ X ≥ 4 \delta_{X}\geq 4 , then X ≅ S × T X\cong S\times T , where dim T = dim X − 2 \dim T=\dim X-2 . In this paper we prove a structure theorem for the case where δ X = 3 \delta_{X}=3 . We show that there exists a smooth Fano variety T T with dim T = dim X − 2 \dim T=\dim X-2 such that X X is obtained from T T with two possible explicit constructions; in both cases there is a ℙ 2 \mathbb{P}^{2} -bundle Z Z over T T such that X X is the blow-up of Z Z along three pairwise disjoint smooth, irreducible, codimension 2 subvarieties. Then we apply the structure theorem to Fano 4-folds, to the case where X X has Picard number 5, and to Fano varieties having an elementary divisorial contraction sending a divisor to a curve. In particular we complete the classification of Fano 4-folds with δ X = 3 \delta_{X}=3 , started in [ CR22 ] . This version of the paper incorporates the published article with its corrigendum [ CRS22 ] , where a missing case in Prop. 7.1 is added.

1.
Introduction

Let
X
be a smooth, complex Fano variety. The
Lefschetz defect

\delta_{X}
is an invariant of
X
which depends on the Picard number of prime divisors in
X
.
More precisely, consider the vector space
\mathcal{N}_{1}(X)
of 1-cycles in
X
, with real coefficients, up to numerical equivalence; its dimension is the Picard number
\rho_{X}
of
X
.
Given a prime divisor
D
in
X
, we consider the pushforward
\iota_{*}\colon\mathcal{N}_{1}(D)\to\mathcal{N}_{1}(X)
induced by the inclusion
\iota\colon D\hookrightarrow X
, and set
\mathcal{N}_{1}(D,X):=\iota_{*}(\mathcal{N}_{1}(D))\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{1}(X)
; finally:

\delta_{X}:=\max\bigl\{\operatorname{codim}\mathcal{N}_{1}(D,X)\,|\,D\text{ a prime divisor in }X\bigr\}.

The main property of the Lefschetz defect is the following.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
Cas12
]
, Th. 3.3)
.

Let
X
be a smooth Fano variety with
\delta_{X}\geq 4
. Then
X\cong S\times T
, where
S
is a del Pezzo surface with
\rho_{S}=\delta_{X}+1
.

In this paper we consider Fano varieties
X
with
\delta_{X}=3
. Even if such
X
does not need to be a product, it turns out that it still has a very rigid and explicit structure. More precisely, we show that there exist a smooth Fano variety
T
with
\dim T=\dim X-2
,
\rho_{T}=\rho_{X}-4
, and
\delta_{T}\leq 3
,
and a
\mathbb{P}^{2}
-bundle
Z
over
T
,
such that
X
is obtained by blowing-up
Z
along three pairwise disjoint smooth, irreducible, codimension 2 subvarieties
S_{1},S_{2},S_{3}
. The
\mathbb{P}^{2}
-bundle
\varphi\colon Z\to T
is the projectivization of a suitable decomposable vector bundle on
T
, and
S_{2}
and
S_{3}
are sections of
\varphi
. Instead
\varphi_{|S_{1}}\colon S_{1}\to T
is finite of degree
1
or
2
.

To state our results, we first describe two explicit ways to obtain
X
from
T
; the two constructions differ in the degree of
S_{1}
over
T
. Then we prove (Th.
1.4
) that every Fano variety
X
with Lefschetz defect
3
is obtained by one of these two constructions; this is our main result.

Proposition 1.2

(Construction A)
.

Let
T
be a smooth Fano variety with
\delta_{T}\leq 3
, and
D_{1},D_{2},D_{3}
divisors on
T
such that
-K_{T}+D_{i}-D_{j}
is ample for every
i,j\in\{1,2,3\}
.
Set

Z:=\mathbb{P}_{T}\bigl(\mathcal{O}(D_{1})\oplus\mathcal{O}(D_{2})\oplus\mathcal{O}(D_{3})\bigr)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi}}{{\longrightarrow}}T,

and let

S_{i}\subset Z
be the section of
\varphi
corresponding to the projection onto the summand
\mathcal{O}_{T}(D_{i})
, for
i=1,2,3
.
Finally let
h\colon X\to Z
be the blow-up of
S_{1},S_{2},S_{3}
, and
\sigma:=\varphi\circ h\colon X\to T
.

Then
X
is a smooth Fano variety with
\delta_{X}=3
,
\dim X=\dim T+2
, and
\rho_{X}=\rho_{T}+4
.

Proposition 1.3

(Construction B)
.

Let
T
be a smooth Fano variety with
\delta_{T}\leq 3
, and
N
a divisor on
T
such that
N\not\equiv 0
and both
-K_{T}+N
and
-K_{T}-N
are ample.
Set

Z:=\mathbb{P}_{T}\bigl(\mathcal{O}(N)\oplus\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{O}\bigr)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi}}{{\longrightarrow}}T,

and let

S_{2},S_{3}\subset Z
be the sections of
\varphi
corresponding to the projections onto the summands
\mathcal{O}
. Let
H
be a tautological divisor of
Z
, and
assume that there exists a smooth, codimension
2
subvariety
S_{1}\subset Z
which is a complete intersection of general elements in the linear systems
|{H}|
and
|2{H}|
.
Finally let
h\colon X\to Z
be the blow-up of
S_{1},S_{2},S_{3}
, and
\sigma:=\varphi\circ h\colon X\to T
.

Then
X
is a smooth Fano variety with
\delta_{X}=3
,
\dim X=\dim T+2
, and
\rho_{X}=\rho_{T}+4
.

Let us note that Constructions A and B give new, explicit ways to construct Fano varieties of any dimension.

Theorem 1.4

(Structure theorem)
.

Every smooth Fano variety
X
with

\delta_{X}=3
is obtained with Construction A or B.

When
\dim X=4
and
\rho_{X}=5
, Th.
1.4
is proved in
[
CR22
]
, and used to obtain the classification of Fano
4
-folds with
\delta_{X}=3
and

. To treat the general case, we apply the same strategy as in
[
CR22
]
, adapting the proof step by step. Our starting point is the existence of a fibration in del Pezzo surfaces

[

, Th. 3.3]
. We give an outline of the proof at the end of the Introduction.

In particular, given a Fano variety

of dimension

with
\delta_{X}=3
, by Th.
1.4
there exist a smooth Fano variety
T

with
\dim T=n-2
,
\rho_{T}=\rho_{X}-4
, and
\delta_{T}\leq 3
, and a morphism

-bundle

The case
\rho_{X}=5

The second case that we consider in detail is that of Fano varieties with Lefschetz defect
3
and Picard number
5
, the minimal one, in Section
8
. After Th.
1.4
there are a smooth Fano variety
T
with
\rho_{T}=1
and a morphism
\sigma\colon X\to T
that realizes

X
as a blow-up of a
\mathbb{P}^{2}
-bundle over
T
as in Construction A or B.
We show that in this case
T
and
\sigma
are uniquely determined, and we list explicitly the different
X
that are obtained from a given
T
. To state the result,
let us fix some notation for the case
\rho_{T}=1
:
\mathcal{O}_{T}(1)
is the ample generator
of
\operatorname{Pic}(T)\cong\mathbb{Z}
, and
\mathcal{O}_{T}(a):=\mathcal{O}_{T}(1)^{\otimes a}
for every
a\in\mathbb{Z}
. Moreover
\mathcal{O}_{T}(-K_{T})\cong\mathcal{O}_{T}(i_{T})
, where

is the index of

.

Proposition 1.6
.

Let
X
be a smooth Fano variety with
\delta_{X}=3
and
\rho_{X}=5
. Then
T
and the morphism
\sigma\colon X\to T
as in Construction A or B are uniquely determined; we have
X=X_{A}
if
\sigma
is smooth, and
X=X_{B}
if
\sigma
is not smooth.

Let
X=X_{A}
. Then there are uniquely determined integers

with

b\leq 0,\quad|b|\leq\frac{i_{T}-1}{2},\quad\text{and}\quad|b|\leq a\leq i_{T}-1-|b|,

such that
X
is obtained with Construction A from
T
with

Z=\mathbb{P}_{T}(\mathcal{O}(a)\oplus\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{O}(b))
.

Let
X=X_{B}
. Then there is a
uniquely determined integer
a
with

1\leq a\leq i_{T}-1

such that
X
is obtained with Construction B from
T
with

Z=\mathbb{P}_{T}(\mathcal{O}(a)\oplus\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{O})
.

Fano varieties containing a divisor with Picard number 2

We note that the assumptions
\rho_{X}=5
and
\delta_{X}=3
imply that

contains a prime divisor

with
\dim\mathcal{N}_{1}(D,X)=2
; in fact it is easy to see that all the varieties as in Prop.

; an analogous result for the case of a prime divisor with

.

Corollary 1.7
.

Let
X
be a smooth Fano variety containing a prime divisor
D
with
\rho_{D}=2
, or more generally with
\dim\mathcal{N}_{1}(D,X)=2
. Then either

where

is a del Pezzo surface and
\rho_{T}=1
, or

.

Elementary divisorial contractions

Corollary
1.7
is also related to the study of
Fano varieties having an elementary divisorial contraction

\tau\colon X\to X^{\prime}
where
\tau(\operatorname{Exc}(\tau))
is a curve,
because then

. It follows from Th.

that

, and Tsukioka

]
has classified the case

We generalize this classification to an arbitrary elementary divisorial contraction

such that

, as follows.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
X
be a smooth Fano variety of dimension
n\geq 4
with
\rho_{X}=5
. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

there is an elementary divisorial contraction
\tau\colon X\to X^{\prime}
such that
\tau(\operatorname{Exc}(\tau))
is a curve;

(ii)

X
is obtained with Construction A or B from a smooth Fano variety
T
such that
\dim T=n-2
,
\rho_{T}=1
,
i_{T}>1
, with
Z=\mathbb{P}_{T}(\mathcal{O}(a)\oplus\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{O})
and

.

If these conditions hold we have
\operatorname{Exc}(\tau)\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}\times T
,
\mathcal{N}_{\operatorname{Exc}(\tau)/X}\cong\pi_{\mathbb{P}_{1}}^{*}\mathcal{O}(-1)\otimes\pi_{T}^{*}\mathcal{O}(-a)
, and
\tau(\operatorname{Exc}(\tau))\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

When
\dim X=4
, Cor.
1.7
and Th.
1.8
are already proved in
[
CR22
]
.

Strategy of the proof of Theorem
1.4

Our starting point is the existence, from
[
Cas12
, Th. 3.3]
, of a flat fibration
\sigma\colon X\to T
, where
T
is a smooth Fano variety with
\dim T=\dim X-2
and
\rho_{T}=\rho_{X}-4
; moreover
\sigma
factors as
X\to Y\to T
, where the first map is a conic bundle, and the second one is smooth with fiber
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. We collect the properties of
\sigma
in Th.
5.1
.

We show that the fibration
\sigma\colon X\to T
has a different factorization as
X\to Z\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi}}{{\to}}T
, where
\varphi
is a
\mathbb{P}^{2}
-bundle, and
X\to Z
is the blow-up of three pairwise disjoint smooth, irreducible subvarieties
S_{i}\subset Z
of codimension
2
, horizontal for
\varphi
.

Then we prove that
S_{2}
and
S_{3}
are always sections of
\varphi
.
When
S_{1}
is a section as well, we show that
\sigma\colon X\to T
is as in Construction A.

Otherwise, we study the degree
d>1
of
S_{1}
over
T
. Fiberwise, for
t\in T
general, we have

Z_{t}:=\varphi^{-1}(t)\cong\mathbb{P}^{2}
,
X_{t}:=\sigma^{-1}(t)
a smooth del Pezzo surface, and
X_{t}\to Z_{t}
the blow-up of the
d+2
points
(S_{1}\cup S_{2}\cup S_{3})\cap Z_{t}
.
This is the hardest part of the proof; we restrict to a general curve
C\subset T
, and construct in
\overline{Z}:=\varphi^{-1}(C)
a divisor
D
which is a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle over
C
and contains
S_{1}\cap\overline{Z}
. Therefore,
for
t\in C
general,
the
d
points
S_{1}\cap Z_{t}
are aligned; since
X_{t}
is del Pezzo, this implies that
d=2
. Finally we show that
\sigma\colon X\to T
is as in Construction B.

2.
Notation and preliminaries

We work over the field of complex numbers. Let
X
be a smooth projective variety of arbitrary dimension.

\mathcal{N}_{1}(X)
(respectively,
\mathcal{N}^{1}(X)
) is the real vector space of one-cycles (respectively, Cartier divisors) with real coefficients, modulo numerical equivalence, and

\dim\mathcal{N}_{1}(X)=\dim\mathcal{N}^{1}(X)=\rho_{X}
is the Picard number of
X
.

Let
C
be a one-cycle of
X
, and
D
a divisor of
X
. We denote by
[C]
(respectively,
[D]
) the numerical equivalence class in
\mathcal{N}_{1}(X)
(respectively,
\mathcal{N}^{1}(X)
). We also denote by
C^{\perp}\subset\mathcal{N}^{1}(X)
(respectively
D^{\perp}\subset\mathcal{N}_{1}(X)
) the orthogonal hyperplanes.

The symbol
\equiv
stands for numerical equivalence (for both one-cycles and divisors), and
\sim
stands for linear equivalence of divisors.

\operatorname{NE}(X)\subset\mathcal{N}_{1}(X)
is the convex cone generated by classes of effective curves. An
extremal ray

R
is a
one-dimensional face of
\operatorname{NE}(X)
. When
X
is Fano, the
length
of
R
is
\ell(R)=\min\{-K_{X}\cdot C\,|\,C\text{ a rational curve in }X\}
.

A facet of a convex polyhedral cone
\mathcal{C}
is a face of codimension one; moreover
\mathcal{C}
is simplicial when it can be generated by linearly independent elements. We denote by
\langle S\rangle
the convex cone generated by a subset
S\subset\mathcal{N}_{1}(X)
.

A
contraction
of
X
is a surjective morphism
\varphi\colon X\to Y
with connected fibers, where
Y
is normal and projective.
The
relative cone

\text{NE}(\varphi)
of
\varphi
is the convex subcone of
\text{NE}(X)
generated by classes of curves contracted by
\varphi
. A contraction is
elementary
if
\rho_{X}-\rho_{Y}=1
.

A
conic bundle

X\to Y
is a contraction of fiber type where every fiber is one-dimensional and
-K_{X}
is relatively ample.

We gather here some preliminary results that we need in the sequel.

Remark 2.1
.

Let
T
be a smooth projective variety and
D_{1},\dotsc,D_{r}
divisors on
T
. Set
Z:=\mathbb{P}_{T}(\mathcal{O}(D_{1})\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathcal{O}(D_{r}))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi}}{{\to}}T
and
let
H
be a tautological divisor.
For every
i=1,\dotsc,r
set

F_{i}:=\mathbb{P}_{T}\bigl(\oplus_{j\neq i}\mathcal{O}(D_{j})\bigr)\hookrightarrow Z

with the embedding given by the projection
\oplus_{j}\mathcal{O}_{T}(D_{j})\twoheadrightarrow\oplus_{j\neq i}\mathcal{O}_{T}(D_{j})
. Then:

F_{i}\sim H-\varphi^{*}(D_{i})\ \text{ for every }i=1,\dotsc,r,\quad\text{and }\ K_{Z}-\varphi^{*}K_{T}\sim-F_{1}-\cdots-F_{r}.

Lemma 2.2
.

Notation as in Rem.
2.1

with
r=3
. Let

S_{3}\subset Z
be the section of
\varphi
corresponding to the projection

\mathcal{O}_{T}(D_{1})\oplus\mathcal{O}_{T}(D_{2})\oplus\mathcal{O}_{T}(D_{3})\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{T}(D_{3})
, and let
h\colon X\to Z
be the blow-up of
S_{3}
, with exceptional divisor
E_{3}\subset X
.

Then
E_{3}\cong\mathbb{P}_{T}(\mathcal{O}(-K_{T}+D_{3}-D_{1})\oplus\mathcal{O}(-K_{T}+D_{3}-D_{2}))
and

(-K_{X})_{|E_{3}}

is the tautological line bundle.

Proof.

We have

\mathcal{N}_{S_{3}/Z}^{\vee}\cong\mathcal{O}_{T}(D_{1}-D_{3})\oplus\mathcal{O}_{T}(D_{2}-D_{3})
,
E_{3}\cong\mathbb{P}_{T}(\mathcal{N}_{S_{3}/Z}^{\vee})
, and
\mathcal{O}_{X}(-E_{3})_{|E_{3}}
is the tautological line bundle.

Let
E_{i}^{\prime}\subset X
be the transform of
F_{i}\subset Z
, for
i=1,2,3
; note that

S_{3}=F_{1}\cap F_{2}
and
S_{3}\cap F_{3}=\emptyset
. Set
\sigma:=\varphi\circ h\colon X\to T
and
\sigma_{3}:=\sigma_{|E_{3}}\colon E_{3}\to T
.
By Rem.
2.1
we have
-K_{Z}\sim\varphi^{*}(-K_{T})+F_{1}+F_{2}+F_{3}
, so that

\displaystyle-K_{X}\sim h^{*}(-K_{Z})-E_{3}\sim\sigma^{*}(-K_{T})+E_{1}^{\prime}+E_{2}^{\prime}+E_{3}^{\prime}+E_{3},

\displaystyle\text{ and }\ \mathcal{O}_{X}(-K_{X})_{|E_{3}}\cong\mathcal{O}_{E_{3}}\bigl(\sigma_{3}^{*}(-K_{T})+G_{1}+G_{2}\bigr)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(E_{3})_{|E_{3}}

where
G_{i}:=E_{i}^{\prime}\cap E_{3}
for
i=1,2
. Note that
G_{1}
and
G_{2}

are the sections of
\sigma_{3}

corresponding to the two summands of
\mathcal{O}_{T}(D_{1}-D_{3})\oplus\mathcal{O}_{T}(D_{2}-D_{3})
,
so by Rem.
2.1
we have
\mathcal{O}_{E_{3}}(G_{1}+G_{2})\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}(-2E_{3}+\sigma^{*}(2D_{3}-D_{1}-D_{2}))_{|E_{3}}
. Finally we get

\mathcal{O}_{X}(-K_{X})_{|E_{3}}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}(-E_{3})_{|E_{3}}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{E_{3}}\bigl(\sigma_{3}^{*}(-K_{T}+2D_{3}-D_{1}-D_{2})\bigr),

which yields the statement.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety. Then
\mathcal{N}_{1}(X)
is generated, as a vector space, by classes of complete intersections of very ample divisors.

Proof.

Let
n
be the dimension of
X

and
H
a very ample divisor. By the hard Lefschetz theorem, the linear map

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{1}(X)\longrightarrow\mathcal{N}_{1}(X)

\displaystyle[D]\mapsto[D\cdot H^{n-2}]

is an isomorphism. Since the ample cone has maximal dimension in
\mathcal{N}^{1}(X)
, we can choose very ample divisors
H_{1},\dotsc,H_{\rho_{X}}
on
X
such that their classes generate
\mathcal{N}^{1}(X)
; then their images
[H_{1}\cdot H^{n-2}],\dotsc,[H_{\rho_{X}}\cdot H^{n-2}]
generate
\mathcal{N}_{1}(X)
.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
Y
be a smooth Fano variety and
\xi\colon Y\to T
a smooth morphism with fiber
\mathbb{P}^{1}
.
Let
A_{1},A_{2}\subset Y
be disjoint prime divisors such that
\xi
is finite on
A_{i}
for
i=1,2
.
Then at least one among
A_{1},A_{2}
is a section of
\xi
.

Proof.

We first show the following claim:

If
[A_{1}]
and
[A_{2}]
are multiples in
\mathcal{N}^{1}(Y)

Lemma 6.8

be a smooth Fano variety.
Suppose that there exists

is contained in

.
Suppose first that

.
By Lemma
3.1
for every

∎

By Th

X

T

\rho_{T}=1

\mathcal{O}_{T}(1)

Proof of Prop.
1.6
.

We show the uniqueness of
\sigma\colon X\to T
.
Let us assume that there exists another
\bar{\sigma}\colon X\to\overline{T}
as in Construction A or B. We show that
\sigma
and
\bar{\sigma}
coincide up to an isomorphism
T\cong\overline{T}
.

Let
R
be an extremal ray of the cone
\operatorname{NE}(\sigma)
.
By Remarks
6.2
,
6.5
, and
6.7
, the contraction associated to
R
is the blow-up of a smooth subvariety
S
which is an irreducible codimension
2
complete intersection, and
either
S\cong T
or
S\cong S_{1}
. Let
E\subset X
be the exceptional divisor;
since the normal bundle of
S
is decomposable, we have
E\cong\mathbb{P}_{S}(\mathcal{O}\oplus L)
with
L\in\operatorname{Pic}(S)
.

We first assume that
n\geq 5
, where
n:=\dim X
.
Recall that
S_{1}
is a ramified double cover of
T
, and
\rho_{T}=1
, so that the ramification divisor is ample;
since
\dim S_{1}=n-2>2
,
[
Cor81
]
yields
\rho_{S_{1}}=1
. Therefore
in any case we have
\rho_{S}=1
and
\rho_{E}=2
.

Since
\rho_{E}=2
,
E
has at most two elementary contractions, one being the
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle
E\to S
.
If
L\cong\mathcal{O}_{S}
, then
E\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}\times S
, and the second elementary contraction is
E\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Otherwise we can assume that
L
is ample; in this case
E
has an elementary divisorial contraction sending a divisor to a point (see for instance
[
CD15
, p. 10768]
).

Consider now the restriction
\bar{\sigma}_{|E}\colon E\to\bar{\sigma}(E)\subseteq\overline{T}
. The Stein factorization gives

E\xrightarrow{\psi}B\xrightarrow{\nu}\bar{\sigma}(E),

where
\psi
is a contraction of
E
, and
\nu
is finite.
Thus
\dim B=\dim\bar{\sigma}(E)\leq\dim\overline{T}=n-2
; on the other hand every fiber of
\bar{\sigma}
is a surface, thus
\dim B\geq\dim E-2=n-3\geq 2
.
By the previous observation on the possible contractions of
E
, we deduce that
B\cong S
and
\psi
is the
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle
E\to S
,
so that
\bar{\sigma}
contracts the fibers of
E\to S
and
R\subset\operatorname{NE}(\bar{\sigma})
.

This holds for every extremal ray
R
of
\operatorname{NE}(\sigma)
, so that

\operatorname{NE}(\sigma)\subseteq\operatorname{NE}(\bar{\sigma})
. Since these two cones are both 4-dimensional faces of
\operatorname{NE}(X)
, we conclude that they are the same, and by
[
Deb01
, Prop. 1.14]

\sigma
and
\bar{\sigma}
coincide up to an isomorphism
T\cong\overline{T}
.

Suppose now that
n=4
, so that
T\cong\overline{T}\cong\mathbb{P}^{2}
.
We repeat the same argument as above with an extremal ray
R
such that
E
is a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle over
T
. We still have
\dim B\geq\dim E-2=1
, and if
\dim B=1
, then
E\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}
and
\psi
the projection onto
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Thus the general fiber of
\bar{\sigma}
over
\bar{\sigma}(E)
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, which is impossible by the description of the fibers in §
6.1
. We conclude that
\dim B\geq 2
, and as before that
R\subset\operatorname{NE}(\bar{\sigma})
. If
X=X_{A}
we get
\operatorname{NE}(\sigma)\subseteq\operatorname{NE}(\bar{\sigma})
and hence
\operatorname{NE}(\sigma)=\operatorname{NE}(\bar{\sigma})
. If
X=X_{B}
, we see from Rem.
6.5
and Fig.
6.2
that the extremal rays
R
of
\operatorname{NE}(\sigma)
such that
E
is a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle over
T
generate a
4
-dimensional cone. We conclude that
\dim(\operatorname{NE}(\sigma)\cap\operatorname{NE}(\bar{\sigma}))=4=\dim\operatorname{NE}(\sigma)=\dim\operatorname{NE}(\bar{\sigma})
, so that again the two cones coincide.

Finally suppose that
n=3
, so that
T\cong\overline{T}\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}
, and repeat the same argument as above.
We conclude that either
R\subset\operatorname{NE}(\bar{\sigma})
, or
E
has two different
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle structures, hence
E\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
with normal bundle
\mathcal{O}(-1,-1)
. It is not difficult to see that there is at most one extremal ray
R
with this property, so we conclude that
\operatorname{NE}(\sigma)=\operatorname{NE}(\bar{\sigma})
as in the case
n=4
.

Let
X=X_{A}
. By Rem.
6.2
and Lemma
6.3
,
\sigma\colon X\to T
has exactly two different factorizations as in Construction A, through

\mathbb{P}_{T}(\mathcal{O}(D_{1})\oplus\mathcal{O}(D_{2})\oplus\mathcal{O}(D_{3}))
and
\mathbb{P}_{T}(\mathcal{O}(-D_{1})\oplus\mathcal{O}(-D_{2})\oplus\mathcal{O}(-D_{3}))
. Moreover
\mathcal{O}_{T}(D_{1}),\mathcal{O}_{T}(D_{2}),\mathcal{O}_{T}(D_{3})
are determined up to
reordering and tensoring with a line bundle.

We may assume that
\mathcal{O}_{T}(D_{1})\cong\mathcal{O}_{T}(a)
,
\mathcal{O}_{T}(D_{2})\cong\mathcal{O}_{T}
, and
\mathcal{O}_{T}(D_{3})\cong\mathcal{O}_{T}(b)
, with
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
. By Lemma
3.1
, we see that
(a,b)
must satisfy the following conditions:

(8.1)

|a|,\ |b|,\ |a-b|\leq i_{T}-1.

We say that two pairs of integers
(a,b)
and
(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})
are equivalent (denoted by
\sim
) if they satisfy (
8.1
) and give isomorphic
X_{A}
’s.
By the previous discussion,
we see that all the pairs equivalent to
(a,b)
can be obtained by the following relations:

(a,b)\sim(b,a),\ (-b,a-b),\ (-a,-b).

Hence up to equivalence we can subsequently assume:

\bullet

a\geq 0
: indeed if
a<0
, we replace
(a,b)
with
(-a,-b)
;

\bullet

a\geq b
: if
a<b
, we replace
(a,b)
with
(b,a)
;

\bullet

b\leq 0
: if
b>0
, we replace
(a,b)
with
(a-b,-b)
;

\bullet

a\geq-b
: if
a<-b
, we replace
(a,b)
with
(-b,-a)
,

so that in the end:

b\leq 0
and
a\geq|b|
.
These conditions, together with (
8.1
), are equivalent to the conditions in the statement:

(8.2)

b\leq 0,\quad|b|\leq\frac{i_{T}-1}{2},\quad\text{and}\quad|b|\leq a\leq i_{T}-1-|b|.

Finally
it is not difficult to see that if
(a,b),(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})
satisfy (
8.2
) and

(a,b)\sim(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})
, then
a=a^{\prime}
and
b=b^{\prime}
.

Let
X=X_{B}
.
By Rem.
6.5
, Prop.
6.6
, and Rem.
6.7
,
\sigma\colon X\to T
has exactly three different factorizations as in Construction B, all through

Z=\mathbb{P}_{T}(\mathcal{O}(N)\oplus\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{O})
, where
N\not\equiv 0
and
2N
is effective. Therefore
\mathcal{O}_{T}(N)
is ample and isomorphic to
\mathcal{O}_{T}(a)
for some integer
a\geq 1
. By Lemma
4.4
we see that

must satisfy
a\leq i_{T}-1
.
∎

Proof of Cor.
1.7
.

We have
\delta_{X}\geq\operatorname{codim}\mathcal{N}_{1}(D,X)=\rho_{X}-2
. If
\rho_{X}\geq 6
, then
\delta_{X}\geq 4
, so that by Th.
1.1
we have

. If

, therefore

\pi_{T}\colon S\times T\to T

, and we conclude that

Suppose that

, and note that the assumptions imply that

. If

, then

X

.
By

CD15
, Lemma 3.1]
this would imply that

, a contradiction. Therefore

\delta_{X}=\rho_{X}-2=3
, and

is as in Prop.

.
∎

Proof of Th.

(ii)\Rightarrow(i)\

In
Z=\mathbb{P}_{T}(\mathcal{O}(a)\oplus\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{O})

S_{2},S_{3}
be the sections of
Z\to T
corresponding to the projections onto the trivial summands, and

in the notation of Construction B), so that

S_{2}

S_{3}

If

We show that

Set

If
2a>i_{T}

.

If instead

, consider the exceptional divisor

over

, and
e_{2}\subset E_{2}
a fiber of the
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle

. We have:

H^{\prime}\cdot e_{1}^{\prime}=1
,
H^{\prime}\cdot\Gamma=0
,
H^{\prime}\cdot e_{2}=2a-1>0

for every extremal ray of
\operatorname{NE}(X)

is nef and
(H^{\prime})^{\perp}\cap\operatorname{NE}(X)=\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}[\Gamma]
.

If

[

Consider the pushforward
\tau_{*}\colon\mathcal{N}_{1}(X)\to\mathcal{N}_{1}(X^{\prime})

\tau

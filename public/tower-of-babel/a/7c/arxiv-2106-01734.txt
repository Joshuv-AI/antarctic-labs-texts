Abstract
. We identify a notion of reducibility between predicates, called instance reducibility , which commonly appears in reverse constructive mathematics. The notion can be generally used to compare and classify various principles studied in reverse constructive mathematics (formal Church’s thesis, Brouwer’s Continuity principle and Fan theorem, Excluded middle, Limited principle, Function choice, Markov’s principle, etc.). We show that the instance degrees form a frame, i.e., a complete lattice in which finite infima distribute over set-indexed suprema. They turn out to be equivalent to the frame of upper sets of truth values, ordered by the reverse Smyth partial order. We study the overall structure of the lattice: the subobject classifier embeds into the lattice in two different ways, one monotone and the other antimonotone, and the ¬ ¬ \lnot\lnot -dense degrees coincide with those that are reducible to the degree of Excluded middle. We give an explicit formulation of instance degrees in a relative realizability topos, and call these extended Weihrauch degrees , because in Kleene-Vesley realizability the ¬ ¬ \lnot\lnot -dense modest instance degrees correspond precisely to Weihrauch degrees. The extended degrees improve the structure of Weihrauch degrees by equipping them with computable infima and suprema, an implication, the ability to control access to parameters and computation of results, and by generally widening the scope of Weihrauch reducibility.

1.
Introduction

A common way of proving an implication between two universally quantified statements,

(\forall y\in B\,.\,\psi(y))\implies(\forall x\in A\,.\,\phi(x)),

is to show that for every
x\in A
there exists
y\in B
such that
\psi(y)
implies
\phi(x)
.
The technique is prevalent in constructive reverse mathematics where we compare undecided universally quantified statements. For example, the total ordering of reals implies the Limited principle of omniscience (LPO),

(\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R}\,.\,x\leq y\lor y<x)\implies\forall\alpha\in{\mathsf{2}^{\mathbb{N}}}\,.\,(\forall n\,.\,\alpha_{n}=0)\lor(\exists n\,.\,\alpha_{n}=1),

(1)

by such a reduction: given
\alpha\in{\mathsf{2}^{\mathbb{N}}}
, construct the real

x=\lim_{n\to\infty}2^{-{\min}\{k\in\mathbb{N}\mid\alpha_{k}=1\lor k=n\}}

and note that
x\leq 0\lor 0<x
implies
(\forall n\,.\,\alpha_{n}=0)\lor(\exists n\,.\,\alpha_{n}=1)
.
One can find many other examples of the method, which therefore deserves to be named.
Let us write
\phi\subseteq A
to indicate that
\phi
is a predicate on
A
.

{defi}

A predicate
\phi\subseteq A
is
instance reducible
to a
predicate
\psi\subseteq B
, written
(\phi,A)\leq_{\mathsf{I}}(\psi,B)
or just

\phi\leq_{\mathsf{I}}\psi
, when

\forall x\in A\,.\,\exists y\in B\,.\,\psi(y)\Rightarrow\phi(x).

(2)

We say that
\phi
and
\psi
are
instance
equivalent
, written
(\phi,A)\equiv_{\mathsf{I}}(\psi,B)
or just
\phi\equiv_{\mathsf{I}}\psi
,
when
\phi\leq_{\mathsf{I}}\psi
and
\psi\leq_{\mathsf{I}}\phi
. The equivalence class of a
predicate
\phi
with respect to
\equiv_{\mathsf{I}}
is its
instance degree
.

It is clear that
\leq_{\mathsf{I}}
is a preorder (reflexive and transitive) in
which harder problems are higher up. There is no shortage of instance degrees, just consider the principles that are commonly studied form a constructive point of view: Excluded Middle and its various special cases, such as the Limited Principle of Omniscience (LPO), Brouwer’s Fan and Continuity Principles, Markov Principle, Church’s Thesis, etc. The relationships between these are well-known
[
Ish06
]
, and shall not be rehashed here.

Overview

In the first part of the paper (
section 2
), which presupposes basic familiarity with intuitionistic logic, we study instance reducibilities, show that they form a frame, and enjoy a rich structure.
The second part (
section 3
) is written classically and relies on the first part only superficially. It starts with a brief overview of relative realizability models, and continues with a calculation of an explicit description of instance reducibility in such models. We relate instance reducibility to Weihrauch reducibility and show that the former is a proper extension of the latter. We study several examples (
section 4
) that demonstrate how the extended Weihrauch reducibility increases the scope of the subject.

2.
Instance reducibility

In this section we work in intuitionistic logic without countable choice.
To be formally precise, the text may be interpreted in the internal language of an elementary topos
[
JL86
,
LM92
]
, although we shall not rely on the topos-theoretic machinery, nor do we expect the reader to be familiar with it.

2.1.
Instance degrees and the Smyth preorder

The instance degrees form a large preorder whose carrier is the proper class of all predicates on all sets. Let us show that the preorder is essentially small, i.e., it is equivalent to a small one.

Recall that a preorder
(L,{\leq_{L}})
is a reflexive transitive relation. Its symmetrization
\equiv_{L}
is the equivalence relation defined by
x\equiv_{L}y\iff x\leq_{L}y\land y\leq_{L}x
. The quotient
L/{\equiv_{L}}
is partially ordered by the relation induced by
\leq_{L}
.
Preorders
(L,{\leq_{L}})
and
(K,{\leq_{K}})
are equivalent when they are equivalent as categories, i.e., there are monotone maps
f:L\to K
and
g:K\to L
such that
f(g(y))\equiv_{K}y
for all
y\in K
and
g(f(x))\equiv_{L}x
for all
x\in L
. If
L
and
K
are equivalent then
L/{\equiv_{L}}
and
K/{\equiv_{K}}
are isomorphic.

In a partially ordered set
(L,{\leq})
, the
upper closure
of
S\subseteq L
is the set

{\uparrow}S=\{y\in L\mid\exists x\in S\,.\,x\leq y\}.

The (reverse)
Smyth preorder

[
Smy78
]
on the power set
\mathcal{P}(L)
is defined by

S\leq_{\mathsf{S}}T\iff{\uparrow}S\subseteq{\uparrow}T,

or equivalently
\forall x\in S\,.\,\exists y\in T\,.\,y\leq x
.
(Beware, the Smyth preorder is normally defined in the opposite way, but we prefer to turn it upside down to match harder instances being higher up.)

We write
\Omega
for the set of all truth values. It may be identified with the powerset
\mathcal{P}(\{\star\})
of a singleton, with
\emptyset
representing falsehood and
\{\star\}
representing truth. Unless specified otherwise, a power set
\mathcal{P}(L)
is always partially ordered by
\subseteq
.

Proposition 1
.

The preorder of instance reducibilities is equivalent to the Smyth preorder on
\mathcal{P}(\Omega)
.

Proof 2.1
.

A predicate
\phi\subseteq A
corresponds to its image

\mathrm{im}(\phi)=\{p\in\Omega\mid\exists x\in A\,.\,p=\phi(x)\}\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega).

Conversely,
S\subseteq\Omega
corresponds to the predicate
\phi_{S}\subseteq S
classified by the inclusion,
\phi_{S}(p)=p
.
One checks easily that these correspondences are monotone with respect to instance reducibility and the Smyth preorder, and that they constitute an equivalence.

The collection
\mathcal{U}(L)=\{S\subseteq L\mid{\uparrow}S=S\}
of the
upper sets
ordered by
\subseteq
is a frame in which infima and suprema are computed as intersections and unions, respectively. Upper closure
{\uparrow}:\mathcal{P}(L)\to\mathcal{U}(L)
is the poset reflection of the Smyth preorder.
The partial order of instance degrees is therefore isomorphic to the frame
\mathcal{U}(\Omega)
, and in a sense that is all that needs to be said.
Nevertheless, let us spell out the ordered-theoretic structure of instance degrees directly in terms of predicates, and study it a bit more closely.

2.2.
Transfer of predicates along maps

It often happens that a reduction
(\phi,A)\leq_{\mathsf{I}}(\psi,B)
is accomplished by means
of a map
f:A\to B
such that
\psi(f(x))\Rightarrow\phi(x)
for all
x\in A
, in which
case we say that
f

witnesses
the reduction
\phi\leq_{\mathsf{I}}\psi
. Not all reductions
need be witnessed by maps, for in general there may be no choice map
for (
2
).

A predicate
\psi\subseteq B
may be transferred along a map
f:A\to B
to a
predicate
f^{*}{\psi}\subseteq A
, defined by

(f^{*}{\psi})(x)\iff\psi(f(x)).

In the other direction, a predicate
\phi\subseteq A
may be transferred to one on
B

in two ways, using either the existential or the universal quantifier, to give predicates

\forall_{f}{\phi}\subseteq B
and
\exists_{f}{\phi}\subseteq B
, defined by

\displaystyle(\forall_{f}{\phi})(y)

\displaystyle\iff\forall x\in A\,.\,f(x)=y\Rightarrow\phi(x)

\displaystyle(\exists_{f}{\phi})(y)

\displaystyle\iff\exists x\in A\,.\,f(x)=y\land\phi(x).

Lemma 2
.

Let
f:A\to B
be a map,
\phi\subseteq A
and
\psi\subseteq B
. Then

f^{*}{\psi}\leq_{\mathsf{I}}\psi
and
\phi\leq_{\mathsf{I}}\forall_{f}{\phi}
. If
f
is surjective then also
\psi\leq_{\mathsf{I}}f^{*}{\psi}
and
\exists_{f}{\phi}\leq_{\mathsf{I}}\phi
.

Proof 2.2
.

The first two reductions are witnessed by
f
. If
f
is surjective then we obtain
the remaining two reductions by reducing
y\in B
to
x\in A
such that
f(x)=y
.

When the map
f
in the previous lemma is a projection
\pi_{1}:A\times B\to B
and
A

is inhabited, we obtain for any
\phi\subseteq A\times B

(\exists_{\pi_{1}}{\phi})\leq_{\mathsf{I}}\phi\leq_{\mathsf{I}}(\forall_{\pi_{1}}{\phi}),

which is more memorable with the abuse notation

(\exists x\in A\,.\,\phi(x,y))\;\leq_{\mathsf{I}}\;\phi(x,y)\;\leq_{\mathsf{I}}\;(\forall x\in A\,.\,\phi(x,y)).

2.3.
The order-theoretic structure

By
1
the instance degrees form a frame, whose structure we describe explicitly in this section.

Proposition 3
.

The instance degrees form a bounded distributive lattice.

Proof 2.3
.

The empty predicate
\emptyset\subseteq\emptyset
on the empty set is instance
reducible to every other predicate and so it represents the smallest instance degree
{\bot\!\!\!\bot}
.

The largest instance degree
{\top\!\!\!\top}
is represented by the empty predicate

\emptyset\subseteq\mathsf{1}
on the singleton
\mathsf{1}=\{\star\}
, or more generally
by any predicate
\phi\subseteq A
with a
counter-example
, which is
a\in A
such that
\lnot\phi(a)
.

The supremum of
\phi\subseteq A
and
\psi\subseteq B
is the predicate

\phi\sqcup\psi
, defined as follows. Let
A+B
be the disjoint union with canonical
inclusions
\iota_{1}:A\to A+B
and
\iota_{2}:B\to A+B
. Define
\phi\sqcup\psi

on
A+B
by

\displaystyle(\phi\sqcup\psi)(\iota_{1}(x))\iff\phi(x)\qquad\text{and}\qquad(\phi\sqcup\psi)(\iota_{2}(y))\iff\psi(y).

The reductions of
\phi
and
\psi
to
\phi\sqcup\psi
are witnessed by the canonical
inclusions.

The infimum of
\phi\subseteq A
and
\psi\subseteq B
is the predicate

\phi\sqcap\psi\subseteq A\times B
, defined by

(\phi\sqcap\psi)(x,y)\iff\phi(x)\lor\psi(y).

The canonical projections witness reductions of
\phi\sqcap\psi
to
\phi
and
\psi
.

The distributivity laws are easily checked. We only indicate how to prove

(\phi\sqcup\psi)\sqcap\theta\leq_{\mathsf{I}}(\phi\sqcap\theta)\sqcup(\psi\sqcap\theta),

for all
\phi\subseteq A
,
\psi\subseteq B
, and
\theta\subseteq C
. The
reduction is witnessed by the canonical isomorphism

(A+B)\times C\to(A\times C)+(B\times C)
so long as, for any
x\in A
,

y\in B
, and
z\in C
,

(\phi(x)\lor\theta(z))\land(\psi(x)\lor\theta(z))\qquad\text{implies}\qquad(\phi(x)\land\psi(y))\lor\theta(z),

which is just distributivity of disjunction over conjunction.

Proposition 4
.

The instance degrees form a Heyting algebra.

Proof 2.4
.

We only have to define implication.
Given
\phi\subseteq A
and
\psi\subseteq B
, define the set

(A\sqsupset B)=\{p\in\Omega\mid\forall x\in A\,.\,\exists y\in B\,.\,\psi(y)\Rightarrow\phi(x)\lor p\},

and the predicate
\phi\sqsupset\psi
on
A\sqsupset B
by
(\phi\sqsupset\psi)(p)=p
.
We need to show that, for all
\theta\subseteq C
,

\theta\sqcap\phi\leq_{\mathsf{I}}\psi\quad\text{if and only if}\quad\theta\leq_{\mathsf{I}}\phi\sqsupset\psi.

To see that
\theta\sqcap\phi\leq_{\mathsf{I}}\psi
implies
\theta\leq_{\mathsf{I}}\phi\sqsupset\psi
, it suffices to check that for any
z\in C
we have
\theta(z)\in A\sqsupset B
, which follows directly from the definition of
\theta\sqcap\phi
and
\theta\sqcap\phi\leq_{\mathsf{I}}\psi
.

Conversely, suppose
\theta\leq_{\mathsf{I}}\phi\sqsupset\psi
.
Consider any
z\in C
and
x\in A
.
There is
p\in A\sqsupset B
such that
p\Rightarrow\theta(z)
.
By definition of
A\sqsupset B
there is
y\in B
such that
\psi(y)\Rightarrow\phi(x)\lor p
, and hence
\psi(y)\Rightarrow\theta(z)\lor\phi(x)
, as required.

The preceding construction of implication is quite obviously just a dressed up version of implication in
\mathcal{U}(\Omega)
. It would be desirable to have a more direct description.

Proposition 5
.

For every set
I
, the instance degrees have
I
-indexed suprema.

Proof 2.5
.

Let
\phi_{i}\subseteq A_{i}
be a family of predicates indexed by
i\in I
. We claim
that the supremum of the family is the predicate
{\textstyle\bigsqcup_{i\in I}}\phi_{i}
on the
coproduct
\coprod_{i\in I}A_{i}
, defined by

({\textstyle\bigsqcup_{i\in I}}\phi_{i})(u)\iff\exists i\in I\,.\,\exists x\in A_{i}\,.\,u=\iota_{i}(x)\land\phi_{i}(x),

where
\iota_{i}:A_{i}\to\coprod_{i\in I}A_{i}
is the
i
-th canonical inclusion.

For any
j\in I
, the reduction
\phi_{j}\leq_{\mathsf{I}}{\textstyle\bigsqcup_{i\in I}}\phi_{i}
is witnessed
by
\iota_{j}
, so
{\textstyle\bigsqcup_{i}}\phi_{i}
is an upper bound of all the
\phi_{i}
’s.
To prove it is the least upper bound, consider any common upper bound

\theta\subseteq C
of the predicates
\phi_{i}
. Given any
u\in\coprod_{i}A_{i}

there are (unique)
j\in I
and
x\in A_{j}
such that
u=\iota_{j}(x)
. Because

\phi_{j}\leq_{\mathsf{I}}\theta
there is
y\in C
such that
\theta(y)\Rightarrow\phi_{j}(x)
,
therefore
\theta(y)\Rightarrow({\textstyle\bigsqcup_{i}}\phi_{i})(u)
and hence

{\textstyle\bigsqcup_{i}}\phi_{i}\leq_{\mathsf{I}}\theta
, as required.

Proposition 6
.

For every set
I
, the instance degrees have
I
-indexed infima.

Proof 2.6
.

Let
\phi_{i}\subseteq A_{i}
be a family of predicates indexed by
i\in I
. For the
underlying set of the infimum of the family we take the product

\prod_{i\in I}\mathcal{P}_{+}(A_{i})
,
where
\mathcal{P}_{+}(X)
is the set of all inhabited subsets of
X
. We define the
predicate
{\textstyle\bigsqcap_{i\in I}}\phi_{i}
by

({\textstyle\bigsqcap_{i\in I}}\phi_{i})(f)\iff\exists i\in I\,.\,\exists x\in f(i)\,.\,\phi_{i}(x).

(3)

For any
j\in I
, the reduction
{\textstyle\bigsqcap_{i\in I}}\phi_{i}\leq_{\mathsf{I}}\phi_{j}
is proved as
follows. Given any
f\in\prod_{i\in I}\mathcal{P}_{+}(A_{i})
, there exists
x\in f(j)

because
f(j)
is inhabited. If
\phi_{j}(x)
then
({\textstyle\bigsqcap_{i\in I}}\phi_{i})(f)

by (
3
).

To prove that
{\textstyle\bigsqcap_{i\in I}}\phi_{i}
is the greatest lower bound, consider any
common lower bound
\psi\subseteq B
of the predicates
\phi_{i}
. The desired
reduction
\psi\leq_{\mathsf{I}}{\textstyle\bigsqcap_{i\in I}}\phi_{i}
is witnessed by the map

r:B\to\prod_{i\in I}\mathcal{P}_{+}(A_{i})
, defined by

r(z)(i)=\{x\in A_{i}\mid\phi_{i}(x)\Rightarrow\psi(z)\}.

For every
i\in I
,
r(z)(i)
is inhabited because
\psi\leq_{\mathsf{I}}\phi_{i}
and so there is

x\in A_{i}
such that
\phi_{i}(x)
implies
\psi(z)
. The map
r
witnesses the reduction
because, for any
z\in B
, if
({\textstyle\bigsqcap_{i\in I}}\phi_{i})(r(z))
then there exists

i\in I
and
x\in r(z)(i)
such that
\phi_{i}(x)
, but then
\psi(z)
follows because

\phi_{i}(x)\Rightarrow\psi(z)
by the definition of
r
.

2.4.
Instance reducibilities and projective sets

Recall that
I
is a
projective
set when every
I
-indexed family
{(A_{i})}_{i\in I}
of inhabited sets has a choice function, i.e., the product
\prod_{i\in I}A_{i}
is
inhabited. Equivalently, a set
I
is projective if every total relation on
I
has a
choice function:

(\forall i\in I\,.\,\exists x\in A\,.\,\phi(i,x))\Rightarrow\exists f:A^{I}\,.\,\forall i\in I\,.\,\phi(i,f(i)).

In
section 3
we shall interpret instance reducibility in
realizability toposes, which validate Aczel’s
presentation
axiom

[
Acz78
]
.

“Every set is the image of a projective set.”

It will be useful to know a couple of consequence of Aczel’s axiom.

Proposition 7
.

If the presentation axiom holds then every predicate is instance equivalent to a
predicate on a projective set.

Proof 2.7
.

If
\phi\subseteq A
and
e:B\twoheadrightarrow A
is a cover of
A
by a projective set
B
,
then
\phi\equiv_{\mathsf{I}}e^{*}{\phi}
by
2
.

When an infimum is indexed by a projective set, a simpler formula than the one given in

6
can be used. Given a family of predicates
\phi_{i}\subseteq A_{i}

indexed by
i\in I
with
I
projective, define the predicate

{\textstyle\bigsqcap^{\prime}_{i\in I}}\phi_{i}
on the product
\prod_{i\in I}A_{i}
by

({{\textstyle\bigsqcap^{\prime}_{i\in I}}\phi_{i}})(f)\iff\exists i\in I\,.\,\phi_{i}(f(i)).

We claim that
{\textstyle\bigsqcap^{\prime}_{i\in I}}\phi_{i}\equiv_{\mathsf{I}}{\textstyle\bigsqcap_{i\in I}}\phi_{i}
. The
reduction
{\textstyle\bigsqcap^{\prime}_{i\in I}}\phi_{i}\leq_{\mathsf{I}}{\textstyle\bigsqcap_{i\in I}}\phi_{i}
is witnessed by
the map which takes
f\in\prod_{i\in I}A_{i}
to the map
i\mapsto\{f(i)\}
. For the
opposite reduction, consider any
g\in\prod_{i\in I}\mathcal{P}_{+}(A_{i})
. Because
I
is
projective there is a choice map
c\in\prod_{i\in I}A_{i}
such that
c(i)\in g(i)
for
all
i\in I
. If there is
j\in I
such that
\phi_{j}(c(j))
then

({\textstyle\bigsqcap_{i\in I}}A_{i})(g)
holds because
j
and
c(j)
witness (
3
)
for
g
.

2.5.
Reduction to many instances

Occasionally a single instance of
\phi
reduces to
many
instances of
\psi
. Such variants are captured by the following operation.

{defi}

For any set
I
and a predicate
\phi\subseteq A
define the

I
-parameterization of
\phi
to be the predicate
\phi^{(I)}\subseteq A^{I}

defined on the set
A^{I}
of all functions from
I
to
A
by

\phi^{(I)}(f)\iff\forall i\in I\,.\,\phi(f(i)).

Proposition 8
.

The following hold for all
\phi\subseteq A
and
\psi\subseteq B
:

(1)

If
I
is inhabited then
\phi\leq_{\mathsf{I}}\phi^{(I)}
.

(2)

If
I
is a retract of
J
then
\phi^{(I)}\leq\phi^{(J)}
.

(3)

If
I
is projective then
\phi\leq_{\mathsf{I}}\psi
implies
\phi^{(I)}\leq_{\mathsf{I}}\psi^{(I)}
.

Proof 2.8
.

The first statement is witnessed by the map taking
x\in A
to the constant map
i\mapsto x
.
The second statement is witnessed by the map taking
f\in A^{I}
to
f\circ r\in A^{J}
,
where
r:J\to I
is a retraction, so that it has a right inverse
s:I\to J
.
To verify the third statement, consider any
f\in A^{I}
. Since
I
is projective and
\phi\leq_{\mathsf{I}}\psi
is assumed, there exists
g:I\to B
such that
\psi(g(i))\Rightarrow\phi(f(i))
for all
i\in I
, hence
\psi^{(I)}(g)\Rightarrow\phi^{(I)}(f)
.

A reduction of
\phi\subseteq A
to
\psi\subseteq B
which uses a
fixed

number
n
of instances of
\psi
for every instance of
\phi
is just instance
reducibility of
\phi
to
\psi^{([n])}
where
[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}
because

\phi\leq_{\mathsf{I}}\psi^{([n])}
unfolds to

\forall x\in A\,.\,\exists y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}\in B\,.\,\psi(y_{1})\land\cdots\land\psi(y_{n})\Rightarrow\phi(x).

This is not to be confused with the reduction of
\phi(x)
to a
variable
finite
number
n
of instances
\psi(y_{1}),\ldots,\psi(y_{n})
, which is expressed as

\phi\leq_{\mathsf{I}}{\textstyle\bigsqcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}}\psi^{([n])},

as it unfolds to

\forall x\in A\,.\,\exists n\in\mathbb{N}\,.\,\exists y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}\in B\,.\,\psi(y_{1})\land\cdots\land\psi(y_{n})\Rightarrow\phi(x).

A third possibility is
\phi\leq_{\mathsf{I}}\psi^{(\mathbb{N})}
which reduces an instance
\phi(x)
to
countably many instances
\psi(y_{0}),\psi(y_{1}),\psi(y_{2}),\ldots

In the Smyth preorder on
\mathcal{P}(\Omega)
the
I
-parameterization is easily seen to amount to passing to
I
-indexed intersections: the
I
-parameterization of
S\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega)
is

S^{(I)}=\{p\in\Omega\mid\exists f\in A^{I}\,.\,p\Leftrightarrow\forall i\in I\,.\,f(i)\}.

2.6.
Embedding of truth values

Given any set
A
let
\top_{\!A}
be the always true predicate on
A
, and let

\mathsf{1}=\{\star\}
be the singleton set containing the element
\star
. For each truth
value
p\in\Omega
define its
extent

\|p\|=\{x\in\mathsf{1}\mid p\}
.

Proposition 9
.

The principal ideal of instance degrees below
\top_{\!\mathsf{1}}
is equivalent to the
poset
\Omega
of truth values, ordered by implication
\Rightarrow
.

Proof 2.9
.

First observe that, for a predicate
\phi\subseteq A
, the reduction

\phi\leq_{\mathsf{I}}\top_{\!\mathsf{1}}
is equivalent to
\forall x\in A\,.\,\phi(x)
, therefore the
predicates below
\top_{\!\mathsf{1}}
are precisely those of the form
\top_{\!A}
.

The desired equivalence of posets is established by the maps

p\mapsto\top_{\!\|p\|}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\top_{\!A}\mapsto(\exists x\in A\,.\,\top)

of truth values to instance degrees, and vice versa. Indeed, the maps are monotone with
respect to
\Rightarrow
and
\leq_{\mathsf{I}}
, and it is easy to check that

p\Leftrightarrow(\exists x\in\|p\|\,.\,\top)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\top_{\!\|\exists x\in A\,.\,\top\|}\equiv_{\mathsf{I}}\top_{\!A}.

There is another embedding of truth values into instance degrees, which is an anti-monotone
semilattice homomorphism.

Proposition 10
.

The map which takes a truth value
p\in\Omega
to the predicate

{p}^{\star}\subseteq\mathsf{1}
, defined by
{p}^{\star}(x)=p
, is an

\displaystyle{\bot}^{\star}

\displaystyle\;\equiv_{\mathsf{I}}\;{\top\!\!\!\top},

\displaystyle{(p\lor q)}^{\star}

\displaystyle{(\exists x\in A\,.\,\phi(x))}^{\star}

Proof 2.10

The map is is an anti-monotone embedding because

We can ask what it would take to also have

\displaystyle{(p\land q)}^{\star}

\displaystyle{(\forall x\in A\,.\,\phi(x))}^{\star}

Proposition 11

For a predicate

\exists x\in A\,.\,(\phi(x)\Rightarrow\forall y\in A\,.\,\phi(y)).

(4)

.

The reduction

3.
Instance reducibility in realizability models

We next work out the interpretation of instance degrees in realizability models. For this purpose we give a brief overview of the relevant concepts and refer to
[
vO08
]
for background material. Henceforth we work in classical mathematics.

3.1.
An overview of realizability models

A
partial combinatory algebra (pca)
is a set
\mathbb{A}
equipped with a partial binary operation
{\cdot}
such that there exist the
\mathtt{k},\mathtt{s}\in\mathbb{A}
satisfying, for all
a,b,c\in\mathbb{A}
,

\mathtt{k}{\cdot}a{\downarrow},\qquad\mathtt{k}{\cdot}a{\cdot}b=a,\qquad\mathtt{s}{\cdot}a{\cdot}b{\downarrow},\quad\text{and}\quad\mathtt{s}{\cdot}a{\cdot}b{\cdot}c=(a{\cdot}c){\cdot}(b{\cdot}c).

Application associates to the left, i.e.,
a{\cdot}b{\cdot}c=(a{\cdot}b){\cdot}c
.
Above we wrote
e{\downarrow}
to indicate that the term
e
is defined, and therefore so are all the subterms.
If we use a particular term
e
in a statement, we tacitly assume that it is defined.
Partial combinatory algebras are so called because of their combinatorial completeness: given any term
e
formed using partial application, elements of
\mathbb{A}
and a variable
x
, we can construct
a\in\mathbb{A}
such that, for all
b\in\mathbb{A}
, if
e[b/x]{\downarrow}
then
a{\cdot}b{\downarrow}
and
a{\cdot}b=e[b/x]
, and similarly for several variables. The element
a
so constructed is denoted
[x]e
, for example
[x]x
is the identity combinator which satisfies
([x]x){\cdot}b=b
.
Every pca has a
pairing
and
projections

\mathtt{pair},\mathtt{fst},\mathtt{snd}\in\mathbb{A}
, which satisfy

\mathtt{fst}{\cdot}(\mathtt{pair}{\cdot}a{\cdot}b)=a\quad\text{and}\quad\mathtt{snd}{\cdot}(\mathtt{pair}{\cdot}a{\cdot}b)=b.

We also write
\langle a,b\rangle
for
\mathtt{pair}\,a\,b
. A non-trivial pca has an embedding
\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{A}
which assigns to every number
n
its
numeral

\overline{n}
in such a way that all partial computable maps are representable in
\mathbb{A}
.
Some well-known pcas are:

(1)

Kleene’s first pca: the natural numbers
\mathbb{N}
with application
\{m\}n
which applies the
m
-th partial computable function to
n
;

(2)

Kleene’s second pca: the Baire space
\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}
with application
\alpha\,{\mid}\,\beta
, as described in
[
vO08
, §1.4.3]
;

(3)

the untyped
\lambda
-calculus is a pca whose elements are the closed terms, with
\mathtt{k}=\lambda x\,y\,.\,x
and
\mathtt{s}=\lambda x\,y\,z\,.\,(x\,z)\,(y\,z)
.

An
elementary sub-pca
is a subset
\mathbb{A}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{A}
which is closed under application and contains some
\mathtt{k}
and
\mathtt{s}
suitable for
\mathbb{A}
. For example, the computable sequences
(\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}})_{\mathrm{eff}}\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}
form an elementary sub-pca of Kleene’s second pca
[
vO08
, §2.6.9]
.

From any pca
\mathbb{A}
with an elementary sub-pca
\mathbb{A}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{A}
, the
relative realizability topos

\mathsf{RT}(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{A}^{\prime})
can be built
[
vO08
, §2.6.9]
. Of special significance is the the topos
\mathsf{RT}(\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}},(\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}})_{\mathrm{eff}})
which embodies
Kleene-Vesley realizability

[
KV65
]
as well as
Type 2 computability

[
Wei00
]
.
Luckily we need not review the construction of relative realizability toposes because the simpler categories of assemblies suffice for our purposes, as they often do.

Suppose
\mathbb{A}
is a pca and
\mathbb{A}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{A}
an elementary sub-pca.
An
assembly

S=(|S|,{\Vdash_{S}})
is a set
|S|
with a
realizability relation

{\Vdash_{S}}\subseteq\mathbb{A}\times|S|
, such that
\forall x\in|S|\,.\,\exists r\in\mathbb{A}\,.\,r\Vdash_{S}x
. When
r\Vdash_{S}x
holds we say that
r
is a
realizer
of
x
.
The realizability relation
\Vdash_{S}
may be transposed in two ways to give equivalent formulations of assemblies.
First, the assembly
S
may be seen a set
|S|
equipped with an
existence predicate

\mathsf{E}_{S}:|S|\to\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{A})
, defined by
\mathsf{E}_{S}(x)=\{r\in\mathbb{A}\mid r\Vdash_{S}x\}
. Second, the assembly may be seen as a
multi-valued representation
, which is a partial map
\delta_{S}:\mathbb{A}\rightharpoonup\mathcal{P}(|S|)
defined by
\delta_{S}(r)=\{x\in|S|\mid r\Vdash_{S}x\}
on the subset
\{r\in\mathbb{A}\mid\exists x\in|S|\,.\,r\Vdash_{S}x\}
. Thus
\mathsf{E}_{S}(x)
is always inhabited, and so is
\delta_{S}(r)
whenever it is defined.

An assembly
S
is
modest
when no two elements share a realizer, i.e., if
r\Vdash_{S}x
and
r\Vdash_{S}y
then
x=y
. Equivalently,
S
is modest when the representation
\delta_{S}
is single-valued. An assembly is
partitioned
when every element has a single realizer, i.e., if
r\Vdash_{S}x
and
s\Vdash_{S}x
then
r=s
. Equivalently,
S
is partitioned when
\mathsf{E}_{S}(x)
is a singleton for all
x\in|S|
.

An
assembly map

f:S\to T
is a map between underlying sets
f:|S|\to|T|
for which there exists
r\in\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
such that if
s\Vdash_{S}x
then
r{\cdot}s{\downarrow}
and
r{\cdot}s\Vdash_{T}f(x)
. We say that
r

tracks
or
realizes

f
.

Identity maps are tracked, and so are compositions of tracked maps. Therefore
assemblies and assembly maps form a category
\mathsf{Asm}(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{A}^{\prime})
, which turns out to be a full subcategory of the realizability topos
\mathsf{RT}(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{A}^{\prime})
.
It is a large category because there is an embedding
\nabla:\mathsf{Set}\to\mathsf{Asm}(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{A}^{\prime})
which maps a set
X
to the assembly
\nabla X=(X,{\Vdash_{\nabla X}})
whose underlying set is
|\nabla X|=X
and the realizability relation is trivial:
r\Vdash_{\nabla X}x
holds for all
r\in\mathbb{A}
.

{exa}

The assembly
N=(\mathbb{N},{\Vdash_{N}})
of natural numbers has numerals as realizers,
i.e., each
n\in\mathbb{N}
is realized by the corresponding numeral
\overline{n}
.
This can be contrasted with the assembly
\nabla\mathbb{N}
in which every realizer realizes every number. Consequently, while the identity
{\rm{id}}:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
is realized as an assembly map
N\to\nabla\mathbb{N}
, for instance by
\mathtt{k}
, every assembly map
f:\nabla\mathbb{N}\to N
in the opposite direction is constant.

{exa}

The category of assemblies is cartesian closed.
The product of
S
and
T
is the assembly
S\times T
given by

\displaystyle|S\times T|=|S|\times|T|

\displaystyle r\Vdash_{S\times T}(x,y)\iff\text{$\mathtt{fst}{\cdot}r\Vdash_{S}x$ and $\mathtt{snd}{\cdot}r\Vdash_{T}y$}.

Projections and pairing are realized respectively by
\mathtt{fst}
,
\mathtt{snd}
, and
\mathtt{pair}
.
The exponential assembly
T^{S}
by

\displaystyle|T^{S}|=\{f:|S|\to|T|\mid\exists r\in\mathbb{A}\,.\,\forall x\in|S|\,.\,\forall s\in\mathbb{A}\,.\,s\Vdash_{S}x\Rightarrow r{\cdot}s\Vdash_{T}f(x)\}

\displaystyle r\Vdash_{T^{S}}f\iff\forall x\in|S|\,.\,\forall s\in\mathbb{A}\,.\,s\Vdash_{S}x\Rightarrow r{\cdot}s\Vdash_{T}f(x),

where it should be noted that it contains maps realized by the elements of
\mathbb{A}
, not
\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
. Evaluation and currying are realized respectively by
[a](\mathtt{fst}{\cdot}a){\cdot}(\mathtt{snd}{\cdot}a)
and
[a][b][c]a{\cdot}\langle b,c\rangle
.

3.2.
The realizability logic

In order to unravel the realizability interpretation of instance reducibilities, we first need an explicit description of realizability logic. Throughout we work with a pca
\mathbb{A}
and an elementary sub-pca
\mathbb{A}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{A}
.

The realizability logic is embodied by
realizability predicates
. A realizability predicate on an assembly
S
is a map
\phi:|S|\to\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{A})
. The set
\mathrm{Pred}(S)=\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{A})^{|S|}
of all such predicates forms a Heyting prealgebra with the preorder relation
\leq_{S}
defined by

\phi\leq_{S}\psi\iff\begin{aligned} &\text{there is $r\in\mathbb{A}^{\prime}$ such that for all $x\in|S|$ and $s\in\mathbb{A}$,}\\
&\text{if $s\Vdash_{S}x$ and $t\in\phi(x)$ then $r{\cdot}s{\cdot}t\in\psi(x)$}.\end{aligned}

If we think of the elements of
\phi(x)
as computational witnesses, then
\phi\leq_{S}\psi
holds when witnesses of
\phi(x)
, together with witnesses of
x
, may be mapped to witnesses of
\psi(x)
using a realizer from
\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
. It is customary to write
r\Vdash\phi(x)
instead of
r\in\phi(x)
, and read it as “
r
realizes
\phi(x)
”.

The Heyting prealgebra structure on
\mathrm{Pred}(S)
is as follows, where
\phi,\psi\in\mathrm{Pred}(S)
,
x\in|S|
, and
r\in\mathbb{A}
:

(1)

r\Vdash\bot(x)
never,

(2)

r\Vdash\top(x)
always,

(3)

r\Vdash(\phi\land\psi)(x)
when
\mathtt{fst}{\cdot}r\Vdash\phi(x)
and
\mathtt{snd}{\cdot}r\Vdash\psi(x)
,

(4)

r\Vdash(\phi\lor\psi)(x)
when either
\mathtt{fst}{\cdot}r=\overline{0}
and
\mathtt{snd}{\cdot}r\Vdash\phi(x)
, or
\mathtt{fst}{\cdot}r=\overline{1}
and
\mathtt{snd}{\cdot}r\Vdash\psi(x)
,

(5)

r\Vdash(\phi\Rightarrow\psi)(x)
when for all
p\in\mathbb{A}
, if
p\Vdash\phi(x)
then
r{\cdot}p\Vdash\psi(x)
.

These clauses say, for example, that
\bot(x)=\emptyset
,
\top(x)=\mathbb{A}
, and that implication is given by

(\phi\Rightarrow\psi)(x)=\{r\in\mathbb{A}\mid\forall p\in\mathbb{A}\,.\,p\in\phi(x)\Rightarrow r{\cdot}p{\downarrow}\land r{\cdot}p\in\psi(x)\}.

Taking into account that
\lnot\phi
is an abbreviation for
\phi\Rightarrow\bot
, we may calculate that
\lnot\phi
and
\lnot\lnot\phi
are characterized by

(6)

r\Vdash(\lnot\phi)(x)
when there is no
s\in\mathbb{A}
such that
s\Vdash\phi(x)
, and

(7)

r\Vdash(\lnot\lnot\phi)(x)
when there is
s\in\mathbb{A}
such that
s\Vdash\phi(x)
.

(The connoisseurs can note that the representation of predicates given here is equivalent to the strict extensional predicates
[
vO08
, §2.2]
because extensionality is automatic for assemblies and we ensure strictness by passing to
r
a realizer
s\Vdash_{S}x
.)

Given a two-place realizability predicate
\rho\in\mathrm{Pred}(S\times T)
, we define realizability predicates
\forall_{S}\rho\in\mathrm{Pred}(T)
and
\exists_{S}\rho\in\mathrm{Pred}(T)
:

(8)

r\Vdash(\forall_{S}\rho)(y)
when for all
s,t,x
, if
s\Vdash_{S}x
and
t\Vdash_{T}y
then

r{\cdot}s{\cdot}t\Vdash\rho(x,y)
,

(9)

r\Vdash(\exists_{S}\rho)(y)
when for all
t
, if
t\Vdash_{T}y
then there is
x\in|S|
such that
\mathtt{fst}{\cdot}(r{\cdot}t)\Vdash_{S}x
and
\mathtt{snd}{\cdot}(r{\cdot}t)\Vdash\rho(x,y)
.

We prefer to write
\forall x{:}S\,.\,\rho(x,y)
and
\exists x{:}S\,.\,\rho(x,y)
instead of
(\forall_{S}\rho)(y)
and
(\exists_{S}\rho)(y)
.
Quantification of an
(n+1)
-place predicate
\phi\in\mathrm{Pred}(S\times T_{1}\times\cdots T_{n})
is carried out as a quantification of an equivalent two-place predicate on
S\times T
where
T=T_{1}\times\cdots\times T_{n}
. As a special case
n=0
we get quantification of a unary predicate
\phi\in\mathrm{Pred}(S)
:

(10)

r\Vdash\forall x{:}S\,.\,\phi(x)
when for all
s,x
, if
s\Vdash_{S}x
then
r{\cdot}s\Vdash\phi(x)
,

(11)

r\Vdash\exists x{:}S\,.\,\phi(x)
when there is
x\in|S|
such that
\mathtt{fst}{\cdot}r\Vdash_{S}x
and
\mathtt{snd}{\cdot}r\Vdash\phi(x)
.

Finally, equality on
S
is the realizability relation
{=_{S}}:|S|\times|S|\to\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{A})
characterized by

(12)

r\Vdash x=_{S}y
when
x=y
.

The Heyting prealgebra structure, together with quantifiers and equality given above, comprises the realizability interpretation of first-order logic with equality, which is intuitionistically sound in the sense that an intuitionistically provable statement has a realizer in
\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
.

3.3.
Instance degrees in assemblies

Every instance degree in a realizability topos already appears as an instance degree on an assembly, thanks to
7
and the following observation.

Proposition 14
.

The partitioned assemblies are (internally) projective in
\mathsf{RT}(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{A}^{\prime})
, and every object of the topos is covered by a partitioned assembly.

Proof 3.1
.

The proof of
[
vO08
, Prop. 3.2.7]
for the effective topos adapts to any relative realizability topos.

We thus lose nothing by narrowing attention from the realizability topos to the subcategory of assemblies.
Given realizability predicates
\phi\in\mathrm{Pred}(S)
and
\psi\in\mathrm{Pred}(T)
on assemblies
S
and
T
, let us unfold the realizability interpretation of
\phi\leq_{\mathsf{I}}\psi
. A realizer
r\in\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
for

\forall x{:}S\,.\,\exists y{:}T\,.\,\psi(y)\Rightarrow\phi(x)

operates as follows: for all
s,x
, if
s\Vdash_{S}x
then there is
y\in|T|
such that
\mathtt{fst}{\cdot}(r{\cdot}s)\Vdash_{T}y
and whenever
p\Vdash\psi(y)
then
\mathtt{snd}{\cdot}(r{\cdot}s){\cdot}p\Vdash\phi(x)
.
Such
r
can be equivalently represented by
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\in\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
satisfying: for all
s,x
, if
s\Vdash_{S}x
then
\ell_{1}{\cdot}s\Vdash_{T}y
for some
y\in|T|
and whenever
p\Vdash\psi(y)
then
\ell_{2}{\cdot}s{\cdot}p\Vdash\phi(x)
.
Indeed, from
r
we obtain
\ell_{1}=[a]\mathtt{fst}{\cdot}(r{\cdot}a)
and
\ell_{2}=[a]\mathtt{snd}{\cdot}(r{\cdot}a)
, whereas from
\ell_{1}
and
\ell_{2}
we reconstruct
r=[a]\langle\ell_{1}{\cdot}a,\ell_{2}{\cdot}a\rangle
.
We have established the following description of instance reducibility in assemblies.

Proposition 15
.

A realizability predicate
\phi\in\mathrm{Pred}(S)
is instance reducible to
\psi\in\mathrm{Pred}(T)
when
there exist
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\in\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
such that, for all
s\in\mathbb{A}
and
x\in|S|
, if
s\Vdash_{S}x
then there is
y\in|T|
such that
\ell_{1}{\cdot}s\Vdash_{T}y
, and for all
p
, if
p\Vdash\psi(y)
then
\ell_{2}{\cdot}s{\cdot}p\Vdash\phi(x)
.

The preceding proposition may be used to translate the constructive statements of
section 2
to any realizability model.
To familiarize ourselves with instance degrees in assemblies, we illustrate the method on several examples.

Because the subobject classifier
\Omega
is not an assembly, the degree of Excluded middle is not directly represented by a realizability predicate on an assembly. However, we may lift Excluded middle along the cover
\nabla(\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{A}))\to\Omega
and calculate it to be the realizability predicate
\mathsf{LEM}\in\mathrm{Pred}(\nabla(\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{A})))
given by

\mathsf{LEM}(\theta)=\{\langle\overline{n},r\rangle\mid(n=1\land r\in\theta)\lor(n=0\land\theta=\emptyset)\}.

Thus
\mathsf{LEM}(\emptyset)=\{\langle\overline{0},r\rangle\mid r\in\mathbb{A}\}
, while for
\theta\neq\emptyset
we have
\mathsf{LEM}(\theta)=\{\langle\overline{1},r\rangle\mid r\in\theta\}
.
Next we characterize the
\lnot\lnot
-dense realizability predicates.

Proposition 16
.

A realizability predicate
\phi\in\mathrm{Pred}(S)
is
\lnot\lnot
-dense if, and only if,
\phi(x)\neq\emptyset
for all
x\in|S|
.

Proof 3.2
.

Suppose
r\Vdash\forall x{:}S\,.\,\lnot\lnot\phi(x)
and consider any
x\in|S|
. There is
s\in\mathbb{A}
such that
s\Vdash_{S}x
, hence
r{\cdot}s\Vdash\lnot\lnot\phi(x)
and so
\phi(x)\neq\emptyset
.
Conversely, if
\phi(x)\neq\emptyset
for all
x\in|S|
, then
\lnot\lnot\phi(x)=\mathbb{A}
for all
x\in|S|
,
therefore
[a]a\Vdash\forall x{:}S\,.\,\lnot\lnot\phi(x)
.

The characterization of
\lnot\lnot
-dense degrees from
13
transfers as follows.

Proposition 17
.

A realizability predicate
\phi\in\mathrm{Pred}(S)
is instance reducible to
\mathsf{LEM}
, if and only if,
\phi(x)\neq\emptyset
for all
x\in|S|
.

Proof 3.3
.

Suppose
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\in\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
realize the reduction
\phi\leq_{\mathsf{I}}\mathsf{LEM}
, and consider any
x\in|S|
. There is

r\in\mathbb{A}
such that
r\Vdash_{S}x
, hence there is
\theta\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{A})
such that
\ell_{2}{\cdot}r{\cdot}p\in\phi(x)
for all
p\in\mathsf{LEM}(\theta)
. Now
\phi(x)\neq\emptyset
because
\mathsf{LEM}(\theta)\neq\emptyset
.

Conversely, suppose
\phi(x)\neq\emptyset
for all
x\in|S|
. We claim that
\ell_{1}=\mathtt{k}{\cdot}\mathtt{k}
and
\ell_{2}=\mathtt{k}{\cdot}\mathtt{snd}
realize
\phi\leq_{\mathsf{I}}\mathsf{LEM}
. For suppose
x\in|S|
and
r\Vdash_{S}x
. Then
\mathsf{LEM}(\phi(x))=\{\langle\overline{1},p\rangle\mid p\in\phi(x)\}
because
\phi(x)\neq\emptyset
and consequently
\ell_{2}{\cdot}s{\cdot}\langle\overline{1},p\rangle=p\in\phi(x)
, as required.

Let us unravel the Heyting implication
\phi\sqsupset\psi
of realizability predicates
\phi\in\mathrm{Pred}(S)
and
\psi\in\mathrm{Pred}(T)
. The underlying assembly
S\sqsupset T
is defined by

\displaystyle|S\sqsupset T|=\{(r,\theta)\in\mathbb{A}\times\mathcal{P}(A)\mid r\Vdash\forall x{:}S\,.\,\exists y{:}T\,.\,\psi(y)\Rightarrow\phi(x)\lor\theta\},

\displaystyle s\Vdash_{S\sqsupset T}(r,\theta)\iff s=r

and the predicate
(\phi\sqsupset\psi):(S\sqsupset T)\to\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{A})
by

(\phi\sqsupset\psi)(r,\theta)=\theta
.
This is hardly an illuminating description. Hopefully, in the future there will be a better one, as well as some interesting applications of Heyting implication of instance degrees.

Suprema and infima of families of realizability predicates must be computed with proper attention to realizers. A family of assemblies
{(S_{i})}_{i\in I}
indexed by an assembly
I
is just an assignment of an assembly
S_{i}
to each index
i\in|I|
. Similarly, a family of realizability predicates
\phi_{i}\in\mathrm{Pred}(S_{i})
indexed by
I
assigns a map
\phi_{i}:|S_{i}|\to\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{A})
to each
i\in|I|
. The supremum of such a family is the realizability predicate
{\textstyle\bigsqcup}\phi:\coprod S\to\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{A})
, where

\displaystyle\textstyle\coprod S=\{(i,x)\mid i\in|I|\land x\in|S_{i}|\},

\displaystyle r\Vdash_{\coprod S}(i,x)\iff\text{$\mathtt{fst}{\cdot}r\Vdash_{I}i$ and $\mathtt{snd}{\cdot}r\Vdash_{S}x$},

is the coproduct of the family, and
{\textstyle\bigsqcup}\phi
is defined by
({\textstyle\bigsqcup}\phi)(i,x)=\phi_{i}(x)
.
The point here is that by using the coproduct
\coprod S
, an instance reduction from the supremum receives not only a realizer
x\in|S_{i}|
but also one for
i\in|I|
.

3.4.
Extended Weihrauch degrees

Because the category of assemblies is large, the instance degrees form a proper class. Here is an equivalent description of instance degrees which forms a set-sized preorder.

{defi}

An
extended Weihrauch predicate
is a map
f:\mathbb{A}\to\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{A}))
.
Its
support
is the set
\|f\|=\{r\in\mathbb{A}\mid f(r)\neq\emptyset\}
.
We say that
f:\mathbb{A}\to\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{A}))
is
Weihrauch reducible
to
g:\mathbb{A}\to\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{A}))
, written
f\leq_{\mathsf{W}}g
, when there exist
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\in\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
such that for all
r\in\|f\|
:

(1)

\ell_{1}{\cdot}r{\downarrow}
and
\ell_{1}{\cdot}r\in\|g\|
,

(2)

for every
\theta\in f(r)
there is
\xi\in g(\ell_{1}{\cdot}r)
such that
\ell_{2}{\cdot}r\Vdash\xi\Rightarrow\theta
.

(Recall from
section 3
that
\ell_{2}{\cdot}r\Vdash\xi\Rightarrow\theta
means: if
s\in\xi
then
\ell_{2}{\cdot}r{\cdot}s{\downarrow}
and
\ell_{2}{\cdot}r{\cdot}s\in\theta
.)

Condition (
2
) above may look unusual to readers who are already familiar with Weihrauch reducibilities, for it looks as if the dependence between
f
and
g
is reversed. This is not the case, however, since
\ell_{2}\cdot r
still reduces
\xi\in g(\ell_{1}\cdot r)
to
\theta\in f(r)
. Also, the first part of the condition, stating that every
\theta\in f(r)
has some
\xi\in g(\ell\cdot r)
, imposes no computability condition on how
\xi
is to be found — it just has to exist — which is precisely the novelty introduced by the extended degrees.
It may help to note that the embedding of ordinary Weihrauch degrees into the extended ones, see
19
below, embeds an ordinary degree in such a way that
f(r)
and
g(\ell_{1}\cdot r)
are singletons, which collapses the first part of (
2
) so that we are left just with the familiar condition.

Weihrauch reductions evidently form a preorder, therefore its symmetrization
\equiv_{\mathsf{W}}
forms an equivalence relation, whose classes we call
extended Weihrauch degrees
.

Proposition 18
.

Weihrauch reductions and instance reductions in
\mathsf{Asm}(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{A}^{\prime})
are equivalent preorders.

Proof 3.4
.

In one direction the equivalence maps a realizability predicate
\phi\in\mathrm{Pred}(S)
to the extended Weihrauch predicate
\mathbf{f}_{(\phi,S)}:\mathbb{A}\to\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{A}))
given by

\mathbf{f}_{(\phi,S)}(r)=\{\theta\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{A})\mid\text{there is $x\in|S|$ such that $r\Vdash_{S}x$ and $\theta=\phi(x)$}\}.

Let us verify that
\mathbf{f}
is monotone.
If
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\in\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
witness
(\phi,S)\leq_{\mathsf{I}}(\psi,T)
, as in
15
, then they witness
\mathbf{f}_{(\phi,S)}\leq_{\mathsf{W}}\mathbf{f}_{(\psi,T)}
too.
First, if
r\in\|\mathbf{f}_{(\phi,S)}\|
then
r\Vdash_{S}x
for some
x\in|S|
, hence
there is
y\in|T|
such that
\ell_{1}{\cdot}r\Vdash_{T}y
, from which we conclude
\ell_{1}{\cdot}r\in\|\mathbf{f}_{(\psi,T)}\|
.
Second, for any
\theta\in\mathbf{f}_{(\phi,S)}(r)
there is
x\in|S|
such that
r\Vdash_{S}x
and
\theta=\phi(x)
, so we may take
\xi=\psi(y)
to satisfy
\ell_{2}{\cdot}r\Vdash\xi\Rightarrow\theta
.

In the opposite direction the equivalence takes an extended Weihrauch predicate
f:\mathbb{A}\to\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{A}))
to the realizability predicate
\boldsymbol{\phi}_{f}\in\mathrm{Pred}(\mathbf{S}_{f})
where

\displaystyle|\mathbf{S}_{f}|=\{(r,\theta)\in\mathbb{A}\times\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{A})\mid\theta\in f(r)\},

\displaystyle s\Vdash_{\mathbf{S}_{f}}(r,\theta)\iff s=r,

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\phi}_{f}(r,\theta)=\theta.

Again, we must establish monotonicity of
(\boldsymbol{\phi},\mathbf{S})
. If
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\in\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
witness
f\leq_{\mathsf{W}}g
then they also witness
(\boldsymbol{\phi}_{f},\mathbf{S}_{f})\leq_{\mathsf{I}}(\boldsymbol{\phi}_{g},\mathbf{S}_{g})
. First, if
s\Vdash_{\mathbf{S}_{f}}(r,\theta)
then
s=r
and
\theta\in f(r)
, therefore
r\in\|f\|
and
\ell_{1}{\cdot}r\in\|g\|
. Second, there is
\xi\in g(\ell_{1}{\cdot}r)
such that
\ell_{2}{\cdot}r\Vdash\xi\Rightarrow\theta
, hence
(\ell_{1}{\cdot}r,\xi)\in|\mathbf{S}_{g}|
and if
p\in\xi
then
\ell_{2}{\cdot}r{\cdot}p\in\theta
, as required.

It remains to be checked that
\mathbf{f}
and
(\boldsymbol{\phi},\mathbf{S})
form an equivalence. Unfolding of definitions reveals that
\mathbf{f}_{(\mathbf{S}_{f},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{f})}=f
for all
f:\mathbb{A}\to\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{A}))
.
To see that
(\boldsymbol{\phi}_{\mathbf{f}_{(\phi,S)}},\mathbf{S}_{\mathbf{f}_{(\phi,S)}})\equiv_{\mathsf{I}}(\phi,S)
for any
\phi\in\mathrm{Pred}(S)
, observe that

\displaystyle|\mathbf{S}_{\mathbf{f}_{(\phi,S)}}|=\{(r,\theta)\in\mathbb{A}\times\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{A})\mid\text{there is $x\in|S|$ such that $r\Vdash_{S}x$ and $\theta=\phi(x)$}\},

\displaystyle s\Vdash_{\mathbf{S}_{\mathbf{f}_{(\phi,S)}}}(r,\theta)\iff s=r.

Now it is clear that both
(\boldsymbol{\phi}_{\mathbf{f}_{(\phi,S)}},\mathbf{S}_{\mathbf{f}_{(\phi,S)}})\leq_{\mathsf{I}}(\phi,S)
and
(\phi,S)\leq_{\mathsf{I}}(\boldsymbol{\phi}_{\mathbf{f}_{(\phi,S)}},\mathbf{S}_{\mathbf{f}_{(\phi,S)}})
are realized by
\ell_{1}=[a]a
and
\ell_{2}=[a][b]b
.

Various properties of realizability predicates may be transferred to extended Weihrauch degrees via the equivalence given in the proof of
18
. For example, if
\phi\subseteq S
is a realizability predicate on a modest assembly then the corresponding extended Weihrauch degree
\mathbf{f}_{(\phi,S)}
satisfies

\mathbf{f}_{(\phi,S)}(r)=\begin{cases}\{\phi(x)\}&\text{if $r\Vdash_{S}x$},\\
\emptyset&\text{if $r$ does not realize any $x\in|S|$.}\end{cases}

Thus we define a
modest extended Weihrauch predicate
to be a map
f:\mathbb{A}\to\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{A}))
such that
f(r)
has at most one element for every
r\in\mathbb{A}
. In a similar fashion the characterization of
\lnot\lnot
-density given in
16
prompts us to define a
\lnot\lnot
-dense extended Weihrauch predicate
to be a map
f:\mathbb{A}\to\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{A}))
such that
\theta\neq\emptyset
for all
r\in\mathbb{A}
and
\theta\in f(r)
.

3.5.
Weihrauch degrees as a sublattice of instance degrees

Weihrauch degrees
[
BG11
]
are defined in the context of Type 2 computability
[
Wei00
]
.
We first generalize them in a straightforward manner to any pca
\mathbb{A}
with an elementary sub-pca
\mathbb{A}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{A}
.

An
(ordinary) Weihrauch predicate
is a relation
U\subseteq\mathbb{A}\times\mathbb{A}
, whose
support
is
\|U\|=\{r\in\mathbb{A}\mid\exists s\in\mathbb{A}\,.\,(r,s)\in U\}
.
By writing
U[r]=\{s\in\mathbb{A}\mid(r,s)\in U\}
we construe
U
as a multi-valued map from
\|U\|
to
\mathbb{A}
.
A
Weihrauch reduction

U\leq_{\mathsf{w}}V
from
V\subseteq\mathbb{A}\times\mathbb{A}
is given by
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\in\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
such that, for all
r\in\|U\|
,

(1)

\ell_{1}{\cdot}r{\downarrow}
and
\ell_{1}{\cdot}r\in\|V\|
, and

(2)

for all
s\in V[\ell_{1}{\cdot}r]
we have
\ell_{2}{\cdot}r{\cdot}s{\downarrow}
and
\ell_{2}{\cdot}r{\cdot}s\in U[r]
.

Once again,
\leq_{\mathsf{w}}
is a preorder whose symmetrization
\equiv_{\mathsf{w}}
is an equivalence relation. Its classes are the
(ordinary) Weihrauch degrees
.

Proposition 19
.

Ordinary Weihrauch reductions form a preorder that is equivalent to extended Weihrauch reductions on the
\lnot\lnot
-dense modest extended Weihrauch predicates.

Proof 3.5
.

To each Weihrauch predicate
U\subseteq\mathbb{A}\times\mathbb{A}
we associate the extended Weihrauch predicate
\widehat{U}:\mathbb{A}\to\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{A}))
, defined by

\widehat{U}(r)=\begin{cases}\{U[r]\}&\text{if $r\in\|U\|$,}\\
\emptyset&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

It is clear that
\widehat{U}
is both
\lnot\lnot
-dense and modest.
The assignment is monotone, for if
U\leq_{\mathsf{w}}V
is witnessed by
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\in\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
then
\widehat{U}\leq_{\mathsf{W}}\widehat{V}
is witnessed by
\ell_{1}
and
\ell_{2}
as well.

For the opposite direction, suppose
\phi:\mathbb{A}\to\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{A}))
is an extended Weihrauch predicate which is
\lnot\lnot
-dense and modest, so that there is a unique map
u:\|\phi\|\to\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{A})\setminus\{\emptyset\}
such that
\phi(r)=\{u(r)\}
for all
r\in\|\phi\|
. Let
U_{\phi}\subseteq\mathbb{A}\times\mathbb{A}
be the Weihrauch predicate characterized by
\|U_{\phi}\|=\|\phi\|
and
U_{\phi}[r]=u(r)
, i.e.,

U_{\phi}=\{(r,s)\in\mathbb{A}\times\mathbb{A}\mid\exists\theta\in\phi(r)\,.\,s\in\theta\}.

Once again it is easy to see that if
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\in\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
witness
\phi\leq_{\mathsf{W}}\psi
then they also witness
U_{\phi}\leq_{\mathsf{w}}U_{\psi}
.
The maps
U\mapsto\widehat{U}
and
\phi\mapsto U_{\phi}
are monotone and inverses of each other, therefore they constitute an equivalence of preorders.

4.
Examples

We make a cursory exploration of the structure of instance degrees in realizability models by way of several examples, and leave a more serious analysis for a future time.

4.1.
Non-trivial non-
\lnot\lnot
-degrees

When
\mathbb{A}^{\prime}=\mathbb{A}
the only degree which is not
\lnot\lnot
-dense is the top degree. Indeed, if
\phi\in\mathrm{Pred}(S)
is not
\lnot\lnot
-dense then
\phi(a)=\emptyset
for some
a\in|S|
, and there is
r\in\mathbb{A}
such that
r\Vdash_{S}a
.
Crucially,
r\in\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
allows us to use
\langle r,\mathtt{k}\rangle\in\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
as a realizer for the statement
\exists x{:}S\,.\,\lnot\phi(x)
, which claims that
\phi
has a counter-example. Therefore
\phi
represents the top degree.
The same trick does not work when
\mathbb{A}^{\prime}\neq\mathbb{A}
, for we have no guarantee that there is
r\in\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
such that
r\Vdash_{S}a
.

The previous observation inspires the following example. Recall from
subsection 3.1
and
subsection 3.1
that the exponential assembly
N^{N}=F=(|F|,{\Vdash_{F}})
is the set of functions
\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
that are realized by elements of
\mathbb{A}
:

\displaystyle|F|=\{f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}\mid\exists r\in\mathbb{A}\,.\,\forall n\in\mathbb{N}\,.\,r{\cdot}\overline{n}=\overline{f(n)}\},

\displaystyle r\Vdash_{F}f\iff\forall n\in\mathbb{N}\,.\,r{\cdot}\overline{n}=\overline{f(n)}.

Let
\varphi
be a standard enumeration of partial computable maps, and
define the realizability predicate
\mathsf{CT}\in\mathrm{Pred}(F)
by

\mathsf{CT}(f)=\{\overline{m}\in\mathbb{A}\mid f=\varphi_{m}\}.

Here
\mathsf{CT}
stands for “Church’s thesis” because the realizers of
\mathsf{CT}(f)
are numerals
\overline{m}
witnessing Turing-computability of
f
.
Construed as an extended Weihrauch predicate,
\mathsf{CT}
is the map
\mathsf{CT}:\mathbb{A}\to\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{A}))
defined by

\textsf{CT}(r)=\begin{cases}\{\{\overline{m}\in\mathbb{A}\mid\forall k\in\mathbb{N}\,.\,r{\cdot}\overline{k}=\varphi_{m}(k)\}\}&\text{if $r{\cdot}\overline{k}$ is a numeral for all $k\in\mathbb{N}$,}\\
\emptyset&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

The formula
\forall f{:}F\,.\,\mathsf{CT}(f)
, which can be read as “all functions are computable”, is realized by
s\in\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
such that if
r\Vdash_{F}f
then
s{\cdot}r=\overline{m}
and
f=\varphi_{m}
for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Whether such a realizer exists depends on the choice of
\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
and
\mathbb{A}
. For example, in the effective topos
\mathbb{A}^{\prime}=\mathbb{A}=\mathbb{N}
and we may simply take
s=[a]a
. In contrast, in Kleene-Vesley realizability
\mathsf{CT}
is an interesting representative of a degree that does not arise as an ordinary Weihrauch degree. Indeed it is not
\lnot\lnot
-dense because
\mathsf{CT}(h)=\emptyset
for non-computable
h:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
, but at the same time
\exists h{:}F\,.\,\lnot\mathsf{CT}(h)
has no computable realizers.

4.2.
Embedding of truth values

Let us reformulate the embeddings of the subobject classifier into instance degrees from
subsection 2.6
as extended Weihrauch degrees.
A short calculation reveals that the embedding from
9
takes a realizability truth value
\theta\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{A})
to the (ordinary) Weihrauch predicate
T_{\theta}:\mathbb{A}\to\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{A}))
, defined by

T_{\theta}(r)=\begin{cases}\{\mathbb{A}\}&\text{if $r\in\theta$,}\\
\emptyset&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

It is easily seen that
T_{\theta}\leq_{\mathsf{W}}T_{\xi}
holds precisely when there is
\ell\in\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
such that
\ell\Vdash\theta\Rightarrow\psi
, which reaffirms monotonicity of the embedding with respect to
\Rightarrow
.
In Kleene-Vesley realizability
\theta\mapsto T_{\theta}
is just the familiar embedding of the Medvedev lattice into Weihrauch degrees.

The embedding from
10
takes
\theta\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{A})
to the modest extended Weihrauch predicate
\theta^{\star}:\mathbb{A}\to\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{A}))
, defined by

\theta^{\star}(r)=\{\theta\}
,
which in Kleene-Vesley realizability provides an anti-monotone semilattice embedding of the Medvedev lattice into extended Weihrauch degrees. It
almost
maps into ordinary Weihrauch degrees, as only
\emptyset^{\star}(r)=\{\emptyset\}
fails to be
\lnot\lnot
-dense.

4.3.
Reductions to and from non-modest degrees

The degrees from
subsection 4.1
and
subsection 4.2
are all modest, so one may wonder whether the
non-modest degrees are of any use. We show examples that convey how non-modest degrees allow us to control
an aspect of reductions that one might refer to as
uniformity
.

A map
\phi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{A})
may be seen either as a realizability predicate on the modest assembly
N
of natural numbers, as defined above, or on the non-modest assembly
\nabla\mathbb{N}
.
Now, assuming we have another such map
\psi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{A})
, there are four possibilities:

(1)

(\phi,N)\leq_{\mathsf{I}}(\psi,N)
when there are
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\in\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
such that, for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
, there is (a unique)
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
\ell_{1}{\cdot}\overline{m}=\overline{n}
and
\ell_{2}{\cdot}\overline{m}\Vdash\psi(n)\Rightarrow\phi(m)
. This case is analogous to the ordinary Weihrauch reductions.

(2)

(\phi,\nabla\mathbb{N})\leq_{\mathsf{I}}(\psi,N)
when there are
\ell_{2}\in\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
we have
\ell_{2}\Vdash\psi(n)\Rightarrow\phi(m)
.
This case is somewhat pathological, as it happens precisely when
\psi\equiv_{\mathsf{I}}{\top\!\!\!\top}
or
\phi\equiv_{\mathsf{I}}\top_{\!\mathsf{1}}
.

(3)

(\phi,N)\leq_{\mathsf{I}}(\psi,\nabla\mathbb{N})
when there is a map
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
and
\ell_{2}\in\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
such that for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
we have
\ell_{2}{\cdot}\overline{m}\Vdash\psi(f(m))\Rightarrow\phi(m)
.
This case is
non-uniform
in the sense that
\ell_{1}
is replaced by a map
f
that need not be realized. An additional example of this kind is given below.

(4)

(\phi,\nabla\mathbb{N})\leq_{\mathsf{I}}(\psi,\nabla\mathbb{N})
when there is a map
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
and
\ell_{2}\in\mathbb{A}^{\prime}
such that for every
m\in\mathbb{N}

\ell_{2}\Vdash\psi(f(m))\Rightarrow\phi(m)
. In contrast to the previous case,
\ell_{2}
must work for every
m

without
being given it as an input.

To summarize, a reduction
\phi\leq_{\mathsf{I}}\psi
from a modest
\phi
to a non-modest
\psi
may be non-uniform in the sense that it need not compute an instance of
\psi
to which a given instance of
\phi
reduces.
In the opposite direction, a reduction
\phi\leq_{\mathsf{I}}\psi
from a non-modest
\phi
to a modest
\psi
must be uniform in the sense that all instances of
\phi
are reduced to the same instance of
\psi
.

The above examples illustrate just the most extreme possibilities. We may calibrate the uniformity aspects of
reductions by using assemblies that are neither modest nor in the image of
\nabla
.

Let us conclude with another example of reductions between modest and non-modest degrees. This time we work directly with (extended) Weihrauch degrees in Kleene-Vesley realizability.
The principle of Weak Excluded Middle

\forall p{:}\Omega\,.\,\lnot p\lor\lnot\lnot p

is represented by the (non-modest) extended Weihrauch predicate
\mathsf{WLEM}:\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}\to\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}))
,

\mathsf{WLEM}(\alpha)=\{\{\overline{0}\},\{\overline{1}\}\},

while the Limited principle of omniscience (
1
) is represented by the (modest) Weihrauch predicate

\mathsf{LPO}:\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}\to\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}))
,

\mathsf{LPO}(\alpha)=\begin{cases}\{\{\overline{0}\}\}&\text{if $\forall n\,.\,\alpha_{n}=0$}\\
\{\{\overline{1}\}\}&\text{if $\exists n\,.\,\alpha_{n}\neq 0$}.\end{cases}

The reduction
\mathsf{LPO}\leq_{\mathsf{I}}\mathsf{WLEM}
is witnessed by computable maps
\ell_{1}:\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}\to\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}
and

\ell_{2}:\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}\times\{\overline{0},\overline{1}\}\to\{\overline{0},\overline{1}\}
such that, for every
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}
:

(1)

\ell_{1}(\alpha)\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}
, and

(2)

for every
\theta\in\mathsf{LPO}(\alpha)
there is
\xi\in\{\{\overline{0}\},\{\overline{1}\}\}
such that

\ell_{2}(\alpha,b)\in\theta
for all
b\in\xi
.

We may drop
\ell_{1}
and simplify the two conditions to a single one:

(3)

if
\forall n\,.\,\alpha_{n}=0
then
\ell_{2}(\alpha,\overline{0})=\overline{0}
and if
\exists n\,.\,\alpha_{n}=1
then
\ell_{2}(\alpha,\overline{1})=\overline{1}
.

The map
\ell_{2}(\alpha,b)=b
clearly satisfies the condition, hence
\mathsf{LPO}\leq_{\mathsf{W}}\mathsf{WLEM}
holds. Notice how the non-modesty allowed us to avoid computing which of the two possibilities in
3
happens.

How about the opposite reduction
\mathsf{WLEM}\leq_{\mathsf{W}}\mathsf{LPO}
? It would be witnessed by computable
\ell_{1}
and
\ell_{2}
such that, for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}
:

(1)

\ell_{1}(\alpha)\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}}
, and

(2)

for every
\theta\in\{\{\overline{0}\},\{\overline{1}\}\}
there is
\xi\in\mathsf{LPO}(\ell_{1}(\alpha))
such that
\ell_{2}(\alpha,b)\in\theta
for all
b\in\xi
.

The second condition is contradictory, as it requires both
\ell_{2}(\alpha,b)=\overline{0}
and
\ell_{2}(\alpha,b)=\overline{1}
. Thus there is no such reduction.

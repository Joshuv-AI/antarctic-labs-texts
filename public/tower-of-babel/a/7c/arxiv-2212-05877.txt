A proof for string three-point functions in AdS 3
Davide Bufalini, Sergio Iguri, and Nicolas Kovensky

Abstract
Correlation functions of the SL(2, R \mathbb{R} )-WZW model involving spectrally flowed vertex operators are notoriously difficult to compute. An explicit integral expression for the corresponding three-point functions was recently conjectured in Dei:2021xgh . In this paper, we provide a proof for this conjecture. For this, we extend the methods of Iguri:2022eat based on the so-called SL(2, R \mathbb{R} ) series identifications, which relate vertex operators belonging to different spectral flow sectors. We also highlight the role of holomorphic covering maps in this context. Our results constitute an important milestone for proving this instance of the AdS 3 /CFT 2 holographic duality at finite ’t Hooft coupling.



1
Introduction

In most instances of the AdS/CFT duality, the bulk theory is approximated by supergravity, due to the notorious difficulty in performing stringy computations. The case of string theory in AdS
3
with pure NS fluxes is a notable exception. The dynamics of closed strings propagating on this background can be described at the worldsheet level in terms of the Wess-Zumino-Witten (WZW) model built upon the universal cover of SL
(2,\mathbb{R})
. This model is believed to be exactly solvable, hence providing a concrete scenario in which the AdS/CFT duality might be proven at finite ’t Hooft coupling.

The worldsheet CFT, being both Lorentzian and non-compact, enjoys a number of unusual features. Perhaps the most important one is the non-trivial action of the so-called spectral flow outer-automorphisms of the affine sl(2,
\mathbb{R}
)
k
algebra. Spectral flow plays a central role in the determination of the string spectrum and partition function
Maldacena:2000hw
;
Maldacena:2000kv
. In a semi-classical description of long string states, the spectral flow charge
\omega
can be thought of as a winding number around the asymptotic boundary of AdS
3
. However, it is not a conserved quantity since the associated circle becomes contractible in the AdS
3
interior.

Spectral flow also introduces important complications, especially concerning the computation of worldsheet correlation functions
Maldacena:2001km
. Indeed, while being Virasoro primaries, spectrally flowed vertex operators are
not
affine primaries. Their operator product expansions (OPEs) with the conserved currents become increasingly complicated with growing
\omega
, and contain many unknown terms. Hence, some conventional techniques of two-dimensional CFT can not be applied directly.

The interest in correlation functions of the SL(2,
\mathbb{R}
)-WZW model is explained by their important holographic applications, especially in the context of superstring theory in AdS
{}_{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{3}\times\mathbb{T}^{4}
(or K3)
Giveon:1998ns
;
Kutasov:1999xu
;
Giribet:2007wp
;
Gaberdiel:2007vu
, and for the dynamics of light probes in black hole microstates
Martinec:2017ztd
;
Martinec:2018nco
;
Martinec:2019wzw
;
Martinec:2020gkv
;
Martinec:2022okx
;
Bufalini:2021ndn
;
Bufalini:2022wyp
;
Bufalini:2022wzu
. Worldsheet vertex operators are functions of the worldsheet coordinate
z
, and of an additional continuous parameter
x
, which plays the role of the holomorphic coordinate on the conformal boundary. Hence,
z
-integrated correlators are identified with
n
-point functions of local operators in the holographic CFT. Although the precise definition of the latter remains elusive, a concrete albeit perturbative proposal was put forward recently in

Eberhardt:2021vsx
;
Balthazar:2021xeh
.
The generators of the spacetime Virasoro algebra are in one-to-one correspondence with those of the
\text{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})_{k}
algebra, and the worldsheet quantum numbers determine the conformal weight
h
of dual CFT operators
Giveon:1998ns
.
Suppressing the anti-holomorphic quantities, we denote the spectrally-flowed worldsheet vertex operators in the so-called
x
-basis as
V_{jh}^{\omega}(x,z)
, where
j
is the SL
(2,\mathbb{R})
spin, while
h
is the spacetime weight.

In this paper, we consider genus-zero worldsheet correlators of spectrally flowed vertex operators.
Recently, progress in the computation of three- and four-point functions has been made in
Eberhardt:2019ywk
;
Dei:2021xgh
;
Dei:2021yom
, thanks to the systematic use of the constraints deriving from global and local symmetries. In particular, the latter imply a set of complicated linear recursion relations among correlators involving different assignments of spectral flow charges. These are difficult to derive in general. However, in
Dei:2021xgh
it has been shown that they can be recast in the form of partial differential equations thanks to the introduction of the so-called ‘
y
-variable’. In this new basis, a vertex operator is now defined as a coherent superposition of states with different spacetime weights, denoted as
V_{j}^{\omega}(x,y,z)
. The authors of
Dei:2021xgh
were able to infer a closed-form expression for all three-point functions

\braket{V_{j_{1}}^{\omega_{1}}(x_{1};y_{1};z_{1})V_{j_{2}}^{\omega_{2}}(x_{2};y_{2};z_{2})V_{j_{3}}^{\omega_{3}}(x_{3};y_{3};z_{3})}\,.

(1)

This proposal can be understood intuitively from the existence, in some cases, of a holomorphic mapping of the worldsheet to the AdS
3
boundary, which has branching points of order
\omega_{i}
at each of the corresponding insertions. The same authors provided a closed-form expression for four-point functions in a follow-up work
Dei:2021yom
. These proposals satisfy a number of highly non-trivial consistency checks. However, in both cases, these expressions were derived on a case-by-case basis, and for a finite set of sufficiently low spectral flow charges.

In this work, we provide a proof for the conjecture of
Dei:2021xgh
concerning the three-point functions (
1
). Our methods rely heavily on the so-called ‘series identifications’ of SL(2,
\mathbb{R}
), originally formulated in
Fateev
;
Maldacena:2000hw
, which constitute a set of isomorphisms among affine modules of the
\text{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})_{k}
algebra with adjacent spectral flow charges. The corresponding identities at the level of
y
-basis operators were obtained recently in
Iguri:2022eat
. This allows us to derive the differential equations satisfied by (and the resulting
y
-dependence of) all spectrally flowed three-point functions, including those that do not admit a holomorphic cover.

The structure of the paper is as follows.
In section
2
, we establish our conventions
and review the derivation of the recursion relations satisfied by the SL(2,
\mathbb{R}
) correlation functions of
Eberhardt:2019ywk
. We also introduce the

-basis operators of
Dei:2021xgh
together with their conjecture for three-point functions.
Our main results are presented in section

y
-basis three-point functions, and show that all the corresponding solutions are compatible with the proposal of
Dei:2021xgh
.
In particular, generic odd parity correlators are considered in section
3.1
, while even parity correlators are obtained in section
3.2
.
Edge cases and correlators with unflowed insertions are treated in sections
3.3
and
3.4

3.5

Iguri:2022eat

We conclude with some discussions and outlook for future work in section

2
Conventions and brief review of the conjecture

Let us consider the bosonic SL(2,
\mathbb{R}
)-WZW model at level
k>3
. In this section, we introduce the spectrum and the associated vertex operators. We also briefly review the analysis of
Eberhardt:2019ywk
leading to the recursion relations satisfied by the correlation functions of the model, and present the conjecture put forward in
Dei:2021xgh
for spectrally flowed three-point functions.

2.1
Vertex operators

We focus mainly on the holomorphic sector. The conserved currents satisfy

J^{a}(z)J^{b}(w)\sim\frac{\eta^{ab}k/2}{(z-w)^{2}}+\frac{f^{ab}_{\phantom{ab}c}J^{c}(w)}{z-w}\,,

(2)

with
a=+,-,3
,
\eta^{+-}=-2\eta^{33}=2
,
f^{+-}_{\phantom{+-}3}=-2
and
f^{3+}_{\phantom{3+}+}=-f^{3-}_{\phantom{3-}-}=1
. In the so-called
x
-basis, vertex operators are denoted as
V^{\omega}_{jh}(x,z)
. They depend both on the worldsheet coordinate
z
and on
x
, the coordinate on the boundary of AdS
3
associated with the holographic CFT. Moreover,
h
and
j
denote the spacetime weight and the SL(2,
\mathbb{R}
) spin, respectively. The worldsheet conformal weight is

\Delta=-\frac{j(j-1)}{k-2}-h\omega+\frac{k}{4}\omega^{2}\,,

(3)

while
\omega\geq 0
is the spectral flow charge.

Two types of states must be taken into account
Maldacena:2000hw
.
For operators defined upon unflowed states in the continuous representations
{\cal{C}}_{j}^{\alpha}
, the relevant quantum numbers are

j\,\in\,\frac{1}{2}+i\mathbb{R}\quad\text{and}\quad m=\alpha\pm n\,,\quad\text{with}\quad\alpha\in[0,1)\quad\text{and}\quad n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\,,

(4)

where
m
stands for the spin projection of the corresponding unflowed state.
On the other hand, for the unflowed discrete highest/lowest-weight representations
{\cal{D}}_{j}^{\pm}
we have

\frac{1}{2}<j<\frac{k-1}{2}\,\quad\text{and}\quad m=\pm(j+n)\,,\quad\text{with}\quad j\in\mathbb{R}\quad\text{and}\quad n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

(5)

In both cases we have
h=m+\frac{k}{2}\omega
when
\omega>0
, while
h=j
for
\omega=0
.

The vertex operators are defined by means of their OPEs with the currents:

\displaystyle J^{+}(w)V^{\omega}_{jh}(x,z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\omega+1}\frac{\left(J_{n-1}^{+}V_{jh}^{\omega}\right)(x,z)}{(w-z)^{n}}+\cdots\,,

(6a)

\displaystyle J^{3}(w)V^{\omega}_{jh}(x,z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x\sum_{n=2}^{\omega+1}\frac{\left(J_{n-1}^{+}V_{jh}^{\omega}\right)(x,z)}{(w-z)^{n}}+\frac{\left(J_{0}^{3}V_{jh}^{\omega}\right)(x,z)}{(w-z)}+\cdots\,,

(6b)

\displaystyle J^{-}(w)V^{\omega}_{jh}(x,z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x^{2}\sum_{n=2}^{\omega+1}\frac{\left(J_{n-1}^{+}V_{jh}^{\omega}\right)(x,z)}{(w-z)^{n}}+\frac{\left(J_{0}^{-}V_{jh}^{\omega}\right)(x,z)}{(w-z)}+\cdots\,,

(6c)

where the ellipsis indicates higher order terms in
(w-z)
. Unflowed vertex operators will be denoted by
V_{j}(x,z)
. The zero modes act as differential operators in
x
,

\displaystyle\left(J_{0}^{+}V_{jh}^{\omega}\right)(x,z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{x}V_{jh}^{\omega}(x,z)\,,

(7a)

\displaystyle\left(J_{0}^{3}V_{jh}^{\omega}\right)(x,z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x\partial_{x}+h)V_{jh}^{\omega}(x,z)\,,

(7b)

\displaystyle\left(J_{0}^{-}V_{jh}^{\omega}\right)(x,z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x^{2}\partial_{x}+2hx)V_{jh}^{\omega}(x,z)\,,

(7c)

while

\left(J_{\pm\omega}^{\pm}V_{jh}^{\omega}\right)(x,z)=\left[h-\frac{k}{2}\omega\pm(1-j)\right]V_{j,h\pm 1}^{\omega}(x,z)\,.

(8)

Importantly, in terms of the currents

J^{+}(x,z)=J^{+}(z)\,,\quad J^{3}(x,z)=J^{3}(z)-xJ^{+}(z)\,,\quad J^{-}(x,z)=J^{-}(z)-2xJ^{3}(z)+x^{2}J^{+}(z)

(9)

we get

\displaystyle J^{3}(x,w)V^{\omega}_{jh}(x,z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{h}{(w-z)}V_{jh}^{\omega}(x,z)+\cdots\,,

(10a)

\displaystyle J^{-}(x,w)V^{\omega}_{jh}(x,z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(w-z)^{\omega-1}\left(J_{-w}^{-}V_{jh}^{\omega}\right)(x,z)+\cdots\,.

(10b)

An alternative (equivalent) definition was given recently in
Iguri:2022eat
, based on
Maldacena:2001km
. This is to be understood as a point-splitting procedure between the corresponding unflowed vertex and the so-called generalized spectral flow operator
V_{\frac{k}{2},\frac{k}{2}\omega}^{\omega-1}(x,z)
, and reads

V_{jh}^{\omega}(x,z)=\lim_{\varepsilon,\bar{\varepsilon}\to 0}\varepsilon^{m\omega}\bar{\varepsilon}^{\bar{m}\omega}\int d^{2}y\,y^{j-m-1}\bar{y}^{j-\bar{m}-1}V_{j}(x+y,z+\varepsilon)V_{\frac{k}{2},\frac{k}{2}\omega}^{\omega-1}(x,z)\,.

(11)

We will come back to this shortly in section
2.3
.

2.2
Recursion relations among correlators

We now discuss the correlation functions of the model. It was shown in
Eberhardt:2019ywk
that they must satisfy a set of recursion relations. Let us briefly review how this works for three-point functions of the form

F=\left\langle\prod_{j=1}^{3}V_{j_{j}h_{j}}^{\omega_{j}}(x_{j},z_{j})\right\rangle.

(12)

We define

F_{n}^{i}=\left\langle\left(J_{n}^{+}V_{j_{i}h_{i}}^{\omega_{i}}\right)(x_{i},z_{i})\prod_{j\neq i}V_{j_{j}h_{j}}^{\omega_{j}}(x_{j},z_{j})\right\rangle,

(13)

so that, in particular,

F_{0}^{i}=\partial_{x_{i}}F\,,

(14)

and

F_{\omega_{i}}^{i}=\left(h_{i}-\frac{k}{2}\omega_{i}+1-j_{i}\right)\left\langle V_{j_{i},h_{i}+1}^{\omega_{i}}(x_{i},z_{i})\prod_{j\neq i}V_{j_{j}h_{j}}^{\omega_{j}}(x_{j},z_{j})\right\rangle\,,

(15)

as implied by Eqs. (
7a
) and (
8
) respectively. Note that all
F^{i}_{n}
with

are, in principle, unknown.

By using the OPEs in Eq. (
6
), one finds that correlators involving a current insertion can be expanded as

\displaystyle\left\langle J^{+}(z)\prod_{j=1}^{3}V_{j_{j}h_{j}}^{\omega_{j}}(x_{j},z_{j})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{3}\left[\frac{\partial_{x_{i}}F}{z-z_{i}}+\sum_{n=1}^{\omega_{i}}\frac{F_{n}^{i}}{(z-z_{i})^{n+1}}\right]+\cdots\,,

(16a)

\displaystyle\left\langle J^{3}(z)\prod_{j=1}^{3}V_{j_{j}h_{j}}^{\omega_{j}}(x_{j},z_{j})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{3}\left[\frac{(h_{i}+x_{i}\partial_{x_{i}})F}{z-z_{i}}+\sum_{n=1}^{\omega_{i}}\frac{x_{i}F_{n}^{i}}{(z-z_{i})^{n+1}}\right]+\cdots\,,

(16b)

\displaystyle\left\langle J^{-}(z)\prod_{j=1}^{3}V_{j_{j}h_{j}}^{\omega_{j}}(x_{j},z_{j})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{3}\left[\frac{(2h_{i}x_{i}+x_{i}^{2}\partial_{x_{i}})F}{z-z_{i}}+\sum_{n=1}^{\omega_{i}}\frac{x_{i}^{2}F_{n}^{i}}{(z-z_{i})^{n+1}}\right]+\cdots.

(16c)

Combining these expressions, we get

G_{j}(z)\equiv\left\langle J^{-}(x_{j},z)\prod_{l=1}^{3}V_{j_{l}h_{l}}^{\omega_{l}}(x_{l},z_{l})\right\rangle=\sum_{i\neq j}\left[\frac{(2h_{i}x_{ij}+x_{ij}^{2}\partial_{x_{i}})F}{z-z_{i}}+\sum_{n=1}^{\omega_{i}}\frac{x_{ij}^{2}F_{n}^{i}}{(z-z_{i})^{n+1}}\right]+\cdots

(17)

where

On the other hand, Eq. (

(z-z_{j})^{1-\omega_{j}}G_{j}(z)=\left(h_{j}-\frac{k}{2}\omega_{j}-1+j_{j}\right)\left\langle V_{j_{j},h_{j}-1}^{\omega_{j}}(x_{j},z_{j})\prod_{i\neq j}V_{j_{i}h_{i}}^{\omega_{i}}(x_{i},z_{i})\right\rangle+\cdots,

(18)

where we have used (

Upon inserting the resulting expressions into Eq. (

2.3
The conjectured solution

The non-trivial OPEs in Eqs. (

y
-basis three-point functions

In this section we prove the conjecture put forward in
Dei:2021xgh

It was shown in

\omega_{i}+\omega_{j}\geq\omega_{k}-1\qquad\forall\,\,i\neq j\neq k.

We first consider the subfamily of odd parity correlators for which an associated holomorphic covering map exists

;

3.1
Holomorphic covering maps and odd parity correlators

For concreteness, and with no loss of generality, we take

3.2

We now prove that the conjecture of

(64)

B=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\frac{(\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}+\omega_{2})}{\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}-\omega_{3}}a_{1}[\Gamma_{3}^{-}]^{2}&\frac{2\omega_{1}}{\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}-\omega_{3}}a_{1}[\Gamma_{3}^{-}]a_{2}[\Gamma_{3}^{-}]&\frac{2\omega_{1}}{\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}+\omega_{3}}a_{1}[\Gamma_{2}^{-}]a_{3}[\Gamma_{2}^{-}]\\
\frac{2\omega_{2}}{\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}-\omega_{3}}a_{1}[\Gamma_{3}^{-}]a_{2}[\Gamma_{3}^{-}]&\frac{(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}-w_{2})}{-\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}+\omega_{3}}a_{2}[\Gamma_{1}^{-}]^{2}&\frac{2\omega_{2}}{-\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}+\omega_{3}}a_{2}[\Gamma_{1}^{-}]a_{3}[\Gamma_{1}^{-}]\\

(65)

and

C=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\frac{4\omega_{1}j_{3}}{\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}+\omega_{3}}a_{1}[\Gamma_{2}^{-}]+\frac{2\omega_{1}(j_{2}+j_{3})+2j_{1}(\omega_{3}-\omega_{2})}{\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}-\omega_{3}}a_{1}[\Gamma_{3}^{-}]\\

\frac{4\omega_{3}j_{1}}{\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}+\omega_{3}}a_{3}[\Gamma_{2}^{-}]-\frac{2\omega_{3}(j_{1}+j_{2})+2j_{3}(\omega_{1}-\omega_{2})}{\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}-\omega_{3}}a_{3}[\Gamma_{1}^{-}]\end{array}\right).

(66)

Consequently, we can write the differential equations for the even parity correlators as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\Big\{\left(y_{1}-a_{1}[\Gamma_{3}^{-}]\right)^{2}\partial_{y_{1}}+2j_{1}\left(y_{1}-a_{1}[\Gamma_{3}^{-}]\right)+\frac{2a_{1}[\Gamma_{3}^{-}]\omega_{1}}{\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}-\omega_{3}}\Big[(y_{1}-a_{1}[\Gamma_{3}^{-}])\partial_{y_{1}}+j_{1}\\

(67)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\Big\{\left(y_{2}-a_{2}[\Gamma_{3}^{-}]\right)^{2}\partial_{y_{2}}+2j_{2}\left(y_{2}-a_{2}[\Gamma_{3}^{-}]\right)+\frac{2a_{2}[\Gamma_{3}^{-}]\omega_{2}}{\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}-\omega_{3}}\Big[(y_{1}-a_{1}[\Gamma_{3}^{-}])\partial_{y_{1}}+j_{1}\\

(68)

and

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\Big\{\left(y_{3}-a_{3}[\Gamma_{1}^{-}]\right)^{2}\partial_{y_{3}}+2j_{3}\left(y_{3}-a_{3}[\Gamma_{1}^{-}]\right)+\frac{2a_{3}[\Gamma_{1}^{-}]\omega_{3}}{\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}-\omega_{3}}\Big[(y_{1}-a_{1}[\Gamma_{2}^{-}])\partial_{y_{1}}+j_{1}\\
&\,-\,(y_{2}-a_{2}[\Gamma_{1}^{-}])\partial_{y_{2}}-j_{2}-(y_{3}-a_{3}[\Gamma_{1}^{-}])\partial_{y_{3}}-j_{3}\Big]\Big\}\,\langle V_{j_{1}}^{\omega_{1}}(y_{1})V_{j_{2}}^{\omega_{2}}(y_{2})V_{j_{3}}^{\omega_{3}}(y_{3})\rangle=0\,.\end{aligned}

(69)

The structural similarity of these equations with those of the odd case in Eq. (
49
) is striking. This suggest that there must be a way to derive Eqs. (
67
), (
68
) and (
69
) directly from the
adjacent
covering maps along the lines of Sec.
3.1
. We will not attempt to do this here.

By solving Eqs. (
67
), (
68
) and (
69
), we find that, up to an overall constant, even parity correlators satisfying (
53
) take the form

\displaystyle\langle V_{j_{1}}^{\omega_{1}}(y_{1})V_{j_{2}}^{\omega_{2}}(y_{2})V_{j_{3}}^{\omega_{3}}(y_{3})\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{y_{2}}{a_{2}[\Gamma_{3}^{+}]}-\frac{y_{3}}{a_{3}[\Gamma_{2}^{+}]}+\frac{y_{2}y_{3}}{a_{2}[\Gamma_{3}^{-}]a_{3}[\Gamma_{2}^{+}]}\right)^{j_{1}-j_{2}-j_{3}}

(70)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{y_{1}}{a_{1}[\Gamma_{3}^{+}]}-\frac{y_{3}}{a_{3}[\Gamma_{1}^{+}]}+\frac{y_{1}y_{3}}{a_{1}[\Gamma_{3}^{-}]a_{3}[\Gamma_{1}^{+}]}\right)^{j_{2}-j_{3}-j_{1}}

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{y_{1}}{a_{1}[\Gamma_{2}^{+}]}-\frac{y_{2}}{a_{2}[\Gamma_{1}^{+}]}+\frac{y_{1}y_{2}}{a_{1}[\Gamma_{2}^{+}]a_{2}[\Gamma_{1}^{-}]}\right)^{j_{3}-j_{1}-j_{2}}.

As in the odd case, one can check that this is consistent with the conjectured expressions in Eq. (
28
) by using the relations between covering map coefficients and ratios of the numbers
P_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}
defined in (
31
). It is also straightforward to see that (
70
) matches all relevant limits of the corresponding odd cases in Eq. (
50
), as implied by the different series identifications in Eqs. (
58
) and (
60
). We also note that, upon using some identities among the
a_{i}
coefficients such as
a_{1}[\Gamma_{2}^{+}]a_{2}[\Gamma_{1}^{-}]=a_{1}[\Gamma_{2}^{-}]a_{2}[\Gamma_{1}^{+}]
, the solution in Eq. (
70
) manifestly enjoys bosonic exchange symmetry.

3.3
Edge cases

In this subsection, we consider spectral flow assignments lying at the “edge” of the inequalities displayed in Eqs. (
37
) and (
53
). More explicitly, we consider three-point functions with spectral flow charges satisfying either

\displaystyle\omega_{3}=\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}\quad\text{or}\quad\omega_{3}=\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}+1\,,\qquad\omega_{i}\geq 1\,,\,\forall\,i\,.

(71)

Following the nomenclature of the previous sections, we refer to these as the even and odd edge cases, respectively. Note that, according to the fusion rules in Eq. (
36
), these include all possibly non-vanishing correlators which were not included in sections
3.1
and
3.2
above, except for correlators with unflowed insertions, which will be discussed later on.

The treatment of the edge cases is slightly different from what we have discussed so far. Indeed, the general method based on series identifications employed in Sec.
3.2
breaks down when considering the even edge cases. We find that many of the coefficients in Eqs. (
64
), (
65
) and (
66
) become either divergent or indeterminate when
\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}=\omega_{3}
, related to the fact that three of the six adjacent maps cease to exist, namely those in (
59
). Although, in principle, the existence of the three maps in (
57
) could give enough constraints, in practice one runs into similar problems with divergent or indeterminate coefficients. As for the odd edge cases, it turns out that the associated covering map does not exist. Moreover, the former are only related to even edge cases by the SL(2,
\mathbb{R}
) series identifications.

We now show that alternative techniques involving current insertions can be used to derive the relevant differential equations satisfied by the
y
-basis edge correlators.
Some of these equations are easier to derive in the limit where the first two vertex operators collide. As discussed in
Dei:2021xgh
, one can take the vertex operators to be inserted at
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=(0,x,\infty)
, and then consider the limit
x\to 0
. More explicitly, we have
Dei:2021xgh

\displaystyle\braket{V^{\omega_{1}}_{j_{1}}(0,y_{1},0)V^{\omega_{2}}_{j_{2}}(x,y_{2},1)V^{\omega_{3}}_{j_{3}}(\infty,y_{3},\infty)}

\displaystyle=x^{-j_{1}-j_{2}+j_{3}+\frac{k}{2}(-\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}+\omega_{3})}\braket{V^{\omega_{1}}_{j_{1}}\left(0,\frac{y_{1}}{x},0\right)V^{\omega_{2}}_{j_{2}}\left(1,\frac{y_{2}}{x},1\right)V^{\omega_{3}}_{j_{3}}\left(\infty,y_{3}x,\infty\right)}\,.

(72)

The three-point functions that remain well-defined in this limit are those for which

|\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}-\omega_{3}|\leq 1\,,

(73)

including both edge cases in (
71
). In these instances, it is possible to obtain the correlator with vertex operators inserted at generic points
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
from the one in the colliding limit with
(x,x,x_{3})
by means of the global Ward identities.
We will derive some of the relevant differential equations satisfied by the edge correlators in the above collision limit, and only recover the full correlators at the end. Note that taking
x\to 0
and
x_{3}\to\infty
precisely corresponds to the limit in which these correlators can be interpreted as
m
-basis correlators of flowed primaries as in
Maldacena:2001km
;
Cagnacci:2013ufa
, where they were denoted as spectral flow
conserving
and spectral flow
violating
three-point functions, depending on the overall parity of the spectral flow charges.

To illustrate how this works, let us derive a constraint that will be satisfied by both edge cases. We set
x_{1}=x_{2}=x
and consider the integral

\oint_{{\cal{C}}}\braket{J^{3}(x,z)V^{\omega_{1}}_{j_{1}}(x,y_{1},z_{1})V^{\omega_{2}}_{j_{2}}(x,y_{2},z_{2})V^{\omega_{3}}_{j_{3}}(x_{3},y_{3},z_{3})}dz\,,

(74)

where
{\cal{C}}
denotes a contour encircling all three insertion points. This trivially vanishes as there is no residue at infinity. On the other hand, the action of the current on the vertex operators at
x
can be read off directly from Eq. (
21
), while for the one inserted at
x_{3}
we use

J^{3}(x,z)=J^{3}(x_{3},z)+(x_{3}-x)J^{+}(x_{3},z)\,,

(75)

which follows from (
9
). We obtain the following differential equation:

\displaystyle\left[\sum_{i=1}^{3}\left(y_{i}\partial_{y_{i}}+j_{i}+\frac{k}{2}\omega_{i}\right)+(x_{3}-x)\partial_{x_{3}}\right]\braket{V^{\omega_{1}}_{j_{1}}(x,y_{1},z_{1})V^{\omega_{2}}_{j_{2}}(x,y_{2},z_{2})V^{\omega_{3}}_{j_{3}}(x_{3},y_{3},z_{3})}=0\,.

(76)

We now Möbius-fix the worldsheet insertions to
(0,1,\infty)
, and further consider the limit
(x,x_{3})\to(0,\infty)
. The
y_{i}
coordinates then get rescaled according to Eq. (
25
).
This leads to

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\Big[y_{1}\partial_{y_{1}}+y_{2}\partial_{y_{2}}-y_{3}\partial_{y_{3}}+j_{1}+j_{2}-j_{3}\\
&\qquad+\frac{k}{2}(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}-\omega_{3})\Big]\braket{V^{\omega_{1}}_{j_{1}}(0,y_{1},0)V^{\omega_{2}}_{j_{2}}(0,y_{2},1)V^{\omega_{3}}_{j_{3}}(\infty,y_{3},\infty)}=0\,.\end{aligned}

(77)

We find that Eq. (
77
) is the
y
-basis version of the usual charge-conservation equation for
m
-basis three-point functions of spectrally flowed primaries. This holds for all correlators satisfying (
73
), including both edge cases.

3.3.1
Even edge cases

In this subsection we focus on the even edge cases, where
\omega_{3}=\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}
. We will derive the remaining two differential equations by considering correlators with an extra insertion of the
J^{-}(x,z)
current multiplied by the appropriate ratio of worldsheet coordinates. This is similar to what was used in
McElgin:2015eho
when discussing the proof of the
m
-basis spectral flow
violation
rules, and also more recently in
Iguri:2022pbp
in the context of the supersymmetric version of this model.

The integral

\oint_{{\cal{C}}}\braket{J^{-}(x_{3},z)V^{\omega_{1}}_{j_{1}}(x_{1},y_{1},z_{1})V^{\omega_{2}}_{j_{2}}(x_{2},y_{2},z_{2})V^{\omega_{3}}_{j_{3}}(x_{3},y_{3},z_{3})}\frac{(z-z_{1})^{\omega_{1}}(z-z_{2})^{\omega_{2}}}{(z-z_{3})^{\omega_{3}}}dz

(78)

vanishes since there is no pole at infinity. Using the OPEs of
J^{-}(x_{3},z)
with the vertex operators, this yields

\displaystyle\Big[x_{31}^{2}\frac{z_{12}^{\omega_{2}}}{z_{13}^{\omega_{3}}}\partial_{y_{1}}

\displaystyle+x_{32}^{2}\frac{z_{21}^{\omega_{1}}}{z_{23}^{\omega_{3}}}\partial_{y_{2}}+z_{31}^{\omega_{1}}z_{32}^{\omega_{2}}(y_{3}^{2}\partial_{y_{3}}+2j_{3}y_{3})\Big]

\displaystyle\braket{V^{\omega_{1}}_{j_{1}}(x_{1},y_{1},z_{1})V^{\omega_{2}}_{j_{2}}(x_{2},y_{2},z_{2})V^{\omega_{3}}_{j_{3}}(x_{3},y_{3},z_{3})}=0\,.

(79)

Proceeding similarly with

\oint_{{\cal{C}}}\braket{J^{-}(x,z)V^{\omega_{1}}_{j_{1}}(x,y_{1},z_{1})V^{\omega_{2}}_{j_{2}}(x,y_{2},z_{2})V^{\omega_{3}}_{j_{3}}(x_{3},y_{3},z_{3})}\frac{(z-z_{3})^{\omega_{3}}}{(z-z_{1})^{\omega_{1}}(z-z_{2})^{\omega_{2}}}dz\,,

(80)

where we have imposed the collision limit mentioned above, we find

\displaystyle\Big[\frac{z_{13}^{\omega_{3}}}{z_{12}^{\omega_{2}}}(y_{1}^{2}\partial_{y_{1}}+2j_{1}y_{1})

\displaystyle+\frac{z_{23}^{\omega_{3}}}{z_{21}^{\omega_{2}}}(y_{2}^{2}\partial_{y_{2}}+2j_{2}y_{2})+\frac{(x-x_{3})^{2}}{z_{31}^{\omega_{1}}z_{32}^{\omega_{2}}}\partial_{y_{3}}\Big]

\displaystyle\braket{V^{\omega_{1}}_{j_{1}}(x,y_{1},z_{1})V^{\omega_{2}}_{j_{2}}(x,y_{2},z_{2})V^{\omega_{3}}_{j_{3}}(x_{3},y_{3},z_{3})}=0\,.

(81)

We now fix the worldsheet coordinates to
(0,1,\infty)
while sending
x\to 0
and
x_{3}\to\infty
, and use Eq. (
25
) for the corresponding rescaling of the
y
variables. Including the charge conservation condition (
77
), the system of differential equations satisfied by the even edge correlator in the collision limit is then

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} 0&=\Big[j_{1}+j_{2}-j_{3}+y_{1}\partial_{y_{1}}+y_{2}\partial_{y_{2}}-y_{3}\partial_{y_{3}}\Big]\braket{\dots}\,,\\
0&=\Big[(-1)^{\omega_{1}}\partial_{y_{1}}+(-1)^{\omega_{3}}\partial_{y_{2}}+(y_{3}^{2}\partial_{y_{3}}+2j_{3}y_{3})\Big]\braket{\dots}\,,\\
0&=\Big[(-1)^{\omega_{1}}(y_{1}^{2}\partial_{y_{1}}+2j_{1}y_{1})+(-1)^{\omega_{3}}(y_{2}^{2}\partial_{y_{2}}+2j_{2}y_{2})+\partial_{y_{3}}\Big]\braket{\dots}\,.\end{aligned}

(82)

where
\braket{\dots}
stands for
\braket{V^{\omega_{1}}_{j_{1}}(0,y_{1},0)V^{\omega_{2}}_{j_{2}}(0,y_{2},1)V^{\omega_{3}}_{j_{3}}(\infty,y_{3},\infty)}
.
Note that only the second equation in (
82
) remains valid away from the collision limit.

Up to an overall
y
-independent constant, the general solution of the system (
82
) can be written as follows:

\displaystyle\braket{V^{\omega_{1}}_{j_{1}}(0,y_{1},0)V^{\omega_{2}}_{j_{2}}(0,y_{2},1)V^{\omega_{3}}_{j_{3}}(\infty,y_{3},\infty)}

(83)

\displaystyle=((-1)^{\omega_{1}}y_{1}-(-1)^{\omega_{3}}y_{2})^{j_{3}-j_{1}-j_{2}}(1+(-1)^{\omega_{3}}y_{2}y_{3})^{j_{1}-j_{2}-j_{3}}(1+(-1)^{\omega_{1}}y_{1}y_{3})^{j_{2}-j_{1}-j_{3}}\,.

This matches the result of
Dei:2021xgh
, see their Eq. (5.37b).

As mentioned above, for more general values of the insertion points the corresponding three-point functions follow from the global Ward identities. As it turns out, we can infer the result in a heuristic way by looking at the general expression given in Eq. (
70
). Indeed, one can verify that, upon setting
\omega_{3}=\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}
, the coefficients
a_{1}(\Gamma_{3}^{+}),a_{2}(\Gamma_{3}^{+})
and
a_{2}(\Gamma_{1}^{-})
diverge, which is a manifestation of the fact that the associated covering maps do not exist. Since all other coefficients remain finite, we obtain

\displaystyle\langle V_{j_{1}}^{\omega_{1}}(y_{1})V_{j_{2}}^{\omega_{2}}(y_{2})V_{j_{3}}^{\omega_{3}}(y_{3})\rangle

\displaystyle=\left(1+(-1)^{\omega_{1}}\frac{(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}-1)!}{(\omega_{1}-1)!\omega_{2}!}y_{3}+(-1)^{\omega_{3}}y_{2}y_{3}\right)^{j_{1}-j_{2}-j_{3}}

(84)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055pt\left(1+(-1)^{\omega_{1}+1}\frac{(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}-1)!}{\omega_{1}!(\omega_{2}-1)!}y_{3}+(-1)^{\omega_{1}}y_{1}y_{3}\right)^{j_{2}-j_{3}-j_{1}}

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055pt\left(1+(-1)^{\omega_{1}+1}\frac{\omega_{1}!\omega_{2}!}{(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2})!}((-1)^{\omega_{1}}y_{1}-(-1)^{\omega_{3}}y_{2})\right)^{j_{3}-j_{1}-j_{2}}.

Up to the normalisation, to be discussed below, this precisely matches the conjecture (
28
).

3.3.2
Odd edge cases

We now turn to the odd edge cases, where
\omega_{3}=\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}+1
. Since the procedure is analogous to what we just described we will skip some of the intermediate steps.

In addition to (
77
), we find two differential equations by considering contour integrals very similar to that in Eq. (
78
). We first take

\oint_{{\cal{C}}}\braket{J^{-}(x_{3},z)V^{\omega_{1}}_{j_{1}}(x_{1},y_{1},z_{1})V^{\omega_{2}}_{j_{2}}(x_{2},y_{2},z_{2})V^{\omega_{3}}_{j_{3}}(x_{3},y_{3},z_{3})}\frac{(z-z_{1})^{\omega_{1}+1}(z-z_{2})^{\omega_{2}}}{(z-z_{3})^{\omega_{3}}}dz\,,

(85)

which again vanishes due to the absence of a residue at infinity. The same holds for

\oint_{{\cal{C}}}\braket{J^{-}(x_{3},z)V^{\omega_{1}}_{j_{1}}(x_{1},y_{1},z_{1})V^{\omega_{2}}_{j_{2}}(x_{2},y_{2},z_{2})V^{\omega_{3}}_{j_{3}}(x_{3},y_{3},z_{3})}\frac{(z-z_{1})^{\omega_{1}}(z-z_{2})^{\omega_{2}+1}}{(z-z_{3})^{\omega_{3}}}dz\,.

(86)

Hence, we find the following system of differential equations:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} 0&=\Big[j_{1}+j_{2}-j_{3}-\frac{k}{2}+y_{1}\partial_{y_{1}}+y_{2}\partial_{y_{2}}-y_{3}\partial_{y_{3}}\Big]\braket{\dots}\,,\\
0&=\Big[(-1)^{\omega_{3}}\partial_{y_{2}}+(y_{3}^{2}\partial_{y_{3}}+2j_{3}y_{3})\Big]\braket{\dots}\,,\\
0&=\Big[(-1)^{\omega_{1}}\partial_{y_{1}}+(y_{3}^{2}\partial_{y_{3}}+2j_{3}y_{3})\Big]\braket{\dots}\,.\end{aligned}

(87)

where
\braket{\dots}
again denotes
\braket{V^{\omega_{1}}_{j_{1}}(0,y_{1},0)V^{\omega_{2}}_{j_{2}}(0,y_{2},1)V^{\omega_{3}}_{j_{3}}(\infty,y_{3},\infty)}
. In this case, only the first of these equations gets eventually modified away from the collision limit.

We find that, up to an overall normalization, the odd edge three-point functions read

\displaystyle\braket{V^{\omega_{1}}_{j_{1}}(0,y_{1},0)V^{\omega_{2}}_{j_{2}}(0,y_{2},1)V^{\omega_{3}}_{j_{3}}(\infty,y_{3},\infty)}

(88)

\displaystyle=y_{3}^{j_{1}+j_{2}-j_{3}-\frac{k}{2}}(1+(-1)^{\omega_{1}}y_{1}y_{3}+(-1)^{\omega_{3}}y_{2}y_{3})^{\frac{k}{2}-j_{1}-j_{2}-j_{3}}\,,

in the collision limit, thus matching the result in
Dei:2021xgh
, see their Eq. (5.37c). Moreover, as in the even edge case, we can infer the solution for generic insertion points from the general expression in Eq. (
51
). For this, we set
\omega_{3}=\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}+1
and carefully take the limit
a_{3}\to 0,\,a_{1,2}\to\infty
with the products
a_{1}a_{3}
and
a_{2}a_{3}
fixed. This yields

\displaystyle\braket{V^{\omega_{1}}_{j_{1}}(0,y_{1},0)V^{\omega_{2}}_{j_{2}}(1,y_{2},1)V^{\omega_{3}}_{j_{3}}(\infty,y_{3},\infty)}=y_{3}^{j_{1}+j_{2}-j_{3}-\frac{k}{2}}

(89)

\displaystyle\times\left(1+(-1)^{\omega_{1}+1}\frac{(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2})!}{\omega_{1}!\omega_{2}!}y_{3}+(-1)^{\omega_{1}}y_{1}y_{3}+(-1)^{\omega_{3}}y_{2}y_{3}\right)^{\frac{k}{2}-j_{1}-j_{2}-j_{3}}\,.

One can check that this matches the
y
-dependence given in the conjecture of Eq. (
27
) for correlators with appropriate spectral flow assignments. Moreover, upon using Eq. (
55
) and (
56
) we also see that, as expected, the expressions in Eq. (
70
) and Eq. (
89
) are related via series identifications.

3.4
Three-point functions with unflowed insertions

So far, we have considered three-point functions where all vertex operators had non-zero spectral flow charges. However, it is natural to expect that the above results include the special cases where some of the insertions are unflowed.
We now show how the latter are obtained. Note that we still assume
\omega_{3}\geq\omega_{1,2}
, as in the previous sections.

Let us start by discussing the case of a single unflowed insertion, namely
\omega_{1}=0
.
The fusion rules in Eq. (
36
) can then be satisfied iff
\omega_{3}=\omega_{2}
or
\omega_{3}=\omega_{2}+1
. These are exactly the two cases that were computed in
Cagnacci:2013ufa
in full generality from
m
-basis techniques. In this sense, the results presented in this section are not new, but we include them for completeness. Hence, the relevant correlators correspond to the spectral flow assignments
(0,\omega,\omega+1)
and
(0,\omega,\omega)
. By means of

V_{j}(x,z)={\cal{N}}\left(j\right)V_{\frac{k}{2}-j,h=j}^{1}(x,z)={\cal{N}}(j)\lim_{y\to\infty}y^{k-2j}V_{\frac{k}{2}-j}^{1}(x,y,z)\,,

(90)

which is a particular case of (
55
), these can be obtained from the three-point functions with charges
(1,\omega,\omega+1)
and
(1,\omega,\omega)
, respectively. More precisely, we have

\langle V_{j_{1}}V_{j_{2}}^{\omega}(y_{2})V_{j_{3}}^{\omega+1}(y_{3})\rangle={\cal{N}}(j_{1})\lim_{y_{1}\to\infty}y_{1}^{k-2j_{1}}\langle V_{\frac{k}{2}-j_{1}}^{1}(y_{1})V_{j_{2}}^{\omega}(y_{2})V_{j_{3}}^{\omega+1}(y_{3})\rangle\,,

(91)

and

\langle V_{j_{1}}V_{j_{2}}^{\omega}(y_{2})V_{j_{3}}^{\omega}(y_{3})\rangle={\cal{N}}(j_{1})\lim_{y_{1}\to\infty}y_{1}^{k-2j_{1}}\langle V_{\frac{k}{2}-j_{1}}^{1}(y_{1})V_{j_{2}}^{\omega}(y_{2})V_{j_{3}}^{\omega}(y_{3})\rangle\,,

(92)

where we have abbreviated
V_{j_{1}}(0,0)\equiv V_{j_{1}}
.

Focusing on (
91
), the RHS involves an even edge correlator. We thus need to consider the appropriate limit of Eq. (
84
), which gives

\langle V_{j_{1}}V_{j_{2}}^{\omega}(y_{2})V_{j_{3}}^{\omega+1}(y_{3})\rangle=y_{3}^{j_{1}+j_{2}-j_{3}-\frac{k}{2}}\left(1-y_{3}-(-1)^{\omega}y_{2}y_{3}\right)^{\frac{k}{2}-j_{1}-j_{2}-j_{3}}\,.

(93)

up to an overall constant. On the other hand, the RHS of (
92
) is obtained as the appropriate limit of the solution in Eq. (
50
), namely

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046pt\langle V_{j_{1}}^{1}(y_{1})V_{j_{2}}^{\omega}(y_{2})V_{j_{3}}^{\omega}(y_{3})\rangle=(y_{1}-\omega)^{j_{2}+j_{3}-j_{1}-\frac{k}{2}}(y_{2}+(-1)^{\omega})^{j_{1}+j_{3}-j_{2}-\frac{k}{2}}(y_{3}-1)^{j_{1}+j_{2}-j_{3}-\frac{k}{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046pt\times\Big((-1)^{\omega+1}(\omega+1)+(-1)^{\omega}y_{1}-y_{2}+(-1)^{\omega}y_{3}-(\omega-1)y_{2}y_{3}+y_{1}y_{2}y_{3}\Big)^{\frac{k}{2}-j_{1}-j_{2}-j_{3}}\,.

(94)

Hence, we get

\langle V_{j_{1}}V_{j_{2}}^{\omega}(y_{2})V_{j_{3}}^{\omega}(y_{3})\rangle=\left(y_{2}+(-1)^{\omega}\right)^{j_{3}-j_{1}-j_{2}}\left(y_{3}-1\right)^{j_{2}-j_{1}-j_{3}}\left((-1)^{\omega}+y_{2}y_{3}\right)^{j_{1}-j_{2}-j_{3}}\,.

(95)

up to the overall constant. Finally, we consider correlators with exactly two unflowed insertions,
\omega_{1}=\omega_{2}=0
. According to (
36
) this can only be non-trivial for
\omega_{3}=1
. By using again Eq. (
90
) we get

\displaystyle\langle V_{j_{1}}V_{j_{2}}V_{j_{3}}^{1}(y_{3})\rangle

\displaystyle={\cal{N}}(j_{2})\lim_{y_{2}\to\infty}y_{2}^{k-2j_{2}}\langle V_{j_{1}}V_{\frac{k}{2}-j_{2}}^{1}(y_{2})V_{j_{3}}^{1}(y_{3})\rangle

\displaystyle=y_{3}^{j_{1}+j_{2}-j_{3}-\frac{k}{2}}(y_{3}-1)^{\frac{k}{2}-j_{1}-j_{2}-j_{3}}\,,

(96)

where in the last line we have ignored an overall normalization factor. In this way, we match all the corresponding results of
Dei:2021xgh
, where the authors showed that this further reproduces the original computations of
Maldacena:2001km
;
Cagnacci:2013ufa
.

3.5
Normalization

So far we have focused on the dependence of the
y
-basis spectrally flowed correlators on the variables
y_{1}
,
y_{2}
and
y_{3}
, and shown that it matches precisely the predictions of
Dei:2021xgh
. We now describe how the overall normalizations in Eqs. (

)-(

) are obtained

5

This was already discussed in
Iguri:2022eat
, assuming the

. We reproduce the argument here for completeness

Once again, the argument relies on the SL(2,
\mathbb{R}
) series identifications. Indeed, identities such as those in Eqs. (
58
) and (

) must hold exactly, including the normalization factors. Having fixed the
y

Teschner:1999ug
;
Maldacena:2001km
. For instance, we consider the following identity:

\lim_{y_{3}\rightarrow\infty}y_{3}^{2j_{3}}\left\langle V^{\omega_{1}}_{j_{1}}(y_{1})\,V^{\omega_{2}}_{j_{2}}(y_{2})\,V^{\omega_{3}}_{j_{3}}(y_{3})\right\rangle={\cal{N}}(j_{3})\left\langle V^{\omega_{1}}_{j_{1}}(y_{1})\,V^{\omega_{2}}_{j_{2}}(y_{2})\,V^{\omega_{3}-1}_{\frac{k}{2}-j_{3}}(0)\right\rangle\,,

(97)

which will give us a recursion relation for
C_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}(j_{1},j_{2},j_{3})
. Since the latter is independent of the
y_{i}
, we can set
y_{1}=y_{2}=0
.
Using the
y
-dependence derived above, written as in Eqs. (

) and (

), one finds that the product of
X_{I}
factors on the left- an right-hand sides of (

), both reduce to either

P_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}^{j_{1}+j_{2}+j_{3}-k}\,P_{\boldsymbol{\omega}+e_{1}+e_{2}}^{j_{3}-j_{1}-j_{2}}\,P_{\boldsymbol{\omega}+e_{2}-e_{3}}^{j_{1}-j_{2}-j_{3}}\,P_{\boldsymbol{\omega}+e_{1}-e_{3}}^{j_{2}-j_{3}-j_{1}},

(98)

or

depending on the overall parity of the spectral flow charges. Consequently, in both cases we find that Eq. (

4
Discussion

In this paper, we have computed the
y
-basis string three-point function in AdS
3
involving vertex operators with arbitrary spectral flow charges. This provides a proof for the conjecture put forward recently in
Dei:2021xgh
, thus establishing integral expressions for all (primary) three-point functions of the SL(2,
\mathbb{R}
)-WZW model at level
k
, for all
k>3
.

The subfamily of (odd parity) correlators for which a holomorphic covering map from the worldsheet to the AdS
3
boundary exists had been obtained in
Eberhardt:2019ywk
;
Dei:2021xgh
. Here we have relied on the general structure of local Ward identities (in their
y
-basis formulation) and made extensive use of the SL(2,
\mathbb{R}
) series identifications, whose importance was recently highlighted in
Iguri:2022eat
. This allowed us to extend the methods based on covering maps to all other non-vanishing correlators, as defined by the fusion rules computed in
Maldacena:2001km
.

Our strategy can be summarised as follows. We first argued that the differential equations satisfied by all
y
-basis three-point functions must take the form given in Eq. (
54
). Obtaining the general expressions for these equations for all even parity correlators then reduces to computing all unknown coefficients in (
54
). We have provided the relations among correlators with adjacent spectral flow assignments that follow from SL(2,
\mathbb{R}
) series identifications in Eqs. (
58
) and (
60
). These provide a considerable number of identities between even and odd parity correlators in the limit where one of the
y
variables is taken to either zero or infinity. This allowed us to evaluate all relevant coefficients
A_{ij}
,
B_{ij}
and
C_{i}
in closed form, as given in Eqs. (
64
)-(
66
). The derivation of these 21 coefficients involves solving a total of 60 conditions, of which 39 can be taken as consistency checks. The latter turn out to be satisfied in a highly non-trivial manner, related to the existence of a set of identities relating the behaviour of different covering maps in the vicinity of the insertion points.

The resulting differential equations satisfied by even parity correlators are provided in Eqs. (
67
)-(
69
). These show a striking similarity with the cases of odd total spectral flow, a hallmark of the existence of a more direct derivation by means of
adjacent
covering maps. We leave this for future work. Here we have shown that the general solution to these equations, namely Eq. (
70
), is compatible with the proposal of
Dei:2021xgh
.

We have also discussed the so-called edge cases, whose spectral flow assignments saturate the fusion rules in Eqs. (
37
) and (
53
). Some subtleties arise when trying to apply the general method described above in this context. For these cases we have provided an alternative approach, based on an improved version of the
m
-basis methods
Maldacena:2001km
;
Cagnacci:2013ufa
. We have then described how to obtain correlators involving unflowed insertions. Finally, we fixed the overall normalization of all
y
-basis three-point functions, following the arguments of
Iguri:2022eat
.

At this point, it is natural to ask if an analogous story holds for four-point functions, which encode crucial dynamical information about the theory. A closed formula for four-point functions in the
y
-basis with arbitrary spectral flow assignments in terms of the corresponding unflowed correlator was conjectured in
Dei:2021yom
.
Here the situation is more subtle: on top of the four
y_{i}
variables, four-point functions also depend non-trivially on the worldsheet and spacetime cross-ratios, and must satisfy the corresponding
Knizhnik-Zamolodchikov equations. It has been known for some time
Minces:2005nb
;
Cagnacci:2015pka
that the latter intertwine non-trivially with the recursion relations of the type described in section
2.2
. If the structure put forward in
Dei:2021yom
is correct, its proof is likely to work in two steps. First, one should use arguments similar to those we have considered in this paper to show that solutions to the
y
-basis differential equations associated with the four-point functions consist of various prefactors given by powers of the generalized differences
X_{I}
, defined in Eq. (
29
), multiplied by an arbitrary function of the so-called generalized cross-ratio. Second, one should prove that this arbitrary function must satisfy the same Knizhnik-Zamolodchikov equation as the corresponding
unflowed
four-point function.
The extension of the proof for the case of correlation functions involving four spectrally-flowed vertex operators is a work in progress.

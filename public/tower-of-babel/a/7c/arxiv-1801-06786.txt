Weak Convergence Of Branched Conformal Immersions With Uniformly Bounded Areas And Willmore Energies
Guodong Wei

Abstract
. In this paper, we firstly extend Theorem 5.1.1 in [ 9 ] due to Hélein to a rescaled branched conformal immersed sequence(c.f. Theorem 1.5). By virtue of this local convergence theorem, we study the blowup behavior of a sequence of branched conformal immersions of closed Riemannian surface in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} with uniformly bounded areas and Willmore energies. Furthermore, we prove that the integral identity of Gauss curvature is true.

[
9
]
) which was established by F. Hélein and has played an important role in the analysis of 2-dimensional conformally invariant problems.

Let

\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}(D)=\left\{f\in C^{\infty}(\bar{D},\mathbb{R}^{n})|f\ \mbox{is a conformal immersion, and}\ \int_{D}|A_{f}|^{2}d\mu_{f}\leq\gamma\right\}.

Here
A_{f}
denotes the second fundamental form of the conformal immersion
f:D\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, where
D
is a disc in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Hélein’s theorem can be stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
9
]
)
.

Let
\overline{\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}(D)}
be the closure of
\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}(D)
with respect to the weak topology of
H^{1}(B,\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then, for all
\gamma\leq\frac{8\pi}{3}
, every map
f\in\overline{\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}(D)}
is either a constant map or a bi-Lipschitz conformal immersion.

The main motivation of the present paper is to study how does the topology change in the process of blowing up for a sequence of branched conformal immersions of a closed Riemann surface into
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with uniformly bounded area and Willmore energy. Before stating the main result, we need to introduce some notions.

For an immersion
f:\Sigma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
of a compact Riemannian surface, we denote by
g_{f}=f^{*}g_{euc}=df\otimes df
the pull-back metric,
\mu_{f}
the induced area measure on
\Sigma
and
\Delta_{g_{f}}
the Laplace-Beltrami operator. It is well known that

\Delta_{g_{f}}f=\overrightarrow{H_{f}},

(1.1)

where
\overrightarrow{H_{f}}
is the mean curvature vector corresponding to the immersion
f
. The Willmore functional (or Willmore energy) of
f
is defined by

W(f)=\frac{1}{4}\int_{\Sigma}|H_{f}|^{2}d\mu_{f}.

By the Gauss equation and the Gauss-Bonnet theorem, the Willmore functional can be also written as

W(f)=\frac{1}{4}\int_{\Sigma}|A_{f}|^{2}d\mu_{f}+\pi\chi(\Sigma),

(1.2)

where
\chi(\Sigma)
is the Euler characteristic of
\Sigma
. It is also well known that the Willmore functional
W(f)
is of the invariance under the conformal transformations of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(c.f.
[
2
,
26
]
).

Next, we recall another two important notions:
W^{2,2}
conformal immersion and
W^{2,2}
branched conformal immersion.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
\left(\Sigma,g\right)
be a Riemann surface. A map
f\in W^{2,2}\left((\Sigma,g),\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
is called a
W^{2,2}
conformal immersion if the induced metric
g_{f}=df\otimes df
is given by

g_{f}=\mathrm{e}^{2u}g\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\mbox{with}\,\,\,\,u\in\ L^{\infty}(\Sigma).

We say
f\in W^{2,2}_{conf,loc}\left((\Sigma,g),\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
if
f\in W^{2,2}_{loc}\left((\Sigma,g),\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
and
u\in L^{\infty}_{loc}(\Sigma)
.

Moreover, a map
f:\Sigma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called a
W^{2,2}
branched conformal immersion with possible branch points
x_{1},\cdots,x_{m}
if
f\in\ W^{2,2}_{conf,loc}(\Sigma\backslash\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{m}\},\mathbb{R}^{n})
with

\int_{\Sigma\backslash\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{m}\}}(1+|A_{f}|^{2})d\mu_{f}<\infty.

Given a Riemann surface
(\Sigma,g)
, we denote the set of all
W^{2,2}
conformal immersions and all
W^{2,2}
branched conformal immersions of
(\Sigma,g)
by
W^{2,2}_{conf}\left((\Sigma,g),\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
and
W^{2,2}_{b,c}((\Sigma,g),\mathbb{R}^{n})
respectively. Obviously,
W^{2,2}_{conf}((\Sigma,g),\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
W^{2,2}_{b,c}((\Sigma,g),\mathbb{R}^{n})
depend only on the conformal class of
(\Sigma,g)
, not on the choice of
g
.

Remark 1.3
.

We note that it follows from Theorem
2.4
(see it in Section 2) that every possible branch point
p\in\Sigma
of a
W^{2,2}
branched conformal immersion
f\in W^{2,2}_{b,c}((\Sigma,g),\mathbb{R}^{n})
has a well-defined branching order
m(p)\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

Here we make a convention that
\Sigma_{k}=(\Sigma,h_{k})
denotes a Riemann surface which is equipped a smooth metric
h_{k}
with constant Gauss curvature
\pm 1
, or
(\Sigma,h_{k})
is just a torus of genus 1 with Gauss curvature
0
.

One pays attention to conformal immersions because of the conformal immersions are of two advantages. One is that the conformal diffeomorphism group of
(\Sigma,h_{k})
is rather small comparing with the group of diffeomorphisms. The other is: if
g_{f}=e^{2u}g_{euc}
in an isothermal coordinate chart, then one can estimate
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}
from the compensated compactness property of
K_{f}e^{2u}
. Thus it is possible to get an upper bound of
\|f\|_{W^{2,2}}
via the equation
\Delta_{g_{f}}f=H_{f}
.

Here we want to list some local convergence results. When
m=0
, the following theorem is due to Hélein with
\gamma_{n}=\frac{8\pi}{3}
(c.f. Theorem
1.1
) and later, Kuwert and Li proved
\gamma_{n}
is the optimal constant(c.f. Theorem 5.1 in
[
11
]
); In the case
m\geq 1
, the theorem was proved by Lamm and Nguyen (c.f. Theorem 3.2 in
[
14
]
).

Theorem 1.4

(
[
14
]
)
.

Let
f_{k}\in W^{2,2}_{conf,loc}(D\backslash\{0\},\ \mathbb{R}^{n})
be a sequence of branched conformal immersions with branching order m at
0
. Denote the induced metric of
f_{k}
by
g_{f_{k}}=e^{2u_{k}}g_{euc}
(i.e.
u_{k}(z)=m\log|z|+\omega_{k}(z)
). Assume that
\mu_{f_{k}}(D)\leq C
,
f_{k}(0)=0
and

\int_{D}|A_{f_{k}}|^{2}d\mu_{f_{k}}\leq\gamma<\gamma_{n}=\left\{\begin{aligned} 8\pi\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\mbox{if}\,\,\,\,n=3,\\
4\pi\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\mbox{if}\,\,\,\,n\geq 4.\end{aligned}\right.

Then
f_{k}
is also bounded in
W^{2,2}_{loc}(D,\mathbb{R}^{n})
and there is a subsequence such that one of the following two alternatives holds:

(1)

f_{k}
converge weakly in
W^{2,2}_{loc}(D,\mathbb{R}^{n})
to a conformal immersion
f
with branching order
m
at
0
, i.e.,
g_{f}=|z|^{2m}e^{\omega(z)}\delta_{ij}
, moreover,

\|\omega_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(D_{r}(0))}+\|D\omega_{k}\|_{L^{2}(D_{r}(0))}\leq C(r).

(2)

\omega_{k}\rightarrow-\infty
and
f_{k}\rightarrow 0
locally uniformly on
D
.

Here, we should mention that Kuwert and Li
[
11
]
discussed the compactness for a sequence of conformal immersions of a compact Riemann surface. More precisely, they proved that, if
f_{k}\in W^{2,2}_{conf}(\Sigma_{k},\mathbb{R}^{n})
are conformal immersions with
W(f_{k})<\Lambda<\infty
, and
\Sigma_{k}
converge to
\Sigma
in moduli space, then there exist Möbius transformations
\sigma_{k}
and diffeomorphisms
\phi_{k}
, such that
\sigma_{k}\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}f_{k}\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}\phi_{k}
converge to
f_{0}
locally in weak
W^{2,2}
sense on
\Sigma
minus finitely many concentration points and the weak limit
f_{0}
is a
W^{2,2}
branched conformal immersion. Moreover,
f_{0}
is unbranched and an embedding if
\Lambda<8\pi
. Riviére
[
22
]
also proved the same results. With the additional condition
\mu_{f_{k}}(\Sigma_{k})\leq C
, furthermore Chen and Li
[
3
]
studied the Hausdorff limit of
f_{k}(\Sigma)
and proved the area identity.

Eschenburg and Tribuzy
[
7
]
have ever studied the Gauss-Bonnet formula for branched conformal immersions. Following the work
[
11
]
,
[
22
]
and
[
3
]
, naturally, one wants to know whether the integral of Gauss curvature is maintained after blowing up? For the present problem, we can not neglect the bubbles which shrink to a point. This is quite different from the case in
[
3
]
, there the bubbles shrinking to a point can be directly ignored when one discussed the Horsdorff limit and area identity. The main reason is that Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.4
does not guarantee the limit is an immersion. For example, let
S^{2}
be the standard unit two-sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and then define a sequence of conformal immersions
f_{k}:S^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}
by
f_{k}(x)=\frac{x}{k}
, then for each
k
, it is easy to check that

\int_{S^{2}}K_{f_{k}}d\mu_{f_{k}}=4\pi\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\int_{S^{2}}|A_{f_{k}}|^{2}d\mu_{f_{k}}=8\pi.

(1.3)

However, the limit of
f_{k}
is a point map.

Based on the above consideration we need to study those bubbles which carry nontrivial topology but shrink to a point. In order to recover the lost curvature integral at these bubbles, a natural attempt is to rescale
f_{k}
by an appropriate constant to make the limit nontrivial. Fortunately, by employing the compensated compactness property of
K_{f_{k}}e^{2u_{k}}
(c.f.
[
20
,
9
,
11
,
22
]
), Simon’s monotonicity formula (c.f.
[
25
]
) and Theorem
1.4
we obtain the following theorem which can be viewed as an extension of Hélein’s local convergence theorem, i.e., Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
f_{k}:D\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a sequence of
W^{2,2}
branched conformal immersions with branching order m at
0
which satisfies

(1)

\int_{D}|A_{f_{k}}|^{2}d\mu_{f_{k}}\leq\gamma<\gamma_{n}=\left\{\begin{aligned} 8\pi\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\mbox{ if }\,\,\,\,n=3,\\
4\pi\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\mbox{ if }\,\,\,\,n\geq 4.\end{aligned}\right.

(2)

f_{k}(D)
can be extended to a closed branched immersed surface
f_{k}:\Sigma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
with

\int_{\Sigma}|A_{f_{k}}|^{2}d\mu_{f_{k}}<\Lambda.

Take a curve
\gamma:[0,1]\longrightarrow D
and set
\lambda_{k}=\mbox{diam}f_{k}(\gamma)
, then we can find a subsequence of
\{f_{k}^{\prime}=\frac{f_{k}-f_{k}(\gamma(0))}{\lambda_{k}}\}
which converges weakly in
W^{2,2}_{loc}(D)
to a
W^{2,2}
branched conformal immersion
f_{0}
with branching order m at
0
. Moreover, there holds true

\|\omega_{k}^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(D_{r}(0))}\leq C(r),

where
\omega_{k}^{\prime}
satisfies

df_{k}^{\prime}\otimes df_{k}^{\prime}=e^{2(\omega_{k}^{\prime}+m\log|z|)}(dx^{1}\otimes dx^{1}+dx^{2}\otimes dx^{2}).

Combining Theorem
1.4
with Theorem
1.5
, we can give a bubble tree construction for
W^{2,2}
conformal branched immersions with uniformly bounded Willmore energies and areas. Moreover, we obtain the following integral identity of Gauss curvature .

Theorem 1.6
.

Suppose that
\{f_{k}\}
is a sequence of
W^{2,2}
branched conformal immersions of closed Riemann surface
(\Sigma,g)
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
which have the same branch points and same branching order at each branch point. Assume
f_{k}(\Sigma)\cap B_{R_{0}}(0)\neq\emptyset
for some
R_{0}>0
and

\sup_{k}\{\mu(f_{k})+W(f_{k})\}<+\infty.

Then, away from a finite set,
f_{k}
will converge either to some
\widetilde{f}_{0}\in W^{2,2}_{b,c}((\Sigma,g),\mathbb{R}^{n})
or to a point. Moreover, we obtain the following Gauss curvature identity:

2\pi\chi(\Sigma)+2\pi b=\int_{\Sigma}K_{f_{0}}d\mu_{f_{0}}+\sum_{i}\int_{\mathbb{C}}K_{f^{i}}d\mu_{f^{i}},

where
b
is the sum of the branching order of all branch points,
f_{0}
is the generalized limit of
f_{k}
(c.f. Definition
3.2
in section
3
) and
f^{1},\cdots,f^{m}
are bubbles.

Next, we consider the convergence behavior of a sequence of branched conformal immersions of
\Sigma_{k}
with varied complex structures and varied branch points. Let
\{f_{k}\}
be a sequence of
W^{2,2}
branched conformal immersions from
\Sigma_{k}
with branch points
\{x_{k,1},\cdots,x_{k,m}\}
. If
\{f_{k}\}
has the same branching order at
x_{k,j}
for
j=1,\cdots,m
and
k\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, and

(\Sigma_{k},x_{k,1},\cdots,x_{k,m})\rightarrow(\Sigma_{0},x_{0,1},\cdots,x_{0,m})\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\mbox{in}\,\,\,\,\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,m},

then we have the following theorem.

Theorem 1.7
.

With the notations as above, assume
f_{k}(\Sigma)\cap B_{R_{0}}(0)\neq\emptyset
and

\sup_{k}\{\mu(f_{k})+W(f_{k})\}<+\infty.

Then we have

2\pi\chi(\Sigma)+2\pi b=\sum_{i}^{s}\int_{{\Sigma_{0}^{i}}}K_{f_{0}^{i}}d\mu_{f_{0}^{i}}+\sum_{j}\int_{\mathbb{C}}K_{f^{j}}d\mu_{f^{j}},

where
\Sigma_{0}^{i}
is the component of
\Sigma_{0}
minus nodal points,
b
is the sum of the branching order of the corresponding branch points,
f_{0}^{i}
is the generalized limit of
f_{k}\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}\psi_{k}
on
\Sigma_{0}^{i}
where
\psi_{k}
is a map from
\Sigma_{0}^{i}
to
(\Sigma,h_{k})
, and
f^{1},\cdots,f^{m}
are bubbles.

Remark 1.8
.

By the theory of muduli space of Riemannian surface with marked points (for example, see proposition 5.1 on page 71 in
[

), for each sufficiently large

\psi_{k}:\Sigma_{0}\backslash\mathcal{N}\rightarrow\Sigma_{k}\backslash\Gamma_{k}
, where
\mathcal{N}
denotes the nadal points on
\Sigma_{0}
,
\Gamma_{k}
is a set constituting pairwise disjoint, simple closed curves in
(\Sigma,h_{k})

with varied complex structures and varied branch points, we firstly consider the convergence behavior of
f_{k}\mathchoice{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.86108pt}{$\scriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}{\mathrel{\raisebox{0.43057pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$}}}\psi_{k}

2.
Preliminary

In this section, we will recall some previous works which will be used in our paper.

Given a map
f\in W^{2,2}_{b,c}((\Sigma,g),\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then, for any
p\in\Sigma
, by Theorem
2.4
we may choose a isothermal coordinate system around
p
such that the induced metric
g_{ij}=\langle\partial_{i}f,\partial_{j}f\rangle
can be given by

g_{ij}=e^{2u}g_{ij}=|z|^{2m}e^{2\omega}\delta_{ij}\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\mbox{where}\,\,\,\,\omega\in C^{0}\cap W^{1,2}_{loc}(U),

where
m
is the branching order of
p
(
p
is unbranched iff
m=0
). Moreover,
u
satisfies the following weak Liouville type equation

-\Delta u\ =K_{f}\mathrm{e}^{2u}-2m\pi\delta_{p}.

By assumption
K_{f}\mathrm{e}^{2u}
belongs to
L^{1}
, it seems that the classical elliptic equation theory can not be used here. In
[
20
]
Müller and Ŝverák discovered that this term can be written as a sum of Jacobi determinants, thus they found that the function
K_{g}e^{2u}
actually belongs to the Hardy space
\mathcal{H}^{1}
by
[
4
]
. Kuwert and Li in
[
11
]
summed up the above as the following result.

Theorem 2.1

(Corollary 2.1 in
[
11
]
)
.

For
f\in W^{2,2}_{conf}(D,\ \mathbb{R}^{n})
with the induced metric
g_{ij}=e^{2u}\delta_{ij},
assume

\int_{D}|A_{f}|^{2}d\mu_{f}\leq\gamma<\gamma_{n}=\left\{\begin{aligned} 8\pi\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\mbox{ if }n=3,\\
4\pi\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\mbox{ if }n\geq 4.\end{aligned}\right.

Then there exists a function
\upsilon:\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
solving the following equation

-\Delta\upsilon=K_{g}e^{2u}\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\mbox{in}\,\,\,\,D,

and satisfying the estimates

\|\upsilon\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{C})}+\|D\upsilon\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{C})}\leq C(\gamma)\int_{D}|A_{f}|^{2}d\mu_{f}.

Remark 2.2
.

With the same condition as above, since
u-v
is a harmonic function, it is easy to see that

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(D_{r})}+\|Du\|_{L^{2}(D_{r})}\leq C(\gamma,r)(\gamma+\|u\|_{L^{1}(D)}).

Let
\Omega^{*}_{r}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|>r\}
. A metric on
\Omega^{*}_{r}
is complete at

if there exists some
z_{0}\in\Omega^{*}_{r}
such that

when

. Müller and Ŝverák
[

]
studied this type surface and obtained the following theorem:

Theorem 2.3

(
[
20
]
, Theorem 4.2.1)
.

Suppose that
f\in W^{2,2}_{conf,loc}(\mathbb{C}\backslash D_{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})
with

\int_{\mathbb{C}\backslash D_{R}}|A_{f}|^{2}d\mu_{f}<\infty,

where
g_{ij}=e^{2u}\delta_{ij}
is the induced metric. If
f(\mathbb{C}\backslash D_{R})
is complete, then

u(z)=m\log|z|+\omega(z),

where
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
\omega\in C^{0}\cap W^{1,2}(\mathbb{C}\backslash D_{R},\,\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Moreover

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\partial D_{t}}\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}=2m\pi.

For a conformal immersion from a punctured disk, Kuwert and Li
[
11
]
proved the following removable singularity result.

Theorem 2.4

(
[
11
]
)
.

Suppose that
f\in W^{2,2}_{loc}(D\backslash\{0\},\ \mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfies

\int_{D\backslash\{0\}}|A_{f}|^{2}d\mu_{f}<\infty\,\,\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,\,\,\mu_{f}(D\backslash\{0\})<\infty,

where

is the induced metric. Then

and

u(z)=m\log|z|+\omega(z)\ \ \ \mbox{where}\ m\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\ \ \omega\in C^{0}\cap W^{1,2}(D,\ \mathbb{R}^{n}),

-\Delta u\ =\ -2m\pi\delta_{0}+K_{g}e^{2u}\ \ \ \mbox{in}\,\,\,\,D.

Moreover, the multiplicity of
f(D_{\delta}(0))
as varifold at

is
m+1
.

The number

in the above Theorem

Remark 2.5
.

It is worthwhile pointing out that if

satisfies the same conditions as Theorem

(1)

\lim\limits_{z\rightarrow 0}\frac{|f(z)-f(0)|}{|z|^{m+1}}=\frac{e^{\omega(0)}}{m+1}.

(2)

Integrating

on

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\int_{\partial D_{t}}\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}=2m\pi.

At the end of this section, we recall the Gauss-Bonnet formula for conformal immersion with branch points.

Theorem 2.6

(
[
3
,
7
]
)

Let

3.
Generalized Hélein’s convergence theorem

3.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.5
:

Firsty, note that
f_{k}^{\prime}(\gamma)\subset B_{1}(0)
. Without loss of generality, we assume
\gamma(0)=0
. Put

\Sigma_{k}^{\prime}=\frac{f_{k}(\Sigma)-f_{k}(\gamma(0))}{\lambda_{k}},

then we need to consider the following two cases:

Case
(1)
:
\mbox{diam}f_{k}^{\prime}(D)<C
. For this case, by inequality
(1.3)
in
[
25
]
(for more general varifold case we refer to the appendix A in
[
13
]
) with
\rho=\infty
we have that

\frac{\mu\{\Sigma_{k}^{\prime}\cap B_{\sigma}(0)\}}{\sigma^{2}}\leq C

for any
\sigma>0
. Hence we get
\mu_{f_{k}^{\prime}}(D)<C
by taking
\sigma=\mbox{diam}f_{k}^{\prime}(D)
. Then, it follows from Theorem
1.4
that
f_{k}^{\prime}
converges weakly in
W^{2,2}_{loc}(D)
. Since
\mbox{diam}f_{k}^{\prime}(\gamma)=1
, the weak limit is not trivial.

Case
(2)
:
\mbox{diam}f_{k}^{\prime}(D)\rightarrow\infty
. As shown in
[
11
]
(page 328), there exist a point
y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a constant

It follow from (

Suppose

When

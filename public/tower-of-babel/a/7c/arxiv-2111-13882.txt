Gravitational wave memory of the binary black hole events in GWTC-2
Zhi-Chao Zhao, Xiaolin Liu, Zhoujian Cao, Xiaokai He

Abstract
Gravitational wave (GW) memory is an important prediction of general relativity. Existing works on the GW memory detection focus on the waveform analysis. It is hard for waveform analysis method to detect the GW memory due to its quasi-direct current behavior and weakness. We implement a completely different scheme in this work to estimate the GW memory. In this scheme, we firstly apply the Bondi-Metzner-Sachs method to calculate the GW memory of binary black hole based on numerical relativity simulation. Then we construct a surrogate model to relate binary black hole’s parameters and the GW memory. Afterwards we apply this surrogate model together with Bayesian techniques to estimate the GW memory of the 48 binary black hole events recorded in GWTC-2. The GW memory corresponding to the all 48 events has been estimated. The most interesting results are for GW190814. The corresponding GW memory is about − 1 × 10 − 23 -1\times 10^{-23} and 1 × 10 − 23 1\times 10^{-23} for Hanford detector and Livingston detector respectively. At the same time we find with 3 σ \sigma C.L. that the memory strain of GW190814 is negative on Hanford detector while positive on Livingston detector.



I
Introduction

The memory of gravitational wave (GW) was firstly found by Zeldovich, Braginsky, Thorne and their coworkers
Zel’dovich and Polnarev 1974
;
Payne 1983
;
Braginsky and Grishchuk 1985
;
Braginsky and
Thorne 1987
. This kind of GW memory is produced by the change of a quadrupole moment for slowly moving sources. Christodoulou found that gravitational wave itself can also produce memory
Christodoulou 1991
;
Frauendiener 1992
. This kind of memory is usually called nonlinear memory. The GW memory detection
Yang and Martynov 2018
;
Hübner et al. 2020
;
Boersma et al. 2020
may be used to study the gravitational theory
Du and Nishizawa 2016
and spacetime dimension
Hollands et al. 2017
. Several works in the past years
Seto 2009
;
Van Haasteren and Levin 2010
;
Pshirkov et al. 2010
;
Cordes and Jenet 2012
;
Madison et al. 2014
;
Arzoumanian et al. 2015
;
Lasky et al. 2016
;
McNeill et al. 2017
;
Divakarla et al. 2020
have investigated the possibility of detecting the nonlinear memory. All of the works focused on the waveform analysis. Because the GW memory behaves mainly as a quasi-direct current signal, detector responses to it weakly. Such fact makes GW memory detection hard.

In this paper, we implement an alternative method to investigate the GW memory of the binary black hole merger events recorded by LIGO and VIRGO. Firstly we design a Bondi-Metzner-Sachs method to calculate the GW memory based on numerical relativity simulation of binary black holes. Then we apply this method to the SXS catalogue
Caltech-Cornell-CITA
and construct a database of binary black hole intrinsic parameters and the corresponding GW memory due to the gravitational radiation. Based on such a database we use Gaussian process regression to construct a surrogate model describing the relationship between the binary black hole’s parameters and the gravitational wave memory. Afterwards we apply such surrogate model together with Bayesian techniques to infer the GW memory of the 48 binary black hole (BBH) events in GWTC-2. We can well estimate the GW memory of these BBH events by the use of parameters’ samples based on the analysis of GW waveforms. And our analysis indicates that our estimation admits high confidence.

Our work is similar but different to that of
Khera et al. 2021
. Ref.
Khera et al. 2021
only studied the spherical harmonic mode
h_{20}
while we investigate the detector’s expected response to the GW memory.
Mode
h_{20}
only depends on GW source’s intrinsic parameters while the detector’s response depends also on extrinsic parameters. But only the detector’s response is observable and measurable for GW experiment.

In the next section we describe the Bondi-Metzner-Sachs (BMS) method of GW memory calculation used in the current work
Liu et al. 2021
. Then we combine the BMS method and numerical relativity simulations to construct a surrogate model of GW memory for BBHs. After that we apply our surrogate model to GW memory estimation of the binary black hole events in GWTC-2 of LIGO. Finally we give a summary and a discussion.

II
Method of gravitational wave memory calculation

The Newmann-Penrose components of Weyl tensor
\Psi_{\mu},\mu=0,...,4
admit the following relation in the wave zone
Bondi et al. 1962
;
Sachs 1962
;
Penrose and Rindler 1988

\displaystyle\dot{\Psi}_{2}=\eth\Psi_{3}+\sigma\Psi_{4},\,\,\Psi_{3}=-\eth\dot{\bar{\sigma}},\,\,\Psi_{4}=-\ddot{\bar{\sigma}}.

(1)

Here
\sigma
corresponds to the shear of the
(\theta,\phi)
coordinate sphere in the Bondi-Sachs coordinate
He and Cao 2015
;
He et al. 2016
;
Sun et al. 2019
. The overbar means complex conjugate. The
\eth
operator is related to the sphere geometry. The overdot means the time derivative. The shear
\sigma
is related to the gravitational wave strain through

\displaystyle\sigma=\frac{D}{2}\left(h_{+}+ih_{\times}\right),

(2)

where
D
is the luminosity distance between the observer and the source,
h_{+}
and
h_{\times}
correspond to the two polarization modes of the gravitational wave.
The relations (
1
) result in

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\Psi_{2}+\bar{\sigma}\dot{\sigma})=|\dot{\sigma}|^{2}-\eth^{2}\dot{\bar{\sigma}}+\bar{\sigma}\ddot{\sigma}-\sigma\ddot{\bar{\sigma}}.

(3)

We can use spin-weighted
-2
spherical harmonic functions to decompose the gravitational wave strain
h\equiv h_{+}-ih_{\times}
as following
Buonanno et al. 2007
;
Brügmann et al. 2008
;
Cao et al. 2008

\displaystyle h(t,\theta,\phi)\equiv\sum_{l=2}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-l}^{l}h_{lm}(t)Y_{-2lm}(\theta,\phi),

(4)

where
Y_{slm}
means spin-weighted
s
spherical harmonic function. Plug the above decomposition into Eq. (
3
) we get

\displaystyle h_{lm}\bigg|_{-\infty}^{+\infty}=-\sqrt{\frac{(l-2)!}{(l+2)!}}\left[\left.\frac{4}{D}\int\Psi_{2}Y_{0l0}\sin\theta d\theta d\phi\right|_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\right.-

\displaystyle D\sum_{l^{\prime}=2}^{\infty}\sum_{l^{\prime\prime}=2}^{\infty}\sum_{m^{\prime}=-l^{\prime}}^{l^{\prime}}\sum_{m^{\prime\prime}=-l^{\prime\prime}}^{l^{\prime\prime}}\Gamma_{l^{\prime}l^{\prime\prime}lm^{\prime}-m^{\prime\prime}0}\times

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\left.\left(\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\dot{h}_{l^{\prime}m^{\prime}}\dot{\bar{h}}_{l^{\prime\prime}m^{\prime\prime}}dt-\dot{h}_{l^{\prime}m^{\prime}}\bar{h}_{l^{\prime\prime}m^{\prime\prime}}\bigg|_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\right)\right].

(5)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{l^{\prime}l^{\prime\prime}lm^{\prime}-m^{\prime\prime}-m}\equiv

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\int Y_{-2l^{\prime}m^{\prime}}\overline{Y}_{-2l^{\prime\prime}m^{\prime\prime}}\cdot\overline{Y}_{0lm}\sin\theta d\theta d\phi.

(6)

Now we decompose
h_{lm}=h^{\rm osc}_{lm}+h^{\rm mem}_{lm}
where
h^{\rm osc}_{lm}
is the oscillation part which means
h^{\rm osc}_{lm}(-\infty)=h^{\rm osc}_{lm}(+\infty)=0
and
h^{\rm mem}_{lm}
is the memory part which means
\dot{h}^{\rm mem}_{lm}\approx 0
due to the quasi-direct current (DC) behavior of the GW memory
Favata 2009a
. Then the above Eq. (
5
) becomes

\displaystyle h^{\rm mem}_{lm}\bigg|_{-\infty}^{+\infty}=-\sqrt{\frac{(l-2)!}{(l+2)!}}\left[\left.\frac{4}{D}\int\Psi_{2}Y_{0l0}\sin\theta d\theta d\phi\right|_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\right.-

\displaystyle D\sum_{l^{\prime}=2}^{\infty}\sum_{l^{\prime\prime}=2}^{\infty}\sum_{m^{\prime}=-l^{\prime}}^{l^{\prime}}\sum_{m^{\prime\prime}=-l^{\prime\prime}}^{l^{\prime\prime}}\Gamma_{l^{\prime}l^{\prime\prime}lm^{\prime}-m^{\prime\prime}0}\times

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\left.\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\dot{h}^{\rm osc}_{l^{\prime}m^{\prime}}\dot{\bar{h}}^{\rm osc}_{l^{\prime\prime}m^{\prime\prime}}dt\right].

(7)

If we take the mass center frame of the BBH system at the past infinity time as the inertial frame, we have
\Psi_{2}(-\infty,\theta,\phi)=M
. Here
M
corresponds to the BBH’s initial total mass (Bondi mass)
Ashtekar et al. 2020
. At the future infinity time, the BBH’s total mass
M^{\prime}=M-E_{\rm GW}
measured in the above inertial frame is smaller than the initial value
M
because the gravitational wave carries away some energy
E_{\rm GW}
. The spacetime will settle down to a Kerr black hole with mass
\tilde{M}
at the future infinity time. But importantly the mass center frame at the future infinity time is different to the above inertial frame corresponding to the mass center frame at the past infinity time due to the kick velocity. These two inertial frames corresponding to the mass center frame at past infinity time and the mass center frame at the future infinity time are related by a boost transformation described by the kick velocity. Consequently
\tilde{M}=M^{\prime}/\gamma
, where
\gamma
is the Lorentz factor. So corresponding to the Eq. (
5
) we have
Ashtekar et al. 2020

\displaystyle\Psi_{2}(+\infty,\theta,\phi)=-\frac{\tilde{M}}{\gamma^{3}}\times

\displaystyle\left(1-v_{x}\sin\theta\cos\phi-v_{y}\sin\theta\sin\phi-v_{z}\cos\theta\right)^{-3},

(8)

\displaystyle\gamma=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-v^{2}}},

(9)

where
v_{x}
,
v_{y}
and
v_{z}
are the Cartesian components of the kick velocity
v
.

Since the gravitational wave energy
E_{\rm GW}
, the kick velocity and the oscillation part
h^{\rm osc}_{lm}
have already been accurately obtained by numerical relativity simulation
Mitman et al. 2021
, we can plug them into the Eq. (
7
) to calculate the gravitational memory
\left.h^{\rm mem}_{lm}\right|_{-\infty}^{+\infty}
. In Ref.
Liu et al. 2021
we investigated the waveform of GW memory. Differently here we care about the overall GW memory of BBH coalescence.

In Fig.
1
we compare our calculation results

\displaystyle h^{t}_{lm}\equiv\left.\frac{D}{M}h^{\rm mem}_{lm}\right|_{-\infty}^{+\infty}

(10)

for spin aligned equal mass BBH systems based on SXS simulations to the numerical relativity results by direct calculation in
Pollney and Reisswig 2011
.
Here
h^{t}_{lm}
refers the “intrinsic” memory and it is not an observable quantity. However, after multiplying the factor
\frac{D}{M}
and
projecting to a detector, it will be observable(see Sec. III).
In addition the recent memory calculation results by SXS group
Mitman et al. 2021
are also shown in the figure for comparison. Following the convention of
Pollney and Reisswig 2011
we use the effective spin
\chi_{\rm eff}\equiv\frac{s_{1z}m_{1}+s_{2z}m_{2}}{m_{1}+m_{2}}
as the horizontal axis
, where
s_{1z,2z}
and
m_{1,2}
denotes the spins’
z
-component and component masses of the two black holes. The perfect consistency indicates the reliability of our method for GW memory calculation. For these spin aligned BBHs, we confirm previous approximation that
h^{\rm mem}_{lm}\approx 0,m\neq 0
and
h^{\rm osc}_{l0}\approx 0
. For precession BBHs, our results got through the Bondi-Metzner-Sachs method are also consistent to that of SXS results
Mitman et al. 2021
like Fig.
1
.

Favata 2009b

\displaystyle h^{\rm mem}

\displaystyle=\frac{M}{D}\Re[(F^{+}(\theta,\phi,\psi)+iF^{\times}(\theta,\phi,\psi))\times

\displaystyle\sum_{l=2}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-l}^{l}h^{t}_{lm}Y_{-2lm}(\iota,\beta)]

\displaystyle F^{+}(\theta,\phi,\psi)

\displaystyle\equiv-\frac{1}{2}(1+\cos^{2}\theta)\cos 2\phi\cos 2\psi

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,-\cos\theta\sin 2\phi\sin 2\psi,

(12)

\displaystyle F^{\times}(\theta,\phi,\psi)

\displaystyle\equiv+\frac{1}{2}(1+\cos^{2}\theta)\cos 2\phi\sin 2\psi

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,-\cos\theta\sin 2\phi\cos 2\psi,

(13)

V
The effect of waveform model on the estimation of gravitational wave memory

We surely know that the gravitational waveform model may affect the parameters estimation. People call such effect the “systematic bias of the waveform template
Lindblom and Cutler 2016
;
Pürrer and Haster 2020
. The most typical example is GW190521. Significant difference shows up among SEOBNR waveform families, IMRPhenom waveform families and NRsurrogate waveform model. These three waveform families are the most advanced waveform templates available to gravitational wave data analysis. The reported analysis results by LSC have already counted the waveform accuracy issue. In addition, LSC has also considered the waveform models combination and prior distribution of related parameters based on detail astrophysical issues. Since the parameters estimation done by LSC group is extremely delicate, the posterior samples given by LSC are the most reasonable start point for the application of our technique to the LIGO GW events. This is the guide idea for the GW memory estimation done in the above section.

But it is still interesting to ask how the waveform model and the prior distribution of related parameters affect the GW memory estimation. We do such investigation here.

V.1

In the GWTC-2 paper

instead of using the combined posterior. The results are plotted in Fig.

V.2
The effect of prior assumptions

According to Bayesian theorem, prior distribution may affect the posterior distribution. Here we check how the prior distribution assumptions would
affect the GW memory estimation.

We use IMRPhenomXPHM
García-Quirós
et al. 2020
as the waveform model to check the dependence of GW memory estimation on the prior distribution. We use two different priors for the comparison.
For both prior distributions, we take uniform distribution for the BH’s spin magnitudes and isotropic distribution for the BH’s spin orientations, binary’s sky location and the orbital orientation. The prior distribution of the luminosity distance corresponds to a uniform merger rate in the co-moving frame of the source. The difference of the two priors is about the masses of the two components.
With prior A, we assume that the chirp mass is uniformly distributed. With prior B, we assume that the component masses of the binary is uniformly distributed. We plot the resulted distribution of the total mass
M_{\rm tot}
and mass ratio
q
in Fig.
12
corresponding respectively to the two priors. From Fig.
12
we can see the two prior distributions respect to the total mass
M_{\rm tot}
and mass ratio
q

VI
Summary and discussion

We have implemented a completely different GW memory measurement scheme (more precisely, estimation scheme
Varma et al. 2019a
) compared to the existing works in the literature. In order to realize our measurement scheme, we have applied the Bondi-Metzner-Sachs method to accurately calculate the GW memory for BBH. Combining this method and the SXS numerical relativity simulation we construct a database to relate BBH initial parameters and the corresponding GW memory. Aided with this database we have constructed a Gaussian process regression type surrogate model
NRSurMemory_7qd4
for GW memory of BBH. With this powerful model, we have done an estimation of the GW memory for the 48 BBH events of GWTC-2. Different to the GW memory waveform models
Talbot et al. 2018
;
Liu et al. 2021
, our surrogate model
NRSurMemory_7qd4
describes the overall GW memory instead of the waveform.

Previous GW memory measurements are all based on waveform analysis. There is no hope to detect GW memory in the near future with such waveform analysis method
Hübner et al. 2020
. The measurement method used in the current work is completely different
Varma et al. 2020
. The key bases for the current method are the Bondi-Metzner-Sachs GW memory calculation technique and the powerful model
NRSurMemory_7qd4
.

Different to previous qualitative estimate on the strength of GW memory
McNeill et al. 2017
, we present the first quantitative measurement of GW memory for the 48 BBH events in GWTC-2. Together with the median value, the posterior distribution of GW memory is also presented. According to the KL divergence between the prior distribution and the posterior distribution, we found 16 GW memory measurements are trustable. This feature is different to the behavior of kick velocity
Varma et al. 2020
. More interestingly we found 4 GW memory measurements definitely tell the signs of the memory on LIGO detectors. Among them we are sure with more than 99.979% confidence that the memory strain of GW190814 is negative on Hanford detector while positive on Livingston detector. In the future, when other GW memory detection results are available
Hübner et al. 2020
, the comparison to our estimation can strongly constrain general relativity
Du and Nishizawa 2016
.

Our estimation technique and our estimation results presented in the current paper can guide
people to more suitably choose the GW events for memory detection with multiple events.
Aided with our estimation technique, the GW memory detection method
with multiple events
McNeill et al. 2017
will become
easier to detect GW memory. In addition, the GW memory features founded in
our work can be used to strongly test general relativity together with the
future GW memory detection.

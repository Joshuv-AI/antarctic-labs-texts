Asymptotic series for low-energy excitations of the Fröhlich Polaron at strong coupling
Morris Brooks, David Mitrouskas

Abstract
Abstract . We consider the confined Fröhlich polaron and establish an asymptotic series for the low-energy eigenvalues in negative powers of the coupling constant. The coefficients of the series are derived through a two-fold perturbation approach, involving expansions around the electron Pekar minimizer and the excitations of the quantum field. Keywords: polaron, strong coupling, eigenvalue expansion 2020 MSC: Primary – 81V70; Secondary – 81Q10.

Introduction and main results

For an open bounded region
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
, let
\Delta_{\Omega}
be the Dirichlet–Laplacian and set
v_{x}(y)=(-\Delta_{\Omega})^{-1/2}(x,y)
. The Fröhlich Hamiltonian of the confined polaron, acting on the Hilbert space
L^{2}(\Omega)\otimes\mathcal{F}
with
\mathcal{F}
the bosonic Fock space over
L^{2}(\Omega)
, is given by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}=-\Delta_{\Omega}+\frac{1}{\alpha}(a^{*}(v_{x})+a(v_{x}))+\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}}\mathcal{N}

(1.1)

with
\alpha>0
a dimensionless coupling constant. The creation and annihilation operators satisfy the usual bosonic commutation relations
[a(f),a^{*}(g)]=\langle f,g\rangle
and the field energy is described by the number operator
\mathcal{N}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}a^{*}(\varphi_{j})a(\varphi_{j})
with
\{\varphi_{j}\}
an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\Omega)
. The definition (
1.1
) is slightly formal since
v_{x}
is not square-integrable. However, it is well known that
\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}
defines a semi-bounded self-adjoint operator, as follows for instance by considering the semi-bounded quadratic form associated with the above expression
[
18
,
27
,
26
]
.

The polaron model was introduced by Herbert Fröhlich
[
15
]
as a model of an electron interacting with the quantized optical phonons of a polarizable crystal. Typically, the model is defined on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with
v_{x}(y)=(2\pi^{2})^{-1}|x-y|^{-2}
and features translation invariance. It provides a simple and well-studied model of non-relativistic quantum field theory and we refer to
[
9
,
26
,
27
,
35
,
7
,
8
,
21
,
28
,
31
,
32
,
2
,
3
,
10
,
33
,
41
,
36
]
for properties, results and further references. The confined version of the polaron was brought to our attention by a recent work of Frank and Seiringer
[
14
]
. While the confinement breaks the translational symmetry, the ultraviolet behavior of the interaction remains unaffected. In
[
14
]
the authors derive a two-term expansion of the ground state energy of
\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}
for large
\alpha
in terms of the semi-classical Pekar energy and a next-order correction of order
\alpha^{-2}
that describes the quantum fluctuations around the classical field. In the present work, we study the complete low-energy spectrum of
\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}
and derive an asymptotic series for each low-energy eigenvalue in negative powers of the coupling constant.

In the case of the translation invariant model, low-energy bound states exist for the fiber Hamiltonians, that is, after fixing the value of the total momentum
[
29
]
. We expect that these eigenvalues admit an asymptotic series expansion, similarly to the eigenvalues of the confined polaron. For the ground state energy, this was predicted already in the 1950s by Bogoliubov and Tjablikov
[
42
,
4
]
.

Remark 1.1
.

By scaling all lengths by a factor of
\alpha
, the operator
\alpha^{2}\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}
becomes unitarily equivalent to the operator

\displaystyle-\Delta_{\Omega/\alpha}+\sqrt{\alpha}(a^{*}(\tilde{v}_{x})+a(\tilde{v}_{x}))+\mathcal{N}

(1.2)

with
\tilde{v}_{x}(y)=(-\Delta_{\Omega/\alpha})^{-1/2}(x,y)
. This Hamiltonian describes a polaron confined to the region
\Omega/\alpha
, which corresponds to the natural length scale for large
\alpha
. This choice of units explains why
\alpha\to\infty
is called the strong-coupling limit. For this work, we find it more convenient to use (
1.1
).

We are interested in the eigenvalues of
\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}
,

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}^{(1)}(\alpha)\leq\mathscr{E}^{(2)}(\alpha)\leq\mathscr{E}^{(3)}(\alpha)\leq\ldots\leq\mathscr{E}^{(n)}(\alpha)\leq\ldots,

(1.3)

where we follow the convention to count degenerate eigenvalues according to their multiplicity, starting from the lowest eigenvalue
\mathscr{E}^{(1)}(\alpha)
. Our main result is an asymptotic series for each such eigenvalue in inverse powers of
\alpha
,

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}^{(n)}(\alpha)=e^{\mathrm{Pek}}+\alpha^{-2}E_{0}+\alpha^{-3}E_{1}+\alpha^{-4}E_{2}+\ldots

(1.4)

with
n
-independent coefficient
e^{\mathrm{Pek}}<0
and
n
-dependent coefficients
E_{0}
,
E_{1},E_{2},\ldots
The crucial point is that the
\alpha
-dependence is exclusively contained in the prefactors. Before stating our result in precise form, let us explain the coefficients in more detail and formulate the assumptions regarding the region
\Omega
.

The first term is given by the semi-classical approximation called the Pekar energy. For the ground state energy, the first-order expansion
\mathscr{E}^{(1)}(\alpha)=e^{\mathrm{Pek}}+o(1)
as
\alpha\to\infty
is a long-known result
[
9
,
26
]
. To define the semi-classical energy, we replace the creation and annihilation operators in (
1.1
) by a real-valued field
\alpha\varphi\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and take the expectation value in a normalized electron state
\psi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. This leads to

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\psi,\varphi)=\int\limits_{\Omega}dx|\nabla\psi(x)|^{2}+2\iint\limits_{\Omega\times\Omega}dxdy\,|\psi(x)|^{2}(-\Delta_{\Omega})^{-1/2}(x,y)\varphi(y)+\int\limits_{\Omega}dy\,\varphi(y)^{2}.

(1.5)

It is straightforward to find the optimal field
\varphi(y)=-\langle\psi,v_{\cdot}(y)\psi\rangle
for given
\psi
, which gives the Pekar energy functional

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{P}}(\psi)=\inf_{\varphi}\mathcal{E}(\psi,\varphi)=\int\limits_{\Omega}dx\,|\nabla\psi(x)|^{2}-\iint\limits_{\Omega\times\Omega}dxdy\,|\psi(x)|^{2}(-\Delta_{\Omega})^{-1}(x,y)|\psi(y)|^{2}.

(1.6)

The Pekar energy is defined as

\displaystyle e^{\mathrm{Pek}}:=\inf_{\psi}\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{P}}(\psi)

(1.7)

where the infimum is taken over all
\psi\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
satisfying
\|\psi\|_{2}=1
. We shall work under the assumption that a unique positive minimizer
\psi^{\mathrm{P}}
exists and that
\mathcal{E}^{\textrm{P}}(\psi)-e^{\mathrm{Pek}}
obeys a lower bound that is quadratic in the distance
\psi-\psi^{\mathrm{P}}
.

Assumption 1
.
The Pekar functional
\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{P}}
has a unique
L^{2}
-normalized minimizer
\psi^{\mathrm{P}}
satisfying
\psi^{\mathrm{P}}>0
.

Assumption 2
.
There exists a constant
\tau>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{P}}(\psi)\geq\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{P}}(\psi^{\mathrm{P}})+\tau\|\psi-\psi^{\mathrm{P}}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}\quad\forall\ \psi\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),\ \|\psi\|_{2}=1.

(1.8)

Both assumptions are satisfied if
\Omega
is a ball
[
11
]
. For the translational-invariant polaron, unique minimizers exist only up to translations
[
22
,
24
]
.

The Euler–Lagrange equation for
\psi^{\mathrm{P}}
reads
-\Delta_{\Omega}\psi^{\mathrm{P}}-(2(-\Delta_{\Omega})^{-1}|\psi^{\mathrm{P}}|^{2}+\mu^{\mathrm{Pek}})\psi^{\mathrm{P}}=0
, where
\mu^{\mathrm{Pek}}=e^{\mathrm{Pek}}-\|\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}\|_{2}^{2}
with
\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}(y)=-\langle\psi^{\mathrm{P}},v_{\cdot}(y)\psi^{\mathrm{P}}\rangle
the optimal classical field. In Lemma
2.4
we will show that
(-\Delta_{\Omega})^{-1}|\psi^{\mathrm{P}}|^{2}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. By positivity of
\psi^{\mathrm{P}}
, it follows from standard arguments for Schrödinger operators that
\psi^{\mathrm{P}}
is the unique ground state with eigenvalue zero of the Pekar Hamiltonian

\displaystyle H_{0}=-\Delta_{\Omega}-2(-\Delta_{\Omega})^{-1}|\psi^{\mathrm{P}}|^{2}-\mu^{\mathrm{Pek}}

(1.9)

and that
H_{0}
has a positive spectral gap above zero. Thus, the reduced resolvent

\displaystyle R:=-Q\frac{1}{QH_{0}Q}Q

(1.10)

with
Q=\mathds{1}-P
and
P=|\psi^{\mathrm{P}}\rangle\langle\psi^{\mathrm{P}}|
defines a bounded negative operator on
L^{2}(\Omega)
.

Let us next focus on the coefficient
E_{0}
in (
1.4
). It corresponds to the
nth
eigenvalue of the quadratic Bogoliubov type Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}_{0}=\mathcal{N}+\langle\psi^{\mathrm{P}},\phi(v_{\cdot})R\phi(v_{\cdot})\psi^{\mathrm{P}}\rangle_{L^{2}}

(1.11)

where
\phi(f):=a^{*}(f)+a(f)
. This operator acts on
\mathcal{F}
and describes the fluctuations of the quantum field around its classical value
\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}
. Note that in the second term, we take the partial inner product with respect to
\psi^{\mathrm{P}}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, hence this is a quadratic operator on
\mathcal{F}
. Using
\mathcal{W}_{\alpha\varphi^{\textrm{P}}}^{-1}(\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}-e^{\mathrm{Pek}}-\alpha^{-2}E_{0})\mathcal{W}_{\alpha\varphi^{\textrm{P}}}=H_{0}+V
with
V=\alpha^{-1}\phi(v_{x}+\varphi^{\mathrm{P}})+\alpha^{-2}(\mathcal{N}-E_{0})
, where
\mathcal{W}_{\alpha\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}}
is the unitary Weyl operator associated with
\alpha\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, we can see that the Bogoliubov Hamiltonian emerges naturally from second-order perturbation theory. Concretely, the ansatz
\Psi_{\xi}=(1+RV+RVRV)\psi^{\mathrm{P}}\otimes\xi
for
\xi\in\mathcal{F}

satisfies

\displaystyle P(H_{0}+V)\Psi_{\xi}

\displaystyle=\psi^{\mathrm{P}}\otimes\alpha^{-2}(\mathbb{H}_{0}-E_{0})\xi+O(\alpha^{-3}),

(1.12a)

\displaystyle Q(H_{0}+V)\Psi_{\xi}

\displaystyle=O(\alpha^{-3}).

(1.12b)

Unlike in usual perturbation theory, the second- and higher-order terms in the eigenspace
\text{Ran}P\otimes\mathcal{F}
of the unperturbed Hamiltonian still involve non-trivial operators. This is a consequence of the fact that
H_{0}
acts trivially on
\mathcal{F}
and, as a result, the Fock space factor
\xi
is determined only at order
\alpha^{-2}
by the operator
\mathbb{H}_{0}
. Being a quadratic operator, it follows that
\mathbb{H}_{0}
is unitarily diagonalizable and that its low-energy spectrum consists of a sequence of discrete eigenvalues
\mathsf{E}^{(1)}<\mathsf{E}^{(2)}\leq\mathsf{E}^{(3)}\leq\ldots
(See Section
1.2
for details.) This reveals that
E_{0}=\mathsf{E}^{(n)}
by choosing
\xi=\Gamma^{(n)}
as the
nth
eigenstate of
\mathbb{H}_{0}
.

The remaining terms in (
1.4
) are obtained through a two-fold perturbation approach. The first expansion is around the ground state of
H_{0}
and extends (
1.12
) to higher orders. The second expansion revolves around the low-energy eigenstates
\Gamma^{(n)}
of
\mathbb{H}_{0}
. Here, we treat the higher-order terms in (
1.12a
) that result from the first expansion as a perturbation of the operator
\alpha^{-2}\mathbb{H}_{0}
. For a detailed explanation, we refer to the sketch of the proof of Proposition
3.2
. The precise formulas for the coefficients
(E_{\ell})_{\ell\geq 1}
are summarized in Theorems
1.5
and
1.6
below.

The unique characteristic of this perturbation series is the presence of two natural scales, which sets it qualitatively apart from the conventional Rayleigh–Schrödinger perturbation expansion. In
[
42
,
4
]
, this is referred to as adiabatic perturbation theory. The origin of this feature can be attributed to the small prefactor in the field energy term of (
1.1
). As a result, our approach may be of interest also for other quantum models exhibiting a similar adiabatic (or semi-classical) decoupling between two subsystems.

Our first result is a precise version of the asymptotic series (
1.4
).

Theorem 1.2
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
let
\mathscr{E}^{(n)}(\alpha)
denote the
nth
eigenvalue of the Fröhlich Hamiltonian
\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}
and
\mathsf{E}^{(n)}
the
nth
eigenvalue of the Bogoliubov Hamiltonian
\mathbb{H}_{0}
. There exists a sequence
(E_{\ell})_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
with
E_{0}=\mathsf{E}^{(n)}
so that for every
b\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
there are constants
C(b),\alpha(b)>0
such that

\displaystyle\Bigg|\,\alpha^{2}\mathscr{E}^{(n)}(\alpha)-\alpha^{2}e^{\mathrm{Pek}}-\sum_{\ell=0}^{b}\alpha^{-\ell}E_{\ell}\,\Bigg|\leq\frac{C(b)}{\alpha^{b+1}}

(1.13)

for all
\alpha\geq\alpha(b)
.

Under the assumption that
\mathsf{E}^{(n)}
is a non-degenerate eigenvalue, we can improve the statement by providing explicit estimates of the coefficients and the remainder of the series. In Remark
1.4
we comment on the potential relevance of these estimates. While we focus on the non-degenerate case in the proof, we expect the result to hold for degenerate eigenvalues as well. Note that the theorem applies, for example, to the ground state energy
\mathscr{E}^{(1)}(\alpha)
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Under the additional assumption that
\mathsf{E}^{(n)}
is a non-degenerate eigenvalue of
\mathbb{H}_{0}
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that
|E_{\ell}|\leq C^{\ell}\sqrt{\ell!}
for all
\ell\geq 0
. Moreover, there is a constant
\alpha_{0}>0
such that

\displaystyle\Bigg|\,\alpha^{2}\mathscr{E}^{(n)}(\alpha)-\alpha^{2}e^{\mathrm{Pek}}-\sum_{\ell=0}^{b}\alpha^{-\ell}E_{\ell}\,\Bigg|\leq\frac{C^{b+1}(b+1)!}{\alpha^{b+1}}

(1.14)

for all
\alpha\geq\alpha_{0}
and
b\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

Remark 1.4
.

The bounds on
E_{\ell}
imply the existence of the Borel transform
B(\alpha):=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\alpha^{-\ell}E_{\ell}/\ell!
. Using
\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\ell}e^{-t\alpha}\text{d}t=\alpha^{-\ell-1}\ell!
together with an unjustified interchange of summation and integration, we would thus obtain the convergent result

\mathscr{E}^{(n)}(\alpha)-e^{\mathrm{Pek}}=\alpha^{-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}B(1/t)e^{-t\alpha}\text{d}t
. If this identity holds, the series is called Borel summable. By the Watson-Nevanlinna theorem
[
40
]
, Borel summability of
\mathscr{E}^{(n)}(\alpha)
is rigorously justified under the following condition: There exists a function
f:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
that is analytic on a disk
D\subseteq\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\Re(z)>0\}
with
0\in\partial D
, such that (i)
f(z)=\mathscr{E}^{(n)}(1/z)
for all
z\in D\cap\mathbb{R}_{0}^{+}
and (ii)
f
has an asymptotic series with remainder estimates of the form (
1.14
) uniformly in
z\in D
. While we do not address the question of analytic continuation of
\mathscr{E}^{(n)}(\alpha)
in this work, we believe that it poses an interesting mathematical problem, in particular since it evades some techniques from singular perturbation theory. The usual approach, as deployed for instance in
[
1
,
37
,
38
,
39
,
16
]
, to infer the required analytic structure of an eigenvalue of an operator
H(z)
relies on the non-degeneracy of the eigenvalue of
H(0)
together with the convergence property
\lim_{z\to 0:z\in D}H(z)=H(0)
in the norm resolvent sense. This approach can not be employed for eigenvalues of
\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}
because the eigenvalue of the unperturbed Hamiltonian
H_{0}=H_{0}\otimes\mathds{1}
has infinite degeneracy.

\displaystyle V_{1}

\displaystyle V_{2}

\displaystyle V_{\ell}

\displaystyle:=-E_{\ell-2}\quad\text{for}\quad\ell\geq 3.

(1.17)

Recalling

) and

, we introduce for

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}_{\ell}=\mathbb{V}_{\ell}(E_{0},\ldots,E_{\ell})

\displaystyle:=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell+2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{N}^{i}\\
|\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}|=\ell+2\end{subarray}}PV_{\varepsilon_{1}}(RV_{\varepsilon_{2}})\ldots(RV_{\varepsilon_{i}})P

(1.18)

where

and

. Note that

acts on

|\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}|=\ell+2\end{subarray}}PV_{\varepsilon_{1}}(RV_{\varepsilon_{2}})\ldots(RV_{\varepsilon_{i}})P.

(1.19)

Note that the coefficient

does not appear on the right-hand side, while

Let
\mathbb{P}
be the orthogonal projection onto the finite-dimensional eigenspace of

associated with the eigenvalue

and define for

the reduced resolvent

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}:=-\mathbb{Q}\frac{1}{\mathbb{Q}(\mathbb{H}_{0}-\mathsf{E}^{(n)})\mathbb{Q}}\,\mathbb{Q}.

(1.20)

By Lemma

.

The next statement provides an iterative formula for the coefficients

in case

.

Assume
\mathsf{E}^{(n)}
to be a non-degenerate eigenvalue of

with normalized eigenfunction

satisfies

,

for all

odd and the iterative relation

\displaystyle E_{\ell}

\displaystyle=\bigg\langle\psi^{\mathrm{P}}\otimes\Gamma,\bigg[\widetilde{\mathbb{V}}_{\ell}+\sum_{i=2}^{\ell}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{N}^{i}\\

Note that the right-hand side of (

Theorem 1.6
.

Choose
\mathscr{E}^{(n_{s})}(\alpha)
,

\textnormal{d}\geq 1

\mathbb{H}_{0}
has a

\mathsf{E}^{(n_{1})}=\ldots=\mathsf{E}^{(n_{\textnormal{d}})}
. Moreover, let
M^{(\ell)}\in\mathbb{C}^{\textnormal{d}\times\textnormal{d}}
be defined as explained above. The coefficients from Theorem
1.2
associated with the eigenvalue
\mathscr{E}^{(n_{s})}(\alpha)
satisfy the recursive relation
E_{0}=\mathsf{E}^{(n_{s})}
and

\displaystyle E_{\ell}=\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}\alpha^{\ell}\bigg(\mu^{(s)}(M^{(\ell)})-\sum^{\ell-1}_{k=1}\alpha^{-k}E_{k}\bigg)

(1.25)

where
\mu^{(s)}(M^{(\ell)})
denotes the

counted in increasing order, i.e.,
\mu^{(1)}(M^{(\ell)})\leq\ldots\leq\mu^{(\textnormal{\text{d}})}(M^{(\ell)})
.

This immediately implies Theorem
1.5
when
\text{d}=1
. Hence it is sufficient to prove the general statement.

Remark 1.7
.

The theorem is based on the observation that the spectrum of
M^{(\ell)}
consists of
d
analytic branches in the variable
\alpha^{-1}
that do not intersect when restricted to a suitable interval
(0,1/\alpha_{0})
. This property follows from
[
20
, Theorem 1.10 and Chapter 2 §6]
. Consequently, the ordered eigenvalues admit a power series expansion, that is, for every
\ell\geq 1
and
s\in\{1,\ldots,\text{d}\}
there exists a sequence
(\mu^{(s)}_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
such that

\displaystyle\mu^{(s)}(M^{(\ell)})=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\alpha^{-k}\mu_{k}^{(s)}

(1.26)

on
[\alpha_{0},\infty)
. From this, we can verify that the limit in (
1.25
) indeed exists by an iterative argument. For this purpose, assume that
E_{\ell-1}=\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}\alpha^{\ell-1}(\mu^{(s)}(M^{(\ell-1)})-\sum_{k=1}^{\ell-2}\alpha^{-k}E_{k})
exists. Note that the expansion of eigenvalues for the matrices
M^{(\ell)}
and
M^{(\ell-1)}
share the same coefficients
\mu^{(s)}_{k}
for
k\in\{1,\ldots,\ell-1\}
, and therefore
\mu^{(s)}_{k}=E_{k}
for all such
k
. Consequently,
\mu^{(s)}(M^{(\ell)})-\sum_{k=1}^{\ell-1}\alpha^{-k}E_{k}=\sum_{k=\ell}^{\infty}\alpha^{-k}\mu^{(s)}_{k}
, which concludes the argument. For completeness, let us mention that the definition of
E_{\ell}
is independent of the choice of the basis
\{\Gamma^{(n_{1})},\ldots,\Gamma^{(n_{\text{d}})}\}
.

As a simple yet illustrative example, consider the case
\text{d}=2
, i.e., assume that
\mathsf{E}^{(n-1)}<\mathsf{E}^{(n)}=\mathsf{E}^{(n+1)}<\mathsf{E}^{(n+2)}
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Theorem
1.6
states that
\alpha^{2}\mathscr{E}^{(n+j)}(\alpha)-\alpha^{2}e^{\mathrm{Pek}}=\mathsf{E}^{(n)}+\alpha^{-1}\mu^{(j+1)}(M_{1})+O(\alpha^{-2})
for
j\in\{0,1\}
with
M_{1}\in\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2}
defined by (
1.24
). Concretely, one finds
(M_{1})_{11}=(M_{1})_{22}=0
and

\displaystyle(M_{1})_{12}=\Big\langle\psi^{\mathrm{P}}\otimes\Gamma^{(n)},P\phi(v_{\cdot})R\phi(v_{\cdot}+\varphi^{\mathrm{P}})R\phi(v_{\cdot})P\psi^{\mathrm{P}}\otimes\Gamma^{(n+1)}\Big\rangle

(1.27)

and thus the eigenvalues of
M_{1}
are
\pm|(M_{1})_{12}|
. If
M_{1}
has two identical eigenvalues, the coefficient
E_{1}=0
will be the same for
n
and
n+1
and thus the degeneracy remains preserved at this order. On the other hand, if
(M_{1})_{12}\neq 0
, then
E_{1}=-|(M_{1})_{12}|
for
n
and
E_{1}=|(M_{1})_{12}|
for
n+1
. In this case, the eigenvalues
\mathscr{E}^{(n+j)}(\alpha)
,
j\in\{0,1\}
, split at this particular order. The extensions to higher orders and
\text{d}\geq 3
, where partial lifting of the degeneracy becomes a possibility, are straightforward.

1.1
Outline of the proof

Our proof consists of two distinct parts. The key idea, which is made precise in the second part, is to consider approximate eigenstates of
\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}
with corresponding approximate eigenvalues
\mathscr{E}^{(n)}_{b}(\alpha)=e^{\mathrm{Pek}}+\sum_{\ell=0}^{b}\alpha^{-2-\ell}E_{\ell}
. We refer to
\Psi_{b}\in L^{2}(\Omega)\otimes\mathcal{F}
as an approximate eigenstate of order
b
and
\mathscr{E}_{b}^{(n)}(\alpha)
the corresponding approximate eigenvalue if they satisfy an inequality of the form

\displaystyle\big\|\big(\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}-\mathscr{E}_{b}^{(n)}\big)\Psi_{b}\big\|\leq C(b)\alpha^{-b-3}

(1.28)

for some constant
C(b)>0
and large
\alpha
. An immediate consequence of the existence of an approximate eigenstate is that the spectrum
\sigma(\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha})
contains eigenvalues
\mathscr{E}(\alpha)
that satisfy
|\mathscr{E}(\alpha)-\mathscr{E}_{b}^{(n)}(\alpha)|\leq C(b)\alpha^{-b-3}
. In Sections
3
and
4
we will construct approximate eigenstates for any
b\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Section
3
covers the general case, while Section
4
focuses on the non-degenerate case with optimized remainder estimates. It is important to note, however, that the existence of approximate eigenstates does not exclude the existence of other eigenvalues of
\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}
.

This brings us to the first part of our proof, where we establish an a priori localization of the low-energy eigenvalues in the vicinity of the eigenvalues
\mathsf{E}^{(n)}
of
\mathbb{H}_{0}
. For this purpose, we derive a two-term expansion of the form

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}^{(n)}(\alpha)=e^{\mathrm{Pek}}+\alpha^{-2}\mathsf{E}^{(n)}+o(\alpha^{-2}).

(1.29)

Based on this two-term localization, it follows that the approximate eigenvalues
\mathscr{E}^{(n)}_{b}(\alpha)
obtained from (
1.28
) approximate indeed all eigenvalues of
\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}
. Combining the two findings implies that every eigenvalue admits an asymptotic series. To demonstrate (
1.29
), we first establish suitable condensation properties for low-energy states of
\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}
, following the strategy deployed in
[
7
,
8
]
. Subsequently, we utilize these properties to compare
\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}
with the Bogoliubov Hamiltonian
\mathbb{H}_{0}
through a Feshbach–Schur decomposition. This approach can be regarded as a rigorous second-order perturbation argument. The details of this proof are presented in Section
2
. Under an additional regularity assumption on
\Omega
, a two-term expansion of the form (
1.29
) for the ground state energy
\mathscr{E}^{(1)}(\alpha)
was previously obtained by Frank and Seiringer
[
14
]
. Our proof takes a distinct approach since the one employed in
[
14
]
does not directly extend to excited eigenvalues and, in particular, does not provide a spectral gap above the ground state energy.

Remark 1.8
.

Let us compare our work with
[
6
]
about the asymptotic expansion for the low-energy eigenvalues of
N
bosons in the mean-field limit in inverse powers of the coupling constant
\lambda_{N}=(N-1)^{-1}
. Although the models and the considered limits are obviously different, the results may appear somewhat similar. Therefore, we believe it would be helpful to highlight some interesting differences that are not immediately evident. The eigenvalue expansion in
[
6
]
requires a power series expansion of the Hamiltonian itself, more specifically, of the
N
-dependent excitation Hamiltonian
\mathbb{H}
, see
[
6
, Def. 2.3 and Eq. (1.8)]
. This type of expansion is not needed in our case since (
1.1
) and (
3.5
) have already the desired form of a polynomial in the coupling constant
\alpha^{-1}
. On the other hand, the interaction in the polaron model is more singular than the one for bosons in the mean-field limit, the implications of which are discussed at the beginning of Section
3
. An important property inherent in the strong coupling limit of the polaron pertains to the adiabatic decoupling between the electron and the quantum field, i.e., the small prefactor of
\mathcal{N}
in (
1.3
). As explained earlier, this gives rise to a two-scale character of the expansion, along with the fact that the leading-order eigenvalue equation exhibits infinite degeneracy. For the Bose gas, the leading-order problem is at most finitely degenerate. Perhaps the most interesting difference concerns the method of proof. As outlined above, our approach is based on the construction of approximate eigenstates. This differs from the approach used in
[
6
, cf. Sect. 3.2]
, which utilizes an expansion of the difference of two Riesz projections

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{(n)}-\mathbb{P}^{(n)}_{0}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\gamma^{(n)}}\bigg[\frac{1}{z-\mathbb{H}}-\frac{1}{z-\mathbb{H}_{0}}\bigg]\,\textnormal{d}z

(1.30)

where
\mathbb{H}_{0}
is an explicitly
N
-independent Bogoliubov type operator and
\gamma^{(n)}\subset\mathbb{C}
an
N
-independent contour that encompasses both, the
n
th eigenvalue of
\mathbb{H}
and
\mathbb{H}_{0}
, while it excludes all other eigenvalues (for simplicity, we assume that the
n
th eigenvalue of
\mathbb{H}_{0}
is non-degnerate), see
[
6
, Def. 3.10]
. That such a contour exists follows from Bogoliubov theory for the Bose gas in the mean-field limit
[
23
,
34
,
17
]
. In the next step, the authors expand the difference of the resolvents
(z-\mathbb{H})^{-1}-(z-\mathbb{H}_{0})^{-1}
in a power series of
\lambda_{N}
with operator-valued coefficients that are of order one when integrated along
\gamma^{(n)}
and tested on low-energy eigenstates of
\mathbb{H}
. This leads to an expansion for
\mathbb{P}^{(n)}
which is then used to derive a series expansion for the low-energy eigenvalues. We now explain why this approach does not work for the Fröhlich Hamiltonian. The analogous starting point for the expansion of the Riesz projection would be

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{(n)}-\mathbb{P}^{(n)}_{0}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\gamma^{(n)}}\bigg[\frac{1}{z-\mathcal{W}_{\alpha\varphi^{\textrm{P}}}^{-1}\alpha^{2}(\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}-e^{\mathrm{Pek}})\mathcal{W}_{\alpha\varphi^{\textrm{P}}}}-\frac{1}{z-P\otimes\mathbb{H}_{0}}\bigg]\,\textnormal{d}z

(1.31)

with
\mathbb{H}_{0}
defined by (
1.11
). Here,
\gamma^{(n)}
is an
\alpha
-independent contour that encompasses the
n
th eigenvalues of
\alpha^{2}(\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}-e^{\mathrm{Pek}})
and
\mathbb{H}_{0}
and excludes all other eigenvalues of these operators. If the considered eigenvalue of
\mathbb{H}_{0}
is non-degenerate, the existence of such a contour is ensured by Proposition
2.1
. However, we do not have an expansion of the difference of the two resolvents in terms of operators that are small compared to one when integrated along
\gamma^{(n)}
and tested on low-energy eigenstates. By closer inspection, Lemmas
2.6
and
2.8
show that apriori, we only have
\alpha^{2}(\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}-e^{\mathrm{Pek}})\cong P\otimes\mathbb{H}_{0}+O(\alpha^{2-\eta})
for some small
\eta
and thus, the perturbation would be much larger than the distance of the eigenvalue of
\mathbb{H}_{0}
to the contour
\gamma^{(n)}
. Consequently, a direct attempt to expand (
1.31
) is deemed to fail. The method of approximate eigenstates, on the other hand, avoids this difficulty as the perturbation is much smaller when applied to the correct states, cf. (
1.12
). Conversely, the method of approximate eigenstates can be directly applied to the Bose gas in the mean-field limit as well. Another crucial advantage of this method is that it requires only estimates for the approximate eigenstates, which are usually much easier to establish compared to estimates for the true eigenstates needed in the expansion of the Riesz projection. While our approach does not directly give an expansion of the spectral projections, let us stress that aposteriori a series expansion for the projections is almost trivial. In the non-degenerate case, for instance, this is an immediate consequence of Theorem
1.3
and Proposition
4.1
. A final aspect worth noting is that we derive the asymptotic expansion for each low-energy eigenvalue, whereas
[
6
, Theorem 2]
considers the averaged sum of eigenvalues for those eigenvalues that become degenerate in the limit.

1.2
Spectrum of the Bogoliubov Hamiltonian

To diagonalize the Hamiltonian (
1.11
), we introduce the operator
\mathfrak{h}:L^{2}(\Omega)\to L^{2}(\Omega)
with integral kernel

\displaystyle\mathfrak{h}(x,y)=\delta(x-y)+4\Re\langle\psi^{\mathrm{P}},v_{\cdot}(x)Rv_{\cdot}(y)\psi^{\mathrm{P}}\rangle_{L^{2}}.

(1.32)

This operator arises as the Hessian of the Pekar functional
\mathcal{E}(\psi^{\textrm{P}},\varphi)=\inf\sigma(-\Delta_{\Omega}-2\langle v_{x},\varphi\rangle)+\|\varphi\|_{2}^{2}
for real-valued
\varphi\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, evaluated at its minimizer
\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}
. For a detailed discussion, we refer to
[
14
, Sec. 2.2]
. Anticipating that
\mathfrak{h}
is positive and has a bounded inverse, we can define the operator

\displaystyle\mathfrak{B}\,=\,\tfrac{1}{2}\big(\mathfrak{h}^{-1/4}-\mathfrak{h}^{1/4}\big).

(1.33)

Lemma 1.9
.

There exist constants
\tau\in(0,1)
and
C>0
such that
\tau\leq\mathfrak{h}\leq 1
,
{\mathrm{Tr}}_{L^{2}}(1-\mathfrak{h})\leq C
and
\mathfrak{B}^{2}\leq C(1-\mathfrak{h})
. Moreover, the discrete part of the spectrum satisfies
\sigma_{\textnormal{dsic}}(\mathfrak{h})=\sigma(\mathfrak{h})\setminus\{1\}
and
|\sigma_{\textnormal{disc}}(\mathfrak{h})|=\infty
.

Proof.

For the first two properties, see
[
14
, Sec. 3.1]
or alternatively
[
28
, Lem. 1.1 and 2.3]
. The bound on
\mathfrak{B}^{2}
follows by using
\mathfrak{h}\leq 1
and the inequality
(1-x)^{-1/2}\leq 1+\lambda^{-3/2}x
for
x\in(0,1-\lambda)
. Since
1-\mathfrak{h}
is trace-class, it is compact and thus
\sigma_{\textnormal{dsic}}(\mathfrak{h})=\sigma(\mathfrak{h})\setminus\{1\}
. Using (
1.32
), it is easy to see that
\varphi\notin\text{Ker}(1-\mathfrak{h})
if
(-\Delta_{\Omega})^{-1/2}\varphi\neq\text{constant}
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. Choosing
\varphi=w_{j}
with
(w_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
the set of eigenfunctions of
-\Delta_{\Omega}
, this proves the last statement of the lemma.
∎

We enumerate the eigenvalues of
\mathfrak{h}
by
0<\tau_{1}\leq\tau_{2}\leq\ldots<1
and the corresponding normalized eigenfunctions by
\mathfrak{u}_{n}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. Note that
\tau_{j}\to 1
as
j\to\infty
.

Since
\mathfrak{B}
is Hilbert–Schmidt, there exists a unitary map
\mathbb{U}
on
\mathcal{F}
, called a Bogoliubov transformation, satisfying

\displaystyle\mathbb{U}a^{*}(f)\mathbb{U}^{*}\,

\displaystyle=\,a^{*}((1+\mathfrak{B}^{2})^{1/2}f)+a(\mathfrak{B}\overline{f})

(1.34)

for all
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
.
The operator
\mathbb{H}_{0}
defined in (
1.11
) is diagonalized by
\mathbb{U}
in the sense of the following lemma, whose proof follows from a direct computation, see
[
28
, Lemma 2.5]
. We note that by Lemma
1.9
the value of the trace is finite and negative.

Lemma 1.10
.

We have

\displaystyle\mathbb{U}\mathbb{H}_{0}\mathbb{U}^{*}\,

\displaystyle=\,\textnormal{d}\Gamma(\mathfrak{h}^{1/2})+\tfrac{1}{2}\textnormal{Tr}_{L^{2}}\big(\mathfrak{h}^{1/2}-1\big)

(1.35a)

\displaystyle\inf\sigma(\mathbb{H}_{0})\,

\displaystyle=\,\tfrac{1}{2}\textnormal{Tr}_{L^{2}}\big(\mathfrak{h}^{1/2}-1\big).

(1.35b)

We are interested in the excitation spectrum of
\mathbb{H}_{0}
below
\inf\sigma(\mathbb{H}_{0})+1
. This part of the spectrum contains only discrete eigenvalues of finite multiplicity, as it corresponds to the free bosonic excitation spectrum with one-particle energies determined by
\mathfrak{h}^{1/2}
. Denoting the lowest eigenvalue by
\mathsf{E}^{(1)}=\inf\sigma(\mathbb{H}_{0})
, the spectrum below
\mathsf{E}^{(1)}+1
can be enumerated as
\mathsf{E}^{(1)}<\mathsf{E}^{(2)}\leq\mathsf{E}^{(3)}\leq\ldots<\mathsf{E}^{(1)}+1
. More specifically, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
there exists some
k\in\mathbb{N}
and a tuple
(\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{k})\in\mathbb{N}^{k}_{0}
such that

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}^{(n)}=\mathsf{E}^{(1)}+\nu_{1}\tau_{1}^{1/2}+\ldots+\nu_{k}\tau_{k}^{1/2}.

(1.36)

Since
\tau_{1}>0
, the value of
k
is uniformly bounded in
n
. In fact, there exist
j,n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
so that for all
n\geq n_{0}
, the eigenvalues are of the form
\mathsf{E}^{(n)}=\mathsf{E}^{(1)}+\tau_{n+j}^{1/2}
, and thus
\mathsf{E}^{(n)}\to\mathsf{E}^{(1)}+1
as
n\to\infty
. For later purposes, let us also introduce the corresponding normalized eigenfunctions of
\mathbb{H}_{0}
, given by

\displaystyle\Gamma^{(n)}=\mathbb{U}^{*}\gamma^{(n)}\quad\text{with}\quad\gamma^{(n)}=\frac{a^{*}(\mathfrak{u}_{\nu_{1}})^{\nu_{1}}}{\sqrt{\nu_{1}!}}\cdots\frac{a^{*}(\mathfrak{u}_{\nu_{k}})^{\nu_{k}}}{\sqrt{\nu_{k}!}}\Omega,

(1.37)

where
\Omega
is the vacuum state in
\mathcal{F}
.

2
Two-term expansion of low-energy eigenvalues

The following statement provides a two-term localization of the low-energy spectrum of
\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}
around the eigenvalues of
\mathbb{H}_{0}
.

Proposition 2.1
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
let
\mathscr{E}^{(n)}(\alpha)
and
\mathsf{E}^{(n)}
be the
nth
eigenvalues of
\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}
and
\mathbb{H}_{0}
. There exist constants
C,\delta,\alpha_{0}>0
such that

\displaystyle\Big|\mathscr{E}^{(n)}(\alpha)-e^{\mathrm{Pek}}-\alpha^{-2}\mathsf{E}^{(n)}\Big|\leq C\alpha^{-2-\delta}

(2.1)

for all
\alpha\geq\alpha_{0}
.

To prove the proposition, we derive upper and lower bounds for the min-max values of the Fröhlich Hamiltonian,

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}^{(n)}(\alpha):=\inf_{\mathcal{V}\subset L^{2}(\Omega)\otimes\mathcal{F}}\,\sup\big\{\langle\Psi,\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}\Psi\rangle\,|\,\Psi\in\mathcal{V},\ \|\Psi\|=1\big\},\quad n\in\mathbb{N},

(2.2)

where the infimum is taken over all subspaces
\mathcal{V}\subset Q(\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha})
with
\text{dim}\mathcal{V}=n
. Here,
Q(\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha})=Q(-\Delta_{\Omega}+\mathcal{N})
is the form domain of
\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}

[
14
,
18
]
.

For convenience of the reader, we recall the definition of the unitary Weyl operator
\mathcal{W}_{\varphi}:=e^{a(\varphi)-a^{*}(\varphi)}
for
\varphi\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and the shift relations
\mathcal{W}_{\varphi}a(f)\mathcal{W}_{\varphi}^{-1}=a(f)+\langle f,\varphi\rangle
and

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}^{-1}_{\varphi}\mathcal{N}\mathcal{W}_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{N}+\phi(\varphi)+\|\varphi\|_{2}^{2}

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}^{-1}_{\varphi}\phi(f)\mathcal{W}_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=\phi(f)+2\Re\langle f,\varphi\rangle

(2.4)

where
\phi(f)=a^{*}(f)+a(f)
. We denote the coherent state associated with
\varphi\in L^{2}(\Omega)
by
\Omega_{\varphi}\in\mathcal{F}
, that is,
\Omega_{\varphi}=\mathcal{W}^{-1}_{\varphi}\Omega
where
\Omega
denotes the vacuum state in
\mathcal{F}
.

is a negative bounded operator on

satisfying
\sup_{\alpha>0}\|\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha}\|<\infty
. The next lemma is a simple variant of the commutator method by Lieb and Yamazaki
[
26
,
27
]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
X,Y\in\{P,(-R)^{1/2},(-\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha})^{1/2}\}
. There is a constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{\alpha>0}\|X\phi(v_{\cdot})Y(\mathcal{N}+1)^{-1/2}\|\leq C.

(2.18)

Proof.

Similarly as in the proof of Lemma
2.3
, write
\phi(v_{x})=\sum_{i=1}^{3}[\nabla_{\Omega}^{(i)},\phi(\nabla_{\Omega}^{(i)}g_{x})]
with
g_{x}
defined in Lemma
2.2
. With the usual bounds for creation and annihilation operators, this gives

\displaystyle\|X\phi(v_{\cdot})Y\Psi\|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{x\in\Omega}\|(-\Delta_{\Omega})^{1/2}g_{x}\|_{2}(\|X\nabla_{\Omega}\|\,+\|\nabla_{\Omega}Y\|)\|(\mathcal{N}+1)^{1/2}\Psi\|

\displaystyle\leq C\|(\mathcal{N}+1)^{1/2}\Psi\|,

(2.19)

where the second step follows from Lemma
2.2
and

\displaystyle\|P\nabla_{\Omega}\|+\|(-R)^{1/2}\nabla_{\Omega}\|+\sup_{\alpha>0}\|(-\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha})^{1/2}\nabla_{\Omega}\|<\infty.

(2.20)

The bound involving
R
is a consequence of
(-\Delta_{\Omega})^{-1}|\psi^{\mathrm{P}}|^{2}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, which follows from Hölder’s inequality using that
|\psi^{\mathrm{P}}|^{2}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
by Sobolev’s inequality and that
(-\Delta_{\Omega})^{-1}(x,\cdot)\in L^{2}(\Omega)
for every
x\in\Omega
, as can be inferred from (
2.8
). The bound involving
\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha}
is obtained by using
\pm\phi(v_{x})\leq\tfrac{1}{2}(-\Delta_{\Omega})+C(\tfrac{1}{\alpha^{2}}\mathcal{N}+1)
.
∎

The next lemma provides bounds for higher moments of the particle number operator after conjugation with the unitary defined in (
2.1
). For a proof, see
[
5
, Lemma 4.4]
.

Lemma 2.5
.

For every
b\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{U}(\mathcal{N}+1)^{b}\,\mathbb{U}^{*}\leq(b\,\mathfrak{c})^{b}(\mathcal{N}+1)^{b},\quad\mathbb{U}^{*}(\mathcal{N}+1)^{b}\,\mathbb{U}\leq(b\,\mathfrak{c})^{b}(\mathcal{N}+1)^{b}

(2.21)

with
\mathfrak{c}=3\|\mathfrak{B}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{2}}+3
, where
\|\cdot\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{2}}
denotes the Hilbert–Schmidt norm.

2.2
Upper bounds

We consider the Weyl-transformed Fröhlich Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathfrak{H}}_{\alpha}:=\mathcal{W}_{\alpha\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}}^{-1}(\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}-e^{\mathrm{Pek}})\mathcal{W}_{\alpha\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}}=H_{0}+\alpha^{-1}\phi(v_{x}+\varphi^{\mathrm{P}})+\alpha^{-2}\mathcal{N}

(2.22)

and for
n\in\mathbb{N}
the trial state

\displaystyle\Psi^{(n)}=(1-\tfrac{1}{\alpha}R\phi(v_{\cdot}))\psi^{\mathrm{P}}\otimes\mathbb{U}^{*}\gamma^{(n)}=:\Upsilon^{(n)}_{0}-\tfrac{1}{\alpha}\Upsilon^{(n)}_{1}

(2.23)

where
\mathbb{U}^{*}\gamma^{(n)}
are the normalized eigenfunctions of
\mathbb{H}_{0}
, see (
1.37
).
A straightforward computation, using
H_{0}\psi^{\mathrm{P}}=0
,
P\phi(v_{x}+\varphi^{\mathrm{P}})P=0
,
PR=0
and Lemma
1.10
, leads to

\displaystyle\langle\Psi^{(n)},\widetilde{\mathfrak{H}}_{\alpha}\Psi^{(m)}\rangle-

\displaystyle\alpha^{-2}\delta_{nm}\mathsf{E}^{(n)}

\displaystyle=\alpha^{-3}\langle\psi^{\mathrm{P}}\otimes\mathbb{U}^{*}\gamma^{(n)},\phi(v_{x})R\phi(v_{x}+\varphi^{\mathrm{P}})R\phi(v_{x})\psi^{\mathrm{P}}\otimes\mathbb{U}^{*}\gamma^{(m)}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad-2\alpha^{-3}\Re\langle\psi^{\mathrm{P}}\otimes\mathbb{U}^{*}\gamma^{(n)},\phi(v_{x})R\mathcal{N}\psi^{\mathrm{P}}\otimes\mathbb{U}^{*}\gamma^{(m)}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\alpha^{-4}\langle\psi^{\mathrm{P}}\otimes\mathbb{U}^{*}\gamma^{(n)},\phi(v_{\cdot})R\mathcal{N}R\phi(v_{\cdot})\psi^{\mathrm{P}}\otimes\mathbb{U}^{*}\gamma^{(m)}\rangle.

(2.24)

Using Lemmas
2.4
,
2.5
, one verifies that the modulus of the right-hand side is bounded uniformly in
n,m
, by
C\|(\mathcal{N}+1)^{2}\mathbb{U}^{*}\gamma^{(n)}\|\alpha^{-3}\leq C\alpha^{-3}
. Similarly, one shows that the trial states are almost orthonormal, i.e. that there is a constant
C
so that
|\langle\Psi^{(n)},\Psi^{(m)}\rangle-\delta_{nm}|\leq C\alpha^{-1}
for all
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
and
\alpha>0
. Now let
\mathcal{V}=\text{Span}(\Psi^{(1)},\ldots,\Psi^{(n)})
. There exists
\alpha(n)>0
such that for all
\alpha\geq\alpha(n)
,
\textnormal{dim}\mathcal{V}=n
. Thus, we can use
\mathcal{V}
as a test subspace in (
2.2
). This implies the desired upper bound for the
nth
min-max value
\mathscr{E}^{(n)}(\alpha)\leq e^{\mathrm{Pek}}+\alpha^{-2}\mathsf{E}^{(n)}+C\alpha^{-3}
.

2.3
Lower bounds

To prove the lower bound on the min-max values, we first estimate
\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}
in terms of its Feshbach–Schur complement associated with the projection
P
. In the second step, we estimate the min-max values of the Feshbach–Schur complement in terms of the eigenvalues of the Bogoliubov Hamiltonian. For the second step, we need to establish suitable condensation properties for low-energy states of
\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]
be a smooth function satisfying
f|_{[0,\frac{1}{2}]}=1
and
f|_{[1,\infty)}=0
, such that
f_{\perp}:=\sqrt{1-f^{2}}
is smooth as well. We use this to define the operators

\displaystyle\pi:=f\Big(\alpha^{\kappa-2}\mathcal{W}_{\alpha\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}}^{-1}\mathcal{N}\mathcal{W}_{\alpha\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}}\Big),\quad\pi_{\perp}:=f_{\perp}\Big(\alpha^{\kappa-2}\mathcal{W}_{\alpha\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}}^{-1}\mathcal{N}\mathcal{W}_{\alpha\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}}\Big)

(2.25)

with
\kappa\in(0,2)
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
Q=\mathrm{1}-|\psi^{\mathrm{P}}\rangle\langle\psi^{\mathrm{P}}|
. There exist constants
\delta,\alpha_{0}>0
, such that
Q(\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}-e^{\mathrm{Pek}})Q\geq\delta Q
for all
\alpha\geq\alpha_{0}
. Further, let
\pi
be defined as above for
\kappa\in(0,\tfrac{2}{7})
. Then, there exists a constant
C>0
so that

\displaystyle\|Q\Psi\|^{2}+1-\|\pi\Psi\|^{2}\leq C\alpha^{\kappa-\frac{2}{7}}\ln\alpha

for all states
\Psi\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F}\big(L^{2}(\Omega)))
satisfying
\braket{\Psi,\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}\Psi}\leq e^{\mathrm{Pek}}+\alpha^{-\frac{2}{7}}\ln\alpha
.

Proof.

For the porpuse of the proof, recall the Fröhlich Hamiltonian with cutoff given in (
2.11
), where
\Pi_{\Lambda}
denotes the orthogonal projection
\mathds{1}(-\Delta_{\Omega}\leq\Lambda^{2})
. Applying Lemma
2.3
for
\Lambda=\alpha^{\frac{4}{7}}
yields
(1+C\alpha^{-\frac{2}{7}}\ln\alpha)\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}\geq\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}^{\Lambda}-C\alpha^{-\frac{2}{7}}\ln\alpha
for a suitable constant
C
. Let us now identify
L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F}(L^{2}(\Omega)))\cong\mathcal{F}(\Pi_{\Lambda}L^{2}(\Omega))\otimes L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F}(\Pi_{\Lambda}L^{2}(\Omega)^{\perp}))
and let
\Theta_{\varphi}
be the orthogonal projection onto elements of the form
\Omega_{\varphi}\otimes\widetilde{\Psi}
with
\widetilde{\Psi}\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{F}(\Pi_{\Lambda}L^{2}(\Omega)^{\perp}))
, where
\Omega_{\varphi}
is the coherent state defined by
a(f)\Omega_{\varphi}=\braket{f,\varphi}\Omega_{\varphi}
for some
\varphi\in\Pi_{\Lambda}L^{2}(\Omega)
.
Furthermore, let
\mathcal{N}_{\perp}
be the particle number operator on
\mathcal{F}(\Pi_{\Lambda}L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)^{\perp})
and let
f_{1},\dots,f_{N}
be an orthonormal basis of
\Pi_{\Lambda}L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)
. With this at hand, we can write

\displaystyle\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}^{\Lambda}

\displaystyle=-\Delta_{\Omega}+\alpha^{-1}\phi(\Pi_{\Lambda}v_{x})+\alpha^{-2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}a(f_{j})a^{*}(f_{j})-\frac{N}{\alpha^{2}}+\alpha^{-2}\mathcal{N}_{\perp}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi^{N}}\int_{\Pi_{\Lambda}L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}\Theta_{\varphi}H_{\alpha^{-1}\varphi}\Theta_{\varphi}\mathrm{d}\varphi-\frac{N}{\alpha^{2}}+\alpha^{-2}\mathcal{N}_{\perp}

(2.26)

with
H_{\varphi}:=-\Delta_{\Omega}-\braket{v_{x},\varphi}-\braket{\varphi,v_{x}}+\|\varphi\|_{2}^{2}
. As a consequence of Assumption
2
, we obtain
\braket{\psi,H_{\varphi}\psi}\geq\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{P}}(\psi)\geq e^{\mathrm{Pek}}+\tau_{1}\|Q\psi\|^{2}_{2}
. Furthermore,
\braket{\psi,H_{\varphi}\psi}\geq e^{\mathrm{Pek}}+\tau_{2}\|\varphi-\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}\|^{2}_{2}
by
[
14
, Proposition 3.2]
, and therefore
H_{\varphi}\geq e^{\mathrm{Pek}}+\tau Q+\tau\|\varphi-\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}\|_{2}^{2}
with
\tau:=\frac{1}{2}\min\{\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\}
. (Note that the proof of
[
14
, Proposition 3.2]
does not depend on
[
14
, Assumption 3]
.) Using this inequality, we proceed with

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi^{N}}\int_{\Pi_{\Lambda}L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}\!\!\!\Theta_{\varphi}H_{\alpha^{-1}\varphi}\Theta_{\varphi}\mathrm{d}\varphi\geq\frac{1}{\pi^{N}}\int_{\Pi_{\Lambda}L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}\!\!\!\Theta_{\varphi}\big(e^{\mathrm{Pek}}+\tau Q+\tau\alpha^{-2}\|\varphi-\alpha\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}\|^{2}_{2}\big)\Theta_{\varphi}\mathrm{d}\varphi

\displaystyle\qquad=e^{\mathrm{Pek}}+\tau Q+\tau\alpha^{-2}\mathcal{W}_{\alpha\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}}^{-1}(\mathcal{N}-\mathcal{N}_{\perp})\mathcal{W}_{\alpha\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}}+\tau\|(1-\Pi_{\Lambda})\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{\tau N}{\alpha^{2}}.

(2.27)

Abbreviating
\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}_{\perp}=(1-\Pi_{\Lambda})\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}
and using the inequality
\alpha^{-1}\phi(\varphi_{\perp}^{\mathrm{P}})\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\alpha^{-2}(\mathcal{N}_{\perp}+1)+2\|\varphi_{\perp}^{\mathrm{P}}\|_{2}^{2}
together with Lemma
2.3
with
\Lambda=\alpha^{\frac{4}{7}}
, we further have

\displaystyle\alpha^{-2}\mathcal{N}_{\perp}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{W}_{\alpha\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}}^{-1}\big(\alpha^{-2}\mathcal{N}_{\perp}-\alpha^{-1}\phi(\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}_{\perp})+\|\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}_{\perp}\|^{2}_{2}\big)\mathcal{W}_{\alpha\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}}

\displaystyle\geq\mathcal{W}_{\alpha\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}}^{-1}\tfrac{1}{2}\alpha^{-2}\mathcal{N}_{\perp}\mathcal{W}_{\alpha\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}}-2\|\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}_{\perp}\|^{2}_{2}-\tfrac{1}{2\alpha^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\mathcal{W}_{\alpha\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}}^{-1}\tfrac{1}{2}\alpha^{-2}\mathcal{N}_{\perp}\mathcal{W}_{\alpha\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}}-C\alpha^{-\frac{2}{7}}\ln\alpha.

(2.28)

Combining what we have so far and using the Weyl asymptotics
N\lesssim\Lambda^{3}=\alpha^{\frac{12}{7}}
, we arrive at

\displaystyle(1+C\alpha^{-\frac{2}{7}}\ln\alpha)\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}\geq e^{\mathrm{Pek}}+\tau Q+\tau^{\prime}\alpha^{-2}\mathcal{W}_{\alpha\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}}^{-1}\,\mathcal{N}\mathcal{W}_{\alpha\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}}-C\alpha^{-\frac{2}{7}}\ln\alpha

(2.29)

for some suitable constant
C
and
\tau^{\prime}=\min\{\tau,\tfrac{1}{2}\}
. As an immediate consequence, we obtain
Q(\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}-e^{\mathrm{Pek}})Q\geq(\tau-C\alpha^{-\frac{2}{7}}\ln\alpha)Q
, which concludes the proof of the first statement of the lemma.

Regarding the second statement, let
\braket{\Psi,\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}\Psi}\leq e^{\mathrm{Pek}}+\alpha^{-\frac{2}{7}}\ln\alpha
. Applying the inequality (
2.29
) yields

\displaystyle\!\tau\|Q\Psi\|^{2}\!+\!\tau^{\prime}\alpha^{-2}\braket{\Psi,\mathcal{W}_{\alpha\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}}^{-1}\,\mathcal{N}\mathcal{W}_{\alpha\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}}\Psi}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq(1\!+\!C\alpha^{-\frac{2}{7}}\!\ln\alpha)\big(e^{\mathrm{Pek}}\!+\!\alpha^{-\frac{2}{7}}\!\ln\alpha\big)\!-e^{\mathrm{Pek}}\!+\!C\alpha^{-\frac{2}{7}}\ln\alpha.

Since
1-\|\pi\Psi\|^{2}=\braket{\Psi,\pi_{\perp}^{2}\Psi}\leq 2\braket{\Psi,\alpha^{\kappa-2}\mathcal{W}_{\alpha\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}}^{-1}\,\mathcal{N}\mathcal{W}_{\alpha\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}}\Psi}
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\|Q\Psi\|^{2}+1-\|\pi\Psi\|^{2}\leq C\alpha^{\kappa-\frac{2}{7}}\ln\alpha.

(2.30)

This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

The next result provides a lower bound for the energy evaluated in a normalized low-energy state
\Psi
in terms of the energy evaluated in the state
\pi\Psi/\|\pi\Psi\|
. Effectively, it shows that cutting off the number of excitations in an approximate ground state only comes at the cost of a subleading error.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
D>0
and consider a normalized state
\Psi\in L^{2}(\Omega)\otimes\mathcal{F}(L^{2}(\Omega))
satisfying
\braket{\Psi,\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}\Psi}\leq\mathscr{E}^{(1)}(\alpha)+D\alpha^{-2}
with
\mathscr{E}^{(1)}(\alpha)=\inf\sigma(\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha})
. Then, there exists a constant
C>0
such that
\braket{\pi\Psi,\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}\pi\Psi}\|\pi\Psi\|^{-2}\leq\braket{\Psi,\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}\Psi}+C\alpha^{-(2+\frac{2}{7}-\kappa)}\ln\alpha
.

Proof.

According to the IMS identity, see
[
25
, Theorem A.1]
and
[
23
, Proposition 6.1]
, we have
\pi\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}\pi+\pi_{\perp}\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}\pi_{\perp}=\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}-\left[\left[\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha},\pi\right],\pi\right]-\left[\left[\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha},\pi_{\perp}\right],\pi_{\perp}\right]
. Furthermore, we can evaluate the double commutators as

\displaystyle\braket{\Psi,\left[\left[\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha},\pi_{\bullet}\right],\pi_{\bullet}\right]\Psi}=\mathfrak{Re}\Big\langle\Psi,\gamma_{\bullet}^{2}\left(\alpha^{-1}a(v_{x}+\varphi^{\mathrm{P}})-\braket{v_{x}+\varphi^{\mathrm{P}},\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}}\right)\Psi\Big\rangle

with

\displaystyle\gamma_{\bullet}:=f_{\bullet}\!\left(\alpha^{\kappa-2}\left(\mathcal{W}_{\alpha\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}}^{-1}\,\mathcal{N}\mathcal{W}_{\alpha\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}}+1\right)\right)-f_{\bullet}\!\left(\alpha^{\kappa-2}\mathcal{W}_{\alpha\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}}^{-1}\,\mathcal{N}\mathcal{W}_{\alpha\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}}\right),

where
\bullet\in\{\varnothing,\perp\}
. Using
v_{x}=[\nabla_{\Omega},\nabla_{\Omega}g_{x}]
with
g_{x}(y)=(-\Delta_{\Omega})^{-3/2}(x,y)
, see Lemma
2.2
, in combination with the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality yields

\displaystyle\braket{\Psi,\left[\left[\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha},\pi_{\bullet}\right],\pi_{\bullet}\right]\Psi}

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{Re}\langle\Psi,\left[\nabla_{\Omega},\gamma_{\bullet}^{2}\left(\alpha^{-1}a(\nabla_{\Omega}g_{x})-\braket{\nabla_{\Omega}g_{x},\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}}\right)\right]\Psi\rangle

\displaystyle\quad-\mathfrak{Re}\langle\Psi,\gamma_{\bullet}^{2}\left(\alpha^{-1}a(\varphi^{\mathrm{P}})-\braket{\varphi^{\mathrm{P}},\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}}\right)\Psi\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\alpha^{2\kappa-4}\braket{\Psi,(-\Delta_{\Omega})\Psi}+\tfrac{3}{2}T_{1,\bullet}+\tfrac{3}{2}T_{2,\bullet}+T_{3,\bullet}+T_{4}

(2.31)

with

\displaystyle T_{1,\bullet}

\displaystyle:=\alpha^{4-2\kappa}\big\|\gamma_{\bullet}^{2}\big(\alpha^{-1}a(\nabla_{\Omega}g_{x})-\braket{\nabla_{\Omega}g_{x},\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}}\big)\Psi\big\|^{2},

(2.32)

\displaystyle T_{2,\bullet}

\displaystyle:=\alpha^{4-2\kappa}\big\|\big(\alpha^{-1}a^{*}(\nabla_{\Omega}g_{x})-\braket{\nabla_{\Omega}g_{x},\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}}\big)\gamma_{\bullet}^{2}\Psi\big\|^{2}

(2.33)

\displaystyle T_{3,\bullet}

\displaystyle:=\alpha^{4-2\kappa}\big\|\gamma_{\bullet}^{2}\Psi\big\|^{2},

(2.34)

\displaystyle T_{4}

\displaystyle:=\alpha^{2\kappa-4}\big\|\big(\alpha^{-1}a(\varphi^{\mathrm{P}})-\braket{\varphi^{\mathrm{P}},\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}}\big)\Psi\big\|^{2}.

(2.35)

Using
\|\gamma_{\bullet}\|\leq\alpha^{\kappa-2}\|f_{\bullet}^{\prime}\|_{\infty}
, we can estimate
T_{3,\bullet}\leq\alpha^{2\kappa-4}\|f_{\bullet}^{\prime}\|_{\infty}^{4}
and

\displaystyle T_{1,\bullet}

\displaystyle\leq 2\alpha^{2\kappa-4}\|f_{\bullet}^{\prime}\|_{\infty}^{4}\big(\alpha^{-2}\|a(\nabla_{\Omega}g_{x})\Psi\|^{2}+\|\braket{\nabla_{\Omega}g_{x},\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}}\Psi\|^{2}\big)

\displaystyle\leq 2\alpha^{2\kappa-4}\|f_{\bullet}^{\prime}\|_{\infty}^{4}\sup_{x\in\Omega}\|\nabla_{\Omega}g_{x}\|^{2}_{2}\big(\braket{\Psi,\alpha^{-2}\mathcal{N}\Psi}+\|\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}\|^{2}_{2}\big).

Similarly
T_{4}\leq 2\alpha^{2\kappa-4}\|\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}\|^{2}_{2}(\braket{\Psi,\alpha^{-2}\mathcal{N}\Psi}+\|\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}\|^{2}_{2})
. In order to estimate
T_{2}
, note the operator inequality

\displaystyle\big(\alpha^{-1}a(\nabla_{\Omega}g_{x})-\braket{\nabla_{\Omega}g_{x},\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}}\big)\!\big(\alpha^{-1}a^{*}(\nabla_{\Omega}g_{x})-\braket{\nabla_{\Omega}g_{x},\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}}\big)\!

\displaystyle\hskip 184.9429pt\leq\!\sup_{x\in\Omega}\|\nabla g_{x}\|^{2}_{2}\alpha^{-2}\big(\mathcal{W}_{\alpha\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}}^{-1}\mathcal{N}\mathcal{W}_{\alpha\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}}+1\big)

(2.36)

and therefore

\displaystyle T_{2,\bullet}

\displaystyle\leq\alpha^{4-2\kappa}\sup_{x\in\Omega}\|\nabla_{\Omega}g_{x}\|^{2}_{2}\braket{\Psi,\gamma_{\bullet}^{2}\big(\alpha^{-2}\mathcal{W}_{\alpha\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}}^{-1}\,\mathcal{N}\mathcal{W}_{\alpha\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}}+\alpha^{-2}\big)\gamma_{\bullet}^{2}\Psi}

\displaystyle=\alpha^{4-2\kappa}\sup_{x\in\Omega}\|\nabla_{\Omega}g_{x}\|_{2}^{2}\braket{\Psi,g_{\bullet}\big(\alpha^{-2}\mathcal{W}_{\alpha\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}}^{-1}\,\mathcal{N}\mathcal{W}_{\alpha\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}}\big)\Psi}

with
g_{\bullet}(t):=\big(f_{\bullet}(\alpha^{\kappa}(t+\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}}))-f_{\bullet}(\alpha^{\kappa}t)\big)^{4}(t+\alpha^{-2})
. Since
|g_{\bullet}(t)|\leq 2\alpha^{4\kappa-8}\|f_{\bullet}^{\prime}\|_{\infty}^{4}
we obtain
T_{2,\bullet}\leq 2\alpha^{2\kappa-4}\sup_{x\in\Omega}\|\nabla_{\Omega}g_{x}\|^{2}_{2}\|f_{\bullet}^{\prime}\|_{\infty}^{4}
. Combining the above estimates with (
2.31
), therefore yields
\braket{\Psi,\left[\left[\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha},\pi_{\bullet}\right],\pi_{\bullet}\right]\Psi}\leq C\alpha^{2\kappa-4}\braket{\Psi,(-\Delta_{\Omega}+\alpha^{-2}\mathcal{N}+1)\Psi}
for a suitable constant
C
. Using Lemma
2.3
and the fact that
\braket{\Psi,\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}\Psi}\leq\mathscr{E}^{(1)}(\alpha)+\frac{D}{\alpha^{2}}
is bounded, we obtain for
\delta=2-2\kappa

\displaystyle\braket{\pi\Psi,\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}\pi\Psi}+\braket{\pi_{\perp}\Psi,\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}\pi_{\perp}\Psi}

\displaystyle=\braket{\Psi,\big(\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}-\left[\left[\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha},\pi\right],\pi\right]-\left[\left[\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha},\pi_{\perp}\right],\pi_{\perp}\right]\big)\Psi}

\displaystyle\leq\braket{\Psi,\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}\Psi}+D_{1}\alpha^{-(2+\delta)}

and therefore

\displaystyle\braket{\pi\Psi,\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}\pi\Psi}+(1-\|\pi\Psi\|^{2})\mathscr{E}^{(1)}(\alpha)

\displaystyle\leq\braket{\pi\Psi,\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}\pi\Psi}+\braket{\pi_{\perp}\Psi,\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}\pi_{\perp}\Psi}

\displaystyle\leq\braket{\Psi,\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}\Psi}+C\alpha^{-(2+\delta)}.

(2.37)

As a direct consequence of our assumption regarding the state
\Psi
, we further have
\braket{\Psi,\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}\Psi}\leq(1-\|\pi\Psi\|^{2})(\mathscr{E}^{(1)}(\alpha)+\frac{C}{\alpha^{2}})+\|\pi\Psi\|^{2}\braket{\Psi,\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}\Psi}
. In combination with Eq. (
2.3
) this yields

\displaystyle\Big\langle\frac{\pi\Psi}{\|\pi\Psi\|},\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}\frac{\pi\Psi}{\|\pi\Psi\|}\Big\rangle\leq\braket{\Psi,\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}\Psi}+\frac{C}{\alpha^{2}}\,\frac{1-\|\pi\Psi\|^{2}}{\|\pi\Psi\|^{2}}+C\frac{\alpha^{-(2+\delta)}}{\|\pi\Psi\|^{2}}.

By Lemma
2.6
we know that
0\leq 1-\|\pi\Psi\|^{2}\leq C\alpha^{\kappa-\frac{2}{7}}\ln\alpha
, which concludes the proof of Lemma
2.7
since
\frac{2}{7}-\kappa\leq\delta=2-2\kappa
for
\kappa<\frac{2}{7}
.
∎

Next, we consider a block decomposition of
{\mathfrak{H}}_{\alpha}
w.r.t. the projections
P
,
Q
and estimate
\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}-e^{\mathrm{Pek}}
by the corresponding Feshbach–Schur complement. To this end, recall the definition of the resolvent
\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha}
from (
2.17
).

Lemma 2.8
.

The following expression defines a quadratic form on
Q(\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha})
,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}_{P}:=P\Big(\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}-e^{\mathrm{Pek}}+(\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}-e^{\mathrm{Pek}})\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha}(\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}-e^{\mathrm{Pek}})\Big)P,

(2.38)

and satisfies the estimate
\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}-e^{\mathrm{Pek}}\geq\mathfrak{F}_{P}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.6
it is clear that
Q(\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}-e^{\mathrm{Pek}})Q
is invertible as an operator on
QL^{2}\!\left(\Omega\right)\otimes\mathcal{F}
with bounded inverse
-\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha}
, and therefore
\mathfrak{F}_{P}
is well-defined. Furthermore, we have the algebraic identity
\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}-e^{\mathrm{Pek}}=\mathfrak{F}_{P}+X^{*}Q(\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}-e^{\mathrm{Pek}})QX
with
X:=Q-\mathfrak{R}_{\alpha}Q(\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}-e^{\mathrm{Pek}})P
. Since
Q(\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}-e^{\mathrm{Pek}})Q\geq 0
by Lemma
2.6
it is clear that
X^{*}Q(\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}-e^{\mathrm{Pek}})QX\geq 0
as well, which concludes the proof of Lemma
2.8
.
∎

In the next lemma, we compare the min-max values of
\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}
with the min-max values of the operator
\pi\mathfrak{F}_{P}\pi
with
\pi
defined by (
2.25
).

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
\mathscr{E}^{(n)}(\alpha)
denote the
nth
min-max value of
\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}
as defined in (
2.2
) and assume that
\mathscr{E}^{(n)}(\alpha)\leq\mathscr{E}^{(1)}(\alpha)+D\alpha^{-2}
for some
D>0
and all large
\alpha
. Then, there exist
C>0
,
\kappa\in(0,\tfrac{2}{7})
such that

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}^{(n)}(\alpha)\geq e^{\mathrm{Pek}}+\underset{\mathrm{dim}\mathcal{V}=n}{\inf_{\mathcal{V}\subset\pi\mathcal{F}}}\,\,\,\sup_{\Psi\in\mathcal{V}:\|\Psi\|=1}\braket{\Psi,\mathfrak{F}_{P}\Psi}-C\alpha^{-(2+\frac{2}{7}-\kappa)}\ln\alpha,

where we interpret
\mathfrak{F}_{P}
as a quadratic form on
\mathcal{F}
via
\textnormal{Ran}P\otimes\mathcal{F}\cong\mathcal{F}
.

Proof.

Given an arbitrary
0\leq\epsilon\leq C\alpha^{-2}
, let
\mathcal{V}\subseteq L^{2}(\Omega)\otimes\mathcal{F}
be an
n
dimensional subspace satisfying
\sup_{\Psi\in\mathcal{V}:\|\Psi\|=1}\braket{\Psi,\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}\Psi}\leq\mathscr{E}^{(n)}(\alpha)+\epsilon
. By our assumption on
\mathscr{E}^{(n)}(\alpha)
, any state
\Psi\in\mathcal{V}
satisfies the assumption of Lemma
2.7
. Consequently,

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}^{(n)}(\alpha)\geq\sup_{\Psi\in\mathcal{V}:\|\Psi\|=1}\Big\langle\tfrac{\pi\Psi}{\|\pi\Psi\|},\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}\tfrac{\pi\Psi}{\|\pi\Psi\|}\Big\rangle-C\alpha^{-(2+\frac{2}{7}-\kappa)}\ln\alpha-\epsilon.

\displaystyle\left(1-\tfrac{\|Q\pi\Psi\|^{2}}{\|\pi\Psi\|^{2}}\right)\Big\langle\tfrac{P\pi\Psi}{\|P\pi\Psi\|},\mathfrak{F}_{P}\tfrac{P\pi\Psi}{\|P\pi\Psi\|}\Big\rangle\geq\Big\langle\tfrac{P\pi\Psi}{\|P\pi\Psi\|},\mathfrak{F}_{P}\tfrac{P\pi\Psi}{\|P\pi\Psi\|}\Big\rangle-C\alpha^{-(2+\frac{2}{7}-\kappa)}\ln\alpha

(2.40)

for any

. Using Eq. (

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}^{(n)}(\alpha)

\displaystyle\geq e^{\mathrm{Pek}}+\sup_{\Psi\in\mathcal{V}:\|\Psi\|=1}\Big\langle\tfrac{P\pi\Psi}{\|P\pi\Psi\|},\mathfrak{F}_{P}\tfrac{P\pi\Psi}{\|P\pi\Psi\|}\Big\rangle-C\alpha^{-(2+\frac{2}{7}-\kappa)}\ln\alpha

\displaystyle=e^{\mathrm{Pek}}+\sup_{\Psi\in P\pi\mathcal{V}:\|\Psi\|=1}\Big\langle\Psi,\mathfrak{F}_{P}\Psi\Big\rangle-C\alpha^{-(2+\frac{2}{7}-\kappa)}\ln\alpha.

\pi\mathcal{F}

3
Asymptotic series for low-energy eigenvalues

The main goal of this section is to derive an inequality of the form (
1.28

2.1
, this will be used to prove Theorem
1.2
. For the definition of approximate eigenstates, we need to pay attention to the domain of
\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}
. To this end, let us recall the fact that the domain of
\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}
is related to the domain of
-\Delta_{\Omega}+\mathcal{N}
via an
x
-dependent Weyl operator, called the Gross transformation
[
14
,
18
]
. In the following sections, we will directly work with the Gross transformed Fröhlich Hamiltonian so that our approximate eigenstates will be elements of the domain of the non-interacting Hamiltonian.

3.1
Gross transformed Hamiltonian

The Gross transformation is defined for
\Lambda\geq 0
as the
x
-dependent Weyl operator

\displaystyle U_{\Lambda}:=\mathcal{W}_{g_{x}^{\Lambda}/\alpha}=e^{\frac{1}{\alpha}(a(g^{\Lambda}_{x})-a^{*}(g^{\Lambda}_{x}))}

(3.1)

with
g^{\Lambda}_{x}=(\Pi_{\Lambda}-1)g_{x}
,
g_{x}(y)=(-\Delta_{\Omega})^{-3/2}(x,y)
and
\Pi_{\Lambda}=\mathds{1}(-\Delta_{\Omega}\leq\Lambda^{2})
. Note that for
\Lambda=\infty
, we have
g_{x}^{\infty}\equiv 0
for all
x\in\Omega
and thus
U_{\infty}=\mathds{1}
.

To simplify the notation, let us from now on abbreviate
p:=-i\nabla_{\Omega}
. A simple computation using the shift properties of Weyl operators shows that

\displaystyle U_{\Lambda}p^{2}U^{*}_{\Lambda}

\displaystyle=p^{2}+\tfrac{1}{\alpha}(2a^{*}(pg^{\Lambda}_{x})p+2pa(pg^{\Lambda}_{x})+\phi(p^{2}g^{\Lambda}_{x}))+\tfrac{1}{\alpha^{2}}\phi(pg^{\Lambda}_{x})^{2}

(3.2)

\displaystyle U_{\Lambda}\mathcal{N}U^{*}_{\Lambda}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{N}+\tfrac{1}{\alpha}\phi(g^{\Lambda}_{x})+\tfrac{1}{\alpha^{2}}\|g^{\Lambda}_{x}\|^{2}_{2}

(3.3)

\displaystyle U_{\Lambda}\phi(v_{x}+\varphi^{\mathrm{P}})U^{*}_{\Lambda}

\displaystyle=\phi(v_{x}+\varphi^{\mathrm{P}})+\tfrac{2}{\alpha}\Re\langle v_{x}+\varphi^{\mathrm{P}},g^{\Lambda}_{x}\rangle.

(3.4)

With these relations, the Gross transformed Fröhlich Hamiltonian (after conjugation with the Weyl operator
\mathcal{W}_{\alpha\varphi}
; note that
[U_{\Lambda},\mathcal{W}_{\alpha\varphi}]=0
) is found to be

\displaystyle\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha,\Lambda}^{{\textnormal{G}}}

\displaystyle:=U_{\Lambda}\mathcal{W}_{\alpha\varphi}({\mathfrak{H}}_{\alpha}-e^{\mathrm{Pek}})\mathcal{W}_{\alpha\varphi}^{-1}U^{*}_{\Lambda}

\displaystyle=U_{\Lambda}\big(H_{0}+\tfrac{1}{\alpha}\phi(v_{x}+\varphi^{\mathrm{P}})+\tfrac{1}{\alpha^{2}}\mathcal{N}\big)U^{*}_{\Lambda}

\displaystyle=H_{0}+\frac{1}{\alpha}\Big(\phi(v_{x}+\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}+p^{2}g_{x}^{\Lambda})+2a^{*}(pg^{\Lambda}_{x})p+2pa(pg^{\Lambda}_{x})\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}}\big(\mathcal{N}+\phi(pg_{x}^{\Lambda})^{2}+2\Re\langle v_{x}+\varphi^{\mathrm{P}},g^{\Lambda}_{x}\rangle\Big)+\frac{1}{\alpha^{3}}\phi(g^{\Lambda}_{x})+\frac{1}{\alpha^{4}}\|g^{\Lambda}_{x}\|^{2}_{2}

(3.5)

where
H_{0}
is the Pekar Hamiltonian defined in (
1.9
).
For coefficients
(E_{\ell})_{\ell\geq 0}
we set

\displaystyle K_{1}

\displaystyle:=\phi(v_{x}+\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}+p^{2}g_{x}^{\Lambda})+2a^{*}(pg_{x}^{\Lambda})p+2pa(pg^{\Lambda}_{x})

(3.6a)

\displaystyle K_{2}

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{N}+\phi(pg^{\Lambda}_{x})^{2}+2\Re\langle v_{x}+\varphi^{\mathrm{P}},g^{\Lambda}_{x}\rangle-E_{0}

(3.6b)

\displaystyle K_{3}

\displaystyle:=\phi(g^{\Lambda}_{x})-E_{1}

(3.6c)

\displaystyle K_{4}

\displaystyle:=\|g^{\Lambda}_{x}\|^{2}_{2}-E_{2}

(3.6d)

\displaystyle K_{\ell}

\displaystyle:=-E_{\ell-2}\quad\forall\ell\geq 5.

(3.6e)

For
\Lambda=\infty
, we have
K_{\ell}=V_{\ell}
with
V_{\ell}
defined by (
1.15
)–(
1.17
).

With the above definitions, we can write

\displaystyle\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha,\Lambda}^{\textnormal{G}}-\sum_{\ell=0}^{b}\alpha^{-\ell-2}E_{\ell}=H_{0}+K\quad\text{with}\quad K:=\sum_{\ell=1}^{b+2}\alpha^{-\ell}K_{\ell}.

(3.7)

The next lemma shows that up to second order, the expansion around
P
of the Gross transformed Hamiltonian coincides with the expansion of the original Fröhlich Hamiltonian. We recall that
\mathbb{H}_{0}
does not depend on
\Lambda
and note that for
\Lambda=\infty
, the statement of the lemma holds by definition.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
P=|\psi^{\mathrm{P}}\rangle\langle\psi^{\mathrm{P}}|
,
\mathbb{H}_{0}
defined by (
1.11
) and
E_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
. For every
\Lambda\geq 0
, the operators
K_{1}
,
K_{2}
defined by (

) and (

) satisfy

\displaystyle PK_{1}P+P(K_{2}+K_{1}RK_{1})P=P\otimes(\mathbb{H}_{0}-E_{0}).

(3.8)

Proof of Lemma
3.1
.

First note that
PK_{1}P=0
, which is a consequence of
\langle\psi^{\mathrm{P}},v_{\cdot}\psi^{\mathrm{P}}\rangle=-\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}
and
2\langle\psi^{\mathrm{P}},(pg^{\Lambda}_{\cdot})p\psi^{\mathrm{P}}\rangle=-\langle\psi^{\mathrm{P}},(p^{2}g^{\Lambda}_{\cdot})\psi^{\mathrm{P}}\rangle
together with integration by parts and the fact that
\psi^{\mathrm{P}}
is real-valued.

For the remaining term, note that

\displaystyle\phi(pg^{\Lambda}_{x})^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\big[[H_{0},a^{*}(g^{\Lambda}_{x})-a(g^{\Lambda}_{x})],a^{*}(g^{\Lambda}_{x})-a(g^{\Lambda}_{x})\big]

(3.9)

and thus, using
H_{0}\psi=0
,

\displaystyle\Big\langle\psi^{\mathrm{P}},\phi(pg^{\Lambda}_{x})^{2}\psi^{\mathrm{P}}\Big\rangle

\displaystyle=-\Big\langle\psi^{\mathrm{P}},(a^{*}(g^{\Lambda}_{x})-a(g^{\Lambda}_{x}))H_{0}(a^{*}(g^{\Lambda}_{x})-a(g^{\Lambda}_{x}))\psi^{\mathrm{P}}\Big\rangle_{L^{2}}.

(3.10)

On the other hand, we use

\displaystyle 2pa(pg^{\Lambda}_{x})+2a^{*}(g^{\Lambda}_{x})p

\displaystyle=\big\{p,a(pg^{\Lambda}_{x})+a^{*}(pg^{\Lambda}_{x})\big\}-\phi(p^{2}g^{\Lambda}_{x})

\displaystyle=\big[H_{0},a^{*}(g^{\Lambda}_{x})-a(g^{\Lambda}_{x})\big]-\phi(p^{2}g^{\Lambda}_{x})

(3.11)

and again
PH_{0}=0
in order to find
PK_{1}Q=P\phi(v_{x})Q-P(a^{*}(g^{\Lambda}_{x})-a(g^{\Lambda}_{x}))QH_{0}

and thus

\displaystyle\langle\psi,K_{1}RK_{1}\psi\rangle_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\langle\psi^{\mathrm{P}},\phi(v_{\cdot})R\phi(v_{\cdot})\psi^{\mathrm{P}}\rangle_{L^{2}}-\langle\psi^{\mathrm{P}},\phi(v_{\cdot})Q(a^{*}(g^{\Lambda}_{\cdot})-a(g^{\Lambda}_{\cdot}))\psi^{\mathrm{P}}\rangle_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\langle\psi^{\mathrm{P}},(a^{*}(g^{\Lambda}_{\cdot})-a(g^{\Lambda}_{\cdot}))Q\phi(v_{\cdot})\psi^{\mathrm{P}}\rangle_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\langle\psi^{\mathrm{P}},(a^{*}(g^{\Lambda}_{\cdot})-a(g_{\cdot}))(H_{0}-\mu)(a^{*}(g^{\Lambda}_{\cdot})-a(g^{\Lambda}_{\cdot}))\psi^{\mathrm{P}}\rangle_{L^{2}}.

(3.12)

Further note that

\displaystyle\langle\psi^{\mathrm{P}},\phi(v_{\cdot})Q(a^{*}(g^{\Lambda}_{\cdot})-a(g^{\Lambda}_{\cdot}))\psi^{\mathrm{P}}\rangle_{L^{2}}-\langle\psi^{\mathrm{P}},(a^{*}(g^{\Lambda}_{\cdot})-a(g^{\Lambda}_{\cdot}))Q\phi(v_{\cdot})\psi^{\mathrm{P}}\rangle_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 156.49014pt=\langle\psi^{\mathrm{P}},[\phi(v_{\cdot}+\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}),a^{*}(g^{\Lambda}_{\cdot})-a(g^{\Lambda}_{\cdot})]\psi^{\mathrm{P}}\rangle_{L^{2}}

(3.13)

since
P\phi(v_{\cdot})Q=P\phi(v_{\cdot}+\varphi^{\mathrm{P}})Q=P\phi(v_{\cdot}+\varphi^{\mathrm{P}})
.

Taking all the above into account, we arrive at

\displaystyle\langle\psi^{\mathrm{P}},(K_{2}+K_{1}RK_{1})\psi^{\mathrm{P}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\mathcal{N}+\langle\psi^{\mathrm{P}},\phi(v_{\cdot})R\phi(v_{\cdot})\psi^{\mathrm{P}}\rangle+2\langle\psi^{\mathrm{P}},\Re\langle v_{x}+\varphi^{\mathrm{P}},g^{\Lambda}_{x}\rangle\psi^{\mathrm{P}}\rangle-E_{0}

\displaystyle\quad-\langle\psi^{\mathrm{P}},[\phi(v_{\cdot}+\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}),a^{*}(g^{\Lambda}_{\cdot})-a(g^{\Lambda}_{\cdot})]\psi^{\mathrm{P}}\rangle.

(3.14)

The commutator gives
[\phi(v_{x}+\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}),a^{*}(g^{\Lambda}_{x})-a(g^{\Lambda}_{x})]=2\Re\langle v_{x}+\varphi^{\mathrm{P}},g^{\Lambda}_{x}\rangle

and thus

\displaystyle\langle\psi^{\mathrm{P}},(K_{2}+K_{1}RK_{1})\psi^{\mathrm{P}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\mathcal{N}+\langle\psi^{\mathrm{P}},\phi(v_{\cdot})R\phi(v_{\cdot})\psi^{\mathrm{P}}\rangle-E_{0}=\mathbb{H}_{0}-E_{0}

as claimed.
∎

We fix
\mathsf{E}^{(n)}

Proposition 3.2

\displaystyle(H_{0}+K)\Psi_{\xi,b}=\bigg(\sum_{i=0}^{2b+3}PK(RK)^{i}\bigg)\psi^{\mathrm{P}}\otimes\xi+QK(RK)^{2b+3}\psi^{\mathrm{P}}\otimes\xi.

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{2b+3}PK(RK)^{i}P

), this provides an extension of (

) (with

replaced by

) to higher orders. This identity is the reason for the definition of

from (

,

, one finds

\displaystyle\alpha^{-2}(\mathbb{H}_{0}-\mathsf{E}^{(n)}+\mathbb{V})\xi_{b}=\alpha^{-2}\langle\Gamma,\mathbb{V}\xi_{b}\rangle\Gamma+\alpha^{-2}\mathbb{Q}\mathbb{V}(\mathbb{R}\mathbb{V})^{b}\Gamma

(3.21)

where
\Gamma
denotes the eigenstate associated with
\mathsf{E}^{(n)}

\Gamma

are chosen such that this term is of order

(H_{0}+K)\Psi_{b}=O\big(\alpha^{-b-3}\big)
.

The rigorous version of this argument is based on the next two lemmas. Recall that

,

and

Lemma 3.3
.

Let

,
\mathbb{X}_{0}=\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{X}_{1}=\mathds{1}

and for all

\displaystyle\|X_{0}(K_{\ell}-V_{\ell})Y(\mathcal{N}+1)^{-1}\|

(3.23)

\displaystyle\|(\mathcal{N}+1)^{\ell^{\prime}/2}\mathbb{X}_{i}\mathbb{V}_{\ell}(\mathcal{N}+1)^{-(\ell^{\prime}+\ell+2i)/2}\|

Recall that
p=-i\nabla_{\Omega}
. First note that

is at most quadratic in creation and annihilation operators. For

one proceeds in close analogy to the proof of Lemma

. That is, we use

in combination with the estimates from Lemma
2.2
, to verify

uniformly in
\Lambda\geq 0
for all
\ell\geq 1
. For
i=1
, there is no operator on the left of

that can be used to improve the
\Lambda

\displaystyle K_{1}=\phi(v_{x}+\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}+p^{2}g_{x}^{\Lambda})+2a^{*}(pg_{x}^{\Lambda})p+2a(pg^{\Lambda}_{x})p+2a(p^{2}g^{\Lambda}_{x})

and using
[

, which states that

\displaystyle\|K_{1}Y(\mathcal{N}+1)^{-1/2}\|\leq C\sup_{x\in\Omega}\Big(\|v_{x}+\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}+p^{2}g_{x}^{\Lambda}\|_{2}+\|pg_{x}^{\Lambda}\|_{2}\Big).

. For
K_{\ell}

2.2
. This completes the bound for

.

For the second bound, note that by Lemma

for

\|X_{0}(K_{\ell}-V_{\ell})Y(\mathcal{N}+1)^{-\ell}\|\leq C(\Lambda^{-1}\ln\Lambda)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\|X_{0}(K_{1}-V_{1})Y(\mathcal{N}+1)^{-1/2}\|

\displaystyle\leq C(\Lambda^{-1}\ln\Lambda)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\|X_{0}\phi((1-\Pi_{\Lambda})v_{x})(\mathcal{N}+1)^{-1/2}\|.

For the third bound, note that

\displaystyle\|(\mathcal{N}+1)^{\ell^{\prime}/2}\mathbb{V}_{\ell}(\mathcal{N}+1)^{-(\ell^{\prime}+\ell+2)/2}\|\leq C(\ell,\ell^{\prime}).

\displaystyle\|(\mathcal{N}+1)^{\ell^{\prime}/2}\mathbb{V}_{\ell}\mathbb{R}(\mathcal{N}+1)^{-(\ell^{\prime}+\ell)/2}\|\leq C(\ell,\ell^{\prime})\|(\mathcal{N}+1)^{(\ell^{\prime}+\ell+2)/2}\mathbb{R}(\mathcal{N}+1)^{-(\ell^{\prime}+\ell)/2}\|

Lemma 3.4
.

Choose
\mathsf{E}^{(n_{s})}
as in Proposition
3.2
and let
\zeta\in\mathcal{F}
satisfy
\mathds{1}(\mathcal{N}\leq m)\mathbb{U}\zeta=\mathbb{U}\zeta
with
\mathbb{U}

) and some

. For every

\Lambda\geq 2
,
\alpha>0

\displaystyle\bigg\|\bigg(\sum_{i=0}^{2b+3}PK(RK)^{i}P-\alpha^{-2}\big(\mathbb{H}_{0}-\mathsf{E}^{(n_{s})}+\mathbb{V}\big)\bigg)\zeta\bigg\|\leq C(b)\bigg(\sqrt{\frac{\ln\Lambda}{\Lambda}}\alpha^{-3}+\alpha^{-b-3}\bigg).

(3.32)

Proof.

By Lemma
3.1
, we have
\sum_{i=0}^{2b+3}PK(RK)^{i}P-\alpha^{-2}\big(\mathbb{H}_{0}-\mathsf{E}^{(n)}\big)=\mathbb{W}_{K}

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{K}=\sum_{\ell=3}^{b+2}\alpha^{-\ell}PK_{\ell}P+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,\ell=1\\
k+\ell\geq 3\end{subarray}}^{b+2}\alpha^{-\ell-k}PK_{\ell}PK_{k}P+\sum_{i=2}^{2b+3}PK(RK)^{i}P.

(3.33)

We compare this operator with

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{V}=\sum_{\ell=3}^{b+2}\alpha^{-\ell}PV_{\ell}P+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,\ell=1\\
k+\ell\geq 3\end{subarray}}^{b+2}\alpha^{-\ell-k}PV_{\ell}RV_{k}P+\sum_{i=2}^{2b+3}PV(RV)^{i}P,

where

. By Lemma
3.3
, each replacement of
K_{\ell}
by
V_{\ell}

\displaystyle\big\|(\mathbb{W}_{K}-\mathbb{W}_{V})\zeta\big\|\leq C(b)\alpha^{-3}(\Lambda^{-1}\ln\Lambda)^{1/2}\|(\mathcal{N}+1)^{(2b+4)}\zeta\|.

(3.35)

To proceed we sort all terms in
\mathbb{W}_{V}
according to powers of
\alpha^{-1}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=3}^{b+2}\alpha^{-\ell}PV_{\ell}P

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=3}^{b+2}\alpha^{-\ell}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{N}\\
|\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}|=\ell\end{subarray}}PV_{\epsilon_{1}}P

(3.36)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,\ell=1\\
k+\ell\geq 3\end{subarray}}^{b+2}\alpha^{-\ell-k}PV_{\ell}RV_{k}P

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=3}^{2(b+2)}\alpha^{-\ell}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{N}^{2}\\
|\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}|=\ell\end{subarray}}PV_{\epsilon_{1}}(RV_{\epsilon_{2}})P

(3.37)

and similarly for the last term,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=2}^{2b+3}PV(RV)^{i}P

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=3}^{2b+4}\sum_{\ell=3}^{(b+2)(2b+4)}\alpha^{-\ell}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{N}^{i}\\
|\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}|=\ell\end{subarray}}PV_{\varepsilon_{1}}(RV_{\varepsilon_{2}})\ldots(RV_{\varepsilon_{i}})P,

(3.38)

where we used that all summands with

are zero. Adding the three contributions and using (
1.18
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{V}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=3}^{b+2}\alpha^{-\ell}\mathbb{V}_{\ell-2}+\sum_{i=2}^{2b+4}\sum_{\ell=b+3}^{(b+2)(2b+4)}\alpha^{-\ell}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{N}^{i}\\

(3.39)

where we also used that

\displaystyle\big\|(\mathbb{W}_{V}-\alpha^{-2}\mathbb{V})\zeta\big\|\leq\frac{C(b)}{\alpha^{b+3}}\big\|(\mathcal{N}+1)^{(b+2)(2b+4)/2}\zeta\big\|.

(3.40)

Finally, we use Lemma

and the assumption on

\displaystyle\|(\mathcal{N}+1)^{(b+2)(2b+4)/2}\zeta\|\leq C(b)\|(\mathcal{N}+1)^{(b+2)(2b+4)/2}\mathbb{U}\zeta\|\leq C(b).

(3.41)

∎

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{b(b+1)}\frac{1}{\alpha^{\ell}}\sum_{i=1}^{\min\{\ell,b+1\}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{N}^{i}\\

\displaystyle\|(P\otimes\mathbb{P})\mathbb{V}\psi^{\mathrm{P}}\otimes\xi_{b}\|

3.3
Proofs of Theorems
1.2
and
1.6

In this section we use Propositions
2.1
and
3.2
to prove Theorems
1.2
and
1.6
. Since
E_{\ell}
is chosen as specified in Eq. (
1.25
), it is sufficient to prove Theorem
1.2
.

Before we begin, we recall the following simple fact. Consider a self-adjoint operator
A
on a Hilbert space
\mathscr{H}
with discrete spectrum and count the eigenvalues as
\mu^{(1)}(A)\leq\mu^{(2)}(A)\leq\ldots
Assume that a set of normalized states
u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\in D(A)
and two positive numbers
\varepsilon,\delta
satisfy
|\langle u_{i},u_{j}\rangle|\leq\varepsilon
for all
i\neq j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
and
\|Au_{i}\|\leq\delta
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Then, there exists a
\delta
-independent constant
C_{\varepsilon}>0
such that
A
has at least
n
(possibly equivalent) eigenvalues in the interval
[-C_{\varepsilon}\delta,C_{\varepsilon}\delta]
. Note that
C_{\varepsilon}=1
in case
n=1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

To enhance the clarity of the argument, let us first focus on eigenvalues
\mathscr{E}^{(n)}(\alpha)
such that
\mathsf{E}^{(n)}
is non-degenerate. We introduce
\varepsilon^{(1)}:=\mathsf{E}^{(2)}-\mathsf{E}^{(1)}>0
and
\varepsilon^{(n)}:=\min\{\mathsf{E}^{(n+1)}-\mathsf{E}^{(n)},\mathsf{E}^{(n)}-\mathsf{E}^{(n-1)}\}>0

for
n\geq 2
. Moreover, we recall the unitary equivalence
\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha,\Lambda}^{\textnormal{G}}-e^{\mathrm{Pek}}\cong\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}
by (
3.5
) and observe that
\|\Psi_{b}\|\geq 1
. With this at hand, we can apply Proposition
3.2
to see that the spectrum
\sigma(\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}-e^{\mathrm{Pek}})
is non-empty around the approximate eigenvalue
\mathscr{E}_{b}^{(n)}(\alpha):=\alpha^{-2}\mathsf{E}^{(n)}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{b}\alpha^{-\ell-2}E_{\ell}
. More precisely,

\displaystyle\sigma\big(\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}-e^{\mathrm{Pek}}\big)\cap\Big[\mathscr{E}_{b}^{(n)}(\alpha)-\frac{C(b)}{\alpha^{b+3}},\mathscr{E}_{b}^{(n)}(\alpha)+\frac{C(b)}{\alpha^{b+3}}\Big]

(3.53)

contains at least one eigenvalue.
For
\alpha\geq\alpha(b)
with large enough
\alpha(b)
, we further have

\displaystyle\Big[\mathscr{E}_{b}^{(n)}(\alpha)-\frac{C(b)}{\alpha^{b+3}},\mathscr{E}_{b}^{(n)}(\alpha)+\frac{C(b)}{\alpha^{b+3}}\Big]\subset\Big[\alpha^{-2}\big(\mathsf{E}^{(n)}-\tfrac{1}{3}\varepsilon^{(n)}\big),\alpha^{-2}\big(\mathsf{E}^{(n)}+\tfrac{1}{3}\varepsilon^{(n)}\big)\Big].

(3.54)

By Proposition
2.1
, on the other hand, we know that the only eigenvalue in the interval on the right side is
\mathscr{E}^{(n)}(\alpha)-e^{\mathrm{Pek}}
. Concretely, we apply Proposition
2.1
to the neighboring eigenvalues to obtain

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}^{(n+1)}(\alpha)-e^{\mathrm{Pek}}

\displaystyle\geq\alpha^{-2}\mathsf{E}^{(n)}+\alpha^{-2}\varepsilon^{(n)}-C\alpha^{-2-\delta}\geq\alpha^{-2}\big(\mathsf{E}^{(n)}+\tfrac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{(n)}\big)

(3.55)

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}^{(n-1)}(\alpha)-e^{\mathrm{Pek}}

\displaystyle\leq\alpha^{-2}\mathsf{E}^{(n)}+\alpha^{-2}\varepsilon^{(n)}-C\alpha^{-2-\delta}\leq\alpha^{-2}\big(\mathsf{E}^{(n)}-\tfrac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{(n)}\big)

(3.56)

for all
\alpha\geq\alpha_{0}
for suitable
\alpha_{0}>0
and
\delta>0
. Thus, we can conclude that
\sigma(\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}-e^{\mathrm{Pek}})\cap[\mathscr{E}^{(n)}_{b}(\alpha)-C(b)\alpha^{-b-3},\mathscr{E}^{(n)}_{b}(\alpha)+C(b)\alpha^{b+3}]=\{\mathscr{E}^{(n)}(\alpha)-e^{\mathrm{Pek}}\}
, which completes the proof of Theorem
1.2
in the non-degenerate case.

Since the general case can be treated in complete analogy, we shall present the argument for a two-fold degenerate eigenvalue, that is, for
\mathsf{E}^{(n-1)}<\mathsf{E}^{(n)}=\mathsf{E}^{(n+1)}<\mathsf{E}^{(n+2)}
.
In this case, we have two sets of coefficients and two approximate eigenstates (
3.16
), which we denote by
E^{(n)}_{\ell}
,
E^{(n+1)}_{\ell}
and
\Psi^{(n)}_{b}
,
\Psi_{b}^{(n+1)}
, respectively. Using Lemma
3.3
, it follows that
\|\Psi^{(j)}_{b}-\psi^{\mathrm{P}}\otimes\mathscr{U}\Gamma^{(j)}\|\to 0
as
\alpha\to\infty
for

and thus,
|\langle\Psi^{(n)}_{b},\Psi^{(n+1)}_{b}\rangle|\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\|\Psi^{(n)}_{b}\|\,\|\Psi^{(n+1)}_{b}\|
for large
\alpha
, since
\langle\Gamma^{(n)},\Gamma^{(n+1)}\rangle=0
by assumption and
\|\Psi^{(j)}_{b}\|\geq 1
.

Now assume that
E_{\ell}^{(n)}=E_{\ell}^{(n+1)}
for
1\leq\ell\leq b
and set
\mathscr{E}_{b}^{(n)}(\alpha)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{b}\alpha^{-2-\ell}E^{(n)}_{\ell}
. As a consequence of Proposition
3.2
, we know that there is a constant
C(b)
such that

\displaystyle\sigma\big(\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}-e^{\mathrm{Pek}}\big)\cap\Big[\mathscr{E}_{b}^{(n)}(\alpha)-\frac{C(b)}{\alpha^{b+3}},\mathscr{E}_{b}^{(n)}(\alpha)+\frac{C(b)}{\alpha^{b+3}}\Big]

(3.57)

contains at least two eigenvalues. In analogy to the non-degenerate case, we know by Proposition
2.1
that the only possible elements of the spectrum in the above set are the eigenvalues
\mathscr{E}^{(n)}(\alpha)-e^{\mathrm{Pek}}
,
\mathscr{E}^{(n+1)}(\alpha)-e^{\mathrm{Pek}}
. This proves the statement if
E_{\ell}^{(n)}=E_{\ell}^{(n+1)}
for all
1\leq\ell\leq b
.

On the other hand, if there is an integer
1\leq\ell_{0}\leq b
with
E^{(n)}_{\ell_{0}}\neq E_{\ell_{0}}^{(n+1)}
, then the two intervals

\displaystyle I^{(j)}:=\Big[\mathscr{E}^{(j)}_{b}(\alpha)-\frac{C(b)}{\alpha^{3+b}},\mathscr{E}^{(j)}_{b}(\alpha)+\frac{C(b)}{\alpha^{3+b}}\Big],\quad j\in\{n,n+1\}

(3.58)

with
\mathscr{E}^{(j)}_{b}(\alpha)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{b}\alpha^{-2-\ell}E^{(j)}_{\ell}
are disjoint for large
\alpha
. By Proposition
3.2
, we know that each set
\sigma(\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}-e^{\mathrm{Pek}})\cap I^{(j)}
, for
j=n
and
j=n+1
, contains at least one eigenvalue. By Proposition
2.1
, we know again that the only possible elements of the spectrum in
\sigma(\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}-e^{\mathrm{Pek}})\cap(I^{(n)}\cup I^{(n+1)})
are the eigenvalues
\mathscr{E}^{(n)}(\alpha)-e^{\mathrm{Pek}}
,
\mathscr{E}^{(n+1)}(\alpha)-e^{\mathrm{Pek}}
. Since
E^{(n)}_{\ell_{0}}<E^{(n+1)}_{\ell_{0}}
by definition and
\mathscr{E}^{(n)}(\alpha)\leq\mathscr{E}^{(n+1)}(\alpha)
by assumption, this implies that
\sigma(\mathfrak{H}_{\alpha}-e^{\mathrm{Pek}})\cap I^{(j)}=\{\mathscr{E}^{(j)}(\alpha)-e^{\mathrm{Pek}}\}
for
j\in\{n,n+1\}
. This completes the proof of the theorem in the two-fold degenerate case. Extending the argument to higher degeneracy is straightforward.
∎

Proof of Proposition

Lemma 4.2
.

For
b\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

\Phi_{b}

Write

\displaystyle=Q(H_{0}+K)\widetilde{\Phi}_{b}+Q(H_{0}+K)(\Phi_{b}-\widetilde{\Phi}_{b})

\alpha

and
\pi_{\mathcal{H}_{r}}
is the orthogonal projection onto
\mathcal{H}_{r}
. Making use of Lemma
3.3
, we know that
\|(YW_{\ell}^{i}Z)|_{\mathcal{H}_{r}}\|\leq D\sqrt{(r+1)\dots(r+\ell)}
and consequently

\displaystyle\|(PW_{\ell_{1}}^{i_{1}}RW_{\ell_{2}}^{i_{2}}R^{\frac{1}{2}})|_{\mathcal{H}_{r}}\|\leq D^{2}\sqrt{(r+1)\dots(r+\ell_{1}+\ell_{2})}.

(4.31)

Further note that
Z_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}^{i_{1},i_{2}}:=PW_{\ell_{1}}^{i_{1}}RW_{\ell_{2}}^{i_{2}}R^{\frac{1}{2}}
satisfies
Z_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}^{i_{1},i_{2}}\mathcal{H}_{=r}\subset\mathcal{H}_{=r+i_{1}+i_{2}}
, where
\mathcal{H}_{=r}:=\mathcal{H}_{r}\cap\mathcal{H}_{r-1}^{\perp}
, and consequently
Z_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}^{i_{1},i_{2}}\mathcal{H}_{=r}\perp Z_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}^{i_{1},i_{2}}\mathcal{H}_{=s}
for
r\neq s
. Decomposing an arbitrary state
\Psi\in\mathcal{H}_{r}
into
\Psi=\sum_{s=0}^{r}\Psi_{s}
such that
\Psi_{s}\in\mathcal{H}_{=s}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{U}^{\dagger}(\mathcal{N}+1)^{-1}\mathbb{U}PW_{\ell_{1}}^{i_{1}}RW_{\ell_{2}}^{i_{2}}R^{\frac{1}{2}}\Psi\|^{2}=\sum_{s=s_{0}}^{r}\|\mathbb{U}^{\dagger}(\mathcal{N}+1)^{-1}\mathbb{U}PW_{\ell_{1}}^{i_{1}}RW_{\ell_{2}}^{i_{2}}R^{\frac{1}{2}}\Psi_{s}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\ \ =\sum_{s=s_{0}}^{r}\frac{1}{(s+i_{1}+i_{2}+1)^{2}}\|PW_{\ell_{1}}^{i_{1}}RW_{\ell_{2}}^{i_{2}}R^{\frac{1}{2}}\Psi_{s}\|^{2}\leq\sum_{s=s_{0}}^{r}D^{2}\frac{(s+1)\dots(s+\ell_{1}+\ell_{2})}{(s+i_{1}+i_{2}+1)^{2}}\|\Psi_{s}\|^{2}

(4.32)

with
s_{0}:=\max\{0,-i_{1}-i_{2}\}
, where we have used Eq. (
4.31
). Since we have the inequality
\frac{(s+1)\dots(s+\ell_{1}+\ell_{2})}{(s+i_{1}+i_{2}+1)^{2}}\leq 7(s+1)\dots(s+\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}-2)\leq 7(r+1)\dots(r+\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}-2)
we obtain
\|(\mathbb{U}^{\dagger}(\mathcal{N}+1)^{-1}\mathbb{U}PW_{\ell_{1}}^{i_{1}}RW_{\ell_{2}}^{i_{2}}R^{\frac{1}{2}})|_{\mathcal{H}_{r}}\|\leq\sqrt{7}D\sqrt{(r+1)\dots(r+\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}-2)}
, which concludes the proof. Here we used that
W_{\ell}^{-i}=0
if
\ell\geq 5
.
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

Assume that
|E_{k}|\leq C_{2}^{k}\sqrt{(k+2)!}
for some
C_{2}>0
and all
k\in\{1,\ldots,\ell-1\}
. Then there exist constants
C_{0},C_{1}>0
, such that

\displaystyle\left\|(X\mathbb{T}_{\ell,\beta})|_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}\right\|\leq C_{0}\,C_{1}^{\ell-\beta}C_{2}^{\max\{\beta-2,0\}}\sqrt{(s+1)\dots(s+\ell-\beta)}\sqrt{\beta!}

for all
\beta\leq\ell
and
X\in\{(-R)^{\frac{1}{2}},P\}
, where we use the convention that
(s+1)\dots(s+\ell-\beta)=1
for
\beta=\ell
. Furthermore,
\left\|(\mathbb{R}P\mathbb{T}_{\ell+2,0})|_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}\right\|\leq C_{1}^{\ell}\sqrt{(s+1)\dots(s+\ell)}
.

Proof of Lemma
4.6
.

Using the fact that
V_{\ell,\beta}\mathcal{H}_{r}\subset\mathcal{H}_{r+\ell-\beta}
and that there exists a constant
D>0
such that
\|(YV_{\ell,\beta}Z)|_{\mathcal{H}_{r}}\|\leq D^{\ell-\beta}C_{2}^{\max\{\beta-2,0\}}\sqrt{(r+1)\dots(r+\ell-\beta)}\sqrt{\beta!}
for
Y,Z\in\{(-R)^{\frac{1}{2}},P\}
, see Lemma
4.5
, we can bound
\left\|(X\mathbb{T}_{\ell,\beta})|_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}\right\|
from above by

\displaystyle D^{\ell-\beta}C_{2}^{\max\{\beta-2,0\}}\sqrt{(s\!+\!1)\dots(s\!+\!\ell\!-\!\beta)}\sum_{p=1}^{\beta}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{p}\in\mathbb{N}\\
\ell_{1}+\ldots+\ell_{p}=\ell\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{p}:0\leq\beta_{i}\leq\ell_{i}\\
\beta_{1}+\ldots+\beta_{p}=\beta\end{subarray}}\sqrt{\beta_{1}!\dots\beta_{p}!}

\displaystyle\ =D^{\ell-\beta}C_{2}^{\max\{\beta-2,0\}}\sqrt{(s\!+\!1)\dots(s\!+\!\ell\!-\!\beta)}\sum_{p=1}^{\beta}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{p}\geq 0\\
\beta_{1}+\ldots+\beta_{p}=\beta\end{subarray}}\sqrt{\beta_{1}!\dots\beta_{p}!}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{p}\\
\ell_{i}\geq\max\{\beta_{i},1\}\\
\ell_{1}+\ldots+\ell_{p}=\ell\end{subarray}}1\right).

For given
\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{p}
satisfying
\beta_{1}+\dots+\beta_{p}=\beta
, let us define the set
I=I(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{p}):=\{j:\beta_{j}\geq 1\}
, the number
p^{\prime}=p^{\prime}(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{p}):=|I|
and the indicator function
\chi=\chi(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{p}):=\mathds{1}_{p^{\prime}\geq p-\ell+\beta}
. Note that the existence of a sequence
\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{p}
satisfying
\ell_{1}+\dots+\ell_{p}=\ell
and
\ell_{j}\geq\max\{\beta_{i},1\}
immediately implies

\displaystyle p-p^{\prime}=|\{1,\dots,p\}\setminus I|\leq\sum_{i\in\{1,\dots,p\}\setminus I}\ell_{i}=\sum_{i\in\{1,\dots,p\}\setminus I}(\ell_{i}-\beta_{i})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{p}(\ell_{i}-\beta_{i})=\ell-\beta,

and therefore
\chi(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{p})=1
. As a consequence

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{p}:\ell_{i}\geq\max\{\beta_{i},1\}\\
\ell_{1}+\ldots+\ell_{p}=\ell\end{subarray}}1

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{p}:\ell_{i}\geq\beta_{i}\\
\ell_{1}+\ldots+\ell_{p}=\ell\end{subarray}}\chi=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\delta_{1},\dots,\delta_{p}:\delta_{i}\geq 0\\
\delta_{1}+\ldots+\delta_{p}=\ell-\beta\end{subarray}}\chi=\sum_{q=1}^{p}\binom{p}{q}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\delta_{1},\dots,\delta_{p}\geq 1\\
\delta_{1}+\ldots+\delta_{p}=\ell-\beta\end{subarray}}\chi

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{q=1}^{p}\binom{p}{q}2^{\ell-\beta}\chi\leq 2^{p}2^{\ell-\beta}\chi.

(4.33)

With this at hand, we can bound

\displaystyle\sum_{p=1}^{\beta}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{p}\geq 0\\
\beta_{1}+\ldots+\beta_{p}=\beta\end{subarray}}\sqrt{\beta_{1}!\dots\beta_{p}!}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{p}:\ell_{i}\geq\max\{\beta_{i},1\}\\
\ell_{1}+\ldots+\ell_{p}=\ell\end{subarray}}1\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\ell-\beta}\!\sum_{p=1}^{\beta}2^{p}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{p}\geq 0\\
\beta_{1}+\ldots+\beta_{p}=\beta\end{subarray}}\sqrt{\beta_{1}!\dots\beta_{p}!}\,\chi=2^{\ell-\beta}\sum_{p=1}^{\beta}\sum_{p^{\prime}=p-\ell+\beta}^{p}\!\!\!\!2^{p}\binom{p}{p^{\prime}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{p^{\prime}}\geq 1\\
\beta_{1}+\ldots+\beta_{p^{\prime}}=\beta\end{subarray}}\sqrt{\beta_{1}!\dots\beta_{p^{\prime}}!}\,.

In order to estimate this further, we are going to verify

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{q}\geq 1\\
\beta_{1}+\ldots+\beta_{q}=\beta\end{subarray}}\sqrt{\beta_{1}!\dots\beta_{q}!}\leq\frac{12^{q}}{\sqrt{q!}}\sqrt{\beta!}

(4.34)

by induction. For
q=1
the statement is trivial. Regarding the step
q\mapsto q+1
, note that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{q+1}\geq 1\\
\beta_{1}+\ldots+\beta_{q+1}=\beta\end{subarray}}\sqrt{\beta_{1}!\dots\beta_{q+1}!}=\sum_{t=1}^{\beta-q}\sqrt{t!}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{q}\geq 1\\
\beta_{1}+\ldots+\beta_{q}=\beta-t\end{subarray}}\sqrt{\beta_{1}!\dots\beta_{q}!}\leq\frac{12^{q}}{\sqrt{q!}}\sum_{t=1}^{\beta-1}\sqrt{t!}\sqrt{(\beta-t)!}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ =\frac{12^{q}}{\sqrt{q!}}\sqrt{\beta!}\sum_{t=1}^{\beta-1}\binom{\beta}{t}^{-\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{12^{q}}{\sqrt{q!}}\sqrt{\beta!}\left(\frac{2}{\sqrt{\beta}}+\frac{4}{\sqrt{\beta(\beta-1)}}+\sum_{t=3}^{\beta-3}\binom{\beta}{t}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \leq\frac{12^{q}}{\sqrt{q!}}\sqrt{\beta!}\left(\frac{2}{\sqrt{\beta}}+\frac{4}{\sqrt{\beta(\beta-1)}}+(\beta-5)\binom{\beta}{3}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)\leq\frac{12^{q+1}}{\sqrt{q!}\sqrt{\beta}}\sqrt{\beta!},

(4.35)

where we have used
\binom{\beta}{t}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\leq\binom{\beta}{3}^{-\frac{1}{2}}
for
3\leq t\leq\beta-3
. Since
\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{q+1}\geq 1\\
\beta_{1}+\ldots+\beta_{q+1}=\beta\end{subarray}}\sqrt{\beta_{1}!\dots\beta_{q+1}!}=0
for
\beta<q+1
, we can assume
\beta\geq q+1
, which concludes the induction. Combining what we have so far allows us to bound
\left\|(X\mathbb{T}_{\ell,\beta})|_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}\right\|
from above by

\displaystyle(2D)^{\ell-\beta}C_{2}^{\max\{\beta-2,0\}}\sqrt{(s\!+\!1)\dots(s\!+\!\ell\!-\!\beta)}\sqrt{\beta!}\sum_{p=1}^{\beta}\sum_{p^{\prime}=\max\{p-\ell+\beta,0\}}^{p}\!\!\!\!2^{p}\binom{p}{p^{\prime}}\frac{12^{p^{\prime}}}{\sqrt{p^{\prime}!}}.

Making use of the estimate

\displaystyle\sum_{p=1}^{\beta}\sum_{p^{\prime}=\max\{p-\ell+\beta,0\}}^{p}\!\!\!\!2^{p}\binom{p}{p^{\prime}}\frac{12^{p^{\prime}}}{\sqrt{p^{\prime}!}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{p^{\prime}=0}^{\beta}\frac{12^{p^{\prime}}}{\sqrt{p^{\prime}!}}\sum_{p=\max\{1,p^{\prime}\}}^{\min\{p^{\prime}+\ell-\beta,\beta\}}\!\!\!\!2^{p}\binom{p}{p^{\prime}}\leq\sum_{p^{\prime}=0}^{\beta}\frac{12^{p^{\prime}}}{\sqrt{p^{\prime}!}}\sum_{p=1}^{p^{\prime}+\ell-\beta}\!\!\!\!4^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{p^{\prime}=0}^{\beta}\frac{12^{p^{\prime}}}{\sqrt{p^{\prime}!}}\frac{4}{3}4^{p^{\prime}+\ell-\beta}\leq C_{0}4^{\ell-\beta}

with
C_{0}:=\frac{4}{3}\sum_{p^{\prime}=0}^{\beta}\frac{(48)^{p^{\prime}}}{\sqrt{p^{\prime}!}}<\infty
concludes the proof of the first statement of the lemma with
C_{1}:=8D
.

Regarding the proof of the second statement of the lemma, let us write

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}P\mathbb{T}_{\ell+2,0}=\sum_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}=1}^{4}\mathbb{R}PV_{\ell_{1},0}RV_{\ell_{2},0}R^{\frac{1}{2}}R^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbb{T}_{\ell+2-\ell_{1}-\ell_{2},0}.

(4.36)

Using
\|(\mathbb{R}PV_{\ell_{1},0}RV_{\ell_{2},0}R^{\frac{1}{2}})|_{\mathcal{H}_{r}}\|\leq D\sqrt{(r+1)\dots(r+\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}-2)}
for a suitable constant
D>0
, see Lemma
4.5
, yields

\displaystyle\left\|(\mathbb{R}P\mathbb{T}_{\ell+2,0})|_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}\right\|\leq DC_{0}C_{1}^{\ell+2-\ell_{1}-\ell_{2}}\sqrt{(s+1)\dots(s+\ell)}.

(4.37)

∎

We are now prepared to prove Lemma
4.4
.

Proof of Lemma
4.4
.

As explained at the beginning of this section, the main step is to derive (
4.23
). To this end, let us write
P\mathbb{P}K_{k}\Upsilon_{\ell+2}=P\mathbb{P}Z_{1}+P\otimes\mathbb{P}Z_{2}
with
Z_{1}
defined as

\displaystyle\sum_{p=1}^{\ell}\!\sum_{\beta=0}^{\ell+2p}\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{p}\geq 1\\
\ell_{1}+\ldots+\ell_{p}=\ell\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{p}\\
0\leq\beta_{i}\leq\ell_{i}+2\\
\beta_{1}+\ldots+\beta_{p}=\beta\end{subarray}}\!\!\!V_{k,0}(R\mathbb{T}_{\ell_{1}+2,\beta_{2}})(\mathbb{R}P\mathbb{T}_{\ell_{2}+2,\beta_{1}})\dots\!(\mathbb{R}\!P\mathbb{T}_{\ell_{p}+2,\beta_{p}})\Upsilon_{0}\!\!

(4.38)

and
Z_{2}
defined as

\displaystyle\sum_{p=1}^{\ell+2}\!\sum_{\beta=0}^{\ell+2+2p}\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{p}\geq 1\\
\ell_{1}+\ldots+\ell_{p}=\ell+2\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{p}\\
0\leq\beta_{i}\leq\ell_{i}+2\\
\beta_{1}+\ldots+\beta_{p}=\beta\end{subarray}}\!\!V_{k,0}(\mathbb{R}P\mathbb{T}_{\ell_{1}+2,\beta_{1}})(\mathbb{R}P\mathbb{T}_{\ell_{2}+2,\beta_{1}})\dots(\mathbb{R}P\mathbb{T}_{\ell_{p}+2,\beta_{p}})\Upsilon_{0},

(4.39)

where we have used that
PK_{k}R=PV_{k,0}R
and
\mathbb{P}K_{k}\mathbb{R}=\mathbb{P}V_{k,0}\mathbb{R}
, see (
4.24
). According to Lemma
4.6
we have
\|(R^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbb{T}_{\ell_{1}+2,0})|_{\mathcal{H}_{3s}}\|\leq(\sqrt{3}C_{1})^{\ell_{1}+2}\sqrt{(s+1)\dots(s+\ell_{1}+2)}
as well as for
\beta_{i}\geq 3

\displaystyle\|(X\mathbb{T}_{\ell_{i}+2,\beta_{i}})|_{\mathcal{H}_{3s}}\|\leq\frac{C_{0}}{\tau}(\sqrt{3}C_{1})^{\ell_{i}+2-\beta_{i}}C_{2}^{\beta_{i}-2}\sqrt{(s+1)\dots(s+\ell_{i}+2-\beta_{i})}

where
X\in\{\mathbb{R}\!P,(-R)^{\frac{1}{2}}\}
and
\tau:=\min\{\|\mathbb{R}\|^{-1},1\}
. For
\beta_{i}=0
we use the estimate

\displaystyle\left\|(\mathbb{R}P\mathbb{T}_{\ell+2,0})|_{\mathcal{H}_{3s}}\right\|\leq(\sqrt{3}C_{1})^{\ell}\sqrt{(s+1)\dots(s+\ell)}

by Lemma
4.6
. Further,
\|\left(PV_{k,0}Y\right)|_{\mathcal{H}_{3s}}\|\leq C_{1}^{k}\sqrt{(s+1)\dots(s+k)}
for
Y\in\{(-R)^{\frac{1}{2}},P\}
, see Lemma
4.5
. Note at this point that we only need to consider values of
q
bounded by
p-1
, since
X\mathbb{T}_{\ell_{p}+2,\beta_{p}}\Upsilon_{0}=0
for
X\in\{\mathbb{R}P,(-R)^{\frac{1}{2}}\}
allowing us to restrict the sum in Eq. (
4.38
) and Eq. (
4.39
) to
\beta_{p}=0
. Furthermore, the index
\beta
is in both Equations bounded by
\beta_{*}:=\ell+k-(p-q)+2q
, since
\beta=\sum_{j=1}^{p}\beta_{j}\leq\sum_{j:\beta_{j}\neq 0}(\ell_{j}+2)=\sum_{j:\beta_{j}\neq 0}\ell_{j}+2q
and in the case of Eq. (
4.38
) we have
\sum_{j:\beta_{j}\neq 0}\ell_{j}\leq\ell-(p-q)\leq\ell+k-(p-q)
, while we have
\sum_{j:\beta_{j}\neq 0}\ell_{j}\leq\ell+2-(p-q)\leq\ell+k-(p-q)
in the case of Eq. (
4.39
). Note that we have used the fact that
PV_{k,0}P=0
for
k=1
and therefore we can assume
k\geq 2
in Eq. (
4.39
). Making use of
\mathbb{T}_{\ell+2,0}\mathcal{H}_{3r}\subset\mathcal{H}_{3(r+\ell)}
,
\mathbb{T}_{\ell+2,\beta}\mathcal{H}_{3r}\subset\mathcal{H}_{3(r+\ell+2-\beta)}
and
\Upsilon_{0}\in\mathcal{H}_{s}
, we obtain for a suitable constant
\widetilde{C}_{0}
and both
Z_{1}
and
Z_{2}
the estimate

\displaystyle\|Z_{i}\|\leq\sum_{p=1}^{\ell+2}\sum_{q=0}^{p-1}\sum_{\beta=0}^{\beta_{*}}\binom{p}{q}\tilde{C}_{0}^{q}(\sqrt{3}C_{1})^{\ell+k+2p-\beta}C_{2}^{\beta-2q}

\displaystyle\quad\times\sqrt{(s\!+\!1)\dots(s\!+\!\ell\!+\!k\!+\!2(q\!+\!1)\!-\!\beta)}\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{q}\geq 3\\
\beta_{1}+\ldots+\beta_{q}=\beta\end{subarray}}\sqrt{\beta_{1}!\dots\beta_{q}!}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{p}\geq\max\{1,\beta_{i}-2\}\\
\ell_{1}+\ldots+\ell_{p}=\ell+2\end{subarray}}1

\displaystyle\leq C\!\sum_{p=1}^{\ell+2}\sum_{q=0}^{p-1}\sum_{\beta=0}^{\beta_{*}}\binom{p}{q}\tilde{C}_{0}^{q}(\sqrt{3(s+1)}C_{1})^{\ell+k+2p-\beta}C_{2}^{\beta-2q}

\displaystyle\ \ \times\sqrt{(\ell+k+2(q+1)-\beta)!}\,2^{p}2^{\ell+2p-\beta}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{q}\geq 3\\
\beta_{1}+\ldots\beta_{q}=\beta\end{subarray}}\,\sqrt{\beta_{1}!\dots\beta_{q}!},

see also Eq. (
4.33
) regarding the last estimate, where we have further used
(s+1)\dots(s+\ell+k+2(q+1)-\beta)\leq(s+1)^{\ell+k+2(q+1)-\beta}(\ell+k+2(q+1)-\beta)!
. We next show by induction with respect to
q
that the remaining sum over
\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{q}
is bounded by
\frac{C_{*}^{q}}{\sqrt{q!}}\sqrt{(\beta+2-2q)!}
for a suitable constant
C_{*}

is clear. Regarding the induction step

4.6

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{q+1}\geq 3\\
\beta_{1}+\ldots+\beta_{q+1}=\beta\end{subarray}}\sqrt{\beta_{1}!\dots\beta_{q+1}!}\leq\frac{C_{*}^{q}}{\sqrt{q!}}\sum_{t=3q}^{\beta-3}\sqrt{(t+2-2q)!}\sqrt{(\beta-t)!}

\displaystyle\ \ \leq\frac{C_{*}^{q}}{\sqrt{q!}}\sqrt{(\beta\!-\!2q\!-\!1)!}\left(2\sqrt{6}+2\sqrt{\frac{24}{\beta\!-\!2q\!-\!1}}+(\beta\!-\!3q\!-\!2)\sqrt{\frac{120}{(\beta\!-\!2q\!-\!2)(\beta\!-\!2q\!-\!1)}}\right)

\displaystyle\ \ \leq\frac{C_{*}^{q+1}}{\sqrt{q!}}\sqrt{(\beta-2q-1)!}\leq\frac{C_{*}^{q+1}}{\sqrt{(q+1)!}}\sqrt{(\beta-2q)!},

where we have used that
t+2-2q
and
\beta-t

\beta-2q-1

\beta+2-2q
as well as
\ell+k+2(q+1)-\beta

\ell+k+2

\sqrt{(\ell+k+2(q+1)-\beta)!}\sqrt{(\beta+2-2q)!}\leq\binom{\ell+k+4}{\ell+k+2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{(\ell+k+4)!}=\sqrt{2}\sqrt{(\ell+k+2)!}
, and therefore we can bound

\displaystyle\|P\mathbb{P}K_{k}\Upsilon_{\ell+2}\|\!\leq\!D\!\sum_{p=1}^{\ell+2}\sum_{q=0}^{p-1}\sum_{\beta=0}^{\beta_{*}}\!2^{p}\binom{p}{q}\!\!\left(\!2\sqrt{3(s\!+\!1)}C_{1}\!\right)^{\ell+k+2p-\beta}\!\!\!\!C_{2}^{\beta\!-\!2q}\!\frac{(\tilde{C}_{0}C_{*})^{q}}{\sqrt{q!}}\sqrt{(\ell\!+\!k\!+\!2)!}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \leq D\sqrt{(\ell+k+2)!}\sum_{q=0}^{\ell+1}\frac{(\tilde{C}_{0}C_{*})^{q}}{\sqrt{q!}}\sum_{p=q+1}^{\ell+2}\sum_{\beta=0}^{\beta_{*}}\left(8\sqrt{3(s+1)}C_{1}\right)^{\ell+k+2p-\beta}C_{2}^{\beta-2q}

for a suitable constant
D
, where we have used that
p\leq\frac{1}{2}(\ell+k+2p-\beta)

. Using the shorthand
C_{3}:=4\sqrt{3(s+1)}C_{1}
, we obtain for
C_{2}>C_{3}

\displaystyle\sum_{\beta=0}^{\beta_{*}}

\displaystyle\!\!\left(4\sqrt{3(s\!+\!1)}C_{1}\right)^{\ell+k+2p-\beta}\!\!C_{2}^{\beta-2q}\!=\!K^{\ell+k+2p}C_{2}^{-2q}\frac{\left(\frac{C_{2}}{K}\right)^{\beta_{*}}-\frac{K}{C_{2}}}{1-\frac{K}{C_{2}}}\leq\!\frac{C_{2}^{\ell+k}}{1-\frac{K}{C_{2}}}\left(\frac{K^{3}}{C_{2}}\right)^{p-q}.

\sum_{p=q+1}^{\ell+2}\left(\frac{C_{3}^{3}}{C_{2}}\right)^{p-q}C_{2}^{\ell+k}\leq\frac{1}{1-\frac{C_{3}^{3}}{C_{2}}}C_{3}^{3}C_{2}^{\ell+k-1}

\displaystyle\|P\mathbb{P}K_{k}\Upsilon_{\ell+2}\|\leq\frac{DK^{3}\sqrt{(\ell+k+2)!}C_{2}^{\ell+k-1}}{\left(1-\frac{K}{C_{2}}\right)\left(1-\frac{K^{3}}{C_{2}}\right)}\sum_{q=0}^{\ell+1}\frac{(\tilde{C}_{0}C_{*})^{q}}{\sqrt{q!}}\leq\widetilde{D}\sqrt{(\ell+k+2)!}C_{2}^{\ell+k-1}.

C_{2}\geq 3\widetilde{D}

4.23
). As a consequence of Lemma
4.3
, we thus obtain the first part of the lemma.

The second statement of the lemma follows in close analogy, by using the established bounds for

\|\left(V_{k,0}Y\right)|_{\mathcal{H}_{3s}}\|\leq(1+\sqrt{\Lambda}\,)C_{1}^{k}\sqrt{(s+1)\dots(s+k)}

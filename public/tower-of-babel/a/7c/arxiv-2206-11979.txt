Abstract
. In this article we prove results concerning upper and lower decay estimates for homogeneous Sobolev norms of solutions to a rather general family of parabolic equations. Following the ideas of Kreiss, Hagstrom, Lorenz and Zingano, we use eventual regularity of solutions to directly work with smooth solutions in physical space, bootstrapping decay estimates from the L 2 L^{2} norm to higher order derivatives. Besides obtaining upper and lower bounds through this method, we also obtain reverse results: from higher order derivatives decay estimates, we deduce bounds for the L 2 L^{2} norm. We use these general results to prove new decay estimates for some equations and to recover some well known results.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Settings

The class of parabolic equations of the form

(1.1)

\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{t}+\mathbb{G}(\mbox{\boldmath$u$})=\nu\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$u$}+\mbox{\boldmath$f$},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},t>0,

where
u
is a vector field,
\mathbb{G}(\mbox{\boldmath$u$})
is a nonlinear function of
u
,
\mbox{\boldmath$f$}=\mbox{\boldmath$f$}(x,t)
is an external force and
\nu>0
is a constant, includes a large number of important equations on which many results concerning upper and lower estimates for evolution of homogeneous Sobolev norms
\dot{H}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),m\in{\mathbb{N}}
, have been proved. As examples of equations that fit in the framework of (
1.1
) we mention the Navier-Stokes equations, advection-diffusion equations, MHD and MHD-like equations, micropolar and magneto-micropolar equations, amongst many others. Our main goal in this article is to prove general results providing upper and lower bounds for the decay rate of the
\dot{H}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
norm, provided solutions
u
have certain natural properties which are known to hold for equations as those listed above.

We describe now the setting in which we work. We assume first that (
1.1
) has global weak solutions
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(\cdot,t)\in C_{\tt w}\left([0,\infty),L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right)
which become eventually smooth and that
f
also becomes eventually smooth, i.e. for some
t_{\ast}>0

(1.2)

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$f$},\mbox{\boldmath$u$}

\displaystyle\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(t_{\ast},\infty)),

(1.3)

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$f$}(\cdot,t),\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(\cdot,t)

\displaystyle\in C^{0}((t_{\ast},\infty),H^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{n}),\quad\,\forall\,m\geq 0.

In order to prove our results, we consider the following set of hypotheses:

•

there exist constants
T_{0},C_{0},\alpha>0
such that for all
t>T_{0}
,

(H1)

\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{0}t^{-\alpha};

•

for some
\widehat{m}\geq 1
we have, for each
0\leq m\leq\widehat{m}
, and some
\tau_{m}>t_{\ast}
,

(H2)

\big|\sum_{\ell_{1},...,\ell_{m}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\langle D_{\ell_{1}}\cdots D_{\ell_{m}}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(x,t),D_{\ell_{1}}\cdots D_{\ell_{m}}\mathbb{G}(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(x,t))\rangle\,dx\,\big|\leq g_{m}(t)\|D^{m+1}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(\cdot,t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}},

for all
t>\tau_{m}
, where the sum is over all indices
1\leq\ell_{1},\cdots,\ell_{m}\leq n
and
g_{m}(t)
is a continuous function that tends to zero when
t
goes to infinity;

•

for some constant
\beta>0
and each
0\leq m\leq\widehat{m}
, there exist
F_{m},\sigma_{m}>0
such that

(H3)

\|D^{m}\mbox{\boldmath$f$}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq F_{m}t^{-\beta-\frac{m}{2}},

for all
t>\sigma_{m}
;

•

for some constants
D_{0},t_{0},\eta
and
t>t_{0}

(H4)

\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}}\geq D_{0}t^{-\eta},

where
\eta\geq\alpha
, for
\alpha
as in (
H1
).

We discuss now the assumption and hypotheses needed to prove our results. We first assume eventual regularity, i.e. (
1.2
) and (
1.3
). This is key to our method, as once the solution is smooth enough we directly work with equation (
1.1
) taking derivatives and obtaining energy equalities and inequalities, where we use the hypotheses to bootstrap estimates as in (
H1
) and (
H4
) from lower to higher order derivatives through (
H2
) (and vice versa) and rather simple calculations. Note that we do not assume a specific functional form for the nonlinearity
\mathbb{G}(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(\cdot,\cdot))
, but we only ask for decay in time estimates on its derivatives through (
H2
). For many equations of the form (
1.1
) these estimates can be naturally proved by induction with (
H1
) and (
H4
) as base step. It is important to note that these estimates and computations all take place in physical space, see Remak
1.7
below. When there is an external force acting on the equation, we need the independent estimate (
H3
). We closely follow the ideas in Hagstrom, Lorenz, J. P. Zingano and P. Zingano
[
16
]
and Kreiss, Hagstrom, Lorenz and P. Zingano
[
19
]
.

Remark 1.7
.

There are well-known methods, different from the one we use here, which have led to results as in Theorems
1.1
,
1.5
and
1.6
for specific cases. In order to illustrate the differences, we briefly describe here the Fourier Splitting Method and the Gevrey estimates and radius of analiticity method. In the Fourier Splitting Method, developed by M.E. Schonbek
[
28
]
,
[
29
]
,
[
30
]
, a differential inequality is established in frequency space, bounding the
L^{2}
or
\dot{H}^{m}
norm by the average of the solution, or its
(-\Delta)^{\frac{m}{2}}
derivative, over small enough frequencies. These estimates are obtained for solutions to a regularized equation and then proved to hold for weak solutions through a standard limiting process, see M.E. Schonbek
[
31
]
and M.E. Schonbek and Wiegner
[
32
]
for the case of the Navier-Stokes equations. A different method, involving Gevrey estimates, radius of analiticity of solutions and analytic function theory, was developed by Oliver and Titi
[
25
]
to establish similar estimates also for the Navier-Stokes equations. These ideas were further developed and extended for other functions spaces by Bae and Biswas
[
2
]
and Biswas
[
4
]
, to study equations with fractional Laplacian and quadratic analytic nonlinearities, thus providing a more general context on which to apply this method. Note that in both these methods the essential estimates are obtained in frequency space.

Remark 1.8
.

We now briefly discuss hypotheses (
H1
) and (
H4
) concerning upper and lower bounds for the decay of
\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(t)\|_{L^{2}}
, which are starting points for the bootstrap methods that are central to our proofs.

Many results concerning upper and lower bounds for
\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(t)\|_{L^{2}}
, where
u
is a solution to an equation as in (
1.1
), are known; for a rather complete description of such results for the Navier-Stokes equations, we refer to Brandolese and M.E. Schonbek
[
7
]
. In order to have a finer understanding of the relation between decay rate and initial data, Bjorland and M.E. Schonbek
[
5
]
introduced the decay character
r^{\ast}=r^{\ast}(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0})
, for
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, defined as
r^{\ast}(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0})
being the unique
r\in\left(-\frac{n}{2},\infty\right)
such that
0<P_{r}(v_{0})<\infty
, for

P_{r}(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0})=\lim_{\rho\to 0}\rho^{-2r-n}\int_{B(\rho)}\bigl|\widehat{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}}(\xi)\bigr|^{2}\,d\xi,

provided this limit exists. Here,
B(\rho)
denotes the ball at the origin with radius
\rho
. Using the decay character, Bjorland and M.E. Schonbek were able to establish tight decay estimates for solutions to the heat equation and as an application they proved that for solutions to the Navier-Stokes equations, when
-\frac{n}{2}<r^{\ast}\leq 1
we have that

C_{1}(1+t)^{-\left(\frac{n}{2}+r^{\ast}\right)}\leq\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C_{2}(1+t)^{-\left(\frac{n}{2}+r^{\ast}\right)},

and for
r^{\ast}\geq 1
, we have

\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C(1+t)^{-\left(\frac{n}{2}+1\right)}.

Note that for
-\frac{n}{2}<r^{\ast}\leq 1
these estimates imply that the decay rates in (
H1
) and (
H4
) are equal, so Theorem
1.5
applies. However, as there are no known results for lower bounds when
r^{\ast}>1
, we have no information on (
H4
) and Theorem
1.6
may apply.

The decay character can be explicitly computed in many important cases, see Cárdenas and Niche
[
11
]
and Ferreira, Niche and Planas
[
15
]
. However, the limit defining
P_{r}(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0})
may not exist, as is required in the definition. Brandolese
[
6
]
constructed initial data in
L^{2}
, highly oscillating near the origin, for which the decay character cannot be defined. He also provided generalizations of the decay character, which he called upper and lower decay characters, which lead to upper and lower estimates for decay rates of solutions to linear systems for diagonalizable operators, as defined in Niche and M.E. Schonbek
[
24
]
. Moreover, he showed that the decay character exists if and only if
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}
belongs to a certain subset of a homogeneous Besov space, i.e.
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0}\in\dot{\mathcal{A}}^{-\left(\frac{n}{2}+r^{\ast}\right)}_{2,\infty}\subset\dot{B}^{-\left(\frac{n}{2}+r^{\ast}\right)}_{2,\infty}
. In that case solutions to the linear system are such that

C_{1}(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}\left(\frac{n}{2}+r^{\ast}\right)}\leq\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C_{2}(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}\left(\frac{n}{2}+r^{\ast}\right)},

if and only if the decay character
r^{\ast}=r^{\ast}(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{0})
exists. These estimates are needed for establishing decay rates for nonlinear equations as in (
1.1
) and, as a consequence, exert influence on whether the rates on (
H1
) and (
H4
) may be equal or not, thus leading the potential use of either Theorem
1.5
or Theorem
1.6
.
\Box

Remark 1.11
.

It will become apparent in the proofs of Theorems
1.9
and
1.10
that we only need
\liminf_{t\,\rightarrow\,\infty}\|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(t)\|_{L^{2}}=0

Since

Hence, the limit

for all
t>T_{0}
. If
\frac{\beta-1}{2}\geq\alpha_{1}-\frac{1}{2}
, we are done; otherwise, we obtain

(2.13)

\|\,\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\,M_{1}t^{\,q_{\mbox{}_{1}}(1-\beta)},\quad\,\forall\,t>T_{0}

with
q_{1}=\frac{1}{2}
and
M_{1}=\sqrt{\frac{4C_{1}^{2}}{2\alpha_{1}-1}+\frac{2F_{0}M_{0}}{\beta-1}}
. Let
\theta=\frac{\alpha_{1}-\frac{1}{2}}{\beta-1}
. If

\|\,\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\,M_{k}\,t^{q_{\mbox{}_{k}}(1-\beta)},\quad\,\forall\,t>T_{0}

for some
q_{k}\in[0,\theta)
, we obtain from (
2.11
)

(2.14)

\|\,\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(t)\|^{2}\leq\frac{4C_{1}^{2}}{\,2\alpha_{1}-1}t^{1-\,2\alpha_{1}}+\frac{2F_{0}M_{k}}{(1+q_{k})(\beta-1)}\,t^{(1+q_{k})(1-\beta)},

for all
t>T_{0}
. Let
q_{k+1}=\frac{1+q_{k}}{2}
. If
q_{k+1}(\beta-1)\geq\alpha_{1}-\frac{1}{2}
, we are done; if not, we have

\|\,\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\,M_{k+1}\,t^{q_{k+1}}(1-\beta),\quad\,\forall\,t>T_{0}

where
M_{k+1}=\sqrt{\frac{4C_{1}^{2}}{2\alpha_{1}-1}+\frac{F_{0}M_{k}}{q_{k+1}(\beta-1)}}
. The constants
q_{k}
are given recursively by

q_{k+1}\,=\frac{\,1\,}{2}\,(1+q_{k}),\quad\,k\geq 0,

with
q_{0}=0
, so that
q_{k}=1-2^{-k}
. Now, in the case (
i
), we have
\theta<1
(since
\beta>\alpha_{1}+\frac{1}{2}
), and there will exist
k_{\ast}\geq 0
such that
q_{k_{\ast}}(\beta-1)<\alpha_{1}-\frac{1}{2}
and
q_{k_{\ast}+1}(\beta-1)\geq\alpha_{1}-\frac{1}{2}
. For such
k
, (
2.14
) leads to

\|\,\mbox{\boldmath$u$}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\,M_{k_{\ast}+1}t^{-\alpha_{1}+\frac{1}{2}}

for all
t>T_{0}
,. Finally, in the case (
ii
), where
\theta=1
, as
2\alpha_{1}-1>(1+q_{k})(\beta-1)
for all
k
, the second term on the right hand side of (
2.14

\epsilon>0
, we have
(1+q_{k})(\beta-1)>2\alpha_{1}-1-2\epsilon
for large

\Box

3.
Examples

3.1.
Advection-diffusion equations

Given a smooth function
\mbox{\boldmath$b$}=(b_{1},b_{2},\cdots,b_{n})
in
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
and an arbitrary initial datum
u_{0}\in L^{2}
, let

u\in C([0,\infty),L^{2})\cap L^{\infty}_{loc}((0,\infty),L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))

be a solution to the problem

(3.1)

u_{t}+\mbox{\boldmath$b$}(u)\cdot\nabla u=\nu\Delta u,\quad u(\cdot,0)=u_{0}.

Under the above conditions, it is known that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}=0,\quad\lim_{t\to\infty}t^{n/4}\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=0,

see Theorem 3.3 in Braz e Silva, Schütz and P.R. Zingano
[
8
]
, and also that

\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq K_{n}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\nu^{-n/4}t^{-n/4}\quad\forall t>0

for some constant
K_{n}
depending only on
n
, see Theorem 3.2 in Braz e Silva, Schütz e P.R. Zingano
[
8
]
and Theorem 2.1 in Porzio
[
27
]
. Conditions (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) hold for
t_{\ast}=0
. From this point on, we restrict our attention to dimension
n\geq 2
.

We check now hypotheses (
H2
), where
\mathbb{G}(u)=\mbox{\boldmath$b$}(u)\cdot\nabla u
. It clearly holds for
m=0
, since

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u(x,t)\mbox{\boldmath$b$}(u)\nabla u\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\nabla\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$D$}(u(x,t))dx=0

because
u(t)\in L^{2}\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, for any
t>0
, where

D(u)=\int_{0}^{u}v\,\mbox{\boldmath$b$}(v)\,dv.

Checking (
H2
) for
m\geq 1
is more involved. We will need the following Lemma, whose proof can be found in page 273 in Moser
[
23
]
or page 70 in Klainerman
[
18
]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
m\geq 1
,
F\in C^{m}(\mathbb{R})
with
F(0)=0
, and
u
in
W^{m,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, for
1\leq p\leq\infty
. Then
F(\mbox{u})
is in
W^{m,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

\|D^{m}F(u)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq K(m,n,p)\,\|D^{m}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

where
K>0
depends only on
m,n,p
and

F_{\scriptstyle\ell}=\sup\{|F^{(\ell)}(v)|:|v|<\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\}

for
1\leq\ell\leq m
, where
F^{(\ell)}
denotes the derivative of order
\ell
of the function
F
.

Given
u\in\mathbb{R}
, it will be convenient to define

\widetilde{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}(u),\widetilde{\mbox{\boldmath$B$}}(u)
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
through

\widetilde{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}(u)=\mbox{\boldmath$b$}(u)-\mbox{\boldmath$b$}(0),\qquad\widetilde{\mbox{\boldmath$B$}}(u)=\int_{0}^{u}\left(\mbox{\boldmath$b$}(v)-\mbox{\boldmath$b$}(0)\right)\,dv,

which leads to

\widetilde{\mathbb{G}}(u)=\left(\mbox{\boldmath$b$}(u)-\mbox{\boldmath$b$}(0)\right)\cdot\nabla u=\mathbb{G}(u)-\mbox{\boldmath$b$}(0)\cdot\nabla u=\nabla\cdot\widetilde{\mbox{\boldmath$B$}}(u),

where
u=u(x,t)
is the solution to (
3.1
) under consideration. From now on, we will work with
\widetilde{\mathbb{G}}(u)
, as (
H2
) is valid for
\mathbb{G}(u)
if and only if is valid for
\widetilde{\mathbb{G}}(u)
, for any
m
.

The next lemma is proved by direct computation.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
v
be a smooth scalar function in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and let
D_{\ell}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{\ell}}
. Then

D_{\ell}\widetilde{\mbox{\boldmath$B$}}(v)=\widetilde{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}(v)D_{\ell}v,

and for
m\geq 2

D_{\ell_{1}}D_{\ell_{2}}\cdots D_{\ell_{m}}\widetilde{\mbox{\boldmath$B$}}(v)=\widetilde{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}(v)D_{\ell_{1}}D_{\ell_{2}}\cdots D_{\ell_{m}}v+\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{a(m,j)}\mathbb{D}_{(k)}^{j}\widetilde{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}(v)\cdot\mathbb{D}_{(k)}^{m-j}v\right),

where

a(m,j)=\frac{(m-1)!}{j!(m-1-j)!},\,\mathbb{D}_{(k)}^{j}=D_{k_{1}}D_{k_{2}}\cdots D_{k_{j}},\,\mathbb{D}_{(k)}^{m-j}=D_{k_{j+1}}D_{k_{j+2}}\cdots D_{k_{m}},

with
\{\!\;\!\;\!k_{1},k_{2},\!\;\!...\!\;\!\;\!,k_{m}\}=\{\;\!\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\!\;\!...\!\;\!\;\!,\ell_{m}\!\;\!\;\!\}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
n\geq 2,m\geq 1
and
F,G\in C^{m}(\mathbb{R})
, with
F(0)=G(0)=0
. Let
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
non-negative multi-indices such that
|\lambda_{1}|+|\lambda_{2}|=m
. Then, for any
v\in H^{m+1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap H^{n-2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have that
D^{\lambda_{1}}F(v)\cdot D^{\lambda_{2}}G(v)
is in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

\|D^{\lambda_{1}}F(v)\cdot D^{\lambda_{2}}G(v)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C(m,n)\,\|\,v\,\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\,D^{n-2}v\,\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\,D^{m+1}v\,\|_{L^{2}}

where
C(m,n)
depends only on
m,n
and

F_{\ell}=\sup\,\{|F^{(\ell)}(w)|:|w|<\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\},\,G_{\ell}=\sup\,\{|G^{(\ell)}(w)|:|w|<\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\},

for
1\leq l\leq m
.

Remark 3.4
.

The assumption that

in the previous Lemma is needed when

and

, but redundant in the other cases, since the inclusion

holds for

except in the single case

,

.

Proof:
The proof is analogous to Lemma 3.1 in Braz e Silva, J.P. Zingano and P.R. Zingano
[
9
]
, to which we refer.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
u(t)\in C([0,\infty),L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}))\cap L^{\infty}_{loc}((0,\infty),L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))

be a solution to (

). Then, for every
m\geq 1
, there exists a constant

, depending only on

,

at, say,

\|D^{m}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\,K(m,n,\nu)\,\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\quad\forall\,t>m.

Proof:
From (

\|u(t)\|^{\>\!2}_{L^{2}}+\,2\nu\int_{0}^{t}\|Du(\tau)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\,d\tau=\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

for all

. Then

\int_{0}^{1}\|Du(\tau)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\,d\tau\leq\frac{1}{2\nu}\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

so there exists
a_{1}\in(0,1)
such that

(3.2)

\|Du(a_{1})\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{2\nu}\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

Then, for all

we have

\displaystyle\|Du(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\,2\nu\int_{a_{1}}^{t}\|D^{2}u(\tau)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\,d\tau\leq\|Du(a_{1})\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle 2\int_{a_{1}}^{t}\|D^{2}u(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}\,\|D[\widetilde{\mbox{\boldmath$B$}}(u)]\|_{L^{2}}\,d\tau.

Using Lemma
3.1
we then obtain

(3.3)

\displaystyle\|Du(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\nu\int_{a_{1}}^{t}\|D^{2}u(\tau)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\,d\tau\leq\|Du(a_{1})\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{K_{1}}{\nu}\int_{a_{1}}^{t}\|D^{2}u(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}\,d\tau.

Then, from (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) we obtain the result for
m=1
and also

\int_{1}^{t}\|D^{2}u(\tau)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\,d\tau\,\leq C(n,\nu)\,\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

for all
t>1
and some constant
C(n,\nu)>0
that depends also on
b
and
\|u(1)\|_{L^{\infty}}
.
In particular, we can find

such that

\|D^{2}u(a_{2})\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\leq C(n,\nu)\,\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}},

starting with which we can consider the energy estimate

\displaystyle\|D^{2}u(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\,2\ \nu\int_{a_{2}}^{t}\|D^{3}u(\tau)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\,d\tau\,\leq\|D^{2}u(a_{2})\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle 2\int_{a_{2}}^{\tau}\|D^{3}u(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}\,\|D^{2}[\widetilde{\mbox{\boldmath$B$}}(u)]\|_{L^{2}}\,d\tau.

We now proceed as above to obtain the result for
m=2
, and go on to prove the estimate for any

.
\Box

\>\!m,\>\!\nu
,

Remark 3.6

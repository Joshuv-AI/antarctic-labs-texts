Completely hyperexpansive operators with finite rank defect operator and de Branges-Rovnyak spaces
Saee A. Joshi, Vinayak. M. Sholapurkar

Abstract
. The process of identifying a Dirichlet-type space D ⁡ ( μ ) D(\mu) for a positive, Borel measure μ \mu , supported on the unit circle 𝕋 , \mathbb{T}, with a de Branges-Rovnyak space was initiated by Sarason [ 15 ] . A characterization of the symbol for a de Branges-Rovnyak spaces for which the shift operator is a 2 2 -isometry, was provided in [ 9 ] . In this paper, capitalizing on the Aleman’s model [ 1 ] for the cyclic, analytic, completely hyperexpansive operators, we provide a characterization of cyclic, analytic, completely hyperexpansive operator with finite rank defect operator in terms of the symbol for a de Branges-Rovnyak space.

1.
Introduction and Preliminaries

It is well known that the class of
2
-isometric operators is a subclass of the class of completely hyperexpansive operators. A theme of the present paper hinges around extending the results in
[
9
]
for
2
-isometric operators to completely hyperexpansive operators, especially in the case when the rank of the defect operators is finite.

In this paper, we describe a model for a cyclic, analytic, completely hyperexpansive operator
T
, if the defect operator
\Delta_{T}=T^{*}T-I
is of finite rank. A special case of such a model for
2
-isometric operators, has been obtained in
[
4
]
. This particular case has a bearing on the classical model for a cyclic, analytic,
2
-isometric operator described by Richter (see
[
13
]
). Here, we capitalize on the classical model for a cyclic, analytic, completely hyperexpansive operator given by Aleman (see
[
1
]
) and impose an additional condition that the defect operator is of finite rank. In both the classical representations, the corresponding operator is shown to be unitarily equivalent to the multiplication operator
M_{z}
on a Dirichlet space
D(\mu)
where the measure is supported on the closed unit disk. In particular, if the operator is
2
-isometric, then the support of
\mu
lies inside the unit circle. In the circumstances where the defect operator is of finite rank, the de Branges-Rovnyak spaces make their appearance. Indeed, in a series of papers
[
4
,
5
,
9
,
15
]
, the authors have established interesting connections between Dirichlet spaces and de Branges-Rovnyak spaces. The most pertinent connections in the context of present paper are
[
9
, Theorem 1 and Corollary 3]
. We record these results for a ready reference.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
b
is non-extreme in the unit ball of
\mathbb{H}^{\infty}
. Then the restriction of the shift operator
S|_{\mathcal{H}(b)}
is concave if and only if

b(z)=\frac{c+\gamma z}{1-\beta z},z\in\mathbb{D}\ \mbox{where}\ c,\gamma,\beta\in\mathbb{C}\ \mbox{with}\ |\beta|<1.

Furthermore,
S|_{\mathcal{H}(b)}
is
2
-isometry if and only if
b(z)=\frac{c+\gamma z}{1-\beta z},z\in\mathbb{D}
where
c,\gamma,\beta\in\mathbb{C}
are such that
1+|\beta|^{2}-|c|^{2}-|\gamma|^{2}=|\beta+\overline{c}\gamma|
.

Corollary 1.2

(Chevrot, Guillot, Ransford
[
5
]
)
.

Let
\mu
be a finite positive measure on
\mathbb{T}
and let
b
be in the unit ball of
\mathbb{H}^{\infty}
. Then
D(\mu)=\mathcal{H}(b)
, with equality of norms, if and only if

\mu=c\delta_{\lambda}\ \mbox{and}\ b(z)=\frac{\gamma z}{1-\beta z},

where
\beta\neq 0,\ |\beta|+|\gamma|=1,\ c=\frac{|\gamma|^{2}}{|\beta|}
and
\lambda=\frac{\overline{\beta}}{|\beta|}
.

In this paper, we obtain an analogue of Theorem
1.1
for completely hyperexpansive operators. Further, we also obtain a version of Corollary
1.2
for the Dirichlet space
D(\mu)
, where the measure
\mu
is supported on the closed unit disk. In the process, we also prove a generalization of
[
4
, Theorem 6.1]
, extending the result to a completely hyperexpansive operator.

The main result proved in this paper is stated below:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\mu
be a finite, positive, Borel measure supported on
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
and let
b\in\mathbb{H}^{\infty}
be nonextreme with
\|b\|_{\infty}\leq 1
.
Then
D(\mu)=\mathcal{H}(b)
with equality of norms, if and only if
\mu=|\alpha|^{2}\delta_{\lambda},\ \mbox{for some }\alpha\in\mathbb{C},\ \lambda\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}
and
b(z)=\frac{A_{\lambda}z}{1-B_{\lambda}z}
where
A_{\lambda}
and
B_{\lambda}
are constants depending on
\alpha
and
\lambda
such that

|A_{\lambda}|^{2}=\begin{cases}\frac{|\alpha|^{2}}{2|\lambda|^{2}}\left((1+|\alpha|^{2}+|\lambda|^{2})-\sqrt{(1+|\alpha|^{2}+|\lambda|^{2})^{2}-4|\lambda|^{2}}\right),&\mbox{ if}\ \lambda\neq 0\\
\displaystyle\frac{1}{1+|\alpha|^{2}},&\mbox{ if}\ \lambda=0\end{cases}

and

B_{\lambda}=\frac{|A_{\lambda}|^{2}\overline{\lambda}}{|\alpha|^{2}}.

We obtain Corollary
1.2
as a special case of Theorem
1.3
.

In the remainder of this section, we set up the notation as well as the context necessary in the sequel. In section
2
, we prove that the defect operator of a multiplication operator
M_{z}
on a Dirichlet space
D(\mu)
, where
\mu
is supported on the closed unit disk, is of finite rank if and only if
\mu
is finitely supported on the closed unit disk. In section

, we prove a generalization of Theorem
1.1
and finally, capitalizing on the results in sections

3

.

For a complex Hilbert space
\cal H
, let
\cal B(\cal H)
denote the
C^{*}
algebra of bounded linear operators on
\cal H.
An operator
T\in\cal B(\cal H)
is called
cyclic
if there exists a vector
f\in\cal H
such that
\vee\{T^{n}f:n\geq 0\}=\cal H.
We say that
T
is
analytic
if
\cap_{n\geq 0}T^{n}\cal H=\{0\}.

An operator
T\in\cal B(\cal H)
is a
2
-isometry
if

I-2T^{*}T+T^{*^{2}}T^{2}=0.

An operator
T\in\cal B(\cal H)
is a

k
-hyperexpansive
if

\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}{n\choose i}T^{*^{i}}T^{i}\leq 0\ ,\ \forall\ 1\leq n\leq k.

An operator
T\in\cal B(\cal H)
is
completely hyperexpansive
if

\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}{n\choose i}T^{*^{i}}T^{i}\leq 0\ ,\ \forall n\geq 1.

For an excellent account of the basic properties of
2
- isometries, the reader is referred to
[
13
,
10
,
11
,
12
]
. The theory of completely hyperexpansive operators has been developed in
[
1
,
2
,
16
]
.
In what follows, we need to recall the definitions of two special types of Hilbert spaces of holomorphic function viz. the Dirichlet space and the de Branges-Rovnyak space. We shall denote the open unit disk in the complex plane by
\mathbb{D}
and the unit circle by
\mathbb{T}.
Let
\mathbb{H}^{2}
denote the classical Hardy space of analytic functions on
\mathbb{D}
having square integrable Taylor coefficients and
\mathbb{H}^{\infty}
denote the space of all bounded analytic functions on
\mathbb{D}
.

We shall be dealing with the Dirichlet type space
D(\mu)
where the measure
\mu
is supported on the closed unit disk
\overline{\mathbb{D}}.
Aleman points out the delicacy in defining the local Dirichlet integrals for the points
\zeta\in\mathbb{T}
(see
[
1
, Proposition 1.7, Chapter IV]
). Indeed, if
\zeta\in\mathbb{D},
then the local Dirichlet integral of a function
f\in\mathcal{H}^{2}
is defined as

D_{\zeta}(f)=\left\|\frac{f-f(\zeta)}{z-\zeta}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|\frac{f(e^{it})-f(\zeta)}{e^{it}-\zeta}\right|^{2}dt.

If
\zeta\in\mathbb{T},
then the local Dirichlet integral is defined as

D_{\zeta}(f)=\left\|\frac{f-f(\zeta)}{z-\zeta}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}

if the nontangential limit
f(\zeta)
of
f
at
\zeta
exists and
D_{\zeta}(f)=\infty
otherwise.

As pointed out in
[
1
]
, the necessity of defining the local Dirichlet integrals for the boundary points, and thereby extending the support of measure to the closed unit disk is forced by the operator theoretic considerations. Indeed, with this type of Dirichlet space, one can incorporate the case of multiplication operator
M_{z}
being a
2
-isometry (see
[
1
, Theorem 1.10, Chapter IV]
). Following
[
1
, Definition 1.8]
, we recall the definition of Dirichlet space under consideration for a ready reference.

Let
\mu
be a finite, non-negative measure supported on
\overline{\mathbb{D}}.

The Dirichlet space
D(\mu)
, consists of those functions
f\in\mathbb{H}^{2}
with the property that
\int D_{\zeta}(f)d\mu(\zeta)<\infty.

The Dirichlet space
D(\mu)
is a reproducing kernel Hilbert space with

\|f\|^{2}=\|f\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}+\int D_{\zeta}(f)\mathrm{d}\mu(\zeta).

Further, as
\mu
is finite, the multiplication operator
M_{z}
is bounded. In fact, it turns out that
M_{z}
on
D(\mu)
is completely hyperexpansive and if
T\in\cal B(\cal H)
is a completely hyperexpansive operator, then there exists a unique nonnegative measure
\mu
supported on the closed unit disk
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
such that
\cal H=D(\mu)
with the equality of norms and
T
is unitarily equivalent to the operator
M_{z}
on
D(\mu)
(see
[
1
, Theorem 2.5, Chapter IV]
). Further,
M_{z}
is a
2
-isometry if and only if
\mu
is supported on
\mathbb{T}
(see
[
1
, Theorem 1.10(ii), Chapter IV ]
).

We now turn our attention to the definition of a de Branges Rovnyak space.

Following
[
8
]
, recall that

the de Branges-Rovnyak space
\mathcal{H}(b)
associated with a symbol
b\in\mathbb{H}^{\infty},\|b\|_{\infty}\leq 1
is the image of
\mathbb{H}^{2}
under the operator
(I-T_{b}T_{\overline{b}})^{\frac{1}{2}}
endowed with the inner product

\langle(I-T_{b}T_{\bar{b}})^{\frac{1}{2}}f\ ,\ (I-T_{b}T_{\bar{b}})^{\frac{1}{2}}g\ \rangle_{b}\ =\ \langle f,g\rangle_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}\ ,\ f,g\in ker((I-T_{b}T_{\bar{b}})^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\perp}

where
\langle\cdot\rangle_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}
is the usual inner product on
\mathbb{H}^{2}
and
T_{b}
denotes the Toeplitz operator with respect to
b
.

For a detailed survey of de Branges-Rovnyak spaces, the reader is referred to
[
3
,
7
,
8
,
14
]
.

Throughout this paper, we choose a symbol
b
to be a nonextreme point in the unit ball of
\mathbb{H}^{\infty}
. The choice of a nonextreme symbol
b
ensures following important results which are useful in the sequel:

(1)

The polynomials are dense in
\mathcal{H}(b)
(see
[
8
, Section 23.4]
)

(2)

The multiplication operator
M_{z}
on
\mathcal{H}^{2}
leaves
\mathcal{H}(b)
invariant (see
[
9
, Proposition 4]
).

(3)

There exists a unique outer function
a
such that
a(0)>0
and
|a(\zeta)|^{2}+|b(\zeta)|^{2}=1,\ \mbox{a.e on }\mathbb{T}
. Such a function is called the
pythagorean mate of
b
and
(b,a)
are said to form a
pair
. Further,

f\in\mathcal{H}(b)\iff T_{\overline{b}}f\in T_{\overline{a}}\mathbb{H}^{2}

and
T_{\overline{a}}\mathbb{H}^{2}
is dense in
\mathcal{H}(b)
(see
[
14
, pg 24-25]
).

2.
Cyclic, Analytic, Completely Hyperexpansive Operators With Finite Rank Defect Operator

In this section, we provide a characterization for the measure
\mu
, so that the multiplication operator
M_{z}
on the Dirichlet space
D(\mu)
, has defect operator of finite rank. At this stage, we refer the readers to the analogous result
[
4
, Theorem 6.1]
, proved in the context of a
2
-isometry. It turns out that a matrix decomposition of a completely hyperexpansive operator, as described in
[
16
]
, turns out to be useful in proving the characterization.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\mu
be a finite positive Borel measure supported on
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
and let
\Delta_{M_{z}}=M_{z}^{*}M_{z}-I
be the defect operator of the multiplication operator on the Dirichlet space
D(\mu)
. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

\Delta_{M_{z}}
is of rank
k

(ii)

There exist constants
c_{i}>0,1\leq i\leq k
and
\zeta_{i}\in\overline{\mathbb{D}},1\leq i\leq k
such that
\mu=\sum_{i=0}^{k}c_{i}\delta_{\zeta_{i}}

Proof.

Suppose that
\Delta_{M_{z}}
is of rank k.

It is known that
M_{z}
on
D(\mu)
is cyclic, analytic and completely hyperexpansive (
[
1
]
). Using the decomposition theorem for completely hyperexpansive operators (
[
16
, Proposition 3.1]
), we have a decomposition for
M_{z}
as follows:

M_{z}=\begin{bmatrix}V&E\\
0&W\\
\end{bmatrix}
defined on
\ker(\Delta_{M_{z}})\oplus\overline{ran(\Delta_{M_{z}})}

where

(a)

V
is an isometry

(b)

V^{*}E=0

(c)

If
D=E^{*}E+W^{*}W-I
, then
D\geq 0
and
\ker(D)=\{0\}

(d)

Letting
M_{W}(T)=W^{*}TW
we have
(I-M_{W})^{m-1}(D)\geq 0,\\
1\leq m\leq k

We note that
\Delta_{M_{z}}=\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\
0&D\\
\end{bmatrix}

where
D
is invertible and self- adjoint.

Now, letting
m=2
in (d) above, we have

D\geq W^{*}DW

(2.1)

Further, as
D
is invertible, the inequality (
2.1
) yields,

\displaystyle(D^{\frac{1}{2}}WD^{-\frac{1}{2}})^{*}(D^{\frac{1}{2}}WD^{-\frac{1}{2}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D^{-\frac{1}{2}}W^{*}DWD^{-\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle D^{-\frac{1}{2}}DD^{-\frac{1}{2}}\ =\ I

Therefore ,
(D^{\frac{1}{2}}WD^{-\frac{1}{2}})
is a contraction. Now, let
B=\{v_{1},v_{2},\cdots v_{k}\}
be an orthonormal basis of
{ran(\Delta_{M_{z}})}
comprising of eigen vectors of
D^{\frac{1}{2}}WD^{-\frac{1}{2}}
corresponding to the eigen values
\zeta_{i},\ 1\leq i\leq k
, where each
\zeta_{i}\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}
.

Now referring to
(
[
1
, Proposition (1.6)]
)
, we have

\|zf\|^{2}_{D(\mu)}-\|f\|^{2}_{D(\mu)}=\int_{\overline{\mathbb{D}}}|f|^{2}\ \mathrm{d}\mu\ ,\ \forall f\in D(\mu).

(2.2)

By polarization identity, one obtains,

\langle zf,zg\rangle-\langle f,g\rangle=\int_{\overline{\mathbb{D}}}f\bar{g}\ \mathrm{d}\mu\ ,\ \forall f,g\in D(\mu).

(2.3)

So, for
m,n\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\langle\Delta_{M_{z}}M_{z}^{n}1,z^{m}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle z^{n+1},z^{m+1}\rangle-\langle z^{n},z^{m}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\overline{\mathbb{D}}}z^{n}\bar{z}^{m}\ \mathrm{d}\mu.

That is

\langle\Delta_{M_{z}}M_{z}^{n}1,M_{z}^{m}1\rangle=\int_{\overline{\mathbb{D}}}z^{n}\bar{z}^{m}\ \mathrm{d}\mu.

(2.4)

Now note that,
\{D^{-\frac{1}{2}}v_{1},D^{-\frac{1}{2}}v_{2},\cdots,D^{-\frac{1}{2}}v_{k}\}
also forms a basis for
{ran(\Delta_{M_{z}})}
.

Let
P
be the projection onto
{ran(\Delta_{M_{z}})}
. Therefore,
P1=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\beta_{i}D^{-\frac{1}{2}}v_{i}
for some
\beta_{i}\in\mathbb{C},1\leq i\leq k
. Therefore, for
m,n\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\langle\Delta_{M_{z}}M_{z}^{n}1,z^{m}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\Delta_{M_{z}}z^{n},M_{z}^{m}1\rangle.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle DW^{n}P1,W^{m}P1\rangle

which implies

\langle\Delta_{M_{z}}M_{z}^{n}1,M_{z}^{m}1\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\zeta_{i}^{n}\bar{\zeta_{i}}^{m}|\beta_{i}|^{2}.

(2.5)

Equations (
2.4
) and (
2.5
) imply that

\int_{\overline{\mathbb{D}}}z^{n}\bar{z}^{m}\ \mathrm{d}\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\zeta_{i}^{n}\bar{\zeta_{i}}^{m}|\beta_{i}|^{2}.

Since polynomials are dense in
D(\mu)
(
[
6
, Corollary 7.3.4]
), by Stone-Weiertrass’s theorem, we have

\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{k}|\beta_{i}|^{2}\delta_{\zeta_{i}}.

Conversely, suppose that there exist
c_{i}>0
and
\zeta_{i}\in\overline{\mathbb{D}},1\leq i\leq k
such that
\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{k}c_{i}\delta_{\zeta_{i}}
.

As
\mu
is supported at
\zeta_{i}\in\overline{\mathbb{D}},\ 1\leq i\leq k
, for every
f\in D(\mu)
,

\|f\|^{2}_{D(\mu)}=\|f\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}+\int_{\overline{\mathbb{D}}}\left\|\ \frac{f-f(\zeta)}{z-\zeta}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu(z)=\|f\|^{2}_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}c_{i}\left\|\ \frac{f-f(\zeta_{i})}{z-\zeta_{i}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}<\infty.

This implies that

f\in D(\mu)\iff\left\|\ \frac{f-f(\zeta_{i})}{z-\zeta_{i}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}<\infty,\ 1\leq i\leq k.

Since
D(\mu)
is a reproducing kernel Hilbert space, point evaluations must be bounded.
Thus by the reproducing property, there exist linearly independent vectors
\kappa_{\zeta_{1}},\kappa_{\zeta_{2}},\cdots\kappa_{\zeta_{k}}
in
D(\mu)
such that

\langle f,\kappa_{\zeta_{i}}\rangle=f(\zeta_{i})\ ,\ \mbox{for every}\ f\in D(\mu),1\leq i\leq k

We now show that
\Delta_{M_{z}}
is of rank
k
.

Let
F=\bigvee\{\kappa_{\zeta_{i}}|1\leq i\leq k\}
.

Claim:
\overline{ran(\Delta_{M_{z}})}=F

Now, for
f\in D(\mu)
consider,

\displaystyle\langle\Delta_{M_{z}}f,f\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|M_{z}f\|^{2}_{D(\mu)}-\|f\|^{2}_{D(\mu)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\overline{\mathbb{D}}}|f|^{2}\ \mathrm{d}\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}c_{i}|f(\zeta_{i})|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}c_{i}|\langle f,\kappa_{\zeta_{i}}\rangle|^{2}.

If
f\in F^{\perp}
then
\langle\Delta_{M_{z}}f,f\rangle=0
. Since the operator
\Delta_{M_{z}}\geq 0
,
\Delta_{M_{z}}f=0
i.e
f\in\ker(\Delta_{M_{z}})
.
Therefore,
F^{\perp}\subseteq\ker(\Delta_{M_{z}})
or
\overline{ran(\Delta_{M_{z}})}\subseteq F
.

Similarly, if
f\in\ker(\Delta_{M_{z}})
then
\Delta_{M_{z}}f=0
implies
\sum_{i=1}^{k}c_{i}|\langle f,\kappa_{\zeta_{i}}\rangle|^{2}=0
. That is,
\langle f,\kappa_{\zeta_{i}}\rangle=0
for every
1\leq i\leq k\implies f\in F^{\perp}
. In other words,
\ker(\Delta_{M_{z}})\subseteq F^{\perp}
or
F\subseteq\overline{ran(\Delta_{M_{z}})}
. Therefore,
\overline{ran(\Delta_{M_{z}})}=F
and hence
\Delta_{M_{z}}
must be of rank
k
.
∎

3.
Completely Hyperexpansive Shift on a
\mathcal{H}(b)
space

Let
b
be a non-extreme point of the unit ball of
\mathbb{H}^{\infty}
and let
S
denote the operator of multiplication by
z
on
\mathbb{H}^{2}
. The space
\mathcal{H}(b)
is invariant under
S
. Let
T
denote the restriction of
S
to
{\mathcal{H}(b)}.
In other words,
T
is the multiplication by
z
on the space
\mathcal{H}(b)
.

We now obtain the necessary and sufficient conditions on
b
, so that
T
is a completely hyperexpansive operator.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
b
be a nonextreme function in the unit ball of
H^{\infty}
. Then the restriction of the shift operator to the de Branges Rovnyak space
H(b)
is completely hyperexpansive if and only if
b(z)=\frac{c+\gamma z}{1-\beta z}
with
c,\gamma,\beta\in\mathbb{C}
and
|\beta|<1
.

Proof.

Suppose that
T
is a complete hyperexpansion. Then, by definition,
T
is a
k
-hyperexpansion for every
k\geq 1
. In particular, it is a
2
-hyperexpansion. Using the necessary and sufficient conditions for
T
to be a
2
-hyperexpansion (Theorem
1.1
) ,
T
is
2
-hyperexpansive (or concave) if and only if
b
has the form
b(z)=\frac{c+\gamma z}{1-\beta z}
where
c,\gamma,\beta\in\mathbb{C}
and
|\beta|<1
.

Conversely, if

b(z)=\frac{c+\gamma z}{1-\beta z}

where
c,\gamma,\beta\in\mathbb{C}
and
|\beta|<1
, then its pythagorean mate,
a(z)=\frac{\rho-\sigma z}{1-\beta z}
where
\rho
and
\sigma
follow the conditions

(i)

\rho\geq|\sigma|

(ii)

\rho^{2}+|\sigma|^{2}=1+|\beta|^{2}-|c|^{2}-|\gamma|^{2}\ \mbox{and }\ \rho^{2}|\sigma|^{2}=|\beta+\overline{c}\gamma|^{2}

(iii)

\mbox{arg}(\rho)=\mbox{arg}(\beta+\overline{c}\gamma)
.

We observe that for any
h\in\mathcal{H}(b)
,

\displaystyle\langle(I-3T^{*}T+3{T^{*}}^{2}T^{2}-{T^{*}}^{3}T^{3})h,h\rangle_{b}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1+\|b\|_{b}^{2})\left(1-\left|\beta-\frac{a^{\prime}(0)}{a(0)}\right|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\left\{|\langle h,{S^{*}b}^{2}\rangle_{b}|^{2}-2\mathrm{Re}\left(\bar{\frac{{a^{\prime}(0)}}{a(0)}}\langle h,S^{*}b\rangle_{b}\overline{\langle h,{S^{*}}^{2}b\rangle_{b}}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\left[\left|\frac{a^{\prime}(0)}{a(0)}\right|^{2}-1\right]|\langle h,S^{*}b\rangle_{b}|^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1-\left|\beta-\frac{a^{\prime}(0)}{a(0)}\right|^{2}\right)\langle(I-2T^{*}T+{T^{*}}^{2}T^{2})h,h\rangle_{b}

Inductively, it follows that, for every
n\geq 2
and every
h\in\mathcal{H}(b)
,

\left\langle\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}{n\choose i}{T^{*}}^{i}T^{i}\right)h,h\right\rangle_{b}=\left(1-\left|\beta-\frac{a^{\prime}(0)}{a(0)}\right|^{2}\right)^{n-2}\langle(I-2T^{*}T+{T^{*}}^{2}T^{2})h,h\rangle_{b}

Therefore,
T
is completely hyperexpansive if

\langle(I-2T^{*}T+{T^{*}}^{2}T^{2})h,h\rangle_{b}\leq 0\ \mbox{or}\ 1-\left|\beta-\frac{a^{\prime}(0)}{a(0)}\right|^{2}=0.

In either case, by appealing to Theorem
1.1
, we conclude that
T
must be
2
-hyperexpansive and thus it follows that
T
is completely hyperexpansive.

∎

We conclude this section by proving a result required in the proof of the main theorem of this paper.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
b
be a non-extreme point of unit ball of
\mathbb{H}^{\infty}
and
a
be its pythagorean mate. Then the defect operator
\Delta_{T}=T^{*}T-I
is of rank 1 and its range is spanned by
S^{*}b
and its non-zero eigen value equals
|a(0)|^{-2}\|S^{*}b\|_{b}^{2}
.

Proof.

Since
(b,a)
forms a pair, we have

\displaystyle T^{*}T

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I+|a(0)|^{-2}S^{*}b\otimes S^{*}b

\displaystyle\therefore\Delta_{T}=T^{*}T-I

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|a(0)|^{-2}S^{*}b\otimes S^{*}b

where
(x\otimes y)
denotes the operator
(x\otimes y)(z)=\langle z,y\rangle_{b}x\ ,\ z\in\mathcal{H}(b).

Hence the matrix of the operator
\Delta_{T}
relative to the decomposition
\mathcal{H}(b)=\mathbb{C}S^{*}b\oplus(\mathbb{C}S^{*}b)^{\perp}
is given by

\begin{bmatrix}|a(0)|^{-2}\|S^{*}b\|_{b}^{2}&0\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}

which implies the required result.
∎

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.3

This section is devoted to the proof of the main result of the paper viz Theorem
1.3
.

Let
b\in\mathbb{H}^{\infty}
be nonextreme with
\|b\|_{\infty}\leq 1
and let
\mu
be a finite positive Borel measure supported on
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
. Let
T=M_{z}|_{\mathcal{H}(b)}
denote the restriction of multiplication by
z
operator to
\mathcal{H}(b)
space.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
:

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathcal{H}(b)=D(\mu)
with equality of norms.

As
M_{z}
on
D(\mu)
is cyclic, analytic and completely hyperexpansive,
T
must be cyclic, analytic and completely hyperexpansive. Therefore, Theorem
3.1
implies that
b
must be of the form

b(z)=\frac{c+\gamma z}{1-\beta z}

where
c,\gamma,\beta\in\mathbb{C}
and
|\beta|<1
.

Similarly, Proposition
3.2
implies that the corresponding defect operator
\Delta_{T}
has rank 1. Therefore, the corresponding defect operator
\Delta_{M_{z}}
on
D(\mu)
must also have rank 1. So, using Proposition
2.1
we conclude that

\mu=|\alpha|^{2}\delta_{\lambda}\ ,\ \mbox{for some}\ \alpha\in\mathbb{C},\lambda\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}.

Now, for Cauchy kernels
k_{w}(z)=\frac{1}{1-\overline{w}z}
we have,

D_{\lambda}(k_{w})=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left|\frac{k_{w}(z)-k_{w}(\lambda)}{z-\lambda}\right||\mathrm{d}z|=\left\|\frac{\overline{w}}{1-\overline{w}\lambda}k_{w}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}

Since,

D_{\mu}(f)=\int_{\overline{\mathbb{D}}}D_{\zeta}(f)\mathrm{d}\mu(\zeta)

therefore we have,

D_{\mu}(k_{w})=|\alpha|^{2}\left\|\frac{\overline{w}}{1-\overline{w}\lambda}k_{w}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}=\frac{|\alpha|^{2}|w|^{2}}{|1-\overline{\lambda}w|^{2}(1-|w|^{2})}

Similarly,

\|k_{w}\|_{b}^{2}=\|k_{w}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}+\|k_{w}^{+}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}=\|k_{w}\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{(1-|w|^{2})}\frac{|b(w)|^{2}}{|a(w)|^{2}}

But as
\mathcal{H}(b)=D(\mu)
with equality of norms we have,

\frac{1}{(1-|w|^{2})}\frac{|b(w)|^{2}}{|a(w)|^{2}}=\|k_{w}^{+}\|_{H^{2}}^{2}=D_{\mu}(k_{w})=\frac{|\alpha|^{2}|w|^{2}}{|1-\overline{\lambda}w|^{2}(1-|w|^{2})}

(4.6)

In particular, letting
w=0
we obtain
b(0)=0
. That is,
b(z)=\frac{\gamma z}{1-\beta z}
.

Further , if
\phi=\frac{b}{a}
, then equation (
4.6
) gives us

|\phi(w)|^{2}=\frac{|\alpha|^{2}|w|^{2}}{|1-\overline{\lambda}w|^{2}}

As the result is true for every
w
, we obtain

\phi(z)=\frac{\alpha z}{1-\overline{\lambda}z}.

Let
p(z)=\alpha z,\ q(z)=1-\overline{\lambda}z
.
We observe that
\phi
is a Smirnov class function. Then, the pair
(b,a)
can be obtained by the formulation,

|a|^{2}=\frac{|q|^{2}}{|p|^{2}+|q|^{2}}\mbox{ and}\ |b|^{2}=\frac{|p|^{2}}{|p|^{2}+|q|^{2}}\ \mbox{a.e on}\ \mathbb{T}\ \mbox{(see \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{vol2}{}{}, Lemma 23.27]})}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{|\gamma|^{2}}{|1-\beta z|^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|\alpha|^{2}}{|1-\overline{\lambda}z|^{2}+|\alpha|^{2}|z|^{2}}\ ,\ \mathrm{a.e\ on}\ \mathbb{T}

\displaystyle\therefore\ |\gamma|^{2}\left((1+|\lambda|^{2}+|\alpha|^{2})+2\mathrm{Re}(\overline{\lambda}z)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\alpha|^{2}\left((1+|\beta|^{2})+2\mathrm{Re}(\beta z)\right),\ \mathrm{a.e\ on}\ \mathbb{T}

Substituting
z=1,\ -1,\ i,\ -i
we obtain the following:

If
\lambda=0
then

|\gamma|^{2}=\frac{1}{1+|\alpha|^{2}}

and therefore
\beta=0

Similarly, if
\lambda\neq 0
, then

|\gamma|^{2}=\frac{|\alpha|^{2}}{2|\lambda|^{2}}\left((1+|\alpha|^{2}+|\lambda|^{2})\pm\sqrt{(1+|\alpha|^{2}+|\lambda|^{2})^{2}-4|\lambda|^{2}}\right).

But,

|\gamma|^{2}=\frac{|\alpha|^{2}}{2|\lambda|^{2}}\left((1+|\alpha|^{2}+|\lambda|^{2})+\sqrt{(1+|\alpha|^{2}+|\lambda|^{2})^{2}-4|\lambda|^{2}}\right)

implies
|\beta|>1
.

Therefore,

|\gamma|^{2}=\frac{|\alpha|^{2}}{2|\lambda|^{2}}\left((1+|\alpha|^{2}+|\lambda|^{2})-\sqrt{(1+|\alpha|^{2}+|\lambda|^{2})^{2}-4|\lambda|^{2}}\right).

Hence

\beta=\frac{\overline{\lambda}}{2|\lambda|^{2}}\left((1+|\alpha|^{2}+|\lambda|^{2})-\sqrt{(1+|\alpha|^{2}+|\lambda|^{2})^{2}-4|\lambda|^{2}}\right).

Letting
A_{\lambda}=\gamma
and
B_{\lambda}=\beta
we obtain the required result.

Conversely, suppose that
\mu=|\alpha|^{2}\delta_{\lambda},\ \mbox{for some }\alpha\in\mathbb{C},\ \lambda\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}
and
b(z)=\frac{A_{\lambda}z}{1-B_{\lambda}z}
where
A_{\lambda}
and
B_{\lambda}
are constants depending on
\alpha
and
\lambda
such that

|A_{\lambda}|^{2}=\begin{cases}\left(\frac{|\alpha|^{2}}{2|\lambda|^{2}}\left((1+|\alpha|^{2}+|\lambda|^{2})-\sqrt{(1+|\alpha|^{2}+|\lambda|^{2})^{2}-4|\lambda|^{2}}\right)\right)&\mbox{, if}\ \lambda\neq 0\\
\left(\dfrac{1}{1+|\alpha|^{2}}\right)&\mbox{, if}\ \lambda=0\end{cases}

and

B_{\lambda}=\frac{|A_{\lambda}|^{2}\overline{\lambda}}{|\alpha|^{2}}.

Then by Proposition
2.1
, the defect operator of
M_{z}
on
D(\mu)
,
\Delta_{M_{z}}
must be of rank 1.

Similarly, applying Proposition
3.1
and Proposition
3.2
, we conclude that the operator
T
must be cyclic, analytic, completely hyperexpansive and its defect operator,
\Delta_{T}
must be of rank 1.

Recall that, by Aleman’s result
[
1
, Theorem 2.5]
, T must be unitarily equivalent to the multiplication by
z
operator on
D(\mu^{\prime})
for some non-negative, Borel measure
\mu^{\prime}
supported on
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
and
\mathcal{H}(b)=D(\mu^{\prime})
with equality of norms. But, previous part of this theorem implies that
\mu
and
\mu^{\prime}
must be equal.∎

The above result provides a formulation for computing the non-extreme symbol
b
, given a positive, point mass measure
\mu
on
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
, so that
D(\mu)=\mathcal{H}(b)
with equality of norms. We use the following examples to illustrate the construction.

Example 4.1 :

Suppose
\mu=\delta_{0}
i.e
|\alpha|^{2}=1
and
\lambda=0
. Then by the formula prescribed in the above theorem, one obtains
A_{\lambda}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
and
B_{\lambda}=0
.

Example 4.2 :

Suppose
\mu=\delta_{\frac{1}{2}}
i.e
|\alpha|^{2}=1
and
\lambda=\frac{1}{2}
. Then,

A_{\lambda}=\left(\dfrac{{9-\sqrt{65}}}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}
and
B_{\lambda}=\dfrac{(9-\sqrt{65})}{4}
.

We conclude this paper by deducing Corollary
1.2
from Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of Corollary
1.2
:

Proof.

Letting
|\lambda|=1
in Theorem
1.3
, we obtain

|A_{\lambda}|^{2}=\frac{|\alpha|^{2}}{2}\left(2+|\alpha|^{2}-|\alpha|\sqrt{(4+|\alpha|^{2})}\right),

Similarly,
B_{\lambda}=\frac{|A_{\lambda}|^{2}\overline{\lambda}}{|\alpha|^{2}}
implies

|B_{\lambda}|=\frac{|A_{\lambda}|^{2}}{|\alpha|^{2}}\neq 0

Now ,

\displaystyle|B_{\lambda}|+|A_{\lambda}|=1

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\frac{|A_{\lambda}|^{2}}{|\alpha|^{2}}+|A_{\lambda}|=1

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle|A_{\lambda}|^{2}=\frac{|\alpha|^{2}}{2}\left(2+|\alpha|^{2}\pm|\alpha|^{2}\sqrt{(4+|\alpha|^{2})}\right)

Therefore,

|\alpha|^{2}=\frac{|A_{\lambda}|^{2}}{|B_{\lambda}|},\ \lambda=\frac{\overline{B_{\lambda}}}{|B_{\lambda}|}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Low regularity well-posedness for KP-I equations: the dispersion-generalized case
Akansha Sanwal, Robert Schippa

Abstract
. We prove new well-posedness results for dispersion-generalized Kadomtsev–Petviashvili I equations in ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} , which family links the classical KP-I equation with the fifth order KP-I equation. For strong enough dispersion, we show global well-posedness in L 2 ​ ( ℝ 2 ) L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}) . To this end, we combine resonance and transversality considerations with Strichartz estimates and a nonlinear Loomis–Whitney inequality. Moreover, we prove that for small dispersion, the equations cannot be solved via Picard iteration. In this case, we use an additional frequency dependent time localization.



1.
Introduction and main results

We consider the Cauchy problem for the fractional Kadomtsev–Petviashvili I (fKP-I) equation

(1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\partial_{t}u-D^{\alpha}_{x}\partial_{x}u-\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{y}^{2}u&=u\partial_{x}u,\quad(t,x,y)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R},\\
u(0)&=u_{0}\in H^{s_{1},s_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),\end{array}\right.

where
2<\alpha<4
, and the operator
D_{x}^{\alpha}
is given by
(D_{x}^{\alpha}f)^{\wedge}(\xi)=|\xi|^{\alpha}\hat{f}(\xi)
. For
2<\alpha\leqslant\frac{5}{2}
, we only consider real-valued solutions; for
\alpha>\frac{5}{2}
we also treat complex-valued solutions. Note that the solution stays real-valued provided that the initial data is real-valued. In this paper, we consider initial data from anisotropic Sobolev spaces
H^{s_{1},s_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, which are defined by

\begin{split}H^{s_{1},s_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})&:=\{\phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}):\|\phi\|_{H^{s_{1},s_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}<\infty\},\\
\|\phi\|_{H^{s_{1},s_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}&=\|\hat{\phi}(\xi,\eta)(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{s_{1}}{2}}(1+|\eta|^{2})^{\frac{s_{2}}{2}}\|_{L_{\xi,\eta}^{2}}.\end{split}

The following quantities are conserved for real-valued solutions:

(2)

\displaystyle M(u)(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}u(x,y)^{2}dxdy,

(3)

\displaystyle E_{\alpha}(u)(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Big(\frac{1}{2}|D_{x}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}u|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}|\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{y}u|^{2}+\frac{1}{6}u^{3}\Big)dxdy.

Hence, the natural energy space is given by

\mathbf{E}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{2})=\{\phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}):\|\phi\|_{E^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}:=\|p(\xi,\eta)\hat{\phi}(\xi,\eta)\|_{L^{2}_{\xi,\eta}}<\infty\},

where

p(\xi,\eta):=1+|\xi|^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}+\frac{|\eta|}{|\xi|}.

We prefer to study the solutions in the scale of anisotropic Sobolev spaces. We believe that adapting the present analysis will yield global well-posedness in the energy space, which is a smaller space, as well. Here we focus on the much larger anisotropic Sobolev spaces. For further remarks on the connection between Sobolev spaces and the energy spaces, we refer to
[
22
]
.

Moreover, if
u
solves the problem (
1
) with initial data
\phi
, then
u_{\lambda}
given by

u_{\lambda}(t,x,y)=\lambda^{-\alpha}u(\lambda^{-(\alpha+1)}t,\lambda^{-1}x,\lambda^{-\frac{\alpha+2}{2}}y)

also solves the same with scaled initial data

(4)

\phi_{\lambda}=\lambda^{-\alpha}\phi(\lambda^{-1}x,\lambda^{-\frac{\alpha+2}{2}}y).

We have

(5)

\|\phi_{\lambda}\|_{\dot{H}^{s_{1},s_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}=\lambda^{-\frac{3\alpha}{4}+1-s_{1}-(\frac{\alpha}{2}+1)s_{2}}\|\phi\|_{\dot{H}^{s_{1},s_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

This shows that for
\alpha=\frac{4}{3}
, (
1
) is
L^{2}
-critical. For
\alpha>2
, which is considered presently, (
1
) is
L^{2}
-subcritical.

By local well-posedness, we refer to existence, uniqueness, and continuity of the data-to-solution mapping locally in time.

The range of dispersion considered in this paper starts with the classical KP-I equation

(6)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\partial_{t}u-D^{2}_{x}\partial_{x}u-\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{y}^{2}u&=u\partial_{x}u,\quad(t,x,y)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R},\\
u(0)&=u_{0}\in H^{s_{1},s_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),\end{array}\right.

which has been extensively studied (see
[
15
,
9
,
6
]
and references therein). Ionescu–Kenig–Tataru
[
9
]
proved global well-posedness in the energy space, and Z. Guo
et al.

[
6
]
showed improved local well-posedness in the anisotropic Sobolev space
H^{1,0}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. The derivative loss in case of unfavorable resonance makes the equation
quasilinear
. This means it is not amenable to Picard iteration in standard Sobolev spaces as observed by Molinet–Saut–Tzvetkov
[
15
]
. In the works
[
9
,
6
]
, short-time Fourier restriction was used to overcome the derivative loss in the nonlinearity. We refer to the PhD thesis of the second author for an overview of short-time Fourier restriction
[
20
]
. Since short-time Fourier restriction also involves energy estimates, the results in
[
9
,
6
]
require real-valued solutions. Likewise, the results we prove for small dispersion require real-valued initial data:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
2<\alpha\leqslant\frac{5}{2}
. Then, (
1
) is locally well-posed in
H^{s,0}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for
s>5-2\alpha
and real-valued initial data.

We give a technically more detailed version of the above theorem in Section
5
.

However, the data-to-solution mapping constructed in the proof of Theorem
1.1
is not analytic. Indeed, we show that for
\alpha<\frac{7}{3}
, the data-to-solution mapping cannot be of class
C^{2}
. Previously, Molinet–Saut–Tzvetkov
[
15
]
showed that the data-to-solution mapping cannot be
C^{2}
for the KP-I equation (see also
[
12
]
). This result was generalized by Linares–Pilod–Saut
[
14
]
for
\alpha<2
. It turns out that the argument extends to
\alpha<\frac{7}{3}
:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\alpha<\frac{7}{3}
,
(s_{1},s_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Then, there exists no
T>0
such that there is a function space
X_{T}\hookrightarrow C([-T,T];H^{s_{1},s_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))
, in which (
1
) admits a unique local solution such that the flow-map for (
1
) given by

\Gamma_{t}:u_{0}\mapsto u(t),~~~t\in[-T,T],

is
C^{2}
-differentiable at zero from
H^{s_{1},s_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
to
H^{s_{1},s_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
.

The problematic nonlinear interaction is a resonant
High\times Low
-interaction in which a free solution with high
x
frequencies interacts with a solution at low
x
frequencies. With the dispersion relation for the fractional KP-I equation given by

(7)

\omega_{\alpha}(\xi,\eta)=|\xi|^{\alpha}\xi+\frac{\eta^{2}}{\xi},\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\},\;\eta\in\mathbb{R},

we find the resonance function to be

\Omega_{\alpha}(\xi_{1},\eta_{1},\xi_{2},\eta_{2})=|\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}|^{\alpha}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})-|\xi_{1}|^{\alpha}\xi_{1}-|\xi_{2}|^{\alpha}\xi_{2}-\frac{(\eta_{1}\xi_{2}-\eta_{2}\xi_{1})^{2}}{\xi_{1}\xi_{2}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})}.

Due to opposite signs of the terms
|\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}|^{\alpha}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})-|\xi_{1}|^{\alpha}\xi_{1}-|\xi_{2}|^{\alpha}\xi_{2}
and
\frac{(\eta_{1}\xi_{2}-\eta_{2}\xi_{1})^{2}}{\xi_{1}\xi_{2}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})}
, the resonance function can become much smaller than the first term, which we refer to as resonant case.
However, we shall see that in the resonant case, we can argue that the interaction between the two nonlinear waves and the dual factor with low modulation is strongly transverse, which we quantify via a nonlinear Loomis–Whitney inequality. This transversality was already observed in
[
9
]
, while in the proof in
[
9
]
this is not related to nonlinear Loomis–Whitney. We believe that pointing out the connection with nonlinear Loomis–Whitney inequalities makes the proof more systematic.

Nonlinear Loomis–Whitney inequalities were first investigated by Bennett–Carbery–Wright
[
3
]
and quantitative versions suitable for application to PDEs were proved by Bejenaru–Herr–Tataru
[
1
,
2
]
. These were all local though. We use a global version to simplify the argument, which is a result of Kinoshita and the second author
[
11
]
. We also refer to references in
[
11
]
for further discussion of nonlinear Loomis–Whitney inequalities.

The crucial ingredient in the resonant case of low modulation is to use the nonlinear Loomis–Whitney inequality to show a genuinely trilinear estimate, which improves on the bilinear estimate. Let
f_{i}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3};\mathbb{R}_{+})
denote functions dyadically localized in spatial frequency in the
x
direction around
N_{i}\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}
and in modulation
L_{i}\in 2^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
with
N_{1}\sim N_{2}\geqslant N_{3}
. With notations explained below, we have
\text{supp}(f_{i})\subseteq\tilde{D}_{N_{i},L_{i}}
. Moreover, let
L_{i}\leqslant N_{1}^{\alpha}N_{2}
. Then, we show the estimate

\int(f_{1}*f_{2})\cdot f_{3}\lesssim N_{1}^{-\frac{3\alpha}{4}+\frac{1}{2}}N_{3}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{3}L_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f_{i}\|_{L^{2}}.

Clearly, for
\alpha\geqslant 2
and
N_{3}\gtrsim 1
, this ameliorates the derivative loss. The observation is that for
N_{3}\gtrsim N_{1}^{-\kappa}
for some
\kappa>0
, this estimate still suffices to overcome the derivative loss, whereas for
N_{3}\lesssim N_{1}^{-\kappa}
, the bilinear Strichartz estimate gains additional powers of
N_{1}^{-1}
. The bilinear Strichartz estimate is another consequence of transversality in case of resonance. It reads for free solutions in the resonant case with
N_{1}\gg N_{2}
:

\|P_{N_{1}}U_{\alpha}(t)u_{0}P_{N_{2}}U_{\alpha}(t)v_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}\lesssim\frac{N_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{N_{1}^{\frac{\alpha}{4}}}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\|v_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}

with
(U_{\alpha}(t)f)^{\wedge}(\xi,\eta)=e^{it\omega_{\alpha}(\xi,\eta)}\hat{f}(\xi,\eta)
.

By combining the nonlinear Loomis–Whitney inequality and the bilinear Strichartz estimate, we note that the fractional KP-I equations are semilinearly well-posed for
\alpha>\frac{5}{2}
. In this range we solve the equations by applying the contraction mapping principle in suitable function spaces. This suggests the choice for frequency dependent time localization obtained by interpolating between
(\alpha,T(N))=(2,N^{-1})
and
(\alpha,T(N))=\big(\frac{5}{2}+,1\big)
, which suggests
T(N)=N^{-(2\alpha-5)-\varepsilon}
. We shall choose
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(\alpha)
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\frac{5}{2}<\alpha<4
. Then, (
1
) is analytically locally well-posed in
H^{s,0}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for
s>\frac{5}{4}-\frac{\alpha}{2}
.

The analyticity of the data-to-solution mapping is a consequence of applying the contraction mapping principle and the analyticity of the nonlinearity.
By conservation of mass and persistence of regularity, we have the following:

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
s\geqslant 0
, and
\frac{5}{2}<\alpha<4
. Then, (
1
) is globally well-posed in
H^{s,0}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for real-valued initial data.

We remark that it was well-known that the fifth order KP-I equation

(8)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\partial_{t}u-D^{4}_{x}\partial_{x}u-\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{y}^{2}u&=u\partial_{x}u,\quad(t,x,y)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R},\\
u(0)&=u_{0}\in H^{s_{1},s_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\end{array}\right.

can be solved via Picard iteration as pointed out by Saut–Tzvetkov
[
18
,
19
]
. Their result was improved by B. Guo
et al.

[
5
]
using short-time Fourier restriction and Yan
et al.

[
23
]
(see also
[
13
]
for an earlier result) recovered the same local well-posedness result without using frequency dependent time localization.

In the limiting cases of
\alpha
presently considered, we recover the currently best local well-posedness results in anisotropic Sobolev spaces. For
\alpha\downarrow 2
we recover the result from
[
6
]
and for
\alpha\uparrow 4
we arrive at the result from
[
23
]
. We note that there is still a mismatch between the range of dispersion, for which we can show failure of Picard iteration and for which we actually use frequency-dependent time localization. It is unclear whether one has to improve the counterexample or the argument to show semilinear local well-posedness.

Moreover, in the companion paper
[
8
]
, we consider the dispersion-generalized KP-I equation (
2<\alpha<4
) in three dimensions in non-periodic, periodic, and mixed settings.

Notation and function spaces

We use
a\pm

a\pm\epsilon

\epsilon>0
sufficiently small. Also, we use notation
A\lesssim B

A\leqslant CB

2.1.
Fourier transform

Spatial variables are denoted by
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and the time variable by
t\in\mathbb{R}
. The corresponding Fourier variables are denoted by
(\xi,\eta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
\tau
, respectively.
We use the following convention for the space-time Fourier transform:

\hat{u}(\tau,\xi,\eta)=(\mathcal{F}_{t,x,y}u)(\tau,\xi,\eta)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{-i(t\tau+x\xi+y\eta)}u(t,x,y)dtdxdy.

We shall also use notation
\hat{u}=\mathcal{F}_{x,y}u
for the purely spatial Fourier transform, which should be clear from context.
The Fourier transform is inverted by

u(t,x,y)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{i(t\tau+x\xi+y\eta)}\hat{u}(\tau,\xi,\eta)d\tau d\xi d\eta.

2.2.
Function spaces

We introduce the short-time
X^{s,b}

\phi_{1}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(-2,2)

\phi_{1}(\xi)=1
for
\xi\in[-1,1]

Let
\mathbb{N}_{0}:=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. We define Littlewood-Paley projections: For

and
N\in 2^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}

(P_{N}f)^{\wedge}(\xi,\eta)=\phi_{N}(\xi)\hat{f}(\xi,\eta).

For
N\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}
, let

\begin{split}A_{N}&=\Big\{(\xi,\eta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:\frac{N}{8}\leqslant|\xi|\leqslant 8N\Big\},\\
\end{split}

with the obvious modification for
A_{1}
. Moreover, for
N\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}
, we let

\tilde{A}_{N}=\Big\{(\xi,\eta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:\frac{N}{8}\leqslant|\xi|\leqslant 8N\Big\}.

Additionally, for
N\in 2^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
,
L\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}
, we define

\begin{split}D_{N,L}&=\Big\{(\tau,\xi,\eta)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}:(\xi,\eta)\in A_{N},\,\frac{L}{4}\leqslant|\tau-\omega_{\alpha}(\xi,\eta)|\leqslant 4L\Big\},\\
D_{N,\leqslant L}&=\bigcup_{L^{\prime}=1}^{L}D_{N,L^{\prime}},\\
D_{N,1}&=\{(\tau,\xi,\eta)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}:(\xi,\eta)\in A_{N},\,|\tau-\omega_{\alpha}(\xi,\eta)|\leqslant 2\}.\end{split}

For
N\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}
,
L\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}
, we define

\tilde{D}_{N,L}=\{(\tau,\xi,\eta)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}:(\xi,\eta)\in\tilde{A}_{N},\,\frac{L}{4}\leqslant|\tau-\omega_{\alpha}(\xi,\eta)|\leqslant 4L\}

with the obvious modification for
L=1
.

In the following we write for notational convenience, in order to distinguish modulation and spatial frequencies,
\eta_{L}(\tau)=\phi_{L}(\tau)
for
L\in 2^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
, and
\eta_{\leqslant L}(\tau)=\sum_{L^{\prime}\in 2^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}\cap[1,L]}\eta_{L^{\prime}}(\tau)
. We let

X_{N}=\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}):f\text{ is supported in }\mathbb{R}\times A_{N},\;\|f\|_{X_{N}}<\infty\},

and

\|f\|_{X_{N}}=\sum_{L\in 2^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}}L^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\eta_{L}(\tau-\omega_{\alpha}(\xi,\eta))f\|_{L^{2}_{\tau}L^{2}_{\xi,\eta}}.

Note that

\Big\|\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(\tau,\xi,\eta)|d\tau\Big\|_{L^{2}_{\xi,\eta}}\lesssim\|f\|_{X_{N}},

and we record the estimate

(9)

\begin{split}&\sum_{L^{\prime}\geqslant L}L^{\prime\frac{1}{2}}\Big\|\eta_{L^{\prime}}(\tau-\omega_{\alpha}(\xi,\eta))\int|f(\tau^{\prime},\xi,\eta)|L^{\prime-1}(1+L^{\prime-1}|\tau-\tau^{\prime}|)^{-4}d\tau^{\prime}\Big\|_{L_{\tau,\xi,\eta}^{2}}\\
&\quad+L^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big\|\eta_{\leqslant L}(\tau-\omega_{\alpha}(\xi,\eta))\int|f(\tau^{\prime},\xi,\eta)|L^{-1}(1+L^{-1}|\tau-\tau^{\prime}|)^{-4}d\tau^{\prime}\Big\|_{L_{\tau,\xi,\eta}^{2}}\lesssim\|f\|_{X_{N}}.\end{split}

We find for Schwartz functions
\gamma\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
,
M,N\in 2^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
,
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
,
f\in X_{N}
, the estimate

\|\mathcal{F}_{t,x,y}[\gamma(M(t-t_{0}))\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{t,x,y}(f)]\|_{X_{N}}\lesssim_{\gamma}\|f\|_{X_{N}}.

We define

E_{N}=\{\phi:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}\,:\,\hat{\phi}\text{ is supported in }A_{N},\;\|\phi\|_{E_{N}}=\|\phi\|_{L^{2}}<\infty\}.

For
\alpha\in(2,5/2]
and dyadic frequency
N\in 2^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
, we choose the time localization as
N^{-(5-2\alpha)-\varepsilon(\alpha)}
. Next, define

\begin{split}&\;F_{N}=\{u_{N}\in C(\mathbb{R};E_{N})\,:\,\\
&\qquad\quad\|u_{N}\|_{F_{N}}=\sup_{t_{N}\in\mathbb{R}}\|\mathcal{F}_{t,x,y}[u_{N}\cdot\eta_{0}(N^{(5-2\alpha)+\varepsilon}(t-t_{N}))]\|_{X_{N}}<\infty\}.\end{split}

The dependence on
\alpha
is suppressed. We place the solution into these short-time function spaces after dyadic frequency localization. For the nonlinearity, we consider correspondingly

\begin{split}\mathcal{N}_{N}&=\{u_{N}\in C(\mathbb{R};E_{N})\,:\,\|u_{N}\|_{\mathcal{N}_{N}}=\sup_{t_{N}\in\mathbb{R}}\|(\tau-\omega_{\alpha}(\xi,\eta)+iN^{(5-2\alpha)+\varepsilon})^{-1}\\
&\quad\times\mathcal{F}_{t,x,y}[u_{N}\cdot\eta_{0}(N^{(5-2\alpha)+\varepsilon}(t-t_{N}))]\|_{X_{N}}<\infty\}.\end{split}

We localize the spaces in time by the usual means: For
T\in(0,1]
, let

\begin{split}F_{N}(T)&=\{u_{N}\in C([-T,T];E_{N})\,:\,\|u_{N}\|_{F_{N}(T)}=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tilde{u}_{N}=u_{N}\text{ in }\\

&\hskip 56.9055pt\,\|u\|^{2}_{E^{s,0}(T)}=\sum_{N\in 2^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}}N^{2s}\sup_{t\in[-T,T]}\|P_{N}u(t)\|^{2}_{E_{N}(T)}<\infty\}.\\

N\in 2^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}

(10)

Lemma 2.2

[

Let

Then, the following estimate holds:

(12)

\|u\|_{F^{s,0}(T)}\lesssim\|u\|_{E^{s,0}(T)}+\|f\|_{\mathcal{N}^{s,0}(T)}.

2.3.
Linear Strichartz estimates

We define the linear propagator
U_{\alpha}(t)
as a Fourier multiplier acting on functions
\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
whose Fourier transform is supported away from the origin

(U_{\alpha}(t)\phi)^{\wedge}(\xi,\eta)=e^{it\omega_{\alpha}(\xi,\eta)}\hat{\phi}(\xi,\eta)=e^{it(|\xi|^{\alpha}\xi+\frac{\eta^{2}}{\xi})}\hat{\phi}(\xi,\eta).

Since
U_{\alpha}(t)
is a linear isometric mapping on

\alpha>2

[
7
]
for dispersion-generalized KP-II equations, but it is easy to see that the argument transfers to KP-I equations, as pointed out for
\alpha=2

[

]

Theorem 2.3

(Linear Strichartz estimate, cf.
[
7
, Theorem 3.1]
)
.

Let
\alpha\geqslant 2
,
2<q\leqslant\infty
, and

\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{2},\quad\gamma:=\Big(1-\frac{2}{r}\Big)\Big(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\alpha}{4}\Big).

Then, we have

(14)

\|D_{x}^{-\gamma}U_{\alpha}(t)u_{0}\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{x,y}^{r}}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{x,y}}

with
(D_{x}^{-\gamma}f)^{\wedge}(\xi,\eta)=|\xi|^{-\gamma}\hat{f}(\xi,\eta)
for
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
.

We record a second linear Strichartz estimate for low
x

Lemma 2.4

(Strichartz estimates for low frequencies)
.

Let
N,K\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}
,

be an interval of length
|I|\sim K
, and
|\xi|\sim N
for any

\hat{u}_{0}(\xi,\eta)=0

(15)

\|U_{\alpha}(t)u_{0}\|_{L_{t}^{4}([0,1];L^{4}_{x,y}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))}\lesssim K^{\frac{1}{4}}N^{\frac{1}{8}}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}.

Proof.

We use Bernstein’s inequality in
x
, Plancherel’s theorem, and Minkowski’s inequality to find

\begin{split}&\quad\Big\|\int e^{i(x\xi+y\eta+t(\xi|\xi|^{\alpha}+\frac{\eta^{2}}{\xi}))}\hat{u}_{0}(\xi,\eta)d\xi d\eta\Big\|_{L_{t}^{4}([0,1];L^{4}_{x,y}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))}\\
&\lesssim K^{\frac{1}{4}}\Big\|\int e^{i(y\eta+t\frac{\eta^{2}}{\xi})}\hat{u}_{0}(\xi,\eta)d\eta\Big\|_{L_{t}^{4}([0,1];L_{y}^{4}L_{\xi}^{2})}\\
&\lesssim K^{\frac{1}{4}}\Big(\int_{I}d\xi\Big\|\int e^{i(y\eta+t\frac{\eta^{2}}{\xi})}\hat{u}_{0}(\xi,\eta)d\eta\big\|^{2}_{L_{t}^{4}([0,1];L^{4}_{y}(\mathbb{R})}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.\end{split}

Hence, it suffices to prove

(16)

\Big\|\int e^{i(y\eta+t\frac{\eta^{2}}{\xi})}\hat{u}_{0}(\xi,\eta)d\eta\Big\|_{L_{t}^{4}([0,1];L^{4}_{y}(\mathbb{R}))}\lesssim N^{\frac{1}{8}}\|\hat{u_{0}}(\xi,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}_{\eta}}.

By a change of variables supposing
\xi>0
without loss of generality and Hölder in time, we find

\begin{split}&\qquad\Big\|\int e^{i(y\eta+t\frac{\eta^{2}}{\xi})}\hat{u}_{0}(\xi,\eta)d\eta\big\|_{L_{t}^{4}([0,1];L^{4}_{y}(\mathbb{R}))}\\
&\lesssim N^{\frac{1}{4}}\Big\|\int e^{i(y\eta+t\eta^{2})}\hat{u}_{0}(\xi,\eta)d\eta\Big\|_{L_{t}^{4}([0,\xi^{-1}];L^{4}_{y}(\mathbb{R}))}\\
&\lesssim N^{\frac{1}{8}}\Big\|\int e^{i(y\eta+t\eta^{2})}\hat{u}_{0}(\xi,\eta)d\eta\Big\|_{L_{t}^{8}([0,\xi^{-1}];L^{4}_{y}(\mathbb{R}))}\\
&\lesssim N^{\frac{1}{8}}\|\hat{u}_{0}(\xi,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}_{\eta}}.\end{split}

The ultimate estimate is an application of the
L_{t}^{8}L_{y}^{4}
-Strichartz estimate for the one-dimensional Schrödinger equation (cf.
[
21
, Section 2.3]
).
∎

As a consequence of the transfer principle (cf.
[
21
, Lemma 2.9]
), we have the following:

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
u\in X_{N}
be supported in

\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}(u)\|_{L^{4}_{t,x,y}}\lesssim[\max(1,N)]^{\frac{1}{4}-\frac{\alpha}{8}}L^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u\|_{L^{2}}.

3.
C^{2}

Recall that the resonance function is given by

\begin{split}\Omega_{\alpha}^{1}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\eta_{1},\eta_{2})&=|\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}|^{\alpha}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})-|\xi_{1}|^{\alpha}\xi_{1}-|\xi_{2}|^{\alpha}\xi_{2},\\

In the following we denote

Proof of Theorem

.

We define the functions

Here

\hat{u}_{2}(t,\xi,\eta)=\frac{c\xi e^{it(|\xi|^{\alpha}\xi+\frac{\eta^{2}}{\xi})}}{|D_{1}|^{\frac{1}{2}}|D_{2}|^{\frac{1}{2}}N^{s_{1}+(1+\frac{\alpha}{2})s_{2}}}\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}{(\xi_{1},\eta_{1})\in D_{1},}\\
{(\xi_{2},\eta_{2})\in D_{2}}\end{subarray}}\frac{e^{-it\Omega_{\alpha}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\eta_{1},\eta_{2})}-1}{\Omega_{\alpha}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\eta_{1},\eta_{2})}d\xi_{1}d\eta_{1}.

Lemma 3.1

(Size of the resonance function)
.

Let
(\xi_{i},\eta_{i})\in D_{i}
,
i=1,2
, then

|\Omega^{1}_{\alpha}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\eta_{1},\eta_{2})|\sim N^{\alpha}\gamma.

Proof.

We carry out a case-by-case analysis:

(i)

\xi_{1}>0,\xi_{2}>0
: Using the mean value theorem,

(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})^{\alpha+1}-\xi_{2}^{\alpha+1}=(\alpha+1)\xi_{1}\xi_{\ast}^{\alpha},\quad\xi_{\ast}\in(\xi_{2},\xi_{2}+\xi_{1}).

This gives

|\Omega^{1}_{\alpha}|\sim|\xi_{1}((\alpha+1)(\xi_{\ast}^{\alpha}-\xi_{1}^{\alpha}))|\sim N^{\alpha}\gamma.

(ii)

|\xi_{1}|<|\xi_{2}|,\xi_{1}>0,\xi_{2}<0
: Define new variables by
\xi_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}=-\xi_{2},\;\xi_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}>0
.
We have
\xi_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}=\xi_{1}-(\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}^{{}^{\prime}})
. Hence,

\begin{split}\Omega_{\alpha}^{1}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\eta_{1},\eta_{2})&=|\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}|^{\alpha}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})-|\xi_{1}|^{\alpha}\xi_{1}-|\xi_{2}|^{\alpha}\xi_{2}\\
&=-(\xi_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}-\xi_{1})^{\alpha+1}-\xi_{1}^{\alpha+1}+(\xi_{2}^{{}^{\prime}})^{\alpha+1}\\
&=(\xi_{2}^{{}^{\prime}})^{\alpha+1}-(\xi_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}-\xi_{1})^{\alpha+1}-\xi_{1}^{\alpha+1}.\end{split}

This is the same form as obtained in case
(i)
. Hence, we can conclude the same for this case.

Remark 3.2
.

The above argument can be used to determine the size of the resonance function in other cases.

Proof of Theorem

(ctd)
. Using Taylor’s theorem, we have

We choose

, which makes the resonance function small. The

4.
Resonance, transversality, and the nonlinear Loomis–Whitney inequality

In this section, we analyze the resonance function and use it to obtain trilinear estimates via the nonlinear Loomis–Whitney inequality. Moreover, we employ transversality in the resonant case to obtain genuinely bilinear estimates.
We recall that the dispersion relation for the fKP-I equation is given by

\omega_{\alpha}(\xi,\eta)=|\xi|^{\alpha}\xi+\frac{\eta^{2}}{\xi},

and the resonance function is given by

\Omega_{\alpha}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\eta_{1},\eta_{2})=|\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}|^{\alpha}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})-|\xi_{1}|^{\alpha}\xi_{1}-|\xi_{2}|^{\alpha}\xi_{2}-\frac{(\eta_{1}\xi_{2}-\eta_{2}\xi_{1})^{2}}{\xi_{1}\xi_{2}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})}.

We say that we are in the resonant case, if

|\Omega_{\alpha}|\ll||\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}|^{\alpha}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})-|\xi_{1}|^{\alpha}\xi_{1}-|\xi_{2}|^{\alpha}\xi_{2}|.

Suppose that we have
N_{\max}\sim|\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}|\sim|\xi_{1}|\gtrsim|\xi_{2}|\sim N_{\min}
, then from the computation done in Lemma
3.1
, we get that the right-hand side in the above equation has size
N_{\max}^{\alpha}N_{\min}
. We find in the resonant case

N_{\max}^{\alpha}N_{\min}\sim\Big|\frac{(\eta_{1}\xi_{2}-\eta_{2}\xi_{1})^{2}}{\xi_{1}\xi_{2}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})}\Big|.

This can be further simplified to

(20)

|\eta_{1}\xi_{2}-\eta_{2}\xi_{1}|\sim N_{\max}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}+1}N_{\min}.

We consider the gradient of the dispersion relation next:

\nabla\omega_{\alpha}(\xi,\eta)=\Big((\alpha+1)|\xi|^{\alpha}-\frac{\eta^{2}}{\xi^{2}},\frac{2\eta}{\xi}\Big).

Using (
20
), we have

(21)

|\nabla\omega_{\alpha}(\xi_{1},\eta_{1})-\nabla\omega_{\alpha}(\xi_{2},\eta_{2})|\gtrsim\Big|\frac{\eta_{1}}{\xi_{1}}-\frac{\eta_{2}}{\xi_{2}}\Big|\sim N_{\max}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}.

The above relation shall be employed to obtain precise multilinear estimates via the nonlinear Loomis–Whitney inequality and bilinear Strichartz estimates.

Assumption

4.1.1
(2) with normals given by (
24
). Let
\sigma=(\omega_{\alpha}(\xi,\eta),\xi,\eta)
and
\tilde{\sigma}=(\omega_{\alpha}(\tilde{\xi},\tilde{\eta}),\tilde{\xi},\tilde{\eta})
. It follows

\frac{|\mathbf{n}_{i}(\sigma)-\mathbf{n}_{i}(\tilde{\sigma})|}{|\sigma-\tilde{\sigma}|}\leq\frac{|\nabla\omega_{\alpha}(\xi,\eta)-\nabla\omega_{\alpha}(\tilde{\xi},\tilde{\eta})|}{|(\xi-\tilde{\xi},\eta-\tilde{\eta})|}\leq|\partial^{2}\omega_{\alpha}(\xi^{*},\eta^{*})|

by the mean-value theorem. Since
\omega_{\alpha}\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash(\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}))
, we have

|\partial^{2}\omega_{\alpha}(\xi^{*},\eta^{*})|\lesssim_{N_{1},N_{2}}1

for
|(\xi^{*},\eta^{*})|\lesssim 1
and
|\xi^{*}|\gtrsim N_{2}/N_{1}
.

We turn to the second size condition, which is reduced likewise:

\begin{split}\frac{|\mathbf{n}_{i}(\sigma)(\sigma-\tilde{\sigma})|}{|\sigma-\tilde{\sigma}|^{1+\beta}}&\leq\frac{|\omega_{\alpha}(\xi,\eta)-\omega_{\alpha}(\tilde{\xi},\tilde{\eta})-\partial_{\xi}\omega_{\alpha}(\xi,\eta)(\xi-\tilde{\xi})-\partial_{\eta}\omega_{\alpha}(\xi,\eta)(\eta-\tilde{\eta})|}{|(\xi-\tilde{\xi},\eta-\tilde{\eta})|^{2}}\\
&\lesssim|\partial^{2}\omega_{\alpha}(\xi^{*},\eta^{*})|\lesssim_{N_{1},N_{2}}1\end{split}

by the mean value theorem. This verifies the Hölder condition.

We continue with the estimate of (
26
). An application of the nonlinear Loomis-Whitney inequality provided by Theorem
4.1
with
\varepsilon=N_{1}^{-(\alpha+1)}
and
A\sim N_{1}/N_{2}
yields

\begin{split}\big|\int(f^{\prime}_{1,N_{1},L_{1}}*f^{\prime}_{2,N_{2},L_{2}})f^{\prime}_{3,N_{3},L_{3}}d\xi^{\prime}d\eta^{\prime}d\tau^{\prime}\big|&\lesssim(N_{2}/N_{1})^{-\frac{1}{2}}N_{1}^{-\frac{3(\alpha+1)}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{3}\|f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\lesssim(N_{2}/N_{1})^{-\frac{1}{2}}N_{1}^{-\frac{3(\alpha+1)}{2}}N_{1}^{-\frac{9}{2}-\frac{9\alpha}{4}}\prod_{i=1}^{3}\|f_{i,N_{i},L_{i}}\|_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

Taking into account the scaling factor from (
26
), we finish the proof.

∎

4.2.
Bilinear Strichartz estimates

We employ transversality in the resonant case to derive bilinear estimates. We first note a trivial result.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
I,J
be intervals and
f:J\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a smooth function. Then,

|\{x:f(x)\in I\}|\leqslant\frac{|I|}{\inf_{y}|f^{\prime}(y)|}.

Proof.

The estimate is a consequence of the mean value theorem. Let
x_{1},x_{2}\in J
be such that
f(x_{1}),f(x_{2})\in I
. Then, for
\xi\in(x_{1},x_{2})
,

|x_{1}-x_{2}|=\frac{|f(x_{1})-f(x_{2})|}{|f^{\prime}(\xi)|}\leqslant\frac{|I|}{\inf_{y}|f^{\prime}(y)|}.

∎

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\alpha>0
, suppose that
u,v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})
have their Fourier supports in
\tilde{D}_{N_{1},L_{1}}
and
\tilde{D}_{N_{2},L_{2}}
, respectively, and that for
(\tau_{1},\xi_{1},\eta_{1})\in\text{supp}(\hat{u})
and
(\tau_{2},\xi_{2},\eta_{2})

\in\text{supp}(\hat{v})
, the resonance condition holds. Then,

(28)

\|uv\|_{L^{2}_{t,x,y}}\lesssim(L_{1}L_{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\min(N_{1},N_{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\max(N_{1},N_{2})^{\frac{\alpha}{4}}}\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|v\|_{L^{2}}.

Proof.

Let
\bar{L}=\max(L_{1},L_{2})
,
\underline{L}=\min(L_{1},L_{2})
. Using Plancherel’s identity and Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we have

(29)

\begin{split}\|uv\|_{L^{2}}&=\Big\|\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}\hat{u}(\tau_{1},\xi_{1},\eta_{1})\hat{v}(\tau-\tau_{1},\xi-\xi_{1},\eta-\eta_{1})d\tau_{1}d\xi_{1}d\eta_{1}\Big\|_{L^{2}_{\tau,\xi,\eta}}\\
&\lesssim\underline{L}^{\frac{1}{2}}|E(\xi,\eta)|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|v\|_{L^{2}},\end{split}

where the set
E
is given by

\begin{split}E(\xi,\eta):=\{(\xi_{1},\eta_{1})\in\tilde{A}_{N_{1}}:&\;|\tau-\omega_{\alpha}(\xi_{1},\eta_{1})-\omega_{\alpha}(\xi-\xi_{1},\eta-\eta_{1})|\lesssim\bar{L},\\
&\quad(\xi-\xi_{1},\eta-\eta_{1})\in\tilde{A}_{N_{2}}\}.\end{split}

The measure of this set can be estimated by Fubini’s theorem.
From (
21
), Lemma
4.3
and almost orthogonality, we have

|E(\xi,\eta)|=\Big|\int d\xi_{1}\int d\eta_{1}\mathbf{1}_{E(\xi,\eta)}(\xi_{1},\eta_{1})\Big|\lesssim\min(N_{1},N_{2})\frac{\bar{L}}{\max(N_{1},N_{2})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}.

Substituting this in (
29
), we obtain

\|uv\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim(L_{1}L_{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\min(N_{1},N_{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\max(N_{1},N_{2})^{\frac{\alpha}{4}}}\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|v\|_{L^{2}}.

∎

Remark 4.5
.

The estimate (
28
) remains true if we replace the functions on the left-hand side of (
28
) by their complex conjugates.

The next lemma allows us to handle the non-resonant case when the smallest frequency has size
\lesssim 1
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\alpha>0
,
N_{1},N_{2},N_{3}\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}
be such that
N_{1}\ll N_{2}\sim N_{3}
, and
L_{1},L_{2},L_{3}\in 2^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
. If
f_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+},i=1,2,3
have their Fourier supports in
\tilde{D}_{N_{i},L_{i}}
, and
\max(L_{1},L_{2},L_{3})\gtrsim N_{1}N_{2}^{\alpha}
, we have

(30)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(f_{1}\ast f_{2})\cdot f_{3}\lesssim\frac{(L_{1}L_{2}L_{3})^{1/2}}{\max(L_{1},L_{2},L_{3})^{1/4}}N_{2}^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}N_{1}^{\frac{1}{4}}\|f_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\|f_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\|f_{3}\|_{L^{2}}.

Proof.

The proof is a generalization of the proof of
[
6
, Lemma 3.1]
to the case
\alpha>2
. We provide the details for the sake of completeness. Define

f_{i}^{\#}(\tau,\xi,\eta):=f_{i}(\tau+\omega_{\alpha}(\xi,\eta),\xi,\eta),i=1,2,3.

Then, for
i=1,2,3
,
\|f_{i}^{\#}\|_{L^{2}}=\|f_{i}\|_{L^{2}}
and
f_{i}^{\#}
are functions supported in
\{(\tau_{i},\xi_{i},\eta_{i}):|\tau_{i}|\sim L_{i},(\xi,\eta)\in A_{N_{i}}\}
. The left-hand side of (
30
) can be bounded by

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}f_{1}^{\#}(\tau_{1},\xi_{1},\eta_{1})f_{2}^{\#}(\tau_{2},\xi_{2},\eta_{2})\\
&\qquad\times f_{3}^{\#}(\tau_{1}+\tau_{2}-\Omega_{\alpha}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\eta_{1},\eta_{2}),\xi_{1}+\xi_{2},\eta_{1}+\eta_{2})\prod_{i=1}^{2}d\tau_{i}d\xi_{i}d\eta_{i}.\end{split}

By using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, it is sufficient to prove

(31)

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}g_{1}(\xi_{1},\eta_{1})&g_{2}(\xi_{2},\eta_{2})g(-\Omega_{\alpha}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\eta_{1},\eta_{2}),\xi_{1}+\xi_{2},\eta_{1}+\eta_{2})d\xi_{1}d\xi_{2}d\eta_{1}d\eta_{2}\\
&\lesssim L_{\max}^{\frac{1}{4}}N_{2}^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}N_{1}^{\frac{1}{4}}\|g_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\|g_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\|g\|_{L^{2}},\end{split}

where
g_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
are
L^{2}
functions supported in
\tilde{A}_{N_{i}}
,
i=1,2
and
g:\mathbb{R}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
is an
L^{2}
function supported in
[-L_{\max},L_{\max}]\times\tilde{A}_{N_{3}}
. After a change of variables,

\xi_{2}\to\xi_{2}-\xi_{1},\quad\eta_{2}\to\eta_{2}-\eta_{1},

and using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we find that the left-hand side of (
31
) is dominated by

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}g_{1}(\xi_{1},\eta_{1})g_{2}(\xi_{2}-\xi_{1},\eta_{2}-\eta_{1})g(-\Omega_{\alpha}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2}-\xi_{1},\eta_{1},\eta_{2}-\eta_{1}),\xi_{2},\eta_{2})\prod_{i=1}^{2}d\xi_{i}d\eta_{i}\\
&\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|g_{1}(\xi_{1},\eta_{1})g_{2}(\xi_{2}-\xi_{1},\eta_{2}-\eta_{1})|^{2}d\xi_{1}d\eta_{1}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\quad\times\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|g(-\Omega_{\alpha}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2}-\xi_{1},\eta_{1},\eta_{2}-\eta_{1}),\xi_{2},\eta_{2})|^{2}d\xi_{1}d\eta_{1}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}d\xi_{2}d\eta_{2}.\end{split}

Define

\begin{split}\beta_{1}(\eta_{1})&=-\Omega_{\alpha}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2}-\xi_{1},\eta_{1},\eta_{2}-\eta_{1})\\
&=|\xi_{2}-\xi_{1}|^{\alpha}(\xi_{2}-\xi_{1})-|\xi_{2}|^{\alpha}\xi_{2}+|\xi_{1}|^{\alpha}\xi_{1}+\frac{(\eta_{1}\xi_{2}-\eta_{2}\xi_{1})^{2}}{\xi_{1}\xi_{2}(\xi_{2}-\xi_{1})},\\
\beta_{2}(\xi_{1})&=-(|\xi_{2}-\xi_{1}|^{\alpha}(\xi_{2}-\xi_{1})-|\xi_{2}|^{\alpha}\xi_{2}+|\xi_{1}|^{\alpha}\xi_{1}).\end{split}

We have
|\beta_{1}|\lesssim L_{\max}
,
|\beta_{2}|\lesssim L_{\max}
,
|\beta_{2}|\lesssim N_{2}^{\alpha}N_{1}
and using

\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}=\frac{(\eta_{1}\xi_{2}-\eta_{2}\xi_{1})^{2}}{\xi_{1}\xi_{2}(\xi_{2}-\xi_{1})},

we have

d\xi_{1}d\eta_{1}=\frac{\xi_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\xi_{2}-\xi_{1})^{\frac{1}{2}}}{2(\alpha+1)(\beta_{1}+\beta_{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}[(\xi_{2}-\xi_{1})^{\alpha}-\xi_{1}^{\alpha}]}d\beta_{1}d\beta_{2}.

Using
|\xi_{2}|\sim|\xi_{2}-\xi_{1}|
and Fubini, we get

\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|g(\Omega_{\alpha}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2}-\xi_{1},\eta_{1},\eta_{2}-\eta_{1}),\xi_{2},\eta_{2})|^{2}d\xi_{1}d\eta_{1}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\frac{N_{1}^{\frac{1}{4}}L_{\max}^{\frac{1}{4}}}{N_{2}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}\|g(\cdot,\xi_{2},\eta_{2})\|_{L^{2}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

5.
Quasilinear well-posedness

This section is devoted to the proof of the theorem below, which yields Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\alpha\in(2,\frac{5}{2}]
,
u_{0}\in H^{\infty,0}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, and
s>5-2\alpha
. Then, there exists continuous
T=T(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s,0}(\mathbb{R}^{2})})>0
such that there is a unique solution

(32)

u=S_{T}^{\infty}(u_{0})\in C([-T,T];H^{\infty,0}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))

of (
1
). In addition for
s^{\prime}\geqslant s

\sup_{|t|\leqslant T}\|S^{\infty}_{T}(u_{0})(t)\|_{H^{s^{\prime},0}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\lesssim C(T,s^{\prime},\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s^{\prime},0}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}).

Moreover, the mapping given by (
32
)
extends uniquely to a continuous mapping

S_{T}^{s^{\prime}}:H^{s^{\prime},0}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\to C([-T,T];H^{s^{\prime},0}(\mathbb{R}^{2})).

Existence of local-in-time solutions for initial data in
H^{2,0}
to the KP-I equation was proved by Molinet–Saut–Tzvetkov
[
16
]
. The proof is a non-trivial variant of the energy method, which relies on commutator estimates. Also, persistence of regularity is discussed in
[
16
]
. These arguments transpire to the fKP-I case and show the existence of a mapping
S_{T}^{\infty}
.

5.1.
Short-time bilinear estimates

In this subsection, we prove short-time bilinear estimates which we need to control the nonlinearity.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
2<\alpha\leqslant\frac{5}{2}
,
T\in(0,1]
. There is
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(\alpha)
such that for the time localization
T(N)=N^{-(5-2\alpha)-\varepsilon}
, such that for
s^{\prime}\geqslant 0
, and
u,v\in F^{s^{\prime},0}(T)

(33)

\|\partial_{x}(uv)\|_{\mathcal{N}^{s^{\prime},0}(T)}\lesssim\|u\|_{F^{0,0}(T)}\|v\|_{F^{s^{\prime},0}(T)}+\|v\|_{F^{0,0}(T)}\|u\|_{F^{s^{\prime},0}(T)}.

Remark 5.3
.

As a particular case of the above proposition, we obtain

\|\partial_{x}(uv)\|_{\mathcal{N}^{0,0}(T)}\lesssim\|u\|_{F^{0,0}(T)}\|v\|_{F^{0,0}(T)}.

Proposition
5.2
will be proved by means of dyadic estimates which we prove in the following. We first consider the

interaction. In this case, we can choose the time localization

for any
\varepsilon>0
to prove a favorable estimate.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and the time localization be given by
T(N)=N^{-(5-2\alpha)-\varepsilon}

Let
N_{1},N_{2},N\in 2^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}

N_{2}\ll N_{1}\sim N

u_{N_{1}}\in F_{N_{1}}

v_{N_{2}}\in F_{N_{2}}

\|P_{N}(\partial_{x}(u_{N_{1}}v_{N_{2}}))\|_{\mathcal{N}_{N}}\lesssim N_{1}^{-c(\varepsilon)}\|u_{N_{1}}\|_{F_{N_{1}}}\|v_{N_{2}}\|_{F_{N_{2}}}.

Proof.

Using the definition of the
\mathcal{N}_{N}
norm, we can bound the left-hand side of (
34
) by

\begin{split}&\;\sup_{t_{N}\in\mathbb{R}}\|(\tau-\omega_{\alpha}(\xi,\eta)+iN^{(5-2\alpha)+\varepsilon})^{-1}N\mathbf{1}_{A_{N}}(\xi)\mathcal{F}[u_{N_{1}}\cdot\eta_{0}(N^{(5-2\alpha)+\varepsilon}(t-t_{N}))]\\
&\quad\ast\mathcal{F}[v_{N_{2}}\cdot\eta_{0}(N^{(5-2\alpha)+\varepsilon}(t-t_{N}))]\|_{X_{N}}\end{split}

Let

f_{N_{1}}:=\mathcal{F}[u_{N_{1}}\cdot\eta_{0}(N^{(5-2\alpha)+\varepsilon}(t-t_{N}))]\quad\text{ and }\quad g_{N_{2}}:=\mathcal{F}[v_{N_{2}}\cdot\eta_{0}(N^{(5-2\alpha)+\varepsilon}(t-t_{N}))].

Using the properties (
9
) and (
10
), it suffices to prove that if
L_{1},L_{2}\geqslant N^{(5-2\alpha)+\varepsilon}
and

f_{N_{1},L_{1}},g_{N_{2},L_{2}}:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}

are functions supported in
D_{N_{1},L_{1}}
and
D_{N_{2},L_{2}}
and for
L_{i}=N^{(5-2\alpha)+\varepsilon}
in
D_{N_{i},\leqslant L_{i}}
, respectively, then

(35)

N\sum_{L\geqslant N^{(5-2\alpha)+\varepsilon}}L^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\mathbf{1}_{D_{N,\leqslant L}}(f_{N_{1},L_{1}}\ast g_{N_{2},L_{2}})\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim N_{1}^{-c(\varepsilon)}L_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f_{N_{1},L_{1}}\|_{L^{2}}~L_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|g_{N_{2},L_{2}}\|_{L^{2}}.

We also note that by duality, it suffices to prove:

(36)

\Big|\int(f_{N_{1},L_{1}}\ast g_{N_{2},L_{2}})\cdot h_{N,L}\Big|\lesssim N_{1}^{-1-c(\varepsilon)}L_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f_{N_{1},L_{1}}\|_{L^{2}}~L_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|g_{N_{2},L_{2}}\|_{L^{2}}~L^{\frac{1}{2}-}\|h_{N,L}\|_{L^{2}},

where
h_{N,L}
is supported in
D_{N,L}
.

Let
L_{\max}=\max(L_{1},L_{2},L)
. In case
N_{2}=1
, we make an additional dyadic decomposition in the low frequencies. Now we abuse notation, and let

Lemma 5.5
.

For any
\alpha\in(2,\frac{5}{2}]

\varepsilon(\alpha)>0

T=T(N)=N^{-(5-2\alpha)-\varepsilon}

N_{1},N_{2},N\in 2^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}

N_{1}\geqslant 2^{10}

u_{N_{1}}\in F_{N_{1}},v_{N_{2}}\in F_{N_{2}}

(37)

\|P_{N}(\partial_{x}(u_{N_{1}}v_{N_{2}}))\|_{\mathcal{N}_{N}}\lesssim N_{1}^{-c(\varepsilon)}\|u_{N_{1}}\|_{F_{N_{1}}}\|v_{N_{2}}\|_{F_{N_{2}}}.

Proof.

Let
\gamma:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[0,1]
be a smooth function supported in
[-1,1]
such that

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\gamma^{2}(t-n)\equiv 1,\quad t\in\mathbb{R}.

We need to further localize the nonlinearity to intervals of size
N_{1}^{(2\alpha-5)-}
. Moreover, we carry out an additional decomposition in case
N=1
into very low frequencies. By abuse of notation, let now
N\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}
and
N_{+}=\max(N,1)
. Using the definition of the
\mathcal{N}_{N}
norm, the left-hand side of (
37

,
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(\alpha,\varepsilon^{\prime})
, and

s\geqslant\begin{cases}6-\frac{11\alpha}{4}+\varepsilon^{\prime},&\quad\alpha\in(2,\frac{24}{11}],\\
0,&\quad\alpha\in(\frac{24}{11},\frac{5}{2}],\end{cases}

and
u_{0}\in H^{\infty,0}
. We can suppose by rescaling and subcriticality that
\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s,0}}\leqslant\varepsilon_{0}\ll 1
with
\varepsilon_{0}
determined later. By the local well-posedness in
H^{2,0}
, we have existence of solutions in
H^{2,0}
for
T_{\max}=T_{\max}(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2,0}})
.

By Lemma
2.2
, Proposition
5.2
, and Proposition
5.8
, we have the following set of estimates for
T\leqslant\min(T_{\max},1)
and time localization
T(N)=N^{-(5-2\alpha)-\varepsilon}
provided that
\varepsilon
is chosen small enough:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\|u\|_{F^{s,0}(T)}&\lesssim\|u\|_{E^{s,0}(T)}+\|\partial_{x}(u^{2})\|_{\mathcal{N}^{s,0}(T)},\\
\|\partial_{x}(u^{2})\|_{\mathcal{N}^{s,0}(T)}&\lesssim\|u\|^{2}_{F^{s,0}(T)},\\
\|u\|^{2}_{E^{s,0}(T)}&\lesssim\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{H^{s,0}}+\|u\|^{3}_{F^{s,0}(T)}.\end{array}\right.

This yields

(64)

\|u\|^{2}_{F^{s,0}(T)}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{H^{s,0}}+\|u\|^{4}_{F^{s,0}(T)}+\|u\|^{3}_{F^{s,0}(T)}.

Secondly, we have (cf.
[
9
, Lemma 4.2, p. 279]
)

\lim_{T\downarrow 0}\|u\|_{E^{s,0}(T)}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s,0}},\quad\lim_{T\downarrow 0}\|\partial_{x}(u^{2})\|_{\mathcal{N}^{s,0}(T)}=0.

Hence, by choosing
\varepsilon_{0}
small enough, we find by (
64
) and a continuity argument that

(65)

\|u\|_{F^{s,0}(T)}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s,0}}

for
T=\min(1,T_{\max})
. Another application of Lemma
2.2
and Propositions
5.2
and
5.8
yields

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\|u\|_{F^{2,0}(T)}&\lesssim\|u\|_{E^{2,0}(T)}+\|\partial_{x}(u^{2})\|_{\mathcal{N}^{2,0}(T)},\\
\|\partial_{x}(u^{2})\|_{\mathcal{N}^{2,0}(T)}&\lesssim\|u\|_{F^{2,0}(T)}\|u\|_{F^{s,0}(T)},\\
\|u\|^{2}_{E^{2,0}(T)}&\lesssim\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{H^{2,0}}+\|u\|^{2}_{F^{2,0}(T)}\|u\|_{F^{s,0}(T)}.\end{array}\right.

This set of estimates yields

\|u\|_{F^{2,0}(T)}^{2}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2,0}}^{2}+\|u\|_{F^{s,0}(T)}\|u\|_{F^{2,0}(T)}^{2}+\|u\|^{2}_{F^{2,0}(T)}\|u\|_{F^{s,0}(T)}^{2},

and therefore, for
\|u\|_{F^{s,0}(T)}\lesssim\varepsilon_{0}
we have
\|u\|_{F^{2,0}(T)}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{2,0}}
. Consequently, we have existence up to
T=1
choosing
\varepsilon_{0}
sufficiently small only depending on
\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s,0}}\leq\varepsilon_{0}
.

Since
s=0
for
\alpha>\frac{24}{11}
, by the above a priori estimates and the conservation of mass (
2
), we can show
global existence
of solutions.

Theorem 5.11

(Global existence for smooth solutions)
.

Let
\alpha\in(\frac{24}{11},\frac{5}{2}]
, and
u_{0}\in H^{\infty,0}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. For any
s\geqslant 0
we have a solution
u\in C(\mathbb{R};H^{s,0})
to (
1
), and there exist
C_{1}(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}),C_{2}(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}})>0
such that

(66)

\|u(t)\|_{H^{s,0}}\leq C_{1}e^{C_{2}|t|}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s,0}}.

Proof.

It is enough to consider
t>0
by time-reversal. Firstly, we can rescale the initial data
u_{0}
to
u_{0\lambda}
, which satisfies
\|u_{0\lambda}\|_{L^{2}}=\varepsilon\ll 1
. By the local well-posedness result in
H^{2,0}
due to Molinet–Saut–Tzvetkov
[
16
]
we have for the corresponding solution
u_{\lambda}\in C([0,T],H^{s,0})
with
T=T(\|u_{0\lambda}\|_{H^{2,0}})
. Let
T^{\prime}\leq 1\wedge T
. We have the following set of estimates:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\|u_{\lambda}\|_{F^{0,0}(T^{\prime})}&\lesssim\|u_{\lambda}\|_{E^{0,0}(T^{\prime})}+\|\partial_{x}(u_{\lambda})^{2}\|_{\mathcal{N}^{0,0}(T^{\prime})},\\
\|\partial_{x}(u_{\lambda})^{2}\|_{\mathcal{N}^{0,0}(T^{\prime})}&\lesssim\|u_{\lambda}\|^{2}_{F^{0,0}(T^{\prime})},\\
\|u_{\lambda}\|_{E^{0,0}(T^{\prime})}^{2}&\lesssim\|u_{0\lambda}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|u_{\lambda}\|^{3}_{F^{0,0}(T^{\prime})}.\end{array}\right.

By
\|u_{0\lambda}\|_{L^{2}}\ll 1
, a continuity argument like above gives for
T^{\prime}\leq 1\wedge T
:

(67)

\|u_{\lambda}\|_{F^{0,0}(T^{\prime})}\lesssim\|u_{0\lambda}\|_{L^{2}}.

Secondly, we have

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\|u_{\lambda}\|_{F^{s,0}(T^{\prime})}&\lesssim\|u_{\lambda}\|_{E^{s,0}(T^{\prime})}+\|\partial_{x}(u_{\lambda}^{2})\|_{\mathcal{N}^{s,0}(T^{\prime})},\\
\|\partial_{x}(u_{\lambda}^{2})\|_{\mathcal{N}^{s,0}(T^{\prime})}&\lesssim\|u_{\lambda}\|_{F^{s,0}(T^{\prime})}\|u_{\lambda}\|_{F^{0,0}(T^{\prime})},\\
\|u_{\lambda}\|^{2}_{E^{s,0}(T^{\prime})}&\lesssim\|u_{0\lambda}\|^{2}_{H^{s,0}}+\|u_{\lambda}\|^{2}_{F^{s,0}(T^{\prime})}\|u\|_{F^{0,0}(T^{\prime})}.\end{array}\right.

By (
67
) we obtain

(68)

\|u_{\lambda}\|_{F^{s,0}(T^{\prime})}\lesssim\|u_{0\lambda}\|_{H^{s,0}}.

Consequently,
\|u_{\lambda}(t)\|_{H^{2,0}}
remains bounded for
t\leq 1\wedge T
and by the local well-posedness result
u_{\lambda}
exists until
t=1
: We have
u_{\lambda}\in C([0,1],H^{s,0})
with
\|u_{\lambda}(1)\|_{H^{s,0}}\lesssim\|u_{0\lambda}\|_{H^{s,0}}
. However,
\|u_{0\lambda}\|_{L^{2}}=\|u_{0\lambda}\|_{L^{2}}=\varepsilon\ll 1
. For this reason, the argument can be iterated and we find
u_{\lambda}\in C(\mathbb{R};H^{s,0})
with

\|u_{\lambda}(t)\|_{H^{s,0}}\leq C_{1}e^{C_{2}t}\|u_{0\lambda}\|_{H^{s,0}},

which follows from iterating (
68
). Hence,

\|u(t)\|_{H^{s,0}}\leq C_{1}(\lambda)e^{C_{2}(\lambda)t}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s,0}}

with
\lambda=\lambda(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}})
. The proof is complete.
∎

Now we prove the continuity of the data-to-solution map. In the first step, we show Lipschitz continuous dependence of the solutions in
L^{2}
for small initial data of higher regularity.

Lipschitz continuous dependence in
L^{2}
:

Let
s>5-2\alpha
and
u_{1},u_{2}
denote two local-in-time solutions with initial data

.
By the above argument, we have for

\|u\|_{F^{s^{\prime},0}(1)}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s^{\prime},0}}.

Let

denote the solution to the difference equation

, Proposition

, Proposition

, we have

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\|v\|_{F^{0,0}(1)}&\lesssim\|v\|_{E^{0,0}(1)}+\|\partial_{x}(v(u_{1}+u_{2}))\|_{\mathcal{N}^{0,0}(1)},\\

This enables us to conclude

(71)

\|v\|_{F^{0,0}(1)}\lesssim\|v(0)\|_{L^{2}},

since
\|u_{i}\|_{F^{s,0}(1)}\lesssim\varepsilon_{0}
are chosen sufficiently small.

Continuity of the data-to-solution mapping:

Also, from Lemma
2.2
, Proposition
5.2
, and Proposition
5.10
, we have

(72)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\|v\|_{F^{s,0}(T)}&\lesssim\|v\|_{E^{s,0}(T)}+\|\partial_{x}(v(u_{1}+u_{2}))\|_{\mathcal{N}^{s,0}(T)}\\

From the above set of estimates, we can conclude a priori estimates for
\|v\|_{F^{s,0}(T)}
:

(73)

\begin{split}\|v\|_{F^{s,0}(T)}^{2}&\lesssim\|v(0)\|_{H^{s,0}}^{2}+\|v\|_{F^{s,0}(T)}^{3}+\|v\|_{F^{0,0}(T)}\|v\|_{F^{s,0}(T)}\|u_{2}\|_{F^{2s,0}(T)}.\end{split}

We use the smallness of
\|u_{i}\|_{H^{s,0}}
to absorb the term from the nonlinear estimate into the left-hand side.

For
s>5-2\alpha
, let
\phi\in H^{s,0}
be fixed and

be such that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\phi_{n}=\phi.

By rescaling and subcriticality, we can again assume that
\|\phi\|_{H^{s,0}}\leqslant\varepsilon_{0}\ll 1
and
\|\phi_{n}\|_{H^{s,0}}\leqslant 2\varepsilon_{0}\ll 1\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let
u_{1}
be the solution corresponding to initial data
\phi_{n}
, and
u_{2}
be the solution corresponding to initial data
P_{\leqslant N}\phi_{n}
. We construct the data-to-solution mapping as an extension of the data-to-solution mapping for smooth initial data. Let

S_{T}^{\infty}(\phi_{n})\in C([-1,1];H^{\infty,0})

denote the solution corresponding to smooth initial data. We can take the existence time as
1
by the a priori estimates and persistence property argued above.

To prove the continuity of the data-to-solution map, we need to show that the sequence
S_{T}^{\infty}(\phi_{n})\in C([-1,1];H^{\infty,0})
is a Cauchy sequence in the space
C([-1,1];H^{s,0})
,
s>5-2\alpha
.

Hence, it suffices to show that for any
\delta>0
, there exists

such that

\|S_{T}^{\infty}(\phi_{n})-S_{T}^{\infty}(\phi_{m})\|_{C([-1,1];H^{s,0})}\leqslant\delta~~\text{for all }m,n\geqslant M_{\delta}.

For

, let
\phi_{n}^{K}:=P_{\leqslant K}\phi_{n}
.
We have

(75)

\begin{split}\|S_{T}^{\infty}(\phi_{n})-S_{T}^{\infty}(\phi_{m})\|_{C([-1,1];H^{s,0})}&\leqslant\|S_{T}^{\infty}(\phi_{n})-S_{T}^{\infty}(\phi_{n}^{K})\|_{C([-1,1];H^{s,0})}\\
&\quad+\|S_{T}^{\infty}(\phi_{m})-S_{T}^{\infty}(\phi_{m}^{K})\|_{C([-1,1];H^{s,0})}\\
&\quad+\|S_{T}^{\infty}(\phi_{n}^{K})-S_{T}^{\infty}(\phi_{m}^{K})\|_{C([-1,1];H^{s,0})}.\end{split}

The third term can be handled by using the continuity of the data-to-solution map for smooth data in

:

(76)

\|S_{T}^{\infty}(\phi_{n}^{K})-S_{T}^{\infty}(\phi_{m}^{K})\|_{C([-1,1];H^{s,0})}\leqslant\|S_{T}^{\infty}(\phi_{n}^{K})-S_{T}^{\infty}(\phi_{m}^{K})\|_{H^{2,0}}\to 0

for

because
\|\phi_{m}^{K}-\phi_{n}^{K}\|_{H^{2,0}}\to 0
.
Let
v=S_{T}^{\infty}(\phi_{n})-S_{T}^{\infty}(\phi_{n}^{K})
. We observe that
v
is the solution corresponding to initial data
P_{>K}\phi_{n}
. From (
71
), we have

\|v\|_{F^{0,0}(T)}\lesssim\|\phi_{n}-\phi_{n}^{K}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim K^{-s}\|P_{>K}\phi_{n}\|_{H^{s,0}}.

From (
69
), we have for

:

(77)

\|u_{2}\|_{F^{2s,0}(T)}\lesssim\|\phi_{n}^{K}\|_{H^{2s,0}}\lesssim K^{s}\|\phi_{n}\|_{H^{s,0}}.

Combining the above with (

), we conclude an a priori estimate for

which now depends on the profile of the initial data, namely on
P_{>K}\phi_{n}
. We have

\begin{split}\|S_{T}^{\infty}(\phi_{n})-S_{T}^{\infty}(\phi_{m})\|_{C([-1,1];H^{s,0})}\lesssim\|P_{>K}\phi_{n}\|_{H^{s,0}}+\|P_{>K}\phi_{m}\|_{H^{s,0}}+C(m,n,K).\end{split}

By the convergence of
\phi_{n}
and choosing

large enough so that

\|P_{>K}\phi_{n}\|_{H^{s,0}}+\|P_{>K}\phi_{m}\|_{H^{s,0}}<\varepsilon,

we conclude that

is a Cauchy sequence in

. This shows that

extends to a continuous map
S_{T}:H^{s,0}\to C([-1,1];H^{s,0})
.

6.
Semilinear well-posedness

For
\alpha>\frac{5}{2}
, we observe via estimates (
23
) and (
28
) that we can remedy the derivative loss completely without having to use frequency-dependent time localization. We show local well-posedness through a fixed point argument. This we carry out within the standard Fourier restriction spaces as our auxiliary spaces.
Let
s,b\in\mathbb{R}
and
\omega_{\alpha}(\xi,\eta)=|\xi|^{\alpha}\xi+\frac{\eta^{2}}{\xi}
. The space
X^{s,b}
corresponding to the fractional KP-I equation (
1
) is defined as the closure of Schwartz functions with respect to the norm

\|u\|_{X^{s,b}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}:=\|\langle\xi\rangle^{s}\langle\tau-\omega_{\alpha}(\xi,\eta)\rangle^{b}\hat{u}(\tau,\xi)\|_{L_{\tau,\xi,\eta}^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}=\|U_{\alpha}(-t)u\|_{H^{b}_{t}H^{s}_{x,y}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})},

where
U_{\alpha}(t)
denotes the solution operator corresponding to the linear equation. We localize in time as usual by setting

X_{T}^{s,b}=\{f:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}\,|\;\exists\,\tilde{f}\in X^{s,b}:\tilde{f}\big|_{[0,T]}=f\}

endowed with norm

\|f\|_{X_{T}^{s,b}}=\inf_{\tilde{f}\big|_{[0,T]}=f}\|\tilde{f}\|_{X^{s,b}}.

With the function spaces introduced, we give a precise version of Theorem
1.3
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
\alpha>\frac{5}{2}
and
s>\frac{5}{4}-\frac{\alpha}{2}
. Then, there is
b>1/2
such that for
T=T(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s,0}})
, (
1
) is analytically locally well-posed in
H^{s,0}
with the solution lying in
X_{T}^{s,b}\hookrightarrow C([0,T];H^{s,0})
.

The section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
6.1
. We begin with a reminder on the basic properties of

spaces, which show that for the proof of the theorem, it suffices to show the bilinear estimate

\|\partial_{x}(uv)\|_{X^{s,b-1}}\lesssim\|u\|_{X^{s,b}}\|v\|_{X^{s,b}}

for some
b>1/2
. The bilinear estimate is proved in Subsection
6.2
.

Some remarks on traces on the infinite-dimensional Iwahori–Hecke algebra
Yury A. Neretin

Abstract




The infinite-dimensional Iwahori–Hecke algebras
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}(q)
are direct
limits of the usual finite-dimensional Iwahori–Hecke algebras.
They arise in a natural way as convolution
algebras of bi-invariant functions on groups
\mathrm{GLB}(\mathbb{F}_{q})

of infinite-dimensional
matrices over finite-fields having only finite number of non-zero matrix
elements under the diagonal. In 1988 Vershik and Kerov classified all
indecomposable positive traces on
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}(q)
. Any such trace
generates a representation of the double
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}(q)\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\infty}(q)

and of the double
\mathrm{GLB}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\times\mathrm{GLB}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
.
We present constructions of such representations; the traces
are some distinguished matrix elements. We also obtain some (simple)
general statements on relations between unitary representations of groups
and representations of convolution algebras of measures bi-invariant with respect to compact subgroups.

1.1. Algebras of bi-invariant measures.

1.4. Traces on

A

3.2. Proof of Proposition
3.1
.

We use notation
I:=(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})
,
J:=(j_{1},\dots,j_{n})
.
For
\sigma\in S_{n}
denote by
\Omega(\sigma)
the set of all
I
invariant with respect
to
\sigma
. In other words we decompose
\sigma
into a product of independent cycles,
and map
k\mapsto i_{k}
is constant on cycles.

Lemma 3.2

\bigl\langle T^{V}(\sigma)D^{V}(\Phi)\,\Xi,\Xi\bigr\rangle=\sum_{I=(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})\in\Omega(\sigma)}\Bigl(\prod_{k=1}^{n}a_{i_{k}}\cdot\Phi(I)\Bigr)=\\
=\Bigl\langle D^{V}(\Phi)T^{V}(\sigma)\,\Xi,\Xi\Bigr\rangle.

(3.4)

Proof.

We have

\Bigl\langle T^{V}(\sigma)D^{V}(\Phi)\Xi,\Xi\Bigr\rangle=\sum_{I}\sum_{J}\,\boxed{\Phi(i_{\sigma^{-1}(1)},\dots,i_{\sigma^{-1}(n)})}\,\prod_{k=1}^{n}a_{i_{\sigma^{-1}(k)}}^{1/2}\prod_{k=1}^{n}a_{j_{k}}^{1/2}\times\\
\times\biggl\langle\eta\begin{bmatrix}i_{\sigma^{-1}(1)}&\dots&i_{\sigma^{-1}(n)}\\
i_{1}&\dots&i_{n}\end{bmatrix},\eta\begin{bmatrix}j_{1}&\dots&j_{n}\\
j_{1}&\dots&j_{n}\end{bmatrix}\biggr\rangle.

(3.5)

A summand can be non-zero only if two basis elements in the brackets
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle

coincide. So
i_{k}=j_{k}
,
i_{\sigma^{-1}k}=j_{k}
. Therefore
\sum_{I}\sum_{J}

transforms to
\sum_{I\in\Omega(\sigma)}
.
Since
I\in\Omega(\sigma)
, we can replace
\Phi(\cdot)
by
\Phi(I)
.
Since
J=I
, the product
\prod_{k}(\dots)\prod_{k}(\dots)
replaces by

\prod_{k}a_{i_{k}}
.

The expression for
\bigl\langle D^{V}(\Phi)T^{V}(\sigma)\,\Xi,\Xi\bigr\rangle

is similar to (
3.5
), we only must replace the boxed

\Phi(\dots)
by
\Phi(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})
.

\square

Lemma 3.3

\Bigl\langle\bigl(T^{V}(\sigma)D^{V}(\Phi)\bigr)\,D^{V}(\Theta)\Xi,\,\Xi\Bigr\rangle=\Bigl\langle D^{V}(\Theta)\,\bigl(T^{V}(\sigma)D^{V}(\Phi)\bigr)\Xi,\,\Xi\Bigr\rangle.

(3.6)

Proof.

To obtain the left hand side of (
3.6
), we must replace the boxed
\Phi(\dots)

in the calculation (
3.5
) by

\Phi(i_{\sigma^{-1}(1)},\dots,i_{\sigma^{-1}(n)})\,\Theta(i_{\sigma^{-1}(1)},\dots,i_{\sigma^{-1}(n)}),

in the right hand side by

\Theta(i_{1},\dots,i_{n})\,\Phi(i_{\sigma^{-1}(1)},\dots,i_{\sigma^{-1}(n)}).

Since
I\in\Omega(\sigma)
, we will get the same result in both sides.

\square

Lemma 3.4

\Bigl\langle T^{V}(\lambda^{-1})\,T^{V}(\sigma)D^{V}(\Phi)\,T^{V}(\lambda)\,\Xi,\,\Xi\Bigr\rangle=\Bigl\langle T^{V}(\sigma)D^{V}(\Phi)\,\Xi,\,\Xi\Bigr\rangle.

(3.7)

Proof.
The left-hand side equals to

\sum_{I}\sum_{J}\Phi(i_{\sigma^{-1}\lambda(1)},\dots,i_{\sigma^{-1}\lambda(n)})\prod_{k=1}^{n}a_{i_{\lambda^{-1}\sigma^{-1}\lambda(k)}}\prod_{k=1}^{n}a_{j_{k}}\times\\
\times\times\\
\times\biggl\langle\eta\begin{bmatrix}i_{\lambda^{-1}\sigma^{-1}\lambda(1)}&\dots&i_{\lambda^{-1}\sigma^{-1}\lambda(n)}\\
i_{1}&\dots&i_{n}\end{bmatrix},\eta\begin{bmatrix}j_{1}&\dots&j_{n}\\
j_{1}&\dots&j_{n}\end{bmatrix}\biggr\rangle.

As in proof of Lemma (
3.4
), we come to

\sum_{I\in\Omega(\lambda^{-1}\sigma^{-1}\lambda)}\Phi(i_{\sigma^{-1}\lambda(1)},\dots,i_{\sigma^{-1}\lambda(n)})\prod_{k=1}^{n}a_{i_{k}}.

(we applied Lemma
3.2
).
Next, we pass to a new index of summation

I^{\prime}:=(i_{\lambda(1)},\dots,i_{\lambda(n)}),\qquad I^{\prime}\in\Omega(\sigma).

Keeping in mind Lemma
3.2
we get the desired expression in the right hand side.

\square

The statement a) of Lemma
3.1
follows from
Lemmas
3.3
and
3.4
.

Proof of the statement b) of Proposition
3.1
.

We must verify the identity

T^{W}(\lambda)D^{W}(\Psi)\,\Xi=D^{V}(\Psi)T^{V}(\lambda^{-1})\,\Xi.

In the left hand-side we have

\sum_{I}\Psi(i_{\lambda^{-1}(1)},\dots,i_{\lambda^{-1}(1)})\prod a^{-1/2}_{j_{\lambda^{-1}k}}\,\eta\begin{bmatrix}i_{1}&\dots&i_{n}\\
i_{\lambda^{-1}(1)}&\dots&i_{\lambda^{-1}(n)}\end{bmatrix}.

We pass to a new index of summation

I^{\prime}:=(i_{\lambda^{-1}(1)},\dots,i_{\lambda^{-1}(n)}),

and get the right hand side.

\square

The statement c) of Proposition
3.1
follows from b).

3.3. The matrix element of
\zeta_{m}
.

Lemma 3.5

Let
\mathcal{H}_{n}
acts in
(V\otimes W)^{\otimes n}
as in Subsect.
2
.
Then

\bigl\langle\zeta_{m}\Xi,\Xi\rangle=\chi^{\alpha,\beta,0}(\zeta).

Lemma 3.6

Decompose the
R
-matrix (
1.10
) as

R:=Q+D,

where

\displaystyle Q:

\displaystyle=-\sqrt{q}\sum_{i\neq j,i\neq 0,j\neq 0}e_{ji}\otimes f_{ij};

\displaystyle D:

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i<0}e_{ii}\otimes f_{ii}+q\sum_{i>0}e_{ii}\otimes f_{ii}+(q-1)\sum_{i<j,i\neq 0,j\neq 0}e_{ij}\otimes f_{ij}.

Then

R_{(m-1)m}\dots R_{23}R_{12}\,\Xi=D_{(m-1)m}\dots D_{23}D_{12}\,\Xi.

Proof of Lemma
3.6
.

Denote

a_{i}:=\begin{cases}\beta_{-i},\quad\text{for $i<0$};\\
\alpha_{i},\quad\text{for $i>0$.}\end{cases}

The operators
D_{j(j+1)}
are
V
-diagonal. Next,

Q_{12}\Xi=-\sqrt{q}\sum_{I=(i_{1},\dots,i_{n}):i_{1}\neq i_{2}}\prod_{k}a_{i_{k}}^{1/2}\eta\begin{bmatrix}i_{2}&i_{1}&i_{3}&\dots&i_{n}\\
i_{1}&i_{2}&i_{3}&\dots&i_{n}\end{bmatrix}

The operators
R_{23}
,
R_{34}
, …can not change
i_{2}
in the first column of
\eta[\cdot]
,
recall also that such operators do not act on the second row of
\eta[\cdot]
.
Therefore all terms of
R_{(m-1)m}\dots R_{23}Q_{12}\,\Xi

have form

c_{s}\eta\begin{bmatrix}\alpha_{1}^{s}&\dots&\alpha_{n}^{s}\\
\beta_{1}^{s}&\dots&\beta_{n}^{s}\end{bmatrix},\qquad\text{where $\alpha_{1}^{s}\neq\beta_{1}^{s}$.}

Inner product of such a term with
\Xi
is 0, i.e.,

\bigl\langle R_{(m-1)m}\dots R_{23}Q_{12}\,\Xi,\Xi\bigr\rangle=0,

hence

\bigl\langle R_{(m-1)m}\dots R_{23}R_{12}\,\Xi,\Xi\bigr\rangle=\bigl\langle R_{(m-1)m}\dots R_{23}D_{12}\,\Xi,\Xi\bigr\rangle.

We repeat the same argument for
R_{23}
,
R_{34}
, etc.
and get the desired statement.

\square

The operator
D_{(m-1)m}\dots D_{23}D_{12}
is
V
-diagonal. Denote by
\delta[\dots]
its eigenvalues

D_{(m-1)m}\dots D_{23}D_{12}\,\eta\begin{bmatrix}i_{1}&\dots&i_{m}&\dots&i_{n}\\
j_{1}&\dots&j_{m}&\dots&j_{n}\end{bmatrix}=\\
=\delta(i_{1},\dots,i_{m})\,\eta\begin{bmatrix}i_{1}&\dots&i_{m}&\dots&i_{n}\\
j_{1}&\dots&j_{m}&\dots&j_{n}\end{bmatrix}.

Lemma 3.7

\langle\zeta_{m}\Xi,\Xi\rangle=\sum_{i_{1},\dots,i_{m}}\Bigl(\delta(i_{1},\dots,i_{m})\prod_{k=1}^{m}a_{i_{k}}\Bigr).

(3.8)

Proof.
A straightforward summation gives

\sum_{i_{1},\dots,i_{n}}\Bigl(\delta(i_{1},\dots,i_{m})\prod_{k=1}^{n}a_{i_{k}}\Bigr)

We transform this expression as

\sum_{i_{1},\dots,i_{m}}\sum_{i_{m+1},\dots,i_{n}}\Bigl(\delta(i_{1},\dots,i_{m})\prod_{k=1}^{m}a_{i_{k}}\prod_{k=m+1}^{n}a_{i_{k}}\Bigr)=\\
=\sum_{i_{1},\dots,i_{m}}\Bigl(\delta(i_{1},\dots,i_{m})\prod_{k=1}^{m}a_{i_{k}}\Bigr)\times\Bigl(\sum_{i}a_{i}\Bigr)^{m-n}.

But under our conditions
\sum a_{i}=\sum\beta_{p}+\sum\alpha_{q}=1
.

\square

Next,

is non-zero iff

Lemma 3.8

Let a tuple

(3.9)

\langle\zeta_{m}\Xi,\Xi\rangle=\frac{1}{q-1}\sum_{\text{$\begin{matrix}\varphi_{1}\geqslant 0,\varphi_{2}\geqslant 0,\dots,\\
\psi_{1}\geqslant 0,\psi_{2}\geqslant 0,\dots:\\
\sum\varphi_{i}+\sum\psi_{j}=m\end{matrix}$}}\,\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}\Bigl((-\beta_{i})^{\varphi_{i}}(1-q)^{\varepsilon(\varphi_{i})}\Bigr)\times\\
\times\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}\Bigl((q\alpha_{j})^{\psi_{l}}(1-q^{-1})^{\varepsilon(\psi_{i})}\Bigr),

(3.10)

where

\varepsilon(\theta):=\begin{cases}0,\qquad\text{if $\theta=0$;}\\
1,\qquad\text{if $\theta>0$.}\end{cases}

Proof.
We transform the expression (
3.9
) for
\delta(\dots)

\frac{1}{q-1}\prod_{k}\Bigl((-1)^{\mu_{k}}(1-q)\Bigr)\cdot\prod_{l}\Bigl(q^{\nu_{l}}(1-q^{-1})\Bigr).

A straightforward summation in formula (

\langle\zeta_{m}\Xi,\Xi\rangle=\frac{1}{q-1}\sum_{u,v:u+v\geqslant 1}\,\,\sum_{\iota_{1}<\dots<\iota_{u},\upsilon_{1}<\dots<\upsilon_{v}}\,\,\sum_{\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{u},\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{v}:\sum\mu_{k}+\sum\nu_{l}=m}\times\\
\times\prod_{k=1}^{u}\Bigl((-\beta_{\iota_{k}})^{\mu_{k}}(1-q)\Bigr)\prod_{k=1}^{v}\Bigl((q\alpha_{\upsilon_{l}})^{\nu_{l}}(1-q^{-1})\Bigr).

\displaystyle\psi_{\upsilon_{l}}

\displaystyle=\nu_{l}\text{and $\psi_{j}=0$ for all other $l$,}

and get (

), all new factors in the products in

\square

Lemma 3.10

The generating function

G(z):=1+(q-1)\Bigl(z+\sum_{m\geqslant 2}\langle\zeta_{m}\Xi,\Xi\rangle z^{m}\Bigr)

is equal to

G(z):=\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{1+\beta_{i}qz}{1+\beta_{i}z}\,\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1-\alpha_{j}z}{1-\alpha_{j}qz}.

(3.11)

Proof.
We represent the term
(q-1)z
as

(q-1)z=(q-1)\Bigl(\sum_{j}\beta_{j}+\sum_{k}\alpha_{k}\Bigr)z=\sum_{j}(-\beta_{j}z)(1-q)+\sum_{k}(\alpha_{k}qz)(1-q^{-1}).

Applying (
3.10
) we come to the following expression for the generating function:

G(z)=\sum_{\varphi_{1}\geqslant 0,\varphi_{2}\geqslant 0,\dots,\psi_{1}\geqslant 0,\psi_{2}\geqslant 0,\dots}\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}\Bigl((-\beta_{i}z)^{\varphi_{i}}(1-q)^{\varepsilon(\varphi_{i})}\Bigr)\times\\
\times\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}\Bigl((q\alpha_{j}z)^{\psi_{j}}(1-q^{-1})^{\varepsilon(\psi_{j})}\Bigr).

It decomposes into a product

\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}\Bigl(\sum_{\varphi_{i}}(-\beta_{i}z)^{\varphi_{i}}(1-q)^{\varepsilon(\varphi_{i})}\Bigr)\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}\Bigl(\sum_{\psi_{j}}(q\alpha_{j}z)^{\psi_{j}}(1-q^{-1})^{\varepsilon(\psi_{j})}\Bigr)=\\
=\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}\Bigl(1+(1-q)\bigl((1+\beta_{i}z)^{-1}-1\bigr)\Bigr)\,\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}\Bigl(1+(1-q^{-1})\bigl((1-q\alpha_{j}z)^{-1}-1\bigr)\Bigr).

This equals to (
3.11
).

\square

Proof of Lemma
3.5
.
We must verify the identity

G(z)=1+(q-1)z+\sum_{m\geqslant 2}z^{m}\chi^{\alpha,\beta,0}(\zeta_{m}),

see (
1.7

G(z)=\exp\{\ln G(z)\}=\\
=\exp\Bigl\{\sum_{i}\bigl(\ln(1+\beta_{i}qz)-\ln(1+\beta_{i}z)\bigr)+\sum_{j}\bigl(\ln(1-\alpha_{j}z)-\ln(1-\alpha_{j}qz)\bigr)\Bigr\}=\\

=\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}\exp\Bigl\{\bigl(\sum_{j}\alpha_{j}^{k}+(-1)^{k-1}\sum_{j}\beta_{i}^{k}\bigr)\cdot\frac{1}{k}\,(q^{k}-1)z^{k}\Bigr\}.

It remains to decompose exponentials.

\square

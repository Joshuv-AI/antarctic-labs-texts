On Adically Complete D-Modules in Characteristic Zero
Amnon Yekutieli

Abstract
. Let ( X , 𝒪 X ) (X,\mathcal{O}_{X}) be an algebraic manifold in characteristic 0 0 , or an analytic manifold over ℂ \mathbb{C} . A standard theorem says that a left 𝒟 X \mathcal{D}_{X} -module ℳ \mathcal{M} , which is coherent as an 𝒪 X \mathcal{O}_{X} -module, is locally free. This theorem has a generalization to the adically complete algebraic setting, in a paper by Ogus from 1973. In the present paper we take a new look at the work of Ogus. We provide a detailed proof of the theorem on 𝒟 \mathcal{D} -modules, and extend it to the non-noetherian setting. We also give another proof of an interesting result of Ogus about adically complete modules (slightly extended). In the Appendix we discuss a related error in a book by Björk.

Introduction

Let
(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
be an algebraic manifold over a field
\mathbb{K}

0

\mathbb{C}

\mathcal{D}_{X}

\mathcal{M}
, which is coherent as an

[

We also provide another proof of an interesting result of

[
Og

),

Thus our paper is not original research, but rather an attempt to throw new

Before stating our first theorem, we need a bit of background.
Consider a commutative ring
A
and a finitely generated ideal
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq A
.
Let
M
be an
A
-module. We do not assume that
A
is noetherian, nor that
M

is finitely generated.
The module
M
is equipped with its
\mathfrak{a}
-adic
filtration
\{\mathfrak{a}^{i}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}M\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}

The

-adic completion

Suppose the

by

is an inverse system of

Both the

Adically Complete Modules

In this section we study adic completions of modules. Theorem
1.5
is
a slight improvement of
[
Og
, Proposition A1]
.

Let
A
be a nonzero commutative ring, and let
M
be an
A
-module.
We do not assume that
A
is noetherian, nor that
M
is finitely generated..

Suppose
\{F^{i}(M)\}_{i\in I}
is an inverse system of
A
-submodules of
M
,
indexed by a directed set
(I,\leq)
. We call such a system a
filtration
of
M
. The collection of quotients
\{M/F^{i}(M)\}_{i\in I}

is also an inverse system, and its inverse limit

(1.1)

\Lambda_{F}(M):=\lim_{\leftarrow i}\mspace{2.5mu}M/F^{i}(M)

is called the
F
-adic completion
of
M
.
There is a canonical homomorphism

\tau_{M,F}:M\to\Lambda_{F}(M)
,
and we say that
M
is
F
-adically complete
if
\tau_{M,F}
is an
isomorphism.

The next proposition is probably known, but we are not aware of a published
reference.

Proposition 1.2
.

Let
\{M_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be an inverse system of
A
-modules, indexed by a
directed set
(I,\leq)
, and let

M:=\lim_{\leftarrow i}\mspace{2.5mu}M_{i}
.
Define the filtration
\{F^{i}(M)\}_{i\in I}
of
M

by
F^{i}(M):=\operatorname{Ker}\bigl(M\to M_{i}\bigr)
.
Then the
A
-module
M
is
F
-adically complete.

We do not assume that the indexing set
I
is countable.

Proof.

For every
i
let

N_{i}:=M/F^{i}(M)
. There are injections

\psi_{i}:N_{i}\rightarrowtail M_{i}
, and in the limit we obtain an injection

\psi:=\lim_{\leftarrow i}\psi_{i}:=\Lambda_{F}(M)=\lim_{\leftarrow i}N_{i}\rightarrowtail\lim_{\leftarrow i}M_{i}=M.

It fits in the commutative diagram of
A
-modules

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Lambda_{F}(M)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{id}_{M}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau_{M,F}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi}

It follows that
\psi
is surjective, and thus it is
bijective. Therefore
\tau_{M,F}
is bijective.
∎

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
M
be an
A
-module, with a filtration

\{F^{i}(M)\}_{i\in I}
indexed by a directed set
(I,\leq)
.
Define
\widehat{M}:=\Lambda_{F}(M)
, and put on
\widehat{M}
the filtration

\{F^{i}(\widehat{M})\}_{i\in I}
, where

F^{i}(\widehat{M}):=\operatorname{Ker}\bigl(\widehat{M}\to M/F^{i}(M)\bigr)
.
Then
\widehat{M}
is
F
-adically complete.

Proof.

For every
i
let
M_{i}:=M/F^{i}(M)
.
Then
\widehat{M}=\lim_{\leftarrow i\in I}\mspace{2.5mu}M_{i}
,
and its filtration
F
is the same as in Proposition
1.2
.
The proposition says that
\widehat{M}
is
F
-adically complete.
∎

An important source of filtrations comes from ideals
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq A
.
Namely, given an ideal
\mathfrak{a}
, we define

F^{i}(M):=\mathfrak{a}^{i}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}M
. This is the
\mathfrak{a}
-adic filtration
of
M
,
indexed by
I=\mathbb{N}
, and the completion

\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(M):=\Lambda_{F}(M)
is called the
\mathfrak{a}
-adic completion
of
M
.
Observe that

\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}:\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod}}(A)\to\operatorname{\mathsf{Mod}}(A)

is an
A
-linear functor, and
\tau_{\mathfrak{a}}:\operatorname{Id}\to\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}
is a morphism
of functors.

We know (see
[
Ye3
, Example 1.8]
) there there are
A
-modules
M
whose

\mathfrak{a}
-adic completion
\widehat{M}
is
not

\mathfrak{a}
-adically complete.
How can this be reconciled with Corollary

?
The answer is that there are two, possibly distinct, filtrations on

\widehat{M}
, namely

\operatorname{Ker}\bigl(\widehat{M}\to M\mspace{2.5mu}/\mspace{2.5mu}\mathfrak{a}^{i}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}M\bigr)
and

\mathfrak{a}^{i}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}\widehat{M}
.
When the ideal
\mathfrak{a}
is finitely generated these two filtrations agree. Indeed:

Proposition 1.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{a}
be a finitely generated ideal in the ring
A
, and let
M
be an
arbitrary
A
-module. Consider the
\mathfrak{a}
-adic filtration

\{\mathfrak{a}^{i}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}M\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
on
M
, and the completion

\widehat{M}:=\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)
. Then:

(1)

The
A
-module
\widehat{M}
is
\mathfrak{a}
-adically complete.

(2)

For every
i
there is equality

\mathfrak{a}^{i}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}M=\operatorname{Ker}(\widehat{M}\to M\mspace{2.5mu}/\mspace{2.5mu}\mathfrak{a}^{i}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}M\bigr)
.

Proof.

According to
[
Ye3
, Corollary 3.6]
, the
A
-module
\widehat{M}
is
\mathfrak{a}
-adically
complete. By
[

∎

Item (1) of the proposition says that the endofunctor

[

, Proposition A1]
, where

was assumed to be countable. We give a new proof, relying

Theorem 1.5

(Ogus)
.

Let
\mathfrak{a}
be a finitely generated ideal in the ring
A
, and let
M
be an
arbitrary
A
-module, with a filtration

\{F^{i}(M)\}_{i\in I}
indexed by some directed set
(I,\leq)
.
Assume that
M
is
F
-adically complete, and that the
F
-adic topology on
M

is weaker than the
\mathfrak{a}
-adic topology on it. Then

is
\mathfrak{a}
-adically complete.

Proof.

Since the
F
-adic topology is weaker than the
\mathfrak{a}
-adic topology, for any

such that

\mathfrak{a}^{j}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}M\subseteq F^{i}(M)
. Composing the canonical surjections

\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)\mspace{2.5mu}\to\mspace{2.5mu}M/\mathfrak{a}^{j}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}M

\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)\to M/F^{i}(M)
.
These homomorphisms assemble into an inverse system indexed by
I
, and they
induce a homomorphism

\psi:\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)\to\Lambda_{F}(M)
.
This homomorphism fits into the commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Lambda_{F}(M)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau_{M,\mathfrak{a}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau_{M,F}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi}

We see that
\tau_{M,\mathfrak{a}}
is a split injection, so there is an

A

\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)\cong M\oplus K
, where
K:=\operatorname{Ker}(\psi)
.

Since
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)
is
\mathfrak{a}
-adically complete (by Proposition

),
and since
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}
is an

is also
\mathfrak{a}
-adically complete.
(So in fact

and

are bijective, and

∎

.

With
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq A

. If

[
Og
, Corollary A3]
.

Corollary 1.7
.

With
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq A
as in the theorem, assume
M
is
\mathfrak{a}
-adically complete, and

N\subseteq M
is an
A
-submodule that is closed for the
\mathfrak{a}
-adic topology. Then

N
is
\mathfrak{a}
-adically complete.

Proof.

Take any submodule
N
of
M
. Consider the filtration

\{F^{i}(N)\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of

F^{i}(N):=N\cap\mathfrak{a}^{i}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}M

An element

-adically

-adic topology on

is weaker than its

-adically complete.

[
Og
, Corollary A2]
;
again we do not require the set

to be countable.

Corollary 1.8
.

With
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq A
as in the theorem, let

\{M_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be an inverse system of
A
-modules,
indexed by a directed set
(I,\leq)
.
Assume every

is an
\mathfrak{a}
-adically complete module. Define the

M:=\lim_{\leftarrow i\in I}M_{i}
. Then

is
\mathfrak{a}

Consider the set

, with this quasi-order:

For

. According to Theorem

-adically complete.

We begin by recalling material from
[
EGA IV
, Section 16]
.
Let
A\to B
be a homomorphism between nonzero commutative rings.
Given
B
-modules
M
and
N
, consider the
A
-central
B
-bimodule

\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,N)
, where the action by
B
is

(b_{1}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}\theta\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}b_{2})(m):=b_{1}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}\theta(b_{2}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}m)

for
b_{i}\in B
,
m\in M
and
\theta\in\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,N)
.
We refer to the homomorphism
\theta
as an
operator
.
The operator
\theta
is said to have
differential order

\leq 0

if it is
B
-linear. For
k\geq 1
the definition is inductive:
an operator
\theta\in\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,N)
has
differential order
\leq k
if for every
b\in B
the operator

[b,\theta]:=b\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}\theta-\theta\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}b
has
differential order
\leq k-1
.
The set of all differential operators of order
\leq k
is the
A
-submodule

\operatorname{Diff}^{k}_{B/A}(M,N)
of
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,N)
, and the set of all
differential operators is

(2.1)

\operatorname{Diff}_{B/A}(M,N):=\bigcup_{k\geq 0}\operatorname{Diff}^{k}_{B/A}(M,N)\subseteq\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,N).

It is not hard to see that for
B
-modules
M_{0}
,
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
, composition
is a function

\operatorname{Diff}^{k_{1}}_{B/A}(M_{0},M_{1})\times\operatorname{Diff}^{k_{2}}_{B/A}(M_{1},M_{2})\to\operatorname{Diff}^{k_{1}+k_{2}}_{B/A}(M_{0},M_{2}).

Thus for a single module
M
,

\operatorname{Diff}_{B/A}(M,M)
is a filtered
B
-ring, central over
A
.

For
M=B
we obtain the ring of differential operators

\mathcal{D}_{B/A}:=\operatorname{Diff}_{B/A}(B,B)
,
which comes with the filtration
\{\mathcal{D}^{k}_{B/A}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
.
In this case
\mathcal{D}^{0}_{B/A}=B
, but
B
is not in the center of

\mathcal{D}_{B/A}
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
M
be a left
\mathcal{D}_{B/A}
-module. For any operator

\theta\in\mathcal{D}^{k}_{B/A}
, the operator

\theta_{M}\in\operatorname{End}_{A}(M)
,
\theta_{M}(m):=\theta\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}m
, is a
differential operator of order
\leq k
.

Proof.

By induction on
k
. For
k=0
we have
\mathcal{D}^{0}_{B/A}=B
, so
\theta_{M}
has
order
\leq 0
. Now take
k\geq 1
. For every
b\in B
and
m\in M
we have

[b,\theta_{M}](m)=b\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}\theta\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}m-\theta\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}b\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}m=[b,\theta]_{M}(m)

in
M
.
Now
[b,\theta]\in\mathcal{D}^{k-1}_{B/A}
, so by the induction hypothesis

[b,\theta]_{M}\in\operatorname{Diff}^{k-1}_{B/A}(M,M)
.
We conclude that
[b,\theta_{M}]
is a differential operator of order
\leq k-1
for all
b
. Therefore
\theta_{M}
is a differential
operator of order
\leq k
.
∎

Let
I
be the kernel of the multiplication homomorphism

B\otimes_{A}B\to B
. The ring

\mathcal{P}^{k}_{B/A}:=(B\otimes_{A}B)/I^{k+1}

is called the
module of principal parts of order
k
.
See
[
EGA IV
, Subsection 16.3]
.
We view
\mathcal{P}^{k}_{B/A}
as a left
B
-module by the ring homomorphism

B\to\mathcal{P}^{k}_{B/A}
,
b\mapsto b\otimes 1
.
Then the ring homomorphism

\mathrm{d}^{k}:B\to\mathcal{P}^{k}_{B/A}
,
b\mapsto 1\otimes b
, is a differential
operator of order
k
. It is universal in the following sense:
for
B
modules
M
and
N
, the
A
-module homomorphism

(2.3)

\operatorname{Hom}_{B}(\mathcal{P}^{k}_{B/A}\otimes_{B}M,N)\to\operatorname{Diff}^{k}_{B/A}(M,N)\ ,\ \phi\mapsto\phi\circ\mathrm{d}^{k}_{M}\ ,

is bijective. Here

\mathrm{d}^{k}_{M}(m):=\mathrm{d}^{k}(1_{B})\otimes m\in\mathcal{P}^{k}_{B/A}\otimes_{B}M
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{b}\subseteq B
be an ideal, and let

\theta\in\operatorname{Diff}^{k}_{B/A}(M,N)
. Then

\theta(\mathfrak{b}^{i+k}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}M)\subseteq\mathfrak{b}^{i}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}N
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

This is
[
Ye1
, Proposition 1.4.6]
.
∎

Suppose
M
and
N
are
B
-modules,
\mathfrak{b}\subseteq B
is a finitely generated
ideal, and
\theta:M\to N
is an
A
-linear homomorphism that’s continuous for
the
\mathfrak{b}
-adic topologies on these modules. Then
\theta
extends uniquely to a
continuous
A
-linear homomorphism

\widehat{\theta}:\widehat{M}\to\widehat{N}
between the
\mathfrak{b}
-adic completions.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
\mathfrak{b}\subseteq B
be a finitely generated ideal, and let
\widehat{B}
be the

\mathfrak{b}
-adic completion of
B
. Let
M
and
N
be
B
-modules,
and let
\theta\in\operatorname{Diff}^{k}_{B/A}(M,N)
.

(1)

The
A
-linear homomorphism
\theta:M\to N
is continuous for the

\mathfrak{b}
-adic topologies.

(2)

The unique continuous extension

\widehat{\theta}:\widehat{M}\to\widehat{N}
of
\theta
belongs
to
\operatorname{Diff}^{k}_{\widehat{B}/A}(\widehat{M},\widehat{N})
.

Proof.

(1) Clear from Lemma
2.4
.

(2) The operator
\widehat{\theta}
is a differential operator of order
\leq k
iff

[\cdots[\widehat{\theta},b_{0}],\ldots,b_{k}]=0

for all
b_{0},\ldots,b_{k}\in\widehat{B}
.
This commutator expression is a continuous multilinear function

\widehat{B}^{\mspace{2.5mu}k+1}\to\widehat{B}
, and it vanishes on the image of

B^{\mspace{2.5mu}k+1}
. Therefore it is zero on
\widehat{B}^{\mspace{2.5mu}k+1}
.
∎

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
\mathfrak{b}\subseteq B
be a finitely generated ideal, and let
\widehat{B}
be the

\mathfrak{b}
-adic completion of
B
.

(1)

Any differential operator
\theta\in\mathcal{D}^{k}_{B/A}

extends uniquely to a differential operator
\widehat{\theta}\in\mathcal{D}^{k}_{\widehat{B}/A}
.

(2)

The function
\theta\mapsto\widehat{\theta}
is a filtered
A
-ring homomorphism

\mathcal{D}_{B/A}\to\mathcal{D}_{\widehat{B}/A}
.

Proof.

(1) This is clear from Proposition
2.5
.

(2) This follows from the uniqueness in (1).
∎

Now assume
B=A[t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}]
, the polynomial ring in
n
variables.

A
is still an arbitrary nonzero commutative ring.
For a multi-index
\boldsymbol{i}=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}

we write

\boldsymbol{t}^{\boldsymbol{i}}:=t_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots t_{n}^{i_{n}}

and
\lvert\boldsymbol{i}\rvert:=i_{1}+\cdots+i_{n}
.
Then (see
[
Ye1
, Section 1.4]
)
\mathcal{P}^{k}_{B/A}
is a free left

B
-module with basis the collection

\{\mathrm{d}^{k}(\boldsymbol{t}^{\boldsymbol{i}})\}_{\lvert\boldsymbol{i}\rvert\leq k}
.
From this we get the dual basis

\{\theta_{\boldsymbol{i}}\}_{\lvert\boldsymbol{i}\rvert\leq k}
of
\mathcal{D}^{k}_{B/A}
.
Namely, the differential operator
\theta_{\boldsymbol{i}}
is the unique differential
operator such that

\theta_{\boldsymbol{i}}(\boldsymbol{t}^{\boldsymbol{i}})=1
and

\theta_{\boldsymbol{i}}(\boldsymbol{t}^{\mspace{2.5mu}\boldsymbol{j}})=0
for
\boldsymbol{j}\neq\boldsymbol{i}
.
For
k=1
we have the partial derivatives

\partial_{i}=\partial/\partial_{t_{i}}\in\mathcal{D}^{1}_{B/A}
,
which satisfy
\partial_{i}(t_{i})=1
, and
\partial_{i}(t_{j})=0
if
j\neq i
.

If
A
contains
\mathbb{Q}
, then, due to the uniqueness above, for every

\boldsymbol{i}=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{n})
there is equality

(2.7)

\theta_{\boldsymbol{i}}=(i_{1}!\cdots i_{n}!)^{-1}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}\partial_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots\partial_{n}^{i_{n}}\in\mathcal{D}_{B/A}.

It follows that as a left
B
-module,
\mathcal{D}_{B/A}
is free with basis the
collection of monomial operators

\{\partial_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots\partial_{n}^{i_{n}}\}_{\boldsymbol{i}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}
.
The relations satisfied by the multiplication in
\mathcal{D}_{B/A}
are these:

\partial_{i}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}\partial_{j}=\partial_{j}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}\partial_{i}
, and

\partial_{i}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}b=b\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}\partial_{i}+\partial_{i}(b)
for
b\in B
.

Consider the ideal
\mathfrak{b}\subseteq B
generated by the variables. The
\mathfrak{b}
-adic
completion of
B
is the ring
\widehat{B}=A[[t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}]]
of power series.
The next proposition describes the structure of the ring

\mathcal{D}_{\widehat{B}/A}
.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
A
be a commutative ring containing
\mathbb{Q}
, let

B:=A[t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}]
, and let

\widehat{B}:=A[[t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}]]
.

(1)

Any differential operator
\theta\in\mathcal{D}_{B/A}
of order
\leq k

extends uniquely to a differential operator

\widehat{\theta}\in\mathcal{D}_{\widehat{B}/A}
of order
\leq k
.

(2)

The function
\theta\mapsto\widehat{\theta}
is a filtered ring homomorphism

\mathcal{D}_{B/A}\to\mathcal{D}_{\widehat{B}/A}
.

(3)

The left
\widehat{B}
-module homomorphism

\widehat{B}\otimes_{B}\mathcal{D}_{B/A}\to\mathcal{D}_{\widehat{B}/A}
is bijective.

(4)

\mathcal{D}_{\widehat{B}/A}
is a free left
\widehat{B}
-module with basis

\{\partial_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots\partial_{n}^{i_{n}}\}_{\boldsymbol{i}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}
.

(5)

The relations satisfied by the multiplication in
\mathcal{D}_{\widehat{B}/A}

are these:

\partial_{i}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}\partial_{j}=\partial_{j}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}\partial_{i}
, and

\partial_{i}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}b=b\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}\partial_{i}+\partial_{i}(b)
for
b\in\widehat{B}
.

Proof.

(1-2) These are special cases of items (1-2) of Corollary
2.6
.

(3)
By item (1) there is a canonical
A
-module isomorphism

(2.9)

\mathcal{D}_{\widehat{B}/A}=\operatorname{Diff}^{k}_{\widehat{B}/A}(\widehat{B},\widehat{B})\cong\operatorname{Diff}^{k}_{B/A}(B,\widehat{B}).

For every
k
, the left
B
-module

\mathcal{P}^{k}_{B/A}
is free of finite rank. Therefore, using (

),
we have isomorphisms of
A
-modules

\mathcal{D}^{k}_{\widehat{B}/A}\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{B}(\mathcal{P}^{k}_{B/A},\widehat{B})\cong\widehat{B}\otimes_{B}\operatorname{Hom}_{B}(\mathcal{P}^{k}_{B/A},B)\cong\widehat{B}\otimes_{B}\mathcal{D}^{k}_{B/A}.

The composed isomorphism

\widehat{B}\otimes_{B}\mathcal{D}^{k}_{B/A}\xrightarrow{\smash{\raisebox{-1.50694pt}{$\scriptstyle\simeq\mspace{2mu}$}}}\mathcal{D}^{k}_{\widehat{B}/A}

is compatible with the ring homomorphism

\mathcal{D}_{B/A}\to\mathcal{D}_{\widehat{B}/A}
from item (1).

(4)
This is clear from item (3) and formula (
2.7
).

(5)
These relations hold in
\mathcal{D}_{B/A}
, so by continuity they also hold in

\mathcal{D}_{\widehat{B}/A}
.
∎

Corollary 2.10
.

In the setting of Proposition
2.8
, let

be a left

\mathcal{D}_{B/A}
-module, and let
\widehat{M}
be the
\mathfrak{b}
-adic completion of

.
Then
\widehat{M}
has a unique left
\mathcal{D}_{\widehat{B}/A}
-module structure, such

Of course
\widehat{M}
is a
\widehat{B}
-module.
By Propositions

and

, the partial derivatives

\partial_{i}
act continuously on

. By continuity, the relations
in Proposition

(5) are satisfied in

∎

Remark 2.11
.

Let
A\to B
be a ring homomorphism,
\mathfrak{b}\subseteq B
a finitely generated ideal,
and
\widehat{B}
the
\mathfrak{b}
-adic completion of
B
. We can give a description of
the ring
\mathcal{D}_{\widehat{B}/A}
as the
dir-inv completion
of the ring

\mathcal{D}_{B/A}
.

Dir-inv structures where introduced in
[
Ye2
, Section 1]
. In our setting
we put on
\mathcal{D}_{B/A}
the dir structure (an ascending filtration)

\{\mathcal{D}^{k}_{B/A}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
, and on each
\mathcal{D}^{k}_{B/A}
we put the inv
structure (a descending filtration)

\{F^{i}(\mathcal{D}^{k}_{B/A})\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
, where

F^{i}(\mathcal{D}^{k}_{B/A}):=\bigl\{\theta\in\mathcal{D}^{k}_{B/A}\mid\theta(\mathfrak{b}^{j})\subseteq\mathfrak{b}^{\operatorname{max}(j+i,\mspace{1.0mu}0)}\ \ \textup{for all}\ \ j\in\mathbb{N}\bigr\}.

The dir-inv completion of
\mathcal{D}_{B/A}
is

\Lambda_{\mathrm{di}}(\mathcal{D}_{B/A}):=\lim_{k\to}\ \lim_{\leftarrow i}\mspace{2.5mu}\mathcal{D}^{k}_{B/A}\mspace{2.5mu}/\mspace{2.5mu}F^{i}(\mathcal{D}^{k}_{B/A}).

This completion has a unique
A
-ring structure such that
the diagram of
A
-rings

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{D}_{B/A}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{D}_{\widehat{B}/A}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Lambda_{\mathrm{di}}(\mathcal{D}_{B/A})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}

is commutative, and the horizontal arrow is an isomorphism.

This description of the ring
\mathcal{D}_{\widehat{B}/A}
can be used to prove a
generalization of Corollary
2.10
to the setting of this remark.

3.
Complete
\mathcal{D}
-Modules – the One Dimensional
Case

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
3.13
,
which is the
1
-dimensional case of Theorem
0.6
from the
introduction. We give a rather detailed proof; but the essential idea
already appears in the prior papers of Ogus and others, see Remark

3.15
.

Throughout this section we assume the following:

Setup 3.1
.

We are given a commutative ring
A
containing
\mathbb{Q}
.
Let
B:=A[[t]]
, the ring of power series in the variable
t
, and let

\mathfrak{b}\subseteq B
be the ideal generated by
t
.
We are also given a left
\mathcal{D}_{B/A}
-module
M
, which is
\mathfrak{b}
-adically
complete as a
B
-module.

Observe that the ring
A
is not assumed to be noetherian, and the
B
-module

M
is not assumed to be finitely generated.

By the definition of the
\mathfrak{b}
-adic topology on
M
, the submodules

\mathfrak{b}^{i}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}M
are open in
M
, and thus – as always in linearly topologized
modules – also closed.

As explained in Section
2
,
the ring of differential operators
\mathcal{D}_{B/A}
is the subring
of
\operatorname{End}_{A}(B)
generated by
B
and the derivation

\partial:=\scalebox{1.1}{$\genfrac{}{}{0.45pt}{1}{\partial}{\partial t {}^{\vphantom{X}}}$}
.
An operator
\phi\in\mathcal{D}_{B/A}
of differential order
\leq i
has a unique
sum expansion

(3.2)

\phi=\sum_{0\leq j\leq i}\ b_{j}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}\partial^{\mspace{2.5mu}j}

in
\mathcal{D}_{B/A}
, with coefficients
b_{j}\in B
.
According to Proposition
2.8
(5), the relation satisfied by the
element
\partial
in the noncommutative ring
\mathcal{D}_{B/A}
is

(3.3)

\partial\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}b=b\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}\partial+\partial(b),

where
\partial(b)\in B
is the result of action
of the operator
\partial
on an element
b\in B
.
For every
m\in M
and
b\in B
we have the equality

(3.4)

\partial(b\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}m)=(\partial\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}b)\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}m=(b\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}\partial+\partial(b))\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}m=b\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}\partial(m)+\partial(b)\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}m.

This formula implies that

\partial(\mathfrak{b}^{i}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}M)\subseteq\mathfrak{b}^{i-1}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}M
, so
\partial
acts continuously on
M

for its
\mathfrak{b}
-adic topology. By formula (
3.2
) we see that every
operator
\phi\in\mathcal{D}_{B/A}
acts continuously on
M
.

Definition 3.5
.

(1)

For every
i\in\mathbb{N}
define the order
i

\psi_{i}:=(i!)^{-1}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}(-t)^{i}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}\partial^{i}\in\mathcal{D}_{B/A}.

(2)

For every element

define the element

\psi(m):=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\mspace{2.5mu}\psi_{i}(m)=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\mspace{2.5mu}(i!)^{-1}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}(-t)^{i}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}\partial^{i}(m)\in M.

Since
\psi_{i}(m)\in\mathfrak{b}^{i}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}M
, the sum in item (2) converges in the

\mathfrak{b}
-adically complete module
M
.
See Remark
3.16
regarding the possible convergence of the operator sum,
namely making sense of the formula

\psi=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\mspace{2.5mu}\psi_{i}
.

Recall that the module of horizontal elements of
M
is

M^{\mathrm{hor}}=\{m\in M\mid\partial(m)=0\}
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Under Setup
3.1
, the following hold for the function

\psi:M\to M
from Definition
3.5
(2)
.

(1)

The function
\psi
is
A
-linear.

(2)

\psi(m)\in M^{\mathrm{hor}}
for every
m\in M
.

(3)

If
m\in M^{\mathrm{hor}}
then
\psi(m)=m
.

(4)

m-\psi(m)\in\mathfrak{b}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}M

.

Proof.

(1) This is because each
\psi_{i}
is
A
-linear, and by the pointwise
convergence of the sum

\psi(m)=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\mspace{2.5mu}\psi_{i}(m)
in

.

(2) Due to the continuity of
\partial
, and using formula (

),
for every

\displaystyle\partial(\psi(m))=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\mspace{2.5mu}\partial\bigl((i!)^{-1}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}(-t)^{i}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}\partial^{i}(m)\bigr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\mspace{2.5mu}\Bigl((i!)^{-1}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}(-t)^{i}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}\partial^{i+1}(m)-(i!)^{-1}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}i\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}(-t)^{i-1}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}\partial^{i}(m)\Bigl)

\displaystyle=\Bigl(\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\mspace{2.5mu}(i!)^{-1}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}(-t)^{i}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}\partial^{i+1}(m)\Bigr)-\Bigl(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\mspace{2.5mu}(j!)^{-1}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}(-t)^{j}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}\partial^{j+1}(m)\Bigr)=0.

(3) Take some

. Then

\psi_{0}(m)=m
, and
\psi_{i}(m)=0

.

.

(4) Take some element

. By definition we have
\psi_{0}(m)=m
,

\psi_{i}(m)\in\mathfrak{b}^{i}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}M\subseteq\mathfrak{b}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}M

Since

\psi(m)-m=\sum_{i\geq 1}\psi_{i}(m)\in\mathfrak{b}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}M
.
∎

Define the

Proposition 3.7

(Existence of a Solution)
.

Under Setup
3.1
, given an element

\widebar{m}\in\widebar{M}
, there exists an element
m\in M

and

.

Later, in Corollary

, we shall see that the element

Proof.

Theorem 3.13
.

Under Setup
3.1
, the
\mathcal{D}_{B/A}
-module homomorphism

\theta:B\mspace{2.5mu}\widehat{\otimes}_{A}\mspace{2.5mu}M^{\mathrm{hor}}\to M

Proof.

The proof is divided into two steps.
As before we write

]
is very easy. They consider a closed point
x\in X
, the
local ring
A:=\mathcal{O}_{X,x}
, and the
A
-module
M:=\mathcal{M}_{x}
. It suffices to
prove that
M
is a free
A
-module. This is done by a calculation
almost identical to the the one in the proof of Theorem
3.13
.

It is possible to deduce the algebraic result above from the corresponding
complete result, namely Theorem
4.1
. Let
\widehat{A}
be the completion
of the local ring
A
at its maximal ideal, and let
\widehat{M}
be the
completion of the module
M
. Since
A\to\widehat{A}
is faithfully flat, it
suffices to prove that
\widehat{M}
is a free
\widehat{A}
-module. The completed
module
\widehat{M}
has a
\mathcal{D}_{\widehat{A}/\mathbb{K}}
structure.
The residue field
\mathbb{K}^{\prime}
of
A
is a finite separable extension of
\mathbb{K}
, so it
can be lifted uniquely into
\widehat{A}
, and then

\widehat{A}\cong\mathbb{K}^{\prime}[[t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}]]
.
Now Theorem
4.1
can be applied.

The reverse direction – namely using
[
HTT
, Theorem 1.4.10]
to prove
Theorem
4.1
– does not work, because we do not know a priori that

\widehat{M}
descends to some
A
-module
M
.

Furthermore, there are plenty of nonisomorphic
\mathcal{D}_{A/\mathbb{K}}
-modules
that trivialize when passing to the completion
\widehat{A}
.
To see this explicitly, let’s assume that
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{C}
,
n=1
and
the rank of
M
is
1
.
For every
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
there is a
\mathcal{D}_{A/\mathbb{C}}
-module

M_{\lambda}
, which is a free
A
-module of rank
1
with basis
e_{\lambda}
, and
such that
\partial(e_{\lambda})=\lambda\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}e_{\lambda}
.
When passing to the completion, the
\widehat{A}
-module

\widehat{M}_{\lambda}
is still free with basis
e_{\lambda}
, but now the ring

\widehat{A}
contains the invertible element

u_{\lambda}:=\operatorname{exp}(-\lambda\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}t)
, and the element

u_{\lambda}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}e_{\lambda}\in\widehat{M}_{\lambda}
is a horizontal basis,
so that
\widehat{M}_{\lambda}
is a trivial
\mathcal{D}_{\widehat{A}/\mathbb{C}}
-module.

Appendix A
An Error in a Book by Björk

While studying the literature on
\mathcal{D}
-modules, we looked at the proof of
Theorem 1.1.25 in the book
[
Bj
]
by J.-E. Björk. This theorem is an
analytic version of Corollary
0.9
, which is a special case of Theorem

0.6
. While reading the proof, we discovered an error in it.
In this appendix we shall explain the error, and how it can be resolved.

Before doing so, we should address a question of etiquette. As customary, we
wanted to communicate our discovery to the author of the book, giving him the
possibility to address it. Unfortunately, Björk passed away several
years ago. Therefore we communicated his student Rolf Källström, who is
working in this same area. He confirmed that there is indeed an
error, that this error had not been observed previously, and that we ought
to write about it.

Now to the mathematics. We prefer to use our notation, and to simplify the
situation as much as possible, so that the salient points will be clear. We
also want to treat two settings at once:

(
*
)

The setting of our paper, specialized:
A=\mathbb{K}
is a field
of characteristic
0
, and
B=\mathbb{K}[[t]]
. Convergence is for the

\mathfrak{b}
-adic topology, where
\mathfrak{b}=(t)\subseteq B
.

(
**
)

The complex analytic setting that is treated in the book

[
Bj
]
. Here
A=\mathbb{C}
, the field of complex numbers with its standard norm,
and
B
is the subring of
\mathbb{C}[[t]]
consisting of convergent power series,
namely series
b=\sum_{k\geq 0}\lambda_{k}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}t^{k}
with coefficients

\lambda_{k}\in\mathbb{C}
satisfying the asymptotic condition

\operatorname{limsup}_{k\to\infty}(\lvert\lambda_{k}\rvert)^{1/k}<\infty
.
In other words,
B
is the ring of germs of analytic functions in one variable
at the origin. It is a noetherian local ring, with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{b}

generated by
t
.

The ring of differential operators
\mathcal{D}_{B/A}
has the same structure in both
settings. As before we write
\partial:=\scalebox{1.1}{$\genfrac{}{}{0.45pt}{1}{\partial}{\partial t {}^{\vphantom{X}}}$}
.

Suppose
M
is a left
\mathcal{D}_{B/A}
-module that’s finitely generated as a

B
-module. We can assume that
M
is a free
B
-module of rank
r
, with basis

\boldsymbol{m}^{\prime}=(m^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,m^{\prime}_{r})
; for this we can either rely on

[
HTT
, Theorem 1.4.10]
, or on the first half of the proof of

[
Bj
, Theorem 1.1.25]
, which is correct (and is similar to the proof in

[
HTT
]
). The issue is how to replace the basis
\boldsymbol{m}^{\prime}
with another basis

\boldsymbol{m}
, which is horizontal, and is congruent to
\boldsymbol{m}^{\prime}
modulo the maximal
ideal
\mathfrak{b}\subseteq B
.
In other words, we want to find a matrix

\boldsymbol{g}=\boldsymbol{g}(t)\in\operatorname{GL}_{r}(B)
,

\boldsymbol{g}\equiv\boldsymbol{1}\mspace{2.5mu}\operatorname{mod}\mspace{1.0mu}t
,
such that

(A.1)

\partial(\boldsymbol{g}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}\boldsymbol{m}^{\prime})=0.

Then the basis
\boldsymbol{m}:=\boldsymbol{g}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}\boldsymbol{m}^{\prime}
of
M
will be horizontal.

The difficulty only arises when
r\geq 2
, so let us assume this.
Björk considers the matrix

\boldsymbol{b}\in\operatorname{Mat}_{r}(B)

such that
\partial(\boldsymbol{m}^{\prime})=\boldsymbol{b}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}\boldsymbol{m}^{\prime}
.
The matrix
\boldsymbol{b}
has a power series expansion

\boldsymbol{b}=\sum_{k\geq 0}\mspace{2.5mu}t^{k}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}\boldsymbol{a}_{k}

with
\boldsymbol{a}_{k}\in\operatorname{Mat}_{r}(A)
.
Convergence of this series in the setting (
*
) is easy; we did not verify
convergence in the setting (
**
), but presumably this is automatic for
standard
reasons.

Next Björk defines the matrix

\boldsymbol{c}:=\sum_{k\geq 0}\mspace{2.5mu}(k+1)^{-1}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}t^{k+1}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}\boldsymbol{a}_{k}\in\operatorname{Mat}_{r}(B),

which satisfies
\partial(\boldsymbol{c})=\boldsymbol{b}
.
Again, convergence is obvious in the adic setting (
*
), and
requires justification in the analytic setting (
**
).
Then he defines the matrix

\boldsymbol{g}:=\operatorname{exp}(-\boldsymbol{c})=\sum_{k\geq 0}\mspace{2.5mu}(k!)^{-1}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}(-\boldsymbol{c})^{k}\in\operatorname{Mat}_{r}(B).

Observe that this sum converges in the adic setting (
*
) because the
matrix
\boldsymbol{c}
is divisible by
t
. In the complex analytic setting
(
**
) convergence is because of the rapid decay of
(k!)^{-1}
.

The erroneous claim of Björk (implicit in the text, yet present
in formula (2) there) is that the matrix

\boldsymbol{g}\in\operatorname{Mat}_{r}(B)
satisfies the differential equation

(A.2)

\partial(\boldsymbol{g})=^{\textup{false}}-\boldsymbol{g}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}\boldsymbol{b},

with initial condition

\boldsymbol{g}(0)=\boldsymbol{1}\in\operatorname{Mat}_{r}(A)
.
For the differential equation

(
A.2
)
to hold it is sufficient, and very likely also necessary,
that the matrices
\boldsymbol{a}_{k}
commute with each other
.

Anyhow, here is a counterexample: take the ring
A:=\mathbb{Q}
, so
B=\mathbb{Q}[[t]]
, and
the rank
r:=2
. The matrix

\boldsymbol{c}=\boldsymbol{c}(t)\in\operatorname{Mat}_{2}(B)
is

\boldsymbol{c}:=t\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}\boldsymbol{a}_{1}+t^{2}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}\boldsymbol{a}_{2}
, with

\boldsymbol{a}_{1}:=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\
0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right]
and

\boldsymbol{a}_{2}:=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right]
.
Recall that
\boldsymbol{b}=\partial(\boldsymbol{c})
and

\boldsymbol{g}=\operatorname{exp}(-\boldsymbol{c})
.
A calculation shows that

\partial(\boldsymbol{g})\neq-\boldsymbol{g}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}\boldsymbol{b}
.

Björk proceeds as follows. He takes the sequence

\boldsymbol{m}:=\boldsymbol{g}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}\boldsymbol{m}^{\prime}
of elements of
M
. Here is our
interpretation of Björk’s formula (2):

(A.3)

\partial(\boldsymbol{m})=\partial(\boldsymbol{g}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}\boldsymbol{m}^{\prime})=\partial(\boldsymbol{g})\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}\boldsymbol{m}^{\prime}+\boldsymbol{g}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}\partial(\boldsymbol{m}^{\prime})=^{\textup{false}}-\boldsymbol{g}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}\boldsymbol{b}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}\boldsymbol{m}^{\prime}+\boldsymbol{g}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}\boldsymbol{b}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}\boldsymbol{m}^{\prime}=0.

The false equality
=^{\textup{false}}
relies on the false formula
(
A.2
).

In the setting (
**
) of complex analytic functions, the correct way
to solve the ODE (
A.1
) is by appealing to the standard existence
results. Presumably this is what Deligne had in mind when he wrote
"Il est bien connu que…" regarding
[
De
, Theorem 2.17]
.
In
[
HTT
, section 4.2]
, when discussing analytic
\mathcal{D}
-modules,
they refer to the Frobenius Theorem.

In our
t
-adic setting (
*
), Lemma
3.6
and its proof
give us an explicit formula for the matrix
\boldsymbol{g}
solving the ODE
(
A.1
). Given the basis

\boldsymbol{m}^{\prime}=(m^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,m^{\prime}_{r})
of
M
, let

m_{i}:=\psi(m^{\prime}_{i})\in M
and

\boldsymbol{m}:=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{r})
,
which is a horizontal basis of
M
. Then define

\boldsymbol{g}\in\operatorname{GL}_{r}(B)
to be the matrix such that

\boldsymbol{g}\mspace{0.8mu}{\cdotB}\mspace{0.8mu}\boldsymbol{m}^{\prime}=\boldsymbol{m}
. This matrix
\boldsymbol{g}
will solve the ODE
(
A.1
).

An alternative approach for obtaining the matrix
\boldsymbol{g}
is by
1
-dimensional nonabelian multiplicative integration
, also known as
path
ordered exponential
.
This method would present
\boldsymbol{g}
as the limit of
Riemann products
, cf.
[
Ye5
, Chapter 3]
.

On the anticyclotomic Iwasawa main conjecture for Hilbert modular forms of parallel weights
Haining Wang

Abstract
. In this article, we study the Iwasawa theory for Hilbert modular forms over the anticyclotomic extension of a CM field. We prove a one sided divisibility result toward the Iwasawa main conjecture. The proof relies on the first and second reciprocity law relating theta elements to Heegner points Euler system. As a by-product we also prove certain Bloch-Kato type result in the rank 0 0 case and a parity conjecture.

Section 6 is devoted to the proof of the so called first and second reciprocity laws. The first reciprocity law relates the residue of
\kappa_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathfrak{l})\in\widehat{\text{Sel}}_{\Delta\mathfrak{l}}(K_{\infty},T_{f,n})

to an element
\theta_{\infty}(\mathcal{D})\in\mathcal{O}_{f,n}[[\Gamma]]
. This element
\theta_{\infty}(\mathcal{D})\in\mathcal{O}_{f,n}[[\Gamma]]
is closely related to the theta element
\theta_{\infty}(f)
via congruences. For two admissible primes
\mathfrak{l}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{l}_{2}
, the second reciprocity law is the assertion that there exists an
n
-admissible form
\mathcal{D}^{\prime\prime}=(\Delta_{B}\mathfrak{l}_{1}\mathfrak{l}_{2},g^{\prime\prime})
such that
v_{\mathfrak{l}_{1}}(\kappa_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathfrak{l}_{2}))=v_{\mathfrak{l}_{2}}(\kappa_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathfrak{l}_{1}))=\theta_{\infty}(\mathcal{D}^{\prime\prime})
. Here
v_{\mathfrak{l}_{1}}(\kappa_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathfrak{l}_{2}))
(resp.

) is the image of
\kappa_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathfrak{l}_{2})
(resp.
\kappa_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathfrak{l}_{1})
) in the finite part of the local Galois cohomology group which is of rank
1
over
\Lambda
. The proof of the first reciprocity law is quite straightforward while the proof of the second reciprocity law relies on the "Ihara’s lemma" which we treat as Assumption
6.3
.

•

The first reciprocity law and second reciprocity law allow one to use an induction argument to prove the important Theorem
7.9
. We sketch the structure of the proof here. Let
\xi:\Lambda\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\xi}
be a character of the Iwasawa algebra of
\Gamma
over
\mathcal{O}_{f}
. For any
\mathcal{O}_{\xi}
module
M
and each

, we denote by
\text{ord}_{\varpi_{\xi}}(x)=\sup\{m\in\mathbf{Z}\cup\{\infty\}\mid x\in\varpi_{\xi}^{m}M\}
the divisibility index of
x
. For each positive integer

and
n
-admissible form
\mathcal{D}=(\Delta,f)
, we define two integers:

(1.1)

\displaystyle s_{\mathcal{D}}=\length_{\mathcal{O}_{\xi}}{\text{Sel}_{\Delta}(K_{\infty},A_{f,n})}^{\!\vee}\otimes_{\xi}\mathcal{O}_{\xi};

\displaystyle t_{\mathcal{D}}=\text{ord}_{\varpi_{\xi}}\xi(\theta_{\infty}(\mathcal{D}))\text{ where $\xi(\theta_{\infty}(\mathcal{D}))\in\mathcal{O}_{f,n}[[\Gamma]]\otimes_{\xi}\mathcal{O}_{\xi}=\mathcal{O}_{\xi}/\varpi_{\xi}^{n}$}.

The first one measures the length of the specialization of the Selmer group and the second one measures the divisibility of the specialization of the theta element. We would like to show
s_{\mathcal{D}}\leq 2t_{\mathcal{D}}
. We do induction on the number
t_{\mathcal{D}}
. It follows from the first reciprocity law that if
t_{\mathcal{D}}=0
, then
s_{\mathcal{D}}=0
and we have the base case. The underlying principle in the base case is the rank
0
case of the Bloch-Kato conjecture which we will treat in chapter 8. For the induction step, we choose a suitable
n
-admissible prime
\mathfrak{l}_{1}
and define

(1.2)

e_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathfrak{l}_{1})=\text{ord}_{\varpi_{\xi}}(\kappa_{\mathcal{D},\xi}(\mathfrak{l}_{1}))

which is the divisibility index of the specialization class
\kappa_{\mathcal{D},\xi}(\mathfrak{l}_{1})
in the Selmer group. By the choice of
\mathfrak{l}_{1}
, we have
e_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathfrak{l}_{1})<t_{\mathcal{D}}
. Now we choose a second
n
-admissible prime
\mathfrak{l}_{2}
and define an
n
-admissible form
\mathcal{D}^{\prime\prime}=(\Delta\mathfrak{l}_{1}\mathfrak{l}_{2},g)
. By carefully choosing the admissible prime
\mathfrak{l}_{2}
, we can show
t_{\mathcal{D}^{\prime\prime}}=e_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathfrak{l}_{1})<t_{\mathcal{D}}
using the second reciprocity law. Thus by the induction hypothesis, we have
s_{\mathcal{D}^{\prime\prime}}\leq 2t_{\mathcal{D}^{\prime\prime}}
. To finish the proof, we show
s_{\mathcal{D}}-s_{\mathcal{D}^{\prime\prime}}\leq 2(t_{\mathcal{D}}-t_{\mathcal{D}^{\prime\prime}})
by comparing the Selmer group ordinary at
\Delta
with the Selmer group ordinary at
\Delta\mathfrak{l}_{1}\mathfrak{l}_{2}
.

The induction argument is based on the following principle which plays a prominent role in the recent proof of the Kolyvagin’s conjecture by Wei Zhang
[
Zha14
]
: level raising will make the theta element more primitive and at the same time will bring down the rank of the Selmer group. The one divisibility of Iwasawa main conjecture follows directly from Theorem

2.1.
Galois cohomology

In this section we define several versions of Selmer groups that are related to the Galois representations attached
f
and prove some standard results about the behavior of the local cohomology over the tower of anticyclotomic extensions.
Let
L
be a finite algebraic extension of
F
and
M
be a discrete
G_{F}
module. For a place
\mathfrak{l}
in
F
, we define the local cohomology groups

H^{1}(L_{\mathfrak{l}},M)=\oplus_{\lambda\mid\mathfrak{l}}H^{1}(L_{\lambda},M)

where the sum is taken over the places in
L
above
\mathfrak{l}
. Similarly we define

H^{1}(I_{\mathfrak{l}},M)=\oplus_{\lambda\mid\mathfrak{l}}H^{1}(I_{{\lambda}},M)

for the inertia group
I_{\lambda}=I_{L_{\lambda}}
.
Let
\text{res}_{\mathfrak{l}}:H^{1}(L,M)\rightarrow H^{1}(L_{\mathfrak{l}},M)
be the restriction map. We define the finite part of the local cohomology group as

H^{1}_{{\rm fin}}(L_{\mathfrak{l}},M)=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\{H^{1}(L_{\mathfrak{l}},M)\rightarrow H^{1}(I_{\mathfrak{l}},M)\}

and the singular quotient as

H^{1}_{{\rm sing}}(L_{\mathfrak{l}},M)=H^{1}(L_{\mathfrak{l}},M)/H^{1}_{{\rm fin}}(L_{\mathfrak{l}},M).

We denote by
\partial_{\mathfrak{l}}
the residual map obtained from the composition of the restriction map with the natural quotient map

(2.1)

\partial_{\mathfrak{l}}:H^{1}(L,M)\rightarrow H^{1}(L_{\mathfrak{l}},M)\rightarrow H_{{\rm sing}}^{1}(L_{\mathfrak{l}},M).

2.2.
Selmer group

In this subsection, we define certain Slemer groups for the Hilbert modular form
f
. First we need to recall some properties of the Galois representation associated to
f
, since we are working in the ordinary setup, this representation is constructed in
[

]
. Let
\rho_{f}:G_{F}\rightarrow\mathop{\rm Aut}\nolimits_{E_{f}}(V_{f})
be the Galois representation attached to

and we fix a Galois stable lattice
T_{f}
in
V_{f}
and put

. Denote by
\rho^{*}_{f}=\rho_{f}\otimes\epsilon^{\frac{2-k}{2}}
the self dual twist of
\rho_{f}
. We collect the following properties of
\rho^{*}_{f}

cf.

[
Nek06
, Chapter 12]
:

(1)

\rho^{*}_{f}
is unramified outside of
\mathfrak{n}p
.

(2)

\rho^{*}_{f}\mid_{G_{F_{v}}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\chi^{-1}_{v}\epsilon^{\frac{k}{2}}&*\\
\O&\chi_{v}\epsilon^{\frac{2-k}{2}}\\
\end{array}\right)
for any

above

is an unramfied character such that
\chi_{v}(\mathrm{Frob}_{v})=\alpha_{v}(f)
with
\alpha_{v}(f)
the unit root of the Hecke polynomial of
f
at

.

(3)

\rho^{*}_{f}\mid_{G_{F_{\mathfrak{l}}}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\pm\epsilon&*\\
\O&\pm 1\\
\end{array}\right)
for any
\mathfrak{l}
dividing
\mathfrak{n}
exactly once.

Let
\varpi
be a uniformizer of
\mathcal{O}_{f}
we set:

(1)

\mathcal{O}_{f,n}=\mathcal{O}_{f}/\varpi^{n}
;

(2)

T_{f,n}=T_{f}/\varpi^{n}
;

(3)

A_{f,n}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\{A_{f}\smash{\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits^{\varpi^{n}}}A_{f}\}
.

Definition 2.1
.

A prime
\mathfrak{l}
in

is said to be

-admissible for

if

(1)

\mathfrak{l}\nmid p\mathfrak{n}
;

(2)

\mathfrak{l}
is inert in
K
;

(3)

{{\bf{N}}(\mathfrak{l})}^{2}-1
is not divisible by
p
;

(4)

\varpi^{n}
divides
{\bf{N}}(\mathfrak{l})^{\frac{k}{2}}+{\bf{N}}(\mathfrak{l})^{\frac{k-2}{2}}-\epsilon_{\mathfrak{l}}\mathbf{a}_{\mathfrak{l}}(f)
where
\epsilon_{\mathfrak{l}}\in\{\pm 1\}
.

Let
L/K
be a finite extension, we now define
F^{+}_{\mathfrak{l}}A_{f,n}
and
H^{1}_{\text{ord}}(L_{\mathfrak{l}},A_{f,n})
for a prime
\mathfrak{l}
of
F
which divides
p\mathfrak{n}^{-}
or is
n
-admissible.

2.2.1.
\mathfrak{l}=v\mid p

We have
0\smash{\mathop{\longrightarrow}}{F^{+}_{v}V_{f}}\smash{\mathop{\longrightarrow}}{V_{f}}\smash{\mathop{\longrightarrow}}{F^{-}_{v}V_{f}}\smash{\mathop{\longrightarrow}}0
where

is the subspace that
G_{F_{v}}
acts by
\chi^{-1}_{v}\epsilon^{\frac{k}{2}}
as in the introduction. We take

in

and

.

2.3.
\mathfrak{l}\mid\mathfrak{n}^{-}

In this case we let

to be the subspace of

such that
G_{F_{\mathfrak{l}}}
acts by
\pm\epsilon
. Then

are defined similarly as above

2.3.1.
\mathfrak{l}
is

-admissible

Since

acts by

Definition 2.2
.

In all three cases we will define the ordinary cohomology group by

H^{1}_{\rm ord}(L_{\mathfrak{l}},A_{f,n})=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\{H^{1}(L_{\mathfrak{l}},A_{f,n})\rightarrow H^{1}(L_{\mathfrak{l}},A_{f,n}/F^{+}_{\mathfrak{l}}A_{f,n})\}.

2.4.
Anticyclotomic tower and Selmer groups

Let
m
be a non-negative integer. Denote by
H_{m}
the ring class field of conductor
{\mathfrak{p}}^{m}
over
K
. Let
G_{m}=\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(H_{m}/K)
and
H_{\infty}=\cup_{m=1}^{\infty}H_{m}
. Let
K_{\infty}
be the unique subfield of
H_{\infty}
such that
\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(K_{\infty}/K)\simeq\mathbf{Z}_{p}^{d_{{\mathfrak{p}}}}
where
d_{{\mathfrak{p}}}
is the inertia degree of
{\mathfrak{p}}
and we call
K_{\infty}
the
{\mathfrak{p}}
-anticyclotomic
\mathbf{Z}_{p}^{d_{{\mathfrak{p}}}}
-extension over
K
. We put
\Gamma=\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(K_{\infty}/K)
. Let
K_{m}=K_{\infty}\cap H_{m}
be the
m
-th layer of this tower of extensions and
\Gamma_{m}=\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(K_{m}/K)
so that
\Gamma=\varprojlim_{m}\Gamma_{m}
. Let
\Lambda=\mathcal{O}_{f}[[\Gamma]]
be the Iwasawa algebra over
\mathcal{O}_{f}
and
\mathfrak{m}_{\Lambda}
be its maximal ideal.

Let

H^{1}(K_{\infty},A_{f,n})=\varinjlim_{m}H^{1}(K_{m},A_{f,n})

where the limit is with respect to the restriction maps and

\widehat{H}^{1}(K_{\infty},T_{f,n})=\varprojlim_{m}H^{1}(K_{m},T_{f,n})

where the limit is with respect to the corestriction(norm) maps.

We can define various local cohomology groups
H^{1}_{*}(K_{\infty,\mathfrak{l}},A_{f,n})
and
\widehat{H}^{1}_{*}(K_{\infty,\mathfrak{l}},T_{f,n})
in the similar fashion. Here
*
can be any of the finite, singular or ordinary structure defined above. Now we can define

\text{Sel}^{S}_{\Delta}(K_{\infty},A_{f,n})=\varinjlim_{m}\text{Sel}^{S}_{\Delta}(K_{m},A_{f,n}),

\widehat{\text{Sel}}^{S}_{\Delta}(K_{\infty},T_{f,n})=\varprojlim_{m}\text{Sel}_{\Delta}^{S}(K_{m},T_{f,n}).

Recall we have defined the minimal Selmer group
\text{Sel}(K_{\infty},M)
in the introduction.

Proposition 2.4
.

Assume
(\text{CR}^{+})
and
(\mathfrak{n}^{+}\text{-DT})
hold. Then
\text{Sel}(K_{\infty},A_{f})
is of finite index in

\varinjlim_{n}\text{Sel}_{\mathfrak{n}^{-}}(K_{\infty},A_{f,n}).

If furthermore
\text{Sel}_{\mathfrak{n}^{-}}(K_{\infty},A_{f})
is
\Lambda
-cotorsion, then they are equal to each other.

Proof.

This follows from the proof of
[
PW11
, Proposition 3.6]
. The exact same computation shows that
\text{Sel}_{\mathfrak{n}^{-}}(K_{\infty},A_{f,n})
is of finite index in
\text{Sel}(K_{\infty},A_{f})[\varpi^{n}]
with index independent of
n
, then the result follows from the fact that
\text{Sel}(K_{\infty},A_{f})
has no finite index submodule if it is
\Lambda
-cotorsion.
∎

2.5.
Properties of local Iwasawa cohomologies

In this subsection we prove some standard results on the local cohomology groups defined before.
Since we know that
A_{f,n}
and
T_{f,n}
are Cartier dual to each other, we have the following local Tate pairing for each prime
\mathfrak{l}
in
K_{m}

\langle,\rangle_{\mathfrak{l}}:H^{1}(K_{m,\mathfrak{l}},T_{f,n})\times H^{1}(K_{m,\mathfrak{l}},A_{f,n})\rightarrow E_{f}/\mathcal{O}_{f}.

Taking limit with respect to
m
, we have

\langle,\rangle_{\mathfrak{l}}:\widehat{H}^{1}(K_{\infty,\mathfrak{l}},T_{f,n})\times H^{1}(K_{\infty,\mathfrak{l}},A_{f,n})\rightarrow E_{f}/\mathcal{O}_{f}.

The following lemma is well-known.

Lemma 2.5
.

Suppose
\mathfrak{l}\nmid p
,
H^{1}_{{\rm fin}}(K_{\infty,\mathfrak{l}},A_{f,n})
and
\widehat{H}^{1}_{{\rm fin}}(K_{\infty,\mathfrak{l}},T_{f,n})
are orthogonal complement to each other under the pairing
\langle,\rangle_{\mathfrak{l}}
.

Proof.

This is a standard result. See
[
Rub00
, Proposition 1.4.2, 1.4.3]
.
∎

It follows from the above lemma that
H^{1}_{{\rm fin}}(K_{\infty,\mathfrak{l}},A_{f,n})
and
\widehat{H}^{1}_{{\rm sing}}(K_{\infty,\mathfrak{l}},T_{f,n})
are dual to each other under the above pairing.

One of the differences between the cyclotomic Iwasawa theory and the anticyclotomic Iwasawa theory is the existence of primes that split completely in the
\mathbf{Z}_{p}
-extension and as a consequence we have the following lemma.

Lemma 2.6
.

Suppose
\mathfrak{l}\nmid p
is a prime in
F
. Then

(1)

if
\mathfrak{l}
splits in
K
, then
H^{1}_{\rm fin}(K_{\infty,\mathfrak{l}},A_{f,n})=\{0\}
and hence
\widehat{H}^{1}_{\rm sing}(K_{\infty,\mathfrak{l}},T_{f,n})=\{0\}
.

(2)

If
\mathfrak{l}
is inert in
K
, then
\widehat{H}^{1}_{\rm sing}(K_{\infty,\mathfrak{l}},T_{f,n})\simeq H^{1}(K_{\mathfrak{l}},T_{f,n})\otimes\Lambda
and it follows that
{H}^{1}_{\rm fin}(K_{\infty,\mathfrak{l}},A_{f,n})\simeq\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits(\widehat{H}^{1}_{\rm sing}(K_{\mathfrak{l}},T_{f,n})\otimes\Lambda,{E_{f}}/{\mathcal{O}_{f}})
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathfrak{l}=\mathfrak{l}_{1}\mathfrak{l}_{2}
in
K
, then the Frobenius element in
\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(K_{\infty}/K)
above
\mathfrak{l}_{i}
is non-trivial. Hence it generates a subgroup of finite index in
G_{\infty}
and
K_{\infty,\mathfrak{l}_{i}}
is a finite product of unramified
\mathbf{Z}_{p}
extension of
K_{\mathfrak{l}_{i}}
. Then
(1)
follows because the pro-
p
part of
\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(K^{ur}_{\mathfrak{l}_{i}}/K_{\infty,\mathfrak{l}_{i}})
is trivial by definition.
If
\mathfrak{l}
is inert in
K
, then by class field theory,
\mathfrak{l}
splits completely in the tower
K_{\infty}/K
. The conclusion of
(2)
follows easily from Shapiro’s lemma.
∎

For
n
-admissible primes, we have a simple decomposition of the local cohomologies.

Lemma 2.7
.

If
\mathfrak{l}
is
n
-admissible, then

(1)

both
\widehat{H}^{1}_{\rm sing}(K_{\infty,\mathfrak{l}},T_{f,n})
and
\widehat{H}^{1}_{\rm fin}(K_{\infty,\mathfrak{l}},T_{f,n}
) are free of rank one over
\Lambda/\varpi^{n}\Lambda
.

(2)

We have the following decompositions:

\widehat{H}^{1}(K_{\infty,\mathfrak{l}},T_{f,n})=\widehat{H}^{1}_{\rm fin}(K_{\infty,\mathfrak{l}},T_{f,n})\oplus\widehat{H}^{1}_{\rm ord}(K_{\infty,\mathfrak{l}},T_{f,n})

and

H^{1}(K_{\infty,\mathfrak{l}},A_{f,n})=H^{1}_{\rm fin}(K_{\infty,\mathfrak{l}},A_{f,n})\oplus H^{1}_{\rm ord}(K_{\infty,\mathfrak{l}},A_{f,n}).

Proof.

By the definition of
n
-admissible primes, we have a decomposition
A_{f,n}=F^{+}_{\mathfrak{l}}A_{f,n}\oplus F^{-}_{\mathfrak{l}}A_{f,n}
. Hence it induces a decomposition
H^{1}(K_{\mathfrak{l}},A_{f,n})=H^{1}(K_{\mathfrak{l}},F^{+}_{\mathfrak{l}}A_{f,n})\oplus H^{1}(K_{\mathfrak{l}},F^{-}_{\mathfrak{l}}A_{f,n})
. By definition, we have
H^{1}_{\text{ord}}(K_{\mathfrak{l}},A_{f,n})=H^{1}(K_{\mathfrak{l}},F^{+}_{\mathfrak{l}}A_{f,n})
. On the other hand, we have

H^{1}_{\rm fin}(K_{\mathfrak{l}},A_{f,n})=F^{-}_{\mathfrak{l}}A_{f,n}/(\mathrm{Frob}_{\mathfrak{l}}-1)F^{-}_{\mathfrak{l}}A_{f,n}=F^{-}_{\mathfrak{l}}A_{f,n}

since
\mathfrak{l}
is
n
-admissible and
\mathrm{Frob}_{\mathfrak{l}}
acts trivially on
F^{-}_{\mathfrak{l}}A_{f,n}
. The claim then follows from the fact that
H^{1}(K_{\mathfrak{l}},F^{-}_{\mathfrak{l}}A_{f,n})=F^{-}_{\mathfrak{l}}A_{f,n}
by local Tate duality and Kummer theory. The rest of the lemma then follows immediately.
∎

Proposition 2.8
.

Assume
(\text{CR}^{+})
,
(\mathfrak{n}^{+}\text{-DT})
and
(\text{PO}
) hold. Then for
\mathfrak{l}
an
n
-admissible prime or a prime dividing
p\mathfrak{n}^{-}
,
H^{1}_{\text{ord}}(K_{\infty,\mathfrak{l}},A_{f,n})
and
\widehat{H}^{1}_{\text{ord}}(K_{\infty,\mathfrak{l}},T_{f,n})
are orthogonal complement to each other.

Proof.

We will treat three different cases.

(
\mathfrak{l}
is admissible) In this case the claim follows directly from the previous lemmas.

(
\mathfrak{l}\mid\mathfrak{n}^{-}
) By definition we have an exact sequence

(2.2)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\smash{\mathop{\longrightarrow}}H^{1}_{\text{ord}}(K_{\mathfrak{l}},A_{f,n})\smash{\mathop{\longrightarrow}}H^{1}(K_{\mathfrak{l}},A_{f,n})\smash{\mathop{\longrightarrow}}H^{1}(K_{\mathfrak{l}},A_{f,n}/F^{+}_{\mathfrak{l}}A_{f,n})\smash{\mathop{\longrightarrow}}

\displaystyle H^{2}(K_{\mathfrak{l}},F^{+}_{\mathfrak{l}}A_{f,n})\smash{\mathop{\longrightarrow}}H^{2}(K_{\mathfrak{l}},A_{f,n})\smash{\mathop{\longrightarrow}}H^{2}(K_{\mathfrak{l}},A_{f,n}/F^{+}_{\mathfrak{l}}A_{f,n})\smash{\mathop{\longrightarrow}}0.

It follows from this exact sequence and the local Euler characteristic formula,
|H^{1}(K_{\mathfrak{l}},A_{f,n})|=|A_{f,n}^{G_{K_{\mathfrak{l}}}}|^{2}
and
|H^{1}_{\text{ord}}(K_{\mathfrak{l}},A_{f,n})|=|A_{f,n}^{G_{K_{\mathfrak{l}}}}|.
This means we only need to prove the ordinary cohomology groups are orthogonal to each other.

By definition, we have an exact sequence

0\smash{\mathop{\longrightarrow}}H^{1}_{{\rm fin}}(K_{\mathfrak{l}},F^{+}_{\mathfrak{l}}A_{f,n})\smash{\mathop{\longrightarrow}}H^{1}(K_{\mathfrak{l}},F^{+}_{\mathfrak{l}}A_{f,n})\smash{\mathop{\longrightarrow}}H^{1}_{{\rm sing}}(K_{\mathfrak{l}},F^{+}_{\mathfrak{l}}A_{f,n})\smash{\mathop{\longrightarrow}}0.

When
{\bf{N}}(\mathfrak{l})^{2}\not\equiv 1\,(\mbox{mod }{\varpi^{n}})
,
H^{1}(K_{\mathfrak{l}},F^{+}_{\mathfrak{l}}A_{f,n})
annihilates
H^{1}(K_{\mathfrak{l}},F^{+}_{\mathfrak{l}}T_{f,n})
since they pair to
H^{2}(K_{\mathfrak{l}},\mathcal{O}_{f,n}(\epsilon^{2}))=0
. As
H^{1}_{\text{ord}}(K_{\mathfrak{l}},A_{f,n})
(resp.
H^{1}_{\text{ord}}(K_{\mathfrak{l}},T_{f,n})
) is contained in the image of
H^{1}(K_{\mathfrak{l}},F^{+}_{\mathfrak{l}}A_{f,n})
(resp.
H^{1}_{\text{ord}}(K_{\mathfrak{l}},T_{f,n})
), the claim in this case follows.

When
{\bf{N}}(\mathfrak{l})^{2}\equiv 1\,(\mbox{mod }{\varpi^{n}})
, a direct calculation shows that
H^{1}_{\text{ord}}(K_{\mathfrak{l}},A_{f,n})=H^{1}_{{\rm fin}}(K_{\mathfrak{l}},A_{f,n})
(resp.
H^{1}_{\text{ord}}(K_{\mathfrak{l}},T_{f,n})=H^{1}_{{\rm fin}}(K_{\mathfrak{l}},T_{f,n})
). In fact, we can show that both
H^{1}_{\text{ord}}(K_{\mathfrak{l}},A_{f,n})
and
H^{1}_{{\rm fin}}(K_{\mathfrak{l}},A_{f,n})
are of rank
1
over
\mathcal{O}_{f,n}
and there is an injection
H^{1}_{\text{ord}}(K_{\mathfrak{l}},A_{f,n})\hookrightarrow H^{1}_{{\rm fin}}(K_{\mathfrak{l}},A_{f,n})
. The orthogonality then follows from Lemma
2.5
.

(
\mathfrak{l}=v\mid p
)
Let
L/K
be a finite extension in
K_{\infty}
.
We need the following lemma.

Lemma 2.9
.

For
v
a place of
L
above
p
, we have
H^{0}(L_{v},F^{-}_{v}A_{f,n})=0
.

Proof.

Notice the cyclotomic character
\epsilon
is surjective on

and hence
F^{-}_{v}A_{f,n}=A_{f,n}/F^{+}_{v}A_{f,n}=\mathcal{O}_{n}(\chi_{v}\epsilon^{1-\frac{k}{2}})
has no fixed part by

unless

. In the case

(F^{-}_{v}A_{f,n})^{G_{L_{v}}}=(\mathcal{O}_{n}(\chi_{v}))^{\langle\mathrm{Frob}_{v}\rangle}
and
\chi_{v}(\mathrm{Frob}_{v})=\alpha_{v}^{p^{s}r_{v}}
. Here

is the inertia degree of
L
over
K
at
v
and

depending on whether
v
splits in
K
. It follows from our assumption (
PO
) that
\alpha_{v}^{r_{v}p^{s}}
is not

. Then we can conclude that

∎

By the definition of the ordinary cohomology and Lemma

The same statement holds if we replace

above by

. The claim in the case

2.6.
Control theorems of Selmer groups

Recall
S
and
\Delta
as in Definition
2.3
and let
L/K
be a finite extension contained in
K_{\infty}
.

Proposition 2.10
.

Assume
(\text{CR}^{+})
,
(\mathfrak{n}^{+}\text{-min})
and
(\text{PO})

hold, then

(1)

the restriction maps

H^{1}(K,A_{f,n})\rightarrow H^{1}(L,A_{f,n})^{\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(L/K)}

and

\text{Sel}_{\Delta}^{S}(K,A_{f,n})\rightarrow\text{Sel}_{\Delta}^{S}(L,A_{f,n})^{\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(L/K)}

are isomorphisms;

(2)

\text{Sel}^{S}_{\Delta}(L,A_{f,n})=\text{Sel}^{S}_{\Delta}(L,A_{f})[\varpi^{n}]
.

Proof.

The first claim in part (1) follows from
(\text{CR}^{+})(1)
and the inflation-restriction exact sequence since by
(\text{CR}^{+})(1)
,
H^{0}(L,A_{f,n})=0
.

By the definition of the Selmer group and an application of the snake lemma, it suffices to show the following claims in order to prove the rest of the assertions in (1):

(1)

H^{1}(K^{\text{ur}}_{\mathfrak{l}},A_{f,n})\smash{\mathop{\longrightarrow}}H^{1}(L^{\text{ur}}_{\mathfrak{l}},A_{f,n})
is injective for
\mathfrak{l}\nmid p\Delta
;

(2)

H^{1}(K_{\mathfrak{l}},F^{-}_{\mathfrak{l}}A_{f,n})\smash{\mathop{\longrightarrow}}H^{1}(L_{\mathfrak{l}},F^{-}_{\mathfrak{l}}A_{f,n})
is injective for
\mathfrak{l}\mid p\Delta
.

When
\mathfrak{l}\nmid p\Delta
or
\mathfrak{l}\mid\Delta
, the first claim is clear as
K_{\infty}
is unramified outside
{\mathfrak{p}}
and hence
K^{ur}_{\mathfrak{l}}=L^{ur}_{\mathfrak{l}}
.
If
\mathfrak{l}=v\mid p
, then by the inflation-restriction exact sequence, we only need to show
F^{-}_{v}A_{f,n}^{G_{L_{\mathfrak{P}}}}=0
for
\mathfrak{P}
a place above
v
. This is Lemma
2.9
and this concludes the proof of (1).

As for (2), we consider the following exact sequence

0\smash{\mathop{\longrightarrow}}A_{f,n}\smash{\mathop{\longrightarrow}}A_{f}\smash{\mathop{\longrightarrow}}A_{f}\smash{\mathop{\longrightarrow}}0.

By
(\text{CR}^{+})(1)
and the fact that
K_{\infty}/K
is Galois, we have
A^{G_{L}}_{f,m}=0
for every
m
. Then
A_{f}^{G_{L}}=0
. Hence by the inflation-restriction exact sequence again, the map

\text{Sel}^{S}_{\Delta}(L,A_{f,n})\smash{\mathop{\longrightarrow}}\text{Sel}^{S}_{\Delta}(L,A_{f})[\varpi^{n}]

is injective. To show surjectivity, by a similar consideration as in (1), we are led to prove the following claims:

•

H^{1}(L^{\text{ur}}_{\mathfrak{l}},A_{f,n})\smash{\mathop{\longrightarrow}}H^{1}(L^{\text{ur}}_{\mathfrak{l}},A_{f})
is injective for
\mathfrak{l}\nmid p\Delta
.

•

H^{1}(L_{\mathfrak{l}},F^{-}_{\mathfrak{l}}A_{f,n})\smash{\mathop{\longrightarrow}}H^{1}(L_{\mathfrak{l}},F^{-}_{\mathfrak{l}}A_{f})
is injective for
\mathfrak{l}\mid p\Delta
.

For the first claim, if
\mathfrak{l}\nmid\mathfrak{n}^{+}
,then the Galois action is trivial and the claim follows immediately. In the case
\mathfrak{l}\mid\mathfrak{n}^{+}
, the claim follows from our assumption in
(2)
since then
H^{0}(L^{\text{ur}}_{\mathfrak{l}},A_{f})
is divisible.

We prove the second. For
\mathfrak{l}\mid\Delta
, the action of
G_{L_{\mathfrak{l}}}
is trivial and hence the claim follows immediately. For
\mathfrak{l}=v\mid p
, we have proved in Lemma
2.9
that
H^{0}(L_{v},F^{-}_{v}A_{f,m})=0
for any
m
and hence
H^{0}(L_{v},F^{-}_{v}A_{f})=0
. Therefore we have the injectivity in the case
\mathfrak{l}=v\mid p
.

∎

3.
Automorphic forms on quaternion algebra

In this section we define the notion of automorphic forms on quaternion algebra over a totally real field. In order to use the congruences between Hecke eigenvalues of the Hilbert modular forms, by the Jaquet-Langlands correspondence it suffices to study the Hecke eigenvalues of these quaternionic automorphic forms.

3.1.
Hecke algebra on quaternion algebras

Let
B
be a quaternion algebra over the totally real field
F
and
\widehat{B}=B\otimes\hat{\mathbf{Z}}
. Set
\Delta_{B}
to be the discriminant of
B
over
F
. Let
\Sigma
be a finite set of places of
F
. We let

\widehat{B}^{(\Sigma),\times}=\{x\in\widehat{B}^{\times}:x_{v}=1,\forall v\in\Sigma\}

and

\widehat{B}^{\times}_{(\Sigma)}=\{x\in\widehat{B}^{\times}:x_{v}=1,\forall v\not\in\Sigma\}.

A similar notation is used for any open compact subset
\mathcal{U}
of
\widehat{B}^{\times}
.

Let
\mathcal{U}
be an open compact subgroup of
\widehat{B}^{\times}
and
\mathcal{U}_{v}
be its component at
v
. Let
{\mathcal{H}}(B^{\times},\mathcal{U})

\mathbf{Z}
on
\widehat{B}^{\times}
which is the space of
\mathbf{Z}
-valued bi-
\mathcal{U}
invariant functions on
\widehat{B}^{\times}
with compact support. Then
{\mathcal{H}}(B^{\times},\mathcal{U})
is equipped with the natural involution product:
(f*g)(z)=\int_{\widehat{B}^{\times}}f(x)g({x}^{-1}z)dx
where
dx
is the normalized Haar measure such that
\mathop{\rm vol}\nolimits(\mathcal{U},dx)=1
.

For
x\in\widehat{B}^{\times}
, we denote by
[\mathcal{U}x\mathcal{U}]
the characteristic function of the double coset
\mathcal{U}x\mathcal{U}
. For each finite place
v
of
F
, we set

\pi_{v}=\begin{pmatrix}{\varpi_{v}}&{0}\\
{0}&{1}\end{pmatrix},z_{v}=\begin{pmatrix}{\varpi_{v}}&{0}\\
{0}&{\varpi_{v}}\end{pmatrix}

as elements in
\mathop{\rm GL}\nolimits_{2}(F_{v})
. We fix an identification
i:\widehat{B}^{(\Delta_{B}),\times}\simeq M_{2}(\widehat{F}^{(\Delta_{B})})^{\times}
.

Let

\mathfrak{m}^{+}\subset\mathcal{O}_{F}

be an integral ideal which is prime to
\Delta_{B}
and let
R_{\mathfrak{m}^{+}}
be an Eichler order(intersection of two maximal orders) of level
\mathfrak{m}^{+}
. Note locally at
v\mid\mathfrak{m}^{+}
with
v^{t}\mid\mid\mathfrak{m}^{+}
,
(R_{\mathfrak{m}^{+}})_{v}=\{\begin{pmatrix}{a}&{b}\\
{c}&{d}\end{pmatrix}\in M_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{F,v}):c\equiv 0\,(\mbox{mod }{\varpi^{t}_{v}})\}
. Suppose
(p,\Delta_{B})=1
. For a non-negative integer
n
and our fixed
{\mathfrak{p}}
, we define a compact open subgroup
\mathcal{U}_{\mathfrak{m}^{+},{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}}
by:

(3.1)

\mathcal{U}_{\mathfrak{m}^{+},{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}}=\{x\in\widehat{R}^{\times}_{\mathfrak{m}^{+}}:x_{{\mathfrak{p}}}\equiv\begin{pmatrix}{a}&{b}\\
{0}&{a}\end{pmatrix}\,(\mbox{mod }{{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}}),a,b\in\mathcal{O}_{F,{\mathfrak{p}}}\}.

We set
\mathcal{U}=\mathcal{U}_{\mathfrak{m}^{+},{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}}
and put
\Sigma={\mathfrak{p}}\mathfrak{m}^{+}\Delta_{B}
. Notice by definition
i
induces an identification
i:(\widehat{B}^{(\Sigma),\times},\mathcal{U}^{(\Sigma)}_{\mathfrak{m}^{+},{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}})\simeq(\mathop{\rm GL}\nolimits_{2}(\widehat{F}^{(\Sigma)}),\mathop{\rm GL}\nolimits_{2}(\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{F}^{(\Sigma)}))
.

3.1.1.
Spherical Hecke algebra

We denote by
{\mathbb{T}}^{(\Sigma)}_{B}(\mathfrak{m}^{+})
the subalgebra of
{\mathcal{H}}(B^{\times},\mathcal{U})
generated by
[\mathcal{U}x\mathcal{U}]
for

. Then we have

{\mathbb{T}}^{(\Sigma)}_{B}(\mathfrak{m}^{+})=\mathbf{Z}[\{T_{v},S_{v},S_{v}^{-1}:v\not\in\Sigma\}],

where

(3.2)

T_{v}=[\mathcal{U}i^{-1}(\pi_{v})\mathcal{U}],S_{v}=[\mathcal{U}i^{-1}(z_{v})\mathcal{U}].

3.1.2.
Complete Hecke algebra

For
v\mid\Delta_{B}
, we choose an element
\pi^{\prime}_{v}\in B^{\times}_{v}
whose norm is a uniformizer of
F_{v}
and put

U_{v}=[\mathcal{U}\pi^{\prime}_{v}\mathcal{U}].

For
v\mid\mathfrak{m}^{+}
, we define

U_{v}=[\mathcal{U}i^{-1}(\pi_{v})\mathcal{U}].

Finally for the prime
{\mathfrak{p}}

U_{{\mathfrak{p}}}=[\mathcal{U}i^{-1}(\pi_{{\mathfrak{p}}})\mathcal{U}],

\langle a\rangle=[\mathcal{U}i^{-1}({\bf d}(a))\mathcal{U}]

where
{\bf d}(a)=\begin{pmatrix}{a}&{0}\\
{0}&{1}\end{pmatrix}
for

.

If
n=0
, we have
\mathcal{U}=\widehat{R}^{\times}_{\mathfrak{m}^{+}}
, then we let

{\mathbb{T}}_{B}(\mathfrak{m}^{+})={\mathbb{T}}^{(\Sigma)}_{B}(\mathfrak{m}^{+})[\{U_{v}:v\mid\Delta_{B}\mathfrak{m}^{+}\}].

If
n>0
, we have
\mathcal{U}=\mathcal{U}_{\mathfrak{m}^{+},{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}}

{\mathbb{T}}_{B}(\mathfrak{m}^{+},{\mathfrak{p}}^{n})={\mathbb{T}}^{(\Sigma)}_{B}(\mathfrak{m}^{+})[\{U_{v}:v\mid{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}\Delta_{B}\mathfrak{m}^{+}\},\{\langle a\rangle:a\in\mathcal{O}^{\times}_{F,{\mathfrak{p}}}\}].

We refer to these as the complete Hecke algebras. And for
\mathfrak{l}
a prime in
\mathcal{O}_{F}

.

Optimal embeddings

Let

3.3.
P
-adic Hilbert modular forms on quaternion algebra

Let
\Phi
be a finite extension of

that contains the image of all embeddings

. Let

be an
\mathcal{O}_{\Phi}

be an even integer and for each

Let

\displaystyle[\mathcal{U}x\mathcal{U}]f(b)=\sum_{u\in\mathcal{U}/\mathcal{U}\cap x\mathcal{U}x^{-1}}f(bux)\text{ for }x\in\widehat{B}^{({\mathfrak{p}}),\times},

6.
Explicit reciprocity laws

In this chapter we explain the relationship between Heegner point and the theta element. While the theta element interpolates
L
-values, Heegner points give rise to elements in the Selmer group. The relationship allows us to bound the Selmer group in terms of
L
-values. The coexistence of Heegner point and theta element is a reflection of the parity of the Selmer group and is a special case of the so called bipartite Euler system
[

]
.

6.1.
The first explicit reciprocity law

Let

Lemma 6.1
.

If we denote the quotient map by

\pi_{m+1,m}(\Theta_{m+1}(\mathcal{D}))=\Theta_{m}(\mathcal{D}).

This follows from the exact same computation in Lemma
5.8
and the fact that

is a
U_{{\mathfrak{p}}}
-eigenform.
∎

Let

By the commutativity of the diagram in Theorem

It follows from the above discussion that

\displaystyle v_{\mathfrak{l}_{2}}(\kappa_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathfrak{l}_{1})_{m})

7.1.
Special elements in the Galois representation

Throughout this section we will assume the assumptions
PO
,
(\text{CR}^{+})
and
(\mathfrak{n}^{+}\text{-min})
. Let
H=\bar{\rho}^{*}_{f}(G_{F})
be the image of the residual Galois representation. We examine the existence of certain special elements in the image. We have the following lemma.

Lemma 7.1
.

The group
H
contains

(1)

the scalar matrix
-1
;

(2)

an element
h\in H
such that
\mathop{\rm Tr}\nolimits(h)=\det(h)+1
with
\det(h)\neq\pm 1\in\mathbb{F}_{{\mathfrak{p}}}
.

Proof.

For (1), let
Z
be the center of
H
. From
(\text{CR}^{+})(1)
, we note that
Z
is contained in the set of scalar matrices. If
p\mid\#H
, then it is well-known that
H
contains a conjugate of
\mathop{\rm SL}\nolimits_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
and the result follows. So we assume that
H
has order prime to
p
but then
H/Z
has the following possibilities:

H/Z
is cyclic. This is impossible in view of
(\text{CR}^{+})(1)
.

H/Z
is the three exceptional groups
A_{4},S_{4},A_{5}
. However if we let
I_{{\mathfrak{p}}}
be the image of the inertia at
{\mathfrak{p}}
in
H/Z
, then we know it is a cyclic group of order
>5
by the assumption that
\#(\mathbb{F}^{\times}_{{\mathfrak{p}}})^{k-1}>5
. Then these possibilities can not happen.

H/Z
is the dihedral group. Since
\#(\mathbb{F}^{\times}_{{\mathfrak{p}}})^{k-1}
is even,
I_{{\mathfrak{p}}}
is contained in a cyclic group of even order and hence there is an element of order
2
in
H/Z
. We can then conclude that there is an element of order
2
in
Z
.

For (2), if
p\nmid{\bf{N}}(\mathfrak{l})^{2}-1
for some
\mathfrak{l}\mid\mathfrak{n}^{-}
, then we can simply choose
\mathrm{Frob}_{\mathfrak{l}}
as our element
h
. If this is not the case, then by our assumption
\text{CR}^{+}(3)
,
\bar{\rho}^{*}_{f}(G_{F})
is ramified at
\mathfrak{l}
and hence
H
contains the image of the inertia which is a subgroup of order divisible by
p
. Therefore
H/Z
contains the group
\mathop{\rm PSL}\nolimits_{2}(F)
for some finite extension

and is contained in
\mathop{\rm PGL}\nolimits_{2}(F)
. Therefore
[\mathop{\rm SL}\nolimits_{2}(F),\mathop{\rm SL}\nolimits_{2}(F)]=\mathop{\rm SL}\nolimits_{2}(F)
is contained in
H
. We take
C=\mathop{\rm GL}\nolimits_{2}(F)\cap H
. Then
C
sits in between
\mathop{\rm SL}\nolimits_{2}(F)
and
\mathop{\rm GL}\nolimits_{2}(F)
. Let
\gamma\in H
be the image of a generator of the inertia group at
{\mathfrak{p}}
. Then we have
\det(\gamma)=u
generates
\mathbb{F}^{\times}_{{\mathfrak{p}}}
and moreover
\mathop{\rm Tr}\nolimits(\gamma)=u^{k/2}+u^{1-k/2}\in\mathbb{F}_{{\mathfrak{p}}}
is non-zero. By the previous discussion, we have
H\subset\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{{\mathfrak{p}}}\mathop{\rm GL}\nolimits_{2}(F)
. We conclude from
\mathop{\rm Tr}\nolimits(\gamma)=u^{k/2}+u^{1-k/2}\in\mathbb{F}_{{\mathfrak{p}}}
that
\gamma\in C
. Thus
C\supset\mathbb{F}_{{\mathfrak{p}}}SL_{2}(F)
. The result follows as then
H
contains
\mathop{\rm GL}\nolimits_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{{\mathfrak{p}}})
.
∎

As a consequence of the above two lemmas, we prove the following key theorem for our Euler system argument.

8.
Some arithmetic consequences

Let
L
be a number field and
V
be a representation of the Galois group
G_{L}
over some
p
-adic field
E
. Recall that the Bloch-Kato Selmer group
\text{Sel}_{f}(L,V)
is defined by

\text{Sel}_{f}(L,V)=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\{H^{1}(L,V)\rightarrow\prod_{v}H^{1}(L_{v},V)/H^{1}_{f}(L_{v},V)\}

where

(8.1)

\displaystyle H^{1}_{f}(L_{v},V)=H^{1}_{{\rm fin}}(L_{v},V)\text{ when $v\nmid p$},

\displaystyle H^{1}_{f}(L_{v},V)=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\{H^{1}(L_{v},V)\rightarrow H^{1}(L_{v},V\otimes_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}}B_{\rm cris})\}\text{ for $v\mid p$}.

The Bloch-Kato conjecture relates the rank of the Selmer group and the order of vanishing of the
L
-function attached to the Galois representation. In this section we prove certain cases of the refined type Bloch-Kato conjecture in the rank zero case.

8.1.
Bloch-Kato type conjecture and parity conjecture

In this chapter, we denote by
H_{{\mathfrak{c}}}
the ring class field of K with conductor
{\mathfrak{c}}
. Let
\chi
be a finite order character of
\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(H_{{\mathfrak{c}}}/K)
, we consider the twisted Bloch-Kato Selmer group
\text{Sel}_{f}(K,V_{f}\otimes\chi)
. We have a canonical isomorphism

\text{Sel}_{f}(K,V_{f}\otimes\chi)\simeq\text{Sel}_{f}(H_{{\mathfrak{c}}},V_{f})^{\chi^{-1}}

where the righthand side is the
\chi^{-1}
-isotypic component of
\text{Sel}_{f}(K,V_{f})
for the action of
\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(H_{{\mathfrak{c}}}/K)
. The complex conjugation of
\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(H_{{\mathfrak{c}}}/K)
induces an isomorphism between

\text{Sel}_{f}(H_{{\mathfrak{c}}},V_{f})^{\chi^{-1}}\text{ and }\text{Sel}_{f}(H_{{\mathfrak{c}}},V_{f})^{\chi}.

The Bloch-Kato conjecture relates the order of the
L
-function
L(f/K,\chi,k/2)
to the rank of these Selmer groups, more precisely we expect:

\text{ord}_{s=k/2}L(f/K,\chi,s)=\text{rank }\text{Sel}_{f}(K,V_{f,\chi}).

In this section we provide some results in this direction. In particular we prove the so called parity conjecture in this setup.

First we provide the following proposition which relates the divisibility index of the theta element to the size of the Selmer group studied in this paper. We remark that the proof of this result only depends on the first reciprocity law in Theorem
6.2
and in particular we don’t have to assume "Ihara’s lemma". Let
\chi
be a finite order character of
\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(K_{m}/K)
and let
\varpi_{\chi}
be the uniformizer for the ring generated over
\mathcal{O}_{f}
by the values of
\chi
.

Proposition 8.1
.

Let
c=\text{ord}_{\varpi_{\chi}}(\chi(\theta_{m}(f)))
. Then
\varpi_{\chi}^{c}\text{Sel}_{\mathfrak{n}^{-}}(K_{m},A_{f,n})^{\chi}=0
for every large
n
.

Proof.

For every integer
n>c
, we can find an admissible form
\mathcal{D}^{f}_{n}=(\mathfrak{n}^{-},f^{\dagger,[k-2]}_{n})
, such that

\theta_{m}(\mathcal{D}^{f}_{n})\equiv\theta_{m}(f)\,(\mbox{mod }{\varpi^{n}}).

By the first reciprocity law and Theorem
7.2
, we therefore can find an admissible prime
\mathfrak{l}
such that
\partial_{\mathfrak{l}}(\kappa_{\mathcal{D}^{f}_{n}}(\mathfrak{l}))\equiv\theta_{m}(f)\,(\mbox{mod }{\varpi_{{\mathfrak{p}}}^{n}})
. Let
s\in\text{Sel}_{\mathfrak{n}^{-}}(K_{m},A_{f,n})^{\chi}
, we can also choose the admissible prime
\mathfrak{l}
such that
{\text{res}}_{\mathfrak{l}}(s)
is non-zero using Theorem
7.2
. Then by considering the sum

\sum_{v}\langle{\text{res}}_{v}(\kappa_{\mathcal{D}^{f}_{n}}(\mathfrak{l})),{\text{res}}_{v}(s)\rangle=0,

we conclude that

\langle\partial_{\mathfrak{l}}(\kappa_{\mathcal{D}^{f}_{n}}(\mathfrak{l})),{\text{res}}_{\mathfrak{l}}(s)\rangle=0

using the same type of argument as in Lemma
7.11
. Hence
\varpi_{\chi}^{c}s=0
by the non-degeneracy of the local Tate pairing. Therefore we see that
\varpi_{\chi}^{c}\text{Sel}_{\mathfrak{n}^{-}}(K_{m},A_{f,n})^{\chi}=0
.
∎

Now an immediate corollary from the above theorem is the proof of the following rank zero case of the refined Bloch-Kato conjecture.

Theorem 8.2
.

Suppose that
L(f,\chi,k/2)\neq 0
. Then
\text{Sel}_{f}(K_{m},V_{f})^{\chi}=0
.

Proof.

By the interpolation formula Theorem
7.15
and Proposition
8.1
, we see that
\text{Sel}_{\mathfrak{n}^{-}}(K_{m},A_{f,n})^{\chi}
is bounded independent of
n
. Therefore it follows that
\text{Sel}_{\mathfrak{n}^{-}}(K_{m},A_{f})^{\chi}
is finite. Since
\text{Sel}_{f}(K_{m},V_{f})^{\chi}\subset\text{Sel}_{\mathfrak{n}^{-}}(K_{m},V_{f})^{\chi}
, we obtain
\text{Sel}_{f}(K_{m},V_{f})^{\chi}=0
.
∎

8.2.

Theorem 8.3

Lemma 10.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{l}
be an
n
-admissible prime for
f

Proof.

First notice that as
\mathfrak{l}

H^{1}(K_{m,\mathfrak{l}},A_{f})=H^{1}(K_{\mathfrak{l}},A_{f})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{f}[\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(K_{m}/K)].

We consider the inflation-restriction exact sequence

0\rightarrow H^{1}(K^{ur}_{\mathfrak{l}}/K_{\mathfrak{l}},A_{f})\rightarrow H^{1}(K_{\mathfrak{l}},A_{f})\rightarrow H^{1}(I_{\mathfrak{l}},A_{f})^{\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(K^{ur}_{\mathfrak{l}}/K)}\rightarrow 0.

for a topological generator of

. On the other hand
H^{1}(I_{\mathfrak{l}},A_{f})\cong\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits(I_{\mathfrak{l}},A_{f})=\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits(\mathbf{Z}_{p}(1),A_{f})
and thus

, the two eigenvalues

and

of
\mathrm{Frob}_{\mathfrak{l}}
on

\alpha\equiv\epsilon_{\mathfrak{l}}\,(\mbox{mod }{\varpi^{n}})
and
\beta\equiv\epsilon_{\mathfrak{l}}{\bf{N}}(\mathfrak{l})\,(\mbox{mod }{\varpi^{n}})

on

10.2.
\Phi
-functor

For each
n\geq 1
, Pollack-Weston defined the following functor:

(10.1)

\Phi:\{\text{cofinitely generated $\Lambda$-module}\}\rightarrow\{\text{finitely generated $\Lambda/\varpi^{n}$-module}\}

where
\Phi_{n}(S)=\varprojlim_{m}S[\varpi^{n}]^{\Gamma^{m}}
for the group
\Gamma^{m}=\mathop{\rm Gal}\nolimits(K_{\infty}/K_{m})
. And the transition map is given by the trace map.

The following lemma collects some properties of this functor.

Lemma 10.5
.

(1)

The functor
\Phi_{n}
is covariant and left exact.

(2)

\Phi_{n}(S)=\Phi_{n}(S[\varpi^{n}])
.

(3)

If
S
is finite, then
\Phi_{n}(S)=0
.

Proof.

Only the third point needs some explanation as the others follow from the definitions. For the third point, notice that
\Gamma^{m}
fixes
S
for
m
sufficiently large. Then the transition map becomes simply multiplication by
p
. The third point follows.
∎

Lemma 10.6
.

Let
0\rightarrow A\rightarrow B\rightarrow C\rightarrow 0
be an exact sequence of cofinitely generated
\Lambda
-modules.

(1)

If
C
is finite, then
\Phi_{n}(A)=\Phi_{n}(B)
.

(2)

If
A
is finite, then
\Phi_{n}(B)=\Phi_{n}(C)
.

Proof.

The first part follows immediately from the previous lemma and the fact that
\Phi
is left exact.

For the second point, we first take the
\varpi^{n}
torsion of the exact sequence to obtain:

0\rightarrow A[\varpi^{n}]\rightarrow B[\varpi^{n}]\rightarrow C[\varpi^{n}]\rightarrow A/\varpi^{n}.

Then for some finite
H
, we have

0\rightarrow A[\varpi^{n}]\rightarrow B[\varpi^{n}]\rightarrow C[\varpi^{n}]\rightarrow H\rightarrow 0.

We split this exact sequence into two short exact sequences:

0\rightarrow A[\varpi^{n}]\rightarrow B[\varpi^{n}]\rightarrow C^{\prime}\rightarrow 0

and

0\rightarrow C^{\prime}\rightarrow C[\varpi^{n}]\rightarrow H\rightarrow 0.

By applying the previous lemma, we have
\Phi_{n}(C^{\prime})\cong\Phi_{n}(C[\varpi^{n}])\cong\Phi_{n}(C)
. For the first exact sequence, we take
\Gamma^{m}
invariant to get

0\rightarrow A[\varpi^{n}]^{\Gamma^{m}}\rightarrow B[\varpi^{n}]^{\Gamma^{m}}\rightarrow(C^{\prime})^{\Gamma^{m}}\rightarrow H^{\prime}\rightarrow 0

for some finite
H^{\prime}
. Again by breaking the this four term exact sequence into two short exact sequence, we have

0\rightarrow A[\varpi^{n}]^{\Gamma^{m}}\rightarrow B[\varpi^{n}]^{\Gamma^{m}}\rightarrow C^{\prime}_{m}\rightarrow 0

and

0\rightarrow C^{\prime}_{m}\rightarrow(C^{\prime})^{\Gamma^{m}}\rightarrow H^{\prime}\rightarrow 0

where
C^{\prime}_{m}
is again finite. Taking inverse limit with respect to
n
yields:

0\rightarrow\Phi_{n}(A)\rightarrow\Phi_{n}(B)\rightarrow\varprojlim_{m}C^{\prime}_{m}\rightarrow 0

where the left exactness is guaranteed by the finiteness of
A
and the Mittag-Leffler condition. Then we get
\Phi_{n}(B)=\varprojlim_{m}C^{\prime}_{m}
. The same reasoning applied to the second exact sequence yields
\varprojlim C_{m}^{\prime}\cong\Phi_{n}(C^{\prime})
. Therefore
\Phi_{n}(B)=\Phi_{n}(C^{\prime})=\Phi_{n}(C)
.
∎

Corollary 10.7
.

If
A
and
B
are two cofinitely generated
\Lambda
-modules which are pseudo-isomorphic, then
\Phi_{n}(A)\cong\Phi_{n}(B)

Proof.

In light of the definition of pseudo-isomorphism, this follows immediately from the two previous lemmas.
∎

Proposition 10.8
.

For a fintely generated cotorsion
\Lambda
-module
Y
with vanishing
\mu
-invariant,
\Phi_{n}({Y}^{\!\vee})=0
for all
n\geq 1
.

Proof.

By the structure theorem of finitely generated cotorsion
\Lambda
-module
Y
, we can assume that
Y=\Lambda/f\Lambda
for some
f
coprime to
p
. Then we have
\Phi_{n}({Y}^{\!\vee})\cong\Phi_{n}({Y}^{\!\vee}[\varpi^{n}])\cong\Phi_{n}({(Y/\varpi^{n})}^{\!\vee})
. As
Y/\varpi^{n}=\Lambda/(f,\varpi^{n})

\Phi_{n}({Y}^{\!\vee})=0

∎

Proposition 10.9
.

If
Y=\Lambda/\varpi^{t}
with
t\geq n
, then
\Phi_{n}({Y}^{\!\vee})\cong\Lambda/\varpi^{n}
.

Proof.

By definition, we have

\begin{aligned} \Phi_{n}({(\Lambda/\varpi^{t}\Lambda)}^{\!\vee})&=\varprojlim_{n}{(\Lambda/\varpi^{t})}^{\!\vee}[\varpi^{n}]^{\Gamma^{m}}\cong\varprojlim_{n}{(\Lambda/\varpi^{n})_{\Gamma^{m}}}^{\!\vee}\\

\end{aligned}

By the Gorensteiness of

{\mathcal{O}_{f,n}[\Gamma_{m}]}^{\!\vee}\cong\mathcal{O}_{f,n}[\Gamma_{m}]
and the transition maps with respect to

\begin{aligned} \Phi_{n}({(\Lambda/\varpi^{t}\Lambda)}^{\!\vee})&\cong\varprojlim_{n}{\mathcal{O}_{f,n}[\Gamma_{m}]}^{\!\vee}\cong\varprojlim_{n}\mathcal{O}_{f,n}[\Gamma_{m}]\\
&\cong\Lambda/\varpi^{n}.\\
\end{aligned}

∎

Abstract
. A general system of n n ordinary differential equations coupled with one reaction-diffusion equation, considered in a bounded N N -dimensional domain, with no-flux boundary condition is studied in a context of pattern formation. Such initial boundary value problems may have different types of stationary solutions. In our parallel work [ Instability of all regular stationary solutions to reaction-diffusion-ODE systems (2021)], regular ( i.e. sufficiently smooth) stationary solutions are shown to exist, however, all of them are unstable. The goal of this work is to construct discontinuous stationary solutions to general reaction-diffusion-ODE systems and to find sufficient conditions for their stability.

Main results

2.1.
Stationary solutions

The goal of this work is to construct a certain class of non-constant stationary solutions of problem (
1.1
)-(
1.3
) and to show their stability. Thus, we deal with a couple
(\boldsymbol{U},V)=\big(\boldsymbol{U}(x),V(x)\big)

with a vector field and a scalar function

(2.1)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{U}(x)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}U_{1}(x)\\
\vdots\\
U_{n}(x)\end{array}\right)\qquad\text{and}\qquad V=V(x),

which is a solution
to the boundary value problem

(2.2)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{f}(\boldsymbol{U},V)

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle x\in\overline{\Omega},

\displaystyle\gamma\Delta_{\nu}V+g(\boldsymbol{U},V)

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle x\in\Omega,

with arbitrary
C^{2}
-functions
\boldsymbol{f}
and
g
of the form (
1.4
), with constant
\gamma>0
, and in a bounded domain

\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
with a
C^{2}
-boundary.

Definition 2.1
.

A pair
(\boldsymbol{U},V)=\big(\boldsymbol{U}(x),V(x)\big)

is
a weak solution of problem (
2.2
)
if

•

\boldsymbol{U}
is measurable,

•

V\in{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}
,

•

g(\boldsymbol{U},V)\in\big({W^{1,2}(\Omega)}\big)^{\star}

(
the dual of
{W^{1,2}(\Omega)})
,

•

the equation
\boldsymbol{f}\big(\boldsymbol{U}(x),V(x)\big)=0
is satisfied for almost all
x\in\overline{\Omega}
,

•

the equation

(2.3)

\displaystyle-\gamma\int_{\Omega}\nabla V(x)\cdot\nabla\varphi(x)\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega}g\big(\boldsymbol{U}(x),V(x)\big)\varphi(x)\,{\rm d}x=0

holds true for all test functions
\varphi\in{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}
.

Remark 2.2
.

The most direct way to construct such stationary solutions is to solve first equation in (
2.2
) with respect to
\boldsymbol{U}
and to substitute the result into the second equation to obtain an elliptic boundary value problem. Namely, we may assume that there exists a
C^{2}
-function
\boldsymbol{k}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\boldsymbol{U}(x)=\boldsymbol{k}(V(x))
for all
x\in\Omega
and

(2.4)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{f}\big(\boldsymbol{U}(x),V(x)\big)=\boldsymbol{f}\big(\boldsymbol{k}(V(x)),V(x)\big)=0\quad\text{for all}\quad x\in\Omega,

where
V=V(x)
is a solution of the elliptic Neumann problem

(2.5)

\displaystyle\gamma\Delta_{\nu}V+h(V)=0\qquad\text{for}\quad x\in\Omega

with
h(V)=g\big(\boldsymbol{k}(V),V\big)
. In our parallel work
[
6
]
, such solutions are called regular and we showed that all of them are unstable.

Remark 2.3
.

Results in this work require that problem (
2.2
) has a constant solution, namely, a constant vector

(2.6)

\displaystyle(\overline{\boldsymbol{U}},\overline{V})\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\quad\text{such that}\quad\boldsymbol{f}(\overline{\boldsymbol{U}},\overline{V})=0\quad\text{and}\quad g(\overline{\boldsymbol{U}},\overline{V})=0.

In the following, we use the constant coefficient matrices

(2.7)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{A}_{0}=\boldsymbol{f}_{\boldsymbol{u}}\left(\overline{\boldsymbol{U}},\overline{V}\right),\quad\boldsymbol{B}_{0}=\boldsymbol{f}_{v}\left(\overline{\boldsymbol{U}},\overline{V}\right),\quad\boldsymbol{C}_{0}=g_{\boldsymbol{u}}\left(\overline{\boldsymbol{U}},\overline{V}\right),\quad d_{0}=g_{v}\left(\overline{\boldsymbol{U}},\overline{V}\right).

On the other hand, if
(\boldsymbol{U},V)\in{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{n+1}
is a non-constant solution to problem (
2.2
) we introduce the matrices

(2.8)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{A}=\boldsymbol{f}_{\boldsymbol{u}}({\boldsymbol{U}},{V}),\quad\boldsymbol{B}=\boldsymbol{f}_{v}({\boldsymbol{U}},{V}),\quad\boldsymbol{C}=g_{\boldsymbol{u}}({\boldsymbol{U}},{V}),\quad d=g_{v}({\boldsymbol{U}},{V})

Let

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\begin{pmatrix}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\\

Remark 2.14

Theorem

where the linearised operator does not generate strongly continuous semigroup of linear operators. Here, the nonlinear stability in
{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
is obtained by using the linear exponential stability in
{L^{p}(\Omega)}
with
p\in(1,\infty)
from Theorem

Remark 2.15
.

Other discontinuous stationary solutions can be constructed under following more general version of Assumption

and different branches

\qquad\vdots\\
\boldsymbol{k}_{J}\big(V(x)\big),\quad x\in\Omega_{J},\end{cases}

using the same reasoning as in Theorem

for each

Remark 2.16

For

Remark 2.17
.

The

-stability in Theorem

requires from initial conditions of problem (

)-(

3.
Construction of discontinuous stationary solutions

The proof of Theorem
2.6
is based on the following preliminary results.

Lemma 3.1
.

Fix
p\in[2,\infty)
and let
\gamma>0
be arbitrary. For each
b\in\mathbb{R}
such that
b\neq\gamma\mu_{k}
for all eigenvalues
\mu_{k}
of
-\Delta_{\nu}
, and for every
f\in L^{p}(\Omega)
the problem

(3.1)

\displaystyle\gamma\Delta_{\nu}v+bv

\displaystyle=f\quad\text{for}\quad x\in\Omega,

has a unique weak solution
v\in{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}
satisfying

(3.2)

\displaystyle\|v\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}\leqslant C(b,\gamma)\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

If
\partial\Omega
is of the class
C^{2}
, then
v\in{W^{2,p}_{\nu}(\Omega)}
and there exists a constant
C=C(b,p,\gamma)>0
such that

(3.3)

\displaystyle\|v\|_{W^{2,p}_{\nu}(\Omega)}\leqslant C\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

Proof.

In the case of
p=2
and
b<0
, this is a well known result on the existence and
W^{2,2}
-regularity of solution to problem (
3.1
), see
e.g.

[
1
, Thm. 9.26]
. In fact, we can extend this result to all
b\neq\gamma\mu_{k}
because the solution to problem (
3.1
) is given by the explicit formula

(3.4)

\displaystyle v=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{{-\gamma\mu_{k}}+b}\langle f,\Phi_{k}\rangle_{{L^{2}(\Omega)}}\Phi_{k},

where
\Phi_{k}
is an eigenfunction corresponding to the eigenvalue
\mu_{k}
.

To prove this Lemma for
p>2
, we rewrite the equation in the form

(3.5)

\displaystyle\gamma\Delta_{\nu}v-v

\displaystyle=f-(b+1)v\quad\text{for}\quad x\in\Omega.

By using the
L^{p}
-elliptic regularity (see
e.g.

[
27
, Thm. 3.1.3]
), the Sobolev embedding
{W^{2,2}_{\nu}(\Omega)}\subseteq{L^{p}(\Omega)}
for each
p\in(1,\infty)
if
N\leqslant 4
and for
p\in\big(2,\,2N/(N-4)\big)
if
N>4
, as well as inequality (
3.3
) with
p=2
, we obtain

(3.6)

\displaystyle\|v\|_{W^{2,p}_{\nu}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\big(\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\big)

\displaystyle\leqslant C\big(\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|v\|_{W^{2,2}_{\nu}(\Omega)}\big)\leqslant C\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

In order to obtain the desired estimate for the whole range of
p\in[2,\infty)
it suffices to apply a bootstrap argument involving inequality (
3.6
) together with the Sobolev embedding
{W^{2,p}_{\nu}(\Omega)}\subseteq{L^{q}(\Omega)}
for
q<Np/(N-2p)
.
∎

We obtain discontinuous stationary solutions of problem (
2.2
) from the following lemma which is a consequence of the Banach fixed point argument.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
p>N/2
with
p\geqslant 2
,
\gamma>0
, and
h_{1},h_{2}\in C_{b}^{2}(\mathbb{R})

(
i.e. all derivatives up to order two are bounded
)
. Assume that for some
\overline{v}\in\mathbb{R}
we have

(3.7)

\displaystyle h_{1}(\overline{v})=0\quad\text{and}\quad h_{1}^{\prime}(\overline{v})\neq\gamma\mu_{k}

for each eigenvalue
\mu_{k}
of
-\Delta_{\nu}
. For arbitrary open subset
\Omega_{1}\subseteq\Omega
with
\Omega_{2}=\Omega\setminus\overline{\Omega_{1}}

one can find
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that for each
\varepsilon\in(0,\;\varepsilon_{0})
there exists
\delta>0
such that if
|\Omega_{2}|\leqslant\delta
then problem

(3.8)

\displaystyle\gamma\Delta_{\nu}v+h_{1}(v)\mathbbm{1}_{\Omega_{1}}+h_{2}(v)\mathbbm{1}_{\Omega_{2}}=0\qquad for\quad x\in\Omega,

has a weak solution
v\in{W^{2,p}_{\nu}(\Omega)}
satisfying
\|v-\overline{v}\|_{W^{2,p}_{\nu}(\Omega)}\leqslant\varepsilon
.

Proof.

We denote by
v=\big(\gamma\Delta_{\nu}+h_{1}^{\prime}(\overline{v})\big)^{-1}f
the solution of problem (
3.1
) with
b=h_{1}^{\prime}(\overline{v})
which by Lemma
3.1
satisfies the estimate

(3.9)

\displaystyle\left\|\big(\gamma\Delta_{\nu}+h_{1}^{\prime}(\overline{v})\big)^{-1}f\right\|_{W^{2,p}_{\nu}(\Omega)}\leqslant C\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

for each
p\geqslant 2
and all
f\in{L^{p}(\Omega)}
.

Replacing
v
by
v+\overline{v}
and
h_{i}(v)
for
i\in\{1,2\}
by
h_{i}(v+\overline{v})
we may assume without loss of generality that
\overline{v}=0
. We write equation (
3.8
) in the equivalent form

(3.10)

\displaystyle v

\displaystyle=\big(\gamma\Delta_{\nu}+h_{1}^{\prime}(0)\big)^{-1}\Big(\big(h^{\prime}_{1}(0)v-h_{1}(v)\big)\mathbbm{1}_{\Omega_{1}}\Big)

\displaystyle+\big(\gamma\Delta_{\nu}+h_{1}^{\prime}(0)\big)^{-1}\Big(\big(h_{1}^{\prime}(0)v-h_{2}(v)\big)\mathbbm{1}_{\Omega_{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}_{1}(v)+\mathcal{T}_{2}(v).

Our goal is to show that the right-hand side of this equation defines a contraction on the ball

(3.11)

\displaystyle B_{\varepsilon}(0)=\{v\in{W^{2,p}_{\nu}(\Omega)}:\|v\|_{W^{2,p}_{\nu}(\Omega)}\leqslant\varepsilon\}

provided
|\Omega_{2}|
and
\varepsilon>0
are sufficiently small.

First, we deal with the operator
\mathcal{T}_{1}
. Since
h_{1}\in C_{b}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and
h_{1}(0)=0
by the Taylor expansion we get

(3.12)

\displaystyle\left|h_{1}^{\prime}(0)v(x)-h_{1}\big(v(x)\big)\right|=\left|h_{1}\big(v(x)\big)-h_{1}(0)-h_{1}^{\prime}(0)v(x)\right|\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\|h_{1}^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\big|v(x)\big|^{2}.

Hence, by estimate (
3.9
) and the Sobolev embedding
{W^{2,p}_{\nu}(\Omega)}\subseteq{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
for
p>N/2
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that for each
v\in B_{\varepsilon}(0)
we have

(3.13)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{T}_{1}(v)\|_{W^{2,p}_{\nu}(\Omega)}\leqslant C\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{2}\leqslant C\varepsilon^{2}.

Analogously, by the Taylor expansion,

(3.14)

\displaystyle\Big|h_{1}^{\prime}(0)v(x)

\displaystyle-h_{1}\big(v(x)\big)-\Big(h_{1}^{\prime}(0)w(x)-h_{1}\big(w(x)\big)\Big)\Big|

\displaystyle\leqslant\left|h_{1}\big(v(x)\big)-h_{1}\big(w(x)\big)+h_{1}^{\prime}\big(w(x)\big)\big(v(x)-w(x)\big)\right|

\displaystyle+\left|\big(h_{1}^{\prime}\big(w(x)\big)-h_{1}^{\prime}(0)\big)\big(v(x)-w(x)\big)\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\|h_{1}^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\big|v(x)-w(x)\big|^{2}+\|h_{1}^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\big|w(x)\big|\big|v(x)-w(x)\big|.

Hence, as in inequalities (
3.13
), for
v,w\in B_{\varepsilon}(0)
, we obtain

(3.15)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{T}_{1}(v)-\mathcal{T}_{1}(w)\|_{W^{2,p}_{\nu}(\Omega)}\leqslant C\varepsilon\|h_{1}^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\|v-w\|_{W^{2,p}_{\nu}(\Omega)}.

Here, we choose
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
we have

(3.16)

\displaystyle C\varepsilon^{2}\leqslant\varepsilon/2\quad\text{and}\quad C\varepsilon\|h_{1}^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}<1.

In the case of the operator
\mathcal{T}_{2}
, we begin by the following inequality

(3.17)

\displaystyle\left|h_{1}^{\prime}(0)v(x)-h_{2}\big(v(x)\big)\right|\leqslant\|h_{1}^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\big|v(x)\big|+\|h_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}.

Thus, by estimate (
3.17
) and the embedding
{W^{2,p}_{\nu}(\Omega)}\subseteq{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
for
p>N/2
, for each
v\in B_{\varepsilon}(0)
we get

(3.18)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{T}_{2}(v)\|_{W^{2,p}_{\nu}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\big(\|h_{1}^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\|v\mathbbm{1}_{\Omega_{2}}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|h_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\|\mathbbm{1}_{\Omega_{2}}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\big)

\displaystyle\leqslant C|\Omega_{2}|^{\frac{1}{p}}\big(\|v\|_{W^{2,p}_{\nu}(\Omega)}+1\big).

Analogously, we have

(3.19)

\displaystyle\Big|h_{1}^{\prime}(0)v(x)

\displaystyle-h_{2}\big(v(x)\big)-\big(h_{1}^{\prime}(0)w(x)-h_{2}\big(w(x)\big)\big)\big|

\displaystyle\leqslant(\|h_{1}^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}+\|h_{2}^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})})\big(v(x)-w(x)\big).

Thus, by inequality (
3.19
), as in estimate (
3.18
), for
v,w\in B_{\varepsilon}(0)
, we obtain

(3.20)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{T}_{2}(v)-\mathcal{T}_{2}(w)\|_{W^{2,p}_{\nu}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leqslant C(\|h_{1}^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})},\|h_{2}^{\prime}||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})})\|(v-w)\mathbbm{1}_{\Omega_{2}}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leqslant C|\Omega_{2}|^{\frac{1}{p}}\|v-w\|_{W^{2,p}_{\nu}(\Omega)}.

Here, we choose
\delta>0
such that for
\Omega_{2}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
satisfying
|\Omega_{2}|<\delta
we have

(3.21)

\displaystyle C\big(\varepsilon+1\big)|\Omega_{2}|^{\frac{1}{p}}\leqslant\varepsilon/2\quad\text{and}\quad C|\Omega_{2}|^{\frac{1}{p}}<1.

Estimates (
3.13
), (
3.15
), (
3.17
) and (
3.20
) together with the Banach fixed point argument complete the proof of the existence of a solution to equation (
3.10
) in
B_{\varepsilon}(0)
.
∎

4.1.
Linearised operator

In order to apply a linearisation procedure, first, we study a stability of the zero solution to the following general linear reaction-diffusion-ODE system

(4.1)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{t}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{\varphi}+\boldsymbol{B}\psi

\displaystyle\text{for}

\displaystyle x\in\overline{\Omega},\quad t>0,

\displaystyle\psi_{t}

\displaystyle=\gamma\Delta_{\nu}\psi+\boldsymbol{C}\boldsymbol{\varphi}+d\psi

\displaystyle\text{for}

\displaystyle x\in\Omega,\quad t>0,

with general matrices

(4.2)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{A}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{A}(x)=\begin{pmatrix}a_{11}(x)&\dots&a_{1n}(x)\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
a_{n1}(x)&\dots&a_{nn}(x)\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{B}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{B}(x)=\begin{pmatrix}b_{1}(x)\\
\vdots\\
b_{n}(x)\\
\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{C}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{C}(x)=\begin{pmatrix}c_{1}(x)&\dots&c_{n}(x)\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle d

\displaystyle=d(x),

with arbitrary coefficients (not necessarily as those in equation (
2.8
))

(4.3)

\displaystyle a_{ij},\;b_{i},\;c_{i},\;d\in L^{\infty}(\Omega),\quad\text{for}\;i,j\in\{1,\cdots,n\}.

First, following our parallel work
[
6
]
, we recall properties of the operator defined by the formula

(4.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{p}\begin{pmatrix}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\\
\psi\end{pmatrix}\equiv\begin{pmatrix}\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{\varphi}+\boldsymbol{B}\psi\\
\gamma\Delta_{\nu}\psi+\boldsymbol{C}\boldsymbol{\varphi}+d\psi\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{with}\quad D(\mathcal{L}_{p})=L^{p}(\Omega)^{n}\times W^{2,p}_{\nu}(\Omega)

for each

\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{p})
its spectrum and by
s(\mathcal{L}_{p})=\sup\{{\rm Re\,}\lambda:\lambda\in\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{p})\}
its spectral bound.

•

\big(\mathcal{L}_{p},D(\mathcal{L}_{p})\big)
generates an analytic semigroup on

, see
[
6
, Prop. 4.1]
.

•

The spectrum of

\displaystyle\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{p})=\sigma\big(\boldsymbol{A}(\cdot)\big)\cup\{\text{eigenvalues of }\;\mathcal{L}_{p}\}.

In particular, the spectrum of

is independent of

[

.

For a completeness of the exposition, we recall also properties of the spectrum
\sigma\big(\boldsymbol{A}(\cdot)\big)
of the multiplication operator
\boldsymbol{A}(\cdot)
acting on
{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{n}
for
p\in[1,\infty)
induced by an arbitrary matrix
\boldsymbol{A}(x)=\big(a_{i,j}(x)\big)_{i,j=1}^{n}
with

-dependent and essentially bounded coefficients. The following results are either well-known or can be found in the works
[
8
,
6
]
,
[
24

•

For a square matrix
\boldsymbol{A}_{0}
with constant coefficients, we have

(4.6)

\displaystyle s(\boldsymbol{A}_{0})=\max\{{\rm Re\,}\lambda:\lambda\text{ is an eigenvalue of }\boldsymbol{A}_{0}\}.

•

The spectral radius

\displaystyle r(\boldsymbol{A}_{0})\leqslant|A_{0}|=\left(\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}a_{i,j}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

For more general matrices

\displaystyle\sigma\big(\boldsymbol{A}(\cdot)\big)=\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:\begin{array}[]{l}\forall{\varepsilon>0}\quad\exists{\Omega_{\varepsilon}\subseteq\Omega}\quad\text{open set}\quad\exists{\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\\
\text{such that}\quad\|\boldsymbol{A}(\cdot)\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\varepsilon}-\lambda\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leqslant\varepsilon|\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\varepsilon}|\end{array}\right\}.

The following equality holds true

\displaystyle s\big(\boldsymbol{A}(\cdot)\big)=\esssup_{x\in\Omega}\big(s(\boldsymbol{A}(x))\big).

By

\boldsymbol{A}(x)
belongs to the spectrum of

For an arbitrary matrix
\boldsymbol{A}_{0}

Therefore, denoting by adj the adjugate matrix we obtain

\displaystyle\left|(\boldsymbol{A}_{0}-\lambda\boldsymbol{I})^{-1}\right|

\boldsymbol{A}(x)

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{C(\lambda^{n}+1)}{\max\big(\left|s\big(\boldsymbol{A}(\cdot)\big)\right|,|\lambda|-\|\boldsymbol{A}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\big)^{n}}\leqslant C,

for almost all
x\in\Omega

For matrices

\boldsymbol{C}&d-\lambda\end{pmatrix}.

We begin by resolvent estimates in the case of constant coefficients.

Lemma 4.1

Assume that matrices
\boldsymbol{A}=\boldsymbol{A}_{0}
,
\boldsymbol{B}=\boldsymbol{B}_{0}
,
\boldsymbol{C}=\boldsymbol{C}_{0}
,

\displaystyle s(\boldsymbol{A}_{0})<0\quad\text{as well as}\quad s\begin{pmatrix}\boldsymbol{A}_{0}&\boldsymbol{B}_{0}\\

\displaystyle(\boldsymbol{A}_{0}-\lambda\boldsymbol{I})\boldsymbol{\varphi}+\boldsymbol{B}_{0}\psi

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{\varphi}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|\psi\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}\leqslant K\big(\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|g\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\big).

A unique solution to system (

\displaystyle(\boldsymbol{A}_{0}-\lambda\boldsymbol{I})\tilde{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}+\boldsymbol{B}_{0}\tilde{\psi}

Proposition 4.3

(One PDE coupled with one ODE)
.

Let
\big(\mathcal{L}_{p},\,D(\mathcal{L}_{p})\big)
be the operator given by formula (

Proof.

Since
s(a(\cdot))<0
, by equation (
4.5
), it suffices to study only eigenvalues of the operator

. We prove that

(4.39)

\displaystyle a\varphi+b\psi

\displaystyle=\lambda\varphi,

\displaystyle x\in\overline{\Omega},

\displaystyle\gamma\Delta_{\nu}\psi+c\varphi+d\psi

\displaystyle=\lambda\psi,

\displaystyle x\in{\Omega}

for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
with
{\rm Re\,}\lambda\geqslant-\kappa

(4.40)

\displaystyle\kappa\in\bigg(0,\ \min\left(-s\big(a(\cdot)\big),-s\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}a(\cdot)&b(\cdot)\\

a(x)\leqslant s(a(\cdot))<0

\displaystyle\varphi=-\frac{b}{a-\lambda}\psi,\quad\text{for}\quad x\in\Omega.

Substituting this expression into second equation in (

\displaystyle\gamma\Delta_{\nu}\psi+\Big(-\frac{cb}{a-\lambda}+d-\lambda\Big)\psi=0,\quad\text{for}\quad x\in\Omega.

We introduce the function

\displaystyle h(x,\lambda)

\displaystyle\equiv{\rm Re\,}\Big(-\frac{c(x)b(x)}{a(x)-\lambda}+d(x)-\lambda\Big)

\displaystyle=-\frac{c(x)b(x)a(x)-c(x)b(x){\rm Re\,}\lambda}{(a(x)-{\rm Re\,}\lambda)^{2}+\operatorname{Im}^{2}\lambda}+d(x)-{\rm Re\,}\lambda,

\displaystyle h(x,\lambda)

\displaystyle={\rm Re\,}\left(\frac{1}{a(x)-\lambda}{\det\begin{pmatrix}a(x)-\lambda&b(x)\\

It is well-known that zero is the only solution of equation (

\displaystyle\esssup_{x\in\Omega,\;\lambda\in\mathbb{C},\ {\rm Re\,}\lambda\geqslant-\kappa}h(x,\lambda)<0.

c(x)&d(x)-\alpha\end{pmatrix}>0

\displaystyle{-\frac{\left(s\begin{pmatrix}a(\cdot)&b(\cdot)\\

.

Lemma 5.8
.

Let

be a nonnegative continuous function on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
satisfying

(5.36)

\displaystyle h(t)\leqslant Ce^{-kt}h(0)+C\int_{0}^{t}e^{-k(t-s)}h^{2}(s)\,{\rm d}s\quad\text{for each}\quad t>0

and some constant
C\geqslant 1
. If

, then
h(t)\leqslant Ch(0)e^{-\frac{k}{2}t}
for all
t>0
.

Proof.

Let
T>0
be such that
h(t)<k/(2C)
for every
t\in[0,\,T]
. After multiplying both sides of inequality (
5.36
) by

we obtain

(5.37)

\displaystyle h(t)e^{kt}\leqslant Ch(0)+\frac{k}{2}\int_{0}^{t}e^{ks}h(s)\,{\rm d}s\quad\text{for each}\quad t\in[0,\,T]

and using the Grönwall inequality we conclude that
h(t)\leqslant Ch(0)e^{-\frac{k}{2}t}
for all
t\in[0,\,T]
.

In order to show that this inequality holds true for all
t>0
we show that
h(t)<k/(4C)
. Indeed, if there
exists

t_{0}\in[0,\,T]
such that
h(t_{0})=k/(4C)
then

(5.38)

\displaystyle\frac{k}{4C}=h(t_{0})\leqslant C\frac{k}{4C^{2}}e^{-\frac{k}{2}t_{0}}<\frac{k}{4C},

which is a contradiction.
∎

6.
Stability of discontinuous stationary solutions

6.1.
Existence of local-in-time solutions

In this work, we consider mild solutions of problem (
1.1
)-(
1.4
), that is, a solution to the corresponding system of integral equations

(6.1)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{u}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{u}_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\boldsymbol{f}\big(\boldsymbol{u}(s),v(s)\big)\,{\rm d}s,

\displaystyle v

\displaystyle={e^{\gamma\Delta_{\nu}t}v_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{\gamma\Delta_{\nu}(t-s)}g\big(\boldsymbol{u}(s),v(s)\big)\,{\rm d}s.}

Theorem 6.1
.

For every
\boldsymbol{u}_{0}\in{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{n}
and
v_{0}\in C(\overline{\Omega})
there exists
T>0
such that system
(
6.1
) has a unique solution
\boldsymbol{u}\in C\big([0,\,T],\,{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{n}\big)
and
v\in C\big([0,\,T],\,C(\overline{\Omega})\big)
.

Proof.

First, we recall that the operator
\big(\Delta_{\nu},D(\Delta_{\nu})\big)
with the domain

(6.2)

\displaystyle D(\Delta_{\nu})=\left\{u\in\bigcap_{p\geqslant 1}W^{2,p}_{loc}(\Omega):\quad u,\,\Delta u\in C(\overline{\Omega}),\quad\partial_{\nu}u=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega\right\},

is a sectorial operator on the space
C(\overline{\Omega})
, see
[
27
, Corollary 3.1.24]
. Now, it is sufficient to apply the Banach fixed point theorem to system (
6.1
) with the locally Lipschitz nonlinearities
\boldsymbol{f}
and
g
. In this approach the Bochner integrals on the right hand side of equation (
6.1
) are convergent in the corresponding spaces. Indeed, for every
\boldsymbol{u}\in C\big([0,\,T],\,{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{n}\big)
and
v\in C\big([0,\,T],\,C(\overline{\Omega})\big)
we have
\boldsymbol{f}\big(\boldsymbol{u},\,v)\in C\big([0,T],\,{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{n}\big)
and consequently the integral is convergent

(6.3)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\boldsymbol{f}\big(\boldsymbol{u}(s),v(s)\big)\,{\rm d}s\in C\big([0,\,T],\,{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{n}\big).

Analogously, we have
g\big(\boldsymbol{u},\,v)\in C\big([0,T],\,{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\big)
and hence by the well-known property of the Neumann semigroup we obtain

(6.4)

\displaystyle{w(s)\equiv e^{\gamma\Delta_{\nu}(t-s)}g\big(\boldsymbol{u}(s),v(s)\big)\in C(\overline{\Omega})}

for each
t\in(0,\,T)
and
s\in[0,\,t)
. Therefore

(6.5)

\displaystyle w\in C\big([0,\,t);\,C(\overline{\Omega})\big)\cap L^{\infty}\big([0,\,t],\,{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\big)

and for every
\varepsilon>0

(6.6)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t-\varepsilon}e^{\gamma\Delta_{\nu}(t-s)}g\big(\boldsymbol{u}(s),v\big)\,{\rm d}s\in C\big([0,T-\varepsilon];\,C(\overline{\Omega})\big).

Since
g(\boldsymbol{u},\,v)\in{L^{\infty}([0,T];{L^{\infty}(\Omega)})}
we have

(6.7)

\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{t-\varepsilon}e^{\gamma\Delta_{\nu}(t-s)}g\big(\boldsymbol{u}(s),v\big)\,{\rm d}s\xrightarrow{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\gamma\Delta_{\nu}(t-s)}g\big(\boldsymbol{u}(s),v(s)\big)\,{\rm d}s}

locally uniformly on
[0,\,T)\times\overline{\Omega}
.
∎

p\in(1,\infty)
. It remains to prove that
s\big(g_{v}(\boldsymbol{U}(\cdot),\,V(\cdot)\big)<0
provided
\varepsilon>0
is sufficiently small. This however is an immediate consequence of condition (
2.21
), inequalities (
2.13
) and the
C^{2}
-regularity of nonlinear terms. By Theorem
5.2
applied to
\boldsymbol{\varphi}=\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{U}
and
\psi=v-V
, if

(6.14)

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{u}_{0}-\boldsymbol{U}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\|v_{0}-V\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}<K/(4C^{2}),

then

(6.15)

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{U}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\|v-V\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leqslant C\big(\|\boldsymbol{u}_{0}-\boldsymbol{U}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\|v_{0}-V\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\big)e^{-\frac{k}{2}t},

for all
t\in[0,\,T]
. Since the constants here are independent of
T
this solution and the inequality can be extended to all
t>0
.
∎

7.
Example

7.1.
Model example

We apply results from this work to the following reaction-diffusion-ODE model

(7.1)

\displaystyle u_{t}

\displaystyle=u(1-u)(u-\beta)-v,

\displaystyle\text{for}\;x\in\overline{\Omega},\;t>0,

\displaystyle v_{t}

\displaystyle=\gamma\Delta_{\nu}v+\sigma u-\delta v-\rho,

\displaystyle\text{for}\;x\in\Omega,\;t>0,

supplemented with the initial conditions

(7.2)

\displaystyle u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\quad v(0,x)=v_{0}(x),

where
\beta
,
\sigma
,
\delta
,
\rho
are positive constants and
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
(with arbitrary
N\geqslant 1
) is an open bounded domain. Here we are motivated by the FitzHugh-Nagumo system

(7.3)

\displaystyle u_{t}=\varepsilon^{2}u_{xx}+u(1-u)(u-\beta)-v,\quad v_{t}=\sigma u-\delta v-\rho

which was used to model pulse propagation in excitable media, see
e.g.

[
11
,
21
,
36
,
41
]
. The following generalisation of the FitzHugh-Nagumo system

(7.4)

\displaystyle u_{t}=\varepsilon^{2}\Delta_{\nu}u+u(1-u)(u-\beta)-v,\quad v_{t}=\gamma\Delta_{\nu}v+\sigma u-\delta v-\rho

was studied in the context of pattern formation (
[
7
,
20
,
38
]
). This system has traveling wave
solutions and other types of solutions with a complex behavior (see, e.g.,
[
40
,
4
,
3
]
and
references therein). In particular, the work
[
38
]
contains some results on a behaviour of solutions to system (
7.4
) when
\varepsilon\to 0
.

A study of the one dimensional version of the reaction-diffusion-ODE system (
7.1
) can be also motivated by
series of papers
[
17
,
25
,
22
,
23
,
28
,
30
,
29
]
where similar receptor-based models were considered.

The paper
[
26
]
contains a construction of a family of discontinuous steady states of system (
7.1
) in the one dimensional case and the proof of their stability. Below, we explain how using theory developed in this work we obtain analogous results in higher dimensions.

7.2.
Constant solutions

An analysis of stationary solutions to system (
7.1
) begins by the assumption that it has a constant stationary solution, namely a vector
(\overline{U},\overline{V})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
satisfying

(7.5)

\displaystyle\overline{U}(1-\overline{U})(\overline{U}-\beta)-\overline{V}=0\qquad\text{and}\qquad\sigma\overline{U}-\delta\overline{V}-\rho=0.

On Fig.
7.1
, we choose the parameters
\beta,\sigma,\delta,\rho
in such a way to have three constant solutions
\left(\overline{U}_{1},\overline{V}_{1}\right)
,
\left(\overline{U}_{2},\overline{V}_{2}\right)
and
\left(\overline{U}_{3},\overline{V}_{3}\right)
.

Figure 7.1.
The nullclines for system (
7.1
), namely, the curves

f(U,V)\equiv{U}(1-{U})({U}-\beta)-{V}=0
and
g(U,V)\equiv\sigma U-\delta V-\rho=0
.

7.3.
Regular stationary solutions

By our previous work
[
6
]
, a stationary solution
(U,V)
is called regular if there exist a
C^{2}
-function
k
such that
U(x)=k(V(x))
. Such regular stationary solutions to problem (
7.1
)
can be immediately obtained from
[
6
, Prop. 2.5]
by a bifurcation around the constant solution
(\overline{U},\overline{V})
provided

(7.6)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\det f_{u}(\overline{U},\overline{V})}\det\begin{pmatrix}f_{u}(\overline{U},\overline{V})&f_{v}(\overline{U},\overline{V})\\
g_{u}(\overline{U},\overline{V})&g_{v}(\overline{U},\overline{V})\end{pmatrix}=\gamma\mu_{k},

where
f(u,v)=u(1-u)(u-\beta)-v
,
g(u,v)=\sigma u-\delta v-\rho
and
\mu_{k}
is an eigenvalue of
-\Delta_{\nu}
, and with the diffusion coefficient
\gamma>0
.
It follows from our work
[
6
]
that all regular stationary solutions to system (
7.1
) are unstable.

7.4.
Discontinuous stationary solutions

Now, we fix a constant stationary solutions, say,
(\overline{U},\overline{V})=(\overline{U}_{1},\overline{V}_{1})
on Fig.
7.1
. Following Assumption
2.5
, we consider two different branches of solutions to equation
{U}(1-{U})({U}-\beta)-{V}=0
in a neighbourhood of
\overline{V}_{1}
. Namely, we choose a set
\mathcal{V}\subseteq\mathbb{R}
and functions
k_{1},k_{2}\in C^{2}(\mathcal{V},\mathbb{R})
such that
\overline{V}_{1}\in\mathcal{V}
and

(7.7)

\displaystyle\begin{matrix}k_{1}(w)\big(1-{k_{1}(w)}\big)\big({k_{1}(w)}-\beta\big)-{V}=0,\\
k_{2}(w)\big(1-{k_{2}(w)}\big)\big({k_{2}(w)}-\beta\big)-{V}=0,\end{matrix}\quad\text{for each}\quad w\in\mathcal{V}

see bold curves on Fig.
7.1
. By Theorem
2.6
, for an arbitrary decomposition
\Omega_{1}\subset\Omega
and
\Omega_{2}=\Omega\setminus\overline{\Omega}_{1}

with sufficiently small
|\Omega_{2}|>0
and for all small
\varepsilon>0
there exist a stationary solution to system (
7.1
) with the following properties

•

(U,V)\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{n}\times{W^{2,p}_{\nu}(\Omega)}
;

•

V=V(x)
is a weak solution to problem

(7.8)

\displaystyle\gamma\Delta_{\nu}V+\sigma U-\delta V-\rho=0\quad\text{for}\quad x\in\Omega

and

(7.9)

\displaystyle U(x)=\begin{cases}k_{1}\big(V(x)\big),\quad x\in\Omega_{1},\\
k_{2}\big(V(x)\big),\quad x\in\Omega_{2},\end{cases}

satisfies
{U}(1-{U})({U}-\beta)-{V}=0
for almost all
x\in\Omega
;

•

the couple
(U,V)
satisfies

(7.10)

\displaystyle\|V-\overline{V}_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}<\varepsilon\quad\text{and}\quad\|U-\overline{U}_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{1})}+\|U-k_{2}(\overline{V}_{1})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{2})}<C\varepsilon.

An analogous analysis can be repeated by choosing the constant solution
(\overline{U}_{1},\overline{V}_{1})
together with the branches
k_{1}
and
k_{3}
, in the neighbourhood
\mathcal{V}\subseteq\mathbb{R}
, see Fig.
7.1
. A discontinuous stationary solution can be also obtained, if we use a constant solution
(\overline{U}_{2},\overline{V}_{2})
or
(\overline{U}_{3},\overline{V}_{3})
with two branches of either
k_{1}
or
k_{2}
or
k_{3}
with a suitable neighbourhood
\mathcal{V}\subseteq\mathbb{R}
of the points
\overline{V}_{2}
or
\overline{V}_{3}
. Here, we can also apply Remark
2.15
and consider three branches
k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}
to obtain discontinuous stationary solutions on three different sets
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2},\Omega_{3}
.

7.5.
Stability of stationary solutions

Let us discuss sufficient conditions for stability of the discontinuous stationary solution
(U,V)=\big(U(x),V(x)\big)
constructed around the point
(\overline{U}_{1},\overline{V}_{1})
with branches
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
. Assumptions
2.8
and condition (
2.19
) reduce to the inequalities

(7.11)

\displaystyle-3\overline{U}_{1}^{2}+2\overline{U}_{1}(1+\beta

\displaystyle)-\beta<0,\quad-3k_{2}(\overline{V}_{1})^{2}+2k_{2}(\overline{V}_{1})(1+\beta)-\beta<0,

\displaystyle s\begin{pmatrix}-3\overline{U}_{1}^{2}+2\overline{U}_{1}(1+\beta)-\beta&-1\\
\sigma&-\delta\end{pmatrix}<0.

The first two hold true because the function
U(1-U)(U-\beta)
is strictly decreasing around points
\overline{U}_{1}
and
k_{2}(\overline{V}_{1})
, see Fig.
7.1
. The matrix in the third inequality has eigenvalues with negative real parts if its trace is negative and the determinant is positive which is equivalent to the inequalities

(7.12)

\displaystyle-3\overline{U}_{1}^{2}+2\overline{U}_{1}(1+\beta)-\beta-\delta<0\quad\text{and}\quad-\delta(-3\overline{U}_{1}^{2}+2\overline{U}_{1}(1+\beta)-\beta)+\sigma>0.

Both of them are immediately obtained from first inequality in (
7.11
). Choosing
\gamma>0
sufficiently large as in Assumption
2.9
(this requirement can be relaxed by applying Proposition
4.3
) as well as
\varepsilon>0
and
|\Omega_{2}|>0
sufficiently small we obtain by Theorem
2.11
that the solution
(U,V)
is linearly exponentially stable. Moreover, since the conditions (
2.21
) reduce to the inequality
-\delta<0
, this solution is also nonlinearly stable in
{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{n+1}
by Theorem
2.13
.

On the other hand, every discontinuous stationary solution containing the branch
k_{3}
is linearly unstable. This is a consequence of the fact that
3k_{3}(w)^{2}+2k_{3}(w)(1+\beta)-\beta>0
for each
w\in\mathcal{V}
and
3k_{3}(U)^{2}+2k_{3}(U)(1+\beta)-\beta
belongs to the spectrum of the corresponding linearisation operator by equation (
4.5
), see Remark
2.12
for more comments.

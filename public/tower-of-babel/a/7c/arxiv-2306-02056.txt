Abstract
. We give a new short proof of the theorem due to Marquis and Sabok, which states that the orbit equivalence relation induced by the action of a finitely generated hyperbolic group on its Gromov boundary is hyperfinite. Our methods permit moreover to show that every such action has finite Borel asymptotic dimension.



1.
Introduction

The study of countable Borel equivalence relations is a prominent subject in descriptive set theory, whose interactions with dynamics have been thriving ever since
Feldman and Moore showed in
[
5
]
that every countable Borel equivalence relation is equal to the orbit equivalence relation of a Borel action
by a countable group. Due to this characterisation, the problem of understanding which Borel actions generate hyperfinite equivalence relations
has attracted considerable attention in the past decades.

This line of research began with Weiss’ seminal work
[
15
]
, where he proved that any Borel action of
{\mathbb{Z}}
gives rise to a hyperfinite orbit equivalence relation, and asked whether this holds for all amenable groups.
His question was
followed by more than 30 years of research during which larger and larger classes of amenable groups have been shown to generate hyperfinite
equivalence relations (
[
9
,
6
,
14
]
), culminating in the recent paper
[
3
]
. The main novelty of
[
3
]
is the
notion of
Borel asymptotic dimension
(Definition
4.1
),
a Borel analogue of the homonymous large-scale invariant introduced by Gromov
in
[
7
]
, which provides a sufficient condition to verify hyperfiniteness. More precisely, if
X
is a standard Borel space
with extended metric
\rho
(i.e. which also allows value
\infty
)
and
(X,\rho)
has finite Borel asymptotic dimension, then the finite-distance
equivalence relation is hyperfinite (
[
3
, Theorem 1.7]
).
Thanks to this innovative approach, the authors of
[
3
]
were able to
provide a positive answer to Weiss’ question for the largest class of amenable groups to date.

Turning to non-amenable groups, Weiss’ question is known to have negative answer. Indeed, every non-amenable group admits a Borel action whose orbit equivalence relation is not hyperfinite (
[
9
, Corollary 1.8]
). Nevertheless, it is still worthwhile to study specific natural actions of non-amenable groups, particularly when the orbit equivalence relation is amenable.
In this direction,
[
4
, Corollary 8.2]
shows that the tail equivalence relation on
S^{\mathbb{N}}
is hyperfinite whenever
S
is countable. An immediate
consequence of this is that the orbit equivalence relation of the action of a finitely generated free group
\mathbb{F}
on its Gromov boundary

\partial\mathbb{F}
is hyperfinite. Indeed,
\partial\mathbb{F}
naturally
embeds into
S^{\mathbb{N}}
, where
S
is a symmetric set of free generators,
and with this identification the orbit equivalence relation on
\partial\mathbb{F}
coincides with the tail equivalence on
S^{\mathbb{N}}
.

Building on this result, in
[
8
]
it is proved that
the orbit equivalence relation of the boundary action
G\curvearrowright\partial G
is hyperfinite for all cubulated hyperbolic groups
G
, and this was later successfully extended in
[
12
]
to all finitely generated hyperbolic groups. More recently, a further generalization of these methods was
carried in
[
10
]
, where a similar theorem is obtained for
relatively hyperbolic groups. More results on hyperfiniteness of orbit equivalence relations of boundary actions can
be found in
[
13
]
.

The key difference between a general hyperbolic group, equipped with a finite generating set
S
, and a free group is that for the former there is no canonical embedding
\partial G\hookrightarrow S^{\mathbb{N}}
that maps the orbit equivalence to a well-understood equivalence relation of the image. To fill this gap, the papers
[
8
,
12
,
10
]
develop sophisticated geometric techniques to study families of geodesic ray bundles, and to adapt the methods used in
[
4
]

to general boundary actions of hyperbolic groups.

The first main contribution of this paper is a new short proof of the main result
of
[
12
]
, stated explicitly below, which avoids most
of the geometric machinery employed in
[
12
]
and provides a direct
connection between the orbit equivalence relation of
G\curvearrowright\partial G
and the tail equivalence on
S^{\mathbb{N}}
.

Theorem A

(
[
12
, Theorem A]
)
.

The orbit equivalence relation induced by the action of a finitely generated hyperbolic group on its Gromov boundary is hyperfinite.

Our strategy to prove Theorem
A
starts with fixing
some linear order on a finite generating set
S
of
G
. This induces a
well-defined Borel function mapping each
\eta\in\partial G
to the unique geodesic ray converging to it which is minimal (or rather whose
type
is minimal, see §
3
) in the lexicographical order on
S^{\mathbb{N}}
. That provides an embedding
\Phi\colon\partial G\hookrightarrow S^{\mathbb{N}}
(Definition
3.2
). While it might not be the case that the orbit equivalence is mapped to the tail equivalence under this map, we show in Lemma
4.3
that, thanks to hyperbolicity, the former contains only finitely many classes of the latter. The result then follows from
[
9
, Proposition 1.3 (vii)]
.

Another advantage of this approach is that our methods can be easily adapted to the framework introduced in
[
3
]
. Indeed, if
\rho_{s}

is the extended metric on
S^{\mathbb{N}}
induced by the left shift
s\colon S^{\mathbb{N}}\to S^{\mathbb{N}}
, then
[
3
, Lemma 8.3]
shows that
(S^{\mathbb{N}},\rho_{s})

has Borel asymptotic dimension 1. Our technical result
Lemma
4.3
shows that we can essentially pull back this information
via the aforementioned embedding
\Phi\colon\partial G\to S^{\mathbb{N}}
to

\partial G
, and obtain the following result.

Theorem B
.

The action of a finitely generated hyperbolic group on its Gromov boundary has finite Borel asymptotic dimension.

The paper is organized as follows. In §
2
we recall some preliminaries on hyperbolic groups and prove a few basic facts about geodesic rays. In §
3
we isolate a specific Borel
uniformizing function from
\partial G
to
S^{\mathbb{N}}
. In §
4
we recall the definitions of hyperfiniteness and Borel asymptotic dimension and give the proofs of Theorems
A
and
B
.

2.
Preliminaries on hyperboilc groups

We start with some essential preliminaries and notation on graphs, geodesic rays and hyperbolicity. We refer to
[
12
]
*§2
for a more detailed, self-contained introduction on these topics (for a
more throughout presentation of this subject we recommend instead
[
2
]
).

A
graph

X=(V,{\mathcal{E}})
is a pair composed of a set of
vertices

V
and a (symmetric) set of
edges

{\mathcal{E}}\subseteq V\times V
.
Two vertices
x,y\in V
are
adjacent

if
(x,y)\in{\mathcal{E}}
, and a
path

\Gamma
in
X
is a (possibly infinite) sequence of vertices
(x_{n})_{n\in I}
indexed by an interval
I\subseteq{\mathbb{N}}
such that
(x_{n},x_{n+1})\in{\mathcal{E}}

for every
n\in I
. We shall sometimes denote
x_{n}
by
\Gamma(n)
.
The
graph metric
on
V
is defined by setting
d(x,y)
as the smallest possible length of a path
joining
x
and
y
, and it has value
\infty
if no such path exists. A
geodesic path
is a path
(x_{n})_{n\in I}

such that
d(x_{n},x_{m})=|n-m|
for every
n,m\in I
.
Two infinite geodesic paths
\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1}
are
asymptotic
if their Hausdorff
distance
d_{H}(\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1})
is finite. The
visual boundary

\partial X
of the graph
X
is the set of equivalence classes
of asymptotic infinite geodesic paths. Given
x\in V
and
\eta\in\partial X
, a
combinatorial geodesic ray
(or simply a
CGR
)
from
x
to
\eta
is a geodesic path
\Gamma=(x_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
whose equivalence class in
\partial X
is
\eta
and such that
x_{0}=x
.
In this case, we say that
\Gamma

starts
at
x_{0}
and
converges
to
\eta
.
Let
{\mathrm{CGR}}(x,\eta)
denote the set of all CGR starting from
x
and
converging to
\eta
.

Given
\delta>0
, a locally finite graph
X=(V,{\mathcal{E}})
(i.e. such that
every vertex has finitely many adjacent vertices)
is
\delta
-hyperbolic
if
for every geodesic triangle (a triangle whose sides are geodesic paths)
\Delta
of
X
,
each side of
\Delta
is contained in the
\delta
-neighborhood of the other
two sides. In this case
\partial X
can be equipped with a compact metrizable topology as follows. Fix a base point
x\in V
.
We say that a sequence
\eta_{n}\in\partial X
converges to some
\eta\in\partial X

if and only if there are
\Gamma_{n}\in{\mathrm{CGR}}(x,\eta_{n})
such that every subsequence of
(\Gamma_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
has a subsequence
converging to some
\Gamma\in{\mathrm{CGR}}(x,\eta)
in the space
V^{\mathbb{N}}
equipped with the product topology resulting from the discrete topology on
V
. The set
\partial X
equipped with such topology, which does not depend on the choice of
the base point
x
, is called the
Gromov boundary
of
X
(see
[
2
]
*§III.3).

Let
G
be a finitely generated group and let
S
be a finite symmetric generating set of
G
. The
Cayley graph

\mathrm{Cay}(G,S)
is the locally finite graph whose vertex set is
G
and where
g,h\in G
are adjacent if and only if
g^{-1}h\in S
.
The group
G
is
hyperbolic
is there exists
\delta>0
such that
\mathrm{Cay}(G,S)
is
\delta
-hyperbolic. It is well-known that being hyperbolic is invariant with respect of quasi-isometry, and therefore it does not depend on the choice of
S
.
We denote
\partial\mathrm{Cay}(G,S)
simply as
\partial G
, since also its topology does not depend on
S
(see
[
2
]
*Theorem 3.9). The action
of
G
on its Cayley graph naturally induces an action on combinatorial
geodesic rays which preserves asymptoticity. This in turn results
in a continuous amenable action
G\curvearrowright\partial G
on its Gromov boundary (see e.g.
[
1
, Theorem B.3.4]
).

We isolate a couple of elementary properties of hyperbolic graphs which will play an important role in our arguments in §
4
.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
2
]
*Lemma III.3.3)
.

Let
X
be a
\delta
-hyperbolic graph and let
\eta\in\partial X
. Suppose that
\Gamma_{0}=\left(x_{n}\right)_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
and
\Gamma_{1}=\left(y_{n}\right)_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
are CGR converging to
\eta
.

(1)

If
x_{0}=y_{0}
then

d(x_{n},y_{n})\leq 2\delta,\text{ for every }n\in{\mathbb{N}}.

(2)

In general, there are

0\leq T_{0},T_{1}\leq 2(d(x_{0},y_{0})+3\delta+2)

such that

d(x_{T_{0}+n},y_{T_{1}+n})\leq 5\delta,\text{ for every }n\in{\mathbb{N}}.

Proof.

Item
(1)
is exactly
[
2
]
*Lemma III.3.3.(1).

For item
(2)
, the existence of
T_{0},T_{1}\geq 0
such that
d(x_{T_{0}+n},y_{T_{1}+n})\leq 5\delta
for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
is granted by
[
2
]
*Lemma III.3.3.(2). The upper bound
T_{0},T_{1}\leq 2(d(x_{0},y_{0})+3\delta+2)
does not appear explicitly in the lemma, but can be deduced from the proof therein.

More precisely, let
c_{n}
be a sequence of geodesic paths from

x_{0}
to
y_{n}
. As
X
is locally finite,
there exists a subsequence of
(c_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
converging to some CGR
\Gamma
whose starting point is
x_{0}
. Given
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, consider the
geodesic triangle whose edges are
c_{0}
,
c_{n}
and the initial segment
(y_{0},\dots,y_{n})
of
\Gamma_{1}
. By
\delta
-hyperbolicity, the path
c_{n}
is contained in a
\delta
-neighborhood of
c_{0}\cup\Gamma_{1}
. We claim that the vertex
c_{n}(m)
belongs to a
\delta
-neighborhood of

\Gamma_{1}
for every
m\geq d(x_{0},y_{0})+\delta+1
and every
n
for which
c_{n}(m)
is defined. Indeed, if this were not the case,
then
c_{n}(m)
would belong to a
\delta
-neighborhood of
c_{0}
, hence
giving

d(c_{n}(m),x_{0})\leq d(x_{0},y_{0})+\delta<m,

and contradicting the fact that

c_{n}
is a geodesic path from
x_{0}
to
c_{n}(m)
. This in particular
implies that
\Gamma
converges to
\eta
. A similar argument shows
that if
R_{0}=d(x_{0},y_{0})+\lceil\delta\rceil+1
and
m\geq 2(d(x_{0},y_{0})+\delta+1)
, then

d(c_{n}(R_{0}),y_{m})>\delta
.

Summarizing, this means
that there exists
R_{1}\leq 2(d(x_{0},y_{0})+\delta+1)
such that

d(\Gamma(R_{0}),y_{R_{1}})\leq\delta
. Notice that, given
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the
vertex
\Gamma(R_{0}+n)
could be in the
\delta
-neighborhood of a
y_{\ell}
for
\ell<R_{1}
,
but by the triangle inequality there exists a minimum
k_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
for every
n\geq k_{0}
there is
n^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{N}}
satisfying

d(\Gamma(R_{0}+n),y_{R_{1}+n^{\prime}})\leq\delta.

Again by the triangle inequality it follows that
|n-n^{\prime}|\leq 2\delta
,
hence

d(\Gamma(R_{0}+n),y_{R_{1}+n})\leq 3\delta\text{ for every }n\geq k_{0}.

Since both
\Gamma_{0}
and
\Gamma
belong to
{\mathrm{CGR}}(x_{0},\eta)
, then

d(\Gamma(n),x_{n})\leq 2\delta
for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}

by part
(1)
, thus we get

d(x_{R_{0}+k_{0}+n},y_{R_{1}+k_{0}+n})\leq 5\delta\text{ for every }n\in{\mathbb{N}}.

We claim that
T_{i}=R_{i}+k_{0}
satisfy the required bound.
If
k_{0}=0
there is nothing to prove since both
R_{0}
and
R_{1}

are smaller than
2(d(x_{0},y_{0})+\delta+1)
. If instead
k_{0}>0
, let
m\in{\mathbb{N}}

be such that

d(\Gamma(R_{0}+k_{0}),y_{R_{1}+m})\leq\delta.

The triangle inequality implies
|k_{0}-m|\leq 2\delta
and,
as
\Gamma(R_{0}+k_{0}-1)
is in the
\delta
-neighborhood of some
y_{s}
for
s<R_{1}
, it also implies
m\leq 2\delta+1
. We can thus conclude that

k_{0}\leq 4\delta+1
, and that
T_{0}
and
T_{1}
satisfy the required upper bound also when
k_{0}>0
.
∎

Given a finite path
\Gamma_{0}=(x_{n})_{n<k}
and a path
\Gamma_{1}=(y_{n})_{n\in I}
in a graph
X=(V,\mathcal{E})
such that
(x_{k-1},y_{0})\in\mathcal{E}
, we denote by

\Gamma_{0}\cdot\Gamma_{1}
the path which starts with
\Gamma_{0}
and then continues along
\Gamma_{1}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
X
be a graph,
let
\Gamma=\left(x_{n}\right)_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a CGR, let
n_{1}>n_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}
and let
\Gamma_{01}
be any geodesic path from
x_{n_{0}}
to
x_{n_{1}}
. Then
\left(x_{n}\right)_{n<n_{0}}\cdot\Gamma_{01}\cdot\left(x_{n}\right)_{n>n_{1}}
is a CGR.

Proof.

By assumption
\Gamma
is a CGR, hence
d(x_{0},x_{n-1})=n-1
for
every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Let
\left(y_{n}\right)_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}=\left(x_{n}\right)_{n<n_{1}}\cdot\Gamma_{01}\cdot\left(x_{n}\right)_{n>n_{2}}
and suppose that it is not a CGR. Then there exists
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that for all
n>N
we have
d(y_{0},y_{n-1})<n-1
. However,
y_{0}=x_{0}
and
\Gamma_{01}
is a geodesic path between
x_{n_{0}}

and
x_{n_{1}}
,
hence
y_{n}=x_{n}
for every
n>n_{1}
. This contradicts the fact that
\left(x_{n}\right)_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is a CGR.
∎

3.
Borel Uniformizing Functions

Given a hyperbolic group
G
with finite symmetric generating set
S
,
in this section we define a specific Borel injection which associates to each
\eta\in\partial G
a sequence in
S^{\mathbb{N}}
. Let
\Gamma=(x_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\in G^{\mathbb{N}}
be a CGR in
\text{Cay}(G,S)
. Its
type

\text{typ}(\Gamma)
is defined as

\text{typ}(\Gamma)=(x_{n}^{-1}x_{n+1})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\in S^{\mathbb{N}}.

We use an analogous notation also for finite paths.

In what follows we consider
\partial G
with its topology as Gromov boundary, while
S^{\mathbb{N}}
and
G^{\mathbb{N}}
are endowed with the product topology resulting from the
discrete topology on
S
and
G
respectively.

Proposition 3.1

(
[
12
]
*Lemma 5.1)
.

The set
{\mathrm{CGR}}(e,\eta)
is compact in
G^{\mathbb{N}}
.

Proof.

As
\text{Cay}(G,S)
is locally finite, any sequence
\left(\Gamma_{n}\right)_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subseteq{\mathrm{CGR}}(e,\eta)
has a subsequence converging to some geodesic ray
\Gamma
. It follows from Lemma
2.1
that
d(\Gamma(m),\Gamma_{0}(m))<2\delta
for all
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
which shows that
\Gamma
converges to
\eta
.
∎

Definition 3.2
.

Let
G
be a hyperbolic group with finite symmetric generating set
S
. Fix a total order on
S
and let
\leq_{\mathrm{lex}}
be the resulting lexicographical order on
S^{\mathbb{N}}
. Consider the map

\displaystyle\Phi\colon\partial G

\displaystyle\to S^{\mathbb{N}}

\displaystyle\eta

\displaystyle\mapsto\sigma_{\eta}

where
\sigma_{\eta}
is the unique element in
S^{\mathbb{N}}
such that

\sigma_{\eta}=\min\nolimits_{\leq_{\mathrm{lex}}}\{\text{typ}(\Gamma)\mid\Gamma\in{\mathrm{CGR}}(e,\eta)\},

with
e
being the identity element in
G
. The existence of such minimal element follows from Proposition
3.1
.

Proposition 3.3
.

The function
\Phi\colon\partial G\to S^{\mathbb{N}}
in Definition

3.2
is a Borel injection.

Proof.

Consider the set

A=\{(\eta,\Gamma)\in\partial G\times G^{\mathbb{N}}\mid\Gamma\in\text{CGR}(e,\eta)\}.

The proof of
[
12
]
*Claim 6.3 shows that
A
is closed in
\partial G\times G^{\mathbb{N}}
.
Moreover, given
\eta\in\partial G
, the section

A_{\eta}=\{\Gamma\in G^{\mathbb{N}}\mid(\eta,\Gamma)\in A\}

is precisely
{\mathrm{CGR}}(e,\eta)
which is a compact subset of
G^{\mathbb{N}}
by Proposition
3.1
. Then by
[
11
]
*Theorem 28.8
the map

\displaystyle\Theta\colon\partial G

\displaystyle\to K(G^{\mathbb{N}})

\displaystyle\eta

\displaystyle\mapsto{\mathrm{CGR}}(e,\eta)

is Borel, where
K(G^{\mathbb{N}})
is the space of compact
subsets if
G^{\mathbb{N}}
with the Vietoris topology (see
[
11
, §I.4.F]
). Consider next the function

\displaystyle\Psi\colon K(G^{\mathbb{N}})

\displaystyle\to S^{\mathbb{N}}

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle\mapsto\min\nolimits_{\leq_{\mathrm{lex}}}\{\text{typ}(\Gamma)\mid\Gamma\in F\}.

We claim that also
\Psi
is Borel. Indeed,
let
U
be the basic open set in
S^{\mathbb{N}}
consisting of all sequences starting with some

(s_{0},\dots,s_{k-1})\in S^{k}
. Let
U^{\prime}
be the open set in
G^{\mathbb{N}}

of all elements starting with
(x_{0},\dots,x_{k-1})
, where
x_{n}=s_{0}s_{1}\dots s_{n}
for every
n<k
. Given
\bar{y}=(y_{0},\dots,y_{k-1})\in G^{k}
denote the corresponding basic open set in
G^{\mathbb{N}}
as

V_{\bar{y}}=\{(z_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\in G^{\mathbb{N}}\mid z_{n}=y_{n}\text{ for all }n<k\}.

Let
C=\{\bar{y}\in G^{k}\mid(s_{0},\dots,s_{k-1})\leq_{\mathrm{lex}}\text{typ}(\bar{y})\}
and consider the open set

V=\bigcup_{\bar{y}\in C}V_{\bar{y}}.

The preimage
\Psi^{-1}(U)
consists of all
F\in K(G^{\mathbb{N}})

which intersect
U^{\prime}
and which are contained in
V
, and it is therefore
and open set in the Vietoris topology of
K(G^{\mathbb{N}})
. We can thus conclude that
\Phi=\Psi\circ\Theta
is Borel.

Injectivity of
\Phi
follows since any two CGR which start from the same point and have the same type must be equal.
∎

4.
Hyperfiniteness and Borel Asymptotic Dimension

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
4.2
, which covers both Theorem
A
and
B
. We begin with
some preliminaries on Borel equivalence relations and by recalling
the notion of Borel asymptotic dimension as introduced in
[
3
]
.

An
extended metric

\rho
on a set
X
is a metric on
X
which can also take
value
\infty
. For
x\in X
and
r>0
, let
B_{\rho}(x,r)
denote
the
\rho
-ball of radius
r
centered in
x
.
Let
E_{\rho}
be the equivalence relation of finite distance between points

E_{\rho}=\{(x,y)\in X\times X\mid\rho(x,y)<\infty\}.

Given a standard Borel space
X
, an equivalence relation
E
on
X
is

Borel
if
E
is a Borel subset of
X\times X
. A Borel equivalence
relation
E
is
finite
(respectively
countable
) if all its equivalence classes are finite (respectively countable),
and it is
hyperfinite
if it can be written as a union
of an increasing sequence of finite Borel equivalence relations.

A
Borel extended metric space

(X,\rho)
is a standard Borel space
paired with a Borel extended metric
\rho
. In this case
E_{\rho}
is a Borel
equivalence relation on
X
. An equivalence relation
E
on a Borel extended metric space
(X,\rho)
is
uniformly bounded
if there exists a uniform finite bound
on the
\rho
-diameter of the
E
-classes.
A graph
X=(V,{\mathcal{E}})

is a
Borel graph
if
V
is a standard Borel space and the edge relation

{\mathcal{E}}
is a Borel subset of
V\times V
. Note that the graph metric endows
V

with a structure of Borel extended metric space.

We will mainly be concerned with two types of Borel graphs.
The first one is
the
Schreier graph
of a Borel action of a finitely generated group
G

on a standard Borel space
X
. Given a finite symmetric generating set
S

of
G
, the Schreier graph of the action
G\curvearrowright X

has
X
as vertex set, and two points
x,y\in X
are adjacent
if and only if there is
g\in S
such that
gx=y
. We remark that all vertices of such graph have degree at most
|S|
, hence for every
r>0

all balls of radius
r
(in the graph metric) have size at most
|S|^{r}
.

The
orbit
equivalence relation

E_{G}^{X}
on
X
is defined as

xE_{G}^{X}y\iff\exists g\in G\text{ s.t. }gx=y,\text{ for }x,y\in X.

Note that the orbit equivalence relation is the same as the finite-distance equivalence relation induced by the graph metric on
X
.

The second kind of Borel graphs we focus on arises from Borel
functions. Let
X
be a standard Borel space and let
f\colon X\to X

be a Borel function. We let
\mathcal{G}_{f}
be the Borel graph whose
vertex set is

, and where
x,y\in X
are adjacent if and only if

f(x)=y
or
f(y)=x
. We denote the graph metric on
\mathcal{G}_{f}
by
\rho_{f}
. The typical example we shall consider is
the left shift on
X=S^{\mathbb{N}}
for a finite set

(4.1)

\displaystyle s\colon S^{\mathbb{N}}

\displaystyle\to S^{\mathbb{N}}

\displaystyle(x_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}

\displaystyle\mapsto(x_{n+1})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}

In this case the finite-distance equivalence relation

corresponds to the

on sequences in

, that
is for
\sigma_{0}=(x_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}},\sigma_{1}=(y_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\in S^{\mathbb{N}}

\sigma_{0}R\sigma_{1}\iff\exists T_{0},T_{1}\in{\mathbb{N}}\text{ s.t. }x_{T_{0}+n}=y_{T_{1}+n},\text{ for every }n\in{\mathbb{N}}.

Finally, we recall the notion of Borel asymptotic dimension.

Definition 4.1

(
[
3
, Definition 3.2]
)
.

Let
(X,\rho)
be a Borel extended metric space such that
E_{\rho}

is countable. The
Borel
asymptotic dimension

\text{asdim}_{B}(X,\rho)
of

is the smallest

such that for every

there exists a
uniformly bounded Borel equivalence relation
E
on

such that for
every

the ball

intersects at most
d+1
classes of

,
and it is
\infty
if no such

exists.

If
G\curvearrowright X
is a Borel action, then we denote the Borel
asymptotic dimension of the corresponding Schreier graph simply as

\text{asdim}_{B}(G\curvearrowright X)
(note that it does not depend on the choice of the generating set
S
of
G
inducing the metric on

by
[
3
, Lemma 2.2]
).

The main goal of this section is proving the following theorem.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
G
be a hyperbolic group with finite symmetric generating set
S
.

(1)

The orbit equivalence relation
E^{\partial G}_{G}
induced by the boundary action
G\curvearrowright\partial G
is hyperfinite.

(2)

Let
\delta>0
be such that
\text{Cay}(G,S)
is

\delta
-hyperbolic and let
M_{5\delta}\in{\mathbb{N}}
be a bound on the size of all balls of radius
5\delta
in
\text{Cay}(G,S)
.
Then
\text{asdim}_{B}(G\curvearrowright\partial G)\leq 2M_{5\delta}^{2}-1
.

The main motivation behind Definition
4.1
is
[
3
, Theorem 1.7]
, stating that
if a Borel extended metric space
(X,\rho)
has finite Borel asymptotic dimension, then the equivalence relation
E_{\rho}
is hyperfinite. In view of this, it would be enough to prove
item
(2)
to also obtain part
(1)
.
We point out, however, that a small modification of the methods employed
to prove the second statement of Theorem
4.2
can be used
to show that
E_{G}^{\partial G}
is hyperfinite, without having to resort
to the theory developed in
[
3
]
. We will include this more direct proof of the first part of Theorem
4.2
for the reader’s convenience.

Let us first consider Theorem
4.2
in the case where
G=\mathbb{F}_{2}
, the free group with two generators
a,b
. Let
S=\{a,b,a^{-1},b^{-1}\}
and let
s\colon S^{\mathbb{N}}\to S^{\mathbb{N}}
be the left shift on
S^{\mathbb{N}}
.
Each
\eta\in\partial\mathbb{F}_{2}

has a unique representative in
{\mathrm{CGR}}(e,\eta)
, and this gives a Borel injection
\Phi:\eta\mapsto\sigma_{\eta}
from
\partial\mathbb{F}_{2}
to
S^{\mathbb{N}}
such that

(4.2)

\text{either}\ \sigma_{g\eta}=s(\sigma_{\eta})\ \text{or}\ \sigma_{\eta}=s(\sigma_{g\eta})\text{ for every }\eta\in\partial\mathbb{F}_{2}\text{ and }g\in S.

It follows that
\Phi
maps the Shreier graph metric on
\partial\mathbb{F}_{2}
to the metric
\rho_{s}
on
S^{\mathbb{N}}
. Thanks to this, the analysis of the orbit equivalence relation on
\partial\mathbb{F}_{2}

is equivalent to that of the tail equivalence relation on
S^{\mathbb{N}}
, hence item
(1)
of Theorem
4.2
directly follows from

[
4
, Corollary 8.2]
and item
(2)
from
[
3
, Lemma 8.3]
. Our strategy to prove Theorem
4.2
is to use the Borel injection
\Phi
from Definition
3.2
to have a similar reduction to the tail equivalence relation
on
S^{\mathbb{N}}
. The situation is however more chaotic for a general hyperbolic group, since we cannot expect for an equality like the one in (
4.2
) to hold.
Nevertheless, hyperbolicity grants a sufficient control of the tail equivalence classes of
\sigma_{g\eta}
, as
g
varies in a given bounded ball around the identity of
G
,
to fill this gap.

This will be made precise in the following lemma, for which we introduce some
notation. Let
G
be a hyperbolic group with finite symmetric generating set
S
,
let
\delta>0
be such that
\text{Cay}(G,S)
is

\delta
-hyperbolic and let
M_{5\delta}\in{\mathbb{N}}
be an upper bound for the cardinality of the balls of radius
5\delta
in
\text{Cay}(G,S)
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
G
be a hyperbolic group with finite symmetric generating set
S
.
Fix
\eta\in\partial G
,

r>0
, let
\rho
be the graph metric on
\partial G
induced by the action
G\curvearrowright\partial G
, and let
\rho_{s}
be the extended
metric on
S^{\mathbb{N}}
induced by the left shift
s\colon S^{\mathbb{N}}\to S^{\mathbb{N}}
. Finally, let
\{\sigma_{\zeta}\}_{\zeta\in B_{\rho}(\eta,r)}\subseteq S^{\mathbb{N}}
be the family of types of CGR given in Definition
3.2
.
Then
\{\sigma_{\zeta}\}_{\zeta\in B_{\rho}(\eta,r)}
can be partitioned into at most
(M_{5\delta})^{2}
sets, each one with
\rho_{s}
-diameter smaller than
8(r+3\delta+2)
.
In particular, the set
\{\sigma_{\zeta}\}_{\zeta\in B_{\rho}(\eta,r)}
meets
at most
(M_{5\delta})^{2}
tail equivalence classes of
S^{\mathbb{N}}
.

Proof.

Given
\zeta\in\partial G
, let
\Gamma_{\zeta}\in{\mathrm{CGR}}(e,\zeta)
be the unique CGR starting from the identity
e\in G
such that

\text{typ}(\Gamma_{\zeta})=\sigma_{\zeta}
. Fix
\zeta\in B_{\rho}(\eta,r)
and let
g_{\zeta}\in G
be such that
d(e,g_{\zeta})<r
and
g_{\zeta}\zeta=\eta
. Set
\Gamma_{\eta}=(x_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
,
\Gamma_{\zeta}=(z_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
and let
g_{\zeta}\Gamma_{\zeta}=(y_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\in G^{\mathbb{N}}
be the sequence where
y_{n}=g_{\zeta}z_{n}
for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Then

g_{\zeta}\Gamma_{\zeta}\in{\mathrm{CGR}}(g_{\zeta},\eta)
.

Set
T=2(r+3\delta+2)
. By Lemma
2.1
, there is
0\leq T_{\zeta}\leq 2T
such that

d\left(x_{T+n},y_{T_{\zeta}+n}\right)\leq 5\delta,\text{ for every }n\in{\mathbb{N}}.

Note that
g_{\zeta}\Gamma_{\zeta}
is the unique ray in
{\mathrm{CGR}}(g_{\zeta},\eta)

such that

(4.3)

\text{typ}(g_{\zeta}\Gamma_{\zeta})=\min\nolimits_{\leq_{\mathrm{lex}}}\{\text{typ}(\Gamma)\mid\Gamma\in{\mathrm{CGR}}(g_{\zeta},\eta)\},

since
\sigma_{\zeta}
is the
\leq_{\mathrm{lex}}
-minimum of
\text{typ}({\mathrm{CGR}}(e,\zeta))
and shifting by
g_{\zeta}
gives a bijection
between
{\mathrm{CGR}}(e,\zeta)
and
{\mathrm{CGR}}(g_{\zeta},\eta)
that preserves the type.

Fix
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
. By Lemma
2.2
and (
4.3
)
the path
p=\left(y_{T_{\zeta}+n}\right)_{n=0}^{N}
is the unique geodesic between
y_{T_{\zeta}}
and
y_{T_{\zeta}+N}
such that

\text{typ}(p)=\min\nolimits_{\leq_{\mathrm{lex}}}\{q\in S^{N}\mid q=\text{typ}(r)\text{ for some geodesic path }r\text{ from }y_{T_{\zeta}}\text{ to }y_{T_{\zeta}+N}\}.

Summarizing, the partial segment of
g_{\zeta}\Gamma_{\zeta}
between the
coordinates
T_{\zeta}
to
T_{\zeta}+N
only depends on the initial point
y_{T_{\zeta}}\in B_{d}(x_{T},5\delta)
and on
y_{T_{\zeta}+N}\in B_{d}(x_{T+N},5\delta)
. Since this is true
for every
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
, there are at most
(M_{5\delta})^{2}
choices for

\left(y_{T_{\zeta}+n}\right)_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
as
\zeta
varies in
B_{\rho}(\eta,r)
.
Since
\text{typ}(g\Gamma_{\zeta})=\text{typ}(\Gamma_{\zeta})=\sigma_{\zeta}
for every
\zeta\in\partial G
and every
g\in G
, the conclusion of the lemma follows.
∎

.

Note that the equivalence relation

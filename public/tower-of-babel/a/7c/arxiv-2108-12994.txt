On the Chromatic Vertex Stability Number of Graphs
Saieed Akbari, Arash Beikmohammadi, Sandi Klavžar, Nazanin Movarraei

Abstract
The chromatic vertex (resp. edge) stability number vs χ ​ ( G ) {\rm vs}_{\chi}(G) (resp. es χ ​ ( G ) {\rm es}_{\chi}(G) ) of a graph G G is the minimum number of vertices (resp. edges) whose deletion results in a graph H H with χ ⁡ ( H ) = χ ⁡ ( G ) − 1 \chi(H)=\chi(G)-1 . In the main result it is proved that if G G is a graph with χ ⁡ ( G ) ∈ { Δ ⁡ ( G ) , Δ ⁡ ( G ) + 1 } \chi(G)\in\{\Delta(G),\Delta(G)+1\} , then vs χ ​ ( G ) = ivs χ ​ ( G ) {\rm vs}_{\chi}(G)={\rm ivs}_{\chi}(G) , where ivs χ ​ ( G ) {\rm ivs}_{\chi}(G) is the independent chromatic vertex stability number. The result need not hold for graphs G G with χ ⁡ ( G ) ≤ Δ ⁡ ( G ) + 1 2 \chi(G)\leq\frac{\Delta(G)+1}{2} . It is proved that if χ ⁡ ( G ) > Δ ⁡ ( G ) 2 + 1 \chi(G)>\frac{\Delta(G)}{2}+1 , then vs χ ​ ( G ) = es χ ​ ( G ) {\rm vs}_{\chi}(G)={\rm es}_{\chi}(G) . A Nordhaus-Gaddum-type result on the chromatic vertex stability number is also given.

Introduction

Throughout this paper all graphs are finite, simple, and having at least one edge. The chromatic edge stability number
{\rm es}_{\chi}(G)
of a graph
G
is the minimum number of edges whose deletion results in a graph
H
with
\chi(H)=\chi(G)-1
. This natural coloring concept was introduced in 1980 by Staton
[
12
]
, and independently rediscovered much later in
[
5
]
. Nevertheless, this concept has become the subject of wider interest only recently. The paper
[
10
]
gives, among other results, a characterization of graphs with equal chromatic edge stability number and chromatic bondage number. In
[
7
]
, edge-stability critical graphs were studied. The paper
[
1
]
brings Nordhaus-Gaddum type inequality for
{\rm es}_{\chi}(G)
(stronger than a related result from
[
5
]
), sharp upper bounds on
{\rm es}_{\chi}(G)
in terms of size and of maximum degree, and a characterization of graphs with
{\rm es}_{\chi}(G)=1
among
k
-regular graphs for
k\leq 5
. In
[
8
]
progress on three open problems from
[
1
]
are reported. The chromatic edge stability number has been generalized to arbitrary graphical invariants in
[
9
]
, where in particular it was considered with respect to the chromatic index, see also
[
2
,
3
]
.

Like edge stability numbers, vertex stability numbers were introduced in the 1980s or earlier. In
[
6
]
, the
\mu
-stability of a graph
G
, where
\mu
is an arbitrary graph invariant, is defined as the minimum number of vertices whose removal changes
\mu
. The paper
[
6
]
then proceeds by investigating the stability with respect to the domination number and the independence number, which in turn led to a series papers investigation the stability with respect to these two invariants. In this paper, however, we are interested in the stability with respect to the chromatic number. At least as far as we know, this concept has not yet been explored (in
[
6
]
, the stability with respect to the chromatic number is briefly mentioned only in one sentence) which we find quite surprising since vertex versions are usually considered before edge versions. The closest investigation we are aware of is the paper
[
4
]
, where the stability with respect to the distinguishing number is investigated.

Let
G
be a graph. The
chromatic vertex stability number

{\rm vs}_{\chi}(G)
of
G
is the minimum number of vertices of
G
such that their deletion results in a graph
H
with
\chi(H)=\chi(G)-1
. For instance, it is straightforward to see that
{\rm vs}_{\chi}(P)=3
, where
P
is the Petersen graph. Note that if
\chi(G)=3
, then
{\rm vs}_{\chi}(G)
is just the minimum cardinality of a set
X\subseteq V(G)
such that the graph induced by
V(G)\setminus X
is bipartite.

We also introduce the
independent chromatic vertex stability number
,
{\rm ivs}_{\chi}(G)
, of
G
as the minimum number of independent vertices such that their deletion results in a graph
H
with
\chi(H)=\chi(G)-1
. Then our main result reads as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

If
G
is a graph with
\chi(G)\in\{\Delta(G),\Delta(G)+1\}
, then
{\rm vs}_{\chi}(G)={\rm ivs}_{\chi}(G)
.

The paper is structured as follows. In the rest of this section we recall needed definitions and concepts. In Section
2
we prove Theorem
1.1
. In the subsequent section we show that Theorem
1.1
need not hold for graphs
G
with
\chi(G)\leq\frac{\Delta(G)+1}{2}
, and discuss a possible threshold function
f(\Delta(G))
that would guarantee that if
\chi(G)\geq f(\Delta(G))
, then
{\rm vs}_{\chi}(G)={\rm ivs}_{\chi}(G)
. In the final section we prove that if
\chi(G)>\frac{\Delta(G)}{2}+1
, then
{\rm vs}_{\chi}(G)={\rm es}_{\chi}(G)
, and give a Nordhaus-Gaddum-type result on the chromatic vertex stability number.

Given a graph
G=(V(G),E(G))
, a function
c:V(G)\to[k]=\{1,\ldots,k\}
with

for each edge

is a
proper
k
-coloring
of
G
. The minimum

for which

admits a proper
k
-coloring is the
chromatic number

\chi(G)
of
G
. If
c
is a proper coloring of
G
, then the set of all vertices of
G
with color
i
,
i\in[\chi(G)]
, is a
color class
and will be denoted by
C_{i}
. The
open neighborhood
of a vertex
v
in
G
is the set of neighbors of
v
, denoted by
N_{G}(v)
, whereas the
closed neighborhood
of
v
is
N_{G}[v]=N_{G}(v)\cup\{v\}
. The
degree
of a vertex
v
in
G
is denoted by

. The subgraph of
G
induced by

will be denoted by

. The complete graph of order

is denoted by

and the complement of a graph

by
\overline{G}
. Finally, the order of

will be denoted by

.

Proof of Theorem

Lemma 2.1

. Let

be a proper
(\Delta(G)-1)
-coloring of

. By contradiction, assume that

Case 1.

There is a color class

and

. So, we can color

by

to obtain a proper
(\Delta(G)-1)
-coloring of

, a contradiction.

Case 2.

.

We may assume that

for each

, for otherwise we may proceed as in Case 1 to get a contradiction. But then we can color

by

to obtain a proper

-coloring of

. Hence,

, a final contradiction.
∎

Note that in Lemma

\Delta(G)=3
. Now, let
t
be the other neighbor of
u
in
H_{u}
in the proper
2
-coloring of
G^{\prime}
. Clearly,
c^{\prime}(x_{2k-1})=1
and
c^{\prime}(t)=2
. Let
c^{\prime\prime}
be a proper
(\Delta(G)-1)
-coloring (
2
-coloring) of
G^{\prime}
switching the color of vertices in
H_{u}-u
(keeping the color of other vertices the same as
c^{\prime}
). Claim
8
still holds for
c^{\prime\prime}
because
|C^{\prime}_{1}|=|C^{\prime\prime}_{1}|
. Now, let
P^{\prime}
be the connected component of
G[S\cup C^{\prime\prime}_{1}]
containing
u
and

. If
P^{\prime}
is a path, then simillar to the Case 1, we are done. Otherwise,
P^{\prime}
is a cycle. Obviously,
V(P)\setminus\{x_{2k-1}\}\subseteq V(P^{\prime})
and

, and since

x_{2k-2}t\in E(G)

3
Discussion on graphs with smaller chromatic number

In the next remark we demonstrate that Theorem
1.1
does not extend to the case when
\chi(G)\leq\frac{\Delta(G)}{2}
.

Remark 3.1
.

Let
G_{n,k}
,
n\geq 2
,
k\geq 3
, be the graph obtained from the cycle
C_{2n}
as follows. For each vertex
u
of the cycle take two disjoint complete graphs
K_{k}
, select a fixed vertex in each of them, and identify the two vertices with
u
. Then it is straightforward to verify that
\chi(G_{n,k})=k
,
\Delta(G_{n,k})=2k
,
{\rm vs}_{\chi}(G_{n,k})=2n
, and
{\rm ivs}_{\chi}(G_{n,k})=3n
.

In view of Remark
3.1
we pose the next problem for which we feel the answer is yes.

Problem 3.2
.

Is it true that if
G
is a graph with
\chi(G)\geq\frac{\Delta(G)}{2}+1
, then
{\rm vs}_{\chi}(G)={\rm ivs}_{\chi}(G)
?

Remark
3.1
demonstrates that in Problem
3.2
the assumption
\chi(G)\geq\frac{\Delta(G)}{2}+1
cannot be weakened to
\chi(G)\geq\frac{\Delta(G)}{2}
. We next demonstrate that furthermore the assumption cannot be weakened to
\chi(G)\geq\frac{\Delta(G)+1}{2}
.

Let
k\geq 3
be an odd positive number and let
H_{k}
be the graph obtained from two copies of
K_{\frac{k+1}{2}}
by identifying a vertex from each of the copies. Let
G_{k}
be the graph obtained from two disjoint copies of
H_{k}
by adding an edge between the maximum degree vertices in the two copies. Then we have
\Delta(G_{k})=k
,
\chi(G_{k})=\frac{k+1}{2}=\frac{\Delta(G_{k})+1}{2}
, and
2={\rm vs}_{\chi}(G_{k})<{\rm ivs}_{\chi}(G_{k})=3
.

Using a similar construction we next show that the ratio

\frac{{\rm ivs}_{\chi}(G_{r})}{{\rm vs}_{\chi}(G_{r})}

can be arbitrary large. Let
k
be a natural number and let
H^{\prime}_{k}
be the graph obtained from
k
copies of
K_{3}
by identifying a vertex from each of the copies. Let further
G^{\prime}_{k}
be the graph obtained from two disjoint copies of
H^{\prime}_{k}
by adding an edge between the two vertices of maximum degrees in the copies. Then
\chi(G_{k}^{\prime})=3
,
{\rm vs}_{\chi}(G_{k}^{\prime})=2
, and
{\rm ivs}_{\chi}(G_{k}^{\prime})=k+1
. This example shows that for any natural number
r
, there exists a graph
G_{r}
such that
\frac{{\rm ivs}_{\chi}(G_{r})}{{\rm vs}_{\chi}(G_{r})}>r
.

4
Relation with
{\rm es}_{\chi}
and a Nordhaus-Gaddum-type result

In this final section we first prove that if the chromatic number of a graph is large, then its chromatic vertex stability number is equal to its chromatic edge stability number. More precisely, we have the following result.

Theorem 4.1
.

If
G
is a graph with
\chi(G)>\frac{\Delta(G)}{2}+1
, then
{\rm vs}_{\chi}(G)={\rm es}_{\chi}(G)
.

Proof.

Clearly,
{\rm vs}_{\chi}(G)\leq{\rm es}_{\chi}(G)
, hence we need to show that
{\rm vs}_{\chi}(G)\geq{\rm es}_{\chi}(G)
. We proceed by induction on
{\rm vs}_{\chi}(G)
.

The base case is when
{\rm vs}_{\chi}(G)=1
. Let
u
be a vertex such that
\chi(G-u)=\chi(G)-1
. Let
c
be a proper
(\chi(G)-1)
-coloring of
G-u
. There is a color class
C_{i}
such that
|N_{G}(u)\cap C_{i}|\leq 1
, because otherwise
d_{G}(u)\geq 2(\chi(G)-1)>\Delta(G)
, a contradiction. If
N_{G}(u)\cap C_{i}=\emptyset
for some color class
C_{i}
, then we can color
u
by
i
to obtain a proper
(\chi(G)-1)
-coloring of
G
, a contradiction. Otherwise,
|N_{G}(u)\cap C_{i}|=1
and let
e
be the edge between
u
and
C_{i}
. So, we can color
u
by
i
to obtain a proper
(\chi(G)-1)
-coloring of
G-e
. Thus,
1={\rm vs}_{\chi}(G)\geq{\rm es}_{\chi}(G)
, and the base case is proved.

Now, suppose
{\rm vs}_{\chi}(G)=k>1
and let
S=\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{k}\}\subseteq V(G)
be a subset such that
\chi(G\setminus S)=\chi(G)-1
. Let
c
be a proper
(\chi(G)-1)
-coloring of
G\setminus S
. Similar to the base case of the induction, there is a color class
C_{i}
such that
|N_{G}(u_{1})\cap C_{i}|=1
. Now, suppose that
e
is the edge between
u_{1}
and
C_{i}
and define
G^{\prime}=G-e
. Clearly,
{\rm vs}_{\chi}(G^{\prime})=k-1
. By induction,
{\rm vs}_{\chi}(G^{\prime})\geq{\rm es}_{\chi}(G^{\prime})
and thus
{\rm es}_{\chi}(G)\leq{\rm es}_{\chi}(G^{\prime})+|\{e\}|\leq k={\rm vs}_{\chi}(G)
, which proves the induction step.
∎

The lower bound given in Theorem
4.1
is sharp. Let
k
be an even positive integer and
G
be the graph obtained from two copies of
K_{\frac{k}{2}+1}
which have only one vertex in common. Note that
\Delta(G)=k
,
\chi(G)=\frac{k}{2}+1=\frac{\Delta(G)}{2}+1
, and
{\rm vs}_{\chi}(G)=1
,
{\rm es}_{\chi}(G)=2
.

To conclude the paper, we give the following Nordhaus-Gaddum-type result.

Proposition 4.2
.

If
G
is a not edgeless and not complete graph, then
{\rm vs}_{\chi}(G)+{\rm vs}_{\chi}(\overline{G})\leq n(G)+1
.

Proof.

Considering a proper
\chi(G)
-coloring of
G
and its smallest color class, we see that
{\rm vs}_{\chi}(G)\leq\frac{n(G)}{\chi(G)}
. The clasical Nordhaus-Gaddum inequalities
[
11
]
assert that
\chi(G)+\chi(\overline{G})\leq n(G)+1
and
\chi(G)\chi(\overline{G})\geq n(G)
, hence we can estimate as follows:

{\rm vs}_{\chi}(G)+{\rm vs}_{\chi}(\overline{G})\leq\frac{n(G)}{\chi(G)}+\frac{n(G)}{\chi(\overline{G})}=n(G)\frac{\chi(G)+\chi(\overline{G})}{\chi(G)\chi(\overline{G})}\leq n(G)\frac{n(G)+1}{n(G)}=n(G)+1

and we are done.
∎

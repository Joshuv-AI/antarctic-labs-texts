Negative moments of the CREM partition function in the high temperature regime
Fu-Hsuan Ho

Abstract
The continuous random energy model (CREM) was introduced by Bovier and Kurkova in 2004 which can be viewed as a generalization of Derrida’s generalized random energy model. Among other things, their work indicates that there exists a critical point β c \beta_{c} such that the partition function exhibits a phase transition. The present work focuses on the high temperature regime where β < β c \beta<\beta_{c} . We show that for all β < β c \beta<\beta_{c} and for all s > 0 s>0 , the negative s s moment of the CREM partition function is comparable with the expectation of the CREM partition function to the power of − s -s , up to constants that are independent of N N . Keywords: branching random walk; continuous random energy model; Gaussian process; negative moments. MSC2020 subject classifications: 60K35; 60G15; 60J80.



1
Introduction

In this paper, we consider the continuous random energy model (CREM) introduced by Bovier and Kurkova in
[
3
]
based on previous work by Derrida and Spohn
[
5
]
. The model is defined as follows: Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
. Denote by
\mathbb{T}_{N}
the binary tree with depth
N
. Given
u\in\mathbb{T}_{N}
, we denote by
\absolutevalue{u}
the depth of
u
, and we write
w\leq u
if
w
is an ancestor of
u
. For all
u,v\in\mathbb{T}_{N}
, let
u\wedge v
be the most recent common ancestor of
u
and
v
. The CREM is a centered Gaussian process
(X_{u})_{u\in\mathbb{T}_{N}}
indexed by the binary tree
\mathbb{T}_{N}
of depth
N
with covariance function

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[X_{v}X_{w}\right]=N\cdot A\left(\frac{\absolutevalue{v\wedge w}}{N}\right),\quad\forall v,w\in\mathbb{T}_{N}.

To study the CREM, one of the key quantities is the partition function defined as

\displaystyle Z_{\beta,N}\coloneqq\sum_{\absolutevalue{u}=N}e^{\beta X_{u}}.

(1.1)

In this paper, we study the negative moments of the partition function which gives us information on the small values of the partition function. This type of study was conducted in the context of other related models such as the homogeneous branching random walks, the multiplicative cascades, or the Gaussian multiplicative chaos, and came with the name of
negative moments
,
left tail
behavior,
small deviations
of the partition function. We give a survey of these results in Section
1.2
.

1.1
Main result

For this paper, we require that the function
A
satisfies the following assumption.

Assumption 1.1
.

We suppose that the function
A
is a non-decreasing function defined on the interval
[0,1]
such that
A(0)=0
and
A(1)=1
. Let
\hat{A}
be the concave hull of
A
, and we denote by
\hat{A}^{\prime}
the right derivative of
\hat{A}
. Throughout this paper, we assume the following regularity conditions.

(i)

The function
A
is differentiable in a neighborhood of
0
, i.e., there is an
x_{0}\in(0,1]
such that
A
is differentiable on the interval
(0,x_{0})
. Furthermore, we assume that
A
has a finite right derivative at
0
.

(ii)

There exists
\alpha\in(0,1)
such that the derivative of
A
is locally Hölder continuous with exponent
\alpha
in a neighborhood of
0
, i.e., there exists
x_{1}\in(0,1]
such that

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in[0,x_{1}]\\
x\neq y\end{subarray}}\frac{\absolutevalue{A'(x)-A'(y)}}{\absolutevalue{x-y}^{\alpha}}<\infty.

(iii)

The right derivative of
\hat{A}
at
x=0
is finite, i.e.,
\hat{A}^{\prime}(0)<\infty
.

Remark 1.2
.

Point (i)—(iii) are technical conditions that are required in the moment estimates perform in Section
2.2
and Section
2.3
so that the estimates only depend on
\hat{A}^{\prime}(0)
.

The free energy of the CREM is defined as

\displaystyle F_{\beta}\coloneqq\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}\left[\log Z_{\beta,N}\right].

The free energy
F_{\beta}
admits an explicit expression. Namely, for all
\beta\geq 0
,

\displaystyle F_{\beta}=\int_{0}^{1}f\left(\beta\sqrt{\hat{A}^{\prime}(t)}\right)\differential{t},\quad\text{where}\quad f(x)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{x^{2}}{2}+\log 2,&x<\sqrt{2\log 2},\\
\\
\sqrt{2\log 2}x,&x\geq\sqrt{2\log 2}.\end{cases}

(1.2)

Formula (
1.2
) was proven by Bovier and Kurkova in Theorem 3.3 of
[
3
]
, based on a Gaussian comparison lemma (Lemma 3.2 of
[
3
]
) and previous work of Capocaccia, Cassandro and Picco
[
4
]
in the 1980s. While Bovier and Kurkova assumed the function
A
to be piecewise smooth (Line 4 after (1.2) in
[
3
]
) to simplify their article, the proof of Theorem 3.3 of
[
3
]
does not use this regularity assumption and the proof also holds for the class of functions that are non-decreasing on the interval
[0,1]
such that
A(0)=0
and
A(1)=1
.

In the same paper, Bovier and Kurkova also showed that the maximum of the CREM satisfies the following.

\displaystyle\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}\left[\max_{\absolutevalue{u}=N}X_{u}\right]=\sqrt{2\log 2}\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{\hat{A}^{\prime}(t)}\differential{t}.

(1.3)

Combining (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) indicates that there exists

\displaystyle\beta_{c}\coloneqq\frac{\sqrt{2\log 2}}{\sqrt{\hat{A}^{\prime}(0)}}

(1.4)

such that the following phase transition occurs. a) For all
\beta<\beta_{c}
, the main contribution to the partition function comes from an exponential amount of the particles. b) For all
\beta>\beta_{c}
, the maximum starts to contribute significantly to the partition function. The quantity
\beta_{c}
is sometimes referred to as the static critical inverse temperature of the CREM. In the following, we refer to the subcritical regime
\beta<\beta_{c}
as the high temperature regime.

Our goal is to study the negative moments of the partition function
Z_{\beta,N}
in the high temperature regime, and we obtain the following result.

Theorem 1.3
.

Suppose Assumption
1.1
is true. Let
\beta<\beta_{c}
. For all
s>0
, there exist
N_{0}=N_{0}(A,\beta,s)\in\mathbb{N}
and a constant
C=C(A,\beta,s)
, independent of
N
, such that for all
N\geq N_{0}
,

\mathbb{E}\left[(Z_{\beta,N})^{-s}\right]\leq C\mathbb{E}\left[Z_{\beta,N}\right]^{-s}.

Remark 1.4
.

For all
\beta>0
,
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
s>0
, we have the trivial lower bound provided by the Jensen inequality and the convexity of
x\mapsto x^{-s}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[(Z_{\beta,N})^{-s}\right]\geq\mathbb{E}\left[Z_{\beta,N}\right]^{-s}.

(1.5)

Thus, combining (
1.5
) with Theorem
1.3
, we see that for all
\beta<\beta_{c}
in the high temperature regime and for all
s>0
,
\mathbb{E}\left[(Z_{\beta,N})^{-s}\right]
is comparable with
\mathbb{E}\left[Z_{\beta,N}\right]^{-s}
.

1.2
Related models

For the (homogeneous) branching random walk, the typical approach is not to study the partition function directly but to consider the additive martingale

\displaystyle W_{\beta,N}=Z_{\beta,N}/\mathbb{E}\left[Z_{\beta,N}\right],

which converges to a pointwise limit
W_{\beta,\infty}
. A standard method to establish that
W_{\beta,\infty}
has negative moments involves the following observation. Suppose that
Y^{\prime}
,
Y^{\prime\prime}
,
W^{\prime}
and
W^{\prime\prime}
are independent random variables such that
Y^{\prime}
and
Y^{\prime\prime}
share the law of the increment of the branching random walk, and each of
W^{\prime}
and
W^{\prime\prime}
follows the same distribution as
W_{\beta,\infty}
. Then, the limit of the additive martingale
W_{\beta,\infty}
satisfies the following fixed point equation in distribution:

\displaystyle W_{\beta,\infty}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(d)}}{{=}}Y^{\prime}W^{\prime}+Y^{\prime\prime}W^{\prime\prime}.

(1.6)

Formula (
1.6
) is a special case of the so-called smoothing transform, which has been studied extensively in the literature. In the context of multiplicative cascades, which is an equivalent model of the branching random walk, Molchan showed in
[
12
]
that if
\mathbb{E}\left[(Y^{\prime})^{-s}\right]<\infty
for some
s>0
, then
\mathbb{E}\left[(W_{\beta,\infty})^{-s}\right]<\infty
. Subsequently, Molchan’s result was extended by Liu in
[
10
,
11
]
. More recently, Hu studied in
[
9
]
the small deviation of the maximum of the branching random walk based on Liu’s result. On the other hand, Nikula provided small deviations for lognormal multiplicative cascades in
[
13
]
, thereby refining Molchan’s result.

In the context of the Gaussian multiplicative chaos, Garban, Holden, Sepúlveda and Sun showed in
[
8
]
that given a subcritical GMC measure with mild conditions on the base measure, the total mass of this GMC measure has negative moments of all orders. Their works expanded on the findings of Robert and Vargas
[
15
]
, Duplantier and Sheffield
[
6
]
and Remy
[
14
]
, with the base measure taken as the Lebesgue measure restricted to some open set, where these authors were interested in quantifying the left tail behavior of the total mass of the GMC near
0
. A final remark is that Robert and Vargas referred the proof of Theorem 3.6 in their paper
[
15
]
, which concerned the negative moment of the total mass of the GMC, to Barral and Mandelbrot
[
1
]
, where they studied a log-Poisson cascade.

1.3
Proof strategy

For the CREM in general,
W_{\beta,N}
is not a martingale, so there is no obvious way to show that
W_{\beta,\infty}
exists. To prove Theorem
1.3
, we adapt the proof of Lemma A.3 in the paper
[
2
]
by Benjamini and Schramm where they study the multiplicative cascade.

While their argument also involved the smoothing transform, it can be adjusted for general CREM. In particular, we show that for all
s>0
, there exist two positive sequences
\varepsilon_{k}
and
\eta_{k}
that both decay double exponentially to
0
as
k\rightarrow\infty
such that for
N
sufficiently large,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(Z_{\beta,N}\leq\varepsilon_{k}\mathbb{E}\left[Z_{\beta,N}\right]\right)\leq\eta_{k},

(1.7)

and that for all
k\in\llbracket 1,C^{\prime}\log N\rrbracket
, where
C^{\prime}>0
, there exist
C=C(A,\beta,s)>0
and
c=c(A,\beta,s)>0
such that

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{k}^{-s}\eta_{k}\leq Ce^{-ce^{ck}}.

(1.8)

The proof of (
1.7
) and (
1.8
) is by establishing an initial left tail estimate, and then using the branching property to bootstrap the estimate.

Outline.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. We provide in Section
2
an initial estimate of the left tail of the partition function. Next, in Section
3
, we improve the estimate obtained in the previous section using a bootstrap argument. Finally, Theorem
1.3
is proven in Section
4
.

2
Initial estimate for the left tail of the partition function

The main goal of this section is to estimate the left tail of
Z_{\beta,N}
. The main idea is to adapt (
1.6
) to the inhomogeneous setting via a recursive argument of
Z_{\beta,N}
which we will explain immediately with the following notation.

We denote by
\llbracket m_{1},m_{2}\rrbracket=\{m_{1},m_{1}+1,\ldots,m_{2}-1,m_{2}\}
for two integers
m-1<m_{2}
. Fix
k\in\llbracket 0,N\rrbracket
. Define
(X^{(k)}_{u})_{\absolutevalue{u}=N-k}
to be the centered Gaussian process with covariance function

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[X^{(k)}_{u}X^{(k)}_{w}\right]=N\left(A\left(\frac{\absolutevalue{v\wedge w}+k}{N}\right)-A\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)\right).

We also introduce the corresponding partition function.

\displaystyle Z^{(k)}_{\beta,N-k}=\sum_{\absolutevalue{u}=N-k}e^{\beta X^{(k)}_{u}}.

By the covariance function of
(X^{(k)}_{u})_{\absolutevalue{u}=N-k}
and the definition of
Z^{(k)}_{\beta,N-k}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[Z^{(k)}_{\beta,N-k}\right]=2^{N-k}\exp(\frac{\beta^2 N}{2}\left(1-A\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)\right)).

(2.1)

With the notation above,
Z_{\beta,N}
admits the following one-step decomposition

\displaystyle Z_{\beta,N}=\sum_{\absolutevalue{u}=1}e^{\beta X_{u}}Z^{u}_{\beta,N-1}

where
(Z^{u}_{\beta,N})_{\absolutevalue{u}}
are independent and has the same distribution as
Z^{(1)}_{\beta,N-1}
. Then, the task of controlling the left tail of
Z_{\beta,N}
can be reduced to controlling
Z^{(1)}_{\beta,N-1}
and
e^{X_{u}}
, for
\absolutevalue{u}=1
. Then, we decompose
Z^{(1)}_{\beta,N-1}
in the same manner. We call this procedure the bootstrap argument, which will be made precise in Section
3
. The point is that for the bootstrap argument to work, it requires an initial bound, independent of
N
large and
k
small, for the left tail of the partition function
Z^{(k)}_{\beta,N-k}
in the high-temperature regime. This bound is provided in the following.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\beta<\beta_{c}
. Let
K=\lfloor 2\log_{\gamma}N\rfloor
, where
\gamma\in(11/10,2)
. Then, there exist
N_{0}=N_{0}(A,\beta)\in\mathbb{N}
and a constant
\eta_{0}=\eta_{0}(A,\beta)<1
such that for all
N\geq N_{0}
and
k\in\llbracket 0,K\rrbracket
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(Z^{(k)}_{\beta,N-k}\leq\frac{1}{2}\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[Z^{(k)}_{\beta,N-k}\right]\right)\leq\eta_{0},

where the expectation of
Z^{(k)}_{\beta,N-k}
is given in (
2.1
).

To prove Proposition
2.1
, one may want to apply the Paley–Zygmund type argument directly to
Z^{(k)}_{\beta,N-k}
. This, however, will not provide the desired result for the whole high temperature regime due to the correlation of the model. Happily, this can be resolved by introducing a good truncated partition function which is defined as follows.
For all
\absolutevalue{u}=N
,
k\in\llbracket 0,N\rrbracket
,
a>0
and
b>0
, define the truncating set

G_{u,k}\coloneqq\Bigg\{\forall n\in\llbracket 1,N-k\rrbracket\,\Bigg|\begin{array}[]{l}X_{w}^{(k)}\leq\beta\cdot N\cdot\left(A\left(\frac{n+k}{N}\right)-A\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)\right)+a\cdot n+b\\
\ \text{where $w$ is an ancestor of $u$ with $\absolutevalue{w}=n$}\end{array}\Bigg\}.

Define the truncated partition function

\displaystyle Z^{(k),\leq}_{\beta,N-k}\coloneqq\sum_{\absolutevalue{u}=N-k}e^{\beta X^{(k)}_{u}}\boldsymbol{1}_{G_{u,k}}.

(2.2)

In Section
2.3
, we will prove that the truncated partition has matching squared first moment and second moment. Once we have these two estimates, by passing to a Paley–Zygmund type argument, we can estimate the left tail of the untruncated partition function.

2.1
Many-to-one and Many-to-two

To perform the first and second moment estimates, we state and prove the following many-to-one/two lemmas.

Lemma 2.2

(Many-to-one)
.

Define
(X^{(k)}_{n})_{n\in\llbracket 0,N-k\rrbracket}
to be a non-homogeneous random walk such that for all
n\in\llbracket 0,N-k-1\rrbracket
, the increment
X^{(k)}_{n+1}-X^{(k)}_{n}
is distributed as a centered Gaussian random variable with variance
N(A((n+1)/N)-A(n/N))
.
Let

\displaystyle G_{k}=\left\{\forall n\in\llbracket 1,N-k\rrbracket:X_{n}^{(k)}\leq\beta\cdot N\cdot\left(A\left(\frac{n+k}{N}\right)-A\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)\right)+a\cdot n+b\right\}

Then, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[Z^{(k),\leq}_{\beta,N-k}\right]=2^{N-k}\mathbb{E}\left[e^{\beta X_{N-k}^{(k)}}\boldsymbol{1}_{G_{k}}\right].

Proof.

By the linearity of expectation, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[Z^{(k),\leq}_{\beta,N-k}\right]=\sum_{\absolutevalue{u}=N-k}\mathbb{E}\left[e^{\beta X_{u}^{(k)}}\boldsymbol{1}_{G_{u,k}}\right].

On the other hand, for each
u
with
\absolutevalue{u}=N-k
, the path
(X_{w}^{(k)})_{w\leq u}
has the same distribution as
(X^{(k)}_{n})_{n\in\llbracket 0,N-k\rrbracket}
, and the proof follows.
∎

Lemma 2.3

(Many-to-two)
.

Define
(X^{(k)}_{\ell,n})_{n\in\llbracket 0,N-k\rrbracket}
and
(\tilde{X}^{(k)}_{\ell,n})_{n\in\llbracket 0,N-k\rrbracket}
to be non-homogeneous random walks such that

•

for all
n\in\llbracket 0,N-k-1\rrbracket
, the increments
X^{(k)}_{\ell,n+1}-X^{(k)}_{\ell,n}
and
\tilde{X}^{(k)}_{\ell,n+1}-\tilde{X}^{(k)}_{\ell,n}
are both distributed as a centered Gaussian random variable with variance
N(A((n+1)/N)-A(n/N))
,

•

X^{(k)}_{\ell,n}=\tilde{X}^{(k)}_{\ell,n}
for all
n\in\llbracket 0,\ell\rrbracket
and

•

(X^{(k)}_{\ell,\ell+m}-X^{(k)}_{\ell,\ell})_{m\in\llbracket 0,N-k-\ell\rrbracket}
and
(\tilde{X}^{(k)}_{\ell,\ell+m}-\tilde{X}^{(k)}_{\ell,\ell})_{m\in\llbracket 0,N-k-\ell\rrbracket}
are independent.

Let

\displaystyle G_{\ell,k}

\displaystyle=\left\{\forall n\in\llbracket 1,N-k\rrbracket:X_{\ell,n}^{(k)}\leq\beta\cdot N\cdot\left(A\left(\frac{n+k}{N}\right)-A\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)\right)+a\cdot n+b\right\},

\displaystyle\tilde{G}_{\ell,k}

\displaystyle=\left\{\forall n\in\llbracket 1,N-k\rrbracket:\tilde{X}_{\ell,n}^{(k)}\leq\beta\cdot N\cdot\left(A\left(\frac{n+k}{N}\right)-A\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)\right)+a\cdot n+b\right\}.

Then, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[(Z^{(k),\leq}_{\beta,N-k})^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=2^{N-k}\mathbb{E}\left[e^{\beta X_{N-k}^{(k)}}\boldsymbol{1}_{G_{k}}\right]+\sum_{\ell=0}^{N-k-1}2^{2(N-k)-\ell-1}\mathbb{E}\left[e^{\beta(X^{(k)}_{\ell,N-k}+\tilde{X}_{\ell,N-k}^{(k)})}\boldsymbol{1}_{G_{\ell,k}\cap\tilde{G}_{\ell,k}}\right].

Proof.

By the linearity of expectation, we have the decomposition

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[(Z^{(k),\leq}_{\beta,N-k})^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\absolutevalue{u}=\absolutevalue{w}=N-k}\mathbb{E}\left[e^{\beta(X^{(k)}_{u}+X_{w}^{(k)})}\boldsymbol{1}_{G_{u}\cap\tilde{G}_{w}}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{N-k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\absolutevalue{u}=\absolutevalue{w}=N-k,\\
\absolutevalue{u\wedge w}=\ell\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}\left[e^{\beta(X^{(k)}_{u}+X_{w}^{(k)})}\boldsymbol{1}_{G_{u}\cap G_{w}}\right].

(2.3)

For all
\ell\in\llbracket 0,N-k-1\rrbracket
, we have

\displaystyle(X^{(k)}_{u_{1}},X^{(k)}_{u_{2}})_{u_{1}\leq u,w\leq u_{2}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(d)}}{{=}}(X^{(k)}_{\ell,n_{1}},\tilde{X}^{(k)}_{\ell,n_{2}})_{n_{1},n_{2}\in\llbracket 0,N-k\rrbracket},

and for
\ell=N-k
,

\displaystyle(X^{(k)}_{u_{1}})_{u_{1}\leq w}=(X^{(k)}_{u_{2}})_{u_{2}\leq w}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(d)}}{{=}}(X^{(k)}_{n})_{n\in\llbracket 0,N-k\rrbracket},

where
(X^{(k)}_{n})_{n\in\llbracket 0,N-k\rrbracket}
is introduced in Lemma
2.2
. On the other hand,

the number of pairs
(u,w)
with
\absolutevalue{u}=\absolutevalue{w}=N-k
and
\absolutevalue{u\wedge w}=\ell

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}2^{2(N-k)-\ell-1},&\ell\in\llbracket 0,N-k-1\rrbracket,\\
2^{N-k},&\ell=N-k.\end{cases}

Therefore, (
2.3
) yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[(Z^{(k),\leq}_{\beta,N-k})^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=2^{N-k}\mathbb{E}\left[e^{\beta X_{N-k}^{(k)}}\boldsymbol{1}_{G_{k}}\right]+\sum_{\ell=0}^{N-k-1}2^{2(N-k)-\ell-1}\mathbb{E}\left[e^{\beta(X^{(k)}_{\ell,N-k}+\tilde{X}_{\ell,N-k}^{(k)})}\boldsymbol{1}_{G_{\ell,k}\cap\tilde{G}_{\ell,k}}\right],

and the proof is completed.
∎

2.2
Two useful lemmas for the moment estimates

The following lemma estimates the difference between
A(y)
and
A(z)
when
y
and
z
are close to
0
.

Lemma 2.4
.

For all
y,z\in[0,x_{1}]
, where
x_{1}
is given in (ii) of Assumption
1.1
, there exists a constant
C=C(A,x_{1})>0
such that,

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{A\left(y\right) - A\left( z\right)}\leq\hat{A}^{\prime}(0)\absolutevalue{y-z}+Cy^{\alpha}\absolutevalue{y-z}+C\absolutevalue{y-z}^{1+\alpha},

(2.4)

where
\alpha
appeared in (ii) of Assumption
1.1
.

Proof.

Fix
y,z\in[0,x_{1}]
, where
x_{1}
is given in (ii) of Assumption
1.1
. Then by applying the
1
st order Taylor expansion of
A
at
y
with Lagrange remainder, we have

\displaystyle A(z)=A(y)+A^{\prime}(y)(z-y)+(z-y)(A^{\prime}(\xi)-A^{\prime}(y)),

where
\xi
is between
z
and
y
. Then, the triangle inequality and the fact that
A
is non-decreasing yield

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{A(z)-A(y)}\leq A^{\prime}(y)\absolutevalue{z-y}+\absolutevalue{z-y}\absolutevalue{A'(\xi)-A'(y)}.

(2.5)

By the local Hölder continuity of
A
in a neighborhood around
0
provided by Assumption
1.1
, there exists
C=C(A,x_{1})>0
such that

\displaystyle A^{\prime}(y)\leq A^{\prime}(0)+Cy^{\alpha}.

(2.6)

Similarly, by the local Hölder continuity of
A
in a neighborhood around
0
provided by Assumption
1.1
, we have

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{A'(\xi)-A'(y)}\leq C\absolutevalue{z-y}^{\alpha},

(2.7)

where
C
is the same constant as in (
2.6
). Combining (
2.5
), (
2.6
) and (
2.7
), we conclude that

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{A(z)-A(y)}\leq\hat{A}^{\prime}(0)\absolutevalue{z-y}+Cy^{\alpha}\absolutevalue{z-y}+C\absolutevalue{z-y}^{1+\alpha},

and this completes the proof.
∎

We state and prove the following exponential tilting formula that we apply in the paper.

Lemma 2.5

(Exponential tilting)
.

Fix
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
(Y_{n})_{n\in\llbracket 1,m\rrbracket}
be a collection of independent centered Gaussian with variance
\sigma_{n}^{2}>0
. For all
n\in\llbracket 1,m\rrbracket
, define
S_{n}=Y_{1}+\cdots+Y_{n}
. Let
g:\llbracket 1,m\rrbracket\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a function and

\displaystyle G_{n}=\{\forall\ell\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket:S_{\ell}\leq g(\ell)\},\quad n\in\llbracket 1,m\rrbracket.

Then for all
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[e^{\beta S_{n}}\boldsymbol{1}_{G_{n}}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[e^{\beta S_{n}}\right]\mathbb{P}\left(\forall\ell\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket:S_{\ell}\leq g(\ell)-\beta\sum_{r=1}^{\ell}\sigma_{r}^{2}\right).

Proof.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Firstly, from the independence of
\{Y_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[e^{\beta S_{n}}\right]=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[e^{\beta Y_{i}}\right]=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\exp(\frac{\beta^2}{2}\sigma_i^2)=\exp(\frac{\beta}{2}\sum_{i=1}^n \sigma_i^2).

(2.8)

Next, for all
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
, we define

\displaystyle G_{n}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})

\displaystyle=\{\forall\ell\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket:x_{1}+\cdots+x_{\ell}\leq g(\ell)\},

\displaystyle\tilde{G}_{n}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})

\displaystyle=\Big\{\forall\ell\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket:x_{1}+\cdots+x_{\ell}\leq g(\ell)-\beta\sum_{r=1}^{\ell}\sigma_{r}^{2}\Big\}.

Then, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[e^{\beta S_{n}}\boldsymbol{1}_{G_{n}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}\sqrt{2\pi\sigma_{i}^{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{\beta\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}}\boldsymbol{1}_{G_{n}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})}\exp(-\sum_{i=1}^n \frac{x^2_i}{2\sigma_i^2})\differential{x_1}\cdots\differential{x_n}

\displaystyle=\exp(\frac{\beta^2}{2}\sum_{i=1}^n \sigma_i^2)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\frac{1}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}\sqrt{2\pi\sigma_{i}^{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\boldsymbol{1}_{G_{n}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})}\exp(-\sum_{i=1}^n \frac{(x_i-\beta\sigma_i^2)^2}{2\sigma_i^2})\differential{x_1}\cdots\differential{x_n}

\displaystyle=\exp(\frac{\beta^2}{2}\sum_{i=1}^n \sigma_i^2)\frac{1}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}\sqrt{2\pi\sigma_{i}^{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\boldsymbol{1}_{\tilde{G}_{n}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})}\exp(-\sum_{i=1}^n \frac{y_i^2}{2\sigma_i^2})\differential{y_1}\cdots\differential{y_n}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[e^{\beta S_{n}}\right]\mathbb{P}\left(\forall\ell\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket:S_{\ell}\leq g(\ell)-\beta\sum_{r=1}^{\ell}\sigma_{r}^{2}\right),

where the second to last line follows from the change of variables
y_{i}=x_{i}-\beta\sigma_{i}^{2}
,
i=1,\ldots,n
, and the last line follows from (
2.8
).
∎

2.3
Moment estimates of the truncated partition function

We now proceed with the first moment estimate.

Lemma 2.6

(First moment estimate)
.

For all

\displaystyle a>0\quad\text{and}\quad b\geq\frac{\hat{A}^{\prime}(0)}{a}\log(10\max\left\{\frac{e^{-a^2/(2\hat{A}'(0))}}{1-e^{-a^2/(2\hat{A}'(0))}},1\right\}),

there exist
N_{0}=N_{0}(A,\beta,a,b)\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
N\geq N_{0}
and
k\in\llbracket 0,K\rrbracket
where
K=\lfloor 2\log_{\gamma}N\rfloor
with
\gamma\in(11/10,2)
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[Z_{\beta,N-k}^{(k),\leq}\right]\geq\frac{7}{10}\mathbb{E}\left[Z_{\beta,N-k}^{(k)}\right].

Proof.

By Lemma
2.2
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[Z^{(k),\leq}_{\beta,N-k}\right]

\displaystyle=2^{N-k}\mathbb{E}\left[e^{\beta X^{(k)}_{N-k}}\boldsymbol{1}_{G_{N-k}}\right].

(2.9)

By Lemma
2.5
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[e^{\beta X^{(k)}_{N-k}}\boldsymbol{1}_{G_{N-k}}\right]

\displaystyle=\exp(\frac{\beta^2 N}{2}\left(1-A\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)\right))\cdot\mathbb{P}\left(\forall n\in\llbracket 1,N-k\rrbracket:X^{(k)}_{N-k}(n)\leq a\cdot n+b\right).

(2.10)

We want to give a lower bound of the probability in (
2.10
). The union bound and the Chernoff bound yield

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\exists n\in\llbracket 1,N-k\rrbracket:X^{(k)}_{N-k}(n)>a\cdot n+b\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=1}^{N-k}\mathbb{P}\left(X^{(k)}_{N-k}(n)>a\cdot n+b\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=1}^{N-k}\exp\left(-\frac{(a\cdot n+b)^{2}}{2N\cdot(A(\frac{n+k}{N})-A(\frac{k}{N}))}\right).

(2.11)

We separate (
2.11
) into two terms

\displaystyle\eqref{chap3.eq:prob.0.0}=\sum_{n=1}^{K}\exp\left(-\frac{(a\cdot n+b)^{2}}{2N\cdot(A(\frac{n+k}{N})-A(\frac{k}{N}))}\right)+\sum_{n=K+1}^{N-k}\exp\left(-\frac{(a\cdot n+b)^{2}}{2N\cdot(A(\frac{n+k}{N})-A(\frac{k}{N}))}\right).

(2.12)

We start with bounding the first term of (
2.12
). Take
N_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
2K/N\leq x_{1}
for all
N\geq N_{1}
. Then by Lemma
2.4
,
for all
n\in\llbracket 1,K\rrbracket
, we have

\displaystyle N\cdot\left(A\left(\frac{n+k}{N}\right)-A\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\hat{A}^{\prime}(0)n+C\frac{n(n+k)^{\alpha}}{N}+C\frac{n^{1+\alpha}}{N^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq\hat{A}^{\prime}(0)n+C\frac{K(2K)^{\alpha}}{N}+C\frac{K^{1+\alpha}}{N^{\alpha}}

(2.13)

\displaystyle=\hat{A}^{\prime}(0)n+h(N),

(2.14)

\displaystyle\leq(\hat{A}^{\prime}(0)+h(N))n.

(2.15)

where
h(N)
is defined as

\displaystyle h(N)=C(1+2^{\alpha})\frac{K^{1+\alpha}}{N},

(2.16)

(
2.13
) follows by the assumption that
n\leq K
and
k\leq K
and (
2.15
) follows from the fact that
n\geq 1
and
h(N)\geq 0
.

By (
2.15
) and the fact that
(an+b)^{2}\geq a^{2}n^{2}+2abn
for all
a,b,n>0
, the first term of (
2.12
) is bounded from above by

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{K}\exp\left(-\frac{(a\cdot n+b)^{2}}{2N\cdot(A(\frac{n+k}{N})-A(\frac{k}{N}))}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=1}^{K}\exp\left(-\frac{a^{2}n^{2}+2abn}{2(\hat{A}^{\prime}(0)+h(N))n}\right)

\displaystyle=\exp(-\frac{ab}{\hat{A}'(0)+h(N)})\sum_{n=1}^{K}\exp(-\frac{a^2n}{2(\hat{A}'(0)+h(N))}).

(2.17)

The summation in (
2.17
) is bounded from above by

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{K}\exp(-\frac{a^2n}{2(\hat{A}'(0)+h(N))})\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\exp(-\frac{a^2n}{2(\hat{A}'(0)+h(N))})\leq\frac{\exp(-\frac{a^2}{2(\hat{A}'(0)+h(N))})}{1-\exp(-\frac{a^2}{2(\hat{A}'(0)+h(N))})}.

(2.18)

Thus, (
2.17
) and (
2.18
) yield

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{K}\exp\left(-\frac{(a\cdot n+b)^{2}}{2N\cdot(A(\frac{n+k}{N})-A(\frac{k}{N}))}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp(-\frac{ab}{\hat{A}'(0)+h(N)})\frac{\exp(-\frac{a^2}{2(\hat{A}'(0)+h(N))})}{1-\exp(-\frac{a^2}{2(\hat{A}'(0)+h(N))})}.

(2.19)

Since
h(N)\rightarrow 0
as
N\rightarrow\infty
, by the choice of parameters
a
and
b
, we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\exp(-\frac{a^2}{2(\hat{A}'(0)+h(N))})}{1-\exp(-\frac{a^2}{2(\hat{A}'(0)+h(N))})}\exp(-\frac{ab}{\hat{A}'(0)+h(N)})

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-a^{2}/(2\hat{A}^{\prime}(0))}}{1-e^{-a^{2}/(2\hat{A}^{\prime}(0))}}e^{-ab/\hat{A}^{\prime}(0)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{10}.

(2.20)

Combining (
2.19
) and (
2.20
), there exists
N_{2}=N_{2}(A,\beta)\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
N\in N_{2}
, the first term of (
2.12
) is bounded from above by

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{K}\exp\left(-\frac{(a\cdot n+b)^{2}}{2N\cdot(A(\frac{n+k}{N})-A(\frac{k}{N}))}\right)\leq\frac{2}{10}.

(2.21)

It remains to bound the second term of (
2.12
). By Assumption
1.1
, we know that
A(x)\geq 0
for all
x\in[0,1]
and
A(x)\leq\hat{A}(0)x
for all
x\in[0,1]
. Thus,

\displaystyle N\cdot\left(A\left(\frac{n+k}{N}\right)-A\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)\right)\leq NA\left(\frac{n+k}{N}\right)\leq\hat{A}^{\prime}(0)(n+k).

(2.22)

For
n\geq K+1
and
k\leq K
, we have

\displaystyle 1+\frac{k}{n}\leq 1+\frac{K}{K+1}\leq 2.

(2.23)

Combining (
2.22
) and (
2.23
) then yield for
n\geq K+1
and
k\leq K
,

\displaystyle N\cdot\left(A\left(\frac{n+k}{N}\right)-A\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)\right)\leq 2\hat{A}^{\prime}(0)n.

(2.24)

Thus, by (
2.24
) and the fact that
(an+b)^{2}\geq a^{2}n^{2}
for all
a,b,n>0
,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=K+1}^{N-k}\exp\left(-\frac{(an+b)^{2}}{2N\cdot(A(\frac{n+k}{N})-A(\frac{k}{N}))}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=K+1}^{N-k}\exp\left(-\frac{a^{2}n^{2}}{4\hat{A}(0)n}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=K+1}^{N-k}\exp\left(-\frac{a^{2}}{4\hat{A}^{\prime}(0)}n\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=K+1}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{a^{2}}{4\hat{A}^{\prime}(0)}n\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp(-\frac{a^2}{4\hat{A}'(0)}(K+1))\frac{1}{1-\exp(-\frac{a^2}{4\hat{A}'(0)})}.

(2.25)

By our choice of
K
, the limit superior of (
2.25
) equals
0
as
N\rightarrow\infty
. In particular, there exists
N_{3}=N_{3}(A,\beta)
such that for all
N\geq N_{2}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=K+1}^{N-k}\exp\left(-\frac{(a\cdot n+b)^{2}}{2N\cdot(A(\frac{n+k}{N})-A(\frac{k}{N}))}\right)\leq\frac{1}{10}.

(2.26)

Let
N_{0}\coloneqq\max\{N_{1},N_{2},N_{3}\}
. Combining (
2.12
), (
2.21
) and (
2.26
), for all
N\geq N_{0}
and for all
k\in\llbracket 0,K\rrbracket
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\exists n\in\llbracket 1,N-k\rrbracket:X^{(k)}_{N-k}(n)>a\cdot n+b\right)\leq\frac{3}{10}.

(2.27)

Combining (
2.1
), (
2.10
), (
2.10
) and (
2.27
), we conclude that for all
N\geq N_{0}
and for all
k\in\llbracket 0,K\rrbracket
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[Z^{(k),\leq}_{\beta,N-k}\right]\geq\frac{7}{10}2^{N-k}\exp(\frac{\beta^2 N}{2}\left(1-A\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)\right))=\mathbb{E}\left[Z^{(k)}_{\beta,N-k}\right],

where the equality above follows from (
2.1
).
∎

It remains to provide a second moment estimate of the truncated partition function.

Lemma 2.7

(Second moment estimate)
.

Let
\beta<\beta_{c}
.
For all

\displaystyle a\in\left(0,\frac{\log 2}{\beta}-\frac{1}{2}\beta\hat{A}^{\prime}(0)\right)\quad\text{and}\quad b>0,

there exist
N_{0}=N_{0}(A,\beta)\in\mathbb{N}
and
C=C(A,\beta)>0
such that for all
N\geq N_{0}
and
k\in\llbracket 0,K\rrbracket
, where

K=\lfloor 2\log_{\gamma}N\rfloor
with
\gamma\in(11/10,2)
,
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[(Z^{(k),\leq}_{\beta,N-k})^{2}\right]\leq C\mathbb{E}\left[Z^{(k)}_{\beta,N-k}\right]^{2}.

Proof.

Fix
\beta<\beta_{c}
and
a\in(0,\frac{\log 2}{\beta}-\frac{1}{2}\beta\hat{A}^{\prime}(0))
. Define

\displaystyle c=c(A,\beta)\coloneqq\log 2-\frac{1}{2}\beta^{2}\hat{A}^{\prime}(0)-a>0,

(2.28)

where the positivity follows from the definition of
\beta_{c}
in (
1.4
).

By Lemma
2.3
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[(Z^{(k),\leq}_{\beta,N-k})^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=2^{N-k}\mathbb{E}\left[e^{2\beta X_{N-k}^{(k)}}\boldsymbol{1}_{G_{k}}\right]+\sum_{\ell=0}^{N-k-1}2^{2(N-k)-\ell-1}\mathbb{E}\left[e^{\beta(X^{(k)}_{\ell,N-k}+\tilde{X}_{\ell,N-k}^{(k)})}\boldsymbol{1}_{G_{\ell,k}\cap\tilde{G}_{\ell,k}}\right].

(2.29)

We now want to estimate the expectations in (
2.29
). For all
\ell\in\llbracket 1,N\rrbracket
and
n\in\llbracket 1,N-\ell\rrbracket
, define

\displaystyle H_{n}^{(\ell)}=\left\{X_{n}^{(\ell)}\leq\beta N\left(A\left(\frac{\ell+n}{N}\right)-A\left(\frac{\ell}{N}\right)\right)+an+b\right\}.

Then, we see that the expectation in the first term of (
2.29
) is bounded from above by

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[e^{2\beta X_{N-k}^{(k)}}\boldsymbol{1}_{G_{k}}\right]\leq\mathbb{E}\left[e^{2\beta X^{(k)}_{N-k}}\boldsymbol{1}_{H_{N-k}^{(k)}}\right].

(2.30)

Fix
\ell\in\llbracket 0,N-k-1\rrbracket
. We have

\displaystyle G_{\ell,k}\cap\tilde{G}_{\ell,k}\subseteq\left\{X_{\ell,\ell}^{(k)}\leq\beta N\left(A\left(\frac{\ell+k}{N}\right)-A\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)\right)+a\ell+b\right\}=H_{\ell,\ell}^{(k)}.

(2.31)

Then, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[e^{\beta(X^{(k)}_{\ell,N-k}+\tilde{X}_{\ell,N-k}^{(k)})}\boldsymbol{1}_{G_{\ell,k}\cap\tilde{G}_{\ell,k}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[e^{\beta(X^{(k)}_{\ell,N-k}+\tilde{X}_{\ell,N-k}^{(k)})}\boldsymbol{1}_{H_{\ell,\ell}^{(k)}}\right]

(2.32)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[e^{2\beta X_{\ell,\ell}^{(k)}}\boldsymbol{1}_{H_{\ell,\ell}^{(k)}}\right]\mathbb{E}\left[e^{\beta(X^{(k)}_{\ell,N-k}+\tilde{X}^{(k)}_{\ell,N-k}-2X^{(k)}_{\ell,\ell})}\right]

(2.33)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[e^{2\beta X_{\ell}^{(k)}}\boldsymbol{1}_{H_{\ell}^{(k)}}\right]\mathbb{E}\left[e^{\beta X^{(\ell+k)}_{N-\ell-k}}\right]^{2}

(2.34)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[e^{2\beta X_{\ell}^{(k)}}\boldsymbol{1}_{H_{\ell}^{(k)}}\right]\exp(\beta^2 N \left(1-A\left(\frac{\ell+k}{N}\right)\right)),

(2.35)

where (
2.32
) follows from (
2.31
), and both (
2.33
) and (
2.34
) from Lemma
2.3
.

For
\ell\in\llbracket 0,N-k\rrbracket
, Lemma
2.5
yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[e^{2\beta X_{\ell}^{(k)}}\boldsymbol{1}_{H_{\ell}^{(k)}}\right]

\displaystyle=\exp(2\beta^2 N \left(A\left(\frac{\ell+k}{N}\right)-A\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)\right))

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\mathbb{P}\left(X_{\ell}^{(k)}\leq-\beta N\left(A\left(\frac{k+\ell}{N}\right)-A\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)\right)+a\ell+b\right).

(2.36)

We distinguish the following two cases.

Case 1:
-\beta N\left(A\left(\frac{\ell+k}{N}\right)-A\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)\right)+a\ell+b\geq 0
.
In this case, by bounding the probability in (
2.36
) by
1
, we obtain

(
2.36
)

\displaystyle\leq\exp(2\beta^2 N \left(A\left(\frac{\ell+k}{N}\right)-A\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)\right))

\displaystyle\leq\exp(\beta^2 N \left(A\left(\frac{\ell+k}{N}\right)-A\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)\right))\exp(\beta(a\ell+b)).

(2.37)

Case 2:
-\beta N\left(A\left(\frac{\ell+k}{N}\right)-A\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)\right)+a\ell+b<0
.
In this case, the Chernoff bound yields

(
2.36
)

\displaystyle\leq\exp(2\beta^2 N \left(A\left(\frac{\ell+k}{N}\right)-A\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)\right))

\displaystyle\quad\quad\cdot\exp( -\frac{\left(\beta N (A\left(\frac{\ell+k}{N}\right)-A\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)) - (a \ell+b)\right)^2}{2N \left(A\left(\frac{\ell+k}{N}\right)-A\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)\right)} ).

(2.38)

By the fact that

\displaystyle\left(\beta N\left(A\left(\frac{\ell+k}{N}\right)-A\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)\right)-(a\ell+b)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\beta^{2}N^{2}\left(A\left(\frac{\ell+k}{N}\right)-A\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)\right)^{2}-2\beta N\left(A\left(\frac{\ell+k}{N}\right)-A\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)\right)(a\ell+b),

(
2.38
) yields the bound

(
2.36
)

\displaystyle\leq\exp(\frac{3}{2}\beta^2 N \left(A\left(\frac{\ell+k}{N}\right)-A\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)\right))\exp(\beta(a \ell+ b)).

(2.39)

From the case study above, we conclude that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[e^{2\beta X_{\ell}^{(k)}}\boldsymbol{1}_{H_{\ell}^{(k)}}\right]\leq\exp(\frac{3}{2}\beta^2 N \left(A\left(\frac{\ell+k}{N}\right)-A\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)\right))\exp(\beta(a \ell+ b)).

(2.40)

Applying the bounds (
2.35
) and (
2.40
) to (
2.29
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[(Z^{(k),\leq}_{\beta,N-k})^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2^{N-k}\exp(\frac{3}{2}\beta^2 N \left(A\left(\frac{1}{N}\right)-A\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)\right))\exp(\beta(a (N-k) + b))

\displaystyle+\sum_{\ell=0}^{N-k-1}2^{2(N-k)-\ell-1}\exp(\frac{3}{2}\beta^2 N \left(A\left(\frac{\ell+k}{N}\right)-A\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)\right))\exp(\beta(a \ell+ b))\exp(\beta^2 N \left(1-A\left(\frac{\ell+k}{N}\right)\right))

\displaystyle\leq 2^{2(N-k)}\exp(\beta^2 N \left(1-A\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)\right))

\displaystyle\quad\quad\sum_{\ell=0}^{N-k}2^{-\ell}\exp(\frac{1}{2}\beta^2 N \left(A\left(\frac{\ell+k}{N}\right)-A\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)\right))\exp(\beta(a \ell+ b)).

(2.41)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[Z_{\beta,N-k}^{(k)}\right]^{2}\sum_{\ell=0}^{N-k}2^{-\ell}\exp(\frac{1}{2}\beta^2 N \left(A\left(\frac{\ell+k}{N}\right)-A\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)\right))\exp(\beta(a \ell+ b)),

(2.42)

where (
2.41
) follows from (
2.1
) and the fact that
2^{-\ell-1}\leq 2^{-\ell}
for any integer
\ell
. Now, we decompose the summation in (
2.42
) into

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{N-k}2^{-\ell}\exp(\frac{1}{2}\beta^2 N \left(A\left(\frac{\ell+k}{N}\right)-A\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)\right))\exp(\beta(a \ell+ b))

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{K_{1}}2^{-\ell}\exp(\frac{1}{2}\beta^2 N \left(A\left(\frac{\ell+k}{N}\right)-A\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)\right))\exp(\beta(a \ell+ b))

\displaystyle+\sum_{\ell=K_{1}+1}^{N-k}2^{-\ell}\exp(\frac{1}{2}\beta^2 N \left(A\left(\frac{\ell+k}{N}\right)-A\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)\right))\exp(\beta(a \ell+ b)),

(2.43)

where
K_{1}=K^{2}
.
We start with bounding the first term in (
2.43
). Take
N_{1}
such that
(K_{1}+K)/N\leq x_{1}
for all
N\geq N_{1}
.
Following the same derivation for (
2.14
), for all
N\geq N_{1}
,
k\in\llbracket 1,K\rrbracket
and
\ell\in\llbracket 0,K_{1}\rrbracket
, we have

\displaystyle N\left(A\left(\frac{\ell+k}{N}\right)-A\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)\right)\leq\hat{A}^{\prime}(0)\ell+h(N),

(2.44)

where
h(N)
is defined in (
2.16
).
Applying (
2.44
) then yields

first term in (
2.43
)

\displaystyle\leq\exp(h(N)+\beta b)\sum_{\ell=0}^{K_{1}}\exp(-(\log 2 - \frac{\beta^2}{2}\hat{A}(0)-\beta a)\ell)

\displaystyle=\exp(h(N)+\beta b)\sum_{\ell=0}^{K_{1}}\exp(-c\ell)

\displaystyle\leq\exp(h(N)+\beta b)\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\exp(-c\ell)

\displaystyle=\exp(h(N)+\beta b)\frac{1}{1-e^{-c}}.

(2.45)

It remains to bound the second term in (
2.43
). As in (
2.22
), Assumption
1.1
implies that
A(x)\geq 0
for all
x\in[0,1]
and
A(x)\leq\hat{A}(0)x
for all
x\in[0,1]
, and so

\displaystyle N\cdot\left(A\left(\frac{\ell+k}{N}\right)-A\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)\right)\leq\hat{A}^{\prime}(0)(\ell+k)\leq\hat{A}^{\prime}(0)\ell+\hat{A}^{\prime}(0)K,

(2.46)

where the second inequality above is by the assumption that
k\leq K
. Thus,

second term in (
2.43
)

\displaystyle\leq\exp(\hat{A}'(0)K+\beta b)\sum_{\ell=K_{1}+1}^{N-k}\exp(-(\log 2 - \frac{\beta^2}{2}\hat{A}'(0)-\beta a)\ell)

\displaystyle=\exp(\hat{A}'(0)K+\beta b)\sum_{\ell=K_{1}+1}^{N-k}\exp(-c\ell)

\displaystyle\leq\exp(\hat{A}'(0)K+\beta b)\sum_{\ell=K_{1}+1}^{\infty}\exp(-c\ell)

\displaystyle=\exp(\hat{A}'(0)K+\beta b)\frac{e^{-c(K_{1}+1)}}{1-e^{-c}}

\displaystyle=\exp(-L(N)+\beta b)\frac{e^{-c}}{1-e^{-c}},

(2.47)

where
L(N)=cK_{1}-\hat{A}^{\prime}(0)K=cK^{2}-\hat{A}^{\prime}(0)K
. Combining (
2.43
), (
2.45
) and (
2.47
), we derive that

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{N-k}2^{-\ell}\exp(\frac{1}{2}\beta^2 N \left(A\left(\frac{\ell+k}{N}\right)-A\left(\frac{k}{N}\right)\right))\exp(\beta(a \ell+ b))

\displaystyle\leq\exp(h(N)+\beta b)\frac{1}{1-e^{-c}}+\exp(-L(N)+\beta b)\frac{e^{-c}}{1-e^{-c}}.

(2.48)

Note that
h(N)\rightarrow 0
and
L(N)\rightarrow\infty
as
N\rightarrow\infty
, so there exists
N_{2}
such that
h(N)\leq 1
and
L(N)\geq 1
for all
N\geq N_{2}
. Take
N_{0}=\max\{N_{1},N_{2}\}
. For all
N\geq N_{0}
,
we conclude from (
2.42
) and (
2.48
)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[(Z^{(k),\leq}_{\beta,N-k})^{2}\right]\leq C\mathbb{E}\left[Z^{(k)}_{\beta,N-k}\right]^{2}

where
C=C(A,\beta)=\exp(1+\beta b)\frac{1}{1-e^{-c}}+\exp(-1+\beta b)\frac{e^{-c}}{1-e^{-c}}
.
∎

Proof of Proposition

The main goal of this section is to provide a finer estimate of the left tail of

Proposition 3.1
.

Lemma 3.2

For all

Fix
N\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(Z^{(k)}_{\beta,N-k}\leq c\delta\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[Z^{(k)}_{\beta,N-k}\right]\right)

Graph with any rational density and no rich subsets of linear size
Seonghyuk Im, Suyun Jiang, Hong Liu, Tuan Tran

Abstract
A well-known application of the dependent random choice asserts that any n n -vertex graph G G with positive edge density contains a ‘rich’ vertex subset U U of size n 1 − o ⁡ ( 1 ) n^{1-o(1)} such that every pair of vertices in U U has at least n 1 − o ⁡ ( 1 ) n^{1-o(1)} common neighbors. In 2003, using a beautiful construction on hypercube, Kostochka and Sudakov showed that this is tight: one cannot remove the o ⁡ ( 1 ) o(1) terms even if the edge density of G G is 1 / 2 1/2 . In this paper, we generalize their result from pairs to tuples. To be precise, we show that given every pair of positive integers p < q p<q , there is an n n -vertex graph G G for all sufficiently large n n with edge density p / q p/q such that any vertex subset U U of size Ω ⁡ ( n ) \Omega(n) contains q q vertices, any p + 1 p+1 of which have o ⁡ ( n ) o(n) common neighbors. The edge density p / q p/q is best possible. Our construction uses isoperimetry and concentration of measure on high dimensional complex spheres.

Introduction

Dependent random choice
is a simple yet surprisingly powerful probabilistic technique which has many striking applications to Extremal Graph Theory, Ramsey Theory, Additive Combinatorics, and Combinatorial Geometry.
Its early version was
proved and applied by various researchers, starting with Gowers
[
13
]
, Kostochka and Rödl
[
15
]
, and Sudakov
[
19
]
.
For more information about dependent random choice and its applications, we refer interested readers to the survey
[
12
]
.
The following lemma
[
2
,
15
,
19
]
is a typical result proved by dependent random choice. Let

be positive integers. Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph with
|V|=n
and average degree

\frac{d^{t}}{n^{t-1}}-\binom{n}{r}\left(\frac{m}{n}\right)^{t}\geq a

then

contains a subset

of at least

have at least

A useful corollary of this lemma reads as follows:

Every
n
-vertex graph with
\Omega(n^{2})
edges contains a vertex subset

of size
n^{1-o(1)}
in which every two vertices have
n^{1-o(1)}
common neighbors.

In many applications, it would be ideal to have the size of

and the number of common neighbors of all its pairs be linear in

.
This problem was raised in the study of the famous Burr-Erdős conjecture
[
7
,
16
]
.
However, Kostochka and Sudakov
[
14
]
gave a negative answer via a surprising construction on hypercube.

Theorem 1.1

(
[

,
14
]
)

For sufficiently large

G
on
n
vertices with edge density
1/2+o(1)
such that any subset

of linear size
contains a pair of vertices with at most
o(n)
common neighbors.

In this paper, we construct graphs with any rational edge density, generalizing
Theorem

1.1
from pairs to tuples.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
p
and
q
be positive integers with
p<q
. Then for sufficiently large
n

G
on
n

\Omega(n)
contains
q
vertices, any

o(n)

The edge density

Proposition 1.3

The following holds for every real

Let

\sum_{v\in X}d_{G}(v)<(\frac{p}{q}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2})n^{2}

Consider any

The sum of the degree of vertices in

Thus, by the pigeonhole principle, there are at least
\varepsilon n/4
vertices in
V(G)\setminus U^{\prime}

each of which has at least

neighbors in

U^{\prime}
.
Again by the pigeonhole principle, there are
p+1

Proof sketch.

We now sketch some of the ideas of the proof of
Theorem

1.2
. Let us first recall the simplest
(p,q)=(1,2)
case in
Theorem

1.1
. In this case, Kostochka and Sudakov
[
14
]
defined a graph
G
on vertex set
V:=\{0,1\}^{m}
in which two vertices
\bm{x},\bm{y}\in V
are adjacent if and only if their Hamming distance is at most
m/2
. It is easy to see that
G
has edge density
1/2
. For any vertex subset
U
of linear size, by isoperimetric inequality for Hamming cube,
U
contains two nearly antipodal vectors
\bm{u}_{1}
and
\bm{u}_{2}
, which can be shown to have
o(n)
common neighbors. This construction seems difficult to generalize as the Hamming cube is intrinsically ‘binary’ with a north and a south pole and can be partitioned evenly into two parts. Even for the triple case
q=3
, there is no natural way to partition the Hamming cube into three ‘nice’ parts of almost equal size to define adjacency. To prove
Theorem

1.2
, we work instead with high dimensional complex spheres.

We distribute vertices ‘uniformly’ over complex spheres and define adjacencies by their geometric relation. More specifically, we make use of the angle between two points on a complex sphere, which allows us to construct a graph of arbitrary rational edge density
p/q
by taking edges
\bm{x}\bm{y}
if
\arg\langle\bm{x},\bm{y}\rangle\in[0,\frac{2\pi p}{q}]
.
This type of geometric construction is inspired by a recent generalization of Bollobás-Erdős graph
[
6
]
by Liu, Reiher, Sharifzadeh and Staden
[
17
]
. To show that this construction has the desired property, we first observe that every linear size vertex set must contain
q
points such that every point is close to a rotation of another by a power of the primitive root
e^{2\pi i/q}
. We then use a convexity argument to show that any
p+1
such points must have
o(n)
common neighbors if
p/q\leq 1/2
. This argument, however, fails when
p/q>1/2
as the set
\{z\in\mathbb{C}\mid|z|\leq 1,\arg z\in[0,\frac{2\pi p}{q}]\}
is not convex. To resolve this issue, we use a trick to reduce it to the case
\frac{p}{q}\leq\frac{1}{2}
. In addition, as the inner product of complex vectors does not possess symmetry, we have to use more than one complex spheres to boost to the optimal edge density.

Preliminaries

In this section, we list some definitions and useful properties of inner product and high dimensional complex spheres. By definition, inner product has the following properties.

Fact 2.1
.

For every
\bm{x},\bm{y}\in\mathbb{C}^{k}
and

\operatorname{Im}(\langle\bm{x},-e^{\theta i}\bm{y}\rangle)=\operatorname{Im}(-e^{-\theta i}\langle\bm{x},\bm{y}\rangle)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\operatorname{Im}(e^{\theta i}\langle\bm{x},\bm{y}\rangle)=-\operatorname{Im}(e^{-\theta i}\langle\bm{y},\bm{x}\rangle).

For

denote the standard

-dimensional real unit sphere, and
write

\textsf{S}^{k-1}(\mathbb{C})=\Big\{\bm{z}\in\mathbb{C}^{k}:\sum_{j=1}^{k}|z_{j}|^{2}=1\Big\}

for the
(k-1)
-dimensional complex unit sphere. When given a unit sphere, we will write
\lambda
for the normalized Lebesgue measure so that the unit sphere has measure
1
. We will need the following result
[
17
, Lemma 3.3]
which is a simple consequence of the isoperimetric inequality for spheres
[
18
]
and lower and upper bounds on the measure of spherical caps
[

,

]

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
q\geq 2
be an integer,
0<\nu\leq 1/q^{2}
and
A\subseteq\textsf{S}^{k-1}(\mathbb{C})
with
\lambda(A)>qe^{-k\nu/32}
. Then there are distinct points
\bm{a}_{0},\ldots,\bm{a}_{q-1}\in A
such that

|\zeta^{r}\bm{a}_{r}-\zeta^{s}\bm{a}_{s}|<\sqrt{\nu}\quad\text{for all $r,s\in\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}$},

where
\zeta:=e^{2\pi i/q}
.

We also require the following folklore result which upper-bounds the measure of spherical strips. Its proof can be extracted from that of

[
17
, Claim 3.6]
. For completeness, we include its proof.

Lemma 2.3
.

For all
k\geq 3,\nu>0
and
\bm{x}\in\textsf{S}^{k-1}(\mathbb{C})
, the measure of the strip

\Big\{\bm{y}\in\textsf{S}^{k-1}(\mathbb{C})\colon|\mathrm{Im}\langle\bm{x},\bm{y}\rangle|\leq\frac{\nu}{\sqrt{2k}}\Big\}

is at most
3\nu
.

Proof.

The statement is obviously true for
\nu>1/3
; so we assume that
\nu\leq 1/3
. Define a spherical cap

C:=\left\{\bm{y}\in\textsf{S}^{k-1}(\mathbb{C})\colon|-\bm{x}i-\bm{y}|\leq\sqrt{2}-\nu/\sqrt{2k}\right\}.

By the known lower bound on the measure of spherical caps (see e.g.
[
17
, Lemma 2.1]
), we have
\lambda(C)\geq\frac{1}{2}-\sqrt{2}\nu
. For any
\bm{y}\in C
, using

\nu/\sqrt{2k}\leq 1/3
, we obtain

2-2\,\mathrm{Im}\langle\bm{x},\bm{y}\rangle=2-2\,\mathrm{Re}\langle-\bm{x}i,\bm{y}\rangle=|-\bm{x}i-\bm{y}|^{2}\leq(\sqrt{2}-\nu/\sqrt{2k})^{2}<2-2\nu/\sqrt{2k},

and so
\mathrm{Im}\langle\bm{x},\bm{y}\rangle>\nu/\sqrt{2k}
. Thus, by symmetry, the measure of the strip is at most

1-2\lambda(C)\leq 1-2\left(\frac{1}{2}-\sqrt{2}\nu\right)=2\sqrt{2}\nu
.
∎

It is well-known that the real unit sphere can be partitioned into small pieces of equal measure (see e.g.

Lemma 2.4

can be partitioned into

Claim 3.6

|\langle\frac{1}{q}(\bm{w}_{0}+\cdots+\bm{w}_{q-1}),\bm{u}-\zeta^{\ell}\bm{u}^{\prime}\rangle|<4\mu

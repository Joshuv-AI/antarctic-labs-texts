Abstract
Probabilistic pushdown automata (pPDA) are a natural operational model for a variety of recursive discrete stochastic processes. In this paper, we study certificates – succinct and easily verifiable proofs – for upper and lower bounds on various quantitative properties of a given pPDA. We reveal an intimate, yet surprisingly simple connection between the existence of such certificates and the expected time to termination of the pPDA at hand. This is established by showing that certain intrinsic properties, like the spectral radius of the Jacobian of the pPDA’s underlying polynomial equation system, are directly related to expected runtimes. As a consequence, we obtain that there always exist easy-to-check proofs for positive almost-sure termination : does a pPDA terminate in finite expected time?

Our Contributions

In this paper, we are concerned with the following fundamental question:
Does there exist a
simple proof
– also called
certificate
– that a claimed bound on a given quantitative pPDA property indeed holds?
By
simple
we mean that the proof should be machine-checkable by a verifier with limited resources; in particular, it should be checkable in
\mathit{PTIME}
.
Moreover, the check should be conceptually simple such that the verifier is straightforward to implement, and ideally amenable to formal verification.
We primarily restrict attention to bounds on the probabilities
[pZ{\mid}q]
as those are at the heart of solving many other problems, see above.

Our motivation for studying certificates is twofold.
First, certificates can help increasing the trust one can put in the outputs of a (possibly very complex) analysis engine or probabilistic model checker.
Indeed, a model checker that provides certificates for its results along with a formally verified certificate checker is as trustworthy as a fully formally verified model checker – the latter is usually not in reach for tools relevant in practice.
This is exactly the idea of
certifying algorithms

[
23
]
.
In fact, the authors of
[
23
]
go one step further and argue that

“
[…]
certifying algorithms are much superior to non-certifying algorithms and
[…]
for complex algorithmic tasks only certifying algorithms are satisfactory.

”

Our second, more theoretical motivation is that the study of certificates may of course also spawn new algorithms for pPDA, because an algorithm for finding a certificate (or proving absence thereof) for a given hypothesis is in particular an algorithm deciding that hypothesis.

To the best of our knowledge, certificates for pPDA have only been considered explicitly in the very recent paper
[
22
]
.
There, the basic idea is to exploit the fairly general rule

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{f}(\boldsymbol{u})\leq\boldsymbol{u}~\implies~\mu\boldsymbol{f}\leq\boldsymbol{u}

\boldsymbol{f}
arising from pPDA where the condition
\boldsymbol{f}(\boldsymbol{u})\leq\boldsymbol{u}
is reasonably simply to check.
Applying (
1

The key contributions of this paper are to extend the ideas of

Proof techniques

As a primary technical vehicle, we prove that the spectral radius
\rho
of the underlying system’s Jacobi matrix
\boldsymbol{f}^{\prime}(\mu\boldsymbol{f})
is closely related to the (conditional) expected runtimes (assuming
\boldsymbol{f}
has been previously cleaned-up by removing
“unnecessary”
variables).
This relation is expressed via the simple (tight) inequality
\rho\leq 1-C^{-1}
, where
C
is the maximal conditional expected runtime ocurring in the pPDA at hand (
C=\infty
is possible).
We prove this using results about general PPS from
[
21
]
as well as standard facts about non-negative matrices.
Another important ingredient are
weighted PDA
over semirings
[
17
]
.

Expected runtimes and, more generally, expected rewards in pPDA are studied in

,
6
]

Theorem

]
,

Apart from

It is intuitively clear that our example pPDA terminates (reaches the empty stack) with probability 1, but this property is difficult to prove in general.

General Notation and Markov Chains

\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}=\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\cup\{\infty\}
denotes the set of
extended
non-negative reals.
Arithmetic and comparison with the

-symbol is done as usual:
\forall a\in\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}\colon a+\infty=\infty+a=\infty
,

,
\forall a\in\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}\setminus\{0\}\colon a\cdot\infty=\infty\cdot a=\infty
, and
\forall a\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\colon a<\infty
.
With these conventions,

is a commutative semiring.
Throughout the paper, we often encounter diverging monotonic sequences

\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}

\lim_{i\to\infty}a_{i}=\infty
(and the resulting

We denote vectors by boldface letters such as
\boldsymbol{v}
.
All vectors in this paper can be thought of as column vectors.
A vector with all entries equal to some

is written

.
For instance,
\boldsymbol{0}
and
\boldsymbol{1}
denote the all-zeros and all-ones vectors, respectively.
We always index a vector’s components by the elements of a countable, but otherwise arbitrary index set

to indicate that
\boldsymbol{v}
is a non-negative real vector with

components.
The individual components of a vector
\boldsymbol{v}
are referred to as
\boldsymbol{v}_{i}
,
i\in J
.
Given vectors
\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}\in\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}^{J}
, we define the following

:

\boldsymbol{u}\leq\boldsymbol{v}
if
\forall i\in J\colon\boldsymbol{u}_{i}\leq\boldsymbol{v}_{i}
,

\boldsymbol{u}<\boldsymbol{v}
if
\boldsymbol{u}\leq\boldsymbol{v}

\boldsymbol{u}\neq\boldsymbol{v}
, and

\boldsymbol{u}\prec\boldsymbol{v}
if
\forall i\in J\colon\boldsymbol{u}_{i}<\boldsymbol{v}_{i}
.
Note that
“
\leq
”
is a partial order on
\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}^{J}
.

For matrices we adopt the same index set convention as for vectors:
A (square) matrix

is indexed by pairs of elements from

For

In this paper, a

\mathfrak{M}=(S,M)

III-B
3
(Unconditional) Expected Runtimes

Intuitively, the expected runtime of a DTMC wrt. to initial state
s
and target set
F
is the total expected number of states visited until reaching
F
from
s
.
In particular, if
F
is not reached with probability
1
, then the expected runtime is
\infty
.
This is the key difference to the 1st termination moment, i.e.,
\mathsf{E}[s{\mid}F]
may be finite even if
[s{\mid}F]<1

It is possible to define the expected runtimes formally as the expectation of a random variable on the probability space of

\mathfrak{M}

Definition 2
(Expected runtimes)

Let
\mathfrak{M}=(S,M)
be a DTMC with a target set

.
The

of
\mathfrak{M}
wrt. target

\displaystyle\mathsf{ert}[s{\mid}F]_{\mathfrak{M}}~=~\Big(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}M_{F}^{k}\boldsymbol{(}\boldsymbol{1}-\boldsymbol{\chi}(F))\Big)_{s}~\in~\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}~.

(10)

We write just

if

is clear from the context.

Note that by
Lemma

1
, the values

constitute the least solution
\boldsymbol{r}\in\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}^{S}
of the linear equation system

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{r}~=~M_{F}\boldsymbol{r}+(\boldsymbol{1}-\boldsymbol{\chi}(F))~.

(11)

It follows from (

(i.e.,
\mathfrak{M}

In general, however, we may have

Probabilistic Termination

Definition 3
(AST, PAST, and cPAST)

Given a DTMC
\mathfrak{M}=(S,M)
with target
F\subseteq S
, we define:

•

\mathfrak{M}
is
almost-surely terminating
(AST) wrt.
F

\forall s\in S
, we have
[s{\mid}F]=1
, i.e.,
F
is reached from every state

1
.

•

\mathfrak{M}
is
positively AST
(PAST) wrt.
F
if
\forall s\in S
,
\mathsf{ert}[s{\mid}F]<\infty
.

•

\mathfrak{M}
is conditionally PAST (cPAST) wrt. F if
\forall s\in S
such that
[s{\mid}F]>0
, it holds that
\frac{\mathsf{E}[s{\mid}F]}{[s{\mid}F]}<\infty
.

Note that, since
[s{\mid}F]=0
implies
\mathsf{E}[s{\mid}F]=0
, a DTMC is cPAST iff the 1st termination moments are all finite.

The notion of cPAST, although less well known than AST and PAST, has been considered in
[
7
,
6
]
(the term
“cPAST”
has not been used before though).
Intuitively, cPAST means that termination either occurs
“fast”
(in finite expected time), or not at all.

Note that we consider
universal
(
“for all initial states”
) definitions of AST, PAST, and cPAST.
This is mostly to simplify the presentation, as otherwise we would have to treat uninteresting special cases such as unconnected DTMC seperately.
It is possible to extend our results to the setting of a distinguished initial state of interest by conducting an appropriate reachability analysis in advance.

cPAST

\land

The omitted implications do not hold in general.

We give a self-contained proof for the implication PAST

]
for alternative proofs and

To see that PAST implies AST, first note that for

which follows by induction using that

Now suppose that

III-C

Weighted and Probabilistic PDA

We now define pushdown automata (PDA) with non-negative
rational
transition weights, an instance of PDA with semiring-valued weights as described e.g. in
[
17
]
.

A weighted pushdown automaton (weighted PDA) is a triple
\Delta=(Q,\Gamma,\delta)
,
where
Q
is a
finite
set of
states
,

\Gamma
is a
finite stack alphabet
,
and
\delta\subseteq Q\times\Gamma\times\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}\times Q\times\Gamma^{\leq 2}
is a
weighted transition relation
(here,
\Gamma^{\leq 2}=\{\varepsilon\}\cup\Gamma\cup\Gamma^{2}
).
Intuitively, a transition
(p,Z,a,q,\alpha)\in\delta
means that if
\Delta
is in state
p
and the stack content is
Z\beta
for some
\beta\in\Gamma^{*}
(i.e.,
Z
is on top of the stack), then there exists a transition with weight
a
to the configuration where
\Delta
is in state
q
and the stack content is
\alpha\beta
(i.e.,
Z
is popped and
\alpha
is pushed on the stack).
Instead of
(p,Z,a,q,\alpha)\in\delta
we also write
pZ\overset{a}{\to}q\alpha
if
\delta
is clear from the context.
In the following, we always write tuples from
Q\times\Gamma^{*}
without parentheses and separating commata, e.g., we write
pZ
instead of
(p,Z)
.

A weighted PDA
\Delta
is a
probabilistic
PDA (pPDA) if
\sum_{pZ\overset{a}{\to}q\alpha}a=1
holds for all
pZ\in Q{\times}\Gamma

[
1
]
.
We emphasize that these notions of weighted (probabilistic) PDA do
not
recognize weighted (probabilistic) languages; they are just
generative
models assigning certain weights (probabilities) to finite computation traces.

More formally, we define the
semantics
of a weighted PDA
\Delta
in terms of a countably infinite weighted transition system
\mathfrak{T}_{\Delta}=(Q\times\Gamma^{*},T_{\Delta})
, i.e.,
T_{\Delta}
is a
\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}
-valued square martix indexed by the
configurations

Q{\times}\Gamma^{*}
of
\Delta
(which are the states of
\mathfrak{T}_{\Delta}
).

T_{\Delta}
is defined as follows:
For all
pZ\overset{a}{\to}q\alpha
and
\beta\in\Gamma^{*}
,
T_{\Delta}(pZ\beta,q\alpha\beta)=a
, and all other entries of
T_{\Delta}
are zero.
Note that if
\Delta
is a pPDA, then
\mathfrak{T}_{\Delta}
can be seen as a DTMC with target set
F=\{p\varepsilon\mid p\in Q\}
because the states of the form
p\varepsilon
have no outgoing transitions in
T_{\Delta}
.

We extend the notion of termination moments (Def.
1
) to the weighted transition systems
\mathfrak{T}_{\Delta}
induced by a weighted PDA

in the natural way.
For reasons explained below, we mostly consider
0
th moments of the form
[pZ{\mid}q\varepsilon]_{\Delta}
, i.e., the sum of the weights of all finite paths from
pZ
to
q\varepsilon
.
In the following, we omit
\varepsilon
and
\Delta
from the notation whenever this causes no confusion and write just
[pZ{\mid}q]
.
In a pPDA,
[pZ{\mid}q]
is the probability to reach
q\varepsilon
from initial configuration
pZ
.
In the context of pPDA, the probabilities
[pZ{\mid}q]
are also called
return probabilities

[
12
]
.

We will also consider 1st termination moments of the form
\mathsf{E}[pZ{\mid}q\varepsilon]_{\Delta}
in pPDA (but never in general weighted PDA).
For the sake of brevity, those are denoted as
\mathsf{E}[pZ{\mid}q]
.

Theorem 1
(Fundamental system of PDA
[
17
]
,
[
1
]
)

Let
\Delta=(Q,\Gamma,\delta)
be a weighted PDA.
Consider
\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
-valued variable symbols of the form
\langle pZ{\mid}q\rangle

pZq\in Q{\times}\Gamma{\times}Q
.
Then the quadratic system of equations

\langle pZ{\mid}q\rangle~=~\sum_{pZ\overset{a}{\to}q\varepsilon}a~+~\sum_{pZ\overset{a}{\to}rY}a\cdot\langle rY{\mid}q\rangle\\
~+~\sum_{pZ\overset{a}{\to}rYX}a\cdot\sum_{t\in Q}\langle rY{\mid}t\rangle\cdot\langle tX{\mid}q\rangle

has the (point-wise) least solution

.

We refer to
[
11

for an intuitive explanation of (

).

Finally, we extend the various notions of termination from Def.
3
to pPDA

and

•

\Delta
is

•

\Delta
is

if

In the context of pPDA, we simply write

because we will only consider expected runtimes wrt. to
F
as defined above.

•

\Delta
is

if
\mathfrak{T}_{\Delta}
is cPAST wrt. to

III-D

Positive Polynomial Systems (PPS)

Let
J
be a finite index set and consider a vector
\boldsymbol{x}=(\boldsymbol{x}_{j})_{j\in J}
of variable symbols.
A
positive polynomial system
(PPS) over the variables
\boldsymbol{x}
is a vector
\boldsymbol{f}=(\boldsymbol{f}_{j})_{j\in J}
of multivariate polynomials
\boldsymbol{f}_{j}
with variables in
\boldsymbol{x}
and
non-negative coefficients
in
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
.

A PPS
\boldsymbol{f}
induces an endofunction
\boldsymbol{f}\colon\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}^{J}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}^{J},\boldsymbol{u}\mapsto\boldsymbol{f}(\boldsymbol{u})=(\boldsymbol{f}_{j}(\boldsymbol{u}))_{j\in J}
.
As the coefficients of the polynomials in
\boldsymbol{f}
are non-negative, the function
\boldsymbol{f}
is
monotone
, i.e.,
\boldsymbol{u}\leq\boldsymbol{v}\implies\boldsymbol{f}(\boldsymbol{u})\leq\boldsymbol{f}(\boldsymbol{v})
, where
\leq
is the element-wise order on
\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}^{J}
.
Since the domain
(\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}^{J},\leq)
of
\boldsymbol{f}
is a
complete lattice
, the Knaster-Tarski fixed point theorem implies that
\boldsymbol{f}
has a
least fixed point
(lfp)
\mu\boldsymbol{f}\in\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}^{J}
.
In other words, the polynomial equation system
\boldsymbol{x}=\boldsymbol{f}(\boldsymbol{x})
is guaranteed to have a
least
solution
\mu\boldsymbol{f}\in\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}^{J}
, wrt. to
\leq
.
A PPS
\boldsymbol{f}
is called
feasible
if
\mu\boldsymbol{f}\prec\boldsymbol{\infty}
, i.e.,
\mu\boldsymbol{f}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{J}
.

Lemma 3
(Inductive upper bounds)

Let
\boldsymbol{f}
be a PPS and let
\boldsymbol{u}\geq\boldsymbol{0}
.
The following statements hold:

•

\boldsymbol{f}(\boldsymbol{u})\leq\boldsymbol{u}\implies\mu\boldsymbol{f}\leq\boldsymbol{u}

•

\boldsymbol{f}(\boldsymbol{u})\prec\boldsymbol{u}\implies\mu\boldsymbol{f}\prec\boldsymbol{u}

Proof:

The first item is an immediate consequence of the Knaster-Tarski fixed point theorem.
For the second item, note that
\boldsymbol{u}\succ\boldsymbol{f}(\boldsymbol{u})\geq\boldsymbol{f}^{1}(\boldsymbol{u})\geq\boldsymbol{f}^{2}(\boldsymbol{u})\ldots
is a monotonic and bounded sequence, hence
\lim_{i\to\infty}\boldsymbol{f}^{i}(\boldsymbol{u})
exists.
Since
\boldsymbol{f}
is a continuous function,
\boldsymbol{f}(\lim_{i\to\infty}\boldsymbol{f}^{i}(\boldsymbol{u}))=\lim_{i\to\infty}\boldsymbol{f}^{i+1}(\boldsymbol{u})=\lim_{i\to\infty}\boldsymbol{f}^{i}(\boldsymbol{u})
, so the latter term is a fixed point of
\boldsymbol{f}
.
Thus
\mu\boldsymbol{f}\leq\lim_{i\to\infty}\boldsymbol{f}^{i}(\boldsymbol{u})\prec\boldsymbol{u}
.
∎

We often denote PPS in the form of an equation system
\boldsymbol{x}=\boldsymbol{f}(\boldsymbol{x})
.
In this paper we are mostly concerned with PPS of type (
13
), as well as with
linear
PPS that can be written in the form
\boldsymbol{x}=A\boldsymbol{x}+\boldsymbol{b}
, for some matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{J\times J}
and vector
\boldsymbol{b}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{J}
(cf.
Lemma

1
).

A PPS
\boldsymbol{f}
is called
clean
if
\mu\boldsymbol{f}\succ\boldsymbol{0}
.
General PPS may be not clean, e.g.,
x=2x
is not clean.
However, it is possible to compute, in linear time
[
4
,
21
]
, a
cleaned-up
variant
1 0 .0 1
\mathbbm{f}
of
\boldsymbol{f}
by identifying and removing variables that are assigned zero in the lfp.
Formally, the cleaned-up system
1 0 .0 1
\mathbbm{f}
satisfies the property that
(i)
1 0 .0 1
\mathbbm{f}
is clean,
(ii)
1 0 .0 1
\mathbbm{f}
is indexed by a subset
J^{\prime}\subseteq J
,
(iii) for all
j\in J^{\prime}
, we have
(\mu\mbox{\hskip 3.05557pt{1 0 .0 1}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\hbox{$\mathbbm{f}$}}})_{j}=(\mu\boldsymbol{f})_{j}
, and
(iv) for all
j\in J\setminus J^{\prime}
,
(\mu\boldsymbol{f})_{j}=0
.
For example, the cleaned-up version of
(x,y)=(2x,1+x+y)
is just

(whose lfp is
\infty
, i.e., it is not feasible).

Throughout the paper we consistently use the symbol
\boldsymbol{f}
for general, not necessarily clean PPS, and we reserve the symbol
1 0 .0 1
\mathbbm{f}
for clean PPS.

Let
\boldsymbol{f}
be a PPS indexed by

The

The Jacobi matrix with all entries

For a finite matrix

The

V
Certifying Lower Bounds

In this section we show that it is also possible to certify lower bounds in general PPS.
The contents of this section are not crucial for understanding the subsequent sections.

Recall that the simple induction principle from
Lemma

3
does
not
work for lower bounds on the lfp.
Indeed, if
f\colon L\to L
is a monotonic function and
L
a complete lattice, then we only have the
“co-induction”
rule

\forall l\in L\colon\quad l\leq f(l)~\implies~l\leq\nu f~,

where
\nu f
is the
greatest
fixed point of
f
.
As a consequence, the implication
l\leq f(l)\implies l\leq\mu f
holds for all
l\in L
if and only if
f
has a
unique
fixed point.
However, a PPS
\boldsymbol{f}
may have multiple fixed points in general.
For example, the one-variable PPS

x=f(x)=x^{2}+\frac{1}{5}

satisfies
1\leq f(1)=\frac{6}{5}
, but
\mu f=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{5}}<1
.

We circumvent this problem by providing a co-inductive point
\boldsymbol{l}
along with a (strict) inductive upper bound
\boldsymbol{u}
:

Theorem 5
(Certificates for lower bounds on the lfp)

Let
\boldsymbol{f}
be a PPS indexed by
J
.
Then for all
\boldsymbol{l},\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{J}
,

\boldsymbol{l}\leq\boldsymbol{f}(\boldsymbol{l})\land\boldsymbol{l}\leq\boldsymbol{u}\land\boldsymbol{f}(\boldsymbol{u})\prec\boldsymbol{u}~\implies~\boldsymbol{l}\leq\mu\boldsymbol{f}\prec\boldsymbol{u}~.

Moreover, if
\boldsymbol{f}
is feasible, non-singular and clean, then for all
\varepsilon>0
there exist
\boldsymbol{l},\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}^{J}
satisfying the above conditions such that
\|{\boldsymbol{l}-\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{\infty}\leq\varepsilon
.

Proof:

Let
\boldsymbol{l},\boldsymbol{u}
be such that
\boldsymbol{l}\leq\boldsymbol{f}(\boldsymbol{l}),\boldsymbol{l}\leq\boldsymbol{u}
, and
\boldsymbol{f}(\boldsymbol{u})\leq\boldsymbol{u}
.
The first step of the proof is to construct a fixed point
\tilde{\boldsymbol{u}}\prec\boldsymbol{u}
.
Let
\tilde{\boldsymbol{u}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\boldsymbol{f}^{n}(\boldsymbol{u})\prec\boldsymbol{u}
.
By monotonicity, we have
\boldsymbol{l}\leq\boldsymbol{f}^{n}(\boldsymbol{l})\leq\boldsymbol{f}^{n}(\boldsymbol{u})
for all
n\geq 0
, so
\boldsymbol{l}\leq\tilde{\boldsymbol{u}}
.
Moreover,
\tilde{\boldsymbol{u}}
is a fixed point of
\boldsymbol{f}
since the latter is continuous, so by the variant of Taylor’s theorem (below
Lemma

4
),

\displaystyle\tilde{\boldsymbol{u}}=\boldsymbol{f}(\tilde{\boldsymbol{u}})=\boldsymbol{f}(\boldsymbol{u}-(\boldsymbol{u}-\tilde{\boldsymbol{u}}))\leq\boldsymbol{f}(\boldsymbol{u})-\boldsymbol{f}^{\prime}(\tilde{\boldsymbol{u}})(\boldsymbol{u}-\tilde{\boldsymbol{u}})

(16)

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{f}^{\prime}(\tilde{\boldsymbol{u}})(\boldsymbol{u}-\tilde{\boldsymbol{u}})\leq\boldsymbol{f}(\boldsymbol{u})-\tilde{\boldsymbol{u}}\prec\boldsymbol{u}-\tilde{\boldsymbol{u}}~.

(17)

The last inequality implies that
\rho({\boldsymbol{f}^{\prime}(\tilde{\boldsymbol{u}})})<1
by
Theorem

3
.
We will need this further below.

We now show that
\mu\boldsymbol{f}=\tilde{\boldsymbol{u}}
.
Towards contradiction assume that
\tilde{\boldsymbol{u}}>\mu\boldsymbol{f}
, i.e.,
\tilde{\boldsymbol{u}}-\mu\boldsymbol{f}>\boldsymbol{0}
.
By the right inequality of Taylor’s theorem,

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{f}(\tilde{\boldsymbol{u}})=\boldsymbol{f}(\mu\boldsymbol{f}+(\tilde{\boldsymbol{u}}-\mu\boldsymbol{f}))\leq\mu\boldsymbol{f}+\boldsymbol{f}^{\prime}(\tilde{\boldsymbol{u}})(\tilde{\boldsymbol{u}}-\mu\boldsymbol{f}).

(18)

From (
18
) and
\tilde{\boldsymbol{u}}=\boldsymbol{f}(\tilde{\boldsymbol{u}})
it follows that

\displaystyle\tilde{\boldsymbol{u}}-\mu\boldsymbol{f}~=~\boldsymbol{f}(\tilde{\boldsymbol{u}})-\mu\boldsymbol{f}~\leq~\boldsymbol{f}^{\prime}(\tilde{\boldsymbol{u}})(\tilde{\boldsymbol{u}}-\mu\boldsymbol{f})~.

(19)

Using (
19
) and the fact that
\boldsymbol{f}^{\prime}(\tilde{\boldsymbol{u}})
is non-negative, it follows that
\tilde{\boldsymbol{u}}-\mu\boldsymbol{f}\leq\boldsymbol{f}^{\prime}(\tilde{\boldsymbol{u}})^{n}(\tilde{\boldsymbol{u}}-\mu\boldsymbol{f})
for all
n\geq 0
.
However, since
\rho({\boldsymbol{f}^{\prime}(\tilde{\boldsymbol{u}})})<1
, we have by
Theorem

3
that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\boldsymbol{f}^{\prime}(\tilde{\boldsymbol{u}})=0
, so there exists
n
such that
\boldsymbol{f}^{\prime}(\tilde{\boldsymbol{u}})^{n}(\tilde{\boldsymbol{u}}-\mu\boldsymbol{f})<\tilde{\boldsymbol{u}}-\mu\boldsymbol{f}
, contradiction.
It follows that
\mu\boldsymbol{f}=\tilde{\boldsymbol{u}}
.
As
\boldsymbol{l}\leq\tilde{\boldsymbol{u}}
we have shown that
\boldsymbol{l}
is indeed a lower bound on the lfp of
\boldsymbol{f}
.

We now prove the second part of the theorem.
Let
\boldsymbol{f}=\mbox{\hskip 3.05557pt{1 0 .0 1}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\hbox{$\mathbbm{f}$}}}
be feasible, non-singular and clean.
A rational
\boldsymbol{u}
with
\mbox{\hskip 3.05557pt{1 0 .0 1}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\hbox{$\mathbbm{f}$}}}(\boldsymbol{u})\prec\boldsymbol{u}
arbitrarily close to
\mu\mbox{\hskip 3.05557pt{1 0 .0 1}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\hbox{$\mathbbm{f}$}}}
exists by
Theorem

4
.
It remains to show existence of a rational co-inductive
\boldsymbol{l}\leq\mbox{\hskip 3.05557pt{1 0 .0 1}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\hbox{$\mathbbm{f}$}}}(\boldsymbol{l})
.
The argument is analogous to the proof of
Theorem

4
, but this time for lower bounds:
Since
1 0 .0 1
\mathbbm{f}
feasible, non-singular and clean,
Lemma

6
and
Theorem

3
yield that there exists
\boldsymbol{v}\succ\boldsymbol{0}
such that
\mbox{\hskip 3.05557pt{1 0 .0 1}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\hbox{$\mathbbm{f}$}}}^{\prime}(\mu\mbox{\hskip 3.05557pt{1 0 .0 1}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\hbox{$\mathbbm{f}$}}})\boldsymbol{v}\prec\boldsymbol{v}
.
Choose such a
\boldsymbol{v}\leq\mu\mbox{\hskip 3.05557pt{1 0 .0 1}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\hbox{$\mathbbm{f}$}}}
.
By the variant of Taylor’s theorem,

\displaystyle\mu\mbox{\hskip 3.05557pt{1 0 .0 1}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\hbox{$\mathbbm{f}$}}}-\boldsymbol{v}~\prec~\mu\mbox{\hskip 3.05557pt{1 0 .0 1}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\hbox{$\mathbbm{f}$}}}-\mbox{\hskip 3.05557pt{1 0 .0 1}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\hbox{$\mathbbm{f}$}}}^{\prime}(\mu\mbox{\hskip 3.05557pt{1 0 .0 1}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\hbox{$\mathbbm{f}$}}})\boldsymbol{v}~\leq~\mbox{\hskip 3.05557pt{1 0 .0 1}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\hbox{$\mathbbm{f}$}}}(\mu\mbox{\hskip 3.05557pt{1 0 .0 1}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\hbox{$\mathbbm{f}$}}}-\boldsymbol{v})

(20)

With
\boldsymbol{l}=\mu\mbox{\hskip 3.05557pt{1 0 .0 1}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\hbox{$\mathbbm{f}$}}}-\boldsymbol{v}
it follows that
\boldsymbol{l}\prec\mbox{\hskip 3.05557pt{1 0 .0 1}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\hbox{$\mathbbm{f}$}}}(\boldsymbol{l})
.
Moreover,
\boldsymbol{v}\succ\boldsymbol{0}
can be chosen arbitrarily small, and hence
\boldsymbol{l}
arbitrarily close to
\mu\mbox{\hskip 3.05557pt{1 0 .0 1}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\hbox{$\mathbbm{f}$}}}
.
Finally, by continuity of
1 0 .0 1
\mathbbm{f}
, the inequality
\boldsymbol{l}^{\prime}\prec\mbox{\hskip 3.05557pt{1 0 .0 1}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\hbox{$\mathbbm{f}$}}}(\boldsymbol{l}^{\prime})
holds for all
\boldsymbol{l}^{\prime}
in an open ball around
\boldsymbol{l}
; such a ball contains rational vectors.
∎

VI
cPAST and Non-singular PPS

In this section we characterize non-singularity in terms of expected runtimes.
In a nutshell, the main result is that the (cleaned-up) fundamental PPS of a pPDA
\Delta
is non-singular iff
\Delta
is cPAST.
We begin by characterizing the 1st termination moments as the solution of a finite linear equation system.

Lemma 7
(System of termination moments)

Let
\Delta=(Q,\Gamma,\delta)
be a pPDA and let
(\mathsf{E}\langle pZ{\mid}q\rangle)_{pZq\in Q{\times}\Gamma{\times}Q}
be
\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
-valued variables.
Then the 1st termination moments
\mathsf{E}[pZ{\mid}q]
constitute the least solution of the following
linear
PPS:

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}\langle pZ{\mid}q\rangle~=~[pZ{\mid}q]+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}pZ\overset{a}{\to}rY\end{subarray}}a\cdot\mathsf{E}\langle rY{\mid}q\rangle

\displaystyle{+}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}pZ\overset{a}{\to}rYX\\
t\in Q\end{subarray}}a{\cdot}\big(\mathsf{E}\langle rY{\mid}t\rangle{\cdot}[tX{\mid}q]+[rY{\mid}t]{\cdot}\mathsf{E}\langle tX{\mid}q\rangle\big)

(21)

where
[pZ{\mid}q]
, etc., are the return probabilities of
\Delta
.

Proof:

We sketch a proof based on a specific type of formal power series known as
probability generating functions
, e.g.
[
34
, App. F]
,
[
38
]
.
We will use the fact that
Theorem

1
actually holds in a more general setting where the transition weights come from a
semiring
with suitable closure properties
[
17
]
.
The
semiring
\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}[[\mathsf{z}]]
of formal power series
with coefficients in
\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
satisfies these properties.
Here,
\mathsf{z}
is a
formal variable
, and the elements in
\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}[[\mathsf{z}]]
are
formal series
of the form
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}\mathsf{z}^{k}
, with coefficients
a_{k}\in\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
.
See
[
17
]
for details.

Let
\mathsf{z}\Delta
be the
\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}[[\mathsf{z}]]
-weighted PDA obtained from
\Delta
by multiplying each transition probability by
\mathsf{z}
.
The 0th termination moment is then a power series
[pZ{\mid}q]_{\mathsf{z}\Delta}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}\mathsf{z}^{k}
where for all
k\geq 0
, the coefficient
a_{k}
equals the sum of the probabilities of the proper paths of length
k
between
pZ
and
q\varepsilon
.
Consider the usual differentiation operator
\partial{}
for formal power series.
We have

\displaystyle\partial{[pZ{\mid}q]_{\mathsf{z}\Delta}}~=~\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k\cdot a_{k}\mathsf{z}^{k-1}

(22)

and thus by definition of the termination moments,

\displaystyle{\partial{[pZ{\mid}q]_{\mathsf{z}\Delta}}}\big\rvert_{\mathsf{z}=1}

\displaystyle~=~\mathsf{E}[pZ{\mid}q]~\text{, and also}

(23)

\displaystyle{[pZ{\mid}q]_{\mathsf{z}\Delta}}\big\rvert_{\mathsf{z}=1}

\displaystyle~=~[pZ{\mid}q]~,

(24)

where
{\cdot}\big\rvert_{\mathsf{z}=1}\colon\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}[[\mathsf{z}]]\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
is the function that substitutes the formal variable
\mathsf{z}
for
1
and takes the resulting limit (which may be
\infty
).
By (the extended)
Theorem

1
, the triples
[pZ{\mid}q]_{\mathsf{z}\Delta}
are the least solution in
\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}[[\mathsf{z}]]
of the following equation system with variables
\langle pZ{\mid}q\rangle
:

\langle pZ{\mid}q\rangle~=~\mathsf{z}\cdot\Big(\sum_{pZ\overset{a}{\to}q\varepsilon}a~+~\sum_{pZ\overset{a}{\to}rY}a\cdot\langle rY{\mid}q\rangle\\
~+~\sum_{pZ\overset{a}{\to}rYX}a\cdot\sum_{t\in Q}\langle rY{\mid}t\rangle\cdot\langle tX{\mid}q\rangle\Big)~.

(25)

Differentiating
all equations in this system using the product rule yields the following
additional
equations:

\partial\langle pZ{\mid}q\rangle~=~\langle pZ{\mid}q\rangle~+~\mathsf{z}\cdot\Big(\sum_{pZ\overset{a}{\to}rY}a\cdot\partial{\langle rY{\mid}q\rangle}\\
{+}\sum_{pZ\overset{a}{\to}rYX}a{\cdot}\sum_{t\in Q}(\partial{\langle rY{\mid}t\rangle}{\cdot}\langle tX{\mid}q\rangle{+}\langle rY{\mid}t\rangle{\cdot}\partial{\langle tX{\mid}q\rangle})\Big)

(26)

where
\partial{\langle pZ{\mid}q\rangle}
,
\partial{\langle rY{\mid}t\rangle}
, etc., are fresh variable symbols.
It can be shown that the power series
\partial{[pZ{\mid}q]_{\mathsf{z}\Delta}}
and
[pZ{\mid}q]_{\mathsf{z}\Delta}
constitute the least solution of the overall system (
25
)
\land
(
26
).
Finally, since the substitution operation
{\cdot}\big\rvert_{\mathsf{z}=1}
is a semiring-homomorphism we may apply it to the equations in (
26
), and use (
23
) and (
24
) to obtain (
7
)
∎

Lemma 9

\mathbbm{f}

VII
Certifying PAST

In this section we are concerned with certificates for positive almost-sure termination (PAST; see Def.
3
).
We begin by characterizing the expected runtimes in terms of a linear equation system.

Theorem 7
(System of expected runtimes)

Let
\Delta=(Q,\Gamma,\delta)
be a pPDA and let
(\mathsf{ert}\langle pZ\rangle)_{pZ\in Q{\times}\Gamma}
be
\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
-valued variables.
The expected runtimes
\mathsf{ert}[pZ]
constitute the least solution of the following
linear
PPS:

\mathsf{ert}\langle pZ\rangle~=~1~+~\sum_{pZ\overset{a}{\to}rY}a\cdot\mathsf{ert}\langle rY\rangle\\
{+}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}pZ\overset{a}{\to}rYX\end{subarray}}a{\cdot}\big(\mathsf{ert}\langle rY\rangle{+}\sum_{t\in Q}[rY{\mid}t]{\cdot}\mathsf{ert}\langle tX\rangle\big)

(32)

Proof:

We give an automata theoretic proof.
The idea is to construct a
non-probabilistic
weighted PDA
\Delta^{\prime}
such that for all
pZ\in Q\times\Gamma
,
\mathsf{ert}[pZ]=[pZ{\mid}q^{*}]_{\Delta^{\prime}}
, where
q^{*}
is a distinguished state in
\Delta^{\prime}
.
In other words, we express the expected runtimes of
\Delta
in terms of the 0th termination moments of a new automaton.

Given a pPDA
\Delta=(Q,\Gamma,\delta)
, we construct the weighted PDA
\Delta^{\prime}=(Q^{\prime},\Gamma,\delta^{\prime})
as follows:
Q^{\prime}=Q\cup\{q^{*}\}
, and

\displaystyle\delta^{\prime}~=~\delta~\cup~

\displaystyle\{pZ\overset{1}{\to}q^{*}Z\mid pZ\in Q\times\Gamma\}

\displaystyle~\cup~

\displaystyle\{q^{*}Z\overset{1}{\to}q^{*}\varepsilon\mid Z\in\Gamma\}~.

(33)

Intuitively,
\Delta^{\prime}
is obtained from
\Delta
by adding a new state
q^{*}
and weight-1 transitions from every state of
\Delta
to
q^{*}
(these new transitions are independent of the current topmost stack symbol).
Moreover, once
q^{*}
is reached,
\Delta^{\prime}
simply empties its stack.
Note that
\Delta^{\prime}
is not probabilistic anymore.

Fix an initial configuration
pZ\in Q\times\Gamma
.
Consider the 0th termination moment
[pZ{\mid}q^{*}]_{\Delta^{\prime}}\in\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
which is the sum of the weights of all proper finite paths

\displaystyle\{pZ\}\,.\,(Q{\times}\Gamma^{+})^{*}\,.\,(\{q^{*}\}\times\Gamma^{+})\,.\,\{q^{*}\varepsilon\}

(34)

between
pZ
and
q^{*}\varepsilon
in
\mathfrak{T}_{\Delta^{\prime}}
.
It follows immediately from the construction of
\Delta^{\prime}
that there is a weight-preserving bijection between the paths (
34
) and the paths in
\mathfrak{T}_{\Delta}
that have not yet reached a configuration with empty stack, i.e., the paths

\displaystyle\{pZ\}\,.\,(Q{\times}\Gamma^{+})^{*}~.

(35)

As explained below Def.
2
, the sum of the probabilities of the paths (
35
) is exactly
\mathsf{ert}[pZ]_{\Delta}
.
Thus,
[pZ{\mid}q^{*}]_{\Delta^{\prime}}=\mathsf{ert}[pZ]_{\Delta}
.

To finish the proof we use the fact that we have access to the 0th moments
[pZ{\mid}q^{*}]_{\Delta^{\prime}}
via the fundamental PPS
\boldsymbol{f}_{\Delta^{\prime}}
of
\Delta^{\prime}
.
By construction of
\Delta^{\prime}
,
\boldsymbol{f}_{\Delta^{\prime}}
contains the equations from
\boldsymbol{f}_{\Delta}
, as well as for each
pZ\in Q\times\Gamma
the additional equations

\displaystyle\langle pZ{\mid}q^{*}\rangle~=~\langle q^{*}Z{\mid}q^{*}\rangle~+~\sum_{pZ\overset{a}{\to}rY}a\cdot\langle rY{\mid}q^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{pZ\overset{a}{\to}rYX}a\cdot\big(\sum_{t\in Q}\langle rY{\mid}t\rangle{\cdot}\langle tX{\mid}q^{*}\rangle+\langle rY{\mid}q^{*}\rangle{\cdot}\langle q^{*}X{\mid}q^{*}\rangle\big)

(36)

\displaystyle\langle q^{*}Z{\mid}q^{*}\rangle~=~1~,

(37)

where the transitions under the summation symbols refer to those of
\Delta
(
not
to those of
\Delta^{\prime}
).
Simplifying and identifying
\langle pZ{\mid}q^{*}\rangle
with
\mathsf{ert}\langle pZ\rangle
yields (
32
).
∎

Remark 2

If one assumes upfront that the pPDA
\Delta
is AST, then it is not difficult to derive equation system (
32
) from
[
7
, Thm 3.1/Ex. 3.3]
(see
Section

-D
for details).
However,
Theorem

7
also holds if
\Delta
is not AST.
That is, if
\sum_{q\in Q}[pZ{\mid}q]<1
for some
pZ\in Q\times\Gamma
, then
\mathsf{ert}[pZ]=\infty
in the least solution of (
32
).
In other words, system (
32
) has a non-negative real-valued solution (i.e., it is feasible) iff
\Delta
is PAST.
The direction from left to right does not easily follow from
[
7
]
.
However, this direction is crucial for proving/certifying PAST without explicitly proving AST beforehand (see
Corollary

1
and
Theorem

8
further below).
The fact that PAST can be proved directly without proving AST first is a major insight of this paper.

System (
32
) can also be written in matrix form as

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{r}~=~M_{\Delta}(\mu\boldsymbol{f})\boldsymbol{r}+\boldsymbol{1}~,

(38)

where
\boldsymbol{r}=(\mathsf{ert}\langle pZ\rangle)_{pZ\in Q{\times}\Gamma}
, and
M_{\Delta}(\mu\boldsymbol{f})
is a matrix that depends on the transitions of
\Delta
and the return probabilities
\mu\boldsymbol{f}
; the exact entries of
M_{\Delta}(\mu\boldsymbol{f})
can be read off from (

).

Corollary 1
(Proving PAST)

Let
\Delta
be a pPDA with fundamental PPS
\boldsymbol{f}
and suppose that
\mu\boldsymbol{f}\leq\boldsymbol{u}
.
If
M_{\Delta}(\boldsymbol{u})\boldsymbol{r}+\boldsymbol{1}\leq\boldsymbol{r}
has a solution
\boldsymbol{r}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 1}^{Q\times\Gamma}
, then
\Delta
is PAST.
In this case,
\mathsf{ert}[pZ]\leq\boldsymbol{r}_{pZ}
for all
pZ\in Q{\times}\Gamma
.

Proof:

We have
M_{\Delta}(\boldsymbol{u})\geq M_{\Delta}(\mu\boldsymbol{f})
entry-wise.
Thus any
\boldsymbol{r}\geq\boldsymbol{0}
with
M_{\Delta}(\boldsymbol{u})\boldsymbol{r}+\boldsymbol{1}\leq\boldsymbol{r}
is a component-wise upper bound on the least solution
\boldsymbol{r}^{\prime}
of
M_{\Delta}(\mu\boldsymbol{f})\boldsymbol{r}^{\prime}+\boldsymbol{1}=\boldsymbol{r}^{\prime}
.
But by
Theorem

7
, the components of
\boldsymbol{r}^{\prime}
are exactly the expected runtimes.
∎

Corollary

1
allows turning any iterative scheme (e.g.
[
21
,
22
]
) for computing a monotonically converging sequence

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{u}^{(0)}\geq\boldsymbol{u}^{(1)}\ldots\geq\mu\boldsymbol{f}~,\quad\lim_{i\to\infty}\boldsymbol{u}^{(i)}=\mu\boldsymbol{f}~,

(39)

of
upper
bounds on the lfp of
\boldsymbol{f}
into a semi-algorithm that detects in finite time if a given pPDA
\Delta
is PAST or non-AST, and that may diverge only if
\Delta
is AST but not PAST.
For
i=0,1,2,\ldots
, after computing
\boldsymbol{u}^{(i)}
, the semi-algorithm simply checks if

1.

M_{\Delta}(\boldsymbol{u}^{(i)})\boldsymbol{r}+\boldsymbol{1}\leq\boldsymbol{r}
is feasible in
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
in which case it reports
“PAST”
, or else

2.

if
\sum_{t}[pZ{\mid}t]<1
for some
pZ\in Q{\times}\Gamma
in which case is reports
“non-AST”
.

3.

If neither (a) nor (b) apply, then continue with
i+1
.

The correctness of this algorithm is straightforward with
Corollary

1
.
Termination in the PAST case follows because
\boldsymbol{u}^{(i)}
is eventually sufficiently close to
\mu\boldsymbol{f}
such that
M_{\Delta}(\boldsymbol{u}^{(i)})\boldsymbol{r}+\boldsymbol{1}\leq\boldsymbol{r}
has a solution in
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
.

Theorem 8
(Certifying PAST)

Let
\Delta=(Q,\Gamma,\delta)
be a pPDA with fundamental PPS
\boldsymbol{f}
.

\Delta
is PAST
if and only if

\exists\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}^{Q\times\Gamma\times Q},\boldsymbol{r}\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 1}^{Q\times\Gamma}\colon\boldsymbol{f}(\boldsymbol{u})\leq\boldsymbol{u}
and
M_{\Delta}(\boldsymbol{u})\boldsymbol{r}+\boldsymbol{1}\leq\boldsymbol{r}
,
where
M_{\Delta}
is the matrix from (
38
).

Proof:

Assume that
\Delta
is PAST.
Recall from
Lemma

2
that PAST implies cPAST.
By
Theorem

6
,
1 0 .0 1
\mathbbm{f}
, the cleaned-up version of
\boldsymbol{f}
, is non-singular.
By
Theorem

4
, there exists
\boldsymbol{u}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}^{J^{\prime}}
, where
J^{\prime}\subseteq Q{\times}\Gamma{\times}Q
is the index set of
1 0 .0 1
\mathbbm{f}
, such that
\mbox{\hskip 3.05557pt{1 0 .0 1}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\hbox{$\mathbbm{f}$}}}(\boldsymbol{u}^{\prime})\prec\boldsymbol{u}^{\prime}
.
Moreover, such a
\boldsymbol{u}^{\prime}
can be chosen arbitrarily close to
\mu\mbox{\hskip 3.05557pt{1 0 .0 1}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\hbox{$\mathbbm{f}$}}}
.
We can extend
\boldsymbol{u}^{\prime}
to a vector indexed by
\boldsymbol{u}
in
Q{\times}\Gamma{\times}Q
by setting the components that belong to variables not present in
1 0 .0 1
\mathbbm{f}
to zero; we then still have
\boldsymbol{f}(\boldsymbol{u})\leq\boldsymbol{u}
.
Since
\Delta
is PAST,
M_{\Delta}(\mu\boldsymbol{f})\boldsymbol{r}+\boldsymbol{1}=\boldsymbol{r}
is feasible by
Theorem

7
, i.e., it has a solution in
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 1}^{Q\times\Gamma}
.
The entries of
M_{\Delta}(\bullet)
depend continuously on the argument
\bullet
, so we can choose
\boldsymbol{u}
sufficiently close to
\mu\boldsymbol{f}
such that
M_{\Delta}(\boldsymbol{u})\boldsymbol{r}+\boldsymbol{1}=\boldsymbol{r}
is still feasible.
As
M_{\Delta}(\boldsymbol{u})\boldsymbol{r}+\boldsymbol{1}=\boldsymbol{r}
is obviously clean,
M_{\Delta}(\boldsymbol{u})
is non-singular by
Lemma

9
.
Since
M_{\Delta}(\boldsymbol{u})
has rational entries, the unique solution of
\boldsymbol{r}=(I-M_{\Delta}(\boldsymbol{u}))^{-1}\boldsymbol{1}
is thus also rational.

The other direction follows from
Corollary

1
using
\boldsymbol{f}(\boldsymbol{u})\leq\boldsymbol{u}\implies\mu\boldsymbol{f}\leq\boldsymbol{u}
.
∎

Our results also admit a simple proof of the following result which is already known in the literature
[
26
, Chap. 4]
,
[
25
, Thm. 3]
.
A
pBPA
is a pPDA with
|Q|=1
.

Corollary 2
(PAST in pBPA)

Let
\Delta
be a pBPA
with fundamental PPS
\boldsymbol{f}
.
Then
\Delta
is PAST iff
\boldsymbol{r}=\boldsymbol{1}+\boldsymbol{f}^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{1})\boldsymbol{r}
has a unique solution
\boldsymbol{r}\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 1}^{\Gamma}
.
In this case,
\mathsf{ert}[Z]=\boldsymbol{r}_{Z}
for all
Z\in\Gamma
.
As a consequence, deciding PAST for pBPA can be done in
\mathit{PTIME}
, and the bit complexity of the expected runtimes is polynomial in the encoding size of
\Delta
.

Proof:

First note that since
\Delta
has a single state, the termination moments and expected runtimes can be written as
\mathsf{E}^{k}[Z]
and
\mathsf{ert}[Z]
, respectively, where
Z\in\Gamma
.
Moreover, the system (
7
) of 1st moments collapses to

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}\langle Z\rangle{=}[Z]{+}\sum_{Z\overset{a}{\to}Y}a{\cdot}\mathsf{E}\langle Y\rangle{+}\sum_{Z\overset{a}{\to}YX}a{\cdot}(\mathsf{E}\langle Y\rangle{\cdot}[X]{+}[Y]{\cdot}\mathsf{E}\langle X\rangle)

(40)

and the system (
32
) of expected runtimes collapses to

\displaystyle\mathsf{ert}\langle Z\rangle{=}1{+}\sum_{Z\overset{a}{\to}Y}a{\cdot}\mathsf{ert}\langle Y\rangle{+}\sum_{Z\overset{a}{\to}YX}a{\cdot}(\mathsf{ert}\langle Y\rangle{+}[Y]{\cdot}\mathsf{ert}\langle X\rangle).

(41)

Now suppose
\Delta
is PAST.
Then
\Delta
is also AST, so for all
Z\in\Gamma
,
[Z]=1
, i.e.,
\mu\boldsymbol{f}=\boldsymbol{1}
.
Then (
40
) and (
41
) are just the
same
equation system, so
\mathsf{E}[Z]=\mathsf{ert}[Z]
for all
Z\in\Gamma
.
By
Lemma

8
, (
40
) can be written in matrix form as
\boldsymbol{x}=\boldsymbol{1}+\boldsymbol{f}^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{1})\boldsymbol{x}
.
The latter is a linear, clean and feasible PPS, so by
Lemma

9
, it has the unique solution
\boldsymbol{x}=(I-\boldsymbol{f}^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{1}))^{-1}\boldsymbol{1}
.
Since
\boldsymbol{f}^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{1})
is has rational entries, it follows from standard linear algebra that the solution is rational and can be computed in polynomial time.
In particular, the rational numbers in the solution can be encoded with polynomially many bits.

For the other direction suppose that
\boldsymbol{r}=\boldsymbol{1}+\boldsymbol{f}^{\prime}(\boldsymbol{1})
is feasible.
But then
(
41
)
is also feasible, so
\Delta
is PAST.
∎

VIII
Complexity of Certificates

Our results so far have been
qualitative
, i.e., we have shown existence of certificates but we have not yet discussed how large (the rational numbers in) these certificates are.
This is addressed in the present section.

From
Theorem

6
it follows that
\rho({\mathbbm{f}_{\Delta}^{\prime}(\mu\mathbbm{f}_{\Delta})})<1
iff
\Delta
is cPAST.
The next lemma is a quantitative generalization:

Lemma 10
(Bound on spectrum)

Let
\Delta
be a pPDA with cleaned-up fundamental PPS
1 0 .0 1
\mathbbm{f}
, and suppose that
\Delta
is cPAST.
Let
C=\max_{pZq}\frac{\mathsf{E}[pZ{\mid}q]}{[pZ{\mid}q]}\geq 1
be the maximal conditional expected runtime of
\Delta
, and
\mu\mbox{\hskip 3.05557pt{1 0 .0 1}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\hbox{$\mathbbm{f}$}}}_{\min}=\min_{pZq}[pZ{\mid}q]
be the minimum non-zero return probability.
Then there exists
\boldsymbol{v}\succ\boldsymbol{0}
,
\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{1}=1
, s.t.
\mbox{\hskip 3.05557pt{1 0 .0 1}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\hbox{$\mathbbm{f}$}}}^{\prime}(\mu\mbox{\hskip 3.05557pt{1 0 .0 1}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\hbox{$\mathbbm{f}$}}})\boldsymbol{v}\leq(1-C^{-1})\boldsymbol{v}
, and
\boldsymbol{v}_{\min}\geq\frac{\mu\mbox{\>{1 0 .0 1}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\hbox{$\mathbbm{f}$}}}_{\min}}{C|Q|^{2}|\Gamma|}
.

As a consequence,
\rho({\mbox{\hskip 3.05557pt{1 0 .0 1}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\hbox{$\mathbbm{f}$}}}^{\prime}(\mu\mbox{\hskip 3.05557pt{1 0 .0 1}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\hbox{$\mathbbm{f}$}}})})\leq(1-C^{-1})
.

Proof:

Let
\boldsymbol{v}
be the vector of positive termination moments, i.e., the unique solution of
\boldsymbol{v}=\mu\mbox{\hskip 3.05557pt{1 0 .0 1}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\hbox{$\mathbbm{f}$}}}+\mbox{\hskip 3.05557pt{1 0 .0 1}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\hbox{$\mathbbm{f}$}}}^{\prime}(\mu\mbox{\hskip 3.05557pt{1 0 .0 1}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\hbox{$\mathbbm{f}$}}})\boldsymbol{v}\succ\boldsymbol{0}
.
Then

\displaystyle\mbox{\hskip 3.05557pt{1 0 .0 1}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\hbox{$\mathbbm{f}$}}}^{\prime}(\mu\mbox{\hskip 3.05557pt{1 0 .0 1}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\hbox{$\mathbbm{f}$}}})\boldsymbol{v}~=~\boldsymbol{v}-\mu\mbox{\hskip 3.05557pt{1 0 .0 1}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\hbox{$\mathbbm{f}$}}}~\leq~c\boldsymbol{v}

(42)

where

\displaystyle c

\displaystyle~=~\max_{pZq}\frac{\mathsf{E}[pZ{\mid}q]-[pZ{\mid}q]}{\mathsf{E}[pZ{\mid}q]}~=~1-\min_{pZq}\frac{[pZ{\mid}q]}{\mathsf{E}[pZ{\mid}q]}

\displaystyle~=~1-\left(\max_{pZq}\frac{\mathsf{E}[pZ{\mid}q]}{[pZ{\mid}q]}\right)^{-1}~=~1-C^{-1}~.

By
Lemma

5
, (
42
) implies that
c=1-C^{-1}
is an upper bound on
\rho({\mbox{\hskip 3.05557pt{1 0 .0 1}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\hbox{$\mathbbm{f}$}}}^{\prime}(\mu\mbox{\hskip 3.05557pt{1 0 .0 1}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\hbox{$\mathbbm{f}$}}})})
.
We can normalize
\boldsymbol{v}
(in the
\|{\cdot}\|_{1}
norm) as follows:

\boldsymbol{v}^{\prime}=\frac{\boldsymbol{v}}{\sum_{pZq}\mathsf{E}[pZ{\mid}q]}
,
\|{\boldsymbol{v}^{\prime}}\|=1
.
Then

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{v}^{\prime}_{\min}

\displaystyle~\geq~\frac{\min_{pZq}\mathsf{E}[pZ{\mid}q]}{|Q|^{2}|\Gamma|\max_{pZq}\mathsf{E}[pZ{\mid}q]}

\displaystyle~\geq~\frac{\min_{pZq}[pZ{\mid}q]}{|Q|^{2}|\Gamma|\max_{pZq}\frac{\mathsf{E}[pZ{\mid}q]}{[pZ{\mid}q]}}~=~\frac{\mu\mbox{\hskip 3.05557pt{1 0 .0 1}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\hbox{$\mathbbm{f}$}}}_{\min}}{|Q|^{2}|\Gamma|C}~.

∎

The bound from
Lemma

10
on the spectral radius
\rho({\mbox{\hskip 3.05557pt{1 0 .0 1}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\hbox{$\mathbbm{f}$}}}^{\prime}(\mu\mbox{\hskip 3.05557pt{1 0 .0 1}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\hbox{$\mathbbm{f}$}}})})
is tight:
Consider e.g. the pPDA
\Delta_{a}
with a single state and stack symbol and the transitions
pZ\overset{a}{\to}\varepsilon
,
pZ\overset{1-a}{\to}pZZ
.
The fundamental PPS of
\Delta_{a}
is
\langle Z\rangle=(1-p)\langle Z\rangle^{2}+p
.
For
a>\frac{1}{2}
,
\Delta_{a}
is PAST with expected runtime
C=\frac{1}{2a-1}
, and the spectral radius is indeed
2(1-a)=1-C^{-1}
.

The next lemma allows bounding the side lengths of a (hyper)cube fully contained in the inductive region
\{\boldsymbol{u}\geq\boldsymbol{0}\mid\mbox{\hskip 3.05557pt{1 0 .0 1}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\hbox{$\mathbbm{f}$}}}(\boldsymbol{u})\leq\boldsymbol{u}\}
(see
Section

-E
for the proof).

Lemma 11

Let
\Delta=(Q,\Gamma,\delta)
be a pPDA with cleaned-up fundamental PPS
1 0 .0 1
\mathbbm{f}
.
If
\Delta
is cPAST, then there exists a vector
\boldsymbol{v}\succ\boldsymbol{0}
,
\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{1}=1
, such that the inequality

\displaystyle\mbox{\hskip 3.05557pt{1 0 .0 1}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\hbox{$\mathbbm{f}$}}}(\mu\mbox{\hskip 3.05557pt{1 0 .0 1}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\hbox{$\mathbbm{f}$}}}+d(\boldsymbol{v}+\boldsymbol{a}))~\leq~\mu\mbox{\hskip 3.05557pt{1 0 .0 1}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\hbox{$\mathbbm{f}$}}}+d(\boldsymbol{v}+\boldsymbol{a})

(43)

holds for all

\displaystyle 0~\leq~d~\leq~\frac{\mu\mbox{\hskip 3.05557pt{1 0 .0 1}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\hbox{$\mathbbm{f}$}}}_{\min}}{C}\frac{1}{4|Q|^{2}|\Gamma|(|Q|^{2}|\Gamma|+1)^{2}}

(44)

and
\boldsymbol{a}\geq\boldsymbol{0}
with
\|{\boldsymbol{a}}\|_{\infty}\leq e
, where

\displaystyle e~=~\frac{\mu\mbox{\hskip 3.05557pt{1 0 .0 1}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\hbox{$\mathbbm{f}$}}}_{\min}}{C^{2}}\frac{1}{4|Q|^{4}|\Gamma|^{2}}

(45)

where
C=\max_{pZq}\frac{\mathsf{E}[pZ{\mid}q]}{[pZ{\mid}q]}<\infty
is the maximal conditional expected runtime in
\Delta
, and
\mu\mbox{\hskip 3.05557pt{1 0 .0 1}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\hbox{$\mathbbm{f}$}}}_{\min}>0
is the minimal entry of
\mu\mbox{\hskip 3.05557pt{1 0 .0 1}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\hbox{$\mathbbm{f}$}}}
.

Theorem 9
(Complexity of inductive upper bounds)

Let
\Delta=(Q,\Gamma,\delta)
be a pPDA with fundamental PPS
\boldsymbol{f}
.
Suppose that
\Delta
is cPAST with maximal conditional expected runtime
C
and minimal non-zero return probability
\mu\mbox{\hskip 3.05557pt{1 0 .0 1}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\hbox{$\mathbbm{f}$}}}_{\min}
.
Then for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}^{Q\times\Gamma\times Q}
,
with
\boldsymbol{f}(\boldsymbol{u})\leq\boldsymbol{u}
and
\|{\mu\boldsymbol{f}-\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{\infty}\leq\varepsilon
,
such that the rational numbers in

IX
Conclusions and Open Questions

We have shown that the (large) class of pPDA that are cPAST admits easy-to-check certificates for two-sided bounds on the return probabilities
[pZ{\mid}q]
.
On the equation system level, we have linked cPAST to the non-singularity of the system’s Jacobi matrix
I-\mbox{\hskip 3.05557pt{1 0 .0 1}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\hbox{$\mathbbm{f}$}}}^{\prime}(\mu\mbox{\hskip 3.05557pt{1 0 .0 1}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\hbox{$\mathbbm{f}$}}})
.
Moreover, we have presented a novel characterization of PAST and showed that it can always be certified (provided the pPDA at hand is in fact PAST).

Based on our results we conjecture the following:

Almost all pPDA are cPAST
, and in particular,
almost all pPDA that are AST are also PAST
.
This is because for a random pPDA
\Delta
(with real-valued weights sampled from continuous intervals), the matrix
\mbox{\hskip 3.05557pt{1 0 .0 1}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\hbox{$\mathbbm{f}$}}}^{\prime}(\mu\mbox{\hskip 3.05557pt{1 0 .0 1}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\hbox{$\mathbbm{f}$}}})
is essentially a random matrix, and random matrices are almost-surely non-singular.
We leave an appropriate formalization of this result for future work.

[
22
]
provides some empirical evidence that singular
I-\mbox{\hskip 3.05557pt{1 0 .0 1}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\hbox{$\mathbbm{f}$}}}^{\prime}(\mu\mbox{\hskip 3.05557pt{1 0 .0 1}\hbox to0.0pt{\hss\hbox{$\mathbbm{f}$}}})
occur indeed very rarely in practice.
Also note that
all
finite DTMC are cPAST, and it would be interesting to see how this evolves as one ascends to even higher levels in the generative Chomsky hierarchy
[
39
]
.

Further open problems include finding a general upper bound on the expected runtimes, and on the exact complexity of computing certificates.

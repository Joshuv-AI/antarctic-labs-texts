Donaldson-Thomas invariants for 3 3 -Calabi-Yau varieties of dihedral quotient type
Sergey Mozgovoy, Markus Reineke

Abstract
. We compute motivic Donaldson-Thomas invariants for crepant resolutions of quotients of affine three-space by even dihedral groups in terms of an affine type D D root system, using double dimensional reduction and the representation theory of affine type D D quivers.



1.
Introduction

The Donaldson-Thomas invariants of Calabi-Yau threefolds, introduced in
[
12
]
, admit a motivic refinement defined in
[
1
,
6
]
, from which several other numerical invariants can be derived.

A complete description of all motivic DT invariants is achieved only for very special classes of Calabi-Yau threefolds,
in particular,
for affine three-space
[
1
]
, for the (resolved) conifold
[
7
]
,
for crepant resolutions related to Kleinian singularities
[
9
]
,
and for small crepant resolutions
of affine toric Calabi-Yau
3
-folds
[
8
]
(except the case of
\mathbb{C}^{3}/(\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2})
, considered in this paper).
See
[
11
]
for an overview of these results.

In the present work, we give a complete description of motivic DT invariants for crepant resolutions of quotients of affine three-space by a natural action of even dihedral groups. Our main result, Theorem
5.1
, shows that the motivic DT invariants are related to
an affine type
D
root system, and that only three different non-zero values of the DT invariants appear.

As in the case of the works cited above, this result is achieved by factoring the motivic generating series of a particular
3
-Calabi-Yau algebra, given as the Jacobian algebra of a quiver with potential naturally associated to the dihedral group action
[
4
]
. Since the relevant potential admits a cut
[
7
]
, dimensional reduction can be performed, leading to a twisted version of a preprojective algebra of an extended Dynkin quiver
[
10
]
. It admits a further dimensional reduction, which reduces the problem to a generating series of representations of an extended Dynkin quiver, twisted by a certain interaction form. Using the known representation theory of extended Dynkin quivers, this quadratic form can be computed on all representations, and an explicit factorization of the motivic generating function can be achieved.

We start in Section
2
by recalling the construction of quivers with potentials from finite group actions on affine three-space, the definition of motivic generating functions and motivic Donaldson-Thomas invariants, and the method of dimensional reduction for potentials with cuts. In Section
3
we compute the quiver with potential corresponding to the dihedral group action we are interested in, relate the algebra resulting from dimensional reduction to twisted preprojective algebras, and state an explicit form of the motivic generating function involving the crucial interaction form. Its explicit computation is the topic of Section
4
. After these preparations, we formulate and prove our main result in Section
5
, and illustrate it in the example of the action of a Klein four-group.

2.
Recollections on 3-CY algebras and their DT invariants

In this section, we collect the main definitions and results on
3
-Calabi-Yau algebras and their Donaldson-Thomas invariants.

2.1.
3-Calabi-Yau algebras

We follow
[
4
, §4.4]
. Let
G
be a finite subgroup of
{\rm SL}_{3}(\mathbb{C})
, and let
L_{1},\ldots,L_{s}
be a system of representatives of the isomorphim classes of irreducible representations of
G
. Let
V=\mathbb{C}^{3}
be the defining representation of
G
, which therefore carries a
G
-invariant determinant map

\operatorname{det}\colon V^{\otimes 3}\to\bigwedge^{3}V\simeq\mathbb{C}
.
We define the McKay quiver,
associated with representation
V
,
as the quiver with vertices
1,\ldots,s
, and the arrows
i\rightarrow j
parametrized by a basis of
{\rm Hom}_{G}(L_{i},L_{j}\otimes V)
. For any oriented triangle
\Delta\colon\,i\rightarrow j\rightarrow k\rightarrow i
in
Q
, we define
\lambda_{\Delta}
as the scalar factor of the identity map in the composition

(1)

L_{i}\rightarrow L_{j}\otimes V\rightarrow L_{k}\otimes V^{\otimes 2}\rightarrow L_{i}\otimes V^{\otimes 3}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{det}}L_{i}.

We define a potential for
Q
as the linear combination

(2)

W=\sum_{\Delta}\lambda_{\Delta}\Delta,

the summation ranging over all oriented triangles in
Q
up to cyclic shift.
Let
J=\mathbb{C}Q/\langle\partial W\rangle
be the Jacobian algebra for the quiver with potential
(Q,W)
.
By
[
4
, Theorem 4.4.6]
,
J
is Morita equivalent to the skew group algebra
\mathbb{C}[V]\#G
.
By
[
2
]
,
its category of modules is derived equivalent to the category of coherent sheaves on any crepant resolution of the quotient
V/\!\!/G
.

2.2.
Donaldson-Thomas invariants

We follow
[
7
]
. Let
(Q,W)
be a pair consisting of a finite quiver
Q
, with set of vertices
Q_{0}
and set of arrows
Q_{1}
, and a potential
W\in\mathbb{C}Q/[\mathbb{C}Q,\mathbb{C}Q]
, that is, a finite linear combination of cyclic paths considered up to cyclic shift.
We denote by

(3)

J=\mathbb{C}Q/\langle\partial W\rangle=\mathbb{C}Q/\left\langle\partial W/\partial a\rcol a\in Q_{1}\right\rangle

the Jacobian algebra of
(Q,W)
. We denote by
\chi_{Q}
the homological Euler form of
Q
.

For a dimension vector
d\in\mathbb{N}Q_{0}
, let
R(Q,d)=\bigoplus_{a\colon i\to j}\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{C}^{d_{i}},\mathbb{C}^{d_{j}})
be the variety of representations of
Q
of dimension vector
d
, which carries a natural action of the base change group
G_{d}=\prod_{i\in Q_{0}}\operatorname{GL}_{d_{i}}(\mathbb{C})
.
Let
R(J,d)\subset R(Q,d)
be the closed subvariety of
J
-representations.
It is known that
R(J,d)=\operatorname{crit}(f_{d})
,
the set of critial points of the function
f_{d}\colon R(Q,d)\to\mathbb{C}

given by the trace of the potential
W
on representations of
Q
.
Consider the quotient stack
\mathcal{M}(J,d)=[\operatorname{crit}(f_{d})/G_{d}]
,
which is the moduli stack of representations of
J
of dimension vector
d
.

In the following, we work in the localized Grothendieck ring

(4)

R=K_{0}(\operatorname{Var}_{\mathbb{C}})[q^{\pm{\frac{1}{2}}},\,(1-q^{i})^{-1}\rcol i\geq 1]

of complex varieties, where
q=[\mathbb{A}^{1}]
denotes the Lefschetz motive. In fact, all calculations in this work will be performed in the subring

R^{\prime}=\mathbb{Q}[q^{\pm{\frac{1}{2}}},\,(1-q^{i})^{-1}\rcol i\geq 1]\subset\mathbb{Q}(q^{\frac{1}{2}})
.
The moduli stack
\mathcal{M}(J,d)
admits a virtual motive
[\mathcal{M}(J,d)]_{\mathrm{vir}}\in R
, defined using motivic nearby cycles. We will not need the precise definition, since in the case of potentials with cuts (see §
2.4
), this virtual motive can be expressed in more elementary terms.
In the situation we are interested in, it is in fact an element of
R^{\prime}
.

Let
R[Q_{0}]=\bigoplus_{d\in\mathbb{N}Q_{0}}Rt^{d}
be the algebra of polynomials over
R
and let
R[\![Q_{0}]\!]
be its completion.
We define the (total) motivic generating series
of
(Q,W)
as

(5)

\mathcal{A}(t)=\sum_{d\in\mathbb{N}Q_{0}}[\mathcal{M}(J,d)]_{\mathrm{vir}}\cdot t^{d}\in R[\![Q_{0}]\!].

We define the plethystic exponential
\operatorname{Exp}
of a series
in
R^{\prime}[\![Q_{0}]\!]
without constant term as

(6)

\operatorname{Exp}\left(\sum_{d\in\mathbb{N}Q_{0}}\sum_{k\geq 0}c_{d,k}\cdot q^{k/2}t^{d}\right)=\prod_{d\in\mathbb{N}Q_{0}}\prod_{k\geq 0}(1-q^{k/2}t^{d})^{-c_{d,k}}.

Assuming that
\mathcal{A}(t)\in R^{\prime}[\![Q_{0}]\!]
, we define the motivic Donaldson–Thomas invariants
\Omega_{d}(q)\in\mathbb{Q}(q^{{\frac{1}{2}}})
of
(Q,W)
by the factorization

(7)

\mathcal{A}(t)={\rm Exp}\left(\frac{\sum_{d}\Omega_{d}(q)t^{d}}{q-1}\right).

2.3.
q
-Pochhammer symbols

Let us define the
q
-Pochhammer symbols

(8)

(t;q)_{n}=\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}(1-q^{i}t),\qquad(t;q)_{\infty}=\prod_{i=0}^{\infty}(1-q^{i}t)=\operatorname{Exp}\left(\frac{t}{q-1}\right).

They satisfy

(9)

\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{(a;q)_{n}}{(q;q)_{n}}t^{n}=\frac{(at;q)_{\infty}}{(t;q)_{\infty}}=\operatorname{Exp}\left(\frac{1-a}{1-q}t\right).

which leads to the versions of the
q
-binomial theorem

(10)

\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{t^{n}}{(q;q)_{n}}=\frac{1}{(t;q)_{\infty}},\qquad\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{(-1)^{n}q^{\binom{n}{2}}t^{n}}{(q;q)_{n}}=(t;q)_{\infty}.

In what follows we will use
(q)_{n}=(q;q)_{n}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-q^{i})
.
Note that

(11)

[\operatorname{GL}_{n}]=q^{n^{2}}\cdot(q^{-1})_{n},\qquad n\geq 1.

2.4.
DT invariants for potentials with cuts

Let
(Q,W)
be a quiver with a potential and
I\subset Q_{1}
be a cut,
meaning a subset such that every non-zero term of
W
contains exactly one element from
I
.
Let
Q^{\prime}
be the quiver
(Q_{0},Q_{1}\backslash I)
and

(12)

J_{I}=\mathbb{C}Q^{\prime}/\left\langle\partial W/\partial a\rcol a\in I\right\rangle.

The required motivic generating series (
5
) is equal to

(13)

\mathcal{A}(t)=\sum_{d\in\mathbb{N}Q_{0}}(-q^{\frac{1}{2}})^{s(d,d)}\frac{[R(J_{I},d)]}{[G_{d}]}t^{d},\qquad s(d,e)=\chi_{Q}(d,e)+2\sum_{(a\colon i\to j)\in I}d_{i}e_{j}

and is independent of a cut.

Let us assume that there is another cut
I^{\prime}\subset Q_{1}
disjoint from
I
.
We define the quiver
Q^{\prime\prime}=(Q_{0},Q_{1}\backslash(I\cup I^{\prime}))
and consider the forgetful map

(14)

\pi\colon R(J_{I},d)\to R(Q^{\prime\prime},d)

having linear fibers.
Given a
Q^{\prime\prime}
-representation
M
, let
\phi(M)
denote the dimension of the fiber
\pi^{-1}(M)
.
In our applications
\phi(M)
will be quadratic, meaning that
there exist values
\phi(M,N)
such that
\phi(M)=\phi(M,M)
and
\phi(\bigoplus_{i}M_{i},\bigoplus_{j}N_{j})=\sum_{i,j}\phi(M_{i},N_{j})
.

Let
S
be the set parameterizing isomorphism classes of all indecomposable
Q^{\prime\prime}
-representations.
Then every representation can be written in the form
M=\bigoplus_{X\in S}X^{\oplus m_{X}}

for some map
m\colon S\to\mathbb{N}
with finite support.
Therefore we have

(15)

\mathcal{A}(t)=\sum_{d\in\mathbb{N}Q_{0}}\sum_{\operatorname{\mathbf{dim}}M=d}\frac{(-q^{\frac{1}{2}})^{s(d,d)+2\phi(M)}}{[\operatorname{Aut}M]}t^{d}\\
=\sum_{m\colon S\to\mathbb{N}}\frac{(-q^{\frac{1}{2}})^{-\sum_{X,Y}m_{X}m_{Y}\sigma(X,Y)}}{\prod_{X}(q^{-1})_{m_{X}}}t^{\sum_{X}m_{X}\operatorname{\mathbf{dim}}X}

where we used the fact that (see
[
9
]
)

(16)

[\operatorname{Aut}M]=[\operatorname{End}M]\cdot\prod_{X\in S}(q^{-1})_{m_{X}},\qquad M=\bigoplus_{X\in S}X^{\oplus m_{X}},

and where

(17)

\sigma(M,N)=2h^{0}(M,N)-2\phi(M,N)-s(d,e),\qquad d=\operatorname{\mathbf{dim}}M,\,e=\operatorname{\mathbf{dim}}N,

and
h^{i}(M,N)=\dim\operatorname{Ext}^{i}(M,N)
for
M,N\in\operatorname{Rep}(Q^{\prime\prime})
.
The formula (
15
),
representing the motivic generating series of
(Q,W)
in terms of representations of
Q^{\prime\prime}
,
is called the (double) dimensional reduction formula.

Remark 2.1
.

In the above formulas we can substitute
\sigma
by its symmetrization.
We call the map
\sigma\colon S\times S\to\mathbb{Z}
an interaction form and we say that
M,N
do not interact if
\sigma(M,N)=0
(for the symmetrized form
\sigma
).
Assume that we can decompose
S=S_{1}\sqcup\dots\sqcup S_{r}
so that elements of
S_{i}
and
S_{j}
do not interact for
i\neq j
.
We can define the generating series
\mathcal{A}_{k}(t)
by restricting the above sum to maps
m\colon S_{k}\to\mathbb{N}
.
Then we obtain
\mathcal{A}(t)=\prod_{k}\mathcal{A}_{k}(t)
.
In what follows we will decompose
S
into simple classes that don’t interact with each other and then we will compute generating series for separate classes.

2.5.
Generating series of
C_{2}

The following result describes the motivic generating series of the cyclic quiver
C_{2}
(equipped with the zero potential) having two vertices
1,2
and arrows
1\to 2
and
2\to 1
.
It will be used later in our study of motivic generating series associated to dihedral quotients.

Theorem 2.2
.

The motivic generating series of the cyclic quiver
C_{2}
is equal to

F(t)=\sum_{d\in\mathbb{N}^{2}}\frac{(-q^{\frac{1}{2}})^{-(d_{1}-d_{2})^{2}}}{\prod_{i}(q^{-1})_{d_{i}}}t^{d}=\operatorname{Exp}\left(\frac{qt_{1}t_{2}-q^{{\frac{1}{2}}}(t_{1}+t_{2})}{q-1}\right).

Proof.

Let
\chi_{C_{2}}
be the Euler form of
C_{2}
.
Then the motivic generating series of
C_{2}
is
(
13
)

F(t)=\sum_{d\in\mathbb{N}^{2}}(-q^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\chi_{C_{2}}(d,d)}\frac{[R(C_{2},d)]}{[G_{d}]}t^{d}=\sum_{d\in\mathbb{N}^{2}}\frac{(-q^{\frac{1}{2}})^{-\chi_{C_{2}}(d,d)}}{\prod_{i}(q^{-1})_{d_{i}}}t^{d}

which is the left hand side of the required equality.

Let us consider the forgetful map
\pi\colon R(C_{2},d)\to R(A_{2},d)
, where
A_{2}
is the quiver
1\to 2
.
Given a representation
M\in R(A_{2},d)
, the corresponding fiber has dimension
\phi(M)=d_{1}d_{2}
.
Let
X_{1}=S_{1},X_{2}=S_{2},X_{3}=P_{1}
be the indecomposable representations of
A_{2}
.
Then every
A_{2}
-representation
M
can be written in the form
M=\bigoplus_{i}X_{i}^{\bigoplus m_{i}}
for some
m\in\mathbb{N}^{3}
.
Note that
d=\operatorname{\mathbf{dim}}M=(m_{1}+m_{3},m_{2}+m_{3})
and

h^{0}(M,M)=\sum_{i,j}m_{i}m_{j}h^{0}(X_{i},X_{j})=\sum_{i}m_{i}^{2}+m_{2}m_{3}+m_{3}m_{1}=\colon h^{0}(m,m).

As in (
15
), we obtain

F(t)=\sum_{d}\sum_{\operatorname{\mathbf{dim}}M=d}\frac{(-q^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\chi_{C_{2}}(d,d)+2d_{1}d_{2}}}{[\operatorname{Aut}M]}t^{d}\\
=\sum_{{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in\mathbb{N}^{3}\\
d=(m_{1}+m_{3},m_{2}+m_{3})\end{subarray}}}\frac{(-q^{\frac{1}{2}})^{d_{1}^{2}+d_{2}^{2}-2h^{0}(m,m)}}{\prod_{i}(q^{-1})_{m_{i}}}t_{1}^{m_{1}+m_{3}}t_{2}^{m_{2}+m_{3}}=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{N}^{3}}\frac{(-q^{\frac{1}{2}})^{-m_{1}^{2}-m_{2}^{2}}}{\prod_{i}(q^{-1})_{m_{i}}}t_{1}^{m_{1}}t_{2}^{m_{2}}(t_{1}t_{2})^{m_{3}}.

We note that (the formulas correspond to the case of a one loop quiver and a zero loop quiver respectively; they also follow from the
q
-binomial theorem)

\sum_{m\geq 0}\frac{t^{m}}{(q^{-1})_{m}}=\operatorname{Exp}\left(\frac{qt}{q-1}\right),\qquad\sum_{m\geq 0}\frac{(-q^{\frac{1}{2}})^{-m^{2}}}{(q^{-1})_{m}}t^{m}=\operatorname{Exp}\left(\frac{-q^{\frac{1}{2}}t}{q-1}\right).

Therefore

F(t)=\operatorname{Exp}\left(\frac{-q^{\frac{1}{2}}t_{1}}{q-1}\right)\cdot\operatorname{Exp}\left(\frac{-q^{\frac{1}{2}}t_{2}}{q-1}\right)\cdot\operatorname{Exp}\left(\frac{qt_{1}t_{2}}{q-1}\right)=\operatorname{Exp}\left(\frac{qt_{1}t_{2}-q^{{\frac{1}{2}}}(t_{1}+t_{2})}{q-1}\right).

∎

3.
McKay quivers for dihedral groups

3.1.
McKay quiver with potential for even dihedral groups

We first collect some elementary facts on the representation theory of even dihedral groups.
So we consider the group
D_{2\ell}=\mathbb{Z}_{2}\ltimes\mathbb{Z}_{2\ell}
with

\ell\geq 1
.
It admits the standard presentation

D_{2\ell}=\left\langle d,s\rcol s^{2}=d^{2\ell}=1,\,sds=d^{-1}\right\rangle

and we can describe complex representations of
D_{2\ell}
by specifying pairs of matrices
(A,B)
representing
d
and
s
, respectively.
We define one-dimensional representations

\rho_{0}\colon\,(1,1),\quad\rho_{1}\colon\,(1,-1),\quad\rho_{2}\colon\,(-1,1),\quad\rho_{3}\colon\,(-1,-1)

and two-dimensional representations

\tau_{k}\colon\,\left(\begin{pmatrix}z^{k}&0\\
0&z^{-k}\end{pmatrix},\ \begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}\right),\qquad k\in\mathbb{Z},

where
z=e^{\pi i/\ell}
.
Note that
\tau_{k}\simeq\tau_{2\ell-k}
.
The following lemma summarizes the well-known representation theory of
D_{2\ell}
:

Lemma 3.1
.

The irreducible representations of
D_{2\ell}
are
\rho_{k}
for
k=0,1,2,3
and
\tau_{k}
for
k=1,\ldots,\ell-1
.
We have

\tau_{0}\simeq\rho_{0}\oplus\rho_{1},\qquad\tau_{\ell}\simeq\rho_{2}\oplus\rho_{3},

\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{1}\simeq\rho_{0}\simeq\rho_{2}\otimes\rho_{2},\qquad\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2}\simeq\rho_{3},

\tau_{1}\otimes\tau_{k}\simeq\tau_{k-1}\oplus\tau_{k+1},\qquad k\in\mathbb{Z},

\rho_{0}\otimes\tau_{k}\simeq\tau_{k}\simeq\rho_{1}\otimes\tau_{k},\qquad k\in\mathbb{Z},

\rho_{2}\otimes\tau_{k}\simeq\tau_{\ell-k}\simeq\rho_{3}\otimes\tau_{k},\qquad k\in\mathbb{Z}.

Consider the standard embedding

D_{2\ell}\subset\operatorname{SO}(3)\subset\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{C})
and the induced representation
V
.
In this embedding
d
acts as rotation in the
\left\langle e_{1},e_{2}\right\rangle
-plane, and
s
acts as inversion in the
\left\langle e_{2},e_{3}\right\rangle
-plane:

d\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}A&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad s\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}B&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad A=\begin{pmatrix}a&-b\\
b&a\end{pmatrix},\qquad B=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix},

where
z=a+bi=e^{\pi i/{\ell}}
.
Note that in the third coordinate we get the representation
\rho_{1}
.
Taking the matrix
M=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\
-i&i\end{smallmatrix}\right)
(its columns are the eigenvectors of
A
), we can see that

M^{-1}AM=\begin{pmatrix}z&0\\
0&z^{-1}\end{pmatrix},\qquad M^{-1}BM=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}.

This implies that the representation
(A,B)
is isomorphic to
\tau_{1}
and
V\simeq\rho_{1}\oplus\tau_{1}
.

Remark 3.2
.

For
\ell=1
, we have
D_{2\ell}=\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
and the standard embedding
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}\subset\operatorname{SO}(3)
is given by

d=(1,0)\mapsto\operatorname{diag}(-1,-1,1),\qquad s=(0,1)\mapsto\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1).

Based on these facts, we can now describe the McKay quiver for
D_{2\ell}
and its potential:

Lemma 3.3
.

The McKay quiver has the following form, where blue arrows correspond to
\rho_{1}
and black arrows correspond to
\tau_{1}
.

For
\ell\geq 2
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho_{0}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau_{\ell-1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho_{3}}

For
\ell=1
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho_{0}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho_{3}}

The potential is a linear combination of cubic terms involving one blue arrow and two black arrows.

Proof.

Using the previous lemma, we find the decompositions

\rho_{0}\otimes V=\rho_{0}\otimes(\rho_{1}\oplus\tau_{1})\simeq\rho_{1}\oplus\tau_{1},

\rho_{1}\otimes V=\rho_{1}\otimes(\rho_{1}\oplus\tau_{1})\simeq\rho_{0}\oplus\tau_{1},

\rho_{2}\otimes V=\rho_{2}\otimes(\rho_{1}\oplus\tau_{1})\simeq\rho_{3}\oplus\tau_{\ell-1},

\rho_{3}\otimes V=\rho_{3}\otimes(\rho_{1}\oplus\tau_{1})\simeq\rho_{2}\oplus\tau_{\ell-1},

\tau_{k}\otimes V=\tau_{k}\otimes(\rho_{1}\oplus\tau_{1})\simeq\tau_{k}\oplus(\tau_{k-1}\oplus\tau_{k+1})

for
k=2,\ldots,\ell-2
, and

\tau_{1}\otimes V=\tau_{1}\otimes(\rho_{1}\otimes\tau_{1})\simeq\tau_{1}\oplus(\rho_{0}\oplus\rho_{1}\oplus\tau_{2}),

\tau_{\ell-1}\otimes V=\tau_{\ell-1}\otimes(\rho_{1}\oplus\tau_{1})\simeq\tau_{\ell-1}\oplus(\tau_{\ell-2}\oplus\rho_{2}\oplus\rho_{3}).

This yields the above McKay quivers.
To describe the potential, we decompose
V^{\otimes 3}
into irreducible representations and single out the contributions of the trivial representation
\rho_{0}
. Namely, we find

V^{\otimes 3}\simeq\rho_{1}^{\otimes 3}\oplus(\rho_{1}^{\otimes 2}\otimes\tau_{1})^{\oplus 3}\oplus(\rho_{1}\otimes\tau_{1}^{\otimes 2})^{\oplus 3}\oplus\tau_{1}^{\otimes 3},

and

\rho_{1}^{\otimes 3}\simeq\rho_{1},\qquad\rho_{1}^{\otimes 2}\otimes\tau_{1}\simeq\tau_{1},

\rho_{1}\otimes\tau_{1}^{\otimes 2}\simeq\rho_{1}\otimes(\rho_{0}\oplus\rho_{1}\oplus\tau_{2})\simeq\rho_{1}\oplus\rho_{0}\oplus\tau_{2},

\tau_{1}^{\otimes 3}\simeq\tau_{1}\otimes(\rho_{0}\oplus\rho_{1}\oplus\tau_{2})\simeq\tau_{1}^{\oplus 3}\oplus\tau_{3}.

We analyse the determinant map
\operatorname{det}\colon V^{\otimes 3}\to\bigwedge^{3}V
with respect to this direct sum decomposition. Since, obviously,
\bigwedge^{3}V\simeq\rho_{0}
, and
\rho_{1}\otimes\tau_{1}^{\otimes 2}
is the only summand containing a copy of
\rho_{0}
, the determinant map
factors through the projection
V^{\otimes 3}\rightarrow(\rho_{1}\otimes\tau_{1}^{\otimes 2})^{\oplus 3}
. Note that this holds true independent of
\ell
. By definition of the potential, this implies that the potential involves only triangles of arrows involving one blue arrow (corresponding to
\rho_{1}
) and two black arrows (corresponding to
\tau_{1}
).
∎

Remark 3.4
.

For odd dihedral groups
D_{2\ell+1}
similar computations can be performed, leading to the McKay quiver

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho_{0}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau_{\ell}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho_{1}}

and potential as before. It admits a cut consisting of blue arrows, but the resulting quiver with relations involves a square of the loop at
\tau_{\ell}
. Therefore, it does not admit a second cut, and our method for factoring the motivic generating function does not apply.

3.2.
Generating series via double dimensional reduction

Based on the dimensional reduction method described in §
2.4
we can now describe the motivic generating series in terms of the representation theory of quivers of extended Dynkin types.
Let
(Q,W)
be the McKay quiver with potential described in Lemma
3.3
.
There is a cut
I\subset Q_{1}
consisting of all black arrows going from right to left and a cut
I^{\prime}\subset Q_{1}
consisting of all blue arrows.
Let
Q^{\prime}=Q\backslash I
and

J_{I}=\mathbb{C}Q^{\prime}/(\partial W/\partial a\rcol a\in I)
.
Then the motivic generating series (
13
) has the form

(18)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(t)=\sum_{d}(-q^{\frac{1}{2}})^{s(d,d)}\frac{[R(J_{I},d)]}{[G_{d}]}t^{d},

(19)

\displaystyle s(d,d)=\chi_{Q}(d,d)+2\sum_{(a\colon i\to j)\in I}d_{i}d_{j}=\chi_{C_{2}\sqcup C_{2}}(d,d),

where
C_{2}\sqcup C_{2}
is the restriction of
Q
to the vertices
\rho_{i}
(and blue arrows between them).
It is indeed just a union of two cyclic quivers.

Consider the quiver
Q^{\prime\prime}=Q\backslash(I\cup I^{\prime})
, which is an extended Dynkin quiver of type
\hat{D}_{\ell+2}
for
\ell\geq 2
(respectively of type
\hat{D}_{3}=\hat{A}_{3}
for
\ell=1
).
The projection
\pi\colon R(J_{I},d)\to R(Q^{\prime\prime},d)
has the fiber over

M\in R(Q^{\prime\prime},d)
encoded by the blue arrows.
More precisely, this fiber is equal to
\operatorname{Hom}(M,\Sigma M)
, where

(20)

\Sigma\colon Q^{\prime\prime}\to Q^{\prime\prime}

is the involution on
Q^{\prime\prime}
induced by the blue arrows.
Applying (
15
),
we can rewrite the motivic generating series as a sum over representations of
Q^{\prime\prime}
:

Lemma 3.5
.

We have

\mathcal{A}(t)=\sum_{d\in\mathbb{N}Q_{0}}\sum_{\operatorname{\mathbf{dim}}M=d}\frac{(-q^{\frac{1}{2}})^{s(d,d)+2h^{0}(M,\Sigma M)}}{[\operatorname{Aut}M]}t^{d}\\
=\sum_{m\colon S\to\mathbb{N}}\frac{(-q^{\frac{1}{2}})^{-\sum_{X,Y}m_{X}m_{Y}\sigma(X,Y)}}{\prod_{X}(q^{-1})_{m_{X}}}t^{\sum_{X}m_{X}\operatorname{\mathbf{dim}}X},

where

\sigma(M,N)=2h^{0}(M,N)-2h^{0}(M,\Sigma N)-s(M,N),

and
S
denotes the set of isomorphism classes of indecomposable
Q^{\prime\prime}
-representations.

Remark 3.6
.

The above quiver with potential
(Q,W)
is an example of a translation potential quiver studied in
[
10
]
.
Given a quiver
Q
with an automorphism
\phi\colon Q\to Q
,
we define a twisted double quiver
Q^{\phi}
by adding arrows
a^{*}\colon\phi j\to i
, for all arrows
a\colon i\to j
in
Q
.
We define a quiver
\tilde{Q}^{\phi}
by adding, furthermore, arrows
\ell_{i}\colon i\to\phi i
, for all vertices
i
in
Q
.
The quiver
\tilde{Q}^{\phi}
is equipped with a potential consisting of cycles of the form

i\xrightarrow{\ell_{i}}\phi i\xrightarrow{\phi a}\phi j\xrightarrow{a^{*}}i,\qquad i\xrightarrow{a}j\xrightarrow{\ell_{j}}\phi j\xrightarrow{a^{*}}i

for arrows
a\colon i\to j
in
Q
.
Applying this construction to the quiver
Q^{\prime\prime}
with the automorphism
\Sigma
, we obtain the McKay quiver
Q

with the potential from Lemma
3.3
.

4.
Computation of
\sigma

To describe the motivic generating series
\mathcal{A}(t)

using its expression from Lemma
3.5
, it is necessary to compute the interaction form
\sigma
(or its symmetrization) on indecomposable representations of the quiver
Q^{\prime\prime}
of type
\hat{D}_{\ell+2}
.
To simplify notation, we will use
r=\ell+2\geq 3

and we will index the vertices of the quiver
Q^{\prime\prime}
of type
\hat{D}_{r}
as

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces r-1}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 2}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 3}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces r-3}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces r-2}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces r}

for
r\geq 4
and

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 2}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 3}

for
r=3
(this is a quiver of type
\hat{D}_{3}=\hat{A}_{3}
).
We first recall standard facts on the representation theory of extended Dynkin quivers of type
\hat{D}_{r}
from
[
3
, Section 6]
.

4.1.
Root system
\hat{D}_{r}

The root system of finite type
D_{r}
is constructed as follows.
Consider a vector space with a basis
(\varepsilon_{1},\dots,\varepsilon_{r})
and a scalar product
(\varepsilon_{i},\varepsilon_{j})=\delta_{ij}
.
Then the set of (positive) roots is

\Delta^{\mathrm{f}}_{+}=\left\{\left.\varepsilon_{i}\pm\varepsilon_{j}\ \right|i<j\right\}

and
the simple roots are
\alpha_{i}=\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{i+1}
for
1\leq i<r
and
\alpha_{r}=\varepsilon_{r-1}+\varepsilon_{r}
.
The maximal root is
\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2}
.
To construct the root system of type
\hat{D}_{r}
, we consider an additional simple root
\alpha_{0}
so that the indivisible imaginary root has the form

(21)

\delta=\alpha_{0}+\alpha_{1}+2(\alpha_{2}+\dots+\alpha_{r-2})+\alpha_{r-1}+\alpha_{r}.

The set of positive real roots of type
\hat{D}_{r}
is

(22)

\Delta^{\mathrm{re}}_{+}=\Delta^{\mathrm{f}}_{+}\cup\left\{\left.\Delta^{\mathrm{f}}+n\delta\ \right|n\geq 1\right\},\qquad\Delta^{\mathrm{f}}=\Delta^{\mathrm{f}}_{+}\cup(-\Delta^{\mathrm{f}}_{+}).

The set of positive imaginary roots is
\Delta^{\mathrm{im}}_{+}=\left\{\left.n\delta\ \right|n\geq 1\right\}
.

In what follows we will identify standard basis vectors
e_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{Q^{\prime\prime}_{0}}
with the simple roots
\alpha_{i}

and we will identify a vector
d\in\mathbb{Z}^{Q^{\prime\prime}_{0}}
with the vector
\sum_{i=0}^{r}d_{i}\alpha_{i}
in the root lattice.
Note that dimension vectors of indecomposable representations of
Q^{\prime\prime}
can be identified with the positive roots of the root system of type
\hat{D}_{r}
.
We define

(23)

\rho=e_{0}+e_{1}+\dots+e_{r}.

4.2.
AR translation

Recall that, for any acyclic quiver
Q
,
the AR translation

(24)

\tau\colon D^{b}(\operatorname{Rep}Q)\to D^{b}(\operatorname{Rep}Q)

is given by
\tau=\nu[-1]
,
where
\nu(M)=\operatorname{RHom}(M,A)^{\vee}
,
A=\mathbb{C}Q
.
We will also denote by
\tau
the induced map on the Grothendieck group
K(Q)\simeq\mathbb{Z}^{Q_{0}}
.
For any vertex
i\in Q_{0}
, let
P_{i}=Ae_{i}

be the corresponding indecomposable projective (left)
A
-module and
I_{i}=(e_{i}A)^{\vee}
be the corresponding indecomposable injective (left)
A
-module.
Then
\nu(P_{i})=I_{i}
.

Recall that the involution
\Sigma
of the quiver
Q^{\prime\prime}

defined in (
20
)
acts on the vertices by

(25)

\Sigma(0)=1,\qquad\Sigma(r-1)=r,\qquad\Sigma(i)=i,\quad 2\leq i\leq r-2.

It induces a functor

(26)

\Sigma\colon\operatorname{Rep}Q^{\prime\prime}\to\operatorname{Rep}Q^{\prime\prime}

as well as an automorphism of the Grothendieck group

K(Q^{\prime\prime})
.
We have
\nu\Sigma P_{i}=I_{i}=\Sigma\nu P_{i}
for
2\leq i\leq r-2
,

\nu\Sigma P_{0}=I_{1}=\Sigma\nu P_{0}
and similarly for
P_{1},P_{r-1}
,
P_{r}
.
This implies that

\tau\Sigma=\Sigma\tau.

Remark 4.1
.

The fact that
\nu=\tau[1]
is the Serre functor implies that
\tau
commutes with any exact auto-equivalence of
D^{b}(\operatorname{Rep}Q)
.

Lemma 4.2
.

The map
\tau
on
\mathbb{Z}^{Q^{\prime\prime}_{0}}
is given by (with
\rho=\sum_{i=0}^{r}e_{i}
)

e_{2}\mapsto e_{3}\mapsto\dots\mapsto e_{r-2}\mapsto\rho\mapsto e_{2},

e_{r-1}\mapsto-\rho+e_{r},\quad e_{r}\mapsto-\rho+e_{r-1},\quad e_{0}\mapsto e_{1}+e_{2},\quad e_{1}\mapsto e_{0}+e_{2}.

Proof.

For
2\leq k\leq r-2
, we have
\tau(P_{k})=\tau(\sum_{i\geq k}e_{i})=-\sum_{i\leq k}e_{i}
.
Therefore
\tau(e_{k})=e_{k+1}
for
2\leq k<r-2
.
On the other hand
\tau(e_{r-2})=-(I_{r-2}-I_{r-1}-I_{r})=\rho
.

We have
\tau(\rho-e_{1})=\tau(P_{0})=-I_{0}=-e_{0}
.
Similarly
\tau(\rho-e_{0})=-e_{1}
.
On the other hand
\tau(\rho-e_{0}-e_{1})=\tau(P_{2})=-(e_{0}+e_{1}+e_{2})
.
Therefore
\tau(\rho)=e_{2}
,
\tau(e_{0})=e_{1}+e_{2}
,
\tau(e_{1})=e_{0}+e_{2}
.
Finally,
\tau(e_{r-1})=-I_{r-1}=-\rho+e_{r}
.
Similarly
\tau(e_{r})=-\rho+e_{r-1}
.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

Note that

\tau(e_{0}-e_{1})=e_{1}-e_{0},\qquad\tau(e_{r-1}-e_{r})=e_{r}-e_{r-1}.

Note also that
\delta=\rho+e_{2}+\dots+e_{r-2}
and
\tau(\delta)=\delta
.

4.3.
Properties of indecomposable representations

The preprojective indecomposables of
Q^{\prime\prime}
are of the form
\tau^{-k}P_{i}
, where

k\geq 0
,
i
is a vertex of
Q^{\prime\prime}
and

\tau
is the AR translation.
Similarly, the preinjective indecomposables are of the form
\tau^{k}I_{i}
.
The remaining indecomposables belong to tubes, namely,
an infinite family of homogeneous tubes
\mathcal{R}(\lambda)
(with the trivial action of
\tau
),
two tubes
\mathcal{R}_{1},\mathcal{R}_{2}
of width
2

(with the action of
\tau
having order
2
), and a tube
\mathcal{R}_{3}
of width
r-2

(respectively, no such tube in the case
r=3
).
We have the following dimension vectors of regular indecomposable representations:

\mathcal{R}(\lambda)
:

\operatorname{\mathbf{dim}}X=\delta
,

\mathcal{R}_{1}
:

\operatorname{\mathbf{dim}}X_{1}=e_{0}+e_{2}+\ldots+e_{r-2}+e_{r-1}
,
\operatorname{\mathbf{dim}}X_{2}=e_{1}+e_{2}+\ldots+e_{r-2}+e_{r}
,

\mathcal{R}_{2}
:

\operatorname{\mathbf{dim}}X_{1}=e_{0}+e_{2}+\ldots+e_{r-2}+e_{r}
,
\operatorname{\mathbf{dim}}X_{2}=e_{1}+e_{2}+\ldots+e_{r-2}+e_{r-1}
,

\mathcal{R}_{3}
:

\operatorname{\mathbf{dim}}X_{1}=e_{2}
,

\operatorname{\mathbf{dim}}X_{2}=e_{3}
,
\ldots
,
\operatorname{\mathbf{dim}}X_{r-3}=e_{r-2}
,
\operatorname{\mathbf{dim}}X_{r-2}=\rho=\sum_{i=0}^{r}e_{i}
.

Inspection of the above dimension vectors of the regular indecomposables shows that
\Sigma
acts as identity on
\mathcal{R}(\lambda)
and
\mathcal{R}_{3}
, and acts as
\tau
on
\mathcal{R}_{1},\mathcal{R}_{2}
.

As before, we define
h^{i}(M,N)=\dim\operatorname{Ext}^{i}(M,N)
.
Recall from Lemma
3.5
that

(27)

\sigma(M,N)=2h^{0}(M,N)-2h^{0}(M,\Sigma N)-s(M,N).

Lemma 4.4
.

For all representations
M
,
N
of
Q^{\prime\prime}

we have

\sigma(M,N)=h^{0}(M,N)+h^{1}(M,N)-h^{0}(M,\Sigma N)-h^{1}(M,\Sigma N).

In particular,
\sigma(\tau M,\tau N)=\sigma(M,N)
.

Proof.

Using the definitions of the Euler forms
\chi
of
Q^{\prime\prime}
and
s
of
C_{2}\sqcup C_{2}
, we can directly verify that

\chi(d,e)-\chi(d,\Sigma e)=s(d,e).

This implies the statement.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

For all representations
M
,
N
of
Q^{\prime\prime}

we have

(1)

\sigma(M,\Sigma N)=\sigma(\Sigma M,N)=-\sigma(M,N)
.

(2)

\sigma(M,N)=\sigma(N,\tau M)
.

(3)

\sigma(M,N)+\sigma(N,M)=\sigma(M,N\oplus\tau N)
.

Proof.

The first statement follows immediately from Lemma
4.4
.
Using the Auslander-Reiten formula
h^{1}(M,N)=h^{0}(N,\tau M)
, we obtain

\sigma(M,N)=h^{0}(M,N)+h^{0}(N,\tau M)-h^{0}(M,\Sigma N)-h^{0}(N,\Sigma\tau M)

Therefore

\sigma(N,\tau M)=h^{0}(N,\tau M)+h^{0}(\tau M,\tau N)-h^{0}(N,\Sigma\tau M)-h^{0}(\tau M,\Sigma\tau N)

and the second statement follows.
The third statement is a direct consequence of the second statement.
∎

Corollary 4.6
.

If
\Sigma M\simeq M
or
\Sigma M\simeq\tau M
, then
\sigma(M,N)+\sigma(N,M)=0
.

Proof.

If
\Sigma M\simeq M
, then
\sigma(M,N)=\sigma(\Sigma M,N)=-\sigma(M,N)
, hence
\sigma(M,N)=0
.
Similarly
\sigma(N,M)=0
.
If
\sigma M\simeq\tau M
, then
\sigma(M,N)=\sigma(N,\Sigma M)=-\sigma(N,M)
,
hence
\sigma(M,N)+\sigma(N,M)=0
.
∎

These facts allow us to compute the symmetrization of
\sigma
on arbitrary pairs of indecomposables:

Lemma 4.7
.

If
M,N
are indecomposable representations of
Q^{\prime\prime}
, then

\sigma(M,N)+\sigma(N,M)\not=0
only when
M
is in the
\tau
-orbit of
P_{i}
or
I_{i}
for
i=0,1,r-1,r
, and
N=M
or
N=\Sigma M
. In this case, we have

\sigma(M,M)=1,\qquad\sigma(M,\Sigma M)=\sigma(\Sigma M,M)=-1.

Proof.

According to our analysis of indecomposables,
\Sigma
acts as identity or as
\tau
on all regular indecomposable representations.
It also acts as identity on representations of the form
\tau^{-k}P_{i},\tau^{k}I_{i}
for all
k\geq 0
,
2\leq i\leq r-2
.
Therefore by the above corollary, we can assume that
M,N
are in the
\tau
-orbits of
P_{i}
or
I_{i}
for
i=0,1,r-1,r
.
We only treat the case where
M
is preprojective, the other case then follows by duality.
By
\tau
-invariance of
\sigma
, we can assume that
M=P_{i}
.
Then we obtain from Lemma
4.4

(28)

\sigma(P_{i},N)=h^{0}(P_{i},N)-h^{0}(P_{i},\Sigma N)=d_{i}-d_{\Sigma i},

where
d
is the dimension vector of
N
.
This functional on dimension vectors alternates under
\tau

by Remark
4.3
, thus assumes the value
0
on a dimension vector of the form
d+\tau d
.
If
N
is not projective, then (
28
) applies to
N\oplus\tau N
, hence
\sigma(P_{i},N\oplus\tau N)=0

and we conclude that
\sigma(P_{i},N)+\sigma(N,P_{i})=0

by Lemma
4.5
.
Let
N=P_{j}
be projective.
If
2\leq j\leq r-2
, then
\Sigma P_{j}=P_{j}
and we conclude that

\sigma(P_{i},P_{j})+\sigma(P_{j},P_{i})=0
by Corollary

4.6
.
Therefore we can assume that
j=0,1,r-1,r
.

If
j\neq i,\Sigma i
, then

\sigma(P_{i},P_{j})=0
by (
28
) and similarly
\sigma(P_{j},P_{i})=0
.

If
j=i
, then
\sigma(P_{i},P_{i})=1
by (
28
).

If
j=\Sigma i
, then
\sigma(P_{i},P_{\Sigma i})=-1=\sigma(P_{\Sigma i},P_{i})
by (
28
).
∎

4.4.
Characterization of some
\tau
-orbits

In this section we will study dimension vectors of indecomposable representations in the above
\tau
-orbits.

Lemma 4.8
.

We have (in the Grothendieck group of
Q^{\prime\prime}
)

\Sigma\tau(I_{k})=e_{2}+I_{k},\qquad k=0,1,r-1,r.

\tau(I_{k})=e_{2}+I_{k+1},\qquad 2\leq k<r-2.

\tau(I_{r-2})=e_{2}+\rho+I_{r-2}.

Proof.

We have
\tau(I_{0})=\tau(e_{0})=e_{1}+e_{2}
, hence
\Sigma\tau(I_{0})=e_{0}+e_{2}=e_{2}+I_{0}
.
We have

\tau(I_{r})=\tau(\rho-e_{r-1})=e_{2}+\rho-e_{r}=e_{2}+I_{r-1},

hence
\Sigma\tau I_{r}=e_{2}+I_{r}
.
For
2\leq k<r-2
, we have

\tau(I_{k})=\tau\bigg(\sum_{i\leq k}e_{i}\bigg)=e_{2}+\sum_{i\leq k+1}e_{i}=e_{2}+I_{k+1}.

Finally,

\tau(I_{r-2})=\tau(\rho-e_{r-1}-e_{r})=e_{2}+2\rho-e_{r}-e_{r-1}=e_{2}+\rho+I_{r-2}
.
∎

.

\tau

p(d)=0
.

5.
Motivic generating series

Recall from Lemma
3.5
that

(29)

\mathcal{A}(t)=\sum_{m\colon S\to\mathbb{N}}\frac{(-q^{\frac{1}{2}})^{-\sum_{X,Y}m_{X}m_{Y}\sigma(X,Y)}}{\prod_{X}(q^{-1})_{m_{X}}}t^{\sum_{X}m_{X}\operatorname{\mathbf{dim}}X}

where
S
is the set of isomorphism classes of indecomposable representations of the quiver
Q^{\prime\prime}

of type
\hat{D}_{\ell+2}

and
\sigma
is the interaction form computed in §
4
.
As in Lemma
4.9
, we define

(30)

p(d)=d_{0}+d_{1}+d_{\ell+1}+d_{\ell+2}\pmod{2}.

We will present an explicit formula for the motivic generating series
\mathcal{A}(t)
in terms of the root system of type
\hat{D}_{\ell+2}
.

Theorem 5.1
.

We have

\mathcal{A}(t)=\operatorname{Exp}\left(\frac{\sum_{d}\Omega_{d}(q)t^{d}}{q-1}\right),

where
\Omega_{d}(q)
is

(1)

q
if
d\in\Delta^{\mathrm{re}}_{+}
with
p(d)=0
or
d=d^{\prime}+\Sigma d^{\prime}
for some
d^{\prime}\in\Delta^{\mathrm{re}}_{+}
with
p(d^{\prime})=1
.

(2)

-q^{\frac{1}{2}}
if
d\in\Delta_{+}^{\mathrm{re}}
with
p(d)=1
.

(3)

q(q+\ell+2)
if
d\in\Delta_{+}^{\mathrm{im}}
.

(4)

zero otherwise.

Remark 5.2
.

Let
\chi
be the Euler form of
Q^{\prime\prime}
.
As
Q^{\prime\prime}
is of affine type, a vector
d\in\mathbb{Z}^{Q^{\prime\prime}_{0}}
is a real root if and only if
\chi(d,d)=1

and it is an imaginary root if and only if
\chi(d,d)=0

(see e.g.
[
5
, Prop. 1.6]
).
In particular, if
d=d^{\prime}+\Sigma d^{\prime}
, for some
d^{\prime}\in\Delta_{+}^{\mathrm{re}}
,
then
\chi(d,d)=2\chi(d^{\prime},d^{\prime})+2\chi(d^{\prime},\Sigma d^{\prime})
is even,
hence
d
is not a real root.
If we also have
p(d^{\prime})=1
, then
d^{\prime}
is the dimension vector of
\tau^{-k}P_{i}
or
\tau^{k}I_{i}
for some
i=0,1,\ell+1,\ell+2
and
k\geq 0
.
Assuming that
d^{\prime}=\operatorname{\mathbf{dim}}\tau^{-k}P_{i}
, we have

\chi(d^{\prime},d^{\prime})=\chi(P_{i},P_{i})=1
and similarly
\chi(d^{\prime},\Sigma d^{\prime})=0
.
Therefore
\chi(d,d)=2
and
d
is not a root.

Remark 5.3

(The case of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
)
.

For the root system of type
\hat{A}_{3}

\Delta^{\mathrm{f}}_{+}=\left\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3},\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2},\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{3},\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}\right\}

\left\{\alpha_{0},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{0}+\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3},\alpha_{0}+\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}\right\}+\mathbb{N}\delta

\Sigma

This implies that
\Omega_{d}(q)=q

We also have

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

As in the previous section, we will use
r=\ell+2
.
We have seen earlier that (the symmetrization of)
\sigma(X,Y)
can be nonzero only if
X,Y
are in the
\tau
-orbits of
P_{i},I_{i}
for
i=0,1,r-1,r
.
The contribution of every indecomposable
X
not of this form is

\sum_{m\geq 0}\frac{1}{(q^{-1})_{m}}t^{m\operatorname{\mathbf{dim}}X}=\operatorname{Exp}\left(\frac{qt^{\operatorname{\mathbf{dim}}X}}{q-1}\right).

Note that the family of regular representations having dimension vector
d=k\delta
(imaginary root) has the motivic class
q+r
. Therefore their contribution is (we take the plethystic power)

\operatorname{Exp}\left(\frac{qt^{d}}{q-1}\right)^{q+r}=\operatorname{Exp}\left(\frac{q(q+r)t^{d}}{q-1}\right).

According to §
4
,
an indecomposable representation
M
having dimension vector
d
with
p(d)=1
has a nonzero interaction only with itself and a representation
\Sigma M
, having dimension vector
\Sigma d
.
Moreover,
\sigma(M,M)=1=-\sigma(M,\Sigma M)
.
By Theorem
2.2
,
the corresponding contribution to the generating series is equal to

F(t^{d},t^{\Sigma d})=\operatorname{Exp}\left(\frac{qt^{d+\Sigma d}-q^{\frac{1}{2}}(t^{d}+t^{\Sigma d})}{q-1}\right).

Summarizing, we obtain

\mathcal{A}(t)=\prod_{{\begin{subarray}{c}d\in\Delta^{\mathrm{re}}_{+}\\
p(d)=0\end{subarray}}}\operatorname{Exp}\left(\frac{qt^{d}}{q-1}\right)\cdot\prod_{d\in\Delta^{\mathrm{im}}_{+}}\operatorname{Exp}\left(\frac{q(q+r)t^{d}}{q-1}\right)\times\\
\times\prod_{{\begin{subarray}{c}d\in\Delta^{\mathrm{re}}_{+}/\Sigma\\
p(d)=1\end{subarray}}}\operatorname{Exp}\left(\frac{qt^{d+\Sigma d}-q^{\frac{1}{2}}(t^{d}+t^{\Sigma d})}{q-1}\right).

This finishes the proof of Theorem
5.1
.

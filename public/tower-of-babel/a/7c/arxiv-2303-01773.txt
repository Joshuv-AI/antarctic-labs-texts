Comparative study of magnetocaloric properties for Gd 3+ compounds with different frustrated lattice geometries Preprint: APS/123-QED
EliseAnne C. Koskelo, Paromita Mukherjee, Cheng Liu, Alice C. Sackville Hamilton, Harapan S. Ong, Claudio Castelnovo, Siân E. Dutton, M. E. Zhitomirsky

Abstract
As materials with suppressed ordering temperatures and enhanced ground state entropies, frustrated magnetic oxides are ideal candidates for cryogenic magnetocaloric refrigeration. While previous materials design has focused on tuning the magnetic moments, their interactions, and density of moments on the lattice, there has been relatively little attention to frustrated lattices. Prior theoretical work has shown that the magnetocaloric cooling rate at the saturation field is proportional to a macroscopic number of soft mode excitations that arise due to the classical ground state degeneracy. The number of these modes is directly determined by the geometry of the frustrating lattice. For corner-sharing geometries, the pyrochlore has 50% more modes than the garnet and kagome lattices, whereas the edge-sharing fcc has only a subextensive number of soft modes. Here, we study the role of soft modes in the magnetocaloric effect of four large-spin Gd 3+ ( L = 0 L=0 , J = S = 7 / 2 J=S=7/2 ) Heisenberg antiferromagnets on a kagome, garnet, pyrochlore, and fcc lattice. By comparing measurements of the magnetic entropy change Δ ​ S m \Delta S_{m} of these materials at fields up to 9 9 T with predictions using mean-field theory and Monte Carlo simulations, we are able to understand the relative importance of spin correlations and quantization effects. We observe that tuning the value of the nearest neighbor coupling has a more dominant contribution to the magnetocaloric entropy change in the liquid-He cooling regime ( 2 2 - 20 20 K), rather than tuning the number of soft mode excitations. Our results inform future materials design in terms of dimensionality, degree of magnetic frustration, and lattice geometry.

Introduction

The ever-increasing need for cooling in modern technologies, as well as the scarcity of helium, has motivated the search for sustainable cooling alternatives, including magnetocaloric materials
Moya and Mathur 2020

Research interests shifted later on from dilute paramagnetic salts and superparamagnets to dense magnetic lattices magnets, when it was shown that frustration can enable an enhanced magnetocaloric effect via: 1) a suppressed ordering temperature at the same magnetic density and 2) a large ground state entropy/soft magnon modes

Soft Modes Model: Validity in Liquid He Regime

In prior work
Zhitomirsky 2003
, one of us predicted a strongly enhanced magnetocaloric
effect for geometrically frustrated magnets with high classical degeneracy in the ground state. The work also included classical Monte Carlo simulations, generally valid for large-
S
magnetic materials, for Heisenberg antiferromagnets with three typical frustrated geometries based on corner-sharing plaquettes: the two-dimensional kagome lattice (a network of corner-sharing triangles), the garnet lattice (its three-dimensional analog), and the pyrochlore lattice (a network of corner-sharing tetrahedra), see Figure
1

Here we revisit these geometries and compare them with an
fcc

The spin Hamiltonian consists of a nearest neighbor (
nn
) superexchange term, assumed to be uniform across all
nn

\hat{\mathcal{H}}=J_{1}\sum_{\braket{ij}}\mathbf{S}_{i}\cdot\mathbf{S}_{j}-\mathbf{H}\cdot\sum_{i}\mathbf{S}_{i}\,,

(1)

where
J_{1}
is the
nn

Dipolar interactions are also present in the system but we shall disregard them in our simulations. Considering the system parameters reported later in Table
2
, we expect this to be a reasonable approximation for the three corner sharing lattices. In the
fcc
system, superexchange and dipolar interactions at nearest-neighbour distance are of similar strength; however, they are much smaller than the temperature regime of interest in our study and therefore truncating them to nearest-neighbour distance is a valid approximation.

In the low-temperature cooperative paramagnetic state, a condensation of a macroscopic number

The magnetocaloric rate,
(\partial S_{m}/\partial H)_{T}
, at the saturation field is predicted to follow the scaling relation:

\left(\frac{\partial S_{m}}{\partial H}\right)_{T,N4}\propto-\frac{N_{4}}{\sqrt{J_{1}T}},\hskip 12.0pt\textrm{at }H=H_{\textrm{sat}}\,.

(2)

This is in addition to the conventional contribution to
(\partial S_{m}/\partial H)_{T}
from the paramagnetic (non-frustrated) ordinary dispersive modes (
N_{2}
) of the system,

\left(\frac{\partial S_{m}}{\partial H}\right)_{T,N2}\propto-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\frac{1}{(H-H_{\rm sat})S+\epsilon(\mathbf{k})}\,,

(3)

where the excitation energies
\epsilon(\mathbf{k})
are nonnegative and vanish for
k
corresponding to the propagation vectors of degenerate classical ground states in zero field
Zhitomirsky 2003
.

For non-frustrated, three-dimensional Heisenberg magnets above the ordering temperature,
\left(\frac{\partial S_{m}}{\partial H}\right)_{T,N2}
is the only contribution and there is no enhancement in the magnetocaloric cooling rate, which is a temperature-independent constant for
H=H_{\rm sat}

Zhitomirsky 2003
.
In frustrated systems where
N_{4}
is macroscopic (i.e.,
N_{4}\propto N
, where
N
is the number of lattice sites), one expects
\left(\frac{\partial S_{m}}{\partial H}\right)_{T,N4}
to dominate at low enough temperatures (where it grows as
\sim 1/\sqrt{T}
). However, when
N_{4}\propto N^{\alpha}
with
0<\alpha<1
, as is the case for
fcc
Ba
2
GdSbO
6
(discussed below), there will always be a system size beyond which
\left(\frac{\partial S_{m}}{\partial H}\right)_{T,N2}
dominates, for any given temperature:
N\gtrsim 1/T^{1/[2(1-\alpha)]}
.

Using classical spin-wave calculations in the saturated (collinear) state, the number of soft modes in the pyrochlore lattice was found to scale with the number of lattice sites,
N_{4}\propto N
, Table
1

Zhitomirsky 2003
. The kagome and garnet lattices are predicted to have
2/3
as many soft modes as the pyrochlore. Thus, it could be expected that the pyrochlore lattice may have a greater magnetocaloric effect than a comparable garnet or kagome lattice. Indeed prior work has shown that the pyrochlore Gd
2
Ti
2
O
7

5

The number of soft modes for an
fcc
lattice has not been reported, but is expected to be lower than that for the corner-sharing geometries, as its zero energy modes correspond to lines (rather than surfaces) in the Brillouin zone

\epsilon_{\mathbf{q}}=2\left(1+\cos q_{x}\cos q_{y}+\cos q_{x}\cos q_{z}+\cos q_{y}\cos q_{z}\right)\,.

(4)

The zeros of this equation correspond to soft modes and are given by
\mathbf{q}=(\pi,q,0)
and the “cubic related lines”

The soft modes theory described here is valid for classical (large-spin) Heisenberg magnets

Magnetocaloric Effect

\left(\frac{\partial S_{m}}{\partial H}\right)_{T}=\left(\frac{\partial M}{\partial T}\right)_{H}

(7)

The implication of this experimental validation is that while the order of soft modes of frustrating lattices can be accurately captured in experiment, soft modes are not the leading contribution in the magnetocaloric performance of a frustrated magnet in the liquid He regime. The strategy of solely reducing the exchange by increasing the distance between magnetic ions, as in the dilute magnetic salts FAA and CMN, is not effective. V
Conclusion

This work examined the role of lattice geometry in maximizing the magnetocaloric effect via soft mode spin excitations. Four representative Heisenberg (3D, Gd-based) spin systems were investigated including the three corner-sharing garnet (Gd
3
Ga
5
O
12
), pyrochlore (Gd
2
Sn
2
O
7
), kagome lattices (Gd
3
Mg
2
Sb
3
O
14
), and the edge-sharing
fcc
lattice (Ba
2
GdSbO
6
). For the liquid-He temperature range investigated (
2-20
K), magnetic entropy change measurements indicate that the smaller superexchange of the
fcc
and garnet lattices allows for better magnetocaloric performance than the strongly-coupled pyrochlore analog, despite its larger number of soft modes. Our results show that a paramagnetic response dominates above geometric frustration in enhancing the magnetocaloric effect. However, the contribution to the magnetocaloric effect in systems with an extensive number of frustrated magnetic soft modes is expected to take over at lower temperatures, thus providing an additional channel for maximizing the magnetocaloric effect in highly frustrated magnetic lattices. Future magnetocaloric materials design should focus on both the superexchange and magnetic lattice to optimize the trade off between spin polarizability and frustrated soft mode enhancement.

Appendix A
Saturation field of the
fcc
model

One can use frustrated block decomposition to obtain the saturation field
H_{\textrm{sat}}
for the nearest neighbor

fcc
antiferrromagnet, above which spins become fully aligned with the applied field. We start with the spin Hamiltonian

{\cal H}=J_{1}\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}\vec{S}_{i}\cdot\vec{S}_{j}-H\sum_{i}S_{i}^{z}\,,

(8)

where the

An

\alpha

\vec{L}_{\alpha}=\sum_{i\in\alpha}\vec{S}_{i}
, and using the fact that every site belongs to
8
tetrahedra, it can be shown that:

\sum_{i}S_{i}^{z}=\frac{1}{8}\sum_{\alpha}L_{\alpha}^{z}\,.

(9)

Similarly, since every
nn

fcc
lattice belongs to
2
tetrahedra, we find that:

\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}\vec{S}_{i}\cdot\vec{S}_{j}=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{\alpha}\bigl(\vec{L}_{\alpha}\bigr)^{2}+{\rm const.}

(10)

The Hamiltonian (
8
) can be now rewritten, up to an irrelevant constant, as:

\displaystyle{\cal H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\sum_{\alpha}\left[J_{1}\vec{L}_{\alpha}^{2}-\frac{H}{2}L_{\alpha}^{z}\right]

(11)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{J_{1}}{4}\sum_{\alpha}\left[\vec{L}_{\alpha}-\frac{H\hat{z}}{4J_{1}}\right]^{2}+{\rm const.}\,.

For a single tetrahedron, the energy is minimized when
\vec{L}_{\alpha}=(H/4J_{1})\hat{z}
. If this condition can be met for
all tetrahedra simultaneously
, then it is the lowest energy state of the system and its zero-temperature state.

Because
|\vec{S}_{i}|=S
,

is upper bounded by the maximum possible value
(L_{\alpha}^{z})_{\rm max}=4S

then the minimum energy condition cannot be achieved and the lowest energy

g\mu_{B}H_{\rm sat}=16J_{1}S\,.

Monte Carlo Simulations

The classical Monte Carlo (MC) simulations have been performed on periodic lattices with
2000
–
4000
spins. Since in the studied temperature range all materials remain in the paramagnetic state, the simulated system sizes are sufficient to obtain the bulk behavior without additional finite-size scaling. We simulate the nearest neighbor antiferromagnetic model

\hat{\cal H}_{c}=\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}\vec{s}_{i}\cdot\vec{s}_{j}-\vec{H}\cdot\sum_{i}\vec{s}_{i}\,,

(16)

where the spins
\vec{s}_{i}
are classical vectors of unit length,
|\vec{s}_{i}|=1
.
A hybrid Monte Carlo algorithm has been employed with canonical Metropolis sweeps over the lattice followed by microcanonical over-relaxation steps.
At each temperature/field point,
10^{4}
Metropolis steps were used for equilibration with subsequent
10^{5}
hybrid MC steps for measurements. In addition, we performed averaging over
10^{2}
independent MC runs. Relative statistical errors do not exceed
0.5
% for all obtained quantities.

The approximate magnetocaloric cooling rate
(\Delta M/\Delta T)_{H}
was computed from the magnetization using the finite difference method, Equation (
19
), at the resolution of the experimental data (i.e., temperature steps of
2
K).

\biggl(\frac{\partial S_{m}}{\partial H}\biggr)_{T}=\biggl(\frac{\partial M}{\partial T}\biggr)_{H}=\frac{1}{T^{2}}\left(\vphantom{\sum}\langle EM\rangle-\langle E\rangle\langle M\rangle\right)\,,

(17)

where
\langle\ldots\rangle
denotes statistical averaging.

9

\displaystyle\biggl(\frac{\partial M}{\partial T}\biggr)_{\rm exp}=\frac{g\mu_{B}N_{A}}{J_{1}(S+1)}\biggl(\frac{\partial M}{\partial T}\biggr)_{\rm MC}\,.

(18)

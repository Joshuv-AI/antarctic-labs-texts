Notes on the SWIFT method based on Shannon Wavelets for Option Pricing - Revisited
Fabien Le Floc’h

Abstract
This note revisits the SWIFT method based on Shannon wavelets to price European options under models with a known characteristic function in 2023. In particular, it discusses some possible improvements and exposes some concrete drawbacks of the method.

1
Introduction

Ortiz-Gracia and
Oosterlee 2016
describe a new method, called SWIFT, based on Shannon wavelets, to price European options for models with a known characteristic function. We will apply the technique mostly to the Heston stochastic volatility model
(
Heston 1993
)
.
Le
Floc’h 2020
explored alternative implementations of the SWIFT method, to conclude that the use of fast Fourier transform (FFT) was key, and suggested that the use of the trapezoidal rule to compute the density coefficients was more accurate than the use of an expansion of the Vieta formula as in
(
Ortiz-Gracia and
Oosterlee 2016
)
.

We revisit here which quadrature is more appropriate for the computation of the density coefficients and of the payoff coefficients. We also pay more attention to the choice of SWIFT parameters, as the various publications on SWIFT propose slight variations. Finally we show through concrete examples that the method suffers from many of the same drawbacks as the COS method.

Proof.

According to
Belov 2014
, the second Euler-Maclaurin expansion of sinc reads

\displaystyle\mathrm{sinc}(x)=\frac{1}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\cos(\omega_{j}x)+\frac{1}{\pi}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{k+1}b_{k}\left(\frac{\pi}{J}\right)^{2k}x^{2k-1}(-1)^{k}\sin(\pi x)\,,

with
b_{k}>0
such that
b_{k}<0.08b_{k-1}
and
b_{1}=\frac{1}{24}
.
We thus have

\displaystyle\left|\mathrm{sinc}(x)-\frac{1}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\cos(\omega_{j}x)\right|\leq\frac{b_{1}}{J}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}0.08^{k-1}\left(\frac{\pi x}{J}\right)^{2k-1}\,,

or equivalently

\displaystyle\left|\mathrm{sinc}(x)-\frac{1}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\cos(\omega_{j}x)\right|\leq\frac{b_{1}\pi x}{J^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\left(\frac{\pi x}{J}\right)^{2}0.08\right)^{k-1}\,.

(13)

The series on the right hand side converges for
\left|\left(\frac{\pi x}{J}\right)^{2}0.08\right|<1
, which implies
J>\pi\sqrt{0.08}|x|
. Furthermore
\pi\sqrt{0.08}\leq 0.89
and thus
J=\lceil\ |x|\rceil
is sufficient. Furthermore, the error is then bounded by
\frac{b_{1}\pi x}{J^{2}}\frac{1}{1-\frac{0.08\pi^{2}x^{2}}{J^{2}}}
.

For the trapezoidal rule (first Euler-Maclaurin formula), the error bound would be very similar, but replacing
b_{1}
by
a_{1}=2b_{1}
.
∎

It may be however worth using

to guarantee a reasonably good accuracy up to the index

4
Vieta, Mid-points or Trapezoidal?

4.1
For the payoff

Truncated to the interval
[-c,c]
the payoff coefficients read

V_{m,k}=2^{\frac{m}{2}}Ke^{x}\int_{-c}^{-x}\left(e^{-x}-e^{y}\right)\frac{\sin\left(\pi\left(2^{m}y-k\right)\right)}{\pi\left(2^{m}y-k\right)}\mathop{}\!\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{d}y\,.

(14)

The integral may be approximated directly by some quadrature rule, such as the mid-point rule, the trapezoidal rule, or higher order quadrature rules such as Simpson or Boole. Applied directly to Equation
14
, the mid-point quadrature is not the same as the approximation based on the Vieta expansion, since the mid-points are applied to the integrand, and thus the sinc function instead of the cosine function.

Alternatively, we may integrate exactly an approximation of the sinc function. Equation
7
uses the second Euler-Maclaurin expansion with first order derivatives. Excluding the second-term, we have the mid-point rule applied to the sinc function integral representation (Equation
9
). In similar fashion we may apply the first Euler-Maclaurin expansion to obtain

\displaystyle V_{m,k}

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{\frac{m}{2}}F}{J}\int_{-c}^{-x}\left(e^{-x}-e^{y}\right)\left[\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\cos\left(\pi\left(2^{m}y-k\right)\right)+\sum_{j=1}^{J-1}\cos\left(\frac{\pi j}{J}\left(2^{m}y-k\right)\right)\right]\mathop{}\!\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle+\frac{\pi 2^{\frac{m}{2}}F}{12J^{2}}\int_{-c}^{-x}\left(2^{m}y-k\right)\left(e^{-x}-e^{y}\right)\sin\left(\pi\left(2^{m}y-k\right)\right)\mathop{}\!\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{d}y\,.

(15)

Excluding the second term, it corresponds to the trapezoidal rule applied to the sinc function integral representation. We may pursue the idea with the Simpson rule. The rule involving
2J+1
terms corresponds to
2/3
of the mid-point rule plus
1/3
of the trapezoidal rule, and the first derivative Euler-Maclaurin correction is just zero.

Equation
15
may be computed via the FFT as we can rewrite it in the following form:

\displaystyle V_{m,k}

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{\frac{m}{2}}F}{J}\Re\left\{\sum_{j=0}^{J}w_{j}e^{-i\pi\frac{kj}{J}}\left[g_{j}(-x)-g_{j}(-c)\right]\right\}

\displaystyle+\frac{(-1)^{k}\pi 2^{\frac{m}{2}}F}{12J^{2}}\Im\left\{-k\left[g_{J}(-x)-g_{J}(-c)\right]+2^{m}\left[-xg_{J}(-x)+cg_{J}(-c)\right]-2^{m}\left[-\frac{e^{-x+ip_{J}z}}{p_{J}^{2}}-\frac{e^{z+ip_{J}z}}{(ip_{J}+1)^{2}}\right]_{z=-c}^{z=-x}\right\}

(16)

with
p_{j}=\frac{2^{m}\pi j}{J}
and
g_{j}(z)=\frac{e^{-x+ip_{j}z}}{ip_{j}}-\frac{e^{z+ip_{j}z}}{ip_{j}+1}
for
j\geq 1
and
g_{0}(z)=z-e^{z}
,
w_{j}=1
for
j=1,...,J-1
and
w_{0}=w_{J}=\frac{1}{2}
.

We may deduce that the mid-point rule is slightly better since it involves
J

\phi(0)=1
, and is thus not
more costly. Furthermore, the FFT is simplified and does not involve
J

More importantly, the trapezoidal rule allows to reuse the previous characteristic function evaluations when
J
is doubled, which may be particularly important in the procedure used to choose the SWIFT parameters.

In addition we may use more accurate quadrature rules. For example, the first Euler-Maclaurin formula with first derivative correction reads

\displaystyle c_{m,k}

\displaystyle=2^{\frac{m}{2}+1}\Re\left\{\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\hat{f}\left(2^{m+1}\pi t\right)e^{2i\pi kt}\mathop{}\!\kern 0.0pt\mathrm{d}t\right\}

(18)

with weights
w_{0}=w_{j}=\frac{1}{2}
and
w_{j}=1

The Simpson quadrature requires double the number of evaluations of the characteristic function (if we halve
J
, the error may explode) and again does not translate into a more precise price.

6
Conclusion

There are not many improvements to propose on the SWIFT method as described in
(
Maree
et al. 2017
;
Leitao
et al. 2018
)
: more precise approximations of sinc do not translate into a more precise option price. The initialization procedure of
Leitao
et al. 2018
is more agnostic to the choice of initial range. And in this regard, the SWIFT method is more robust than the COS method. We also proved that the expansions were converging with a slightly smaller
J
than the choice presented in
(
Ortiz-Gracia and
Oosterlee 2016
)
and subsequent papers.

The use of the trapezoidal rule is preferable to the use of the Vieta based expansion, in order to reuse points when the number of points is doubled.

Similarly to the COS method, the SWIFT method requires too many points to be practical on many corner cases. This may however not be a problem for the calibration of stochastic volatility models or Levy models, if a good initial guess and a proper error measure are used.

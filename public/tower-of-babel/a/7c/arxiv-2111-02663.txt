Charged rotating black strings
in Einsteinian cubic gravity

Abstract




We construct, for the first time, charged rotating black string solutions in four-dimensional Einsteinian cubic gravity, which are asymptotically anti–de Sitter. By assuming that the solutions are completely regular at the horizon and studying their near-horizon behavior, we find some thermodynamic properties, which can be accessed analytically. We compute independently the Hawking temperature, the Wald entropy, the mass, the angular momentum, the charge, and the electrostatic potential of the solutions, analytically. Using these, we show that the first law of thermodynamics for rotating black strings is exactly satisfied in both charged and uncharged cases. We also observe that, in the absence of Maxwell field, some of the solutions have positive specific heat, which makes them thermodynamically stable.

2.4
Thermal stability

The local stability, in an arbitrary ensemble, requires that the entropy
{\cal S}
be a convex function of their extensive variables, or its Legendre transformation must be a concave function of their intensive variables. Thus, the local stability can be explored by finding the determinant of the Hessian matrix of
{\cal S}
with respect to its extensive variables
X_{i}
,
{\bf H}_{X_{i}X_{j}}^{\cal S}=\partial^{2}{\cal S}/\partial X_{i}\partial X_{j}
, or the determinant of the Hessian of the Gibbs function, with respect to its intensive variables
Y_{i}
,
{\bf H}_{Y_{i}Y_{j}}^{G}=\partial^{2}{G}/\partial Y_{i}\partial Y_{j}
, where the thermodynamic variables of the system depend on the ensemble, which is used
[
46
]
. In the other words, the more
X_{i}
or
Y_{i}
we regard as variable parameters, the smaller is the region of stability. In the case of uncharged solutions, the entropy is a function of mass and angular momentum per unit length. In canonical ensemble, the angular momentum is the fixed parameter, and therefore, the positivity of specific heat,

C=T\left(\frac{\partial{\cal S}}{\partial T}\right)_{\cal J},

(2.42)

guarantees the local stability
[
46
]
. Writing the entropy (
2.29
) in terms of temperature using Eq. (
2.30
) and then substituting it into the above expression, one finds that the specific heat parametrized by horizon radius
r_{h}
is given by

C=\frac{\pi r_{h}^{2}\Xi\left(1-\frac{108G^{2}\lambda}{l^{4}}\right)\left(1+\Xi^{2}\right)}{2Gl\left(5\Xi^{2}-4\right)}.

(2.43)

It can be seen that for
\lambda\leq l^{4}/108G^{2}
, the solutions are locally stable in the canonical ensemble.

In the grand canonical ensemble, the thermodynamic variables are the temperature and the angular velocity. So, it is more convenient to work with thermodynamic potential
G(T,\Omega)={\cal M}-T{\cal S}-\Omega{\cal J}
, which is indeed the Legendre transformation of the energy
{\cal M}({\cal S},{\cal J})
with respect to
{\cal S},{\cal J}
. Here, the determinant of Hessian matrix is given by

|{\bf H}_{T,\Omega}^{G}|=\left[\frac{\pi\Xi^{2}r_{h}^{2}}{G}\left(1-\frac{108G^{2}\lambda}{l^{4}}\right)\right]^{2}\left(1-\frac{\Xi^{2}}{2}\right).

(2.44)

It is a matter of calculation to show that the solutions are locally stable in grand canonical ensemble if
\Xi\leq\sqrt{2}
or using Eq. (
2.4
),
a\leq l
.

3
Asymptotically AdS charged solutions

In this section we add a Maxwell field to the action as

S=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{1}{16\pi G}\left(R-2\Lambda-2G^{2}\lambda{\cal P}\right)-\frac{1}{4}F_{ab}F^{ab}\right],

(3.1)

where
F_{ab}=2\partial_{[a}A_{b]}
. The vector potential and nonzero components of electromagnetic field strength tensor are given by
[
24
]

\displaystyle A_{a}=A_{0}(r)\left(\Xi\delta_{a}^{t}-a\delta_{a}^{\phi}\right);

\displaystyle F_{tr}=-F_{rt}=-\Xi A^{\prime}_{0}(r),F_{\phi r}=-F_{r\phi}=aA^{\prime}_{0}(r).

(3.2)

Having had the electromagnetic field strength tensor, it is not difficult to show that both
t
and
\phi
components of the Maxwell equation,
\triangledown_{a}F^{ab}=0
, lead to the same equation,

rA^{\prime\prime}_{0}g-rA^{\prime}_{0}g^{\prime}+2A^{\prime}_{0}g=0.

(3.3)

Here also, as the uncharged case, we find that
g^{\prime}=0
and therefore set
g=1
. This yields the following value for the gauge potential

A_{0}(r)=\frac{q}{4\pi r},

(3.4)

where
q
is an integration constant, which is indeed the electric charge of the black string. As before, the equation for
f
can be obtained from the variation of (
3.1
) with respect to
g
and then setting
g=1
, that is

-\frac{1}{3}\Lambda r^{2}+\frac{GQ^{2}}{r}-rf-G^{2}\lambda\left[4f^{\prime 3}-12ff^{\prime}f^{\prime\prime}-24\frac{f^{2}\left(f^{\prime}-rf^{\prime\prime}\right)}{r^{2}}\right]=r_{0},

(3.5)

where we have defined
Q^{2}\equiv q^{2}/4\pi
, and
r_{0}
is an integration constant, which is related to the mass of the black hole as the uncharged case,
r_{0}=GM
. In the next section, we will perform an asymptotic expansion around
r\rightarrow\infty
.

3.1
Asymptotic solution

In the case of charged solutions, by setting
\lambda=0
in Eq. (
2.8
), one finds

f(r)=\frac{r^{2}}{l^{2}}-\frac{GM}{r}+\frac{GQ^{2}}{r^{2}},

(3.6)

which is the usual asymptotically AdS charged solution of rotating black strings
[
24
,
23
]
. As the uncharged case, one can examine the large-
r
behavior of the solution. To this end, we assume that
f
can be expressed as a particular solution in the form of a
1/r
expansion, plus the general solution of the corresponding homogeneous equation. Just like the uncharged case, this expansion is given by Eq. (
2.10
). Plugging this series expansion into Eq. (
3.5
), one observes that the large-
r
expansion takes the following form:

f_{1/r}(r)=\frac{r^{2}}{l_{\rm eff}^{2}}-\frac{G_{\rm eff}M}{r}+\frac{G_{\rm eff}Q^{2}}{r^{2}}+{\cal O}(r^{-3}),

(3.7)

where the effective radius of the AdS space
l_{\rm eff}
, and the effective gravitational constant
G_{\rm eff}
are defined by Eqs. (
2.12
) and (
2.13
), respectively. The linearized homogeneous equation satisfied by
f_{h}(r)
is given by

f_{h}^{\prime\prime}(r)+\frac{12M}{4Q^{2}-3Mr}f_{h}^{\prime}(r)+\frac{3M\gamma^{2}r^{2}}{4Q^{2}-3Mr}f_{h}(r)=0,

(3.8)

at the large-
r
limit. Notice that when
Q=0
, we recover Eq. (
2.14
). Unfortunately, this equation can not be solved analytically. But due to the existence of
\left(4Q^{2}-3Mr\right)
in the denominator, it is possible to expand the above equation at large
r
one more time and find a solution and discuss the boundary conditions. By doing so, we arrive at

f_{h}^{\prime\prime}(r)-\frac{\left(4Q^{2}+3Mr\right)\gamma^{2}}{3M}f_{h}(r)=0.

(3.9)

Here,
\gamma^{2}
is given by Eq. (
2.15
). The solution reads,

f_{h}(r)=A^{\prime}\text{Ai}\left(\frac{4Q^{2}+3Mr}{3M}\left|\gamma^{2}\right|^{1/3}\right)+B^{\prime}\text{Bi}\left(\frac{4Q^{2}+3Mr}{3M}\left|\gamma^{2}\right|^{1/3}\right),

(3.10)

where
Ai
and
Bi
are the Airy functions of the first and second kinds, and
A^{\prime}
and
B^{\prime}
are some constants. In the case of
\gamma^{2}>0
, and at large
r
,

\displaystyle f_{h}^{(+)}(r)

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\left(\frac{4Q^{2}+3Mr}{3M}\gamma^{2/3}\right)^{1/4}\times

(3.11)

\displaystyle\left\{A^{\prime}\exp\left[-\frac{2}{3}\left(\frac{4Q^{2}+3Mr}{3M}\gamma^{2/3}\right)^{3/2}\right]+B^{\prime}\exp\left[\frac{2}{3}\left(\frac{4Q^{2}+3Mr}{3M}\gamma^{2/3}\right)^{3/2}\right]\right\},

and so we must set
B^{\prime}=0
to ensure the AdS boundary conditions are satisfied. As a result, no ghost excitations can propagate to infinity. If
\gamma^{2}<0
, then the homogeneous solution asymptotically takes the following form at large
r
:

\displaystyle f_{h}^{(-)}(r)

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\left(\frac{4Q^{2}+3Mr}{3M}\gamma^{2/3}\right)^{-1/4}\times

(3.12)

\displaystyle\left\{A^{\prime}\sin\left[\frac{2}{3}\left(\frac{4Q^{2}+3Mr}{3M}\gamma^{2/3}\right)^{3/2}\right]+B^{\prime}\cos\left[\frac{2}{3}\left(\frac{4Q^{2}+3Mr}{3M}\gamma^{2/3}\right)^{3/2}\right]\right\}.

Here also the solution oscillates rapidly and therefore does not approach AdS asymptotically, and so we set
A^{\prime}=B^{\prime}=0
. As the uncharged case, to avoid any oscillating behavior at infinity, we restrict the solutions to the constraint
\gamma^{2}>0
. Observe that all the leading asymptotic corrections to the metric come from the solution (
3.7
), while the contributions from the homogeneous equation are extremely subleading. Hence, the term proportional to
A^{\prime}
above can be discarded from the asymptotic expansion.

3.2
Near-horizon solution

In a similar way with the uncharged case, by making a Taylor expansion around the horizon, one finds the following equations at two lowest-order of expansion:

-GM-32G^{2}\lambda\kappa_{g}^{3}\Xi^{3}+\frac{r_{h}^{3}}{l^{2}}+\frac{GQ^{2}}{r_{h}}=0,

(3.13)

\frac{3r_{h}^{2}}{l^{2}}-2\kappa_{g}\Xi r_{h}-\frac{GQ^{2}}{r_{h}^{2}}=0,

(3.14)

which solving them yields the following values for the surface gravity, as well as mass as a function of horizon radius and charge of black strings,

\kappa_{g}=\frac{1}{2\Xi r_{h}^{3}}\left(\frac{3r_{h}^{4}}{l^{2}}-GQ^{2}\right),

(3.15)

M=\frac{r_{h}^{3}}{Gl^{2}}\left[1+\frac{Gl^{2}Q^{2}}{r_{h}^{4}}-\frac{4G^{2}l^{2}\lambda}{r_{h}^{12}}\left(\frac{3r_{h}^{4}}{l^{2}}-GQ^{2}\right)^{3}\right].

(3.16)

Using these expressions, we are able to check the first law of black string thermodynamics in the presence of the Maxwell field in the next section.

3.3
Thermodynamics

Using the entropy (
2.28
) and
f^{\prime}(r_{h})=2\Xi\kappa_{g}
, where
\kappa_{g}
is given by (
3.15
), we find the following quantity for the Wald entropy per unit length for charged solutions:

{\cal S}=\frac{\pi r_{h}^{2}\Xi}{2Gl}\left[1-\frac{12G^{2}\lambda}{r_{h}^{8}}\left(\frac{3r_{h}^{4}}{l^{2}}-GQ^{2}\right)^{2}\right].

(3.17)

The Hawking temperature can easily be obtained in terms of charge and the horizon radius by multiplying the surface gravity (
3.15
) with a factor
1/2\pi
,

T=\frac{1}{4\pi r_{h}^{3}\Xi}\left(\frac{3r_{h}^{4}}{l^{2}}-GQ^{2}\right).

(3.18)

The total mass and angular momentum per unit length for charged solutions can also be found by inserting Eq. (
3.16
) into the Eqs. (
2.37
) and (
2.38
),

{\cal M}=\frac{r_{h}^{3}}{8Gl^{3}}\left(3\Xi^{2}-1\right)\left[1+\frac{Gl^{2}Q^{2}}{r_{h}^{4}}-\frac{4G^{2}l^{2}\lambda}{r_{h}^{12}}\left(\frac{3r_{h}^{4}}{l^{2}}-GQ^{2}\right)^{3}\right],

(3.19)

{\cal J}=\frac{3r_{h}^{3}}{8Gl^{2}}\Xi\sqrt{\Xi^{2}-1}\left[1+\frac{Gl^{2}Q^{2}}{r_{h}^{4}}-\frac{4G^{2}l^{2}\lambda}{r_{h}^{12}}\left(\frac{3r_{h}^{4}}{l^{2}}-GQ^{2}\right)^{3}\right].

(3.20)

In the following we are going to find the electric potential and total charge of black strings. The electric potential
\Phi
, measured at infinity with respect to the horizon, is defined by
[
29
]

\Phi=A_{a}\chi^{a}\lvert_{r\rightarrow\infty}-A_{a}\chi^{a}\lvert_{r=r_{h}},

(3.21)

where, as already mentioned,
\chi
is the null generator of the horizon. Calculating the above expression yields the following value for electric potential

\displaystyle\Phi=\frac{q}{4\pi\Xi r_{h}}=\frac{Q}{2\sqrt{\pi}\Xi r_{h}}.

(3.22)

Finally, we calculate the electric charge of the black string. To this end, we first determine the electric field by considering the projections of the electromagnetic field tensor on a special hypersurface. The normal vectors to such a hypersurface are

u^{0}=\frac{1}{N},u^{r}=0,u^{i}=-\frac{V^{i}}{N},

(3.23)

where
N
and
V^{i}
are the lapse function and shift vector, respectively. The electric field is also given by
E^{\mu}=g^{\mu\rho}F_{\rho\nu}u^{\nu}
. The electric charge per unit length of black strings can be found by calculating the flux of the electric field at infinity, yielding

\displaystyle{\cal Q}=\frac{q\Xi}{2l}=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}Q\Xi}{l}.

(3.24)

Using the above expressions, one can easily show that the first law of thermodynamics for charged rotating black strings,

d{\cal M}=Td{\cal S}+\Omega d{\cal J}+\Phi d{\cal Q},

(3.25)

is fully satisfied in the context of ECG theory.

4
Conclusions

In this paper we have constructed generalizations of the charged rotating black strings in four-dimensional ECG with AdS asymptotic and studied some of their properties.

We have shown that the theory admits solutions with a single function
f(r)
, which is given by a nonlinear second-order differential equation and studied some of their thermodynamic properties which can be obtained analytically. The new solutions represent the first nontrivial four-dimensional generalizations of the asymptotically AdS charged rotating black strings in higher-order gravity, whose thermodynamic properties can be computed exactly. Using those results, we have been able to check analytically that the solutions exactly satisfy the first law of thermodynamics for black strings in both charged and uncharged cases.

Let us close with some possible future explorations. It would be interesting to perform this kind of study, in higher dimensions. Finding the solutions in the presence of nonlinear Born-Infeld electrodynamics is also an interesting feature. Finally, a study of the solutions including Einsteinian quartic gravity would be perhaps worth pursuing.

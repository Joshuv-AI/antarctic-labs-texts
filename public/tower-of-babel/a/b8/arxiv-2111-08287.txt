Abstract
. This note is a sequel to Shu-Xue-Yao’s paper [ 2 ] where the author studied the so-called enhanced groups and related dualities for type A A . In this note, we continue to investigate the enhanced dualities for classical groups of type B B , C C , and D D . To show this, we first introduce an enhanced Brauer algebra. Due to Proposition 1.4 , we can easily obtain the restricted and Levi dualities, hence the parabolic duality is naturally described. Moreover, a special case is listed in § 3 by some explicit calculations.

1.2.
Classical Schur-Weyl duality.

We can define a representation
(\underline{V}^{\otimes r},\Phi)
of
\text{GL}(\underline{V})

\Phi(g)(v_{1}\otimes v_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{r}):=g(v_{1})\otimes g(v_{2})\otimes\cdots\otimes g(v_{r}).

(1.5)

for any
g\in\text{GL}(\underline{V})
and any monomial tensor product
v_{1}\otimes v_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{r}\in\underline{V}^{\otimes r}
.

In the meanwhile,
(\underline{V}^{\otimes r},\Psi)
naturally becomes a representation of
\mathfrak{S}_{r}
with following permutation action

\Psi(\sigma)(v_{1}\otimes v_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{r}):=v_{\sigma^{-1}(1)}\otimes v_{\sigma^{-1}(2)}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{\sigma^{-1}(r)}.

(1.6)

for any
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{r}
and any monomial tensor product
v_{1}\otimes v_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{r}\in\underline{V}^{\otimes r}
.

The classical Schur-Weyl duality implies that the images of
\Phi
and
\Psi
are double centralizers in
\text{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\underline{V}^{\otimes r})
, namely

\text{End}_{\text{GL}(\underline{V})}(V^{\otimes r})=\mathbb{C}\Psi(\mathfrak{S}_{r}),\text{ }\text{End}_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}}(\underline{V}^{\otimes r})=\mathbb{C}\Phi(\mathfrak{\text{GL}}(\underline{V})).

(1.7)

Here
\mathbb{C}\Psi(\mathfrak{S}_{r})
and
\mathbb{C}\Phi(\mathfrak{\text{GL}}(\underline{V}))
are the subalgebras of
\text{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\underline{V}^{\otimes r})
that generated by
\Psi(\mathfrak{S}_{r})
and
\Phi(\mathfrak{\text{GL}}(\underline{V}))
respectively.

For any
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, we define
\underline{m}:=\{1,2,\cdots,m\}
. Suppose
V=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{C}\eta_{i}
, then
\{\eta_{1},\cdots,\eta_{n},\eta\}
is a basis of
\underline{V}
. Clearly, all
\eta_{\textbf{i}}:=\eta_{i_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\eta_{i_{r}}
for
\textbf{i}=\{i_{1},\cdots,i_{r}\}\in(\underline{n+1})^{r}
(i.e.
1\leq i_{1},\cdots i_{r}\leq n+1
) form a basis of
\underline{V}^{\otimes r}
where
\eta_{n+1}:=\eta
. For any
I\subseteq\underline{r}
, if
i\in I
, we set
V_{i}:=V
, otherwise
V_{i}:=\mathbb{C}\eta
. Now we define a subspace
\underline{V}_{I}^{\otimes r}
of
\underline{V}^{\otimes r}
as
\underline{V}_{I}^{\otimes r}:=V_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes V_{r}
. In particular,
\underline{V}_{\phi}^{\otimes r}=\mathbb{C}\eta\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbb{C}\eta=\mathbb{C}\eta\otimes\cdots\otimes\eta
with
r
copies (Here
\phi
is the empty set). Suppose
I=\{i_{1},\cdots,i_{l}\}
where
i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{l}
, it is not hard to see that
\underline{V}_{I}^{\otimes r}
has a basis as follows

\displaystyle\{\eta_{\textbf{j}}:=\eta_{j_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\eta_{j_{r}}\mid j_{i_{k}}\in\underline{n},k=1,\cdots,l;j_{d}=n+1\text{ for }d\neq i_{k}\}.

(1.8)

Let
\underline{V}_{l}^{\otimes r}:=\bigoplus\limits_{I\subseteq\underline{r},~\sharp I=l}\underline{V}_{I}^{\otimes r}
, thus
\underline{V}^{\otimes r}
has a decomposition as a linear space
\underline{V}^{\otimes r}=\bigoplus_{l=0}^{r}\underline{V}_{l}^{\otimes r}
.

Clearly, each
\underline{V}^{\otimes r}_{l}
is stabilized under the action of
\mathfrak{S}_{r}
. Thus this action gives rise to a representation of
\mathfrak{S}_{r}
on
\underline{V}^{\otimes r}_{l}
, denoted by
\Psi|_{l}
. Similarly, each
\underline{V}^{\otimes r}_{l}
and
\underline{V}^{\otimes r}_{I}
with
0\leq l\leq r,I\subseteq\underline{r}
are stabilized under the action of
\text{GL}(V)\times\textbf{G}_{m}
. Hence they give rise to the representations of
\text{GL}(V)\times\textbf{G}_{m}
on
\underline{V}^{\otimes r}_{l}
and
\underline{V}^{\otimes r}_{I}
, denoted by
\Phi|_{l}
and
\Phi|_{I}
respectively.

By classical Schur-Weyl duality, we have

\text{End}_{\text{GL}(V)}(V^{\otimes r})=\mathbb{C}\Psi|_{r}(\mathfrak{S}_{r}),\text{ }\text{End}_{\mathfrak{S}_{r}}(V^{\otimes r})=\mathbb{C}\Phi|_{r}(\mathfrak{\text{GL}}(V)).

(1.9)

Here
\mathbb{C}\Psi|_{r}(\mathfrak{S}_{r})
and
\mathbb{C}\Phi|_{r}(\mathfrak{\text{GL}}(V))
are the subalgebras of
\text{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(V^{\otimes r})
that generated by
\Psi|_{r}(\mathfrak{S}_{r})
and
\Phi|_{r}(\mathfrak{\text{GL}}(V))
respectively.

\textbf{v}^{\prime}=w_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes w_{l}\otimes\eta^{(n-l)}\in\underline{V}_{\underline{l}}^{\otimes r}
, then we have
\rho(\delta)(D_{\textbf{v}^{\prime}}^{f_{t}})=0
. If
\rho(\delta_{2})(D_{\textbf{v}^{\prime}}^{f_{t}})\neq 0
, then each summand of
\rho(\delta_{2})(D_{\textbf{v}^{\prime}}^{f_{t}})=\rho(\delta_{2})(w_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes w_{l}\otimes{f_{t}}^{(n-l)}+\cdots)
can not be cancelled out in
\rho(\delta)(D_{\textbf{v}^{\prime}}^{f_{t}})
. This contradicts with the fact
\rho(\delta)(D_{\textbf{v}^{\prime}}^{f_{t}})=0
. So we have
\rho(\delta_{2})(D_{\textbf{v}^{\prime}}^{f_{t}})=0
. Hence
\rho(\delta_{1})(D_{\textbf{v}^{\prime}}^{f_{t}})=0
. The fact
\rho(\delta_{1})(D_{\textbf{v}^{\prime}}^{f_{t}})=0
implies that all the summand of
\rho(\delta_{1})(D_{\textbf{v}^{\prime}}^{f_{t}})=\rho(\delta_{1})(w_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes w_{l}\otimes{f_{t}}^{(n-l)}+\cdots)
cancel each other out. So
\rho(\delta_{1})(w_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes w_{l}\otimes{f_{t}}^{(n-l)}+\cdots)=0
is also valid when
f_{t}
is replaced by
\eta
, i.e.
\rho(\delta_{1})(w_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes w_{l}\otimes\eta^{(n-l)}+\cdots)=0
. Hence
\rho(\delta_{1})(w_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes w_{l}\otimes\eta^{(n-l)})=0
. Since
\textbf{v}^{\prime}=w_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes w_{l}\otimes\eta^{(n-l)}
runs through a basis of
\underline{V}_{J}^{\otimes r}
, then
\rho(\delta_{1})(\underline{V}_{J}^{\otimes r})=0
. It is clear that
\rho(\delta_{2})(\underline{V}_{J}^{\otimes r})=0
by definition, then
\rho(\delta)(\underline{V}_{J}^{\otimes r})=0
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume
G=\text{Sp}(V)
with
\textsf{{dim}}V=n>2r
. Suppose that
\delta\in\mathcal{B}_{r}(-n)
and
J\subsetneq\underline{r}
. If
\forall w\in V
and
\forall\textbf{v}\in\underline{V}_{J}^{\otimes r}
, we always have
\rho(\delta)(D_{\textbf{v}}^{w})=0
, then
\rho(\delta)(\underline{V}_{J}^{\otimes r})=0
, i.e.
\rho_{J}(\delta)=0
.

Proof.

The proof is similar to Lemma
3.1
.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Assume
G=\text{Sp}(V)
with
\textsf{{dim}}V>2r
, or
G=\text{O}(V)
with
\textsf{{dim}}V\geq 2r
, then we have
\mathcal{B}(\varepsilon,n,r)^{V}=\{\rho(f)\mid f\in\mathcal{B}_{r}(\varepsilon n)\}
, i.e.
\text{End}_{\underline{G}\rtimes\textbf{G}_{m}}(\underline{V}^{\otimes r})=\{\rho(f)\mid f\in\mathcal{B}_{r}(\varepsilon n)\}
.

Proof.

Suppose
\textsf{{dim}}V=n
, and assume that
\sigma\in\mathcal{B}(\varepsilon,n,r)^{V}
. Equation (
1.26
) implies that there exist
f_{I}\in\mathcal{B}_{r}(\varepsilon n)
with all
I\subseteq\underline{r}
, such that
\sigma=\sum_{I\subseteq\underline{r}}\rho_{I}(f_{I})
. Now we use induction to prove.

If
\sharp I=r
, then it must have
I=\underline{r}
. So we assume that there exist
f\in\mathcal{B}_{r}(\varepsilon n)
with
0\leq k\leq r-1
, such that
\forall I\subseteq\underline{r}
with
\sharp I\geq k+1
we have
\rho_{I}(f_{I})=\rho_{I}(f)
. Then
\sigma=\sum\limits_{I\subseteq\underline{r},~\sharp I\leq k}\rho_{I}(f_{I})+\sum\limits_{I\subseteq\underline{r},~\sharp I>k}\rho_{I}(f)
. It is easy to see
\forall w\in V,e^{w}(\eta)=w+\eta
and
e^{w}|_{V}=\text{id}_{V}
. For all
v_{1},\cdots,v_{k}\in V
and
\forall w\in V
, we have
v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{k}\otimes(w+\eta)^{\otimes(r-k)}=v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{k}\otimes\eta^{\otimes(r-k)}+D_{\textbf{v}}^{w}
, where
\textbf{v}:=v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{k}\otimes\eta^{\otimes(r-k)}
. By (
1.5
), we have

\displaystyle(\sigma\circ\Phi(e^{w}))(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{k}\otimes\eta^{\otimes(r-k)})=\sigma(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{k}\otimes(w+\eta)^{\otimes(r-k)})

\displaystyle=(\sum\limits_{I\subseteq\underline{r},~\sharp I\leq k}\rho_{I}(f_{I})+\sum\limits_{I\subseteq\underline{r},~\sharp I>k}\rho_{I}(f))(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{k}\otimes(w+\eta)^{\otimes(r-k)})

\displaystyle=(\rho_{\underline{k}}(f_{\underline{k}})+\sum\limits_{I\subseteq\underline{r},~\sharp I>k}\rho_{I}(f))(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{k}\otimes(w+\eta)^{\otimes(r-k)})

\displaystyle=\rho_{\underline{k}}(f_{\underline{k}})(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{k}\otimes\eta^{\otimes(r-k)})+\rho(f)(D_{\textbf{v}}^{w})

\displaystyle=\rho(f_{\underline{k}})(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{k}\otimes\eta^{\otimes(r-k)})+\rho(f)(D_{\textbf{v}}^{w}).

(3.3)

By Lemma
1.7
, we suppose that
f_{\underline{k}}=\sum_{s\in\mathfrak{S}_{r}}\sum_{z\in Z_{r}}c_{s,z}\Psi|_{r}(s)\tau_{z}

with
c_{s,z}\in\mathbb{C}
. Let
g=\sum_{s\in\mathfrak{S}_{r}}\sum_{z\in Z_{r}\atop e(z)\subseteq\underline{k}}c_{s,z}\Psi|_{r}(s)\tau_{z}
and
h=\sum_{s\in\mathfrak{S}_{r}}\sum_{z\in Z_{r}\atop e(z)\nsubseteq\underline{k}}c_{s,z}\Psi|_{r}(s)\tau_{z}
, then
f_{\underline{k}}=g+h
(Note that
e(z)
is defined in §
1.4
). Hence we have
\rho(f_{\underline{k}})=\rho(g)+\rho(h)
by the definition of
\rho
. Obviously,
\rho(h)(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{k}\otimes\eta^{\otimes(r-k)})=\rho_{\underline{k}}(h)(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{k}\otimes\eta^{\otimes(r-k)})=0
. Hence

\displaystyle\rho(f_{\underline{k}})(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{k}\otimes\eta^{\otimes(r-k)})=\rho(g)(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{k}\otimes\eta^{\otimes(r-k)}).

(3.4)

Hence, we have

\displaystyle\rho_{\underline{k}}(f_{\underline{k}})=\rho_{\underline{k}}(g).

(3.5)

Equation (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) implies that

\displaystyle(\sigma\circ\Phi(e^{w}))(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{k}\otimes\eta^{\otimes(r-k)})=\rho(g)(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{k}\otimes\eta^{\otimes(r-k)})+\rho(f)(D_{\textbf{v}}^{w}).

(3.6)

On the other hand,

\displaystyle(\Phi(e^{w})\circ\sigma)(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{k}\otimes\eta^{\otimes(r-k)})=\Phi(e^{w})(\rho_{\underline{k}}(f_{\underline{k}})(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{k}\otimes\eta^{\otimes(r-k)}))

\displaystyle=\Phi(e^{w})\left(\rho(f_{\underline{k}})(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{k}\otimes\eta^{\otimes(r-k)})\right)\overset{(\ref{eq3-1})}{=}\Phi(e^{w})\left(\rho(g)(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{k}\otimes\eta^{\otimes(r-k)})\right)

\displaystyle=\rho(g)\left(\Phi(e^{w})(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{k}\otimes\eta^{\otimes(r-k)})\right)\text{ }(\text{By Proposition \ref{B dual}})

\displaystyle=\rho(g)(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{k}\otimes(w+\eta)^{\otimes(r-k)})

\displaystyle=\rho(g)(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{k}\otimes\eta^{\otimes(r-k)})+\rho(g)(D_{\textbf{v}}^{w})

(3.7)

Since
\sigma\in\mathcal{B}(\varepsilon,n,r)^{V}
, i.e.
\Phi(e^{w})\circ\sigma=\sigma\circ\Phi(e^{w})
, (
3.6
) and (
3.7
) yields that
\forall w\in V
,
\rho(f)(D_{\textbf{v}}^{w})=\rho(g)(D_{\textbf{v}}^{w})
. So we have
\rho(f)(D_{\textbf{u}}^{w})=\rho(g)(D_{\textbf{u}}^{w})
for all
\textbf{u}\in\underline{V}_{\underline{k}}^{\otimes r}
and
\forall w\in W
, namely
\rho(f-g)(D_{\textbf{u}}^{w})=0
for all
\textbf{u}\in\underline{V}_{\underline{k}}^{\otimes r}
and
\forall w\in W
. Since
k\leq r-1
, due to Lemma
3.1
and Lemma
3.2
, we conclude that
\left(\rho(f)-\rho(g)\right)(\underline{V}_{\underline{k}}^{\otimes r})=0
. Namely,
\rho(f)|_{\underline{V}_{\underline{k}}^{\otimes r}}=\rho(g)|_{\underline{V}_{\underline{k}}^{\otimes r}}
, which implies that
\rho_{\underline{k}}(f)=\rho_{\underline{k}}(g)
. Due to (
3.5
), we have
\rho_{\underline{k}}(f)=\rho_{\underline{k}}(f_{\underline{k}})
. Generally, we can similarly prove that
\rho_{I}(f)=\rho_{I}(f_{I})
for all
I\subseteq\underline{r}
with
\sharp I=k
. By induction on
\sharp I
, we have
\rho_{I}(f)=\rho_{I}(f_{I})
for all
I\subseteq\underline{r}
. Thus
\sigma=\sum_{I\subseteq\underline{r}}\rho_{I}(f_{I})=\sum_{I\subseteq\underline{r}}\rho_{I}(f)=\rho(f)
. So we prove
\mathcal{B}(\varepsilon,n,r)^{V}\subseteq\{\rho(f)\mid f\in\mathcal{B}_{r}(\varepsilon n)\}
.

Conversely, for
f\in\mathcal{B}_{r}(\varepsilon n)
, we need to show that
\rho(f)\in\mathcal{B}(\varepsilon,n,r)^{V}
. Lemma
1.17
implies that
\rho(f)\in\mathcal{B}(\varepsilon,n,r)
. For all
v_{1},\cdots,v_{k}\in V
and
\forall w\in V
, imitate the proof of (
3.6
) and (
3.7
), we can yield that
(\rho(f)\circ\Phi(e^{w}))(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{k}\otimes\eta^{\otimes(r-k)})=(\Phi(e^{w})\circ\rho(f))(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{k}\otimes\eta^{\otimes(r-k)})
. Hence
\forall I\subseteq\underline{r}
and
\textbf{v}\in\underline{V}_{I}^{\otimes r}
, we have
(\rho(f)\circ\Phi(e^{w}))(\textbf{v})=(\Phi(e^{w})\circ\rho(f))(\textbf{v})
. This shows that
\rho(f)\circ\Phi(e^{w})=\Phi(e^{w})\circ\rho(f)
, which means that
\rho(f)\in\mathcal{B}(\varepsilon,n,r)^{V}
. Namely,
\{\rho(f)\mid f\in\mathcal{B}_{r}(\varepsilon n)\}\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\varepsilon,n,r)^{V}
. ∎

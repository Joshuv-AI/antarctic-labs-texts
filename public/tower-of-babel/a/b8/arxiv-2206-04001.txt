Classification of equilibria for the spatially homogeneous Boltzmann equation for Fermi-Dirac particles
Xuguang Lu

Abstract
The classification of equilibria for the spatially homogeneous Boltzmann equation for Fermi-Dirac particles is proved for any n n -dimensional velocity space with n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 . The same classification has been proven in [ 7 ] for n = 3 n=3 . Now the proof for n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 is based on a recent result on a characterization of Euclidean balls for all dimensions ≥ 2 \geq 2 . Key words : Boltzmann equation, Fermi-Dirac particles, classification of equilibria, Euclidean balls. Mathematics Subject Classification: 82C10, 82C40, 51M04, 52A10

Introduction

The spatially homogeneous Boltzmann equation for Fermi-Dirac particles
under consideration is given by (after normalizing parameters)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}f(v,t)=\int\!\!\!\!\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}B(v-v_{*},\sigma)\big(f^{\prime}f_{*}^{\prime}(1-f)(1-f_{*})-ff_{*}(1-f^{\prime})(1-f_{*}^{\prime})\big){\rm d}v_{*}{\rm d}\sigma

[formula]

with
(v,t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times(0,\infty)
. Throughout this paper we assume that

n\geq 2
.
For
n=3
, such Boltzmann equations for Bose-Einstein particles and for Fermi-Dirac particles were first derived by Nordheim
[
11
]
and Uehling
\&
Uhlenbeck
[
13
]

and then taken attention and developed by
[
2
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
8
]
.
The solution
f=f(v,t)\geq 0
is the number density of particles at time
t
with the velocity
v
,
and as usual we denote briefly

f_{*}=f(v_{*},t),f^{\prime}=f(v^{\prime},t),f_{*}^{\prime}=f(v_{*}^{\prime},t)
where
v,v_{*}
and
v^{\prime},v_{*}^{\prime}
are velocities of two particles just before and after
their collision:

v^{\prime}=\frac{v+v_{*}}{2}+\frac{|v-v_{*}|\sigma}{2},\quad v_{*}^{\prime}=\frac{v+v_{*}}{2}-\frac{|v-v_{*}|\sigma}{2},\qquad\sigma\in{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}

(1.1)

which conserves the momentum and kinetic energy

v^{\prime}+v_{*}^{\prime}=v+v_{*},\quad|v^{\prime}|^{2}+|v_{*}^{\prime}|^{2}=|v|^{2}+|v_{*}|^{2}.

The right hand side of Eq.(BFD) is called collision integral
which describes
the rate of change of
f
due to the binary collision (
1.1
). The function

B(v-v_{*},\sigma)
(the collision kernel) is a positive Borel function of

(|v-v_{*}|,\langle v-v_{*},\sigma\rangle/|v-v_{*}|)
only, and satisfies some conditions
so that Eq.(BFD) has solutions.

A solution
f
of Eq.(BFD) is not only required having finite mass and energy, i.e. for every
t\in[0,\infty)

f(\cdot,t)\in L^{1}_{2}({{\mathbb{R}}^{n}})=\{f\in L^{1}({{\mathbb{R}}^{n}})\,\,|\,\,\|f\|_{L^{1}_{2}}:=\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}}(1+|v|^{2})|f(v)|{\rm d}v<\infty\}

but also satisfying the
L^{\infty}
-condition (due to the Pauli’s exclusion principle):

0\leq f(v,t)\leq 1\qquad\forall\,(v,t)\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\times[0,\infty).

(1.2)

It is well-known that under some conditions on
B(v-v_{*},\sigma)
,
if an initial datum
f|_{t=0}=f_{0}\in L^{1}_{2}({{\mathbb{R}}^{n}})
satisfies

0\leq f_{0}\leq 1
on
{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}
, then the corresponding
solution
f
satisfies (
1.2
).

The entropy functional for Eq.(BFD) is

S(f)=\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}}\big\{-(1-f(v))\log(1-f(v))-f(v)\log(f(v))\big\}{\rm d}v

from which it is easily seen that

0\leq S(f)<\infty\quad{\rm for\,\,\,all}\,\,\,f\in L^{1}({{\mathbb{R}}^{n}})\,\,\,{\rm satisfying}\,\,\,0\leq f\leq 1\quad{\rm on}\quad{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}.

The corresponding entropy identity for solutions of Eq.(BFD) is

S(f(t))=S(f_{0})+\int_{0}^{t}D(f(s)){\rm d}s,\ \ \ t\geq 0

(1.3)

where
f(t)=f(\cdot,t)
and

D(f)=\frac{1}{4}\int\!\!\!\!\int\!\!\!\!\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\times{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}B(v-v_{*},\sigma)\Gamma\big(f^{\prime}f^{\prime}_{*}(1-f)(1-f_{*})\,,\,ff_{*}(1-f^{\prime})(1-f_{*}^{\prime})\big){\rm d}v_{*}{\rm d}\sigma{\rm d}v,

\Gamma(a,b)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}(a-b)\log(a/b)\,,&\mbox{ $a>0,\ b>0;$}\\
\ \ \ \infty,&\mbox{ $a>0,\ b=0$ \ \ or
\ \ $a=0,\ b>0;$}\\
\ \ \ \ 0,&\ \mbox{ $a=b=0.$}\end{array}\right.

An equilibrium
f\in L^{1}_{2}({{\mathbb{R}}^{n}})
of Eq.(BFD) is a
time-independent solution of the equation.
By entropy identity (
1.4
) and
B(\cdot,\cdot)>0
a.e.,
this is equivalent to saying that an equilibrium

f(v)
of Eq.(BFD) is a
solution of the following functional equation

f^{\prime}f_{*}^{\prime}(1-f)(1-f_{*})=f\,f_{*}(1-f^{\prime})(1-f_{*}^{\prime})\ \ \ {\rm a.e.\,\,\,\,on}\quad{{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\times{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}

(1.4)

together with the physical conditions

f\in L^{1}_{2}({{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}),\,\,\,\,\|f\|_{L^{1}_{2}}\neq 0\,\,\,\,{\rm and}\,\,\,\,0\leq f\leq 1\,\,\,\,{\rm on}\,\,\,\,{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}.

(1.5)

Let
mes(E)
be the Lebesgue measure of a Lebesgue measurable set
E\subset{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}
.
Let
|{{\mathbb{S}}}^{n-1}|
denote the area of the unit sphere
{{\mathbb{S}}}^{n-1}\subset{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}
.
As usual we denote
{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(0<f<1)=\{v\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\,|\,0<f(v)<1\},{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(f=1)=\{v\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\,|\,f(v)=1\}
, etc.
The main result of the paper is the following theorem which gives a classification
of solutions of Eq.(
1.4
) with (
1.5
).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
n\geq 2
. Let
f
be a solution of Eq.(
1.4
) with (
1.5
) and let

M_{0}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}f(v){\rm d}v,\ \ \ \ M_{2}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}f(v)|v-v_{0}|^{2}{\rm d}v,\ \ \ \ v_{0}=\frac{1}{M_{0}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}f(v)v{\rm d}v.

Then
f
is classified into (I) and (II) that are characterized as follows:

(I)
f
satisfies one of the following three conditions that are equivalent to each other:

\displaystyle S(f)>0\quad{\rm i.e.}\quad mes({{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(0<f<1))>0.

(1.6)

\displaystyle\frac{M_{2}}{(M_{0})^{(n+2)/n}}>\frac{n}{n+2}\bigg(\frac{n}{|{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}|}\bigg)^{2/n}\,.

(1.7)

\displaystyle f(v)=\frac{ae^{-b|v-v_{0}|^{2}}}{1+ae^{-b|v-v_{0}|^{2}}}\qquad{\rm a.e.}\quad v\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}

(1.8)

for some constants
0<a,b<\infty
.

(II)
f
satisfies one of the following three conditions that are equivalent to each other:

\displaystyle S(f)=0\quad{\rm i.e.}\quad mes({{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(0<f<1))=0.

(1.9)

\displaystyle\frac{M_{2}}{(M_{0})^{(n+2)/n}}=\frac{n}{n+2}\bigg(\frac{n}{|{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}|}\bigg)^{2/n}\,.

(1.10)

\displaystyle f(v)={\bf 1}_{\{|v-v_{0}|\leq R\}}\qquad{\rm a.e.}\quad v\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}

(1.11)

for some constant
0<R<\infty
.

For
n=3
, the classification given in Theorem
1.1
has been proven in
[
7
]
where the inequality (
1.7
) and the equality
(
1.10
) are written in terms of the kinetic temperature
T>0
and the critical kinetic temperature

T_{F}>0
(the Fermi-temperature) as follows:

T/T_{F}>2/5\qquad{\rm and}\qquad T/T_{F}=2/5.

Theorem
1.1
now shows that the same classification holds also true for all
n\geq 2
. In particular it holds true for
n=2
, which was once the most difficult case. The method of proof of Theorem
1.1
is similar to that of
[
7
]
where
the key step is to deduce a characterization of Euclidean balls for
n\geq 3
.
Now since a recent work (see Appendix) proves that the same characterization of Euclidean balls
holds also true for all
n\geq 2
, it follows that Theorem
1.1
holds true. For completeness we give here a proof of Theorem
1.1
. To do this we first collect and prove some lemmas in Section 2, and then in Section 3 we prove Theorem
1.1
. The characterization of Euclidean balls mentioned above
and its proof are put in the Appendix.

2
Some Lemmas

Lemma 2.1
.

(
[
7
]
) Given constants
0<p<q<\infty
. Let

\phi
be measurable on
[0,\infty)

with
0\leq\phi\leq 1
on
[0,\infty)
and
0<\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{q-1}\phi(r){\rm d}r<\infty.

Then

\bigg(p\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{p-1}\phi(r){\rm d}r\bigg)^{1/p}\leq\bigg(q\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{q-1}\phi(r){\rm d}r\bigg)^{1/q}

and the equality holds if and only if
there is a constant
0<R<\infty
such that
\phi={\bf 1}_{[0,R]}
a.e. on
[0,\infty).

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
f\in L^{1}_{2}({{\mathbb{R}}^{n}})
satisfy
\|f\|_{L^{1}_{2}}\neq 0
and

0\leq f\leq 1
on
{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}
, and let

M_{0}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}f(v){\rm d}v,\ \ \ \ M_{2}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}f(v)|v-v_{0}|^{2}{\rm d}v,\ \ \ \ v_{0}=\frac{1}{M_{0}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}f(v)v{\rm d}v.

Then

\frac{M_{2}}{(M_{0})^{(n+2)/n}}\geq\frac{n}{n+2}\bigg(\frac{n}{|{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}|}\bigg)^{2/n}

(2.1)

and the equality holds if and only if
f(v)={\bf 1}_{\{|v-v_{0}|\leq R\}}
for some
constant
0<R<\infty
.

Proof.

Let

{\bar{f}}(r)=\frac{1}{|{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}|}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}f(v_{0}+r\sigma){\rm d}\sigma.

Then
0\leq\bar{f}(r)\leq 1
for all
r\in[0,\infty)
and
(
2.1
) is equivalent to the inequality

\bigg((n+2)\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{n+1}{\bar{f}}(r){\rm d}r\bigg)^{1/(n+2)}\geq\bigg(n\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{n-1}{\bar{f}}(r){\rm d}r\bigg)^{1/n}

which does hold by Lemma
2.1
. Also,
since
0\leq f\leq 1
on
{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}
, it is easily seen that the two equalities

{\bar{f}}(r)={\bf 1}_{[0,R]}(r)
a.e.
r\in[0,\infty)

and

f(v)={\bf 1}_{\{|v-v_{0}|\leq R\}}
a.e.
v\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}
are
equivalent. This proves the lemma.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
M_{0}>0,M_{2}>0,
and
v_{0}\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}.

Then there exists a unique Fermi-Dirac distribution

{F}_{a,b}(v)=\frac{ae^{-b|v-v_{0}|^{2}}}{1+ae^{-b|v-v_{0}|^{2}}}

with the coefficients

a>0,b>0
such that

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}F_{a,b}(v){\rm d}v=M_{0},\quad\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}F_{a,b}(v)|v-v_{0}|^{2}{\rm d}v=M_{2}

(2.2)

if and only if

M_{0},M_{2}
satisfy the strict inequality

\frac{M_{2}}{(M_{0})^{(n+2)/n}}>\frac{n}{n+2}\bigg(\frac{n}{|{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}|}\bigg)^{2/n}.

(2.3)

Proof.

Let

I_{s}(t)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{r^{s}}{1+te^{r^{2}}}{\rm d}r,\quad t>0,\quad s\geq 0.

For any
k\geq 0
we compute

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}F_{a,b}(v)|v-v_{0}|^{k}{\rm d}v=|{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}|\big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{b}}\big)^{n+k}I_{n-1+k}(1/a).

Then (
2.2
) is rewritten

|{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}|\big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{b}}\big)^{n}I_{n-1}(1/a)=M_{0},\quad|{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}|\big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{b}}\big)^{n+2}I_{n+1}(1/a)=M_{2}.

(2.4)

Let

P(t)=\frac{I_{n+1}(t)}{\big(I_{n-1}(t)\big)^{\frac{n+2}{n}}},\quad t>0.

Then (
2.4
) is equivalent to

\frac{1}{|{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}|^{2/n}}P(1/a)=\frac{M_{2}}{(M_{0})^{(n+2)/n}},\quad b=\Big(\frac{I_{n-1}(1/a)|{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}|}{M_{0}}\Big)^{2/n}.

(2.5)

We now prove that

\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}P(t)>0\quad\forall\,t>0;\quad\lim\limits_{t\to 0+}P(t)=\frac{n^{(n+2)/n}}{n+2},\quad\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}P(t)=\infty.

(2.6)

Compute

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}P(t)=\big(\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}I_{n+1}(t)\big)\big(I_{n-1}(t)\big)^{-\frac{n+2}{n}}+I_{n+1}(t)(-\frac{n+2}{n})\big(I_{n-1}(t)\big)^{-\frac{n+2}{n}-1}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}I_{n-1}(t),

\displaystyle-\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}I_{s}(t)=J_{s}(t):=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{r^{s}e^{r^{2}}}{(1+te^{r^{2}})^{2}}{\rm d}r,\quad t>0,\quad s\geq 0.

And integration by parts gives

\displaystyle I_{s}(t)=\frac{2t}{s+1}J_{s+2}(t),\quad t>0,\quad s\geq 0.

So

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}P(t)=\big(\frac{2t}{n}\big)^{-\frac{n+2}{n}}\big(J_{n+1}(t)\big)^{-\frac{n+2}{n}-1}\big(J_{n-1}(t)J_{n+3}(t)-[J_{n+1}(t)]^{2}\big),\quad t>0.

Since by Cauchy-Schwarz inequality

[J_{n+1}(t)]^{2}<J_{n-1}(t)J_{n+3}(t)\quad\forall\,t>0

it follows that
\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}P(t)>0
for all
t>0
.

In order to prove the first limit in (
2.6
), we let

t=e^{-\rho},\rho>0
, and compute

\displaystyle I_{s}(e^{-\rho})=\frac{\rho^{\frac{s+1}{2}}}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{u^{\frac{s-1}{2}}}{1+e^{\rho(u-1)}}{\rm d}u,\quad s\geq 0,

\displaystyle P(e^{-\rho})=\frac{I_{n+1}(e^{-\rho})}{\big(I_{n-1}(e^{-\rho})\big)^{\frac{n+2}{n}}}=2^{\frac{2}{n}}\frac{K_{n}(\rho)}{\big(K_{n-2}(\rho)\big)^{\frac{n+2}{n}}}

where

K_{s}(\rho)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{u^{\frac{s}{2}}}{1+e^{\rho(u-1)}}{\rm d}u,\quad s\geq 0.

Using Lebesgue dominated convergence we have

\displaystyle K_{s}(\rho)=\int_{[0,1)}\frac{u^{\frac{s}{2}}}{1+e^{\rho(u-1)}}{\rm d}u+\int_{(1,\infty)}\frac{u^{\frac{s}{2}}}{1+e^{\rho(u-1)}}{\rm d}u\to\int_{[0,1)}u^{\frac{s}{2}}{\rm d}u=\frac{2}{s+2}\quad(\rho\to\infty).

So

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{t\to 0+}P(t)=\lim_{\rho\to\infty}P(e^{-\rho})=2^{\frac{2}{n}}\frac{\frac{2}{n+2}}{\big(\frac{2}{n}\big)^{\frac{n+2}{n}}}=\frac{n^{\frac{n+2}{n}}}{n+2}

i.e.

\frac{1}{|{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}|^{2/n}}P(0+)=\lim_{\rho\to\infty}\frac{1}{|{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}|^{2/n}}P(e^{-\rho})=\frac{n}{n+2}\bigg(\frac{n}{|{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}|}\bigg)^{2/n}.

And for all
t>1
we have

\displaystyle P(t)\geq t^{\frac{2}{n}}\frac{\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{r^{n-1}}{1+e^{r^{2}}}{\rm d}r}{\big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{r^{n-1}}{e^{r^{2}}}{\rm d}r\big)^{\frac{n+2}{n}}}\to\infty\quad(t\to\infty).

So

P(\infty)=\infty
.

Now suppose that (
2.2
) or equivalently (
2.5
) has a solution
a>0,b>0
. Then

\frac{M_{2}}{(M_{0})^{(n+2)/n}}=\frac{1}{|{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}|^{2/n}}P(1/a)>\frac{1}{|{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}|^{2/n}}P(0+)=\frac{n}{n+2}\bigg(\frac{n}{|{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}|}\bigg)^{2/n}.

Conversely if
M_{0},M_{2}
satisfy the inequality (
2.3
), then

\frac{1}{|{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}|^{2/n}}P(0+)<\frac{M_{2}}{(M_{0})^{(n+2)/n}}<\infty=\frac{1}{|{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}|^{2/n}}P(\infty)

and so there is a unique
a>0
such that

\frac{1}{|{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}|^{2/n}}P(1/a)=\frac{M_{2}}{(M_{0})^{(n+2)/n}}.

With this unique
a>0
, the equation (
2.5
), or equivalently, the equation
(
2.2
), has a unique solution
a>0,b>0
.

Remark.
If
f
in Lemma
2.2

is an indicator function
f(v)={\bf 1}_{E}(v)

of a measurable set
E\subset{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}
which is essentially not a ball, i.e.

mes\big((E\setminus B)\cup(B\setminus E)\big)>0
for every ball
B\subset{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}
, then according
to Lemma
2.2
the strict inequality (
2.3
) holds for this
f={\bf 1}_{E}
.
This shows that Lemma
2.2
and Lemma
2.3
are far from enough
to prove the classification of solutions of Eq.(
1.4
) with (

).

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
b(t),\Psi(r),f(v)
be nonnegative Borel functions on
[0,1],[0,\infty)
and

{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}
respectively, and let
{\bf n}=(v-v_{*})/|v-v_{*}|
. Then

\displaystyle\int\!\!\!\!\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}}b(|\langle{\bf n},\sigma\rangle|)\Psi(|v-v_{*}|)f(v^{\prime}){\rm d}v_{*}{\rm d}\sigma

\displaystyle=\int\!\!\!\!\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}}b(|\langle{\bf n},\sigma\rangle|)\Psi(|v-v_{*}|)f(v_{*}^{\prime}){\rm d}v_{*}{\rm d}\sigma

\displaystyle=2^{n-1}|{{\mathbb{S}}}^{n-2}|\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\frac{\sin^{n-2}(\theta)}{\cos^{2}(\theta)}b(|\cos(2\theta)|)\Big(\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}}\Psi\big(\frac{|v-v_{*}|}{\cos(\theta)}\big)f(v_{*}){\rm d}v_{*}\Big){\rm d}\theta.

Here for the case
n=2
, we define
|{{\mathbb{S}}}^{0}|=2
.

Proof.

The first equality follows from the definition of
v^{\prime},v_{*}^{\prime}
.
The second equality is a special case of Lemma
4.4
in the Appendix by taking
(for every fixed
v\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}
)
F(\langle{{\bf n},\sigma}\rangle,v_{*}^{\prime},v_{*})=b(|\langle{{\bf n},\sigma\rangle}|)\Psi(|v-v_{*}|)f(v_{*}^{\prime})
and noting that
if
\tilde{\sigma}\in{{\mathbb{S}}}^{n-1}
satisfies
\tilde{\sigma}\bot{\bf n}
,
then
|v-v_{*}-|v-v_{*}|\tan(\theta)\tilde{\sigma}|\\
=|v-v_{*}||{\bf n}-\tan(\theta)\tilde{\sigma}|=|v-v_{*}|/\cos(\theta)
.

Note.
Since the Lebesgue measure is regular, when dealing with
measurability problems for functions in
L^{1}({{\mathbb{R}}^{n}})
,
we may automatically assume that they are all Borel functions on
{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}
.
In other words, for notational
convenience we will do not distinguish between two measurable functions that differ by a null set.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
b(t)=1-t^{2},t\in[0,1];{\bf n}=(v-v_{*})/|v-v_{*}|
.
For any function
f\in L^{1}({{\mathbb{R}}^{n}})
satisfying
0\leq f\leq 1
on
{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}
, define

\displaystyle{\cal I}_{f}(v)=\int\!\!\!\!\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}b(|\langle{\bf n},\sigma\rangle|)f(v^{\prime})f(v_{*}^{\prime})(1-f(v_{*})){\rm d}v_{*}{\rm d}\sigma,\quad v\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}

\displaystyle{\cal J}_{f}(v)=\int\!\!\!\!\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}b(|\langle{\bf n},\sigma\rangle|)f(v_{*})(1-f(v^{\prime}))(1-f(v_{*}^{\prime})){\rm d}v_{*}{\rm d}\sigma,\quad v\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}.

Then
{\cal I}_{f},{\cal J}_{f}
are well-defined and have the following properties:

(a). If
g\in L^{1}({{\mathbb{R}}^{n}})
satisfying
0\leq g\leq 1
on
{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}
, then

|{\cal I}_{f}(v)-{\cal I}_{g}(v)|,\ \ |{\cal J}_{f}(v)-{\cal J}_{g}(v)|\leq C\|f-g\|_{L^{1}}\quad\forall\,v\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}

where
C=(2^{n+2}+2)|{{\mathbb{S}}}^{n-2}|\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\sin^{n}(\theta){\rm d}\theta
.
In particular, if
f=g
a.e. on
{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}
, then
{\cal I}_{f}\equiv{\cal I}_{g}
,

{\cal J}_{f}\equiv{\cal J}_{g}

on
{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}
.

(b).
{\cal I}_{f}
,
{\cal J}_{f}
are continuous on
{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}
.

(c). If
mes({{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(0<f<1))>0
, then
{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}({\cal I}_{f}>0)\cap{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}({\cal J}_{f}>0)
is non-empty.

Proof.

The proof of that
{\cal I}_{f},{\cal J}_{f}
are well-defined on
{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}

is included in the proof of property (a): choose
g=0
in property (a) we have

{\cal I}_{g}=0
,
{\cal J}_{g}=0
on
{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}
and so
0\leq{\cal I}_{f}(v),{\cal J}_{f}(v)\leq C\|f\|_{L^{1}}
for all
v\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}
.

Proof of (a): Using Lemma
2.4
and
b(|\cos(2\theta)|)=\sin^{2}(2\theta)
we have

\displaystyle|{\cal I}_{f}(v)-{\cal I}_{g}(v)|,\ \ |{\cal J}_{f}(v)-{\cal J}_{g}(v)|

\displaystyle\leq\int\!\!\!\!\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}b(|\langle{\bf n},\sigma\rangle|)\big[|(f-g)(v^{\prime})|+|(f-g)(v_{*}^{\prime})|+|(f-g)(v_{*})|\big]{\rm d}v_{*}{\rm d}\sigma

\displaystyle\leq C\|f-g\|_{L^{1}}\qquad\forall\ v\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}.

Proof of (b): Denote

f_{h}(v)=f(v+h)
. We have

{\cal I}_{f}(v+h)={\cal I}_{f_{h}}(v),{\cal J}_{f}(v+h)={\cal J}_{f_{h}}(v)
and it follows from
property (a) that

|{\cal I}_{f}(v+h)-{\cal I}_{f}(v)|,\ \ |{\cal J}_{f}(v+h)-{\cal J}_{f}(v)|\leq C\|f_{h}-f\|_{L^{1}}\quad\forall\,v,h\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}.

So
{\cal I}_{f}
,
{\cal J}_{f}
are continuous on
{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}
.

Proof of (c): Suppose
mes({{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}(0<f<1))>0
.
Then there is a Lebesgue point
v\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}
of
f
satisfying

0<f(v)<1
. Let
B_{r}(v)
denote a closed ball in
{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}
with center
v
and radius

4.1.
Let

denote by

and

the interior, boundary and

respectively.
It is also denoted by

of a Lebesgue measurable set

.
An
n
-dimensional convex body in
{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}
is defined as a compact convex set
in
{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}
with non-empty interior. Throughout this Appendix we assume that

.

It is well-known that the Euclidean ball can be characterized by
convex bodies of constant width. For instance a result of L.Montejano
[
10
]

(see also
[
9
]
)
states that

through a point

is a convex body of constant width,
then

is a Euclidean
n
-ball.

A closely related and perhaps more intuitive characterization of balls is as follows which is the first main result of the Appendix:

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
n\geq 2,
let
K\subset{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}
be a compact set having at least two elements and
satisfying the following condition

\forall\,x,y\in\partial K,\,\,\forall\,\sigma\in{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}\Longrightarrow\,{\rm either}\,\,\,\frac{x+y}{2}+\frac{|x-y|}{2}\sigma\in K\,\,\,{\rm or}\,\,\,\frac{x+y}{2}-\frac{|x-y|}{2}\sigma\in K.

(4.1)

Then
K
is an
n
-dimensional Euclidean ball.

The condition (
4.1
) means that for every pair
\{x,y\}\subset\partial K
, the
sphere with the centre
\frac{1}{2}(x+y)
and radius
\frac{1}{2}|x-y|
has the property that
every pair
\{\frac{1}{2}(x+y)+\frac{1}{2}|x-y|\sigma,\,\frac{1}{2}(x+y)-\frac{1}{2}|x-y|\sigma\}
of antipodal points of the sphere meets

. The necessity of the condition (

) is obvious, which is due to the identity

\Big|\frac{x+y}{2}+\frac{|x-y|}{2}\sigma-x_{0}\Big|^{2}+\Big|\frac{x+y}{2}-\frac{|x-y|}{2}\sigma-x_{0}\Big|^{2}=|x-x_{0}|^{2}+|y-x_{0}|^{2}

for all

and all

, from which
one sees that for any

the ball

satisfies the condition (
4.1
).

The Reuleaux triangle
K_{\rm R}\subset{{\mathbb{R}}}^{2}
, which is a typical example of convex body of constant width which is not a disk, does not satisfies
(
4.1

lie in a symmetric axis of

with

(diameter of

), and consider the middle point

and

be perpendicular to

.

a\wedge b=\min\{a,b\},\,a\vee b=\max\{a,b\},\,(t)_{+}=t\vee 0
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
F,f
be nonnegative Borel functions on
[-1,1]\times{{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\times{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}}
and

{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}
respectively. Then

\displaystyle\int\!\!\!\!\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}}F\big(\langle{\bf n},\sigma\rangle,v_{*}^{\prime},v_{*}\big){\rm d}v_{*}{\rm d}\sigma

\displaystyle=2^{n-1}\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}}{\rm d}v_{*}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\frac{\sin^{n-2}(\theta)}{\cos^{2}(\theta)}{\rm d}\theta\int_{{{\mathbb{S}}}^{n-2}({\bf n})}F\big(\cos(2\theta),\,v_{*},\,v_{*}+|v-v_{*}|\tan(\theta){\tilde{\sigma}}\big){\rm d}{\tilde{\sigma}},\qquad

(4.8)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r^{n}}\int_{B_{r}(v)}f(v_{*}){\rm d}v_{*}

\displaystyle\geq\int\!\!\!\!\int_{{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}\times{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}}\frac{\sqrt{1-\langle\omega,\sigma\rangle^{2}}}{2^{n+1}|{{\mathbb{S}}}^{n-2}|}\Big(f\big(v+\frac{r}{2}(\omega+\sigma)\big)+f\big(v+\frac{r}{2}(\omega-\sigma)\big)\Big){\rm d}\sigma{\rm d}\omega

(4.9)

for all
v\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}
and all
r>0
, where
{\bf n}=(v-v_{*})/|v-v_{*}|,v_{*}^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2}(v+v_{*})-\frac{1}{2}|v-v_{*}|\sigma
.

Proof.

Making change of variables
v_{*}=v-z,z=r\omega\,\Longrightarrow\,{\rm d}v_{*}=r^{n-1}{\rm d}r{\rm d}\omega
, then letting
\omega=\cos(\theta)\sigma+\sin(\theta)\tilde{\sigma},\tilde{\sigma}\in{{\mathbb{S}}}^{n-2}(\sigma)\,\Longrightarrow\,{\rm d}\omega=\sin^{n-2}(\theta){\rm d}\theta{\rm d}\tilde{\sigma}
, and then changing
\theta\to 2\theta
,

r\to\frac{r}{\cos(\theta)}
, we compute

\displaystyle\int\!\!\!\!\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}}F\big(\langle{\bf n},\sigma\rangle,v_{*}^{\prime},v_{*}\big){\rm d}v_{*}{\rm d}\sigma=2^{n-1}\int_{{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}}{\rm d}\sigma\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{n-1}{\rm d}r\int_{0}^{\pi}{\bf 1}_{\{\cos(\theta)>0\}}\frac{\sin^{n-2}(\theta)}{\cos^{2}(\theta)}{\rm d}\theta

\displaystyle\times\int_{{{\mathbb{S}}}^{n-2}(\sigma)}F\Big(\cos(2\theta),v-r\big(\cos(\theta)\sigma+\sin(\theta)\tilde{\sigma}\big),v+\frac{r}{\cos(\theta)}\sigma-2r\big(\cos(\theta)\sigma+\sin(\theta)\tilde{\sigma}\big)\Big){\rm d}\tilde{\sigma}

\displaystyle=2^{n-1}\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}}{\rm d}v_{*}\int_{{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}}{\bf 1}_{\{\langle{\bf n},\sigma\rangle>0\}}\frac{1}{\langle{\bf n},\sigma\rangle^{2}}F\Big(2\langle{\bf n},\sigma\rangle^{2}-1,v_{*},v+\frac{|v-v_{*}|}{\langle{\bf n},\sigma\rangle}\sigma-2(v-v_{*})\Big){\rm d}\sigma

\displaystyle=2^{n-1}\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}}{\rm d}v_{*}\int_{0}^{\pi}{\bf 1}_{\{\cos(\theta)>0\}}\frac{\sin^{n-2}(\theta)}{\cos^{2}(\theta)}{\rm d}\theta\int_{{{\mathbb{S}}}^{n-2}({\bf n})}F\big(\cos(2\theta),v_{*},v_{*}+|v-v_{*}|\tan(\theta)\tilde{\sigma}\big){\rm d}\tilde{\sigma}

which is equal to the right hand side of (
4.8
).

To prove (
4.9
), we first prove that
for any nonnegative Borel measurable functions
h,g
on
(0,1)

and
{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}

respectively, it holds

\displaystyle\int_{{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}}|\langle\omega,\sigma\rangle|^{n-2}h(|\langle\omega,\sigma\rangle|)g\big(\omega-2\langle\omega,\sigma\rangle\sigma\big){\rm d}\sigma

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{n-1}}\int_{{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}}\Big(h(\sin(\theta/2))g(\sigma)+h(\cos(\theta/2))g(-\sigma)\Big)\Big|_{\theta=\arccos(\langle\omega,\sigma\rangle)}{\rm d}\sigma.

(4.10)

To do this we observe that the mapping
\sigma\mapsto\langle\omega,\sigma\rangle\sigma
is even and so using the formula (
4.5
) we compute

\displaystyle\int_{{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}}|\langle\omega,\sigma\rangle|^{n-2}h(|\langle\omega,\sigma\rangle|)g\big(\omega-2\langle\omega,\sigma\rangle\sigma\big){\rm d}\sigma=2\int_{{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}}{\bf 1}_{\{\langle\omega,\sigma\rangle>0\}}\{\cdots\}{\rm d}\sigma

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\sin^{n-2}(\theta)\cos^{n-2}(\theta)h(\cos(\theta)){\rm d}\theta\int_{{{\mathbb{S}}}^{n-2}(\omega)}g\big(-\cos(2\theta)\omega-\sin(2\theta)\tilde{\sigma}\big)\big){\rm d}\tilde{\sigma}.

Then changing variable
\theta\to\theta/2
and using the identity

\int_{{{\mathbb{S}}}^{n-1}}\varphi(-\sigma){\rm d}\sigma=\int_{{{\mathbb{S}}}^{n-1}}\varphi(\sigma){\rm d}\sigma
it is
easily shown that (
4.10
) holds true. From (
4.10
)
we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}}|\langle\omega,\sigma\rangle|^{n-2}h(|\langle\omega,\sigma\rangle|)g\big(\omega-2\langle\omega,\sigma\rangle\sigma\big){\rm d}\sigma

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2^{n-1}}\int_{{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}}\big[h(\sin(\theta/2))\wedge h(\cos(\theta/2))\big]\big(g(\sigma)+g(-\sigma)\big)\Big|_{\theta=\arccos(\langle\omega,\sigma\rangle)}{\rm d}\sigma.

(4.11)

Now we choose
h(t)=t/(\sqrt{1-t^{2}})^{(n-3)}=t^{n-2}/(t\sqrt{1-t^{2}})^{n-3},t\in(0,1)
, and write

v+r\langle\omega,\sigma\rangle\sigma=v+\frac{r}{2}\big(\omega-(\omega-2\langle\omega,\sigma\rangle\sigma)\big).

Then we deduce from (
4.11
) and
\cos(\theta)=\langle\omega,\sigma\rangle,\theta\in[0,\pi],
that

\displaystyle\int_{{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}}\frac{|\langle\omega,\sigma\rangle|^{n-1}}{\big(\sqrt{1-\langle\omega,\sigma\rangle^{2}\,}\big)^{n-3}}f\big(v+r\langle\omega,\sigma\rangle\sigma\big){\rm d}\sigma

\displaystyle\geq\int_{{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}}\frac{\sin^{n-2}(\theta/2)\wedge\cos^{n-2}(\theta/2)}{2^{n-1}\sin^{n-3}(\theta/2)\cos^{n-3}(\theta/2)}\Big(f\big(v+\frac{r}{2}(\omega+\sigma)\big)+f\big(v+\frac{r}{2}(\omega-\sigma)\big)\Big){\rm d}\sigma

\displaystyle\geq\int_{{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}}\frac{\sin(\theta)}{2^{n}}\Big(f\big(v+\frac{r}{2}(\omega+\sigma)\big)+f\big(v+\frac{r}{2}(\omega-\sigma)\big)\Big){\rm d}\sigma.

On the other hand, taking integration for
\omega\in{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}

and noting again that
\sigma\mapsto\langle\omega,\sigma\rangle\sigma
is even, we compute using
the formula (
4.5
) that

\displaystyle\int\!\!\!\!\int_{{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}\times{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}}\frac{|\langle\omega,\sigma\rangle|^{n-1}}{\big(\sqrt{1-\langle\omega,\sigma\rangle^{2}\,}\big)^{n-3}}f\big(v+r\langle\omega,\sigma\rangle\sigma\big){\rm d}\sigma{\rm d}\omega=2\int\!\!\!\!\int_{{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}\times{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}}{\bf 1}_{\{\langle\omega,\sigma\rangle>0\}}\{\cdots\}{\rm d}\sigma{\rm d}\omega

\displaystyle=2|{{\mathbb{S}}}^{n-2}|\int_{{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}}{\rm d}\sigma\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\sin(\theta)\cos^{n-1}(\theta)f\big(v+r\cos(\theta)\sigma\big){\rm d}\theta=\frac{2|{{\mathbb{S}}}^{n-2}|}{r^{n}}\int_{B_{r}(v)}f(v_{*}){\rm d}v_{*}.

This gives (
4.9
).

As mentioned above, in this Appendix we use
|S|
to denote the

n-1
-dimensional Hausdorff measure of a Hausdorff measurable subset

S\subset{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}
. When dealing with measure and measurability, two things may be reminded:

(1)
If a Lebesgue measurable set
Z\subset{{\mathbb{R}}}^{N}
, a Hausdorff measurable set
S\subset{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}
, etc.,
satisfy
mes(Z)=0
,
|S|=0
, etc., then they are sometimes simply called

null
sets.

(2)
Since for instance the Lebesgue measure is regular, a Lebesgue measurable set (with finite measure) under consideration can be replaced by one of its Borel subset with the same measure
when dealing with measurability on lower dimensional sets. In other words, for notational convenience
we will do not distinguish between two measurable sets that differ by a null set.

For any Borel set
E\subset{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}
and
v,v_{*}\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}
, we define a Borel subset of
{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}
by

{{\mathbb{S}}}^{n-1}_{v,v_{*}}(E)=\Big\{\sigma\in{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}\,\,\big|\,\,\,\frac{v+v_{*}}{2}+\frac{|v-v_{*}|}{2}\sigma\in E\,\,\,\,{\rm or}\,\,\,\,\frac{v+v_{*}}{2}-\frac{|v-v_{*}|}{2}\sigma\in E\Big\}.

For the set
E
in Theorem
4.2
, after a modification on a null set we may assume that

E
is a Borel set, so that
the condition (
4.3
) is equivalent to

|{{\mathbb{S}}}^{n-1}_{v,v_{*}}(E)|=|{{\mathbb{S}}}^{n-1}|
for almost all
(v,v_{*})\in E\times E
. The following lemma deals with a general case; it also shortens the proof of Theorem
4.2
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
E\subset{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}
be a Borel set with
0<mes(E)<\infty
satisfying for some
constant
0<\lambda\leq 1

|{{\mathbb{S}}}^{n-1}_{v,v_{*}}(E)|\geq\lambda|{{\mathbb{S}}}^{n-1}|\qquad\,\,\,\,{\rm a.e.}\quad(v,v_{*})\in E\times E.

(4.12)

Then there exists a subset
Z_{0}\subset E
with
mes(Z_{0})=0

such that
E\setminus Z_{0}
is a bounded set and the compact set
K=\overline{E\setminus Z_{0}}
satisfies

mes(K^{\circ}\setminus E)=0
and

|{{\mathbb{S}}}^{n-1}_{v,v_{*}}(K)|\geq\lambda|{{\mathbb{S}}}^{n-1}|\qquad\forall\,(v,v_{*})\in K\times K.

(4.13)

Furthermore if
mes(\partial K)=0
, then

mes\big((K\setminus E)\cup(E\setminus K)\big)=0
.

Remarks.
(1) It is not clear whether (
4.13
) already
implies
mes\big((K\setminus E)\cup(E\setminus K)\big)=0
.

(2) There are many compact but non-convex sets
K
that
satisfy (
4.13
) for
0<\lambda<1
with
\lambda\approx 1
. A typical example is the
annulus

K_{\varepsilon}=\{x\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\,|\,\varepsilon\leq|x|\leq 1\},0<\varepsilon<1.

It is easily proven that
K_{\varepsilon}
satisfies (
4.13
) with

\lambda\geq\int_{0}^{\frac{1-\varepsilon^{2}}{1+\varepsilon^{2}}}(1-t^{2})^{\frac{n-3}{2}}{\rm d}t\Big/\int_{0}^{1}(1-t^{2})^{\frac{n-3}{2}}{\rm d}t\to 1\quad(\varepsilon\to 0).

Proof of Lemma

4.5
.

Step1
. We first prove that for any Borel subset
\widetilde{E}\subset E
satisfying

mes(E\setminus\widetilde{E})=0
, the set
\widetilde{E}
also satisfies condition
(
4.12
).
Using the notation (
4.2
) we have

{\bf 1}_{{{\mathbb{S}}}^{n-1}_{v,v_{*}}(E)}(\sigma)={\bf 1}_{E}(v^{\prime})\vee{\bf 1}_{E}(v_{*}^{\prime})\qquad\forall\,(v,v_{*},\sigma)\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\times{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\times{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}.

(4.14)

So
|{{\mathbb{S}}}^{n-1}_{v,v_{*}}(E)|=\int_{{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}}\big({\bf 1}_{E}(v^{\prime})\vee{\bf 1}_{E}(v_{*}^{\prime})\big){\rm d}\sigma
and so the condition
(
4.12
) is equivalent to

\int\!\!\!\!\int_{E\times E}\left(\lambda|{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}|-\int_{{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}}\big({\bf 1}_{E}(v^{\prime})\vee{\bf 1}_{E}(v_{*}^{\prime})\big){\rm d}\sigma\right)_{+}{\rm d}v{\rm d}v_{*}=0.

(4.15)

Take any Borel subset
\widetilde{E}\subset E
satisfying

mes(E\setminus\widetilde{E})=0
. Let
Z=E\setminus\widetilde{E}
, then

{\bf 1}_{E}(v^{\prime})\leq{\bf 1}_{\widetilde{E}}(v^{\prime})+{\bf 1}_{Z}(v^{\prime})
, etc. so that

{\bf 1}_{E}(v^{\prime})\vee{\bf 1}_{E}(v_{*}^{\prime})\leq{\bf 1}_{\widetilde{E}}(v^{\prime})\vee{\bf 1}_{\widetilde{E}}(v_{*}^{\prime})+{\bf 1}_{Z}(v^{\prime})\vee{\bf 1}_{Z}(v_{*}^{\prime})

and so

\lambda|{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}|-\int_{{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}}\big({\bf 1}_{\widetilde{E}}(v^{\prime})\vee{\bf 1}_{\widetilde{E}}(v_{*}^{\prime})\big){\rm d}\sigma\leq\lambda|{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}|-\int_{{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}}\big({\bf 1}_{E}(v^{\prime})\vee{\bf 1}_{E}(v_{*}^{\prime})\big){\rm d}\sigma+\int_{{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}}\big({\bf 1}_{Z}(v^{\prime})\vee{\bf 1}_{Z}(v_{*}^{\prime})\big){\rm d}\sigma
and so

\displaystyle\left(\lambda|{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}|-\int_{{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}}\big({\bf 1}_{\widetilde{E}}(v^{\prime})\vee{\bf 1}_{\widetilde{E}}(v_{*}^{\prime})\big){\rm d}\sigma\right)_{+}\leq\left(\lambda|{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}|-\int_{{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}}\big({\bf 1}_{E}(v^{\prime})\vee{\bf 1}_{E}(v_{*}^{\prime})\big){\rm d}\sigma\right)_{+}

\displaystyle+\int_{{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}}\big({\bf 1}_{Z}(v^{\prime})\vee{\bf 1}_{Z}(v_{*}^{\prime})\big){\rm d}\sigma.

Then taking integration over
(v,v_{*})\in\widetilde{E}\times\widetilde{E}\subset E\times E
and notice that

\int\!\!\!\!\int\!\!\!\!\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\times{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}}\big({\bf 1}_{Z}(v^{\prime})\vee{\bf 1}_{Z}(v_{*}^{\prime})\big){\rm d}v{\rm d}v_{*}{\rm d}\sigma=|{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}|\int\!\!\!\!\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\times{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}}}\big({\bf 1}_{Z}(v)\vee{\bf 1}_{Z}(v_{*})\big){\rm d}v{\rm d}v_{*}=0

we see that
\widetilde{E}
satisfies (
4.15
), or equivalently,

\widetilde{E}
satisfies (
4.12
).

Let
E_{1}=\{v\in E\,|\,\lim\limits_{r\to 0^{+}}mes(E\cap B_{r}(v))/mes(B_{r}(v))=1\}

be the set of all density points of
E
. By Lebesgue’s density theorem we know that

mes(E\setminus E_{1})=0
. By the regularity of the Lebesgue measure and the property just proved above
we may assume without loss of generality that
E
is a Borel set and every point in

E
is a density point of
E
.
Let

{\cal Z}_{E}=\big\{(v,v_{*})\in E\times E\,\,\,\big|\,\,\,|{{\mathbb{S}}}^{n-1}_{v,v_{*}}(E)|<\lambda|{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}|\big\}.

(4.16)

By assumption (
4.12
),

{\cal Z}_{E}
is a null set in
{{\mathbb{R}}}^{2n}
.
We next prove the following inequalities:

\int_{{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}}\big(1-\langle\omega,\sigma\rangle^{2}\big){\bf 1}_{{{\mathbb{S}}}^{n-1}_{v,v_{*}}(E)}(\sigma){\rm d}\sigma\geq C_{n}|{{\mathbb{S}}}^{n-1}_{v,v_{*}}(E)|^{5}\qquad\forall\,(v,v_{*},\omega)\in{{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\times{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}\qquad

(4.17)

\int_{{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}}\big(1-\langle\omega,\sigma\rangle^{2}\big){\bf 1}_{{{\mathbb{S}}}^{n-1}_{v,v_{*}}(E)}(\sigma){\rm d}\sigma\geq C_{n,\lambda}\qquad\forall\,(v,v_{*})\in(E\times E)\setminus{\cal Z}_{E},\,\,\forall\,\omega\in{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}\quad\qquad

(4.18)

where
C_{n}=\big(|{{\mathbb{S}}}^{n-2}|\int_{0}^{\pi}\sin^{n-5/2}(\theta){\rm d}\theta\big)^{-4},C_{n,\lambda}=C_{n}(\lambda|{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}|)^{5}
.
In fact for any
(v,v_{*},\omega)\in{{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\times{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}
, using
Hölder inequality we have

\displaystyle|{{\mathbb{S}}}^{n-1}_{v,v_{*}}(E)|=\int_{{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}}{\bf 1}_{{{\mathbb{S}}}^{n-1}_{v,v_{*}}(E)}(\sigma){\rm d}\sigma

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}}\big(1-\langle\omega,\sigma\rangle^{2}\big)\,{\bf 1}_{{{\mathbb{S}}}^{n-1}_{v,v_{*}}(E)}(\sigma){\rm d}\sigma\right)^{1/5}\left(\int_{{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}}\big(1-\langle\omega,\sigma\rangle^{2}\big)^{-1/4}{\rm d}\sigma\right)^{4/5}

which gives (
4.17
). The inequality (
4.18
) follows from
(
4.17
) and the definition of
{\cal Z}_{E}
.

Step2.
In this step we prove that there is a null set
Z_{0}\subset E
such that

is a bounded Borel set and the compact set
K=\overline{E\setminus Z_{0}}
satisfies
(
4.13
).

Since the set

{\cal Z}_{E}
defined in (
4.16
)
has measure zero, it follows from Fubini theorem and the regularity of the Lebesgue measure that
there is a null set
Z_{0}\subset E
, such that
E\setminus Z_{0}
is a Borel set and
for every
v\in E\setminus Z_{0}
there is a null set
Z_{v}\subset E
such that
(v,v_{*})\in(E\times E)\setminus{\cal Z}_{E}
for all
v_{*}\in E\setminus Z_{v}
.
Given any
v\in E\setminus Z_{0}
. We now use
Lemma
4.4
to find a relation between
mes(E)
and
|v|
. In the
integral identity (
4.8
) let us choose

F(\langle{\bf n},\sigma\rangle,v_{*}^{\prime},v_{*})=(1-\langle{\bf n},\sigma\rangle^{2}){\bf 1}_{E}(v_{*}^{\prime})(\frac{|v-v_{*}|}{|v_{*}|})^{1/2}
. Then for all
v\in E\setminus Z_{0}

\displaystyle\int\!\!\!\!\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}}(1-\langle{\bf n},\sigma\rangle^{2}){\bf 1}_{E}(v_{*}^{\prime})\Big(\frac{|v-v_{*}|}{|v_{*}|}\Big)^{1/2}{\rm d}v_{*}{\rm d}\sigma

\displaystyle=2^{n+1}\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}}{\bf 1}_{E}(v_{*}){\rm d}v_{*}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\sin^{n}(\theta){\rm d}\theta\int_{{{\mathbb{S}}}^{n-2}({\bf n})}\Big(\frac{|{\bf n}-\tan(\theta){\tilde{\sigma}}|}{\big||v-v_{*}|^{-1}v_{*}+\tan(\theta){\tilde{\sigma}}\big|}\Big)^{1/2}{\rm d}{\tilde{\sigma}}.

\displaystyle\leq 2^{n+1}|{{\mathbb{S}}}^{n-2}|\int_{E}{\rm d}v_{*}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\frac{[\tan(\theta)]^{1/2}}{|a-\tan(\theta)|^{1/2}}{\rm d}\theta\leq C^{*}_{n}mes(E)

\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\frac{[\tan(\theta)]^{1/2}}{|a-\tan(\theta)|^{1/2}}{\rm d}\theta=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{|a-t|^{1/2}}\frac{t^{1/2}}{1+t^{2}}{\rm d}t<7.
On the other hand

4.14

{\bf 1}_{E}(v^{\prime})+{\bf 1}_{E}(v_{*}^{\prime})\geq{\bf 1}_{{{\mathbb{S}}}^{n-1}_{v,v_{*}}(E)}(\sigma)\quad\forall\,(v,v_{*},\sigma)\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\times{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\times{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}.

(4.19)

and so we deduce for all
v\in E\setminus Z_{0}

\displaystyle\int\!\!\!\!\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}}(1-\langle{\bf n},\sigma\rangle^{2}){\bf 1}_{E}(v_{*}^{\prime})\Big(\frac{|v-v_{*}|}{|v_{*}|}\Big)^{1/2}{\rm d}v_{*}{\rm d}\sigma

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}}\Big(\frac{|v-v_{*}|}{|v_{*}|}\Big)^{1/2}{\rm d}v_{*}\int_{{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}}(1-\langle{\bf n},\sigma\rangle^{2})\big({\bf 1}_{E}(v_{*}^{\prime})+{\bf 1}_{E}(v_{*}^{\prime})\big){\rm d}\sigma

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{E}\Big(\frac{|v-v_{*}|}{|v_{*}|}\Big)^{1/2}{\rm d}v_{*}\int_{{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}}(1-\langle{\bf n},\sigma\rangle^{2}){\bf 1}_{{{\mathbb{S}}}^{n-1}_{v,v_{*}}(E)}(\sigma){\rm d}\sigma\geq\frac{1}{2}C_{n,\lambda}\int_{E}\frac{|v-v_{*}|^{1/2}}{|v_{*}|^{1/2}}{\rm d}v_{*}

where we have used (
4.18

Connecting the above estimates we obtain

C^{*}_{n}mes(E)\geq\frac{1}{2}C_{n,\lambda}\int_{E}\frac{|v-v_{*}|^{1/2}}{|v_{*}|^{1/2}}{\rm d}v_{*}\qquad\forall\,v\in E\setminus Z_{0}.

(4.20)

Since

We next prove that the compact set

E

E\setminus Z_{0}
.

[(E\setminus Z_{0})\times(E\setminus Z_{0}))]\setminus{\cal Z}_{E\setminus Z_{0}}

(v,v_{*})\in K\times K

.

{\bf 1}_{{{\mathbb{S}}}_{v,v_{*}}^{n-1}(K)}(\sigma)\geq\limsup_{k\to\infty}{\bf 1}_{{{\mathbb{S}}}_{v_{k},v_{*k}}^{n-1}(E\setminus Z_{0})}(\sigma)\qquad\forall\,\sigma\in{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}

\displaystyle|{{\mathbb{S}}}_{v,v_{*}}^{n-1}(K)|=\int_{{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}}{\bf 1}_{{{\mathbb{S}}}_{v,v_{*}}^{n-1}(K)}(\sigma){\rm d}\sigma\geq\int_{{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}}\limsup_{k\to\infty}{\bf 1}_{{{\mathbb{S}}}_{v_{k},v_{*k}}^{n-1}(E\setminus Z_{0})}(\sigma){\rm d}\sigma

K
satisfies (
4.13
).

Step3.
Prove that
mes(K^{\circ}\setminus E)=0.
We may assume that

is non-empty.
It suffices to prove that there is a

\alpha=\frac{nC_{n,\lambda}}{4^{n+1}|{{\mathbb{S}}}^{n-2}|}\lambda
, such that
for any
v_{0}\in K^{\circ}
and any

satisfying
B_{R}(v_{0})\subset K^{\circ}
we have

mes(E\cap B_{R}(v_{0}))\geq\alpha mes(B_{R}(v_{0})).

(4.21)

In fact if this holds true, then for any
v\in K^{\circ}\setminus E
and
any
r>0

Proof of Theorem

Step1
. Prove that

-dimensional convex body.

Let

. We need to prove that
a_{0}:=\frac{1}{2}(a+b)\in K
.
To do this
we take
\omega\in{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}
satisfying

\langle\omega,a-b\rangle=0
and let

x_{0}=\frac{a+b}{2}+\frac{|a-b|}{2}\omega=a_{0}+\frac{h}{2}\omega,\quad y_{0}=\frac{a+b}{2}-\frac{|a-b|}{2}\omega=a_{0}-\frac{h}{2}\omega.

(4.27)

By condition (
4.1
), one of
x_{0},y_{0}

K
.
By replacing
\omega
with

Let

Let
a_{0}^{*}\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}
satisfy

\frac{1}{2}(a_{0}+a_{0}^{*})=\frac{1}{2}(a+x_{0}^{*})
, i.e.

a_{0}^{*}=a+x_{0}^{*}-a_{0}=a+t_{0}\frac{1}{2}h\omega.

Then using
\omega\,\bot\,a-b
we compute
|a_{0}-a_{0}^{*}|=|a-x_{0}^{*}|=\frac{1}{2}h\sqrt{1+t_{0}^{2}}>0
,
and

.

Then

\displaystyle a_{0}=\frac{a+x_{0}^{*}}{2}+\frac{|a-x_{0}^{*}|}{2}\sigma,\quad a_{0}^{*}=\frac{a+x_{0}^{*}}{2}-\frac{|a-x_{0}^{*}|}{2}\sigma.

Since

Replacing

{\rm conv}(\{a_{0},a_{1},...,a_{n}\})

n
-dimensional convex body. This implies

({\rm conv}(K))^{\circ}\supset({\rm conv}(\{a_{0},a_{1},...,a_{n}\}))^{\circ}\neq\emptyset

Next we prove that

Let

From

.

a_{0}\in(\partial B_{r})\cap\partial K,b_{0}\in(\partial B_{R})\cap\partial K

|a_{0}-b_{0}|={\rm dist}\big((\partial B_{r})\cap\partial K,(\partial B_{R})\cap\partial K\big).

(4.33)

It is easily seen that

\langle a_{0},b_{0}\rangle\geq 0.

(4.34)

In fact, applying Lemma
4.3
to the set
K
and
the unit vector
{\bf n}=b_{0}/|b_{0}|
we have

(\partial B_{r})_{\bf n}^{+}\cap\partial K\neq\emptyset
.
Take
x_{0}\in(\partial B_{r})_{\bf n}^{+}\cap\partial K
. Then

\langle x_{0},{\bf n}\rangle\geq 0
, i.e.

\langle x_{0},b_{0}\rangle\geq 0
. So

|a_{0}-b_{0}|^{2}\leq|x_{0}-b_{0}|^{2}\leq r^{2}+R^{2}
which implies that
\langle a_{0},b_{0}\rangle\geq 0
.

Step4
. Assuming
n=2
, we
prove that
r=R
, i.e. prove that
K
is a disk. We will use contradiction argument, so
in the following we assume that
r<R
. We will prove that there exists a rectangular

a_{1}a_{2}a_{3}a_{4}
in
K
such that
the three vertices
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}
belong to
\partial K
but the
fourth vertex
a_{4}
belongs to
K^{\circ}
. Then we can deduce a contradiction.

To do this we denote

u(\theta):=(\cos(\theta),\sin(\theta)),\quad\theta\in[0,2\pi].

In the following derivation the equality

\langle u(\theta),u(\vartheta)\rangle=\cos(\theta-\vartheta)\,\,(\forall\,\theta,\vartheta\in[0,2\pi])
will be often used.
And we use notations in
Step3
and recall that
a_{0},b_{0}

are given by (
4.33
) and satisfy (
4.34
).
After making an orthogonal linear transform we can assume that

b_{0}=Ru(\theta_{1}),\,\,\theta_{1}=\pi/2;\quad a_{0}=ru(\theta_{2})\,\,\,{\rm for\,\,some\,\,}\,0\leq\theta_{2}\leq\pi/2.

Then, since
a_{0}\in(\partial B_{r})\cap\partial K,b_{0}\in(\partial B_{R})\cap\partial K
, we know from (
4.32
) that

a_{1}:=-\frac{r}{R}b_{0}=-ru(\theta_{1})\in(\partial B_{r})\cap\partial K,\quad a_{2}:=-\frac{R}{r}a_{0}=-Ru(\theta_{2})\in(\partial B_{R})\cap\partial K.

(4.35)

Let
0<\theta_{0}<\pi/2
be such that
\sin(\theta_{0})=\frac{r}{R}
. Using the inequality in (
4.31
) for
n=2
, i.e.
r\geq(\sqrt{3}-1)R
, we have
\sin(\theta_{0})\geq\sqrt{3}-1>\sqrt{2}/2
and so

\theta_{0}>\pi/4
.

We first prove that the
angular
\theta_{2}
in (
4.35
) satisfies

\pi/6\leq\theta_{2}<\theta_{0}.

(4.36)

To do this let
c_{0}=ru(\theta_{0}).
Then

\langle c_{0},b_{0}-c_{0}\rangle=0
. We show that

c_{0}\in K^{\circ}
. Otherwise,
c_{0}\in\partial K
hence

c_{0}\in(\partial B_{r})\cap\partial K
, and it follows from
the implication relation in (
4.32
) that
-\frac{R}{r}c_{0}\in\partial K
.
Using

c_{0}\,\bot\,b_{0}-c_{0}
we deduce a contradiction:

(r+R)^{2}=({\rm diam}(K))^{2}\geq|b_{0}-(-\frac{R}{r}c_{0})|^{2}=|b_{0}-c_{0}|^{2}+(r+R)^{2}>(r+R)^{2}.
Thus we must have
c_{0}\in K^{\circ}.
From this we see that
there is
\delta>0
small enough such that
c_{0}^{*}:=c+\delta u(\theta_{0})=(r+\delta)u(\theta_{0})\in K
.
Next we show that
\theta_{2}<\theta_{0}
. If
\theta_{2}=\theta_{0}
, then
a_{0}=c_{0}\in K^{\circ}
which contradicts
a_{0}\in\partial K
;
if
\theta_{0}<\theta_{2}<\pi/2
, then
a_{0}\in({\rm conv}(\{0,b_{0},c_{0}^{*}\}))^{\circ}\subset K^{\circ}
,
which also contradicts
a_{0}\in\partial K
; if

\theta_{2}=\pi/2
, then
a_{0}=ru(\pi/2)=\frac{R-r}{R+r}a_{1}+\frac{2r}{R+r}b_{0}\in K^{\circ}
(because
a_{1},b_{0}\in\partial K,a_{1}\neq b_{0},0<r<R,
and
every point in
\partial K
is an extreme point of
K
) which also contradicts
a_{0}\in\partial K
.
Thus we must have
\theta_{2}<\theta_{0}
. In order to prove
\theta_{2}\geq\pi/6
,
we need to prove that

ru(\theta)\in K^{\circ}\qquad\forall\,\theta\in(\theta_{2},\pi-\theta_{2}).

(4.37)

In fact for any
\theta\in(\theta_{2},\pi-\theta_{2})
we have
\sin(\theta)>\sin(\theta_{2})
.
Since
|ru(\theta)-b_{0}|^{2}=|ru(\theta)-Ru(\pi/2)|^{2}=r^{2}+R^{2}-2rR\sin(\theta),|a_{0}-b_{0}|^{2}=|ru(\theta_{2})-Ru(\pi/2)|^{2}=r^{2}+R^{2}-2rR\sin(\theta_{2})
, this implies that

|ru(\theta)-b_{0}|<|a_{0}-b_{0}|
. By the minimality of

|a_{0}-b_{0}|
we conclude that
ru(\theta)\not\in\partial K
and so

ru(\theta)\in K^{\circ}.
This proves (
4.37
).

Now take a sequence
\{\vartheta_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset(\theta_{2},\theta_{0})

satisfying
\vartheta_{k}\to\theta_{2}\,\,(k\to\infty)
.
For every
k\in{{\mathbb{N}}}
, let
{\bf n}_{k}=u(\vartheta_{k}+\pi/2)
.
By Lemma
4.3
,

(\partial B_{r})_{{\bf n}_{k}}^{+}\cap\partial K
is non-empty.
Take
ru(\vartheta_{k}^{*})\in(\partial B_{r})_{{\bf n}_{k}}^{+}\cap\partial K
with

\vartheta_{k}^{*}\in[0,2\pi)
. Then

\langle ru(\vartheta_{k}^{*}),{\bf n}_{k}\rangle\geq 0
i.e.

\langle u(\vartheta_{k}^{*}),u(\vartheta_{k}+\pi/2)\rangle=\sin(\vartheta_{k}^{*}-\vartheta_{k})\geq 0
. Since
\vartheta_{k}^{*}\in[0,2\pi)
and

0\leq\theta_{2}<\vartheta_{k}<\pi/2
, this implies that
\vartheta_{k}^{*}-\vartheta_{k}\in[0,\pi]

and so
\theta_{2}<\vartheta_{k}\leq\vartheta_{k}^{*}\leq\pi+\vartheta_{k}
.
Also from
ru(\vartheta_{k}^{*})\in\partial K
and
(
4.37
) we deduce that
\vartheta_{k}^{*}\not\in(\theta_{2},\pi-\theta_{2})
and thus

\pi-\theta_{2}\leq\vartheta_{k}^{*}\leq\pi+\vartheta_{k}.
Now choose a convergent
subsequence
\{\vartheta_{k_{j}}^{*}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
of
\{\vartheta_{k}^{*}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}

and let

\vartheta^{*}_{\infty}=\lim\limits_{j\to\infty}\vartheta_{k_{j}}^{*}
. Then,
from
\vartheta_{k}\to\theta_{2}\,\,(k\to\infty)
, we have that

\pi-\theta_{2}\leq\vartheta_{\infty}^{*}\leq\pi+\theta_{2}
and

ru(\vartheta^{*}_{\infty})=\lim\limits_{j\to\infty}ru(\vartheta_{k_{j}}^{*})\in(\partial B_{r})\cap\partial K
.
By (
4.32
) we conclude that
b^{*}:=-Ru(\vartheta^{*}_{\infty})\in(\partial B_{R})\cap\partial K
, and thus
by the minimality of
|a_{0}-b_{0}|
we have

|a_{0}-b_{0}|\leq|a_{0}-b^{*}|
.
Since
|a_{0}-b_{0}|^{2}=|ru(\theta_{2})-Ru(\pi/2)|^{2}=r^{2}+R^{2}-2rR\cos(\theta_{2}-\pi/2),|a_{0}-b^{*}|^{2}=|ru(\theta_{2})+Ru(\vartheta^{*}_{\infty})|^{2}=r^{2}+R^{2}+2rR\cos(\theta_{2}-\vartheta^{*}_{\infty})
, this implies that

\cos(\pi/2+\theta_{2})=-\cos(\theta_{2}-\pi/2)\leq\cos(\theta_{2}-\vartheta^{*}_{\infty})=\cos(\vartheta^{*}_{\infty}-\theta_{2}).

Since

0<\pi-2\theta_{2}\leq\vartheta^{*}_{\infty}-\theta_{2}\leq\pi,0<\pi/2+\theta_{2}<\pi
and since
\cos(\cdot)
is strictly decreasing on
[0,\pi]
, it follows
that
\pi/2+\theta_{2}\geq\vartheta^{*}_{\infty}-\theta_{2}\geq\pi-2\theta_{2}
. Thus

\theta_{2}\geq\pi/6.

As mentioned in
Step3
,
\partial K
and
{{\mathbb{S}}}^{1}
are homeomorphic
and the mapping
\varphi:{{\mathbb{S}}}^{1}\to\partial K
,

\varphi(u)=\rho(u)u,u\in{{\mathbb{S}}}^{1}
, is a homeomorphism,
where

u\mapsto\rho(u)=\max\{\rho\in[r,R]\,\,|\,\,\rho u\in\partial K\}

is continuous on
{{\mathbb{S}}}^{1}
. Thus using
u(\theta)=(\cos(\theta),\sin(\theta))
, the boundary

\partial K
can be written

\partial K=\{\rho(\theta)u(\theta)\,\,|\,\,\theta\in[0,2\pi]\},\qquad\rho(\theta):=\rho(u(\theta)).

Note that
\theta\mapsto\rho(\theta)
is continuous on
[0,2\pi]
and

r\leq\rho(\theta)\leq R
for all
\theta\in[0,2\pi].

Now we prove that

{\rm there\,\,\,exists}\,\,\,a_{3}\in\partial K\,\,\,{\rm such\,\,\,that}\,\,\,\langle a_{3}-a_{2},a_{1}-a_{2}\rangle=0

(4.38)

{\rm and}\,\,\,a_{4}\in K^{\circ}\,\,\,{\rm where}\,\,\,\frac{a_{2}+a_{4}}{2}=\frac{a_{1}+a_{3}}{2}\,\,\,\,{\rm i.e.}\,\,\,a_{4}=a_{1}+a_{3}-a_{2}.

(4.39)

To prove (
4.38
) we use a continuous function

\displaystyle f(\theta):=\langle\rho(\theta)u(\theta)-a_{2},a_{1}-a_{2}\rangle=\big\langle\rho(\theta)u(\theta)+Ru(\theta_{2}),-ru(\pi/2)+Ru(\theta_{2})\big\rangle

\displaystyle=R^{2}+\rho(\theta)R\cos(\theta-\theta_{2})-r\rho(\theta)\sin(\theta)-rR\sin(\theta_{2}),\quad\theta\in[\pi/2,\pi].

Using
0<r<R
,
r\leq\rho(\cdot)\leq R
, and
\cos(\theta_{2})>0
we have

\displaystyle f(\pi/2)=R^{2}-r\rho(\pi/2)+\big(\rho(\pi/2)-r\big)R\sin(\theta_{2})\geq R^{2}-r\rho(\pi/2)>0,

\displaystyle f(\pi)\leq R^{2}g(\theta_{2}),\quad{\rm where}\,\,\,g(\theta):=1-\frac{r}{R}\big(\cos(\theta)+\sin(\theta)\big).

We need to prove that
g(\theta_{2})\leq 0
. To do this we observe that

\theta\mapsto g(\theta)
is convex on
[\pi/6,\theta_{0}]

so that it holds
g(\theta)\leq\max\{g(\pi/6),g(\theta_{0})\}
for all
\theta\in[\pi/6,\theta_{0}]
.
Using
r\geq(\sqrt{3}-1)R
,
\sin(\theta_{0})=\frac{r}{R}
, and
\pi/4<\theta_{0}<\pi/2
we have

\displaystyle g(\pi/6)=1-\frac{r}{R}\big(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\big)=1-\frac{r}{R}\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}-1}\leq 0,

\displaystyle g(\theta_{0})=\cos(\theta_{0})\big(\cos(\theta_{0})-\sin(\theta_{0})\big)<0.

This proves that

g(\theta)\leq 0
for
\theta\in[\pi/6,\theta_{0}]
and therefore
g(\theta_{2})\leq 0
hence
f(\pi)\leq 0

because
\theta_{2}\in[\pi/6,\theta_{0})
. By intermediate value theorem,
there exists
\theta_{3}\in(\pi/2,\pi]
such that
f(\theta_{3})=0.

Thus the point

a_{3}:=\rho(\theta_{3})u(\theta_{3})\in\partial K\,\,\,{\rm satisfies}\,\,\,(\ref{6.11}).

Next let
a_{4}
be defined in (
4.39
) i.e.

a_{4}=a_{1}+a_{3}-a_{2}
. Using (
4.38
) we have

|a_{2}|^{2}+|a_{4}|^{2}=|a_{1}|^{2}+|a_{3}|^{2},\quad|a_{2}-a_{4}|^{2}=|a_{1}-a_{3}|^{2}.

(4.40)

From the first equality in (
4.40
) we have

|a_{4}|^{2}=|a_{1}|^{2}+|a_{3}|^{2}-|a_{2}|^{2}=r^{2}+\rho(\theta_{3})^{2}-R^{2}\leq r^{2}
and so
a_{4}\in B_{r}\subset K
.
We then come to prove that

a_{4}\in K^{\circ}.

(4.41)

Suppose to the contrary that
a_{4}\not\in K^{\circ}
. Then

a_{4}\in(\partial B_{r})\cap\partial K
,
|a_{3}|=\rho(\theta_{3})=R
, and thus

a_{3}=Ru(\theta_{3})\in\partial K
(with
\pi/2<\theta_{3}\leq\pi
).
Write
a_{4}=ru(\theta_{4})
for some
\theta_{4}\in[0,2\pi)
. From

a_{4}-a_{3}=a_{1}-a_{2}
we have
|a_{4}-a_{3}|^{2}=|a_{1}-a_{2}|^{2}
.
Since
|a_{4}-a_{3}|^{2}=|ru(\theta_{4})-Ru(\theta_{3})\big)|^{2}=r^{2}+R^{2}-2rR\cos(\theta_{4}-\theta_{3}),\ |a_{1}-a_{2}|^{2}=|-ru(\pi/2)+Ru(\theta_{2})|^{2}=r^{2}+R^{2}-2rR\sin(\theta_{2})
, this gives

\cos(\theta_{4}-\theta_{3})=\sin(\theta_{2})\quad(>0).

(4.42)

On the other hand from
a_{4}-a_{1}=a_{3}-a_{2}
and (
4.38
) we have

0=\langle a_{3}-a_{2},a_{1}-a_{2}\rangle=\langle a_{4}-a_{1},a_{1}-a_{2}\rangle=rR\cos(\theta_{4})\cos(\theta_{2})-r\big(1+\sin(\theta_{4})\big)\big(r-R\sin(\theta_{2})\big)

which gives

rR\cos(\theta_{4})\cos(\theta_{2})=r(1+\sin(\theta_{4}))\big(r-R\sin(\theta_{2})\big)\geq 0

where we have used
R\sin(\theta_{2})<R\sin(\theta_{0})=r.

Since
\pi/6\leq\theta_{2}<\pi/2
so that
rR\cos(\theta_{2})>0
, this implies that

\cos(\theta_{4})\geq 0
and thus

\theta_{4}\in[0,\pi/2]\cup[3\pi/2,2\pi).

Next from
a_{4}\in(\partial B_{r})\cap\partial K
we have

|a_{4}-b_{0}|\geq|a_{0}-b_{0}|.

Since

|a_{4}-b_{0}|^{2}=|ru(\theta_{4})-Ru(\pi/2)|^{2}=r^{2}+R^{2}-2rR\sin(\theta_{4}),|a_{0}-b_{0}|^{2}=r^{2}+R^{2}-2rR\sin(\theta_{2}),
this implies that

\sin(\theta_{4})\leq\sin(\theta_{2}).
If

\theta_{4}\in[0,\pi/2]
, then

0\leq\pi/2-\theta_{4}<\theta_{3}-\theta_{4}\leq\pi
, and so from (
4.42
)
we have
\cos(\theta_{3}-\theta_{4})=\sin(\theta_{2})=\cos(\pi/2-\theta_{2})

hence

\theta_{3}-\theta_{4}=\pi/2-\theta_{2}
and so
\pi/2\geq\theta_{4}=\theta_{3}-\pi/2+\theta_{2}>\theta_{2}>0
hence
\sin(\theta_{4})>\sin(\theta_{2})

(here we used the strict inequality
\theta_{3}>\pi/2
). This
contradicts
\sin(\theta_{4})\leq\sin(\theta_{2})
. If

\theta_{4}\in[3\pi/2,2\pi)
, then

\pi/2\leq\theta_{4}-\theta_{3}<3\pi/2

so that we still get a contradiction:

0<\sin(\theta_{2})=\cos(\theta_{3}-\theta_{4})=\cos(\theta_{4}-\theta_{3})\leq 0.

Thus it must hold
a_{4}\in K^{\circ}
.

Note that from
a_{1}\in\partial K,|a_{1}|=r,a_{4}\in K^{\circ},|a_{4}|\leq r
and
|a_{2}|=R>r
, we have

a_{4}\neq a_{1}
,
a_{2}\neq a_{1}
,
a_{2}\neq a_{4}
, hence
a_{2}-a_{3}=a_{1}-a_{4}\neq 0,|a_{1}-a_{3}|=|a_{2}-a_{4}|>0
.
Recalling the facts mentioned in
Step3
, for the boundary points
a_{1},a_{3}\in\partial K
,
there are
\omega_{1},\omega_{3}\in{{\mathbb{S}}}^{1}
such that

\langle x-a_{1},\omega_{1}\rangle>0\quad\forall\,x\in K\setminus\{a_{1}\};\quad\langle x-a_{3},\omega_{3}\rangle>0\quad\forall\,x\in K\setminus\{a_{3}\}.

(4.43)

By
a_{4}\in K^{\circ}
we have

t^{*}:=\max\{t\in[1,\infty)\,|\,a_{2}+t(a_{4}-a_{2})\in K\}>1
.
Let

a_{4}^{*}=a_{2}+t^{*}(a_{4}-a_{2}),\quad\omega=\frac{a_{1}-a_{3}}{|a_{1}-a_{3}|}.

Then
a_{4}^{*}\in\partial K,\omega\in{{\mathbb{S}}}^{1}
, and so by the condition (
4.1
) we have

{\rm either}\quad u:=\frac{a_{2}+a_{4}^{*}}{2}+\frac{|a_{2}-a_{4}^{*}|}{2}\omega\in K\quad{\rm or}\quad v:=\frac{a_{2}+a_{4}^{*}}{2}-\frac{|a_{2}-a_{4}^{*}|}{2}\omega\in K.

(4.44)

On the other hand using
|a_{1}-a_{3}|=|a_{2}-a_{4}|
we compute

\displaystyle\frac{a_{2}+a_{4}^{*}}{2}=a_{2}+\frac{t^{*}}{2}(a_{4}-a_{2}),\quad\frac{|a_{2}-a_{4}^{*}|}{2}\omega=\frac{t^{*}}{2}(a_{1}-a_{3})

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow\quad u-a_{1}=(1-t^{*})(a_{2}-a_{1}),\quad v-a_{3}=(1-t^{*})(a_{2}-a_{3}).

Since
a_{2}\in K\setminus\{a_{1},a_{3}\}
, using (
4.43
) and
t^{*}>1
we deduce

\langle u-a_{1},\omega_{1}\rangle=(1-t^{*})\langle a_{2}-a_{1},\omega_{1}\rangle<0,\quad\langle v-a_{3},\omega_{3}\rangle=(1-t^{*})\langle a_{2}-a_{3},\omega_{3}\rangle<0

and so using (
4.43
) again we conclude that
u\not\in K
and
v\not\in K
. But this
contradicts (
4.44
). This contradiction proves that
r=R
, i.e.
K
is a disk.

Step5
. We now make induction argument on the dimension

. In

we have proved that the theorem holds for
n=2
. Suppose the theorem holds
for a dimension

. Let

Step3

,

, and

.

We now prove that

.
Let
a_{0}\in(\partial B_{r}^{n})\cap\partial K,b_{0}\in(\partial B_{R}^{n})\cap\partial K

4.33

By (
4.32
) we have

-\frac{r}{R}b_{0}\in(\partial B_{r}^{n})\cap\partial K
. Since

{\bf e}_{0}\in{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}

. Let

4.1
) for the

n-1
-dimensional case. To do this we need to prove that

(4.45)

Take any

Since

Write

\widetilde{{\bf e}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}t_{i}{\bf e}_{i}\in\Pi.

It is easily seen that
\widetilde{{\bf e}}\neq 0.
In fact from
B^{n}_{r}(0)\subset K
,
(
4.46
), and

From

{\bf n}:={\cal A}^{-1}(\widetilde{{\bf e}}/|\widetilde{{\bf e}}|)\in{{\mathbb{S}}}^{n-2}
. For any

,

)

Next for any

, since
{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n-1}={\cal A}^{-1}(\Pi)
,

v_{0}={\cal A}(x_{0})
, then
v_{0}\in\Pi

.
By definition of boundary point, for any

(B_{\varepsilon}^{n-1}(x_{0}))^{\circ}\cap K_{1}\neq\emptyset,(B_{\varepsilon}^{n-1}(x_{0}))^{\circ}\cap K_{1}^{c}\neq\emptyset
. Let

x_{1}\in(B_{\varepsilon}^{n-1}(x_{0}))^{\circ}\cap K_{1},x_{2}\in(B_{\varepsilon}^{n-1}(x_{0}))^{\circ}\cap K_{1}^{c}
.
Then, since
K_{1}={\cal A}^{-1}\big(K\cap\Pi\big)
and
K_{1}^{c}={\cal A}^{-1}\big(K^{c}\cap\Pi\big)
, we can write

for some
v_{1}\in K\cap\Pi,v_{2}\in K^{c}\cap\Pi
.
Then from

|v_{1}-v_{0}|={\cal A}(x_{1}-x_{0})|=|x_{1}-x_{0}|<\varepsilon,|v_{2}-v_{0}|={\cal A}(x_{2}-x_{0})|=|x_{2}-x_{0}|<\varepsilon
we see that

(B_{\varepsilon}^{n}(v_{0}))^{\circ}\cap K\neq\emptyset,(B_{\varepsilon}^{n}(v_{0}))^{\circ}\cap K^{c}\neq\emptyset

and so
v_{0}\in\partial K
and thus
v_{0}\in(\partial K)\cap\Pi
.
Thus
x_{0}={\cal A}^{-1}(v_{0})\in{\cal A}^{-1}((\partial K)\cap\Pi)
.
This proves that
\partial K_{1}\subset{\cal A}^{-1}((\partial K)\cap\Pi)
and thus (
4.45
) holds true.

To see that
K_{1}
satisfies the condition (
4.1
) for the

n-1
-dimensional case, we take any
x,y\in\partial K_{1}={\cal A}^{-1}\big((\partial K)\cap\Pi\big),

and any
\sigma\in{{\mathbb{S}}}^{n-2}
. Let
v={\cal A}(x),v_{*}={\cal A}(y),\omega={\cal A}(\sigma)
. Then
v,v_{*}\in(\partial K)\cap\Pi
and from

|\omega|=|{\cal A}(\sigma)|=|\sigma|=1
we have
\omega\in{{\mathbb{S}}}^{n-1}\cap\Pi
.
Since
K
satisfies the condition (
4.1
) for dimension
n
, this implies that
either
\frac{1}{2}(v+v_{*})+\frac{1}{2}|v-v_{*}|\omega\in K
or
\frac{1}{2}(v+v_{*})-\frac{1}{2}|v-v_{*}|\omega\in K
. Since all

v,v_{*},\omega
belong to
\Pi
, this also implies that either

\frac{1}{2}(v+v_{*})+\frac{1}{2}|v-v_{*}|\omega\in K\cap\Pi,
or

\frac{1}{2}(v+v_{*})-\frac{1}{2}|v-v_{*}|\omega\in K\cap\Pi
. Thus using

|x-y|=|{\cal A}^{-1}(v-v_{*})|=|v-v_{*}|

we deduce

\frac{x+y}{2}\pm\frac{|x-y|}{2}\sigma={\cal A}^{-1}\Big(\frac{v+v_{*}}{2}\pm\frac{|v-v_{*}|}{2}\omega\Big)

and so
either
\frac{1}{2}(x+y)+\frac{1}{2}|x-y|\sigma\in{\cal A}^{-1}(K\cap\Pi)=K_{1}
, or
\frac{1}{2}(x+y)-\frac{1}{2}|x-y|\sigma\in{\cal A}^{-1}(K\cap\Pi)=K_{1}
. This proves that

K_{1}
satisfies the condition (
4.1
) for the
n-1
-dimensional case.

By induction hypotheses,
K_{1}\subset{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n-1}
is a
ball. Write
K_{1}=B^{n-1}_{r_{0}}(c_{0})
.
From
{\bf e}_{0}\,\bot\,\{a_{0},b_{0}\}
we have

a_{0},b_{0},-\frac{r}{R}b_{0}\in(\partial K)\cap\Pi
and thus from (
4.45
)
we see that

x_{0}:={\cal A}^{-1}(a_{0}),\,\,p_{0}:={\cal A}^{-1}(b_{0}),\,\,q_{0}:={\cal A}^{-1}(-\frac{r}{R}b_{0})\,\,\,{\rm all\,\,beong\,\,to}\,\,\partial K_{1}.

Since
{\cal A}
is an isometry,
we have
{\rm diam}(K_{1})={\rm diam}(K\cap\Pi)\leq{\rm diam}(K).

On the other hand we have

{\rm diam}(K_{1})\geq|p_{0}-q_{0}|=(1+\frac{r}{R})|{\cal A}^{-1}(b_{0})|=R+r={\rm diam}(K).

Thus
{\rm diam}(K_{1})=|p_{0}-q_{0}|=R+r
and so

K_{1}=B^{n-1}_{r_{0}}(c_{0})\quad{\rm with}\quad r_{0}=\frac{R+r}{2},\quad c_{0}=\frac{p_{0}+q_{0}}{2}.

By
q_{0}=-\frac{r}{R}p_{0}
we have

c_{0}=\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{r}{R})p_{0}=\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{r}{R}){\cal A}^{-1}(b_{0}).

Since
x_{0}={\cal A}^{-1}(a_{0})\in\partial K_{1}=\partial B_{r_{0}}^{n-1}(c_{0})

and since
\langle a_{0},b_{0}\rangle\geq 0
, it follows that

(\frac{R+r}{2})^{2}=r_{0}^{2}=|x_{0}-c_{0}|^{2}=|a_{0}-\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{r}{R})b_{0}|^{2}\leq|a_{0}|^{2}+|\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{r}{R})b_{0}|^{2}=r^{2}+(\frac{R-r}{2})^{2}
and so

Rr=(\frac{R+r}{2})^{2}-(\frac{R-r}{2})^{2}\leq r^{2}.

Since
0<r\leq R
, this implies
r=R
and so

K
is an
n
-dimensional ball. This completes the proof of Theorem

4.1
.
\Box

Efficient and reliable divergence-conforming methods for an elasticity-poroelasticity interface problem
Santiago Badia 1, Martin Hornkjøl 2, Arbaz Khan 3, Kent-André Mardal 2, Alberto F. Martín 4, Ricardo Ruiz-Baier 5

Abstract
. We present a finite element discretisation to model the interaction between a poroelastic structure and an elastic medium. The consolidation problem considers fully coupled deformations across an interface, ensuring continuity of displacement and total traction, as well as no-flux for the fluid phase. Our formulation of the poroelasticity equations incorporates displacement, fluid pressure, and total pressure, while the elasticity equations adopt a displacement-pressure formulation. Notably, the transmission conditions at the interface are enforced without the need for Lagrange multipliers. We demonstrate the stability and convergence of the divergence-conforming finite element method across various polynomial degrees. The a priori error bounds remain robust, even when considering large variations in intricate model parameters such as Lamé constants, permeability, and storativity coefficient. To enhance computational efficiency and reliability, we develop residual-based a posteriori error estimators that are independent of the aforementioned coefficients. Additionally, we devise parameter-robust and optimal block diagonal preconditioners. Through numerical examples, including adaptive scenarios, we illustrate the scheme’s properties such as convergence and parameter robustness. Keywords. Biot–elasticity transmission equations, mixed finite element methods, divergence-conforming schemes, a priori error analysis, a posteriori error analysis, operator preconditioning.

1.
Introduction

In a variety of engineering and biomedical applications, poroelastic bodies are either surrounded or in contact with a purely elastic material. Examples include filter design, prosthetics, simulation of oil extraction from reservoirs, carbon sequestration, and sound insulation structures. From the viewpoint of constructing and analysing numerical methods, recent works for the interfacial Biot/elasticity problem can be found in
[
5
,
4
,
25
,
26
,
27
]
. These contributions include mortar-type discretisations, formulations using rotations, and extensions to lubrication models. In this work we focus on H(div)-conforming discretisations of displacement for the transmission problem, in combination with a total pressure formulation for both the elastic and poroelastic sub-domains. Divergence-conforming methods with tangential jump penalisation for elasticity were already proposed in
[
32
]
. Their counterpart for Biot poroelasticity equations (in the two-field formulation) have been introduced in
[
34
,
47
,
48
]
, while a much more abundant literature is available for Brinkman flows (as well as coupled flow-transport problems) in
[
16
,
17
,
30
,
35
]
. The extension to interfacial porous media flow has been addressed in
[
18
,
19
,
24
,
33
]
. In general, such type of discretisations offer appealing features such as local conservation of mass, and the ability to produce robust and locking–free schemes. These type of schemes are required because of the large number of parameters upon which robustness is targeted (especially in the limits of near incompressibility and near impermeability).

As regularity of the solution is not always available (due to possibly high contrast in material parameters, domain singularities, etc), we are also interested in deriving
a posteriori
error estimates which allow us to apply adaptive mesh refinement in the regions where it is most required. A coupled elliptic–parabolic
a posteriori
error analysis for Biot poroelasticity and multiple network poroelasticity is available from the works
[
1
,
22
,
38
]
. On the other hand, robust estimates for the elasticity–poroelasticity coupling have been obtained only recently, for enriched Galerkin methods in
[
26
]
, and in
[
4
]
for rotation-based formulations.

Here the analysis is carried out considering two examples of fluid pressure approximation: either continuous or discontinuous piecewise polynomials. For the DG case we use a classical symmetric interior penalty (SIP) method. In all cases the proposed formulation is robust with respect to material parameters that can assume very small or very large values, including the extreme cases of near incompressibility, near impermeability, and near zero storativity. This parameter independence in the stability of the discrete problem is critical in the
a priori
error bounds, in the derivation of
a posteriori
error estimates, and in the design of robust preconditioners.

Finally, we design optimal preconditioner that are robust with respect to parameters. The preconditioner is block-diagonal and its definition relies on the stability properties of the proposed numerical scheme, i.e., it consists in a discretisation of the continuous Riesz map (see
[
37
,
30
]
for similar approaches). The definition of the pressure block is motivated by
[
42
]
, where a robust preconditioner for the interface Stokes problems with high contrast is proposed.

The remainder of the paper has been structured as follows. Section
2

2.2.
The transmission problem

Following the problem setup from
[
25
,
26
]
, let us consider a bounded Lipschitz domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\in\{2,3\}
,
together with a partition into non-overlapping and connected subdomains
\Omega^{\mathrm{E}}
,
\Omega^{\mathrm{P}}
representing zones occupied by an elastic body (e.g., a non-pay rock, in the context of reservoir modelling) and a fluid-saturated poroelastic region (e.g., a reservoir), respectively. The interface between the two subdomains is denoted as
\Sigma=\partial\Omega^{\mathrm{P}}\cap\partial\Omega^{\mathrm{E}}
, and on it the normal vector
\bm{n}
is assumed to point from
\Omega^{\mathrm{P}}
to
\Omega^{\mathrm{E}}
. The boundary of the domain
\Omega
is separated in terms of the boundaries of two individual subdomains, that is
\partial\Omega:=\Gamma^{\mathrm{P}}\cup\Gamma^{\mathrm{E}}
, and then subdivided as the disjoint Dirichlet and Neumann type condition as
\Gamma^{\mathrm{P}}:=\Gamma^{\mathrm{P}}_{D}\cup\Gamma^{\mathrm{P}}_{N}
and
\Gamma^{\mathrm{E}}:=\Gamma^{\mathrm{E}}_{D}\cup\Gamma^{\mathrm{E}}_{N}
, respectively. We assume that all sub-boundaries have positive
(d-1)
–Hausdorff measure.

In the overall domain we state the momentum balance of the fluid and solid phases on the poroelastic region, the mass conservation of the total amount of fluid, and the balance of linear momentum on the elastic region. In doing so, and following
[
5
]
, in addition to the usual variables of elastic displacement, poroelastic displacement, and fluid pressure, we employ the total pressure in the poroelastic subdomain, and the Herrmann pressure in the elastic subdomain. For given body loads
\bm{b}^{\mathrm{P}}(t):\Omega^{\mathrm{P}}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\bm{b}^{\mathrm{E}}(t):\Omega^{\mathrm{E}}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and a volumetric source or sink
\ell^{\mathrm{P}}(t):\Omega^{\mathrm{P}}\to\mathbb{R}
, one seeks for each time
t\in(0,t_{\mathrm{final}}]
, the vector of solid displacements
\bm{u}^{\mathrm{E}}:\Omega^{\mathrm{E}}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
of the non-pay zone, the elastic pressure
\varphi^{\mathrm{E}}:\Omega^{\mathrm{E}}\to\mathbb{R}
, the displacement
\bm{u}^{\mathrm{P}}(t):\Omega^{\mathrm{P}}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the pore fluid pressure
p^{\mathrm{P}}(t):\Omega^{\mathrm{P}}\to\mathbb{R}
, and the total pressure
\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}(t):\Omega^{\mathrm{P}}\to\mathbb{R}
of the reservoir, satisfying:

(2.1a)

\displaystyle-\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits(2{\mu^{\mathrm{P}}}\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{u}^{\mathrm{P}})-\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}\mathbf{I})

\displaystyle=\bm{b}^{\mathrm{P}}

\displaystyle\text{in $\Omega^{\mathrm{P}}\times(0,t_{\mathrm{final}}]$},

(2.1b)

\displaystyle\biggl(c_{0}+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{{\lambda^{\mathrm{P}}}}\biggr)\partial_{t}p^{\mathrm{P}}-\frac{\alpha}{{\lambda^{\mathrm{P}}}}\partial_{t}\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}-\frac{1}{\eta}\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits(\kappa\nabla p^{\mathrm{P}})

\displaystyle=\ell^{\mathrm{P}}

\displaystyle\text{in $\Omega^{\mathrm{P}}\times(0,t_{\mathrm{final}}]$},

(2.1c)

\displaystyle\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}-\alpha p^{\mathrm{P}}+{\lambda^{\mathrm{P}}}\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\bm{u}^{\mathrm{P}}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{in $\Omega^{\mathrm{P}}\times(0,t_{\mathrm{final}}]$},

(2.1d)

\displaystyle-\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits(2{\mu^{\mathrm{E}}}\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{u}^{\mathrm{E}})-\varphi^{\mathrm{E}}\mathbf{I})

\displaystyle=\bm{b}^{\mathrm{E}}

\displaystyle\text{in $\Omega^{\mathrm{E}}\times(0,t_{\mathrm{final}}]$},

(2.1e)

\displaystyle\varphi^{\mathrm{E}}+{\lambda^{\mathrm{E}}}\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\bm{u}^{\mathrm{E}}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{in $\Omega^{\mathrm{E}}\times(0,t_{\mathrm{final}}]$}.

Here
\kappa(\bm{x})
is the hydraulic conductivity of the porous medium,
\eta
is the constant viscosity of the interstitial fluid,
c_{0}
is the storativity coefficient,
\alpha
is the Biot–Willis consolidation parameter, and
\mu^{\mathrm{E}},\lambda^{\mathrm{E}}
and
\mu^{\mathrm{P}},\lambda^{\mathrm{P}}
are the Lamé parameters associated with the constitutive law of the solid on the elastic and on the poroelastic subdomain, respectively. The poroelastic stress
\widetilde{\bm{\sigma}}=\bm{\sigma}-\alpha p^{\mathrm{P}}\mathbf{I}
is composed by the effective mechanical stress
{\lambda^{\mathrm{P}}}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\bm{u}^{\mathrm{P}})\mathbf{I}+2{\mu^{\mathrm{P}}}\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{u}^{\mathrm{P}})
plus the non-viscous fluid stress (the fluid pressure scaled with
\alpha
). This system is complemented by the following set of boundary conditions

(2.2a)

\displaystyle\bm{u}^{\mathrm{P}}=\bm{0}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{\kappa}{\eta}\nabla p^{\mathrm{P}}\cdot\bm{n}^{\Gamma}=0

\displaystyle\text{on $\Gamma^{\mathrm{P}}_{D}\times(0,t_{\mathrm{final}}]$},

(2.2b)

\displaystyle[2{\mu^{\mathrm{P}}}\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{u}^{\mathrm{P}})-\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}\mathbf{I}]\bm{n}^{\Gamma}=\bm{0}\quad\text{and}\quad p^{\mathrm{P}}=0

\displaystyle\text{on $\Gamma^{\mathrm{P}}_{N}\times(0,t_{\mathrm{final}}]$},

(2.2c)

\displaystyle\bm{u}^{\mathrm{E}}=\bm{0}

\displaystyle\text{on $\Gamma^{\mathrm{E}}_{D}\times(0,t_{\mathrm{final}}]$},

(2.2d)

\displaystyle[2{\mu^{\mathrm{E}}}\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{u}^{\mathrm{E}})-\varphi^{\mathrm{E}}\mathbf{I}]\bm{n}^{\Gamma}=\bm{0}

\displaystyle\text{on $\Gamma^{\mathrm{E}}_{N}\times(0,t_{\mathrm{final}}]$}.

Here, the partition
\Gamma^{\mathrm{P}}:=\Gamma^{\mathrm{P}}_{D}\cup\Gamma^{\mathrm{P}}_{N}
denotes the sub-boundaries where we
impose essential (i.e.,
\bm{u}^{\mathrm{P}}=\bm{0}
) and natural boundary conditions (i.e.,
\widetilde{\bm{\sigma}}\bm{n}^{\Gamma}=\bm{0}
) corresponding to equation (
2.1a
). For ease of notation, we note that, in this definition, we are assuming that the essential and natural sub-boundaries corresponding to equation (
2.1b
) (i.e., the ones where we impose
p^{\mathrm{P}}=0
and
\nabla p^{\mathrm{P}}\cdot\bm{n}^{\Gamma}=0
, respectively) match the natural and essential sub-boundaries associated to (
2.1a
), respectively. However, in general, this does not have to be case, and one may
choose separately the partition into essential and natural for each of these
two equations separately.

Along with the previous set of boundary conditions, the system is also complemented
by transmission conditions in the absence of external forces (derived by means of homogenisation in
[
41
]
) that take the following form

(2.3)

\bm{u}^{\mathrm{P}}=\bm{u}^{\mathrm{E}},\quad[2{\mu^{\mathrm{P}}}\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{u}^{\mathrm{P}})-\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}\mathbf{I}]\bm{n}=[2{\mu^{\mathrm{E}}}\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{u}^{\mathrm{E}})-\varphi^{\mathrm{E}}\mathbf{I}]\bm{n},\quad\frac{\kappa}{\eta}\nabla p^{\mathrm{P}}\cdot\bm{n}=0\quad\text{on $\Sigma\times(0,t_{\mathrm{final}}]$},

[
5

Homogeneity of the boundary and initial conditions is only assumed to simplify the exposition of the subsequent analysis, however the results remain valid for more general assumptions. We also note that non-homogeneous boundary conditions are used in the numerical experiments.

\displaystyle(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\bm{v}_{0}^{\mathrm{P}},\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}_{0})_{0,\Omega^{\mathrm{P}}}\geq C_{\Omega^{\mathrm{P}}}/\mu^{\mathrm{P}}\|\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}_{0}\|_{0,\Omega^{\mathrm{P}}}^{2},\quad\sqrt{2\mu^{\mathrm{P}}}\|\bm{\nabla}\bm{v}_{0}^{\mathrm{P}}\|_{0,\Omega^{\mathrm{P}}}\leq 1/\sqrt{2\mu^{\mathrm{P}}}\|\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}_{0}\|_{0,\Omega^{\mathrm{P}}}.

Hence, there exists
\bm{v}_{0}\in\mathbf{V}
such that
\bm{v}_{0}|_{\Omega^{\mathrm{E}}}=\bm{v}_{0}^{\mathrm{E}}
and
\bm{v}_{0}|_{\Omega^{\mathrm{P}}}=\bm{v}_{0}^{\mathrm{P}}
. Moreover we have

\sup_{\bm{0}\neq\bm{v}\in\mathbf{V}}\frac{b_{1}(\bm{v},\varphi_{0})}{\|\sqrt{2\mu}\bm{v}\|_{1,\Omega}}\geq\tilde{C}\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\mu}}\varphi_{0}\|_{0,\Omega},

or, following also
[
42
]
, we can write

\sup_{\bm{0}\neq\bm{v}\in\mathbf{V}}\frac{b_{1}(\bm{v},\varphi_{0})}{\|\sqrt{2\mu}\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{v})\|_{0,\Omega}}\geq\tilde{C}\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\mu}}\varphi_{0}\|_{0,\Omega},

for a positive constant
\tilde{C}
independent of
\mu
.

3.
An H(div)-conforming finite element approximation

We denote by
\{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{\mathrm{P}}\}_{h}
and
\{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}^{\mathrm{E}}\}_{h}
sequences of triangular (or tetrahedral in 3D) partitions of the poroelastic and elastic subdomains
\Omega^{\mathrm{P}}
and
\Omega^{\mathrm{E}}
, respectively having diameter
h_{K}
, and being such that the partitions are conforming with the interface
\Sigma
. We label by
K^{-}
and
K^{+}
the two elements adjacent to a facet (an edge in 2D or a face in 3D), while
h_{e}
stands for the maximum diameter of the facet. By
\mathcal{E}_{h}
we will denote the set of all facets and will distinguish between facets lying on the elastic, poroelastic, and interfacial regions
\mathcal{E}_{h}=\mathcal{E}_{h}^{\mathrm{E}}\cup\mathcal{E}_{h}^{\mathrm{P}}\cup\mathcal{E}_{h}^{\Sigma}
.

For a smooth vector, scalar, or tensor field
w
defined on
\mathcal{T}_{h}
,
w^{\pm}
denote its traces taken from the interior of
K^{+}
and
K^{-}
, respectively. We also denote by
\bm{n}^{\pm}
the outward unit normal vector to
K^{\pm}
. The symbols
{\left\{\kern-3.01385pt\left\{\cdot\right\}\kern-3.01385pt\right\}}
and
\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket
denote, respectively, the average and jump operators, defined as

(3.1)

\displaystyle{\left\{\kern-3.01385pt\left\{w\right\}\kern-3.01385pt\right\}}\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}(w^{-}+w^{+}),\quad\llbracket w\odot\bm{n}\rrbracket\coloneqq(w^{-}\odot\bm{n}^{-}+w^{+}\odot\bm{n}^{+}),

for a generic multiplication operator
\odot
, which applies to interior edges, whereas for boundary jumps and averages we adopt the conventions
{\left\{\kern-3.01385pt\left\{w\right\}\kern-3.01385pt\right\}}=w
, and
\llbracket w\odot\bm{n}\rrbracket=w\odot\bm{n}
. The element-wise action of a differential operator is denoted with a subindex
h
, for example,
\nabla_{h}
,
\bm{\nabla}_{h}
will denote the broken gradient operators for scalar and vector quantities, respectively and
\bm{\varepsilon}_{h}(\cdot)=\frac{1}{2}(\bm{\nabla}_{h}\cdot+(\bm{\nabla}_{h}\cdot)^{T})
is the symmetrised vector broken gradient.

Let
\mathbb{P}_{k}(K)
denote the local space spanned by polynomials of degree up to
k\geq 0
, and let us consider the following discrete spaces

(3.2)

\begin{split}\mathbf{V}_{h}&\coloneqq\bigl\{\bm{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{H}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits;\Omega):\bm{v}_{h}|_{K}\in[\mathbb{P}_{k+1}(K)]^{d}\quad\forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h},\quad\bm{v}_{h}\cdot\bm{n}|_{\Gamma_{D}^{\mathrm{E}}\cup\Gamma_{D}^{\mathrm{P}}}=0\bigr\},\\
\mathrm{Q}^{\mathrm{P}}_{h}&\coloneqq\bigl\{q^{\mathrm{P}}_{h}\in\mathrm{Q}^{\mathrm{P}}:q_{h}|_{K}\in\mathbb{P}_{k+1}(K)\quad\forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\mathrm{P}}\bigr\},\\
\mathrm{Z}_{h}&\coloneqq\bigl\{\psi_{h}\in\mathrm{Z}:\psi_{h}|_{K}\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(K)\quad\forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\bigr\},\end{split}

which, in particular, satisfy the so-called equilibrium property

(3.3)

\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\mathbf{V}_{h}=\mathrm{Z}_{h}.

Note that in this case
\mathbf{V}_{h}
is the space of divergence-conforming BDM elements
[
15
]
, and it is not conforming with
\mathbf{V}
. Its basic approximation property, locally on
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, is that for all
\bm{v}\in\mathbf{H}^{s}(K)
, there exists an interpolant
\bm{v}_{I}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}(K)
such that

(3.4)

\|\bm{v}-\bm{v}_{I}\|_{0,K}+h_{K}|\bm{v}-\bm{v}_{I}|_{1,K}+h_{K}^{2}|\bm{v}-\bm{v}_{I}|_{2,K}\lesssim h_{K}^{s}|\bm{v}|_{s,K},\qquad 2\leq s\leq k,

see, e.g.,
[
15
,
17
,
35
]
.

3.1.
Formulation with continuous fluid pressure

The Galerkin finite element formulation then reads: Find
(\bm{u}_{h},p^{\mathrm{P}}_{h},\varphi_{h})\in\mathbf{V}_{h}\times\mathrm{Q}^{\mathrm{P}}_{h}\times\mathrm{Z}_{h}
such that:

(3.5a)

\displaystyle a_{1}^{h}(\bm{u}_{h},\bm{v}_{h})

\displaystyle\;+

\displaystyle\;{b}_{1}(\bm{v}_{h},\varphi_{h})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;F(\bm{v}_{h})

\displaystyle\quad\forall\bm{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h},

(3.5b)

\displaystyle{-}\tilde{a}_{2}(p_{h}^{\mathrm{P}},q_{h}^{\mathrm{P}})

\displaystyle\;{-}

\displaystyle a_{2}(p_{h}^{\mathrm{P}},q_{h}^{\mathrm{P}})

\displaystyle\;{+}

\displaystyle\;b_{2}(q_{h}^{\mathrm{P}},\,\varphi_{h})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;G(q_{h}^{\mathrm{P}})

\displaystyle\quad\forall q_{h}^{\mathrm{P}}\in\mathrm{Q}_{h}^{\mathrm{P}},

(3.5c)

\displaystyle{b}_{1}(\bm{u}_{h},\psi_{h})

\displaystyle\;+\;

\displaystyle b_{2}(p_{h}^{\mathrm{P}},\psi_{h})

\displaystyle\;-

\displaystyle\;a_{3}(\varphi_{h},\psi_{h})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\quad\forall\psi_{h}\in\mathrm{Z}_{h},

where
a_{1}^{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
is the discrete version of the bilinear form
a_{1}(\cdot,\cdot)
and it is defined using a symmetric interior penalty from
[
35
]
(see also
[
33
]
for its use in the context of poroelasticity)

\displaystyle a^{h}_{1}(\bm{u}_{h},\bm{v}_{h})

\displaystyle\coloneqq 2(\mu\bm{\varepsilon}_{h}(\bm{u}_{h}),\bm{\varepsilon}_{h}(\bm{v}_{h}))_{0,\Omega}-2\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}{\cup\Gamma_{D}^{*}}}\bigl(\langle{\left\{\kern-3.01385pt\left\{\mu\bm{\varepsilon}_{h}(\bm{u}_{h})\right\}\kern-3.01385pt\right\}},\llbracket\bm{v}_{h}\otimes\bm{n}\rrbracket\rangle_{0,e}+\langle{\left\{\kern-3.01385pt\left\{\mu\bm{\varepsilon}_{h}(\bm{v}_{h})\right\}\kern-3.01385pt\right\}},\llbracket\bm{u}_{h}\otimes\bm{n}\rrbracket\rangle_{0,e}\bigr)

(3.6)

\displaystyle\quad+2\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}{\cup\Gamma_{D}^{*}}\setminus\mathcal{E}_{h}^{\Sigma}}\frac{\beta_{\bm{u}}}{h_{e}}\langle\mu\llbracket\bm{u}_{h}\otimes\bm{n}\rrbracket,\llbracket\bm{v}_{h}\otimes\bm{n}\rrbracket\rangle_{0,e}+2\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{\Sigma}}\frac{\beta_{\bm{u}}}{h_{e}}\langle\mu^{0}\llbracket\bm{u}_{h}\otimes\bm{n}\rrbracket,\llbracket\bm{v}_{h}\otimes\bm{n}\rrbracket\rangle_{0,e},

where
\Gamma_{D}^{*}=\Gamma_{D}^{\mathrm{P}}\cup\Gamma_{D}^{\mathrm{E}}
denotes the part of the boundary where Dirichlet conditions are imposed on displacement,
\beta_{\bm{u}}>0
is a parameter penalising the (tangential) displacement jumps (and also serving as a Nitsche parameter to enforce the tangential part of the displacement boundary condition) so that the bilinear form
a^{h}_{1}(\cdot,\cdot)
is positive definite, and

\mu^{0}=\max\{\mu^{\mathrm{E}},\mu^{\mathrm{P}}\}
.
Now we write down the above weak formulation (
3.5
) in the following compact form: Find
(\bm{u}_{h},p^{\mathrm{P}}_{h},\varphi_{h})\in\mathbf{V}_{h}\times\mathrm{Q}^{\mathrm{P}}_{h}\times\mathrm{Z}_{h}
such that

\displaystyle M_{h}(\bm{u}_{h},p^{\mathrm{P}}_{h},\varphi_{h};\bm{v}_{h},q^{\mathrm{P}}_{h},\psi_{h})

\displaystyle=F(\bm{v}_{h})+G(q_{h}^{\mathrm{P}})\quad\forall(\bm{v}_{h},q^{\mathrm{P}}_{h},\psi_{h})\in\mathbf{V}_{h}\times\mathrm{Q}^{\mathrm{P}}_{h}\times\mathrm{Z}_{h},

where the multilinear form is defined as

\displaystyle M_{h}(\bm{u}_{h},p^{\mathrm{P}}_{h},\varphi_{h};\bm{v}_{h},q^{\mathrm{P}}_{h},\psi_{h})

\displaystyle=a_{1}^{h}(\bm{u}_{h},\bm{v}_{h})+{b}_{1}(\bm{v}_{h},\varphi_{h}){-}\tilde{a}_{2}(p_{h}^{\mathrm{P}},q_{h}^{\mathrm{P}}){-}a_{2}(p_{h}^{\mathrm{P}},q_{h}^{\mathrm{P}})

\displaystyle\qquad+b_{2}(q_{h}^{\mathrm{P}},\,\varphi_{h})+{b}_{1}(\bm{u}_{h},\psi_{h})+b_{2}(p_{h}^{\mathrm{P}},\psi_{h})-a_{3}(\varphi_{h},\psi_{h}).

Continuity and coercivity also hold for the modified bilinear form
a_{1}^{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
, but they do over the discrete space
\mathbf{V}_{h}
and with respect to the following mesh-dependent and parameter-dependent broken norms

\displaystyle\left\|\bm{v}_{h}\right\|_{*,\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left\|\sqrt{2\mu}\bm{\varepsilon}_{h}(\bm{v}_{h})\right\|_{0,K}^{2}+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}\cup\Gamma_{D}^{*}\setminus\mathcal{E}_{h}^{\Sigma}}2{\mu}\frac{\beta_{\bm{u}}}{h_{e}}\left\|\llbracket\bm{v}_{h}\otimes\bm{n}\rrbracket\right\|_{0,e}^{2}+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{\Sigma}}2{\mu_{0}}\frac{\beta_{\bm{u}}}{h_{e}}\left\|\llbracket\bm{v}_{h}\otimes\bm{n}\rrbracket\right\|_{0,e}^{2},

(3.7)

\displaystyle\left\|\bm{v}_{h}\right\|_{1,\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\|\sqrt{2\mu}\bm{v}_{h}\right\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}+\left\|\bm{v}_{h}\right\|_{*,\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}\ \text{for all $\bm{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})$,}

\displaystyle\left\|\bm{v}_{h}\right\|_{2,\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\|\bm{v}_{h}\right\|_{1,\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}+\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{K}^{2}|\sqrt{2\mu}\bm{v}|_{2,K}^{2}\ \text{for all $\bm{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{H}^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{h})$,}

where, for a generic
s\geq 0
, the broken vectorial Sobolev spaces are defined as

\mathbf{H}^{s}(\mathcal{T}_{h})\coloneqq\bigl\{\bm{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega):\bm{v}_{h}|_{K}\in\mathbf{H}^{s}(K),\ K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\bigr\}.

Thanks to the discrete version of Korn’s inequality
[
14
, eq. (1.12)]
, the norms above are uniformly equivalent over the appropriate spaces.
Similarly,
for the present choice of finite element spaces, a discrete inf-sup condition for
b_{1}(\cdot,\cdot)
holds naturally in the discrete norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{1,\mathcal{T}_{h}}
when we do not have a weighting with the space-dependent parameter
\mu
(cf.
[
35
]
). However, combined with the arguments in Section
2.4
, we can assert that there exists
\hat{\xi}>0
, independent of
h
and of
\mu
, such that

\sup_{\bm{v}_{h}(\neq\bm{0})\in\mathbf{V}_{h}}\frac{b_{1}(\bm{v}_{h},\psi_{h})}{\|\bm{v}_{h}\|_{1,\mathcal{T}_{h}}}\geq\hat{\xi}\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\mu}}\psi_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}\qquad\forall\psi_{h}\in\mathrm{Z}_{h}.

3.2.
Formulation with discontinuous fluid pressure

Consider now the spaces

(3.8)

\begin{split}\mathbf{V}_{h}\coloneqq\bigl\{\bm{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{H}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits;\Omega):\bm{v}_{h}|_{K}\in[\mathbb{P}_{k+1}(K)]^{d}\quad\forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h},\quad\bm{v}_{h}\cdot\bm{n}|_{\Gamma_{D}^{\mathrm{E}}\cup\Gamma_{D}^{\mathrm{P}}}=0\bigr\},\\
\widetilde{\mathrm{Q}}^{\mathrm{P}}_{h}\coloneqq\bigl\{q^{\mathrm{P}}_{h}\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega^{\mathrm{P}}):q_{h}|_{K}\in\mathbb{P}_{k+1}(K)\quad\forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\mathrm{P}}\bigr\},\qquad\mathrm{Z}_{h}\coloneqq\bigl\{\psi_{h}\in\mathrm{Z}:\psi_{h}|_{K}\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(K)\quad\forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\bigr\}.\end{split}

The formulation in this case reads: Find
(\bm{u}_{h},p^{\mathrm{P}}_{h},\varphi_{h})\in\mathbf{V}_{h}\times\widetilde{\mathrm{Q}}^{\mathrm{P}}_{h}\times\mathrm{Z}_{h}
such that:

(3.9a)

\displaystyle a_{1}^{h}(\bm{u}_{h},\bm{v}_{h})

\displaystyle\;+

\displaystyle\;{b}_{1}(\bm{v}_{h},\varphi_{h})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;F(\bm{v}_{h})

\displaystyle\quad\forall\bm{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h},

(3.9b)

\displaystyle{-}\tilde{a}_{2}(p_{h}^{\mathrm{P}},q_{h}^{\mathrm{P}})

\displaystyle\;{-}

\displaystyle a_{2}^{h}(p_{h}^{\mathrm{P}},q_{h}^{\mathrm{P}})

\displaystyle\;{+}

\displaystyle\;b_{2}(q_{h}^{\mathrm{P}},\,\varphi_{h})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;G(q_{h}^{\mathrm{P}})

\displaystyle\quad\forall q_{h}^{\mathrm{P}}\in\widetilde{\mathrm{Q}}_{h}^{\mathrm{P}},

(3.9c)

\displaystyle{b}_{1}(\bm{u}_{h},\psi_{h})

\displaystyle\;+\;

\displaystyle b_{2}(p_{h}^{\mathrm{P}},\psi_{h})

\displaystyle\;-

\displaystyle\;a_{3}(\varphi_{h},\psi_{h})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\quad\forall\psi_{h}\in\mathrm{Z}_{h},

where
a_{2}^{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
is the discrete version of the bilinear form
a_{2}(\cdot,\cdot)
and it is defined using a symmetric interior penalty from, e.g., the classical paper
[
6
]

\displaystyle a^{h}_{2}(p_{h}^{\mathrm{P}},q_{h}^{\mathrm{P}})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\biggl(\frac{\kappa}{\eta}\nabla_{h}p_{h}^{\mathrm{P}},\nabla_{h}q_{h}^{\mathrm{P}}\biggr)_{0,\Omega}-\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{\mathrm{P}}{\cup\Gamma_{D}^{\mathrm{P}}}}\biggl(\biggl\langle{\left\{\kern-3.01385pt\left\{\frac{\kappa}{\eta}\nabla_{h}p_{h}^{\mathrm{P}}\right\}\kern-3.01385pt\right\}},\llbracket q_{h}^{\mathrm{P}}\bm{n}\rrbracket\biggr\rangle_{0,e}+\biggl\langle{\left\{\kern-3.01385pt\left\{\frac{\kappa}{\eta}\nabla_{h}q_{h}^{\mathrm{P}}\right\}\kern-3.01385pt\right\}},\llbracket p_{h}^{\mathrm{P}}\bm{n}\rrbracket\biggr\rangle_{0,e}\biggr)

(3.10)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{\mathrm{P}}{\cup\Gamma_{D}^{\mathrm{P}}}}\frac{\beta_{p^{\mathrm{P}}}}{h_{e}}\biggl\langle\frac{\kappa}{\eta}\llbracket p_{h}^{\mathrm{P}}\bm{n}\rrbracket,\llbracket q_{h}^{\mathrm{P}}\bm{n}\rrbracket\biggr\rangle_{0,e},

where
\beta_{p^{\mathrm{P}}}>0
is a parameter penalising the pressure jumps. If
\beta_{p^{\mathrm{P}}}
is sufficiently large, this yields the coercivity of the bilinear form
a^{h}_{2}
.

Next, and as done for the case of continuous pressure approximation, we write down the compact form of the above weak formulation (
3.9
): Find
(\bm{u}_{h},p^{\mathrm{P}}_{h},\varphi_{h})\in\mathbf{V}_{h}\times\widetilde{\mathrm{Q}}^{\mathrm{P}}_{h}\times\mathrm{Z}_{h}
such that

\displaystyle\widetilde{M}_{h}(\bm{u}_{h},p^{\mathrm{P}}_{h},\varphi_{h};\bm{v}_{h},q^{\mathrm{P}}_{h},\psi_{h})

\displaystyle=F(\bm{v}_{h})+G(q_{h}^{\mathrm{P}})\quad\forall(\bm{v}_{h},q^{\mathrm{P}}_{h},\psi_{h})\in\mathbf{V}_{h}\times\widetilde{\mathrm{Q}}^{\mathrm{P}}_{h}\times\mathrm{Z}_{h},

where

\displaystyle\widetilde{M}_{h}(\bm{u}_{h},p^{\mathrm{P}}_{h},\varphi_{h};\bm{v}_{h},q^{\mathrm{P}}_{h},\psi_{h})

\displaystyle=a_{1}^{h}(\bm{u}_{h},\bm{v}_{h})+{b}_{1}(\bm{v}_{h},\varphi_{h}){-}\tilde{a}_{2}(p_{h}^{\mathrm{P}},q_{h}^{\mathrm{P}}){-}a_{2}^{h}(p_{h}^{\mathrm{P}},q_{h}^{\mathrm{P}})

\displaystyle\qquad+b_{2}(q_{h}^{\mathrm{P}},\,\varphi_{h})+{b}_{1}(\bm{u}_{h},\psi_{h})+b_{2}(p_{h}^{\mathrm{P}},\psi_{h})-a_{3}(\varphi_{h},\psi_{h}).

4.
Unique solvability of the discrete problems and
a priori
error estimates

4.1.
Well-posedness analysis for formulation (
3.5
)

We proceed by means of a Fortin argument and consider the canonical interpolation operator
\Pi_{h}:\mathbf{V}\rightarrow\mathbf{V}_{h}
such that

(4.1a)

\displaystyle b_{1}(\bm{v}-\Pi_{h}\bm{v},\varphi_{h})

\displaystyle=0\qquad\forall\varphi_{h}\in\mathrm{Z}_{h},

(4.1b)

\displaystyle|\bm{v}-\Pi_{h}\bm{v}|_{s,K}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{t-s}|\bm{v}|_{t,K}\quad\forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h},

where
C
is a positive constant which depends only on the shape of
K
and
1\leq t\leq r+1
(see, e.g.,
[
13
]
).

Using the trace inequality and property (
4.1b
), we have the following bound in one of the norms from (
3.1
)

\|\bm{v}-\Pi_{h}\bm{v}\|_{\ast,\mathcal{T}_{h}}\leq\|\bm{v}\|_{\ast,\mathcal{T}_{h}}\quad\forall\bm{v}\in\mathbf{V}.

Moreover, we have

\|\Pi_{h}\bm{v}\|_{\ast,\mathcal{T}_{h}}\leq C\|\bm{v}\|_{\ast,\mathcal{T}_{h}}.

With these properties satisfied by the operator
\Pi_{h}
, we can use the continuous inf-sup condition to readily show that there exists
\xi>0
such that
a discrete inf-sup condition for the bilinear form
b_{1}
:

\displaystyle\sup_{\bm{v}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}}\frac{b_{1}(\bm{v},\varphi_{h})}{\|\bm{v}\|_{\ast,\mathcal{T}_{h}}}\geq\sup_{\Pi_{h}\bm{v}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}}\frac{b_{1}(\Pi_{h}\bm{v},\varphi_{h})}{\|\Pi_{h}\bm{v}\|_{\ast,\mathcal{T}_{h}}}=\sup_{\bm{v}\in\mathbf{V}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}}\frac{b_{1}(\bm{v},\varphi_{h})}{\|\Pi_{h}\bm{v}\|_{\ast,\mathcal{T}_{h}}}\geq\frac{1}{C}\sup_{\bm{v}\in\mathbf{V}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}}\frac{b_{1}(\bm{v},\varphi_{h})}{\|\bm{v}\|_{\ast,\mathcal{T}_{h}}}\geq\xi\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\mu}}\varphi_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}\quad\forall\varphi_{h}\in\mathrm{Z}_{h}.

We are now in a position to establish a global inf-sup condition.

Theorem 4.1
.

For every
(\bm{u}_{h},p^{\mathrm{P}}_{h},\varphi_{h})\in\mathbf{V}_{h}\times\mathrm{Q}^{\mathrm{P}}_{h}\times\mathrm{Z}_{h}
, there exists
(\bm{v}_{h},q^{\mathrm{P}}_{h},\psi_{h})\in\mathbf{V}_{h}\times\mathrm{Q}^{\mathrm{P}}_{h}\times\mathrm{Z}_{h}
with
\VERT(\bm{v}_{h},q^{\mathrm{P}}_{h},\psi_{h})\VERT\lesssim\VERT(\bm{u}_{h},p^{\mathrm{P}}_{h},\varphi_{h})\VERT
such that

M_{h}(\bm{u}_{h},p^{\mathrm{P}}_{h},\varphi_{h};\bm{v}_{h},q^{\mathrm{P}}_{h},\psi_{h})\gtrsim\VERT(\bm{u}_{h},p^{\mathrm{P}}_{h},\varphi_{h})\VERT^{2},

where

(4.2)

\VERT(\bm{v}_{h},q^{\mathrm{P}}_{h},\psi_{h})\VERT^{2}:=\left\|\bm{v}_{h}\right\|_{*,\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}+\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\mu}}\psi_{h}\|^{2}_{0,\Omega}+\frac{1}{\lambda^{\mathrm{E}}}\|\psi_{h}\|^{2}_{0,\Omega^{\mathrm{E}}}+\frac{1}{\lambda^{\mathrm{P}}}\|\psi_{h}-\alpha q^{\mathrm{P}}_{h}\|^{2}_{0,\Omega^{\mathrm{P}}}+c_{0}\|q^{\mathrm{P}}_{h}\|^{2}_{0,\Omega^{\mathrm{P}}}+\|\frac{\kappa}{\eta}\nabla q^{\mathrm{P}}_{h}\|^{2}_{0,\Omega^{\mathrm{P}}}.

Moreover, we have that

\bigl|M_{h}(\bm{u}_{h},p^{\mathrm{P}}_{h},\varphi_{h};\bm{v}_{h},q^{\mathrm{P}}_{h},\psi_{h})\bigr|\lesssim\VERT(\bm{u}_{h},p^{\mathrm{P}}_{h},\varphi_{h})\VERT\VERT(\bm{v}_{h},q^{\mathrm{P}}_{h},\psi_{h})\VERT,

for all
(\bm{u}_{h},p^{\mathrm{P}}_{h},\varphi_{h}),(\bm{v}_{h},q^{\mathrm{P}}_{h},\psi_{h})\in\mathbf{V}_{h}\times\mathrm{Q}^{\mathrm{P}}_{h}\times\mathrm{Z}_{h}
.

Proof.

Let
(\bm{u}_{h},p^{\mathrm{P}}_{h},\varphi_{h})\in\mathbf{V}_{h}\times\mathrm{Q}^{\mathrm{P}}_{h}\times\mathrm{Z}_{h}
be arbitrary. Using the definition of the multilinear form
M_{h}
, we easily obtain

\displaystyle M_{h}(\bm{u}_{h},p^{\mathrm{P}}_{h},\varphi_{h};\bm{v}_{h},0,0)=a_{1}^{h}(\bm{u}_{h},\bm{v}_{h})+{b}_{1}(\bm{v}_{h},\varphi_{h})\geq\left(\xi-\frac{1}{2\epsilon_{1}}\right)\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\mu}}\varphi_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}-\frac{\epsilon_{1}}{2}\|\bm{u}_{h}\|_{\ast,\mathcal{T}_{h}}.

Selecting
\bm{v}=\bm{u}_{h}
,
q^{\mathrm{P}}=-p_{h}^{\mathrm{P}}
and
\psi=-\varphi_{h}
we have

\displaystyle M_{h}(\bm{u}_{h},p^{\mathrm{P}}_{h},\varphi_{h},\bm{u}_{h},{-}p^{\mathrm{P}}_{h},-\varphi_{h})

\displaystyle=a_{1}^{h}(\bm{u}_{h},\bm{u}_{h})+{\tilde{a}_{2}(p_{h}^{\mathrm{P}},p_{h}^{\mathrm{P}})}+a_{2}(p_{h}^{\mathrm{P}},p_{h}^{\mathrm{P}})-2b_{2}(p_{h}^{\mathrm{P}},\,\varphi_{h})+a_{3}(\varphi_{h},\varphi_{h})

\displaystyle\geq C_{2}\|\bm{u}_{h}\|_{\ast,\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}+c_{0}\|p_{h}^{\mathrm{P}}\|_{0,\Omega^{\mathrm{P}}}^{2}+1/\lambda^{\mathrm{E}}\|\varphi_{h}\|^{2}_{0,\Omega^{\mathrm{E}}}+1/\lambda^{\mathrm{P}}\|\varphi_{h}-\alpha p^{\mathrm{P}}_{h}\|^{2}_{0,\Omega^{\mathrm{P}}}

\displaystyle\qquad+c_{0}\|p^{\mathrm{P}}_{h}\|^{2}_{0,\Omega^{\mathrm{P}}}+\|\kappa/\eta\nabla p^{\mathrm{P}}_{h}\|^{2}_{0,\Omega^{\mathrm{P}}}.

Then we can make the choice
\bm{v}=\bm{u}_{h}+\delta_{1}\bm{v}_{h}
,
q^{\mathrm{P}}=-p_{h}^{\mathrm{P}}
and
\psi=-\varphi_{h}
, leading to

\displaystyle M_{h}(\bm{u}_{h},p^{\mathrm{P}}_{h},\varphi_{h},\bm{u}_{h}+\delta_{1}\bm{v}_{h},-p^{\mathrm{P}}_{h},-\varphi_{h})

\displaystyle=M_{h}(\bm{u}_{h},p^{\mathrm{P}}_{h},\varphi_{h},\bm{u}_{h},-p^{\mathrm{P}}_{h},-\varphi_{h})+\delta_{1}M_{h}(\bm{u}_{h},p^{\mathrm{P}}_{h},\varphi_{h},\bm{v}_{h},0,0)

\displaystyle\geq C_{2}\|\bm{u}_{h}\|_{\ast,\mathcal{T}_{h}}^{2}+c_{0}\|p_{h}^{\mathrm{P}}\|_{0,\Omega^{\mathrm{P}}}^{2}+1/\lambda^{\mathrm{E}}\|\varphi_{h}\|^{2}_{0,\Omega^{\mathrm{E}}}+1/\lambda^{\mathrm{P}}\|\varphi_{h}-\alpha p^{\mathrm{P}}_{h}\|^{2}_{0,\Omega^{\mathrm{P}}}

3.5
), respectively. Then the following reliability bound holds

\VERT(\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h},p^{\mathrm{P}}-p^{\mathrm{P}}_{h},\varphi-\varphi_{h})\VERT\leq C_{\mathrm{rel}}(\Xi+\Upsilon),

where
C_{\mathrm{rel}}>0
is a constant independent of the mesh size and of the delicate model parameters.

Proof.
Using triangle inequality, we have

(5.2)

\displaystyle\VERT(\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h},p^{\mathrm{P}}-p^{\mathrm{P}}_{h},\varphi-\varphi_{h})\VERT\leq\VERT(\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h}^{c},p^{\mathrm{P}}-p^{\mathrm{P}}_{h},\varphi-\varphi_{h})\VERT+\VERT(\bm{u}_{h}^{r},0,0)\VERT.

Since
(\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h}^{c},p^{\mathrm{P}}-p_{h}^{\mathrm{P}},\varphi-\varphi_{h})\in\mathbf{V}\times\mathrm{Q}^{\mathrm{P}}\times\mathrm{Z}
, then from the stability result in Theorem
5.1
, we have

(5.3)

\displaystyle C_{2}\VERT(\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h}^{c},p^{\mathrm{P}}-p_{h}^{\mathrm{P}},\varphi-\varphi_{h})\VERT^{2}\leq\widehat{M}_{h}((\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h}^{c},p^{\mathrm{P}}-p_{h}^{\mathrm{P}},\varphi-\varphi_{h});(\bm{v},q^{\mathrm{P}},\psi)),

with
\VERT(\bm{v},q^{\mathrm{P}},\psi)\VERT\leq C_{1}\VERT(\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h}^{c},p^{\mathrm{P}}-p_{h}^{\mathrm{P}},\varphi-\varphi_{h})\VERT
.
Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\widehat{M}_{h}((\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h}^{c},p^{\mathrm{P}}-p_{h}^{\mathrm{P}},\varphi-\varphi_{h});(\bm{v},q^{\mathrm{P}},\psi))

\displaystyle\qquad=\widehat{M}_{h}((\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h},p^{\mathrm{P}}-p_{h}^{\mathrm{P}},\varphi-\varphi_{h});(\bm{v},q^{\mathrm{P}},\psi))+\widehat{M}_{h}((\bm{u}_{h}^{r},0,0;(\bm{v},q^{\mathrm{P}},\psi))

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\widehat{M}_{h}((\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h},p^{\mathrm{P}}-p_{h}^{\mathrm{P}},\varphi-\varphi_{h});(\bm{v},q^{\mathrm{P}},\psi))+\left\|\bm{u}_{h}^{r}\right\|_{*,\mathcal{T}_{h}}\VERT(\bm{v},q^{\mathrm{P}},\psi)\VERT

\displaystyle\qquad\leq F(\bm{v})+G(q^{\mathrm{P}})-\widetilde{M}_{h}((\bm{u}_{h},p_{h}^{\mathrm{P}},\varphi_{h});(\bm{v},q^{\mathrm{P}},\psi))+\left\|\bm{u}_{h}^{r}\right\|_{*,\mathcal{T}_{h}}\VERT(\bm{v},q^{\mathrm{P}},\psi)\VERT.

Then, we can employ the following identity

\displaystyle F(\textbf{I}_{h}\bm{v})+G(I_{h}q^{\mathrm{P}})-\widehat{M}_{h}((\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h},p^{\mathrm{P}}-p_{h}^{\mathrm{P}},\varphi-\varphi_{h});\textbf{I}_{h}\bm{v},I_{h}q^{\mathrm{P}},0))-K_{h}(\bm{u}_{h},\textbf{I}_{h}\bm{v})=0,

to arrive at

\displaystyle\widehat{M}_{h}((\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h}^{c},p^{\mathrm{P}}-p_{h}^{\mathrm{P}},\varphi-\varphi_{h});(\bm{v},q^{\mathrm{P}},\psi))

\displaystyle\quad\leq F(\bm{v}-\textbf{I}_{h}\bm{v})+G(q^{\mathrm{P}}-I_{h}q^{\mathrm{P}})-\widetilde{M}_{h}((\bm{u}_{h},p_{h}^{\mathrm{P}},\varphi_{h});(\bm{v}-\textbf{I}_{h}\bm{v},q^{\mathrm{P}}-I_{h}q^{\mathrm{P}},\psi))+K_{h}(\bm{u}_{h},\textbf{I}_{h}\bm{v})+\left\|\bm{u}_{h}^{r}\right\|_{*,\mathcal{T}_{h}}\VERT(\bm{v},q^{\mathrm{P}},\psi)\VERT.

Using integration by parts and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, gives

(5.4)

\displaystyle\widehat{M}_{h}

\displaystyle((\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h}^{c},p^{\mathrm{P}}-p_{h}^{\mathrm{P}},\varphi-\varphi_{h});(\bm{v},q^{\mathrm{P}},\psi))\leq C(\Xi+\Upsilon)\VERT(\bm{v},q^{\mathrm{P}},\psi)\VERT.

And then it suffices to combine (
5.2
), (
5.3
) and (
5.4
) to prove the desired assertion.

\square

5.3.1.

For each
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}

b_{K}

K

From

where

Lemma 5.2
.

The following bound for the local vectorial estimator in the bulk elasticity sub-domain holds true:

h_{K}(\mu^{\mathrm{E}})^{-1/2}\|\mathbf{R}_{1}^{\mathrm{E}}\|_{0,K}\lesssim(\mu^{\mathrm{E}})^{-1/2}h_{K}\|\bm{b}^{\mathrm{E}}-\bm{b}_{h}^{\mathrm{E}}\|_{0,K}+(\mu^{\mathrm{E}})^{-1}\|\varphi^{\mathrm{E}}-\varphi_{h}^{\mathrm{E}}\|_{0,K}+(2\mu^{\mathrm{E}})^{1/2}\|\bm{u}^{\mathrm{E}}-\bm{u}_{h}^{\mathrm{E}}\|_{0,K},\qquad K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\mathrm{E}}.

Proof.

For each
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, we can define

\bm{\zeta}|_{K}=(\mu^{\mathrm{E}})^{-1}h_{K}^{2}\mathbf{R}_{1}^{\mathrm{E}}b_{K}
.
Using (
5.5
) gives

h_{K}^{2}(\mu^{\mathrm{E}})^{-1}\|\mathbf{R}_{1}^{\mathrm{E}}\|_{0,K}^{2}\lesssim\int_{K}\mathbf{R}_{1}^{\mathrm{E}}\cdot((\mu^{\mathrm{E}})^{-1}h_{K}^{2}\mathbf{R}_{1}^{\mathrm{E}}b_{K})=\int_{K}\mathbf{R}_{1}^{\mathrm{E}}\cdot\bm{\zeta}.

Note that
\{\bm{b}^{\mathrm{E}}+\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits(2{\mu^{\mathrm{E}}}\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{u}^{\mathrm{E}})-\varphi^{\mathrm{E}}_{h}\mathbf{I})\}=\bm{0}|_{K}
.
We subtract this from the last term and then integrate using
\bm{\zeta}|_{\partial K}=\bm{0}

h_{K}^{2}(\mu^{\mathrm{E}})^{-1}\|\mathbf{R}_{1}^{\mathrm{E}}\|_{0,K}^{2}\lesssim\int_{K}(\bm{b}_{h}^{\mathrm{E}}-\bm{b}^{\mathrm{E}})\cdot\bm{\zeta}-2{\mu^{\mathrm{E}}}\int_{K}\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{u}^{\mathrm{E}}-\bm{u}_{h}^{\mathrm{E}})\cdot\nabla\bm{\zeta}-\int_{K}(\varphi^{\mathrm{E}}-\varphi_{h}^{\mathrm{E}})\nabla\cdot\bm{\zeta}.

Then we proceed to apply Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, which readily implies

\displaystyle h_{K}^{2}(\mu^{\mathrm{E}})^{-1}\|\mathbf{R}_{1}^{\mathrm{E}}\|_{0,K}^{2}\lesssim

\displaystyle((\mu^{\mathrm{E}})^{-1/2}h_{K}\|\bm{b}^{\mathrm{E}}-\bm{b}_{h}^{\mathrm{E}}\|_{0,K}+(2\mu^{\mathrm{E}})^{1/2}h_{K}\|\nabla(\bm{u}^{\mathrm{E}}-\bm{u}_{h}^{\mathrm{E}})\|_{0,K}+(\mu^{\mathrm{E}})^{-1/2}h_{K}\|\varphi^{\mathrm{E}}-\varphi_{h}^{\mathrm{E}}\|_{0,K}))

\displaystyle((\mu^{\mathrm{E}})^{1/2}\|\bm{\nabla}\bm{\zeta}\|_{0,K}+(\mu^{\mathrm{E}})^{1/2}h_{K}^{-1}\|\bm{\zeta}\|_{0,K}).

We can complete the proof using the following result

\displaystyle(\mu^{\mathrm{E}})^{1/2}\|\bm{\nabla}\bm{\zeta}\|_{0,K}+(\mu^{\mathrm{E}})^{1/2}h_{K}^{-1}\|\bm{\zeta}\|_{0,K}

\displaystyle\lesssim(\mu^{\mathrm{E}})^{1/2}(\|\bm{\nabla}\bm{\zeta}\|_{0,K}+h_{K}^{-1}\|\bm{\zeta}\|_{0,K})

\displaystyle\lesssim(\mu^{\mathrm{E}})^{1/2}h_{K}^{-1}\|\bm{\zeta}\|_{0,K}

\displaystyle=h_{K}(\mu^{\mathrm{E}})^{-1/2}\|\mathbf{R}_{1}^{\mathrm{E}}\|_{0,K}.

\square

.

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{1}{\frac{1}{\mu^{\mathrm{E}}}+\frac{1}{\lambda^{\mathrm{E}}}}}\|\mathbf{R}_{2}^{\mathrm{E}}\|_{0,K}\lesssim(\lambda^{\mathrm{E}})^{-1/2}\|\varphi-\varphi_{h}\|_{0,K}+\sqrt{2\mu^{\mathrm{E}}}\|\bm{\nabla}(\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{h})\|_{0,K},\qquad K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\mathrm{E}}.

\square

\displaystyle=\sum_{K\in P_{e}\cap\Omega^{\mathrm{E}}}\int_{K}(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits(2{\mu^{\mathrm{E}}}(\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{u}^{\mathrm{E}}_{h})-\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{u}^{\mathrm{E}})))+\nabla(\varphi_{h}^{\mathrm{E}}-\varphi^{\mathrm{E}}))\cdot\bm{\zeta}_{e}

\displaystyle\qquad-\sum_{K\in P_{e}\cap\Omega^{\mathrm{E}}}\int_{K}(2{\mu^{\mathrm{E}}}(\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{u}^{\mathrm{E}}_{h})-\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{u}^{\mathrm{E}}))-(\varphi_{h}^{\mathrm{E}}-\varphi^{\mathrm{E}})\mathbf{I}):\bm{\nabla}\bm{\zeta}_{e}

\displaystyle\qquad-\sum_{K\in P_{e}\cap\Omega^{\mathrm{P}}}\int_{K}(\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits(2{\mu^{\mathrm{P}}}(\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{u}^{\mathrm{P}}_{h})-\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{u}^{\mathrm{P}})))+\nabla(\varphi_{h}^{\mathrm{P}}-\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}))\cdot\bm{\zeta}_{e}

\displaystyle\qquad-\sum_{K\in P_{e}\cap\Omega^{\mathrm{P}}}\int_{K}(2{\mu^{\mathrm{P}}}(\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{u}^{\mathrm{P}}_{h})-\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{u}^{\mathrm{P}}))+(\varphi_{h}^{\mathrm{P}}-\varphi^{\mathrm{P}})\mathbf{I}):\bm{\nabla}\bm{\zeta}_{e}.

Note that
\bm{b}^{\mathrm{P}}+\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits(2{\mu^{\mathrm{P}}}\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{u}^{\mathrm{P}})-p^{\mathrm{P}}\mathbf{I})=\bm{0}|_{K}
and
\bm{b}^{\mathrm{E}}+\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits(2{\mu^{\mathrm{E}}}\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{u}^{\mathrm{E}})-p^{\mathrm{E}}\mathbf{I})=\bm{0}|_{K}
. Then, we can assert that

\displaystyle\frac{h_{e}}{\mu^{\mathrm{E}}+\mu^{\mathrm{P}}}\|\mathbf{R}_{\Sigma}\|_{0,e}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{K\in P_{e}\cap\Omega^{\mathrm{E}}}\int_{K}\left((\bm{b}_{h}^{\mathrm{E}}-\bm{b}^{\mathrm{E}})\cdot\bm{\zeta}_{e}-\int_{K}2{\mu^{\mathrm{E}}}(\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{u}^{\mathrm{E}}_{h})-\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{u}^{\mathrm{E}})):\bm{\nabla}\bm{\zeta}_{e}+\int_{K}(p_{h}^{\mathrm{E}}-p^{\mathrm{E}})\nabla\cdot\bm{\zeta}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{K\in P_{e}\cap\Omega^{\mathrm{E}}}\int_{K}\mathbf{R}_{1}^{\mathrm{E}}\cdot\bm{\zeta}_{e}+\sum_{K\in P_{e}\cap\Omega^{\mathrm{P}}}\int_{K}\mathbf{R}_{1}^{\mathrm{P}}\cdot\bm{\zeta}_{e}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{K\in P_{e}\cap\Omega^{\mathrm{P}}}\int_{K}\left((\bm{b}_{h}^{\mathrm{P}}-\bm{b}^{\mathrm{P}})\cdot\bm{\zeta}_{e}-\int_{K}2{\mu^{\mathrm{P}}}(\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{u}^{\mathrm{P}}_{h})-\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{u}^{\mathrm{P}})):\bm{\nabla}\bm{\zeta}_{e}+\int_{K}(p_{h}^{\mathrm{P}}-p^{\mathrm{P}})\nabla\cdot\bm{\zeta}\right).

Applying Cauchy–Schwarz inequality gives

\displaystyle\frac{h_{e}}{\mu^{\mathrm{E}}+\mu^{\mathrm{P}}}\|\mathbf{R}_{e}\|_{0,e}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{K\in P_{e}\cap\Omega^{\mathrm{E}}}((2\mu^{\mathrm{E}})^{-1/2}h_{K}\|\bm{b}^{\mathrm{E}}-\bm{b}_{h}^{\mathrm{E}}\|_{0,K}+(2\mu^{\mathrm{E}})^{-1/2}\|\varphi^{\mathrm{E}}-\varphi_{h}^{\mathrm{E}}\|_{0,K}+(2\mu)^{1/2}\|\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{u}^{\mathrm{E}}_{h})-\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{u}^{\mathrm{E}})\|_{0,K})\times

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad((2\mu^{\mathrm{E}})^{1/2}\|\bm{\nabla}\bm{\zeta}\|_{0,K}+(2\mu^{\mathrm{E}})^{1/2}h_{K}^{-1}\|\bm{\zeta}\|_{0,K})

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{K\in P_{e}\cap\Omega^{\mathrm{P}}}((2\mu^{\mathrm{P}})^{-1/2}h_{K}\|\bm{b}^{\mathrm{P}}-\bm{b}_{h}^{\mathrm{P}}\|_{0,K}+(2\mu^{\mathrm{P}})^{-1/2}\|\varphi^{\mathrm{P}}-\varphi_{h}^{\mathrm{P}}\|_{0,K}+(2\mu)^{1/2}\|\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{u}^{\mathrm{P}}_{h})-\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{u}^{\mathrm{P}})\|_{0,K})\times

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad((\mu^{\mathrm{P}})^{1/2}\|\bm{\nabla}\bm{\zeta}\|_{0,K}+(\mu^{\mathrm{P}})^{1/2}h_{K}^{-1}\|\bm{\zeta}\|_{0,K}).

And as a consequence of the bounds

\displaystyle(2\mu^{\mathrm{E}})^{1/2}\|\bm{\nabla}\bm{\zeta}\|_{0,K}+(2\mu^{\mathrm{E}})^{1/2}h_{K}^{-1}\|\bm{\zeta}\|_{0,K}

\displaystyle\lesssim(2\mu^{\mathrm{E}})^{1/2}h_{K}^{-1}\|\bm{\zeta}\|_{0,K}\lesssim h_{e}^{1/2}(\mu^{\mathrm{E}}+\mu^{\mathrm{P}})^{-1/2}\|\mathbf{R}_{e}\|_{0,e},

\displaystyle(2\mu^{\mathrm{P}})^{1/2}\|\bm{\nabla}\bm{\zeta}\|_{0,K}+(2\mu^{\mathrm{P}})^{1/2}h_{K}^{-1}\|\bm{\zeta}\|_{0,K}

\displaystyle\lesssim(2\mu^{\mathrm{P}})^{1/2}h_{K}^{-1}\|\bm{\zeta}\|_{0,K}\lesssim h_{e}^{1/2}(\mu^{\mathrm{E}}+\mu^{\mathrm{P}})^{-1/2}\|\mathbf{R}_{e}\|_{0,e},

the desired estimates hold true.

\square

6.
Robust block preconditioning

Building upon the analysis results in Sections
3
and
4
, our goal now is to construct norm-equivalent block diagonal preconditioners for the discrete systems (
3.5
) and (
3.9
) that are robust with respect to (e.g., high interface contrast in the) physical parameters and mesh size
h
.

To this end, we begin by writing system (
2.4
) in the following operator form
\mathcal{M}\vec{x}=\mathcal{J}
, with
\vec{x}=(\bm{u},p^{\mathrm{P}},\varphi)
,
\mathcal{J}=(F,G,0)
, and

(6.1)

\mathcal{M}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{A}_{1}&0&\mathcal{B}^{\prime}_{1}\\
0&-\mathcal{C}_{1}&\mathcal{B}^{\prime}_{2}\\
\mathcal{B}_{1}&\mathcal{B}_{2}&-\mathcal{C}_{2}\end{pmatrix}.

The block operators in
\mathcal{M}
are induced by the respective bilinear forms as:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{1}

\displaystyle:\mathbf{V}\to\mathbf{V}^{\prime},\quad\langle\mathcal{A}_{1}(\bm{u}),\bm{v}\rangle:=a_{1}(\bm{u},\bm{v})=\int_{\Omega}2\mu\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{u}):\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{v}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{1}

\displaystyle:\mathbf{V}\to\mathrm{Z}^{\prime},\quad\langle\mathcal{B}_{1}(\bm{v}),\psi\rangle:=b_{1}(\bm{v},\psi)=-\int_{\Omega}\psi\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\bm{v},

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{2}

\displaystyle:\mathrm{Q}^{\mathrm{P}}\to\mathrm{Z}^{\prime},\quad\langle\mathcal{B}_{2}(p^{\mathrm{P}}),\psi\rangle:=b_{2}(p^{\mathrm{P}},\psi)=\int_{\Omega^{\mathrm{P}}}\frac{\alpha}{\lambda}p^{\mathrm{P}}\psi,

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{1}

\displaystyle:\mathrm{Q}^{\mathrm{P}}\to{\mathrm{Q}^{\mathrm{P}}}^{\prime},\quad\langle\mathcal{C}_{1}(p^{\mathrm{P}}),q^{\mathrm{P}}\rangle:=\tilde{a}_{2}(p^{\mathrm{P}},q^{\mathrm{P}})+a_{2}(p^{\mathrm{P}},q^{\mathrm{P}})=\int_{\Omega^{\mathrm{P}}}\left(\left(c_{0}+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{\lambda}\right)p^{\mathrm{P}}q^{\mathrm{P}}+\frac{\kappa}{\eta}\nabla p^{\mathrm{P}}\nabla q^{\mathrm{P}}\right),

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{2}

\displaystyle:\mathrm{Z}\to\mathrm{Z}^{\prime},\quad\langle\mathcal{C}_{2}(\varphi),\psi\rangle=a_{3}(\phi,\psi):=\frac{1}{\lambda}\int_{\Omega}\varphi\psi.

Similarly, the discrete systems (
3.5
) and (
3.9
) can be cast, respectively, in the following matrix block-form:

(6.2)

\underbrace{\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{A}_{1h}&0&\mathcal{B}^{\prime}_{1}\\
0&-\mathcal{C}_{1}&\mathcal{B}^{\prime}_{2}\\
\mathcal{B}_{1}&\mathcal{B}_{2}&-\mathcal{C}_{2}\end{pmatrix}}_{=:\mathcal{M}_{h}}\begin{pmatrix}\bm{u}_{h}\\
p_{h}^{\mathrm{P}}\\
\varphi_{h}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}F\\
G\\
0\end{pmatrix},\quad\text{and}\quad\underbrace{\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{A}_{1h}&0&\mathcal{B}^{\prime}_{1}\\
0&-\mathcal{C}_{1h}&\mathcal{B}^{\prime}_{2}\\
\mathcal{B}_{1}&\mathcal{B}_{2}&-\mathcal{C}_{2}\end{pmatrix}}_{=:\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{h}}\begin{pmatrix}\bm{u}_{h}\\
p_{h}^{\mathrm{P}}\\
\varphi_{h}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}F\\
G\\
0\end{pmatrix},

where
\mathcal{M}_{h}
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{h}
are induced by the multilinear forms
M_{h}
and
\widetilde{M}_{h}
, respectively, and

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{1h}

\displaystyle:\mathbf{V}_{h}\to\mathbf{V}_{h}^{\prime},\quad\langle\mathcal{A}_{1h}(\bm{u}_{h}),\bm{v}_{h}\rangle:=a_{1}^{h}(\bm{u}_{h},\bm{v}_{h}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{1h}

\displaystyle:\mathrm{Q}_{h}^{\mathrm{P}}\to{\mathrm{Q}_{h}^{\mathrm{P}}}^{\prime},\quad\langle\mathcal{C}_{1}(p^{\mathrm{P}}_{h}),q^{\mathrm{P}}_{h}\rangle:=\tilde{a}_{2}(p^{\mathrm{P}}_{h},q^{\mathrm{P}}_{h})+a^{h}_{2}(p^{\mathrm{P}}_{h},q^{\mathrm{P}}_{h}).

Next, and following
[
37
]
(see also
[
31
, Remark 5]
and
[
30
]
), a preconditioner for the linear systems in (
6.2
) can be constructed from the discrete version of the continuous Riesz map block-diagonal operator. This latter continuous map is defined as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}:\mathbf{V}\times\mathrm{Q}^{\mathrm{P}}\times\mathrm{Z}\to(\mathbf{V}\times\mathrm{Q}^{\mathrm{P}}\times\mathrm{Z})^{\prime},

(6.3)

\displaystyle\quad\mathcal{P}:=\begin{pmatrix}[\mathcal{A}_{1}]^{-1}&0&0\\
0&\![\mathcal{C}_{1}]^{-1}&0\\
0&0&[\mathcal{C}_{2}^{\prime}]^{-1}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}2\mu\mathop{\mathbf{div}}\nolimits\bm{\varepsilon}&0&0\\
0&\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\left(C_{0}+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{\lambda}\right)\mathbf{I}-\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits(\frac{\kappa}{\eta}\nabla)&0\\
0&0&\!\!\!\!\!\!\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}+\frac{1}{2\mu}\right)\mathbf{I}\end{pmatrix}^{-1}.

Note that, when comparing the previous expression with the main block diagonal of (
6.1
),
\mathcal{C}_{2}
is replaced by
\mathcal{C}_{2}^{\prime}
, which contains the additional term
\frac{1}{2\mu}
. Furthermore, we define the discrete weighted space
\bm{X}_{h,\epsilon}:=\mathbf{V}_{h}\times\mathrm{Q}_{h}^{\mathrm{P}}\times\mathrm{Z}_{h}
which contains all triplets
(\bm{u}_{h},p_{h}^{\mathrm{P}},\varphi_{h})
that are bounded in the discrete weighted norm
\VERT\cdot\VERT
, and, similarly,
\bm{X}_{h,\epsilon,\ast}:=\mathbf{V}_{h}\times\widetilde{\mathrm{Q}}_{h}^{\mathrm{P}}\times\mathrm{Z}_{h}
, with the norm
\VERT\cdot\VERT_{\ast}
, in the discontinuous fluid pressure case. Here the subindex
\epsilon
represents all weighting parameters
(\mu,c_{0},\alpha,\lambda,\kappa,\eta)
.

Note that the discrete solution operator
\mathcal{M}_{h}
(and also
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{h}
for the case of discontinuous pressure) is self-adjoint and indefinite on
\bm{X}_{h,\epsilon}
(resp. on
\bm{X}_{h,\epsilon,\ast}
). The stability of this operator in the triple norm has been proven in Theorem
4.1
, which implies that it is a uniform isomorphism (see also Theorem
4.2
for the case of discontinuous pressure and using the norm
\VERT\cdot\VERT_{\ast}
). Based on the discrete solution operators and the Riesz map (
6
), we have the following form for the discrete preconditioners:

(6.4)

\mathcal{P}_{h}=\begin{pmatrix}[\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{1h}]^{-1}&0&0\\
0&[\mathcal{C}_{1}]^{-1}&0\\
0&0&[\mathcal{C}_{2}^{\prime}]^{-1}\end{pmatrix},\quad\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{h}=\begin{pmatrix}[\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{1h}]^{-1}&0&0\\
0&[\mathcal{C}_{1h}]^{-1}&0\\
0&0&[\mathcal{C}_{2}^{\prime}]^{-1}\end{pmatrix},

where
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{1h}
is defined as follows:

\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{1h}:\mathbf{V}_{h}\to\mathbf{V}_{h}^{\prime},\quad\langle\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{1h}(\bm{u}_{h}),\bm{v}_{h}\rangle:=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}2\mu(\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{u}_{h}),\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{v}_{h}))_{K}+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}\cup\Gamma_{D}^{*}}\frac{2\mu\beta_{\bm{u}}}{h_{e}}\langle\llbracket\bm{u}_{h}\otimes\bm{n}\rrbracket,\llbracket\bm{v}_{h}\otimes\bm{n}\rrbracket\rangle_{e},

i.e., the operator defining the
\|\cdot\|_{*,\mathcal{T}_{h}}
norm.

The preconditioners
\mathcal{P}_{h},\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{h}
represent self-adjoint and positive-definite operators. They can be thus used to accelerate the convergence of the MINRES iterative solver for the solution of the symmetric indefinite linear systems (
3.5
) and (
3.9
), respectively. A suitable norm equivalence result (see, e.g., Remark
4.2
) implies that the matrices in (
6.4
) are indeed canonical block-diagonal preconditioners which are robust with respect to all model parameters. In addition, and owing to the discrete inf-sup conditions stated in Section
3
, we can state that the discrete preconditioners are also robust with respect to the discretisation parameter
h
. This will be confirmed with suitable numerical experiments in Section
7.5
.

\Omega^{\mathrm{P}}=(0,1)\times(0,0.5)

\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{\Sigma}}\left({\left\{\kern-3.01385pt\left\{\bm{v}\right\}\kern-3.01385pt\right\}},\llbracket(2\mu\bm{\varepsilon}(\bm{u})-\varphi\mathbf{I})\bm{n}\rrbracket\right)_{0,e},

7.2.
Verification of
a posteriori
error estimates

To assess the performance of the proposed estimators, we use the L-shaped domain
\Omega=(-1,1)^{2}\setminus(0,1)^{2}
, the interface is zig-zag-shaped and going from the reentrant corner
(0,0)
to the bottom-left corner of the domain
(-1,-1)
, and the porous domain is the one above the interface. We consider manufactured solutions with high gradients near the reentrant corner

\bm{u}=10^{-2}\begin{pmatrix}((x-x_{a})^{2}+(y-y_{a})^{2})^{-2/3}\\
((x-x_{a})^{2}+(y-y_{a})^{2})^{-2/3}\end{pmatrix},\quad p^{\mathrm{P}}=((x-x_{a})^{2}+(y-y_{a})^{2})^{-2/3},

with
(x_{a},y_{a})=(0.01,0.01)
.
We employ adaptive mesh refinement consisting in the usual steps of solving, then computing the local and global estimators, marking, refining, and smoothing. The marking of elements for refinement follows the classical Dörfler approach
[
21
]
: a given
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
is
marked
(added to the marking set
\mathcal{M}_{h}\subset\mathcal{T}_{h}
) whenever the local error indicator
\Xi_{K}
satisfies

\sum_{K\in\mathcal{M}_{h}}\Xi^{2}_{K}\geq\zeta\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\Xi_{K}^{2},

where
\zeta
is a user-defined bulk density parameter. All edges in the elements in
\mathcal{M}_{h}
are marked for refinement. Additional edges are marked for the sake of closure, and an additional smoothing step (Laplacian smoothing on the refined mesh to improve the shape regularity of the new mesh) is applied before starting a new iteration of the algorithm. When computing convergence rates under adaptive mesh refinement, we use the expression

\texttt{r}_{(\cdot)}=-2\log(e_{(\cdot)}/\tilde{e}_{(\cdot)})[\log({\tt DoF}/\widetilde{{\tt DoF}})]^{-1}.

7.5.
Evaluation of preconditioning robustness

We thoroughly evaluated the robustness of
\mathcal{P}_{h}
in (
6.4
) for a wide span of physical parameter-value ranges of interest, different mesh resolutions, H(div)-conforming approximations for the displacements (in particular, BDM and Raviart–Thomas), different rectangular domain shapes, and elastic and poroelastic rectangular subdomain shapes.
Overall, our results confirm an asymptotically constant number of MINRES iterations with mesh resolution even with material parameters that exhibit very large jumps accross the interface (e.g., we tested up to 3 orders of magnitude jumps in
\mu
and
\lambda
), and/or very small or very large values
(e.g.,
\kappa\in[10^{-3},10^{-5},10^{-7}]\text{ m}^{2}
;
\lambda,\mu\in[1,10^{3},10^{6},10^{9}]
Pa), including the extreme cases of near incompressibility, near impermeability, and near zero storativity. We note, however, that the
value of the penalty parameter
\beta_{\bm{u}}
has to be chosen carefully (typically via numerical experimentation), as it can have a significant impact on preconditioner efficiency.

For conciseness, in this section, we only show results for the particularly challenging (and realistic) combination of physical parameter values corresponding to the problem in Section
7.4
. We use the upper part of the domain in this problem, namely the rectangular domain
\Omega=[0,0.25]\times[0.17,0.25]
, with elastic subdomain spanning the
thin stripe
[0,0.25]\times[0.17,0.1705]
. As usual, the poroelastic domain is defined as the complement of
the elastic domain. We used a triangular uniform mesh generator parametrizable by the number of layers of triangles in the thinner dimension of the elastic domain, which we refer to as
\ell
. Note that
h=0.05/\ell
. We tested in particular with three mesh resolutions corresponding to
\ell=2,4,8
. We solve problem (
3.5
) with the known manufactured solution described in Section
7.1
. We report results only for BDM with
k=0
, although we stress that the number of iterations obtained for Raviart-Thomas with
k=1
were very similar to those reported herein. The preconditioned MINRES solver is used in conjunction with
\mathcal{P}_{h}
in (
6.4
), and convergence is claimed whenever the Euclidean norm of the (unpreconditioned) residual of the whole system is reduced by a factor of
10^{6}
. The action of the preconditioner was computed by LU decomposition in all cases. As an illustration of the challenge at hand, for
\ell=2
,
\beta_{\bm{u}}=20
, the condition number of the unpreconditioned system (
3.5
) is approximately as large as
1.25\times 10^{26}
(as computed by the
cond
Julia function).
By a suitable scaling of the system, this large number could be reduced to
1.25\times 10^{14}
(i.e., by 12 orders of magnitude). In particular, we scale (
2.1a
) and (
2.1d
) with
1/\max(\mu^{\mathrm{P}},\mu^{\mathrm{E}})
and solve for the scaled pressures
\varphi^{\mathrm{P,E}}\leftarrow\varphi^{\mathrm{P,E}}/\max(\mu^{\mathrm{P}},\mu^{\mathrm{E}})
and
p^{\mathrm{P}}\leftarrow p^{\mathrm{P}}/\max(\mu^{\mathrm{P}},\mu^{\mathrm{E}})
; see also
scale_parameters
function in
[
7
]
for full details. We stress that, while the number of preconditioned MINRES iterations
to solve the original and scaled systems was almost equivalent in all cases tested, we observed a much more reliable behaviour of the error curves for the scaled equations, in particular, under the assumption of a relatively coarse fixed residual tolerance. Thus, in the sequel, we report the errors we obtained in the presence of such an scaling. Finally, in order to study the dependence of the number of iterations and error on the penalisation parameter
\beta_{\bm{u}}
, we tested with different values of
\beta_{\bm{u}}\in[10,5000]
.

In Figure
7.5
(left), we report the number of preconditioned MINRES iterations versus number of degrees of freedom. While the value chosen for
\beta_{\bm{u}}
has an impact on the number of iterations for fixed mesh resolution, we can observe nevertheless an asymptotically constant number of iterations with mesh resolution for most of the values of
\beta_{\bm{u}}
tested. This can be better observed in Figure
7.5
(right), where we report the number of iterations versus
\beta_{\bm{u}}
for different values of mesh resolution
\ell
; the number of iterations to achieve convergence is approximately constant with mesh resolution except for values of
\beta_{\bm{u}}
within
[50,200]
. In order to have a more complete picture, in the curves labeled as “solver=pminres” in Figure
7.6
, we report the errors in the displacements (left), fluid pressure (center), and global pressure (right) versus
\beta_{\bm{u}}
, obtained with the preconditioned MINRES solver. As a reference, in the curves labeled as “solver=lu” in Figure
7.6
, we report the corresponding errors obtained with a robust sparse direct solver (UMFPACK). We can observe that the value of
\beta_{\bm{u}}
has an impact on the error obtained,
particularly noticeable in the case of the preconditioned MINRES solver; only for the smallest value of
\beta_{\bm{u}}=10
, the accuracy of the sparse direct solver can be matched for all unknowns. We have checked that, as expected, as we use finer residual tolerances, the error curves for the MINRES solver become closer to those obtained with the sparse direct solver (at the price of some more iterations).

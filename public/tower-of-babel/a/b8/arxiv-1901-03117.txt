Ergodic Decomposition for Inverse Wishart Measures on Infinite Positive-Definite Matrices
Theodoros ASSIOTIS

Abstract
The ergodic unitarily invariant measures on the space of infinite Hermitian matrices have been classified by Pickrell and Olshanski–Vershik. The much-studied complex inverse Wishart measures form a projective family, thus giving rise to a unitarily invariant measure on infinite positive-definite matrices. In this paper we completely solve the corresponding problem of ergodic decomposition for this measure.

Introduction

1.1
Informal introduction and historical overview

Since their introduction in the 1920’s
[
34
]
the Wishart measures have been ubiquitous in mathematics, physics and statistics. They appear in diverse fields, from statistical analysis
[
17
,
19
]
to stochastic processes
[
3
,
28
]
and free probability
[
8
,
22
]
. More recently, there has been renewed interest in connection to their applications in quantum transport
[
10
,
11
]
and their enumerative properties, in particular relations to Hurwitz numbers
[
9
]
.

This paper stems from the following remarkable property, and its consequences, of these measures: the inverse Wishart measures
\big\{\mathsf{M}^{(\nu),N}\big\}_{N\geq 1}
(
\nu
is a real parameter greater than
-1
) defined in (
1.3
) on (positive-definite) Hermitian matrices form a projective family under the so-called corners maps given in (
1.1
) below. The goal of this paper is to study the corresponding unitarily invariant, namely invariant under conjugation by unitary matrices, measure
\mathsf{M}^{(\nu)}
on infinite (positive-definite) Hermitian matrices and describe explicitly how it decomposes into ergodic components.

These ergodic measures for the action by conjugation of the inductive limit of unitary groups on infinite Hermitian matrices have been classified in classical works of Pickrell
[
26
]
and Olshanski and Vershik
[
24
]
. They are parametrized by the infinite dimensional space
\Omega
defined in (
1.2
) below and depend on a set of parameters
\big(\{\alpha^{+}\},\{\alpha^{-}\},\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\big)\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\infty}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\infty}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
. As we will see in Section
3
, the
\alpha
parameters are asymptotic eigenvalues and
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
are related to the asymptotic trace and asymptotic sum of squares respectively; the parameter
\gamma_{2}
is also called the ‘Gaussian component’. The study of
\gamma_{1}
especially and also
\gamma_{2}
is a much more difficult task compared to describing the
\alpha
’s.

The problem of ergodic decomposition of certain distinguished unitarily invariant measures on infinite Hermitian matrices was initiated by Borodin and Olshanski in
[
2
]
. They considered the Hua–Pickrell measures depending on a complex parameter
\mathsf{s}
. These measures were first studied by Hua in his classical book
[
18
]
and implicit in his calculations is consistency under the corners maps, see also Neretin’s generalization
[
23
]
. Borodin and Olshanski described the
\alpha
parameters and proved that for
\mathsf{s}=0
,
\gamma_{2}=0
. The determination of
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
was left open for many years until recently in a breakthrough work
[
27
]
Qiu proved that
\gamma_{2}=0
for general parameter
\mathsf{s}
and completely described
\gamma_{1}
for real
\mathsf{s}
(see Remark
5.5
for more on this restriction). In the case of the inverse Wishart measures
\mathsf{M}^{(\nu)}
we are able to completely describe all the parameters for all
\nu>-1
. This is achieved in Theorem
1.6
, the main result of this paper.

A closely related problem is the ergodic decomposition of the so-called Pickrell measures
[
25
]
(depending on a real parameter
\mathfrak{s}
) on infinite square complex matrices. The ergodic unitarily invariant (by multiplication to the left and to the right) measures on infinite square complex matrices have also been classified. These are parametrized by a different infinite dimensional space that is a subset of
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\infty}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
(there is no analogue of
\gamma_{1}
). The explicit description of the ergodic decomposition has been settled in a series of papers by Bufetov
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
(see also
[
7
]
), which have been very influential for us. We should mention that the papers of Bufetov
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
and Qiu
[
27
]
also study the infinite case of the problem of ergodic decomposition, namely when the corresponding matrix measures no longer have finite mass. Since this requires quite different techniques we will not consider it in this work.

Finally, before closing this informal introduction we remark that a key role in all these papers
[
2
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
27
]
is played by orthogonal polynomials. In the case of the Hua–Pickrell measures these are the pseudo-Jacobi polynomials and in the case of the Pickrell measures these are the Jacobi. The analogous role in this paper is played by the Bessel
[
21
]
and also Laguerre polynomials.

In the next subsections we give the necessary background to make the informal discussion above precise and state our results rigorously.

1.2
Ergodic unitarily invariant measures on infinite Hermitian matrices

Let
\mathbb{U}(N)
and
H(N)
be the group of
N\times N
unitary matrices and the space of
N\times N
Hermitian matrices respectively. Let
\mathbb{U}(\infty)
be the inductive limit of unitary groups

\displaystyle\mathbb{U}(\infty)=\mathop{\underset{\rightarrow}{\lim}}\mathbb{U}(N),

under the natural embeddings. In more explicit terms an element of
\mathbb{U}(\infty)
is an infinite block matrix whose top corner is an
N\times N
unitary matrix for some finite
N
and the other block is the (infinite) identity matrix.

Consider the so called corners maps
\pi^{N}_{N-1}\colon H(N)\to H(N-1)
defined by

\displaystyle\pi_{N-1}^{N}\big[(h_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{N}\big]=(h_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{N-1}.

(1.1)

Let
H
be the space of infinite Hermitian matrices defined as the projective limit

\displaystyle H=\mathop{\underset{\leftarrow}{\lim}}H(N),

under the corners maps. Moreover, let
H_{+}(N)\subset H(N)
denote the space of
N\times N
positive-definite matrices; namely matrices with positive eigenvalues. By Cauchy’s interlacing theorem we get that
\pi^{N}_{N-1}\colon H_{+}(N)\to H_{+}(N-1)
. Thus, we can also correctly define the projective limit
H_{+}=\underset{\leftarrow}{\lim}H_{+}(N)\subset H
.

Now,
\mathbb{U}(\infty)
acts on
H
by conjugation: for each
u\in\mathbb{U}(\infty)
we have a map
T_{u}\colon H\to H
given by
T_{u}(h)=u^{*}hu
. It is a classical theorem of Pickrell
[
26
]
and also Olshanski and Vershik
[
24
]
that ergodic measures on
H
for this action (namely ones such that all
\mathbb{U}(\infty)
-invariant subsets have mass 0 or 1) are parametrized by the infinite dimensional space
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2\infty+2}
:

\displaystyle\Omega=\Big\{\omega=(\alpha^{+},\alpha^{-},\gamma_{1},\delta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2\infty+2}=\mathbb{R}^{\infty}\times\mathbb{R}^{\infty}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\,|

\displaystyle\hphantom{\Omega=\Big\lx@text@lbrace}{}\alpha^{+}=(\alpha_{1}^{+}\geq\alpha_{2}^{+}\geq\cdots\geq 0);\,\alpha^{-}=(\alpha_{1}^{-}\geq\alpha_{2}^{-}\geq\cdots\geq 0);

\displaystyle\hphantom{\Omega=\Big\lx@text@lbrace}{}\gamma_{1}\in\mathbb{R};\,\delta\geq 0;\,\sum(\alpha_{i}^{+})^{2}+\sum(\alpha_{i}^{-})^{2}\leq\delta\Big\},

\displaystyle\gamma_{2}=\delta-\sum\big(\alpha_{i}^{+}\big)^{2}-\sum\big(\alpha_{i}^{-}\big)^{2}.

(1.2)

Observe that
\Omega
is a locally compact space. We then have the following classification theorem, see
[
24
,
26
]
, also
[
12
]
.

Theorem 1.1

(Pickrell, Olshanski–Vershik)
.

There exists a parametrization of ergodic
\mathbb{U}(\infty)
-invariant probability measures on the space
H
by the points of the space
\Omega
described as follows. Given
\omega\in\Omega
the characteristic function of the ergodic measure
M_{\omega}
is given by

\displaystyle\int_{X\in H}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{diag}(r_{1},\dots,r_{n},0,0,\dots)X)}M_{\omega}({\rm d}X)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}F_{\omega}(r_{j}),

where

\displaystyle F_{\omega}(x)={\rm e}^{{\rm i}\gamma_{1}x-\frac{\gamma_{2}}{2}x^{2}}\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{{\rm e}^{-{\rm i}\alpha_{k}^{+}x}}{1-{\rm i}\alpha_{k}^{+}x}\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{{\rm e}^{{\rm i}\alpha_{k}^{-}x}}{1+{\rm i}\alpha_{k}^{-}x}.

Note that the characteristic function
F_{\omega}
is well defined for all
\omega\in\Omega
by the fact that the sum of squares of the
\alpha
’s is finite. Also, observe that parameter
\gamma_{2}
corresponds to an infinite random Hermitian matrix with independent (subject to the Hermitian constraint) complex Gaussian entries with mean 0 and variance
\gamma_{2}
on the diagonal.

1.3
The inverse Wishart measures
\boldsymbol{\mathsf{M}^{(\nu)}}
on infinite positive-definite matrices

For
\nu>-1
, consider the complex Wishart (or Laguerre ensemble) probability measure on
N\times N
Hermitian matrices, supported on
H_{+}(N)
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{(\nu),N}({\rm d}Y)=\widetilde{\operatorname{const}_{\nu,N}}\det(Y)^{\nu}{\rm e}^{-\operatorname{Tr}Y}\mathbf{1}_{\{Y\in H_{+}(N)\}}{\rm d}Y\,

where throughout the paper for a Hermitian matrix
Y
,
{\rm d}Y
denotes Lebesgue measure on
H(N)
(we suppress dependence on
N
):

\displaystyle{\rm d}Y=\prod_{j=1}^{N}{\rm d}Y_{jj}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq N}{\rm d}\operatorname{Re}Y_{jk}{\rm d}\operatorname{Im}Y_{jk}.

The restriction
\nu>-1
is so that the normalization constant
\widetilde{\operatorname{const}_{\nu,N}}
is finite.

Under the change of variables
Y=2X^{-1}
we obtain the central object of study in this paper, the inverse Wishart probability measures on
H_{+}(N)

\displaystyle\mathsf{M}^{(\nu),N}({\rm d}X)=\operatorname{const}_{\nu,N}\det(X)^{-\nu-2N}{\rm e}^{-2\operatorname{Tr}X^{-1}}\mathbf{1}_{\{X\in H_{+}(N)\}}{\rm d}X.

(1.3)

Observe that, for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
the measure
\mathsf{M}^{(\nu),N}
is unitarily invariant, namely invariant under the action of
\mathbb{U}(N)
by conjugation. Then, we have the following consistency result:

Proposition 1.2
.

For
\nu>-1
the measures
\big\{\mathsf{M}^{(\nu),N}\big\}_{N\geq 1}
form a projective family

\displaystyle\big(\pi_{N-1}^{N}\big)_{*}\mathsf{M}^{(\nu),N}=\mathsf{M}^{(\nu),N-1}.

Thus, by Kolmogorov’s theorem we obtain a unitarily invariant measure
\mathsf{M}^{(\nu)}
on
H
that is supported on
H_{+}
.

Proposition
1.2
is proven in Section
2
as Proposition
2.3
. A key role in the proof is played by the Bessel orthogonal polynomials
[
21
]
.

Remark 1.3
.

Although we have not been able to locate Proposition
1.2
anywhere in the literature, in an equivalent form a close variant of it, for the analogous measure on real symmetric positive-definite matrices, appears to be folklore in the statistical literature, see for example
[
17
, Chapter 3]
. The argument there makes use of the technique of Schur complementation, the formula for the determinant of a block matrix and the special form of the density of
\mathcal{M}^{(\nu),N}
. The proof presented in Section
2
is completely different and makes a novel use of the connection to orthogonal polynomials.

1.4
Description of ergodic decomposition of
\boldsymbol{\mathsf{M}^{(\nu)}}

Consider the measure
\mathsf{M}^{(\nu)}
defined above for
\nu>-1
. It is a result of Borodin and Olshanski, see Proposition 4.4 in
[
2
]
, that there exists a unique probability measure
\mathfrak{m}^{(\nu)}
on
\Omega
such that

\displaystyle\mathsf{M}^{(\nu)}=\int_{\Omega}M_{\omega}\mathfrak{m}^{(\nu)}({\rm d}\omega).

Here, the equality is interpreted as integrated against a test function, see Section
3
.
The main result of this paper is the explicit description of
\mathfrak{m}^{(\nu)}
. To proceed and state it precisely we need some more definitions and background on determinantal measures.

Definition 1.4
.

Define the subset
\Omega_{0}^{+}\subset\Omega
such that
\omega=\left(\alpha^{+},\alpha^{-},\gamma_{1},\delta\right)\in\Omega_{0}^{+}
iff

\displaystyle\alpha_{i}^{-}(\omega)\equiv 0,\qquad\alpha_{j}^{+}(\omega)\neq 0,\qquad\text{for all}\quad i,j\in\mathbb{N};

\displaystyle\gamma_{2}(\omega)=\delta(\omega)-\sum_{i}\big(\alpha_{i}^{+}(\omega)\big)^{2}-\sum_{i}\big(\alpha_{i}^{-}(\omega)\big)^{2}=0;

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}(\omega)=\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}^{+}(\omega)<\infty.

In particular, on
\Omega_{0}^{+}
there is no ‘Gaussian component’ namely
\gamma_{2}(\omega)\equiv 0
and the parameter
\gamma_{1}(\omega)
is completely determined by the
\alpha_{i}^{+}(\omega)
.

Determinantal measures

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a locally compact Polish space equipped with a
\sigma
-finite reference measure
\mu
. Let
\mathsf{Conf}(\mathcal{X})
be the space of point configurations over
\mathcal{X}
. Points in a point configuration
\mathfrak{X}\in\mathsf{Conf}(\mathcal{X})
will be called particles. We can embed
\mathsf{Conf}(\mathcal{X})
in the space of finite measures on
\mathcal{X}
by
\mathfrak{X}\mapsto\sum\limits_{x\in\mathfrak{X}}\delta_{x}
and with the induced topology
\mathsf{Conf}(\mathcal{X})
is a Polish space.

A Borel probability measure
\mathbb{P}
on
\mathsf{Conf}(\mathcal{X})
is called a determinantal point process or measure, see
[
29
]
, with (Hermitian) kernel
\mathsf{K}\colon\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{C}
if for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and function
\Phi\in C_{c}\big(\mathcal{X}^{n}\big)
, continuous and of compact support, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathsf{Conf}(\mathcal{X})}\sum_{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in\mathfrak{X}}\Phi(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\mathbb{P}({\rm d}\mathfrak{X})

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\int_{\mathcal{X}^{n}}\Phi(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\det(\mathsf{K}(x_{i},x_{j}))_{i,j=1}^{n}{\rm d}\mu(x_{1})\cdots{\rm d}\mu(x_{n}),

(1.4)

where the sum is taken over ordered
n
-tuples of particles with pairwise distinct labels. The measure
\mathbb{P}
satisfying (
1.4
) completely determines the pair
(\mathsf{K},\mu)
and dropping dependence on
\mu
(usually fixed), we denote it by
\mathbb{P}_{\mathsf{K}}
.

Throughout this paper the reference measure will be Lebesgue measure. We now define a distinguished determinantal measure on
(0,\infty)
.

Definition 1.5
.

Define the Bessel kernel
\mathbb{J}_{\nu}
by

\displaystyle\mathbb{J}_{\nu}(x,y)=\frac{\sqrt{x}J_{\nu+1}(\sqrt{x})J_{\nu}(\sqrt{y})-\sqrt{y}J_{\nu+1}(\sqrt{y})J_{\nu}(\sqrt{x})}{2(x-y)},

where
J_{\nu}
is the Bessel function of order
\nu
. This kernel gives rise to the Bessel determinantal point process
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{J}_{\nu}}
on
(0,\infty)
of Tracy and Widom
[
32
]
.

We are ready to give the explicit description of the ergodic decomposition of the inverse Wishart measures
\mathsf{M}^{(\nu)}
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
\nu>-1
. The spectral measure
\mathfrak{m}^{(\nu)}
associated to
\mathsf{M}^{(\nu)}
is concentrated on
\Omega_{0}^{+}

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}^{(\nu)}\big(\Omega_{0}^{+}\big)=1.

Moreover, the law of the parameters
\alpha^{+}=\big(\alpha_{1}^{+}\geq\alpha_{2}^{+}\geq\alpha_{3}^{+}\geq\cdots\geq 0\big)
under
\mathfrak{m}^{(\nu)}
viewed as a point configuration on
(0,\infty)
is given by the determinantal point process
\mathbb{P}_{K^{\nu}_{\infty}}
with correlation kernel
K_{\infty}^{\nu}(x,y)
defined by

\displaystyle K_{\infty}^{\nu}(x,y)=\frac{8}{xy}\mathbb{J}_{\nu}\left(\frac{8}{x},\frac{8}{y}\right).

Theorem
1.6
above will be proven by an approximation procedure, the so-called ‘ergodic method’ of Olshanski and Vershik
[
24
]
, that is explained in Section
3
. For this asymptotic analysis we will exploit the relation with the Laguerre ensemble.

1.5
Organisation of the paper

In Section
2
, we prove consistency for the inverse Wishart measures. This is proved at the level of eigenvalue measures first and then lifted to matrices. The key ingredient is the backward shift equation for Bessel polynomials. In Section
3
, we recall in detail the approximation procedure of Vershik, Borodin and Olshanski. In Section
4
, we study the
\alpha
parameters. We obtain the parameters
\alpha^{+}
as limits of the Bessel orthogonal polynomial ensemble, which is a determinantal point process. In Section
5
, we obtain certain estimates for the kernel of the Bessel orthogonal polynomial ensemble. These are essential for the study of
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
. In Section
6
, we prove that
\gamma_{2}\equiv 0
. In Section
7
, we treat the
\gamma_{1}
parameter. A key role is played by positivity along with the estimates from Section
5
. Finally, in Section
8
we simply put everything together to conclude the proof of Theorem
1.6
.

2
Consistency of
\boldsymbol{\mathsf{M}^{(\nu),N}}

Let
\nu>-1
. Recall that, we are interested in the normalized (probability) measure

\displaystyle\mathsf{M}^{(\nu),N}({\rm d}X)=\operatorname{const}_{\nu,N}\det(X)^{-\nu-2N}{\rm e}^{-2\operatorname{Tr}X^{-1}}\mathbf{1}_{\{X\in H_{+}(N)\}}{\rm d}X,

where the normalizing constant
\operatorname{const}_{\nu,N}
will be given explicitly below.

By Weyl’s integration formula we get that the induced probability measure on eigenvalues in the Weyl chamber
W^{N}
(we write
W^{N}_{+}
if all coordinates are positive)

\displaystyle W^{N}=\big\{x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\colon x_{1}\geq x_{2}\geq\cdots\geq x_{N}\big\}

is given by

\displaystyle\mu_{N}^{\nu}({\rm d}x)=\overline{\operatorname{const}}_{\nu,N}\prod_{i=1}^{N}w_{N}^{\nu}(x_{i})\Delta_{N}(x)^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\{x\in W_{+}^{N}\}}{\rm d}x_{1}\cdots{\rm d}x_{N}

with the Bessel weight
w_{N}^{\nu}(\cdot)
on
(0,\infty)

\displaystyle w_{N}^{\nu}(x)=x^{-\nu-2N}{\rm e}^{-\frac{2}{x}}

and we will write

\displaystyle\Delta_{N}(x)=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq N}(x_{i}-x_{j})

for the Vandermonde determinant.

Moreover, the constants
\operatorname{const}_{\nu,N}
and
\overline{\operatorname{const}}_{\nu,N}
are related by

\displaystyle\operatorname{const}_{\nu,N}=\overline{\operatorname{const}}_{\nu,N}\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{N(N-1)}{2}}}\prod_{k=1}^{N}k!.

It will be convenient to introduce the following notation: we define the map
\mathsf{eval}_{N}\colon H(N)\to W^{N}
by
\mathsf{eval}_{N}(H)=x=(x_{1}\geq x_{2}\geq\cdots\geq x_{N})
the vector of eigenvalues of
H
in weakly decreasing order. In this notation we have

\displaystyle(\mathsf{eval}_{N})_{*}\mathsf{M}^{(\nu),N}=\mu_{N}^{\nu}.

Bessel polynomials.
The orthogonal polynomials with respect to
w_{N}^{\nu}(x)
, that are called the Bessel polynomials
[
21
]
, will be important for us. The reference for all the facts stated below is
[
20
, Chapter 9.13, pp. 244–247]
.

For
\nu>-1
, there exist
p_{0}(\cdot;\nu,N),\dots,p_{N-1}(\cdot;\nu,N)

monic
orthogonal polynomials of degree
0,\dots,N-1
with respect to
w_{N}^{\nu}
. These can be expressed in terms of hypergeometric functions but we shall not need any explicit expression here. Their norms are given by, for
n=0,\dots,N-1
,

\displaystyle\|p_{n}(\cdot;\nu,N)\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}w_{N}^{\nu}(x)p_{n}^{2}(x;\nu,N){\rm d}x

\displaystyle=-\frac{2^{2n-\nu-2N+1}}{(n-\nu-2N+1)_{n}^{2}(2n-\nu-2N+1)}\Gamma(-n+\nu+2N)n!.

Here,
(a)_{k}=\prod\limits_{i=1}^{k}(a+i-1),(a)_{0}=1
is the Pochhammer symbol.

Also, we have the following key relation, called the backward shift equation, that relates orthogonal polynomials with respect to
w_{N}^{\nu}
to orthogonal polynomials with respect to
w_{N+1}^{\nu}

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}x}\big[w_{N}^{\nu}(x)p_{n}(x;\nu,N)\big]

\displaystyle=\frac{(n+1-\nu-2N+1)_{n+1}}{(n-\nu-2N+1)_{n}}w_{N+1}^{\nu}(x)p_{n+1}(x;\nu,N+1)

\displaystyle=c(n,\nu,N)w_{N+1}^{\nu}(x)p_{n+1}(x;\nu,N+1).

Furthermore, by writing the Vandermonde determinant in terms of the monic orthogonal polynomials
p_{n}(\cdot;\nu,N)
a standard calculation gives that

\displaystyle\overline{\operatorname{const}}_{\nu,N}=\prod_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\|p_{n-1}(\cdot;\nu,N)\|_{2}^{2}}=\prod_{n=1}^{N}-\frac{(n-\nu-2N+1)_{n}^{2}(2n-\nu-2N+1)}{2^{2n-\nu-2N+1}\Gamma(-n+\nu+2N)n!}

and thus we also obtain an explicit expression for
\operatorname{const}_{\nu,N}
.

Now, we introduce the following Markov kernel
\Lambda_{N}^{N+1}
from
W^{N+1}
to
W^{N}
, defined for
x\in\mathring{W}^{N+1}
(the interior of
W^{N+1}
) as

\displaystyle\Lambda_{N}^{N+1}(x,{\rm d}y)=\frac{N!\Delta_{N}(y)}{\Delta_{N+1}(x)}\textbf{1}(y\prec x){\rm d}y,

where
y\prec x
denotes interlacing

\displaystyle x_{1}\geq y_{1}\geq x_{2}\geq\cdots\geq y_{N}\geq x_{N+1}.

This Markov kernel has a random matrix interpretation, due to Baryshnikov
[
1
]
(the required computation is already implicit in the classical book of Gelfand and Naimark
[
15
]
), which easily extends to any
x\in W^{N+1}
(even when the coordinates coincide)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{N}^{N+1}(x,\cdot)=\mathsf{Law}\big[\mathsf{eval}_{N}\big(\pi_{N}^{N+1}\big(U^{*}\mathsf{diag}(x_{1},\dots,x_{N+1})U\big)\big)\big],

where
U
is a random (Haar distributed) unitary matrix from
\mathbb{U}(N+1)
.

We will first prove consistency at the level of eigenvalues:

Proposition 2.1
.

For
\nu>-1

\displaystyle\mu_{N+1}^{\nu}\Lambda_{N}^{N+1}=\mu_{N}^{\nu},\qquad\forall\,N\geq 1.

Proof.

Since both sides are probability measures, it suffices to prove that they are equal up to multiplicative constant (we will use the notation
\propto
for this). Since
\mu_{N+1}^{\nu}
is supported on
\mathring{W}^{N+1}
we can write (note that since
y\prec x
we have
\big\{x\in W^{N+1}_{+}\big\}\Rightarrow\big\{y\in W^{N}_{+}\big\}
)

\displaystyle\big[\mu_{N+1}^{\nu}\Lambda_{N}^{N+1}\big]({\rm d}y)=N!\Delta_{N}(y)\mathbf{1}_{\{y\in W_{+}^{N}\}}{\rm d}y\int_{y\prec x\in W^{N+1}_{+}}\prod_{i=1}^{N+1}w_{N+1}^{\nu}(x_{i})\Delta_{N+1}(x){\rm d}x.

Note that we can write

\displaystyle\Delta_{N+1}(x)=\det[p_{i-1}(x_{N+2-j};\nu,N+1)]^{N+1}_{i,j=1}.

We thus need to show

\displaystyle\int_{y\prec x\in W^{N+1}_{+}}\prod_{i=1}^{N+1}w_{N+1}^{\nu}(x_{i})\det[p_{i-1}(x_{N+2-j};\nu,N+1)]^{N+1}_{i,j=1}{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\qquad{}\propto\prod_{i=1}^{N}w_{N}^{\nu}(y_{i})\det[p_{i-1}(y_{N+1-j};\nu,N)]^{N}_{i,j=1}=\prod_{i=1}^{N}w_{N}^{\nu}(y_{i})\Delta_{N}(y).

(2.1)

By multilinearity of the determinant we obtain that the l.h.s. of (
2.1
) is

\displaystyle\det\left[\int_{y_{N+2-j}}^{y_{N+1-j}}w_{N+1}^{\nu}(z)p_{i-1}(z;\nu,N+1){\rm d}z\right]^{N+1}_{i,j=1},

where
y_{0}=\infty,y_{N+1}=0
.

Now, by the backward shift equation we can perform the integral inside the determinant and obtain for
i\geq 2
:

\displaystyle\int_{y_{N+2-j}}^{y_{N+1-j}}w_{N+1}^{\nu}(z)p_{i-1}(z;\nu,N+1){\rm d}z=\frac{1}{c(i-2,\nu,N)}\big[w_{N}^{\nu}(z)p_{i-2}(z;\nu,N)\big]^{y_{N+1-j}}_{y_{N+2-j}}.

We note that when evaluated at
y_{0}=\infty
and
y_{N+1}=0

Remark 2.2

As pointed out to me by Grigori Olshanski a similar idea of using the backwards shift equation to prove consistency appears in the study of the q-zw measures on the quantized Gelfand–Tsetlin graph
[

. The corresponding orthogonal polynomials are the pseudo big

We will now, making use of Baryshnikov’s result
[

, prove consistency for the matrix measures.

Proposition 2.3
.

For
\nu>-1
, the inverse Wishart measures form a projective family

\displaystyle\big(\pi_{N}^{N+1}\big)_{*}\mathsf{M}^{(\nu),N+1}=\mathsf{M}^{(\nu),N},\qquad\forall\,N\geq 1.

Thus, by Kolmogorov’s theorem there exists a unique unitarily invariant measure
\mathsf{M}^{(\nu)}
on
H
that is supported on
H_{+}
.

Proof.

First, observe that if a measure
\mathfrak{N}
is unitarily invariant on
H(N+1)
then
\big(\pi_{N}^{N+1}\big)_{*}\mathfrak{N}
is unitarily invariant on
H(N)
. Thus, it suffices to show that

\displaystyle(\mathsf{eval}_{N})_{*}\big(\pi_{N}^{N+1}\big)_{*}\mathsf{M}^{(\nu),N+1}=(\mathsf{eval}_{N})_{*}\mathsf{M}^{(\nu),N}.

On the other hand from Proposition
2.1
we have

\displaystyle\left(\mathsf{eval}_{N+1}\right)_{*}\mathsf{M}^{(\nu),N+1}\circ\Lambda_{N}^{N+1}=\left(\mathsf{eval}_{N}\right)_{*}\mathsf{M}^{(\nu),N}.

Hence, we need to show

\displaystyle(\mathsf{eval}_{N})_{*}\big(\pi_{N}^{N+1}\big)_{*}\mathsf{M}^{(\nu),N+1}=(\mathsf{eval}_{N+1})_{*}\mathsf{M}^{(\nu),N+1}\circ\Lambda_{N}^{N+1}.

Now, let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{N+1})
be fixed and consider the orbital measure on
H(N+1)

\displaystyle\mathsf{n}_{\lambda}=\mathsf{Law}[U^{*}\mathsf{diag}(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{N+1})U],

where
U
is Haar distributed in
\mathbb{U}(N+1)
.

Then, since
(\mathsf{eval}_{N+1})_{*}\mathsf{n}_{\lambda}=\delta_{\lambda}
, Baryshnikov’s result
[
1
]
can be written as

\displaystyle\big(\mathsf{eval}_{N}\circ\pi_{N}^{N+1}\big)_{*}\mathsf{n}_{\lambda}=(\mathsf{eval}_{N+1})_{*}\mathsf{n}_{\lambda}\circ\Lambda_{N}^{N+1}.

By Weyl’s integration formula we have

\displaystyle\mathsf{M}^{(\nu),N+1}({\rm d}X)=\int\mu_{N+1}^{\nu}({\rm d}\lambda)\mathsf{n}_{\lambda}({\rm d}X).

So we obtain

\displaystyle\int\big(\mathsf{eval}_{N}\circ\pi_{N}^{N+1}\big)_{*}\mathsf{n}_{\lambda}(\cdot)\mu_{N+1}^{\nu}({\rm d}\lambda)=\int(\mathsf{eval}_{N+1})_{*}\mathsf{n}_{\lambda}\circ\Lambda_{N}^{N+1}(\cdot)\mu_{N+1}^{\nu}({\rm d}\lambda).

By linearity of the operations involved

\displaystyle\big(\mathsf{eval}_{N}\circ\pi_{N}^{N+1}\big)_{*}\int\mathsf{n}_{\lambda}(\cdot)\mu_{N+1}^{\nu}({\rm d}\lambda)=(\mathsf{eval}_{N+1})_{*}\int\mu_{N+1}^{\nu}({\rm d}\lambda)\mathsf{n}_{\lambda}\circ\Lambda_{N}^{N+1}(\cdot).

and so we finally arrive at

\displaystyle\big(\mathsf{eval}\circ\pi_{N}^{N+1}\big)_{*}\mathsf{M}^{(\nu),N+1}=(\mathsf{eval}_{N+1})_{*}\mathsf{M}^{(\nu),N+1}\circ\Lambda_{N}^{N+1}.

∎

3
Approximation of spectral measures

In order to proceed we will need to get a handle on the abstract spectral measure
\mathfrak{m}^{(\nu)}
. The following approximation procedure of Olshanski–Vershik
[
24
]
and Borodin–Olshanski
[
2
]
, based on the ergodic method of Vershik
[
33
]
allows us to do so.

For
\lambda^{(N)}\in W^{N}
we define the numbers

\displaystyle\alpha_{i,N}^{+}\big(\lambda^{(N)}\big)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{\max\big\{\lambda^{(N)}_{i},0\big\}}{N},&i=1,\dots,N,\\
0&i=N+1,N+2,\dots,\end{cases}

\displaystyle\alpha_{i,N}^{-}\big(\lambda^{(N)}\big)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{\max\big\{{-}\lambda^{(N)}_{N+1-i},0\big\}}{N},&i=1,\dots,N,\\
0&i=N+1,N+2,\dots.\end{cases}

Equivalently, if
k
and
l
denote the number of strictly positive terms in
\big\{\alpha_{i,N}^{+}\big(\lambda^{(N)}\big)\big\}
and
\big\{\alpha_{i,N}^{-}\big(\lambda^{(N)}\big)\big\}
respectively, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda^{(N)}}{N}=\big(\alpha_{1,N}^{+}\big(\lambda^{(N)}\big),\dots,\alpha_{k,N}^{+}\big(\lambda^{(N)}\big),0,\dots,0,-\alpha_{l,N}^{-}\big(\lambda^{(N)}\big),\dots,-\alpha_{1,N}^{-}\big(\lambda^{(N)}\big)\big).

Define the corner map
\pi_{N}^{\infty}\colon H\to H(N)
by
\pi_{N}^{\infty}(X)=(X_{ij})^{N}_{j,j=1}
. Let
X\in H
be given. Then, we define the numbers
\big\{\alpha_{i,N}^{+}(X)\big\}
,
\big\{\alpha_{i,N}^{-}(X)\big\}
by

\displaystyle\alpha_{i,N}^{+}(X)=\alpha_{i,N}^{+}\big(\mathsf{eval}_{N}\big(\pi_{N}^{\infty}(X)\big)\big),\qquad\forall\,i\geq 1,

\displaystyle\alpha_{i,N}^{-}(X)=\alpha_{i,N}^{-}\big(\mathsf{eval}_{N}\big(\pi_{N}^{\infty}(X)\big)\big),\qquad\forall\,i\geq 1.

We also set,

\displaystyle c^{(N)}(X)=\frac{\operatorname{Tr}[\pi_{N}^{\infty}(X)]}{N}=\sum_{i}\alpha_{i,N}^{+}(X)-\sum_{i}\alpha_{i,N}^{-}(X),

\displaystyle d^{(N)}(X)=\frac{\operatorname{Tr}[\pi_{N}^{\infty}(X)^{2}]}{N^{2}}=\sum_{i}\big(\alpha_{i,N}^{+}(X)\big)^{2}+\sum_{i}\big(\alpha_{i,N}^{-}(X)\big)^{2}.

Having all these notations in place we now arrive at the following important definition (see Theorem
3.2
below for the motivation behind it):

Definition 3.1
.

A matrix
X\in H
is called regular and we will write
X\in H_{{\rm reg}}
if the following limits exist

\displaystyle\alpha_{i}^{\pm}(X)=\lim_{N\to\infty}\alpha_{i,N}^{\pm}(X),\qquad\forall\,i\geq 1,

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}(X)=\lim_{N\to\infty}c^{(N)}(X),

\displaystyle\delta(X)=\lim_{N\to\infty}d^{(N)}(X).

We can easily see that
\sum_{i}(\alpha_{i}^{+}(X))^{2}+(\alpha_{i}^{-}(X))^{2}\leq\delta(X)
and we define

\displaystyle\gamma_{2}(X)=\delta(X)-\sum_{i}\big(\alpha_{i}^{+}(X)\big)^{2}-\sum_{i}\big(\alpha_{i}^{-}(X)\big)^{2}\geq 0.

We also define
\mathfrak{r}_{N}\colon H\to\Omega
by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{r}_{N}(X)=\big(\big\{\alpha_{i,N}^{+}(X)\big\},\big\{\alpha_{i,N}^{-}(X)\big\},c^{(N)}(X),d^{(N)}(X)\big)

and similarly
\mathfrak{r}_{\infty}\colon H\to\Omega
, that is defined correctly on
H_{{\rm reg}}
and thus almost everywhere on
H
as we shall see in Theorem
3.2
below

\displaystyle\mathfrak{r}_{\infty}(X)=\big(\big\{\alpha_{i}^{+}(X)\big\},\big\{\alpha_{i}^{-}(X)\big\},\gamma_{1}(X),\delta(X)\big).

With all these definitions in place we can now state the following result from
[
2
, Section 5]
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{M}
be any
\mathbb{U}(\infty)
-invariant probability measure on
H
. Then,
\mathfrak{M}
is supported on
H_{{\rm reg}}
. Moreover, there exists a unique spectral measure
\mathbb{P}^{\mathfrak{M}}
associated to
\mathfrak{M}

(
in the particular case
\mathfrak{M}=\mathsf{M}^{(\nu)}
we write as we did in the introduction
\mathfrak{m}^{(\nu)}=\mathbb{P}^{\mathsf{M}^{(\nu)}})
defined by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}=\int_{\Omega}M_{\omega}\mathbb{P}^{\mathfrak{M}}({\rm d}\omega),

where the equality is understood as follows: for all Borel functions
\mathfrak{F}
on
H

\displaystyle\int_{H}\mathfrak{F}(X)\mathfrak{M}({\rm d}X)=\int_{\Omega}\int_{H}\mathfrak{F}(X)M_{\omega}({\rm d}X)\mathbb{P}^{\mathfrak{M}}({\rm d}\omega).

Furthermore, the spectral measure
\mathbb{P}^{\mathfrak{M}}
is given explicitly by

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{\mathfrak{M}}=(\mathfrak{r}_{\infty})_{*}(\mathfrak{M}|_{H_{{\rm reg}}}).

Finally, we have the following weak convergence of probability measures

\displaystyle(\mathfrak{r}_{N})_{*}\mathfrak{M}\implies(\mathfrak{r}_{\infty})_{*}(\mathfrak{M}|_{H_{{\rm reg}}})=\mathbb{P}^{\mathfrak{M}}.

We will need some more definitions and notations used in later sections. For
X
(deterministic or random) in
H
and
H_{{\rm reg}}
respectively define the point configurations

\displaystyle\mathsf{C}_{N}(X)=\big\{\alpha_{i,N}^{+}(X)\big\}\sqcup\big\{{-}\alpha_{i,N}^{-}(X)\big\},

\displaystyle\mathsf{C}(X)=\big\{\alpha_{i}^{+}(X)\big\}\sqcup\big\{{-}\alpha_{i}^{-}(X)\big\},

omitting possible zeroes. We will write
\mathsf{C}^{(\nu)}_{N}(X),\mathsf{C}^{(\nu)}(X)
if
\mathsf{Law}(X)=\mathsf{M}^{(\nu)}
.

For
\mathfrak{M}
on

\mathbb{U}(\infty)

\mathcal{P}_{N}
and

and

if

.

We need a final definition for a general measure
\mathbb{P}
on
\mathsf{Conf}(\mathcal{X})
. We define its correlation functions

for each

In particular, for a determinantal measure

4
Limit of the correlation kernel and the
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
parameters

4.1
The
\boldsymbol{\alpha^{-}}
parameters

The following proposition is obvious from the definitions in Section
3
and Theorem
3.2
, since the measure
\mathsf{M}^{(\nu)}
is supported on
H_{+}
:

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\nu>-1
. Then,

\displaystyle\alpha_{i}^{-}(X)=0,\qquad\forall\,i\geq 1\qquad\text{for}\quad\mathsf{M}^{(\nu)}-\operatorname{a.e.}X\in H_{{\rm reg}}.

Thus, we can restrict our attention on the parameters
\alpha^{+}
which form a random point configuration on
\mathcal{X}=(0,\infty)
(throughout, the reference measure
\mu
on
(0,\infty)
will be Lebesgue measure), which we go on to study next.

4.2
Explicit expression for the correlation kernel and the
\boldsymbol{\alpha^{+}}
parameters

It is a standard result from random matrix theory that the measure
\mu^{\nu}_{N}
gives rise to a determinantal point process on
(0,\infty)
with
N
particles. It is the orthogonal polynomial ensemble associated to the Bessel weight
w_{N}^{\nu}
. We will denote its correlation kernel by
\mathsf{K}_{N}^{\nu}
. This is given explicitly in terms of Bessel polynomials but for the purposes of the asymptotic analysis we will instead exploit the connection to the Laguerre ensemble.

First, we need to recall a simple fact about transformations of determinantal measures on
\mathcal{X}=(I_{1},I_{2})\subset\mathbb{R}
. Let
f\colon\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}
be a
C^{1}
bijection and let
g=f^{-1}
be its inverse. Then,
f
induces a homeomorphism of
\mathsf{Conf}(\mathcal{X})
: for a configuration
\mathfrak{X}
the particles of
f(\mathfrak{X})
are of the form
f(x)
,
x\in\mathfrak{X}
. Let
\mathbb{P}_{\mathsf{K}}
be a determinantal measure on
\mathcal{X}
. Then, its pushforward under
f
is also a determinantal measure

\displaystyle f_{*}\mathbb{P}_{\mathsf{K}}=\mathbb{P}_{\hat{\mathsf{K}}^{f}}

with correlation kernel

\displaystyle\hat{\mathsf{K}}^{f}(x,y)=\sqrt{g^{\prime}(x)g^{\prime}(y)}\mathsf{K}(g(x),g(y)).

The Laguerre ensemble
\mathfrak{L}_{N}^{\nu}
, defined for
\nu>-1
, is the probability measure on
W^{N}_{+}

\displaystyle\mathfrak{L}_{N}^{\nu}({\rm d}x)=\tilde{c}_{\nu,N}\prod_{i=1}^{N}x_{i}^{\nu}{\rm e}^{-x_{i}}\Delta_{N}(x)^{2}\textbf{1}_{\{x\in W^{N}_{+}\}}{\rm d}x_{1}\cdots{\rm d}x_{N}.

We will denote for
n\geq 1
, by
L_{n}^{\nu}
the monic Laguerre polynomials, orthogonal with respect to the measure (note that this, unlike
w^{\nu}_{N}
, does not depend on
N
)

\displaystyle\lambda_{\nu}(x){\rm d}x=x^{\nu}{\rm e}^{-x}\textbf{1}_{\{x>0\}}{\rm d}x.

Their squared norm is given by:

\displaystyle\|L_{n}^{\nu}\|_{2}^{2}=n!\Gamma(n+\nu+1).

It is a standard result that the Laguerre ensemble
\mathfrak{L}_{N}^{\nu}({\rm d}x)
gives rise to a determinantal point process on
(0,\infty)
with
N
particles and its correlation kernel
\mathsf{L}_{N}^{\nu}
, with respect to Lebesgue measure, is given by

\displaystyle\mathsf{L}_{N}^{\nu}(x,y)=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\frac{L_{i}^{\nu}(x)L_{i}^{\nu}(y)}{\|L_{n}^{\nu}\|_{2}^{2}}\sqrt{\lambda_{\nu}(x)\lambda_{\nu}(y)}.

Using the Christoffel–Darboux formula we can further write

\displaystyle\mathsf{L}_{N}^{\nu}(x,y)=\frac{1}{\|L_{N-1}^{\nu}\|_{2}^{2}}\frac{L_{N}^{\nu}(x)L_{N-1}^{\nu}(y)-L_{N-1}^{\nu}(x)L_{N}^{\nu}(y)}{x-y}\sqrt{\lambda_{\nu}(x)\lambda_{\nu}(y)}.

Now, note that under the transformation
f(x)=\frac{2}{x}
we have

\displaystyle(f_{*}\mathfrak{L}_{N}^{\nu})({\rm d}x)=\mu_{N}^{\nu}({\rm d}x),\qquad\forall\,N\geq 1.

Thus, the correlation kernel
\mathsf{K}_{N}^{\nu}
of the determinantal point process associated to
\mu_{N}^{\nu}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathsf{K}_{N}^{\nu}(x,y)=\frac{2}{xy}\mathsf{L}_{N}^{\nu}\left(\frac{2}{x},\frac{2}{y}\right).

Furthermore, the scaled point process
\mathsf{C}_{N}^{(\nu)}(X)=\{\alpha_{i,N}^{+}(X)\}_{i=1}^{N}
where
\mathsf{Law}(X)=\mathsf{M}^{(\nu)}
is determinantal on
(0,\infty)
with correlation kernel
K_{N}^{\nu}
, with respect to Lebesgue measure, given by

\displaystyle K_{N}^{\nu}(x,y)=N\mathsf{K}_{N}^{\nu}(Nx,Ny).

And also in terms of the Laguerre kernel

\displaystyle K_{N}^{\nu}(x,y)=\frac{2}{Nxy}\mathsf{L}_{N}^{\nu}\left(\frac{2}{Nx},\frac{2}{Ny}\right).

(4.1)

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\nu>-1
. Then, we have uniformly on compacts in
(0,\infty)

\displaystyle K_{N}^{\nu}(x,y)\underset{N\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}K_{\infty}^{\nu}(x,y)=\frac{8}{xy}\mathbb{J}_{\nu}\left(\frac{8}{x},\frac{8}{y}\right).

Furthermore, the determinantal point process
\mathsf{C}^{(\nu)}_{N}(X)=\{\alpha_{i,N}^{+}(X)\}
with
\mathsf{Law}(X)=\mathsf{M}^{(\nu)}
on
(0,\infty)
convergences weakly as
N\to\infty
to the determinantal point process
\mathsf{C}^{(\nu)}(X)=\{\alpha_{i}^{+}(X)\}
such that

\displaystyle\mathsf{Law}\big(\mathsf{C}^{(\nu)}(X)\big)=\mathbb{P}_{K_{\infty}^{\nu}}

and so do all the correlation functions.
Finally, under the transformation
x\mapsto\frac{8}{x}
we get the Bessel point process

\displaystyle\mathsf{Law}\left(\frac{8}{\mathsf{C}^{(\nu)}(X)}\right)=\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{J}_{\nu}}.

Proof.

We first write using relation (
4.1
)

\displaystyle K_{N}^{\nu}(x,y)=\frac{8}{xy}\frac{1}{4N}\mathsf{L}_{N}^{\nu}\left(\frac{1}{4N}\frac{8}{x},\frac{1}{4N}\frac{8}{y}\right).

Then, the first statements of the proposition are immediate consequences of the following well-known facts: the uniform convergence on compacts in
(0,\infty)
, with
z_{1}=\frac{8}{x},z_{2}=\frac{8}{y}

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{4N}\mathsf{L}_{N}^{\nu}\left(\frac{1}{4N}z_{1},\frac{1}{4N}z_{2}\right)=\mathbb{J}_{\nu}(z_{1},z_{2})

and convergence of the Laguerre ensemble determinantal point process at the hard edge scaling limit to the Bessel process, see
[
13
,
14
,
32
]
. These results follow from uniform on compacts asymptotics for Laguerre polynomials, see
[
31
]
. The final statement is immediate from the transformation rule for determinantal point processes.
∎

Bounds for Laguerre polynomials.
In order to prove Proposition

[
31
]
.

Lemma 5.3
.

We have the following, uniform in
n
, estimate for the Laguerre wavefunction
\mathcal{W}_{n}^{\nu}

(
defined by the first equality below
)
, with
\mathsf{w}\geq 1
being an arbitrary fixed constant

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{n}^{\nu}(x)=\frac{L_{n}^{\nu}(x)^{2}x^{\nu}{\rm e}^{-x}}{\|L_{n}^{\nu}\|_{2}^{2}}\leq\begin{cases}\mathsf{c}(\mathsf{w})\times x^{-\frac{1}{2}}n^{-\frac{1}{2}},&n^{-1}\leq x\leq\mathsf{w},\\
C\times x^{\nu}n^{\nu},&0\leq x\leq n^{-1}.\end{cases}

Here,
\mathsf{c}(\mathsf{w})
only depends on
\mathsf{w}
and in particular is independent of
n
, while
C
is a generic constant independent of all quantities involved in the statement above.

Proof.

We make use of results from Szegö
[
31
]
. We note that in
[
31
]
statements involving Laguerre polynomials are for the ones normalized to have leading coefficient
\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n!}
. Since we are interested in the
monic
Laguerre polynomials
L_{n}^{\nu}
we simply need to multiply the formulae therein by the inverse of this coefficient. Then, Theorem 7.6.4 in
[
31
]
reads as follows in our setting, for some fixed constant
\mathsf{w}\geq 1
and uniformly in
n

\displaystyle L_{n}^{\nu}(x)\leq\begin{cases}\tilde{\mathsf{c}}(\mathsf{w})\times n!x^{-\frac{\nu}{2}-\frac{1}{4}}n^{\frac{\nu}{2}-\frac{1}{4}},&n^{-1}\leq x\leq\mathsf{w},\\
\tilde{C}\times n!n^{\nu},&0\leq x\leq n^{-1}.\end{cases}

Here,
\tilde{\mathsf{c}}(\mathsf{w})
only depends on
\mathsf{w}
and is independent of
n
, while
\tilde{C}
is a generic constant independent of all quantities involved in the statement above.

Now, recall that
\|L_{n}^{\nu}\|_{2}^{2}=n!\Gamma(n+\nu+1)
. Then, using the classical fact for ratios of Gamma functions

\displaystyle\frac{n!}{\Gamma(n+\nu+1)}=\frac{\Gamma(n+1)}{\Gamma(n+1+\nu)}=n^{-\nu}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{n^{\nu+1}}\right)

and the trivial bound
{\rm e}^{-x}\leq 1
we obtain the statement of the lemma.
∎

With these preliminaries in place we are ready to prove Proposition
5.2
.

Proof of Proposition
5.2
.

Let
\epsilon>0
be arbitrary (and for convenience assume
\epsilon<1
). Below, we will pick (in this order) constants
\mathsf{w},l,R
depending on
\epsilon
. This choice will be made in display (
5.2
) based on the bound (
5.1
).

We will use the notation
\lesssim
to mean
\leq
up to a constant; with the implicit constant being independent of
\epsilon
(and obviously of the quantities
\mathsf{w}
,
l
,
R
depending on it) and uniform in
N
(in particular we keep track of the constant
\mathsf{c}(\mathsf{w})
from Lemma
5.3
but not of the generic constant
C
). It is clear that it suffices to prove that we can find
R
large enough such that uniformly in
N\in\mathbb{N}
:

\displaystyle\int_{\frac{R}{N}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{N}\frac{\mathsf{L}_{N}^{\nu}(y,y)}{y}{\rm d}y\lesssim\epsilon.

First, recall that

\displaystyle\mathsf{L}_{N}^{\nu}(y,y)=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\mathcal{W}_{k}^{\nu}(y).

We split the integral, with
\mathsf{w}
to be picked according to
\epsilon
(large)

\displaystyle\int_{\frac{R}{N}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{N}\frac{\mathsf{L}_{N}^{\nu}(y,y)}{y}{\rm d}y=\int_{\frac{R}{N}}^{\mathsf{w}}\frac{1}{N}\frac{\mathsf{L}_{N}^{\nu}(y,y)}{y}{\rm d}y+\int_{\mathsf{w}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{N}\frac{\mathsf{L}_{N}^{\nu}(y,y)}{y}{\rm d}y.

We then, using the fact that
\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{W}_{n}^{\nu}(y){\rm d}y=1
for all
n
, easily estimate

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\int_{\mathsf{w}}^{\infty}\frac{\mathsf{L}_{N}^{\nu}(y,y)}{y}{\rm d}y\leq\frac{1}{\mathsf{w}N}\int_{\mathsf{w}}^{\infty}\mathsf{L}_{N}^{\nu}(y,y){\rm d}y\leq\frac{1}{\mathsf{w}N}N=\frac{1}{\mathsf{w}}.

We now focus on the integral

\displaystyle\int_{\frac{R}{N}}^{\mathsf{w}}\frac{1}{N}\frac{\mathsf{L}_{N}^{\nu}(y,y)}{y}{\rm d}y=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{1\leq n<N}\int_{\frac{R}{N}}^{\mathsf{w}}\frac{\mathcal{W}_{n}^{\nu}(y)}{y}{\rm d}y

and split the range of summation, for
l
to be picked depending on
\epsilon
(small), as follows

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{1\leq n<lN}\int_{\frac{R}{N}}^{\mathsf{w}}\frac{\mathcal{W}_{n}^{\nu}(y)}{y}{\rm d}y+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{lN\leq n<N}\int_{\frac{R}{N}}^{\mathsf{w}}\frac{\mathcal{W}_{n}^{\nu}(y)}{y}{\rm d}y.

We will moreover, in the range
1\leq n<lN
, split the integral further as

\displaystyle\int_{\frac{R}{N}}^{\mathsf{w}}\frac{\mathcal{W}_{n}^{\nu}(y)}{y}{\rm d}y=\int_{\frac{R}{N}}^{\frac{1}{n}}\frac{\mathcal{W}_{n}^{\nu}(y)}{y}{\rm d}y+\int_{\frac{1}{n}}^{\mathsf{w}}\frac{\mathcal{W}_{n}^{\nu}(y)}{y}{\rm d}y.

Note that, since the integrand is positive, in case
\frac{R}{N}>\frac{1}{n}
, we simply estimate

\displaystyle\int_{\frac{R}{N}}^{\mathsf{w}}\frac{\mathcal{W}_{n}^{\nu}(y)}{y}{\rm d}y\leq\int_{\frac{1}{n}}^{\mathsf{w}}\frac{\mathcal{W}_{n}^{\nu}(y)}{y}{\rm d}y.

Thus, we have the bound

\displaystyle\int_{\frac{R}{N}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{N}\frac{\mathsf{L}_{N}^{\nu}(y,y)}{y}{\rm d}y\leq\frac{1}{\mathsf{w}}+J_{1}+J_{2}+J_{3},

where we define

\displaystyle J_{1}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{1\leq n<lN}\textbf{1}_{\left(\frac{1}{n}\geq\frac{R}{N}\right)}\int_{\frac{R}{N}}^{\frac{1}{n}}\frac{\mathcal{W}_{n}^{\nu}(y)}{y}{\rm d}y,

\displaystyle J_{2}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{1\leq n<lN}\int_{\frac{1}{n}}^{\mathsf{w}}\frac{\mathcal{W}_{n}^{\nu}(y)}{y}{\rm d}y,\qquad J_{3}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{lN\leq n<N}\int_{\frac{R}{N}}^{\mathsf{w}}\frac{\mathcal{W}_{n}^{\nu}(y)}{y}{\rm d}y.

We now go on to estimate each of these terms individually. Using the bound for
\mathcal{W}_{n}^{\nu}
from Lemma
5.3
in the range
0\leq x\leq n^{-1}
, we estimate
J_{1}
:

\displaystyle J_{1}\lesssim\frac{1}{N}\sum_{1\leq n<lN}\textbf{1}_{\left(\frac{1}{n}\geq\frac{R}{N}\right)}n^{\nu}\int_{\frac{R}{N}}^{\frac{1}{n}}y^{\nu-1}{\rm d}y.

We split into three cases. For
\nu>0
we have

\displaystyle J_{1}\lesssim\frac{1}{N}\sum_{1\leq n<lN}\textbf{1}_{\left(\frac{1}{n}\geq\frac{R}{N}\right)}n^{\nu}\left[\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)^{\nu}-\left(\frac{R}{N}\right)^{\nu}\right]\lesssim l.

While, for
\nu=0

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{N}\sum_{1\leq n<lN}\textbf{1}_{\left(\frac{1}{n}\geq\frac{R}{N}\right)}\log\left(\frac{N}{nR}\right)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{1\leq n<lN}\left[-\textbf{1}_{\left(\frac{1}{n}\geq\frac{R}{N}\right)}\log\left(\frac{n}{N}\right)-\textbf{1}_{\left(\frac{1}{n}\geq\frac{R}{N}\right)}\log\left(R\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{N}\sum_{1\leq n<lN}-\log\left(\frac{n}{N}\right)\lesssim\int_{0}^{l}-\log(x){\rm d}x=-l\log(l)-l.

Finally, for
-1<\nu<0

\displaystyle J_{1}\lesssim\frac{1}{N}\sum_{1\leq n<lN}\textbf{1}_{\left(\frac{1}{n}\geq\frac{R}{N}\right)}\left(\frac{Rn}{N}\right)^{\nu}\lesssim\frac{1}{N^{\nu+1}}\left(lN\right)^{\nu+1}R^{\nu}=l^{\nu+1}R^{\nu}.

Observe that, the last bound decreases as
R
increases since
\nu
is negative.

Now, using the bound for
\mathcal{W}_{n}^{\nu}
from Lemma
5.3
in the range
n^{-1}\leq x\leq\mathsf{w}
, we estimate
J_{2}
:

\displaystyle J_{2}\lesssim\frac{\mathsf{c}(\mathsf{w})}{N}\sum_{1\leq n<lN}\int_{\frac{1}{n}}^{\mathsf{w}}\frac{1}{y^{\frac{3}{2}}}n^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\rm d}y\lesssim\frac{\mathsf{c}(\mathsf{w})}{N}\sum_{1\leq n<lN}n^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left[n^{\frac{1}{2}}-\frac{1}{\mathsf{w}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right]\lesssim\mathsf{c}(\mathsf{w})l.

Finally, we turn to
J_{3}
. We assume that
R
is large enough so that
R\geq\frac{1}{l}
. The fact that this is possible (not trivial apriori since both quantities depend on
\epsilon
) will be clear by the choices made in (
5.2
) below. Thus, the bound for
\mathcal{W}_{n}^{\nu}
from Lemma
5.3
valid in the range
n^{-1}\leq x\leq\mathsf{w}
, is valid throughout the range of integration
\frac{R}{N}\leq x\leq\mathsf{w}
. Hence, we estimate

\displaystyle J_{3}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\mathsf{c}(\mathsf{w})}{N}\sum_{lN\leq n<N}\int_{\frac{R}{N}}^{\mathsf{w}}n^{-\frac{1}{2}}\frac{1}{y^{\frac{3}{2}}}{\rm d}y\lesssim\frac{\mathsf{c}(\mathsf{w})}{N^{\frac{3}{2}}l^{\frac{1}{2}}}\sum_{lN\leq n<N}\int_{\frac{R}{N}}^{\mathsf{w}}\frac{1}{y^{\frac{3}{2}}}{\rm d}y\leq\frac{\mathsf{c}(\mathsf{w})}{N^{\frac{3}{2}}l^{\frac{1}{2}}}\sum_{lN\leq n<N}\int_{\frac{R}{N}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{y^{\frac{3}{2}}}{\rm d}y

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\mathsf{c}(\mathsf{w})}{N^{\frac{3}{2}}l^{\frac{1}{2}}}N\times\frac{N^{\frac{1}{2}}}{R^{\frac{1}{2}}}=\frac{\mathsf{c}(\mathsf{w})}{R^{\frac{1}{2}}l^{\frac{1}{2}}}.

Putting everything together we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\frac{R}{N}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{N}\frac{\mathsf{L}_{N}^{\nu}(y,y)}{y}{\rm d}y\lesssim\begin{cases}\dfrac{1}{\mathsf{w}}+l+\mathsf{c}(\mathsf{w})l+\dfrac{\mathsf{c}(\mathsf{w})}{R^{\frac{1}{2}}l^{\frac{1}{2}}},&\text{for }\nu>0,\\
\dfrac{1}{\mathsf{w}}-l\log(l)-l+\mathsf{c}(\mathsf{w})l+\dfrac{\mathsf{c}(\mathsf{w})}{R^{\frac{1}{2}}l^{\frac{1}{2}}},&\text{for }\nu=0,\\
\dfrac{1}{\mathsf{w}}+l^{\nu+1}R^{\nu}+\mathsf{c}(\mathsf{w})l+\dfrac{\mathsf{c}(\mathsf{w})}{R^{\frac{1}{2}}l^{\frac{1}{2}}},&\text{for }-1<\nu<0.\end{cases}

(5.1)

We now pick the quantities
(\mathsf{w},l,R)
depending on
\epsilon
so that each summand in bound (
5.1
) is of order at most
\epsilon
(for
\epsilon
small). We first choose
\mathsf{w}=\mathsf{w}(\epsilon)=\frac{1}{\epsilon}
. It is also convenient to define the following constant

\displaystyle\mathsf{c}_{\epsilon}=\max\left\{\mathsf{c}\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right),1\right\}.

Observe that, with this choice of
\mathsf{w}(\epsilon)
the bound in (
5.1
) is clearly still valid if we replace
\mathsf{c}(\mathsf{w}(\epsilon))
by
\mathsf{c}_{\epsilon}
.

Hence, it is not hard to see that we can choose
(\mathsf{w},l,R)
as follows (of course other choices are possible)

\displaystyle(\mathsf{w}(\epsilon),l(\epsilon),R(\epsilon))=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon},\frac{\epsilon}{\mathsf{c}_{\epsilon}},\frac{\mathsf{c}_{\epsilon}^{3}}{\epsilon^{3}}\right),&\text{for }\nu>0,\\
\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon},\frac{\epsilon^{\kappa}}{\mathsf{c}_{\epsilon}},\frac{\mathsf{c}_{\epsilon}^{3}}{\epsilon^{2+\kappa}}\right),\ \kappa>1,&\text{for }\nu=0,\\
\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon},\frac{\epsilon}{\mathsf{c}_{\epsilon}},\frac{\mathsf{c}_{\epsilon}^{3}}{\epsilon^{3}}\right),&\text{for }-1<\nu<0.\end{cases}

(5.2)

Note that, in all cases above the requirement
R\geq\frac{1}{l}
, that was used to bound
J_{3}
, is satisfied. Thus, if we take
R=R(\epsilon)
as above, we get uniformly in
N\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle\int_{\frac{R(\epsilon)}{N}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{N}\frac{\mathsf{L}_{N}^{\nu}(y,y)}{y}{\rm d}y\lesssim\epsilon.

The proof is complete.
∎

Proposition 7.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{M}
be a
\mathbb{U}(\infty)
-invariant measure on
H

\displaystyle\big(\pi_{N}^{\infty}\big)_{*}\mathfrak{M}\quad\text{is supported on}\quad H_{+}(N),\quad\forall\,N\geq 1.

In particular,

\displaystyle\alpha_{i,N}^{-}(X)\equiv 0,\quad\forall\,i\geq 1,\quad N\geq 1,\qquad\alpha_{i}^{-}(X)\equiv 0,\quad\forall\,i\geq 1\quad\text{for}\quad\mathfrak{M}-\operatorname{a.e.}X\in H_{{\rm reg}}.

Moreover, assume that

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}(X)<\infty,\qquad\text{for}\quad\mathfrak{M}-\operatorname{a.e.}X\in H_{{\rm reg}}.

Let
\mathcal{P}_{N},\mathcal{P}
be the corresponding point processes
(
of
\alpha^{+}
’s
)
on
(0,\infty)
and let
\rho_{1}^{(N)}
and
\rho_{1}
be their first correlation functions with respect to Lebesgue measure
(
assuming they exist
)
. Assume that for any
\Phi\in C_{c}((0,\infty))

\displaystyle\int\Phi(x)\rho_{1}^{(N)}(x){\rm d}x\to\int\Phi(x)\rho_{1}(x){\rm d}x.

Finally, assume that

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\int_{0}^{\epsilon}x\rho_{1}^{(N)}(x){\rm d}x=0,\qquad\text{uniformly in $N$}.

Then, we have

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}(X)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{i}^{+}(X),\qquad\text{for}\quad\mathfrak{M}-\operatorname{a.e.}X\in H_{{\rm reg}}.

We first need an elementary lemma.

Lemma 7.3
.

Assume we are given numbers
\forall\,N\geq 1

\displaystyle\alpha_{1,N}^{+}\geq\alpha_{2,N}^{+}\geq\cdots\geq 0

such that

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\alpha_{i,N}^{+}=\alpha_{i}^{+},\qquad\forall\,i\geq 1.

Moreover, assume the following limit exists and is finite

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{i,N}^{+}=\gamma_{1}<\infty.

Note that by Fatou’s lemma
(
and positivity
)

\displaystyle\Delta=\gamma_{1}-\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{i}^{+}\geq 0.

Let
\Phi
be a continuous function on
(0,\infty)
such that

\displaystyle\Phi(x)=x,\qquad x<\epsilon

for a certain
\epsilon>0
. Then,

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\Phi\big(\alpha_{i,N}^{+}\big)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\Phi\big(\alpha_{i}^{+}\big)+\Delta.

Proof.

Observe that, there exists
k
such that
\alpha_{k+1}^{+}<\epsilon
. Then
\alpha_{k+1,N}^{+}<\epsilon
for
N
sufficiently large and
\alpha_{i,N}^{+}<\epsilon
for
i\geq k+1
by monotonicity. Also,
\alpha_{i}^{+}<\epsilon
for
i\geq k+1
. Therefore

\displaystyle\Phi(\alpha_{i,N}^{+})=\alpha_{i,N}^{+},\quad N\ \text{large},\qquad\Phi(\alpha_{i}^{+})=\alpha_{i}^{+},\quad\forall\,i\geq k+1.

Thus,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\Phi\big(\alpha_{i,N}^{+}\big)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Phi\big(\alpha_{i,N}^{+}\big)+\sum_{i=k+1}^{\infty}\alpha_{i,N}^{+}

and

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\Phi\big(\alpha_{i}^{+}\big)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Phi\big(\alpha_{i}^{+}\big)+\sum_{i=k+1}^{\infty}\alpha_{i}^{+}.

As
N\to\infty
by continuity of
\Phi

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Phi\big(\alpha_{i,N}^{+}\big)\to\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Phi\big(\alpha_{i}^{+}\big)

and by the assumptions of the lemma

\displaystyle\sum_{i=k+1}^{\infty}\alpha_{i,N}^{+}\to\sum_{i=k+1}^{\infty}\alpha_{i}^{+}+\Delta.

The statement now follows.
∎

Proof of Proposition
7.2
.

First, observe that
\mathfrak{M}
is supported on the subset
H^{*}_{{\rm reg}}\subset H_{{\rm reg}}
that we now define. An element
X\in H^{*}_{{\rm reg}}
iff

\displaystyle\alpha_{i,N}^{-}(X)\equiv 0,\quad\forall\,i\geq 1,\quad N\geq 1,\qquad\alpha_{i}^{-}(X)\equiv 0,\quad\forall\,i\geq 1,\qquad\gamma_{1}(X)<\infty.

Fix a continuous function
\Phi(x)\geq 0
, vanishing for
x
large enough, such that
\Phi(x)=x
near 0. For any
X\in H^{*}_{{\rm reg}}
we set

\displaystyle\phi_{N}(X)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\Phi\big(\alpha_{i,N}^{+}(X)\big),\qquad\phi_{\infty}(X)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\Phi\big(\alpha_{i}^{+}(X)\big).

Apply the previous lemma to the sequences
\alpha_{i,N}^{+}=\alpha_{i,N}^{+}(X)
,
\alpha_{i}^{+}=\alpha_{i}^{+}(X)
for

(note that all conditions are satisfied) to get

\displaystyle\phi_{N}(X)\to\phi_{\infty}(X)+\Delta(X).

Observe that all three functions
\phi_{N}

\phi_{\infty}

are non-negative and thus Fatou’s lemma gives

\displaystyle\liminf_{N\to\infty}\int_{X\in H^{*}_{{\rm reg}}}\phi_{N}(X)\mathfrak{M}({\rm d}X)\geq\int_{X\in H^{*}_{{\rm reg}}}\phi_{\infty}(X)\mathfrak{M}({\rm d}X)+\int_{X\in H^{*}_{{\rm reg}}}\Delta(X)\mathfrak{M}({\rm d}X).

Associate the point configurations
\mathsf{C}_{N}(X)
,
\mathsf{C}(X)
to
X\in H^{*}_{{\rm reg}}
. Then

\displaystyle\phi_{N}(X)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\Phi\big(\alpha_{i,N}^{+}(X)\big)=\sum_{x\in\mathsf{C}_{N}(X)}\Phi(x)

so that by the definition of the correlation functions

\displaystyle\int_{X\in H^{*}_{{\rm reg}}}\phi_{N}(X)\mathfrak{M}({\rm d}X)=\int\Phi(x)\rho_{1}^{(N)}(x){\rm d}x

and similarly

\displaystyle\int_{X\in H^{*}_{{\rm reg}}}\phi_{\infty}(X)\mathfrak{M}({\rm d}X)=\int\Phi(x)\rho_{1}(x){\rm d}x.

Thus

\displaystyle\liminf_{N\to\infty}\int\Phi(x)\rho_{1}^{(N)}(x){\rm d}x\geq\int\Phi(x)\rho_{1}(x){\rm d}x+\int_{X\in H^{*}_{{\rm reg}}}\Delta(X)\mathfrak{M}({\rm d}X).

\displaystyle\limsup_{N\to\infty}\int\Phi(x)\rho_{1}^{(N)}(x){\rm d}x\leq\int\Phi(x)\rho_{1}(x){\rm d}x.

Since

on

we get that

for
\mathfrak{M}-\operatorname{a.e.}X\in H^{*}_{{\rm reg}}

\mathfrak{M}
is supported on

where

\Phi_{\epsilon}(x)=x

and

Thus by Fatou’s lemma for any

Taking the limit

we finally get, by convergence of the first correlation function

\displaystyle\limsup_{N\to\infty}\int\Phi(x)\rho_{1}^{(N)}(x){\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\int\Phi_{\epsilon}(x)\rho_{1}^{(N)}(x){\rm d}x+\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\int\Psi_{\epsilon}(x)\rho_{1}^{(N)}(x){\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\int\Psi_{\epsilon}(x)\rho_{1}(x){\rm d}x=\int\Phi(x)\rho_{1}(x){\rm d}x.

Proposition 7.4
.

Let
\nu>-1
. Then

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}(X)<\infty,\qquad\text{for}\quad\mathsf{M}^{(\nu)}-\operatorname{a.e.}X\in H_{{\rm reg}}.

Proof.

First of all, we note that
\mathsf{M}^{(\nu)}
is supported on the subset
H^{+}_{{\rm reg}}\subset H_{{\rm reg}}
that we now define. An element
X\in H^{+}_{{\rm reg}}
iff

\displaystyle\alpha_{i,N}^{-}(X)\equiv 0,\quad\forall\,i\geq 1,\quad N\geq 1,\qquad\alpha_{i}^{-}(X)\equiv 0,\quad\forall\,i\geq 1.

Moreover, if we define for
R>0
the subset
H^{+,R}_{{\rm reg}}\subset H^{+}_{{\rm reg}}
such that
X\in H^{+,R}_{{\rm reg}}
iff
\alpha_{1}^{+}(X)<R
we easily see that

\displaystyle H^{+}_{{\rm reg}}=\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}H^{+,k}_{{\rm reg}}.

Hence it will suffice to show that for any fixed
R>0

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}(X)<\infty,\qquad\text{for}\quad\mathsf{M}^{(\nu)}-\operatorname{a.e.}X\in H^{+,R}_{{\rm reg}}.

Furthermore, by positivity it actually suffices to show

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\gamma_{1}(X)\textbf{1}\big(X\in H_{{\rm reg}}^{+,R}\big)\big]<\infty,

where the expectation
\mathbb{E}
is with respect to
\mathsf{M}^{(\nu)}
. We calculate, using Fatou’s lemma and the underlying determinantal structure

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\gamma_{1}(X)\textbf{1}\big(X\in H_{{\rm reg}}^{+,R}\big)\big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\big[\gamma_{1}(X)\textbf{1}\big(\alpha_{1}^{+}(X)<R\big)\big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\lim_{N\to\infty}\left(\textbf{1}\big(\alpha_{1}^{+}(X)<R\big)\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{i,N}^{+}(X)\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\lim_{N\to\infty}\left(\textbf{1}\big(\alpha_{1,N}^{+}(X)<R\big)\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{i,N}^{+}(X)\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\textbf{1}\big(\alpha_{1,N}^{+}(X)<R\big)\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{i,N}^{+}(X)\right]

\displaystyle=\liminf_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{x\in\mathsf{C}_{N}^{(\nu)}(X)}x\textbf{1}(x<R)\right]

\displaystyle=\liminf_{N\to\infty}\int_{0}^{R}xK_{N}^{\nu}(x,x){\rm d}x<\infty.

The last claim is the statement of Lemma
5.4
.
∎

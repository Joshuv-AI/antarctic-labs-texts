Physical properties of the layered f f -electron van der Waals magnet Ce 2 Te 5
Yu Liu, M. M. Bordelon, A. Weiland, P. F. S. Rosa, S. M. Thomas, J. D. Thompson, F. Ronning, and E. D. Bauer

Abstract
We report a detailed study of the magnetic, transport, and thermodynamic properties of Ce 2 Te 5 single crystals, a layered f f -electron van der Waals magnet. Four consecutive transitions at ∼ \sim 5.2, 2.1, 0.9, and 0.4 K were observed in the a ​ c ac -plane electrical resistivity ρ \rho (T), which were further confirmed in specific heat C p C_{\textrm{p}} (T) measurements. Analysis of the magnetic susceptibility χ \chi (T), the magnetic-field variation of ρ \rho (T), and the increase of the first transition temperature ( T c ∼ T_{\textrm{c}}\sim 5.2 K) with applied magnetic field indicates ferromagnetic order, while the decrease of the other transitions with field suggests different states with dominant antiferromagnetic interactions below T 2 ∼ T_{2}\sim 2.1 K, T 3 ∼ T_{3}\sim 0.9 K, and T 4 T_{4} = 0.4 K. Critical behavior analysis around T c T_{\textrm{c}} that gives critical exponents β = 0.31 ​ ( 2 ) \beta=0.31(2) , γ = 0.99 ​ ( 2 ) \gamma=0.99(2) , δ = 4.46 ​ ( 1 ) \delta=4.46(1) , T c = 5.32 ​ ( 1 ) T_{\textrm{c}}=5.32(1) K indicates that Ce 2 Te 5 shows a three-dimensional magnetic critical behavior. Moreover, the Hall resistivity ρ xy \rho_{\textrm{xy}} indicates that Ce 2 Te 5 is a multi-band system with a relatively high electron mobility ∼ 2900 \sim 2900 cm 2 V -1 s -1 near T c T_{\textrm{c}} , providing further opportunities for future device applications.

Single crystals of Ce

The magnetization was measured in a Quantum Design Magnetic Property Measurement System (MPMS) from 2 to 350 K up to magnetic fields

II.2
Scaling analysis

A second-order phase transition around the Curie temperature
T_{\textrm{c}}
is characterized by a set of interrelated critical exponents
\beta
,
\gamma
,
\delta
and a magnetic equation of state
Stanley
. The critical exponents
\beta
and
\gamma
are associated with the spontaneous magnetization
M_{\textrm{s}}
and the inverse initial susceptibility
\chi_{\textrm{ini}}^{-1}
, below and above
T_{\textrm{c}}
, respectively, while
\delta
is the critical isotherm exponent. The definitions of
\beta
,
\gamma
,
\delta
from magnetization measurement are given below:

M_{\textrm{s}}(T)=M_{0}(-\varepsilon)^{\beta},\varepsilon<0,T<T_{\textrm{c}},

(1)

\chi_{\textrm{ini}}^{-1}(T)=(\mu_{0}h_{0}/m_{0})\varepsilon^{\gamma},\varepsilon>0,T>T_{\textrm{c}},

(2)

M=D(\mu_{0}H_{\textrm{int}})^{1/\delta},T=T_{\textrm{c}},

(3)

where
\varepsilon=(T-T_{\textrm{c}})/T_{\textrm{c}}
is the reduced temperature, and
M_{0}
,
\mu_{0}h_{0}/m_{0}
and
D
are critical amplitudes
Fisher
.

The magnetic equation of state in the critical region (
\varepsilon\leq 0.1
) can be expressed as

M(\mu_{0}H_{\textrm{int}},\varepsilon)=\varepsilon^{\beta}f_{\pm}(\mu_{0}H_{\textrm{int}}/\varepsilon^{\beta+\gamma}),

(4)

where
f_{-}
for
T<T_{\textrm{c}}
and
f_{+}
for
T>T_{\textrm{c}}
, respectively, are regular functions. Eq.(4) can be further written in terms of scaled magnetization
m\equiv\varepsilon^{-\beta}M(\mu_{0}H_{\textrm{int}},\varepsilon)
and scaled field
\mu_{0}h\equiv\varepsilon^{-(\beta+\gamma)}\mu_{0}H_{\textrm{int}}
as

m=f_{\pm}(\mu_{0}h).

(5)

This suggests that for true scaling relations and the right choice of
\beta
,
\gamma
,
\delta
values, the scaled
m
and
\mu_{0}h
will fall on universal curves above
T_{\textrm{c}}
and below
T_{\textrm{c}}
, respectively.

In summary, we studied the magnetic, transport, and thermodynamic properties of the

= 5.2,
T_{2}

Determining Riemannian Manifolds From Nonlinear Wave Observations at a Single Point
Leo Tzou

Abstract
. We show that on an a-priori unknown Riemannian manifold ( M , g ) (M,g) , measuring the source-to-solution map for the semilinear wave equation at a single point determines its topological, differential, and geometric structure.



2000 Mathematics Subject Classification
53C20, 35R30, 35L70

1.
Introduction

The work of
[
KLU14
,
KLU18
,
KLOU14
]
developed the new idea of exploiting nonlinearity to solve inverse problems involving nonlinear hyperbolic equations. Roughly speaking, these results reconstruct the topological, differential, and conformal structure of a Lorentzian manifold from the source-to-solution data of some nonlinear hyperbolic equation measured in an open space-time cylinder of a timelike path. Other works involving the use of this idea to solve inverse problems for nonlinear hyperbolic equations can be found in
[
UW20
,
UW18
,
UZ19
,
LUW18
,
LUW17
,
dHUW19
,
dHUW20
,
FO19
,
OSSU20
,
CLOP21
]

Formally, the source-to-solution data considered in all of the above works are over-determined so it is natural to ask whether we really need so much data. Furthermore, these works model tomography where data is collected by
infinitely
many receivers, when in reality many imaging methods in seismology and astronomy are best modeled by only
one
receiver. Another motivation for reducing the amount of data necessary is that it can be seen as a nonlinear hyperbolic analogue to the “partial data” elliptic Calderón problem
[
KSU07
]
.

This paper provides a proof-of-concept model for a
single observer
inverse problems for nonlinear hyperbolic equations.
We show that when the Lorentzian metric is the direct product of
\mathbb{R}
with a time-independent Riemannian manifold, the Riemannian manifold is determined by data collected at a
single spacial point
. Of course, the technique we develop here is far more robust than just the ultra-static Lorentzian case. In a forthcoming paper
[
MN21
]
we will adapt these methods to recover conformal structure of general hyperbolic Lorentzian metrics with just a single observer.

Consider two Riemannian manifolds
(M_{1},g_{1})
and
(M_{2},g_{2})
, and the corresponding Lorentzian manifold
({\mathcal{M}}_{j},-dt^{2}+g_{j})
where
{\mathcal{M}}_{j}=\mathbb{R}\times M_{j}
. For each
j=1,2
, fix
p_{j}\in M_{j}
and consider small open neighborhoods
{\mathcal{U}}_{j}\subset M_{j}
containing
p_{j}
, and a diffeomorphism identifying
{\mathcal{U}}_{1}\equiv{\mathcal{U}}_{2}
and
p_{1}\equiv p_{2}
. We will denote the common point to be
{\bf 0}
and the common neighborhood to be
{\mathcal{U}}
.

For a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
, denote by
\Box_{g}=-\partial_{t}^{2}+\Delta_{g}
the corresponding wave operator. The choice of nonlinearity we make here is cubic, and this is motivated by the cubic nonlinear interaction in Yang-Mills equations (see introduction of
[
CMOP19
]
for this motivation),

(1.1)

\displaystyle\Box_{g}u+u^{3}=f{\rm\ in\ }{\mathcal{M}}

\displaystyle u=0,\ t<-1.

This Cauchy problem has a unique solution (see e.g.
[
KLU18
]
or
[
CMOP19
]
) if we assume
f
is compactly supported in the cylinder
(-1,\infty)\times\mathcal{U}
and has small
C^{4}_{0}((-1,\infty)\times\mathcal{U})
norm.

The data we wish to collect is the
single point
source-to-solution map

(1.2)

\displaystyle L_{M,g,2T+1}f:=u(t,{\bf 0})\mid_{t\in(0,2T+1)},\ \ \ f\in C^{4}_{0}((-1,2T+1)\times\mathcal{U})

for a fixed
T>>1
. Note that while we are allowed to place sources in a cylindrical neighborhood of the timelike segment
\{(t,{\bf 0})\in{\mathcal{M}}\mid t\in(-1,2T+1)\}
, we are only collecting data on the time-like curve
(0,2T+1)\times{\bf 0}
. We denote by
B_{g_{j}}(x_{j},r_{j})
the Riemannian open ball of
(M_{j},g_{j})
centered at
x_{j}\in M_{j}
with radius
r_{j}
.
Our result is the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M_{1},g_{1})
and
(M_{2},g_{2})
be three-dimensional Riemannian manifolds. If their the source-to-solution map coincide
L_{M_{1},g_{1},2T+1}=L_{M_{2},g_{2},2T+1}
, then the Riemannian balls
(B_{g_{1}}({\bf 0},T),g_{1})
and
(B_{g_{2}}({\bf 0},T),g_{2})
are isometric.

To the best of the author’s knowledge, this is the first inverse problems result where measurements are done at a single point. Note also that a-priori the two Riemannian balls need not to have the same topological structure.

Remark 1.2
.

The intervals
[-1,2T+1]
in (
4.1
) and
(0,2T+1)
in the definition of source-to-solution can be replaced by
[-\delta,2T+\delta]
and
(0,2T+\delta)
for any fixed
\delta>0
. As this article already uses an abundance of small parameters in proving Theorem
1.1
, we consider the constant
1
in place of a parameter
\delta>0
to avoid having to keep track of yet another small parameter.

In this article we will assume that

(1.3)

\displaystyle g_{1}\mid_{{\mathcal{U}}}=g_{2}\mid_{\mathcal{U}}:=g_{0}\mid_{{\mathcal{U}}}

since deducing this fact from the source-to-solution map is trivial. We may assume without loss of generality that

(1.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}=B_{g_{0}}({\bf 0};\delta_{0})

where

(1.5)

\displaystyle\delta_{0}<\frac{1}{2}{\rm min}\left({\rm inj}(B_{g_{1}}({\bf 0},2T),g_{1}),{\rm inj}(B_{g_{2}}({\bf 0},2T),g_{2})\right)

is chosen small enough for
{\mathcal{U}}
to be convex.

Our method uses the nonlinear interactions idea of
[
KLU14
,
KLU18
,
KLOU14
]
. We motivate the idea of our approach by considering the special case when
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
are both Riemannian metrics on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and assume the absence of conjugate points. Let
y_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
x_{1}\in{\mathcal{U}}
. At time
t=0
we create a point source at
{\bf 0}
which produces a wave whose singularity propagates along the unit speed
g_{1}
-geodesic ray
[{\bf 0},y_{1}]_{g_{1}}
joining
{\bf 0}
and
y_{1}
(see
[
MU79
]
). At another time
t_{1}
(which may be greater or less than
0
), we send another singularity along the unit speed
g_{1}
-geodesic ray
[x_{1},y_{1}]_{g_{1}}
so that the two rays collide at
y_{1}
at time
t=R_{0}:=d_{g_{1}}({\bf 0},y_{1})
. Arrange for an auxiliary singularity to go along unit speed
g_{1}
-geodesic ray
[x_{2},y_{1}]_{g_{1}}
from
x_{2}\in{\mathcal{U}}
to
y_{1}
so that all three ray collide at the same time. When arranged judiciously (see Section
3
), the nonlinear interaction given by (
4.1
) will result in a singularity coming back from
y_{1}
to
{\bf 0}
along the
g_{1}
-geodesic ray
[{\bf 0},y_{1}]_{g_{1}}
. The single observer sitting at
{\bf 0}
will then see this singularity at time
t=2R_{0}
. We then deduce, using the fact that source-to-solution maps are equal, that the same collisions must have occurred for the
g_{2}
metric at time
R_{0}
at some point
y_{2}
which is distance
R_{0}
away from
{\bf 0}
. This allows us to conclude that
d_{g_{1}}(y_{1},x)=d_{g_{2}}(y_{2},x)=R_{0}-t_{1}
for all
x\in{\mathcal{U}}
(see Prop
4.7
and its corollary). This, together with a simple Riemannian argument (Prop.
4.12
), implies that the Riemannian balls
(B_{g_{1}}({\bf 0},T),g_{1})
and
(B_{g_{2}}({\bf 0},T),g_{2})
are isometric.

The difficulty in carrying out this program is that sources do not produce waves which propagate along a single light ray but rather traveling planes. The interaction of three traveling plane waves generically produce a spacelike curve as an artificial source (see Sect
3.2
) and light emanating from such a curve can produce focal points even if we assume the absence of conjugate points. A single observer along a timelike path may not be able to “see” such singularity. See Remark
2.13
.

To overcome this issue we place small spherical “mirrors” near
{\bf 0}
. When the location and timing of these mirrors are chosen judiciously, further nonlinear interaction between the returning light wave and these mirrors will reflect a visible
conormal
wave back to
{\bf 0}
(see Sect
3.3
) which is visible to the lone observer, allowing us to circumvent the difficulty of focal points.

We note that it may be possible to avoid focal points by replacing the cubic nonlinearity in (
4.1
) by a quadratic nonlinearity and work with artificial point sources resulting from a four wave interaction. In addition to its relationship to Yang-Mills equations, we have chosen to consider the cubic nonlinearity because it motivates us to develop a robust method which has the potential to be applied to a broader range of problems. For example, this approach of using mirrors can potentially allow us to recover the “earliest light observation set” in the general Lorentzian setting (see
[
KLU18
]
) from artificial sources to a narrow cylinder in space-time by only looking at singularity along a timelike curve contained in the cylinder.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Basic Definitions

Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian manifold. Associated to this Riemannian structure is a stationary Lorentzian metric
-dt^{2}+g
on the space-time

{\mathcal{M}}=\{(t,x)\mid t\in\mathbb{R},x\in M\}.

The time orientation on
{\mathcal{M}}
is given by
\partial_{t}
.
It is sometimes convenient to have a Riemannian structure on
{\mathcal{M}}
and the obvious choice is
G:=dt^{2}+g
.

Throughout this article, covectors in
{\mathcal{M}}
are denoted by
\xi=\tau dt+\xi^{\prime}
, where
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
and
\xi^{\prime}\in T^{*}M
. We denote by
{\rm Char}_{g}\subset T^{*}{\mathcal{M}}
the subspace of lightlike covectors, which are those covectors that are multiple of
\mp dt+\xi^{\prime}
, with
\xi^{\prime}\in S^{*}M
. Here, the covectors
-dt+\xi^{\prime}
and
+dt+\xi^{\prime}
are future and past pointing respectively. Notice that
{\rm Char}_{g}
is the characteristic set of the homogeneous principal symbol of
\Box_{g}
.

If
\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}\subset T^{*}M
we use the notation

\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{2}:=\{(t,x,\xi)\mid(t,x)\in\pi(\Lambda_{1})\cap\pi(\Lambda_{2}),\ \xi=\xi_{1}+\xi_{2},\ {\rm with}\ (t,x,\xi_{j})\in\Lambda_{j}\}.

When
\Lambda_{1}\cap T^{*}_{(t,x)}{\mathcal{M}}
and
\Lambda_{2}\cap T^{*}_{(t,x)}{\mathcal{M}}
are linearly independent for all
(t,x)\in\pi(\Lambda_{1})\cap\pi(\Lambda_{2})
, we will emphasize this fact with
\oplus
in place of just
+
.

A smooth curve
s\mapsto(t(s),\gamma(s))\in{\mathcal{M}}
is causal when its tangent vectors are timelike (i.e.
-\dot{t}(s)^{2}+\|\dot{\gamma}(s)\|_{g}^{2}<0
) or lightlike (i.e.
-\dot{t}(s)^{2}+\|\dot{\gamma}(s)\|_{g}^{2}=0
). The curve is future-directed if
\dot{t}(s)>0
, and past-directed if
\dot{t}(s)<0
. The future and past causal cones at
(t_{0},x_{0})\in{\mathcal{M}}
are defined respectively by

\displaystyle I_{g}^{+}(t_{0},x_{0})

\displaystyle=\{(t,x)\mid\mbox{ there exists a future-directed causal curve from}\ (t_{0},x_{0})\ \mbox{to}\ (t,x)\},

\displaystyle I_{g}^{-}(t_{0},x_{0})

\displaystyle=\{(t,x)\mid\mbox{ there exists a past-directed causal curve from}\ (t_{0},x_{0})\ \mbox{to}\ (t,x)\}.

2.2.
Geodesics and Conjugate Points

Let
H
denote the Hamiltonian vector field on
T^{*}{\mathcal{M}}
associated to the principal symbol of
\Box_{g}
, that is, to the Hamiltonian function

(2.1)

\displaystyle(t,x,\tau dt+\xi^{\prime})\mapsto\tfrac{1}{2}\big(\|\xi^{\prime}\|_{g}^{2}-\tau^{2}\big).

The flow of
H
preserves
{\rm Char}_{g}
, and its orbits on
{\rm Char}_{g}
are precisely the lifts of the lightlike geodesics of
{\mathcal{M}}
. We define the future oriented flow on
{\rm Char}_{g}
by

\displaystyle e_{+}(s,t,x,\xi)=e_{+}^{s}(t,x,\xi):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{@{}ll}e^{sH}(t,x,\xi),&\mbox{if }\xi\ \mbox{ is future pointing,}\\
e^{-sH}(t,x,\xi),&\mbox{if }\xi\ \mbox{ is past pointing}\end{array}\right.

for
s\geq 0
. If
(t,x,\xi)\in{\rm Char}_{g}
we denote its future oriented flowout by

{\rm FLO}_{g}^{+}(t,x,\xi):=\{e_{+}^{s}(t,x,\xi)\mid s\geq 0\}.

Since the Lorentzian metric is a direct sum of
-dt
with a Riemannian metric
g
, the projection to
M
of the lightlike geodesics in
{\mathcal{M}}
are Riemannian geodesics. If
(t,x,\xi)\in{\rm Char}_{g}
and
\xi=-dt+\xi^{\prime}
with
\xi^{\prime}\in S^{*}_{x}M
, then

(2.5)

\displaystyle\pi\circ e_{+}^{s}(t,x,\xi)=(t+s,\gamma_{g}(s,\xi^{\prime}))

where
\gamma_{g}(s,\xi^{\prime})
is the unit speed geodesic in
M
with initial condition
(x,\xi^{\prime})
. We say that a Riemannian geodesic segment is length minimizing when it is the shortest curve joining its endpoints; we further say that it is unique length minimizing if every other curve joining the same endpoints is strictly longer.

Lemma 2.1
.

Assume that, for some
(y_{0},\eta_{0}^{\prime})\in S^{*}M
and
R>0
, the geodesic segment
\gamma_{g}([0,R],\eta_{0}^{\prime})
is unique length minimizing and does not contain conjugate points.
Then, there exist
\delta>0
and an open neighborhood
U^{\prime}\subset S^{*}M
of
(y_{0},\eta^{\prime}_{0})
such that, for each
y\in\pi(U^{\prime})
, the map

(2.6)

\displaystyle(0,R+\delta]\times(S^{*}_{y}M\cap U^{\prime})\to M,\qquad(t,\eta^{\prime})\mapsto\exp_{y}(t\eta^{\prime})

is a diffeomorphism onto its image.
Up to reducing both
U^{\prime}
and
\delta
, for any
(y,\eta^{\prime})\in U^{\prime}
and
t\in(0,R+\delta)
, the geodesics segment
\gamma_{g}([0,t],\eta^{\prime})
is unique length minimizing; in fact, there exists
c_{0}>0
such that, for all
(y,\eta^{\prime})\in U^{\prime}
,
t\in(R-\delta,R+\delta)
,
\hat{\eta}^{\prime}\in S^{*}M\setminus\{\eta^{\prime}\}
, and
\hat{t}>0
, if
\exp_{y}(t\eta^{\prime})=\exp_{y}(\hat{t}\hat{\eta}^{\prime})
then
\hat{t}>t+c_{0}
.

Proof.

The fact that (
2.6
) is a diffeomorphism onto its image is well known due to the fact that the endpoints of
\gamma([0,R],\eta^{\prime}_{0})
are not conjugate along the geodesic segment. As for the second part of the statement, assume by contradiction that there exist sequences
S^{*}M\ni(y_{j},\eta^{\prime}_{j})\to(y_{0},\eta^{\prime}_{0})
,
t_{j}\to t_{0}\in(0,R]
,
\hat{\eta}_{j}^{\prime}\in S^{*}_{y_{j}}M\setminus\{\eta_{j}^{\prime}\}
, and
\hat{t}_{j}\leq t_{j}+j^{-1}
such that

(2.7)

\displaystyle\gamma_{g}(\hat{t}_{j},\hat{\eta}^{\prime}_{j})=\gamma_{g}(t_{j},\eta^{\prime}_{j}).

Since the map (
2.6
) is a diffeomorphism onto its image, we must have that
\hat{\eta}_{j}^{\prime}\notin U^{\prime}
. Therefore, up to extracting a subsequence,
\hat{\eta}_{j}^{\prime}\to\hat{\eta}^{\prime}\in S^{*}_{y_{0}}M\backslash U^{\prime}
and
\hat{t}_{j}\to\hat{t}_{0}
. In particular
\hat{\eta}^{\prime}\neq\eta^{\prime}_{0}
. This implies
\gamma_{g}(\hat{t}_{0},\hat{\eta}^{\prime})=\gamma_{g}(t_{0},\eta^{\prime}_{0})
, contradicting the fact that
\gamma_{g}([0,t_{0}],\eta^{\prime}_{0})
is unique length minimizing.
∎

We recall that a Lagrangian submanifold
\Lambda\subset T^{*}{\mathcal{M}}
is conic when
\lambda\xi\in\Lambda
for all
\lambda>0
and
\xi\in\Lambda
. The following Lemmas are classical, and we will not provide a proof.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
K\subset{\mathcal{M}}
be a spacelike curve then the intersection
N^{*}K\cap{\rm Char}_{g}\subset T^{*}{\mathcal{M}}
is a three-dimensional submanifold. ∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\Lambda\subset T^{*}{\mathcal{M}}
be a conic Lagrangian submanifold such that the intersection
\Lambda\cap{\rm Char}_{g}
is a smooth three-dimensional submanifold nowhere tangent to
H
. Then its flowout

{\rm FLO}_{g}^{+}(\Lambda\cap{\rm Char}_{g})

is Lagrangian. ∎

Let
\xi_{0}\in T_{(t_{0},x_{0})}^{*}{\mathcal{M}}
. For any
h>0
small, we set

{\mathcal{B}}_{h}(\xi_{0}):=\big\{\xi\in T_{(t_{0},x_{0})}^{*}{\mathcal{M}}\ \big|\ \|\xi-\xi_{0}\|_{G}<h\big\}

Denote by
{\rm ccl}\mathcal{B}_{h}(\xi_{0})
its cone closure.
We recall that a hypersurface
S\subset{\mathcal{M}}
is called lightlike when the Lorentzian metric
-dt^{2}+g
restricts to a degenerate bilinear form on
TS
, or equivalently its conormal bundle consists of lightlike covectors.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\xi^{\prime}_{0}\in S_{x_{0}}^{*}M
and
t_{0}>0
be such that the end points of the geodesic
\gamma([0,t_{0}],\xi^{\prime}_{0})
are not conjugate. We set
\xi_{0}:=-dt+\xi^{\prime}_{0}\in T^{*}_{(0,x_{0})}{\mathcal{M}}\cap{\rm Char}_{g}
, and consider for
h>0
small enough the Lagrangian submanifolds

\displaystyle\Lambda^{\pm}:={\rm FLO}_{g}^{+}({\rm ccl}{\mathcal{B}}_{h}(\pm\xi_{0})\cap{\rm Char}_{g}).

Then, for any sufficiently small open neighborhood
{\mathcal{V}}\subset{\mathcal{M}}
of
(t_{0},\gamma(t_{0},\xi^{\prime}_{0}))
, there exists a lightlike codimension-one hypersurface
S\subset\mathcal{V}
such that

(\Lambda^{+}\cup\Lambda^{-})\cap T^{*}{\mathcal{V}}=N^{*}S.

Proof.

Let
U\subset S_{x_{0}}^{*}M
be a small open neighborhood of
\xi_{0}^{\prime}
, and
\epsilon>0
a small positive quantity. We define the maps

\displaystyle\iota_{\pm}:(t_{0}-\epsilon,t_{0}+\epsilon)\times U\to{\rm Char}_{g}\subset T^{*}{\mathcal{M}},

\displaystyle\iota_{\pm}(t,\xi^{\prime})=e_{+}(t,0,x_{0},\mp dt\pm\xi^{\prime})=(t,\gamma(t,\xi^{\prime}),\mp dt\pm\dot{\gamma}(t,\xi^{\prime})^{\flat}),

where
\dot{\gamma}(t,\xi^{\prime}):=\partial_{t}\gamma(t,\xi^{\prime})
and
\dot{\gamma}(t,\xi^{\prime})^{\flat}:=g(\dot{\gamma}(t,\xi^{\prime}),\cdot)
. Since the end points of the geodesic
\gamma([0,t_{0}],\xi^{\prime}_{0})
are not conjugate,
\pi_{2}\circ\iota
is a diffeomorphism onto its image, where
\pi_{2}:T{\mathcal{M}}\to M
is the projection onto the factor
M
of the base
{\mathcal{M}}=\mathbb{R}\times M
.
If
{\mathcal{V}}\subset{\mathcal{M}}
is a sufficiently small open neighborhood of
(t_{0},\gamma(t_{0},\xi^{\prime}_{0}))
, and
\tilde{U}:=\iota^{-1}_{+}(T^{*}\mathcal{V})=\iota^{-1}_{-}(T^{*}\mathcal{V})
, then clearly

\displaystyle\Lambda^{\pm}\cap T^{*}{\mathcal{V}}={\rm ccl}(\iota_{\pm}(\tilde{U})).

The codimension-one hypersurface
S\subset\mathcal{V}
of the statement is given by

\displaystyle S:=\{(t,\gamma(t,\xi^{\prime}))\ |\ (t,\xi^{\prime})\in\tilde{U}\}.

If
J_{1},J_{2}
are two linearly independent Jacobi fields along the geodesic
\gamma(\cdot,\xi^{\prime})
that are orthogonal to it and satisfy
J_{1}(0)=J_{2}(0)=0
, then the tangent spaces of
S
are given by

\displaystyle T_{(t,\gamma(t,\xi^{\prime}))}S=\mathrm{span}\{\partial_{t}+\dot{\gamma}(t,\xi^{\prime}),J_{1}(t),J_{2}(t)\}.

We claim that
\iota_{\pm}(\tilde{U})\subset N^{*}S
. Indeed

\displaystyle(\mp dt\pm\dot{\gamma}(t,\xi^{\prime})^{\flat})(\partial_{t}+\dot{\gamma}(t,\xi^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=\mp 1\pm\|\dot{\gamma}(t,\xi^{\prime})\|_{g}^{2}=0,

\displaystyle(\mp dt\pm\dot{\gamma}(t,\xi^{\prime})^{\flat})(J_{i}(t))

\displaystyle=g(\pm\dot{\gamma}(t,\xi^{\prime}),J_{i}(t))=0.

Since the conormal bundle
N^{*}S
has rank one, we conclude
{\rm ccl}(\iota_{+}(\tilde{U})\cup\iota_{-}(\tilde{U}))=N^{*}S
. Finally, since
\iota_{\pm}
has image inside the characteristic set
{\rm Char}_{g}
,
S
is a lightlike hypersurface.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
S_{j}\subset{\mathcal{M}}
,
j=1,2,3
, be lightlike hypersurfaces with a triple transverse intersection at
(0,{\bf 0})
, i.e.

(2.8)

\displaystyle{\rm Dim}\left(N^{*}_{(0,{\bf 0})}S_{1}+N^{*}_{(0,{\bf 0})}S_{2}+N^{*}_{(0,{\bf 0})}S_{3}\right)=3.

Then there is an open set
\mathcal{O}\subset{\mathcal{M}}
containing
(0,{\bf 0})
so that
K:=S_{1}\cap S_{2}\cap S_{3}\cap\mathcal{O}
is a spacelike segment. Furthermore,

(2.9)

\displaystyle N_{(t,x)}^{*}K=N^{*}_{(t,x)}S_{1}+N^{*}_{(t,x)}S_{2}+N^{*}_{(t,x)}S_{3},\qquad\forall(t,x)\in K.

Proof.

The transversality condition (
2.8
) ensures that near
(0,{\bf 0})
the triple intersection
S_{1}\cap S_{2}\cap S_{3}
is an open curve segment
K
and that (
2.9
) holds.

It remains to check that
K
is spacelike. Observe that due to (
2.9
) if
V\in T_{(0,{\bf 0})}K\subset T_{(0,{\bf 0})}{\mathcal{M}}
then there are three independent covectors
\xi_{j}\in T^{*}_{(0,{\bf 0})}{\mathcal{M}}\cap{\rm Char}_{g}
,
j=1,2,3
, so that
\xi_{j}(V)=0
. Without loss of generality
\xi_{j}=-dt+\xi^{\prime}_{j}
with
\xi^{\prime}_{j}\in S^{*}M
. By linear independence we have that

(2.10)

\displaystyle\xi_{1}^{\prime}\neq\xi_{2}^{\prime}

Without loss of generality we can write
V=a\partial_{t}+V^{\prime}
for some
V^{\prime}\in S_{{\bf 0}}M
. Using
\xi_{1}(V)=\xi_{2}(V)=0
, we see that
\xi^{\prime}_{1}(V^{\prime})=\xi^{\prime}_{2}(V^{\prime})=a
. Due to (
2.10
) we have that
|a|<1
. Therefore
V
is spacelike and this implies that the segment
K
is spacelike near
(0,{\bf 0})
.
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
S\subset{\mathcal{M}}
be a lightlike hypersurface and let
\xi\in N^{*}_{(t,x)}S
. If
V\in T_{(t,x)}S
is a lightlike vector then
V\in\spn\{\xi^{\sharp}\}
where
\sharp
is taken with respect to the Lorentzian metric
-dt^{2}+g
.

Proof.

Write
\xi=-dt+\xi^{\prime}
with
\xi^{\prime}\in S^{*}_{x}M
then
\xi^{\sharp}=\partial_{t}+\xi^{\prime\sharp}
where
\xi^{\prime\sharp}
is the raising of index with respect to the Riemannian metric
g
on
M
. Since
V\in T_{(t,x)}S
is lightlike it is of the form (modulo multiplication by
\mathbb{R}
),
V=\partial_{t}+V^{\prime}
for some
V^{\prime}\in S_{x}M
. By the fact that
\xi\in N^{*}_{(t,x)}S
,
\xi(V)=0
meaning that

1=\xi^{\prime}(V^{\prime})=\langle\xi^{\prime\sharp},V^{\prime}\rangle_{g}.

Since both
\xi^{\prime\sharp}
and
V^{\prime}
are unit vectors, we must have that
V^{\prime}=\xi^{\prime\sharp}
. That is
V=\xi^{\sharp}
.
∎

Lemma 2.7
.

For all linearly independent covectors
\eta^{\prime}_{0},\eta^{\prime}_{1}\in S^{*}_{y}M
, there exists
\eta^{\prime}_{2}\in\spn\{\eta_{0}^{\prime},\eta_{1}^{\prime}\}\cap S^{*}_{y}M
arbitrarily close to
\eta^{\prime}_{0}
such that

(2.11)

\displaystyle{\rm Dim}\left(\spn\{-dt-\eta^{\prime}_{0},-dt-\eta^{\prime}_{1},-dt-\eta^{\prime}_{2}\}\right)=3.

Proof.

We choose orthonormal coordinates on
T^{*}_{y}M
so that

\displaystyle-\eta^{\prime}_{1}=(1,0,0),\qquad-\eta^{\prime}_{0}=(s,\sqrt{1-s^{2}},0),

for some
s\in[-1,1]
.
Since
\eta^{\prime}_{1}
and
\eta^{\prime}_{0}
are linearly independent, we have
s\neq\pm 1

4.4.
Recover Riemannian Structure from Distance Data

We now complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
with the following proposition. Let
(M_{i},g_{i})
,
i=1,2
, be complete Riemannian manifolds. For some
x_{i}\in M_{i}
and
R>0
, we define

\displaystyle V_{i}:=\big\{\xi^{\prime}\in T_{x_{i}}M_{i}\ \big|\ d_{g_{i}}(x_{i},\exp_{x_{i}}(\xi^{\prime}))=\|\xi^{\prime}\|_{g_{i}}<T\big\}.

Notice that
V_{i}
is precisely the set of those tangent vectors
\xi^{\prime}\in T_{x_{i}}M_{i}
whose associated geodesic
\gamma(\cdot,\xi^{\prime}):[0,1]\to M_{i}
,
\gamma(t,\xi^{\prime})=\exp_{x_{i}}(t\xi^{\prime})
is length-minimizing.

Proposition 4.12
.

Assume that, for some open neighborhoods
{\mathcal{U}}_{i}\subset B_{g_{i}}(x_{i},T)
of
x_{i}
, there exists an isometry
\psi:({\mathcal{U}}_{1},g_{1})\to({\mathcal{U}}_{2},g_{2})
such that

(i)

d\psi(x_{1})V_{1}=V_{2}
,

(ii)

d_{g_{1}}(y,\exp_{x_{1}}(\xi^{\prime}))=d_{g_{2}}(\psi(y),\exp_{x_{2}}(d\psi(x_{1})\xi^{\prime}))
for all

and
\xi^{\prime}\in V_{1}
.

Then, there is an extension of
\psi
to an isometry

\psi:(B_{g_{1}}(x_{1},T),g_{1})\to(B_{g_{2}}(x_{2},T),g_{2}).

Proof.

Since the Riemannian manifolds
(M_{i},g_{i})
are complete, every point of
B_{g_{i}}(x_{i},T)
can be connected to
x_{i}
by means of a length-minimizing geodesic. Namely,

\exp_{x_{i}}(V_{i})=B_{g_{i}}(x_{i},T).

We claim that there exists a continuous bijection
\phi:B_{g_{1}}(x_{1},T)\to B_{g_{2}}(x_{2},T)

\displaystyle\phi(\exp_{x_{1}}(\xi^{\prime}))=\exp_{x_{2}}(d\psi(x_{1})\xi^{\prime}),\qquad\forall\xi^{\prime}\in V_{1}

Indeed, since the isometry
\psi
maps geodesics to geodesics, such a
\phi
is clearly well defined on a Riemannian ball
B_{g_{1}}(x_{1},\epsilon)\subset{\mathcal{U}}_{1}
, and indeed

\phi|_{B_{g_{1}}(x_{1},\epsilon)}=\psi|_{B_{g_{1}}(x_{1},\epsilon)}
.
Assume that there exist two distinct vectors
\xi^{\prime},\eta^{\prime}\in V_{1}
such that

y_{1}:=\exp_{x_{1}}(\xi^{\prime})=\exp_{x_{1}}(\eta^{\prime})
. All we need to show in order to have a well defined continuous bijection

\displaystyle\exp_{x_{2}}(d\psi(x_{1})\xi^{\prime})=\exp_{x_{2}}(d\psi(x_{1})\eta^{\prime}).

We set

small enough,

, and

. We have

\displaystyle d_{g_{2}}(x_{2},y_{2})

\displaystyle=\|\xi^{\prime}\|_{g_{1}}=d_{g_{1}}(x_{1},y_{1})=d_{g_{1}}(x_{1},z_{1})+d_{g_{1}}(z_{1},y_{1})=d_{g_{2}}(x_{2},z_{2})+d_{g_{2}}(z_{2},y_{2}),

where the last equality follows by assumption (ii). This implies that
z_{2}
lies on a length-minimizing geodesic segment joining
x_{2}
and
y_{2}
, and therefore (
4.78
) holds.

We now prove that
\phi
is a diffeomorphism. Fix an arbitrary point

. Notice that

is the unique length-minimizing geodesic segment joining
z_{1}
and
y_{1}
, and does not contain conjugate points. Analogously,
\gamma_{2}:=\phi\circ\gamma_{1}|_{[\delta,\ell]}
is the unique length-minimizing geodesic segment joining
z_{2}:=\phi(z_{1})
and
y_{2}:=\phi(y_{1})
, and does not have conjugate points. Therefore, the Riemannian distances
d_{g_{i}}
are smooth in an open neighborhood
Z_{i}\times Y_{i}
of
(z_{i},y_{i})
. We take
Z_{i}
to be small enough so that

and
\phi(Z_{1})=Z_{2}
. For each

, the derivative
\partial_{y_{i}}d_{g_{i}}(w,y_{i})\in S_{y_{i}}^{*}M
is precisely the
g_{i}
-dual of the tangent vector to the unique length-minimizing geodesic segment joining

and
y_{i}
. Therefore, for a generic triple of points

. This implies that, up to shrinking the open neighborhoods

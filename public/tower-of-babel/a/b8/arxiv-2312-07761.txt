F F -Thresholds of Filtrations of Ideals
Mitra Koley, Arvind Kumar

Abstract
. In this article we extend the notion of the F F -thresholds of ideals to the F F -thresholds for filtrations of ideals. Existence of F F -thresholds of filtrations are established for various types of filtrations. Moreover various necessary and sufficient conditions for finiteness of F F -thresholds are given. We also give some effective upper bounds of the symbolic F F -thresholds. A sufficient condition for symbolic F F -splitting in terms of its symbolic F F -threshold is given. We compute the symbolic F F -thresholds of various determinantal ideals, F F -König ideals, and many more.

1.
Introduction

The
F
-thresholds were introduced by Mustaţă, Takagi and Watanabe for
F
-finite regular rings as a prime characteristic invariant of singularities
[
32
]
. This invariant is positive characteristic analogue of log canonical thresholds, an invariant of singularities defined in characteristics
0
. In the introductory paper
[
32
]
, it was shown that the
F
-thresholds coincide with the jumping exponents for the generalized test ideals defined by Hara and Yoshida in
[
15
]
.

In a subsequent paper by Huneke, Mustaţă, Takagi and Watanabe, this notion was generalized for arbitrary rings of prime characteristics
[
23
]
. Given a ring
R
of prime characteristic
p>0
and ideals
\mathfrak{a}
and
I
, such that
\mathfrak{a}
is contained in radical of
I
, the
F
-threshold asymptotically measures the containment of the powers of
\mathfrak{a}
in the Frobenius powers of
I
.
Given
\mathfrak{a}
and
I
as described above, they defined
for all
q=p^{e}
,

\nu^{I}_{\mathfrak{a}}(q):=\max\{r:\mathfrak{a}^{r}\not\subseteq I^{[q]}\},

where
I^{[q]}=(f^{q}:f\in I)
and

\mathcal{C}^{I}_{+}(\mathfrak{a}):=\limsup_{e\to\infty}\frac{\nu^{I}_{\mathfrak{a}}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}\hskip 14.22636pt\textit{and}\hskip 14.22636pt\mathcal{C}^{I}_{-}(\mathfrak{a}):=\liminf_{e\to\infty}\frac{\nu^{I}_{\mathfrak{a}}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}.

When these two limits coincide, the common value is denoted by

\mathcal{C}^{I}(\mathfrak{a})
and is called the
F
-threshold of
\mathfrak{a}
with respect to
I
. In the same article, the existence of the limit is shown for
F
-pure rings. When the ring is regular the limit always exists as in this case the sequence
\{\frac{\nu^{I}_{\mathfrak{a}}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}\}
becomes a bounded, monotone increasing sequence. Later in
[
8
]
, De-Stefani, Núñez Betancourt and Pérez
showed the existence of the
F
-thresholds in full generality.

Furthermore in
[
23
]
, the authors showed its relations with the Hilbert-Samuel multiplicity, tight closure, and integral closure. Because of its important connections with various areas of commutative algebra and geometry
[
2
,
3
,
5
,
18
,
19
,
30
,
31
,
33
,
38
]
, this invariant becomes an interesting object to study. The
F
-thresholds of defining ideals of Calabi-Yau hypersurfaces with respect to the homogeneous maximal ideals were computed by Bhatt and Singh in
[
1
]
. In
[
16
]
, Hernández computed the
F
-threshold of a principle ideal generated by a binomial a polynomial ring with respect to the homogeneous maximal ideal. In
[
31
]
, Miller, Singh, Varbaro computed
F
-thresholds for determinantal ideals with respect to again the homogeneous maximal ideal. Linear programs turn out to be useful in order to compute
F
-thresholds of various classes of ideals in polynomial rings, see
[
28
]
.

The main aim of this article is to extend the notion of the
F
-thresholds for filtrations of ideals, study their existence and various kinds of effective bounds. We compute
F
-thresholds of symbolic power filtrations of various ideals. Our work is inspired by
[
7
]
and
[
13
]
. The idea of studying the containment behaviour of a filtration of ideals inside Frobenius powers of an ideal comes from the recent works of Hà, Kumar, Nguyen and Nguyen see
[
13
]
where the authors studied containment behaviour between two filtrations. In
[
7
]
, the authors studied
F
-singularities defined for filtrations, precisely they introduced
F
-split filtrations, a new notion of
F
-singularity defined for filtraions. In this article, we give a numerical criterion in terms of symbolic
F
-threshold for a symbolic
F
-split filtration (Theorem 5.12). The paper is organised as follows.

In Section 2, we recall some definitions and useful results on filtrations, valuations,
F
-singularities, and graphs which are required in later sections. We also give a brief discussion on
F
-thresholds of ideals.

In Section 3, we extend the notion of the
F
-thresholds for filtrations of ideals as follows:
Let
I

\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}=\{\mathfrak{a}_{i}\}_{i\geq 0}
be a filtration of ideals in

\nu_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e}):=\sup\{r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\;:\;\mathfrak{a}_{r}\not\subseteq I^{[p^{e}]}\}.

Define

\mathcal{C}_{+}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}):=\limsup_{e\to\infty}\frac{\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}{\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\cup\{\pm\infty\}}\text{ and }\mathcal{C}_{-}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}):=\liminf_{e\to\infty}\frac{\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}{\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\cup\{\pm\infty\}}.

If

-threshold of
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}
with respect to

. When

is a local ring and

, the maximal ideal of

\mathcal{C}^{\mathfrak{m}}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})
(if exists) is called the
F
-threshold of
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}
. When
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}=\{{\mathfrak{a}}^{(i)}\}_{i\geq 0}

-threshold of
J
.
We show that any positive real number can be attained as an
F
-threshold of a filtration. After discussing some basic properties of
F
-thresholds for filtrations we compare
F
-thresholds of two filtrations and give conditions for the equality.
As consequences we get the existence of the
F
-thresholds for integral closure power filtration (for analytically unramified rings), tight closure filtration of an ideal. Rest of the section is devoted to discussions on various conditions for finiteness of
\mathcal{C}_{+}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})
of any filtration
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}
. We show that
F
-thresholds exist for Noetherian filtrations, symbolic power filtrations in polynomial rings.

In Section 4, we identify another class of filtrations for which the
F
-thresholds with respect to an ideal

exist. We call them the

-admissible filtration. Ordinary power filtrations, symbolic power filtrations and certain integral closure power filtrations appear as examples of

In Section 5 we study

is bounded above by

-thresholds are height of the respective ideals. More generally we give an equivalent criterion for

We show that if the symbolic

-split. We conjecture that the converse is also holds.

Conjecture 1.1
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be an
F
-finite regular local ring and

In section 6, we focus our attention to

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we recall some definitions and facts which we use in later sections. All rings considered in this article are Noetherian commutative ring with unity.

2.1.
Filtrations and Rees Algebras:

Let
R
be a ring. A
filtration
of ideals in
R
is a sequence of ideals
\{\mathfrak{a}_{i}\}_{i\geq 0}
that satisfy the followings:

(1)

\mathfrak{a}_{0}=R
,

(2)

\mathfrak{a}_{i+1}\subseteq{\mathfrak{a}_{i}}
for all
i\geq 0
,

(3)

\mathfrak{a}_{i}\mathfrak{a}_{j}\subseteq\mathfrak{a}_{i+j}
for all
i,j\geq 0
.

We use
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}
to denote the filtration
\{\mathfrak{a}_{i}\}_{i\geq 0}
. Let
R
be a graded ring, a filtration
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}
is called
homogeneous
, if for each
i
,
{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}
is a homogeneous ideal of
R
.

Well known examples of filtrations arise from a non-zero proper ideal in
R
. In the following, we illustrate a few standard examples of filtrations.

Example 2.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{a},{\mathfrak{b}}
be non-zero proper ideals in
R
.

(1)

The most well known and well studied example of a filtration is the
{\mathfrak{a}}
-adic filtration. We use
{\mathfrak{a}}^{\bullet}
to denote the
{\mathfrak{a}}
-adic filtration, i.e.,
\mathfrak{a}^{\bullet}=\{\mathfrak{a}^{i}\}_{i\geq 0}
.

(2)

The integral closure of an ideal
{\mathfrak{a}}
, denoted by
\overline{{\mathfrak{a}}}
, is defined as the ideal
\overline{{\mathfrak{a}}}:=(x\in R:\text{ there exist }n\in{\mathbb{N}}\text{ and }a_{i}\in{\mathfrak{a}}^{i}\text{ for }0\leq i\leq n\text{ such that }x^{n}+a_{1}x^{n-1}+\cdots+a_{n}=0).
An ideal
{\mathfrak{a}}
is called
integrally closed
if
{\mathfrak{a}}=\overline{{\mathfrak{a}}}
.
Integral closure of powers of
{\mathfrak{a}}
forms a filtration and it is known as the
normal filtration
of
{\mathfrak{a}}
. We use
\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{\bullet}}
to denote it, i.e.,
\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{\bullet}}=\{\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{i}}\}_{i\geq 0}
.

(3)

Saturation of powers of
{\mathfrak{a}}
with respect to
{\mathfrak{b}}
forms a filtration, see
[
12
]
. We use
{\mathfrak{a}}^{(\bullet)}_{{\mathfrak{b}}}=\{{\mathfrak{a}}^{i}:{\mathfrak{b}}^{\infty}\}_{i\geq 0}
, the notation from
[
12
]
, to denote this filtration. Here
{\mathfrak{a}}^{(i)}_{\mathfrak{b}}=\mathfrak{a}^{i}:{\mathfrak{b}}^{\infty}=\cup_{n\geq 1}({\mathfrak{a}}^{i}:{\mathfrak{b}}^{n})
. In
[
12
, Lemmas 2.1, 2.2]
, the authors proved that both the notions of symbolic powers of
{\mathfrak{a}}
can be realized as the saturated powers of
{\mathfrak{a}}
with respect to appropriate ideals.

(4)

The
i
-th symbolic power of
{\mathfrak{a}}
, denoted by
{\mathfrak{a}}^{(i)}
, is defined as
\cap_{{\mathfrak{p}}\in\Ass({\mathfrak{a}})}({\mathfrak{a}}^{i}R_{{\mathfrak{p}}}\cap R).
As said above,
symbolic powers
of
{\mathfrak{a}}
forms a filtration. We denote this filtration by
{\mathfrak{a}}^{(\bullet)}=\{{\mathfrak{a}}^{(i)}\}_{i\geq 0}.

(5)

Let
f:{\mathbb{N}}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
be a subadditive sequence. Then
\mathfrak{a}_{n}:=\mathfrak{a}^{\left\lceil f(n)\right\rceil}
is a filtration of ideals.

Let
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}
be a filtration of ideals in
R
. It is easy to note that
\sqrt{{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}}=\sqrt{{\mathfrak{a}}_{1}}
for all
i\geq 1
. Therefore, we call
\sqrt{{\mathfrak{a}}_{1}}
to be the
radical of

{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}
, and denote it by
\sqrt{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}}.
Similar to Rees algebra associated to any ideal in a ring, one can also define Rees algebra associated to any filtration
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}
of ideals. The
Rees Algebra
of
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}
is defined as

\mathcal{R}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}):=\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}t^{i}\subseteq R[t].

We say that a filtration
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}
is
Noetherian
if the asociated Rees Algebra
\mathcal{R}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})
is Noetherian. For
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the
k
-th Veronese subalgebra
of
{\mathcal{R}}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})
is defined to be

{\mathcal{R}}^{[k]}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}):=\bigoplus_{r\geq 0}{\mathfrak{a}}_{kr}t^{kr}\subseteq R[t].

It is well known that, see
[
36
, Proposition 2.1]
, if
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}
is a Noetherian filtration, then
{\mathcal{R}}^{[k]}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})
is a standard graded
R
-algebra for some
k
.

2.2.
Valuations

Here we review some basic facts about valuations. Our basic reference for valuations is
[
24
]
.

Let
K
be a field and
G
be a totally ordered Abelian group (written additively). A valuation on
K

is a group homomorphism
v:K\setminus\{0\}\to G\cup\{\infty\}
such that

(1)

v(0)=\infty
, and

(2)

for all
x
and
y
in
K
,
v(x+y)\geq\min\{v(x),v(y)\}
.

Let
R
be a domain and
K
denote its field of fractions. A valuation
v
on
K
is
supported on
R
if
v(x)\geq 0
for all
x\in R\setminus\{0\}
. Here in this article we only consider valuations on the fields of fractions of polynomial rings with
G=\mathbb{R}
, i.e. only real valuations. A valuation
v
on the field of fractions
K
of a polynomial ring
R
is said to be a
monomial valuation
, if for every non-zero polynomial

f\in R
,
v(f)=\min\{v(u):u\text{ is a monomial term in }f\}
.

Since in this article we only consider valuations on the fields of fractions of polynomial rings, in the following we recall the definition of Rees valuations of an ideal in a domain.

Let
R
be a domain with field of fractions
K
and
I
be an ideal in
R
. A set of
Rees valuation domains
of
I
is a set
\{V_{1},\cdots,V_{s}\}
consisting of valuation domains, with the following conditions:

(1)

For every
i
,
R\subseteq V_{i}\subseteq K
. Moreover, each
V_{i}
is Noetherian and it is not a field.

(2)

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\overline{I^{n}}=\bigcap_{i=1}^{s}(I^{n}V_{i})\cap R
.

(3)

The set
\{V_{1},\cdots,V_{s}\}
satisfying the previous conditions is minimal possible.

The valuations
\{v_{1},\cdots,v_{s}\}
that correspond to valuation domains
V_{1},\cdots,V_{s}
respectively are called a set of
Rees valuations
of
I
.

Theorem 2.2
.

(1)

[
24
, Theorem 10.1.6, Theorem 10.2.2]
Every ideal
I
in a Noetherian domain has a unique set (up to equivalence) of Rees valuations.

(2)

[
24
, Proposition 10.3.4]
Let
I
be a monomial ideal in a polynomial ring. Then every Rees valuation of
I
is a monomial valuation.

Remark 2.3
.

Let
R
be a domain with field of fractions
K
and
I
be an ideal in
R
. For any valuation
v
on
K
, we denote
v(I):=\min\{v(x):x\in I\setminus\{0\}\}
. Let
\{v_{1},\cdots,v_{s}\}
be the Rees valuations of
I
, then for each
n>0
, integral closure of
I^{n}
is given by

\overline{I^{n}}=\{r\in R:v_{i}(r)\geq nv_{i}(I),\text{ for all }1\leq i\leq s\}.

Next we recall the definition of (skew) Waldschmidt constant of a filtration from
[
13
]
.
Let
R
be a graded domain and
K
denote its field of fractions.
Let
v
be a valuation on
K
which is supported on
R
. Let
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}
be a filtration of ideals in
R
.
Then
skew Waldschmidt constant
of
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}
with respect to
v
is defined to be

\widehat{v}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}):=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{v({\mathfrak{a}}_{n})}{n}=\inf\limits_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\dfrac{v({\mathfrak{a}}_{n})}{n}.

It can be seen that for any filtration
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}
,
\{v({\mathfrak{a}}_{i})\}_{i\geq 1}
is a non-negative sub-additive sequence. Thus, by Fekete’s Lemma, the last equality of the definition follows.

Assume
R
is standard graded. Let
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}
be a homogeneous filtration of ideals in
R
and
\alpha
denote the valuation on
K
, defined as follows: let
f=f_{d_{1}}+\cdots+f_{d_{k}}
be a polynomial in
R
, where each
i
,
f_{d_{i}}
denotes its nonzero degree
d_{i}
-th homogeneous part, define
\alpha(f):=\min_{i}d_{i}
. Then
\alpha
is called the
degree valuation
and
\hat{\alpha}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})

is called the
Waldschmidt constant
of the filtration
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}
.

Remark 2.4
.

(1)

One can note that
0\leq\widehat{v}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})\leq\frac{v({\mathfrak{a}}_{n})}{n}\leq v({\mathfrak{a}}_{1}).

(2)

In general, the skew-Waldschmidt constant of a filtration
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}
with respect to a valuation
v
need not be positive, for example, see
[
13
, Example 2.8]
.

2.3.
Singularities in prime characteristics and
F
-thresholds

Let
R
be a Noetherian ring of prime characteristic
p>0
, and let
F:R\to R
denote the Frobenius homomorphism that sends
r\mapsto r^{p},
for any
r\in R
.
A ring
R
of prime characteristic
p>0
is called
F
-finite
if
R
becomes a finite
R
-module via the Frobenius map. For a reduced ring
R
, let
R^{1/q}
denote the ring of all
q
-th roots of
R
. One can note that a reduced ring
R
is
F
-finite if and only of
R^{1/p}
is module finite over
R
.

For every positive integer
e
, we write
q=p^{e}
. For any
R
-ideal
I=(f_{1},\cdots,f_{n})
, let
I^{[q]}:=(f_{1}^{q},\cdots,f_{n}^{q})
.

In the following we recall a few definition of singularities in prime characteristic that are defined in terms of action of the Frobenius.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
R
be a ring of prime characteristics
p>0
and
I
be an ideal of
R
.

(1)

The
Frobenius closure
of
I
, is the ideal, denoted by
I^{F}
, formed by the elements
x\in R
such that
x^{q}\in I^{[q]}
for all
q=p^{e}\gg 0
. An ideal
I
is called
Frobenius closed
if
I^{F}=I
.

(2)

The
tight closure
of
I
, is the ideal denoted by
I^{*}
and formed by the elements
x\in R
such that there exists a
c\in R\setminus{\cup}_{\mathfrak{p}\in Min(R)}\mathfrak{p}
and
cx^{q}\in I^{[q]}
for all
q=p^{e}\gg 0
.
An
R
-ideal
I
is
tightly closed
if
I^{*}=I
. One notes that
I\subseteq I^{F}\subseteq I^{*}\subseteq\overline{I}
.
Tight closure of powers of an ideal
{\mathfrak{a}}
forms a filtration of ideals in
R
. We use
({\mathfrak{a}}^{\bullet})^{*}=\{({\mathfrak{a}}^{i})^{*}\}_{i\geq 0}
to denote this filtration, and we call it as the
tight closure filtration
of
{\mathfrak{a}}
.

(3)

The ring
R
is called
F
-pure
if the Frobenius map is a pure homomorphism, that is for any
R
-module
M
,
M\otimes_{R}R\xrightarrow{1_{M}\otimes F}M\otimes_{R}R
is injective.

(4)

The ring
R
is called
weakly
F
-regular
if all
R
-ideals are tightly closed. An
F
-finite reduced ring
R
is called
strongly
F
-regular
if for every element
c
of
R
which is not any minimal prime of
R
, there exists a
e>0
, such that such that the
R
-module map
R\to R^{1/q}
sending
1\mapsto c^{1/q}
splits as a map of
R
-modules, where
q=p^{e}
. Every strongly
F
-regular ring is
F
-split.

(5)

[
7
, Definition 5.2]
Assume further that
R
is
F
-finite, regular which is either local or
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq}0
graded. Then
I
is called
symbolic
F
-split
if for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, there exists a splitting
\phi:R^{1/p}\to R
such that

\phi((I^{(np+1)})^{1/p})\subseteq I^{(n+1)}
.

In the following we summarize some basic results from literature.

Theorem 2.6
.

(1)

A ring is regular if and only if the Frobenius map
F:R\to R
is flat. In particular, in a regular ring every ideal is tightly closed.

(2)

An
F
-finite regular ring is strongly
F
-regular, hence it is
F
-split.

(3)

In an
F
-pure ring, every ideal is Frobenius closed.

(4)

[
7
]
Every symbolic
F
-split ideal is
F
-split. But the converse is false
[
7
, Example 5.13]
.

At the end we briefly discuss about
F
-thresholds for ideals in a ring.

Definition 2.7
.

[
23
]

Let
R
be a ring and
\mathfrak{a}
and
I
be two
R
-ideals, such that
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\sqrt{I}
.
For all
q=p^{e}
, define,

\nu^{I}_{\mathfrak{a}}(q):=\max\{r:\mathfrak{a}^{r}\not\subseteq I^{[q]}\},

and the limit

\mathcal{C}^{I}(\mathfrak{a}):=\lim_{e\to\infty}\frac{\nu^{I}_{\mathfrak{a}}(q)}{q}\text{ is called the \emph{$F$-threshold} of $\mathfrak{a}$ with respect to $I$}.

Remark 2.8
.

(1)

[
23
, Remark 2.1]

Note that since
{\mathfrak{a}}\subseteq\sqrt{I}
, for each
q
,
\nu^{I}_{\mathfrak{a}}(q)
a non negative integer. If
\mu({\mathfrak{a}})
is the number of elements in a generating set of
{\mathfrak{a}}
and
{\mathfrak{a}}^{N}\subseteq I
for some
N>0

\nu^{I}_{\mathfrak{a}}(q)\leq N(\mu({\mathfrak{a}})(q-1)+1)-1.

Hence the sequence
\{\frac{\nu^{I}_{\mathfrak{a}}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}\}
is a bounded sequence and bounded by
N\mu({\mathfrak{a}})
.

(2)

The notion of the
F
-threshold of an ideal was first introduced for an
F
-finite regular ring by Mustaţă, Takagi and Watanabe
[
32
]
. For regular rings, the sequence

Later the definition was generalized for arbitrary rings by Huneke, Mustaţă, Takagi and Watanabe in

. In that paper the authors prove the existence of the limit for

Graphs, Hypergraphs and associated ideals:

A

of

of

. A matching

A fractional matching is a function

such that, for each vertex

\sum f(e)\leq 1
where the sum is taken over all edges

incident to
v
.
The fractional matching number
of a graph
G
is the
\sup_{f}\{\sum_{e\in E({\mathcal{H}})}f(e):\text{where }f\text{ is a fractional matching}\}
.
The

of a hypergraph

so that adjacent vertices have different colors.
A

-fold chromatic number of

. Define the fractional chromatic number to be

\chi_{f}(\mathcal{H}):=\lim_{b\to\infty}\frac{\chi_{b}({\mathcal{H}})}{b}=\inf_{b}\frac{\chi_{b}({\mathcal{H}})}{b},

where the equality occurs because
\chi_{a+b}({\mathcal{H}})\leq\chi_{a}({\mathcal{H}})+\chi_{b}({\mathcal{H}})

Let

, and let

The edge ideal of a hypergraph is an Alexander dual of the cover ideal of that hypergraph and vice versa, i.e.,

I({\mathcal{H}})=\bigcap_{W\text{ is a vertex cover }}(x_{i}:i\in W)\text{ and }J({\mathcal{H}})=\bigcap_{e\in E({\mathcal{H}})}(x_{i}:i\in e).

The square-free monomial ideals are in a one to one correspondence with simple hypergraphs via edge ideals.

A hypergraph with each edge of cardinality two is known as
graph
. Let

A

3.
F
-thresholds of filtrations: their properties and existence

In this section, we extend the notion of
F
-thresholds of ideals to the notion of
F
-thresholds of filtrations of ideals and study their basic properties. Throughout this article,
R
denote a Noetherian commutative ring of prime characteristic
p>0
,

I
is an ideal of
R
and
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}=\{\mathfrak{a}_{i}\}_{i\geq 0}
is a filtration of ideals in
R
. For every non-negative integer
e
, we define

\nu_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e}):=\sup\{r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\;:\;\mathfrak{a}_{r}\not\subseteq I^{[p^{e}]}\}.

We define

\mathcal{C}_{+}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}):=\limsup_{e\to\infty}\frac{\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}{\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\cup\{\pm\infty\}}\text{ and }\mathcal{C}_{-}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}):=\liminf_{e\to\infty}\frac{\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}{\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\cup\{\pm\infty\}}.

If
\mathcal{C}_{+}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})=\mathcal{C}_{-}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}){\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}}
, then we denote it by
\mathcal{C}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})
and call it the
F
-threshold of
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}
with respect to
I
. When
(R,{\mathfrak{m}})
is a local ring, for simplicity, we call
{\mathcal{C}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})
to be the
F
-threshold of
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\mathfrak{m}}({\mathfrak{a}}^{(\bullet)})
to be the
symbolic
F
-threshold of
{\mathfrak{a}}
.

Throughout this article, we assume that
I
is a non-zero proper ideal of
R
as the trivial cases are discussed in the following remark:

Remark 3.1
.

If
I=R
, then
\mathfrak{a}_{r}\subseteq I^{[p^{e}]}
for all
r,e\geq 0
. Thus,
\mathcal{C}^{I}_{\pm}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})=-\infty.
Assume that if
I=(0)
. If
\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}\neq(0)
, then
\mathfrak{a}_{r}\not\subseteq I^{[p^{e}]}
for all
r,e\geq 0
. Hence,
\mathcal{C}^{I}_{\pm}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})=\infty.
And if
\mathfrak{a}_{r}=(0)
for
r\gg 0
, then
\nu_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e})=k
for some
k
. Thus,
\mathcal{C}^{I}_{\pm}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})=0.

Suppose that
I
is proper. In this case
0\leq\nu_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e})\leq\infty
, and therefore,
0\leq\mathcal{C}_{-}^{I}(p^{e})\leq\mathcal{C}_{+}^{I}(p^{e})\leq\infty
.

Next, we show that any non-negative real number can be realized as F-threshold of a filtration in a ring
R
. Indeed, given any non-negative real number
\alpha
, we provide a homogeneous filtration
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}
in a polynomial ring
R
such that
\mathcal{C}^{\mathfrak{m}}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})=\alpha
, where
\mathfrak{m}
denotes the unique homogeneous maximal ideal of
R
.

Example 3.2
.

Let
\alpha
be a non-negative real number. Let
R=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
, where
\mathbb{K}
is a field of characteristic
p>0
and
\mathfrak{m}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
. When
\alpha=0
, then one can take
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}
to be the zero filtration, i.e.,
{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}=0
for
i\geq 1
. Then,
\mathcal{C}^{\mathfrak{m}}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})=0
. Next, assume that
\alpha>0
. Take
{\mathfrak{a}}_{r}=\mathfrak{m}^{\left\lceil\frac{nr}{\alpha}\right\rceil}
for every
r\in\mathbb{N}
. It is easy to see that
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}
is a filtration, see also
[
13
, Example 2.14]
. We claim that
\mathcal{C}^{\mathfrak{m}}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})=\alpha
. If
r\geq\alpha p^{e}
, then
\frac{rn}{\alpha}\geq np^{e}
which implies that
{\mathfrak{a}}_{r}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{np^{e}}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{[p^{e}]}
, where the last containment follows from the Pigeonhole Principle, (cf.
[
20
, Lemma 2.4(1)]
).
Therefore,
\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}}^{\mathfrak{m}}(p^{e})<\alpha p^{e}
for all
e
, and hence,
\mathcal{C}_{+}^{\mathfrak{m}}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})=\limsup_{e\to\infty}\frac{\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}}^{\mathfrak{m}}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}\leq\alpha.
Next, take
r=\lceil\alpha(p^{e}-1)\rceil-1.
Then,
\lceil\frac{rn}{\alpha}\rceil\leq n(p^{e}-1),
and therefore,
{\mathfrak{a}}_{r}\not\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{[p^{e}]}
as
(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})^{p^{e}-1}\in{\mathfrak{a}}_{r}
. Thus, for all
e
,
\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}}^{\mathfrak{m}}(p^{e})\geq\lceil\alpha(p^{e}-1)\rceil-1.
Consequently,

\frac{\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}}^{\mathfrak{m}}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}\geq\frac{\lceil\alpha(p^{e}-1)\rceil-1}{p^{e}}\geq\frac{\alpha(p^{e}-1)-1}{p^{e}}.

Thus, by definition of liminf, we get
\mathcal{C}_{-}^{\mathfrak{m}}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})\geq\alpha.
Hence,
\mathcal{C}^{\mathfrak{m}}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})
exists and it is equal to
\alpha.

Next, we study some basic properties of
F
-threshold of filtration. Before that we recall the definition of cyclic pure extension. A ring extension
R\to S
is said to be
cyclic pure
, if for every ideal
J
in
R
,
JS\cap R=J
.

Proposition 3.3
.

(cf.
[
23
, Proposition 2.2]
)
Let
I,J
be proper non-zero ideals and
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}
be filtration in
R
. Then, we have the followings:

(1)

Let
e_{0}\geq 0
be a fixed integer. If
I^{[p^{e}]}=(I^{[p^{e}]})^{F}
for all
e\geq e_{0}
, then
p\nu_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e})\leq\nu_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e+1})
for all
e\geq e_{0}
. Moreover,

{\mathcal{C}^{I}_{\pm}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})}=\lim_{e\to\infty}\frac{\nu_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}=\sup_{e\geq e_{0}}\frac{\nu_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}.

In particular, when
R
is
F
-pure,

{\mathcal{C}^{I}_{\pm}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})}=\lim_{e\to\infty}\frac{\nu_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}=\sup_{{e\geq 0}}\frac{\nu_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}.

(2)

If
\sqrt{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}}\cap R^{o}\neq\emptyset
,
I^{[p^{e}]}=(I^{[p^{e}]})^{*}
for
e\geq e_{0}
and
0<{\lim_{e\to\infty}\frac{\nu_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}}<\infty
, then
\frac{\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}<\mathcal{C}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})
for
e\geq e_{0}
.

(3)

If
I\subseteq J
, then
\mathcal{C}^{J}_{\pm}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})\leq\mathcal{C}^{I}_{\pm}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})
.

(4)

For any
q=p^{e_{0}}
,
\mathcal{C}^{I^{[q]}}_{\pm}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})=q\;\mathcal{C}^{I}_{\pm}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})
.

(5)

If
R\to S
is a cyclic pure extension, i.e., for every ideal
J
in
R
, we have
JS\cap R=J
, then
C^{I}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})=C^{IS}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}S),
where
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}S=\{{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}S\}_{i\geq 0}
.

Proof.

(1)

Let
e\geq e_{0}
be any. Suppose that
{\mathfrak{a}}_{r}\not\subseteq I^{[p^{e}]}
. Since
I^{[p^{e}]}=(I^{[p^{e}]})^{F}
for
e\geq e_{0}
, we have
{\mathfrak{a}}_{r}^{[p]}\not\subseteq I^{[p^{e+1}]}
which implies that
{\mathfrak{a}}_{r}^{p}\not\subseteq I^{[p^{e+1}]}
. Now, as
{\mathfrak{a}}_{r}^{p}\subseteq{\mathfrak{a}}_{rp}
, we have
{\mathfrak{a}}_{rp}\not\subseteq I^{[p^{e+1}]}.
Thus,
p\nu_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e})\leq\nu_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e+1})
for
e\geq e_{0}
. This implies that the sequence
\left\{\frac{\nu_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}\right\}
is an eventually increasing sequence, and therefore,
\mathcal{C}^{I}_{\pm}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})=\sup_{e\geq e_{0}}\frac{\nu_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}.
Hence, the second part follows.

(2)

We prove this by contradiction. Suppose that for some
e\geq e_{0}
,
\frac{\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}={\lim_{e\to\infty}\frac{\nu_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}}.
Since
I^{[p^{e}]}=(I^{[p^{e}]})^{*}
for
e\geq e_{0}
, by
(2)
,

\frac{\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}\leq\frac{\nu_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e+f})}{p^{e+f}}\leq\lim_{e\to\infty}\frac{\nu_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}=\frac{\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}

for all
f
.
Thus,
\nu_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e+f})=p^{f}\nu_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e})
which implies that
{\mathfrak{a}}_{p^{f}\nu_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e})+1}\subseteq I^{[p^{e+f}]}
for all
f
. Observe that
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\nu_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e})}^{p^{f}+1}\subseteq{\mathfrak{a}}_{p^{f}\nu_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e})+1}\subseteq I^{[p^{e+f}]}
for all
f
. Since
I^{[p^{e}]}
is tightly closed and
\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}\cap R^{o}\neq\emptyset
,
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\nu_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e})}\subseteq I^{[p^{e}]}
which is a contradiction.

(3)

Suppose that
{\mathfrak{a}}_{r}\not\subseteq J^{[p^{e}]}
. Then,
{\mathfrak{a}}_{r}\not\subseteq I^{[p^{e}]}
as
I^{[p^{e}]}\subseteq J^{[p^{e}]}
which implies that
\nu_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}^{J}(p^{e})\leq\nu_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e}).
Now, the rest follows from definition of limsup and liminf.

(4)

Note that
{\mathfrak{a}}_{r}\not\subseteq I^{[p^{e+e_{0}}]}
if and only if
{\mathfrak{a}}_{r}\not\subseteq(I^{[p^{e_{0}}]})^{[p^{e}]}
as
I^{[p^{e+e_{0}}]}=(I^{[e_{0}]})^{[p^{e}]}.
This implies that
\nu_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e+e_{0}})=\nu_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}^{I^{[q]}}(p^{e}).
Now, using this equality in the definition of
\mathcal{C}^{I}_{\pm}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})
, we get the assertion.

(5)

Suppose that
{\mathfrak{a}}_{r}\subseteq I^{[p^{e}]}
. Then,
{\mathfrak{a}}_{r}S\subseteq I^{[p^{e}]}S=(IS)^{[p^{e}]}
which implies that
\nu^{I}_{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}}(p^{e})\geq\nu^{IS}_{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}S}(p^{e})
for all
e
. Since

R\rightarrow S
is cyclic pure extension, if
{\mathfrak{a}}_{r}S\subseteq(IS)^{[p^{e}]}=I^{[p^{e}]}S
, then
{\mathfrak{a}}_{r}\subseteq I^{[p^{e}]}
. Therefore,
\nu^{I}_{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}}(p^{e})\leq\nu^{IS}_{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}S}(p^{e})
for all
e
, and the proof follows.

∎

Remark 3.4
.

Let
R
be a Noetherian domain of prime characteristic
p>0
and
I
be a non-zero proper ideal of
R
such that
I^{[p^{e}]}=(I^{[p^{e}]})^{F}
for
e\gg 0
. We claim that
\mathcal{C}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})=0
if and only if
{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}=0
for all
i\geq 1
. If
\mathcal{C}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})=0,
then by
Proposition

3.3

(1)
,
\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e})=0
for
e\gg 0
, and therefore,
{\mathfrak{a}}_{1}\subseteq\cap_{e\gg 0}I^{[p^{e}]}=(0)
. Thus,
{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}=0
for all
i\geq 1
. The converse hold trivially.

We now compare
F
-threshold of filtration of ideals when one filtration contains another filtration.
Let
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}=\{{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}
and
{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet}=\{{\mathfrak{b}}_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}
be two filtrations in
R
. We write
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}\leq{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet}
if
{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}\subseteq{\mathfrak{b}}_{i}
for all

.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}=\{{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}
and
{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet}=\{{\mathfrak{b}}_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}
be two filtrations in
R
. If
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}\leq{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet}
, then
\mathcal{C}_{\pm}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})\leq\mathcal{C}_{\pm}^{I}({\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet})
. Moreover, if
\mathcal{R}({\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet})
is a finitely generated
\mathcal{R}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})
-module, then
\mathcal{C}_{\pm}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})=\mathcal{C}_{\pm}^{I}({\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet})
.

Proof.

If
\mathfrak{a}_{r}\not\subseteq I^{[p^{e}]}
, then
\mathfrak{b}_{r}\not\subseteq I^{[p^{e}]}
as
\mathfrak{a}_{r}\subseteq\mathfrak{b}_{r}
. Therefore,
\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}}^{I}(p^{e})\leq\nu_{\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e})
for all
e
. Thus, by the definition of limsup and liminf,
\mathcal{C}_{\pm}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})\leq\mathcal{C}_{\pm}^{I}({\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet})
.

Since
\mathcal{R}({{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet}})
is finitely generated as an
\mathcal{R}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})
-module, it follows from the proof of
[
13
, Theorem 3.2]
that there exists a positive integer
k
such that
{{\mathfrak{b}}_{n+k}}\subseteq{\mathfrak{a}}_{n}
for all
n
. We claim that
\nu_{\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e})\leq\nu_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e})+k
for all
e
. Let
r
be a non-negative integer such that
\mathfrak{b}_{r}\not\subseteq I^{[p^{e}]}
. If
r>k
, then
\mathfrak{a}_{r-k}\not\subseteq I^{[p^{e}]}
as
\mathfrak{b}_{r}\subseteq\mathfrak{a}_{r-k}
. Thus,
r-k\leq\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}}^{I}(p^{e})
which further implies that
\nu_{\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e})\leq\nu_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e})+k
. Therefore, for all
e
,
\frac{\nu_{{\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}}}^{I}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}\leq\frac{\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}}^{I}(p^{e})+k}{p^{e}}
, and hence,
\liminf_{e\to\infty}\frac{\nu_{{\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}}}^{I}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}\leq\liminf_{e\to\infty}\frac{\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}}^{I}(p^{e})+k}{p^{e}}
and
\limsup_{e\to\infty}\frac{\nu_{{\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}}}^{I}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}\leq\limsup_{e\to\infty}\frac{\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}}^{I}(p^{e})+k}{p^{e}}.
Note that

\liminf_{e\to\infty}\frac{\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}}^{I}(p^{e})+k}{p^{e}}=\liminf_{e\to\infty}\frac{\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}}^{I}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}\text{ and }\limsup_{e\to\infty}\frac{\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}}^{I}(p^{e})+k}{p^{e}}=\limsup_{e\to\infty}\frac{\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}}^{I}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}.

Thus,
\mathcal{C}_{\pm}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})\geq\mathcal{C}_{\pm}^{I}({\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet})
. Hence, the assertion follows.
∎

Let
{\mathfrak{a}}
be an ideal of

. A filtration

in

-admissible

As an immediate consequence, we obtain the following:

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
{\mathfrak{a}},I
be non-zero proper ideals of

such that

. Then, for any

-admissible filtration

.

Proof.

Since

is an

-admissible filtration,
{\mathfrak{a}}^{\bullet}\leq{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}
, and therefore, by

,
\mathcal{C}_{\pm}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}^{\bullet})\leq\mathcal{C}_{\pm}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})
. Also, there exists a positive integer

such that

for all

. Thus,
\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e})\leq\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}}^{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e})+k
for all

Now, applying limsup and liminf to the sequences, we get that
\mathcal{C}_{\pm}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})\leq\mathcal{C}_{\pm}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}^{\bullet})
. Hence, by
[

, Theorem 3.4]
, the assertion follows.
∎

As another immediate consequence, we obtain the following:

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
{\mathfrak{a}},I
be non-zero proper ideals of

. Then,
\mathcal{C}_{\pm}^{I}(\overline{{\mathfrak{a}}^{\bullet}})=\mathcal{C}_{\pm}^{I}(({\mathfrak{a}}^{\bullet})^{*}).

Proof.

It follows from the well known fact that

. Next, it follows from

that
{\mathcal{R}}(\overline{{\mathfrak{a}}^{\bullet}})
is finitely generated
{\mathcal{R}}(({\mathfrak{a}}^{\bullet})^{*})
-module. Hence, the assertion follows from Theorem
3.5
.
∎

Let
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
be a filtration of ideals in
R
.
Take
\overline{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}=\{\overline{\mathfrak{a}_{i}}\}_{i\geq 0}
and
({\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}})^{*}=\{({\mathfrak{a}_{i}})^{*}\}_{i\geq 0}
. Observe that
\overline{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}}
and
({\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}})^{*}
are filtrations of ideals in
R
and
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}\leq({\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}})^{*}\leq\overline{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}}.

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
be a filtration in
R
. Suppose that
{\mathcal{R}(\overline{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}})}
is a finitely generated
\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})
-module. Then,

\mathcal{C}_{\pm}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})=\mathcal{C}_{\pm}^{I}(({{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}})^{*})=\mathcal{C}_{\pm}^{I}(\overline{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}}).

Proof.

The assertion follows from Theorem
3.5
.
∎

Since for an analytically unramified local ring
(R,{\mathfrak{m}})
,
{\mathcal{R}(\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{\bullet}})}
is a finitely generated
\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{a}^{\bullet})
-module for every ideal
{\mathfrak{a}}
of
R
, (see
[
24
, Corollary 9.2.1]
) we have:

Corollary 3.9
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be an analytically unramified local ring of prime characteristic
p>0
. Let
{\mathfrak{a}},I
be non-zero proper ideals of
R
with
{\mathfrak{a}}\subseteq\sqrt{I}
. Then,
\mathcal{C}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}^{\bullet})=\mathcal{C}^{I}(\overline{{\mathfrak{a}}^{\bullet}}).

With the following example we illustrate
that
Theorem

3.5
and
Corollary

3.8
are not necessarily true without the hypothesis that
\mathcal{R}({\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet})
is finitely generated as an
\mathcal{R}(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})
-module.

Example 3.10
.

Let
S
be a regular local ring of characteristic
p>0
and
{\mathfrak{a}},I\subseteq S
be non-zero proper ideals with
{\mathfrak{a}}\subseteq\sqrt{I}
. Consider
R=S[[x]]/(x^{n})
, for some
n\geq 2
. First note that
R
being non-reduced,
R
is not analytically unramified. Let
J=IR
and
{\mathfrak{b}}={\mathfrak{a}}R
, by Proposition
3.3

(5)
,
\mathcal{C}^{J}({\mathfrak{b}})=\mathcal{C}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}).
Note that
x\in\overline{{\mathfrak{b}}^{i}}
for all
i\geq 0
. We claim that
\mathcal{R}(\overline{{\mathfrak{b}}^{\bullet}})
is not a finitely generated
\mathcal{R}({\mathfrak{b}}^{\bullet})
-module. For, if
\mathcal{R}(\overline{{\mathfrak{b}}^{\bullet}})
is a finitely generated
\mathcal{R}({\mathfrak{b}}^{\bullet})
-module, then there exists a positive integer
k
such that
\overline{{\mathfrak{b}}^{n+k}}\subseteq{\mathfrak{b}}^{n}
for all
n
. This implies that
x\in{\mathfrak{b}}^{n}
for all
n
which is a contradiction. Thus, the claim follows. Next, observe that
\overline{{\mathfrak{b}}^{n}}\not\subseteq J^{[p^{e}]}
for all
n,e
. Consequently,
\nu_{\overline{{\mathfrak{b}}^{\bullet}}}^{J}(p^{e})=\infty
for all
e\geq 0
. Hence,
\mathcal{C}^{J}_{\pm}(\overline{{\mathfrak{b}}^{\bullet}})=\infty.

Next, we provide a necessary condition for
\mathcal{C}_{+}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})
to be a non-negative real number.

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
be a filtration. Then
\mathcal{C}_{+}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})<\infty
if and only if there exists a positive integer
M
such that
{\mathfrak{a}}_{Mp^{e}}\subseteq I^{[p^{e}]}
for all
e\gg 0
. Moreover, in this case, we have
\sqrt{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}}\subseteq\sqrt{I}
.

Proof.

Note that
\mathcal{C}_{+}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})<\infty
if and only if there exists an integer
M>0
such that
\sup_{f\geq e}\frac{\nu_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{f})}{p^{f}}<M
for all
e\gg 0
, which happens if and only if
\nu_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e})<Mp^{e}
for all
e\gg 0
. Thus,
\mathcal{C}_{+}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})<\infty
if and only if
{\mathfrak{a}}_{Mp^{e}}\subseteq I^{[p^{e}]}
for all
e\gg 0
. Since
\sqrt{{\mathfrak{a}}_{Mp^{e}}}\subseteq\sqrt{I}
for all
e\gg 0
and
\sqrt{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}}=\sqrt{{\mathfrak{a}}_{Mp^{e}}}
, the second assertion follows.
∎

It is natural to ask whether
\sqrt{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}}\subseteq\sqrt{I}
is a sufficient condition for
\mathcal{C}_{+}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})
to be a non-negative real number. Here, we provide an example which shows that the condition
\sqrt{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}}\subseteq\sqrt{I}
is not sufficient for
\mathcal{C}_{+}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})
to be a non-negative real number.

Example 3.12
.

Let
S
be a Noetherian domain of prime characteristic
p
and
I
be a non-zero proper ideal of
S
. Take
R=S[x]
and
{\mathfrak{a}}_{n}=Ix^{n}
for every
n
. Clearly,
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}
is a filtration and
\sqrt{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}}=\sqrt{Ix}\subseteq\sqrt{IR}.
However, for every
e,r\geq 1
,
{\mathfrak{a}}_{r}\not\subseteq(IR)^{[p^{e}]}.
Therefore,
\mathcal{C}_{\pm}^{IR}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})=\infty.

In the following, we provide a sufficient condition on a filtration of ideals so that
\mathcal{C}^{I}_{+}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})<\infty.

Theorem 3.13
.

Let
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}
be a filtration such that for some
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
{{\mathfrak{a}}_{Ns}}\subseteq{I}^{s}
for all
s\geq 0
. Then
0\leq\mathcal{C}^{I}_{\pm}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})\leq N\mu(I)
, where
\mu(I)
denotes the number of elements in a minimal generating set of
I
. Particularly, if
R
is F-pure, then
\mathcal{C}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})
exists and

0\leq\mathcal{C}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})=\sup_{e\geq 0}\frac{\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}\leq N\mu(I).

Proof.

First, we claim that
\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e})\leq N\mu(I)(p^{e}-1)+N-1
for all
e\geq 0
. For every
e\geq 0
, if
s\geq N\mu(I)(p^{e}-1)+N
, then

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{a}}_{s}

\displaystyle\subseteq{\mathfrak{a}}_{N\mu(I)(p^{e}-1)+N}\subseteq I^{\mu(I)(p^{e}-1)+1}\subseteq I^{[p^{e}]},

where the last containment follows from Pigeonhole Principle (
[
20
, Lemma 2.4(1)]
). Therefore,
\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e})\leq N\mu(I)(p^{e}-1)+N-1
which implies that

\frac{\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}\leq\frac{N\mu(I)(p^{e}-1)+N-1}{p^{e}}.

Now, applying
\limsup
and
\liminf
we get that
0\leq\mathcal{C}^{I}_{\pm}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})\leq N\mu(I).

Now, if
R
is an
F
-pure ring,
I^{[p^{e}]}=(I^{[p^{e}]})^{F}
for all
e\geq 0
. By Proposition
3.3

(1)
,

{\mathcal{C}_{\pm}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})=\lim_{e\to\infty}\frac{\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}=\sup_{e\geq 0}\frac{\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}.}

Since
\left\{\frac{\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}\right\}
is a bounded sequence,
\mathcal{C}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})
exists, and hence, the assertion follows.
∎

We now illustrate the above result with the following example.

Example 3.14
.

Let
R=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
and
\mathfrak{m}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
, where
\mathbb{K}
is a field of prime characteristic
p>0
. Let
{\mathfrak{a}}
and
I
be non-zero proper homogeneous ideals of
R
such that
{\mathfrak{a}}\subseteq I
and
k
be a positive integer. Take
{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}={\mathfrak{a}}^{\lceil\frac{i}{k}\rceil+1}
for
i\geq 1
. It is easy to see that
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}
is a filtration of ideals in
R
. Note that
{\mathfrak{a}}_{ki}={\mathfrak{a}}^{i+1}\subseteq I^{i}
for all
i
. Thus, by Theorem
3.13
,
\mathcal{C}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})
exists. One can also observe that
{\mathcal{R}}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})
is not finitely generated
R
-algebra, see
[
14
]
.

Next we prove that if
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}
is a Noetherian filtration, then
\sqrt{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}}\subseteq\sqrt{I}
is sufficient for
\mathcal{C}_{\pm}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})
to be non-negative real number as well as for the existence of
\mathcal{C}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})
.

Theorem 3.15
.

If
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}
is a Noetherian filtration with
\sqrt{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}}\subseteq\sqrt{I}
, then
0\leq\mathcal{C}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})<\infty
, and it is equal to
r\mathcal{C}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}_{r}^{\bullet})
for some
r\in\mathbb{N}
. Moreover, if
R
is regular
F
-finite ring and essentially of finite type over a field, then
\mathcal{C}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})
is rational.

Proof.

Since
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}
is a Noetherian filtration, by
[
36
, Proposition 2.1]
, there exists a positive integer
r
such that
{\mathcal{R}}^{[r]}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})
is standard graded
R
-algebra, i.e.,
{\mathfrak{a}}_{nr}={\mathfrak{a}}_{r}^{n}
for all
n\geq 0
. Note that
{\mathfrak{a}}_{r}\subseteq\sqrt{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}}\subseteq\sqrt{I}
. So, there exists a positive integer
N
such that
{\mathfrak{a}}_{r}^{N}\subseteq I.
This implies that
{\mathfrak{a}}_{rNs}={\mathfrak{a}}_{r}^{Ns}\subseteq I^{s}
for all
s\geq 1
. By Theorem
3.13
,
0\leq\mathcal{C}^{I}_{\pm}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})<\infty
. Consequently,
\nu_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e})<\infty
for all
e\geq 0
. Next, we claim that for all
e\geq 0
,

r\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}_{r}^{\bullet}}}^{I}(p^{e})\leq\nu_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e})\leq r(\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}_{r}^{\bullet}}}^{I}(p^{e})+1)-1.

Let
e\geq 0
be any. Suppose that
{\mathfrak{a}}_{r}^{n}\not\subseteq I^{[p^{e}]}
for some
n\geq 0
, then
{\mathfrak{a}}_{rn}={\mathfrak{a}}_{r}^{n}\not\subseteq I^{[p^{e}]}
which implies that
r\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}_{r}^{\bullet}}}^{I}(p^{e})\leq\nu_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e})
. For
s=\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}_{r}^{\bullet}}}^{I}(p^{e})+1
, we have
{\mathfrak{a}}_{r}^{s}\subseteq I^{[p^{e}]}
which implies that
{\mathfrak{a}}_{rs}\subseteq I^{[p^{e}]}
. Thus,
\nu_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e})\leq r(\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}_{r}^{\bullet}}}^{I}(p^{e})+1)-1
, and hence, the claim follows. Consequently, we get

r\frac{\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}_{r}^{\bullet}}}^{I}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}\leq\frac{\nu_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}\leq r\frac{\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}_{r}^{\bullet}}}^{I}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}+\frac{r-1}{p^{e}},

for
e\geq 0
. By
[
8
, Theorem 3.4]
, we know that
\mathcal{C}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}_{r}^{\bullet})
exists. Now, by applying Sandwich theorem in the above inequality, we get

\lim_{e\to\infty}\frac{\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}}^{I}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}=r\lim_{e\to\infty}\frac{\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}_{r}^{\bullet}}}^{I}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}=r\mathcal{C}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}_{r}^{\bullet}).

Hence,
\mathcal{C}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})
exists and it is equal to
r\mathcal{C}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}_{r}^{\bullet})
. The rationality of
\mathcal{C}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})
follows from
[
3
, Theorem 3.1]
.
∎

Example 3.16
.

Let
{\mathfrak{a}},{\mathfrak{b}}
be non-zero proper ideals of
R
such that
{\mathfrak{a}}^{2}\subsetneq{\mathfrak{b}}\subsetneq{\mathfrak{a}}
and
{\mathfrak{b}}\subseteq\sqrt{I}.
By
[
8
, Theorem 3.4]
,
{\mathcal{C}}^{I}({\mathfrak{b}}^{\bullet})
exists. Now, take
{\mathfrak{a}}_{0}=R,{\mathfrak{a}}_{1}={\mathfrak{a}},{\mathfrak{a}}_{2}={\mathfrak{b}}
,
{\mathfrak{a}}_{2i+1}={\mathfrak{a}}{\mathfrak{b}}^{i}
and
{\mathfrak{a}}_{2i}={\mathfrak{b}}^{i}
. One can verify that
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}
is a Noetherian filtration of ideals in
R
with
{\mathfrak{a}}_{2i}={\mathfrak{b}}^{i}={\mathfrak{a}}_{2}^{i}
for all
i
. Therefore, by Theorem
3.15
,
\mathcal{C}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})
exists and it is equal to
2\mathcal{C}^{I}({\mathfrak{b}}^{\bullet})
.

The Rees algebra of a filtration of ideals in general is not necessarily Noetherian, see Example
3.14
or even when the filtration is of symbolic powers of an ideal in
R
(cf.
[
6
,
22
,
35
]
). So, next, we prove that symbolic
F
-threshold of a non-zero ideal
{\mathfrak{a}}
with respect to
I
exists, i.e.,
\mathcal{C}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}^{(\bullet)})
is a non-negative real number if
{\mathfrak{a}}\subseteq\sqrt{I}.
Recall that the
big-height
of an ideal
I
, denoted by
\text{big-height}(I)
, is
\max\{\hgt(\mathfrak{p}):\mathfrak{p}\in\Ass(I)\}
, where
\Ass(I)
denotes the set of associated primes of
I
.

Theorem 3.17
.

Assume that
R
is a regular ring. Let
{\mathfrak{a}}
and
I
be a non-zero proper ideals in
R
with
{\mathfrak{a}}\subseteq\sqrt{I}
. Then
\mathcal{C}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}^{(\bullet)})
exists, where
{\mathfrak{a}}^{(\bullet)}=\{{\mathfrak{a}}^{(i)}\}_{i\geq 0}
is the symbolic power filtration of
{\mathfrak{a}}
.

Proof.

First, note that
{\mathfrak{a}}^{(\bullet)}\leq\sqrt{{\mathfrak{a}}}^{(\bullet)}
. Since
R
is a regular ring,
I^{[p^{e}]}=(I^{[p^{e}]})^{F}
for all
e\geq 0
.
By Proposition
3.3
and Theorem
3.5
,

{\mathcal{C}_{\pm}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}^{(\bullet)})=\sup_{e\geq 0}\frac{\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}}^{(\bullet)}}^{I}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}\leq\sup_{e\geq 0}\frac{\nu_{\sqrt{{\mathfrak{a}}}^{(\bullet)}}^{I}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}=\mathcal{C}_{\pm}^{I}(\sqrt{{\mathfrak{a}}}^{(\bullet)}).}

So, it is enough to prove the assertion for radical ideals. So without loss of generality we may assume that
{\mathfrak{a}}
is a radical ideal. By
[
20
, Theorem 2.6]
,
{\mathfrak{a}}^{(Hs)}\subseteq{\mathfrak{a}}^{s}
for all
s
, where
H=\text{big-height}({\mathfrak{a}})
. Since
{\mathfrak{a}}\subseteq\sqrt{I}
, there exists a positive integer
t
such that
{\mathfrak{a}}^{t}\subseteq I
. Now, for
N=Ht
,
{\mathfrak{a}}^{(Ns)}\subset{\mathfrak{a}}^{ts}\subseteq I^{s}
for all
s.
Thus, by Theorem
3.13
,
\mathcal{C}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}^{(\bullet)})
exists. Hence, the assertion follows.
∎

Since
{\mathfrak{a}}^{\bullet}\leq{\mathfrak{a}}^{(\bullet)}
, we always have
\mathcal{C}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}^{\bullet})\leq\mathcal{C}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}^{(\bullet)})
.
In the following example, we illustrate that in general,
F
-threshold of symbolic filtration need not be equal to
F
-threshold of ordinary power filtration.

Example 3.18
.

Let
R=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
and
\mathfrak{m}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
, where
\mathbb{K}
is a field of prime characteristic
p>0
. Let
{\mathfrak{a}}=\bigcap_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(x_{i},x_{j})=\left(\frac{x_{1}\cdots x_{n}}{x_{i}}:1\leq i\leq n\right)
. Observe that
{\mathfrak{a}}^{(n)}=\bigcap_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(x_{i},x_{j})^{n}
for all
n.
We claim that
{\mathfrak{a}}^{(r)}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{[p^{e}]}
if and only if
r\geq 2(p^{e}-1)+1.
If
r\geq 2(p^{e}-1)+1
, then
(x_{i},x_{j})^{r}\subseteq(x_{i},x_{j})^{2(p^{e}-1)+1}\subseteq(x_{i},x_{j})^{[p^{e}]}
where the last inclusion follows from Pigeonhole Principle (cf.
[
20
, Lemma 2.4 (1)]
). Consequently,
{\mathfrak{a}}^{(r)}=\bigcap_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(x_{i},x_{j})^{r}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{[p^{e}]}.
One can note that
(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})^{p^{e}-1}\in\bigcap_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(x_{i},x_{j})^{2(p^{e}-1)}={\mathfrak{a}}^{(2(p^{e}-1))}
and that
(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})^{p^{e}-1}\not\in\mathfrak{m}^{[p^{e}]}.
Thus, the claim follows. Consequently, we get
\nu_{\mathfrak{a}^{(\bullet)}}^{\mathfrak{m}}(p^{e})=2(p^{e}-1)
for all
e
.
Hence,

\mathcal{C}^{\mathfrak{m}}({\mathfrak{a}}^{(\bullet)})=\lim_{e\to\infty}\frac{\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}^{(\bullet)}}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}=\lim_{e\to\infty}\frac{2(p^{e}-1)}{p^{e}}=2.

Next, we claim that
{\mathcal{C}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}({\mathfrak{a}}^{\bullet})=\frac{n}{n-1}.
First, note that
(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})^{n-1}\in{\mathfrak{a}}^{n}
, and therefore, for all
e
,
\left((x_{1}\cdots x_{n})^{n-1}\right)^{\lfloor\frac{p^{e}-1}{n-1}\rfloor}\in{\mathfrak{a}}^{n\lfloor\frac{p^{e}-1}{n-1}\rfloor}
. Since
p^{e}-1\geq(n-1)\lfloor\frac{p^{e}-1}{n-1}\rfloor
, we get that for all
e
,
n\lfloor\frac{p^{e}-1}{n-1}\rfloor\leq\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}}^{\bullet}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}(p^{e}).
Next, we claim that if
r\geq n\left(\frac{p^{e}-1}{n-1}\right)+1
, then
{\mathfrak{a}}^{r}\subseteq{\mathfrak{m}}^{[p^{e}]}
. Note that for any non-negative integers
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}
with
\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{n}=r
,
\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{x_{1}\cdots x_{n}}{x_{i}}\right)^{\alpha_{i}}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{r-\alpha_{i}}.
If
r-\alpha_{i}\leq p^{e}-1
for all
i
, then
nr-r\leq n(p^{e}-1)
which is a contradiction to the fact that
r\geq n\left(\frac{p^{e}-1}{n-1}\right)+1
. Thus, the second claim follows, and hence,
n\lfloor\frac{p^{e}-1}{n-1}\rfloor\leq\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}}^{\bullet}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}(p^{e})\leq n\left(\frac{p^{e}-1}{n-1}\right)+1
for all
e
. Hence, it follows immediately from Sandwich Theorem of Limits that

\mathcal{C}^{\mathfrak{m}}({\mathfrak{a}}^{\bullet})=\lim_{e\to\infty}\frac{\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}^{\bullet}}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}=\frac{n}{n-1}.

4.
(I,p)
-admissible filtrations and their
F
-thresholds

In this section, we introduce a class of filtrations which we call as
(I,p)
-admissible filtrations and study their
F
-thresholds. We show that the ordinary power filtration, the symbolic power filtration and the integral closure power filtration of an ideal are standard examples of
(I,p)
-admissible filtrations. Moreover, we show that
(I,p)
-admissible filtration are closed under products, intersections (for regular rings) and binomial sums. We begin with the definition of
(I,p)
-admissible filtration.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
R
be a ring of prime characteristic
p>0
. Let
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}
be a filtration and
I
be an ideal in
R
. We say that
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
is an
(I,p)
-admissible filtration
, if

(1)

for some
k
,
\mathfrak{a}_{k}\subset I
, and

(2)

there exist integers
h>0
and
c
such that

\mathfrak{a}_{(h+m)p^{e}+c}\subseteq\mathfrak{a}_{m+1}^{[p^{e}]}\text{ for all }m,e\geq 0.

Next, we see that for a non-zero ideal
{\mathfrak{a}}
with
{\mathfrak{a}}\subset\sqrt{I}
, the standard filtrations associated with
{\mathfrak{a}}
are
(I,p)
-admissible filtrations when
R
or
{\mathfrak{a}}
satisfy some nice properties.

Example 4.2
.

Let
{\mathfrak{a}},I
be proper non-zero ideals such that
{\mathfrak{a}}\subseteq\sqrt{I}
. Let
{\mathfrak{a}}
be minimally generated by
f_{1},\ldots,f_{h}
and let
H
be the big height of
{\mathfrak{a}}
.

(a) It follows from
[
20
, Lemma 2.4]
that
{\mathfrak{a}}^{(h+m)p^{e}}\subseteq({\mathfrak{a}}^{m+1})^{[p^{e}]}
for all
m,e\geq 0.
Therefore,
\mathfrak{a}^{\bullet}
is an
(I,p)
-admissible filtration.

(b) Assume that
R
is a regular ring and
{\mathfrak{a}}
is a radical ideal. By
[
20
, Theorem 2.6]
,
{\mathfrak{a}}^{(Hs)}\subseteq{\mathfrak{a}}^{s}
for all
s
. Since
{\mathfrak{a}}\subseteq\sqrt{I}
, there exists a positive integer
k
such that
{\mathfrak{a}}^{k}\subseteq I
. Therefore,
{\mathfrak{a}}^{(Hk)}\subseteq I
. Now, by
[
11
, Lemma 2.6]
, for all
m,e\geq 0
,

{\mathfrak{a}}^{((H+m)p^{e}-H+1)}\subseteq\left({\mathfrak{a}}^{(m+1)}\right)^{[p^{e}]}.

Thus
{\mathfrak{a}}^{(\bullet)}
is an
(I,p)
-admissible filtration.

(d) Assume that
{\mathfrak{a}}
satisfies Briançon-Skoda condition, i.e., there exists a positive integer
k
such that
\overline{{\mathfrak{a}}^{k+m}}\subseteq{\mathfrak{a}}^{m}
for all
m\geq 0
(for example every ideal in reduced
F
-finite ring of positive characteristic
p
satisfy Briançon-Skoda condition, see
[
24
, Theorem 13.4.8]
). Then, for all
m,e\geq 0
, using
[
20
, Lemma 2.4]
, we get
\overline{{\mathfrak{a}}^{(k+h+m)p^{e}}}\subseteq\overline{{\mathfrak{a}}^{(h+m)p^{e}+k}}\subseteq{\mathfrak{a}}^{(h+m)p^{e}}\subseteq({\mathfrak{a}}^{m+1})^{[p^{e}]}\subseteq(\overline{{\mathfrak{a}}^{m+1}})^{[p^{e}]}
. Therefore,
\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{\bullet}}
is an
(I,p)
-admissible filtration.

(e) Let
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}
be a filtration of ideals in
R
such that
I^{\bullet}\leq{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}
and there exists a positive integer
N
such that
{{\mathfrak{a}}_{(N+s)p^{e}}}\subseteq({I}^{s+1})^{[p^{e}]}
for all
s,e\geq 0
. Then,
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}
is an
(I,p)
-admissible filtration.

Next, we show that one can construct new
(I,p)
-admissible filtrations using given
(I,p)
-admissible filtrations. One can see that if
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}
and
{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet}
are two filtrations then

(1)

{\mathfrak{c}}_{\bullet}=\{{\mathfrak{a}}_{n}{\mathfrak{b}}_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
is a filtration;

(2)

{\mathfrak{d}}_{\bullet}=\{{\mathfrak{a}}_{n}\cap{\mathfrak{b}}_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
is a filtration;

(3)

{\mathfrak{e}}_{\bullet}=\{\sum_{i=0}^{n}{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}{\mathfrak{b}}_{n-i}\}_{n\geq 0}
is a filtration.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}
and
{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet}
be two
(I,p)
-admissible filtrations. Then,

(1)

{\mathfrak{c}}_{\bullet}=\{{\mathfrak{a}}_{n}{\mathfrak{b}}_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
is an
(I,p)
-admissible filtration;

(2)

if
R
is regular, then
{\mathfrak{d}}_{\bullet}=\{{\mathfrak{a}}_{n}\cap{\mathfrak{b}}_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
is an
(I,p)
-admissible filtration;

(3)

{\mathfrak{e}}_{\bullet}=\{\sum_{i=0}^{n}{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}{\mathfrak{b}}_{n-i}\}_{n\geq 0}
is an
(I,p)
-admissible filtration.

Proof.

Since
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}
and
{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet}
are
(I,p)
-admissible filtrations, there exist integers
k,k^{\prime},h,h^{\prime},c,c^{\prime}
such that
\mathfrak{a}_{k}\subset I
,
{\mathfrak{b}}_{k^{\prime}}\subseteq I
, and for all
m,e\geq 0
,

\mathfrak{a}_{(h+m)p^{e}+c}\subseteq\mathfrak{a}_{m+1}^{[p^{e}]}\text{ and }\mathfrak{b}_{(h^{\prime}+m)p^{e}+c^{\prime}}\subseteq\mathfrak{b}_{m+1}^{[p^{e}]}.

(1-2) Note that
{\mathfrak{c}}_{k}\subseteq{\mathfrak{d}}_{k}\subseteq{\mathfrak{a}}_{k}\subseteq I
. Let
l=\max\{h,h^{\prime}\},n=\max\{c,c^{\prime}\}.
Then, for all
m,e\geq 0
,

\mathfrak{c}_{(l+m)p^{e}+n}=\mathfrak{a}_{(l+m)p^{e}+n}\mathfrak{b}_{(l+m)p^{e}+n}\subseteq\mathfrak{a}_{(h+m)p^{e}+c}\mathfrak{b}_{(h^{\prime}+m)p^{e}+c^{\prime}}\subseteq\mathfrak{a}_{m+1}^{[p^{e}]}\mathfrak{b}_{m+1}^{[p^{e}]}={\mathfrak{c}}_{m+1}^{[p^{e}]}

and

\mathfrak{d}_{(l+m)p^{e}+n}=\mathfrak{a}_{(l+m)p^{e}+n}\cap\mathfrak{b}_{(l+m)p^{e}+n}\subseteq\mathfrak{a}_{(h+m)p^{e}+c}\cap\mathfrak{b}_{(h^{\prime}+m)p^{e}+c^{\prime}}\subseteq\mathfrak{a}_{m+1}^{[p^{e}]}\cap\mathfrak{b}_{m+1}^{[p^{e}]}={\mathfrak{d}}_{m+1}^{[p^{e}]},

where the last equality follows from the flatness of the Frobenius map on
R
. Thus,
{\mathfrak{c}}_{\bullet}
and
{\mathfrak{d}}_{\bullet}
are
(I,p)
-admissible filtrations.

(3) Let
k^{\prime\prime}=k+k^{\prime}
. Then, for every
0\leq i\leq k^{\prime\prime}
, either
i\geq k
or
k^{\prime\prime}-i\geq k^{\prime}
. This implies that either
{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}\subseteq{\mathfrak{a}}_{k}\subseteq I
or
{\mathfrak{b}}_{k^{\prime\prime}-i}\subseteq{\mathfrak{b}}_{k^{\prime}}\subseteq I
which further implies that
{\mathfrak{d}}_{k^{\prime\prime}}\subseteq I.
Next, let
l=h+h^{\prime},n=c+c^{\prime}
and
m,e\geq 0
be any. Consider,

{\mathfrak{d}}_{(l+m)p^{e}+n}=\sum_{i=0}^{(l+m)p^{e}+n}{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}{\mathfrak{b}}_{(l+m)p^{e}+n-i}.

Suppose that
0\leq i<hp^{e}+c
. Then,
(l+m)p^{e}+n-i\geq(h^{\prime}+m)p^{e}+c^{\prime}
which implies that
{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}{\mathfrak{b}}_{(l+m)p^{e}+n-i}\subseteq{\mathfrak{b}}_{(h^{\prime}+m)p^{e}+c^{\prime}}\subseteq{\mathfrak{b}}_{m+1}^{[p^{e}]}.
Now, for
0\leq j<m
, if
(h+j)p^{e}+c\leq i<(h+j+1)p^{e}+c
, then
(l+m)p^{e}+n-i\geq(h^{\prime}+m-j-1)p^{e}+c^{\prime}
. Therefore,
{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}\subseteq{\mathfrak{a}}_{(h+j)p^{e}+c}\subseteq{\mathfrak{a}}_{j+1}^{[p^{e}]}
and
{\mathfrak{b}}_{(l+m)p^{e}+n-i}\subseteq{\mathfrak{b}}_{(h^{\prime}+m-j-1)p^{e}+c^{\prime}}\subseteq{\mathfrak{b}}_{m-j}^{[p^{e}]}
which implies that
{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}{\mathfrak{b}}_{(l+m)p^{e}+n-i}\subseteq{\mathfrak{a}}_{j+1}^{[p^{e}]}{\mathfrak{b}}_{m-j}^{[p^{e}]}=({\mathfrak{a}}_{j+1}{\mathfrak{b}}_{m-j})^{[p^{e}]}.
Now, for
i\geq(h+m)p^{e}+c
, we have
{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}\subseteq{\mathfrak{a}}_{(h+m)p^{e}+c}\subseteq{\mathfrak{a}}_{m+1}^{[p^{e}]}.
Therefore,

{\mathfrak{d}}_{(l+m)p^{e}+n}=\sum_{i=0}^{(l+m)p^{e}+n}{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}{\mathfrak{b}}_{(l+m)p^{e}+n-i}\subseteq\sum_{j=0}^{m+1}({\mathfrak{a}}_{j}{\mathfrak{b}}_{m+1-j})^{[p^{e}]}={\mathfrak{d}}_{m+1}^{[p^{e}]}.

Thus,
{\mathfrak{d}}_{\bullet}
is an
(I,p)
-admissible filtration.
∎

Now, we prove that the
F
-threshold of an
(I,p)
-admissible filtration exists. The strategy of the proof is the same as the proof of
[
8
, Theorem 3.4]
.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
be an
(I,p)
-admissible filtration. Then

{\mathcal{C}}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})=\lim\limits_{e\to\infty}\frac{\nu^{I}_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}
exists.

Proof.

Since
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}
is an
(I,p)
-admissible filtration, there exist integers
k,c
and
h>0
such that
{\mathfrak{a}}_{k}\subseteq I
and
\mathfrak{a}_{(h+m)p^{e}+c}\subseteq\mathfrak{a}_{m+1}^{[p^{e}]}\text{ for all }m,e\geq 0.
Therefore,
\mathfrak{a}_{(h+k-1)p^{e}+c}\subseteq\mathfrak{a}_{k}^{[p^{e}]}\subseteq I^{[p^{e}]}
which implies that
0\leq{\nu^{I}_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}(p^{e})}\leq(h+k-1)p^{e}+c-1\text{ for all }e\geq 0.
Thus for all large
q=p^{e}\gg 0
,
0\leq\frac{\nu^{I}_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}\leq h+k
, i.e.
\{\frac{\nu^{I}_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}\}
is an eventually bounded sequence.
Now, it suffices to prove that

\limsup\limits_{e\to\infty}\frac{\nu^{I}_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}\leq\liminf\limits_{e\to\infty}\frac{\nu^{I}_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}.

Let
e,e^{\prime}\geq 0
and
m=\nu^{I}_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}(p^{e})
. Then

\mathfrak{a}_{(h+m)p^{e^{\prime}}+c}\subseteq\mathfrak{a}_{m+1}^{[p^{e^{\prime}}]}\subseteq{(I^{[p^{e}]})}^{[p^{e^{\prime}}]}=I^{[p^{e+e^{\prime}}]}.

Therefore,
\nu^{I}_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}(p^{e+e^{\prime}})<(h+m)p^{e^{\prime}}+c
which implies that

\frac{\nu^{I}_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}(p^{e+e^{\prime}})}{p^{e+e^{\prime}}}<\frac{h}{p^{e}}+\frac{\nu^{I}_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}+\frac{c}{p^{e+e^{\prime}}}.

Now, for each
e\geq 1
, we get

\limsup\limits_{e^{\prime}\to\infty}\frac{\nu^{I}_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}(p^{e+e^{\prime}})}{p^{e+e^{\prime}}}\leq\frac{h}{p^{e}}+\frac{\nu^{I}_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}.

Therefore,

{\mathcal{C}}_{+}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})=\limsup\limits_{e^{\prime}\to\infty}\frac{\nu^{I}_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}(p^{e+e^{\prime}})}{p^{e+e^{\prime}}}\leq\frac{h}{p^{e}}+\frac{\nu^{I}_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}.

Now, applying
\liminf
as
e\to\infty
, we get

{\mathcal{C}}_{+}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})\leq\liminf\limits_{e\to\infty}\left(\frac{h}{p^{e}}+\frac{\nu^{I}_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}\right)=\liminf\limits_{e\to\infty}\frac{\nu^{I}_{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}

which implies that

{\mathcal{C}}_{+}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})\leq{\mathcal{C}}_{-}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}).
Hence,
{\mathcal{C}}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})
exists.
∎

Now, we study
F
-thresholds of filtrations
{\mathfrak{c}}_{\bullet},{\mathfrak{d}}_{\bullet}
and
{\mathfrak{e}}_{\bullet}
in terms of
F
-thresholds of filtrations
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}
and
{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet}
.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}
and
{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet}
be two filtrations. Let
{\mathfrak{c}}_{\bullet}=\{{\mathfrak{a}}_{n}{\mathfrak{b}}_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
,
{\mathfrak{d}}_{\bullet}=\{{\mathfrak{a}}_{n}\cap{\mathfrak{b}}_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
and
{\mathfrak{e}}_{\bullet}=\{\sum_{i=0}^{n}{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}{\mathfrak{b}}_{n-i}\}_{n\geq 0}
. Then,

\mathcal{C}_{\pm}^{I}({\mathfrak{c}}_{\bullet})\leq\mathcal{C}_{\pm}^{I}({\mathfrak{d}}_{\bullet})\leq\mathcal{C}_{\pm}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}),\mathcal{C}_{\pm}^{I}({\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet})\leq\mathcal{C}_{\pm}^{I}({\mathfrak{e}}_{\bullet})\text{ and }\mathcal{C}_{+}^{I}({\mathfrak{e}}_{\bullet})\leq\mathcal{C}_{+}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})+\mathcal{C}_{+}^{I}({\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet}).

Proof.

First, note that
{\mathfrak{c}}_{n}\subseteq{\mathfrak{d}}_{n}\subseteq{\mathfrak{a}}_{n},{\mathfrak{b}}_{n}\subseteq{\mathfrak{e}}_{n}
for all
n
. Therefore,
{\mathfrak{c}}_{\bullet}\leq{\mathfrak{d}}_{\bullet}\leq{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet},{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet}\leq{\mathfrak{e}}_{\bullet}
. Now, applying Theorem
3.5
, we get
\mathcal{C}_{\pm}^{I}({\mathfrak{c}}_{\bullet})\leq\mathcal{C}_{\pm}^{I}({\mathfrak{d}}_{\bullet})\leq\mathcal{C}_{\pm}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}),\mathcal{C}_{\pm}^{I}({\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet})\leq\mathcal{C}_{\pm}^{I}({\mathfrak{e}}_{\bullet})
. So, it remains to prove that
\mathcal{C}_{+}^{I}({\mathfrak{e}}_{\bullet})\leq\mathcal{C}_{+}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})+\mathcal{C}_{+}^{I}({\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet}).

We claim that
\nu_{{\mathfrak{e}}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e})\leq\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e})+\nu_{{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e})
for all
e\geq 0
. If
\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e})+\nu_{{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e})=\infty
, then vacuously the claim follows. So, we assume that
\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e})+\nu_{{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e})<\infty.
Let
k>\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e})+\nu_{{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e}).
Then for every
0\leq i\leq k
, either
i>\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e})
or
k-i>\nu_{{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e}).
Therefore, either
{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}\subseteq I^{[p^{e}]}
or
{\mathfrak{b}}_{k-i}\subseteq I^{[p^{e}]}
which implies that
{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}{\mathfrak{b}}_{k-i}\subseteq I^{[p^{e}]}
for every
0\leq i\leq k
. Consequently,
{\mathfrak{e}}_{k}\subseteq I^{[p^{e}]}.
Thus, the claim follows. Using claim, we get

\frac{\nu_{{\mathfrak{e}_{\bullet}}}^{I}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}\leq\frac{\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}}^{I}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}+\frac{\nu_{{\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}}}^{I}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}

for all
e\geq 0
. Therefore,

\limsup_{e\to\infty}\frac{\nu_{{\mathfrak{e}_{\bullet}}}^{I}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}\leq\limsup_{e\to\infty}\left(\frac{\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}}^{I}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}+\frac{\nu_{{\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}}}^{I}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}\right)\leq\limsup_{e\to\infty}\frac{\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}}}^{I}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}+\limsup_{e\to\infty}\frac{\nu_{{\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}}}^{I}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}.

Hence, the assertion follows.
∎

As an immediate consequence of
Proposition

4.3
,
Theorem

4.4
and
Proposition

4.5
, we get the following:

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}
and
{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet}
be two
(I,p)
-admissible filtrations. Let
{\mathfrak{c}}_{\bullet}=\{{\mathfrak{a}}_{n}{\mathfrak{b}}_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
,
{\mathfrak{d}}_{\bullet}=\{{\mathfrak{a}}_{n}\cap{\mathfrak{b}}_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
and
{\mathfrak{e}}_{\bullet}=\{\sum_{i=0}^{n}{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}{\mathfrak{b}}_{n-i}\}_{n\geq 0}
. Then,

(1)

{\mathcal{C}}^{I}({\mathfrak{c}}_{\bullet})\leq\min\left\{{\mathcal{C}}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}),{\mathcal{C}}^{I}({\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet})\right\}.

(2)

Assume that
R
is regular, then
{\mathcal{C}}^{I}({\mathfrak{c}}_{\bullet})\leq{\mathcal{C}}^{I}({\mathfrak{d}}_{\bullet})\leq\min\left\{{\mathcal{C}}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}),{\mathcal{C}}^{I}({\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet})\right\}.

(3)

\max\left\{{\mathcal{C}}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}),{\mathcal{C}}^{I}({\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet})\right\}\leq{\mathcal{C}}^{I}({\mathfrak{e}}_{\bullet})\leq{\mathcal{C}}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})+{\mathcal{C}}^{I}({\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet}).

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}
be filtration and let
{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet}=\{{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}^{s}\}_{i\geq 0}
. Then,
s\;{\mathcal{C}}^{I}_{\pm}({\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet})\leq{\mathcal{C}}^{I}_{\pm}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}).
Moreover, if
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}={\mathfrak{a}}^{\bullet}
for some non-zero proper ideal
{\mathfrak{a}}\subseteq\sqrt{I},
then
{\mathcal{C}}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}^{\bullet})=s\;{\mathcal{C}}^{I}(({\mathfrak{a}}^{s})^{\bullet}).

Proof.

Observe that
s\nu_{{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e})\leq\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e})
as if
{\mathfrak{b}}_{r}\not\subseteq I^{[p^{e}]}
, then
{\mathfrak{a}}_{r}^{s}\not\subseteq I^{[p^{e}]}
which implies that
{\mathfrak{a}}_{rs}\not\subseteq I^{[p^{e}]}
. Now, using definition of
\mathcal{C}^{I}_{\pm}(\bullet)
, we get
s\;{\mathcal{C}}^{I}_{\pm}({\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet})\leq{\mathcal{C}}^{I}_{\pm}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}).

The second assertion follows from
[
23
, Proposition 2.2 (iii)]
.
∎

The above mentioned results are very useful in the computation of
F
-thresholds of filtrations of monomial ideals as is illustrated below. A filtration
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}
in a polynomial ring is said to be a
monomial filtration
, if for each
i\geq 1
,
{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}
is a monomial ideal. First, we prove an auxiliary lemma:

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
R=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
, where
\mathbb{K}
is a field of prime characteristic
p>0
and
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}
be a non-trivial monomial filtration in
R
and let
I=(x_{1}^{m_{1}},\ldots,x_{n}^{m_{n}})
. Then, for all
e\geq 0
,

\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e})=\sup\left\{r:x_{1}^{p^{e}m_{1}-1}\cdots x_{n}^{p^{e}m_{n}-1}\in{\mathfrak{a}}_{r}\right\}.

Proof.

Let
r
be any positive integer. We claim that
{\mathfrak{a}}_{r}\not\subseteq I^{[p^{e}]}
if and only if
x_{1}^{p^{e}m_{1}-1}\cdots x_{n}^{p^{e}m_{n}-1}\in{\mathfrak{a}}_{r}.
Suppose that
x_{1}^{p^{e}m_{1}-1}\cdots x_{n}^{p^{e}m_{n}-1}\in{\mathfrak{a}}_{r}.
Then,
{\mathfrak{a}}_{r}\not\subseteq I^{[p^{e}]}
as
x_{1}^{p^{e}m_{1}-1}\cdots x_{n}^{p^{e}m_{n}-1}\not\in I^{[p^{e}]}
. Conversely, suppose we have
{\mathfrak{a}}_{r}\not\subseteq I^{[p^{e}]}
for some
r
. Therefore, there exists a monomial
u\in{\mathfrak{a}}_{r}\setminus I^{[p^{e}]}.
Since
u\not\in I^{[p^{e}]}
, for each
1\leq j\leq n
,
u
is not divisible by
x_{j}^{p^{e}m_{j}}.
This implies that if
u
is divisible by
m
-th power of
x_{j}
, then
m\leq p^{e}m_{j}-1.
Therefore,
u
divides
x_{1}^{p^{e}m_{1}-1}\cdots x_{n}^{p^{e}m_{n}-1}
which implies that
x_{1}^{p^{e}m_{1}-1}\cdots x_{n}^{p^{e}m_{n}-1}\in{\mathfrak{a}}_{r}
. This completes the claim. Now, the assertion follows from the definition of
\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}}^{I}(p^{e})
.
∎

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
R=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
, where
\mathbb{K}
is a field of prime characteristic
p>0
and
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet},{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet}
be nontrivial monomial filtrations in
R
. Let
{\mathfrak{d}}_{\bullet}=\{{\mathfrak{a}}_{n}\cap{\mathfrak{b}}_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
. Then,
{\mathcal{C}}_{\pm}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}({\mathfrak{d}}_{\bullet})=\min\{{\mathcal{C}}_{\pm}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}),{\mathcal{C}}_{\pm}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}({\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet})\},
where
{\mathfrak{m}}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}).

Proof.

First, we claim that
\nu_{{\mathfrak{d}}_{\bullet}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}(p^{e})=\min\{\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}(p^{e}),\nu_{{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}(p^{e})\}
for all
e
. Let
r\leq\min\{\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}(p^{e}),\nu_{{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}(p^{e})\}
be any. Then, by
Lemma

4.8
,
x_{1}^{p^{e}-1}\cdots x_{n}^{p^{e}-1}\in{\mathfrak{a}}_{r}\cap{\mathfrak{b}}_{r}
which implies that
x_{1}^{p^{e}-1}\cdots x_{n}^{p^{e}-1}\in{\mathfrak{d}}_{r}
. Thus, applying
Lemma

4.8
, we get
r\leq\nu_{{\mathfrak{d}}_{\bullet}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}(p^{e})
, and hence,
\nu_{{\mathfrak{d}}_{\bullet}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}(p^{e})\geq\min\{\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}(p^{e}),\nu_{{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}(p^{e})\}
. Next, if
\min\{\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}(p^{e}),\nu_{{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}(p^{e})\}=\infty
, then
\nu_{{\mathfrak{d}}_{\bullet}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}(p^{e})=\min\{\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}(p^{e}),\nu_{{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}(p^{e})\}
. So, we assume that
\min\{\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}(p^{e}),\nu_{{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}(p^{e})\}<\infty
. Suppose
r>\min\{\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}(p^{e}),\nu_{{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}(p^{e})\}
, then either
x_{1}^{p^{e}-1}\cdots x_{n}^{p^{e}-1}\not\in{\mathfrak{a}}_{r}
or
x_{1}^{p^{e}-1}\cdots x_{n}^{p^{e}-1}\not\in{\mathfrak{b}}_{r}
. Therefore,
x_{1}^{p^{e}-1}\cdots x_{n}^{p^{e}-1}\not\in{\mathfrak{a}}_{r}\cap{\mathfrak{b}}_{r}
. Thus, by
Lemma

4.8
, the claim follows. Now, by
Proposition

3.3
and applying limit to the sequence
\{\frac{\nu_{{\mathfrak{d}}_{\bullet}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}\}
, we get the desired result.
∎

In the following, we show how useful is
Proposition

4.9
in computation of symbolic
F
-thresholds of monomial ideals.

Example 4.10
.

Let
R=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
, where
\mathbb{K}
is a field of prime characteristic
p>0
and
{\mathfrak{m}}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}).
Let
{\mathfrak{p}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathfrak{p}}_{k}
be monomial primes in
R
and
\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{k}
be positive integers. Let
{\mathfrak{a}}=\cap_{i=1}^{k}{\mathfrak{p}}^{\omega_{i}}
, then,
{\mathfrak{a}}
is a monomial ideal. By
Theorem

3.17
, we know that
{\mathcal{C}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}({\mathfrak{a}}^{(\bullet)})
exists. First note that
{\mathfrak{a}}^{(\bullet)}=\cap_{i=1}^{k}\left({\mathfrak{p}}_{i}^{\omega_{i}}\right)^{(\bullet)}.
Therefore, by
Proposition

4.9
,
{\mathcal{C}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}({\mathfrak{a}}^{(\bullet)})=\min_{1\leq i\leq k}{\mathcal{C}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}(\left({\mathfrak{p}}_{i}^{\omega_{i}}\right)^{(\bullet)}).
So, it is enough to compute
{\mathcal{C}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}(\left({\mathfrak{p}}^{\omega}\right)^{(\bullet)}),
where
{\mathfrak{p}}
is a monomial prime ideal in
R
and
\omega
is a positive integer. Note that
\left({\mathfrak{p}}^{\omega}\right)^{(\bullet)}=\left({\mathfrak{p}}^{\omega}\right)^{\bullet}
. Now, by
Proposition

4.7
,
{\mathcal{C}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}(\left({\mathfrak{p}}^{\omega}\right)^{\bullet})=\frac{{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathfrak{m}}({\mathfrak{p}}^{\bullet})}{\omega}=\frac{\hgt({\mathfrak{p}})}{\omega}
, where the equality
{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathfrak{m}}({\mathfrak{p}}^{\bullet})=\hgt({\mathfrak{p}})
is well known and easy to show. Thus,
{\mathcal{C}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}({\mathfrak{a}}^{(\bullet)})=\min_{1\leq i\leq k}\frac{\hgt({\mathfrak{p}}_{i})}{\omega_{i}}.

Example 4.11
.

Let
G
be a chordal graph on the vertex set
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
,
J(G)
be the cover ideal of
G
in
R=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
, where
\mathbb{K}
is a field of prime characteristic
p>0
and
{\mathfrak{m}}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}).
We claim that
{\mathcal{C}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}(J(G)^{\bullet})=\frac{\omega(G)}{\omega(G)-1}
, where
\omega(G)
is the clique number of
G.
Let
K_{m_{1}},\ldots,K_{m_{k}}
be the maximal cliques of
G
. It is well known fact that
G
can be obtained as clique sums of maximal cliques (cf.
[
9
, Proposition 5.5.1]
). Then, if follows from
[
25
, Theorem 4.9]
,
J(G)^{\bullet}=\cap_{i=1}^{k}J(K_{m_{i}})^{\bullet}.
Thus, by
Proposition

4.9
,
{\mathcal{C}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}(J(G)^{\bullet})=\min_{1\leq i\leq k}{\mathcal{C}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}(J(K_{m_{i}})^{\bullet})=\min_{1\leq i\leq k}\frac{m_{i}}{m_{i}-1}
where the last equality follows from
Example

3.18
. Finally note that
\min_{1\leq i\leq k}\frac{m_{i}}{m_{i}-1}=\frac{\omega(G)}{\omega(G)-1}
.

Next, we show that one of the inequality in
Proposition

4.5
becomes an equality when the filtrations are in polynomial rings in disjoint set of variables over the same field.

Theorem 4.12
.

Let
R_{1}=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
and
R_{2}=\mathbb{K}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}]
, where
\mathbb{K}
is a field of prime characteristic
p>0
and let
R=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}].
Let
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}
and
{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet}
be two filtrations in
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
respectively. Let
I\subseteq R_{1}
and
J\subseteq R_{2}
be non-zero proper ideals. Let
{\mathfrak{c}}_{\bullet}=\{{\mathfrak{a}}_{n}{\mathfrak{b}}_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
and
{\mathfrak{e}}_{\bullet}=\{\sum_{i=0}^{n}{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}{\mathfrak{b}}_{n-i}\}_{n\geq 0}
. Then,

(1)

\mathcal{C}_{\pm}^{I+J}({\mathfrak{e}}_{\bullet})={\mathcal{C}}_{\pm}^{I+J}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})+{\mathcal{C}}_{\pm}^{I+J}({\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet})=\mathcal{C}_{\pm}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})+\mathcal{C}_{\pm}^{J}({\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet});

(2)

\mathcal{C}_{\pm}^{IJ}({\mathfrak{c}}_{\bullet})=\max\{\mathcal{C}_{\pm}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}),\mathcal{C}_{\pm}^{J}({\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet})\}.

Proof.

(1)
First, observe that
{\mathfrak{a}}_{r}\not\subseteq I^{[p^{e}]}
if and only if
{\mathfrak{a}}_{r}\not\subseteq(I+J)^{[p^{e}]}=I^{[p^{e}]}+J^{[p^{e}]}.
Therefore,
\nu^{I+J}_{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}}(p^{e})=\nu^{I}_{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}}(p^{e})
, and hence, by
Proposition

3.3

{\mathcal{C}}_{\pm}^{I+J}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})={\mathcal{C}}_{\pm}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})
. Similarly,
{\mathcal{C}}_{\pm}^{I+J}({\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet})={\mathcal{C}}_{\pm}^{J}({\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet})
. Now, it follows from
Proposition

3.3
and from
Proposition

4.5
that
\mathcal{C}_{\pm}^{I+J}({\mathfrak{e}}_{\bullet})\leq{\mathcal{C}}_{\pm}^{I+J}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})+{\mathcal{C}}_{\pm}^{I+J}({\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet})=\mathcal{C}_{\pm}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})+\mathcal{C}_{\pm}^{J}({\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet}).
Fix a positive integer
e
, take
r\leq\nu^{I}_{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}}(p^{e})
and
s\leq\nu^{J}_{{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet}}(p^{e})
be any. Then,
{\mathfrak{e}}_{r+s}\not\subseteq(I+J)^{[p^{e}]}
as
{\mathfrak{a}}_{r}{\mathfrak{b}}_{s}\subseteq{\mathfrak{e}}_{r+s}
. Therefore,
\nu^{I}_{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}}(p^{e})+\nu^{J}_{{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet}}(p^{e})\leq\nu^{I+J}_{{\mathfrak{e}}_{\bullet}}(p^{e})
for all
e
. Now, by
Proposition

3.3
,
\mathcal{C}_{\pm}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})+\mathcal{C}_{\pm}^{J}({\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet})\leq\mathcal{C}_{\pm}^{I+J}({\mathfrak{e}}_{\bullet}).
Hence,
\mathcal{C}_{\pm}^{I+J}({\mathfrak{e}}_{\bullet})={\mathcal{C}}_{\pm}^{I+J}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})+{\mathcal{C}}_{\pm}^{I+J}({\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet})=\mathcal{C}_{\pm}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})+\mathcal{C}_{\pm}^{J}({\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet}).

(2)

First, note that if
s>\max\{\nu^{I}_{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}}(p^{e}),\nu^{J}_{{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet}}(p^{e})\}
, then
{\mathfrak{a}}_{s}\subseteq I^{[p^{e}]}
and
{\mathfrak{b}}_{s}\subseteq J^{[p^{e}]}
which implies that
{\mathfrak{c}}_{s}\subseteq(IJ)^{[p^{e}]}.
Also, if
r=\max\{\nu^{I}_{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}}(p^{e}),\nu^{J}_{{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet}}(p^{e})\}.
Then,
{\mathfrak{c}}_{r}={\mathfrak{a}}_{r}{\mathfrak{b}}_{r}\not\subseteq I^{[p^{e}]}J^{[p^{e}]}
. Consequently,
\nu^{IJ}_{{\mathfrak{c}}_{\bullet}}(p^{e})=\max\{\nu^{I}_{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}}(p^{e}),\nu^{J}_{{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bullet}}(p^{e})\}
for all
e
. Hence, the assertion follows from
Proposition

3.3
.
∎

5.
F
-thresholds of symbolic power filtrations

In this section, we focus on symbolic
F
-threshold of an ideal and its connection with the notion of symbolic
F
-split ideals, recently introduced in
[
7
]
. We prove that symbolic
F
-threshold of an ideal being big-height of that ideal is a sufficient condition for that ideal to be symbolic
F
-split. Throughout this section, we assume that
R
is a regular ring of prime characteristic
p>0
.

We now obtain an upper bound for symbolic
F
-threshold of an ideal.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
I
and
{\mathfrak{a}}
be non-zero proper ideals of
R
such that
{\mathfrak{a}}\subseteq\sqrt{I}.
Then,

\mathcal{C}^{\mathfrak{p}}({\mathfrak{a}}^{(\bullet)})\leq\mathcal{C}^{\sqrt{I}}({\mathfrak{a}}^{(\bullet)})\leq\mathcal{C}^{\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}}}(\sqrt{{\mathfrak{a}}}^{(\bullet)})\leq\text{big-height}(\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}}),

for any prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
containing
I
. In particular,
\mathcal{C}^{\sqrt{I}}({\mathfrak{a}}^{\bullet})\leq\text{big-height}(\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}}).

Proof.

It follows from Theorem
3.17
that symbolic
F
-threshold of
{\mathfrak{a}}
exists with respect to any ideal whose radical contains
{\mathfrak{a}}
. For any prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
R
that contains
I
, by Proposition
3.3
,
\mathcal{C}^{\mathfrak{p}}({\mathfrak{a}}^{(\bullet)})\leq\mathcal{C}^{\sqrt{I}}({\mathfrak{a}}^{(\bullet)})\leq\mathcal{C}^{\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}}}({{\mathfrak{a}}}^{(\bullet)})
as
\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}}\subseteq\sqrt{I}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
. Note that
{\mathfrak{a}}^{(\bullet)}\leq\sqrt{{\mathfrak{a}}}^{(\bullet)}
. Consequently, by Theorem
3.5
,
\mathcal{C}^{\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}}}({{\mathfrak{a}}}^{(\bullet)})\leq\mathcal{C}^{\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}}}(\sqrt{{\mathfrak{a}}}^{(\bullet)}).
Now, it is enough to prove that if
{\mathfrak{b}}
is a non-zero proper radical ideal of
R
, then
\mathcal{C}^{{\mathfrak{b}}}({\mathfrak{b}}^{(\bullet)})\leq\text{big-height}({\mathfrak{b}}).
Let
H=\text{big-height}({\mathfrak{b}}).
Then, by
[
20
, Lemma 2.4]
,
{\mathfrak{b}}^{(Hp^{e})}\subseteq{\mathfrak{b}}^{[p^{e}]}
for all
e\geq 0
. Therefore,
\nu_{{\mathfrak{b}}^{(\bullet)}}^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(p^{e})\leq Hp^{e}-1
which implies that
\frac{\nu_{{\mathfrak{b}}^{(\bullet)}}^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}<H
for all
e\geq 0
. Consequently,
\mathcal{C}^{{\mathfrak{b}}}({\mathfrak{b}}^{(\bullet)})=\sup_{e\geq 0}\frac{\nu_{{\mathfrak{b}}^{(\bullet)}}^{{\mathfrak{b}}}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}\leq H.
Thus, the assertions follow.
∎

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
I
and
{\mathfrak{a}}
be non-zero proper ideals of
R
such that
\sqrt{{\mathfrak{a}}}\subseteq I.
Then,

\mathcal{C}^{\mathfrak{p}}({\mathfrak{a}}^{(\bullet)})\leq\mathcal{C}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}^{(\bullet)})\leq\mathcal{C}^{I}(\sqrt{{\mathfrak{a}}}^{(\bullet)})\leq\mathcal{C}^{\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}}}(\sqrt{{\mathfrak{a}}}^{(\bullet)})\leq\text{big-height}(\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}}),

for any prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
containing
I
. In particular,
\mathcal{C}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}^{\bullet})\leq\text{big-height}(\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}}).

Proof.

The proof is similar to the proof of Proposition
5.1
.
∎

Setup 5.3
.

Let
(R,{\mathfrak{m}},\mathbb{K})
be a
F
-finite regular ring which is either local or positively graded polynomial ring over
\mathbb{K}
with homogeneous maximal ideal
{\mathfrak{m}}
. In case when
R
is graded, elements and ideals considered are homogeneous.

If we replace
I
by a maximal ideal containing
{\mathfrak{a}}
in Proposition
5.1
, then
\text{ht}({\mathfrak{a}})
serve as an upper bound for symbolic
F
-threshold. We prove this in the following:

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
(R,{\mathfrak{m}},\mathbb{K})
be a regular ring with
5.3
and let
{\mathfrak{a}}
be a non-zero proper ideal of
R
. Then,

\mathcal{C}^{\mathfrak{m}}(\mathfrak{a}^{\bullet})\leq\mathcal{C}^{\mathfrak{m}}(\mathfrak{a}^{({\bullet})})\leq\text{ht}({\mathfrak{a}}).

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a minimal prime of
{\mathfrak{a}}
such that
\text{ht}(\mathfrak{p})=\text{ht}({\mathfrak{a}}).
Note that
{\mathfrak{a}}^{\bullet}\leq{\mathfrak{a}}^{(\bullet)}\leq\mathfrak{p}^{(\bullet)}.
By Proposition
3.3
and Theorem
3.5
,

\mathcal{C}^{\mathfrak{m}}(\mathfrak{a}^{\bullet})\leq\mathcal{C}^{\mathfrak{m}}(\mathfrak{a}^{({\bullet})})\leq\mathcal{C}^{\mathfrak{m}}(\mathfrak{p}^{(\bullet)})\leq\mathcal{C}^{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathfrak{p}^{(\bullet)}).

Now, applying Proposition
5.1
,
\mathcal{C}^{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathfrak{p}^{(\bullet)})\leq\text{ht}(\mathfrak{p}).
Hence the assertion follows.
∎

Now, we give a series of results to show that the upper bound obtain in
Corollary

5.4
is a sharp bound. First, we see some examples:

Example 5.5
.

In
[
7
, Section 6]
, the authors studied various types of determinantal ideals in prime characteristic
p>0
. They proved that if
{\mathfrak{a}}
is an ideal of minors of a generic matrix or an ideal of minors of a symmetric matrix or an ideal of Pfaffians of a skew symmetric matrix or an ideal of minors of a Hankel matrix, then

is symbolic
F
-split for
p\gg 0
, for more details see
[
7

f\in{\mathfrak{a}}^{(\hgt({\mathfrak{a}}))}
and
f^{p-1}\not\in{\mathfrak{m}}^{[p]}
, see proofs in
[
7
, Section 6]
for more details. By the construction of the polynomial
f
in
[
7
]
, it is easy to show that, for every
e>0
,
f^{p^{e}-1}\not\in{\mathfrak{m}}^{[p^{e}]}.
Now, for each

,
f^{p^{e}-1}\in\left({\mathfrak{a}}^{(\hgt({\mathfrak{a}}))}\right)^{p^{e}-1}\subseteq{\mathfrak{a}}^{((p^{e}-1)\hgt({\mathfrak{a}}))}
and

Consequently,
{\mathfrak{a}}^{((p^{e}-1)\hgt({\mathfrak{a}}))}\not\subseteq{\mathfrak{m}}^{[p^{e}]}

\hgt(a)(p^{e}-1)\leq\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}}^{(\bullet)}}^{\mathfrak{m}}(p^{e})
for all

. Now, applying limit
e
tends to infinity to
\frac{\nu_{{{\mathfrak{a}}^{(\bullet)}}}^{\mathfrak{m}}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}
, we get
C^{\mathfrak{m}}({{\mathfrak{a}}^{(\bullet)}})\geq\text{ht}({\mathfrak{a}}).
Thus
from Corollary
5.4
,
C^{\mathfrak{m}}({{\mathfrak{a}}^{(\bullet)}})=\text{ht}({\mathfrak{a}}).

Next we show that

Definition 5.6

[

Let

be a non-zero proper ideal of

-split.

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
(R,{\mathfrak{m}},\mathbb{K})
be a regular ring with
5.3
and let
{\mathfrak{a}}
be a
F
-König ideal in

. Then,

\mathcal{C}^{\mathfrak{m}}(\mathfrak{a}^{\bullet})=\mathcal{C}^{\mathfrak{m}}(\mathfrak{a}^{(\bullet)})=\text{ht}({\mathfrak{a}}).

Proof.

Since
{\mathfrak{a}}
is an
F
-König ideal in
R
with
\text{ht}({\mathfrak{a}})=h
, there exists a regular sequence

in

Note that

Thus,

. Applying limit

\frac{\nu_{{{\mathfrak{a}}^{\bullet}}}^{\mathfrak{m}}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}

Now the rest follows from Corollary
5.4
.
∎

Example 5.11
.

Let
R=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
, where
\mathbb{K}
is a field of prime characteristic
p>0
.

(1)
Assume that
n
is an even integer. Let
{\mathfrak{a}}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})^{\frac{n}{2}}
. Then, it follows from
Example

3.2
that
{\mathcal{C}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}({\mathfrak{a}}^{\bullet})=2
. Observe that
{\mathfrak{a}}^{(i)}={\mathfrak{a}}^{i}
for all
i\geq 0
. Therefore,
{\mathcal{C}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}({\mathfrak{a}}^{(\bullet)})<\hgt({\mathfrak{a}})
if
n\geq 3
.

(2)
Let
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
be positive integers, and let
{\mathfrak{a}}=(x_{1}^{a_{1}},\cdots,x_{n}^{a_{n}})
. We claim that

{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathfrak{m}}({\mathfrak{a}}^{(\bullet)})=\frac{1}{a_{1}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{a_{n}}
. Since
{\mathfrak{m}}
is associated prime of
{\mathfrak{a}}
, by
[
12
, Lemma 2.2]
,
{\mathfrak{a}}^{(i)}={\mathfrak{a}}^{i}
for all
i
. By Lemma
4.8
,
\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}}^{(\bullet)}}^{\mathfrak{m}}(p^{e})=\max\{r:x_{1}^{p^{e}-1}\cdots x_{n}^{p^{e}-1}\in{\mathfrak{a}}^{r}\}.
Let
r
be a maximal positive integer such that
x_{1}^{p^{e}-1}\cdots x_{n}^{p^{e}-1}\in{\mathfrak{a}}^{r}
. Then, there exist non-negative integers
r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}=r
and
x_{1}^{a_{1}r_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{a_{n}r_{n}}
divides
x_{1}^{p^{e}-1}\cdots x_{n}^{p^{e}-1}
. Therefore,
a_{i}r_{i}\leq p^{e}-1
for all
1\leq i\leq n
which implies that
r=\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}\leq(p^{e}-1)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{a_{i}}
. Thus,
\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}}^{(\bullet)}}^{\mathfrak{m}}(p^{e})=\lfloor(p^{e}-1)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{a_{i}}\rfloor
for all

. Consequently,
{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathfrak{m}}({\mathfrak{a}}^{(\bullet)})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{a_{i}}
. Now, if
a_{i}\geq 2
for some

, then
{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathfrak{m}}({\mathfrak{a}}^{(\bullet)})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{a_{i}}<\text{ht}({\mathfrak{a}})
.

(3)
Let
{\mathfrak{a}}
be an ideal as in part
(2)
with
a_{i}\geq n+1
for all
i
, and let
{\mathfrak{b}}={\mathfrak{a}}+(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})
. We claim that
{\mathcal{C}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}({\mathfrak{b}}^{(\bullet)})=1.
Since
{\mathfrak{m}}
is associated prime of
{\mathfrak{b}}
, by
[
12
, Lemma 2.2]
,
{\mathfrak{b}}^{(i)}={\mathfrak{b}}^{i}
for all
i
. Since
{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}\geq n+1
for all
i
,
(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})^{r}
is element of minimum degree in
{\mathfrak{b}}^{r}
. Let
r
be a maximal positive integer such that
x_{1}^{p^{e}-1}\cdots x_{n}^{p^{e}-1}\in{\mathfrak{b}}^{r}
. Then, there exist non-negative integers
r_{1},\ldots,r_{n},r_{n+1}
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}r_{i}=r
and
x_{1}^{a_{1}r_{1}+r_{n+1}}\cdots x_{n}^{a_{n}r_{n}+r_{n+1}}
divides
x_{1}^{p^{e}-1}\cdots x_{n}^{p^{e}-1}
. Therefore,

for all

which implies that

. Thus,

for all

. Now, the rest follows from the definition.

We now characterize radical ideals whose symbolic
F
-thresholds are
big-height
of that ideals in terms of ideal containment.

Theorem 5.12
.

Let
I
and
{\mathfrak{a}}
be non-zero proper radical ideals of
R
with
{\mathfrak{a}}\subseteq I
and
\text{big-height}({\mathfrak{a}})=H
. Then, we have the followings:

(1)

if
{\mathfrak{a}}^{(H(p^{e}-1))}\subseteq I^{[p^{e}]}
, then
\mathcal{C}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}^{(\bullet)})\leq H-\frac{1}{p^{e}};

(2)

\mathcal{C}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}^{(\bullet)})=H
if and only if
{\mathfrak{a}}^{(H(p^{e}-1))}\not\subseteq I^{[p^{e}]}
for all
e
;

(3)

if
(R,{\mathfrak{m}},\mathbb{K})
is an
F
-finite regular ring with
5.3
and
\mathcal{C}^{\mathfrak{m}}({\mathfrak{a}}^{(\bullet)})=H
, then
{\mathfrak{a}}
is unmixed and symbolic
F
-split. Moreover, in this case,
R/{\mathfrak{a}}
is equi-dimensional and
F
-split.

Proof.

(1) Suppose that
{\mathfrak{a}}^{(H(p^{e_{0}}-1))}\subseteq I^{[p^{e_{0}}]}
for some
e_{0}
. Take
k=H(p^{e_{0}}-1)-1
. By
[
11
, Lemma 2.6]
,
{\mathfrak{a}}^{(Hp^{e}+kp^{e}-H+1)}\subseteq\left({\mathfrak{a}}^{(k+1)}\right)^{[p^{e}]}
for all
e
. Thus, for all
e
,
{\mathfrak{a}}^{(Hp^{e}+kp^{e}-H+1)}\subseteq\left({\mathfrak{a}}^{(k+1)}\right)^{[p^{e}]}=\left({\mathfrak{a}}^{(H(p^{e_{0}}-1))}\right)^{[p^{e}]}\subseteq\left(I^{[p^{e_{0}}]}\right)^{[p^{e}]}=I^{[p^{e_{0}+e}]}.
Consequently, we have
\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}}^{(\bullet)}}^{I}(p^{e_{0}+e})\leq Hp^{e}+kp^{e}-H
which implies that
\frac{\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}}^{(\bullet)}}^{I}(p^{e_{0}+e})}{p^{e_{0}+e}}\leq\frac{Hp^{e}+kp^{e}-H}{p^{e_{0}+e}}=\frac{Hp^{e_{0}+e}-p^{e}-H}{p^{e_{0}+e}}=H-\frac{1}{p^{e_{0}}}-\frac{H}{p^{e_{0}+e}}
for all
e
. Now, applying limit on both side, we get
{\mathcal{C}}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}^{(\bullet)})\leq H-\frac{1}{p^{e_{0}}}.

(2) If
{\mathcal{C}}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}^{(\bullet)})=H
, then it follows from
(1)
that
{\mathfrak{a}}^{(H(p^{e}-1))}\not\subseteq I^{[p^{e}]}
for all
e
. Conversely, assume that
{\mathfrak{a}}^{(H(p^{e}-1))}\not\subseteq I^{[p^{e}]}
for all
e
. Then,
\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}}^{(\bullet)}}^{I}(p^{e})\geq H(p^{e}-1)
for all
e
, and hence,
{\mathcal{C}}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}^{(\bullet)})\geq H.
Now, by
Proposition

5.1
,
{\mathcal{C}}^{I}({\mathfrak{a}}^{(\bullet)})=H.

(3) From
(2)
, we get
{\mathfrak{a}}^{(H(p-1))}\not\subseteq{\mathfrak{m}}^{[p]}.
Thus, by
[
7
, Corollary 5.10]
,
{\mathfrak{a}}
is symbolic
F
-split, and hence, by
[
7
, Remark 4.4]
,
R/{\mathfrak{a}}
is
F
-split. Also, by
Corollary

5.4
,
H=\mathcal{C}^{\mathfrak{m}}(\mathfrak{a}^{({\bullet})})\leq\text{ht}({\mathfrak{a}})
which implies that
{\mathfrak{a}}
is an unmixed ideal. Hence, the assertion follows.
∎

Corollary 5.13
.

Let
(R,{\mathfrak{m}},\mathbb{K})
be a

-finite regular ring with
5.3
. Let
{\mathfrak{a}}
be a non-zero proper radical ideal of

{\mathfrak{a}}^{(H(p-1))}\subseteq{\mathfrak{m}}^{[p]}.
Hence, by

5.12
, the assertion follows.
∎

R=\mathbb{K}[a,b,c,d]
,
{\mathfrak{m}}=(a,b,c,d)
.
Let
{\mathfrak{a}}
be the ideal generated by
2\times 2
minors of the following matrix:

\begin{bmatrix}a^{2}&b&d\\
c&a^{2}&b-d\\
\end{bmatrix},

i.e.,

{\mathfrak{a}}=(a^{4}-bc,b^{2}-bd-a^{2}d,a^{2}b-a^{2}d-cd)
. It follows from

[
37
, Proposition 4.3]
that
R/{\mathfrak{a}}
is strongly
F
-regular, and hence,
F
-split. However,
{\mathfrak{a}}
is not symbolic
F
-split, by
[
7
, Example 5.13]
. We claim that
{\mathcal{C}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}({\mathfrak{a}}^{\bullet})=\frac{7}{4}
. By
[
11
, Example 4]
,
{\mathfrak{a}}
is a prime ideal of height two, and
{\mathfrak{a}}^{s}={\mathfrak{a}}^{(s)}
for all
s
.
Assume that
\deg(a)=1
and
\deg(b)=\deg(c)=\deg(d)=2
. Let
f
be a homogeneous element of
R
such that
f\not\in{\mathfrak{m}}^{[p^{e}]}
. Therefore, there exists a monomial term, say
u=\beta a^{\alpha_{1}}b^{\alpha_{2}}c^{\alpha_{3}}d^{\alpha_{4}}
, in
f
such that
u\not\in{\mathfrak{m}}^{[p^{e}]},
where
\beta\in\mathbb{K}\setminus\{0\}.
Consequently,
\alpha_{i}\leq p^{e}-1
for each
i
, and hence,
\deg(f)=\deg(u)=\alpha_{1}+2\alpha_{2}+2\alpha_{3}+2\alpha_{3}\leq 7(p^{e}-1).
Let
s
be positive integers such that
s\geq\frac{7p^{e}}{4}
. Since
{\mathfrak{a}}
is homogeneous ideal of
R
generated by elements of degree
4
,
{\mathfrak{a}}^{s}
is generated by elements of degree
4s
. Thus,
{\mathfrak{a}}^{s}\subseteq{\mathfrak{m}}^{[p^{e}]}
as
4s\geq 7p^{e}
which implies that
\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}}^{\bullet}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}(p^{e})\leq\frac{7p^{e}}{4}.
Now, applying limit as
e
tends to infinity to
\frac{\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}}^{\bullet}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}
, we get
{\mathcal{C}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}({\mathfrak{a}}^{\bullet})\leq\frac{7}{4}.

Next, for
s=7\lfloor\frac{(p^{e}-1)}{4}\rfloor
,
{\mathfrak{a}}^{s}\not\subseteq{\mathfrak{m}}^{[p^{e}]}
as
u=\beta a^{4\lfloor\frac{(p^{e}-1)}{4}\rfloor}b^{4\lfloor\frac{(p^{e}-1)}{4}\rfloor}(cd)^{4\lfloor\frac{(p^{e}-1)}{4}\rfloor}
is a monomial term in

f=(a^{4}-bc)^{\lfloor\frac{(p^{e}-1)}{4}\rfloor}(b^{2}-bd-a^{2}d)^{2\lfloor\frac{(p^{e}-1)}{4}\rfloor}(a^{2}b-a^{2}d-cd)^{4\lfloor\frac{(p^{e}-1)}{4}\rfloor}

for some
\beta\in\mathbb{K}\setminus\{0\}
and
u\not\in{\mathfrak{m}}^{[p^{e}]}.
Thus,
\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}}^{\bullet}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}(p^{e})\geq 7\lfloor\frac{(p^{e}-1)}{4}\rfloor
for all
e
. Now, applying limit as
e
tends to infinity to
\frac{\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}}^{\bullet}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}
, we get
{\mathcal{C}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}({\mathfrak{a}}^{\bullet})\geq\frac{7}{4}.
Hence,
{\mathcal{C}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}({\mathfrak{a}}^{\bullet})=\frac{7}{4}.

6.
F
-thresholds of filtrations via valuations

In this section, we study
F
-threshold of filtration in a polynomial ring with respect to the unique monomial maximal ideal via valuations. Throughout this section,
R=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
is a polynomial ring (need not be a standard graded unless otherwise stated) over a field of prime characteristic
p>0
and
\mathfrak{m}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
is the monomial maximal ideal of
R
. Since
R
is regular, by
Proposition

3.3
, in order to show the existence of
F
-threshold of a filtration
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}
with respect to
\mathfrak{m}
, it is enough to show that the sequence
\left\{\frac{\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}}^{\mathfrak{m}}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}\right\}
is a bounded sequence. We provide a general upper bound for
F
-threshold of a filtration with respect to
{\mathfrak{m}}
in terms of skew-Waldschmidt constant of that filtration. We also provide a formula for
F
-threshold of a monomial ideal in terms of its Rees valuations. Also, we obtain symbolic F-threshold of any square-free monomial ideal in terms of the monomial valuations associated to its minimal primes.

Here, we give an equivalent criterion for the positivity of the skew-Waldschmidt constant of a filtration with respect to a monomial valuation in terms of
F
-threshold of that filtration.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}
be a filtration of non-zero proper ideals and
v
be a monomial valuation of
K
. If
\widehat{v}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})>0
, then

\mathcal{C}^{\mathfrak{m}}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})\leq\frac{v(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})}{\widehat{v}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})}.

Moreover, if
v({\mathfrak{m}})>0
and
\mathcal{C}^{\mathfrak{m}}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})
exists, then
\widehat{v}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})>0
.

Proof.

Assume that
\widehat{v}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})>0
. Suppose that
{\mathfrak{a}}_{r}\not\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{[p^{e}]}.
Then, there exists a polynomial
f\in{\mathfrak{a}}_{r}
such that
f\not\in\mathfrak{m}^{[p^{e}]}.
Since
f\not\in{\mathfrak{m}}^{[p^{e}]}
, there is a monomial term of
f
, say
u
, which is not in
{\mathfrak{m}}^{[p^{e}]}
. This implies that exponent of each variable in
u
is at most
p^{e}-1
, and therefore,
u
divides
x_{1}^{p^{e}-1}\cdots x_{n}^{p^{e}-1}.
Consequently,
v(x_{1}^{p^{e}-1}\cdots x_{n}^{p^{e}-1})\geq v(u)\geq v(f)\geq v({\mathfrak{a}}_{r})
which implies that
(p^{e}-1)v(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})\geq v({\mathfrak{a}}_{r}).
Thus, if
{\mathfrak{a}}_{r}\not\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{[p^{e}]},
then
r\leq\frac{r(p^{e}-1)v(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})}{v({\mathfrak{a}}_{r})}\leq\frac{(p^{e}-1)v(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})}{\widehat{v}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})}
as
\widehat{v}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})=\inf_{r\geq 1}\frac{v({\mathfrak{a}}_{r})}{r}
. Therefore, for all
e
,
\frac{\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}}^{\mathfrak{m}}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{e}}\right)\frac{v(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})}{\widehat{v}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})}
, and hence, by
Proposition

3.3
,
\mathcal{C}^{\mathfrak{m}}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})\leq\frac{v(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})}{\widehat{v}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})}<\infty.

Conversely, we assume that
v({\mathfrak{m}})>0
and
{\mathcal{C}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})
exists. Therefore, for some positive integer
M
,
{\mathfrak{a}}_{Mp^{e}}\subseteq{\mathfrak{m}}^{[p^{e}]}
for all
e
. Consequently,
v({\mathfrak{a}}_{Mp^{e}})\geq v({\mathfrak{m}}^{[p^{e}]})=p^{e}\min\{v(x_{i}):1\leq i\leq n\}=p^{e}v({\mathfrak{m}}).
Now,
\widehat{v}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})=\lim_{e\to\infty}\frac{v({\mathfrak{a}}_{Mp^{e}})}{Mp^{e}}\geq\frac{v({\mathfrak{m}})}{M}>0.
Hence, the assertion follows.
∎

Next, we provide a class of filtration that attain the bound given in
Theorem

6.1
.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}
be monomial valuations of
K
, and let
m_{1},\ldots,m_{k}
be positive real numbers. Let

{\mathfrak{a}}_{n}=(f\in R~:~v_{i}(f)\geq m_{i}n\text{ for all }1\leq i\leq k)

for all
n\geq 0
. Then,

{\mathcal{C}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})=\min_{1\leq i\leq k}\frac{v_{i}(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})}{\widehat{v}_{i}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})}=\min_{1\leq i\leq k}\frac{v_{i}(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})}{m_{i}}.

Furthermore, if
v_{i}
’s are rational monomial valuations and
m_{i}
’s are rational, then
{\mathcal{C}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})
is rational.

Proof.

First of all note that
v_{i}({\mathfrak{a}}_{n})\geq m_{i}n
for all
n
, and therefore,
\widehat{v}_{i}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})\geq m_{i}.
Thus, by
Theorem

6.1
,
{\mathcal{C}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})\leq\min_{1\leq i\leq k}\frac{v_{i}(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})}{\widehat{v}_{i}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})}=\min_{1\leq i\leq k}\frac{v_{i}(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})}{m_{i}}.
Take
\alpha=\min_{1\leq i\leq k}\frac{v_{i}(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})}{m_{i}}.
Then, for each
1\leq i\leq k
and
e\geq 1
,
v_{i}(x_{1}^{p^{e}-1}\cdots x_{n}^{p^{e}-1})=(p^{e}-1)v_{i}(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})\geq m_{i}\lfloor(p^{e}-1)\alpha\rfloor
, which implies that
x_{1}^{p^{e}-1}\cdots x_{n}^{p^{e}-1}\in{\mathfrak{a}}_{\lfloor(p^{e}-1)\alpha\rfloor}
. Thus, by
Lemma

4.8
,
\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}(p^{e})\geq{\lfloor(p^{e}-1)\alpha\rfloor}
for each
e
. Now, applying limit to the sequence
\frac{\nu_{{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}
we get that
{\mathcal{C}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})\geq\alpha=\min_{1\leq i\leq k}\frac{v_{i}(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})}{m_{i}}.
Hence, the assertion follows.
∎

Next, we study
F
-thresholds of homogeneous filtrations when
R
is standard graded in terms of the degree valuation.

Corollary 6.3
.

Assume that
R
is standard graded. Let
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}
be a filtration of non-zero proper homogeneous ideals. Then,
\widehat{\alpha}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})>0
if and only if
{\mathcal{C}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})<\infty.
Furthermore, in this case,

\mathcal{C}^{\mathfrak{m}}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})\leq\frac{n}{\widehat{\alpha}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})}.

Proof.

The assertion immediately follows from Theorem
6.1
and the fact that
\alpha
is a monomial valuation with
\alpha({\mathfrak{m}})>0.

∎

As an immediate consequence, we obtain an upper bound for
F
-threshold and symbolic
F
-threshold of a non-zero proper homogeneous ideal
{\mathfrak{a}}
.

Corollary 6.4
.

Assume that
R
is standard graded. Let
{\mathfrak{a}}
be a non-zero proper homogeneous ideal in
R
. Then,

(1)

\mathcal{C}^{\mathfrak{m}}({\mathfrak{a}}^{\bullet})\leq\frac{n}{\alpha({\mathfrak{a}})}.

(2)

\mathcal{C}^{\mathfrak{m}}({\mathfrak{a}}^{(\bullet)})\leq\frac{n}{\widehat{\alpha}({\mathfrak{a}}^{(\bullet)})}
.

Remark 6.5
.

The above upper bound for
F
-thresholds is known. When the ideal is principle, another proof is given in
[
27
, Proposition 2.2]
.

We now obtain an upper bound for
F
-threshold of a square-free monomial ideal in terms of combinatorial invariants of associated hypergraph.

Corollary 6.6
.

Assume that
R
is standard graded. Let
{\mathfrak{a}}
be a non-zero proper square-free monomial ideal in
R
and let
\mathcal{H}
be the associated hypergraph whose edge ideal is
{\mathfrak{a}}
, i.e.,
I({\mathcal{H}})={\mathfrak{a}}
. Then,

(1)

\mathcal{C}^{\mathfrak{m}}({\mathfrak{a}}^{\bullet})\leq\frac{n}{d},
where
d=\min\{|e|~:~e\in E(\mathcal{H})\}.

(2)

\mathcal{C}^{\mathfrak{m}}({\mathfrak{a}}^{(\bullet)})\leq\frac{n(\chi_{f}(\mathcal{H})-1)}{\chi_{f}(\mathcal{H})}
, where
\chi_{f}(\mathcal{H})
is the fractional chromatic number of
\mathcal{H}
.

Proof.

The first assertion immediately follows from
Corollary

6.4
and the fact that
\alpha({\mathfrak{a}})=d
. The second part follows from
Corollary

6.4
and
[
4
, Theorem 4.6]
.
∎

The bound obtained in
Corollary

6.4
and
Corollary

6.6
are sharp, as the following examples illustrate it:

Example 6.7
.

Assume that
R
is standard graded. Let
G
be a Hamiltonian graph on the vertex set
\{1,\ldots,n\}
and
I_{G}
be the corresponding edge ideal in
R
. We claim that
\mathcal{C}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}(I_{G}^{\bullet})=\frac{n}{2}.
In view of
Corollary

6.6
, it is enough to prove that
\mathcal{C}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}(I_{G}^{\bullet})\geq\frac{n}{2}.
Since
G
is a Hamiltonian graph, we have
(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})^{2}\in I_{G}^{n}
. For any
e
, write
p^{e}-1=2q_{e}+r_{e}
with
0\leq r_{e}\leq 1
and
q_{e}\geq 0
. Consider
(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})^{p^{e}-1}=(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})^{2q_{e}+r_{e}}\in I_{G}^{nq_{e}+r_{e}}
. Thus,
I_{G}^{nq_{e}+r_{e}}\not\subseteq{\mathfrak{m}}^{[p^{e}]}
which implies that
\nu_{I_{G}^{\bullet}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}(p^{e})\geq nq_{e}=\frac{n(p^{e}-1-r_{e})}{2}\geq\frac{n(p^{e}-2)}{2}.
Now, by applying limit to the both sides of the inequality, we get
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}(I_{G}^{\bullet})=\lim_{e\to\infty}\frac{\nu_{I_{G}^{\bullet}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}\geq\lim_{e\to\infty}\frac{n(p^{e}-2)}{2p^{e}}=\frac{n}{2}.
Hence, the assertion follows.

Example 6.8
.

Assume that
R
is standard graded. It is well known that the
F
-threshold of edge ideal of a hypergraph is equal to the fractional matching number of that graph, see
[
21
,
28
]
. In particular, if
\mathcal{H}
is a hypergraph on the vertex set
\{1,\ldots,n\},
then
\mathcal{C}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}(I_{\mathcal{H}}^{\bullet})\geq m(\mathcal{H}),
where
m(\mathcal{H})
is the matching number of
\mathcal{H}
. Thus, if
G
is a graph with perfect matching, then by
Corollary

6.6
,
\mathcal{C}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}(I_{G}^{\bullet})=m(G)=\frac{n}{2},
as every vertex is part of some edge in the perfect matching.

Example 6.9
.

Assume that
R
is standard graded. Let
G=C_{n}
be an odd cycle on the vertex set
{\{1,\ldots,n\}}
and
J(G)
be the vertex cover ideal of
G
. It follows from the proof of
[
25
, Theorem 4.7]
that
\widehat{\alpha}(J(G)^{(\bullet)})=\frac{n}{2}
and
(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})^{s}\in J(G)^{(2s)}
for all
s
. Consequently, by
Corollary

6.4
,
\mathcal{C}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}(J(G)^{(\bullet)})\leq 2.
One can see that
(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})^{p^{e}-1}\in J(G)^{(2(p^{e}-1))}\setminus{\mathfrak{m}}^{[p^{e}]}
. Therefore,
\nu_{J(G)^{(\bullet)}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}(p^{e})\geq 2(p^{e}-1).
Now, by
Proposition

3.3
,
\mathcal{C}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}(J(G)^{(\bullet)})=\sup_{e\geq 0}\frac{\nu^{{\mathfrak{m}}}_{J(G)^{(\bullet)}}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}\geq\sup_{e\geq 0}\frac{2(p^{e}-1)}{p^{e}}\geq 2.
Thus,
\mathcal{C}^{\mathfrak{m}}(J(G)^{(\bullet)})=\frac{n}{\widehat{\alpha}(J(G)^{(\bullet)})}
.

Lemma 6.10
.

Let
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}
be a filtration of monomial ideals in
R
. Let
v
be a valuation of quotient field of
R
. If
\widehat{v}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})>0
, then

\mathcal{C}^{\mathfrak{m}}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})\leq\frac{v(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})}{\widehat{v}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})}.

Moreover, if
v({\mathfrak{m}})>0
and
\mathcal{C}^{\mathfrak{m}}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})
exists, then
\widehat{v}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})>0
.

Proof.

The proof is similar to the proof of
Theorem

6.1
.
∎

Next, we study
F
-thresholds and symbolic
F
-thresholds of monomial ideals in terms of Rees valuations.

Theorem 6.11
.

Let
{\mathfrak{a}}
be a non-zero proper monomial ideal in
R
. Then,

C^{\mathfrak{m}}({\mathfrak{a}}^{\bullet})=\min\left\{\frac{v(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})}{v({\mathfrak{a}})}:v\in\text{RV}({\mathfrak{a}})\right\},

where
\text{RV}({\mathfrak{a}})
denote the set of Rees valuations of
{\mathfrak{a}}.
Moreover,
C^{\mathfrak{m}}({\mathfrak{a}}^{\bullet})
is a rational number.

Proof.

Consider the filtration
\overline{{\mathfrak{a}}^{\bullet}}=\{\overline{{\mathfrak{a}}^{i}}\}_{i\geq 0}
. It follows from
Corollary

3.9
that
C^{\mathfrak{m}}({\mathfrak{a}}^{\bullet})=C^{\mathfrak{m}}(\overline{{\mathfrak{a}}^{\bullet}}).
Thus it is enough to prove that

C^{\mathfrak{m}}(\overline{{\mathfrak{a}}^{\bullet}})=\min\left\{\frac{v(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})}{v({\mathfrak{a}})}:v\in\text{RV}({\mathfrak{a}})\right\}.

Let
w\in\text{RV}({\mathfrak{a}})
such that

\frac{w(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})}{w({\mathfrak{a}})}=\min\left\{\frac{v(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})}{v({\mathfrak{a}})}:v\in\text{RV}({\mathfrak{a}})\right\}.

Let
r
be a non-negative integer. Then,
x_{1}^{p^{e}-1}\cdots x_{n}^{p^{e}-1}\in\overline{{\mathfrak{a}}^{r}}
if and only if
v(x_{1}^{p^{e}-1}\cdots x_{n}^{p^{e}-1})\geq rv({\mathfrak{a}})
for all
v\in\text{RV}({\mathfrak{a}})
which happens if and only if
r\leq\frac{w(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})}{{w}({\mathfrak{a}})}(p^{e}-1)
. Thus, by
Lemma

4.8
,
\nu_{\overline{{\mathfrak{a}}^{\bullet}}}^{\mathfrak{m}}(p^{e})=\left\lfloor\frac{w(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})}{{w}({\mathfrak{a}})}(p^{e}-1)\right\rfloor.
Now, by
Proposition

3.3
and
applying limit
e
tends to infinity to
\frac{\nu_{\overline{{\mathfrak{a}}^{\bullet}}}^{\mathfrak{m}}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}
, we get

C^{\mathfrak{m}}(\overline{{\mathfrak{a}}^{\bullet}})=\frac{w(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})}{{w}({\mathfrak{a}})}.

Hence, the assertion follows.
∎

Using Theorem
6.11
, one can characterize monomial ideals for which the inequality in
Corollary

6.4

(1)
becomes an equality.

Remark 6.12
.

Assume that
R
is standard graded. Let
{\mathfrak{a}}
be a monomial ideal in
R
. We claim that
{\mathcal{C}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}({\mathfrak{a}}^{\bullet})=\frac{n}{\alpha({\mathfrak{a}})}
if and only if
(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})^{\alpha({\mathfrak{a}})}\in\overline{{\mathfrak{a}}^{n}}.
First, we assume that
{\mathcal{C}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}({\mathfrak{a}}^{\bullet})=\frac{n}{\alpha({\mathfrak{a}})}
. By
Theorem

6.11
,
\frac{n}{\alpha({\mathfrak{a}})}={\mathcal{C}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}({\mathfrak{a}}^{\bullet})\leq\frac{v(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})}{v({\mathfrak{a}})}
for all
v\in\text{RV}({\mathfrak{a}})
. Therefore,
v((x_{1}\cdots x_{n})^{\alpha({\mathfrak{a}})})\geq v({\mathfrak{a}}^{n})
for all
v\in\text{RV}({\mathfrak{a}})
. Consequently, by valuation criteria of integral closure (Remark
2.3
),
(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})^{\alpha({\mathfrak{a}})}\in\overline{{\mathfrak{a}}^{n}}.

Conversely, we have
(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})^{\alpha({\mathfrak{a}})}\in\overline{{\mathfrak{a}}^{n}}.
Then,
v((x_{1}\cdots x_{n})^{\alpha({\mathfrak{a}})})\geq v({\mathfrak{a}}^{n})
for all
v\in\text{RV}({\mathfrak{a}})
. Equivalently,
\frac{n}{\alpha({\mathfrak{a}})}\leq\frac{v(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})}{v({\mathfrak{a}})}
for all
v\in\text{RV}({\mathfrak{a}})
. Now, by Theorem
6.11
and
Corollary

6.4
,
{\mathcal{C}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}({\mathfrak{a}}^{\bullet})=\frac{n}{\alpha({\mathfrak{a}})}
. Hence, the claim follows.

In
Corollary

5.4
, we have seen that
\hgt({\mathfrak{a}})
is an upper bound of
{\mathcal{C}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}({\mathfrak{a}}^{\bullet})
. In the following remark, we provide a necessary and a sufficient condition which impose monomial ideals to have maximal
F
-threshold.

Remark 6.13
.

Let
{\mathfrak{a}}
be a monomial ideal in
R
. We claim that
{\mathcal{C}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}({\mathfrak{a}}^{\bullet})=\hgt({\mathfrak{a}})
if and only if
x_{1}\cdots x_{n}\in\overline{{\mathfrak{a}}^{\hgt({\mathfrak{a}})}}.
Note that
{\mathcal{C}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}({\mathfrak{a}}^{\bullet})=\hgt({\mathfrak{a}})
if and only if
\hgt({\mathfrak{a}})\leq\frac{v(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})}{v({\mathfrak{a}})}
for all
v\in\text{RV}({\mathfrak{a}})
(by
Theorem

6.11
), if and only if
v(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})\geq v({\mathfrak{a}}^{\hgt({\mathfrak{a}})})
for all
v\in\text{RV}({\mathfrak{a}})
which is equivalent to
x_{1}\cdots x_{n}\in\overline{{\mathfrak{a}}^{\hgt({\mathfrak{a}})}}
, by valuation criteria of integral closure (Remark
2.3
). Hence, the claim follows.

We now obtain
F
-threshold of Noetherian monomial filtration in terms of Rees valuations.

Theorem 6.14
.

Let
{\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}
be a filtration of monomial ideals in
R
such that
{\mathcal{R}}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})
is Noetherian. Then

{\mathcal{C}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})=\min_{v\in\text{RV}({\mathfrak{a}}_{r})}\frac{v(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})}{\widehat{v}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})},

where
r
is a positive integer such that the
r
-th Veronese subalgebra
{\mathcal{R}}^{[r]}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet}):=\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}{\mathfrak{a}}_{ri}t^{ri}
of
{\mathcal{R}}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})
is a standard graded
R
-algebra. Moreover,
C^{\mathfrak{m}}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})
is a rational number.

Proof.

It follows from
Theorem

3.15
that
{\mathcal{C}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})=r{\mathcal{C}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}({\mathfrak{a}}_{r}^{\bullet})
, and from the proof, it follows that
{\mathcal{R}}^{[r]}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})
is a standard graded
R
-algebra. Therefore, by
[
13
, Lemma 2.11]
, for any valuation
v
of
K
,
\widehat{v}({\mathfrak{a}}_{\bullet})=\frac{v({\mathfrak{a}}_{r})}{r}.
The rest now follows from
Theorem

6.11
.
∎

As a consequence, we obtain symbolic
F
-threshold of monomial ideals in terms of Rees valuations.

Corollary 6.15
.

Let
{\mathfrak{a}}
be a non-zero proper monomial ideal in
R
. Then,

C^{\mathfrak{m}}({\mathfrak{a}}^{(\bullet)})=\min_{v\in\text{RV}({\mathfrak{a}}_{r})}\left\{\frac{v(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})}{\widehat{v}({\mathfrak{a}}^{(\bullet)})}:v\in\text{RV}({\mathfrak{a}}^{(r)})\right\},

where
r
is a positive integer such that
{\mathcal{R}}^{[r]}({\mathfrak{a}}^{(\bullet)})
is a standard graded
R
-algebra.
In particular,
C^{\mathfrak{m}}({\mathfrak{a}}^{(\bullet)})
is a rational number.

Proof.

It follows from
[
17
, Theorem 3.2]
that
{\mathcal{R}}({\mathfrak{a}}^{(\bullet)})
is Noetherian. Now, the assertion follows from
Theorem

6.14
.
∎

Next, we obtain symbolic
F
-threshold of a square-free monomial ideal in term of monomial valuations given by minimal primes of that ideal.

Theorem 6.16
.

Let
{\mathfrak{a}}
be a square-free monomial ideal in
R
. Then,

C^{\mathfrak{m}}({\mathfrak{a}}^{(\bullet)})=\min\left\{\frac{v_{\mathfrak{p}}(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})}{\widehat{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}({\mathfrak{a}}^{(\bullet)})}:\mathfrak{p}\in\text{Min}({\mathfrak{a}})\right\}=\text{ht}({\mathfrak{a}}),

where for
\mathfrak{p}\in\text{Min}({\mathfrak{a}})
,
v_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a monomial valuation defined as
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(x_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{m_{n}})=\sum_{x_{i}\in\mathfrak{p}}m_{i}
for all monomials
x_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{m_{n}}
in
R
.

Proof.

Note that
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})=\sum_{x_{i}\in\mathfrak{p}}1=\text{ht}(\mathfrak{p})
for any
\mathfrak{p}\in\text{Min}({\mathfrak{a}})
. Since
{\mathfrak{a}}^{(s)}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}^{s}
,
s=v_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathfrak{p}^{s})\leq v_{\mathfrak{p}}({\mathfrak{a}}^{(s)})
for all
\mathfrak{p}\in\text{Min}({\mathfrak{a}}).
Therefore,
\widehat{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}({\mathfrak{a}}^{(\bullet)})=\inf_{s\geq 1}\frac{v_{\mathfrak{p}}({\mathfrak{a}}^{(s)})}{s}\geq 1
for every
\mathfrak{p}\in\text{Min}({\mathfrak{a}}).
By
Lemma

6.10
,

C^{\mathfrak{m}}({\mathfrak{a}}^{(\bullet)})\leq\min\left\{\frac{v_{\mathfrak{p}}(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})}{\widehat{v}_{\mathfrak{p}}({\mathfrak{a}}^{(\bullet)})}:\mathfrak{p}\in\text{Min}({\mathfrak{a}})\right\}\leq\text{ht}({\mathfrak{a}}).

Next, take
r=\text{ht}({\mathfrak{a}})(p^{e}-1).
Since
x_{1}^{p^{e}-1}\cdots x_{n}^{p^{e}-1}\in\mathfrak{p}^{\text{ht}(\mathfrak{p})(p^{e}-1)}
for all
\mathfrak{p}\in\text{Min}({\mathfrak{a}})
,
x_{1}^{p^{e}-1}\cdots x_{n}^{p^{e}-1}\in{{\mathfrak{a}}^{(r)}}
. Thus, by
Lemma

4.8
,
\nu_{{{\mathfrak{a}}^{(\bullet)}}}^{\mathfrak{m}}(p^{e})\geq\text{ht}({\mathfrak{a}})(p^{e}-1)
for all
e\geq 0
. Now, by
Proposition

3.3
and
applying limit
e
tends to infinity to
\frac{\nu_{{{\mathfrak{a}}^{(\bullet)}}}^{\mathfrak{m}}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}
, we get that
C^{\mathfrak{m}}({{\mathfrak{a}}^{(\bullet)}})\geq\text{ht}({\mathfrak{a}}).
Hence, the assertion follows.
∎

One could observe that if
{\mathfrak{a}}
is not a square-free monomial ideal, then Theorem
6.16
may not hold, see
Example

5.11
. We end this section by characterizing square-free monomial ideals for which the inequality in
Corollary

6.6

(2)
becomes an equality.

Example 6.17
.

Assume that
R
is standard graded. Let
{\mathfrak{a}}
be a square-free monomial ideal in
R
and
{\mathcal{H}}
be the associated hypergraph on the vertex set
{\{1,\ldots,n\}}
, i.e.,
I_{\mathcal{H}}={\mathfrak{a}}
. We claim that
{\mathcal{C}}^{{\mathfrak{m}}}({\mathfrak{a}}^{(\bullet)})=\frac{n(\chi_{f}(\mathcal{H})-1)}{\chi_{f}(\mathcal{H})}
if and only if
\chi_{f}({\mathcal{H}})=\frac{n}{n-\tau({\mathcal{H}})}
, where
\tau({\mathcal{H}})
is the minimum among cardinality of vertex covers of
{\mathcal{H}}
. Note that hypergraph with
\chi_{f}({\mathcal{H}})=\frac{n}{n-\tau({\mathcal{H}})}
exists, for example, any vertex transitive graph. The claim immediately follows from
Theorem

6.16
and the fact that the minimal primes of
{I_{\mathcal{H}}}
are in one to one correspondence with the minimal vertex covers of
{{\mathcal{H}}}
.

Abstract
. We give a generalization of Quillen’s S − 1 ​ S \displaystyle S^{-1}S construction for arbitrary 𝔼 n \displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{n} -monoids as an 𝔼 n − 1 \displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{n-1} -monoidal ∞ \displaystyle\infty -category and show that its realization models the group completion provided that n ≥ 2 \displaystyle n\geq 2 . 1.
Introduction

After Quillen’s original construction of higher algebraic
\displaystyle K
-theory
[
Qui73
]
, many different constructions to group complete a variety of sufficiently commutative higher algebraic gadgets, such as a homotopy commutative topological monoid, or a symmetric monoidal groupoid, have been considered in the literature. These constructions have been of fundamental importance in the study of higher
\displaystyle K
-theory as well as
\displaystyle L
-theory. We want to highlight the following examples.

(1)

If
\displaystyle(M,+)
is an ordinary commutative monoid, we can put an equivalence relation on the set
\displaystyle M\times M
, by defining

(a,b)\sim(c,d)\iff\exists k\in M:a+d+k=c+b+k.

The resulting quotient
\displaystyle M\times M/\sim
will be an abelian group, and it is the initial abelian group under
\displaystyle M
, also called the Grothendieck group of
\displaystyle M
.

(2)

Suppose
\displaystyle(S,\oplus)
is symmetric monoidal groupoid. Quillen
[
Gra76
]
constructs a symmetric monoidal category
\displaystyle S^{-1}S
with objects given by pairs
\displaystyle(x,y)\in S
, and morphisms being equivalence classes of

(x_{1},y_{1})\xrightarrow{k,\alpha,\beta}(x_{2},y_{2})

consisting of
\displaystyle k\in S
together with isomorphisms
\displaystyle\alpha:k\oplus x_{1}\cong x_{2}
and
\displaystyle\beta:k\oplus y_{1}\cong y_{2}
. Two morphisms
\displaystyle(k,\alpha,\beta)
and
\displaystyle(k^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})
are considered equivalent if there exists an isomorphism
\displaystyle\gamma:k\cong k^{\prime}
in
\displaystyle S
that commutes with the structure involved. Quillen shows that in the case that
\displaystyle S=\text{Proj}(R)^{\cong}
is the symmetric monoidal groupoid of projective, finitely generated
\displaystyle R
-modules for a discrete ring
\displaystyle R
, with symmetric monoidal structure given by the coproduct, the realization
\displaystyle|S^{-1}S|
is equivalent to the
\displaystyle K
-theory space of
\displaystyle R
.

(3)

Suppose
\displaystyle M
is a homotopy commutative topological monoid. Then for a given
\displaystyle m\in M
one can consider the telescope

M_{m}=\mathrm{colim}(M\xrightarrow{m}M\xrightarrow{m}M\xrightarrow{m}\cdots).

By iterating this construction over a generating set of
\displaystyle\pi_{0}M
, one can construct a space
\displaystyle M_{\infty}
. Further, taking a plus construction one obtains a space
\displaystyle(M_{\infty})^{+}
, together with the map

M\rightarrow M_{\infty}\rightarrow(M_{\infty})^{+}

which is a model for the group completion of
\displaystyle M
. This construction has been more or less considered by Quillen and has later been formalized by McDuff and Segal in
[
MS76
]
in the case when only a single generator
\displaystyle m
is needed. A framework on how to deal with an arbitrary set of generators has been discussed by Randal-Williams
[
Ran13
]
(See also
[
Nik17
]
).

(4)

A first definition of what would now be called an
\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-monoid has been provided by Segal
[
Seg74
]
with his notion of a
\displaystyle\Gamma
-space. Segal constructs a functor
\displaystyle B
that deloops such a
\displaystyle\Gamma
-space. The group completion of a
\displaystyle\Gamma
-space
\displaystyle X
can then be defined as
\displaystyle\Omega BX
.

The purpose of this paper is twofold. The first is to put all these variations into one common framework, using the now established language of
\displaystyle\infty
-categories. Our model for what it means to be a “sufficiently commutative higher algebraic gadget” will be what is called an
\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{n}
-monoid, also called
\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{n}
-space, for
\displaystyle n\geq 2

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{n}
-space is a coherently associative and unital monoid in spaces, that is furthermore commutative up to coherent homotopy, whereby
\displaystyle n

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{n}
-monoid is called grouplike if its set of path components, which naturally has the structure of a monoid, is a group. For purely formal reasons it holds that the inclusion of the full subcategory of the
\displaystyle\infty

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{n}
-monoids given by the grouplike objects has a left adjoint
\displaystyle(-)^{\mathrm{gp}}

-monoidal
\displaystyle\infty

3.1.
Actions and left modules

If
\displaystyle f:X\rightarrow Y
is a map of
\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{1}
-monoids, we can think of
\displaystyle f
as equipping
\displaystyle Y
with an
\displaystyle X
-action via left multiplication. To make this observation precise, let us discuss actions of an
\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{1}
-monoid in this section.

Definition 3.11
.

Let
\displaystyle X
be an
\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{1}
-monoid. A
left
\displaystyle X
-action
on a space
\displaystyle Z
is a functor
\displaystyle\underline{Z}:\mathbf{B}X\rightarrow\mathrm{Spc}
such that
\displaystyle\underline{Z}(*)\simeq Z
. Define the
\displaystyle\infty
-category of left actions as
\displaystyle\mathrm{Fun}(\mathbf{B}X,\mathrm{Spc})
.

Similarly,
right
\displaystyle X
-actions
are defined as functors
\displaystyle\mathbf{B}X^{\mathrm{op}}\rightarrow\mathrm{Spc}
, and the
\displaystyle\infty
-category of right actions is defined as
\displaystyle\mathrm{Fun}(\mathbf{B}X^{\mathrm{op}},\mathrm{Spc})
.

If
\displaystyle f:X\rightarrow Y
as a map of
\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{1}
-monoids, there is an induced adjunction on the
\displaystyle\infty
-categories of left actions

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Fun}(\mathbf{B}X,\mathrm{Spc})}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Fun}(\mathbf{B}Y,\mathrm{Spc})}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{!}}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{*}}

\displaystyle\scriptstyle\dashv

where
\displaystyle f^{*}
is given by precomposition with
\displaystyle\mathbf{B}(f)
and
\displaystyle f_{!}
is given by left Kan extension.

The notion of left action for an
\displaystyle E_{1}
-monoid has a generalization to what is called a
left module
for an algebra object. The theory of left modules for an algebra is developed in
[
Lur17
, Chapter 4]
. To make the relation to Lurie’s more general framework understandable, let us sketch how the relation works.

For a given monoidal
\displaystyle\infty
-category
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}
, and
\displaystyle A
an
\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{1}
-algebra object, there is an associated
\displaystyle\infty
-category

\mathrm{LMod}_{A}(\mathcal{C})

of

over
\displaystyle A
. If
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}
is presentably monoidal, i.e.
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}
is presentable and the tensor product on
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}
commutes with colimits in both variables, then
\displaystyle\mathrm{LMod}_{A}(\mathcal{C})
is presentable as well,
[
Lur17
, Corollary 4.2.3.7]
. Moreover, it contains a natural choice of object, with
\displaystyle A\in\mathrm{LMod}_{A}(\mathcal{C})
considered as a left module acting on itself. Structurally, the construction of the
\displaystyle\infty
-category of left
\displaystyle A
-modules can be understood in the following way.

Theorem 3.12

(
[
Lur17
]
, p. 750)

Let
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}
be a presentably monoidal
\displaystyle\infty
-category, and denote by
\displaystyle\mathrm{Alg}(\mathcal{C})
the
\displaystyle\infty

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{1}
-algebras in
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\theta:\mathrm{Alg}(\mathcal{C})&\rightarrow&\mathrm{LMod}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathrm{Pr}^{L})_{\mathcal{C}/}\\

Moreover,
\displaystyle\theta
is fully faithful and has a right adjoint
\displaystyle\mathrm{End}
.

The right adjoint
\displaystyle\mathrm{End}
works the following way. If
\displaystyle i_{!}:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}
is a
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}

\displaystyle 1\in\mathcal{C}
gives an object

in

\displaystyle\mathrm{End}(i_{!})

\displaystyle M

In the concrete case of

\displaystyle\infty

is the unit for the Lurie tensor product on

\displaystyle\infty

\displaystyle\infty

\displaystyle\infty

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{1}
-monoids
\displaystyle X

\displaystyle\infty

Definition 5.2
.

Let
\displaystyle X

-monoid. Define

Theorem 5.3

(Additivity)
.

Let
\displaystyle X
be an
\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{n}
-monoid for
\displaystyle n\geq 2

\displaystyle k\geq 0

\mathbf{G}_{k}(X)\rightarrow\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathbf{G}X

becomes an equivalence after realization.

Proof.

The case
\displaystyle k=0
is clear, as the realization of
\displaystyle\mathbf{E}X
is contractible. Now let
\displaystyle k\geq 1
. Under straightening the statement is equivalent to the natural map

\mathrm{colim}_{\mathbf{E}X}(X^{\rightarrow}\times\cdots\times X^{\rightarrow})\rightarrow\mathrm{colim}_{\mathbf{E}X}X^{\rightarrow}\times\cdots\times\mathrm{colim}_{\mathbf{E}X}X^{\rightarrow}

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}X
acts diagonally on the left hand side. Since both sides realize to
\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{1}
-groups, it suffices to check that it is a homology equivalence. Using Theorem

the map becomes

\pi_{0}X^{-1}H\mathbb{Z}[X\times\cdots\times X]\rightarrow\pi_{0}X^{-1}H\mathbb{Z}[X]\otimes\cdots\otimes\pi_{0}X^{-1}H\mathbb{Z}[X].

Note that

\pi_{0}X^{-1}H\mathbb{Z}[X\times\cdots\times X]\simeq\pi_{0}X^{-1}(H\mathbb{Z}[X]\otimes\cdots\otimes H\mathbb{Z}[X]).

The statement now follows by applying the Künneth Spectral sequence and using the following lemma.
∎

In the following, a ring
\displaystyle R

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle S

respects the coalgebra structure
if
\displaystyle\Delta(S)
and
\displaystyle S\otimes S
are multiplicatively equivalent subsets of
\displaystyle R\otimes R

\displaystyle s\in S\otimes S
there exists
\displaystyle s^{\prime}\in S\otimes S
such that
\displaystyle s\cdot s^{\prime}\in\Delta S
and similarly with
\displaystyle\Delta S
and
\displaystyle S\otimes S
reversed. An example of such a bialgebra and multiplicative subset is the group ring
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}[\pi_{0}(X)]
with the subset

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{2}

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\displaystyle R
be a bialgebra and
\displaystyle S
a central multiplicative subset that respects the coalgebra structure. Let
\displaystyle M_{i}
for
\displaystyle i=1,\cdots,k
be a finite set of
\displaystyle R

is an isomorphism of abelian groups, where the left hand side is equipped with the diagonal

Proof.

An inverse map is given by

\frac{x_{1}}{s_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\frac{x_{k}}{s_{k}}\mapsto\frac{1}{s}(\hat{s}_{1}x_{1}\otimes\cdots\hat{s}_{k}x_{k})

where
\displaystyle s=s_{1}\cdot s_{2}\cdots s_{k}
and

is the same product but with
\displaystyle s_{i}

∎

6.
How to model
\displaystyle\Omega X^{\mathrm{gp}}

In the following section, we always assume that
\displaystyle X
is an
\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{n}
-monoid for
\displaystyle n\geq 2
. A very important question in practice is how to get some understanding of the higher homotopy groups of
\displaystyle X^{\mathrm{gp}}
. This amounts to understanding
\displaystyle\Omega X^{\mathrm{gp}}=\text{Map}_{|\mathbf{G}X|}((1,1),(1,1))
. This space of course only depends on the path component of
\displaystyle(1,1)
in
\displaystyle|\mathbf{G}X|
.

Lemma 6.1
.

The path component of
\displaystyle(1,1)
in
\displaystyle|\mathbf{G}X|
can be identified as spanned by the pairs
\displaystyle(x,y)\in\mathbf{G}(X)
such that
\displaystyle x
and
\displaystyle y
are
stably equivalent
, which means there exists
\displaystyle k\in X
such that
\displaystyle k\cdot x\simeq k\cdot y
in
\displaystyle X
.

Proof.

It is clear that
\displaystyle(1,1)
is a stably equivalent pair. If
\displaystyle(x,y)
is stably equivalent, choose
\displaystyle k\in X
such that
\displaystyle k\cdot x\simeq k\cdot y
. We have the path

(1,1)\xrightarrow{k\cdot x}(k\cdot x,k\cdot x)\leftarrow(x,y),

which means that
\displaystyle(x,y)
lies in the same path component as
\displaystyle(1,1)
. We now claim that the subcategory of
\displaystyle\mathbf{G}X
spanned by stably equivalent pairs is closed under ingoing and outgoing arrows. Suppose
\displaystyle(x,y)
is stably equivalent, and let
\displaystyle k\in X
such that
\displaystyle k\cdot x\simeq k\cdot y
.

•

If

(x,y)\xrightarrow{k^{\prime},\alpha,\beta}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})

is an arrow, then it follows that

k\cdot x^{\prime}\simeq_{k\cdot\alpha}k\cdot k^{\prime}\cdot x\simeq k^{\prime}\cdot k\cdot x\simeq k^{\prime}\cdot k\cdot y\simeq k\cdot k^{\prime}\cdot y\simeq_{k\cdot\beta}k\cdot y^{\prime},

hence the target is stably equivalent, where commutativity of
\displaystyle X
was used in the second and fourth equivalence.

•

If

(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\xrightarrow{k^{\prime},\alpha,\beta}(x,y)

is an arrow, then it follows that

k\cdot k^{\prime}\cdot x^{\prime}\simeq_{k\cdot\alpha}k\cdot x\simeq k\cdot y\simeq_{k\cdot\beta}k\cdot k^{\prime}\cdot y^{\prime},

hence the source is stably equivalent.

∎

It turns out, in order to compute the mapping space
\displaystyle\text{Map}_{|\mathbf{G}X|}((1,1),(1,1))
the entire path component of
\displaystyle(1,1)
is not needed. Consider the subcategory
\displaystyle\mathbf{Bin}(X)
of
\displaystyle\mathbf{G}X
defined as the full subcategory spanned by objects of the form
\displaystyle(x,x)
for
\displaystyle x\in X
. We will for simplicity identify the objects of
\displaystyle\mathbf{Bin}(X)
with the objects of
\displaystyle X
. Note that
\displaystyle\mathbf{E}X
forms a wide, non-full subcategory of
\displaystyle\mathbf{Bin}(X)
. In particular,
\displaystyle\mathbf{Bin}(X)
is a connected
\displaystyle\infty
-category.

Proposition 6.2
.

The inclusion of
\displaystyle\mathbf{Bin}(X)
into the subcategory
\displaystyle\mathbf{G}^{st.eq}X
of
\displaystyle\mathbf{G}X
spanned by stably equivalent pairs is cofinal. In particular there are equivalences

\Omega X^{\mathrm{gp}}\simeq\Omega|\mathbf{Bin}(X)|\simeq\mathrm{Map}_{\mathbf{Bin}(X)[\mathbf{E}X^{-1}]}(1,1).

Proof.

The monoidal functor
\displaystyle\Delta:\mathbf{E}X\rightarrow\mathbf{G}X
equips
\displaystyle\mathbf{G}X
with an
\displaystyle\mathbf{E}X
-action via multiplication from the left. Note that this functor factors through
\displaystyle\mathbf{Bin}(X)
, hence
\displaystyle\mathbf{Bin}(X)
is stable under this action. Now let
\displaystyle(x,y)
be a stably equivalent pair and denote by
\displaystyle\iota
the inclusion
\displaystyle\mathbf{Bin}(X)
into
\displaystyle\mathbf{G}^{st.eq}X
. Chose
\displaystyle k
such that
\displaystyle k\cdot x\simeq k\cdot y
and denote the resulting arrow
\displaystyle(x,y)\rightarrow(k\cdot x,k\cdot x)
by
\displaystyle\hat{k}
. The arrow
\displaystyle 1\rightarrow k
in
\displaystyle\mathbf{E}X
induces a natural transformation of functors
\displaystyle id_{\mathbf{G}X}\Rightarrow k\otimes-
. This altogether gives maps

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(x,y)/\iota}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(k\cdot x,k\cdot x)/\iota}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces k\otimes-}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\hat{k}^{*}}

together with a homotopy from

to
\displaystyle\hat{k}^{*}\circ k\otimes-
. This implies that
\displaystyle|(x,y)/\iota|
is a retract of
\displaystyle|(k\cdot x,k\cdot x)/\iota|

\displaystyle(k\cdot x,k\cdot x)
lies in the subcategory given by
\displaystyle\mathbf{Bin}(X)

\displaystyle(x,y)/\iota
is contractible for all pairs
\displaystyle(x,y)
that are stably equivalent, which implies that

The claimed equivalences come from the following observation. The
\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-monoid
\displaystyle\Omega X^{\mathrm{gp}}=\Omega|\mathbf{G}X|

\displaystyle\mathrm{Map}_{|\mathbf{G}X|}((1,1),(1,1))

\displaystyle\mathbf{G}^{st.eq}X

\displaystyle\mathbf{Bin}(X)
sits cofinally in this

In order to compute
\displaystyle|\mathbf{Bin}(X)|=\mathbf{Bin}(X)[\mathbf{Bin}(X)^{-1}]

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}X
in
\displaystyle\mathbf{Bin}(X)

∎

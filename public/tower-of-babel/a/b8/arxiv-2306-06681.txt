New asymptotic representations of the noncentral t t -distribution
Amparo Gil, Javier Segura, Nico M. Temme

Abstract
New asymptotic approximations of the non-central t t distribution are given, a generalization of the Student’s t t distribution. Using new integral representations, we give new asymptotic expansions for large values of the noncentrality parameter but also for large values of the degrees of freedom parameter. In some case we accept more than one large parameter. These results are in terms of elementary functions, but also in terms of the complementary error function and the incomplete gamma function. A number of numerical tests demonstrate the performance of the asymptotic approximations.

&=&\displaystyle{{{\lower 2.58334pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 1$}}\over{\raise 3.01385pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 2$}}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\delta^{2}}\frac{\sqrt{y}\,(1-y)^{\frac{1}{2}n}}{2\pi i}\int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty}\frac{e^{\frac{1}{2t}\delta^{2}y}{{\rm erf}}\left(\delta\sqrt{\frac{y}{2t}}\,\right)}{\sqrt{t}\,(1-t)^{\frac{1}{2}n}(t-y)}\,dt.}\end{array}

(3.3)

When we use
{{\rm erf}}\,z=1-{\rm erfc}\,z
, we obtain

Q_{n}(x;\delta)=P_{n}(x;\delta)-{{\lower 2.58334pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 1$}}\over{\raise 3.01385pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 2$}}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\delta^{2}}\frac{\sqrt{y}\,(1-y)^{\frac{1}{2}n}}{2\pi i}\int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty}\frac{e^{\frac{1}{2t}\delta^{2}y}{\rm erfc}\left(\delta\sqrt{\frac{y}{2t}}\,\right)}{\sqrt{t}\,(1-t)^{\frac{1}{2}n}(t-y)}\,dt.

(3.4)

We conclude:

Lemma 3.1

We have derived the following representation

F_{n}(x;\delta)=2P_{n}(x;\delta)+R_{n}(x;\delta),

(3.5)

where

\begin{array}[]{@{}r@{\;}c@{\;}l@{}}R_{n}(x;\delta)&=&{{\lower 2.58334pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 1$}}\over{\raise 2.1097pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 2$}}}{\rm erfc}\left(\delta/\sqrt{2}\right)+Q_{n}(x;\delta)-P_{n}(x;\delta)\\[8.0pt]
&=&{{\lower 2.58334pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 1$}}\over{\raise 2.1097pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 2$}}}{\rm erfc}\left(\delta/\sqrt{2}\right)\ -\\[8.0pt]
&&\displaystyle{{{\lower 2.58334pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 1$}}\over{\raise 3.01385pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 2$}}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\delta^{2}}\frac{\sqrt{y}\,(1-y)^{\frac{1}{2}n}}{2\pi i}\int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty}\frac{e^{\frac{1}{2t}\delta^{2}y}{\rm erfc}\left(\delta\sqrt{\frac{y}{2t}}\,\right)}{\sqrt{t}\,(1-t)^{\frac{1}{2}n}(t-y)}\,dt}\\[8.0pt]
&=&\displaystyle{{{\lower 2.58334pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 1$}}\over{\raise 3.01385pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 2$}}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\delta^{2}}\frac{\sqrt{y}\,(1-y)^{\frac{1}{2}n}}{2\pi i}\int_{d-i\infty}^{d+i\infty}\frac{e^{\frac{1}{2t}\delta^{2}y}{\rm erfc}\left(\delta\sqrt{\frac{y}{2t}}\,\right)}{\sqrt{t}\,(1-t)^{\frac{1}{2}n}(y-t)}\,dt,}\end{array}

(3.6)

where
0<y<c<1
and
0<d<y<1
.

We find, using
{\rm erfc}(-z)=2-{\rm erfc}\,z
and because
P_{n}(x;\delta)
is even with respect to
\delta
and
Q_{n}(x;\delta)
is odd:

F_{n}(x;-\delta)=1-{{\lower 2.58334pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 1$}}\over{\raise 3.01385pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 2$}}}{\rm erfc}\left(\delta/\sqrt{2}\right)+P_{n}(x;\delta)-Q_{n}(x;\delta),

(3.7)

and we conclude

Corollary 3.2

With
R_{n}(x;\delta)
given in (
3.6
), the following representation holds

F_{n}(x;-\delta)=1-R_{n}(x;\delta),\quad G_{n}(x;-\delta)=R_{n}(x;\delta),

(3.8)

where
G_{n}(x;\delta)
is the complementary function defined in (
1.5
).

When we compare this with the representation in (

R_{n}(x;\delta)={{\lower 2.58334pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 1$}}\over{\raise 3.01385pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 2$}}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\delta^{2}}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-\delta/\sqrt{2})^{j}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}j+1\right)}I_{1-y}\left({{\lower 2.58334pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 1$}}\over{\raise 3.01385pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 2$}}}n,{{\lower 2.58334pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 1$}}\over{\raise 3.01385pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 2$}}}+{{\lower 2.58334pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 1$}}\over{\raise 3.01385pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 2$}}}j\right).

(3.9)

This also follows by using in (
3.6

w(z)=e^{-z^{2}}{\rm erfc}(-iz),\quad w(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(iz)^{k}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}k+1\right)},

(3.10)

and the integral representation of the incomplete beta function in (

Theorem 3.3

The function

has the following representation

(3.11)

Proof.

The proof follows from shifting the contour of the second integral in (

{\rm erfc}\,z=\frac{e^{-z^{2}}}{z\sqrt{\pi}}\left(1+{\cal O}\left(z^{-2}\right)\right),\quad z\to\infty.

\square

4
Preliminary observations for large values of
\delta

We will show in this section that we only need to consider the role of
P_{n}(x;\delta)
in the asymptotic analysis of
F_{n}(x;\delta)
for large values of
\delta
.
We explain this in the simple example where we start with the even and odd power series of
e^{x}
:

e^{x}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{2k}}{(2k)!}+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{2k+1}}{(2k+1)!}=2\cosh x+\left(\sinh x-\cosh x\right)=2\cosh x-e^{-x}.

(4.1)

When we want to compute
e^{x}
for large values of
x
by using the Taylor series, it is possible to use twice the expansion of
\cosh x
, with a relative error term of order
e^{-2x}
.

In the present case we have the following theorem.

Theorem 4.1

The noncentral
t
distribution
F_{n}(x;\delta)
defined in (
1.1
) has for large values of the noncentrality parameter
\delta
the asymptotic representation

F_{n}(x;\delta)=2P_{n}(x;\delta)+\frac{e^{-\frac{1}{2}\delta^{2}}\sqrt{y}\,(1-y)^{\frac{1}{2}n}}{2\pi}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}n+\frac{1}{2}\right)}{\zeta^{\frac{1}{2}n+\frac{1}{2}}}\left(1+{\cal O}\left(\delta^{-2}\right)\right),

(4.2)

where
\zeta=\frac{1}{2}\delta^{2}y
, and
y\in(0,1)
and
n
are fixed.

Proof.

The proof follows from (
3.5
), the representation of
R_{n}(x;\delta)
in (
3.11
) and a straightforward application of Watson’s lemma; see
[
19
, Chapter 2]
.

\square

When we compute
F_{n}(x;\delta)
by using the relation in (
3.5
) it is needed to compute
R_{n}(x;\delta)
. However, in the transition case
\delta\sim x
, where
F_{n}(x;\delta)\sim\frac{1}{2}
,
R_{n}(x;\delta)
can be neglected when
\delta
is large enough. This follows from (
4.2
). More details on the asymptotic expansion of
R_{n}(x;\delta)
will be given in Theorem
6.5
of Section

, where we shall see that there is an extra exponentially small behaviour noticeable for large values of
n
and
\delta
.

From Theorem

5.1
An expansion in terms of elementary functions

The result of this subsection is a modification with respect to notation of a result for the noncentral beta distribution as derived in

Theorem 5.1

The function

F_{n}(x;\delta)\sim\frac{e^{-\frac{1}{2}(1-y)\delta^{2}}\sqrt{y}(1-y)^{\frac{1}{2}n}\zeta^{\frac{1}{2}n-1}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}n\right)}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\left(1-{{\lower 2.58334pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 1$}}\over{\raise 3.01385pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 2$}}}n\right)_{k}\frac{c_{k}}{\zeta^{k}},

Remark 5.3

2.

Proof.

6
Asymptotic expansions for large
n

For large values of
n
we consider two cases: one with
\delta
and
x
small with respect to
n
, and a second case with larger values of these parameters.

6.1
Bounded values of
\delta
and
x

We use the representation of
F_{n}(x;\delta)
in (
2.3
) and write

\begin{array}[]{@{}r@{\;}c@{\;}l@{}}F_{n}(x;\delta)&=&\displaystyle{A_{n}e^{-\frac{1}{2}n}\int_{0}^{\infty}E(t)e^{-\frac{1}{2}n\phi(t)}\,\frac{dt}{t},}\\[8.0pt]
A_{n}&=&\displaystyle{\frac{(n/2)^{n/2}}{\Gamma(n/2)},\quad E(t)={\rm erfc}\left((\delta-xt)/\sqrt{2}\right),}\\[8.0pt]
\phi(t)&=&\displaystyle{t^{2}-\ln\left(t^{2}\right)-1,\quad\phi^{\prime}(t)=\frac{2(t^{2}-1)}{t}.}\end{array}

(6.1)

The asymptotic analysis focuses on the positive saddle point
t=1
and to represent the coefficients in a convenient form we first split off a term by writing
E(t)=\left(E(t)-E(1)\right)+E(1)
, where
E(1)={\rm erfc}\left((\delta-x)/\sqrt{2}\right)
. The part with
E(1)
gives

E(1)A_{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{1}{2}nt^{2}}t^{n-1}\,dt={{\lower 2.58334pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 1$}}\over{\raise 3.01385pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 2$}}}{\rm erfc}\left((\delta-x)/\sqrt{2}\right),

(6.2)

and we continue with the following theorem.

Theorem 6.1

For large values of
n
and bounded values of
\delta
and
x
we have the expansion

F_{n}(x;\delta)\sim{{\lower 2.58334pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 1$}}\over{\raise 3.01385pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 2$}}}{\rm erfc}\left((\delta-x)/\sqrt{2}\right)+B(x;\delta)\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{c_{k}}{n^{k}},\quad n\to\infty,

(6.3)

where
B(x;\delta)
and the first coefficients
c_{k}
are given by

\begin{array}[]{ll}&B(x;\delta)=\displaystyle{x\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\,e^{-\frac{1}{2}(\delta-x)^{2}}},\\[8.0pt]
&c_{1}=\frac{1}{8}\left(x\delta-x^{2}-1\right),\\[8.0pt]
&c_{2}=\frac{1}{192}\left(3\delta^{3}x^{3}-9\delta^{2}x^{4}+9\delta x^{5}-3x^{6}-2\delta^{2}x^{2}-5\delta x^{3}+7x^{4}-3\delta x+5x^{2}+3\right).\end{array}

(6.4)

Proof.

With the transformation

\phi(t)={{\lower 2.58334pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 1$}}\over{\raise 3.01385pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 2$}}}w^{2},\quad{\rm sign}(t-1)={\rm sign}(w),

(6.5)

we obtain

\begin{array}[]{@{}r@{\;}c@{\;}l@{}}F_{n}(x;\delta)&=&\displaystyle{{{\lower 2.58334pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 1$}}\over{\raise 3.01385pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 2$}}}{\rm erfc}\left((\delta-x)/\sqrt{2}\right)+A_{n}e^{-\frac{1}{2}n}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{1}{4}nw^{2}}f(w)\,dw,}\\[8.0pt]
f(w)&=&\displaystyle{\frac{E(t)-E(1)}{t}\frac{dt}{dw}.}\end{array}

(6.6)

We invert the relation in (
6.5
) in the form of the expansion

t=1+{{\lower 2.58334pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 1$}}\over{\raise 3.01385pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 2$}}}w+{{\lower 2.58334pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 1$}}\over{\raise 3.01385pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 24$}}}w^{2}+{\cal O}(w^{3}),

(6.7)

which is valid for small
|w|
. Next we expand for small
|t-1|

\frac{E(t)-E(1)}{t}=B(x;\delta)(t-1)\left(1+{{\lower 2.58334pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 1$}}\over{\raise 3.01385pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 2$}}}(\delta x-x^{2}-2)(t-1)+{\cal O}(t-1)^{2})\right),

(6.8)

where
B(x;\delta)
is given in (
6.4
).
With these two expansions we obtain

f(w)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}f_{k}w^{k}=B(x;\delta)\left({{\lower 2.58334pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 1$}}\over{\raise 3.01385pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 4$}}}w+{{\lower 2.58334pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 1$}}\over{\raise 3.01385pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 16$}}}(\delta x-x^{2}-1)w^{2}+{\cal O}(w^{3})\right),

(6.9)

from which we can obtain the first
f_{k}
. With the expansion of
f(w)
we find the asymptotic expansion

F_{n}(x;\delta)\sim{{\lower 2.58334pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 1$}}\over{\raise 3.01385pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 2$}}}{\rm erfc}\left((\delta-x)/\sqrt{2}\right)+\frac{B(x;\delta)}{\Gamma^{*}(n/2)}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{b_{k}}{n^{k}},

(6.10)

where

b_{0}=0,\quad b_{k}=\frac{1}{B(x;\delta)}\left({{\lower 2.58334pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 1$}}\over{\raise 3.01385pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 2$}}}\right)_{k}2^{2k}f_{2k},\quad k=1,2,3,\ldots\,.

(6.11)

Here we used (see (
6.1
))

2A_{n}e^{-\frac{1}{2}n}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{n}}=2\frac{(n/2)^{n/2}}{\Gamma(n/2)}e^{-\frac{1}{2}n}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{n}}=\frac{1}{\Gamma^{*}(n/2)},

(6.12)

and the scaled gamma function
\Gamma^{*}(z)
defined by

\Gamma(z)=\sqrt{2\pi}\,e^{-z}z^{z-\frac{1}{2}}\Gamma^{*}(z).

(6.13)

The first coefficients
b_{k}
are

\begin{array}[]{@{}r@{\;}c@{\;}l@{}}b_{0}&=&0,\quad\displaystyle{b_{1}={{\lower 2.58334pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 1$}}\over{\raise 3.01385pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 8$}}}\left(x\delta-x^{2}-1\right),}\\[8.0pt]
b_{2}&=&\frac{1}{192}\left(3\delta^{3}x^{3}-9\delta^{2}x^{4}+9\delta x^{5}-3x^{6}-2\delta^{2}x^{2}-5\delta x^{3}+7x^{4}+\delta x+x^{2}-1\right).\end{array}

(6.14)

Because
n
is large we can use the asymptotic expansion of the scaled gamma function. We have
(see
[
19
, Chapter 6]
)

\Gamma^{*}\left({{\lower 2.58334pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 1$}}\over{\raise 3.01385pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 2$}}}n\right)\sim\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_{k}}{n^{k}},\quad\frac{1}{\Gamma^{*}\left(\frac{1}{2}n\right)}\sim\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\frac{a_{k}}{n^{k}},\quad n\to\infty,

(6.15)

with first coefficients
a_{0}=1
,
a_{1}=\frac{1}{6}
,
a_{2}=\frac{1}{72}
,
a_{3}={{\lower 2.58334pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 139$}}\over{\raise 2.1097pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 6480$}}}
,
and we can write the expansion in (
6.10
) as in (
6.3
),
where the
c_{k}
follow from the formal power series multiplication

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{c_{k}}{n^{k}}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\frac{a_{k}}{n^{k}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{b_{k}}{n^{k}},

(6.16)

which gives

c_{0}=0,\quad c_{k}=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(-1)^{j}a_{j}b_{k-j},\quad k\geq 1.

(6.17)

\square

Theorem 6.2

Consider the integral of

given in (
3.13

\begin{array}[]{@{}r@{\;}c@{\;}l@{}}P_{n}(x;\delta)&=&\displaystyle{{{\lower 2.58334pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 1$}}\over{\raise 3.01385pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 2$}}}\frac{\sqrt{y}\,e^{-\frac{1}{2}n\phi(1/y)}}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{(1+)}e^{\frac{1}{2}n\phi(t)}\,\frac{dt}{(1-yt)\sqrt{t}},}\\[8.0pt]

(6.19)

Let
t_{0}
be the positive saddle point that follows from

\phi^{\prime}(t)=\frac{zt^{2}-zt-1}{t(t-1)}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad t_{0}=\frac{z+\sqrt{z(z+4)}}{2z},

(6.20)

and let the real number
\eta
be defined by

\phi(1/y)-\phi(t_{0})=\eta^{2},\quad{\rm sign}(\eta)={\rm sign}(1/y-t_{0}).

(6.21)

Then, as

we have the asymptotic result

P_{n}(x;\delta)\sim{{\lower 2.58334pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 1$}}\over{\raise 3.01385pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 4$}}}{\rm erfc}\left(\eta\sqrt{n/2}\right)+{{\lower 2.58334pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 1$}}\over{\raise 3.01385pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 2$}}}\frac{e^{-\frac{1}{2}n\eta^{2}}}{\sqrt{2\pi n}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{c_{k}}{n^{k}},\quad n\to\infty.

(6.22)

The first two coefficients
c_{k}
are given in the proof.

relative to the pole at
1/y

, that is, if
\delta=x

. Initially we assume that

Proof.

(6.23)

with condition

\begin{array}[]{@{}r@{\;}c@{\;}l@{}}P_{n}(x;\delta)&=&\displaystyle{{{\lower 2.58334pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 1$}}\over{\raise 3.01385pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 2$}}}\frac{e^{-\frac{1}{2}n\left(\phi(1/y)-\phi(t_{0})\right)}}{2\pi i}\int_{-i\infty}^{+i\infty}e^{{{\lower 1.80835pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 1$}}\over{\raise 1.50693pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 2$}}}ns^{2}}f(s)\,ds,}\\[8.0pt]
f(s)&=&\displaystyle{\frac{\sqrt{y}}{(1-yt)\sqrt{t}}\frac{dt}{ds}}=\displaystyle{\sqrt{\frac{\rho}{t}}\,\frac{1}{\rho-t}\frac{dt}{ds},\quad\rho=\frac{1}{y}.}\end{array}

(6.24)

The pole at
1/y
in the

\eta

-plane, and
\eta
follows from the equation given in (

Because we have assumed

We split off the pole, writing

P_{n}(x;\delta)={{\lower 2.58334pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 1$}}\over{\raise 3.01385pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 4$}}}{\rm erfc}\left(\eta\sqrt{n/2}\right)+{{\lower 2.58334pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 1$}}\over{\raise 3.01385pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 2$}}}\frac{e^{-\frac{1}{2}n\eta^{2}}}{2\pi i}\int_{-i\infty}^{+i\infty}e^{\frac{1}{2}ns^{2}}g(s)\,ds,

(6.27)

where we have used (see

Theorem 6.5

Let us define

\psi(t)=zt-\ln t+\ln(1+t),\quad t>0,\quad z=y\sigma^{2}.

Then, as

R_{n}(x;\delta)\sim\frac{e^{-\frac{1}{2}\delta^{2}}\sqrt{y}\,(1-y)^{\frac{1}{2}n}}{\sqrt{2\pi n}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}n\psi(t_{p})}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{d_{k}}{n^{k}},\quad

(6.35)

where

is the positive saddle point that follows from

\psi^{\prime}(t)=\frac{zt^{2}+zt-1}{t(1+t)}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad t_{p}=\frac{-z+\sqrt{z(z+4)}}{2z}.

(6.36)

The first coefficients
d_{k}
are given in the proof.

We assume that
x
and
\delta
are bounded. In particular, when
\delta
is large,

will be large, and the saddle point

will approach the origin, where the integral has a singularity. Other asymptotic methods can be used in which

-Bessel functions are the main approximants, similarly as for the Kummer
U

[
19

.

Proof.

We write the integral of
R_{n}(x;\delta)
as

R_{n}(x;\delta)=\frac{e^{-\frac{1}{2}\delta^{2}}\sqrt{y}\,(1-y)^{\frac{1}{2}n}}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{1}{2}n\psi(t)}\,\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}\,(1+yt)}.

(6.37)

The transformation

\psi(t)-\psi(t_{p})=w^{2},\quad{\rm sign}(t-t_{p})={\rm sign}(w),

(6.38)

gives

\begin{array}[]{@{}r@{\;}c@{\;}l@{}}R_{n}(x;\delta)&=&\displaystyle{\frac{e^{-\frac{1}{2}\delta^{2}}\sqrt{y}\,(1-y)^{\frac{1}{2}n}}{2\pi}e^{-\frac{1}{2}n\psi(t_{p})}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{1}{2}nw^{2}}h(w)\,dw,}\\[8.0pt]
h(w)&=&\displaystyle{\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}\,(1+yt)}\frac{dt}{dw}.}\end{array}

(6.39)

To find the coefficients we can use a standard method, first by determining the coefficients

of the expansion
\displaystyle{t=t_{p}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}r_{k}w^{k}}
and from these we can find the coefficients

of the expansion
\displaystyle{h(w)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}h_{k}w^{k}}
. Substitution of this series in (

) gives the asymptotic expansion in (

) with coefficients
\displaystyle{d_{k}=2^{k}\left({{\lower 2.58334pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 1$}}\over{\raise 3.01385pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 2$}}}\right)_{k}h_{2k}}
. The first coefficients are

\begin{array}[]{@{}r@{\;}c@{\;}l@{}}d_{0}&=&h_{0}=\displaystyle{\frac{r_{1}}{\sqrt{t_{p}}\,(1+yt_{p})},}\\[8.0pt]
r_{1}&=&\displaystyle{\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{\psi^{\prime\prime}(t_{p})}}=\frac{\sqrt{2}\,t_{p}(t_{p}+1)}{\sqrt{2t_{p}+1}}=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{z\sqrt{z(z+4)}}},}\\[8.0pt]

b_{3}&=&6y(11y-31),\quad b_{4}=3y(43y-72),\quad b_{5}=24y(3y-4).\end{array}

(6.40)

\square

7.1
Using the defining series

The series expansions in (
1.1
) is an excellent starting point to compute the functions

P_{n}(x;\delta)
and
Q_{n}(x;\delta)
when the noncentrality parameter
\delta
is small. The incomplete beta functions can be computed by using the recurrence relation

(p+j)f_{j+1}=\left(p+j+(p+j+q-1)y\right)f_{j}-(p+j+q-1)yf_{j-1},

(7.1)

where
f_{j}=I_{y}(p+j,q)
. Initial values for the recursion can be computed using, for example, our recent algorithm
[
3
]
.
It should be observed that the recursion is not stable in the forward direction, but we can use a backward recursion scheme (see
[
4
, §4.6]
). Because
0<I_{y}(p,q)<1
the convergence of the series is always better than that of the Taylor series of
e^{\frac{1}{2}\delta^{2}}
, and we may obtain a starting value of the backward recursion by examining the series of this exponential function.

For example, when we take
x=5
,
n=10
, and
\delta=7.5
, we need 73 terms in the series of

in (
1.1
) to get

\frac{p_{72}I_{y}(72+\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}n)}{\sum_{j=0}^{72}p_{j}I_{y}(j+\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}n)}<\varepsilon,\quad p_{j}=\frac{z^{j}}{j!},\quad z={{\lower 2.58334pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 1$}}\over{\raise 3.01385pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle 2$}}}\delta^{2},\quad\varepsilon=10^{-16}.

(7.2)

The smallest
j_{0}
for which
e^{-z}\sum_{j}^{j_{0}}p_{j}<\varepsilon
is
j_{0}=85
. A simple inversion method to find
j_{0}
numerically follows from solving for
j>z
the equation

e^{-z}\frac{z^{j}}{e^{-j}j^{j}}=\varepsilon.

(7.3)

In a second example we take
x=15
,
n=510
, and
\delta=17.5
, and we need 251 terms in the series of
P_{n}(x;\delta)
in (
1.1
). By solving the equation in (
7.3

j\doteq 271

In this way, by solving the equation in (

j
-value to start the backward recursion for the evaluation of the series in (

P_{n}(x;\delta)

In

Entropy of Compositions of Covering Correspondences
V. Matus de la Parra

Abstract
We use the measure theoretical entropy from [ 14 ] and the topological entropies from [ 8 ] and [ 11 ] to prove that the equidistribution measures μ − \mu_{-} and μ + \mu_{+} found in [ 13 ] maximize entropy for the 1 1 -parameter family { ℱ a } a \{\mathcal{F}_{a}\}_{a} studied in [ 4 ] . We find as well a measure of maximal entropy for the composition of two deleted covering correspondences studied in [ 3 ] .

1
Holomorphic Correspondences

In this section we will define holomorphic correspondences on compact complex manifolds, as well as covering correspondences. These have been studied by Bullett and his collaborators in several articles, and are the main objects of study in this work.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
X
be a compact Riemann surface. A
holomorphic
1
-chain
in
X\times X
is a formal sum
\sum_{j=1}^{N}n_{j}\Gamma_{j}
of distinct irreducible complex subvarieties
\Gamma_{j}
of dimension
1
, where
n_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. Let
\pi_{i}:X\times X\to X
be the projection to the
i
-th coordinate,
i=1,2
.

Suppose that
{\left.\kern-1.2pt\pi_{i}\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{\Gamma_{j}}}
is surjective, for
i=1,2
and
j=1,\cdots,N
, and that
\pi_{1}^{-1}(z)\cap\Gamma_{j}
is finite for all
z\in X
and all
1\leq j\leq N
. The
holomorphic correspondence
F
given by
\Gamma
is the multivalued map

F(z)\coloneqq\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{N}\pi_{2}\left(\pi_{1}^{-1}(z)\cap\Gamma_{j}\right).

We say
\Gamma
is the graph of the holomorphic correspondence
F
. The projections
{\left.\kern-1.2pt\pi_{i}\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{\Gamma_{j}}}
are proper, for
1\leq i\leq 2
,
1\leq j\leq N
. The correspondence is said to be
(d_{1}:d_{2})
, where
d_{1}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}n_{j}\deg(\pi_{2})
and
d_{2}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}n_{j}\deg(\pi_{1})
. That is,
d_{1}
is the number of images of a point, and
d_{2}
is the number of pre-images of a point, both counted with multiplicity.

Remark 1.2
.

Since
X
is compact, then
\pi_{2}:X\times X\to X
is closed. Moreover,
\pi_{1}
is continuous and
\Gamma\subset X\times X
is closed. Hence, for ever closed set
C\subset X
, we have that

F(C)=\pi_{2}(\pi_{1}^{-1}(C)\cap\Gamma)

is closed.

Definition 1.3
.

For a rational map
R:\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
of degree
\deg{R}=r
, we define its
deleted covering correspondence

\operatorname{Cov^{R}_{0}}
on
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
to be the
(r-1:r-1)
correspondence whose graph is the curve

\Gamma_{R}\coloneqq\overline{\{(z,w)\in(\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\times\widehat{\mathbb{C}})\smallsetminus\mathfrak{D}_{\widehat{\mathbb{C}}}:R(z)=R(w)\}},

where
\mathfrak{D}_{\widehat{\mathbb{C}}}\coloneqq\{(z,z):z\in\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\}
is the diagonal in
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\times\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
, and the closure is taken in
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\times\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
.

Remark 1.4
.

It is clear from the definition that
(z,w)\in\Gamma_{R}
if and only if
(w,z)\in\Gamma_{R}
. That is,
\Gamma_{R}
is symmetric with respect to the diagonal
\mathfrak{D}_{\widehat{\mathbb{C}}}=\{(z,z)|z\in\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\}
of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\times\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
.

Another useful way to think of
\Gamma_{R}
is as the zero set of the map

\frac{R(z)-R(w)}{z-w}.

That is,
\operatorname{Cov^{Q}_{0}}
relates pre-images under
R
, deleting the obvious relation of
z
with itself. Note that it is still possible for some
(z,z)
to belong to
\Gamma
.

Observe that the only involutions on
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
are the identity and those of the form
J(z)=\frac{Az+B}{Cz-A}
, for constants
A,B,C
so that
A
and
C
do not vanish simultaneously.

Proposition 1.5
.

On
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
, every involution other than the identity is the deleted covering correspondence of some quadratic rational map, and vice versa. Moreover, the involution fixes infinity if and only if the quadratic map is a polynomial.

Proof.

Put
R(z)=\frac{az^{2}+bz+c}{dz^{2}+ez+f}
of degree
2
, and note that for
z,w\notin R^{-1}(\infty)
,
z\neq w
,

\frac{R(z)-R(w)}{z-w}=\frac{(ae-bd)zw+(af-cd)(z+w)+(bf-ce)}{(dz^{2}+ez+f)(dw^{2}+ew+f)}.

Since neither
(dz^{2}+ez+f)
or
(dw^{2}+ew+f)
vanish,

\operatorname{Cov^{R}_{0}}(z)=\overline{\{w|(ae-bd)zw+(af-cd)(z+w)+(bf-ce)=0\}}.

This yields that
\operatorname{Cov^{R}_{0}}(z)=\frac{(cd-af)z+(ce-bf)}{(ae-bd)z-(cd-af)}
. This equality extends to the closure. Therefore,
\operatorname{Cov^{R}_{0}}
is an involution different from the identity.

On the other hand, for
J(z)=\frac{Az+B}{Cz-A}
be an involution other than the identity. Making
a,b,c,d,e,f
be so that
cd-af=A
,
ce-bf=B
and
ae-bd=C
we get that
J=\operatorname{Cov^{R}_{0}}
for
R(z)
as above.

Now observe that for a polynomial
P(z)=az^{2}+bz+c
with
a\neq 0
, we have that
\frac{P(z)-P(w)}{z-w}=a(z+w)+b,

which implies that

\operatorname{Cov^{P}_{0}}(z)=\{w:az+aw+b=0\}=\left\{\frac{1}{a}(-az-b)\right\}=\left\{-z-\frac{b}{a}\right\}.

By the symmetry of
\operatorname{Cov^{P}_{0}}
, it is an involution. Moreover,
\operatorname{Cov^{P}_{0}}
clearly fixes
\infty
. If
J(z)=\frac{Az+B}{Cz-A}
be an involution other than the identity that fixes infinity. Then
C=0
and

J(z)=-z+\frac{B}{A}.

Taking
P(z)=Az^{2}+Bz
we get that
\operatorname{Cov^{P}_{0}}(z)=J(z)
.
∎

Definition 1.6
.

Let
\Gamma=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}n_{i}\Gamma(i)
be the graph of a holomorphic correspondence
F
on a compact Riemann surface
X
. We denote

A_{j}(\Gamma)\coloneqq\bigcup_{i}A_{j}(\Gamma(i)),

where each
A_{j}(\Gamma(i))
is defined to be

A_{j}(\Gamma)\coloneqq\left\{\alpha\in\Gamma:\mbox{ for all open neighborhoods }W\mbox{ of }\alpha,{\left.\kern-1.2pt\pi_{j}\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{W\cap\Gamma}}\mbox{ is not injective}\right\}.

We call
A_{2}(\Gamma)
(respectively,
A_{1}(\Gamma)
) the set of
ramification points
of
F
(respectively, of
F^{-1}
). We define as well
B_{j}(\Gamma)=\pi_{j}(A_{j}(\Gamma))
, and we call
B_{2}(\Gamma)
(respectively,
B_{1}(\Gamma)
) the set of
critical values
of
F
(respectively,
F^{-1}
).

Denote by
\operatorname{CritPt}(R)
the set of critical points of the rational map
R
. We have the following.

Proposition 1.7
.

Let
R
be a rational map on
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
with degree
\deg(R)\geq 2
, and let
\Gamma_{R}
be the graph of the deleted covering correspondence associated to
R
. Then:

1.

A_{2}\left(\Gamma_{R}\right)=\left\{(z,w)\in\Gamma_{R}:z\in\operatorname{CritPt}(R)\right\}
.

2.

A_{1}\left(\Gamma_{R}\right)=\left\{(z,w)\in\Gamma_{R}:w\in\operatorname{CritPt}(R)\right\}
.

3.

\operatorname{Cov^{R}_{0}}
sends open sets to open sets.

Proof.

Using implicit differentiation on the equation
\frac{R(z)-R(w)}{z-w}=0
we get that

\frac{dw}{dz}=\frac{(R(z)-R(w))-R^{\prime}(z)(z-w)}{(R(z)-R(w))-R^{\prime}(w)(z-w)}.

Therefore,
\frac{dw}{dz}=0
if and only if
R^{\prime}(z)=\frac{R(z)-R(w)}{z-w}
, but since
(z,w)\in\Gamma_{\operatorname{Cov^{R}_{0}}}
, then
z\in\operatorname{CritPt}(R)
. This proves part 1.

Part 2 follows from part 1 and the fact that
\Gamma_{R}
is symmetric with respect to the diagonal, since
\operatorname{Cov^{R}_{0}}
is a symmetric relation.

Now we proceed to prove part 3. Let
U\subset\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
be open and put
(z_{0},w_{0})\in\pi_{1}^{-1}(U)\cap\Gamma_{R}
. We will show that
w_{0}\in\operatorname{int}(\operatorname{Cov^{R}_{0}}(U))
. The case where
(z_{0},w_{0})\notin A_{1}(\Gamma_{R})\cap A_{2}(\Gamma_{R})
is identical to the case where
R(z)=z^{3}-3z
(see
[
13
, Remark 2.6]
). Now let
(z_{0},w_{0})
belong to
A_{1}(\Gamma_{R})\cap A_{2}(\Gamma_{R})
. Then both
z_{0}
and
w_{0}
are critical points of
R
. Let
f(z,w)=\frac{R(z)-R(w)}{z-w}
and note that

\frac{\partial f}{\partial z}=\frac{(R^{\prime}(z)-R^{\prime}(w)\frac{dw}{dz})(z-w)-(R(z)-R(w))(1-\frac{dw}{dz})}{(z-w)^{2}}=-\frac{R(z)-R(w)}{(z-w)^{2}}.

Therefore
\frac{\partial f}{\partial z}(z_{0},w_{0})=0
, and similarly,
\frac{\partial f}{\partial w}(z_{0},w_{0})=0
. By the definition of
\Gamma_{R}
, we have that
(z_{0},w_{0})
is a singular point of
\Gamma_{R}
.

By composing with a translation
t
, we can suppose that the singular point is the origin replacing
R
by
R_{\tau}
. We blow-up
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
at the singularity
(0,0)
by taking

\operatorname{Bl}_{(0,0)}(\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1})\coloneqq\{(z,w,(Z:W))\in(\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1})\times\mathbb{P}^{1}|zW=wZ\}

and
\beta:\operatorname{Bl}_{(0,0)}(\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1})\to\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
be the projection given by
(z,w,(Z:W))\mapsto(z,w)
.

Put
\widetilde{\Gamma_{R_{\tau}}}\coloneqq\overline{\beta^{-1}(\Gamma_{R_{\tau}}\smallsetminus\{(0,0)\})}
and note that
\beta^{-1}(\Gamma_{R_{\tau}}\smallsetminus\{(0,0)\})=\widetilde{\Gamma_{R_{\tau}}}\smallsetminus\beta^{-1}(0,0)
.

Let
p_{j}:(\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1})\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
be the projection to the
j
-th coordinate for
j=1,2
, and let
U\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}
be an open neighborhood of
z=0
. We have that
\pi_{1}\circ\beta=p_{1}
and

\beta^{-1}(\pi_{1}^{-1}(U)\cap(\Gamma_{R_{\tau}}\smallsetminus\{(0,0)\}))=\pi_{1}^{-1}(U)\cap(\widetilde{\Gamma_{R_{\tau}}}\smallsetminus\beta^{-1}(0,0)).

Similarly, we have that
p_{2}=\pi_{2}\circ\beta
. We proceed to show that
\pi_{2}(\pi_{1}^{-1}(U)\cap\Gamma_{R_{\tau}})
is open. Indeed,

\displaystyle\pi_{2}(\pi_{1}^{-1}(U)\cap\Gamma_{R_{\tau}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p_{2}\circ\beta^{-1}(\beta(p_{1}^{-1}(U)\cap(\widetilde{\Gamma_{R_{\tau}}}\smallsetminus\beta^{-1}(0,0)))\cup(\beta\circ p_{1}^{-1}(0)\cap\widetilde{\Gamma_{R_{\tau}}}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p_{2}((p_{1}^{-1}(U)\cap(\widetilde{\Gamma_{R_{\tau}}}\smallsetminus\beta^{-1}(0,0))\cup\beta^{-1}(0,0)))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p_{2}(p_{1}^{-1}(U)\cap\widetilde{\Gamma_{R_{\tau}}}).

After finitely many blow-ups, the proper transform of the curve is non-singular. Suppose without of generality that it happens after the first blow-up, meaning that
\widetilde{\Gamma_{R_{\tau}}}
is non-singular. Observe that the translation does not affect whether or not a point is singular. Let

f_{1}(z,w,(Z:W))\coloneqq zW-Zw\hskip 28.45274pt\mbox{ and }\hskip 28.45274ptf_{2}(z,w,(Z:W))\coloneqq\frac{R_{\tau}(z)-R_{\tau}(w)}{z-w}.

Let
u
be an affine coordinate of
(Z:W)
on a neighborhood of a point
(0,0,u(0,0))\in\beta^{-1}(0,0)\cap\widetilde{\Gamma_{R_{\tau}}}
. There are no solutions to

\frac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial z}=\frac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial w}=\frac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial u}=0.

Since
\widetilde{\Gamma_{R_{\tau}}}
is non-singular and
\frac{\partial f_{2}}{\partial u}=0
, this implies that either
\frac{\partial f_{2}}{\partial z}\neq 0
or
\frac{\partial f_{2}}{\partial w}\neq 0
.

We prove by contrapositive that this implies that either
\frac{dw}{dz}\neq 0
or
\frac{dz}{dw}\neq 0
. Indeed, note that

\frac{\partial f_{2}}{\partial z}=\frac{(R^{\prime}_{\tau}(z)-R^{\prime}_{\tau}(w)\frac{dw}{dz})(z-w)-(R_{\tau}(z)-R_{\tau}(w))(1-\frac{dw}{dz})}{(z-w)^{2}}.

From Proposition
1.7
we have that if
\frac{dw}{dz}=\frac{dz}{dw}=0
, then
R^{\prime}_{\tau}(z)=R^{\prime}_{\tau}(w)=0
. Thus,

\frac{\partial f_{2}}{\partial z}=-\frac{R_{\tau}(z)-R_{\tau}(w)}{(z-w)^{2}}=0\hskip 28.45274pt\mbox{ and }\hskip 28.45274pt\frac{\partial f_{2}}{\partial z}=\frac{R_{\tau}(z)-R_{\tau}(w)}{(z-w)^{2}}=0.

Suppose without loss of generality that
\frac{dw}{dz}\neq 0
. The Implicit Function Theorem gives the existance of a neighborhood
\Omega
of
0
and a holomorphic function
g:\Omega\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
so that the graph
\widetilde{\Gamma_{R_{\tau}}}
is locally given by
\{(z,w,u(z,w)):w=g(z)\}
at
(0,0,u(0,0))
. Since
\Gamma_{R_{\tau}}
contains no lines, then
g
is not constant, and hence it is open. Therefore,
(z_{0},w_{0},u(z_{0},w_{0}))\in\operatorname{int}(p_{2}(p_{1}^{-1}(U)\cap\widetilde{\Gamma_{R_{\tau}}}))
. We have that
\pi_{2}(\pi_{1}^{-1}(U)\cap\Gamma_{R_{\tau}})
is open, as desired, and then so is
\operatorname{Cov^{R}_{0}}
.
∎

2
Composition of Covering Correspondences

In this section we will define the families
\{\mathcal{F}_{a}\}_{a\in\mathcal{K}}
and
\{\mathcal{F}_{R,S}\}_{(R,S)\in\mathcal{K}^{\prime}}
whose topological and metric entropy we compute in this work. In addition, we prove Theorem
0.1
. In order to do so, we start by defining the composition of holomorphic correspondences.

Let
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
be two holomorphic correspondences on a compact Riemann surface
X
, whose graphs are
\Gamma_{F_{1}}
and
\Gamma_{F_{2}}
, respectively. Let
\Gamma_{F_{1}\circ F_{2}}
be the projection of
(\Gamma_{F_{1}}\times\Gamma_{F_{2}})\cap\{z_{2}=z_{3}\}
, to the first and fourth coordinates (counting multiplicities). The composition of
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
is the holomorphic correspondence
F_{1}\circ F_{2}
whose graph is
\Gamma_{F_{1}\circ F_{2}}
.

In terms of multivalued maps,

F_{1}\circ F_{2}(z)=\bigcup_{w\in F_{2}(z)}F_{1}(w).

For
a\neq 1
, we take the involution

\operatorname{J}_{a}(z)=\frac{(a+1)z-2a}{2z-(a+1)}

and define
\mathcal{F}_{a}
to be the correspondence given by
\mathcal{F}_{a}\coloneqq\operatorname{J}_{a}\circ\operatorname{Cov^{Q}_{0}}
, where
Q(z)=z^{3}-3z
. Observe that
d_{1}(\mathcal{F}_{a})=d_{2}(\mathcal{F}_{a})=2
and that
\mathcal{F}_{a}(1)=\{1\}
with multiplicity
2
. In the next section we will talk about the behavior around this fixed point, which is work by Bullett-Lomonaco
[
4
]
. We note that, from the previous section, we can write
\operatorname{J}_{a}(z)=\operatorname{Cov^{R}_{0}}
, where
R(z)=\frac{z^{2}-a}{(z-1)^{2}}.

We define as well
\mathcal{F}_{R,S}\coloneqq\operatorname{Cov^{R}_{0}}\circ\operatorname{Cov^{S}_{0}}
for
R,S
rational maps of degree greater than or equal to
2
. It is immediate from Proposition
1.5
that
\{\mathcal{F}_{a}\}_{a\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{1\}}\subset\{\mathcal{F}_{R,S}\}_{R,S}
, where the latter runs over all rational maps
R
and
S
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
Klein combination pair

(\Delta_{\operatorname{J}_{a}},\Delta_{\operatorname{Cov^{Q}_{0}}})
for
\mathcal{F}_{a}
is a pair of open subsets of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
so that
\operatorname{J}_{a}(\Delta_{\operatorname{J}_{a}})\cap\Delta_{\operatorname{J}_{a}}=\emptyset
,
\operatorname{Cov^{Q}_{0}}(\Delta_{\operatorname{Cov^{Q}_{0}}})\cap\Delta_{\operatorname{Cov^{Q}_{0}}}=\emptyset,
and

\Delta_{\operatorname{J}_{a}}\cup\Delta_{\operatorname{Cov^{Q}_{0}}}=\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{1\}.

We denote by
\mathcal{K}
the collection of
a\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{1\}
for which there exists a Klein combination pair
(\Delta_{\operatorname{J}_{a}},\Delta_{\operatorname{Cov^{Q}_{0}}})
for
\mathcal{F}_{a}
.

The restricted family
\{\mathcal{F}_{a}\}_{a\in\mathcal{K}}
is described as a mating of parabolic quadratic rational maps and
\operatorname{PSL}2(\mathbb{Z})
in
[
4
]
.

Definition 2.2
.

A
transversal

\Delta_{R}\subset\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
for
\operatorname{Cov^{R}_{0}}
is a maximal set where the rational map
R
is injective.

A
Klein combination pair

(\Delta_{R},\Delta_{S})
of
\mathcal{F}_{R,S}
is a pair of subsets of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
so that:

•

\Delta_{R}
and
\Delta_{S}
are transversals for
R
and
S
, respectively,

•

\overline{\operatorname{int}(\Delta_{R})}=\overline{\Delta_{R}}
and
\overline{\operatorname{int}(\Delta_{S})}=\overline{\Delta_{S}}
,

•

\operatorname{int}(\Delta_{R})\cup\operatorname{int}(\Delta_{S})=\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
,

•

\mathcal{F}_{R,S}(\Delta_{S})

Definition 2.3

Definition 2.4

Let

We define

For

Proposition 2.5
.

Let

(a)

(b)

\|\frac{1}{d}F^{*}\phi\|_{L^{2}}=\|\phi\|_{L^{2}}

(c)

Proof of Theorem
0.1
.

We will use the above proposition to show that

is weakly-modular. Indeed, the
(1,0)
-form
\phi_{R}(z)=e^{-|R(z)|}dz
satisfies

\Big|\int_{\widehat{\mathbb{C}}}e^{-|R(z)|}\omega_{FS}\Big|\leq\int_{\widehat{\mathbb{C}}}\omega_{FS}=\pi.

Therefore
\phi_{R}\in L^{2}_{(1,0)}
. Let
U\subset\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus A_{2}(\Gamma_{R})

\operatorname{Cov^{R}_{0}}
sends

\|\frac{1}{(\deg R-1)}\operatorname{Cov^{R}_{0}}^{*}\phi_{R}\|_{L^{2}}=\|\phi_{R}\|_{L^{2}}
and

By Proposition
2.5

\mathcal{F}_{R,S}
is weakly-modular.

Now observe that

\left(\operatorname{Cov^{R}_{0}}\circ\operatorname{Cov^{S}_{0}}\right)(\Delta_{S})\subseteq\operatorname{Cov^{R}_{0}}\left(\operatorname{int}\left(\Delta_{R}\right)\right)\subsetneq\operatorname{int}\left(\Delta_{S}\right).

Suppose that

was modular. Then there would be a Borel measure

that assigns positive measure to nonempty open sets, and so that

However,

\operatorname{Cov^{R}_{0}}\circ\operatorname{Cov^{S}_{0}}(\overline{\Delta_{S}})=\pi_{2}((\overline{\Delta_{S}}\times\widehat{\mathbb{C}})\cap\Gamma)

is closed, and hence

∎

3.2
Topological Entropy

Let
F:X\to 2^{X}
be a multivalued map on a compact metric space
(X,\operatorname{dist})
. For
x\in X
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we define

\operatorname{Orb}_{n}(F)=\{(x_{0},x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1})\in X^{n}:x_{i}\in F(x_{i-1})\mbox{ for all }1\leq i\leq n-1\}.

We say that
A\subset\operatorname{Orb}_{n}(F)
is
\epsilon
-separated in the sense of Kelly-Tennant
if for every two distinct
(x_{0},x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1}),(y_{0},y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-1})\in A
, there exists
0\leq i\leq n-1
so that
\operatorname{dist}(x_{i},y_{i})\geq\epsilon
. Given
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\epsilon>0
, we define
s_{n,\epsilon}(F)
to be the largest cardinality of an
\epsilon
-separated set collection of
n
-orbits.
In
[
11
]
, they defined
topological entropy
of the multivalued map
F
as the quantity

h_{top}(F)\coloneqq\lim\limits_{\epsilon\to 0}\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log(s_{n,\epsilon}(F)).

The multivalued map
F
is said to be
surjective
if for all
y\in X
, there exists
x\in X
so that
y\in F(x)
. They prove that whenever
F
is surjective, we have that
h_{top}(F^{-1})=h_{top}(F)
, where
F^{-1}
is the multivalued map assigning
F^{-1}(y)=\{x\in X:y\in F(x)\}
.

In the case of a holomorphic correspondence
F
with graph
\Gamma
on a compact Riemann surface
X
, Dinh-Sibony
[
8
]
defined topological entropy as follows. Write
\Gamma=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\Gamma_{j}
for the graph of
F
, where the
\Gamma_{j}^{\prime}s
are allowed to be repeated. For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, an
n
-orbit is a
(2n+1)
-tuple

(x_{0},x_{1},\cdots,x_{n};j_{1},j_{2},\cdots,j_{n})\in X^{n+1}\times\{1,\cdots,N\}^{n}

satisfying that for each
1\leq i\leq n
,
(x_{i-1},x_{i})\in\Gamma_{j_{i}}
. Put
\epsilon>0
. We say that a collection
\mathcal{A}
of
n
-orbits is
\epsilon
-separated in the sense of Dinh-Sibony
if for all

(x_{0},x_{1},\cdots,x_{n};j_{1},j_{2},\cdots,j_{n}),(y_{0},y_{1},\cdots,y_{n};k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{n})\in\mathcal{A},

either
\operatorname{dist}(x_{i},y_{i})>\epsilon\mbox{ for some }0\leq i\leq n
, or
j_{i}\neq k_{i}
for some
1\leq i\leq n
, where
\operatorname{dist}(\cdot,\cdot)
denotes a distance on
X
. We denote

N_{\Gamma}(\epsilon,n)=\max\{|\mathcal{A}|:\mathcal{A}\mbox{ is an }\epsilon\mbox{-separated collection of }n\mbox{-orbits}\}.

Definition 3.5
.

The
topological entropy
of
\Gamma
is defined to be the number

h(\Gamma)\coloneqq\sup\limits_{\epsilon>0}\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log N_{\Gamma}(\epsilon,n).

By compactness of
X
, every
\epsilon
-separated family of
n
-orbits is finite. Moreover,
h(\Gamma)
does not depend on the choice of
\operatorname{dist}(\cdot,\cdot)
.

The following result is from
[
8
]
. They state a more general result that is a generalization of Gromov’s result in
[
10
]
to meromorphic correspondences on compact Kähler manifolds.

Theorem 3.6

(Gromov’s Inequality)
.

Let
X
be a compact Riemann surface and let
\Gamma
be the graph of a holomorphic correspondence
F
on
X
. Then

h(\Gamma)\leq\log\max\{d_{1}(F),d_{2}(F)\}.

Observe that both
h_{top}
and
h
generalize the topological entropy of maps. Furthermore, holomorphic correspondences can be thought of as multivalued maps, and we have the following lemma.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
X
be a compact Riemann surface and let
F
be a holomorphic correspondence on
X
with graph
\Gamma
. Then
h_{top}(F)\leq h(\Gamma)
.

Proof.

Put
\Gamma=\sum_{i=1}^{m}n_{i}\Gamma_{i}
. Note that if
(x_{0},x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1})\in\operatorname{Orb}_{n}(F)
, then there exists
(j_{1},\cdots,j_{n-1})\in\{1,\cdots,m\}^{n-1}
so that

(x_{0},x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1};j_{1},\cdots,j_{n-1})

satisfies that for each
1\leq i\leq n-1
,
(x_{i-1},x_{i})\in\Gamma_{j_{i}}
. Thus, if
A\subset\operatorname{Orb}_{n}(F)
is
\epsilon
-separated in the sense of Kelly-Tennant, then

\mathcal{A}=\{(x_{0},x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1};j_{1},\cdots,j_{n-1}):(x_{0},x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1})\in A\}

obtained this way, is a
\delta
-separated collection of
(n-1)
-orbits in the sense of Dinh-Sibony, for all
\delta<\epsilon
. Therefore,
s_{n,\epsilon}(F)\leq N_{\Gamma}(n-1,\delta)
, for all
\delta<\epsilon
. Now observe that
s_{n,\delta}
is decreasing with
\delta
. Taking limit as
\delta\to\epsilon
, we get that

s_{n,\epsilon}(F)\leq N_{\Gamma}(n-1,\epsilon)

. Moreover,

\displaystyle h_{top}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{\epsilon\to 0}\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log(s_{n,\epsilon}(F))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{\epsilon>0}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log(s_{n,\epsilon}(F))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{\epsilon>0}\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log(N_{\Gamma}(n-1,\epsilon))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{\epsilon>0}\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{n-1}{n}\frac{1}{n-1}\log(N_{\Gamma}(n-1,\epsilon))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h(\Gamma_{a}).

∎

This proof holds for meromorphic correspondences on compact Kähler manifolds as well.

3.3
Metric Entropy

We proceed to define metric entropy in the sense of
[
14
]
, and state their Half-Variational Principle.

Let
X
be a compact metric space. Let
\mu
be a Borel probability measure on
X
, and let
F
be a multivalued map on
X
satisfying that
F(C)
is closed for every closed set
C
. We say that
\mu
is
F
-invariant
if
\mu(F^{-1}(A))\geq\mu(A)
, for all
A\in\mathcal{B}
. We denote by
\mathcal{M}_{1}(F)
the collection of all such measures. For a finite ordered partition
\mathcal{P}=\{P_{1},\cdots,P_{k}\}
of
X
, the
entropy of
\mathcal{P}
is defined by

H_{\mu}(\mathcal{P})\coloneqq\sum\limits_{P\in\mathcal{P}}-\mu(P)\log\mu(P).

Now let
\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{n}
be the finite ordered partition given by

P_{1,n}\coloneqq F^{-n}(P_{1}),\mbox{ and }P_{j,n}\coloneqq F^{-n}(P_{j})\setminus\bigcup\limits_{\ell<j}F^{-n}(P_{\ell}),\hskip 14.22636pt2\leq j\leq k.

If
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{N}=\bigvee\limits_{n=0}^{N-1}\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{n}
, then the
metric entropy of
F
with respect to
\mathcal{P}
is defined as the limit
h_{\mu}(F,\mathcal{P})\coloneqq\limsup\limits_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}H_{\mu}(\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{n})
. Finally, the
metric entropy of the system
(F,\mu)
is defined as

h_{\mu}(F)\coloneqq\sup\limits_{\mathcal{P}}h_{\mu}(F,\mathcal{P}),

where the supremum is taken over all the finite totally ordered partitions.

The main result of
[
14
]
is the next theorem.

Theorem 3.8

(Half-Variational Principle)
.

Let
X
be a compact metric space
X
and let
F:X\to 2^{X}
be a multivalued map on
X
with the property that
F(C)\subset X
is closed whenever
C\subset X
is closed. Then,

h_{top}(F)\geq\sup\limits_{\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{1}(F)}h_{\mu}(F).

Observe that holomorphic correspondences on compact Riemann Surfaces always satisfy the conditions in the above theorem.

3.4
Entropy for the families
\{\mathcal{F}_{a}\}_{a\in\mathcal{K}}
and
\{\mathcal{F}_{R,S}\}_{R,S}

In this section, we prove theorems
0.2
and
0.3
.

Let
(S,\mathcal{A},\mu)
be a measure space with
S\subset X
. Put
\hat{\mathcal{A}}=\{A\subset X|A\cap S\in\mathcal{A}\}
and
\hat{\mu}
be defined by

\hat{\mu}(A)=\mu(A\cap S).

Then
(X,\hat{\mathcal{A}},\hat{\mu})
is a measure space as well. Furthermore,
\operatorname{supp}(\hat{\mu})=\operatorname{supp}(\mu)
. With this notation, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
F
be a multivalued map on a compact metric space
X
. Suppose there exists a set
S\subset X
satisfying
F^{-1}(S)=S
, for which the two-sided restriction
F|:S\to S
given by
F|(z)=F(z)\cap S
for
z\in S
is a measurable function. Let
\hat{\mu}
as above. Then, for all
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{1}(F|)

h_{\mu}(F|)\leq h_{\hat{\mu}}(F).

Proof.

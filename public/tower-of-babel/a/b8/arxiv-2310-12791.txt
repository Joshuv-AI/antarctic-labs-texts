Stability of the one electron atom Schrödinger model with magnetic field in two dimensions
Ayoub Arraji, Saad Benjelloun, Salma Lahbabi

Abstract
We study the stability of the one electron atom Schrödinger model with self-generated magnetic field in two dimensions. The magnetic energy is taken of the general form K ​ ∫ ℝ 2 | B | p K\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|B|^{p} and we study the stability of the model as a function of the power p p and the coupling constant K K . We show that for p > 3 / 2 p>3/2 , the model is always stable, and for p < 3 / 2 p<3/2 , the model is always unstable. In the critical case p = 3 / 2 p=3/2 , there is a critical stability constant K c K_{c} , that we characterize in terms of zero modes of the Dirac-Weyl operator. The value of K c K_{c} is approximated using analytic and numerical methods.

Introduction and main results

One of the most important triumphs of quantum mechanics is the explanation of the stability of matter, i.e., the fact that the energy of a given system is finite (stability of the first kind)
E_{0}>-\infty

[
22
]
, and is asymptotically bounded from below by a linear function of the number of particles (stability of the second kind)
E_{0}(N)\geq-C\,N

[
8
]
.

In the non relativistic Schrödinger model, when the interaction between particles is modeled with the Couloumb potential
V(\mathbf{x})=\frac{1}{|\mathbf{x}|}
, the uncertainty principle guarantees the stability by making the kinetic energy increase for states concentrating around the nucleus. When the atom interacts with a magnetic field, stability only holds under a condition on the fine structure constant
\alpha

[
15
,
17
,
19
]
. The reason why there may be instability is the existence of the so-called zero modes of the Dirac-Weyl operator, which are states with zero kinetic energy. Three dimensional zero modes had first been exhibited in
[
21
]
and studied subsequently in numerous works such as
[
1
,
2
,
10
,
7
]
(see below for more details).

In the one electron atom case, a critical
K_{c,3}
has been exhibited in
[
15
]
, below which there is stability and above which there is instability. An upper and lower bound of
K_{c,3}
are also given, which insure the stability of the model for the physically relevant constant. The case of the one-electron-many-atoms, and one-nucleus-many-electrons systems were studied in
[
17
]
, and the general molecule case was studied in
[
19
]
. In these cases, sufficient conditions for stability are given. The model for a self-generated magnetic field has also been treated in the framework of the Hartree Fock model
[
6
]
and the reduced Hartree Fock model
[
16
]
. In this last work, a characterisation of the critical constant
K_{c,3}^{\rm rHF}
is given as a function of the number of electrons
N
.

In
[
15
]
and
[
16
]
, the critical constant is given as the optimum of a variational problem posed on the set of zero modes. In three dimensions, zero modes are rare
[
5
,
9
]
, and they are very difficult to obtain. This makes the estimation of the critical constant not accurate; for example, in
[
15
]
, there is a factor
10
between the upper and lower bound. In two dimensions, zero modes are abundant as per the Aharonov-Casher theorem, a symbolic method described for the first time in
[
3
]
and made rigorous in
[
11
]
. Our work aims to study the stability of matter in dimension two and benefits from the abundance of zero modes to find an accurate estimate of the critical constant
K_{c}
. We restrict ourselves to the one electron atom model, similar to the one studied in
[
15
]
.

Let us first recall the results of
[
15
]
. In three dimensions, the kinetic operator is the Pauli operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C}^{2})

\left(\boldsymbol{\sigma}\cdot(\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{A})\right)^{2}=(\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{A})^{2}+\sigma\cdot\mathbf{B},

where
\mathbf{p}=-i\nabla
is the momentum operator and
\sigma=(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3})
are the Pauli matrices

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}0&&1\\
1&&0\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\sigma_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}0&&-i\\
i&&0\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\sigma_{3}=\begin{pmatrix}1&&0\\
0&&-1\end{pmatrix}.

For a magnetic field
\mathbf{B}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{R}^{3})
, it was shown in
[
15
]
that there exists a unique vector potential
\mathbf{A}\in L^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{R}^{3})
satisfying

\operatorname{curl}\mathbf{A}=B\quad\text{and}\quad\operatorname{div}\mathbf{A}=0.

Assuming that the nucleus of charge
z
is located at the origin
\mathbf{x}=0
, the total energy of an electron in the state described by a spinor
\psi=\begin{pmatrix}\psi_{1}\\
\psi_{2}\end{pmatrix}\in H^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{C}^{2}\right)
is

\mathcal{E}_{K,3}(\psi,\mathbf{A}):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|(\sigma\cdot\left(\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{A}\right))\psi|^{2}-z\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{|\psi(\mathbf{x})|^{2}}{|\mathbf{x}|}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}+K\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|B|^{2}.

The first term is the kinetic energy, taking into account the interaction of the magnetic field with the spin of the electron. The second term is the potential energy of the Couloumb force generated by the nucleus. The last term is the coupling with the magnetic energy, which represents in some sense the cost of creating the magnetic field. The coupling constant
K
is equal to
(8\pi\alpha^{2})^{-1}
, where
\alpha
is the fine structure constant. In the system of units where
e=1
,
\hbar=1
and
m=1
, we have
\alpha=1/c\simeq 1/137
. Although
K
has a fixed value, it is considered as a variable and the stability of the model is studied as a function of
K
.
The instability is caused by the existence of zero modes of the Pauli operator, which are the normalized solutions
(\psi,\mathbf{A})
of the Dirac-Weyl equation

\boldsymbol{\sigma}\cdot(\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{A})\psi=0.

Indeed, for a zero mode, the kinetic energy vanishes, and the remaining terms, i.e., the magnetic energy and the potential energy, scale as the inverse of a length. When the coupling constant
K
is small enough, the total energy is negative and goes to
-\infty
by the effect of scaling. This critical constant is then given as the optimum of a functional on the set of zero modes. It is shown in
[
15
]
that for

K_{c,3}:=\sup\left\{\frac{z\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{|\psi|^{2}}{|\mathbf{x}|}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|B|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}},\;(\psi,\mathbf{A})\text{ a zero mode }\right\},

we have

•

if
K>K_{c,3}
, then
\mathcal{E}_{K,3}
is bounded from below,

•

if
K<K_{c,3}
, then
\mathcal{E}_{K,3}
is not bounded from below.

In three dimensions, a first example of zero modes was exhibited in
[
21
]
. It is given by

\psi(\mathbf{x})=\frac{1+i\sigma\cdot\mathbf{x}}{(1+|\mathbf{x}|^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}}\phi_{0},\quad\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{x})=3\,\left(1+|\mathbf{x}|^{2}\right)\langle\psi,\sigma\psi\rangle,\quad\mathbf{B}(\mathbf{x})=12\,\langle\psi,\sigma\psi\rangle,

(1)

where
\phi_{0}
is a normalizing spinor. Afterward, many other zero modes have been constructed and several methods were explored to produce them
[
1
,
2
,
10
,
7
]
. However, until now there is no general rule to characterize their existence, and they are still difficult to obtain because of their rarity
[
9
,
5
]
, which makes the computation of the critical constant
K_{c,3}
delicate. For example, in
[
15
]
, a bounding of
K_{c,3}
is proved, with a factor
10
between the upper and the lower bound.
The lower bound obtained using the Loss-Yau zero mode (
1
) is

K_{c,3}\geq\frac{1}{9\pi^{3}}z=0.0035z
. The upper bound is found using different functional inequalities that ensure stability above the value

K_{c,3}\leq\frac{z}{8\pi}=0.0397z
.
This result was improved by a factor of
3/4
using a diamagnetic inequality for zero modes proved in
[
14
]
. Combining techniques from
[
15
]
and
[
14
]
, we could improve the bound by a factor of
4/9
(see Appendix
C
). Hence, to the knowledge of the authors, the best known bounds are

0.0035z\leq K_{c,3}\leq 0.0176z.

(2)

In this paper we are mainly interested in the one electron atom Schrödinger model with self generating magnetic field in two dimensions. This corresponds to an electron confined in the
(x_{1},x_{2})
-plane subject to the Coulomb potential created by a nucleus of charge
z
and a magnetic potential
\mathbf{B}=B{\bf e_{3}}
pointing in the
x_{3}
direction and depending only on
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
.
In this case the kinetic energy operator is the Pauli operator acting on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{C}^{2})

(\sigma\cdot\left(\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{A}\right))^{2}=\left(\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{A}\right)^{2}+B\,\sigma_{3},

(3)

where the vector potential
\mathbf{A}\in L^{2}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{R}^{2})
satisfies

\operatorname{curl}\mathbf{A}=B\quad\text{and}\quad\operatorname{div}\mathbf{A}=0.

(4)

Here, we consider a general expression of the magnetic energy of the form
K\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|B\right|^{p}
, with
p=2
being the physically relevant case, and
K
being the coupling constant. We are interested in studying the model when
p
and
K
vary.
For
1<p<2
, the set of admissible vector potentials
\mathbf{A}\in L_{\rm loc}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{R}^{2})
satisfying
\operatorname{curl}\mathbf{A}=B\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
\operatorname{div}\mathbf{A}=0
is well characterized as (see Appendix
B
)

\mathcal{A}^{p}=\left\{\mathbf{A}\in L^{\frac{2p}{2-p}}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{R}^{2}),\;\operatorname{div}\mathbf{A}=0\right\}.

For
p\geq 2
and
p=1
, we consider

\mathcal{A}^{p}=\left\{\mathbf{A}\in L^{2}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{R}^{2}),\;\operatorname{curl}\mathbf{A}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),\;\operatorname{div}\mathbf{A}=0\right\}.

Note that for
p=1
, there might not be an
\mathbf{A}\in L^{2}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{R}^{2})
that generate
B\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})

[
11
]
. In order to accommodate measure valued magnetic potentials, an alternative definition of the Pauli operator is proposed in
[
11
]
using quadratic forms, but we do not treat this case here.

The total energy of a spinor
\psi=\begin{pmatrix}\psi_{1}\\
\psi_{2}\end{pmatrix}\in H^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{C}^{2}\right)
is then

\mathcal{E}_{K}^{p}(\psi,\mathbf{A}):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|(\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{A})\psi|^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}B\langle\psi,\sigma_{3}\psi\rangle-z\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{|\psi(\mathbf{x})|^{2}}{|\mathbf{x}|}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}+K\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|B|^{p}.

(5)

The first term is the kinetic energy, the second is the Zeeman term which represents the energy of the interaction of the magnetic field with the spin of the electron. Also, the first two terms add up to the Pauli kinetic energy
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\sigma\cdot(\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{A})\psi\right|^{2}
. The third term is the potential energy from the Couloumb force generated by the nucleus of charge
z
and located at the origin
\mathbf{x}=0
. The last term is the coupling with the magnetic energy. This energy is invariant under the following transformations:

•

gauge transformation
\mathbf{A}\rightarrow\mathbf{A}+\nabla\phi
and
\psi\rightarrow e^{-i\phi}\psi
,

•

rotation of the plane by angle
\theta
.

The set of admissible states
(\psi,\mathbf{A})
is then

\mathcal{S}^{p}:=\left\{\psi\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{C}^{2}),\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\psi|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}=1\right\}\times\mathcal{A}^{p}.

(6)

The ground state energy is denoted by

E_{K}^{p}:=\inf\left\{\mathcal{E}_{K}^{p}(\psi,\mathbf{A}),\;(\psi,\mathbf{A})\in\mathcal{S}^{p}\right\}.

(7)

Our first main result gives the stability (and instability) of the system,
depending on the values of
p
and
K
.

Theorem 1.1
.

For all
p>3/2
and for all
K>0
, we have

E_{K}^{p}>-\infty.

For
p=3/2
, there exists a critical constant
0<K_{c}<+{\infty}
such that

•

if
K>K_{c}
, then
E_{K}^{3/2}>-\infty
,

•

if
K<K_{c}
, then
E_{K}^{3/2}=-\infty
.

For
1\leq p<3/2
and for all
K>0
, we have

E_{K}^{p}=-\infty.

Hence, in two dimensions, the stability is always insured for the physically relevant case
p=2
and the criticality appears for
p=3/2
.

Remark 1.2
.

If the Coulomb potential
-1/\left|\mathbf{x}\right|
is replaced by a potential
V
such that
V_{-}\in L^{1+\epsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{2})+L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, then, using similar techniques, we can show that
\mathcal{E}^{p}_{K}
is bounded from below for any
p\geq 2
and
K>0
. Besides, if
V_{-}\in L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, then
\mathcal{E}^{p}_{K}
is bounded from below for any
p>1+\frac{1}{r}
. Note that the Coulomb kernel in
d=2
satisfies
V_{-}(x)=\log(\left|x\right|)1_{\left|x\right|\leq 1}\in L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for any
r>1
, thus the model is stable for any
p>1
.

The following theorem gives the conditions for the existence of a ground state.

Theorem 1.3
.

For
3/2<p<2
, and for
p=3/2
and
K>K_{c}
, the minimization problem (
7
) admits a minimizer.

We see that for
1<p<2
, whenever the energy is bounded from below, there exists a minimizer. For

, although the system is stable, we cannot prove the existence of a minimizer, because of the lack of control of the vector potential
\mathbf{A}

Our following result characterizes the critical value
K_{c}
in term of two-dimensional zero modes, which are normalized solutions to the Dirac-Weyl equation

\sigma\cdot(\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{A})\psi=0.

(8)

In
[
10
]
, a geometric approach is proposed to derive three-dimension zero modes from a given zero modes in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. By this geometric construction, the two dimensional zero mode corresponding to the three-dimension zero mode (
1
) from

]
, is given by

\displaystyle\psi(\mathbf{x})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
\frac{1}{1+|\mathbf{x}|^{2}}\end{array}\right),\;\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{x})=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\frac{-2x_{2}}{1+|\mathbf{x}|^{2}}\\[8.53581pt]
\frac{2x_{1}}{1+|\mathbf{x}|^{2}}\end{array}\right)\;\text{and}\;B(\mathbf{x})=\frac{4}{(1+|\mathbf{x}|^{2})^{2}}.

In dimension two, zero modes are abundant as per Aharonov-Casher theorem. Hence, we could derive a good numerical approximation of the critical constant by variational methods (see Section

). We denote by

\mathcal{Z}:=\left\{(\psi,\mathbf{A})\in\mathcal{S}^{3/2},\;\sigma\cdot(\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{A})\psi=0\right\}

(13)

.

Theorem 1.4
.

The critical value
K_{c}
is given by

K_{c}=\sup\left\{\frac{z\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{|\psi|^{2}}{|\mathbf{x}|}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|B|^{3/2}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}},\;(\psi,\mathbf{A})\in\mathcal{Z}\right\}.

(14)

The maximization problem (

and satisfies

0.13z\leq K_{c}\leq 0.166z.

(15)

In dimension two, the estimation of the critical constant in significantly better than the three dimensional case.

Remark 1.5
.

The zero mode (
1
) proposed in
[
21
]
is very particular as it has many symmetries. It has been shown to optimize
\inf\|A\|_{6}
over the set of magnetic fields that admit zero modes
[

and it is conjectured to optimize
\inf\|B\|_{3/2}
in the work of Frank and Loss

We note that the 2D version of this zero mode (

.

In dimension two, a simple characterization of zero modes was given in

11
]
when

is integrable. The dimension of the kernel of the Dirac-Weyl operator is given in terms of the flux
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}B\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}
and the solutions of the Dirac-Weyl equation are explicitly given in terms of the real generating potential function

that satisfies the Laplace equation with

This explicit construction allows us to derive, when

If

is not integrable, there is no simple characterization of the solutions for the Dirac-Weyl equation as shown in

Theorem 1.6
.

Let

\mathcal{Z}_{1}:=\left\{(\psi,\mathbf{A})\in\mathcal{S}^{3/2},\;\sigma\cdot(\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{A})\psi=0,\;B\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\right\},

(16)

and

K_{c,1}=\sup\left\{\frac{z\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{|\psi|^{2}}{|\mathbf{x}|}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|B|^{3/2}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}},\;(\psi,\mathbf{A})\in\mathcal{Z}_{1}\right\}.

(17)

If the problem (

) admits a maximizer, then the corresponding Euler-Lagrange equation reads

\alpha(\psi,B)\,\frac{|\psi|^{2}}{|\mathbf{x}|}-\beta(\psi,B)\,|\psi|^{2}+\gamma(\psi)\,\Delta(|B|^{1/2})=0,

with

\begin{cases}\alpha(\psi,B):=2\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|B|^{3/2}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\right)\,\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\psi|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\right)\\
\beta(\psi,B):=2\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|B|^{3/2}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\right)\,\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{|\psi|^{2}}{|\mathbf{x}|}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\right)\\
\gamma(\psi):=\frac{3}{2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{|\psi|^{2}}{|\mathbf{x}|}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\right)\,\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\psi|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\right).\end{cases}

The constant

2
Stability results: Proof of Theorem
1.1

The proof for Theorem
1.1
is divided into three steps.

Step 1: Stability for
p>3/2
.

We will show that there exists a bounded from below and coercive real function
g_{p}
such that for all
(\psi,\mathbf{A})\in\mathcal{S}^{p}
, we have

\mathcal{E}_{K}^{p}(\psi,\mathbf{A})\geq g_{p}(\left\|\nabla\left|\psi\right|\right\|_{2}).

Let
(\psi,\mathbf{A})\in\mathcal{S}^{p}
and denote by
X:=\left\|\nabla\left|\psi\right|\right\|_{2}
. We first bound, from below, the Coulomb interaction term. By the Hydrogen uncertainty principle (see e.g. (
36
)) applied to
|\psi|
with
N=2
, we have

-z\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{|\psi|^{2}}{|\mathbf{x}|}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\geq-2z\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\nabla|\psi|\;|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\right)^{1/2}=-2zX.

(20)

Next, we bound from below the kinetic energy. We have (recall (
3
))

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\sigma\cdot(\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{A})\psi|^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|(\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{A})\psi|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}B\langle\psi,\sigma_{3}\psi\rangle\geq 0.

(21)

Thus,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|(\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{A})\psi|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\geq-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}B\langle\psi,\sigma_{3}\psi\rangle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}B\left(|\psi_{1}|^{2}-|\psi_{2}|^{2}\right).

By interchanging the roles of
\psi_{1}
and
\psi_{2}
, we deduce that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|(\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{A})\psi|^{2}\geq\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}B\langle\psi,\sigma_{3}\psi\rangle\right|.

(22)

Therefore, there exists
0\leq\alpha\leq 1
such that
\alpha\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|(\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{A})\psi|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}B\langle\psi,\sigma_{3}\psi\rangle\right|

and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\sigma\cdot(\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{A})\psi|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\geq(1-\alpha)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|(\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{A})\psi|^{2}\geq(1-\alpha)X^{2},

where the last inequality is obtained using the diamagnetic inequality

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\nabla|\psi|\;|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|(\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{A})\psi|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}

(see e.g.
[
18
, Theorem 7.21]
). We turn now to the magnetic energy. Using Hölder inequality, we have

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}B\langle\psi,\sigma_{3}\psi\rangle\right|\leq\left\|B\right\|_{p}\left\|\langle\psi,\sigma_{3}\psi\rangle\right\|_{q},

with

\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
. Besides, we have

\langle\psi,\sigma_{3}\psi\rangle\;=|\psi_{1}|^{2}-|\psi_{2}|^{2}\leq|\psi_{1}|^{2}+|\psi_{2}|^{2}=|\psi|^{2}=:\rho.

By Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (see e.g. (
39
)) there exists a universal constant
D_{q}=S_{q}^{2}
such that

\left\|\rho\right\|_{q}\leq\frac{1}{D_{q}^{1/q}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\nabla|\psi|\;|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\right)^{1/p}.

Thus

\alpha X^{2}\leq\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}B\langle\psi,\sigma_{3}\psi\rangle\right|\leq\frac{1}{D_{q}^{1/q}}\left\|B\right\|_{p}X^{2/p}

and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|B\right|^{p}\geq D_{q}^{p/q}\alpha^{p}X^{2p-2}.

We finally find

\mathcal{E}^{p}_{K}(\psi,\mathbf{A})\geq(1-\alpha)X^{2}+KD_{q}^{p/q}\alpha^{p}X^{2p-2}-2zX.

(23)

We introduce the function
f_{p}(\alpha,x)=(1-\alpha)x^{2}+C\alpha^{p}x^{2p-2}-2zx
. Optimizing over
\alpha\in[0,1]
, we find that

-\alpha x^{2}+C\alpha^{p}x^{2p-2}\geq-\left(C^{\prime}x^{\frac{2}{p-1}}\right)1_{x^{2(2-p)}\leq pC}+\left(Cx^{2(p-1)}-x^{2}\right)1_{x^{2(2-p)}>pC},

where
C^{\prime}=\left(p-1\right)\left(p^{p/(p-1)}C^{1/(p-1)}\right)^{-1}
. Therefore

f_{p}(\alpha,x)\geq g_{p}(x):=\left(x^{2}-C^{\prime}x^{\frac{2}{p-1}}-2zx\right)1_{x^{2(2-p)}\leq pC}+\left(Cx^{2(p-1)}-2zx\right)1_{x^{2(2-p)}>pC}.

For
p>3/2
or for
p=3/2
and
K>K_{u}=2z/D_{q}^{p/q}
,
g_{p}
is bounded from below and coercive.

Step 2: Instability for
p<3/2
.

We will exhibit a sequence
(\psi_{n},\mathbf{A}_{n})\in\mathcal{S}^{p}
such that
\mathcal{E}^{p}_{K}(\psi_{n},\mathbf{A}_{n})\longrightarrow-\infty
. Let
(\psi,\mathbf{A})
be a zero mode. We have

\mathcal{E}^{p}_{K}(\psi,\mathbf{A})=-z\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{|\psi|^{2}}{|\mathbf{x}|}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}+K\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|B(\mathbf{x})|^{p}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}.

We introduce the scaled sequences

\displaystyle\psi_{n}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=n\psi(n\mathbf{x}),\quad\mathbf{A}_{n}(\mathbf{x})=n\mathbf{A}(n\mathbf{x}),\quad\text{and}\quad B_{n}(\mathbf{x})=n^{2}B(n\mathbf{x}).

We check that
(\psi_{n},\mathbf{A}_{n})
is also a zero mode and
\operatorname{curl}\mathbf{A}_{n}=B_{n}
. Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{p}_{K}(\psi_{n},\mathbf{A}_{n})

\displaystyle=-n^{2}z\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{|\psi(\mathbf{x})|^{2}}{|\mathbf{x}|}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}+n^{2p-1}K\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|B(\mathbf{x})|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}.

Hence, for
p<3/2
or for
p=3/2
and
K
smaller that
K_{l}(\psi,\mathbf{A})=\frac{z\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{|\psi(\mathbf{x})|^{2}}{|\mathbf{x}|}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|B(\mathbf{x})|^{p}}
, we have

\mathcal{E}_{K}^{p}(\psi_{n},\mathbf{A}_{n}){\operatorname*{\longrightarrow}_{n\rightarrow{\infty}}}-\infty.

Step 3: Criticality for
p=3/2
.

We first note that the function
K\mapsto E_{K}
(we discard the upper-script
3/2
here) is the minimum of the functions
\mathcal{E}_{K}
that are linear and non-decreasing in
K
. This shows that
K\mapsto E_{K}
is concave and non-decreasing, hence it is continuous on its domain. We can define

K_{c}=\sup\left\{K>0,E_{K}=-{\infty}\right\}.

In the end of
Step 1
, we have shown that if
K>K_{u}
,
E_{K}\neq-{\infty}
. Thus
K_{c}\leq K_{u}\neq+{\infty}
. Besides, in the end of
Step 2
, we showed that if
K<K_{l}(\psi,\mathbf{A})
, for any zero mode
(\psi,\mathbf{A})
, then
E_{K}=-{\infty}
. Thus

K_{c}\geq\sup\left\{K_{l}(\psi,\mathbf{A}),\;(\psi,\mathbf{A})\in\mathcal{Z}\right\}>0.

3
Existence of ground states: Proof of Theorem
1.3

In this section, we prove that when the energy
\mathcal{E}_{K}^{p}
is bounded from below and
p<2
, then it admits a minimizer. We start by stating and proving a compactness result that will be useful in the rest of the paper.

Lemma 3.1

(Compactness result)
.

Let
1<p<2
and let
(\psi_{n},\mathbf{A}_{n})
be a sequence in
\mathcal{S}^{p}
and let
B_{n}=\operatorname{curl}(\mathbf{A}_{n})
. If, for some
C>0
, we have

\left\|\sigma\cdot\left(\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{A}_{n}\right)\psi_{n}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|\left|\psi_{n}\right|\right\|_{H^{1}}+\left\|B_{n}\right\|_{L^{p}}<C,

then there exist
(\psi,\mathbf{A})\in\mathcal{S}^{p}
such that

•

\mathbf{A}_{n}\rightharpoonup\mathbf{A}
in
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{R}^{2})
, with
q=\frac{2p}{2-p}
,

•

\psi_{n}\rightharpoonup\psi
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{C}^{2})
,

•

\sigma\cdot\left(\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{A}_{n}\right)\psi_{n}\rightharpoonup\sigma\cdot\left(\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{A}\right)\psi
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{C}^{2})
.

As a consequence, we have

\left\|\psi\right\|_{2}\leq 1\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{E}_{K}^{p}(\psi,\mathbf{A})\leq\liminf\mathcal{E}_{K}^{p}(\psi_{n},\mathbf{A}_{n}).

Proof.

Since
(B_{n})
is bounded in
L^{p}
, so is
(\mathbf{A}_{n})
in
L^{q}
(see Theorem
B.1
). Thus, there exist weak limits, up to subsequences,
B\in L^{p}
and
\mathbf{A}\in L^{q}
, which satisfy
\operatorname{curl}(\mathbf{A})=B
and
\operatorname{div}(\mathbf{A})=0
in the distributional sense and
\left\|B\right\|_{p}\leq\liminf\left\|B_{n}\right\|_{p}
. Besides,
(\left|\psi_{n}\right|)
is bounded in
H^{1}
. Thus, it admits a weak limit, up to a subsequence,
\widetilde{\psi}\in H^{1}
which satisfies
\|\widetilde{\psi}\|_{2}\leq 1
. By the weak continuity of the potential energy
[
18
, Theorem 11.4]
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\left|\psi_{n}(\mathbf{x})\right|^{2}}{\left|\mathbf{x}\right|}d\mathbf{x}\to\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{|\widetilde{\psi}(\mathbf{x})|^{2}}{\left|\mathbf{x}\right|}d\mathbf{x}.

We also have
(\left|\psi_{n}\right|)
(resp.
(\rho_{n}=\left|\psi_{n}\right|^{2})
) that converges to
\widetilde{\psi}
(resp.
\widetilde{\rho}=|\widetilde{\psi}|^{2}
), weakly in
L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and strongly in
L^{r}(\Omega)
for any bounded
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
and any
2\leq r<{\infty}
(resp.
1\leq r<{\infty}
), as
H^{1}(\Omega)
is compactly embedded in
L^{r}(\Omega)
.

Finally, we note that for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\left\|\sigma\cdot\mathbf{A}_{n}\psi_{n}\right\|_{2}\leq\left\|A_{n}\right\|_{q}\left\|\rho_{n}\right\|_{q^{\prime}}^{1/2}
, with
2/q+1/q^{\prime}=1
. Thus, the sequence
(\left\|\sigma\cdot\mathbf{p}\psi_{n}\right\|_{2}=\left\|\nabla\psi_{n}\right\|_{2})
is bounded, and
(\psi_{n})
admits a weak limit
\psi\in H^{1}
. Using a compactly supported test function, we obtain that
\sigma\cdot\left(\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{A}_{n}\right)\psi_{n}
converges weakly in
L^{2}
to
\sigma\cdot\left(\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{A}\right)\psi
. The lower semi-continuity of the
L^{2}
-norm concludes the proof.
∎

Using Lemma
3.1
, we can now show the existence of minimizers.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Let
(\psi_{n},\mathbf{A}_{n})
be a minimizing sequence for
\mathcal{E}_{K}^{p}
. Let
K^{\prime}<K
for
p>3/2
and
K_{c}<K^{\prime}<K
for
p=3/2
. We have

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{K}^{p}(\psi_{n},\mathbf{A}_{n})=\mathcal{E}_{K^{\prime}}^{p}(\psi_{n},\mathbf{A}_{n})+(K-K^{\prime})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|B_{n}\right|^{p}\geq E_{K^{\prime}}^{p}+(K-K^{\prime})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|B_{n}\right|^{p}.

It follows that
(B_{n})
is bounded in
L^{p}
. Besides, in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we have shown that there is a coercive function
g_{p}
such that for
p>3/2
and
\forall K\geq 0
or for
p=3/2
and
K>K_{u}

\mathcal{E}_{K}^{p}(\psi_{n},\mathbf{A}_{n})\geq g_{p}(\left\|\nabla\left|\psi_{n}\right|\right\|_{2}),

Therefore
(\left|\psi_{n}\right|)
is bounded in
H^{1}
in these cases.
As well, for
p=3/2
and
K_{c}<K\leq K_{u}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{K}^{3/2}(\psi_{n},\mathbf{A}_{n})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}_{K_{u}}^{3/2}(\psi_{n},\mathbf{A}_{n})+(K-K_{u})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|B_{n}\right|^{3/2}\geq g_{p}(\left\|\nabla\left|\psi_{n}\right|\right\|_{2})-C.

Therefore,
(\left|\psi_{n}\right|)
is bounded in
H^{1}
in this case as well.
Therefore

\left\|\sigma\cdot(\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{A}_{n})\psi\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+K\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|B_{n}|^{p}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\leq C+z\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{|\psi_{n}|^{2}}{|\mathbf{x}|}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\leq C+2z\left\|\nabla\left|\psi_{n}\right|\right\|_{2}<C,

where we have used the Hydrogen atom uncertainty principle. Thus
(\psi_{n},\mathbf{A}_{n})
satisfy the conditions for Lemma
3.1
and we obtain the existence of
\psi\in H^{1}
and
\mathbf{A}\in\mathcal{A}^{p}
such that

\left\|\psi\right\|_{2}\leq 1\quad\mathcal{E}^{p}_{K}(\psi,\mathbf{A})\leq E_{K}^{p}.

We now show that
\psi\neq 0
and can be normalized. Indeed, we know that the ground state energy of the non-magnetic problem is negative, thus there exists a normalized
\psi_{0}\in H^{1}
such that
\mathcal{E}_{K}^{p}(\psi_{0},\mathbf{0})<0
. It follows that
E_{K}^{p}<0
and
\psi\neq 0
. Let us consider
\widetilde{\psi}=\frac{\psi}{\left\|\psi\right\|_{2}}
. We have

\displaystyle 0>E_{K}^{p}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\sigma\cdot(\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{A})\psi|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}+K\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|B|^{p}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}-z\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{|\psi|^{2}}{|\mathbf{x}|}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle=\left\|\psi\right\|_{2}^{2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\sigma\cdot(\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{A})\widetilde{\psi}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}-z\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{|\widetilde{\psi}|^{2}}{|\mathbf{x}|}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\right)+K\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|B|^{p}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\sigma\cdot(\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{A})\widetilde{\psi}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}-z\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{|\widetilde{\psi}|^{2}}{|\mathbf{x}|}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}+K\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|B|^{p}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}=\mathcal{E}_{K}^{p}(\widetilde{\psi},\mathbf{A})\geq E_{K}^{p}.

So, we necessarily have
\left\|\psi\right\|_{2}=1
, and

\mathcal{E}_{K}^{p}(\psi,\mathbf{A})=E_{K}^{p}.

∎

4
Critical value
K_{c}
: Proof of Theorem
1.4

4.1
Variational characterization of
K_{c}

The proof of the variational characterization for the critical value of
K
follows the same lines as in
[
15
]
. We are interested in the critical case
p=3/2
, so, in this section, we remove the upper-script
3/2
when not necessary. We denote by

\widetilde{K}_{c}=\sup\left\{\frac{z\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{|\psi|^{2}}{|\mathbf{x}|}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|B|^{3/2}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}},\;(\psi,\mathbf{A})\in\mathcal{Z}\right\}.

In
Step 3
of the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we have shown that

\widetilde{K}_{c}\leq K_{c}.

Let now show that
\widetilde{K}_{c}\geq K_{c}
. The idea is to prove that for
K<K_{c}
, i.e.,
\inf\mathcal{E}_{K}(\psi,\mathbf{A})=-\infty
, then we can construct a zero mode
(\phi,\boldsymbol{\alpha})
with magnetic potential
\beta=\operatorname{curl}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
, such that

K\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\beta(\mathbf{x})|^{3/2}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}-z\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{|\phi(\mathbf{x})|^{2}}{|\mathbf{x}|}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\leq 0,

(24)

which would imply that
K_{c}\leq\widetilde{K}_{c}
. Assume that
E_{K}=-\infty
and let
(\psi_{n},\mathbf{A}_{n})\in\mathcal{S}
be a minimizing sequence. As the Coulomb term is the only negative term in the energy, we should have

z\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{|\psi_{n}(\mathbf{x})|^{2}}{|\mathbf{x}|}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\longrightarrow+\infty.

(25)

It means that
\psi_{n}
concentrates around the nucleus. We normalize this sequence by the scaling
\lambda_{n}=\left\|\nabla\psi_{n}\right\|_{2}\to+{\infty}
as follows

\displaystyle\psi_{n}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=\lambda_{n}\phi_{n}(\lambda_{n}\mathbf{x}),

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}_{n}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=\lambda_{n}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{n}(\lambda_{n}\mathbf{x}),

\displaystyle B_{n}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=\lambda_{n}^{2}\beta_{n}(\lambda_{n}\mathbf{x}).

These fields satisfy

\displaystyle\left\|\phi_{n}\right\|_{2}=1\quad\text{and }\quad\left\|\nabla\phi_{n}\right\|_{2}=1.

Besides, for
n
large enough, we have
\mathcal{E}_{K}(\psi_{n},\mathbf{A}_{n})<0
, thus

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\sigma\cdot(\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{A_{n}})\psi_{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}+K\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|B_{n}(\mathbf{x})|^{3/2}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\leq z\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{|\psi_{n}(\mathbf{x})|^{2}}{|\mathbf{x}|}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\leq 2z\lambda_{n}.

(26)

and

\lambda_{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\sigma\cdot(\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{n}})\phi_{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}+K\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\beta_{n}|^{3/2}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\leq z\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{|\phi_{n}(\mathbf{x})|^{2}}{|\mathbf{x}|}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\leq 2z.

(27)

The sequence
(\phi_{n},\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{n})
satisfies the conditions in Lemma
3.1
. Thus, there exists
\widetilde{\phi}\in H^{1}
and
\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in\mathcal{A}
such that
\|\widetilde{\phi}\|_{2}\leq 1
, and

\phi_{n}\overset{H^{1}}{\rightharpoonup}\widetilde{\phi},\qquad\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{n}\overset{L^{6}}{\rightharpoonup}\boldsymbol{\alpha}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\sigma\cdot\left(\mathbf{p}+\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{n}\right)\phi_{n}\overset{L^{2}}{\rightharpoonup}\sigma\cdot\left(\mathbf{p}+\boldsymbol{\alpha}\right)\phi.

(28)

Besides,
\lambda_{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\sigma\cdot(\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{n}})\phi_{n}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}
is bounded, and since
\lambda_{n}\rightarrow+\infty
, then

\left\|\sigma\cdot(\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{\boldsymbol{\alpha}})\phi\right\|_{2}\leq\liminf\left\|\sigma\cdot(\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{n}})\phi_{n}\right\|_{2}=0.

Thus,

\sigma\cdot(\mathbf{p}+\boldsymbol{\alpha})\widetilde{\phi}=0.

(29)

It remains to show that
\widetilde{\phi}\neq 0
and then normalize it. Indeed, we have

\displaystyle 1-\left\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{n}\right\|_{6}=\left\|\nabla\phi_{n}\right\|_{2}-\left\|\nabla\phi_{n}\right\|_{2}\left\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{n}\right\|_{6}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\nabla\phi_{n}\right\|_{2}-C\left\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{n}\phi_{n}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\sigma\cdot(\mathbf{p}+\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{n})\phi_{n}\right\|_{2}\to 0.

We deduce that
(\left\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{n}\right\|_{6})
is bounded from below by a positive constant.
Thus

0<C\leq\left\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{n}\right\|_{6}\leq C\left\|\beta_{n}\right\|_{3/2}\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{|\phi_{n}(\mathbf{x})|^{2}}{|\mathbf{x}|}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\to C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{|\widetilde{\phi}(\mathbf{x})|^{2}}{|\mathbf{x}|}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}.

Thus
\phi\neq 0
. We set
\phi=\frac{\widetilde{\phi}}{\left\|\widetilde{\phi}\right\|_{2}}
. Hence,
(\phi,\boldsymbol{\alpha})\in\mathcal{Z}
and
by the lower semi-continuity of the
L^{3/2}
norm, and the fact that
\left\|\tilde{\phi}\right\|_{2}\leq 1
, we have

\displaystyle K\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\beta|^{3/2}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\leq\liminf K\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\beta_{n}|^{3/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\lim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{|\phi_{n}(\mathbf{x})|^{2}}{|\mathbf{x}|}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle=C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{|\widetilde{\phi}(\mathbf{x})|^{2}}{|\mathbf{x}|}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\leq z\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{|\phi(\mathbf{x})|^{2}}{|\mathbf{x}|}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}.

Therefore (
24
) is hence proved, thus
K\leq K_{c}
, which concludes the proof.

4.3
Euler-Lagrange equation: Proof of Theorem
1.6

In dimension two, a simple characterization of zero modes was given in
[
3
,
11
]
. We use this explicit construction to derive the Euler-Lagrange equation (
18
). Without loss of generality, we may assume that
\Phi=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}B>0
. If
\Phi<1
, then the corresponding Dirac equation does not have any non-zero solution. Otherwise (
\Phi\geq 1
), the solutions for the Dirac equation are explicitly given in terms of the ”potential”
\phi
satisfying

{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}-}\Delta\phi=B
, as

\psi=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
Pe^{-\phi}\end{array}\right),

where
P
is a polynomial.
Therefore

K_{c,1}=\sup\left\{K(\phi,P),\;\phi\in\mathcal{F},\;P\text{ polynomial such that }Pe^{-\phi}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\right\},

where

K(\phi,P)=\frac{z\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{|Pe^{-\phi(x)}|^{2}}{|\mathbf{x}|}d\mathbf{x}}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\Delta\phi|^{3/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|Pe^{-\phi}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}},

and the set of admissible potentials
\phi
is

\mathcal{F}:=\{\phi\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),\;\Delta\phi\in L^{3/2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\}.

If
\Phi\notin\mathbb{Z}
, then the set of admissible polynomials is
\mathcal{P}_{[\Phi]-1}(X)
, the set of polynomials of degree less or equal to
[\Phi]-1
, while if
\Phi\in\mathbb{Z}
then the set of admissible polynomials is
\mathcal{P}_{\Phi-1}(X)
or
\mathcal{P}_{\Phi-2}(X)
. In order to derive the Euler-Lagrange equation, we consider the first order equation

\delta\ln{K}=0,

where
\delta
is the functional derivative with respect to
\phi
.
Denoting by

\psi:=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
Pe^{-\phi}\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad B:=-\Delta\phi,

the optimality condition becomes

\frac{\delta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{|\psi|^{2}}{|\mathbf{x}|}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{|\psi|^{2}}{|\mathbf{x}|}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}}+\frac{\delta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|B|^{3/2}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|B|^{3/2}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}}-\frac{\delta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\psi|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\psi|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}}=0.

(33)

We have

\delta|\psi|^{2}=2|\psi|^{2}\delta\phi,

thus

\delta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{|\psi|^{2}}{|\mathbf{x}|}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}=2\frac{|\psi|^{2}}{|\mathbf{x}|}\delta\phi\quad\text{and}\quad\delta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\psi|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}=2|\psi|^{2}\delta\phi.

The operator
\Delta
is linear and symmetric, so

\delta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\Delta\phi|^{3/2}d\mathbf{x}=\frac{3}{2}\Delta|\Delta\phi|^{1/2}\delta\phi=\frac{3}{2}\Delta|B|^{1/2}\delta\phi.

Using this in (
33
), we obtain the desired result.

The functional
K
is symmetric under rotation, so we suspect the optimizer to be radially symmetric. In this case the Euler-Lagrange equation becomes

\boldsymbol{\alpha}(\psi,B)\frac{|\psi|^{2}}{r}-\beta(\psi,B)|\psi|^{2}+\gamma(\psi)\left(\frac{\partial^{2}|B|^{1/2}}{\partial r^{2}}+\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial|B|^{1/2}}{\partial r}\right)=0.

(34)

N\geq 2
. A Similar procedure was proposed in
[
12
]
.

Theorem A.1
.

For any
\psi\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we have

•

Heisenberg uncertainty principle: for
N\geq 1
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\psi(\mathbf{\mathbf{x}})|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\mathbf{x}|^{2}|\psi(\mathbf{x})|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\geq\frac{N^{2}}{4}.

(35)

•

Hydrogen uncertainty principle: for
N\geq 2
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\psi(\mathbf{\mathbf{x}})|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\geq\frac{(N-1)^{2}}{4}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\psi(\mathbf{x})|^{2}}{|\mathbf{x}|}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\right)^{2}.

(36)

•

Hardy inequality: for
N\geq 3
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\psi(\mathbf{\mathbf{x}})|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\geq\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\psi(\mathbf{x})|^{2}}{|\mathbf{x}|^{2}}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}.

(37)

•

Linearized Sobolev inequality: for
N\geq 3
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\psi(\mathbf{\mathbf{x}})|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\geq N(N-2)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|\psi(\mathbf{x})|^{2}}{(1+|\mathbf{x}|^{2})^{2}}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}.

(38)

To prove Theorem
A.1
, we use the following lemma.

Lemma A.2
.

Let
\psi\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and let
g
be a real radial function,
g(\mathbf{x})=G(\left|\mathbf{x}\right|)
, with
G(r)=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{r^{2}}\right)
and
rG^{\prime}(r)=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{r^{2}}\right)
in the neighborhood of
0
. Then, we have the following inequality

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\psi(\mathbf{\mathbf{x}})|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\right)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\mathbf{x}|^{2}\,g(\mathbf{x})^{2}\,|\psi(\mathbf{x})|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\geq\frac{1}{4}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left[N\,g(\mathbf{x})+|\mathbf{x}|\,G^{\prime}(\left|\mathbf{x}\right|)\,\right]\,|\psi(\mathbf{x})|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\right)^{2}.

Before proving Lemma
A.2
, let us show how to use it to prove Theorem
A.1
.

Proof of Theorem
A.1
.

We apply Lemma
A.2
for different functions
g
as follows.

•

Heisenberg uncertainty principle: we take
G(r)=1
.

•

Hydrogen uncertainty principle: we take
G(r)=\frac{1}{r}
.

•

Hardy inequality: we take
G(r)=\frac{1}{r^{2}}
.

•

Linearized Sobolev inequality: we take
G(r)=\frac{1}{1+r^{2}}
.

We extend the results to
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
by a density argument.
∎

Proof of Lemma
A.2
.

Let
1\leq k\leq N
, and let
\lambda
be a real number. We have

F_{k}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\,\left|\left(-i\partial_{k}-i\lambda\,x_{k}\,g(\mathbf{x})\right)\,\psi(\mathbf{x})\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\geq 0.

Besides, a simple calculation shows that

\displaystyle F_{k}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\partial_{k}\psi(\mathbf{x})|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}+\lambda^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}x_{k}^{2}\,g(\mathbf{x})^{2}\,|\psi|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad-\lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\frac{x_{k}^{2}}{|\mathbf{x}|}G^{\prime}(\left|\mathbf{x}\right|)+g(\mathbf{x})\right)|\psi(\mathbf{x})|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}.

We sum this inequality for all values of
1\leq k\leq N

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N}F_{k}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\psi(\mathbf{x})|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}+\lambda^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\mathbf{x}|^{2}g(\mathbf{x})^{2}|\psi|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad-\lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(|\mathbf{x}|G^{\prime}(\left|\mathbf{x}\right|)+Ng(\mathbf{x})\right)|\psi(\mathbf{x})|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\geq 0.

Optimizing over
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, we obtain the desired inequality.
∎

We recall the folowing Sobolev type inequality, and give a lower bound on constants of interest for us.

Theorem A.3
.

For
N\geq 2
and
q\geq 1
, there is a constant
S_{q}^{N}>0
such that for any
\psi\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{C}^{2})
with
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\psi|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}=1
, we have

\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\psi|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\right)^{\frac{N}{2}(q-1)}\geq S_{q}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho^{q}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x},

(39)

with
\rho=|\psi|^{2}
. In particular, for
q=\frac{N+1}{N-1}
, we have

\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\psi|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\right)^{\frac{N}{N-1}}\geq S_{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho^{\frac{N+1}{N-1}}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x},

(40)

with
S_{2}\geq 18.28
and
S_{3}\geq 24.05
.

Proof.

Inequality (
39
) is a direct consequence of
[
12
, corollary 1.12]
. We will prove the particular case (
40
) and give a lower bound on
S_{2}
and
S_{3}
. Let
e<0
be
the ground state energy of
-\Delta-{V(x)}
. In
[
20
]
, it is proved that

\left|e\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq L_{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left|V\right|^{\frac{N+1}{2}},

for some constants
L_{N}
. Thus, for a normalized
\psi\in H^{1}
,

-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left|\nabla\psi\right|^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V\rho\leq-e=\left|e\right|.

Thus,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\psi|^{2}\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V\rho-L_{N}^{2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left|V\right|^{\frac{N+1}{2}}\right)^{2}.

Taking a potential of the form
V=C\rho^{\frac{2}{N-1}}
, we obtain

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\psi|^{2}\geq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho^{\frac{N+1}{N-1}}-C^{N+1}L_{N}^{2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho^{\frac{N+1}{N-1}}\right)^{2}.

Optimizing over

gives

\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\psi|^{2}\right)^{\frac{N}{N-1}}\geq\frac{N^{\frac{N}{N-1}}}{\left(1+N\right)^{\frac{N+1}{N-1}}L_{N}^{\frac{2}{N-1}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\rho^{\frac{N+1}{N-1}}.

Using the values in
[
20
]

L_{2}\approx 0.09
and
L_{3}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}=}0.0135
, we obtain

S_{2}\geq 18.28\quad\text{and}\quad S_{3}\geq 24.05.

∎

Appendix B
2D Magnetic vector potential

In this section, we characterize the vector potentials
\mathbf{A}
corresponding to a magnetic field
B
.

Theorem B.1
.

For a magnetic field
B\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, with
1<p<2
, there is a unique solution
\mathbf{A}\in L^{\frac{2p}{2-p}}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{R}^{2})
to the equation

\operatorname{curl}(\mathbf{A})=B,

satisfying
\operatorname{div}(\mathbf{A})=0
. This field is given by

\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{x})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbf{G}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})B(\mathbf{y})d\mathbf{y},

(41)

where

G(\mathbf{x})=\frac{1}{2\pi}\left(\frac{-x_{2}}{|\mathbf{x}|^{2}}\,,\frac{x_{1}}{|\mathbf{x}|^{2}}\right).

Moreover, there is a constant
S
independent of
\mathbf{A}
and
B
such that

\left\|\mathbf{A}\right\|_{q}\leq S\left\|B\right\|_{p}.

(42)

Proof.

We prove that

\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{x}):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathbf{G}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})B(\mathbf{y})d\mathbf{y}\in L^{\frac{2p}{p-2}}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{R}^{2}).

Let
\phi\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
be a test function, with
q=2p/(3p-2)
being the Hölder conjugate of
\frac{2p}{2-p}
. We have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{x})\phi(\mathbf{x})|\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{|B(\mathbf{y})|}{|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}|}\left|\phi(\mathbf{x})\right|d\mathbf{y}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}.

Besides,
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{2}=2.

Thus, by the Hardy-Litllewood-Sobolev inequality (see e.g. Theorem 4.3
[
18
]
), there exists a universal constant
S>0
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{x})\phi(\mathbf{x})|\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\leq S\left\|B\right\|_{p}\left\|\phi\right\|_{q}.

Therefore,
\mathbf{A}
is indeed in
L^{\frac{2p}{2-p}}
and

\left\|\mathbf{A}\right\|_{\frac{2p}{2-p}}\leq S\left\|B\right\|_{p}.

It remains to prove that
\mathbf{A}
is unique. Let
\mathbf{A},\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\in L^{\frac{2p}{2-p}}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{R}^{2})
be two vector potentials satisfying

\operatorname{curl}(\mathbf{A})=\operatorname{curl}(\mathbf{A}^{\prime})=B,\quad\text{and}\quad\operatorname{div}(\mathbf{A})=\operatorname{div}(\mathbf{A}^{\prime})=0.

Hence, for
\mathbf{U}=\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{A^{\prime}}
,

\operatorname{curl}(\mathbf{U})=0,\quad\text{and}\quad\operatorname{div}(\mathbf{U})=0.

(43)

It follows that
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
are harmonic functions. Since
U_{1},U_{2}\in L^{\frac{2p}{2-p}}
, then they vanish.
∎

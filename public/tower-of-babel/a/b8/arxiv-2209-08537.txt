Introduction

In this paper, we consider the existence of solutions for the following fractional coupled Hartree-Fock type system

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &(-\Delta)^{s}u+V_{1}(x)u+\lambda_{1}u=\mu_{1}(I_{\alpha}\star|u|^{p})|u|^{p-2}u+\beta(I_{\alpha}\star|v|^{r})|u|^{r-2}u\\
&(-\Delta)^{s}v+V_{2}(x)v+\lambda_{2}v=\mu_{2}(I_{\alpha}\star|v|^{q})|v|^{q-2}v+\beta(I_{\alpha}\star|u|^{r})|v|^{r-2}v\end{aligned}\right.~\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

(1.1)

under the constraint

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2}=a^{2},~\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|v|^{2}=b^{2}.

(1.2)

where
s\in(0,1),~N\geq 3,~\mu_{1}>0,~\mu_{2}>0,~\beta>0,~\alpha\in(0,N),~1+\frac{\alpha}{N}<p,~q,~r<\frac{N+\alpha}{N-2s}
and
I_{\alpha}(x)=|x|^{\alpha-N}
.

When studying normalized solutions to the fractional equation

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s}u+\lambda u=(I_{\alpha}\star|u|^{p})|u|^{p-2}u,~x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

the
L^{2}
-critical exponent
p=1+\frac{\alpha+2s}{N}
, the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev upper critical exponent
\bar{p}=\frac{\alpha+N}{N-2s}
and lower critical exponent
\b{p}=1+\frac{\alpha}{N}
play an important role.

For
s=1
, this nonlocal type problem was considered in the basic
quantum chemistry model of small number of electrons interacting with static nucleii which can be approximated by Hartree or Hartree-Fock minimization problems(see
[
16
,
22
,
23
]
). When
N=3,\alpha=2
and
p=q=r=2
, the system (
1.1
)(
s=1
) arises in multi-component Bose-Einstein condensates
[
1
]
and nonlinear optics
[
24
]
. Actually, considering the generalized time-dependent Schrödinger system

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}-i\frac{\partial\Phi_{1}}{\partial t}+V_{1}(x)\Phi_{1}=\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\Delta\Phi_{1}+\mu_{1}(I_{\alpha}\star|\Phi_{1}|^{2})\Phi_{1}+\beta(I_{\alpha}\star|\Phi_{2}|^{2})\Phi_{1},\\
-i\frac{\partial\Phi_{2}}{\partial t}+V_{2}(x)\Phi_{2}=\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\Delta\Phi_{2}+\mu_{2}(I_{\alpha}\star|\Phi_{2}|^{2})\Phi_{2}+\beta(I_{\alpha}\star|\Phi_{1}|^{2})\Phi_{2},\\
\Phi_{j}=\Phi_{j}(x,t)\in\mathbb{C},\Phi_{j}(x,t)\to 0,\quad\text{~as~}|x|\to\infty,t>0,j=1,2,\end{array}\right.

where
i
is the imaginary unit,
m
is the mass of the particles,
\hbar
is the Plank constant,
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}>0
, and
\beta\neq 0
is a coupling constant which describes the scattering length of the attractive or repulsive interaction,
V_{1}(x)
and
V_{2}(x)
are the external potentials. Physically, the solution
\Phi_{i}
denotes the
i
-th component of the beam in Kerr-like photorefractive media. The positive constants
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}
indicate the self-focusing strength in the component of the beam, and the coupling constant
\beta
measures the interaction between the two components of the beam. The sign of
\beta
determines whether the interactions of states are repulsive or attractive. Any solution of system (
1
) subjects to conservation of mass, that is the following two norms:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\Phi_{1}(t,x)|^{2}dx\text{~and~}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\Phi_{2}(t,x)|^{2}dx

are independent of
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Moreover, the
L^{2}
-norms
|\Phi_{1}(t,\cdot)|_{2}
and
|\Phi_{2}(t,\cdot)|_{2}
have important physical significance, for example, in Bose-Einstein condensates,
|\Phi_{1}(t,\cdot)|_{2}
and
|\Phi_{2}(t,\cdot)|_{2}
represent the number of particles of each component; in nonlinear optics framework,
|\Phi_{1}(t,\cdot)|_{2}
and
|\Phi_{2}(t,\cdot)|_{2}
represent the power supply. Therefore, it is natural to consider the masses as preserved, and the solution of (
1
) with prescribed mass is called normalized solution.
To study the solitary wave solution of (
1
), we set
\Phi_{1}(x,t)=e^{i\lambda_{1}t}u(x)
and
\Phi_{2}(x,t)=e^{i\lambda_{2}t}v(x)
. Then, system (
1
) is reduced to a general elliptic system (
1.1
) with
s=1
.

Condition (
1.2
) is called as the normalization condition, which imposes a normalization on the
L^{2}
-masses of
u
and
v
. The solutions to the Schrödiger system (
1.1
) under the constraint (
1.2
) are normalized solutions. In order to obtain the solution to the fractional Schrödiger system (
1.1
) satisfying the normalization condition (
1.2
), one need to consider
the critical point of the functional
E_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\beta}(u,v)
on
S_{a}\times S_{b}
(see (
1.6
) and (
1.7
)). And then,
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
appear as Lagrange multipliers with respect to the mass constraint, which cannot be determined a priori, but are part of the unknown. Some literature called this problem as fixed mass problem.

Recently, the normalized solutions of nonlinear
Schrödinger equations and systems has attracted many researchers, see more details
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
12
,
14
,
15
,
18
,
28
,
29
,
32
]
. In particular, for
s=1
, Wang in
[
30
]
considered the system (
1.1
) with
1+\frac{\alpha+2s}{N}<p,q<\frac{N+\alpha}{N-2s}
, by min-max principle and Liouville type theorem, he gave the existence of normalized solutions. Wang and Yang in
[
31
]
considered (
1.1
) with
L^{2}-
critical exponent, they gave the existence and asymptotic behaviours of normalized solutions.

Up to our knowledge, there is no paper about the normalized solutions of (
1.1
) for
s\in(0,1)
. Therefore, we consider the existence of normalized solutions for (
1.1
) under the different assumptions of
p,q,r
and give the positivity of normalized solutions under the trapping potentials.

The corresponding energy functional with (
1.1
) is

\displaystyle\begin{split}&E_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\beta}(u,v)\\
=&\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u|^{2}+|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}v|^{2})dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(V_{1}(x)|u|^{2}+V_{2}(x)|v|^{2})dx\\
&-\frac{\mu_{1}}{2p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\star|u|^{p})|u|^{p}dx-\frac{\mu_{2}}{2q}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\star|v|^{q})|v|^{q}dx-\frac{\beta}{r}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\star|u|^{r})|v|^{r}dx.\end{split}

(1.6)

It is standard to check that
E_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\beta}\in C^{1}
under some assumptions on
p,q
and
r
. The critical point of
E_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\beta}
constrained to
S_{a}\times S_{b}
gives rise to a solution for (
1.1
), where

\displaystyle S_{a}=\{u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\|u\|_{2}^{2}=a^{2}\}.

(1.7)

In this paper, we mainly consider different potentials as follows:

\displaystyle\textbf{(V1)}:V_{1}(x)=V_{2}(x)=0,

and

\displaystyle\textbf{(V2)}:V_{i}(x)\geq 0,~V_{i}\in L_{loc}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),~\lim_{|x|\to\infty}V_{i}(x)=\infty,\forall~i=1,2.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
3\leq N\leq 4s,\alpha\in(0,N),p,q,r\in(1+\frac{\alpha}{N},1+\frac{\alpha+2s}{N})
and
V_{i}(x)(i=1,2)
satisfies
(V1)
. Let
\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\beta,a
and
b
be fixed. Then the system (
1.1
) has a solution
(u,v,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
with
(u,v)\in S_{a}\times S_{b}
, where
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}>0
and
u,v
are positive and radial.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that
N\geq 3,\alpha\in(0,N),p,q,r\in(1+\frac{\alpha}{N},1+\frac{\alpha+2s}{N})
and
V_{i}(x)(i=1,2)
satisfies
(V2)
. Let
\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\beta,a
and
b
be fixed. Then the system (
1.1
) has a solution
(u,v,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
with
(u,v)\in S_{a}\times S_{b}
, where
u,v
are nonnegative.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
3\leq N<4s,\frac{N^{2}-8s^{2}}{4s-N}<\alpha<N
. Suppose that
p,q,r\in(\max\{2,1+\frac{\alpha}{N}\},1+\frac{\alpha+2s}{N})
and
V_{i}(x)(i=1,2)
satisfies
(V2)
. Let
\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\beta,a
and
b
be fixed. Furthermore, there exist
\theta\in(0,N+2s)
and a constant
C>0
such that

\limsup_{|x|\to\infty}|x|^{-\theta}V(x)\leq C.

Then there exist
\eta\in(0,1)
and a constant
\bar{C}>0
such that
u_{0},v_{0}\in C^{0,\eta}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and

u_{0}(x),v_{0}(x)\leq\frac{\bar{C}}{|x|^{N+2s}}\quad\text{for}~|x|\geq 1.

In addition,
(u_{0},v_{0})
is a positive solution of the system (
1.1
) with
(u_{0},v_{0})\in S_{a}\times S_{b}
.

Denote that
a^{*}=\|Q\|_{2}^{2(p-1)}
, where
Q
is the ground state solution of
(-\Delta)^{s}Q+Q-(I_{\alpha}\star|Q|^{p})|Q|^{p-2}Q=0
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Suppose that
N\geq 3,\alpha\in(0,N),p=q=r=1+\frac{\alpha+2s}{N}
and
V_{i}(x)(i=1,2)
satisfies
(V2)
. There holds

(i)

if
0<\mu_{1}a^{2(p-1)},\mu_{2}b^{2(p-1)}<a^{*}
and
0<\beta<\sqrt{(a^{*}-\mu_{1}a^{2(p-1)})(a^{*}-\mu_{2}b^{2(p-1)})}
, then the system (
1.1
) has a solution
(u,v,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
with
(u,v)\in S_{a}\times S_{b}
, where
u
and
v
are nonnegative.

(ii)

if
\mu_{1}a^{2(p-1)}>a^{*}
or
\mu_{2}b^{2(p-1)}>a^{*}
or
\beta>\frac{(a^{2}+b^{2})a^{*}-\mu_{1}a^{2p}-\mu_{2}b^{2p}}{2a^{p}b^{p}}
, then the system (
1.1
) has no solution.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
3\leq N<4s,\frac{N^{2}-8s^{2}}{4s-N}<\alpha<N
.
Suppose that
p=q=r=1+\frac{\alpha+2s}{N}
and
V_{i}(x)(i=1,2)
satisfies
(V2)
. Let
0<\mu_{1}a^{2(p-1)}<a^{*},0<\mu_{2}b^{2(p-1)}<a^{*}
and
\beta<\sqrt{(a^{*}-\mu_{1}a^{2(p-1)})(a^{*}-\mu_{2}b^{2(p-1)})}
. Furthermore, there exist
\theta\in(0,N+2s)
and a constant
C>0
such that

\limsup_{|x|\to\infty}|x|^{-\theta}V(x)\leq C.

Then there exist
\eta\in(0,1)
and a constant
\bar{C}>0
such that
u_{0},v_{0}\in C^{0,\eta}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and

u_{0}(x),v_{0}(x)\leq\frac{\bar{C}}{|x|^{N+2s}}\quad\text{for}~|x|\geq 1.

In addition,
(u_{0},v_{0})
is a positive solution of the system (
1.1
) with
(u_{0},v_{0})\in S_{a}\times S_{b}
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Suppose that
N\geq 3,p,q,r\in(\max\{2,1+\frac{\alpha+2s}{N}\},\frac{N+\alpha}{N-2s}),\alpha\in(0,N)
and
V_{i}(x)(i=1,2)
satisfies
(V1)
. Let
\mu_{1},\mu_{2},a
and
b
be fixed and let
\beta_{1}>0
be large enough. If
\beta>\beta_{1}
, then the system (
1.1
) has a solution
(u,v,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
with

such that
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}>0
and

are positive and radial.

There are some difficulties to overcome in our paper. First, due to the condition (
1.2
), the Lagrange multipliers
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
are unknown, hence the
L^{2}
-convergence of critical sequence is difficult. If
V_{i}(x)(i=1,2)
satisfies
(V2)
, then Lemma
3.5
solves this difficulty easily. However, if
V_{i}(x)(i=1,2)
satisfies
(V1)
, then Lemma
3.5
is no longer applicable. In this case, the sign of
\lambda_{i}(i=1,2)
plays a key role in
L^{2}
-convergence of critical sequence. Therefore, we devote to showing the sign of
\lambda_{i}(i=1,2)
by different ways. For
L^{2}-
subcritical case, by proving
u\not\equiv 0
and
v\not\equiv 0
, combining with Liouville type lemma(see Lemma
2.12
), it yields the the sign of
\lambda_{i}(i=1,2)
. While for
L^{2}-
supercritical case, by
E^{\prime}_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\beta}\to 0
and Pohozaev identity, we determine the sign of one of Lagrange multipliers, the sign of another Lagrange multiplier is determined by Liouville type lemma and energy comparison method.

Second,
\lim\limits_{|x|\to\infty}V_{i}(x)=\infty(i=1,2)
brings some difficulties to obtain the positivity of the solutions for (
1.1
). In this case, we can not obtain the positivity of nonnegative solutions directly by strong maximum principle of fractional equations. To overcome this difficulty, by giving some restrictions about
N,\alpha
and some conditions of growth for potential functions
V_{i}(x)(i=1,2)
, we construct the decay estimates and the Höder continuity of nonnegative solutions. Therefore, by the definition of fractional Laplacian operator, the the positivity of nonnegative solutions can be obtained.

Finally, compare to local terms
|u|^{p-2}u
and
|v|^{q-2}v
, the nonlocal terms
(I_{\alpha}\star|u|^{p})|u|^{p-2}u
,
(I_{\alpha}\star|v|^{r})|u|^{r-2}u
,
(I_{\alpha}\star|v|^{q})|v|^{q-2}v
and
(I_{\alpha}\star|v|^{r})|u|^{r-2}u
are more complicated. Therefore, we need some careful analysis.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we list some useful lemmas which will be used in later. For simplicity, denote that

\displaystyle A(u,v)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u|^{2}+|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}v|^{2})dx,

and

B(u,v,r)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(I_{\alpha}\star|u|^{r})|v|^{r}.

First, we give some important inequalities.

Lemma 2.1
.

(Fractional Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality)
Let
s\in(0,1),~N>2s
and
q\in(2,2^{*}_{s})
. Then there exists a constant
C(N,s,q)>0
such that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{q}\leq C(N,s,q)\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u\|_{2}^{\gamma_{q}}\|u\|_{2}^{1-\gamma_{q}},~\forall u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

where
\gamma_{q}=\frac{N(q-2)}{2qs}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

(Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality)
Let
t,r>1
and
\alpha\in(0,N)
with
\frac{1}{t}+\frac{1}{r}=1+\frac{\alpha}{N},f\in L^{t}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
h\in L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Then there exists a sharp constant
C(N,\alpha,t,r)>0
such that

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|f(x)||h(y)|}{|x-y|^{N-\alpha}}dxdy\bigg|\leq C(N,\alpha,t,r)\|f\|_{t}\|h\|_{r}.

Moreover, if
t=r=\frac{2N}{N+\alpha}
, then

\displaystyle C(N,\alpha):=C(N,\alpha,t,r)=\pi^{\frac{N-\alpha}{2}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{\alpha}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{N+\alpha}{2})}\bigg\{\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N}{2})}{\Gamma(N)}\bigg\}^{-\frac{\alpha}{N}}.

In the following, we give the sharp Gagliardo-Nirenberg type inequality which can be seen in
[
10
,
26
]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
1+\frac{\alpha}{N}<p<\frac{N+\alpha}{N-2s}
. Then the best constant in the generalized Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality

\displaystyle B(u,u,p)\leq C(N,p,s,\alpha)\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u\|_{2}^{2p\delta_{p}}\|u\|_{2}^{2p(1-\delta_{p})}

(2.1)

is

C(N,p,s,\alpha)=\frac{2sp}{2sp-Np+N+\alpha}\bigg(\frac{2sp-Np+N+\alpha}{N(p-1)-\alpha}\bigg)^{p\delta_{p}}\|Q\|_{2}^{2-2p},

where
Q
is the ground state solution of the elliptic equation

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s}Q+Q-(I_{\alpha}\star|Q|^{p})|Q|^{p-2}Q=0.

(2.2)

In particular, if
p=1+\frac{\alpha+2s}{N}
, then
C(N,p,s,\alpha)=p\|Q\|_{2}^{2-2p}
.

Remark 2.1
.

If
p=1+\frac{\alpha+2s}{N}
, then

\displaystyle B(u,u,1+\frac{\alpha+2s}{N})\leq\frac{1+\frac{\alpha+2s}{N}}{\|Q\|_{2}^{\frac{2(\alpha+2s)}{N}}}\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u\|_{2}^{2}\|u\|_{2}^{\frac{2(\alpha+2s)}{N}}.

(2.3)

By (
2.2
), we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}Q\|_{2}^{2}=\frac{N}{N+\alpha+2s}B(Q,Q,1+\frac{\alpha+2s}{N}),\\
&\|Q\|_{2}^{2}=\frac{\alpha+2s}{N+\alpha+2s}B(Q,Q,1+\frac{\alpha+2s}{N}).\end{split}

(2.4)

Lemma 2.4
.

(Weak Young inequality
[
21
]
) Let
N\in\mathbb{N},\alpha\in(0,N),p,r>1
and
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{\alpha}{N}+\frac{1}{r}
. If
v\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, then
I_{\alpha}\star v\in L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

and

\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|I_{\alpha}\star v|^{r}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{r}}\leq C(N,\alpha,p)\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|v|^{p}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

In particular, we can set
p=\frac{N}{\alpha}
and
r=+\infty
.

Lemma 2.5
.

If

\frac{N(2p-1)}{N+\alpha}\leq q<\frac{Np}{\alpha},

and
u\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, then

(I_{\alpha}\star|u|^{p})|u|^{p-2}u\in L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})~\text{for~}\frac{1}{r}=\frac{2p-1}{q}-\frac{\alpha}{N}.

Lemma 2.6
.

[

]

and

\displaystyle-\frac{C(N,q,s,\alpha)\mu_{2}b^{2q(1-\delta_{q})}}{2q}\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}v\|_{2}^{2q\delta_{q}}

\displaystyle-\frac{C(N,r,s,\alpha)\beta a^{r(1-\delta_{r})}b^{r(1-\delta_{r})}}{r}\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u\|_{2}^{r\delta_{r}}\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}v\|_{2}^{r\delta_{r}}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}A(u,v)-\frac{C(N,p,s,\alpha)\mu_{1}a^{2p(1-\delta_{p})}}{2p}\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u\|_{2}^{2p\delta_{p}}

\displaystyle-\frac{C(N,q,s,\alpha)\mu_{2}b^{2q(1-\delta_{q})}}{2q}\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}v\|_{2}^{2q\delta_{q}}

&B(v_{n},v_{n},q)\rightarrow B(v,v,q),\\

(3.1)

Furthermore, due to

&(-\Delta)^{s}v+v=-\lambda_{2}v_{0}+\mu_{2}(I_{\alpha}\star|v_{0}|^{q})|v_{0}|^{q-2}v_{0}+\beta(I_{\alpha}\star|u_{0}|^{r})|v_{0}|^{r-2}v_{0}:=f_{2}(x).\end{aligned}\right.

(3.5)

By Lemma
2.2
, we have
\hat{u}\in L^{\chi(p)}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{\chi(r)}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
\hat{v}\in L^{\chi(q)}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{\chi(r)}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for
\chi(p)=\frac{2Np}{N+\alpha}
. Moreover,

\frac{N(2p-1)}{N+\alpha}\leq\chi(p)<\frac{Np}{\alpha},~\frac{N(2r-1)}{N+\alpha}\leq\chi(r)<\frac{Nr}{\alpha}\text{~and~}\frac{N(2q-1)}{N+\alpha}\leq\chi(q)<\frac{Nq}{\alpha}.

From Lemma
2.5
, we can derive that

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &(I_{\alpha}\star|u_{0}|^{p})|u_{0}|^{p-2}u_{0}\in L^{t(p)}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\\
&(I_{\alpha}\star|v_{0}|^{r})|u_{0}|^{r-2}u_{0}\in L^{t(r)}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\\
&(I_{\alpha}\star|v_{0}|^{q})|v_{0}|^{q-2}v_{0}\in L^{t(q)}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\end{aligned}\right.

where
\frac{1}{t(p)}=\frac{2p-1}{\chi(p)}-\frac{\alpha}{N}
.
Direct computation shows that

\displaystyle\min\{t(p),t(q),t(r),2\}>\frac{N}{2s}.

Denote that

\Theta_{0}=\max\{t(p),t(q),t(r),2\}.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be the Bessel kernel

\mathcal{K}(x)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\bigg(\frac{1}{1+|\xi|^{2s}}\bigg),

where
\mathcal{F}^{-1}g(\xi)
stand for the inverse Fourier transform of
g(\xi)
. Then

\hat{u}(x)=(\mathcal{K}\star f_{1})(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathcal{K}(x-y)f_{1}(y)dy,~\hat{v}(x)=(\mathcal{K}\star f_{2})(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathcal{K}(x-y)f_{2}(y)dy.

From Lemma 5.2 in
[
9
]
, it yields that
\hat{u},\hat{v}\in L^{\Theta}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for any

. Hence the solutions for the coupled system (

) is well-defined. Therefore,

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &(-\Delta)^{s}(\hat{u}-u_{0})+(\hat{u}-u_{0})=(V_{1}(x)-1)u_{0}\geq 0,\\
&(-\Delta)^{s}(\hat{v}-v_{0})+(\hat{v}-v_{0})=(V_{2}(x)-1)v_{0}\geq 0.\end{aligned}\right.

It is easy to observe that
0\leq u_{0}\leq\hat{u}
and
0\leq v_{0}\leq\hat{v}
.

By Lemma
2.11
, there exists a constant
\eta\in(0,1)
such that

. Furthermore, the fact that

as

. Therefore,
u_{0},v_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
u_{0}(x),v_{0}(x)\to 0

Proof.

First we prove that

\displaystyle\frac{a^{*}}{\max\{(\mu_{1}+\beta)a^{2(p-1)},(\mu_{2}+\beta)b^{2(p-1)}\}}\leq\mathcal{O}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\beta)\leq\frac{(a^{2}+b^{2})a^{*}}{\mu_{1}a^{2p}+\mu_{2}b^{2p}+2\beta a^{p}b^{p}}.

\displaystyle\begin{split}B(u,v,p)&\leq C(N,\alpha)\|u\|^{p}_{\frac{2Np}{N+\alpha}}\|v\|^{p}_{\frac{2Np}{N+\alpha}}\leq\frac{C(N,\alpha)}{2}\bigg[\|u\|^{2p}_{\frac{2Np}{N+\alpha}}+\|v\|^{2p}_{\frac{2Np}{N+\alpha}}\bigg]\\

(4.4)

By Lemma
2.3
and (
4.4
), we hace

\displaystyle\frac{A(u,v)}{\frac{\mu_{1}}{p}B(u,u,p)+\frac{\mu_{2}}{p}B(v,v,p)+\frac{2\beta}{p}B(u,v,p)}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{A(u,v)a^{*}}{(\mu_{1}+\beta)a^{2(p-1)}\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}u\|_{2}^{2}+(\mu_{2}+\beta)b^{2(p-1)}\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}v\|_{2}^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{a^{*}}{\max\{(\mu_{1}+\beta)a^{2(p-1)},(\mu_{2}+\beta)b^{2(p-1)}\}}.

For the upper bound of

.

By Lemma
2.3
and (

), it yields that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathcal{O}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\beta)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\frac{\hat{\mu}_{1}}{p}B(u_{n},u_{n},p)+\frac{\hat{\mu}_{2}}{p}B(v_{n},v_{n},p)+\frac{2\hat{\beta}}{p}B(u_{n},v_{n},p)}{A(u_{n},v_{n})}

\displaystyle+\frac{\mu_{1}-\hat{\mu}_{1}}{p}B(u_{n},u_{n},p)+\frac{\mu_{2}-\hat{\mu}_{2}}{p}B(v_{n},v_{n},p)+\frac{2(\beta-\hat{\beta})}{p}B(u_{n},v_{n},p)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathcal{O}(\hat{\mu}_{1},\hat{\mu}_{2},\hat{\beta})}+\frac{a^{2(p-1)}}{a^{*}}|\mu_{1}-\hat{\mu}_{1}|+\frac{b^{2(p-1)}}{a^{*}}|\mu_{2}-\hat{\mu}_{2}|+\frac{a^{2(p-1)}+b^{2(p-1)}}{a^{*}}|\beta-\hat{\beta}|.

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{\mathcal{O}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\beta)-\mathcal{O}(\hat{\mu}_{1},\hat{\mu}_{2},\hat{\beta})}{\mathcal{O}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\beta)\mathcal{O}(\hat{\mu}_{1},\hat{\mu}_{2},\hat{\beta})}\bigg|

\displaystyle|\mathcal{O}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\beta)-\mathcal{O}(\hat{\mu}_{1},\hat{\mu}_{2},\hat{\beta})|

\displaystyle\frac{3(a^{2(p-1)}+b^{2(p-1)})(a^{2}+b^{2})^{2}a^{*}}{(\mu_{1}a^{2p}+\mu_{2}b^{2p}+2\beta a^{p}b^{p})(\hat{\mu}_{1}a^{2p}+\hat{\mu}_{2}b^{2p}+2\hat{\beta}a^{p}b^{p})}|(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\beta)-(\hat{\mu}_{1},\hat{\mu}_{2},\hat{\beta})|.

), we have

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &(\bar{u}_{n},\bar{v}_{n})\rightharpoonup(\bar{u},\bar{v})\text{~weakly in }H^{s}_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\times H^{s}_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\\
&B(\bar{u}_{n},\bar{u}_{n},p)\to B(\bar{u},\bar{u},p),B(\bar{u}_{n},\bar{v}_{n},r)\to B(\bar{u},\bar{v},r),B(\bar{v}_{n},\bar{v}_{n},q)\to B(\bar{v},\bar{v},q)\\
&(\bar{u}_{n},\bar{v}_{n})\to(\bar{u},\bar{v})\text{~a.e. in }\mathbb{R}^{N}.\end{aligned}\right.

(5.8)

From (
5.5
), it yields that

\displaystyle\langle E_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\beta}^{\prime}(\bar{u}_{n},\bar{v}_{n})-\lambda_{1n}(\bar{u}_{n},0),(\bar{u}_{n},0)\rangle\rightarrow\langle E_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\beta}^{\prime}(\bar{u},\bar{v})-\lambda_{1}(\bar{u},0),(\bar{u},0)\rangle~~~\text{as }n\rightarrow\infty.

Together with (
5.8
), we can obtain that

\displaystyle\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}\bar{u}_{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\lambda_{1n}\|\bar{u}_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\rightarrow\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}\bar{u}\|_{2}^{2}+\lambda_{1}\|\bar{u}\|_{2}^{2}.

From
\lambda_{1}>0
, it means that
\bar{u}_{n}\rightarrow\bar{u}
strongly in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. For
\lambda_{2}>0
, the proof is similar. Hence we omit the details. Therefore, we complete the proof.
∎

Lemma 5.7
.

There exists
\beta_{1}>0
such that if
\beta>\beta_{1}
, then

\displaystyle 0<c<min\{n(a,\mu_{1},p),n(b,\mu_{2},q)\}.

(5.9)

Proof.

It is obvious to check that
c>0
. We only show the right side of (
5.9
) holds. Denote that
u_{0}
is the ground state solution of (
2.5
) with
\mu=\mu_{1},p=p,c=a
and
v_{0}
is the ground state solution of (
2.5
) with
\mu=\mu_{2},p=q,c=b
.
By Lemma
5.1
, it follows that there exists sufficient small
l_{0}<0
and large enough
l_{1}>0
such that

\displaystyle l_{0}\star(u_{0},v_{0})\in\Omega\text{~and~}l_{1}\star(u_{0},v_{0})\in\Delta.

Taking the path
\gamma_{0}(t)=[(1-t)l_{0}+tl_{1}]\star(u_{0},v_{0})(t\in[0,1])
, it is easy to check that
\gamma_{0}\in\Gamma
. By the definition of
c
, we have

\displaystyle c\leq\max_{t\in[0,1]}E_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\beta}(\gamma_{0}(t))\leq\sup_{l\in\mathbb{R}}E_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\beta}(l\star(u,v)).

Now we show that
\sup\limits_{l\in\mathbb{R}}E_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\beta}(l\star(u,v))<min\{n(a,\mu_{1},p),n(b,\mu_{2},q)\}
.
Since
\beta>0
, we can compute that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&E_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\beta}(l\star(u_{0},v_{0}))\\
=&\frac{1}{2}A(l\star u_{0},l\star v_{0})-\frac{\mu_{1}}{2p}B(l\star u_{0},l\star u_{0},p)\\
&-\frac{\mu_{2}}{2q}B(l\star v_{0},l\star v_{0},q)-\frac{\beta}{r}B(l\star u_{0},l\star v_{0},r)\\
\leq&\frac{1}{2}A(l\star u_{0},l\star v_{0})-\frac{\mu_{1}}{2p}B(l\star u_{0},l\star u_{0},p)-\frac{\mu_{2}}{2q}B(l\star v_{0},l\star v_{0},q)\end{split}

(5.10)

In addition, we notice that

\displaystyle M_{\mu_{1}}^{p}(l\star u_{0})\rightarrow 0,~M_{\mu_{2}}^{q}(l\star v_{0})\rightarrow 0,\text{~as~}l\rightarrow-\infty,

combining with (
5.10
), there exists sufficient small
\bar{l}<0
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{l\leq\bar{l}}E_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\beta}(l\star(u_{0},v_{0}))\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{l\leq\bar{l}}\{M_{\mu_{1}}^{p}(l\star u_{0})+M_{\mu_{2}}^{q}(l\star u_{0})\}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\min\{n(a,\mu_{1},p),n(b,\mu_{2},q)\}.

On the other hand, for
l\geq\bar{l}
, we have

\displaystyle B(l\star u_{0},l\star v_{0},r)=e^{l\frac{N(p-1)-\alpha}{s}}B(u_{0},v_{0},r)\geq Ce^{\bar{l}\frac{N(p-1)-\alpha}{s}},

together with (
5.10
), we can derive that

\displaystyle\sup_{l\geq\bar{l}}E_{\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\beta}(l\star(u_{0},v_{0}))\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{l\geq\bar{l}}\{M_{\mu_{1}}^{p}(l\star u_{0})+M_{\mu_{2}}^{q}(l\star u_{0})\}-Ce^{\bar{l}\frac{N(p-1)-\alpha}{s}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle n(a,\mu_{1},p)+n(b,\mu_{2},q)-Ce^{\bar{l}\frac{N(p-1)-\alpha}{s}}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\min\{n(a,\mu_{1},p),n(b,\mu_{2},q)\},

provided
\beta
sufficient large. As a consequence, there exists
\beta_{1}>0
sufficient large such that
c<min\{n(a,\mu_{1},p),n(b,\mu_{2},q)\}
for
\beta>\beta_{1}
. Therefore, we finish the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

From Lemma

Toeplitz algebra and Symbol map via Berezin transform on H 2 ​ ( 𝔻 n ) H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
Mo Javed, Amit Maji†

Abstract
. Let 𝒯 ​ ( L ∞ ​ ( 𝕋 ) ) \mathscr{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})) be the Toeplitz algebra, that is, the C ∗ C^{*} -algebra generated by the set { T φ : φ ∈ L ∞ ​ ( 𝕋 ) } \{T_{\varphi}:\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})\} . Douglas’s theorem on symbol map states that there exists a C ∗ C^{*} -algebra homomorphism from 𝒯 ​ ( L ∞ ​ ( 𝕋 ) ) \mathscr{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})) onto L ∞ ​ ( 𝕋 ) L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}) such that T φ ↦ φ T_{\varphi}\mapsto\varphi and the kernel of the homomorphism coincides with commutator ideal in 𝒯 ​ ( L ∞ ​ ( 𝕋 ) ) \mathscr{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})) . 1.
Introduction

One of the major concerns of operator theory and function theory has generally been the study of operators associated with the spaces of holomorphic functions. We often come across linear operators induced by functions (usually called the symbol of the operator), such as Toeplitz operators, Hankel operators, and composition operators; on the other hand, we can investigate functions that are induced by an operator. A great illustration of the relationship between operators and functions is the
Berezin transform
.

The
Berezin transform
, which was first introduced in
[
3
]
as a tool in quantization, is named after F. A. Berezin. Usually, it involves an open subset
\mathcal{G}
of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
(
n\geq 1
) and a reproducing kernel Hilbert space
{H}(\mathcal{G})
with a reproducing kernel, say,
K_{\bm{z}}
for

\bm{z}\in\mathcal{G}
. Then, for a bounded operator
A
on
{H}(\mathcal{G})
, its
Berezin transform
, denoted as
\widetilde{A}
, is a complex-valued function
on
\mathcal{G}
defined by

\widetilde{A}(\bm{z})=\langle Ak_{\bm{z}},k_{\bm{z}}\rangle_{{H}(\mathcal{G})}\quad(\bm{z}\in\mathcal{G}),

where
k_{\bm{z}}={K_{\bm{z}}}/{\|K_{\bm{z}}\|_{{H}(\mathcal{G})}}
is the normalized reproducing kernel of
{H}(\mathcal{G})
. Thus every bounded linear operator
A
on
{H}(\mathcal{G})
induces a bounded function
\widetilde{A}
on
\mathcal{G}
. Indeed,
\|\widetilde{A}\|_{\infty}\leq\|A\|
. Therefore, the
Berezin transform
of an operator yields a crucial information about the operator. Beyond its original purpose, the
Berezin transform
has shown usefulness in a number of contexts, including the Hardy space, the Bergman space (
[
17
]
,
[
1
]
), the Bargmann-Segal space
[
5
]
. It has also established important relationships with the Bloch space and functions of bounded mean oscillation, and the detailed investigation of these connections can be found in
[
18
]
. It has also found many applications in many areas of mathematical analysis and mathematical physics (see
[
4
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
19
]
).

Toeplitz operators emerge to be one of the most distinguishable classes of concrete
operators because of their immensely useful and widespread applications including operator theory, function theory, operator algebras, and associated disciplines.
Furthermore, it is preferable to think of these operators as elements within operator algebras, like the Toeplitz algebra
\mathscr{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}))
), that is, the
C^{*}
-algebra generated by the set
\{T_{\varphi}:\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})\}
of Toeplitz operators on the Hardy space
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
(see
[
9
]
,
[
11
]
). The
symbol map
, which was initially discovered and utilized by Douglas, proves to be an important tool in the study of the Toeplitz algebra. The classical result of Douglas’s
symbol map

[
11
, chapter 7]
is as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathscr{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}))
denote the
C^{*}
-algebra generated by the set

\{T_{\varphi}:\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})\}
. Then there exists a
C^{*}
-homomorphism

\sigma:\mathscr{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}))\to L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})

such that kernel of
\sigma
is the commutator ideal, that is, the ideal in

\mathscr{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}))
generated by the commutators
[A,B]=AB-BA
;
A,B\in\mathscr{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}))
, and
\sigma(T_{\varphi})=\varphi
for all
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
.

The
C^{*}
-homomorphism
\sigma
is called the
symbol map
. However, Douglas’s proof of Theorem
1.1
was mere existential and much abstract in nature until 1982, when Barría and Halmos in
[
2
]
presented a constructive proof of Theorem
1.1
. More specifically, Barría and Halmos obtained the symbol of an operator
A\in\mathscr{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}))
as the symbol of the Toeplitz operator in the SOT-limit of the sequence
\{T_{z}^{*m}AT_{z}^{m}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
, where
T_{z}
denotes the unilateral shift on the Hardy space
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
. However, due to the nuances of limits in the strong operator topology, it is still very difficult to find the symbol of any operator
A
in
\mathscr{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}))
. The
Berezin transform
is a useful tool in this way to obtain the symbol of an operator
A\in\mathscr{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}))
more concretely. Indeed, the
Berezin transform
of a Toeplitz operator on Hardy space reduces to the Poisson integral of the symbol of that Toeplitz operator (see Theorem
3.2
below). In particular, Engliš
[
12
]
developed a technique using the
Berezin transform
in which he obtained the symbol of an operator
A\in\mathscr{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}))
as the radial boundary function of the
Berezin transform
of
A
. The key observation in his proof is that

T_{\varphi}K_{\xi}=\varphi(\xi)K_{\xi},

for the densely defined Toeplitz operator
T_{\varphi}
with symbol
\varphi\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D})^{\perp}

and
K_{\xi}
is the reproducing kernel of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
. It uses the fact that

\overline{\varphi}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
whenever
\varphi\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D})^{\perp}
. However, this technique fails in the case
\mathbb{D}^{n}
for
n>1
, for an example: if we take
\varphi(z_{1},z_{2})=\overline{z_{1}}z_{2}
then both
\varphi
and
\overline{\varphi}
are in
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})^{\perp}
, and
T_{\varphi}K_{\bm{\xi}}\neq\varphi(\bm{\xi})K_{\bm{\xi}}
for
\bm{\xi}\in\mathbb{D}^{2}
, where
K_{\bm{\xi}}(\bm{z})=\dfrac{1}{(1-\overline{\xi_{1}}z_{1})(1-\overline{\xi_{2}}z_{2})}
is a reproducing kernel of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})
. Thus the natural question arises:

Question: Is the Douglas’s symbol map valid in the setting of the open unit polydisc
\mathbb{D}^{n}
for
n>1
?

In this paper we present a comprehensive study of symbols of operators on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. The main contribution of this article is to address the above question with the help of the
Berezin transform
. More precisely, we obtain the following result
(see Theorem
4.3
below):

Theorem
.

Let
T\in\mathscr{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))
, the Toeplitz algebra of operators on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
for
n\geq 1
. Then
\widetilde{T}\to\varphi
radially for some
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
. Moreover, the map

\Sigma:\mathscr{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))\to L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}),\qquad T\mapsto\varphi

is a
C^{*}
-algebra homomorphism with ker
(\Sigma)=\mathscr{S}
, the
semi-commutator ideal
in
\mathscr{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))
and
\Sigma(T_{\varphi})=\varphi
for any Toeplitz operator
T_{\varphi}
.

The rest of the paper is outlined as follows: Section
2
is devoted to establishing the necessary background, and along the way, we fix some notations and state some properties of Poison integral and
Berezin transform
. In Section 3, we prove an important result concerning the radial limits, which serves as a key ingredient in the proof of the
symbol map
. Section 4 concerns with the main result, that is, the
symbol map
(see Theorem
4.3
), and we depict through an example that Theorem
4.3
can not be extended to the whole of
\mathfrak{B}(H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n}))
, the
C^{*}
-algebra of all bounded operators on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. In section 5, we present a family of larger
C^{*}
-algebras than the Toeplitz algebra

\mathscr{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))
for which the analog of symbol map still holds true. In section 6, we conclude with some open questions.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
denote the set of all non-negative integers,
\mathbb{D}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
be the open unit disc in the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
,
\mathbb{T}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|=1\}
the unit circle in
\mathbb{C}
, and
\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{H})
denote the set of all bounded linear operators on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. For
n\geq 1
, the Cartesian product of
n
copies of
\mathbb{D}
, denoted by
\mathbb{D}^{n}
, is the unit polydisc in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and the
n
-torus
\mathbb{T}^{n}
denotes the distinguished boundary of
\mathbb{D}^{n}
. Then
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
denotes as the Hilbert space of all analytic functions
f
on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
for which

\|f\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}:=\underset{0\leq r<1}{\text{sup}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\left|f(r\bm{\zeta})\right|^{2}\,d\mathfrak{m}_{n}(\bm{\zeta})\right)^{1/2}<\infty,

where
\mathfrak{m}_{n}
stands for the normalised Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
. We shall write bold faced
\bm{\xi}
and
\bm{\zeta}
, respectively as vectors
\bm{\xi}=(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n})\in\mathbb{D}^{n}
and
\bm{\zeta}=(\zeta_{1},\dots,\zeta_{n})\in\mathbb{T}^{n}
for
n>1
. For
1\leq p<\infty
,
L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}):=L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n},d\mathfrak{m}_{n})
is the Banach space of equivalence classes of Lebesgue complex measurable functions
f
such that
|f|^{p}
is integrable on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
with respect to
\mathfrak{m}_{n}
and
p=2
gives the Hilbert space
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
of square-integrable equivalence classes of functions with respect to
\mathfrak{m}_{n}
. Also
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
denotes the commutative
C^{*}
-algebra of essentially bounded complex measurable functions on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
with respect to
\mathfrak{m}_{n}
, and
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
stands for the space of bounded holomorphic functions on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
. Let
\{e_{\bm{k}}\}_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}
denote the standard orthonormal basis for
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
, where
e_{\bm{k}}(\bm{\zeta})=\zeta_{1}^{k_{1}}\dots\zeta_{n}^{k_{n}}
for
\bm{k}=(k_{1},\dots,k_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.
Consider the closed subspace
H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
of
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
defined by

H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}):=\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}):{\langle}f,e_{\bm{k}}{\rangle}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}=0\;\mbox{for all~}\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\setminus\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}\}.

We then identify the Hardy space
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
(via radial limits) with
H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
by the unitary map

H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\ni\widetilde{f}=\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}}a_{\bm{k}}\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}\mapsto\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}}a_{\bm{k}}e_{\bm{k}}=f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}).

Now onwards, without any ambiguity, we shall interchangeably write
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
(respectively, its elements
\widetilde{f}
) in place of
H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
(respectively, its elements
f
) and vice-versa. Furthermore, for
\widetilde{f},\widetilde{g}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
and
f,g\in H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
, we have

\langle\widetilde{f},\widetilde{g}\rangle_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}=\langle f,g\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}.

For
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
, the Toeplitz operator
T_{\varphi}
is a bounded operator on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
given by

T_{\varphi}f=P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}(\varphi f)\qquad(f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})),

and the Hankel operator
H_{\varphi}
with symbol
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
is a bounded operator from
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
to
{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})^{\perp}}
defined by

H_{\varphi}f=P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})^{\perp}}(\varphi f)\qquad(f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})),

where
P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}
is the orthogonal projection of
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
onto
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
and

P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})^{\perp}}=I_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}-P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}
. Clearly, the adjoint operator
T_{\varphi}^{*}=T_{\bar{\varphi}}
, where
{\bar{\varphi}}
is the complex conjugate of
\varphi
.
It is easy to check that
[T_{\varphi},T_{\psi})=H_{\overline{\varphi}}^{*}H_{\psi}

for
\varphi,\psi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
.

We frequently use the identification of the Hardy space over the polydisc
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
with
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})\otimes\cdots\otimes H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
, the
n
-fold (
n>1
) tensor product of Hilbert space
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
via the canonical unitary map
z_{1}^{m_{1}}\dots z_{n}^{m_{n}}\mapsto z^{m_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes z^{m_{n}}
, where
m_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. Thus, we shall often write
(f_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes f_{n})=f_{1}\cdots f_{n}
, where
f_{j}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
. The Hardy space over the polydisc
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
is a reproducing kernel Hilbert space on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
for
n\geq 1
. For
\bm{\xi}=(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n})\in\mathbb{D}^{n}
, the (Szegö) kernel
K_{\bm{\xi}}
of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
is defined as

K_{\bm{\xi}}(\bm{z})=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(1-\overline{\xi_{j}}z_{j})},\qquad(\bm{z}\in\mathbb{D}^{n})

and it reproduces the value of each
f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
, that means
f(\bm{\xi})=\langle f,K_{\bm{\xi}}\rangle
. The normalised reproducing kernel is denoted by

k_{\bm{\xi}}(\bm{z})=\frac{K_{\bm{\xi}}(\bm{z})}{\|K_{\bm{\xi}}\|}=\frac{\sqrt{(1-|\xi_{1}|^{2})\cdots(1-|\xi_{n}|^{2})}}{(1-\overline{\xi_{1}}z_{1})\cdots(1-\overline{\xi_{n}}z_{n})},\qquad(\bm{z}\in\mathbb{D}^{n}).

In particular, the reproducing kernel
K_{\xi}
(
\xi\in\mathbb{D}
) of the Hardy space over the disc
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
is given by

K_{\xi}(z)=\frac{1}{1-\overline{\xi}z}\quad(z\in\mathbb{D}),

and
k_{\xi}(z)=\frac{\sqrt{1-|\xi|^{2}}}{1-\overline{\xi}z}
is the normalised reproducing kernel of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
.

Let
\bm{\xi}=(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n})\in\mathbb{D}^{n},\bm{\zeta}=(\zeta_{1},\dots,\zeta_{n})\in\mathbb{T}^{n}
,

\xi_{j}=r_{j}e^{i\theta_{j}}
and
\zeta_{j}=e^{it_{j}}
where
0\leq r_{j}<1
and
\theta_{j},t_{j}\in[0,2\pi)
for all
1\leq j\leq n
. The Poisson kernel
P(\bm{\xi},\bm{\zeta})
for the unit polydisc
\mathbb{D}^{n}
is defined as

\displaystyle P(\bm{\xi},\bm{\zeta})

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1-|\xi_{j}|^{2}}{|1-\xi_{j}\overline{\zeta_{j}}|^{2}}

\displaystyle=P_{r_{1}}(\theta_{1}-t_{1})\cdots P_{r_{n}}(\theta_{n}-t_{n}),

where
P_{r}(\theta)=\frac{1-r^{2}}{1-2r\cos\theta+r^{2}}
is the Poisson kernel for the unit disc
\mathbb{D}
. For a complex Borel measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
, its Poisson integral, denoted by
P[d\mu]
, is the integral given by

P[d\mu](\bm{\xi})=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}P(\bm{\xi},\bm{\zeta})\,d\mu(\bm{\zeta})\qquad(\bm{\xi}\in\mathbb{D}^{n}).

For
\varphi\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
, its harmonic extension
\widetilde{\varphi}
into
\mathbb{D}^{n}
is defined by the Poisson integral formula

\widetilde{\varphi}(\bm{\xi})=P[\varphi d\mathfrak{m}_{n}](\bm{\xi})=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}P(\bm{\xi},\bm{\zeta})\varphi(\bm{\zeta})\,d\mathfrak{m}_{n}(\bm{\zeta})\qquad(\bm{\xi}\in\mathbb{D}^{n}).

Let
\Phi:\mathbb{D}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
and
\varphi:\mathbb{T}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
be any two complex functions. Define
\hat{\Phi}
as

\hat{\Phi}(\bm{\zeta})=\lim_{r\to 1^{-}}\Phi(r\bm{\zeta})

for all
\bm{\zeta}\in\mathbb{T}^{n}
at which the (radial) limit on the right exists. We say that ‘
\Phi
approaches
\varphi
radially’, denoted by ‘
\Phi\to\varphi

radially
’ or ‘
\Phi(\bm{\xi})\to\varphi

radially
’ if

\hat{\Phi}(\bm{\zeta})=\varphi(\bm{\zeta})\quad\text{for a.e.~ }\bm{\zeta}\in\mathbb{T}^{n}.

Theorem 2.1
.

(See Theorem 2.3.1 in
[
16
]
)

Let
\varphi\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
, and
\nu
be a measure on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
such that
\nu
is singular
with respect to
\mathfrak{m}_{n}
. If
\Phi=P[\varphi d\mathfrak{m}_{n}+d\nu]
, then

\Phi\to\varphi\;\text{ radially }.

In particular, if
\nu=0
, then
\widetilde{\varphi}\to\varphi
radially.

The
Berezin transform
of
T\in\mathfrak{B}(H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n}))
is a complex function on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
defined by

\widetilde{T}(\bm{\xi})=\langle Tk_{\bm{\xi}},k_{\bm{\xi}}\rangle\quad\text{ for all }\bm{\xi}\in\mathbb{D}^{n}.

Here
k_{\bm{\xi}}
is the normalised reproducing kernel of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
.

The following notions are important and used throughout the paper:

(1)

For
\varphi
,
\psi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
, the
semicommutator
is defined as

[T_{\varphi},T_{\psi})=T_{\varphi\psi}-T_{\varphi}T_{\psi}.

(2)

\mathscr{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})):=C^{*}
-algebra generated by the set
\{T_{\varphi}:\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})\}
, called the Toeplitz algebra of operators on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
.

(3)

\mathscr{C}:=
closed ideal in
\mathscr{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))
generated by the set
\{[A,B]=AB-BA:A,B\in\mathscr{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))\},
called the
commutator ideal
.

(4)

\mathscr{S}:=
closed ideal in
\mathscr{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))
generated by the set

\{[T_{\varphi},T_{\psi}):\varphi,\psi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})\},
called the

semicommutator ideal
.

3.
On Radial Limits

In this section we shall discuss about some key results on radial limits which will be used in the next section. Before moving into our main result, we will fix some notations first.

Suppose
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})^{1}:=H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
and
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})^{-1}:=H^{2}(\mathbb{D})^{\perp}
.
For
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})\in\{-1,1\}^{n}
and
\bm{k}=(k_{1},\dots,k_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, we write

H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})^{\alpha}:=\bigvee\{e_{\bm{k}}:k_{j}<0\text{ if }\alpha_{j}=-1\text{ and }k_{j}\geq 0\text{ if }\alpha_{j}=1\text{ for }1\leq j\leq n\}.

It is easy to check that
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})^{\alpha}
is a closed subspace of
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
for all
\alpha\in\{-1,1\}^{n}
. We identify
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})^{\alpha}
with the
n
-fold tensor product of Hibert spaces
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})^{\alpha_{j}}
for
1\leq j\leq n
by the unitary map
e_{\bm{k}}\mapsto e_{k_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{k_{n}}
for all
k_{j}<0
(provided
\alpha_{j}=-1
) and
k_{j}\geq 0
(provided
\alpha_{j}=1
), where

(e_{k_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{k_{n}})(\bm{\zeta})=\zeta_{1}^{k_{1}}\dots\zeta_{n}^{k_{n}},

for all
\bm{\zeta}\in\mathbb{T}^{n}
,
k_{j}<0
(provided
\alpha_{j}=-1
) and
k_{j}\geq 0
(provided
\alpha_{j}=1
). We shall simply write
\otimes_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}=\prod_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}
for
f_{j}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D})^{\alpha_{j}}
, and also write bold faced
\bm{1}=(1,\dots,1)\in\{-1,1\}^{n}
.

The following lemma is an easy consequence of the Poisson integral:

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
f,g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
. Then

{\langle}fk_{\bm{\xi}},gk_{\bm{\xi}}{\rangle}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}=(\widetilde{f\overline{g}})(\bm{\xi}),\qquad(\bm{\xi}\in\mathbb{D}^{n}).

Moreover,
{\langle}fk_{\bm{\xi}},gk_{\bm{\xi}}{\rangle}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}\rightarrow f\overline{g}
radially.

Proof.

Suppose
f,g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
. Now

\displaystyle{\langle}fk_{\bm{\xi}},gk_{\bm{\xi}}{\rangle}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}(fk_{\bm{\xi}})(\bm{\zeta})(\overline{gk_{\bm{\xi}}})(\bm{\zeta})\,d\mathfrak{m}_{n}(\bm{\zeta})

(3.1)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}(f\overline{g})(\bm{\zeta})|k_{\bm{\xi}}(\bm{\zeta})|^{2}\,d\mathfrak{m}_{n}(\bm{\zeta}).

Note that

|k_{\bm{\xi}}(\bm{\zeta})|^{2}=\frac{(1-|\xi_{1}|^{2})\cdots(1-|\xi_{n}|^{2})}{\left|1-\overline{\xi_{1}}\zeta_{1}\right|^{2}\cdots\left|1-\overline{\xi_{n}}\zeta_{n}\right|^{2}}=P(\bm{\xi},\bm{\zeta}),

where
\bm{\xi}=(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n})\in\mathbb{D}^{n}
and
\bm{\zeta}=(\zeta_{1},\dots,\zeta_{n})\in\mathbb{T}^{n}
. Therefore, the equation (
3.1
) yields

{\langle}fk_{\bm{\xi}},gk_{\bm{\xi}}{\rangle}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}=\widetilde{(f\overline{g})}(\bm{\xi}).

Since
f,g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
,
f\overline{g}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
. Hence, Theorem
2.1
infers that
\langle fk_{\bm{\xi}},gk_{\bm{\xi}}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}\to f\overline{g}
radially.

The following theorem establishes the fact that the Berezin transform of
T_{\varphi}

is the harmonic extension of
\varphi
:

Theorem 3.2
.

For
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
,
\widetilde{T_{\varphi}}(\bm{\xi})=\widetilde{\varphi}(\bm{\xi})
for all
\bm{\xi}\in\mathbb{D}^{n}
. Furthermore,
\widetilde{T_{\varphi}}\to\varphi
radially.

Proof.

Let
\bm{\xi}=(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n})\in\mathbb{D}^{n}
. Then

\displaystyle\widetilde{T_{\varphi}}(\bm{\xi})=\langle T_{\varphi}k_{\bm{\xi}},k_{\bm{\xi}}\rangle=\langle P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}(\varphi k_{\bm{\xi}}),k_{\bm{\xi}}\rangle=\langle\varphi k_{\bm{\xi}},k_{\bm{\xi}}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}=\widetilde{\varphi}(\bm{\xi}),

where the last equality follows from Lemma
3.1
.

Now Theorem
2.1
gives
\widetilde{T_{\varphi}}\to\varphi
radially.

Lemma 3.3
.

If
x\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D})^{\perp}
, then
P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D})}(xk_{\xi})=\widetilde{x}(\xi)k_{\xi}

for all
\xi\in\mathbb{D}
.

Proof.

Let
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Then

\displaystyle\langle P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D})}(xK_{\xi}),K_{z}\rangle_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D})}

\displaystyle=\langle xK_{\xi},K_{z}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle=\langle K_{\xi},\overline{x}K_{z}\rangle_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D})}.

Since
x\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D})^{\perp}
,
\overline{x}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
, and hence

\overline{x}K_{z}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
. Thus, by reproducing property

\displaystyle\langle P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D})}(xK_{\xi}),K_{z}\rangle_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D})}

\displaystyle=\overline{\widetilde{\overline{x}}(\xi)K_{z}(\xi)}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{x}(\xi)\langle K_{\xi},K_{z}\rangle_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D})}

\displaystyle=\langle\widetilde{x}(\xi)K_{\xi},K_{z}\rangle_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D})}\qquad(\text{for all }z\in\mathbb{D}).

Therefore,
P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D})}(xk_{\xi})=\widetilde{x}(\xi)k_{\xi}
.

We now state the main result of this section.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})^{\alpha}
and
g\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})^{\beta}
for
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}),\beta=(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{n})\in\{-1,1\}^{n}
. Then

\left\langle P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}(fk_{\bm{\xi}}),P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}(gk_{\bm{\xi}})\right\rangle\to f\overline{g}\;\text{ radially},

that is,

\left\langle P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}\left(fk_{(re^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,re^{i\theta_{n}})}\right),P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}\left(gk_{(re^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,re^{i\theta_{n}})}\right)\right\rangle\to f\left(e^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,e^{i\theta_{n}}\right)\overline{g}\left(e^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,e^{i\theta_{n}}\right)

as
r\to 1^{-}
a.e.
(e^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,e^{i\theta_{n}})\in\mathbb{T}^{n}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})^{\alpha}
and
g\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})^{\beta}
for
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}),\beta=(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{n})\in\{-1,1\}^{n}
.
If either
\alpha=\bm{1}
or
\beta=\bm{1}
, then

\langle P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}(fk_{\bm{\xi}}),P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}(gk_{\bm{\xi}})\rangle=\langle fk_{\bm{\xi}},gk_{\bm{\xi}}\rangle,

and hence
\langle P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}(fk_{\bm{\xi}}),P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}(gk_{\bm{\xi}})\rangle\to f\overline{g}
radially by using Lemma
3.1
.

We now assume that
\alpha\neq\bm{1}
and
\beta\neq\bm{1}
. Firstly, consider

I=\{j:\alpha_{j}=-1\}\quad\mbox{and}\quad J=\{j:\beta_{j}=-1\}.

Let
f=f_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes f_{n}
and
g=g_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes g_{n}
, where
f_{j}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D})^{\alpha_{j}}
and
g_{j}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D})^{\beta_{j}}
for all
1\leq j\leq n
. Then

\displaystyle\langle P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}(fK_{\bm{\xi}}),K_{\bm{z}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle fK_{\bm{\xi}},K_{\bm{z}}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\langle(f_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes f_{n})(K_{\xi_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes K_{\xi_{n}}),K_{z_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes K_{z_{n}}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\langle f_{1}K_{\xi_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes f_{n}K_{\xi_{n}},K_{z_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes K_{z_{n}}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\langle f_{j}K_{\xi_{j}},K_{z_{j}}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\langle P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D})}(f_{j}K_{\xi_{j}}),K_{z_{j}}\rangle_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D})}

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{j\in I}\widetilde{f_{j}}(\xi_{j})\right)\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}\langle P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D})}(f_{j}^{\prime}K_{\xi_{j}}),K_{z_{j}}\rangle_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D})}\right),

where

f_{j}^{\prime}=\begin{cases}f_{j}&\text{ if }j\notin I;\\
1&\text{ if }j\in I.\end{cases}
, and the last equality follows from Lemma
3.3
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\langle P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}(fK_{\bm{\xi}}),K_{\bm{z}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{j\in I}\widetilde{f_{j}}(\xi_{j})\right)\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}\langle f_{j}^{\prime}K_{\xi_{j}},K_{z_{j}}\rangle_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D})}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{j\in I}\widetilde{f_{j}}(\xi_{j})\right)\left\langle\bigotimes_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}^{\prime}K_{\xi_{j}},\bigotimes_{j=1}^{n}K_{z_{j}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\left(\prod_{j\in I}\widetilde{f_{j}}(\xi_{j})\right)\left(\bigotimes_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}^{\prime}K_{\xi_{j}}\right),K_{\bm{z}}\right\rangle,

for all
\bm{z}\in\mathbb{D}^{n}
. Hence

P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}(fk_{\bm{\xi}})=\left(\prod_{j\in I}\widetilde{f_{j}}(\xi_{j})\right)\left(\bigotimes_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}^{\prime}k_{\xi_{j}}\right),~\text{where }f_{j}^{\prime}=\begin{cases}f_{j}&\text{ if }j\notin I;\\
1&\text{ if }j\in I.\end{cases}

Similarly, we have

P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}(gk_{\bm{\xi}})=\left(\prod_{j\in J}\widetilde{g_{j}}(\xi_{j})\right)\left(\bigotimes_{j=1}^{n}g_{j}^{\prime}k_{\xi_{j}}\right),~\text{where }g_{j}^{\prime}=\begin{cases}g_{j}&\text{ if }j\notin J;\\
1&\text{ if }j\in J.\end{cases}

Now

\displaystyle\langle P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}(fk_{\bm{\xi}}),P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}(gk_{\bm{\xi}})\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\left(\prod_{j\in I}\widetilde{f_{j}}(\xi_{j})\right)\left(\bigotimes_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}^{\prime}k_{\xi_{j}}\right),\left(\prod_{j\in J}\widetilde{g_{j}}(\xi_{j})\right)\left(\bigotimes_{j=1}^{n}g_{j}^{\prime}k_{\xi_{j}}\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{j\in I}\widetilde{f_{j}}(\xi_{j})\right)\overline{\left(\prod_{j\in J}\widetilde{g_{j}}(\xi_{j})\right)}\left\langle\left(\bigotimes_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}^{\prime}k_{\xi_{j}}\right),\left(\bigotimes_{j=1}^{n}g_{j}^{\prime}k_{\xi_{j}}\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{j\in I}\widetilde{f_{j}}(\xi_{j})\right)\overline{\left(\prod_{j\in J}\widetilde{g_{j}}(\xi_{j})\right)}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\langle f_{j}^{\prime}k_{\xi_{j}},g_{j}^{\prime}k_{\xi_{j}}\rangle

(By Lemma
3.1
)

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{j\in I}\widetilde{f_{j}}(\xi_{j})\right)\overline{\left(\prod_{j\in J}\widetilde{g_{j}}(\xi_{j})\right)}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\widetilde{(f_{j}^{\prime}\overline{g_{j}^{\prime}})}(\xi_{j})

(By Theorem
2.1
)

\displaystyle\rightarrow\left(\prod_{j\in I}f_{j}\right)\overline{\left(\prod_{j\in J}g_{j}\right)}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}^{\prime}\overline{g_{j}^{\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{j\in I}f_{j}\right)\overline{\left(\prod_{j\in J}g_{j}\right)}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}^{\prime}\right)\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}\overline{g_{j}^{\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{j\in I}f_{j}\right)\left(\prod_{j\in J}\overline{g_{j}}\right)\left(\prod_{j\in I^{c}}f_{j}\right)\left(\prod_{j\in J^{c}}\overline{g_{j}}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}\right)\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}\overline{g_{j}}\right)

\displaystyle=f\overline{g}.

This proves that

\langle P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}(fk_{\bm{\xi}}),P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}(gk_{\bm{\xi}})\rangle\rightarrow f\overline{g}~\text{ radially}.

Suppose that
f
is a finite sum of elements of the form
f_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes f_{n}
, where
f_{j}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D})^{\alpha_{j}}
, and
g
is a finite sum of elements of the form
g_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes g_{n}
, where
g_{j}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D})^{\beta_{j}}

for all
1\leq j\leq n
. That is,

f=\sum_{p=1}^{l}(f_{1p}\otimes\cdots\otimes f_{np})\;\text{ and }\;g=\sum_{q=1}^{l^{\prime}}(g_{1q}\otimes\cdots\otimes g_{nq}),

where
f_{jp}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D})^{\alpha_{j}}
and
g_{jq}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D})^{\beta_{j}}
for all
1\leq j\leq n
. Therefore,

\langle P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}(fk_{\bm{\xi}}),P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}(gk_{\bm{\xi}})\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}\left(\left(\sum_{p=1}^{l}(f_{1p}\otimes\cdots\otimes f_{np})\right)k_{\bm{\xi}}\right),P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}\left(\left(\sum_{q=1}^{l^{\prime}}(g_{1q}\otimes\cdots\otimes g_{nq})\right)k_{\bm{\xi}}\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\sum_{p=1}^{l}P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}((f_{1p}\otimes\cdots\otimes f_{np})k_{\bm{\xi}}),\sum_{q=1}^{l^{\prime}}P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}((g_{1q}\otimes\cdots\otimes g_{nq})k_{\bm{\xi}})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{p=1}^{l}\sum_{q=1}^{l^{\prime}}\langle P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}((f_{1p}\otimes\cdots\otimes f_{np})k_{\bm{\xi}}),P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}((g_{1q}\otimes\cdots\otimes g_{nq})k_{\bm{\xi}})\rangle.

From the above, we obtain that

\displaystyle\langle P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}(fk_{\bm{\xi}}),P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}(gk_{\bm{\xi}})\rangle

\displaystyle\to\sum_{p=1}^{l}\sum_{q=1}^{l^{\prime}}(f_{1p}\otimes\cdots\otimes f_{np})\overline{(g_{1q}\otimes\cdots\otimes g_{nq})}=f\overline{g}~\text{ radially. }

Finally, assume that
f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D})^{\alpha_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes H^{2}(\mathbb{D})^{\alpha_{n}}

and
g\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D})^{\beta_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes H^{2}(\mathbb{D})^{\beta_{n}}
. Then we shall show that

\langle P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}(fk_{\bm{\xi}}),P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}(gk_{\bm{\xi}})\rangle\to f\overline{g}\;\text{ radially. }

Now there exist sequences
(f^{(m)})_{m=1}^{\infty}
and
(g^{(m)})_{m=1}^{\infty}

such that
\|f^{(m)}-f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}\to 0
and
\|g^{(m)}-g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}\to 0
as
m\to\infty
, where each
f^{(m)}
and
g^{(m)}
are the finite sums of elements of the form
f_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes f_{n}
and
g_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes g_{n}
, respectively, and
f_{j}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D})^{\alpha_{j}}
and
g_{j}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D})^{\beta_{j}}
for all
1\leq j\leq n
.
Since
f^{(m)}\to f
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
, there exists a subsequence
\left(f^{(m_{t})}\right)_{t=1}^{\infty}
of
\left(f^{(m)}\right)_{m=1}^{\infty}
which converges to
f
pointwise a.e. on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
. Similarly, there exists a subsequence
\left(g^{(m_{t})}\right)_{t=1}^{\infty}
of
\left(g^{(m)}\right)_{m=1}^{\infty}
such that
\left(g^{(m_{t})}\right)_{t=1}^{\infty}
converges to
g
pointwise a.e. on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
. Thus

f^{(m_{t})}\overline{g^{(m_{t})}}\to f\overline{g}\text{ pointwise a.e.\ }(e^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,e^{i\theta_{n}})\in\mathbb{T}^{n}.

Suppose
E_{0}
is a measurable null subset of
\mathbb{T}^{n}
such that

f^{(m_{t})}(e^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,e^{i\theta_{n}})\overline{g^{(m_{t})}}(e^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,e^{i\theta_{n}})\to f(e^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,e^{i\theta_{n}})\overline{g}(e^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,e^{i\theta_{n}})

as
t\to\infty
for all
(e^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,e^{i\theta_{n}})\in\mathbb{T}^{n}\setminus E_{0}
.
We have also shown that for each
m\geq 1
,

\left\langle P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}\left(f^{(m)}k_{\bm{\xi}}\right),P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}\left(g^{(m)}k_{\bm{\xi}}\right)\right\rangle\to f^{(m)}\overline{g^{(m)}}\;\text{ radially. }

That means there exists a measurable null subset
E_{m}
of
\mathbb{T}^{n}
such that

\left\langle P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}\left(f^{(m)}k_{(re^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,re^{i\theta_{n}})}\right),P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}\left(g^{(m)}k_{(re^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,re^{i\theta_{n}})}\right)\right\rangle\to f^{(m)}(e^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,e^{i\theta_{n}})\overline{g^{(m)}}(e^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,e^{i\theta_{n}})

as
r\to 1^{-}
, and
(e^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,e^{i\theta_{n}})\in\mathbb{T}^{n}\setminus E_{m}
.
Let
E=\bigcup_{m=0}^{\infty}E_{m}
. Then
E
is clearly a measurable null subset of

\mathbb{T}^{n}
. Also
f^{(m)}k_{\bm{\xi}}\to fk_{\bm{\xi}}
and
g^{(m)}k_{\bm{\xi}}\to gk_{\bm{\xi}}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
because
k_{\bm{\xi}}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
. Hence

P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}\left(f^{(m)}k_{\bm{\xi}}\right)\to P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}\left(fk_{\bm{\xi}}\right)\;\text{ and }\;P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}\left(g^{(m)}k_{\bm{\xi}}\right)\to P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}\left(gk_{\bm{\xi}}\right)\text{ in }H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n}).

as
m\to\infty
. Let
\varepsilon>0
be any number and fix
(e^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,e^{i\theta_{n}})\in\mathbb{T}^{n}\setminus E
. Then there exists
N_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for
m\geq N_{1}

|\left\langle P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}\left(fk_{(re^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,re^{i\theta_{n}})}\right),P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}\left(gk_{(re^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,re^{i\theta_{n}})}\right)\right\rangle-

\hskip 142.26378pt\left\langle P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}\left(f^{(m)}k_{(re^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,re^{i\theta_{n}})}\right),P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}\left(g^{(m)}k_{(re^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,re^{i\theta_{n}})}\right)\right\rangle|<\frac{\varepsilon}{3}.

Now choose
M>N_{1}
such that

\left|f^{(m_{M})}(e^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,e^{i\theta_{n}})\overline{g^{(m_{M})}}(e^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,e^{i\theta_{n}})-f(e^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,e^{i\theta_{n}})\overline{g}(e^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,e^{i\theta_{n}})\right|<\frac{\varepsilon}{3}.

Also there exists
s\in(0,1)
such that

|\left\langle P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}\left(f^{(m_{M})}k_{(re^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,re^{i\theta_{n}})}\right),P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}\left(g^{(m_{M})}k_{(re^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,re^{i\theta_{n}})}\right)\right\rangle-

\hskip 142.26378ptf^{(m_{M})}(e^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,e^{i\theta_{n}})\overline{g^{(m_{M})}}(e^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,e^{i\theta_{n}})|<\frac{\varepsilon}{3}

for all
r\in(s,1)
. Note that

\left|\left\langle P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}\left(fk_{(re^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,re^{i\theta_{n}})}\right),P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}\left(gk_{(re^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,re^{i\theta_{n}})}\right)\right\rangle-f(e^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,e^{i\theta_{n}})\overline{g}(e^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,e^{i\theta_{n}})\right|

\displaystyle\leq|\left\langle P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}\left(fk_{(re^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,re^{i\theta_{n}})}\right),P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}\left(gk_{(re^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,re^{i\theta_{n}})}\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt-\left\langle P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}\left(f^{(m_{M})}k_{(re^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,re^{i\theta_{n}})}\right),P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}\left(g^{(m_{M})}k_{(re^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,re^{i\theta_{n}})}\right)\right\rangle|

\displaystyle+|\left\langle P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}\left(f^{(m_{M})}k_{(re^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,re^{i\theta_{n}})}\right),P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}\left(g^{(m_{M})}k_{(re^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,re^{i\theta_{n}})}\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt-f^{(m_{M})}(e^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,e^{i\theta_{n}})\overline{g^{(m_{M})}}(e^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,e^{i\theta_{n}})|

\displaystyle+\left|f^{(m_{M})}(e^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,e^{i\theta_{n}})\overline{g^{(m_{M})}}(e^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,e^{i\theta_{n}})-f(e^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,e^{i\theta_{n}})\overline{g}(e^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,e^{i\theta_{n}})\right|.

\displaystyle|\left\langle P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}\left(fk_{(re^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,re^{i\theta_{n}})}\right),P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}\left(gk_{(re^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,re^{i\theta_{n}})}\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip-85.35826pt-f(e^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,e^{i\theta_{n}})\overline{g}(e^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,e^{i\theta_{n}})|<\frac{\varepsilon}{3}+\frac{\varepsilon}{3}+\frac{\varepsilon}{3}=\varepsilon

whenever

.

This completes the proof.

We have an immediate consequence of the above result as follows.

Corollary 3.5
.

If
f,g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
, then

\left\langle P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}(fk_{\bm{\xi}}),P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}(gk_{\bm{\xi}})\right\rangle\to f\overline{g}\;\text{ radially.}

Proof.

Note that

L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\{-1,1\}^{n}}\negthickspace\negthickspace H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})^{\alpha}.

Let
f,g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
such that

f=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\{-1,1\}^{n}}\negthickspace\negthickspace f_{\alpha}\quad\text{ and }\quad g=\bigoplus_{\beta\in\{-1,1\}^{n}}\negthickspace\negthickspace g_{\beta},

where
f_{\alpha}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})^{\alpha}
and
g_{\beta}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})^{\beta}
for all

\alpha,\beta\in\{-1,1\}^{n}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\left\langle P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}(fk_{\bm{\xi}}),P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}(gk_{\bm{\xi}})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in\{-1,1\}^{n}}\sum_{\beta\in\{-1,1\}^{n}}\left\langle P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}(f_{\alpha}k_{\bm{\xi}}),P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}(g_{\beta}k_{\bm{\xi}})\right\rangle

\displaystyle\to\sum_{\alpha\in\{-1,1\}^{n}}\sum_{\beta\in\{-1,1\}^{n}}f_{\alpha}\overline{g_{\beta}}=f\overline{g}\quad\text{ radially},

which follows from Theorem
3.4
.

4.
Symbol map for
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})

In this section we shall discuss about the
symbol map
on the Toeplitz algebra on

H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. Before that, we need some results in the sequel.

Theorem 4.1
.

For
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
,
\|H_{\varphi}k_{\bm{\xi}}\|\to 0
radially. Moreover, if

\varphi,\psi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
, then
\widetilde{[T_{\varphi},T_{\psi})}\to 0
radially.

Proof.

Suppose
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
. Then by definition

\displaystyle H_{\varphi}k_{\bm{\xi}}

\displaystyle=P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})^{\perp}}(\varphi k_{\bm{\xi}})

\displaystyle=P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})^{\perp}}\left((P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}\varphi+P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})^{\perp}}\varphi)k_{\bm{\xi}}\right)

\displaystyle=P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})^{\perp}}\left((P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}\varphi)k_{\bm{\xi}}\right)+P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})^{\perp}}\left((P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})^{\perp}}\varphi)k_{\bm{\xi}}\right).

Since
k_{\bm{\xi}}\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
, clearly
(P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}\varphi)k_{\bm{\xi}}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
.
Therefore,

H_{\varphi}k_{\bm{\xi}}=P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})^{\perp}}\left((P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})^{\perp}}\varphi)k_{\bm{\xi}}\right).

Set
y=P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})^{\perp}}\varphi
. Then

H_{\varphi}k_{\bm{\xi}}=P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})^{\perp}}(yk_{\bm{\xi}}).

Again

yk_{\bm{\xi}}=P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}(yk_{\bm{\xi}})+P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})^{\perp}}(yk_{\bm{\xi}}).

Thus

\|yk_{\bm{\xi}}\|^{2}=\|P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}(yk_{\bm{\xi}})\|^{2}+\|P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})^{\perp}}(yk_{\bm{\xi}})\|^{2}
,
and hence

\|H_{\varphi}k_{\bm{\xi}}\|^{2}=\|yk_{\bm{\xi}}\|^{2}-\|P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}(yk_{\bm{\xi}})\|^{2}.

Now Lemma
3.1
yields

\|yk_{\bm{\xi}}\|^{2}=\langle yk_{\bm{\xi}},yk_{\bm{\xi}}\rangle=\widetilde{|y|^{2}}(\bm{\xi}).

Therefore,

(4.1)

\|H_{\varphi}k_{\bm{\xi}}\|^{2}=\widetilde{|y|^{2}}(\bm{\xi})-\|P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}(yk_{\bm{\xi}})\|^{2}.

Now Corollary
3.5
yields

\|P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}(yk_{\bm{\xi}})\|^{2}\to|y|^{2}\quad\text{radially}.

Hence, from equation (
4.1
), we conclude that

\|H_{\varphi}k_{\bm{\xi}}\|\to 0\quad\text{ radially}.

Note that

\displaystyle\left|\widetilde{[T_{\varphi},T_{\psi})}(\bm{\xi})\right|=\left|\langle H_{\overline{\varphi}}^{*}H_{\psi}k_{\bm{\xi}},k_{\bm{\xi}}\rangle\right|=\left|\langle H_{\psi}k_{\bm{\xi}},H_{\overline{\varphi}}k_{\bm{\xi}}\rangle\right|\leq\|H_{\psi}k_{\bm{\xi}}\|\,\|H_{\overline{\varphi}}k_{\bm{\xi}}\|\to 0\quad\text{ radially.}

This finishes the proof of the second part.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
S\in\mathscr{S}
. Then
\widetilde{S}\to 0
radially.

Proof.

Note that the set of all finite sums of operators of the form
A[T_{\varphi},T_{\psi})B
, where
A,B\in\mathscr{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))
, forms a dense subset of
\mathscr{S}
. Now the set of all finite sums of operators of the form
T_{\varphi_{1}}T_{\varphi_{2}}\cdots T_{\varphi_{n}}
is dense in
\mathscr{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))
and hence the span of the operators of the form

T_{\varphi_{1}}T_{\varphi_{2}}\cdots T_{\varphi_{n}}[T_{\varphi},T_{\psi})T_{\psi_{1}}T_{\psi_{2}}\cdots T_{\psi_{m}}

form a dense subset of
\mathscr{S}
. Therefore, it suffices to prove for
S=T_{\varphi_{1}}T_{\varphi_{2}}\cdots T_{\varphi_{n}}[T_{\varphi},T_{\psi})T_{\psi_{1}}T_{\psi_{2}}\cdots T_{\psi_{m}}
, where
\varphi,\varphi_{i},\psi,\psi_{i}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
. Again for any symbol

u,v,w\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
,

\displaystyle T_{w}[T_{u},T_{v})

\displaystyle=T_{w}T_{uv}-T_{w}T_{u}T_{v}

\displaystyle=(T_{w}T_{uv}-T_{wuv})+(T_{wuv}-T_{wu}T_{v})+(T_{wu}T_{v}-T_{w}T_{u}T_{v})

\displaystyle=-[T_{w},T_{uv})+[T_{wu},T_{v})+[T_{w},T_{u})T_{v}.

Thus, it is enough to assume that
S\in\mathscr{S}
is of the form

S=[T_{\varphi},T_{\psi})A=H_{\overline{\varphi}}^{*}H_{\psi}A\qquad(A\in\mathscr{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})),\varphi,\psi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\left|\widetilde{S}(\bm{\xi})\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\langle H_{\overline{\varphi}}^{*}H_{\psi}Ak_{\bm{\xi}},k_{\bm{\xi}}\rangle\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\langle H_{\psi}Ak_{\bm{\xi}},H_{\overline{\varphi}}k_{\bm{\xi}}\rangle\right|

\displaystyle=\|H_{\psi}Ak_{\bm{\xi}}\|\,\|H_{\overline{\varphi}}k_{\bm{\xi}}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|H_{\psi}A\|\,\|H_{\overline{\varphi}}k_{\bm{\xi}}\|\to 0\quad\text{ radially, }

which follows from Theorem
4.1
.

We are now in a position to state the main result of this section.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
T\in\mathscr{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))
. Then
\widetilde{T}\to\varphi
radially for some
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
. Moreover, the map

\Sigma:\mathscr{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))\to L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}),\qquad T\mapsto\varphi

is a
C^{*}
-algebra homomorphism with ker
(\Sigma)=\mathscr{S}
and
\Sigma(T_{\varphi})=\varphi
for any Toeplitz operator
T_{\varphi}
.

Proof.

We shall prove by induction that

\mbox{for any $m\geq 2$ if~}\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{m}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}),~\mbox{then~}T_{\varphi_{1}}\cdots T_{\varphi_{m}}-T_{\varphi_{1}\cdots\varphi_{m}}\in\mathscr{S}.

Recall that for any two symbols
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
,

T_{\varphi_{1}}T_{\varphi_{2}}-T_{\varphi_{1}\varphi_{2}}=-[T_{\varphi_{1}},T_{\varphi_{2}})\in\mathscr{S}
.

Suppose now
T_{\varphi_{1}}\cdots T_{\varphi_{m}}-T_{\varphi_{1}\cdots\varphi_{m}}\in\mathscr{S}
for some
m>2
and
\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{m}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}).

Note that

\displaystyle T_{\varphi_{1}}\cdots T_{\varphi_{m}}T_{\varphi_{m+1}}-T_{\varphi_{1}\cdots\varphi_{m}\varphi_{m+1}}

\displaystyle=(T_{\varphi_{1}}\cdots T_{\varphi_{m}}T_{\varphi_{m+1}}-T_{\varphi_{1}\cdots\varphi_{m}}T_{\varphi_{m+1}})

\displaystyle+(T_{\varphi_{1}\cdots\varphi_{m}}T_{\varphi_{m+1}}-T_{(\varphi_{1}\cdots\varphi_{m})\varphi_{m+1}})

\displaystyle=(T_{\varphi_{1}}\cdots T_{\varphi_{m}}-T_{\varphi_{1}\cdots\varphi_{m}})T_{\varphi_{m+1}}

\displaystyle-[T_{\varphi_{1}\cdots\varphi_{m}},T_{\varphi_{m+1}}).

Since
\mathscr{S}
is a closed ideal in
\mathscr{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))
, we have

T_{\varphi_{1}}\cdots T_{\varphi_{m}}T_{\varphi_{m+1}}-T_{\varphi_{1}\cdots\varphi_{m}\varphi_{m+1}}\in\mathscr{S}.

Now the collection of all finite sums of operators of the form
T_{\varphi_{1}}\cdots T_{\varphi_{m}}
is dense in
\mathscr{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))
. Thus operators of the form

T=T_{\varphi}+S\qquad(\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}),~S\in\mathscr{S})

is a dense subset of
\mathscr{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))
. Consider

\mathscr{T}_{0}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))=\{\,T_{\varphi}+S:\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}),\,S\in\mathscr{S}\,\}.

Suppose
T=T_{\varphi}+S
for some
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
and
S\in\mathscr{S}
.
Then, Theorem
3.2
and Theorem
4.2
yield
\widetilde{T}\to\varphi
radially. Define a map
\Sigma_{0}:\mathscr{T}_{0}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))\to L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
as

(4.2)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{0}(T)=\varphi\quad(T=T_{\varphi}+S\in\mathscr{T}_{0}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))).

We shall show that
{\Sigma_{0}}
is well-defined. Suppose
T_{\varphi_{1}}+S_{1}=T_{\varphi_{2}}+S_{2}
. Then

\displaystyle T_{\varphi_{1}}-T_{\varphi_{2}}=S_{2}-S_{1}\implies T_{\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2}}=S_{2}-S_{1}.

Now Theorem
4.2
gives

\widetilde{T}_{\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2}}=\widetilde{S_{2}-S_{1}}\to 0\text{ radially}
. Again
Theorem
3.2
infers
\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2}=0\text{ a.e.}
and hence
\varphi_{1}=\varphi_{2}\text{ a.e.}
.

Suppose
T_{\varphi_{1}}+S_{1},T_{\varphi_{2}}+S_{2}\in\mathscr{T}_{0}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
. Then

\displaystyle\Sigma_{0}\left(\alpha(T_{\varphi_{1}}+S_{1})+(T_{\varphi_{2}}+S_{2})\right)

\displaystyle=\Sigma_{0}(T_{\alpha\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2}}+\alpha S_{1}+S_{2})

(By (
4.2
))

\displaystyle=\alpha\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2}

\displaystyle=\alpha\Sigma_{0}(T_{\varphi_{1}}+S_{1})+\Sigma_{0}(T_{\varphi_{2}}+S_{2}).

Again if
T\in\mathfrak{B}(H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n}))
, then

\left|\widetilde{T}(\bm{\xi})\right|=\left|\langle Tk_{\bm{\xi}},k_{\bm{\xi}}\rangle\right|\leq\|T\|\qquad(\bm{\xi}\in\mathbb{D}^{n}).

Suppose
T=T_{\varphi}+S
for
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
and
S\in\mathscr{S}
. Then

\left|\widetilde{T}(re^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,re^{i\theta_{n}})\right|\leq\|T\|\quad\mbox{for all~}0\leq r<1,\,(e^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,e^{i\theta_{n}})\in\mathbb{T}^{n}.

Letting
r\to 1^{-}
, we obtain

\left|\varphi(e^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,e^{i\theta_{n}})\right|\leq\|T\|
a.e.
(e^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,e^{i\theta_{n}})\in\mathbb{T}^{n}
. Hence

\|\Sigma_{0}(T)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}=\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}\leq\|T\|.

Therefore,
\Sigma_{0}
is a linear and bounded map from
\mathscr{T}_{0}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))
to
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
.

We now extend
\Sigma_{0}
to
\overline{\mathscr{T}_{0}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))}=\mathscr{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))
, denoting its extension by
\Sigma
. Thus
\Sigma:\mathscr{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))\to L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
is a linear and bounded map. Suppose
T\in\mathscr{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))
and set
\varphi=\Sigma(T)
. Then there exists a sequence
\{T_{m}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
, where each
T_{m}=T_{\varphi_{m}}+S_{m}\in\mathscr{T}_{0}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))
, such that
\|T_{m}-T\|\to 0
as
m\to\infty
. Therefore,

\|T_{\varphi_{m}}-T_{\varphi}\|=\|T_{\varphi_{m}-\varphi}\|=\|\varphi_{m}-\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}=\|\Sigma(T_{m})-\Sigma(T)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})}\to 0~~\mbox{~as~}m\to\infty.

Thus

S_{m}=T_{m}-T_{\varphi_{m}}\to T-T_{\varphi}\text{ as }m\to\infty.

Let
S=T-T_{\varphi}
. Then
S\in\mathscr{S}
as
\{S_{m}\}
in
\mathscr{S}
and
\mathscr{S}
is closed,
and hence if
T\in\mathscr{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))
, then
T=T_{\varphi}+S
, where
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
and
S\in\mathscr{S}
. Thus

\mathscr{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))=\{\,T_{\varphi}+S:\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}),\,S\in\mathscr{S}\,\}.

Therefore, Theorem
3.2
and Theorem
4.2
give
\widetilde{T}\to\varphi
radially.

To show
\Sigma
is a
C^{*}
-algebra homomorphism, let
T=T_{\varphi}+S
, where
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
and
S\in\mathscr{S}
. Note that

\displaystyle\widetilde{(T_{\varphi}+S)^{*}}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{T_{\overline{\varphi}}+S^{*}}=\widetilde{T_{\overline{\varphi}}}+\widetilde{S^{*}}=\widetilde{T_{\overline{\varphi}}}+\overline{\widetilde{S}}\to\overline{\varphi}\quad radially.

Therefore,
\Sigma(T^{*})=\overline{\Sigma(T)}
. Suppose
T_{1}=T_{\varphi_{1}}+S_{1}
and
T_{2}=T_{\varphi_{2}}+S_{2}
, where
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
and
S_{1},S_{2}\in\mathscr{S}
. Then

\displaystyle T_{1}T_{2}

\displaystyle=T_{\varphi_{1}}T_{\varphi_{2}}+T_{\varphi_{1}}S_{2}+S_{1}T_{\varphi_{2}}+S_{1}S_{2}

\displaystyle=T_{\varphi_{1}\varphi_{2}}+(T_{\varphi_{1}}T_{\varphi_{2}}-T_{\varphi_{1}\varphi_{2}})+(T_{\varphi_{1}}S_{2}+S_{1}T_{\varphi_{2}}+S_{1}S_{2}).

Since
\mathscr{S}
is an ideal in
\mathscr{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))
, clearly
(T_{\varphi_{1}}T_{\varphi_{2}}-T_{\varphi_{1}\varphi_{2}})+(T_{\varphi_{1}}S_{2}+S_{1}T_{\varphi_{2}}+S_{1}S_{2})\in\mathscr{S}
. Hence

\widetilde{T_{1}T_{2}}\to\varphi_{1}\varphi_{2}~~\mbox{radially}.

It follows that
\Sigma(T_{1}T_{2})=\Sigma(T_{1})\Sigma(T_{2})
for any
T_{1},T_{2}\in\mathscr{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))
.

Finally, it is clear from the definition that ker(
\Sigma
)
=\mathscr{S}
, the semicommutator ideal
\mathscr{S}
in
\mathscr{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))
, and
\Sigma(T_{\varphi})=\varphi
for all
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
. This finishes the proof.

The next result readily follows from the above theorem.

Corollary 4.4
.

For
T\in\mathscr{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))
,
\widetilde{[T^{*},T]}\to 0
radially.

We shall now show that the symbol map can not be extended to the whole of

\mathfrak{B}(H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n}))
in the following example (see
[
12
]
for
n=1
case).

Example 4.5
.

Consider the operator
T
on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
, defined by

Tz^{k}=z^{2k+1}\qquad(k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}),

where
\{z^{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
denotes the standard orthonormal basis for
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
.
Now

\langle Tz^{k},Tz^{l}\rangle=\langle z^{2k+1},z^{2l+1}\rangle=\delta_{k,l}=\langle z^{k},z^{l}\rangle,\qquad(k,l\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}).

Therefore,
T
is an isometry on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
. Thus,
T^{*}T=I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D})}
and
TT^{*}=P_{\mathcal{R}(T)}
, the orthogonal projection onto the range
\mathcal{R}(T)=\bigvee_{k=0}^{\infty}\{z^{2k+1}\}
. Hence

(T^{*}T-TT^{*})z^{k}=(I-P_{\mathcal{R}(T)})z^{k}=\begin{cases}0\quad&\text{if $k$ is odd;}\\
z^{k}\quad&\text{if $k$ is even.}\end{cases}

That means
[T^{*},T]
is an orthogonal projection onto
\mathcal{M}=\bigvee\{z^{2k}:k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\}
. Therefore, for
\xi\in\mathbb{D}

\displaystyle\widetilde{[T^{*},T]}(\xi)=\|P_{\mathcal{M}}k_{\xi}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=(1-|\xi|^{2})\|P_{\mathcal{M}}K_{\xi}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=(1-|\xi|^{2})\left\|P_{\mathcal{M}}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\overline{\xi}^{k}z^{k}\right)\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=(1-|\xi|^{2})\left\|\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\overline{\xi}^{2k}z^{2k}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=(1-|\xi|^{2})\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|\xi|^{4k}

\displaystyle=\frac{(1-|\xi|^{2})}{(1-|\xi|^{4})}=\frac{1}{1+|\xi|^{2}}\to\frac{1}{2}\text{ radially.}

Now we consider the operator
S
on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
as

S=\overbrace{T\otimes\cdots\otimes T}^{n\text{-times}}.

Then

\displaystyle S^{*}S-SS^{*}

\displaystyle=(T\otimes\cdots\otimes T)^{*}(T\otimes\cdots\otimes T)-(T\otimes\cdots\otimes T)(T\otimes\cdots\otimes T)^{*}

\displaystyle=(T^{*}T\otimes T^{*}T\otimes\cdots\otimes T^{*}T)-(TT^{*}\otimes TT^{*}\otimes\cdots\otimes TT^{*})

\displaystyle=I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-(TT^{*}\otimes TT^{*}\otimes\cdots\otimes TT^{*}).

Thus for
\bm{k}=(k_{1},\dots,k_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}
,

[S^{*},S]\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}=\begin{cases}\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}\quad&\text{if $k_{j}$ is even for some $j$;}\\
0\quad&\text{if $k_{j}$ is odd for all $j$.}\end{cases}

Hence
[S^{*},S]
is an orthogonal projection onto

\mathcal{N}=\bigvee\{\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}:\bm{k}=(k_{1},\dots,k_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},k_{j}\text{ is even for some }1\leq j\leq n\},

that is,
[S^{*},S]=P_{\mathcal{N}}
.

Now for
\bm{\xi}=(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n})\in\mathbb{D}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\widetilde{[S^{*},S]}(\bm{\xi})=\|P_{\mathcal{N}}k_{\bm{\xi}}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-|\xi_{j}|^{2})\right)\left\|P_{\mathcal{N}}\left(\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}}\overline{\bm{\xi}}^{\bm{k}}\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}\right)\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-|\xi_{j}|^{2})\right)\left\|\sum_{\underset{\text{some $k_{j}$ even}}{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n}}}\overline{\bm{\xi}}^{\bm{k}}\bm{z}^{\bm{k}}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-|\xi_{j}|^{2})\right)\left(\sum_{\text{some $k_{j}$ even}}|\xi_{1}|^{2k_{1}}\dots|\xi_{n}|^{2k_{n}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-|\xi_{j}|^{2})\right)\left(\sum_{\text{all $k_{j}$ even}}|\xi_{1}|^{2k_{1}}\dots|\xi_{n}|^{2k_{n}}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-|\xi_{j}|^{2})\right)\left(\sum_{k_{1}\text{ even}}|\xi_{1}|^{2k_{1}}\right)\dots\left(\sum_{k_{n}\text{ even}}|\xi_{n}|^{2k_{n}}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-|\xi_{j}|^{2})\right)\left(\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}|\xi_{1}|^{4m}\right)\dots\left(\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}|\xi_{n}|^{4m}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-|\xi_{j}|^{2})}{\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-|\xi_{j}|^{4})}={\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1+|\xi_{j}|^{2})^{-1}}\geq\frac{1}{2^{n}}>0.

Hence, by corollary
4.4
we conclude that the symbol calculus can not be extended to any
C^{*}
-algebra that contains
S
.

5.
Extension of Toeplitz Algebra

In this section, we present a family of
C^{*}
-algebras containing the Toeplitz algebra

\mathscr{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))
and the analogs of Theorem
4.3
still hold.

Let
\psi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
and
S\in\mathscr{S}
. Consider

(5.1)

\mathscr{B}(\psi,S)=\{A\in\mathfrak{B}(H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})):\|A(T_{\psi}+S)k_{\bm{\xi}}\|\text{ and }\|A^{*}(T_{\psi}+S)k_{\bm{\xi}}\|\to 0\text{ radially}\}.

In particular, if
\psi=1
a.e. on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
and
S=\bm{0}
, where
\bm{0}
denotes the zero operator on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
, then we simply write
\mathscr{B}=\mathscr{B}(1,\bm{0})
.

Lemma 5.1
.

\mathscr{B}(\psi,\bm{0})
is a
C^{*}
-algebra for all
\psi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
.

Proof.

Let
A_{1},A_{2}\in\mathscr{B}(\psi,\bm{0})
and
c\in\mathbb{C}
. Then
cA_{1}+A_{2},A_{1}A_{2}\in\mathscr{B}(\psi,\bm{0})

as

\displaystyle\|(cA_{1}+A_{2})T_{\psi}k_{\bm{\xi}}\|

\displaystyle\leq|c|\|A_{1}T_{\psi}k_{\bm{\xi}}\|+\|A_{2}T_{\psi}k_{\bm{\xi}}\|,

\displaystyle\|(cA_{1}+A_{2})^{*}T_{\psi}k_{\bm{\xi}}\|

\displaystyle\leq|c|\|A_{1}^{*}T_{\psi}k_{\bm{\xi}}\|+\|A_{2}^{*}T_{\psi}k_{\bm{\xi}}\|,

\|A_{1}A_{2}T_{\psi}k_{\bm{\xi}}\|\leq\|A_{1}\|\|A_{2}T_{\psi}k_{\bm{\xi}}\|\quad\text{and}\quad\|(A_{1}A_{2})^{*}T_{\psi}k_{\bm{\xi}}\|\leq\|A_{2}^{*}\|\|A_{1}^{*}T_{\psi}k_{\bm{\xi}}\|.

From the definition, it follows that if
A\in\mathscr{B}(\psi,\bm{0})
, then
A^{*}\in\mathscr{B}(\psi,\bm{0})
.

To see that
\mathscr{B}(\psi,\bm{0})
is closed, let
(A_{m})
be a sequence in
\mathscr{B}(\psi,\bm{0})
such that
\|A-A_{m}\|\to 0
as
m\to\infty
for some
A\in\mathfrak{B}(H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n}))
.
Now for any
\varepsilon>0
choose
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\|A-A_{N}\|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2(1+\|T_{\psi}\|)}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\|AT_{\psi}k_{\bm{\xi}}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|(A-A_{N})T_{\psi}k_{\bm{\xi}}\|+\|A_{N}T_{\psi}k_{\bm{\xi}}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|A-A_{N}\|\|T_{\psi}\|+\|A_{N}T_{\psi}k_{\bm{\xi}}\|

(5.2)

\displaystyle<\varepsilon/2+\|A_{N}T_{\psi}k_{\bm{\xi}}\|.

Since
\|A_{m}T_{\psi}k_{\bm{\xi}}\|\to 0
radially for each
m\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a measure zero subset
E_{m}
of
\mathbb{T}^{n}
such that

\|A_{m}T_{\psi}k_{(re^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,re^{i\theta_{n}})}\|\to 0\quad\text{as $r\to 1^{-}$},

for all
(e^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,e^{i\theta_{n}})\in\mathbb{T}^{n}\setminus E_{m}
. Consider
E=\cup_{m=1}^{\infty}E_{m}
of
\mathbb{T}^{n}
, and hence
E
is a measure zero subset. Thus for a fixed

(e^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,e^{i\theta_{n}})\in\mathbb{T}^{n}\setminus E
, we choose
0<s<1
such that

\|A_{N}T_{\psi}k_{(re^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,re^{i\theta_{n}})}\|<\varepsilon/2
whenever
s<r<1
. Hence the equation (
5.2
) implies that

s<r<1\implies\|AT_{\psi}k_{(re^{i\theta_{1}},\dots,re^{i\theta_{n}})}\|<\varepsilon.

Therefore,
\|AT_{\psi}k_{\bm{\xi}}\|\to 0
radially. Now
\|A_{m}^{*}-A^{*}\|=\|A_{m}-A\|\to 0
as
m\to\infty
and
\|A_{m}^{*}T_{\psi}k_{\bm{\xi}}\|\to 0
radially for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
. It follows that
\|A^{*}T_{\psi}k_{\bm{\xi}}\|\to 0
radially. Thus
A\in\mathscr{B}(\psi,\bm{0})
and hence

\mathscr{B}(\psi,\bm{0})
is closed.

Lemma 5.2
.

\mathscr{C}\subseteq\mathscr{S}\subseteq\mathscr{B}
.

Proof.

Firstly, it is easy to see that
\mathscr{C}\subseteq\mathscr{S}
. Let
\varphi,\psi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
. Then

\displaystyle\|[T_{\varphi},T_{\psi})k_{\bm{\xi}}\|

\displaystyle=\|H_{\overline{\varphi}}^{*}H_{\psi}k_{\bm{\xi}}\|\leq\|H_{\overline{\varphi}}^{*}\|\|H_{\psi}k_{\bm{\xi}}\|,

\displaystyle\text{ and }\|[T_{\varphi},T_{\psi})^{*}k_{\bm{\xi}}\|

\displaystyle=\|(H_{\overline{\varphi}}^{*}H_{\psi})^{*}k_{\bm{\xi}}\|\leq\|H_{\psi}^{*}\|\|H_{\overline{\varphi}}k_{\bm{\xi}}\|.

Now Theorem
4.1
infers that
[T_{\varphi},T_{\psi})\in\mathscr{B}
,
and hence
\mathscr{C}\subseteq\mathscr{S}\subseteq\mathscr{B}
.

Lemma 5.3
.

\mathscr{B}(\psi,S)=\mathscr{B}(\psi,\bm{0})
for all
\psi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
and
S\in\mathscr{S}
.

Proof.

It follows easily from Lemma
5.2
that
\mathscr{B}(\psi,\bm{0})\subseteq\mathscr{B}(\psi,S)
.

Suppose
\psi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
and
S\in\mathscr{S}
, and let
A\in\mathscr{B}(\psi,S)
.
Then
\|Sk_{\bm{\xi}}\|\to 0
radially, and we also have

\displaystyle\|AT_{\psi}k_{\bm{\xi}}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|A(T_{\psi}+S)k_{\bm{\xi}}\|+\|A\|\|Sk_{\bm{\xi}}\|\to 0,

\displaystyle\|A^{*}T_{\psi}k_{\bm{\xi}}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|A^{*}(T_{\psi}+S)k_{\bm{\xi}}\|+\|A\|\|Sk_{\bm{\xi}}\|\to 0.

Therefore,
A\in\mathscr{B}(\psi,\bm{0})
.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
\varphi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
. Then
\|P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}(\varphi k_{\bm{\xi}})-\widetilde{\varphi}(\bm{\xi})k_{\bm{\xi}}\|\to 0
radially. In particular,
\|T_{\varphi}k_{\bm{\xi}}-\widetilde{\varphi}(\bm{\xi})k_{\bm{\xi}}\|\to 0
radially for
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
.

Proof.

First, assume that
\varphi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
. Then we have

\displaystyle\|P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}(\varphi k_{\bm{\xi}})-\widetilde{\varphi}(\bm{\xi})k_{\bm{\xi}}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}(\varphi k_{\bm{\xi}})\|^{2}+\|\widetilde{\varphi}(\bm{\xi})k_{\bm{\xi}}\|^{2}-{\langle}P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}(\varphi k_{\bm{\xi}}),\widetilde{\varphi}(\bm{\xi})k_{\bm{\xi}}{\rangle}-{\langle}\widetilde{\varphi}(\bm{\xi})k_{\bm{\xi}},P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}(\varphi k_{\bm{\xi}}){\rangle}

\displaystyle=\|P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}(\varphi k_{\bm{\xi}})\|^{2}+\|\widetilde{\varphi}(\bm{\xi})k_{\bm{\xi}}\|^{2}-\overline{\widetilde{\varphi}(\bm{\xi})}{\langle}P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}(\varphi k_{\bm{\xi}}),k_{\bm{\xi}}{\rangle}-\widetilde{\varphi}(\bm{\xi}){\langle}k_{\bm{\xi}},P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}(\varphi k_{\bm{\xi}}){\rangle}.

Now Corollary
3.5
yields

\|P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}(\varphi k_{\bm{\xi}})-\widetilde{\varphi}(\bm{\xi})k_{\bm{\xi}}\|\to 0\quad\text{ radially}.

Finally, if
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
, then
P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}(\varphi k_{\bm{\xi}})=T_{\varphi}k_{\bm{\xi}}
and hence the required result follows.

Proposition 5.5
.

If
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
and
A\in\mathscr{B}(\psi,\bm{0})
, then
T_{\varphi}A,AT_{\varphi}\in\mathscr{B}(\psi,\bm{0})
.

Proof.

To show
T_{\varphi}A
and
AT_{\varphi}
are in
\mathscr{B}(\psi,\bm{0})
for
A\in\mathscr{B}(\psi,\bm{0})
and
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
, it suffices to check that
\|AT_{\varphi}T_{\psi}k_{\bm{\xi}}\|\to 0
radially. Note that

\displaystyle\|AT_{\varphi}T_{\psi}k_{\bm{\xi}}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|AT_{\varphi}T_{\psi}k_{\bm{\xi}}-\widetilde{\varphi}(\bm{\xi})AT_{\psi}k_{\bm{\xi}}\|+\|\widetilde{\varphi}(\bm{\xi})AT_{\psi}k_{\bm{\xi}}\|

(5.3)

\displaystyle\leq\|A\|\|T_{\varphi}T_{\psi}k_{\bm{\xi}}-\widetilde{\varphi}(\bm{\xi})T_{\psi}k_{\bm{\xi}}\|+\|\varphi\|_{\infty}\|AT_{\psi}k_{\bm{\xi}}\|.

Also

\|T_{\varphi}T_{\psi}k_{\bm{\xi}}-\widetilde{\varphi}(\bm{\xi})T_{\psi}k_{\bm{\xi}}\|^{2}=(T_{\psi}^{*}T_{\varphi}^{*}T_{\varphi}T_{\psi}\widetilde{)}(\bm{\xi})+|\widetilde{\varphi}(\bm{\xi})|^{2}\|T_{\psi}k_{\bm{\xi}}\|^{2}-2\text{Re}\left(\overline{\widetilde{\varphi}(\bm{\xi})}(T_{\psi}^{*}T_{\varphi}T_{\psi}\widetilde{)}(\bm{\xi})\right).

Applying Theorems
2.1
,
4.3
, and
3.2
, we obtain

\|T_{\varphi}T_{\psi}k_{\bm{\xi}}-\widetilde{\varphi}(\bm{\xi})T_{\psi}k_{\bm{\xi}}\|^{2}\to|\varphi\psi|^{2}+|\varphi\psi|^{2}-2|\varphi\psi|^{2}=0\quad\text{radially.}

Hence
\|AT_{\varphi}T_{\psi}k_{\bm{\xi}}\|\to 0
radially follows from (
5.3
).

An immediate consequence of Proposition
5.5
is the following.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
\psi_{1}
and
\psi_{2}
be two inner functions on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
and let
\psi=\overline{\psi_{1}}\psi_{2}
. Then
\mathscr{B}(\psi,\bm{0})=\mathscr{B}
.

Proof.

Follows from Proposition
5.5
.

Now we record the following properties of the collection
\mathscr{B}
defined
in (
5.1
):

Remark 5.7
.

Suppose
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(T^{n})
and
T_{\varphi}\in\mathscr{B}(\psi,\bm{0})
, where
|\psi|>0
a.e. on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
. Then

|\widetilde{T_{\varphi\psi}}(\bm{\xi})|\leq\|T_{\varphi\psi}k_{\bm{\xi}}\|\leq\|[T_{\varphi},T_{\psi})k_{\bm{\xi}}\|+\|T_{\varphi}T_{\psi}k_{\bm{\xi}}\|\to 0\quad\text{radially.}

Now Theorem
3.2
yields that
\varphi\psi\equiv 0
a.e. on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
. But
|\psi|>0
a.e. on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
, hence
\varphi\equiv 0
a.e. on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
.
Thus, any Toeplitz operator in
\mathscr{B}(\psi,\bm{0})
corresponds to the symbol
0
.

For
\psi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
with
|\psi|=1
a.e. on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
, consider

\mathscr{A}_{\psi}:=\{T_{\varphi}+A:\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}),A\in\mathscr{B}(\psi,\bm{0})\}.

Theorem 5.8
.

The collection
\mathscr{A}_{\psi}
satisfies the following:

(1)

If
T\in\mathscr{A}_{\psi}
, then
\widetilde{T}\to\varphi
radially for some
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
.

(2)

\mathscr{A}_{\psi}
is a
C^{*}
-algebra.

(3)

Define

\Sigma_{\psi}:\mathscr{A}_{\psi}\to L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})\quad\text{by}\quad\Sigma_{\psi}(T)=\varphi,

where
\varphi
is as in
(1)
. Then
\Sigma_{\psi}
induces a

C^{*}
-algebra homomorphism from
\mathscr{A}_{\psi}
onto
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
such that

\Sigma_{\psi}(T_{\varphi})=\varphi
for all
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
.

Proof.

(1)

Let
T\in\mathscr{A}_{\psi}
. Then
T=T_{\varphi}+A
for some
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}),A\in\mathscr{B}(\psi,\bm{0})
. Now Theorem
3.2

implies that

\widetilde{T}=\widetilde{T_{\varphi}}+\widetilde{A}\to\varphi\quad\text{radially}.

Also

\displaystyle|\widetilde{A}(\bm{\xi})|

\displaystyle=|{\langle}Ak_{\bm{\xi}},k_{\bm{\xi}}{\rangle}|

\displaystyle=|{\langle}\psi Ak_{\bm{\xi}},\psi k_{\bm{\xi}}{\rangle}|

\displaystyle\leq|{\langle}P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}(\psi Ak_{\bm{\xi}}),P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}(\psi k_{\bm{\xi}}){\rangle}|+|{\langle}P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})^{\perp}}(\psi Ak_{\bm{\xi}}),P_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})^{\perp}}(\psi k_{\bm{\xi}}){\rangle}|

\displaystyle=|{\langle}T_{\psi}Ak_{\bm{\xi}},T_{\psi}k_{\bm{\xi}}{\rangle}|+|{\langle}H_{\psi}Ak_{\bm{\xi}},H_{\psi}k_{\bm{\xi}}{\rangle}|

\displaystyle\leq\|A^{*}T_{\psi}^{*}T_{\psi}k_{\bm{\xi}}\|+\|H_{\psi}A\|\|H_{\psi}k_{\bm{\xi}}\|.

Thus, Proposition
5.5
and Theorem
4.1
infer that
\widetilde{A}(\bm{\xi})\to 0
radially.

(2)

It is immediate from Lemma
5.1
that
\mathscr{A}_{\psi}
is linear space and closed under adjoint. Let
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
, and
A_{1},A_{2}\in\mathscr{B}(\psi,\bm{0})
. Then

\displaystyle(T_{\varphi_{1}}+A_{1})(T_{\varphi_{2}}+A_{2})

\displaystyle=T_{\varphi_{1}}T_{\varphi_{2}}+T_{\varphi_{1}}A_{2}+A_{1}T_{\varphi_{2}}+A_{1}A_{2}

(5.4)

\displaystyle=T_{\varphi_{1}\varphi_{2}}-([T_{\varphi_{1}},T_{\varphi_{2}})-T_{\varphi_{1}}A_{2}-A_{1}T_{\varphi_{2}}-A_{1}A_{2}).

Using Lemma
5.1
, Lemma
5.2
, and Proposition
5.5
, we have
\mathscr{A}_{\psi}
is closed
under multiplication.

To show that
\mathscr{A}_{\psi}
is closed, we show
\mathscr{A}_{\psi}=\overline{\mathscr{A}_{\psi}}
. Consider

\eta_{0}:\mathscr{A}_{\psi}\to L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})\quad\text{defined by}\quad\eta_{0}(T_{\varphi}+A)=\varphi.

It is easily seen that
\eta_{0}
is a well-defined bounded linear map from
\mathscr{A}_{\psi}
onto
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}).
Suppose
\eta
is the bounded linear extension of
\eta_{0}
from
\overline{\mathscr{A}_{\psi}}
onto
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
. Assume that
T\in\overline{\mathscr{A}_{\psi}}
. Then there exists a sequence
\{T_{\varphi_{m}}+A_{m}\}
in

\mathscr{A}_{\psi}
such that
\|(T_{\varphi_{m}}+A_{m})-T\|\to 0
as
m\to\infty
, and hence

\varphi_{m}=\eta(T_{\varphi_{m}}+A_{m})\to\eta(T)
in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}).
Thus

\|T_{\varphi_{m}}-T_{\eta(T)}\|=\|\varphi_{m}-\eta(T)\|_{\infty}\to 0\quad\mbox{as}~m\to\infty.

Therefore,
A_{m}=(T_{\varphi_{m}}+A_{m})-T_{\varphi_{m}}\to T-T_{\eta(T)}
as
m\to\infty
.
Since
\mathscr{B}(\psi,\bm{0})
is closed,
T-T_{\eta(T)}\in\mathscr{B}(\psi,\bm{0})
. Therefore,
T=T_{\eta(T)}+(T-T_{\eta(T)})\in\mathscr{A}_{\psi}
.

(3)

It is easy to check that the mapping
\Sigma_{\psi}
is well-defined linear, preserves conjugation. Also (
5.4
) yields that the map is multiplicative too. Thus
\Sigma_{\psi}
is a
C^{*}
-algebra homomorphism from
\mathscr{A}_{\psi}
onto
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
, and also
\Sigma_{\psi}(T_{\varphi})=\varphi
with ker
(\Sigma_{\psi})=\mathscr{B}(\psi,\bm{0})
.

It is obvious from Lemma
5.2
that

\mathscr{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))\subseteq\mathscr{A}_{\psi}
. Following example shows that Theorem
5.8
is indeed an extension of Theorem
4.3
.

Example 5.9
.

Let
P=P_{\mathcal{M}}
be the orthogonal projection of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
onto the closed subspace
\mathcal{M}
, where
\mathcal{M}:=\bigvee_{m=0}^{\infty}\{z^{2^{m}}\}
. Then for
m\geq 1

\|(T_{z}P-PT_{z})z^{2^{m}}\|=\|z^{2^{m}+1}-Pz^{2^{m}+1}\|=1.

But
z^{2^{m}}\to 0
weakly as
m\to\infty
, and hence
T_{z}P-PT_{Z}
is not a compact operator. Therefore
P\notin\mathscr{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))
(see
[
2
]
). Also it is shown in
[
12
, Example 8]
that
\|Pk_{re^{i\theta}}\|\to 0
as
r\to 1^{-}
for a.e.
e^{i\theta}\in\mathbb{T}.
Consider an operator
A
on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
defined as

A=\overbrace{P\otimes I\otimes\dots\otimes I}^{n\text{-times}},

where
I
denotes the identity operator on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
. Now for
\bm{\xi}=(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n})\in\mathbb{D}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\|Ak_{\bm{\xi}}\|

\displaystyle=\|(P\otimes I\otimes\cdots\otimes I)(k_{\xi_{1}}\otimes k_{\xi_{2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes k_{\xi_{n}})\|

\displaystyle=\|Pk_{\xi_{1}}\otimes k_{\xi_{2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes k_{\xi_{n}}\|

\displaystyle=\|Pk_{\xi_{1}}\|\|k_{\xi_{2}}\|\cdots\|k_{\xi_{n}}\|

\displaystyle=\|Pk_{\xi_{1}}\|.

It follows that
\|Ak_{\bm{\xi}}\|\to 0
radially, and
A\in\mathscr{A}_{\psi}
, in fact
A\in\mathscr{B}
. Let
T_{z_{i}}
denote the multiplication operator on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
by the
i
-th coordinate function
z_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq n
, that is,
(T_{z_{i}}f)(\bm{\xi})=\xi_{i}f(\bm{\xi})
for all
\bm{\xi}\in\mathbb{D}^{n},f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. Consider

\mathbb{E}:=\{T\in\mathfrak{B}(H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})):T_{z_{i}}^{*}TT_{z_{i}}-T\text{ is compact for all }1\leq i\leq n\}.

Then
\mathbb{E}
is a norm-closed algebra. Now if
T_{\varphi}
is Toeplitz operator on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
, then
T_{z_{i}}^{*}T_{\varphi}T_{z_{i}}=T_{\varphi}
for all
1\leq i\leq n
(see
[
15
]
). Hence
\mathbb{E}
contains all Toeplitz operators on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. We show that
A\notin\mathscr{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))
by showing that
A\notin\mathbb{E}
. Now

T_{z_{1}}^{*}AT_{z_{1}}-A=(T_{z}^{*}PT_{z}-P)\otimes I\otimes\cdots\otimes I,

and
T_{z}^{*}PT_{z}-P
is not compact. Therefore,
T_{z_{1}}^{*}AT_{z_{1}}-A
is not compact
(see
[
14
]
) and hence
A\notin\mathbb{E}
.

Denote

\mathscr{A}=\{T_{\varphi}+A:\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}),A\in\mathscr{B}\}.

Note that if
A\in\mathscr{B}
then Proposition
5.5
gives that
A\in\mathscr{B}(\psi,\bm{0})
for each
\psi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
, and hence

\mathscr{A}\subseteq\mathscr{A}_{\psi}
. In the next result we prove that the class
\mathscr{A}
is maximal onto which the
symbol map
exists and
T_{\varphi}\to\varphi
, provided that the symbol of an operator is obtained as the radial limit of its
Berezin transform
.

Proposition 5.10
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be any closed
C^{*}
-subalgebra of
\mathfrak{B}(H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n}))
containing Toeplitz operators such that there exists a
C^{*}
-homomorphism
\mathfrak{S}
from
\mathcal{A}
onto
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
with
\mathfrak{S}(T_{\varphi})=\varphi
for all
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
, and for each
T\in\mathcal{A}
,
\mathfrak{S}(T)
is the radial limit of
\widetilde{T}
. Then
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathscr{A}.
In particular,
\mathscr{A}_{\psi}=\mathscr{A}
, where
\mathscr{A}_{\psi}
is as in Theorem
5.8
.

Proof.

Let
T\in\mathcal{A}
. Suppose
\mathfrak{S}(T)=\varphi
for some
\varphi
in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
. Then
T-T_{\varphi}\in ker(\mathfrak{S})
and hence

\mathcal{A}=\{T_{\varphi}+A:\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}),A\in ker(\mathfrak{S})\}.

Now if
A\in ker(\mathfrak{S})
, then
A^{*}A
is so. Therefore,

\|Ak_{\bm{\xi}}\|^{2}=\langle Ak_{\bm{\xi}},Ak_{\bm{\xi}}\rangle=\langle A^{*}Ak_{\bm{\xi}},k_{\bm{\xi}}\rangle=\widetilde{(A^{*}A)}(\bm{\xi})\to 0\text{ radially.}

In the similar fashion, it can be shown that
\|A^{*}k_{\bm{\xi}}\|\to 0
radially. Thus
ker(\mathfrak{S})\subseteq\mathscr{B}
, and hence
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathscr{A}
.

Following Theorem
5.8
, in particular, we have

\mathscr{A}_{\psi}=\mathscr{A}
for all
\psi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
with
|\psi|=1
a.e. on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
.

We shall conclude this section with the following application of our
symbol map

result, which gives a partial answer in some sense to the question raised by Barría and Halmos in
[
2
]
:

Which projections belong to the Toeplitz algebra
\mathscr{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}))
?

Using the
symbol map
, that is, Theorem
4.3
, we shall show that if
P
is a projection in
\mathscr{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}))
which is a finite rank perturbation of a Toeplitz operator, then the rank of
P
is finite or
I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D})}-P
is finite rank, that is, co-finite. However, for
n>1
, if a projection in

\mathscr{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))
is compact perturbation of a Toeplitz operator, then its rank
is either finite or co-finite. More precisely, we have the following result:

Theorem 5.11
.

Let
P
be an orthogonal projection in the Toeplitz algebra
\mathscr{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))
.
For
n=1
, if
P=T_{\varphi}+F
, where
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
and
F
is of finite rank, then either
P
or
I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D})}-P
is of finite rank. For
n>1
, if
P=T_{\varphi}+K
, where
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
and
K
is compact, then either
P
or
I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-P
is of finite rank.

Proof.

Suppose
P
is an orthogonal projection in the Toeplitz algebra
\mathscr{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))

such that
P
is a either finite or compact perturbation of a Toeplitz operator
T_{\varphi}
for
n\geq 1
. Then Theorem
4.3
yields
\overline{\varphi}=\varphi
and
\varphi^{2}=\varphi
a.e. on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
. Hence
\varphi=\chi_{E}
, where
E=\{\bm{\zeta}\in\mathbb{T}^{n}:\varphi(\bm{\zeta})=1\}
, a measurable subset of
\mathbb{T}^{n}
.

For
n=1
: If
P=T_{\chi_{E}}+F
, where
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
and
F
is of finite rank, then
P^{2}=P
gives

T_{\chi_{E}}-T^{2}_{\chi_{E}}=F^{2}-F+T_{\chi_{E}}F+FT_{\chi_{E}},

and hence
T_{\chi_{E}}T_{\chi_{E^{c}}}=T_{\chi_{E}}-T^{2}_{\chi_{E}}
is of finite rank. Therefore, either
T_{\chi_{E}}=0
or
T_{\chi_{E^{c}}}=0
by
[
7
, Corollary 2.2]
. Thus, either
P
or
I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D})}-P
is of finite rank.

For
n>1
: we shall get

T_{\chi_{E}}-T^{2}_{\chi_{E}}=K^{2}-K+T_{\chi_{E}}K+KT_{\chi_{E}},

where
K
is a compact operator. But
T_{\chi_{E}}T_{\chi_{E^{c}}}=T_{\chi_{E}}-T^{2}_{\chi_{E}}=H_{\chi_{E}}^{*}H_{\chi_{E}}
which is compact, and hence
H_{\chi_{E}}
is compact. Now
[
6
, Corollary 5]
, gives all compact Hankel operators are zero whenever
n>1
. Thus
T_{\chi_{E}}T_{\chi_{E^{c}}}=0
implies either
T_{\chi_{E}}=0
or
T_{\chi_{E^{c}}}=0
by Theorem 2.1 in
[
7
]
. Therefore,
P=T_{\chi_{E}}+K
gives either
P
or
I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-P
is compact. Since both
P
and
I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-P
are orthogonal projections and all compact orthogonal projections are of finite rank; therefore either
P
or
I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-P
is of finite rank.

This completes the proof.

6.
Concluding Remarks

The Toeplitz algebra
\mathscr{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))
provides an effective way to study the Toeplitz operators as elements of a
C^{*}
-algebra. More specifically, Theorem
4.3
shows that the study of the Toeplitz algebra

\mathscr{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))
is largely reduces to that of the semi-commutator ideal
\mathscr{S}
.
Let
\theta
be an inner function on
\mathbb{D}^{n}
, and
\mathcal{K}_{\theta}=H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})\ominus\theta H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
. Suppose
P_{\theta}
denotes the orthogonal projection of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})
onto

\mathcal{K}_{\theta}
. Then

P_{\theta}=I_{H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n})}-T_{\theta}T_{\overline{\theta}}=[T_{\theta},T_{\overline{\theta}})\in\mathscr{S}.

In fact, if
\varphi=\theta_{1}\overline{\eta_{1}}
and
\psi=\theta_{2}\overline{\eta_{2}}
, where
\theta_{i},\eta_{i}
are inner functions, then

\displaystyle[T_{\varphi},T_{\psi})

\displaystyle=T_{\varphi\psi}-T_{\varphi}T_{\psi}

\displaystyle=T_{\theta_{1}\overline{\eta_{1}}\theta_{2}\overline{\eta_{2}}}-T_{\theta_{1}\overline{\eta_{1}}}T_{\theta_{2}\overline{\eta_{2}}}

\displaystyle=T_{\overline{\eta_{1}}}T_{\overline{\eta_{2}}}T_{\theta_{1}}T_{\theta_{2}}-T_{\overline{\eta_{1}}}T_{\theta_{1}}T_{\overline{\eta_{2}}}T_{\theta_{2}}

\displaystyle=T_{\overline{\eta_{1}}}T_{\overline{\eta_{2}}}T_{\theta_{1}}(I-T_{\eta_{2}}T_{\overline{\eta_{2}}})T_{\theta_{2}}

\displaystyle=T_{\overline{\eta_{1}}}T_{\overline{\eta_{2}}}T_{\theta_{1}}P_{\eta_{2}}T_{\theta_{2}}.

In the case for
n=1
, the semi-commutator ideal coincides with the commutator ideal in

\mathscr{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}))
due to the availability of Douglas-Rudin theorm; again from the above calculation the semi-commutator ideal coincides with the closed ideal in

\mathscr{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}))
generated by all orthogonal projections on the model space.
Thus, Douglas-Rudin theorem (see
[
10
]
) plays an important role in this study. We now conclude this paper by raising some questions which naturally arise in our paper:

Question 1. Is it true that the class
\{\overline{\varphi}\psi:\varphi,\psi\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})\}
is norm-dense in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
for
n>1
?

Question 2. Does the semi-commutator ideal coincide with the commutator ideal in

\mathscr{T}(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))
for
n>1
?

It is noteworthy to point out that the affirmative answer to Question 1 gives an affirmative answer to Question 2, which we shall try to carry out in the future paper.

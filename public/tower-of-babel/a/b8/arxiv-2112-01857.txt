Disentangling modes with crossover instantaneous frequencies by synchrosqueezed chirplet transforms, from theory to application
Ziyu Chen, Hau-Tieng Wu

Abstract
. Analysis of signals with oscillatory modes with crossover instantaneous frequencies is a challenging problem in time series analysis. One way to handle this problem is lifting the 2-dimensional time-frequency representation to a 3-dimensional representation, called time-frequency-chirp rate (TFC) representation, by adding one extra chirp rate parameter so that crossover frequencies are disentangled in higher dimension. The chirplet transform is an algorithm for this lifting idea, which leads to a TFC representation. However, in practice, we found that it has a strong “blurring” effect in the chirp rate axis, which limits its application in real-world data. Moreover, to our knowledge, we have limited mathematical understanding of the chirplet transform in the literature. Motivated by the need for the real-world data analysis, in this paper, we propose the synchrosqueezed chirplet transform (SCT) that enhances the TFC representation given by the chirplet transform. The resulting concentrated TFC representation has high contrast so that one can better distinguish different modes with crossover instantaneous frequencies. The basic idea is to use the phase information in the chirplet transform to determine a reassignment rule that sharpens the TFC representation determined by the chirplet transform. We also analyze the chirplet transform and provide theoretical guarantees of SCT.



1.
Introduction

Time series (or signals) are ubiquitous in almost all scientific fields. Often, the observed/recorded signal is composed of multiple components.
Due to the nonstationary dynamics of our natural system, if the component is oscillatory, it usually oscillates with time-varying amplitudes, frequencies and even oscillatory patterns. On the other hand, if the component is stochastic, it could be a nonstationary random process with time-varying statistical properties. These time-varying quantities encode rich information about the dynamics of the underlying system of interest. By properly extracting and parsing these time-varying quantities, we could peek into nature and decode the underlying dynamics. In this paper, we focus on analyzing signals with oscillatory components and extracting the dynamics.

To properly extract and parse the time-varying quantities of each oscillatory component from the input signal is however not easy. For example, due to the nonstationarity, the traditional Fourier transform cannot properly capture such information, since the Fourier transform is a global operator. An intuitive and successful solution to handle the nonstationarity is “divide-and-conquer”. By dividing the signal into pieces, and assuming that each piece is stationary, we could apply Fourier transform over each piece to capture the nonstationarity.
This intuitive idea has led to a rich field called time-frequency (TF) analysis, where the “spectrum” at each moment is evaluated so that the time-varying frequency and amplitude could be evaluated. It includes various algorithms, ranging from linear-type, polynomial-type to nonlinear-type algorithms. For example, the short-time Fourier transform (STFT), the continuous wavelet transform (CWT) and the S transform are linear-type algorithms. The Wigner-Ville, Cohen’s class and affine class are bilinear-type algorithms
[
18
]
. The polynomial Wigner-Ville distributions are polynomial-type algorithms
[
4
,
5
,
2

[

, the synchrosqueezing transform (SST)
[

[

[

[

and its variations are nonlinear-type algorithms. We refer readers with interest to a recent review of the topic
[
44

. In short, these algorithms convert an input signal into a TF representation and/or oscillatory components. From the output, we could more efficiently visualize or extract different time-varying properties of the input signal.
While the linear-type algorithms have been widely applied, one well-known property shared by these algorithms is the uncertainty principle. This uncertainty principle leads to a blurred TF representation so that the information of interest, like the ridges
[

While we have advanced the analysis tools from various fronts in the past 20 years, there are still several challenges. In this paper, we focus on the specific crossover frequency challenge. Recall that most of the above algorithms are based on the assumption that different oscillatory components have “well-separated” time-varying frequencies in the TF plane. Mathematically, this well-separation condition is defined in the following way. Suppose a signal is a superposition of multiple
intrinsic-mode-type

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\phi_{k}^{\prime}(t)-\phi_{j}^{\prime}(t)\right\rvert}\geq\Delta

When this sell-separation condition is broken, for example, when the time-varying frequencies of two oscillatory components are too close, or even crossover, the algorithm fails.
Mathematically, when

While CT has been proposed to analyze signals with crossover IFs, there are several interesting open problems. First, the TFC representation determined by CT with a window

It is thus beneficial if we could enhance the TFC representation for some specific applications. This blurring effect also raised our curiosity about the theoretical properties of CT. For example, if the input signal is

In this paper, we seek to answer these questions, at least partially, and provide a practical algorithm to separate components with crossover IFs from a multicomponent signal. We propose to combine the “squeezing” idea with CT, and synchrosqueezed chirplet transform (SCT) that gives a sharper TFC representation of a signal, even if the signal has crossover IFs, from which one can separate different components. In addition to providing several theoretical properties of CT and a theoretical guarantee of SCT, we provide a series of numerical experiments with real data illustration. We shall mention that a seemingly similar squeezing idea has been shown in
[
47
]
. However, despite the nomination “squeeze”, the squeezing part was only applied to the frequency axis that is based on the chirp rate information and only the TF representation was considered, and only limited theoretical analysis of the squeezing step is provided.

2.
Chirplet transform and its squeezing

2.1.
Chirplet transform

We start our exploration by introducing the definition of CT.
Denote
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
to be the Schwartz space and
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
to be the space of tempered distributions.

Definition 1

(Chirplet transforms (CT)
[
29
]
)
.

The chirplet transform of a tempered distribution
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
associated with a window function
g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
at
(t,\xi,\lambda)
is defined as

T_{f}^{(g)}(t,\xi,\lambda)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\langle f(\cdot),\,g(\cdot-t)e^{2\pi i\xi(\cdot-t)}e^{\pi i\lambda(\cdot-t)^{2}}\rangle,

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
indicates the evaluation of a tempered distribution at a Schwartz function,
t\in\mathbb{R}
indicates
time
,
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
indicates
frequency
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
indicates the
chirp rate
.

In general, since
g
is Schwartz,
T_{f}^{(g)}(t,\xi,\lambda)
is well defined at each
(t,\xi,\lambda)
, and it is a complex function on the TFC domain. Note that when
f
is a function,

T_{f}^{(g)}(t,\xi,\lambda)
has an integration form
\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)\overline{g(x-t)}e^{-2\pi i\xi(x-t)}e^{-\pi i\lambda(x-t)^{2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}
.

Definition 2

(Time-frequency-chirp rate representation and time-frequency representation determined by the chirplet transform)
.

Following Definition
1
, we call
|T_{f}^{(g)}(t,\xi,\lambda)|
the
TFC representation
of a signal
f
with window
g
.
We define the
TF representation determined by CT
as the projection of the TFC representation onto the TF domain, denoted as
\mathfrak{T}_{f}^{(g)}(t,\xi)
, which is defined as

\mathfrak{T}_{f}^{(g)}(t,\xi)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert T_{f}^{(g)}(t,\xi,\lambda)\right\rvert}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{\lambda}\,.

From the dictionary learning perspective,
T_{f}^{(g)}(t,\xi,\lambda)
comes from fitting an atom with frequency
\xi
and chirp rate
\lambda
when the signal is centered at
t
and truncated by the Gaussian window.

In the bottom-right subfigure of Figure
1
, although we can observe two peaks that correspond to the chirp rates of
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
, those peaks are not sharp in the chirp rate direction. An immediate question we would like to ask is the uncertainty principle associated with CT. We illustrate this point by evaluating CT of a pure linear chirp
f(x)=e^{2\pi i\xi_{0}x+\pi i\lambda_{0}x^{2}}
, where
\xi_{0},\lambda_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, and show that we cannot simultaneously sharply localize a signal in both the time domain and chirp rate domain.
By directly expanding the CT with the window
g(x)=e^{-\pi\alpha x^{2}}
, where
\alpha>0
, we have

\displaystyle T_{f}^{(g)}(t,\xi,\lambda)

\displaystyle=\int e^{2\pi i\xi_{0}x+\pi i\lambda_{0}x^{2}}e^{-\pi\alpha(x-t)^{2}}e^{-2\pi i\xi(x-t)}e^{-\pi i\lambda(x-t)^{2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle=e^{2\pi i\xi_{0}t+\pi i\lambda_{0}t^{2}}\int e^{-\pi(\alpha-i\lambda_{0}+i\lambda)x^{2}}e^{-2\pi i(-\xi_{0}-\lambda_{0}t+\xi)x}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}

(1)

\displaystyle=e^{2\pi i\xi_{0}t+\pi i\lambda_{0}t^{2}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha+i(\lambda-\lambda_{0})}}e^{\frac{-\pi(\xi-\xi_{0}-\lambda_{0}t)^{2}}{\alpha+i(\lambda-\lambda_{0})}},

whose magnitude is

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert T_{f}^{(g)}(t,\xi,\lambda)\right\rvert}=\frac{1}{\sqrt[4]{\alpha^{2}+(\lambda-\lambda_{0})^{2}}}e^{\frac{-\pi\alpha(\xi-\xi_{0}-\lambda_{0}t)^{2}}{\alpha^{2}+(\lambda-\lambda_{0})^{2}}}.

The decay of
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert T_{f}^{(g)}(t,\xi,\lambda)\right\rvert}
is related to the window
g(x)=e^{-\pi\alpha x^{2}}
in the following Lemma.

Lemma 1
.

Take
f(x)=e^{2\pi i\xi_{0}x+\pi i\lambda_{0}x^{2}}
. For a window
g(x)=e^{-\pi\alpha x^{2}}
with fixed
\alpha>0
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
, when
\xi
is sufficiently close to
\xi_{0}+\lambda_{0}t
, we have

\frac{\sqrt{\alpha}}{(\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\lambda-\lambda_{0}\right\rvert}^{2}+\alpha^{2})^{\frac{1}{4}}}\max_{\lambda}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert T_{f}^{(g)}(t,\xi,\lambda)\right\rvert}\leq\mathinner{\!\left\lvert T_{f}^{(g)}(t,\xi,\lambda)\right\rvert}\leq\frac{e^{\frac{1}{4}}\sqrt{\alpha}}{\sqrt{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\lambda-\lambda_{0}\right\rvert}}}\max_{\lambda}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert T_{f}^{(g)}(t,\xi,\lambda)\right\rvert}.

The proof of Lemma
1
can be found in
A.1
. This lemma indicates that for the linear chirp, the CT in the chirp rate axis decays slowly. To be more specific, the window
g(x)
becomes more localized in time as
\alpha
gets larger; larger
\alpha
indicates the magnitude of the chirplet transform decays slower in the chirp rate direction near
\lambda=\lambda_{0}
as can be seen in the lower bound. It also explains the blurring of the TFC representation shown in Figure
1
.

2.2.
Some properties of chirp transform

Before introducing our algorithm we discuss some properties of the general chirp transform.

Definition 3

(Chirp transforms)
.

The chirp transform of a function
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
is defined as

(2)

Tf(\lambda)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)e^{-\pi i\lambda x^{2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x},

where
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
.

Note that replacing
f(x)
by
f(x+t)g(x)e^{-2\pi i\xi x}
in (
2
) yields the chirplet transform of
f
with window
g
, where
g\in\mathcal{S}
, and the results below can be translated to that case.
We start by asking a basic question – since the chirp transform is similar to the Fourier transform, may we obtain those basic properties of the Fourier transform for the chirp transform? For example, is the chirp transform of a
L^{1}
function continuous and decay like that stated in the Riemann-Lebegue theorem? Does the chirp transform preserve the
L^{2}
norm like the Plancherel theorem? Or is it bounded from
L^{p}
to
L^{p}
for some
p
? Note that this problem is closely related to the polynomial Carleson operator and Elias Stein’s conjecture. See, for example
[
24
]
, for more details.
To study these properties, we introduce some function spaces that we will use in the following propositions. We say a function
f(x)\in W_{0}^{1,1}(\mathbb{R})
, if
\displaystyle\lim_{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert x\right\rvert}\to\infty}f(x)=0
, and both
f(x)
and
f^{\prime}(x)
in the weak sense are in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
; a function
f(x)\in C_{0}^{2n}(\mathbb{R})
, if
f
is
2n
times continuously differentiable and
\lim\limits_{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert x\right\rvert}\to\infty}f^{(k)}(x)=0
for
k=1,\dots,2n
. Also, denote
C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
to be the set of smooth functions with compact support.

We start with investigating if the magnitude of the chirp transform has some decay property in terms of chirp rate. Note that the following analysis of the decay rate is a special case of the oscillatory integral analysis widely known in the harmonic analysis community
[
38
]
, and we provide details for the sake of self-containedness.
First we have

Lemma 2
.

For
-\infty\leq a<b\leq\infty
, we have

\left|\int_{a}^{b}e^{-\pi i\lambda x^{2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}\right|\leq\frac{2\sqrt{6}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,.

Proof.

We only show the case where
0\in(a,b)
, the argument for
0\in\mathbb{R}\backslash(a,b)
is similar.
Choose
\delta>0
and split the integral into

\int_{a}^{-\delta}e^{-\pi i\lambda x^{2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}+\int_{-\delta}^{\delta}e^{-\pi i\lambda x^{2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}+\int_{\delta}^{b}e^{-\pi i\lambda x^{2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}

For the first integral,integration by parts gives

\displaystyle\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\int_{a}^{-\delta}e^{-\pi i\lambda x^{2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}\right\rvert}

\displaystyle=\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\int_{a}^{-\delta}\frac{1}{-2\pi i\lambda x}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{e^{-\pi i\lambda x^{2}}}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}\right\rvert}

\displaystyle\leq\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\frac{e^{-\pi i\lambda x^{2}}}{2\pi\lambda x}\bigg|_{x=a}^{-\delta}\right\rvert}+\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\int_{a}^{-\delta}e^{-\pi i\lambda x^{2}}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}}(\frac{1}{2\pi i\lambda x})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}\right\rvert}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\pi\lambda\delta}+\frac{1}{2\pi\lambda}\int_{a}^{-\delta}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}}(\frac{1}{x})\right\rvert}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi\lambda\delta}+\frac{1}{2\pi\lambda}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\int_{a}^{-\delta}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}}(\frac{1}{x})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}\right\rvert}\quad(\text{by the mononoticity of $\frac{1}{x}$ on $(a,-\delta)$})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\pi\lambda\delta}+\frac{1}{2\pi\lambda\delta}=\frac{3}{2\pi\lambda\delta}.

By the same argument, we also have

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\int_{\delta}^{b}e^{-\pi i\lambda x^{2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}\right\rvert}\leq\frac{3}{2\pi\lambda\delta},

combined with
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\int_{-\delta}^{\delta}e^{-\pi i\lambda x^{2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}\right\rvert}\leq 2\delta
, we have

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\int_{a}^{b}e^{-\pi i\lambda x^{2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}\right\rvert}\leq\frac{3}{\pi\lambda\delta}+2\delta.

Take
\delta=\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{\frac{3}{2\pi}}
that minimizes the right hand side, and we have

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\int_{a}^{b}e^{-\pi i\lambda x^{2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}\right\rvert}\leq\frac{2\sqrt{6}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

∎

Proposition 1
.

Suppose
f(x)\in W_{0}^{1,1}(\mathbb{R})
, then
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert Tf(\lambda)\right\rvert}\leq\frac{2\sqrt{6}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert f^{\prime}\right\rVert}_{1}\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}
.

Proof.

When
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})\subset W_{0}^{1,1}(\mathbb{R})
, by the change of variable, we have

Tf(\lambda)=\int f(x)\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{x}e^{-\pi i\lambda t^{2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{t}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}=-\int f^{\prime}(x)\left(\int_{-\infty}^{x}e^{-\pi i\lambda t^{2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{t}\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}\,,

so by Lemma
2
, we have

\left|Tf(\lambda)\right|\leq\int\left|f^{\prime}(x)\right|\left|\int_{-\infty}^{x}e^{-\pi i\lambda t^{2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{t}\right|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}\leq\frac{2\sqrt{6}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int\mathinner{\!\left\lvert f^{\prime}(x)\right\rvert}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}.

Since
C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
is dense in
W_{0}^{1,1}(\mathbb{R})
, we get the proof by a direct extension argument.
∎

We have several properties of the chirp transforms that are parallel to some properties of the Fourier transforms.

Proposition 2
.

Let
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, then
Tf(\lambda)
is uniformly continuous.

Proof.

Take
\epsilon>0
. For any
h,r>0
, we have the following control:

\displaystyle\mathinner{\!\left\lvert Tf(\lambda+h)-Tf(\lambda)\right\rvert}

\displaystyle=\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\int f(x)(e^{-\pi i(\lambda+h)x^{2}}-e^{-\pi i\lambda x^{2}})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}\right\rvert}

\displaystyle\leq\int\mathinner{\!\left\lvert f(x)\right\rvert}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert e^{-\pi ihx^{2}}-1\right\rvert}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert x\right\rvert}\leq r}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert f(x)\right\rvert}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert e^{-\pi ihx^{2}}-1\right\rvert}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}+\int_{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert x\right\rvert}>r}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert f(x)\right\rvert}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert e^{-\pi ihx^{2}}-1\right\rvert}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert x\right\rvert}\leq r}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert f(x)\right\rvert}\pi\mathinner{\!\left\lvert h\right\rvert}x^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}+2\int_{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert x\right\rvert}>r}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert f(x)\right\rvert}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}.

We only need to choose
h
and
r
so that
\int_{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert x\right\rvert}>r}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert f(x)\right\rvert}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}<\frac{\epsilon}{4}
, and
\pi\mathinner{\!\left\lvert h\right\rvert}r^{2}<\frac{\epsilon}{2\mathinner{\!\left\lVert f\right\rVert}_{1}}
.
∎

By Propositions
1
and
2
, we know that the chirp transform, and hence CT, has a similar property as the Riemann-Lebesgue Theorem of the Fourier transform.

Proposition 3
.

For
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, if
x^{2}f(x)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, then
Tf(\lambda)
is differentiable and

\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{Tf(\lambda)}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{\lambda}}=-\pi iT(x^{2}f)(\lambda).

Proof.

The proof is straightforward by applying the dominated convergence theorem.
∎

Unfortunately, we do not have a norm-preserving theorem for the chirp transforms similar to the Plancherel theorem in the Fourier transforms partially due to the slow decay rate in
\lambda
. On the contrary, we show that the chirp transforms do not satisfy “most” of the weak type inequalities. Before we state the proposition, we recall the definition of the weak type inequalities.
Let
(X,\nu_{1})
and
(Y,\nu_{2})
be measure spaces, and let
T
be an operator from
L^{p}(X,\nu_{1})
into the space of measurable functions from
Y
to
\mathbb{C}
. We say that
T
is of weak-type
(p,q)
,
p,q<\infty
if for
\lambda\geq 0
we have

\nu_{2}(\{y\in Y\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}|Tf(y)|>\lambda\})\leq\left(\frac{C\mathinner{\!\left\lVert f\right\rVert}_{p}}{\lambda}\right)^{q}.

This is a condition weaker than that if
T
is bounded from
L^{p}(X,\nu_{1})
to
L^{q}(Y,\nu_{2})
. In our case of CT,
X=Y=\mathbb{R}
and
\nu_{1},\nu_{2}
are the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}
.

Proposition 4
.

The chirp transform operator
T
is not weak-type
(p,q)
, if
\displaystyle\frac{2q}{p}-2q+4\neq 0
.

Proof.

Fix
p>1
. We show that for any
C>0
, there exists
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
and
\eta>0
, such that

m(\{\lambda\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert Tf(\lambda)\right\rvert}>\eta\})>\frac{C\mathinner{\!\left\lVert f\right\rVert}_{p}^{q}}{\eta^{q}},

where
\frac{2q}{p}-2q+4\neq 0
and
m
is the Lebesgue measure.
Let
f_{\alpha}(x)=\alpha^{\frac{1}{p}}e^{-\frac{\pi\alpha^{2}x^{2}}{p}}
,
\alpha>0
. One can verify that
\mathinner{\!\left\lVert f_{\alpha}\right\rVert}_{p}=1
for any
\alpha>0
. It suffices to show that for any
C>0
, we can always find
\alpha,\eta>0
, such that

(3)

m(\{\lambda\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert Tf_{\alpha}(\lambda)\right\rvert}>\eta\})>\frac{C\mathinner{\!\left\lVert f_{\alpha}\right\rVert}_{p}^{q}}{\eta^{q}}=\frac{C}{\eta^{q}}\,.

It is easy to obtain
Tf_{\alpha}(\lambda)=\frac{\alpha^{\frac{1}{p}}}{\sqrt{\frac{\alpha^{2}}{p}+i\lambda}}
, so
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert Tf_{\alpha}(\lambda)\right\rvert}=\frac{\alpha^{\frac{1}{p}}}{\sqrt[4]{\frac{\alpha^{4}}{p^{2}}+\lambda^{2}}}
. By solving the inequality
\frac{\alpha^{\frac{1}{p}}}{\sqrt[4]{\frac{\alpha^{4}}{p^{2}}+\lambda^{2}}}>\eta
, we have

(4)

m(\{\lambda\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert Tf_{\alpha}(\lambda)\right\rvert}>\eta\})=2\sqrt{\frac{\alpha^{\frac{4}{p}}}{\eta^{4}}-\frac{\alpha^{4}}{p^{2}}}\,,

for
\frac{\alpha^{\frac{4}{p}}}{\eta^{4}}-\frac{\alpha^{4}}{p^{2}}>0
; otherwise the measure is zero. Note that the condition
\frac{\alpha^{\frac{4}{p}}}{\eta^{4}}-\frac{\alpha^{4}}{p^{2}}>0
gives

3

2\sqrt{\frac{\alpha^{\frac{4}{p}}}{(\frac{1}{2}\alpha^{\frac{1}{p}-1}p^{\frac{1}{2}})^{4}}-\frac{\alpha^{4}}{p^{2}}}>\frac{C}{(\frac{1}{2}\alpha^{\frac{1}{p}-1}p^{\frac{1}{2}})^{q}}.

Simplifying this inequality gives us

\alpha^{\frac{2q}{p}-2q+4}>\frac{2^{2q-2}}{15}C^{2}p^{2-q}.

Therefore, if

Corollary 1
.

The chirp transform operator
T
is not weak-type
(2,2)
.

We next show that the decay rate of
Tf(\lambda)
is related to the vanishing order of
f
at the origin. Note that this is intuitively correct. Unlike
e^{i\xi_{0}x}
for some
\xi_{0}>0
,
e^{i\lambda x^{2}}
for some
\lambda\neq 0
does not oscillate around
0
. This is the main resource of the slow decay rate of

. Thus, if we want to have a faster decay rate of
Tf(\lambda)
, we shall force the function

to be zero around
0
. This intuition is made clear in the following proposition.

Proposition 5
.

Suppose
f(x)\in C_{0}^{2n}(\mathbb{R})
,
n\geq 1
and
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. If we assume that
f(0)=f^{\prime\prime}(0)=\cdots=f^{(2n-2)}(0)=0
and
f^{(2n)}(0)\neq 0
, then

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert Tf(\lambda)\right\rvert}\leq C\lambda^{-\frac{2n+1}{3}},

for some contant
C>0
and
C
depends on
n
and
f
.

Proof.

Fix
\lambda>0
. Let
D
be a differential operator such that
Df\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=-\frac{1}{2\pi i\lambda x}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{f}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}}
, and
\tilde{D}
such that
\tilde{D}f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{1}{2\pi i\lambda}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}}(\frac{f}{x})
. First, if
f
satisfies the above conditions and is zero in
(-\epsilon,\epsilon)
for some
\epsilon>0
, since
D^{k}(e^{-\pi i\lambda x^{2}})=e^{-\pi i\lambda x^{2}}
for any

and
x\neq 0
, we have

\displaystyle Tf(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}\backslash(-\epsilon,\epsilon)}f(x)e^{-\pi i\lambda x^{2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}\backslash(-\epsilon,\epsilon)}f(x)D^{2n}(e^{-\pi i\lambda x^{2}})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}=\int_{\mathbb{R}\backslash(-\epsilon,\epsilon)}\tilde{D}^{2n}(f)e^{-\pi i\lambda x^{2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}.

One can easily show by induction on

together with the boundedness of the derivatives of

that
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert Tf(\lambda)\right\rvert}\leq C\lambda^{-2n}\epsilon^{-(4n-1)}
for some
C>0
that depends on
n
and
f
.

Then we prove the statement of the proposition. Suppose for some
\epsilon>0
, we can write
f
in its Taylor expansion:

\displaystyle f(x)

\displaystyle=f(0)+f^{\prime}(0)x+\cdots+\frac{f^{(2n-1)}(0)}{(2n-1)!}x^{2n-1}+\frac{f^{(2n)}(\xi(x))}{(2n)!}x^{2n}

\displaystyle=f^{\prime}(0)x+\cdots+\frac{f^{(2n-3)}(0)}{(2n-3)!}x^{2n-3}+\frac{f^{(2n-1)}(0)}{(2n-1)!}x^{2n-1}+\frac{f^{(2n)}(\xi(x))}{(2n)!}x^{2n}

for
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert x\right\rvert}<2\epsilon
, and
\xi(x)
is some number between
0
and
x
.
Suppose
\psi(x)\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
with
\psi(x)=1
for
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert x\right\rvert}\leq 1
, and
\psi(x)=0
for
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert x\right\rvert}\geq 2
, and we also assume
\psi
is even. Then we can decompose
f
into
f(x)=f(x)\psi(\frac{x}{\epsilon})+f(x)(1-\psi(\frac{x}{\epsilon}))
. From the first part of the proof, we know that
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert T((1-\psi(\frac{x}{\epsilon}))f)(\lambda)\right\rvert}\leq C\lambda^{-2n}\epsilon^{-(4n-1)}
. On the other hand,
T(x^{k}\psi(\frac{x}{\epsilon}))(\lambda)=0
for odd

by symmetry. What remains is controlling
\frac{f^{(2n)}(\xi(x))}{(2n)!}x^{2n}\psi(\frac{x}{\epsilon})
. By the boundedness of

, we have

\displaystyle\mathinner{\!\left\lvert T\left(\frac{f^{(2n)}(\xi(x))}{(2n)!}x^{2n}\psi\left(\frac{x}{\epsilon}\right)\right)(\lambda)\right\rvert}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert x\right\rvert}\leq 2\epsilon}x^{2n}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}\leq C\epsilon^{2n+1}\,,

where
C>0
depends on

and

Combining these estimations, we have

\displaystyle\mathinner{\!\left\lvert Tf(\lambda)\right\rvert}\leq C\left(\lambda^{-2n}\epsilon^{-(4n-1)}+\epsilon^{2n+1}\right)\,.

If we select
\epsilon=\lambda^{-\frac{1}{3}}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathinner{\!\left\lvert Tf(\lambda)\right\rvert}\leq C\lambda^{-\frac{2n+1}{3}}

∎

3.
Proposed algorithm – Synchrosqueezed chirplet transform

The above result leads us to consider if it is possible to sharpen the TFC representation by some idea similar to the reassignment technique, like synchrosqueezing
[
13
]
, used to handle the uncertainty principle of the linear-type TF analysis algorithms. Indeed, we ask if it is possible to manipulate the phase information in CT so that the TFC representation can be sharpened. Below we introduce this idea with a linear chirp
f(x)=e^{2\pi i\xi_{0}x+\pi i\lambda_{0}x^{2}}
, where
\lambda_{0}
is allowed to be large.
Note that the idea of the original SST might not be suitable for our purpose due to the non-trivial chirp rate. Instead, we need to consider the higher order phase information, like that considered in the second order SST
[
31
,
3
]
, to handle the possibly non-trivial chirp rate.
We take any real-valued window function
g(x)
in the Schwartz space. Since

\displaystyle T_{f}^{(g)}(t,\xi,\lambda)

\displaystyle=\int e^{2\pi i\xi_{0}x+\pi i\lambda_{0}x^{2}}g(x-t)e^{-2\pi i\xi(x-t)}e^{-\pi i\lambda(x-t)^{2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle=e^{2\pi i\xi_{0}t+\pi i\lambda_{0}t^{2}}\int g(x)e^{-\pi i(\lambda-\lambda_{0})x^{2}}e^{-2\pi i(\xi-\xi_{0}-\lambda_{0}t)x}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}\,

by a direct expansion we have

(5)

\partial_{t}T_{f}^{(g)}(t,\xi,\lambda)=(2\pi i\xi_{0}+2\pi i\lambda_{0}t)T_{f}^{(g)}(t,\xi,\lambda)+2\pi i\lambda_{0}T_{f}^{(xg)}(t,\xi,\lambda)

and

(6)

\partial_{\xi}T_{f}^{(g)}(t,\xi,\lambda)=-2\pi iT_{f}^{(xg)}(t,\xi,\lambda).

Then from (
5
) and (
6
) we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left(\frac{\partial_{t}T_{f}^{(g)}(t,\xi,\lambda)}{T_{f}^{(g)}(t,\xi,\lambda)}\right)

\displaystyle=2\pi i\lambda_{0}\left(1+\partial_{t}\left(\frac{T_{f}^{(xg)}(t,\xi,\lambda)}{T_{f}^{(g)}(t,\xi,\lambda)}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{0}\left(2\pi i-\partial_{t}\left(\frac{\partial_{\xi}T_{f}^{(g)}(t,\xi,\lambda)}{T_{f}^{(g)}(t,\xi,\lambda)}\right)\right),

so

\lambda_{0}=\frac{\partial_{t}\left(\frac{\partial_{t}T_{f}^{(g)}(t,\xi,\lambda)}{T_{f}^{(g)}(t,\xi,\lambda)}\right)}{2\pi i-\partial_{t}\left(\frac{\partial_{\xi}T_{f}^{(g)}(t,\xi,\lambda)}{T_{f}^{(g)}(t,\xi,\lambda)}\right)}.

In addition, from (
5
) we can solve for the frequency,

\xi_{0}+\lambda_{0}t=\frac{\partial_{t}T_{f}^{(g)}(t,\xi,\lambda)-2\pi i\lambda_{0}T_{f}^{(xg)}(t,\xi,\lambda)}{2\pi iT_{f}^{(g)}(t,\xi,\lambda)}.

The above equalities motivate us to propose the reassignment operators:

\mu_{f}^{(g)}(t,\xi,\lambda)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\Re{\left(\frac{\partial_{t}\left(\frac{\partial_{t}T_{f}^{(g)}(t,\xi,\lambda)}{T_{f}^{(g)}(t,\xi,\lambda)}\right)}{2\pi i-\partial_{t}\left(\frac{\partial_{\xi}T_{f}^{(g)}(t,\xi,\lambda)}{T_{f}^{(g)}(t,\xi,\lambda)}\right)}\right)},

\displaystyle\omega_{f}^{(g)}(t,\xi,\lambda)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=

\displaystyle\,\Re{\Bigg(\frac{\partial_{t}T_{f}^{(g)}(t,\xi,\lambda)-2\pi i\mu_{f}^{(g)}(t,\xi,\lambda)T_{f}^{(xg)}(t,\xi,\lambda)}{2\pi iT_{f}^{(g)}(t,\xi,\lambda)}\Bigg)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\Re{\Bigg(\frac{\partial_{t}T_{f}^{(g)}(t,\xi,\lambda)-\mu_{f}^{(g)}(t,\xi,\lambda)\partial_{\xi}T_{f}^{(g)}(t,\xi,\lambda)}{2\pi iT_{f}^{(g)}(t,\xi,\lambda)}\Bigg)},

where
\Re{(z)}
denotes the real part of a complex number
z
. We can calculate the partial derivatives, and the expression for
\mu_{f}^{(g)}
at
(t,\xi,\lambda)
becomes

\mu_{f}^{(g)}=\Re{\left(\frac{T_{f}^{(g)}\partial_{tt}^{2}T_{f}^{(g)}-(\partial_{t}T_{f}^{(g)})^{2}}{2\pi i\big(T_{f}^{(g)}\big)^{2}+2\pi i\big(T_{f}^{(g)}\partial_{t}T_{f}^{(xg)}-T_{f}^{(xg)}\partial_{t}T_{f}^{(g)}\big)}\right)}.

The above calculations lead us to the following definition.

Definition 4

(Synchrosqueezed chirplet transform)
.

Take
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
and
g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
as the window. For a small
\alpha>0
,
define
the synchrosqueezed chirplet transform
(SCT) with
resolution
\alpha
as

S_{f}^{(g,\alpha)}(t,\xi,\lambda)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}T_{f}^{(g)}(t,\eta,\gamma)h_{\alpha}\left(\xi-\omega_{f}^{(g)}(t,\eta,\gamma)\right)h_{\alpha}\left(\lambda-\mu_{f}^{(g)}(t,\eta,\gamma)\right)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{\eta}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{\gamma},

where
h_{\alpha}
is an “approximate
\delta
-function” (i.e.
h
is smooth and decays fast with
\int h(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}=1
, so that
h_{\alpha}(t)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{1}{\alpha}h(\frac{t}{\alpha})
tends weakly to the delta measure
\delta
as
\alpha\to 0
).
Similar to
\mathfrak{T}_{f}^{(g)}(t,\xi)
, we define the TF representation associated with SCT via projecting the TFC representation onto the TF domain:

\mathfrak{S}_{f}^{(g,\alpha)}(t,\xi)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|S_{f}^{(g,\alpha)}(t,\xi,\lambda)|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{\lambda}\,.

A main practical concern of SCT is its numerical stability, since differentiations in the calculation of
\mu_{f}^{(g)}
and
\omega_{f}^{(g)}
are numerically unstable. This concern is handled by noting that
\omega_{f}^{(g)}
and
\mu_{f}^{(g)}
can be calculated directly with different window functions without any numerical differentiation. Indeed, note that by a direct expansion, we have at
(t,\xi,\lambda)

\partial_{t}T_{f}^{(g)}=-T_{f}^{(g^{\prime})}+2\pi i\xi T_{f}^{(g)}+2\pi i\lambda T_{f}^{(xg)},

and

\displaystyle\partial_{tt}^{2}T_{f}^{(g)}=

\displaystyle T_{f}^{(g^{\prime\prime})}-4\pi i\xi T_{f}^{(g^{\prime})}-2\pi i\lambda T_{f}^{(xg^{\prime})}+(2\pi i\xi)^{2}T_{f}^{(g)}

\displaystyle+2(2\pi i\xi)(2\pi i\lambda)T_{f}^{(xg)}-2\pi i\lambda T_{f}^{(g+xg^{\prime})}+(2\pi i\lambda)^{2}T_{f}^{(x^{2}g)}.

Therefore, at
(t,\xi,\lambda)
we have

\displaystyle\mu_{f}^{(g)}=\Re\left(\frac{T_{f}^{(g)}\partial_{tt}^{2}T_{f}^{(g)}-(\partial_{t}T_{f}^{(g)})^{2}}{2\pi i(T_{f}^{(g)})^{2}+2\pi i(T_{f}^{(g)}\partial_{t}T_{f}^{(xg)}-T_{f}^{(xg)}\partial_{t}T_{f}^{(g)})}\right)=\Re\Big(\frac{M_{1}}{M_{2}}\Big),

where

\displaystyle M_{1}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=

\displaystyle T_{f}^{(g)}T_{f}^{(g^{\prime\prime})}-4\pi i\lambda T_{f}^{(g)}T_{f}^{(xg^{\prime})}-2\pi i\lambda(T_{f}^{(g)})^{2}+(2\pi i\lambda)^{2}T_{f}^{(g)}T_{f}^{(x^{2}g)}

\displaystyle-(T_{f}^{(g^{\prime})})^{2}-(2\pi i\lambda)^{2}(T_{f}^{(xg)})^{2}+4\pi i\lambda T_{f}^{(g^{\prime})}T_{f}^{(xg)}

and

M_{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=-2\pi iT_{f}^{(g)}T_{f}^{(xg^{\prime})}+(2\pi i)^{2}\lambda T_{f}^{(g)}T_{f}^{(x^{2}g)}+2\pi iT_{f}^{(xg)}T_{f}^{(g^{\prime})}-(2\pi i)^{2}\lambda(T_{f}^{(xg)})^{2}

and

\displaystyle\omega_{f}^{(g)}

\displaystyle=\Re\left(\frac{\partial_{t}T_{f}^{(g)}-2\pi i\mu_{f}^{(g)}T_{f}^{(xg)}}{2\pi iT_{f}^{(g)}}\right)=\Re\left(\xi+\frac{-T_{f}^{(g^{\prime})}+2\pi i\lambda T_{f}^{(xg)}-2\pi i\mu_{f}^{(g)}T_{f}^{(xg)}}{2\pi iT_{f}^{(g)}}\right)

at
(t,\xi,\lambda)
.

The numerical implementation of SCT will be detailed in Section
3.3
.

Remark 1
.

We shall compare SCT with SST and its 2nd-order variation. Recall that the Short-time Fourier transform (STFT) of a tempered distribution
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
associated with a window function

at

With the same

with
g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})

and

is then defined as
[
39
]

(7)

S_{f}^{1,(g,\alpha)}(t,\xi)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}W_{f}^{(g)}(t,\eta)h_{\alpha}(\xi-\omega_{f}^{1,(g)}(t,\eta))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{\eta},

where

the STFT-based 2nd-order SST of

[

In short, the SST reassigns the TF content based on the first-order Taylor expansion of the phase function, and the 2nd-order SST approximates the phase function by the second-order Taylor expansion, and we can view the 2nd-order SST as a special case of SCT in which the chirp rate is fixed and set to be zero.

At the first glance, the behavior of
S_{f}^{(g,\alpha)}(t,\xi,\lambda)
might not be clear even if

is a function. To better understand its properties, and design algorithms to solve the challenge of crossover IFs, we consider the following model.

Definition 5

(
Intrinsic chirp type function
)
.

Fix
\epsilon>0
. A function
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
is said to be of the
\epsilon

with

and

having the following properties:

\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\phi^{\prime}(x)>0,\quad\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\phi^{\prime}(x)<\infty,

A(x)>0,\quad\mathinner{\!\left\lvert A^{\prime}(x)\right\rvert},\mathinner{\!\left\lvert A^{\prime\prime}(x)\right\rvert},\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\phi^{\prime\prime\prime}(x)\right\rvert}\leq\epsilon\phi^{\prime}(x),\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}.

By definition, an
\epsilon
-ICT function is an oscillatory function that is locally close to a linear chirp function, where the closeness is quantified by
\epsilon
. In addition to the chirp rate variation, the AM variations are both controlled by the IF.

Definition 6

(

\epsilon

A function

\epsilon
-ICT function, and their respective phase functions

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\phi_{k}^{\prime}(t)-\phi_{l}^{\prime}(t)\right\rvert}+\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\phi_{k}^{\prime\prime}(t)-\phi_{k}^{\prime\prime}(t)\right\rvert}\geq 2\Delta.

To describe the main property of SCT, for a Schwartz function

\widecheck{g}(\xi,\lambda)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(x)e^{-2\pi i\xi x}e^{-\pi i\lambda x^{2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}\,.

3
]
for the second-order SST. We provide a specific quantitative relation here.

An example of a window function
g
that satisfies the window condition in Theorem
1
is
g(x)=e^{-\pi x^{2}}
. To see why, first, note that we have
\widecheck{g}(\xi,\lambda)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\pi x^{2}}e^{-2\pi i\xi x}e^{-\pi i\lambda x^{2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+i\lambda}}e^{-\frac{\pi\xi^{2}}{1+i\lambda}}
, so
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\widecheck{g}(\xi,\lambda)\right\rvert}=(1+\lambda^{2})^{-\frac{1}{4}}e^{-\frac{\pi\xi^{2}}{1+\lambda^{2}}}
. It suffices to show that
(1+\lambda^{2})^{-\frac{1}{4}}e^{-\frac{\pi\xi^{2}}{1+\lambda^{2}}}\leq\frac{G_{0}}{\sqrt{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert}+\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\lambda\right\rvert}}}
for some constant
G_{0}>0
, or equivalently
\frac{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert}+\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\lambda\right\rvert}}{\sqrt{1+\lambda^{2}}}e^{-\frac{2\pi\xi^{2}}{1+\lambda^{2}}}\leq G_{0}^{2}
. We could easily see the boundedness of
\frac{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert}}{\sqrt{1+\lambda^{2}}}e^{-\frac{2\pi\xi^{2}}{1+\lambda^{2}}}
by setting
y=\frac{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert}}{\sqrt{1+\lambda^{2}}}
, so that
\frac{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert}}{\sqrt{1+\lambda^{2}}}e^{-\frac{2\pi\xi^{2}}{1+\lambda^{2}}}=ye^{-2\pi y^{2}}
. On the other hand,
\frac{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\lambda\right\rvert}}{\sqrt{1+\lambda^{2}}}e^{-\frac{2\pi\xi^{2}}{1+\lambda^{2}}}\leq\frac{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\lambda\right\rvert}}{\sqrt{1+\lambda^{2}}}
is clearly also bounded. We thus get the claim for
n=0
.
Secondly,

\displaystyle\widecheck{(xg)}(\xi,\lambda)

\displaystyle\,=\int_{\mathbb{R}}xe^{-\pi x^{2}}e^{-2\pi i\xi x}e^{-\pi i\lambda x^{2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle\,=\frac{1}{-2\pi i}\partial_{\xi}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+i\lambda}}e^{-\frac{\pi\xi^{2}}{1+i\lambda}}\right)=\frac{1}{-2\pi i}\frac{-2\pi\xi}{(1+i\lambda)^{\frac{3}{2}}}e^{-\frac{\pi\xi^{2}}{1+i\lambda}},

so
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\widecheck{(xg)}(\xi,\lambda)\right\rvert}=\frac{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert}}{(1+\lambda^{2})^{\frac{3}{4}}}e^{-\frac{\pi\xi^{2}}{1+\lambda^{2}}}
. We need to show that
\frac{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert}}{(1+\lambda^{2})^{\frac{3}{4}}}e^{-\frac{\pi\xi^{2}}{1+\lambda^{2}}}\leq\frac{G_{1}}{\sqrt{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert}+\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\lambda\right\rvert}}}
for some constant
G_{1}>0
, or
\frac{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert}^{3}+\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\lambda\right\rvert}\xi^{2}}{(1+\lambda^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}}e^{-\frac{2\pi\xi^{2}}{1+\lambda^{2}}}\leq G_{1}^{2}
. Let
y=\frac{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert}}{\sqrt{1+\lambda^{2}}}
, then
\frac{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert}^{3}}{(1+\lambda^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}}e^{-\frac{2\pi\xi^{2}}{1+\lambda^{2}}}=y^{3}e^{-2\pi y^{2}}
is bounded. Also, we have
\frac{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\lambda\right\rvert}\xi^{2}}{(1+\lambda^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}}e^{-\frac{2\pi\xi^{2}}{1+\lambda^{2}}}=\frac{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\lambda\right\rvert}}{\sqrt{1+\lambda^{2}}}y^{2}e^{-2\pi y^{2}}
, in which both
\frac{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\lambda\right\rvert}}{\sqrt{1+\lambda^{2}}}
and
y^{2}e^{-2\pi y^{2}}
are bounded. We thus get the case when
n=1
.
Finally,

\displaystyle\widecheck{(x^{2}g)}(\xi,\lambda)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}x^{2}e^{-\pi x^{2}}e^{-2\pi i\xi x}e^{-\pi i\lambda x^{2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{-2\pi i}\right)^{2}\partial^{2}_{\xi\xi}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+i\lambda}}e^{-\frac{\pi\xi^{2}}{1+i\lambda}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{-1}{4\pi^{2}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+i\lambda}}\left[\frac{4\pi^{2}\xi^{2}}{(1+i\lambda)^{2}}-\frac{2\pi\xi}{1+i\lambda}\right]e^{-\frac{\pi\xi^{2}}{1+i\lambda}},

so
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\widecheck{(x^{2}g)}(\xi,\lambda)\right\rvert}\leq\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\frac{1}{(1+\lambda^{2})^{\frac{1}{4}}}\left[\frac{4\pi^{2}\xi^{2}}{1+\lambda^{2}}+\frac{2\pi\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert}}{\sqrt{1+\lambda^{2}}}\right]e^{-\frac{\pi\xi^{2}}{1+\lambda^{2}}}
. We just showed that
\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\frac{1}{(1+\lambda^{2})^{\frac{1}{4}}}\frac{2\pi\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert}}{\sqrt{1+\lambda^{2}}}e^{-\frac{\pi\xi^{2}}{1+\lambda^{2}}}\leq\frac{\tilde{G}_{2,1}}{\sqrt{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert}+\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\lambda\right\rvert}}}
for some
\tilde{G}_{2,1}>0
and it suffices to show that
\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\frac{1}{(1+\lambda^{2})^{\frac{1}{4}}}\frac{4\pi^{2}\xi^{2}}{1+\lambda^{2}}e^{-\frac{\pi\xi^{2}}{1+\lambda^{2}}}\leq\frac{\tilde{G}_{2,2}}{\sqrt{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert}+\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\lambda\right\rvert}}}
for some
\tilde{G}_{2,2}>0
. This is equivalent to
\frac{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert}^{5}+\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\lambda\right\rvert}\xi^{4}}{(1+\lambda^{2})^{\frac{5}{2}}}e^{-\frac{2\pi\xi^{2}}{1+\lambda^{2}}}\leq\tilde{G}_{2,2}^{2}
. Again, let
y=\frac{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert}}{\sqrt{1+\lambda^{2}}}
, then
\frac{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert}^{5}}{(1+\lambda^{2})^{\frac{5}{2}}}e^{-\frac{2\pi\xi^{2}}{1+\lambda^{2}}}=y^{5}e^{-2\pi y^{2}}
is bounded. On the other hand,
\frac{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\lambda\right\rvert}\xi^{4}}{(1+\lambda^{2})^{\frac{5}{2}}}e^{-\frac{2\pi\xi^{2}}{1+\lambda^{2}}}=\frac{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\lambda\right\rvert}}{\sqrt{1+\lambda^{2}}}y^{4}e^{-2\pi y^{2}}
is also bounded by some absolute constant. We thus finish the claim.

3.1.
Window effect on SCT

The slow decay of CT shown in Lemma
1
motivates us to consider SCT. It is a natural question to ask if it is possible to “speed up” the decay rate by considering a different window? As is considered in the widely applied multitaper technique
[
34
]
and its generalization
[
14
]
, we know that different windows, particularly the Hermite windows that are widely used in practice, may provide information of a signal from different aspects. Inspired by the Hermite windows and Proposition
5
, we consider windows that have higher vanishing order at the origin.
The first result is the following corollary that comes from Proposition
5
.

Corollary 2
.

Suppose
f(x)\in C^{2n}(\mathbb{R})\cap\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
,
n\geq 1
and
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. If
g(x)=x^{2n}e^{-\pi\alpha x^{2}}
, for any
t
and
\xi
, we have

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert T_{f}^{(g)}(t,\xi,\lambda)\right\rvert}\leq C\lambda^{-\frac{2n+1}{3}},

for some constant
C>0
, where
C
depends on
n
and
f
.

This result shows that the higher order of the kernel vanishing at
0
, the faster decay the CT is in the chirp rate axis. This is intuitively correct since at time
0
, the chirp component
e^{i\pi\lambda t^{2}}
does not oscillate at all near
0
. Thus, if the kernel does not vanish near or at
0
, the integration against
e^{i\pi\lambda t^{2}}
does not vanish. See Proposition
5
for details.
Now, we provide an analysis of how the windows that vanish at the origin affect the concentration of the SCT of a two-component signal in the chirp rate direction, particularly at the time that the crossover IF happens.

Proposition 6
.

Suppose
f(x)=f_{1}(x)+f_{2}(x)
, where
f_{1}(x)=e^{2\pi i\xi_{1}x+\pi i\lambda_{1}x^{2}}
and
f_{2}(x)=e^{2\pi i\xi_{2}x+\pi i\lambda_{2}x^{2}}
, and
\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
,
\lambda_{1}\neq\lambda_{2}
. We also assume that at time
t_{0}
, the IFs of
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
intersect. Set
\bar{\xi}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\xi_{1}+\lambda_{1}t_{0}=\xi_{2}+\lambda_{2}t_{0}
. The window function is
g(x)=x^{n}e^{-\pi\alpha x^{2}}
, where
\alpha>0
and
n
is a nonnegative integer. Then there exists a constant
c_{\alpha}
that depends only on
\alpha
, such that if
\lambda
is closer to
\lambda_{1}
, i.e.
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\lambda-\lambda_{1}\right\rvert}<\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\lambda-\lambda_{2}\right\rvert}
, we have

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\mu_{f}^{(g)}(t_{0},\bar{\xi},\lambda)-\lambda_{1}\right\rvert}\leq\begin{cases}c_{\alpha}\left(\frac{\alpha^{2}+(\lambda-\lambda_{1})^{2}}{\alpha^{2}+(\lambda-\lambda_{2})^{2}}\right)^{\frac{n+1}{4}},&\text{if}\ n\ \text{is even};\\
c_{\alpha}\left(\frac{\alpha^{2}+(\lambda-\lambda_{1})^{2}}{\alpha^{2}+(\lambda-\lambda_{2})^{2}}\right)^{\frac{n+2}{4}},&\text{if}\ n\ \text{is odd}.\end{cases}

Proof.

We have already computed that for
j=1,2

T_{f_{j}}^{(e^{-\pi\alpha x^{2}})}(t,\xi,\lambda)=e^{2\pi i\xi_{j}t+\pi i\lambda_{j}t^{2}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha+i(\lambda-\lambda_{j})}}e^{\frac{-\pi(\xi-\xi_{j}-\lambda_{j}t)^{2}}{\alpha+i(\lambda-\lambda_{j})}},

and we know from (
6
) that

\partial_{\xi}T_{f_{j}}^{(e^{-\pi\alpha x^{2}})}(t,\xi,\lambda)=-2\pi iT_{f_{j}}^{(xe^{-\pi\alpha x^{2}})}(t,\xi,\lambda).

Therefore,

\displaystyle T_{f_{j}}^{(g)}(t,\xi,\lambda)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{-1}{2\pi i}\right)^{n}e^{2\pi i\xi_{j}t+\pi i\lambda_{j}t^{2}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha+i(\lambda-\lambda_{j})}}\partial_{\xi}^{n}\left(e^{\frac{-\pi(\xi-\xi_{j}-\lambda_{j}t)^{2}}{\alpha+i(\lambda-\lambda_{j})}}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{-1}{2\pi i}\right)^{n}e^{2\pi i\xi_{j}t+\pi i\lambda_{j}t^{2}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha+i(\lambda-\lambda_{j})}}e^{\frac{-\pi(\xi-\xi_{j}-\lambda_{j}t)^{2}}{\alpha+i(\lambda-\lambda_{j})}}P_{n,j}(\xi-\xi_{j}-\lambda_{j}t),

where
P_{n,i}(x)
is a Hermite-like polynomial, and contains only even powers of
x
if
n
is even; or contains only odd powers of
x
if
n
is odd. We proceed with
g(x)=x^{2n}e^{-\pi\alpha x^{2}}
for
n\geq 0
, and omit the computation for
g(x)=x^{2n+1}e^{-\pi\alpha x^{2}}
which would be similar. Let us compute the reassignment rule
\mu_{f}^{(g)}
at
(t_{0},\bar{\xi},\lambda)
. First we have for
j=1,2

\displaystyle\partial_{t}T_{f_{j}}^{(g)}(t,\xi,\lambda)

\displaystyle=(2\pi i\xi_{j}+2\pi i\lambda_{j}t+\frac{2\pi\lambda_{j}(\xi-\xi_{j}-\lambda_{j}t)}{\alpha+i(\lambda-\lambda_{j})})T_{f_{j}}^{(g)}(t,\xi,\lambda)

\displaystyle\quad+T_{f_{j}}^{(g)}(t,\xi,\lambda)\frac{\partial_{t}P_{2n,j}(\xi-\xi_{j}-\lambda_{j}t)}{P_{2n,j}(\xi-\xi_{j}-\lambda_{j}t)}.

To simplify the calculation, we denote

B_{1}(t,\xi,\lambda)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=T_{f_{1}}^{(g)}(t,\xi,\lambda),\quad B_{2}(t,\xi,\lambda)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=T_{f_{2}}^{(g)}(t,\xi,\lambda);

C_{j}(t,\xi,\lambda)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=2\pi i\xi_{j}+2\pi i\lambda_{j}t+\frac{2\pi\lambda_{j}(\xi-\xi_{j}-\lambda_{j}t)}{\alpha+i(\lambda-\lambda_{j})},\quad j=1,2;

D_{j}(t,\xi,\lambda)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\partial_{t}C_{j}=2\pi i\lambda_{j}-\frac{2\pi\lambda_{j}^{2}}{\alpha+i(\lambda-\lambda_{j})},\quad j=1,2;

E_{j}(t,\xi,\lambda)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{-2\pi(\xi-\xi_{j}-\lambda_{j}t)}{\alpha+i(\lambda-\lambda_{j})},\quad j=1,2;

F_{j}(t,\xi,\lambda)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\partial_{t}E_{j}=\frac{2\pi\lambda_{j}}{\alpha+i(\lambda-\lambda_{j})},\quad j=1,2;

Q_{j}(t,\xi,\lambda)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{\partial_{t}P_{2n,j}(\xi-\xi_{j}-\lambda_{j}t)}{P_{2n,j}(\xi-\xi_{j}-\lambda_{j}t)},\quad j=1,2;

\tilde{Q}_{j}(t,\xi,\lambda)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{\partial_{\xi}P_{2n,j}(\xi-\xi_{j}-\lambda_{j}t)}{P_{2n,j}(\xi-\xi_{j}-\lambda_{j}t)},\quad j=1,2;

Z_{j}(t,\xi,\lambda)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\partial_{t}Q_{j},\quad j=1,2;\ \ \tilde{Z}_{j}(t,\xi,\lambda)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\partial_{t}\tilde{Q}_{j},\quad j=1,2.

With these notations, we have at
(t,\xi,\lambda)

\partial_{t}T_{f}^{(g)}=B_{1}C_{1}+B_{1}Q_{1}+B_{2}C_{2}+B_{2}Q_{2},

and

\displaystyle T_{f}^{(g)}\partial_{tt}T_{f}^{(g)}-(\partial_{t}T_{f}^{(g)})^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,(B_{1}C_{1}^{2}+2B_{1}C_{1}Q_{1}+B_{1}Q_{1}^{2}+B_{1}D_{1}+B_{1}Z_{1}+B_{2}C_{2}^{2}+2B_{2}C_{2}Q_{2}+B_{2}Q_{2}^{2}+B_{2}D_{2}+B_{2}Z_{2})(B_{1}+B_{2})

\displaystyle-(B_{1}C_{1}+B_{1}Q_{1}+B_{2}C_{2}+B_{2}Q_{2})^{2}.

At
(t_{0},\bar{\xi},\lambda)
, since
\partial_{t}P_{2n,i}(\xi-\xi_{i}-\lambda_{i}t)
is a polynomial with only odd powers of
\xi-\xi_{i}-\lambda_{i}t
, so
Q_{i}(t_{0},\bar{\xi},\lambda)=0
for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
i=1,2
; also
C_{1}(t_{0},\bar{\xi},\lambda)-C_{2}(t_{0},\bar{\xi},\lambda)=0
. So at
(t_{0},\bar{\xi},\lambda)
,

\displaystyle T_{f}^{(g)}\partial_{tt}T_{f}^{(g)}-(\partial_{t}T_{f}^{(g)})^{2}

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle(B_{1}C_{1}^{2}+B_{1}D_{1}+B_{1}Z_{1}+B_{2}C_{2}^{2}+B_{2}D_{2}+B_{2}Z_{2})(B_{1}+B_{2})-(B_{1}C_{1}+B_{2}C_{2})^{2}

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle(B_{1}D_{1}+B_{1}Z_{1}+B_{2}D_{2}+B_{2}Z_{2})(B_{1}+B_{2})+B_{1}B_{2}(C_{1}-C_{2})^{2}

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle(B_{1}+B_{2})(B_{1}D_{1}+B_{1}Z_{1}+B_{2}D_{2}+B_{2}Z_{2}).

Then we have for
j=1,2

\displaystyle\partial_{\xi}T_{f_{j}}^{(g)}(t,\xi,\lambda)=\left(\frac{-2\pi(\xi-\xi_{j}-\lambda_{j}t)}{\alpha+i(\lambda-\lambda_{j})}\right)T_{f_{j}}^{(g)}(t,\xi,\lambda)+T_{f_{j}}^{(g)}(t,\xi,\lambda)\frac{\partial_{\xi}P_{2n,j}(\xi-\xi_{j}-\lambda_{j}t)}{P_{2n,j}(\xi-\xi_{j}-\lambda_{j}t)}.

So at any
(t,\xi,\lambda)
,

\partial_{\xi}T_{f}^{(g)}=B_{1}E_{1}+B_{1}\tilde{Q}_{1}+B_{2}E_{2}+B_{2}\tilde{Q}_{2},

and

\displaystyle\partial_{t\xi}^{2}T_{f}^{(g)}=\partial_{t}\partial_{\xi}T_{f}^{(g)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,(B_{1}C_{1}+B_{1}Q_{1})E_{1}+B_{1}F_{1}+(B_{1}C_{1}+B_{1}Q_{1})\tilde{Q}_{1}+B_{1}\tilde{Z}_{1}+(B_{2}C_{2}+B_{2}Q_{2})E_{2}+B_{2}F_{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+(B_{2}C_{2}+B_{2}Q_{2})\tilde{Q}_{2}+B_{2}\tilde{Z}_{2}.

Similar to
Q_{i}
, at
(t_{0},\bar{\xi},\lambda)
, we have
\tilde{Q}_{i}(t_{0},\bar{\xi},\lambda)=0
for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
i=1,2
. Moreover, we have
E_{i}(t_{0},\bar{\xi},\lambda)=0
, and hence
\partial_{\xi}T_{f}^{(g)}(t_{0},\bar{\xi},\lambda)=0
. It is easy to check that
Z_{i}(t_{0},\bar{\xi},\lambda)=-\lambda_{i}\tilde{Z}_{i}(t_{0},\bar{\xi},\lambda)
. Therefore, we have at
(t_{0},\bar{\xi},\lambda)

\displaystyle T_{f}^{(g)}\partial_{t\xi}^{2}T_{f}^{(g)}-\partial_{\xi}T_{f}^{(g)}\partial_{t}T_{f}^{(g)}=(B_{1}+B_{2})(B_{1}F_{1}+B_{1}\tilde{Z}_{1}+B_{2}F_{2}+B_{2}\tilde{Z}_{2}).

As a result, at
(t_{0},\bar{\xi},\lambda)
, we have

\displaystyle\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\mu_{f}^{(g)}-\lambda_{1}\right\rvert}

\displaystyle\leq\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\frac{\partial_{t}\left(\frac{\partial_{t}T_{f}^{(g)}}{T_{f}^{(g)}}\right)}{2\pi i-\partial_{t}\left(\frac{\partial_{\xi}T_{f}^{(g)}}{T_{f}^{(g)}}\right)}-\lambda_{1}\right\rvert}

\displaystyle=\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\frac{T_{f}^{(g)}\partial_{tt}T_{f}^{(g)}-(\partial_{t}T_{f}^{(g)})^{2}}{2\pi i(T_{f}^{(g)})^{2}-(T_{f}^{(g)}\partial_{t\xi}^{2}T_{f}^{(g)}-\partial_{\xi}T_{f}^{(g)}\partial_{t}T_{f}^{(g)})}-\lambda_{1}\right\rvert}

\displaystyle=\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\frac{B_{1}D_{1}+B_{1}Z_{1}+B_{2}D_{2}+B_{2}Z_{2}}{2\pi i(B_{1}+B_{2})-(B_{1}F_{1}+B_{1}\tilde{Z}_{1}+B_{2}F_{2}+B_{2}\tilde{Z}_{2})}-\lambda_{1}\right\rvert}

\displaystyle=\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\frac{D_{1}+Z_{1}+\frac{B_{2}}{B_{1}}(D_{2}+Z_{2})}{(2\pi i-F_{1}-\tilde{Z}_{1})+\frac{B_{2}}{B_{1}}(2\pi i-F_{2}-\tilde{Z}_{2})}-\frac{D_{1}+Z_{1}}{2\pi i-F_{1}-\tilde{Z}_{1}}\right\rvert}

\displaystyle=\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\frac{B_{2}}{B_{1}}\right\rvert}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\frac{(D_{2}+Z_{2})(2\pi i-F_{1}-\tilde{Z}_{1})-(D_{1}+Z_{1})(2\pi i-F_{2}-\tilde{Z}_{2})}{(2\pi i-F_{1}-\tilde{Z}_{1})\big[(2\pi i-F_{1}-\tilde{Z}_{1})+\frac{B_{2}}{B_{1}}(2\pi i-F_{2}-\tilde{Z}_{2})\big]}\right\rvert}

since
\frac{D_{1}+Z_{1}}{2\pi i-F_{1}-\tilde{Z}_{1}}=\lambda_{1}
.
One can verify that the constant term in
P_{2n,j}(\xi-\xi_{j}-\lambda_{j}t)
is
\frac{c_{2n}}{(\alpha+i(\lambda-\lambda_{j}))^{n}}
for some
c_{2n}\in\mathbb{R}
and
j=1,2
. Therefore at
(t_{0},\bar{\xi},\lambda)
, for the first term, we have

\displaystyle\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\frac{B_{2}}{B_{1}}\right\rvert}=\left(\frac{\alpha^{2}+(\lambda-\lambda_{1})^{2}}{\alpha^{2}+(\lambda-\lambda_{2})^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}+\frac{n}{2}}

and the second term
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\frac{(D_{2}+Z_{2})(2\pi i-F_{1}-\tilde{Z}_{1})-(D_{1}+Z_{1})(2\pi i-F_{2}-\tilde{Z}_{2})}{(2\pi i-F_{1}-\tilde{Z}_{1})\left[(2\pi i-F_{1}-\tilde{Z}_{1})+\frac{B}{A}(2\pi i-F_{2}-\tilde{Z}_{2})\right]}\right\rvert}
is uniformly bounded in
2n
for fixed
\alpha
, since only magnitudes of
Z_{i}
and
\tilde{Z}_{i}
grow with
2n
, and in the numerator
-Z_{2}\tilde{Z}_{1}+Z_{1}\tilde{Z}_{2}=0
.
Hence

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\mu_{f}^{(g)}(t_{0},\bar{\xi},\lambda)-\lambda_{1}\right\rvert}\leq c_{\alpha}\left(\frac{\alpha^{2}+(\lambda-\lambda_{1})^{2}}{\alpha^{2}+(\lambda-\lambda_{2})^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}+\frac{n}{2}}
, where
c_{\alpha}
only depends on
\alpha
.
∎

3.2.
Reconstruction

A common mission in TF analysis is to reconstruct the constituent components from the recorded signal. In the SST setup, provided the window function
g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
does not vanish at the origin, the reconstruction
\tilde{f}_{k}(x)
of the
k
-th component
f_{k}(x)
of the original signal
f(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{K}f_{i}(x)
is

\tilde{f}(x)=\frac{1}{g(0)}\int_{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\xi-\phi_{k}^{\prime}(x)\right\rvert}\leq\delta}S_{f}^{1,(g,\alpha)}(t,\xi)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{\xi}

for some small
\delta>0
. Replacing
S_{f}^{1,(g,\alpha)}(t,\xi)
by
S_{f}^{2,(g,\alpha)}(t,\xi)
gives us a reconstruction by the 2nd-order SST. Since the synthesis formula for the SST and the 2nd-order SST are based on the inverse Fourier transform, it fails at the time when the IFs of two components cross.

In
[
22
]
, a reconstruction algorithm based on the CT was proposed to handle this situation. We briefly summarize the ad hoc argument and the algorithm here. First, each component
f_{k}(x)=A_{k}(x)e^{2\pi i\phi_{k}(x)}
is approximated locally by a linear chirp:
f_{k}(t+x)\approx f_{k}(t)e^{2\pi i\phi_{k}^{\prime}(t)x+\pi i\phi_{k}^{\prime\prime}(t)x^{2}}
, and the CT of
f_{k}(x)
with window
g
is thus approximately

T_{f_{k}}^{(g)}(t,\xi,\lambda)\approx f_{k}(t)\widecheck{g}(\xi-\phi_{k}^{\prime}(t),\lambda-\phi_{k}^{\prime\prime}(t))\,.

Hence,

T_{f}^{(g)}(t,\xi,\lambda)\approx\sum_{k=1}^{K}f_{k}(t)\widecheck{g}(\xi-\phi_{k}^{\prime}(t),\lambda-\phi_{k}^{\prime\prime}(t))\,.

Now, if
\phi_{k}^{\prime}(t)
and
\phi_{k}^{\prime\prime}(t)
can be well approximated by some algorithms; for example, by
\omega_{k}(t)
and
\mu_{k}(t)
via (
10
) by SCT in our case, then

T_{f}^{(g)}(t,\xi,\lambda)\approx\sum_{k=1}^{K}\widecheck{g}(\xi-\omega_{k}(t),\lambda-\mu_{k}(t))f_{k}(t)\,.

By setting
(\xi,\lambda)=(\omega_{l}(t),\mu_{l}(t))
for
l=1,\dots,K
, we obtain a linear system:

\mathbf{\hat{X}}_{t}=\mathbf{A}_{t}\mathbf{X}_{t},

where

\mathbf{\hat{X}}_{t}=\begin{bmatrix}T_{f}^{(g)}(t,\omega_{1}(t),\mu_{1}(t))\\
T_{f}^{(g)}(t,\omega_{2}(t),\mu_{2}(t))\\
\vdots\\
T_{f}^{(g)}(t,\omega_{K}(t),\mu_{K}(t))\end{bmatrix},\ \mathbf{A}_{t}=\begin{bmatrix}a_{1,1}&a_{1,2}&\cdots&a_{1,K}\\
a_{2,1}&a_{2,2}&\cdots&a_{2,K}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
a_{K,1}&a_{K,2}&\cdots&a_{K,K}\end{bmatrix},\ \mathbf{X}_{t}=\begin{bmatrix}f_{1}(t)\\
f_{2}(t)\\
\vdots\\
f_{K}(t)\end{bmatrix}

and
a_{i,j}=\widecheck{g}(\omega_{i}(t)-\omega_{j}(t),\mu_{i}(t)-\mu_{j}(t))
, which can be obtained via an explicit expression of
\widecheck{g}(\xi,\lambda)
for some windows. For example, if
g(x)=e^{-\pi\alpha x^{2}}
, then

\widecheck{g}(\xi,\lambda)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha+i\lambda}}e^{\frac{-\pi\xi^{2}}{\alpha+i\lambda}}
.
The reconstruction of
f_{k}
at time
t
is thus achieved by

(11)

e_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{t}=e_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{t}^{-1}\mathbf{\hat{X}}_{t}\,,

where
e_{k}
is a unit vector with
1
in the
k
-th entry.

The key to the success of this algorithm is having an accurate estimate of
\phi_{k}^{\prime}(t)
and
\phi_{k}^{\prime\prime}(t)
for the construction of
\mathbf{A}_{t}
and
\mathbf{\hat{X}}_{t}
; in other words, an accurate knowledge of “ridges” associated with each component in the TFC domain. As is indicated in Lemma
1
, the slow decay rate of CT, and hence the low contrast of the ridges and their background, might increase the difficulty of ridge detection. To this end, we propose the following solution based on the SCT.

First, we evaluate the SCT of a given signal
f
with window
g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
. Second, we extract ridges by the following algorithm. Suppose we know
K\geq 2
, and we want to extract
K\geq 2
ridges by means of a fast implementation
[
36
]
of the (multiway) spectral clustering
[
41
]
. Note that
K
could be determined from the background knowledge. Without the background knowledge, an adaptive estimate of
K
from the given data is out of the scope of this paper.

(1)

Select entries in the 3-dim matrix that represent the discretization of
S_{f}^{(g)}(t,\xi,\lambda)
whose modules are greater than
q
quantile of the module over all entries of the 3-dim matrix. Here we set
q=0.9995
or higher, and denote those entries as
\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

(2)

Form the affinity matrix
W
for the selected points with Gaussian kernel
g(x_{i},x_{j})=\exp(-\|x_{i}-x_{j}\|_{2}^{2}/(2\sigma^{2}))
, and the associated degree matrix
D
. Here we set
\sigma
to be about the 15% percentile of all pairwise distances of
\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
.

(3)

Compute the top
2(K-1)
eigenvectors of the
D^{-1}W
, denoted as
u_{1},\ldots,u_{2(K-1)}
. Embed
x_{i}
from
\mathbb{R}^{3}
to
(u_{1}(i),u_{2}(i),\ldots,u_{2(K-1)}(i))^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{2(K-1)}
. In practice, when
n
is large, we suggest applying the Roseland algorithm
[
36
]
.

(4)

Run
k
-means on the embedding to split them into
K
clusters (each refers to one component of the signal). For each cluster, we track back to their original samples in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and set those entries as the ridge of the associated component.

The ridges then represent the estimate
\phi_{k}^{\prime}(t)
and
\phi_{k}^{\prime\prime}(t)
according to Theorem
1
. We could then plug these estimates into (
11
). See
[
15
]
for an argument for the choice of the bandwidth
\sigma
.

3.3.
Numerical implementation of SCT

The numerical implementation of SCT is by a direct discretization, which we detail now. The Matlab codes of CT and SCT and those used to generate the figures below in Section
4
are available in
https://github.com/ziyuchen7/Synchrosqueezed-chirplet-transforms
.

Below we index our vectors and matrices beginning with 1. Suppose the continuous signal
f
is uniformly sampled over a discrete set
of time points with the sampling interval
\Delta_{t}>0
second. The sampling rate is thus
f_{s}=\Delta_{t}^{-1}
. We denote the discretization of
f
as a column vector
\mathbf{f}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, where
\mathbf{f}(n)=f(n\Delta_{t})
, where
1\leq n\leq N
; that is, we “record” the signal for
N\Delta_{t}
seconds.
Choose a discrete window function
\mathbf{h}\in\mathbb{R}^{2K+1}
, where
K\in\mathbb{N}
. For example,
\mathbf{h}
can be a discretization of a Gaussian, so that
\mathbf{h}(K+1)
is the center of the Gaussian.
Write
\mathbf{h}^{\prime},\mathbf{h}^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{2K+1}
for the discretization of the first and second derivative of the window function. Write
\mathbf{th},\mathbf{th}^{\prime},\mathbf{t^{2}h}
for the discretization of the entry-wise product of
\mathbf{t}
or
\mathbf{t^{2}}
with
\mathbf{h}
or
\mathbf{h}^{\prime}
, where
\mathbf{t},\mathbf{t^{2}}\in\mathbb{R}^{2K+1}
,
\mathbf{t}(n)=n-K-1
and
\mathbf{t^{2}}(n)=(n-K-1)^{2}
,
n=1,2,\dots,2K+1
.
Choose a resolution
\alpha>0
, set
M=\lfloor\frac{0.5}{\alpha}\rfloor
so that
M+1
is the number of bins in the (positive) frequency axis and
2M
is the number of bins in the chirp rate axis of our targeting TFC representation. Then, the CT of
\mathbf{f}
with window
\mathbf{h}
would be a matrix
\mathbf{T_{f}^{h}}\in\mathbb{C}^{2M\times(M+1)\times N}
, whose entries are

\mathbf{T_{f}^{h}}(l,m,n)=\sum_{k=1}^{2K+1}\mathbf{f}(n+k-K-1)\mathbf{h}(k)e^{\frac{-2\pi i(k-1)(m-1)}{2M}}e^{\frac{-\pi il(k-1)^{2}}{4M^{2}}},

where we define
\mathbf{f}(n)=0
when
n<1
or
n>N
,
n=1,2,\dots,N
is the time index,
m=1,2,\dots,M+1
is the frequency index and
l=-(M-1),\dots,M
is the chirp rate index. The CT of
\mathbf{f}
with windows
\mathbf{h}^{\prime},\mathbf{h}^{\prime\prime},\mathbf{th},\mathbf{th}^{\prime},\mathbf{t^{2}h}
, denoted by
\mathbf{T_{f}^{h^{\prime}}},\mathbf{T_{f}^{h^{\prime\prime}}},\mathbf{T_{f}^{th}},\mathbf{T_{f}^{th^{\prime}}},\mathbf{T_{f}^{t^{2}h}}
can be defined likewise. Let

\displaystyle\mathbf{\lambda_{f}}(l,m,n)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}(\mathbf{T_{f}^{h}}(l,m,n)\mathbf{T_{f}^{h^{\prime\prime}}}(l,m,n)-4\pi i\frac{l}{4M^{2}}\mathbf{T_{f}^{h}}(l,m,n)\mathbf{T_{f}^{th^{\prime}}}(l,m,n)-2\pi i\frac{l}{4M^{2}}(\mathbf{T_{f}^{h}}(l,m,n))^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+(2\pi i\frac{l}{4M^{2}})^{2}\mathbf{T_{f}^{h}}(l,m,n)\mathbf{T_{f}^{t^{2}h}}(l,m,n)-(\mathbf{T_{f}^{h^{\prime}}}(l,m,n))^{2}-(2\pi i\frac{l}{4M^{2}})^{2}(\mathbf{T_{f}^{th}}(l,m,n))^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+4\pi i\frac{l}{4M^{2}}\mathbf{T_{f}^{h^{\prime}}}(l,m,n)\mathbf{T_{f}^{th}}(l,m,n))(-\mathbf{T_{f}^{h}}(l,m,n)\mathbf{T_{f}^{th^{\prime}}}(l,m,n)

\displaystyle\quad+2\pi i\frac{l}{4M^{2}}\mathbf{T_{f}^{h}}(l,m,n)\mathbf{T_{f}^{t^{2}h}}(l,m,n)+\mathbf{T_{f}^{th}}(l,m,n)\mathbf{T_{f}^{h^{\prime}}}(l,m,n)

\displaystyle\quad-2\pi i\frac{l}{4M^{2}}(\mathbf{T_{f}^{th}}(l,m,n))^{2})^{-1}.

We choose a threshold
\nu>0
and calculate the reassignment operator:

\displaystyle\mathbf{\mu_{f}}(l,m,n)=4M^{2}\text{Im}(\mathbf{\lambda_{f}}(l,m,n)),

and

\displaystyle\mathbf{\omega_{f}}(l,m,n)

\displaystyle=m-(2M)\text{Im}\left(\frac{-\mathbf{T_{f}^{h^{\prime}}}(l,m,n)}{2\pi\mathbf{T_{f}^{h}}(l,m,n)}-\frac{(\frac{il}{4M^{2}}-\mathbf{\lambda_{f}}(l,m,n))\mathbf{T_{f}^{th}}(l,m,n)}{\mathbf{T_{f}^{h}}(l,m,n)}\right),

if
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\mathbf{T_{f}^{h}}(l,m,n)\right\rvert}>\nu
; otherwise set
\mathbf{\mu_{f}}(l,m,n)=-\infty
and
\mathbf{\omega_{f}}(l,m,n)=-\infty
. The SCT of
\mathbf{f}
, a matrix
\mathbf{S_{f}}(l,m,n)\in\mathbb{C}^{2M\times(M+1)\times N}
is finally given by

\mathbf{S_{f}}(l,m,n)=\sum_{r,j\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbf{\mu_{f}}(r,j,n)+M=l,\mathbf{\omega_{f}}(r,j,n)=m}\mathbf{T_{f}^{h}}(r,j,n).

The reconstruction of each component follows immediately.

4.2.
Simulated time-varying chirp rate signal

Next, we demonstrate the performance of SCT on signals with time-varying amplitudes, frequencies and chirp rates. We use the smoothed Brownian path realizations to model the AM, IF and the time-varying chirp rates of the constituent components of the signal. This simulation scheme is a generalization of that proposed in
[
14
]
and we summarize it here. If
W
is the standard Brownian motion defined on
[0,\infty)
, then we define the smoothed Brownian motion with bandwidth
B>0
as
\Phi_{B}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=W\star K_{B}
, where
K_{B}
is the Gaussian function with standard deviation
B>0
and
\star
denotes the convolution operator. Given
T>0
and parameters
\zeta_{1},\dots,\zeta_{6}>0
, we then define the following family of random processes on
[0,T]
:

\Psi_{[\zeta_{1},\dots,\zeta_{7}]}(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\zeta_{1}+\zeta_{2}x+\zeta_{3}x^{2}+\zeta_{4}\frac{\Phi_{\zeta_{5}}(x)}{\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\Phi_{\zeta_{5}}\right\rVert}_{L^{\infty}[0,T]}}+\zeta_{6}\int_{0}^{x}\int_{0}^{u}\frac{\Phi_{\zeta_{7}}(s)}{\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\Phi_{\zeta_{7}}\right\rVert}_{L^{\infty}[0,T]}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{s}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{u}.

For the amplitude
A(x)
of each component, we set
\zeta_{2}=\zeta_{3}=\zeta_{6}=0
; every realization then varies smoothly between
\zeta_{1}
and
\zeta_{1}+\zeta_{4}
. In the example shown below, the signal consists of two components (i.e.
K=2
) on
[0,10]
; their two amplitudes are independent realizations of
\Psi_{[2,0,0,1,200,0,0]}(x)
. To simulate phase functions, we set
\zeta_{1}=\zeta_{4}=0
. In this example we consider, we take for

a realization of
\Psi_{[0,1,4.5,0,0,0.2,400]}(x)
, and for
\phi_{2}(t)
a realization of
\Psi_{[0,12,-4,0,0,0.25,300]}(x)
. The signal is then

where

, and the sampling rate is 100Hz.
Finally, we add white noise

to

.

5.
Discussion and future work

While the proposed SCT algorithm could help disentangle components with crossover IFs, there are still several open problems. First, we need a fast implementation of the SCT algorithm for large databases, probably by including the existing fast implementation for CT
[
26
]
. Unlike SST and its variations, at each time, the computational time depends on the frequency axis only, in SCT, at each time, the computational time depends on both the frequency axis and chirp rate axis. An intuitive solution is taking a reliable TF representation to determine those frequency entries that are “informative”, and focus on those frequency entries to evaluate information on the chirp rate. However, this solution might be challenged by the nonlinear relationship between frequency and chirp rate shown in Figure
6
. We will systematically study how to design a fast implementation of SCT (or even CT) in our future work.

The oscillatory components, or modes, considered in this paper are all sinusoidal; that is, it is the cosine function as an
1
-periodic signal that is stretched (by the phase function) and/or scaled (by the AM). In practice, we found many signals oscillate with a non-sinusoidal pattern (or wave-shape function)
[
43
]
. It would be an interesting topic to extend SCT to the signals with non-sinusoidal wave-shape functions. For example, while the cepstrum is used to handle the impact of non-sinusoidal wave-shape function on the spectrogram
[
25
]
, what is the proper geometric, or algebraic structure, on the TFC plane that we can count on to handle the non-sinusoidal wave-shape functions?

The
\epsilon
-ICT function is nothing but a generalization of the linear chirp function, where locally it behaves like a linear chirp function; that is, locally, the phase function of an
\epsilon
-ICT function is close to a quadratic function. A natural question to ask is what if the phase function is a polynomial? Such a problem has been considered in
[
2
,
40
]
, among many others. Since our theory for SCT is based on the
\epsilon
-ICT assumption, we cannot guarantee what may happen when we apply SCT to a signal with polynomial phase. However, based on our experience in applying SST to estimate IF when the chirp rate is non-trivial (the estimate is biased, and the bias depends on the chirp rate, which leads to the second
[
32
]
and higher
[
35
]
order SST and other variations), we would expect biased IF and chirp rate estimates under some relationships between IF and chirp rates.

Last but not the least, while we empirically see that SCT is robust to noise (e.g. Figure
8
), we do not have a theoretical justification at this moment. Note that while we use differentiation to motivate the squeezing step of SCT, that squeezing step can be evaluated by different windows without any differentiation. We could thus apply the same argument as that in
[
10
]
. Moreover, we may generalize the technique used in
[
37
]
to obtain the impact of noise on the TFC representation, or the distribution of the TF representation under the non-null setup.
We will leave the above interesting problems in our future work.

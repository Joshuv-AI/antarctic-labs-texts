Crossing tribes of tangles in a thickened surface
Igor Nikonov

Abstract
Crossings of knot diagrams can be divided into classes (tribes) compatible with Reidemeister moves. Tribes can be considered as localization of the notion of weak chord index introduced by M. Xu. In the article we describe tribes of crossings for tangles in a fixed surface and show that crossings are differentiated by their component, order and homotopy types. As a consequence, we conclude that there are no nontrivial indices on diagrams of classical knots.



Introduction

In 2004 V. Turaev
[
23
]
assigned an index
n(e)
to each crossing
e
of a flat knot diagram. Using the index, he defined a polynomial invariant (index polynomial), see also
[
24
]
. This polynomial was numerously rediscovered in various forms afterwards
[
11
,
2
,
15
,
8
,
17
]
. A. Henrich
[
10
]
presented indices valued in flat link diagrams. Other types of indices can be found in
[
7
,
13
,
3
,
16
,
5
]
. The notion of index was axiomatized by Z. Cheng
[
4
]
, and finally, M. Xu
[
25
]
found the general conditions for an index to define an invariant polynomial.

In
[
21
,
22
]
we localized the notion of index and defined tribes as classes of crossings compatible in some sense with the Reidemeister moves (see Definition
2
). It follows from the definition that crossings which belong to one tribe have the same value for any (weak chord) index. The aim of this paper is to describe tribe of crossings and the universal index for knots, links and tangles in a fixed surface.

The paper is organized as follows. Section
1
contains the main definitions of tangles, tribes and indices. Using technics of
[
19
]
, in Section
2
we prove the main theorem of the paper which shows that a tribe of crossings is determined by the component, order and homotopy types. This result allows to give the negative answer to the question
[
5
,
6
]
whether nontrivial indices for classical knots exist (we postpone this conclusion to Corollary

4
we find the universal index for tangles and flat tangles in the surface. It appears that in order to pass from tribes to the index we need to factorize the homotopy type by the action of the inner monodromy group. We finish the paper with Section
5

1.1
Tribes and indices

Let
T
be a tangle and
\mathfrak{T}
be the set of its diagram. The set
\mathfrak{T}
can be considered as the objects of a diagram category whose morphisms are compositions of isotopies and Reidemeister moves.

Given tangle diagrams

and a morphism

between them, there is a correspondence

between the crossings of the diagrams. The map

is a partial bijection between the crossing sets because some crossing can disappear or appear when

is a first or second Reidemeister move.

Remark 1
.

Below we will often denote the crossing
f_{*}(v)
in the diagram

which corresponds to a crossing

D
, by the same letter
v
when the morphism

is clear from the context.

Recall the notion of a tribal system
[
21
,
22
]

Definition 2
.

Let
T
be a tangle and
\mathfrak{T}
be the category of its diagrams. A
tribal system
on diagrams of the tangle

is a family of partitions
\mathfrak{C}(D)
of the sets
\mathcal{V}(D)
,
D\in\mathfrak{T}
, into subsets
C\in\mathfrak{C}(D)
called
tribes
such that

(T0)

Let

be a Reidemeister move,
v_{1},v_{2}\in\mathcal{V}(D)
and
v^{\prime}_{1}=f_{*}(v_{1})
and
v^{\prime}_{2}=f_{*}(v_{2})
be the correspondent crossings in

. Then if

and

belong to one tribe in

then

and

;

(T2)

If

is a decreasing second Reidemeister move and

and

and

Below in the paper, we will always consider the

on the diagram category

, i.e. the equivalence relations on the crossing sets
\mathcal{V}(D)
,
D\in\mathfrak{T}
, generated by the properties (T0) and (T2).

For crossings

,
D\in\mathfrak{T}

if the crossings

The sign function splits any tribe

,
D\in\mathfrak{T}
, into two subsets

and

we call

The phratries

and

are called

.

The notion of tribes is a localization of the notion a (weak chord) index defined by M. Xu
[
25
]
(see also
[
4
]
for the original axiomatic definition of chord index, and
[
5
,
6
]
for examples of chord indices).

Definition 3
.

Let
I
be an arbitrary set. An
index on the diagrams of a tangle
T
in the surface
F
with coefficients in the set
I
is a map
\iota
which assigns some value
\iota(v)
to any crossing
v
in any diagram
D
of the tangle

and possesses the following properties:

(I0)

for any Reidemeister move

and any crossings

and
v^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}(D^{\prime})
such that
v^{\prime}=f_{*}(v)
, one has
\iota(v)=\iota(v^{\prime})

(I2)

\iota(v_{1})=\iota(v_{2})
for any crossings

Example 1
.

Let
K
be a knot in the surface
F
. For any diagram

of
K

, define its index to be the intersection number of two cycles:

. Then the map

obeys the conditions (I0) and (I2).

The following statement follows from the definitions.

Proposition 1
.

Let
\iota
be an index on the diagrams of a tangle
T
in the surface

with coefficients in a set
I
. Let
v
and
w
be two vertices of a diagram

of

. If

and

belong to one tribe then
\iota(v)=\iota(w)
.

Definition 4
.

An index

on the diagrams of a tangle

in the surface

with coefficients in a set

is called the

on diagrams of the tangle

on
T

such that
\iota=\psi\circ\iota^{u}
.

The main result of the paper is a description of the tribes (and phratries) of the finest tribal system

on diagrams of any tangle

, and the universal index on

Remark 8

in the category

5.2

A

is a pair
(F,D)
where
F
is an oriented closed connected surface and
D\subset F
is an embedded
4
-valent graph. A

is an equivalence class of flat link diagrams modulo isotopies of the surface, Reidemeister moves, orientation preserving diffeomorphisms of pairs
(F,D)
and (de)stabilizations. We assume that the components of a flat link are numbered and oriented.

An analogue of Kuperberg’s theorem is the following statement
[
9
]
(see also
[
12
]
for the knot case).

(F,D)

2.
Preliminary results

Let
G=(V,E)
be a finite graph, where
V
denotes the vertex set and
E
denotes the edge set. Throughout this paper, all graphs are supposed to be connected. We write
y\sim x
if
xy\in E
.
Let
\omega:V\times V\to[0,+\infty)
be an edge weight function that satisfies
\omega_{xy}=\omega_{yx}
for all
x,y\in V
and
w_{xy}>0
if and only if
x\sim y
. Let
\mu:V\to(0,+\infty)
be a finite measure, and

|V|=\text{Vol}(V):=\sum\limits_{x\in V}\mu(x)

be the volume of
V
. Define
V^{\mathbb{R}}:=\{u|u~\text{is a real function}:V\to\mathbb{R}\}
. For any function
u\in V^{\mathbb{R}}
, the Laplacian of
u
is defined by

(3)

\Delta u(x)=\frac{1}{\mu(x)}\sum_{y\sim x}w_{yx}(u(y)-u(x)),

where
y\sim x
means
xy\in E
. The gradient form of
u
reads

(4)

\Gamma(u,v)(x)=\frac{1}{2\mu(x)}\sum_{y\sim x}w_{xy}(u(y)-u(x))(v(y)-v(x)).

We denote the length of the gradient of
u
by

(5)

|\nabla u|(x)=\sqrt{\Gamma(u,u)(x)}=\left(\frac{1}{2\mu(x)}\sum_{y\sim x}w_{xy}(u(y)-u(x))^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Denote, for any function
u:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, the integral of
u
on
V
by

\int\limits_{V}ud\mu=\sum\limits_{x\in V}\mu(x)u(x).

For
p\geq 1
, denote

||u||_{p}:=(\int\limits_{V}|u|^{p}d\mu)^{\frac{1}{p}}

and

L^{p}(V)=\{f\in V^{\mathbb{R}}:||f||_{p}<\infty\}.

As in
[
16
]
, we define a Sobolev space and a norm by

H^{1}(V)=W^{1,2}(V)=\left\{u:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}:\int\limits_{V}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+u^{2}\right)d\mu<+\infty\right\},

and

\|u\|_{H^{1}(V)}=\|u\|_{W^{1,2}(V)}=\left(\int\limits_{V}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+u^{2}\right)d\mu\right)^{1/2}.

The following Sobolev embedding, Maximum principle and Poincaré inequality will be used later in the paper.

Lemma 2.1
.

(
[
16
, Lemma 5]
)
Let
G=(V,E)
be a finite graph. The Sobolev space
W^{1,2}(V)
is precompact. Namely, if
{u_{j}}
is bounded in
W^{1,2}(V)
, then there exists some
u\in W^{1,2}(V)
such that up to a subsequence,
u_{j}\to u
in
W^{1,2}(V)
.

Lemma 2.2
.

(
[
20
, Lemma 4.1]
)
Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph, where
V
is a finite set, and
K>0
is constant. Suppose a real function
u(x):V\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies
(\Delta-K)u(x)\geq 0
for all
x\in V
, then
u(x)\leq 0
for all
x\in V
.

Lemma 2.3
.

(
[
16
, Lemma 6]
)
Let
G=(V,E)
be a finite graph. For all functions
u:V\to\mathbb{R}
with
\int\limits_{V}ud\mu=0
, there
exists some constant
C
depending only on
G
such that

\int\limits_{V}u^{2}d\mu\leq C\int\limits_{V}|\nabla u|^{2}d\mu.

Let
f\in{V}^{\mathbb{R}}
, we are going to consider the equation

(6)

-\Delta u=f

on
G
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Assume
G=(V,E)
is a graph. Then there exists a solution to (
6
) iff
\int_{V}fd\mu=0
. Furthermore, the solution is unique up to a constant.

Proof.

We first prove sufficiency. If
f\equiv 0
, then by the definition of
\Delta
we know that
u
is a constant. Next, we suppose that
f\not\equiv 0
and that
\int_{V}fd\mu=0
. Define

I(u)=\int\limits_{V}|\nabla u|^{2}d\mu

and
\alpha:=\inf\limits_{u\in A}I(u),
where

A=\left\{u\in H^{1}(V)\mid\int_{V}ud\mu=0\text{ and }\int_{V}ufd\mu=1\right\}\text{. }

Clearly,
0\leq\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, and
0\leq\alpha\leq I\left(\frac{f}{\|f\|_{2}^{2}}\right)
. Let
\{u_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
be a minimizing sequence in
A
satisfying
I(u_{k})\to\alpha
. In view of
I(u_{k})=||\nabla u_{k}||_{2}^{2}
, we see that
\{|\nabla u_{k}|\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
is bounded in
L^{2}(V)
.
Furthermore, since
\int_{V}u_{k}d\mu=0
, by Lemma
2.3
, we see that

\int_{V}u_{k}^{2}d\mu\leq C\int_{V}\left|\nabla u_{k}\right|^{2}d\mu.

Thus
\{u_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
is bounded in
H^{1}(V)
. By Lemma
2.1
, we can find a sequence
\{u_{i}\}
of
\{u_{k}\}
and
u_{0}\in H^{1}(V)
so that
u_{i}\to u_{0}
in
H^{1}(V)
. It follows that
\lim\limits_{i\rightarrow+\infty}u_{i}(x)=u_{0}(x)
for all
x\in V
. Thus we obtain

\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\int_{V}\left|\nabla u_{i}\right|^{2}d\mu=\int_{V}\left|\nabla u_{0}\right|^{2}d\mu=\alpha,

\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\int_{V}fu_{i}d\mu=\int_{V}fu_{0}d\mu=1,

and

\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\int_{V}u_{i}d\mu=\int_{V}u_{0}d\mu=0.

Hence,
u_{0}
is the minimizer of the variational problem
\inf\limits_{u\in A}I(u)
.

We next show that there exist constants
\beta
and
\gamma
such that

(7)

\int_{V}\Gamma(u_{0},\psi)d\mu=-\beta\int_{V}f\psi d\mu-\gamma\int_{V}\psi d\mu\text{ for all }\psi\in H^{1}(V)\text{.}

by the method of Lagrange multiplies.
Define

L(t,\beta,\gamma)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{V}\left|\nabla\left(u_{0}+t\psi\right)\right|^{2}d\mu+\gamma\int_{V}\left(u_{0}+t\psi\right)d\mu+\beta\left(\int_{V}\left(u_{0}+t\psi\right)fd\mu-1\right),

where
\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
and
\psi\in H^{1}(V)
.
Then we get

(8)

\left.\frac{\partial L}{\partial\beta}\right|_{t=0}=0,\left.\quad\frac{\partial L}{\partial\gamma}\right|_{t=0}=0\text{ and }\left.\frac{\partial L}{\partial t}\right|_{t=0}=0\text{. }

By (
4
) and (
5
), we see that

\displaystyle\left.\frac{d}{dt}\Gamma\left(u_{0}+t\psi,u_{0}+t\psi\right)(x)\right|_{t=0}

\displaystyle\left.=\frac{d}{dt}\left\{\frac{1}{2\mu(x)}\sum_{y\sim x}\omega_{xy}\left[\left(u_{0}+t\psi\right)(y)-\left(u_{0}+t\psi\right)(x)\right]^{2}\right\}\right|_{t=0}

\displaystyle=\left.\sum_{y\sim x}\frac{\omega_{xy}}{2\mu(x)}2\left[\left(u_{0}+t\psi\right)(y)-\left(u_{0}+t\psi\right)(x)\right][\psi(y)-\psi(x)]\right|_{t=0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\mu(x)}\sum_{y\sim x}\omega_{xy}2\left[u_{0}(y)-u_{0}(x)\right][\psi(y)-\psi(x)]

\displaystyle=2\Gamma\left(u_{0},\psi\right),

and hence that

\left.\frac{\partial L}{\partial t}\right|_{t=0}=\int_{V}\Gamma\left(u_{0},\psi\right)d\mu+\gamma\int_{V}\psi d\mu+\beta\int_{V}\psi fd\mu=0

by (
8
) and the definition of
L
.
Taking
\psi\equiv 1
. Since

\int_{V}\Gamma(u_{0},1)d\mu=0

and
\int_{V}fd\mu=0
, we get
\gamma=0
. Choosing
\psi=u_{0}
then we get
\beta=-\alpha
. Hence,

(9)

\int_{V}\Gamma(u_{0},\psi)d\mu=\alpha\int_{V}f\psi d\mu~\text{ for all }~\psi\in H^{1}(V).

Since
\int_{V}u_{0}fd\mu=1
,
u_{0}
is not a constant. We claim that
\alpha>0
. Otherwise
\alpha=0
. Then
\int_{V}|\nabla u_{0}|^{2}d\mu=0
, which implies that
|\nabla u_{0}|\equiv 0
. By (
5
), we deduce that
u\equiv C_{2}
, where
C_{2}>0
is a constant. This is a contradiction. Thus,
\alpha=I(u_{0})>0
. Letting

\tilde{u}_{0}=\frac{u_{0}}{\alpha}.

Then it follows from (
9
) that

\int_{V}\Gamma(\tilde{u}_{0},\psi)d\mu=\int_{V}f\psi d\mu\text{ for all }\psi\in H^{1}(V).

Integrating by parts, we have

-\int_{V}\psi\Delta\tilde{u}_{0}d\mu=\int_{V}f\psi d\mu\text{ for all }\psi\in H^{1}(V).

For any
x_{0}\in V
, taking

\psi(x)=\begin{cases}1,&x=x_{0},\\
0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Then we deduce that
-\Delta\tilde{u}_{0}(x_{0})=f(x_{0})
. Therefore,
\tilde{u}_{0}
is a solution to (
3
).

We next prove the necessity. Suppose
-\Delta u=f
. Since
\int_{V}\Delta ud\mu=0
, we know that
\int_{V}fd\mu=0
.

If
f\equiv 0
, then
u
is a constant by the definition of
\Delta
. Thus, we know that the solution to (
6
) is unique up to a constant.

We now complete the proof.
∎

We state our main result as follows.

Theorem 2.5
.

The equation (
1
) admits a unique solution, if and only if

(10)

n_{1}+n_{2}+\cdots+n_{k}=:N<\frac{|V|}{4\pi}.

3.
The proof of Theorem
2.5

Throughout this section, we assume that
N=\sum\limits_{s=1}^{k}n_{s}
and that
f
is a function on
V
satisfying

\int_{V}fd\mu=1.

Since

\int_{V}-4\pi Nf+4\pi\sum_{j=1}^{k}n_{j}\delta_{z_{j}}d\mu=-4\pi N+4\pi N=0,

by Lemma
2.4
, we know that there exists a unique solution to the Poisson equation

\Delta u_{0}=-4\pi Nf+4\pi\sum_{j=1}^{k}n_{j}\delta_{z_{j}},

in the sense of differing by a constant. Assume
u
is a solution of (
1
), let
v:=u-u_{0}
, then
v
satisfies

\Delta v=e^{v+u_{0}}-1+4\pi Nf.

Define an operator
P:=\Delta-e^{u_{0}}-1:W^{1,2}(V)\to L^{2}(V)
, then we have the following proposition.

Proposition 3.1
.

P
is bijective.

Proof.

For any
u,v\in H^{1}(V)
, define

B(u,v):=\int\limits_{V}\Gamma(u,v)+(e^{u_{0}}+1)uvd\mu.

By Cauchy Schwartz inequality and H
\ddot{\text{o}}
lder inequality, we deduce that

\displaystyle|B(u,v)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{V}|\Gamma(u,v)|+\left(e^{u_{0}}+1\right)|u||v|d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\int_{V}\sum_{y\sim x}\frac{w_{xy}}{2\mu(x)}|u(y)-u(x)||v(y)-v(x)|d\mu

\displaystyle+\max_{V}\left(e^{u_{0}}+1\right)\left(\int_{V}u^{2}d\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{V}v^{2}d\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{V}\left(\sum_{y\sim x}\frac{w_{xy}}{2\mu(x)}(u(y)-u(x))^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{y\sim x}\frac{w_{xy}}{2\mu(x)}(v(y)-v(x))^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}d\mu

\displaystyle+C_{1}||u||_{2}||v||_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{V}[\Gamma(u,u)]^{\frac{1}{2}}[\Gamma(v,v)]^{\frac{1}{2}}d\mu+C_{1}||u||_{2}||v||_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{V}|\nabla u||\nabla v|d\mu+C_{1}||u||_{2}||v||_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{V}|\nabla u|^{2}d\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{V}|\nabla v|^{2}d\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+C_{1}||u||_{2}||v||_{2},

where
C_{1}=\max\limits_{V}(e^{u_{0}}+1)
.
By (
4
) , we have

(11)

B(u,u)\geq\int\limits_{V}|\nabla u|^{2}+\min\limits_{V}(e^{u_{0}}+1)u^{2}d\mu.

Therefore, we can find constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that

(12)

|B(u,v)|\leq C_{1}||u||_{H^{1}(V)}||v||_{H^{1}(V)},

and

(13)

B(u,u)\geq C_{2}||u||^{2}_{H^{1}(V)}.

It is easy to check that
B:~H^{1}(V)\times H^{1}(V)\to\mathbb{R}
is a bilinear mapping.
Thus, by the Lax-Milgram Theorem, for any function
g
on
V
, there exists a unique element
u\in H^{1}(V)
such that

(14)

B(u,v)=-\int\limits_{V}gvd\mu,

for any
v\in H^{1}(V)
. Since (
14
) is equivalent to
Pu=g
, we see that
P:H^{1}(V)\to L^{2}(V)
is bijective.

We next prove necessity. Suppose
\Delta u=f
. Since
\int_{V}\Delta ud\mu=0
, we know that
\int_{V}fd\mu=0
.

We now complete the proof.
∎

By Proposition
3.1
, we can define the inverse operator of
P
by
P^{-1}
. Furthermore, we have the following proposition.

Proposition 3.2
.

P^{-1}:L^{2}(V)\to H^{1}(V)
is compact.

Proof.

For any
g\in L^{2}(V)
, by Proposition
3.3
, there exists
u\in H^{1}(V)
such that
Pu=g
, which is equivalent to

B(u,v)=-\int\limits_{V}gvd\mu,

for any
v\in H^{1}(V)
. By Cauchy inequality with
\epsilon
, (
\epsilon>0
), we see that

B(u,u)=-\int\limits_{V}gud\mu\leq\frac{1}{4\epsilon}\int\limits_{V}g^{2}d\mu+\epsilon\int\limits_{V}u^{2}d\mu.

Thus, from (
11
), we conclude that

(15)

\int\limits_{V}|\nabla u|^{2}+C_{2}u^{2}d\mu\leq\frac{1}{4\epsilon}\int\limits_{V}g^{2}d\mu+\epsilon\int\limits_{V}u^{2}d\mu.

Taking
\epsilon=\frac{C_{2}}{2}
in (
15
), we deduce that

\int\limits_{V}|\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{C_{2}}{2}u^{2}d\mu\leq\frac{1}{2C_{2}}\int\limits_{V}g^{2}d\mu.

Therefore, there exists
C_{3}>0
such that

||u||^{2}_{H^{1}(V)}\leq C_{3}\int\limits_{V}g^{2}d\mu.

Thus, by Lemma
2.1
, we know that
P^{-1}:L^{2}(V)\to H^{1}(V)
is compact.
∎

Next, we give a necessary condition for equation (

Lemma 3.3
.

If the equation (

Proof.

Assume that

is a solution of the equation (

0=\int\limits_{V}\Delta vd\mu=\int\limits_{V}e^{v+u_{0}}-1+4\pi Nfd\mu=\int\limits_{V}e^{v+u_{0}}d\mu-|V|+4\pi N.

The following lemma gives the uniqueness of solutions of the equation (
3
).

Lemma 3.4
.

There exists at most one solution of (
3
).

Proof.

If
u
and
v
both satisfy equation (
3
), by mean value theorem, there exists
\xi
such that

\Delta u-\Delta v=e^{u+u_{0}}-e^{v+u_{0}}=e^{\xi+u_{0}}(u-v).

Let
M=\max\limits_{V}(u-v)=(u-v)(x_{0})
. We claim that
M\leq 0
. Otherwise,
M>0
. Thus, we deduce that

\Delta(u-v)(x_{0})=[e^{\xi+u_{0}}(u-v)](x_{0})>0.

By (
3
), we have
\Delta(u-v)(x_{0})\leq 0.
This is a contradiction. Thus, we have
u(x)\leq v(x)
on
V
. Therefore, we obtain
u\equiv v
on
V
.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Suppose that
|V|>4\pi N
. Then there exist
U
and
Z
satisfying
U\geq Z
such that

\Delta U-e^{U+u_{0}}-(4\pi Nf-1)\leq 0,

and

\Delta Z-e^{Z+u_{0}}-(4\pi Nf-1)\geq 0.

Proof.

By Propositions
3.1
and
3.2
, we see that
-P^{-1}
is a compact operator. Suppose
(1+P^{-1})U=0,
then we deduce that
(\Delta-e^{u_{0}})U=0.
By a similar argument as Lemma
3.4
, we obtain
U=0
. Thus, by Fredholm alternative, we deduce that there exists
U\in H^{1}(V)
such that

(1+P^{-1})U=P^{-1}(4\pi Nf-1).

Thus,
U
satisfies

(\Delta-e^{u_{0}})U=4\pi Nf-1.

Therefore, we obtain

\Delta U-e^{U+u_{0}}-(4\pi Nf-1)\leq\Delta U-e^{U+u_{0}}-(4\pi Nf-1)+e^{u_{0}}(e^{U}-U)=0.

Let
Z
satisfying

\Delta Z=4\pi Nf-\frac{4\pi N}{|V|}.

It is easy to see that

\Delta Z-e^{Z+u_{0}}-(4\pi Nf-1)=1-\frac{4\pi N}{|V|}-e^{Z+u_{0}}\geq 0.

\Delta Z-\Delta U\geq e^{Z+u_{0}}-e^{U+u_{0}}.

By mean value theorem, there exists
\eta

\Delta Z-\Delta U\geq e^{Z+u_{0}}-e^{U+u_{0}}=e^{\eta+u_{0}}(Z-U).

By a similar argument as Lemma
3.4
, we deduce that
Z\leq U
on
V
.

The proof is complete.
∎

.

\displaystyle(\Delta-K)w_{n+1}

\displaystyle=e^{u_{0}+w_{n}}-Kw_{n}+4\pi Nf-1,n=0,1,2,\cdots,

\displaystyle w_{0}

\displaystyle=U.

. Suppose that
w_{k}\leq U

\displaystyle(\Delta-K)(w_{k+1}-U)

\displaystyle\geq 0,

We next show that

Assume that
w_{k}\leq w_{k-1}
on

\displaystyle\Delta(w_{k+1}-w_{k})-K(w_{k+1}-w_{k})

\displaystyle=(e^{u_{0}+w_{k}}-e^{u_{0}+w_{k-1}})-K(w_{k}-w_{k-1})

\displaystyle\geq 0,

The Beta Flexible Weibull Distribution
Beih S. El-Desouky, Abdelfattah Mustafa and Shamsan AL-Garash

Abstract
We introduce in this paper a new generalization of the flexible Weibull distribution with four parameters. This model based on the Beta generalized (BG) distribution, Eugene et al. [ 7 ] , they first using the BG distribution for generating new generalizations. This new model is called the beta flexible Weibull BFW distribution. Some statistical properties such as the mode, the r r th moment, skewness and kurtosis are derived. The moment generating function and the order statistics are obtained. Moreover, the estimations of the parameters are given by maximum likelihood method and the Fisher’s information matrix is derived. Finally, we study the advantage of the BFW distribution by an application using real data set.



Keywords:

Beta Flexible Weibull; Beta Generalized distribution; Modified Flexible Weibull Distribution; Beta Weibull Distribution; Maximum Likelihood Method.

F_{BG}(x;p,q,\varphi)=I_{F(x;\varphi)}(p,q)=\frac{1}{B(p,q)}\int\limits_{0}^{F(x,\varphi)}u^{p-1}(1-u)^{q-1}du,\hskip 9.24994ptp,q>0,

f^{{}^{\prime}}(x;\eta)=0.

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(p+q)}{\Gamma(p).\Gamma(q)}e^{\alpha x-\frac{\beta}{x}}e^{-qe^{\alpha x-\frac{\beta}{x}}}\left[1-e^{-e^{\alpha x-\frac{\beta}{x}}}\right]^{p-1}\times

,
X_{2}
,
\cdots
,
X_{n}

and density function

can be written as

f_{r:n}(x;\eta)=\frac{1}{B(r,n-r+1)}\left[F_{BFW}(x;\eta)\right]^{r-1}\left[1-F_{BFW}(x;\eta)\right]^{n-r}f_{BFW}(x;\eta),

where

and

are the CDF and PDF of the BFWD
(\eta)
given by Eq. (
2.1
) and Eq. (
2.2

X_{1:n}=\min(X_{1},X_{2},\cdots,X_{n})
, and the last order statistics as
X_{n:n}=\max(X_{1},X_{2},\cdots,X_{n})
, where
0<F_{BFW}(x;\eta)<1,

\forall x>0.

The binomial expansion of
[1-F_{BFW}(x;\eta)]^{n-r}
can be expressed as

\left[1-F_{BFW}(x;\eta)\right]^{n-r}=\sum_{i=0}^{n-r}\begin{pmatrix}n-r\\
i\end{pmatrix}(-1)^{i}[F_{BFW}(x;\eta)]^{i}.

(5.2)

Using Eq. (
5.2
) in Eq. (
5.1
), we get

f_{r:n}(x;\eta)=\frac{1}{B(r,n-r+1)}f_{BFW}(x;\eta)\sum_{i=0}^{n-r}\begin{pmatrix}n-r\\
i\end{pmatrix}(-1)^{i}\left[F_{BFW}(x;\eta)\right]^{i+r-1}.

(5.3)

Using Eq. (
2.1
) and Eq. (
2.2
) in Eq. (

f_{r:n}(x;\eta)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-r}\frac{(-1)^{i}n!}{i!(r-1)!(n-r-i)!}\left[F_{BFW}(x,\eta)\right]^{i+r-1}f_{BFW}(x;\eta).

6
Parameters Estimation

Parameters estimation of the BFWD
(\alpha,\beta,p,q)
, point and interval estimation are derived by using maximum likelihood estimation MLE method with a complete sample.

6.1
Maximum likelihood estimation

Let
x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n}
denote a random sample of complete data from the BFWD
(\eta)
. The Likelihood function
L
is defined as follows

L=\prod_{i=1}^{n}f(x_{i};\vartheta).

(6.1)

Using Eq.(
2.2
) in Eq.(
6.1
), we have

L=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\Gamma(p+q)}{\Gamma(p).\Gamma(q)}\left(\alpha+\frac{\beta}{x_{i}^{2}}\right)e^{\alpha x_{i}-\frac{\beta}{x_{i}}}e^{-qe^{\alpha x_{i}-\frac{\beta}{x_{i}}}}\left[1-e^{-e^{\alpha x_{i}-\frac{\beta}{x_{i}}}}\right]^{p-1}.

The log-likelihood function is

\mathcal{L}=n\left[\ln\left(\frac{\Gamma(p+q)}{\Gamma(p).\Gamma(q)}\right)\right]+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\ln\left(\alpha+\frac{\beta}{x_{i}^{2}}\right)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\alpha x_{i}-\frac{\beta}{x_{i}}\right)-q\sum_{i=1}^{n}e^{\alpha x_{i}-\frac{\beta}{x_{i}}}+(p-1)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\ln\left(1-e^{-e^{\alpha x_{i}-\frac{\beta}{x_{i}}}}\right).

(6.2)

The MLE of the parameters
\alpha,\beta,p
and
q
are obtained by differentiating the function
\mathcal{L}
in Equation (
6.2
) with respect to
\alpha,\beta,p
and
q
, then equating it to zero, as follows

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{x_{i}^{2}}{\beta+\alpha x_{i}^{2}}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}-q\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}e^{\alpha x_{i}-\frac{\beta}{x_{i}}}+(p-1)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{x_{i}e^{\alpha x_{i}-\frac{\beta}{x_{i}}}}{e^{e^{\alpha x_{i}-\frac{\beta}{x_{i}}}}-1}=0,

(6.3)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\beta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\beta+\alpha x_{i}^{2}}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{x_{i}}+q\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{x_{i}}e^{\alpha x_{i}-\frac{\beta}{x_{i}}}-(p-1)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{e^{\alpha x_{i}-\frac{\beta}{x_{i}}}}{x_{i}\left(e^{e^{\alpha x_{i}-\frac{\beta}{x_{i}}}}-1\right)}=0,

(6.4)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n\psi_{p}(p+q)-n\psi(p)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\ln\left(1-e^{-e^{\alpha x_{i}-\frac{\beta}{x_{i}}}}\right)=0,

(6.5)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n\psi_{q}(p+q)-n\psi(q)-\sum_{i=1}^{n}e^{\alpha x_{i}-\frac{\beta}{x_{i}}}=0.

(6.6)

The MLEs can be obtained by solving the equations, (
6.3
)-(
6.6
), numerically for
\alpha,\beta,p
and
q
by using Mathcad or Maple software.

6.2
Asymptotic confidence bounds

The asymptotic confidence intervals can be obtained by using variance covariance matrix
I^{-1}
, since the parameters (
\alpha,\beta,p,q
) are positive and the
I^{-1}
is inverse of the observed information matrix given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{I^{-1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}-\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{L}}{\partial\alpha^{2}}&-\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{L}}{\partial\alpha\partial\beta}&-\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{L}}{\partial\alpha\partial p}&-\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{L}}{\partial\alpha\partial q}\\
-\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{L}}{\partial\beta\partial\alpha}&-\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{L}}{\partial\beta^{2}}&-\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{L}}{\partial\beta\partial p}&-\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{L}}{\partial\beta\partial q}\\

cov(\hat{q},\hat{\alpha})&cov(\hat{q},\hat{\beta})&cov(\hat{q},\hat{p})&var(\hat{q})\end{array}\right),

where

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{L}}{\partial\alpha^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{x_{i}^{4}}{\left(\beta+\alpha x_{i}^{2}\right)^{2}}-q\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{2}e^{\alpha x_{i}-\frac{\beta}{x_{i}}}+(p-1)\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}H_{i},

(6.17)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{L}}{\partial\alpha\partial\beta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{x_{i}^{2}}{\left(\beta+\alpha x_{i}^{2}\right)^{2}}+q\sum_{i=1}^{n}e^{\alpha x_{i}-\frac{\beta}{x_{i}}}-(p-1)\sum_{i=1}^{n}H_{i},

(6.18)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{L}}{\partial\alpha\partial p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{x_{i}e^{\alpha x_{i}-\frac{\beta}{x_{i}}}}{\left[e^{e^{\alpha x_{i}-\frac{\beta}{x_{i}}}}-1\right]},

(6.19)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{L}}{\partial\alpha\partial q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}e^{\alpha x_{i}-\frac{\beta}{x_{i}}},

(6.20)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\left(\beta+\alpha x_{i}^{2}\right)^{2}}-q\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{e^{\alpha x_{i}-\frac{\beta}{x_{i}}}}{x_{i}^{2}}+(p-1)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{H_{i}}{x_{i}^{2}},

(6.21)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{e^{\alpha x_{i}-\frac{\beta}{x_{i}}}}{x_{i}\left[e^{e^{\alpha x_{i}-\frac{\beta}{x_{i}}}}-1\right]},

(6.22)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{L}}{\partial\beta\partial q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{e^{\alpha x_{i}-\frac{\beta}{x_{i}}}}{x_{i}},

(6.23)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{L}}{\partial p^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial p^{2}}\ln\left[\Gamma(p+q)\right]-n\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial p^{2}}\ln\left[\Gamma(p)\right],

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{L}}{\partial p\partial q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial p\partial q}\ln\left[\Gamma(p+q)\right],

(6.25)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{L}}{\partial q^{2}}

8
Conclusion

We studied a new generalized distribution, based on the beta generated method. This new generalization is called the beta flexible Weibull BFWD distribution . Its definition and some of statistical properties are studied. The maximum likelihood method is used for estimating parameters. The advantage of the BFWD is interpreted by an application using real data. Moreover, it is shown that the beta flexible Weibull BFWD distribution fits better than existing generalizations of the Weibull distribution.

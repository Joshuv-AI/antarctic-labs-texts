Non-uniform Lattices of Large Systole Containing a Fixed 3-Manifold Group
Paige Hillen

Abstract
. Let d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 be a square free integer and ℚ ⁡ ( d ) \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d}) a totally real quadratic field over ℚ \mathbb{Q} . We show there exists an arithmetic lattice ℒ \mathcal{L} in S ​ L ​ ( 8 , ℝ ) SL(8,\mathbb{R}) with entries in the ring of integers of ℚ ⁡ ( d ) \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d}) and a sequence of lattices Λ n \Lambda_{n} commensurable to ℒ \mathcal{L} such that the systole of the locally symmetric finite volume manifold Λ n ​ ╲ ​ S ​ L ​ ( 8 , ℝ ) ​ ╱ ​ S ​ O ​ ( 8 ) \Lambda_{n}\diagdown SL(8,\mathbb{R})\diagup SO(8) goes to infinity as n → ∞ n\rightarrow\infty , yet every Λ n \Lambda_{n} contains the same hyperbolic 3-manifold group Π \Pi , a finite index subgroup of the arithmetic hyperbolic 3-manifold vol3. 1.
Introduction

The relationship between the the
systolic genus
, the minimal genus surface subgroup, and the
systole
, the minimal length of a non-contractible closed geodesic, is notably different in higher rank. In 2012, Belolipetsky
[
1
]
(see also
[
2
]
) showed that in the hyperbolic setting, the systolic genus is bounded from below in terms of the systole. In contrast, Long and Reid
[
14
]
found a family of sequences of lattices in
SL(3,\mathbb{R})
(commensurable to an arbitrary non-uniform arithmetic lattice not commensurable to
SL(3,\mathbb{Z})
) with systole going to infinity, yet each contains a genus 3 surface subgroup. They found a similar result in the uniform case. Hence in higher rank, the systolic genus is not linked to the systole in the same way as in the hyperbolic setting.

Our result continues in this line of research. We expand Long and Reid’s result to the existence of a fixed 3-manifold group in a sequence of commensurable lattices with arbitrarily large systole. More specifically, we find an infinite family of non-uniform arithmetic lattices in
SL(8,\mathbb{R})
each with a sequence of commensurable lattices whose systole
\rightarrow\infty
, however every lattice in the sequence contains the same hyperbolic 3-manifold group. Our non-uniform arithmetic lattices are indexed by square free numbers
d\geq 2
: for each such
d
, we consider the integral special unitary group

SU(I_{8};\mathcal{O}_{d},\tau):=\{A\in SL(8,\mathcal{O}_{d}):\tau(A)^{\top}A=I_{8}\}<SL(8,\mathbb{R})

\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})
and

is the non-trivial involution sending
\sqrt{d}
to
-\sqrt{d}
.

Consider the following measures of the geometry and topology of a space:

For hyperbolic manifolds, the behavior of the systole puts some restrictions on the behavior of the systolic genus. This is clear for hyperbolic surfaces, since Besicovitch’s inequality

\rightarrow\infty

[
1
]
):

Theorem 2
.

Let

be a sequence of closed hyperbolic
m

(\Gamma_{n})\rightarrow\infty

(M_{n})\rightarrow\infty

Intuitively, if the manifolds are getting complicated enough for the systole to grow arbitrarily large, their topology must be getting complicated as well. However, once we leave the hyperbolic setting, this is no longer true. In particular, the presence of flats seems to allow enough space for systoles to grow, without the systolic genus increasing.

Systolic Growth

Further Questions and Observations

(1)

Thin 3-manifold Group:
Our 8 dimensional representation
\omega
is conjugate to two copies of the original representation
\rho
. By the proof of Theorem 2.1 in
[
8
]
,
\rho_{t}(\text{vol3})
is Zariski dense in
SL(4,\mathbb{R})
. Therefore the Zariski closure of
\omega_{t}(
vol3
)
in
SL(8,\mathbb{R})
is a subgroup isomorphic to
SL(4,\mathbb{R})
.
We also observe that for any of our chosen values of
t
,
\omega_{t}(\Pi)
is infinite index in
SU(J_{t};\mathcal{O}_{d},\tau)
. To see this, observe that
SU(J_{t};\mathcal{O}_{d},\tau)
is an irreducible arithmetic lattice of
\mathbb{R}-
rank
\geq 2
. Thus, by Margulis’s Normal Subgroup Theorem, if
\omega_{t}(
vol3
)
were finite index in
SU(J_{t};\mathcal{O}_{d},\tau)
, then the abelianization of
\omega_{t}(
vol3
)
would be finite. However, vol3 is an arithmetic hyperbolic 3-manifold with positive first Betti number. Thus vol3 must have infinite virtual first Betti number, so the abelianization cannot be finite (see
[
6
]
).
It would be interesting to know whether
\omega_{t}(
vol3
)
is infinite index in the intersection of
SU(J_{t};\mathcal{O}_{d},\tau)
and Zcl
(\omega_{t}(
vol3
))\cong SL(4,\mathbb{R})
. If so, then
\omega_{t}(
vol3
)
is a thin 3-manifold group.

(2)

Non-Hitchin Surface Subgroups:
At
v=2
the image
\rho_{2}(\text{vol3})
lies in
SO(3,1)
, so every non-trivial semi-simple element has a pair of eigenvalues with the same modulus. Thus
\rho_{2}
is not Borel Anosov (for example, by the characterization of Anosov in theorem 4.3 of
[
15
]
). Moreover, the restriction to any subgroup is also not Borel Anosov. Hence for any value of
v\geq 2
and any surface subgroup
H
,
\rho_{v}(H)
is a discrete and faithful representation in
SL(4,\mathbb{R})
which is not on the Hitchin component. This is notable, since many examples of interesting representations of surface subgroups in the literature are in the Hitchin component. In particular, if one can find a surface subgroup of
\rho_{v}(
vol3
)
which is Borel Anosov for some value of
v>2
, then it would be an example of a non-Hitchin Borel Anosov representation, answering a question posed by Canary
[
4
]
, Question 50.6.

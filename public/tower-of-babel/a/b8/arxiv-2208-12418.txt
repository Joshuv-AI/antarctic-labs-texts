Direct summands of klt singularities
Ziquan Zhuang

Abstract
. We show that direct summands (or more generally, pure images) of klt type singularities are of klt type. As a consequence, we give a different proof of a recent result of Braun, Greb, Langlois and Moraga that reductive quotients of klt type singularities are of klt type.



Unless otherwise stated, throughout the paper we work over an algebraically closed field
\mathbbm{k}
of characteristic zero.

1.
Introduction

Given a morphism
Y=\mathbf{Spec}(B)\to X=\mathbf{Spec}(A)
between affine algebraic varieties, we say that
X
is a direct summand of
Y
if
A
is a direct summand of
B
as
A
-modules. More generally, we say the morphism is pure if
A
is a pure subring of
B
, i.e.
M\to M\otimes_{A}B
is injective for any
A
-module
M
. Some interesting examples are GIT quotients by reductive groups and faithfully flat morphisms between normal varieties. The purpose of this note is to show that certain classes of singularities from the Minimal Model Program (see e.g.
[
15
]
) are preserved under pure morphisms.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
f\colon Y\to X
be a pure morphism between affine varieties. Assume that
Y
is of klt type
1
1

1

This means there exists some effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
D
on
Y
such that the pair
(Y,D)
is klt. In some literature this is also called potentially klt. More generally we say a pair
(X,\Delta)
(where
\Delta
is an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on
X
) is of klt (resp. plt, resp. lc) type if locally there exists some effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
D
such that
(X,\Delta+D)
is klt (resp. plt, resp. lc).
. Then
X
is also of klt type. In particular, direct summands of klt type singularities are of klt type.

This refines Boutot’s theorem
[
2
]
that pure images of rational singularities are rational. We also prove an analogous statement in the plt case, see Theorem
2.10
. As immediate applications, we deduce that images of klt type singularities under equidimensional morphisms are of klt type (Corollary
2.9
), and give a new proof of a recent result of Braun, Greb, Langlois and Moraga
[
3
]
that reductive quotients of klt type singularities are of klt type.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
X=\mathbf{Spec}(A)
be an affine variety of klt type and let
G
be a reductive group acting on
X
. Then the quotient
X/\!/G=\mathbf{Spec}(A^{G})
is also of klt type.

We refer to
[
3
]
for several further applications of the above result.

Theorem
1.1
is suggested by positive characteristic considerations: the characteristic
p>0
analog of klt singularities is the class of strongly
F
-regular singularities, which by definition is preserved by taking direct summands (the Hochster-Roberts theorem
[
13
]
that reductive quotient singularities are Cohen-Macaulay was based on a similar circle of ideas). In fact, several special cases of Theorem
1.1
, e.g. when
X
and

come from section rings of Mori dream spaces
[
9
]
, or when
X
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein
[
20
]
, have been established by using the connection between klt and strongly
F
-regular singularities. Our proof of Theorem
1.1
does not rely on the theory of
F
-singularities (in particular we also give a new proof of Schoutens’ result
[
20
]
), but it would still be interesting to find a proof of Theorem
1.1
through the conjecture of Schwede and Smith (see
[
21
]
*Section 7) that strongly

-regular type singularities are also of klt type. We also expect the log canonical version of Theorem
1.1
to hold (see Question

, c.f.
[

]
*Question 8.5,
[

*Corollary B), although our current method does not seem to apply to the lc setting.

2.
Proof

2.1.
Some preliminary observations

We start by collecting some preliminary results that will be useful in the proof of Theorem
1.1
. First we recall a criterion for purity.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
14
]
*Corollary 5.3)
.

Let
A
be a Noetherian ring which is subring of
B
. Then
A
is a pure subring of
B
if and only if
A
is a direct summand, as an
A
-module, of every finitely generated
A
-submodule of
B
that contains it.

Eventually the goal is to reduce Theorem
1.1
to the case when
X
is Gorenstein, as
[
2
]
has shown that pure images of rational singularities remain rational, and for Gorenstein singularities being rational is equivalent to being klt. The next result will provide the basis for performing various reduction steps.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
f\colon Y=\mathbf{Spec}(B)\to X=\mathbf{Spec}(A)
be a pure morphism between affine Noetherian schemes. Let
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq A
be an ideal, let
U\subseteq X
be an open subset, let
V=f^{-1}(U)\subseteq Y
, and let
L
be a line bundle on
U
. Then

(1)

A/\mathfrak{a}
is a pure subring of
B/\mathfrak{a}B
.

(2)

H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{U})
is a pure subring of
H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{V})
.

(3)

\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{N}}H^{0}(U,\mathcal{O}_{U}(mL))
is a pure subring of
\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{N}}H^{0}(V,\mathcal{O}_{V}(mf^{*}L))
.

Proof.

[
13
]
*Lemma 6.2 gives (1). To see (2), note that any finitely generated
H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{U})
-submodule
N
of
H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{V})=H^{0}(U,f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{V})
is contained in
H^{0}(U,\mathcal{F}|_{U})
for some coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}\subseteq f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y}
. Since
A
is a pure subring of
B
, by Lemma
2.1
we get a spitting map
p\colon\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}
, which induces a splitting
N\subseteq H^{0}(U,\mathcal{F}|_{U})\to H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{U})
. Another application of Lemma
2.1
then gives (2) (note that
a priori

H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{U})
may fail to be Noetherian, but the Noetherian condition is not needed for the “if” direction of Lemma
2.1
; the same remark applies to the proof of part (3) below).

For any quasi-coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on
X
, write
R(\mathcal{F}):=\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{N}}H^{0}(U,\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{U}(mL))
. By projection formula, we have
H^{0}(V,\mathcal{O}_{V}(mf^{*}L))=H^{0}(U,f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{V}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{U}(mL))
. Thus the two rings in part (3) are
R(\mathcal{O}_{X})
and
R(f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y})
. The proof of (3) then proceeds just as in (2), noting that any finitely generated
R(\mathcal{O}_{X})
-submodule of

is contained in
R(\mathcal{F})
for some coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}\subseteq f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y}
.
∎

2.2.
A special case

We next consider a special case of Theorem
1.1
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
f\colon Y\to X
be a pure morphism between affine normal varieties. Assume that
Y
is of klt type,
K_{X}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier, and
f^{-1}(X_{\mathrm{sing}})
has codimension at least two in

has klt singularities.

Note that the above statement, even without the assumption that

has codimension at least two in

[
20
]
using the techniques of

Proof.

Let

be a closed point. The statement is local on
X
, so after shrinking

we may assume that
\mathcal{O}_{X}(rK_{X})\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}
where

is the index of
K_{X}
at
x
. Let
s\in H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(rK_{X}))
be a nowhere vanishing section, let
\pi\colon X^{\prime}\to X
be the corresponding index one cover
[
15
]
*Definition 5.19, and let
Y^{\prime}
be the normalization of the main components of
X^{\prime}\times_{X}Y
. For simplicity, we also denote the map
Y^{\prime}\to Y
by
\pi
. Let
U
be the smooth locus of
X
, let
V=f^{-1}(U)
, and let
U^{\prime}
(resp.
V^{\prime}
) be the preimage of
U
(resp.
V
) in
X^{\prime}
(resp.
Y^{\prime}
). Then
\pi
is étale over both
U
and
V
, the map
V^{\prime}\to V
is the cyclic cover corresponding to the section
f^{*}s\in H^{0}(V,\mathcal{O}_{V}(rf^{*}K_{U}))
, and we have

\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{U^{\prime}}=\left(\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{O}_{U}(mK_{U})\cdot t^{m}\right)/(st^{r}-1),\,\,\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{V^{\prime}}=\left(\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{O}_{V}(mf^{*}K_{U})\cdot t^{m}\right)/(st^{r}-1).

By assumption, the complement of
U
(resp.
V
) in
X
(resp.
Y
) has codimension at least two. It follows that

\displaystyle H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}})=H^{0}(U,\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{U^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=\left(\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{N}}H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{U}(mK_{U}))\cdot t^{m}\right)/(st^{r}-1),

\displaystyle H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{Y^{\prime}})=H^{0}(V,\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{V^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=\left(\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{N}}H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{V}(mf^{*}K_{U}))\cdot t^{m}\right)/(st^{r}-1).

Combined with Lemma
2.2
(3) and Lemma
2.2
(1), we see that the morphism

is pure. Note that

may have several connected components. Nonetheless, by
[

]
*Corollary 6.12 and the fact that
\pi^{-1}(x)
consists of a single point in

, we know that after possible shrinking

around

, there exists some component (say
Y^{\prime}_{1}
) of
Y^{\prime}
such that
Y^{\prime}_{1}\to X^{\prime}
is pure. As
Y^{\prime}_{1}\to Y
is quasi-étale (it is étale over

), we see that
Y^{\prime}_{1}
is of klt type by
[

]
*Proposition 5.20, hence it has rational singularities by
[

]
*Theorem 5.22. By
[

]
, this implies that its pure image

also has rational singularities. By construction,

is Cartier, thus
[

]
*Corollary 5.24 yields that

has klt singularities. As

is quasi-étale, another application of
[

]
*Proposition 5.20 gives the desired result that the singularities of

are klt.
∎

2.3.
A klt type criterion

To deduce Theorem
1.1
from Lemma
2.3
, two difficulties lie ahead: the image
X
is not
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein in general, and even if it is
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein, the preimage
f^{-1}(X_{\mathrm{sing}})
of the singular locus may well contain a divisor in
Y
. We will address the latter issue at the very end: the point is that after a small birational modification one can “throw away” the divisorial part of
f^{-1}(X_{\mathrm{sing}})
from
Y
while retaining purity. Here we focus on the
\mathbb{Q}
-Gorenstein part. The key is to use the following klt type criterion.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
X
be a normal variety and
\Delta
an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on
X
. Then
(X,\Delta)
is of klt
(
resp. plt
)
type if and only if the following two conditions are simultaneously satisfied:

(1)

R(X,-r(K_{X}+\Delta)):=\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{O}_{X}(-mr(K_{X}+\Delta))
is a sheaf of finitely generated
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-algebras for some positive integer
r
such that
\mathrm{Coef}(r\Delta)\in\mathbb{Z}
.

(2)

The pair
(X^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime})
, where
X^{\prime}:=\mathbf{Proj}_{X}R(X,-r(K_{X}+\Delta))
and
\Delta^{\prime}
is the strict transform of
\Delta
, is klt
(
resp. plt
)
.

Proof.

We only prove the klt case since the plt case is very similar. Since the statement is local, we may assume that
X
is affine. By
[
15
]
*Lemma 6.2, as long as
R(X,-r(K_{X}+\Delta))
is finitely generated, the morphism
\pi\colon X^{\prime}\to X
is an isomorphism in codimension one and
-(K_{X^{\prime}}+\Delta^{\prime})=-\pi^{-1}_{*}(K_{X}+\Delta)
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier and
\pi
-ample. It follows that
-(K_{X^{\prime}}+\Delta^{\prime})
is ample since
X
is affine.

First suppose that
(X,\Delta)
is of klt type. Then
R(X,-r(K_{X}+\Delta))
is finitely generated by the following Lemma
2.5
. If
(X,\Delta+D)
is klt, then its crepant pullback to
X^{\prime}
remains klt. In particular,
(X^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime})
is of klt type. Since
K_{X^{\prime}}+\Delta^{\prime}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier, this implies that
(X^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime})
is klt.

Suppose next that (1) and (2) holds. Since
-(K_{X^{\prime}}+\Delta^{\prime})
is ample and
(X^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime})
is klt, by Bertini’s theorem we may choose some
0\leq D^{\prime}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}-(K_{X^{\prime}}+\Delta^{\prime})
such that the pair
(X^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime}+D^{\prime})
is klt. Note that
K_{X^{\prime}}+\Delta^{\prime}+D^{\prime}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}0
, hence
K_{X^{\prime}}+\Delta^{\prime}+D^{\prime}=\pi^{*}(K_{X}+\Delta+D)
for some effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
D
on
X
such that
(X,\Delta+D)
is klt. In other words,
(X,\Delta)
is of klt type.
∎

The following result is used in the above proof. For a more general version, see
[
4
]
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
X
be a variety of klt type and let
D
be a Weil divisor on
X
. Then there exists a small birational modification
\pi\colon X^{\prime}\to X
such that the strict transform
D^{\prime}=\pi^{-1}_{*}D
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier and
\pi
-ample. In particular, the sheaf
\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{O}_{X}(mD)
of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-algebras is finitely generated.

Proof.

This follows easily from
[
1
]
, see e.g.
[
24
]
*Lemma 4.7.
∎

2.4.
Finite generation

We thus proceed to show that the two conditions in Lemma
2.4
are satisfied by pure images of klt type singularities. In this subsection, we deal with the finite generation part; the other condition will be verified afterwards by reducing to the special case treated in Lemma
2.3
. We need an auxiliary result.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
Y
be an affine variety of klt type, let
V\subseteq Y
be an open subset, and let
L
be a Weil divisor on
V
. Then:

(1)

Y_{0}:=\mathbf{Spec}(H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{V}))
is of klt type.

(2)

The algebra
R(V,L)=\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{N}}H^{0}(V,\mathcal{O}_{V}(mL))
is finitely generated over
H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{V})
.

(3)

The birational map
V\dashrightarrow Y_{0}
identifies
V
with a big open set of
Y_{0}

(
i.e.
Y_{0}\setminus V
has codimension at least two
)
.

(4)

If
\Delta
is an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on
Y
such that
(Y,\Delta)
is of klt
(
resp. plt
)
type, then so is the strict transform
(Y_{0},\Delta_{0})
.

Proof.

We shall give a geometric construction of
Y_{0}
. Let
D
be the divisorial part of
Z:=\mathrm{Supp}(Y\setminus V)
(if
Z
has codimension
\geq 2
in
Y
then
D=0
). By Lemma
2.5
, there exists a small birational morphism
\pi\colon Y^{\prime}\to Y
such that
D^{\prime}=\pi_{*}^{-1}D
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier and
\pi
-ample. By construction, we have:

•

Y^{\prime}\setminus D^{\prime}
is affine (
Y^{\prime}
is projective over the affine variety
Y
, and we remove from it an ample divisor
D^{\prime}
).

•

Y^{\prime}
is of klt type (take the crepant pullback of a klt boundary on
Y
).

•

\pi
is an isomorphism over
V
(the exceptional set is contained in
D
).

By the last property, we see that
V
is a big open subset of
Y^{\prime}\setminus D^{\prime}
, hence
H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{Y^{\prime}\setminus D^{\prime}})=H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{V})
. Combined with the first property we deduce that
Y_{0}\cong Y^{\prime}\setminus D^{\prime}
, hence (3) holds and the second property above implies (1). Similarly (4) holds. The closure of
L
gives a Weil divisor
L_{0}
on
Y_{0}
such that
R(V,L)=R(Y_{0},L_{0})
. By Lemma
2.5
, the algebra
R(Y_{0},L_{0})
is finitely generated. This finishes the proof. ∎

The following lemma gives the desired finite generation.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
f\colon Y\to X
be a pure morphism between normal Noetherian affine schemes. Assume that for any open subset
V\subseteq Y
and any Weil divisor
L
on
V
, the section ring
R(V,L)
is finitely generated
(
as an
H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{Y})
-algebra
)
. Then
R(X,D)
is finitely generated
(
as an
H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X})
-algebra
)
for any Weil divisor
D
on
X
.

By Lemma
2.6
, the above assumption on
Y
holds when
Y
is a variety of klt type over
\mathbbm{k}
. Lemma
2.7
also applies when
Y
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial: in this case the divisorial part of
Y\setminus V
supports some Cartier divisor
D
, and replacing
V
with the open set
Y\setminus D
does not change the section ring; but as
L
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier and
Y\setminus D
is affine, the section ring
R(Y\setminus D,L)
is finitely generated.

Proof.

Let
U
be the regular locus of
X
. By Lemma
2.2
(3),
R(X,D)=R(U,D_{U})
is a pure subalgebra of
R(f^{-1}(U),f^{*}D_{U})
, and the latter is finitely generated by our assumption. If
f
is of finite type, it then follows from
[
11
]
that
R(X,D)
is finitely generated. For the general case, denote
R:=R(X,D)
and
S:=R(f^{-1}(U),f^{*}D_{U})
. Note that
R
is a
graded
pure subalgebra of
S
and its degree
0
part is exactly
H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X})
. Let
I=\bigoplus_{m\geq 1}R_{m}:=\bigoplus_{m\geq 1}H^{0}(X,mD)
. Since
S
is Noetherian, the ideal
IS
is also finitely generated, hence we may write
IS=(g_{1},\dots,g_{m})S
for some homogeneous element
g_{1},\dots,g_{m}\in I
. By purity, the map

R/(g_{1},\cdots,g_{m})R\to S/(g_{1},\cdots,g_{m})S=S/IS

is injective. On the other hand its kernel contains
I/(g_{1},\cdots,g_{m})R
by construction, thus
I=(g_{1},\cdots,g_{m})R
. In other words, any homogeneous element
g\in R
of positive degree can be written as
g=g_{1}h_{1}+\dots+g_{m}h_{m}
for some
h_{1},\dots,h_{m}\in R
. By induction on the degree, this implies that
R=R_{0}[g_{1},\dots,g_{m}]
, hence
R
is finitely generated.
∎

2.5.
Completion of the proof

Returning to the setup of Theorem
1.1
, we now let
X^{\prime}:=\mathbf{Proj}_{X}(R(X,-K_{X}))
. Recall that
X^{\prime}\to X
is a small modification, and
-K_{X^{\prime}}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier and ample. It remains to show:

Lemma 2.8
.

X^{\prime}
has klt singularities.

Proof.

Let
m
be a sufficiently divisible positive integer. Let
0\leq D^{\prime}\sim-mK_{X^{\prime}}
and let
U_{1}=X^{\prime}\setminus D^{\prime}
. Then
U_{1}
is an affine open subset of
X^{\prime}
and as we vary

, the corresponding
U_{1}
cover

. Thus it suffices to show that
U_{1}
has klt singularities. Let
D
be the strict transform of

on
X
, let
U=X\setminus(X_{\mathrm{sing}}\cup D)
, and let

. Then
U
is a big open subset of
U_{1}
, hence
H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{U_{1}})=H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{U})
. By Lemma
2.2
(2), this is a pure subring of
H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{V})
. Consider the induced morphism
V_{1}:=\mathbf{Spec}(H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{V}))\to U_{1}
. By Lemma

,

is of klt type and

has codimension at least two. By construction,

is

-Cartier and the singular locus of

are satisfied and we deduce that

Proof of Theorem

By

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
f\colon Y\to X
be a pure morphism between affine varieties, let
P
be a prime divisor on
X
, and let
Q=f^{*}P
be the cycle-theoretic pullback. Assume that
(Y,Q)
is of plt type. Then
(X,P)
is of plt type.

Here we define the cycle-theoretic pullback
f^{*}P
of a prime divisor
P
as the Weil divisor associated to the divisorial part of the scheme-theoretic pullback
f^{-1}(P)
. For general
\mathbb{Q}
-divisors
D=\sum a_{i}D_{i}
, the cycle-theoretic pullback is defined by linearity, i.e.
f^{*}D:=\sum a_{i}f^{*}D_{i}
.

Proof.

By replacing
Y
with
\mathbf{Spec}(H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{V}))
where
V=Y\setminus f^{-1}(X_{\mathrm{sing}})
and applying Lemma
2.6
, we may assume throughout the proof that
f^{-1}(X_{\mathrm{sing}})
has codimension at least two in
Y
. In this case, the cycle-theoretic pullback
f^{*}P
is determined by its restriction to
V
, where it becomes the pullback of a Cartier divisor, and hence commutes with finite base change over
X
. By
[
15
]
*Proposition 2.43, we know that
Y
is of klt type, thus so is
X
by Theorem
1.1
. In particular,
X
is normal. By Lemma
2.5

Equality of the Hilbert Hamiltonian and the canonical Hamiltonian for gauge theories in a static spacetime
H. Arthur Weldon

Abstract
The Hilbert energy-momentum tensor for gauge-fixed non-Abelian gauge theories, defined by the variational derivative of the action with respect to the space-time metric, is a tensor under general coordinate transformations, symmetric in its indices, and BRST invariant. The canonical energy-momentum tensor has none of these properties but the canonical Hamiltonian does correctly generate the time dependence of the fields. It is shown that the Hilbert Hamiltonian ∫ d 3 ​ x ​ g ​ T 0 0 \int d^{3}x\,\sqrt{g}\;T^{0}_{\;\;0} is equal to the canonical Hamiltonian for a general gauge theory coupled to spin 1/2 and spin 0 matter fields (including an R ​ ϕ 2 R\phi^{2} term) in a static background metric ( ∂ 0 g μ ​ ν = 0 \partial_{0}g_{\mu\nu}=0 and g 0 ​ j = 0 g_{0j}=0 ). The equality depends on on the Gauss’s law constraint but not on the dynamical Euler-Lagrange equations.



I
Introduction

The Hilbert energy-momentum tensor
Hilbert
in an arbitrary background metric is determined from the Lagrangian density for matter and radiation by

{\delta\over\delta g^{\mu\nu}(x)}\int d^{4}z\;{\cal L}={\sqrt{g}\over 2}T_{\mu\nu}(x)

(1)

where
g\equiv-{\rm det}(g_{\alpha\beta})>0
.

{\cal L}
transforms as a scalar density under general coordinate transformations (i.e.
{\cal L}/\sqrt{g}
is a coordinate scalar) and is
gauge invariant except for a gauge-fixing term.
T_{\mu\nu}
transforms as a tensor
under general coordinate transformations, is symmetric in
\mu\nu
,
and is BRST invariant
Becchi
;
Tyutin
. It is the source of the gravitational field in the Einstein field equations.
The covariant divergence of the mixed tensor is

(T^{\mu}_{\;\;\nu})_{;\mu}={1\over\sqrt{g}}\partial_{\mu}(\sqrt{g}\,T^{\mu}_{\;\;\nu})-{1\over 2}(\partial_{\nu}g_{\alpha\beta})T^{\alpha\beta}.

(2)

If the fields that appear in
{\cal L}
are required to satisfy the field equations then
(T^{\mu}_{\;\;\nu})_{;\mu}=0
(see Sec 94 of
Landau

or Sec 12.3 of
Weinberg1
);
but it is not a true conservation law because of the second term in (
2
).

The so-called canonical energy-momentum tensor that results from Noether’s first theorem
Noether
applied to the invariance of
{\cal L}
under
global spacetime translations is

\sqrt{g}\;\Theta^{\mu}_{\;\;\nu}=\sum_{s}{\partial{\cal L}\over\partial(\partial_{\mu}\chi_{s})}\partial_{\nu}\chi_{s}-\delta^{\mu}_{\;\nu}{\cal L}

(3)

where
\chi_{s}
runs over all the fields: gauge bosons, ghosts, spin 1/2 fermions, and scalar bosons.
Though
\Theta^{\mu}_{\;\;\nu}
is not a
coordinate tensor it will be referred to as the canonical energy-momentum tensor because when
g_{\alpha\beta}(x)
is replaced by the Minkowski metric
(\eta_{\alpha\beta})={\rm diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)
the resulting quantity is a Lorentz tensor.

For fields obeying Fermi statistics the ordering in (
3
) is not accurate. The ghost fields
\eta_{a}
and

\overline{\eta}_{a}
are independent as are the spin 1/2 fields
\psi
and
\psi^{\dagger}
; the correct statement of the
first term in (
3
) is

{\partial{\cal L}\over\partial(\partial_{\mu}\eta_{a})}\partial_{\nu}\eta_{a}+(\partial_{\nu}\overline{\eta}_{a}){\partial{\cal L}\over\partial(\partial_{\mu}\overline{\eta}_{a})}

(4)

for ghosts and

{\partial{\cal L}\over\partial(\partial_{\mu}\psi)}\partial_{\nu}\psi+(\partial_{\nu}\psi^{\dagger}){\partial{\cal L}\over\partial(\partial(\partial_{\mu}\psi^{\dagger})}

(5)

for spin 1/2 fermions. This is rather cumbersome to repeatedly make explicit and so the simpler form (
3
) will
often be used.

\Theta^{\mu}_{\;\;\nu}
improvements in Minkowski spacetime:

The differences between
T^{\mu}_{\;\;\nu}
and
\Theta^{\mu}_{\;\;\nu}
when
g_{\alpha\beta}(x)
is replaced by the Minkowski metric was resolved for electrodynamics by Belinfante and Rosenfeld
Belinfante
;
Rosenfeld
.
In modern approaches
Weinberg2
;
Munoz
;
Saravi
;
Pons
;
Blaschke
;
Ilin
;
Freese
an improved
\Theta^{\mu}_{\;\;\nu}
is obtained by using
both global translation invariance and global Lorentz invariance or with Noether’s second theorem
using local translation invariance. This plus the field equations lead to an improved Lorentz tensor
that agrees with the Hilbert tensor in Minkowski spacetime:

T^{\mu\nu}=\Theta^{\mu\nu}+\partial_{\alpha}\Lambda^{\alpha\mu\nu},

(6)

where the super-potential satisfies
\Lambda^{\alpha\mu\nu}=-\Lambda^{\mu\alpha\nu}
.
Two results follow from the antisymmetry:

\displaystyle T^{0\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Theta^{0\nu}\!+\!\partial_{j}\Lambda^{j0\nu}

(7)

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}(T^{\mu\nu})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}(\Theta^{\mu\nu}).

(8)

The first shows that

\int d^{3}x\,T^{0\nu}=\int d^{3}x\;\Theta^{0\nu};

(9)

the second shows that the integrals (
9
) are time-independent. Both require imposing the field equations.

An interesting generalization is presented in Ref.
Ilin
. If
{\cal L}
contains second derivatives or higher of the fields then
\Lambda^{\alpha\mu\nu}
is not antisymmetric
in the first two indices; nevertheless
\Lambda^{00\nu}
is a spatial divergence
which adds another term to (
7
) so that (
9
) is still valid;
and
\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\alpha}\Lambda^{\alpha\mu\nu}=0
so (
8
) holds which makes (
9
) time independent.

Derivations of
T_{\mu\nu}
for an arbitrary metric:

The Hilbert energy-momentum tensor can be derived in a more
geometrical manner using
the spacetime diffeomorphism group
Gotay
or fibre bundles
Forger
.
Though it is natural to expect that a canonical energy-momentum tensor satisfying the requirements of gauge invariance,
\mu\nu
symmetry, and covariant conservation
would necessarily be equal to the Hilbert tensor,
Baker
treats an example of spin 2 fields, the linearized Gauss-Bonnet gravity model, in which
this is not true.

Static metric with field equations:

A static metric satisfies both
\partial_{0}g_{\alpha\beta}=0
and
g_{0j}=0
. The Schwarzschild and Reissner-Nordström metrics are of this type
and the geometry shares many features of Minkowski spacetime
Sanchez
;
Fulling
;
Derezinski
.
The vanishing of
g_{0j}
means that Lagrangian terms like
\sqrt{g}\,g^{\mu\nu}(\partial_{\mu}\phi)(\partial_{\nu}\phi)
, and
analogous terms for gauge bosons and spin 1/2 fermions, do not
violate time-reversal invariance; the field equations allow separation of variables (time vs three-space); and the propagators
are invariant under global time translation.
If the field equations are satisfied and the metric is static then
\partial_{\mu}(\sqrt{g}\,\Theta^{\mu}_{\;\;0})=0
; thus the canonical Hamiltonian is time-independent

{d\over dt}\int d^{3}x\sqrt{g}\,\Theta^{0}_{\;\;0}=0.

(10)

Under the same conditions (static metric plus field equations) the Hilbert tensor satisfies
\partial_{\mu}(\sqrt{g}\,T^{\mu}_{\;\;0})=0

Landau
;
Weinberg1
and the Hilbert Hamiltonian is time-independent

{d\over dt}\int d^{3}x\sqrt{g}\,T^{0}_{\;\;0}=0.

(11)

It is plausible, but not guaranteed, that the two Hamiltonians are equal; more importantly, the argument gives no information
about what happens when the field equations are not satisfied, as is the case in the functional integral formulation of field theory.

Outline:

This paper investigates what happens when the metric is static and only the non-Abelian
Gauss’s law is imposed but none of the other field equations. It is assumed throughout that the field
decrease at spatial infinity is sufficiently rapid as to allow spatial integration by parts with no boundary terms.

Section II introduces the Lagrangian density
{\cal L}
for a general non-Abelian gauge theory
containing five parts: gauge bosons, gauge fixing, ghosts, spin 1/2 fermions, and scalar bosons:

{\cal L}=\sum_{n=1}^{5}{\cal L}^{n}.

(12)

The spin 1/2 fermions and the scalar bosons are in arbitrary representations of the gauge group.
The scalar bosons
have Yukawa couplings to fermions and a coupling
\xi R\phi_{i}^{2}
to the Ricci scalar curvature.
The variational derivative of each action
\int d^{4}x\,{\cal L}^{n}
gives the Hilbert energy-momentum
tensor
T_{\mu\nu}^{n}
, which is then evaluated for a static metric. The value of
\sqrt{g}\,g^{00}T_{00}^{n}+{\cal L}^{n}

is computed for a static metric.

Section III employs Gauss’s law to obtain

\int d^{3}x\sum_{n=1}^{5}\sqrt{g}\,g^{00}T_{00}^{n}=\int d^{3}x\Big[\sum_{s}\Pi_{s}\partial_{0}\chi_{s}-\sum_{n=1}^{5}{\cal L}^{n}\Big],

(13)

which shows the equality of the Hilbert and the canonical Hamiltonians:

\int d^{3}x\sqrt{g}\,g^{00}T_{00}=\int d^{3}x\sqrt{g}\,g^{00}\Theta_{00}.

(14)

The dynamical Euler-Lagrange equations are not used. The integrals (
14
) could be called
proto-Hamiltonians since they are time-dependent.

At this point the equality of the two Hamiltonians could be a special feature of non-Abelian gauge theories, particularly
since the curvature appeared only in the term
\xi R\phi_{i}^{2}
. To see if the Hamiltonian equality is more general
a term of the form

\sqrt{g}\,R^{\mu\nu}(\partial_{\mu}\phi)(\partial_{\nu}\phi)

(15)

is investigated. Explicit calculation shows that the Hilbert energy density and the canonical energy density
are very different but the Hamiltonians are equal. This rather tedious calculation is
contained in Appendix
D
.

Section IV derives the Hamiltonian equality (
14
) in a general manner in which the details of the Lagrangian density
are not specified except that it has only first derivatives of the fields.
The result is

\partial_{\mu}(\sqrt{g}\,T^{\mu}_{\;\;0})=\partial_{\mu}(\sqrt{g}\,\Theta^{\mu}_{\;\;0})-\partial_{j}N^{j},

(16)

using Gauss’s law but not the dynamical field equations.
The spatial integral is

{d\over dt}\int d^{3}x\sqrt{g}\,T^{0}_{\;\;0}={d\over dt}\int d^{3}x\sqrt{g}\,\Theta^{0}_{\;\;0}.

(17)

Since the fields have arbitrary time dependence the integrals must be equal, which confirms (
14
)
in the more general case.

II
Explicit results for the Hilbert energy-momentum tensor

This section will compute
T_{\mu\nu}^{n}
for
n=1,\dots 5
for a general time-dependent metric and then catalogue
for a static metric the combination
\sqrt{g}\,g^{00}T_{00}^{n}+{\cal L}^{n}
for gauge bosons, ghosts, and spin 1/2 fermions (
n=1,3,4
)
and the integrated form
\int d^{3}x[\sqrt{g}\,g^{00}T_{00}^{n}+{\cal L}^{n}]
for the gauge-fixing term and scalar bosons (
n=2,5

II.1
Gauge bosons

Gauge bosons in a general metric:

The covariant field strength tensor

F^{a}_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}^{a}-\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu}^{a}-ef^{abc}A_{\mu}^{b}A_{\nu}^{c}

(18)

is independent of the metric tensor. (
e
will be used for the gauge coupling since
g
is reserved for the
absolute value of the determinant of the metric.). To make the metric dependence explicit
{\cal L}^{1}
is written in terms of the covariant field strengths

{\cal L}^{1}=-{\sqrt{g}\over 4}F_{\mu\alpha}^{a}F_{\nu\beta}^{a}g^{\mu\nu}g^{\alpha\beta}.

(19)

The variational derivative of the action gives

T_{\mu\nu}^{1}=-F_{\mu\alpha}^{a}F_{\nu\beta}^{a}g^{\alpha\beta}+{g_{\mu\nu}\over 4}F_{\kappa\alpha}^{a}F_{\lambda\beta}^{a}g^{\kappa\lambda}g^{\alpha\beta}.

(20)

Gauge bosons with a static metric:

In this case

g^{00}T_{00}^{1}=-{1\over 2}F^{0j}_{a}F^{a}_{0j}+{1\over 4}F_{jk}^{a}F_{a}^{jk},

(21)

and therefore

\sqrt{g}\,g^{00}\,T_{00}^{1}+{\cal L}^{1}=-\sqrt{g}\,F^{0j}_{a}F_{0j}^{a}.

(22)

II.2
Gauge-fixing

Gauge-fixing with general metric:

The Lagrange density

\displaystyle{\cal L}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\lambda\over 2}\sqrt{g}\,[(A^{\mu}_{a})_{;\mu}]^{2}

(23)

\displaystyle(A^{\mu}_{a})_{;\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over\sqrt{g}}\partial_{\mu}(\sqrt{g}\,g^{\mu\nu}A_{\nu}^{a})

(24)

results in BRST invariance
Becchi
;
Tyutin
.
Variation of the action with respect to the metric
gives for the energy-momentum tensor

T_{\mu\nu}^{2}=\lambda\Big[-A_{\mu}^{a}\partial_{\nu}W_{a}-A_{\nu}^{a}\partial_{\mu}W_{a}+g_{\mu\nu}[{1\over 2}(W^{a})^{2}+A_{a}^{\beta}\partial_{\beta}W_{a}]\Big].

(25)

where for conciseness

W_{a}\equiv(A^{\alpha}_{a})_{;\alpha}={1\over\sqrt{g}}\partial_{\alpha}[\sqrt{g}\,g^{\alpha\beta}A_{\beta}^{a}].

(26)

Gauge-fixing with a static metric:

Equation (

) with
\mu=\nu=0
leads to

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\sqrt{g}g^{00}\,T_{00}^{2}+{\cal L}^{2}

(27)

\displaystyle=\lambda\sqrt{g}\,[-2A^{0}_{a}\;\partial_{0}W_{a}+(W_{a})^{2}+A^{\beta}_{a}\,\partial_{\beta}W_{a}].

In the term

if one factor of

is expressed in terms of derivatives then a spatial integration

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt\int d^{3}x[\sqrt{g}g^{00}\,T_{00}^{2}+{\cal L}^{2}]

(28)

\displaystyle=\lambda\int d^{3}x\sqrt{g}\,[(\partial_{0}A^{0}_{a})W_{a}-A^{0}_{a}\,\partial_{0}W_{a}].

II.3
Ghost fields

Ghosts with general time-dependent metric:

The Lagrangian density for the ghost fields is

{\cal L}^{3}=\sqrt{g}\,g^{\mu\nu}(\partial_{\mu}\overline{\eta}_{a})(D_{\nu}\eta)_{a}

(29)

where
\overline{\eta}_{a}
and
\eta_{a}
obey Fermi statistics, transform in the adjoint representation,
are not conjugates of each other, and

(D_{\nu}\eta)_{a}=\partial_{\nu}\eta_{a}-ef_{abc}A^{b}_{\nu}\eta_{c}.

(30)

Varying the action with respect to the metric gives

\displaystyle T_{\mu\nu}^{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\partial_{\mu}\overline{\eta}_{a})(D_{\nu}\eta)_{a}+(\partial_{\nu}\overline{\eta}_{a})(D_{\mu}\eta)_{a}

(31)

\displaystyle-g_{\mu\nu}\,g^{\alpha\beta}(\partial_{\alpha}\overline{\eta}_{a})(D_{\beta}\eta)_{a}.

Ghosts with static metric:

Equation (

g^{00}\,T_{00}^{3}=g^{00}(\partial_{0}\overline{\eta}_{a})(D_{0}\eta)_{a}-g^{jk}(\partial_{j}\overline{\eta}_{a})(D_{k}\eta)_{a}

which leads to

\sqrt{g}\,g^{00}\,T_{00}^{3}+{\cal L}^{3}=2\sqrt{g}\,g^{00}(\partial_{0}\overline{\eta}_{a})(D_{0}\eta)_{a}.

(33)

II.5
Scalar bosons

Scalars with general metric:

For a set of real scalar fields
\phi_{i}

the Lagrangian density is

{\cal L}^{5}=\sqrt{g}\Big[{g^{\mu\nu}\over 2}(D_{\mu}\phi)_{i}(D_{\nu}\phi)_{i}-U(\phi)-{1\over 2}\xi R\phi_{i}^{2}\Big],

(46)

where the gauge covariant derivative is

(D_{\mu}\phi)_{i}=\partial_{\mu}\phi_{i}-ieA_{\mu}^{a}(t^{a})_{ij}\phi_{j}

(47)

with
t^{a}
imaginary and antisymmetric,
U(\phi)
is a polynomial in the fields invariant under local gauge transformations,

R
is the Ricci scalar, and
\xi
is an arbitrary parameter.

The presence of the Ricci scalar
R
causes some complications in varying the action with respect to the metric tensor.
It is convenient to separate the calculation into two parts:

\delta\int d^{4}x\,{\cal L}^{5}={1\over 2}\int d^{4}x\sqrt{g}\,\big[\,T_{\mu\nu}^{5A}\delta g^{\mu\nu}-\xi(\delta R)\phi_{i}^{2}\big],

(48)

where
T^{5A}_{\mu\nu}
results from varying everything except

:

\displaystyle T_{\mu\nu}^{5A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(D_{\mu}\phi)_{i}(D_{\nu}\phi)_{i}

(49)

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt-g_{\mu\nu}\Big[{1\over 2}g^{\alpha\beta}(D_{\alpha}\phi)_{i}(D_{\beta}\phi)_{i}-U(\phi)-{1\over 2}\xi R\phi_{i}^{2}\Big].

The variation of

required in (
48
) is available in Sec 10.9 of

\delta R=R_{\mu\nu}\delta g^{\mu\nu}-(\delta g^{\mu\nu})_{;\mu;\nu}+g_{\mu\nu}(\delta g^{\mu\nu})^{;\rho}_{\;\;;\rho}.

(50)

The result of the variation is

-{1\over 2}\xi\int d^{4}x\sqrt{g}\,(\delta R)\phi_{i}^{2}={1\over 2}\int d^{4}x\sqrt{g}\,T_{\mu\nu}^{5B}\,\delta g^{\mu\nu},

(51)

where

T_{\mu\nu}^{5B}=\xi\Big[-R_{\mu\nu}\phi_{i}^{2}+(\phi_{i}^{2})_{;\mu;\nu}-g_{\mu\nu}(\phi_{i}^{2})^{;\rho}_{\;\;;\rho}\Big].

(52)

In summary

{\delta\over\delta g^{\mu\nu}}\int d^{4}x\,{\cal L}^{5}={1\over 2}\sqrt{g}\Big[T_{\mu\nu}^{5A}+T_{\mu\nu}^{5B}\Big]

(53)

Comment:

T_{\mu\nu}^{5B}
has the property that its
covariant divergence is

(g^{\mu\lambda}T^{5B}_{\lambda\nu})_{;\mu}=-{1\over 2}\xi(\partial_{\nu}R)\phi_{i}^{2}

(54)

with no derivatives of the fields.

Scalars with static metric:

Eq. (
49
) with
\mu=\nu=0
and a static metric gives

\sqrt{g}\,g^{00}\,T_{00}^{5A}+{\cal L}^{5}=\sqrt{g}\,g^{00}\,(D_{0}\phi)_{i}(D_{0}\phi)_{i}.

(55)

For a static metric (

) is a true conservation law,
\partial_{\mu}[\sqrt{g}\,g^{00}T^{5B}_{00}]=0

and so

{d\over dt}\int d^{3}x\sqrt{g}\,g^{00}\,T_{00}^{5B}=0.

(56)

Since the spatial integral is constant and the fields have arbitrary spacetime dependence, it is natural to expect that

confirms this:

\int d^{3}x\sqrt{g}\,g^{00}T_{00}^{5B}=0.

(57)

This and (

\displaystyle\int d^{3}x[\sqrt{g}\,g^{00}\,(T_{00}^{5A}+T_{00}^{5B})+{\cal L}^{5}]

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt=\int d^{3}x\sqrt{g}\,g^{00}\,(D_{0}\phi)_{i}(D_{0}\phi)_{i}.

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt\int d^{3}x[\sqrt{g}\,T^{0}_{\;\;0}+{\cal L}]=\int d^{3}x\sqrt{g}\Big\{\!-\!F^{0j}_{a}\partial_{0}A_{j}^{a}

\displaystyle+g^{00}[\lambda(\partial_{0}A_{0}^{a})W^{a}+(\partial_{0}\overline{\eta}_{a})(\partial_{0}\eta_{a})+(\partial_{0}\overline{\eta}_{a})(D_{0}\eta)_{a}

\displaystyle+{i\over 2}\big(\psi^{\dagger}h\gamma_{0}\partial_{0}\psi-(\partial_{0}\psi)^{\dagger}h\gamma_{0}\psi\big)

(66)

\displaystyle+(D_{0}\phi)_{i}(\partial_{0}\phi_{i})\Big\}.

III.2
Canonical momenta

There are seven canonical momenta

\displaystyle\pi^{j}_{a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\partial{\cal L}\over\partial(\partial_{0}A_{j}^{a})}=\sqrt{g}\,F^{j0}

\displaystyle\pi_{a}^{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\partial{\cal L}\over\partial(\partial_{0}A_{0}^{a})}=\lambda\sqrt{g}\,g^{00}\,W_{a}

\displaystyle p_{a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\partial{\cal L}\over\partial(\partial_{0}\eta_{a})}=\sqrt{g}\,g^{00}\,\partial_{0}\overline{\eta}_{a}

\displaystyle\overline{p}_{a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\partial{\cal L}\over\partial(\partial_{0}\overline{\eta}_{a})}=\sqrt{g}\,g^{00}\,(D_{0}\eta)_{a}

(67)

\displaystyle\pi_{\psi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\partial{\cal L}\over\partial(\partial_{0}\psi)}=i{\sqrt{g}\over 2}\psi^{\dagger}h\gamma^{0}

\displaystyle\pi_{\psi^{\dagger}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\partial{\cal L}\over\partial(\partial_{0}\psi^{\dagger})}=-i{\sqrt{g}\over 2}h\gamma^{0}\psi

\displaystyle\pi^{\phi}_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\partial{\cal L}\over\partial(\partial_{0}\phi_{i})}=\sqrt{g}\,g^{00}\,(D_{0}\phi)_{i}\,.

The rather cumbersome equation (
66
) is more recognizable when expressed in terms of the canonical momenta:

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt\int d^{3}x[\sqrt{g}\,T^{0}_{\;\;0}+{\cal L}]=\int d^{3}x\Big\{\pi^{j}_{a}\,\partial_{0}A_{j}^{a}+\pi_{a}^{0}\,\partial_{0}A_{0}^{a}

\displaystyle\hskip 42.67912pt+p_{a}\partial_{0}\eta_{a}+(\partial_{0}\overline{\eta}_{a})\overline{p}_{a}

\displaystyle\hskip 42.67912pt+\pi_{\psi}\partial_{0}\psi+(\partial_{0}\psi^{\dagger})\pi_{\psi^{\dagger}}+\pi^{\phi}_{i}\partial_{0}\phi_{i}\Big\}.

(68)

Of the seven terms involving the canonical momenta the fourth and the sixth have the canonical momenta on the right, as they should be.
Eq. (
68
) may be summarized as

\int d^{3}x\sqrt{g}\,T^{0}_{\;\;0}=\int d^{3}x\bigg[\sum_{s}\Pi_{s}\partial_{0}\chi_{s}-{\cal L}\bigg].

(69)

The right hand side is the Legendre transform of the
\partial_{0}\chi_{s}
dependence of the Lagrangian
to the
\Pi_{s}
dependence of the canonical Hamiltonian. This proves the equality

\int d^{3}x\sqrt{g}\,T^{0}_{\;\;0}=\int d^{3}x\sqrt{g}\,\Theta^{0}_{\;\;0}

(70)

using Gauss’s law but not the dynamical Euler-Lagrange equations.

It is perhaps worth noting that the constraint
[D_{j}(\sqrt{g}\,F^{0j}]_{a}=\sqrt{g}J^{0}_{a}
may be written in
terms of the canonical momenta

\displaystyle[D_{j}\pi^{j}]_{a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi^{0}_{a}\,\partial_{0}W_{a}+ef_{abc}p_{b}\eta_{c}

(71)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle ie\pi_{\psi}T^{a}\psi-ie\,\pi_{i}^{\phi}t^{a}_{ij}\phi_{j}

and this relation is independent of the metric.

As mentioned in the Introduction, Appendix
D
contains another test of the equality with a Lagrangian density
that contains the Ricci tensor
R^{\mu\nu}
.

IV
General argument

This section investigates a more general context in which the form of the Lagrangian density
is not specified, except that it has only first derivatives of the fields.
The equality of the two Hamiltonians for a static background metric is again demonstrated.

Divergence of the canonical EMT:

The divergence of the canonical tensor (
3
) is

\partial_{\mu}\big(\sqrt{g}\,\Theta^{\mu}_{\;\;\nu}\big)=\sum_{s}\partial_{\mu}\Big[{\partial{\cal L}\over\partial(\partial_{\mu}\chi_{s})}\partial_{\nu}\chi_{s}\Big]-\partial_{\nu}{\cal L}.

(72)

The spacetime dependence of
{\cal L}
occurs in both the fields and the metric:

\partial_{\nu}{\cal L}=\sum_{s}\Big[{\partial{\cal L}\over\partial\chi_{s}}(\partial_{\nu}\chi_{s})+{\partial{\cal L}\over\partial(\partial_{\mu}\chi_{s})}\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}\chi_{s}\Big]-\partial_{\nu}{\cal L}\big|_{\chi}.

(73)

The last term requires differentiating the spacetime dependence of the metric while keeping all the fields
\chi_{s}
fixed.
Substitution above gives

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}\big(\sqrt{g}\,\Theta^{\mu}_{\;\;\nu}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{s}{\cal M}_{s}(\partial_{\nu}\chi_{s})-\partial_{\nu}{\cal L}\big|_{\chi}

(74)

\displaystyle{\cal M}_{s}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}\Big({\partial{\cal L}\over\partial(\partial_{\mu}\chi_{s})}\Big)-{\partial{\cal L}\over\partial\chi_{s}}.

(75)

{\cal M}_{s}
vanishes if the Euler-Lagrange equations are imposed but even then
\partial_{\mu}(\sqrt{g}\,\Theta^{\mu}_{\;\;\nu})\neq 0

for a general metric.

Divergence of the Hilbert EMT:

The covariant divergence of
T^{\mu}_{\;\;\nu}
is computed from the fact that the action is invariant under a coordinate
transformation
x^{\nu}\to x^{\prime\nu}
; see Sec 94 of
Landau
or Sec 12.3 of
Weinberg1
. The metric and the fields (scalar, ghost, spin 1/2 fermion, and gauge) transform as follows:

\displaystyle g^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}(x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\partial x^{\alpha}\over\partial x^{\prime\mu}}{\partial x^{\beta}\over\partial x^{\prime\nu}}\,g_{\alpha\beta}(x)

(76)

\displaystyle\phi_{i}^{\prime}(x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi_{i}(x)

\displaystyle\eta_{a}^{\prime}(x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta_{a}(x)

\displaystyle\psi^{\prime}(x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\psi(x)

(77)

\displaystyle A_{\alpha}^{\prime a}(x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\partial x^{\lambda}\over\partial x^{\prime\alpha}}\,A_{\lambda}^{a}(x).

(The covariant vierbein transforms the same as the covariant gauge field but it will not be needed.) Invariance of the action means that

\int d^{4}x^{\prime}\,{\cal L}(g_{\mu\nu}^{\prime}(x^{\prime}),\chi^{\prime}(x^{\prime}))=\int d^{4}x\,{\cal L}(g_{\mu\nu}(x),\chi(x)).

Relabeling of the integration variable gives

\int d^{4}x\,{\cal L}(g_{\mu\nu}^{\prime}(x),\chi^{\prime}(x))=\int d^{4}x\,{\cal L}(g_{\mu\nu}(x),\chi(x)).

The action is invariant under a change in the functional form of the metric and the fields at the same position
x
:

\displaystyle\Delta g_{\mu\nu}(x)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle g^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}(x)-g_{\mu\nu}(x)

(78)

\displaystyle\Delta\chi_{s}(x)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\chi^{\prime}_{s}(x)-\chi_{s}(x).

(79)

Let

x^{\prime\nu}=x^{\nu}+\xi^{\nu}(x)

(80)

where
\xi^{\nu}(x)
is an arbitrary, infinitesimal function. The change in the metric is

\Delta g_{\mu\nu}=-(\xi_{\mu})_{;\nu}-(\xi_{\nu})_{;\mu}.

(81)

For the scalars, ghosts, and spin 1/2 fermions

\Delta\chi_{s}=-\xi^{\nu}\partial_{\nu}\chi_{s};

(82)

for the vector potential there is an additional term

\Delta A_{\alpha}^{a}=-\xi^{\nu}\partial_{\nu}A_{\alpha}^{a}-(\partial_{\alpha}\xi^{\nu})A_{\nu}^{a}.

(83)

Under these variations the action is invariant. The variation with respect to covariant metric gives the contravariant energy-momentum tensor
but with the opposite sign from (
1
):

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!-\!\int d^{4}x{\sqrt{g}\over 2}\,T^{\mu\nu}\Delta g_{\mu\nu}+\sum_{s}\int d^{4}x\,{\cal M}_{s}\,\xi^{\nu}\partial_{\nu}\chi_{s}

(84)

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\int d^{4}x\,{\cal M}^{\mu}_{a}(\partial_{\mu}\xi^{\nu})A_{\nu}^{a}.

In the second term the sum on fields
s
includes a term for the gauge potential; in the third term the explicit form is

\displaystyle{\cal M}_{a}^{\mu}=\partial_{\lambda}\Big({\partial{\cal L}\over\partial(\partial_{\lambda}A_{\mu}^{a})}\Big)-{\partial{\cal L}\over\partial A_{\mu}^{a}}.

(85)

An integration by parts in the first and third term yields

\displaystyle\int d^{4}x\sqrt{g}\,(T^{\mu\nu})_{:\mu}\xi_{\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{s}\int d^{4}x\,{\cal M}_{s}\,\xi^{\nu}\partial_{\nu}\chi_{s}

(86)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\int d^{4}x\,\partial_{\mu}({\cal M}_{a}^{\mu}A_{\nu}^{a})\xi^{\nu}.

Since
\xi^{\nu}(x)
is an arbitrary function, the integrands must be equal:

\sqrt{g}\,(T^{\mu}_{\;\;\nu})_{;\mu}=\sum_{s}{\cal M}_{s}(\partial_{\nu}\chi_{s})-\partial_{\mu}({\cal M}_{a}^{\mu}A_{\nu}^{a}).

(87)

The first term in (
85
) is
-\partial_{\mu}(\sqrt{g}\,F^{\mu\alpha})_{a}
which combines with
\partial{\cal L}^{1}/\partial A_{\alpha}^{a}

to give the gauge covariant derivative
-[D_{\mu}(\sqrt{g}\,F^{\mu\alpha})]_{a}
. The remaining terms from the gauge fixing, ghosts, spin 1/2 fermions,
and scalars define the current density

\sqrt{g}\,J^{\alpha}_{a}=-\lambda\partial_{\nu}(\sqrt{g}\,g^{\nu\alpha}W_{a})+\sum_{n=3}^{5}{\partial{\cal L}^{n}\over\partial A_{\alpha}^{a}}

(88)

and so

{\cal M}_{a}^{\alpha}=[D_{\mu}(\sqrt{g}\,F^{\mu\alpha})]_{a}+\sqrt{g}\,J^{\alpha}_{a}.

(89)

The constraint of Gauss’s law requires

{\cal M}^{0}_{a}=0.

(90)

Therefore the last term in (
87
)
is really only a spatial divergence
\partial_{j}({\cal M}_{a}^{j}A_{\nu}^{a})
though the form
\partial_{\mu}(M^{\mu}_{a}A_{\nu}^{a})
will
at times be used below.

Comparison of divergences of the Hilbert EMT and the canonical EMT.

The terms of the form
{\cal M}_{s}(\partial_{\nu}\chi_{s})
in (
87
) also appear in the canonical divergence (
74
)
and so (
87
) can be expressed as

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}(\sqrt{g}\,T^{\mu}_{\;\;\nu})-{\sqrt{g}\over 2}(\partial_{\nu}g_{\alpha\beta})T^{\alpha\beta}

(91)

\displaystyle\hskip 42.67912pt=\partial_{\mu}(\sqrt{g}\,\Theta^{\mu}_{\;\;\nu})+\partial_{\nu}{\cal L}\big|_{\chi}-\partial_{\mu}({\cal M}_{a}^{\mu}A_{\nu}^{a}).

This holds for a general metric and arbitrary fields using Gauss’s law but not the dynamical Euler-Lagrange equations.

For simplicity suppose
{\cal L}
has only first and second derivatives of the metric, as is the case for the
general gauge theory in Secs. II and III.
The second and fourth terms of Eq. (
91
) require the derivatives

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046pt-{\sqrt{g}\over 2}T^{\alpha\beta}={\partial{\cal L}\over\partial g_{\alpha\beta}}-\partial_{\mu}\bigg[{\partial{\cal L}\over\partial g_{\alpha\beta,\mu}}\bigg]+\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\rho}\bigg[{\partial{\cal L}\over\partial g_{\alpha\beta,\mu\rho}}\bigg]

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046pt\partial_{\nu}{\cal L}\Big|_{\chi}={\partial{\cal L}\over\partial g_{\alpha\beta}}g_{\alpha\beta,\nu}+{\partial{\cal L}\over\partial g_{\alpha\beta,\mu}}g_{\alpha\beta,\nu\mu}+{\partial{\cal L}\over\partial g_{\alpha\beta,\mu\rho}}g_{\alpha\beta,\nu\mu\rho}.

The difference between these two is a total derivative; Eq. (
91
) becomes

\partial_{\mu}(\sqrt{g}\,T^{\mu}_{\;\;\nu})=\partial_{\mu}(\sqrt{g}\,\Theta^{\mu}_{\;\;\nu})+\partial_{\mu}\Sigma^{\mu}_{\;\;\nu}-\partial_{\mu}(A_{\nu}^{a}{\cal M}^{\mu}_{a})

(92)

where

\Sigma^{\mu}_{\;\;\nu}\equiv{\partial{\cal L}\over\partial g_{\alpha\beta,\mu}}g_{\alpha\beta,\nu}\!+\!{\partial{\cal L}\over\partial g_{\alpha\beta,\mu\rho}}g_{\alpha\beta,\nu\rho}\!-\!\partial_{\rho}\Big({\partial{\cal L}\over\partial g_{\alpha\beta,\mu\rho}}\Big)g_{\alpha\beta,\nu}.

The derivatives of the metric are not tensors and so
\Sigma^{\mu}_{\;\;\nu}
is not a tensor.
(If
{\cal L}
has any number of metric derivatives of the metric the form (
92
) still holds but
\Sigma^{\mu}_{\;\;\nu}
is more complicated.)
Because of Gauss’s law (
90
) the spatial integral of (
92
) is

{d\over dt}\int d^{3}x\sqrt{g}\,T^{0}_{\;\;\nu}={d\over dt}\int d^{3}x\,\Big[\sqrt{g}\,\Theta^{0}_{\;\;\nu}+\Sigma^{0}_{\;\;\nu}\Big].

(93)

Since the time dependence of the fields and the metric is arbitrary the integrals must be equal:

\int d^{3}x\sqrt{g}\,T^{0}_{\;\;\nu}=\int d^{3}x\Big[\sqrt{g}\,\Theta^{0}_{\;\;\nu}+\Sigma^{0}_{\;\;\nu}\Big].

(94)

Case 1: Minkowski metric.

If the metric is chosen to be

(\eta_{\alpha\beta})={\rm diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)
, then
\Sigma^{\mu}_{\;\;\nu}=0
in (
94
). The resulting equality
holds for arbitrary fields and thus is more general than the usual result (
9
).

Case 2: Arbitrary time-dependent metric but
{\cal L}
has no derivatives of the metric.

These conditions are satisfied by the gauge boson
{\cal L}^{1}
and by the scalar boson
{\cal L}^{5}
if the

\xi R\phi_{i}^{2}
term is omitted. Obviously
\Sigma^{\mu}_{\;\;\nu}=0
.

Case 3: Static metric.

This includes the case of principle interest since
{\cal L}
for a general gauge theory contains first derivatives of the metric in the gauge-fixing term
and the spin connection
\Gamma_{\mu}
for fermions, and second derivatives of the metric in the
\xi R\phi_{i}^{2}
term.
For a static metric
\Sigma^{0}_{\;\;0}=0
and so

\int d^{3}x\sqrt{g}\,T^{0}_{\;\;0}=\int d^{3}x\sqrt{g}\,\Theta^{0}_{\;\;0}.

(95)

The dynamical field equations have not been used.
This agrees with the explicit calculations leading to (
70
) and explains the miraculous cancellations
for the example considered in Appendix
D
.

\Gamma_{00}^{0}=\Gamma_{k0}^{j}=\Gamma_{k\ell}^{0}=0.

\displaystyle=

(97)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over 2}g^{00}\partial_{k}g_{00}.

(98)

\Gamma^{j}_{k\ell}
is non-vanishing and independent of
g_{00}
. Two useful contractions are

\displaystyle\Gamma^{j}_{jk}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over\sqrt{\gamma}}\partial_{k}\sqrt{\gamma}

(99)

\displaystyle g^{jk}\Gamma^{\ell}_{jk}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-{1\over\sqrt{\gamma}}\partial_{j}(\sqrt{\gamma}\,g^{j\ell})

(100)

.

.

(101)

(102)

where

(103)

{}^{(4)}R_{0k}=0
.
The Ricci scalar is

{}^{(4)}R=2g^{00}g^{jk}R_{0j0k}+\,^{(3)}R

(104)

Appendix C
Proof that

The “extra” piece (
52

(116)

where
\Psi=\xi\phi_{i}^{2}
.

-{1\over 2}\xi\sqrt{g}\,(\delta R)\phi_{i}^{2}

-{1\over 2}\int d^{4}x\sqrt{g}\,\Psi\,R

(117)

where
\Psi

\sqrt{g}\,X^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}R

\sqrt{g}\,Y^{\mu\nu}R_{;\mu;\nu}

T_{00}^{5B}

\mu=\nu=0

\displaystyle\sqrt{g}\,g^{00}\,T_{00}^{5B}

\displaystyle A^{0}_{\;\;0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-{\sqrt{g}\over g_{00}}R_{00}\Psi

(118)

\displaystyle B^{0}_{\;\;0}

\displaystyle=

Appendix D
A complicated example

This appendix presents the details of the example mentioned in Sec. I in which
{\cal L}
depends on the Ricci curvature tensor
R^{\mu\nu}
. Despite the
complications the result is again that the Hilbert Hamiltonian is equal to the canonical Hamiltonian for a static metric even without using the field equation.
The Lagrangian density is

{\cal L}=\sqrt{g}\,R^{\mu\nu}(\partial_{\mu}\phi)(\partial_{\nu}\phi)

(124)

and could be added to the conventional Lagrangian density for a scalar field if multiplied by
a coefficient of mass dimension
M^{-2}
.
The question is not whether this
{\cal L}
is a physically acceptable addition but whether the resulting Hamiltonians, Hilbert and canonical, are equal.
The canonical energy-momentum pseudo-tensor (
3
) is

\sqrt{g}\,\Theta^{\mu}_{\;\;\nu}=2\sqrt{g}\,R^{\mu\alpha}(\partial_{\alpha}\phi)(\partial_{\nu}\phi)-\delta^{\mu}_{\;\;\nu}{\cal L}.

(125)

For a static background metric

\Theta^{0}_{\;\ 0}=R^{00}\partial_{0}\phi\,\partial_{0}\phi-R^{jk}\partial_{j}\phi\,\partial_{k}\phi.

(126)

The field equations have not been used.

Hilbert
T_{\mu\nu}
for a general metric:

The Hilbert energy-momentum tensor for a general metric is more complicated to compute. It is convenient to organize
{\cal L}
as

{\cal L}=\sqrt{g}\,R_{\alpha\beta}\,g^{\alpha\mu}g^{\beta\nu}\,\Phi_{\mu\nu},

On various notions of distance between subalgebras of operator algebras Thanks: The second named author was supported by the Council of Scientific and Industrial Research (Government of India) through a Senior Research Fellowship with CSIR File No. 09/0263(12012)/2021-EMR-I
Ved Prakash Gupta, Sumit Kumar

Abstract
. Given any irreducible inclusion ℬ ⊂ 𝒜 \mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A} of unital C ∗ C^{*} -algebras with a finite-index conditional expectation E : 𝒜 → ℬ E:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B} , we show that the set of E E -compatible intermediate C ∗ C^{*} -subalgebras is finite, thereby generalizing a finiteness result of Ino and Watatani (from [ 11 ] ). A finiteness result for a certain collection of intermediate C ∗ C^{*} -subalgebras of a non-irreducible inclusion of simple unital C ∗ C^{*} -algebras is also obtained, which provides a C ∗ C^{*} -version of a finiteness result of Khoshkam and Mashood (from [ 18 ] ). Apart from these finiteness results, comparisons between various notions of distance between subalgebras of operator algebras by Kadison-Kastler, Christensen and Mashood-Taylor are made. Further, these comparisons are used satisfactorily to provide some concrete calculations of distance between operator algebras associated to two distinct subgroups of a given discrete group.

We briefly recall the notions of finite-index conditional

2.1.
Watatani’s
C^{*}
-basic construction

For any inclusion
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A}
of unital
C^{*}
-algebras with common
unit and a faithful conditional expectation
E:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
,
\mathcal{A}
becomes
a pre-Hilbert
\mathcal{B}
-module with respect to the
\mathcal{B}
-valued inner product

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{B}}:\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
given by
\langle x,y\rangle_{\mathcal{B}}=E(x^{*}y)
for
x,y\in\mathcal{A}
. We denote by
\mathcal{E}

the Hilbert
\mathcal{B}
-module completion of
\mathcal{A}
.

In order to distinguish the elements of the
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
and the
pre-Hilbert
\mathcal{B}
-module
\mathcal{A}
, following
[
24
]
, we consider the
inclusion map
\eta:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{E}
. Thus,

(2.1)

\|\eta(x)\|:=\|E(x^{*}x)\|^{1/2}\leq\|x\|

for all
x\in\mathcal{A}
. Let
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{E})
denote the unital

C^{*}
-algebra consisting of adjointable maps on
\mathcal{E}
. Every
member of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{E})
is a
\mathcal{B}
-module map (see
[
24
,
\lx@sectionsign 2.10
]
). Further, there is a natural
C^{*}
-embedding
\lambda:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{E})
satisfying

\lambda(a)\eta(x)=\eta(ax)\text{ for all }a,x\in\mathcal{A}.

Thus, we can identify
\mathcal{A}
with its image
\lambda(\mathcal{A})
in

\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{E})
. Further, there is a natural projection

e_{1}\in\lambda(\mathcal{B})^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{E})

(called the Jones projection corresponding to
E
) satisfying, via the
above identification, the relations

e_{1}(\eta(x))=\eta(E(x))\text{ and }e_{1}xe_{1}=E(x)e_{1}

for all
x\in\mathcal{A}
. The Watatani’s
C^{*}
-basic construction for the
inclusion
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A}
with respect to the conditional
expectation
E
is defined as the
C^{*}
-algebra

\mathcal{A}_{1}=\overline{\mathrm{span}}\{xe_{1}y:x,y\in\mathcal{A}\}\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{E}).

Like the ‘Jones’ basic construction for
a subfactor, Watatani’s basic construction also satisfies a natural
universal property - see
[
24
, Proposition 2.2.11]
.

Some standard notations:
Recall that for an inclusion
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A}
of
C^{*}
-algebras, the
centralizer of
\mathcal{B}
in
\mathcal{A}
is defined by

\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B})=\{x\in\mathcal{A}:bx=xb\text{ for all }b\in\mathcal{B}\};

and, if
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A}
are unital (with common unit), the normalizer of
\mathcal{B}
in
\mathcal{A}
is defined by

\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B})=\{u\in\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{A}):u\mathcal{B}u^{*}=\mathcal{B}\}.

The centralizer
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B})
is a
C^{*}
-subalgebra of
\mathcal{A}

and is also denoted by
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{A}
and called the relative
commutant of
\mathcal{B}
in
\mathcal{A}
. The normalizer
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B})

is a closed subgroup of
\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{A})

2.2.
Finite-index conditional expectations and index

For any inclusion
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A}
of unital
C^{*}
-algebras, a
conditional expectation
E:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
is said to have finite index
if there exists a finite set
\{\lambda_{i}:1\leq i\leq n\}\subset\mathcal{A}
such that
x=\sum_{i=1}^{n}E(x\lambda_{i})\lambda_{i}^{*}
for all

x\in\mathcal{A}
. Such a set
\{\lambda_{i}\}
is called a quasi-basis for

E
and the Watatani index of
E
is defined as
\mathrm{Ind}_{W}(E)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\lambda_{i}^{*}
, which is a positive invertible
element in
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{A})
and is independent of the quasi-basis

\{\lambda_{i}\}
. Every such
E
is faithful and preserves the unit, i.e.,
1_{\mathcal{B}}=E(1_{\mathcal{A}})=1_{\mathcal{A}}
.

The set of finite-index conditional expectations from
\mathcal{A}
onto
\mathcal{B}

is denoted by
\mathcal{E}_{0}(A,B)
. If
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{A})=\mathbb{C}
and
\mathcal{E}_{0}(A,B)\neq\emptyset
, then an
F\in\mathcal{E}_{0}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
is said to be minimal if

\mathrm{Ind}_{W}(F)=\inf\{\mathrm{Ind}_{W}(E):E\in\mathcal{E}_{0}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})\}.

In some nice cases, a minimal conditional expectation
exists and is unique as well.

.

Let
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A}
be an inclusion of

C^{*}
-algebras with
\mathcal{E}_{0}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})\neq\emptyset

\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{A})=\mathbb{C}=\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{B})

(1)

There exists a unique minimal conditional expectation (denoted usually by)

. (
[

(2)

The index of such an inclusion is defined as
(
[

)

(2.2)

[\mathcal{A}:\mathcal{B}]_{0}=\mathrm{Ind}_{W}(E_{0})=\min\{\mathrm{Ind}_{W}(E):E\in\mathcal{E}_{0}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}).

Compatible intermediate

-subalgebras

For any inclusion
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A}

Let

Lemma 2.3

Let

It is enough to prove for

. Hence,

(2): Now, suppose that

. Let

be

. Then,

and

, by

2.2
(6). Further, for every

, we have

3.
Kadison-Kastler distance

For any normed space
X
, as is standard, its closed unit ball will be
denoted by
B_{1}(X)
and for any subset
K
of
X
and an element
x\in X
, the distance between
x
and
K
is defined as

d(x,K)=\inf\{\|x-y\|:y\in K\}.

Definition 3.1
.

[
14
]

The Kadison-Kastler distance between any two subalgebras
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}

of a normed algebra
\mathcal{A}
(which we denote by
d_{KK}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D})
) is
defined as the Hausdorff distance between the closed unit balls of

\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
, i.e.,

d_{KK}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D})=\max\left\{\sup_{x\in B_{1}(\mathcal{C})}d(x,B_{1}(\mathcal{D})),\sup_{z\in B_{1}(\mathcal{D})}d(z,B_{1}(\mathcal{C}))\right\}.

The Kadison-Kastler distance makes sense even for two subspaces of a
normed space but we shall work mainly with the distance between
subalgebras of a normed algebra.

We must remark that the notation
d_{KK}
is not standard. We have
used it for the Kadison-Kastler distance in order to keep a
distinction between it and two other notions of distance introduced by
Christensen and a distance introduced by Mashood and
Taylor, which shall be discussed in
Section

5
.

Notations:
For a normed algebra
\mathcal{A}
, let

\mathrm{Sub}_{\mathcal{A}}:=\big\{\text{subalgebras of }\mathcal{A}\big\};

C\text{-}\mathrm{Sub}_{\mathcal{A}}:=\big\{\text{closed subalgebras of }\mathcal{A}\big\};

and, if
\mathcal{A}
is a
C^{*}
-algebra, then let

C^{*}\text{-}\mathrm{Sub}_{\mathcal{A}}:=\big\{C^{*}\text{-subalgebras of }\mathcal{A}\big\}.

Here are some well known elementary observations related to the
Kadison-Kastler distance.

Remark 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a normed algebra.

(1)

d_{KK}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D})\leq 1
for all
\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}\in\mathrm{Sub}_{\mathcal{A}}
.

(2)

If
\mathcal{A}
is a Banach algebra, then
d_{KK}
is a metric on

C\text{-}\mathrm{Sub}_{\mathcal{A}}
.

(3)

If
\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}\in C\text{-}\mathrm{Sub}_{\mathcal{A}}
and
\mathcal{C}
is a proper
subalgebra of
\mathcal{D}
, then
d_{KK}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D})=1
. (
[
11
, Lemma 2.1]
)

The following elementary observation is obvious and will be used to
calculate the distance between certain
C^{*}
-subalgebras in
Section

6
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a normed algebra. Then,

d_{KK}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D})=d_{KK}\big(\mathcal{C},\overline{\mathcal{D}}\big)=d_{KK}\big(\overline{\mathcal{C}},\mathcal{D}\big)=d_{KK}\big(\overline{\mathcal{C}},\overline{\mathcal{D}}\big)

for all
\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}\in\mathrm{Sub}_{\mathcal{A}}
.

It is natural to ask whether for a given subalgebra
\mathcal{B}
of a

C^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
, can one always find a subalgebra as close as one
desires. In
[
10
, Example, 2.2.2]
, it was shown that
d_{KK}(\mathcal{B},u\mathcal{B}u^{*})\leq\|u-\mathbf{1}\|
for all
u\in\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{A})
. Thus, because of
the following lemma, one can get a conjugate subalgebra as close as
one wishes. (Its proof follows from an elementary continuous
functional calculus argument and we leave it to the reader.)

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a unital
C^{*}
-algebra. Then, for each

\epsilon>0
, there exists a unitary
u
in
\mathcal{A}
such
that
0<\|u-\mathbf{1}\|<\epsilon
.

Further, for any inclusion
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A}
of unital

C^{*}
-algebras, it is also quite natural to ask whether for a unitary

u\in\mathcal{U}(A)
, there exists any relationship between its
distance from
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B})
and the Kadison-Kastler distance
between
\mathcal{B}
and its conjugate
u\mathcal{B}u^{*}
.

Interestingly, motivated by an inequality given by Popa-Sinclair-Smith
(
[
22
, Lemma 6.3]
), we obtain the following pleasing relationship
without much effort, which then has a nice consequence that if
E\in\mathcal{E}_{0}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
, then for any
\mathcal{C}\in\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A},E)

(see (
3.1
)), a unitary which normalizes
\mathcal{B}
and is
sufficiently close to
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{C})
must belong to
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{C})

- see
Proposition

3.6
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a unital
C^{*}
-algebra and
\mathcal{B}
be a unital

C^{*}
-subalgebra of
\mathcal{A}
. Then,

d_{KK}\big(\mathcal{B},u\mathcal{B}u^{*}\big)\leq 2d\big(u,\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B})\big)\leq 2\|u-\mathbf{1}\|

for all
u\in\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{A})
.

Proof.

Let
u\in\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{A})
. Clearly,
d\big(u,\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B})\big)\leq\|u-\mathbf{1}\|
.

Next, let
v\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B})
and
w:=uv^{*}
. Then,
w\mathcal{B}w^{*}=(uv^{*})\mathcal{B}(uv^{*})^{*}=u(v^{*}\mathcal{B}v)u^{*}=u\mathcal{B}u^{*}
. So, for any
x\in B_{1}(\mathcal{B})
,

\|x-wxw^{*}\|=\|xw-wx\|\leq\|xw-x\|+\|x-wx\|\leq 2\|w-\mathbf{1}\|.

This implies that
d\big(x,B_{1}(w\mathcal{B}w^{*})\big)\leq 2\|w-\mathbf{1}\|.
Hence,

\sup_{x\in B_{1}(\mathcal{B})}d\Big(x,B_{1}(w\mathcal{B}w^{*})\Big)\leq 2\|w-\mathbf{1}\|.

Likewise,

\sup_{y\in B_{1}(w\mathcal{B}w^{*})}d\big(y,B_{1}(\mathcal{B})\big)\leq 2\|w-\mathbf{1}\|.

So,

d_{KK}\big(\mathcal{B},w\mathcal{B}w^{*}\big)\leq 2\|w-\mathbf{1}\|.

Thus,
d_{KK}\big(\mathcal{B},w\mathcal{B}w^{*}\big)\leq 2\|u-v\|
for all
v\in N_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B})
. Hence,

d_{KK}\big(\mathcal{B},u\mathcal{B}u^{*}\big)=d_{KK}\big(\mathcal{B},w\mathcal{B}w^{*}\big)\leq 2d(u,N_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B})).

∎

In the reverse direction, Kadison and Kastler (in
[
14
]
) had
conjectured that sufficiently close subalgebras must be conjugates of
each other. This was answered in the affirmative for various cases in
a series of some fundamental papers by Christensen, Phillips, Raeburn
and others in the decade of 70s. Since then there have been
several other such so-called “perturbation results”. People have
also employed such perturbation results to answer other important
questions. One such result with a nice application is due to Ino and
Watatani (from
[
11
]
). In fact, some of the results in this article are
direct applications of the perturbation result of Ino and Watatani.

Consider the following question, which arises naturally from

Lemma

3.5
:

Question: Given a unital
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
and a

C^{*}
-subalgebra
\mathcal{B}
, is every unitary sufficiently close to
\mathbf{1}

(or to
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B})
) in the normalizer of the subalgebra

\mathcal{B}
?

It is easily seen - see, for instance,
Example

6.17
- that it has
a negative answer. However, interestingly,
Lemma

3.5
along
with a perturbation result of Ino and Watatani (
[
11
, Proposition
3.6]
) immediately yields a somewhat positive answer for
compatible intermediate
C^{*}
-subalgebras - see

Proposition

3.6
and
3.7
below.

Notation:
Given an inclusion
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A}
of
unital
C^{*}
-algebras with a finite-index conditional expectation
E:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
, let

(3.1)

\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A},E):=\big\{\mathcal{C}\in\mathrm{IMS}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A},E):\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B})\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{C})\cup\mathcal{C}\big\}.

Note that
\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A},E)=\mathrm{IMS}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A},E)
if

\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B})\subseteq\mathcal{B}
.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A}
be an inclusion of unital
C^{*}
-algebras with a
finite-index conditional expectation
E:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
. Then, there
exists a constant
\alpha>0
such that

\big\{u\in{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B}):d(u,\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{C}))<\alpha\big\}\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{C})
for every
\mathcal{C}\in\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A},E)
.

In particular, if
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B})\subseteq\mathcal{B}
, then

\big\{u\in{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B}):d(u,\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{C}))<\alpha\big\}\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{C})
for every
\mathcal{C}\in\mathrm{IMS}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A},E)
.

Proof.

Let
N
be the number of elements (which is not unique) in a
quasi-basis for
E
and let
\alpha=\frac{0.5}{(10N)^{4}}
. First,
note that for any
\mathcal{C}\in\mathrm{IMS}(B,A,E)
, with respect to the compatible
finite-index conditional expectation
F:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{C}
, and any
u\in\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{A})
, there exists a faithful conditional expectation
F_{u}:\mathcal{A}\to u\mathcal{C}u^{*}
given by
F_{u}=\mathrm{Ad}_{u}\circ F\circ\mathrm{Ad}_{u^{*}}
.

Now, let
\mathcal{C}\in\mathcal{F}(B,A,E)
and
u\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B})
with

d(u,\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B}))<\alpha
. Then,
\mathcal{B}\subseteq u\mathcal{C}u^{*}\subseteq\mathcal{A}

and
d_{KK}(\mathcal{C},u\mathcal{C}u^{*})<\frac{1}{(10N)^{4}}
, by

Lemma

3.5
. Thus, by
[
11
, Proposition 3.6]
, there
exists a
v\in\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{A})
such that
v\mathcal{C}v^{*}=u\mathcal{C}u^{*}
. Since
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{A}\subseteq(\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{A})\cup\mathcal{C}
, it
follows that
v\mathcal{C}v^{*}=\mathcal{C}
. Hence,
u\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{C})
, and we
are done.
∎

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}
,
E
and
\alpha
be as in
Proposition

3.6
. Then,

\big\{u\in{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B}):\|u-\mathbf{1}\|<\alpha\big\}\subseteq\bigcap\Big\{\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{C}):\mathcal{C}\in\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A},E)\Big\}.

In particular, if
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B})\subseteq\mathcal{B}
, then

\big\{u\in{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B}):\|u-\mathbf{1}\|<\alpha\big\}\subseteq\bigcap\Big\{\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{C}):\mathcal{C}\in\mathrm{IMS}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A},E)\Big\}.

As an application of a perturbation result by Dickson (from
[
8
]
)
and a fundamental result regarding finiteness of the index of a
conditional expectation by Izumi (from
[
12
]
), we have a slightly more
general variant of
Proposition

3.6
for inclusions of
simple unital
C^{*}
-algebras.

Notation:
For any inclusion
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A}
of
unital
C^{*}
-algebras (with common unit), let

(3.2)

\mathcal{I}_{0}(\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A}):=\big\{\mathcal{C}\in\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A}):\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B})\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{C})\cup\mathcal{C}\big\}.

Clearly,
\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A},E)\subseteq\mathcal{I}_{0}(\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A})
for every
E\in\mathcal{E}_{0}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
; and,
\mathcal{I}_{0}(\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A})=\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A})
if

\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B})\subseteq\mathcal{B}
.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A}
be an inclusion of simple unital
C^{*}
-algebras
with a finite-index conditional expectation
E:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
. Then,
for any
0<\gamma<\frac{1}{10^{6}}
,

\big\{u\in{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B}):d(u,\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{C}))<\frac{\gamma}{2}\big\}\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{C})
for every
\mathcal{C}\in\mathcal{I}_{0}(\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A})
.

In particular, if
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B})\subseteq\mathcal{B}
, then

\big\{u\in{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B}):d(u,\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{C}))<\frac{\gamma}{2}\big\}\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{C})
for every
\mathcal{C}\in\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A})
.

Proof.

First, note that for any
\mathcal{C}\in\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A})
, by

[
12
, Proposition 6.1]
, there exists a conditional expectation
F:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}
of finite index. Hence, it is faithful.

Now, let
\mathcal{C}\in\mathcal{I}_{0}(\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A})
,
0<\gamma<\frac{1}{10^{6}}
and
u\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B})
with
d(u,\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{C}))<\frac{\gamma}{2}
. Then,
\mathcal{B}\subseteq u\mathcal{C}u^{*}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
and

d_{KK}(\mathcal{C},u\mathcal{C}u^{*})<\gamma<10^{-6}
, by

Lemma

3.5
. Note that, as
E
is of finite Watatani index, it
satisfies the Pimsner-Popa inequality; in particular, so does the
restriction
E_{\restriction u\mathcal{C}u^{*}}:u\mathcal{C}u^{*}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}
.
Since
\mathcal{B}
is simple, it follows from
[
12
, Corollary 3.4]
that

E_{\restriction u\mathcal{C}u^{*}}
also has a finite quasi-basis. Thus, by

[
8
, Theorem 3.7]
, there exists a
v\in\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{A})

such that
v\mathcal{C}v^{*}=u\mathcal{C}u^{*}
. Since
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{A}\subseteq(\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{A})\cup\mathcal{C}
, it follows that
v\mathcal{C}v^{*}=\mathcal{C}
. Hence,
u\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{C})
, and we are done.
∎

Corollary 3.9
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
,
\mathcal{B}
and
E
be as in
Proposition

3.8
. Then,
for any
0<\gamma<\frac{1}{10^{6}}
,

\Big\{u\in{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B}):\|u-\mathbf{1}\|<\frac{\gamma}{2}\Big\}\subseteq\bigcap\Big\{\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{C}):\mathcal{C}\in\mathcal{I}_{0}(\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A})\Big\}.

In particular, if
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B})\subseteq\mathcal{B}
, then

\Big\{u\in{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B}):\|u-\mathbf{1}\|<\frac{\gamma}{2}\Big\}\subseteq\bigcap\Big\{\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{C}):\mathcal{C}\in\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A})\Big\}.

For any group
G
, let
\text{Sub}_{G}
denote the collection of
subgroups of
G
. Then,
G
admits a canonical action on

\text{Sub}_{G}
via conjugation, i.e.,

G\times\text{Sub}_{G}\ni(g,H)\mapsto gHg^{-1}\in\text{Sub}_{G}.

Corollary 3.10
.

Let
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B},E
and
\alpha
be as in

Proposition

3.6
and let
G:=\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B})
. Then, with
respect to the natural conjuation action of
G
on
\mathrm{Sub}_{G}
,
the following hold:

(1)

The open ball

\{u\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B}):\|u-\mathbf{1}\|<\alpha\}\subseteq\bigcap\big\{\mathrm{Stab}_{G}\big(\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B})\cap\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{C})\big):\mathcal{C}\in\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A},E)\big\};

and, in particular, if
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B})\subseteq\mathcal{B}
, then

\{u\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B}):\|u-\mathbf{1}\|<\alpha\}\subseteq\bigcap\big\{\mathrm{Stab}_{G}\big(\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B})\cap\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{C})\big):\mathcal{C}\in\mathrm{IMS}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A},E)\big\}.

(2)

In addition, if
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
are both simple, then for any

0<\gamma<\frac{1}{10^{6}}
, the open ball

\Big\{u\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B}):\|u-\mathbf{1}\|<\frac{\gamma}{2}\Big\}\subseteq\bigcap\big\{\mathrm{Stab}_{G}\big(\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B})\cap\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{C})\big):\mathcal{C}\in\mathcal{I}_{0}(\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A})\big\};

and, in particular, if
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B})\subseteq\mathcal{B}
, then

\Big\{u\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B}):\|u-\mathbf{1}\|<\frac{\gamma}{2}\Big\}\subseteq\bigcap\big\{\mathrm{Stab}_{G}\big(\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B})\cap\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{C})\big):\mathcal{C}\in\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A})\big\}.

4.
Some finiteness results

This section is devoted to some more applications of certain
perturbation results from
[
11
]
and
[
8
]
to generalize
some finiteness results by Ino-Watatani (
[
11
]
) and
Khoshkam-Mashood (
[
18
]
).

4.1.
Finiteness of certain compatible intermediate
C^{*}
-subalgebras

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A}
be an inclusion of unital
C^{*}
-algebras with
a finite-index conditional expectation
E:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}
. If
one (equivalently, any) of the algebras
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B})
,

\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{B})
and
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{A})
is finite dimensional, then the
collection
\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A},E)
(as in (
3.1
)) is finite.

Proof.

First, note that, since there exists a finite-index conditional
expectation from
\mathcal{A}
onto
\mathcal{B}
, it follows from
[
24
, Proposition
2.7.3]
that the
C^{*}
-subalgebras
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B})
,

\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{B})
and
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{A})
are either all finite
dimensional or none of them is finite dimensional.

Consider the Watatani’s
C^{*}
-basic construction
\mathcal{A}_{1}:=C^{*}(\mathcal{A},e_{1})
of the inclusion
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A}
with respect to the
conditional expectation
E
and Jones projection
e_{1}
. In view of
the preceding paragraph and the given hypothesis,
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{B})
is finite
dimensional; so, by
[
24
, Proposition 2.7.3]
again, the
relative commutant
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{A}_{1}
is finite-dimensional. Therefore,
the set

\mathcal{P}:=\{p\in\mathcal{B}^{{}^{\prime}}\cap\mathcal{A}_{1}:p\mbox{\, is a projection }\}

is a compact Hausdorff space with respect to the operator norm.

Further, note that if
\mathcal{C}\in\mathrm{IMS}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A},E)
with respect to the
compatible conditional expectation
F:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{C}
, then
F
has
finite index and
\mathcal{C}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{1}
(see
[
9
, Prop. 2.7(2)]
; so, the
corresponding Jones projection
e_{\mathcal{C}}
belongs to
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{C}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{A}_{1}
, where

\mathcal{C}_{1}
denotes the
C^{*}
-basic construction of the inclusion
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathcal{A}
with respect to the finite-index conditional expectation

F
and Jones projection
e_{\mathcal{C}}
.

Fix a
0<\gamma<10^{-6}
and let
\varepsilon=\frac{\gamma}{2\,\|\mathrm{Ind}_{W}(E)\|}
. By the compactness of

\mathcal{P}
, there exists a finite cover of
\mathcal{P}
consisting of
open balls of radius
\varepsilon
. So, it suffices to show that each
such
\varepsilon
-ball contains only finitely many Jones projections
corresponding to the members of
\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A},E)
.

Note that, for any two
\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}\in\mathrm{IMS}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A},E)
, their corresponding
Jones projections
e_{\mathcal{C}}
and
e_{\mathcal{D}}
are in
\mathcal{B}^{{}^{\prime}}\cap\mathcal{A}_{1}
and
satisfy

d_{KK}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D})\leq\|\mathrm{Ind}_{W}(E)\|\|e_{\mathcal{C}}-e_{\mathcal{D}}\|,

by
[
11
, Lemma 3.3]
. In particular, if
e_{\mathcal{C}}
and
e_{\mathcal{D}}
are
in one of the
\varepsilon
-open balls, then
d_{KK}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D})\leq\frac{\gamma}{2}<\gamma
. Thus, by
[
8
, Theorem 3.7]
, there exists a unitary
u
in
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{A}

such that
\mathcal{C}=u\mathcal{D}u^{*}
(and
\|u-1\|\leq 16\sqrt{110}\gamma^{\frac{1}{2}}+880\gamma
).

Let
\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}\in\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A},E)
and they be in one

\varepsilon
-ball as above. As
u\in\mathcal{B}^{{}^{\prime}}\cap\mathcal{A}
and

\mathcal{B}^{{}^{\prime}}\cap\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{D})\cup\mathcal{D}
, it follows that

\mathcal{C}=u\mathcal{D}u^{*}=\mathcal{D}
. Thus, each
\varepsilon
-open ball of the cover
contains at most one Jones projection for some member of

\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A},E)
, as was desired.
∎

We can then immediately deduce the following generalization of

[
11
, Corollary 3.9]
. (The second part follows from

Lemma

2.3
.)

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A}
be an irreducible inclusion of unital

C^{*}
-algebras with a finite-index conditional expectation
E:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
. Then,
\mathrm{IMS}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A},E)
is finite.

In particular,
\mathrm{IMS}(\mathcal{A}_{k},\mathcal{A}_{k+1},E_{k+1})
is finite for every
k\geq 0
.

For any unital inclusion of von Neumann algebras
\mathcal{N}\subset\mathcal{M}
, let
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{N}\subset\mathcal{M})
denote the lattice
of intermediate von Neumann subalgebras. For any such inclusion, if
there exists a faithful normal tracial state
\mathrm{tr}
on
\mathcal{M}
and
the unique
\mathrm{tr}
-preserving conditional expectation
E_{\mathcal{N}}:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{N}
has finite Watatani index, then it was shown in

[
3
]
that if
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{N})
is finite-dimensional and the relative
commutant
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{M}
equals either
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{N})
or
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{M})
, then

\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{N}\subset\mathcal{M})
is finite. Analogous to
[
18
, Theorem
1.3]

4.1

Proposition 4.3

4.2.
Finiteness results for non-irreducible inclusions of simple
C^{*}
-algebras

In general, if
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A}
are simple unital
C^{*}
-algebras and
the inclusion is not irreducible, then the lattice
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A})
need not be finite. For instance, consider the following
easy example:

Example 4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=M_{2}(\mathbb{C})
,
\mathcal{B}=\mathbb{C}I_{2}
,
\Delta=\{\mathrm{diag}(\lambda,\mu)\,\,:\,\,\lambda,\,\mu\,\in\mathbb{C}\}
,

E:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}
denote the canonical (tracial) conditional
expectation given by

E([a_{ij}])=\frac{(a_{11}+a_{22})}{2}I_{2},\ \ [a_{ij}]\in\mathcal{A};

and, let
F:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\Delta
denote another canonical conditional expectation given by

F([a_{ij}])=\mathrm{diag}(a_{11},a_{22})
,
[a_{ij}]\in\mathcal{A}
.

Note that
u\Delta u^{*}\in\mathrm{IMS}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A},E)
for all
u\in U(2)
(by
[
9
, Lemma
2.8]
). Also, the set
\{u\Delta u^{*}:u\in U(2)\setminus\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(\Delta)\}
is infinite because the set of left cosets
of
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(\Delta)
in
U(2)
is infinite. Hence,

\mathrm{IMS}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A},E)
and, therefore,
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A})

are infinite sets.

Here is an indirect way of seeing why
U(2)/\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(\Delta)
is
infinite. Suppose, on contrary, that
\{[u]:=u\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(\Delta):u\in U(2)\}
is finite. Then, for
any element
w\in u\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(\Delta)
,
w=uv
for some
v\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(\Delta)
; so,

\alpha(\Delta,w\Delta w^{*})=\alpha(\Delta,uv\Delta(uv)^{*})=\alpha(\Delta,u\Delta u^{*}),

where
\alpha
is the interior angle (see
[
9
]
). This implies that
\alpha(\Delta,w\Delta w^{*})=\alpha(\Delta,u\Delta u^{*})
for every
w\in[u]
. Hence, the set

\{\alpha(\Delta,u\Delta u^{*}):u\in U(2)\}=\left\{\alpha(\Delta,u\Delta u^{*})\,\,:\,\,[u]\in\frac{U(2)}{\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(\Delta)}\right\}

is finite. This contradicts the fact that
\{\alpha(\Delta,u\Delta u^{*}):u\in U(2)\}=[0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
(see
[
9
, Corollary
4.6]
). Thus, the set of left cosets of
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(\Delta)
in

U(2)
must be infinite.

However, for a non-irreducible inclusion
\mathcal{B}\subset A
of simple unital
C^{*}
-algebras with a finite-index conditional
expectation, we shall show in this section that the sublattice
consisting of intermediate
C^{*}
-subalgebras of
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A}
which
contain the centralizer algebra
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B})
is finite.

The following useful observation (whose first part comes from
[
12
]

and the second part is comparable with
[
9
, Proposition 2.7 (1,
2)]
) will be needed ahead.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A}
be an inclusion of simple unital

C^{*}
-algebras with a finite-index conditional expectation
E:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}
. Then, for any
\mathcal{C}\in\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A})
,
there exists a finite-index conditional expectation
F:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{C}

and, moreover, if
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B})\subseteq\mathcal{C}
, then

\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathcal{A})\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{A}),
where

\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathcal{A})
is defined with respect to
F
.

In particular,
\mathcal{C}_{1}\subset\mathcal{A}_{1}
, where
\mathcal{A}_{1}
(resp.,
\mathcal{C}_{1}
) denotes
the
C^{*}
-basic construction of the inclusion
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A}
(resp.,

\mathcal{C}\subset\mathcal{A}
) with respect to
E
(resp.,
F
).

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{C}\in\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A})
. Then, by
[
12
, Proposition 6.1]
,
there exists a conditional expectation
F:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}
of finite
index.

Next, suppose that
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B})=\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{C}
.

Note that, as
E
is of finite Watatani index, it satisfies the
Pimsner-Popa inequality (
[
24
]
); in particular, so does the restriction

E_{\restriction C}:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}
. Since
\mathcal{B}
is simple, it then follows
from
[
12
, Corollary 3.4]
that
E_{\restriction_{\mathcal{C}}}
also has a
finite quasi-basis. Thus,
G:=E_{\restriction\mathcal{C}}\circ F
is of finite
Watatani index by
[
24
, Proposition 1.7.1]
. Since
E
and
G
are
two finite-index conditional expectations from
\mathcal{A}
onto
\mathcal{B}
, by

[
24
, Proposition 2.10.9]
, there exists a
q\in\mathcal{B}^{{}^{\prime}}\cap\mathcal{A}

such that

E(x)=G(q^{*}xq)\ \mbox{for all }x\in\mathcal{A}.

Then, for any
T\in\mathcal{L}_{C}(\mathcal{A})
, we observe that

\displaystyle\langle T(x),y\rangle_{\mathcal{B}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E(T(x)^{*}y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G(q^{*}T(x)^{*}yq)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{\restriction_{C}}(F((T(x)q)^{*}yq))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{\restriction_{C}}(\langle T(x)q,yq\rangle_{C})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{\restriction C}(\langle xq,T^{*}(yq)\rangle_{C})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{\restriction C}\big(F(q^{*}x^{*}T^{*}(yq))\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{\restriction C}\big(F(q^{*}x^{*}T^{*}(y)q)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G(q^{*}x^{*}T^{*}(y)q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E(x^{*}T^{*}(y))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle x,T^{*}(y)\rangle_{\mathcal{B}}

for all
x,y\in\mathcal{A}
. Hence,
T\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{A})
.
∎

Notation:
For any inclusion
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A}
of unital

C^{*}
-algebras, let

\mathcal{I}_{1}(\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A}):=\{\mathcal{C}\in\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A}):\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B})\subseteq\mathcal{C}\}.

Clearly,
\mathcal{I}_{1}
a sublattice of
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A})
,

\mathcal{I}_{1}(\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A})\subseteq\mathcal{I}_{0}(\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A})
and
\mathcal{I}_{1}(\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A})=\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A})
if
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B})\subseteq\mathcal{B}
.

We shall need the following adaptation of
[
11
, Lemma 3.3]
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A}
be an inclusion of simple unital
C^{*}
-algebras with a
finite-index conditional expectation
E:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}
. Then,

(4.1)

d_{KK}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D})\leq\mathrm{Ind}_{W}(E)\,\|e_{\mathcal{C}}-e_{\mathcal{D}}\|

for all
\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}\in\mathcal{I}_{1}(\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A})
.

Proof.

We shall simply write
\mathcal{I}_{1}
for the set
\mathcal{I}_{1}(\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A})
.

Since
E
has finite index, it satisfies the Pimsner-Popa
inequality (by
[
24
, Proposition 2.6.2]
), i.e.,

E(x^{*}x)\geq(\mathrm{Ind}_{W}(E))^{-1}x^{*}x

for all
x\in\mathcal{A}
; and, since

\mathrm{Ind}_{W}(E)\geq 1
(by
[
24
, Lemma 2.3.1]
), it follows that

E(x^{*}x)\geq(\mathrm{Ind}_{W}(E))^{-2}x^{*}x

for all
x\in\mathcal{A}
. In particular,
\|\eta(x)\|\geq(\mathrm{Ind}_{W}(E))^{-1}\|x\|
for all
x\in\mathcal{A}
. Moreover,
\|\eta(a)\|\leq\|a\|\leq 1
for every

a\in B_{1}(\mathcal{C})
.

Since
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A}
are both simple, it follows from
Proposition

4.5
that

\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathcal{A})\cup\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{A})\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{A})
, for any two
\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}\in\mathcal{I}_{1}
. Thus,
e_{\mathcal{C}},e_{\mathcal{D}}\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{A})
, and

\|e_{\mathcal{C}}-e_{\mathcal{D}}\|\geq\frac{\|\eta(E_{\mathcal{C}}(a)-E_{\mathcal{D}}(a))\|}{\|\eta(a)\|}\geq\|\eta(a-E_{\mathcal{D}}(a))\|\geq\frac{1}{\mathrm{Ind}_{W}(E)}\|a-E_{\mathcal{D}}(a)\|

for all
a\in B_{1}(\mathcal{C})\setminus\{0\}
. Therefore, for each
a\in B_{1}(\mathcal{C})
, there exists an element
b:=E_{\mathcal{D}}(a)\in B_{1}(\mathcal{D})
such that

\|a-b\|\leq\mathrm{Ind}_{W}(E)\,\|e_{\mathcal{C}}-e_{\mathcal{D}}\|
. By a symmetric
arguement, we deduce that

d_{KK}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D})\leq\mathrm{Ind}_{W}(E)\,\|e_{\mathcal{C}}-e_{\mathcal{D}}\|.

∎

The following finiteness result is an adaptation (as well as a mild
generalization) of
[
11
, Corollary 3.9]
(also see
[
18
, Theorem
1.3]
). Its proof is an imitation of that of

Theorem

4.1
. However, we provide all steps for the sake of
completeness.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A}
be an inclusion of simple unital
C^{*}
-algebras
with a finite-index conditional expectation
E:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}
. Then, the sublattice
\mathcal{I}_{1}(\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A})
is finite.

Proof.

Consider the Watatani’s
C^{*}
-basic construction
\mathcal{A}_{1}:=C^{*}(\mathcal{A},e_{1})
of the inclusion
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A}
with respect to the
finite-index conditional expectation
E
and Jones projection

e_{1}
. Let
\widetilde{E}:\mathcal{A}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
be the
finite-index dual conditional expectation of
E
. Then,

\widetilde{E}\circ{E}:\mathcal{A}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}
is also a
conditional expectation of finite index. Since
\mathcal{A}
is simple, it
follows from
[
24
, Proposition 2.7.3]
that the relative
commutant
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{A}_{1}
is finite-dimensional. Therefore, the set

\mathcal{P}:=\{p\in\mathcal{B}^{{}^{\prime}}\cap\mathcal{A}_{1}:p\mbox{\, is a projection}\}

is a compact Hausdorff space with respect to the operator norm.

Since
\mathcal{A}
is simple,
\mathrm{Ind}_{W}(E)
is a positive scalar. Fix a
0<\gamma<10^{-6}
and let
\varepsilon=\frac{\gamma}{2\,\mathrm{Ind}_{W}(E)}
. By the compactness of
\mathcal{P}
, there
exists a finite cover of
\mathcal{P}
consisting of open balls of radius

\epsilon
. Now, it suffices to show that each such
\epsilon
-ball
contains only finitely many Jones projections corresponding to members
of
\mathcal{I}_{1}
.

Note that, for any two
\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}\in\mathcal{I}_{1}
, their
corresponding Jones projections
e_{\mathcal{C}}
and
e_{\mathcal{D}}
are in
\mathcal{B}^{{}^{\prime}}\cap\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{A})=\mathcal{B}^{{}^{\prime}}\cap\mathcal{A}_{1}
, by

Proposition

4.5
; and, by
Lemma

4.6
, they also satisfy the
inequality

d_{KK}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D})\leq\mathrm{Ind}_{W}(E)\|e_{\mathcal{C}}-e_{\mathcal{D}}\|.

So, if
e_{\mathcal{C}}
and
e_{\mathcal{D}}
are in one of the

\varepsilon
-open balls, then
d_{KK}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D})\leq\frac{\gamma}{2}<\gamma
. Thus, by
[
8
, Theorem 3.7]
, there exists a unitary

u
in
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{A}
such that
\mathcal{D}=u\mathcal{C}u^{*}
(and
\|u-1_{\mathcal{A}}\|\leq 16\sqrt{110}\gamma^{\frac{1}{2}}+880\gamma
). Since

\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B})\subseteq\mathcal{C}
, this implies that
u\in\mathcal{C}
. Hence,
\mathcal{D}=u\mathcal{C}u^{*}=\mathcal{C}
. This shows that each

\varepsilon
-open ball of the cover contains at most one Jones
projection for some intermediate
C^{*}
-subalgebra in

\mathcal{I}_{1}
, and we are done.
∎

Since countably decomposable type
III
factors are simple, we obtain
the following generalization of
[
23
, Theorem 2.5]
(and a partial
generalization of
[
18
, Theorem 1.3]
).

Corollary 4.8
.

Let
\mathcal{N}\subset\mathcal{M}
be an inclusion of countably
decomposable type
III
factors with a finite-index conditional
expectation. Then, the set
\mathcal{L}_{1}(\mathcal{N}\subset\mathcal{M}):=\{\mathcal{Q}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{N}\subset\mathcal{M}):\mathcal{N}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathcal{Q}\}
is finite.

4.3.
Cardinality of
\mathrm{IMS}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A},E)

Longo (in
[
19
]
) showed that the cardinality of the lattice of
intermediate subfactors of any finite-index irreducible inclusion
N\subset M
of factors (of type
II_{1}
or
III
) is bounded by

([M:N]^{2})^{[M:N]^{2}}
. More recently, a similar bound was obtained for
the lattice of intermediate
C^{*}
-algebras of a finite-index inclusion
of simple unital
C^{*}
-algebras by Bakshi and the first named author
in
[
3
]
, which was achieved by introducing the notion of (interior)
angle between two intermediate
C^{*}
-subalgebras. Further, Bakshi et
al (in
[
2
]
) improved the bound by proving that the cardinality
of the lattice of intermediate
C^{*}
-subalgebras of an irreducible
inclusion
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A}
of simple unital
C^{*}
-algebras with finite
index is bounded by
9^{[\mathcal{A}:\mathcal{B}]_{0}}
.

In this subsection, for any irreducible inclusion
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A}
of
unital
C^{*}
-algebras with a finite index conditional expectation
E:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
,
through some elementary group action technique, we provide an
expression for the cardinality of
\mathrm{IMS}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A},E)
in terms of the
indices of the unitary normalizers of its members in
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B})
- see

Proposition

4.10
. On face, the expression looks a bit dry,
but it might prove useful while performing some concrete calculations.

In
[
9
, Lemma 2.8(2)]
, it was shown that for any inclusion
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A}
of unital
C^{*}
-algebras with a tracial finite-index
conditional expectation
E:\mathcal{A}\to B
,
u\mathcal{C}u^{*}\in\mathrm{IMS}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A},E)

for every
\mathcal{C}\in\mathrm{IMS}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A},E)
and
u\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B})
. We now
have its following (more obvious) variant (which does not require
E

to be tracial), which gives an abundance of compatible intermediate

C^{*}
-algebras and will also allow us to get an expression for the
cardinality of
\mathrm{IMS}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A},E)
when the inclusion
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A}
is irreducible.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A}
be an inclusion of unital
C^{*}
-algebras with a
finite-index conditional expectation
E:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}
such that

\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B})\subseteq\mathcal{B}
. Let
\mathcal{C}\in\mathrm{IMS}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A},E)

with respect to the compatible finite-index conditional expectation

F:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}
. Then, for every
u\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B})
,
u\mathcal{C}u^{*}\in\mathrm{IMS}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A},E)
with respect to the conditional expectation

F_{u}:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow u\mathcal{C}u^{*}
given by
F_{u}=\mathrm{Ad}_{u}\circ F\circ\mathrm{Ad}_{u^{*}}
.

Proof.

As
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B})\subseteq\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{E}_{0}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})\neq\emptyset
, it follows from
[
24
, Corollary 1.4.3]
that
E
is
the unique conditional from
\mathcal{A}
onto
\mathcal{B}
. Also,
from
[
9
, Lemma 2.8(1)]
we know that
F_{u}
is of finite
index. So,
E_{\restriction_{u\mathcal{C}u^{*}}}\circ F_{u}
is again a finite-index
conditional expectation from
\mathcal{A}
onto
\mathcal{B}
. Thus, by uniqueness of

E
, we get
E_{\restriction_{u\mathcal{C}u^{*}}}\circ F_{u}=E
. Hence,
u\mathcal{C}u^{*}\in\mathrm{IMS}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A},E)
with respect to the compatible conditional
expectation
F_{u}
.
∎

With notations as in
Lemma

4.9
, we thus observe that the group

G:=\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B})
admits an action on the set
\mathrm{IMS}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A},E)
via
the map

G\times\mathrm{IMS}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A},E)\ni(u,\mathcal{C})\mapsto u\mathcal{C}u^{*}\in\mathrm{IMS}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A},E).

For any
\mathcal{C}\in\mathrm{IMS}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A},E)
, its stabilizer

\mathrm{Stab}_{G}(\mathcal{C})=\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B})\cap\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{C}).

Let
\widehat{\mathrm{IMS}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A},E)}
denote a set of representatives of
the orbits of
\mathrm{IMS}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A},E)
with respect to this action. Then, in
view of
4.2
, from some basic theory of group actions,
we immediately conclude the following:

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A}
be an irreducible inclusion of unital

C^{*}
-algebras with a finite-index conditional expectation
E:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}
and
G:=\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B})
. Then,

(1)

\big[\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B}):\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B})\cap\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{C})\big]<\infty

for every
C\in\mathrm{IMS}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A},E)
; and,

(2)

\big|\mathrm{IMS}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A},E)\big|=\sum_{\mathcal{C}\in\widehat{\mathrm{IMS}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A},E)}}\big[\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B}):\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B})\cap\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{C})\big].

Lemma 4.11
.

Let
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}
,
E
and
G
be as in
Proposition

4.10

and
\mathcal{C}\in\mathrm{IMS}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A},E)
. Then,

[\mathcal{A}:\mathcal{D}]_{0}=[\mathcal{A}:\mathcal{C}]_{0}\ \text{and}\ [\mathcal{D}:\mathcal{B}]_{0}=[\mathcal{C}:\mathcal{B}]_{0}

for very
\mathcal{D}\in\mathrm{Orb}_{G}(\mathcal{C})
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A}
is an irreducible inclusion, the conditional
expectation
E:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}
is unique, by
[
24
, Corollary
1.4.3]
. Hence, it is minimal.

Suppose that
\mathcal{C}\in\mathrm{IMS}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A},E)
with respect to the
compatible finite-index conditional expectation
F:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow C
. Note that
\mathrm{Orb}_{G}(\mathcal{C})=\{u\mathcal{C}u^{*}:u\in G\}
.

Let
\mathcal{D}\in\mathrm{Orb}_{G}(\mathcal{C})
. Then,
\mathcal{D}=u\mathcal{C}u^{*}
for some
u\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B})
and
\mathcal{D}\in\mathrm{IMS}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{A},E)
with respect to
the conditional expectation
F_{u}:=\mathrm{Ad}_{u}\circ F\circ\mathrm{Ad}_{u^{*}}
. Also,
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{D})=\mathcal{Z}(u\mathcal{C}u^{*})=u\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})u^{*}=\mathbb{C}=\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{A})
,
\mathcal{D}{{}^{\prime}}\cap\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{B}{{}^{\prime}}\cap\mathcal{A}=\mathbb{C}
and

\mathcal{B}{{}^{\prime}}\cap\mathcal{D}\subseteq\mathcal{B}{{}^{\prime}}\cap\mathcal{A}=\mathbb{C}
; so, by
[
24
, Corollary
1.4.3]
again,
F_{u}
and
E_{\restriction_{\mathcal{D}}}
are both
unique and, hence, minimal. Thus, from
[
16
, Theorem 3]
, we get
[\mathcal{A}:\mathcal{B}]_{0}=[\mathcal{A}:\mathcal{D}]_{0}[\mathcal{D}:\mathcal{B}]_{0}
for every
\mathcal{D}\in\mathrm{Orb}_{G}(\mathcal{C})
. Note that,
[\mathcal{A}:\mathcal{D}]_{0}=[\mathcal{A}:u\mathcal{C}u^{*}]_{0}=[\mathcal{A}:\mathcal{C}]_{0}
, by
[
9
, Lemma 2.8]
, which then shows that

[\mathcal{D}:\mathcal{B}]_{0}=[\mathcal{C}:\mathcal{B}]_{0}
, as well.
∎

5.
Comparisons between various notions of distance

In this section, we discuss the various notions of distance between
subalgebras of a given operator algebra by Christensen and
Mashood-Taylor and make comparisons between them and the
Kadison-Kastler distance.

5.1.
Christensen’s distances

Christensen has given two notions of distances and used them
effectively in proving some significant perturbation results. The
first one is defined between subalgebras of any tracial von Neumann
algebra (
[
5
]
) and, more generally, the other one is
defined between subalgebras of any
C^{*}
-algebra (
[
6
]
). We
briefly recall both notions in reversed order and state some facts
related to them.

5.1.1.
Christensen’s distance between subalgebras of a normed algebra

Though Christensen gave the notion of distance between subalgebras of a

C^{*}
-algebra, the same can be used in the normed algebra context as
well.

Let
A
be a normed algebra. Recall from
[
6
]
that, for
C,\,D\in\mathrm{Sub}_{A}
and a scalar
\gamma>0
,
C\subseteq_{\gamma}D
if for each
x\in B_{1}(C)
, there exists a
y\in D
such that
\|x-y\|\leq\gamma
; and, the Christensen
distance between
C
and
D
is defined by

(5.1)

d_{0}(C,D)=\inf\{\gamma>0\,:\,C\subseteq_{\gamma}D\text{ and }D\subseteq_{\gamma}C\}.

Here are some elementary observations regarding the Christensen
distance, some of which will be used ahead.

Remark 5.1
.

Let
A
be a normed algebra.

(1)

d_{0}(C,D)\leq 1
for all
C,D\in\text{Sub}_{A}
.

(2)

d_{0}(C,D)=d_{0}(\overline{C},D)=d_{0}(\overline{C},\overline{D})
for all
C,D\in\text{Sub}_{A}
.

(3)

d_{0}
is not a metric on

\mathrm{Sub}_{A}
(as it does
not satisfy the triangle inequality). However,
d_{0}
and
d_{KK}

are “equivalent” in the sense that

d_{0}(C,D)\leq d_{KK}(C,D)\leq 2d_{0}(C,D)

for all
C,D\in\mathrm{Sub}_{A}
. (
[
7
, Remark 2.3]
)

(4)

If
C,D\in\mathrm{Sub}_{A}
and
C
is a norm closed proper
subalgebra of
D
, then
d_{0}(C,D)=1
. (Follows from
[
7
, Proposition
2.4]
- also compare with
Remark

3.2
(3).)

5.1.2.
Christensen’s distance between subalgebras of a tracial von Neumann algebra

For a von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{M}
, let

*\text{-Sub}^{u}_{\mathcal{M}}:=\{\text{ *-subalgebras of }\mathcal{M}\text{ containing }1_{\mathcal{M}}\}\text{ and }

W^{*}\text{-}\mathrm{Sub}_{\mathcal{M}}:=\{\text{von Neumann subalgebras of }\mathcal{M}\ \text{(\,possibly with different unit)}\}.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a von Neumann
algebra with a (fixed) faithful normal tracial state
\tau
. Then,

\mathcal{M}
inherits a natural inner product structure with respect to the
inner product
\langle x,y\rangle_{\tau}=\tau(y^{*}x)
,
x,y\in\mathcal{M}
. The corresponding norm on
\mathcal{M}
will be denoted by
\|x\|_{\tau}
,
i.e.,
\|x\|_{\tau}=\tau(x^{*}x)^{1/2}
,
x\in\mathcal{M}
; and, the Hilbert
space completion of
\mathcal{M}
is denoted by
L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
. There is a
natural embedding of
\mathcal{M}
into
B(L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau))
via left
multiplication and this allows us to consider
\mathcal{M}
as a von Neumann
algebra on
L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
.

Further, in order to distinguish the elements of the inner-product
structure
\mathcal{M}
from that of the von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{M}
, as is
standard, we shall use the notation
\widehat{x}
for the elements of the
inner-product space
\mathcal{M}
. Thus,
\langle\widehat{x},\widehat{y}\rangle_{\tau}=\tau(y^{*}x)
and
x(\widehat{1})=\widehat{x}
for all
x,y\in\mathcal{M}
.

Recall from
[
5
]
that, for
P,\,Q\in\mathrm{Sub}_{\mathcal{M}}

and a scalar
\gamma>0
,
P\subset_{\gamma}Q
if for each
x\in B_{1}(P)
(w.r.t.
\|\cdot\|
), there exists a
y\in Q
such
that
\|\widehat{x}-\widehat{y}\|_{\tau}\leq\gamma
; and, the Christensen distance
between
P
and
Q
is defined by

(5.2)

d_{C}(P,Q)=\inf\{\gamma>0\,|\,P\subset_{\gamma}Q\,\text{and}\,Q\subset_{\gamma}P\}.

Here are some facts related to the Christensen distance
d_{C}
.

Remark 5.2
.

With running notations, the following hold:

(1)

d_{C}(P,Q)\leq 1
for all
P,Q\in\mathrm{Sub}_{\mathcal{M}}
.

(2)

d_{C}
is a complete metric on

W^{*}\text{-}\mathrm{Sub}_{\mathcal{M}}\,
. (
[
5
, Theorem, 5.1]
)

(3)

From
Proposition

5.8
and
Proposition

5.5
ahead, it follows that

d_{C}(P,Q)=d_{C}(\overline{P}^{S.O.T.},\overline{Q}^{S.O.T.})=d_{C}(P^{\prime\prime},Q^{\prime\prime})

for all
P,Q\in*\text{-}\mathrm{Sub}_{\mathcal{M}}^{u}
.

Here is a well known observation which will be essential
for our discussion - see, for instance,
[
1
, Proposition 2.6.4]
.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
M
be a unital
C^{*}
-algebra equipped with a faithful tracial
state
\tau
. Then,
M
is a von Neumann algbra on
L^{2}(M,\tau)

iff
\widehat{B_{1}(M)}
is complete w.r.t.
\|.\|_{\tau}
.

In particular,
M
is a
W^{*}
-algebra iff
\widehat{B_{1}(M)}
is
complete w.r.t.
\|.\|_{\tau}
.

5.2.
Mashood- Taylor distance between subalgebras of a tracial von Neumann algebra

Given a
II_{1}
-factor
M
with a unique faithful normal tracial state

\tau
and any two subfactors
P
and
Q
of
M
, Mashood and Taylor
(in
[
20
]
) consider the Hausdorff distance
(w.r.t.
\|\cdot\|_{\tau}
) between
{B_{1}(P)}
and

{B_{1}(Q)}
and prove some nice results related to continuity
of ‘Jones’ index. They also mention (on
[
20
, Page
56]
) that this distance gives the same topology on the set of
subfactors of
M
as the one given by the Christensen’s distance
d_{C}
. We shall
show in this section that
d_{C}=d_{MT}
.

Mashood-Taylor distance can, in fact, be defined in a slightly more
general setup as follows:

For a von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{M}
with a faithful normal tracial state
\tau
, for
any pair
P,Q\in\text{Sub}_{\mathcal{M}}
, the Mashood-Taylor distance between

P
and
Q
is defined as

(5.3)

d_{MT}(P,Q)=d_{H,\|\cdot\|_{\tau}}(\widehat{B_{1}(P)},\widehat{B_{1}(Q)}),

where
d_{H,\|\cdot\|_{\tau}}
denotes the Hausdorff distance with
respect to the metric induced by the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\tau}
and

\widehat{S}\subseteq L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
for
S\subseteq\mathcal{M}
(as in

Section

5.1.2
).

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a von Neumann algebra with a faithful normal
tracial state
\tau
. Then, the following hold:

(1)

d_{MT}
is a semi-metric on
\mathrm{Sub}_{\mathcal{M}}
.

(2)

d_{MT}
is a metric on
W^{*}\text{-}\mathrm{Sub}_{\mathcal{M}}
.

(3)

d_{C}(P,Q)\leq d_{MT}(P,Q)
for all
P,Q\in\text{Sub}_{\mathcal{M}}
.

Proof.

(1) follows readily from the definition.

(2): Note that
\widehat{B_{1}(\mathcal{Q})}
is closed and bounded in
L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
for every
\mathcal{Q}\in W^{*}\text{-Sub}_{\mathcal{M}}
by

Proposition

5.3
. And, it is well known that the Hausdorff distance is a metric on the collection of closed and bounded subsets of a metric space.

(3): Let
P,Q\in\text{Sub}_{\mathcal{M}}
and
\epsilon>0
. By the definition of
d_{MT}(P,Q)
, for each
x\in B_{1}(P)
,
there exists a
z\in B_{1}(Q)
such that

\|\widehat{x}-\widehat{z}\|_{\tau}\leq d_{MT}(P,Q)+\epsilon
. This implies
that
P\subset_{d_{MT}(P,Q)+\epsilon}Q
. Similiarly, we have
Q\subset_{d_{MT}(P,Q)+\epsilon}P
. Thus,
d_{C}(P,Q)\leq d_{MT}(P,Q)+\epsilon
for all
\epsilon>0
and we are done.

∎

The following observation is a nice tool to calculate the distance
between two von Neumann subalgebras and will be used ahead on more
than occasion.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},\tau)
be as in
Lemma

5.4
. Then,

d_{MT}(P,Q)=d_{MT}\Big(P,\overline{Q}^{S.O.T.}\Big)=d_{MT}\Big(\overline{P}^{S.O.T.},\overline{Q}^{S.O.T.}\Big)=d_{MT}\Big(P^{\prime\prime},Q^{\prime\prime}\Big)

for all
P,Q\in*\text{-}\mathrm{Sub}^{u}_{\mathcal{M}}
.

Proof.

In order to avoid any possible confusion, for every
x\in\mathcal{M}
and
S\subseteq\mathcal{M}
, let
d_{\tau}\big(\widehat{x},\widehat{S}\big):=\inf\{\|\widehat{x}-\widehat{s}\|_{\tau}:s\in S\}
.

Now, let
P,Q\in*\text{-Sub}^{u}_{\mathcal{M}}
. We first assert that

d_{\tau}\Big(\widehat{a},\widehat{B_{1}(Q)}\Big)=d_{\tau}\Big(\widehat{a},\widehat{B_{1}(\overline{Q}^{S.O.T.})}\Big)
for all
a\in B_{1}(P)
.

Clearly,
d_{\tau}\Big(\widehat{a},\widehat{B_{1}(\overline{Q}^{S.O.T.}\Big)}\leq d_{\tau}\Big(\widehat{a},\widehat{B_{1}(Q)}\Big)
for all
a\in B_{1}(P)
. For the
reverse inequality, fix an
a\in B_{1}(P)
and an
\epsilon>0
. Then,
there exists a
b_{0}\in B_{1}(\overline{Q}^{S.O.T.})
such that

d_{\tau}\Big(\widehat{a},\widehat{B_{1}(\overline{Q}^{S.O.T.})}\Big)\leq\|\widehat{a}-\widehat{b_{0}}\|_{\tau}<d_{\tau}\Big(\widehat{a},\widehat{B_{1}(\overline{Q}^{S.O.T.})}\Big)+\epsilon.

As
B_{1}\Big(\overline{Q}^{S.O.T.}\Big)=\overline{B_{1}(Q)}^{S.O.T.}
(by
Kaplansky density theorem), there exists a net

\{b_{\alpha}\}\subseteq B_{1}(Q)
such that

b_{\alpha}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle S.O.T.}}{{\longrightarrow}}b_{0}
. Hence, there
exists an
\alpha_{0}
such that

\|\widehat{a}-\widehat{b}_{\alpha_{0}}\|_{\tau}<d_{\tau}\Big(\widehat{a},\widehat{B_{1}(\overline{Q}^{S.O.T.})}\Big)+\epsilon.

This implies that

d_{\tau}\big(\widehat{a},\widehat{B_{1}(Q)}\big)\leq\|\widehat{a}-\widehat{b}_{\alpha_{0}}\|_{\tau}<d_{\tau}\Big(\widehat{a},\widehat{B_{1}(\overline{Q}^{S.O.T.})}\Big)+\epsilon.

As
\epsilon>0
was arbitrary, we get
d_{\tau}\Big(\widehat{a},\widehat{B_{1}(Q)}\Big)\leq d_{\tau}\Big(\widehat{a},\widehat{B_{1}(\overline{Q}^{S.O.T.})}\Big)
. This proves our assertion.

Then, we get

\beta:=\sup_{a\in B_{1}(P)}d_{\tau}\big(\widehat{a},\widehat{B_{1}(Q)}\big)=\sup_{a\in B_{1}(P)}d_{\tau}\Big(\widehat{a},\widehat{B_{1}(\overline{Q}^{S.O.T.})}\Big)

\leq\sup_{z\in B_{1}(\overline{P}^{S.O.T.})}d_{\tau}\Big(\widehat{z},\widehat{B_{1}(\overline{Q}^{S.O.T.})}\Big)=:\alpha.

Further, for any
\eta>0
, there exists a
z_{0}\in B_{1}\Big(\overline{P}^{S.O.T.}\Big)
such that

\alpha-\eta<d_{\tau}\Big(\widehat{z_{0}},\widehat{B_{1}(\overline{Q}^{S.O.T.})}\Big)\leq\alpha.

Then, by Kaplansky density theorem again, there exists a net

\{z_{\lambda}\}\subset B_{1}(P)
such that
z_{\lambda}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle S.O.T.}}{{\longrightarrow}}z_{0}
. In particular,

\widehat{z_{\lambda}}{\longrightarrow}\widehat{z_{0}}
with respect
to
\|\cdot\|_{\tau}
; and, since
d_{\tau}
is continuous with respect to

\|\cdot\|_{\tau}
, it follows that
d_{\tau}\Big(\widehat{{z}_{\lambda}},\widehat{B_{1}(\overline{Q}^{S.O.T.})}\Big)\rightarrow d_{\tau}\Big(\widehat{z_{0}},\widehat{B_{1}(\overline{Q}^{S.O.T.})}\Big).

Thus, there exists a
\lambda_{0}
such that
d_{\tau}\Big(\widehat{z}_{\lambda_{0}},\widehat{B_{1}(\overline{Q}^{S.O.T.})}\Big)>\alpha-\eta;
so that,
\beta\geq\alpha-\eta
. Since
\eta>0
was
arbitrary, we have
\beta\geq\alpha
. Thus,
\alpha=\beta
, i.e.,
d_{MT}(P,Q)=d_{MT}\Big({P},\overline{Q}^{S.O.T.}\Big).
By a similar
argument, we conclude that

d_{MT}(P,Q)=d_{MT}\Big(\overline{P}^{S.O.T.},\overline{Q}^{S.O.T.}\Big).

Rest follows from von Neumann’s Double
Commutant theorem.
∎

5.3.
Comparisons between
d_{KK}
,
d_{C}
and
d_{MT}

We first present the following comparison between
d_{MT}
and
d_{KK}
.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a von Neumann algebra with a faithful normal tracial
state
\tau
. Then,

d_{MT}(P,Q)\leq d_{KK}(P,Q)

for all
P,Q\in\mathrm{Sub}_{\mathcal{M}}
.

Proof.

Let
P,Q\in\mathrm{Sub}_{\mathcal{M}}
and

p\in B_{1}(P)
. Then,

d_{\tau}\Big(\widehat{p},\widehat{B_{1}(Q)}\Big)\leq\|\widehat{p}-\widehat{q}\|_{\tau}\leq\|p-q\|

for all
q\in B_{1}(Q)
. Thus,

d_{\tau}\Big(\widehat{p},\widehat{B_{1}(Q)}\Big)\leq d\big(p,B_{1}(Q)\big)
for all
p\in B_{1}(P)
. In particular,

\sup_{p\in B_{1}(P)}d_{\tau}\Big(\widehat{p},\widehat{B_{1}(Q)}\Big)\leq\sup_{p\in B_{1}(P)}d\big(p,B_{1}(Q)\big).

Similarly,

\sup_{q\in B_{1}(Q)}d_{\tau}\Big(\widehat{q},\widehat{B_{1}(P)}\Big)\leq\sup_{q\in B_{1}(Q))}d\big(q,B_{1}(P)\big).

Hence,

d_{MT}(P,Q)\leq d_{KK}(P,Q).

∎

The following useful observation is well known to the experts
(see, for instance,
[
5
, Eqn.(6)]
).

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},\tau)
be as in
Lemma

5.6
and let

\mathcal{N}
be a von Neumann subalgebra of
\mathcal{M}
(with common unit). Then,

d_{\tau}\Big(\widehat{x},\widehat{B_{1}(\mathcal{N})}\Big)=\|\widehat{x}-\widehat{E_{\mathcal{N}}(x)}\|_{\tau}=d_{\tau}\Big(\widehat{x},L^{2}(\mathcal{N})\Big)

for all
x\in B_{1}(\mathcal{M})
, where
E_{\mathcal{N}}
denotes the unique
\tau
-preserving normal conditional expectation from
\mathcal{M}
onto
\mathcal{N}
.

We are now all set to show that the Mashood-Taylor distance agrees with
the Christensen’s distance on
*\text{-Sub}_{M}^{u}
.

Proposition 5.8
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},\tau)
be as in
Lemma

5.7
. Then,

d_{C}(P,Q)=d_{MT}(P,Q)

for all
P,Q\in*\text{-}\mathrm{Sub}_{\mathcal{M}}^{u}
.

Proof.

Let
P,Q\in*\text{-}\mathrm{Sub}_{\mathcal{M}}^{u}
and consider

\widetilde{P}:=\overline{P}^{S.O.T.}
and
\widetilde{Q}:=\overline{Q}^{S.O.T.}
. In view of
Lemma

5.4
and

Proposition

5.5
, it suffices to show that
d_{C}(P,Q)\geq d_{MT}\big(\widetilde{P},\widetilde{Q}\big)
.

We first assert that
d_{\tau}\Big(\widehat{p},\widehat{(B_{1}(\widetilde{Q}))}\Big)=d_{\tau}\Big(\widehat{p},L^{2}(\widetilde{Q})\Big)=d_{\tau}\Big(\widehat{p},\widehat{Q}\Big)
for all
p\in B_{1}(P)
.

Let
p\in B_{1}(P)
. Then, by
Lemma

5.7
,

d_{\tau}\left(\widehat{p},\widehat{B_{1}(\widetilde{Q})}\right)=\|\widehat{p}-\widehat{E_{\widetilde{Q}}(p)}\|_{\tau}=d_{\tau}\left(\widehat{p},L^{2}(\widetilde{Q})\right).

Thus, as
\widehat{B_{1}(Q)}\subset\widehat{Q}\subset L^{2}(\widetilde{Q})
, we see that

d_{\tau}\left(\widehat{p},\widehat{B_{1}(\widetilde{Q})}\right)=d_{\tau}\left(\widehat{p},L^{2}(\widetilde{Q})\right)\leq d_{\tau}\left(\widehat{p},\widehat{Q}\right)\leq d_{\tau}\left(\widehat{p},\widehat{B_{1}(Q)}\right)=d_{\tau}\left(\widehat{p},\widehat{B_{1}(\widetilde{Q})}\right)

for all
p\in B_{1}(P)
. Hence, our first assertion is true.

Now, suppose that
P\subset_{\gamma}Q
and
Q\subset_{\gamma}P
for
some
\gamma>0
. We then assert that

d_{\tau}\left(\widehat{p},\widehat{B_{1}(\widetilde{Q})}\right)\leq\gamma\text{ for all }p\in B_{1}(\widetilde{P}).

So, let
p\in B_{1}(\widetilde{P})
and, by Kaplansky density theorem, fix
a net
\{p_{\alpha}\}\subset B_{1}(P)
such that

p_{\alpha}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle S.O.T.}}{{\longrightarrow}}p
in
B(L^{2}(\mathcal{M},\tau))
. Since
P\subset_{\gamma}Q
, for each

\alpha
, there exists a
q_{\alpha}\in Q
such that
\|\widehat{p}_{\alpha}-\widehat{q}_{\alpha}\|_{\tau}\leq\gamma.
So,

\gamma\geq\|\widehat{p}_{\alpha}-\widehat{q}_{\alpha}\|_{\tau}\geq d_{\tau}(\widehat{p}_{\alpha},\widehat{Q})=d_{\tau}\left(\widehat{p}_{\alpha},\widehat{B_{1}(\widetilde{Q})}\right)=\|\widehat{p}_{\alpha}-\widehat{E_{\widetilde{Q}}(p_{\alpha})}\|_{\tau}

for all
\alpha
, where
E_{\widetilde{Q}}
is the unique
\tau
-preserving normal
conditional expectation from
\mathcal{M}
onto
\widetilde{Q}
.
Thus,

d_{\tau}\left(\widehat{p},\widehat{B_{1}(\widetilde{Q})}\right)\leq\inf_{\alpha}\|\widehat{p}-\widehat{E_{\widetilde{Q}}(p_{\alpha})}\|_{\tau}\leq\inf_{\alpha}\|\widehat{p}-\widehat{p}_{\alpha}\|_{\tau}+\inf_{\alpha}\|\widehat{p}_{\alpha}-\widehat{E_{\widetilde{Q}}(p_{\alpha})}\|_{\tau}\leq\gamma,

where the last inequality holds because
\|\widehat{p}_{\alpha}-\widehat{p}\|_{\tau}\rightarrow 0\,
(as
p_{\alpha}\rightarrow p
in
S.O.T.) and
\|\widehat{p_{\alpha}}-\widehat{E_{\widetilde{Q}}(p_{\alpha})}\|_{\tau}\leq\gamma
for all

\alpha
. Thus, our second assertion is also true.

As a consequence,
\sup_{p\in B_{1}(\widetilde{P})}d_{\tau}\left(\widehat{p},\widehat{B_{1}(\widetilde{Q})}\right)\leq\gamma.
Likewise,
\sup_{q\in B_{1}(\widetilde{Q})}d_{\tau}\left(\widehat{q},\widehat{B_{1}(\widetilde{P})}\right)\leq\gamma
as well. Hence,

d_{MT}(\widetilde{P},\widetilde{Q})\leq\gamma
, which then implies that

d_{MT}\big(\widetilde{P},\widetilde{Q}\big)\leq d_{C}(P,Q),

and we are done.
∎

In view of
Lemma

5.6
, we also have the following:

Corollary 5.9
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},\tau)
be as in
Lemma

5.7
. Then,

d_{C}(P,Q)\leq d_{KK}(P,Q)

for all

P,Q\in\mathrm{Sub}_{\mathcal{M}}
.

6.
Kadison-Kastler and Christensen distance between subalgebras corresponding to subgroups

6.1.
Distances between crossed-product subalgebras associated to subgroups

Let
G
be a discrete group acting on a
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
via the
map
\alpha:G\rightarrow\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{A})
. Consider the space
C_{c}(G,\mathcal{A})
consisting of compactly supported
\mathcal{A}
-valued functions on
G
,
which can be identified naturally with the vector space

\{\sum_{\text{finite}}a_{g}g\,\,:\,\,a_{g}\in\mathcal{A}\,,\,g\in G\}

consisting of formal finite sums. Further,
C_{c}(G,\mathcal{A})
is a

*
-algebra with respect to the (so called, twisted) multiplication
given by the convolution operation

\Big(\sum_{s\in I}a_{s}s\Big)\Big(\sum_{t\in J}b_{t}t\Big)=\sum_{s\in I,t\in J}a_{s}\alpha_{s}(b_{t})st

and involution given by

\Big(\sum_{s\in I}a_{s}s\Big)^{*}=\sum_{s\in I}\alpha_{s^{-1}}(a_{s}^{*})s^{-1}

for any two finite sets
I
and
J
in
G
. The reduced crossed
product
\mathcal{A}\rtimes^{r}_{\alpha}G
and the universal crossed product

\mathcal{A}\rtimes^{u}_{\alpha}G
are defined, respectively, as the completions of

C_{c}(G,\mathcal{A})
with respect to the reduced norm and the universal norm
on
C_{c}(G,\mathcal{A})
, as described below:

The reduced norm is given by

\Big\|\sum_{\text{finite}}a_{g}g\Big\|_{r}:=\Big\|\sum_{\text{finite}}\pi(a_{g})(1\otimes\lambda_{g})\Big\|_{B(H\otimes l^{2}(G))},

where
\mathcal{A}\subset B(H)
is a (equivalently, any) fixed faithful
representation of
\mathcal{A}
,
\lambda:G\rightarrow B(l^{2}(G))
is the left
regular representation and
\pi:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow B(H\otimes l^{2}(G))
is
the representation satisfying
\pi(a)(\xi\otimes\delta_{g})=\alpha_{g^{-1}}(a)(\xi)\otimes\delta_{g}
for all
\xi\in H
and
g\in G
.

The universal norm is given by

\|x\|_{u}:=\sup_{\pi}\|\pi(x)\|_{B(H_{\pi})}\,\mbox{\,\,for\,\,}\,x\in C_{c}(G,\mathcal{A}),

where the supremum runs over all (cyclic)
*
-homomorphisms
\pi:C_{c}(G,\mathcal{A})\rightarrow B(H_{\pi})
. Note that

\|x\|_{r}\leq\|x\|_{u}\ \text{ for all}\ x\in C_{c}(G,\mathcal{A}).

We refer the reader to
[
4
]
for more on crossed
products.

6.1.1.
Distances between subalgebras of reduced crossed product

First, we gather some relevant facts related to the
reduced crossed product construction.

Remark 6.1
.

Let
G
and
\mathcal{A}
be as above and
H
be a subgroup of
G
.

(1)

The canonical injective
*
-homomorphism

C_{c}(H,\mathcal{A})\ni\sum_{\mathrm{finite}}a_{h}h\mapsto\sum_{\mathrm{finite}}a_{h}h\in C_{c}(G,\mathcal{A})

extends to an injective
*
-homomorphism from
\mathcal{A}\rtimes^{r}_{\alpha}H
into
\mathcal{A}\rtimes^{r}_{\alpha}G
. Hence, we can consider

\mathcal{A}\rtimes^{r}_{\alpha}H
as
C^{*}
-subalgebra of

\mathcal{A}\rtimes^{r}_{\alpha}G
. (
[
17
, Remark 3.2]
)

(2)

There exists a faithful conditional expectation
E_{H}:\mathcal{A}\rtimes_{\alpha}^{r}G\rightarrow\mathcal{A}\rtimes_{\alpha}^{r}H
satisfying

E_{H}(\sum_{g\in I}a_{g}g)=\sum_{h\in I\cap H}a_{h}h
for all
\sum_{g\in I}a_{g}g\in C_{c}(G,\mathcal{A})
(
[
17
, Remark 3.2]
); and,
if
[G:H]<\infty
, then
E_{H}
has finite (Watatani) index with a
quasi-basis given by any set of left coset representatives
\{g_{i}:1\leq i\leq[G:H]\}
of
H
in
G
.

(3)

Thus, as in
Section

2
, the vector
space
\mathcal{A}\rtimes^{r}_{\alpha}G
is a natural left
(\mathcal{A}\rtimes^{r}_{\alpha}H)
-module via multiplication from left. Hence,
following
[
24
]
, we consider
\mathcal{A}\rtimes^{r}_{\alpha}G
as a
pre-Hilbert (left)
(\mathcal{A}\rtimes^{r}_{\alpha}H)
-module with respect
to the
(\mathcal{A}\rtimes^{r}_{\alpha}H)
-valued inner product given by

\langle x,y\rangle=E_{H}(x^{*}y),\text{ for }x,y\in\mathcal{A}\rtimes^{r}_{\alpha}G.

Further, in order to distinguish the elements of the
C^{*}
-algebra

\mathcal{A}\rtimes^{r}_{\alpha}G
and the pre-Hilbert
(\mathcal{A}\rtimes^{r}_{\alpha}H)
-module
\mathcal{A}\rtimes^{r}_{\alpha}G
, following
[
24
]
, we
consider the identity map
\eta_{H}:\mathcal{A}\rtimes^{r}_{\alpha}G\rightarrow\mathcal{A}\rtimes^{r}_{\alpha}G
, where we consider the co-domain
as the pre-Hilbert
(\mathcal{A}\rtimes^{r}_{\alpha}H)
-module. Since
E_{H}
is
a contraction, we have

(6.1)

\|\eta_{H}(x)\|=\|E_{H}(x^{*}x)\|^{1/2}_{r}\leq\|x\|_{r}

for all
x\in\mathcal{A}\rtimes^{r}_{\alpha}G
.

(4)

Note that, for two subgroups
H\subsetneq K
of a discrete
group
G
,
\|\eta_{H}(x)\|
need not be equal to

Lemma 6.8

With running notations,

(6.2)

\left\|\eta\left(\sum_{g\in G}\alpha_{g}g\right)\right\|^{2}\leq\sum_{g\in G}|\alpha_{g}|\left(\sum_{x\in gH}|\alpha_{x}|\right)

Remark 6.9

Let

(1)

Lemma 6.15

Recall that, for a discrete group

Further, for each

we define

. Then,

is the multiplicative identity
for
\ell^{1}(G)
.

.

With running notations,

(1)

with a unital Banach

Proposition 6.19
.

Let

and

\lambda(H)^{\prime\prime}\subset L(G)
; thus,

L(H)
can be considered as a von Neumann subalgebra of

L(G)
. Further,
L(G)
always admits a faithful normal tracial state

\tau
given by
\tau(x)=\langle x(\delta_{e}),\delta_{e}\rangle
for

x\in L(G)
. Thus,
L(G)
admits an inner-product structure via

\tau
which induces a norm
\|\cdot\|_{\tau}
on
L(G)
given by

\|x\|_{\tau}=\tau(x^{*}x)^{1/2}
,
x\in L(G)
.

Proposition 6.20
.

Let
G
be a discrete group and
H
and
K
be two distinct
non-trivial subgroups of
G
. Then, in
L(G)
, the distances

d_{C}\big(\mathbb{C}[H],\mathbb{C}[K]\big),d_{MT}\big(\mathbb{C}[H],\mathbb{C}[K]\big),d_{KK}\big(\mathbb{C}[H],\mathbb{C}[K]\big),

d_{C}\big(L(H),L(K)\big),d_{MT}\big(L(H),L(K)\big)\text{ and }d_{KK}\big(L(H),L(K)\big)

are all equal to
1
.

Proof.

By
Lemma

5.6
,
d_{MT}\big(\mathbb{C}[H],\mathbb{C}[K]\big)\leq d_{KK}\big(\mathbb{C}[H],\mathbb{C}[K]\big)\leq 1
. So, in view of
Proposition

5.5

and
Proposition

5.8
, it is enough to show that
d_{MT}\big(\mathbb{C}[H],\mathbb{C}[K]\big)\geq 1
.

We prove this by considering the following two
possibilities separately.

Case 1.
Suppose that
H\subsetneq K
or
K\subsetneq H
.

Without loss of generality, assume that
H\subsetneq K
. Then,

\mathbb{C}[H]\subsetneq\mathbb{C}[K]
and
d_{\tau}\Big(\widehat{a},\widehat{B_{1}(\mathbb{C}[K])}\Big)=0
for all
a\in B_{1}(\mathbb{C}[H])
. Further, for any
k\in K\setminus H\cap K
, we have,
\|\widehat{k}-\widehat{x}\|_{\tau}\geq 1
for all
x\in B_{1}(\mathbb{C}[H])
. Thus,

\sup_{z\in B_{1}(\mathbb{C}[K]))}d_{\tau}\Big(\widehat{z},\widehat{B_{1}(\mathbb{C}[H])}\Big)\geq 1;

and, hence,
d_{MT}(\mathbb{C}[H],\mathbb{C}[K])\geq 1
.

Case 2.
Suppose that
H\neq H\cap K\neq K
.

Then, again for any
h\in H\setminus H\cap K
, we have

\|\widehat{h}-\widehat{x}\|_{\tau}\geq 1
for all
x\in B_{1}(\mathbb{C}[K])
.
Thus, as above,
d_{MT}(\mathbb{C}[H],\mathbb{C}[K])\geq 1
, and we are done.
∎

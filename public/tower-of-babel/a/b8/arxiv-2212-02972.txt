Carlitz twists: their motivic cohomology, regulators, zeta values and polylogarithms
Quentin Gazda and Andreas Maurischat

Abstract
Abstract: The integral t t -motivic cohomology and the class module of a (rigid analytically trivial) Anderson t t -motive were introduced by the first author in [ Gaz22b ] . This paper is devoted to their determination in the particular case of tensor powers of the Carlitz t t -motive, namely, the function field counterpart A ¯ ​ ( n ) \underline{A}(n) of Tate twists ℤ ⁡ ( n ) \mathbb{Z}(n) . We find out that these modules are in relation with fundamental objects of function field arithmetic: integral t t -motivic cohomology governs linear relations among Carlitz polylogarithms, its torsion is expressed in terms of the denominator of Bernoulli-Carlitz numbers and the Fitting ideal of class modules is a special zeta value. We also express the regulator of A ¯ ​ ( n ) \underline{A}(n) for positive n n in terms of generalized Carlitz polylogarithms ; after establishing their algebraic relations using difference Galois theory together with the Anderson-Brownawell-Papanikolas criterion, we prove that the regulator is an isomorphism if, and only if, n n is prime to the characteristic.

th Bernoulli number (e.g.
\operatorname{B}_{1}=-\frac{1}{2}
,
\operatorname{B}_{2}=\frac{1}{6}
,
\operatorname{B}_{3}=0
) – is rather the rational fraction
\operatorname{BC}_{n}\cdot\Pi(n-1)/\Pi(n)
. The following amusing corollary of Theorem
B
, obtained from explicit computations due to Alejandro-Rodríguez
[
AlR12
]
, relates the torsion of the integral
t
-motivic cohomology of
\underline{A}(n)
to the denominator of those elements:

Corollary
.

Let
\varepsilon_{n}
be as in Theorem
B
. Then
\varepsilon_{n}(\theta)\cdot\operatorname{BC}_{n}\cdot\Pi(n-1)/\Pi(n)
is in
\mathbb{F}[\theta]
.

That is, the torsion of
t
-motivic cohomology is killed by the denominator of
\operatorname{BC}_{n}\cdot\Pi(n-1)/\Pi(n)
written in lowest term. This is somehow similar to the classical situation where the the torsion in
K_{2n-1}(\mathbb{Z})
is killed by the denominator of
\operatorname{B}_{n}/n
.

On the other-hand, the class module
\operatorname{Cl}(\underline{A}(n))
seems to play a role
dual
to that of
\Ext^{1,\operatorname{reg}}_{A,\infty}(\mathbbm{1},\underline{A}(n))
, as we next show (see Theorems
3.1
,
3.5
and also Proposition
4.9
):

Theorem C
.

For all integers
n
, we have isomorphisms of
\mathbb{F}[t]
-modules:

\operatorname{Cl}(\underline{A}(n))\cong\begin{cases}(0)&\text{if~}n\geq 0,\\
\operatorname{Cl}(\underline{A}(n))_{\operatorname{tors}}&\text{if~}n<0\text{~ and~ }q-1\nmid n,\\
\mathbb{F}[t]\oplus\operatorname{Cl}(\underline{A}(n))_{\operatorname{tors}}&\text{if~}n>0\text{~ and~ }q-1|n,\end{cases}

where
\operatorname{Cl}(\underline{A}(n))_{\operatorname{tors}}
is the torsion submodule of
\operatorname{Cl}(\underline{A}(n))
.

Determining the torsion module
\operatorname{Cl}(\underline{A}(n))_{\operatorname{tors}}
seems to be a challenging task. However, we are able to describe its Fitting ideal. Reconsider the series
Z(x,n)
introduced earlier in (
1.1
), although this time in the variable
t
instead of
\theta
, as we now interpret zeta values in the coefficient ring
\mathbb{F}[t]
. Let us write
Z(x,n)
uniquely as

Z(x,n)=\zeta_{C}^{*}(n)(x-1)^{h_{n}}+O((x-1)^{h_{n}+1})\in\mathbb{F}[t][\![x-1]\!],

where
h_{n}\geq 0
and
\zeta_{C}^{*}(n)\in\mathbb{F}[t]
. Using the trace formula in the form described by Taelman in
[
Tae15
]
, we obtain (see Theorem
3.7
):

Theorem D
.

For all integers
n
, the rank of
\operatorname{Cl}(\underline{A}(n))
is equal to
h_{n}
. For non positive
n
,
\zeta_{C}^{*}(n)
generates the Fitting ideal of
\operatorname{Cl}(\underline{A}(n))
.

We believe that Theorem
D
could be explained by elementary means. In Remark
3.9
below, we give an elementary strategy to tackle this result, and challenge the interested reader to solve it this way.

As an application of Theorems
B
,
D
, and together with classical results on Carlitz zeta values due to Carlitz, Lee and Thakur, we obtained the following result which describes situations when the module of regulated extensions is computed solely in terms of Galois cohomology (see Corollary
3.10
):

Corollary
.

Assume that
q=p
is prime and let
n\geq 0
. Then, the map

r_{B}:\Ext^{1,\operatorname{reg}}_{A}(\mathbbm{1},\underline{A}(-n))\longrightarrow\operatorname{H}^{1}(G_{\infty},\underline{A}(-n)_{B}),

deduced from (
1.7
) with
\underline{N}=\mathbbm{1}
and
\underline{M}=\underline{A}(-n)
, is an isomorphism if, and only if, the sum of the digits of
n
written in base
p
is
<p-1
.

Our last task is devoted to the study of the regulator of
\underline{A}(n)
and the counterpart of

(
BC
)

2

. To express the regulator, we introduce variants of the Carlitz polylogarithm, defined as follows. For
x\in K_{\infty}^{s}
with
\deg x<\frac{nq}{q-1}
, the series

\xi_{x,n}(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}{\frac{x^{q^{i}}}{(t-\theta)^{n}(t-\theta^{q})^{n}\cdots(t-\theta^{q^{i}})^{n}}}

converges to a meromorphic function in the variable
t\in K_{\infty}^{s}
having a pole of order
n
at
t=\theta
. This series, which has already been introduced by Chang-Yu
[
ChY07
, (3.3)]
, can be suitably interpreted as a
deformation
of
\Li_{n}(x)
. This allows to introduce
generalized Carlitz polylogarithms

\Li_{n}^{[j]}(x)\in K_{\infty}^{s}
by mean of the expansion:

\xi_{x,n}(t)=\frac{\Li_{n}^{[0]}(x)}{(t-\theta)^{n}}+\frac{\Li_{n}^{[1]}(x)}{(t-\theta)^{n-1}}+\ldots+\frac{\Li_{n}^{[n-1]}(x)}{(t-\theta)}+O(K_{\infty}^{s}[\![t-\theta]\!]).

(1.9)

In particular,
\Li_{n}^{[0]}(x)
is the Carlitz
n
th polylogarithm defined in (
1.3
). Using
prolongation
t
-motives
as defined by the second author in
[
Mau18
]
, difference Galois theory and the Anderson-Brownawell-Papanikolas criterion, we are able to show:

Theorem E
.

Assume that
n
is prime to the characteristic
p
of
\mathbb{F}
. Let
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{s})
be elements of
\mathbb{F}[\theta]
of degree
<n
such that the family of classes
\left([\underline{L}(\alpha_{1})],\ldots,[\underline{L}(\alpha_{s})]\right)
are linearly independent in

\Ext^{1,\operatorname{reg}}_{\infty}(\mathds{1},\underline{A}(n))
. Then, the family of elements in
K_{\infty}^{s}
,

(\tilde{\pi},\Li_{n}^{[j]}(\alpha_{i}),~i\in\{1,\ldots,s\},~j\geq 0)

is algebraically independent over
\mathbb{F}(\theta)
.

Our main interest in Theorem
E
stands in the next description of the regulator map. We show in Proposition
6.1
below:

Proposition
.

Let
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{s})
be elements of
\mathbb{F}[\theta]
of degree
<n
such that the family of classes
\left([\underline{L}(\alpha_{1})],\ldots,[\underline{L}(\alpha_{s})]\right)
is a basis of

\Ext^{1,\operatorname{reg}}_{\infty}(\mathds{1},\underline{A}(n))
. Then,
\mathrm{Reg}(\underline{A}(n))
is represented by the matrix

\begin{pmatrix}\Li_{n}^{[n-s]}(\alpha_{1})&\Li_{n}^{[n-s]}(\alpha_{2})&\cdots&\Li_{n}^{[n-s]}(\alpha_{s})\\
\Li_{n}^{[n-s+1]}(\alpha_{1})&\Li_{n}^{[n-s+1]}(\alpha_{2})&\cdots&\Li_{n}^{[n-s+1]}(\alpha_{s})\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\Li_{n}^{[n-1]}(\alpha_{1})&\Li_{n}^{[n-1]}(\alpha_{2})&\cdots&\Li_{n}^{[n-1]}(\alpha_{s})\end{pmatrix}\in\mathsf{Mat}_{s}(K_{\infty}).

In particular, the combination of Theorem
E
and the above proposition gives the following counterpart of Beilinson’s conjecture:

Corollary
.

If
p\nmid n
, the regulator of
\underline{A}(n)
is an isomorphism.

In retrospect, this is surely the most sophisticated way we ever encountered to prove the non-vanishing of a determinant!

However, the above proposition also implies that the conclusion of this corollary does not hold whenever
p|n
. From the relation
\xi_{\alpha,n}(t)^{p}=\xi_{\alpha^{p},np}(t)
, where
p
is the characteristic of
\mathbb{F}
, we have

\text{For~}k\geq 0:\quad\Li_{np}^{[k]}(\alpha^{p})=\begin{cases}0&\text{if~}p\nmid k,\\
\Li_{n}^{[k/p]}(\alpha)^{p}&\text{if~}p\mid k\end{cases}

(1.10)

which implies the vanishing of some lines in the above matrix. Concretely, one derives that the rank of
\mathrm{Reg}(\underline{A}(n))
is
\frac{n}{p^{v_{p}(n)}}-\mathbf{1}_{q-1|n}
(see Corollary
6.2
). This is quite a surprise and contrasts with the number field situation. For general
t
-motives
\underline{M}
, it is not clear – at least to the authors – what to expect regarding the rank of
\mathrm{Reg}(\underline{M})
.

Tools and notations from the general theory

2.1
Notation

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a finite field with
q
elements and of characteristic
p
. Let
A=\mathbb{F}[\theta]
, and
K=\mathbb{F}(\theta)
its fraction field. There are in fact two isomorphic versions of
K
(or
A
) playing a role in this paper: one for the
coefficient field
and one for the
base field
. As it is often done in the literature, we will distinguish two variables
t
and
\theta
for
coefficients
and
base
respectively.

As an instance of this, we denote by
K[t]
the polynomial ring in the variable
t
with coefficients in
K
, and by
\tau:K[t]\to K[t]
the ring endomorphism raising the coefficients to the
q
th power. Still in accordance with the literature, we also denote by
p^{(k)}
the element
\tau^{k}(p)
and call it
the
k
th twist of
p
(for
p\in K[t]
and
k\geq 1
).

On
K
, we consider the norm
\left\lvert\cdot\right\rvert=q^{\deg(\cdot)}
, and we denote by
K_{\infty}
the completed field, identified with
\mathbb{F}(\!(\theta^{-1})\!)
. The norm
\left\lvert\cdot\right\rvert
extends to a separable closure
K_{\infty}^{s}
of
K_{\infty}
. The completion of
K_{\infty}^{s}
, denoted by
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
, is well-known to be algebraically closed and complete.

For
L\subset\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
a complete subfield, we denote by
L\langle t\rangle
the
Tate algebra
over
L
, that is, the
L
-algebra of power series in the variable
t
whose coefficients tend to
0
:

L\langle t\rangle=\left\{\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i}t^{i}~\bigg|~\text{for~all~}i:~a_{i}\in L,~\lim_{i\to\infty}\left\lvert a_{i}\right\rvert=0\right\}.

For
f=\sum_{i}{a_{i}t^{i}}\in L\langle t\rangle
, we still denote by
\tau(f)=f^{(1)}
its
twist

\sum_{i}{a_{i}^{q}t^{i}}\in L\langle t\rangle
. We also equip
L\langle t\rangle
with the
Gauss norm

\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert
defined by means of the formula

\left\lVert\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i}t^{i}\right\rVert:=\max\{\left\lvert a_{i}\right\rvert\mid i\geq 0\}.

The Tate algebra
L\langle t\rangle
is complete with respect to
\|\cdot\|
, and
L[t]
is a dense subring. Note that we also have
\left\lVert f^{(1)}\right\rVert=\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{q}
.

We also let
L\langle\!\langle t\rangle\!\rangle
be the ring of
entire series
on
L
, that is :

L\langle\!\langle t\rangle\!\rangle:=\left\{\sum_{i\geq 0}{a_{i}t^{i}}\in L[\![t]\!]~|~\forall\rho>0:~\lim_{i}|a_{i}|\rho^{i}=0\right\}.

(2.1)

It is a subring of
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\langle t\rangle
which is stable under
\tau
and hyperdifferentiation of any order.

2.2
Anderson
t
-motives

Let
R
be either be
A
or
K
. We recall the definition of a (non necessarily effective)
Anderson
t
-motive over
R
: it is a pair
\underline{M}=(M,\tau_{M})
where
M
is a finite free
R[t]
-module and where
\tau_{M}
is an isomorphism of
R[t]
-modules:

\tau_{M}:(\tau^{*}M)\left[\frac{1}{t-\theta}\right]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}M\left[\frac{1}{t-\theta}\right].

Recall that
\tau^{*}M
is the
R[t]
-module which, as an abelian group, is
R[t]\otimes_{\tau,R[t]}M
- where
R[t]
on the left-hand side of the tensor product is seen as a
R[t]
-module through
\tau
- and where the
R[t]
-module structure is obtained by multiplying elements on the left-hand side of the tensor product. For
m\in M
, we denote by
\tau^{*}m\in\tau^{*}M
the element
(1\otimes_{\tau}m)
; in particular, the map

\tau^{*}:M\longrightarrow\tau^{*}M,\quad m\longmapsto\tau^{*}m

is not linear, but only
\tau
-linear
.

A morphism of
t
-motives
f:\underline{M}\to\underline{N}
over
R
is an
R[t]
-linear map of the underlying modules such that
f\circ\tau_{M}=\tau_{N}\circ\tau^{*}f
. We gather
t
-motives and their morphisms in an
\mathbb{F}[t]
-linear category
t\mathbf{Mot}_{R}
. We call a sequence
0\to\underline{M}\to\underline{E}\to\underline{N}\to 0
in
t\mathbf{Mot}_{R}

exact
if its underlying sequence of modules is. It is not hard to check that this equip
t\mathbf{Mot}_{R}
with the structure of an exact category
[
Gaz22a
, Prop. 2.13]
.

We also endow
t\mathbf{Mot}_{R}
with a symmetric monoidal structure as it is usually done. Given two
t
-motives
\underline{M}
and
\underline{N}
, we define their tensor product
\underline{M}\otimes\underline{N}
as the
t
-motive over
R
having for module
M\otimes_{R[t]}N
and for morphism:

\tau^{*}(M\otimes_{R[t]}N)\left[\frac{1}{t-\theta}\right]\cong(\tau^{*}M)\left[\frac{1}{t-\theta}\right]\otimes_{R[t]}(\tau^{*}N)\left[\frac{1}{t-\theta}\right]\\
\xrightarrow{\tau_{M}\otimes\tau_{N}}M\left[\frac{1}{t-\theta}\right]\otimes_{R[t]}N\left[\frac{1}{t-\theta}\right]\cong(M\otimes_{R[t]}N)\left[\frac{1}{t-\theta}\right].

One shows that
t\mathbf{Mot}_{R}
is
rigid
in the sense that it possesses all internal
\operatorname{Hom}
. In particular, all objects are dualizable.

Following Anderson
[
And86
]
, we define the
Betti realization of
\underline{M}
as the
\mathbb{F}[t]
-module:

\underline{M}_{B}:=\left\{f\in M\otimes_{R[t]}\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\langle t\rangle~|~\tau_{M}(\tau^{*}f)=f\right\}.

It is well-known to be a finite free
\mathbb{F}[t]
-module. By continuity,
\underline{M}_{B}
carries a compatible action of
G_{\infty}=\operatorname{Gal}(K_{\infty}^{s}|K_{\infty})
as acting on
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\langle t\rangle
coefficient-wise, and one can show that this action is continuous (see
[
Gaz22b
, Prop. 3.11.]
). As in
[
Gaz22b
]
, we will make use of the submodule
\underline{M}^{+}_{B}
of elements fixed by
G_{\infty}
.

The following definition goes back to Anderson’s original paper:

Definition 2.1
.

The
t
-motive
\underline{M}
is said to be
rigid analytically trivial
if the multiplication map:

\underline{M}_{B}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}[t]}\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\langle t\rangle\longrightarrow M\otimes_{R[t]}\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\langle t\rangle

is bijective.

We denote by
t\mathbf{Mot}^{\operatorname{rat}}_{R}
the full subcategory of rigid analytically trivial objects. It is an exact subcategory stable by extensions
[
Gaz22b
, §3]
which, since the functor
\underline{M}\mapsto\underline{M}_{B}
also commutes with
\otimes
, is again symmetric monoidal and rigid.

Along this paper, we will be interested in
Carlitz twists

\underline{A}(n)
as defined in Definition
1.8
. The notation is here to stress that
\underline{A}(n)
is the function field counterpart of the Tate twist
\mathbb{Q}(n)
. In fact, the same additive relations hold:

\underline{A}(0)=\mathbbm{1},\quad\underline{A}(n)^{\vee}\cong\underline{A}(-n),\quad\underline{A}(n)\otimes\underline{A}(m)\cong\underline{A}(n+m).

Let
\lambda_{\theta}\in K_{\infty}^{s}
be a
(q-1)
th root of
-\theta
. The
Anderson-Thakur function
\omega
is defined as the convergent product in the Tate algebra:

\omega(t)=\lambda_{\theta}\prod_{i=0}^{\infty}{\left(1-\frac{t}{\theta^{q^{i}}}\right)^{-1}}\in K_{\infty}(\lambda_{\theta})\langle t\rangle.

It is well-known that all Carlitz twists are rigid analytically trivial, with Betti realization being of rank
1
over
\mathbb{F}[t]
with generator
\omega^{n}
, i.e.,

\mathbb{F}[t]\cdot\omega^{n}=\left\{f\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\langle t\rangle\,\middle|\,(t-\theta)^{n}f-f^{(1)}=0\right\}.

(2.2)

2.3
Integral
t
-motivic cohomology and class group

Let
\underline{M}
be a rigid analytically trivial
t
-motive over

. The exactness of the Betti realization functor
[
Gaz22b
, Cor. 3.17]
induces an
\mathbb{F}[t]
-linear morphism

\Ext^{1,\operatorname{reg}}_{A}(\mathbbm{1},\underline{M})\longrightarrow\operatorname{H}^{1}(G_{\infty},\underline{M}_{B}).

(2.3)

The following definitions were made in
[
Gaz22b
]
:

Definition 2.2
.

-

An extension of
\mathbbm{1}
by
\underline{M}
has
analytic reduction at
\infty
if it splits as a continuous representation of
G_{\infty}
; i.e. belongs to the kernel of (
2.3
). We denote by
\Ext^{1}_{A,\infty}(\mathbbm{1},\underline{M})
this kernel and by
\Ext^{1,\operatorname{reg}}_{A,\infty}(\mathbbm{1},\underline{M})
the submodule of regulated extensions.

-

The cokernel of (
2.3
), denoted
\operatorname{Cl}(\underline{M})
, is called
the class module of
\underline{M}
.

The main tool to access these two modules is a long exact sequence which we recall next. Let
G_{\underline{M}}
be the following complex of
\mathbb{F}[t]
-modules sitting in degrees
\{0,1\}

G_{\underline{M}}=\left[\frac{M\otimes_{A[t]}K_{\infty}\langle t\rangle}{M}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}-\tau_{M}}\frac{M\otimes_{A[t]}K_{\infty}\langle t\rangle}{M+\tau_{M}(\tau^{*}M)}\right]

(2.4)

and where the boundary map is induced from
m\mapsto m-\tau_{M}(\tau^{*}m)
. This complex was introduced in
[
Gaz22b
, Def. 4.5]
as a main ingredient in the proof of the finite generation of
\Ext^{1,\operatorname{reg}}_{A,\infty}(\mathbbm{1},\underline{M})
and

. The next proposition was proved in Prop. 4.6,

; we extract the statement of interest from the latter reference and its proof:

Proposition 2.3
.

There is a long exact sequence of
\mathbb{F}[t]
-modules:

\underline{M}_{B}^{+}\longrightarrow\operatorname{H}^{0}(G_{\underline{M}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle b}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Ext^{1,\operatorname{reg}}_{A}(\mathbbm{1},\underline{M})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a}}{{\longrightarrow}}\operatorname{H}^{1}(G_{\infty},\underline{M}_{B})\longrightarrow\operatorname{H}^{1}(G_{\underline{M}})\longrightarrow 0

where
a
denotes the map (
2.3
). In particular,
\operatorname{Cl}(\underline{M})
is isomorphic to
\operatorname{H}^{1}(G_{\underline{M}})
. In addition, the composition
a\circ b
maps the class of a representative
\xi\in M\otimes_{A[t]}K_{\infty}\langle t\rangle
to the class of the cocycle
G_{\infty}\to\underline{M}_{B}
,
\sigma\mapsto\,^{\sigma}\xi-\xi
.

We make profit of the latter sequence to derive explicit formulas for the integral
t
-motivic cohomology in the situation of Carlitz twists. Let
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and let
\Delta=\Delta_{n}:K_{\infty}\langle t\rangle\to K_{\infty}\langle t\rangle
be the
\mathbb{F}[t]
-linear
\tau
-difference operator:

\Delta f(t)=f(t)-\frac{f(t)^{(1)}}{(t-\theta)^{n}}.

(2.5)

Corollary 2.4
.

There is a natural isomorphism of
\mathbb{F}[t]
-modules:

\Ext^{1,\operatorname{reg}}_{A,\infty}(\mathds{1},\underline{A}(n))\cong\frac{\{\xi\in K_{\infty}\langle t\rangle~|~\Delta\xi\in(t-\theta)^{\min\{0,-n\}}\mathbb{F}[\theta,t]\}}{\mathbb{F}[\theta,t]+\mathbf{1}_{q-1|n}\mathbb{F}[t]\omega(t)^{n}},

where
\mathbf{1}_{q-1|n}
equals

if

divides

, and
0
otherwise. For
n>0
, the rank of

if

and is
n-1
otherwise.

Proof.

Set
\underline{M}=\underline{A}(n)
. We have
\underline{A}(n)_{B}=\mathbb{F}[t]\omega(t)^{n}
where
\omega(t)^{n}\in K_{\infty}\langle t\rangle
if, and only if,

. That is,
\underline{A}(n)_{B}^{+}=\mathbf{1}_{q-1|n}\mathbb{F}[t]\omega(t)^{n}
. On the other-hand, from

G_{\underline{A}(n)}=\left[\frac{K_{\infty}\langle t\rangle}{\mathbb{F}[\theta,t]}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Delta}}{{\longrightarrow}}\frac{K_{\infty}\langle t\rangle}{(t-\theta)^{\min\{0,-n\}}\mathbb{F}[\theta,t]}\right].

(2.6)

The formula is thus deduced from Proposition

.

Now assume

[

, Thm 4.4]

\Ext^{1,\operatorname{ha}}_{\mathbf{HPk}^{+}_{K_{\infty}},\infty}(\mathbbm{1}^{+},\underline{H}^{+})

(the reader is invited to refer to

from which the dimension of (

∎

We can already detect from Corollary

\xi_{(t-\theta)^{n}d-d^{(1)}}=\xi_{(t-\theta)^{n}d}-\xi_{d^{(1)}}=d

for all

If
\left\lVert e^{\prime}\right\rVert<q^{n}

\left\lVert e^{\prime}\right\rVert\geq q^{n}
. As

, there exists an integer
\delta>0
for which

. The polynomial division algorithm of

by
(t-\theta)^{n}
as polynomials in the variable

provides

such that

\left\lVert c_{1}\right\rVert<q^{n}
.
By equation (
4.4
), the element

satisfies

\xi_{e^{\prime}}-\xi_{e_{1}}=\xi_{(t-\theta)^{n}d_{1}-d_{1}^{(1)}}=d_{1}\in\mathscr{R}[t],

since
\left\lVert d_{1}\right\rVert=\left\lVert e^{\prime}\right\rVert\cdot q^{-n}<q^{\frac{n}{q-1}}
.
If
\left\lVert e_{1}\right\rVert<q^{n}
, this is the desired

. Otherwise, we repeat this process to

, to obtain inductively a family of elements
(e_{k})_{k\geq 1}
in
\mathscr{R}[t]
. We claim that there is some

for which
\left\lVert e_{m}\right\rVert<q^{n}
; this implies the result, as then

\xi_{e^{\prime}}-\xi_{e_{m}}=d_{1}+d_{2}+\ldots+d_{m}\in\mathscr{R}[t],

where the

are the corresponding elements in the process. It remains to prove the claim. If at a step, one has
e_{j}=d_{j}^{(1)}+c_{j}
with
\left\lVert e_{j}\right\rVert\geq q^{n}
, then

\left\lVert e_{j}\right\rVert=\left\lVert d_{j}\right\rVert^{q}=\left(\left\lVert e_{j-1}\right\rVert\cdot q^{-n}\right)^{q}.

Hence,

\frac{\,\,\left\lVert e_{j}\right\rVert\,\,}{q^{\frac{nq}{q-1}}}=\frac{\left(\left\lVert e_{j-1}\right\rVert\cdot q^{-n}\right)^{q}}{q^{\frac{nq}{q-1}}}=\frac{\left\lVert e_{j-1}\right\rVert^{q}}{q^{nq}\cdot q^{\frac{nq}{q-1}}}=\left(\frac{\,\,\left\lVert e_{j-1}\right\rVert\,\,}{q^{\frac{nq}{q-1}}}\right)^{q}.

Therefore, since
\left\lVert e^{\prime}\right\rVert<q^{\frac{nq}{q-1}}
, the sequence of quotients
\frac{\,\,\left\lVert e_{j}\right\rVert\,\,}{q^{\frac{nq}{q-1}}}
will go below
\frac{q^{n}}{q^{\frac{nq}{q-1}}}
, hence
(e_{k})_{k}
reaches an element
e_{m}
with
\left\lVert e_{m}\right\rVert<q^{n}
.

We turn to
3
. Uniqueness is again clear from point
1
. So we have to show existence; more precisely, we show that the polynomial

constructed in (

) is as required. Using the functional equation of

, we have

and hence

But since
(t-\theta)^{n}\xi_{e}-\xi_{e}^{(1)}=e
, we get

(t-\theta)^{n}(\xi_{e}-g_{n}+\omega^{n})=(\xi_{e}-g_{n}+\omega^{n})^{(1)}

and hence

. Yet
\left\lVert\xi_{e}-g_{n}+\omega^{n}\right\rVert<1<\left\lVert\omega^{n}\right\rVert
which is only possible if
\xi_{e}-g_{n}+\omega^{n}=0
, and which amounts to
\xi_{e}+\omega^{n}=g_{n}\in\mathscr{O}_{\overline{K}}[t]
.

It remains to prove

. Again, uniqueness is given by point

. Similarly, it is easily verified that

satisfies the given conditions.
∎

In order to explicit generators of

\Psi:=\,^{\operatorname{tr}}\rho_{[k]}\left((\omega^{n}\Xi)^{(1)}\right)^{-1}

which is a solution of the difference equation

\Psi^{(-1)}=\Phi\cdot\Psi

where

\Phi:=\rho_{[k]}(\begin{pmatrix}(t-\theta)^{n}&0&\cdots&\cdots&0\\
-\alpha_{1}&1&\ddots&&\vdots\\
\vdots&0&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
-\alpha_{s}&0&\cdots&0&1\end{pmatrix})\in L[t].

Observe that the determinant of
\Phi
is
(t-\theta)^{n(k+1)}
and hence we can apply Papanikolas’ theorem to this system. Let

T_{k}:=\mathrm{trdeg}_{\overline{K}(t)}~\overline{K}(t)\left(\partial_{t}^{(j)}\!\!\left(\omega^{n}\right),\partial_{t}^{(j)}\!\!\left(\xi_{\alpha_{i}}\right)\,|\,i\in\{1,\ldots,s\},j\in\{0,\ldots,k\}\right).

By Theorem
5.2
,
T_{k}
is
maximal
, that is
T_{k}=(s+1)(k+1)
. As the entries of an invertible matrix and those of its inverse generate the same field, we obtain:

\displaystyle T_{k}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{trdeg}_{\overline{K}(t)}~\overline{K}(t)\left(\rho_{[k]}(\omega^{n}\Xi)^{(1)}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{trdeg}_{\overline{K}(t)}~\overline{K}(t)(\Psi)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{trdeg}_{\overline{K}}~\overline{K}(\Psi(\theta))

\displaystyle=\mathrm{trdeg}_{\overline{K}}~\overline{K}\left(\rho_{[k]}(\omega^{n}\Xi)^{(1)}(\theta)\right)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{trdeg}_{\overline{K}}~\overline{K}\left(\pi_{n}^{[j]},\Li_{n}^{[j]}(\alpha_{i})\,|\,i\in\{1,\ldots,s\},j\in\{0,\ldots,k\}\right).

We deduce that the elements
\pi_{n}^{[j]},\Li_{n}^{[j]}(\alpha_{i})
for
i\in\{1,\ldots,s\},j\in\{0,\ldots,k\}
are algebraically independent over
\overline{K}
, hence over
K
. Since
k\geq 0
was taken arbitrarily, this concludes.
∎

Appendix A
Picard-Vessiot theory for linear difference equations

We present in this section a short handout of difference Galois theory insisting on results that are used in this article. Statements – in perhaps different form or phrasing – can be found in
[
Mai14
, Sect. 2]
. For proofs, one is referred to
[
Mai12
]
. The book of van der Put and Singer
[
PS03
]
is too restrictive in its assumptions to fit our setting. We still recommend it as an introduction to the subject for the interested reader.

A.1
Setup

Let
\mathscr{F}
be a field,
\tau:\mathscr{F}\to\mathscr{F}
be a field endomorphism, and let

\mathsf{k}:=\mathscr{F}^{\tau}:=\{f\in\mathscr{F}\mid\tau(f)=f\}

be the subfield of
\tau
-invariant elements.

Let
r
be a positive integer. Given an invertible matrix
\Theta\in\operatorname{GL}_{r}(\mathscr{F})
, we consider the linear difference equation

\tau\begin{pmatrix}y_{1}\\
\vdots\\
y_{r}\end{pmatrix}=\Theta\cdot\begin{pmatrix}y_{1}\\
\vdots\\
y_{r}\end{pmatrix}.

(A.1)

As is usual in the theory, the notation on the left-hand side means applying
\tau
coefficient-wise to vectors, respectively entry-wise for matrices.

We assume that there exists an integral domain
\mathscr{L}
, containing
\mathscr{F}
as a subring, equipped with an extension of the endomorphism
\tau
to
\mathscr{L}
– which we shall still denote by
\tau
– such that the difference equation (
A.1
) admits a
fundamental solution matrix

\Psi
; that is, there exists
\Psi\in\operatorname{GL}_{r}(\mathscr{L})
satisfying

\tau(\Psi)=\Theta\Psi.

We assume further that
\mathscr{L}^{\tau}=\mathscr{F}^{\tau}=\mathsf{k}
.

Remark A.1
.

Observe that any solution
\Psi_{0}\in\mathsf{Mat}_{r}(\mathscr{L})
of (
A.1
) differs from
\Psi
by a matrix with coefficients in
\mathsf{k}
; indeed,
C=\Psi^{-1}\Psi_{0}
satisfies
\tau(C)=C
.

Example A.2
.

In the core of the paper, we used
\mathscr{F}=F=\mathbb{F}(\theta,t)(y_{1},y_{2},\ldots)
,
\tau:\mathscr{F}\to\mathscr{F}
for the
\mathbb{F}(t)
-algebra endomorphism mapping
\theta\mapsto\theta^{q}
, and
\mathscr{L}
for the ring
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\langle t\rangle
.

A.2
Definitions from Picard-Vessiot theory

The Picard-Vessiot ring.

In view of Remark
A.1
, the subring
\mathscr{R}:=\mathscr{F}[\Psi,\det(\Psi)^{-1}]
of
\mathscr{L}
, generated by the entries of
\Psi
and the inverse of its determinant, does not depend on the choice of the fundamental solution matrix
\Psi
. It is called the
Picard-Vessiot ring
for the difference equation (
A.1
) and will be denoted
\mathscr{R}=\mathscr{F}[\Psi,\Psi^{-1}]
for short.

The Picard-Vessiot ideal
I
.

Let
\mathscr{F}[X,X^{-1}]:=\mathscr{F}[X_{ij},\det(X)^{-1}\mid 0\leq i,j\leq r]
be the localization of the polynomial ring over
\mathscr{F}
in
r^{2}
indeterminates
X_{ij}
, localized at the polynomial
\det(X)
– the determinant of the square matrix
X=(X_{ij})_{i,j}
. We equip this localized polynomial ring with an endomorphism extending
\tau
on
\mathscr{F}
by setting

\tau(X)=\Theta\cdot X

coefficient-wise. Then, by construction, the Picard-Vessiot ring
\mathscr{R}
is the image of the difference homomorphism

\pi:\mathscr{F}[X,X^{-1}]\longrightarrow\mathscr{L},\quad X_{ij}\longmapsto\Psi_{ij}.

This way,
\mathscr{R}
becomes isomorphic, as a difference ring, to the quotient
\mathscr{F}[X,X^{-1}]/I
where
I:=\ker(\pi)
. We name
I\subseteq\mathscr{F}[X,X^{-1}]
the
Picard-Vessiot ideal
.

The difference Galois group.

The
difference Galois group of
\mathscr{R}/\mathscr{F}
, denoted by
\mathscr{G}=\underline{\operatorname{Gal}}(\mathscr{R}|\mathscr{F})
, is given as the group functor

\displaystyle\mathscr{G}:~

\displaystyle\mathsf{Alg}_{\mathsf{k}}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow

\displaystyle\quad\mathsf{Grp},

\displaystyle~\mathsf{A}

\displaystyle\longmapsto

\displaystyle\quad\mathscr{G}(\mathsf{A}):=\left\{\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathscr{F}\otimes_{\mathsf{k}}\mathsf{A}}(\mathscr{R}\otimes_{\mathsf{k}}\mathsf{A})\,\,\middle|\,\,(\tau\otimes\operatorname{id})\circ\alpha=\alpha\circ(\tau\otimes\operatorname{id})\right\}

Any element
\alpha\in\mathscr{G}(\mathsf{A})
acts on the set of solutions of (
A.1
) via
\Psi_{0}\mapsto\alpha(\Psi_{0})
where, once again,
\alpha
is applied entry-wise. By Remark
A.1
, there exists a matrix
C(\alpha)\in\mathsf{Mat}_{r}(\mathsf{k})
such that
\alpha(\Psi)=\Psi C(\alpha)
. If
C(\alpha)=C(\beta)
, then
\alpha(\Psi)=\beta(\Psi)
and thus
\alpha=\beta
on
\mathscr{R}
. Consequently, the map:

C:\mathscr{G}(\mathsf{A})\longrightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{r}(\mathsf{A}),\quad\alpha\longmapsto C(\alpha)

is an injective group homomorphism. It is clear that this embedding is natural in the
\mathsf{k}
-algebra
\mathsf{A}
, thus interpreting the difference Galois group
\underline{\operatorname{Gal}}(\mathscr{R}|\mathscr{F})
as subgroup functor of
\operatorname{GL}_{r/\mathsf{k}}
.

The Hopf ideal
J
.

A main result in difference Galois theory states that
\mathscr{G}
is a closed subgroup of
\operatorname{GL}_{r/\mathsf{k}}
for the Zariski topology. As such it is given as the spectrum of a Hopf algebra. More precisely, in the same way as above, we consider the algebra

\mathsf{k}[Z,Z^{-1}]:=\mathsf{k}[Z_{ij},\det(Z)^{-1}\mid 0\leq i,j\leq r]
as the Hopf algebra associated to
\operatorname{GL}_{r/\mathsf{k}}
, then

\mathscr{G}=\operatorname{Spec}(\mathsf{k}[Z,Z^{-1}]/J),

for some Hopf ideal
J\subseteq\mathsf{k}[Z,Z^{-1}]
.

A.3
Results from Picard-Vessiot theory

The main results in Picard-Vessiot theory are derived from the relation among the Picard-Vessiot ideal
I
and the Hopf ideal
J
that we now describe. Because
\Psi
is invertible, we have an isomorphism of
\mathscr{L}
-algebras

\iota:\mathscr{L}\otimes_{\mathscr{F}}\mathscr{F}[X,X^{-1}]\longrightarrow\mathscr{L}\otimes_{\mathsf{k}}\mathsf{k}[Z,Z^{-1}],\quad 1\otimes_{\mathscr{F}}X_{ij}\longmapsto\sum_{\ell=1}^{r}\Psi_{i\ell}\otimes_{\mathsf{k}}Z_{\ell j},

or rather, in obvious matrix notations,
\operatorname{id}_{r}\otimes_{\mathscr{F}}X\mapsto\Psi\otimes_{\mathsf{k}}Z
. Then, it is a fact that

\iota(\mathscr{L}\otimes_{\mathscr{F}}I)=\mathscr{L}\otimes_{\mathsf{k}}J.

(A.2)

Equivalently, we have an induced isomorphism:

\bar{\iota}:\mathscr{L}\otimes_{\mathscr{F}}\mathscr{R}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathscr{L}\otimes_{\mathsf{k}}\mathsf{k}[\mathscr{G}]

where
\mathsf{k}[\mathscr{G}]:=\mathsf{k}[Z,Z^{-1}]/J
is the ring of global sections of
\mathscr{G}
. In geometric terms, this means that the Picard-Vessiot ring is a principal homogeneous space – or torsor – over the Galois group scheme
\mathscr{G}
.

Remark A.3
.

The isomorphism
\iota
and the induced isomorphism
\bar{\iota}
are indeed compatible with the difference operator
\tau
, in the way that

(\tau\otimes\operatorname{id})\circ\iota=\iota\circ(\tau\otimes\tau).

Let
\bar{Z}_{ij}\in\mathsf{k}[\mathscr{G}]
be the image of
Z_{ij}
modulo
J
. Then the matrix
\bar{Z}=(\bar{Z}_{ij})_{i,j}
is the universal element in
\mathscr{G}(\mathsf{k}[\mathscr{G}])
, i.e. for every
\mathsf{k}
-algebra
\mathsf{A}
, every element of
\mathscr{G}(\mathsf{A})
is a specialization of
\bar{Z}
. The relation among
I
and
J
(c.f. (
A.2
)) has the following consequences which were used in the core of the paper.

Proposition A.4
.

With notation as above.

1.

The transcendence degree of
\mathscr{R}
over
\mathscr{F}
equals the dimension of the algebraic group
\underline{\operatorname{Gal}}(\mathscr{R}/\mathscr{F})
;

2.

For all
i,j\in\{1,...,r\}
:
\bar{Z}_{ij}=\bar{\iota}\left((\Psi^{-1}\otimes\Psi)_{ij}\right)
;

3.

If
\tilde{\mathscr{G}}\subseteq\operatorname{GL}_{r/\mathsf{k}}
is a closed subgroup defined over
\mathsf{k}
such that
\Psi\in\tilde{\mathscr{G}}(\mathscr{L})
, then
\mathscr{G}\subseteq\tilde{\mathscr{G}}
;

4.

Every linear polynomial in
J
corresponds to a linear polynomial in
I
, and vice versa. More precisely, the elements
Z_{1j}
are mapped to a linear combinations of the
X_{1j}
via
\iota^{-1}
. In particular, if
J
contains a linear polynomial
P
in the
Z_{1j}
, then

\iota^{-1}(P)
is a linear polynomial in the
X_{1j}
, and by (
A.2
) is in
\mathscr{L}\otimes_{\mathscr{F}}I
.

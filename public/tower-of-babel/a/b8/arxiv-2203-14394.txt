1.
Introduction

Let
W_{t}
be Brownian motion in the plane started at the origin and let
\theta
be the first exit time of the unit disk
D_{1}
. In
[
6
]
we showed that

(1.1)

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\sup_{x\in D_{1}}\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}\log^{2}(\epsilon)}\int_{0}^{\theta}1_{\{B(x,\epsilon)\}}(W_{t})\,dt=2,\quad a.s.,

where
B(x,\epsilon)
is the ball of radius
\epsilon
centered at
x
. The integral above is the occupation measure of
B(x,\epsilon)
, and points
x
with large occupation measure are referred to as thick points. Taking square roots we can write this as

(1.2)

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\log(\epsilon^{-1})}\sqrt{\sup_{x\in D_{1}}\frac{1}{\pi\epsilon^{2}}\int_{0}^{\theta}1_{\{B(x,\epsilon)\}}(W_{t})\,dt}=\sqrt{2/\pi},\quad a.s.

Let

(1.3)

\mu_{\theta}(x,\epsilon)=\frac{1}{\pi\epsilon^{2}}\int_{0}^{\theta}1_{\{B(x,\epsilon)\}}(W_{t})\,dt,

and set
\mu^{\ast}_{\theta}(\epsilon)=\sup_{x\in D_{1}}\mu_{\theta}(x,\epsilon)
.
Then (
1.2
) says that
\sqrt{\mu^{\ast}_{\theta}(\epsilon)}\sim\sqrt{2/\pi}\log\epsilon^{-1}
, as
\epsilon\to 0
. In this paper we obtain more detailed asymptotics. Let

(1.4)

m_{\epsilon}=\sqrt{2/\pi}\left(\log\epsilon^{-1}-\frac{1}{2}\log\log\epsilon^{-1}\right).

We will say that the thick points in
D_{1}
are tight if
\sqrt{\mu^{\ast}_{\theta}(\epsilon)}-m_{\epsilon}
is a tight family of random variables. That is,

(1.5)

\lim_{K\to\infty}\varlimsup_{\epsilon\to 0}\mathbb{P}\left(\Big|\sqrt{\mu^{\ast}_{\theta}(\epsilon)}-m_{\epsilon}\Big|>K\right)=0.

Theorem 1.1
.

The thick points in
D_{1}
are tight.

In fact we obtain the following improvement on the right tail of (
1.5
), where
c^{\ast}:=2\sqrt{2}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

On
D_{1}
, for some
0<C,C^{\prime},z_{0}<\infty
and all
z\geq z_{0}
,

(1.6)

\varlimsup_{\epsilon\to 0}\mathbb{P}\left(\sqrt{\mu^{\ast}_{\theta}(\epsilon)}-m_{\epsilon}\geq z\right)\leq Cze^{-c^{\ast}\sqrt{\pi}\,z},

and

(1.7)

\varliminf_{\epsilon\to 0}\mathbb{P}\left(\sqrt{\mu^{\ast}_{\theta}(\epsilon)}-m_{\epsilon}\geq z\right)\geq C^{\prime}ze^{-c^{\ast}\sqrt{\pi}\,z}.

It follows from Brownian scaling that Theorems
1.1
and
1.2

hold if
D_{1}
is replaced by any disc centered at the origin.

For reasons of symmetry it is easier to work on the sphere
{\bf S}^{2}
, and derive our results for thick points in
D_{1}
from results for thick points on
{\bf S}^{2}
. We use
B_{d}(x,r)
for the ball centered at
x
of radius
r
, in the spherical metric
d
.

Let
X_{t}
be Brownian motion on
{\bf S}^{2}
started at some point
v
(the ‘South Pole’). For some (small)
r^{\ast}
let
\tau
be the first hitting time of
\partial B_{d}(v,r^{\ast})
(the ‘Antarctic Circle’). Let
\omega_{\epsilon}=2\pi(1-\cos\epsilon)
, the area of
B_{d}(x,\epsilon)
, and set

(1.8)

\bar{\mu}_{\tau}(x,\epsilon)=\frac{1}{\omega_{\epsilon}}\int_{0}^{\tau}1_{\{B_{d}(x,\epsilon)\}}(X_{t})\,dt,

With
\bar{\mu}_{\tau,\epsilon}^{\ast}=\sup_{x\in{\bf S}^{2}}\bar{\mu}_{\tau}(x,\epsilon)
we will say that the thick points on
{\bf S}^{2}
are tight if
\sqrt{\bar{\mu}_{\tau,\epsilon}^{\ast}}-m_{\epsilon}
is a tight family of random variables.

Theorem 1.3
.

The thick points on
{\bf S}^{2}
are tight.

As in Theorem
1.2
we obtain the following improvement for the right tail.

Theorem 1.4
.

On
{\bf S}^{2}
, for some
0<C,C^{\prime},z_{0}<\infty
and all
z\geq z_{0}
,

(1.9)

\varlimsup_{\epsilon\to 0}\mathbb{P}\left(\sqrt{\bar{\mu}_{\tau,\epsilon}^{\ast}}-m_{\epsilon}\geq z\right)\leq Cze^{-c^{\ast}\sqrt{\pi}\,z},

and

(1.10)

\varliminf_{\epsilon\to 0}\mathbb{P}\left(\sqrt{\bar{\mu}_{\tau,\epsilon}^{\ast}}-m_{\epsilon}\geq z\right)\geq C^{\prime}ze^{-c^{\ast}\sqrt{\pi}\,z}.

Theorems
1.3
and
1.4
are stated and first proven for
r^{\ast}
sufficiently small.
In Section
8
we show that they hold for any
0<r^{\ast}<\pi
.

In analogy with
[
6
]
, rather than work directly with occupation measures,
we work with excursion counts. To define this let

h_{l}=2\arctan(r_{0}e^{-l}/2)
with
r_{0}
small. For some
d_{0}\leq 1/1000
let
F_{l}
be the centers of a
d_{0}h_{l}
covering of
{\bf S}^{2}
.

Let
\mathcal{T}_{x,l}^{\tau}
be the number of excursions from
\partial B_{d}\left(x,h_{l-1}\right)
to
\partial B_{d}\left(x,h_{l}\right)
prior to
\tau
.
We will obtain the following result for
\sup_{x\in F_{L}}\mathcal{T}_{x,L}^{\tau}
.

Theorem 1.5
.

On
{\bf S}^{2}
, for some
0<z_{0},C,C^{\prime}<\infty
, all
L
large and all
z_{0}\leq z\leq\log L
,

(1.11)

C^{\prime}ze^{-2z}\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{x\in F_{L}}\sqrt{2\mathcal{T}_{x,L}^{\tau}}-(2L-\log L)\geq z\right)\leq Cze^{-2z}.

Equivalently

(1.12)

C^{\prime}ze^{-2z}\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{x\in F_{L}}\mathcal{T}_{x,L}^{\tau}\geq 2L\left(L-\log L+z\right)\right)\leq Cze^{-2z}.

Since
L\sim\log h_{L}^{-1}
, Theorem
1.5
is then suggestive of Theorem
1.4
if we knew that on average the occupation measure of
B_{d}\left(x,h_{L}\right)
during an excursion from
\partial B_{d}\left(x,h_{L}\right)
to
\partial B_{d}\left(x,h_{L-1}\right)
was ‘about’
h^{2}_{L}
. While this is basically known for our choices of
h_{L},h_{L-1}
, see
[
6
, Lemma 6.2]
, it is more delicate to get the precision necessary to show the equivalence of Theorem
1.5
with (
1.9
).

We now write (
1.11
) in a more convenient form.
Set

(1.13)

\rho_{L}=2-\frac{\log L}{L}.

We will prove the following version of Theorem
1.5
.

Theorem 1.6
.

On
{\bf S}^{2}
, for some
0<z_{0},C,C^{\prime}<\infty
, all
L
large and all
z_{0}\leq z\leq\log L
,

(1.14)

C^{\prime}ze^{-2z}\leq\mathbb{P}\left[\sup_{x\in F_{L}}\sqrt{2\mathcal{T}_{x,L}^{\tau}}\,\geq\rho_{L}L+z\right]\leq Cze^{-2z}.

1.1.
Background

This paper is based in many ways on my work
[
4
]
with Belius and Zeituni on tightness for the cover time of
{\bf S}^{2}
. The general approach is similar, and whenever results of that paper could be used directly I did so. However, the mathematics often necessitated different arguments.

The family

\{\bar{\mu}_{\tau}(x,\epsilon);x\in B_{d}(v,r^{\ast}),\epsilon>0\}

is associated with a second order Gaussian chaos

H(x,\epsilon)
,
x\in B_{d}(v,r^{\ast}),\epsilon>0
by an isomorphism
theorem of Dynkin
[
12
]
. Intuitively,

(1.15)

H(x,\epsilon)=\int_{B_{d}(x,\epsilon)}G^{2}_{y}\,dm(y)-E\left(\int_{B_{d}(x,\epsilon)}G^{2}_{y}\,dm(y)\right)

where
G_{x}
is the mean zero Gaussian process with covariance
u(x,y)
, the Green’s function for
B_{d}(v,r^{\ast})
and
m
denotes the standard surface measure on
S^{2}
. Since
u(x,x)=\infty
for all
x
, (
1.15
) is not a priori well defined. Nevertheless, this would suggest that there is a close relationship between
\bar{\mu}_{\tau,\epsilon}^{\ast}=\sup_{x\in R^{2}}\bar{\mu}_{\tau}(x,\epsilon)
and the supremum of Gaussian fields. For details on
H
and the isomorphism
theorem see
[
19
, Section 2]
.

For related work see
[
1
,
15
,

,

,

]
.

(2.1)

and let

(2.2)

for some
r_{0}<1
. We can take
r_{0}<1
sufficiently small that for all

(2.3)

x-x^{3}\leq h(x)\leq x\hskip 14.45377pt\mbox{and}\hskip 14.45377pt|h^{\prime}(x)-1|\leq x^{2}.

For some
d_{0}\leq 1/1000

be the centers of an
d_{0}h_{l}

.
We record for future
use that

(2.4)

\left|F_{l}\right|\asymp cr_{l}^{-2}=cr^{-2}_{0}e^{2l},l\geq 0.

is the number of excursions from
\partial B_{d}\left(x,h_{l-1}\right)
to
\partial B_{d}\left(x,h_{l}\right)

\tau

so that for all

{\bf S}^{2}
, see
[
4

. For any

(2.5)

\mathbb{P}^{x\in\partial B_{d}\left(0,h(u_{2})\right)}\left[H_{\partial B_{d}\left(0,h(u_{1})\right)}<H_{\partial B_{d}\left(0,h(u_{3})\right)}\right]=\frac{\log\left(\frac{u_{2}}{u_{3}}\right)}{\log\left(\frac{u_{1}}{u_{3}}\right)}.

The next Lemma provides simple bounds which will be adequate to handle points which are close to the ‘South Pole‘
v

Lemma 2.1
.

For
L

y\in B_{d}^{c}\left(v,h_{k}\right)
and all

\mathbb{P}\left[\sqrt{2\mathcal{T}_{y,L}^{\tau}}\,\geq\rho_{L}L+z\right]\leq cke^{-2L}Le^{-2z},

for some

1\leq k\leq L-1

If

(2.7)

\mathbb{P}\left[\sqrt{2\mathcal{T}_{y,L}^{\tau}}\,\geq\rho_{L}L+z\right]\leq ce^{-2L}L^{2}e^{-2z}.

Proof:

For
k\leq l-1
, let
\mathcal{T}_{y,l}^{k\to 0}
be the number of excursions from
\partial B_{d}\left(y,h_{l-1}\right)
to
\partial B_{d}\left(y,h_{l}\right)

H_{\partial B_{d}\left(y,h_{k}\right)}
and
H_{\partial B_{d}\left(y,h_{0}\right)}

\mathcal{T}_{y,l}^{k\to 0}

Using (
2.5

\partial B_{d}\left(y,h_{k}\right)

, and then the probability to hit

before exiting

is

. Thus, using the strong Markov property,

(2.8)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\mathcal{T}_{y,l}^{k\to 0}\geq n\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{k}{l-1}\left(1-\frac{1}{l}\right)^{n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{k}{l-1}e^{-\frac{n}{l}},

for
n
large. Since (recall (
1.13
))

(2.9)

\displaystyle(\rho_{L}L+z)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\rho_{L}L)^{2}+2z\rho_{L}L+z^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4L^{2}-4L\log L+4zL+z^{2}-2z\log L+\log^{2}L,

it follows that for
L
large

(2.10)

\mathbb{P}\left[\sqrt{2\mathcal{T}_{y,L}^{k\to 0}}\,\geq\rho_{L}L+z\right]\leq cke^{-2L}Le^{-2z}.

) we note that for

we have

.

Lemma 2.2
.

For
L

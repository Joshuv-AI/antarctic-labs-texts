Convergence of the volume preserving fractional mean curvature flow for convex sets
Vesa Julin, Domenico Angelo La Manna

Abstract
. We prove that the volume preserving fractional mean curvature flow starting from a convex set does not develop singularities along the flow. By the recent result of Cesaroni-Novaga [ 6 ] this then implies that the flow converges to a ball exponentially fast. In the proof we show that the a priori estimates due to Cinti-Sinestrari-Valdinoci [ 10 ] imply the C 1 + α C^{1+\alpha} -regularity of the flow and then provide a regularity argument which improves this to C 2 + α C^{2+\alpha} -regularity of the flow. The regularity step from C 1 + α C^{1+\alpha} into C 2 + α C^{2+\alpha} does not rely on convexity and can be adopted to more general setting.



1.
Introduction

We say that a given set
E_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
evolves under the volume preserving fractional mean curvature flow if there exists a flow
(E_{t})_{t\in[0,T)}
of sets starting from
E_{0}
, which satisfies the equation

(1.1)

V_{t}=-(H_{E_{t}}^{s}-\bar{H}_{E_{t}}^{s})\qquad\text{on }\,\partial E_{t},

where
V_{t}
is the normal velocity,
H_{E}^{s}
with
s\in(0,1)
is the fractional mean curvature defined as

(1.2)

H_{E}^{s}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus E}\frac{dy}{|y-x|^{n+1+s}}-\int_{E}\frac{dy}{|y-x|^{n+1+s}}

and
\bar{H}_{E_{t}}^{s}
is its integral average. The flow (
1.1
) bears many similarities to the classical volume preserving mean curvature flow, in particular, it can be seen as a gradient flow of the fractional perimeter. The flow (
1.1
) can also be seen as a perturbation of the fractional mean curvature flow, with the difference that (
1.1
) preserves the volume and can thus be interpreted as the evolutionary counterpart of the fractional isoperimetric inequality.

The study of the fractional perimeter problem goes back to
[
3
]
, where the authors prove so called
\varepsilon
-regularity result, i.e., partial regularity of the perimeter minimizers. As with minimal surfaces, also in the fractional case the perimeter minimizers may have singularities in higher dimensions. The question of optimal regularity has been studied extensively and we merely refer to
[
25
]
, which is the best known result on the size of the singular set at the moment. We also refer to
[
1
]
, where the authors provide a bootstrap argument to obtain higher order regularity outside the singular set. The related isoperimetric problem, which states that the ball minimizes the fractional perimeter under the volume constraint, follows from standard symmetrization argument and the sharp quantitative version is proven in
[
15
]
(see also
[
16
]
). Moreover, the fractional version of the Alexandrov theorem, which states that the only regular set with constant fractional mean curvature is the ball, is proven independently in
[
2
,
11
]
. This is important as it essentially implies that, if the flow (
1.1
) is well defined for all times, then it converges to the ball.

In
[
19
]
we prove the short time existence of the classical solution of (
1.1
) for
C^{1,1}
-regular initial sets. This result also applies to the fractional mean curvature flow, in which case the existence of the level set solution is proven in
[
18
]
and another weak solution is constructed in
[
7
]
. As usual with geometric flows, the fractional mean curvature flow may develop singularities in finite time such as neckpinching
[
9
]
and fattening
[
4
]
. However, at the moment there is no systematic classification of the possible types of singularities in the fractional setting similar to the classical mean curvature flow
[
21
]
. We are also not aware of a construction of a weak solution for (
1.1
).

A natural problem is whether we may avoid the formation of singularities, if we constraint ourselves to special type of initial sets. In
[
6
]
the authors prove, that if the initial set is close enough to the ball, then the flow (
1.1
) remains smooth and converges exponentially fast to a translation of the ball. Another special case is when the initial set is a subgraph of a Lipschitz function. Note that then the set is unbounded and therefore, instead of (
1.1
), it is natural to study the standard fractional mean curvature flow. In this setting the authors
[
24
]
prove important a priori estimates, which essentially imply that if the flow starts from Lipschitz graph with bounded fractional curvature, these quantities are preserved along the flow. This problem is studied further in
[
5
]
, where the authors show that in the above setting the fractional mean curvature flow does not develop singularities and converges to the flat surface exponentially fast. This is the fractional counterpart of the Ecker-Huisken result
[
13
]
for the mean curvature flow with the only difference that the initial set is assumed to have bounded fractional curvature. Due to the results
[
22
,
26
]
one might expect the result to hold without the curvature assumption, but to the best of our knowledge this is not known at the moment.

As we already mentioned, here we consider the case of convex initial sets, which are bounded and
C^{1,1}
-regular. In the case of the classical volume preserving mean curvature flow the result by Huisken
[
17
]
implies that the flow remains smooth and converges expontially fast to the ball. In the fractional setting, this result is proven in
[
6
]
and
[
8
]
under the additional assumption that the flow remains
C^{2+\alpha}
-regular. Here we show that this additional regularity assumption is conditional, or in other words, this regularity assumption can proven for convex initial sets, and thus we obtain the Huisken result in the fractional setting.

Main Theorem
.

Let
E_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be
C^{1,1}
-regular convex set with
|E_{0}|=|B_{1}|
. Then the classical solution of (
1.1
) exists for all times
(E_{t})_{t\geq 0}
and converges to the ball
B_{1}(x_{0})
, for some
x_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, in
C^{k}
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
exponentially fast. To be more precise, there is
T\geq 1
depending on the initial set
E_{0}
and on the dimension, such that we may write the moving boundaries by the height functions over the unit sphere
h(\cdot,t):\mathbb{S}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
as

\partial E_{t}=\{h(x,t)x+x_{0}:x\in\mathbb{S}^{n}\}

for
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
when
t\geq T
and it holds
\|h(\cdot,t)-1\|_{C^{k}(\mathbb{S}^{n})}\leq C_{k}e^{-c_{k}t}
,
where the constants
c_{k}>0
and
C_{k}\geq 1
depend on
n,k,T
and on the initial set
E_{0}
.

Let us comment the proof of the Main Theorem. First, we remark that the
C^{1,1}
assumption on
E_{0}
is needed only to guarantee the existence of the flow, as our proof relies on the short time existence result in
[
19
]
.
As we mentioned above, the proof relies on the a priori estimates proven in
[
6
,
8
,
10
]
. Indeed, by the result in
[
8
]
the sets
E_{t}
along the flow remain convex, while the estimates in
[
10
]
imply that their fractional mean curvature remains uniformly bounded, i.e.

\sup_{t\in[0,T)}H_{E_{t}}^{s}(x)\leq C

and there are radii
0<r<1<R
such that
B_{r}(x_{t})\subset E_{t}\subset B_{R}(x_{t})
as long as the flow is classically defined. These important estimates are our starting point and we first prove in Proposition
2.3
that for convex sets the above fractional curvature bound implies uniform
C^{1+s}
-Hölder regularity. We then recall that the authors in
[
6
]
(see also
[
10
]
) show that, if we would be able to improve the
C^{1+s}
-regularity into uniform
C^{2+\alpha}
-regularity, then the flow (
1.1
) does not develop singularities and converges exponentially fast to a ball. Our main contribution here is to prove this regularity step.
Roughly speaking, the idea is that geometric flows do not develop singularities under uniform
C^{1+\alpha}
-regularity estimate, unless the singularity is due to change of topology. This is due to the fact that one may parametrize the equation such that it becomes a quasilinear PDE. We use this idea in
[
20
]
in a more complicated equation, and since we know that in our case the flow (
1.1
) remains convex, and thus the topology does not change, we may adopt it in this setting.

The first technical issue in the proof is to find a good parametrization of the flow. The idea is to differentiate the equation (
1.1
) with respect to space and parametrize the equation

\nabla_{X(t)}V_{t}=-\nabla_{X(t)}H_{E_{t}^{s}}\qquad\text{on }\,\partial E_{t},

where
X(t)
is a vector field on the moving boundary
\partial E_{t}
. By doing this we obtain a quasilinear PDE for the derivative of the height function, where all the error terms are lower order. We point out that we could also first parametrize the original equation (
1.1
) by the height function and differentiate that, but this leads to much more complicated calculations. We then improve some of the methods developed in
[
19
]
and prove the
C^{2+\alpha}
regularity of the flow in Section 5. The main difference to
[
19
]
is that here we need sharp estimates for the error terms in order to show that they do not blow up in finite time. Another issue is that here our equation is not a small parturbation of the fractional heat equation with respect to the sphere, and we need Schauder estimates for general linear parabolic equations defined on the unit sphere (see Section 4). Similar Schauder estimates are proven in
[
22
]
in the case of evolving periodic graphs.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we recall some results in the existing literature and prove the
C^{1+s}
-estimate of the flow. Section 3 is mostly devoted to the calculations of the parametrization of the equation. In Section 4 we generalize the Schauder estimates for general linear parabolic equations in the Euclidean setting proven in
[
23
]
(see Theorem
2.2
) to the case of the sphere (see Theorem
4.1
). Since the result in the Euclidian setting is scattered in literature, we decide to give a self-contained proof in the Appendix. Finally in Section 5 we prove that
C^{1+s}
-regularity of the flow implies uniform
C^{2+s+\alpha}
-regularity, which concludes the proof of the Main Theorem.

2.
Notation and preliminary results

Throughout the paper
C\geq 1
and
c>0
stand for generic constants which may change from line to line. If needed, we specify their dependence on relevant parameters. We denote the open ball with radius
r
centered at
x
by
B_{r}(x)
and by
B_{r}
if it is centered at the origin. The notation does not specify the dimension of the ball, but in order to avoid possible confusion, we write
B_{r}(x)\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
to point out that the ball is
k
-dimensional. We denote the inner product of
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
by
\langle x,y\rangle
.

For a given smooth and bounded set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
the fractional mean curvature of order
s\in(0,1)
at
x\in\partial E
is defined as (
1.2
)
in the principal valued sense. In order to define the normal velocity we say that a family of smooth sets
(E_{t})_{t\in[0,T)}
is a smooth flow starting from
E_{0}
, if there is a family of diffeomorphisms
\Phi_{t}:\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\to\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
such that
t\mapsto\Phi_{t}(x)
is smooth,
\Phi_{t}(E_{0})=E_{t}
and
\Phi_{0}(x)=x
. The normal velocity at
y=\Phi_{t}(x)\in\partial E_{t}
is then defined as
V_{t}(y)=\langle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Phi_{t}(x),\nu_{E_{t}}(y)\rangle
. Finally we say that a smooth flow
(E_{t})_{t\in[0,T)}
starting from
E_{0}
is a solution to (
1.1
), if it satisfies the associated equation point-wise.

We recall that if the initial set
E_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is bounded and
C^{1,1}
-regular, i.e., its boundary is
C^{1,1}
-regular hypersurface, then by
[
19
]
the equation (
1.1
) has a solution for a short time
(E_{t})_{t\in[0,T)}
and the sets
E_{t}
for
t>0
are smooth (
C^{\infty}
-regular hypersurfaces). In this paper we restrict to convex initial sets
E_{0}
, which are
C^{1,1}
-regular. Then by the results in
[
8
,
10
]
we know that the sets
E_{t}
remain convex, there are
r>0,R>1
such that
B_{r}(x_{t})\subset E_{t}\subset B_{R}(x_{t})
for some points
x_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and it holds

\sup_{t\in(0,T)}\sup_{x\in\partial E_{t}}H_{E_{t}}^{s}(x)\leq C,

where the constant is independent of the time of existence
T
. We remark that by these results, we may parametrize the flow via the height function over the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, which means that for every
t\in[0,T)
there is a function
h(\cdot,t):\mathbb{S}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

(2.1)

\partial E_{t}=\{h(x,t)x+x_{t}:x\in\mathbb{S}^{n}\}

for some
x_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Moreover there are
c>0
and
C\geq 1
such that
c\leq h(x,t)\leq C
for every
x\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
. We show later in Proposition
2.3
, that the above estimate on the fractional mean curvature implies that the functions
h(\cdot,t)
are uniformly
C^{1+s}
-regular.

Recall that our aim is to improve the
C^{1+s}
-regularity into
C^{2+\alpha}
-regularity. In other words, we show that the height functions
h(\cdot,t)
defined in (
2.1
) are in fact, uniformly
C^{2+s+\alpha}
regular for small
\alpha>0
.

2.1.
Hölder norms and Schauder estimate

The notation
\nabla^{k}F
stands for the
k
:th order differential of a vector field
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
. For matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times k}
we denote by
|A|
its Frobenius norm. We define the usual Hölder norms of
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
by

\|F\|_{C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|F(x)|\quad\text{and}\quad\|F\|_{C^{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\sup_{x\neq y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|F(y)-F(x)|}{|y-x|^{\beta}}+\|F\|_{C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for
\beta\in(0,1]
. We extend this to
C^{l}
-norm, for
l=k+\beta
, with
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\beta\in[0,1)
as

\|F\|_{C^{l}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\|\nabla^{j}F\|_{C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|\nabla^{k}F\|_{C^{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

We then have the standard interpolation inequality
[
27
, Section 2.7]
i.e., assume
l_{1}
and
l_{2}
are positive numbers,
\theta\in(0,1)
and denote

l=\theta l_{1}+(1-\theta)l_{2}.

Then there is a constant
C\geq 1
such that for every smooth and bounded function
u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
it holds

(2.2)

\|u\|_{C^{l}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|u\|_{C^{l_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\theta}\|u\|_{C^{l_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{1-\theta}.

We will mostly deal with functions defined on the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, which we interpret as the boundary of the unit ball
\partial B_{1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Since
\mathbb{S}^{n}
is embedded in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, we may extend every continuously differentiable vector field
F:\mathbb{S}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
to
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and denote the extension still by
F
. We define the tangential differential of
F
at
x\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
as

\nabla_{\tau}F(x):=\nabla F(x)-(\nabla F(x)x)\otimes x

and note that it does not depend on the chosen extension. For real valued functions
u:\mathbb{S}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
we define the tangential gradient
\nabla_{\tau}u
as the transpose of the tangential differential, and define the tangential Hessian of
u:\mathbb{S}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
as
\nabla_{\tau}^{2}u(x)=\nabla_{\tau}(\nabla_{\tau}u)(x)
. If
e_{i}
is a basis vector for
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
we denote

\nabla_{i}u=\langle\nabla_{\tau}u,e_{i}\rangle

for the tangential partial derivative and
\nabla_{j}\nabla_{i}u=\langle\nabla_{\tau}^{2}u\,e_{j},e_{i}\rangle
. We may then define the third order tangential derivatives as
\nabla_{\tau}\nabla_{j}\nabla_{i}u
and iteratively for every
k=3,4,\dots
. We note that such a definition of
\nabla_{\tau}^{k}u
does not agree with the standard definition of a covariant derivative.

We define the Hölder norms on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
as in the Euclidian setting. That is for
u:\mathbb{S}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
we define

\|u\|_{C^{0}(\mathbb{S}^{n})}:=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{n}}|u(x)|\quad\text{and}\quad\|u\|_{C^{\beta}(\mathbb{S}^{n})}:=\sup_{x\neq y\in\mathbb{S}^{n}}\frac{|u(y)-u(x)|}{|y-x|^{\beta}}+\|u\|_{C^{0}(\mathbb{S}^{n})}

for
\beta\in(0,1]
and for
C^{l}
-norm, with
l=k+\beta
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\beta\in[0,1)
, as

\|u\|_{C^{l}(\mathbb{S}^{n})}:=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\|\nabla_{\tau}^{j}u\|_{C^{0}(\mathbb{S}^{n})}+\|\nabla_{\tau}^{k}u\|_{C^{\beta}(\mathbb{S}^{n})}.

The interpolation inequality (
2.2
) extends immediately to
\mathbb{S}^{n}
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Assume
l_{1}
and
l_{2}
are positive numbers,
\theta\in(0,1)
and denote

l=\theta l_{1}+(1-\theta)l_{2}.

Then there is a constant
C\geq 1
such that for every smooth function
u:\mathbb{S}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
it holds

\|u\|_{C^{l}(\mathbb{S}^{n})}\leq C\|u\|_{C^{l_{1}}(\mathbb{S}^{n})}^{\theta}\|u\|_{C^{l_{2}}(\mathbb{S}^{n})}^{1-\theta}.

Let us return to functions defined in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We recall the definition of the fractional Laplacian of order
\gamma\in(0,2)

\Delta^{\gamma}u(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{u(y+x)-u(x)}{|y|^{n+\gamma}}\,dy,

which has to be interpreted in the principal valued sense. We need parabolic Schauder estimates for equations with general linear fractional-type operator of order
\gamma=1+s
. To this aim we say that
A(\cdot):\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
is
symmetric and elliptic
matrix field, if there are constants
\lambda>0
and
\Lambda\geq 1
such that for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
the matrix
A(x)
is symmetric and satisfies

(2.3)

\lambda|\xi|^{2}\leq\langle A(x)\xi,\xi\rangle\leq\Lambda|\xi|^{2}\qquad\text{for all }\,x,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

For a symmetric and elliptic matrix field
A(\cdot)
we define linear operator as

(2.4)

L_{A}[u](x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{u(y+x)-u(x)}{\langle A(x)y,y\rangle^{\frac{n+1+s}{2}}}\,dy.

Our aim is to give a simple proof of Schauder estimate
for the Cauchy problem

(2.5)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u(x,t)=L_{A}[u]+f(x,t)\\
u(x,0)=0,\end{cases}

where
A(\cdot,t)
is symmetric, elliptic and uniformly Hölder continuous for every
t\in[0,T)
.

The following Schauder estimate can be found for instance in
[
23
]
. For the sake of completeness and for the reader’s convenience, we provide a proof based purely on PDE methods in the Appendix.

Theorem 2.2
.

Assume
\alpha>0
is such that
\alpha<\min\{s,1-s\}
and fix
T>0
. Assume that for every
t\in[0,T)
,
A(\cdot,t)
is symmetric and elliptic, and assume that
\sup_{t<T}\|A(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C_{0}
. Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times[0,T)\to\mathbb{R}
be such that
\sup_{t<T}\|f(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}<\infty
and assume
u
is the solution of (
2.5
) such that
\lim_{|x|\to\infty}u(x,t)\leq C
. Then there exists a constant
C_{T}
, depending on
\alpha,\lambda,\Lambda,C_{0},\gamma,T
and on the dimension, such that

\sup_{t<T}\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{1+s+\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C_{T}\sup_{t<T}\|f(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

We will prove later, in Theorem
4.1
, that the analogous result holds on the sphere.

2.2.
Preliminary results

As we already mentioned, we know that the evolving sets
E_{t}
remain convex and satisfy the a priori estimates from
[
10
]
stated at the beginning of this section. Next we show that these a priori estimates imply that the sets
E_{t}
are uniformly
C^{1+s}
-regular.

Proposition 2.3
.

Assume that
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is a convex set such that
B_{r}\subset E\subset B_{R}
and

\sup_{x\in\partial E}H_{E}^{s}(x)\leq C.

Then
E
is uniformly
C^{1+s}
-regular and the boundary can be written as

\partial E=\{h(x)x:x\in\mathbb{S}^{n}\}

with
\|h\|_{C^{1+s}(\mathbb{S}^{n})}\leq C
.

Proof.

The assumptions on
E
imply that there is
r_{0}>0
such that for every
x\in\partial E
we may write the boundary locally as a graph of a bounded convex function, i.e.,
\partial E\cap B_{r_{0}}(x)
is contained on graph of a convex function which is uniformly bounded. Our goal is to prove

(2.6)

\sup_{y\neq x\in\partial E}\frac{\langle x-y,\nu_{E}(x)\rangle}{|y-x|^{1+s}}\leq C.

The estimate (
2.6
) will then imply the claim by the following argument. Indeed, let us fix a point
x_{0}\in\partial E
and without loss of generality we may assume
x_{0}=0
and
\nu_{E}(x_{0})=-e_{n+1}
. As we mentioned above, we may write

\partial E\cap B_{r_{0}}\subset\{(x^{\prime},u(x^{\prime}))\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}:x^{\prime}\in B_{r_{0}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\}

for a bounded convex function
u:B_{r_{0}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
. Let us choose
x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\in B_{r_{0}/2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Note that since
u
is convex and bounded in
B_{r_{0}}
, it is uniformly Lipschitz continuous in
B_{r_{0}/2}
. Then (
2.6
) implies

\begin{split}\langle\big(y^{\prime}-x^{\prime}&,u(y^{\prime})-u(x^{\prime})\big),(-\nabla u(x^{\prime}),1)\rangle\\
&\leq C\sqrt{1+|\nabla u(x^{\prime})|^{2}}(|y^{\prime}-x^{\prime}|^{2}+(u(y^{\prime})-u(x^{\prime}))^{2})^{\frac{1+s}{2}}\leq C|y^{\prime}-x^{\prime}|^{1+s}.\end{split}

In other words

\langle u(y^{\prime})-u(x^{\prime})-\nabla u(x^{\prime}),y^{\prime}-x^{\prime}\rangle\leq C|y^{\prime}-x^{\prime}|^{1+s}\quad\text{for every }\,x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\in B_{r_{0}/2}.

Since
u
is convex, then
u(y^{\prime})-u(x^{\prime})\geq\langle\nabla u(x^{\prime}),y^{\prime}-x^{\prime}\rangle
.
The above then implies that
\|u\|_{C^{1+s}(B_{r_{0}/4})}\leq C
(see e.g.
(H4)
in
[
14
, Appendix A]
). Hence, we need to prove (
2.6
).

We argue by contradiction and assume that there are sets
E_{k}
which satisfy the assumptions and points
y_{k}\neq x_{k}\in\partial E_{k}
such that
|y_{k}-x_{k}|=r_{k}\leq 2^{-4n}r_{0}
, with
r_{k}\to 0
, and

(2.7)

\frac{\langle x_{k}-y_{k},\nu_{E_{k}}(x_{k})\rangle}{|y_{k}-x_{k}|^{1+s}}\geq k.

Again we may assume that
x_{k}=0
,
\nu_{E_{k}}(0)=-e_{n+1}
. Since
E_{k}
is convex then

E_{k}\subset\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}:\langle y-y_{k},\nu_{E_{k}}(y_{k})\rangle\leq 0\}.

Then the following wedge-type region

U_{k}:=\{y\in B_{2r_{k}}:y_{n+1}>0,\,\,\langle y-y_{k},\nu_{E_{k}}(y_{k})\rangle>0\}

is contained in the complement of
E_{k}
, i.e.,
U_{k}\cap E_{k}=\emptyset
. Note that (
2.7
) implies that

\langle y_{k},e_{n+1}\rangle\geq\,kr_{k}^{1+s}.

Therefore we deduce that there is a uniform constant
c>0
such that

(2.8)

|U_{k}|\geq c\,k\,r_{k}^{n+1+s}.

Recall the definition of the fractional mean curvature

H_{E_{k}}^{s}(0)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus E_{k}}\frac{dy}{|y|^{n+1+s}}-\int_{E_{k}}\frac{dy}{|y|^{n+1+s}}\leq C.

By convexity and by
\nu_{E_{k}}(0)=-e_{n+1}
it holds
E_{k}\subset\{y:y_{n+1}\geq 0\}
. Therefore, denoting by
\tilde{E}_{k}
the reflection of
E_{k}
with respect to the hyperplane
\{x_{n+1}=0\}
, we obtain that
\tilde{E}_{k}\subset E_{k}^{c}
and

where the constant

does not depend on

. Hence, we obtain a contradiction when

At the end of the section we prove continuity estimates for functions defined via convolution over kernels. We define elliptic and symmetric matrix field on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
,
A(\cdot):\mathbb{S}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\times\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
as in (
2.3
), i.e., there are
0<\lambda<1<\Lambda
such that

\lambda|\xi|^{2}\leq\langle A(x)\xi,\xi\rangle\leq\Lambda|\xi|^{2}\quad\text{for all }\,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\,\text{and }\,x\in\mathbb{S}^{n}.

We consider kernels
K:\mathbb{S}^{n}\times\mathbb{S}^{n}\setminus\{(x,x):x\in\mathbb{S}^{n}\}\to\mathbb{R}
of the type

(2.9)

K_{A}(y,x)=\frac{1}{\langle A(x)(y-x),(y-x)\rangle^{\frac{n+1+s}{2}}},

where
A(\cdot)
is symmetric, elliptic and
\|A\|_{C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{n})}\leq C
. In order to simplify the notation, we denote the induced norm by

(2.10)

\|\xi\|_{A(x)}^{2}:=\langle A(x)\xi,\xi\rangle\qquad\text{for }\,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}.

We may then denote
K
defined in (
2.9
) as
K(y,x)=\|y-x\|_{A(x)}^{-n-1-s}
.

The following lemma is easy to prove and thus we omit it.

Lemma 2.4
.

Assume that
A(\cdot)
is symmetric, elliptic and
\|A\|_{C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{n})}\leq C
for
0<\alpha<\min\{s,1-s\}
. Then the kernel
K_{A}
defined in (
2.9
) satisfies the following two conditions:

(i)

K_{A}
is continuous on
\mathbb{S}^{n}\times\mathbb{S}^{n}\setminus\{(x,x):x\in\mathbb{S}^{n}\}
and satisfies

|K_{A}(y,x)|\leq\frac{C}{|y-x|^{n+1+s}}.

(ii)

For all
x,y,z\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
with
0<|z-x|\leq\frac{1}{2}|y-x|
it holds

|K_{A}(y,z)-K_{A}(y,x)|\leq C\frac{|z-x|}{|y-x|^{n+2+s}}+C\frac{|z-x|^{\alpha}}{|y-x|^{n+1+s}}.

We note that the above conditions agree with the assumptions in
[
19
, Definition 4.1]
with the difference that the second condition is slightly weaker.
The following lemma is almost the same as
[
19
, Lemma 4.3]
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Assume that
A(\cdot)
is symmetric, elliptic and
\|A\|_{C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{n})}\leq C
for
0<\alpha<\min\{s,1-s\}
, and the kernel
K_{A}
is defined in (
2.9
). Assume that the function
F\in C(\mathbb{S}^{n}\times\mathbb{S}^{n})
satisfies the following conditions.

(1)

For all
x,y\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
it holds

|F(y,x)|\leq\kappa_{0}|y-x|^{1+s+\alpha}.

(2)

For all
x,y,z\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
with
|z-x|\leq\frac{1}{2}|y-x|
it holds

|F(y,z)-F(y,x)|\leq\kappa_{0}\big(|z-x|^{\alpha}|y-x|^{1+s+\alpha/2}+|z-x|^{s+\alpha}|y-x|\big).

Then the function

\psi(x)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}F(y,x)K_{A}(y,x)\,d\mathcal{H}_{y}^{n}

is Hölder continuous and
\|\psi\|_{C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{n})}\leq C\kappa_{0}
.

Proof.

The proof is almost the same as in
[
19
, Lemma 4.3]
but we write it for the reader’s convenience since the assumptions are slightly different. The condition (i) in Lemma
2.4
and the assumption (1) for
F
immediately implies that
\|\psi\|_{C^{0}}\leq C\kappa_{0}
. To show the Hölder continuity we fix
z,x\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
and divide the sphere into two sets as
S_{-}=\{y\in\mathbb{S}^{n}:|y-x|\leq 2|z-x|\}
and
S_{+}=\{y\in\mathbb{S}^{n}:|y-x|>2|z-x|\}
. Note that for
y\in S_{-}
it holds
|y-z|\leq|y-x|+|z-x|\leq 3|z-x|
. We use the condition (i) in Lemma
2.4
and the assumption (1) for
F
to deduce

\int_{S_{-}}|F(y,z)K_{A}(y,z)|\,d\mathcal{H}_{y}^{n}\leq C\kappa_{0}\int_{S_{-}}\frac{1}{|y-z|^{n-\alpha}}\,d\mathcal{H}_{y}^{n}\leq C\kappa_{0}\int_{0}^{3|z-x|}\rho^{\alpha-1}\,d\rho=C\kappa_{0}|z-x|^{\alpha}.

Similarly it holds
\int_{S_{-}}|F(y,x)K_{A}(y,x)|\,d\mathcal{H}_{y}^{n}\leq C\kappa_{0}|z-x|^{\alpha}
.

Note that for
y\in S_{+}
it holds
|y-z|\geq|y-x|-|z-x|\geq\frac{1}{2}|y-x|
. We use the conditions (i) and (ii) in Lemma
2.4
and the assumption (1) and (2) for
F
to estimate

\begin{split}\int_{S_{+}}&|F(y,z)K_{A}(y,z)-F(y,x)K_{A}(y,x)|\,d\mathcal{H}_{y}^{n}\\
&\leq\int_{S_{+}}|F(y,z)-F(y,x)||K_{A}(y,z)|\,d\mathcal{H}_{y}^{n}+\int_{S_{-}}|F(y,x)||K_{A}(y,z)-K_{A}(y,x)|\,d\mathcal{H}_{y}^{n}\\
&\leq C\kappa_{0}|z-x|^{\alpha}\int_{S_{+}}\frac{1}{|y-x|^{n-\alpha/2}}\,d\mathcal{H}_{y}^{n}+C\kappa_{0}|z-x|^{s+\alpha}\int_{S_{+}}\frac{1}{|y-x|^{n+s}}\,d\mathcal{H}_{y}^{n}\\
&\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,+C\kappa_{0}|z-x|\int_{S_{+}}\frac{1}{|y-x|^{n+1-\alpha}}\,d\mathcal{H}_{y}^{n}.\end{split}

Since
\frac{1}{|y-x|^{n-\alpha/2}}
is not singular, we obtain
\int_{S_{+}}\frac{1}{|y-x|^{n-\alpha/2}}\,d\mathcal{H}_{y}^{n}\leq C
. Since
|z-x|\leq|y-x|
we estimate the last term by

\frac{|z-x|}{|y-x|^{n+1-\alpha}}\leq\frac{|z-x|^{s+\alpha}}{|y-x|^{n+s}}

which then implies

|z-x|\int_{S_{+}}\frac{1}{|y-x|^{n+1-\alpha}}\,d\mathcal{H}_{y}^{n}\leq|z-x|^{s+\alpha}\int_{S_{+}}\frac{1}{|y-x|^{n+s}}\,d\mathcal{H}_{y}^{n}\leq C|z-x|^{s+\alpha}\int_{|z-x|}^{2}\rho^{-s-1}\leq C|z-x|^{\alpha}.

Therefore we have

\int_{S_{+}}|F(y,z)K_{A}(y,z)-F(y,x)K_{A}(y,x)|\,d\mathcal{H}_{y}^{n}\leq C\kappa_{0}|z-x|^{\alpha}

and the claim follows.
∎

Remark 2.6
.

The above lemma holds also in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

2.9
), if we change the assumptions on
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
as follows.

(1)

For all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
it holds

|F(y,x)|\leq\kappa_{0}\min\{|y-x|,1\}^{1+s+\alpha}.

(2)

For all
x,y,z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
|z-x|\leq\frac{1}{2}|y-x|
it holds

|F(y,z)-F(y,x)|\leq\kappa_{0}\big(|z-x|^{\alpha}\min\{|y-x|,1\}^{1+s+\alpha/2}+|z-x|^{s+\alpha}\min\{|y-x|,1\}\big).

3.
Equation for the height function

As we mentioned in the introduction, instead of the original equation (
1.1
), we will differentiate it with respect to space and parametrize the equation

(3.1)

\nabla_{X(t)}V_{t}=-\nabla_{X(t)}H_{E_{t}^{s}}\qquad\text{on }\,\partial E_{t},

where
X(t)
is a vector field on the moving boundary
\partial E_{t}
, which we will define shortly, and
\nabla_{X(t)}
is the derivative in the direction of
X(t)
. Note that the derivative of the integral average
\bar{H}_{E_{t}}^{s}
vanishes.

We parametrize the flow using the height function similarly as in (
2.1
), with the difference that we assume we may write locally in time

\partial E_{t}=\{h(x,t)x+x_{0}:x\in\mathbb{S}^{n}\},

for some fixed
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and for
t\in[0,T)
. We denote

\eta(x,t):=h(x,t)x.

We choose the vector field
X(t)
as follows. Fix a basis vector
e_{i},\,i=1,\dots,n+1
of the ambient space
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. We obtain a tangent field on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
as

\tau_{i}(x)=e_{i}-x_{i}\,x,\qquad\text{for }\,x\in\mathbb{S}^{n},

where
x_{i}=\langle x,e_{i}\rangle
. This induces a tangent field on the moving boundary
\partial E_{t}
, which we denote by
X(t)=X(\cdot,t)
, defined as

X(\eta(x,t),t):=D\eta(x,t)\tau_{i}(x)=h(x,t)\tau_{i}(x)+\nabla_{i}h(x,t)x\qquad\text{for }\,x\in\mathbb{S}^{n}.

We may then write the derivative
\nabla_{X(t)}f
of any given function
f\in C^{1}(\partial E_{t})
at the point
\eta(x,t)\in\partial E_{t}
for
x\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
as

(\nabla_{X(t)}f)(\eta(x,t))=\nabla_{i}(f\circ\eta(\cdot,t))(x).

We will assume throughout the section that the height function satisfies the uniform bound
\|h(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{1+s}}\leq C
and
c\leq h(x,t)\leq C
for all
t
and
x\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
.

Let us first deal the LHS of (
3.1
). This is rather easy as we may write the normal as

\nu_{E_{t}}(\eta(x,t))=\frac{h(x,t)x-\nabla_{\tau}h(x,t)}{\sqrt{h^{2}(x,t)+|\nabla_{\tau}h(x,t)|^{2}}}

and the normal velocity then is

(3.2)

V_{t}(\eta(x,t))=\partial_{t}h(x,t)\,\frac{h(x,t)}{\sqrt{h^{2}(x,t)+|\nabla_{\tau}h(x,t)|^{2}}}.

Hence, it holds

(3.3)

\begin{split}(\nabla_{X(t)}V_{t})(\eta(x,t))&=\nabla_{i}\left(\partial_{t}h(x,t)\,\frac{h(x,t)}{\sqrt{h^{2}(x,t)+|\nabla_{\tau}h(x,t)|^{2}}}\right)\\
&=\partial_{t}\nabla_{i}h(x,t)\,\frac{h(x,t)}{\sqrt{h^{2}(x,t)+|\nabla_{\tau}h(x,t)|^{2}}}\\
&\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,+\partial_{t}h(x,t)\nabla_{i}\left(\frac{h(x,t)}{\sqrt{h^{2}(x,t)+|\nabla_{\tau}h(x,t)|^{2}}}\right).\end{split}

Let us then focus on the RHS of (
3.1
). We recall that following the argument in
[
15
,
24
]
we may write

(3.4)

\nabla_{X(t)}H_{E_{t}}^{s}=2\int_{\partial E_{t}}\frac{\langle X(t),\nu_{E_{t}}(y)\rangle}{|y-x|^{n+1+s}}\,d\mathcal{H}_{y}^{n}.

Indeed, by regularizing the kernel, say by
K_{\delta}(t)=(t^{2}+\delta)^{-\frac{n+1+s}{2}}
, and differentiating in
x_{i}
direction
(see
[
15
, Formula (6.17)]
) we have

\begin{split}\nabla_{x_{i}}&\left(\int_{E_{t}^{c}}K_{\delta}(|y-x|)\,dy-\int_{E_{t}}K_{\delta}(|y-x|)\,dy\right)\\
&=-\int_{E_{t}^{c}}K_{\delta}^{\prime}(|y-x|)\frac{\langle y-x,e_{i}\rangle}{|y-x|}\,dy+\int_{E_{t}}K_{\delta}^{\prime}(|y-x|)\frac{\langle y-x,e_{i}\rangle}{|y-x|}\,dy\\
&=-\int_{E_{t}^{c}}\text{div}_{y}(K_{\delta}(|y-x|)e_{i})\,dy+\int_{E_{t}}\text{div}_{y}(K_{\delta}(|y-x|)e_{i})\,dy\\
&=\int_{\partial E_{t}}2K_{\delta}(|y-x|)\langle e_{i},\nu_{E_{t}}(y)\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}_{y}^{n},\end{split}

where the last equality follows from divergence theorem. Letting
\delta\to 0
yields (
3.4
).

We parametrize (
3.4
) on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, by recalling the formula for the normal
\nu_{E_{t}}(\eta(x,t))
above and the Jacobian determinant of
\eta
as

J_{\eta(\cdot,t)}(x)=h(x,t)^{n-1}\sqrt{h^{2}(x,t)+|\nabla_{\tau}h(x,t)|^{2}}.

For simplicity we omit the time-dependence for a moment and write
h(x)=h(x,t)
. Then we may write

\nabla_{X(t)}H_{E_{t}}^{s}=2\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}h(y)^{n-1}\frac{\langle h(x)\tau_{i}(x)+\nabla_{i}h(x)x,h(y)y-\nabla_{\tau}h(y)\rangle}{|h(y)y-h(x)x|^{n+1+s}}\,d\mathcal{H}_{y}^{n}

We continue and write the terms as

\begin{split}h(x)\tau_{i}(x)+\nabla_{i}h(x)x=&h(y)\tau_{i}(y)+\nabla_{i}h(x)y\\
&+\big(h(x)\tau_{i}(x)-h(y)\tau_{i}(y)\big)+(x-y)\nabla_{i}h(x).\end{split}

This gives us the following expression

\begin{split}\nabla_{X(t)}H_{E_{t}}^{s}=2\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}h(y)^{n-1}\frac{\langle h(y)\tau_{i}(y)+\nabla_{i}h(x)y,h(y)y-\nabla_{\tau}h(y)\rangle}{|h(y)y-h(x)x|^{n+1+s}}\,d\mathcal{H}_{y}^{n}+R_{1}\end{split}

where the error terms are

\begin{split}R_{1}&=2\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\frac{\langle h(x)\tau_{i}(x)-h(y)\tau_{i}(y),h(y)y-\nabla_{\tau}h(y)\rangle}{|h(y)y-h(x)x|^{n+1+s}}h(y)^{n-1}\,d\mathcal{H}_{y}^{n}\\
&+2\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\frac{\langle x-y,h(y)y-\nabla_{\tau}h(y)\rangle}{|h(y)y-h(x)x|^{n+1+s}}\nabla_{i}h(x)h(y)^{n-1}\,d\mathcal{H}_{y}^{n}.\end{split}

Note that we may write the error terms as

R_{1}=\sum_{k}a_{k}(x,h,\nabla_{\tau}h)\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\frac{F_{k}(y,h(y))-F_{k}(x,h(x))}{|h(y)y-h(x)x|^{n+1+s}}b_{k}(y,h,\nabla_{\tau}h)\,d\mathcal{H}_{y}^{n}

for smooth functions
F_{k},a_{k},b_{k}
. But since

\langle h(y)\tau_{i}(y)+\nabla_{i}h(x)y,h(y)y-\nabla_{\tau}h(y)\rangle=\big(\nabla_{i}h(x)-\nabla_{i}h(y)\big)h(y)

we obtain

(3.5)

-\nabla_{X(t)}H_{E_{t}}^{s}=2\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\frac{\nabla_{i}h(y)-\nabla_{i}h(x)}{|h(y)y-h(x)x|^{n+1+s}}h(y)^{n}\,d\mathcal{H}_{y}^{n}+R_{1}.

We further linearize the first term on the RHS of (
3.5
), which is the leading order term in the equation. We write using
\langle x-y,y\rangle=-\frac{1}{2}|x-y|^{2}

\begin{split}|h(y)y-h(x)x|^{2}&=|h(x)(x-y)+(h(x)-h(y))y|^{2}\\
&=h^{2}(x)|y-x|^{2}+h(x)(h(y)-h(x))|x-y|^{2}+|h(y)-h(x)|^{2}.\end{split}

We write the Taylor expansion of
h
at
x
as

T_{x}[h](y)=h(y)-h(x)-\langle\nabla_{\tau}h(x),y-x\rangle

and note that for
\beta\in[0,1]
it holds

(3.6)

|T_{x}[h](y)|\leq\|h\|_{C^{1+\beta}}|y-x|^{1+\beta}.

Therefore we deduce that we may write

(3.7)

\begin{split}|h(y)y-h(x)x|^{2}&=h^{2}(x)|y-x|^{2}+\langle\nabla_{\tau}h(x),y-x\rangle^{2}+g_{h}(y,x)\\
&=\|x-y\|_{A(x)}^{2}+g_{h}(y,x)\end{split}

where the matrix is given by tensor product
A(x)=h^{2}(x)I+\nabla_{\tau}h(x)\otimes\nabla_{\tau}h(x)
, the norm
\|\xi\|_{A(x)}
is defined in (
2.10
), and the error term is

g_{h}(y,x)=h(x)(h(y)-h(x))|x-y|^{2}+2(h(y)-h(x))T_{x}[h](y)-|T_{x}[h](y)|^{2}.

The precise form of
g_{h}
is not relevant. Instead, it is important to notice that it satisfies the following two conditions.
For all
x,y\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
and
0<\alpha<\min\{s,1-s\}
it holds

(3.8)

|g_{h}(y,x)|\leq C\|h\|_{C^{1+s+\alpha}}|y-x|^{2+s+\alpha}

and for all
x,y,z\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
with
|z-x|\leq\frac{1}{2}|y-x|
it holds

(3.9)

|g_{h}(y,z)-g_{h}(y,x)|\leq C\|h\|_{C^{1+s+\alpha}}|z-x|^{s+\alpha}|y-x|^{2}.

Indeed, the estimate (
3.8
) follows by using (
3.6
) for
\beta=0
and
\beta=s+\alpha
, and recalling that
\|h\|_{C^{1+s}}\leq C
. The estimate (
3.9
) follows from the fact that for
|z-x|\leq\frac{1}{2}|y-x|
it holds

(3.10)

\begin{split}\big|T_{z}[h](y)-T_{x}[h](y)\big|&=\big|\big(h(y)-h(z)-\langle\nabla_{\tau}h(z),y-z\rangle\big)-\big(h(y)-h(x)-\langle\nabla_{\tau}h(x),y-x\rangle\big)\big|\\
&=\big|\big(h(x)-h(z)-\langle\nabla_{\tau}h(z),x-z\rangle\big)+\big(\langle\nabla_{\tau}h(x)-\nabla_{\tau}h(z),y-x\rangle\big)\big|\\
&\leq\|h\|_{C^{1+s+\alpha}}|z-x|^{1+s+\alpha}+\|h\|_{C^{1+s+\alpha}}|z-x|^{s+\alpha}|y-x|\\
&\leq 2\|h\|_{C^{1+s+\alpha}}|z-x|^{s+\alpha}|y-x|.\end{split}

Therefore we use the notation for
g_{h}(y,x)
, which is defined in (
3.7
), and the norm
\|\xi\|_{A(x)}
, which is defined in (
2.10
), and write

(3.11)

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}&\frac{\nabla_{i}h(y)-\nabla_{i}h(x)}{|h(y)y-h(x)x|^{n+1+s}}h(y)^{n}\,d\mathcal{H}_{y}^{n}\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\frac{\nabla_{i}h(y)-\nabla_{i}h(x)}{\|y-x\|_{A(x)}^{n+1+s}}h(y)^{n}\,d\mathcal{H}_{y}^{n}\\
&+\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\frac{\nabla_{i}h(y)-\nabla_{i}h(x)}{\|y-x\|_{A(x)}^{n+1+s}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d}{d\mu}\left(\frac{\|y-x\|_{A(x)}^{2}}{\|y-x\|_{A(x)}^{2}+\mu g_{h}(y,x)}\right)^{\frac{n+1+s}{2}}h(y)^{n}\,d\mu\,d\mathcal{H}_{y}^{n},\end{split}

where
A(x)=h^{2}(x)I+\nabla_{\tau}h(x)\otimes\nabla_{\tau}h(x)
.

Recall again the a priori estimates for the height function, i.e.,

\sup_{t\in[0,T)}\|h(\cdot,t))\|_{C^{1+s}}\leq C\quad\text{and}\quad h(x,t)\geq c.

We may thus finally write the equation (
3.1
) by (
3.3
), (
3.5
) and (
3.11
) as

(3.12)

\partial_{t}\nabla_{i}h=L_{A}[\nabla_{i}h]+R_{0}(x,t)+R_{1}(x,t)+\partial_{t}h\,B(x,h,\nabla_{\tau}h):\nabla_{\tau}^{2}h.

Here
B
is a smooth matrix-field and the linear operator is defined as

(3.13)

L_{A}[u](x):=a_{0}(x,t)\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\frac{u(y)-u(x)}{\|y-x\|_{A(x,t)}^{n+1+s}}b_{0}(y,t)\,d\mathcal{H}_{y}^{n},

for symmetric elliptic matrix field

A(x,t)=h^{2}(x,t)I+\nabla_{\tau}h(x,t)\otimes\nabla_{\tau}h(x,t)

and the coefficients
a_{0},b_{0}\geq c_{0}
satisfy

(3.14)

\sup_{t<T}\|a_{0}(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{s}(\mathbb{S}^{n})}\leq C\quad\text{and}\quad\sup_{t<T}\|b_{0}(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n})}\leq C.

The error terms are

(3.15)

R_{0}(x,t)=a_{0}(x,t)\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\nabla_{i}h(y,t)-\nabla_{i}h(x,t)}{\|y-x\|_{A(x,t)}^{n+1+s}}\frac{d}{d\mu}\left(\frac{\|y-x\|_{A(x,t)}^{2}}{\|y-x\|_{A(x,t)}^{2}+\mu g_{h}(y,x)}\right)^{\frac{n+1+s}{2}}b_{0}(y,t)\,d\mu d\mathcal{H}_{y}^{n}

and

(3.16)

R_{1}(x,t)=\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{k}(x,h,\nabla_{\tau}h)\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\frac{F_{k}(y,h(y,t))-F_{k}(x,h(x,t))}{|h(y,t)y-h(x,t)x|^{n+1+s}}b_{k}(y,h,\nabla_{\tau}h)\,d\mathcal{H}_{y}^{n},

where
a_{k},b_{k}
and
F_{k}
for
k\geq 1
are smooth functions.

Next we state the following technical lemma, which we use later to bound the error term
R_{0}
defined in (
3.15
).

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume that
A(\cdot)
is symmetric elliptic matrix field as defined in (
2.3
) such that
\|A(\cdot)\|_{C^{\alpha}}\leq C
for
0<\alpha<\min\{s,1-s\}
and assume that
g:\mathbb{S}^{n}\times\mathbb{S}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies the following two conditions.
For all
x,y\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
it holds
\|y-x\|_{A(x)}^{2}+g(y,x)\geq c|y-x|^{2}
, for some
c>0
, and

|g(y,x)|\leq\kappa|y-x|^{2+s+\alpha},

and for all
x,y,z\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
with
|z-x|\leq\frac{1}{2}|y-x|
it holds

|g(y,z)-g(y,x)|\leq\kappa(|z-x|^{s+\alpha}|y-x|^{2}+|z-x|^{\alpha}|y-x|^{2+s+\alpha}).

Denote
G(y,x)=\frac{d}{d\mu}\left(\frac{\|y-x\|_{A(x)}^{2}}{\|y-x\|_{A(x)}^{2}+\mu g(y,x)}\right)^{q}
, for
\mu\in(0,1),q\geq 1
, and assume
v_{1}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n})
,
v_{2}\in C(\mathbb{S}^{n})
and
v_{3}\in C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{n})
.
Then the function

\psi(x)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\big(v_{1}(y)-v_{1}(x)\big)v_{2}(y)v_{3}(x)G(y,x)K_{A}(y,x)\,d\mathcal{H}_{y}^{n}

is Hölder continuous and

\|\psi\|_{C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{n})}\leq C\kappa\|v_{1}\|_{C^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n})}\|v_{2}\|_{C^{0}(\mathbb{S}^{n})}\|v_{3}\|_{C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{n})}.

Proof.

Let us first prove that it holds for all
y,x\in\mathbb{S}^{n},y\neq x

(3.17)

|G(y,x)|\leq C\kappa|y-x|^{s+\alpha}

and for all
x,y,z\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
with
0<|z-x|\leq\frac{1}{2}|y-x|

(3.18)

|G(y,z)-G(y,x)|\leq C\kappa\big(|z-x|^{\alpha}|y-x|^{s+\alpha}+|z-x|^{s+\alpha}\big).

To this aim we denote
\Phi(t)=(1+t)^{-q}
,

\tilde{G}(y,x)=\frac{g(x,y)}{\|y-x\|_{A(x)}^{2}}

and notice that we may write
G(y,x)=\Phi^{\prime}(\mu\tilde{G}(y,x))\tilde{G}(y,x)
. Since
\Phi
is smooth we deduce that it is enough to show that
\tilde{G}
satisfies the conditions (
3.17
) and (
3.18
). We obtain (
3.17
) immediately from the ellipticity of
A
and from the first assumption on
g
as

|\tilde{G}(y,x)|\leq C\kappa\frac{|y-x|^{2+s+\alpha}}{|y-x|^{2}}\leq C\kappa|y-x|^{s+\alpha}.

To prove (
3.18
) we fix
x,y,z\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
with
0<|z-x|\leq\frac{1}{2}|y-x|
. Note that then
\frac{1}{2}|y-x|\leq|y-z|\leq 2|y-x|
. We then write

\begin{split}|\tilde{G}(y,z)-\tilde{G}(y,x)|\leq|g(y,z)|\big|\|y-z\|_{A(z)}^{-2}-\|y-x\|_{A(x)}^{-2}\big|+\frac{|g(x,z)-g(y,z)|}{\|y-x\|_{A(x)}^{2}}.\end{split}

We may estimate the last term by the second condition on
g
as

\frac{|g(x,z)-g(y,z)|}{\|y-x\|_{A(x)}^{2}}\leq C\kappa\frac{|z-x|^{s+\alpha}|y-x|^{2}+|z-x|^{\alpha}|y-x|^{2+s+\alpha}}{|y-x|^{2}}\leq C\kappa(|z-x|^{s+\alpha}+|z-x|^{\alpha}|y-x|^{s+\alpha}).

For the first term we observe that

\begin{split}\big|\|y-z\|_{A(z)}^{2}&-\|y-x\|_{A(x)}^{2}\big|\\
&=\big|\langle A(z)(y-z),(y-z)\rangle-\langle A(x)(y-x),(y-x)\rangle\big|\\
&\leq\big|\langle(A(z)-A(x))(y-z),(y-z)\rangle\big|+\big|\langle A(x)\big((y-z)+(y-x)\big),(x-z)\rangle\big|\\
&\leq C|z-x|^{\alpha}|y-x|^{2}+C|y-x||z-x|.\end{split}

We use the fact that
|z-x|\leq\frac{1}{2}|y-x|
to deduce
|z-x|\leq|z-x|^{s+\alpha}|y-x|^{1-s-\alpha}
.
Therefore it holds

\begin{split}|g(y,z)|\big|\|y-z\|_{A(z)}^{-2}-\|y-x\|_{A(x)}^{-2}\big|&\leq\frac{|g(y,z)|}{|y-x|^{4}}\big|\|y-z\|_{A(z)}^{2}-\|y-x\|_{A(x)}^{2}\big|\\
&\leq\frac{C\kappa}{|y-x|^{2-s-\alpha}}(|z-x|^{\alpha}|y-x|^{2}+|y-x||z-x|)\\
&\leq C\kappa(|z-x|^{\alpha}|y-x|^{s+\alpha}+|z-x|^{s+\alpha})\end{split}

and (
3.18
) follows.

We proceed by defining
F:\mathbb{S}^{n}\times\mathbb{S}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
as

F(y,x):=\big(v_{1}(y)-v_{1}(x)\big)v_{2}(y)v_{3}(x)G(y,x)

when
y\neq x
and
F(x,x)=0
. We prove that
F
satisfies the assumptions of Lemma
2.5
for

\tilde{\kappa}:=\kappa\|v_{1}\|_{C^{1}}\|v_{2}\|_{C^{0}}\|v_{3}\|_{C^{\alpha}}.

This will then yield the claim. The first condition follows immediately from (
3.17
) as

|F(y,x)|=|v_{1}(y)-v_{1}(x)|\,|v_{2}(y)|\,|v_{3}(x)|\,|G(y,x)|\leq C\tilde{\kappa}|y-x|^{1+s+\alpha}

for all
x\neq y\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
.

To show the second condition we fix
x,y,z\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
with
0<|z-x|\leq\frac{1}{2}|y-x|
. We will in fact show that

|F(y,z)-F(y,x)|\leq C\tilde{\kappa}\big(|z-x|^{\alpha}|y-x|^{1+s+\alpha}+|z-x|^{s+\alpha}|y-x|\big),

which is slightly stronger than what we need. To this aim we write

\begin{split}|F(y,z)-F(y,x)|&=|v_{2}(y)||\big(v_{1}(y)-v_{1}(z)\big)v_{3}(z)G(y,z)-\big(v_{1}(y)-v_{1}(x)\big)v_{3}(x)G(y,x)|\\
&\leq|v_{2}(y)||v_{3}(z)||G(y,z)||v_{1}(z)-v_{1}(x)|\\
&\,\,\,\,\,\,\,+|v_{2}(y)||v_{3}(z)||v_{1}(y)-v_{1}(x)||G(y,z)-G(y,x)|\\
&\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,+|v_{2}(y)||v_{1}(y)-v_{1}(x)||G(y,x)||v_{3}(z)-v_{3}(x)|.\end{split}

We estimate the first term term on the RHS by (
3.17
) and by
|z-x|\leq|y-x|
as

|v_{2}(y)||v_{3}(z)||G(y,z)||v_{1}(z)-v_{1}(x)|\leq C\tilde{\kappa}|y-x|^{s+\alpha}|z-x|\leq C\tilde{\kappa}|y-x||z-x|^{s+\alpha}.

We estimate the last similarly

|v_{2}(y)||v_{1}(y)-v_{1}(x)||G(y,x)||v_{3}(z)-v_{3}(x)|\leq C\tilde{\kappa}|y-x|^{1+s+\alpha}|z-x|^{\alpha}.

Finally, we estimate the second term by (
3.18
) as

|v_{2}(y)||v_{3}(z)||v_{1}(y)-v_{1}(x)||G(y,z)-G(y,x)|\leq\tilde{\kappa}\big(|z-x|^{\alpha}|y-x|^{1+s+\alpha}+|z-x|^{s+\alpha}|y-x|\big).

We thus have the claim by Lemma
2.5
.
∎

We conclude the section with a technical lemma, which is analogous to
[
19
, Lemma 4.4]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume that
A(\cdot)
is symmetric, elliptic and
\|A\|_{C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{n})}\leq C
for
0<\alpha<\min\{s,1-s\}
, and the kernel
K_{A}
is defined in (
2.9
). Assume further
v_{1}\in C^{1+s+\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{n})
,
v_{2}\in C^{s+\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{n})
and
v_{3}\in C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{n})
.
Then the function

\psi(x)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\big(v_{1}(y)-v_{1}(x)\big)v_{2}(y)v_{3}(x)K_{A}(y,x)\,d\mathcal{H}_{y}^{n}

is Hölder continuous and

\|\psi\|_{C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{n})}\leq C\|v_{1}\|_{C^{1+s+\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{n})}\|v_{2}\|_{C^{s+\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{n})}\|v_{3}\|_{C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{n})}.

Proof.

The claim follows from
[
19
, Lemma 4.4]
once we show that the function

\psi(x)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}(y-x)K_{A}(y,x)\,\,d\mathcal{H}_{y}^{n}

is uniformly
\alpha
-Hölder continuous. Indeed, even if the condition (ii) in Lemma
2.4
is slightly weaker than
[
19
, Definition 4.1]
, the claim still follows from the proof of
[
19
, Lemma 4.4]
. Let us first prove the Hölder continuity of
\psi
under the additional assumption that at every point
x\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
it holds

(3.19)

\langle A(x)x,\omega\rangle=0\qquad\text{for every }\,\omega\quad\text{orthogonal to }\,x.

To this aim fix
x
and notice that, by the symmetry of the sphere and by (
3.19
) for all vectors
\omega
orthogonal to
x
, it holds

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\langle y-x,\omega\rangle K_{A}(y,x)\,\,d\mathcal{H}_{y}^{n}=0.

Therefore

\psi(x)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}(y-x)K_{A}(y,x)\,\,d\mathcal{H}_{y}^{n}=x\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\langle y-x,x\rangle K_{A}(y,x)\,\,d\mathcal{H}_{y}^{n}.

For all
y\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
it holds
\langle y-x,x\rangle=-\frac{1}{2}|y-x|^{2}
. Therefore we have

\psi(x)=-\frac{x}{2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}|y-x|^{2}K_{A}(y,x)\,\,d\mathcal{H}_{y}^{n}.

Obviously the function
F(y,x)=-\frac{x}{2}|y-x|^{2}
satisfies the assumptions of Lemma
2.5
and therefore we deduce that
\|\psi\|_{C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{n})}\leq C
.

Let us then consider the general case. We write
A(x)=A_{0}(x)+A_{1}(x)
, where

A_{0}=A-(Ax)\otimes x-x\otimes(Ax)+2\langle Ax,x\rangle(x\otimes x)

and
A_{1}=A-A_{0}
. Then
A_{0}(\cdot)
is symmetric, elliptic and satisfies the condition (
3.19
). Moreover, recalling
\langle y-x,x\rangle=-\frac{1}{2}|y-x|^{2}
and arguing as in (
3.8
) and (
3.9
) we observe that
g(y,x)=\langle A_{1}(x)(y-x),y-x\rangle

satisfies the following two conditions: for
x,y\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
it holds

(3.20)

|g(y,x)|\leq C|y-x|^{3}

and for all
x,y,z\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
with
|z-x|\leq\frac{1}{2}|y-x|
it holds

(3.21)

|g(y,z)-g_{\varphi}(y,x)|\leq C(|z-x||y-x|^{2}+|z-x|^{\alpha}|y-x|^{3}).

Since the argument is similar as before, we leave the details for the reader. At the end, since we may write

\begin{split}\psi(x)=&\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}(y-x)K_{A}(y,x)\,\,d\mathcal{H}_{y}^{n}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}(y-x)K_{A_{0}}(y,x)\,d\mathcal{H}_{y}^{n}\\
&+\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}(y-x)K_{A_{0}}(y,x)\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d}{d\mu}\left(\frac{\|y-x\|_{A_{0}(x)}^{2}}{\|y-x\|_{A_{0}(x)}^{2}+\mu g(y,x)}\right)^{\frac{n+1+s}{2}}\,d\mu\,d\mathcal{H}_{y}^{n}\end{split}

the conclusion follows from Lemma
3.1
.
∎

4.
Schauder estimates for the fractional parabolic equation

In this section we extend Theorem
2.2
from
\mathbb{R}^{n}
to the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. We consider linear operator of the form

(4.1)

L_{A}[u](x):=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\frac{u(y)-u(x)}{\|y-x\|_{A(x,t)}^{n+1+s}}a(x,t)b(y,t)\,d\mathcal{H}_{y}^{n},

where the norm
\|\cdot\|_{A}
is defined in (
2.10
),
A(\cdot,t)
is symmetric elliptic matrix field for every
t\in[0,T)
such that
\sup_{t<T}\|A(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{n})}\leq C
and the coefficients satisfy
a(x,t),b(x,t)\geq c>0
for all
x\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
and
t\in[0,T)
and

\sup_{t<T}\|a(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{s}(\mathbb{S}^{n})}\leq C\quad\text{and}\quad\sup_{t<T}\|b_{0}(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n})}\leq C.

We also assume without mentioning that all functions are continuous with respect to time.

We prove the following Schauder estimate on the sphere.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
T>0
and
\alpha
such that
0<\alpha<\min\{s,1-s\}
. Assume that
A(\cdot,t)
is symmetric and elliptic such that
\sup_{t<T}\|A(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{n})}\leq C
and let
L_{A}
be the operator defined in (
4.1
). Let
f:\mathbb{S}^{n}\times[0,T)\to\mathbb{R}
be such that
\sup_{t<T}\|f(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{n})}<\infty
and let
u:\mathbb{S}^{n}\times[0,T)\to\mathbb{R}
be the solution of the problem

(4.2)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u=L_{A}[u]+f(x,t)\qquad&(x,t)\in\mathbb{S}^{n}\times(0,T)\\
u(x,0)=0&x\in\mathbb{S}^{n}.\end{cases}

There exists a constant
C_{T}
such that

\sup_{t<T}\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{1+s+\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{n})}\leq C_{T}\sup_{t<T}\|f(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{n})}.

Proof.

By approximation we may assume that all the functions in the assumptions are smooth. Moreover, we note that we may absorb the coefficient
a(x,t)
in (
4.1
) into the matrix field
A(x,t)
and thus obtain another symmetric elliptic matrix field which we still denote by
A(x,t)
. We also adopt the notation (
2.9
) for the kernel
K_{A}
, which means that

K_{A}(y,x)=\frac{1}{\|y-x\|_{A(x)}^{n+1+s}}.

First, it follows from the maximum principle that

(4.3)

\sup_{t<T}\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{0}(\mathbb{S}^{n})}\leq T\sup_{t<T}\|f(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{0}(\mathbb{S}^{n})}.

Indeed, fix a small
\varepsilon>0
and define
w_{\varepsilon}(x,t)=(t+\varepsilon)^{-1}u(x,t)
. Then
w
is smooth and
w(x,0)=0
. Assume that
w
attains its maximum on
\mathbb{S}^{n}\times(0,T-\varepsilon]
at
(x_{0},t_{0})
with
t_{0}\in(0,T-\varepsilon]
. Then by the maximum principle it holds

0\leq\partial_{t}w(x_{0},t_{0})=\frac{\partial_{t}u(x_{0},t_{0})}{t_{0}+\varepsilon}-\frac{u(x_{0},t_{0})}{(t_{0}+\varepsilon)^{2}}\quad\text{and}\quad 0\geq L_{A}[w](x_{0},t_{0})=\frac{L_{A}[u](x_{0},t_{0})}{t_{0}+\varepsilon}.

The equation (
4.2
) implies

\frac{u(x_{0},t_{0})}{t_{0}+\varepsilon}\leq f(x_{0},t_{0}).

By letting
\varepsilon\to 0
and repeating the argument for
-u
we obtain (
4.3
).

Let us fix
x_{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
and by rotating the coordinates we may assume that it is the north pole, i.e.,
x_{0}=e_{n+1}
. Let us first localize the equation around
x_{0}
. To this aim fix a small
\delta>0
and choose a smooth cutoff function
\eta:\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]
such that
\eta(r)=1
for
|r|<\delta/2
and
\eta(r)=0
for
r\geq\delta
.
In the following we write
x=(x^{\prime},x_{n+1})\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with
x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We denote

v(x,t)=\eta(4|x^{\prime}|)u(x,t).

Then clearly
\|v(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{\beta}(\mathbb{S}^{n})}\leq C\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{\beta}(\mathbb{S}^{n}\cap B_{\delta}(x_{0}))}
for
\beta\in[0,2]
. In order to find an equation for
v
we multiply the equation (
4.2
) by
\eta(4|x^{\prime}|)
and obtain

\partial_{t}v(x,t)=\eta(4|x^{\prime}|)L_{A}[u](x,t)+\eta(4|x^{\prime}|)\,f(x,t).

We organize the terms as

\eta(4|x^{\prime}|)\big(u(y,t)-u(x,t)\big)=v(y,t)-v(x,t)-u(y,t)(\eta(4|y^{\prime}|)-\eta(4|x^{\prime}|))

and write

\begin{split}\eta(4|x^{\prime}|)L_{A}[u](x,t)=&\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\eta(|y^{\prime}|)\big(v(y,t)-v(x,t)\big)K_{A}(y,x)\,b(y,t)\,d\mathcal{H}_{y}^{n}\\
&+\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}(1-\eta(|y^{\prime}|))\big(v(y,t)-v(x,t)\big)K_{A}(y,x)\,b(y,t)\,d\mathcal{H}_{y}^{n}\\
&-\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}u(y,t)\big(\eta(4|y^{\prime}|)-\eta(4|x^{\prime}|)\big)K_{A}(y,x)\,b(y,t)\,d\mathcal{H}_{y}^{n}.\end{split}

The last term is Hölder continuous due to Lemma
3.2
. Since
v(x,t)=0
for
|x^{\prime}|\geq\delta/4
, then the Kernel
(1-\eta(|y^{\prime}|))K_{A}(y,x)
in the function

R_{1}(x,t)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}(1-\eta(|y^{\prime}|))\big(v(y,t)-v(x,t)\big)K_{A}(y,x)\,b(y,t)\,d\mathcal{H}_{y}^{n}

is non-singular and therefore
\|R_{1}(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{n})}\leq C_{\delta}\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{n})}
, where the constant depends on
\delta>0
. Therefore we deduce that it holds

(4.4)

\partial_{t}v(x,t)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\eta(|y^{\prime}|)\big(v(y,t)-v(x,t)\big)K_{A}(y,x)\,b(y,t)\,d\mathcal{H}_{y}^{n}+f_{1}(x,t)

and
f_{1}
satisfies

(4.5)

\|f_{1}(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{n})}\leq C_{\delta}\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{s+\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{n})}+C_{\delta}\|f(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{n})}.

We further manipulate the leading order term in (
4.4
) by writing

(4.6)

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}&\eta(|y^{\prime}|)\big(v(y,t)-v(x,t)\big)K_{A}(y,x)\,b(y,t)\,d\mathcal{H}_{y}^{n}\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\eta(|y^{\prime}|)\big(v(y,t)-v(x,t)\big)K_{A}(y,x)\,b(e_{n+1},t)\sqrt{1-|y^{\prime}|^{2}}\,d\mathcal{H}_{y}^{n}+R_{2}(x,t),\end{split}

where

R_{2}(x,t):=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\eta(|y^{\prime}|)\big(v(y,t)-v(x,t)\big)K_{A}(y,x)\,(b(y,t)-b(e_{n+1},t)\sqrt{1-|y^{\prime}|^{2}})\,d\mathcal{H}_{y}^{n}

Let us define
\psi(y,t)=\eta(|y^{\prime}|)(b(y,t)-b(e_{n+1},t)\sqrt{1-|y^{\prime}|^{2}})
. Then at the north pole it holds
\psi(e_{n+1},t)=0
. Therefore, since
\psi
is
C^{1}
-regular we have

\|\psi(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{s+\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{n})}\leq C\delta^{1-s-\alpha}.

Therefore again Lemma
3.2
yields

(4.7)

\|R_{2}(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{n})}\leq C\delta^{1-s-\alpha}\|u\|_{C^{1+s+\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{n}\cap B_{\delta}(x_{0}))}.

Note that since
b(e_{n+1},t)
depends only on time, we may absorb it into the matrix field
A(y,t)
on the RHS of (
4.6
) and denote the new matrix field still by
A(y,t)
.

Let us then consider the RHS in (
4.6
). We write the unit sphere locally as a graph of the function
\varphi(x^{\prime})=\sqrt{1-|x^{\prime}|^{2}}
. Then we may write
y,x\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
with
|y^{\prime}|,|x^{\prime}|<\delta
as

y-x=\begin{pmatrix}y^{\prime}-x^{\prime}\\
\varphi(y^{\prime})-\varphi(x^{\prime})\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}y^{\prime}-x^{\prime}\\
\langle\nabla\varphi(x^{\prime}),y^{\prime}-x^{\prime}\rangle\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}0\\
T_{x^{\prime}}[\varphi](y^{\prime})\end{pmatrix},

where
T_{x^{\prime}}[\varphi](y^{\prime})=\varphi(y^{\prime})-\varphi(x^{\prime})-\langle\nabla\varphi(x^{\prime}),y^{\prime}-x^{\prime}\rangle
. Let us define the
(n+1\times n)
- matrix field
J_{\varphi}(x^{\prime})
such that for
\xi^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
it holds

J_{\varphi}(x^{\prime})\xi^{\prime}=\begin{pmatrix}\xi^{\prime}\\
\langle\nabla\varphi(x^{\prime}),\xi^{\prime}\rangle\end{pmatrix}.

Then we may write
y-x=J_{\varphi}(x^{\prime})(y^{\prime}-x^{\prime})+T_{x^{\prime}}[\varphi](y^{\prime})e_{n+1}
. We may write

\langle A(x,t)(y-x),(y-x)\rangle=\langle\tilde{A}(x,t)(y^{\prime}-x^{\prime}),(y^{\prime}-x^{\prime})\rangle+g(y,x)

for

(4.8)

\tilde{A}(x,t)=J_{\varphi}(x^{\prime})^{T}A(x,t)J_{\varphi}(x^{\prime})

and

g(y,x)=2T_{x^{\prime}}[\varphi](y^{\prime})\,\langle A(x,t)J_{\varphi}(x^{\prime})(y^{\prime}-x^{\prime}),e_{n+1}\rangle+(T_{x^{\prime}}[\varphi](y^{\prime}))^{2}\langle A(x,t)e_{n+1},e_{n+1}\rangle.

Using
|T_{x^{\prime}}[\varphi](y^{\prime})|\leq C|y-x|^{2}
and arguing as in (
3.8
) and (
3.9
), we deduce that
g
satisfies the conditions (
3.20
) and (
3.21
).
We also note immediately that the new
(n\times n)
- matrix field
\tilde{A}(\cdot,t)
defined in (
4.8
) is symmetric and elliptic in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and also in this case, with a little abuse of notation, we write
\|\xi\|_{\tilde{A}(x,t)}^{2}=\langle\tilde{A}(x,t)\xi,\xi\rangle
for
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
We denote

G_{\mu}(y,x)=\frac{d}{d\mu}\left(\frac{\|y^{\prime}-x^{\prime}\|_{\tilde{A}(x,t)}^{2}}{\|y^{\prime}-x^{\prime}\|_{\tilde{A}(x,t)}^{2}-\mu g(y,x)}\right)^{\frac{n+1+s}{2}}

and write the RHS term in (
4.6
) as

(4.9)

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}&\eta(|y^{\prime}|)\big(v(y,t)-v(x,t)\big)K_{A}(y,x)\sqrt{1-|y^{\prime}|^{2}}\,d\mathcal{H}_{y}^{n}\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\eta(|y^{\prime}|)\frac{v(y,t)-v(x,t)}{\|y^{\prime}-x^{\prime}\|_{\tilde{A}(x,t)}^{n+1+s}}\sqrt{1-|y^{\prime}|^{2}}\,d\mathcal{H}_{y}^{n}+R_{3}(x,t),\end{split}

where

R_{3}(x,t)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\int_{0}^{1}\eta(|y^{\prime}|)G_{\mu}(y,x)\frac{v(y,t)-v(x,t)}{\|y^{\prime}-x^{\prime}\|_{\tilde{A}(x,t)}^{n+1+s}}\sqrt{1-|y^{\prime}|^{2}}\,d\mu\,d\mathcal{H}_{y}^{n}.

We may use Lemma
3.1
to deduce

(4.10)

\|R_{3}(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{n})}\leq C\|u\|_{C^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n})}.

Finally we map the RHS term in (
4.9
) to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, by slight abuse of notation we write
v(x^{\prime},t)=v\big((x^{\prime},\varphi(x^{\prime})),t\big)
,
\tilde{A}(x^{\prime},t)=\tilde{A}\big((x^{\prime},\varphi(x^{\prime})),t\big)
, and obtain

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\eta(|y^{\prime}|)\frac{v(y,t)-v(x,t)}{\|y^{\prime}-x^{\prime}\|_{\tilde{A}(x,t)}^{n+1+s}}\sqrt{1-|y^{\prime}|^{2}}\,d\mathcal{H}_{y}^{n}&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\eta(|y^{\prime}|)\frac{v(y^{\prime},t)-v(x^{\prime},t)}{\|y^{\prime}-x^{\prime}\|_{\tilde{A}(x^{\prime},t)}^{n+1+s}}\,dy^{\prime}\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{v(y^{\prime},t)-v(x^{\prime},t)}{\|y^{\prime}-x^{\prime}\|_{\tilde{A}(x^{\prime},t)}^{n+1+s}}\,dy^{\prime}+R_{4}(x^{\prime},t),\end{split}

where

), (

In the appendix we give a self-contained proof of Theorem

. Recall that we consider linear operator

L_{A}[u](x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{u(y+x)-u(x)}{\langle A(x,t)y,y\rangle^{\frac{n+1+s}{2}}}dy=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{u(y)-u(x)}{\langle A(x,t)(y-x),(y-x)\rangle^{\frac{n+1+s}{2}}}dy.

In the following, we denote by
\mathcal{F}[u](\xi)
the Fourier transform of
u
evaluated at a point
\xi

\mathcal{F}[u](\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u(x)e^{-2\pi i\langle\xi,x\rangle}\,dx

and denote by
\mathcal{F}^{-1}
the inverse Fourier transform, i.e.,

\mathcal{F}^{-1}[v](x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v(\xi)e^{2\pi i\langle\xi,x\rangle}\,d\xi.

The proof of Theorem
2.2
follows from a small perturbation argument. We begin with an easy lemma.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
A(t)
be elliptic and symmetric matrix which is constant w.r.t
x
. Then

(A.1)

\mathcal{F}\big(L_{A}[u]\big)(\xi)=-a(\xi,t)|\xi|^{1+s}\mathcal{F}[u](\xi)

where

0
-homogeneous function w.r.t to
\xi

a(\xi,t)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1-\cos(2\pi r\langle\frac{\xi}{|\xi|},\omega\rangle)}{r^{2+s}\langle A(t)\omega,\omega\rangle^{\frac{n+1+s}{2}}}\,dr\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}_{\omega}.

Moreover, it holds

(A.2)

\frac{1}{C}\leq a(\xi,t)\leq C\;\;\;\text{and}\;\;\;|\partial^{\beta}a(\xi,t)|\leq C_{|\beta|}\frac{1}{|\xi|^{|\beta|}}\qquad\text{for }\xi\not=0\text{ and }t\in(0,T).

Since

Setting

\mathcal{F}[u_{y}](\xi)=e^{2\pi i\langle\xi,y\rangle}\mathcal{F}[u](\xi)
.
By linearity of the Fourier transform, we get

\begin{split}\mathcal{F}[L_{A}u](\xi)&=\mathcal{F}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{u(y+x)-u(x)}{\langle A\,y,y\rangle^{\frac{n+1+s}{2}}}\,dy\right](\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\mathcal{F}[u_{y}](\xi)-\mathcal{F}[u](\xi)}{\langle A\,y,y\rangle^{\frac{n+1+s}{2}}}\,dy\\

From this formula and the fact that

is

-homogeneous it is immediate to obtain the bounds (
A.2
)

In order to prove Theorem
2.2
, we first consider the case of coefficients which are constant in space.
To that aim we recall that we may characterize the Hölder continuity by its Fourier transform using the Hardy-Littlewood decomposition. Let

\eta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

,
\text{supp}\,\eta\subset\bar{B}_{2}
, and
\eta(\xi)=1
for
\xi\in B_{1}

\delta(\xi)=\eta(\xi)-\eta(2\xi)
. Then the functions
\delta(2^{-j}\xi)
form a partition of unity, i.e.

1=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\delta(2^{-j}\xi),\qquad\text{for }\,\xi\neq 0.

Next we define
\Psi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
via its Fourier transform

\mathcal{F}[\Psi](\xi)=\delta(\xi)=\eta(\xi)-\eta(2\xi).

We write
\Psi_{t}(x)=t^{-n}\Psi(x/t)
. Then we have by scaling
\mathcal{F}[\Psi_{2^{-j}}](\xi)=\delta(2^{-j}\xi)
. Finally we define the operator
\Delta_{j}

\Delta_{j}(f):=f*\Psi_{2^{-j}}.

Note that since
\mathcal{F}[u*v]=\mathcal{F}[u]\cdot\mathcal{F}[v]
we may write

\Delta_{j}(f)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}[\mathcal{F}[f]\cdot\delta(2^{-j}\xi)].

We recall that when
\gamma>0
is not an integer it holds (see e.g.
[
27
]
)

(A.3)

\frac{1}{C}\|f\|_{C^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\sup_{j\geq 1}\,2^{j\gamma}\|\Delta_{j}(f)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|f\|_{C^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

We assume that
A(t)
is an elliptic and symmetric matrix field which is continuous w.r.t to time.

Theorem A.2
.

Let
u\in C^{1+s+\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a solution of

(A.4)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u=L_{A(t)}[u]+f(x,t),\\
u(x,0)=0\end{cases}

where the matrix field is symmetric, elliptic, continuous w.r.t. time and constant in space. For
\alpha<\min\{s,1-s\}
it holds

\sup_{t<T}\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{1+s+\alpha}}\leq C_{T}\sup_{t<T}\|f(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{\alpha}},

where the constant
C
depends only on
\alpha,s,n,T
and on the ellipticity constants of
A(\cdot)
.

Proof.

Applying Fourier transform to the equation (
2.5
) and using Lemma
A.1
we obtain

\partial_{t}\mathcal{F}[u](\xi,t)=-a(\xi,t)|\xi|^{1+s}\mathcal{F}[u](\xi,t)+\mathcal{F}[f](\xi,t).

Multiplying the above by
e^{|\xi|^{1+s}\int_{0}^{t}a(\xi,\tau)\,d\tau}
we
have

\partial_{t}\big(\mathcal{F}[u](\xi,t)e^{g(\xi,t)|\xi|^{1+s}}\big)=\mathcal{F}[f](\xi,t)e^{|\xi|^{1+s}g(\xi,t)}

where we have set
g(\xi,t)=\int_{0}^{t}a(\xi,\tau)\,d\tau
.
Integrate over
(0,t)
, recall that
u(x,0)=0
, and get

\mathcal{F}[u](\xi,t)e^{g(t,\xi)|\xi|^{1+s}}=\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{F}[f](\xi,\tau)e^{g(\tau,\xi)|\xi|^{1+s}}\,d\tau.

Thus we may write the solution as

\mathcal{F}[u](\xi,t)=\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{F}[f](\xi,\tau)e^{-(g(\xi,t)-g(\xi,\tau))|\xi|^{1+s}}\,d\tau.

We need to estimate
\|\Delta_{j}(u(\cdot,t))\|_{L^{\infty}}
. Since
\delta
is a cutoff function it is enough to estimate

\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\big(\mathcal{F}[u](\xi,t)\cdot\delta^{2}(2^{-j}\xi)\big)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\big(e^{-(g(\xi,t)-g(\xi,\tau))|\xi|^{1+s}}\mathcal{F}[f](\xi,\tau)\delta^{2}(2^{-j}\xi)\big)\|_{L^{\infty}}\,d\tau.

Since the product becomes a convolution we have

\begin{split}\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}&\big(e^{-(g(\xi,t)-g(\xi,\tau))|\xi|^{1+s}}\mathcal{F}[f](\xi,\tau)\delta^{2}(2^{-j}\xi)\big)\|_{L^{\infty}}\\
&=\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\big(e^{-(g(\xi,t)-g(\xi,\tau))|\xi|^{1+s}}\delta(2^{-j}\xi)\big)\,*\,\mathcal{F}^{-1}\big(\mathcal{F}[f](\xi,\tau)\delta(2^{-j}\xi)\big)\|_{L^{\infty}}\\
&\leq\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\big(e^{-(g(\xi,t)-g(\xi,\tau))|\xi|^{1+s}}\delta(2^{-j}\xi)\big)\|_{L^{1}}\,\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\big(\mathcal{F}[f](\xi,\tau)\delta(2^{-j}\xi)\big)\|_{L^{\infty}}.\end{split}

Since
f(\cdot,t)\in C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for every
\tau<t
we have by (
A.3
)

\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\big(\mathcal{F}[f](\xi,\tau)\delta(2^{-j}\xi)\big)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C2^{-j\alpha}\sup_{\tau<t}\|f(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

We need therefore to show

(A.5)

\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\big(e^{-(g(\xi,t)-g(\xi,\tau))|\xi|^{1+s}}\delta(2^{-j}\xi)\big)\|_{L^{1}}\,d\tau\leq C2^{-j(1+s)}.

Obviously it is enough to prove (
A.5
) for
j\geq 2
. Recalling the definition of
g(\xi,t)
and using (
A.2
) we have for
\tau<t

g(\xi,t)-g(\xi,\tau)\geq c_{0}(t-\tau)\quad\text{and}\quad|\partial^{\beta}(g(\xi,t)-g(\xi,\tau))|\leq C_{|\beta|}\frac{t-\tau}{|\xi|^{|\beta|}}.

Using these one may prove that, if
\gamma=(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n})
is a multi index of length
n
, then we have

\begin{split}\big|\partial_{\xi}^{\gamma}e^{-(g(\xi,t)-g(\xi,\tau))|\xi|^{1+s}}\big|&\leq C\sum_{k\leq n}(1+(t-\tau)|\xi|^{1+s})^{k}|\xi|^{-n}e^{-(g(\xi,t)-g(\xi,\tau))|\xi|^{1+s}}\\
&\leq C(1+(t-\tau)|\xi|^{1+s})^{n}|\xi|^{-n}e^{-c_{0}(t-\tau)|\xi|^{1+s}},\end{split}

where the constant
C
depends on the index
n
. Therefore we have by the above and by
\delta(2^{-j}\xi)=0
for
\xi\notin B_{2^{j+1}}\setminus B_{2^{j-1}}
that

|\partial_{\xi}^{\gamma}(e^{-(g(\xi,t)-g(\xi,\tau))|\xi|^{1+s}}\delta(2^{-j}\xi))|\leq C(1+(t-\tau)|\xi|^{1+s})^{n}|\xi|^{-n}e^{-c_{0}(t-\tau)|\xi|^{1+s}}\tilde{\delta}(2^{-j}\xi),

where
\tilde{\delta}
is a smooth function such that
\tilde{\delta}(\xi)=0
for
\xi\notin B_{2^{j+1}}\setminus B_{2^{j-1}}
. Using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, Plancherel’s theorem and recalling that
\delta(2^{-j}\xi)=0
for
\xi\notin B_{2^{j+1}}\setminus B_{2^{j-1}}
have that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}

\displaystyle\big(e^{-(g(\xi,t)-g(\xi,\tau))|\xi|^{1+s}}\delta(2^{-j}\xi)\big)\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{(1+|2^{j}x|^{n})^{2}}dx\right)^{\tfrac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(1+|2^{j}x|^{n})^{2}|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\big(e^{-(g(\xi,t)-g(\xi,\tau))|\xi|^{1+s}}\delta(2^{-j}\xi)\big)|^{2}\,dx\right)^{\tfrac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C2^{-\frac{jn}{2}}\left(\sum_{|\gamma|\leq n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big|2^{jn}\partial_{\xi}^{\gamma}(e^{-(g(\xi,t)-g(\xi,\tau))|\xi|^{1+s}}\delta(2^{-j}\xi))\big|^{2}d\xi\right)^{\tfrac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C2^{-\frac{jn}{2}}\left(\int_{B_{2^{j+1}}\setminus B_{2^{j-1}}}|2^{jn}(1+(t-\tau)|\xi|^{1+s})^{n}|\xi|^{-n}e^{-c_{0}(t-\tau)|\xi|^{1+s}}|^{2}dx\right)^{\tfrac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+(t-\tau)2^{(j+1)(1+s)})^{n}e^{-c_{0}(t-\tau)2^{(j-1)(1+s)}},

where in the last inequality we used that
\xi\in B_{2^{j+1}}\setminus B_{2^{j-1}}
and the assumption
j\geq 2
.
Finally we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\big(e^{-(g(\xi,t)-g(\xi,\tau))|\xi|^{1+s}}

\displaystyle\delta(2^{-j}\xi)\big)\|_{L^{1}}\,d\tau\leq C\int_{0}^{t}(1+(t-\tau)2^{(j+1)(1+s)})^{n}e^{-c_{0}(t-\tau)2^{(j-1)(1+s)}}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq C2^{-(j-1)(1+s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}(1+2^{2(1+s)}\mu)^{n}e^{-c_{0}\mu}\,d\mu\leq C2^{-j(1+s)},

and we obtain (
A.5
).
∎

We are now ready prove Theorem
2.2
. The proof follows from small perturbation argument, where we localize the equation and freeze the coefficients. The argument is similar to the proof
of Theorem
4.1
, with the difference that the Euclidean space is not compact. We may overcome this by using Remark
2.6
instead of Lemma
2.5
.

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

We begin by noticing that applying the maximum principle, as in the proof of Theorem
4.1
, we have

(A.6)

\sup_{t<T}\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C(1+T)\sup_{t<T}\|f(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

For the Hölder continuity we localize the equation. First, fix
\delta\in(0,1)
and choose
t_{0}\in(0,T)
and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

(A.7)

\sup_{t<T}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\neq x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\\
|y-x|<\delta/2\end{subarray}}\frac{|\nabla u(y,t)-\nabla u(x,t)|}{|y-x|^{s+\alpha}}\leq 2\sup_{y\in B_{\delta/2}(x_{0})}\frac{|\nabla u(y,t_{0})-\nabla u(x_{0},t_{0})|}{|y-x_{0}|^{s+\alpha}}

Let
\eta:\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]
be a smooth cutoff function such that
\eta(r)=1
for
|r|\leq\frac{1}{2}
,
\eta(r)=0
for
|r|\geq 1
, denote

\eta_{x_{0}}(x)=\eta\left(\frac{|x-x_{0}|}{\delta}\right)

and let
v=u(x,t)\eta_{x_{0}}(x)
. Notice that

L_{A}[v]=\eta_{x_{0}}L_{A}[u]+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u(y,t)\frac{\eta_{x_{0}}(y)-\eta_{x_{0}}(x)}{\|y-x\|_{A(x,t)}^{n+1+s}}\,dy.

If
u
is a solution of the equation
\partial_{t}u=L_{A}[u]+f(x,t)
, then
v=u\eta_{x_{0}}
solves

\partial_{t}v=L_{A_{x_{0}}}[v]+\tilde{f}(x,t),

where
A_{x_{0}}=A(x_{0},t)
and

\begin{split}\tilde{f}=\eta_{x_{0}}f+\overbrace{(L_{A}-L_{A_{x_{0}}})[v]}^{=I_{1}}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u(y,t)\frac{\eta_{x_{0}}(y)-\eta_{x_{0}}(x)}{\|y-x\|_{A(x,t)}^{n+1+s}}\,dy=\eta_{x_{0}}f+I_{1}-I_{2}.\end{split}

Theorem
A.2
implies

\sup_{(0,T)}\|v(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{1+s+\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\sup_{(0,T)}\|\tilde{f}(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

We claim that it holds

(A.8)

\sup_{(0,T)}(\|I_{1}\|_{C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|I_{2}\|_{C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})})\leq C\delta^{\alpha}\sup_{(0,T)}\|v(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{1+s+\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+C_{\delta}\sup_{(0,T)}\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{1+s+\alpha/2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Since in the argument to prove (
A.8
) the time does not play any role, we drop it in order to simplify the notation. For
\mu\in[0,1]
we denote
A_{\mu}(x)=(1-\mu)A(x_{0})+\mu A(x)
and write
I_{1}
as

\begin{split}&(L_{A}-L_{A_{x_{0}}})[v](x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(v(y)-v(x))\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d}{d\mu}\left(\frac{1}{\langle A_{\mu}(x)(y-x),(y-x)\rangle}\right)^{\frac{n+1+s}{2}}\,d\mu\,dy\\
&=-\tfrac{n+1+s}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(v(y)-v(x))}{\langle A_{\mu}(x)(y-x),(y-x)\rangle^{\frac{n+1+s}{2}}}\left(\frac{\langle(A(x)-A(x_{0}))(y-x),(y-x)\rangle}{\langle A_{\mu}(x)(y-x),(y-x)\rangle}\right)\,d\mu\,dy\\
&=-\tfrac{n+1+s}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(v(y)-v(x)-\eta(|y-x|)\langle\nabla v(x),y-x\rangle)}{\langle A_{\mu}(x)(y-x),(y-x)\rangle^{\frac{n+1+s}{2}}}\left(\frac{\langle(A(x)-A(x_{0}))(y-x),(y-x)\rangle}{\langle A_{\mu}(x)(y-x),(y-x)\rangle}\right)\,d\mu\,dy,\end{split}

where the last equality follows from symmetry. Let us define
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
as

F(y,x)=(v(y)-v(x)-\eta(|y-x|)\langle\nabla v(x),y-x\rangle)\left(\frac{\langle(A(x)-A(x_{0}))(y-x),(y-x)\rangle}{\langle A_{\mu}(x)(y-x),(y-x)\rangle}\right),

for
y\neq x
and
F(x,x)=0
. Then one may check that
F
satisfies the conditions in Remark
2.6
with a constant

\kappa_{0}=C\delta^{\alpha}\|v\|_{C^{1+s+\alpha}}+C_{\delta}\|v\|_{C^{1+s+\alpha/2}}.

We leave the details for the reader as it follows from (
3.10
) and using an argument similar to the one in the proof of Lemma
3.1
. We may thus use Remark
2.6
and obtain

\begin{split}\|I_{1}(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}&\leq C\delta^{\alpha}\|v(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{1+s+\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+C_{\delta}\|v(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{1+s+\alpha/2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\\
&\leq C\delta^{\alpha}\|v(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{1+s+\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+C_{\delta}\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{1+s+\alpha/2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.\end{split}

The argument to bound the term
I_{2}
is similar. We write it as

\begin{split}I_{2}=&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u(y)\frac{(\eta_{x_{0}}(y)-\eta_{x_{0}}(x))}{\|y-x\|_{A(x)}^{n+1+s}}\,dy=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{(u(y)-u(x))(\eta_{x_{0}}(y)-\eta_{x_{0}}(x))}{\|y-x\|_{A(x)}^{n+1+s}}\,dy\\
&+u(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\eta_{x_{0}}(y)-\eta_{x_{0}}(x)-\eta(|y-x|)\langle\nabla\eta_{x_{0}}(x),y-x\rangle}{\|y-x\|_{A(x)}^{n+1+s}}\,dy.\end{split}

We apply Remark
2.6
, by first choosing

F_{1}(y,x)=(u(y)-u(x))(\eta_{x_{0}}(y)-\eta_{x_{0}}(x))

and then

F_{2}(y,x)=\eta_{x_{0}}(y)-\eta_{x_{0}}(x)-\eta(|y-x|)\langle\nabla\eta_{x_{0}}(x),y-x\rangle

to infer

\|I_{2}(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C_{\delta}\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Hence we have (
A.8
).

Let us finally show how the claim follows from (
A.8
). First, by choosing
\delta
small we have

\sup_{t<T}\|v(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{1+s+\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C_{\delta}\sup_{t<T}(\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{1+s+\alpha/2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|f(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}).

By using
\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{1+s+\alpha}(B_{\delta/2}(x_{0}))}\leq\|v(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{1+s+\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
and by the choice of
x_{0}
and
t_{0}
in (
A.7
) we deduce

\sup_{t<T}\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{1+s+\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C_{\delta}\sup_{t<T}(\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{1+s+\alpha/2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|f(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}).

By interpolation (
2.2
) we have

\begin{split}\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{1+s+\alpha/2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}&\leq C\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{1+s+\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\theta}\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{1-\theta}\\
&\leq\varepsilon\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{1+s+\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+C_{\varepsilon}\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.\end{split}

By choosing
\varepsilon
small we then have

\sup_{t<T}\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{1+s+\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\sup_{t<T}(\|f(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}).

The claim then follows from (
A.6
).
∎

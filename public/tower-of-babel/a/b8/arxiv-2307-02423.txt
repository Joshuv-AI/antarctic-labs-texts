Matroids Arising From Nested Sequences of Flats In Projective And Affine Geometries
Matthew Mizell, James Oxley

Abstract
. Targets are matroids that arise from a nested sequence of flats in a projective geometry. This class of matroids was introduced by Nelson and Nomoto, who found the forbidden induced restrictions for binary targets. This paper generalizes their result to targets arising from projective geometries over G ​ F ​ ( q ) GF(q) . We also consider targets arising from nested sequences of affine flats and determine the forbidden induced restrictions for affine targets.

Throughout this paper, we follow the notation and terminology of [
References
]. All matroids considered here are simple. This means, for example, that when we contract an element, we always simplify the result. An

Let

. We call

. For a subset

of
E(PG(r-1,q))

a

, or a

is the union of all sets

for

Consider an analogous construction for graphs, that is, take a sequence

of complete graphs where

that is adjacent to every vertex

whose vertex set is

, that is a

2.
Preliminary Results

Throughout the paper, we will refer to flats and hyperplanes of
PG(r-1,q)
as

and

, respectively. Let

be a restriction of

in the matroid

is uniquely determined by the sequence
(r_{0},r_{1},\dots,r_{k})
of ranks of the nested sequence

of projective flats. Note that we shall often write

and

and

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
(E_{0},E_{1},\dots,E_{k})
and
(F_{0},F_{1},\dots,F_{k})
be nested sequences of flats in

such that
r(E_{i})=r(F_{i})
for all

. Let

and

be the union, respectively, of all

and of all

for the even numbers

. Then
PG(r-1,q)|G_{E}\cong PG(r-1,q)|G_{F}
.

Proof.

Let

be the smallest

such that
r(E_{i})>0
. Let
\{b_{h,1},b_{h,2},\dots,b_{h,m_{h}}\}
and
\{d_{h,1},d_{h,2},\dots,d_{h,m_{h}}\}
be bases

and

of

and
PG(r-1,q)|F_{h}
, respectively. Let
B_{0}=B_{1}=\dots=B_{h-1}=\emptyset
and
D_{0}=D_{1}=\dots=D_{h-1}=\emptyset
. For

, assume that

and

have been defined. Let

and

be bases of

and

, respectively, such that

and

. Let

and

The last result means that we can refer to a simple

-representable matroid

-representations of

for

The class of targets over

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
(G,R)
be a
2
-coloring of

is a target and

. Then exactly one of

.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.2
,
PG(r-1,q)|(G\cap F)
is a target corresponding to a nested sequence
(F_{0}^{\prime},F_{1}^{\prime},\dots,F^{\prime}_{k-1},F)
of projective flats. By, for example, [

or

is

. Either

or

is contained in some proper projective flat of

. Therefore, either

or

.
∎

We refer to the rank of the set of green elements in a projective flat

as the

of

has green rank

, we say that

is a

. Furthermore, if a projective hyperplane has green rank

, then it is a

. Red rank, red flats, and red hyperplanes are defined analogously. From the last lemma, it follows that a projective flat can either be a green flat or a red flat, but not both.

We now show that every contraction of a target is a target. Consider contracting a green element
e
in
M
. If a parallel class in the contraction contains at least one green point, then, after the simplification, the resulting point will be green. If there are only red points in the parallel class, then, after the simplification, the resulting point is red.

Proposition 2.4
.

The class of targets over
GF(q)
is closed under contractions.

Proof.

Let
(G,R)
be a

, so

Now assume

. Then

(\si(F_{m}-e),\si(F_{m+1}-e),\dots,\si(F_{k}-e))

(F_{m}^{\prime},F_{m+1}^{\prime},\dots,F_{k}^{\prime})

is entirely green and, for each

is a rank-
4
affine flat that contains exactly four green points
g_{1},g_{2},g_{3},
and
g_{4}
. Since
AG(r-1,2)|\{g_{1},g_{2},g_{3},g_{4}\}\cong U_{4,4}
, we have a contradiction. We conclude that
4.17.4
holds.

By
4.17.3
and
4.17.4
, we must have that

is half-green and half-red. As

. Thus
V_{2,1}=Z_{2,1}

X_{2}
is green, there is a green point
g_{1}
in
F_{2,2}-V

g_{2}
to be a point in

4.16
,
\cl(P_{1}\cup P_{2})

4

and
g_{4}
. Moreover,
AG(r-1,2)|\{g_{1},g_{2},g_{3},g_{4}\}\cong U_{4,4}

∎

Proof of Theorem

2

r

does not contain a rank-
3
flat
F

4.11

r(R)=r
. Now, by Lemma
4.13
, there is a green hyperplane

and a red

r(F_{i,j})=r-2

. We proceed by showing there are no possible colorings of the hyperplanes in

4.18.5
.

If

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Assume that
G
is an affine target over

for

. If there is an affine flat

4.7

4.13

4.19.7

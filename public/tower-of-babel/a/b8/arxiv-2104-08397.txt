Fermionic and bosonic fluctuation-dissipation theorem from a deformed AdS holographic model at finite temperature and chemical potential
Nathan G. Caldeira 1, Eduardo Folco Capossoli 2, Carlos A. D. Zarro 1, Henrique Boschi-Filho 1

Abstract
In this work we study fluctuations and dissipation of a string in a deformed anti-de Sitter (AdS) space at finite temperature and density. The deformed AdS space is a charged black hole solution of the Einstein-Maxwell-Dilaton action. In this background we take into account the backreaction on the horizon function from an exponential deformation of the AdS space. From this model we compute the admittance and study the influence of the temperature and the chemical potential on it. We calculate the two-point correlations functions, and the mean square displacement for bosonic and fermionic cases, from which we obtain the short and large time approximations. For the long time, we obtain a sub-diffusive regime ∼ log ⁡ t \sim\log t . Combining the results from the admittance and the correlations functions we check the fluctuation-dissipation theorem for bosonic and fermionic systems.

II
Fluctuation-dissipation theorem

In this section we are going to state the fluctuation-dissipation theorem, for the bosonic and fermionic cases which depend crucially on the sign of the chemical potential
\mu
.

First of all, let us define the symmetric Green’s function in terms of the thermal correlation functions as:

G_{\rm Sym}(t)\equiv\frac{1}{2}\left(\langle x(t)x(0)\rangle+\langle x(0)x(t)\rangle\right)\,.

(3)

For the bosonic case (
\mu<0
), we have that the fluctuation-dissipation theorem in the presence of a chemical potential can be written as
Zubarev

\displaystyle G_{\rm Sym}(t)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[\left(1+2n_{B}\right)\Im\chi(\omega)\right]\,,

(4)

and for the fermionic case (
\mu>0
)one has
Markov:2009ue

\displaystyle G_{\rm Sym}(t)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[\left(1+2n_{F}\right)\Im\chi(\omega)\right]\,,

(5)

where
\mathcal{F}^{-1}[\cdots]
is the inverse Fourier transform,
n_{B}
is the Bose-Einstein distribution,
n_{F}
the Fermi-Dirac distribution, and
\Im\chi(\omega)
is the imaginary part of the admittance.

In the following, we are going to define our holographic set up of a fluctuating string in a deformed AdS background with finite temperature and chemical potential from where we obtain the admittance and the correlation functions. As far as we are concerned, we could not find a discussion of the fluctuation-dissipation theorem in the holographic approach for bosons and fermions at finite density.

IV
Admittance, correlation functions and mean square displacement

In this section we will obtain the equations of motion from Nambu-Goto action and compute the main quantities related to the Brownian motion, such as, the admittance, the mean square displacement and the correlation functions.

IV.1
Nambu-Goto action and equations of motion

Here, we will consider our probe string attached to a probe brane in the EMD background. This system is dual to a particle in a thermal bath with a finite chemical potential. The metric of the deformed and backreacted AdS-RN spacetime Eq. (
1
), in the
r

ds^{2}=e^{\frac{k}{r^{2}}}\left[-r^{2}f(r)dt^{2}+{r^{2}}\left(\eta_{ij}dx^{i}dx^{j}\right)+\frac{dr^{2}}{r^{2}f(r)}\right].

(30)

Besides, the dynamics of such a string will be given by the Nambu-Goto action:

S_{NG}=-\frac{1}{2\pi\alpha^{\prime}}\int d\tau d\sigma\sqrt{-\gamma}\,,

(31)

where
\alpha^{\prime}
is the Regge slope,
\gamma={\rm det}(\gamma_{\alpha\beta})
and
\gamma_{\alpha\beta}=g_{mn}\partial_{\alpha}X^{m}\partial_{\beta}X^{n}
is the induced metric on the worldsheet with
m,n=0,1,2,3,5
.

We choose a static gauge, where
t=\tau
,
r=\sigma
and
X=X(\tau,\sigma)
, as done in Refs.
Edalati:2012tc
;
Giataganas:2018ekx
. By using the metric, Eq. (
30
), and expanding the Nambu-Goto action, Eq. (
31

\displaystyle S_{NG}\approx-\frac{1}{4\pi\alpha^{\prime}}\int d\tau d\sigma\left[\;\dot{X}^{2}\frac{e^{\frac{k}{r^{2}}}}{f(r)}-X^{\prime 2}r^{4}f(r)e^{\frac{k}{r^{2}}}\right]\,,

where
\dot{X}=\partial_{\tau=t}X

X^{\prime}=\partial_{\sigma=r}X

The equation of motion is

\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(r^{4}f(r)e^{\frac{k}{r^{2}}}X^{\prime}(t,r)\right)-\frac{e^{\frac{k}{r^{2}}}}{f(r)}\ddot{X}(t,r)=0\,,

where

Using the ansatz

we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{f(r)}\frac{d^{2}h_{\omega}(r_{*})}{dr^{2}_{*}}+\left(-\frac{2k}{r}+2r\right)\frac{dh_{\omega}}{dr_{*}}+\frac{\omega^{2}}{f(r)}h_{\omega}(r)=0\,.

we obtain a Schrödinger-like equation

\displaystyle V(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-f(r)\left[\left(-\frac{k^{2}}{r^{2}}+k-2r^{2}\right)f(r)+r\left(k-r^{2}\right)f^{\prime}(r)\right]\,.

From the above equation, one has

The Eq. (

\displaystyle h^{C}_{\omega}(r)\approx A_{1}\left(\frac{e^{-\frac{k}{2r_{h}^{2}}}}{r_{h}}+\frac{i\omega r_{h}e^{\frac{k}{2r_{h}^{2}}}}{3r^{3}}\right)\,,

E=E(A_{t},\vec{A})
and taking into account the approximate Nambu-Goto action, one has:

S\approx-\frac{1}{4\pi\alpha^{\prime}}\int d\tau d\sigma\left[\;\dot{X}^{2}\frac{e^{\frac{k}{r^{2}}}}{f(r)}-X^{\prime 2}r^{4}f(r)e^{\frac{k}{r^{2}}}\right]+\int dt\left(A_{t}+\vec{A}\cdot\vec{\dot{x}}\right)\Big|_{r=r_{b}}\,,

(39)

where the second integral is a surface term. Such a term was chosen in an arbitrary direction, and it does not participate in the bulk dynamics. Choosing
A_{t}=0
and integrating by parts the surface term, one gets:

S\approx-\frac{1}{4\pi\alpha^{\prime}}\int dtdr\left[\;\dot{X}^{2}\frac{e^{\frac{k}{r^{2}}}}{f(r)}-X^{\prime 2}r^{4}f(r)e^{\frac{k}{r^{2}}}\right]-\int dt\;F(t)\,\left.\left(\frac{\partial X(t,r)}{\partial r}\right)\right|_{r=r_{b}},

(40)

with
\tau=t
and
\sigma=r
. Computing
\delta S/\delta X^{\prime}=0
and imposing Neumann boundary condition on the brane, one can write the force in a Fourier domain as:

\displaystyle F(\omega)=\frac{A_{1}}{2\pi\alpha^{\prime}}\left[-i\omega r_{h}e^{\frac{k}{2r_{h}^{2}}}f(r_{b})e^{\frac{k}{r_{b}^{2}}}\right]\,.

(41)

More details on the calculation of
F(\omega)
can be found with in Refs.
Tong:2012nf
;
Edalati:2012tc
;
Giataganas:2018ekx
;
Caldeira:2020sot
;
Caldeira:2020rir
.

Therefore the admittance is

\displaystyle\chi(\omega)\equiv\frac{h^{C}_{\omega}(\omega)}{F(\omega)}=\frac{2\pi i\alpha^{\prime}}{\omega r_{h}^{2}e^{\frac{k}{r_{h}^{2}}}f(r_{b})e^{\frac{k}{r_{b}^{2}}}}\left(1+\frac{i\omega r_{h}^{2}e^{\frac{k}{r_{h}^{2}}}}{r_{b}^{3}}\right)\,.

(42)

In the limit
r_{b}\gg r_{h}
,
r_{b}\gg k
and
f(r_{b})\to 1
the admittance reads

\chi(\omega)=-2\pi\alpha^{\prime}\frac{e^{-\frac{k}{r_{h}^{2}}}}{i\omega r_{h}^{2}}=\frac{2\pi i\alpha^{\prime}e^{-x}}{\omega r_{h}^{2}}\,.

(43)

Note that the horizon radius
r_{h}
is a function of the Hawking temperature, Eq.(
25
), so that

r_{h}=\frac{\pi T}{\left|g(x)-\frac{\mu^{2}}{k}c(x)\right|}

(44)

Then the admittance, Eq.(
43
), can be written as

\chi(\omega)=\frac{2i\alpha^{\prime}e^{-x}}{\omega\pi T^{2}}\left|g(x)-\frac{\mu^{2}}{k}c(x)\right|^{2}\,,

(45)

where we recall that
x=k/r_{h}^{2}
, and then one can see that the admittance is a non-trivial function of the temperature
T
.
This is one of the main results of this work.
Note that this result is independent of the sign of the chemical potential although it will be relevant in the correlations functions to be calculated in the next section.

Using the fact that the admittance can be written as
Giataganas:2018ekx

\chi(\omega)=2\pi\alpha^{\prime}\left(\frac{i}{\gamma\omega}-\frac{\Delta m}{\gamma^{2}}+\mathcal{O}(\omega)\right),

(46)

we find in our case

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-\frac{k}{r_{h}^{2}}}}{r_{h}^{2}}\left(1+\frac{k}{r_{b}^{2}}+O\left(\frac{k^{2}}{r_{b}^{4}}\right)\right),

(47)

\displaystyle\Delta m

\displaystyle=\frac{r_{h}^{4}e^{\frac{2k}{r_{h}^{2}}}}{r_{b}^{3}}\left(1+\frac{k}{r_{b}^{2}}+O\left(\frac{k^{2}}{r_{b}^{4}}\right)\right),

(48)

where
\gamma
is the friction coefficient and
\Delta m
corresponds to the change in the bare mass
m
of the particle described by the Langevin equation
Kubo1966
;
Kubo1991

(see also
Banerjee:2015vmo
).

If one considers the admittance for case of the string in pure AdS-Schwarzschild, one finds
deBoer:2008gu

\chi_{AdS}(\omega)=\frac{2i\alpha^{\prime}}{\omega\pi T^{2}}.

(49)

In Refs.
Caldeira:2020sot
;
Caldeira:2020rir
, the problem of a probe string immersed in a black hole metric deformed by an exponential conformal factor
\exp(k/r^{2})
was studied. Regarding backreaction effects, the Ref.
Caldeira:2020sot
did not consider them and the admittance found was:

\chi_{NBR}(\omega)=\frac{2i\alpha^{\prime}e^{-x}}{\omega\pi T^{2}}\,,\qquad{\rm(No\,\,Backreaction)}\,,

(50)

while in Ref.
Caldeira:2020rir
, taking into account backreaction, it was obtained that

\chi_{BR}(\omega)=\frac{2i\alpha^{\prime}e^{-x}}{\omega\pi T^{2}}(g(x))^{2}\,,\qquad{\rm(Backreacted)}.

(51)

Note that these results are particular cases of the admittance
\chi(\omega)
computed in the present work, Eq. (
45
), where finite density and an exponential deformation with backreaction were included, so that

\displaystyle\chi(\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\chi_{AdS}(\omega)\left|g(x)-\frac{\mu^{2}}{k}c(x)\right|^{2}e^{-x}\;;

(52)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\chi_{NBR}(\omega)\left|g(x)-\frac{\mu^{2}}{k}c(x)\right|^{2}\;;

(53)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\chi_{BR}(\omega)\left|1-\frac{\mu^{2}}{k}\frac{c(x)}{g(x)}\right|^{2}\;.

(54)

These equations show the influence of the chemical potential
\mu
on the admittance
\chi(\omega)
.
In particular, Eq. (
54
), displays this extension codified in the term
\mu^{2}c(x)/kg(x)
. The admittance given by Eqs. (
50
), (
51
), and
(
45
) are depicted in Fig.
3
. This picture shows that the presence of a finite chemical potential and its increase dislocates the curve of the imaginary part of the admittance towards low temperatures.

IV.3
Correlation functions and mean square displacement with chemical potential

In this section, we will calculate the correlation functions and the mean square displacement of the string endpoint on the brane with non-zero chemical potential.
Starting with the definition of the density operator in the grand canonical ensemble

\rho_{0}=\frac{e^{-\beta(\sum_{\omega>0}\omega a_{\omega}^{\dagger}a_{\omega}-\mu a_{\omega}^{\dagger}a_{\omega}})}{\text{Tr}\left(\,e^{-\beta(H-\mu N)}\right)}\,,

(55)

where the creation and annihilation operators satisfy

\displaystyle\langle a^{\dagger}_{\omega}a_{\omega}\rangle=\frac{\delta_{\omega\omega^{\prime}}}{e^{\beta\left(\omega-\mu\right)}\mp 1};\qquad\langle a^{\dagger}_{\omega}a^{\dagger}_{\omega}\rangle=0;\qquad\langle a_{\omega}a_{\omega}\rangle=0,

(56)

and the minus (plus) sign corresponds to the bosonic (fermionic) case, one can calculate
the correlation function:

\displaystyle\langle x(t)x(0)\rangle

\displaystyle\equiv\langle X(t,r_{b})X(0,r_{b})\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\omega>0}\sum_{\omega^{\prime}>0}\Big(\frac{h^{*}_{\omega}(r_{b})h_{\omega^{\prime}}(r_{b})e^{i\omega t}+h_{\omega}(r_{b})h^{*}_{\omega^{\prime}}(r_{b})e^{-i\omega t}}{e^{\beta\left(\omega-\mu\right)}\mp 1}+h_{\omega}(r_{b})h^{*}_{\omega^{\prime}}(r_{b})e^{-i\omega t}\Big)\delta_{\omega\omega^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi\alpha^{\prime}r_{h}^{2}f^{\prime}(r_{h})}{2\log\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)}\sum_{\omega>0}\frac{1}{\omega}\frac{e^{-\frac{k}{r^{2}_{h}}}}{r^{2}_{h}}\left\{\left(1+\frac{\omega^{2}r^{4}_{h}e^{\frac{2k}{r^{2}_{h}}}}{9r_{b}^{6}}\right)\right.

(57)

\displaystyle\quad\left.+\,\Re\left[B^{*}\left(1+\frac{\omega^{2}r^{4}_{h}e^{\frac{2k}{r^{2}_{h}}}}{9r_{b}^{6}}-\frac{2i\omega r^{2}_{h}e^{\frac{k}{r^{2}_{h}}}}{3r_{b}^{3}}\right)\right]\right\}\left(\frac{2\cos(\omega t)}{e^{\beta\left(\omega-\mu\right)}\mp 1}+e^{-i\omega t}\right)\,,

(58)

where we used the solution in terms of the ingoing and outgoing modes
h_{\omega}(r)=A[h^{out}_{\omega}(r)+Bh^{in}_{\omega}(r)]
(see the Appendix
B
).
Then, we get:

\displaystyle\langle x(t)x(0)\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi\alpha^{\prime}r_{h}^{2}f^{\prime}(r_{h})}{\log\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)}\sum_{\omega>0}\frac{1}{\omega}\frac{e^{-\frac{k}{r^{2}_{h}}}}{r^{2}_{h}}\left(\frac{2\cos(\omega t)}{e^{\beta\left(\omega-\mu\right)}\mp 1}+e^{-i\omega t}\right)\,.

(59)

This sum can be approximated by an integral. For this purpose we use that

\Delta\omega={\pi r_{h}^{2}f^{\prime}(r_{h})}/{\log\left({\epsilon^{-1}}\right)}\sim d\omega\,,

so that we write

\displaystyle\langle x(t)x(0)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha^{\prime}e^{-\frac{k}{r_{h}^{2}}}}{r^{2}_{h}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\omega}{\omega}\left(\frac{2\cos(\omega t)}{e^{\beta\left(\omega-\mu\right)}\mp 1}+e^{-i\omega t}\right)

(60)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle x(0)x(t)\rangle^{*}\,.

(62)

In a similar way we get that

\displaystyle\langle x(t)x(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle X(t,r_{b})X(t,r_{b})\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\omega>0}|h^{C}_{\omega}(r_{b})|^{2}\Big(\frac{2}{e^{\beta(\omega-\mu)}\mp 1}+1\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha^{\prime}e^{-\frac{k}{r_{h}^{2}}}}{r^{2}_{h}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\omega}{\omega}\left(\frac{2}{e^{\beta(\omega-\mu)}\mp 1}+1\right)

\displaystyle=\langle x(0)x(0)\rangle

(63)

Now, we can compute the regularized mean square displacement defined by:

s^{2}_{\rm reg}(t)=\langle:[x(t)-x(0)]^{2}:\rangle\equiv\langle:[X(t,r_{b})-X(0,r_{b})]^{2}:\rangle\,,

(64)

where we used the notation
:[\cdots]:
to represent the normal ordering prescription.

Plugging Eqs. (
60
), (
62
) and (
IV.3
) into the definition of the mean square displacement, we find:

s^{2}_{\rm reg}(t)=\frac{\alpha^{\prime}e^{-\frac{k}{r_{h}^{2}}}}{r^{2}_{h}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\omega}{\omega}\left(\frac{4(1-\cos(\omega t))}{e^{\beta(\omega-\mu)}\mp 1}\right)=\frac{8\alpha^{\prime}e^{-\frac{k}{r_{h}^{2}}}}{r^{2}_{h}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\omega}{\omega}\left(\frac{\sin^{2}(\frac{\omega t}{2})}{e^{\beta(\omega-\mu)}\mp 1}\right).

(65)

Up to now, we have been considering the bosonic and fermionic together. From now on, we will split this analysis into two separate cases since the sign of the chemical potential will be crucial.

IV.3.1
Bosonic case

In order to calculate the mean square displacement given by Eq. (
65
),
for the bosonic case, we take
\mu<0
, and consider the series expansion

\frac{1}{e^{\beta(\omega-\mu)}-1}=\frac{e^{-\beta(\omega-\mu)}}{1-e^{-\beta(\omega-\mu)}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-\beta(\omega-\mu)(n+1)}\,.

(66)

Then, the bosonic regularized mean square displacement reads

s^{2}_{\rm Breg}(t)=\frac{8\alpha^{\prime}e^{-\frac{k}{r_{h}^{2}}}}{r^{2}_{h}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\omega}{\omega}e^{-\beta(\omega-\mu)n}\sin^{2}(\frac{\omega t}{2}).

(67)

Performing the integral, one gets

s^{2}_{\rm Breg}(t)=\frac{2\alpha^{\prime}e^{-\frac{k}{r_{h}^{2}}}}{r^{2}_{h}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{\beta\mu n}\log\left(1+\frac{t^{2}}{n^{2}\beta^{2}}\right)\,.

(68)

This expression can be rewritten as

\displaystyle s^{2}_{\rm Breg}(t)=\frac{2\alpha^{\prime}e^{-\frac{k}{r_{h}^{2}}}}{r^{2}_{h}}\left(2{\rm Li_{0}}^{(1,0)}\left(0,e^{\mu}\right)-e^{\mu}\left({\Phi}^{(0,1,0)}\left(e^{\mu},0,1+i\frac{t}{\beta}\right)+{\Phi}^{(0,1,0)}\left(e^{\mu},0,1-i\frac{t}{\beta}\right)\right)\right),

(69)

where Li
{}_{n}^{(1,0)}(x,y)
is the first derivative of the polylogarithm function of order
n
with respect to its first argument
x
and
\Phi^{(0,1,0)}(x,y,z)
is the first derivative of the Lerch transcendent function with respect to the second argument
y
.

Now, it is interesting to consider the late time approximation
t\gg\beta
for Eq. (
68
) as

\displaystyle s^{2}_{\rm Breg}(t)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{2\alpha^{\prime}e^{-\frac{k}{r_{h}^{2}}}}{r^{2}_{h}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{\beta\mu n}\log\left(\frac{t^{2}}{n^{2}\beta^{2}}\right)

(71)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\alpha^{\prime}e^{-\frac{k}{r_{h}^{2}}}}{r^{2}_{h}}\left(-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{\beta\mu n}\log\left(n^{2}\right)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{\beta\mu n}\log\left(\frac{t^{2}}{\beta^{2}}\right)\right)

(72)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\alpha^{\prime}e^{-\frac{k}{r_{h}^{2}}}}{r^{2}_{h}}\left(-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{\beta\mu n}\log\left(n^{2}\right)+\frac{2}{e^{-\beta\mu}-1}\log\left(\frac{t}{\beta}\right)\right)\,,

(73)

which converges since
\mu<0
. This result corresponds to a sub-diffusive regime as
s^{2}\sim\log t
, as seen in Ref.
sinai-like
.
This behavior is shown in Fig.
4
, for various values of the time
t
against the temperature
T
or the chemical potential
\mu
for positive and negative
k
.

If one considers the limit
\mu/T\to 0
in Eq. (
68
), one can rewrite that sum as

\displaystyle s^{2}_{\rm Breg}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\alpha^{\prime}e^{-\frac{k}{r_{h}^{2}}}}{r^{2}_{h}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{\beta\mu n}\log\left(1+\frac{t^{2}}{n^{2}\beta^{2}}\right)\,

(74)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{2\alpha^{\prime}e^{-\frac{k}{r_{h}^{2}}}}{r^{2}_{h}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\log\left(1+\frac{t^{2}}{n^{2}\beta^{2}}\right)\,

(75)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\alpha^{\prime}e^{-\frac{k}{r_{h}^{2}}}}{r^{2}_{h}}\log\left(\frac{\beta\sinh\left(\frac{\pi t}{\beta}\right)}{\pi t}\right)\,.

(76)

In this case, for long times,
t\gg\beta/\pi
, one has

\displaystyle s^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\alpha^{\prime}e^{-\frac{k}{r_{h}^{2}}}}{r^{2}_{h}}\left[\log\left(e^{\frac{\pi t}{\beta}}-e^{-\frac{\pi t}{\beta}}\right)-\log\left(\frac{2\pi t}{\beta}\right)\right]\,

(77)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi\alpha^{\prime}e^{-\frac{k}{r_{h}^{2}}}}{\beta r^{2}_{h}}t\,.

(78)

So, in the limit
\mu/T\to 0
, one recovers the usual linear behavior for the mean square displacement.

IV.3.2
Fermionic case

Here, we are going to consider the fermionic regularized mean square displacement. From Eq. (
65
), taking the Fermi Dirac distribution with
\mu>0
, we have

s^{2}_{\rm Freg}(t)=\frac{8\alpha^{\prime}e^{-\frac{k}{r_{h}^{2}}}}{r^{2}_{h}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\omega}{\omega}\left(\frac{\sin^{2}(\frac{\omega t}{2})}{e^{\beta(\omega-\mu)}+1}\right)\,.

(80)

We can calculate this integral using the Sommerfeld expansion:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\omega}{\omega}\left(\frac{\sin^{2}(\frac{\omega t}{2})}{e^{\beta(\omega-\mu)}+1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\mu}\frac{\sin^{2}(\frac{\omega t}{2})}{\omega}d\omega+\left.\frac{\pi^{2}}{6\beta^{2}}\frac{d}{d\omega}\left(\frac{\sin^{2}(\frac{\omega t}{2})}{\omega}\right)\right|_{\omega=\mu}

(81)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(-\text{Ci}(t\mu)+\log(t\mu)+\gamma)+\frac{\pi^{2}}{6\beta^{2}}\frac{\mu t\sin(\mu t)-2\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\mu t}{2}\right)}{2\mu^{2}},

(82)

where we kept terms up to order
{1}/{\mu^{2}\beta^{2}}
,
\text{Ci}(z)=-\int_{z}^{\infty}\frac{\cos(s)}{s}\,ds
is the Cosine integral function and
\gamma
is Euler’s constant, with numerical value
\gamma\approx 0.577216
.

In the low temperature regime,
\mu\beta=\frac{\mu}{T}\gg 1
, we can disregard the second term in the above equation and obtain for large times
t\gg\mu

s^{2}_{\rm Freg}(t)\approx\frac{4\alpha^{\prime}e^{-\frac{k}{r_{h}^{2}}}}{r^{2}_{h}}\left(\log\left(t\mu\right)+\gamma\right)

(83)

which is again a sub-diffusive regime

s^{2}\sim\log t
, as in the bosonic case, or the ones seen in Ref.
sinai-like
.

V
Checking the fluctuation-dissipation theorem for bosonic and fermionic cases

Now, after calculating the admittance and the correlation functions for the bosonic and fermionic systems, we are ready to check the fluctuation-dissipation theorem in both cases, as stated in section
II
.

First of all, we can rewrite the r.h.s of Eqs. (
4
) and (
5
) as

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[\left(1+2n_{B}\right)\Im\chi(\omega)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\omega\left(1+\frac{2}{e^{\beta(|\omega|-\mu)}-1}\right)\Im\chi(|\omega|)e^{i\omega t}\,,

(86)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[\left(1+2n_{F}\right)\Im\chi(\omega)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\omega\left(1+\frac{2}{e^{\beta(|\omega|-\mu)}+1}\right)\Im\chi(|\omega|)e^{i\omega t}\,,

(87)

where we are considering the frequencies as positive in the physical quantities. From the admittance, Eq. (
43
), the above equations can be calculated as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\omega\left(1+\frac{2}{e^{\beta(|\omega|-\mu)}\mp 1}\right)\Im\chi(|\omega|)e^{i\omega t}=\frac{\alpha^{\prime}e^{-\frac{k}{r_{h}^{2}}}}{r_{h}^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{d\omega}{|\omega|}\left(1+\frac{2}{e^{\beta(|\omega|-\mu)}\mp 1}\right)e^{i\omega t}\,,

(88)

where the minus (plus) sign represents bosons (fermions),

On the other hand, from the bosonic and fermionic correlation functions, Eqs. (
60
) and (
62
), the l.h.s. of Eqs. (
4
) and (
5
) become

\displaystyle G_{\rm Sym}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha^{\prime}e^{-\frac{k}{r_{h}^{2}}}}{r^{2}_{h}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\omega}{\omega}\left(\frac{4\cos(\omega t)}{e^{\beta\left(\omega-\mu\right)}\mp 1}+e^{-i\omega t}+e^{i\omega t}\right)

(89)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha^{\prime}e^{-\frac{k}{r_{h}^{2}}}}{r^{2}_{h}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\omega}{|\omega|}\left(\frac{2\left(e^{-i\omega t}+e^{i\omega t}\right)}{e^{\beta\left(|\omega|-\mu\right)}\mp 1}+e^{-i\omega t}+e^{i\omega t}\right)\,,

(90)

where again the minus (plus) sign represents bosons (fermions).
Noting that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega f(|\omega|)e^{i\omega t}+\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega f(|\omega|)e^{-i\omega t}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\omega f(|\omega|)e^{i\omega t}\,,

(91)

we finally have that the symmetric Green’s function given by Eq. (
90
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle G_{\rm Sym}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha^{\prime}e^{-\frac{k}{r_{h}^{2}}}}{r^{2}_{h}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{d\omega}{|\omega|}\left(\frac{2}{e^{\beta\left(|\omega|-\mu\right)}\mp 1}+1\right)e^{i\omega t}\,,

(92)

which coincides with Eq. (
88
). Then, we checked the Eqs. (
4
) and (
5
), completing our verification of the fluctuation-dissipation theorem in the holographic string/gauge deformed metric with bakcreaction at finite temperature and chemical potential.

VI
Conclusions

In this work we investigated the effects of a conformal exponential deformation and its backreaction in an AdS-RN spacetime. Then using the AdS/CFT dictionary we studied the fluctuation and dissipation of a test particle in a thermal bath with finite chemical potential. Solving the EMD field equations we obtain the backreacted horizon function
f(r)
depending on the chemical potential
\mu
and the deformation parameter
k
. From this horizon function we obtain the Hawking temperature which is also the temperature of the dual field theory, as a function of the horizon radius
r_{h}
, as can be seen in Fig.
2
. From the Nambu-Goto action we obtain the equation of motion for the probe string in the metric considered. The solutions of this equation in different regions are obtained from a monodromy patch procedure. Then, we calculate the admittance or linear response, and the friction coefficient
\gamma
.

An important contribution of this work is given by the admittance calculated in Eq. (
45
), which depends on the deformed metric with backreaction at finite temperature and chemical potential. The temperature dependence of this quantity is a non-trivial one. Fig.

summarizes this result in comparison with others discussed previously in the literature. Note that these other models can be understood as particular cases of the one presented here.

Another important result obtained here is the diffusion of the particle represented by the string endpoint. It was analyzed considering bosonic and fermionic statistical distributions. In the two cases we observed that the mean square displacement behaves as
s^{2}\sim\log t
for large times indicating a sub-diffusive regime.
Such a behavior was found in ultra-slow scaled Brownian motion, known as Sinai diffusion. Note that Ref.
sinai-like
reports this diffusion process with many physical realizations.

Therefore the sub-diffusive regime obtained here differs, for instance, from the one of a Brownian motion studied as a dual for the AdS-BTZ black hole presented in Ref.
deBoer:2008gu
. We interpret this difference due to the presence of a chemical potential in our set up. This assumption is supported by the result presented in Ref.
Caldeira:2020rir
. There, the authors considered a similar gravitational background in absence of a chemical potential and achieved the typical diffusive behavior
s^{2}\sim t
. We recovered this result in Sec.
IV.3
for bosons and fermions in the limit
\mu/T\to 0
, in the large time regime.

\frac{1}{2}\left(\langle x(t)x(0)\rangle+\langle x(0)x(t)\rangle\right)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[\left(1+2n_{B/F}\right)\Im\chi(\omega)\right]
.
This was accomplished from the
admittance and the correlation functions calculated in sections

Appendix A
Monodromy Patch

Here in this Appendix we will find approximate analytical solution for the Schrödinger-like Eq. equation (
36
) in three different regions in the bulk of the deformed AdS space.

A.1
Region A:
\omega^{2}\gg V(r)

The first region, dubbed as
A
, is nearby the event horizon,
i.e.

r\approx r_{h}
. In this case,
V(r)\ll\omega^{2}
, and the Schrödinger-like equation reads

\frac{d^{2}\psi(r_{*})}{dr_{*}^{2}}+\omega^{2}\psi(r_{*})=0,

(93)

which has the ingoing solution

\psi(r_{*})=A_{1}e^{-i\omega r_{*}}.

(94)

Close to the horizon (
r\approx r_{h}
), we can assume that for low frequencies we have
\omega r_{*}\ll 1
. Then one can expand Eq. (
94
) as:

\psi(r_{*})=A_{1}-iA_{1}\omega r_{*}.

(95)

Using this equation and the Bogoliubov transformation,
B(r)=-{k}/({2r^{2}})-\log(r)
, we can compute
h_{\omega}(r_{*})
in this region as:

h_{\omega}^{A}(r_{*})=\frac{e^{-\frac{k}{2r^{2}_{h}}}}{r_{h}}\left(A_{1}-i\omega A_{1}r_{*}\right)\,.

(96)

Close to the horizon, the tortoise coordinate
r_{*}
is given by

\displaystyle\int^{r}\frac{1}{r_{h}^{2}f^{\prime}(r_{h})(r^{\prime}-r_{h})}\,dr^{\prime}=\frac{1}{r_{h}^{2}f^{\prime}(r_{h})}\log\left(\frac{r}{r_{h}}-1\right)\,.

(97)

Then, Eq. (
96
) gives the expression for
h_{\omega}^{a}(r_{*})

h_{\omega}^{A}(r_{*})=A_{1}\frac{e^{-\frac{k}{2r^{2}_{h}}}}{r_{h}}\left(1-i\frac{\omega}{r_{h}^{2}f^{\prime}(r_{h})}\log\left(\frac{r}{r_{h}}-1\right)\right)\,.

(98)

A.2
Region B:
V(r)\gg\omega^{2}

Now, we analyze the region where
V(r)\gg\omega^{2}
. First, note that the potential term in Eq. (
36
) comes from the term without
\omega^{2}
in Eq. (
34
). Then, in this second region we can drop this last term and work with (
34
) in the form

\frac{d}{dr}\left(r^{4}f(r)e^{\frac{k}{r^{2}}}h_{\omega}^{\prime}\right)=0.

(99)

Solving this equation we obtain

h^{B}_{\omega}(r)=\int^{r}\frac{B_{1}}{r^{\prime 4}f(r^{\prime})e^{\frac{k}{r^{\prime 2}}}}dr^{\prime}+B_{2}.

(100)

In the IR regime, we can approximate the integral above using the fact that
f(r)
has a simple pole at the horizon. Then we can write

\displaystyle h^{B}_{\omega(\rm IR)}(r)=\frac{B_{1}}{e^{\frac{k}{r_{h}^{2}}}r_{h}^{4}f^{\prime}(r_{h})}\log\left(\frac{r}{r_{h}}-1\right)+B_{2}\,.

(101)

Comparing the last expression with (
98
) we have

\displaystyle B_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-iA_{1}r_{h}\omega e^{\frac{k}{2r_{h}^{2}}}\,,

(102)

\displaystyle B_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{1}\frac{e^{-\frac{k}{2r_{h}^{2}}}}{r_{h}}\,.

(103)

For the UV regime,
f(r)\approx 1
and the solution becomes

\displaystyle h^{B}_{\omega(\rm UV)}(r)=\int^{r}\frac{B_{1}}{r^{\prime 4}e^{\frac{k}{r^{\prime 2}}}}dr^{\prime}+B_{2}.

(104)

After integrating we get large values of
r

h^{B}_{\omega(\rm UV)}(r)\approx-\frac{B_{1}}{3r^{3}}+B_{2}\,.

(105)

A.3
Region C: The Deep UV

In the deep UV region we have that the horizon function becomes close to the unity and we must solve the equation of motion in the form

\frac{d}{dr}\left(r^{4}e^{\frac{k}{r^{2}}}h_{\omega}^{\prime}\right)+\omega^{2}e^{\frac{k}{r^{2}}}h_{\omega}=0\,,

(106)

whose general solution is

h^{C}_{\omega}(r)=C_{1}\,{}_{1}F_{1}\left(\frac{\omega^{2}}{4k},-\frac{1}{2},-\frac{k}{r^{2}}\right)+C_{2}\frac{k^{3/2}}{r^{3}}\,_{1}F_{1}\left(\frac{3}{2}+\frac{\omega^{2}}{4k},\frac{5}{2},-\frac{k}{r^{2}}\right)\,.

(107)

Close to the boundary this solution can be expanded as a power series in
1/r

\displaystyle h^{C}_{\omega}(r)=C_{1}+\frac{C_{1}\omega^{2}}{2r^{2}}+\frac{C_{2}k^{3/2}}{r^{3}}+O\left(\left(\frac{1}{r}\right)^{4}\right)\,.

(108)

Keeping only terms until
O(\omega)
,we have:

\displaystyle h^{C}_{\omega}(r)\approx C_{1}+\frac{C_{2}k^{3/2}}{r^{3}}\,.

(109)

Matching this result with Eq. (
105
), we find

\displaystyle C_{1}=B_{2}=A_{1}\frac{e^{-\frac{k}{2r_{h}^{2}}}}{r_{h}},

\displaystyle C_{2}=-\frac{B_{1}}{3k^{3/2}}=\frac{iA_{1}r_{h}\omega e^{\frac{k}{2r_{h}^{2}}}}{3k^{3/2}}\,.

(110)

Now we can write the solution close to the boundary as

\displaystyle h^{C}_{\omega}(r)\approx A_{1}\left(\frac{e^{-\frac{k}{2r_{h}^{2}}}}{r_{h}}+\frac{i\omega r_{h}e^{\frac{k}{2r_{h}^{2}}}}{3r^{3}}\right)\,.

(111)

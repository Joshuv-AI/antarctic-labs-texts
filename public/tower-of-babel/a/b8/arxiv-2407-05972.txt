One-Dimensional Carrollian Fluids III: Global Existence and Weak Continuity in L ∞ L^{\infty} Thanks: Centre de Physique Théorique Preprint Number. CPHT-RR028.052024.
Marios Petropoulos, Simon Schulz, Grigalius Taujanskas

Abstract
. The Carrollian fluid equations arise as the c → 0 c\to 0 limit of the relativistic fluid equations and have recently experienced a surge of activity in the flat-space holography community. However, the rigorous mathematical well-posedness theory for these equations does not appear to have been previously studied. This paper is the third in a series in which we initiate the systematic analysis of the Carrollian fluid equations. In the present work we prove the global-in-time existence of bounded entropy solutions to the isentropic Carrollian fluid equations in one spatial dimension for a particular constitutive law ( γ = 3 \gamma=3 ). Our method is to use a vanishing viscosity approximation for which we establish a compensated compactness framework. Using this framework we also prove the compactness of entropy solutions in L ∞ L^{\infty} , and establish a kinetic formulation of the problem. This global existence result in L ∞ L^{\infty} extends the C 1 C^{1} theory presented in [ APST24 ] .

\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\big(\eta(\mathbf{u})\varphi_{t}+q(\mathbf{u})\varphi_{x}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\geqslant 0.

Furthermore, we will show in §
5
that we may generate entropy pairs
(\eta,q)
of (
2.3
) using two linearly independent sets of kernels,
(\chi_{1},\varsigma_{1})
and
(\chi_{2},\varsigma_{2})
, which we now define. We shall also use these to prove the kinetic formulation of the problem.

Definition 2.3

(Entropy Kernels)
.

We define the
first entropy kernel and its entropy-flux kernel

(\chi,\varsigma_{1})
to be

(2.7)

\displaystyle\chi_{1}(\sigma,\beta,s)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\delta(\beta-\sigma-s)+\delta(\beta+\sigma-s),

\displaystyle\varsigma_{1}(\sigma,\beta,s)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\mathds{1}_{[\beta-\sigma,\beta+\sigma]}(s)\sgn(s-\beta)\frac{1}{s}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}+2\mathds{1}_{[0,\beta]}(s)\frac{1}{s}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s},

and the
second entropy kernel and its entropy-flux kernel

(\chi_{2},\varsigma_{2})
to be

(2.8)

\displaystyle\chi_{2}(\sigma,\beta,s)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\mathds{1}_{[\beta-\sigma,\beta+\sigma]}(s),

\displaystyle\varsigma_{2}(\sigma,\beta,s)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\mathds{1}_{[\beta-\sigma,\beta+\sigma]}(s)\frac{1}{s}.

For a suitable test function
\varphi
, we will generate entropy pairs
(\eta_{j}^{\varphi},q_{j}^{\varphi})
(
j=1,2
) using the formulas

(2.9)

\eta_{j}^{\varphi}(\sigma,\beta)=\langle\chi_{j}(\sigma,\beta,\cdot),\varphi\rangle,\qquad q_{j}^{\varphi}(\sigma,\beta)=\langle\varsigma_{j}(\sigma,\beta,\cdot),\varphi\rangle;

we refer the reader to §
5.1
for further details.

3.
Main Results

Our first main theorem is as follows, and is obtained as a consequence of the compensated compactness framework established in §
5.2
(
cf.
Proposition
5.3
).

Theorem 3.1

(Global Existence)
.

Let
c_{0}>0
and
(\sigma_{0},\beta_{0})\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
be such that

(3.1)

\mathrm{ess\,inf}_{\mathbb{R}}\big(\sigma_{0}-|\beta_{0}|\big)\geqslant c_{0}.

Then there exists
(\sigma,\beta)\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})\cap L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(0,\infty;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))
an entropy solution of (
1.1
) for
\gamma=3
with initial data
(\sigma_{0},\beta_{0})
. Moreover,

\mathrm{ess\,inf}_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\big(\sigma-|\beta|\big)\geqslant c_{0}.

We emphasise that
c_{0}>0
in (
3.1
) is fixed arbitrarily in the statement of Theorem
3.1
and does not change throughout the rest of the paper; this condition is of course equivalent to

\mathrm{ess\,inf}_{\mathbb{R}}\big(\sigma_{0}-|\beta_{0}|\big)>0,

however we prefer this formulation to simplify notation in §
6
and to be uniform in our statement of condition (
3.2
) in Theorem
3.2
. While the requirement
(\sigma_{0},\beta_{0})\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
may seem excessive at first sight, this is a natural assumption given the special entropy pair (
2.5
), which is in a sense the most natural underlying strongly convex entropy of the system.

Our second main theorem is concerned with the weak continuity of the system of equations, which shows the weak compactness of entropy solutions of (
1.1
) for
\gamma=3
. Once again, this result is obtained as a consequence of the compensated compactness framework established in §
5.2
; Proposition
5.3
.

Theorem 3.2

(Weak Continuity)
.

Let
c_{0}>0
and
\{(\sigma^{n},\beta^{n})\}_{n}\subset L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
be a sequence of entropy solutions of (
1.1
) with
\gamma=3
, uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
and satisfying

(3.2)

\mathrm{ess\,inf}_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\big(\sigma^{n}-|\beta^{n}|\big)\geqslant c_{0}\quad\text{for all }\ n\in\mathbb{N}.

Then there exists
(\sigma,\beta)\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
and a subsequence, still labelled
\{(\sigma^{n},\beta^{n})\}_{n}
, such that

(\sigma^{n},\beta^{n})\longrightarrow(\sigma,\beta)\quad\text{a.e.~in }\ \mathbb{R}^{2}_{+},

and furthermore
(\sigma,\beta)
is an entropy solution of (
1.1
) with
\gamma=3
.

Our third main theorem quantifies the entropy dissipation (
2.6
) in our low regularity framework; it is called the
kinetic formulation
of the system of conservation laws (
2.3
) (
cf.

[
Per02
]
), and makes use of the entropy kernels introduced in Definition
2.3
and the function spaces
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
introduced in (
2.1
). This theorem establishes a PDE version of the second law of thermodynamics for the isentropic Carrollian fluid system.

Theorem 3.3

(Kinetic Formulation)
.

Let
(\sigma,\beta)
be an entropy solution obtained in Theorem
3.1
. Then there exists
\mu_{1}
a non-negative bounded Radon measure on
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}
, and
\mu_{2}
a signed bounded Radon measure on
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}
such that

(3.3)

\partial_{t}\big(\chi_{1}(\sigma(t,x),\beta(t,x),s)\big)+\partial_{x}\big(\varsigma_{1}(\sigma(t,x),\beta(t,x),s)\big)=-\partial^{2}_{s}\mu_{1}(t,x,s)\quad\text{in }\big(C^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})\times\mathcal{A}\big)^{\prime},

and

(3.4)

\partial_{t}\big(\chi_{2}(\sigma(t,x),\beta(t,x),s)\big)+\partial_{x}\big(\varsigma_{2}(\sigma(t,x),\beta(t,x),s)\big)=-\partial_{s}\mu_{2}(t,x,s)\quad\text{in }\big(C^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})\times\mathcal{B}\big)^{\prime}.

4.
Hyperbolicity, Genuine Nonlinearity, and Riemann Invariants

In this section, we establish the hyperbolicity and genuine nonlinearity of the system; both of which are essential ingredients to apply the compensated compactness method (
cf.
§
5.2
). We also compute the Riemann invariants of the systems, which shall subsequently be used in §
6
to compute the invariant regions of the phase space to which solutions are constrained.

4.1.
Hyperbolicity

Lemma 4.1

(Strict hyperbolicity)
.

For
\gamma=3
, the system (
2.3
) is strictly hyperbolic in the subregion

\mathcal{H}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\Big\{(\sigma,\beta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:\,\sigma\neq 0,\,\beta\neq\pm\sigma\Big\}.

Proof.

The eigenvalues of
M
are given by

\lambda_{1}^{\prime}=\beta-\sigma,\quad\lambda_{2}^{\prime}=\beta+\sigma,

whence the eigenvalues of
M^{-1}
are given by

\lambda_{1}=\frac{1}{\beta-\sigma},\quad\lambda_{2}=\frac{1}{\beta+\sigma},

and thus the system is strictly hyperbolic except along the curves
\{\sigma=0\}\cup\{\beta=\pm\sigma\}
; the eigenvalues are real and distinct on
\mathcal{H}
.
∎

Furthermore, a set of normalised right eigenvectors of
M
is provided by

\mathbf{r}_{1}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\begin{matrix}1\\
-1\end{matrix}\right),\quad\mathbf{r}_{2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\begin{matrix}1\\
1\end{matrix}\right);

they are also right eigenvectors for
M^{-1}
, with eigenvalues given by
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}
respectively. In turn, we have the diagonalised form:

(4.1)

\nabla_{\mathbf{u}}\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{u})=\frac{1}{2}\left(\begin{matrix}1&1\\
-1&1\end{matrix}\right)\left(\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{\beta-\sigma}&0\\
0&\frac{1}{\beta+\sigma}\end{matrix}\right)\left(\begin{matrix}1&-1\\
1&1\end{matrix}\right).

4.2.
Genuine Nonlinearity

Lemma 4.2

(Genuine Nonlinearity)
.

For
\gamma=3
, the system (
2.3
) is genuinely nonlinear, including along the curves
\{\sigma=0\}
and
\{\beta=\pm\sigma\}
.

Proof.

We verify the condition of genuine nonlinearity,
i.e.

\nabla_{\mathbf{u}}\lambda_{j}\cdot\mathbf{r}_{j}\neq 0

for
j\in\{1,2\}
. To this end, we compute

\nabla_{\mathbf{u}}\lambda_{1}=\frac{1}{(\beta-\sigma)^{2}}\left(\begin{matrix}1\\
-1\end{matrix}\right),\qquad\nabla_{\mathbf{u}}\lambda_{2}=-\frac{1}{(\beta+\sigma)^{2}}\left(\begin{matrix}1\\
1\end{matrix}\right),

whence

\nabla_{\mathbf{u}}\lambda_{1}\cdot\mathbf{r}_{1}=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{(\beta-\sigma)^{2}},\qquad\nabla_{\mathbf{u}}\lambda_{2}\cdot\mathbf{r}_{2}=-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{(\beta+\sigma)^{2}},

and genuine nonlinearity follows.
∎

4.3.
Riemann Invariants

Lemma 4.3

(Riemann Invariants)
.

The functions

(4.2)

w_{1}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sigma+\beta,\qquad w_{2}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sigma-\beta,

are Riemann invariants of the system (
2.3
) for
\gamma=3
. Furthermore, the mapping
(\sigma,\beta)\mapsto(w_{1},w_{2})
is bijective.

Proof.

Direct computations show that for
j\in\{1,2\}

\nabla_{\mathbf{u}}w_{j}\cdot\mathbf{r}_{j}=0,

i.e.
that
w_{1},w_{2}
is a set of Riemann invariants for the system. Furthermore, the curvilinear coordinate transformation

(\sigma,\beta)\longmapsto(w_{1},w_{2})

is manifestly bijective on the full phase plane of solutions.
∎

We will see in §
6
that the Riemann invariants will allow us to obtain a priori
L^{\infty}
estimates on
\sigma
and
\beta
. Note also that

\nabla_{\mathbf{u}}w_{1}=\left(\begin{matrix}1\\
1\end{matrix}\right)=\sqrt{2}\mathbf{r}_{2},\qquad\nabla_{\mathbf{u}}w_{2}=\left(\begin{matrix}1\\
-1\end{matrix}\right)=\sqrt{2}\mathbf{r}_{1}.

5.
Entropy Structure and Compensated Compactness

In this section, we compute two linearly independent families of entropy pairs of the system (
1.1
) in §
5.1
, and then proceed to apply the compensated compactness method to obtain a general compactness framework in
5.2
. This compactness framework will subsequently be used in §
7
to prove both the existence of a global entropy solution and the weak continuity of the system (
1.1
).

5.1.
Entropy Structure

We seek pairs of functions
(\eta,q)
for which one can obtain additional conservation laws from (
2.3
). To this end, let
\eta\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and pre-multiply the system (
2.3
) by
\nabla_{\mathbf{u}}\eta^{\intercal}
. Formally,
i.e.
assuming smooth
\mathbf{u}
, we get

\underbrace{\nabla_{\mathbf{u}}\eta^{\intercal}\mathbf{u}_{t}}_{=\eta(\mathbf{u})_{t}}+\nabla_{\mathbf{u}}\eta^{\intercal}\nabla_{\mathbf{u}}\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{u})\mathbf{u}_{x}=0.

We seek to find a corresponding entropy-flux
q
such that

\nabla_{\mathbf{u}}q^{\intercal}=\nabla_{\mathbf{u}}\eta^{\intercal}\nabla_{\mathbf{u}}\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{u}),

i.e.

q_{\sigma}=\frac{\beta\eta_{\sigma}-\sigma\eta_{\beta}}{\beta^{2}-\sigma^{2}},\qquad q_{\beta}=\frac{\beta\eta_{\beta}-\sigma\eta_{\sigma}}{\beta^{2}-\sigma^{2}}.

Using the equality of the mixed partial derivatives
q_{\beta\sigma}=q_{\sigma\beta}
, we obtain the
entropy equation

(5.1)

\sigma(\beta^{2}-\sigma^{2})^{2}(\eta_{\sigma\sigma}-\eta_{\beta\beta})=0.

In turn, all solutions of the linear wave equation

\eta_{\sigma\sigma}-\eta_{\beta\beta}=0

are entropies for our system. Recall that the general solution of the above is given by the usual d’Alembert representation formula:

(5.2)

\eta(\sigma,\beta)=f(\beta-\sigma)+f(\beta+\sigma)+\int_{\beta-\sigma}^{\beta+\sigma}g(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\quad(\sigma>0),

where
f=\frac{1}{2}\eta|_{\sigma=0}
,
g=\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\sigma}|_{\sigma=0}
; by varying the choice of
f,g
, we generate
two linearly independent families
of solutions.

To compute the corresponding entropy-flux
q
, we have

\displaystyle\eta_{\sigma}=f^{\prime}(\beta+\sigma)-f^{\prime}(\beta-\sigma)+g(\beta+\sigma)+g(\beta-\sigma),

\displaystyle\eta_{\beta}=f^{\prime}(\beta+\sigma)+f^{\prime}(\beta-\sigma)+g(\beta+\sigma)-g(\beta-\sigma),

and thus

\displaystyle q_{\sigma}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\beta^{2}-\sigma^{2}}(\beta\eta_{\sigma}-\sigma\eta_{\beta})

\displaystyle=\frac{f^{\prime}(\beta+\sigma)}{\beta+\sigma}-\frac{f^{\prime}(\beta-\sigma)}{\beta-\sigma}+\frac{1}{\beta+\sigma}g(\beta+\sigma)+\frac{1}{\beta-\sigma}g(\beta-\sigma),

while

\displaystyle q_{\beta}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\beta^{2}-\sigma^{2}}(\beta\eta_{\beta}-\sigma\eta_{\sigma})

\displaystyle=\frac{f^{\prime}(\beta+\sigma)}{\beta+\sigma}+\frac{f^{\prime}(\beta-\sigma)}{\beta-\sigma}+\frac{1}{\beta+\sigma}g(\beta+\sigma)-\frac{1}{\beta-\sigma}g(\beta-\sigma).

It follows that

(5.3)

q(\sigma,\beta)=\int_{\beta}^{\beta+\sigma}\frac{f^{\prime}(s)}{s}\,\mathrm{d}s-\int_{\beta-\sigma}^{\beta}\frac{f^{\prime}(s)}{s}\,\mathrm{d}s+2\int_{0}^{\beta}\frac{f^{\prime}(s)}{s}\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{\beta-\sigma}^{\beta+\sigma}\frac{g(s)}{s}\,\mathrm{d}s

is a suitable entropy-flux for
\eta
, provided these integrals are well-defined. Recalling Definition
2.3
, we have shown that a generic entropy pair may be written as claimed in (
2.9
),
i.e.

(5.4)

\displaystyle\eta(\sigma,\beta)=\langle\chi_{1}(\sigma,\beta,\cdot),f\rangle+\langle\chi_{2}(\sigma,\beta,\cdot),g\rangle,\quad

\displaystyle q(\sigma,\beta)=\langle\varsigma_{1}(\sigma,\beta,\cdot),f\rangle+\langle\varsigma_{2}(\sigma,\beta,\cdot),g\rangle.

We have the following result concerning the admissible test functions for the kernels, for which we recall for convenience the spaces of functions
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
introduced in (
2.1
):

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=\big\{f\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}):\,f^{\prime}(0)=0\big\}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{B}=\big\{g\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}):\,g(0)=0\big\}.

Lemma 5.1
.

The kernels
\chi_{1}
and
\chi_{2}
are well-defined on
C^{0}(\mathbb{R})
, while
\varsigma_{1}
is well-defined on
\mathcal{A}
and
\varsigma_{2}
is well-defined on

\mathcal{B}
.

Proof.

Note that
\chi_{1}
and
\chi_{2}
are well-defined on elements of
C^{0}(\mathbb{R})
. On the other hand, for all
g\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, if
\beta+\sigma>0
and
\beta-\sigma=0
,

|\langle\varsigma_{2}(\sigma,\beta,\cdot),g\rangle|=\bigg|\int^{\beta+\sigma}_{0}\frac{g(s)}{s}\,\mathrm{d}s\bigg|

and the above is well-defined if and only if
g(0)=0
; this can be seen from an application of Taylor’s Theorem. If
\beta+\sigma>0
and
\beta-\sigma<0
, then one may rewrite the duality product as a principal value plus a remainder term:

\displaystyle|\langle\varsigma_{2}(\sigma,\beta,\cdot),g\rangle|

\displaystyle\leqslant\bigg|\int^{\beta+\sigma}_{\sigma-\beta}\frac{g(s)}{s}\,\mathrm{d}s\bigg|+\bigg|\int^{\sigma-\beta}_{-(\sigma-\beta)}\frac{g(s)}{s}\,\mathrm{d}s\bigg|

\displaystyle\leqslant\min\{\beta+\sigma,\sigma-\beta\}^{-1}\|g\|_{L^{\infty}([\sigma-\beta,\beta+\sigma])}+\int^{\sigma-\beta}_{0}\frac{|g(s)-g(-s)|}{s}\,\mathrm{d}s,

and one estimates the above right-hand side using Taylor’s Theorem. It is also easy to verify that the duality product is well-defined whenever both
\beta+\sigma>0
and
\beta-\sigma>0
. We deduce that
\varsigma_{2}
is well-defined on elements
g\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
such that
g(0)=0
. An analogous argument shows that
\varsigma_{1}
is well-defined on elements
f\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R})
such that
f^{\prime}(0)=0
.
∎

We therefore have the following lemma.

Lemma 5.2

(Entropy pairs)
.

Let
(f,g)
be any pair of functions in
\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{B}
. Then the pairs of functions
(\eta_{1},q_{1})
and
(\eta_{2},q_{2})
defined by

\displaystyle\eta_{1}(\sigma,\beta)=f(\beta-\sigma)+f(\beta+\sigma),\qquad q_{1}(\sigma,\beta)=\int_{\beta-\sigma}^{\beta+\sigma}\sgn(s-\beta)\frac{f^{\prime}(s)}{s}\,\mathrm{d}s+2\int_{0}^{\beta}\frac{f^{\prime}(s)}{s}\,\mathrm{d}s,

and

\displaystyle\eta_{2}(\sigma,\beta)=\int_{\beta-\sigma}^{\beta+\sigma}g(s)\,\mathrm{d}s,\qquad q_{2}(\sigma,\beta)=\int_{\beta-\sigma}^{\beta+\sigma}\frac{g(s)}{s}\,\mathrm{d}s,

are entropy pairs for the system (
2.3
); moreover, in the region
\{\beta\neq\pm\sigma\}
, they are
C^{2}
. In particular, the choice
(f(s),g(s))=(\frac{1}{4}s^{2},0)\in\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{B}
yields the special entropy pair
(\eta^{*},q^{*})=(\frac{1}{2}(\sigma^{2}+\beta^{2}),\beta)
of (
2.5
).

Proof.

Let
(f,g)\in\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{B}
as in the statement. Then by
Lemma

5.1
the pairs of functions
(\eta_{j},q_{j})
(
j=1,2
) in the statement of
Lemma

5.2
are well-defined. Furthermore, they are entropy pairs in view of (
5.4
) and the computations preceding (
5.4
).
It remains to verify their regularity. We compute the first derivatives

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mathbf{u}}\eta_{1}=f^{\prime}(\beta-\sigma)\left(\begin{matrix}-1\\
1\end{matrix}\right)+f^{\prime}(\beta+\sigma)\left(\begin{matrix}1\\
1\end{matrix}\right),\quad

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mathbf{u}}q_{1}=\frac{f^{\prime}(\beta-\sigma)}{\beta-\sigma}\left(\begin{matrix}-1\\
1\end{matrix}\right)+\frac{f^{\prime}(\beta+\sigma)}{\beta+\sigma}\left(\begin{matrix}1\\
1\end{matrix}\right),

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mathbf{u}}\eta_{2}=g(\beta-\sigma)\left(\begin{matrix}1\\
-1\end{matrix}\right)+g(\beta+\sigma)\left(\begin{matrix}1\\
1\end{matrix}\right),\quad

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mathbf{u}}q_{2}=\frac{g(\beta-\sigma)}{\beta-\sigma}\left(\begin{matrix}1\\
-1\end{matrix}\right)+\frac{g(\beta+\sigma)}{\beta+\sigma}\left(\begin{matrix}1\\
1\end{matrix}\right),

along with the second derivatives

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mathbf{u}}^{2}\eta_{1}=f^{\prime\prime}(\beta-\sigma)\left(\begin{matrix}1&-1\\
-1&1\end{matrix}\right)+f^{\prime\prime}(\beta+\sigma)\left(\begin{matrix}1&1\\
1&1\end{matrix}\right),\quad

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}_{\mathbf{u}}q_{1}\!\!=\!\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(\beta-\sigma)}{\beta-\sigma}\!\!\left(\begin{matrix}1&-1\\
-1&1\end{matrix}\right)\!\!+\!\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(\beta+\sigma)}{\beta+\sigma}\!\!\left(\begin{matrix}1&1\\
1&1\end{matrix}\right)\!\!+\!\frac{f^{\prime}(\beta-\sigma)}{(\beta-\sigma)^{2}}\!\!\left(\begin{matrix}-1&1\\
1&-1\end{matrix}\right)\!\!+\!\frac{f^{\prime}(\beta+\sigma)}{(\beta+\sigma)^{2}}\!\!\left(\begin{matrix}-1&-1\\
-1&-1\end{matrix}\right),

and

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}_{\mathbf{u}}\eta_{2}=g^{\prime}(\beta-\sigma)\left(\begin{matrix}-1&1\\
1&-1\end{matrix}\right)+g^{\prime}(\beta+\sigma)\left(\begin{matrix}1&1\\
1&1\end{matrix}\right),

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}_{\mathbf{u}}q_{2}\!\!=\!\frac{g^{\prime}(\beta-\sigma)}{\beta-\sigma}\!\!\left(\begin{matrix}-1&1\\
1&-1\end{matrix}\right)\!\!+\!\frac{g^{\prime}(\beta+\sigma)}{\beta+\sigma}\!\!\left(\begin{matrix}1&1\\
1&1\end{matrix}\right)\!\!+\!\frac{g(\beta-\sigma)}{(\beta-\sigma)^{2}}\!\!\left(\begin{matrix}1&-1\\
-1&1\end{matrix}\right)\!\!+\!\frac{g(\beta+\sigma)}{(\beta+\sigma)^{2}}\!\!\left(\begin{matrix}-1&-1\\
-1&-1\end{matrix}\right).

It is immediate from the formulas above that
(\eta_{j},q_{j})
(
j=1,2
) are
C^{2}
in the region
\{\beta\neq\pm\sigma\}
.
∎

5.2.
Compensated Compactness Framework

In this subsection, we establish our compactness framework; this will be used in §
7
in order to pass to the limit in the regularised problem which we study in §
6
, thus obtaining an entropy solution of (
1.1
).

Proposition 5.3

(Compensated Compactness Framework)
.

Let
\{\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}=(\sigma^{\varepsilon},\beta^{\varepsilon})\}_{\varepsilon}
be a uniformly bounded sequence in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
such that
:

(i)

there exists a positive constant
c>0
such that
\sigma^{\varepsilon}-|\beta^{\varepsilon}|\geqslant c,
and

(ii)

for all entropy pairs
(\eta,q)
generated via Lemma
5.2
, the sequence of entropy dissipation measures

\big\{\eta(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})_{t}+q(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})_{x}\big\}_{\varepsilon}

is pre-compact in
H^{-1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}),
i.e. for all compact
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
there exists a compact
\kappa_{K}\subset H^{-1}(K)
such that

\big\{\eta(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})_{t}+q(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})_{x}\big\}_{\varepsilon}\subset\kappa_{K}.

Then there exists a subsequence, which we do not relabel, and
\mathbf{u}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
such that there holds

\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}\to\mathbf{u}\quad\text{a.e.~and strongly in }L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})\text{ for all }\,p\in[1,\infty).

As per the notations introduced in §
2
, we remind the reader that in what follows, for a measure
\nu_{t,x}
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
indexed by
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
, we employ the notation

\overline{h}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}h\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{t,x}\,,

where no confusion arises regarding the subscript
t,x
.

Proof.

The uniform boundedness in
L^{\infty}
implies, using Alaoglu’s Theorem, the existence of a weakly-* convergent subsequence, still labelled
\{\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
, such that
\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}\overset{*}{\rightharpoonup}\mathbf{u}
in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})^{2}
. The remainder of this proof is focused on improving this convergence to convergence almost everywhere.

1.
Young measure and commutation relation
. The Fundamental Theorem of Young measures (
cf.

[
Ser96a
, §2, Theorem 9.1.5]
) implies that the sequence
\{\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
generates a family
\{\nu_{t,x}\}_{t,x}
of probability measures on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, for which, for a further subsequence which we do not relabel, there holds

h(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})\overset{*}{\rightharpoonup}\overline{h}\quad\text{in }L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})

for all
h\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. In turn, using the continuity of the entropy pairs generated by Lemma
5.2
, we find that

\eta_{i}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})q_{j}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})\overset{*}{\rightharpoonup}\overline{\eta_{i}q_{j}}\qquad(i\neq j),

for all entropy pairs generated by Lemma
5.2
. Meanwhile, using the
H^{-1}_{\mathrm{loc}}
-compactness criterion (assumption (ii) of the lemma), the Div-Curl Lemma implies that

\eta_{1}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})q_{2}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})-\eta_{2}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})q_{1}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})\to\overline{\eta_{1}}\,\overline{q_{2}}-\overline{\eta_{2}}\,\overline{q_{1}}\qquad\text{in }\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}).

We deduce that there holds the commutation relation

(5.5)

\overline{\eta_{1}q_{2}-\eta_{2}q_{1}}=\overline{\eta_{1}}\,\overline{q_{2}}-\overline{\eta_{2}}\,\overline{q_{1}}

for all entropy pairs
(\eta_{1},q_{1})
and
(\eta_{2},q_{2})
generated by Lemma
5.2
.

2.
Young measure reduction
. The system (
2.3
) is strictly hyperbolic and genuinely nonlinear by virtue of assumption (i) and Lemmas
4.1
and
4.2
. Furthermore, Lemma
5.2
provides us with two linearly independent families of entropy pairs, which are pre-compact in
H^{-1}_{\mathrm{loc}}
by assumption (ii) of the proposition. In turn, using (
5.5
) and the classical arguments of Serre
[
Ser96a
, §2]
or DiPerna
[
DiP83
]
, we deduce that there exists a measurable function
\mathbf{u}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})^{2}
such that

\nu_{t,x}=\delta_{\mathbf{u}(t,x)}.

It follows that
\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}\to\mathbf{u}
almost everywhere. Using the Dominated Convergence Theorem and the uniform boundedness, we deduce that
\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}\to\mathbf{u}
strongly in
L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})^{2}
for all
p\in[1,\infty)
.
∎

6.
Regularised Problem and Uniform Estimates

We turn now to establishing the existence and uniform estimates for the vanishing viscosity approximation of our system (
1.4
). For
\varepsilon>0
we consider the initial value problem

(6.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}_{t}+\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})_{x}=\varepsilon\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}_{xx},\\
&\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}|_{t=0}=\mathbf{u}_{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

Ultimately, our objective will be to take the limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
and show that the sequence
\{\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
converges almost everywhere to
\mathbf{u}
an entropy solution of (
1.4
). We emphasise that, in view of the singularities contained in the term
\nabla_{\mathbf{u}}\mathbf{F}
,
cf.
(
2.2
), the existence of solutions to (
6.1
) cannot be deduced by straightforward application of the general theory for parabolic systems
[
LSU88
]
.

Our main result in this section is as follows, where we recall that the positive constant
c_{0}
was fixed in the statement of Theorem
3.1
.

Proposition 6.1

(Existence for Regularised Problems)
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
,
T>0
, and
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
satisfy

\mathrm{ess\,inf}_{\mathbb{R}}w_{1}(\mathbf{u}_{0}),~\mathrm{ess\,inf}_{\mathbb{R}}w_{2}(\mathbf{u}_{0})\geqslant c_{0}.

Then there exists
\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})\cap L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{R}))
with
\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}_{t}\in L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(0,T;H^{-1}(\mathbb{R}))
, smooth away from the initial time, a classical solution of the regularised problem (
6.1
) on
(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}
, i.e.

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}_{t}+\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})_{x}=\varepsilon\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}_{xx},\\
&\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}|_{t=0}=\mathbf{u}_{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

Furthermore,
\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}
satisfies

w_{1}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}),~w_{2}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})\geqslant c_{0}\quad\text{a.e.~in }\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+},

and there exists a positive constant
C
independent of
\varepsilon
and
T
such that

\|\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\leqslant C.

The previous proposition rests on the fact that the system of equations admits invariant regions in phase space; namely, for initial data satisfying
\mathrm{ess\,inf}_{\mathbb{R}}w_{1}(\mathbf{u}_{0}),\,\mathrm{ess\,inf}_{\mathbb{R}}w_{2}(\mathbf{u}_{0})\geqslant c_{0}
, there holds
w_{1}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}),\,w_{2}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})\geqslant c_{0}
a.e., whence the term
\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})
is well-defined. This can be seen from the following
formal
argument: by multiplying (
6.1
) with
\nabla_{\mathbf{u}}w_{1}^{\intercal}
, we obtain

\displaystyle w_{1}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})_{t}+\nabla_{\mathbf{u}}w_{1}^{\intercal}\nabla_{\mathbf{u}}\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}_{x}=\varepsilon\nabla_{\mathbf{u}}w_{1}^{\intercal}\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}_{xx},

so using that
\nabla_{\mathbf{u}}\mathbf{F}
is symmetric and
\nabla_{\mathbf{u}}w_{1}=\sqrt{2}\mathbf{r}_{2}
, there holds
\nabla_{\mathbf{u}}w_{1}^{\intercal}\nabla_{\mathbf{u}}\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})=\lambda_{2}\nabla_{\mathbf{u}}w_{1}^{\intercal}
, whence

\displaystyle w_{1}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})_{t}+\lambda_{2}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})w_{1}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})_{x}=\varepsilon w_{1}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})_{xx}-\varepsilon(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}_{x})^{\intercal}\nabla_{\mathbf{u}}^{2}w_{1}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}_{x};

similarly, we get

\displaystyle w_{2}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})_{t}+\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})w_{2}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})_{x}=\varepsilon w_{2}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})_{xx}-\varepsilon(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}_{x})^{\intercal}\nabla_{\mathbf{u}}^{2}w_{2}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}_{x}.

Note that since
w_{j}
(
j=1,2)
are affine, their Hessians are null, so the previous equations reduce to

(6.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &w_{1}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})_{t}+(\log w_{1}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}))_{x}=\varepsilon w_{1}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})_{xx},\\
&w_{2}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})_{t}-(\log w_{2}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}))_{x}=\varepsilon w_{2}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})_{xx},\end{aligned}\right.

where we used that

\lambda_{2}=\frac{1}{w_{1}}\quad\text{and}\quad\lambda_{1}=-\frac{1}{w_{2}}.

Each of the equations in (
6.2
) is an autonomous strongly parabolic semilinear equation. An application of the Maximum Principle thereby ensures

c_{0}\leqslant\mathrm{ess\,inf}_{\mathbb{R}}w_{j}(\mathbf{u}_{0})\leqslant w_{j}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})\leqslant\mathrm{ess\,sup}_{\mathbb{R}}w_{j}(\mathbf{u}_{0})\quad\text{a.e.}\quad(j=1,2).

The arguments presented above can only be made rigorous
a posteriori
,
i.e.
one can only perform these manipulations once the existence of
\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}
is established. For this reason, we shall study the
modified regularised problem
:

(6.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}_{t}+\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}_{x}=\varepsilon\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}_{xx},\\
&\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}|_{t=0}=\mathbf{u}_{0},\end{aligned}\right.

where, analogous to the diagonal representation (
4.1
),
\mathbf{M}
is defined by

(6.4)

\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{u})\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\begin{matrix}-1&1\\
1&1\end{matrix}\right)\left(\begin{matrix}\phi_{1}(\mathbf{u})&0\\
0&\phi_{2}(\mathbf{u})\end{matrix}\right)\left(\begin{matrix}1&-1\\
-1&-1\end{matrix}\right),

where
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
are
C^{1}
approximations of
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
, respectively, with cut-off at the value
c_{0}
. In detail, we define, for all
\delta>0
, the function
\psi_{\delta}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
by

(6.5)

\begin{split}\psi_{\delta}(s)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{\delta}{2}\quad&&\text{for }s\leqslant 0,\\
&\frac{1}{2\delta}(s^{2}+\delta^{2})\quad&&\text{for }0<s\leqslant\delta,\\
&s\quad&&\text{for }s>\delta,\end{aligned}\right.\end{split}

and we note that
\delta/2\leqslant\psi_{\delta}(s)\leqslant C(1+s)
, whence
\frac{1}{\psi_{\delta}}\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R})
. Define

\phi_{1}(\mathbf{u})\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=-\frac{1}{\psi_{c_{0}}(\sigma-\beta)}\quad\text{and}\quad\phi_{2}(\mathbf{u})\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\frac{1}{\psi_{c_{0}}(\sigma+\beta)},

and observe that there holds

(6.6)

\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{u})=\nabla_{\mathbf{u}}\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{u})\quad\text{whenever }w_{1}(\mathbf{u}),\,w_{2}(\mathbf{u})\geqslant c_{0}.

Furthermore, it is clear from the diagonal representations (
4.1
) and (
6.4
) that
\mathbf{M}
has eigenvectors given by
\mathbf{r}_{1},\,\mathbf{r}_{2}
, with eigenvalues given by
\phi_{1},\,\phi_{2}
, respectively. In turn, the Riemann invariant coordinates
w_{1},\,w_{2}
from (
4.2
) are the same for the equation

\mathbf{u}_{t}+\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{u})\mathbf{u}_{x}=0

as for the original equation (
2.3
). It follows that, in Riemann invariant coordinates,

(6.7)

\phi_{1}=-\frac{1}{\psi_{c_{0}}(w_{2})},\quad\phi_{2}=\frac{1}{\psi_{c_{0}}(w_{1})}.

Finally, observe that there holds

0<|\phi_{j}(\mathbf{u})|\leqslant\frac{2}{c_{0}}\quad(j=1,2),

and note that
\mathbf{u}\mapsto\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{u})
is
C^{1}
. Using the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality for the matrix operator norm, we have

(6.8)

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{u})\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\leqslant C\big(|\phi_{1}(\mathbf{u})|+|\phi_{2}(\mathbf{u})|\big)\leqslant C

for some positive constant
C
depending only on
c_{0}
, and in particular independent of
\mathbf{u}
.

6.1.
Existence for the Modified Regularised Problem

We therefore turn to establishing the following result.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. There exists
\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{R}))
with
\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}_{t}\in L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(0,T;H^{-1}(\mathbb{R}))
be a weak solution of the modfiied regularised problem (
6.3
), i.e.

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}_{t}+\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}_{x}=\varepsilon\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}_{xx},\\
&\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}|_{t=0}=\mathbf{u}_{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

Our approach is classical; nevertheless, for clarity and completeness we give a self-contained exposition. We shall build the solution
\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}
using a fixed-point argument, by interpreting (
6.3
) as a perturbation of the heat equation and using the Duhamel Principle.

Suppose that
\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}
is a solution of (
6.3
). Then
\mathbf{v}(t,x)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}(t/\varepsilon,x)
is a solution of

\mathbf{v}_{t}-\mathbf{v}_{xx}=-\varepsilon^{-1}\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{v})\mathbf{v}_{x}

with the same initial data
\mathbf{v}_{0}=\mathbf{u}_{0}
. By the Duhamel Principle,
\mathbf{v}
must then verify the representation formula

\mathbf{v}(t,x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Gamma(t,x-y)\mathbf{u}_{0}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y-\varepsilon^{-1}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Gamma(t-s,x-y)\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{v}(s,y))\mathbf{v}_{y}(s,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}s,

where
\Gamma
is the one-dimensional heat kernel,
i.e.

\Gamma(t,x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi t}}e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{4t}}.

Rewriting in terms of
\mathbf{u}
, this formula corresponds to

\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}(t,x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Gamma(\varepsilon t,x-y)\mathbf{u}_{0}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Gamma(\varepsilon(t-s),x-y)\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}(s,y))\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}_{y}(s,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}s.

Define the mapping

(6.9)

L\mathbf{u}(t,x)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Gamma(\varepsilon t,x-y)\mathbf{u}_{0}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Gamma(\varepsilon(t-s),x-y)\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{u}(s,y))\mathbf{u}_{y}(s,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}s.

Our objective is to show that there exists a fixed point
\mathbf{u}_{*}=L\mathbf{u}_{*}
; such a
\mathbf{u}_{*}
is manifestly a solution of (
6.3
), as direct computation shows that
\mathbf{w}=L\mathbf{u}
solves

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathbf{w}_{t}-\varepsilon\mathbf{w}_{xx}=-\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{u})\mathbf{u}_{x},\\
&\mathbf{w}|_{t=0}=\mathbf{u}_{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

In what follows we will employ Schaefer’s Fixed Point Theorem:

Theorem 6.1

(Schaefer’s Theorem)
.

Let
X
be a Banach space and
L:X\to X
a continuous and compact mapping of
X
into itself, such that the set

A\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\Big\{\mathbf{v}\in X:\,\mathbf{w}=\lambda L\mathbf{w}\text{ for some }\lambda\in[0,1]\Big\}

is bounded in
X
. Then
L
admits a fixed point
\mathbf{w}_{*}\in X
such that
L\mathbf{w}_{*}=\mathbf{w}_{*}
.

Let
T>0
, and define the Banach space

X\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\Big\{\mathbf{v}\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{R})):\,\mathbf{v}_{t}\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{-1}(\mathbb{R}))\Big\},

and, for
\lambda\in[0,1]
,

A_{\lambda}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\big\{\mathbf{w}\in X:\,\mathbf{w}=\lambda L\mathbf{w}\big\}.

Note that

(6.10)

\Big\{\mathbf{w}\in X:\,\mathbf{w}=\lambda L\mathbf{w}\text{ for some }\lambda\in[0,1]\Big\}=\bigcup_{\lambda\in[0,1]}A_{\lambda}.

Lemma 6.3
.

The mapping
L:X\to X
is continuous and compact.

Proof.

We divide the proof into several steps.

1.
L
is self-mapping, i.e.
L(X)\subset X
.
Let
\mathbf{w}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=L\mathbf{v}
, and note that there holds

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathbf{w}_{t}-\varepsilon\mathbf{w}_{xx}=-\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{v})\mathbf{v}_{x},\\
&\mathbf{w}|_{t=0}=\mathbf{u}_{0}\end{aligned}\right.

in the weak sense. In turn, performing the standard parabolic estimate by testing the equation with
\mathbf{w}
, we obtain

\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mathbf{w}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mathbf{w}_{x}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbf{w}\cdot\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{v})\mathbf{v}_{x}\,\mathrm{d}x\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mathbf{w}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+C\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mathbf{v}_{x}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x,

and Grönwall’s Lemma implies, for a.e.
t\in(0,T)
,

\|\mathbf{w}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leqslant\|\mathbf{u}_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\exp\Big(C\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathbf{v}_{x}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\,\mathrm{d}s\Big).

It therefore follows that

(6.11)

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{w}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}+

\displaystyle\varepsilon\|\mathbf{w}_{x}\|^{2}_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leqslant C(1+T)\|\mathbf{u}_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\exp\Big(C\|\mathbf{v}_{x}\|^{2}_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R})}\Big)+CT\|\mathbf{v}_{x}\|^{2}_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R})},

whence
L
is self-mapping since
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))\subset L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))
for
T
finite.

2.
Estimate on time derivatives
. We begin with an estimate on the time derivative which will enable us to employ the Aubin–Lions Lemma. Let
\boldsymbol{\zeta}\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{R}))
. Testing against the equation, we obtain

\displaystyle|\langle\mathbf{w}_{t},\boldsymbol{\zeta}\rangle|

\displaystyle\leqslant\varepsilon\Big|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbf{w}_{x}\cdot\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{x}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\Big|+\Big|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\boldsymbol{\zeta}\cdot\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{v})\mathbf{v}_{x}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\Big|

\displaystyle\leqslant\varepsilon\|\mathbf{w}_{x}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R})}\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{x}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R})}+C\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R})}\|\mathbf{v}_{x}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R})},

whence

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{w}_{t}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{-1}(\mathbb{R}))}\leqslant\varepsilon\|\mathbf{w}_{x}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R})}+C\|\mathbf{v}_{x}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R})},

i.e.
, using (
6.11
),

(6.12)

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{w}_{t}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{-1}(\mathbb{R}))}\leqslant

\displaystyle\sqrt{\varepsilon}C(1+T)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\mathbf{u}_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\exp\Big(C\|\mathbf{v}_{x}\|^{2}_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R})}\big)

\displaystyle+C(1+T^{\frac{1}{2}})\|\mathbf{v}_{x}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R})}.

3.
Weak compactness in
X
. Let
\{\mathbf{v}_{n}\}_{n}
be a bounded sequence in
X
and define
\mathbf{w}_{n}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=L\mathbf{v}_{n}
for all
n
. The Aubin–Lions Lemma implies that, up to a subsequence which we do not relabel, there holds

(6.13)

\mathbf{v}_{n}\to\mathbf{v}\quad\text{weakly in }X,\text{ strongly in }L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}),\text{ and a.e.}

for some
\mathbf{v}\in X
, where we also used Alaoglu’s Theorem and the fact that
X
is reflexive. Furthermore, the estimates (
6.11
) and (
6.12
) imply that
\{\mathbf{w}_{n}\}_{n}
also satisfies the assumptions of the Aubin–Lions Lemma, so there exists
\mathbf{w}\in X
such that, up to a subsequence which we do not relabel, using also Alaoglu’s Theorem,

(6.14)

\mathbf{w}_{n}\to\mathbf{w}\quad\text{weakly in }X,\text{ strongly in }L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}),\text{ and a.e.}

4.
Strong compactness in
L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{R}))
. It remains to show that the convergence also occurs strongly with respect to the topology of
X
,
i.e.
it remains to show that
\lim_{n}\|(\mathbf{w}_{n})_{x}-\mathbf{w}_{x}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R})}=0
and an analogous strong convergence for the time derivatives in the space
L^{2}(0,T;H^{-1}(\mathbb{R}))
. Concerning the first convergence, since we already know that
\{\mathbf{w}_{n}\}_{n}
converges weakly to
\mathbf{w}
in
L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{R}))
, it suffices to show

(6.15)

\lim_{n}\|(\mathbf{w}_{n})_{x}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R})}=\|\mathbf{w}_{x}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R})}.

The weak formulation for
\mathbf{w}_{n}
reads

(6.16)

-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbf{w}_{n}\cdot\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbf{w}_{n})_{x}\cdot\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{x}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t=-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{v}_{n})(\mathbf{v}_{n})_{x}\cdot\boldsymbol{\zeta}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

for all
\boldsymbol{\zeta}\in C^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R})
such that
\boldsymbol{\zeta}(0,\cdot)=\boldsymbol{\zeta}(T,\cdot)=0
. The matrix
\mathbf{M}
is continuous, so
\mathbf{v}_{n}\to\mathbf{v}
a.e. implies that
\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{v}_{n})\to\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{v})
a.e. Furthermore, the bound (
6.8
) gives
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{v}_{n})\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\leqslant C
, whence
C|\boldsymbol{\zeta}|\in L^{1}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R})
is a suitable dominating function for
\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\intercal}\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{v}_{n})
which converges a.e. to
\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\intercal}\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{v})
. The Dominated Convergence Theorem then yields

\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\intercal}\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{v}_{n})\to\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{\intercal}\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{v})\quad\text{strongly in }L^{p}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R})\text{ for all }p\in[1,\infty).

Since
(\mathbf{v}_{n})_{x}\to\mathbf{v}_{x}
weakly in
L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R})
, it follows that there holds the convergence for the product

\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{v}_{n})(\mathbf{v}_{n})_{x}\cdot\boldsymbol{\zeta}\rightharpoonup\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{v})\mathbf{v}_{x}\cdot\boldsymbol{\zeta}\quad\text{weakly in }L^{1}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}).

Using the above and the aforementioned weak convergences, we deduce from (
6.16
) that there holds

(6.17)

-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbf{w}\cdot\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbf{w}_{x}\cdot\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{x}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t=-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{v})\mathbf{v}_{x}\cdot\boldsymbol{\zeta}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

A standard approximation argument allows us to introduce
\mathbf{w}_{n}
into the weak formulation (
6.16
) and
\mathbf{w}
into (
6.17
) on an arbitrary time interval
[t_{1},t_{2}]\subsetneq[0,T]
. Subtracting the two resulting equations, we get

(6.18)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big(|\mathbf{w}_{n}(t_{2},x)|^{2}-|\mathbf{w}(t_{2},x)|^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big(|\mathbf{w}_{n}(t_{1},x)|^{2}-|\mathbf{w}(t_{1},x)|^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big(|(\mathbf{w}_{n})_{x}|^{2}-|\mathbf{w}_{x}|^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t-\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big(\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{v}_{n})(\mathbf{v}_{n})_{x}\cdot\mathbf{v}_{n}-\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{v})\mathbf{v}_{x}\!\cdot\!\mathbf{v}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

Recall that the convergence
\mathbf{w}_{n}\to\mathbf{w}
strongly in
L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R})
implies, up to a subsequence which we do not relabel,

\mathbf{w}_{n}(t,\cdot)\to\mathbf{w}(t,\cdot)\quad\text{strongly in }L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\text{ for a.e.~}t.

It follows that the left-hand side of (
6.18
) vanishes in the limit as
n\to\infty
. Hence, using also the continuity of
s\mapsto|s|
to interchange the limit with the absolute value,

(6.19)

\displaystyle\lim_{n}\Big|\|(\mathbf{w}_{n})_{x}\|^{2}_{L^{2}((t_{1},t_{2})\times\mathbb{R})}-

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{w}_{x}\|^{2}_{L^{2}((t_{1},t_{2})\times\mathbb{R})}\Big|

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{-1}\bigg|\lim_{n}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big(\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{v}_{n})(\mathbf{v}_{n})_{x}\cdot\mathbf{v}_{n}-\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{v})\mathbf{v}_{x}\cdot\mathbf{v}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\bigg|.

For the term on the right-hand side, we write

(6.20)

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{v}_{n}^{\intercal}\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{v}_{n})-\mathbf{v}^{\intercal}\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{v})\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{v}_{n})\cdot(\mathbf{v}_{n}-\mathbf{v})\|_{L^{2}}+\|\big(\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{v}_{n})-\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{v})\big)\cdot\mathbf{v}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\|\mathbf{v}_{n}-\mathbf{v}\|_{L^{2}}+\bigg(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{v}_{n})-\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{v})\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}|\mathbf{v}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

The first term on the right-hand side manifestly vanishes due to the strong convergence in
L^{2}
for the sequence
\{\mathbf{v}_{n}\}_{n}
. Recall that
\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{v}_{n})\to\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{v})
a.e. and
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{v}_{n})-\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{v})\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}\leqslant C
, so the Dominated Convergence Theorem implies that the second term on the right-hand side vanishes in the limit as
n\to\infty
. It follows that

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}_{n}^{\intercal}\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{v}_{n})\to\mathbf{v}^{\intercal}\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{v})\quad\text{strongly in }L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}).

Since
(\mathbf{v}_{n})_{x}\rightharpoonup\mathbf{v}_{x}
weakly in
L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R})
, it follows that

\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{v}_{n})(\mathbf{v}_{n})_{x}\cdot\mathbf{v}_{n}\rightharpoonup\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{v})\mathbf{v}_{x}\cdot\mathbf{v}\quad\text{weakly in }L^{1}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}),

and hence

\displaystyle\lim_{n}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big(\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{v}_{n})(\mathbf{v}_{n})_{x}\cdot\mathbf{v}_{n}-\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{v})\mathbf{v}_{x}\cdot\mathbf{v}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t=0.

Returning to (
6.19
), we obtain

\lim_{n}\|(\mathbf{w}_{n})_{x}\|_{L^{2}((t_{1},t_{2})\times\mathbb{R})}=\|\mathbf{w}_{x}\|^{2}_{L^{2}((t_{1},t_{2})\times\mathbb{R})}\quad\text{for a.e.~}t_{1},\,t_{2}.

We now relax the assumption
[t_{1},t_{2}]\subsetneq[0,T]
(by taking a further subsequence if necessary), and deduce

\lim_{n}\|(\mathbf{w}_{n})_{x}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R})}=\|\mathbf{w}_{x}\|^{2}_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R})};

this, in addition to the weak convergence in
L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{R}))
, implies

(6.21)

\mathbf{w}_{n}\to\mathbf{w}\quad\text{strongly in }L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{R})).

5.
Strong compactness of time derivatives in
L^{2}(0,T;H^{-1}(\mathbb{R}))
. It remains to show that
(\mathbf{w}_{n})_{t}\to\mathbf{w}_{t}
strongly in
L^{2}(0,T;H^{-1}(\mathbb{R}))
. Taking the difference of the two weak formulations, there holds

\displaystyle|\langle(\mathbf{w}_{n})_{t}

\displaystyle-\!\mathbf{w}_{t},\boldsymbol{\zeta}\rangle|

\displaystyle\leqslant\varepsilon\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big|\big((\mathbf{w}_{n})_{x}\!-\!\mathbf{w}_{x}\big)\cdot\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{x}\big|\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big|\big(\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{v}_{n})(\mathbf{v}_{n})_{x}\!-\!\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{v})\mathbf{v}_{x}\big)\!\cdot\!\boldsymbol{\zeta}\big|\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leqslant\Big(\varepsilon\|\mathbf{w}_{n}\!-\!\mathbf{w}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{R}))}\!+\!\|\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{v}_{n})(\mathbf{v}_{n})_{x}\!-\!\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{v})\mathbf{v}_{x}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R})}\Big)\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{R}))}

for all
\boldsymbol{\zeta}\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{R}))
. We compute

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{v}_{n})(\mathbf{v}_{n})_{x}-\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{v})

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}_{x}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{v}_{n})(\mathbf{v}_{n}-\mathbf{v})_{x}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R})}+\|(\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{v})-\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{v}_{n}))\mathbf{v}_{x}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\|\mathbf{v}_{n}\!-\!\mathbf{v}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{R}))}+\|(\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{v}_{n})\!-\!\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{v}))\mathbf{v}_{x}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R})}

so that there holds

\displaystyle\|(\mathbf{w}_{n})_{t}-\mathbf{w}_{t}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{-1}(\mathbb{R}))}\leqslant

\displaystyle\varepsilon\|\mathbf{w}_{n}-\mathbf{w}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{R}))}+C\|\mathbf{v}_{n}-\mathbf{v}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{R}))}

\displaystyle+\|(\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{v}_{n})\!-\!\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{v}))\mathbf{v}_{x}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R})},

giving

\limsup_{n}\|(\mathbf{w}_{n})_{t}-\mathbf{w}_{t}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{-1}(\mathbb{R}))}=\limsup_{n}\|(\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{v}_{n})\!-\!\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{v}))\mathbf{v}_{x}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R})}.

Arguing as we did to control the second term on the right-hand side of the final line of (
6.20
), we use the Dominated Convergence Theorem to deduce

\displaystyle\limsup_{n}\|(\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{v}_{n})\!-\!\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{v}))\mathbf{v}_{x}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leqslant\limsup_{n}\bigg(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{v}_{n})-\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{v})\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}|\mathbf{v}_{x}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=0,

and it follows that

(6.22)

(\mathbf{w}_{n})_{t}\to\mathbf{w}_{t}\quad\text{strongly in }L^{2}(0,T;H^{-1}(\mathbb{R})).

6.
Compactness in
X
and continuity
. By combining (
6.21
) and (
6.22
), we have shown that, given any bounded sequence
\{\mathbf{v}_{n}\}_{n}
in
X
, the sequence
\{L\mathbf{v}_{n}\}_{n}
admits a strongly convergent subsequence in
X
,
i.e.
the mapping
L:X\to X
is compact. The continuity of
L
is immediate from the compactness and the uniqueness of limits,
i.e.
given a sequence
\{\mathbf{v}_{n}\}_{n}
in
X
converging to
\mathbf{v}\in X
, the previous proof shows that
\{\mathbf{w}_{n}=L\mathbf{v}_{n}\}_{n}
converges to
\mathbf{w}=L\mathbf{v}\in X
.
∎

Lemma 6.4
.

The set
\big\{\mathbf{v}\in X:\,\mathbf{v}=\lambda L\mathbf{v}\text{ for some }\lambda\in[0,1]\big\}
is bounded in
X
.

Proof.

1.
Case
\lambda\neq 0
. Let
\mathbf{v}\in A_{\lambda}
for
\lambda>0
. Then
\mathbf{v}
solves the equation

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathbf{v}_{t}-\varepsilon\mathbf{v}_{xx}=-\lambda\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{v})\mathbf{v}_{x},\\
&\mathbf{v}|_{t=0}=\lambda\mathbf{u}_{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

It follows that the usual parabolic estimate, performed by testing the equation against
\mathbf{v}
and using the boundedness of
\mathbf{M}
from (
6.8
), yields

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mathbf{v}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mathbf{v}_{x}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leqslant C\lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mathbf{v}||\mathbf{v}_{x}|\,\mathrm{d}x\leqslant\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mathbf{v}_{x}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+C\lambda^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mathbf{v}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x,

where we used Young’s inequality. We deduce that for all
t\in[0,T]

(6.23)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mathbf{v}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mathbf{v}_{x}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leqslant\lambda^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mathbf{u}_{0}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+C\lambda^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mathbf{v}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime},

whence an application of Grönwall’s Lemma gives

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{v}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leqslant\lambda^{2}\|\mathbf{u}_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}e^{C\lambda^{2}t}\quad\text{for a.e.~}t\in[0,T].

By returning to (
6.23
), we deduce that for all
\lambda>0
there holds

(6.24)

\|\mathbf{v}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{R}))}^{2}\leqslant C\lambda^{2}\|\mathbf{u}_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}e^{C\lambda^{2}T}\quad\text{for }\mathbf{v}\in A_{\lambda},

for some positive constant
C
independent of
\lambda,\,\varepsilon,\,T
.

Returning to the equation, there holds, for all
\boldsymbol{\zeta}\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{R}))
,

\displaystyle|\langle\mathbf{v}_{t},\boldsymbol{\zeta}\rangle|

\displaystyle\leqslant\varepsilon\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mathbf{v}_{x}\cdot\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{x}|\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\lambda\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{v})\mathbf{v}_{x}\cdot\boldsymbol{\zeta}|\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leqslant\varepsilon\|\mathbf{v}_{x}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R})}\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{x}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R})}+C\lambda\|\mathbf{v}_{x}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R})}\|\boldsymbol{\zeta}\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R})},

and thus

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{v}_{t}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{-1}(\mathbb{R}))}\leqslant(\varepsilon+C\lambda)\|\mathbf{v}_{x}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{R}))},

which is bounded in view of (
6.24
). By combining the previous bound with (
6.24
), we deduce

(6.25)

\|\mathbf{v}\|_{X}\leqslant C\lambda(\varepsilon+\lambda)\|\mathbf{u}_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}e^{C\lambda^{2}T}\quad\text{for }\mathbf{v}\in A_{\lambda},

for some positive constant
C
independent of
\lambda,\,\varepsilon,\,T
.

2.
Case
\lambda=0
. In this case,
\mathbf{v}
solves the heat equation
\mathbf{v}_{t}-\mathbf{v}_{xx}=0
with zero initial data. Uniqueness implies that
\mathbf{v}\equiv 0
, whence

(6.26)

\|\mathbf{v}\|_{X}=0\quad\text{for }\mathbf{v}\in A_{0}.

3.
Conclusion
. From (
6.25
) and (
6.26
), we deduce that there holds

\|\mathbf{v}\|_{X}\leqslant C\lambda(\varepsilon+\lambda)\|\mathbf{u}_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}e^{C\lambda^{2}T}\quad\text{for }\mathbf{v}\in A_{\lambda},

for all
\lambda\in[0,1]
, and thus there exists a positive constant
C_{\varepsilon,T}
independent of
\lambda
such that

\|\mathbf{v}\|_{X}\leqslant C_{\varepsilon,T}\quad\text{for }\mathbf{v}\in A_{\lambda}

for all
\lambda\in[0.1]
. Using the identification (
6.10
), it follows that
\big\{\mathbf{v}\in X:\,\mathbf{v}=\lambda L\mathbf{v}\text{ for some }\lambda\in[0,1]\big\}
is a bounded set.
∎

We are now ready to give the proof of Lemma
6.2
.

Proof of Lemma
6.2
.

Let
T>0
be arbitrary. Lemmas
6.3
and
6.4
imply that the conditions of Schaefer’s Fixed Point Theorem (Theorem
6.1
) are satisfied, so there exists
\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}\in X
a weak solution of the modified regularised problem (
6.3
),

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}_{t}+\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}_{x}=\varepsilon\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}_{xx},\\
&\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}|_{t=0}=\mathbf{u}_{0}\end{aligned}\right.

on the interval
[0,T]
. We repeat this procedure on the intervals
[nT,(n+1)T]
using the initial data
\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}(nT,\cdot)
for
n\geqslant 1
to construct a globally-defined
\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{R}))
weak solution.
∎

6.2.
Invariant Regions

This subsection is devoted to rigorously proving the formal invariant region argument presented at the start of §
6
. We establish these invariant regions first for the modified regularised problem (
6.3
), and then prove Proposition
6.1
.

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
satisfy

\mathrm{ess\,inf}_{\mathbb{R}}w_{1}(\mathbf{u}_{0}),~\mathrm{ess\,inf}_{\mathbb{R}}w_{2}(\mathbf{u}_{0})\geqslant c_{0}

and let
\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})\cap L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{R}))
with
\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}_{t}\in L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(0,T;H^{-1}(\mathbb{R}))
be a weak solution of the modified regularised problem (
6.3
). Then
\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}
is smooth away from the initial time, is a classical solution, satisfies

(6.27)

w_{1}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}),~w_{2}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})\geqslant c_{0}\quad\text{a.e.~in }\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+},

and there exists a positive constant
C
independent of
\varepsilon
such that

\|\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\leqslant C.

Proof.

The proof consists of several steps.

1.
Scalar equations for the Riemann invariants
. We argue as we did in order to obtain (
6.2
). By multiplying the equation for the modified regularised problem (
6.3
) with
\nabla_{\mathbf{u}}w_{j}^{\intercal}
(
j=1,2
), we find that there holds

\left\{\begin{aligned} &w_{1}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})_{t}+\phi_{2}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})w_{1}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})_{x}=\varepsilon w_{1}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})_{xx},\\
&w_{2}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})_{t}+\phi_{1}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})w_{2}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})_{x}=\varepsilon w_{2}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})_{xx}\end{aligned}\right.

in the weak sense. Observe that, by defining the primitive

h(s)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\int_{0}^{s}\frac{1}{\psi_{c_{0}}(\tau)}\,\mathrm{d}\tau,

where
\psi_{c_{0}}
is as defined in (
6.5
) and we note that
h\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, using (
6.7
), the equations for the Riemann invariants may be rewritten as

(6.28)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &w_{1}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})_{t}+h(w_{1}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}))_{x}=\varepsilon w_{1}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})_{xx},\\
&w_{2}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})_{t}-h(w_{2}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}))_{x}=\varepsilon w_{2}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})_{xx}.\end{aligned}\right.

2.
Application of the Maximum Principle
. Applying a standard bootstrapping argument to (
6.28
) using also the De Giorgi–Nash–Moser Theorem shows that
w_{j}
(
j=1,2
) are smooth away from the initial time; we omit the details for brevity. In turn,
w_{1},w_{2}
are classical solutions of (
6.28
). Furthermore, using the formulas
\sigma=\frac{1}{2}(w_{1}+w_{2})
and
\beta=\frac{1}{2}(w_{1}-w_{2})
, it follows that
\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}
is smooth away from the initial time and is a classical solution of (
6.3
).

It then follows from the fact that
w_{j}
(
j=1,2
) are classical solutions of the autonomous scalar equations (
6.28
) that we may apply the Maximum Principle (
cf. e.g.

[
Ser96
, §2.3]
). We deduce

\mathrm{ess\,inf}_{\mathbb{R}}w_{j}(\mathbf{u}_{0})\leqslant w_{j}(\mathbf{u})\leqslant\mathrm{ess\,sup}_{\mathbb{R}}w_{j}(\mathbf{u}_{0})\quad(j=1,2).

Since the initial data is chosen such that
c_{0}\leqslant\mathrm{ess\,inf}_{\mathbb{R}}w_{j}(\mathbf{u}_{0})
, we obtain (
6.27
). Furthermore, it follows from
\sigma=\frac{1}{2}(w_{1}+w_{2})
and
\beta=\frac{1}{2}(w_{1}-w_{2})
that there holds a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\big(\mathrm{ess\,inf}_{\mathbb{R}}w_{1}(\mathbf{u}_{0})+\mathrm{ess\,inf}_{\mathbb{R}}w_{2}(\mathbf{u}_{0})\big)\leqslant\sigma^{\varepsilon}(t,x)\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\big(\mathrm{ess\,sup}_{\mathbb{R}}w_{1}(\mathbf{u}_{0})+\mathrm{ess\,sup}_{\mathbb{R}}w_{2}(\mathbf{u}_{0})\big),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\big(\mathrm{ess\,inf}_{\mathbb{R}}w_{1}(\mathbf{u}_{0})-\mathrm{ess\,sup}_{\mathbb{R}}w_{2}(\mathbf{u}_{0})\big)\leqslant\beta^{\varepsilon}(t,x)\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\big(\mathrm{ess\,sup}_{\mathbb{R}}w_{1}(\mathbf{u}_{0})-\mathrm{ess\,inf}_{\mathbb{R}}w_{2}(\mathbf{u}_{0})\big),

and thus there exists a positive constant
C
, independent of
\varepsilon
, such that
\|\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\leqslant C
, which completes the proof.
∎

The proof of Proposition
6.1
now follows as a simple corollary of Lemmas
6.2
and
6.5
.

Proof of Proposition
6.1
.

The existence of a global weak solution
\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}
follows from Lemma
6.2
. Then, using Lemma
6.5
, we get

c_{0}\leqslant w_{j}(\mathbf{u})\quad\text{a.e.~in }\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\quad(j=1,2),

whence (
6.6
) implies, using the chain rule (which is justified due to the regularity of
h
and
\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}
), that

\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})_{x}=\nabla_{\mathbf{u}}\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}_{x}=\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}_{x}\quad\text{a.e.~in }\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}.

Thus
\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}
solves the original regularised problem (
6.1
). As Lemma
6.5
showed that
\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}
is smooth away from the initial time and solves (
6.3
) in the classical sense, it follows that it is a classical solution of (
6.1
).
∎

6.3.
Dissipation Estimate and
H^{-1}_{\mathrm{loc}}
Compactness of Entropy Dissipation Measures

In this subsection we derive the main dissipation estimate required for the pre-compactness in
H^{-1}_{\mathrm{loc}}
of the entropy dissipation measures. Our main result is the following.

Lemma 6.6

(Dissipation Estimate)
.

Let
\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}
be a solution of the regularised problem (
6.1
). Then there exists a finite increasing function
C:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
independent of
\varepsilon
such that, for all
t>0
,

(6.29)

\|\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(0,t;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}+\|\sqrt{\varepsilon}\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}_{x}\|^{2}_{L^{2}((0,t)\times\mathbb{R})}\leqslant C(t).

Proof.

We divide the proof into two steps.

1.
Special entropy pair
. Select
\eta^{*}
to be the special entropy of (
2.5
),
i.e.

\eta^{*}(\sigma,\beta)=\frac{1}{2}(\sigma^{2}+\beta^{2}),

which has gradient given by
\nabla_{\mathbf{u}}\eta^{*}=(\sigma,\beta)=\mathbf{u}
and Hessian
\nabla^{2}_{\mathbf{u}}\eta^{*}=\mathrm{Id}
; it is strongly convex. It follows from testing (
6.1
) with
(\nabla_{\mathbf{u}}\eta^{*})^{\intercal}
that there holds

(6.30)

\eta^{*}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})_{t}+q^{*}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})_{x}=\varepsilon\nabla_{\mathbf{u}}\eta^{*}\cdot\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}_{xx}=\varepsilon\eta^{*}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})_{xx}-\underbrace{\varepsilon(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}_{x})^{\intercal}\nabla^{2}_{\mathbf{u}}\eta^{*}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})\mathbf{u}_{x}^{\varepsilon}}_{=\varepsilon|\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}_{x}|^{2}}.

Testing this equation against non-negative
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
depending only on
x
and integrating by parts, we obtain

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi\eta^{*}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi|\sqrt{\varepsilon}\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}_{x}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}}q^{*}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})\varphi_{x}\,\mathrm{d}x+\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}}\eta^{*}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})\varphi_{xx}\,\mathrm{d}x,

i.e.
, using the uniform bound on
\{\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
and the continuity of
\eta^{*}
and
q^{*}
, we get

(6.31)

\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi|\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}(t,x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi|\sqrt{\varepsilon}\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}_{x}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi|\mathbf{u}_{0}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+C\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}}\!\!\big(|\varphi_{x}|+\varepsilon|\varphi_{xx}|\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

2.
Relaxation of test function
. For all
R>0
, we define
\varphi^{R}
as follows

\varphi^{R}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\mathds{1}_{[-R,R]}*\varrho_{\delta},

where
\varrho
is the standard non-negative Friedrichs bump function supported on
[-1,1]
and having unit mass, and
\varrho_{\delta}(x)=\delta^{-1}\varrho(x/\delta)
, with
\delta
chosen sufficiently small such that
\varphi^{R}\equiv 1
on
[-R,R]
and
\varphi^{R}\in C^{\infty}_{c}((-R-1,R+1))
. It follows that there exists a positive constant
C
independent of
R
such that

|\varphi^{R}_{x}|+|\varphi^{R}_{xx}|\leqslant C\mathds{1}_{[-R-1,-R]\cup[R,R+1]},

whence, inserting
\varphi^{R}
as the test function in (
6.31
), the final term on the right-hand side of (
6.31
) is estimated by

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big(|\varphi^{R}_{x}|+\varepsilon|\varphi^{R}_{xx}|\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\leqslant CT(1+\varepsilon)

for some positive constant
C
independent of
\varepsilon
and
T
. By returning to (
6.31
) and letting
R\to\infty
, the Monotone Convergence Theorem implies

\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}(t,x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\sqrt{\varepsilon}\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}_{x}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mathbf{u}_{0}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+CT(1+\varepsilon).

We deduce that there exists a positive constant
C
independent of
\varepsilon
and
T
such that

\|\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))}+\|\sqrt{\varepsilon}\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}_{x}\|^{2}_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R})}\leqslant C(1+T).

By replicating this strategy on each time-interval
[nT,(n+1)T]
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we obtain (
6.29
).
∎

We are now in a position to prove the compactness in
H^{-1}_{\mathrm{loc}}
of the entropy dissipation measures. This is encapsulated in the following lemma.

Lemma 6.7

(
H^{-1}_{\mathrm{loc}}
Compactness of Entropy Dissipation Measures)
.

Let
(\eta,q)
be a
C^{2}
entropy pair of the system (
2.3
) and
\{\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
be the sequence of regularised solutions provided by Proposition
6.1
. Then
\big\{\eta(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})_{t}+q(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})_{x}\big\}_{\varepsilon}
is pre-compact in
H^{-1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
, i.e. for all compact subsets
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
there exists a compact set
\kappa_{K}\subset H^{-1}(K)
such that

\big\{\eta(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})_{t}+q(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})_{x}\big\}_{\varepsilon}\subset\kappa_{K}.

Proof.

Recall from the computation (
6.30
) that, for a general
C^{2}
entropy pair, there holds

(6.32)

\eta(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})_{t}+q(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})_{x}=\varepsilon\eta(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})_{xx}-\varepsilon(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}_{x})^{\intercal}\nabla^{2}_{\mathbf{u}}\eta(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})\mathbf{u}_{x}^{\varepsilon}.

We divide the proof into three simple steps.

1. The dissipation estimate of Lemma
6.6
implies the estimate

(6.33)

\|\varepsilon\eta(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})_{xx}\|_{H^{-1}(K)}\leqslant C_{\eta}\sqrt{\varepsilon}\|\sqrt{\varepsilon}\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}_{x}\|_{L^{2}(K)}\leqslant C_{\eta,K}\sqrt{\varepsilon}.

2. Meanwhile, using the min-max theorem for the Rayleigh quotient of a symmetric matrix, we have

(6.34)

|\varepsilon(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}_{x})^{\intercal}\nabla^{2}_{\mathbf{u}}\eta(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})\mathbf{u}_{x}^{\varepsilon}|\leqslant C_{\eta}|\sqrt{\varepsilon}\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}_{x}|^{2},

where we used that
\eta
is
C^{2}
and
\{\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}
. The dissipation estimate of Lemma
6.6
therefore implies

(6.35)

\|\varepsilon(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}_{x})^{\intercal}\nabla^{2}_{\mathbf{u}}\eta(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})\mathbf{u}_{x}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}(K)}\leqslant C_{\eta,K},

and it follows that
\{\varepsilon(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}_{x})^{\intercal}\nabla^{2}_{\mathbf{u}}\eta(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})\mathbf{u}_{x}^{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
is pre-compact in
W^{-1,r}(K)
for all
r\in(1,2)
by the Rellich Theorem.

3. Furthermore, using the uniform
L^{\infty}
estimate on
\{\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
, there holds

\|\eta(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})_{t}+q(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})_{x}\|_{W^{-1,\infty}(K)}\leqslant C_{\eta,K}.

Using the interpolation compactness lemma of Ding, Chen, and Luo
[
DCL85
, Chapter 4]
(
cf.
Murat’s Lemma
[
Mur81
]
), the estimates (
6.33
)–(
6.35
) now imply that the sequence
\{\eta(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})_{t}+q(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})_{x}\}_{\varepsilon}
is contained in
\kappa
a compact subset of
H^{-1}(K)
.
∎

In fact, we can give a more precise expression for the entropy dissipation
-\varepsilon(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}_{x})^{\intercal}\nabla^{2}_{\mathbf{u}}\eta(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})\mathbf{u}_{x}^{\varepsilon}
when considering entropies generated by the kernels
(\chi_{j},\varsigma_{j})
(
j=1,2
) as per Lemma
5.2
. We compute explicitly, with the notation
\eta_{j}
(
j=1,2
) as in the statement of Lemma
5.2
:

(6.36)

-\varepsilon(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}_{x})^{\intercal}\nabla^{2}_{\mathbf{u}}\eta_{1}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})\mathbf{u}_{x}^{\varepsilon}=-\varepsilon\Big(f^{\prime\prime}(\beta^{\varepsilon}-\sigma^{\varepsilon})(\sigma^{\varepsilon}_{x}-\beta^{\varepsilon}_{x})^{2}+f^{\prime\prime}(\beta^{\varepsilon}+\sigma^{\varepsilon})(\sigma^{\varepsilon}_{x}+\beta^{\varepsilon}_{x})^{2}\Big),

and

(6.37)

-\varepsilon(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}_{x})^{\intercal}\nabla^{2}_{\mathbf{u}}\eta_{2}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})\mathbf{u}_{x}^{\varepsilon}=-\varepsilon\Big(-g^{\prime}(\beta^{\varepsilon}-\sigma^{\varepsilon})(\sigma^{\varepsilon}_{x}-\beta^{\varepsilon}_{x})^{2}+g^{\prime}(\beta^{\varepsilon}+\sigma^{\varepsilon})(\sigma^{\varepsilon}_{x}+\beta^{\varepsilon}_{x})^{2}\Big),

whence (
6.34
) follows immediately from the uniform
L^{\infty}
bounds for
\{\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
and the regularity of
f
and
g
, as claimed. The formulas (
6.36
) and (
6.37
) will be used in the proof of the kinetic formulation (Theorem
3.3
).

7.
Proofs of the Main Theorems

This section is devoted to the proofs of the existence of a global entropy solution (Theorem
3.1
), the weak continuity of the system (Theorem
3.2
), and the kinetic formulation (Theorem
3.3
).

7.1.
Global Existence

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

The uniform estimates provided by Proposition
6.1
as well as the
H^{-1}_{\mathrm{loc}}
compactness result of Lemma
6.7
imply that the sequence
\{\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
of solutions to the regularised problems of Proposition
6.1
satisfy the assumptions of the compensated compactness framework established in Proposition
5.3
. As such, there exists a subsequence, which we do not relabel, as well as
\mathbf{u}=(\sigma,\beta)\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
, such that

(7.1)

\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}\to\mathbf{u}\quad\text{a.e.~and strongly in $L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})$ for all }p\in[1,\infty);

additionally, the dissipation estimate of Lemma
6.6
implies that
\mathbf{u}\in L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(0,\infty;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))
. Moreover, there holds, for any
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}([0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R})
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\big(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}\varphi_{t}+\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})\varphi_{x}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbf{u}_{0}(x)\varphi(0,x)\,\mathrm{d}x+\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}_{x}\varphi_{x}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

We are now ready to conclude that the limit
\mathbf{u}
is a solution of (
2.3
) in the following senses.

1.
Distributional solution
. Let
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}([0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R})
be a test function. The continuity of
\mathbf{F}
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus(\{\sigma=0\}\cup\{\beta=\pm\sigma\})
and the invariant regions provided by Proposition
6.1
, as well as the convergence (
7.1
) and the Dominated Convergence Theorem, imply that

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\big(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}\varphi_{t}+\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})\varphi_{x}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\big(\mathbf{u}\varphi_{t}+\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{u})\varphi_{x}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t,

while the dissipation estimate of Lemma
6.6
implies that

\bigg|\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}_{x}\varphi_{x}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\bigg|\leqslant C_{\varphi}\sqrt{\varepsilon}\to 0.

It therefore follows that
\mathbf{u}=(\sigma,\beta)
is a distributional solution of (
2.3
) for
\gamma=3
.

2.
Entropy solution
. Let
(\eta,q)
be any
C^{2}
convex entropy pair. It follows from the dissipation relation (
6.32
) and the convexity of
\eta
that, given any non-negative test function
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
, there holds

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\big(\eta(\mathbf{u})\varphi_{t}+q(\mathbf{u})\varphi_{x}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\big(\eta(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})\varphi_{t}+q(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})\varphi_{x}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\geqslant-\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\eta(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})_{x}\varphi_{x}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t,

and the final term on the right-hand side is estimated using the dissipation estimate of Lemma
6.6
as

\bigg|\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\eta(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})_{x}\varphi_{x}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\bigg|\leqslant C_{\eta,\varphi}\sqrt{\varepsilon}\to 0.

It therefore follows that there holds, in the sense of distributions,

\eta(\mathbf{u})_{t}+q(\mathbf{u})_{x}\leqslant 0.

Both requirements of Definition
2.2
are fulfilled, which concludes the proof.
∎

7.2.
Weak Continuity

Proof of Theorem
3.2
.

Let
(\eta,q)
be any
C^{2}
entropy pair and define
\mathbf{u}^{n}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=(\sigma^{n},\beta^{n})
and

D^{n}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\eta(\mathbf{u}^{n})_{t}+q(\mathbf{u}^{n})_{x}.

We must show that
\{D^{n}\}_{n}
is pre-compact in
H^{-1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
; once this is proved, we may directly apply Proposition
5.3
to deduce that
\mathbf{u}^{n}\to\mathbf{u}
a.e. to some
\mathbf{u}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
. The same strategy as that used in the Proof of Theorem
3.1
is then employed to show that
\mathbf{u}
is also an entropy solution of (
2.3
) for
\gamma=3
. We proceed in two steps.

1. Define

d^{n}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\eta^{*}(\mathbf{u}^{n})_{t}+q^{*}(\mathbf{u}^{n})_{x},

where we recall that
(\eta^{*},q^{*})
is the special entropy pair given by (
2.5
). As this entropy pair is
C^{2}
, it follows directly from the definition of entropy solution that there holds

d^{n}\leqslant 0

in the sense of distributions. Furthermore, since the sequence
\{\mathbf{u}^{n}\}_{n}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}
, we deduce that
\{d^{n}\}_{n}
is pre-compact in
W^{-1,\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
. An application of Murat’s Lemma
[
Mur81
]
then implies that

\{d^{n}\}_{n}\text{ is pre-compact in }W^{-1,r}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})

for all
r\in(1,\infty)
. The same argument implies that, given any
C^{2}

convex
entropy pair
(\eta_{c},q_{c})
, the sequence
\{E^{n}\}_{n}
determined by

E^{n}=\eta_{c}(\mathbf{u}^{n})_{t}+q_{c}(\mathbf{u}^{n})_{x}

is pre-compact in
W^{-1,r}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
for all
r\in(1,\infty)
.

2. Consider the sequence of entropy dissipation measures given by

F^{n}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=D^{n}+C_{0}d^{n}

for some positive constant
C_{0}
to be determined. Note that, by the linearity of the entropy equation (
5.1
), the pair of functions determined by

\eta_{c}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\eta+C_{0}\eta^{*},\quad q_{c}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=q+C_{0}q^{*}

is also an entropy pair of the system. Furthermore, since
\eta,q
are
C^{2}
and
\{\mathbf{u}^{n}\}_{n}
is uniformly bounded (
i.e.
there exists a bounded
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that
\mathbf{u}^{n}(t,x)\in B
for a.e.
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
for all
n
), there exists a positive constant
C_{0}
such that

C_{0}\,\mathrm{Id}+\nabla^{2}\eta\text{ is positive definite on }B.

As such, the entropy pair
(\eta_{c},q_{c})
is convex on the range of all of the
\mathbf{u}^{n}
, and hence the conclusion of Step 1 implies that

\eta_{c}(\mathbf{u}^{n})_{t}+q_{c}(\mathbf{u}^{n})_{x}=F^{n}\text{ is pre-compact in }W^{-1,r}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})

for all
r\in(1,\infty)
. Meanwhile, we already know from Step 1 that
\{d^{n}\}_{n}
is pre-compact in
W^{-1,r}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
, whence the linearity
D^{n}=F^{n}-C_{0}d^{n}
implies, in particular, that

\{D^{n}\}_{n}\text{ is pre-compact in }H^{-1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}).

As per the proof of Theorem
3.1
, we deduce that
\mathbf{u}^{n}\to\mathbf{u}
a.e. and strongly in
L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
for all
p\in[1,\infty)
and that
\mathbf{u}
is an entropy solution, which completes the proof.
∎

7.3.
Kinetic Formulation

Proof of Theorem
3.3
.

We follow the strategy of,
e.g.
,
[
SS19
, §6]
. By rewriting (
6.32
) in terms of the kernels
(\chi_{j},\varsigma_{j})
(
j=1,2
) and using the formulas (
6.36
)–(
6.37
), we have for all
(f,g)\in\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{B}

(7.2)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\langle\chi_{1}(\sigma^{\varepsilon}(t,x),\beta^{\varepsilon}(t,x),\cdot),f\rangle+\partial_{x}\langle\varsigma_{1}(\sigma^{\varepsilon}(t,x),\beta^{\varepsilon}(t,x),\cdot),f\rangle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon\partial_{xx}\langle\chi_{1}(\sigma^{\varepsilon}(t,x),\beta^{\varepsilon}(t,x),\cdot),f\rangle

\displaystyle-\langle\partial^{2}_{s}\mu_{1}^{\varepsilon}(t,x,\cdot),f\rangle

and

(7.3)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\langle\chi_{2}(\sigma^{\varepsilon}(t,x),\beta^{\varepsilon}(t,x),\cdot),g\rangle+\partial_{x}\langle\varsigma_{2}(\sigma^{\varepsilon}(t,x),\beta^{\varepsilon}(t,x),\cdot),g\rangle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon\partial_{xx}\langle\chi_{2}(\sigma^{\varepsilon}(t,x),\beta^{\varepsilon}(t,x),\cdot),g\rangle

\displaystyle-\langle\partial_{s}\mu_{2}^{\varepsilon}(t,x,\cdot),g\rangle,

where we have defined

(7.4)

\displaystyle\mu_{1}^{\varepsilon}(t,x)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\varepsilon\Big(\delta(\beta^{\varepsilon}-\sigma^{\varepsilon}-s)(\sigma^{\varepsilon}_{x}-\beta^{\varepsilon}_{x})^{2}+\delta(\beta^{\varepsilon}+\sigma^{\varepsilon}-s)(\sigma^{\varepsilon}_{x}+\beta^{\varepsilon}_{x})^{2}\Big),

\displaystyle\mu_{2}^{\varepsilon}(t,x)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\varepsilon\Big(\delta(\beta^{\varepsilon}-\sigma^{\varepsilon}-s)(\sigma^{\varepsilon}_{x}-\beta^{\varepsilon}_{x})^{2}-\delta(\beta^{\varepsilon}+\sigma^{\varepsilon}-s)(\sigma^{\varepsilon}_{x}+\beta^{\varepsilon}_{x})^{2}\Big).

We note in passing that a standard argument involving Riemann sums shows that we may interchange the derivatives with the duality brackets in equations (
7.2
)–(
7.3
). By testing against any
\psi\in C^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})
, we get

(7.5)

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\Big\langle\Big(\psi_{t}\chi_{1}(\sigma^{\varepsilon}(t,x),\beta^{\varepsilon}(t,x),\cdot)+\psi_{x}\varsigma_{1}

\displaystyle(\sigma^{\varepsilon}(t,x),\beta^{\varepsilon}(t,x),\cdot),f\Big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\psi_{xx}\langle\chi_{1}(\sigma^{\varepsilon}(t,x),\beta^{\varepsilon}(t,x),\cdot),f\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\psi\langle\partial^{2}_{s}\mu_{1}^{\varepsilon}(t,x,\cdot),f\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t,

and

(7.6)

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\Big\langle\Big(\psi_{t}\chi_{2}(\sigma^{\varepsilon}(t,x),\beta^{\varepsilon}(t,x),\cdot)+\psi_{x}\varsigma_{2}

\displaystyle(\sigma^{\varepsilon}(t,x),\beta^{\varepsilon}(t,x),\cdot),f\Big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\psi_{xx}\langle\chi_{2}(\sigma^{\varepsilon}(t,x),\beta^{\varepsilon}(t,x),\cdot),g\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\psi\langle\partial_{s}\mu_{2}^{\varepsilon}(t,x,\cdot),g\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t,

Observe that, for
j=1,2
, with
\phi\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R})
, using the explicit formulas for
\eta_{1}
and
\eta_{2}
in Lemma
5.2
as well as the uniform bounds
\sup_{\varepsilon}\|\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}<\infty
, we have

\big|\langle\chi_{j}(\sigma^{\varepsilon}(t,x),\beta^{\varepsilon}(t,x),\cdot),\phi\rangle\big|=|\eta_{j}^{\psi}(\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon})|\leqslant C_{\phi}

for some positive constant
C_{\phi}
independent of
\varepsilon
. It therefore follows that, for any
\varphi_{1}\in\mathcal{A}
and
\varphi_{2}\in\mathcal{B}
, there holds (
j=1,2
)

\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\psi_{xx}\langle\chi_{j}(\sigma^{\varepsilon}(t,x),\beta^{\varepsilon}(t,x),\cdot),\varphi_{j}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\bigg|\leqslant C_{\varphi_{j}}\varepsilon\|\psi_{xx}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})}\to 0\quad\text{as }\varepsilon\to 0.

Meanwhile, the strong convergence
\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}\to\mathbf{u}
pointwise almost everywhere implies that, for any
\varphi_{1}\in\mathcal{A}
and
\varphi_{2}\in\mathcal{B}
, there holds (
j=1,2
)

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\Big\langle\Big(\psi_{t}\chi_{j}

\displaystyle(\sigma^{\varepsilon}(t,x),\beta^{\varepsilon}(t,x),\cdot)+\psi_{x}\varsigma_{j}(\sigma^{\varepsilon}(t,x),\beta^{\varepsilon}(t,x),\cdot),\varphi_{j}\Big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\Big\langle\Big(\psi_{t}\chi_{j}(\sigma(t,x),\beta(t,x),\cdot)+\psi_{x}\varsigma_{j}(\sigma(t,x),\beta(t,x),\cdot),\varphi_{j}\Big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

Also, we see directly from the formulas (
7.4
) that the distributions
\mu^{\varepsilon}_{j}
(
j=1,2
) are such that, given any
\phi\in C^{0}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\times\mathbb{R})
,

(7.7)

\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\langle\mu^{\varepsilon}_{j}(t,x,\cdot),\phi\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\bigg|\leqslant 2\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\supp\phi)}\int_{\supp\phi}|\sqrt{\varepsilon}\mathbf{u}^{\varepsilon}_{x}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\leqslant C,

for some positive constant
C
independent of
\varepsilon
, where we used the uniform dissipation estimate of
Lemma

6.6
to obtain the final inequality. It follows that the sequences
\{\mu^{\varepsilon}_{j}\}_{\varepsilon}
(

C^{0}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\times\mathbb{R})

\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}

\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}

\mu^{\varepsilon}_{j}\overset{*}{\rightharpoonup}\mu_{j}\text{ in }\big(C^{0}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\times\mathbb{R})\big)^{\prime}\qquad(j=1,2);

we note in passing that an analogous estimate to (
7.7
) shows that this convergence also occurs in the sense of duality with

7.5
)–(
7.6

3.3
)–(
3.4

7.4

\mu_{1}

∎

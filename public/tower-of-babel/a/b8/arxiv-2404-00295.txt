A system of hypergeometric differential equations in m m variables of rank p m p^{m}
Kaneko Jyoichi, Matsumoto Keiji, Ohara Katsuyoshi, Terasoma Tomohide

Abstract
. We define a hypergeometric series in m m variables with p + ( p − 1 ) ​ m p+(p-1)m parameters, which reduces to the generalized hypergeometric series F p − 1 p {}_{p}F_{p-1} when m = 1 m=1 , and to Lauricella’s hypergeometric series F C F_{C} in m m variables when p = 2 p=2 . We give a system of hypergeometric differential equations annihilating the series. Under some non-integral conditions on parameters, we give an Euler type integral representation of the series, and linearly independent p m p^{m} solutions to this system around a point near to the origin. We show that this system is of rank p m p^{m} , and determine its singular locus.

The hypergeometric series is defined by

{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\begin{matrix}a_{1},a_{2}\\
b_{1}\end{matrix};x\right)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(a_{1},n)(a_{2},n)}{(b_{1},n)(1,n)}x^{n},

2-b_{1}\end{matrix};x\right)

span the space of solutions to the hypergeometric differential equation
around a point near to the origin.
The hypergeometric series and the hypergeometric differential

Proposition 2.1
.

This series absolutely converges in the domain

\mathbb{D}=\{x\in\mathbb{C}^{m}\mid\sqrt[p]{|x_{1}|}+\cdots+\sqrt[p]{|x_{m}|}<1\}.

Proof.

We modify the proof of
[

By the formula

\frac{1}{\varGamma(s)}=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{(s,N)}{(N-1)!N^{s}},

we have

(a_{i},n_{i})\sim\frac{(n_{i}-1)!n_{i}^{a_{i}}}{\varGamma(a_{i})}=\frac{n_{i}!n_{i}^{a_{i}-1}}{\varGamma(a_{i})}\quad(\mathbb{N}\ni n_{i}\to\infty).

Since

\displaystyle A_{n}=\frac{(a_{1},n_{1}+\cdots+n_{m})\cdots(a_{p},n_{1}+\cdots+n_{m})}{\prod_{k=1}^{m}\{(b_{1,k},n_{k})\cdots(b_{p-1,k},n_{k})n_{k}!\}}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq p-1\\
1\leq k\leq m\end{subarray}}\varGamma(b_{j,k})}{\varGamma(a_{1})\cdots\varGamma(a_{p})}\cdot\frac{(n_{1}\!+\!\cdots\!+\!n_{m})^{a_{1}+\cdots+a_{p}-p}}{\prod_{k=1}^{m}n_{k}^{b_{1,k}+\cdots+b_{p-1,k}-p+1}}\cdot\left(\frac{(n_{1}\!+\!\cdots\!+\!n_{m})!}{n_{1}!\cdots n_{m}!}\right)^{p},

as

\prod_{k=1}^{m}n_{k}^{p-1-b_{1,k}-\cdots-b_{p-1,k}}\leq n_{1}^{\beta}\cdots n_{m}^{\beta}\leq\frac{(n_{1}+\cdots+n_{m})^{m\beta}}{m^{m\beta}},

there exists a positive constant

such that

\displaystyle|A_{n}x_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots x_{m}^{n_{m}}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\cdot(n_{1}\!+\!\cdots\!+\!n_{m})^{\alpha}\cdot\left(\frac{(n_{1}\!+\!\cdots\!+\!n_{m})!}{n_{1}!\cdots n_{m}!}\right)^{p}\cdot|x_{1}|^{n_{1}}\cdots|x_{m}|^{n_{m}},

\displaystyle C\sum_{N=0}^{\infty}N^{\alpha}\left(\sum_{n_{1}+\cdots+n_{m}=N}\frac{(n_{1}+\cdots+n_{m})!}{n_{1}!\cdots n_{m}!}|x_{1}|^{\frac{n_{1}}{p}}\cdots|x_{m}|^{\frac{n_{m}}{p}}\right)^{p}

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose that

\displaystyle L_{j}(t)

\displaystyle 1-t_{j,1}-\cdots-t_{j,m},

\displaystyle\sum_{N=0}^{\infty}\frac{(a_{p},N)}{N!}\sum_{n_{1}+\cdots+n_{m}=N}\frac{N!}{n_{1}!\cdots n_{m}!}\Big(\frac{x_{1}}{T_{1}}\Big)^{n_{1}}\cdots\Big(\frac{x_{m}}{T_{m}}\Big)^{n_{m}}

\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1},\dots,n_{m}=0}^{\infty}\frac{(a_{p},n_{1}+\cdots+n_{m})}{n_{1}!\cdots n_{m}!}(T_{1}^{-n_{1}}\cdots T_{m}^{-n_{m}})\cdot(x_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots x_{m}^{n_{m}}).

\displaystyle\frac{\varGamma(1\!-\!b_{j,1}\!-\!n_{1})\cdots\varGamma(1\!-\!b_{j,m}\!-\!n_{m})}{\varGamma(1-a_{j}-n_{1}-\cdots-n_{m})}\varGamma(1\!+\!b_{j,1}\!+\!\cdots\!+\!b_{j,m}\!-\!a_{j}\!-\!m)

By using the formulas

we transform

\displaystyle\varGamma(1-b_{j,k}-n_{k})=\frac{\pi}{\sin(\pi(b_{j,k}+n_{k}))\varGamma(b_{j,k}+n_{k})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{(-1)^{n_{k}}\sin(\pi b_{j,k})\varGamma(b_{j,k})(b_{j,k},n_{k})}=\frac{\varGamma(1-b_{j,k})}{(-1)^{n_{k}}(b_{j,k},n_{k})}.

Similarly we have

\varGamma(1-a_{j}-n_{1}-\cdots-n_{m})=\frac{\varGamma(1-a_{j})}{(-1)^{n_{1}+\cdots+n_{m}}(a_{j},n_{1}+\cdots+n_{m})}.

Hence the following gamma factors including
n_{1},\dots,n_{m}

can be expressed in terms of Pochhammer’s symbols as

\displaystyle\frac{\varGamma(1\!-\!b_{j,1}\!-\!n_{1})\cdots\varGamma(1\!-\!b_{j,m}\!-\!n_{m})}{\varGamma(1-a_{j}-n_{1}-\cdots-n_{m})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\varGamma(1\!-\!b_{j,1})\cdots\varGamma(1\!-\!b_{j,m})}{\varGamma(1-a_{j})}\cdot\frac{(a_{j},n_{1}+\cdots+n_{m})}{(b_{j,1},n_{1})\cdots(b_{j,m},n_{m})},

which yield the coefficient of
x_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots x_{m}^{n_{m}}
in

F_{C}^{p,m}(a,B;x)
.
∎

4.
A system of hypergeometric differential equations

We can easily show the following lemma by using actions of Euler’s operators

\theta_{k}=x\partial_{k}

(k=1,\dots,m)
on power functions

of
x_{j}

(j=1,\dots,m)

(4.1)

\theta_{k}(x_{j}^{\alpha_{j}})=\delta_{j,k}\alpha_{j}x_{j}^{\alpha_{j}},

where
\partial_{k}
denotes the holomorphic partial differential operator

\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}
and

denotes Kronecker’s symbol.

Lemma 4.1
.

The series

satisfies hypergeometric
differential equations

(4.2)

\displaystyle\theta_{k}(b_{1,k}-1+\theta_{k})\cdots(b_{p-1,k}-1+\theta_{k})F_{C}^{p,m}(a,B;x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x_{k}(a_{1}+\theta_{1}+\cdots+\theta_{m})\cdots(a_{p}+\theta_{1}+\cdots+\theta_{m})F_{C}^{p,m}(a,B;x)

for
k=1,\dots,m
.

Definition 4.1
.

We define
\mathcal{F}_{C}^{p,m}(a,B)
as the system of the
m

hypergeometric differential equations in Lemma
4.1
.

We set
\mathbb{Z}_{p}=\{1,2,\dots,p\}=\mathbb{Z}/(p\mathbb{Z})
. The element
p\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is
often regarded as
0
.
From now on we assume the following conditions:

(4.3)

a_{i}-\sum_{k=1}^{m}b_{j_{k},k},\notin\mathbb{Z},

(4.4)

b_{j,k}-b_{j^{\prime},k}\notin\mathbb{Z},

where
1\leq i\leq p
,
1\leq j<j^{\prime}\leq p
,
1\leq k\leq m
, and

J=(j_{1},\dots,j_{m})\in(\mathbb{Z}_{p})^{m}
.
There are
p^{m+1}
conditions for (

),
and

conditions for (
4.4
).

The following proposition gives explicit expressions of independent
solutions to the system of differential equations
(
4.2
).
To give the statement of the proposition, we introduce
maps
\eta_{j_{k}}
(

,
j_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
)
on the space of exponent parameters

\eta_{j_{k}}(b_{k})=b_{k}+(1-b_{j_{k},k})(\mathbf{1}_{p}+e_{j_{k}}-e_{p}),

where

and

is the
j
-th unit column vector.

Remark 4.1
.

If

then

and

is the identity map
id_{p}
,
otherwise it is a reflection

\eta_{j_{k}}=id_{p}-\frac{2}{\;{}^{t}v_{j_{k}}Wv_{j_{k}}}v_{j_{k}}\;^{t}v_{j_{k}}W,

where
v_{j_{k}}=\mathbf{1}_{p}+e_{j_{k}}-e_{p}=\;^{t}(1,\dots,1,\overset{j_{k}\textrm{-th}}{2},1,\dots,1,0)

W=p\cdot id_{p}-\mathbf{1}_{p}\;^{t}\mathbf{1}_{p}=\begin{pmatrix}p-1&-1&\cdots&-1\\
-1&p-1&\cdots&-1\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
-1&-1&\cdots&p-1\end{pmatrix},

which is a positive semi-definite matrix with

.

Proposition 4.1
.

Under the conditions (
4.3
) and
(
4.4
),

solutions

to

,
where

Remark 4.2
.

If
j_{k}=p(=0)
then
\mu_{J,k}=0
and
\eta_{j_{k}}(b_{k})=b_{k}
. Thus we have

Proof.

We act the operators

Move the factor

Then we have the system

The linear independence of the

∎

By Proposition

5.1.
Weyl algebras and systems of differential equations

In this section, we recall basic facts on systems of
differential equations and the Weyl algebra

D_{m}=\mathbb{C}\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{m},\partial_{1},\dots,\partial_{m}\rangle
,
which is a non-commutative
\mathbb{C}
-algebra generated by

x_{1},\dots,x_{m}
and
\partial_{1},\dots,\partial_{m}
with relations

(5.3)

x_{i}x_{j}=x_{j}x_{i},\quad\partial_{i}\partial_{j}=\partial_{j}\partial_{i},\quad\partial_{i}x_{j}=x_{j}\partial_{i}+\delta_{i,j},

for
1\leq i,j\leq m
.
By the relation (
5.3
), any element
\Theta\in D_{m}
can be
expressed as a finite sum

(5.4)

\Theta=\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{m}}c_{\alpha,\beta}x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{m}^{\alpha_{m}}\partial_{1}^{\beta_{1}}\cdots\partial_{m}^{\beta_{m}},

where
c_{\alpha,\beta}\in\mathbb{C}
vanish for sufficiently large

|\alpha|=\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{m}
and
|\beta|=\beta_{1}+\cdots+\beta_{m}

for
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{m})
,
\beta=(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{m})
.
We set

\operatorname{ord}(\Theta)=\max\{|\beta|\mid c_{\alpha,\beta}\neq 0\}.

The subspaces

D_{m}^{\leq d}=\{\Theta\in D_{m}\mid\operatorname{ord}(\Theta)\leq d\}\quad(d\in\mathbb{N})

form a filtration of
D_{m}
compatible with multiplication,
and we have the associated graded ring

\operatorname{gr}(D_{m})=\bigoplus_{d\in\mathbb{N}}\operatorname{gr}^{d}(D_{m}),\quad\operatorname{gr}^{d}(D_{m})=D_{m}^{\leq d}/D_{m}^{\leq d-1},

of
D_{m}
.
Note that
\operatorname{gr}(D_{m})
is a commutative ring
isomorphic to the polynomial ring

\mathbb{C}[x,\xi]=\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\dots,x_{m},\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{m}]

in
2m
variables

x_{1},\dots,x_{m},

\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{m}
, where
\xi_{i}
is the image of
\partial_{i}

under the natural projection.
For
\Theta\in D_{m}^{\leq d}
in (
5.4
),
its image in
\operatorname{gr}^{d}(D_{m})
is equal to

\operatorname{in}^{d}(\Theta)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{m}\\
\beta_{1}+\cdots+\beta_{m}=d\end{subarray}}c_{\alpha,\beta}x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{m}^{\alpha_{m}}\xi_{1}^{\beta_{1}}\cdots\xi_{m}^{\beta_{m}},

which is called the
d
-initial form of
\Theta
.

Let
\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{r}
be elements in
D_{m}
,
and
\mathfrak{I}
be the left ideal of
D_{m}
generated by them.
By regarding the symbols
\partial_{1},\dots,\partial_{m}
as the
holomorphic partial differential
operators, we have a system of linear differential equations

(5.5)

\ell_{1}\cdot f(x)=0,\dots,\ell_{r}\cdot f(x)=0,

with respect to unknown function
f(x)=f(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})
.
Note that this system is equivalent to

\ell\cdot f(x)=0

for any
\ell\in\mathfrak{I}
.
In this way, systems of linear differential equations correspond
one to one to left ideals of
D_{m}
.
By this correspondence, we identify a left ideal of

D_{m}
with a system of linear differential equations.
For the left ideal
\mathfrak{I}
of
D_{m}
, its
initial ideal
\operatorname{in}(\mathfrak{I})
in
\mathbb{C}[x,\xi]

is defined by the ideal

\bigoplus_{d}\{\operatorname{in}^{d}(\Theta)\mid\Theta\in\mathfrak{I}\cap D_{m}^{\leq d}\}.

Fact 5.1

(
[
SST
, Definition 1.4.8]
)
.

The rank of
\mathfrak{I}
is defined by

\operatorname{rank}\,(\mathfrak{I})=\dim_{\mathbb{C}(x)}\big(\mathbb{C}(x)[\xi]\,/\,(\mathbb{C}(x)[\xi]\cdot\operatorname{in}\,(\mathfrak{I}))\big),

where the quotient space in its right hand side is regarded as
a vector space over the rational function field
\mathbb{C}(x)
.
It is known that
\operatorname{rank}\,(\mathfrak{I})
coincides with the dimension of
the space of solutions to
(
5.5
)
around a general point.

The characteristic variety of
\mathfrak{I}

is defined by

\operatorname{Ch}(\mathfrak{I})=\{(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{C}^{2m}\mid L(x,\xi)=0\ \textrm{ for any }L\in\operatorname{in}(\mathfrak{I})\}.

Fact 5.2

(
[
SST
, Section 1.4]
)
.

The singular locus
\operatorname{Sing}\,(\mathfrak{I})
of
\mathfrak{I}

is defined by the Zariski closure of

\operatorname{pr}_{x}(\operatorname{Ch}(\mathfrak{I})\setminus\{(x,\mathbf{0}_{m})\in\mathbb{C}^{2m}\mid x\in\mathbb{C}^{m}\}),

where
\operatorname{pr}_{x}:(x_{1},\dots,x_{m},\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{m})\mapsto(x_{1},\dots,x_{m}).

The space of solutions form a local system on the complement
of the singular locus.

5.2.

{\operatorname{A}}

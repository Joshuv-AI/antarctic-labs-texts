Volume preserving spacetime mean curvature flow and foliations of initial data sets
Jacopo Tenan

Abstract
. We consider a volume preserving curvature evolution of surfaces in an asymptotically Euclidean initial data set with positive ADM-energy. The speed is given by a nonlinear function of the mean curvature which generalizes the spacetime mean curvature recently considered by Cederbaum-Sakovich (Calc. Var. PDE, 2021). Following a classical approach by Huisken-Yau (Invent. Math., 1996), we show that the flow starting from suitably round initial surfaces exists for all times and converges to a constant (spacetime) curvature limit. This provides an alternative construction of the CSTMC foliation by Cederbaum-Sakovich and has applications in the definition of center of mass of an isolated system in General Relativity.



1.
Introduction

We consider a triple
(\textup{M},\overline{\textup{g}},\overline{\textup{K}})
, where
(\textup{M},\overline{\textup{g}})
is a smooth complete three-dimensional Riemannian manifold and
\overline{\textup{K}}
a symmetric
(0,2)
-tensor field on
M
, such that

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\overline{\textup{S}}-|\overline{\textup{K}}|^{2}+(\textup{tr}_{\overline{\textup{g}}}\overline{\textup{K}})^{2}=2\overline{\mu}\\
\overline{\nabla}\cdot\left(\overline{\textup{K}}\right)-\overline{\textup{d}}\left(\textup{tr}_{\overline{\textup{g}}}\overline{\textup{K}}\right)=\overline{\textup{J}}\end{cases}

holds for a smooth function
\overline{\mu}\in C^{\infty}(\textup{M})
and a smooth 1-form
\overline{\textup{J}}\in\Omega^{\infty}(\textup{M})
. For our purposes, we will assume that
(\textup{M},\overline{\textup{g}},\overline{\textup{K}})
and
(\overline{\mu},\overline{\textup{J}})
satisfy the following definitions.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\delta\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
. A
C_{\frac{1}{2}+\delta}^{2}
-
asymptotically flat initial data set
is a triple
(\textup{M},\overline{\textup{g}},\overline{\textup{K}})
such that there exist a compact subset
\textup{C}\subset\textup{M}
, a constant
\overline{c}>0
and a diffeomorphism
\vec{x}:\textup{M}\setminus\textup{C}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{B}}_{1}(\vec{0})
such that

(1.2)

|\overline{\textup{g}}_{\alpha\beta}-\delta_{\alpha\beta}|+|\vec{x}|\left|\partial_{\gamma}\overline{\textup{g}}_{\alpha\beta}\right|+|\vec{x}|^{2}\left|\partial_{\gamma}\partial_{\epsilon}\overline{\textup{g}}_{\alpha\beta}\right|\leq\overline{c}|\vec{x}|^{-\frac{1}{2}-\delta},

(1.3)

|\overline{\textup{K}}_{\alpha\beta}|+|\vec{x}||\partial_{\gamma}\overline{\textup{K}}_{\alpha\beta}|\leq\overline{c}|\vec{x}|^{-\frac{3}{2}-\delta},

where
\overline{\textup{g}}_{\alpha\beta}=\left(\vec{x}^{*}\overline{\textup{g}}\right)_{\alpha\beta}
and
\overline{\textup{K}}_{\alpha\beta}=(\vec{x}^{*}\overline{\textup{K}})_{\alpha\beta}
. Here
\partial_{\gamma},\partial_{\epsilon}
are partial derivatives with respect to the Euclidean coordinates, and the norms are taken in the Euclidean metric.

Definition 1.2
.

We say that
(\textup{M},\overline{\textup{g}},\overline{\textup{K}})
is
constrained
by the pair
(\overline{\mu},\overline{\textup{J}})
if (
1.1
) holds together with

(1.4)

|\overline{\mu}|+|\overline{\textup{J}}|\leq\overline{c}|\vec{x}|^{-3-\delta}.

These triples
(\textup{M},\overline{\textup{g}},\overline{\textup{K}})
arise in Mathematical General Relativity as spacelike hypersurfaces of a Lorentzian spacetime
(\textup{M},\overline{\textup{g}})
modelling an isolated gravitating system, with
\overline{\textup{K}}
the second fundamental form of
M
. In this description, it is important to give an appropriate definition of the center of mass of the system which is physically meaningful. A classical definition, inspired by the one of ADM-mass, was provided by Beig-Ó Murchadha
[
4
]
as the limit of a flux integral on Euclidean spheres with increasing radius. A different approach was introduced by Huisken and Yau
[
22
]
, who proved the existence of a unique constant mean curvature (CMC)-foliation of the outer part of M, and defined the center of mass as the limit of the Euclidean barycenter of the leaves of the foliation as the radius tends to infinity. The result in
[
22
]
considered manifolds
M
which are asymptotically Schwarzschild and with strong decay assumptions. The construction was then generalized by various authors (e.g.
[
33
]
,
[
28
]
,
[
17
]
) culminating in the work of Nerz
[
30
]
who considered asymptotically flat spaces with optimal decay assumptions. Under suitable symmetry assumptions, known as
strong Regge-Teitelboim conditions
, it was proved that the CMC and the Beig-Ó Murchadha centers of mass both exist and coincide. On the other hand, on an asymptotically flat manifold where the (weak) Regge-Teitelboim condition are not satisfied, these centers of mass may not be well-defined. In particular, Cederbaum and Nerz
[
9
]
constructed explicit examples where both these objects do not converge.

For this reason, Cederbaum and Sakovich
[
8
]
have introduced a new notion of center of mass based on foliations involving a modified curvature. For a smooth surface
\Sigma\hookrightarrow\textup{M}
, let us denote by
H
the usual mean curvature, by
g=\iota^{*}\overline{\textup{g}}
the induced metric on
\Sigma
, and by
P=\textup{tr}_{g}(\overline{\textup{K}})
the trace of
\overline{\textup{K}}
on
\Sigma
. We then call the quantity
\mathcal{H}=\sqrt{H^{2}-P^{2}}
the
spacetime mean curvature
of
\Sigma
. We observe that
\mathcal{H}
equals the (Minkowski) length of the mean curvature vector of the 2-codimensional surface
\Sigma
. In
[
8
]
, Cederbaum and Sakovich prove the existence of a
constant spacetime mean curvature
(CSTMC)
foliation
of the outer part of
M
, and define a corresponding CSTMC-center of mass as the limit of the barycenters of the leaves. It is proved that the CSTMC-center of mass exists in some relevant cases in which the CMC-center of mass does not, see
[
8
, Sect. 9]
.

From a spacelike point of view, the equation satisfied by each CSTMC surface looks like a
prescribed mean curvature equation
. A similar equation was present in the work of Metzger
[
28
]
, who constructed surfaces satisfying the so called
constant expansion equations

\Theta_{\pm}:=H\pm P\equiv\textup{const}
, where
\Theta_{\pm}
are the
null curvatures
of
\Sigma
. Instead, each leaf in the foliation constructed by Cederbaum and Sakovich in
[
8
]
satisfies the equation

\textup{const}\equiv\mathcal{H}=\sqrt{H^{2}-P^{2}}=\sqrt{H+P}\sqrt{H-P}=:\sqrt{\Theta_{+}}\sqrt{\Theta_{-}}.

The two equations are different if the right hand side is strictly positive, as in the case of our interest. On the other hand, it is interesting to notice that they coincide if the right-hand side is zero, in which case we recover a well-known class called
trapped surfaces
, or MOTS, which has been studied by various authors (for example
[
1
]
or
[
12
]
).

The existence of the foliations described above has been obtained by different methods through the various papers. The original approach by Huisken and Yau considered the volume preserving mean curvature flow starting from an Euclidean sphere with sufficiently large radius, showed global existence of the flow and obtained the CMC surface as the limit for large times. This flow was no longer studied in the context of asymptotically flat manifolds, except for the work of Corvino and Wu
[
10
]
. The foliations were constructed instead by strategies based on the implicit function theorem (
[
33
]
,
[
17
]
), a continuity method (
[
28
]
,
[
30
]
,
[
8
]
) or a Lyapunov-Schmidt reduction
[
13
]
. The volume preserving mean curvature flow has been recently studied by Sinestrari and the author
[
32
]
in the context of asymptotically flat spaces, in order to recover the CMC-foliation under the optimal hypothesis of Nerz. We also mention the recent work
[
16
]
where an area preserving mean curvature flow is analyzed in the context of the Schwarzschild space.

In this paper we study a modification of the volume preserving mean curvature flow. Let us describe our setting in more detail. We consider an initial data set
(\textup{M},\overline{\textup{g}},\overline{\textup{K}})
which is
C_{\frac{1}{2}+\delta}^{2}
-asymptotically flat and constrained by the densities
(\overline{\mu},\overline{\textup{J}})
. For a general but fixed power
q\geq 2
we set
\mathcal{H}=\sqrt[q]{H^{q}-|P|^{q}}
, whenever this quantity is defined. Notice that this is the Minkowski
q
-length of the vector
(\vec{H},P)
, where
\vec{H}
is the vector mean curvature. The
volume preserving spacetime mean curvature flow
(VPSTMCF) is a family of time dependent immersions
F:\Sigma\times[0,T]\to\textup{M}
, with
\Sigma
a closed 2-surface, which evolves according to

(1.5)

\frac{\partial F}{\partial t}(t,\cdot)=-\left[\mathcal{H}(t,\cdot)-\hbar(t)\right]\nu(t,\cdot),

where
\hbar(t)
is the integral average of
\mathcal{H}(t,\cdot)
. Observe that, for
q=2
,
\mathcal{H}
is the spacetime mean curvature defined in
[
8
]
. As initial data for the flow (
1.5
), we consider a well-centered (in the sense of Nerz
[
30
]
) CMC-surface. As in
[
22
]
, the evolution is parametrized by a non-physical time parameter and takes place in a fixed spacelike slice, but in the present paper it has a speed that takes into account the spacetime texture of the initial data set. We aim to prove long-time existence of this flow, together with a convergence result. See the statement of Theorem
1.3
for details.

The approach we employ here to study the flow partly follows the one we have used in
[
32
]
in the spacelike case.
We introduce a class of round surfaces defined by integral inequalities and prove that the solution
of the flow belongs to this class for every positive time. As in the construction in
[
8
]
, which is based on the continuity method, we build the results on the spacelike case
[
30
,
32
]
by considering as initial value for the flow (
1.5
) a surface with constant
(classical) mean curvature, which satisfies better estimates than a Euclidean coordinate sphere.
In this way we can assume on our solution a stronger condition on the oscillation of
H
than in
[
32
]
,
see equation (
3.1
), which allows us to extend the spectral analysis of the stability operator
of
[
8
,
30
]
without the remainder which occurs in
[
32
]
. On the other hand, the estimates
of the integral norms of the curvatures along the flow become more complicate and contain
nontrivial additional terms due to the nonlinearity of the speed. However, we are able to show that
the behaviour of the speed function is sufficiently close to linear for large radius of our surfaces
in order to estimate the additional terms and prove that our roundness class is preserved.

We now state the main result of the paper.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(\textup{M},\overline{\textup{g}},\overline{\textup{K}})
be a constrained
C_{\frac{1}{2}+\delta}^{2}
-asymptotically flat initial data set in the sense of Definition
1.1
and
1.2
, and suppose that
E_{\textup{ADM}}>0
. Fix
q\geq 2
. Let
\iota:\Sigma\hookrightarrow\textup{M}
be a
closed CMC-surface immersed
in
(\textup{M},\overline{\textup{g}})
and, setting
\sigma=\sigma_{\Sigma}:=\sqrt{|\Sigma|/4\pi}
, suppose that there exists
C_{0}>0
such that

(1.6)

\|\overset{\circ}{A}\|_{L^{4}(\Sigma)}\leq C_{0}\sigma^{-1-\delta},\qquad|\vec{z}_{\Sigma}|\leq C_{0}\sigma^{1-\delta},\qquad\frac{\sigma}{r_{\Sigma}}\leq 1+C_{0}^{-1},

where
r_{\Sigma}:=\displaystyle\min_{x\in\Sigma}|\vec{x}(x)|
. Then, there exists
\sigma_{0}=\sigma_{0}(C_{0},\overline{c},\delta,q)>1
such that if
\sigma>\sigma_{0}
, the solution
\Sigma_{t}
to the spacetime mean curvature flow (
1.5
) exists for every
t\in[0,\infty)
and converges exponentially fast to a surface
\Sigma^{\textup{st}}_{\infty}
satisfying the prescribed mean curvature equation

(1.7)

H_{\Sigma_{\infty}^{\textup{st}}}^{q}=|P|_{\Sigma_{\infty}^{\textup{st}}}^{q}+\hbar_{\Sigma_{\infty}^{\textup{st}}}^{q}

for some constant
\hbar_{\Sigma_{\infty}^{\textup{st}}}>0
.

This flow approach provides an alternative construction of the foliation obtained in
[
8
]
. In particular, when
q=2
, letting evolve the CMC-foliation
\{\Sigma^{\sigma}\}_{\sigma\geq\sigma_{0}}
constructed in
[
30
]
or in
[
32
]
, we obtain when
t\to+\infty
a CSTMC-foliation
\{\Sigma_{\textup{st}}^{\sigma}\}_{\sigma\geq\sigma_{0}}
which coincides with the one introduced by Cederbaum-Sakovich. Our construction has an independent interest in the analysis of the behaviour of curvature flows in asymptotically flat spaces and proves that the foliation exists also when
q>2
. While in the case
q=2
equation (
1.7
) is related to the conjugate momentum tensor
\overline{\pi}:=\left(\textup{tr}_{\overline{\textup{g}}}\overline{\textup{K}}\right)\overline{\textup{g}}-\overline{\textup{K}}
,
see
[
8
, Lemma 10]
, no similar relation is known, to the knowledge of the author, for general
q
. Thus the physical interpretation of the center of mass of the CSTMC-foliation, when existing, is not clear, at least when
q
is greater than but close to 2.

We finally recall that the Ricci flow plays an important role in the mathematics of high energy theoretical physics, see
[
5
]
. In our opinion, extrinsic curvature flows like the ones considered in this paper coupled with a Ricci flow evolution of a background metric may prove to be useful in physical applications. A further motivation for our study comes from the recent work of Huisken and Wolff, where the authors studied a spacetime version of the inverse mean curvature flow, see
[
23
]
. Finally, an area preserving spacetime mean curvature flow, in the context of the Schwarzschildean lightcones, has been studied by Kröncke and Wolff in
[
24
]
.

2.
Preliminaries and notations

2.1.
Definitions and basic properties

A
spacetime
, say
\mathcal{M}
, is a connected 4-manifold equipped with a
(0,2)
-type tensor field
g
non-degenerate and
Lorentzian
, i.e. with signature
(-1,1,1,1)
, which satisfies the so called
Einstein
(constraint)
equations

(2.1)

\textbf{{Ric}}-\left(\frac{\textbf{{S}}}{2}\right)\textbf{{g}}=\textbf{{T}},

where
\textbf{{Ric}}=\textbf{{Ric}}(\cdot,\cdot)
is the Ricci tensor of
(\mathcal{M},\textbf{{g}})
,
\textbf{{S}}=\textup{tr}_{\textbf{{g}}}\textbf{{Ric}}
and
T
is a symmetric smooth
(0,2)
-type tensor field called
energy-momentum tensor
. In the following, we will be interested in 3-dimensional submanifolds
M
of a spacetime such that the restriction of the Lorentzian metric
g
to
M
is a Riemannian metric. These submanifolds are known as
spacelike submanifolds
. We will always require
(\mathcal{M},\textbf{g})
to be
time-orientable
. In particular, we suppose that there exists a globally defined timelike smooth vector field
\bm{e}_{0}
. We also denote by
\bm{A}
the second fundamental form of the immersion
\bm{j}:\textup{M}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{M}
. We then define the metric
\overline{\textup{g}}:=\bm{j}^{*}\textbf{g}
and

(2.2)

\overline{\textup{K}}(\cdot,\cdot):=\langle\bm{A}(\cdot,\cdot),-\bm{e}_{0}\rangle,\qquad\overline{\mu}:=\textbf{T}(\bm{e}_{0},\bm{e}_{0}),\qquad\overline{\textup{J}}(\cdot):=\textbf{T}(\bm{e}_{0},\cdot),

which are, respectively, the (scalar)
spacetime second fundamental form
of
M
, the
energy density
and the
momentum density
. Through the Gauss-Mainardi-Codazzi equations, it can be proved that (
2.1
) implies the the system (
1.1
) holds. Conversely, Choquet-Bruhat
[
6
]
proved, in the vacuum and some special cases, that the validity of the system (
1.1
) implies the existence of a spacetime
(\mathcal{M},\textbf{g})
such that
(\textup{M},\overline{\textup{g}},\overline{\textup{K}})
sits into
(\mathcal{M},\textbf{g})
and (
2.2
) are satisfied. See also
[
15
]
for relations with the (strict) dominant energy condition. This remark allows us to define an initial data set as a notion independent from the one of spacetime manifold (at least formally), but which includes the whole information that a spacetime encodes.

In the following, we will always assume that
(\textup{M},\overline{\textup{g}},\overline{\textup{K}})
is an asymptotically flat initial data set in the sense of Definition
1.1
. We denote by
\overline{\Gamma}_{\alpha\beta}^{\gamma}
the Christoffel symbols of the Riemannian manifold
(\textup{M},\overline{\textup{g}})
, and by
\overline{\nabla}
the Riemannian connection. By
\overline{\textup{Rm}}
,
\overline{\textup{Ric}}
and
\overline{\textup{S}}
we denote the Riemann and Ricci curvature tensors on
(\textup{M},\overline{\textup{g}})
. We denote the Euclidean coordinate spheres on
(\textup{M},\overline{\textup{g}})
by
\mathbb{S}_{R}(\vec{z})
, for some
R>1
and
\vec{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, by an abuse of notation using that
\textup{M}\setminus\textup{C}
is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{B}}_{1}(\vec{0})
.

We say that
(\textup{M},\overline{\textup{g}})
satisfies the
mass condition
if the scalar curvature satisfies the inequality
|\overline{\textup{S}}|\leq\overline{c}|\vec{x}|^{-3-\delta}
, possibly enlarging the constant
\overline{c}
. We remark that, in the case of constrained initial data sets, the mass condition is implied by (
1.1
) together with (
1.4
). This is the only reason we need
constrained
initial data sets. We will use the following equivalent characterization of the ADM-energy introduced by Arnowitt, Deser and Misner in
[
3
]
. The equivalence of the definitions has been proved by Miao and Tam in
[
29
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(\textup{M},\overline{\textup{g}})
be a
C_{\frac{1}{2}+\delta}^{2}
-asymptotically flat 3-manifold that satisfies the mass condition. The ADM-energy is defined as

(2.3)

E_{\textup{ADM}}:=-\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{R}{8\pi}\int_{\vec{x}^{-1}(\mathbb{S}_{R}(\vec{0}))}\overline{\textup{G}}(\nu_{R},\nu_{R})\ d\mu_{R},

where
\overline{\textup{G}}:=\overline{\textup{Ric}}-\left(\frac{\overline{\textup{S}}}{2}\right)\overline{\textup{g}}
is the (spacelike) Einstein tensor,
\vec{x}^{-1}(\mathbb{S}_{R}(\vec{0}))
is the Euclidean sphere immersed in
(\textup{M},\overline{\textup{g}})
, and
\nu_{R}
and
d\mu_{R}
are, respectively, its normal vector and its volume form in
(\textup{M},\overline{\textup{g}})
.

In the following, by
surface
we mean an immersion
\iota:\Sigma\hookrightarrow\textup{M}\setminus\textup{C}
, with
\dim\Sigma=2
, which is closed, connected and 2-faced. Since
\textup{M}\setminus\textup{C}
is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{B}}_{1}(\vec{0})
, the surface
\Sigma
inherits two Riemannian metrics: a
physical
metric
g:=\iota^{*}\overline{\textup{g}}
and a
Euclidean
metric
g^{e}:=\iota^{*}\overline{\textup{g}}^{e}
, where
\overline{\textup{g}}^{e}
is the Euclidean metric on
M
. From now on, we will use the apex
e
each time a quantity is computed with respect to the Euclidean metric, and we will omit the apex if it is computed using the physical metric. Then, fixed an outer unit normal
\nu:\Sigma\to T\textup{M}
, we represent with
A=\{h_{ij}\}
,
H
and
d\mu
, respectively, the second fundamental form, the mean curvature and the volume form of
\Sigma
with respect to
g
. On the other hand, if
\nu^{e}:\Sigma\to T\textup{M}
is the outer unit normal of
\Sigma
with respect to
\overline{\textup{g}}^{e}
, we represent the same quantities with
A^{e}=\{h^{e}_{ij}\}
,
H^{e}
and
d\mu^{e}
. Observe that, when we are on a hypsersurface
\Sigma
, we use the latin indexes
i,j,k,l
, etc, to distinguish from the ambiental coordinates, which are indicated with the greek indexes
\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\epsilon
, etc. Finally, we define

h:=\frac{1}{|\Sigma|}\int_{\Sigma}H\ d\mu,\qquad h^{e}:=\frac{1}{|\Sigma^{e}|}\int_{\Sigma}H^{e}\ d\mu^{e},

which are, respectively, the mean of the mean curvature computed with respect to the physical and the Euclidean metric. Here
|\Sigma|=\int_{\Sigma}d\mu
and
|\Sigma^{e}|=\int_{\Sigma}d\mu^{e}
. The asymptotic flatness of the ambient manifold allows to deduce the following estimates, which can be proved by standard computations, see e.g.
[
28
, Lemma 1.5]
,
[
8
, Lemma 11]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\iota:\Sigma\hookrightarrow\textup{M}
be a surface immersed in a
C_{\frac{1}{2}+\delta}^{2}
-flat 3-manifold
M
. Then there exists
C>0
, only depending on the constant
\overline{c}
in Definition
1.1
, such that

(2.4)

|g-g^{e}|\leq C|\vec{x}|^{-\frac{1}{2}-\delta},\qquad|\Gamma_{ij}^{k}-\left(\Gamma^{e}\right)_{ij}^{k}|\leq C|\vec{x}|^{-\frac{3}{2}-\delta},

(2.5)

|d\mu-d\mu^{e}|\leq C|\vec{x}|^{-\frac{1}{2}-\delta}d\mu,

(2.6)

|\nu-\nu^{e}|\leq C|\vec{x}|^{-\frac{1}{2}-\delta},\qquad|\nabla\nu-\nabla^{e}\nu^{e}|\leq C|\vec{x}|^{-\frac{3}{2}-\delta},

(2.7)

|A-A^{e}|\leq C\left(|\vec{x}|^{-\frac{3}{2}-\delta}+|\vec{x}|^{-\frac{1}{2}-\delta}|A^{e}|\right),

(2.8)

|\nabla A-\nabla^{e}A^{e}|\leq C\left(|\vec{x}|^{-\frac{5}{2}-\delta}+|\vec{x}|^{-\frac{1}{2}-\delta}|\nabla^{e}A^{e}|\right).

In addition, if
|A|\leq 10|\vec{x}|^{-1}
, then

(2.9)

|H-H^{e}|\leq C|\vec{x}|^{-\frac{3}{2}-\delta},\qquad|\overset{\circ}{A}-\overset{\circ}{A^{e}}|\leq C|\vec{x}|^{-\frac{3}{2}-\delta}.

We also define the barycenter of
\Sigma
as

\vec{z}_{\Sigma}:=\int_{\Sigma}\iota\ d\mu.

Finally, in order to estimate the Euclidean position of an immersed surface and its area, we introduce the following definitions.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
(\textup{M},\overline{\textup{g}})
be a 3-manifold, and consider an immersed surface
\iota:\Sigma\to\textup{M}\setminus\textup{C}
with induced metric
g=\iota^{*}\overline{\textup{g}}
. Then we set

r_{\Sigma}:=\min_{x\in\Sigma}|\vec{x}(\iota(x))|,\qquad R_{\Sigma}:=\max_{x\in\Sigma}|\vec{x}(\iota(x))|,\qquad\sigma_{\Sigma}:=\sqrt{\frac{|\Sigma|_{g}}{4\pi}}.

These radii are called Euclidean radius, Euclidean diameter and area radius, respectively.

As in the previous literature, see
[
30
]
,
[
8
]
, we define the Sobolev norms on
\Sigma
as follows

\|f\|_{W^{0,p}(\Sigma)}:=\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Sigma)},\qquad\|f\|_{W^{k+1,p}(\Sigma)}:=\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Sigma)}+\sigma_{\Sigma}\|\nabla f\|_{W^{k,p}(\Sigma)}

for
p\in[1,\infty]
and
k\in\mathbb{N}^{\geq 0}
. As usual, we set
H^{k}=W^{2,k}
.

2.2.
Surfaces in initial data sets

If
(\textup{M},\overline{\textup{g}},\overline{\textup{K}})
is an asymptotically flat initial data set and
\Sigma\hookrightarrow\textup{M}
, the surface
\Sigma
can be seen as a 2-codimensional submanifold of the 4-dimensional spacetime manifold associated to
(\textup{M},\overline{\textup{g}},\overline{\textup{K}})
. Since in this case we have the decomposition of the tangent space of a point
p\in\mathcal{M}
given by
T_{p}\mathcal{M}=\langle\bm{e}_{0}\rangle_{p}\oplus^{\perp_{\mathcal{M}}}T_{p}\Sigma\oplus^{\perp_{\textup{M}}}N_{p}\Sigma
. This decomposition allows to define the so called
null mean curvatures
, which are given by

(2.10)

\theta^{\pm}=P\pm H,

where
P:=\textup{tr}_{g}(\overline{\textup{K}})\equiv g^{ij}\overline{\textup{K}}_{ij}
is the so called
timelike mean curvature
. At this point, we define the
spacetime mean curvature
, if it exists, as

\mathcal{H}:=\sqrt{H^{2}-P^{2}}.

It is essentially the Minkowski 2-length of the vector
(\vec{H},P)
, where
\vec{H}
is the
vector mean curvature
of
\Sigma
. In this paper, we will mainly consider the Minkowski
q
-norm
|(\vec{H},P)|_{q}=\left(H^{q}-|P|^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}
, with
q\geq 2
. Whenever there are no ambiguities, for a fixed
q\geq 2
we will continue to call this quantity
\mathcal{H}
. In all the cases, we set

\hbar:=\fint_{\Sigma}\mathcal{H}\ d\mu.

In the following preliminary Lemma, we show some properties of surfaces in initial data sets.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
(\textup{M},\overline{\textup{g}},\overline{\textup{K}})
be a
C_{\frac{1}{2}+\delta}^{2}
-asymptotically flat initial data set. Then there exist constants
C=C(\overline{c})>0
and
\textup{c}_{\textup{in}}=\textup{c}_{\textup{in}}(\overline{c})>0
, also depending on the choice of
q
, such that if
\iota:\Sigma\hookrightarrow\textup{M}\setminus\textup{C}
is a
surface
with induced metric
g:=\iota^{*}\overline{\textup{g}}
and there exists
\sigma>1
such that

(2.11)

2\sigma\geq r_{\Sigma}\geq\frac{\sigma}{2},\qquad\frac{1}{\sigma}\leq H_{x}\leq\frac{\sqrt{5}}{\sigma}\quad\forall x\in\Sigma,

then
\mathcal{H}_{x}
is well defined for every
x\in\Sigma
and the following inequalities hold:

(2.12)

\|P\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma)}+\sigma\|\nabla P\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma)}\leq C\sigma^{-\frac{3}{2}-\delta},

\sup_{\Sigma}|\mathcal{H}-H|\leq C\sigma^{-1-\frac{1}{2}q-q\delta},\qquad|h-\hbar|\leq C\sigma^{-1-\frac{1}{2}q-q\delta}.

Finally, if
H
is constant on
\Sigma
, i.e.
H\equiv h
, then
\|\mathcal{H}-\hbar\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma)}+\sigma\|\nabla\mathcal{H}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma)}\leq\textup{c}_{\textup{in}}\sigma^{-1-\frac{1}{2}q-q\delta}
.

In the following, we will mainly use the
H^{1}
-estimate on
\mathcal{H}-\hbar
, which follows from the
W^{1,\infty}
bound in the above statement, and we will continue to call
\textup{c}_{\textup{in}}
the constant at the right-hand side. Observe that this is the only case in which we use the lowercase in order to indicate a constant depending on the setting and not on the "roundness of the surface" (in a sense we will make more clear later, see Definition
2.5
below).

Proof.

Equation (
2.12
) follows from the fact that
|P_{x}|\leq 2|\overline{\textup{K}}|_{\overline{\textup{g}}}\leq 2\overline{c}r_{\Sigma}^{-\frac{3}{2}-\delta}=\textup{O}(\sigma^{-\frac{3}{2}-\delta})
,

(\nabla P)_{i}=g^{ik}g^{jl}\nabla_{k}\overline{\textup{K}}_{jl},

and the fact that
|\nabla_{k}\overline{\textup{K}}_{jl}|\leq\overline{c}\sigma^{-\frac{5}{2}-\delta}
. The other inequalities follow from the Lagrange mean value theorem and the identity

(2.13)

\mathcal{H}^{q-1}\nabla\mathcal{H}=H^{q-1}\nabla H-|P|^{q-1}\left(\frac{P}{|P|}\right)\nabla P.

∎

2.3.
Round surfaces

We now introduce a class of surfaces, which are close to a Euclidean sphere of the same area radius in a precise (quantitative) way. The aim is to find a class which
is invariant under the volume preserving spacetime mean curvature flow under an appropriate choice of the
parameters and for large enough radius. Our class of round surfaces coincide with the one introduced in
[
32
]
. Other classes of round surfaces, which are related to
the methods used there, have been introduced in
[
22
]
,
[
28
]
,
[
30
]
,
[
8
]
.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
(\textup{M},\overline{\textup{g}},\overline{\textup{K}})
be a
C_{\frac{1}{2}+\delta}^{2}
-asymptotically flat initial data set and let
\iota:\Sigma\to M
be a surface.

(i) For a given approximate radius
\sigma>1
and parameters
\eta>0
,
B_{1}
,
B_{2}>0
, we say that
(\Sigma,g)
is a round surface in
(\textup{M},\overline{\textup{g}},\overline{\textup{K}})
, and we write quantitatively
\Sigma\in\mathcal{W}_{\sigma}^{\eta}(B_{1},B_{2})
if the following inequalities are satisfied

(2.14)

|A|<\sqrt{\frac{5}{2\sigma^{2}}},\qquad\kappa_{i}\geq\frac{1}{2\sigma},

(2.15)

\frac{7}{2}\pi\sigma^{2}<|\Sigma|_{g}<5\pi\sigma^{2},\qquad\frac{3}{4}\leq\frac{r_{\Sigma}}{\sigma}\leq\frac{R_{\Sigma}}{\sigma}\leq\frac{5}{4},

(2.16)

\|\overset{\circ}{A}\|_{L^{4}(\Sigma,\mu)}<B_{1}\sigma^{-1-\delta},

(2.17)

\eta\sigma^{-4}\|\mathcal{H}-\hbar\|_{L^{4}(\Sigma)}^{4}+\|\nabla\mathcal{H}\|_{L^{4}(\Sigma)}^{4}<B_{2}\sigma^{-8-4\delta}.

(ii) For given
\sigma>1
and
\eta
,
B_{1}
,
B_{2}
,
B_{\textup{cen}}>0
, we say that
(\Sigma,g)
is a well-centered round surface, and we write
\Sigma\in{\mathcal{B}}_{\sigma}^{\eta}(B_{1},B_{2},B_{\textup{cen}})
if it satisfies the above properties and in addition

(2.18)

|\vec{z}_{\Sigma}|<B_{\textup{cen}}\sigma^{1-\delta}

The decay rates in conditions (
2.16
)-(
2.17
) are modelled on the properties of the leaves of the CMC-foliation constructed by Nerz in
[
30
]
. See also the construction proposed in
[
32
]
. In particular, in his Theorem 5.1, Nerz proved the existence of an exhaustive family of constant mean curvature surfaces which foliate an asymptotically flat manifold with non-zero ADM-energy. We denote by
\{\Sigma^{s}\}_{s\geq s_{0}}
, for a certain
s_{0}>1
, the CMC-foliation constructed by Nerz in
[
30
]
. We avoid to use
\sigma
as a parameter of this foliation as Nerz does since in the present paper it plays a different role, as we can see in Definition
2.5
. Nerz proved that this foliation satisfies

(2.19)

H^{\Sigma^{s}}=\frac{2}{s},\qquad\|\overset{\circ}{A}\|_{H^{1}(\Sigma^{s})}\leq C_{\textup{Nerz}}s^{-\frac{3}{2}-\delta},\qquad|\vec{z}_{\Sigma^{s}}|\leq C_{\textup{Nerz}}s^{1-\delta},

for some
C_{\textup{Nerz}}>0
. Moreover,
[
30
, Prop. 4.4]
proves that
|s-\sigma_{s}|\leq C\sigma_{s}^{\frac{1}{2}-\delta}
, where
\sigma_{s}:=\sigma_{\Sigma^{s}}
. Note also that
[
30
, Prop. 4.4]
, combined with (
2.19
), implies

(2.20)

\sigma_{\Sigma^{s}}-C\sigma_{\Sigma^{s}}^{1-\delta}\leq|\vec{x}|=|\vec{z}^{s}+\sigma_{\Sigma^{s}}\nu^{s}+f^{s}\nu^{s}|\leq\sigma_{\Sigma^{s}}+C\sigma_{\Sigma^{s}}^{1-\delta},

that is
|r_{\Sigma^{s}}-\sigma_{\Sigma^{s}}|\leq C\sigma_{\Sigma^{s}}^{1-\delta}
. Then

(2.21)

\frac{r_{\Sigma^{s}}}{\sigma_{s}}\geq 1-C\sigma_{s}^{-\delta}.

Thus, for
s
large, this foliation satisfies (
1.6
) with
\sigma=\sigma_{s}
. Finally observe that, by Lemma
2.4
, the leaves
\Sigma^{s}
also satisfy

\|\mathcal{H}-\hbar\|_{W^{1,2}(\Sigma^{s})}\leq\textup{c}_{\textup{in}}\sigma^{-\frac{q}{2}-q\delta},

for some
\textup{c}_{\textup{in}}=\textup{c}_{\textup{in}}(\overline{c})>0
. For this reason, we will use a fixed leaf of Nerz’s foliation as the initial datum of our flow.

Notation for the constants.
Throughout the paper, when deriving estimates on geometric quantities on a surface
\Sigma
, we denote by
C,C_{1},C_{2},...
constants which only depend on properties of the ambient manifold, such as
\overline{c},\delta
in Definition
1.1
or the energy
E_{\textup{ADM}}
and by
c,c_{1},c_{2},\tilde{c}
,… constants which in addition depend on the constants
B_{1},B_{2},B_{\textup{cen}}
in the previous conditions. We say that a constant is universal if it is independent on any other parameter of our problem. As
usual, the letters
c
or
C
will often denote constants which may change from one line to the other, but each time depending on the same parameters.

Remark 2.6
.

Property (
2.15
) implies that the three radii of Definition
2.3
are comparable among each other and with
\sigma
.
In particular this property implies, because of the asymptotic flatness of
M
as in (
1.2
), the bound on the Riemann tensor

(2.22)

|\overline{\textup{Rm}}|\leq C(\bar{c})\sigma^{-\frac{5}{2}-\delta}\mbox{ on }\Sigma.

In the next Lemma, we collect some well-known properties of round surfaces. See
[
32
, Remark 2.6]
and
[
32
, Lemma 2.7]
for more details and a proof.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
(\textup{M},\overline{\textup{g}})
be a
C_{\frac{1}{2}+\delta}^{2}
-asymptotically flat manifold. Fix any
\eta
,
B_{1}
and
B_{2}>0
. Then there exists
\sigma_{0}=\sigma_{0}(B_{1},B_{2},\eta,\overline{c},\delta)>0
such that any surface which belongs to
\mathcal{W}_{\sigma}^{\eta}(B_{1},B_{2})
for some
\sigma>\sigma_{0}
satisfies the following properties:

(i)

There exists
\textup{c}_{\textup{s}}>0
such that it holds

(2.23)

\|\psi\|_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{\textup{c}_{\textup{s}}}{\sigma}\|\psi\|_{W^{1,1}(\Sigma)},\qquad\forall\ \psi\in W^{1,1}(\Sigma)

and, for every
p>2
,

(2.24)

\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 2^{\frac{2(p-1)}{p-2}}\textup{c}_{\textup{s}}\sigma^{-\frac{2}{p}}\|\psi\|_{W^{1,p}},\qquad\forall\psi\in W^{1,p}(\Sigma)

Moreover, there exists a constant
c(B_{2},\eta)>0
such that

(2.25)

\|H-h\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq c(B_{2},\eta)\sigma^{-\frac{3}{2}-\delta}.

(ii)

It holds the estimate

(2.26)

\left|h-\frac{2}{\sigma}_{\Sigma}\right|\leq c(B_{1},B_{2},\overline{c})\sigma^{-\frac{3}{2}-\delta}.

(iii)

There exists a constant
B_{\infty}=B_{\infty}(B_{1},B_{2},\eta,\delta,\overline{c})
such that
\|\overset{\circ}{A}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma)}\leq B_{\infty}\sigma^{-\frac{3}{2}-\delta}
.

(iv)

There exists
c=c(\delta,\overline{c},B_{1},B_{2},\eta)
,
c_{0}=c(B_{1},\overline{c},\delta)
,
\vec{z}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and
f:\mathbb{S}_{\sigma_{\Sigma}}(\vec{z}_{0})\to\mathbb{R}
such that

(2.27)

\Sigma^{e}=\textup{graph}(f),\qquad\|f\|_{W^{2,\infty}}\leq c\sigma^{\frac{1}{2}-\delta},\qquad|\vec{z}_{0}-\vec{z}_{\Sigma}|\leq c_{0}\sigma^{\frac{1}{2}-\delta}.

(v)

It holds
\|A\|_{L^{4}(\Sigma)}\leq 4^{\frac{1}{4}}\sqrt{5/2}\pi^{\frac{1}{4}}\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}
and thus there exists a constant
\textup{c}_{\textup{Per}}=\textup{c}_{\textup{Per}}(\delta,\overline{c})
such that

\|H-h\|_{L^{4}(\Sigma)}\leq\textup{c}_{\textup{Per}}\|\overset{\circ}{A}\|_{L^{4}(\Sigma)}+\textup{c}_{\textup{Per}}\sigma^{-1-\delta}.

Remark 2.8
.

Most of the results in the previous Lemma follows from the fundamental result of DeLellis-Müller
[
11
]
. We also remark that, by Theorem 1.1 in
[
11
]
, the assumptions in (
1.6
) imply that
\frac{\sigma}{r_{\Sigma}}
is also bounded away from zero. In fact, combining (
2.7
) with the latter assumption in (

), and using the DeLellis-Müller’s Theorem, Point (iv) of Lemma

, combined with the second assumption in (

\frac{\sigma}{r_{\Sigma}}\geq 1-C_{0}^{-1}

3.
Spectral theory

3.1.
Mass and stability operator

In this section we consider closed surfaces
\Sigma
belonging to a roundness
class
{\mathcal{W}}_{\sigma}^{\eta}(B_{1},B_{2})
for fixed parameters
\eta
,
B_{1}
,
B_{2}
and a general large
\sigma
. Moreover, we will suppose that
E_{\textup{ADM}}>0
and that
\Sigma
satisfies

(3.1)

\|H-h\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}\leq\textup{c}_{\textup{in}}\sigma^{-1-2\delta}.

We sometimes say that a surface
\Sigma
satisfying (
3.1
) is
(\textup{c}_{\textup{in}},\sigma)
-
almost CMC
. We simply say that
\Sigma
is
\textup{c}_{\textup{in}}
-
almost CMC
if
\Sigma
is
(\textup{c}_{\textup{in}},\sigma_{\Sigma})
-almost CMC. Thus, we will tacitly mean that the constants
c
and
\sigma_{0}
which appear in the statements below only depend on
\eta,B_{1},B_{2}
, on the constants
\textup{c}_{\textup{in}},\overline{c},\delta
and possibly on the energy
E_{\textup{ADM}}
.

Since our aim is mostly to investigate the extrinsic geometry of surfaces, we recall a (quasi-) local notion of mass, which goes back to
[
27
]
.

Definition 3.1

(Hawking energy)
.

Let
(\textup{M},\overline{\textup{g}})
be a 3-dimensional manifold, and
\iota:\Sigma\hookrightarrow\textup{M}
be a surface. Let
g:=\iota^{*}\overline{\textup{g}}
be the induced metric. The Hawking energy of
\Sigma
is defined as

(3.2)

m_{H}(\Sigma):=\sqrt{\frac{|\Sigma|_{g}}{16\pi}}\left(1-\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{\Sigma}H^{2}\ d\mu\right).

Remark 3.2
.

The original definition the Hawking energy involves the spacetime mean curvature
\mathcal{H}
instead of the (spacelike) mean curvature
H
. Precisely, the definition in (
3.2
) takes the name of Geroch mass. However, it is well-known that the two definition are asymptotic for large round surfaces (see
[
8
]
) and thus we continue to use the (local) notion of mass in (
3.2
).

If
\Sigma\in\mathcal{W}_{\sigma}(B_{1},B_{2})
, the Gauss-Bonnet theorem shows that the Hawking mass is asymptotic to the ADM-energy when considering round surfaces of large radius, proving in particular the following Lemma. See
[
30
, Appendix A]
for a proof.

Lemma 3.3
.

There exist
\tilde{c}
and
\sigma_{0}
such that, for every
\Sigma\in\mathcal{W}_{\sigma}^{\eta}(B_{1},B_{2})
with
\sigma\geq\sigma_{0}
we have

(3.3)

\left|m_{H}(\Sigma)+\frac{\sigma_{\Sigma}}{8\pi}\int_{\Sigma}\overline{\textup{G}}(\nu,\nu)\ d\mu\right|\leq\tilde{c}\sigma^{-\delta},\qquad\left|E_{\textup{ADM}}+\frac{\sigma_{\Sigma}}{8\pi}\int_{\Sigma}\overline{\textup{G}}(\nu,\nu)\ d\mu\right|\leq\tilde{c}\sigma^{-\delta}.

We now introduce the stability operator, which occurs as the second variation of the area
functional.

Definition 3.4
.

Given a surface
\iota:\Sigma\hookrightarrow\textup{M}
and a smooth function
\textup{f}\in H^{2}(\Sigma)
, we define the stability operator associated to
\Sigma
,
L^{\Sigma}:H^{2}(\Sigma)\to L^{2}(\Sigma)
, as

(3.4)

L^{\Sigma}\textup{f}:=-\Delta\textup{f}-(|A|^{2}+\overline{\textup{Ric}}(\nu,\nu))\textup{f}.

We simply write
L
instead of
L^{\Sigma}
whenever the role of the surface
\Sigma
is not ambiguous. In
[
30
]
and
[
8
]
the spectral properties of
L
are studied in the case of CMC-surfaces. In particular, they show that
L
is invertible if
E_{\textup{ADM}}\neq 0
and positive definite on functions with zero mean if
E_{\textup{ADM}}>0
. Here we generalize this analysis to round surfaces where we only assume that
H
has a small oscillations. See
[
32
, Section 3]
for the general case of round surfaces. In the context of the initial data sets, Cederbaum-Sakovich
[
8
]
proved results analogous to those of Nerz, modifying the operator
L^{\Sigma}
with additional terms. Even if we work in initial data sets, we continue to use the definition of stability operator given in (
3.4
). The reason is that, as we will see in the next Sections, we will obtain the stability operator in computing the evolution of some geometric quantities. In estimating the terms involved in these computations, the main addend will be the operator in (
3.4
), while the remainder will be small with a precise order of decay (for round surfaces with large radius).

Since the "differential part" of the stability operator is totally given by the Laplace-Beltrami operator, in order to understand the properties of
L
we are interested in we have to briefly review the spectral theory for the operator
-\Delta
. The following Lemma is taken from
[
8
, Lemma 2]
. Observe that, together with adapting the notations of the Lemma with our definition of roundness class, we remove the hypothesis of having a CMC-surface. In fact, reading the proof of
[
8
, Lemma 2]
with attention, one can observe that the CMC-hypothesis is just needed in order to compare the area radius with the
curvature radius
used in
[
30
]
and
[
8
]
.

Remark 3.5
.

At the light of Lemma
2.7
, i.e. of the De Lellis-Müller theorem
[
11
, Thm. 1.1]
, scalar functions on a round surface
\Sigma
can be also meant as functions on the approximating sphere
\mathbb{S}_{\sigma_{\Sigma}}
. With an abuse of notation, we identify such kind of functions.

We first recall some properties of the Laplace-Beltrami operator on a round sphere
\mathbb{S}_{\sigma}(\vec{0})\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
with the Euclidean metric.
On a general closed surface, the eigenvalues of the Laplace operator are all positive, except the first one which is zero, with eigenspace given by the constant functions. For the Euclidean sphere, the first nonzero eigenvalue has multiplicity three and is given by

\lambda^{e}_{\alpha}=\frac{2}{\sigma^{2}},\ \alpha=1,2,3.

An orthonormal basis for the eigenspace is given by the normalized coordinate functions

f_{\alpha}^{e}(\vec{x})=\sqrt{\frac{3}{4\pi\sigma_{\Sigma}^{4}}}\vec{x}_{\alpha},\ \alpha=1,2,3,

restricted on
\mathbb{S}_{\sigma}(\vec{0})
. The remaining eigenvalues satisfy the bound

\lambda^{e}_{i}\geq\lambda^{e}_{4}=\frac{6}{\sigma^{2}},\ \forall\,i\geq 4.

We recall the statement of Lemma 2 of
[
8
]
, which measures how much the first eigenvalues and the corresponding eigenfunctions of the Laplace-Beltrami operator on a round surface in the physical metric differ from the ones of the approximating sphere in the Euclidean metric.

Lemma 3.6
.

There exist
c>0
and
\sigma_{0}>1
such that, if
\Sigma\in{\mathcal{W}}_{\sigma}^{\eta}(B_{1},B_{2})
with
\sigma\geq\sigma_{0}
, there is a complete orthonormal system in
L^{2}(\Sigma)
consisting of the eigenfunctions
\{f_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha=0}^{\infty}
such that

-\Delta f_{\alpha}=\lambda_{\alpha}f_{\alpha},\qquad\text{with $0=\lambda_{0}<\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq...$}

Set
\mathbb{S}{\sigma_{\Sigma}}
to be the round sphere approximating
\Sigma
in the sense of
Lemma
2.7
. Then there exists an orthonormal triple
\{f_{1}^{e},f_{2}^{e},f_{3}^{e}\}
of eigenfunctions of
-\Delta^{\mathbb{S}_{\sigma_{\Sigma}}}
such that, for
\alpha=1,2,3
,

\left|\lambda_{\alpha}-\frac{2}{\sigma_{\Sigma}^{2}}\right|\leq c\sigma^{-\frac{5}{2}-\delta},\quad\|f_{\alpha}-f_{\alpha}^{e}\|_{W^{2,2}(\Sigma)}\leq c\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}-\delta},\quad\left\|\overset{\circ}{\textup{Hess}}(f_{\alpha})\right\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}\leq c\sigma^{-\frac{5}{2}-\delta},

Moreover

(3.5)

\int_{\Sigma}\left|\langle\nabla f_{\alpha},\nabla f_{\beta}\rangle-\frac{3\delta_{\alpha\beta}}{\sigma_{\Sigma}^{2}|\Sigma|_{g}}+\frac{f_{\alpha}f_{\beta}}{\sigma_{\Sigma}^{2}}\right|\ d\mu_{g}\leq c\sigma^{-\frac{5}{2}-\delta}.

On the other hand, for
\alpha>3
we have

(3.6)

\lambda_{\alpha}>\frac{5}{\sigma_{\Sigma}^{2}}.

Remark 3.7
.

As a byproduct of the proof of
[
8
, Lemma 2]
, it is important to keep in mind that such an orthonormal system also satisfies the inequality
\|f_{i}\|_{H^{2}(\Sigma)}\leq c
, for
i\in\{1,2,3\}
.

This description of the spectrum of the Laplace-Beltrami operator allows us to define a decomposition of
L^{2}(\Sigma)
in terms of the eigenfunctions of
-\Delta
.

Definition 3.8
.

Let
\Sigma
be a surface and consider the Hilbert space
L^{2}(\Sigma)
equipped with the standard scalar product in
L^{2}(\Sigma)
. Consider the orthonormal system constructed in Lemma
3.6
. Then, for every
\textup{w}\in L^{2}(\Sigma)
we define

\textup{w}^{0}:=\langle w,f_{0}\rangle_{2}f_{0}=\fint_{\Sigma}\textup{w}\ d\mu_{g},\qquad\textup{w}^{t}:=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{3}\langle\textup{w},f_{\alpha}\rangle_{2}f_{\alpha}.

We call
\textup{w}^{0}
the mean part of
w
, and
\textup{w}^{t}
the translational part of
w
. Finally, we set

\textup{w}^{d}:=\textup{w}-\textup{w}^{t},

the so called difference part, which obviously also contains the information about
\textup{w}^{0}
. Since
\textup{w}^{t}
and
\textup{w}^{d}
are spanned by distinct eigenvalues of
-\Delta
, which are orthonormal, it follows that
\langle\textup{w}^{t},\textup{w}^{d}\rangle_{2}=0
. When
\textup{w}^{0}=0
, we will often call
\textup{w}^{d}
also deformational part.

Remark 3.9
.

(i)

First of all, since
\Sigma
is compact,
f_{0}\equiv|\Sigma|^{-\frac{1}{2}}
. It is then clear that if
w
has zero mean, then
\textup{w}^{0}=0
.

(ii)

The so called traslational part of a function
w
is the projection of
w
on the finite-dimensional space
\textup{span}\{f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}\}
, the eigenfunctions of
-\Delta
obtained as a perturbation of the coordinate functions on the sphere.

Lemma 3.10
.

There exist
c>0
and
\sigma_{0}>1
such that, if
\Sigma\in\overline{\mathcal{W}}_{\sigma}^{\eta}(B_{1},B_{2})
and it also is
(\textup{c}_{\textup{in}},\sigma)
-almost CMC with
\sigma\geq\sigma_{0}
, the complete orthonormal system in
L^{2}(\Sigma)
given by
Lemma
3.6
is such that

(3.7)

\left|\lambda_{\alpha}-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2}-\frac{6m_{\mathcal{H}}(\Sigma)}{\sigma_{\Sigma}^{3}}-\int_{\Sigma}\overline{\textup{Ric}}(\nu,\nu)f_{\alpha}^{2}\ d\mu_{g}\right|\leq c\sigma^{-3-\delta},\qquad\alpha\in\{1,2,3\},

and the corresponding eigenfunctions
f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}
satisfy

(3.8)

\left|\int_{\Sigma}\overline{\textup{Ric}}(\nu,\nu)f_{\alpha}f_{\beta}\right|\leq c\sigma^{-3-\delta},\qquad\alpha\neq\beta,\quad\alpha,\beta\in\{1,2,3\}.

Proof.

The proof is analogous to the one of
[
8
, Lemma 3]
. We remark that, using that
|\overset{\circ}{A}|=\textup{O}(\sigma^{-\frac{3}{2}-\delta})
, we can write the Gauss equation as

(3.9)

\displaystyle\textup{S}^{\Sigma}

\displaystyle=\overline{\textup{S}}-2\overline{\textup{Ric}}(\nu,\nu)-|\overset{\circ}{A}|^{2}+\frac{H^{2}}{2}

\displaystyle=\overline{\textup{S}}-2\overline{\textup{Ric}}(\nu,\nu)+\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2}+\frac{(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)^{2}}{2}+\hbar(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)+\textup{O}(\sigma^{-3-\delta}).

since, using Lemma
2.4
and the roundness, we find
H-\mathcal{H}=\textup{O}(\sigma^{-2-2\delta})
and
\mathcal{H}=\textup{O}(\sigma^{-1})
. We thus set
\mathcal{R}:=\frac{(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)^{2}}{2}+\hbar(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)+\textup{O}(\sigma^{-3-\delta})
and, analogously to the proof of
[
32
, Prop. 3.6]
, we get

(3.10)

\displaystyle\left|\lambda_{\alpha}^{2}\delta_{\alpha\beta}-\int_{\Sigma}\textup{S}^{\Sigma}\langle\nabla f_{\alpha},\nabla f_{\beta}\rangle\ d\mu_{g}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\lambda_{\alpha}^{2}\delta_{\alpha\beta}-\int_{\Sigma}\left(\left(\overline{\textup{S}}-2\overline{\textup{Ric}}(\nu,\nu)\right)+\left(\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2}+\mathcal{R}\right)\right)\langle\nabla f_{\alpha},\nabla f_{\beta}\rangle\ d\mu_{g}\right|.

Since the spacelike case corresponds to
\mathcal{R}\equiv 0
, in the spacetime case we just have to estimate

(3.11)

\left|\int_{\Sigma}\mathcal{R}\langle\nabla f_{\alpha},\nabla f_{\beta}\rangle\ d\mu\right|=\left|\int_{\Sigma}\left(\frac{(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)^{2}}{2}+\hbar(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)+\textup{O}(\sigma^{-3-\delta})\right)\langle\nabla f_{\alpha},\nabla f_{\beta}\rangle\ d\mu\right|.

Remember, comparing this proof with the one of
[
32
, Prop. 3.6]
, that the aim is to show that this remainder is of order
\textup{O}(\sigma^{-5-\delta})
. Since
\|f_{\alpha}\|_{H^{2}(\Sigma)}=\textup{O}(1)
, and so
\|\nabla f_{\alpha}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}\leq\frac{C}{\sigma}
, notice that
\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Sigma}\langle\nabla f_{\alpha},\nabla f_{\beta}\rangle\ d\mu\right|\leq C\sigma^{-2}
, which bounds the latter term in (
3.11
). The other two terms can be bounded, using Young’s inequality, equation (
3.1
) and
\mathcal{H}-H=\textup{O}(\sigma^{-2-2\delta})
, by

(3.12)

C\sigma^{-1}\|\mathcal{H}-\hbar\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla f_{\alpha}\|_{L^{4}}\|\nabla f_{\beta}\|_{L^{4}}\leq C\sigma^{-5-2\delta}

using that
\|f_{\alpha}\|_{W^{1,4}}\leq C\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|f_{\alpha}\|_{H^{2}}
, and so
\|\nabla f_{i}\|_{4}\leq C\sigma^{-\frac{3}{2}}
. Thus we obtain

(3.13)

\left|\left(\lambda_{\alpha}^{2}-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2}\lambda_{\alpha}\right)\delta_{\alpha\beta}-\int_{\Sigma}\left(\overline{\textup{S}}-2\overline{\textup{Ric}}(\nu,\nu)\right)\left(\frac{3\delta_{\alpha\beta}}{\sigma^{2}|\Sigma|_{g}}-\frac{f_{\alpha}f_{\beta}}{\sigma^{2}}\right)\ d\mu_{g}\right|\leq C\sigma^{-5-\delta},

which is analogous to inequality obtained in
[
32
, Prop. 3.6]
.
∎

Remark 3.11
.

Observe that equation (
3.7
) gives the following bound. Since
\delta\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]

4.
Volume preserving spacetime mean curvature flow

4.1.
Definition of the flow and evolution equations

Definition 4.1
.

Let
(\textup{M},\overline{\textup{g}},\overline{\textup{K}})
be an initial data set and let
\iota:\Sigma\hookrightarrow\textup{M}
be a closed surface. A time dependent family of immersions
F_{t}:\Sigma\hookrightarrow\textup{M}
, with
t\in[0,T)
for some
0<T\leq\infty
, which satisfies

(4.1)

\begin{cases}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}F_{t}(\cdot)=-\left(\mathcal{H}(\cdot,t)-\hbar(t)\right)\nu(\cdot,t)\\
F_{0}=\iota\end{cases}

is called a solution to the
volume preserving spacetime mean curvature flow
, with initial value
\iota
.

We highlight that the function
\mathcal{H}
is an increasing function of the mean curvature
H
. The function
P=g^{ij}\overline{\textup{K}}_{ij}
in
\mathcal{H}=\sqrt[q]{H^{q}-|P|^{q}}
depends on the metric induced on
\Sigma_{t}
, which only involves first order derivatives of the immersion. Thus, without the volume preserving term, the equation is parabolic. However, this term only depends on time, and thus it does not affect the parabolicity and local existence of solutions and uniqueness are ensured.

In the following, we will assume that the ambient initial data set is
C_{\frac{1}{2}+\delta}^{2}
-asymptotically flat. We write
\Sigma_{t}:=F_{t}(\Sigma)
to denote the solution of the flow at time
t
and we call
g(t)
the induced metric and denote by
d\mu_{t}
the induced 2-dimensional measure.

We recall the evolution equations satisfied by the main geometric quantities on
\Sigma_{t}
. At each fixed
t
, we choose a frame
\{\vec{e}_{\alpha}(t)\}_{\alpha=1}^{3}
on
(\textup{M},\overline{\textup{g}})
such that
\{\vec{e}_{1}(t),\vec{e}_{2}(t)\}
are tangent vectors on
\Sigma_{t}
and
\vec{e}_{3}(t):=\nu_{t}
. The following Lemma collects the equations satisfied by the main geometric quantities on

, see

]

\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\int_{\Sigma}|\overset{\circ}{A}|^{4}\ d\mu_{t}\leq-2\int_{\Sigma}|\overset{\circ}{A}|^{2}|\nabla\overset{\circ}{A}|^{2}\ d\mu_{t}-\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}}\int_{\Sigma}|\overset{\circ}{A}|^{4}\ d\mu_{t}+C\sigma^{-6-4\delta}.

As a consequence, if
\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}|\overset{\circ}{A}|^{4}\ d\mu_{0}<B_{1}\sigma^{-4-4\delta}
and
B_{1}>2C
, then
\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}|\overset{\circ}{A}|^{4}\ d\mu_{t}<B_{1}\sigma^{-4-4\delta}
for every
t\in[0,T]
.

Proof.

The proof consists in studying the evolution of the quantity
\|\overset{\circ}{A}\|_{L^{4}(\Sigma_{t})}^{4}
. Notice that, using Lemma
4.4
and comparing the evolution (
4.14
) with the one in
[
32
, Prop. 3.6]
, we only have to study the "new" term

\int_{\Sigma}\langle\mathcal{T},\overset{\circ}{A}\rangle|\overset{\circ}{A}|^{2}\ d\mu.

Once this term have been estimated, we can conclude the proof following the one of
[
32
, Prop. 3.6]
combined with Lemma
2.4
in order to deal with the difference between
H
and
\mathcal{H}
. Thus, it only remains to prove that there exists
C
such that, for every fixed
\varepsilon>0
and
\sigma_{0}
suitably large it holds

(4.27)

\left|\int_{\Sigma}\langle\mathcal{T},\overset{\circ}{A}\rangle|\overset{\circ}{A}|^{2}\ d\mu\right|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{\sigma^{2}}\int_{\Sigma}|\overset{\circ}{A}|^{4}\ d\mu+\varepsilon\int_{\Sigma}|\overset{\circ}{A}|^{2}|\nabla\overset{\circ}{A}|^{2}\ d\mu+C\sigma^{-6-4\delta}.

Multiplying equation (
4.16
) by
\overset{\circ}{h}_{ij}|\overset{\circ}{A}|^{2}
and integrating we get

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}T_{ij}\overset{\circ}{h}_{ij}|\overset{\circ}{A}|^{2}\ d\mu

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma}\Big\{\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}H\beta\left(\frac{|P|}{H}\right)+\nabla_{j}H\beta^{\prime}\left(\frac{|P|}{H}\right)\nabla_{i}\left(\frac{|P|}{H}\right)

\displaystyle+\nabla_{i}H\beta^{\prime}\left(\frac{|P|}{H}\right)\nabla_{j}\left(\frac{|P|}{H}\right)+H\beta^{\prime\prime}\left(\frac{|P|}{H}\right)\nabla_{i}\left(\frac{|P|}{H}\right)\nabla_{j}\left(\frac{|P|}{H}\right)

\displaystyle+H\beta^{\prime}\left(\frac{|P|}{H}\right)\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\left(\frac{|P|}{H}\right)\Big\}\overset{\circ}{h}_{ij}|\overset{\circ}{A}|^{2}\ d\mu.

Integration by parts, Lemma
2.4
and
\nabla_{i}\left(\overset{\circ}{h_{ij}}|\overset{\circ}{A}|^{2}\right)=\nabla_{i}\overset{\circ}{h_{ij}}|\overset{\circ}{A}|^{2}+2\overset{\circ}{h_{ij}}|\overset{\circ}{A}|\nabla_{i}|\overset{\circ}{A}|
imply

(4.28)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Sigma}(\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}H)\beta\left(\frac{P}{H}\right)\overset{\circ}{h_{ij}}|\overset{\circ}{A}|^{2}\ d\mu\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}-\delta}\int_{\Sigma}\nabla_{j}H\left|\frac{(\nabla_{i}P)H-P\nabla_{i}H}{H^{2}}\right||\overset{\circ}{A}|^{3}\ d\mu

\displaystyle+C\sigma^{-1-2\delta}\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla H||\overset{\circ}{A}|^{2}|\nabla\overset{\circ}{A}|\ d\mu.

Using again Lemma
2.4
, the parametric Young’s inequality,
|\nabla H|^{2}\leq C|\nabla\overset{\circ}{A}|^{2}+C|\overline{\textup{Ric}}|^{2}
(see
[
19
]
) and (
1.2
) we get

(4.29)

\left|\int_{\Sigma}(\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}H)\beta\left(\frac{P}{H}\right)\overset{\circ}{h_{ij}}|\overset{\circ}{A}|^{2}\ d\mu\right|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{\sigma^{2}}\int_{\Sigma}|\overset{\circ}{A}|^{4}\ d\mu+\varepsilon\int_{\Sigma}|\overset{\circ}{A}|^{2}|\nabla\overset{\circ}{A}|^{2}\ d\mu+C\sigma^{-6-4\delta}.

Moreover, we estimate

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Sigma}(\nabla_{i}H)\beta^{\prime}\left(\frac{P}{H}\right)\nabla_{j}\left(\frac{P}{H}\right)\overset{\circ}{h}_{ij}|\overset{\circ}{A}|^{2}\ d\mu\right|\leq

\displaystyle C\sigma^{-2-2\delta}\int_{\Sigma}\left(|\nabla H||\overset{\circ}{A}|\right)|\overset{\circ}{A}|^{2}\ d\mu

\displaystyle+C\sigma^{-2\delta}\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla H|^{2}|\overset{\circ}{A}|^{3}\ d\mu.

The second addend can be estimated as in (
4.29
), while the first addend, using Young’s inequality, is bounded by

(4.30)

C\sigma^{-2-2\delta}\int_{\Sigma}\left(\frac{|\nabla H|^{2}}{2}+\frac{|\overset{\circ}{A}|^{2}}{2}\right)|\overset{\circ}{A}|^{2}\ d\mu.

Again, the first addend of (
4.30
) can be treated as in (
4.29
), for
\sigma
large.

We can also bound the term

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Sigma}H\beta^{\prime\prime}\left(\frac{P}{H}\right)\nabla_{i}\left(\frac{P}{H}\right)\nabla_{j}\left(\frac{P}{H}\right)\overset{\circ}{h_{ij}}|\overset{\circ}{A}|^{2}\ d\mu\right|\leq

\displaystyle C\sigma^{-2\delta}\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla H|^{2}|\overset{\circ}{A}|^{2}|\overset{\circ}{A}|\ d\mu

\displaystyle+C\sigma^{-4-2\delta}\int_{\Sigma}|\overset{\circ}{A}|^{3}\ d\mu

\displaystyle+2\int_{\Sigma}\frac{|P||\nabla H||\nabla P|}{H^{2}}|\overset{\circ}{A}|^{3}\ d\mu.

We conclude as in (
4.29
), also using
|\overset{\circ}{A}|\leq C\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}
, for
\sigma
large and Young’s inequality.

Finally, integrating by parts and using the decay of
\beta
we get

(4.31)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Sigma}H\,\beta^{\prime}\left(\frac{P}{H}\right)\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\left(\frac{P}{H}\right)\overset{\circ}{h_{ij}}|\overset{\circ}{A}|^{2}\,d\mu\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}-\delta}\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla H|\,\left|\frac{(\nabla P)H-(\nabla H)P}{H^{2}}\right||\overset{\circ}{A}|^{3}\,d\mu

\displaystyle+C\int_{\Sigma}H\,\left|\frac{(\nabla P)H-(\nabla H)P}{H^{2}}\right|^{2}|\overset{\circ}{A}|^{3}\,d\mu

\displaystyle+C\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}-\delta}\int_{\Sigma}H\,\left|\frac{(\nabla P)H-(\nabla H)P}{H^{2}}\right||\nabla\overset{\circ}{A}|\,|\overset{\circ}{A}|^{2}\,d\mu.

The first addend can be dealt with as (
4.29
), while the second as in (
4.30
). The third addend in (
4.31
) is bounded by

C\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}-\delta}\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla P|\left|\nabla|\overset{\circ}{A}|\right||\overset{\circ}{A}|^{2}\ d\mu+C\sigma^{-1-2\delta}\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla H|\left|\nabla|\overset{\circ}{A}|\right||\overset{\circ}{A}|^{2}\ d\mu,

and we conclude by Young’s inequality, combined with the estimate
|\nabla H|^{2}\leq C|\nabla\overset{\circ}{A}|^{2}+C|\overline{\textup{Ric}}|^{2}
.
∎

We next estimate the rate of change of the volume preserving term
\hbar(t)
and of the
L^{4}
norm of
\mathcal{H}-\hbar
. In particular the following Lemma employs some techniques learned in
[
26
]
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
(\Sigma,F_{t})
be a solution to the volume preserving spacetime mean curvature flow for
t\in[0,T]
, satisfying properties (
2.14
), (
2.15
), (
4.23
) and (
4.24
). Then, there exist
C=C(\overline{c})>0
and
\sigma_{0}=\sigma_{0}(\overline{c})>1
, such that, if
\sigma>\sigma_{0}
,

(4.32)

\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\int_{\Sigma}(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)^{2}\ d\mu_{t}\leq-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla\mathcal{H}|^{2}\ d\mu_{t}+C\sigma^{-2}\int_{\Sigma}(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)^{2}\ d\mu_{t}.

Moreover, there exists a constant
c=c(\textup{c}_{\textup{in}},\overline{c})>0
and a universal constant
C=C(\overline{c})>0
such that

(4.33)

|\dot{\hbar}(t)|\leq c\sigma^{-\frac{7}{2}-\frac{1}{2}q-\delta-\delta q},

\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\int_{\Sigma}(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)^{4}\ d\mu_{t}\leq-12\int_{\Sigma}(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)^{2}|\nabla\mathcal{H}|^{2}\ d\mu_{t}+C\sigma^{-2}\int_{\Sigma}(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)^{4}\ d\mu_{t}+cB_{\infty}\sigma^{-5-\frac{3}{2}q-2\delta-3\delta q},

provided
\sigma\geq\sigma_{0}
, for a suitably
\sigma_{0}=\sigma_{0}(B_{\infty},\textup{c}_{\textup{in}},\overline{c},\delta)
.

Proof.

We first prove (
4.33
). By definition of
\hbar
, we get

\displaystyle|\Sigma_{t}|\dot{\hbar}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma}\left(\frac{\partial H}{\partial t}+\left(\Upsilon-1\right)\frac{\partial H}{\partial t}+\Psi\right)\ d\mu_{t}+\int_{\Sigma}\mathcal{H}H(\hbar-\mathcal{H})\ d\mu_{t}-\hbar\int_{\Sigma}H(\hbar-\mathcal{H})\ d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma}(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)\left(|\overset{\circ}{A}|^{2}+\overline{\textup{Ric}}(\nu,\nu)\right)\ d\mu_{t}+\int_{\Sigma}\left(\frac{H^{2}}{2}-\mathcal{H}H\right)\left(\mathcal{H}-\hbar\right)\ d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle-\hbar\int_{\Sigma}H(\hbar-\mathcal{H})\ d\mu_{t}+\int_{\Sigma}\left((\Upsilon-1)\frac{\partial H}{\partial t}+\Psi\right)\ d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma}(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)\left(|\overset{\circ}{A}|^{2}+\overline{\textup{Ric}}(\nu,\nu)\right)\ d\mu_{t}-\int_{\Sigma}\frac{H^{2}}{2}\left(\mathcal{H}-\hbar\right)\ d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle-\int_{\Sigma}(\mathcal{H}-H)H(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)\ d\mu_{t}-\hbar\int_{\Sigma}H(\hbar-\mathcal{H})\ d\mu_{t}+\int_{\Sigma}\left((\Upsilon-1)\frac{\partial H}{\partial t}+\Psi\right)\ d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma}(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)\left(|\overset{\circ}{A}|^{2}+\overline{\textup{Ric}}(\nu,\nu)\right)\ d\mu_{t}-\int_{\Sigma}\frac{\mathcal{H}^{2}}{2}\left(\mathcal{H}-\hbar\right)\ d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle+\int_{\Sigma}\left(\frac{\mathcal{H}^{2}-H^{2}}{2}\right)(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)\ d\mu_{t}-\int_{\Sigma}(\mathcal{H}-H)H(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)\ d\mu_{t}-\hbar\int_{\Sigma}H(\hbar-\mathcal{H})\ d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle+\int_{\Sigma}\left((\Upsilon-1)\frac{\partial H}{\partial t}+\Psi\right)\ d\mu_{t}

To estimate the above terms, we first note that

(4.34)

\left|\int_{\Sigma}(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)\left(|\overset{\circ}{A}|^{2}+\overline{\textup{Ric}}(\nu,\nu)\right)\ d\mu_{t}\right|\leq c\sigma^{-\frac{3}{2}-\frac{q}{2}-q\delta},

and that, in addition, the following identity holds

(4.35)

\displaystyle-\int_{\Sigma}\frac{\mathcal{H}^{2}}{2}\left(\mathcal{H}-\hbar\right)\ d\mu_{t}-\hbar\int_{\Sigma}H(\hbar-\mathcal{H})\ d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\Sigma}\frac{\mathcal{H}^{2}}{2}\left(\mathcal{H}-\hbar\right)\ d\mu_{t}+\hbar\int_{\Sigma}\left(\mathcal{H}-\hbar\right)^{2}\ d\mu_{t}-\hbar\int_{\Sigma}\left\{(H-h)-(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)\right\}\left(\hbar-\mathcal{H}\right)\ d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Sigma}(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)^{3}\ d\mu_{t}-\hbar\int_{\Sigma}\left\{(H-h)-(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)\right\}\left(\hbar-\mathcal{H}\right)\ d\mu_{t},

using also
\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)\ d\mu_{t}=0
, where, thanks to Lemma
2.4
,

(4.36)

\left|\hbar\int_{\Sigma}\left\{(H-h)-(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)\right\}\left(\hbar-\mathcal{H}\right)\ d\mu_{t}\right|\leq c\sigma^{-1-q-2q\delta}.

Since the remaining addend can be estimated in a similar way to (
4.36
), we get

(4.37)

|\Sigma_{t}||\dot{\hbar}(t)|\leq\left|\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Sigma}(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)^{3}\ d\mu_{t}\right|+c\sigma^{-\frac{3}{2}-\frac{q}{2}-q\delta}+\left|\int_{\Sigma}\left((\Upsilon-1)\frac{\partial H}{\partial t}+\Psi\right)\ d\mu_{t}\right|

Observe that the term
\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)^{3}\ d\mu_{t}
can be easily bounded using (
4.23
) and (
4.24
). Finally, we estimate

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\left((\Upsilon-1)\frac{\partial H}{\partial t}+\Psi\right)\ d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Sigma}\nabla\Upsilon\cdot\nabla\mathcal{H}\ d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\Sigma}(\Upsilon-1)\left(|A|^{2}+\overline{\textup{Ric}}(\nu,\nu)\right)(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)\ d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\Sigma}\Psi\ d\mu_{t}.

To estimate this term, we observe that equation (
4.3
), together with the inequalities

\left|\frac{P^{p}}{\mathcal{H}^{2}H^{p-1}}\right|\leq c_{q}\sigma^{1-\frac{1}{2}q-q\delta},\qquad\left|\frac{1}{\mathcal{H}}\frac{|P|^{q-2}P}{H^{q-1}}\right|\leq c_{q}\sigma^{\frac{3}{2}-\frac{1}{2}q-\delta(q-1)},

imply

\left|\int_{\Sigma}\nabla\Upsilon\cdot\nabla\mathcal{H}\ d\mu_{t}\right|\leq c\sigma^{-\frac{3}{2}-\frac{q}{2}-\delta q-\delta}.

Similarly, we also have

(4.38)

\left|\int_{\Sigma}(\Upsilon-1)\left(|A|^{2}+\overline{\textup{Ric}}(\nu,\nu)\right)(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)\ d\mu_{t}\right|\leq c\sigma^{-1-q-q\delta}.

We conclude combining (
4.4
), (
4.6
) and assumption (
4.24
), in order to get

(4.39)

\int_{\Sigma}\left|\Psi\right|\ d\mu_{t}\leq c\sigma^{-1-\frac{1}{2}q-\delta q}\|\mathcal{H}-\hbar\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t})}+c\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}q-\delta q}\|\nabla\mathcal{H}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t})}\leq c\sigma^{-1-q-2\delta q}.

Equation (
4.33
) follows dividing by
|\Sigma_{t}|\geq(7/2)\pi\sigma^{2}
.

We now prove (
4.32
). We compute the evolution of
\|\mathcal{H}-\hbar\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma,\mu_{t})}^{2}
, obtaining

\displaystyle\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\int_{\Sigma}(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)^{2}\ d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle=2\int_{\Sigma}(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)\left(\Delta\mathcal{H}+(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)(|A|^{2}+\overline{\textup{Ric}}(\nu,\nu))\right)\,d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle+2\int_{\Sigma}(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)(\Upsilon-1)\left(\Delta\mathcal{H}+(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)(|A|^{2}+\overline{\textup{Ric}}(\nu,\nu))\right)\,d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle+2\int_{\Sigma}\Psi(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)\ d\mu_{t}-\int_{\Sigma}H(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)^{3}\,d\mu_{t}.

Using integration by parts, the estimate
H+|H-h|+|A|\leq C\sigma^{-1}
, and the inequality

(4.40)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Sigma}(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)(\Upsilon-1)\Delta\mathcal{H}\ d\mu_{t}\right|=

\displaystyle\left|-\int_{\Sigma}(\Upsilon-1)|\nabla\mathcal{H}|^{2}\ d\mu_{t}-\int_{\Sigma}(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)\nabla\Upsilon\nabla\mathcal{H}\ d\mu_{t}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla\mathcal{H}|^{2}\ d\mu_{t}+C\int_{\Sigma}\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}q+1-q\delta}|\mathcal{H}-\hbar||\nabla\mathcal{H}|^{2}\ d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle+C\int_{\Sigma}\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}q-1-q\delta}|\mathcal{H}-\hbar||\nabla\mathcal{H}|\ d\mu_{t}

and Young’s inequality we get

(4.41)

\displaystyle\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\int_{\Sigma}(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)^{2}\ d\mu_{t}\leq-(2-2\varepsilon)\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla\mathcal{H}|^{2}\ d\mu_{t}+C\sigma^{-2}\int_{\Sigma}(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)^{2}\ d\mu_{t},

where we estimated
\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\Psi(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)\ d\mu_{t}
combining (
4.4
) and (
4.6
), i.e.

(4.42)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Sigma}(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)\Psi\ d\mu_{t}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\sigma^{-2-\frac{1}{2}q-\delta q}\int_{\Sigma}(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)^{2}\ d\mu+\sigma^{-1-\frac{1}{2}q-\delta q}\int_{\Sigma}|\mathcal{H}-\hbar||\nabla\mathcal{H}|\ d\mu\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\sigma^{-2}\int_{\Sigma}(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)^{2}\ d\mu+\varepsilon\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla\mathcal{H}|^{2}\ d\mu.

We conclude choosing
\varepsilon
suitably small.

We finally compute, using Lemma
4.2
, the evolution

\displaystyle\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\int_{\Sigma}(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)^{4}\ d\mu_{t}=

\displaystyle 4\int_{\Sigma}(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)^{3}\left(\Delta\mathcal{H}+(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)(|A|^{2}+\overline{\textup{Ric}}(\nu,\nu))-\dot{\hbar}\right)\ d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle+4\int_{\Sigma}(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)^{3}(\Upsilon-1)(\Delta\mathcal{H}+(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)(|A|^{2}+\overline{\textup{Ric}}(\nu,\nu)))\ d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle-\int_{\Sigma}H(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)^{5}\ d\mu+\int_{\Sigma}\Psi(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)^{3}\ d\mu_{t}.

We obtain the desired inequality (
4.6
) using integration

(4.43)

\int_{\Sigma}(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)^{3}\Delta\mathcal{H}\ d\mu_{t}=-3\int_{\Sigma}(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)^{2}|\nabla\mathcal{H}|^{2}\ d\mu_{t},

together with the estimate

\displaystyle|\dot{\hbar}|\int_{\Sigma}|\mathcal{H}-\hbar|^{3}

\displaystyle\leq c\sigma^{-\frac{7}{2}-\frac{1}{2}q-\delta-\delta q}\left(B_{\infty}\sigma^{-\frac{3}{2}-\delta}\right)\|\mathcal{H}-\hbar\|_{2}^{2}\leq cB_{\infty}\sigma^{-5-\frac{3}{2}q-2\delta-3\delta q},

and

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Sigma}(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)^{3}(\Upsilon-1)\Delta\mathcal{H}\ d\mu\right|

\displaystyle\leq c\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}q-q\delta}\int_{\Sigma}(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)^{2}|\nabla\mathcal{H}|^{2}\ d\mu

\displaystyle\quad+\left|-\int_{\Sigma}(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)^{3}\nabla\Upsilon\nabla\mathcal{H}\ d\mu\right|

\displaystyle\leq c\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}q-q\delta}\int_{\Sigma}(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)^{2}|\nabla\mathcal{H}|^{2}\ d\mu

\displaystyle\quad+c\int_{\Sigma}\sigma^{-\frac{q}{2}-1-q\delta}|\nabla\mathcal{H}||\mathcal{H}-\hbar|^{3}\ d\mu.

Finally, we conclude combining (
4.4
) and (
4.6
), and thus estimating

(4.44)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Sigma}\Psi(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)^{3}\ d\mu_{t}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\sigma^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2}q-\delta q+\delta}\int_{\Sigma}\sigma^{-\frac{5}{2}-\delta}|\mathcal{H}-\hbar|^{4}+\sigma^{-\frac{3}{2}-\delta}|\mathcal{H}-\hbar|^{3}|\nabla\mathcal{H}|\ d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle\leq C\sigma^{-2}\int_{\Sigma}(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)^{4}\ d\mu_{t}+C\sigma^{-1-\frac{1}{2}q-\delta q}\int_{\Sigma}(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)^{2}|\mathcal{H}-\hbar||\nabla\mathcal{H}|\ d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle\leq C\sigma^{-2}\int_{\Sigma}(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)^{4}\ d\mu_{t}+C\sigma^{-q-2\delta q}\int_{\Sigma}(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)^{2}|\nabla\mathcal{H}|^{2}\ d\mu_{t},

where we used Young’s inequality in the latter estimate. The conclusion holds for
\sigma
suitably large.
∎

A similar estimate, but independent of the evolution of
\hbar
, can be also given for
\nabla\mathcal{H}
. The hypothesis on
\overset{\circ}{A}
and the
H^{1}
-norm of
\mathcal{H}-\hbar
are not needed in order to prove the following Lemma.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
(\Sigma,F_{t})
,
t\in[0,T]
, such that (
2.14
) and (
2.15
) hold for every
t\in[0,T]
. Then there exists a constant
C=C(\overline{c})>0
such that

(4.45)

\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla\mathcal{H}|^{2}\ d\mu_{t}\leq-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla^{2}\mathcal{H}|^{2}\ d\mu_{t}+C\sigma^{-2}\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla\mathcal{H}|^{2}\ d\mu_{t}+C\sigma^{-4}\int_{\Sigma}(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)^{2}\ d\mu_{t},

and

(4.46)

\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla\mathcal{H}|^{4}\ d\mu_{t}\leq-\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla^{2}\mathcal{H}|^{2}|\nabla\mathcal{H}|^{2}\ d\mu_{t}+C\sigma^{-2}\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla\mathcal{H}|^{4}\ d\mu_{t}+C\sigma^{-6}\int_{\Sigma}(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)^{4}\ d\mu_{t}.

Proof.

We start proving inequality (
4.45
).
From Lemma
4.4
, after integration we get

\displaystyle\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla\mathcal{H}|^{2}\ d\mu_{t}=

\displaystyle 2\int_{\Sigma}\langle\nabla\left(\Delta\mathcal{H}+(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)(|A|^{2}+\overline{\textup{Ric}}(\nu,\nu)\right),\nabla\mathcal{H}\rangle\ d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle+2\int_{\Sigma}\langle\nabla\left(\left(\Upsilon-1\right)\left(\Delta\mathcal{H}+(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)(|A|^{2}+\overline{\textup{Ric}}(\nu,\nu))\right)\right),\nabla\mathcal{H}\rangle\ d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle+\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla\mathcal{H}|^{2}H(\hbar-\mathcal{H})\ d\mu_{t}+2\int_{\Sigma}(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)|\nabla\mathcal{H}|^{2}h^{ij}\nabla_{i}\mathcal{H}\nabla_{j}\mathcal{H}\ d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle+2\int_{\Sigma}\langle\nabla\Psi,\nabla\mathcal{H}\rangle d\mu_{t}.

Since
H
,
|\mathcal{H}-\hbar|
are bounded by
C\sigma^{-1}
and
\left||A|^{2}+\overline{\textup{Ric}}(\nu,\nu)\right|\leq C\sigma^{-2}
and
|\textup{Ric}^{\Sigma}|\leq C\sigma^{-2}
, using Bochner’s identity and integration by part we get

(4.47)

\displaystyle\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla\mathcal{H}|^{2}\ d\mu_{t}\leq

\displaystyle C\sigma^{-2}\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla\mathcal{H}|^{2}\ d\mu_{t}+C\sigma^{-4}\int_{\Sigma}(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)^{2}\ d\mu_{t}-\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla^{2}\mathcal{H}|^{2}\ d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle+2\left|\int_{\Sigma}\left(\Upsilon-1\right)\left(\Delta\mathcal{H}+(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)(|A|^{2}+\overline{\textup{Ric}}(\nu,\nu))\right)\Delta\mathcal{H}\ d\mu_{t}\right|

\displaystyle+2\left|\int_{\Sigma}\Psi\Delta\mathcal{H}\ d\mu_{t}\right|.

Note that, combining (
4.4
) and (
4.6
), we get

(4.48)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Sigma}\Psi\Delta\mathcal{H}\ d\mu_{t}\right|\leq

\displaystyle C\sigma^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2}q-\delta q+\delta}\int_{\Sigma}\left(\sigma^{-\frac{5}{2}-\delta}|\mathcal{H}-\hbar|+\sigma^{-\frac{3}{2}-\delta}|\nabla\mathcal{H}|\right)|\nabla^{2}\mathcal{H}|\ d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sigma^{-4}\int_{\Sigma}(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)^{2}\ d\mu_{t}+C\sigma^{-2}\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla\mathcal{H}|^{2}\ d\mu_{t}+C\sigma^{-q-2\delta q}\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla^{2}\mathcal{H}|^{2}\ d\mu_{t}.

Since
|\Upsilon-1|=\textup{O}(\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}q-q\delta})
and using Young’s inequality, we conclude, for
\sigma
large,

(4.49)

\displaystyle\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla\mathcal{H}|^{2}\ d\mu_{t}\leq

\displaystyle C\sigma^{-2}\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla\mathcal{H}|^{2}\ d\mu_{t}+C\sigma^{-4}\int_{\Sigma}(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)^{2}\ d\mu_{t}-\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla^{2}\mathcal{H}|^{2}\ d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle+C\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}q-q\delta}\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla^{2}\mathcal{H}|^{2}\ d\mu_{t}+C\sigma^{-2-\frac{1}{2}q-q\delta}\int_{\Sigma}|\mathcal{H}-\hbar||\nabla^{2}\mathcal{H}|\ d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle+C\sigma^{-q-2q\delta}\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla^{2}\mathcal{H}|^{2}\ d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sigma^{-2}\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla\mathcal{H}|^{2}\ d\mu_{t}+C\sigma^{-4}\int_{\Sigma}(\mathcal{H}-\hbar)^{2}\ d\mu_{t}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla^{2}\mathcal{H}|^{2}\ d\mu_{t}.

We now prove (

\partial_{t}\left(|\Sigma_{t}||\vec{z}(t)|\right)\leq C\sigma\|\mathcal{H}-\hbar\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma,\mu_{t})}<C(C_{1}+1)\sigma^{1-\frac{q}{2}-q\delta}e^{-\frac{E_{\textup{ADM}}t}{2\sigma^{3}}}.

Integrating (
4.69
) over
[0,T_{\textup{max}}]
, we get

|\Sigma_{T_{\textup{max}}}||\vec{z}(T_{\textup{max}})|-|\Sigma_{0}||\vec{z}(0)|\leq C(C_{1}+1)\sigma^{1-\frac{q}{2}-q\delta}\left(\frac{2\sigma^{3}}{E_{\textup{ADM}}}\right)\left(1-e^{-\frac{E_{\textup{ADM}}T_{\textup{max}}}{2\sigma^{3}}}\right).

By the hypotheses
|\vec{z}_{\Sigma_{0}}|\leq C_{0}\sigma^{1-\delta}
, we find

(4.70)

|\vec{z}(T_{\textup{max}})|\leq\frac{2}{7\pi}\left(5\pi(C_{0}\sigma^{1-\delta})+\frac{2C(C_{1}+1)}{E_{\textup{ADM}}}\sigma^{2-\frac{q}{2}-q\delta}\right)<B_{\textup{cen}}\sigma^{1-\delta}

if
B_{\textup{cen}}
suitably large, depending on
C_{0}
,
C
,
C_{1}
and
E_{\textup{ADM}}
. Thus
\Sigma_{T_{\textup{max}}}
belongs to the class
\mathcal{B}_{\sigma}(B_{1},B_{2},B_{\textup{cen}})
, and combining this with (
4.67
) we obtain that necessarily
T_{\textup{max}}=T
.
∎

Local regularity of the flow.
We now review the regularity theory of the non-linear flow we are considering. Since in a local interval of existence
[0,t_{0})
the principal curvatures are uniformly bounded (by the preservation of the roundness), it follows that
\Sigma_{t}
can be locally written as a graph. Suppose in particular that
\Sigma_{t}\cap B_{\epsilon}(x_{0})=\{\left(x_{1},x_{2},u(t,x_{1},x_{2})\right):\ (x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathcal{A}\}
, with
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
open. Since the metric, the unit normal vector, and the mean curvature of
\Sigma_{t}
are locally given by

(4.71)

\displaystyle g_{ij}=\delta_{ij}+D_{i}uD_{j}u,\qquad\nu=\frac{\left(-D_{1}u,-D_{2}u,1\right)}{\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}},

\displaystyle H=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}}\left(\delta^{ij}-\frac{D^{i}uD^{j}u}{1+|Du|^{2}}\right)D_{ij}^{2}u,

the equation (
1.5
), written in a tangential fashion, translates into an equation for
u

(4.72)

\partial_{t}u=\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}\left(\Phi\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+|Du|^{2}}}\left(\delta^{ij}-\frac{D^{i}uD^{j}u}{1+|Du|^{2}}\right)D_{ij}^{2}u,P\right)-\hbar\right),

where
P=g^{ij}\overline{\textup{K}}_{ij}
is a smooth function and
\Phi(s,\gamma)=\sqrt[q]{s^{q}-|\gamma|^{q}}
. We rewrite equation (
4.72
) as

(4.73)

\partial_{t}u=\mathcal{F}(D^{2}u,Du,x,t).

Note that

(4.74)

\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{ij}:=\frac{\partial\mathcal{F}}{\partial D_{ij}^{2}u}=\left(\delta^{ij}-\frac{D^{i}uD^{j}u}{1+|Du|^{2}}\right)\partial_{s}\Phi

and
\partial_{s}\Phi=q^{-1}\left(s^{q}-|\gamma|^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}-1}(qs^{q-1})>0
. Thus, as a matrix,

(4.75)

\displaystyle|w|^{2}\left(\inf_{\mathcal{A}}\partial_{s}\Phi\right)\left(1-\frac{\sup|Du|^{2}}{1+\sup|Du|^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{ij}w_{i}w_{j}=\left(|w|^{2}-\frac{(Du\cdot w)^{2}}{1+|Du|^{2}}\right)\partial_{s}\Phi

\displaystyle\leq|w|^{2}\left(\sup_{\mathcal{A}}\partial_{s}\Phi\right).

Finally note that if
\dot{\mathcal{F}}^{ij}M_{ij}=0
then
\left(\delta^{ij}-\frac{D^{i}uD^{j}u}{1+|Du|^{2}}\right)M_{ij}=0
. Thus, computing
\ddot{\mathcal{F}}^{ij,kl}:=\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{F}}{\partial D_{ij}^{2}u\partial D_{kl}^{2}u}
, it follows that this implies that
\ddot{\mathcal{F}}^{ij,kl}M_{ij}M_{kl}=0
.

This means that we are in the hypothesis of Theorem 6 in
[
2
]
, which let us obtain a
C^{2,\alpha}
estimate on
u
, for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
. By standard arguments, this means that the coefficients of the linearization of the non-linear equation are
C^{0,\alpha}
-Hölder, and thus the standard theory (see for example
[
25
]
) implies uniform bounds on all higher derivatives of
u
. Covering
\Sigma_{t}
with graphs over balls of the same radius, we obtain Hölder estimates on the curvature and its derivatives.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.
Consider a CMC surface
\Sigma
such that, setting
\sigma=\sigma_{\Sigma}
,

(4.76)

\|\overset{\circ}{A}\|_{L^{4}(\Sigma)}\leq C_{0}\sigma^{-1-\delta},\qquad|\vec{z}_{\Sigma}|\leq C_{0}\sigma^{1-\delta},\qquad\frac{\sigma}{r_{\Sigma}}\leq 1+C_{0}^{-1},

for some
C_{0}>0
large enough. Notice that, for
B_{1}
,
B_{2}
and
B_{\textup{cen}}
suitably large
\Sigma
belongs to
\mathcal{B}_{\sigma}(B_{1},B_{2},B_{\textup{cen}})
. See also (
2.21
). Suppose that the maximal time of existence of the flow, say
T_{\textup{max}}
, is finite. Then, by Theorem
4.12
we find that also
\Sigma_{T_{\textup{max}}}
belongs to
\mathcal{B}_{\sigma}(B_{1},B_{2},B_{\textup{cen}})
and thus, by the regularity theory, we can extend the flow past

, which contradicts the maximality. Thus
T_{\textup{max}}=\infty
.

\square

Convergence.
From Lemma
4.10
we see that
\|H-h\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma_{t})}
decays exponentially as
t\to+\infty
. Since the derivatives of any order of
H
are uniformly bounded, interpolation estimates imply that they also decay exponentially. Then Sobolev immersion implies that
\|H-h\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma_{t})}
decays exponentially as well. By the bootstrapping argument described in the paragraph above, the boundedness of the curvatures and
[
18
, Lemma 8.2]
show that
F(\cdot,t)
converges to a smooth immersion
F_{\infty}(\cdot)
. In particular, since
\mathcal{H}-\hbar\to 0
, the limit surface
\Sigma_{\infty}:=F_{\infty}(\Sigma)
satisfies
\mathcal{H}\equiv\hbar
. Finally, Theorem
4.12
also shows that the requirements in the definition of
\mathcal{B}_{\sigma}(B_{1},B_{2},B_{\textup{cen}})
still hold as strict inequalities on
\Sigma_{\infty}
.

4.4.
CSTMC foliation and centers of mass

In conclusion, we remark that the computation carried out in the above Lemmas also have some consequences on the center of mass of the foliation we constructed. In this Section, we suppose that the initial datum of our flow is a leaf of Nerz’s foliation, as recalled in (
2.21
). In particular, we assume that there exists the CMC-center of mass of Nerz’s foliation, i.e. the limit as
s\to\infty
of the Euclidean barycenters of the foliation
\left\{\Sigma^{s}\right\}_{s\geq s_{0}}
constructed by Nerz (see
[
30
]
). In the following, we will suppose the change of variable
s\longleftrightarrow\sigma
, with
\sigma(s):=\sigma_{\Sigma^{s}}
. Thus, we have

Corollary 4.14
.

Let
(\textup{M},\overline{\textup{g}},\overline{\textup{K}})
be a
C_{\frac{1}{2}+\delta}^{2}
-asymptotically flat initial data set
which is constrained and with positive ADM-energy
E_{\textup{ADM}}>0
. Let
\iota^{\sigma}:\Sigma^{\sigma}\hookrightarrow\textup{M}\setminus\textup{C}
the inclusion of the family
\left\{\Sigma^{\sigma}\right\}_{\sigma\geq\sigma_{0}}
of
CMC
-surfaces as above and suppose that there exists the
CMC
-center of mass of
\Sigma^{\sigma}
, i.e.

(4.77)

\vec{\mathcal{C}}_{\textup{CMC}}:=\lim_{\sigma\to\infty}\fint_{\Sigma^{\sigma}}\vec{x}\ d\mu^{\sigma},

where
d\mu^{\sigma}
is the 2-dimensional measure induced by
\overline{\textup{g}}
on
\Sigma^{\Sigma}
. Consider the
CSTMC
foliation constructed above, and let
\vec{z}_{\Sigma_{\textup{st}}^{\sigma}}
be the barycenter of
\Sigma_{\textup{st}}^{\sigma}:=\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}F_{t}(\Sigma^{\sigma})
.

(i)

If
q>\frac{2}{\frac{1}{2}+\delta}
then

(4.78)

\lim_{\sigma\to\infty}\vec{z}_{\Sigma^{\sigma}_{\textup{st}}}=\vec{\mathcal{C}}_{\textup{CMC}}.

(ii)

If
2\leq q\leq\frac{2}{\frac{1}{2}+\delta}
, then there exists
C>0
such that

(4.79)

|\vec{z}_{\Sigma_{\textup{st}}^{\sigma}}-\vec{z}_{\Sigma^{\sigma}}|\leq C\sigma^{2-\frac{q}{2}-q\delta}.

Proof.

Integrating (
4.68
) in
[0,t]
we get

(4.80)

||\vec{z}(t)|-|\vec{z}(0)||\leq C\int_{0}^{t}|\Sigma_{t}|^{-1}\|\mathcal{H}-\hbar\|_{L^{1}(\Sigma_{t})}\ dt\leq C\sigma^{2-\frac{q}{2}-q\delta}\left(1-e^{-\frac{E_{\textup{ADM}}t}{\sigma^{3}}}\right).

Since, by construction,
\vec{z}(0)=\vec{z}_{\Sigma^{\sigma}}

|\vec{z}_{\Sigma_{\textup{st}}^{\sigma}}-\vec{z}_{\Sigma^{\sigma}}|\leq C\sigma^{2-\frac{q}{2}-q\delta}.

∎

Remark 4.15
.

(i)

Since
\frac{1}{2}+\delta\in\left(\frac{1}{2},1\right]
, we have that

(4.81)

2\leq\frac{2}{\frac{1}{2}+\delta}<4.

Thus, if
q\geq 4
, the volume preserving spacetime mean curvature flow recovers the center of mass
\vec{\mathcal{C}}_{\textup{CMC}}
for every
\delta\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
.

(ii)

For
q=2
, we recover the foliation constructed in
[
8
]
. In this case, the right hand side of equation (
4.79
) is divergent, and the theory developed by Cederbaum and Sakovich in
[
8
]
let us to conclude that

converges if and only if the correction term converges

(4.82)

\lim_{r\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}_{r}^{2}}\frac{x^{i}\left(\sum_{k,l}\pi_{kl}x^{k}x^{l}\right)^{2}}{r^{3}}\ d\mu^{e},

under the additional hypothesis that

(iii)

Finally, also in the case

) holds with a positive exponent, and thus, in a case in which the CMC-barycenter does not converge, this does not necessarily imply the non convergence of the CSTMC-barycenter. However, differently from point (ii), where the convergence of the limit (

) allows to deduce a relation between the two barycenters, for a general

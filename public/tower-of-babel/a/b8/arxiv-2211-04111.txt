1.
Introduction

In
(
[
15
, Corollary 1.4]
)
, Suslin established the normality of the elementary
linear subgroup
\mbox{\rm E}_{n}(R)
in
\mbox{\rm GL}_{n}(R)
, for
n\geq 3
. This was a major
surprise at that time as it was known due to the work of Cohn in

[
6
]
that in general
\mbox{\rm E}_{2}(R)
is not normal in
\mbox{\rm GL}_{2}(R)
. This is
the initial precursor to study the non-stable
K_{1}
groups
{\mbox{\rm SL}_{n}(R)}/{\mbox{\rm E}_{n}(R)},~n\geq 3
.

This theorem can also be got as a consequence of the local-global principle of
Quillen (for projective modules) in
[
9
]
; and its analogue for the
linear group of elementary matrices
\mbox{\rm E}_{n}(R[X])
, when
n\geq 3
due to Suslin in
[
15
]
. In fact, in
[
3
]
it is shown that, in some sense,
the normality property of the elementary group
\mbox{\rm E}_{n}(R)
in
SL_{n}(R)
is
equivalent to having a local-global principle for
\mbox{\rm E}_{n}(R[X])
.

In
[
2
]
, Bak proved the following beautiful result:

Theorem 1.1
.

(
Bak
)
For an almost commutative ring
R
with identity with
centre
C(R)
. The group
{\mbox{\rm SL}_{n}(R)}/{\mbox{\rm E}_{n}(R)}

is nilpotent of class atmost
\delta(C(R))+3-n
, where

\delta(C(R))<\infty~and~n\geq 3
, where
\delta(C(R))
is
the Bass–Serre- dimension of
C(R)
.

This theorem, which is proved by a localization and completion technique,
which evolved from an adaptation of the proof of the
Suslin’s
K_{1}
-analogue of Quillen’s
local-global principle was further investigated in
[
10
]
. In
[
10
]
, we proved that

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
R
be a local ring, and let
A=R[X]
. Then the
group
{\mbox{\rm SL}_{n}(A)}/{\mbox{\rm E}_{n}(A)}
is an abelian group for
n\geq 3
.

This theorem is a simple consequence of the following principle:

Theorem 1.3
.

(
[
10
, Theorem 2.19]
)

(
Homotopy and commutativity principle
):

Let
R
be a commutative ring. Let
\alpha\in\mbox{\rm SL}_{n}(R)
,
n\geq 3
, be homotopic to the identity. Then, for any
\beta\in\mbox{\rm SL}_{n}(R)
,

\alpha\beta=\beta\alpha\varepsilon
, for some
\varepsilon\in\mbox{\rm E}_{n}(R)
.

This principle is a consequence of the Quillen–Suslin’s local-global principle;
and using a
non-symmetric
application of it as done by Bak in

[
2
]
.

Using Bak’s localization method, in
[
13
]
, Stepanov proved the following result for all simply connected Chevalley group of rank
>1
:

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
G
be a simply connected group of rank
>1
with
G(R)=E(R)
when
R
is a local ring. Then for any commutative ring
R
with 1,

[\overset{\sim}{E}(R),G(R)]=E(R)

where
\overset{\sim}{E}(R)=\bigcap_{(s_{1},\ldots s_{l})\in Um_{l}(R)}\prod G(R,s_{1}R),\ldots,G(R,s_{l}R),
denotes the extended elementary group.

In this paper, we generalise the homotopy and commutativity principle to any
n\times m
right invertible matrix over a commutative ring

R
. In particular, we prove that :

Theorem 1.5
.

(
Generalised homotopy and commutativity principle
)

Let
R
be a commutative ring and
V\in\mbox{\rm Um}_{n,m}(R)
with
m>n\geq 2~\mbox{or}~m=n\geq 3.

Let
\delta\in\mbox{\rm SL}_{n}(R)
be homotopic to identity.
Let
\delta(T)
be a homotopy of
\delta.

Then
\exists~\sigma(T)\in\mbox{\rm SL}_{m}(R[T],(T))
such that

\delta(T)V=V\sigma(T)~\mbox{and}~\sigma(T)^{-1}(\delta(T)\perp I_{m-n})\in\mbox{\rm E}_{m}(R[T],(T)).

Moreover, if
\sigma(1)=\sigma
, then we have

\delta V=V\sigma
and
\sigma^{-1}(\delta\perp I_{m-n})\in\mbox{\rm E}_{m}(R).

We also prove similar results in the case of symplectic groups (see theorem
3.13
). We prove the similar statement in the case of orthogonal groups as well with

(see theorem
4.10
).
As a consequence we prove that linear and symplectic quotients are abelian, but in the

Theorem 1.6

Let

,

be a local ring,
\frac{1}{2}\in R.
Then
([\rm{O}_{2m}R[X],\rm{O}_{2m}R[X]]\perp I_{2})\subseteq\mbox{\rm EO}_{2m+2}(R[X]).

We do believe that orthogonal quotient groups are also abelian; as it is the case when the base ring is regular local ring
containing a field (see

)

2.
Generalised Homotopy and Commutativity Principle for Linear Groups

Let
v=(a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}),w=(b_{0},b_{1},\ldots,b_{r})
be two rows of length
r+1
over a commutative ring

R
. A row
v\in R^{r+1}
is said to be unimodular if there is a
w\in R^{r+1}
with

\langle v,w\rangle=\Sigma_{i=0}^{r}a_{i}b_{i}=1
and
{\mbox{\rm Um}}_{r+1}(R)
will denote
the set of unimodular rows (over
R
) of length

r+1
.

The group of elementary matrices is a subgroup of
\mbox{\rm GL}_{r+1}(R)
, denoted by
\mbox{\rm E}_{r+1}(R)
, and is generated by the matrices
of the form
e_{ij}(\lambda)=I_{r+1}+\lambda\mbox{\rm E}_{ij}
, where
\lambda\in R,~i\neq j,~1\leq i,j\leq r+1,~\mbox{\rm E}_{ij}\in M_{r+1}(R)
whose
ij^{th}
entry is
1
and all other entries are zero. The elementary linear group

\mbox{\rm E}_{r+1}(R)
acts on the rows of length
r+1
by right multiplication. Moreover, this action takes unimodular rows to
unimodular
rows :
{{\mbox{\rm Um}}_{r+1}(R)}/{\mbox{\rm E}_{r+1}(R)}
will denote the set of orbits of this action; and we shall denote by
[v]
the
equivalence class of a row
v
under this equivalence relation.

Definition 2.1
.

An
\alpha\in M_{n\times m}(R)
is said to be right invertible if
\exists~\beta\in M_{m\times n}(R)

such that
\alpha\beta=I_{n}.

We will denote set of all
n\times m
right invertible matrices by
{\mbox{\rm Um}}_{n,m}(R).

Definition 2.2
.

An
R
-module
P
is said to be stably free of type
n
, if
P\oplus R^{n}
is a free module.

To every
\alpha\in{\mbox{\rm Um}}_{n,m}(R)
, we can associate a stably free module
P
of type
n
, in the following way:

Since
\alpha\in{\mbox{\rm Um}}_{n,m}(R)
, it gives rise to a surjective map
R^{m}\overset{\alpha}{\longrightarrow}R^{n}
.
Let
P=\mbox{Ker}({\alpha})
, then we have a short
exact sequence

0\longrightarrow P\longrightarrow R^{m}\longrightarrow R^{n}\longrightarrow 0.

Since
R^{n}
is a free module, the above short exact sequence splits and we have
P\oplus R^{n}\simeq R^{m}.

To every stably free module
P
of type
n
, we can associate an element
\alpha
of
{\mbox{\rm Um}}_{n,m}(R)
, for some
m
, in the
following way:

Since
P
is stably free, we have a short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow P\longrightarrow R^{m}\longrightarrow R^{n}\longrightarrow 0.

Let
\alpha
to be the matrix of the
map
R^{m}\longrightarrow R^{n}
. Since
R^{n}
is a free module,
the above short exact sequence splits and we have
\alpha\in{\mbox{\rm Um}}_{n,m}(R).

Lemma 2.3
.

(
[
8
, Chapter 1, Proposition 4.3]
)
An
\alpha\in{\mbox{\rm Um}}_{n,m}(R)
is completable to an invertible
matrix of determinant
1
if and only if the corresponding stably free module is free.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
R
be a local ring and
V\in{\mbox{\rm Um}}_{n,m}(R)
for
m~(\mbox{or}~n)\geq 2
. Then
V
is completable to an elementary
matrix.

{{\bf Proof:}}
Every
V\in\mbox{Um}_{n,m}(R)
corresponds to a stably free module
P.
Since a projective module over a local ring is
free,
P
is free. In view of lemma
2.3
, V is
completable to a matrix
W\in\mbox{\rm SL}_{m}(R)=\mbox{\rm E}_{m}(R).

~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\hfill\Box

Definition 2.5
.

Let R be a ring. A matrix
\alpha\in\mbox{\rm SL}_{n}(R)
is said to be
homotopic to identity if there exists a matrix
\gamma(X)\in\mbox{\rm SL}_{n}(R[X])
such that
\gamma(0)=\textit{Id}~and~\gamma(1)=\alpha
.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
R
be a local ring and
V\in\mbox{\rm Um}_{n,m}(R)
for
m>n\geq 2
or
m=n\geq 3.

Let
\delta\in\mbox{\rm SL}_{n}(R)
be homotopic to identity.
Let
\delta(T)
be a homotopy of
\delta.
Then there exists,

\sigma(T)\in\mbox{\rm SL}_{m}(R[T])
with
\sigma(0)=\textit{Id}
and
\sigma(T)^{-1}(\delta(T)\perp I_{m-n})\in\mbox{\rm E}_{m}(R[T])

such that

\delta(T)V=V\sigma(T).

{{\bf Proof:}}
In view of lemma
2.4
,
V
is completable to a matrix
W\in\mbox{\rm SL}_{m}(R)
. Since
R
is a local ring,

W\in\mbox{\rm E}_{m}(R).
By
(
[
15
, Corollary 1.4]
)
,
\mbox{\rm E}_{m}(R[T])\trianglelefteq\mbox{\rm SL}_{m}(R[T])
, for
m\geq 3.

Thus there exists

\varepsilon_{1}(T)\in\mbox{\rm E}_{m}(R[T])
such that

(\delta(T)\perp I_{m-n})W(\delta(T)\perp I_{m-n})^{-1}=\varepsilon_{1}(T).

Thus we have
(\delta(T)\perp I_{m-n})W=\varepsilon_{1}(T)W^{-1}W(\delta(T)\perp I_{m-n}).
Again by normality of
\mbox{\rm E}_{m}(R[T])
in
\mbox{\rm SL}_{m}(R[T]
for

m\geq 3,
there exists
\varepsilon(T)\in\mbox{\rm E}_{m}(R[T])
such that

(\delta(T)\perp I_{m-n})W=W(\delta(T)\perp I_{m-n})\varepsilon(T).

Note that
\varepsilon(0)=\textit{Id}.

Upon taking
\sigma(T)=(\delta(T)\perp I_{m-n})\varepsilon(T)
and multiplying above equation by
\begin{bmatrix}I_{n}&0\\
0&0\\
\end{bmatrix}
, we gets desired result.

~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\hfill\Box

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
R
be a commutative ring and
V\in\mbox{\rm Um}_{n,m}(R)
with
m>n\geq 2~\mbox{or}~m=n\geq 3.

Let
\delta\in\mbox{\rm SL}_{n}(R)
be homotopic to identity.
Let
\delta(T)
be a homotopy of
\delta.

Then
\exists~\sigma(T)\in\mbox{\rm SL}_{m}(R[T],(T))
such that

\delta(T)V=V\sigma(T)~\mbox{and}~\sigma(T)^{-1}(\delta(T)\perp I_{m-n})\in\mbox{\rm E}_{m}(R[T],(T)).

Moreover
, if
\sigma(1)=\sigma
, then we have

\delta V=V\sigma
and
\sigma^{-1}(\delta\perp I_{m-n})\in\mbox{\rm E}_{m}(R).

{{\bf Proof:}}
Define,

\displaystyle J=

\displaystyle\{s\in R~|~\delta(T)_{s}V_{s}=V_{s}\sigma(T)~\mbox{for ~some}~\sigma(T)\in\mbox{\rm SL}_{m}(R_{s}[T],(T))~

\displaystyle\mbox{with}~\sigma(T)^{-1}(\delta(T)_{s}\perp I_{m-n})\in\mbox{\rm E}_{m}(R_{s}[T])\}.

\bf{Claim:}

J
is an ideal.

For
s\in J,\lambda\in R
, clearly
\lambda s\in J
.
So we need to prove that if
s_{1},s_{2}\in J
then

s_{1}+s_{2}\in J.
Since
s_{1},s_{2}\in J
,
we have
(s_{1}+s_{2})s_{1}
,
(s_{1}+s_{2})s_{2}\in J.

We rename
R_{s_{1}+s_{2}}
by
R
, now
it suffices to show that

\displaystyle\delta(T)V

\displaystyle=V\sigma(T)~\mbox{for}~\mbox{some}~\sigma(T)\in\mbox{\rm SL}_{m}(R[T],(T))

\displaystyle\mbox{with}~\sigma(T)^{-1}(\delta(T)\perp I_{m-n})\in\mbox{\rm E}_{m}(R[T],(T))~\mbox{provided that}~s_{1}+s_{2}=1~\mbox{ and}

(1)

\delta(T)_{s_{1}}V_{s_{1}}=V_{s_{1}}\sigma_{1}(T)~\mbox{with}~\sigma_{1}(T)^{-1}(\delta(T)_{s_{1}}\perp I_{m-n})\in\mbox{\rm E}_{m}(R_{s_{1}}[T],(T)),

(2)

\delta(T)_{s_{2}}V_{s_{2}}=V_{s_{2}}\sigma_{2}(T)~\mbox{with}~\sigma_{2}(T)^{-1}(\delta(T)_{s_{2}}\perp I_{m-n})\in\mbox{\rm E}_{m}(R_{s_{2}}[T],(T)).

Let

~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\sigma_{1}(T)(\delta(T)_{s_{1}}\perp I_{m-n})^{-1}=\varepsilon_{1}(T)\in\mbox{\rm E}_{m}(R_{s_{1}}[T],(T)),

~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\sigma_{2}(T)(\delta(T)_{s_{2}}\perp I_{m-n})^{-1}=\varepsilon_{2}(T)\in\mbox{\rm E}_{m}(R_{s_{2}}[T],(T)).

Now,
\begin{bmatrix}V_{s_{1}s_{2}}\\
0\\
\end{bmatrix}\varepsilon_{1}(T)_{s_{2}}\varepsilon_{2}(T)_{s_{1}}^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}V_{s_{1}s_{2}}\\
0\\
\end{bmatrix}.
Let
\theta(T)=\varepsilon_{1}(T)_{s_{2}}\varepsilon_{2}(T)_{s_{1}}^{-1}.

By Quillen’s splitting property,
\mbox{for}~b\in(s_{2}^{N}),~N>>0
, we have

(3)

\theta(T)=\theta(bT)\{\theta(bT)^{-1}\theta(T)\}

~\mbox{with}~\theta(bT)\in\mbox{\rm E}_{m}(R_{s_{1}}[T]),~\mbox{and}~\theta(bT)^{-1}\theta(T)\in\mbox{\rm E}_{m}(R_{s_{2}}[T]).

Since
\begin{bmatrix}V_{s_{1}s_{2}}\\
0\\
\end{bmatrix}\theta(T)=\begin{bmatrix}V_{s_{1}s_{2}}\\
0\\
\end{bmatrix}
. We have,
\begin{bmatrix}V_{s_{1}s_{2}}\\
0\\
\end{bmatrix}\theta(bT)=\begin{bmatrix}V_{s_{1}s_{2}}\\
0\\
\end{bmatrix}
and

\begin{bmatrix}V_{s_{1}s_{2}}\\
0\\
\end{bmatrix}\theta(bT)^{-1}\theta(T)=\begin{bmatrix}V_{s_{1}s_{2}}\\
0\\
\end{bmatrix}.

Define,
\theta(bT)=\eta_{1}(T)^{-1},~\theta(bT)^{-1}\theta(T)=\eta_{2}(T)
. Thus we have

V_{i}\eta_{i}(T)=V_{i}
with
\eta_{i}(0)=\textit{Id}
and
\eta_{i}(T)\in\mbox{\rm E}_{m}(R_{s_{i}}[T])

for
i=1,2.

In view of equation
3
, we gets

(4)

(\eta_{1}(T)\varepsilon_{1}(T))_{s_{2}}=(\eta_{2}(T)\varepsilon_{2}(T))_{s_{1}}.

Now, by equation
3
and equation
4
,

\delta(T)_{s_{1}}V_{s_{1}}=V_{s_{1}}\eta_{1}(T)\sigma_{1}(T)

\delta(T)_{s_{2}}V_{s_{2}}=V_{s_{2}}\eta_{2}(T)\sigma_{2}(T).

In view of equation 4, we have
(\eta_{1}(T)\sigma_{1}(T))_{s_{2}}=(\eta_{2}(T)\sigma_{2}(T))_{s_{1}}
. Since

s_{1}+s_{2}=1
,

\exists~\sigma(T)\in\mbox{\rm SL}_{m}(R[T])
such that
\sigma(T)_{s_{1}}=\eta_{1}(T)\sigma_{1}(T)
and

\sigma(T)_{s_{2}}=\eta_{2}(T)\sigma_{2}(T)
with
\sigma(T)_{s_{i}}^{-1}(\delta(T)_{s_{i}}\perp I_{m-n})\in\mbox{\rm E}_{m}(R_{s_{i}}[T])
for

Since
s_{1}
and

are comaximal,

(
[

, we have

\displaystyle\delta(T)V

\displaystyle=V\sigma(T)~\mbox{for}~\mbox{some}~\sigma(T)\in\mbox{\rm SL}_{m}(R[T],(T))~

This proves that

is an ideal.

In view of Proposition
2.6

Corollary 2.8
.

(
[
10
, Theorem 2.19]
)

Let
n\geq 3
and
\alpha,\beta\in\mbox{\rm SL}_{n}(R).
Let
either
\alpha
or
\beta
be homotopic to identity. Then
\alpha\beta=\beta\alpha\varepsilon,~~\mbox{for~ some}~\varepsilon\in\mbox{\rm E}_{n}(R).

{{\bf Proof:}}
Let us assume that
\alpha
is homotopic to identity, so there exists

\delta(T)\in\mbox{\rm SL}_{n}(R[T])
such that
\delta(0)=\textit{Id}~\mbox{and}~\delta(1)=\alpha.
By theorem

2.7
,
there exists
\varepsilon(T)\in\mbox{\rm E}_{n}(R[T])
with
\varepsilon(0)=\textit{Id}
such that

\delta(T)\beta=\beta\delta(T)\varepsilon(T).

Put

to get the desired result.

Corollary 2.9
.

(
Vaserstein
)

Let
\delta\in\mbox{\rm SL}_{n}(R)
and
V\in\mbox{\rm Um}_{n,m}(R),m>n\geq 2~\mbox{or}~n=m\geq 3.

Then
\delta V=V\sigma
for some
\sigma\in\mbox{\rm SL}_{m}(R)
with

(\sigma\perp\delta^{-1})\in\mbox{\rm E}_{n+m}(R).

{{\bf Proof:}}
By Whitehead’s Lemma,
(\delta\perp\delta^{-1})\in\mbox{\rm E}_{2m}(R).
Since every elementary matrix is
homotopic to identity,
thus by theorem
2.7
,

(\delta\perp\delta^{-1})(V\perp I_{n})=(V\perp I_{n})\sigma^{{}^{\prime}},~\mbox{with}~\sigma^{{}^{\prime}}\in\mbox{\rm E}_{n+m}(R).

Write
\sigma^{{}^{\prime}}=\begin{bmatrix}\alpha&\beta\\
\gamma&\zeta\\
\end{bmatrix}
where
\alpha\in M_{m\times m}(R),~\beta\in M_{m\times n}(R),~\gamma\in M_{n\times m}(R),~\zeta\in M_{n\times n}(R)
. Thus we have,

(\delta V\perp\delta^{-1})=\begin{bmatrix}V\alpha&V\beta\\
\gamma&\zeta\\
\end{bmatrix}.

Upon compairing both sides we gets
\gamma=0
and
\zeta=\delta^{-1}.
Therefore

\begin{bmatrix}\alpha&\beta\\
0&\delta^{-1}\\
\end{bmatrix}\in\mbox{\rm E}_{n+m}(R).

Now, take
\alpha=\sigma
, so we have
(\sigma\perp\delta^{-1})\in E_{n+m}(R)
and
\delta V=V\sigma.

Corollary 2.12
.

(

)

(
[
11

)

Let I be an ideal of

\end{bmatrix}.

Assume that

Then

(x_{0},\ldots,x_{k-1},x_{k},\ldots,x_{n})\overset{E}{\sim}(x_{0},\ldots,x_{k-1},y_{k},\ldots,y_{n}).

{{\bf Proof:}}
Consider the ring
\overset{-}{R}=R/Rx_{0}+\ldots+Rx_{k-1}

\overset{-}{\alpha}\in\mbox{Um}_{2,n-k+1}(\overset{-}{R}).

Let

be a lift of
\overset{-}{\varepsilon}.

Therefore,

V^{\prime\prime}\in\rm{OUm}_{2(n-1),2(m-1)}(R).
Now, we get the desired result by induction hypothesis.

~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\hfill\Box

Following the steps of the proof of Proposition
3.12
, one gets the following result :

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
R
be a local ring and
V\in\rm{OUm}_{2n,2m}(R)
for
m\geq n+2,n\geq 2.

Let
\delta\in\rm{SO}_{2n}(R)
be orthogonal homotopic to identity.
Let
\delta(T)
be a homotopy of
\delta.
Then there exists,

\sigma(T)\in\mbox{SO}_{2m}(R[T])
with
\sigma(0)=\textit{Id}
and
\sigma(T)^{-1}(\delta(T)\perp I_{2m-2n})\in\mbox{\rm EO}_{2m}(R[T])

such that

\delta(T)V=V\sigma(T).

By making appropriate modifications in the proof of theorem
2.7
and theorem
3.13
, one can prove the following result :

Theorem 4.10
.

Let
R
be a commutative ring and
V\in\rm{OUm}_{2n,2m}(R)
with
m\geq n+2,n\geq 2.
Let
\delta\in\rm{SO}_{2n}(R)
be orthogonal homotopic to identity.
Let
\delta(T)
be a homotopy of
\delta.

Then
\exists~\sigma(T)\in\mbox{SO}_{2m}(R[T],(T))
such that

\delta(T)V=V\sigma(T)~\mbox{and}~\sigma(T)^{-1}(\delta(T)\perp I_{2m-2n})\in\mbox{\rm EO}_{2m}(R[T],(T)).

Moreover
, if
\sigma(1)=\sigma
, then we have

\delta V=V\sigma
and
\sigma^{-1}(\delta\perp I_{2m-2n})\in\mbox{\rm EO}_{2m}(R).

Due to the size restrictions in lemma
4.8
one is not able to deduce whether a similar homotopy and commutativity principle holds in the orthogonal case. We began this study in
[
10
]
. We add a few more observations on this below.

Lemma 4.11
.

(
L.N. Vaserstein
)

(
[
18
, Theorem 3.5]
)
Let
m\geq 3
and
R

be a local ring,
\frac{1}{2}\in R
. Then
{\rm{O}_{2m}(R)}/{\mbox{\rm EO}_{2m}(R)}={\rm{O}_{2}(R)}/{\mbox{\rm EO}_{2}(R)}=O_{2}(R).

Observation 4.12
.

Every element
\alpha\in\rm{O}_{2}(R)
is either of the type
\begin{bmatrix}u&0\\
0&u^{-1}\\
\end{bmatrix}
or of the type
\begin{bmatrix}0&u\\
u^{-1}&0\\
\end{bmatrix}
for some
u\in R^{\ast}.

Theorem 4.13
.

Let
R
be a local ring,
m\geq 3
and
1/2\in R
. Then we have,

([\rm{O}_{2m}(R[X]),\rm{O}_{2m}(R[X])]\perp I_{2})\subseteq\mbox{\rm EO}_{2m+2}(R[X]).

{{\bf Proof:}}
Let
\alpha(X),\beta(X)\in\rm{O}_{2m}(R[X])
, we need to prove that
([\alpha(X),\beta(X)]\perp I_{2})\in\mbox{\rm EO}_{2m+2}(R[X]).
Define,

\gamma(X,T)=[\alpha(XT)\perp I_{2},\beta(X)\perp I_{2}]

For every maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of
R[X]
, we have

\gamma(X,T)_{\mathfrak{m}}=[(\alpha(XT)\perp I_{2})_{\mathfrak{m}},(\beta(X)\perp I_{2})_{\mathfrak{m}}].
In view of
lemma
4.11
,
(\beta(X)\perp I_{2})_{\mathfrak{m}}=(I_{2m}\perp\delta(X))\varepsilon(X)

for some
\delta(X)\in\rm{O}_{2}(R[X]_{\mathfrak{m}})
and
\varepsilon(X)\in\mbox{\rm EO}_{2m+2}(R[X])_{\mathfrak{m}}.

By observation
4.12
, either
\delta(X)=\begin{bmatrix}u&0\\
0&u^{-1}\\
\end{bmatrix}

or
\delta(X)=\begin{bmatrix}0&u\\
u^{-1}&0\\
\end{bmatrix},~\mbox{for~some}~u\in R[X]_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\ast}.
Therefore

\gamma(X,T)_{\mathfrak{m}}\in\mbox{\rm EO}_{2m+2}(R[X]_{\mathfrak{m}}[T]).

Now,
\gamma(X,0)=[\alpha(0)\perp I_{2},\beta(X)\perp I_{2}].
Since
R
is a local ring, by lemma

4.11
,
\alpha(0)\perp I_{2}=(I_{2m}\perp\delta)\varepsilon_{1}
for
\delta\in\rm{O}_{2}(R)
and
\varepsilon_{1}\in\mbox{\rm EO}_{2m+2}(R).
By
observation
4.12
, either
\delta=\begin{bmatrix}a&0\\
0&a^{-1}\\
\end{bmatrix}~or~\begin{bmatrix}0&a\\
a^{-1}&0\\
\end{bmatrix},~\mbox{for~some}~a\in R^{\ast}.
Therefore,

\gamma(X,0)\in\mbox{\rm EO}_{2m+2}(R[X]).
Now by local-global principle for othogonal groups
(
[
17
, Theorem 4.2]
)
,
we have

\gamma(X,1)=[\alpha(X)\perp I_{2},\beta(X)\perp I_{2}]=([\alpha(X),\beta(X)]\perp I_{2})\in\mbox{\rm EO}_{2m+2}(R[X]).

~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\hfill\Box

Notation 4.14
.

We will denote set of all special orthogonal matrices which are special orthogonally homotopic to identity by
\rm{HSO}_{2m}(R).

Theorem 4.15
.

Let
m\geq 2
and
R
be a commutative ring,
\frac{1}{2}\in R
. Then,

[\rm{HSO}_{2m}(R)\perp I_{2},\rm{O}_{2m}(R)\perp I_{2}]\subseteq\mbox{\rm EO}_{2m+2}(R).

{{\bf Proof:}}
Let
\alpha\in\rm{HSO}_{2m}(R),\beta\in\rm{O}_{2m}(R)
, we need to prove that
[\alpha\perp I_{2},\beta\perp I_{2}]\in\mbox{\rm EO}_{2m+2}(R).
Let
\alpha(T)
be a homotopy of
\alpha
and define,

\gamma(T)=[\alpha(T)\perp I_{2},\beta\perp I_{2}].

Clearly,
\gamma(0)=Id.
For every maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of
R
, we have

\gamma(T)_{\mathfrak{m}}=[(\alpha(T)\perp I_{2})_{\mathfrak{m}},(\beta\perp I_{2})_{\mathfrak{m}}].

In view of lemma

4.11
,
(\beta\perp I_{2})_{\mathfrak{m}}=(I_{2m}\perp\delta)\varepsilon
for some
\delta\in\rm{O}_{2}(R_{\mathfrak{m}})
and
\varepsilon\in\mbox{\rm EO}_{2m+2}(R_{\mathfrak{m}}).

By observation
4.12
, either
\delta=\begin{bmatrix}a&0\\
0&a^{-1}\\
\end{bmatrix}~or~\begin{bmatrix}0&a\\
a^{-1}&0\\
\end{bmatrix},~\mbox{for~some}~a\in R_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\ast}.
Therefore,

\gamma(T)_{\mathfrak{m}}\in\mbox{\rm EO}_{2m}(R_{\mathfrak{m}}[T]).
In view of local-global principle for
orthogonal groups
(
[
17
, Theorem 4.2]
)
, we have

\gamma(T)\in\mbox{\rm EO}_{2m+2}(R[T]).
Therefore,
\gamma(1)=[\alpha\perp I_{2},\beta\perp I_{2}]\in\mbox{\rm EO}_{2m+2}(R).

~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\hfill\Box

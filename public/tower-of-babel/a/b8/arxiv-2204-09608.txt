1.
Introduction

Let
E
be an elliptic curve over the rationals. The systematic construction of rational points on
E
, akin to the construction of Heegner points when
{\rm ord}_{s=1}L(E,s)\leq 1
in the works of Gross–Zagier and Kolyvagin from the 1980s, is a well-known open problem lying behind further progress on the Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture in situations of higher order of vanishing. As an approximation to this problem, and following the reformulation and vast generalisation of the conjecture by Bloch–Kato, one might also attempt to construct non-torsion Selmer classes in such situations.

Suppose
L(E,s)
has sign
+1
in its functional equation and vanishes at
s=1
(so
{\rm ord}_{s=1}L(E,s)\geq 2
). Fix a prime
p\geq 5
, and suppose
E
has CM by an imaginary quadratic field in which
p
splits. In this paper, we introduce a
generalised Kato class

\kappa_{p}\in{\rm Sel}(\mathbf{Q},V_{p}E)
in the
p
-adic Selmer group fitting into the exact sequence

0\rightarrow E(\mathbf{Q})\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}_{p}}\mathbf{Q}_{p}\rightarrow{\rm Sel}(\mathbf{Q},V_{p}E)\rightarrow\mathbf{Q}_{p}\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}_{p}}{\rm Ta}_{p}\Sha(E/\mathbf{Q})\rightarrow 0,

where
{\rm Ta}_{p}\Sha(E/\mathbf{Q})
is the
p
-adic Tate module for the Shafarevich–Tate group of
E
. The class
\kappa_{p}
is obtained from a “twisted variant” of a construction due to Darmon–Rotger
[
DR16
]
, whence the terminology. One of the main results in this paper is a proof of the following rank two analogue of Kolyvagin’s result
[
Kol88
]
:

\kappa_{p}\neq 0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad{\rm dim}_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}}{\rm Sel}(\mathbf{Q},V_{p}E)=2.

Conversely, still in the setting that
L(E,s)
vanishes to positive even order at
s=1
, we show that if
{\rm dim}_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}}{\rm Sel}(\mathbf{Q},V_{p}E)=2
then
\kappa_{p}\neq 0

if and only if
the restriction map

{\rm Sel}(\mathbf{Q},V_{p}E)\rightarrow E(\mathbf{Q}_{p})\hat{\otimes}\mathbf{Q}_{p}

is nonzero, where
E(\mathbf{Q}_{p})\hat{\otimes}\mathbf{Q}_{p}
is the
p
-adic completion
\varprojlim_{n}E(\mathbf{Q}_{p})/p^{n}E(\mathbf{Q}_{p})
tensored with
\mathbf{Q}_{p}
.

In the non-CM case, similar results (for the generalised Kato classes
\kappa_{p}^{\rm DR}
introduced in
[
DR16
]
) were obtained in a joint work for the author with M.-L. Hsieh
[
CH22
]
. The approach introduced in this paper to handle the CM case (a case that is essentially excluded by the approach in
loc. cit.
; for instance, it requires
E
to have some prime
\ell\neq p
of multiplicative reduction) also yields a new proof of the original results in the non-CM case (see
[
Cas23
]
).

In the rest of this Introduction we explain our results more precisely, and some key ideas behind the proof.

1.1.
Diagonal cycle main conjecture

Following the work of Darmon–Rotger
[
DR17
]
, as further systematically developed also by Bertolini–Seveso–Venerucci (see
[
DR22
,
BSV22
]
), attached to a triple of Hida families
({\boldsymbol{\varphi}},{\boldsymbol{g}},{\boldsymbol{h}})
with tame characters having product
\chi_{\varphi}\chi_{g}\chi_{h}={1}
one has a
big diagonal class

\kappa({\boldsymbol{\varphi}},{\boldsymbol{g}},{\boldsymbol{h}})\in{\mathrm{H}}^{1}(\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{V}^{\dagger}),

where
\mathbf{V}^{\dagger}
is a self-dual twist of the triple tensor product of the Galois representations associated to
{\boldsymbol{\varphi}},{\boldsymbol{g}},{\boldsymbol{h}}
. It follows from its geometric construction (interpolating
p
-adic étale Abel–Jacobi images of generalised Gross–Kudla–Schoen diagonal cycles in
p
-adic families) that
\kappa({\boldsymbol{\varphi}},{\boldsymbol{g}},{\boldsymbol{h}})
lands in the
balanced
Selmer group
{\rm Sel}^{\rm bal}(\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{V}^{\dagger})
.

Denote by
\mathcal{R}=\mathbb{I}_{\varphi}\hat{\otimes}_{\mathscr{O}}\mathbb{I}_{g}\hat{\otimes}_{\mathscr{O}}\mathbb{I}_{h}
the completed tensor product of the ring of definition of
{\boldsymbol{\varphi}},{\boldsymbol{g}},{\boldsymbol{h}}
; this is a finite extension of the three-variable Iwasawa algebra
\Lambda\hat{\otimes}_{\mathscr{O}}\Lambda\hat{\otimes}_{\mathscr{O}}\Lambda
, where
\Lambda=\mathscr{O}\llbracket{1+p\mathbf{Z}_{p}}\rrbracket
and
\mathscr{O}
is a finite extension of
\mathbf{Z}_{p}
. Under the root number condition

\varepsilon_{\ell}(\mathbf{V}_{\underline{Q}_{0}})=+1\quad\textrm{for all primes}\quad\ell\mid N_{\varphi}N_{g}N_{h}

(see (
2.4
) for the unexplained notation), the range of specialisations
\underline{Q}\in{\rm Spec}(\mathcal{R})(\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{p})
for which
\kappa({\boldsymbol{\varphi}},{\boldsymbol{g}},{\boldsymbol{h}})
recovers a generalised Gross–Kudla–Schoen diagonal cycle, is also a range in which the sign of the triple product
L
-function
L(\mathbf{V}_{\underline{Q}}^{\dagger},s)
is
-1
.

Motivated by Perrin-Riou’s Heegner point main conjecture
[
PR87
]
(and more precisely, Howard’s extension in the context of big Heegner points
[
How07
]
), the following is expected about
\kappa({\boldsymbol{\varphi}},{\boldsymbol{g}},{\boldsymbol{h}})
:

Conjecture A

(Big diagonal class main conjecture)
.

The class
\kappa({\boldsymbol{\varphi}},{\boldsymbol{g}},{\boldsymbol{h}})
is not
\mathcal{R}
-torsion, the modules
{\rm Sel}^{\rm bal}(\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{V}^{\dagger})
and
X^{\rm bal}(\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{A}^{\dagger})
both have
\mathcal{R}
-rank one, and

{\rm char}_{\mathcal{R}}\bigl(X^{\rm bal}(\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{A}^{\dagger})_{\rm tors}\bigr)={\rm char}_{\mathcal{R}}\biggl(\frac{{\rm Sel}^{\rm bal}(\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{V}^{\dagger})}{\mathcal{R}\cdot\kappa({\boldsymbol{\varphi}},{\boldsymbol{g}},{\boldsymbol{h}})}\biggr)^{2}

in
\mathcal{R}\otimes\mathbf{Q}_{p}
, where the subscript
{\rm tors}
denotes the
\mathcal{R}
-torsion submodule.

Here
X^{\rm bal}(\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{A}^{\dagger})={\rm Hom}({\rm Sel}(\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{A}^{\dagger}),\mathbf{Q}_{p}/\mathbf{Z}_{p})
is the Pontryagin dual of the balanced Selmer group with coefficients in
\mathbf{A}^{\dagger}={\rm Hom}_{\mathbf{Z}_{p}}(\mathbf{V}^{\dagger},\mu_{p^{\infty}})
(see
\lx@sectionsign\ref{subsec:Sel-triple}
).

When the Hida families
{\boldsymbol{g}},{\boldsymbol{h}}
are specialised to classical modular forms
g,h
of weights
l,m\geq 2
with
l\equiv m\pmod{2}
and
{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}
is a CM Hida family, denoting by
\mathbb{V}^{\dagger}_{{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}gh}
the resulting specialisation of
\mathbf{V}^{\dagger}
, the divisibility

{\rm char}_{\mathcal{R}_{{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}gh}}\bigl(X^{\rm bal}(\mathbf{Q},\mathbb{A}_{{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}gh}^{\dagger})_{\rm tors}\bigr)\supset{\rm char}_{\Lambda_{\rm ac}}\biggl(\frac{{\rm Sel}^{\rm bal}(\mathbf{Q},\mathbb{V}_{{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}gh}^{\dagger})}{\mathcal{R}_{{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}gh}\cdot\kappa({\boldsymbol{\varphi}},g,h)}\biggr)^{2}

in
\mathcal{R}_{{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}gh}\otimes\mathbf{Q}_{p}

was proved under some hypotheses in
[
ACR23b
, Thm. 9.10]
by constructing an anticyclotomic Euler system (in the sense of Jetchev–Nekovář–Skinner
[
JNS
]
) having the specialised big diagonal class
\kappa({\boldsymbol{\varphi}},g,h)
as its bottom class. On the other hand, closer to the setting of this paper, when
{\boldsymbol{\varphi}}
is specialised to a classical modular form
\varphi
of even weight
k\geq 2
and
{\boldsymbol{g}},{\boldsymbol{h}}
are both CM Hida families with respect to the same imaginary quadratic field, a similar divisibility is obtained in
[
CD23
, Thm. 3.3.3]
by building an anticyclotomic Euler system containing the resulting specialisation of
\kappa({\boldsymbol{\varphi}},{\boldsymbol{g}},{\boldsymbol{h}})
as its bottom class. However, both of these results are subject to a “big image” hypothesis which in particular for the results of
[
CD23
]
excludes the case where
\varphi
has CM.

The first main result of this paper is the proof of a two-variable specialisation of Conjecture
A
in the CM case. We consider the case in which
\varphi=\theta(\lambda_{0})\in S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(N_{\varphi}))
has CM by an imaginary quadratic field
K
in which
p=\mathfrak{p}\overline{\mathfrak{p}}
splits, and

{\boldsymbol{g}}=\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\lambda_{1}}(S_{1}),\quad{\boldsymbol{h}}=\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\lambda_{2}}(S_{2})

are both CM Hida families by the same
K
(see
\lx@sectionsign\ref{subsec:CM-hida}
for the construction of
\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\lambda_{i}}(S_{i})
).
Here
\lambda_{0}
(resp.
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}
) is a Hecke character of
K
of infinity type
(-1,0)
(resp. finite order). We assume the conductor
N_{\varphi}
of
\varphi
is prime-to-
p
, and let
N_{g},N_{h}
denote the tame conductor of
{\boldsymbol{g}},{\boldsymbol{h}}
, respectively.

Letting
\mathbb{V}_{\varphi{\boldsymbol{g}}{\boldsymbol{h}}}^{\dagger}
denote the resulting two-variable specialisation of
\mathbf{V}^{\dagger}
, under mild hypotheses on
{\boldsymbol{g}}
and
{\boldsymbol{h}}
(see Proposition
4.3.1
) it is easy to see that

(1.1)

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}_{\varphi{\boldsymbol{g}}{\boldsymbol{h}}}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle\simeq{\rm Ind}_{K}^{\mathbf{Q}}\bigl(\lambda_{0}^{-1}\lambda_{1}^{-1}\lambda_{2}^{-1}\Psi_{W_{1}}^{1-\mathbf{c}}\bigr)\oplus{\rm Ind}_{K}^{\mathbf{Q}}\bigl(\lambda_{0}^{-1}\lambda_{1}^{-\mathbf{c}}\lambda_{2}^{-\mathbf{c}}\Psi_{W_{1}}^{\mathbf{c}-1}\bigr)

\displaystyle\oplus{\rm Ind}_{K}^{\mathbf{Q}}\bigl(\lambda_{0}^{-1}\lambda_{1}^{-1}\lambda_{2}^{-\mathbf{c}}\Psi_{W_{2}}^{1-\mathbf{c}}\bigr)\oplus{\rm Ind}_{K}^{\mathbf{Q}}\bigl(\lambda_{0}^{-1}\lambda_{1}^{-\mathbf{c}}\lambda_{2}^{-1}\Psi_{W_{2}}^{\mathbf{c}-1}\bigr),

where
W_{1}=(1+S_{1})^{1/2}(1+S_{2})^{1/2}-1
and

W_{2}=(1+S_{1})^{1/2}(1+S_{2})^{-1/2}-1
are formal variables parametrising anticyclotomic weight space. By the self-duality condition
\chi_{\varphi}\chi_{g}\chi_{h}=1
, the Hecke character

\lambda:=\lambda_{0}\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}

is self-dual, and so are the other three characters appearing in (
1.1
). Let
{\rm sign}(\lambda)\in\{\pm{1}\}
be the sign in the functional equation for the Hecke
L
-function
L(\lambda^{-1},s)
(with center at
s=0
), and similarly for the other three characters

\lambda_{0}\lambda_{1}^{\mathbf{c}}\lambda_{2}^{\mathbf{c}}=\lambda(\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2})^{\mathbf{c}-1},\quad\lambda_{0}\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}^{\mathbf{c}}=\lambda\lambda_{2}^{\mathbf{c}-1},\quad\lambda_{0}\lambda_{1}^{\mathbf{c}}\lambda_{2}=\lambda\lambda_{1}^{\mathbf{c}-1}

appearing in (
1.1
).
The theta series
\varphi
can be obtained as a weight
2
specialisation of a unique CM Hida family
\boldsymbol{\varphi}
, and we let
\mathcal{R}_{\varphi{\boldsymbol{g}}{\boldsymbol{h}}}\simeq\mathscr{O}\llbracket{S_{1},S_{2}}\rrbracket
be the resulting specialisation of the coefficient ring
\mathcal{R}
. Denote by
\bar{\rho}_{{\boldsymbol{g}}},\bar{\rho}_{{\boldsymbol{h}}}
the residual Galois representations associated to
{\boldsymbol{g}},{\boldsymbol{h}}
, respectively.

Theorem A
.

Let the triple

(\varphi,{\boldsymbol{g}},{\boldsymbol{h}})=(\theta(\lambda_{0}),\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\lambda_{1}}(S_{1}),\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\lambda_{2}}(S_{2}))

be as above, and suppose that:

(i)

{\rm gcd}(N_{\varphi},N_{g},N_{h})
is squarefree.

(ii)

\bar{\rho}_{{\boldsymbol{g}}},\bar{\rho}_{{\boldsymbol{h}}}
are both absolutely irreducible and
p
-distinguished.

(iii)

{\rm sign}(\lambda)={\rm sign}(\lambda(\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2})^{\mathbf{c}-1})={\rm sign}(\lambda\lambda_{2}^{\mathbf{c}-1})={\rm sign}(\lambda\lambda_{1}^{\mathbf{c}-1})=+1
.

Then
\kappa(\varphi,{\boldsymbol{g}},{\boldsymbol{h}})
is not
\mathcal{R}_{\varphi{\boldsymbol{g}}{\boldsymbol{h}}}
-torsion, the modules
{\rm Sel}^{\rm bal}(\mathbf{Q},\mathbb{V}_{\varphi{\boldsymbol{g}}{\boldsymbol{h}}}^{\dagger})
and
X^{\rm bal}(\mathbf{Q},\mathbb{A}_{\varphi{\boldsymbol{g}}{\boldsymbol{h}}}^{\dagger})
both have
\mathcal{R}_{\varphi{\boldsymbol{g}}{\boldsymbol{h}}}
-rank one, and

{\rm char}_{\mathcal{R}_{\varphi{\boldsymbol{g}}{\boldsymbol{h}}}}\bigl(X^{\rm bal}(\mathbf{Q},\mathbb{A}_{\varphi{\boldsymbol{g}}{\boldsymbol{h}}}^{\dagger})_{\rm tors}\bigr)={\rm char}_{\mathcal{R}_{\varphi{\boldsymbol{g}}{\boldsymbol{h}}}}\biggl(\frac{{\rm Sel}^{\rm bal}(\mathbf{Q},\mathbb{V}_{\varphi{\boldsymbol{g}}{\boldsymbol{h}}}^{\dagger})}{\mathcal{R}_{\varphi{\boldsymbol{g}}{\boldsymbol{h}}}\cdot\kappa(\varphi,{\boldsymbol{g}},{\boldsymbol{h}})}\biggr)^{2}

in
\mathcal{R}_{\varphi{\boldsymbol{g}}{\boldsymbol{h}}}\otimes\mathbf{Q}_{p}
. In other words, Conjecture
A
holds for
(\varphi,{\boldsymbol{g}},{\boldsymbol{h}})
.

A key input in the proof of this result is the relation between Conjecture
A
and the anticyclotomic main conjecture for Hecke characters. Indeed, in the setting of Theorem
A
, we show that the balanced Selmer group
{\rm Sel}^{\rm bal}(\mathbf{Q},\mathbb{V}_{\varphi{\boldsymbol{g}}{\boldsymbol{h}}}^{\dagger})
decomposes as

(1.2)

\displaystyle{\rm Sel}^{\rm bal}(\mathbf{Q},\mathbb{V}_{\varphi{\boldsymbol{g}}{\boldsymbol{h}}}^{\dagger})

\displaystyle\simeq{\rm Sel}_{\emptyset,0}(K,T_{\lambda}\otimes\Psi_{W_{1}}^{1-\mathbf{c}})\oplus{\rm Sel}_{0,\emptyset}(K,T_{\lambda(\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2})^{\mathbf{c}-1}}\otimes\Psi_{W_{1}}^{\mathbf{c}-1})

\displaystyle\oplus{\rm Sel}_{\emptyset,0}(K,T_{\lambda\lambda_{2}^{\mathbf{c}-1}}\otimes\Psi_{W_{2}}^{1-\mathbf{c}})\oplus{\rm Sel}_{\emptyset,0}(K,T_{\lambda\lambda_{1}^{\mathbf{c}-1}}\otimes\Psi_{W_{2}}^{\mathbf{c}-1}).

The Selmer groups in the right-hand side of this decomposition correspond to usual (i.e., Bloch–Kato) anticyclotomic Selmer groups attached to Hecke characters, except for the Selmer group

{\rm Sel}_{0,\emptyset}(K,T_{\lambda(\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2})^{\mathbf{c}-1}}\otimes\Psi_{W_{1}}^{\mathbf{c}-1}),

which is obtained from the usual anticyclotomic Selmer group for
T_{\lambda(\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2})^{\mathbf{c}-1}}
by
reversing
the local conditions at the primes above
\mathfrak{p}
and
\overline{\mathfrak{p}}
.
We exploit the fact that Conjecture
A
follows from the Iwasawa–Greenberg main conjecture for the
p
-adic triple product
L
-function
\mathscr{L}_{p}^{\varphi}(\varphi,{\boldsymbol{g}},{\boldsymbol{h}})\in\mathcal{R}_{\varphi{\boldsymbol{g}}{\boldsymbol{h}}}
constructed by Hsieh
[
Hsi21
]
. Under certain conditions preventing the vanishing of
\mathscr{L}_{p}^{\varphi}(\varphi,{\boldsymbol{g}},{\boldsymbol{h}})
for sign reasons, this main conjecture predicts that the
\varphi
-unbalanced
Selmer group
X^{\varphi}(\mathbf{Q},\mathbb{A}_{\varphi{\boldsymbol{g}}{\boldsymbol{h}}}^{\dagger})
is
\mathcal{R}_{\varphi{\boldsymbol{g}}{\boldsymbol{h}}}
-torsion, with characteristic ideal generated by
\mathscr{L}_{p}^{\varphi}(\varphi,{\boldsymbol{g}},{\boldsymbol{h}})^{2}
. We prove a decomposition for
X^{\varphi}(\mathbf{Q},\mathbb{A}_{\varphi{\boldsymbol{g}}{\boldsymbol{h}}}^{\dagger})
analogous to (
1.2
) in which (contrary to the case of the balanced Selmer groups)
all
direct summands agree with classical anticyclotomic Selmer groups for Hecke characters. Together with a parallel factorisation for
\mathscr{L}_{p}^{\varphi}(\varphi,{\boldsymbol{g}},{\boldsymbol{h}})^{2}
into a product of four anticyclotomic Katz
p
-adic
L
-functions, we thus deduce from the works of Agboola–Howard
[
AH06
]
and Arnold
[
Arn07
]
(an anticyclotomic specialision of Rubin’s proof of the Iwasawa main conjecture for
K

[
Rub91
]
) a proof of the Iwasawa–Greenberg main conjecture for
X^{\varphi}(\mathbf{Q},\mathbb{A}^{\dagger}_{\varphi{\boldsymbol{g}}{\boldsymbol{h}}})
. (The sign conditions in (iii) of Theorem
A

are needed at this point,
as otherwise our results imply that
X^{\varphi}(\mathbf{Q},\mathbb{A}_{\varphi{\boldsymbol{g}}{\boldsymbol{h}}}^{\dagger})
has
\mathcal{R}_{\varphi{\boldsymbol{g}}{\boldsymbol{h}}}
-rank two.) The proof of Theorem
A
then follows.

1.2.
Generalised Kato classes attached to
E

We keep
K
to be an imaginary quadratic field in which
p=\mathfrak{p}\overline{\mathfrak{p}}
splits, and now let
E/\mathbf{Q}
be an elliptic curve with CM by the ring of integers of
K
. Note that the splitting condition on
p
implies that
E
has good ordinary reduction of
p
. Let
\psi_{E}
be the Hecke character of
K
attached to
E
, so that

L(E,s)=L(\psi_{E},s).

Suppose the sign is the functional equation of
L(E,s)
is
w=+1
.
In the second part of the paper, we choose Hecke characters
\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}
for
K
as above satisfying

(1.3)

\psi_{E}=\lambda_{0}\lambda_{1}^{\mathbf{c}}\lambda_{2}^{\mathbf{c}},\quad L(\psi_{E}^{-1}\lambda_{1}^{\mathbf{c}-1},0)\cdot L(\psi_{E}^{-1}\lambda_{2}^{\mathbf{c}-1},0)\cdot L(\psi_{E}^{-1}(\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2})^{\mathbf{c}-1},0)\neq 0,

whose existence follows from nonvanishing results due to Greenberg and Rohrlich
[
Gre85
,
Roh84
]
, and — inspired by a construction of generalised Kato classes due to Darmon–Rotger
[
DR17
,
DR16
]
— we let

\kappa_{p}\in{\mathrm{H}}^{1}(\mathbf{Q},V_{p}E)

be the image of the resulting big diagonal class
\kappa(\varphi,{\boldsymbol{g}},{\boldsymbol{h}})
(for
(\varphi,{\boldsymbol{g}},{\boldsymbol{h}})=(\theta(\lambda_{0}),\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\lambda_{1}}(S_{1}),\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\lambda_{2}}(S_{2}))
as above) under the composition

{\mathrm{H}}^{1}(\mathbf{Q},\mathbb{V}_{\varphi{\boldsymbol{g}}{\boldsymbol{h}}}^{\dagger})\rightarrow{\mathrm{H}}^{1}\bigl(\mathbf{Q},{\rm Ind}_{K}^{\mathbf{Q}}(T_{\lambda_{0}\lambda_{1}^{\mathbf{c}}\lambda_{2}^{\mathbf{c}}}\otimes\Psi_{W_{1}}^{\mathbf{c}-1})\bigr)\rightarrow{\mathrm{H}}^{1}(\mathbf{Q},{\rm Ind}_{K}^{\mathbf{Q}}(T_{\psi_{E}}))\simeq{\mathrm{H}}^{1}(\mathbf{Q},T_{p}E)

arising from projection onto the second direct summand in (
1.1
) and the specialisation at
W_{1}=0
. The construction of
\kappa_{p}
might be seen as a twisted variant of the construction of geneneralised Kato classes in
[
DR16
]
, which for an elliptic curve
E/\mathbf{Q}
as above would take
\lambda_{0}=\psi_{E}
and the finite order Hecke characters
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}
to be inverses of each other (similarly as in
[
CH22
]
).
Nevertheless, from the explicit reciprocity law of
[
DR22
]
and
[
BSV22
]
we deduce the implication

L(E,1)=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\kappa_{p}\in{\rm Sel}(\mathbf{Q},V_{p}E).

Since we assume that
L(E,s)
has sign
+1
, the vanishing of
L(E,1)
implies that
{\rm ord}_{s=1}L(E,s)\geq 2
, and so by the Bloch–Kato conjecture
[
BK90
]
, the Selmer group
{\rm Sel}(\mathbf{Q},V_{p}E)
is expected to be at least
2
-dimensional. Our next result is consistent with this expectation, and further justifies the view of
\kappa_{p}
as a “rank
2

p
-adic regulator”.

Theorem B
.

Suppose
L(E,s)
vanishes to positive even order at
s=1
.
Let
\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}
be any triple of Hecke characters of
K
as above satisfying (
1.3
) and the conditions (i)–(iii) in Theorem
A
, and let
\kappa_{p}
be the associated generalised Kato class. Then

\kappa_{p}\neq 0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad{\rm dim}_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}}{\rm Sel}(\mathbf{Q},V_{p}E)=2.

Conversely, if
{\rm dim}_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}}{\rm Sel}(\mathbf{Q},V_{p}E)=2
then
\kappa_{p}\neq 0
if and only if the restriction map

{\rm res}_{p}:{\rm Sel}(\mathbf{Q},V_{p}E)\rightarrow E(\mathbf{Q}_{p})\hat{\otimes}\mathbf{Q}_{p}

is nonzero.

The existence of (infinitely many) triples
\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}
satisfying (
1.3
) and the conditions in Theorem
A
follows easily from the aforementioned nonvanishing results due to Greenberg and Rorhlich (note that the sign condition (iii) is implied by (
1.3
)).

In addition to Theorem
A
, the ingredients in the proof of Theorem
B
are a version of Mazur’s control theorem for the Selmer groups in the decomposition (
1.1
) and a global duality argument allowing us to relate the rank of
{\rm Sel}_{0,\emptyset}(K,T_{\psi_{E}})
and
{\rm dim}_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}}{\rm Sel}(\mathbf{Q},V_{p}E)
.

The result of Theorem
B
is consistent with predictions by Darmon–Rotger
[
DR16
]
, and it offers some new insights. More precisely, that the conditions
{\rm dim}_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}}{\rm Sel}(\mathbf{Q},V_{p}E)=2
and
{\rm res}_{p}\neq 0
imply
\kappa_{p}\neq 0
is suggested by [
op. cit.
, Conj. 3.12] (in the “rank
(2,0)
setting” of
\lx@sectionsign{4.5.3}
); a new insight of Theorem
B
is that the condition
{\rm res}_{p}\neq 0
is also
necessary
for the nonvanishing of
\kappa_{p}
.

1.3.
Application to rank two Selmer basis

The construction of
\kappa_{p}\in{\rm Sel}(\mathbf{Q},V_{p}E)
depends on a choice
\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}
, but it follows from our results that different choices give rise to the same Selmer class up to scaling. (In fact, whenever nonzero,
\kappa_{p}
generates the one-dimensional subspace
{\rm ker}({\rm res}_{p})\subset{\rm Sel}(\mathbf{Q},V_{p}E)
.)
It is then natural to ask for a class in the two-dimensional
{\rm Sel}(\mathbf{Q},V_{p}E)
complementary to the line spanned by
\kappa_{p}
. Our results also yield an answer to this question under some hypotheses.

Let
{\mathcal{W}}
the completion of the ring of integers of the maximal unramified extension of
\mathbf{Q}_{p}
, and let
{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathfrak{p}}={\mathcal{L}}_{\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{f}}\in{\mathcal{W}}\llbracket{Z(\mathfrak{f})}\rrbracket
be the Katz
p
-adic
L
-function recalled in Theorem
2.1.1
below, where
Z(\mathfrak{f})
is the Galois group of the extension
K(E[p^{\infty}])/K
and
\mathfrak{f}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}
is the conductor of
\psi_{E}
. For
s\in\mathbf{Z}_{p}
define

L_{\mathfrak{p}}(s)={\mathcal{L}}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\psi_{E}\langle\psi_{E}\rangle^{s-1}),\quad L_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}(s)={\mathcal{L}}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\psi_{E}^{\mathbf{c}}\rangle\psi_{E}^{\mathbf{c}}\rangle^{s-1}),

where
\langle-\rangle:\mathbf{Z}_{p}^{\times}\rightarrow 1+p\mathbf{Z}_{p}
is the projection onto the
1
-units.

Theorem C
.

Let the hypotheses be as in Theorem
B
, and assume in addition that
\Sha(E/\mathbf{Q})[p^{\infty}]
is finite and the following conditions hold:

{\rm ord}_{s=1}L_{\mathfrak{p}}(s)=2,\quad{\rm ord}_{s=1}L_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}(s)=1.

Then
{\rm Sel}(\mathbf{Q},V_{p}E)
is
2
-dimensional, with

{\rm Sel}(\mathbf{Q},V_{p}E)=\mathbf{Q}_{p}\kappa_{p}\oplus\mathbf{Q}_{p}x_{\mathfrak{p}}^{(2)},

where
\kappa_{p}
is a generalised Kato class and
x_{\mathfrak{p}}^{(2)}
is a “derived” elliptic unit.

Proof.

The assumption that
{\rm ord}_{s=1}L_{\mathfrak{p}}(s)=2
implies that

r:={\rm dim}_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}}{\rm Sel}(\mathbf{Q},V_{p}E)\leq 2

by Rubin’s proof of the Iwasawa main conjecture for
K
and the work of Perrin-Riou (see
[
Rub91
, Thm. 4.1]
and
[
PR84
, Ch. IV, Thm. 22]
). On the other hand, it also implies
r\geq 2
by
the theorem of Coates–Wiles
[
CW77
]
and the
p
-parity conjecture
[
Guo93
]
. Therefore
r=2
, and by
[
Rub92
, Prop. 4.4]
the construction of derived elliptic units in [
op. cit.
, §6] yields a class

x_{\mathfrak{p}}^{(2)}\in{\rm Sel}(K,T_{\mathfrak{p}}E)\simeq{\rm Sel}(\mathbf{Q},T_{p}E)

where
T_{\mathfrak{p}}E
is the
\mathfrak{p}
-adic Tate module of
E
.
Since by
[
Rub92
, Thm. 9.5(ii)]
and our assumptions we have
{\rm res}_{p}(x_{\mathfrak{p}}^{(2)})\neq 0
, the result follows from Theorem
B
.
∎

Denote by

is the Artin symbol of

Theorem 2.1.1
.

There exists an element

such that for all Hecke characters

\mathfrak{C}p^{\infty}
and infinity type

and

L
-function of
\chi
deprived from the Euler factors at the primes dividing
p\mathfrak{C}
. Moreover, we have the functional equation

{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{C}}(\chi)={\mathcal{L}}_{\mathfrak{p},\overline{\mathfrak{C}}}((\chi^{\mathbf{c}})^{-1}\mathbf{N}^{-1}),

where
\chi^{\mathbf{c}}
is the composition of
\chi
with the action of the non-trivial element
\mathbf{c}\in{\rm Aut}(K/\mathbf{Q})
, and the equality is up to a
p
-adic unit.

Proof.

See
[
Kat78
]
,
[
HT93
]

We review the construction of CM Hida families following the exposition in

Since it will suffice for our purposes, we assume that the class number

\mathfrak{C}
prime to
p
, and for any finite order character

of tame conductor

-torsion with

{\rm char}_{\Lambda}\bigl(X^{{\varphi}}(\mathbf{Q},{\mathbb{A}_{\varphi\underline{\boldsymbol{g}\boldsymbol{h}}}^{\dagger}})\bigr)=\bigl(\mathscr{L}_{p}^{\varphi}(\varphi,\underline{{\boldsymbol{g}}{\boldsymbol{h}}})\bigr)^{2}

as ideals in
\Lambda_{\mathcal{W}}\otimes\mathbf{Q}_{p}
. By Proposition
5.1.2
, it follows that

(6.3)

{\rm rank}_{\Lambda}\bigl(X^{\rm bal}(\mathbf{Q},{\mathbb{A}_{\varphi\underline{\boldsymbol{g}\boldsymbol{h}}}^{\dagger}})\big)={\rm rank}_{\Lambda}\bigl({\rm Sel}^{\rm bal}(\mathbf{Q},{\mathbb{V}_{\varphi\underline{\boldsymbol{g}\boldsymbol{h}}}^{\dagger}})\bigr)=1,

and

(6.4)

{\rm char}_{\Lambda}\bigl(X^{\rm bal}(\mathbf{Q},{\mathbb{A}_{\varphi\underline{\boldsymbol{g}\boldsymbol{h}}}^{\dagger}})_{\rm tors}\bigr)={\rm char}_{\Lambda}\biggl(\frac{{\rm Sel}^{\rm bal}(\mathbf{Q},{\mathbb{V}_{\varphi\underline{\boldsymbol{g}\boldsymbol{h}}}^{\dagger}})}{\Lambda\cdot\kappa(\varphi,\underline{{\boldsymbol{g}}{\boldsymbol{h}}})}\biggr)^{2}

as ideals in
\Lambda_{\mathcal{W}}\otimes\mathbf{Q}_{p}
. Now, the implications

\displaystyle L(\psi_{E}^{-1}\phi_{1}^{-1}\phi_{2}^{-1},0)\neq 0\quad

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow\quad{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathfrak{p},\psi_{E}\phi_{1}\phi_{2}}^{-}(0)\neq 0

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow\quad\left|X_{0,\emptyset}(K,A_{\psi_{E}^{\mathbf{c}}\phi_{1}^{\mathbf{c}}\phi_{2}^{\mathbf{c}}}\otimes\Psi_{S}^{\mathbf{c}-1})_{/S}\right|<\infty.

follow from the interpolation property of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathfrak{p},\psi_{E}\phi_{1}\phi_{2}}^{-}
and the combination of Theorem
4.1.3
and Mazur’s control theorem, respectively. The nonvanishing of
L(\psi_{E}^{-1}\phi_{1}^{-1},0)
and
L(\psi_{E}^{-1}\phi_{2}^{-1},0)
similarly implies

\left|X_{0,\emptyset}(K,A_{\phi_{E}^{\mathbf{c}}\phi_{1}^{\mathbf{c}}}\otimes\Psi_{S}^{\mathbf{c}-1})_{/S}\right|<\infty,\quad\left|X_{0,\emptyset}(K,A_{\phi_{E}^{\mathbf{c}}\phi_{2}^{\mathbf{c}}}\otimes\Psi_{S}^{\mathbf{c}-1})_{/S}\right|<\infty,

and so from (
6.3
) and the balanced Selmer group decomposition from Corollary
4.3.4
:

\displaystyle X^{\rm bal}(\mathbf{Q},{\mathbb{A}_{\varphi\underline{\boldsymbol{g}\boldsymbol{h}}}^{\dagger}})

\displaystyle\simeq X_{0,\emptyset}(K,A_{\psi_{E}^{\mathbf{c}}\phi_{1}^{\mathbf{c}}\phi_{2}^{\mathbf{c}}}\otimes\Psi_{S}^{\mathbf{c}-1})\oplus X_{\emptyset,0}(K,A_{\psi_{E}^{\mathbf{c}}}\otimes\Psi_{S}^{1-\mathbf{c}})

\displaystyle\quad\oplus X_{0,\emptyset}(K,A_{\psi_{E}^{\mathbf{c}}\phi_{1}^{\mathbf{c}}}\otimes\Psi_{S}^{\mathbf{c}-1})_{/S}\oplus X_{0,\emptyset}(K,A_{\psi_{E}^{\mathbf{c}}\phi_{2}^{\mathbf{c}}}\otimes\Psi_{S}^{1-\mathbf{c}})_{/S},

we deduce that

(6.5)

{\rm rank}_{\Lambda}\bigl(X_{\emptyset,0}(K,A_{\psi_{E}^{\mathbf{c}}}\otimes\Psi_{S}^{1-\mathbf{c}})\bigr)=1.

On the other hand, by the control theorem of Proposition
6.2.1
the
\mathcal{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}}
-rank of the specialisation
X_{\emptyset,0}(\mathbf{Q},A_{\psi_{E}^{\mathbf{c}}}\otimes\Psi_{S}^{1-\mathbf{c}})_{/S}
satisfies

(6.6)

\displaystyle{\rm rank}_{\mathcal{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}}}\bigl(X_{\emptyset,0}(K,A_{\psi_{E}^{\mathbf{c}}}\otimes\Psi_{S}^{1-\mathbf{c}})_{/S}\bigr)

\displaystyle={\rm corank}_{\mathcal{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}}}\bigl({\rm Sel}_{\emptyset,0}(K,A_{\psi_{E}^{\mathbf{c}}})\bigr)

\displaystyle={\rm rank}_{\mathcal{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}}}\bigl({\rm Sel}_{\emptyset,0}(K,T_{\psi_{E}^{\mathbf{c}}})\bigr)

\displaystyle={\rm rank}_{\mathcal{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}}}\bigl({\rm Sel}_{0,\emptyset}(K,T_{\psi_{E}})\bigr),

using the isomorphism
{\rm Sel}_{\emptyset,0}(K,T_{\psi_{E}^{\mathbf{c}}})\simeq{\rm Sel}_{0,\emptyset}(K,T_{\psi_{E}})
given by the action of complex conjugation for the last equality.

Denote by
\kappa_{E}(\varphi,\underline{{\boldsymbol{g}}{\boldsymbol{h}}})\in{\rm Sel}_{0,\emptyset}(K,T_{\psi_{E}}\otimes\Psi_{S}^{\mathbf{c}-1})
the projection of
\kappa(\varphi,\underline{{\boldsymbol{g}}{\boldsymbol{h}}})
onto the second direct summand in the balanced Selmer groups decomposition from Proposition
4.3.2
:

\displaystyle{\rm Sel}^{\rm bal}(\mathbf{Q},{\mathbb{V}_{\varphi\underline{\boldsymbol{g}\boldsymbol{h}}}^{\dagger}})

\displaystyle\simeq{\rm Sel}_{\emptyset,0}(K,T_{\psi_{E}\phi_{1}\phi_{2}}\otimes\Psi_{S}^{1-\mathbf{c}})\oplus{\rm Sel}_{0,\emptyset}(K,T_{\psi_{E}}\otimes\Psi_{S}^{\mathbf{c}-1})

\displaystyle\quad\oplus{\rm Sel}_{\emptyset,0}(K,T_{\psi_{E}\phi_{1}}\otimes\Psi_{S}^{1-\mathbf{c}})_{/S}\oplus{\rm Sel}_{\emptyset,0}(K,T_{\psi_{E}\phi_{2}}\otimes\Psi_{S}^{\mathbf{c}-1})_{/S}.

Since our assumptions imply that
\psi_{E}\phi_{1},\psi_{E}\phi_{2},\psi_{E}\phi_{1}\phi_{2}
have all root number
+1
, by Theorem
4.1.3
we have

\frac{{\rm Sel}^{\rm bal}(\mathbf{Q},{\mathbb{V}_{\varphi\underline{\boldsymbol{g}\boldsymbol{h}}}^{\dagger}})}{\Lambda\cdot\kappa(\varphi,\underline{{\boldsymbol{g}}{\boldsymbol{h}}})}\simeq\frac{{\rm Sel}_{0,\emptyset}(K,T_{\psi_{E}}\otimes\Psi_{S}^{\mathbf{c}-1})}{\Lambda\cdot\kappa_{E}(\varphi,\underline{{\boldsymbol{g}}{\boldsymbol{h}}})}.

Together with Theorem
5.2.1
(note that the choice of
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}
in Proposition
6.1.1
guarantee that the associated primtitive CM Hida families
{\boldsymbol{g}},{\boldsymbol{h}}
satisfy the conditions in that result), this gives that
{\rm Sel}_{0,\emptyset}(K,T_{\psi_{E}}\otimes\Psi_{S}^{\mathbf{c}-1})
has
\Lambda
-rank one, and we have the following equality of ideals in
\Lambda_{\mathcal{W}}\otimes\mathbf{Q}_{p}
:

(6.7)

{\rm char}_{\Lambda}\bigl(X_{\emptyset,0}(K,A_{\psi_{E}^{\mathbf{c}}}\otimes\Psi_{S}^{1-\mathbf{c}})_{\rm tors}\bigr)=\mathcal{L}_{\mathfrak{p},\psi_{E}^{\mathbf{c}}\phi_{1}^{\mathbf{c}}\phi_{2}^{\mathbf{c}}}^{-}(S)^{\iota}\cdot{\rm char}_{\Lambda}\bigl(\mathfrak{Z}_{E}\bigr)^{2},

where
\mathfrak{Z}_{E}={\rm Sel}_{0,\emptyset}(K,T_{\psi_{E}}\otimes\Psi_{S}^{\mathbf{c}-1})/\Lambda\cdot\kappa_{E}(\varphi,\underline{{\boldsymbol{g}}{\boldsymbol{h}}})
. From (
6.5
), (
6.6
), and (
6.7
), we thus see that

\displaystyle{\rm rank}_{\mathcal{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}}}\bigl({\rm Sel}_{0,\emptyset}(K,T_{\psi_{E}})\bigr)

\displaystyle=1-{\rm ord}_{S}\bigl(\mathcal{L}_{\mathfrak{p},\psi_{E}\phi_{1}\phi_{2}}^{-}(S)^{\iota}\bigr)+2\,{\rm rank}_{\mathcal{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}}}\bigl((\mathfrak{Z}_{E})_{/S}\bigr)

\displaystyle=1+2\,{\rm rank}_{\mathcal{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}}}\bigl((\mathfrak{Z}_{E})_{/S}\bigr),

using Theorem
2.1.1
and the nonvanishing of
L(\psi_{E}^{-1}\phi_{1}^{-1}\phi_{2}^{-1},0)
for the last equality.

Since by construction the injection

{\rm Sel}_{0,\emptyset}(K,T_{\psi_{E}}\otimes\Psi_{S}^{\mathbf{c}-1})_{/S}\rightarrow{\rm Sel}_{0,\emptyset}(K,T_{\psi_{E}})

of Proposition
6.2.1
sends

\kappa_{E}(\varphi,\underline{{\boldsymbol{g}}{\boldsymbol{h}}})\;{\rm mod}\;S
into
\kappa_{p}
, we conclude that

(6.8)

{\rm rank}_{\mathcal{O}_{K,\mathfrak{p}}}\bigl({\rm Sel}_{0,\emptyset}(K,T_{\psi_{E}})\bigr)=1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\kappa_{p}\neq 0.

The first claim in the Theorem now follows from (
6.8
) and Lemma
6.2.2
, noting that by the work of Rubin
[
Rub91
]
proving the Iwasawa main conjecture for
K
, the vanishing of
L(E,1)
implies the non-triviality of
{\rm Sel}(\mathbf{Q},V_{p}E)
. Similarly, the last claim in the Theorem is a direct consequence of (
6.8
) and Lemma
6.2.2
.
∎

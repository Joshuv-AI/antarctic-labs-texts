High Dimensional Expanders:

We study (weighted)

The bipartite graph corresponding to the marginal

This holds even conditioning on any (feasible) values of

Note

Basic Analysis on Hypergraphs

Denote the space of functions on
X
as
C_{d}\coloneqq\{f:X\to\mathbb{R}\}
. Assuming the weights of
X
, denoted
\Pi(x)
, form a distribution, they induce a natural expectation operator and
L_{p}
-space over
C_{d}
defined by
\mathbb{E}[f]=\sum\limits_{x\in X}\Pi(x)f(x)
and
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{p}=\mathbb{E}[|f|^{p}]^{1/p}
.
Given a linear operator
T:C_{d}\to C_{d}
, we define its
p
-norm in the standard way as
\max_{f\in C_{d}}\left\{\frac{\left\lVert Tf\right\rVert_{p}}{\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{p}}\right\}
.

It will often be useful for us to analyze
f\in C_{d}
inside
restrictions
of
X
. Formally, given a sub-assignment
x_{S}
to the marginal
X_{S}
, define
f|_{x_{S}}(x_{\bar{S}})=f(x)
to be the localization of
f
to the restricted domain
X_{\bar{S}}

conditioned on fixing
X_{S}=x_{S}
. This domain is called the link and denoted by
X_{x_{S}}
(informally, the link just fixes the
S
-coordinates to value
x_{S}
, and we think of
f|_{x_{S}}
as a function of the remaining coordinates).

Noise Operator and Total Influence:

The
noise operator

T_{\rho}:C_{d}\to C_{d}
is a classical smoothing procedure in boolean analysis which given a string
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\in X
, averages over
y
generated by re-sampling each coordinate of
x
independently with probability
1-\rho
. The
total influence
of a
\{\pm 1\}
-valued function
f\in C_{d}
measures its average sensitivity to re-sampling each
individual
coordinate

\mathbf{I}[f]=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d}\mathbb{E}_{x}[\Pr[f(x)\neq f(x^{(i)})]],

where
x^{(i)}
is generated by re-sampling the
i
th coordinate of
x
.

Fourier Analysis on Hypergraphs:

The classical theory of Fourier analysis on products is given by the
Efron-Stein Decomposition
, whose components
\{f^{=S}\}_{S\subseteq[d]}
are

f^{=S}(y)=\sum\limits_{T\subseteq S}(-1)^{|S\setminus T|}\mathbb{E}[f(x)~|~x_{T}=y_{T}].

It is easy to check that
f=\sum\limits_{S\subseteq[d]}f^{=S}
. We write
f^{\leq i}=\sum\limits_{|S|\leq i}f^{=S}
as the degree-
i
component of
f
. On product spaces,
\{f^{=S}\}
is an orthogonal eigenbasis for
T_{\rho}
:

T_{\rho}f=\sum\limits_{S\subseteq[d]}\rho^{|S|}f^{=S}.

This leads to a standard generalization of the noise operator to vectors
r\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

T_{r}f\coloneqq\sum\limits_{S\subseteq[d]}r_{S}f^{=S},

where
r_{S}=\prod\limits_{i\in S}r_{i}
. Finally, the
symmetrization
of
f
, denoted
\tilde{f}:\{\pm 1\}^{d}\times X\to\mathbb{R}
is the function

\widetilde{f}(r,x)=T_{r}f(x)=\sum\limits_{S\subseteq[d]}r_{S}f^{=S}(x).

Note
\tilde{f}
is the unique function whose
X
-restrictions
\tilde{f}|_{x}(r)=\tilde{f}(x,r)
have Fourier coefficients
f^{=S}(x)
.

Results

Our first main contribution is the following extension of Bourgain’s symmetrization theorem to HDX:

Theorem 1.1

(The Symmetrization Theorem (Informal

Let

The symmetrization theorem is a powerful tool for extending classical structure theorems on the cube to product spaces. In this work, we focus mainly on its application to the
hypercontractive inequalities
. On the boolean cube, the classical
(2{\to}q)
-hypercontractive theorem (or rather its equivalent form as the ‘Bonami Lemma’) states the degree-
i

q>2

\left\lVert f^{\leq i}\right\rVert_{q}\leq\sqrt{q-1}^{i}\left\lVert f^{\leq i}\right\rVert_{2}.

This statement has incredibly far reaching consequences in analysis, combinatorics, and theoretical computer science, including the KKL Theorem
[
48
]
and its myriad applications.

Unfortunately, the Bonami lemma is false on unbalanced product spaces. The obvious counter-examples are dictators, or more generally ‘local’ functions that are dense in some restriction. Consider, for instance, the
i
th dictator on the
p
-biased cube. While
\mathbf{1}_{i}
is a degree-
1

\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{1}_{i}^{4}]=p\gg\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{1}_{i}^{2}]^{2}=p^{2}.

This gives rise to the theory of
global
hypercontractivity that aims to show such local functions are the only counter-examples. Variants of global hypercontractivity have been known for product spaces since the 90s where they lead to the theory of sharp thresholds
[
32
]
, but tight bounds were only achieved after a long line of work culminating in the recent work of
[
52
]
(see

1.3

With this in mind, our second main contribution is a proof of global hypercontractivity for HDX via symmetrization that matches sharp bounds for products up to a log factor.

.

In this latter vein, it is worth discussing in slightly more detail the connection of our work to Bafna, Minzer, and Vyas’
[

It is also worth highlighting that due to its use of symmetrization, the proof of
Theorem

1.2
is substantially simpler than prior proofs of hypercontractivity for HDX

Coordinate-Wise Analysis on (near)-Product Spaces:

Coordinate-wise methods are among the most classical techniques for proving high dimensional analytic inequalities (see
[
68
]
). Some of these techniques, such as tensorization of variance and entropy, have already found great success in the study of approximate products and high dimensional expanders. This was first made explicit by Chen, Liu, and Vigoda
[
17
]
, though similar ideas were explored earlier by Kaufman and Mass
[
55
]
. Chen and Eldan
[
16
]
observed that many methods in the approximate sampling literature, such as spectral and entropic independence (variants of high dimensional expansion), can also be viewed from the of standpoint coordinate-wise localization schemes.

Our coordinate-wise method is somewhat different than the above approaches. First, all coordinate-wise methods in the literature beyond the study of variance strongly relied on
density
and
balance
of the underlying complex. Indeed many of these works (see e.g.
[
17
,
45
]
among others) actually prove hypercontractive inequalities for all functions, which cannot hold in the sparse setting. Second, unlike typical tensorization methods, we localize more explicitly at the level of the
operator
, splitting
T_{\rho}
itself into coordinate operators still acting over the full space
X
, then localizing one-by-one in a replacement style method. We do note that the former part of this approach was also considered in a somewhat different context in concurrent and independent work
of Alev and Parzanchevski
[
5
]
, who analyzed a coordinate-wise decomposition of the down-up walk.

1.4
Technical Overview

Our proof of the symmetrization theorem relies on two elementary new tools in the theory of high dimensional expansion. First, we’ll introduce the notion of ‘
q
-norm HDX’ which bounds the local divergence of
X
from a product in
q
-norm. Second, we introduce our coordinate-wise treatment of the noise operator and sketch how the technique is used to prove the symmetrization theorem. Finally, we sketch how symmetrization is used to prove optimal global hypercontractivity. We omit any technical overview of the booster theorem which is more technically involved but uses morally similar methods.

(q,\gamma)
-Products:

A core problem identified in
[
37
]
is that standard HDX and
\gamma
-products only bound the

[

]

q

2

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{q}^{q}\leq\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\infty}^{q-2}

We handle this and analogous issues by introducing
q
-norm HDX. Given a partite complex

denote the (normalized) bipartite adjacency matrix of
(X_{i},X_{j})
, and
\Pi_{i,j}
be
A_{i,j}

5
5

5

In this case, this is simply the matrix where every row is the marginal distribution over
X_{i}

(similarly for

Definition 1.6

(

-Products)

A

-product if for every distinct

1.

The marginal
(X_{i},X_{j})

\left\lVert A_{i,j}-\Pi_{i,j}\right\rVert_{q}\leq\gamma

2.

This holds under all feasible conditionings
X_{S}=z_{S}
.

Note that setting

exactly recovers the notion of a

-product. While
q

Lemma 1.7

(Informal

Any

This simple observation allows us to work directly with the

Coordinate-Wise Analysis on HDX:

The second critical tool in our analysis is a simplified method of coordinate-wise analysis on HDX inspired by Bourgain’s proof of the symmetrization theorem. We focus in particular on the noise operator, though the same method can be applied to other random walks on HDX.

On a product space, the noise operator
T_{\rho}
can naturally be expressed as the product of coordinate-wise operators
T_{\rho}^{i}
. When
\rho\in[0,1]
, this simply corresponds to re-sampling the
i
th coordinate with probability
1-\rho
and keeping all other coordinates fixed. For general
r\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the corresponding coordinate noise operator is often defined as

T_{r_{i}}^{i}f\coloneqq\sum\limits_{S\ni i}r_{i}f^{=S}+\sum\limits_{S\not\ni i}f^{=S}.

Unfortunately, while this generalization makes manipulation of coordinate-wise noise operators simpler, it does not interact well with
\gamma
-products where the Efron-Stein decomposition is not closed (i.e. the Efron-Stein decomposition of
f^{=S}
itself is not exactly
f^{=S}
). Instead, it turns out the right choice is to generalize
T_{r}
in terms of the
projection operators

E_{S}f(y)=\mathbb{E}_{x}[f(x)~|~x_{S}=y_{S}],

which re-sample all coordinates outside of
S
. The extended noise operator may then be defined as a ‘binomial’ combination of projection operators:

T_{r}=\sum\limits_{S\subseteq[d]}r_{S}\prod_{i\notin S}(1-r_{i})E_{S},

where
r_{S}=\prod\limits_{i\in S}r_{i}
. The coordinate-wise noise operators are then naturally defined as

T_{r_{i}}^{i}\coloneqq r_{i}I+(1-r_{i})E_{[d]\setminus i}.

On a product space, it is easy to check that for either definition
T_{r}=T_{r_{1}}^{1}\ldots T_{r_{d}}^{d}
. Our first key lemma shows this continues to hold approximately on
(q,\gamma)
-products.

Lemma 1.8

(Decorrelation (Informal
Lemma

4.2
))
.

Let
X
be a
d
-partite
(q,\gamma)
-product and
r\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then

\left\lVert T_{r}f-T^{1}_{r_{1}}\ldots T^{d}_{r_{d}}f\right\rVert_{q}\leq O_{\gamma}(\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{q})

Second, we argue that for the operator formalization, the coordinate-wise operators
localize
correctly to the
1
-D marginals of
X
.

Lemma 1.9

(Localization (Informal
Lemma

4.3
))
.

Let
X
be a
d
-partite complex and
r\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then for any
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
and
i\in[d]
:

T^{i}_{r}f(x)=T_{r_{i}}f|_{x_{-i}}(x_{i}),

where
f|_{x_{-i}}:X[i]\to\mathbb{R}
is the localization of
f
defined by
f|_{x_{-i}}(x_{i})=f(x)
.
6
6

6

Formally, this function lives on the
link
of
x_{-i}
, see
Section

2.1
.

Decorrelation and localization allows us to reduce analysis of the ‘high dimensional’ quantity
T_{\rho}f(x)
to the
1-dimensional quantities

T_{\rho}f|_{x_{-i}}(x_{i})
. One can then apply standard
1
-dimensional inequalities, and ‘lift’ the result back to the original complex
X
by reversing the lemmas. This is a powerful (and standard) trick on the hypercube and product spaces in boolean analysis—many classical results are proved via this type of reduction to the
1
-D case (often properties proved this way, like hypercontractivity, are said to ‘tensorize’).

The Symmetrization Theorem:

We now sketch the proof of the symmetrization theorem based on these three components: a (standard)
1
-D version of the inequality, decorrelation into coordinate-wise operators, and localization to the
1
-D case. We will show only the upper bound; the lower bound follows via similar reasoning. The proof follows the outline of the original proof on products (as presented in
[
68
]
).

Slightly re-phrasing the theorem statement, our goal is to show

\left\lVert T_{\frac{1}{2}}f\right\rVert_{q}\leq(1+o_{\gamma}(1))\left\lVert T_{r}f\right\rVert_{q}.

By
Lemma

1.8
, we can break these terms into their coordinate-wise components and instead argue

\left\lVert T^{1}_{\frac{1}{2}}\ldots T^{d}_{\frac{1}{2}}f\right\rVert_{q}\leq(1+o_{\gamma}(1))\left\lVert T^{1}_{r_{1}}\ldots T^{d}_{r_{d}}f\right\rVert_{q}.

Written in this form, there is really only one natural strategy: the
replacement method
. In particular, we’ll argue that each of the coordinate-wise operators
T_{1/2}^{i}
can be sequentially replaced by
T_{r_{i}}^{i}
, incurring only
O(\gamma)\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{q}
error in each step. Toward this end, define the ‘
j
-partially symmetrized’ operator as

T^{(j)}\coloneqq T^{1}_{r_{1}},\ldots T^{j}_{r_{j}}T^{j+1}_{\frac{1}{2}}\ldots T^{d}_{\frac{1}{2}}.

Since
T^{(0)}=T^{1}_{1/2}\ldots T^{d}_{1/2}\approx T_{\rho}
, and
T^{(d)}=T^{r}_{r_{1}}\ldots T^{d}_{r_{d}}\approx T_{r}
by telescoping it is enough to show for all
j

\left\lVert T^{(j)}f\right\rVert_{q}\leq(1+o_{\gamma}(1))\left\lVert T^{(j+1)}f\right\rVert_{q}.

Un-wrapping the lefthand side, by
Lemma

1.8
we can permute the order of the
T_{j}
’s to move the
(j+1)
st operator to the front, and localize to the
(j+1)
st coordinate via
Lemma

1.9

\displaystyle\left\lVert T^{(j)}f\right\rVert_{q}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert T^{1}_{r_{1}},\ldots T^{j}_{r_{j}}T^{j+1}_{\frac{1}{2}}\ldots T^{d}_{\frac{1}{2}}f\right\rVert_{q}

\displaystyle\approx\left\lVert T^{j+1}_{\frac{1}{2}}(T^{1}_{r_{1}},\ldots T^{j}_{r_{j}}T^{j+2}_{\frac{1}{2}}\ldots T^{d}_{\frac{1}{2}}f)\right\rVert_{q}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\left\lVert T_{\frac{1}{2}}(T^{1}_{r_{1}},\ldots T^{j}_{r_{j}}T^{j+2}_{\frac{1}{2}}\ldots T^{d}_{\frac{1}{2}}f)|_{x_{-(j+1)}}\right\rVert_{q,x_{j+1}}\right\rVert_{q,x_{-(j+1)}}.

The inner norm is now over a
1
-dimensional domain, so we can apply the standard
1
-D symmetrization theorem to replace
T_{1/2}g
with
T_{r_{j+1}}g
, then ‘un’-localize and ‘un’-permute by the reverse directions of
Lemma

1.9
and
Lemma

1.8
:

\displaystyle\left\lVert\left\lVert T_{\frac{1}{2}}(T^{1}_{r_{1}},\ldots T^{j}_{r_{j}}T^{j+2}_{\frac{1}{2}}\ldots T^{d}_{\frac{1}{2}}f)|_{x_{-(j+1)}}\right\rVert_{q,x_{j+1}}\right\rVert_{q,x_{-(j+1)}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\left\lVert T_{r_{j+1}}(T^{1}_{r_{1}},\ldots T^{j}_{r_{j}}T^{j+2}_{\frac{1}{2}}\ldots T^{d}_{\frac{1}{2}}f)|_{x_{-(j+1)}}\right\rVert_{q,x_{j+1}}\right\rVert_{q,x_{-(j+1)}}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert T^{j+1}_{r_{j+1}}T^{1}_{r_{1}},\ldots T^{j}_{r_{j}}T^{j+2}_{\frac{1}{2}}\ldots T^{d}_{\frac{1}{2}}f\right\rVert_{q}

\displaystyle\approx\left\lVert T^{1}_{r_{1}},\ldots T^{j}_{r_{j+1}}T^{j+2}_{\frac{1}{2}}\ldots T^{d}_{\frac{1}{2}}f\right\rVert_{q}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert T^{(j+1)}f\right\rVert_{q},

as desired.

Global Hypercontractivity:

Our proof of global hypercontractivity builds on the elegant proof of O’Donnell and Zhao
[
74
]
for
(2{\to}4)
-hypercontractivity of products. Here we largely focus on how to extend the
(2{\to}4)
-case to HDX and achieve the correct
2^{O(i)}
-scaling and remark when applicable how the result can be extended to near-sharp bounds for the
(2{\to}q)
-case.

The key to extending
[
74
]
’s proof to HDX lies in combining symmetrization with
q
-norm variants of several techniques from
[
37
]
and
[
8
]
. We start with the basic fact that the Efron-Stein decomposition is close to an eigenbasis of the noise operator in
4
-norm:

Lemma 1.10

(Approximate Eigenbasis)
.

Let
X
be a
\gamma
-product. For any
f
and
r\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

\left\lVert T_{r}f^{=S}-r_{S}f^{=S}\right\rVert_{4}\leq O_{d,r}(\gamma)\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{4}.

In fact, since we want errors scaling in
\ell_{2}
-norm rather than
\ell_{4}
, we’ll also use the following stronger bound for global functions:

\left\lVert T_{r}f^{=S}-r_{S}f^{=S}\right\rVert_{4}\leq O_{d,r}(\gamma)\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{1/2}_{2}\max_{T\subseteq S,x_{T}}\{\left\lVert f|_{x_{T}}\right\rVert_{2}^{1/2}\}.

We now follow the core idea of
[
74
]
: simply bound the 4-norm by symmetrizing and applying standard hypercontractivity. In particular by
Theorem

1.1
and
Lemma

1.10
:

\displaystyle\left\lVert f^{\leq i}\right\rVert^{4}_{4}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert T_{r}T_{2}f^{\leq i}\right\rVert_{4}^{4}

(Symmetrization)

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\lVert\left(\sum\limits_{|S|\leq i}r_{S}2^{|S|}f^{=S}\right)\right\rVert^{4}_{4}

(
4
-Approximate Eigenbasis)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\mathbb{E}_{r}\left[\left(\sum\limits_{|S|\leq i}r_{S}2^{|S|}f^{=S}(x)\right)^{4}\right]\right].

Notice that the inner expectation, now over the boolean hypercube, is a degree-
i
function with Fourier coefficients
2^{|S|}f^{=S}(x)
. Thus we may apply the standard Bonami Lemma and Parseval’s Theorem to move from the
4
-norm to the
2
-norm:

\displaystyle\leq 2^{O(i)}\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\mathbb{E}_{r}\left[\left(\sum\limits_{|S|\leq i}r_{S}f^{=S}(x)\right)^{2}\right]^{2}\right]

(Bonami Lemma)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\left(\sum\limits_{|S|\leq i}f^{=S}(x)^{2}\right)\left(\sum\limits_{|T|\leq i}f^{=T}(x)^{2}\right)\right].

(Parseval)

If the two inner terms were independent, we’d be done, as each term individually is roughly the 2-norm
\left\lVert f^{\leq i}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}
by Parseval. Unfortunately the terms are correlated by shared variables within
x
.
[
74
]
’s main insight is that if we condition on their intersection, the remaining variables can be handled independently at the cost of using the conditional norm of
f
instead of the global norm for one of the factors.

More formally, we re-index our sum over intersections
I=S\cap T
:

\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\left(\sum\limits_{|S|\leq i}f^{=S}(x)^{2}\right)\left(\sum\limits_{|T|\leq i}f^{=T}(x)^{2}\right)\right]\leq\sum\limits_{I\leq i}\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\sum\limits_{S\supset I}f^{=S}(x)^{2}\sum\limits_{T:T\cap S=I}f^{=T}(x)^{2}\right].

(2)

On a product space, one could factor out the intersecting variables and bound the above as

\sum\limits_{I\leq i}\mathbb{E}_{x_{I}}\left[\left(\sum\limits_{S\supset I}\mathbb{E}_{x_{S\setminus I}}[f^{=S}(x_{S})^{2}]\right)\left(\sum\limits_{T\supset I}\mathbb{E}_{x_{T\setminus I}}[f^{=T}(x_{T})^{2}]\right)\right].

(3)

where critically the inner expectations are de-correlated. Once the bound is in this form, one argues that the maximum of the righthand term can be bounded by at most
2^{O(i)}
times the worst conditional
2
-norm of
f
over
x_{I}
. The remaining term is at most
2^{O(i)}
times the global
2
-norm of
f
which completes the proof.

In the
(2{\to}q)
-case, the argument is similar but there are
q/2
‘
2
-norm’ terms to handle after applying symmetrization and the Bonami Lemma. We argue it is still possible to pull out the final such term at the cost of
O(q)^{i}
times the worst conditional
2
-norm. Repeating iteratively
q/2-1
times results in a dependency of
O(q)^{qi/2}
, which combined with an additional factor of
O(q)^{qi/2}
coming from applying the traditional Bonami Lemma at the start of the process gives the desired near-sharp
O(q)^{qi}
scaling.

Returning to the
(2{\to}4)
-setting on
\gamma
-products, we remark one cannot naively simplify to
Equation

3
because the variables
x_{S\setminus I}
and
x_{T\setminus I}
are not independent. To break the correlation, we’ll use a standard tool in the HDX literature called the (partite)
swap walks
:

A_{S,T}f(y)=\mathbb{E}[f(x_{S})~|~x_{T}=y_{T}],

which correspond to the (normalized) bipartite adacency matrix of
(X_{S},X_{T})
. It is by now a widely used fact that the swap walks on HDX are highly expanding (see e.g.
[
18
,
3
,
37
,
5
,
23
]
).

Formally, expanding out
Equation

2
one encounters many terms of the form

\underset{x_{S\setminus I}}{\mathbb{E}}\left[f^{=S}(x_{S})^{2}\underset{x_{T\setminus I}\sim X_{x_{S}}}{\mathbb{E}}\left[f^{=T}(x_{T})^{2}\right]\right].

The inner expectation is exactly an application of the swap walk in the link of
x_{I}
, so we can de-correlate the terms using spectral expansion as:

\displaystyle\underset{x_{S\setminus I}}{\mathbb{E}}\left[f^{=S}(x_{S})^{2}\underset{x_{T\setminus I}\sim X_{x_{S}}}{\mathbb{E}}\left[f^{=T}(x_{T})^{2}\right]\right]

\displaystyle=\left\langle(f^{=S}|_{x_{I}})^{2},A^{x_{I}}_{T\setminus I,S\setminus I}(f^{=T}|_{x_{I}})^{2}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\lesssim\underset{x_{S\setminus I}\sim X_{x_{I}}}{\mathbb{E}}[(f^{=S}|_{x_{I}})^{2}]\underset{x_{T\setminus I}\sim X_{x_{I}}}{\mathbb{E}}[(f^{=T}|_{x_{I}})^{2}]

and continue the proof as in the product case. Most of the actual work in the proof (once one has symmetrization) comes down to careful analysis of the error terms we’ve swept under the rug. With enough effort, we show the above inequalities hold up to error

O_{d}(\gamma\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\max_{|S|\leq i,x_{S}}\{\left\lVert f|_{x_{S}}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\})
. This is dominated by the main term for small enough
\gamma
, so we may proceed with no asymptotic loss.

Preliminaries

2.1
Simplicial Complexes

A
pure simplicial complex
is a collection of disjoint sets

X=X(0)\cup\ldots\cup X(d)

where
X(d)\subseteq{[n]\choose d}
is an arbitrary
d
-uniform hypergraph and
X(i)\subseteq{[n]\choose i}
is given by downward closure, that is the family of
i
-sets that sit inside any element of
X(d)
. A
weighted
simplicial complex is a pure simplicial complex equipped with a measure
\Pi_{d}
over
X(d)
. This induces a natural measure
\Pi_{i}
over
X(i)
given by drawing a
d
-set
s
, then an
i
-set
t\subset s
uniformly at random. For simplicity, we will typically drop the weight function from notation and simply write
X
to mean a pure weighted simplicial complex.

We denote the space of functions over
i
-faces of
X
as
C_{i}\coloneqq\{f:X(i)\to\mathbb{R}\}
. The measures
\Pi_{i}
induce a natural inner product over this space defined by

\langle f,g\rangle=\underset{t\sim\Pi_{i}}{\mathbb{E}}[f(t)g(t)]

We will usually just write
t\sim X(i)
, where it is understood that
\Pi_{i}
is the underlying distribution.

Links:

It will frequently be useful to look at the local structure of a given simplicial complex
X
. Given a face
t\in X
, the
link
of
t
is the simplicial complex

X_{t}\coloneqq\{s:t\cup s\in X\land s\cap t=\emptyset\}

whose underlying distribution
\Pi^{t}
is the natural induced weight function generated by sampling
s^{\prime}\in X(d)
conditioned on
s^{\prime}
containing
t
, that is
\Pi^{t}(s)=\Pr_{s^{\prime}\sim\Pi_{d}}[s^{\prime}=s\cup t~|~t\subset s^{\prime}]
. Equivalently, one may think of the link
X_{t}
as being the distribution over
X
conditioned on
t
appearing in the face, marginalized to the remaining variables.

Partite Complexes:

A simplicial complex
X
is called
partite
if it is possible to partition its vertices
X(0)=\Omega_{1}\cup\ldots\cup\Omega_{d}
such that every face
s\in X(d)
has exactly one vertex from each component. We can always think of a partite simplicial complex as a (possibly very sparse) distribution over
\bigotimes\limits_{i=1}^{d}\Omega_{i}
. With this in mind, we will typically denote elements in
X(d)=\bigotimes\limits_{i=1}^{d}\Omega_{i}
as
d
-dimensional tuples
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})
. This allows us to align with more typical notation in the analysis literature over product spaces.

It will frequently be useful to work on the
projection
of
X(d)
to a certain subset of colors (coordinates). In particular, given
S\subset[d]
, a face
x_{S}\in X[S]
is simply generated by drawing
x\in X(d)
and projecting onto the
S
-coordinates of
x
. Note these projections can be thought of as the marginals of
X=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})
. It is often useful to write nested expectations over the marginals of
x
, e.g. of the form
\mathbb{E}_{x_{S}}[[\mathbb{E}_{x_{T}}]...
for disjoint
S
and
T
. We emphasize that this notation always means
x_{T}
is drawn
conditionally
on the value of
x_{S}
in the outer expectation.

2.2
High Order Random Walks

Hypergraphs come equipped with a sequence of natural random walks generalizing the standard random walk on graphs. In the context of high dimensional expanders, such walks were first studied by Kaufman and Mass
[
54
]
, and have since become an integral part of almost all work on high dimensional expanders.

In this work, we focus mostly on partite complexes. In this setting, there is a natural family of generalizations of the bipartite graph operator. Given subsets
S,T\subset[d]
, the random walk operator
A_{S,T}
maps functions on
X[S]
to functions on
X[T]
by averaging. In particular, given a function
f:X[S]\to\mathbb{R}
define

A_{S,T}f(y_{T})=\mathbb{E}[f(x_{S})~|~x_{T}=y_{T}].

Equivalently, this is the expected value of
f
when sampled from the link of
y_{T}
. Note that in the graph case, this is simply the underlying random walk on a bipartite graph. We write
\Pi_{S,T}
to denote the stationary distribution of the operator
A_{S,T}
. Note this is simply the distribution given by drawing
x\in X(d)
and projecting onto
S
.

We will frequently make use of the special case where
S=[d]
, which averages
f
outside the specified coordinate subset
T
. In particular, given a subset
T\subset[d]
and function
f:X(k)\to\mathbb{R}
, we define

E_{T}f(y_{T})\coloneqq A_{[d],T}f(y_{T})=\mathbb{E}[f(x)~|~x_{T}=y_{T}].

Similarly,
E_{T}f(y_{T})
is simply expectation of
f
over the link of
y_{T}
.

The Noise Operator:

The noise operator is one of the best studied random walks in the analysis of boolean functions. The standard operator is typically defined via the following probabilistic interpretation describing its transition matrix as a Markov chain: given a face
x\in X(d)
, fix each coordinate in
x
with probability
\rho
, and re-sample all remaining coordinates. Formally, we can write the noise operator as a convex combination of the
\{E_{S}\}_{S\subseteq[d]}
operators defined above.

Definition 2.1

(Noise Operator)
.

Let
X
be a
d
-partite complex,
f\in C_{d}
, and
\rho\in[0,1]
. The noise operator
T_{\rho}
acts on
f
by re-randomizing over each coordinate with probability
1-\rho
:

T_{\rho}f=\sum\limits_{S\subset[d]}\rho^{|S|}(1-\rho)^{d-|S|}E_{S}f

Finally, we note that when working on a link
X_{\tau}
, we will write any corresponding walk operator as
M^{\tau}
to denote the specification to the link. E.g.
A_{i,j}^{\tau}
is the bipartite graph between color
i
and color
j
within the link of
\tau
, and
\Pi_{i,j}^{\tau}
to denote its stationary distribution.

Total Influence:

Total influence is a critical notion in boolean analysis measuring the total ‘sensitivity’ of a function to flipping individual coordinates. On product spaces, total influence is most naturally defined via the
Laplacian operators

L_{i}=I-A_{[d]\setminus\{i\}}.

The total influence of a function
f\in C_{d}
is

\mathbf{I}[f]=\sum\limits_{i\in[d]}\langle f,L_{i}f\rangle.

When
f
is boolean, it is easy to check that the inner product
\langle f,L_{i}f\rangle
is proportional to the expected probability over
x
that re-sampling
i
th coordinate flips the value of
f
. The study of the structure of low influence functions is core to boolean analysis, and a major motivation behind this work.

2.3
High Dimensional Expanders and
\gamma

We focus in this work on an elegant reformulation of spectral high dimensional expansion of Dikstein and Dinur

Definition 2.2

(
\gamma

A

of co-dimension at least

A similar notion was considered in the non-partite case by Bafna, Hopkins, Kaufman, and Lovett

]
.

of Dinur and Kaufman

]
and Oppenheim

Definition 2.3

(Local-Spectral Expander)
.

A weighted complex

\gamma

-spectral expander.

Dikstein and Dinur
[

]
prove that any
\gamma

-product. On the other hand, seminal work of Oppenheim
[

]
implies any
\gamma
-product is roughly an
O(\gamma)
-one-sided local-spectral expander for small enough

(see
[
5
]
for a more detailed conversion between the two).

Gur, Lifshitz, and Liu

37

also observe that any two-sided local-spectral expander can be

into a partite complex as a
\gamma
-product. This is done by embedding of a
d
-dimensional complex
X
into

by adding every permutation of each top level face.

Observation 2.4
.

If

As a result, most results proved for

3
Fourier Analysis on
(q,\gamma)
-Products

In this section, we introduce a natural generalization of local-spectral expanders to higher moments we call
(q,\gamma)
-Products, and extend the useful Fourier analytic machinery of
[
37
,
8
]
to this setting.

Definition 3.1

(
(q,\gamma)
-Products)
.

For any
q\in(1,\infty]
,
\gamma>0
, and
d\in\mathbb{N}
, a
d
-partite complex
X
is a
(q,\gamma)
-Product if for every link
X_{\tau}
of co-dimension at least
2
and every
i\neq j

\left\lVert A_{i,j}^{\tau}-\Pi_{i,j}^{\tau}\right\rVert_{q}\leq\gamma.

Since
\left\lVert A^{\tau}_{i,j}-\Pi_{i,j}\right\rVert_{2}
is exactly the second largest eigenvalue of
A^{\tau}_{i,j}
, setting
p=2
recovers the notion of a
\gamma
-product. One can define the notion of
(q,\gamma)
-HDX on non-partite complexes analogously.

Definition 3.2

(
(q,\gamma)
-HDX)
.

A simplicial complex
X
is a
(q,\gamma)
-HDX if every link
X_{\tau}
of co-dimension at least
2
satisfies:

\left\lVert A_{\tau}-\Pi_{1}^{\tau}\right\rVert_{q}\leq\gamma

Similarly, setting
q=2
recovers the notion of a two-sided
\gamma
-local-spectral expander. It is easy to see that embedding a
(q,\gamma)
-HDX into a partite complex by including every ordering of the faces results in a
(q,\gamma)
-product by the same argument as above. All results we cover in the partite case therefore translate to the former, and we focus only on the partite case in what follows.

To relate
(q,\gamma)
-products to standard HDX, we will rely on the following special case of the classical Riesz-Thorin Interpolation theorem

Theorem 3.3

(Riesz-Thorin Interpolation
[
71
,
73
]
)
.

Let
X
be a
d
-partite complex and
T:C_{d}\to C_{d}
a linear operator. For any
0<p_{0}<p_{1}\leq\infty
,
\theta\in(0,1)
, and
\frac{1}{p_{\theta}}=\frac{1-\theta}{p_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{p_{1}}

\left\lVert T\right\rVert_{p_{\theta}}\leq\left\lVert T\right\rVert_{p_{0}}^{1-\theta}\left\lVert T\right\rVert_{p_{1}}^{\theta}.

Because the infinity norm of the operators
A-\Pi
is universally bounded,
Theorem

3.3
implies any
\gamma
-product is a
(q,\gamma_{q})
-product for
\gamma_{q}
an appropriate function of
\gamma
and
q
:

Lemma 3.4
.

For any
q>1
and
\gamma>0
, any
\gamma
-product is a
(q,\gamma_{q})
-product for

\gamma_{q}=\begin{cases}\gamma^{2/q}2^{1-2/q}&q\geq 2\\
\gamma^{\frac{2(q-1)}{q}}2^{1-{\frac{2(q-1)}{q}}}&$1 < q < 2$\end{cases}

Proof.

It is enough to prove the
q\geq 2
case. In particular, since the adjoint of
A_{i,j}
is
A_{j,i}
, the
1<q<2
case follows from Hölder duality, that for any
M
:

\left\lVert M\right\rVert_{q}=\left\lVert M^{*}\right\rVert_{q^{\prime}}

where
q^{\prime}=\frac{q}{q-1}
is
q
’s Hölder conjugate. Assuming now that
q\geq 2
, for all
A_{i,j}^{\tau}
we have:

\left\lVert A_{i,j}^{\tau}-\Pi_{i,j}^{\tau}\right\rVert_{2}\leq\gamma\quad\text{and}\quad\left\lVert A_{i,j}^{\tau}-\Pi_{i,j}^{\tau}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq 2,

where the former is from definition and the latter is due to stochasticity of
A_{i,j}^{\tau}
and
\Pi_{i,j}^{\tau}
. Riesz-Thorin Interpolation then gives

\left\lVert A_{i,j}^{\tau}-\Pi^{\tau}\right\rVert_{q}\leq\left\lVert A_{i,j}^{\tau}-\Pi^{\tau}\right\rVert_{2}^{2/q}\left\lVert A_{i,j}^{\tau}-\Pi^{\tau}\right\rVert^{1-2/q}_{\infty}\leq\gamma^{2/q}2^{1-2/q}

as desired.
∎

Lemma

3.4
allows us to easily control the higher norm behavior of classical constructions of high dimensional expanders such as the Ramanujan complexes
[
64
]
and coset complexes
[
59
]
over sufficiently large fields.

3.1
q
-Norm Expansion of Swap Walks

A critical property of classical high dimensional expanders, and the core of
[
37
]
’s Fourier analysis on HDX, is that the operators
A_{S,T}
are strongly expanding when
S\cap T=\emptyset
(these specific operators are often called the ‘swap-walks’)
[
3
,
18
,
37
,
5
]
. Prior arguments for expansion of the swap walks take specific advantage of being in the spectral (
q=2
) setting. We give an elementary argument showing swap walks expand in
q
-norm on
(q,\gamma)
-products.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
X
be a
(q,\gamma)
-product and
q^{\prime}=\frac{q}{q-1}
. For all coordinate subsets
S\cap T=\emptyset
:

\left\lVert A_{S,T}-\Pi_{S,T}\right\rVert_{q}\leq|S||T|\gamma\quad\text{and}\quad\left\lVert A_{S,T}-\Pi_{S,T}\right\rVert_{q^{\prime}}\leq|S||T|\gamma

Proof.

We induct on
|S|+|T|
. For the base case
|S|=|T|=1
, observe we have
\left\lVert A_{S,T}-\Pi_{S,T}\right\rVert_{q}\leq\gamma
by definition. To bound the
q^{\prime}
-norm, recall Hölder duality promises that for any operator
M

\left\lVert M\right\rVert_{q}=\left\lVert M^{*}\right\rVert_{q^{\prime}},

where
M^{*}
is the adjoint. Thus we also have

\left\lVert A_{S,T}-\Pi_{S,T}\right\rVert_{q^{\prime}}=\left\lVert A_{T,S}-\Pi_{T,S}\right\rVert_{q}\leq\gamma

as desired. Note by the same argument, this also holds within every link of
X
.

Assume now by induction that, for some fixed
|S|+|T|>2
, all
S^{\prime},T^{\prime}
such that
|S^{\prime}|+|T^{\prime}|<|S|+|T|
and
\tau\in X
satisfy

\left\lVert A^{\tau}_{S^{\prime},T^{\prime}}-\Pi^{\tau}_{S^{\prime},T^{\prime}}\right\rVert_{q}\leq|S^{\prime}||T^{\prime}|\lambda.

We first argue we may assume without loss of generality that
|T|>1
. This is again by Hölder duality since when
|S|>|T|
, we have

\left\lVert A_{S,T}-\Pi_{S,T}\right\rVert_{q}=\left\lVert A_{T,S}-\Pi_{T,S}\right\rVert_{q^{\prime}}\leq\gamma.

Thus we are done if we show the result for all
q\in[1,\infty]
and
|T|\geq|S|
(in particular when
|T|>1
).

Assume without loss of generality that
1\in T
. Fix any
f:X[S]\to\mathbb{R}
. As is typically the case, the idea is to sample
t\in X[T]
by first sampling
t^{\prime}\in X[T\setminus\{1\}]
, then
v\in X_{t}[1]
:

\displaystyle\left\lVert A_{S,T}f-\Pi_{S,T}f\right\rVert_{q}=\Bigg|\Bigg|\left\lVert(A_{S,T}f)|_{t^{\prime}}-(\Pi_{S,T}f)|_{t^{\prime}}\right\rVert_{q,v\in X_{t}[1]}\Bigg|\Bigg|_{q,t^{\prime}\in X[T\setminus\{1\}]}

where for any
g:X[T]\to\mathbb{R}
,
g|_{t^{\prime}}(v)=g(t^{\prime}\cup v)
denotes the localization of
g
to the link of
t^{\prime}
. The trick is now to observe that as a function of
X_{t^{\prime}}[1]
,
A_{S,T}f|_{t^{\prime}}
is exactly
A_{S,1}^{t^{\prime}}f^{t^{\prime}}
, where
f^{t^{\prime}}:X_{t^{\prime}}[S]\to\mathbb{R}
is the restriction
f^{s}(\tau)=f(\tau)
. Then by adding and subtracting the corresponding local stationary operator:

\displaystyle\left\lVert A_{S,T}f-\Pi_{S,T}f\right\rVert_{q}

\displaystyle=\Bigg|\Bigg|\left\lVert A^{t^{\prime}}_{S,1}f^{t^{\prime}}-\Pi^{t^{\prime}}_{S,1}f^{t^{\prime}}+\Pi^{t^{\prime}}_{S,1}f^{t^{\prime}}-(\Pi_{S,T}f)|_{t^{\prime}}\right\rVert_{q,v\in X_{t^{\prime}}(1)}\Bigg|\Bigg|_{q,t^{\prime}\in X[T\setminus\{1\}]}

\displaystyle\leq\Bigg|\Bigg|\left\lVert A^{t^{\prime}}_{S,1}f^{t^{\prime}}-\Pi^{t^{\prime}}_{S,1}f^{t^{\prime}}\right\rVert_{q,v\in X_{t^{\prime}}[1]}\Bigg|\Bigg|_{q,s\in X[T\setminus\{1\}]}

\displaystyle+\Bigg|\Bigg|\left\lVert\Pi^{t^{\prime}}_{S,1}f^{t^{\prime}}-(\Pi_{S,T}f)|_{t^{\prime}}\right\rVert_{q,v\in X_{t^{\prime}}[1]}\Bigg|\Bigg|_{q,t^{\prime}\in X[T\setminus\{1\}]}

by the triangle inequality. The first term is now bounded by the inductive hypothesis applied in the link of
t^{\prime}
:

\displaystyle\Bigg|\Bigg|\left\lVert A^{t^{\prime}}_{S,1}f^{t^{\prime}}-\Pi^{t^{\prime}}_{S,1}f^{t^{\prime}}\right\rVert_{q,v\in X_{t^{\prime}}[1]}\Bigg|\Bigg|_{q,t^{\prime}\in X[T\setminus\{1\}]}

\displaystyle\leq i\gamma\Bigg|\Bigg|\left\lVert f^{t^{\prime}}\right\rVert_{q,X_{t^{\prime}}[S]}\Bigg|\Bigg|_{q,t^{\prime}\in X[T\setminus\{1\}]}

\displaystyle=\underset{t^{\prime}\in X[T\setminus 1]}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\underset{s\sim X_{t^{\prime}}[S]}{\mathbb{E}}[|f(s)|^{q}]\right]^{1/q}

\displaystyle=|S|\gamma\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{q}

where in the final step we have used the fact that drawing
t^{\prime}\in X[T\setminus 1]
, then
s
from
X_{t^{\prime}}[S]
is equidistributed with simply drawing
s\in X[S]
directly.

Toward the second term, observe that, as a function of
t^{\prime}
:

\Pi^{t^{\prime}}_{S,1}f^{t^{\prime}}(v)=A_{S,T^{\prime}}f(t^{\prime})\quad~\text{and}~\quad(\Pi_{S,T}f)|_{t^{\prime}}(v)=\Pi_{S,T^{\prime}}f(t^{\prime})

so we finally have

\displaystyle\Bigg|\Bigg|\left\lVert\Pi^{t^{\prime}}_{S,1}f^{t^{\prime}}-(\Pi_{S,T}f)|_{t^{\prime}}\right\rVert_{q,v\in X_{t^{\prime}}[1]}\Bigg|\Bigg|_{q,t^{\prime}\in X[T^{\prime}\setminus\{1\}])}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert A_{S,T^{\prime}}f-\Pi_{S,T^{\prime}}f\right\rVert_{q,t^{\prime}\in X[T\setminus\{1\}]}

\displaystyle\leq(|T|-1)|S|\gamma\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{q}

by the inductive hypothesis. Altogether this gives

\left\lVert A_{S,T}f-\Pi_{S,T}f\right\rVert_{q}\leq\gamma|S|+\gamma(|T|-1)|S|=|S||T|\gamma

as desired.
∎

3.2
The Efron-Stein Decomposition

Most of our analysis of
(q,\gamma)
-products is based on the Efron-Stein decomposition, introduced in the context of HDX independently in
[
37
]
(for
\gamma
-products) and
[
8
]
(for two-sided HDX).

Definition 3.6

(Efron-Stein Decomposition)
.

Let
X
be a
d
-partite complex,
0\leq k\leq d
, and
f\in C_{k}
. The Efron-Stein Decomposition of f is given by the collection of functions
\{f^{=S}\}_{S\subseteq[d]}
where:

f^{=S}\coloneqq\sum\limits_{T\subset S}(-1)^{|S\setminus T|}E_{T}f

We write
f^{\leq i}=\sum\limits_{|S|\leq i}f^{=S}
to denote the degree at most
i
components of
f
, and
f^{=i}
degree exactly
i
.

Note that
f^{=S}
is only a function of the
S
-projection of
x
. With this in mind, we sometimes write
f^{=S}(x_{S})
instead of
f^{=S}(x)
.

In the remainder of the section, we overview useful properties of the Efron-Stein decomposition that continue to hold approximately on
(q,\gamma)
-products. We start with by stating a core result of
[
37
]
, who show the Efron-Stein decomposition on
\gamma
-products is an approximate Fourier basis.

Theorem 3.7

(
[
37
]
)
.

Let
X
be a
d
-partite
\gamma
-product and
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
. The Efron-Stein Decomposition satisfies the following properties:

1.

Decomposition:
f=\sum\limits_{S\subset[d]}f^{=S}

2.

Approximate Orthogonality:
\langle f^{=S},f^{=T}\rangle\leq 2^{O(|S|+|T|)}\gamma\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{2}^{2}

3.

Approximate Parseval:
\left|\langle f^{\leq i},f^{\leq i}\rangle-\sum\limits_{|S|\leq i}\langle f^{=S},f^{=S}\rangle\right|\leq 2^{O(d)}\gamma\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{2}^{2}

We will require variants of a few other standard properties of Efron-Stein extended to high norms. First, we observe the
q
-norm of
f^{=S}
is not much larger than the
q
-norm of
f
itself.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
X
be a
d
-partite complex. Then for any
f\in C_{d}
,
S\subseteq[d]
, and
q\geq 1

\left\lVert f^{=S}\right\rVert_{q}\leq 2^{|S|}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{q}.

The following
\ell_{2}
-bound also holds:

\left\lVert f^{=S}\right\rVert_{q}\leq 2^{O(|S|)}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{2}^{2/q}\max_{T\subseteq S,x_{T}}\left\{\left\lVert f|_{x_{T}}\right\rVert_{2}^{1-\frac{2}{q}}\right\}

Proof.

E_{T}
is an averaging operator and therefore contracts
q
-norms. As such we have:

\displaystyle\left\lVert f^{=S}\right\rVert_{q}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\sum\limits_{T\subset S}(-1)^{|S|-|T|}E_{T}f\right\rVert_{q}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{|S|}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{q}

by the triangle inequality. For the second bound, we can instead use Cauchy-Schwarz to write:

\displaystyle\left\lVert\sum\limits_{T\subset S}(-1)^{|S|-|T|}E_{T}f\right\rVert_{q}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum\limits_{T\subset S}(-1)^{|S|-|T|}E_{T}f\right)^{q}\right]^{1/q}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{O(|S|)}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum\limits_{T\subset S}(E_{T}f)^{2}\right)^{q/2}\right]^{1/q}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{O(|S|)}\max_{T\subseteq S,x_{T}}\left\{(E_{T}f)^{2}\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}}\left(\sum\limits_{T\subset S}\mathbb{E}\left[(E_{T}f)^{2}\right]\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{O(|S|)}\max_{T\subseteq S,x_{T}}\left\{\left\lVert f|_{x_{T}}\right\rVert_{2}^{1-\frac{2}{q}}\right\}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{2}^{2/q}

where we’ve used the fact that
\mathbb{E}[f|_{x_{T}^{\prime}}]^{2}\leq\left\lVert f|_{x_{T^{\prime}}}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}
and
E_{T}
contracts
p
-norms.
∎

Second, we will crucially rely on the fact that
E_{T}f^{=S}
is small so long as
T
does not contain
S
.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
X
be a
d
-partite
(q,\gamma)
-product and
f\in C_{d}
. Then for any
T,S\subset[d]
such that
T\not\supset S
:

\left\lVert E_{T}f^{=S}\right\rVert_{q}\leq|T|2^{O(|S|)}\gamma\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{q}.

If
X
is a
\gamma
-product, the following
\ell_{2}
-bound also holds:

\left\lVert E_{T}f^{=S}\right\rVert_{q}\leq|T|2^{O(|S|)}\gamma\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{2/q}_{2}\max_{T^{\prime}\subseteq S,x_{T^{\prime}}}\left\{\left\lVert f|_{x_{T}^{\prime}}\right\rVert_{2}^{1-\frac{2}{q}}\right\}

Proof.

The proof is similar to the
q=2
case in
[
37
]
, but with somewhat more care required for the latter bound. The key is to prove the following claim

Claim 3.10
.

In the
(q,\gamma)
-product case we have for any
f:X(d)\to\mathbb{R}
:

\left\lVert E_{T}E_{T^{\prime}}f-E_{T\cap T^{\prime}}f\right\rVert_{q}\leq\gamma|T||T^{\prime}|\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{q}.

Similarly for the
\gamma
-product case

\left\lVert E_{T}E_{T^{\prime}}f-E_{T\cap T^{\prime}}f\right\rVert_{q}\leq\gamma|T||T^{\prime}|\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{2}^{2/q}\max_{T^{\prime\prime}\subset T^{\prime},x_{T^{\prime\prime}}}\left\{\left\lVert f|_{x_{T}^{\prime\prime}}\right\rVert_{2}^{1-\frac{2}{q}}\right\}

Proof.

First, we observe it is sufficient to consider the setting where
T\cap T^{\prime}=\emptyset
. We prove this for the latter bound — the former follows from an analogous argument similar to the
q=2
case in
[
37
]
. Assume the claim holds when
T\cap T^{\prime}=\emptyset
, and let
I=T\cap T^{\prime}
for a generic choice of
T,T^{\prime}
. We localize within
I
and apply the claim:

\displaystyle\left\lVert E_{T}E_{T^{\prime}}f-E_{T\cap T^{\prime}}f\right\rVert_{q}

\displaystyle=\underset{x_{I}}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\underset{x_{\bar{I}}}{\mathbb{E}}\left[(E^{x_{I}}_{T\setminus I}E^{x_{I}}_{T^{\prime}\setminus I}-E^{x_{I}}_{\emptyset})f|_{x_{I}})^{q}\right]\right]^{1/q}

\displaystyle=\underset{x_{I}}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\lVert(E^{x_{I}}_{T\setminus I}E^{x_{I}}_{T^{\prime}\setminus I}-E^{x_{I}}_{\emptyset})f\right\rVert_{x_{I},q}^{q}\right]^{1/q}

\displaystyle\leq\gamma|T||T^{\prime}|\underset{x_{I}}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\lVert f|_{x_{I}}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\max_{T^{\prime\prime}\subset T^{\prime}\setminus I,x_{T^{\prime\prime}}}\left\{\left\lVert f|_{x_{T^{\prime\prime}\cup I}}\right\rVert_{2}^{q-2}\right\}\right]^{1/q}.

We can now pull the inner maximum out by its maximum over
x_{I}
as well to upper bound this as

\displaystyle\leq\gamma|T||T^{\prime}|\max_{T^{\prime\prime}\subset T^{\prime},x_{T^{\prime\prime}}}\left\{\left\lVert f|_{x_{T^{\prime\prime}}}\right\rVert_{2}^{1-\frac{2}{q}}\right\}\underset{x_{I}}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\lVert f|_{x_{I}}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\right]^{1/q}

\displaystyle=\gamma|T||T^{\prime}|\max_{T^{\prime\prime}\subset T^{\prime},x_{T^{\prime\prime}}}\left\{\left\lVert f|_{x_{T^{\prime\prime}}}\right\rVert_{2}^{1-\frac{2}{q}}\right\}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{2}^{2/q}

as desired.

We therefore turn our attention to the case
T\cap T^{\prime}=\emptyset
. Here we use the observation of
[
37
]
that one may write
E_{T}E_{T^{\prime}}=A_{T^{\prime},T}E_{T^{\prime}}
and
\mathbb{E}[E_{T^{\prime}}f]=E_{\emptyset}[f]
. Thus to prove the
q
-norm variant of the bound we simply write

\displaystyle\left\lVert E_{T}E_{T^{\prime}}f-E_{\emptyset}f\right\rVert_{q}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert(E_{T^{\prime},T}-\Pi_{T^{\prime},T})E_{T^{\prime}}f]\right\rVert_{q}

\displaystyle\leq\gamma|T^{\prime}||T|\left\lVert E_{T^{\prime}}f\right\rVert_{q}

\displaystyle\leq\gamma|T^{\prime}||T|\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{q}

since averaging contracts
q
-norms.

The
\ell_{2}
-bound follows similarly, but pulling out a factor of the conditional
2
-norm as in
Lemma

3.8
. Namely we can instead bound
\left\lVert E_{T^{\prime}}f\right\rVert_{q}
as

\displaystyle\left\lVert E_{T^{\prime}}f\right\rVert_{q}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[(E_{T^{\prime}}f)^{q}\right]^{1/q}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{x}\{E_{T^{\prime}}f(x)^{1-\frac{2}{q}}\}\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[(E_{T^{\prime}}f)^{2}\right]^{1/q}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{x_{T^{\prime}}}\left\{\left\lVert f|_{x_{T^{\prime}}}\right\rVert_{2}^{1-\frac{2}{q}}\right\}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{2}^{2/q}

∎

The result now follows simply by expanding
f^{=S}
:

\displaystyle\left\lVert E_{T}f^{=S}\right\rVert_{q}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert E_{T}\sum\limits_{T^{\prime}\subset S}(-1)^{|S|-|T^{\prime}|}E_{T^{\prime}}f\right\rVert_{q}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\sum\limits_{T^{\prime}\subset S}(-1)^{|S|-|T^{\prime}|}E_{T}E_{T^{\prime}}f\right\rVert_{q}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\sum\limits_{T^{\prime}\subset S}(-1)^{|S|-|T^{\prime}|}E_{T\cap T^{\prime}}f+\sum\limits_{T^{\prime}\subset S}(-1)^{|S|-|T^{\prime}|}(E_{T}E_{T^{\prime}}-E_{T\cap T^{\prime}})f\right\rVert_{q}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{O(|S|)}\left\lVert(E_{T}E_{T^{\prime}}-E_{T\cap T^{\prime}})f\right\rVert_{q}

where we have used the standard fact that
\sum\limits_{T^{\prime}\subset S}(-1)^{|S|-|T^{\prime}|}E_{T\cap T^{\prime}}
is identically zero when
T\not\supset S
. Plugging in the claim now gives the desired results.
∎

A critical property of the standard Efron-Stein decomposition on product spaces is that it is an eigenbasis for standard random walks such as the noise operator. Using the above lemma, it is elementary to show this extends approximately to
(q,\gamma)
-products.

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
X
be a
d
-partite
(q,\gamma)
-product and
M=\sum\limits_{T\subset[d]}\alpha_{T}E_{T}
for some
\alpha_{T}\in\mathbb{R}
. Then for
f\in C_{d}
and
S\subset[d]
:

\left\lVert Mf^{=S}-\lambda_{S}f^{=S}\right\rVert_{q}\leq|\alpha|_{1}2^{O(d)}\gamma\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{q}

where
\lambda_{S}=\sum\limits_{T\supseteq S}\alpha_{T}
and
|\alpha|_{1}
is the counting norm of
\alpha
. Moreover if
X
is a
\gamma
-product we also have:

\left\lVert Mf^{=S}-\lambda_{S}f^{=S}\right\rVert_{q}\leq|\alpha|_{1}2^{O(d)}\gamma\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{2}^{2/q}\max_{T\subseteq S,x_{T}}\left\{\left\lVert f|_{x_{T}}\right\rVert_{2}^{1-\frac{2}{q}}\right\}

Proof.

Expanding
Mf^{=S}
we have

\displaystyle Mf^{=S}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{T\subset[d]}\alpha_{T}E_{T}f^{=S}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{T\supseteq S}\alpha_{T}f^{=S}+\sum\limits_{T\not\supseteq S}\alpha_{T}E_{T}f^{=S}

where the second equality holds since
f^{=S}
depends only on coordinates in
S
, and
E_{T}
fixes such coordinates when
T\supseteq S
. Applying
Lemma

3.9
and the triangle inequality to the latter term completes the proof.
∎

A similar application also shows that the Efron-Stein basis is ‘approximately closed’.

Lemma 3.12
.

Let
X
be a
d
-partite
(q,\gamma)
-product. Then for any
f\in C_{d}
and
T,S\subseteq[d]

•

For
S\neq T
:

\left\lVert(f^{=S})^{=T}\right\rVert_{q}\leq 2^{O(d)}\gamma\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{q}

•

For
S=T
:

\left\lVert(f^{=S})^{=S}-f^{=S}\right\rVert_{q}\leq 2^{O(d)}\gamma\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{q}

For
X
a
\gamma
-product we may similarly replace
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{q}
by
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{2}^{2/q}\underset{T\subseteq S,x_{T}}{\max}\left\{\left\lVert f|_{x_{T}}\right\rVert_{2}^{1-\frac{2}{q}}\right\}
.

Proof.

Using the definition of Efron-Stein, we have

\displaystyle(f^{=S})^{=T}=\sum\limits_{T^{\prime}\subseteq T}(-1)^{T\setminus T^{\prime}}E_{T^{\prime}}f^{=S}

If
S\neq T
, then
T^{\prime}\not\supset S
for every term in the sum, and we are done by the triangle inequality and
Lemma

3.9
. If
S=T
, the only ‘surviving’ term is
E_{S}f^{=S}=f^{=S}
, and we are similarly done.
∎

Finally, we will need the following behavior of the Efron-Stein decomposition under restriction.

Lemma 3.13
.

Let
X
be a
d
-partite complex and
f\in C_{d}
. Let
I,B\subset[d]
be any two disjoint sets. Then

f^{=I\cup B}(y_{I},x_{B},z)=\sum\limits_{J\subset I}(-1)^{|I|-|J|}(f|_{y_{J}})^{=B}(x_{B},z).

Proof.

This is a standard fact on product spaces. We give a proof that generalizes to any partite complex. Expanding the righthand side:

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{J\subset I}(-1)^{|I|-|J|}(f|_{y_{J}}^{=B})(x_{B},z)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{J\subset I}(-1)^{|I|-|J|}\sum\limits_{A\subset B}(-1)^{|B|-|A|}E^{y_{J}}_{A}f|_{y_{J}}(x_{B},z)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{J\subset I}(-1)^{|I|-|J|}\sum\limits_{A\subset B}(-1)^{|B|-|A|}\underset{z^{\prime}\sim X_{y_{J}\cup x_{A}}}{\mathbb{E}}[f(y_{J},x_{A},z^{\prime})]

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{J\subset I}(-1)^{|I|-|J|}\sum\limits_{A\subset B}(-1)^{|B|-|A|}E_{J\cup A}f(y_{I},x_{B},z)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{T\subset I\cup B}(-1)^{|I\cup B|-|T|}E_{T}f(y_{I},x_{B},z)

\displaystyle=f^{=I\cup B}(y_{I},x_{B},z)

as desired.
∎

3.3
Efron-Stein, Total Influence, and the Noise Operator

Our work relies heavily on the following operator-wise generalization of the noise operator.

Definition 3.14

(Vector-Valued Noise Operator)
.

Let
X
be a
d
-partite complex,
f\in C_{d}
, and
r\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The noise operator
T_{r}
is defined as:

T_{r}f=\sum\limits_{S\subset[d]}\prod_{i\in S}r_{i}\prod_{i\notin S}(1-r_{i})E_{S}f

While the individual Efron-Stein components are only approximate eigenvectors of the above operator, it turns out the full decomposition behaves exactly as in the product case.

Claim 3.15
.

Let
X
be a
d
-partite complex,
f\in C_{d}
, and
r\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then

T_{r}f=\sum\limits_{S\subset[d]}r_{S}f^{=S},

where
r_{S}=\prod\limits_{i\in S}r_{i}

Proof.

By inclusion-exclusion, as in the product setting we have

E_{S}f=\sum_{T\subseteq S}f^{=T}.

With this in hand, expanding
T_{r}f
gives:

\displaystyle T_{r}f

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{S\subset[d]}\prod_{i\in S}r_{i}\prod_{i\notin S}(1-r_{i})E_{S}f

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{S\subset[d]}\prod_{i\in S}r_{i}\prod_{i\notin S}(1-r_{i})\sum\limits_{T\subseteq S}f^{=T}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{T\subset[d]}f^{=T}\left(\sum\limits_{S\supset T}\prod_{i\in S}r_{i}\prod_{i\notin S}(1-r_{i})\right)

(Re-indexing)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{T\subset[d]}r_{T}f^{=T}\left(\sum\limits_{S\supset T}\prod_{i\in S\setminus T}r_{i}\prod_{i\notin S}(1-r_{i})\right)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{T\subset[d]}r_{T}f^{=T}

where in the final step we have observed that

\sum\limits_{S\supset T}\prod_{i\in S\setminus T}r_{i}\prod_{i\notin S}(1-r_{i})=\prod_{i\in[d]\setminus T}(1-r_{i}+r_{i})=1.

∎

One can also view this as an equivalent method of defining the operator
T_{r}
. We will move freely between these two equivalent notions in what follows. We can similarly express the Laplacians in terms of Efron-Stein. The proofs are standard (see e.g.
[
37
, Lemma 6.2]
) and similar to the above so we omit them.

Lemma 3.16
.

Let
X
be a
d
-partite complex and
f\in C_{d}
. Then:

1.

L_{i}f=\sum\limits_{S\ni i}f^{=S}

2.

\sum\limits_{i\in[d]}L_{i}f=\sum\limits_{S\subseteq[d]}|S|f^{=S}

This implies the following useful relation between total influence and the Efron-Stein decomposition.

Corollary 3.17
.

Let
X
be a
d
-partite complex and
f\in C_{d}
. Then

\mathbf{I}[f]=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{d}i\langle f,f^{=i}\rangle

4
Coordinate-Wise Analysis on HDX

The cornerstone of our analysis is a new elementary method of coordinate-wise analysis on high dimensional expanders inspired by the proof of Bourgain’s symmetrization theorem. We break the argument into two main parts, a
de-correlation
step where we break the noise operator into coordinate-wise components, and a
localization
step where we show the coordinate-wise noise operators may be viewed as the standard noise operator on the projection/localization to the relevant coordinates. We will see in the next section that these lemmas facilitate classic analytic tools such as the replacement method.

We first define a coordinate-wise version of the noise operator that operates only over a specific subset.

Definition 4.1

(Coordinate-Wise Noise Operator)
.

Let
X
be a
d
-partite complex,
S\subseteq[d]
, and
r\in\mathbb{R}^{S}
. The coordinate-wise noise operator
T^{S}_{r}
acts on
f
by:

T_{r}^{S}f\coloneqq\sum\limits_{T\subseteq S}r_{S\setminus T}\prod_{i\in T}(1-r_{i})E_{[d]\setminus T}f

Note that we have inverted the sum in the sense that index
T
corresponds to
E_{[d]\setminus T}
. When
r\in[0,1]^{d}
, the above should still be thought of as re-sampling each coordinate within
S
with probability
1-r_{i}
.

Our first key lemma shows that
T_{r}
can be approximately decomposed into its constituent coordinate operators (under any ordering) up to some error in
q
-norm. For simplicity of notation, given a permutation
\pi\in S_{d}
and vector
r\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we write

T^{\pi}_{r}\coloneqq T^{\pi(1)}_{{r_{\pi}(1)}}\ldots T^{\pi(d)}_{r_{\pi(d)}}.

Lemma 4.2

(Decorrelation)
.

Let
X
be a
d
-partite
(q,\gamma)
-product,
r\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
\pi\in S_{d}
any permutation, then:

\left\lVert T_{r}f-T^{\pi}_{r}f\right\rVert_{q}\leq c_{d,r}\gamma\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{q}

where
c_{d,r}=d^{3}\sum\limits_{S}\left|r_{S}\prod\limits_{i\notin S}(1-r_{i})\right|

Proof.

The proof is essentially an immediate application of
Claim

3.10
. Assume
\pi=I
without loss of generality (the analysis is invariant under ordering). Expanding out the coordinate-wise product we have

\displaystyle T^{1}_{r_{1}}\ldots T^{d}_{r_{d}}

\displaystyle=\prod\limits_{i\in[d]}(r_{i}I+(1-r_{i})E_{[d]\setminus i})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{S}\left(r_{S}\prod_{i\notin S}(1-r_{i})\right)\prod_{i\notin S}E_{[d]\setminus i}.

It is therefore enough to bound the
q
-norm

\left\lVert E_{S}-\prod_{i\notin S}E_{[d]\setminus i}\right\rVert_{q}\leq d^{3}\gamma.

This follows from iterated application of
Claim

3.10
. More formally, assume without loss of generality that
S=\{1,\ldots,j\}
. Then we can write the following telescopic sum:

\displaystyle E_{S}-\prod_{i\notin S}E_{[d]\setminus i}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{d-j}\left(\left(\prod_{i=j+1}^{d-\ell}E_{[d]\setminus i}\right)E_{[d-\ell]}-\left(\prod_{i=j+1}^{d-\ell+1}E_{[d]\setminus i}\right)E_{[d-\ell+1]}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{d-j}\left(\left(\prod_{i=j+1}^{d-\ell}E_{[d]\setminus i}\right)E_{[d-\ell]}-\left(\prod_{i=j+1}^{d-\ell}E_{[d]\setminus i}\right)E_{[d]\setminus\{d-\ell+1\}}E_{[d-\ell+1]}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{d-j}\left(\prod_{i=j+1}^{d-\ell}E_{[d]\setminus i}\right)\left(E_{[d-\ell]}-E_{[d]\setminus\{d-\ell+1\}}E_{[d-\ell+1]}\right)

By
Claim

3.10
and the triangle inequality, we finally have:

\displaystyle\left\lVert E_{S}-\prod_{i\notin S}E_{[d]\setminus i}\right\rVert_{q}

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{d-j}\left\lVert\left(\prod_{i=j+1}^{d-\ell}E_{[d]\setminus i}\right)\left(E_{[d-\ell]}-E_{[d]\setminus\{d-\ell+1\}}E_{[d-\ell+1]}\right)\right\rVert_{q}

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{d-j}\left\lVert E_{[d-\ell]}-E_{[d]\setminus\{d-\ell+1\}}E_{[d-\ell+1]}\right\rVert_{q}

\displaystyle\leq d^{3}\gamma

where we have additionally taken advantage of the fact that averaging operators contract
q
-norm.
∎

The second critical lemma is our ‘localization’ process, allowing us to view the coordinate-wise noise operators as local copies of the full noise operator on the links of
X
.

Lemma 4.3

(Localization)
.

Let
X
be a
d
-partite simplicial complex, and
r\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then for any
S\subseteq[d]
:

T^{S}_{r}f(x)=T^{x_{\bar{S}}}_{r}f|_{x_{\bar{S}}}(x_{S})

Proof.

The proof is essentially immediate from the fact that the partite averaging operators ‘respect’ restriction, namely for any
T\subseteq S
we have by definition:

E_{[d]\setminus T}f(x)=E^{x_{\bar{S}}}_{S\setminus T}f|_{x_{\bar{S}}}(x_{S}).

Then expanding out
T_{r}^{S}f
gives:

\displaystyle T_{r}^{S}f(x)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{T\subseteq S}r_{T\setminus S}\prod_{i\in T}(1-r_{i})E_{[d]\setminus T}f(x)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{T\subseteq S}r_{T\setminus S}\prod_{i\in T}(1-r_{i})E^{x_{\bar{S}}}_{S\setminus T}f|_{x_{\bar{S}}}(x_{S})

\displaystyle=T^{x_{\bar{S}}}_{r}f|_{x_{\bar{S}}}(x_{S})

as desired.
∎

5
The Symmetrization Theorem

We are now ready to turn to our first major result, Bourgain’s symmetrization theorem for HDX.

Theorem 5.1

(Symmetrization on HDX)
.

Let
q>1
and
X
be a
d
-partite
(q,\gamma)
-product for
\gamma\leq 2^{-\Omega(d)}
. Then for any
f\in C_{d}
:

(1-2^{O(d)}\gamma)\left\lVert\widetilde{T_{c_{q}}f}\right\rVert_{q}\leq\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{q}\leq(1+2^{O(d)}\gamma)\left\lVert\widetilde{T_{2}f}\right\rVert_{q}

for some constant
0\leq c_{q}\leq 1
dependent only on
q
.

Combined with
Lemma

3.4
, we immediately get symmetrization for standard
\gamma
-products and HDX.

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
q\geq 1
and
X
be a
\gamma
-product with
\gamma\leq 2^{-\Omega(\max\{q,q^{\prime}\}d)}
. For any
f\in C_{d}
:

\left(1-2^{O(d)}\gamma^{\frac{2}{\max\{q,q^{\prime}\}}}\right)\left\lVert\widetilde{T_{c_{q}}f}\right\rVert_{q}\leq\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{q}\leq\left(1+2^{O(d)}\gamma^{\frac{2}{\max\{q,q^{\prime}\}}}\right)\left\lVert\widetilde{T_{2}f}\right\rVert_{q}

for
q^{\prime}
the Hölder conjugate of
q
. For
q\in\{4,4/3\}
, one may take
c_{q}=2/5
.

The proof closely follows the ideas of Bourgain, as presented by O’Donnell
[
68
]
. Based on the machinery developed in the previous section, the idea is to decompose
T_{\rho}
into coordinate-wise operators and handle each coordinate as a single-variate problem, replacing each copy of
T_{\rho}^{i}
with
T_{r}^{i}
for
r\in\{-1,1\}
. To this end, we first need the single-variate symmetrization theorem.

Lemma 5.3

(Symmetrization for random variables (10.14,10.15 in
[
68
]
))
.

Let
X
be a
0
-mean, real-valued random variable satisfying
\left\lVert X\right\rVert_{q}\leq\infty
. Then for any
a\in\mathbb{R}
, we have:

\left\lVert a+\frac{1}{2}X\right\rVert_{q}\leq\left\lVert a+rX\right\rVert_{q}

where
r\sim\{-1,1\}
is a uniformly distributed random bit.

With this in hand, we can prove a single-coordinate variant of the result by restricting our function to the relevant variable and applying the above.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
X
be a
d
-partite complex and
f\in C_{d}
. For any
i\in[d]
:

\left\lVert T_{1/2}^{i}f\right\rVert_{q}\leq\left\lVert T^{i}_{r}f\right\rVert_{q}

where
r\sim\{-1,1\}
is a uniformly distributed random bit.

Proof.

\displaystyle\left\lVert T^{i}_{1/2}f\right\rVert_{q}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\left\lVert T^{x_{-i}}_{1/2}f|_{x_{-i}}\right\rVert_{q,x_{i}}\right\rVert_{q,x_{-i}}

(
Lemma

4.3
)

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\left\lVert\left(f|_{x_{-i}}\right)^{\{\emptyset\}}+\frac{1}{2}\left(f|_{x_{-i}}\right)^{=\{i\}}\right\rVert_{q,x_{i},r}\right\rVert_{q,x_{-i}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\left\lVert\left(f|_{x_{-i}}\right)^{\{\emptyset\}}+r\left(f|_{x_{-i}}\right)^{=\{i\}}\right\rVert_{q,x_{i},r}\right\rVert_{q,x_{-i}}

(
Lemma

5.3
)

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\left\lVert T_{r}f|_{x_{-i}}\right\rVert_{q,x_{i}}\right\rVert_{q,x_{-i},r}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert T^{i}_{r}f\right\rVert_{q}

(
Lemma

4.3
)

∎

We are now ready to prove the upper bound of
Theorem

5.1
via the replacement method.

Proof of
Theorem

5.1
(Upper Bound).

We first argue it is sufficient to show

\left\lVert T_{1/2}f\right\rVert_{q}\leq\left\lVert T_{r}f\right\rVert_{q}+2^{O(d)}\gamma\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{q}

(4)

In particular we may write:

\displaystyle\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{q}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert T_{1/2}T_{2}g\right\rVert_{q}+2^{O(d)}\gamma\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{q}

(
Lemma

3.12
)

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert T_{r}T_{2}g\right\rVert_{q}+2^{O(d)}\gamma\left\lVert T_{2}g\right\rVert_{q}+2^{O(d)}\gamma\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{q}

(
Equation

4
)

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert T_{r}T_{2}g\right\rVert_{q}+2^{O(d)}\gamma\left\lVert\sum\limits_{S\subseteq[d]}2^{|S|}g^{=S}\right\rVert_{q}+2^{O(d)}\gamma\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{q}

(
Claim

3.15
)

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert T_{r}T_{2}g\right\rVert_{q}+2^{O(d)}\gamma\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{q}

(
Lemma

3.8
)

Re-arranging and using the fact that
\frac{1}{1-2^{O(d)}\gamma}\leq 1+2^{O(d)}\gamma
for small enough
\gamma
gives the desired bound.

We prove
Equation

4
by the replacement method. In particular, we first start with de-correlating the relevant noise operators into their coordinate-wise components by
Lemma

4.2
:

1.

\left\lVert T_{1/2}f-T^{1}_{1/2}\ldots T^{d}_{1/2}f\right\rVert_{q}\leq 2^{O(d)}\gamma\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{q}.

2.

\left\lVert T_{r}f-T^{1}_{r_{1}}\ldots T^{d}_{r_{d}}f\right\rVert_{q}\leq 2^{O(d)}\gamma\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{q}.

Thus it is sufficient to prove

\left\lVert T^{1}_{1/2}\ldots T^{d}_{1/2}f\right\rVert_{q}\leq\left\lVert T^{1}_{r_{1}}\ldots T^{d}_{r_{d}}f\right\rVert_{q}+2^{O(d)}\gamma\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{q}.

We now iterate through coordinates one by one replacing
T_{1/2}
with
T_{r_{i}}
. Toward this end, define
T^{(j)}
to be the partially replaced operator at step
j
:

T^{(j)}\coloneqq T^{1}_{r_{1}}\ldots T^{j}_{r_{j}}T^{j+1}_{1/2}\ldots T^{d}_{1/2}.

We’ll prove for all
j\in\{0,\ldots,d\}
:

\left\lVert T^{(j)}f\right\rVert_{q}\leq\left\lVert T^{(j+1)}f\right\rVert_{q}+2^{O(d)}\gamma\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{q}.

(5)

Then we clearly have

\displaystyle\left\lVert T^{1}_{1/2}\ldots T^{d}_{1/2}f\right\rVert_{q}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert T^{(0)}f\right\rVert_{q}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert T^{(d)}f\right\rVert_{q}+2^{O(d)}\gamma\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{q}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert T^{1}_{r_{1}}\ldots T^{d}_{r_{d}}f\right\rVert_{q}+2^{O(d)}\gamma\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{q}

as desired.

It remains to prove
Equation

5
, which now follows exactly as in the standard proof up to the accumulation of error. In particular, we may simply permute the
(j+1)
st operator to the front using
Lemma

4.2
, apply our single-coordinate symmetrization theorem to the remaining function, and permute back. Formally, observe that by
Lemma

4.2
any two orderings
\pi
and
\sigma
and
r\in[-1,1]^{d}
satisfy:

\left\lVert T^{\pi}_{r}f-T^{\sigma}_{r}f\right\rVert_{q}=\left\lVert(T^{\pi}_{r}f-T_{r}f)+(T_{r}f-T^{\sigma}_{r}f)\right\rVert_{q}\leq 2^{O(d)}\gamma\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{q}

(6)

by the triangle inequality. Thus we can write:

\displaystyle\left\lVert T^{(j)}f\right\rVert

\displaystyle=\left\lVert T^{1}_{r_{1}}\ldots T^{j}_{r_{j}}T^{j+1}_{1/2}\ldots T^{d}_{1/2}f\right\rVert_{q}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert T^{j+1}_{1/2}(T^{1}_{r_{1}}\ldots T^{j}_{r_{j}}T^{j+2}_{1/2}\ldots T^{d}_{1/2}f)\right\rVert_{q}+2^{O(d)}\gamma\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{q}

(
Equation

6
)

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert T^{j+1}_{r_{j+1}}(T^{1}_{r_{1}}\ldots T^{j}_{r_{j}}T^{j+2}_{1/2}\ldots T^{d}_{1/2}f)\right\rVert_{q}+2^{O(d)}\gamma\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{q}

(
Lemma

5.4
)

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert(T^{1}_{r_{1}}\ldots T^{j+1}_{r_{j+1}}T^{j+2}_{1/2}\ldots T^{d}_{1/2}f)\right\rVert_{q}+2^{O(d)}\gamma\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{q}

(
Equation

6
)

\displaystyle=\left\lVert T^{(j+1)}f\right\rVert_{q}+2^{O(d)}\gamma\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{q},

completing the proof.
∎

The proof of the lower bound is similar. We start again with a closely related single-variate lemma.

Lemma 5.5

(
[
68
, Lemma 10.43]
)
.

For any
q\geq 2
, there is an absolute constant
c_{q}\in(0,1)
such that for any mean-
0
, real-valued random variable
X
satisfying
\left\lVert X\right\rVert_{q}\leq\infty
and
a\in\mathbb{R}
:

\left\lVert a-c_{q}X\right\rVert_{q}\leq\left\lVert a+X\right\rVert_{q}.

For
q\in\{4,4/3\}
, we may take
c_{q}=2/5
.

We can now prove the lower bound in
Theorem

5.1
by similar arguments to the above.

Proof of
Theorem

5.1
(Lower Bound).

Similar to the upper bound, it suffices to show

\left\lVert T^{1}_{r_{1}c_{q}}\ldots T^{d}_{r_{d}c_{q}}f\right\rVert_{q}\leq\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{q}+2^{O(d)}\gamma\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{q}.

Observe that we may re-write the lefthand side of the above inequality by breaking the norm into its boolean and product components:

\left\lVert T^{1}_{r_{1}c_{q}}\ldots T^{d}_{r_{d}c_{q}}f\right\rVert_{q}=\left\lVert\left\lVert T^{1}_{r_{1}c_{q}}\ldots T^{d}_{r_{d}c_{q}}f\right\rVert_{q,X}\right\rVert_{q,r}.

It is therefore sufficient to prove that the operators
T^{i}_{r_{i}c_{q}}
contract the inner
q
-norm for fixed
r_{i}\in\{\pm 1\}
. Since
0\leq c_{q}\leq 1
,
T^{i}_{c_{q}}
is an averaging operator and contracts
q
-norms by Jensen’s inequality. Thus it is enough to show that for any
g
with finite
q
-norm:

\left\lVert T^{i}_{-c_{q}}g\right\rVert_{q}\leq\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{q}.

By our localization lemma,
Lemma

4.3
, it is sufficient to show the ‘single-variate’ version of this contraction for
T_{-c_{q}}
on any co-dimension 1 link, as then:

\left\lVert T^{i}_{-c_{q}}g\right\rVert_{q}=\left\lVert\left\lVert T^{x_{-i}}_{-c_{q}}g|_{x^{-i}}\right\rVert_{q,x_{i}}\right\rVert_{q,x_{-i}}\leq\left\lVert\left\lVert g|_{x^{-i}}\right\rVert_{q,x_{i}}\right\rVert_{q,x_{-i}}\leq\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{q}.

Since
T^{x_{-i}}_{-c_{q}}
is self-adjoint, it is also enough to prove the inequality just for
q\geq 2
case by Hölder conjugation (c.f.
Lemma

3.5
). We can now appeal to
Lemma

5.5
. In particular for any single variate function

\displaystyle\left\lVert T_{-c_{q}}h\right\rVert_{q}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert h^{=\emptyset}-c_{q}h^{=1}\right\rVert_{q}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert h^{=\emptyset}+h^{=1}\right\rVert

(
Lemma

5.5
)

\displaystyle=\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{q}

since
h^{=1}
is mean-
0
and bounded. This holds in particular for
h=g|_{x^{-i}}
on the co-dim 1 links of
X
, completing the proof.
∎

6
Global Hypercontractivity

We now give our first application of the symmetrization theorem, a (relatively) simple proof of nearly-sharp global hypercontractivity on HDX.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
q\geq 2
be an even integer and
X
any
d
-partite
\gamma

\gamma\leq 2^{-\Omega(qd)}
. For any

\left\lVert f^{\leq i}\right\rVert_{q}^{q}\leq(500q)^{qi}\left\lVert f^{\leq i}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\max_{|S|\leq i,x_{S}}\{\left\lVert f|_{x_{S}}\right\rVert_{2}^{q-2}\}+c_{d,q}\gamma\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\max_{|S|\leq i,x_{S}}\{\left\lVert f|_{x_{S}}\right\rVert_{2}^{q-2}\}

.

Note this implies
Theorem

1.2
by replacing
\left\lVert f^{\leq i}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}
with
(1+2^{O(d)}\gamma)\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{2}^{2}
via approximate Parseval, and observing the error term is everywhere dominated by the main term for small enough

.
8
8

8

To be fully formal, one should note the above can be proved with a constant slightly less than
500
, the error term then increases the constant to
500
as stated in intro. Note we have made no attempt to optimize the constant.

Before proving the result, we prove the two corollaries discussed in the introduction. Recall we call a function

r

\forall S\subset[d],x_{S}\in\text{Supp}(X[S]):\left\lVert f|_{x_{S}}\right\rVert_{2}\leq r^{|S|}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{2}.

Our first corollary is hypercontractivity of the noise operator for global functions.

Corollary 6.2
.

Let
q>2
be an even integer,
r\geq 1
, and
X
be a
d
-partite
\gamma
-product satisfying
\gamma\leq 2^{-\tilde{\Omega}(qd)}

-global function

\left\lVert T_{\rho}f\right\rVert_{q}\leq(1+c_{d,q}(\gamma))\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{2},

where

goes to

\gamma\to 0

Proof.

We use a variant of the standard proof of hypercontractivity from the Bonami lemma (c.f.
[
68
, Exercise 9.6]
) adapted for global functions and lack of independence.

First, observe that applying
Lemma

3.11
and

6.1
directly we have the weaker bound

\displaystyle\left\lVert T_{\rho}f\right\rVert_{q}

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{i=0}^{d}\rho^{i}\left\lVert f^{=i}\right\rVert_{q}+2^{O(d)}\gamma\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{i=0}^{d}\rho^{i}q^{O(i)}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{2}+2^{O(d)}\gamma\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{2}

whenever
\gamma\leq 2^{-\Omega(q\log(q)d)}
.
9
9

9

Note we have applied
Theorem

6.1
to
\left\lVert f^{=j}\right\rVert_{q}
instead of
\left\lVert f^{\leq j}\right\rVert_{q}
and used the form of the bound from
Theorem

1.2
— it is easily seen the statement still holds for this restricted case, or one can simply use
q
-approximate Efron Stein to move to
\left\lVert f^{\leq j}\right\rVert_{q}
first.

To improve the constant from
2
to
(1+o_{\gamma}(1))
, we will actually apply the above argument instead to the powered complex
X^{t}
containing all faces

(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t}):x_{i}\in X(d)

endowed with the corresponding product measure
\prod_{i=1}^{t}\Pi(x_{i})
. Note that this is a complex of dimension

. Similarly given

, we define the corresponding powered function on

as

f^{\oplus t}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})=\prod\limits_{i=1}^{t}f(x_{i}).

Because the
x_{i}

acts independently on each part. As a result it is easy to check that for any
\rho\in[0,1]
,
f:X(d)\to\mathbb{R}
, and

\left\lVert T_{\rho}f^{\oplus t}\right\rVert_{p}=\left\lVert T_{\rho}f\right\rVert_{p}^{t},

and moreover that if

r

is
r
-global as well. This means that we can apply the above argument for any

\gamma\leq 2^{-\Omega(q\log(q)dt)}
to get

\left\lVert T_{\rho}f\right\rVert_{q}=\left\lVert T_{\rho}f^{\oplus t}\right\rVert^{1/t}_{q}\leq 2^{1/t}\left\lVert f^{\oplus t}\right\rVert_{2}^{1/t}\leq(1+O(1/t))\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{2}.

Finally setting
t=O(\frac{\log(\frac{1}{\gamma})}{q\log(q)d})
gives the claimed result.
∎

Our second corollary is an optimal characterization of low influence functions on HDX.

Corollary 6.3
.

Let
X
be a
d
-partite
\gamma
-product with
\gamma\leq 2^{-\Omega(d)}
and
f:X\to\mathbb{F}_{2}
any function with influence
\mathbf{I}[f]\leq K\text{Var}(f)
. There exists
S\subset[d]
with
|S|\leq O(K)
and
x_{S}\in X[S]
such that

\mathbb{E}[f|_{x_{S}}]\geq 2^{-O(K)}.

Proof.

The proof follows from a standard ‘level-
i
inequality’ implied by global hypercontractivity. Namely for any boolean function
f
, we claim the degree at most
i
Fourier mass is bounded by

\langle f,f^{\leq i}\rangle\leq 2^{O(i)}\mathbb{E}[f]\max_{|S|\leq i,x_{S}}\{\mathbb{E}[f|_{x_{S}}]^{1/4}\}

This follows from a basic application of Hölder’s inequality:

\displaystyle\langle f,f^{\leq i}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{4/3}\left\lVert f^{\leq i}\right\rVert_{4}

(Hölder)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{O(i)}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{4/3}\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{1/2}_{2}\max_{|S|\leq i,x_{S}}\{\left\lVert f|_{x_{S}}\right\rVert_{2}^{1/2}\}

(
Theorem

6.1
,
q=4
)

\displaystyle=2^{O(i)}\mathbb{E}[f]\max_{|S|\leq i,x_{S}}\{\mathbb{E}[f|_{x_{S}}]^{1/4}\}

(Booleanity)

On the other hand, recall the influence of
f
can be written as
\mathbf{I}[f]=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{d}i\langle f,f^{=i}\rangle
, so in particular

K\text{Var}(f)\geq\mathbf{I}[f]\geq(K+1)\left(\text{Var}(f)-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{K}\langle f,f^{=i}\rangle\right)-2^{O(d)}\gamma\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{2}^{2}.

Re-arranging and using Booleanity, for small enough
\gamma
we then have

\langle f,f^{\leq K}\rangle\geq\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}[f],

which combined with the level-
i
inequality implies the claimed bound.
∎

Proof of

Finally we apply

\displaystyle 2^{O(dt)}\gamma\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\max_{|I|\leq i,x_{I}}\{\left\lVert f|_{x_{I}}\right\rVert_{2}^{2t-2}\}

∎

Bourgain’s Booster Theorem

In this section we prove
\gamma

[

. We first define the relevant notion of a

Definition 7.1

Given

We prove that low influence functions on HDX have many (small support) boosters.

Theorem 7.2
.

Let
X

\gamma

\gamma\leq 2^{-\Omega(d)}
and

such that

\Pr_{x\sim X(d)}\left[\exists T\subset[d]:~|T|\leq O(K)\text{ and $x_{T}$ is a $\tau$-booster}\right]\geq\tau

Our proof closely follows O’Donnell’s treatment of Bourgain’s Theorem for product spaces in
[
68
]
, adjusting where necessary to handle lack of independence. The key technical component is to show that for any low influence function, it is possible to identify a small

set of coordinates which account for most of the Fourier mass.

Proposition 7.3

Let
X

\gamma

\gamma\leq 2^{-\Omega(d)}

J_{x}
is small:

\forall x,|J_{x}|\leq 2^{O(\ell)}

Small subsets of

\mathbb{E}\left[\sum\limits_{S\notin\mathcal{F}_{x}}f^{=S}(x)^{2}\right]\leq 3\varepsilon

We first prove the booster theorem assuming this fact. The proof is essentially exactly as in the product case (see e.g.

Proof of

We first argue that it is enough to show that with good probability over

With this in mind, our goal is now to define such sets

so that

Set
\varepsilon

First, we observe that the

Covers of fractal interpolation surfaces with finite families of octahedrons
Bogdan Anghelina, Radu Miculescu

Abstract




Abstract.
In our previous work,
On the localization of Hutchinson-Barnsley fractals
, Chaos Solitons Fractals, 173 (2023), 113-674, we presented a method for finding a finite family of closed balls whose union contains the attractor of a given iterated function system. In this paper, for the particular framework of fractal interpolation surfaces, we provide an improved version of it. This approach is more efficient, from the computational point of view, as it is based on finding the maximum of certain sets, in contrast to the previous method which uses a sorting algorithm.

Keywords:
fractal interpolation surfaces, octahedrons, covers

In

we presented a method for finding a finite family of closed balls whose union contains the attractor of a given iterated functions system, and we explained its relevance. More precisely, if
S=((X,d),(f_{i})_{i\in I})
is such a system consisting of at least two functions and
\rho_{i}\in[0,\infty)
,
i\in I
, represent a solution of the system of equations

\rho_{i}=\text{Lip}(f_{i})(\max_{i,j\in I}d(e_{i},e_{j})+\max_{j\in I\setminus\{i\}}\rho_{j}),

where
e_{i}
is the unique fixed point of
f_{i}
, then

\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}}\subseteq\bigcup_{i\in I}B[e_{i},\rho_{i}],

where
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}}
designates the attractor of
\mathcal{S}
.

Let us recall that in order to obtain a solution of
(\star)
we relabeled the functions
f_{i}
such that
\text{Lip}(f_{i_{1}})\leq\text{Lip}(f_{i_{2}})\leq\dots\leq\text{Lip}(f_{i_{n}})
, i.e. we ordered the set
\{\text{Lip}(f_{i})|i\in I\}
, where
I=\{i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{n}\}
. From the computational point of view this approach is not very efficient.

In this paper we provide an improved version of the above-mentioned method for the particular framework of fractal interpolation surfaces. One inconvenience that occurs in this case is the difficulty to find a concrete form of
\text{Lip}(f_{i})
in terms of the given data set. As, in view of the work presented in
[
8
]
, some constants
c_{i}\in[0,1)
, having the property that
d(f_{i}(x),f_{i}(y))\leq c_{i}d(x,y)
for all
x,y\in X
, are available, we remark that our previously mentioned result from
[
1
]
is still valid if one replaces
\text{Lip}(f_{i})
with
c_{i}
.

Moreover, we find a solution of the system
(\star)
via a more efficient approach, which is based on determining
\displaystyle\max_{i\in I}c_{i}
and
\displaystyle\max_{{j\in I},\;{c_{j}\neq\max_{i\in I}c_{i}}}c_{j}
rather than on the ordering of
\{\text{Lip}(f_{i})|i\in I\}
.

Some graphical representations for such covers of fractal interpolation surfaces are given.

Preliminaries

Given a metric space
(X,d)
,
x\in X
and
r>0
we shall use the following notation:

•

B[x,r]=\{y\in X\;|\;d(y,x)\leq r\}

•

P_{cp}(X)=\{A\subseteq X|A\text{ is non-empty and compact}\}

•

h
is the Hausdorff-Pompeiu metric.

For a Lipschitz function
f:X\to X
we shall denote by
\text{Lip}(f)
the Lipschitz constant of
f
.

Definition 2.1
.

An iterated function system (for short IFS) is a pair
\mathcal{S}=\textbf{(}(X,d),(f_{i})_{i\in I}\textbf{)}
, where
(X,d)
is a complete metric space and
f_{i}:X\to X
,
i\in I
, are Banach contractions.

The function
F_{\mathcal{S}}:P_{cp}(X)\to P_{cp}(X)
, given by

F_{\mathcal{S}}(K)=\bigcup_{i\in I}f_{i}(K),

for every
K\in P_{cp}(X)
, is called the fractal operator (or the Hutchinson operator) associated with
\mathcal{S}
.

Proposition 2.1
.

If
\mathcal{S}=\bm{(}(X,d),(f_{i})_{i\in I}\bm{)}
is an iterated function system, then
F_{\mathcal{S}}
is a contraction with respect to
h
. Its unique fixed point is denoted by
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}}
and it is called the attractor of
\mathcal{S}
since

\lim_{n\to\infty}\underbrace{(F_{\mathcal{S}}\circ F_{\mathcal{S}}\circ\dots\circ F_{\mathcal{S}})}_{n\text{ times }}(K)=\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}},

for each
K\in P_{cp}(X).

We shall consider

•

\mathcal{S}=((X,d),(f_{i})_{i\in\{1,\dots,n\}})

•

\gamma_{i}

•

•

•

.

•

The system
\mathfrak{S}
, with the unknowns
\rho_{1},\dots\rho_{n}
, consisting on the following
n
equations

\rho_{i}=c_{i}\left(M+\max_{j\in\{1,\dots,n\}\setminus\{i\}}\rho_{j}\right),

where
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
.

The arguments used on the proof of Proposition 3.3 from
[
1
]
ensure the validity of the following result:

Proposition 3.1
.

In the above-mentioned framework,

\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{S}}\subseteq\bigcup_{i\in\{1,\dots,n\}}B[\gamma_{i},\rho_{i}],

for each solution
\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{n}
of the system
\mathfrak{S}
.

Proposition 3.2
.

In the above-mentioned framework,

\rho_{i^{\prime}}=Mc_{i^{\prime}}\frac{1+c_{i^{\prime\prime}}}{1-c_{i^{\prime}}c_{i^{\prime\prime}}}

and

\rho_{i}=Mc_{i}\frac{1+c_{i^{\prime}}}{1-c_{i^{\prime}}c_{i^{\prime\prime}}},

for all
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}\setminus\{i^{\prime}\}
, represent a solution of the system
\mathfrak{S}
.

Proof.

Claim.

\rho_{i^{\prime}}\geq\rho_{i^{\prime\prime}}\geq\rho_{i},

for all
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}\setminus\{i^{\prime}\}
.

Justification of the Claim.
We have

c_{i^{\prime\prime}}(1+c_{i^{\prime}})=c_{i^{\prime\prime}}+c_{i^{\prime}}c_{i^{\prime\prime}}\leq c_{i^{\prime}}+c_{i^{\prime}}c_{i^{\prime\prime}}=c_{i^{\prime}}(1+c_{i^{\prime\prime}}),

so

\rho_{i^{\prime\prime}}=Mc_{i^{\prime\prime}}\frac{1+c_{i^{\prime}}}{1-c_{i^{\prime}}c_{i^{\prime\prime}}}\leq Mc_{i^{\prime}}\frac{1+c_{i^{\prime\prime}}}{1-c_{i^{\prime}}c_{i^{\prime\prime}}}=\rho_{i^{\prime}}.

Since
c_{i^{\prime\prime}}\geq c_{i}
for all
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}\setminus\{i^{\prime}\}
, we have

\rho_{i}=Mc_{i}\frac{1+c_{i^{\prime}}}{1-c_{i^{\prime}}c_{i^{\prime\prime}}}\leq Mc_{i^{\prime\prime}}\frac{1+c_{i^{\prime}}}{1-c_{i^{\prime}}c_{i^{\prime\prime}}}=\rho_{i^{\prime\prime}},

for all
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}\setminus\{i^{\prime}\}
.
Hence, the justification of the Claim is complete.

Therefore, taking into account the previous Claim, we get

\max_{j\in\{1,\dots,n\}\setminus\{i^{\prime}\}}\rho_{j}=\rho_{i^{\prime\prime}}.

(1)

and

\max_{j\in\{1,\dots,n\}\setminus\{i\}}\rho_{j}=\rho_{i^{\prime}},

(2)

for all
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}\setminus\{i^{\prime}\}
.

We have

\displaystyle\rho_{i^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=Mc_{i^{\prime}}\frac{1+c_{i^{\prime\prime}}}{1-c_{i^{\prime}}c_{i^{\prime\prime}}}=Mc_{i^{\prime}}\frac{1-c_{i^{\prime}}c_{i^{\prime\prime}}+c_{i^{\prime}}c_{i^{\prime\prime}}+c_{i^{\prime\prime}}}{1-c_{i^{\prime}}c_{i^{\prime\prime}}}=c_{i^{\prime}}\left(M+Mc_{i^{\prime\prime}}\frac{1+c_{i^{\prime}}}{1-c_{i^{\prime}}c_{i^{\prime\prime}}}\right)

\displaystyle=c_{i^{\prime}}(M+\rho_{i^{\prime\prime}})\overset{(1)}{=}c_{i^{\prime}}(M+\max_{j\in\{1,\dots,n\}\setminus\{i^{\prime}\}}\rho_{j})

and

\displaystyle\rho_{i}

\displaystyle=Mc_{i}\frac{1+c_{i^{\prime}}}{1-c_{i^{\prime}}c_{i^{\prime\prime}}}=Mc_{i}\frac{1-c_{i^{\prime}}c_{i^{\prime\prime}}+c_{i^{\prime}}c_{i^{\prime\prime}}+c_{i^{\prime}}}{1-c_{i^{\prime}}c_{i^{\prime\prime}}}=c_{i}\left(M+Mc_{i^{\prime}}\frac{1+c_{i^{\prime\prime}}}{1-c_{i^{\prime}}c_{i^{\prime\prime}}}\right)

\displaystyle=c_{i}(M+\rho_{i^{\prime}})\overset{(2)}{=}c_{i}(M+\max_{j\in\{1,\dots,n\}\setminus\{i\}}\rho_{j}),

for all
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}\setminus\{i^{\prime}\}

Hence, the above-mentioned values of

give a solution of the system
\mathfrak{S}
.

\displaystyle\{(x_{0},y_{l},z_{0,l})|l\in\{0,1,\dots,m\}\},

For

\begin{cases}a_{k}x_{0}+b_{k}&=x_{k-1}\\

\begin{cases}a_{k}x_{n}+b_{k}&=x_{k}\\

Using the first two equations of each system we obtain

.

Then

(a_{k}x_{k,l}+b_{k},c_{l}y_{k,l}+d_{l},e_{k,l}x_{k,l}+f_{k,l}y_{k,l}+g_{k,l}z_{k,l}+\alpha_{k,l}x_{k,l}y_{k,l}+\beta_{k,l})=(x_{k,l},y_{k,l},z_{k,l}),

hence

\begin{cases}a_{k}x_{k,l}+b_{k}&=x_{k,l}\\
c_{l}y_{k,l}+d_{l}&=y_{k,l}\\
e_{k,l}x_{k,l}+f_{k,l}y_{k,l}+g_{k,l}z_{k,l}+\alpha_{k,l}x_{k,l}y_{k,l}+\beta_{k,l}&=z_{k,l}\end{cases}.

From the first two equation we deduce that

x_{k,l}=\frac{b_{k}}{1-a_{k}}\text{ and }y_{k,l}=\frac{d_{l}}{1-c_{l}}.

Substituting in the third equation we obtain

e_{k,l}\frac{b_{k}}{1-a_{k}}+f_{k,l}\frac{d_{l}}{1-c_{l}}+g_{k,l}z_{k,l}+\alpha_{k,l}\frac{b_{k}d_{l}}{(1-a_{k})(1-c_{l})}+\beta_{k,l}=z_{k,l},

therefore

z_{k,l}=\frac{1}{1-g_{k,l}}\left[e_{k,l}\frac{b_{k}}{1-a_{k}}+f_{k,l}\frac{d_{l}}{1-c_{l}}+\alpha_{k,l}\frac{b_{k}d_{l}}{(1-a_{k})(1-c_{l})}+\beta_{k,l}\right].

We conclude that

\gamma_{k,l}=\left(\frac{b_{k}}{1-a_{k}},\frac{d_{l}}{1-c_{l}},\frac{1}{1-g_{k,l}}\left[e_{k,l}\frac{b_{k}}{1-a_{k}}+f_{k,l}\frac{d_{l}}{1-c_{l}}+\alpha_{k,l}\frac{b_{k}d_{l}}{(1-a_{k})(1-c_{l})}+\beta_{k,l}\right]\right).

Describing balls in respect of
\rho

The closed ball with in respect of
\rho
, having radius
r>0
and center
(x_{0},y_{0},z_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, is the set

\displaystyle\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}|\rho((x,y,z),(x_{0},y_{0},z_{0}))\leq r\}=\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}||x-x_{0}|+|y-y_{0}|+\theta|z-z_{0}|\leq r\},

denoted by
O[(x_{0},y_{0},z_{0}),r]
.

This set is an octahedron having the following vertices

V_{1}\left(x_{0}+r,y_{0},z_{0}\right)
,

V_{2}\left(x_{0},y_{0}+r,z_{0}\right)
,

V_{3}\left(x_{0},y_{0},z_{0}+\frac{r}{\theta}\right)
,

V_{4}\left(x_{0}-r,y_{0},z_{0}\right)
,

V_{5}\left(x_{0},y_{0}-r,z_{0}\right)
and

V_{6}\left(x_{0},y_{0},z_{0}-\frac{r}{\theta}\right)
.

Obtaining a cover for
G_{f}

Summarizing, we have

\displaystyle\bullet\delta:=\max\{|a|,|b|,|c|,|d|\}.

\displaystyle\bullet\theta=\min\left\{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\right\},\text{ where }

\displaystyle\theta_{1}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\qquad\qquad\qquad 1,&&e_{1,1}=\dots=e_{n,m}=\alpha_{1,1}=\dots=\alpha_{n,m}=0;\\
&\frac{\displaystyle 1-\max_{k\in\{1,\dots,n\}}a_{k}}{\displaystyle 2\max_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k\in\{1,\dots,n\}}{l\in\{1,\dots,m\}}}(|e_{k,l}|+\delta|\alpha_{k,l}|)},&&\text{otherwise},\end{aligned}\right.

\displaystyle\theta_{2}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\qquad\qquad\qquad 1,&&f_{1,1}=\dots=f_{n,m}=\alpha_{1,1}=\dots=\alpha_{n,m}=0;\\
&\frac{\displaystyle 1-\max_{l\in\{1,\dots,m\}}c_{l}}{\displaystyle 2\max_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k\in\{1,\dots,n\}}{l\in\{1,\dots,m\}}}(|f_{k,l}|+\delta|\alpha_{k,l}|)},&&\text{otherwise},\end{aligned}\right.

\displaystyle\bullet M=\max_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{i,k\in\{1,\dots,n\}}{j,l\in\{1,\dots,m\}}}\rho(\gamma_{i,j},\gamma_{k,l})

and

\displaystyle\bullet\gamma_{k,l}=\left(\frac{b_{k}}{1-a_{k}},\frac{d_{l}}{1-c_{l}},\frac{1}{1-g_{k,l}}\left[e_{k,l}\frac{b_{k}}{1-a_{k}}+f_{k,l}\frac{d_{l}}{1-c_{l}}+\alpha_{k,l}\frac{b_{k}d_{l}}{(1-a_{k})(1-c_{l})}+\beta_{k,l}\right]\right)

\displaystyle\bullet C_{k,l}=\max\left\{a_{k}+\theta(|e_{k,l}|+\delta|\alpha_{k,l}|),\;c_{l}+\theta(|f_{k,l}|+\delta|\alpha_{k,l}|),g_{k,l}\right\}

for all
k\in\{1,\dots,n\}\text{ and }l\in\{1,\dots,m\}
.

Let us choose
k^{\prime},k^{\prime\prime}\in\{1,\dots,n\}
and
l^{\prime},l^{\prime\prime}\in\{1,\dots,m\}
such that

C_{k^{\prime},l^{\prime}}=\max_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k\in\{1,\dots,n\}}{l\in\{1,\dots,m\}}}C_{k,l}\qquad\text{and}\qquad C_{k^{\prime\prime},l^{\prime\prime}}=\max_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k\in\{1,\dots,n\}\setminus\{k'\}}{l\in\{1,\dots,m\}\setminus\{l'\}}}C_{k,l}.

Taking into account the previous results, we get our main result:

Theorem 4.1
.

In the previous framework, we have

G_{f}\subseteq\left(\bigcup_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k\in\{1,\dots,n\}\setminus\{k'\}}{l\in\{1,\dots,m\}\setminus\{l'\}}}O\left[\gamma_{k,l},MC_{k,l}\frac{1+C_{{k^{\prime},l^{\prime}}}}{1-C_{k^{\prime},l^{\prime}}C_{k^{\prime\prime},l^{\prime\prime}}}\right]\right)\bigcup O\left[\gamma_{k^{\prime},l^{\prime}},MC_{k^{\prime},l^{\prime}}\frac{1+C_{{k^{\prime\prime},l^{\prime\prime}}}}{1-C_{k^{\prime},l^{\prime}}C_{k^{\prime\prime},l^{\prime\prime}}}\right].

The set

\left(\bigcup_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{k\in\{1,\dots,n\}\setminus\{k'\}}{l\in\{1,\dots,m\}\setminus\{l'\}}}O\left[\gamma_{k,l},MC_{k,l}\frac{1+C_{{k^{\prime},l^{\prime}}}}{1-C_{k^{\prime},l^{\prime}}C_{k^{\prime\prime},l^{\prime\prime}}}\right]\right)\bigcup O\left[\gamma_{k^{\prime},l^{\prime}},MC_{k^{\prime},l^{\prime}}\frac{1+C_{{k^{\prime\prime},l^{\prime\prime}}}}{1-C_{k^{\prime},l^{\prime}}C_{k^{\prime\prime},l^{\prime\prime}}}\right],

which is a cover of
\mathcal{A}_{\mathscr{S}}=G_{f}
will be denoted by
\mathcal{C_{\mathscr{S}}}
.

For
p\in\mathbb{N}
, by
\mathcal{C}_{p}
we designate
\mathcal{C}_{\mathscr{S}_{p}}
, which is also a cover of
G_{f}
.

Remark 4.1
.

In view of Remark 3.1, by increasing
p
, the diameters of the octahedrons occurring in
\mathcal{C}_{p}
could be as small as we want.

Appendix

We provide the following algorithms, which were used to determine the covers presented in the previous examples.

Input:

x=[x_{0},\dots,x_{n}]
,
y=[y_{0},\dots,y_{m}]
,
z=\left[\begin{aligned} &z_{0,0},\dots,z_{0,m}\\
&\vdots\qquad\qquad\vdots\\
&z_{n,0},\dots,z_{n,m}\end{aligned}\right],

g=\left[\begin{aligned} &g_{1,1},\dots,g_{1,m}\\
&\vdots\qquad\qquad\vdots\\
&g_{n,1},\dots,g_{n,m}\end{aligned}\right].

Output:

\theta
,

F=\left[\begin{aligned} &F_{1,1},\dots,F_{1,m}\\
&\vdots\qquad\qquad\vdots\\
&F_{n,1},\dots,F_{n,m}\end{aligned}\right]
.

function

coeff(
x
,
y
,
z
,
g
)

:

for

i\leftarrow 1

to

n

do

F(i,j)(1)\leftarrow(x(i)-x(i-1))/(x(n)-x(0));

F(i,j)(2)\leftarrow(x(i)*x(n)-x(i-1)*x(0))/(x(n)-x(0));

for

j\leftarrow 1

to

m

do

p=z(i,j)-g(i,j)*z(n,m);

q=z(i-1,j)-g(i,j)*z(0,m);

r=z(i,j-1)-g(i,j)*z(n,0);

t=z(i-1,j-1)-g(i,j)*z(0,0);

F(i,j)(3)\leftarrow(y(j)-y(j-1))/(y(n)-y(0));

F(i,j)(4)\leftarrow(y(j)*y(n)-y(j-1)*y(0))/(y(n)-y(0));

F(i,j)(5)\leftarrow(y(0)*(q-p)-y(m)*(t-r))/((x(n)-x(0))*(y(m)-y(0)));

F(i,j)(6)\leftarrow(x(0)*(r-p)-x(n)*(t-q))/((x(n)-x(0))*(y(m)-y(0)));

F(i,j)(7)\leftarrow g(i,j);

F(i,j)(8)\leftarrow(p-q-r+t)/((x(n)-x(0))*(y(m)-y(0)));

F(i,j)(9)\leftarrow(y(0)*(x(0)*p-x(n)*q)-y(m)*(x(0)*r

-x(n)*t))/((x(n)-x(0))*(y(m)-y(0)));

return

F
;

function

theta(
F
,
\delta
)
:

for

i\leftarrow 1

to

n
and
j\leftarrow 1

to

m

do

[
a,b,c,d,e,f,g,\alpha,\beta]\leftarrow
F(i, j);

max_{a}\leftarrow\max(max_{a},a)
;

max_{c}\leftarrow\max(max_{c},c)
;

max_{1}\leftarrow\max(max_{1},|e|+\delta*|\alpha|)
;

max_{2}\leftarrow\max(max_{2},|f|+\delta*|\alpha|)
;

\theta_{1}=\theta_{2}=1

if

max_{1}\neq 0

then

\theta_{1}\leftarrow(1-max_{a})/(2*max_{1})
if

max_{2}\neq 0

then

\theta_{2}\leftarrow(1-max_{c})/(2*max_{2})

\theta=\min(\theta_{1},\theta_{2});

return

\theta
;

function

fixPT(
F
)
:

for

i\leftarrow 1

to

n
and
j\leftarrow 1

to

m

do

[a,b,c,d,e,f,g,\alpha,\beta]\leftarrow F(i,j);

\gamma(i,j)\leftarrow[b/(1-a),d/(1-c),(1/(1-g(i,j)))*(e*b/(1-a)

+f*d/(1-c)+\alpha*b*d/((1-a)*(1-c))+\beta)];

return

\gamma
;

const(
F
,
\theta
)
:

for

i\leftarrow 1

to

n
and
j\leftarrow 1

to

m

do

[a,b,c,d,e,\alpha,\beta]\leftarrow F(i,j);

c(i,j)\leftarrow\max(a+\theta*(|e|+\delta|\alpha|),c+\theta*(|f|+\delta|\alpha|),g(i,j)));

function

comp2(
[a_{1},b_{1},c_{1},d_{1},e_{1},f_{1},g_{1},\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}]
,
[a_{2},b_{2},c_{2},d_{2},e_{2},f_{2},g_{2},\alpha_{2},\beta_{2}]
)
:

a\leftarrow a_{1}*a_{2}
;

b\leftarrow a_{1}*b_{2}+b_{1};

c\leftarrow c_{1}*c_{2};

e\leftarrow e_{1}*a_{2}+g_{1}*e_{2}+\alpha_{1}*a_{2}*d_{2};

f\leftarrow f_{1}*c_{2}+g_{1}*f_{2}+\alpha_{1}*b_{2}*c_{2};

g\leftarrow g_{1}*g_{2};

\alpha\leftarrow\alpha_{1}*a_{2}*c_{2}+g_{1}*\alpha_{2};

\beta\leftarrow e_{1}*b_{2}+f_{1}*d_{2}+\alpha_{1}*b_{2}*d_{2}+g_{1}*\beta_{2}+\beta_{1};

return

[a,b,c,d,e,f,g,\alpha,\beta]
;

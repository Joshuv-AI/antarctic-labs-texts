Leibniz’s law and paraconsistent models of ZFC
Aldo Figallo-Orellano

Abstract
Full models for some non-classical Set Theories are exhibited, where these theories are built over Nelson’s logics N4 , N3 and da Costa’s logics C ω C_{\omega} and C ω 2 C^{2}_{\omega} . These models are constructed over Fidel semantics in which they are specific first-order structures in the sense of Model Theory. These structures are known in the literature as F F -structures and they are not algebras in the universal algebra sense. With these structures, it is possible to give Adequacy Theorems for each logic mentioned before, for the propositional level, using the saturated structures as Fidel and Odintsov have previously exhibited. In this paper, we start by considering Set Theories type ZF over both Nelson’s and da Costa’s logics using the propositional axioms for first-order formulas and set-theoretic axioms of Zermelo-Fraenkel. Looking for models for these Theories, we construct F F -structures valued models as adapting the machinery developed to Boolean and Heyting valued models cases. In this sense, we can mention that the first presentation of the F F -structures valued models was given by A. Figallo-Orellano and J. Slagter. As a by-product, we display how it is possible to present paraconsistent models for ZFC based on da Costa’s logics treated in this paper.

Introduction

Paraconsistency is the study of logic systems having a negation
\neg
which is not explosive; that is, formulas
\alpha

\beta

\beta

\{\alpha,\neg\alpha\}

Going back to Nelson logic. a paraconsistent version of Nelson logic was first introduced in
[
1
]

[
2
]
) by deleting the axiom schema

[
22
]
, who calls the logic
N4
, in terms of what is known in the literature as Fidel structures and twist-structures. In the case of Fidel’s structures (
F
-structures) for

-structures given by himself for

in
[
12
]
.

On the other hand, the C-systems

(

) and

Going to the central affair of this paper, recall that Boolean-valued models were introduced with the intention to simplify the

In this paper, we start by considering Set Theories type ZF over both Nelson’s and da Costa’s logics using the propositional axioms for first-order formulas and set-theoretic axioms of Zermelo-Fraenkel. Looking for models for these Theories, we construct

2.3
Zermelo-Fraenkel Set Theory: Boolean and Heyting valued models

In this part of the paper, we will present
Non-classical Set Theories
build over (paraconsistent) propositional logics, expressing its axioms on a suitable first-order version for Set Theory. We start by considering any logic
X
over the first-order signature
{\Theta}_{\omega}
which contains an equality predicate
\approx
and a binary predicate
\in
, where this logic
X
could be
N4
,
N3
,
C_{\omega}
or
C^{2}_{\omega}
over a fixed signature
\{\to,\wedge,\vee,\neg,\bot\}
; that is to say, for every formula
\alpha
we have
\sim\alpha:=\alpha\to\bot
. Clearly, these logics have a
\top
given by
\top:=\alpha\to\alpha
. At the same time, we will consider also the language in terms of the signature
\{\to,\wedge,\vee,\neg,\bot\}
for our Set Theories, where the negation
\neg
is in the original language of mentioned (paraconsistent) propositional logics; and with the bottom, we are able to express the classical or intuitionistic negation depending on the requirements.
The system
ZF_{X}
is the first-order system obtained from the logic
X
over
\Theta_{\omega}
by adding the following set-theoretic axiom schemas:

(Extensionality)

\forall x\forall y[\forall z(z\in x\leftrightarrow z\in y)\to(z\approx y)]
,

(Pairing)

\forall x\forall y\exists w\forall z[z\in w\leftrightarrow(z\approx x\vee z\approx y)]
,

(Collection)

\forall x[(\forall y\in x\exists z\phi(y,z))\to\exists w\forall y\in x\exists z\in w\phi(y,z)]
,
where
w
is not free in
\phi(y,z)
,

(Powerset)

\forall x\exists w\forall z[z\in w\leftrightarrow\forall y\in z(y\in x)]
,

(Separation)

\forall x\exists w\forall z[z\in w\leftrightarrow(z\in x\wedge\phi(z))]
,
where
w
is not free in
\phi(z)
,

(Empty set)

\exists x\forall z[z\in x\leftrightarrow((z\approx z)\to\bot)]
,
The set satisfying this axiom is, by extensionality, unique and we refer to it with notation
\emptyset
.

(Union)

\forall x\exists w\forall z[z\in w\leftrightarrow\exists y\in x(z\in y)]
,

(Infinity)

\exists x[\emptyset\in x\wedge\forall y\in x(y^{+}\in x)]
,
From union and pairing and extensionality, we can note by
y^{+}
the unique set
y\cup\{y\}
.

(Regularity)

\forall x[(\forall y\in x\phi(y))\to\phi(x)]\to\forall x\phi(x)
.
where
y
is not free in the formula
\phi(x)

These axioms are typically used to define Zermelo-Fraenkel Set Theory (for short
ZF
), however they are expressing expressing the classical and intuitionistic negation mentioned before for the axiom (Empty Set), see
[
5
]
. In this paper, we will focus on the task of presenting models for
ZF_{\bf N4}
,
ZF_{\bf N3}
,
ZF_{C^{2}_{\omega}}
and
ZF_{C_{\omega}}
, where these theories are constructed over the logic
X
mentioned before. It is important to note that both the logic
N4
,
C_{\omega}

Lemma 3.2

For a given complete Heyting algebra
\bf A
. Then,

As usual, we will adopt the following notation, for every formula

{\mathbf{V}}^{\langle{\bf A},N\rangle}\vDash c\,\,\text{is a maximal element of}\,\,x
.
Indeed, let us take
t\in{\mathbf{V}}^{\langle{\bf A},N\rangle}
and apply Corollary
5.7
, there is
z\in y
such that
[\![t\in x]\!]^{\langle{\bf A},N\rangle}=[\![t\approx z]\!]^{\langle{\bf A},N\rangle}
.
So,
[\![c\leq_{x}t\wedge t\in x]\!]^{\langle{\bf A},N\rangle}=[\![c\leq_{x}t\wedge t\approx z]\!]^{\langle{\bf A},N\rangle}\leq[\![c\leq_{x}z]\!]^{\langle{\bf A},N\rangle}
, by Leibniz’s law. Let us now
u
the mixture
a\cdot z+a^{*}\cdot c
where
a=[\![c\leq_{x}t]\!]^{\langle{\bf A},N\rangle}
and
a^{*}
is the Boolean complement of
a
. Then, by Mixing Lemma we have
a\leq[\![t\approx u]\!]^{\langle{\bf A},N\rangle}
and
a^{*}\leq[\![c\approx u]\!]^{\langle{\bf A},N\rangle}
. So,
[\![u\in x]\!]^{\langle{\bf A},N\rangle}=1
. From the latter and by the definition of
y
, there is
k\in y
such that
[\![k\approx u]\!]^{\langle{\bf A},N\rangle}=1
. On the other hand, it is not hard to see that
[\![c\leq_{x}u]\!]^{\langle{\bf A},N\rangle}=1
. So, by Leibniz law, we have
[\![c\leq_{x}k]\!]^{\langle{\bf A},N\rangle}=1
and then

c=k

c

[\![c\leq_{x}z]\!]^{\langle{\bf A},N\rangle}\leq[\![c\approx z]\!]^{\langle{\bf A},N\rangle}
and so
[\![c\leq_{x}t\wedge t\in x]\!]^{\langle{\bf A},N\rangle}\leq[\![t\approx c]\!]^{\langle{\bf A},N\rangle}
. Thus,
{\mathbf{V}}^{\langle{\bf A},N\rangle}\vDash\forall t\in x[(c\leq_{x}t)\to(t\approx c)]
as desired.
\square

Paraconsistent models

For every saturated
C_{\omega}
-structure valued model
{\mathbf{V}}^{\langle{\bf A},N\rangle}
, let us consider
u\in{\mathbf{V}}^{\langle{\bf A},N\rangle}
and every formula
\phi(x)
, we can always take
1=[\![\neg\psi(u)]\!]^{\langle{\bf A},N\rangle}\in N_{[\![\psi(u)]\!]^{\langle{\bf A},N\rangle}}
(recall that
1\in N_{[\![\psi(u)]\!]^{\langle{\bf A},N\rangle}}
) and
[\![\neg\neg\psi(u)]\!]^{\langle{\bf A},N\rangle}=[\![\psi(u)]\!]^{\langle{\bf A},N\rangle}
where
\psi(x)
is not the form
\neg\phi(x)
for some formula
\phi(x)
.
In this case, we are taking a unique interpretation of
[\![\cdot]\!]^{\langle{\bf A},N\rangle}
; i.e., it is a mapping. So, it is possible to see that Leibniz law is held and the axioms
(C1)
and
(C2)
(also
(C3)
) are sound. These conditions work perfectly for induction over the complexity of the formula; and more interestingly,
{\mathbf{V}}^{\langle{\bf A},N\rangle}
is paraconsistent with this interpretation. In order to see it, let us by considering the following
relation of consequence
, we write:

\Gamma\vDash_{{\mathbf{V}}^{\langle{\bf A},N\rangle}}\varphi
, if
[\![\gamma]\!]^{\langle{\bf A},N\rangle}=1
for every
\gamma\in\Gamma
,

then
[\![\varphi]\!]^{\langle{\bf A},N\rangle}=1
for arbitrary set of sentences
\Gamma\cup\{\varphi\}
.

Now, take the formula
\phi(x):=x\approx x
and for any
u\in{\mathbf{V}}^{\langle{\bf A},N\rangle}
, let us permit to take
[\![\neg(u\approx u)]\!]^{\langle{\bf A},N\rangle}=[\![u\approx u]\!]^{\langle{\bf A},N\rangle}=1
. Consider now the following saturated
C_{\omega}
-structure
{\cal N}_{3}=\langle\{0,\frac{1}{2},1\},\wedge,\vee,\to,\neg 1,N_{0},N_{\frac{1}{2}},N_{1}\rangle
where
N_{0}=\{1\}
,
N_{\frac{1}{2}}=\{1\}
and
N_{1}=\{0,\frac{1}{2},1\}
. Suppose that
v=\{\langle w,1\rangle\}
,
u=\{\langle w,\frac{1}{2}\rangle\}
, then we have that
[\![u\approx v]\!]^{{\cal N}_{3}}=\frac{1}{2}
. Therefore,
\{(u\approx u),\neg(u\approx u)\}\not\vDash_{{\cal N}_{3}}(u\approx v)
.

Here, we must to pay attention that this new notion of
semantic consequence
is different from what was considered above in the text, but clearly they share the same logical theorems. The systems could be very different in some ways. To see that, in the paper

]

6
Conclusions and future works

The problem of independence of Continuum Hypothesis (HC) from
ZF_{C_{\omega}}
can be treated using Boolean-valued models. It is clear that Boolean-valued models validate the axioms of
C_{\omega}
. Therefore, the classical proof could be used to see that (HC) is independent of
ZF_{C_{\omega}}
. The paraconsistent view of these affairs will be part of our future research. Moreover, we are interested in developing a technique to obtain a
paraconsistent forcing
. In particular, we will focus on paraconsistent set theories where Boolean-valued models are not models of these theories.

On the other hand, in this note we have presented some paraconsistent set theories where the models validate
Choice Axiom
(CA). In this setting, we consider to be of high interest the problem of independence in different PSTs.

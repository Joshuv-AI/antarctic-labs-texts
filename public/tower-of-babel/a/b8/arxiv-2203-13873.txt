Improved higher-order Sobolev inequalities on CR sphere
Zetian Yan

Abstract
. We improve higher-order CR Sobolev inequalities on S 2 ​ n + 1 S^{2n+1} under the vanishing of higher order moments of the volume element. As an application, we give a new and direct proof of the classification of minimizers of the CR invariant higher-order Sobolev inequalities. In the same spirit, we prove almost sharp Sobolev inequalities for GJMS operators to general CR manifolds, and obtain the existence of minimizers in C 2 ​ k ​ ( N ) C^{2k}(N) of higher-order CR Yamabe-type problems when Y k ​ ( N ) < Y k ​ ( ℍ n ) Y_{k}(N)<Y_{k}(\mathbb{H}^{n}) .

1.
Introduction

The main aim of this note is to improve CR Sobolev inequalities on
S^{2n+1}
under the vanishing of higher order moments of the area elements; see Theorem
1.1
for a precise statement. As a direct application, we simplify Frank and Lieb’s proof
[
FL12
]
of the existence of minimizers of the sharp Sobolev inequalities for the embeddings
S^{k,2}(\mathbb{H}^{n})\hookrightarrow L^{\frac{2n+2}{n+1-k}}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
. Moreover, using the Frank–Lieb argument developed in
[
Cas21
]
, we give a simple and direct classification of optimizers. In particular, our proof avoids complicated computations, partially addressing an open problem of Frank and Lieb
[
FL12
, pg.352]
.

Our derivation of Theorem
1.1
is motivated by recent work in
[
CH22
]
and
[
HW20
]
, where Aubin’s Moser–Trudinger–Onofri inequality on
S^{n}
and Sobolev inequalities on
S^{n}
are improved under the vanishing of higher order moments of the volume element, respectively. The key observation in
[
HW20
]
is that, if it is not true, there exists a sequence of functions
\{F_{i}\}
in
W^{1,p}(S^{n})
such that

\|\nabla F_{i}\|^{p}_{p}\leqslant\frac{1}{\alpha},\quad\|F_{i}\|_{p^{*}}=1,

and
\{F_{i}\}
converges to zero weakly in
L^{p}(S^{n})
, where
p^{*}=\frac{np}{n-p}
and
\alpha
is the leading coefficient of the improved Sobolev inequality
[
HW20
, Theorem 1.1]
. Combining this with the concentration compactness principle
[
Lio85
, Lemma 4.1]
, this contradicts the choice of
\alpha
. We will apply this idea to prove Theorem
1.1
in Section 3. The crucial point of the proof is the concentration compactness principle Lemma
3.1
on general CR manifolds.

Let
P_{k}^{\theta_{c}}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
with
k<n+1
, be the CR GJMS operators associated with the canonical contact form
\theta_{c}
on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. For any
f\in S^{k,2}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
, where
S^{k,2}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
is the Folland–Stein space introduced in
[
FS74
]
,
p=\frac{2Q}{Q-2k}
, the sharp Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequalities are given by

(1.1)

\begin{split}\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}|f(u)|^{p}du\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}&\leqslant C_{n,2k}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\bar{f}P_{k}^{\theta_{c}}fdu,\\
C_{n,2k}&=\left(\frac{1}{4\pi}\right)^{k}\frac{\Gamma^{2}(\frac{n+1-k}{2})}{\Gamma^{2}(\frac{n+1+k}{2})},\end{split}

where
du=\theta_{c}\wedge(d\theta_{c})^{n}
is the volume form associated with
\theta_{c}
on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
; see
[
FL12
]
for more details.

Equivalently, via the Cayley transform
\mathcal{C}:\mathbb{H}^{n}\to S^{2n+1}\backslash(0,\cdots,-1)
given by

\mathcal{C}(z,t)=\left(\frac{2z}{1+|z|^{2}+it},\frac{1-|z|^{2}-it}{1+|z|^{2}+it}\right),

we have for any
F\in S^{k,2}(S^{2n+1})
,

(1.2)

\left(\int_{S^{2n+1}}|F|^{p}d\xi\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}\leqslant C_{n,2k}\int_{S^{2n+1}}\bar{F}P_{k}^{\theta_{0}}Fd\xi,

where
d\xi=\theta_{0}\wedge(d\theta_{0})^{n}
is the volume form associated with the canonical contact form
\theta_{0}
on
S^{2n+1}
.

Recall that a homogeneous polynomial on
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
with bidegree
(w,w^{\prime})
is a polynomial
g
satisfying for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
,
z\in\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
,

g(\lambda z)=\lambda^{w}\bar{\lambda}^{w^{\prime}}g(z).

For a pair of nonnegative integers
(w,w^{\prime})
, we denote

\mathcal{P}_{w,w^{\prime}}:=\{
all homogeneous polynomials on
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
with bidegree
(w,w^{\prime})\}
,

\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{w,w^{\prime}}:=\bigcup\limits_{(\tilde{w},\tilde{w}^{\prime})}\mathcal{P}_{\tilde{w},\tilde{w}^{\prime}}
,  for all
\tilde{w}\leqslant w
,
\tilde{w}^{\prime}\leqslant w^{\prime}
,

\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{w,w^{\prime}}:=\left\{g\in\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{w,w^{\prime}};\int_{S^{2n+1}}gd\xi=0\right\}
.

For
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
0\leqslant\theta\leqslant 1
, as in
[
HW20
]
we define

\mathcal{M}^{c}_{w,w^{\prime}}(S^{2n+1}):=\{\nu
;
\nu
is a probability measure on
S^{2n+1}
supported on countably many points such that
\int_{S^{2n+1}}gd\nu=0
, for all
g\in\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{w,w^{\prime}}\}

and

\Theta({w,w^{\prime}};\theta,2n+1):=\inf\left\{\sum_{i}\nu^{\theta}_{i};\nu\in\mathcal{M}^{c}_{w,w^{\prime}}(S^{2n+1}),\mathrm{supp}(\nu)=\{x_{i}\}\subset S^{2n+1},\nu_{i}=\nu(x_{i})\right\}.

Our first result gives improved CR Sobolev inequalities under the assumption that higher moments vanish.

Theorem 1.1
.

Denote
Q=2n+2
,
p=\frac{2Q}{Q-2k}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
with
k<n+1
. Then for any
\epsilon>0
, and
F\in S^{k,2}(S^{2n+1})
with

(1.3)

\int_{S^{2n+1}}g|F|^{p}d\xi=0

for all
g\in\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{w,w^{\prime}}
, we have

(1.4)

\begin{split}\left(\int_{S^{2n+1}}|F|^{p}d\xi\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}&\leqslant\left(\frac{{C}_{n,2k}}{\Theta({w,w^{\prime}};\frac{Q-2k}{Q},2n+1)}+\epsilon\right)\int_{S^{2n+1}}\bar{F}P_{k}^{\theta_{0}}Fd\xi\\
&+C(\epsilon)\int_{S^{2n+1}}|F|^{2}d\xi,\end{split}

where
C(\epsilon)
is a constant depending on
\epsilon
.

When
w+w^{\prime}=1
, the condition
\int_{S^{2n+1}}gd\nu=0
for all
g\in\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{w,w^{\prime}}
is equivalent to the balanced conditions

(1.5)

\int_{S^{2n+1}}\xi_{i}d\nu=0,\quad i=1,\cdots,2n+2,

where
\{\xi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{2n+2}
are coordinate functions on
\mathbb{R}^{2n+2}
. Therefore, the exact value of
\Theta({w,w^{\prime}};\theta,2n+1)
is
2^{1-\theta}
given in
[
HW20
]
. We have the following corollary for balanced functions on
S^{2n+1}
.

Corollary 1.2
.

Denote
Q=2n+2
,
p=\frac{2Q}{Q-2k}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
with
k<n+1
. Then for any
\epsilon>0
, and
F\in S^{k,2}(S^{2n+1})
with

(1.6)

\int_{S^{2n+1}}\xi_{i}|F|^{p}d\xi=0,\quad i=1,\cdots,2n+2,

we have

(1.7)

\left(\int_{S^{2n+1}}|F|^{p}d\xi\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}\leqslant\left(\frac{{C}_{n,2k}}{2^{\frac{k}{n+1}}}+\epsilon\right)\int_{S^{2n+1}}\bar{F}P_{k}^{\theta_{0}}Fd\xi+C(\epsilon)\int_{S^{2n+1}}|F|^{2}d\xi,

where
C(\epsilon)
is a constant depending on
\epsilon
.

The author recently learned that P. Ho obtained a similar result independently. In his preprint, he gets improved inequalities for the embedding
S^{1,r}(S^{2n+1})\hookrightarrow L^{\frac{Qr}{Q-r}}(S^{2n+1})
with
0<r<Q
.

Remark 1.3
.

On the standard unit sphere
(S^{n},g_{0})
, it is known in
[
Aub76
, Theorem 2.45]
that for all
\varphi\in H^{1,2}(S^{n})
satisfying

(1.8)

\int_{S^{n}}\xi_{i}|\varphi|^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}d\xi=0,\quad i=1,\cdots,n+1,

we have

(1.9)

\|\varphi\|^{2}_{\frac{2n}{n-2}}\leqslant\left(\frac{K_{n,2}}{2^{\frac{2}{n}}}+\epsilon\right)\|\nabla\varphi\|^{2}_{2}+C(\epsilon)\|\varphi\|^{2}_{2},

where
K_{n,2}
is the sharp constant of the Sobolev inequality on
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and
C(\epsilon)
is a constant depending on
\epsilon
. Corollary
1.2
is a CR analogue of Aubin’s estimate which works for all orders
2k<Q
.

Remark 1.4
.

If we replace
\mathcal{P}_{w,w^{\prime}}
by
\mathcal{P}_{l}
defined in
[
HW20
]

\mathcal{P}_{l}:=\{
all polynomials on
\mathbb{R}^{2n+2}
with degree at most
l\}

with
w+w^{\prime}=l\in\mathbb{N}
, we can adapt the proof of Theorem
1.1
to obtain

(1.10)

\begin{split}\left(\int_{S^{2n+1}}|F|^{p}d\xi\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}&\leqslant\left(\frac{{C}_{n,2k}}{\Theta(l;\frac{Q-2k}{Q},2n+1)}+\epsilon\right)\int_{S^{2n+1}}\bar{F}P_{k}^{\theta_{0}}Fd\xi\\
&+C(\epsilon)\int_{S^{2n+1}}|F|^{2}d\xi,\end{split}

under the restriction

(1.11)

\int_{S^{2n+1}}g|F|^{p}d\xi=0

for all
g\in\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{l}
, where
\Theta(l;\frac{Q-2k}{Q},2n+1)
and
\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{l}
are analogues associated with
\mathcal{P}_{l}
. Since
\mathcal{P}_{w,w^{\prime}}
is a proper subspace of
\mathcal{P}_{l}
when
l\geqslant 2
, condition (
1.11
) is more restrictive than (
3.16
). Consequently, we have

\frac{{C}_{n,2k}}{\Theta(l;\frac{Q-2k}{Q},2n+1)}\leqslant\frac{{C}_{n,2k}}{\Theta(w,w^{\prime};\frac{Q-2k}{Q},2n+1)}.

Remark 1.5
.

Some known exact values of
\Theta(l;\theta,n-1)
are
\Theta(1;\theta,n-1)=2^{1-\theta}
and
\Theta(2;\theta,n-1)=(n+1)^{1-\theta}
by Hang–Wang
[
HW20
]
, and
\Theta(3;\theta,n-1)=(2n)^{1-\theta}
by Putterman
[
Put20
, Theorem 5.1]
, whose method is related to the idea of deriving cubature formulas, such as the technique of reproducing kernels on spheres, etc. In Proposition
3.2
, we compute
\Theta(1,1;\theta,2n+1)
following the idea in
[
HW20
]
. Indeed, it has been proved in
[
Put20
, Proposition 3.1]
that
\Theta(l;\theta,n-1)
can only be achieved by a Dirac probability measure supported on finitely many points. This directly implies that the infimum for
\Theta(l;\theta,n-1)
is a minimum, which follows from
[
Put20
, Corollary 3.2]
or
the proof of Proposition
3.2
below implicitly.

As a direct application, we give a new proof of sharp CR Sobolev inequalities on
S^{2n+1}
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
(S^{2n+1},\theta_{0})
be the CR unit sphere with the volume element
d\xi=\theta_{0}\wedge(d\theta_{0})^{n}
. Denote
Q=2n+2
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
with
k<n+1
. Then
u
is a positive minimizer of (
1.2
) if and only if

u(\eta)=\frac{C}{|1-\xi\cdot\bar{\eta}|^{\frac{Q-2k}{2}}},\quad\eta\in S^{2n+1}

for some
C\in\mathbb{C},\xi\in\mathbb{C}^{n+1},|\xi|<1
.

The key ideas in the proof of Theorem
1.6
are the following. After using the CR automorphism group
\mathcal{A}(S^{2n+1})
(see, for example,
[
BFM13
]
), without loss of generality, we may assume that any minimizing sequence
\{F_{i}\}
in
S^{k,2}(S^{2n+1})
is balanced. By the Lieb trick in
[
Lie83
, Lemma 2.6]
and the improved Sobolev inequalities in Corollary
1.2
, up to a subsequence, we show that any balanced minimizing sequence
\{F_{i}\}
converges strongly to an optimizer
F\in S^{k,2}(S^{2n+1})
of (
1.2
). Next, we follow the Frank–Lieb argument developed in
[
Cas21
]
to classify the optimizers. The Frank–Lieb argument involves three elements. First, the assumption of a local miminizer implies, via the second variation, a nice spectral estimate; see Proposition
4.4
for a precise statement. Second, CR covariance implies that one can assume that the optimizer
F
satisfies the balanced condition (
1.5
), and in particular use first spherical harmonics as test functions in the previous spectral estimate. Third, one deduces that a balanced positive local minimizer is constant by computing a particular commutator formula involving the relevant CR GJMS operator and a first spherical harmonic. The commutator formula needed for Theorem
1.6
is given in Theorem
4.3
.

In the same spirit as Theorem
1.1
, we prove Aubin-type almost Sobolev inequalities involving GJMS operators on general CR manifolds.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
(N,T^{1,0}N,\theta)
be a compact strictly pseudoconvex manifold of dimension
2n+1
. Then for any
F\in S^{k,2}(N)
,
p=\frac{2Q}{Q-2k}
, we have

(1.12)

\left(\int_{N}|F|^{p}d\zeta\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}\leqslant\left(C_{n,2k}+\epsilon\right)\int_{N}\bar{F}\tilde{P}^{\theta}_{k}Fd\zeta+C(\epsilon)\int_{N}|F|^{2}d\zeta,

where
d\zeta=\theta\wedge d\theta^{n}
,
C_{n,2k}
is the optimal constant on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
introduced in (
1.1
), and
\tilde{P}^{\theta}_{k}
is the principle part of
P^{\theta}_{k}
given in (
2.5
).

In the Yamabe problem, it is known
[
LP87
]
that
Y(M^{n})\leqslant Y(S^{n})
, where the equality holds if and only if
(M,g)
is conformal to the standard sphere
(S^{n},g_{c})
. When
Y(M^{n})<Y(S^{n})
, the Yamabe constant
Y(M^{n})
can be attained by a positive minimizer; the proof of this relies on Aubin’s almost sharp inequality
[
LP87
, Theorem 2.3]
. In the CR Yamabe problem, Jerison and Lee proved the existence of minimizers in
[
JL87
, Theorem 3.4]
. However, they did not have an almost sharp Sobolev inequality on general CR manifolds. To overcome this technical difficulty, they proved the existence by contradiction by lifting a divergent sequence of functions to
\mathbb{H}^{n}
in CR normal coordinates.

With help of Theorem
1.7
, we obtain the existence of minimizers in
C^{2k}(N)
of higher-order CR Yamabe-type problems when
Y_{k}(N)<Y_{k}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
. In particular, this provides a new, direct proof of Theorem 3.4(c) in
[
JL87
]
. However, when
k\geqslant 2
, it is difficult to know whether the minimizer is positive.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
(N^{2n+1},T^{1,0}N)
be a compact strictly pseudoconvex CR manifold with contact form
\theta
. Denote
Q=2n+2
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
with
k<n+1
. We consider the extremal problem

(1.13)

Y_{k}(N)=\inf\left\{A^{\theta}_{k}(u);u\in S^{k,2}(N;\mathbb{R}),B^{\theta}_{k}(u)=1\right\},

where

(1.14)

\displaystyle A^{\theta}_{k}(u)

\displaystyle=\int_{N}uP_{k}^{\theta}(u)\theta\wedge d\theta^{n},

(1.15)

\displaystyle B^{\theta}_{k}(u)

\displaystyle=\int_{N}|u|^{p}\theta\wedge d\theta^{n},\quad p=\frac{2Q}{Q-2k}.

If
Y_{k}(N)<Y_{k}(\mathbb{H}^{n})=C_{n,2k}^{-1}
and
\{F_{i}\}
is a minimizing sequence of
Y_{k}(N)
with
B^{\theta}_{k}(F_{i})=1
, then, after passing to a subsequence, we can find
F\in S^{k,2}(N;\mathbb{R})
such that
F_{i}\to F
strongly in
S^{k,2}(N)
. In particular,
F
is a minimizer of
Y_{k}(N)
in
C^{2k}(N)
.

In the CR Yamabe problem, the infimum may not be achieved when the CR Yamabe constant
Y_{1}(N)=Y_{1}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
. Recently, in
[
CMY19
]
, authors exhibit examples of compact three-dimensional CR manifolds of positive Webster class for which the pseudohermitian mass
[
CMY17
]
is negative, and for which
Y_{1}(N)
is not attained. This phenomenon is in striking contrast to the Riemannian case.

This article is organized as follows.

In Section 2, we review some basic concepts on CR manifolds, ambient spaces and CR GJMS operators. In Section 3, we prove Theorem
1.1
and compute
\Theta(1,1;\theta,2n+1)
following the idea in
[
HW20
]
. Section 4 deals with the commutator identity and spectral estimate needed for the Frank–Lieb argument. As a direct application, we give a
new proof of sharp CR Sobolev inequalities on
S^{2n+1}
. In Section 5, we provide the proof of Theorem
1.7
. In Section 6, using Lemma
3.1
, we prove Theorem
1.8
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
CR manifolds

Let
N
be a smooth
(2n+1)
-dimensional manifold without boundary. A CR structure on
N
is a complex
n
-dimensional subbundle
T^{1,0}N
of the complexified tangent bundle
\mathbb{C}TN
such that

•

T^{1,0}N\cap T^{0,1}N=\{0\}
, where
T^{0,1}N=\overline{T^{1,0}N}
;

•

[\Gamma(T^{1,0}N),\Gamma(T^{1,0}N)]\subset\Gamma(T^{1,0}N).

For example, if
N
is a real hypersurface in a complex manifold
X
, then
N
has the natural CR structure

T^{1,0}N=(TN\otimes\mathbb{C})\cap T^{1,0}X.

We set
HN=\mathrm{Re}

(T^{1,0}N\oplus T^{0,1}N)
. In the following, assume that there exists a global nonvanishing
1
-form
\theta
that annihilates
HN
. The
Levi form

L_{\theta}
with respect to
\theta
is the Hermitian form on
T^{1,0}N
defined by

L_{\theta}(V,\overline{W})=\langle-id\theta,V\wedge\overline{W}\rangle,\quad V,W\in T^{1,0}N.

We consider only
strictly pseudoconvex CR manifolds
, i.e. CR manifolds that have a positive definite Levi form for a suitable choice of
\theta
. In this case,
\theta
defines a contact structure on
N
, and we call
\theta
a contact form. We denote by
T
the
Reeb vector field
with respect to
\theta
; that is, the unique vector field satisfying

\theta(T)=1,\quad\iota_{T}d\theta=0.

Then the tangent bundle has the decomposition
TN=HN\oplus\mathbb{R}T
.
Take a local frame
(Z_{\alpha})
of
T^{1,0}N
. Then we have a local frame
(T,Z_{\alpha},Z_{\bar{\beta}}=\overline{Z_{\beta}})
of
\mathbb{C}TN
, and take the dual frame
(\theta,\theta^{\alpha},\theta^{\bar{\beta}})
. For this frame, the
2
-form
d\theta
can be written

(2.1)

d\theta=ih_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}\theta^{\alpha}\wedge\theta^{\bar{\beta}},

where
(h_{\alpha\bar{\beta}})
is a positive Hermitian matrix. In the following, we will use
h_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}
and its inverse
h^{\alpha\bar{\beta}}
to lower and raise indices of various tensors.

A
pseudohermitian

structure
on
N
is a CR structure together with a given contact form
\theta
. With this choice,
N
is equipped with a natural volume form
\theta\wedge d\theta^{n}
. The inner product
L_{\theta}
determines an isomorphism
HN\cong HN^{*}
, which in turn determines a dual form
L^{*}_{\theta}
on
HN^{*}
, which extends naturally to
T^{*}N
. This defines a norm
|\omega|_{\theta}
on real
1
-forms
\omega
, which satisfies

|\omega|^{2}_{\theta}=L_{\theta}^{*}(\omega,\omega)=2\sum^{n}_{j=1}\left|\omega(Z_{j})\right|^{2},

where
Z_{1},\cdots,Z_{n}
form an orthonormal basis for
T^{1,0}N
with respect to the Levi form.

The
sub
-
Laplacian

operator

\Delta_{b}
is defined on real functions
u\in C^{\infty}(N)
by

(2.2)

\int_{N}\left(\Delta_{b}u\right)v\theta\wedge d\theta^{n}=\int_{N}L^{*}_{\theta}(du,dv)\theta\wedge d\theta^{n},\forall v\in C^{\infty}_{0}(N).

Since evidently
|\theta|_{\theta}=0
,
L^{*}_{\theta}
is degenerate on
T^{*}N
. Kohn’s work
[
Koh65
]
shows that the operator
\Delta_{b}
is subelliptic rather than elliptic. It is known
[
Lee86
]
that
\Delta_{b}=\mathrm{Re}\Box_{b}
, where
\Box_{b}
is the Kohn Laplacian acting on functions.

2.2.
Ambient space and ambient construction

Let
X
be an
(n+1)
-dimensional complex manifold and
\pi_{\mathcal{X}}:\mathcal{X}=K^{\times}_{X}\to X
the total space of the canonical bundle of
X
with the zero section removed. For
\mu\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
, define the dilation
\boldsymbol{{\delta}}_{\mu}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}
by the scalar multiplication
\boldsymbol{{\delta}}_{\mu}(\varsigma)=\mu^{n+2}\varsigma
for
\varsigma\in\mathcal{X}
. Denote by
Z
the holomorphic vector field generating
\boldsymbol{{\delta}}_{\mu}
; that is,
\boldsymbol{{\delta}}_{\mu}=(d/d\mu)|_{\mu=1}\boldsymbol{{\delta}}_{\mu}^{*}
. A smooth function
\boldsymbol{f}
on an open set of
\mathcal{X}
is said to be
homogeneous of bidegree

(w,w^{\prime})
for
w,w^{\prime}\in\mathbb{C}
satisfying
w-w^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}
if
Z\boldsymbol{f}=w\boldsymbol{f}
and
\overline{Z}\boldsymbol{f}=w^{\prime}\boldsymbol{f}
. Without further comment, we will assume implicitly that
w,w^{\prime}\in\mathbb{C}
and
w-w^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}
whenever we write
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}(w,w^{\prime})
for the sheaf of smooth homogeneous functions of bidegree
(w,w^{\prime})
. To simplify notation, write
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}(w)=\tilde{\mathcal{E}}(w,w)
.

Let
N
be a strictly pseudoconvex real hypersurface in
X
and
\mathcal{N}=\pi_{\mathcal{X}}^{-1}(N)
. Then
\mathcal{N}
is a strictly pseudoconvex real hypersurface in
\mathcal{X}
. Denote by
\mathcal{E}(w,w^{\prime})
the sheaf of homogeneous functions of bidegree
(w,w^{\prime})
on
\mathcal{N}
.

For a defining function
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\in\tilde{\mathcal{E}}(1)
of
\mathcal{N}
, the
(1,1)
-form
dd^{c}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}
defines a Lorentz-Kähler metric
{\boldsymbol{g[\rho]}}
in a neighborhood of
\mathcal{N}
, where
d^{c}=(i/2)(\overline{\partial}-\partial)
. We normalize
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}
by a complex Monge-Ampère equation. Take the tautological
(n+1,0)
-form
{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}
on
K_{X}
. Then

\mathrm{vol}_{\mathcal{X}}=i^{(n+2)^{2}}d{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}\wedge\overline{d{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}}

gives a volume form on
\mathcal{X}
.

Proposition 2.1

(
[
HMM17
, Proposition 2.2]
)
.

There exists a defining function
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\in\tilde{\mathcal{E}}(1)
of
\mathcal{N}
such that

\left(dd^{c}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right)^{n+2}=-\frac{(n+1)!}{n+2}\left(1+{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}^{n+2}\right)\mathrm{vol}_{\mathcal{X}},

where
{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}}\in\tilde{\mathcal{E}}(-n-2)
. Moreover, such a
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}
is unique modulo
O\left({\boldsymbol{\rho}}^{n+3}\right)
, and
{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}}
modulo
O\left({\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right)
is independent of the choice of
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}
.

We call such a
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}
a
Fefferman defining function
and Lorentz-Kähler metric
{\boldsymbol{g[\rho]}}
with respect to
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}
an
ambient metric
. The function
{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{O}}}
is called the
obstruction function
.

Next, we recall CR invariant powers of the sub-Laplacian. Let
{\boldsymbol{\Delta}}
be the Laplacian with respect to an ambient metric
{\boldsymbol{g[\rho]}}
. This operator maps
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}(w,w^{\prime})
to
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}(w-1,w^{\prime}-1)

Lemma 2.2

(
[
GG05
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

Let
(w,w^{\prime})
be such that
k=n+1+w+w^{\prime}
is a positive integer. Then for
{\boldsymbol{\tilde{f}}}\in\tilde{\mathcal{E}}(w,w^{\prime})
,

\left({\boldsymbol{\Delta^{k}\tilde{f}}}\right)\big|_{\mathcal{M}}\in\mathcal{E}(w-k,w^{\prime}-k)

depends only on
{\boldsymbol{{f}}}={\boldsymbol{\tilde{f}}}\big|_{\mathcal{M}}
and defines a differential operator

{\boldsymbol{P}}_{w,w^{\prime}}:\mathcal{E}(w,w^{\prime})\to\mathcal{E}(w-k,w^{\prime}-k)

by
{\boldsymbol{P}}_{w,w^{\prime}}=\frac{{\boldsymbol{\Delta^{k}}}}{2^{k}}
.
Moreover, the operator
{\boldsymbol{P}}_{w,w^{\prime}}
is independent of the choice of a Fefferman defining function if
k\leqslant n+1
.

It is known
[
GG05
, Proposition 5.1]
that
{\boldsymbol{P}}_{w,w^{\prime}}
defined in Lemma
2.2
is formally self-adjoint. In particular, when
w=w^{\prime}=\frac{k-1-n}{2}
,
1\leqslant k\leqslant n+1
,
P_{k}^{\theta}=P_{w,w}:C^{\infty}(N)\to C^{\infty}(N)
satisfies

(2.3)

P_{k}^{e^{2f}\theta}u=e^{-(n+1+k)f}P_{k}^{\theta}\left(e^{(n+1-k)f}u\right),\quad f\in C^{\infty}(N,\mathbb{R}).

For
k=1
, the operator
P_{1}^{\theta}
agrees with the conformal sub-Laplacian of Jerison-Lee; i.e.

(2.4)

P_{1}^{\theta}=\Delta_{b}+\frac{n}{4(n+1)}R

where
R
is the associated scalar curvature. In general, if we let
T
denote the Reeb vector field of
\theta
, then
P_{k}^{\theta}
has the same principal part (in the Heisenberg sense) as

(2.5)

\left(\Delta_{b}+i(k-1)T\right)\left(\Delta_{b}+i(k-3)T\right)\cdots\left(\Delta_{b}-i(k-1)T\right).

This implies that the operator
P_{k}^{\theta}
is subelliptic of order
2k
when
k<n+1
, see
[
Pon08
, Section 2.2.4]
for more details. We denote the principal part of
P_{k}^{\theta}
by
\tilde{P}_{k}^{\theta}
.

2.3.
Factorization of CR GJMS operators on
S^{2n+1}

This subsection deals with the factorization of CR GJMS operators on
S^{2n+1}
; we follow the argument in
[
Tak18
]
.

Let
S^{2n+1}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
be the unit sphere centered at the origin with canonical CR structure, and
\theta_{0}
be the contact form on
S^{2n+1}
defined by

(2.6)

\theta_{0}=\frac{i}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}\left(z^{i}d\bar{z}^{i}-\bar{z}^{i}dz^{i}\right)\big|_{S^{2n+1}}.

Then the triple
(S^{2n+1},T^{1,0}S^{2n+1},\theta_{0})
is a Sasakian–Einstein manifold; i.e. the Tanaka–Webster Ricci curvature
{\mathrm{Ric}}_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}
of
\theta_{0}
satisfies

{\mathrm{Ric}}_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}=(n+1)h_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}

where
(h_{\alpha\bar{\beta}})
is a positive Hermitian matrix defined in (
2.1
). Consider the Tanaka–Webster connection with respect to
\theta_{0}
. Note that the index
0
is used for the component
T
or
\theta_{0}
in our index notation. The commutators of the derivatives for
f\in C^{\infty}(S^{2n+1})
are given by

f_{[\alpha\beta]}=0,\quad 2f_{[\alpha\bar{\beta}]}=ih_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}f_{0},\quad f_{[0\alpha]}=0,

where
[\cdots]
means the antisymmetrization over the enclosed indices. The Kohn Laplacian
\Box_{b}
and sub-Laplacian
\Delta_{b}
are given by

(2.7)

\Box_{b}f=-f_{\bar{\alpha}}^{\;\;\bar{\alpha}},\quad\Delta_{b}f=-f_{\bar{\alpha}}^{\;\;\bar{\alpha}}-f_{{\alpha}}^{\;\;{\alpha}}=\Box_{b}f+\overline{\Box}_{b}f,

respectively. From the above commutation relations, we have

(2.8)

\Box_{b}-\overline{\Box}_{b}=inT.

The third covariant derivatives of
f
satisfy

\begin{split}f_{\alpha[0\beta]}&=0,\quad f_{\alpha[0\bar{\beta}]}=0,\\
2f_{\alpha[\beta\bar{\gamma}]}&=ih_{\beta\bar{\gamma}}f_{\alpha 0}+R_{\alpha\;\beta\bar{\gamma}}^{\;\;\delta\;\;\;}f_{\delta}.\end{split}

From these, it follows that the Kohn Laplacian and sub-Laplacian commute with the Reeb vector field
T
.

The Fefferman defining function
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}
of the null space
\mathcal{N}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n+2}
is given by
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}(\zeta)=\zeta_{0}\bar{\zeta}_{0}-\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}\zeta_{j}\bar{\zeta}_{j}
,
where
\zeta
is the homogeneous coordinate system defined by

(2.9)

\zeta_{0}=z_{0},\quad\zeta_{j}=z_{0}z_{j},\quad j=1,\cdots,n+1.

The corresponding ambient metric
{\boldsymbol{{g}[\rho]}}
is a flat Lorentz-Kähler metric defined on
\mathbb{C}^{n+2}\backslash\{0\}
by

(2.10)

{\boldsymbol{{g}[\rho]}}=i\left(-d\zeta_{0}\wedge d\bar{\zeta}_{0}+d\zeta_{1}\wedge d\bar{\zeta}_{1}+\cdots d\zeta_{n+1}\wedge d\bar{\zeta}_{n+1}\right).

In homogeneous coordinates, the holomorphic Euler field
Z
and anti-holomorphic Euler field
\overline{Z}
are given by

(2.11)

Z=\sum_{i=0}^{n+1}\zeta_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\zeta_{i}},\quad\overline{Z}=\sum_{i=0}^{n+1}\bar{\zeta}_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{\zeta}_{i}}.

Notice that for each function
{\boldsymbol{f}}\in\tilde{\mathcal{E}}(w,w^{\prime})
, there exists a smooth function
f
defined on
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}\backslash\{0\}
such that
{\boldsymbol{f}}=\left(z_{0}\right)^{w}\left(\bar{z}_{0}\right)^{w^{\prime}}f
. For convenience, we define the multiplication operator
{\boldsymbol{M}}_{v,v^{\prime}}
to raise the lower the bidegree.

Definition 2.3
.

On the Lorentz-Kähler manifold
(\mathbb{C}^{n+2}\backslash\{0\},{\boldsymbol{{g}[\rho]}})
, the operator
{\boldsymbol{M}}_{v,v^{\prime}}:\tilde{\mathcal{E}}(w,w^{\prime})\to\tilde{\mathcal{E}}(w+v,w^{\prime}+v^{\prime})
is defined by the multiplication by
\left(z_{0}\right)^{v}\left(\bar{z}_{0}\right)^{v^{\prime}}
.

These multiplication operators
{\boldsymbol{M}}_{v,v^{\prime}}
induce differential operators on
\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}
corresponding to
{\boldsymbol{P}}_{w,w^{\prime}}
defined in Lemma
2.2
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
(w,w^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that
k=w+w^{\prime}+n+1
is a positive integer. The differential operator
P_{w,w^{\prime}}
on
C^{\infty}(S^{2n+1})
is defined by

(2.12)

P_{w,w^{\prime}}={\boldsymbol{M}}_{k-w,k-w^{\prime}}\circ{\boldsymbol{P}}_{w,w^{\prime}}\circ{\boldsymbol{M}}_{w,w^{\prime}}.

The main result in
[
Tak18
]
is the factorization of
P_{w,w^{\prime}}
on a Sasakian
\eta
-Einstein manifold. In this paper, we only need the corresponding result on
S^{2n+1}
.

Theorem 2.5

(
[
Tak18
]
)
.

Let
(S^{2n+1},T^{1,0}S^{2n+1},\theta_{0})
be a Sasakian manifold of dimension
2n+1
with Einstein constant
(n+1)
. Then
P_{w,w^{\prime}}
for
k=w+w^{\prime}+n+1
has the formula

(2.13)

P_{w,w^{\prime}}=\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}L_{w^{\prime}-w+k-2j-1}.

Here
L_{\mu}
is the differential operator on
\mathbb{S}^{2n+1}
defined by

(2.14)

L_{\mu}=\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{b}+\frac{i}{2}\mu T+\frac{1}{4}(n-\mu)(n+\mu),

where
\Delta_{b}
is the sub-Laplacian operator and

is the Reeb vector field.

3.
Improved CR Sobolev inequalities

In this section, we improve the CR Sobolev inequalities on
S^{2n+1}
under the vanishing of higher order moments of the volume elements. We start from the concentration compactness principle on CR manifolds
(N,\theta)
. The original version in
[
Lio84
,
Lio85
]
is on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, but it can be carried out on
(N,\theta)
because, as mentioned in Remark I.5 in
[
Lio84
]
, the crucial ingredient in Lemma I.2 there is valid in an arbitrary measure space.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
N
be a compact strictly pseudoconvex CR manifold of dimension
2n+1
. We assume that
F_{i}
is a sequence of complex-valued functions bounded in
S^{k,2}(N)
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
with
k<n+1
and
p=\frac{2Q}{Q-2k}
, such that
F_{i}\rightharpoonup F
weakly in
S^{k,2}(N)
. Moreover, we assume as measures,

(3.1)

|F_{i}|^{p}d\zeta\to|F|^{p}d\zeta+\nu,\quad\bar{F_{i}}\tilde{P}^{\theta}_{k}F_{i}d\zeta\to\bar{F}\tilde{P}^{\theta}_{k}Fd\zeta+\sigma,

where
d\zeta=\theta\wedge d\theta^{n}
. Then we can find countably many points
\{x_{i}\}\subset N
such that

\nu=\sum_{i}\nu_{i}\delta_{x_{i}},\quad\nu_{i}^{\frac{2}{p}}\leqslant C_{n,2k}\sigma_{i}

where
\nu_{i}=\nu(x_{i})
and
\sigma_{i}=\sigma(x_{i})
.

Proof.

Let
G_{i}:=F_{i}-F
. Then, up to a subsequence, as
i\to\infty
,
G_{i}\rightharpoonup 0
weakly in
S^{k,2}(N)
and
G_{i}\to 0
in
L^{2}(N)
. Also,
\nu_{i}:=|F_{i}|^{p}d\zeta-|F|^{p}d\zeta\to\nu
and
\sigma_{i}:=\bar{F_{i}}\tilde{P}^{\theta}_{k}F_{i}d\zeta-\bar{F}\tilde{P}^{\theta}_{k}Fd\zeta\to\sigma
as measures. For any measurable set
A\subset N
, by
[
Lie83
, Lemma 2.6]
and the weak convergence in
S^{k,2}(N)
, respectively, we have

(3.2)

\begin{split}\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{A}|G_{i}|^{p}d\zeta&=\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{A}|F_{i}|^{p}d\zeta-\int_{A}|F|^{p}d\zeta=\nu(A),\\
\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{A}\bar{G_{i}}\tilde{P}^{\theta}_{k}G_{i}d\zeta&=\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{A}\bar{F_{i}}\tilde{P}^{\theta}_{k}F_{i}d\zeta-\int_{A}\bar{F}\tilde{P}^{\theta}_{k}Fd\zeta=\sigma(A),\end{split}

which implies that
|G_{i}|^{p}d\zeta\to\nu
and
\bar{G_{i}}\tilde{P}^{\theta}_{k}G_{i}d\zeta\to\sigma
as measures.

Firstly, we claim that
\sigma
is a real measure. From
[
GG05
, Theorem 5.1]
, we know that the operator
P^{\theta}_{k}
is self-adjoint. We have for any real function
\phi\in C^{\infty}(N)
,

(3.3)

\int_{N}\phi\bar{G_{i}}P^{\theta}_{k}G_{i}d\zeta=\int_{N}G_{i}P^{\theta}_{k}(\phi\bar{G_{i}})d\zeta\\
=\int_{N}\phi G_{i}P^{\theta}_{k}\bar{G_{i}}d\zeta+l.o.t,

Moreover,

(3.4)

\begin{split}&\int_{N}\phi\bar{G_{i}}P^{\theta}_{k}G_{i}d\zeta=\int_{N}\phi\bar{G_{i}}\tilde{P}^{\theta}_{k}G_{i}d\zeta+l.o.t,\\
&\int_{N}\phi G_{i}P^{\theta}_{k}\bar{G_{i}}d\zeta=\int_{N}\phi G_{i}\tilde{P}^{\theta}_{k}\bar{G_{i}}d\zeta+l.o.t,\end{split}

where all lower order terms in (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) involve lower order derivatives of
G_{i}
.

By the compact embedding
S^{k,2}(N)\hookrightarrow S^{k^{\prime},2}(N)
for
k^{\prime}<k
in
[
FS74
]
and the weak convergence, all lower order terms in (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) will vanish as
i\to\infty
. Therefore, since
\bar{G_{i}}\tilde{P}^{\theta}_{k}G_{i}\theta\wedge d\theta^{n}\to\sigma
, combining (
3.3
) and (
3.4
), we have

(3.5)

\int_{N}\phi d\overline{\sigma}=\int_{N}\phi d\sigma,

which implies that
\sigma
is a real measure.

Similarly, by the compact embedding
S^{k,2}(N)\hookrightarrow S^{k^{\prime},2}(N)
for
k^{\prime}<k
in
[
FS74
]
and (
3.2
), for any real function
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(N)
, we have

(3.6)

\begin{split}&\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{N}|G_{i}\varphi|^{p}\theta\wedge d\theta^{n}=\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{N}|\varphi|^{p}d\nu_{i}=\int_{N}|\varphi|^{p}d\nu,\\
&\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{N}(\bar{G}_{i}\varphi)P^{\theta}_{k}(G_{i}\varphi)\theta\wedge d\theta^{n}=\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{N}\varphi^{2}\bar{G}_{i}\tilde{P}^{\theta}_{k}G_{i}\theta\wedge d\theta^{n}=\int_{N}\varphi^{2}d\sigma.\end{split}

Therefore, by the embedding
S^{k,2}(N)\hookrightarrow L^{p}(N)
in
[
FS74
]
, we obtain a reversed Hölder inequality; i.e. there exists a constant
C
such that for any real function
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(N)
,

\left(\int_{N}|\varphi|^{p}d\nu\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leqslant C\left(\int_{N}\varphi^{2}d\sigma\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Following the same argument in
[
Lio85
, Lemma I.2]
, we know that
\nu
is supported on countably many points
\{x_{i}\}\subset N
.

We claim that for each atom
x_{j}
, there does not exist a neighborhood of
x_{j}
in which
\bar{G}_{i}\tilde{P}^{\theta}_{k}G_{i}
is uniformly bounded. If this is not true, we assume that in a neighborhood
U
of
x_{j}
,
\bar{G}_{i}\tilde{P}^{\theta}_{k}G_{i}
has a uniform upper bound
C
. By Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem, for any ball
B_{R}\subset U
containing
x_{j}
, we have

(3.7)

\sigma(B_{R})=\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{B_{R}}\bar{G}_{i}\tilde{P}^{\theta}_{k}G_{i}d\zeta=\int_{B_{R}}\lim_{i\to\infty}\bar{G}_{i}\tilde{P}^{\theta}_{k}G_{i}d\zeta\leqslant C\int_{B_{R}}d\zeta.

By (
3.7
), we obtain that
\sigma(x_{j})=0
as
R\to 0
, which implies that
\sigma(x_{j})
and
\nu(x_{j})
are zero, a contradiction.

Therefore, there exists a sequence of points
\{\xi^{j}_{i}\}
on
N
such that as
i\to\infty
,

(3.8)

\xi^{j}_{i}\to x_{j},\quad\left(\bar{G}_{i}\tilde{P}^{\theta}_{k}G_{i}\right)(\xi^{j}_{i})\to\infty.

If
(N,\theta)
is the unit sphere
S^{2n+1}
with the canonical contact form
\theta_{0}
, without loss of generality, we can assume that the south pole
(0,\cdots,-1)
is not an atom. Then,
(\mathbb{H}^{n},\theta_{c})
and
(S^{2n+1},\theta_{0})
are related by the Cayley transform
\mathcal{C}:\mathbb{H}^{n}\to S^{2n+1}\backslash(0,\cdots,-1)
. Composed with a translation if necessary,
\mathcal{C}
is a diffeomorphism of a neighborhood
\Omega_{\xi^{j}_{i}}
of
\xi^{j}_{i}
onto a neighborhood
U
of the origin in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. Moreover, it is known
[
BFM13
]
that the contact form and CR GJMS operators on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
and
S^{2n+1}
are related by

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{*}\theta_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{(1+|z|^{2})^{2}+t^{2}}\theta_{c},

\displaystyle P^{\theta_{c}}_{k}\left(\left(2|J_{\mathcal{C}}|\right)^{\frac{Q-2k}{2Q}}\left(F\circ\mathcal{C}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\left(2|J_{\mathcal{C}}|\right)^{\frac{Q+2k}{2Q}}\left(P^{\theta_{0}}F\right)\circ\mathcal{C},

where
|J_{\mathcal{C}}|
is the determinant of the Jacobian of
\mathcal{C}
and
F\in C^{\infty}(S^{2n+1})
.

On general CR manifolds
(N,\theta)
, at each point
\xi^{j}_{i}
, we introduce the pseudohermitian normal coordinates
[
JL87
, Theorem 4.3]
.
\Theta_{\xi^{j}_{i}}
is a diffeomorphism of a neighborhood
\Omega_{\xi^{j}_{i}}
of
\xi^{j}_{i}
onto a neighborhood
U
of the origin in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
.

We will use
\mathcal{C}
or
\Theta_{\xi^{j}_{i}}
to identify
U
with the neighborhood
\Omega_{\xi^{j}_{i}}
of
{\xi^{j}_{i}}
. Therefore, under the dilation on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
,
T^{\delta}(z,t)=(\delta^{-1}z,\delta^{-2}t)
, for
\delta
sufficiently small, we have

(3.9)

\begin{split}&(T^{\delta}\circ\Theta_{\xi^{j}_{i}})_{*}\theta=\delta^{2}(1+\delta O^{1})\theta_{c},\\
&(T^{\delta}\circ\Theta_{\xi^{j}_{i}})_{*}(\theta\wedge d\theta^{n})=\delta^{2n+2}(1+\delta O^{1})\theta_{c}\wedge d\theta_{c}^{n},\\
&(T^{\delta}\circ\Theta_{\xi^{j}_{i}})_{*}\Delta_{b}=\delta^{-2}(P^{\theta_{c}}_{2}+\mathfrak{C}(\partial_{z})+\delta O^{1}\mathfrak{C}(\partial_{t},\partial^{2}_{z})+\delta^{2}O^{2}\mathfrak{C}(\partial_{z}\partial_{t})+\delta^{3}O^{3}\mathfrak{C}(\partial^{2}_{t})),\end{split}

where
P^{\theta_{c}}_{2}
is the sub-Laplacian on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
and
O^{r}\mathfrak{C}
indicates an operator involving linear combinations of the indicated derivatives with coefficients in
O^{r}
. For convenience, we consider general CR manifolds because all arguments in the following hold on
(S^{2n+1},\theta_{0})
by replacing
\Theta_{\xi^{j}_{i}}
by
\mathcal{C}
.

Now consider the change of coordinates
(\tilde{z},\tilde{t})=T^{\delta}(z,t)=(\delta^{-1}z,\delta^{-2}z)
on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
, and set

\tilde{\theta}_{c}=d\tilde{t}+i\sum^{n}_{j}\left(\tilde{z}^{j}d\bar{\tilde{z}}^{j}-\bar{\tilde{z}}^{j}d\tilde{z}^{j}\right)=\delta^{-2}T^{\delta}_{*}\theta_{c},\quad T^{\delta}_{*}=\left(\left(T^{\delta}\right)^{-1}\right)^{*}.

On the set
\delta^{-1}U
with coordinates
(\tilde{z},\tilde{t})
define
\tilde{G}_{i}(\tilde{z},\tilde{t})=\delta^{\frac{Q}{p}}G_{i}(\delta\tilde{z},\delta^{2}\tilde{t})
with
\delta=\delta_{i}>0
chosen so that
\left(\bar{\tilde{G}}_{i}\tilde{P}^{\tilde{\theta}_{c}}_{k}\tilde{G}_{i}\right)(0)=1
. Observe that
\theta=\theta_{c}
at
0
, from (
3.9
), we have

\left(\bar{\tilde{G}}_{i}\tilde{P}^{\tilde{\theta}_{c}}_{k}\tilde{G}_{i}\right)(0)=\delta_{i}^{Q}\left(\bar{G}_{i}\tilde{P}^{\theta}_{k}G_{i}\right)(\xi^{j}_{i}).

In particular,
\delta_{i}\to 0
as
i\to\infty
, and hence
\delta_{i}^{-1}U
tends to the full space
\mathbb{H}^{n}
as
i\to\infty
. We define
\theta_{i}=\delta_{i}^{-2}(T^{\delta_{i}}\circ\Theta_{\xi^{j}_{i}})_{*}\theta
in coordinates
(\tilde{z},\tilde{t})
on the region
\delta_{i}^{-1}U
.

If we denote by
\left(W^{i}_{1},\cdots,W^{i}_{n}\right)
the pseudohermitian frame used to define
\Theta_{\xi^{j}_{i}}
, we may assume that
\left(W^{i}_{1},\cdots,W^{i}_{n}\right)
converges in
C^{r}
for all
r
to a frame
\left(W_{1},\cdots,W_{n}\right)
. Now set
Z^{i}_{l}=\delta_{i}(T^{\delta_{i}}\circ\Theta_{\xi^{j}_{i}})_{*}W^{i}_{l}
, so that
\left(Z^{i}_{1},\cdots,Z^{i}_{n}\right)
is a pseudohermitian frame for
\theta_{i}
. By examining the error terms in the expression for

[
JL87
, Remark 4.4]
, it is easy to show that, for any
R>0
,
Z^{i}_{l}
converges in
C^{r}(B_{R})
to
Z_{l}
for every

. Similarly,
\theta_{i}
and

converge uniformly in

to
\theta_{c}
and

, respectively.

Now fix a radius
R>0
, note that

(3.10)

\int_{B_{R}(0)}|\tilde{G}_{i}(\tilde{z},\tilde{t})|^{p}\theta_{i}\wedge d\theta_{i}^{n}=\int_{B_{\delta_{i}R}(\xi^{j}_{i})}|G_{i}(z,t)|^{p}\theta\wedge d\theta^{n}.

The volume element
\theta\wedge d\theta^{n}
is equal to
C\left(1+\delta_{i}O^{1}\right)\theta_{c}\wedge d\theta_{c}^{n}
on

). Therefore,
\tilde{G}_{i}\in L^{p}(B_{2R},\theta_{i})
with uniform bounds on the norm. In particular,
\tilde{G}_{i}\in L^{k}(B_{2R},\theta_{i})
uniformly as

. Combined with the uniform bound on
\bar{\tilde{G}}_{i}\tilde{P}^{\theta_{i}}_{k}\tilde{G}_{i}
, this gives
\tilde{G}_{i}\in S^{k,r}(B_{2R},\theta_{i})
for every

with uniform bounds on the norm by
[
FS74
, Section 19]
. Thus by
[
JL87
, Proposition 5.15]
,
\tilde{G}_{i}
is uniformly bounded in
C^{r}(B_{R})
for every
r
.

Now we can take a subsequence
i_{l}\to\infty
for which
\tilde{G}_{i_{l}}
converges in
C^{1}(B_{R})
. Define a function
G
on all of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
by first choosing a subsequence
\tilde{G}_{i_{l}}
converging in
C^{1}(B_{1})
, and then a subsequence converging in
C^{1}(B_{2})
, etc.

Notice that
G\in C^{1}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
and
G\neq 0
because
\left(\bar{G}\tilde{P}^{\theta_{c}}_{k}G\right)(0)=1
. Since
\theta_{i}\wedge d\theta_{i}^{n}
approaches
\theta_{c}\wedge d\theta_{c}
uniformly on compact sets, we verify

\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\bar{G}P^{\theta_{c}}_{k}G\theta_{c}\wedge d\theta_{c}\leqslant C<\infty.

In fact,

(3.11)

We now turn to the desired spectral estimate. The fact that
P^{\theta_{0}}_{k}
is formally self-adjoint implies that if
u_{t}
is a one-parameter family of functions in
S^{k,2}(S^{2n+1};\mathbb{R})
with
u_{0}=u
, then

(4.18)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{t=0}A^{\theta_{0}}_{k}(u)

\displaystyle=2\int_{S^{2n+1}}\dot{u}P^{\theta_{0}}_{k}(u)d\xi,

(4.19)

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\bigg|_{t=0}A^{\theta_{0}}_{k}(u)

\displaystyle=2\int_{S^{2n+1}}\dot{u}P^{\theta_{0}}_{k}(\dot{u})d\xi+2\int_{S^{2n+1}}\ddot{u}P^{\theta_{0}}_{k}(u)d\xi.

for
\dot{u}:=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\big|_{t=0}u_{t}
and
\ddot{u}:=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}\big|_{t=0}u_{t}
.

Proof of Proposition
4.4
.

Let
v\in C^{\infty}(S^{2n+1};\mathbb{R})
be such that

(4.20)

\int_{S^{2n+1}}vu^{p-1}d\xi=0.

Then
u_{t}=\|u+tv\|^{-1}_{p}(u+tv)
defines a smooth curve with
B^{\theta_{0}}_{k}(u_{t})=1
and
u_{0}=u
,
\dot{u}_{t}\big|_{t=0}=v
. Since
u
is a critical point of
A^{\theta_{0}}_{k}
, it follows from (
4.18
) that

P^{\theta_{0}}_{k}(u)=A^{\theta_{0}}_{k}(u)u^{p-1}.

Since
u
is a local minimizer,
\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\bigg|_{t=0}A^{\theta_{0}}_{k}(u_{t})\geqslant 0
. Expanding this using (
4.19
) and the above display yields

(4.21)

\int_{S^{2n+1}}vP^{\theta_{0}}_{k}(v)d\xi\geqslant(p-1)A^{\theta_{0}}_{k}(u)\int_{S^{2n+1}}u^{p-2}v^{2}d\xi.

The assumption (
4.8
) implies that for each coordinate function
z_{j}=x_{j}+iy_{j}
,
1\leqslant j\leqslant n+1
, the functions
x_{j}u
and
y_{j}u
satisfy (
4.20
). Since
P^{\theta_{0}}_{k}
is self-adjoint, it follows from (
4.21
) that

\begin{split}\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}\int_{S^{2n+1}}\bar{z}_{j}uP_{k}^{\theta_{0}}(z_{j}u)d\xi&=\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}\left(\int_{S^{2n+1}}x_{j}uP_{k}^{\theta_{0}}(x_{j}u)d\xi+\int_{S^{2n+1}}y_{j}uP_{k}^{\theta_{0}}(y_{j}u)d\xi\right)\\
&\geqslant(p-1)A^{\theta_{0}}_{k}(u)\int_{S^{2n+1}}\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}(x_{j}^{2}+y_{j}^{2})u^{p}d\xi\\
&=(p-1)A^{\theta_{0}}_{k}(u).\end{split}

The final conclusion follows from the definition of
[P_{k}^{\theta_{0}},z_{j}]
.
∎

Proposition
4.4
and Corollary
4.2
reduce the problem of classifying positive local minimizers of
A^{\theta_{0}}_{k}
to the problem of showing that the only functions which satisfy (
4.9
) are the constants. This can be done for the operators
P_{w,w}
and
P_{w-1,w}
using the commutator identity in Theorem
4.3
.

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

All computations in this proof are carried out with respect to the CR unit sphere
(S^{2n+1},T^{1,0}S^{2n+1},\theta_{0})
with the volume element
d\xi
. As discussed in
[
FL12
, Theorem 1.3]
, we may assume that the optimizer
u
of (
1.2
) is a positive real function; i.e.
u
is a positive real minimizer of

A^{\theta_{0}}_{k}(u)=\int_{S^{2n+1}}uP^{\theta_{0}}_{k}(u)d\xi

with
B^{\theta_{0}}_{k}(u)=1
. By Corollary
4.2
we may assume that
u
satisfies (
4.8
). We conclude from Proposition
4.4
that

\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}\int_{S^{2n+1}}\bar{z}_{j}u[P_{k}^{\theta_{0}},z_{j}](u)d\xi\geqslant(p-2)\int_{S^{2n+1}}uP^{\theta_{0}}_{k}(u)d\xi.

Since
P_{k}^{\theta_{0}}=P_{w,w}
with

, combining this with Theorem
4.3
yields

(4.22)

0\geqslant\int_{S^{2n+1}}u\left((p-2)P_{w,w}+k(w-k+1)P_{w-1,w}\right)(u)d\xi.

It follows from the factorization in Theorem
2.5
that

(4.23)

P_{w,w}=\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}L_{k-2j-1},\quad P_{w-1,w}=\prod_{j=0}^{k-2}L_{k-2j-1}

where
L_{\mu}

(p-2)P_{w,w}+k(w-k+1)P_{w-1,w}=(p-2)\left(\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{b}+\frac{i}{2}(1-k)T\right)\prod_{j=0}^{k-2}L_{k-2j-1}.

From (

(4.25)

\Delta_{b}+i\alpha T=\frac{n+\alpha}{n}\Box_{b}+\frac{n-\alpha}{n}\overline{\Box}_{b}.

Thus,

u=1

|J_{\Phi}|(\eta)=\frac{C}{|1-\xi\cdot\bar{\eta}|^{Q}},\quad\eta\in S^{2n+1}

∎

5.
Almost Sobolev inequalities

In this section, we use Lemma
3.1
to prove Aubin’s almost sharp inequalities for GJMS operators on general CR manifolds. This argument is similar to the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.7
:

Let
\alpha=C_{n,2k}+\epsilon
. If (
1.12
) is not true, then for any
j\in\mathbb{N}
, we can find a
F_{j}\in S^{k,2}(N)
satisfying

(5.1)

\left(\int_{N}|F_{j}|^{p}d\zeta\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}>\alpha\int_{N}\bar{F}_{j}\tilde{P}^{\theta}_{k}F_{j}d\zeta+j\int_{N}|F_{j}|^{2}d\zeta.

We may assume

\left(\int_{N}|F_{j}|^{p}d\zeta\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}=1.

Then

(5.2)

\int_{N}\bar{F}_{j}\tilde{P}^{\theta}_{k}F_{j}d\zeta<\frac{1}{\alpha},\quad\int_{N}|F_{j}|^{2}d\zeta<\frac{1}{j}.

It follows that
F_{j}\rightharpoonup 0
weakly in
S^{k,2}(N)
. After passing to a subsequence, we assume as measures,

(5.3)

\bar{F}_{j}\tilde{P}^{\theta}_{k}F_{j}d\zeta\to\sigma,\quad|F_{j}|^{p}d\zeta\to\nu.

By Lemma
3.1
we can find countably many points
\{x_{i}\}\in N
such that

(5.4)

\nu=\sum_{i}\nu_{i}\delta_{x_{i}},\quad\nu_{i}^{\frac{2}{p}}\leqslant C_{n,2k}\sigma_{i}

here
\nu_{i}=\nu(x_{i})
,
\sigma_{i}=\sigma(x_{i})
. Then

(5.5)

\nu(N)=1,\quad\sigma(N)<\frac{1}{\alpha}.

Then by the concavity of the function
x^{\frac{2}{p}}
, we have

(5.6)

1\leqslant\sum_{i}\nu_{i}^{\frac{2}{p}}\leqslant\sum_{i}C_{n,2k}\sigma_{i}=C_{n,2k}\sigma(N)=\frac{C_{n,2k}}{\alpha}.

Hence,

\alpha\leqslant C_{n,2k}.

This contradicts the choice of
\alpha
.
∎

6.
Existence of minimizers

Analogous to the CR Yamabe problem
[
JL87
]
, we prove that minimizers of
Y_{k}(N)
exist if
Y_{k}(N)<Y_{k}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
.

Proof of Theorem
1.8
.

Let
\{F_{i}\}
be a minimizing sequence for
Y_{k}(N)
with
B^{\theta}_{k}(F_{i})=1
; i.e.

(6.1)

Y_{k}(N)=\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{N}F_{i}P_{k}^{\theta}(F_{i})\theta\wedge d\theta^{n}.

In particular,
\{F_{i}\}
is bounded in
S^{k,2}(N;\mathbb{R})
. By taking a subsequence if necessary, we assume that there exists a limit function
F\in S^{k,2}(N;\mathbb{R})
such that

•

F_{i}\rightharpoonup F
in
S^{k,2}(N;\mathbb{R})
;

•

F_{i}\to F
in
L^{2}(N;\mathbb{R})
; and

•

F_{i}\to F
almost everywhere in
N
.

Now, Theorem
1.7
implies that for any
\epsilon>0
, there is a constant
C(\epsilon)>0
such that for all
i
,

(6.2)

\left(\int_{N}|F_{i}|^{p}d\zeta\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}\leqslant\left(C_{n,2k}+\epsilon\right)\int_{N}F_{i}\tilde{P}^{\theta}_{k}F_{i}d\zeta+C(\epsilon)\int_{N}|F_{i}|^{2}d\zeta.

If the limit function
F
is zero, by the compact embedding
S^{k,2}(N)\hookrightarrow S^{k^{\prime},2}(N)
for
k^{\prime}<k
, all lower order terms in
\int_{N}F_{i}{P}^{\theta}_{k}F_{i}d\zeta
will vanish as
i\to\infty
, i.e.

(6.3)

Y_{k}(N)=\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{N}F_{i}{P}^{\theta}_{k}F_{i}d\zeta=\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{N}F_{i}\tilde{P}^{\theta}_{k}F_{i}d\zeta.

Thus for any
\delta>0
, there is an integer
I
such that for all
i>I
, we have

\int_{N}F_{i}\tilde{P}^{\theta}_{k}F_{i}d\zeta<Y_{k}(N)+\delta.

Combining this with (
6.2
) yields

1\leqslant(Y_{k}^{-1}(\mathbb{H}^{n})+\epsilon)(Y_{k}(N)+\delta).

Since
Y_{k}(N)<Y_{k}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
, we may choose
\epsilon
and
\delta
such that
(Y_{k}^{-1}(\mathbb{H}^{n})+\epsilon)(Y_{k}(N)+\delta)<1
, a contradiction.

Finally, we set
G_{i}=F_{i}-F
. Then

•

G_{i}\rightharpoonup 0
in
S^{k,2}(N;\mathbb{R})
;

•

G_{i}\to 0
in
L^{2}(N;\mathbb{R})
; and

•

G_{i}\to 0
almost everywhere in
N
.

Then Lemma 2.6 in
[
Lie83
]
implies that

\|F\|^{p}_{p}=1-\lim_{i\to\infty}\|G_{i}\|^{p}_{p}.

In particular,
\|F\|^{p}_{p}\in(0,1]
and
\lim_{i\to\infty}\|G_{i}\|^{p}_{p}\in[0,1)
. We deduce that

(6.4)

\lim_{i\to\infty}\|G_{i}\|^{2}_{p}\geqslant 1-\|F\|^{2}_{p}

with equality if and only if
\|G_{i}\|_{p}\to 0
as
i\to\infty
. Since
G_{i}\rightharpoonup 0
, it holds that

\begin{split}\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{N}F_{i}{P}_{k}^{\theta}(F_{i})\theta\wedge d\theta^{n}&=\int_{N}F{P}_{k}^{\theta}(F)\theta\wedge d\theta^{n}+\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{N}G_{i}{P}_{k}^{\theta}(G_{i})\theta\wedge d\theta^{n}\\
&=\int_{N}F{P}_{k}^{\theta}(F)\theta\wedge d\theta^{n}+\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{N}G_{i}\tilde{P}_{k}^{\theta}(G_{i})\theta\wedge d\theta^{n}.\end{split}

Therefore

(6.5)

Y_{k}(N)=A^{\theta}_{k}(F)+\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{N}G_{i}\tilde{P}_{k}^{\theta}(G_{i})\theta\wedge d\theta^{n}.

From (
6.4
) and the definition of
Y_{k}(N)
, when
Y_{k}(N)\geqslant 0
, we deduce that

(6.6)

\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{N}G_{i}{P}_{k}^{\theta}(G_{i})\theta\wedge d\theta^{n}\geqslant Y_{k}(N)\lim_{i\to\infty}\|G_{i}\|^{2}_{p}\geqslant Y_{k}(N)(1-\|F\|^{2}_{p}).

When
Y_{k}(N)<0
, it holds automatically that

(6.7)

\lim_{i\to\infty}\int_{N}G_{i}{P}_{k}^{\theta}(G_{i})\theta\wedge d\theta^{n}\geqslant Y_{k}(N)(1-\|F\|^{2}_{p}).

because
\lim_{i\to\infty}A^{\theta}_{k}(G_{i})
is nonnegative. Moreover, equalities can be attained in (
6.6
) and (
6.7
) if and only if
\|G_{i}\|_{p}\to 0
as
i\to\infty
. Combining this with (
6.5
) yields

(6.8)

Y_{k}(N)B^{\theta}_{k}(F)\geqslant A^{\theta}_{k}(F).

It follows from the definition of
Y_{k}(N)
that the equality holds. In particular,
A^{\theta}_{k}(F)=Y_{k}(N)
and
B^{\theta}_{k}(F)=1
which implies that
F_{i}\to F
in
S^{k,2}(N;\mathbb{R})
and
F
is a minimzier of
Y_{k}(N)
.

As a critical point of
Y_{k}(N)
,
F
satisfies

(6.9)

P^{\theta}_{k}F=Y_{k}(N)|F|^{p-2}F.

By Lemma
6.2
below, we obtain that
F\in L^{r}(N)
for all
r\geqslant 1
. Then,
P^{\theta}_{k}F\in L^{r}(N)
for all
r\geqslant 1
. It follows from the regularity theorem
[
Fol75
, Theorem 6.1]
that
F\in S^{2k,r}(N)
for all
r\geqslant 1
, and from the embedding theorem in
[
JL87
, Proposition 5.7(a)]
that
F\in\Gamma_{\beta}(N)
for all
\beta<2k
. Plugging this result into (
6.9
) yields
F\in C^{2k}(N)
.
∎

Remark 6.1
.

When
k\geqslant 2
, it is difficult to know whether the minimizer is positive. We refer the readers to
[
HY16
]
for some sufficient conditions in the study of
Q
-curvature in conformal geometry. If the minimizer
F
is positive, iterating the regularity argument in Theorem
1.8
shows that
F
is smooth.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
(N^{2n+1},T^{1,0}N)
be a compact strictly pseudoconvex CR manifold with contact form
\theta
. Denote
Q=2n+2
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
with
k<n+1
. Let
f
be a real-valued function defined on
N
and
u\in S^{k,2}(N;\mathbb{R})
be a weak solution of

(6.10)

P^{\theta}_{k}u=fu.

If
f\in L^{\frac{Q}{2k}}(N)
, then
u\in L^{r}(N)
for all
r\geqslant 1
.

Proof.

We proceed as in
[
DHL00
]
. As a starting point, we claim that for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
g_{\epsilon}\in L^{\frac{Q}{2k}}(N)
, an
h_{\epsilon}\in L^{\infty}(N)
, and a constant
C(\epsilon)>0
such that

fu=g_{\epsilon}u+h_{\epsilon},\quad\|g_{\epsilon}\|_{\frac{Q}{2k}}<\epsilon,\quad\|h_{\epsilon}\|\leqslant C(\epsilon).

Here we may assume that
f\neq 0
, and we let

\begin{split}E_{l}:&=\left\{x\in N;|f|<l\right\},\\
F_{m}:&=\left\{x\in N;|u|<m\right\},\end{split}

where if
\hat{\epsilon}
is such that
(2\hat{\epsilon})^{\frac{2k}{Q}}=\frac{\epsilon}{2}
,
l
and
m
are chosen such that

\begin{split}\|f\|_{L^{\frac{Q}{2k}}(N\backslash E_{l})}<\hat{\epsilon},&\quad\|f\|_{L^{\frac{Q}{2k}}(N\backslash F_{m})}<\hat{\epsilon},\\
E_{l}\cap F_{m}\neq\emptyset,&\quad f\big|_{E_{l}\cap F_{m}}\neq 0.\end{split}

Given
K\geqslant 1
an integer that we fix below, we define

g_{\epsilon}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{1}{K}f(x),&x\in E_{l}\cap F_{m}\\
f(x),&x\in N\backslash E_{l}\cap F_{m}\\
\end{array}\right.

and

h_{\epsilon}=(f-g_{\epsilon})u.

Clearly,
h_{\epsilon}=0
on
N\backslash E_{l}\cap F_{m}
. On the other hand,

\begin{split}\|g_{\epsilon}\|_{\frac{Q}{2k}}^{\frac{Q}{2k}}&=\int_{E_{l}\cap F_{m}}|g_{\epsilon}|^{\frac{Q}{2k}}\theta\wedge d\theta^{n}+\int_{N\backslash E_{l}\cap F_{m}}|g_{\epsilon}|^{\frac{Q}{2k}}\theta\wedge d\theta^{n}\\
&\leqslant\int_{E_{l}\cap F_{m}}|g_{\epsilon}|^{\frac{Q}{2k}}\theta\wedge d\theta^{n}+\int_{N\backslash E_{l}}|g_{\epsilon}|^{\frac{Q}{2k}}\theta\wedge d\theta^{n}+\int_{N\backslash F_{m}}|g_{\epsilon}|^{\frac{Q}{2k}}\theta\wedge d\theta^{n}\\
&\leqslant\left(\frac{1}{K}\right)^{\frac{Q}{2k}}\int_{E_{l}\cap F_{m}}|f|^{\frac{Q}{2k}}\theta\wedge d\theta^{n}+2\hat{\epsilon},\end{split}

so that

\|g_{\epsilon}\|_{\frac{Q}{2k}}\leqslant\frac{1}{K}\|f\|_{\frac{Q}{2k}}+\frac{\epsilon}{2}.

Choosing
K
such that
\frac{1}{K}\|f\|_{\frac{Q}{2k}}<\frac{\epsilon}{2}
, we get

\|g_{\epsilon}\|_{\frac{Q}{2k}}<\epsilon.

Now, since
h_{\epsilon}=0
on
N\backslash E_{l}\cap F_{m}
,

\|h_{\epsilon}\|_{\infty}\leqslant\Big|1-\frac{1}{K}\Big|lm,

and this proves the above claim.

Now the equation (
6.10
) can be written as

(6.11)

P^{\theta}_{k}u=g_{\epsilon}u+h_{\epsilon}.

Adding a positive constant if necessary, we may assume that
P^{\theta}_{k}
is positive define. Let
\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}
be the operator

\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}u=\left(P^{\theta}_{k}\right)^{-1}\left(g_{\epsilon}u\right).

The equation (
6.11
) becomes

(6.12)

u-\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}u=\left(P^{\theta}_{k}\right)^{-1}(h_{\epsilon}).

For any
r>1
and any
\tilde{f}\in L^{r}(N)
, by the regularity theorem
[
Fol75
, Theorem 6.1]
, there exists a unique solution
\tilde{u}\in S^{k,r}(N)
such that
P^{\theta}_{k}\tilde{u}=\tilde{f}
. Let
v\in L^{r}(N)
,
r\geqslant\frac{2Q}{Q-2k}
, and
u_{\epsilon}\in S^{k,r}(N)
be such that

P^{\theta}_{k}u_{\epsilon}=g_{\epsilon}v.

We set
\hat{r}=\frac{Qr}{Q+2kr}
. By Hölder’s inequality,

(6.13)

\|g_{\epsilon}v\|_{\hat{r}}\leqslant\|g_{\epsilon}\|_{\frac{Q}{2k}}\|v\|_{r}.

It follows from
[
Fol75
, Theorem 6.1]
and
[
JL87
, Theorem 5.5]
that there exists a constant
C
such that

\|u_{\epsilon}\|_{r}\leqslant C\|g_{\epsilon}v\|_{\hat{r}}.

Combing this with (
6.13
), we obtain that

\|u_{\epsilon}\|_{r}\leqslant C\epsilon\|v\|_{r}.

In other words, for all
r\geqslant\frac{2Q}{Q-2k}
, we have

\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}:L^{r}(N)\to L^{r}(N),\quad\|\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{r}\to L^{r}}\leqslant C\epsilon.

Let
r\geqslant\frac{2Q}{Q-2k}
be given. For
\epsilon>0
sufficiently small, we obtain that

\|\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{r}\to L^{r}}\leqslant\frac{1}{2}.

This implies that the operator

\mathrm{Id}-\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}:L^{r}(N)\to L^{r}(N)

has an inverse. Since

\mathrm{Id}-\mathcal{H}_{\epsilon}u=\left(P^{\theta}_{k}\right)^{-1}(h_{\epsilon})

and
u\in L^{\frac{2Q}{Q-2k}}
,
h_{\epsilon}\in L^{\infty}(N)
, we get that
u\in L^{r}(N)
.
∎

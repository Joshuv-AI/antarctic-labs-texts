Extension of topological groupoids and Serre, Hurewicz morphisms
Saikat Chatterjee, Praphulla Koushik

Abstract
. In this paper, we introduce the notion of a topological groupoid extension and relate it to the already existing notion of a gerbe over a topological stack. We further study the properties of a gerbe over a Serre, Hurewicz stack.

Let
{\rm Top}
be the category of topological spaces. We fix the open cover Grothendieck topology on
{\rm Top}
.
We define a topological groupoid
\mathcal{G}=[\mathcal{G}_{1}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{G}_{0}]
to be a groupoid internal to the category of topological spaces
{\rm Top}
.
A
\mathcal{G}
-principal bundle or a
\mathcal{G}
-torsor is a map of local sections
\pi\colon P\,\rightarrow M
with an action of
\mathcal{G}
on
P
satisfying certain conditions. Then it is a common knowledge that the category of
\mathcal{G}
bundles
B\mathcal{G}
defines a stack over
{\rm Top}
. In particular a stack over
{\rm Top}
is called a topological stack if it is isomorphic to
B\mathcal{G}
for some topological groupoid
\mathcal{G}
. A gerbe is basically a morphism of topological stacks
F\colon\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
with some additional properties (namely both
F\colon\mathcal{D}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}
and the diagonal morphism
\Delta_{F}\colon\mathcal{D}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}\times_{\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{D}
are epimorphism of topological stacks)
[
13
]
. A topological groupoid extension is a morphism of topological groupoids
(F,1_{M})\colon[\mathcal{G}_{1}\rightrightarrows M]\rightarrow[\mathcal{H}_{1}\rightrightarrows M]
such that
F\colon\mathcal{G}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{1}
admits local sections.
We call a morphism of topological groupoids
[\Delta_{1}\rightrightarrows\Delta_{0}]\rightarrow[\Gamma_{1}\rightrightarrows\Gamma_{0}]
Morita morphism of topological groupoids if
\Delta_{0}\rightarrow\Gamma_{0}
is a map of local sections and
[\Delta_{1}\rightrightarrows\Delta_{0}]
is the pullback groupoid of
[\Gamma_{1}\rightrightarrows\Gamma_{0}]
along the morphism
\Delta_{0}\rightarrow\Gamma_{0}
. If there exists a pair of Morita morphisms
[P_{1}\rightrightarrows P_{0}]\rightarrow[\Delta_{1}\rightrightarrows\Delta_{0}]
and
[P_{1}\rightrightarrows P_{0}]\rightarrow[\Gamma_{1}\rightrightarrows\Gamma_{0}]
, then we say
[\Delta_{1}\rightrightarrows\Delta_{0}]
and
[\Gamma_{1}\rightrightarrows\Gamma_{0}]
are Morita equivalent. This definition of Morita equivalence naturally extends to the definition of Morita equivalence of topological groupoid extensions.

In this paper, we show that a Morita equivalence class of topological groupoid extension defines a gerbe over a topological stack. On the other hand a gerbe
F\colon\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
for which the diagonal morphism
\Delta_{F}\colon\mathcal{D}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}\times_{\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{D}
is a representable map of local sections defines a Morita equivalence class of topological groupoid extensions. It should be noted here that a representable map of local sections is a slightly stronger condition than an epimorphism. The corresponding result in smooth set-up has been proven by the authors in
[
7
]
.

Section

is a Hurewicz gerbe (respectively, a Serre gerbe).

2.
Topological groupoids and topological stacks

We begin with the simplest and most general definition of a topological groupoid. We will mostly rely on the definitions in
[
5
]
.
By a
topological groupoid
, we mean a groupoid
\mathcal{G}
whose object and morphism sets are topological spaces, and all structure maps are continuous. We denote the object and morphism sets, respectively, by
\mathcal{G}_{0}
and
\mathcal{G}_{1}
. Some other authors have defined a topological groupoid requiring the structure maps to satisfy some additional conditions and studied properties of such topological groupoids. It would also be our interest to study some of those properties. However, instead of incorporating the conditions in the definition itself, we will impose the conditions when and where required.
A morphism
\phi\colon\mathcal{G}\,\rightarrow\,\mathcal{H}
between topological groupoids
is a continuous functor compatible with all the structure maps of the groupoids. We say a continuous map
f\colon X\rightarrow Y

admits local sections
or is a
map of local sections
if there is an open cover
\{U_{\alpha}\}
of
Y
and a continuous section
U_{\alpha}\,\rightarrow\,X
for each
\alpha
.

Definition 2.1

(topological groupoid extension)
.

Let
[\mathcal{H}_{1}\rightrightarrows M]
be a topological groupoid. A
topological groupoid extension of
[\mathcal{H}_{1}\rightrightarrows M]
is a morphism of topological groupoids
(F,1_{M})\colon[\mathcal{G}_{1}\rightrightarrows M]\rightarrow[\mathcal{H}_{1}\rightrightarrows M]
such that
F\colon\mathcal{G}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{1}
admits local sections. We often denote such a topological groupoid extension by
F\colon\mathcal{G}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{1}\rightrightarrows M
.

The Morita equivalence of Lie groupoids and Lie groupoid extensions have very interesting properties and have been studied in
[
11
]
and
[
7
]
. The counterpart of the same in the topological set-up has not been studied. In fact, even we are not aware of any existing definition of Morita equivalent topological groupoid extensions. Here we introduce the notion of Morita equivalent topological groupoid extensions and study some of their properties. Particularly we are interested in its association with the topological gerbes.

Let
[\Gamma_{1}\rightrightarrows\Gamma_{0}]
be a topological groupoid and
J:P_{0}\rightarrow\Gamma_{0}
be a map of local sections. Let
P_{1}
denote the fiber product

P_{0}\times_{J,\Gamma_{0},s}\Gamma_{1}\times_{t,\Gamma_{0},J}P_{0}
. Then
[P_{1}\rightrightarrows P_{0}]
has a topological groupoid structure with source and target maps being the projection maps
{\rm pr}_{1},{\rm pr}_{3}\colon P_{1}\rightarrow P_{0}
respectively. We call
[P_{1}\rightrightarrows P_{0}]
to be the
pullback groupoid
of
[\Gamma_{1}\rightrightarrows\Gamma_{0}]
along
J\,\colon\,P_{0}\rightarrow\Gamma_{0}
.

A morphism of topological groupoids
[\Delta_{1}\rightrightarrows\Delta_{0}]\rightarrow[\Gamma_{1}\rightrightarrows\Gamma_{0}]
is said to be a
Morita morphism of topological groupoids
if
\Delta_{0}\rightarrow\Gamma_{0}
is a map of local sections and
[\Delta_{1}\rightrightarrows\Delta_{0}]
is isomorphic to the pullback groupoid of
[\Gamma_{1}\rightrightarrows\Gamma_{0}]
along the morphism
\Delta_{0}\rightarrow\Gamma_{0}
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
[\Gamma_{1}\rightrightarrows\Gamma_{0}]
and
[\Delta_{1}\rightrightarrows\Delta_{0}]
be a pair of topological groupoids. We say that
[\Gamma_{1}\rightrightarrows\Gamma_{0}]
and
[\Delta_{1}\rightrightarrows\Delta_{0}]
are
Morita equivalent
if there exists a third topological groupoid
[P_{1}\rightrightarrows P_{0}]
with a pair of Morita morphisms

[P_{1}\rightrightarrows P_{0}]\rightarrow[\Delta_{1}\rightrightarrows\Delta_{0}]
and
[P_{1}\rightrightarrows P_{0}]\rightarrow[\Gamma_{1}\rightrightarrows\Gamma_{0}]
.

Definition 2.4

(Morita equivalent topological groupoid extensions)
.

Let
\phi^{\prime}\colon X_{1}^{\prime}\rightarrow Y_{1}^{\prime}\rightrightarrows M^{\prime}

Definition 2.5

(
[
5
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=[\mathcal{G}_{1}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{G}_{0}]
be a topological groupoid. Let
P
be a topological space. A
left action of the topological groupoid
\mathcal{G}
on the topological space
P
is given by a pair of continuous maps
(a_{\mathcal{G}}\colon P\rightarrow\mathcal{G}_{0},\mu\colon\mathcal{G}_{1}\times_{s,\mathcal{G}_{0},a_{\mathcal{G}}}P\rightarrow P)

satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

a_{\mathcal{G}}(\mu(\gamma,p))=t(\gamma)
for all
(\gamma,p)\in\mathcal{G}_{1}\times_{s,\mathcal{G}_{0},a_{\mathcal{G}}}P
,

(2)

\mu(1_{a_{\mathcal{G}}(p)},p)=p
for all
p\in P
,

(3)

\mu(\gamma,\mu(\gamma^{\prime},p))=\mu(\gamma\circ\gamma^{\prime},p)
for all
(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime},p)\in\mathcal{G}_{1}\times_{s,\mathcal{G}_{0},t}\mathcal{G}_{1}\times_{s,\mathcal{G}_{0},a_{\mathcal{G}}}P
.

Similarly a
right action
is a pair
of continuous maps
(a_{\mathcal{G}}\colon P\rightarrow\mathcal{G}_{0},\mu\colon P\times_{a_{\mathcal{G}},\mathcal{G}_{0},t}\mathcal{G}_{1}\rightarrow P)
satisfying

(1)

a_{\mathcal{G}}(\mu(p,\gamma))=s(\gamma)
for all
(p,\gamma)\in P\times_{a_{\mathcal{G}},\mathcal{G}_{0},t}\mathcal{G}_{1}
,

(2)

\mu(p,1_{a_{\mathcal{G}}(p)})=p
for all
p\in P
,

(3)

\mu(\mu(p,\gamma),\gamma^{\prime})=\mu(p,\gamma\circ\gamma^{\prime})
for all
(p,\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})\in P\times_{a_{\mathcal{G}},\mathcal{G}_{0},t}\mathcal{G}_{1}\times_{s,\mathcal{G}_{0},t}\mathcal{G}_{1}
.

Note that the topological spaces
\mathcal{G}_{1}\times_{s,\mathcal{G}_{0},a_{\mathcal{G}}}P
and
P\times_{a_{\mathcal{G}},\mathcal{G}_{0},t}\mathcal{G}_{1}
will respectively be denoted as
\mathcal{G}_{1}\times_{\mathcal{G}_{0}}P

and
P\times_{\mathcal{G}_{0}}\mathcal{G}_{1}
. We will also write
p\cdot\gamma
instead of
\mu(p,\gamma)
.

Definition 2.6

(
[
5
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=[\mathcal{G}_{1}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{G}_{0}]
be a topological groupoid and
M
be a topological space.
A
principal
\mathcal{G}
-bundle over the topological space
M
is a topological space
P
with a continuous map
\pi\colon P\rightarrow M
admitting local sections and a right action
(a_{\mathcal{G}},\mu)
of
\mathcal{G}
on
P
such that

(1)

\pi(p\cdot\gamma)=\pi(p)
for all
(p,\gamma)\in P\times_{\mathcal{G}_{0}}\mathcal{G}_{1}
,

(2)

the map
P\times_{\mathcal{G}_{0}}\mathcal{G}_{1}\rightarrow P\times_{M}P
,
(p,\gamma)\mapsto(p,p\cdot\gamma)
is a homeomorphism.

Observe that the condition (1) above ensures that the action is fiber preserving, whereas (2) ensures that the action is transitive and free on the fibers. Note that in
[
5
]
, the
\mathcal{G}
acts on the left, whereas, here we have considered as a right action.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=[\mathcal{G}_{1}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{G}_{0}]
be a topological groupoid and
(P,\pi,M),(P^{\prime},\pi^{\prime},M^{\prime})
principal
\mathcal{G}
-bundles. A
morphism
from
P(\pi,M)
to
P^{\prime}(\pi^{\prime},M^{\prime})
is given by a pair of continuous maps

(F\colon P\rightarrow P^{\prime},f\colon M\rightarrow M^{\prime})
, satisfying the following compatibility conditions

(1)

a_{\mathcal{G}}^{\prime}\circ F=a_{\mathcal{G}}
, and

F(p\cdot\gamma)=F(p)\cdot\gamma
for all

(p,\gamma)\in P\times_{\mathcal{G}_{0}}\mathcal{G}_{1}
,

(2)

\pi^{\prime}\circ F=f\circ\pi
.

Example 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{H}=[\mathcal{H}_{1}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{H}_{0}]
be a topological groupoid. Identity assigning map
\mathcal{H}_{0}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{1}
defines a section of the target map
t\colon\mathcal{H}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{0}
. Then
t\colon\mathcal{H}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{0}
can be considered a principal
\mathcal{H}
-bundle, with action of
\mathcal{H}
given by the pair, composition of arrows and the source map
s\colon\mathcal{H}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{0}
.

Given a continuous map
f\colon M^{\prime}\to M
between topological spaces, and a principal
\mathcal{G}
-bundle
(P,\pi,M)
over
M
, we can obtain a principal
\mathcal{G}
-bundle
(f^{*}P,M^{\prime})
by the usual pull-back of
(P,\pi,M)
. The action of
\mathcal{G}
on
f^{*}P
is given by
((p,n),\gamma))\mapsto(\mu(p,\gamma),n)
.
One of the advantages of working with topological spaces (instead of, let us say, smooth spaces) is that the pullback exists in the category of topological spaces. Let
Top
be the category of (small) topological spaces. Some authors work with the Cartesian closed category of compactly generated Hausdorff spaces, and they often use the notation
Top
for the same. However, we do not have any such requirement in this paper until Section
5
, and we stick to the conventional meaning of
Top
. In Section
5
we will restrict to the category of compactly generated Hausdorff spaces; the same will be denoted as
{\rm CGTop}
.

According to our convenience and needs, we will be using either of the two equivalent definitions of a stack, which we introduce in the following passages. One requires the notion of a fibered category, and the other the notion of a pseudo-functor. We refer to Chapter
3
of
[
22
]
to see a detailed correspondence between pseudo-functors and fibered categories. Here we will very briefly describe the correspondence.
First, we recall the definition in terms of a pseudo-functor.

Definition 2.8

(
[

]

.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a category.
A
pseudo-functor on
\mathcal{C}
, denoted by
\mathcal{F}\colon\mathcal{C}^{op}\rightarrow\text{Cat}
, consists of the following data:

(1)

a category
\mathcal{F}(U)
for each object
U
of
\mathcal{C}
,

(2)

a functor
f^{*}\colon\mathcal{F}(V)\rightarrow\mathcal{F}(U)
for each morphism
f\colon U\rightarrow V
of
\mathcal{C}
,

(3)

an isomorphism
\epsilon_{U}\colon(1_{U})^{*}\Rightarrow 1_{\mathcal{F}(U)}\colon\mathcal{F}(U)\rightarrow\mathcal{F}(U)
for each object
U
of
\mathcal{C}
,

(4)

an isomorphism
\alpha_{f,g}\colon(g\circ f)^{*}\Rightarrow f^{*}g^{*}\colon\mathcal{F}(W)\rightarrow\mathcal{F}(U)
for each pair of morphisms
U\xrightarrow{f}V\xrightarrow{g}W
of
\mathcal{C}
,

satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

for every morphism

in
\mathcal{C}
and every object

we have

\alpha_{f,1_{V}}(\eta)=f^{*}(\epsilon_{V}\eta),\text{\penalty\ and\penalty\ }\alpha_{1_{U},f}(\eta)=\epsilon_{U}(f^{*}\eta),

(2)

for every triple of morphisms
U\xrightarrow{f}V\xrightarrow{g}W\xrightarrow{h}T
in
\mathcal{C}
and an object
\eta
of
\mathcal{F}(T)
, we have

\alpha_{gf,h}(\eta)\circ\alpha_{f,g}(h^{*}\eta)=\alpha_{f,hg}(\eta)\circ f^{*}\alpha_{g,h}(\eta).

denote the category of
\mathcal{G}
-bundles over the topological space

, with arrows being morphisms of

-bundles of the form

-bundles over the topological space

Now we turn the category

An alternate way to define a stack is in terms of fibered categories. In fact, this alternate definition sometimes can be easier to work with.
For our future purpose, we introduce the definition of a fibered category here.

Let
\pi_{\mathcal{D}}\colon\mathcal{D}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}
be a functor. For an object
U
of
\mathcal{C}
, let
\mathcal{D}(U)
be the category with following description:

•

\text{Obj}(\mathcal{D}(U))=\{\eta\in\text{Obj}(\mathcal{D})|\pi_{\mathcal{D}}(\eta)=U\}
,

•

\text{Mor}_{\mathcal{D}(U)}(\eta,\eta^{\prime})=\{f\colon\eta\rightarrow\eta^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}|\pi_{\mathcal{D}}(f)=1_{U}\}
.

We call the category
\mathcal{D}(U)
to be
the fiber of
U
in
\mathcal{D}
.

We end this section by making a note of the following properties of a topological stack, which obviously fail in the smooth set-up.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a topological stack. Then, for every topological space
X
, any morphism of stacks
X\rightarrow\mathcal{X}
is representable.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a topological stack. That means there exists a topological space
M
and a morphism of stacks
M\rightarrow\mathcal{X}
that is a representable epimorphism.

Let
Y
be a topological space and
g:Y\rightarrow\mathcal{X}
a morphism of stacks. We show that
X\times_{\mathcal{X}}Y
is a representable stack.

As
M\rightarrow\mathcal{X}
is representable, for the morphism
X\rightarrow\mathcal{X}
, the fiber product
X\times_{\mathcal{X}}M
is representable and likewise
M\times_{\mathcal{X}}Y
is also representable. Considering the morphisms
X\times_{\mathcal{X}}M\rightarrow M
and
M\times_{\mathcal{X}}Y\rightarrow M
, the fiber product
(X\times_{\mathcal{X}}M)\times_{M}(M\times_{\mathcal{X}}Y)
is representable by topological spaces (as all three stacks are representable by topological spaces). Identifying
(X\times_{\mathcal{X}}M)\times_{M}(M\times_{\mathcal{X}}Y)
with
X\times_{\mathcal{X}}Y
, we conclude that the fiber product
X\times_{\mathcal{X}}Y
is representable by a topological space.
∎

3.
Morphism of topological groupoids and topological stacks

Definition 3.1

(
[
13
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=[\mathcal{G}_{1}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{G}_{0}]
and
\mathcal{H}=[\mathcal{H}_{1}\rightrightarrows\mathcal{H}_{0}]
be topological groupoids. A
\mathcal{G}-\mathcal{H}
bibundle
consists of

•

a topological space
P
,

•

a left action of
\mathcal{G}
on
P
given by the pair
(a_{\mathcal{G}}\colon P\rightarrow\mathcal{G}_{0},\mu_{\mathcal{G}}\colon\mathcal{G}_{1}\times_{s,\mathcal{G}_{0},a_{\mathcal{G}}}P\rightarrow P)
,

•

a right action of
\mathcal{H}
on
P
given by the pair
(a_{\mathcal{H}}\colon P\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{0},\mu_{\mathcal{H}}\colon P\times_{a_{\mathcal{H}},\mathcal{H}_{0},t}\mathcal{H}_{1}\rightarrow P)
,

satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

the map
a_{\mathcal{G}}\colon P\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{0}
is a principal
\mathcal{H}
-bundle,

(2)

the map
a_{\mathcal{H}}\colon P\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{0}
is
\mathcal{G}
-invariant, ; that is
a_{\mathcal{H}}(\gamma\cdot p)=a_{\mathcal{H}}(p)
for all
(\gamma,p)\in\mathcal{G}_{1}\times_{\mathcal{G}_{0}}P
,

(3)

the action of
\mathcal{G}
on
P

is compatible with the action of
\mathcal{H}
on
P
, that is

\gamma\cdot(p\cdot\delta)=(\gamma\cdot p)\cdot\delta
for each
(\gamma,p,\delta)\in\mathcal{G}_{1}\times_{s,\mathcal{G}_{0},a_{\mathcal{G}}}P\times_{a_{\mathcal{H}},\mathcal{H}_{0},t}\mathcal{H}_{1}
.

We denote a
\mathcal{G}-\mathcal{H}
-bibundle by
P\colon\mathcal{G}\to{\mathcal{H}}
.

.

\mathcal{H}
-bundle

5.
Serre, Hurewicz fibrations and gerbes

In the preceding section we have seen that a topological groupoid extension
F\colon[\mathcal{G}_{1}\rightrightarrows M]\rightarrow[\mathcal{H}_{1}\rightrightarrows M]
associates a gerbe
F\colon B\mathcal{G}\rightarrow B\mathcal{H}
over a topological stack. In this section, we are going to recall the definition of Serre and Hurewicz stacks introduced in
[
20
]
and study certain properties of a gerbe over such stacks.

Consistent with the definition in
[
20
]
, here we restrict to the category
CGTop
of compactly generated (Hausdorff) topological spaces with the usual open-cover topology.

Definition 5.1

(
[
20
]
)
.

A topological stack
\mathcal{D}
is said to be a
Hurewicz stack
(respectively,
Serre stack
) if it admits a presentation
\mathbb{X}=[R\rightrightarrows X]
by a topological groupoid in which the source (hence also the target) map
s\colon R\rightarrow X
is locally a Hurewicz fibration (respectively, Serre fibration).

Definition 5.2

(
[
20
]
)
.

A morphism of topological stacks
F\colon\mathcal{D}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}
is said to be a Hurewicz (respectively, Serre) morphism if for every object
U
of
Top
and a morphism of topological stacks
U\rightarrow\mathcal{C}
, the fiber product
\mathcal{D}\times_{\mathcal{C}}U
is a Hurewicz (respectively, Serre) stack.

Here, we say a gerbe
F\colon\mathcal{D}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}
is a
Hurewicz gerbe
(respectively, a
Serre gerbe
), if the underline map of stacks is Hurewicz (respectively, Serre).

Let
F\colon(\mathcal{G}_{1}\rightrightarrows M)\rightarrow(\mathcal{H}_{1}\rightrightarrows M)
be a topological groupoid extension. Suppose
(\mathcal{G}_{1}\rightrightarrows M)
is a topological groupoid with the source map
s\colon\mathcal{G}_{1}\rightarrow M
a locally a Hurewicz fibration (respectively, Serre fibration). Then
B\mathcal{G}
is a Hurewicz stack (respectively, Serre stack). Following result directly follows from Theorem
4.1
and
[
20
, Lemma 2.4.]
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=(\mathcal{G}_{1}\rightrightarrows M)
be a topological groupoid with the source map
s\colon\mathcal{G}_{1}\rightarrow M
a locally a Hurewicz fibration (respectively, Serre fibration) and
F\colon(\mathcal{G}_{1}\rightrightarrows M)\rightarrow(\mathcal{H}_{1}\rightrightarrows M)
a topological groupoid extension. Then the gerbe
F\colon B\mathcal{G}\rightarrow B{\mathcal{H}}
constructed in Theorem
4.1
is a Hurewicz gerbe (respectively, Serre gerbe).

B[X\rightrightarrows X]\rightarrow B\mathcal{H}
induced by a morphism of topological groupoids
[X\rightrightarrows X]\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
is a Serre morphism as well as a Hurewicz morphism.

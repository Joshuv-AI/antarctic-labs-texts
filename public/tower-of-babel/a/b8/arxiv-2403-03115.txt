An elliptic problem in dimension N N with a varying drift term bounded in L N L^{N} .
Juan Casado-Díaz Dpto. de Ecuaciones Diferenciales y Análisis Numérico Universidad de Sevilla jcasadod@us.es

Abstract
The present paper is devoted to study the asymptotic behavior of a sequence of linear elliptic equations with a varying drift term, whose coefficients are just bounded in L N ​ ( Ω ) L^{N}(\Omega) , with N N the dimension of the space. It is known that there exists a unique solution for each of these problems in the Sobolev space H 0 1 ​ ( Ω ) H^{1}_{0}(\Omega) . However, because the operators are not coercive, there is no uniform estimate of the solutions in this space. We use some estimates in [ 2 ] , and a regularization obtained by adding a small nonlinear first order term, to pass to the limit in these problems.



Keywords:
asymptotic behavior, elliptic problem, drift term, varying coefficients.

1
Introduction.

For a bounded open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we are interested in passing to the limit in a sequence on elliptic equations with a varying drift term, whose coefficients are just bounded in
L^{N}(\Omega)^{N}
, (
L^{p}(\Omega)^{N}
,
p>2
if
N=2
). The problem is written as

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle-{\rm div}(A\nabla u_{n}+E_{n}u_{n})=f_{n}\ \hbox{ in }\Omega\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle u_{n}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

with
A\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{N\times N}
satisfying the usual uniform ellipticity condition, and
f_{n}
bounded in
H^{-1}(\Omega)
. Thanks to Sobolev’s inequality,
the integrability assumption on
E_{n}
is the weaker one to get the first order term well defined in
H^{-1}(\Omega)
.
It is known that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
problem (
1.1
) has a unique solution (
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
15
]
), however, the problem is known to be not coercive and thus, there is no estimate for
u_{n}
in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. We recall some of these results in Section
2
.

L. Boccardo found in
[
2
]
an estimate for

\ln(1+|u_{n}|)
in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
depending only on
\|f_{n}\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}
and
\|E_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)^{N}}
. As a consequence of this result we get that the measure of the sets
\{|u_{n}|>k\}
tends to zero when
k
tends to infinity uniformly in
n
. Thanks to this result L. Boccardo proved in
[
5
]
that
f_{n}
converging weakly to some
f
in
H^{-1}(\Omega)
,
E_{n}
converging weakly to
E_{0}
in
L^{N}(\Omega)
and
|E_{n}|^{N}
equi-integrable, imply that the solution
u_{n}
of (
1.1
) converges weakly in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
to the solution
u_{0}
of

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle-{\rm div}(A\nabla u_{0}+E_{0}u_{0})=f\ \hbox{ in }\Omega\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle u_{0}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).\end{array}\right.

In the case where
|E_{n}|^{N}
is not equi-integrable, we do not have an estimate for
u_{n}
in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and then the proof is more involved. The ideas in
[
2
]
imply (see
[
5
]
) that
\ln^{q}(1+|u_{n}|)
is bounded in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
for any
q>1
. Using also the approximation of
u_{n}
given by the solution of a perturbation of (
1.1
) by a nonlinear zero order term (see (
3.12
) below), we still manage to pass to the limit in (
1.1
) but, instead of the weak convergence in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, we only get

\ln^{q}(1+|u_{n}|)\,{\rm sgn}(u_{n})\rightharpoonup\ln^{q}(1+|u_{0}|)\,{\rm sgn}(u_{0})\ \hbox{ in }H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\quad\forall\,q\geq 1.

This is the main result of the paper, which is proved in Theorem
3.1
.

Another problem related to (
1.1
) is given by its adjoint formulation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle-{\rm div}(A^{T}\nabla u_{n})+E_{n}\cdot\nabla u_{n}=f_{n}\ \hbox{ in }\Omega\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle u_{n}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\end{array}\right.

(1.2)

Some results about the asymptotic behavior of the solutions of this problem have been obtained in
[
5
]
.
Namely, assuming the equi-integrability condition on
|E_{n}|
and reasoning by duality, we deduce from the results stated above for problem (
1.1
) that the solutions of (
1.2
) are bounded in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. Moreover, the results in
[
9
]
prove that
u_{n}
is compact in
W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega)
for
1\leq q<2
. Then, it is immediate to pass to the limit in this problem. In the case where
|E_{n}|^{N}
is not equi-integrable, we do not have any estimate for
u_{n}
and then we are not able to passing to the limit in (
1.2
). However, adding a sequence of zero order terms
a_{n}u_{n}
with
a_{n}
satisfying

a_{n}\geq\gamma\ \hbox{ a.e. in }\Omega,

for some
\gamma>0
, and assuming
f_{n}
bounded in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, it is proved in
[
5
]
that
u_{n}
is bounded in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. This allows us to pass to the limit in (
1.2
).

The homogenization of a sequence of elliptic PDE’s with a singular term of first order has also been carried out in other papers. In this sense we refer to
[
6
]
where it is considered problem (
1.1
) with the matrix function
A
also depending on

, the sequence

converging weakly in

, and

converging strongly in

. The definition of solution in this case is related to the definition of entropy or renormalized solution (see e.g.
[
1
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
14
]
). In
[
10
]
and
[
13
]
it is considered the case of a first order term of the form

E_{n}\nabla u_{n}+{\rm div}(E_{n}u_{n}).

Since this term is skew-symmetric we can obtain an estimate in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
which is independent of
E_{n}
(we refer to
[
7
]
for a related existence result). Assuming

just bounded in

it is proved that the limit problem contains a new term of zero order. This is related to the results
obtained in
[
16
]
for the Stokes equation with an oscillating Coriolis force. We also refer to
[

]
,
[
12
]
for related results in the case of the evolutive elastic system submitted to an oscillating magnetic field. Now, the limit problem is nonlocal in general.

Some reminders about elliptic problems with a drift or convection term.

For a bounded open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N\geq 2
, a matrix function
A\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{N\times N}
, such that there exists
\alpha>0
satisfying

A(x)\xi\cdot\xi\geq\alpha|\xi|^{2},\quad\forall\,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\ \hbox{ a.e. }x\in\Omega,

(2.1)

two measurable functions
E:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
a:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
,
a\geq 0
a.e. in
\Omega
, and a distribution
f\in H^{-1}(\Omega)
,
we recall in this section, some results about the existence and uniqueness of solution for problems

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}{\cal A}u=f\ \hbox{ in }\Omega\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\end{array}\right.\qquad\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}{\cal A}^{\ast}u=f\ \hbox{ in }\Omega\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\end{array}\right.

(2.2)

with

{\cal A}u=-{\rm div}(A\nabla u+Eu)+au,\qquad{\cal A}^{\ast}u=-{\rm div}(A^{T}\nabla u)+E\cdot\nabla u+au,

Observe that due to Sobolev imbedding theorem, in order to have the terms
{\rm div}(Eu)
,
E\cdot\nabla u
and
au
in
H^{-1}(\Omega)
, when
u
is in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, we need to assume

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}E\in L^{N}(\Omega)^{N}&\hbox{ if }N>2\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle E\in L^{p}(\Omega)^{N},\ p>2&\hbox{ if }N=2,\end{array}\right.\qquad\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}a\in L^{N\over 2}(\Omega)&\hbox{ if }N>2\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle a\in L^{q}(\Omega)^{N},\ q>1&\hbox{ if }N=2,\end{array}\right.

(2.3)

In this case,
{\cal A}
and
{\cal A}^{\ast}
are continuous linear operators from
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
into
H^{-1}(\Omega)
and
{\cal A}^{\ast}
is the adjoint operator of
{\cal A}
.

Assuming the further assumption

E\in L^{p}(\Omega)^{N},\quad p>N,

(2.4)

the compact imbedding of
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
into
L^{2p\over p-2}(\Omega)
proves that for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
C_{\varepsilon}>0
such that

\|u\|_{L^{2p\over p-2}(\Omega)}\leq\alpha\varepsilon\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)^{N}}+C_{\varepsilon}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},\quad\forall\,u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

(2.5)

Thus, Hölder’s and Young’s inequalities imply

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\langle{\cal A}u,u\rangle_{H^{-1}(\Omega),H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}=\langle{\cal A}^{\ast},u\rangle_{H^{-1}(\Omega),H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}=\int_{\Omega}\big(A\nabla u\cdot\nabla u\,dx+uE\cdot\nabla u+au^{2}\big)dx\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle\geq\alpha\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)^{N}}^{2}-\|E\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)^{N}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)^{N}}\|u\|_{L^{2p\over p-2}(\Omega)}\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle\geq\alpha\Big({1\over 2}-\varepsilon\|E\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)^{N}}\Big)\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)^{N}}-{C_{\varepsilon}^{2}\|E\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)^{N}}^{2}\over 2\alpha}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.\end{array}

Therefore, taking
\varepsilon\|E\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)^{N}}<1/2
, we deduce from Lax-Milgram theorem that replacing
a

by
a+\gamma
, with

\gamma\geq{C_{\varepsilon}^{2}\|E\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)^{N}}^{2}\over 2\alpha},

there exists a unique solution for both problems in (
2.2
).
The compactness of
({\cal A}+\gamma I)^{-1}
, considered as an operator in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, allows then to use Fredholm theory to deduce that the existence and uniqueness of solutions for both problems in (
2.2
) and every
f\in H^{-1}(\Omega)
is equivalent to the uniqueness of solutions for one of them.

In
[
15
]
, Theorem 8.1, it is proved that problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}{\cal A}^{\ast}u=f\ \hbox{ in }\Omega\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle u-\phi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\end{array}\right.

with

satisfies the weak maximum principle so that the second problem in (

) has at most one solution (in

We observe however that although the above result implies the existence and continuity of the operators

If we assume

Passing to the limit with a varying drift term.

\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N},

3.7
), we finally get

\int_{\Omega}\Big|\nabla\Big(\ln^{t+2\over 2}(1+|u_{n}|)\,{\rm sgn}(u_{n})\Big)\Big|^{2}dx\leq C\left(\int_{\Omega}|E_{n}|^{r}dx\right)^{t+2\over r}+C\|f_{n}\|^{2}_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}.

(3.9)

Since
t
can be taken as large as we want, we conclude

\ln^{q}(1+|u_{n}|)\,{\rm sgn}(u_{n})\ \hbox{ bounded in }H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\quad\forall\,q>0.

(3.10)

In particular,
T_{m}(u_{n})
is bounded in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
. These estimates and the Rellich-Kondrachov’s compactness theorem prove the existence of a subsequence of
n
, still denoted by
n
, and a measurable function
u
such that for every
m,q>0
, we have

T_{m}(u_{n})\rightharpoonup T_{m}(u)\hbox{ in }H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\quad\ln^{q}(1+|u_{n}|)\rightharpoonup\ln^{q}(1+|u|)\hbox{ in }H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

(3.11)

Now, we have to prove that
u=u_{0}
.

Step 2.
Let us first consider the case
N=2
or
N\geq 3
,
|E_{n}|^{N}
equi-integrable. It has been first carried out in
[
5
]
. Indeed, since for
N=2
,
p
in (
3.1
) is any number bigger than 2, the problem reduces to assume
|E_{n}|^{r}
equi-integrable with
r
given by (
3.8
). Taking as test function in (
3.2
)
u_{n}-T_{m}(u_{n})
, we deduce

\int_{\{m<|u_{n}|\}}\big(A\nabla u_{n}+E_{n}u_{n}\big)\cdot\nabla u_{n}\,dx=\langle f_{n},u_{n}-T_{m}(u_{n})\rangle_{H^{-1}(\Omega),H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)},

which similarly to (
2.12
) implies

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\|u_{n}-T_{m}(u_{n})\|^{2}_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\big(\alpha-C_{S}\|E_{n}\|_{L^{r}(\{m<|u_{n}|\})^{N}}\big)\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle\leq\big(\|f_{n}\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}+mC_{S}\|E_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)^{N}}\big)\|u_{n}-T_{m}(u_{n})\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}.\end{array}

Thanks to (
3.10
), we have

\lim_{m\to\infty}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\big|\{|u_{n}|>m\big\}\big|=0,

which combined with the equi-integrability of
|E_{n}|^{r}
implies the existence of
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\|E_{n}\|_{L^{r}(\{m<|u_{n}|\})^{N}}<\alpha/(2C_{S})
, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Therefore
u_{n}-T_{m}(u_{n})
is bounded in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. Since
T_{m}(u_{n})
is bounded in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
too, we get
u_{n}
bounded in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. Taking into account (
3.4
), (
3.5
) we have that
u_{n}
converges weakly to
u
in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. By the Rellich-Kondrachov’s compactness theorem, we can now easily pass to the limit in (
3.2
) in the distributional sense to deduce that
u=u_{0}
, the solution of (
3.6
).

Step 3.
In this and the following step we assume
N\geq 3
,
|E_{n}|^{N}
not necessarily equi-integrable.

For
\delta>0
, we define
w_{n,\delta}
as the solution of

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle-{\rm div}\big(A\nabla w_{n,\delta}+E_{n}w_{n,\delta}\big)+\delta|w_{n,\delta}|^{4\over N-2}w_{n,\delta}=f_{n}\ \hbox{ in }\Omega\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle w_{n,\delta}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).\end{array}\right.

(3.12)

Using
w_{n,\delta}
as test function in (
3.12
), we have

\int_{\Omega}A\nabla w_{n,\delta}\cdot\nabla w_{n,\delta}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}w_{n,\delta}E_{n}\cdot\nabla w_{n,\delta}\,dx+\delta\int_{\Omega}|w_{n,\delta}|^{2N\over N-2}dx=\langle f_{n},w_{n,\delta}\rangle.

(3.13)

In the second term of this equality we use Young’s inequality with exponents
2N/(N-2)
,
N
and 2, to get

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega}w_{n,\delta}E_{n}\cdot\nabla w_{n,\delta}\,dx\right|=\left|\int_{\Omega}\Big(\delta^{N-2\over 2N}w_{n,\delta}\Big)\Big({2^{1\over 2}\over\alpha^{1\over 2}\delta^{N-2\over 2N}}E_{n}\Big)\cdot\Big({\alpha^{1\over 2}\over 2^{1\over 2}}\nabla w_{n,\delta}\Big)dx\right|\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle\leq{N-2\over 2N}\delta\int_{\Omega}|w_{n,\delta}|^{2N\over N-2}dx+{2^{N\over 2}\over N\alpha^{N\over 2}\delta^{N-2\over 2}}\int_{\Omega}|E_{n}|^{N}dx+{\alpha\over 4}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla w_{n,\delta}|^{2}dx.\end{array}

In the last term, Young’s inequality also gives

\big|\langle f_{n},w_{n,\delta}\rangle\big|\leq\|f_{n}\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}\|w_{n,\delta}\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}\leq{1\over\alpha}\|f_{n}\|^{2}_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}+{\alpha\over 4}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla w_{n,\delta}|^{2}dx.

Using also (
2.1
) in the first term, we deduce from (
3.12
)

{\alpha\over 2}\int_{\Omega}\big|\nabla w_{n,\delta}\big|^{2}dx+{N+2\over 2N}\delta\int_{\Omega}|w_{n,\delta}|^{2N\over N-2}dx\leq{2^{N\over 2}\over N\alpha^{N\over 2}\delta^{N-2\over 2}}\int_{\Omega}|E_{n}|^{N}dx+{1\over\alpha}\|f_{n}\|^{2}_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}.

(3.14)

Thus, for every
\delta>0
,
w_{n,\delta}
is bounded in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. Thanks to Rellich-Kondrakov’s compactness theorem this allows us to pass to the limit in (
3.12
)
in the distributional sense, to deduce

w_{n,\delta}\rightharpoonup w_{\ast,\delta}\ \hbox{ in }H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),

(3.15)

with
w_{\ast,\delta}
the solution of

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle-{\rm div}(A\nabla w_{\ast,\delta}+E_{0}w_{\ast,\delta})+\delta|w_{\ast,\delta}|^{4\over N-2}w_{\ast,\delta}=f\ \hbox{ in }\Omega\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle w_{\ast,\delta}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).\end{array}\right.

(3.16)

We take
u_{0}
the solution of (
3.6
). Taking
T_{\rho}(w_{\ast,\delta}-u_{0})
, with
\rho>0
as test function in the difference of (
3.16
) and (
3.6
), we have

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\int_{\{|w_{\ast,\delta}-u_{0}|<\rho\}}\hskip-10.0pt\big(A\nabla(w_{\ast,\delta}-u_{0})+E(w_{\ast,\delta}-u_{0})\big)\cdot\nabla(w_{\ast,\delta}-u_{0})\,dx\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle+\delta\int_{\Omega}\big(|w_{\ast,\delta}|^{4\over N-2}w_{\ast,\delta}-|u_{0}|^{4\over N-2}u_{0}\big)T_{\rho}(w_{\ast,\delta}-u_{0})\,dx=-\delta\int_{\Omega}|u_{0}|^{4\over N-2}u_{0}T_{\rho}(w_{\ast,\delta}-u_{0})\,dx.\end{array}

Using Young’s inequality in the second term of the first integral and

\exists c_{N}>0:\quad c_{N}\big||x|+|y|\big|^{4\over N-2}|x-y|^{2}\leq\big(|x|^{4\over N-2}x-|y|^{4\over N-2}y\big)(x-y),\quad\forall\,x,y\in\mathbb{R},

in the second integral, we get

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{\alpha\over 2}\int_{\{|w_{\ast,\delta}-u_{0}|<\rho\}}\hskip-16.0pt|\nabla(w_{\ast,\delta}-u_{0})|^{2}dx+c_{N}\delta\int_{\Omega}\big(|w_{\ast,\delta}|+|u_{0}|\big)^{4\over N-2}|w_{\ast,\delta}-u_{0}||T_{\rho}(w_{\ast,\delta}-u_{0})|\,dx\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle\leq{1\over 2\alpha}\int_{\{|w_{\ast,\delta}-u_{0}|<\rho\}}\hskip-16.0pt|E|^{2}|w_{\ast,\delta}-u_{0}|^{2}dx+\delta\int_{\Omega}|u_{0}|^{N+2\over N-2}|T_{\rho}(w_{\ast,\delta}-u_{0})|\,dx.\end{array}

(3.17)

This proves that for every
\rho>0
,
T_{\rho}(w_{\ast,\delta}-u_{0})
is bounded in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. Dividing by
\rho
and taking the limit when
\rho
tends to zero, thanks to the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem, we get

\int_{\Omega}\big(|w_{\ast,\delta}|+|u_{0}|\big)^{4\over N-2}|w_{\ast,\delta}-u_{0}|dx\leq{1\over c_{N}}\int_{\Omega}|u_{0}|^{N+2\over N-2}dx,

and thus
w_{\ast,\delta}
is bounded in
L^{N+2\over N-2}(\Omega)
.
Hence, for a subsequence of
\delta
which converges to zero, still denoted by
\delta
, there exits
w_{\ast}
such that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle w_{\ast,\delta}\to w_{\ast}\ \hbox{ a.e. in }\Omega,\ \ w_{\ast,\delta}\rightharpoonup w_{\ast}\ \hbox{ in }L^{N+2\over N-2}(\Omega)\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle T_{\rho}(w_{\ast,\delta}-u_{0})\rightharpoonup T_{\rho}(w_{\ast}-u_{0})\hbox{ in }H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\ \forall\,\rho>0.\end{array}\right.

(3.18)

By the lower semicontinuity of the norm in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, this allows us to pass to the limit when
\delta
tends to zero in (
3.17
) to deduce

\int_{\{|w_{\ast}-u_{0}|<\rho\}}\hskip-10.0pt|\nabla(w_{\ast}-u_{0})|^{2}dx\leq{1\over\alpha^{2}}\int_{\{|w_{\ast}-u_{0}|<\rho\}}\hskip-10.0pt|E|^{2}|w_{\ast}-u_{0}|^{2}dx.

Dividing by
\rho^{2}
and taking the limit when
\rho
tends to zero, this proves

{1\over\rho}T_{\rho}(w_{\ast}-u_{0})\rightarrow 0\ \hbox{ in }H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\ \hbox{ when }\rho\to 0.

Combined with

{1\over\rho}T_{\rho}(w_{\ast}-u_{0})\rightharpoonup{\rm sgn}(w_{\ast}-u_{0})\ \hbox{ a.e. in }\Omega,

we get

w_{\ast}=u_{0}\ \hbox{ a.e. in }\Omega.

(3.19)

Step 4.
For
\rho>0
we take, similarly to the Step 3,
T_{\rho}(w_{n,\delta}-u_{n})
as test function in the difference of (
3.12
) and (
3.2
). We get

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{\alpha\over 2}\int_{\{|w_{n,\delta}-u_{n}|<\rho\}}\hskip-12.0pt|\nabla(w_{n,\delta}-u_{n})|^{2}dx+\delta\int_{\Omega}|w_{n,\delta}|^{4\over N-2}w_{n,\delta}T_{\rho}(w_{n,\delta}-u_{n})\,dx\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle\leq{1\over 2\alpha}\int_{\{|w_{n,\delta}-u_{n}|<\rho\}}\hskip-12.0pt|E_{n}|^{2}|w_{n,\delta}-u_{n}|^{2}dx.\end{array}

Taking into account (
3.11
) and (
3.15
), and defining
{\cal E}
by (it exists for a subsequence)

|E_{n}|^{2}\rightharpoonup{\cal E}\ \hbox{ in }L^{N\over 2}(\Omega),

we can pass to the limit in
n
in this inequality by semicontinuity and the Rellich-Kondrachov’s compactnes theorem to deduce

{\alpha\over 2}\int_{\{|w_{\ast,\delta}-u|<\rho\}}\hskip-12.0pt|\nabla(w_{\ast,\delta}-u)|^{2}dx+\delta\int_{\Omega}|w_{\ast,\delta}|^{4\over N-2}w_{\ast,\delta}T_{\rho}(w_{\ast,\delta}-u)\,dx\leq{1\over 2\alpha}\int_{\{|w_{\ast,\delta}-u|\leq\rho\}}\hskip-12.0pt{\cal E}|w_{\ast,\delta}-u|^{2}dx.

Now we pass to the limit when
\delta
tends to zero thanks to (
3.18
) and (
3.19
), to get

\int_{\{|u_{0}-u|<\rho\}}\hskip-10.0pt|\nabla(u_{0}-u)|^{2}dx\leq{1\over\alpha^{2}}\int_{\{|u_{0}-u|\leq\rho\}}\hskip-10.0pt{\cal E}|u_{0}-u|^{2}dx

Dividing by
\rho^{2}
and passing to the limit when
\rho
tends to zero we deduce as at the end of Step 3 that
u=u_{0}
. This finishes the proof.

Remark 3.2
.

One of the applications of Theorem
3.1
is the existence of solutions for some control problems in the coefficients. In this way, combined with Fatou’s Lemma it immediately proves the existence of solution for

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle\min\left\{\int_{\Omega}\big(G(x,u)+\mu|E|^{p}\big)dx:\ E\in L^{p}(\Omega)^{N}\right\}\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}-{\rm div}(A\nabla u+Eu)=f\ \hbox{ in }\Omega\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\displaystyle u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\end{array}\right.\end{array}

with
p>2
if
N=2
,
p\geq N
if
N>2
,
f\in H^{-1}(\Omega)
and
G:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

G(.,s)\ \hbox{ measurable, }\forall\,s\in\mathbb{R},\quad G(x,.)\hbox{ continuous for a.e. }x\in\Omega,

\exists a\in\mathbb{R},b\geq 0,\ \hbox{ such that }\ G(x,s)\geq a-b|s|,\quad\forall\,s\in\mathbb{R},\ \hbox{ a.e. }x\in\Omega.

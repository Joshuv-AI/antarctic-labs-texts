Abstract
. The Centre Symmetry Set of a planar curve M M is the envelope of affine chords of M M , i.e. the lines joining points on M M with parallel tangent lines. In this paper we study global geometrical properties of this set including the number of singularities and the number of asymptotes.

1.
Introduction

Let
M
be a smooth curve in an affine space
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Consider an affine chord of
M
, i.e. an infinite line joining pairs of points of
M
at which the tangent lines to
M
are parallel. Then, the Centre Symmetry Set of
M
(
\textsc{css}(M)
) is the envelope of all affine chords of
M
. Note that for a convex
M
centrally symmetric about a point
p
the Centre Symmetry Set degenerates to
p
. Therefore, the
css
measures in some way the central symmetric property of a curve. The origin of this investigation is the paper
[
24
]
by S. Janeczko. He described the Centre Symmetry Set by the bifurcation set of a certain family of ratios. Then, P.J. Giblin and P.A. Holtom in
[
17
]
redefined the
css
in the way stated above.
The various kinds of symmetries are a vital part of the study of manifolds in Euclidean spaces and in their geometry applications. The medial axis transform is one of the most popular examples of such applications (see
[
1
,
25
]
).

The Centre Symmetry Set of a curve can be viewed also as the set of all singular points of a family of affine
\lambda
-equidistants of the curve (see for instance
[
9
,
10
,
13
,
16
,
21
,
29
,
31
]
and the references given there. See also
[
5
,
7
]
for the Centre Symmetry Set in a non-smooth, polygonal case). Generically, the Centre Symmetry Set of a planar curve can admit at most cusp singularities. It was studied by many authors, also in higher dimensions, and in Minkowski spaces. For instance, authors in
[
20
,
21
,
22
,
26
]
analyse the local properties of the
css
based on the theory of Lagrange and Legendre singularities. In
[
18
,
19
]
the authors study the Centre Symmetry Set of families of plane curves and of families of surfaces. The
css
is a part of the Global Centre Symmetry Set studied in
[
12
]
. To see art created from the Centre Symmetry Set (and the Wigner caustic) see
[
8
]
. In the articles cited, the authors obtained properties of the
css
that are inherently local. One of the few global properties is the result in
[
17
]
that the number of cusps of
css
of a generic oval is odd and not smaller than
3.
The results in
[
2
,
12
]
show that the number of singularities of the Centre Symmetry Set is greater than the number of singularities of the Wigner caustic of a planar oval (in a generic situation). Moreover, it is known that the Wigner caustic is contained in a region bounded by the Centre Symmetry Set of an oval (
[
2
]
). In this article we will prove many global geometrical properties of the Centre Symmetry Set of a planar curve, not necessarily an oval. These theorems will concern the number of the so-called smooth branches of the
css
, the number of singular points, and the number of asymptotes of this set. Before we come to these theorems, in the next section we will precisely state the conditions for specific types of singularities and directly define the term of generic curves, used throughout the article (see Theorem
2.13
).

In Section
3
we adapt the decomposition of a curve into parallel arcs from
[
16
]
. This decomposition is necessary to prove some of the global properties of the Centre Symmetry Set of a planar curve in the last section.

2.
The Geometry of the Centre Symmetry Set

Let
C^{\infty}(X,Y)
denote the set of smooth
(C^{\infty})
maps between smooth manifolds
X
and
Y
with the smooth Whitney topology. A set in a topological space is
generic
if it is a countable intersection of open and dense sets. A
Baire space
is a topological space in which any generic set is dense. It is well known that
C^{\infty}(X,Y)
is a Baire space (e.g. see
[
23
]
).
Throughout the paper we will denote by
M
the image of a
smooth curve
(i.e. an element in
C^{\infty}(I,\mathbb{R}^{2})
, where
I\subset\mathbb{R}
is an open interval) on the affine plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}
or the smooth curve itself if this does not lead to ambiguity. If a smooth map is from
S^{1}
to
\mathbb{R}^{2},
we will call its image
closed
. A point of a curve is called
regular
if its velocity vector (the derivative) does not vanish. Otherwise – it is called
singular
. A curve without a singular point will be called
regular
. Otherwise –
singular
. A regular curve is
convex
if its curvature does not vanish at any point. The
rotation number
of a regular curve is the rotation number of its velocity vector field. An
n
-rosette
is a closed convex curve with
n
as its rotation number. A point
p
of a smooth regular curve is an
inflexion point
if its curvature changes sign at
p
, and is an
undulation point
if its curvature vanishes at
p
but does not change sign at
p
. If a regular curve
M
is parameterized by
f
, then an inflexion point
f(s_{0})
is
non-degenerate
if
\det\left(f^{\prime}(s_{0}),f^{\prime\prime\prime}(s_{0})\right)\neq 0
. A singular point is a
cusp
if its locally diffeomorphic (in the source and in the target) to the map
t\mapsto(t^{2},t^{3})
at
t=0
. It is well known (e.g. see Theorem B.9.1 in
[
27
]
) that a map
f
is a cusp at
s_{0}
if and only if
f^{\prime}(s_{0})=0
and
\det\big(f^{\prime\prime}(s_{0}),f^{\prime\prime\prime}(s_{0})\big)\neq 0.

We will denote by
\kappa_{f}
or
\kappa_{M}
the signed curvature of a curve
M
parameterized by
f.

Definition 2.1
.

A pair of points
a,b\in M

(a\neq b)
is called a
parallel pair
if the lines tangent to
M
at
a
and
b
are parallel.

Definition 2.2
.

A
chord
passing through a pair
a,b\in M,
is the line:

(2.1)

\ell(a,b)=\{\lambda a+(1-\lambda)b\ |\ \lambda\in\mathbb{R}\}.

If
a,b
is a parallel pair of
M,
then
\ell(a,b)
is called an
affine chord
.

Definition 2.3
.

The
Centre Symmetry Set
of
M,
denoted by
\textsc{css}(M),
is the envelope of all affine chords of
M.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. An affine
\lambda
equidistant of
M
is the following set

{\text{E}}_{\lambda}(M):=\left\{\lambda a+(1-\lambda)b\,\big|\,a,b\text{ is a parallel pair of }M\right\}.

The set
{\text{E}}_{0.5}(M)
is also known as the
Wigner caustic
of
M
.

The Centre Symmetry Set of
M
can be also viewed as the set of all singular points in the family of affine
\lambda
-equidistants for
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
(
[
21
,
22
]
). There are many articles related with the geometry of singularities of affine equidistants – see
[
9
,
13
,
29
,
30
,
31
]
and their references. In particular, the geometry of the Wigner caustic is strictly related with isoperimetric problems (
[
28
]
–
[
31
]
) and with the construction of improper affine spheres (
[
3
,
4
,
6
]
).

Definition 2.5
.

Let
f
be a regular parameterization of a curve
M.
We say that
M
is
parameterized at opposite directions
at points
f(s_{1})
and
f(s_{2})
if
f^{\prime}(s_{1})=\alpha f^{\prime}(s_{2}),
where
\alpha<0
.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
M
be a regular closed curve.
A pair of points
a,b\in M
is called
standard
if
a,

b
is a parallel pair, such that
M
is parameterized at
a
and
b
at opposite directions and
\kappa_{M}(b)\neq 0.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
M
be a regular closed curve.
A line that is tangent to
M
at two distinct points is called a
double tangent
.

In this paper a singular point of the Centre Symmetry Set will be understood as a singular point of its so called
natural parameterization
. This parameterization will be given locally and we will describe it in Subsection
2.1
.

2.1.
Local Parameterization of
\textsc{css}(M)

Let
a,b\in M
be a standard pair of points.
Let
f(s)
and
g(t)
be the local arc length parameterizations of
M
at
a
and
b,
respectively.

Since
a
and
b
are a parallel pair and
f
and
g
are parameterized at opposite directions, we have that
f^{\prime}(s)=-g^{\prime}(t),
where
′
signifies the derivative with respect to the corresponding parameter
t
or
s.
By the implicit function theorem one can show that there exists a smooth function
t(s)
such that
f^{\prime}(s)=-g^{\prime}(t(s))
and

(2.2)

t^{\prime}(s)=\dfrac{\kappa_{f}(s)}{\kappa_{g}(t(s))}.

Using this and the fact that the Centre Symmetry Set is an envelope of the following family of lines:

(2.3)

\ell_{s}(u)=f(s)+u(g(t(s))-f(s)),

one can get that double tangents are part of the
css
and the other part can be locally parameterized as follows:

(2.4)

\displaystyle\gamma_{\textsc{css}}(s):=\ell_{s}\left(\frac{\kappa_{g}(t(s))}{\kappa_{f}(s)+\kappa_{g}(t(s))}\right)=\dfrac{\kappa_{f}(s)\cdot f(s)+\kappa_{g}(t(s))\cdot g(t(s))}{\kappa_{f}(s)+\kappa_{g}(t(s))}

whenever
\kappa_{f}(s)+\kappa_{g}(t(s))\neq 0.

Remark 2.8
.

In the case when
\kappa_{f}(s)+\kappa_{g}(t(s))=0,
the affine chord through
f(s)
and
g(t(s))
is an asymptote of the
\textsc{css}.

Remark 2.9
.

In the neighbourhood of every inflexion point
p_{i}
there is a sequence of parallel pairs, in which both points converge to the inflexion point
p_{i}.
In other words, for each member
(a_{k},b_{k})
of the sequence,
a_{k}\to p_{i}
and
b_{k}\to p_{i}.
This observation leads to the fact that every ordinary inflexion point is the beginning of a component of
\textsc{css}(M)
(
[
17
]
). This component of
\textsc{css}(M)
we will call
a
css
on shell
(similarly to the notion of the Wigner caustic on shell – see
[
10
,
16
]
).

Using the parameterization (
2.4
), by direct calculations one can obtain the following lemma.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
a,b\in M
be a standard pair of points.
The set
\textsc{css}(M)
has a singular point at
\frac{\kappa_{M}(a)\cdot a+\kappa_{M}(b)\cdot b}{\kappa_{M}(a)+\kappa_{M}(b)}
if and only if
\left(\frac{\kappa_{M}(a)}{\kappa_{M}(b)}\right)^{\prime}=0
(which is equivalent to
\kappa_{M}^{\prime}(a)\kappa_{M}^{2}(b)=\kappa_{M}^{\prime}(b)\kappa_{M}^{2}(a)
), where
′
signifies the derivative with respect to the arc parameter of the curve. The singular point
p
of
\textsc{css}(M)
is a cusp if and only if
\det\left(f(a)-f(b),f^{\prime}(b)\right)\neq 0
and
\left(\frac{\kappa_{M}(a)}{\kappa_{M}(b)}\right)^{\prime\prime}\neq 0
(which is equivalent to
\kappa_{M}^{\prime\prime}(a)\kappa_{M}^{3}(b)\neq\kappa_{M}^{\prime\prime}(b)\kappa_{M}^{3}(a)
).

The above lemma and Corollary
2.12
was also obtained in
[
17
]
using a slightly different approach.

Directly by the local parameterization of the Centre Symmetry Set we obtain the following formula for the curvature function of the
\textsc{css}.

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
a,b\in M
be a standard pair of points and let
q=\frac{\kappa_{M}(a)\cdot a+\kappa_{M}(b)\cdot b}{\kappa_{M}(a)+\kappa_{M}(b)}
be a regular point of
\textsc{css}(M).

Then the curvature of
\textsc{css}(M)
at
q
is expressed by the formula

(2.5)

\kappa_{\textsc{css}(M)}(q)=\mathrm{sgn}(\kappa_{M}(b))\cdot\dfrac{\big(\kappa_{M}(a)+\kappa_{M}(b)\big)^{3}}{\left|\kappa^{2}_{M}(b)\kappa^{\prime}_{M}(a)-\kappa^{2}_{M}(a)\kappa^{\prime}_{M}(b)\right|}\cdot\dfrac{\det(a-b,\mathbbm{t}(a))}{|a-b|^{3}},

where
\mathbbm{t}(a)
is a tangent unit vector field compatible with the orientation of
M
at
a
and
′
signifies the derivative with respect to the arc parameter of a curve.

Corollary 2.12
.

Let
a,b\in M
be a standard pair of points. Any curve contained in
\textsc{css}(M)
has curvature equal to
0
at point
p
if and only if
p
lies on a double tangent.

When two asymptotes of a curve are covering each other, which in fact means that they are the same line, this line is called a
double asymptote
.

Before we describe known local properties of the Centre Symmetry Set, we will define a generic set
\mathcal{G}
in
C^{\infty}(S^{1},\mathbb{R}^{2})
.
Whenever in the paper we say that the curve
f
is generic we mean that
f
belongs to the set described in the following theorem.

Theorem 2.13
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be the subset of
C^{\infty}(S^{1},\mathbb{R}^{2})
such that any
f\in\mathcal{G}
satisfies the following properties:

(i)

The curve
f
is a regular curve with only transversal self-crossings, with only non-degenerate inflexion points and with no undulation points.

(ii)

If
f(s_{1}),f(s_{2})
is a parallel pair, then at least one of
f(s_{1})
,
f(s_{2})
is not an inflexion point.

(iii)

Let
f(s_{1}),f(s_{2})
be a parallel pair. If
\det\big(f(s_{1})-f(s_{2}),f^{\prime}(s_{1})\big)=0
, then
\kappa_{f}(s_{1})\kappa_{f}(s_{2})\neq 0
.

(iv)

Let
f(s_{1}),f(s_{2})
be a parallel pair. If
\kappa_{f}^{\prime}(s_{1})\kappa_{g}^{2}(s_{2})=\kappa_{f}^{2}(s_{1})\kappa^{\prime}_{g}(s_{2})
, then
\kappa_{f}^{\prime\prime}(s_{1})\kappa_{g}^{3}(s_{2})\neq\kappa_{f}^{3}(s_{1})\kappa^{\prime\prime}_{g}(s_{2})
.

(v)

If
f(s_{1}),f(s_{2})
is a parallel pair and
\kappa_{f}(s_{1})-\operatorname{sgn}\left(\left<f^{\prime}(s_{1}),f^{\prime}(s_{2})\right>\right)\kappa_{f}(s_{2})=0.
Then
\kappa_{f}^{\prime}(s_{1})\kappa_{g}^{2}(s_{2})\neq\kappa_{f}^{2}(s_{1})\kappa^{\prime}_{g}(s_{2})
.

(vi)

If
f(s_{1}),f(s_{2})
is a parallel pair, then the conditions
\kappa_{f}^{\prime}(s_{1})\kappa_{g}^{2}(s_{2})=\kappa_{f}^{2}(s_{1})\kappa^{\prime}_{g}(s_{2})
and
\det\big(f(s_{1})-f(s_{2}),f^{\prime}(s_{1})\big)=0
do not occur simultaneously.

(vii)

If
f(s_{1}),f(s_{2})
and
f(s_{3}),f(s_{4})
are two distinct parallel pairs which have the same affine chord, then
\kappa_{f}(s_{1})-\operatorname{sgn}\left(\left<f^{\prime}(s_{1}),f^{\prime}(s_{2})\right>\right)\kappa_{f}(s_{2})\neq 0
or
\kappa_{f}(s_{3})-\operatorname{sgn}\left(\left<f^{\prime}(s_{3}),f^{\prime}(s_{4})\right>\right)\kappa_{f}(s_{4})\neq 0
.

(viii)

If
f(s_{1}),f(s_{2})
and
f(s_{3}),f(s_{4})
are two distinct parallel pairs such that
\kappa_{f}(s_{1})-\operatorname{sgn}\left(\left<f^{\prime}(s_{1}),f^{\prime}(s_{2})\right>\right)\kappa_{f}(s_{2})\neq 0,

\kappa_{f}(s_{3})-\operatorname{sgn}\left(\left<f^{\prime}(s_{3}),f^{\prime}(s_{4})\right>\right)\kappa_{f}(s_{4})\neq 0,
and

\frac{\kappa_{f}(s_{1})f(s_{1})-\operatorname{sgn}\left(\left<f^{\prime}(s_{1}),f^{\prime}(s_{2})\right>\right)\kappa_{f}(s_{2})f(s_{2})}{\kappa_{f}(s_{1})-\operatorname{sgn}\left(\left<f^{\prime}(s_{1}),f^{\prime}(s_{2})\right>\right)\kappa_{f}(s_{2})}=\frac{\kappa_{f}(s_{3})f(s_{3})-\operatorname{sgn}\left(\left<f^{\prime}(s_{3}),f^{\prime}(s_{4})\right>\right)\kappa_{f}(s_{4})f(s_{4})}{\kappa_{f}(s_{3})-\operatorname{sgn}\left(\left<f^{\prime}(s_{3}),f^{\prime}(s_{4})\right>\right)\kappa_{f}(s_{4})},

then

\det\left(f(s_{2})-f(s_{1}),f(s_{4})-f(s_{3})\right)\neq 0,

\kappa_{f}^{\prime}(s_{1})\kappa_{f}^{2}(s_{2})\neq\kappa_{f}^{\prime}(s_{2})\kappa_{f}^{2}(s_{1}),

and

\kappa_{f}^{\prime}(s_{3})\kappa_{f}^{2}(s_{4})\neq\kappa_{f}^{\prime}(s_{4})\kappa_{f}^{2}(s_{3}).

In the above properties
′
denote the derivative with respect to the arc length parameter.

Then the set
\mathcal{G}
is a generic subset of
C^{\infty}(S^{1},\mathbb{R}^{2})
.

Furthermore, if
f\in\mathcal{G},
then the Centre Symmetry Set of
f
has the following properties.

(a)

It is the union of smooth curves with at most cusp singularities.

(b)

It has only transversal self-crossings.

(c)

It has inflexion points only on double tangents of
f
.

(d)

No asymptote of the
css
is a double asymptote.

(e)

In a neighbourhood of an asymptote the curvature function of the
css
changes sign.

Proof.

We will show that each property is generic by the Thom Transversality Theorems – the one for maps and the one for multijets (see Theorems 4.9 and 4.13 in
[
23
]
). We will denote by
J^{k}_{s}(S^{1},\mathbb{R}^{2})
the
s
-fold
k
-jet bundle
and let
(S^{1})^{(k)}
denote the set
\left(S^{1}\right)^{\!k}\,\setminus\,\left\{(s_{i})_{i=1}^{k}\ |\ s_{i}\neq s_{j}\text{ if }i\neq j\right\}
.

The genericity of properties (i), (ii), and (v) are proved in
[
11
]
(see Theorem 2.12) using Thoms Transversality Theorems, too.

We will show that Property (iii) is generic. Let
f\in C^{\infty}(S^{1},\mathbb{R}^{2})
be regular. It is easy to calculate that the transversality of the map
j^{2}_{2}f:\left(S^{1}\right)^{(2)}\to J_{2}^{2}(S^{1},\mathbb{R}^{2})
to the submanifold

\displaystyle\Big\{\left(j^{2}g(s_{1}),j^{2}h(s_{2})\right)\in J^{2}_{2}(S^{1},\mathbb{R}^{2})\ \Big|\

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(s_{1})\neq 0,h^{\prime}(s_{2})\neq 0,\det\left(g^{\prime}(s_{1}),h^{\prime}(s_{2})\right)=0,

\displaystyle\det\left(g(s_{1})-h(s_{2}),g^{\prime}(s_{1})\right)=0\Big\}

is equivalent to
\kappa_{g}(s_{1})\kappa_{h}(s_{2})\neq 0
. Therefore, Property (iii) is generic by the Thom Transversality Theorem. In a similar way one can prove genericity of Properties (iv), (v), and (vii).

Now, let us deal with genericity of Property (vi). It directly follows from the Thom Transversality Theorem and the transversality of the map
j^{4}_{2}f:(S^{1})^{(2)}\to J^{4}_{2}(S^{1},\mathbb{R}^{2})
to the submanifold

\displaystyle\Big\{\left(j^{4}g(s_{1}),j^{4}h(s_{2})\right)\in J^{4}(S^{1},\mathbb{R}^{2})\,\Big|\,g^{\prime}(s_{1})\neq 0,h^{\prime}(s_{2})\neq 0,\det\left(g^{\prime}(s_{1}),h^{\prime}(s_{2})\right)=0,

\displaystyle\kappa_{f}^{\prime}(s_{1})\kappa_{g}^{2}(s_{2})-\kappa_{g}^{\prime}(s_{2})\kappa_{f}^{2}(s_{1})=0\}.

The set

\displaystyle S_{\text{vi}}:=\Big\{\left(j^{3}g(s_{1}),j^{3}h(s_{2})\right)\in J^{3}(S^{1},\mathbb{R}^{2})\,\Big|\,g^{\prime}(s_{1})\neq 0,h^{\prime}(s_{2})\neq 0,\det\left(g^{\prime}(s_{1}),h^{\prime}(s_{2})\right)=0,

\displaystyle\kappa_{f}^{\prime}(s_{1})\kappa_{g}^{2}(s_{2})-\kappa_{g}^{\prime}(s_{2})\kappa_{f}^{2}(s_{1})=0,\det\left(g(s_{1})-h(s_{2}),g^{\prime}(s_{1})\right)=0\Big\}.

is a submanifold of codimension
3
in
J^{3}(S^{1},\mathbb{R}^{2})
. If
j^{3}f
is transversal to
S_{\text{vi}}
, then
j^{3}f
does not intersect
S_{\text{iv}}
.

Finally, genericity of Property (vii) follows from the Thom Transversality Theorem and the transversality of the map
j_{4}^{1}f:\left(S^{1}\right)^{(4)}\to J_{4}^{1}\left(S^{1},\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)
to the following submanifold:

\displaystyle\Big\{

\displaystyle\left(j^{1}g_{k}(s_{k})\right)_{k=1}^{4}\in J_{4}^{1}\left(S^{1},\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)\,\Big|\,g_{k}^{\prime}(s_{k})\neq 0\text{ for }k=1,2,3,4,

\displaystyle\det\left(g_{1}^{\prime}(s_{1}),g_{2}^{\prime}(s_{2})\right)=0,\det\left(g_{3}^{\prime}(s_{3}),g_{4}^{\prime}(s_{4})\right)=0,

\displaystyle\det\left(g_{2}(s_{2})-g_{1}(s_{1}),g_{3}(s_{3})-g_{1}(s_{1})\right)=0,\det\left(g_{2}(s_{2})-g_{1}(s_{1}),g_{4}(s_{4})-g_{1}(s_{1})\right)=0\Big\}.

In a similar way we can prove genericity of the last property.

Now let’s assume that
f
is in the generic set
\mathcal{G}
satisfying properties (i-viii).

Note that Property (a) follows From Properties (i-iv) – see the local parameterization of the Centre Symmetry Set (we need to have at least one non-inflexion point in each parallel pair) and Lemma
2.10
. Property (b) is true by virtue of Property (viii). Then, Property (c) follows directly from Property (vi) and Proposition
2.11
, and the property (d) from Property (vii). Finally, by Property (v) we obtain Property (e), which ends the proof.

Proposition 2.14

(Proposition 2.13 in
[
11
]
)
.

Let
M
be a smooth regular closed curve. If
M
has only non-degenerate inflexion points and no undulation points, then the rotation number of
M
and the number of inflexion points of
f
are finite.

Remark 2.15
.

Since generic curves satisfy the assumptions of Proposition
2.14
, the rotation number and the number of inflexion points of each generic curve are finite.

3.
A Decomposition of a Curve into Parallel Arcs

Let’s assume that
M
is a generic regular closed curve. In this section we will adapt an algorithm of decomposition of
M
into parallel arcs (for further details see Section
3
in
[
16
]
) that will help us to study the geometry of Centre Symmetry Set of
M
.

We already know that if
M
is a generic regular closed curve, then
\textsc{css}(M)
is a union of smooth parameterized curves and double tangents.
Each of the smooth parameterized curves belonging to
\textsc{css}(M)
will be called an
smooth semi-branch
of
\textsc{css}(M)
(see Figure
1
).

Figure 1
.
A curve
M
on the left, and
M
together with
4
smooth semi-branches of
\textsc{css}(M)
on the right

Notice that some of the smooth semi-branches might have asymptotes.
Each of the asymptotes is either a bi-asymptote of one smooth semi-branch or a single asymptote of two different smooth semi-branches.
When we use the term
bi-asymptote
, we mean that there are exactly two parts of the curve approaching an asymptote.

Smooth semi-branches that have the same asymptote we identify into one smooth semi-branch.
Since
M
is generic, it follows that we have a finite number of smooth semi-branches.
After all possible identifications are done, the elements of the resulting set of smooth semi-branches will be called
smooth branches
of
\textsc{css}(M)
.

In Figure
1
we illustrate a non-convex curve
M
,
\textsc{css}(M)
, and two different smooth branches of
\textsc{css}(M)
(one smooth branch is created from the left and right smooth semi-branch, the other smooth branch is created from the bottom and top smooth semi-branch).

Informally, we can say that asymptotes connect almost-smooth branches into smooth branches.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
S^{1}\ni s\mapsto f(s)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a parameterization of a smooth closed curve
M
, such that
f(s_{0})
is not an inflexion point. A function
\varphi_{M}:S^{1}\to[0,\pi]
is called an
angle function of

M
if

Let

Proof.

The secant caustic of

The glueing scheme presented in Section

produces not only smooth branches of

Recall that the image of every maximal glueing scheme of

The

Then, the image of every maximal glueing scheme of

We will now consider three possible versions of maximal glueing schemes whose images are smooth branches of

\begin{array}[]{ccccccc}a&\frown&\tilde{a}&\frown&\ldots&\frown&a\\

If the maximal glueing scheme of

Similarly to the previous case, if the maximal glueing scheme of

\begin{array}[]{ccccccc}a&\frown&\tilde{a}&\frown&\ldots&\frown&b\\

In such case the number of asymptotes of
\textsc{css}(M)

A cusp singularity parameterized by

is a unit continuous normal vector field to

Let

Let us fix a point

Theorem 4.18

be a generic

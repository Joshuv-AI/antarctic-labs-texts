3.
Modular Curves and Modular Functions

3.1.
Congruence subgroups

Let
\Gamma
be a congruence subgroup of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}}).
The level of
\Gamma
is the smallest positive integer
N
for which
\Gamma
contains

\Gamma(N):=\{A\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})~|~A\equiv I\pmod{N}\}.

The group
\operatorname{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})
acts on the complex upper half plane
{\mathcal{H}}
by linear fractional transformations.

We define the extended upper half plane
{\mathcal{H}}^{*}
as
{\mathcal{H}}\cup{\mathbb{Q}}\cup\{\infty\}.
The action of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})
on
{\mathcal{H}}
naturally extends to the action on
{\mathcal{H}}^{*}.
A
cusp
of
\Gamma
is a
\Gamma
-orbit of
{\mathbb{Q}}\cup\{\infty\}.
After adding cusps to the quotient
\Gamma\setminus{\mathcal{H}}
, we get a smooth compact Riemann surface which we denote by
X(\Gamma).
We will use the notation
X_{N}
to denote
X(\Gamma(N))
, where
N
is a positive integer.

3.2.
Modular functions

Let
\Gamma
be a congruence subgroup of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}}).
A
modular function

f
for
\Gamma
is a meromorphic function of
X(\Gamma)
i.e.,
f
is a meromorphic function on the upper half plane
{\mathcal{H}}
that satisfies
f(\gamma\tau)=f(\tau)
for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
and is meromorphic at the cusps.

The
width
of the cusp at
\infty
is the smallest positive integer
w
such that
(\begin{smallmatrix}1&w\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix})\in\Gamma.
Define
q:=e^{2\pi\dot{\iota}\tau}
and
q^{1/w}:=e^{2\pi\dot{\iota}\tau/w}
, where
\tau\in{\mathcal{H}}.
If
f
is a modular function for
\Gamma
, then
f
has a unique
q
-expansion
f(\tau)=\sum\limits_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}c_{n}q^{n/w}
with
c_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
and
c_{n}=0
for all but finitely many
n
less than
0.

Fix a positive integer
N.
Let
{\mathcal{F}}_{N}
denote the field of meromorphic functions of the Riemann surface
X_{N}
whose
q
-expansion
has coefficients in
K_{N}
. For
N=1
, we have
{\mathcal{F}}_{1}={\mathbb{Q}}(j)
where
j
is the modular
j
-invariant. The first few terms of
q
-expansion of
j
are
q^{-1}+744+196884q+21493760q^{2}+\dotsb.

If
N^{\prime}
is a divisor of
N
, then
{\mathcal{F}}_{N^{\prime}}\subseteq{\mathcal{F}}_{N}.
In particular, we have that
{\mathcal{F}}_{1}\subseteq{\mathcal{F}}_{N}.

Take
g\in\operatorname{GL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})
and let
d=\det(g).
Then,
g=(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&d\end{smallmatrix})g^{\prime}
for some
g^{\prime}\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})
. There is a unique right action
*
of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})
on
{\mathcal{F}}_{N}
that satisfies the following properties (see Chapter
2
, section
2
in
[
MR648603
]
for details):

•

If
g\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})
, then

f*g:=f|_{\gamma},

where
\gamma\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})
is congruent to
g
modulo
N
and
f|_{\gamma}(\tau)=f(\gamma\tau)
.

•

If
g=(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&d\end{smallmatrix})
, then

f*g:=\sigma_{d}(f):=\sum\limits_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\sigma_{d}(c_{n})q^{n/w},

where
f=\sum\limits_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}c_{n}q^{n/w}
and
\sigma_{d}
is the automorphism of
K_{N}
that satisfies
\sigma_{d}(\zeta_{N})=\zeta_{N}^{d}.

Proposition 3.1
.

The following properties hold.

(i)

-I
acts trivially on
{\mathcal{F}}_{N}
and the action
*
of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})/\{\pm I\}
on
\mathcal{F}_{N}
is faithful.

(ii)

We have
{\mathcal{F}}_{N}^{\operatorname{GL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})/\{\pm I\}}={\mathbb{Q}}(j).

(iii)

The extension

over

is the opposite group of

(i.e., the same underlying set with the group law reversed.)

(iv)

The algebraic closure of

in

is

. We have
\mathcal{F}_{N}^{\operatorname{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})/\{\pm I\}}=K_{N}(j).

Refer to Theorem

, Chapter

in

MR1291394
]

3.3.
Modular Curves

Let

be an open subgroup of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}({\hat{{\mathbb{Z}}}})
satisfying

and

Recall that the level of

is the inverse image of its image under the reduction modulo

Let

be the level of

Let

fixed under the action of

Let

It will follow that

is algebraically closed in

by

, (
iii
). The field

has transcendence degree

over

since it is a finite extension of

.

Definition 3.2
.

The

is the smooth compact curve defined over

whose function field is

The map

Let

be a scheme over
{\mathbb{Z}}[1/N].
One could also define

as the compactification of the coarse moduli scheme

defined over

that parametrises elliptic curves

defined over

with

-level structure. (refer to
[
MR0337993
]
chapter

, section 3 for details).

Let
\Gamma
be a congruence subgroup of level
N
that contains
-I.
Let
\Gamma_{N}\subseteq\operatorname{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})
be the image of
\Gamma
under reduction modulo

Definition 3.3
.

The algebraic curve
X_{\Gamma}
is the nice curve over
K_{N}
whose function field is
{\mathcal{F}}_{N}^{\Gamma_{N}}.
The map
\pi_{\Gamma}\colon X_{\Gamma}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{K_{N}}
is the morphism corresponding to the inclusion

Using the inclusion

, we can identify

with the Riemann surface

Using

3.1
, (

which is the function field of the base extension of

to

is isomorphic to the function field

, where

is conjugate to the congruence subgroup

Equivalently, there exists an isomorphism

4.
Twists

4.1.
Background and preliminaries

For background in Galois cohomology see section 5.1, Chapter 1 of
[
MR1466966
]
. We recall the definitions and concepts that we need for reading this article.

Let
G
be a topological group and
A
be a
G
-group i.e.,
A
is a topological group with the discrete topology, with a continuous
G
-action that respects the group law of
A
. We will assume that
G\times G
has product topology.

Definition 4.1
.

•

A
cocycle

\zeta\colon G\to A
, is a continuous function satisfying the
cocycle property
, i.e.,
\zeta(\sigma\tau)=\zeta(\sigma)\cdot\sigma(\zeta(\tau))
for every
\sigma,\tau\in G.
We say that two cocycles
\zeta_{1}
and
\zeta_{2}
are
cohomologous
if there is an
a\in A
such that
\zeta_{1}(\sigma)=a\cdot\zeta_{2}(\sigma)\cdot\sigma(a^{-1})
for every
\sigma\in G.

•

A
2-cocycle

\mu\colon G\times G\to A
, is a continuous function satisfying the property

\sigma_{1}(\mu(\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3}))\cdot\mu(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\sigma_{3})=\mu(\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3})\cdot\mu(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2})

for every
\sigma_{i}\in G
and
i\in\{1,2,3\}.
We say that a
2
-cocycle
\mu
is a
2
-coboundary if there exists a function
\alpha\colon G\to A
such that
\mu(\sigma,\tau)=\alpha(\sigma)\cdot\sigma(\alpha(\tau))\cdot\alpha(\sigma\tau)^{-1}
for every
\sigma,\tau\in G.

It is easy to check that being cohomologous is an equivalence relation. We will denote the set of cocycles modulo this relation by
H^{1}(G,A).
Let
e\colon G\to A
be the cocycle which sends
g
to
1
for every
g\in G
, where
1
is the identity element of
A.
We say that a cocycle
\zeta\colon G\to A
is a
coboundary
if
\zeta
is cohomologous to
e.

Fix a number field
K.
Fix a nice curve
X
over
K
with a nonconstant morphism
\pi_{X}\colon X\to\mathbb{P}^{1}_{K}.
Let
L
be a Galois extension of
K.

Definition 4.2
.

A
L
-twist
of
(X,\pi_{X})
is a pair
(Y,\pi_{Y})
where
Y
is a curve over
K
and
\pi_{Y}
is a morphism
Y\to\mathbb{P}^{1}_{K}
such that there exists an isomorphism
f\colon X_{L}\to Y_{L}
which satisfies
\pi_{Y}\circ f=\pi_{X}.
We say that
(X,\pi_{X})
and
(Y,\pi_{Y})
are
K
-isomorphic
if
(Y,\pi_{Y})
is a
K
-twist of
(X,\pi_{X}).

Let
\Aut_{L}(X,\pi_{X})
be the subgroup of
\Aut(X_{L})
consisting of
f
such that
\pi_{X}\circ f=\pi_{X}.
Since
\pi_{X}
is nonconstant,
\Aut_{L}(X,\pi_{X})
is a finite
\Gal(L/K)
-group.

We will now define a map
\theta
between the set of
L
-twists of
(X,\pi_{X})
up to
K
-isomorphism and
H^{1}(\Gal(L/K),\Aut_{L}(X,\pi_{X}))
.

Let
(Y,\pi_{Y})
be a
L
-twist of
(X,\pi_{X}).
So, there is an isomorphism
f\colon X_{L}\to Y_{L}
such that
\pi_{Y}\circ f=\pi_{X}.
The map
\xi\colon\Gal(L/K)\to\Aut_{L}(X,\pi_{X})
defined as
\xi(\sigma)=f^{-1}\circ\sigma(f)
is a cocycle. Using that
\pi_{Y}
and
\pi_{X}
are defined over
K
, we get that
\pi_{X}\circ\xi(\sigma)=\pi_{X}
for every
\sigma\in\Gal(L/K).
We define
\theta((Y,\pi_{Y}))=[\xi]\in H^{1}(\Gal(L/K),\Aut_{L}(X,\pi_{X})).

Proposition 4.3
.

The map
\theta
defined above is well-defined and is a bijection between the set of
L
-twists of
(X,\pi_{X})
up to
K
-isomorphism and
H^{1}(\Gal(L/K),\Aut_{L}(X,\pi_{X})).

Proof.

Proof is similar to as given in Proposition
5.3
of
[
Rakvi21
]
.

4.2.
Determining if a cocycle is a coboundary

In this section, we describe a method that we use to determine whether a cocycle

\zeta\colon\Gal(L/K)\to\operatorname{PGL}_{2}(L)

is a coboundary or not. If it is a coboundary
\Gal(L/K)
is finite and cyclic, then we also explain how to compute a matrix
A\in\operatorname{PGL}_{2}(L)
such that
\zeta(\sigma)=A^{-1}\sigma(A)
for every
\sigma\in\Gal(L/K).

Let

\zeta\colon\Gal(L/K)\to\operatorname{PGL}_{2}(L)

be a cocycle. The cocycle
\zeta
determines a twist
C
of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{K}
up to isomorphism. In this section, we explain how to explicitly compute
C
as a conic in
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}_{K}.
The Hasse principle can then be used to determine if
C(K)
is empty or not. If
C(K)
is nonempty, then it is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{K}
and equivalently
\zeta
is a coboundary. When
\zeta
is a coboundary, we will explain how to compute an
A\in\operatorname{PGL}_{2}(L)
such that
\zeta(\sigma)=A^{-1}\sigma(A)
for every
\sigma\in\Gal(L/K).

Let
Q_{0}
be the quadratic form
y^{2}-xz.
Let
C_{0}
be the conic defined by
Q_{0}=0
in
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{K}
.

Let
\phi\colon\operatorname{PGL}_{2}(L)\to\operatorname{GL}_{3}(L)
be the map

[(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix})]\mapsto\frac{1}{ad-bc}\bigg(\begin{smallmatrix}a^{2}&2ab&b^{2}\\
ac&ad+bc&bd\\
c^{2}&2cd&d^{2}\end{smallmatrix}\bigg).

The map
\phi
comes from remark 3.3 in
[
MR3906177
]
. It is an injective group homomorphism that respects the
\Gal(L/K)
-action. The image of
\phi
gives automorphisms of
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}_{L}
that stabilizes
(C_{0})_{L}.
The map
\phi
induces an isomorphism
\bar{\phi}:\Aut({\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{L})=\operatorname{PGL}_{2}(L)\to\Aut((C_{0})_{L})
that respects the
\Gal(L/K)
- action. One can check that
\bar{\phi}
is induced by the isomorphism
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{K}\to C_{0}
that sends
[x:y]
to
[x^{2}:xy:y^{2}].

Define the map

\bar{\zeta}:=\phi\circ\zeta\colon\Gal(L/K)\to\operatorname{GL}_{3}(L).

Since
\phi
is a group homomorphism and respects the
\Gal(L/K)
-action,
\bar{\zeta}:\Gal(L/K)\to\operatorname{GL}_{3}(L)
is a cocycle. By Hilbert 90, there is an
M\in\operatorname{GL}_{3}(L)
such that
\bar{\zeta}(\sigma)=M^{-1}\sigma(M)
for every
\sigma\in\Gal(L/K).
We explain how to compute
M
in
section

4.3
.

Lemma 4.6

Since the cocycle

Suppose that the conic

The cocycle
\zeta\colon\Gal(L/K)\to\operatorname{PGL}_{2}(L)

\zeta

Assume that

, where

is some element of

Then we can check that

Therefore,

is a

Now assume that
\mu
is
2
-coboundary, i.e., there exists a function
\alpha\colon\Gal(L/K)\to L^{\times}
such that
\mu(\sigma,\tau)=\alpha(\sigma)(\sigma(\alpha(\tau)))\alpha(\sigma\tau)^{-1}.
Define

Using the definition of

for every
\sigma\in\Gal(L/K).
So, it follows that

∎

We know that

is a coboundary if and only if there exists a map

We are interested in computing the function

and the following Corollary

from

]

Proposition 4.8
.

Suppose

is a finite cyclic group of order

generated by

is a

f(\sigma^{i}):=(\mu(\sigma^{i},\sigma^{n}))(\prod_{j=0}^{i-1}((\mu(\sigma^{j},\sigma)(\sigma^{j}.a^{\prime})))^{-1}

for every

Here

(\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\mu(\sigma^{j},\sigma))^{-1}.

After we compute

We can check that

4.3.
Computing Hilbert 90 matrices

Let
K
be a number field and let

be a finite Galois extension of

and define
G:=\Gal(L/K)
. Hilbert 90 states that
H^{1}(G,\operatorname{GL}_{n}(L))=1.
Equivalently, given a cocycle
\psi\colon G\to\operatorname{GL}_{n}(L)
there exists a matrix
A\in\operatorname{GL}_{n}(L)
such that
\psi(\sigma)=A^{-1}\sigma(A)
for every
\sigma\in G.
We now describe how to find
A
.

•

Define the
L
-vector space
V:=L^{n}.
Define a new
G
-action on
V
by
\sigma*v=\psi(\sigma)\sigma(v)
; it acts
K
-linearly. Let
W
be the
K
-subspace of
V
fixed by this
G
-action. Using Proposition
7(A.V.63)
of
[
MR1994218
]
, the natural map

of

-vector spaces is an isomorphism. In particular,

has dimension
n
over
K
.

•

Fix a basis
{\mathfrak{A}}
for

considered as a vector space over

and a basis
{\mathfrak{B}}
for

considered as a vector space over
L
. The set
B=\{b.v~|~b\in{\mathfrak{A}},v\in{\mathfrak{B}}\}
is a basis of

over

•

The

-linear map

that sends

to
1/|G|(\Sigma_{g\in G}g*v)
is a projection. Therefore, the set
C=\{1/|G|(\Sigma_{g\in G}g*u):u\in B\}
spans

over

.

•

Choose
n
linearly independent vectors from

; they form a basis for

and hence also a basis of

over

Let
A\in\operatorname{GL}_{n}(L)
be the change of basis matrix from our basis in C to the standard basis of

\psi(\sigma)=A^{-1}\sigma(A)
for all
\sigma\in G.

5.
Fields of Definition

We are interested in computing genus
0
modular curves
(X_{G},\pi_{G})
of prime power levels with a point such that
\det(G)
is a proper subgroup of
{\hat{{\mathbb{Z}}}}^{\times}.
As explained in
section

3
such a curve
(X_{G},\pi_{G})
will be defined over a subfield of
K_{p^{m}}
, where
p^{m}
is the level of

In this section we explain that it is sufficient to consider the subfields of
K_{p^{n}}
, where
p^{n}
is the level of
G\cap\operatorname{SL}_{2}({\hat{{\mathbb{Z}}}}).

Let us discuss the procedure that we use to compute
(X_{G},\pi_{G}).
We fix a genus
0
congruence subgroup
\Gamma
of prime power level
p^{n}
that contains
-I.
Let
h
be the normalized hauptmodul of
\Gamma.
Please refer to section
4
of
[
Rakvi21
]
for a discussion on hauptmoduls and their computations for any genus
0
congruence subgroup. Since the coefficients of
q
-expansion of
h
lie in
K_{p^{n}}
, the function field of
X_{\Gamma}
is
K_{p^{n}}(h).
There is a unique
\pi_{\Gamma}
in
K_{p^{n}}(t)
such that
\pi_{\Gamma}(h)=j
where

is the modular
j
-invariant. To compute
\pi_{\Gamma}
we use the method described in section
4.4
of
[
MR3671434
]
. The function
\pi_{\Gamma}
describes a morphism
\pi_{\Gamma}\colon X_{\Gamma}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}_{K_{p^{n}}}.

Suppose there is an open subgroup
G
of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}({\hat{{\mathbb{Z}}}})
containing
-I
for which
G\cap\operatorname{SL}_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})
is
\Gamma.
Let
p^{m}
be the level of
G.
We know that
m
is greater than or equal to
n.
There is an isomorphism
f\colon(X_{\Gamma})_{K_{p^{m}}}\to(X_{G})_{K_{p^{m}}}
satisfying
\pi_{G}\circ f=\pi_{\Gamma}.
We observe that
K^{\det(G)}_{p^{m}}(h)
is a function field for a model of

, isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{K^{\det(G)}_{p^{m}}}.
Since we have models for
X_{\Gamma}
and

over

there is a natural action of
\Gal(K_{p^{m}}/K^{\det(G)}_{p^{m}})
on

Using that
\pi_{G}
is defined over
K^{\det(G)}_{p^{m}}
we see that
f
satisfies the following condition

(5.1)

\sigma(\pi_{\Gamma})=\pi_{\Gamma}\circ f^{-1}\circ\sigma(f)

for every
\sigma\in\Gal(K_{p^{m}}/K^{\det(G)}_{p^{m}}).

eq.

5.1
gives us a condition on the image of the cocycle

\zeta\colon\Gal(K_{p^{m}}/K^{\det(G)}_{p^{m}})\to\Aut((X_{\Gamma})_{K_{p^{m}}})=\operatorname{PGL}_{2}(K_{p^{m}})

that sends
\sigma
to
f^{-1}\circ\sigma(f).
Further, we have also checked that image of
\zeta
lies in
\operatorname{PGL}_{2}(K_{p^{n}})
, where
p^{n}
is the level of
\Gamma.

For a fixed
m
, there are only finitely many cocycles from
\Gal(K_{p^{m}}/K^{\det(G)}_{p^{m}})
to
\operatorname{PGL}_{2}(K_{p^{m}})
that satisfy
eq.

5.1
. We compute the cocycles on a set of generators of

and extend to the whole group using the cocycle property. Let
\zeta
be one such cocycle. From

4.5
and discussion in the paragraph following it, we know that if
\zeta
is a coboundary in

, then

gives a pair

which is isomorphic to modular curve

We will now proceed to show that

is defined over a subfield of
K_{p^{n}}
. Recall that
p^{n}
is the level of congruence subgroup

Suppose that

is an odd prime. We know that

is a cyclic extension of
{\mathbb{Q}}
for any positive integer

Therefore, the number of subfields of

that contain

When we increase our field to

such that

In case the level of

is

for some positive integer

is bigger than or equal to

is equal to

or

or

to

When we increase the field from

For each of these subfields

Define

The Galois group

We know that

5.1.
Extending the upper field

Fix a genus
0
congruence subgroup
(\Gamma,\pi_{\Gamma})
containing
-I
of level
p^{n}.
If the level is

or
4
, we extend the level to

Fix a field

Let

In this section, we will explain that there are finitely many cocycles from

that satisfy

5.1
. Let

Let

be the least common multiple of orders of elements in

that divide some power of

Any cocycle from

factors through

Let

be a cocycle that satisfies

5.1
. We observe that the condition given by

means that the image of

Choose a large enough positive integer

factors through

Recall that if

Therefore, the Galois group

Then by our choice of

Also observe that

It follows that

factors through
\Gal(K_{bp^{n}}/K).

∎

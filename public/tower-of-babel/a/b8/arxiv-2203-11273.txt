Congruences of Hurwitz class numbers on square classes
Olivia Beckwith, Martin Raum, Olav K. Richter

Abstract
Abstract: We extend a holomorphic projection argument of our earlier work to prove a novel divisibility result for non-holomorphic congruences of Hurwitz class numbers. This result allows us to establish Ramanujan-type congruences for Hurwitz class numbers on square classes, where the holomorphic case parallels previous work by Radu on partition congruences. We offer two applications. The first application demonstrates common divisibility features of Ramanujan-type congruences for Hurwitz class numbers. The second application provides a dichotomy between congruences for class numbers of imaginary quadratic fields and Ramanujan-type congruences for Hurwitz class numbers. H
urwitz class numbers
H(D)
play a significant role in classical number theory. If
-D<-4
is a negative fundamental discriminant, then
h(-D)=H(D)
is the class number of the imaginary quadratic field
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-D})
. Despite intensive studies, divisibility properties of these class numbers have remained mysterious.

In this work, we investigate Ramanujan-type congruences for Hurwitz class numbers (see Theorem
A
,
B
, and
C
). Furthermore, in Theorem
D
we connect Ramanujan-type congruences for Hurwitz class numbers
H(D)
to congruences for class numbers
h(-D)
in certain families of fundamental discriminants
-D
.

In
[
2
]
, we explored Ramanujan-type congruences for Hurwitz class numbers
H(D)
such as the following examples:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} H(5^{3}n+5^{2})&\equiv 0\;\;(\mathrm{mod}\,5)\text{,}\\
H(7^{3}n+3\cdot 7^{2})&\equiv 0\;\;(\mathrm{mod}\,7)\text{,}\\
H(11^{3}n+7\cdot 11^{2})&\equiv 0\;\;(\mathrm{mod}\,11)\text{.}\end{aligned}

These congruences are of the form
H(an+b)\equiv 0\,\;(\mathrm{mod}\,\ell)
, where
\ell>3
is a prime and
a>0
and
b
are integers such that
-b
is a square modulo
a
. We refer to such congruences as non-holomorphic Ramanujan-type congruences, because the generating series for
H(an+b)
is a mock modular form, i.e., it has a non-holomorphic modular completion. In particular, one cannot access such congruences via standard techniques from the theory of holomorphic modular forms.

In our earlier work
[
2
]
we employed a holomorphic projection argument to prove that for such non-holomorphic congruences the divisibility
\ell\mathop{\mid}a
holds. The above examples also suggest the divisibility
\ell\mathop{\mid}b
, and in this current paper we use another holomorphic projection argument to prove:

Theorem A
.

Let
\ell>3
be a prime,
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, and
b\in\mathbb{Z}
. If
-b
is a square modulo
a
and

\displaystyle H(an+b)\equiv 0\;\;(\mathrm{mod}\,\ell)

for all integers
n
, then
\ell\mathop{\mid}b
.

There are also holomorphic Ramanujan-type congruences for Hurwitz class numbers, i.e., congruences
H(an+b)\equiv 0\,\;(\mathrm{mod}\,\ell)
where
-b
is not a square modulo
a
:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} H(3^{3}n+3^{2})&\equiv 0\;\;(\mathrm{mod}\,5)\text{,}\\
H(5^{3}n+2\cdot 5^{2})&\equiv 0\;\;(\mathrm{mod}\,7)\text{,}\\
H(2^{9}n+3\cdot 2^{6})&\equiv 0\;\;(\mathrm{mod}\,11)\text{.}\end{aligned}

In these examples
\ell
does not divide
a
or
b
. While such congruences can be studied with tools from the theory of holomorphic modular forms, the relation between
a
and
b
has not yet been resolved, either.

Our examples of holomorphic and non-holomorphic congruences indicate that
\operatorname{ord}_{p}\big(a/\penalty\gcd(a,b)\big)\leq 1
for odd primes
p
and
\operatorname{ord}_{2}\big(a/\penalty\gcd(a,b)\big)\leq 3
. To prove these phenomena, we first establish the following result on congruences of square classes:

Theorem B
.

Let
\ell>3
be a prime,
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, and
b\in\mathbb{Z}
. Suppose
H(an+b)\equiv 0\,\;(\mathrm{mod}\,\ell)
for all integers
n
. Then
H(an+bu^{2})\equiv 0\,\;(\mathrm{mod}\,\ell)
for all integers
u
with
\gcd(u,a)=1
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

In the case of holomorphic Ramanujan-type congruences, the proof of Theorem
B
extends ideas of Radu’s study of partition congruences
[
11
,
11
]
. The proof of Theorem
B
in the non-holomorphic case requires a deeper analysis, where Theorem
A
is an essential ingredient.

We give two applications of Theorem
B
. In our first application, we call a Ramanujan-type congruences for
H(D)
modulo
\ell
on
a\mathbb{Z}+b

maximal
, if
H(D)
has no Ramanujan-type congruence modulo
\ell
on any arithmetic progression
a^{\prime}\mathbb{Z}+b^{\prime}
that is properly contained in
a\mathbb{Z}+b
.

Theorem C
.

Let
\ell>3
be a prime. Suppose that we have a maximal Ramanujan-type congruence modulo
\ell
for the Hurwitz class numbers on
a\mathbb{Z}+b
. Then for odd primes
p
,

\displaystyle\operatorname{ord}_{p}\big(a/\penalty\gcd(a,b)\big)\leq 1\quad\text{and}\quad\operatorname{ord}_{2}\big(a/\penalty\gcd(a,b)\big)\leq 3\text{.}

As a further application of Theorem
B
, we provide a dichotomy between Ramanujan-type congruences for Hurwitz class numbers and congruences for class numbers of imaginary quadratic fields whose discriminant varies in a square class modulo
a
. For the next statement, we require the usual Legendre symbol and also the divisor sum
\sigma_{1}(b):=\sum_{d\mathop{\mid}b}d
. For a prime
p
and an integer
a
, we call the largest
p
-power that divides
a
its
p
-part.

Theorem D
.

Let
\ell>3
be a prime. Suppose that we have a Ramanujan-type congruence modulo
\ell
for the Hurwitz class numbers on
a\mathbb{Z}+b
. For all odd primes
p\mathop{\mid}a
assume that
\operatorname{ord}_{p}\big(a/\penalty\gcd(a,b)\big)\geq 1
and if
a
is even, assume that
\operatorname{ord}_{2}\big(a/\penalty\gcd(a,b)\big)\geq 2
. Then either:

(i)

We have
h(-D)\equiv 0\,\;(\mathrm{mod}\,\ell)
for all fundamental discriminants
-D<-4
for which there is
f\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
with
Df^{2}\in a\mathbb{Z}+b
.

(ii)

There is a prime
p
dividing
a
such that

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}(f_{p})\equiv\left(\frac{-D}{p}\right)\sigma_{1}(f_{p}/\penalty p)\;\;(\mathrm{mod}\,\ell)

for every fundamental discriminant
-D<0
and integer
f
satisfying
Df^{2}\equiv b\;(\mathrm{mod}\,a)
, where
f_{p}
is the
p
-part of
f
. Both
(-D/\penalty p)
and
f_{p}
are uniquely determined by
a\mathbb{Z}+b
. In this case, we have a Ramanujan-type congruence for Hurwitz class numbers on
a_{p}\mathbb{Z}+b
, where
a_{p}
is the
p
-part of
a
.

Additionally, if the congruence in
(ii)
holds for any
Df^{2}\in a\mathbb{Z}+b
, then it holds for all
Df^{2}\in a\mathbb{Z}+b
for which
-Df^{2}
is a discriminant and we have a Ramanujan-type congruence modulo
\ell
on
a\mathbb{Z}+b
.

Remark 1
.

(1)

The assumptions on the orders of
a/\penalty\gcd(a,b)
can always be achieved by replacing
a
with a suitable multiple of it. They can be removed at the expense of a more technical statement involving the factorizations
Df^{2}\in a\mathbb{Z}+b
that appear in case
(i)
.

(2)

From the Hurwitz class number formula alone, one could deduce a statement similar to case
(ii)
for some prime
p
, not necessarily dividing
a
. To show that one must have
p\mathop{\mid}a
, we use Theorem
B
.

(3)

One can verify that all congruences given in this introduction fall under case
(ii)
. We do not expect that the first case in the theorem ever occurs, i.e., we expect that Ramanujan-type congruences for
H(D)
modulo
\ell
on
a\mathbb{Z}+b
occur if and only if there is
p\mathop{\mid}a
and
Df^{2}\in a\mathbb{Z}+b
satisfying the above condition in
(ii)
. This belief is supported by extensive numerical evidence in addition to well-known theorems on the divisibility of class numbers, such as
[
15
]
, which implies that if the first case occurs, we must have
p\equiv\pm 1\,\;(\mathrm{mod}\,\ell)
for any odd primes
p
with
2\mathop{\nmid}\operatorname{ord}_{p}(a)
.

(4)

For any congruences for Hurwitz class numbers that do not fall under case
(ii)
, Theorem
D
implies the existence of fundamental discriminants
-D\equiv u^{2}b\;(\mathrm{mod}\,a)
for which
h(-D)\equiv 0\,\;(\mathrm{mod}\,\ell)
for some
u
co-prime to
a
.

The proof of Theorem
D
relies on the Hurwitz class number formula and Theorem
B
. In accordance with Theorem
B
, the case of holomorphic Ramanujan-type congruences is accessible via methods from the classical theory of modular forms, while the non-holomorphic case is not.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section

2

3
we prove Theorem

1.1

Generating series of Hurwitz class numbers

Zagier
[
18
]
investigated the generating series
\sum_{D}H(D)e(D\tau)
of Hurwitz class numbers, and proved that it has a modular completion:

\displaystyle E_{\frac{3}{2}}(\tau)\;:=\;\sum_{D=0}^{\infty}H(D)e(D\tau)\,+\,\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{1}{16 \pi}$}}\theta^{\ast}(\tau)\;\in\;\mathbb{M}_{\frac{3}{2}}(\Gamma_{0}(4))\text{,}

(1.2)

where

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \theta&\;:=\;\theta_{1,0}\,\in\,\mathrm{M}_{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma_{0}(4))\text{\, with}\\
\theta_{a,b}(\tau)&\;:=\;\hskip-14.40004pt\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{Z}\\
n\equiv b\,\;(\mathrm{mod}\,a)\end{subarray}}e\big(\tfrac{n^{2}\tau}{a}\big)\,\in\,\mathrm{M}_{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma(4a))\text{,}\quad a\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1},b\in\mathbb{Z}\text{.}\end{aligned}

(1.3)

For
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
and
b\in\mathbb{Z}
, we recall the operators
\mathrm{U}_{a,b}
from our earlier work
[
2
]
, which act on Fourier series expansions of non-holomorphic modular forms by:

\displaystyle\mathrm{U}_{a,b}\,\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}c(f;\,n;\,y)e(n\tau)\;:=\;\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{Z}\\
n\equiv b\,\;(\mathrm{mod}\,a)\end{subarray}}c\big(f;\,n;\,\tfrac{y}{a}\big)e\big(\tfrac{n\tau}{a}\big)\text{.}

(1.4)

In particular, the holomorphic part of
\mathrm{U}_{a,b}\,E_{\frac{3}{2}}(\tau)
is the generating series of Hurwitz class numbers
H(an+b)
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, and one finds that (see also
[
4
,
8
]
for the holomorphic case)

\displaystyle\mathrm{U}_{a,b}\,E_{\frac{3}{2}}\,\in\,\mathbb{M}_{\frac{3}{2}}(\Gamma(4a))\text{.}

(1.5)

The action of the
\mathrm{U}
-operators on theta series can be described by

\displaystyle\mathrm{U}_{a,b}\,\theta\;=\;\sum_{\beta^{2}\equiv b\,\;(\mathrm{mod}\,a)}\theta_{a,\beta}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{U}_{a,b}\,\theta^{\ast}\;=\;\sum_{\beta^{2}\equiv-b\,\;(\mathrm{mod}\,a)}\sqrt{a}\,\theta^{\ast}_{a,\beta}\text{.}

(1.6)

Note that if
-b
is not a square modulo
a
, then
\mathrm{U}_{a,b}\,E_{\frac{3}{2}}
is a holomorphic modular form.

Holomorphic projection

Holomorphic projection plays an important role in our proofs of Theorems
A
and
B
. We briefly review the holomorphic projection operator from
[
7

in the scalar-valued case (see also

,

,
N\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, and

\displaystyle f(\tau)\;=\;\sum_{n\in\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{Z}}c(f;\,n;\,y)e(\tau n)

(1.7)

Recall the following key properties of the holomorphic projection operator in (

]
states that if

. Theorem 5 of

asserts that if

Conditions on non-holomorphic congruences

We have already proved in
[
2
]
that non-holomorphic congruences modulo

for Hurwitz class numbers on an arithmetic progression

We will prove Theorem

by contraposition. Proposition

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\tilde{a}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
and

(i)

(ii)

(iii)

For every prime

\displaystyle a^{\prime}:=\prod_{p\mathop{\mid}\tilde{a}}p^{\operatorname{max}\{\operatorname{ord}_{p}(\tilde{a)},\,\operatorname{ord}_{p}(2\beta)+1\}}=\mathrm{lcm}(\tilde{a},\,2\beta a_{\mathrm{s}\mathrm{f}})\text{,}

\displaystyle 0\;=\;\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{1}{N}$}}\sum_{m=1}^{N}f\big|_{2}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&m\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)=cE_{2}^{\mathrm{hol}}\,+\,\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{1}{N}$}}\sum_{m=1}^{N}g\big|_{2}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&m\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\text{.}

Since the second summand is a modular form, we conclude that
c=0
as desired.

Part 2 of the proof of Theorem
B
.

We now prove the theorem in the case that
-b
is a square modulo
a
. We start with the congruences

\displaystyle\mathrm{U}_{a,b}\,E^{\mathrm{hol}}_{\frac{3}{2}}\cdot\mathrm{U}_{a,\tilde{b}}\,\theta\;\equiv\;0\;\;(\mathrm{mod}\,\ell)\text{,}

which hold for all integer
\tilde{b}
, since the first factor vanishes modulo
\ell
by our assumptions. The main theorem of
[
2
]
informs us that
\ell\mathop{\mid}a
, and Theorem
A
asserts that
\ell\mathop{\mid}b
. If
a\mathop{\mid}b
, there is nothing to show. We assume the opposite. Then there is some integer
b^{\prime}
with
\ell\mathop{\mid}b^{\prime}
and
-b\not\equiv b^{\prime}\,\;(\mathrm{mod}\,a)
and
b^{\prime}
is a square modulo
a
. Lemma
3.2
now yields the following congruence of quasi-modular forms:

\displaystyle\pi^{\mathrm{hol}}_{2}\big(\mathrm{U}_{a,b}\,E_{\frac{3}{2}}\cdot\mathrm{U}_{a,b^{\prime}}\,\theta\big)\;\equiv\;0\;\;(\mathrm{mod}\,\ell)\text{.}

(3.2)

Since
b+b^{\prime}\not\equiv 0\,\;(\mathrm{mod}\,a)
, we can apply Lemma
3.3
to the left hand side of (
3.2
) to see that it is a modular form as opposed to only a quasi-modular form. In particular, we can apply the Fourier expansion principle (in its weak form) due to Katz
[
10
]
(see also the strong form by Deligne-Rapoport
[
5
]
). Similar to our strategy in the first of part of this proof, let
\gamma\in\Gamma(4a)
satisfy
\gamma\equiv\left(\begin{smallmatrix}u&0\\
0&\overline{u}\end{smallmatrix}\right)\,\;(\mathrm{mod}\,4a)
where
\overline{u}\,\;(\mathrm{mod}\,4a)
is a multiplicative inverse of
u
modulo
4a
, and assume that
c_{\gamma}/\penalty 4a
is relatively prime to
4a
, where
c_{\gamma}
is the bottom left entry of
\gamma
. The Fourier expansion principle yields the congruence

\displaystyle\pi^{\mathrm{hol}}_{2}\big(\mathrm{U}_{a,b}\,E_{\frac{3}{2}}\cdot\mathrm{U}_{a,b^{\prime}}\,\theta\big)\big|_{2}\,\gamma\;\equiv\;0\;\;(\mathrm{mod}\,\ell)\text{.}

To determine the left hand side of this congruence, recall that the slash action intertwines with the holomorphic projection (see
[
7
]
). We have

\displaystyle\pi^{\mathrm{hol}}_{2}\Big(\big(\mathrm{U}_{a,b}\,E_{\frac{3}{2}}\big|_{\frac{3}{2}}\,\gamma\big)\cdot\big(\mathrm{U}_{a,b^{\prime}}\,\theta\big|_{\frac{1}{2}}\,\gamma\big)\Big)\;\equiv\;0\;\;(\mathrm{mod}\,\ell)\text{.}

Lemma
3.1
yields the congruence

\displaystyle\pi^{\mathrm{hol}}_{2}\big(\mathrm{U}_{a,bu^{2}}\,E_{\frac{3}{2}}\cdot\mathrm{U}_{a,b^{\prime}u^{2}}\,\theta\big)\;\equiv\;0\;\;(\mathrm{mod}\,\ell)\text{.}

Observe that
bu^{2}
and
b^{\prime}u^{2}
are divisible by
\ell
, so that we can apply Lemma
3.2
to find that

\displaystyle\mathrm{U}_{a,bu^{2}}\,E^{\mathrm{hol}}_{\frac{3}{2}}\cdot\mathrm{U}_{a,b^{\prime}u^{2}}\,\theta\;\equiv\;0\;\;(\mathrm{mod}\,\ell)\text{.}

(3.3)

Since
b^{\prime}
is a square modulo
a
by assumption, the second factor on the left hand side of (
3.3
) is not congruent to zero modulo
\ell
, and more specifically its first non-zero Fourier coefficient equals one or two. From this we infer that
\mathrm{U}_{a,bu^{2}}\,E^{\mathrm{hol}}_{\frac{3}{2}}\equiv 0\,\;(\mathrm{mod}\,\ell)
.

Proofs of Theorems
C
and
D

Proof of Theorem
C
.

For simplicity, we say that
(a,b)
is a mod
\ell
Hurwitz congruence pair if we have the Ramanujan-type congruence
H(an+b)\equiv 0\,\;(\mathrm{mod}\,\ell)
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Furthermore, we say that a mod
\ell
Hurwitz congruence pair
(a,b)
is maximal if the corresponding Ramanujan-type congruence is maximal.

Let
p
be a prime, let
k=\operatorname{ord}_{p}(\gcd(a,b))
and
r=\operatorname{ord}_{p}(a/\penalty\gcd(a,b))
. After replacing
b
by
b+a
, if needed, we then have
a=p^{k+r}a^{\prime}
and
b=p^{k}b^{\prime}
for integers
a^{\prime}
and
b^{\prime}
with
\gcd(a^{\prime}b^{\prime},p)=1
.

First, we assume that
p
is odd, that
r\geq 2
, and that
(a,b)
is a Hurwitz congruence pair modulo
\ell
. We will show that
(a,b)
is not a maximal Hurwitz congruence pair mod
\ell
. If
m\equiv b\,\;(\mathrm{mod}\,a/\penalty p)
, then
p^{k}\|m
and
m/p^{k}\equiv b^{\prime}\,\;(\mathrm{mod}\,p^{r-1})
. From Hensel’s Lemma, there exists
u\in\mathbb{Z}
with
\gcd(u,p)=1
such that
m/\penalty p^{k}\equiv b^{\prime}u^{2}\;(\mathrm{mod}\,p^{r})
. Using the Chinese Remainder Theorem, one can find such a
u
with
u\equiv 1\;(\mathrm{mod}\,a^{\prime})
so that we have
m\equiv bu^{2}\,\;(\mathrm{mod}\,a)
. By Theorem
B
, we have
H(m)\equiv 0\;(\mathrm{mod}\,\ell)
. Hence
(a/\penalty p,b)
is a Hurwitz congruence pair modulo
\ell
, so
(a,b)
is not a maximal Hurwitz congruence pair.

The
p=2
case is almost exactly the same. We assume
r\geq 4
and we will show that
(a,b)
cannot be a maximal Hurwitz congruence pair modulo
\ell
. Suppose
m\equiv b\,\;(\mathrm{mod}\,a/\penalty 2)
. Then
2^{k}|m
, and using a Hensel’s lemma type argument, one easily checks that there exists an integer
u
which is relatively prime to
a
such that
m\equiv bu^{2}\;(\mathrm{mod}\,a)
(this is where we require
r\geq 4
rather than
r\geq 2
). By the Theorem, we have
H(m)\equiv 0\;(\mathrm{mod}\,\ell)
, which means
(a,b)
is not a maximal Hurwitz congruence pair.

Proof of Theorem
D
.

Assume that
(i)
of Theorem
D
does not hold. That is, there is a fundamental discriminant
-D
and a positive integer
f
such that
Df^{2}\in a\mathbb{Z}+b
and
H(D)\not\equiv 0\,\;(\mathrm{mod}\,\ell)
. Given a prime
p\mathop{\mid}f
we write
f_{p}
for its
p
-part. We will show that there is a prime
p\mathop{\mid}\gcd(f,a)
such that

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}(f_{p})-\sigma_{1}\Big(\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{f_p}{p}$}}\Big)\Big(\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{-D}{p}$}}\Big)\equiv 0\;\;(\mathrm{mod}\,\ell)\text{.}

(4.1)

We factor
f=f_{a}u
into the product of two positive integers
f_{a}
and
u
, where every prime dividing
f_{a}
also divides
a
and
u
is co-prime to
a
. In particular, there is an inverse
\overline{u}
of
u
modulo
a
.

Theorem
B
asserts that we have a Ramanujan-type congruence modulo
\ell
for Hurwitz class numbers on
a\mathbb{Z}+\overline{u}^{2}b\ni Df_{a}^{2}
. The Hurwitz class number formula asserts that

\displaystyle H(Df_{a}^{2})

\displaystyle{}=H(D)\,\frac{\omega(-Df_{a}^{2})}{\omega(-D)}\sum_{d\mathop{\mid}f_{a}}d\prod_{p\mathop{\mid}d}\Big(1-\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{1}{p}$}}\Big(\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{-D}{p}$}}\Big)\Big)

\displaystyle{}=H(D)\,\frac{\omega(-Df_{a}^{2})}{\omega(-D)}\prod_{p\mathop{\mid}f_{a}}\Big(\sigma_{1}(f_{p})-\sigma_{1}\Big(\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{f_p}{p}$}}\Big)\Big(\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\genfrac{}{}{0.4pt}{}{-D}{p}$}}\Big)\Big)\text{,}

where
\omega(-Df_{a}^{2})
is the number of units in the imaginary quadratic order of discriminant
-Df_{a}^{2}
. By assumption on
D
, we have
H(D)\not\equiv 0\,\;(\mathrm{mod}\,\ell)
. Further, we note that
\omega(-Df_{a}^{2})\not\equiv 0\,\;(\mathrm{mod}\,\ell)
, since
\ell>3
and
\omega(-Df_{a}^{2})\mathop{\mid}6
. Therefore, we conclude the existence of some prime
p\mathop{\mid}\gcd(f,a)
satisfying (
4.1
) as desired.

To finish the proof, we will show that for any other fundamental discriminant
-D^{\prime}
and integer
f^{\prime}
with
D^{\prime}f^{\prime 2}\in a\mathbb{Z}+b
, we have
f_{p}=f_{p}^{\prime}
. We note that
(\frac{-D^{\prime}}{p})=(\frac{-D}{p})
follows from
D^{\prime}f^{\prime 2},Df^{2}\in a\mathbb{Z}+b
and
f_{2}^{\prime}=f_{2}
. Case
(ii)
follows immediately from these two claims and (
4.1
).

By assumption
\operatorname{ord}_{p}(a/\penalty\gcd(a,b))>0
, and hence
a_{p}\mathop{\nmid}b
and
\operatorname{ord}_{p}(b)=\operatorname{ord}_{p}(Df^{2})=\operatorname{ord}_{p}(D^{\prime}f^{\prime 2})
. In other words, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{ord}_{p}(D^{\prime})+2\operatorname{ord}_{p}(f^{\prime})=\operatorname{ord}_{p}(b)=\operatorname{ord}_{p}(D)+2\operatorname{ord}_{p}(f)\text{.}

(4.2)

Now consider the case of odd
p
. Since
-D^{\prime}
and
-D
are fundamental discriminants,
\operatorname{ord}_{p}(D^{\prime})\leq 1
and
\operatorname{ord}_{p}(D)\leq 1
are given by the parity of
\operatorname{ord}_{p}(b)
and hence
f_{p}=f_{p}^{\prime}
as required.

Next, consider the case of
p=2
. We have
\operatorname{ord}_{2}(D)\in\{0,2,3\}
. If
\operatorname{ord}_{2}(D)=3
or
\operatorname{ord}_{2}(D^{\prime})=3
, the argument for odd
p
extends. From now on, we assume that
\operatorname{ord}_{2}(D)
and
\operatorname{ord}_{2}(D^{\prime})
are both in
\{0,2\}
. If
\operatorname{ord}_{2}(D)=0
and
\operatorname{ord}_{2}(D^{\prime})=2
, then from (
4.2
) we have

\displaystyle 2+2\operatorname{ord}_{p}(f^{\prime})=\operatorname{ord}_{2}(b)=2\operatorname{ord}_{p}(f).

From
D^{\prime}f^{\prime 2},Df^{2}\in a\mathbb{Z}+b
and
\operatorname{ord}_{2}(a/\penalty\gcd(a,b))\geq 2
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{D^{\prime}f^{\prime 2}}{4f_{2}^{\prime 2}}\equiv\frac{D}{f_{2}^{2}}\equiv 0\;\;(\mathrm{mod}\,4)\text{,}

from which we have
D\equiv D^{\prime}/\penalty 4\,\;(\mathrm{mod}\,4)
. Since
-D^{\prime}
is a fundamental discriminant and
\operatorname{ord}_{2}(D^{\prime})=2
, we would have
D^{\prime}/\penalty 4\equiv 1\;(\mathrm{mod}\,4)
. Therefore
D\equiv 1\;(\mathrm{mod}\,4)
. This is a contradiction, since
-D
is a discriminant. The case of
\operatorname{ord}_{2}(D)=2
and
\operatorname{ord}_{2}(D^{\prime})
is excluded by a symmetric argument. We conclude that we must have
\operatorname{ord}_{2}(D^{\prime})=\operatorname{ord}_{2}(D)
, which implies
f_{2}=f_{2}^{\prime}
as desired.

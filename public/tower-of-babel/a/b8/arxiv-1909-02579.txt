Abstract
. Vector addition systems with states (VASS) are widely used for the formal verification of concurrent systems. Given their tremendous computational complexity, practical approaches have relied on techniques such as reachability relaxations, e.g. , allowing for negative intermediate counter values. It is natural to question their feasibility for VASS enriched with primitives that typically translate into undecidability. Spurred by this concern, we pinpoint the complexity of integer relaxations with respect to arbitrary classes of affine operations. More specifically, we provide a trichotomy on the complexity of integer reachability in VASS extended with affine operations (affine VASS). Namely, we show that it is 𝖭𝖯 \mathsf{NP} -complete for VASS with resets, 𝖯𝖲𝖯𝖠𝖢𝖤 \mathsf{PSPACE} -complete for VASS with (pseudo-)transfers and VASS with (pseudo-)copies, and undecidable for any other class. We further present a dichotomy for standard reachability in affine VASS: it is decidable for VASS with permutations, and undecidable for any other class. This yields a complete and unified complexity landscape of reachability in affine VASS. We also consider the reachability problem parameterized by a fixed affine VASS, rather than a class, and we show that the complexity landscape is arbitrary in this setting.

1.
Introduction

Vector addition systems with states (VASS)
, which can
equivalently be seen as Petri nets, form a widespread general model of
infinite-state systems with countless applications ranging from the
verification of concurrent programs to the modeling of biological,
chemical and business processes (see,
e.g.
,
[
GS92
,
KKW14
,
EGLM17
,
HGD08
,
van98
]
). They comprise a finite-state controller with
counters ranging over
\mathbb{N}
and updated via instructions of the form
x\leftarrow x+c
which are executable if
x+c\geq 0
. The central
decision problem concerning VASS is the
reachability problem
:
given configurations
x
and
y
, is it possible to reach
y
starting
from
x
? Such queries allow,
e.g.
, to verify whether unsafe states can
be reached in concurrent programs. The notorious difficulty of the
reachability problem led to many proofs of its decidability over the
last decades
[
ST77
,
May81
,
Kos82
,
Lam92
,
Ler10
,
Ler11
,
Ler12
]
. While the problem has been known to be
\mathsf{EXPSPACE}
-hard since
1976
[
Lip76
]
, its computational complexity has remained unknown
until very recently, where it was shown to be

\mathsf{TOWER}
-hard
[
CLL+19
]
and solvable in Ackermannian
time
[
LS15
,
LS19
]
.

Given the potential applications on the one hand, and the tremendously
high complexity on the other hand, researchers have investigated
relaxations of VASS in search of a tradeoff between expressiveness and
algorithmic complexity. Two such relaxations consist in permitting
either:

(1)

transitions to be executed fractionally, and consequently
counters to range over
\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}
(
continuous
reachability
); or

(2)

counters to range over
\mathbb{Z}
(
integer reachability
).

In both cases, the complexity drops drastically: continuous
reachability is
\mathsf{P}
-complete and
\mathsf{NP}
-complete for Petri nets and VASS
respectively
[
FH15
,
BH17
]
, while integer reachability is

\mathsf{NP}
-complete for both models
[
HH14
,
CHH18
]
. Moreover, these two types of
reachability have been used successfully to prove safety of real-world
instances like multithreaded program skeletons,

e.g.
, see
[
ELM+14
,
ALW16
,
BFHH17
]
.

Although VASS are versatile, they are sometimes too limited to model
common primitives. Consequently, their modeling power has been
extended with various operations. For example,

(multi-)transfers
,
i.e.
, operations of the form

x\leftarrow x+\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i};\ y_{1}\leftarrow 0;\ y_{2}\leftarrow 0;\ \cdots;\ y_{n}\leftarrow 0,

allow,
e.g.
, for the verification of
multi-threaded C and Java program skeletons with communication
primitives
[
KKW14
,
DRV02
]
. Another example is the case of

resets
,
i.e.
, operations of the form
x\leftarrow 0
, which
allow,
e.g.
, for the validation of some business
processes
[
WvdAtHE09
]
, and the generation of program loop
invariants
[
SK19
]
. Many such extensions fall under the generic
family of
affine VASS
,
i.e.
, VASS with instructions of the form

\bm{x}\leftarrow\mathbf{A}\cdot\bm{x}+\bm{b}
. As a general
rule of thumb, reachability is undecidable for essentially any class
of affine VASS introduced in the literature; in particular, for
transfers and resets
[
AK76
,
DFPS98
]
.

Given the success of relaxations for the practical analysis of
(standard) VASS, it is tempting to employ the same approach for affine
VASS. Unfortunately, continuous reachability becomes undecidable for
mild affine extensions such as resets and transfers. However,

integer reachability
was recently shown decidable for affine
operations such as resets (
\mathsf{NP}
-complete) and transfers
(
\mathsf{PSPACE}
-complete)
[
HH14
,
BHM18
]
. While such complexity results
do not translate immediately into practical procedures, they arguably
guide the design of algorithmic verification strategies.

Contribution
. Thus, these recent results raise two
natural questions: for
what classes
of affine VASS is integer
reachability decidable? And, whenever it is decidable, what is its

exact
computational complexity? We fully answer these questions
in this paper by giving a precise trichotomy: integer reachability is

\mathsf{NP}
-complete for VASS with resets,
\mathsf{PSPACE}
-complete for VASS with
(pseudo-)transfers and VASS with (pseudo-)copies, and undecidable for

any
other class. In particular, this answers a question left
open in
[
BHM18
]
: integer reachability is undecidable for

any
class of affine VASS with
infinite
matrix monoid.

This clear complexity landscape is obtained by formalizing classes of
affine VASS and by carefully analyzing the structure of arbitrary
affine transformations; which could be of independent interest. In
particular, it enables us to prove a dichotomy on
(standard)
reachability
for affine VASS: it is decidable for VASS with
permutations, and undecidable for
any
other class. To the best
of our knowledge, this is the first proof of the folkore rule of thumb
stating that “reachability is undecidable for essentially any class
of affine VASS”.

We further complement these trichotomy and dichotomy by showing that
the (integer or standard) reachability problem has an arbitrary
complexity when it is parameterized by a fixed affine VASS rather than
a fixed class.

Related work
. Our work is related
to
[
BHM18
]
which shows that integer reachability is decidable for
affine VASS whose matrix monoid is finite (refined to
\mathsf{EXPSPACE}
by
[
BHK+20
]
); and more particularly
\mathsf{PSPACE}
-complete in general
for VASS with transfers and VASS with copies. While it is also
recalled in
[
BHM18
]
that integer reachability is undecidable in
general for affine VASS, the authors do not provide any necessary
condition for undecidability to hold. Moreover, the complexity
landscape for affine VASS with finite monoids is left blurred,
e.g.
, it
does not give necessary conditions for
\mathsf{PSPACE}
-hardness results to
hold, and the complexity remains unknown for monoids with negative
coefficients. This paper completes the work initiated in
[
BHM18
]

by providing a unified framework, which includes the notion
of
matrix class
, that allows us to precisely characterize the
complexity of integer reachability for
any
class of affine
VASS.

Our work is also loosely related to a broader line of research on
(variants of) affine VASS dealing with,
e.g.
, modeling
power
[
Val78
]
, accelerability
[
FL02
]
, formal
languages
[
CFM12
]
, coverability
[
BFP12
]
, and the complexity
of integer reachability for restricted counters
[
FGH13
]
and
structures
[
IS16
]
.

Structure of the paper
. Section
2

introduces general notation and affine VASS. In
Section
3
, we prove our main result, namely the

Section
4

Classes of matrices
. Let us formalize the informal
notion of
classes
of affine VASS, such as “VASS with resets”,
“VASS with transfers”, “VASS with doubling”, etc., used throughout
the literature.

Such classes depend on the extra operations they provide,
i.e.
, by
their affine transformations, and more precisely by their linear part
(matrices) rather than their additive part (vectors). Affine VASS extend
standard VASS since they always include the identity matrix, which
amounts to not applying any extra operation. Moreover, as
transformations can be composed along sequences of transitions, their
matrices are closed under multiplication,
i.e.
, if matrices
M(s)

and
M(t)
are allowed on transitions
s
and
t
of two affine
VASS of a given class, then
M(s)\cdot M(t)
is typically
also allowed in an affine VASS of the class as it is understood that

s
and
t
can be composed. In other words, matrices form
a
monoid
. In addition, classes of affine VASS typically
considered do not pose restrictions on the number of counters that can
be used, or on the subset of counters on which operations can be
applied. In other words, their affine transformations can be extended
to
arbitrary dimensions
and can be applied on
any subset
of counters
,
e.g.
, general “VASS with resets” allow to reset any
counter, not just say the first one.

We formalize these observations as follows. For every
k\geq 1
, let

\mathbf{I}_{k}
be the
k\times k
identity matrix and let
\mathcal{S}_{k}
denote
the set of permutations over
[k]
. Let
\mathbf{P}_{\sigma}\in{\{0,1\}}^{k\times k}
be the permutation matrix of
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{k}
. For
every matrix
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{Z}^{k\times k}
, every permutation
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{k}
and every
n\geq 1
, let

\sigma({\mathbf{A}})\coloneqq\mathbf{P}_{\sigma}\cdot\mathbf{A}\cdot\mathbf{P}_{\sigma^{-1}},

and let

{\mathbf{A}}\!\!\restriction_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{(k+n)\times(k+n)}
be the matrix such
that:

{\mathbf{A}}\!\!\restriction_{n}\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{A}&\mathbf{0}_{\phantom{n}}\\
\mathbf{0}&\mathbf{I}_{n}\end{pmatrix}.

A
class (of matrices)
is a set of matrices
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\bigcup_{k\geq 1}\mathbb{Z}^{k\times k}
that satisfies

\{\sigma({\mathbf{A}}),{\mathbf{A}}\!\!\restriction_{n},\allowbreak\mathbf{I}_{n},\mathbf{A}\cdot\mathbf{B}\}\subseteq\mathcal{C}
for every
\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}\in\mathcal{C}
, every
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{\dim{\mathbf{A}}}
and every
n\geq 1
. In
other words,
\mathcal{C}
is closed under counter renaming; each matrix of

\mathcal{C}
can be extended to larger dimensions; and
\mathcal{C}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{k\times k}
is a monoid under matrix multiplication for every
k\geq 1
.

Note that “counter renaming” amounts to choosing a set of counters
on which to apply a given transformation,
i.e.
, it renames the
counters, applies the transformation, and renames the counters back to
their original names. Let us illustrate this. Consider the classical
case of transfer VASS,
i.e.
, where the contents of a counter can be
transferred onto another counter with operations of the form “
x\leftarrow x+y;\ y\leftarrow 0
”. In matrix notation, this
amounts to:

\mathbf{O}\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}.

Now, consider a system with three counters
c_{1}
,
c_{2}
and

c_{3}
. This system should be able to compute “
c_{1}\leftarrow c_{1}+c_{2}+c_{3};\ c_{2}\leftarrow 0;\ c_{3}\leftarrow 0
”, but matrix

\mathbf{O}
cannot achieve this on its own. However, it can be done with
the following matrix:

\mathbf{O}^{\prime}\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}1&1&\textit{0}\\
0&0&\textit{0}\\
\textit{0}&\textit{0}&\textit{1}\end{pmatrix}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}1&\textit{0}&1\\
\textit{0}&\textit{1}&\textit{0}\\
0&\textit{0}&0\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&1\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix}.

We have
\mathbf{O}^{\prime}={\mathbf{O}}\!\!\restriction_{1}\cdot\sigma({{\mathbf{O}}\!\!\restriction_{1}})
where
\sigma\coloneqq(2;3)
. Thus,
the operation can be achieved by any class containing
\mathbf{O}
. The
symmetric operation “
c_{3}\leftarrow c_{1}+c_{2}+c_{3};\ c_{1}\leftarrow 0;\ c_{2}\leftarrow 0
”,
e.g.
, can also be achieved with appropriate
permutations. Hence, this corresponds to the usual notion of
transfers: we are allowed to choose some counters and apply transfers
in either direction.

Note that requiring
\mathbf{P}_{\sigma}\cdot\mathbf{A}\in\mathcal{C}
for classes
would be too strong as it would allow to permute the contents of
counters even for classes with no permutation matrix, such as resets.

3.
A complexity trichotomy for integer reachability

This section is devoted to the proof of our main result, namely the
trichotomy on
\mathbb{Z}\text{-}\textsc{Reach}_{\mathcal{C}}
:

Theorem 1
.

The integer reachability problem
\mathbb{Z}\text{-}\textsc{Reach}_{\mathcal{C}}
is:

(1)

\mathsf{NP}
-complete if
\mathcal{C}
only contains reset matrices;

(2)

\mathsf{PSPACE}
-complete, otherwise, if either
\mathcal{C}
only contains
pseudo-transfer matrices or
\mathcal{C}
only contains pseudo-copy
matrices;

(3)

Undecidable otherwise.

It is known from
[
HH14
, Cor. 10]
that
\mathsf{NP}
-hardness holds for
affine VASS using only the identity matrix (recall that our definition
of reset matrices include the identity matrix), and that

\mathsf{NP}
membership holds for any class of reset matrices. Hence,

1
follows immediately. Thus, the rest of this section is
dedicated to proving
2
and
3
.

3.1.
PSPACE-hardness

For the rest of this subsection, let us fix some class
\mathcal{C}
that
either only contains pseudo-transfer matrices or only contains
pseudo-copy matrices. We prove
\mathsf{PSPACE}
-hardness of
\mathbb{Z}\text{-}\textsc{Reach}_{\mathcal{C}}
by
first proving that
\mathsf{PSPACE}
-hardness holds if either:

•

\mathcal{C}
contains a matrix with an entry equal to
-1
; or

•

\mathcal{C}
contains a matrix with entries from
\{0,1\}
and a
nonzero entry outside of its diagonal.

For these two cases, we first show that
\mathcal{C}
can implement operations

x\leftarrow-x
or
(x,y)\leftarrow(y,x)
respectively,

i.e.
,
sign flips
or
swaps
. Essentially, each of these
operations is sufficient to simulate linear bounded automata. Before
investigating these two cases, let us carefully formalize what it
means to implement an operation:

{defi}

Let

\tau\in\{0,?\}
. Given

,

otherwise. We say that

\mathcal{C}

\tau
-implements

f
if for every
n\geq k
, there
exist counters

, matrices

\{\mathbf{F}_{\sigma}:\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{k}\}\subseteq\mathcal{C}
and

such that the following holds for every
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{k}
and

\bm{v}\in V_{X}
:

(1)

\dim{\mathbf{F}_{\sigma}}=m
;

(2)

(\mathbf{F}_{\sigma}\cdot\bm{v})(x_{\sigma(i)})=f(x_{\sigma(1)},x_{\sigma(2)},\ldots,x_{\sigma(k)})(i)
for
all
i\in[k]
;

(3)

(\mathbf{F}_{\sigma}\cdot\bm{v})(x_{\sigma(i)})=\bm{v}(x_{\sigma(i)})
for every
i\not\in[k]
;

(4)

\mathbf{F}_{\sigma}\cdot\bm{v}\in V_{X}
.

We further say that
\mathcal{C}

implements

f
if it either

0
-implements or
?
-implements
f
.

Definition
3.1

2
and
3

state that it is possible to obtain arbitrarily many counters
X
such
that
f
can be applied on any
k
-subset of
X
, provided that the
counter values belong to
V_{X}
. Moreover,
4
states
that vectors resulting from applying operation
f
also belong to

V_{X}
, which ensures that
f
can be applied arbitrarily many
times. Note that
1
allows for extra auxiliary counters
whose values are only restricted by

.

Informally,
?
-implementation means that we use additional counters
that can hold arbitrary values, while
0
-implementation requires
the extra counters to be initialized with zeros but promises to keep

0

Proposition 2
.

If

\mathbf{C}\coloneqq\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\cdot\sigma({\mathbf{B}^{\prime}})
, where
\sigma\colon[2d]\to[2d]

is the permutation:

\sigma\coloneqq\prod_{\ell\in[d]\setminus\{j,k\}}(\ell;\ell+d).

Let
i^{\prime\prime}\coloneqq i^{\prime}+d
if
i^{\prime}\not\in\{j,k\}
and
i^{\prime\prime}\coloneqq i^{\prime}
otherwise. We have:

\displaystyle\mathbf{C}_{i,i^{\prime\prime}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell\in[2d]}\mathbf{A}^{\prime}_{i,\ell}\cdot\mathbf{B}^{\prime}_{\sigma(\ell),i^{\prime}}

(by def. of
\mathbf{C}
and by
\sigma(i^{\prime\prime})=i^{\prime}
)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell\in[d]\text{ s.t. }\sigma(\ell)\in[d]}\mathbf{A}^{\prime}_{i,\ell}\cdot\mathbf{B}^{\prime}_{\sigma(\ell),i^{\prime}}

(by def. of
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
and
\mathbf{B}^{\prime}
, and by
i,i^{\prime}\in[d]
)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{A}^{\prime}_{i,j}\cdot\mathbf{B}^{\prime}_{j,i^{\prime}}+\mathbf{A}^{\prime}_{i,k}\cdot\mathbf{B}^{\prime}_{k,i^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=a\cdot a^{\prime}+b\cdot b^{\prime}.

Since
a
and
b
(resp.
a^{\prime}
and
b^{\prime}
) have the same sign, and
since
a,b,a^{\prime},b^{\prime}\neq 0
, we conclude that
|\mathbf{C}_{i,i^{\prime\prime}}|\geq 2
and consequently that
\lVert\mathcal{C}\rVert\geq\lVert\mathbf{C}\rVert\geq 2
.

To avoid cumbersome subscripts, we write
\bm{e}
for
\bm{e}_{1}
in
the rest of the section. Moreover, let
\lambda_{\ell}(\mathbf{C})\coloneqq(\mathbf{C}^{\ell}\cdot\bm{e})(1)
for every matrix
\mathbf{C}
and

\ell\in\mathbb{N}
.

The following technical lemma will be key to mimic doubling. It shows
that, from any class of norm at least
2
, we can extract a matrix
with sufficiently fast growth.

Lemma 7
.

For every class of matrices
\mathcal{C}
such that
\lVert\mathcal{C}\rVert\geq 2
, there
exists
\mathbf{C}\in\mathcal{C}
with
\lambda_{n+1}(\mathbf{C})\geq 2\cdot\lambda_{n}(\mathbf{C})
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof 3.7
.

Let
\mathbf{A}\in\mathcal{C}
be a matrix with some entry
c
such that
|c|\geq 2
. We can assume that
c\geq 2
. Indeed, if it is negative,
then we can multiply
\mathbf{A}
by a suitable permutation of itself
to obtain an entry equal to
c\cdot c
. We can further assume that

c
is the largest positive coefficient occurring within
\mathbf{A}
,
and that it lies on the first column of
\mathbf{A}
, i.e.,
\mathbf{A}_{k,1}=c
for some
k\in[d]
where
d\coloneqq\dim{\mathbf{A}}
. We
consider the case where
k=1
. The case where
k\neq 1
will be
discussed later.

For readability, we rename counters
\{1,2,\ldots,d\}

respectively by
X\coloneqq\{x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{d}\}
. Note that

(\mathbf{A}\cdot\bm{e})(x_{1})=c\geq 2\cdot\bm{e}(x_{1})
as
desired. However, vector
\mathbf{A}\cdot\bm{e}
may now hold
nonzero values in counters
x_{2},\ldots,x_{d}
. Therefore, if we
multiply this vector by
\mathbf{A}
, some “noise” will be added to
counter
x_{1}
. If this noise is too large, then it may cancel the
growth of
x_{1}
by
\approx c
. We address this issue by introducing
extra auxiliary counters replacing
x_{2},\ldots,x_{d}
at each
“iteration”. Of course, we cannot have infinitely many auxiliary
counters. Fortunately, after a sufficiently large number
m
of
iterations, the auxiliary counters used at the first iteration
will contain sufficiently small noise so that the process can
restart from there.

More formally, let
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\coloneqq{\mathbf{A}}\!\!\restriction_{|Y|}
where
Y\coloneqq\{y_{i,j}:0\leq i<m,j\in[2,d]\}
is the set of
auxiliary counters, and
m\geq 1
is a sufficiently large constant
whose value will be picked later. Let
V
be the set of vectors

\bm{v}\in\mathbb{Z}^{|X|+|Y|}
satisfying
\bm{v}(x_{1})>0

and

|\bm{v}(y_{i,j})|\leq\left(\frac{3c}{4}\right)^{i}\cdot\frac{\bm{v}(x_{1})}{4d}\text{ for every }y_{i,j}\in Y.

Let us fix some vector
\bm{v}_{0}\in V
. For every
0\leq i<m
,
let
\mathbf{B}_{i}\coloneqq\sigma_{i}({\mathbf{A}^{\prime}})
and
\bm{v}_{i+1}\coloneqq\mathbf{B}_{i}\cdot\bm{v}_{i}
where
\sigma_{i}
is the
permutation

\sigma_{i}\coloneqq\prod_{j\in[2,d]}(x_{j};y_{i,j}).

We claim that:

\bm{v}_{m}(x_{1})\geq 2\cdot\bm{v}_{0}(x_{1})\text{ and }\bm{v}_{m}\in V.

The validity of this claim proves
the lemma. Indeed,
\mathbf{C}\cdot\bm{v}_{0}=\bm{v}_{m}
where

\mathbf{C}\coloneqq\mathbf{B}_{m-1}\cdots\mathbf{B}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{B}_{0}
. Hence, an application of
\mathbf{C}
yields a vector whose
first component has at least doubled. Since
\bm{e}\in V
and the
resulting vector also belong to
V
, this can be iterated
arbitrarily many times.

Let us first establish the following properties for every
0\leq i<m
and
j\in[2,d]
:

(1)

\bm{v}_{i}(y_{i,j})=\bm{v}_{i-1}(y_{i,j})=\cdots=\bm{v}_{0}(y_{i,j})
and
\bm{v}_{i+1}(y_{i,j})=\bm{v}_{i+2}(y_{i,j})=\cdots=\bm{v}_{m}(y_{i,j})
;

(2)

\bm{v}_{i+1}(x_{1})\in\left[\frac{3c}{4}\cdot\bm{v}_{i}(x_{1}),\frac{5c}{4}\cdot\bm{v}_{i}(x_{1})\right]
;
and

(3)

|\bm{v}_{i+1}(y_{i,j})|\leq 2c\cdot\bm{v}_{i}(x_{1})
.

Property
1
, which follows from the definition of

\mathbf{B}_{i}
, essentially states that the contents of counter
y_{i,j}
is only altered from
\bm{v}_{i}
to

\bm{v}_{i+1}
. Properties
2

and
3
bound the growth of the counters in terms
of
x_{1}
. Let us prove these two latter properties by induction on

i
.

By definition of
\bm{v}_{i+1}
, we have
\bm{v}_{i+1}(x_{1})=c\cdot\bm{v}_{i}+\delta
where

\delta\coloneqq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\neq x_{1}\end{subarray}}(\mathbf{B}_{i})_{x_{1},z}\cdot\bm{v}_{i}(z).

Therefore,

\bm{v}_{i+1}(x_{1})\in[c\cdot\bm{v}_{i}(x_{1})-|\delta|,c\cdot\bm{v}_{i}(x_{1})+|\delta|]
, and hence
property
2
follows from:

\displaystyle|\delta|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in X\cup Y\\
z\neq x_{1}\end{subarray}}|(\mathbf{B}_{i})_{x_{1},z}|\cdot|\bm{v}_{i}(z)|

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in[2,d]}|\mathbf{A}^{\prime}_{x_{1},x_{j}}|\cdot|\bm{v}_{i}(y_{i,j})|

(by def. of
\mathbf{B}_{i}
and
\sigma_{i}
)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in[2,d]}|\mathbf{A}^{\prime}_{x_{1},x_{j}}|\cdot|\bm{v}_{0}(y_{i,j})|

(by
1
)

\displaystyle\leq dc\cdot(3c/4)^{i}\cdot\frac{\bm{v}_{0}(x_{1})}{4d}

(by
\bm{v}_{0}\in V
and by maximality of

)

\displaystyle\leq dc\cdot\frac{\bm{v}_{i}(x_{1})}{4d}

(by
\bm{v}_{i}(x_{1})\geq(3c/4)^{i}\cdot\bm{v}_{0}(x_{1})

from
2
)

\displaystyle=\frac{c}{4}\cdot\bm{v}_{i}(x_{1}).

Similarly, property

holds since, for every

\displaystyle|\bm{v}_{i+1}(y_{i,j})|

\displaystyle\leq|\mathbf{A}^{\prime}_{x_{j},x_{1}}|\cdot\bm{v}_{i}(x_{1})+\sum_{\ell\in[2,d]}|\mathbf{A}^{\prime}_{x_{j},x_{\ell}}|\cdot|\bm{v}_{i}(y_{i,\ell})|

(by def. of
\mathbf{B}_{i}
and
\sigma_{i}
)

\displaystyle\leq c\cdot\bm{v}_{i}(x_{1})+dc\cdot(3c/4)^{i}\cdot\frac{\bm{v}_{0}(x_{1})}{4d}

(by
1

\bm{v}_{0}\in V
)

\displaystyle\leq c\cdot\bm{v}_{i}(x_{1})+dc\cdot\frac{\bm{v}_{i}(x_{1})}{4d}

(by
2
)

\displaystyle\leq 2c\cdot\bm{v}_{i}(x_{1}).

We may now prove the claim. Let

be sufficiently large so that

(3c/4)^{m}\geq 8cd
. We have
\bm{v}_{m}(x_{1})\geq(3c/4)^{m}\cdot\bm{v}_{0}(x_{1})\geq 8cd\cdot\bm{v}_{0}(x_{1})

by
2
and definition of
m
. Hence, since
c\geq 2
and
d\geq 1
, we have
\bm{v}_{m}(x_{1})\geq 2\cdot\bm{v}_{0}(x_{1})
, which satisfies the first part of the
claim. Moreover, the second part of the claim, namely
\bm{v}_{m}\in V
, holds since for every
y_{i,j}\in Y
, we have:

\displaystyle|\bm{v}_{m}(y_{i,j})|

\displaystyle=|\bm{v}_{i+1}(y_{i,j})|

(by
1
)

\displaystyle\leq 2c\cdot\bm{v}_{i}(x_{1})

(by
3
)

\displaystyle\leq 2c\cdot\frac{\bm{v}_{m}(x_{1})}{(3c/4)^{m-i}}

(by
\bm{v}_{m}(x_{1})\geq(3c/4)^{m-i}\cdot\bm{v}_{i}(x_{1})
from
2
)

\displaystyle=2c\cdot(3c/4)^{i}\cdot\frac{\bm{v}_{m}(x_{1})}{(3c/4)^{m}}

\displaystyle\leq 2c\cdot(3c/4)^{i}\cdot\frac{\bm{v}_{m}(x_{1})}{8cd}

(by def. of
m
)

\displaystyle=(3c/4)^{i}\cdot\frac{\bm{v}_{m}(x_{1})}{4d}.

We are done proving the lemma for the case
\mathbf{A}_{k,1}=c\geq 2
with
k=1
. This case is slightly simpler as
c
lies on the
main diagonal of
\mathbf{A}
which means that
\bm{v}_{i+1}(x_{1})\approx c\cdot\bm{v}_{i}(x_{1})
. If
k\neq 1
, then we have

\bm{v}_{i+1}(x_{k})\approx c\cdot\bm{v}_{i}(x_{1})

breaks composability for the next iteration. However, this is easily
fixed by swapping the names of counters

and

.

Let us fix a class
\mathcal{C}
such that
\lVert\mathcal{C}\rVert\geq 2
and the matrix

\mathbf{C}
obtained for
\mathcal{C}
from Lemma

. For

instead of

can encode binary strings. Let

for both

and every

. Let

be the identity function, and let

for every

Proposition 8
.

For every

, the following holds:
\gamma_{x}=\lambda_{|x|}+\sum_{i\in\llbracket x\rrbracket}\lambda_{|x|-i}

Proof 3.8
.

It suffices to show that

for every

. Let us prove this by induction on

and

\displaystyle f_{x}(\bm{e})

\displaystyle=\mathbf{C}\cdot f_{w}(\bm{e})+b\cdot\bm{e}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{C}\cdot\left(\mathbf{C}^{|w|}\cdot\bm{e}+\sum_{i\in\llbracket w\rrbracket}\mathbf{C}^{|w|-i}\cdot\bm{e}\right)+b\cdot\bm{e}

(by induction hypothesis)

\displaystyle=\left(\mathbf{C}^{|w|+1}\cdot\bm{e}+\sum_{i\in\llbracket w\rrbracket}\mathbf{C}^{|w|+1-i}\cdot\bm{e}\right)+b\cdot\bm{e}

\displaystyle=\left(\mathbf{C}^{|x|}\cdot\bm{e}+\sum_{i\in\llbracket x\rrbracket\setminus\{|x|\}}\mathbf{C}^{|x|-i}\cdot\bm{e}\right)+b\cdot\mathbf{C}^{|x|-|x|}\cdot\bm{e}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{C}^{|x|}\cdot\bm{e}+\sum_{i\in\llbracket x\rrbracket}\mathbf{C}^{|x|-i}\cdot\bm{e}

(by def. of

).

Proposition 9
.

For every

Proof 3.9
.

Let

Let us prove the claim. Let

be such that

. We either have

or

\displaystyle\gamma_{x}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{|x|}+\sum_{i\in\llbracket x\rrbracket}\lambda_{|x|-i}

(by Proposition

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{|x|}+\sum_{i=1}^{|x|}\lambda_{|x|}/2^{i}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{|x|}\cdot\left(1+\sum_{i=1}^{|x|}1/2^{i}\right)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{|x|}\cdot(2-1/2^{|x|})

\displaystyle<2\cdot\lambda_{|x|}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{|y|}

(by Lemma

(by Proposition

It remains to prove the case where

such that

and

. The proof is completed by

\displaystyle\gamma_{y}-\gamma_{x}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{\ell}+\sum_{i\in\llbracket w\rrbracket}\lambda_{\ell-i}-\sum_{i\in\llbracket v\rrbracket}\lambda_{\ell-i}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{\ell}\cdot\left(1-\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}1/2^{i}\right)

\displaystyle>0.

4.
A complexity dichotomy for reachability

This section is devoted to the following complexity dichotomy on

\textsc{Reach}_{\mathcal{C}}
, which is mostly proven by exploiting notions and results
from the previous section:

Theorem 12
.

The reachability problem
\textsc{Reach}_{\mathcal{C}}
is equivalent to the
(standard) VASS reachability problem if
\mathcal{C}
only contains
permutation matrices, and is undecidable otherwise.

4.1.
Decidability

Note that the (standard) VASS reachability problem is the problem

\textsc{Reach}_{\mathcal{I}}
where
\mathcal{I}\coloneqq\bigcup_{n\geq 1}\mathbf{I}_{n}
. Clearly
\textsc{Reach}_{\mathcal{I}}\leq\textsc{Reach}_{\mathcal{C}}
for any class

\mathcal{C}
. Therefore, it suffices to show the following:

Proposition 13
.

\textsc{Reach}_{\mathcal{C}}\leq\textsc{Reach}_{\mathcal{I}}
for every
\mathcal{C}
that only contains
permutation matrices.

Proof 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{V}=(d,Q,T)
be an affine VASS that belongs to
\mathcal{C}
. We
construct a (standard) VASS
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}=(d,Q^{\prime},T^{\prime})
that simulates

\mathcal{V}
. Recall that a (standard) VASS is an affine VASS that only uses
the identity matrix. For readability, we omit the identity matrix on
the transitions of
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
. We assume without loss of generality that
each transition
t\in T
satisfies either
\Delta(t)=\bm{0}
or

M(t)=\mathbf{I}
. Indeed, since permutation matrices are
nonnegative, every transition of
T
can be splitted in two parts:
first applying its matrix, and then its vector.

The control-states and transitions of
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
are defined as

Q^{\prime}\coloneqq\{q_{\sigma}:q\in Q,\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{d}\}
and
T^{\prime}\coloneqq S_{\bm{\circlearrowright}}\cup S_{\text{vec}}
, which are to be defined
shortly. Intuitively, each control-state of
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
stores the current
control-state of
\mathcal{V}
together with the current renaming of its
counters. Whenever a transition
t\in T
such that
\Delta(t)=\bm{0}
is to be applied, this means that the counters must be
renamed by the permutation
M(t)
. This is achieved
by:

S_{\bm{\circlearrowright}}\coloneqq\{(p_{\sigma},\bm{0},q_{\pi\circ\sigma}):(p,\mathbf{P}_{\pi},\bm{0},q)\in T,\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{d}\}.

Similarly, whenever a transition
t\in T

such that
M(t)=\mathbf{I}
is to be applied, this means that

\Delta(t)
should be added to the counters, but in accordance to the
current renaming of the counters. This is achieved by:

S_{\text{vec}}\coloneqq\{(p_{\sigma},\mathbf{P}_{\sigma}\cdot\bm{b},q_{\sigma}):(p,\mathbf{I},\bm{b},q),\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{d}\}.

A routine induction shows that

p(\bm{u})\xrightarrow{*}_{\mathbb{N}}q(\bm{v})\text{ in $\mathcal{V}$ iff }p_{\varepsilon}(\bm{u})\xrightarrow{*}_{\mathbb{N}}q_{\sigma}(\mathbf{P}_{\sigma}\cdot\bm{v})\text{ in $\mathcal{V}^{\prime}$},

where

\varepsilon
denotes the identity permutation. Since this amounts
to finitely many reachability queries, i.e.,
|\mathcal{S}_{d}|=d!
queries,
this yields a Turing reduction

6.
Conclusion and further work

Motivated by the use of relaxations to alleviate the tremendous
complexity of reachability in VASS, we have studied the complexity of
integer reachability in affine VASS.

Namely, we have shown a trichotomy on the complexity of integer
reachability for affine VASS: it is
\mathsf{NP}
-complete for any class of
reset matrices;
\mathsf{PSPACE}
-complete for any class of pseudo-transfers
matrices and any class of pseudo-copies matrices; and undecidable for

any
other class. Moreover, the notions and techniques
introduced along the way allowed us to give a complexity dichotomy for
(standard) reachability in affine VASS: it is decidable for any class
of permutation matrices, and undecidable for any other class. This
provides a complete general landscape of the complexity of
reachability in affine VASS.

We further complemented these trichotomy and dichotomy by showing
that, in contrast to the case of classes, the (integer or standard)
reachability problem has an arbitrary complexity when it is
parameterized by a fixed affine VASS. A further direction of study is
the range of possible complexities for integer reachability relations
for specific matrix monoids, which is entirely open.

Another direction lies in the related
coverability problem

which asks whether
p(\bm{u})\xrightarrow{*}_{\mathbb{D}}q(\bm{v}^{\prime})
for some

\bm{v}^{\prime}\geq\bm{v}
rather than
\bm{v}^{\prime}=\bm{v}
. As shown in
the setting of
[
HH14
,
CHH18
]
, this problem is equivalent to the
reachability problem for
\mathbb{D}=\mathbb{Z}
. Indeed, given an affine VASS
\mathcal{V}
,
it is possible to construct an affine VASS
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
such that

p(\bm{u})\xrightarrow{*}_{\mathbb{Z}}q(\bm{v})\text{ in }\mathcal{V}\iff p(\bm{u},-\bm{u})\xrightarrow{*}_{\mathbb{Z}}q(\bm{v},-\bm{v})\text{ in }\mathcal{V}^{\prime}.

(*)

This can be achieved by replacing each affine transformation

\mathbf{A}\bm{x}+\bm{b}
of
\mathcal{V}
by

\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{A}&\mathbf{0}\\
\mathbf{0}&\mathbf{A}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\bm{x}\\
\bm{x}^{\prime}\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}\bm{b}\\
-\bm{b}\end{bmatrix}.

Furthermore, note that classes are closed under this construction,
which shows that integer coverability and integer reachability are
equivalent w.r.t. classes. However, (
*
‣
6
) does not
hold for
\mathbb{D}=\mathbb{N}
. In this case, it is well-known,
e.g.
, that the
coverability problem is decidable for VASS with resets or transfers,
while the reachability problem is not. Hence, a precise
characterization of the complexity landscape remains unknown in this
case.

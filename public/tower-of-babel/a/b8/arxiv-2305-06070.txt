Abstract
. We analyze the long-time behavior of numerical schemes for a class of monotone stochastic partial differential equations (SPDEs) driven by multiplicative noise. By deriving several time-independent a priori estimates for the numerical solutions, combined with the ergodic theory of Markov processes, we establish the exponential ergodicity of these schemes with a unique invariant measure, respectively. Applying these results to the stochastic Allen–Cahn equation indicates that these schemes always have at least one invariant measure, respectively, and converge strongly to the exact solution with sharp time-independent rates. We also show that these numerical invariant measures are exponentially ergodic and thus give an affirmative answer to a question proposed in (J. Cui, J. Hong, and L. Sun, Stochastic Process. Appl. (2021): 55–93), provided that the interface thickness is not too small.

1.
Introduction

Recently, a lot of researchers investigated numerical analysis of the following SPDE under the homogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition in a finite time horizon:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&{\rm d}X(t,\xi)=(\Delta X(t,\xi)+f(X(t,\xi))){\rm d}t+g(X(t,\xi)){\rm d}W(t,\xi),\\
&X(t,\xi)=0,\quad(t,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\partial\mathscr{O};\\
&X(0,\xi)=X_{0}(\xi),\quad\xi\in\mathscr{O},\end{split}

where the physical domain
\mathscr{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
(
d=1,2,3
) is a bounded open set with smooth boundary.
Here,
f
is assumed to be of monotone type with polynomial growth,
g
satisfies the usual Lipschitz condition in an infinite-dimensional setting, and
W
is an infinite-dimensional Wiener process, see Section
2.1
.
Eq. (
1.1
) includes the following semiclassical stochastic Allen–Cahn equation, arising from phase transition in materials science by stochastic perturbation, as a special case:

(1.2)

\displaystyle{\rm d}X=\Delta X{\rm d}t+\alpha^{-2}(X-X^{3}){\rm d}t+{\rm d}W,\quad X(0)=X_{0},

where
\alpha>0
is the interface thickness; see, e.g.,
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
15
]
,
[
19
]
,
[
30
]
,
[
31
]
,
[
32
]
,
[
33
]
and references therein.

The numerical behavior in the infinite time horizon, especially the numerical ergodicity of SPDEs, including Eq. (
1.1
) (and Eq. (
1.2
)), is a natural and intriguing question.
As a significant asymptotic behavior, the ergodicity characterizes the case of temporal average coinciding with spatial average, which has vital applications in quantum mechanics, fluid dynamics, financial mathematics, and many other fields
[
17
]
,
[
20
]
.
The spatial average, i.e., the mean of a given function for the stationary law, called the invariant measure of diffusion, is also known as the ergodic limit, which is desirable to compute in many practical applications.
Then, one has to investigate a stochastic system over long time intervals, which is one of the main difficulties from the computational perspective.
Generally, the explicit expression of the invariant measure for a nonlinear infinite-dimensional stochastic system is rarely available.
Therefore, it is usually impossible to precisely compute the ergodic limit for a nonlinear SPDE driven by multiplicative noise; exceptional examples are gradient Langevin systems (driven by additive noise), see, e.g.,
[
5
]
,
[
23
]
.
For this reason, it motivated and fascinated a lot of investigations in the recent decade for constructing numerical algorithms that can inherit the ergodicity of the original system and approximate the ergodic limit efficiently.

In finite-dimensional case, much progress has been made in the design and analysis of numerical approximations of the desired ergodic limits; see, e.g.,
[
34
]
,
[
36
]
,
[
26
]
and references therein for numerical ergodicity of stochastic
ordinary differential equations (SODEs).
On the contrary, the construction and analysis of numerical ergodic limits for SPDEs are still in their early stages.
The authors in
[
6
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
10
]
firstly studied Galerkin-based linear implicit Euler scheme and high order integrator to approximate the invariant measures of a parabolic SPDE driven by additive white noise; see also
[
13
]
for the ergodic limit of a spectral Galerkin modified implicit Euler scheme of the damping stochastic Schrödinger equation driven by additive trace-class noise.
Recently, exponential Euler-type schemes of full discretization and tamed semi-discretization were used in
[
14
]
and
[
7
]
, respectively, to approximate the invariant measure of a stochastic Allen–Cahn type equation driven by additive colored noise.
The authors in
[
16
]
investigated the spectral Galerkin drift implicit Euler (DIE) scheme to approximate the invariant measure of the same type of equation and proposed a question of whether the invariant measure of the temporal DIE scheme is unique.
Almost all of the above literature focuses on the numerical ergodicity of SPDEs driven by additive noise; the numerical ergodicity in the multiplicative noise case is more subtle and challenging.

Apart from the approximation of the invariant measure, the strong approximation, i.e., in the moments’ sense, is also an important issue.
There exists a general theory of strong error analysis of numerical approximations for Lipschitz SPDEs in a finite time horizon; see, e.g.,
[
1
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
21
]
and references therein.
Recently, we
[
32
]
give a theory of strong error analysis of numerical approximations for monotone SPDEs in a finite time horizon.
Whether there is a theory of strong error analysis in the infinite time horizon is a natural question.

The above two questions on long-time behaviors of numerical approximations for Eq. (
1.1
) motivate the present study.
On the one hand, we aim to establish the exponential ergodicity of the numerical schemes studied in
[
19
]
,
[
32
]
,
[
33
]
for Eq. (
1.1
).
It should be pointed out that there is no further restriction on the time step size for the DIE scheme (
DIE
) in addition to the usual requirement of the implicit scheme (see Theorem
3.1
); see
[
26
]
for an upper bound requirement on the time step size for numerical ergodicity of monotone SODEs.
Meanwhile, applying one of our main results, Theorem
3.1
, we conclude that the DIE scheme (
DIE
) is unique ergodic (and exponentially mixing) and thus give an affirmative answer to the question proposed in
[
16
]
(see Remark
4.1
), provided that the monotone coefficient of the drift function is not too large.
On the other hand, we develop a time-independent strong error analysis theory for these fully discrete schemes, see Theorem
3.3
, which generalizes the main results of
[
32
]
to the infinite time horizon.

The paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2

1.1
).
These uniform estimates are utilized in Section
3
to establish the exponential ergodicity of the proposed semi-discrete and fully discrete schemes.
In Section

Finally, the main results in Section

1.2

2.
Uniform A Priori Estimates and Exponential Ergodicity of Monotone SPDEs

In this section, we first present the required preliminaries and main assumptions that will be used throughout the paper.
Then, we derive several necessary uniform a priori estimates of Eq. (
1.1
).

2.1.
Main Assumptions

Denote
a\vee b:=\max(a,b)
and
a\wedge b:=\min(a,b)
for
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
.
For
r\in[2,\infty]
and
\theta=-1
or
1
, we use the notations
(L^{r}=L^{r}(\mathscr{O}),\|\cdot\|_{L^{r}})
and
(\dot{H}^{\theta}=\dot{H}^{\theta}(\mathscr{O}),\|\cdot\|_{\theta})
to denote the usual Lebesgue and Sobolev interpolation spaces, respectively.
In particular, we denote
H:=L^{2}
,
V:=\dot{H}^{1}
, and
V^{*}:=\dot{H}^{-1}
.
The space
H
is equipped with Borel
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{B}(H)
and with the inner product and norm given by
\|\cdot\|
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
, respectively.
The norms in
\dot{H}^{1}
and
\dot{H}^{-1}=(\dot{H}^{1})^{*}
and the duality product between them are denoted by
\|\cdot\|_{1}
,
\|\cdot\|_{-1}
, and
{}_{1}\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{-1}
, respectively.
We use
\mathcal{C}_{b}(H)
and
{\rm Lip}_{b}(H)
to denote the class of bounded, continuous, and Lipschitz continuous, respectively, functions on
H
.
For
\phi\in{\rm Lip}_{b}(H)
, define

|\phi|_{\rm Lip}:=\sup_{x\neq y}|\phi(x)-\phi(y)|/\|x-y\|
.

Let
{\bf Q}
be a self-adjoint and positive definite linear operator on
H
.
Denote
(H_{0}:=Q^{1/2}H,\|\cdot\|_{H_{0}}:=\|Q^{-1/2}\cdot\|)
and denote by

(\mathcal{L}_{2}^{0}:=HS(H_{0};H),\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}^{0}})
and
(\mathcal{L}_{2}^{1}:=HS(H_{0};\dot{H}^{1}),\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}^{1}})
the space of Hilbert–Schmidt operators from
H_{0}
to
H
and
\dot{H}^{1}
, respectively.
Let
W
be an
H
-valued
Q
-Wiener process on a filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{F}:=(\mathscr{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
, which has the Karhunen–Loève expansion

W(t)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\sqrt{q_{k}}g_{k}\beta_{k}(t),\quad t\geq 0.

Here
\{g_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
forming an orthonormal basis of
H
are the eigenvectors of
Q
subject to the eigenvalues
\{q_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
, and
\{\beta_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
are mutually independent one-dimensional Brownian motions in
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{F},\mathbb{P})
.
We mainly focus on trace-class noise, i.e.,
Q
is a trace-class operator, or equivalently,
{\rm Trace}({\bf Q}):=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}q_{k}<\infty
.

Our main conditions on the coefficients of Eq. (
1.1
) are the following two assumptions.

Assumption 2.1
.

f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is differentiable and there exist scalars
L_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
,
i=1,2,3,4,5
, and
q\geq 1
such that

(2.1)

\displaystyle(f(\xi)-f(\eta))(\xi-\eta)\leq L_{1}(\xi-\eta)^{2},\quad\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R},

(2.2)

\displaystyle f(\xi)\xi\leq L_{2}|\xi|^{2}+L_{3},\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R},

(2.3)

\displaystyle|f^{\prime}(\xi)|\leq L_{4}|\xi|^{q-1}+L_{5},\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}.

Example 2.1
.

A concrete example such that all the conditions in Assumption
2.1
holds is an odd polynomial (with order
q
and) with a negative leading coefficient.

By the condition (
2.1
) and the differentiability of
f
, it is clear that

(2.4)

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(\xi)\leq L_{1},\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}.

We also note that one can always assume that
L_{1}\leq L_{2}
, as one can take
L_{2}=L_{1}+\varepsilon
in (
2.2
) from (
2.1
) and Young inequality; here and in the rest of the paper,
\varepsilon
denotes an arbitrarily small positive constant which would be different in each appearance.

Define by
F:L^{q+1}\rightarrow L^{(q+1)^{\prime}}
the Nemytskii operator associated with
f
:

(2.5)

\displaystyle F(x)(\xi):=f(x(\xi)),\quad x\in\dot{H}^{1},\ \xi\in\mathscr{O},

where
(q+1)^{\prime}
denotes the conjugation of
q+1
, i.e.,
1/(q+1)^{\prime}+1/(q+1)=1
.
Then it follows from the monotonicity condition (
2.1
) and the coercivity condition (
2.2
) that the operator
F
defined in (
2.5
) has a continuous extension from
L^{q+1}
to
L^{(q+1)^{\prime}}
and satisfies

(2.6)

{}_{L^{q+1}}\langle x-y,F(x)-F(y)\rangle_{L^{(q+1)^{\prime}}}\leq L_{1}\|x-y\|^{2},

\displaystyle\quad x,y\in L^{q+1},

(2.7)

{}_{L^{q+1}}\langle x,F(x)\rangle_{L^{(q+1)^{\prime}}}\leq L_{2}\|x\|^{2}+L_{3},

\displaystyle\quad x\in L^{q+1},

where
{}_{L^{(q+1)^{\prime}}}\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{L^{q+1}}
denotes the duality product between
L^{(q+1)^{\prime}}
and
L^{q+1}
.

Throughout, we assume that
q\geq 1
when
d=1,2
and
q\in[1,3]
when
d=3
,
so that the following frequently used Sobolev embeddings hold:

(2.8)

\displaystyle\dot{H}^{1}\subset L^{2q}\subset L^{q+1}\subset H\subset L^{(q+1)^{\prime}}\subset L^{(2q)^{\prime}}\subset\dot{H}^{-1}.

In this case, (
2.6
) and (
2.7
) can be expressed as

(2.9)

{}_{1}\langle x-y,F(x)-F(y)\rangle_{-1}\leq L_{1}\|x-y\|^{2},

\displaystyle\quad x,y\in\dot{H}^{1},

(2.10)

{}_{1}\langle x,F(x)\rangle_{-1}\leq L_{2}\|x\|^{2}+L_{3},

\displaystyle\quad x\in\dot{H}^{1}.

The inequalities (
2.9
) and (
2.10
), in combination with the Poincaré inequality that

(2.11)

\displaystyle\|\nabla x\|^{2}\geq\lambda_{1}\|x\|^{2},\quad x\in\dot{H}^{1},

where
\lambda_{1}
denotes the first eigenvalue of the Dirichlet Laplacian operator, denoted by
A
with domain
{\rm Dom}(A)=H^{2}\cap\dot{H}^{1}
, implies that

{}_{1}\langle x-y,A(x-y)+F(x)-F(y)\rangle_{-1}

(2.12)

\displaystyle\leq-(\lambda_{1}-L_{1})\|x-y\|^{2},\quad x,y\in\dot{H}^{1},

and that

(2.13)

{}_{1}\langle x,Ax+F(x)\rangle_{-1}

\displaystyle\leq-(\lambda_{1}-L_{2})\|x\|^{2}+L_{3},\quad x\in\dot{H}^{1}.

Similarly, noting that
L_{1}\leq L_{2}
, we have

(2.14)

\displaystyle\langle x,Ax+F(x)\rangle_{1}

\displaystyle\leq-(\lambda_{1}-L_{2})\|x\|_{1}^{2}+L_{3},\quad x\in\dot{H}^{2}.

Denote by
G:H\rightarrow\mathcal{L}_{2}^{0}
the Nemytskii operator associated with
g:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
:

(2.15)

\displaystyle G(x)g_{k}(\xi):=g(x(\xi))g_{k}(\xi),\quad x\in H,~k\in\mathbb{N},~\xi\in\mathscr{O}.

Our primary condition on the diffusion operator
G
defined in (
2.15
) is the following usual Lipschitz continuity and linear growth conditions.

Assumption 2.2
.

There exist nonnegative constants
L_{6}
,
L_{7}
, and
L_{8}
such that

(2.16)

\displaystyle\|G(x)-G(y)\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}\leq L_{6}\|x-y\|^{2},

\displaystyle\quad x,y\in H,

(2.17)

\displaystyle\|G(x)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{L}_{2}^{0}}\leq L_{7}\|x\|^{2}+L_{8},

\displaystyle\quad x\in H.

Moreover,
G(\dot{H}^{1})\subset\mathcal{L}_{2}^{1}
and there exist nonnegative constants
L_{9}
and
L_{10}
such that

(2.18)

\displaystyle\|G(z)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{L}_{2}^{1}}\leq L_{9}\|z\|^{2}_{1}+L_{10},\quad z\in\dot{H}^{1}.

Remark 2.1
.

In the additive noise case
G\equiv{\rm Id}
, the identity operator in
H
, the conditions (
2.16
)–(
2.18
) reduce to the assumption
\|(-A)^{1/2}{\bf Q}^{1/2}\|_{HS(H;H)}<\infty
which was imposed in
[
35
]
and
[
16
]
; see
[
32
, Remark 2.2]
for an example of multiplicative noise case.

With these preliminaries, (
1.1
) is equivalent to the following infinite-dimensional stochastic evolution equation:

(2.19)

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\rm d}X_{t}&=(AX_{t}+F(X_{t})){\rm d}t+G(X_{t}){\rm d}W,\quad t\geq 0;\\
X(0)&=X_{0},\end{split}

where the random initial datum
X_{0}
is assumed to vanish on the boundary
\partial\mathscr{O}
of the physical domain throughout the present paper.

2.2.
Uniform Moments’ Estimates of The Exact Solution

The existence of a unique variational solution of Eq. (
2.19
) in
H
and
\dot{H}^{1}
was shown in
[
12
]
or
[
22
]
and
[
25
]
, respectively, under similar conditions as in Assumptions
2.1
–
2.2
.
This solution is also the mild solution of (
2.19
) in
[
32
]
, where the author also derived that the solution is indeed continuous a.s. and satisfies certain moments’ estimates in a finite time horizon.

Taking advantage of the dissipativity conditions (
2.13
) and (
2.14
), we can show the following uniform stability estimate both in
H
and
\dot{H}^{1}
of Eq. (
2.19
).
Throughout, we assume without loss of generality that the initial datum
X_{0}
of Eq. (
2.19
) is a deterministic function;
X_{t}^{x}
denotes the solution of Eq. (
2.19
) starting from
X_{0}=x
.
We will frequently use
C
to denote generic time-independent constants independent of various discrete parameters that would differ in each appearance.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
p\geq 2
,
X_{0}\in H
, Assumption
2.1
and conditions (
2.16
)–(
2.17
) hold.
Assume that
L_{2}+\frac{p-1}{2}L_{7}<\lambda_{1}
.
Then there exist positive constants
\gamma_{1}
and
C_{\gamma_{1}}
such that

(2.20)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|X_{t}\|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\gamma_{1}t}\|X_{0}\|^{p}+C_{\gamma_{1}},\quad t\geq 0.

Moreover, if
X_{0}\in\dot{H}^{1}
,
L_{2}+\frac{p-1}{2}L_{9}<\lambda_{1}
,
and condition (
2.18
) hold, then there exist positive constants
\gamma_{2}
and
C_{\gamma_{2}}
such that

(2.21)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|X_{t}\|^{p}_{1}

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\gamma_{2}t}\|X_{0}\|^{p}_{1}+C_{\gamma_{2}},\quad t\geq 0.

Proof.

Applying Itô formula (see, e.g.,
[
27
, Theorem 4.2.5]
) to the functional
\|X\|^{p}
leads to

\displaystyle{\rm d}\|X\|^{p}

\displaystyle=\Big[\frac{p(p-2)}{2}\|X\|^{p-4}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}~\langle X,G(X){\bf Q}^{\frac{1}{2}}g_{k}\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{p}{2}\|X\|^{p-2}\Big(2~_{1}\langle X,AX+F(X)\rangle_{-1}+\|G(X)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{L}_{2}^{0}}\Big)\Big]{\rm d}t

(2.22)

\displaystyle\quad+p\|X\|^{p-2}\langle X,G(X){\rm d}W\rangle.

We omit the temporal variable when an integral appears here and after to lighten the notations.
For
\gamma_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
, it follows from the product rule that

\displaystyle e^{\gamma_{1}t}\|X_{t}\|^{p}-\|X_{0}\|^{p}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}e^{\gamma_{1}r}\Big[\frac{p(p-2)}{2}\|X\|^{p-4}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}\langle X,G(X){\bf Q}^{\frac{1}{2}}g_{k}\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{p}{2}\|X\|^{p-2}(2~_{1}\langle X,AX+F(X)\rangle_{-1}+\|G(X)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{L}_{2}^{0}})+\gamma_{1}\|X\|^{p}\Big]{\rm d}r

(2.23)

\displaystyle\quad+p\int_{0}^{t}e^{\gamma_{1}r}\|X\|^{p-2}\langle X,G(X){\rm d}W\rangle.

By the condition (
2.17
) and Young inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|X\|^{p-4}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}\langle X,G(X){\bf Q}^{\frac{1}{2}}g_{k}\rangle^{2}\leq(L_{7}+\varepsilon)\|X\|^{p}+C_{\varepsilon},

\displaystyle\|X\|^{p-2}\|G(X)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{L}_{2}^{0}}\leq(L_{7}+\varepsilon)\|X\|^{p}+C_{\varepsilon},

for
X\in H
.
Combining the above two estimates and (
2.13
), taking expectation on Eq. (
2.2
), and noting that
\int_{0}^{t}e^{\gamma_{1}r}\|X\|^{p-2}\langle X,G(X){\rm d}W\rangle
is a (real-valued) martingale, we obtain

(2.24)

\displaystyle e^{\gamma_{1}t}\mathbb{E}\|X_{t}\|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\|X_{0}\|^{p}-\gamma_{1}^{*}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\gamma_{1}r}\mathbb{E}\|X\|^{p}{\rm d}r+C_{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\gamma_{1}r}{\rm d}r,

with
\gamma_{1}^{*}:=p(\lambda_{1}-L_{2}-\frac{p-1}{2}(L_{7}+\varepsilon))-\gamma_{1}
.
As
L_{2}+\frac{p-1}{2}L_{7}<\lambda_{1}
, one can take
\varepsilon>0
and
\gamma_{1}>0
to be sufficiently small such that
\gamma_{1}^{*}>0
.
Then it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|X_{t}\|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\gamma_{1}t}\|X_{0}\|^{p}+C_{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\gamma_{1}r}{\rm d}r,

from which we obtain (
2.20
).

Now let
X_{0}\in\dot{H}^{1}
.
Applying Itô formula to the functional
e^{\gamma_{1}t}\|X_{t}\|^{p}_{1}
leads to

\displaystyle e^{\gamma_{2}t}\|X_{t}\|^{p}_{1}-\|X_{0}\|^{p}_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}e^{\gamma_{2}r}\Big[\frac{p(p-2)}{2}\|X\|_{1}^{p-4}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}\langle X,G(X){\bf Q}^{\frac{1}{2}}g_{k}\rangle_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{p}{2}\|X\|_{1}^{p-2}(2\langle X,AX+F(X)\rangle_{1}+\|G(X)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{L}_{2}^{1}})+\gamma_{2}\|X\|^{p}_{1}\Big]{\rm d}r

\displaystyle\quad+p\int_{0}^{t}e^{\gamma_{2}r}\|X\|_{1}^{p-2}\langle X,G(X){\rm d}W\rangle_{1}.

By the conditions (
2.16
) and (
2.18
), we have

\displaystyle\|X\|_{1}^{p-4}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}\langle X,G(X){\bf Q}^{\frac{1}{2}}g_{k}\rangle_{1}^{2}\leq(L_{9}+\varepsilon)\|X\|_{1}^{p}+C_{\varepsilon},\quad u\in\dot{H}^{1},

\displaystyle\|X\|_{1}^{p-2}\|G(X)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{L}_{2}^{1}}\leq(L_{9}+\varepsilon)\|X\|_{1}^{p}+C_{\varepsilon},\quad u\in\dot{H}^{1}.

Using techniques similar to those used to establish (
2.24
), together with the above two estimates, (
2.14
), and the fact that
\int_{0}^{t}e^{\gamma_{2}r}\|X\|_{1}^{p-2}\langle X,G(X){\rm d}W\rangle_{1}
is a martingale, implies that

\displaystyle e^{\gamma_{2}t}\mathbb{E}\|X_{t}\|^{p}_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\|X_{0}\|^{p}_{1}-\gamma_{2}^{*}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\gamma_{2}r}\mathbb{E}\|X\|_{1}^{p}{\rm d}r+C_{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\gamma_{2}r}{\rm d}r,

with
\gamma_{2}^{*}:=p(\lambda_{1}-L_{2}-\frac{p-1}{2}(L_{9}+\varepsilon))-\gamma_{2}>0
for some sufficiently small and positive
\varepsilon
and
\gamma_{2}
, as
L_{2}+\frac{p-1}{2}L_{9}<\lambda_{1}
.
Then it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|X_{t}\|_{1}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\gamma_{2}t}\|X_{0}\|^{p}+C_{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\gamma_{2}(t-r)}{\rm d}r,

from which we conclude (
2.21
).
∎

As a by-product of the uniform stability estimate (
2.21
), we have the following uniform temporal Hölder continuity estimate; see

Corollary 2.1
.

Let
p\geq 2
,
X_{0}\in\dot{H}^{1}
, and Assumptions
2.1
–
2.2
hold.
Assume that
L_{1}+\frac{p-1}{2}L_{9}<\lambda_{1}
.
Then, there exists a constant
C
such that

(2.25)

\displaystyle\sup_{t>s\geq 0}\frac{\mathbb{E}\|X_{t}-X_{s}\|^{p}}{(t-s)^{p/2}}\leq C(1+\|X_{0}\|_{1}^{pq}).

Proof.

For any
t>s\geq 0
, it was shown in
[
32
, Theorem 3.2]
that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|X_{t}-X_{s}\|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C(t-s)^{p/2}(1+\|X_{0}\|_{1}^{p}+\sup_{t\geq 0}\mathbb{E}\|F(X_{t})\|^{p}+\sup_{t\geq 0}\mathbb{E}\|G(X_{t})\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}^{0}}^{p}).

This estimate, together with the conditions (
2.3
), (
2.17
), the embeddings (
2.8
), and the stability estimate (
2.21
), implies the desired estimate (
2.25
).
∎

2.3.
Exponential Ergodicity of The Exact Solution

It is clear that the solution of Eq. (
2.19
) is a Markov process, see, e.g.,
[
27
, Proposition 4.3.5]
, so we define the family of Markov semigroup
\{P_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
associated with Eq. (
2.19
) as

\displaystyle(P_{t}\phi)(x):=\mathbb{E}\phi(X_{t}^{x}),\quad x\in H,~t\geq 0.

A probability measure
\mu
on
H
is called invariant for
\{P_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{I}}
with either
\mathbb{I}=\mathbb{R}_{+}
or
\mathbb{I}=\mathbb{N}
, if

\displaystyle\int_{H}P_{t}\phi(x)\mu({\rm d}x)=\mu(\phi):=\int_{H}\phi(x)\mu({\rm d}x),\quad\phi\in\mathcal{C}_{b}(H),\ t\in\mathbb{I}.

An invariant (probability) measure
\mu
is called ergodic for
\{P_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
(or Eq. (
2.19
)) or
\{P_{m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
if for all
\phi\in L^{2}(H;\mu)
, it holds, respectively, that

\displaystyle\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}P_{t}\phi{\rm d}t\Big(\text{or}~\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k=0}^{m}P_{k}\phi\Big)=\mu(\phi)\quad\text{in}~L^{2}(H;\mu).

The following unique ergodicity of Eq. (
2.19
), with an exponential convergence to the equilibrium, was proved using a remote initial condition method in
[
27
, Theorem 4.3.9]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let Assumption
2.1
and conditions (
2.16
)-(
2.17
) hold.
Assume that
(L_{1}+L_{6}/2)\vee(L_{2}+L_{7}/2)<\lambda_{1}
.
Then a unique invariant measure
\pi
in
H
exists for Eq. (
2.19
).
Moreover, for any
t\geq 0
,
x\in H
, and
\phi\in{\rm Lip}_{b}(H)
, there exist a positive constant
\gamma_{3}
such that

(2.26)

\displaystyle|P_{t}\phi(x)-\pi(\phi)|\leq\exp(-\gamma_{3}t/2)|\phi|_{\rm Lip}\int_{H}\|x-y\|\pi({\rm d}y).

Remark 2.2
.

A Markov semigroup
\{P_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{I}}
with
\mathbb{I}=\mathbb{R}_{+}
or
\mathbb{I}=\mathbb{N}
satisfying an exponential convergence to the equilibrium, like (
2.26
), is called exponentially mixing.
It is clear that once
\{P_{t}\}
is exponentially mixing for the invariant measure
\mu
, then the following strong law of large numbers (implying the ergodicity) holds:

\displaystyle\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}P_{t}\phi(x){\rm d}t~\Big(\text{or}~\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k=0}^{m}P_{k}\phi(x)\Big)=\mu(\phi),\quad\forall~x\in H,~\mu\text{-a.s.}

3.
Exponential Ergodicity and Uniform Estimates of Galerkin-based Euler Scheme

This section will first establish the exponential ergodicity of the drift-implicit Euler (DIE) semi-discrete and Galerkin-based fully discrete schemes.
Then, we will derive uniform, strong error estimates of this fully discrete scheme.
At the end of this section, some remarks on the generalization to the Milstein-type scheme and its Galerkin discretization are given.

3.1.
Exponential Ergodicity of DIE Scheme

Let
\tau\in(0,1)
. For an

, define
t_{m}:=m\tau
.
We discrete Eq. (
2.19
) in time with a DIE scheme, and the resulting temporal semi-discrete problem is to find an
\dot{H}^{1}
-valued Markov chain

such that

(DIE)

\displaystyle X_{m+1}=X_{m}+\tau AX_{m+1}+\tau F(X_{m+1})+G(X_{m})\delta_{m}W,

starting from
X_{0}\in H
, where
\delta_{m}W=W(t_{m+1})-W(t_{m})
,

.
This DIE scheme had been widely studied; see, e.g.,
[
16
]
,
[
18
]
,
[
32
]
,
[
35
]
, and references therein.
Throughout this part we assume that

with

[

We begin with the following uniform moments’ stability of the above DIE scheme (
DIE
).
It should be pointed out that there is no further restriction on the time step size

; see
[
26
, Theorems 4.1 and 4.2]
for an upper bound requirement on

for the numerical ergodicity of monotone SODEs.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
X_{0}\in H
, Assumption
2.1
and conditions (
2.16
)-(
2.17
) hold, and
\{X_{m}:m\in\mathbb{N}\}
be the solution of the DIE scheme (
DIE
) starting from
X_{0}
.
Assume that
L_{2}+L_{7}/2<\lambda_{1}
.
Then there exist positive constants
\gamma_{5}
,
C_{\gamma_{5}}
, and
C
such that for any
\tau\in(0,1)
,

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|X_{m}\|^{2}\leq e^{-\gamma_{5}t_{m}}\|X_{0}\|^{2}+C_{\gamma_{5}},\quad m\in\mathbb{N},

(3.2)

\displaystyle\sup_{m\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\mathbb{E}\|X_{k}\|_{1}^{2}\leq C(1+\|X_{0}\|^{2}).

Proof.

For simplicity, set
F_{k}=F(X_{k})
and
G_{k}=G(X_{k})
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Testing (
DIE
) using
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
-inner product with
X_{k+1}
and using the elementary equality

(3.3)

\displaystyle 2\langle x-y,x\rangle=\|x\|^{2}-\|y\|^{2}+\|x-y\|^{2},\quad x,y\in H,

we have

\displaystyle\|X_{k+1}\|^{2}-\|X_{k}\|^{2}+\|X_{k+1}-X_{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=2~_{1}\langle X_{k+1},\Delta X_{k+1}+F_{k+1}\rangle_{-1}\tau

(3.4)

\displaystyle\quad+2\langle X_{k+1}-X_{k},G_{k}\delta_{k}W\rangle+2\langle X_{k},G_{k}\delta_{k}W\rangle.

By the estimate (
2.13
) and Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|X_{k+1}\|^{2}-\|X_{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq-2(\lambda_{1}-L_{2})\|X_{k+1}\|^{2}\tau+\|G_{k}\delta_{k}W\|^{2}+2\langle X_{k},G_{k}\delta_{k}W\rangle+2L_{3}\tau.

It follows that

\displaystyle(1+2(\lambda_{1}-L_{2})\tau)\|X_{k+1}\|^{2}\leq\|X_{k}\|^{2}+\|G_{k}\delta_{k}W\|^{2}+2\langle X_{k},G_{k}\delta_{k}W\rangle+2L_{3}\tau.

Taking expectations on both sides, noting the fact that both
G_{k}
and
X_{k}
are independent of
\delta_{k}W
, using Itô isometry and (
2.17
), we get

\displaystyle(1+2(\lambda_{1}-L_{2})\tau)\mathbb{E}\|X_{k+1}\|^{2}\leq(1+L_{7}\tau)\mathbb{E}\|X_{k}\|^{2}+(2L_{3}+L_{8})\tau,

from which we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|X_{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\frac{1+L_{7}\tau}{1+2(\lambda_{1}-L_{2})\tau}\Big)^{k}\|X_{0}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{(2L_{3}+L_{8})\tau}{1+2(\lambda_{1}-L_{2})\tau}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\Big(\frac{1+L_{7}\tau}{1+2(\lambda_{1}-L_{2})\tau}\Big)^{j},\quad k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}.

Note that

(3.5)

\displaystyle a^{k}<e^{-(1-a)k},\quad\forall~a\in(0,1),

and that
\frac{1+L_{7}\tau}{1+2(\lambda_{1}-L_{2})\tau}<1
for any
\tau\in(0,1)
, which is ensured by the condition
L_{2}+L_{7}/2<\lambda_{1}
, we conclude (
3.1
) with

\gamma_{5}:=\frac{2(\lambda_{1}-L_{2})-L_{7}}{1+2(\lambda_{1}-L_{2})\tau}
and

C_{\gamma_{5}}:=\frac{2L_{3}+L_{8}}{2(\lambda_{1}-L_{2})-L_{7}}
.

Subtracting
2\varepsilon~_{1}\langle X_{k+1},\Delta X_{k+1}\rangle_{-1}\tau
, with small
\varepsilon>0
, from the first term on the right-hand side in Eq. (
3.1
), and using the arguments in the above, we obtain

\displaystyle(1+2(\lambda_{1}-L_{2}-\varepsilon)\tau)\mathbb{E}\|X_{k+1}\|^{2}+2\varepsilon\mathbb{E}\|\nabla X_{k+1}\|^{2}\tau

\displaystyle\leq(1+L_{7}\tau)\mathbb{E}\|X_{k}\|^{2}+(2L_{3}+L_{8})\tau,

from which we derive

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|X_{k}\|^{2}+\frac{2\varepsilon}{1+2(\lambda_{1}-L_{2}-\varepsilon)\tau}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\mathbb{E}\|\nabla X_{j}\|^{2}\tau

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\frac{1+L_{7}\tau}{1+2(\lambda_{1}-L_{2}-\varepsilon)\tau}\Big)^{k}\|X_{0}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{(2L_{3}+L_{8})\tau}{1+2(\lambda_{1}-L_{2})\tau}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\Big(\frac{1+L_{7}\tau}{1+2(\lambda_{1}-L_{2})\tau}\Big)^{j},\quad k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}.

Then we conclude (
3.2
) by the above estimate, the elementary estimate (
3.5
), and (
3.1
).
∎

In the discrete case, there is no Itô formula anymore.
Whether
\{e^{\gamma t_{m}}\|X_{m}^{x}-X_{m}^{y}\|^{2}:m\in\mathbb{N}\}
, for some

\{X_{m}^{x}:m\in\mathbb{N}\}
and

are denoted by the solution of the DIE scheme (

Fortunately, using the monotonicity conditions in Assumptions
2.1
-

, one can show the following discrete uniform continuity dependence estimate similar to the finite-dimensional case in
[
24
, Theorem 7.6]
or
[

, Lemma 4.2]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
x,y\in H
, Assumption
2.1
and conditions (
2.16
)-(
2.17
) hold.
Assume that
L_{1}+L_{6}/2<\lambda_{1}
.
Then there exist a positive constant
\gamma_{6}
such that for any
\tau\in(0,1)
,

(3.6)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|X_{m}^{x}-X_{m}^{y}\|^{2}\leq e^{-\gamma_{6}t_{m}}\|x-y\|^{2},\quad m\in\mathbb{N}.

Consequently,
\{P_{m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}
is Feller, i.e., if
\phi\in\mathcal{C}_{b}(H)
, then
P_{m}\phi\in\mathcal{C}_{b}(H)
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
(or equivalently,
P_{1}\phi\in\mathcal{C}_{b}(H)
).

Proof.

For
k\in\mathbb{N}
, set
F_{k}^{x}=F(X_{k}^{x})
,
F_{k}^{y}=F(X_{k}^{y})
,
G_{k}^{x}=G(X_{k}^{x})
,
G_{k}^{y}=G(X_{k}^{y})
, and
E_{k}=X_{k}^{x}-X_{k}^{y}
.
Using the same technique in the proof of Lemma
3.1
to derive (
3.1
), we have

\displaystyle\|E_{k+1}\|^{2}-\|E_{k}\|^{2}+\|E_{k+1}-E_{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=2~_{1}\langle E_{k+1},\Delta E_{k+1}+F^{x}_{k+1}-F^{y}_{k+1}\rangle_{-1}\tau

\displaystyle\quad+2\langle E_{k+1}-E_{k},(G^{x}_{k}-G^{y}_{k})\delta_{k}W\rangle+2\langle E_{k},(G^{x}_{k}-G^{y}_{k})\delta_{k}W\rangle.

It follows from the estimate (
2.12
) and Cauchy–Schwarz inequality that

\displaystyle\|E_{k+1}\|^{2}-\|E_{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq-2(\lambda_{1}-L_{1})\|E_{k+1}\|^{2}\tau+\|(G^{x}_{k}-G^{y}_{k})\delta_{k}W\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+2\langle E_{k},(G^{x}_{k}-G^{y}_{k})\delta_{k}W\rangle.

Taking expectations on both sides, noting the fact that both
G^{x}_{k}-G^{y}_{k}
and
X_{k}
are independent of
\delta_{k}W
, using Itô isometry and (
2.16
), we obtain

\displaystyle(1+2(\lambda_{1}-L_{1})\tau)\mathbb{E}\|E_{k+1}\|^{2}\leq(1+L_{6}\tau)\mathbb{E}\|E_{k}\|^{2},

from which we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|E_{k}\|^{2}\leq\Big(\frac{1+L_{6}\tau}{1+2(\lambda_{1}-L_{1})\tau}\Big)^{k}\|x-y\|^{2},\quad k\in\mathbb{N}.

Then we conclude (
3.6
) with

\gamma_{6}:=\frac{2(\lambda_{1}-L_{1})-L_{6}}{2(\lambda_{1}-L_{1})+1}
by the above estimate and (
3.5
).
∎

We can show that the DIE scheme (
DIE
) is ergodic with a unique invariant measure.
Moreover, it converges exponentially to the equilibrium.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let Assumption
2.1
and conditions (
2.16
)-(
2.17
) hold.
Assume that
(L_{1}+L_{6}/2)\vee(L_{2}+L_{7}/2)<\lambda_{1}
.
Then for any
\tau\in(0,1)
, the DIE scheme (
DIE
) possesses a unique invariant measure
\pi_{\tau}
in
H
which is exponentially mixing:

(3.7)

\displaystyle|P_{m}\phi(x)-\pi_{\tau}(\phi)|\leq\exp(-\gamma_{6}t_{m}/2)|\phi|_{\rm Lip}\int_{H}\|x-y\|\pi_{\tau}({\rm d}y),

for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
x\in H
, and
\phi\in{\rm Lip}_{b}(H)
, where
\gamma_{6}
appeared at (
3.6
).

Proof.

Define a sequence
\{\mu^{\tau}_{m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}
of probability measures by

\displaystyle\mu^{\tau}_{m}(B):=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k=0}^{m}P_{k}\chi_{B}(0)dt,\quad B\in\mathcal{B}(H),~m\in\mathbb{N}_{+}.

By Chebyshev inequality and the temporal average estimate (
3.2
) (with
X_{0}=0
),
for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a positive scalar
I_{\varepsilon}
and a compact set
B=\{x\in H:~\|x\|_{1}\leq I_{\varepsilon}\}\subset\mathcal{B}(H)
such that

\displaystyle\mu^{\tau}_{m}(B^{c})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\mathbb{P}(\|X_{k}^{0}\|_{1}>I_{\varepsilon})dt\leq\frac{I^{-2}_{\varepsilon}}{m}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\mathbb{E}\|X_{k}^{0}\|_{1}^{2}\leq CI^{-2}_{\varepsilon}<\varepsilon,

from which we have that
\{\mu^{\tau}_{m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}
is tight.
Then we conclude the existence of an invariant measure
\pi_{\tau}
of
\{P_{m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
with
\pi_{\tau}(\|\cdot\|^{2})<\infty
, via Krylov–Bogoliubov theorem (see, e.g.,
[
20
, Theorem 1.2]
), by the tightness of
\{\mu^{\tau}_{m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}
and the Feller property of
\{P_{m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
in Lemma
3.2
.

Now let
\mu
be any invariant measure of
\{P_{m}\}_{m\geq 0}
.
By the property of invariant measure and continuous dependence estimate (
3.6
),

\displaystyle|P_{m}\phi(x)-\mu(\phi)|

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{H}(P_{m}\phi(x)-P_{m}\phi(y))\mu({\rm d}y)\Big|

\displaystyle\leq|\phi|_{\rm Lip}\int_{H}\mathbb{E}\|X_{t}^{x}-X_{t}^{y}\|\mu({\rm d}y)

\displaystyle\leq\exp(-\gamma_{6}t_{m}/2)|\phi|_{\rm Lip}\int_{H}\|x-y\|\mu({\rm d}y),

for any
x\in H
and
m\geq 0
.
Taking

, we have that all such invariant measures coincide, i.e.,
\mu=\pi_{\tau}
, and thus (
3.7
) holds.
∎

3.2.
Exponential Ergodicity of DIEG Scheme

Next, let us generalize the arguments in the previous two parts to spatial discretizations via Galerkin approximations of the temporal DIE scheme (
DIE

Then, we derive the time-independent strong convergence rate for the fully discrete scheme towards the exact solution of Eq. (

Let

with maximal length

DIE

]

\displaystyle=S_{h,\tau}^{m+1}X^{h}_{0}+\tau\sum_{i=0}^{m}S_{h,\tau}^{m+1-i}\mathcal{P}_{h}F(X(t_{i+1}))

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{i=0}^{m}S_{h,\tau}^{m+1-i}\mathcal{P}_{h}G(X(t_{i}))\delta_{i}W.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
X_{0}\in\dot{H}^{1}
and Assumptions
2.1
-
2.2
hold.
Assume that
p\geq 2
and
L_{1}+\frac{p(2q-1)-1}{2}L_{9}<\lambda_{1}

Then there exist a positive constant
C
such that for any

(3.13)

\displaystyle\sup_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{E}\|X(t_{m})-\widetilde{X}^{h}_{m}\|^{p}\leq C(1+\|X_{0}\|_{1}^{p(2q-1)})(h^{p}+\tau^{p/2}).

Proof.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
.
In
[
32
, Lemma 4.2]
, the error

\displaystyle J^{m+1}:=(\mathbb{E}\|X(t_{m+1})-\widetilde{X}^{h}_{m+1}\|^{p})^{1/p}

is divided into three parts
J^{m+1}_{i}
,
i=1,2,3
, respectively, which satisfies

\displaystyle J^{m+1}_{1}

\displaystyle=(\mathbb{E}\|E_{h,\tau}(t_{m+1})X_{0}\|^{p})^{1/p}\leq C(h+\tau^{1/2})\|X_{0}\|_{1},

\displaystyle J^{m+1}_{2}

\displaystyle=\Big(\mathbb{E}\Big\|\int_{0}^{t_{m+1}}S_{t_{m+1}-r}F(X_{r}){\rm d}r-\tau\sum_{i=0}^{m}S_{h,\tau}^{m+1-i}\mathcal{P}_{h}F(X(t_{i+1}))\Big\|^{p}\Big)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(1+\sup_{t\geq 0}(\mathbb{E}\|X_{t}\|_{1}^{2p(q-1)})^{\frac{1}{2p}}\Big)\Big(\sup_{t\neq s}\frac{\mathbb{E}\|X_{t}-X_{s}\|^{2p}}{|t-s|^{p}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2p}}\tau^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+C(h+\tau^{1/2})\sup_{t\geq 0}(\mathbb{E}\|F(X_{t})\|^{p})^{1/p},

\displaystyle J^{m+1}_{3}

\displaystyle=\Big(\mathbb{E}\Big\|\int_{0}^{t_{m+1}}S_{t_{m+1}-r}G(X_{r}){\rm d}W_{r}-\sum_{i=0}^{m}S_{h,\tau}^{m+1-i}\mathcal{P}_{h}G(X(t_{i}))\delta_{i}W\Big\|^{p}\Big)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\sup_{t\neq s}\frac{\mathbb{E}\|X_{t}-X_{s}\|^{2p}}{|t-s|^{p}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2p}}\tau^{\frac{1}{2}}+C(h+\tau^{\frac{1}{2}})(1+\sup_{t\geq 0}(\mathbb{E}\|X_{t}\|_{1}^{p})^{\frac{1}{p}}).

Putting the above three estimates together results in

\displaystyle J^{m+1}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(1+\|X_{0}\|_{1}+\Big(1+\sup_{t\geq 0}(\mathbb{E}\|X_{t}\|_{1}^{2p(q-1)})^{\frac{1}{2p}}\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\Big(\sup_{t\neq s}\frac{\mathbb{E}\|X_{t}-X_{s}\|^{2p}}{|t-s|^{p}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2p}}

\displaystyle\quad+\sup_{t\geq 0}(\mathbb{E}\|F(X_{t})\|^{p})^{\frac{1}{p}}+\sup_{t\geq 0}(\mathbb{E}\|X_{t}\|_{1}^{p})^{\frac{1}{p}}\Big)(h+\tau^{\frac{1}{2}}).

This inequality, in combination with the estimate on
J^{0}
that

\displaystyle J^{0}=\|X_{0}-\mathcal{P}_{h}X_{0}\|_{L_{\omega}^{p}L^{2}}\leq Ch\|X_{0}\|_{1},

the condition (
2.3
), the embeddings (
2.8
), the uniform stability estimate (
2.21
), and the uniform Hölder estimate (
2.25
), completes the proof of (
3.13
).
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
X_{0}\in\dot{H}^{1}

C

\displaystyle\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{E}\|X_{t_{k}}-X^{h}_{k}\|^{2}\leq C(1+\|X_{0}\|_{1}^{2(q^{2}+q-1)})(h^{2}+\tau).

Proof.

For
k\in\mathbb{N}
, denote

e^{h}_{k}:=\widetilde{X}^{h}_{k}-X^{h}_{k}
.
From (
3.3
), it is clear that

\displaystyle\widetilde{X}^{h}_{m+1}=\widetilde{X}_{m}^{h}+\tau A_{h}\widetilde{X}^{h}_{m+1}+\tau\mathcal{P}_{h}F(X_{t_{m+1}})+\mathcal{P}_{h}G(X_{t_{m}})\delta_{m}W.

Then, by (
DIEG
) and the above equality, we have

\displaystyle e^{h}_{k+1}-e^{h}_{k}

\displaystyle=[\Delta_{h}e^{h}_{k+1}+\mathcal{P}_{h}(F(X_{t_{k+1}})-F(X^{h}_{k+1})]\tau

\displaystyle\quad+\mathcal{P}_{h}(G(X_{t_{k}})-G(X^{h}_{k}))\delta_{k}W.

Testing with
e^{h}_{k+1}
and using the inequality (
3.3
), the condition (
2.12
), Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, and the elementary inequality
\|x+y\|^{2}\leq(1+\varepsilon)\|x\|^{2}+(1+\varepsilon^{-1})\|y\|^{2}
for

\displaystyle\|e^{h}_{k+1}\|^{2}-\|e^{h}_{k}\|^{2}+\|e^{h}_{k+1}-e^{h}_{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=2~_{1}\langle e^{h}_{k+1},F(X_{t_{k+1}})-F(\widetilde{X}^{h}_{k+1})\rangle_{-1}\tau

\displaystyle\quad+2~_{1}\langle e^{h}_{k+1},\Delta e^{h}_{k+1}+F(\widetilde{X}^{h}_{k+1})-F(X^{h}_{k+1})\rangle_{-1}\tau

\displaystyle\quad+2\langle e^{h}_{k+1}-e^{h}_{k},(G(X_{t_{k}})-G(X^{h}_{k}))\delta_{k}W\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+2\langle e^{h}_{k},(G(X_{t_{k}})-G(X^{h}_{k}))\delta_{k}W\rangle

\displaystyle\leq-2(\lambda_{1}-L_{1}-\varepsilon)\tau\|e^{h}_{k+1}\|^{2}+\|e^{h}_{k+1}-e^{h}_{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+2\langle e^{h}_{k},(G(X_{t_{k}})-G(X^{h}_{k}))\delta_{k}W\rangle+C_{\varepsilon}\tau\|F(X_{t_{k+1}})-F(\widetilde{X}^{h}_{k+1})\|^{2}_{-1}

\displaystyle\quad+(1+\varepsilon^{-1})\|(G(X_{t_{k}})-G(\widetilde{X}^{h}_{k}))\delta_{k}W\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+(1+\varepsilon)\|(G(\widetilde{X}^{h}_{k})-G(X^{h}_{k}))\delta_{k}W\|^{2}.

It follows that

\displaystyle(1-2(\lambda_{1}-L_{1}-\varepsilon)\tau)\|e^{h}_{k+1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|e^{h}_{k}\|^{2}+2\langle e^{h}_{k},(G(X_{t_{k}})-G(X^{h}_{k}))\delta_{k}W\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+(1+\varepsilon^{-1})\|(G(X_{t_{k}})-G(\widetilde{X}^{h}_{k}))\delta_{k}W\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+C_{\varepsilon}\tau\|F(X_{t_{k+1}})-F(\widetilde{X}^{h}_{k+1})\|^{2}_{-1}

\displaystyle\quad+(1+\varepsilon)\|(G(\widetilde{X}^{h}_{k})-G(X^{h}_{k}))\delta_{k}W\|^{2}.

Now, taking expectation on both sides of the above inequality and using the fact

\mathbb{E}\langle e^{h}_{k},(G(X_{t_{k}})-G(X^{h}_{k}))\delta_{k}W\rangle=0

and the estimates

\displaystyle\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{E}\|F(X_{t_{k+1}})-F(\widetilde{X}^{h}_{k+1})\|^{2}_{-1}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+\|X_{0}\|_{1}^{2(q^{2}+q-1)})(h^{2}+\tau),

\displaystyle\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{E}\|G(X_{t_{k}})-G(\widetilde{X}^{h}_{k})\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}^{0}}^{2}

\displaystyle(1+2(\lambda_{1}-L_{1}-\varepsilon)\tau)\mathbb{E}\|e^{h}_{k+1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(1+L_{6}(1+\varepsilon)\tau)\mathbb{E}\|e^{h}_{k}\|^{2}+C_{\varepsilon}(1+\|X_{0}\|_{1}^{2(2q-1)})(h^{2}+\tau)\tau,

from which we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|e^{h}_{k}\|^{2}

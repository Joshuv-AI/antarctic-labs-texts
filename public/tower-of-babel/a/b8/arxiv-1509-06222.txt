Electromagnetic triangle anomaly and neutral pion condensation in QCD vacuum
Gaoqing Cao, Xu-Guang Huang

Abstract
We study the QCD vacuum structure under the influence of an electromagnetic field with a nonzero second Lorentz invariant I 2 = 𝑬 ⋅ 𝑩 I_{2}={\bm{E}}\cdot{\bm{B}} . We show that the presence of I 2 I_{2} can induce neutral pion ( π 0 \pi^{0} ) condensation in the QCD vacuum through the electromagnetic triangle anomaly. Within the frameworks of chiral perturbation theory at leading small-momenta expansion as well as the Nambu–Jona-Lasinio model at leading 1 / N c 1/N_{c} expansion, we quantify the dependence of the π 0 \pi^{0} condensate on I 2 I_{2} . The stability of the π 0 \pi^{0} -condensed vacuum against the Schwinger charged pair production due to electric field is also discussed.

I
Introduction

The vacuum structure of Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD) has been intensively studied over many years and a variety of interesting properties have been extracted. For example, it is well-known that the normal QCD vacuum possesses the spontaneous (approximate) chiral symmetry breaking (CSB) which can be characterized by the scalar quark-antiquark condensate
\langle{\bar{\psi}}\psi\rangle\neq 0
; while the pseudoscalar condensates like
\langle{\bar{\psi}}i\gamma_{5}\tau_{3}\psi\rangle
with
\tau_{3}
the third Pauli matrix are not permitted — an assertion known as the Vafa-Witten theorem
Vafa:1984xg
.

Under proper conditions, the QCD vacuum structure can be altered, usually through phase transitions. Examples include the pion superfluid phase at high isospin chemical potential
Son:2000xc
;
Kogut:2002zg
;
He:2005nk
and the color superconducting phase at high baryon chemical potential
Alford:1997zt
;
Rapp:1997zu
;
Alford:2007xm
; in the first case the isospin symmetry is broken while the chiral symmetry is restored and in the second case the color symmetry is broken while the chiral symmetry may or may not be broken.

Strong electromagnetic (EM) fields provide another way to modify the QCD vacuum. Strong magnetic fields may exist in compact stars

;
Thompson:1993hn
;
Olausen:2013bpa
, heavy-ion collisions
Skokov:2009qp
;
Deng:2012pc
;
Huang:2015oca

;
Baym:1995fk
;
Grasso:2000wj
. Strong electric fields may also be accessible in heavy-ion collisions
Hirono:2012rt
;
Deng:2014uja
;
Voronyuk:2014rna
.
The presence of the magnetic field is known to enhance the scalar
\langle{\bar{\psi}}\psi\rangle
condensate at zero quark chemical potentials which is called the magnetic catalysis of the CSB
Gusynin:1994re
;
Gusynin:1995nb
;
Miransky:2015ava
;
Klimenko:1991he
, while the effect of the magnetic field on the CSB at finite temperature or chemical potentials shows novel features which are still not fully understood

;

;
Bali:2012zg
;
Fukushima:2012kc
;

;
Chao:2013qpa

|e{\bm{B}}|\gtrsim m_{\rho}^{2}
with
m_{\rho}
the mass of the rho meson, the QCD vacuum will become a superconductor due to charged rho condensation
Chernodub:2011mc
. The effect of electric field on the CSB was also studied
Klevansky:1989vi
;
Babansky:1997zh
;
Cohen:2007bt
and it was found that the electric field always tends to break the scalar quark-antiquark pairs and thus weaken the CSB.

Throughout this article, we restrict to the zero quark chemical potential and zero temperature case. Before we proceed, we note that the neutral pion condensation we will study is different from the disoriented chiral condensate (DCC)

II
Invalidation of the Vafa-Witten Theorem

The EM-triangle-anomaly induced neutral pion condensation does not contradict the Vafa-Witten theorem. The validity of the Vafa-Witten theorem relies on the positivity,
\det{\cal D}>0
, where
{\cal D}
is the Euclidean Dirac operator
Vafa:1984xg
;
Vafa:1983tf
. However, this is not the case in the presence of a real electric field. To see this, we write down
\cal D
explicitely,

\displaystyle{\cal D}=\gamma_{\mu}(\partial_{\mu}-ig{\cal A}_{\mu})+Q\gamma_{4}A_{0}+iQ\gamma_{i}A_{i}+M,

(4)

where
{\cal A}_{\mu}
(
\mu=1-4
) is the gluon field,
A_{\alpha}
(
\alpha=0-3
) is the EM field, and
M
is the mass matrix. We choose the gauge such that
{\bm{E}}=-{\bm{\nabla}}A_{0}
and
A_{i}
is time independent.
For two-flavor case, the charge matrix is
Q/e={\rm{diag}}(2/3,-1/3)=1/6+\tau_{3}/2
and the mass matrix is
M=m_{0}
. The crucial observation is that
Q
is
not
traceless and thus its role is similar with the baryon chemical potential
\mu
. It is well-known that the presence of
\mu
destroys the positivity of
\det{\cal D}
, and so does
A_{0}
. In fact, when
A_{0}\neq 0
there does not exist a matrix
\Gamma
such that
\Gamma^{-1}{\cal D}\Gamma={\cal D}^{\dagger}
to guarantee the positivity. Choosing another gauge, e.g.
A_{0}=0
, does not change the conclusion. Because in this case,
A_{i}
must depend on time, e.g. for constant
{\bm{E}}
,
A_{i}=-E_{i}t
, which after Wick rotation spoils again the positivity
footnote
. Note that if
q_{u}=-q_{d}
,
\det{\cal D}
is semi-positive
Yamamoto:2012bd
.

The invalidation of the Vafa-Witten theorem in the electric field has important physical contents. Physically, in the presence of
{\bm{E}}
, the Dirac determinant has to be complex or at most semi-positive because it always receives contributions from the Schwinger poles which represent the particle-antiparticle pair creation due to
{\bm{E}}

Schwinger:1951nm
.

III
Chiral perturbation theory calculation

We start with the two-flavor ChPT described by the Lagrangian

\displaystyle{\cal L}={\cal L}_{0}+{\cal L}_{\rm WZW},

(5)

where
{\cal L}_{0}
is the usual chiral Lagrangian given by (we keep only
O(p^{2})
terms)

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{0}=\frac{f_{\pi}^{2}}{4}{\rm tr}\left[D_{\mu}U^{\dagger}D^{\mu}U+m_{\pi}^{2}(U+U^{\dagger})\right],

(6)

and the Wess-Zumino-Witten term
{\cal L}_{\rm WZW}
is given by
Wess:1971yu
;
Witten:1983tw
;
Scherer:2012xha
;
Son:2007ny
;
Fukushima:2012fg

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{\rm WZW}=\frac{N_{c}}{48\pi^{2}}A_{\mu}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}[{\rm tr}\left(QL_{\nu}L_{\alpha}L_{\beta}+QR_{\nu}R_{\alpha}R_{\beta}\right)

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;-iF_{\alpha\beta}T_{\nu}],

(7)

\displaystyle T_{\nu}={\rm tr}\left[Q^{2}(L_{\nu}+R_{\nu})+\frac{1}{2}\left(QUQU^{\dagger}L_{\nu}+QU^{\dagger}QUR_{\nu}\right)\right],

\displaystyle L_{\mu}=U\partial_{\mu}U^{\dagger},

\displaystyle R_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}U^{\dagger}U.

(8)

In the above, the covariant derivative is given by

\displaystyle D_{\mu}U=\partial_{\mu}U+A_{\mu}[Q,U],

(9)

and
U
is a
2\times 2
unitary matrix representing the chiral fields, for which we choose the Weinberg parametrization,

\displaystyle U=\frac{1}{f_{\pi}}(s+i{\bm{\tau}}\cdot{\bm{t}}),

(10)

where the fields
s
and
{\bm{t}}
fulfill the constraint
s^{2}+{\bm{t}}^{2}=f_{\pi}^{2}
.

Now we consider constant EM field and make the approximation such that we treat all the chiral fields in (
5
) as uniform condensates. We note that the
\sigma
and
\pi^{0}
in Eq. (
I
) are proportional to
s
and
t_{3}
, respectively.
In the absence of the EM field, it is well-known that the CSB takes place along the
\sigma
-direction so that
s=f_{\pi}
and
{\bm{t}}={\bm{0}}
. The EM field with a configuration where
I_{2}={\bm{E}}\cdot{\bm{B}}\neq 0
is coupled to the
\pi^{0}
field via the triangle anomaly and raises the possibility of
\pi^{0}
condensation. If this happens, the CSB will be rotated from the
\sigma
-direction to
\pi^{0}
-direction. To explore this possibility, we make the following ansatz for the condensates,

\displaystyle s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{\pi}\cos\phi,\;\;t_{3}=f_{\pi}\sin\phi,

(11)

\displaystyle t_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t_{2}=0,

(12)

where without loss of generality, we choose
\phi\in[-\pi/2,\pi/2]
so that
s\geq 0
. The Lagrangian
{\cal L}
then becomes

\displaystyle{\cal L}(\phi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f^{2}_{\pi}m_{\pi}^{2}\cos\phi+\frac{N_{c}I_{2}}{4\pi^{2}}{\rm tr}(Q^{2}\tau_{3})\phi,

(13)

where we have omitted a total derivative term. As obviously, the second term is responsible for the triangle anomaly effects such as
\pi^{0}\rightarrow 2\gamma
. It is easy to minimize
-{\cal L}(\phi)
and obtain

\displaystyle\sin\phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{N_{c}I_{2}}{4\pi^{2}f_{\pi}^{2}m_{\pi}^{2}}{\rm tr}(Q^{2}\tau_{3})

(14)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{N_{c}I_{2}}{4\pi^{2}f_{\pi}^{2}m_{\pi}^{2}}(q_{u}^{2}-q_{d}^{2}),\;\;\;\;{\rm for}\;\;|I_{2}|<I_{2}^{c},

\displaystyle\phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm sgn}\left(I_{2}\right)\frac{\pi}{2},\;\;{\rm for}\;\;|I_{2}|>I_{2}^{c},

(15)

where
I_{2}^{c}=4\pi^{2}f_{\pi}^{2}m_{\pi}^{2}/[N_{c}(q_{u}^{2}-q_{d}^{2})]
. This is equivalent to Eq. (
I
).

Several comments are in order. (1) In the chiral limit
m_{\pi}\rightarrow 0
, thus
I_{2}^{c}\rightarrow 0
, an infinitesimal
I_{2}
will drive the maximum
\pi^{0}
condensation, namely, the CSB is completely driven to
\pi^{0}
-direction once
I_{2}
is applied. (2) The neutral pion condensation induced by
I_{2}
is merely a chiral rotation; it is not a phase transition as no symmetry is broken along this process. (3) As a low-energy effective theory of QCD, the ChPT is reliable only when all the physical parameters,
m_{\pi},p,\sqrt{|e{\bm{B}}|},\sqrt{|e{\bm{E}}|}
, are much smaller than the typical hadronic scale
\Lambda_{\chi}\sim 1
GeV. We can estimate that

\displaystyle\frac{e^{2}I_{2}^{c}}{\Lambda_{\chi}^{4}}\sim\frac{m_{\pi}^{2}}{\Lambda_{\chi}^{2}}\ll 1,

(16)

which justifies the validity of our result for
I_{2}
not too larger than
I_{2}^{c}
. (4) As well-known, when
|e{\bm{E}}|\gg m_{\pi}^{2}
(but not larger than
\Lambda_{\chi}^{2}
), the vacuum will be unstable because of the Schwinger pair production of
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
. We will discuss this issue in another section.

IV
Nambu–Jona-Lasinio Model calculation

The ChPT is built on hadronic degrees of freedom, we now use the NJL model which is based on quark degrees of freedom to test the consequences of the ChPT. The Lagrangian of the two-flavor NJL model is given by
Klevansky:1992qe

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{\rm NJL}={\bar{\psi}}(i\not{D}-m_{0})\psi+G[({\bar{\psi}}\psi)^{2}+({\bar{\psi}}i\gamma_{5}{\bm{\tau}}\psi)^{2}],

(17)

where
\psi=(u,d)^{T}
represents the two-flavor quark fields,
m_{0}
is the current mass of quarks,
G
is the four-fermion coupling constant, and

\displaystyle D_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}+iQA_{\mu},

(18)

is the covariant derivative. Note that, in addition to the coupling constant
G
, the NJL model contains implicitly another parameter, the ultraviolet cutoff
\Lambda
, which specifies the applicable region of the model. The values of
G
and
\Lambda
are determined by fitting NJL predictions with the physical hadronic observables like
f_{\pi}
and
\langle{\bar{\psi}}\psi\rangle
in the normal vacuum
Klevansky:1992qe
.

The NJL model was widely used to study a number of non-perturbative properties of QCD, especially those related to the chiral symmetry. In fact, the NJL model shares the same global symmetries with QCD. At the mean-field level where only the leading-order terms in the
1/N_{c}
expansion are kept,

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{\rm NJL}={\bar{\psi}}(i\not{D}-m-i\pi^{0}\gamma_{5}\tau_{3})\psi-\frac{\sigma^{2}+(\pi^{0})^{2}}{4G},

(19)

where
m=m_{0}+\sigma
is the constitute mass of quarks and
\sigma=-2G\langle{\bar{\psi}}\psi\rangle
and
\pi^{0}=-2G\langle{\bar{\psi}}i\gamma_{5}\tau_{3}\psi\rangle
represent the sigma and neutral pion condensates. The quark fields can then be integrated out analytically, which is most conveniently done in Euclidean path integral and the resultant thermodynamic potential reads

\displaystyle\Omega=\frac{(m-m_{0})^{2}+(\pi^{0})^{2}}{4G}-\frac{1}{V_{4}}{\rm Tr}\ln S^{-1},

(20)

where
V_{4}
is the Euclidean spacetime volume,
{\rm Tr}
acts on all physical spaces, and the quark propagator is

\displaystyle S(x,x^{\prime})=-(i\not{D}-m-i\pi^{0}\gamma_{5}\tau_{3})^{-1}\delta^{(4)}(x-x^{\prime}).

(21)

It is diagonal in flavor space which allows us to calculate it analytically by adopting the Schwinger proper-time formalism

\displaystyle{\cal S}_{f}(x,x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{-i\over(4\pi)^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}{ds\over s^{2}}\;e^{-iq_{f}\int_{x^{\prime}}^{x}A\cdot dx}\big[-{1\over 2}\gamma\big(q_{f}F\coth(q_{f}Fs)-q_{f}F\big)(x-x^{\prime})+m-{\rm sgn}(q_{f})i\gamma^{5}\pi^{0}\big]

\displaystyle\times\exp\Big\{-i\big[m^{2}+(\pi^{0})^{2}\big]s+{i\over 4}(x-x^{\prime})q_{f}F\coth(q_{f}Fs)(x-x^{\prime})+{i\over 2}q_{f}\sigma Fs\Big\}{-(q_{f}s)^{2}I_{2}\over\text{Im}\cosh\big(iq_{f}s(I_{1}+2iI_{2})^{1/2}\big)},

where

,

is the first Lorentz invariant, and we have suppressed some of the Lorentz scripts, e.g.,
\sigma F
should be understood as
\sigma^{\mu\nu}F_{\nu\mu}

\sigma^{\mu\nu}=\frac{i}{2}[\gamma^{\mu},\gamma^{\nu}]

V
Stability of the
\pi^{0}
-condensed vacuum

The neutral pion condensation found above requires a nonzero electric field. As we know, strong electric field can efficiently generate particle-antiparticle pairs (in the confined phase, mostly
\pi^{+}
and
\pi^{-}
; in the deconfined phase, quarks and antiquarks) through the Schwinger mechanism and drive the vacuum unstable. So it is necessary to analyze that under which condition the
\pi^{0}
-condensed vacuum is stable. For this purpose, we need to calculate the pair creation rate. This can be achieved within the ChPT framework by using the Schwinger proper-time method, see, e.g., Refs.
Kim:2003qp
;
Cohen:2007bt
. We will not repeat their calculations here, but just quote their results relevant to our discussion.

In case that
I_{2}\neq 0
, we can always find a frame in which
{\bm{E}}
and
{\bm{B}}
are parallel. We will work in such a frame. The imaginary part of the leading order effective Lagrangian, which is responsible for the
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
production, reads

\displaystyle{\rm{Im}}{\cal L}\!=\!\frac{e^{2}|EB|}{16\pi^{2}}\!\!\!\sum_{n,l=1}^{\infty}\!\!\!\frac{(\!-\!1)^{n+1}}{n}\exp\!\!\left[\!-n\pi\frac{m_{\pi}^{2}\!+\!|eB|(2l\!-\!1)}{|eE|}\right],

where
n
runs over all Schwinger poles and
l
runs over all Landau levels.
The first term in the summation over
n
defines the
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
production rate per unit volume which reads,

\displaystyle w_{\pi^{+}\pi^{-}}=\frac{e^{2}|EB|}{16\pi^{2}}\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\exp\left[-\pi\frac{m_{\pi}^{2}+|eB|(2l-1)}{|eE|}\right].

(26)

It is clear that the presence of the magnetic field effectively enhances the mass of charged pions,

\displaystyle m^{2}_{\pi^{\pm}}=m^{2}_{\pi}+|eB|,

(27)

and therefore as long as

\displaystyle|eE|\ll m^{2}_{\pi}+|eB|,

(28)

the rate of
\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
production is strongly suppressed. In this case, the pair-producing instability takes place over a very long time
t\sim 1/(w_{\pi^{+}\pi^{-}}V)
(
V
the volume) and it is meaningful to consider the “static” property of the vacuum. The “equilibrium state” neutral pion condensate we have studied so far is sensible when the condition (
28
) is satisfied.

VI
Summary

In this article, we propose a mean to tune the QCD vacuum by applying parallel electric and magnetic fields. The underlying mechanism is the electromagnetic triangle anomaly, through which the second Lorentz invariant
I_{2}={\bm{E}}\cdot{\bm{B}}
of the EM field can induce a neutral pion condensate in QCD vacuum and lead to a chiral rotation from the isosinglet scalar
\sigma
-direction toward the isotriplet pseudoscalar
\pi^{0}
-direction. By adopting the chiral perturbation theory at leading order plus the Wess-Zumino-Witten term and the Nambu–Jona-Lasinio model at the mean-field approximation (leading order in
1/N_{c}
expansion), we show that the sine of the rotation angle is universally given by Eq. (
I
).For weak electric field, our results may be testable in lattice QCD by adopting similar strategies as the ones used in lattice simulations at finite baryon chemical potential
DElia:2012zw
.

Our finding may have experimental implications in heavy-ion collisions. In ultra-peripheral heavy-ion collisions the hot quark-gluon matter may not form but quite large
I_{2}
with opposite signs above and below the reaction plane may exist
Deng:2012pc
which can induce transient
\pi^{0}
condensation. The diphoton spectrum may be used to distinguish the
\pi^{0}
-condensed vacuum from the normal vacuum, because in the
\pi^{0}
-condensed vacuum the
\sigma
and
\pi^{0}
excitations are mixed which will change the diphoton emission rate and spectrum. We will study these experimental signatures in near future.

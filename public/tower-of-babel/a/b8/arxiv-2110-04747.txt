1.
Introduction

Let
n\geqslant 2
and
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open.
The Banach spaces
BV(\Omega)
and
SBV(\Omega)
consist, respectively, of functions of bounded variation
[
2
, 3.1]
and special functions of bounded variation
[
2
, §4.1]
.
A long-standing open problem
[
1
, 7.4]
is to provide a useful description of the dual of
BV(\Omega)
.
This seems to be still open, despite important contributions by N.G. Meyers and W.P. Ziemer
[
9
]
, N.C. Phuc and M. Torres
[
10
]
, and N. Fusco and D. Spector
[
8
]
.
Regarding the dual of
SBV(\Omega)
, the second author contributed the article
[
5
]
.
In the present paper, we describe the dual of
SBV(\Omega)
in a way
that is “optimal” in some specific universal sense.

For each

u\in SBV(\Omega)
, the distributional gradient
Du
of
u

decomposes as
Du=\mathscr{L}^{n}\>{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt}\hskip-0.2pt\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt,width=7.0pt,depth=0.0pt}\,\nabla u+\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\>{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt}\hskip-0.2pt\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt,width=7.0pt,depth=0.0pt}\,j_{u}
, where

\nabla u
is the pointwise a.e. approximate gradient of
u
, and

j_{u}
is a vector field carried on the approximate discontinuity set

S_{u}
of
u
, on which we have
j_{u}=(u^{+}-u^{-})\nu_{u}
,
\nu_{u}
being a
unitary field normal to
S_{u}
, and
u^{+},u^{-}
the approximate limits
of
u
on either sides of
S_{u}
.
We have

\|u\|_{SBV(\Omega)}=\int_{\Omega}|u|\,d\mathscr{L}^{n}+\int_{S_{u}}\|j_{u}\|\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}+\int_{\Omega}\|\nabla u\|\,d\mathscr{L}^{n}.

Thus,
u\mapsto(u,j_{u},\nabla u)
is an isometric embedding

SBV(\Omega)\to L^{1}(\Omega,\mathscr{B}(\Omega),\mathscr{L}^{n})\times L^{1}(\Omega,\mathscr{B}(\Omega),\mathscr{H}^{n-1};\mathbb{R}^{n})\times L^{1}(\Omega,\mathscr{B}(\Omega),\mathscr{L}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Since
(\Omega,\mathscr{B}(\Omega),\mathscr{L}^{n})
is
\sigma
-finite, the dual of the corresponding
L^{1}
space is, of course, the corresponding
L^{\infty}
space.
This remark does not apply to the middle measure space
(\Omega,\mathscr{B}(\Omega),\mathscr{H}^{n-1})
.
An attempt at understanding the dual of the corresponding
L^{1}
space leads to a study of the canonical map

\Upsilon\colon L^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathscr{B}(\Omega),\mathscr{H}^{n-1})\to L^{1}(\Omega,\mathscr{B}(\Omega),\mathscr{H}^{n-1})^{*}\,.

This relates to the Radon-Nikodým Theorem.
In this respect, we note that:

•

\Upsilon
is not surjective. If
\mathscr{B}(\Omega)
is replaced
with
\mathscr{M}_{\mathscr{H}^{n-1}}(\Omega)
, whether
\Upsilon
is surjective
or not is undecidable in ZFC.

•

\Upsilon
is injective. If
\mathscr{B}(\Omega)
is replaced with

\mathscr{M}_{\mathscr{H}^{n-1}}(\Omega)
,
\Upsilon
is not injective.

Here,
\mathscr{M}_{\mathscr{H}^{n-1}}(\Omega)
is the
\sigma
-algebra of

\mathscr{H}^{n-1}
-measurable subsets of
\Omega
.
For a detailed treatment of these, see
[
4
]
.
Our goal now is to stick with the axioms of ZFC.

The problem is with measurability.
Let
\varphi\in L^{1}(\Omega,\mathscr{B}(\Omega),\mathscr{H}^{n-1})
and let
E\subseteq\Omega
be Borel such that
\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(E)<\infty
.
If
\iota_{E}\colon L^{1}(E,\mathscr{B}(E),\mathscr{H}^{n-1})\to L^{1}(\Omega,\mathscr{B}(\Omega),\mathscr{H}^{n-1})
is the obvious injection, then
\varphi\circ\iota_{E}
belongs to the dual of
L^{1}(E,\mathscr{B}(E),\mathscr{H}^{n-1})
and, by the classical Riesz Theorem, is represented by a Borel measurable function
g_{E}\colon E\to\mathbb{R}
, i.e.
(\varphi\circ\iota_{E})(f)=\int_{E}fg_{E}d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}
whenever
f\in L^{1}(E,\mathscr{B}(E),\mathscr{H}^{n-1})
.
The family
\langle g_{E}\rangle_{E}
is compatible in the sense that
\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(E\cap E^{\prime}\cap\{g_{E}\neq g_{E^{\prime}}\})=0
for all
E,E^{\prime}
.
There does not exist, in general, a
gluing
of this family, i.e. a Borel measurable function
g\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(E\cap\{g\neq g_{E}\})=0
for all
E
, and whether an
\mathscr{H}^{n-1}
-measurable gluing of
\langle g_{E}\rangle_{E}
exists is undecidable.

In fact, a “proper” gluing of
\langle g_{E}\rangle_{E}
at
x\in\Omega
depends both on
x
and
E
.
However, it will depend on
(x,E)
only according to the behavior of
E
near
x
.
This prompts us to introduce a notion of approximate germ of
E
at
x
– finer than the first order tangential behavior in case
E
is, for instance, a smooth hypersurface.
Carrying out these ideas to describe the dual of
L^{1}(\Omega,\mathscr{B}(\Omega),\mathscr{H}^{n-1})
presents technical difficulties that we avoid by considering a slightly different measure space.
The jump set
S_{u}
of a function of bounded variation being countably
rectifiable, one has
\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\>{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt}\hskip-0.2pt\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt,width=7.0pt,depth=0.0pt}\,S_{u}=\mathscr{I}^{n-1}_{\infty}\>{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt}\hskip-0.2pt\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt,width=7.0pt,depth=0.0pt}\,S_{u}
, where
\mathscr{I}^{n-1}_{\infty}
is an integral geometric measure.
The application to studying
SBV(\Omega)^{*}
is not affected either when we replace
\mathscr{I}^{n-1}_{\infty}
by its semi-finite version
\mathscr{I}^{n-1}
.
Both measures are described in
2.2
.
What we have gained is that each set
E\in\mathscr{B}(\Omega)
with
\mathscr{I}^{n-1}(E)<\infty
has the property that
\Theta^{n-1}(E,x)=1
for
\mathscr{I}^{n-1}
-almost every
x\in E
, according to the Structure Theorem.
One recognizes a density property.
This is an essential ingredient of our proof below and would not hold with
\mathscr{H}^{n-1}
in place of
\mathscr{I}^{n-1}
.

Notice that a family
\langle g_{E}\rangle_{E}
is, in fact, the same thing as a function
g(x,E)
that depends on both
x
and
E
, as in
[
5
]
, but the compatibility of the family
\langle g_{E}\rangle_{E}
considered above means that there is then some redundancy in the corresponding space of variables
(x,E)
.
The gist of the present paper is to consider a special quotient of the space of pairs
(x,E)
and to equip it with a structure of a measure space
(\hat{\Omega},\hat{\mathscr{A}},\hat{\mathscr{I}}^{n-1})
, see
2.2
below.
One can of course wonder if the quotient presented here is “optimal”, i.e. the smallest one for the purpose of representing the dual of
L^{1}
, or if we are still left with some redundancy in the variables.
The optimality of the measure space
(\hat{\Omega},\hat{\mathscr{A}},\hat{\mathscr{I}}^{n-1})
is stated in
[
3
]
and established there, in an appropriate categorical setting.
Thus, the combination of
[
3
]
and the present paper can
be considered a solution to describing the dual of
SBV(\Omega)
, see
Theorem
2.10
, as explicitly as one possibly can in ZFC.

1.1
Notations. —

Our choice of notation is mostly compatible with that of
[
6
]

and
[
2
]
. As an exception, we let
\ominus

be the set theoretic symmetric difference. In
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the

(n-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure is
\mathscr{H}^{n-1}
and the
Lebesgue measure is
\mathscr{L}^{n}
. The
\sigma
-algebras consisting of
Borel subsets and
\mathscr{H}^{n-1}
-measurable subsets of
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
are respectively denoted by
\mathscr{B}(E)
and

\mathscr{M}_{\mathscr{H}^{n-1}}(E)
. The restriction symbol for measures is

[
6
, 2.1.2]
. The lower and upper
(n-1)
-dimensional
densities of
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
at
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, denoted

\Theta_{*}^{n-1}(E,x)
and
\Theta^{*\,n-1}(E,x)
, respectively, are
defined in
[
6
, 2.10.19]
(with
\mu=\mathscr{L}^{n}\>{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt}\hskip-0.2pt\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt,width=7.0pt,depth=0.0pt}\,E
). Both

x\mapsto\Theta_{*}^{n-1}(E,x)
and
x\mapsto\Theta^{*\,n-1}(E,x)

are Borel measurable. When they coincide at
x
, the common value is
denoted
\Theta^{n-1}(E,x)
.

2.
Results

2.1
. —

A set
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called
countably

(n-1)
-rectifiable
whenever there are countably many Lipschitz
maps
f_{k}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\left(E\setminus\bigcup_{k=0}^{\infty}f_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})\right)=0.

Also,
E
is called
(n-1)
-rectifiable
if it is

\mathscr{H}^{n-1}
-measurable, of finite
\mathscr{H}^{n-1}
-measure, and
countably
(n-1)
-rectifiable.

Throughout the paper, we fix an open subset
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For an
(n-1)
-rectifiable set
E\subseteq\Omega
, the
following hold:

(A)

\Theta^{n-1}(E,x)=1
for
\mathscr{H}^{n-1}
-almost all
x\in E
,
[
6
, 3.2.19]
;

(B)

\Theta^{n-1}(E,x)=0
for
\mathscr{H}^{n-1}
-almost all
x\in\Omega\setminus E
,
[
6
, 2.10.19(4)]
.

The set
\tilde{E}=\{x\in\Omega:\Theta^{n-1}(E,x)=1\}

coincides
\mathscr{H}^{n-1}
-almost everywhere with
E
, i.e.
\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(\tilde{E}\ominus E)=0
. It satisfies:

(C)

\tilde{E}
is a Borel measurable
(n-1)
-rectifiable
subset of
\Omega
;

(D)

A point
x\in\Omega
is in
\tilde{E}
iff

\Theta^{n-1}(\tilde{E},x)=1
.

2.2
. —

For
S\subseteq\Omega
, we let

\mathscr{I}^{n-1}(S)=\sup\left\{\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(S\cap E):E\subseteq\Omega\text{ is an }(n-1)\text{-rectifiable set}\right\}.

It is easily checked that
\mathscr{I}^{n-1}
is a Borel regular outer
measure on
\Omega
. In fact, the measure space

(\Omega,\mathscr{B}(\Omega),\mathscr{I}^{n-1})
is the semi-finite version, in
the sense of
[
7
, 213X(c)]
, of

(\Omega,\mathscr{B}(\Omega),\mathscr{I}^{n-1}_{\infty})
, where the integral
geometric measure
\mathscr{I}^{n-1}_{\infty}
is defined in

[
6
, 2.10.5(1)]
. The latter is a consequence of the Structure
Theorem
[
6
, 3.3.14]
and
[
6
, 3.2.26]
. The measure

\mathscr{I}^{n-1}
is much more manageable than the Hausdorff measure

\mathscr{H}^{n-1}
, as it ignores purely unrectifiable sets. We notice
that
\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\>{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt}\hskip-0.2pt\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt,width=7.0pt,depth=0.0pt}\,E=\mathscr{I}^{n-1}\>{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt}\hskip-0.2pt\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt,width=7.0pt,depth=0.0pt}\,E
for any countably

(n-1)
-rectifiable Borel set
E\subseteq\Omega
. To find an
integral representation for the elements of
SBV(\Omega)^{*}
, it is
crucial to describe the dual of
L^{1}(\Omega,\mathscr{B}(\Omega),\mathscr{I}^{n-1})
. We will do this in Theorem
2.7
. Prior to
that, we must construct a particular measure space
(\hat{\Omega},\hat{\mathscr{A}},\hat{\mathscr{I}}^{n-1})
.

2.3
The measure space
(\hat{\Omega},\hat{\mathscr{A}},\hat{\mathscr{I}}^{n-1})
. —

Let
\mathscr{E}
be the collection of sets that
satisfy conditions (C) and (D) of
2.1
. For each

x\in\Omega
, we let
\mathscr{E}_{x}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{E\in\mathscr{E}:x\in E\}
. We define an equivalence relation
\sim_{x}
on
\mathscr{E}_{x}
:

E\sim_{x}E^{\prime}\text{ iff }\Theta^{n-1}(E\ominus E^{\prime},x)=0\text{
iff }\Theta^{n-1}(E\cap E^{\prime},x)=1.

The equivalence class
[E]_{x}
under
\sim_{x}
of some
E\in\mathscr{E}_{x}

is called the
approximate germ of
E
at
x
. We will use
repeatedly the following consequence of
2.1
(A):
if
E,E^{\prime}\in\mathscr{E}
, then
[E]_{x}=[E^{\prime}]_{x}
for
\mathscr{H}^{n-1}
-almost
all
x\in E\cap E^{\prime}
.

We let
\hat{\Omega}
be the set consisting of the pairs
(x,[E]_{x})
, where
x\in\Omega
and
[E]_{x}
is some approximate germ
at
x
. This set should be thought of as a fiber space over
\Omega
,
with respect to the obvious projection map
p\colon\hat{\Omega}\to\Omega

that sends each
(x,[E]_{x})
to
x
. Below, we equip
\hat{\Omega}

with a
\sigma
-algebra and a measure.

For each
E\in\mathscr{E}
, we let
\gamma_{E}\colon E\to\hat{\Omega}

be the map defined by
\gamma_{E}(x)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=(x,[E]_{x})
. We define

\hat{\mathscr{A}}
to be the finest
\sigma
-algebra on
\hat{\Omega}
so
that all maps
\gamma_{E}
are
(\mathscr{M}_{\mathscr{H}^{n-1}}(E),\hat{\mathscr{A}})
-measurable, that is

\hat{\mathscr{A}}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\left\{A\subseteq\hat{\Omega}:\gamma_{E}^{-1}(A)\in\mathscr{M}_{\mathscr{H}^{n-1}}(E)\text{ for all }E\in\mathscr{E}\right\}.

For all
E\in\mathscr{E}
, we denote by
\mu_{E}
the measure on

(\hat{\Omega},\hat{\mathscr{A}})
defined by
\mu_{E}(A)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(\gamma_{E}^{-1}(A))
. We let
\hat{\mathscr{I}}^{n-1}
be the
least upper bound of the measures
\mu_{E}
, with
E
running over

\mathscr{E}
(see e.g.
[
2
, 1.68]
). The measure
space
(\hat{\Omega},\hat{\mathscr{A}},\hat{\mathscr{I}}^{n-1})
is complete:
a set
N\subseteq\hat{\Omega}
is negligible iff
\gamma_{E}^{-1}(N)

is
\mathscr{H}^{n-1}
-negligible for all
E\in\mathscr{E}
.

From the definition of the
\sigma
-algebra
\hat{\mathscr{A}}
, it is
easy to show that a map
g\colon\hat{\Omega}\to Z
with values in
a measurable space
(Z,\mathscr{C})
is
(\hat{\mathscr{A}},\mathscr{C})
-measurable
iff all maps
g\circ\gamma_{E}
are
(\mathscr{M}_{\mathscr{H}^{n-1}}(E),\mathscr{C})
-measurable. As a consequence, the projection map
p
is

(\hat{\mathscr{A}},\mathscr{B}(\Omega))
-measurable, for the maps
p\circ\gamma_{E}
are the inclusion maps
E\to\Omega
and, therefore, are

\mathscr{H}^{n-1}
-measurable.

2.4 Lemma
. —

The following hold:

(1)

For all
A\in\hat{\mathscr{A}}
, one has
\hat{\mathscr{I}}^{n-1}(A)=\sup\left\{\mu_{E}(A):E\in\mathscr{E}\right\}
.

(2)

p_{\#}\hat{\mathscr{I}}^{n-1}=\mathscr{I}^{n-1}
.

(3)

For
E\in\mathscr{E}
, we have
\hat{\mathscr{I}}^{n-1}(p^{-1}(E)\setminus\gamma_{E}(E))=0

Proof.

(1) Letting
\nu(A)
denote the right hand side of the claimed equality, it suffices to check that
A\mapsto\nu(A)
defines a
measure. Clearly,
\nu
is
non-decreasing and
\sigma
-subadditive.

It remains to check that
\nu(A_{1}\cup A_{2})\geqslant\nu(A_{1})+\nu(A_{2})
for any pair of
disjoint
A_{1},A_{2}\in\hat{\mathscr{A}}
. Let
E_{1},E_{2}\in\mathscr{E}
. Recalling the discussion in
2.1
, there is
a set
E\in\mathscr{E}
that coincides
\mathscr{H}^{n-1}
-almost everywhere
with
E_{1}\cup E_{2}
. For
i\in\{1,2\}
, we have
[E]_{x}=[E_{i}]_{x}

for
\mathscr{H}^{n-1}
-almost all
x\in E\cap E_{i}
. From this, it
follows that

\gamma_{E_{i}}^{-1}(A_{i})\setminus\gamma_{E}^{-1}(A_{i})\subseteq(E_{i}\setminus E)\cup\{x\in E\cap E_{i}:[E]_{x}\neq[E_{i}]_{x}\}

is
\mathscr{H}^{n-1}
-negligible. Hence,
\mu_{E}(A_{i})\geqslant\mu_{E_{i}}(A_{i})
.
As
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are disjoint, one has
\mu_{E}(A_{1}\cup A_{2})=\mu_{E}(A_{1})+\mu_{E}(A_{2})\geqslant\mu_{E_{1}}(A_{1})+\mu_{E_{2}}(A_{2})
. The
proof is then completed by taking the supremum over
E_{1},E_{2}\in\mathscr{E}
.

(2) For all
B\in\mathscr{B}(\Omega)
, one has by (1)

\displaystyle\hat{\mathscr{I}}^{n-1}(p^{-1}(B))

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(\gamma_{E}^{-1}p^{-1}(B)):E\in\mathscr{E}\right\}

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(B\cap E):E\in\mathscr{E}\right\},

which is
\mathscr{I}^{n-1}(B)
.

(3) Let
E^{\prime}\in\mathscr{E}
. The set

\gamma_{E^{\prime}}^{-1}(p^{-1}(E)\setminus\gamma_{E}(E))=\{x\in E\cap E^{\prime}:[E]_{x}\neq[E^{\prime}]_{x}\}

has
\mathscr{H}^{n-1}
measure zero. By the arbitrariness of
E^{\prime}
, it
follows that
p^{-1}(E)\setminus\gamma_{E}(E)\in\hat{\mathscr{A}}
and

\hat{\mathscr{I}}^{n-1}(p^{-1}(E)\setminus\gamma_{E}(E))=0
.
∎

2.5
Density points. —

We recall the following well-known facts regarding the existence of
Lebesgue density points for functions defined on rectifiable
sets. Let
E
be an
(n-1)
-rectifiable set and
h\colon E\to\mathbb{R}

be a function in
L^{1}(E,\mathscr{M}_{\mathscr{H}^{n-1}}(E),\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\>{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt}\hskip-0.2pt\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt,width=7.0pt,depth=0.0pt}\,E)
, we have

\frac{1}{\boldsymbol{\alpha}(n-1)r^{n-1}}\int_{E\cap B(x,r)}h\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\xrightarrow[r\to 0]{}h(x)

at
\mathscr{H}^{n-1}
-almost every
x\in E
. When the limit exists,
x

is called a density point of
h
. The proof of the next lemma is
elementary.

2.6 Lemma
. —

Let
E,E^{\prime}
be two Borel measurable
(n-1)
-rectifiable susbsets of

\Omega
,
h\in L^{\infty}(E,\mathscr{M}_{\mathscr{H}^{n-1}}(E),\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\>{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt}\hskip-0.2pt\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt,width=7.0pt,depth=0.0pt}\,E)
,
h^{\prime}\in L^{\infty}(E^{\prime},\mathscr{M}_{\mathscr{H}^{n-1}}(E^{\prime}),\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\>{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt}\hskip-0.2pt\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt,width=7.0pt,depth=0.0pt}\,E^{\prime})
be two functions, and
x\in E\cap E^{\prime}
. Assume that

(A)

h=h^{\prime}

\mathscr{H}^{n-1}
-almost everywhere on
E\cap E^{\prime}
;

(B)

\Theta^{n-1}(E,x)=\Theta^{n-1}(E^{\prime},x)=\Theta^{n-1}(E\cap E^{\prime},x)=1
;

(C)

x
is a density point of
h
.

Then
x
is a density point of
h^{\prime}
and

\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{1}{\boldsymbol{\alpha}(n-1)r^{n-1}}\int_{E\cap B(x,r)}h\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}=\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{1}{\boldsymbol{\alpha}(n-1)r^{n-1}}\int_{E^{\prime}\cap B(x,r)}h^{\prime}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}.

We now come to our main result.

2.7 Theorem
. —

The map
\Upsilon\colon L^{\infty}(\hat{\Omega},\hat{\mathscr{A}},\hat{\mathscr{I}}^{n-1})\to L^{1}(\Omega,\mathscr{B}(\Omega),\mathscr{I}^{n-1})^{*}

defined by

\Upsilon(g)(f)=\int_{\hat{\Omega}}g(f\circ p)\,d\hat{\mathscr{I}}^{n-1}

is an isometric isomorphism.

Proof.

Let
g\in L^{\infty}(\hat{\Omega},\hat{\mathscr{A}},\hat{\mathscr{I}}^{n-1})

and
f\in L^{1}(\Omega,\mathscr{B}(\Omega),\mathscr{I}^{n-1})
. Then

|\Upsilon(g)(f)|\leqslant\|g\|_{\infty}\int_{\hat{\Omega}}|f\circ p|\,d\hat{\mathscr{I}}^{n-1}=\|g\|_{\infty}\int_{\hat{\Omega}}|f|\,dp_{\#}\hat{\mathscr{I}}^{n-1}=\|g\|_{\infty}\|f\|_{1},

by Lemma
2.4
(2). This shows that
\Upsilon
is
well-defined and
\|\Upsilon\|\leqslant 1
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. The set
A\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{|g|>\|g\|_{\infty}-\varepsilon\}
has positive
\hat{\mathscr{I}}^{n-1}
measure, hence we
infer the existence of an
(n-1)
-rectifiable set
E\in\mathscr{E}
such
that
\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(\gamma_{E}^{-1}(A))>0
. The function
g\circ\gamma_{E}
is
\mathscr{H}^{n-1}
-measurable. As the measure space
(E,\mathscr{M}_{\mathscr{H}^{n-1}}(E),\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\>{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt}\hskip-0.2pt\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt,width=7.0pt,depth=0.0pt}\,E)
is the completion of

(E,\mathscr{B}(E),\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\>{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt}\hskip-0.2pt\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt,width=7.0pt,depth=0.0pt}\,E)
, we can find a Borel measurable
function
g_{E}\colon E\to\mathbb{R}
that coincides almost everywhere with

g\circ\gamma_{E}
. We let
f\in L^{1}(\Omega,\mathscr{B}(\Omega),\mathscr{I}^{n-1})
be the map

f\colon x\mapsto\begin{cases}1&\text{if }x\in E\text{ and }g_{E}(x)>\|g\|_{\infty}-\varepsilon\\
-1&\text{if }x\in E\text{ and }g_{E}(x)<-\left(\|g\|_{\infty}-\varepsilon\right)\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

of norm
\|f\|_{1}=\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(\gamma_{E}^{-1}(A))
. As
p\circ\gamma_{E}
is the inclusion map
E\to\Omega
, we have

\gamma_{E}(p(z))=z
for all
z\in\gamma_{E}(E)
. By
Lemma
2.4
(3), it then follows that
\gamma_{E}(p(z))=z

for
\hat{\mathscr{I}}^{n-1}
-almost all
z\in p^{-1}(E)
. Hence,
g
and

g\circ\gamma_{E}\circ p
coincide
\hat{\mathscr{I}}^{n-1}
-everywhere
on
p^{-1}(E)
. Moreover, we have

p^{-1}(E)\cap\{g\circ\gamma_{E}\circ p\neq g_{E}\circ p\}\subseteq p^{-1}(\{g\circ\gamma_{E}\neq g_{E}\})

By the Borel regularity of
\hat{\mathscr{I}}^{n-1}
and
Lemma
2.4
(2), we also infer that
g\circ\gamma_{E}\circ p=g_{E}\circ p
almost everywhere on
p^{-1}(E)
. Whence

\displaystyle\Upsilon(g)(f)=\int_{p^{-1}(E)}g(f\circ p)\,d\hat{\mathscr{I}}^{n-1}

\displaystyle=\int_{p^{-1}(E)}(g_{E}\circ p)(f\circ p)\,d\hat{\mathscr{I}}^{n-1}

\displaystyle=\int_{E}g_{E}f\,d\mathscr{I}^{n-1},

Thus, by construction of
f
, we have
\Upsilon(g)(f)\geqslant(\|g\|_{\infty}-\varepsilon)\|f\|_{1}
. This shows, as
\varepsilon>0
can be taken arbitrarily small, that
\|\Upsilon(g)\|=1
and

\Upsilon
is an isometry.

Now we turn to establishing the surjectivity of
\Upsilon
. For any
E\in\mathscr{E}
, we define a map
\alpha_{E}\in L^{1}(E,\mathscr{B}(E),\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\>{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt}\hskip-0.2pt\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt,width=7.0pt,depth=0.0pt}\,E)^{*}
that is the localized version of
\alpha
: for an
integrable function
f\in L^{1}(E,\mathscr{B}(E),\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\>{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt}\hskip-0.2pt\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt,width=7.0pt,depth=0.0pt}\,E)
, we
set
\alpha_{E}(f)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\alpha(\overline{f})
, where
\overline{f}

is the extension by zero of
f
to
\Omega
. Since
(E,\mathscr{B}(E),\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\>{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt}\hskip-0.2pt\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt,width=7.0pt,depth=0.0pt}\,E)
is a finite measure space, the standard duality
between
L^{1}
and
L^{\infty}
spaces holds, so there is a function

g_{E}\in L^{\infty}(E,\mathscr{B}(E),\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\>{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt}\hskip-0.2pt\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt,width=7.0pt,depth=0.0pt}\,E)
such that

\alpha_{E}(f)=\int_{E}g_{E}f\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\text{ for all }f\in L^{1}(E,\mathscr{B}(E),\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\>{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt}\hskip-0.2pt\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt,width=7.0pt,depth=0.0pt}\,E).

Obviously, for any
E,E^{\prime}\in\mathscr{E}
, we have

\int_{E\cap E^{\prime}}g_{E}f\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}=\int_{E\cap E^{\prime}}g_{E^{\prime}}f\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}

for any integrable function
f
on
E\cap E^{\prime}
, which implies that

g_{E}=g_{E^{\prime}}
almost everywhere on
E\cap E^{\prime}
.

Let
g\colon\hat{\Omega}\to\mathbb{R}
be the function partially defined
by

g(x,[E]_{x})\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{1}{\boldsymbol{\alpha}(n-1)r^{n-1}}\int_{E\cap B(x,r)}g_{E}\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}

whenever
x
is a density point of
g_{E}
. The compatibility between
the local Radon-Nikodým derivatives
g_{E}
,
E\in\mathscr{E}
,
together with Lemma
2.6
, guarantees that
g
is
well-defined. Denoting by
N\subseteq\hat{\Omega}
the set of points
at which
g
is not defined, we readily see that, for any
E\in\mathscr{E}
, the set
\gamma_{E}^{-1}(N)
consists of the
x\in E
that
are not density points of
g_{E}
. We readily have

\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(\gamma_{E}^{-1}(N))=0
, and this shows that

\hat{\mathscr{I}}^{n-1}(N)=0
. We can extend
g
to
\hat{\Omega}
by
sending the elements of
N
to an arbitrary value. Then
g
is

\hat{\mathscr{A}}
-measurable. Indeed, for any
E\in\mathscr{E}
the function

g\circ\gamma_{E}
coincides
\mathscr{H}^{n-1}
-almost every with
g_{E}
,
and therefore is
\mathscr{H}^{n-1}
-measurable.

Finally, we claim that
\alpha=\Upsilon(g)
. Let
f\in L^{1}(\Omega,\mathscr{B}(\Omega),\mathscr{I}^{n-1})
. Choose
\varepsilon>0
and set
E\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{|f|>\varepsilon\}
. As
\mathscr{I}^{n-1}(E)<\infty
, we can
suppose, up to a modification of
f
on a negligible set, that
E\in\mathscr{E}
. Let
f_{E}
be the restriction of
f
to
E
. Then,
recalling that
\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\>{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt}\hskip-0.2pt\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt,width=7.0pt,depth=0.0pt}\,E=\mathscr{I}^{n-1}\>{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt}\hskip-0.2pt\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt,width=7.0pt,depth=0.0pt}\,E
, we have

\alpha(\mathbbm{1}_{\{|f|>\varepsilon\}}f)=\alpha_{E}(f_{E})=\int_{E}g_{E}f_{E}\,d\mathscr{I}^{n-1}.

By Lemma
2.4
(2), we deduce that

\alpha(\mathbbm{1}_{\{|f|>\varepsilon\}}f)=\int_{p^{-1}(E)}(g_{E}\circ p)(f_{E}\circ p)\,d\hat{\mathscr{I}}^{n-1}.

Clearly,
f_{E}\circ p=f\circ p
on
p^{-1}(E)
and
g_{E}\circ p=g
almost everywhere on
\gamma_{E}(E)
. By Lemma
2.4
(3),
this ensures that
g_{E}\circ p=g\circ p
almost everywhere on

p^{-1}(E)
. Thus,

\alpha(\mathbbm{1}_{\{|f|>\varepsilon\}}f)=\int_{p^{-1}(E)}g(f\circ p)\,d\hat{\mathscr{I}}^{n-1}=\int\mathbbm{1}_{\{|f\circ p|>\varepsilon\}}g(f\circ p)\,d\hat{\mathscr{I}}^{n-1}.

Letting
\varepsilon\to 0
yields
\alpha(f)=\Upsilon(g)(f)
.
∎

2.8 Remark
. —

The above proof exhibits a construction of the density
g
, by
gluing the local Radon-Nikodým derivatives
g_{E}
in the measure
space
(\hat{\Omega},\hat{\mathscr{A}},\hat{\mathscr{I}}^{n-1})
. The gluing
is not possible within the measure space
(\Omega,\mathscr{B}(\Omega),\mathscr{I}^{n-1})
, as it is not localizable (if
\Omega
is non
empty). It was proven in
[
3
, Section 11]
that

(\hat{\Omega},\hat{\mathscr{A}},\hat{\mathscr{I}}^{n-1})
is the “minimal”
measure space in which a compatible family of functions defined on
subsets of
\Omega
can be glued. Using the concept introduced in

[
3
]
,
(\hat{\Omega},\hat{\mathscr{A}},\hat{\mathscr{I}}^{n-1})

is the strictly localizable version of the completion of
(\Omega,\mathscr{B}(\Omega),\mathscr{I}^{n-1})
.

Assuming that
(\hat{\Omega},\hat{\mathscr{A}},\hat{\mathscr{I}}^{n-1})
is
localizable, we can give an alternate proof of Theorem
2.7
:
the map
\iota\colon L^{1}(\Omega,\mathscr{B}(\Omega),\mathscr{I}^{n-1})\to L^{1}(\hat{\Omega},\hat{\mathscr{A}},\hat{\mathscr{I}}^{n-1})
that sends
f
to

f\circ p
is an isometry, as
\|f\circ p\|_{1}=\|f\|_{1}
by
Lemma
2.4
(3). By the localizability of
(\hat{\Omega},\hat{\mathscr{A}},\hat{\mathscr{I}}^{n-1})
, the dual of
L^{1}(\hat{\Omega},\hat{\mathscr{A}},\hat{\mathscr{I}}^{n-1})
is
L^{\infty}(\hat{\Omega},\hat{\mathscr{A}},\hat{\mathscr{I}}^{n-1})
, in such a way that
\Upsilon
is
just the adjoint map of
\iota
. Reasoning as before,
\Upsilon
is
injective, which entails that
\iota
and then
\Upsilon
are
isometric isomorphisms.

2.9
Approximate tangent cone of an approximate germ. —

Let
E\in\mathscr{E}
and
x\in E
. The approximate tangent cone

\rmTan^{n-1}(E,x)
(see
[
6
, 3.2.16]
) is unchanged if we
substitute
E
with any
E^{\prime}\in\mathscr{E}_{x}
such that
E\sim_{x}E^{\prime}
. Because of this, we can define the approximate tangent cone

\rmTan^{n-1}([E]_{x})\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\rmTan^{n-1}(E,x)
of an approximate
germ. We recall that
\rmTan^{n-1}(E,x)
is an
(n-1)
-plane at

\mathscr{H}^{n-1}
-almost every point
x\in E
, and that the function
x\mapsto\rmTan^{n-1}(E,x)\in\mathbf{G}(n-1,n)
, defined almost
everywhere on
E
, is
\mathscr{H}^{n-1}
-measurable. Here
\mathbf{G}(n-1,n)

denotes the Grassmann manifold of hyperplanes in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

2.10 Theorem
. —

Let
\varphi\colon SBV(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous linear
functional. There are fields
f\in L^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathscr{B}(\Omega),\mathscr{L}^{n})
,
g\in L^{\infty}(\hat{\Omega},\hat{\mathscr{A}},\hat{\mathscr{I}}^{n-1};\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
h\in L^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathscr{B}(\Omega),\mathscr{L}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that

(1)

\varphi(u)=\int_{\Omega}fu\,d\mathscr{L}^{n}+\int_{\hat{\Omega}}g\cdot(j_{u}\circ p)d\hat{\mathscr{I}}^{n-1}+\int_{\Omega}h\cdot\nabla u\,d\mathscr{L}^{n}

where the distributional gradient
Du
of
u
is decomposed into its
Lebesgue part
\mathscr{L}^{n}\>{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt}\hskip-0.2pt\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt,width=7.0pt,depth=0.0pt}\,\nabla u
and its jump part
D^{j}u=j_{u}\,\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\>{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt}\hskip-0.2pt\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt,width=7.0pt,depth=0.0pt}\,S_{u}
. Moreover, one can select
g
in such a way
that
g(x,[E]_{x})
is normal to
\rmTan^{n-1}([E]_{x})
for

\hat{\mathscr{I}}^{n-1}
-almost all
(x,[E]_{x})\in\hat{\Omega}
.

Proof.

For each
u\in SBV(\Omega)
, we have

\|u\|_{SBV(\Omega)}=\int_{\Omega}|u|\,d\mathscr{L}^{n}+\int_{S_{u}}\|j_{u}\|\,d\mathscr{H}^{n-1}+\int_{\Omega}\|\nabla u\|\,d\mathscr{L}^{n}.

Here, the approximate discontinuity set
S_{u}
(see

[
2
, 3.63]
) is Borel measurable and
countably
(n-1)
-rectifiable
[
2
, 3.64(a) and
3.78]
. We recall that
j_{u}=0
outside

S_{u}
, whereas
j_{u}=(u^{+}-u^{-})\nu_{u}
almost everywhere on

S_{u}
. In particular,
\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\>{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt}\hskip-0.2pt\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt,width=7.0pt,depth=0.0pt}\,j_{u}=\mathscr{I}^{n-1}\>{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt}\hskip-0.2pt\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt,width=7.0pt,depth=0.0pt}\,j_{u}
. Therefore,
u\mapsto(u,j_{u},\nabla u)
is an
isometric embedding

SBV(\Omega)\to L^{1}(\Omega,\mathscr{B}(\Omega),\mathscr{L}^{n})\times L^{1}(\Omega,\mathscr{B}(\Omega),\mathscr{I}^{n-1};\mathbb{R}^{n})\times L^{1}(\Omega,\mathscr{B}(\Omega),\mathscr{L}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Hence,
\varphi
can be split into three parts
\varphi\colon u\mapsto\varphi_{1}(u)+\varphi_{2}(j_{u})+\varphi_{3}(\nabla u)
, where

\varphi_{1}\in L^{1}(\Omega,\mathscr{B}(\Omega),\mathscr{L}^{n})^{*}
,
\varphi_{2}\in L^{1}(\Omega,\mathscr{B}(\Omega),\mathscr{I}^{n-1};\mathbb{R}^{n})^{*}
, and
\varphi_{3}\in L^{1}(\Omega,\mathscr{B}(\Omega),\mathscr{L}^{n-1};\mathbb{R}^{n})^{*}
. The
duality theorem
2.7
provides an integral representation for the term

\varphi_{2}
that acts on the jump part of the derivative and yields
the formula (
1
).

Regarding the second assertion, we claim that
j_{u}\circ p(x,[E]_{x})=j_{u}(x)
is normal to
\rmTan^{n-1}([E]_{x})
for almost all
(x,[E]_{x})\in\hat{\Omega}
. For this, we need to show that, for all
E\in\mathscr{E}
,

\{x\in E:j_{u}(x)\text{ not normal to }\rmTan^{n-1}(E,x)\}\\
=\{x\in E\cap S_{u}:j_{u}(x)\text{ not normal to }\rmTan^{n-1}(E,x)\}

is
\mathscr{H}^{n-1}
-negligible. This is clear, once we remark that

\rmTan^{n-1}(E,x)=\rmTan^{n-1}(S_{u},x)
for almost every
x\in E\cap S_{u}
and that
j_{u}
is normal to
S_{u}
almost everywhere on

S_{u}
. Hence, we can replace the vector field
g
by its normal part

\tilde{g}
(that is,
\tilde{g}(x,[E]_{x})
is the orthogonal
projection of
g(x,[E]_{x})
onto
\rmTan^{n-1}([E]_{x})^{\perp}
). The
measurability of
\tilde{g}
follows from the discussion in
Paragraph
2.9
.
∎

Mordell–Tornheim Zeta Values, Their Alternating Version, and Their Finite Analogs Email: crystal.wang.19@bishops.com, zhaoj@ihes.fr
Crystal Wang, Jianqiang Zhao

Abstract
The purpose of this paper is two-fold. First, we consider the classical Mordell–Tornheim zeta values and their alternating version. It is well-known that these values can be expressed as rational linear combinations of multiple zeta values (MZVs) and the alternating MZVs, respectively. We show that, however, the spaces generated by these values over the rational numbers are in general much smaller than the MZV space and the alternating MZV space, respectively, which disproves a conjecture of Bachmann, Takeyama and Tasaka. Second, we study supercongruences of some finite sums of multiple integer variables. This kind of congruences is a variation of the so called finite multiple zeta values when the moduli are primes instead of prime powers. In general, these objects can be transformed to finite analogs of the Mordell–Tornheim sums which can be reduced to multiple harmonic sums. This approach not only simplifies the proof of a few previous results but also generalizes some of them. At the end of the paper, we also provide a conjecture supported by strong numerical evidence.

Introduction

Let
{\mathbb{N}}
and
{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
be the set of positive integers and nonnegative integers, respectively.
The classical Mordell–Tornheim zeta values (MTZVs)
are defined as follows. Let
k\geq 2
be a positive integer.
For all
s_{1},\dots,s_{k+1}\in{\mathbb{N}}

T(s_{1},\dots,s_{k};s_{k+1}):=\sum_{m_{1}=1}^{\infty}\cdots\sum_{m_{k}=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots m_{k}^{s_{k}}(m_{1}+\cdots+m_{k})^{s_{k+1}}}.

(1)

Note that in the literature this function is also denoted
by
\zeta_{\rm{MT},k}(s_{1},\dots,s_{k};s_{k+1})
.
They were first investigated by Tornheim
[
16
]
in the case
k=2
,
and later by Mordell
[
14
]
and Hoffman
[
11
]
with

s_{1}=\cdots=s_{k}=1
. On the other hand, by
[
4
, Lemma 3.1]

every such value can be expressed
as a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-linear combination of multiple zeta values (MZVs)
which are defined by

\zeta(s_{1},\dots,s_{d}):=\sum_{0<k_{1}<\cdots<k_{d}}\frac{1}{k_{1}^{s_{1}}\dots k_{d}^{s_{d}}}

for all
s_{1},\dots,s_{d-1}\geq 1,s_{d}\geq 2
.

After the seminal work of Hoffman
[
11
]
and
Zagier
[
19
]
much more results concerning MZVs have been found
(see the book by the second author
[
23
]
and Hoffman’s webpage
[
10
]
).
Therefore, we can derive a lot of relations between MTZVs. A natural question now arises:
can every MZV be expressed as a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-linear combination of MTZVs? We will give a negative
answer in section 1.

Similar to the study of MZVs, we may add some alternating signs to MTZVs and call them
alternating MTZVs. Or even more generally, we may consider the multiple variable function

MT\left({{s_{1},\dots,s_{k};s_{k+1}}\atop{z_{1},\dots,z_{k};z_{k+1}}}\right):=\sum_{m_{1}=1}^{\infty}\cdots\sum_{m_{k}=1}^{\infty}\frac{z_{1}^{m_{1}}\dots z_{k}^{m_{k}}z_{k+1}^{m_{1}+\cdots+m_{k}}}{m_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots m_{k}^{s_{k}}(m_{1}+\cdots+m_{k})^{s_{k+1}}}.

(2)

For instance, Matsumoto et al.’s Mordell–Tornheim
L
-functions (see
[
12
]
)
and the second author’s colored Tornheim’s double series (see
[
22
]
) are
both special cases of (
2
). When
z_{1},\dotsc,z_{k+1}=\pm 1
we call these values

alternating MTZVs
and abbreviate them by putting a bar on top of the arguments
whenever the corresponding
z_{j}
’s are
-1
. For example,

T(\bar{3},2;4)=MT\left({{\,3\,,2;4}\atop{-1,1;1}}\right):=\sum_{m_{1},m_{2}=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{m_{1}}}{m_{1}^{3}m_{2}^{2}(m_{1}+m_{2})^{4}}.

For any
n,d\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
{\bf s}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{d})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{d}
, we define
the
multiple harmonic sums
(MHSs) and their
p
-restricted version for primes
p
by

\displaystyle H_{n}({\bf s}):=\sum_{0<k_{1}<\cdots<k_{d}<n}\frac{1}{k_{1}^{s_{1}}\dots k_{d}^{s_{d}}},\quad H_{n}^{(p)}({\bf s}):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<k_{1}<\cdots<k_{d}<n\\
p\nmid k_{1},\dots,p\nmid k_{d}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{k_{1}^{s_{1}}\dots k_{d}^{s_{d}}}.

Here,
d
is called the
depth
, and
|{\bf s}|:=s_{1}+\dots+s_{d}
the
weight
of the MHS.
For example,
H_{n+1}(1)
is often called the
n
th harmonic number.
In general, as
n\to\infty
, we see that
H_{n}({\bf s})\to\zeta({\bf s})
which are
the multiple zeta values (MZVs) when
s_{d}>1
.

More than fifteen years ago, the second author
[
20
]
discovered the curious congruence

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j+k=p\\
i,j,k>0\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{ijk}\equiv-2B_{p-3}\pmod{p}

(3)

for all primes
p\geq 3
. Since then, several different types of generalizations
have been found. For example, see the references

[
6
,
7
,
13
,
15
,
17
,
24
]
.
In this paper, we will concentrate on congruences of the following type of sums.
Let
{\mathcal{P}}_{p}
be the set of positive integers not divisible by
p
. For
any positive integers
r
,
d
,
s_{1},\dots,s_{d}
and any prime
p
, we define

Z_{p^{r}}(s_{1},\dots,s_{d}):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}+k_{2}+\dots+k_{d}=p^{r}\\
k_{1},\dots,k_{d}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{p}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{k_{1}^{s_{1}}k_{2}^{s_{2}}\dots k_{d}^{s_{d}}}.

We will first decompose the above sums into finite Mordell–Tornheim sums (see (
9
)) which in turn can
be studied using the theory of multiple harmonic sum congruences.

For example, Yang and Cai generalize (
3
) in
[
18
]
as follows.
For
{\alpha},{\beta},{\gamma}\in{\mathbb{N}}
, if
w={\alpha}+{\beta}+{\gamma}
is odd and prime
p>w
, then we have

Z_{p^{r}}({\alpha},{\beta},{\gamma})\equiv p^{r-1}Z_{p}({\alpha},{\beta},{\gamma})\pmod{p^{r}},

(4)

where

\displaystyle Z_{p}({\alpha},{\beta},{\gamma})\equiv

\displaystyle\,\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\max\{{\alpha},{\beta}\}}\frac{(-1)^{{\alpha}+{\beta}-j}}{{\alpha}+{\beta}+{\gamma}}\left(\binom{{\alpha}+{\beta}-j-1}{{\alpha}-1}+\binom{{\alpha}+{\beta}-j-1}{{\beta}-1}\right)\binom{{\alpha}+{\beta}+{\gamma}}{j}\right.

\displaystyle\,\left.+2(-1)^{\gamma}\binom{{\alpha}+{\beta}}{{\alpha}}{\delta}_{p-1,{\alpha}+{\beta}+{\gamma}}\right)B_{p-{\alpha}-{\beta}-{\gamma}}\pmod{p}.

In section
3
we will extend (

) to the following:
if
{\alpha}+{\beta}+{\gamma}
is even, then for all
r\geq 1

Z_{p^{r}}({\alpha},{\beta},{\gamma})\equiv Z_{p}({\alpha},{\beta},{\gamma})p^{2r-2}\pmod{p^{2r}},

(5)

We also determine the value
Z_{p}({\alpha},{\beta},{\gamma},{\lambda})
when the weight is odd in Theorem
3.3
.

At the end of the paper, we will present a conjecture related to some families of finite Mordell–Tornheim sums.

2
Classical (alternating) Mordell–Tornheim zeta values

It is well-known that every MTZV can be expressed as a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-linear combination of MZVs (see
[
4
]
).
However, it turns out that the space generated by MZVs is much larger so that MTZVs do not generate whole MZV space over
{\mathbb{Q}}
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
{\mathsf{MZV}}_{w}
and
{\mathsf{MTZV}}_{w}
be the
{\mathbb{Q}}
-vector spaces generated by MZVs and MTZVs of weight
w\geq 3
, respectively.
Then
{\mathsf{MTZV}}_{w}={\mathsf{MZV}}_{w}
for all
3\leq w\leq 14
. Further, let
P_{w}
be the Padovan numbers defined by

P_{2}=P_{3}=P_{4}=1
and
P_{w}=P_{w-2}+P_{w-3}
for all
w\geq 5
.
Then
\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}{\mathsf{MTZV}}_{15}<P_{15}=28
and for all
w\geq 40
,

\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}{\mathsf{MTZV}}_{w}<P_{w}.

Proof.

Let
p(n)
be the partition function of any positive integer
n
. A celebrated theorem of
Hardy and Ramanujan
[
9
]
gives the asymptotic formula (see also
[
1
, p. 70, (5.1.2)]
)

p(n)\sim\frac{1}{4n\sqrt{3}}\exp(\pi\sqrt{2n/3})\qquad\text{as }n\to\infty.

For any fixed weight
w>2
, let
N_{w}
be the number of MZTVs of weight
n
. Then clearly

N_{w}=\sum_{j=2}^{w-1}(p(j)-1)

and

\log(N_{w})=O(\sqrt{w}).

On the other hand, it is well-known by Zagier’s conjecture that
d_{w}=\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}{\mathsf{MZV}}_{w}
form the Padovan sequence as given
in the theorem. Further, Brown
[
5
]
showed that
d_{w}\leq P_{w}
. Thus it is not hard to see that

\log(d_{w})=O(w).

Consequently the space generated by MTVs of fixed weight
w
should be much
smaller than
{\mathsf{MZV}}_{w}
for all sufficiently large
w
.
It turns out that for all
w\geq 40
, we can use the more accurate bound of
N_{w}
by partition functions
to obtain that
N_{w}<P_{w}
with the computer-aided computation. Hence

\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}{\mathsf{MTZV}}_{w}<P_{w}

for all
w\geq 40
. By straightforward computation with the aid of MAPLE one can
see that for all weight
3\leq w\leq 14
, the two
{\mathbb{Q}}
-spaces are the same.
But for weight
w=15
, one already sees that
{\mathsf{MTZV}}_{15}\leq 27<P_{15}=28
.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

We want to remark that our Theorem
2.1
clearly disproves a conjecture of Bachmann, Takeyama and Tasaka (see

[
2
, Conjecture 2.4]
).

Similarly, every alternating MTZV can be expressed as a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-linear combination of alternating MZVs.
For any
w\geq 3
, let
{\mathsf{AMT}}_{w}
and
{\mathsf{AMZ}}_{w}
be the
{\mathbb{Q}}
-vector spaces generated by alternating MTZVs
and alternating MZVs of weight
w
, respectively. For example,

\displaystyle T(1,1;1)=2\zeta(3),\qquad

\displaystyle T(\bar{1},1;1)=-\frac{5}{8}\zeta(3),\quad

\displaystyle T(\bar{1},\bar{1};1)=\frac{1}{4}\zeta(3),

\displaystyle T(1,1,1;1)=\frac{\pi^{4}}{15},\quad

\displaystyle T(\bar{1},\bar{1},1;1)=\frac{\pi^{4}}{240},\quad

\displaystyle T(\bar{1},1,1;1)=-\frac{\pi^{4}}{72}-\zeta(1,\bar{3}),

\displaystyle T(1,2;1)=\frac{\pi^{4}}{72},\quad

\displaystyle T(\bar{1},\bar{2};1)=\frac{\pi^{4}}{288},\quad

\displaystyle T(\{\bar{1}\}_{3};1)=-\frac{\pi^{4}}{240}+3\zeta(1,\bar{3}),

\displaystyle T(1,1;2)=\frac{\pi^{4}}{180},\quad

\displaystyle T(\bar{1},\bar{1};2)=2\zeta(1,\bar{3}),\quad

\displaystyle T(\bar{1},2;1)=-\frac{\pi^{4}}{240}-\zeta(1,\bar{3}),

\displaystyle T(1,\bar{2};1)=-\frac{7\pi^{4}}{720}+\zeta(1,\bar{3}),\quad

\displaystyle T(\bar{1},1;2)=-\frac{\pi^{4}}{480}-\zeta(1,\bar{3}).

Therefore
{\mathsf{AMT}}_{3}=\langle\zeta(3)\rangle_{\mathbb{Q}}
and
{\mathsf{AMT}}_{4}=\langle\zeta(4),\zeta(1,\bar{3})\rangle_{\mathbb{Q}}
. As an alternating analog to Theorem
2.1
we have the following result.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
F_{w}
be the Fibonacci numbers defined by
F_{0}=F_{1}=1
and
F_{w}=F_{w-1}+F_{w-2}
for all
w\geq 2
.
Then for all
3\leq w\leq 12
and
w\geq 34
, we have

\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}{\mathsf{AMT}}_{w}<F_{w}

Proof.

When
3\leq w\leq 12
we computed the set of generators of
{\mathsf{AMT}}_{w}
(see Remark
2.6
).
Let
A_{w}
be the number of alternating MTZVs of weight
w
. For each MTZV of weight
w
, we first determine
how many ways to put some alternating signs. Suppose we have such an MTZV

T(\{s_{1}\}_{j_{1}},\dotsc,\{s_{r}\}_{j_{r}};w-i),\quad s_{1}<\dotsm<s_{r},

where for each string
{\bf s}
we denote the string obtained by repeating
{\bf s}
exactly
j
times by
\{{\bf s}\}_{j}
.
Now for each
\{s_{\ell}\}_{j_{\ell}}
(
1\leq\ell\leq r
) there are
j_{\ell}+1
ways to put alternating signs because
of the symmetry. Thus the number of ways to put some alternating signs on this MTZV is

\prod_{\ell=1}^{r}(j_{\ell}+1)\qquad\text{subject to the condition}\qquad\sum_{\ell=1}^{r}j_{\ell}s_{\ell}=i.

(6)

Then it is not hard to see that for fixed
i
the maximal value of (
6
) is achieved when

i=m(m+1)/2
is a triangular number and the MTZV is
T(1,2,\dotsc,m;w-i)
. In this case,
the value of (
6
) is
2^{m}
where
m=(\sqrt{8i+1}-1)/2
. Moreover, the subspace
{\mathsf{MTZV}}_{34}
has dimension bounded by the Padovan number
P_{34}
. Thus

\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}{\mathsf{AMT}}_{w}\leq A_{w}-N_{w}+P_{w}\leq\sum_{i=2}^{w-1}\left\lfloor\frac{\sqrt{8i+1}-1}{2}\right\rfloor(p(i)-1)-N_{w}+P_{w}<F_{w}

for all
w\geq 34
by computer computation.
∎

In general, we have the following conjecture.

Conjecture 2.4
.

For every
w\geq 3
,
{\mathsf{AMT}}_{w}
can be generated by the following set of elements

C_{w}:=\left\{\prod\zeta({\bf k};1,\dots,1,-1)(2\pi i)^{2n}:2n+\sum_{\bf k}\lambda({\bf k})|{\bf k}|=w,n\geq 0\right\},

(7)

where the product runs through all possible Lyndon words
{\bf k}\neq(1)

on odd numbers (with
1<3<5<\cdots
) with multiplicity
\lambda({\bf k})
so that
2n+\sum_{{\bf k}}\lambda({\bf k})|{\bf k}|=w
.

Proposition 2.5
.

If Conjecture
2.4
holds then

\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}{\mathsf{AMT}}_{w}\leq F_{w-2}\quad\forall w\geq 3.

Proof.

Deligne proved that
[
8
, Thm. 7.2]
for all
w\geq 1
the
{\mathbb{Q}}
-vector space
{\mathsf{AMZ}}_{w}

of alternating MZVs can be generated by

B_{w}:=\left\{\prod\zeta({\bf k};1,\dots,1,-1)(2\pi i)^{2n}:2n+\sum_{\bf k}\lambda({\bf k})|{\bf k}|=w,n\geq 0\right\},

(8)

where the product runs through all possible Lyndon words
{\bf k}

on odd numbers (with
1<3<5<\cdots
) with multiplicity
\lambda({\bf k})
so that
2n+\sum_{{\bf k}}\lambda({\bf k})|{\bf k}|=w
.
Note that the ordering of indices in the definition of Euler sums is opposite in loc. sit.
So the definition of Lyndon words here has opposite order, too. Furthermore, if a period conjecture of
Grothendieck
[
8
, Conjecture 5.6]
holds
then
B_{w}
is a basis of
{\mathsf{AMZ}}_{w}
. In particular,
\sharp B_{w}=F_{w}
is the Fibonacci number.
Hence, if Conjecture
2.4
holds then
{\mathsf{AMT}}_{w}
is generated by
C_{w}=B_{w}\setminus\zeta(\bar{1})B_{w-1}
and
therefore

\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}{\mathsf{AMT}}_{w}\leq F_{w}-F_{w-1}=F_{w-2}\quad\forall w\geq 3,

as desired.
∎

3
Supercongrence related to finite Mordell–Tornheim zeta values

Recall that
{\mathcal{P}}_{p}
is the set of positive integers not divisible by
p
.
For any prime
p
and positive integer
r
, we call the sum

T_{p^{r}}({\alpha}_{1},\dots,{\alpha}_{m};{\lambda}):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}+\dots+k_{m},k_{1},\dots,k_{m}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{p}\\
k_{1}+\dots+k_{m}<p^{r}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{k_{1}^{{\alpha}_{1}}\cdots k_{m}^{{\alpha}_{m}}(k_{1}+\dots+k_{m})^{\lambda}}

a
finite Mordell–Tornheim sum
.
We then define the
p
-restricted finite Mordell–Tornheim sums as follows.
For any
m,n,r\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
{\alpha}_{1},\dots,{\alpha}_{m},{\lambda}_{1},\dots,{\lambda}_{n}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, we set

T_{p^{r}}({\alpha}_{1},\dots,{\alpha}_{m};{\lambda}_{1},\dots,{\lambda}_{n}):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}+\dots+k_{m}=u_{1}<\dots<u_{n}<p^{r}\\
k_{1},\dots,k_{m},u_{1},u_{2}-u_{1},\dots,u_{n}-u_{n-1},u_{n}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{p}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{k_{1}^{{\alpha}_{1}}\cdots k_{m}^{{\alpha}_{m}}u_{1}^{{\lambda}_{1}}\cdots u_{n}^{{\lambda}_{n}}}.

Here, we call
m+n-1
the depth and
{\alpha}_{1}+\dots+{\alpha}_{m}+{\lambda}_{1}+\dots+{\lambda}_{n}
the weight
of this sum.

By definition we have

\displaystyle\,Z_{p^{r}}({\alpha}_{1},\dots,{\alpha}_{n+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}+\dots+k_{n+1}=p^{r}\\
k_{1},\dots,k_{n+1}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{p}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{k_{1}^{{\alpha}_{1}}\cdots k_{n+1}^{{\alpha}_{n+1}}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u=k_{1}+\dots+k_{n}<p^{r}\\
k_{1},\dots,k_{n},u\in{\mathcal{P}}_{p}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{k_{1}^{{\alpha}_{1}}\cdots k_{n}^{{\alpha}_{n}}(p^{r}-u)^{{\alpha}_{n+1}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,(-1)^{{\alpha}_{n+1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u=k_{1}+\dots+k_{n}<p^{r}\\
k_{1},\dots,k_{n},u\in{\mathcal{P}}_{p}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{k_{1}^{{\alpha}_{1}}\cdots k_{n}^{{\alpha}_{n}}u^{{\alpha}_{n+1}}}\left(1-\frac{p^{r}}{u}\right)^{-{\alpha}_{n+1}}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\,(-1)^{{\alpha}_{n+1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u=k_{1}+\dots+k_{n}<p^{r}\\
k_{1},\dots,k_{n},u\in{\mathcal{P}}_{p}\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{1}{k_{1}^{{\alpha}_{1}}\cdots k_{n}^{{\alpha}_{n}}u^{{\alpha}_{n+1}}}+\frac{{\alpha}_{n+1}p^{r}}{k_{1}^{{\alpha}_{1}}\cdots k_{n}^{{\alpha}_{n}}u^{{\alpha}_{n+1}+1}}\right)\pmod{p^{2r}}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\,(-1)^{{\alpha}_{n+1}}\Big(T_{p^{r}}({\alpha}_{1},\dots,{\alpha}_{n};{\alpha}_{n+1})+{\alpha}_{n+1}p^{r}T_{p^{r}}({\alpha}_{1},\dots,{\alpha}_{n};{\alpha}_{n+1}+1)\Big)\pmod{p^{2r}}.

(9)

Therefore, we have decomposed
Z_{p^{r}}({\alpha}_{1},\dots,{\alpha}_{n+1})

as a sum of finite Mordell–Tornheim sums.

Define

{\mathcal{H}}_{p^{r}}(s_{1},\dots,s_{d}):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<u_{1}<\cdots<u_{d}<p^{r}\\
u_{1},u_{2}-u_{1},\dots,u_{d}-u_{d-1},u_{d}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{p}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{u_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots u_{d}^{s_{d}}}.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
p
be a prime,
a,b,r\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
p>w=a+b
. If
w
is odd then

{\mathcal{H}}_{p^{r}}(a,b)\equiv p^{r-1}{\mathcal{H}}_{p}(a,b)\pmod{p^{r}}.

If
w
is even then

{\mathcal{H}}_{p^{r}}(a,b)\equiv p^{2r-2}{\mathcal{H}}_{p}(a,b)\pmod{p^{2r}}.

Proof.

The case
r=1
is trivial so we may assume
r\geq 2
.

First we assume the weight is even.
By Euler’s theorem, setting
m=\varphi(p^{2r})-a
and

n=\varphi(p^{2r})-b
we get

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}_{p^{r}}(a,b)

\displaystyle\,\equiv\sum_{k<l<p^{r};k,l,l-k\in{\mathcal{P}}_{p}}k^{m}l^{n}

\displaystyle\pmod{p^{2r}}

\displaystyle\,\equiv\sum_{k<l<p^{r};k,l\in{\mathcal{P}}_{p}}k^{m}l^{n}-\sum_{t<p^{r-1},\ k+pt<p^{r};k\in{\mathcal{P}}_{p}}k^{m}(k+pt)^{n}

\displaystyle\pmod{p^{2r}}

\displaystyle\,\equiv\sum_{k<l<p^{r}}k^{m}l^{n}-\sum_{t<p^{r-1}}\sum_{k<p^{r}-pt}k^{m}(k+pt)^{n}

\displaystyle\pmod{p^{2r}}

since
\min\{m,n\}>\varphi(p^{2r})-w\geq(p^{2r-1}-1)(p-1)\geq 2r.
Now

\displaystyle\sum_{k<l<p^{r}}k^{m}l^{n}=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\binom{m+1}{j}\frac{B_{j}}{m+1}\sum_{l<p^{r}}l^{m+1-j+n}

\displaystyle\,=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\binom{m+1}{j}\frac{B_{j}}{m+1}\sum_{i=0}^{m+n+1-j}\binom{m+n+2-j}{i}\frac{B_{i}p^{r(m+n+2-j-i)}}{m+n+2-j}

\displaystyle\,\equiv p^{r}\sum_{j=0}^{m}\binom{m+1}{j}\frac{B_{j}B_{m+n+1-j}}{m+1}+p^{2r}\sum_{j=0}^{m}\binom{m+1}{j}\frac{(m+n+1-j)B_{j}B_{m+n-j}}{2(m+1)}

\displaystyle\pmod{p^{2r}}

\displaystyle\,\equiv-\frac{p^{r}B_{m+n}}{2}+p^{2r}\sum_{j=0}^{m}\binom{m+1}{j}\frac{(m+n+1-j)B_{j}B_{m+n-j}}{2(m+1)}

\displaystyle\pmod{p^{2r}}

since
m+n
is even. Note that in the last sum above, if
(p-1)|j
or
(p-1)|(m+n-j)

then
B_{j}B_{m+n-j}
may not be
p
-integral, but
pB_{j}B_{m+n-j}
must be since

(p-1)\nmid(m+n)
. On the other hand,

\displaystyle\,\sum_{t<p^{r-1}}\sum_{k<p^{r}-pt}k^{m}(k+pt)^{n}

\displaystyle\,=\sum_{s=0}^{n}\binom{n}{s}\sum_{t<p^{r-1},\ k<p^{r}-pt}(pt)^{n-s}k^{m+s}

\displaystyle\,\equiv\sum_{s=0}^{n}\binom{n}{s}\sum_{j=0}^{m+s}\binom{m+s+1}{j}\frac{B_{j}}{m+s+1}\sum_{t<p^{r-1}}(p^{r}-pt)^{m+s+1-j}(pt)^{n-s}\pmod{p^{2r}}

\displaystyle\,\equiv\sum_{s=0}^{n}\binom{n}{s}\sum_{j=0}^{m+s}\binom{m+s+1}{j}\frac{B_{j}}{m+s+1}

\displaystyle\,\ \hskip 71.13188pt\sum_{t<p^{r-1}}\big[(m+s+1-j)p^{r}(-pt)^{m+s-j}+(-pt)^{m+s+1-j}\big](pt)^{n-s}\pmod{p^{2r}}

\displaystyle\,\equiv B_{m+n}\sum_{t<p^{r-1}}p^{r}+\sum_{s=0}^{n}\sum_{\ j=0;\ j\neq m+n}^{m+s}\sum_{i=0}^{m+n-j}\binom{n}{s}\binom{m+s+1}{j}\binom{m+n+1-j}{i}

\displaystyle\,\ \hskip 71.13188pt\cdot\frac{(m+s+1-j)(-1)^{m+s-j}B_{j}B_{i}}{(m+s+1)(m+n+1-j)}p^{r+m+n-j+(r-1)(m+n+1-j-i)}

\displaystyle\,-pB_{m+n}\sum_{t<p^{r-1}}t+\sum_{s=0}^{n}\binom{n}{s}\sum_{\ j=0;\ j\neq m+n}^{m+s}\sum_{i=0}^{m+n+1-j}\binom{m+s+1}{j}\binom{m+n+2-j}{i}

\displaystyle\,\ \hskip 71.13188pt\cdot\frac{(-1)^{m+s+1-j}B_{j}B_{i}}{(m+s+1)(m+n+2-j)}p^{m+n+1-j+(r-1)(m+n+2-j-i)}\pmod{p^{2r}}

\displaystyle\,\equiv B_{m+n}\frac{p^{r}(p^{r-1}-1)}{2}\pmod{p^{2r}}.

Combining all the above together, we see that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}_{p^{r}}(a,b)\equiv

\displaystyle\,-\frac{p^{2r-1}B_{m+n}}{2}+p^{2r}\sum_{j=0}^{m}\binom{m+1}{j}\frac{(m+n+1-j)B_{j}B_{m+n-j}}{2(m+1)}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\,p^{2r-2}{\mathcal{H}}_{p}(a,b)\pmod{p^{2r}}

which follows from the proof of the case with
r=1
while keeping
m=\varphi(p^{2r})-a
and

n=\varphi(p^{2r})-b
.
This completes the proof of the theorem when the weight is even. The proof
of the odd weight case is similar but simpler so we leave it
to the interested reader.
∎

Theorem 3.2
.

For all
r,{\alpha},{\beta},{\gamma}\in{\mathbb{N}}
and primes
p>{\alpha}+{\beta}+{\gamma}
, if
{\alpha}+{\beta}+{\gamma}
is odd then we have

Z_{p^{r}}({\alpha},{\beta},{\gamma})\equiv Z_{p}({\alpha},{\beta},{\gamma})p^{r-1}\pmod{p^{r}}.

(10)

Furthermore, if
{\alpha}+{\beta}+{\gamma}
is even, then

Z_{p^{r}}({\alpha},{\beta},{\gamma})\equiv Z_{p}({\alpha},{\beta},{\gamma})p^{2r-2}\pmod{p^{2r}}.

(11)

Proof.

By a result of Bradley and Zhou,
it can be shown that all Mordell–Tornheim sums can be reduced to the
finite multiple zeta values defined in the introduction.
Indeed, by
[
4
, Lemma 3.1]
, when
n=2
, we have

\displaystyle T_{p^{r}}({\alpha},{\beta};{\gamma})=

\displaystyle\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u=k_{1}+k_{2}<p^{r}\\
k_{1},k_{2},u\in{\mathcal{P}}_{p}\end{subarray}}\left(\sum_{a=0}^{{\alpha}-1}\binom{a+{\beta}-1}{a}\frac{k_{1}^{a}k_{2}^{\beta}}{u^{a+{\beta}}}+\sum_{b=0}^{{\beta}-1}\binom{b+{\alpha}-1}{b}\frac{k_{1}^{\alpha}k_{2}^{b}}{u^{{\alpha}+b}}\right)\frac{1}{k_{1}^{\alpha}k_{2}^{\beta}u^{\gamma}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\sum_{a=0}^{{\alpha}-1}\binom{a+{\beta}-1}{a}{\mathcal{H}}_{p^{r}}({\alpha}-a,a+{\beta}+{\gamma})+\sum_{b=0}^{{\beta}-1}\binom{b+{\alpha}-1}{b}{\mathcal{H}}_{p^{r}}({\beta}-b,b+{\alpha}+{\gamma})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\sum_{a=1}^{{\alpha}}\binom{{\alpha}+{\beta}-a-1}{{\alpha}-a}{\mathcal{H}}_{p^{r}}(a,w-a)+\sum_{b=1}^{{\beta}}\binom{{\alpha}+{\beta}-b-1}{{\beta}-b}{\mathcal{H}}_{p^{r}}(b,w-b),

where
w={\alpha}+{\beta}+{\gamma}
. Thus the theorem follows from the decomposition
formula (
9
) and Theorem
3.1
immediately.
∎

When
n=3
, the situation is completely similar although the formulas are more involved.
By straight-forward computation, we get

\displaystyle T_{p^{r}}({\alpha},{\beta},{\gamma};{\lambda})=

\displaystyle\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u=k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3}<p^{r}\\
k_{1},k_{2},k_{3},u\in{\mathcal{P}}_{p}\end{subarray}}\left(\sum_{a=0}^{{\alpha}-1}\sum_{b=0}^{{\beta}-1}\binom{a+b+{\gamma}-1}{a,b,{\gamma}-1}\frac{k_{1}^{a}k_{2}^{b}k_{3}^{\gamma}}{u^{{\gamma}+a+b}}\right.

\displaystyle\,\ \qquad\ \qquad+\sum_{a=0}^{{\alpha}-1}\sum_{c=0}^{{\gamma}-1}\binom{a+c+{\beta}-1}{a,c,{\beta}-1}\frac{k_{1}^{a}k_{2}^{\beta}k_{3}^{c}}{u^{{\beta}+a+c}}

\displaystyle\,\left.\ \qquad\ \qquad+\sum_{b=0}^{{\beta}-1}\sum_{c=0}^{{\gamma}-1}\binom{b+c+{\alpha}-1}{b,c,{\alpha}-1}\frac{k_{1}^{\alpha}k_{2}^{b}k_{3}^{c}}{u^{{\alpha}+b+c}}\right)\frac{1}{k_{1}^{\alpha}k_{2}^{\beta}k_{3}^{\gamma}u^{\lambda}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\sum_{a=0}^{{\alpha}-1}\sum_{b=0}^{{\beta}-1}\binom{a+b+{\gamma}-1}{a,b,{\gamma}-1}T_{p^{r}}({\alpha}-a,{\beta}-b,0;{\lambda}+{\gamma}+a+b)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\,\sum_{a=0}^{{\alpha}-1}\sum_{c=0}^{{\gamma}-1}\binom{a+c+{\beta}-1}{a,c,{\beta}-1}T_{p^{r}}({\alpha}-a,{\gamma}-c,0;{\lambda}+{\beta}+a+c)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\,\sum_{b=0}^{{\beta}-1}\sum_{c=0}^{{\gamma}-1}\binom{b+c+{\alpha}-1}{b,c,{\alpha}-1}T_{p^{r}}({\beta}-b,{\gamma}-c,0;{\lambda}+{\alpha}+b+c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\sum_{a=1}^{{\alpha}}\sum_{b=1}^{{\beta}}\binom{n-a-b-1}{{\alpha}-a,{\beta}-b,{\gamma}-1}T_{p^{r}}(a,b,0;w-a-b)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\,\sum_{a=1}^{{\alpha}}\sum_{c=1}^{{\gamma}}\binom{n-a-c-1}{{\alpha}-a,{\gamma}-c,{\beta}-1}T_{p^{r}}(a,c,0;w-a-c)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\,\sum_{b=1}^{{\beta}}\sum_{c=1}^{{\gamma}}\binom{n-b-c-1}{{\beta}-b,{\gamma}-c,{\alpha}-1}T_{p^{r}}(b,c,0;w-b-c)

where
n={\alpha}+{\beta}+{\gamma}
and
w={\alpha}+{\beta}+{\gamma}+{\lambda}
.
Applying
[
4
, Lemma 3.1]
again (or
[
18
, Lemma 2.8]
) we see
that

\displaystyle T_{p^{r}}({\alpha},{\beta},0;{\lambda})=

\displaystyle\,\sum_{s=0}^{{\alpha}-1}\binom{s+{\beta}-1}{s}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}<u_{2}<u_{3}<p^{r}\\
k_{1},u_{2}-k_{1},u_{3}-u_{2},u_{3}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{p}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{k_{1}^{{\alpha}-s}u_{2}^{{\beta}+s}u_{3}^{\lambda}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\,\sum_{t=0}^{{\beta}-1}\binom{t+{\alpha}-1}{t}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{2}<u_{2}<u_{3}<p^{r}\\
k_{2},u_{2}-k_{2},u_{3}-u_{2},u_{3}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{p}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{k_{2}^{{\beta}-t}u_{2}^{{\alpha}+t}u_{3}^{\lambda}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\sum_{s=0}^{{\alpha}-1}\binom{s+{\beta}-1}{s}{\mathcal{H}}_{p^{r}}({\alpha}-s,{\beta}+s,{\lambda})+\sum_{t=0}^{{\beta}-1}\binom{t+{\alpha}-1}{t}{\mathcal{H}}_{p^{r}}({\beta}-t,{\alpha}+t,{\lambda}).

Then we get

\displaystyle\,(-1)^{\lambda}Z_{p^{r}}({\alpha},{\beta},{\gamma},{\lambda})

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\,\sum_{a=1}^{{\alpha}}\sum_{b=1}^{{\beta}}\binom{n-a-b-1}{{\alpha}-a,{\beta}-b,{\gamma}-1}\sum_{s=0}^{a-1}\binom{s+b-1}{s}

\displaystyle\ \quad\Big({\mathcal{H}}_{p^{r}}(a-s,b+s,w-a-b)+{\lambda}p^{r}{\mathcal{H}}_{p^{r}}(a-s,b+s,w-a-b+1)\Big)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\,\sum_{a=1}^{{\alpha}}\sum_{b=1}^{{\beta}}\binom{n-a-b-1}{{\alpha}-a,{\beta}-b,{\gamma}-1}\sum_{t=0}^{b-1}\binom{t+a-1}{t}

\displaystyle\ \quad\Big({\mathcal{H}}_{p^{r}}(b-t,a+t,w-a-b)+{\lambda}p^{r}{\mathcal{H}}_{p^{r}}(b-t,a+t,w-a-b+1)\Big)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\,\sum_{a=1}^{{\alpha}}\sum_{c=1}^{{\gamma}}\binom{n-a-c-1}{{\alpha}-a,{\gamma}-c,{\beta}-1}\sum_{s=0}^{a-1}\binom{s+c-1}{s}

\displaystyle\ \quad\Big({\mathcal{H}}_{p^{r}}(a-s,c+s,w-a-c)+{\lambda}p^{r}{\mathcal{H}}_{p^{r}}(a-s,c+s,w-a-c+1)\Big)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\,\sum_{a=1}^{{\alpha}}\sum_{c=1}^{{\gamma}}\binom{n-a-c-1}{{\alpha}-a,{\beta}-b,{\beta}-1}\sum_{t=0}^{c-1}\binom{t+a-1}{t}

\displaystyle\ \quad\Big({\mathcal{H}}_{p^{r}}(c-t,a+t,w-a-c)+{\lambda}p^{r}{\mathcal{H}}_{p^{r}}(c-t,a+t,w-a-c+1)\Big)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\,\sum_{b=1}^{{\beta}}\sum_{c=1}^{{\gamma}}\binom{n-b-c-1}{{\beta}-b,{\gamma}-c,{\alpha}-1}\sum_{s=0}^{b-1}\binom{s+c-1}{s}

\displaystyle\ \quad\Big({\mathcal{H}}_{p^{r}}(b-s,c+s,w-b-c)+{\lambda}p^{r}{\mathcal{H}}_{p^{r}}(b-s,c+s,w-b-c+1)\Big)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\,\sum_{b=1}^{{\beta}}\sum_{c=1}^{{\gamma}}\binom{n-b-c-1}{{\beta}-b,{\gamma}-c,{\alpha}-1}\sum_{t=0}^{c-1}\binom{t+b-1}{t}

\displaystyle\ \quad\Big({\mathcal{H}}_{p^{r}}(c-t,b+t,w-b-c)+{\lambda}p^{r}{\mathcal{H}}_{p^{r}}(c-t,b+t,w-b-c+1)\Big)\pmod{p^{2r}}.

The above computation quickly yields the following result.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
p
be a prime and
{\alpha},{\beta},{\gamma},{\lambda}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
w={\alpha}+{\beta}+{\gamma}+{\lambda}
is odd.
If
p>w+2
then we have

\displaystyle Z_{p}({\alpha},{\beta},{\gamma},{\lambda})\equiv

\displaystyle\,(-1)^{\lambda}\left(\sum_{a=1}^{{\alpha}}\sum_{b=1}^{{\beta}}\binom{n-a-b-1}{{\alpha}-a,{\beta}-b,{\gamma}-1}t_{p}(a,b;w-a-b)\right.

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\,\sum_{a=1}^{{\alpha}}\sum_{c=1}^{{\gamma}}\binom{n-a-c-1}{{\alpha}-a,{\gamma}-c,{\beta}-1}t_{p}(a,c;w-a-c)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\,\left.\sum_{b=1}^{{\beta}}\sum_{c=1}^{{\gamma}}\binom{n-b-c-1}{{\beta}-b,{\gamma}-c,{\alpha}-1}t_{p}(b,c;w-b-c)\right)B_{p-w}\pmod{p}

where
n={\alpha}+{\beta}+{\gamma}
,

\displaystyle t_{p}({\alpha},{\beta};{\lambda})=\sum_{s=0}^{{\alpha}-1}\binom{s+{\beta}-1}{s}h_{p}({\alpha}-s,{\beta}+s,{\lambda})+\sum_{t=0}^{{\beta}-1}\binom{t+{\alpha}-1}{t}h_{p}({\beta}-t,{\alpha}+t,{\lambda})

and

h_{p}({\alpha},{\beta},{\gamma})=\frac{1}{2n}\left((-1)^{\alpha}\binom{n}{{\alpha}}-(-1)^{\gamma}\binom{n}{{\gamma}}\right).

Proof.

Observe that

{\mathcal{H}}_{p}({\alpha},{\beta},{\gamma})=H^{(p)}_{p}({\alpha},{\beta},{\gamma})=H_{p}({\alpha},{\beta},{\gamma}).

Taking
r=1
in the above computation, we see that the theorem follows from

[
21
, Thm. 1.8]
or
[
23
, Thm. 8.5.13]
quickly.
∎

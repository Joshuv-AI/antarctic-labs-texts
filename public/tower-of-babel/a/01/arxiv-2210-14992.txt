On the exactness of a stability test for Lur’e systems with slope-restricted nonlinearities
Andrey Kharitenko, Carsten W. Scherer

Abstract
In this note it is shown that the famous multiplier absolute stability test of R. O’Shea, G. Zames and P. Falb is necessary and sufficient if the set of Lur’e interconnections is lifted to a Kronecker structure and an explicit method to construct the destabilizing static nonlinearity is presented.



1
Introduction

A classical problem in control theory is the stability analysis of the so-called Lur’e systems.
Studied by A. Lur’e and V. Postnikov in 1944
[
1
]
explicitly for the first time, they consist of a feedback interconnection between a linear time-invariant system
G
and a nonlinear static operator
\Delta
.

The method of stability multipliers
[
2
,
3
]
is used to establish stability of such systems by searching for an artificial system
M\in\mathcal{M}_{\Delta}
(called multiplier) such that
-MG
is strictly passive. Here

\mathcal{M}_{\Delta}
is a suitable set of multipliers such that
\Delta M^{-1}
is a positive operator for any
M\in\mathcal{M}_{\Delta}
.
Under suitable assumptions, if such a multiplier is found, stability of the interconnection can be deduced from the passivity- or IQC-theorem

[
2
]
,
[
4
]
.
Different classes of nonlinearities
\boldsymbol{\Delta}
allow for different multiplier classes
\mathcal{M}_{\boldsymbol{\Delta}}
, and a larger multiplier class implies a less conservative stability test.

Yet the question of whether a stability criterion created in this way is necessary is dependent on
\mathcal{M}_{\boldsymbol{\Delta}}
and remains open in general.
Many well-known multiplier stability criteria, such as the Popov or circle criterion, were shown already early on to be only sufficient
[
5
,
6
,
7
]
.
For the class of monotone or slope-restricted nonlinearities, a particularly rich class of multipliers is given by the so-called O’Shea-Zames-Falb (OZF) multipliers
[
8
,
9
,
10
]
, which were introduced in
[
8
]
and
[
9
]
for continuous- and discrete-time SISO interconnections and extended in
[
11
]
to the MIMO case for monotone nonlinearities.
In fact, for this class of nonlinearities in discrete-time, OZF-multipliers form the largest possible passivity multiplier set
[
12
]
and give the least conservative results.
Along with the discovery of suitable search methods
[
13
,
14
,
10
,
15
,
16
]
, this has motivated its extensive use in recent times, e.g.
[
17
,
18
,
19
]
.

The question about the conservatism of this multiplier set is, therefore, of great interest
[
20
,
21
,
22
]
and its exactness was conjectured in
[
10
]
.
Moreover, this question is closely related to the Kalman problem from the 1950s, which asks for necessary and sufficient conditions for the stability of Lur’e interconnections with slope-restricted nonlinearities.

In this note we show that the stability test generated by OZF-multipliers cannot differentiate between Lur’e interconnections in different dimensions, if the linear part is lifted from
G
to the Kronecker form
G\otimes I_{d}
, and could, hence, be potentially conservative.
As our main result, we prove that, up to this lifting, the test is indeed necessary and sufficient and provide an explicit construction of the destabilizing nonlinearity.
We also point out connections to the duality bounds obtained in
[
20
]
and to criteria for the absence of periodic oscillations in nonlinear filters
[
23
]
.

The paper is structured as follows.
After introducing the necessary concepts of absolute stability and integral quadratic constraints as well as stating the main stability test in Section
2
, we proceed in Section
3.1
to show that the test cannot differentiate between interconnections up to a lifting.
In Section
3.2
we then state our main exactness result.
To prove it, we reformulate the infeasibility of the main stability test in Section
3.3
as a condition on a linear program and explicitly construct a nonlinearity using duality in Section
3.4
.
Finally, in Section
3.5
we prove our main result and discuss its connections to other results in the literature in Section
5
.
All technical proofs, except the one of the main exactness result, can be found in Appendix
A.1
, while Appendix
A.2
contains two auxiliary facts.

2.2
Well-posedness and stability

Let
G:\ell_{m}^{2e}\to\ell_{l}^{2e}
be linear and bounded and
\Delta\subseteq\ell_{l}^{2e}\times\ell_{m}^{2e}

Consider the standard feedback interconnection (Fig.
1
) between
G
and
\Delta

\displaystyle e_{2}=Ge_{1}+u_{2}\text{\ and\ }(e_{2},e_{1}-u_{1})\in\Delta,

(1)

\ell_{m}^{2e}\times\ell_{l}^{2e}=\ell_{m+l}^{2e}

The interconnection (
1

\displaystyle R(\Delta)=\{(u,e)\in\ell_{l+m}^{2e}\times\ell_{l+m}^{2e}\mid\eqref{eq:iqcRelationInterconnection}\text{\ is satisfied}\}

(2)

2.3
Slope-restricted relations

In the following we consider any relation
f\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
as a multifunction with domain
\operatorname{dom}(f)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid\exists y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:(x,y)\in f\}
and the value
f(x)=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid(x,y)\in f\}
at
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
; we write
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

A multifunction
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{d}
is said to be cyclically monotone if for any cyclic sequence
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in\operatorname{dom}(f)
,
x_{n+1}=x_{1}
, and
y_{j}\in f(x_{j})
,
j=1,\ldots,n
it holds that
\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\langle y_{j},x_{j+1}-x_{j}\right\rangle\leqslant 0
.

Following
[
17
]
and with
\lambda\in(-\infty,0]
,
\kappa\in(0,\infty]
, we define for every
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{d}
the multifunction
T_{\lambda,\kappa,f}=(f-\lambda I)\circ(I-\frac{1}{\kappa}f)^{-1}
so that
y\in T_{\lambda,\kappa,f}(x)
iff there exists some
z\in\operatorname{dom}(f)
and
u\in f(z)
with
x=z-\frac{1}{\kappa}u
and
y=u-\lambda z
.

Here, we use the convention
1/\infty=0
and note
that
T_{0,\infty,f}=f
by definition.
The multifunction
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{d}
is said to be
[\lambda,\kappa]
-slope-restricted (written
f\in\operatorname{slope}_{d}[\lambda,\kappa]
) if
T_{\lambda,\kappa,f}
is cyclically monotone.

Every relation
f\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
defines a static relation
\Delta=\Delta_{f}\subseteq\ell_{d}^{2e}\times\ell_{d}^{2e}
on signals by
(w,z)\in\Delta
iff
z_{k}\in f(w_{k})
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
In the sequel we work with the class

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{d}(\lambda,\kappa):=\{\Delta_{f}\mid f\in\operatorname{slope}_{d}[\lambda,\kappa]\text{\ total and\ }0\in f(0)\}\,.

2.4
Stability multipliers

Any shift-invariant operator
M\in\mathcal{L}(\ell_{d}^{2})
has an infinite block-Toeplitz representation
M=(M_{i-j})_{i,j=0}^{\infty}
with
M_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
.
Conversely, any block-Toeplitz matrix
(M_{i-j})_{i,j=0}^{\infty}
that satisfies
\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\lVert M_{j}\rVert<\infty
defines an operator
M\in\mathcal{L}(\ell_{d}^{2})
by
Mx=(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}M_{ij}x_{j})_{i=0}^{\infty}
. A scalar matrix
M=(m_{i-j})_{i,j=0}^{\infty}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}_{0}\times\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is said to be doubly hyperdominant, if
m_{j}\leqslant 0
for
j\neq 0
and
\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}m_{j}\geqslant 0

where the convergence of the series is assumed by definition.
We denote the set of all doubly hyperdominant Toeplitz matrices by
\mathcal{H}
.
The above terminology is also used for finite matrices
M\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
whenever
N
is clear from the context.
A block-Toeplitz matrix
M=(M_{i-j})_{i,j=0}^{\infty}
is said to be doubly hyperdominant, if it is doubly-hyperdominant as a scalar matrix.

The IQC theorem
[
24
,
25
,
26
,
27
]
permits to verify the stability of the interconnection of
G
and
\Delta_{f}
as follows.

Theorem 1
.

Let
G
be linear, bounded and causal on
\ell_{d}^{2}
. Assume that the feedback interconnection of
G
and
\Delta
is well-posed for every
\Delta\in\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{d}(\lambda,\kappa)
.
Then the interconnection is robustly stable against
\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{d}(\lambda,\kappa)
if there exists some
\epsilon>0
and
M\in\mathcal{H}\otimes I_{d}
such that

\displaystyle\left\langle\left(\begin{matrix}Gw\\
w\end{matrix}\right),\Pi_{M}(\lambda,\kappa)\left(\begin{matrix}Gw\\
w\end{matrix}\right)\right\rangle\leqslant-\epsilon\lVert w\rVert_{2}^{2}\text{\ for all\ }w\in\ell_{d}^{2}\,,

(3)

where

\displaystyle\Pi_{M}(\lambda,\kappa):=\left(\begin{matrix}-\lambda(M+M^{*})&M^{*}+\frac{\lambda}{\kappa}M\\
M+\frac{\lambda}{\kappa}M^{*}&-\frac{1}{\kappa}(M+M^{*})\end{matrix}\right).

This is the Zames-Falb stability test that is the subject of study in this note.

2.5
Positive definite functions and sequences

A function
f:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{C}
is said to be positive definite (p.d.) if
f(z^{-1})=f(z)^{*}
for all
z\in\mathbb{T}
and for any finite subset
\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}\}\subseteq\mathbb{T}
the matrix
(f(z_{k}z_{j}^{-1}))_{k,j=1}^{n}
is hermitian and positive semi-definite.
Any p.d. function
f
satisfies
f(1)\geqslant 0
as well as
f(z)^{*}=f(z^{*})
and
\lvert f(z)\rvert\leqslant f(1)
for any
z\in\mathbb{T}
.
A well-known theorem from harmonic analysis reads as follows.

Theorem 2

(Bochner)
.

For a function
f:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{C}
the following statements are equivalent:

1.

f
is continuous and positive definite

2.

there exists some finite, nonnegative measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{Z}
such that
f(z)=\int_{\mathbb{Z}}z^{t}\,\text{d}\mu(t)
for all
z\in\mathbb{T}
.

A finite set
\{c_{j}\}_{j=0}^{N-1}
of complex numbers is said to be positive definite if the circulant matrix
C=(c_{j-k\operatorname{mod}N})_{j,k=0}^{N-1}
is hermitian and positive semi-definite.
If
f:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{C}
is p.d. and
\{z_{0},\ldots,z_{N-1}\}\subseteq\mathbb{T}
is the set of the
N
-th roots of unity, then the sequence defined by
c_{j}=f(z_{j})
for
j=0,\ldots,N-1
is p.d..
Conversely, if we are given a p.d. sequence
\{c_{j}\}_{j=0}^{N-1}
, one possible p.d. interpolant is given by the next theorem, which is a discrete analogue of the one proven in
[
28
]
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
and let
\{z_{0},\ldots,z_{N-1}\}
be the collection of
N
-th roots of unity.
Then the piecewise-linear function
f
with interpolation nodes
\{z_{0},\ldots,z_{N-1}\}
satisfying
f(z_{j})=c_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
for
j\in\{0,\ldots,N-1\}
is positive definite iff
\{c_{j}\}_{j=0}^{N-1}
is positive definite.

Proof.

The proof is similar to the one of
[
28
, Theorem 2]
and can be found in
[
27
]
.
∎

Here we call a function
f:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{C}
piecewise-linear with interpolation nodes
\{e^{i\omega_{0}},\ldots,e^{i\omega_{N-1}}\}\subseteq\mathbb{T}
for

0\leqslant\omega_{0}<\ldots<\omega_{N-1}<2\pi
and
\omega_{N}=\omega_{0}
, if

\displaystyle f(e^{i((1-t)\omega_{j}+t\omega_{j+1})})=(1-t)f(e^{i\omega_{j}})+tf(e^{i\omega_{j+1}})

holds for all
t\in[0,1]
and
j=0,\ldots,N-1
.

3
Exactness results

Now we come to the analysis of the criterion given by Theorem
1
.

3.1
An observation about Theorem
1

Assume that
G:\ell^{2e}\to\ell^{2e}
and that (
3
) is satisfied for some
\epsilon>0
and
M\in\mathcal{H}
, where
d=1
.
If we denote

\displaystyle T=\left(\begin{matrix}G\\
I\end{matrix}\right)^{*}\Pi_{M}(\lambda,\kappa)\left(\begin{matrix}G\\
I\end{matrix}\right)\in\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2})

and stack
d
copies of (
3
), this condition is equivalent to

\displaystyle\left\langle(I_{d}\otimes T)w,w\right\rangle\leqslant-\epsilon\lVert w\rVert^{2}\text{\ for all\ }w=\left(\begin{matrix}w^{(1)}\\
\vdots\\
w^{(d)}\end{matrix}\right)\in\bigoplus_{k=1}^{d}\ell^{2}\,,

(4)

where
I_{d}\otimes T=\textstyle\bigoplus_{k=1}^{d}T\in\mathcal{L}(\bigoplus_{k=1}^{d}\ell^{2})
.
By identifying
\bigoplus_{k=1}^{d}\ell^{2}
with
\ell_{d}^{2}
it is easy to see that (
4
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\left\langle(T\otimes I_{d})w,w\right\rangle\leqslant-\epsilon\lVert w\rVert^{2}\text{\ for all\ }w\in\ell_{d}^{2}\,.

(5)

Then, again by a simple computation, we note that

\displaystyle T\otimes I_{d}=\left(\begin{matrix}G\otimes I_{d}\\
I\end{matrix}\right)^{*}\Pi_{M\otimes I_{d}}(\lambda,\kappa)\left(\begin{matrix}G\otimes I_{d}\\
I\end{matrix}\right)\,.

Thus (
3
) holds for the system
G\otimes I_{d}\in\mathcal{L}(\ell_{d}^{2})
, the multiplier
\bar{M}=M\otimes I_{d}\in\mathcal{H}\otimes I_{d}
and the same
\epsilon>0
.
Conversely, if (
3
) holds for
G\otimes I_{d}\in\mathcal{L}(\ell_{d}^{2})
and with the multiplier
M\otimes I_{d}
, then (
3
) is also satisfied for
G
with the multiplier
M
.

This permits us to draw the following conclusion:

The stability test of Theorem
1
cannot differentiate between the robust stability of the interconnection between
G
and
\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{1}(\lambda,\kappa)
and robust stability of the interconnection between
G\otimes I_{d}
and
\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{d}(\lambda,\kappa)
for any
d\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

3.2
Main theorem

In view of the latter observation and the fact that (
3
) is homogeneous in
\epsilon
and
M
, we focus on multipliers that are normalized as
M\in\mathcal{H}_{1}:=\{M=(m_{i-j})_{i,j=0}^{\infty}\in\mathcal{H}\mid m_{0}\leqslant 1\}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
.
For the ease of exposition, we also set
\lambda=0
.
The following theorem is main result of this note.

Theorem 4
.

Let
G:\ell^{2}\to\ell^{2}
be LTI, bounded and causal with a finite-dimensional state-space representation. If for
d=1
and some
\epsilon>0
there exists no
M\in\mathcal{H}_{1}
such that (
3
) is satisfied, then there exists some
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
\Delta\in\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{d}(0,\kappa)
such that the interconnection (
2
) between
G\otimes I_{d}
and
\Delta
has a gain of at least
1/\sqrt{8\epsilon}
.

In short, the test provided by OZF-multipliers is exact up to a lifting of the original system
G
to
G\otimes I_{d}
. Precisely,
let
\epsilon_{\max}
be the supremum of all
\epsilon\geqslant 0
such that (
3
) is satisfied for some
M\in\mathcal{H}_{1}
.
Then the Zames-Falb stability test is successful iff
\epsilon_{\max}>0
, and
our main result implies

\sup_{d\in\mathbb{N},\,\Delta\in\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{d}(0,\kappa)}\lVert R(\Delta)\rVert\geqslant\frac{1}{\sqrt{8\epsilon_{\max}}}.

This not only shows robust instability of the loop in case of
\epsilon_{\max}=0
, but also
reveals that
\epsilon_{\max}
can be viewed as a stability margin in case it is positive.

3.3
Linear program formulation

In order to understand what the infeasibility of (
3
) entails, we restate (
3
) as a frequency domain inequality (FDI).
We first set
\kappa=\infty
.
It is easy to see that a generic
M\in\mathcal{H}_{1}
can be written as
M=I-H
with
H
being Toeplitz and doubly substochastic, i.e.
H=(h_{j-i})_{i,j=0}^{\infty}
for some nonnegative sequence
h_{k}
such that
\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}h_{k}\leqslant 1
.
Taking the z-transform of (
3
) yields the FDI

\displaystyle\operatorname{Re}(G(z)(1-H(z))\leqslant-\frac{\epsilon}{2}\text{\ for all\ }z\in\mathbb{T}\,.

(6)

Here
H(z)=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}h_{k}z^{-k}
is continuous and p.d. by Theorem
2
and satisfies
H(1)\leqslant 1
.
Hence the test in Theorem
1
is equivalent to finding some continuous, p.d.
H:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{C}
with (
6
).

As a next step we relate the FDI (
6
) to a family of finite-dimensional convex programs.
For this purpose, we evaluate the FDI on the
N
-th roots of unity

z_{j}=\exp(2\pi ij/N)\in\mathbb{T}
,
j=0,\ldots,N-1
.

On the one hand, if there exists a function
H:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{C}
with
H(1)\leqslant 1
which satisfies (
6
), we infer from Section
2.5
that,
for every
N\in\mathbb{N}
, the convex program

\displaystyle\operatorname{Re}(G(z_{j})(1-c_{j}))\leqslant-\delta\text{\ for all\ }j=0,\ldots,N-1

(7)

with
\delta=\epsilon/2
is feasible in the convex set of p.d. sequences
(c_{j})_{j=0}^{N-1}
with
c_{0}\leqslant 1
.
Note that
this observation is closely related to the duality results obtained in
[
20
]
, which will be discussed further below.

It has the following converse.

Lemma 5
.

Let
\epsilon>0
and assume that there is no
M\in\mathcal{H}_{1}
such that (
3
) is satisfied.
Then for each
\delta>\epsilon/2
there is some
N=N_{\delta}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that (
7
) is infeasible.

The proof relies on multipliers with transfer functions
M(z)=H(1)-H(z)
for some piecewise-linear positive definite
H:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{C}
.
These are called piecewise-linear OZF-multipliers.
Since they have non-rational transfer functions, they do not have a finite-dimensional state-space representation and are, hence, different from the popular so-called finite impulse response (FIR) multipliers
[
15
,
16
]
.

Fig.
2
shows the Nyquist plot of a piecewise-linear Zames-Falb multiplier.

Nyquist plot of the piecewise-linear ZF-multiplier
H(z)=1-\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}h_{k}z^{k}
given by the p.d. sequence
c=\sqrt{N}V\alpha_{*}
with optimal values
\alpha_{*}
and
t_{*}
in (

) for the plant
G_{1}(z)=\frac{-z}{z^{2}-1.8z+0.81}
,

and

1-\sum_{k=-N_{-}}^{N_{+}}h_{k}z^{k}
.

In order to further analyze the convex program (
7
) for a fixed
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
we parametrize the circulant matrix
(c_{j-k})_{j,k=0}^{N-1}
by its eigenvalues
[
29
]
and stack the coefficients
(c_{j})_{j=0}^{N-1}
as well as the eigenvalues
(\lambda_{j})_{j=0}^{N-1}
into column vectors
c
and
\lambda
, respectively.
This gives the relationship
c=V\lambda/\sqrt{N}
, where
V=(z_{j}^{l})_{l,j=0}^{N-1}/\sqrt{N}
is the DFT-matrix.
Moreover, we define
Q=\operatorname{diag}(G(z_{0}),\ldots,G(z_{N-1}))
and
e=(1,\ldots,1)^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

With these ingredients, we consider the linear program

\displaystyle\inf-t\text{\ \ s.t.\ }\Bigg\{\begin{split}t\in\mathbb{R}\,,\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\,,e^{\top}\alpha\leqslant 1\,,\alpha\geqslant 0\,,\\
\operatorname{Re}(Q(e-\sqrt{N}V\alpha))\leqslant-te\end{split}

(8)

where we maximize the margin
t=\epsilon/2
for the FDI (
6
) evaluated at the
N
-th roots of unity.
Note that its optimal value
-t_{*}
is nonpositive since
\alpha=(1,0,\ldots,0)^{\top}
and
t=0
are always feasible for (
8
).

Lemma 6
.

5

5.1
SISO exactness and results for SSV

The question about whether the multipliers are exact for the original SISO interconnection remains unanswered by Theorem
4

will be larger than
1

It is easy to see that if the interconnection between

and

is robustly stable for some

and
\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{1}(\lambda,\kappa)

The converse may or may not be true.

In
[

it is shown that if for some coprime

such that

the main plant

satisfies the phase constraint

\displaystyle-\frac{\pi}{N}\leqslant\arg(G(\exp(i\pi\tfrac{\alpha}{\beta}))\leqslant\frac{\pi}{N}\,,

One can show that (

) implies

in (

).
This reveals that our “instability criterion” in Remark

is not less conservative than the one given in
[
31
]
.
But it is the construction of a SISO nonlinearity (
d=1
)
in
[
31
]
that makes this approach different from ours.

The procedure to lift the interconnection from

to

is reminiscent of a result shown in
[

33
]
for the structured singular value (SSV).
To state it, we define for

and the complex block structure
\boldsymbol{\Delta}=\{\operatorname{diag}(\delta_{1}I_{n_{1}},\ldots,\delta_{r}I_{n_{r}},\Delta_{r+1},\ldots,\Delta_{r+c})\mid\delta_{j}\in\mathbb{C}\,,\Delta_{j}\in\mathbb{C}^{n_{j}\times n_{j}}\}\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
the SSV and its convex upper bound:

\displaystyle\mu_{\boldsymbol{\Delta}}(M)

\displaystyle=\inf\{\lVert\Delta\rVert\mid\Delta\in\boldsymbol{\Delta}\,,-1\in\sigma(M\Delta)\}\,,

\displaystyle\hat{\mu}_{\boldsymbol{\Delta}}(M)

\displaystyle=\inf\{\lVert XMX^{-1}\rVert\mid X\in\boldsymbol{\Delta}^{\prime}\,,X\text{\ invertible}\}\,.

Here,

.

Theorem 10

(Theorem 4.1,
[
32
]
)
.

For

and

\displaystyle\hat{\mu}_{\boldsymbol{\Delta}}(M)=\mu_{\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{d}}(I_{d}\otimes M)\,.

6
Conclusion

In this note it was shown that the robust stability test induced by O’Shea-Zames-Falb multipliers is exact if the interconnection structure is extended by lifting.
An explicit method for the construction of the destabilizing nonlinearities as well as connections to duality bounds and criteria for absence of internal periodic oscillations were presented.
Exactness of the test for the original interconnection remains open and we conjecture it to be false, based on analogous results for the structured singular value.

Part 2.
The matrix

\displaystyle\tr\left(MTyy^{\top}\right)\geqslant-\tr(M)t_{*}/N

A.2
Auxiliary results

In this section we collect two well-known auxiliary results that we need in this note.

Lemma 16
.

If
R\subseteq\ell_{m}^{2e}\times\ell_{l}^{2e}
and
(x,y)\in R
is such that
\operatorname{pow}(y)>0
, then
\lVert R\rVert\geqslant\frac{\operatorname{pow}(y)}{\operatorname{pow}(x)}
, where
1/0:=\infty
.

Proof.

If
\gamma>\lVert R\rVert
, then there is some
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
with
\lVert P_{N}y\rVert\leqslant\gamma\lVert P_{N}x\rVert+\beta
for all
T\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
Dividing by
\sqrt{N}
and taking
\limsup_{N\to\infty}
on both sides yields
\operatorname{pow}(y)\leqslant\gamma\operatorname{pow}(x)
.
For
\operatorname{pow}(x)=0
, this is a contradiction, which shows
\lVert R\rVert=\infty
.
For
\operatorname{pow}(x)>0
this implies
\gamma\geqslant\frac{\operatorname{pow}(y)}{\operatorname{pow}(x)}
.
∎

Lemma 17

Let

Set

\displaystyle\xi_{0}=(I-A^{N})^{-1}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}A^{N-1-j}By_{j}\,.

A semigroup approach to the reconstruction theorem and the multilevel Schauder estimate for singular modelled distributions
Masato Hoshino, Ryoji Takano

Abstract
We extend the semigroup approach used in [ 23 , 21 ] to provide alternative proofs of the reconstruction theorem and the multilevel Schauder estimate for singular modelled distributions. As an application of them, we construct the local-in-time solution of the two dimensional parabolic Anderson model with a non-translation invariant differential operator.



1
Introduction

The theory of regularity structures established by Hairer
[
16
]
provides a robust framework adapted to a wide class of (subcritical) singular stochastic PDEs.
One of the most important concepts in this theory is the notion of
modelled distributions
, which are considered as “generalized Taylor expansions” of the solutions to the underlying equations.
The analytic core of the theory is to prove two key theorems for modelled distributions: the
reconstruction theorem

[
16
, Theorem 3.10]
and the
multilevel Schauder estimate

[
16
, Theorem 5.12]
.
The former theorem constructs a global distribution by gluing local distributions derived from a given modelled distribution together.
The latter translates an integral operator such as the convolution operator with Green function into the operator on the space of modelled distributions.
Since Hairer first proved the reconstruction theorem, some alternative proofs have been proposed using various approaches, such as Littlewood–Paley theory
[
15
]
, the heat semigroup approach
[
23
,
3
]
, the mollification approach
[
26
]
, and the convolution approach
[
12
]
.
Inspired by
[
23
]
, the first author of this paper proved both theorems by using the operator semigroup in
[
21
]
.
On the other hand, Caravenna and Zambotti
[
11
]
introduced the notion of
germs
to describe the analytic core of the proof of the reconstruction theorem, and later, they and Broux
[
7
]
proved the multilevel Schauder estimate at the level of germs.
See also
[
17
,
10
,
20
,
22
,
24
,
8
,
27
,
19
]
for extensions of the theorems into different settings, such as Besov or Triebel–Lizorkin norms, or Riemannian manifolds.
See also
[
13
]
for a Besov extension of the sewing lemma, which plays a role similar to the reconstruction theorem in rough path theory.

In the aforementioned literatures, modelled distributions are often defined on the entire space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
to avoid technical difficulties related to boundary conditions.
However, it is not sufficient for applications.
To apply the theory of regularity structures to parabolic equations, it is necessary to define modelled distributions on the time-space region
(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
allowing a singularity at the hyperplane
\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
This modified version of modelled distributions is called
singular modelled distributions
.
In
[
16
, Section 6]
, the reconstruction theorem and the multilevel Schauder estimate were extended to the class of singular modelled distributions.
An extension to Besov norms is demonstrated in
[
18
]
, and boundary conditions on both time and space variables are considered in
[
14
]
.
However, compared to the case of modelled distributions without boundary conditions, there seems to be a less number of studies on alternative proofs and extensions.
It should be mentioned that, in the context of rough path theory, the sewing lemma is extended into the singular path spaces allowing a singularity at time
t=0
by
[
6
]
.

The aim of this paper is to extend the semigroup approach used in
[
21
]
and provide alternative proofs of the reconstruction theorem (see Corollary
3.9
) and the multilevel Schauder estimate (see Corollary
4.6
) for singular modelled distributions.
The proofs use arguments similar to
[
21
]
, but require the following technical modifications.

(i)

Following
[
21
]
, we define Besov norms using the operator semigroup
\{Q_{t}\}_{t>0}
.
The associated integral kernel
Q_{t}(x,y)
is inhomogeneous and has restricted regularities with respect to
x
and
y
in general.
Hence the equivalence between the norm associated with
\{Q_{t}\}_{t>0}
and the standard norm defined from Littlewood–Paley theory is uncertain.
For this reason, we need some nontrivial arguments to prove the uniqueness of the reconstruction.

(ii)

Since
Q_{t}
is an integral operator defined over the entire spacetime, we always require global bounds on models and modelled distributions, unlike the original definitions in
[
16
]
that assume only local bounds.
Consequently, in addition to the definition of singular modelled distributions (see Definition
3.4
) which is closer to the original one, we use a different definition that assumes global bounds (see Proposition
3.5
-
(iii)
).
For this reason, as for the existence of the reconstruction, we assume a stronger condition “
\eta-\gamma>-\mathfrak{s}_{1}
” for the parameters appearing in the definition of singular modelled distributions than the condition “
\eta>-\mathfrak{s}_{1}
” as in
[
16
]
.
It is not actually a serious problem in applications because we can switch to a small
\gamma
to apply the reconstruction theorem.

Moreover, as an application, we discuss the parabolic Anderson model (PAM)

\big(\partial_{1}-a(x)\Delta\big)u(t,x)=b\big(u(t,x)\big)\xi(x)\qquad((t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{T}^{2})

with a spatial white noise
\xi
.
Here
b:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is in the class
C_{b}^{3}
and
a:\mathbb{T}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
is an
\alpha
-Hölder continuous function for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
and satisfies

C_{1}\leq a(x)\leq C_{2}\qquad(x\in\mathbb{T}^{2})

for some constants
0<C_{1}<C_{2}
.
When
a
is a constant, the above equation is one of the simplest examples of subcritical singular stochastic PDEs, as studied in
[
16
,
10
]
.
We show that the equation with general coefficients as above can be renormalized, with the spacetime dependent renormalization function (see Theorem
5.14
).
Such “non-translation invariant” equations are more generally studied by
[
2
,
25
]
.
The aim of this paper is to deepen the analytic core of
[
2
]
, which uses the semigroup approach.
On the other hand,
[
25
]
is a direct extension of
[
16
]
.
One of the differences between this paper and
[
25
]
is in the requirements of the smoothness of coefficients.
In
[
25
]
, a bit of smoothness is required, but in this paper the coefficients only need to have positive Hölder continuities.

This paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we recall from
[
21
]
Besov norms associated with the operator semigroup, and prove important inequalities used throughout this paper.
In Section
3
, we recall the basics of regularity structures and prove the reconstruction theorem for singular modelled distributions.
Section
4
is devoted to the proof of the multilevel Schauder estimate for singular modelled distributions.
In Section
5
, we discuss an application to the two-dimensional PAM.

Notations

The symbol
\mathbb{N}
denotes the set of all nonnegative integers.
Until Section
4
, we fix an integer
d\geq 1
, the
scaling

\mathfrak{s}=(\mathfrak{s}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{s}_{d})\in[1,\infty)^{d}
, and a number
\ell>0
. We define
|\mathfrak{s}|=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\mathfrak{s}_{i}
.
For any multiindex
{\bf k}=(k_{i})_{i=1}^{d}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
, any
x=(x_{i})_{i=1}^{d}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and any
t>0
, we use the following notations.

\displaystyle{\bf k}!:=\prod_{i=1}^{d}k_{i}!,\quad|{\bf k}|_{\mathfrak{s}}:=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\mathfrak{s}_{i}k_{i},\quad\|x\|_{\mathfrak{s}}:=\sum_{i=1}^{d}|x_{i}|^{1/\mathfrak{s}_{i}},

\displaystyle x^{\bf k}:=\prod_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}^{k_{i}},\quad t^{\mathfrak{s}/\ell}x:=(t^{\mathfrak{s}_{i}/\ell}x_{i})_{i=1}^{d},\quad t^{-\mathfrak{s}/\ell}x:=(t^{-\mathfrak{s}_{i}/\ell}x_{i})_{i=1}^{d}.

We define the set
\mathbb{N}[\mathfrak{s}]:=\{|{\bf k}|_{\mathfrak{s}}\,;\,{\bf k}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}\}
, which will be used in Section
4
. The parameter
t
is not a physical time variable, but an auxiliary variable used to define regularities of distributions.
For multiindices
{\bf k}=(k_{i})_{i=1}^{d}
and
{\bf l}=(l_{i})_{i=1}^{d}
, we write
{\bf l}\leq{\bf k}
if
l_{i}\leq k_{i}
for any
1\leq i\leq d
, and then define
\binom{{\bf k}}{{\bf l}}:=\prod_{i=1}^{d}\binom{k_{i}}{l_{i}}
.

We use the notation
A\lesssim B
for two functions
A(x)
and
B(x)
of a variable
x
, if there exists a constant
c>0
independent of
x
such that
A(x)\leq cB(x)
for any
x
.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we introduce some function spaces and prove important inequalities used throughout this paper.
Until Section
4
, we fix a nonnegative measurable function
G:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}

and define for any
t>0
,

G_{t}(x)=t^{-|\mathfrak{s}|/\ell}G\big(t^{-\mathfrak{s}/\ell}x\big).

2.1
Weighted Besov space

In this subsection, we recall from
[
21
]
some basics of Besov norms associated with the operator semigroup.
For simplicity, we consider only
L^{\infty}
type norms.

Definition 2.1
.

A continuous function
w:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,1]
which is strictly positive outside a set of Lebesgue measure
0
is called a
weight
.
For any weight
w
, we define the weighted
L^{\infty}
norm of a measurable function
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
by

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(w)}:=\|fw\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

We denote by
L^{\infty}(w)
the space of all measurable functions with finite
L^{\infty}(w)
norms, and define
C(w)=C(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{\infty}(w)
.

While we assumed that
w(x)>0
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
in
[
21
]
, we impose a weaker condition to consider a weight vanishing on the hyperplane
\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
in next subsection.
Note that
\|\cdot\|_{L^{\infty}(w)}
is nondegenerate because
w(x)>0
for almost every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
If
w(x)>0
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, then
C(w)
is a closed subspace of
L^{\infty}(w)
.

Definition 2.2
.

A weight
w
is said to be
G
-controlled
if
w(x)>0
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and there exists a continuous function
w^{*}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[1,\infty)
such that

\displaystyle w(x+y)\leq w^{*}(x)w(y)

(2.1)

for any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and

\displaystyle\sup_{0<t\leq T}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big\{\|x\|_{\mathfrak{s}}^{n}\,w^{*}\big(t^{\mathfrak{s}/\ell}x\big)G(x)\Big\}<\infty

(2.2)

for any
n\geq 0
and
T>0
.

From the properties (
2.1
) and (
2.2
), we have that

\displaystyle\|G_{t}*f\|_{L^{\infty}(w)}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(w)}

(2.3)

uniformly over
f\in L^{\infty}(w)
and
t\in(0,T]
for any
T>0
.
This is a particular case of
[
21
, Lemma 2.4]
.
Next we introduce a semigroup of integral operators.

Definition 2.3
.

We call a family of continuous functions
\{Q_{t}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}\}_{t>0}
a
G
-type semigroup
if it satisfies the following properties.

(i)

(Semigroup property)
For any
0<s<t
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}Q_{t-s}(x,z)Q_{s}(z,y)dz=Q_{t}(x,y).

(ii)

(Conservativity)
For any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}Q_{t}(x,y)dy=1.

(iii)

(Upper
G
-type estimate)
There exists a constant
C_{1}>0
such that, for any
t>0
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

|Q_{t}(x,y)|\leq C_{1}G_{t}(x-y).

(iv)

(Time derivative)
For any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
Q_{t}(x,y)
is differentiable with respect to
t
. Moreover, there exists a constant
C_{2}>0
such that, for any
t>0
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

|\partial_{t}Q_{t}(x,y)|\leq C_{2}\,t^{-1}G_{t}(x-y).

We fix a
G
-type semigroup
\{Q_{t}\}_{t>0}
until Section
4
.
If
w
is a
G
-controlled weight, the linear operator on
L^{\infty}(w)
defined by

\displaystyle(Q_{t}f)(x):=:Q_{t}(x,f):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}Q_{t}(x,y)f(y)dy\qquad(f\in L^{\infty}(w),\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d})

is bounded in
L^{\infty}(w)
uniformly over
t\in(0,1]
, by Definition
2.3
-
(iii)
and the inequality (
2.3
).
As an important fact,
Q_{t}f
is a continuous function for any
f\in L^{\infty}(w)
and
t>0
.
Moreover, if
f\in C(w)
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{t\downarrow 0}(Q_{t}f)(x)=f(x)

(2.4)

for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. See
[
21
, Proposition 2.8]
for the proofs.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
w
be a
G
-controlled weight and let
\{Q_{t}\}_{t>0}
be a
G
-type semigroup.
For every
\alpha\leq 0
, we define the Besov space
C^{\alpha,Q}(w)
as the completion of
C(w)
under the norm

\|f\|_{C^{\alpha,Q}(w)}:=\sup_{0<t\leq 1}t^{-\alpha/\ell}\|Q_{t}f\|_{L^{\infty}(w)}.

By the property (
2.4
), the norm
\|\cdot\|_{C^{\alpha,Q}(w)}
is nondegenerate on
C(w)
.
When
\mathfrak{s}=(1,1,\dots,1)
,
\ell=2
, and
Q_{t}
is the heat semigroup
e^{t\Delta}
, the above norm (with
\alpha<0
and
w=1
) is equivalent to the classical Besov norm in Euclidean setting, see e.g.,
[
1
, Theorem 2.34]
. For more general semigroups, a similar equivalence is obtained by
[
9
, Theorem 5.1]
when the adjoint operator of
Q_{t}
also satisfies some conditions similar to those in Definition
2.3
. As far as the authors know, without such an additional assumption for the semigroup, it is unclear whether the equivalence holds even for the case of isotropic scaling and no weight.

Remark 2.5
.

As stated in
[
21
, Proposition 2.14]
, for any
\alpha_{1}<\alpha_{2}\leq 0
, the identity
\iota_{\alpha_{1}}:C(w)\hookrightarrow C^{\alpha_{1},Q}(w)
is uniquely extended to the continuous injection

\iota_{\alpha_{1}}^{\alpha_{2}}:C^{\alpha_{2},Q}(w)\hookrightarrow C^{\alpha_{1},Q}(w).

Moreover, for any
\alpha\leq 0
, the operator
Q_{t}:C(w)\to C(w)
is continuously extended to the operator
Q_{t}^{\alpha}:C^{\alpha,Q}(w)\to C(w)
and they satisfy the relation

Q_{t}^{\alpha_{1}}\circ\iota_{\alpha_{1}}^{\alpha_{2}}=Q_{t}^{\alpha_{2}}

for any
\alpha_{1}<\alpha_{2}\leq 0
. For this compatibility, we can omit the letter
\alpha
and use the notation
Q_{t}
to mean its extension
Q_{t}^{\alpha}
regardless of its domain.

2.2
Temporal weights

In what follows, the first variable
x_{1}
in
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is regarded as the temporal variable, and the others
(x_{2},\dots,x_{d})
are spatial variables, denoted by
x^{\prime}=(x_{2},\dots,x_{d})
.
Accordingly, we denote
\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}=(\mathfrak{s}_{2},\dots,\mathfrak{s}_{d})
.
The aim of this paper is to extend the results in
[
21
]
to norms allowing a singularity at the hyperplane
\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
.
We define the weight
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,1]
by

\varphi(x):=|x_{1}|^{1/\mathfrak{s}_{1}}\wedge 1

and set
\varphi(x,y):=\varphi(x)\wedge\varphi(y)
.
The following inequalities are used frequently throughout this paper.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
w
be a
G
-controlled weight.
For any
\alpha\geq 0
and
\beta\in[0,\mathfrak{s}_{1})
, there exists a constant
C
such that, for any
t\in(0,1]
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(y)^{-\beta}\|x-y\|_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\alpha}\,w^{\ast}(x-y)G_{t}(x-y)dy\leq Ct^{\alpha/\ell}\big\{\varphi(x)^{-\beta}\wedge t^{-\beta/\ell}\big\}

and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(x,y)^{-\beta}\|x-y\|_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\alpha}\,w^{\ast}(x-y)G_{t}(x-y)dy\leq Ct^{\alpha/\ell}\varphi(x)^{-\beta}.

Proof.

The second inequality immediately follows from the first one because of the trivial inequality
\varphi(x,y)^{-\beta}\leq\varphi(x)^{-\beta}+\varphi(y)^{-\beta}
.
Hence we focus on the first inequality. To obtain the bound
Ct^{(\alpha-\beta)/\ell}
, we divide the integral into two parts.
In the region
\{|y_{1}|^{1/\mathfrak{s}_{1}}>t^{1/\ell}\}
, since
\varphi(y)^{-\beta}\leq t^{-\beta/\ell}
we have

\displaystyle\int_{|y_{1}|^{1/\mathfrak{s}_{1}}>t^{1/\ell}}\varphi(y)^{-\beta}\|x-y\|_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\alpha}\,w^{\ast}(x-y)G_{t}(x-y)dy

\displaystyle\leq t^{-\beta/\ell}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\|z\|_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\alpha}\,w^{\ast}(z)G_{t}(z)dz

\displaystyle\leq t^{(\alpha-\beta)/\ell}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\|z\|_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\alpha}\,w^{\ast}\big(t^{\mathfrak{s}/\ell}z\big)G(z)dz\lesssim t^{(\alpha-\beta)/\ell}.

In the region
\{|y_{1}|^{1/\mathfrak{s}_{1}}\leq t^{1/\ell}\}
, by treating the temporal variable and spatial variables separately, we have

\displaystyle\int_{|y_{1}|^{1/\mathfrak{s}_{1}}\leq t^{1/\ell}}\varphi(y)^{-\beta}\|x-y\|_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\alpha}\,w^{\ast}(x-y)G_{t}(x-y)dy

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\int_{|y_{1}|^{1/\mathfrak{s}_{1}}\leq t^{1/\ell}}|y_{1}|^{-\beta/\mathfrak{s}_{1}}dy_{1}\bigg)\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}\sup_{z_{1}\in\mathbb{R}}\|(z_{1},z^{\prime})\|^{\alpha}_{\mathfrak{s}}\,w^{\ast}(z_{1},z^{\prime})G_{t}(z_{1},z^{\prime})dz^{\prime}\bigg)

\displaystyle\lesssim(t^{\mathfrak{s}_{1}/\ell})^{1-\beta/\mathfrak{s}_{1}}

\displaystyle\hskip 35.0pt\times\bigg(t^{-\mathfrak{s}_{1}/\ell}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}\sup_{z_{1}\in\mathbb{R}}\|(t^{\mathfrak{s}_{1}/\ell}z_{1},t^{\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}/\ell}z^{\prime})\|^{\alpha}_{\mathfrak{s}}\,w^{\ast}(t^{\mathfrak{s}_{1}/\ell}z_{1},t^{\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}/\ell}z^{\prime})G(z_{1},z^{\prime})dz^{\prime}\bigg)

\displaystyle=(t^{\mathfrak{s}_{1}/\ell})^{1-\beta/\mathfrak{s}_{1}}\bigg(t^{-\mathfrak{s}_{1}/\ell+\alpha/\ell}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}\sup_{z_{1}\in\mathbb{R}}\|(z_{1},z^{\prime})\|^{\alpha}_{\mathfrak{s}}\,w^{\ast}(t^{\mathfrak{s}_{1}/\ell}z_{1},t^{\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}/\ell}z^{\prime})G(z_{1},z^{\prime})dz^{\prime}\bigg)

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{(\alpha-\beta)/\ell}.

Therefore, we obtain the upper bound
Ct^{(\alpha-\beta)/\ell}
.
Moreover, by decomposing

\displaystyle\varphi(x)^{\beta}\lesssim|x_{1}-y_{1}|^{\beta/\mathfrak{s}_{1}}+\varphi(y)^{\beta}\lesssim\|x-y\|_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\beta}+\varphi(y)^{\beta},

(2.5)

we have

\displaystyle\varphi(x)^{\beta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(y)^{-\beta}\|x-y\|_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\alpha}\,w^{\ast}(x-y)G_{t}(x-y)dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\{\varphi(y)^{-\beta}\|x-y\|_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\alpha+\beta}+\|x-y\|_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\alpha}\}\,w^{\ast}(x-y)G_{t}(x-y)dy

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{\alpha/\ell}.

This yields another bound
Ct^{\alpha/\ell}\varphi(x)^{-\beta}
.
∎

From the above lemma, we obtain an inequality similar to (
2.3
).

Corollary 2.7
.

Let
w
be a
G
-controlled weight.
For any
\beta\in[0,\mathfrak{s}_{1})
, there exists a constant
C
such that, for any
f\in L^{\infty}(\varphi^{\beta}w)
we have

\sup_{0<t\leq 1}\|G_{t}*f\|_{L^{\infty}(\varphi^{\beta}w)}+\sup_{0<t\leq 1}t^{\beta/\ell}\|G_{t}*f\|_{L^{\infty}(w)}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\varphi^{\beta}w)}.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.6
, we have

\displaystyle w(x)|(G_{t}*f)(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(y)^{-\beta}w^{*}(x-y)G_{t}(x-y)\varphi(y)^{\beta}w(y)|f(y)|dy

\displaystyle\leq C\big\{\varphi(x)^{-\beta}\wedge t^{-\beta/\ell}\big\}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\varphi^{\beta}w)}.

∎

We obtain the following assertions by arguments similar to
[
21
]
.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
w
be a
G
-controlled weight and let
\{Q_{t}\}_{t>0}
be a
G
-type semigroup.
We consider the weight
\tilde{w}:=\varphi^{\beta}w
for any fixed
\beta\in[0,\mathfrak{s}_{1})
.

(i)

For any
f\in L^{\infty}(\tilde{w})
and
t>0
, the function
Q_{t}f
belongs to
C(w)
.

(ii)

For any
\alpha\leq 0
, the Besov norm

\|f\|_{C^{\alpha,Q}(\tilde{w})}:=\sup_{0<t\leq 1}t^{-\alpha/\ell}\|Q_{t}f\|_{L^{\infty}(\tilde{w})}

is nondegenerate on
C(\tilde{w})
, so we can define
C^{\alpha,Q}(\tilde{w})
as the completion of
C(\tilde{w})
under this norm.

(iii)

For any
\alpha_{1}<\alpha_{2}\leq 0
, the identity
\tilde{\iota}_{\alpha_{1}}:C(\tilde{w})\hookrightarrow C^{\alpha_{1},Q}(\tilde{w})
is uniquely extended to the continuous injection
\tilde{\iota}_{\alpha_{1}}^{\alpha_{2}}:C^{\alpha_{2},Q}(\tilde{w})\hookrightarrow C^{\alpha_{1},Q}(\tilde{w})
.
For any
\alpha\leq 0
, the operator
Q_{t}:C(\tilde{w})\to C(\tilde{w})
is continuously extended to the operator
\tilde{Q}_{t}^{\alpha}:C^{\alpha,Q}(\tilde{w})\to\overline{C(\tilde{w})}
, where
\overline{C(\tilde{w})}
is the closure of
C(\tilde{w})
under the norm
\|\cdot\|_{L^{\infty}(\tilde{w})}
.
Moreover, they satisfy
\tilde{Q}_{t}^{\alpha_{1}}\circ\tilde{\iota}_{\alpha_{1}}^{\alpha_{2}}=\tilde{Q}_{t}^{\alpha_{2}}
for any
\alpha_{1}<\alpha_{2}\leq 0
.

(iv)

For any
\alpha\leq 0
, the identity
i:C(w)\hookrightarrow C(\tilde{w})
is uniquely extended to the continuous injection
i_{\alpha}:C^{\alpha,Q}(w)\hookrightarrow C^{\alpha,Q}(\tilde{w})
.
Moreover, the extensions
\tilde{Q}_{t}^{\alpha}:C^{\alpha,Q}(\tilde{w})\to\overline{C(\tilde{w})}
and
Q_{t}^{\alpha}:C^{\alpha,Q}(w)\to C(w)
defined in
(iii)
and Remark
2.5
satisfy the relation

i\circ Q_{t}^{\alpha}=\tilde{Q}_{t}^{\alpha}\circ i_{\alpha}.

Consequently, we can use the same notation
Q_{t}
to denote both
Q_{t}^{\alpha}
and
\tilde{Q}_{t}^{\alpha}
.

(v)

For any
\alpha\leq 0
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that, for any
f\in C^{\alpha,Q}(\tilde{w})
,
t\in(0,1]
, and
\varepsilon\in[0,\ell]
, we have

\|(Q_{t}-\mathop{\text{\rm id}})f\|_{C^{\alpha-\varepsilon,Q}(\tilde{w})}\leq C\,t^{\varepsilon/\ell}\|f\|_{C^{\alpha,Q}(\tilde{w})}.

The norm
C^{\alpha,Q}(\varphi^{\beta}w)
is used in the proof of Theorem
3.7
.

Proof.

(i)

We have
Q_{t}f\in L^{\infty}(w)
by Corollary
2.7
.
To show the continuity of
(Q_{t}f)(x)
with respect to
x
, it is sufficient to consider the case
t=1
.
By the property (
2.2
), for any fixed
R>0
and
n\geq 0
, the inequalities

\displaystyle w(x)|Q_{1}(x,y)f(y)|

\displaystyle\lesssim w^{*}(x-y)w(y)G(x-y)|f(y)|\lesssim\frac{\varphi(y)^{-\beta}}{1+\|y\|_{\mathfrak{s}}^{n}}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\tilde{w})}

hold uniformly over
\|x\|_{\mathfrak{s}}\leq R
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Since
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(y)^{-\beta}/(1+\|y\|_{\mathfrak{s}}^{n})dy<\infty
for
n>|\mathfrak{s}|
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{z\to x}(Q_{1}f)(z)w(z)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\lim_{z\to x}Q_{1}(z,y)f(y)w(z)dy=(Q_{1}f)(x)w(x)

by Lebesgue’s convergence theorem.
Since
w
is strictly positive and continuous, we have
\lim_{z\to x}(Q_{1}f)(z)=(Q_{1}f)(x)
.

(ii)

It is sufficient to show that

\lim_{t\downarrow 0}(Q_{t}f)(x)=f(x)

for any
f\in C(\tilde{w})
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
For any
\varepsilon>0
, we can choose
\delta>0
such that
|f(y)-f(x)|<\varepsilon
if
\|y-x\|_{\mathfrak{s}}<\delta
, and have

\displaystyle|w(x)(Q_{t}f-f)(x)|

\displaystyle=w(x)\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}Q_{t}(x,y)\big(f(y)-f(x)\big)dy+\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}Q_{t}(x,y)dy-1\bigg)f(x)\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq w(x)\varepsilon\int_{\|y-x\|_{\mathfrak{s}}<\delta}G_{t}(x-y)dy+w(x)\int_{\|y-x\|_{\mathfrak{s}}\geq\delta}G_{t}(x-y)|f(y)|dy

\displaystyle\quad+w(x)|f(x)|\int_{\|y-x\|_{\mathfrak{s}}\geq\delta}G_{t}(x-y)dy+w(x)|f(x)|\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}Q_{t}(x,y)dy-1\bigg|.

In the far right-hand side, the only nontrivial part is the second term. We bound it from above by

\displaystyle\int_{\|y-x\|_{\mathfrak{s}}\geq\delta}G_{t}(x-y)w^{*}(x-y)|f(y)|w(y)dy

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\tilde{w})}\int_{\|y-x\|_{\mathfrak{s}}\geq\delta}\varphi(y)^{-\beta}w^{*}(x-y)G_{t}(x-y)dy

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\tilde{w})}\delta^{-\mathfrak{s}_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\|y-x\|_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\mathfrak{s}_{1}}\varphi(y)^{-\beta}w^{*}(x-y)G_{t}(x-y)dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\tilde{w})}\delta^{-\mathfrak{s}_{1}}t^{(\mathfrak{s}_{1}-\beta)/\mathfrak{s}_{1}}.

Since
\beta<\mathfrak{s}_{1}
, we obtain the convergence as
t\downarrow 0
.

The proofs of
(iii)
and
(iv)
are similar to
[
21
, Proposition 2.14]
,
and the proof of
(v)
is similar to
[
21
, Lemma 2.15]
.
∎

3
Reconstruction of singular modelled distributions

In this section, we recall from
[
16
]
the definitions of regularity structures, models, and singular modelled distributions, and prove the reconstruction theorem for singular modelled distributions using the operator semigroup.
For simplicity, we consider only regularity structures, rather than general regularity-integrability structures as in
[
21
]
.
Throughout this and next sections, we fix a
G
-type semigroup
\{Q_{t}\}_{t>0}
.

3.1
Regularity structures and models

Definition 3.1
.

A
regularity structure

\mathscr{T}=(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{T},\mathbf{G})
consists of the following objects.

(1)

(Index set)
\mathbf{A}
is a locally finite subset of
\mathbb{R}
bounded below.

(2)

(Model space)
\mathbf{T}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{T}_{\alpha}
is an algebraic sum of Banach spaces
(\mathbf{T}_{\alpha},\|\cdot\|_{\alpha})
.

(3)

(Structure group)
\mathbf{G}
is a group of continuous linear operators on
\mathbf{T}
such that, for any
\Gamma\in\mathbf{G}
and
\alpha\in\mathbf{A}
,

(\Gamma-\mathop{\text{\rm id}})\mathbf{T}_{\alpha}\subset\mathbf{T}_{<\alpha}:=\bigoplus_{{\beta}\in\mathbf{A},\,{\beta}<\alpha}\mathbf{T}_{\beta}.

The smallest element
\alpha_{0}
of
\mathbf{A}
is called the
regularity
of
\mathscr{T}
.
For any
\alpha\in\mathbf{A}
, we denote by
P_{\alpha}:\mathbf{T}\to\mathbf{T}_{\alpha}
the canonical projection and write

\|\tau\|_{\alpha}:=\|P_{\alpha}\tau\|_{\alpha}

for any
\tau\in\mathbf{T}
, by abuse of notation.

Following
[
21
]
, we define the topology on the space of models by using
\{Q_{t}\}_{t>0}
.
For two Banach spaces
X
and
Y
, we denote by
\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
the Banach space of all continuous linear operators
X\to Y
. When
Y=\mathbb{R}
, we write
X^{*}:=\mathcal{L}(X,\mathbb{R})
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
w
be a
G
-controlled weight.
A
smooth model

M=(\Pi,\Gamma)
is a pair of two families of continuous linear operators
\Pi=\{\Pi_{x}:\mathbf{T}\to C(w)\}_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}
and
\Gamma=\{\Gamma_{xy}\}_{x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\subset\mathbf{G}
with the following properties.

(1)

(Algebraic conditions)

\Pi_{x}\Gamma_{xy}=\Pi_{y}
,
\Gamma_{xx}=\mathop{\text{\rm id}}
, and
\Gamma_{xy}\Gamma_{yz}=\Gamma_{xz}
for any
x,y,z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

(2)

(Analytic conditions)
For any
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\|\Pi\|_{\gamma,w}

\displaystyle:=\max_{\alpha\in\mathbf{A},\,\alpha<\gamma}\,\sup_{0<t\leq 1}\,\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big(t^{-\alpha/\ell}w(x)\big\|Q_{t}\big(x,\Pi_{x}(\cdot)\big)\big\|_{\mathbf{T}_{\alpha}^{*}}\Big)

\displaystyle=\max_{\alpha\in\mathbf{A},\,\alpha<\gamma}\,\sup_{0<t\leq 1}\,\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\,\sup_{\tau\in\mathbf{T}_{\alpha}\setminus\{0\}}\Bigg(t^{-\alpha/\ell}w(x)\frac{|Q_{t}(x,\Pi_{x}\tau)|}{\|\tau\|_{\alpha}}\Bigg)<\infty

and

\displaystyle\|\Gamma\|_{\gamma,w}

\displaystyle:=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta\in\mathbf{A}\\
\beta<\alpha<\gamma\end{subarray}}\,\sup_{x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,x\neq y}\frac{w(x)\|\Gamma_{yx}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{T}_{\alpha},\mathbf{T}_{\beta})}}{w^{\ast}(y-x)\|y-x\|_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\alpha-\beta}}

\displaystyle=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha,\beta\in\mathbf{A}\\
\beta<\alpha<\gamma\end{subarray}}\,\sup_{x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,x\neq y}\,\sup_{\tau\in\mathbf{T}_{\alpha}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{w(x)\|\Gamma_{yx}\tau\|_{\beta}}{w^{\ast}(y-x)\|y-x\|_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\alpha-\beta}\|\tau\|_{\alpha}}<\infty.

We write
|\!|\!|M|\!|\!|_{\gamma,w}:=\|\Pi\|_{\gamma,w}+\|\Gamma\|_{\gamma,w}
.
In addition, for any two smooth models
M^{(i)}=(\Pi^{(i)},\Gamma^{(i)})
with
i\in\{1,2\}
, we define the pseudo-metrics

|\!|\!|M^{(1)};M^{(2)}|\!|\!|_{\gamma,w}:=\|\Pi^{(1)}-\Pi^{(2)}\|_{\gamma,w}+\|\Gamma^{(1)}-\Gamma^{(2)}\|_{\gamma,w}

by replacing
\Pi
and
\Gamma
above with
\Pi^{(1)}-\Pi^{(2)}
and
\Gamma^{(1)}-\Gamma^{(2)}
respectively.
Finally, we define the space
\mathscr{M}_{w}(\mathscr{T})
as the completion of the set of all smooth models, under the pseudo-metrics
|\!|\!|\cdot;\cdot|\!|\!|_{\gamma,w}
for all
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
.
We call each element of
\mathscr{M}_{w}(\mathscr{T})
a
model
for
\mathscr{T}
. We still use the notation
M=(\Pi,\Gamma)
to denote a generic model.

When
\ell=2
and
Q_{t}
is the heat semigroup
e^{t\Delta}
, the above definition essentially coincides with the original definition of models
[
16
, Definition 2.17]
if we ignore the difference between local and global bounds. For more general semigroups, such an equivalence is unclear by the same reason as the case of Besov norms.

Remark 3.3
.

As stated in
[
21
, Proposition 3.3]
, if there exist two
G
-controlled weights
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
that satisfy

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big\{\|x\|_{\mathfrak{s}}^{n}\,w^{*}(x)w_{1}(x)\big\}+\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big\{\|x\|_{\mathfrak{s}}^{n}\,w^{*}_{1}(x)w_{2}(x)\big\}<\infty

for any

, and such that
ww_{1}
and
ww_{2}
are also
G
-controlled, then we can regard
\Pi_{x}
as a continuous linear operator from
\mathbf{T}
to
C^{\alpha_{0}\wedge 0,Q}(ww_{1})
, where
\alpha_{0}
is the regularity of
\mathscr{T}
.
More precisely, for any
\alpha<\gamma
and
\tau\in\mathbf{T}_{\alpha}
we have

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}(ww_{2})(x)\|\Pi_{x}\tau\|_{C^{\alpha_{0}\wedge 0,Q}(ww_{1})}\lesssim\|\Pi\|_{\gamma,w}(1+\|\Gamma\|_{\gamma,w})\|\tau\|_{\alpha}.

In what follows, we assume the existence of
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
as above, and regard
\Pi_{x}\tau
as an element of
C^{\alpha_{0}\wedge 0,Q}(ww_{1})
for any
\tau\in\mathbf{T}
.

3.2

Throughout the rest of this section, we fix a regularity structure

Recall the definitions of functions

Definition 3.4
.

Let
M=(\Pi,\Gamma)\in\mathscr{M}_{w}(\mathscr{T})

and
\eta\leq\gamma

\mathcal{D}_{v}^{\gamma,\eta}(\Gamma)

\displaystyle\llparenthesis\,f\,\rrparenthesis_{\gamma,\eta,v}

\displaystyle:=\max_{\alpha<\gamma}\sup_{x\in(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})\times\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}\frac{v(x)\|f(x)\|_{\alpha}}{\varphi(x)^{(\eta-\alpha)\wedge 0}}<\infty,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\gamma,\eta,v}

\displaystyle:=\max_{\alpha<\gamma}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})\times\mathbb{R}^{d-1},\,x\neq y\\

|\!|\!|f|\!|\!|_{\gamma,\eta,v}:=\llparenthesis\,f\,\rrparenthesis_{\gamma,\eta,v}+\|f\|_{\gamma,\eta,v}

In addition, for any two models

with

|\!|\!|f^{(1)};f^{(2)}|\!|\!|_{\gamma,\eta,v}:=\llparenthesis\,f^{(1)}-f^{(2)}\,\rrparenthesis_{\gamma,\eta,v}+\|f^{(1)};f^{(2)}\|_{\gamma,\eta,v}
by

\displaystyle\llparenthesis\,f^{(1)}-f^{(2)}\,\rrparenthesis_{\gamma,\eta,v}

\displaystyle:=\max_{\alpha<\gamma}\sup_{x\in(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})\times\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}\frac{v(x)\|f^{(1)}(x)-f^{(2)}(x)\|_{\alpha}}{\varphi(x)^{(\eta-\alpha)\wedge 0}},

\displaystyle\|f^{(1)};f^{(2)}\|_{\gamma,\eta,v}

\displaystyle:=\max_{\alpha<\gamma}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})\times\mathbb{R}^{d-1},\,x\neq y\\
\|y-x\|_{\mathfrak{s}}\leq\varphi(x,y)\end{subarray}}\frac{v(x)\|\Delta^{\Gamma}_{yx}f^{(1)}-\Delta^{\Gamma}_{yx}f^{(2)}\|_{\alpha}}{\varphi(x,y)^{\eta-\gamma}\,v^{*}(x-y)\|y-x\|_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\gamma-\alpha}}.

In
[
16
]
, the topologies of the space of models and the space of modelled distributions are defined by the family of pseudo-metrics parametrized by compact subsets

of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where

and

In this paper, we employ weight functions

and

We consider the relations between
\mathcal{D}_{v}^{\gamma,\eta}
under varying parameters
\gamma,\eta
, as well as the relation between
\mathcal{D}_{v}^{\gamma,\eta}
and a variant.
We say that the function
u:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is

for any

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
M=(\Pi,\Gamma)\in\mathscr{M}_{w}(\mathscr{T})
and
\eta\leq\gamma
.

(i)

For any
\theta\leq\eta
, we have the continuous embedding
\mathcal{D}_{v}^{\gamma,\eta}(\Gamma)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{D}_{v}^{\gamma,\theta}(\Gamma)
.

(ii)

Assume that
w^{*}
is symmetric.
For each
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, we denote by
P_{<\alpha}:\mathbf{T}\to\mathbf{T}_{<\alpha}
the canonical projection.
For any
\eta\leq\delta\leq\gamma
, the map
P_{<\delta}
extends to a continuous linear map
\mathcal{D}_{v}^{\gamma,\eta}(\Gamma)\to\mathcal{D}_{wv}^{\delta,\eta}(\Gamma)
.
To be precise, we have the inequality

\|P_{<\delta}f\|_{\delta,\eta,wv}\lesssim\|\Gamma\|_{\gamma,w}\llparenthesis\,f\,\rrparenthesis_{\gamma,\eta,v}+\|f\|_{\gamma,\eta,v}.

(iii)

Instead of the norm
\|f\|_{\gamma,\eta,v}
, we define

\|f\|_{\gamma,\eta,v}^{\#}:=\max_{\alpha<\gamma}\sup_{x,y\in(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})\times\mathbb{R}^{d-1},\,x\neq y}\frac{v(x)\|\Delta^{\Gamma}_{yx}f\|_{\alpha}}{\varphi(x,y)^{\eta-\gamma}\,v^{*}(x-y)\|y-x\|_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\gamma-\alpha}}.

Then the inequality
\|f\|_{\gamma,\eta,v}\leq\|f\|_{\gamma,\eta,v}^{\#}
obviously holds.
Conversely, if
w^{*}
is symmetric, then we also have

\|f\|_{\gamma,\eta\wedge\alpha_{0},wv}^{\#}\lesssim(1+\|\Gamma\|_{\gamma,w})\llparenthesis\,f\,\rrparenthesis_{\gamma,\eta,v}+\|f\|_{\gamma,\eta,v}.

Proof.

(i)

The assertion immediately follows from the inequalities
\varphi(x)^{(\eta-\alpha)\wedge 0}\leq\varphi(x)^{(\theta-\alpha)\wedge 0}
and
\varphi(x,y)^{\eta-\gamma}\leq\varphi(x,y)^{\theta-\gamma}
.

(ii)

For any
x,y\in(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})\times\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
such that
\|y-x\|_{\mathfrak{s}}\leq\varphi(x,y)
and any
\alpha<\delta
, we decompose

\displaystyle(wv)(x)\|\Delta_{yx}^{\Gamma}P_{<\delta}f\|_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq v(x)\|\Delta_{yx}^{\Gamma}f\|_{\alpha}+(wv)(x)\sum_{\beta\in[\delta,\gamma)}\|\Gamma_{yx}P_{\beta}f(x)\|_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=:A_{1}+A_{2}.

For
A_{1}
, by definition of the norm
\|f\|_{\gamma,\eta,v}
we have

\displaystyle A_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{\gamma,\eta,v}\,v^{*}(x-y)\varphi(x,y)^{\eta-\gamma}\|y-x\|_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\gamma-\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{\gamma,\eta,v}\,v^{*}(x-y)\varphi(x,y)^{\eta-\delta}\|y-x\|_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\delta-\alpha}.

For
A_{2}
, by definitions of the model and the norm
\llparenthesis\,f\,\rrparenthesis_{\gamma,\eta,v}
we have

\displaystyle A_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\beta\in[\delta,\gamma)}w(x)\|\Gamma_{yx}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{T}_{\beta},\mathbf{T}_{\alpha})}v(x)\|f(x)\|_{\beta}

\displaystyle\leq\|\Gamma\|_{\gamma,w}\llparenthesis\,f\,\rrparenthesis_{\gamma,\eta,v}\,w^{*}(y-x)\sum_{\beta\in[\delta,\gamma)}\|y-x\|_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\beta-\alpha}\varphi(x)^{\eta-\beta}

\displaystyle\leq\|\Gamma\|_{\gamma,w}\llparenthesis\,f\,\rrparenthesis_{\gamma,\eta,v}\,w^{*}(x-y)\|y-x\|_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\delta-\alpha}\varphi(x,y)^{\eta-\delta}.

Thus we obtain the desired inequality for
\|P_{<\delta}f\|_{\delta,\eta,wv}
.

(iii)

It is sufficient to show the estimate of
\Delta_{yx}^{\Gamma}f
on the region
\|y-x\|_{\mathfrak{s}}>\varphi(x,y)
.
For any
\alpha<\gamma
we decompose

(wv)(x)\|\Delta^{\Gamma}_{yx}f\|_{\alpha}\\
\leq v(x)\|f(y)\|_{\alpha}+(wv)(x)\sum_{\beta\in[\alpha,\gamma)}\|\Gamma_{yx}P_{\beta}f(x)\|_{\alpha}=:B_{1}+B_{2}.

For
B_{1}
, by definition of the norm
\llparenthesis\,f\,\rrparenthesis_{\gamma,\eta,v}
we have

\displaystyle B_{1}

\displaystyle\leq v^{*}(x-y)v(y)\|f(y)\|_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq\llparenthesis\,f\,\rrparenthesis_{\gamma,\eta,v}\,v^{*}(x-y)\varphi(y)^{(\eta-\alpha)\wedge 0}

\displaystyle\leq\llparenthesis\,f\,\rrparenthesis_{\gamma,\eta,v}\,v^{*}(x-y)\varphi(x,y)^{(\eta-\alpha)\wedge 0}

\displaystyle\leq\llparenthesis\,f\,\rrparenthesis_{\gamma,\eta,v}\,v^{*}(x-y)\varphi(x,y)^{\eta\wedge\alpha-\gamma}\|y-x\|_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\gamma-\alpha}.

For
B_{2}
, by an argument similar to
A_{2}
in the proof of
(ii)
, we have

\displaystyle B_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\Gamma\|_{\gamma,w}\llparenthesis\,f\,\rrparenthesis_{\gamma,\eta,v}\,w^{*}(y-x)\sum_{\beta\in[\alpha,\gamma)}\|y-x\|_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\beta-\alpha}\varphi(x)^{(\eta-\beta)\wedge 0}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\Gamma\|_{\gamma,w}\llparenthesis\,f\,\rrparenthesis_{\gamma,\eta,v}\,w^{*}(x-y)\|y-x\|_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\gamma-\alpha}\varphi(x,y)^{\eta\wedge\alpha-\gamma}.

Thus we obtain the desired inequality.
∎

3.3
Reconstruction Theorem

In this subsection, we provide a short proof of the reconstruction theorem.
First, we prove the theorem for the subclass
\mathcal{D}_{v}^{\gamma,\eta}(\Gamma)^{\#}
of
\mathcal{D}_{v}^{\gamma,\eta}(\Gamma)
consisting of all functions
f:(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})\times\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\to\mathbf{T}_{<\gamma}
such that

|\!|\!|f|\!|\!|_{\gamma,\eta,v}^{\#}:=\llparenthesis\,f\,\rrparenthesis_{\gamma,\eta,v}+\|f\|_{\gamma,\eta,v}^{\#}<\infty.

In addition, for any
M^{(i)}=(\Pi^{(i)},\Gamma^{(i)})\in\mathscr{M}_{w}(\mathscr{T})
and
f^{(i)}\in\mathcal{D}_{v}^{\gamma,\eta}(\Gamma^{(i)})^{\#}
with

, we define
|\!|\!|f^{(1)};f^{(2)}|\!|\!|_{\gamma,\eta,v}^{\#}:=\llparenthesis\,f^{(1)}-f^{(2)}\,\rrparenthesis_{\gamma,\eta,v}+\|f^{(1)};f^{(2)}\|_{\gamma,\eta,v}^{\#}
similarly to definition
3.4

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
\gamma>0
and
\eta\in(\gamma-\mathfrak{s}_{1},\gamma]
. Then for any
M=(\Pi,\Gamma)\in\mathscr{M}_{w}(\mathscr{T})
and
f\in\mathcal{D}_{v}^{\gamma,\eta}(\Gamma)^{\#}
, there exists a unique reconstruction
\mathcal{R}f\in C^{\zeta,Q}(wv)
of
f
for
M
with
\zeta:=\eta\wedge\alpha_{0}\wedge 0
and it holds that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{R}f\|_{C^{\zeta,Q}(wv)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\Pi\|_{\gamma,w}|\!|\!|f|\!|\!|_{\gamma,\eta,v}^{\#},

(3.1)

\displaystyle\llbracket\mathcal{R}f\rrbracket_{\gamma,\eta,wv}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\Pi\|_{\gamma,w}\|f\|_{\gamma,\eta,v}^{\#}.

(3.2)

Moreover, there is an affine function
C_{R}>0
of
R>0
such that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{R}f^{(1)}-\mathcal{R}f^{(2)}\|_{C^{\zeta,Q}(wv)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{R}\big(\|\Pi^{(1)}-\Pi^{(2)}\|_{\gamma,w}+|\!|\!|f^{(1)};f^{(2)}|\!|\!|_{\gamma,\eta,v}^{\#}\big),

\displaystyle\llbracket\mathcal{R}f^{(1)};\mathcal{R}f^{(2)}\rrbracket_{\gamma,\eta,wv}

\displaystyle\leq C_{R}\big(\|\Pi^{(1)}-\Pi^{(2)}\|_{\gamma,w}+\|f^{(1)};f^{(2)}\|_{\gamma,\eta,v}^{\#}\big)

for any
M^{(i)}=(\Pi^{(i)},\Gamma^{(i)})\in\mathscr{M}_{w}(\mathscr{T})
and
f^{(i)}\in\mathcal{D}_{v}^{\gamma,\eta}(\Gamma^{(i)})
with
i\in\{1,2\}
such that
|\!|\!|M^{(i)}|\!|\!|_{\gamma,w}\leq R
and
|\!|\!|f^{(i)}|\!|\!|_{\gamma,\eta,v}^{\#}\leq R
.

Proof.

The proof is carried out by a method similar to that of
[
21
, Theorem 4.1]
, but we have to treat the temporal weight more carefully.
For
t>0
and
0<s\leq t\wedge 1
, we define the functions

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{s}^{t}f(x):=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}Q_{t-s}(x,y)Q_{s}\big(y,\Pi_{y}f(y)\big)dy,\quad&&s<t,\\
&Q_{t}\big(x,\Pi_{x}f(x)\big),&&s=t.\end{aligned}\right.

Note that

\displaystyle(wv)(x)\big|Q_{t}\big(x,\Pi_{x}f(x)\big)\big|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\alpha<\gamma}w(x)\big\|Q_{t}\big(x,\Pi_{x}(\cdot)\big)\big\|_{\mathbf{T}_{\alpha}^{*}}v(x)\|f(x)\|_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq\|\Pi\|_{\gamma,w}\llparenthesis\,f\,\rrparenthesis_{\gamma,\eta,v}\sum_{\alpha<\gamma}t^{\alpha/\ell}\varphi(x)^{(\eta-\alpha)\wedge 0}.

Thus, by Proposition
2.8
-
(i)
, for any
s\in(0,t)
we have
\mathcal{R}_{s}^{t}f\in C(wv)
and

\|\mathcal{R}_{s}^{t}f\|_{L^{\infty}(wv)}\lesssim\|\Pi\|_{\gamma,w}\llparenthesis\,f\,\rrparenthesis_{\gamma,\eta,v}\sum_{\alpha<\gamma}s^{\alpha/\ell}(t-s)^{(\eta-\alpha)\wedge 0}.

(3.3)

We separate the proof into four steps.

(1) Cauchy property.
Set
F_{x}:=\Pi_{x}f(x)
. By the definition of norms, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &(wv)(y)|Q_{t}(x,F_{y}-F_{x})|\\
&=(wv)(y)\big|Q_{t}\big(x,\Pi_{x}\big\{\Gamma_{xy}f(y)-f(x)\big\}\big)\big|\\
&\leq w^{*}(y-x)\sum_{\alpha<\gamma}w(x)\|Q_{t}(x,\Pi_{x}(\cdot))\|_{\mathbf{T}_{\alpha}^{*}}v(y)\|\Gamma_{xy}f(y)-f(x)\|_{\alpha}\\
&\leq\|\Pi\|_{\gamma,w}\|f\|_{\gamma,\eta,v}^{\#}(w^{*}v^{*})(y-x)\sum_{\alpha<\gamma}t^{\alpha/\ell}\varphi(x,y)^{\eta-\gamma}\|y-x\|_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\gamma-\alpha}.\end{aligned}

(3.4)

By the semigroup property, for any
0<u<s<t\wedge 1
we have

\displaystyle(wv)(x)|\mathcal{R}_{s}^{t}f(x)-\mathcal{R}_{u}^{t}f(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{2}}(w^{*}v^{*})(x-y)(wv)(y)|Q_{t-s}(x,y)Q_{s-u}(y,z)Q_{u}(z,F_{y}-F_{z})|dydz

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\Pi\|_{\gamma,w}\|f\|_{\gamma,\eta,v}^{\#}\sum_{\alpha<\gamma}u^{\alpha/\ell}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{2}}(w^{*}v^{*})(x-y)(w^{*}v^{*})(y-z)

\displaystyle\hskip 100.0pt\times G_{t-s}(x-y)G_{s-u}(y-z)\varphi(y,z)^{\eta-\gamma}\|y-z\|_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\gamma-\alpha}dydz.

By applying the second inequality of Lemma
2.6
to the integral with respect to
z

and then applying the first inequality of Lemma
2.6
to the integral with respect to
y
, we obtain

\displaystyle(wv)(x)|\mathcal{R}_{s}^{t}f(x)-\mathcal{R}_{u}^{t}f(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\Pi\|_{\gamma,w}\|f\|_{\gamma,\eta,v}^{\#}

\displaystyle\hskip 30.0pt\times\sum_{\alpha<\gamma}u^{\alpha/\ell}(s-u)^{(\gamma-\alpha)/\ell}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{2}}(w^{*}v^{*})(x-y)G_{t-s}(x-y)\varphi(y)^{\eta-\gamma}dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\Pi\|_{\gamma,w}\|f\|_{\gamma,\eta,v}^{\#}\sum_{\alpha<\gamma}u^{\alpha/\ell}(s-u)^{(\gamma-\alpha)/\ell}\varphi(x)^{\eta-\gamma}.

Consequently, when
u\in[s/2,s)
we have the inequality

\displaystyle(wv)(x)|\mathcal{R}_{s}^{t}f(x)-\mathcal{R}_{u}^{t}f(x)|\lesssim\|\Pi\|_{\gamma,w}\|f\|_{\gamma,\eta,v}^{\#}\,\varphi(x)^{\eta-\gamma}s^{\gamma/\ell}.

(3.5)

Similarly to the proof of
[
21
, Theorem 4.1]
, we can also extend it into
u\in(0,s/2)
by decomposing

\displaystyle|\mathcal{R}_{s}^{t}f(x)-\mathcal{R}_{u}^{t}f(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|\mathcal{R}_{(s/2^{n})\wedge u}^{t}f(x)-\mathcal{R}_{(s/2^{n+1})\wedge u}^{t}f(x)|.

The same inequality for the case
s=t\leq 1
can be obtained by a similar argument.
In the end, the inequality (
3.5
) holds for any
0<u<s\leq t\wedge 1
.

(2) Convergence as
s\downarrow 0
.
Note that
Q_{s}\mathcal{R}_{u}^{t}f=\mathcal{R}_{u}^{t+s}f
follows from the semigroup property.
By the inequality (
3.5
), for any
0<u<s\leq t/2
we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &(wv)(x)|\mathcal{R}_{s}^{t}f(x)-\mathcal{R}_{u}^{t}f(x)|\\
&\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(w^{*}v^{*})(x-y)(wv)(y)|Q_{t/2}(x,y)(\mathcal{R}_{s}^{t/2}f-\mathcal{R}_{u}^{t/2}f)(y)|dy\\
&\lesssim\|\Pi\|_{\gamma,w}\|f\|^{\#}_{\gamma,\eta,v}\,s^{\gamma/\ell}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(w^{*}v^{*})(x-y)G_{t/2}(x-y)\varphi(y)^{\eta-\gamma}dy\\
&\lesssim\|\Pi\|_{\gamma,w}\|f\|^{\#}_{\gamma,\eta,v}\,s^{\gamma/\ell}t^{(\eta-\gamma)/\ell}.\end{aligned}

(3.6)

Since
\gamma>0
, this implies that
\{\mathcal{R}_{s}^{t}f\}_{0<s\leq t/2}
is Cauchy in
C(wv)
as
s\downarrow 0
. We denote its limit by

\mathcal{R}_{0}^{t}f:=\lim_{s\downarrow 0}\mathcal{R}_{s}^{t}f.

We also have
Q_{s}\mathcal{R}_{0}^{t}f=\mathcal{R}_{0}^{t+s}f
by taking the limit
u\downarrow 0
in
Q_{s}\mathcal{R}_{u}^{t}f=\mathcal{R}_{u}^{t+s}f
.

(3) Convergence as
t\downarrow 0
.
Combining the Cauchy property (
3.6
) and the bound (
3.3
) with
s=t/2
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|\mathcal{R}_{0}^{t}f\|_{L^{\infty}(wv)}&\leq\|\mathcal{R}_{t/2}^{t}f\|_{L^{\infty}(wv)}+\|\mathcal{R}_{t/2}^{t}f-\mathcal{R}_{0}^{t}f\|_{L^{\infty}(wv)}\\
&\lesssim\|\Pi\|_{\gamma,w}|\!|\!|f|\!|\!|_{\gamma,\eta,v}^{\#}\,t^{(\eta\wedge\alpha_{0})/\ell}.\end{aligned}

Since
Q_{s}\mathcal{R}_{0}^{t}f=\mathcal{R}_{0}^{t+s}f
, this implies

\sup_{0<t\leq 1}\|\mathcal{R}_{0}^{t}f\|_{C^{\eta\wedge\alpha_{0}\wedge 0,Q}(wv)}\lesssim\|\Pi\|_{\gamma,w}|\!|\!|f|\!|\!|_{\gamma,\eta,v}^{\#}.

From here onward, in exactly the same way as the part (4) of the proof of
[
21
, Theorem 4.1]
, we can show the existence of
\mathcal{R}f\in C^{\zeta,Q}(wv)
with
\zeta=\eta\wedge\alpha_{0}\wedge 0
which satisfies the bound (
3.1
) and

\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\|\mathcal{R}f-\mathcal{R}_{0}^{t}f\|_{C^{\zeta-\varepsilon,Q}(wv)}=0

for any
\varepsilon\in(0,\ell]
. Moreover, we have
Q_{t}\mathcal{R}f=\mathcal{R}_{0}^{t}f
by taking the limit
s\downarrow 0
in
Q_{t}\mathcal{R}_{0}^{s}f=\mathcal{R}_{0}^{t+s}f
.
We have another bound (
3.2
) by letting
u\downarrow 0
and
s=t
in the inequality (
3.5
).

(4) Uniqueness.
Let
\Lambda,\Lambda^{\prime}\in C^{\zeta,Q}(wv)
be reconstructions of
f
for
M
.
By the property of reconstruction,
g:=\Lambda-\Lambda^{\prime}
satisfies

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(x)^{\gamma-\eta}(wv)(x)|Q_{t}g(x)|\lesssim t^{\gamma/\ell}.

Set
\tilde{w}:=\varphi^{\gamma-\eta}wv
.
By Proposition
2.8
-
(iv)
and
(v)
, for any
\varepsilon\in(0,\ell]
we have

\displaystyle\|g\|_{C^{\zeta-\varepsilon,Q}(\tilde{w})}

\displaystyle\leq\|(Q_{t}-\mathop{\text{\rm id}})g\|_{C^{\zeta-\varepsilon,Q}(\tilde{w})}+\|Q_{t}g\|_{C^{\zeta-\varepsilon,Q}(\tilde{w})}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{\varepsilon/\ell}\|g\|_{C^{\zeta,Q}(\tilde{w})}+\|Q_{t}g\|_{L^{\infty}(\tilde{w})}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{\varepsilon/\ell}\|g\|_{C^{\zeta,Q}(wv)}+t^{\gamma/\ell}.

By taking the limit
t\downarrow 0
, we have
g=0
in
C^{\zeta-\varepsilon,Q}(\tilde{w})
.
By Proposition
2.8
-
(iii)
and
(iv)
, we also have
g=0
in
C^{\zeta,Q}(wv)
.
∎

The following result is used in Section
5
.

Proposition 3.8
.

In addition to the setting of Theorem
3.7
, we assume that the model
M
is smooth in the sense of Definition
3.2
and

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sup_{\tau\in\mathbf{T}_{\alpha}\setminus\{0\}}w(x)\frac{|(\Pi_{x}\tau)(x)|}{\|\tau\|_{\alpha}}<\infty

for any
\alpha\in\mathbf{A}
.
Then the reconstruction
\mathcal{R}f
of
f\in\mathcal{D}_{v}^{\gamma,\eta}(\Gamma)^{\#}
is realized as a continuous function on
(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})\times\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
such that

(\mathcal{R}f)(x)=\big(\Pi_{x}f(x)\big)(x)

for any
x\in(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})\times\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
.

Proof.

Set
\Lambda(x)=\big(\Pi_{x}f(x)\big)(x)
.
Since
(\Pi_{x}\tau)(x)=\lim_{t\downarrow 0}Q_{t}(x,\Pi_{x}\tau)=0
if
\tau\in\mathbf{T}_{\alpha}
with
\alpha>0
, we have

\displaystyle(wv)(x)|\Lambda(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\alpha\leq 0}w(x)\big\|\big(\Pi_{x}(\cdot)\big)(x)\big\|_{\mathbf{T}_{\alpha}^{*}}v(x)\|f(x)\|_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\alpha\leq 0}\varphi(x)^{(\eta-\alpha)\wedge 0}\lesssim\varphi(x)^{\eta\wedge 0}.

Since
\eta>-\mathfrak{s}_{1}
, we have
\Lambda\in C^{\eta\wedge 0,Q}(wv)\subset C^{\zeta,Q}(wv)
by Corollary
2.7
. Moreover, since

\displaystyle(wv)(x)|Q_{t}(x,\Lambda_{x})|=(wv)(x)\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}Q_{t}(x,y)\Pi_{y}\big(f(y)-\Gamma_{yx}f(x)\big)(y)dy\bigg|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\alpha\leq 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}w^{*}(x-y)G_{t}(x-y)w(y)\big\|\big(\Pi_{y}(\cdot)\big)(y)\big\|_{\mathbf{T}_{\alpha}^{*}}v(x)\|f(y)-\Gamma_{yx}f(x)\|_{\alpha}dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\alpha\leq 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(w^{*}v^{*})(x-y)G_{t}(x-y)\|y-x\|_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\gamma-\alpha}\varphi(x,y)^{\eta-\gamma}dy

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\alpha\leq 0}t^{(\gamma-\alpha)/\ell}\varphi(x)^{\eta-\gamma}\lesssim t^{\gamma/\ell}\varphi(x)^{\eta-\gamma},

we have
\llbracket\Lambda\rrbracket_{\gamma,\eta,wv}<\infty
.
Hence
\mathcal{R}f=\Lambda
by the uniqueness of the reconstruction.
∎

4
Multilevel Schauder estimate

This section is devoted to the proof of the multilevel Schauder estimate for singular modelled distributions.
After recalling from
[
21
]
the basics of regularizing kernels in the first subsection, we prove the multilevel Schauder estimate in the second subsection.

4.1
Regularizing kernels

We recall from
[
21
, Section 5.1]
the definition of regularizing kernels.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\bar{\beta}>0
.
A
\bar{\beta}
-regularizing (integral) kernel admissible for
\{Q_{t}\}_{t>0}
is a family of continuous functions
\{K_{t}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}\}_{t>0}
which satisfies the following properties for some constants
\delta>0
and
C_{K}>0
.

(i)

(Convolution with
Q
)
For any
0<s<t
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}K_{t-s}(x,z)Q_{s}(z,y)dz=K_{t}(x,y).

(ii)

(Upper estimate)
For any
{\bf k}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
with
|{\bf k}|_{\mathfrak{s}}<\delta
, the
{\bf k}
-th partial derivative of
K_{t}(x,y)
with respect to
x
exists, and we have for any
t>0
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

|\partial_{x}^{\bf k}K_{t}(x,y)|\leq C_{K}t^{(\bar{\beta}-|{\bf k}|_{\mathfrak{s}})/\ell-1}G_{t}(x-y).

(iii)

(Hölder continuity)
For any
{\bf k}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
with
|{\bf k}|_{\mathfrak{s}}<\delta
, any
t>0
and
x,y,h\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
\|h\|_{\mathfrak{s}}\leq t^{1/\ell}
,

\displaystyle\bigg|\partial_{x}^{\bf k}K_{t}(x+h,y)-\sum_{|{\bf l}|_{\mathfrak{s}}<\delta-|{\bf k}|_{\mathfrak{s}}}\frac{h^{\bf l}}{{\bf l}!}\partial_{x}^{{\bf k}+{\bf l}}K_{t}(x,y)\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq C_{K}\|h\|_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\delta-|{\bf k}|_{\mathfrak{s}}}\,t^{(\bar{\beta}-\delta)/\ell-1}G_{t}(x-y).

We fix a
\bar{\beta}
-regularizing kernel
\{K_{t}\}_{t>0}
throughout this section.
For any
f\in L^{\infty}(w)
with a
G
-controlled weight
w
and any
|{\bf k}|_{\mathfrak{s}}<\delta
, we define

(\partial^{\bf k}K_{t}f)(x):=:\partial^{\bf k}K_{t}(x,f):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\partial_{x}^{\bf k}K_{t}(x,y)f(y)dy.

Moreover, we write
\partial^{\bf k}Kf:=\int_{0}^{1}\partial^{\bf k}K_{t}fdt
if the integral makes sense.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
w
and
v
be
G
-controlled weights such that
w^{2}
and
wv
are also
G
-controlled.
Let
\mathscr{T}=(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{T},\mathbf{G})
be a regularity structure
and let
M=(\Pi,\Gamma)\in\mathscr{M}_{w}(\mathscr{T})
.

(i)

[
21
, Lemma 5.4]

For any
\alpha\leq 0
,
|{\bf k}|_{\mathfrak{s}}<\delta
, and
f\in L^{\infty}(w)
, we have

\|\partial^{\bf k}K_{t}f\|_{L^{\infty}(w)}\lesssim C_{K}\,t^{(\alpha+\bar{\beta}-|{\bf k}|_{\mathfrak{s}})/\ell-1}\|f\|_{C^{\alpha,Q}(w)},

where the implicit proportional constant depends only on
G
and
w
.
Consequently, if
|{\bf k}|_{\mathfrak{s}}<(\alpha+\bar{\beta})\wedge\delta
, the integral
\partial^{\bf k}Kf:=\int_{0}^{1}\partial^{\bf k}K_{t}fdt
converges in
C(w)
.

(ii)

[
21
, Lemma 5.6]

For any
\alpha<\gamma
,
\tau\in\mathbf{T}_{\alpha}
,
|{\bf k}|_{\mathfrak{s}}<\delta
, and
t\in(0,1]
, we have

\|\partial^{\bf k}K_{t}(x,{\Pi}_{x}\tau)\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}(w^{2})}\lesssim C_{K}\,t^{(\alpha+\bar{\beta}-|{\bf k}|_{\mathfrak{s}})/\ell-1}\|\Pi\|_{\gamma,w}(1+\|\Gamma\|_{\gamma,w})\|\tau\|_{\alpha},

where the implicit proportional constant depends only on
G,w
, and
\mathbf{A}
.
Consequently, if
|{\bf k}|_{\mathfrak{s}}<(\alpha+\bar{\beta})\wedge\delta
, the integral
\partial^{\bf k}K(x,\Pi_{x}\tau):=\int_{0}^{1}\partial^{\bf k}K_{t}(x,\Pi_{x}\tau)dt
converges for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

(iii)

Let
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
,
\eta\in(\gamma-\mathfrak{s}_{1},\gamma]
, and
\zeta\leq 0
.
For any
f\in\mathcal{D}_{v}^{\gamma,\eta}(\Gamma)^{\#}
and its reconstruction
\Lambda\in C^{\zeta,Q}(wv)
,
|{\bf k}|_{\mathfrak{s}}<\delta
, and
t\in(0,1]
, we have

\displaystyle(wv)(x)|\partial^{\bf k}K_{t}(x,\Lambda_{x})|

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{K}\,t^{(\gamma+\bar{\beta}-|{\bf k}|_{\mathfrak{s}})/\ell-1}\,\varphi(x)^{\eta-\gamma}\big(\llbracket\Lambda\rrbracket_{\gamma,\eta,wv}+\|\Pi\|_{\gamma,w}\|f\|_{\gamma,\eta,v}^{\#}\big),

where the implicit proportional constant depends only on
G,w,v
, and
\mathbf{A}
.
Consequently, if
|{\bf k}|_{\mathfrak{s}}<(\gamma+\bar{\beta})\wedge\delta
, the integral
\partial^{\bf k}K(x,\Lambda_{x}):=\int_{0}^{1}\partial^{\bf k}K_{t}(x,\Lambda_{x})dt
converges for any
x\in(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})\times\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
.

Proof.

We prove only
(iii)
.
By Definition
4.1
-
(i)
, we can decompose

\displaystyle|\partial^{\bf k}K_{t}(x,\Lambda_{x})|

\displaystyle\leq\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\partial^{\bf k}K_{t/2}(x,y)Q_{t/2}(y,\Lambda_{y})dy\bigg|

\displaystyle\quad+\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\partial^{\bf k}K_{t/2}(x,y)Q_{t/2}\big(y,\Pi_{y}f(y)-\Pi_{x}f(x)\big)dy\bigg|.

For the first term, by Definition
4.1
-
(ii)
and by the property of reconstruction, we have

\displaystyle(wv)(x)\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\partial^{\bf k}K_{t/2}(x,y)Q_{t/2}(y,\Lambda_{y})dy\bigg|

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{K}\,t^{(\bar{\beta}-|{\bf k}|_{\mathfrak{s}})/\ell-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(w^{*}v^{*})(x-y)G_{t/2}(x-y)(wv)(y)|Q_{t/2}(y,\Lambda_{y})|dy

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{K}\,t^{(\gamma+\bar{\beta}-|{\bf k}|_{\mathfrak{s}})/\ell-1}\llbracket\Lambda\rrbracket_{\gamma,\eta,wv}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(y)^{\eta-\gamma}(w^{*}v^{*})(x-y)G_{t/2}(x-y)dy

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{K}\,t^{(\gamma+\bar{\beta}-|{\bf k}|_{\mathfrak{s}})/\ell-1}\,\varphi(x)^{\eta-\gamma}\,\llbracket\Lambda\rrbracket_{\gamma,\eta,wv}.

For the second term, by using the inequality (
3.4
) obtained in the proof of Theorem
3.7
with
x
and
y
swapped, we have

\displaystyle(wv)(x)\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\partial^{\bf k}K_{t/2}(x,y)Q_{t/2}\big(y,\Pi_{y}f(y)-\Pi_{x}f(x)\big)dy\bigg|

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{K}\,t^{(\bar{\beta}-|{\bf k}|_{\mathfrak{s}})/\ell-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G_{t/2}(x-y)(wv)(x)\big|Q_{t/2}\big(y,\Pi_{y}f(y)-\Pi_{x}f(x)\big)\big|dy

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{K}\|\Pi\|_{\gamma,w}\|f\|_{\gamma,\eta,v}^{\#}\sum_{\alpha<\gamma}t^{(\alpha+\bar{\beta}-|{\bf k}|_{\mathfrak{s}})/\ell-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(x,y)^{\eta-\gamma}\|y-x\|_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\gamma-\alpha}

\displaystyle\hskip 200.0pt\times(w^{*}v^{*})(x-y)G_{t/2}(x-y)dy

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{K}\,t^{(\gamma+\bar{\beta}-|{\bf k}|_{\mathfrak{s}})/\ell-1}\,\varphi(x)^{\eta-\gamma}\,\|\Pi\|_{\gamma,w}\|f\|_{\gamma,\eta,v}^{\#}.

∎

4.2
Compatible models and multilevel Schauder estimate

We recall from
[
21
, Section 5.2]
the notions of abstract integrations and compatible models.
Hereafter, we use the
polynomial structure
generated by dummy variables
X_{1},\dots,X_{d}
as in
[
16
, Section 2]
.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
\bar{\mathscr{T}}=(\bar{\mathbf{A}},\bar{\mathbf{T}},\bar{\mathbf{G}})
be a regularity structure satisfying the following properties.

(1)

\mathbb{N}[\mathfrak{s}]\subset\bar{\mathbf{A}}
.

(2)

For each
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}[\mathfrak{s}]
, the space
\bar{\mathbf{T}}_{\alpha}
contains all
X^{\bf k}:=\prod_{i=1}^{d}X_{i}^{k_{i}}
with
|{\bf k}|_{\mathfrak{s}}=\alpha
.

(3)

The subspace
\mathop{\text{\rm span}}\{X^{\bf k}\}_{{\bf k}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}
of
\bar{\mathbf{T}}
is closed under
\bar{\mathbf{G}}
-actions.

Let
\mathscr{T}=(\mathbf{A},\mathbf{T},\mathbf{G})
be another regularity structure.
A continuous linear operator
\mathcal{I}:\mathbf{T}\to\bar{\mathbf{T}}
is called an
abstract integration
of order
\beta\in(0,\bar{\beta}]
if

\mathcal{I}:\mathbf{T}_{\alpha}\to\bar{\mathbf{T}}_{\alpha+\beta}

for any
\alpha\in\mathbf{A}
.
For a fixed
G
-controlled weight
w
, we say that the pair
(M,\bar{M})
of two models
M=(\Pi,\Gamma)\in\mathscr{M}_{w}(\mathscr{T})
and
\bar{M}=(\bar{\Pi},\bar{\Gamma})\in\mathscr{M}_{w}(\bar{\mathscr{T}})
is
compatible for
\mathcal{I}
if it satisfies the following properties.

(i)

For any
{\bf k}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
,

(\bar{\Pi}_{x}X^{\bf k})(\cdot)=(\cdot-x)^{\bf k},\qquad\bar{\Gamma}_{yx}X^{\bf k}=\sum_{{\bf l}\leq{\bf k}}\binom{\bf k}{\bf l}(y-x)^{\bf l}X^{{\bf k}-{\bf l}}.

(ii)

For each
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we define the linear map
\mathcal{J}(x):\mathbf{T}_{<\delta-\beta}\to\mathop{\text{\rm span}}\{X^{\bf k}\}_{|{\bf k}|_{\mathfrak{s}}<\delta}\subset\bar{\mathbf{T}}
by setting

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}(x)\tau=\sum_{|{\bf k}|_{\mathfrak{s}}<\alpha+\beta}\frac{X^{\bf k}}{{\bf k}!}\partial^{\bf k}K(x,\Pi_{x}\tau)

(4.1)

for any
\alpha\in\mathbf{A}
such that
\alpha+\beta<\delta
and
\tau\in\mathbf{T}_{\alpha}
.
Then for any
\tau\in\mathbf{T}_{<\delta-\beta}
,

\displaystyle\bar{\Gamma}_{yx}\big(\mathcal{I}+\mathcal{J}(x)\big)\tau=\big(\mathcal{I}+\mathcal{J}(y)\big)\Gamma_{yx}\tau.

In addition, if the regularity
\alpha_{0}
of
\mathscr{T}
is greater than
-\bar{\beta}
and

\displaystyle(\bar{\Pi}_{x}\mathcal{I}\tau)(\cdot)=K(\cdot,\Pi_{x}\tau)-\sum_{|{\bf k}|_{\mathfrak{s}}<\alpha+\beta}\frac{(\cdot-x)^{\bf k}}{{\bf k}!}\partial^{\bf k}K(x,\Pi_{x}\tau)

(4.2)

for any
\tau\in\mathbf{T}_{\alpha}
with
\alpha+\beta<\delta
, then we say that the pair
(M,\bar{M})
is
K
-admissible for
\mathcal{I}
.

In (
4.1
) and (
4.2
),
the function
K(\cdot,\Pi_{x}\tau)
and the coefficients
\partial^{\bf k}K(x,\Pi_{x}\tau)
are well-defined by Lemma
4.2
.
The following theorem is the second main result of this paper.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\mathscr{T}
and
\bar{\mathscr{T}}
be regularity structures satisfying the setting of Definition
4.3
and let
\mathcal{I}:\mathbf{T}\to\bar{\mathbf{T}}
be an abstract integration of order
\beta\in(0,\bar{\beta}]
.
Let
w
and
v
be
G
-controlled weights such that
w^{2}v
is also
G
-controlled.
Given
(\Pi,\Gamma)\in\mathscr{M}_{w}(\mathscr{T})
,
f\in\mathcal{D}_{v}^{\gamma,\eta}(\Gamma)^{\#}
with
\gamma+\bar{\beta}<\delta
and
\eta\in(\gamma-\mathfrak{s}_{1},\gamma]
, and its reconstruction
\Lambda\in C^{\zeta,Q}(wv)
, we define the functions

\mathcal{N}(x;f,\Lambda)=\sum_{|{\bf k}|_{\mathfrak{s}}<\gamma+\beta}\frac{X^{\bf k}}{{\bf k}!}\partial^{\bf k}K(x,\Lambda_{x})

and

\mathcal{K}f(x):=\mathcal{I}f(x)+\mathcal{J}(x)f(x)+\mathcal{N}(x;f,\Lambda)

for
x\in(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})\times\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
.
We assume
\zeta\leq\eta\wedge\alpha_{0}
and either of the following conditions.

(1)

\beta<\bar{\beta}
.

(2)

\beta=\bar{\beta}
and
\big\{\alpha+\bar{\beta}\,;\,\alpha\in\mathbf{A}\cup\{\gamma,\zeta\}\big\}\cap\mathbb{N}[\mathfrak{s}]=\emptyset
.

Lemma 5.8

We fix a smooth non-increasing function

Proof.

We can check that
|\!|\!|L\chi_{t}|\!|\!|_{\gamma^{\prime},0;\mathbb{R}}\lesssim 1

by definition, so by applying the continuity of the multiplication of modelled distributions

|\!|\!|E_{t}f|\!|\!|_{\gamma,\eta\wedge\alpha_{0};(0,2t)}\lesssim|\!|\!|f|\!|\!|_{\gamma,\eta;(0,2t)}.

By setting

\displaystyle\lesssim|\!|\!|f|\!|\!|_{\gamma,\eta;(0,2t)}\,\varphi(x,y)^{\eta\wedge\alpha_{0}-\gamma}\|y-x\|_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\gamma-\alpha}.

For the case that

\displaystyle v_{r}(x)\|(E_{t}f)(y)-\Gamma_{yx}(E_{t}f)(x)\|_{\alpha}=v_{r}(x)\|\Gamma_{yx}\{\Gamma_{xy}(E_{t}f)(y)-(E_{t}f)(x)\}\|_{\alpha}

5.5
Convergence of models

In this subsection, we define the sequence of smooth admissible models associated with regularized noises and show its probabilistic convergence.
We fix an even function

We define the smooth approximation of the spatial white noise

\displaystyle(\Pi_{x}^{n}\Xi)(y)=\xi_{n}(y^{\prime}),\qquad(\Pi_{x}^{n}X_{i}\Xi)(y)=(y_{i}-x_{i})\xi_{n}(y^{\prime}),

\displaystyle\big(\Pi_{x}^{n}\mathcal{I}(\Xi)\Xi\big)(y)=\big(K\xi_{n}(y)-K\xi_{n}(x)\big)\xi_{n}(y^{\prime})-C_{n}(y),

. By definition,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[Q_{t}(x,\Pi_{x}^{n}\tau)\big]

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}Q_{t}(x,y)\mathbb{E}[(K\xi_{n})(x)\xi_{n}(y^{\prime})]dy

\displaystyle=-\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{2}}Q_{t}(x,y)K(x,z)c_{n}(z^{\prime}-y^{\prime})dydz.

To estimate this integral, we decompose
K=\int_{0}^{1}K_{s}ds
and set

I_{t,s}^{n}(x)=-\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{2}}Q_{t}(x,y)K_{s}(x,z)c_{n}(z^{\prime}-y^{\prime})dydz.

By the Gaussian estimates of
Q_{t}
and
K_{s}
, their time integral is estimated as

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}|Q_{t}(x,y)|dy_{1}\lesssim h_{t}^{(C)}(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}),\qquad\int_{\mathbb{R}}|K_{s}(x,z)|dz_{1}\lesssim s^{-1/2}h_{s}^{(C)}(x^{\prime}-z^{\prime}),

for some constant
C>0
, where
h_{t}^{(C)}(x^{\prime}):=t^{-1/2}e^{-C\{(|x_{2}|^{4}/t)^{1/3}+(|x_{3}|^{4}/t)^{1/3}\}}
.
Thus we have

\displaystyle|I_{t,s}^{n}(x)|\lesssim s^{-1/2}(h_{t}^{(C)}*h_{s}^{(C)}*|c_{n}|)(0).

Since
|h_{t}^{(C)}*h_{s}^{(C)}(x)|\lesssim h_{t+s}^{(c)}(x)
for some constant
c\in(0,C)
(see
[
5
, Lemma 55]
for instance), we have

|I_{t,s}^{n}(x)|\lesssim s^{-1/2}(t+s)^{-1/2}.

Since we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}|I_{t,s}^{n}(x)|ds\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}s^{-1/2}t^{-1/2}ds+\int_{t}^{1}s^{-1}ds\lesssim-\log t\lesssim t^{-\varepsilon/2}

for any
\varepsilon>0
, we obtain the estimate (
5.6
) for
\tau=\mathcal{I}(\Xi)\Xi
.
∎

5.6
Renormalization of PAM

For a fixed initial condition
u_{0}\in C^{\theta}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
and the sequence of random models
\{M^{n}\}
constructed in the previous subsection, we denote by

U_{n}=S_{t}(u_{0},M^{n})

the solution of the equation (
5.5
) with
\gamma=1+2\varepsilon
and with the random time

t=t_{0}\bigg(\|u_{0}\|_{C^{\theta}(\mathbb{T}^{2})},\ \sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|M^{n}\|_{\gamma,v_{r}}\bigg).

Combining Theorem
5.13
with Theorem
5.12
, we have the following theorem.

Theorem 5.14
.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote by
\mathcal{R}^{n}
the reconstruction operator associated with
M^{n}
.
Then the function
u_{n}=\mathcal{R}^{n}(E_{t}U_{n})
converges in
L^{\infty}((0,t)\times\mathbb{T}^{2})
in probability as
n\to\infty
and coincides with the unique solution of the equation

\big(\partial_{1}-a(x^{\prime})\Delta+c\big)u_{n}(x)=b\big(u_{n}(x)\big)\xi_{n}(x^{\prime})-C_{n}(x)(bb^{\prime})\big(u_{n}(x)\big)

(5.7)

with the initial value
u_{0}\in C^{\theta}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
on
x\in(0,t)\times\mathbb{T}^{2}
.

As noted in Remark
5.3
, the constant
c
in the equation (
5.7
) can be arbitrary.

Proof.

On the region
x\in(0,t)\times\mathbb{T}^{2}
, since
u_{n}(x)=\big(\Pi_{x}^{n}U_{n}(x)\big)(x)
, we can assume that
U_{n}
is of the form

U_{n}(x)=u_{n}(x){\bf 1}+v_{n}(x)\mathcal{I}(\Xi)+u_{2,n}(x)X_{2}+u_{3,n}(x)X_{3}.

(5.8)

The convergence of
\{u_{n}\}
in
L^{\infty}((0,t)\times\mathbb{T}^{2})
follows from the convergence of
\{U_{n}\}
and the definition of the norm
\llparenthesis\,\cdot\,\rrparenthesis_{\gamma,\eta;(0,t)}
.

Finally, we show that
u_{n}
satisfies the equation (
5.7
) on the region
(0,t)\times\mathbb{T}^{2}
.
For any
x\in(0,t)\times\mathbb{T}^{2}
, the function
b(U_{n})(x)
is of the form

b(U_{n})(x)=b(u_{n}(x)){\bf 1}+b^{\prime}(u_{n}(x))\{v_{n}(x)\mathcal{I}(\Xi)+u_{2,n}(x)X_{2}+u_{3,n}(x)X_{3}\},

and then
\mathcal{P}_{t}^{1+2\varepsilon}\big(b(U)\Xi\big)
is of the form

\mathcal{P}_{t}^{1+2\varepsilon}\big(b(U)\Xi\big)(x)=w_{n}(x){\bf 1}+b(u_{n}(x))\mathcal{I}(\Xi)+w_{2,n}(x)X_{2}+w_{3,n}(x)X_{3}

for some functions
w_{n},w_{2,n}
, and
w_{3,n}
.
For
U_{n}
to solve the equation (
5.5
), the coefficient
v_{n}(x)
in (
5.8
) must be equal to
b(u_{n}(x))
for any
x\in(0,t)\times\mathbb{T}^{2}
.
By Theorem
5.11
, the function
u_{n}
satisfies

\displaystyle u_{n}(x)=Pu_{0}(x)+\int_{[0,x_{1}]\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}P_{x_{1}-y_{1}}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})(\mathcal{R}^{n}E_{t}f(U_{n})\Xi)(y)dy.

Since
y\in(0,t)\times\mathbb{T}^{2}
, from the definition of
\Pi_{x}^{n}\mathcal{I}(\Xi)\Xi
, we obtain

\displaystyle(\mathcal{R}E_{t}b(U_{n})\Xi)(y)

\displaystyle=\big(\Pi_{y}E_{t}b(U_{n})(y)\Xi\big)(y)=\big(\Pi_{y}b(U_{n})(y)\Xi\big)(y)

\displaystyle=b(u_{n}(y))\xi_{n}(y^{\prime})-C_{n}(y)(bb^{\prime})(u_{n}(y)).

This implies that
u_{n}
satisfies the equation (
5.7
) (in mild sense) on
(0,t)\times\mathbb{T}^{2}
.
∎

We also have a stronger convergence result.

Corollary 5.15
.

In the setting of Theorem
5.14
, the convergence of
\{u_{n}\}
also holds in the space
C_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\theta}((0,t)\times\mathbb{T}^{2})
.

Proof.

We only show the uniform bounds of
\{u_{n}\}
in the
\theta
-Hölder norm, since the proof of the convergence is a simple modification. First, we set
\bar{U}_{n}=\mathcal{P}_{t}^{1+2\varepsilon}\big(b(U_{n})\Xi\big)\in\mathcal{D}^{1+2\varepsilon,\theta}((0,2t),\mathbf{S};\Gamma^{n})
and decompose

u_{n}=Pu_{0}+\bar{u}_{n},\qquad\bar{u}_{n}:=\mathcal{R}^{n}(E_{t}\bar{U}_{n}).

Since the uniform bounds of
\{Pu_{0}\}
in the
\theta
-Hölder norm is more elementary (see e.g.
[
5
, Proposition 62]
), we focus on the remaining term. By definition, for any
x\in(0,t)\times\mathbb{T}^{2}
,
\bar{u}_{n}(x)
coincides with the
{\bf 1}
-component of
\bar{U}_{n}(x)
, and also with that of
P_{<\theta}\bar{U}_{n}(x)
. Since
\{P_{<\theta}\bar{U}_{n}\}
is uniformly bounded in the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\theta,\theta;(0,t)}
by Proposition
3.5
-
(ii)
, we have

|\bar{u}_{n}(y)-\bar{u}_{n}(x)|\lesssim\|y-x\|_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\theta}

for any
x,y\in(0,t)\times\mathbb{T}^{2}
such that
\|y-x\|_{\mathfrak{s}}\leq\varphi(x,y)
.
Here and in what follows, we omit proportional constants polynomially depending on the norms of
\{\bar{U}_{n}\}
and
\{\Gamma^{n}\}
, which are uniform over
n
.
It remains to show the same Hölder-type inequality in the region
\varphi(x,y)<\|y-x\|_{\mathfrak{s}}
. In this region, by using the inequality (
2.5
), we have
\varphi(x)\vee\varphi(y)\lesssim\|y-x\|_{\mathfrak{s}}
.
On the other hand, we also have that
\{\bar{U}_{n}\}
is uniformly bounded in the norm
\llparenthesis\,\cdot\,\rrparenthesis_{1+2\varepsilon,\theta;(0,t)}^{\circ}
by Lemma
5.10
. Hence

\displaystyle|\bar{u}_{n}(y)-\bar{u}_{n}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq|\bar{u}_{n}(y)|+|\bar{u}_{n}(x)|\lesssim\varphi(y)^{\theta}+\varphi(x)^{\theta}\lesssim\|y-x\|_{\mathfrak{s}}^{\theta}

in the region
\varphi(x,y)<\|y-x\|_{\mathfrak{s}}
. This completes the proof.
∎

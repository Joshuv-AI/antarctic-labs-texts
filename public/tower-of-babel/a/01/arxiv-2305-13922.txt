Derivation and well-posedness for asymptotic models of cold plasmas
Diego Alonso-Orán, Angel Durán, Rafael Granero-Belinchón

Abstract
. In this paper we derive three new asymptotic models for an hyperbolic-hyperbolic-elliptic system of PDEs describing the motion of a collision-free plasma in a magnetic field. The first of these models takes the form of a non-linear and non-local Boussinesq system (for the ionic density and velocity) while the second is a non-local wave equation (for the ionic density). Moreover, we derive a unidirectional asymptotic model of the later which is closely related to the well-known Fornberg-Whitham equation. We also provide the well-posedness of these asymptotic models in Sobolev spaces. To conclude, we demonstrate the existence of a class of initial data which exhibit wave breaking for the unidirectional model.

1.
Introduction

The motion of a cold plasma in a magnetic field consisting of singly-charged particles can be described by the following system of PDEs
[
2
,
12
]

(1a)

\displaystyle n_{t}+(un)_{x}

\displaystyle=0,

(1b)

\displaystyle u_{t}+uu_{x}+\frac{bb_{x}}{n}

\displaystyle=0,

(1c)

\displaystyle b-n-\left(\frac{b_{x}}{n}\right)_{x}

\displaystyle=0

where
n,u
and
b
are the ionic density, the ionic velocity and the magnetic field, respectively. Moreover, it has also been used as a simplified model to describe the motion of collission-free two fluid model where the electron inertial, charge separation and displacement current are neglected and the Poisson equation (
1
c) is initially satisfied,
[
2
,
20
]
. In (
1
) the spatial domain
\Omega
is either
\Omega=\mathbb{R}
or
\Omega=\mathbb{S}^{1}
(
i.e.

x\in\mathbb{R}
or
x\in[-\pi,\pi]
with periodic boundary conditions) and the time variable satisfies
t\in[0,T]
for certain
0<T\leq\infty
. The corresponding initial-value problem (ivp) consists of the system (
1
) along with initial conditions

(2)

n(x,0)=n_{0}(x),\;u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),

which are assumed to be smooth enough for the purposes of the work.

System (
1
) was introduced by Gardner & Morikawa
[
12
]
. Furthermore, Gardner & Morikawa formally showed that the solutions of (
1
) converge to solutions of the Korteveg-de Vries equation (see also the paper by Su & Gardner
[
25
]
). Berezin & Karpman extended this formal limit to the case where the wave propagates at certain angles with respect to the magnetic field
[
2
]
,
i.e.
for angles satisfying certain size conditions. Later on, Kakutani, Ono, Taniuti & Wei
[
20
]
removed the hypothesis on the angle. This formal KdV limit was recently justified by Pu & Li
[
24
]
.

1.1.
Contributions and main results

The purpose of this paper is two-fold. First, we derive three asymptotic models for the hyperbolic-hyperbolic-elliptic system of PDEs describing the motion of a collision-free plasma in a magnetic field given in (
1
). The method to obtain the new asymptotic models relies on a multi-scale expansion (cf. e. g.
[
1
,
3
,
4
,
9
,
5
,
13
,
14
]
) which reduces the full system (
1
) to a cascade of linear equations which can be closed up to some order of precision.

More specifically, writing

(3)

n=1+N,\;U=u,\;b=1+B,

and, for
\varepsilon>0
, introducing the formal expansions

(4)

N=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\varepsilon^{\ell+1}N^{(\ell)},\;B=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\varepsilon^{\ell+1}B^{(\ell)},\;U=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\varepsilon^{\ell+1}U^{(\ell)},

then the first model is an
O(\epsilon^{2})
approximation of (
1
) and takes the form of
the following Boussinesq type system

(5a)

\displaystyle h_{t}+(hv)_{x}+v_{x}

\displaystyle=0,

(5b)

\displaystyle v_{t}+vv_{x}+\left[\mathscr{L},\mathscr{N}h\right]h+\mathscr{N}h

\displaystyle=0,

for
h=\varepsilon N^{(0)}+\varepsilon^{2}N^{(1)},\;v=\varepsilon U^{(0)}+\varepsilon^{2}U^{(1)}
. The nonlocal terms in (
5
) are given by

(6)

\mathscr{L}=-\partial_{x}^{2}(1-\partial_{x}^{2})^{-1},\;\mathscr{N}=\partial_{x}(1-\partial_{x}^{2})^{-1}\;(\text{so }\partial_{x}\mathscr{N}=-\mathscr{L}),

which are Fourier multiplier operators with symbols

(7)

\widehat{\mathscr{L}h}(\xi)=\frac{\xi^{2}}{1+\xi^{2}}\hat{h}(\xi),\;\widehat{\mathscr{N}h}(\xi)=\frac{i\xi}{1+\xi^{2}}\hat{h}(\xi).

(where
\widehat{h}(\xi)
denotes the Fourier transform of
h
at
\xi
) and
\left[\mathscr{L},\cdot\right]\cdot
denotes the commutator

(8)

\left[\mathscr{L},f\right]g=\mathscr{L}(fg)-f\mathscr{L}g.

The extra assumption
U^{(0)}=N^{(0)}
in (
4
) leads to the formal derivation of the second asymptotic model, as a bidirectional single non-local wave equation

(9)

\displaystyle h_{tt}+\mathscr{L}h

\displaystyle=\left(hh_{x}+\left[\mathscr{L},\mathscr{N}h\right]h\right)_{x}-2\left(hh_{t}\right)_{x}.

The formal reduction of (
9
) to the corresponding unidirectional version, cf.
[
29
]
, yields

(10)

h_{t}=-\frac{1}{2}\left(3hh_{x}-[\mathscr{L},\mathscr{N}h]h-\mathscr{N}h-h_{x}\right),

being the third asymptotic model introduced in the present paper. We note that the
unidirectional equation (
10
) has strong similarities with the well-known equation

(11)

u_{t}+\frac{3}{2}uu_{x}=\mathscr{N}u,

proposed by Fornberg & Whitham as a model for breaking waves
[
11
]
. The latter equation has been intensively studied during the last decades and several results regarding the well-posedness of the ivp in different functional spaces as well as various wave-breaking criteria have appeared in the literature
[
19
,
30
,
15
,
18
,
17
,
16
]
. A significant difference between the structure of system (
11
) and the unidirectional equation (
10
) derived in the present paper is the emergence of the nonlocal commutator-type term.

The second purpose of this work is the study of several analytical properties of the models (
5
), (
9
), and (
10
). They are mainly concerned with the existence of conserved quantities, well-posedness (in the sense of existence and uniqueness of solutions of the corresponding ivp), and the formation of special solutions. This paper will be focused on the first two points, while the existence and dynamics of solitary-wave solutions will be the object of a separate forthcoming study.

Specifically, the results shown in this paper can be summarized as follows:

•

The system (
5
) and the unidirectional model (
10
) admit several quantities preserved by the solutions in suitable spaces, including a Hamiltonian structure. On the other hand, (
9
) can be written in a conservation form, leading in a natural way to the existence of a conserved quantity.

•

The system (
5
) is locally well posed on a modified Sobolev space involving the operator
\mathscr{L}
.

•

The bidirectional non-local wave equation (
9
) has a unique local solution close to the equilibrium and for initial data with sufficiently small
L^{\infty}
norm.

•

The ivp for the equation (
10
) is locally well-posed in Sobolev spaces
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
for
s>\frac{3}{2}
. Furthermore, a blow-up criteria for the solution by means of a logarithmic Sobolev inequality is provided. In addition, smooth solutions of (
10
) are shown to exhibit wave breaking under suitable hypothesis on the initial condition.

1.3.
Preliminaries and notation

Let us next introduce some notation that will be used throughout the rest of the paper.

The functional spaces

For
1\leq p\leq\infty
, let
L^{p}=L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
be the usual normed space of
L^{p}
-functions on
\mathbb{R}
with
||\cdot||_{p}
as the associated norm. For
s\in\mathbb{R}
, the inhomogeneous Sobolev space
H^{s}=H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
is defined as

\displaystyle H^{s}(\mathbb{R})\triangleq\left\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}):\|f\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}(1+\xi^{2})^{s}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}<+\infty\right\},

with norm

\left\|f\right\|_{H^{s}}=\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\|f\right\|_{\dot{H}^{s}},

where
\left\|f\right\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}=\left\|\Lambda^{s}f\right\|_{L^{2}}
and
\Lambda^{s}
is defined by

\widehat{\Lambda^{s}f}(\xi)=|\xi|^{s}\widehat{f}(\xi)
, where
\widehat{f}
is the Fourier transform of
f
.

The space of functions with bounded mean oscillation
\text{BMO}(\mathbb{R})
(cf.
[
26
,
27
]
) is defined by

\displaystyle\text{BMO}(\mathbb{R})\triangleq\left\{f\in L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}):\|f\|_{\text{BMO}(\mathbb{R})}=\displaystyle\sup_{r>0,x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}}\int_{x_{0}-r}^{x_{0}+r}\left|f(x)-\bar{f}(x)\right|\ dx<+\infty\right\},

where
\bar{f}(x)=\frac{1}{2r}\int_{x_{0}-r}^{x_{0}+r}f(y)\ dy
.

Next, let us introduce two lemmas estimates regarding Sobolev spaces. The first one deals with the so called Kato-Ponce commutator estimate.

Lemma 1.1

(
[
21
,
22
]
)
.

If
f,g\in H^{s}\bigcap W^{1,\infty}
with
s>0
, then for
p,p_{i}\in(1,\infty)
with
i=1,\ldots,4
and

\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}=\frac{1}{p_{3}}+\frac{1}{p_{4}}
, we have

\|\left[\Lambda^{s},f\right]g\|_{L^{p}}\leq C_{s,p}(\|\partial_{x}f\|_{L^{p_{1}}}\|\Lambda^{s-1}g\|_{L^{p_{2}}}+\|\Lambda^{s}f\|_{L^{p_{3}}}\|g\|_{L^{p_{4}}}),

and

\|\Lambda^{s}(fg)\|_{L^{p}}\leq C_{s,p}(\|f\|_{L^{p_{1}}}\|\Lambda^{s}g\|_{L^{p_{2}}}+\|\Lambda^{s}f\|_{L^{p_{3}}}\|g\|_{L^{p_{4}}}).

The second gives a logarithmic Sobolev inequality.

Lemma 1.2

(
[
10
]
)
.

Let
s>\frac{1}{2}
. There exists a constant
C=C(s)>0
such that

\left\|f\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\left(1+\left\|f\right\|_{\text{BMO}}\big[1+\displaystyle\log(1+\left\|f\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R})})\big]\right),

holds for all
f\in H^{s}.

The Helmholtz operator

We denote by
\mathscr{Q}
the operator
(1-\partial_{x}^{2})^{-1}
which acting on functions
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
has the representation

(12)

\mathscr{Q}f(x)=[G\star f](x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}G(x-\eta)f(\eta)\ d\eta,G(x)=\frac{1}{2}e^{-|x|},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}.

Furthermore, the Fourier symbol of
\mathscr{Q}
is

\widehat{\mathscr{Q}f}(\xi)=\frac{1}{1+|\xi|^{2}}\widehat{f}(\xi),

and by a simple computation we have that
\mathscr{Q}f\in H^{2}
if
f\in L^{2}
and

(13)

\partial_{x}^{2}\mathscr{Q}f(x)=\left(\mathscr{Q}-\text{I}\right)f(x),\;x\in\mathbb{R},

where
I
denotes the identity operator.

Constants

Throughout the paper
C=C(\cdot)
will denote a positive constant that may depend on fixed parameters and
x\lesssim y
(
x\gtrsim y
) means that
x\leq Cy
(
x\geq Cy
) holds for some
C
.

2.1.
The non-local Boussinesq model

Using (
3
), the
system (
1
) can be equivalently written as

(14a)

\displaystyle N_{t}+(UN)_{x}+U_{x}

\displaystyle=0,

(14b)

\displaystyle U_{t}+UU_{x}+\frac{(1+B)B_{x}}{1+N}

\displaystyle=0,

(14c)

\displaystyle B-N-\left(\frac{B_{x}}{1+N}\right)_{x}

\displaystyle=0.

In this new variables, the initial data (
2
) takes the form

(15)

N(x,0)=n_{0}(x)-1,\;U(x,0)=u_{0}(x).

Furthermore, we can rewrite (

U_{t}+UU_{x}+B_{x}+BB_{x}+U_{t}N+NUU_{x}=0.

Similarly, (

B-N+BN^{2}-N^{3}+2NB-2N^{2}-B_{xx}-NB_{xx}+B_{x}N_{x}=0.

Then, (

(16a)

\displaystyle N_{t}+U_{x}

\displaystyle=-(NU)_{x},

(16b)

\displaystyle U_{t}+B_{x}

\displaystyle=-UU_{x}-BB_{x}-U_{t}N-NUU_{x},

(16c)

\displaystyle B-N-B_{xx}

\displaystyle=-BN^{2}+N^{3}-2NB+2N^{2}+NB_{xx}-B_{x}N_{x}.

Now, from the ansatz (
4
)
and equating in powers of
\epsilon

, and

(17a)

\displaystyle N^{(0)}_{t}+U^{(0)}_{x}

\displaystyle=0,

(17b)

\displaystyle U^{(0)}_{t}+B^{(0)}_{x}

\displaystyle=0,

(17c)

\displaystyle B^{(0)}-N^{(0)}-B^{(0)}_{xx}

\displaystyle=0,

(18a)

\displaystyle N^{(0)}_{t}+U^{(0)}_{x}

\displaystyle=0,

(18b)

\displaystyle U^{(0)}_{t}+\partial_{x}(1-\partial_{x}^{2})^{-1}N^{(0)}

\displaystyle=0,

(18c)

\displaystyle B^{(0)}

\displaystyle=(1-\partial_{x}^{2})^{-1}N^{(0)}.

Then, (

N^{(0)}_{tt}+\mathscr{L}N^{(0)}=0,\;U^{(0)}_{tt}+\mathscr{L}U^{(0)}=0.

The second term in the expansion solves

(20a)

\displaystyle N^{(1)}_{t}+U^{(1)}_{x}

\displaystyle=-(N^{(0)}U^{(0)})_{x},

(20b)

\displaystyle U^{(1)}_{t}+B^{(1)}_{x}

\displaystyle=-U^{(0)}U^{(0)}_{x}-B^{(0)}B^{(0)}_{x}-U^{(0)}_{t}N^{(0)},

(20c)

\displaystyle B^{(1)}-N^{(1)}-B^{(1)}_{xx}

\displaystyle=-2N^{(0)}B^{(0)}+2(N^{(0)})^{2}+N^{(0)}B_{xx}^{(0)}-B_{x}^{(0)}N_{x}^{(0)}.

Note that, using (

\displaystyle B^{(1)}-N^{(1)}-B^{(1)}_{xx}

\displaystyle=-2N^{(0)}(B^{(0)}-N^{(0)}-B_{xx}^{(0)})-N^{(0)}B_{xx}^{(0)}-B_{x}^{(0)}N_{x}^{(0)}

\displaystyle=-(N^{(0)}B_{x}^{(0)})_{x}.

Then, from (

(21)

B^{(1)}=(1-\partial_{x}^{2})^{-1}N^{(1)}-\mathscr{N}(N^{(0)}B_{x}^{(0)})=(1-\partial_{x}^{2})^{-1}N^{(1)}-\mathscr{N}(N^{(0)}\mathscr{N}N^{(0)}).

Now, using (
18

20
b) can be written as

U^{(1)}_{t}+B^{(1)}_{x}=-U^{(0)}U^{(0)}_{x}-B^{(0)}(\mathscr{N}N^{(0)})+(\mathscr{N}N^{(0)})N^{(0)}.

Furthermore, from (

-B^{(0)}(\mathscr{N}N^{(0)})+(\mathscr{N}N^{(0)})N^{(0)}=-\mathscr{N}N^{(0)}B^{(0)}_{xx},

and using (

(22)

U^{(1)}_{t}+B^{(1)}_{x}=-U^{(0)}U^{(0)}_{x}+\mathscr{N}N^{(0)}\mathscr{L}N^{(0)}.

Substitution of
B^{(1)}
from (

) into (

) leads to

\displaystyle U^{(1)}_{t}+\mathscr{N}N^{(1)}

\displaystyle=-\mathscr{L}(N^{(0)}\mathscr{N}N^{(0)})-U^{(0)}U^{(0)}_{x}+\mathscr{N}N^{(0)}\mathscr{L}N^{(0)}

(23)

\displaystyle=-U^{(0)}U^{(0)}_{x}-\left[\mathscr{L},\mathscr{N}N^{(0)}\right]N^{(0)},

where the commutator is given by (

). The approximate model (

) for the truncations
h=\varepsilon N^{(0)}+\varepsilon^{2}N^{(1)},\;v=\varepsilon U^{(0)}+\varepsilon^{2}U^{(1)},

2.2.
The non-local single wave equation model

From (
19
) we observe that
N^{(0)}
and
U^{(0)}
satisfy the same linear nonlocal wave equation. Thus, under the extra assumption of having the same initial data, we can conclude that

(24)

U^{(0)}=N^{(0)}.

This will allow to further simplify the system (
5
). Taking the time derivative of (
20
a) and using (
23
) we find that

\displaystyle N^{(1)}_{tt}-\mathscr{N}N^{(1)}_{x}

\displaystyle=(U^{(0)}U^{(0)}_{x})_{x}-\left[\mathscr{L},\mathscr{N}N^{(0)}\right]N^{(0)}_{x}-(N^{(0)}U^{(0)})_{xt}.

Furthermore, from (

) and (

), we conclude that

\displaystyle N^{(1)}_{tt}+\mathscr{L}N^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\left(N^{(0)}N^{(0)}_{x}+\left[\mathscr{L},\mathscr{N}N^{(0)}\right]N^{(0)}\right)_{x}-2\left(N^{(0)}N^{(0)}_{t}\right)_{x}.

Considering now the truncation
h=\varepsilon N^{(0)}+\varepsilon^{2}N^{(1)},
and neglecting contributions of order
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{3})
in the last expression, the single wave equation (

2.3.
The unidirectional non local wave model

In this section, we derive the unidirectional asymptotic model (
10
). We introduce the following far field variables

(25)

\chi=x-t,\quad\tau=\varepsilon t.

Using the chain rule we have that

\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}h(\chi(x,t),\tau(t))=h_{\chi\chi}-\varepsilon h_{\chi\tau}-\varepsilon h_{\tau\chi}+\varepsilon^{2}h_{\tau\tau}.

On the other hand, using the representation (
12
) of the Helmholtz operator
\mathscr{Q}
, it is not hard to see that

\mathscr{Q}=(1-\partial_{\chi\chi})^{-1}.

Therefore, from the change of variables (
25
) and neglecting terms of order
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{3})
(notice that by construction
h\sim\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)
), we find that equation (
9
) becomes

\left(h_{\chi}-2\varepsilon h_{\tau}\right)_{\chi}+(\mathscr{N}h)_{\chi}=\left(3hh_{\chi}-[\mathscr{L},\mathscr{N}h]h\right)_{\chi}

which after integrating in
\chi
, reordering terms and going back by abuse of notation to variables
x
and

we obtain (
10
).

3.
Conserved quantities

In this section we derive some conserved quantities of the models above. We start with the system (
5
). Note first that we can write

(26)

\left[\mathscr{L},\mathscr{N}h\right]h=\mathscr{L}\left(h\mathscr{N}h\right)+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{x}\left(\mathscr{N}h\right)^{2}.

Property (
26
) leads to the formulation of (
5
)
in conservation form

\partial_{t}\begin{pmatrix}h\\
v\end{pmatrix}+\partial_{x}f(h,v)=0,

where

f(h,v)=\begin{pmatrix}v(1+h)\\
\frac{v^{2}}{2}-\mathscr{N}\left(h\mathscr{N}h\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathscr{N}h\right)^{2}+\mathscr{Q}h\end{pmatrix}.

Then, if
u=\mathscr{Q}h
and we assume that
h,v,u,u_{x}\rightarrow 0
as
|x|\rightarrow\infty
, we obtain the preservation of

I_{1}(h,v)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}hdx,\quad I_{2}(h,v)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}vdx.

On the other hand, the following lemma is used below to derive a third conserved quantity.

Lemma 3.1
.

If
h\rightarrow 0
as
|x|\rightarrow\infty
, then:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}h\mathscr{L}\left(h\mathscr{N}h\right)dx=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}h\partial_{x}\left(\mathscr{N}h\right)^{2}dx,

(27a)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}h\mathscr{N}hdx=0.

Proof.

We use the Fourier symbols of the operators
\mathscr{L},\mathscr{N}
, the relation
\partial_{x}\mathscr{N}=-\mathscr{L}
, and Plancherel identity to have
the following identities:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}h\mathscr{L}\left(h\mathscr{N}h\right)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}h\mathscr{N}h\mathscr{L}hdx=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}h\mathscr{N}h\partial_{x}\mathscr{N}hdx=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}h\partial_{x}\left(\mathscr{N}h\right)^{2}dx,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}h\mathscr{N}hdx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\mathscr{N}h\right)hdx.

∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
h,v
be solutions of (
5
) with
h,v\rightarrow 0
as
|x|\rightarrow\infty
and let

(28)

I(h,v)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}hvdx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}(uv+u_{x}v_{x})dx,

where
u=\mathscr{Q}h
. Then

\frac{d}{dt}I(h,v)=0.

Proof.

Using (
26
), we write (
5
) in the form

(29a)

\displaystyle h_{t}+(hv)_{x}+v_{x}

\displaystyle=0,

(29b)

\displaystyle v_{t}+vv_{x}+\mathscr{L}\left(h\mathscr{N}h\right)+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{x}\left(\mathscr{N}h\right)^{2}+\mathscr{N}h

\displaystyle=0.

Multiplying (
29
a) by
v
, (
29
b) by
h
, adding these two amounts, using Lemma
3.1
, and the hypotheses on
h
and
v
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(h_{t}v+v_{t}h+v((1+h)v)_{x}+vv_{x}h\right)dx=0.

Finally, using that
h,v\rightarrow 0
as
|x|\rightarrow\infty
again, note that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(v((1+h)v)_{x}+vv_{x}h\right)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(vv_{x}+v(hv)_{x}+vv_{x}h\right)dx,

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial_{x}}\left(\frac{v^{2}}{2}\right)-v_{x}hv+v_{x}hv\right)dx=0.

∎

A final result on (
5
) is the Hamiltonian formulation. The proof is direct.

Theorem 1
.

The system (
5
) admits a Hamiltonian structure

\partial_{t}\begin{pmatrix}h\\
v\end{pmatrix}=\mathscr{J}\delta E(h,v),

where the solution pair
(h,v)
is smooth enough and vanishes at infinity,

\delta E=\left(\frac{\delta E}{\delta h},\frac{\delta E}{\delta v}\right)^{T},

denotes the variational derivative,

\mathscr{J}=-\partial_{x}\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},

and

E(h,v)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(v^{2}(1+h)+(\mathscr{B}h)^{2}+h(\mathscr{N}h)^{2}\right)dx,\quad\mathscr{Q}=\mathscr{B}^{2}.

On the other hand, using (
26
), the bidirectional model (
9
) can be written in a conservation form

\partial_{t}(h_{t}+\partial_{x}h^{2})-\partial_{x}\left(\mathscr{N}h+\partial_{x}\left(\frac{h^{2}}{2}\right)-\partial_{x}\mathscr{N}(h\mathscr{N}h)+\partial_{x}\left(\frac{(\mathscr{N}h)^{2}}{2}\right)\right)=0,

which, assuming that
h
is sufficiently smooth and that
h_{x}
vanishes at infinity, implies that

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(h_{t}+\partial_{x}h^{2})dx=0.

As far as the unidirectional model (
10
) is concerned, using again (
26
), the model is written in conservation form

(31)

h_{t}+\partial_{x}\left(\frac{3}{4}h^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\mathscr{N}(h\mathscr{N}h)-\frac{1}{4}(\mathscr{N}h)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\mathscr{Q}h-\frac{h}{2}\right)=0,

which implies, when

as

If, in addition, we multiply (

and use Lemma

norm

\int_{\mathbb{R}}h^{2}dx,

is the second conserved quantity. Finally,
the unidirectional model (

also admits a Hamiltonian structure

where now

and

and where the phase space for (

4.
Well-posedness for the non-local Boussinesq system

In this section we will show the well-posedness of system (
5
). Due to the coupled nature of the equations when performing the a priori energy estimates we need to
symmetrize
the system. To this end, let us introduce the following functional space

(33)

\mathcal{X}=\{(h,v):\left\|(h,v)\right\|_{\mathcal{X}}=\left\|(h,v)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\times L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}+\left\|\sqrt{\mathscr{L}}\partial_{x}^{2}h\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}+\left\|\partial_{x}^{3}v\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}<\infty\}.

If
m(\xi)=\frac{\xi^{2}}{1+\xi^{2}}
denotes the Fourier multiplier of the operator
\mathscr{L}
(cf. (
7
)) then in (
33
)
\mathscr{T}=\sqrt{\mathscr{L}}
denotes the operator with Fourier symbol
\sqrt{m(\xi)}
, and therefore it formally satisfies
\mathscr{T}^{2}={\mathscr{L}}
. Then the norm introduced in the definition of
\mathcal{X}
in (
33
) is related to a classical seminorm in
{H}^{k}(\mathbb{R})
as follows.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
be smooth enough. Then, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

(34)

\left\|\partial_{x}^{k}f\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\left(\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|\sqrt{\mathscr{L}}\partial_{x}^{k}f\right\|_{L^{2}}\right).

Proof.

Let
R>0
and
{B(0,R)}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}:|x|\leq R\}
.
Using Parseval identity we have that

\left\|\partial_{x}^{k}f\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\left\|\widehat{\partial_{x}^{k}f}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\xi^{2k}|\hat{f}(\xi)|^{2}=\int_{B(0,R)}\xi^{2k}|\hat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\ d\xi+\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus B(0,R)}\xi^{2k}|\hat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\ d\xi.

The first integral can be bounded by

(35)

\int_{B(0,R)}\xi^{2k}|\hat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\ d\xi\leq R^{2k}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\hat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\ d\xi=R^{2k}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

On the other hand, note that for
\xi\in\mathbb{R}\setminus B(0,R)
we have the pointwise bound

\frac{\xi^{2}}{1+\xi^{2}}\geq\frac{R^{2}}{1+R^{2}}.

Then it holds that

(36)

\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus B(0,R)}\xi^{2k}|\hat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\ d\xi\leq\frac{1+R^{2}}{R^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus B(0,R)}\frac{\xi^{2+k}}{1+\xi^{2}}\xi^{k}|\hat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\ d\xi\leq\frac{1+R^{2}}{R^{2}}\left\|\sqrt{\mathscr{L}}\partial_{x}^{k}f\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Therefore, choosing for instance
R=1
, (
35
), (
36
) yield (
34
).
∎

Then we see that
\mathcal{X}
is a modified version of
H^{2}\times H^{3}
.

Theorem 2
.

For
(h_{0},v_{0})\in H^{2}\times H^{3}
there exist a time
0<T_{max}
and a unique solution

(h,v)\in C((0,T_{max}),H^{2}\times H^{3})

of the ivp of (
5
) with
h(x,0)=h_{0}(x),v(x,0)=v_{0}(x),x\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

We first focus on obtaining some a priori estimates.
We define the energy

(37)

\mathcal{E}(t)=\left\|(h,v)\right\|_{L^{2}\times L^{2}}^{2}+\left\|\sqrt{\mathscr{L}}\partial_{x}^{2}h\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\|\partial_{x}^{3}v\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

where the norm considered in the space
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\times L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
is given by

\left\|(f,g)\right\|_{L^{2}\times L^{2}}=\left(||f||_{L^{2}}^{2}+||g||_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)^{1/2},f,g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}).

Multiplying the first equation of (
5
) by
h
, the second by
v
, adding the resulting equalities and integrating on
\mathbb{R}
we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\|(h,v)\right\|_{L^{2}\times L^{2}}^{2}=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left((hv)_{x}+v_{x}\right)h\ dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(vv_{x}+[\mathscr{L},\mathscr{N}h]h+\mathscr{N}h\right)v\ dx.

Now integration by parts, the application of Hölder and Young inequalities, and the Sobolev embedding
H^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}(\mathbb{R})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
for
\epsilon>0
lead to

(38)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\|(h,v)\right\|_{L^{2}\times L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\left\|v_{x}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|h\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\leq\left\|\partial_{x}^{3}v\right\|^{3}_{L^{2}}+\left\|h\right\|_{L^{2}}^{3}.

Next, we deal with the other terms in (
37
). Multiplying the first equation in (
5

\mathscr{L}\partial_{x}^{4}h
and integrating we have

(39)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathscr{L}\partial_{x}^{4}hh_{t}\ dx=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}(hv)_{x}\mathscr{L}\partial_{x}^{4}h\ dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}}v_{x}\mathscr{L}\partial_{x}^{4}h\ dx.

On the other hand, multiplying the second equation in (
5

\partial_{x}^{6}v
and integrating we obtain

(40)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}v_{t}\partial_{x}^{6}v\ dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}vv_{x}\partial_{x}^{6}v\ dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}}[\mathscr{L},\mathscr{N}h]h\partial_{x}^{6}v\ dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathscr{N}h\partial_{x}^{6}v\ dx.

Since
-\mathscr{L}=\partial_{x}\mathscr{N}
, integrating by parts we find that the last term in (

(41)

-\int_{\mathbb{R}}v_{x}\mathscr{L}\partial_{x}^{4}h\ dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}v_{x}\mathscr{N}\partial_{x}^{5}h\ dx=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}^{6}v\mathscr{N}h\ dx.

Moreover, we have that

(42)

\int_{\mathbb{R}}h_{t}\mathscr{L}\partial_{x}^{4}h\ dx=\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\|\sqrt{\mathscr{L}}\partial_{x}^{2}h\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}},\quad-\int_{\mathbb{R}}v_{t}\partial_{x}^{6}v\ dx=\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\|\partial_{x}^{3}v\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

Then, adding (
39
) and (
40
), and using (
41
),(
42
) lead to

(43)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\left\|\sqrt{\mathscr{L}}\partial_{x}^{2}h\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\|\partial_{x}^{3}v\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)=\underbrace{-\int_{\mathbb{R}}(hv)_{x}\mathscr{L}\partial_{x}^{4}h\ dx}_{I_{1}}+\underbrace{\int_{\mathbb{R}}vv_{x}\partial_{x}^{6}v\ dx}_{I_{2}}+\underbrace{\int_{\mathbb{R}}[\mathscr{L},\mathscr{N}h]h\partial_{x}^{6}v\ dx}_{I_{3}}.

We now estimate each of the integrals
I_{i}
in (

).
First, notice that integration by parts yields

I_{2}=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}^{2}(vv_{x})\partial_{x}^{3}v\ dx=-\frac{3}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial_{x}^{3}v)^{2}v_{x}\ dx,

and thus

(44)

\displaystyle|I_{2}|\leq C\left\|\partial_{x}v\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|\partial_{x}^{3}v\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\leq C\left\|\partial_{x}^{3}v\right\|^{3}_{L^{2}},

where in the second inequality we used the Sobolev embedding
H^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}(\mathbb{R})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
for
\epsilon>0.

Integrating by parts the first term
I_{1}
we find that

I_{1}=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}^{3}(hv)\partial_{x}^{2}\mathscr{L}h\ dx=\underbrace{-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\partial_{x}^{3}vh+\partial_{x}^{2}vh_{x}+v_{x}\partial_{x}^{2}h\right)\partial_{x}^{2}\mathscr{L}h\ dx}_{J_{1}}\underbrace{-\int_{\mathbb{R}}v\partial_{x}^{3}h\partial_{x}^{2}\mathscr{L}h\ dx}_{J_{2}}.

We use Hölder inequality to estimate

\displaystyle|J_{1}|

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left\|h\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|\partial_{x}^{3}v\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|\partial_{x}^{2}v\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|\partial_{x}h\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|v_{x}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|h_{xx}\right\|_{L^{2}}\right)\left\|\partial_{x}^{2}\mathscr{L}h\right\|_{L^{2}}

(47)

In a similar way, expanding the commutator and using (

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}^{4}\left((\mathscr{Q}h)^{2}\right)\partial_{x}^{3}v\ dx=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}^{2}\left((\mathscr{Q}h_{x})^{2}-\mathscr{Q}h\mathscr{L}h\right)\partial_{x}^{3}v\ dx.

Using the fact that, cf. (

for some constant

. As far as

(50)

|K_{2}|\leq C\left\|\mathscr{Q}h\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|\partial_{x}^{3}v\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\sqrt{\mathscr{L}}\partial_{x}^{2}h\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\left\|\partial_{x}^{3}v\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|h_{xx}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\sqrt{\mathscr{L}}\partial_{x}^{2}h\right\|_{L^{2}},

for some constant
C
. From (

4

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\left\|\sqrt{\mathscr{L}}\partial_{x}^{2}h\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\|\partial_{x}^{3}v\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\bigg(\left\|\partial_{x}^{3}v\right\|^{3}_{L^{2}}+\left\|\partial_{x}^{3}v\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|h_{xx}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

(51)

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt+\left\|\partial_{x}^{3}v\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|h_{xx}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\sqrt{\mathscr{L}}\partial_{x}^{2}h\right\|_{L^{2}}\bigg).

Applying Lemma

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\left\|\sqrt{\mathscr{L}}\partial_{x}^{2}h\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\|\partial_{x}^{3}v\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\leq C\left(\left\|\sqrt{\mathscr{L}}\partial_{x}^{2}h\right\|^{3}_{L^{2}}+\left\|h\right\|^{3}_{L^{2}}+\left\|\partial_{x}^{3}v\right\|^{3}_{L^{2}}\right),

(54a)

\displaystyle h^{\epsilon}_{t}+\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}(\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}h^{\epsilon}\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}v^{\epsilon})_{x}+\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}v^{\epsilon}_{x}

\displaystyle=0,

∎

5.
Well-posedness in Sobolev spaces for the bidirectional non-local wave equation

In this section, we provide the local-well posedness on the bidirectional non-local wave equation (

Theorem 3
.

Let
(h_{0},h_{1})
be such that
(h_{0}-1,h_{1})\in H^{4}\times H^{3}
and

\|h_{0}-1\|_{L^{\infty}}<1/2.

Then, there exist
0<T
and a unique solution to (

)

(h-1,h_{t})\in C([0,T],H^{4}\times H^{3}),

with initial condition
(h_{0},h_{1})
.

Proof.

As before, existence and uniqueness of solutions of (
9
) are based on deriving useful a priori energy estimates. To this end, we write
h=1+w
and then equation (
9
) becomes

(55)

w_{tt}+\mathscr{L}w=\left((1+w)w_{x}+\left[\mathscr{L},\mathscr{N}w\right]w\right)_{x}-2\left((1+w)w_{t}\right)_{x}.

We define the energy

(56)

\mathcal{E}(t)=\left\|w_{t}\right\|^{2}_{H^{3}}+\left\|\sqrt{\mathscr{L}}\partial_{x}^{3}w\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\left\|w\right\|^{2}_{H^{4}}.

Testing equation (
55
) against
w_{t}
and integrating by parts we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\left\|w_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\|\sqrt{\mathscr{L}}w\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}(w_{x}^{2}+(1+w)w_{xx})w_{t}\ dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\left[\mathscr{L},\mathscr{N}w\right]w\right)_{x}w_{t}\ dx

\displaystyle\quad-2\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left((1+w)w_{tx}w_{t}+w_{x}w_{t}^{2}\right)\ dx\leq C\mathcal{E}(t)^{3/2}.

In particular,

\frac{d}{dt}\left\|w_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\mathcal{E}(t)^{3/2}.

Furthermore, from Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and (
56

\frac{d}{dt}\left\|w\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}=2\int_{\mathbb{R}}w_{t}w\ dx\leq 2\left\|w\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|w_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq\mathcal{E}(t).

On the other hand, it holds that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\left\|\partial_{x}^{3}w_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\|\sqrt{\mathscr{L}}\partial_{x}^{3}w\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\underbrace{-\int_{\mathbb{R}}(w_{x}^{2}+(1+w)w_{xx})\partial_{x}^{6}w_{t}\ dx}_{M_{1}}\underbrace{-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left[\mathscr{L},\mathscr{N}w\right]w\partial_{x}^{7}w_{t}\ dx}_{M_{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\underbrace{2\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left((1+w)w_{tx}\partial_{x}^{6}w_{t}+w_{x}w_{t}\partial_{x}^{6}w_{t}\right)\ dx}_{M_{3}}.

In order to estimate each of the integrals
M_{i}
, we first notice a hiding energy extra term in

. Integrating by parts we obtain

\displaystyle M_{1}=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\|\partial_{x}^{4}w\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\underbrace{-\int_{\mathbb{R}}w_{x}^{2}\partial_{x}^{6}w_{t}\ dx}_{M_{12}}\underbrace{-\int_{\mathbb{R}}ww_{xx}\partial_{x}^{6}w_{t}\ dx}_{M_{13}}.

It is easy to check that after integration by parts

(57)

\displaystyle\left|M_{12}\right|\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\partial_{x}^{3}(\partial_{x}w)^{2}\partial_{x}^{3}w_{t}\ dx\right|\leq C\left(\left\|\partial_{x}^{4}w\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|w_{x}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}+\left\|\partial_{x}^{3}w\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|w_{xx}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)\left\|\partial_{x}^{3}w_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}.

Similarly, integrating by parts we have that

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}}w\partial_{x}^{5}w\partial_{x}^{3}w_{t}\ dx=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}w\partial_{t}(\partial_{x}^{4}w)^{2}\ dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}}w_{x}\partial_{x}^{4}w\partial_{x}^{3}w_{t}\ dx.

The last term in (

Thus, from (

\displaystyle|\text{l.o.t}|\leq C\left(\left\|\partial_{x}^{3}w\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|w_{xx}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}+\left\|w_{xx}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|w_{xx}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|w_{x}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|\partial_{x}^{4}w\right\|_{L^{2}}\right)\left\|\partial_{x}^{3}w_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\mathcal{E}(t)^{3/2}.

In order to estimate the commutator term

, let us recall that from (

Using a duality

\displaystyle\left|M_{2}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left\|\mathscr{Q}(\mathscr{Q}w_{x}w)\right\|_{\dot{H}^{4}}+\left\|\partial_{x}(\mathscr{Q}w)^{2}\right\|_{\dot{H}^{4}}\right)\left\|\partial_{x}^{7}w_{t}\right\|_{\dot{H}^{-4}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left\|\mathscr{Q}w_{x}w\right\|_{\dot{H}^{2}}+\left\|(\mathscr{Q}w)^{2}\right\|_{\dot{H}^{5}}\right)\left\|\partial_{x}^{3}w_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}

from some constant

\displaystyle|M_{3}|

\displaystyle=\left|2\int_{\mathbb{R}}w_{tx}\partial_{x}^{6}w_{t}+ww_{tx}\partial_{x}^{6}w_{t}+w_{x}w_{t}\partial_{x}^{6}w_{t}\ dx\right|

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\left\|\partial_{x}^{3}w\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|w_{xt}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\bigg)\times\left\|\partial_{x}^{3}w_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\mathcal{E}(t)^{3/2}

Hence, combining estimates (

5

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}(t)\leq C\left(\mathcal{E}(t)^{3/2}+\mathcal{E}(t)\right)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}w\partial_{t}\left(\partial_{x}^{4}w\right)\ dx.

Integrating in time leads to

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{E}(0)+C\int_{0}^{t}\left(\mathcal{E}^{3/2}(s)+\mathcal{E}(s)\right)\ ds-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}w_{s}(x,s)(\partial_{x}^{4}w(x,s))^{2}\ dxds

(65)

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt-\frac{1}{2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}w_{0}(x)(\partial_{x}^{4}w_{0}(x))^{2}\ dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}}w(x,t)(\partial_{x}^{4}w(x,t))^{2}\ dx\right).

Defining, for
T>0

\mathsf{E}_{T}=\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\mathcal{E}(t)=\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left(\left\|w_{t}\right\|^{2}_{H^{3}}+\left\|\sqrt{\mathscr{L}}\partial_{x}^{3}w\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\left\|w\right\|^{2}_{H^{4}}\right),

taking supremum in time in (

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}_{T}

\displaystyle\leq\mathsf{E}_{0}+Ct\left(\mathsf{E}_{T}^{3/2}+\mathsf{E}_{T}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left\|w_{0}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|\partial_{x}^{4}w_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left(\left\|w\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|\partial_{x}^{4}w\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)

(66)

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt+\frac{1}{2}\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|w_{s}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|\partial_{x}^{4}w\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds.

Furthermore, writing

w(x,t)=w_{0}(x)+w(x,t)-w_{0}(x)=w_{0}(x)+\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{s}w(x,s)\ ds,

then Sobolev embedding yields

(67)

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\|w\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\left\|w_{0}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}+t\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\|\partial_{x}^{3}w_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}.

Therefore combining (
5
), (
67
), and Young’s inequality we find that

(68)

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}_{T}

\displaystyle\leq\mathsf{E}_{0}+\mathsf{E}^{3/2}_{0}+Ct\left(\mathsf{E}_{T}^{3/2}+\mathsf{E}_{T}\right)+\left\|w_{0}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\|\partial_{x}^{4}w\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

for some constant
C
. Now taking
w_{0}
with
\left\|w_{0}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}=\frac{1}{2}
, from the definition of
E_{T}
and (
68
) it holds that

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}_{T}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(\mathsf{E}_{0}+\mathsf{E}^{3/2}_{0}\right)+2Ct\left(\mathsf{E}_{T}^{3/2}+\mathsf{E}_{T}\right),

which is valid for
t\in(0,t_{1}),t_{1}=\displaystyle\min\{1,T\}
. (Sharper inequalities can be obtained, if necessary, from smaller choices of
\left\|w_{0}\right\|_{L^{\infty}})
.

Noticing that
\mathsf{E}^{q}_{T}\leq\mathsf{E}^{2}_{T}+1
for
q=1
and
q=\frac{3}{2}
and the fact that
t\in(0,t_{1}),t_{1}=\displaystyle\min\{1,T\}
(in particular
0<t<1
), we find that the polynomial estimate

(69)

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}_{T}

\displaystyle\leq\mathsf{N}_{0}+4Ct\mathsf{E}_{T}^{2},

holds. Here we have used the notation
\mathsf{N}_{0}=2\left(\mathsf{E}_{0}+\mathsf{E}^{3/2}_{0}+2C\right)
. Similar polynomial estimates as (
69
) have been derived in
[
8
]
. Let us define the polynomial
\mathcal{P}_{t}(y)=4Cty^{2}-y+\mathsf{N_{0}}
, so that
\mathcal{P}_{t}(\mathsf{E_{T}})\geq 0
. The roots of
\mathcal{P}_{t}
are computed as

y_{\pm}=\frac{1\pm\sqrt{1-16Ct\mathsf{N}_{0}}}{8Ct}.

P_{t}(y)
for

Taking

0<y_{-}=4\mathsf{N_{0}}\left(1-\sqrt{1/2}\right)<y_{+}=4\mathsf{N_{0}}\left(1+\sqrt{1/2}\right)

0<y_{-}<4\mathsf{N}_{0}<y_{+}.

Furthermore, the application

is continuous for

. This, together with the fact that

\mathsf{E}_{0}<\mathsf{N}_{0}
and
\mathcal{P}_{t}(\mathsf{N}_{0})>0

\forall t\in(0,t^{\star}),\quad\mathsf{E}_{T}<4\mathsf{N}_{0},

where

.

Similarly as before, in order to perform the a priori estimates rigorously and construct the local existence of solutions we follow a regularization procedure. More precisely, the regularized version of (

(71)

w^{\epsilon}_{tt}+\mathscr{L}w^{\epsilon}=\left(\mathcal{J}^{\epsilon}\left((1+\mathcal{J}^{\epsilon}w^{\epsilon})\mathcal{J}^{\epsilon}w^{\epsilon}_{x}\right)+\left[\mathscr{L},\mathscr{N}w^{\epsilon}\right]w^{\epsilon}\right)_{x}-2\mathcal{J}^{\epsilon}\left((1+\mathcal{J}^{\epsilon}w^{\epsilon})\mathcal{J}^{\epsilon}w^{\epsilon}_{t}\right)_{x}.

Repeating the same a priori estimates for the regularized equation (

) we can deduce the analogue of (

) namely

(72)

\forall t\in(0,t^{\star}),\quad\mathsf{E}_{T}<4\mathsf{N}_{0},

where

. Notice that the time of existence of the solution depends a priori on the regularization parameter
\epsilon
. Nevertheless, the following argument shows that (

) holds independently of the parameter
\epsilon
. Indeed, take

the first time such that

\mathsf{E}_{T^{\epsilon}}=8\mathsf{N}_{0}.

We observe that the precise choice of the quantity

is the minimum) that

of the regularized problem, and using that by construction

6.
Well-posedness in Sobolev spaces for unidirectional non-local wave model

The main goal of this section is to prove the local well-posedness of equation (
10
) in
L^{2}
based Sobolev spaces. Note first that, using (
13
), we have

-\mathscr{L}=\mathscr{Q}-1,

and (
10
) can be rewritten as (we set
\varepsilon=1
for simplicity)

(73)

h_{t}=-\frac{1}{2}\left(3hh_{x}+[\mathscr{Q},\mathscr{Q}h_{x}]h-\mathscr{Q}h_{x}-h_{x}\right).

For this equation, we show the following local well-posedness result.

Theorem 4
.

Let
s>\frac{3}{2}
and
h_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
with mean zero. Then there exist
T_{\max}>0
and a unique local solution
h\in C([0,T_{\max}),H^{s}(\mathbb{R}))
of (
73
).

Proof.

The proof follows from the combination of appropriate a priori energy estimates and the use of a suitable approximation procedure using mollifiers, cf.
[
23
]
. Thus, we first focus in deriving a priori energy estimates and later comment briefly on the approximation procedure to construct the solution. We begin by reminding (cf. Section
3
) that the mean property is conserved in time by the solutions

\int_{\mathbb{R}}h(x,t)\ dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}h_{0}(x)\ dx,

as well as the
L^{2}
norm

\int_{\mathbb{R}}h^{2}(x,t)\ dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}h^{2}_{0}(x)\ dx.

Furthermore, applying
\Lambda^{s}
to (
73
), multiplying by
\Lambda^{s}h
and integrating we obtain

(74)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\|h\right\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{3}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Lambda^{s}(hh_{x})\Lambda^{s}h\ dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Lambda^{s}\left([\mathscr{Q},\mathscr{Q}_{x}h]h\right)\Lambda^{s}h\ dx

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Lambda^{s}\mathscr{Q}h_{x}\Lambda^{s}h\ dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Lambda^{s}h_{x}\Lambda^{s}h\ dx.

Using the self-adjointness of the operator
\mathscr{Q}
, a straightforward computation shows that the last two terms on the right hand-side of (
74
) are zero. The first term can be estimated by means of the classical Kato-Ponce commutator as follows. Using integration by parts, we rewrite

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Lambda^{s}(hh_{x})\Lambda^{s}h\ dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}[\Lambda^{s},h]h_{x}\Lambda^{s}h\ dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}}h\Lambda^{s}h_{x}\Lambda^{s}h\ dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}[\Lambda^{s},h]h_{x}\Lambda^{s}h\ dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}h_{x}|\Lambda^{s}h|^{2}\ dx.

Now, invoking the first equation in Lemma
1.1
with
p=2,p_{1}=\infty,p_{2}=2
, we have that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Lambda^{s}(hh_{x})\Lambda^{s}h\ dx\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|[\Lambda^{s},h]h_{x}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\Lambda^{s}h\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|h_{x}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|\Lambda^{s}h\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

(75)

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|h_{x}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|\Lambda^{s}h\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

The second term on the right hand side in (
74
) can be bounded as follows.
Expanding the commutator and using the self-adjointness of
\mathscr{Q}
yield

(76)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Lambda^{s}\left([\mathscr{Q},\mathscr{Q}_{x}h]h\right)\Lambda^{s}h\ dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Lambda^{s}(\mathscr{Q}h_{x}h)\Lambda^{s}\mathscr{Q}h\ dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Lambda^{s}(\mathscr{Q}h_{x}\mathscr{Q}h)\Lambda^{s}h\ dx.

Then applying the second estimate in Lemma
1.1
with
p=2,p_{1},p_{3}=\infty,p_{2},p_{4}=2
, the Sobolev embedding
H^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}(\mathbb{R})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
for
\epsilon>0
, and the fact that
\mathscr{Q}
is continuous between
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
and
H^{s+2}(\mathbb{R})
for any
s\in\mathbb{R}
we find that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Lambda^{s}(\mathscr{Q}h_{x}h)\Lambda^{s}\mathscr{Q}h\ dx\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\Lambda^{s}(\mathscr{Q}h_{x}h)\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\Lambda^{s}\mathscr{Q}h\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\left\|\Lambda^{s}\mathscr{Q}h_{x}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|h\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|\mathscr{Q}h_{x}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|\Lambda^{s}h\right\|_{L^{2}}\right)\left\|\Lambda^{s}\mathscr{Q}h\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\left\|h\right\|_{H^{s-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}}\left\|h\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|h\right\|_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}}\left\|h\right\|_{H^{s}}\right)\left\|h\right\|_{H^{s-2}}.

Similarly, one can show that

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Lambda^{s}(\mathscr{Q}h_{x}\mathscr{Q}h)\Lambda^{s}h\ dx\right|\lesssim\left(\left\|h\right\|_{H^{s-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}}\left\|h\right\|_{H^{-2}}+\left\|h\right\|_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}}\left\|h\right\|_{H^{s-2}}\right)\left\|h\right\|_{H^{s}}.

Thus,

(77)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Lambda^{s}\left([\mathscr{Q},\mathscr{Q}_{x}h]h\right)\Lambda^{s}h\ dx\right|\leq C\left\|h_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left(\left\|h\right\|_{H^{s-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}}+\left\|h\right\|_{H^{s-2}}\right)\left\|h\right\|_{H^{s}}

where we have used that
\left\|h\right\|_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}}+\left\|h\right\|_{H^{-2}}\leq C\left\|h\right\|_{L^{2}}=C\left\|h_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}
, for some constant
C
.

Therefore, combining estimates (
75
), (
77
) leads to

(78)

\frac{d}{dt}\left\|h\right\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\leq C\left\|h_{x}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|h\right\|_{H^{s}}^{2}+C\left\|h_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left(\left\|h\right\|_{H^{s-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}}+\left\|h\right\|_{H^{s-2}}\right)\left\|h\right\|_{H^{s}}

which in particular gives the following inequality

(79)

\frac{d}{dt}\left\|h\right\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\leq C\left(\left\|h_{x}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}+1\right)\left\|h\right\|_{H^{s}}^{2}.

Using the Sobolev embedding
H^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}(\mathbb{R})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
,
\epsilon>0
, we observe that

(80)

\frac{d}{dt}\left\|h\right\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\leq C\left\|h\right\|_{H^{s}}^{3},

for some constant
C
independent of
t
.
Defining
\mathcal{E}(t)=\left\|h\right\|_{H^{s}}^{2}
, estimate (
80
) leads to the following differential equation

(81)

\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(t)\leq C\mathcal{E}^{3/2}(t),

with
C=C_{s}>0
which ensures a uniform time of existence
T_{\max}>0
such that

\mathcal{E}(t)\leq 4\mathcal{E}(0),0<t<T_{\max}.

Once this uniform time of existence has been obtained, the local existence result follows classical regularization procedure (cf.
[
23
]
) as follows. First, we consider a symmetric and positive mollifier
\mathcal{J}\in C^{\infty}_{c}
,
\mathcal{J}(x)=J(|x|)
such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{J}=1
. For
\epsilon>0
we define
\mathcal{J}^{\epsilon}=\frac{1}{\epsilon}\mathcal{J}(\frac{x}{\epsilon})
and consider the regularized problem

(82)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}h^{\epsilon}=-\frac{1}{2}\big(\mathcal{J}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{J}^{\epsilon}h\partial_{x}\mathcal{J}^{\epsilon}h)+[\mathscr{Q},\mathscr{Q}h^{\epsilon}_{x}]h^{\epsilon}-\mathscr{Q}h^{\epsilon}_{x}-\mathcal{J}^{\epsilon}\mathcal{J}^{\epsilon}h^{\epsilon}_{x}\big).

In (
82
), the conserved quantities are also preserved by the flow and the previous bounds (
75
)-(
77
) and thus (
78
) hold. Therefore, we may find a time of existence
T^{\star}>0
for the sequence of regularized problems.
Using compactness arguments and passing to the limit conclude the proof of existence.
The continuity in time for the solution (instead of merely weak continuity) is obtained by classical arguments (cf.
[
23
]
): On the one hand, the differential equation (
81
) yields the strong right continuity at
t=0
. On the other hand, it is easy to check that changing variables
\tilde{t}=-t
, we can repeat once again the same bounds and provide the strong left continuity at
t=0
. Combining both arguments shows the continuity in time of the solution.

As for uniqueness, let
h^{1},h^{2}\in C([0,T_{\max}),H^{s}(\mathbb{R}))
be two solutions corresponding to the same initial condition and denote
\widehat{h}=h^{2}-h^{1}
. Then
\widehat{h}
satisfies

(83)

\partial_{t}\widehat{h}=-\frac{1}{2}\left(3h^{2}h^{2}_{x}-3h^{1}h^{1}_{x}+[\mathscr{Q},\mathscr{Q}h^{2}_{x}]h^{2}-[\mathscr{Q},\mathscr{Q}h^{1}_{x}]h^{1}-\mathscr{Q}\widehat{h}_{x}-\widehat{h}_{x}\right).

Then, multiplying (
83
) by
\widehat{h}
and integrating,
we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\|\widehat{h}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=-\frac{3}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\widehat{h}h^{2}_{x}+h^{1}\widehat{h}_{x}\right)\widehat{h}\ dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bigg[[\mathscr{Q},\mathscr{Q}h^{2}_{x}]h^{2}-[\mathscr{Q},\mathscr{Q}h^{1}_{x}]h^{1}\bigg]\widehat{h}\ dx.

Using (
27
b), it is not hard to check that each commutator can be rewritten as a Burgers-type nonlinear-term and therefore

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bigg[[\mathscr{Q},\mathscr{Q}h^{2}_{x}]h^{2}-[\mathscr{Q},\mathscr{Q}h^{1}_{x}]h^{1}\bigg]\widehat{h}\ dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left\|h^{1}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}+\left\|h^{2}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)\left\|\widehat{h}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C,

for some constant
C
.
Similarly, integrating by parts,

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\widehat{h}h^{2}_{x}+h^{1}\widehat{h}_{x}\right)\widehat{h}\ dx\right|\leq C\left(\left\|\partial_{x}h^{1}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}+\left\|\partial_{x}h^{2}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)\left\|\widehat{h}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Defining
\beta(t)=\left\|h^{1}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}+\left\|h^{2}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}+\left\|\partial_{x}h^{1}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}+\left\|\partial_{x}h^{2}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}
, then

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\|\widehat{h}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\beta(t)\left\|\widehat{h}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Uniqueness holds applying Grönwall inequality.
∎

Remark 1
.

One can readily check that a direct consequence of the derived estimates in the proof of Theorem
4
provides the following blow-up criterion when combined with the logarithmic Sobolev inequality (cf. Lemma
1.2
) which we state as a theorem for the sake of clarity.

Theorem 5

(Blow-up criteria)
.

Let
s>\frac{3}{2}
,

h_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
with zero mean and let
T_{\max}>0
be the lifespan associated to the solution
h
to (
73
) with
h(x,0)=h_{0}(x)
. Then
h
blows-up in finite time
T_{\max}
if and only if

(84)

\int_{0}^{T_{\max}}\left\|h_{x}(\tau)\right\|_{\text{BMO}}\ d\tau=\infty.

Proof.

The result follows as a direct consequence of estimate (
79
). Using the logarithmic inequality in Lemma
1.2
we find that

(85)

\frac{d}{dt}\left\|h\right\|_{H^{s}}\leq C\left(1+\left\|h_{x}\right\|_{\text{BMO}}\big[1+\displaystyle\log(1+\left\|h\right\|_{H^{s}})\big]\right)\left\|h\right\|_{H^{s}},

for some constant
C
. From (
85
), it holds that

1+\left\|h\right\|_{H^{s}}\leq\bigg[(1+\left\|h_{0}\right\|_{H^{s}})\mbox{exp}\left(1+CT_{\max}\right)\bigg]^{\mbox{exp}\left(C\int_{0}^{T_{\max}}(\left\|h_{x}(\tau)\right\|_{\text{BMO}}\ d\tau\right)}.

Therefore, if there exists
L>0
such that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T_{\max}}\left\|h_{x}(\tau)\right\|_{\text{BMO}}\ d\tau<L,

then

1+\left\|h\right\|_{H^{s}}\leq\bigg[(1+\left\|h_{0}\right\|_{H^{s}})\mbox{exp}\left(1+CT_{\max}\right)\bigg]^{\mbox{exp}\left(CL\right)}.

On the contrary, if
\int_{0}^{T_{\max}}\left\|h_{x}(t)\right\|_{\text{BMO}}\ dt=\infty
, by means of the embedding
H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R})\subset\text{BMO}(\mathbb{R})
, we deduce that the solution
h(x,t)
will blow up in finite time and (
84
) follows.
∎

7.
A wave breaking result for the unidirectional non-local wave model

In this section, we investigate the possible wave breaking phenomena for equation (
10
), that is, the formation of an infinite slope in the solution in the
x
-direction. As we did for the well-posedness result in Section
6
, it is more convenient to work with the alternative formulation (
73
).
The following lemma shows that for the maximal time of existence
T_{\max}>0
, we have that the solutions remains bounded. More precisely,

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
s>{7/2}
,
h_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
, and let
T_{max}>0
be the maximal time of existence of the unique solution
h

of (
73
) given by Theorem
4
. Then

(86)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T_{\max})}\left\|h(t)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}<\infty.

Proof.

To establish the
L^{\infty}
bound, we follow a pointwise method (cf.
[
6
,
7
]
). Due to Theorem
4
, we have that
h\in C([0,T_{\max}),H^{s})\cap C^{1}([0,T_{\max}),H^{s-1})
, hence by the Sobolev embedding theorem, if
s>7/2
we have that
h\in C^{1}([0,T_{\max})\times\mathbb{R})
. In particular,

\displaystyle m(t)=\displaystyle\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}}h(x,t)=h(\underline{x}_{t},t),\quad M(t)=\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}h(x,t)=h(\overline{x}_{t},t),\mbox{ for }t>0,

(for some
\underline{x}_{t},\overline{x}_{t}
)
are Lipschitz functions. Following
[
7
]
, one can readily check that
M(t)
satisfies

\displaystyle\left|M(t)-M(s)\right|

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}h(\overline{x}_{t},t)-h(\overline{x}_{s},s)\text{ if }M(t)>M(s)\\
h(\overline{x}_{s},s)-h(\overline{x}_{t},t)\text{ if }M(s)>M(t)\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}h(\overline{x}_{t},t)-h(\overline{x}_{t},s)\text{ if }M(t)>M(s)\\
h(\overline{x}_{s},s)-h(\overline{x}_{s},t)\text{ if }M(s)>M(t)\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}|\partial_{t}h(\overline{x}_{t},z)||t-s|\text{ if }M(t)>M(s)\\
|\partial_{t}h(\overline{x}_{s},z)||t-s|\text{ if }M(s)>M(t)\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle\leq\max_{y,z}|\partial_{t}h(y,z)||t-s|.

Similarly

\left|m(t)-m(s)\right|\leq\max_{y,z}|\partial_{t}h(y,z)||t-s|.

From Rademacher’s theorem it holds that
M(t)
and
m(t)
are differentiable in
t
almost everywhere. Furthermore

\displaystyle M^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\frac{h(\overline{x}_{t+\delta},t+\delta)-h(\overline{x}_{t},t)}{\delta}=\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\frac{h(\overline{x}_{t+\delta},t+\delta)-h(\overline{x}_{t},t)\pm h(\overline{x}_{t+\delta},t)}{\delta}

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\frac{h(\overline{x}_{t+\delta},t+\delta)-h(\overline{x}_{t+\delta},t)}{\delta}\leq\partial_{t}h(\overline{x}_{t},t).

In a similar fashion, we obtain that

\displaystyle M^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\frac{h(\overline{x}_{t+\delta},t+\delta)-h(\overline{x}_{t},t)}{\delta}=\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\frac{h(\overline{x}_{t+\delta},t+\delta)-h(\overline{x}_{t},t)\pm h(\overline{x}_{t},t+\delta)}{\delta}

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\frac{h(\overline{x}_{t},t+\delta)-h(\overline{x}_{t},t)}{\delta}\geq\partial_{t}h(\overline{x}_{t},t).

As a consequence

(87)

M^{\prime}(t)=\partial_{t}h(\overline{x}_{t},t)\text{ a.e.}

Similarly

m^{\prime}(t)=\partial_{t}h(\underline{x}_{t},t)\text{ a.e.}

Therefore, from (
87
), evaluating (
73
) at
x=\overline{x}_{t}
and noticing that
h_{x}(\overline{x}_{t},t)=0
, we have

(88)

\displaystyle M^{\prime}(t)=-\frac{1}{2}\left([\mathscr{Q},\mathscr{Q}h_{x}(\overline{x}_{t})]h(\overline{x}_{t})-\mathscr{Q}h_{x}(\overline{x}_{t})\right).

Moreover, from (
12
), the estimates

\left\|G\right\|_{L^{\infty}}=\frac{1}{2},\quad\left\|\partial_{x}G\right\|_{L^{\infty}}=\frac{1}{2},

and Young’s inequality, it holds that

(89)

\displaystyle\mathscr{Q}h(x_{t})

\displaystyle\leq\left\|G\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\left\|h\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=\frac{1}{2}\left\|h\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})},

(90)

\displaystyle[\mathscr{Q},\mathscr{Q}h_{x}(x_{t})]h(x_{t})

\displaystyle\leq\left\|G\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\left\|\mathscr{Q}h_{x}h\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\left\|\mathscr{Q}h_{x}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\left\|\mathscr{Q}h\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\|h\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

From (

), the estimates (

), (

) and the preservation of the

norm by the solutions of (

M^{\prime}(t)\leq\frac{1}{4}\left(1+\left\|h_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right),

and therefore

for

In a similar way, we have

for

. Thus combining (

Next, let us state the wave breaking result.

Theorem 6
.

Let
s>{\frac{9}{2}},h_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
, and let
h
be the solution of the ivp of (
73
) with initial condition
h_{0}
. Assume that

(94)

\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}}h_{0,x}(x)\leq-H_{0},

for some positive constant
H_{0}
, which depends on
\left\|h_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})},\|h_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}
and specified below. Then there exists
T_{b}<\infty
such that

(95)

\displaystyle\lim\inf_{t\to T_{b}}\left(\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}}h_{x}(x,t)\right)=-\infty.

Proof.

Similarly as in the proof of Lemma
7.1
, we follow a pointwise method to derive an ODE which breaks down in finite time. Again, due to Theorem
4
, we have that
h\in C([0,T_{\max}),H^{s})\cap C^{1}([0,T_{\max}),H^{s-1})
, hence by the Sobolev embedding theorem, if
s>9/2
then
h_{x}\in C^{1}([0,T_{\max})\times\mathbb{R})
.
We begin by setting

m(t)=\displaystyle\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}}h_{x}(x,t)=h_{x}(\underline{x}_{t},t),\quad\mbox{for }t\in[0,T_{\max}),

for some
\underline{x}_{t}
.
Arguing as in Lemma
7.1
, one can check that
m(t)
is Lipschitz and hence via Radamacher’s Theorem it is inferred that

m^{\prime}(t)=\partial_{t}h_{x}(\underline{x}_{t},t),\ \mbox{a.e}.

Differentiating (
73
) with respect to
x
and using (
46
) yield

(96)

h_{xt}=-\frac{1}{2}\left(3h_{x}^{2}+3hh_{xx}+\partial_{x}[\mathscr{Q},\mathscr{Q}h_{x}]h-\mathscr{Q}h+h-h_{xx}\right).

Evaluating (
96
) at
x=\underline{x}_{t}
and noticing that
h_{xx}(\underline{x}_{t},t)=0
we have

(97)

m^{\prime}(t)=-\frac{3}{2}m(t)^{2}+\mathcal{K}(t),

with

\mathcal{K}(t)=\frac{1}{2}\big(\partial_{x}[\mathscr{Q},\mathscr{Q}h_{x}]h(\underline{x}_{t},t)-\mathscr{Q}h(\underline{x}_{t},t)+h(\underline{x}_{t},t)\big).

We now estimate the quantity
\mathcal{K}(t)
.
First notice, from (
12
), that

(98)

\left\|\mathscr{Q}h+h\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\left\|G\star h\right\|_{L^{\infty}}+\left\|h\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\left\|G\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|h\right\|_{L^{2}}=\frac{1}{2}\left\|h\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|h\right\|_{L^{\infty}}.

On the other hand, from the Sobolev embedding
H^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}(\mathbb{R})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
for
\epsilon>0
and the fact that
\mathscr{Q}
is continuous between
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
and
H^{s+2}(\mathbb{R})
for any
s\in\mathbb{R}
, then the commutator term is bounded by

(99)

\partial_{x}[\mathscr{Q},\mathscr{Q}h_{x}]h(\underline{x}_{t},t)\leq\left\|[\mathscr{Q},\mathscr{Q}h_{x}]h\right\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\left\|\mathscr{Q}h_{x}h\right\|_{H^{-\frac{3}{2}+\epsilon}(\mathbb{R})}+\left\|(\mathscr{Q}h)^{2}\right\|_{H^{\frac{3}{2}+\epsilon}}.

The first term on the right hand-side of (
99
) is bounded by

(100)

\left\|\mathscr{Q}h_{x}h\right\|_{H^{-\frac{3}{2}+\epsilon}}\leq C\left\|h\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2},

and the second term can be estimated from the Sobolev algebra property as

(101)

\left\|(\mathscr{Q}h)^{2}\right\|_{H^{\frac{3}{2}+\epsilon}}\leq C\left\|\mathscr{Q}h\right\|^{2}_{H^{\frac{3}{2}+\epsilon}}\leq C\left\|h\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})},

for some constant
C
.
The bounds (
98
)-(
101
), the
L^{\infty}
estimate (
93
), and the time preservation of the
L^{2}
norm by
h
lead to

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(t)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left\|h\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\left\|h\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\left\|h\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\right)

(102)

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\left\|h_{0}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}+\left(1+\left\|h_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)(t+1)\big).

Using (
102
), then (
97
) implies

(103)

m^{\prime}(t)\leq-\frac{3}{2}m(t)^{2}+A+Bt,

where
A
and
B
are the positive constants, depending on
\left\|h_{0}\right\|_{L^{\infty}},\left\|h_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}
, determined in (
102
).

In order to show the wave breaking result, let us assume that the initial data is such that (cf.
[
28
]
)

(104)

m(0)\leq-C,

for some positive constant
C
such that
C^{2}>4A
.
Then
2A-m(0)^{2}/2<0
and

m^{\prime}(t)\bigg|_{t=0}\leq-m(0)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}m(0)^{2}+2A\leq-m(0)^{2}<0.

Therefore there exists a sufficiently small
0<\delta
such that
m^{\prime}(t)<0
, for
0\leq t<\delta
.
This implies
m(t)<m(0)\leq-C
and similarly
2A-m(t)^{2}/2<0
for
0\leq t<\delta
.
Thus, if

0<t\leq\min\{\delta,A/B\}

from (
103
) it holds that

m^{\prime}(t)\leq-m(t)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}m(t)^{2}+2A\leq-m(t)^{2}.

This leads to

(105)

m(t)\leq\frac{m(0)}{m(0)t+1}.

Taking the initial datum such that

\frac{1}{-m(0)}\leq\frac{A}{B},

note that (

), (

H_{0}
for which
m(0)
satisfies (

T_{b}
where (

) holds.
∎

X_{11}
be in the domain of attraction of the normal distribution with the distribution of
X_{11}
being non-degenerate and symmetric around
0
. Define
\omega_{n}=\big[\delta_{n,0}]^{-1/6}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\tau_{n,\mathcal{A}^{re}}\rightarrow 0\;\;\;\text{as}\;\;n\rightarrow\infty,

provided
\log p=o(\omega_{n}^{2})
. Moreover there exists a positive constant
c\leq 1/8
such that whenever
\log p\leq\epsilon\omega_{n}^{2}
with
0<\epsilon<c

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\tau_{n,\mathcal{A}^{re}}<\epsilon^{5/3}.

The above result is remarkable in the sense that sub-exponential growth of the dimension is possible in the UCLT of self-normalized sums even when no moment higher than two exists. Note that
X_{11}
is in the domain of attraction of normal distribution if and only if
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\big[t^{2}\mathbf{P}\big(|X_{11}|>t\big)\big]/\big[\mathbf{E}X_{11}^{2}I\big(|X_{11}|\leq t\big)\big]

=0
(cf. Theorem 4 at page 323 of
[
11
]
). Since due to Lemma 1.3 of
[
3
]
,
\kappa^{2}_{n,0}/n=\mathbf{E}\big\{X_{11}^{2}I(|X_{11}|\leq\kappa_{n,0})\big\}

\delta_{n,0}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
due to Lemma 1 of Cörgő et al. (2003). However,
\mathbf{E}|X_{11}|^{2+\delta}
may not exist for any
\delta>0
and hence we can not use Theorem
1
. In particular, if we only know that
\mathbf{E}X_{11}^{2}[\log(1+|X_{11}|)]^{\alpha}<\infty
for some
\alpha>0
, then we can not apply Theorem
1
. However here
\delta_{n,0}\leq\big(\log(1+\sqrt{n})\big)^{-\alpha}
and hence using theorem
2
, UCLT over
{\mathcal{A}}^{re}
holds if
\log p=o\big((\log n)^{\alpha/3}\big)
. Therefore,
\log p
can still grow sub-exponentially with
n
even without existence of absolute polynomial moment
\geq 2
. The growth in this case depends on how fast
\delta_{n,0}
goes to
0
or in other words it depends on how fat the tail of the distribution of

is.

Remark 2.2
.

Our results of this section should be compared with the UCLT results available in the literature in case of the normalized sums. The rate
\log p=o\big(n^{1/5}\big)
in the UCLT

of the normalized sum

is established in

2.2.
Rate of growth of
p
under fourth moment

In this sub-section we try to improve the results obtained in the previous sub-section on the rate of growth of
p
under higher moment conditions. In particular, we show that

can grow like

for a non trivial set of

can be improved further from

as a power of

even when

and

is stochastically bounded. The rate of growth of

in the UCLT of

over
{\mathcal{A}}^{re}
essentially stabilizes at

whenever fourth or higher absolute moments of

exists. We are now ready to state our first theorem of this sub-section.

Theorem 3
.

Let
\max_{i=1,\dots,n}\mathbf{E}|X_{i1}|^{4}=O(1)
,
\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sigma_{n}^{2}>0
and

. Then we have

\tau_{n,\mathcal{A}^{re}}\rightarrow 0\;\;\;\text{as}\;\;n\rightarrow\infty,

. Moreover, there exists a positive constant
c\leq 1/8
such that whenever
\log p\leq\epsilon n^{1/2}
with
0<\epsilon<c
,

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\tau_{n,\mathcal{A}^{re}}<\epsilon.

The next proposition shows that for a UCLT
even over the smaller class of sets
{\mathcal{A}}^{max}
, the
o\big(n^{1/2}\big)
upper
bound on
\log p
can not be significantly improved upon even for the smaller class
\mathcal{A}^{max}
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
X_{ij}
’s be iid Rademacher variables, i.e.
X_{ij}=1
or
-1
each with probability

and be independent across
i\in\{1\dots,n\}
and

. If
\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\big[(n\log n)^{-1/2}\log p\big]>0
, then

(2.1)

\displaystyle\tau_{n,\mathcal{A}^{max}}\nrightarrow 0\;\;\text{as}\;\;n\rightarrow\infty.

3.
An application to Student’s t statistic

In this section we apply the results obtained in the previous section for finding UCLT for high dimensional component-wise Student’s t statistic. Recall that based on the mean zero random vectors
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
, the corresponding student’s t statistic is
W_{n}=(W_{n1},\dots,W_{n,p})^{\prime}
, where

W_{nj}=\frac{\sqrt{n}\bar{X}_{nj}}{\sqrt{(n-1)^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(X_{ij}-\bar{X}_{nj})^{2}}},

where
\bar{X}_{nj}=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{ij}
. The statistic
W_{n}
can be used for drawing high dimensional inference based on the sample
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
. In fact in most of the practical applications, component-wise studentization is more natural to perform than component-wise standardization since component-wise population variances are not in general available. In statistical terms,
W_{n}
is always a statistic irrespective of whether underlying variance structure is known or unknown. This is not true for the standardized sum
U_{n}
. To that end, we are interested in high dimensional UCLT for
W_{n}
by investigating the quantity given by

(3.1)

\displaystyle\gamma_{n,{\mathcal{A}}}\equiv\sup_{A\in{\mathcal{A}}}\Big|P(W_{n}\in A)-P(Z\in A)\Big|

for some class
{\mathcal{A}}
of convex subsets of
\mathcal{R}^{p}
.
We show that results similar to Section
2
continue to hold for
\gamma_{n,{\mathcal{A}}}
whenever
{\mathcal{A}}={\mathcal{A}}^{re}
or
{\mathcal{A}}^{max}
or
{\mathcal{A}}^{dist}
. Recall the relation (
1.3
) between
T_{nj}
and
W_{nj}
that is crucial in establishing a high dimensional UCLT for
W_{n}
from that for
T_{n}
. We are now ready to state the high dimensional UCLT result for
W_{n}
.

Theorem 4
.

Statements of the Theorems
1
,
2
and
3
are true if
\tau_{n,\mathcal{A}^{re}}
is replaced by
\gamma_{n,\mathcal{A}^{re}}
.

Theorem
4
shows that all the high dimensional UCLT results that we developed for self-normalized sums, also hold for component-wise Student’s t-statistic. Therefore the same rate of
\log p
can be achieved in the high dimensional UCLT over
{\mathcal{A}}^{re}
under much weaker moment conditions if we use studentzation in place of standardization.
[
15
]
obtained the rate
\log p=o\big(n^{1/3}(\log n)^{-2/3}\big)
in
\rho_{n,{\mathcal{A}}^{re}}\rightarrow 0
under the assumption of the random vectors
X_{i}
’s having log-concave densities (i.e. when
X_{ij}
are sub-exponential for all
(i,j)
). Whereas for the rate
\log p=o\big(n^{1/3}\big)
in
\tau_{n,{\mathcal{A}}^{re}}\rightarrow 0
, we only need the existence of third absolute moments of
X_{ij}
for all
(i,j)
. Again to achieve
\log p=o\big(n^{1/2}\big)
in
\gamma_{n,{\mathcal{A}}^{re}}\rightarrow 0
we need
\mathbf{E}|X_{ij}|^{4}<\infty
and
\mathbf{E}X_{ij}^{3}=0
, for all
(i,j)
. Whereas
X_{ij}
’s are assumed to be sub-Gaussian to achieve

in

, as established in
[

]
.
Additionally under the more specialized iid structure of

’s, sub-exponential growth rate of

can be achieved in (

) with

without any moment conditions, only the assumption of

being in the domain of attraction of normal distribution is sufficient.

4.

In this section we establish near classical Berry-Esseen rate for both self-nomalized and studentized random vectors in high dimension. Let us consider following two cases separately for describing the results.

Case I:
X_{ij}
’s are all iid and

is in the domain of attraction of the normal distribution.

’s are iid,

where the constant

. Now let us consider Case II. Under this setup,

Remark 4.2
.

Our proofs of the theorems mainly depend on regrouping the endpoints of the

[
18
]
). However, the sub-Gaussian tail bound of self-normalized sums (required in the alternative strategy) generally requires the assumption of

being iid ( cf. Theorem 2.5 of

) or the assumption of the components of

’s being symmetric about

are assumed to be independent only (cf. Theorem 2.1 of

]
). Neither of these assumptions is required in our proof strategy and the theorems are established even when

’s are independent but not necessarily identically distributed. The alternative proof technique for Theorem

\displaystyle+n(1+x^{2})^{3}\big[\kappa_{n,0}(Z)\big]^{-3}\mathbf{E}\big\{|Z|^{3}I\big(|Z|\leq\kappa_{n,0}(Z)\big)\big\}

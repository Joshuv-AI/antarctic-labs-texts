Theory of topological exciton insulators and condensates in flat Chern bands
Hong-Yi Xie, Pouyan Ghaemi, Matteo Mitrano, Bruno Uchoa

Abstract
Excitons are the neutral quasiparticles that form when Coulomb interactions create bound states between electrons and holes. Due to their bosonic nature, excitons are expected to condense and exhibit superfluidity at sufficiently low temperatures. In interacting Chern insulators, excitons may inherit the nontrivial topology and quantum geometry from the underlying electron wavefunctions. We theoretically investigate the excitonic bound states and superfluidity in flat-band insulators pumped with light. We find that the exciton wavefunctions exhibit vortex structures in momentum space, with the total vorticity being equal to the difference of Chern numbers between the conduction and valence bands. Moreover, both the exciton binding energy and the exciton superfluid density are proportional to the Brillouin-zone average of the quantum metric and the Coulomb potential energy per unit cell. Spontaneous emission of circularly polarized light from radiative decay is a detectable signature of the vorticity of excitons. We propose that the exciton vorticity can also be experimentally measured by the nonlinear anomalous Hall effect, whereas the exciton superfluidity can be detected by voltage-drop quantization through a combination of the quantum geometry and the Aharonov-Casher effect. Topological excitons and their superfluid phase could be realized in flat bands of twisted Van der Waals heterostructures.



I
Introduction

Topological phases that do not depend on protected symmetries can be traced back to the concept of quantum geometry first introduced by Michael Berry, and then developed to explain the quantization of the Hall conductivity and of various quantum Hall effects (QHE)
Thouless1982
;
Haldane1988
;
Mele2005
;
Berry1984
;
Provost1980
;
Anandan1990
. This framework explains the way properties of solids can be connected to the continuous evolution in momentum of the electronic wavefunctions. It can be formalized through the idea of parallel transport of quantum states, in which periodic wavefunctions are adiabatically cycled through a variable in parameter space. In going through the adiabatic cycle, the wavefunctions pick up phases that reflect on the quantum geometry of the bands
Vanderbilt
. The non-trivial topology of Bloch wave functions is universally captured by a quantized Chern number, and the systems that display it in two dimensions (2D) are commonly termed Chern insulators
Haldane1988
;
Chang2013
.

No continuous symmetry is spontaneously broken in these insulators and hence no gapless collective excitation is expected in their bulk. However, electrons can be excited from the valence band into the conduction band through the absorption of photons. Coulomb interactions form midgap excitons, bound states comprised of excited electrons in the conduction band and holes left in the valence band, which dominate the optical response of the insulators
Haug1993
. Excitonic effects are expected to be strong in 2D because of reduced screening and enhanced electron-hole overlaps as a consequence of geometric confinement
Wang2018
.

Electron-hole (e-h) excitations in 2D Chern insulators can inherit a Berry phase from the underlying valence (v) and conduction (c) bands shown in Fig.
1
(a). In the reciprocal space with states
|i,{\bf k}\rangle
, with
i\in\{1,2\}
a generic two-orbit index, the valence- and conduction-band states can be written in terms of the orbital basis as
|a,{\bf k}\rangle=\sum_{i}U_{i,a,{\bf k}}|i,{\bf k}\rangle
, where
a\in\{\mathrm{v,c}\}
and
U_{a,i,{\bf k}}
is the periodic part of the Bloch wave function. In the electron basis, where creating a hole in the valence band with momentum
-{\bf k}
is equivalent to the annihilation of an electron in the same band with momentum
{\bf k}
, the e-h pair state is defined as
|\mathrm{P},{\bf k}\rangle=|\mathrm{c},{\bf k}\rangle|\mathrm{v},{\bf k}\rangle^{*}
, where
“
*
” denotes complex conjugation. The Berry connection of the pair state reads
\mathbf{A}_{\mathrm{P},{\bf k}}\equiv-i\langle\mathrm{P},{\bf k}|\partial_{\bf k}|\mathrm{P},{\bf k}\rangle=\mathbf{A}_{\mathrm{v},{\bf k}}-\mathbf{A}_{\mathrm{c},{\bf k}}
where
\mathbf{A}_{a,{\bf k}}=-i\sum_{i}U^{\ast}_{i,a,{\bf k}}\partial_{\bf k}U_{i,a,{\bf k}}
is the Berry connection of band
a
. The Chern number of the pair state reads
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{P}}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathrm{BZ}}\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{k}\cdot\nabla_{{\bf k}}\times\mathbf{A}_{\mathrm{P},\mathbf{k}}=\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{v}}-\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{c}}
, with BZ the Brillouin zone. Therefore, if the two bands have different Chern numbers, the e-h pair state has a finite Chern number. Based on the same type of argument, it has been proposed that in superconductors with electron pairing between two disconnected Fermi surfaces, the Cooper pair state inherits a nontrivial geometric phase from the underlying Fermi surfaces
Nagaosa2003
;
LiHaldane2018
.

The information about the topological nature of excitons is encoded in their profile wavefunction. Excitons are linear superpositions of electron-hole pair states,
|X,\mathbf{Q}\rangle=\sum_{{\bf k}\in\mathrm{BZ}}\mathcal{R}_{\bf k}(\mathbf{Q})|e,\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{Q}\rangle|h,-\mathbf{k}\rangle
forming an envelope function
\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{k}}(\mathbf{Q})
with a finite center of mass (COM) momentum
\mathbf{Q}
even in centrosymmetric materials. Excitonic bands are defined by the midgap energy dispersion of excitons with respect to their COM momentum. In conventional semiconductors, excitons can be very extended real space objects, permitting a simplification
Haug1993
;
Wang2018
in which their effective mass is approximated by a profile wavefunction with zero COM momentum. In flat bands, where the effective mass is infinite and the mobility of excitons is governed by the quantum geometry of the bands, the size of the excitons is of the order of a lattice constant and their envelope wavefunction extends over COM momenta in the whole BZ.

Topological flat bands hosting quantum anomalous Hall states have been experimentally observed in twisted bilayer graphene
Sharpe
;
Serlin
, which offer a valuable opportunity for the exploration of topological and quantum geometric effects.
Non-hydrogenic exciton states arising from the quantum geometry have been predicted in theory
Bychkov1980
;
Kallin1984
;
Srivastava2015
;
Wu2015
;
Zhou2015
;
Wang2019-1
and observed in transition metal dichalcogenide (TMD) monolayers
Beaulieu2023
. The effect of the quantum geometry on the COM motion of excitons has also been theoretically discussed
Yao2008
;
Kwan2021
;
Tang2023
. The onset of superconductivity in a partial filled flat band has been investigated theoretically and it is found that the superfluid stiffness arises purely from the quantum metric of the band
Volovik2011
;
PeottaTorma2015
;
Huhtinen2022
;
Jiang2023
.

In this work we theoretically show the existence of topological excitons in the midgap of generic flat Chern bands when the conduction and valence bands are topologically distinct. Although excitons can spontaneously form in narrow gap insulators
Lu
;
Cercellier
;
Bucher
, they are much easier to observe with pumped light
Koch2006
. Monochromatic photons can promote coherent population inversion between flat bands over states spanning the whole BZ. For that purpose, we develop the nonequilibrium theory for light-pumped excitons in topological flat bands and investigate the manifestations of the quantum geometry on the exciton profile functions, as well as on the exciton COM motion. We demonstrate that the envelope wavefunction of topological excitons has a finite vorticity that is mandated by gauge invariance. We corroborate our analysis by explicitly solving the out-of-equilibrium equation of state for excitons on the lattice for the flattened Haldane model.

Those excitons condense below the Kostelitz-Thouless transition temperature and form a novel type of topological neutral superfluid with profile wavefunctions in momentum space that carry a finite vorticity, reflecting the Chern number of the state. Exciton condensates have been observed in double-layer systems, where Fermi surfaces of electrons and holes are spatially separated by design
Wang2019
;
Li2017
, and also in the topologically trivial bands of monolayer transition-metal dichalchogenides
Kogar
;
Sun
.
We propose observables that signal the nontrivial topology and quantum geometry of excitons in both insulating and superfluid phases, including the non-linear Hall effect and a realization of the Aharonov-Casher effect.
Topological excitons are chiral and expected to spontaneously emit circularly polarized light through radiative decay. Those states can be a potential platform for realizing photonic qubit gates
adami1999
;
Knill2001
;
Kok2007
and novel optoelectronic devices, such as superradiant pulse emitters
Gross1982
;
Scheibner2007
, in the superfluid phase.

Results

II.1
Exciton theory in two-band insulators

As a minimal model, we study a two-band electron system driven by monochromatic light incident normally to the system, i.e.,
\bm{E}(t)=\mathbf{E}e^{-i\Omega t}+\mathbf{E}^{\ast}e^{i\Omega t}

Via the Peierls substitution in momentum space
{\bf k}\to{\bf k}+\frac{e}{\hbar}\mathbf{A}(t)

;

where
\Psi_{\bf k}
is a two-spinor in orbit space,

is the
2\times 2

is the density fluctuation operator and

describes the repulsive density-density interaction.
A
is the system area, and
we have denoted
\sum_{{\bf k}}\equiv\frac{A}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int\!d^{2}{\bf k}
for a shorthand.
We are interested in the regime where the light frequency
\Omega
is near the optical resonance and the system is coupled to a reservoir in a steady state. For more details see Methods section.

Exciton states are defined in electron band basis. Diagonalizing the single-particle Hamiltonian by a unitary matrix

\hat{U}_{\bf k}^{\dagger}\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\bf k}\hat{U}_{\bf k}=\hat{{\varepsilon}}_{\bf k}\equiv\mathrm{diag}({\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{c},{\bf k}},{\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{v},{\bf k}})

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{S}(t)=

\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf k}}\psi_{\bf k}^{\dagger}\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\bf k}(t)\psi_{\bf k}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{A}\sum_{{\bf q},{\bf q}^{\prime},{\bf Q}}W_{{\bf q},{\bf q}^{\prime};{\bf Q}}\Phi_{{\bf q}+\frac{{\bf Q}}{2},{\bf q}-\frac{{\bf Q}}{2}}^{\dagger}\Phi_{{\bf q}^{\prime}+\frac{{\bf Q}}{2},{\bf q}^{\prime}-\frac{{\bf Q}}{2}}.

(1)

Here

is the two-spinor in band space, where

\Phi_{{\bf q}+\frac{{\bf Q}}{2},{\bf q}-\frac{{\bf Q}}{2}}^{\dagger}=\psi_{\mathrm{c},{\bf q}+\frac{{\bf Q}}{2}}^{\dagger}\psi_{\mathrm{v},{\bf q}-\frac{{\bf Q}}{2}}

\Phi_{{\bf q}+\frac{{\bf Q}}{2},{\bf q}-\frac{{\bf Q}}{2}}=\psi_{\mathrm{v},{\bf q}-\frac{{\bf Q}}{2}}^{\dagger}\psi_{\mathrm{c},{\bf q}+\frac{{\bf Q}}{2}}

{\bf Q}
and relative momentum
{\bf q}
.
The effective pair interaction is composed of two parts,

W_{{\bf q},{\bf q}^{\prime};{\bf Q}}=W^{(\mathrm{d})}_{{\bf q},{\bf q}^{\prime};{\bf Q}}-W^{(\mathrm{e})}_{{\bf q},{\bf q}^{\prime};{\bf Q}}
,
with

\displaystyle W^{(\mathrm{d})}_{{\bf q},{\bf q}^{\prime};{\bf Q}}=v_{{\bf q}^{\prime}-{\bf q}}\,\mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{c}\mathrm{c}}_{{\bf q}+\frac{{\bf Q}}{2},{\bf q}^{\prime}+\frac{{\bf Q}}{2}}\mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{v}\mathrm{v}}_{{\bf q}^{\prime}-\frac{{\bf Q}}{2},{\bf q}-\frac{{\bf Q}}{2}},

\displaystyle W^{(\mathrm{e})}_{{\bf q},{\bf q}^{\prime};{\bf Q}}=v_{\bf Q}\,\mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{c}\mathrm{v}}_{{\bf q}+\frac{{\bf Q}}{2},{\bf q}-\frac{{\bf Q}}{2}}\mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{v}\mathrm{c}}_{{\bf q}^{\prime}-\frac{{\bf Q}}{2},{\bf q}^{\prime}+\frac{{\bf Q}}{2}},

(2)

the direct and exchange e-h interactions, respectively

;
Wang2018
, where
\hat{\mathcal{U}}_{{\bf k}_{1},{\bf k}_{2}}\equiv\hat{U}^{\dagger}_{{\bf k}_{1}}\hat{U}_{{\bf k}_{2}}
represents the overlap of the Bloch wave functions encoding the quantum geometry of the band electrons. For Coulomb interactions of the Rytova-Keldaysh form, that is widely used in layered semiconductors

;
Keldysh1979
,
v_{\bf q}\sim 1/|{\bf q}|
for

.

The single-particle Hamiltonian matrix coupled to light is given by
\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\bf k}(t)=\hat{U}_{\bf k}^{\dagger}\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{{\bf k}+\frac{e}{\hbar}\mathbf{A}(t)}\hat{U}_{\bf k}

\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\bf k}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\{\alpha_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}}A_{\{\alpha_{i}\}}^{(n)}(t)\,\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{\alpha_{n},\cdots\alpha_{1};{\bf k}}^{(n)},

(3)

where
\alpha_{i}\in\{x,y\}
, and

\mathcal{D}_{{\bf k}}=\mathcal{\partial}_{{\bf k}}+i[\hat{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}_{\bf k},\quad]

\hat{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}_{\bf k}=-i\hat{U}_{\bf k}^{\dagger}\partial_{{\bf k}}\hat{U}_{\bf k}
is the Berry connection matrix. We note that in general

involves only

Up to linear order of the electric field, which captures the single-photon processes, the driven band Hamiltonian (
3

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\bf k}(t)=\begin{pmatrix}{\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{c},{\bf k}}&e^{-i\Omega t}\,\mathbf{E}\cdot\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{cv},{\bf k}}\\

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{cv},{\bf k}}=\frac{e\Delta_{{\bf k}}}{\Omega}\bm{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathrm{cv},{\bf k}},

(4)

with
\Delta_{{\bf k}}={\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{c},{\bf k}}-{\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{v}{\bf k}}

We investigate the dynamics of the driven system exploiting the Keldysh formalism

\displaystyle\mathsf{X}_{{\bf q}}(\Omega)=\mathbf{E}\cdot\bm{\mathcal{P}}_{\bf q},

\displaystyle\bm{\mathcal{P}}_{\bf q}=\sum_{{\bf q}^{\prime},{\bf q}^{\prime\prime}}\Pi^{R}_{{\bf q},{\bf q}^{\prime}}(\Omega,{\bf Q}=0)\,W_{{\bf q}^{\prime},{\bf q}^{\prime\prime};{\bf Q}=0}\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{cv},{\bf q}^{\prime\prime}},

(5)

is the electric polarization of the e-h pair, which is a linear combination of the band dipole moments averaged over the whole BZ. Equation (

(6)

II.2
Exciton bound states with nontrivial vorticity

We first examine the topology of the exciton states implied by charge U(1) gauge symmetry. Applying local U(1) gauge transformations to the conduction and valence electron wave functions
\hat{U}_{\bf k}\to\hat{U}_{\bf k}\mathrm{diag}\{e^{i\phi_{\mathrm{c},{\bf k}}},e^{i\phi_{\mathrm{v},{\bf k}}}\}
, where
\phi_{a,{\bf k}}
with
a\in\{\mathrm{c,v}\}
are arbitrary phase fields in reciprocal space, the exciton Hamiltonian transforms as
h_{{\bf q},{\bf q}^{\prime}}({\bf Q})\to e^{i(\phi_{\mathrm{c},{\bf q}^{\prime}+{\bf Q}/2}-\phi_{\mathrm{c},{\bf q}+{\bf Q}/2})}e^{i(\phi_{\mathrm{v},{\bf q}-{\bf Q}/2}-\phi_{\mathrm{v},{\bf q}^{\prime}-{\bf Q}/2})}h_{{\bf q},{\bf q}^{\prime}}({\bf Q})

In order to keep the Wannier equation (
7
) gauge-invariant, one should define the U(1) gauge transformation of the exciton wavefunction as

\mathcal{R}_{\nu,{\bf q}}({\bf Q})\to e^{-i(\phi_{\mathrm{c},{\bf q}+{\bf Q}/2}-\phi_{\mathrm{v},{\bf q}-{\bf Q}/2})}\mathcal{R}_{\nu,{\bf q}}({\bf Q}).

(11)

To incorporate the geometric phase of the electron wave functions we take
\phi_{a,{\bf k}}=\int_{{\bf k}_{0}}^{{\bf k}}d{\bf k}^{\prime}\cdot\bm{\mathcal{A}}_{a,{\bf k}^{\prime}}

\bm{\mathcal{A}}_{a,{\bf k}}

{\bf k}_{0}
and the integral path from
{\bf k}_{0}
to
{\bf k}
are gauge degrees of freedom. Choosing an exciton wave function

\tilde{\varphi}_{\nu,{\bf q}}({\bf Q})\equiv\varphi_{\nu,{\bf q}}({\bf Q})+\int_{{\bf k}_{0}}^{{\bf q}+{\bf Q}/2}d{\bf k}^{\prime}\cdot\bm{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathrm{c},{\bf k}^{\prime}}-\int_{{\bf k}_{0}}^{{\bf q}-{\bf Q}/2}d{\bf k}^{\prime}\cdot\bm{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathrm{v},{\bf k}^{\prime}}

\mathbf{u}_{\bf q}({\bf Q})\equiv\partial_{\bf q}\tilde{\varphi}_{\nu,{\bf q}}({\bf Q})=\partial_{\bf q}\varphi_{\nu,{\bf q}}({\bf Q})-\bm{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathrm{c},{\bf q}+{\bf Q}/2}+\bm{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathrm{v},{\bf q}-{\bf Q}/2}
is a gauge-invariant observable. The corresponding vorticity is defined as
\bm{\zeta}_{{\bf q}}({\bf Q})\equiv\bm{\nabla}_{\bf q}\times\mathbf{u}_{\bf q}({\bf Q})=-\bm{\Omega}_{\mathrm{c},{\bf q}+{\bf Q}/2}+\bm{\Omega}_{\mathrm{v},{\bf q}-{\bf Q}/2}=\zeta_{{\bf q}}({\bf Q})\hat{\mathbf{z}}

\hat{\mathbf{z}}
is the unit vector in
z

\bm{\Omega}_{a,{\bf k}}=\bm{\nabla}_{\bf k}\times\bm{\mathcal{A}}_{a,{\bf k}}
is the
a

\zeta=\int_{\mathrm{BZ}}\!\frac{d^{2}{\bf q}}{2\pi}\zeta_{{\bf q}}({\bf Q})=\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{v}}-\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{c}},

(12)

which is independent of the COM momentum
{\bf Q}
. For
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{c}}=-\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{v}}
, we obtain exciton wavefunctions with vorticity that is twice of the valence-band Chern number
\nu=2\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{v}}
. Note that this analysis is valid for excitons in general, not only for those in flat bands.

One can readily see formally why the topology of the electron wavefunctions is inherited by the exciton wavefunctions. For a non-vanishing Chern number,
we could choose a gauge in which the electron wave function is analytic anywhere in the BZ except at isolated points
Dirac1931
, or else divide the BZ into distinct analytic regions separated by non-analytic boundaries
WuYang1975
. This non-analyticity is incorporated in the exciton Hamiltonian (
6
) through wavefunction overlaps
\hat{\mathcal{U}}
that appear in the effective interaction in Eq. (
II.1
). The conventional group-theoretical analysis of exciton wavefunctions does not apply to topological excitons, since the wavefunctions of the latter are not analytic
Nagaosa2003
;
LiHaldane2018
. Due to the emergence of gauge degrees of freedom of the exciton states in Eq. (
11
), the pairing “symmetry” is gauge-dependent, even though the vorticity is invariant.

In order to show an explicit example of the vortex exciton states we study a simple model of two flat bands,
\mathcal{H}_{\bf k}=\frac{\Delta}{2}\hat{\mathbf{d}}({\bf k})\cdot\bm{\sigma}
, where
\hat{\mathbf{d}}({\bf k})
is a 3D unit vector. We parametrize the
\hat{\mathbf{d}}({\bf k})
vector by an arbitrary meromorphic function
\chi(z)
of the complex variable
z\equiv k_{x}+ik_{y}
as
\hat{d}_{1}+i\hat{d}_{2}=\frac{2\chi(z)}{1+|\chi(z)|^{2}}
,
\hat{d}_{1}-i\hat{d}_{2}=\frac{2\chi^{\ast}(z)}{1+|\chi(z)|^{2}}
and
\hat{d}_{3}=\frac{1-|\chi(z)|^{2}}{1+|\chi(z)|^{2}}

Jian2013

\hat{U}_{\bf k}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+|\chi|^{2}}}\begin{pmatrix}1&\chi^{\ast}\\
\chi&-1\end{pmatrix}
up to a U(1) gauge transformation. One can prove that the Chern number of the conduction band is equal to the number of poles of
\chi(z)
, with “Dirac strings” along the third extra dimension piercing the BZ.
We can simply study the
{\bf Q}=0
component of the Wannier equation since the total vorticity is independent of the COM motion. The effective interaction reads

W_{{\bf k},{\bf k}^{\prime};{\bf Q}=0}=v_{{\bf k}^{\prime}-{\bf k}}\frac{\left(1+\chi^{\ast}(z)\chi(z^{\prime})\right)^{2}}{(1+|\chi(z)|^{2})(1+|\chi(z^{\prime})|^{2})}
.
Assuming a single pole at
{\bf k}=0
, one has
\chi(z)\approx a/z
in the vicinity of
{\bf k}=0
, where
a
is the residue of the pole characterizing the pole strength and the c-band Berry curvature is
\Omega_{\mathrm{c}}\approx 4\pi/|a|^{2}
. For
z,z^{\prime}
in the vicinity of
z=0
and up to quadratic order in
{\bf k},{\bf k}^{\prime}
, we obtain

W_{{\bf k},{\bf k}^{\prime};{\bf Q}=0}\approx v_{{\bf k}^{\prime}-{\bf k}}e^{2i(\phi-\phi^{\prime})}\lambda_{{\bf k},{\bf k}^{\prime}},

(13)

where
\lambda_{{\bf k},{\bf k}^{\prime}}=1-\frac{\Omega_{\mathrm{c}}}{4\pi}[|{\bf k}-{\bf k}^{\prime}|^{2}-2i({\bf k}\times{\bf k}^{\prime})_{z}]
and we have defined the polar coordinates
{\bf k}=k(\cos\phi,\sin\phi)
.

The correction from the quantum geometry to the bare interaction in equation (
13
) is composed of two parts. The first one is the topological factor
e^{2i(\phi-\phi^{\prime})}
, that incorporates the pole of
\chi(z)
. This term modifies the exciton wave function but does not influence the energy spectrum. The presence of the poles (nontrivial Chern number) is not a perturbation and the Taylor expansion of
\chi(z^{\prime})
about
z
cannot capture the pole at
z=0
. The second one is the geometric factor
\lambda_{{\bf k},{\bf k}^{\prime}}
, that is perturbative in the Berry curvature
\Omega_{\mathrm{c}}
, which modifies the exciton energy spectrum.
The vorticity is incorporated in the topological factor. We assume that for
e^{2i(\phi-\phi^{\prime})}=1
the Wannier equation (
7

{\bf Q}=0
takes an eigenstate
\mathcal{R}_{\nu,{\bf k}}=|\mathcal{R}_{\nu,{\bf k}}|e^{i\varphi_{\nu,{\bf k}}}
, that is an analytic function. Absorbing the topological factor into the eigenstate, the wave functions can be written as
\mathcal{R}_{\nu,{\bf k}}e^{2i\phi}
, so that vorticity is the winding number
\zeta=2
. Equation (

\lambda_{{\bf k},{\bf k}^{\prime}}
is invariant under a global rotation about
{\bf k}=0

\Omega_{\mathrm{c}}\to 0
, the ground state wave function should take a
d+id
-wave form
\mathcal{R}_{0,{\bf k}}=\mathcal{R}_{0,k}e^{i2\phi}
for conventional Coulomb interaction.

For concreteness, we explicitly verify this analysis by numerically solving the eigenvalue equation (

\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{v}}=-\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{c}}=1

II.3
Effective exciton masses and superfluidity

The COM motion of excitons in flat bands originates from the effective e-h interaction in the Wannier equation (
7

\displaystyle\tilde{h}_{\mathrm{ex},\nu\nu^{\prime}}({\bf Q})=

\displaystyle\delta_{\nu\nu^{\prime}}{\varepsilon}_{\nu}+\delta{f}\sum_{\alpha=x,y}\mathcal{V}_{\alpha,\nu\nu^{\prime}}Q_{\alpha}

\displaystyle+\frac{\delta{f}}{2}\sum_{\alpha,\beta=x,y}\mathcal{M}_{\alpha\beta,\nu\nu^{\prime}}^{-1}Q_{\alpha}Q_{\beta},

\mathcal{V}_{\alpha,\mu\mu^{\prime}}

Nonlinear Hall conductivity

Photocurrents can be induced in insulators by pumping light. Application of a static electric field

II.5
Aharonov-Casher effect and electric field quantization

Neutral particles with a finite magnetic moment
\mathbf{m}
couple to an external static electric field
\bm{\mathcal{E}}
through a gauge field
\mathbf{A}=\mu\epsilon\,\mathbf{m}\times\bm{\mathcal{E}}

AC1984
;
Cimmino1989
;
Mooij1993
;
Molenkamp2006
;
Nakata2014
, where
\mu
and
\epsilon
are the magnetic and electric permeabilities, respectively. In the superfluid phase, phase coherence dictates that the circulation of the gauge field
\theta_{\mathrm{AC}}=\frac{1}{\hbar}\oint\mathrm{d}\mathbf{r}\cdot\mathbf{A}=2\pi N
produces a quantized geometric phase, with
N
an integer. This quantization is known as the Aharonov-Casher effect
AC1984
, in analogy with the superconducting case.

One could exploit the Aharonov-Casher effect to detect the superfluidity of the excitons, since they can inherit a magnetic dipole moment from the electron bands. We start from the thermodynamic definition of the orbital magnetization of the bound state
\nu
,
\mathbf{m}_{\alpha}({\bf Q})\equiv-\partial_{\mathbf{B}}{\varepsilon}_{\nu}(\mathbf{B},{\bf Q})|_{\mathbf{B=0}}
, where
{\varepsilon}_{\nu}(\mathbf{B})
is the bound state energy in the presence of a uniform static magnetic field
\mathbf{B}

Shi2007
. Up to leading order in the magnetic field
\mathbf{B}
, the exciton Hamiltonian reads
h_{\mathrm{ex},{\bf q},{\bf q}^{\prime}}^{\prime}({\bf Q})=h_{\mathrm{ex},{\bf q},{\bf q}^{\prime}}({\bf Q})-\mathbf{B}\cdot\mathbf{M}_{{\bf q},{\bf q}^{\prime}}({\bf Q})
, where
\mathbf{M}_{{\bf q},{\bf q}^{\prime}}({\bf Q})
are the orbital magnetization matrix element in e-h space, so that
\mathbf{m}_{\nu}({\bf Q})=\sum_{{\bf q},{\bf q}^{\prime}}\mathcal{R}_{\nu,{\bf q}}^{\ast}({\bf Q})\mathbf{M}_{{\bf q},{\bf q}^{\prime}}({\bf Q})\mathcal{R}_{\nu,{\bf q}^{\prime}}({\bf Q})
. At zero temperature, the leading contributions to the magnetization arise from the Zeeman effect in the single-electron band and from the quantum geometry.

For a pair of conduction and valence bands embedded in a set of flat bands, both the effective gap and the interaction term are modified by the magnetic field perturbation. At
{\bf Q}=0
, the magnetic moment

m_{\nu}({\bf Q}=0)=\frac{1}{\hbar}\sum_{\bf k}|\mathcal{R}_{\nu,{\bf k}}|^{2}\sum_{a=\mathrm{c,v}}\sum_{b\neq\mathrm{c,v}}[2(\Delta-{\varepsilon}_{\nu})+\sigma_{a}\Delta_{ab}]D_{ab,{\bf k}}
, where
\sigma_{\mathrm{c(v)}}=+1(-1)
and
D_{ab,{\bf k}}=-e\mathrm{Im}(\mathcal{A}_{ab,x,{\bf k}}\mathcal{A}_{ab,y,{\bf k}}^{\ast})
. For a two-band model with particle-hole symmetry, the magnetic dipole moment vanishes since the orbital magnetizations of the two bands exactly compensate each other. For finite COM momentum
{\bf Q}\neq 0
, both the effective gap and the interaction couple to the magnetic field,

m_{\nu}({\bf Q})=\frac{{\varepsilon}_{\nu}}{\hbar}\sum_{\bf k}|\mathcal{R}_{\nu,{\bf k}}({\bf Q})|^{2}\left(D_{\mathrm{cv},{\bf k}+{\bf Q}/2}-D_{\mathrm{cv},{\bf k}-{\bf Q}/2}\right)
. The derivation is presented in Supplementary Information

In practice, for any multi-flat band system away from half filling, as in twisted bilayer graphene at 3/4 filling
Sharpe
;
Serlin
, the exciton magnetic moment can be finite and is of the same order as that of the corresponding electron bands. From dimensional analysis, one can estimate an upper bound for the orbital magnetization in flat bands,
m_{z}\lesssim\frac{e}{\hbar}\Delta a_{0}^{2}
, with
a_{0}
the effective lattice constant in the system. The effective Landé
g
-factor reads
g\equiv m_{z}/\mu_{\mathrm{B}}\lesssim\frac{2m_{e}\Delta a^{2}}{\hbar^{2}}
, where
\mu_{\mathrm{B}}\equiv\frac{e\hbar}{2m_{e}}
is the Bohr magneton. For TBG at the first magic angle,
\Delta\sim 10\,\mathrm{meV}
and
a_{0}\sim 200\,\text{\r{A}}
, we estimate that the Landé
g
-factor can be as large as
g\sim 10^{2}
.

As in the flux quantization in a superconductor, one could investigate the topological exciton condensate between two plates of a cylindrical capacitor [see Fig.
3
(a)]. The applied electric field reads
\bm{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbf{r})=\frac{\lambda}{2\pi{\epsilon}}\frac{\hat{\mathbf{e}}_{r}}{r}
, where
\lambda
is the linear charge density in the cylinder and
\epsilon
the dielectric constant in the medium of the condensate, resulting in a voltage drop between the two plates
\delta V\equiv V_{1}-V_{2}=\frac{\lambda}{2\pi{\epsilon}}\ln(D/d)
. For a magnetic moment
\mathbf{m}=g\mu_{\mathrm{B}}\hat{\mathbf{z}}
moving on a circle of radius

\theta_{\mathrm{AC}}=\frac{\mu{\epsilon}}{\hbar}\oint\!d\mathbf{r}\cdot\left(\mathbf{m}\times\bm{\mathcal{E}}\right)=\frac{g\mu_{\mathrm{B}}\lambda\mu}{\hbar}=2\pi N
. Therefore, the electric field is screened inside the superfluid, with a quantized effective linear charge density
\lambda=N\frac{2\pi\hbar}{g\mu_{\mathrm{B}}\mu}
. The voltage drop between
\mathrm{T}_{1}
and
\mathrm{T}_{2}
[see Fig.
3
(a)] changes in integer steps

V_{0}^{\prime}=\frac{\hbar c^{2}}{g\mu_{\mathrm{B}}}\ln(r_{2}/r_{1})\sim\frac{\tilde{c}^{2}}{g}\ln(r_{2}/r_{1})\times 10^{6}\,\mathrm{V}
, where
r_{1(2)}
is the distance from
\mathrm{T}_{1(2)}
to the disk center.

is the speed of light in the exciton condensate, and
\tilde{c}=c/c_{0}
is the reduced speed of light, with
c_{0}
the speed of light in vacuum. In order to observe quantization steps, we require significantly small values of

\ln(r_{2}/r_{1})\sim 1

III
Discussion

Moire heterostructures of graphene and TMDs offer a possible platform for the observation of topological excitons
Sharpe
;
Serlin
.
The most common experimentally studied samples with excitonic effects combine a semiconductor with a substrate. In the effective medium approximation, the dielectric constant of the semiconductor
{\epsilon}
largely exceeds the dielectric constant of the substrate and produces mostly dielectric screening. As a result, the effective Coulomb interaction takes the form of
1/r
, where
r
is the e-h relative distance, when
r
is much larger than the dielectric screening length
r_{0}
. For
r\ll r_{0}
, the Coulomb interaction is proportional to
\ln{r}

Rytova1967
;
Keldysh1979
. The Wannier equation (
7
) in the presence of Coulomb interactions is expected to result in a series of midgap exciton states with energy spectrum that strongly deviates from that of the hydrogen atom, since the effective mass approximation is not applicable in flat bands. We note that the specific form of the Coulomb interaction will change the energy spectrum of the excitons, but will have no effect in the topology of the excitonic ground state.

The electric dipole moment of excitons
\bm{\ell}_{\nu}
[Eq. (
9
)] can be very different compared to the electric dipole moment
\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{cv}}
of the e-h excitations at any given momentum. The optical selection rules for exciton states in flat Chern bands can strongly depend on the vorticity of the exciton profile function
\mathcal{R}_{\nu,\mathbf{q}}
. This is distinct from the exciton selection rules in conventional materials, where the effective mass approximation is valid. In that approximation,
\bm{\ell}_{\nu}\approx|\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{\nu,0}|^{2}\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{cv}}
, where
\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{\nu,0}=\sum_{{\bf k}}\mathcal{R}_{\nu,{\bf k}}
is a constant
and
\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{cv}}
takes the value at the band extremes
Haug1993
. For example, in TMD materials, circularly polarized light selects e-h excitations as well as excitons in only one of the two valleys
Wang2018
. In general, quantum geometry and topological constraints can dictate distinct selection rules for excitons and e-h excitations.

We finally remark that exciton superfluidity can also be detected via photoluminescence. At the onset of superfluidity, the spectrally integrated photoluminescence intensity can be substantially enhanced, while the photon statistics revealed by the emission can strongly deviate from a Poissonian distribution, revealing a bunching transition
Wang2019
. We expect that radiative decay of topological excitons should lead to the spontaneous emission of circularly polarized light at the exciton resonance frequency. In order to observe the exciton superfluidity, the superfluid coherence time, limited by the light pump, should be larger than the radiative life time, which again depends on the exciton dipole moment and energy spectrum. We leave the exploration of these directions to a future study
XieUchoa2023
.

\gamma

\nu

\hat{A}_{\bf k}(\omega)=\hat{R}_{\bf k}^{\dagger}(\omega)

\hat{K}_{\bf k}(\omega)=\hat{R}_{\bf k}(\omega)\hat{\Sigma}(\omega)\hat{A}_{\bf k}(\omega)
, where
\hat{\Sigma}(\omega)=-i\hbar\Gamma(1-2\hat{f}_{F}(\omega))
is the Keldysh component of the self-energy arising from the coupling to the bath. The matrix
f_{F,mn}(\omega)=\delta_{mn}f_{F}(\omega+m\Omega)
is diagonal in Floquet space and encodes the Fermi-Dirac distribution function of the bath. Moreover, the observables are directly related to the lesser Green’s function
\hat{G}_{\bf k}^{<}(\omega)=(\hat{K}_{\bf k}(\omega)-\hat{R}_{\bf k}(\omega)+\hat{A}_{\bf k}(\omega))/2

IV.2
Flat-band Haldane model

In general a two-band flat-band model reads
H_{{\bf k}}=\frac{\Delta}{2}\hat{\mathbf{d}}({\bf k})\cdot\bm{\sigma}
where
\hat{\mathbf{d}}({\bf k})
is a unit vector, i.e.,
|\hat{\mathbf{d}}({\bf k})|=1
. Exploiting the Haldane model on honeycomb lattice, we introduce the
\mathbf{d}
-vector

\displaystyle d_{1}({\bf k})=t_{1}\sum_{j}\cos({\bf k}\cdot\mathbf{a}_{j}),\quad d_{2}({\bf k})=t_{1}\sum_{j}\sin({\bf k}\cdot\mathbf{a}_{j}),

\displaystyle d_{3}({\bf k})=-t_{2}\sum_{j}\sin({\bf k}\cdot\mathbf{b}_{j}),

(32)

where
t_{1,2}
are real parameters and
\mathbf{a}_{1}=a(1,0)
,
\mathbf{a}_{2}=a(-1/2,\sqrt{3}/2)
,
\mathbf{a}_{3}=-\mathbf{a}_{1}-\mathbf{a}_{2}
,
\mathbf{b}_{1}=\mathbf{a}_{2}-\mathbf{a}_{3}
,
\mathbf{b}_{2}=\mathbf{a}_{3}-\mathbf{a}_{1}
,
\mathbf{b}_{3}=\mathbf{a}_{1}-\mathbf{a}_{2}
. We define
\hat{\mathbf{d}}({\bf k})=\mathbf{d}({\bf k})/|\mathbf{d}({\bf k})|
. The conduction and valence bands take the energies

and the eigenstates

, with
\sigma_{\mathrm{c(v)}}=+1\,(-1)
. For this mode
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{c}}=-\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{v}}=1

7
) for a constant interaction, we obtain a single exciton bound state and the wave function is shown in Fig.

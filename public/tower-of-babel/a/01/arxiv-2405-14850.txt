Floer-theoretic filtration on Painlevé Hitchin systems
Szilárd Szabó, Filip Živanović

Abstract
. We classify equivariant ℂ ∗ \mathbb{C}^{*} -actions on moduli spaces of Higgs bundles corresponding to the Painlevé equations. Using this, we compute the Floer-theoretic filtrations on the cohomology of these spaces, introduced by Ritter and the second author in [ RŽ23a ] . We compare it with the “ P = W P=W ” and the filtration obtained by multiplicities of the irreducible components of the nilpotent cone, ultimately deducing that the Floer-theoretic filtration coincides with the multiplicity filtration, for all 2-dimensional Higgs moduli.

in the first place.

3.8.
Classification of 2-dimensional
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-equivariant Higgs moduli spaces

Proposition 3.15
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a moduli space of Higgs bundles over some curve
X
.
Assume that

(1)

\mathcal{M}
is of complex dimension
2
;

(2)

there exists a smooth Hitchin map
h\colon\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{C}
turning
\mathcal{M}
into a smooth elliptic fibration;

(3)

\mathbb{C}^{*}
acts holomorphically on
\mathcal{M}
and
h
is equivariant for some contracting action on
\mathbb{C}.

Then,
\mathcal{M}
is biregular to one of the spaces
\mathcal{M}^{PX}
or
\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}
described in Sections
3.1
–
3.7
.
Moreover,
h
agrees with the corresponding Hitchin map of
\mathcal{M}^{PX}
and
\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}
too.

Proof.

It follows from assumption (
2
) that
\mathcal{M}
is a Zariski open subset of the rational elliptic surface
\operatorname{Bl}_{9}\mathbb{C}P^{2}
(
\mathbb{C}P^{2}
blown up
9
times).
The Hitchin map is algebraic, therefore it extends to an elliptic fibration

\tilde{h}\colon\operatorname{Bl}_{9}\mathbb{C}P^{2}\to\mathbb{C}P^{1}.

From assumption (
3
) and smoothness of
h
we infer that
\tilde{h}
may have at most two singular fibers, namely the fibers over
0,\infty\in\mathbb{C}P^{1}
.

If
h
admits no singular fiber, then
\mathcal{M}
is diffeomorphic to
T^{*}E
for an elliptic curve
E
, i.e.
\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}
for
\Gamma=0
.

Assume that
h
admits exactly one singular fiber, and
\tilde{h}
has exactly two singular fibers.
Then, from the structure of the monodromy of the Kodaira fibers, it is well-known that the only possibilities for couples of singular fibers over
\operatorname{Bl}_{9}\mathbb{C}P^{2}
are
I_{0}^{*}+I_{0}^{*}
,
\widetilde{E}_{6}+IV
,
\widetilde{E}_{7}+III
or
\widetilde{E}_{8}+II
.

The case
I_{0}^{*}+I_{0}^{*}
is symmetric.
In the other cases, the fiber over
0
can be either of the two singular fibers.
In Sections
3.1
–
3.7
we have found all these possibilities.
∎

Remark 3.16
.

We would like to emphasize that there exist different moduli problems from the ones detailed here that have diffeomorphic moduli spaces.
Namely, in
[
Boa12
, Section 11]

different ”readings” of a quiver are introduced and studied.
For instance, in
[
Boa12
, Section 11.3]
, infinitely many Lax representations of
PIV
are described.
It would be interesting to work out in detail the Hitchin map for such other spaces explicitly.
We leave this question for further study.
The main challenge is that the spectral correspondence produces many-sheeted covers of
\mathbb{C}P^{1}
, which then get birationally transformed to high degree (and thus high genus) curves in
\mathbb{C}P^{2}
, whose Jacobi varieties are correspondingly of high dimension.
Therefore, in general it is not clear to the authors how they give rise to elliptic surfaces.
However, for the rank
3
Lax pairs of the Painlevé equations, in
[
ES25
]
it is proven that Fourier–Laplace transformation establishes symplectic isomorphisms with the Hitchin moduli spaces treated here.

4.
Three filtrations compared

The imaginary root labels mentioned above have another geometric meaning.
Recall from
3.11
that
\mathrm{Core}(\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma})=h_{\Gamma}^{-1}(0)=\cup E_{i}

has irreducible components
E_{i}
labeled by vertices
i
of
Q_{\Gamma}.

Moreover, its canonical scheme structure comes with multiplicities
m_{i}
of curves
E_{i}

h_{\Gamma}^{-1}(0)=\sum_{i\in Q_{\Gamma}^{0}}m_{i}E_{i}

An intrinsic definition of the
m_{i}
is that the Weil divisor on the right-hand side is linearly equivalent to the generic fiber of
h_{\Gamma}
(that is a smooth abelian variety).
An interesting phenomenon is that these coincide

(39)

m_{i}=n_{i},

with the labelings
n_{i}
of the imaginary root of
Q_{\Gamma}.
This is
a consequence of

(1)

Self-intersection of
h_{\Gamma}^{-1}(0)
(with multiplicities) in the sublattice spanned by
\mathrm{Irr}(\mathrm{Core}(\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}))
is equal to zero, as a fiber class.

(2)

By definition, the imaginary root is the positive integer generator of the radical of the symmetric bilinear form with given Cartan matrix, which equals minus the intersection matrix of the elements
E_{i}
of
\mathrm{Irr}(\mathrm{Core}(\mathcal{M}))
.

Hence, we can now rephrase

3.13

as

(40)

\mathrm{rk}\,\mathscr{F}_{k/|\Gamma|}=\#\{E_{i}\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathrm{Core}(\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}))\mid m_{i}\leq k\}

This is an interesting phenomenon, motivating us to define another filtration.
Consider any Higgs moduli space
\mathcal{M}
with a
I
-holomorphic
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action,
and its Hitchin fibration
h:\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}\cong\mathbb{C}^{\frac{1}{2}\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{M}}
. As above, its core together with its inherited scheme structure has irreducible components with multiplicities

\mathrm{Core}(\mathcal{M})=h^{-1}(0)=\sum_{i}m_{i}E_{i},\ \ m:=\mathrm{max}_{i}\,m_{i}

Definition 4.1
.

Define the
multiplicity filtration
on
H^{\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{M})
to be

\mathscr{M}_{\lambda}:=\langle[E_{i}]\mid m_{i}/m\leq\lambda\rangle.

Obviously,
\mathscr{M}_{1}=H^{\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{M}).

Thus,
3.13
reads now as

Corollary 4.2
.

For any 2-dimensional parabolic Higgs bundle
\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma},
we have

(41)

(\forall\lambda)\ \mathrm{rk}\,\mathscr{F}_{\lambda}=\mathrm{rk}\,\mathscr{M}_{\lambda},

Proof.

Notice by (
39
) that the maximal multiplicity
m
of the irreducible core components
corresponds to the maximal labeled number of the associated imaginary root.
Looking at
Figure

5
, we see that this maximal root label is precisely
|\Gamma|
, as for

\widetilde{D}_{4},\widetilde{E}_{6},\widetilde{E}_{7},\widetilde{E}_{8}
,
the corresponding groups
\Gamma
are (as mentioned before)

\mathbb{Z}/2,\mathbb{Z}/3,\mathbb{Z}/4,\mathbb{Z}/6,
respectively. For
\widetilde{A}_{0},
the multiplicity of the (only) component is
1,
so again equal to
|\Gamma|
, as
\Gamma=0
in that case.
Thus,
m=|\Gamma|
, and by (
40
)

\mathrm{rk}\,\mathscr{F}_{k/\Gamma}=\#\{E_{i}\mid m_{i}\leq k\}=\mathrm{rk}\,\langle[E_{i}]\mid m_{i}/|\Gamma|\leq k/|\Gamma|\rangle=\langle[E_{i}]\mid m_{i}/m\leq k/|\Gamma|\rangle=\mathrm{rk}\,\mathscr{M}_{k/\Gamma},

and both filtrations only jump in rank for
\lambda=k/\Gamma,
hence (
41
) is proved.
∎

Following ideas from
[
RŽ23a
]
, one expects that these filtrations actually coincide, not only rank-wise:

Conjecture 4.3
.

For any 2-dimensional parabolic Higgs bundle
\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma},
the Floer-theoretic and multiplicity filtration coincide:

\mathscr{F}_{\lambda}=\mathscr{M}_{\lambda}.

In summary, together with the results from
Section

3
, we have the following:

Theorem 4.4
.

Let us be given any Higgs moduli space
\mathcal{M}
satisfying the conditions of Proposition
3.15
.
Then, its multiplicity and Floer-theoretic filtration coincide rank-wise

(\forall\lambda)\ \mathrm{rk}\,\mathscr{F}_{\lambda}=\mathrm{rk}\,\mathscr{M}_{\lambda}.

For moduli spaces of parabolic Higgs bundles, they both refine the
P=W
filtration, whereas
for Painlevé spaces,
P=W
refines the former two filtrations.
Thus, in the intersection, i.e. for Painlevé VI space, these three filtrations coincide.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.15
, we only need to deal with the cases covered in Sections
3.1
–
3.7
.
By (
41
), the multiplicity and Floer-theoretic filtration coincide rank-wise for parabolic spaces
\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}.

For Painlevé spaces
\mathcal{M}^{PX},\ X\in\{I,II,IV\}
, both filtrations are trivial, so they coincide.
The Floer-theoretic filtration
\mathscr{F}=\mathscr{F}_{\mathbb{B}}(H^{2}(\mathcal{M}^{PX}))
is trivial due to (
22
),(
29
),(
33
), respectively.
The multiplicity filtration is trivial as the multiplicities of core components
E_{i}
are all
m_{i}=1

in these cases.
The reasoning for this is similar to that for (
39
), as the self-intersection of

\sum_{i}E_{i}
is zero. The latter has already been proved at the
ends of their respective
Sections

3.1
,
3.3
and
3.4
.

For the parabolic moduli spaces
\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}
, the multiplicity filtration
\mathscr{M}
refines the
P=W
, as for
\Gamma\neq 0,
the latter has two
steps (see
[
Zha17
, Proposition 5.4]
):

(42)

P_{0}=0\subset P_{1}=\langle[E_{i}]\mid i\text{ not the central vertex of }Q_{\Gamma}\rangle\subset P_{2}=H^{2}(\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}),

whereas the central vertex has the biggest multiplicity (see
Figure

5
), thus
P_{1}

is the penultimate filtered subspace of
\mathscr{M}
. For
\Gamma=0,
i.e.
\mathcal{M}=T^{*}E
,
both filtrations are trivial.

For Painlevé I, II, and IV spaces,
\mathscr{M}
and
\mathscr{F}
are both trivial, hence
P=W
refines them. This is a strict refinement for Painlevé II and IV, as there
P=W
has two steps, see
[
Sza21
, Table 2.]
.

Finally, for
Painlevé VI space , i.e.
\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{Z}/2},

P=W
filtration, on (
42
), coincides with

\mathscr{F}
, on (
36
), and
\mathscr{M}
, as multiplicities are 2 for the central vertex and 1 for the others, see
Figure

5
(a).
∎

Thus, the last theorem compares the three filtrations for all 2-dimensional Higgs moduli
with
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-actions, i.e. for which filtrations
\mathscr{F}
and
\mathscr{M}
make sense.
An interesting question is what happens in higher-dimensional examples? Due to
[
Gro14
]
, we know that the Hilbert schemes of parabolic 2-dimensional moduli,

\mathrm{Hilb}^{n}(\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma})
, are parabolic Higgs moduli spaces as well.
It would be interesting to understand the interplay between
P=W
, Floer-theoretic,
and multiplicity filtration, for these spaces.

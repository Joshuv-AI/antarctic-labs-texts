Mirror Symmetry for Log Calabi-Yau Surfaces II
Jonathan Lai, Yan Zhou

Abstract
. We show that the ring of regular functions of every smooth affine log Calabi-Yau surface with maximal boundary has a vector space basis parametrized by its set of integer tropical points and a ℂ \mathbb{C} -algebra structure with structure coefficients given by the geometric construction of [ KY ] . To prove this result, we first give a canonical compactification of the mirror family associated with a pair ( Y , D ) (Y,D) constructed in [ GHK ] where Y Y is a smooth projective rational surface, D D is an anti-canonical cycle of rational curves and Y ∖ D Y\setminus D is the minimal resolution of an affine surface with, at worst, du Val singularities. Then, we compute periods for the compactified family using techniques from [ RS19 ] and use this to give a modular interpretation of the compactified mirror family.



1.
Introduction

For this paper, we work over the ground field
\mathbb{C}
.

1.1.
Main results

Let
(Y,D)
be a pair where
Y
is a smooth projective rational surface and
D\in|-K_{Y}|
is either an irreducible rational nodal curve or a cycle of
n\geq 2
smooth rational curves. We call such a pair a
Looijenga pair
. Write
D=D_{1}+..+D_{n}
where
D_{i}
is an irreducible component. We say that
(Y,D)
is
positive
if the intersection matrix
(D_{i}\cdot D_{j})
is not negative semi-definite.

Recall that a smooth surface
U
is log Calabi-Yau with maximal boundary if and only if
U
can be realized as the complement of
D
in
Y
for a Looijenga pair
(Y,D)
. Additionally,
U
is affine if and only if
D
supports an ample divisor. Another equivalent condition for a Looijenga pair
(Y,D)
to be positive is that the complement
U=Y\setminus D
is the minimal resolution of an affine log Calabi-Yau surface with at worst du Val singularities.

Let
U
be a smooth affine log-Calabi-Yau surface with maximal boundary. Let
\omega
be the canonical volume holomorphic form (unique up to scaling) of
U
and
\mathbb{C}(U)
the function field of
U
. The set of integer tropical points
U^{\operatorname{trop}}(\mathbb{Z})
of
U
is the set of divisorial discrete valuations
\nu:\mathbb{C}(U)\setminus\{0\}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
such that
\nu(\omega)<0
together with the zero valuation.

The main result of the paper is the proof of a refined version of Conjecture 0.6 announced in
[
GHK
]
in the case of dimension
2
:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
U
be a smooth affine log Calabi-Yau surface with maximal boundary. Then, there is a decomposition

(1.1)

\displaystyle H^{0}(U,\mathcal{O}_{U})=\bigoplus_{q\in U^{\operatorname{trop}}(\mathbb{Z})}\mathbb{C}\cdot\theta_{q}

of the vector space of regular functions on
U
into one-dimensional subspaces, canonical up to the choice of one of the two possible orientations of
U^{\operatorname{trop}}(\mathbb{Z})
.

The canonical decomposition
1.1
implies the existence of distinguished vector space basis elements, unique up to scaling, called theta functions in
[
GHK
]
. The original conjecture of
[
GHK
]
says that the multiplication rule of theta functions has a combinatorial description in terms of broken lines defined in
[
GHK
]
. After
[
GHK
]
, there has been rapid development of the geometric construction of structure constants for the multiplication of theta functions. Using these recent developments, we are able to prove a more refined version of the original conjecture in
[
GHK
]
, as stated later in Theorem
1.9
, which states that the structure constants for the multiplication rule of the canonical basis elements of
H^{0}(U,\mathcal{O}_{U})
has a nice geometric construction via naive counts of analytic discs in the sense of non-archimedean geometry as in
[
KY
]
. There, in
[
KY
]
, broken lines are geometrically realized as the tropicalization, or the essential skeletons, of punctured non-archimedean analytic discs. Alternatively, these structure constants can also be constructed via the punctured Gromov-Witten invariants in
[
ACGS20
]
as we will explain later in the introduction.

Theorem
1.1
, and later Theorem
1.9
, follow from another main result of the paper, the identification of the mirror family associated with a positive Looijenga pair
(Y,D)
constructed in
[
GHK
]
as the universal family over a certain moduli space, as stated in Theorem
1.8
. The proof of this result forms the bulk of this paper.

Before we give the precise statement of Theorem
1.8
, let us briefly recall the construction from
[
GHK
]
. Let
(Y,D)
be a positive Looijenga pair and
\operatorname{NE}(Y)
the monoid generated by effective curve classes on
Y
. We can assume that
U=Y\setminus D
is affine. The mirror algebra
A
constructed in
[
GHK
]
, as a
\mathbb{C}[\operatorname{NE}(Y)]
-module, has a basis of theta functions parametrized by
U^{\operatorname{trop}}(\mathbb{Z})
. The structure coefficients of
A
in
[
GHK
]

are obtained combinatorially via the machinery of the canonical scattering diagram built from the data of relative Gromov-Witten invariants of
(Y,D)
, which counts rational curves on
Y
meeting
D
at a single point. Taking the spectrum of
A
, we get an algebraic family

\mathcal{X}:=\operatorname{Spec}(A)\rightarrow S:=\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbb{C}[\operatorname{NE}(Y)]).

In
[
GHK
]
, it is proved that the mirror family is a flat affine family of Gorenstein semi-log canonical surfaces with smooth generic fiber.
However, it is hard to give a further concrete description of what fibers in the mirror family are by simply studying the mirror algebra
A
itself.

We aim to describe the mirror family associated with a positive Looijenga pair
(Y,D)
as the universal family of deformations of
U
plus some extra data. To achieve this goal, we first give a canonical, fiberwise compactification of the mirror family to obtain a family of pairs,
(\bar{\mathcal{X}},\mathcal{D})\rightarrow S
. We will be interested in studying the restriction of this family to the algebraic torus
T_{Y}=\operatorname{Pic}(Y)\otimes\mathbb{G}_{m}\subset S
. Over the torus, each fiber,
(\bar{\mathcal{X}}_{t},\mathcal{D}_{t})
, is a pair where
\bar{\mathcal{X}}_{t}
is a projective rational surface with at worst du Val singularity and smooth along
\mathcal{D}_{t}
, and
\mathcal{D}_{t}\in|-K_{\bar{\mathcal{X}}_{t}}|
is a rational singular nodal curve. Naturally, we would like to know what surface pairs appear as fibers in this family. To this end, we are able to realize our family as the universal family of, what we call,
generalized marked pairs
. The moduli functor in question is closely related to those considered in
[
GHK12
]
, which gives moduli stacks for Looijenga pairs (with and without markings). The main difference in our work is that we have to account for certain types of singular surfaces. We briefly describe the objects that our mirror family will parametrize.

Definition 1.2
.

A
generalized pair
is a pair
(Y,D)
where
Y
is a projective rational surface,
D\in|-K_{Y}|
is either an irreducible rational nodal curve or a cycle of
n\geq 2
smooth rational curves,
Y
is smooth along
D
and
Y\setminus D
is an affine surface with at worst du Val singularities.

In particular, if
(X,\tilde{D})
is the minimal resolution of a generalized pair
(Y,D)
together with the strict transform
\tilde{D}
of
D
,
\tilde{D}
is anti-canonical. Then,
(X,\tilde{D})
is a positive Looijenga pair.

We will then decorate a generalized pair with two types of markings.

Definition 1.3
.

Given a generalized pair
(Y,D)
, a
marking
of
D
is a choice of point
p_{i}\in D_{i}^{\circ}
for each
i
, where
D_{i}^{\circ}
is the intersection of
D_{i}
with the smooth locus of
D
.

Definition 1.4
.

An
internal
(-2)-
curve
on a Looijenga pair
(Y,D)
means a smooth rational curve of self-intersection -2 disjoint from
D
. A pair
(Y,D)
is called
generic
if
Y
has no internal
-2
curves.

Definition 1.5
.

Suppose the minimal resolution of
Y
is given by
f:X\rightarrow Y
. Then, define

R(Y):=\{\delta=\sum a_{i}\delta_{i}\in\operatorname{Pic}(X):\delta_{i}\text{ is a (-2)-curve}\,\text{contracted by}f\}

and let
I(Y)=R(Y)^{\perp}
.

Definition 1.6
.

Fix a generic Looijenga pair
(Y_{0},D)
. A
marking of

\operatorname{Pic}(Y)

by

\operatorname{Pic}(Y_{0})
is an embedding
\mu:I(Y)\rightarrow\operatorname{Pic}(Y_{0})
which extends to an isometry
\tilde{\mu}:\operatorname{Pic}(X)\rightarrow\operatorname{Pic}(Y_{0})
where
X\rightarrow Y
is the minimal resolution of
Y
and
\tilde{\mu}
is a marking as in Definition
2.8
.

The objects that our mirror family will parametrize are then given by the following objects.

Definition 1.7
.

A
generalized marked pair
is a tuple
((Y,D),p_{i},\mu)
such that
(Y,D)
is a generalized pair,
p_{i}
is a marking of the boundary and
\mu
is a marking of
\operatorname{Pic}(Y)
. We say
p_{i}
together with
\mu
gives a
marking of

(Y,D)
.

Now, we give the second main theorem of this paper which leads to Theorem
1.1
:

Theorem 1.8
.

Given a positive Looijenga pair
(Y,D)
and its mirror family
\mathcal{X}\rightarrow S
, there exists a canonical compactification of
\mathcal{X}
to a family of surface pairs
(\bar{\mathcal{X}},\mathcal{D})\rightarrow S
such that the restriction to the algebraic torus
T_{Y}=\operatorname{Pic}(Y)\otimes\mathbb{G}_{m}
is a family
(\bar{\mathcal{X}}\mid_{T_{Y}},\mathcal{D}\mid_{T_{Y}})
of generalized pairs deformation equivalent to
(Y,D)
. Furthermore,
(\bar{\mathcal{X}}\mid_{T_{Y}},\mathcal{D}\mid_{T_{Y}})
comes with a natural marking
p_{i}
of the boundary
\mathcal{D}
and a marking
\mu
of the Picard group by
\operatorname{Pic}(Y)
such that the resulting marked family,
((\bar{\mathcal{X}}\mid_{T_{Y}},\mathcal{D}\mid_{T_{Y}}),p_{i},\mu)
, is isomorphic to the universal family of generalized marked pairs.

Now, given a smooth affine log Calabi-Yau surface
U
, we fix a Looijenga pair
(Y,D)
that compactifies
U
and a marking of
D
. By Proposition 4.10 (cf. Proposition
7.2
) of
[
HKY
]
, the structure constants of the mirror algebra
A
associated with
(Y,D)
can be equivalently given by the geometric construction of
[
KY
]
. By Theorem
1.8
,
(Y,D)
will appear in the canonically compactified mirror family. So, to get a basis of theta functions for
H^{0}(U,\mathcal{O}_{U})
, it suffices to specialize the coefficients of
A
. However, the identification of
(Y,D)
with a fiber of the compactified mirror family is not unique. As a result, the structure constants of
H^{0}(U,\mathcal{O}_{U})
we get by specializing coefficients depend on the identification we choose. However, once we fix a marking of
D
, we will fix the set of basis elements of theta functions. Thus, we are able to prove Theorem
1.1
and the following theorem, whose detailed proofs are carried out in Section
7
:

Theorem 1.9
.

Let
U
be a smooth affine log Calabi-Yau surface with maximal boundary. Fix a Looijenga pair
(Y,D)
that compactifies
U
and a marking of
D
. Then the decomposition given in
1.1
has a canonical basis of theta functions such that the structure constants for multiplication of theta functions are given by the geometric construction in
[
KY
]
. Different choices of compactifications and markings will only result in the rescaling of basis elements and not affect the decomposition in
1.1
.

The structure constants of the mirror algebra
A
in the construction of
[
KY
]
are given by naive counts of non-archimedean analytic discs in the Berkovich analytification
[
Ber90
]
of
U
. In particular, the structure coefficients of
A
are actually in
\mathbb{N}[\operatorname{NE}(Y)]
. We refer readers to Section
7
for the background of
[
KY
]
and Section 1.1 of
[
KY
]
for the technical definition of the structure constants of mirror algebras.

We want to emphasize that the structure constants of the multiplication rule of theta functions in Theorem
1.9
can be constructed through other geometric approaches. As a special instance of the more general result in
[
Man19
]
, the structure constants of theta functions for smooth affine log Calabi-Yau surfaces can be given the geometric interpretation as descendant log Gromov-Witten invariants. Another more systematic approach is that of punctured Gromov-Witten invariants. Originally announced in
[
GS19
]
and realized in
[
GS19
]
and
[
GS21
]
, the authors construct the mirror of a log Calabi-Yau pair
(X,D)
as the spectrum of a degree zero relative quantum cohomology ring for
(X,D)
. The definition of the ring and the corresponding structure constants use punctured Gromov-Witten invariants, as developed in
[
ACGS20
]
. Furthermore, Example 3.14 of
[
GS21
]
shows that the canonical wall structure in
[
GS21
]
associated with a Looijenga pair agrees with that of
[
GHK
]
and Theorem C of
[
GS21
]
shows that mirror families constructed in
[
GS21
]
agree with those in
[
GS19
]
, where structure constants of mirror algebras are constructed by the punctured Gromov-Witten invariants of
[
ACGS20
]
.

1.2.
Connections to other works.

1.2.1.
Tropicalization of theta functions in dimension 2

In
[
Man16
]
, the tropicalization of theta functions of an affine log Calabi-Yau surface is studied and used to generalize the dual pairing between the character lattice and cocharacter lattice of an algebraic torus. Moreover,
[
Man16
]
generalizes the notion of Newton polytopes, Minkowski sums, and finite Fourier series expansions in toric geometry for regular functions on an affine log Calabi-Yau surface. It would be interesting to generalize techniques of
[
Man16
]
and Section
4
of the current work to the study of higher dimensional log Calabi-Yau varieties.

1.2.2.
Theta functions in algebraic geometry

Generalized from classical theta functions of Abelian varieties, theta functions give canonical coordinates on moduli spaces of (log) Calabi-Yau varieties and therefore have a deep reach into moduli and deformation problems in classical algebraic geometry. Theorem
7.3
and Theorem
1.9
in the present work give an instance of the existence of theta functions on classical objects in algebraic geometry. For some explicit equations for mirror families of del Pezzo surface pairs where the boundary
D
is maximally degenerate, see
[
Bar18
,
GHKS
]
. Other examples of applications of mirror symmetry and theta functions to algebraic geometry include the proof of Looijenga’s conjecture on smoothability of 2-dimensional cusp singularities
[
GHK
]
, theta functions and canonical compactifications of moduli spaces of polarized K3 surfaces
[
GHKS
]
and the study of unirationality of Kollár-Shepherd-Barron-Alexeev moduli spaces of stable pairs
[
HKY
]
.

1.2.3.
The symplectic cohomology of smooth affine log Calabi-Yau surfaces.

In this paper, we do not justify the use of the adjective ‘mirror’ before ‘family’, i.e., we do not prove how the family is the mirror family to the original Looijenga pair in the sense of Homological Mirror Symmetry. In the initial arXiv version of
[
GHK
]
, a series of HMS conjectures are formulated for the mirror family. Since then, there has been significant progress toward proving these conjectures.

One main HMS conjecture in
[
GHK
]
states that the wrapped Fukaya category of a smooth affine log Calabi-Yau surface
U
, viewed as a symplectic manifold with a choice of symplectic form, should be equivalent to the bounded derived category of coherent of its corresponding mirror in the mirror family. Under this conjecture, the Hochschild cohomology of the two categories is isomorphic and in particular, the degree zero part of the symplectic cohomology
SH^{0}(U)
of
U
will be isomorphic to the ring of regular functions of its mirror. Theorem
7.3
and Theorem
1.9
of the present work then imply that
SH^{0}(U)
should be equipped with a canonical basis as a vector space.
[
Pa15
]
computed
SH^{0}(U)
and showed that it is equipped with a basis parametrized by
U^{\rm{trop}}(\mathbb{Z})
.

1.3.
Outline of the paper.

The paper starts off with a review of notions pertaining to Looijenga pairs, namely markings, marked periods, and the Torelli theorem, as in
[
GHK12
]
. We then modify those constructions to allow du Val singularities on
Y
and provide an adapted Torelli theorem for the new class of objects, which we call generalized marked pairs. The moduli space and universal family are made explicit as well.

After a review of the mirror construction made in
[
GHK
]
, we look at how to construct compactifications of the mirror families. This is carried out in Section
4
. Similar to how a convex polygon in a lattice gives rise to a polarized toric variety, the mirror family is compactified using a more general notion of a convex polygon, which no longer lives in a lattice, but in an integral affine manifold, the tropicalization of
U=Y\setminus D
. Once the compactification is given, we then investigate the modular meaning of this family. It ends up that the compactified family can be realized as a family of generalized marked pairs, as defined in Subsection
2.1
.

In order to make this identification, two problems must be addressed. The first is to determine the deformation type of the family, and the second is to compute the marked periods of the compactified mirror family. To this end, modern techniques from mirror symmetry and tropical geometry are used to construct curves that correspond to exceptional curves of a toric model of
(Y,D)
and to compute their corresponding periods. In doing so, we are able to determine the deformation type of the family along the way. The fibers in the family are deformation equivalent to the original pair,
(Y,D)
. This is consistent with SYZ mirror symmetry where mirrors are given by dual torus fibrations. In this perspective, the compactified mirror should be diffeomorphic to the original pair by Poincaré Duality in dimension
2
. The deformation type and period computations are carried out in Section
6
. The final result is that when restricted over the algebraic torus, the marked period map to
T_{Y}\simeq\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Pic}(Y),\mathbb{G}_{m})
is the isomorphism (except for one special case, it is actually the identity map). The work done in these sections uses results from
[
RS19
]
. This readily gives us the interpretation of our compactified family as the universal family.

In Section
7
, we prove Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.9
.

2.
Periods for Looijenga Pairs

Definition 2.1
.

A
Looijenga pair
is a pair
(Y,D)
where
Y
is a smooth projective surface with
D\in|-K_{Y}|
a reduced nodal curve. We write
D=D_{1}+..+D_{n}
where
D_{i}
is an irreducible component of
D
. Denote
U:=Y\setminus D
, which has a holomorphic symplectic
2
-form
\omega
, unique up to scaling, with simple poles along
D
, making
U
a log Calabi-Yau surface.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
(\bar{Y},\bar{D})
be a Looijenga pair given by a smooth projective toric surface and its toric boundary. Let
\pi:Y\rightarrow\bar{Y}
be the blowup of
\bar{Y}
at a finite number of smooth points of
\bar{D}
and let
D
be the strict transform of
\bar{D}
. Then
(Y,D)
is also a Looijenga pair and we call
(\bar{Y},\bar{D})
a
toric model
for
(Y,D)
.

For a Looijenga pair
(Y,D)
, a blowup at a point in
D
is a
toric blowup
if the blowup point is a node. Otherwise, it is called a
non-toric blowup
. Many constructions we consider are not affected by toric blowups. For convenience, we will use the following proposition:

Proposition 2.3

(Proposition
1.3
,
[
GHK
]
)
.

Every Looijenga pair
(Y,D)
has a toric blowup
(Y^{\prime},D^{\prime})
such that
(Y^{\prime},D^{\prime})
has a toric model.

We now cover the notion of periods for Looijenga pairs. Fix a Looijenga pair
(Y,D)
and let

D^{\perp}:=\{\alpha\in\operatorname{Pic}(Y):\alpha\cdot[D_{i}]=0,\text{ for all }i\}.

We have an exact sequence

0\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow H_{2}(U,\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow D^{\perp}.

The kernel of the above exact sequence is generated by the homology class
\Gamma
of a real 2-torus in
U
. In this paper, we always assume that we normalize the holomorphic volume form
\omega
of
U
so that

\int_{\Gamma}\omega=(2\pi i)^{2}.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
T_{(D^{\perp})^{*}}=\operatorname{Hom}(D^{\perp},\mathbb{G}_{m})
. The
period point

\varphi_{Y}\in T_{(D^{\perp})^{*}}
of
(Y,D)
is the homomorphism given by

\varphi_{Y}:D^{\perp}\rightarrow\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(D)=\mathbb{G}_{m},\hskip 14.22636ptL\mapsto L|_{D}.

In justification of calling these homomorphisms periods, the mixed Hodge structure of
H^{2}(Y\setminus D)
is classified by the period (see
[
F84
]
). Briefly, given
\gamma\in D^{\perp}
,
\gamma
can be represented by a class
\Sigma=\Sigma_{k}\pm[C_{k}]
where
[C_{k}]
are smooth curves in
Y
meeting
D
transversally at distinct points of the smooth locus of
D
. A cycle,
\Sigma^{\prime}
, that is contained in
U
and homologous to
\Sigma
, can be formed and used to define the period map
\varphi_{Y}^{\prime}:D^{\perp}\rightarrow\mathbb{G}_{m}
by

\gamma\mapsto\exp\left(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Sigma^{\prime}}\omega\right).

The mixed Hodge structures on
H^{2}(U)
are indeed classified by such integrals. Then, Proposition
3.12
of
[
F84
]
shows that these integrals are equal to the periods as defined in Definition
2.4
.

We will use an extended notion of the period point, which is that of a marked period point.

Definition 2.5
.

For a Looijenga pair
(Y,D)
, a marking of the boundary is a choice of points
p_{i}\in D_{i}^{\circ}
, for each
i
, where
D_{i}^{\circ}
is the intersection of
D_{i}
with the smooth locus of
D
.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
(Y,D)
be a Looijenga pair.

(1)

\{x\in\operatorname{Pic}(Y)_{\mathbb{R}}:x^{2}>0\}
is a cone with two connected components, one of which contains all ample classes. We denote this component by
C^{+}
.

(2)

For an ample class
H
, let
\tilde{M}
be the set of classes
E
such that
E^{2}=K_{Y}\cdot E=-1
and
E\cdot H>0
. Then
C^{++}
is defined by the inequalities
x\cdot E\geq 0
for all
E\in\tilde{M}
.

(3)

Let
C_{D}^{++}\subset C^{++}
be the subcone where additionally
x\cdot\left[D_{i}\right]\geq 0
for all
i
.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
(Y,D)
be a Looijenga pair.

(1)

The roots
\Phi\subset\operatorname{Pic}(Y)
are classes in
D^{\perp}
with square
-2
.

(2)

\Delta_{Y}\subset\operatorname{Pic}(Y)
is the set of internal
(-2)
-curves. Here, an internal curve is a smooth rational curve disjoint from
D
with self-intersection
-2
.

(3)

Let
\Phi_{Y}\subset\Phi\subset\operatorname{Pic}(Y)
be the subset of roots,
\alpha
, with
\varphi_{Y}(\alpha)=1
. Note that
\Delta_{Y}\subset\Phi_{Y}\subset\Phi
.

(4)

W\subset\operatorname{Aut}(\operatorname{Pic}(Y))
is the subgroup generated by reflections
\{r_{\alpha}:\alpha\in\Phi\}
where

r_{\alpha}(\beta)=\beta+\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle\alpha.

Let
W_{Y}\subset W
be the subgroup generated by
\{r_{\alpha}:\alpha\in\Delta_{Y}\}
.

By Proposition 3.4 of
[
GHK12
]
, we have
\Phi_{Y}=W_{Y}\cdot\Delta_{Y}
.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
(Y,D)
be a Looijenga pair and fix a generic Looijenga pair
(Y_{0},D)
. Then a marking of
\operatorname{Pic}(Y)
by
\operatorname{Pic}(Y_{0})
is an isomorphism of lattices
\mu:\operatorname{Pic}(Y)\rightarrow\operatorname{Pic}(Y_{0})
such that
\mu([D_{i}])=[D_{i}]
for each
i
and
\mu(C^{++})=C^{++}
.

The marked period can now be defined as follows.

Definition 2.9
.

Given a tuple
((Y,D),p_{i},\mu)
, the
marked period point
is the homomorphism
\varphi_{((Y,D),p_{i},\mu)}\in\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Pic}(Y_{0}),\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(D))
given by

\varphi(L):=(\mu^{-1}(L)|_{D})^{-1}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{D}\left(\sum(L\cdot D_{i})p_{i}\right).

Definition 2.10
.

Two marked Looijenga pairs
((Y,D),p_{i},\mu)
and
((Y^{\prime},D^{\prime}),p^{\prime}_{i},\mu^{\prime})
are said to be isomorphic if there is an isomorphism
f:Y\rightarrow Y^{\prime}
such that
f(D_{i})=D^{\prime}_{i}
,
f(p_{i})=f(p_{i}^{\prime})
and the induced map
f^{*}:\operatorname{Pic}(Y^{\prime})\rightarrow\operatorname{Pic}(Y)
satisfies
f^{*}=\mu^{-1}\circ\mu^{\prime}
.

The following is a wide known result that we reproduce to make future computations explicit.

Lemma 2.11

(Lemma
1.6
,
[
Fr2
]
and Lemma
2.1

2.1.
Generalized marked pairs

The fibers of the mirror family are not necessarily smooth and this section presents adjustments to the definition of a Looijenga pair in a way that allows for the singularities that appear. The construction here is similar to the situation for the moduli space of polarized K3 surfaces with simple surface singularities (see
[
Mor
]
).

Assume that the Looijenga pair
(Y,D)
is positive. As in Lemma
6.9
of
[
GHK
]
, being positive is equivalent to the existence of
c_{i}
such that the divisor
-(K_{Y}+\sum c_{i}D_{i})
is big and nef. It follows that for

sufficiently large, the linear system

defines a birational morphism with the exceptional locus given by all curves disjoint from

(which is a configuration of
-2
curves). After contracting the internal curves, we get a pair
(Y^{\prime},D)
where

has du Val singularities and the push forward of
-(K_{Y}+\sum c_{i}D_{i})
is supported on

and is ample. Motivated by this, we now consider generalized pairs
(Y,D)
as in Definition
1.2
.

Recall now Definition
1.7
and Definition
1.5
. Suppose
(Y,D)
is a generalized pair such that
Y

has a minimal resolution given by

and a marking of
\operatorname{Pic}
given by

has no internal

Definition 2.15
.

An isomorphism of generalized marked pairs

and

and

\tilde{f}^{*}=\mu^{-1}\circ\mu^{\prime}

The marked period point of a generalized marked pair

where the right-hand side is as defined in Definition

.

Remark 2.17
.

The definition of the marked period does not depend on the choice of extension,
\tilde{\mu}
. Fix a generalized mark pair
((Y,D),p_{i},\mu)
with minimal resolution

and extensions
\tilde{\mu},\tilde{\mu^{\prime}}:\operatorname{Pic}(X)\rightarrow\operatorname{Pic}(Y_{0})

and they only differ on

is generated by irreducible internal (-2)-curves on

Proposition 2.18

(Global Torelli for Generalized Marked Pairs)
.

Two generalized marked pairs,
((Y_{1},D_{1}),p_{1},\mu_{1})
and
((Y_{2},D_{2}),p_{2},\mu_{2})

\varphi_{((Y_{1},D_{1}),p_{1},\mu_{1})}=\varphi_{((Y_{2},D_{2}),p_{2},\mu_{2})}
.

Proof.

Let
f_{i}:X_{i}\rightarrow Y_{i}
be the minimal resolution of
Y_{i}
. If the two marked generalized marked pairs are isomorphic, then it is clear that the marked periods must coincide.

Suppose the two generalized marked pairs have the same period. Consider the induced isometry on
I(Y_{i})^{\perp}
which gives an isomorphism of root systems. Let
\Phi
be the set of roots of
\operatorname{Pic}(Y_{0})
. Let
\Phi^{\prime}=\{\delta\mid\delta\in\Phi,\,\varphi(\delta)=1\}
. By Proposition 3.4 of
[
GHK12
]
,
\Phi^{\prime}
is contained in
\tilde{\mu_{i}}(R({Y_{i}}))
.
By Lemma
2.15
and Theorem
3.2
of
[
GHK12
]
, each
\tilde{\mu_{i}}(\operatorname{Nef}(X_{i}))
is the closure of a connected component of

and the action of the Weyl group

on the set of connected components of

is transitive.
Thus, there must exist

such that

. Composing an isometry with

is still an isometry, and

does not affect the isometry on the level of

keeps the marked period points for

((Y_{i},D_{i}),p_{i},\mu_{i})(i=1,2)

3.
Review of Mirror Symmetry for Log Calabi-Yau Surfaces

We review notions and results related to the construction of the mirror family in
[
GHK
]
. Let
(Y,D)
be a positive Looijenga pair and denote

. To each pair, we will associate to it an integral affine manifold with singularities.

Definition 3.1
.

An
integral affine manifold
is a real manifold such that the transition functions are integral affine.

The spaces we consider do not have a global integral affine structure, so we consider integral affine manifolds with singularities. These are real manifolds
B
such that there is an open subset
B_{0}\subset B
that is an integral affine manifold such that the singular locus of
B
(where the affine structure is not defined) given by
B\setminus B_{0}
is a locally finite union of closed submanifolds of codimension at least two. There is a local system
\Lambda_{B}
on
B_{0}
given by flat integral vector fields. Given any simply connected subset
\tau\subset B_{0}
, we write
\Lambda_{\tau}
for the stalk of
\Lambda
at any point of
\tau
(any two such stalks are canonically identified via parallel transport). We also use
\lambda_{\tau}
to denote the local system of flat integral vector fields on
\tau
since what we mean will be clear from the context.
We denote by
\check{\Lambda}_{B}
the local system dual to
\Lambda
.

Now, to a fixed Looijenga pair
(Y,D)
, we will associate an integral affine manifold,
B_{(Y,D)}
. Fix a lattice
M\simeq\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and denote
M_{\mathbb{R}}=M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
. Let
D=D_{1}+..+D_{n}
. For each node
p_{i,i+1}=D_{i}\cap D_{i+1}
, we take a rank
2
lattice
M_{i,i+1}
with a basis
v_{i},v_{i+1}
. Then we take the cone
\sigma_{i,i+1}\subset{M_{i,i+1}}_{\mathbb{R}}
generated by the two basis vectors. We then form
B_{(Y,D)}
by gluing the
\sigma_{i,i+1}
to
\sigma_{i-1,i}
along the ray
\rho_{i}
. Note that
B_{(Y,D)}
is homeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Now we define the affine structure on
B_{(Y,D)}\setminus\{0\}
. Let
U_{i}
be the interior of
\sigma_{i,i+1}\cup\sigma_{i-1,i}
. Then we define a chart
\psi_{i}:U_{i}\rightarrow M_{\mathbb{R}}
given by:

\psi_{i}(v_{i-1})=(1,0),\psi_{i}(v_{i})=(0,1),\psi_{i}(v_{i+1})=(-1,-D_{i}^{2}).

Remark 3.2
.

Note that the above construction even makes sense in the case where

, i.e., when the anticanonical cycle

is an irreducible nodal curve. In this case, we only have one cone
\sigma_{1,1}
whose two sides are identified. The subtle point is how we define the integral affine charts. Instead of using

to

When

is clear from context, we will often drop the subscript and just denote the associated manifold

. Note that if the pair

is toric, the affine structure extends over the origin (see Lemma

Denote by

,
P(W)=P(W^{\prime})
. The statements that

is bounded, integral, and contains

in its interior is straightforward. By Lemma
4.32
and the fact that
\Sigma_{(Y^{\prime},D^{\prime})}
is a refinement of

, near each vertex of

, the chopping down of

gives a convex polygon. Therefore,

itself is convex. That

is nonsingular follows from the parallel construction of the polygon and the fact that wedge products between integral vector fields are preserved under parallel transport.
∎

Thus, we have shown that for each
D
-ample Weil divisor

, we get a bounded, nonsingular, integral, convex polygon

which then gives a compactification
\bar{\mathcal{X}}(P(W))\rightarrow\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{C}[\mathrm{NE}(Y)])
of the mirror family
\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{C}[\mathrm{NE}(Y)])
. As we will show in Proposition
4.36
, the compactification is actually canonical, in the sense that it is not affected by the choice of the
D
-ample Weil divisor

.

Lemma 4.34
.

Let
(Y,D)
be a positive Looijenga pair and
W
a
D
-ample Weil divisor on
Y
. Let
\pi:(Y,D)\rightarrow(Y^{\prime},D^{\prime})
be a toric blowdown and
W^{\prime}=\pi_{*}(W)
. If

is not an exceptional divisor of
\pi
, then

is the supporting half space of both
P(W)
and
P(W^{\prime})
. Moreover, in this case, if we let

and

be the edges lying on

in

and

respectively, then

is contained in

.

Proof.

Write
\pi^{*}(W^{\prime})=\sum_{i}a^{\prime}_{i}D_{i}
. We want to show that
a_{i}=a^{\prime}_{i}
if
D_{i}
is not an exceptional divisor and
a^{\prime}_{i}>a_{i}
if

is. It suffices to prove this for the case where
\pi
has only one exceptional divisor

. In this case,

for

is trivial. Since

\pi^{*}(W^{\prime})\cdot D_{k}=-a^{\prime}_{k}+a_{k-1}+a_{k+1}=0<W\cdot D_{k}=-a_{k}+a_{k-1}+a_{k+1},

we have
a_{k}<a^{\prime}_{k}
.

By Proposition
4.33
, for each

,

is a supporting half space of

and it is also a supporting half space for

if and only if

is
not
an exceptional divisor of
\pi
. If this is the case, by the numerical condition we just proved in the previous paragraph, it is easy to see that

is contained in

.
∎

Let
E\subset P(W)
be an edge and denote the corresponding boundary divisor by

be the complement of the nodes of

Let

When

When

Proposition 4.36

Let

and

When

Definition 4.37
.

We call the compactification of the mirror family using the parallel polygon associated with a

Consider the restriction of the canonical compactification

By Proposition

4.4.1.
Parallel configurations.

Now, let us introduce the notion of parallel configurations.

Definition 4.40
.

Given a convex polygon
0\in F\subset B_{(Y,D)}
and an edge
L
of
F
, the
forward ray

R_{L}
of
L
is the ray we get by extending
L
in the forward direction (given by the canonical orientation of
L
).
We say
F
has a
correct corner
in
\sigma_{i,i+1}
if there is a unique vertex
v\in F
such that
v
is in the interior of
\sigma_{i,i+1}
,
F
has no vertex on
\rho_{i}
or
\rho_{i+1}
, the forward ray of one edge at
v
goes to infinity in
\sigma_{i+1,i+2}
parallel to
\rho_{i+2}
, and the forward ray of the other edge at
v
goes to infinity in
\sigma_{i,i+1}
parallel to
\rho_{i+1}
.

Definition 4.41
.

Given a parallel polygon
0\in P(W)\subset B_{(Y,D)}
, we say
P(W)
is a
parallel configuration
for
(Y,D)
if
P(W)
has a correct corner in
\sigma_{i,i+1}
for each
1\leq i\leq n
.

Proposition 4.42
.

Let
(Y,D)
be positive,
n\geq 2
,
D_{i}^{2}\geq 0
for some
i
. Then,
(Y,D)
has a parallel configuration.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, suppose
D_{1}^{2}\geq 0
. Then, using the development map, we see that any immersed line
L
goes to infinity parallel to
\rho_{2}
with positive lattice distance
a_{2}
from
\rho_{2}
is actually an embedded line on
B
contained in the union
\sigma_{1,2}\cup\sigma_{n,1}
. We set
a_{2}=1
. Suppose we have defined
a_{i}
, then we set
a_{i+1}=1
if
-D_{i}^{2}\leq 0
and
a_{i+1}=(-D_{i}^{2})a_{i}+1
if
-D_{i}^{2}>0
. All our indices are modulo
n
. Then,
W=\sum_{i}a_{i}D_{i}
is a
D
-ample divisor and using the standard chart for
\sigma_{i-1,i}\cup\sigma_{i,i+1}
, it is straightforward to see that
P(W)
has a correct corner in
\sigma_{i-1,i}
for each
i
. Thus,
P(W)
is a parallel configuration for
(Y,D)
.
∎

Proposition 4.43
.

Let
(Y,D)
be positive with
n=1
. Then there exists a parallel configuration for
(Y,D)
if and only if
D^{2}\geq 2
.

Proof.

A polygon with a parallel configuration for
(Y,D)
is a
1
-gon so it must be the half space
Z(L)
for some
L
. Let
L
be an immersed line with escape ray
\rho_{1}
. Then by considering the developing map,
Z(L)
has the correct corner if and only if
D_{1}^{2}\geq 2
.
∎

Lemma 4.44
.

If
(Y,D)
is positive and
D_{i}^{2}\neq-1
for all
i
, then
D_{j}^{2}\geq 0
for some
j
.

Proof.

Suppose that
(Y,D)
is positive and
D_{i}^{2}\leq-2
for all
i
. By the positivity, there exists positive integers
b_{i}
such that
\left(\sum b_{i}D_{i}\right)\cdot D_{j}>0
for all
j
. Then for every
j
, we have
a_{j+1}+a_{j-1}>-a_{j}D_{j}^{2}
. Summing up all such inequalities, we get

2\sum a_{j}>\sum a_{j}|D_{j}|^{2}.

However, since
|D_{j}|^{2}\geq 2
for all
j
, no such collection of
a_{i}
’s can exist.
∎

Lemma 4.45
.

Let
(Y,D)
be a positive pair. Then, there exists a toric blow-down
(Y,D)\rightarrow(Y^{\prime},D^{\prime})
such that either
(Y^{\prime},D^{\prime})
has a parallel configuration or
(Y^{\prime},D^{\prime})
is a del Pezzo surface of degree
1
together with an irreducible anti-canonical cycle.

Proof.

Suppose
n\geq 2
. If there exists
i
such that
D_{i}^{2}\geq 0
, then by Proposition
4.42
, there exists a parallel configuration. Otherwise,
D_{j}^{2}<0
for all
j
and by Lemma

, there exists

such that
D_{i}^{2}=-1
. We then blowdown
D_{i}
to obtain a new boundary,

. If
D^{\prime}
has only one component, then
D^{\prime 2}>0
by positivity. By Proposition
4.43
, either
(Y^{\prime},D^{\prime})
has a parallel configuration or is the case of the degree
1
del Pezzo surface together with an irreducible anti-canonical cycle. If there is more than one component in

, then again, there is either a component such that
D^{\prime 2}_{i}\geq 0
, in which case there is a parallel configuration, or there is a component we can blow down. We continue to blow down until the pair we get either has a parallel configuration or is the degree

del Pezzo surface together with an irreducible anti-canonical cycle.

∎

While many pairs in consideration do have a parallel configuration, some well-known pairs do not. For example, a cubic surface obtained by blowing up toric

twice on each boundary gives a pair

We can now prove the main statement, given in Theorem

Proof of Theorem

The compactification of the family comes from the choice of a Weil divisor

Remark 7.4
.

In the mirror symmetry program for affine log Calabi-Yau varieties (with maximal boundary), it is expected that
H^{0}(U,\mathcal{O}_{U})
has a vector space basis parametrized by the integer tropical points of its mirror and the structure constants of
H^{0}(U,\mathcal{O}_{U})
, as an algebra, should be given by the enumerative geometry of its mirror. Here, the surprising fact is that for a smooth affine log Calabi-Yau surface with maximal boundary, it appears as a fiber in its own mirror family. However, since dual Lagrangian torus fibrations in dimension 2 are topologically equivalent, this is consistent with the SYZ mirror symmetry picture.

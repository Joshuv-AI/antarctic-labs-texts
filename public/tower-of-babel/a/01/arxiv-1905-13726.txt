Remark 1.2
.

We warn the reader, however, that while the qualitative analysis of the Allen–Cahn functionals does not depend on the precise choice of the double-well potential

To get some idea of the role played by gauge invariance, note that unit sections of a Hermitian line bundle are indistinguishable up to change of gauge (when no preferred connection has been selected), and for a given unit section

1.1.
Organization of the paper

In Section
2
we fix notation and record some basic properties satisfied by critical pairs
(u_{\epsilon},\nabla_{\epsilon})
for the energies
E_{\epsilon}
.

In Section
3

, and use them to establish an initial rough estimate on

in the Allen–Cahn setting. Under suitable assumptions on the curvature of

, the fact that

follows quickly from the aforementioned Bochner identities and the maximum principle. Without the curvature assumptions, some nontrivial additional work is required to obtain the pointwise upper bound

. This estimate is the key ingredient to obtain the sharp

-monotonicity of the energy.

In Section
4
we derive the stationarity equation for inner variations, from which an obvious
(n-4)
-monotonicity property of the energy follows rather immediately. Using our rough initial bounds on
\xi_{\epsilon}
from Section
3
, we deduce an intermediate

-monotonicity; we use this to reach the pointwise bound
\xi_{\epsilon}\leq C(M,E_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla))
, from which we finally infer the sharp
(n-2)
-monotonicity.

In Section
5
we show that, similar to the Allen–Cahn setting, the energy density

decays exponentially away from the set
u^{-1}(0)
—more precisely, away from
\{|u|^{2}\geq 1-\beta_{d}\}
for some
\beta_{d}
independent of
\epsilon

Section
6
, which constitutes the main part of the paper, contains an initial description of the limiting varifold, showing that it is stationary,
(n-2)
-rectifiable, and has a lower density bound on the support. Then we establish its integrality with a blow-up analysis, employing the estimates from the preceding sections to reduce the problem to a statement for entire planar solutions, already contained in the work of Jaffe and Taubes
[
21
]
.
We then use this analysis to show that the level sets
u_{\epsilon}^{-1}(0)
converge to the support of

in the Hausdorff topology, and conclude the section with a discussion of the asymptotics for the curvature forms
\frac{1}{2\pi}F_{\nabla_{\epsilon}}
.

In Section
7

satisfies a variant of the Palais–Smale property on suitable function spaces, allowing us to produce critical points via classical min-max methods. We provide a variational construction to get nontrivial critical points satisfying the assumptions of our main theorem, with energy bounded from above and below, both for nontrivial and trivial line bundles.

Finally, the Appendix addresses the issue of obtaining regularity of critical points, as obtained from Section
7
, when they are read in a local or global Coulomb gauge.

In computing inner products for two-forms, we follow the convention

\displaystyle|\omega|^{2}=\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq n}\omega(e_{j},e_{k})^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}\omega(e_{j},e_{k})^{2}

It is easy to check that the smooth pair

\displaystyle\nabla^{*}\nabla u

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\epsilon^{2}}(1-|u|^{2})u,

\displaystyle\epsilon^{2}d^{*}\omega

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla u,iu\rangle.

Note that, in our convention, the adjoint to

(d^{*}\omega)(e_{k})=-\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\nabla_{e_{j}}\omega)(e_{j},e_{k}).

Since the curvature form

\displaystyle\epsilon^{2}(\Delta_{H}\omega)(e_{j},e_{k})

\displaystyle=(d\langle\nabla u,iu\rangle)(e_{j},e_{k})

) and (
2.4
), we apply the standard Bochner–Weitzenböck formulas to obtain some identities which will play a central role in our analysis. For the curvature two-form
\omega
, it will be useful to record the Bochner identity

(3.1)

\Delta\frac{1}{2}|\omega|^{2}=|D\omega|^{2}+\epsilon^{-2}(|u|^{2}|\omega|^{2}-\langle\psi(u),\omega\rangle)+\mathcal{R}_{2}(\omega,\omega),

where
\mathcal{R}_{2}
denotes the Weitzenböck curvature operator for two-forms on the base Riemannian manifold
M
.
For any
\delta>0
we have

\displaystyle(|\omega|^{2}+\delta^{2})^{1/2}\Delta(|\omega|^{2}+\delta^{2})^{1/2}+|D|\omega||^{2}\geq\Delta\frac{1}{2}(|\omega|^{2}+\delta^{2})=\Delta\frac{1}{2}|\omega|^{2}.

Since
|D|\omega||^{2}\leq|D\omega|^{2}
, (
3.1
) implies

\displaystyle(|\omega|^{2}+\delta^{2})^{1/2}\Delta(|\omega|^{2}+\delta^{2})^{1/2}\geq\epsilon^{-2}(|u|^{2}|\omega|^{2}-\langle\psi(u),\omega\rangle)+\mathcal{R}_{2}(\omega,\omega).

Dividing by
(|\omega|^{2}+\delta^{2})^{1/2}
and letting
\delta\to 0
, we obtain

(3.2)

\Delta|\omega|\geq\epsilon^{-2}(|u|^{2}|\omega|-|\psi(u)|)-|\mathcal{R}_{2}^{-}||\omega|,

in the distributional sense (and classically on
\{|\omega|>0\}
).
Note that, by (
2.7
), the relation (
3.2
) also gives us the cruder subequation

(3.3)

\Delta|\omega|\geq\epsilon^{-2}|u|^{2}|\omega|-\epsilon^{-2}|\nabla u|^{2}-|\mathcal{R}_{2}^{-}||\omega|.

For the modulus
|u|^{2}
of the Higgs field
u
, we record

(3.4)

\Delta\frac{1}{2}|u|^{2}=|\nabla u|^{2}-\frac{1}{2\epsilon^{2}}(1-|u|^{2})|u|^{2},

and observe that a simple application of the maximum principle yields the pointwise bound

|u|^{2}\leq 1\text{ on }M.

For the energy density
|\nabla u|^{2}
of the Higgs field
u
, we see that

\displaystyle\Delta\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}

\displaystyle=|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}-\langle\nabla(\nabla^{*}\nabla u),\nabla u\rangle+\langle d^{*}\omega,\langle iu,\nabla u\rangle\rangle

\displaystyle\quad-2\langle\omega,\psi(u)\rangle+\mathcal{R}_{1}(\nabla u,\nabla u)

\displaystyle=|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}-2\langle\omega,\psi(u)\rangle+\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}|\langle iu,\nabla u\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2\epsilon^{2}}(1-|u|^{2})|\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}|\langle u,\nabla u\rangle|^{2}+\mathcal{R}_{1}(\nabla u,\nabla u)

\displaystyle=|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}+\frac{1}{2\epsilon^{2}}(3|u|^{2}-1)|\nabla u|^{2}-2\langle\omega,\psi(u)\rangle+\mathcal{R}_{1}(\nabla u,\nabla u),

where at
p\in M
we let
\mathcal{R}_{1}(\nabla u,\nabla u)=\operatorname{Ric}(e_{i},e_{j})\langle\nabla_{e_{i}}u,\nabla_{e_{j}}u\rangle
and
\nabla^{2}_{e_{i},e_{j}}u=\nabla_{e_{i}}(\nabla_{e_{j}}u)
, for any local orthonormal frame
\{e_{i}\}
with
De_{i}(p)=0
.

Next, we introduce the function

(3.5)

\xi_{\epsilon}:=\epsilon|F_{\nabla}|-\frac{1}{2\epsilon}(1-|u|^{2}),

and combine (
3.3
) with (
3.4
) to see that

\displaystyle\Delta\xi_{\epsilon}

\displaystyle\geq\epsilon^{-1}|u|^{2}|\omega|-\epsilon^{-1}|\nabla u|^{2}-\epsilon|\mathcal{R}_{2}||\omega|+\epsilon^{-1}|\nabla u|^{2}-\frac{1}{2\epsilon^{3}}(1-|u|^{2})|u|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\epsilon^{-2}|u|^{2}\xi_{\epsilon}-\epsilon\|\mathcal{R}_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}|\omega|.

From a simple application of the maximum principle, we see in particular that if
\mathcal{R}_{2}>0
, then
\xi_{\epsilon}\leq 0
everywhere on
M
, and consequently (cf.
[
21
, Theorem III.8.1]
)

(3.6)

\epsilon^{2}|F_{\nabla}|^{2}\leq\frac{W(u)}{\epsilon^{2}}\text{ pointwise, provided }\mathcal{R}_{2}>0\text{ on }M.

This balancing of the Yang–Mills and potential terms,
which should be compared with Modica’s gradient estimate in the asymptotic analysis of the Allen–Cahn equations
(cf.
[
19
, Proposition 3.3]
), will play a key role in our analysis, allowing us to upgrade the obvious
{(n-4)}
-monotonicity typical of Yang–Mills–Higgs problems to the much stronger
{(n-2)}
-monotonicity
\frac{d}{dr}(r^{2-n}\int_{B_{r}}e_{\epsilon}(u_{\epsilon},\nabla_{\epsilon}))\geq 0
.

Without the positive curvature assumption, we may still employ the subequation

(3.7)

\Delta\xi_{\epsilon}\geq\frac{|u|^{2}}{\epsilon^{2}}\xi_{\epsilon}-C(M)\epsilon|F_{\nabla}|,

to obtain strong estimates for the positive part
\xi_{\epsilon}^{+}
of
\xi_{\epsilon}
. To begin, denote by
G(x,y)
the nonnegative Green’s function for the Laplacian on
M
, so that
\Delta_{x}G(x,y)=\frac{1}{\operatorname{vol}(M)}-\delta_{y}
, and set

(3.8)

h_{\epsilon}(x):=\int_{M}G(x,y)\epsilon|F_{\nabla}|(y)\,dy\geq 0,

so that

(3.9)

\Delta h_{\epsilon}(x)=\frac{1}{\operatorname{vol}(M)}\|\epsilon F_{\nabla}\|_{L^{1}}-\epsilon|F_{\nabla}|(x).

Taking
C^{\prime}
to be the constant appearing in (
3.7
), for the difference
\xi_{\epsilon}-C^{\prime}h_{\epsilon}
, we then have

\displaystyle\Delta(\xi_{\epsilon}-C^{\prime}h_{\epsilon})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{|u|^{2}}{\epsilon^{2}}(\xi_{\epsilon}-C^{\prime}h_{\epsilon})+C^{\prime}\frac{|u|^{2}}{\epsilon^{2}}h_{\epsilon}-C^{\prime}\frac{\|\epsilon F_{\nabla}\|_{L^{1}}}{\operatorname{vol(M)}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{|u|^{2}}{\epsilon^{2}}(\xi_{\epsilon}-C^{\prime}h_{\epsilon})-C^{\prime}\frac{\|\epsilon F_{\nabla}\|_{L^{1}}}{\operatorname{vol}(M)}.

Observe that the
L^{1}
norm of
\xi_{\epsilon}-C^{\prime}h_{\epsilon}
is bounded by the energy:

\displaystyle\|\xi_{\epsilon}-C^{\prime}h_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\xi_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{1}}+C(M)\|h_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\xi_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{1}}+C(M)\|\epsilon F_{\nabla}\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq C(M)E_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla)^{1/2}.

Thus, applying Moser iteration to the positive part
(\xi_{\epsilon}-C^{\prime}h_{\epsilon})^{+}
, we deduce that

(3.10)

\xi_{\epsilon}-C^{\prime}h_{\epsilon}\leq C(M)E(u,\nabla)^{1/2}.

(Where the constant
C(M)
may of course change from line to line.)

As a simple application of (
3.10
), we note that by definition (
3.8
) of
h_{\epsilon}
and the standard estimate (see, e.g.,
[
7
, Chapter 4]
)

G(x,y)\leq C(M)d(x,y)^{2-n}

if
n\geq 3
(or
G(x,y)\leq-C(M)\log(d(x,y))+C(M)
if
n=2
), we have the
L^{\infty}
estimate

\|h_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C(M)\|\epsilon F_{\nabla}\|_{L^{n-1}}.

If
n=2
, this inequality and (
3.10
) give a pointwise bound

\displaystyle\|\xi_{\epsilon}^{+}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C(M)\|\epsilon F_{\nabla}\|_{L^{1}}+C(M)E_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla)^{1/2}\leq C(M)E_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla)^{1/2}.

In the sequel, we assume
n\geq 3
and aim for a similar pointwise bound. We have

\|h_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C(M)\|\epsilon F_{\nabla}\|_{L^{n-1}}\leq C\epsilon\|F_{\nabla}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\frac{n-3}{n-1}}\|F_{\nabla}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2}{n-1}}.

Using this in (
3.10
), we compute at a maximum point for
|F_{\nabla}|
to see that

\|\epsilon F_{\nabla}\|_{L^{\infty}}-\frac{1}{2\epsilon}(1-|u|^{2})=\xi_{\epsilon}\leq C\|\epsilon F_{\nabla}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\frac{n-3}{n-1}}E_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla)^{\frac{1}{n-1}}+CE_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla)^{1/2},

and, by an application of Young’s inequality, it follows that

(1-C\delta)\|\epsilon F_{\nabla}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\frac{1}{2\epsilon}+C\delta^{\frac{3-n}{2}}E_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla)^{1/2}

for any
\delta\in(0,1)
. Taking
\delta=\epsilon^{2/n}
, we arrive at the crude preliminary estimate

\displaystyle\|\epsilon F_{\nabla}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(1-C\epsilon^{2/n})}\left(\frac{1}{2\epsilon}+C\epsilon^{3/n}\epsilon^{-1}E_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla)\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\epsilon}+\frac{\alpha(\epsilon)}{2\epsilon}(1+E_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla)^{1/2}),

where
\alpha(\epsilon)\to 0
as
\epsilon\to 0
.
Now, consider the function

f:=\epsilon|\omega|-\frac{1+\alpha(\epsilon)(1+E_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla)^{1/2})}{2\epsilon}(1-|u|^{2}).

By virtue of the preceding estimate for
\|F_{\nabla}\|_{L^{\infty}}
, we then see that

f\leq\frac{1+\alpha(\epsilon)(1+E_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla)^{1/2})}{2\epsilon}|u|^{2}

pointwise. Appealing once again to (
3.4
) and (
3.3
), we see that

\Delta f\geq\frac{|u|^{2}}{\epsilon^{2}}f-C\epsilon|F_{\nabla}|,

so at a point where
f
achieves its maximum we have

\frac{|u|^{2}}{\epsilon^{2}}f\leq C\epsilon|F_{\nabla}|\leq\frac{C(1+E_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla)^{1/2})}{\epsilon}.

On the other hand, we know that
|u|^{2}\geq\frac{\epsilon}{C(1+E_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla)^{1/2})}f
everywhere, so the preceding computations yield an estimate of the form

\frac{(\max f)^{2}}{\epsilon}\leq\frac{C(M,E_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla))}{\epsilon},

and we deduce that
f\leq C(M,E_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla))
everywhere. Putting all this together, we arrive at the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(u,\nabla)
solve (
2.4
) and (
2.5
) on a line bundle
L\to M
, and suppose
E_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla)\leq\Lambda
. Then there exists a constant
C(M,\Lambda)
and a function
\alpha(M,\Lambda,\epsilon)
, with
\alpha(\epsilon)\to 0
as
\epsilon\to 0
, such that

(3.11)

\xi_{\epsilon}\leq\alpha(\epsilon)\frac{(1-|u|^{2})}{\epsilon}+C.

In the next section, we will improve the rough preliminary estimate of Lemma
3.1
to a uniform pointwise bound of the form
\xi_{\epsilon}\leq C(M,\Lambda)
, but this will require some additional effort.

4.
Inner variations and improved monotonicity

In this section, we derive the inner variation equation for solutions of (
2.4
)–(
2.5
), and explore the scaling properties of the energy
E_{\epsilon}(u_{\epsilon},\nabla_{\epsilon})
over balls of small radius. Under the assumption that the curvature operator
\mathcal{R}_{2}
appearing in (
3.3
) is positive-definite (so that (
3.6
) holds), the analysis simplifies considerably, leading with little effort to the desired monotonicity of the
(n-2)
-energy density. Without this curvature assumption, more work is required, first building on the cruder estimates of the preceding section to obtain a uniform pointwise bound for
\xi_{\epsilon}
.

Fixing notation, with respect to a local orthonormal basis
\{e_{i}\}
for
TM
, define the
(0,2)
-tensors
\nabla u^{*}\nabla u
and
\omega^{*}\omega
by

(4.1)

\displaystyle(\nabla u^{*}\nabla u)(e_{i},e_{j})

\displaystyle:=\langle\nabla_{e_{i}}u,\nabla_{e_{j}}u\rangle,

(4.2)

\displaystyle\omega^{*}\omega(e_{i},e_{j})

\displaystyle:=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\omega(e_{i},e_{k})\omega(e_{j},e_{k}).

Note that
\operatorname{tr}(\nabla u^{*}\nabla u)=|\nabla u|^{2}
and
\operatorname{tr}(\omega^{*}\omega)=2|\omega|^{2}
. Denote by
e_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla)
the energy integrand

e_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla):=|\nabla u|^{2}+\epsilon^{2}|F_{\nabla}|^{2}+\frac{W(u)}{\epsilon^{2}}.

The fact that
d\omega=0
reads

\displaystyle D\omega(e_{i},e_{j})=D_{e_{i}}\omega(\cdot,e_{j})+D_{e_{j}}\omega(e_{i},\cdot),

where
D
is the Levi–Civita connection of
M
.
Using this identity, it is straightforward to check that

\displaystyle de_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla)

\displaystyle=2\operatorname{div}(\nabla u^{*}\nabla u)+2\langle\nabla u,\nabla^{*}\nabla u\rangle+d\frac{W(u)}{\epsilon^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+2\omega(\langle iu,\nabla u\rangle^{\#},\cdot)+2\epsilon^{2}\operatorname{div}(\omega^{*}\omega)-2\epsilon^{2}\omega((d^{*}\omega)^{\#},\cdot).

In particular, defining the stress-energy tensor
T_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla)
by

(4.3)

T_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla):=e_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla)g-2\nabla u^{*}\nabla u-2\epsilon^{2}\omega^{*}\omega,

for
(u,\nabla)
solving (
2.4
) and (
2.5
) it follows that

(4.4)

\operatorname{div}(T_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla))=0,

meaning that
\sum_{i}(D_{e_{i}}T)(e_{i},\cdot)=0
.
Integrating (
4.4
) against a vector field
X
on some domain
\Omega\subseteq M
, we arrive at the usual inner-variation equation

(4.5)

\int_{\Omega}\langle T_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla),DX\rangle=\int_{\partial\Omega}T_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla)(X,\nu),

where we identify
T_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla)
with a
(1,1)
-tensor and denote by
\nu
the outer unit normal to
\Omega
. Taking
\Omega=B_{r}(p)
to be a small geodesic ball of radius
r
about a point
p\in M
, and taking
X=\operatorname{grad}(\frac{1}{2}d_{p}^{2})
, where
d_{p}
is the distance function to
p
, (
4.5
) gives

\displaystyle r\int_{\partial B_{r}(p)}(e_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla)-2|\nabla_{\nu}u|^{2}-2\epsilon^{2}|\iota_{\nu}\omega|^{2})

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{r}(p)}\langle T_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla),DX\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{r}(p)}\langle T_{\epsilon}(u),g\rangle+\int_{B_{r}(p)}\langle T_{\epsilon}(u),DX-g\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{r}(p)}(ne_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla)-2|\nabla u|^{2}-4\epsilon^{2}|F_{\nabla}|^{2})

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{B_{r}(p)}\langle T_{\epsilon}(u),DX-g\rangle.

Now, by the Hessian comparison theorem, we know that

|DX-g|\leq C(M)d_{p}^{2};

applying this in the relations above, we see that

\displaystyle r\int_{\partial B_{r}(p)}e_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla)

\displaystyle\geq 2r\int_{\partial B_{r}(p)}(|\nabla_{\nu}u|^{2}+\epsilon^{2}|\iota_{\nu}\omega|^{2})

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{B_{r}(p)}\Big((n-2)|\nabla u|^{2}+(n-4)\epsilon^{2}|F_{\nabla}|^{2}+n\frac{W(u)}{\epsilon^{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad-C^{\prime}(M)r^{2}\int_{B_{r}(p)}e_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla).

Setting

(4.6)

f(p,r):=e^{C^{\prime}r^{2}}\int_{B_{r}(p)}e_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla),

it follows from the computations above (temporarily throwing out the additional nonnegative boundary terms) that

(4.7)

\frac{\partial f}{\partial r}\geq\frac{e^{C^{\prime}r^{2}}}{r}\int_{B_{r}(p)}\Big((n-2)|\nabla u|^{2}+(n-4)\epsilon^{2}|F_{\nabla}|^{2}+n\frac{W(u)}{\epsilon^{2}}\Big)

At this point, one easily observes that the right-hand side of (
4.7
) is bounded below by
\frac{n-4}{r}f(p,r)
, to obtain the monotonicity of the
(n-4)
-energy density

\frac{\partial}{\partial r}(r^{4-n}f(p,r))\geq 0.

For general Yang–Mills and Yang–Mills–Higgs problems, this codimension-four energy growth is well known to be sharp (cf., e.g.,
[
32
]
,
[
40
]
). For solutions of (
2.4
) and (
2.5
) on Hermitian line bundles, however, we show now that this can be improved to (near-) monotonicity of the
(n-2)
-density
r^{2-n}f(p,r)
on small balls, which constitutes a key technical ingredient in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

To begin, we rearrange (
4.7
), to see that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f}{\partial r}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n-2}{r}f(r)+\frac{2e^{C^{\prime}r^{2}}}{r}\int_{B_{r}(p)}\Big(\frac{W(u)}{\epsilon^{2}}-\epsilon^{2}|F_{\nabla}|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{n-2}{r}f(r)-\frac{2e^{C^{\prime}r^{2}}}{r}\int_{B_{r}(p)}\xi_{\epsilon}\Big(\epsilon|F_{\nabla}|+\frac{1}{2\epsilon}(1-|u|^{2})\Big),

recalling the notation
\xi_{\epsilon}:=\epsilon|F_{\nabla}|-\frac{1}{2\epsilon}(1-|u|^{2})
. Now, by Lemma
3.1
, assuming
E_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla)\leq\Lambda
, we have the pointwise bound

\displaystyle\xi_{\epsilon}\Big(\epsilon|F_{\nabla}|+\frac{1}{2\epsilon}(1-|u|^{2})\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\left(C+\alpha(\epsilon)\frac{1-|u|^{2}}{\epsilon}\right)e_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq Ce_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla)^{1/2}+C\alpha(\epsilon)e_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla).

Applying this in our preceding computation for
\frac{\partial f}{\partial r}
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f}{\partial r}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n-2}{r}f(r)-\frac{e^{C^{\prime}r^{2}}}{r}\int_{B_{r}(p)}Ce_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla)^{1/2}-\alpha(\epsilon)\frac{e^{C^{\prime}r^{2}}}{r}\int_{B_{r}(p)}Ce_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n-2-C\alpha(\epsilon)}{r}f(r)-\frac{e^{C^{\prime}r^{2}}}{r}Cr^{n/2}\Big(\int_{B_{r}(p)}e_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla)\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n-2-C^{\prime\prime}\alpha(\epsilon)}{r}f(r)-C^{\prime\prime}r^{n/2-1}f(r)^{1/2}

for some constant
C^{\prime\prime}(M,\Lambda)
and
0<r<c(M)
.
Taking
\epsilon
sufficiently small, we arrive next at the following coarse estimate for the
(n-3)
-energy density, which we will then use to establish an improved bound for
\xi_{\epsilon}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

For
\epsilon\leq\epsilon_{m}(M,\Lambda)
sufficiently small, we have a uniform bound

(4.8)

\sup_{r>0}r^{3-n}\int_{B_{r}(p)}e_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla)\leq C(M,\Lambda).

Proof.

The statement is trivial if
n=2,3
, so assume
n\geq 4
. In the preceding computation, take
\epsilon\leq\epsilon_{m}(M,\Lambda)
sufficiently small that
C^{\prime\prime}\alpha(\epsilon)<\frac{1}{2}
. Then the estimate gives

f^{\prime}(r)\geq\frac{n-2-1/2}{r}f(r)-C^{\prime\prime}r^{n/2-1}f(r)^{1/2},

from which it follows that, for
0<r<c(M)
,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dr}(r^{3-n}f(r))

\displaystyle\geq r^{3-n}f^{\prime}(r)+(3-n)r^{2-n}f(r)

\displaystyle\geq r^{2-n}\Big(\Big(n-\frac{5}{2}\Big)f(r)-Cr^{n/2}f(r)^{1/2}+(3-n)f(r)\Big)

\displaystyle\geq r^{2-n}\Big(\frac{1}{2}f(r)-Cr^{n/2}f(r)^{1/2}\Big).

If
r^{3-n}f(r)
has a maximum in
(0,\operatorname{inj}(M))
, it follows that
f(r)\leq Cr^{n/2}f(r)^{1/2}
there, and therefore
r^{3-n}f(r)\leq Cr^{3}\leq C
. Obviously the desired estimate holds at
r=0
and
r=c(M)
, so (
4.8
) follows.
∎

With Lemma
4.1
in hand, we can now improve the bounds of Lemma
3.1
to a uniform pointwise estimate, as follows.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
(u,\nabla)
solve (
2.4
)–(
2.5
) on a line bundle
L\to M
, with
\epsilon\leq\epsilon_{m}
and the energy bound
E_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla)\leq\Lambda
. Then there is a constant
C(M,\Lambda)
such that

(4.9)

\xi_{\epsilon}:=\epsilon|F_{\nabla}|-\frac{1}{2\epsilon}(1-|u|^{2})\leq C(M,\Lambda).

Proof.

We can assume
n\geq 3
, as we already obtained the claim for
n=2
in Section
3
. Recall from that section the function

h_{\epsilon}(x):=\int_{M}G(x,y)\epsilon|F_{\nabla}|(y)dy,

where
G
is the nonnegative Green’s function on
M
. As discussed in Section
3
, we can deduce from (
3.7
) a pointwise estimate of the form

(4.10)

\xi_{\epsilon}\leq C(M,\Lambda)h_{\epsilon}+C(M)E_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla)^{1/2}.

Thus, to arrive at the desired bound (
4.9
), it will suffice to establish a pointwise bound of the same form for
h_{\epsilon}
.

To this end, recall again that
G(x,y)\leq C(M)d(x,y)^{2-n}
, so that by definition we have

\displaystyle h_{\epsilon}(x)

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{M}d(x,y)^{2-n}\epsilon|F_{\nabla}|(y)\,dy

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{M}d(x,y)^{2-n}e_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla)^{1/2}(y)\,dy

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{M}(d(x,y)^{-n+1/2}+d(x,y)^{3-n+1/2}e_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla))\,dy,

where the last line is a simple application of Young’s inequality. Since the integral
\int_{M}d(x,y)^{-n+1/2}\,dy
is finite, it follows that

\displaystyle h_{\epsilon}(x)

\displaystyle\leq C(M)+C(M)\Lambda+C(M)\int_{0}^{\operatorname{inj}(M)}r^{3-n+1/2}\Big(\int_{\partial B_{r}(x)}e_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla)\Big)\,dr

\displaystyle=C(M,\Lambda)+C(M)\int_{0}^{\operatorname{inj}(M)}\frac{d}{dr}\Big(r^{-n+7/2}\int_{B_{r}(x)}e_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla)\Big)\,dr

\displaystyle\quad+(n-7/2)C(M)\int_{0}^{\operatorname{inj}(M)}r^{3-n-1/2}\Big(\int_{B_{r}(x)}e_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla)\Big)\,dr

\displaystyle\leq C(M,\Lambda)+C(M)\int_{0}^{\operatorname{inj}(M)}r^{3-n-1/2}\left(\int_{B_{r}(x)}e_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla)\right)dr.

On the other hand, by Lemma
4.1
, we know that
r^{3-n}\int_{B_{r}(x)}e_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla)\leq C(M,\Lambda)
for every
r
, so we see finally that

\displaystyle h_{\epsilon}(x)\leq C(M,\Lambda)+C(M,\Lambda)\int_{0}^{\operatorname{inj}(M)}r^{-1/2}\,dr\leq C(M,\Lambda),

as desired.
∎

Applying (
4.9
) in our original computation for
f^{\prime}(r)
, we see now that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f}{\partial r}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n-2}{r}f(r)-\frac{2e^{C^{\prime}r^{2}}}{r}\int_{B_{r}(p)}\xi_{\epsilon}\Big(\epsilon|F_{\nabla}|+\frac{1}{2\epsilon}(1-|u|^{2})\Big)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n-2}{r}f(r)-\frac{2e^{C^{\prime}r^{2}}}{r}\int_{B_{r}(p)}C(M,\Lambda)e_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n-2}{r}f(r)-C(M,\Lambda)r^{\frac{n-2}{2}}f(r)^{1/2}.

In fact, bringing in the extra boundary terms that we have been neglecting, and applying Young’s inequality to the term
r^{\frac{n-2}{2}}f(r)^{1/2}
, we see that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f}{\partial r}

\displaystyle\geq 2e^{C^{\prime}r^{2}}\int_{\partial B_{r}(p)}(|\nabla_{\nu}u|^{2}+\epsilon^{2}|\iota_{\nu}F_{\nabla}|^{2})

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{n-2}{r}f(r)-Cr^{\frac{n-2}{2}}f(r)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\geq 2e^{C^{\prime}r^{2}}\int_{\partial B_{r}(p)}(|\nabla_{\nu}u|^{2}+\epsilon^{2}|\iota_{\nu}F_{\nabla}|^{2})

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{n-2}{r}f(r)-Cf(r)-Cr^{n-2}.

With this differential inequality in place, a straightforward computation leads us finally to one of our key technical theorems, the monotonicity formula for the
(n-2)
-density.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
(u,\nabla)
solve (
2.4
)–(
2.5
) on a Hermitian line bundle
L\to M
, with an energy bound
E_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla)\leq\Lambda
. Then there exists positive constants
\epsilon_{m}(M,\Lambda)
and
C_{m}(M,\Lambda)
such that the normalized energy density

(4.11)

\widetilde{E}_{\epsilon}(x,r):=e^{C_{m}r}r^{2-n}\int_{B_{r}(x)}e_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla)

satisfies

(4.12)

\widetilde{E}_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(r)\geq 2r^{2-n}\int_{\partial B_{r}(x)}(|\nabla_{\nu}u|^{2}+\epsilon^{2}|\iota_{\nu}F_{\nabla}|^{2})-C_{m},

for
0<r<\operatorname{inj}(M)
and
\epsilon\leq\epsilon_{m}
.

As a simple corollary of the monotonicity result (together with a pointwise bound for
|\nabla u|
derived in the following section), we deduce that
(u,\nabla)
must have positive
(n-2)
-energy density wherever
|u|
is bounded away from
1
.

Corollary 4.4

(clearing-out)
.

Let
(u,\nabla)
solve (
2.4
)–(
2.5
) on a line bundle
L\to M
, with
E_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla)\leq\Lambda
and
\epsilon\leq\epsilon_{m}
. Given
\delta>0
, if

\displaystyle r^{2-n}\int_{B_{r}(x)}e_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla)\leq\eta(M,\Lambda,\delta)

with
x\in M
and
\epsilon<r<\operatorname{inj}(M)
, then we must have
|u(x)|>1-\delta
.

Proof.

For
\epsilon\leq\epsilon_{m}
, Theorem (
4.3
) gives

\displaystyle\epsilon^{2-n}\int_{B_{\epsilon}(x)}e_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla)\leq C(M,\Lambda)\eta+C(M,\Lambda)r.

The gradient bound (
5.3
) in Proposition
5.1
of the following section gives

|d|u||\leq C\epsilon^{-1}
. Hence, if
|u(x)|\leq 1-\delta
then
|u(y)|<1-\frac{\delta}{2}
on
B_{\epsilon\delta/(2C)}(x)
, so that
1-|u(y)|^{2}\geq 1-|u(y)|>\frac{\delta}{2}
. We deduce that

\displaystyle\frac{\delta^{2}}{16}\operatorname{vol}(B_{\epsilon\delta/(2C)}(x))\leq\int_{B_{\epsilon}(x)}W(u)\leq\epsilon^{2}\int_{B_{\epsilon}(x)}e_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla)\leq C\epsilon^{n}(\eta+r).

Since
\operatorname{vol}(B_{\epsilon\delta/(2C)}(x))
is bounded below by
c(M,\Lambda,\delta)\epsilon^{n}
, we can choose
\widetilde{\eta}(M,\Lambda,\delta)\leq\operatorname{inj}(M)
so small that we get a contradiction if
r,\eta\leq\widetilde{\eta}
. On the other hand, if
r>\widetilde{\eta}
then

\displaystyle\widetilde{\eta}^{2-n}\int_{B_{\widetilde{\eta}}(x)}e_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla)\leq\Big(\frac{\operatorname{inj}(M)}{\widetilde{\eta}}\Big)^{n-2}\eta.

Hence, setting
\eta:=\Big(\frac{\widetilde{\eta}}{\operatorname{inj}(M)}\Big)^{n-2}\widetilde{\eta}\leq\widetilde{\eta}
, we can reduce to the previous case (replacing
r
with
\widetilde{\eta}
), reaching again a contradiction.
∎

5.
Decay away from the zero set

Again, let
(u,\nabla)
solve (
2.4
)–(
2.5
) on a line bundle
L\to M
, with the energy bound
E_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla)\leq\Lambda
. In the preceding section, we obtained the pointwise estimate

(5.1)

|F_{\nabla}|\leq\frac{1}{2\epsilon^{2}}(1-|u|^{2})+\frac{1}{\epsilon}C(M,\Lambda)

when
\epsilon\leq\epsilon_{m}
.
As a first step toward establishing strong decay of the energy away from the zero set of
u
, we show in the following proposition that the full energy density
e_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla)
is controlled by the potential
\frac{W(u)}{\epsilon^{2}}
.

Proposition 5.1
.

For
(u,\nabla)
as above, we have the pointwise estimates

(5.2)

\epsilon^{2}|F_{\nabla}|^{2}\leq C(M,\Lambda)\frac{W(u)}{\epsilon^{2}}+C(M,\Lambda)\epsilon

and

(5.3)

|\nabla u|^{2}\leq C(M,\Lambda)\frac{W(u)}{\epsilon^{2}}+C(M,\Lambda)\epsilon^{2},

provided
\epsilon\leq\epsilon_{d}
, for some
\epsilon_{d}=\epsilon_{d}(M,\Lambda)
.

Proof.

To begin, let
C_{1}=C_{1}(M,\Lambda)
be the constant from (
5.1
), and consider the function

f:=\epsilon|F_{\nabla}|-\frac{1+2C_{1}\epsilon}{2\epsilon}(1-|u|^{2})=\xi_{\epsilon}-C_{1}+C_{1}|u|^{2}.

Similar to the proof of Lemma
3.1
, observe that
C_{1}|u|^{2}\geq f
pointwise, by (
5.1
), while the computations from Section
3
give

\Delta f\geq\frac{|u|^{2}}{\epsilon^{2}}f-C^{\prime}(M)\epsilon|F_{\nabla}|.

By (
4.9
) we have
|F_{\nabla}|\leq\frac{1}{2\epsilon^{2}}+C(M,\Lambda)
, so at a maximum for
f
it follows that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\geq\frac{|u|^{2}}{\epsilon^{2}}f-C^{\prime}\epsilon|F_{\nabla}|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{f^{2}}{C_{1}\epsilon^{2}}-\frac{C(M,\Lambda)}{\epsilon},

so that

(\max f)^{2}\leq C\epsilon,

and consequently
f\leq C\epsilon^{1/2}
everywhere. As a consequence, at any point, we have either
f<0
, in which case

\epsilon^{2}|F_{\nabla}|^{2}\leq(1+2C_{1}\epsilon)^{2}\frac{W(u)}{\epsilon^{2}},

or
f\geq 0
, in which case

\displaystyle\epsilon^{2}|F_{\nabla}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2f^{2}+2(1+2C_{1}\epsilon)^{2}\frac{W(u)}{\epsilon^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 2C_{3}^{2}\epsilon+2(1+2C_{1}\epsilon)^{2}\frac{W(u)}{\epsilon^{2}}.

In either scenario, we obtain a bound of the desired form (
5.2
).

To bound
|\nabla u|^{2}
, recall from Section
3
the identity

(5.4)

\Delta\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}=|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}+\frac{1}{2\epsilon^{2}}(3|u|^{2}-1)|\nabla u|^{2}-2\langle\omega,\psi(u)\rangle+\mathcal{R}_{1}(\nabla u,\nabla u).

In view of the estimate (
5.1
) for
|F_{\nabla}|=|\omega|
and (
2.7
), we can estimate the term
2\langle\omega,\psi(u)\rangle
from above by

2|F_{\nabla}||\nabla u|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}(1-|u|^{2})|\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{C}{\epsilon}|\nabla u|^{2},

to obtain the existence of
C_{1}(M,\Lambda)
such that

\frac{1}{2}\Delta|\nabla u|^{2}\geq|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}+\frac{1}{2\epsilon^{2}}(5|u|^{2}-3)|\nabla u|^{2}-\frac{C_{1}}{\epsilon}|\nabla u|^{2}.

For
\Delta|\nabla u|
, this then gives

(5.5)

\Delta|\nabla u|\geq\frac{1}{2\epsilon^{2}}(5|u|^{2}-3)|\nabla u|-\frac{C_{1}}{\epsilon}|\nabla u|.

Recalling once again the equation (
3.4
) for
\Delta\frac{1}{2}|u|^{2}
, we define

w:=|\nabla u|-\frac{1}{\epsilon}(1-|u|^{2}),

and observe that

\displaystyle\Delta w

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2\epsilon^{2}}(5|u|^{2}-3)|\nabla u|-\frac{C_{1}}{\epsilon}|\nabla u|

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{2}{\epsilon}|\nabla u|^{2}-\frac{1}{\epsilon^{3}}|u|^{2}(1-|u|^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{|u|^{2}}{\epsilon^{2}}w+|\nabla u|\Big(\frac{2}{\epsilon}|\nabla u|-\frac{3}{2}\frac{(1-|u|^{2})}{\epsilon^{2}}-\frac{C_{1}}{\epsilon}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{|u|^{2}}{\epsilon^{2}}w+\frac{|\nabla u|}{\epsilon}\Big(2w+\frac{1}{2\epsilon}(1-|u|^{2})-C_{1}\Big).

We then have

(5.6)

\Delta w\geq\frac{|u|^{2}}{\epsilon^{2}}w+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\Big(w+\frac{1}{\epsilon}(1-|u|^{2})\Big)\Big(2w+\frac{1}{2\epsilon}(1-|u|^{2})-C_{1}\Big).

If
w
has a positive maximum, it follows that

2w+\frac{1}{2\epsilon}(1-|u|^{2})\leq C_{1}

at this maximum point; in particular, we deduce then that

|u|^{2}\geq 1-2C_{1}\epsilon

at this point, and see from (
5.6
) that here

0\geq\frac{1-2C_{1}\epsilon}{\epsilon^{2}}w-\frac{1}{\epsilon}\Big(w+\frac{1}{\epsilon}(1-|u|^{2})\Big)C_{1}\geq\frac{1-3C_{1}\epsilon}{\epsilon^{2}}w-2\frac{C_{1}^{2}}{\epsilon}.

If
\epsilon\leq\epsilon_{d}(M,\Lambda)
is small enough, it follows that
\max w\leq C\epsilon
; as a consequence, we check that

|\nabla u|^{2}\leq C\frac{W(u)}{\epsilon^{2}}+C\epsilon^{2},

completing the proof of (
5.3
).
∎

As a simple consequence of the estimates in Proposition
5.1
, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 5.2
.

There exist constants
0<\beta_{d}(M,\Lambda)<1
and
C(M,\Lambda)
such that, for
(u,\nabla)
as above, we have

(5.7)

\Delta\frac{1}{2}(1-|u|^{2})\geq\frac{1}{4\epsilon^{2}}(1-|u|^{2})-C\epsilon^{2}

on the set
\{|u|^{2}\geq 1-\beta_{d}\}
.

Proof.

By the formula (
3.4
) for
\Delta\frac{1}{2}|u|^{2}
, we know that

\Delta\frac{1}{2}(1-|u|^{2})=\frac{1}{2\epsilon^{2}}|u|^{2}(1-|u|^{2})-|\nabla u|^{2}.

Combining this with the estimate (
5.3
) for
|\nabla u|^{2}
, we then deduce the existence of a constant
\widehat{C}(M,\Lambda)
such that

\Delta\frac{1}{2}(1-|u|^{2})\geq|u|^{2}\frac{1}{2\epsilon^{2}}(1-|u|^{2})-\widehat{C}(M,\Lambda)\frac{(1-|u|^{2})^{2}}{2\epsilon^{2}}-C\epsilon^{2}.

By taking
\beta=\beta(M,\Lambda)>0
sufficiently small, we can arrange that

|u|^{2}-\widehat{C}(1-|u|^{2})\geq 1-\beta-\widehat{C}\beta\geq\frac{1}{2}

on
\{|u|^{2}\geq 1-\beta\}
, from which the claimed estimate follows.
∎

Next, we employ the result of Corollary
5.2
to show that the quantity
(1-|u|^{2})
vanishes rapidly away from from
Z_{\beta_{d}}(u)
(compare
[
21
, Sections III.7–III.8]
).

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
(u,\nabla)
be as before,
with
\epsilon\leq\epsilon_{d}
, and define the set

Z_{\beta_{d}}:=\{x\in M:|u(x)|^{2}\leq 1-\beta_{d}\},

where
\beta_{d}(M,\Lambda)
is the constant provided by Corollary
5.2
.
Defining
r:M\to[0,\infty)
by

r(p):=\operatorname{dist}(p,Z_{\beta}),

we have an estimate of the form

(5.8)

(1-|u|^{2})(p)\leq Ce^{-a_{d}r(p)/\epsilon}+C\epsilon^{4}

for some
C=C(M,\Lambda)
and
a_{d}=a_{d}(M)>0
.

Proof.

Fix a point
p\in M
, and let
r=r(p)=\operatorname{dist}(p,Z_{\beta})
as above. We can clearly assume
r(p)<\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{inj}(M)
. On the ball
B_{r}(p)
, for some constant
a=a_{d}>0
to be chosen later, consider the function

\varphi(x):=e^{(a/\epsilon)(d_{p}(x)^{2}+\epsilon^{2})^{1/2}},

where
d_{p}(x):=\operatorname{dist}(p,x)
.
A straightforward computation then gives

\displaystyle\Delta\varphi

\displaystyle=\frac{a}{\epsilon}\varphi\left(\frac{(a/\epsilon)d_{p}^{2}}{d_{p}^{2}+\epsilon^{2}}-\frac{d_{p}^{2}}{(d_{p}^{2}+\epsilon^{2})^{3/2}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{a}{2\epsilon}\varphi\frac{\Delta d_{p}^{2}}{(d_{p}^{2}+\epsilon^{2})^{1/2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{a^{2}}{\epsilon^{2}}\varphi+\frac{a}{2\epsilon}\varphi\frac{\Delta d_{p}^{2}}{(d_{p}^{2}+\epsilon^{2})^{1/2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{a^{2}+C_{1}a}{\epsilon^{2}}\varphi

for some
C_{1}=C_{1}(M)
.
Now, fix some constant
c_{2}>0
to be chosen later, and let

f:=\frac{1}{2}(1-|u|^{2})-c_{2}\varphi.

Combining the preceding computation with (
5.7
), we see that, on
B_{r}(p)
,

\displaystyle\Delta f

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4\epsilon^{2}}(1-|u|^{2})-C(M,\Lambda)\epsilon^{2}-\frac{a^{2}+C_{1}a}{\epsilon^{2}}c_{2}\varphi

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\epsilon^{2}}f+\frac{1-2a^{2}-2C_{1}a}{2\epsilon^{2}}c_{2}\varphi-C(M,\Lambda)\epsilon^{2}.

Choosing
a=a_{d}(M)>0
sufficiently small, we can arrange that
2a^{2}+C_{1}a\leq 1
, so that the above computation gives

(5.9)

\Delta f\geq\frac{f}{2\epsilon^{2}}-C\epsilon^{2}.

On the boundary of the ball
\partial B_{r}(p)
, it follows from definition of
r=r(p)
that
|u|^{2}\geq 1-\beta_{d}
, and therefore

f(x)\leq\frac{\beta_{d}}{2}-c_{2}\varphi\leq\frac{\beta_{d}}{2}-c_{2}e^{ar/\epsilon}\text{ on }\partial B_{r}(p).

Taking
c_{2}(M,\Lambda):=\beta_{d}e^{-ar/\epsilon}
, it then follows that
f<0
on
\partial B_{r}(p)
, so we can apply the maximum principle with (
5.9
) to deduce that

f\leq C\epsilon^{4}\text{ in }B_{r}(p).

Evaluating at
p
, this gives

C\epsilon^{4}\geq f(p)=\frac{1}{2}(1-|u|^{2})(p)-\beta_{d}e^{-ar(p)/\epsilon}e^{a},

so that

(1-|u|^{2})(p)\leq C(M,\Lambda)e^{-ar(p)/\epsilon}+C(M,\Lambda)\epsilon^{4},

as desired.
∎

Combining these estimates with those of Proposition
5.2
, we arrive immediately at the following decay estimate for the energy integrand
e_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla)
.

Corollary 5.4
.

Defining
Z_{\beta_{d}}
and
r(p)=\operatorname{dist}(p,Z_{\beta_{d}})
as in Corollary
5.2
, there exist
a_{d}(M)>0
and
C_{d}(M,\Lambda)
such that

(5.10)

e_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla)(p)\leq C_{d}\frac{e^{-a_{d}r(p)/\epsilon}}{\epsilon^{2}}+C_{d}\epsilon.

6.
The energy-concentration varifold

This section is devoted to the proof of the main result of the paper, which we recall now.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
(u_{\epsilon},\nabla_{\epsilon})
be a family of solutions to (
2.4
)–(
2.5
) satisfying a uniform energy bound
E_{\epsilon}(u_{\epsilon},\nabla_{\epsilon})\leq\Lambda
as
\epsilon\to 0
. Then, as
\epsilon\to 0
, the energy measures

\mu_{\epsilon}:=\frac{1}{2\pi}e_{\epsilon}(u_{\epsilon},\nabla_{\epsilon})\,\operatorname{vol}_{g}

converge subsequentially, in duality with
C^{0}(M)
, to the weight measure of a stationary, integral
(n-2)
-varifold
V
. Also, for all
0\leq\delta<1
,

\operatorname{spt}(V)=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\{|u_{\epsilon}|\leq\delta\}

in the Hausdorff topology. If
M
is oriented, the
(n-2)
-currents dual to the curvature forms
\frac{1}{2\pi}\omega_{\epsilon}
converge subsequentially to an integral
(n-2)
-cycle
\Gamma
, with
|\Gamma|\leq\mu
.

6.1.
Convergence to a stationary rectifiable varifold

Let
(u_{\epsilon},\nabla_{\epsilon})
be as in Theorem
6.1
, and pass to a subsequence
\epsilon_{j}\to 0
such that the energy measures
\mu_{\epsilon_{j}}
converge weakly-* to a limiting measure
\mu
,
in duality with
C^{0}(M)
.

Note that, for
0<r<R<\operatorname{inj}(M)
, Theorem
4.3
yields

\displaystyle e^{CR}R^{2-n}\mu(\overline{B}_{R}(x))+CR

\displaystyle\geq\limsup_{\epsilon\to 0}e^{CR}R^{2-n}\mu_{\epsilon}(\overline{B}_{R}(x))+CR

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{\epsilon\to 0}e^{Cr}r^{2-n}\mu_{\epsilon}(B_{r}(x))+Cr

\displaystyle\geq e^{Cr}r^{2-n}\mu(B_{r}(x))+Cr,

so approximating
R
with smaller radii we deduce

(6.1)

\displaystyle e^{CR}R^{2-n}\mu(B_{R}(x))+CR\geq e^{Cr}r^{2-n}\mu(B_{r}(x))+Cr

and in particular the
(n-2)
-density

\displaystyle\Theta_{n-2}(\mu,x):=\lim_{r\to 0}(\omega_{n-2}r^{n-2})^{-1}\mu(B_{r}(x))

is defined.
As a first step toward the proof of Theorem
6.1
, we show that this density is bounded from above and below on the support
\operatorname{spt}(\mu)
.

Proposition 6.2
.

There exists a constant
0<C(M,\Lambda)<\infty
such that

(6.2)

C(M,\Lambda)^{-1}\leq r^{2-n}\mu(B_{r}(x))\leq C(M,\Lambda)\quad\text{for }x\in\operatorname{spt}(\mu),\ 0<r<\operatorname{inj}(M)

and thus
C(M,\Lambda)^{-1}\leq\Theta_{n-2}(\mu,x)\leq C(M,\Lambda)
for all
x\in\operatorname{spt}(\mu)
.

Proof.

The upper bound follows fairly immediately from the monotonicity formula in Theorem
4.3
. In particular, for any
0<r<\operatorname{inj}(M)
, note that

r^{2-n}\mu_{\epsilon}(B_{r}(x))\leq\widetilde{E}_{\epsilon}(x,r),

where
\widetilde{E}_{\epsilon}(x,r):=e^{C_{m}r}r^{2-n}\int_{B_{r}(x)}e_{\epsilon}(u_{\epsilon},\nabla_{\epsilon})
,
and by Theorem
4.3
we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{E}_{\epsilon}(x,r)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{E}_{\epsilon}(x,\operatorname{inj}(M))-\int_{r}^{\operatorname{inj}(M)}\widetilde{E}_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(s)ds

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{E}_{\epsilon}(x,\operatorname{inj}(M))+\int_{r}^{\operatorname{inj}(M)}C_{m}

\displaystyle\leq C(M,\Lambda)\Lambda+C\operatorname{inj}(M),

so that

r^{2-n}\mu_{\epsilon}(B_{r}(x))\leq C^{\prime}(M,\Lambda)

for all
0<r<\operatorname{inj}(M)
.

To see the lower bound, let
\beta=\beta(M,\Lambda)\in(0,1)
be the constant given by Corollary
5.2
, and again set

Z_{\beta}(u_{\epsilon}):=\{x\in M:|u_{\epsilon}(x)|^{2}\leq 1-\beta\}.

Let
\Sigma
be the set of all limits
x=\lim_{\epsilon}x_{\epsilon}
, with
x_{\epsilon}\in Z_{\beta}(u_{\epsilon})
; that is, take

\Sigma:=\bigcap_{\eta>0}\,\overline{\bigcup_{0<\epsilon<\eta}Z_{\beta}(u_{\epsilon})}.

We then claim that

(6.3)

\operatorname{spt}(\mu)\subseteq\Sigma

and

(6.4)

\mu(B_{r}(x))\geq c(M,\Lambda)r^{n-2}\quad\text{for }x\in\Sigma,\ 0<r<\operatorname{inj}(M)

Once both (
6.3
) and (
6.4
) are established, the lower bound in (
6.2
) follows immediately.

To establish (
6.3
), fix some
p\in M\setminus\Sigma
; by definition of
\Sigma
, there must exist
\delta=\delta(p)>0
such that

\operatorname{dist}(p,Z_{\beta}(u_{\epsilon}))\geq 2\delta

for all
\epsilon
sufficiently small. Applying Corollary
5.4
for all
x\in B_{\delta}(p)
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\mu(B_{\delta}(p))

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\int_{B_{\delta}(p)}e_{\epsilon}(u_{\epsilon},\nabla_{\epsilon})

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\int_{B_{\delta}(p)}(C\epsilon^{-2}e^{-a\delta/\epsilon}+C\epsilon)

\displaystyle=0.

In particular,
p\notin M\setminus\operatorname{spt}(\mu)
, confirming (
6.3
).

To see (
6.4
), let
x\in\Sigma
. Note that, by definition of
\Sigma
, there exist points
x_{\epsilon}\in Z_{\beta}(u_{\epsilon})
with
x_{\epsilon}\to x
as
\epsilon\to 0
(along a subsequence). We then see that

|u_{\epsilon}(x_{\epsilon})|^{2}\leq 1-\beta

and Corollary
4.4
gives
c(M,\Lambda)
such that

\mu_{\epsilon}(B_{r}(x_{\epsilon}))\geq c(M,\Lambda)r^{n-2}

for
\epsilon<r<\operatorname{inj}(M)
. Since for any
\delta>0
we have
B_{r}(x_{\epsilon})\subseteq\overline{B}_{r+\delta}(x)
eventually,
it follows that
\mu(\overline{B}_{r+\delta}(x))\geq cr^{n-2}
, hence

\mu(B_{r}(x))\geq cr^{n-2}

for
0<r<\operatorname{inj}(M)
, which is (
6.4
).
∎

With Proposition
6.2
in place, we will invoke a result by Ambrosio and Soner
[
6
]
to conclude that the limiting measure
\mu=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\mu_{\epsilon}
coincides with the weight measure of a stationary, rectifiable
(n-2)
-varifold. Recall from Section
4
the stress-energy tensors

T_{\epsilon}=e_{\epsilon}(u_{\epsilon},\nabla_{\epsilon})g-2\nabla_{\epsilon}u_{\epsilon}^{*}\nabla_{\epsilon}u_{\epsilon}-2\epsilon^{2}F_{\nabla_{\epsilon}}^{*}F_{\nabla_{\epsilon}}.

We record first the following lemma; in its statement, we canonically identify (and pair with each other) tensors of rank
(2,0)
,
(1,1)
, and
(0,2)
, using the underlying metric
g
.

Lemma 6.3
.

As
\epsilon\to 0
, the tensors
T_{\epsilon}
converge (subsequentially) as
\operatorname{Sym}(TM)
-valued measures (in duality with
C^{0}(M,\operatorname{Sym}(TM))
) to a limit
T
satisfying

(6.5)

\langle T,DX\rangle=0\text{ for all }C^{1}\text{ vector fields }X\in C^{1}(M,TM),

(6.6)

\langle T,\varphi g\rangle\geq(n-2)\langle\mu,\varphi\rangle\text{ for every }0\leq\varphi\in C^{0}(M),

and

(6.7)

-\int_{M}|X|^{2}\,d\mu\leq\langle T,X\otimes X\rangle\leq\int_{M}|X|^{2}\,d\mu\text{ for all }X\in C^{0}(M,TM).

Proof.

For each
\epsilon>0
, note that, by definition of
T_{\epsilon}
, for every continuous vector field
X\in C^{0}(M,TM)
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{M}\langle T_{\epsilon},X\otimes X\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{M}e_{\epsilon}(u_{\epsilon},\nabla_{\epsilon})|X|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{M}2|(\nabla_{\epsilon})_{X}u_{\epsilon}|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{M}2\epsilon^{2}|\iota_{X}F_{\nabla_{\epsilon}}|^{2}.

Evaluating (
2.3
) in an orthonormal basis such that
X
is a multiple of
e_{1}
, we see that
|\iota_{X}F_{\nabla_{\epsilon}}|^{2}\leq|F_{\nabla_{\epsilon}}|^{2}|X|^{2}
, while
|(\nabla_{\epsilon})_{X}u_{\epsilon}|^{2}\leq|\nabla_{\epsilon}u_{\epsilon}|^{2}|X|^{2}
. We deduce that

(6.8)

-\int_{M}|X|^{2}e_{\epsilon}(u_{\epsilon},\nabla_{\epsilon})\leq\int_{M}\langle T_{\epsilon},X\otimes X\rangle\leq\int_{M}e_{\epsilon}(u_{\epsilon},\nabla_{\epsilon})|X|^{2}.

As an immediate consequence, we see that the uniform energy bound
E_{\epsilon}(u_{\epsilon},\nabla_{\epsilon})\leq\Lambda
gives a uniform bound on
\|T_{\epsilon}\|_{(C^{0})^{*}}
as
\epsilon\to 0
, so we can indeed extract a weak-* subsequential limit
T\in C^{0}(M,\operatorname{Sym}(TM))^{*}
, for which (
6.7
) follows from (
6.8
).

The stationarity condition (
6.5
) for the limit
T
follows from (
4.5
). It remains to establish the trace inequality (
6.6
). For this, we simply compute, for nonnegative
\varphi\in C^{0}(M)
,

\displaystyle\int_{M}\langle T_{\epsilon},\varphi g\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\varphi(ne_{\epsilon}(u_{\epsilon},\nabla_{\epsilon})-2|\nabla_{\epsilon}(u_{\epsilon})|^{2}-4\epsilon^{2}|F_{\nabla_{\epsilon}}|^{2})

\displaystyle=\int_{M}(n-2)\varphi e_{\epsilon}(u_{\epsilon},\nabla_{\epsilon})+2\int_{M}\varphi\Big(\frac{W(u_{\epsilon})}{\epsilon^{2}}-\epsilon^{2}|F_{\nabla_{\epsilon}}|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\geq(n-2)\langle\mu_{\epsilon},\varphi\rangle-2\int_{M}\varphi e_{\epsilon}(u_{\epsilon},\nabla_{\epsilon})^{1/2}\Big(|F_{\nabla_{\epsilon}}|-\frac{(1-|u_{\epsilon}|^{2})}{2\epsilon}\Big)^{+}.

Recalling from Proposition
4.2
that

|F_{\nabla_{\epsilon}}|-\frac{(1-|u_{\epsilon}|^{2})}{2\epsilon}\leq C(M,\Lambda),

we then see that

\displaystyle\langle T,\varphi g\rangle

\displaystyle=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\int_{M}\langle T_{\epsilon},\varphi g\rangle

\displaystyle\geq(n-2)\langle\mu,\varphi\rangle-C\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\int_{M}\varphi e_{\epsilon}(u_{\epsilon},\nabla_{\epsilon})^{1/2}.

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{\delta}(\Sigma)}e_{\epsilon}(u_{\epsilon},\nabla_{\epsilon})^{1/2}+\int_{M\setminus B_{\delta}(\Sigma)}e_{\epsilon}(u_{\epsilon},\nabla_{\epsilon})^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{vol}(B_{\delta}(\Sigma))^{1/2}\Lambda^{1/2}+\operatorname{vol}(M)^{1/2}\mu_{\epsilon}(M\setminus B_{\delta}(\Sigma))^{1/2}.

6.3.
Limiting behavior of the curvature

As before, we identify the curvature

Theorem 6.10

Assume

is oriented. Let
(u_{\epsilon},\nabla_{\epsilon})
be a family as in Theorem
6.1
. The curvature forms
\frac{1}{2\pi}\omega_{\epsilon}

Recall from Section

where

\displaystyle J(u_{\epsilon},\nabla_{\epsilon})-\omega_{\epsilon}=d\langle\nabla_{\epsilon}u_{\epsilon},iu_{\epsilon}\rangle,

\Gamma

By means of a diagonal selection of a subsequence, as in the previous subsection, we can deduce integrality at those points

where

\Theta_{n-2}(\mu,p)\mathcal{H}^{n-2}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}T_{p}\Sigma

d^{*}A_{\epsilon}=0
, and
A_{\epsilon}(\nu)=0
on
\partial B_{2}^{n}
. We then have the
L^{2}
control

(6.36)

\int_{B_{2}^{n}}|A_{\epsilon}|^{2}\leq C\int_{B_{2}^{n}}|dA_{\epsilon}|^{2}\leq C\epsilon^{-2}\Lambda,

and consequently

\displaystyle\Big|\int\eta(1-|u_{\epsilon}|^{2})A_{\epsilon}\wedge d\psi\wedge dx^{3}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx^{n}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq C\|1-|u_{\epsilon}|^{2}\|_{C^{0}(\operatorname{spt}(\eta d\psi))}\|A_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{1}(B_{2}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq C\Lambda^{1/2}\|\epsilon^{-1}(1-|u_{\epsilon}|^{2})\|_{C^{0}(\operatorname{spt}(\eta d\psi))}

\displaystyle\leq C\Lambda^{1/2}\|e_{\epsilon}(u_{\epsilon},\nabla_{\epsilon})\|_{C^{0}(\operatorname{spt}(\eta d\psi))}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\to 0

as
\epsilon\to 0
, where we have used the fact that
d\psi(x^{1},x^{2})=0
for
|(x^{1},x^{2})|\leq\frac{1}{2}
, and the assumption that
e_{\epsilon}(u_{\epsilon},\nabla_{\epsilon})\to 0
in
C^{0}_{loc}(B_{2}^{n}\setminus P)
.

Combining our computations thus far, we have arrived at the identity

\theta_{1}\int_{P}\eta dx^{3}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx^{n}=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\int\eta|u_{\epsilon}|^{2}A_{\epsilon}\wedge d\psi\wedge dx^{3}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx^{n}.

Noting next that

||u_{\epsilon}|^{2}A_{\epsilon}-\langle du_{\epsilon},iu_{\epsilon}\rangle|=|\langle\nabla_{\epsilon}u_{\epsilon},iu_{\epsilon}\rangle|\leq e_{\epsilon}(u_{\epsilon},\nabla_{\epsilon})^{1/2},

and using again the hypothesis that
e_{\epsilon}(u_{\epsilon},\nabla_{\epsilon})\to 0
uniformly on
\operatorname{spt}(\eta d\psi)
, the preceding identity yields

\displaystyle\theta_{1}\int_{P}\eta dx^{3}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx^{n}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\int\eta\langle du_{\epsilon},iu_{\epsilon}\rangle\wedge d\psi\wedge dx^{3}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx^{n}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\int\eta|u_{\epsilon}|^{2}(u_{\epsilon}/|u_{\epsilon}|)^{*}(d\theta)\wedge d\psi\wedge dx^{3}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx^{n}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\int\eta(u_{\epsilon}/|u_{\epsilon}|)^{*}(d\theta)\wedge d\psi\wedge dx^{3}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx^{n}.

Finally, since the one-form
(u_{\epsilon}/|u_{\epsilon}|)^{*}(d\theta)
is closed on
\{u_{\epsilon}\neq 0\}

and
d\eta\wedge dx^{3}\wedge\dots\wedge dx^{n}=0
, integrating by parts on
(\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus B_{1/2}^{2})\times\mathbb{R}^{n-2}
we see that

\displaystyle\int\eta(u_{\epsilon}/|u_{\epsilon}|)^{*}(d\theta)\wedge d\psi\wedge dx^{3}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx^{n}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-2}}\eta(t)\int_{\partial B_{1/2}^{2}\times\{t\}}(u_{\epsilon}/|u_{\epsilon}|)^{*}(d\theta)\,dt

\displaystyle=2\pi\operatorname{deg}(u_{\epsilon},P)\int_{P}\eta,

where
\operatorname{deg}(u_{\epsilon},P)
stands for the degree of
(u_{\epsilon}/|u_{\epsilon}|)(\frac{1}{2}e^{i\theta},0)
.
The statement follows.
∎

7.
Examples from variational constructions

The goal of this section is to show that, for every closed manifold
M
and every line bundle
L\to M
endowed with a Hermitian metric, there exist critical couples
(u_{\epsilon},\nabla_{\epsilon})
for the Yang–Mills–Higgs functional
E_{\epsilon}
, for
\epsilon
small enough, in such a way that

(7.1)

\displaystyle 0<\liminf_{\epsilon\to 0}E_{\epsilon}(u_{\epsilon},\nabla_{\epsilon})\leq\limsup_{\epsilon\to 0}E_{\epsilon}(u_{\epsilon},\nabla_{\epsilon})<\infty.

This will be easier when the line bundle is nontrivial, as in this case we can just take
(u_{\epsilon},\nabla_{\epsilon})
to be a global minimizer for
E_{\epsilon}
. The upper and lower bounds in (
7.1
) have the following immediate consequence—proved previously by Almgren
[
5
]
using GMT methods.

Corollary 7.1
.

Every closed Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},g)
supports a nontrivial stationary, integral
(n-2)
-varifold.

Proof.

We can always equip
M
with the trivial line bundle
L:=M\times\mathbb{C}
. As shown in the next subsection,
there exists a sequence of critical couples
(u_{\epsilon},\nabla_{\epsilon})
satisfying (
7.1
).
The statement now follows from Theorem
6.1
.
∎

7.1.
Min-max families for the trivial line bundle

In this section we will show how min-max methods may be applied to the functionals
E_{\epsilon}
to produce nontrivial critical points in the trivial bundle
L=M\times\mathbb{C}
on an arbitrary closed, oriented manifold
M
of dimension
n\geq 2
. The min-max construction that we consider here is based on two-parameter families parametrized by the unit disk, similar to the constructions employed in
[
10
]
and
[
33
]
for the Ginzburg–Landau functionals—with several technical adjustments to account for the gauge-invariance and other features particular to the Yang–Mills–Higgs energies. We remark that the families we consider induce a nontrivial class in
\pi_{2}(\mathcal{M})
for the quotient

\mathcal{M}:=\{(u,\nabla)\mid 0\not\equiv u\in\Gamma(L),\text{ }\nabla\text{ a hermitian connection}\}/\{\text{gauge transformations}\},

and the analysis that follows can be reformulated in terms of min-max methods applied directly to the Banach manifold
\mathcal{M}
.

Without loss of generality, we assume henceforth that
M
is connected.

Definition 7.2
.

Fix
n=\dim(M)<p<\infty
.
In what follows,
\widehat{X}
will denote the Banach space of couples
(u,A)
, where
u\in L^{p}(M,\mathbb{C})
and
A\in\Omega^{1}(M,\mathbb{R})
, both of class
W^{1,2}
, with the norm

\displaystyle\|(u,A)\|:=\|u\|_{L^{p}}+\|du\|_{L^{2}}+\|A\|_{L^{2}}+\|DA\|_{L^{2}}.

Denote by
X:=\{(u,A)\in\widehat{X}:d^{*}A=0\}
the subspace consisting of those couples for which the connection form
A
is co-closed.

Note that for
(u,A)\in X
, the full covariant derivative
\int_{M}|DA|^{2}
is bounded by
C(M)\int_{M}(|A|^{2}+|dA|^{2})
.

Definition 7.3
.

Given a form
A\in\Omega^{1}(M,\mathbb{R})
in
L^{2}
, we denote by
h(A)
the harmonic part of its Hodge decomposition, or equivalently the orthogonal projection of
A
onto the (finite-dimensional) space
\mathcal{H}^{1}(M)
of harmonic one-forms.

Remark 7.4
.

Selection of a Coulomb gauge gives a continuous retraction
\mathcal{R}:\widehat{X}\to X
: namely, given a couple
(u,A)\in\widehat{X}
, consider the unique solution
\theta\in W^{2,2}(M,\mathbb{R})
to the equation

\displaystyle\Delta\theta=d^{*}A,

with
\int_{M}\theta=0
, and set

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}((u,A)):=(e^{i\theta}u,A+d\theta).

Note that the continuity of
(u,A)\mapsto d(e^{i\theta}u)=e^{i\theta}(du+iud\theta)
, from
\widehat{X}
to
L^{2}
, follows from the fact that
L^{p}\cdot L^{2^{*}}\subseteq L^{2}
, where
2^{*}=\frac{2n}{n-2}
.

Throughout this section,
W(u)=f(|u|)
will be a smooth radial function given by

W(u)=\frac{(1-|u|^{2})^{2}}{4}
for
|u|\leq 3/2
, and satisfying
W(u)>0
for all
|u|>1
.
For technical reasons, we also find it convenient to require that

(G)

\displaystyle W(u)=|u|^{p}\text{ for }|u|\geq 2,

which evidently gives the additional estimates
|u|W^{\prime}(|u|)+|u|^{2}W^{\prime\prime}(|u|)\leq C|u|^{p}
for
|u|\geq 2
, for some constant
C
. For future use, observe also that the potential
W(u)
then satisfies a simple bound of the form

(7.2)

\displaystyle(1-|u|)^{2}\leq CW(u).

Proposition 7.5
.

The functional
E_{\epsilon}
is of class
C^{1}
on
\widehat{X}
. Moreover, a couple
(u,A)
is critical in
\widehat{X}
for
E_{\epsilon}
if and only if
\mathcal{R}((u,A))
is critical in
X
.

Proof.

Given a point
(u,A)\in\widehat{X}
and a pair
(v,B)\in\widehat{X}
with
\|(v,B)\|_{\widehat{X}}\leq 1
, direct computation gives

\displaystyle E_{\epsilon}(u+v,A+B)=

\displaystyle E_{\epsilon}(u,A)+2\int_{M}\langle du-iuA,dv-ivA-iuB\rangle+2\epsilon^{2}\langle dA,dB\rangle

\displaystyle+\epsilon^{-2}\int_{M}W^{\prime}(u)[v]+O(\|(v,B)\|_{\widehat{X}}^{2}),

where we are using the fact that
\widehat{X}\cdot\widehat{X}\subseteq L^{n}\cdot L^{2^{*}}\subseteq L^{2}
to see that

\|Av\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|Bu\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|Bv\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+E_{\epsilon}(u,A)^{1/2}\|Bv\|_{L^{2}}=O(\|(v,B)\|_{\widehat{X}}^{2}),

and we invoke our assumptions on the structure of
W
to see that

\int_{M}(W(u+v)-W(u))=\int_{M}W^{\prime}(u)[v]+O(\|(v,B)\|_{\widehat{X}}^{2})

for fixed
(u,A)\in\widehat{X}
.
It follows immediately that
E_{\epsilon}
is
C^{1}
on
\widehat{X}
, with gradient

dE_{\epsilon}(u,A)[v,B]=2\int_{M}\langle du-iuA,dv-ivA-iuB\rangle+2\epsilon^{2}\langle dA,dB\rangle+\epsilon^{-2}W^{\prime}(u)[v].

To confirm the second statement, assume without loss of generality that
v
and
B
are smooth, and observe that

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}((u+tv,A+tB))=(e^{ti\psi}\widetilde{u}+e^{i\theta+ti\psi}v,\widetilde{A}+tB+td\psi),

where
(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{A}):=\mathcal{R}((u,A))=(e^{i\theta}u,A+d\theta)
and
\psi
solves
\Delta\psi=d^{*}B
. This easily gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}((u+tv,A+tB))=\mathcal{R}((u,A))+t(e^{i\theta}v+i\psi\widetilde{u},B+d\psi)+o(t)\qquad\text{in }X

and, using the gauge invariance
E_{\epsilon}=E_{\epsilon}\circ\mathcal{R}
, we deduce that

(7.3)

\displaystyle dE_{\epsilon}(u,A)[v,B]=dE_{\epsilon}(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{A})[e^{i\theta}v+i\psi\widetilde{u},B+d\psi].

It follows that if
(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{A})
is critical for
E_{\epsilon}
in
X
then
(u,A)
is critical for
E_{\epsilon}
in
\widehat{X}
, as claimed.
∎

We next show that the functionals
E_{\epsilon}
satisfy a suitable variant of the Palais–Smale condition on
X
, giving compactness of critical sequences for
E_{\epsilon}
after an appropriate change of gauge. (Cf.
[
23
]
for similar results in the Seiberg–Witten setting.)

Proposition 7.6
.

The functional
E_{\epsilon}
satisfies the following form of the Palais–Smale condition: every sequence
(u_{j},A_{j})
in
X
with bounded energy and
dE_{\epsilon}(u_{j},A_{j})\to 0
in
X^{*}
admits a subsequence converging strongly in
X
to a critical couple
(u_{\infty},A_{\infty})
, up to possibly replacing
(u_{j},A_{j})
with

\displaystyle(\widetilde{u}_{j},\widetilde{A}_{j}):=(u_{j}v_{j},A_{j}+v_{j}^{*}(d\theta))

for suitable smooth harmonic functions
v_{j}:M\to S^{1}
.

Proof.

First, by assumption, we have that

(7.4)

\displaystyle dE_{\epsilon}(u_{j},A_{j})[u_{j},0]=o(\|(u_{j},0)\|)\qquad\text{as }j\to\infty;

that is,

\displaystyle 2\int_{M}|du_{j}-iu_{j}A_{j}|^{2}+\int_{M}W^{\prime}(u_{j})[u_{j}]=o(\|(u_{j},0)\|).

The first term is bounded by
2E_{\epsilon}(u_{j},A_{j})
, hence uniformly bounded as
j\to\infty
. Moreover, it is clear from (G) that
W^{\prime}(u_{j})[u_{j}]\geq p|u_{j}|^{p}-C
, so that

(7.5)

\displaystyle\|u_{j}\|_{L^{p}}^{p}\leq C+o(\|du_{j}\|_{L^{2}}).

Denote by
\Lambda\subset\mathcal{H}^{1}(M)
the lattice in the space of harmonic one-forms given by

\displaystyle\Lambda

\displaystyle:=\{-v_{j}^{*}(d\theta)\mid v_{j}:M\to S^{1}\text{ harmonic }\}

\displaystyle=\Big\{h\in\mathcal{H}^{1}(M):\int_{\gamma}h\in 2\pi\mathbb{Z}\text{ for every }\gamma\in C^{1}(S^{1},M)\Big\},

and let
\lambda_{j}\in\Lambda
be a closest integral harmonic one-form to
h(A_{j})
(with respect to the
L^{2}
norm, say, on
\mathcal{H}^{1}(M)
). Then
\lambda_{j}=-v_{j}^{*}(d\theta)
for a suitable harmonic map
v_{j}:M\to S^{1}
, and

\|\lambda_{j}-h(A_{j})\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(M).

Replacing
(u_{j},A_{j})
with the change of gauge
(v_{j}u_{j},A_{j}-\lambda_{j})\in X
, we can then assume that
h(A_{j})
is bounded. By standard Hodge theory we can write

\displaystyle A_{j}=h(A_{j})+d^{*}\xi_{j}

for some closed
\xi_{j}\in W^{2,2}
satisfying
\Delta_{H}\xi_{j}=dA_{j}
and
\|d^{*}\xi_{j}\|_{W^{1,2}}\leq C(M)\|dA_{j}\|_{L^{2}}
. Thus, given the energy bound
E_{\epsilon}(u_{j})\leq C
, we see that

\displaystyle\|A_{j}\|_{W^{1,2}}^{2}\leq C+2\|d^{*}\xi_{j}\|_{W^{1,2}}^{2}\leq C+C\|dA_{j}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C,

whereby
A_{j}
is bounded in
W^{1,2}
and, consequently, in
L^{2^{*}}
. As a consequence, we see next that

\displaystyle\|du_{j}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 2\int_{M}|du_{j}-iu_{j}A_{j}|^{2}+2\int_{M}|u_{j}A_{j}|^{2}\leq C+C\|u_{j}\|_{L^{p}}^{2}\|A_{j}\|_{L^{2^{*}}}^{2}\leq C+C\|u_{j}\|_{L^{p}}^{p};

taking into account (
7.5
), we infer then that
\|du_{j}\|_{L^{2}}
is also bounded as
j\to\infty
.

We have therefore shown that
(u_{j},A_{j})
is uniformly bounded in
X
as
j\to\infty
, so passing to subsequences we can assume that
(u_{j},A_{j})
converges pointwise a.e. and weakly (in
X
) to a limiting couple
(u_{\infty},A_{\infty})
. In particular, defining
r
by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r}:=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}>\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{n}=\frac{1}{2^{*}},

where
n<q<p
is an arbitrary fixed exponent, it follows from the compactness of the embedding
W^{1,2}\hookrightarrow L^{r}
that

A_{j}\to A_{\infty}\text{ strongly in }L^{r}.

Moreover, the boundedness of
u_{j}
in
L^{p}
and the pointwise convergence to
u_{\infty}
give

(7.6)

\displaystyle u_{j}\to u_{\infty}\text{ strongly in }L^{q}.

By definition of
r
, this implies in particular that

\displaystyle\lim_{j,k\to\infty}u_{j}A_{k}=u_{\infty}A_{\infty}\text{ strongly in }L^{2}.

Next, compute

\displaystyle dE_{\epsilon}(u_{j},A_{j})[u_{j}-u_{k},A_{j}-A_{k}]

\displaystyle=\int 2\langle(d-iA_{j})u_{j},(d-iA_{j})(u_{j}-u_{k})-iu_{j}(A_{j}-A_{k})\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\int(2\epsilon^{2}\langle dA_{j},d(A_{j}-A_{k})\rangle+\epsilon^{-2}W^{\prime}(u_{j})[u_{j}-u_{k}]),

and observe that the
L^{2}
convergence
u_{j}A_{k}\to u_{\infty}A_{\infty}
gives

\displaystyle dE_{\epsilon}(u_{j},A_{j})[u_{j}-u_{k},A_{j}-A_{k}]

\displaystyle=\int 2\langle(d-iA_{j})u_{j},d(u_{j}-u_{k})\rangle+2\epsilon^{2}\langle dA_{j},d(A_{j}-A_{k})\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\epsilon^{-2}W^{\prime}(u_{j})[u_{j}-u_{k}]+o(1)

as
j,k\to\infty
. For the difference

D_{j,k}:=dE_{\epsilon}(u_{j},A_{j})[u_{j}-u_{k},A_{j}-A_{k}]-dE_{\epsilon}(u_{k},A_{k})[u_{j}-u_{k},A_{j}-A_{k}],

we then see that

D_{j,k}=\int 2|d(u_{j}-u_{k})|^{2}+2\epsilon^{2}|d(A_{j}-A_{k})|^{2}+\epsilon^{-2}(W^{\prime}(u_{j})-W^{\prime}(u_{k}))[u_{j}-u_{k}]+o(1)

as
j,k\to\infty
.

Now, by our assumptions (G) on the structure of
W(u)
, it is not difficult to check (see, e.g.,
[
17
, Corollary 1]
) that the zeroth order term in our computation for
D_{j,k}
satisfies a lower bound

(W^{\prime}(u_{j})-W^{\prime}(u_{k}))[u_{j}-u_{k}]\geq|u_{j}-u_{k}|^{p}-C|u_{j}-u_{k}|

for some constant
C
. In particular, it follows now from the preceding computations and the
L^{1}
convergence
u_{j}\to u_{\infty}
that

D_{j,k}\geq\int 2|d(u_{j}-u_{k})|^{2}+2\epsilon^{2}|d(A_{j}-A_{k})|^{2}+\epsilon^{-2}|u_{j}-u_{k}|^{p}+o(1)

as
j,k\to\infty
. On the other hand, since
dE_{\epsilon}(u_{j},A_{j})\to 0
and
(u_{j}-u_{k},A_{j}-A_{k})
is bounded in
X
, we know also that

D_{j,k}\to 0\text{ as }j,k\to\infty,

and it then follows that
(u_{j},A_{j})
is Cauchy in
X
. In particular,
(u_{j},A_{j})
converges strongly to
(u_{\infty},A_{\infty})
, which necessarily satisfies

\displaystyle dE_{\epsilon}(u_{\infty},A_{\infty})=\lim_{j\to\infty}dE_{\epsilon}(u_{j},A_{j})=0.\qed

Having confirmed that the energies
E_{\epsilon}
satisfy a Palais–Smale condition, we now argue in roughly the same spirit as
[
10
]
,
[
33
]
to produce nontrivial critical points via min-max methods. To begin, note that the space
X
splits as
\mathbb{C}\oplus Y
, where
\mathbb{C}
is identified with the set of constant couples
(\alpha,0)
and

\displaystyle Y:=\Big\{(u,A)\in X:\int_{M}u=0\Big\}.

Definition 7.7
.

Let
\Gamma
denote the set of continuous families of couples
F:\overline{D}\to X
parametrized by the closed unit disk
\overline{D}
, with

\displaystyle F(e^{i\theta})=(e^{i\theta},0)

for all
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
. Equivalently, under the above identification
\mathbb{C}\subset X
, we require
F|_{\partial D}=\operatorname{id}
. We denote by
\omega_{\epsilon}(M)
the “width” of
\Gamma
with respect to the energy
E_{\epsilon}
, namely

\displaystyle\omega_{\epsilon}(M):=\inf_{F\in\Gamma}\max_{y\in\overline{D}}E_{\epsilon}(F(y)).

Thanks to Proposition
7.6
, we can apply classical min-max theory for
C^{1}
functionals on Banach spaces (see e.g.
[
15
, Theorem 3.2]
) to conclude that
\omega_{\epsilon}
is achieved as the energy of a smooth critical couple
(u_{\epsilon},A_{\epsilon})
. In the following proposition, we show that
\omega_{\epsilon}(M)
is positive, so that the corresponding critical couples
(u_{\epsilon},A_{\epsilon})
are nontrivial.

Proposition 7.8
.

We have
\omega_{\epsilon}(M)>0
.

Proof.

We argue by contradiction, though the proof could be made quantitative. Since we are proving only the positivity
\omega_{\epsilon}(M)>0
at this stage—making no reference to the dependence on
\epsilon
—in what follows we take
\epsilon=1
for convenience.
Assume that we have a family
F\in\Gamma
with
\max_{y\in\overline{D}}E(F(y))<\delta
, with
\delta
very small.
Writing
F(y)=(u,A)
, this implies that

(7.7)

\displaystyle\|A-h(A)\|_{W^{1,2}}\leq C\|dA\|_{L^{2}}<C\delta^{1/2},\qquad\|DA\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(\delta^{1/2}+\|h(A)\|).

When
b_{1}(M)\neq 0
, some additional work is required to deduce that the harmonic part
h(A)
of
A
must also be small for all couples
(u,A)=F(y)
in the family. In particular, we will need to employ the following lemma, showing that
h(A)
lies close to the integral lattice
\Lambda\subset\mathcal{H}^{1}(M)
when
E(u,A)<\delta.

Lemma 7.9
.

There exists
C(M)<\infty
such that if
(u,A)\in X
satisfies
E(u,A)<\delta
, then

\operatorname{dist}(h(A),\Lambda)\leq C\delta^{1/2}.

Proof.

As in
[
33
]
, it is convenient to define a box-type norm
|\cdot|_{b}
on the space
\mathcal{H}^{1}(M)
of harmonic one-forms as follows. Fix a collection
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{b_{1}(M)}\in C^{\infty}(S^{1},M)
of embedded loops generating
H_{1}(M;\mathbb{Q})
and, for
h\in\mathcal{H}^{1}(M)
, set

(7.8)

|h|_{b}:=\max_{1\leq i\leq b_{1}(M)}\Big|\int_{\gamma_{i}}h\Big|.

Since
\mathcal{H}^{1}(M)
is finite-dimensional, this is of course equivalent to any other norm on
\mathcal{H}^{1}(M)
. Since
M
is orientable, we may fix a collection of diffeomorphims
\Phi_{i}:B_{1}^{n-1}(0)\times S^{1}\to T(\gamma_{i})
onto tubular neighborhoods
T(\gamma_{i})
of
\gamma_{i}
, such that
\Phi_{i}(0,\theta)=\gamma_{i}(\theta)
. For every
t\in B_{1}^{n-1}
, set
\gamma_{i}^{t}(\theta):=\Phi_{i}(t,\theta)
.

Suppose now that
(u,A)\in X
satisfies the energy bound

(7.9)

E(u,A)=\int_{M}|du-iAu|^{2}+|dA|^{2}+W(u)<\delta.

As a consequence of the curvature bound
\|dA\|_{L^{2}}\leq\delta^{1/2}
and the definition of
X
, it follows that

\|A-h(A)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\leq C\delta

as well. As in the proof of Proposition
7.6
, applying a gauge transformation
\phi\cdot(u,A)
by an appropriate choice of harmonic map
\phi:M\to S^{1}
, we may assume moreover that

|h(A)|_{b}=\operatorname{dist}(h(A),\Lambda)\leq\pi,

which together with the energy bound (
7.9
) and the definition of
X
leads us to the estimate

(7.10)

\int_{M}|A|^{2}\leq C(M).

(Note that making a harmonic change of gauge preserves not only the energy
E(u,A)
, but also the distance
\operatorname{dist}(h(A),\Lambda)
, so it indeed suffices to establish the desired estimate in this gauge.)

Combining these estimates with a simple Fubini argument, we see that there exists a nonempty set
S
of
t\in B_{1}^{n-1}
for which

(7.11)

\int_{\gamma_{i}^{t}}|du-iAu|^{2}+|dA|^{2}+W(u)<C\delta,

(7.12)

\int_{\gamma_{i}^{t}}|A-h(A)|^{2}<C\delta,

and

(7.13)

\int_{\gamma_{i}^{t}}|A|^{2}\leq C.

Recalling the pointwise bound (
7.2
) for
W(u)
, observe next that

|d(1-|u|)^{2}|=2(1-|u|)|d|u||\leq CW(u)+|du-iAu|^{2},

so that, along a curve
\gamma_{i}^{t}
satisfying (
7.11
), it follows that

(7.14)

\|(1-|u|)^{2}\|_{C^{0}}\leq C\|(1-|u|)^{2}\|_{W^{1,1}}\leq C\delta.

Now, choose
\delta<\delta_{1}(M)
sufficiently small that (
7.14
) gives

\|1-|u|\|_{C^{0}}\leq\eta<\frac{1}{2}

on
\gamma_{i}^{t}
, so that
\phi=u/|u|
defines there an
S^{1}
-valued map
\phi:\gamma_{i}^{t}\to S^{1}
, whose degree is given by

2\pi\operatorname{deg}(\phi)=\int_{\gamma_{i}^{t}}|u|^{-2}\langle du,iu\rangle.

When (
7.11
)–(
7.13
) hold, we observe next that

\int_{\gamma_{i}^{t}}|u|^{2}|A-|u|^{-2}\langle iu,du\rangle|=\int_{\gamma_{i}^{t}}|\langle iu,iAu-du\rangle|\leq C\delta^{1/2}.

Since
|u|\geq\frac{1}{2}
on
\gamma_{i}^{t}
, it follows that

(7.15)

\Big|2\pi\operatorname{deg}(\phi)-\int_{\gamma_{i}^{t}}A\Big|\leq\int_{\gamma_{i}^{t}}|A-|u|^{-2}\langle iu,du\rangle|\leq C\delta^{1/2}

as well. Combining this with (
7.12
), we then deduce that

\Big|2\pi\operatorname{deg}(\phi)-\int_{\gamma_{i}^{t}}h(A)\Big|\leq C\delta^{1/2}.

On the other hand, we already made a gauge transformation so that

\Big|\int_{\gamma_{i}}h(A)\Big|=\Big|\int_{\gamma_{i}^{t}}h(A)\Big|\leq\pi,

so for
\delta
chosen sufficiently small that
C\delta^{1/2}<\pi
, it follows that the degree
\operatorname{deg}(\phi)=0
.
In particular, we can now conclude that

|h(A)|_{b}=\max_{i}\Big|\int_{\gamma_{i}}h(A)\Big|\leq C\delta^{1/2},

giving the desired estimate.
∎

Returning to the proof of Proposition
7.8
, suppose again that we have a family
\overline{D}\ni y\mapsto F(y)\in X
in
\Gamma
with

\max_{y\in\overline{D}}E(F(y))<\delta.

For
\delta<\delta_{1}(M)
sufficiently small, it follows from the lemma that
\operatorname{dist}_{b}(h(A),\Lambda)<\pi
for every couple
(u,A)=F(y)
in the family. In particular, since the assignment
(u,A)\mapsto h(A)
gives a continuous map
X\to\mathcal{H}^{1}(M)
, and since
h(A)=A=0
for
y\in\partial\overline{D}
, it follows that
0
is the nearest point in the lattice
\Lambda
to
h(A)
for every
y\in\overline{D}
, and the estimate therefore becomes

\|h(A)\|\leq C\delta^{1/2}.

In particular, combining this with (
7.7
), we see now that

(7.16)

\|A\|_{W^{1,2}}\leq C\delta^{1/2}

for every couple
(u,A)=F(y)
in the family.

Now, for
(u,A)=F(y)
, our structural assumption (G) on
W(u)
gives

\|u\|_{L^{p}}^{p}\leq C+E(u,A)\leq C+\delta,

which together with the smallness

\|A\|_{L^{2^{*}}}\leq C\|A\|_{W^{1,2}}\leq C\delta^{1/2}

of
A
in
L^{2^{*}}
(recalling that
p>n
) gives

\int_{M}|uA|^{2}\leq C\delta.

Combining this with the fact that
\int|du-iuA|^{2}\leq E(u,A)<\delta
by assumption, we then deduce that

\int_{M}|du|^{2}\leq C\delta

as well.

Finally, by (
7.2
) and the Poincaré inequality, we have

\displaystyle 1-\Big|\frac{1}{\operatorname{vol}(M)}\int_{M}u\Big|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{M}|1-|u||+C\int_{M}\Big|u-\frac{1}{\operatorname{vol}(M)}\int_{M}u\Big|

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\int_{M}W(u)\Big)^{1/2}+C\Big(\int_{M}|du|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\delta^{1/2}.

As a consequence, we find that
\int_{M}u_{y}
is nonzero for all
(u_{y},A_{y})=F(y)
in the family. But then the averaging map

(7.17)

\displaystyle\overline{D}\to\mathbb{C},\qquad y\mapsto\frac{\int_{M}u_{y}}{|\int_{M}u_{y}|}.

gives a retraction
\overline{D}\to\partial\overline{D}
, whose nonexistence is well known. This gives the desired contradiction.
∎

Having shown positivity
\omega_{\epsilon}(M)>0

) from the following simple fact.

Proposition 7.10
.

There exists

and

If

E_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla)=0
.

Remark 7.11
.

For future reference, we make the obvious observation that the trivial case
E_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla)=0
can only occur when the bundle

is trivial.

Proof.

As discussed in the appendix, it is straightforward to see that critical points are smooth up to change of gauge.
We claim that, whenever
E_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla)>0
,
u
has to vanish at some point
x_{0}\in M
.
Once we have this, Corollary
4.4
implies that
r^{2-n}E_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla,B_{r}(x_{0}))
has a lower bound independent of
\epsilon
for any
\epsilon<r<\operatorname{inj}(M)
, and the statement follows.

Indeed, if the claim fails, then
u
is nowhere vanishing, so
L
must be trivial and we can use the section
\frac{u}{|u|}
to identify
L
isometrically with the trivial line bundle
M\times\mathbb{C}
, equipped with the canonical Hermitian metric.
Under this identification,
u:M\to\mathbb{C}
takes values into positive real numbers. Writing
\nabla=d-iA
and observing that
\langle\nabla u,iu\rangle=-|u|^{2}A
, (
2.5
) becomes

\displaystyle\epsilon^{2}d^{*}dA+|u|^{2}A=0.

Integrating against
A
we get
\int_{M}(\epsilon^{2}|dA|^{2}+u^{2}|A|^{2})=0
, so
A=0
and
\nabla
is the trivial connection.
At a minimum point
y_{0}
for

, (
3.4
) gives

\displaystyle 0\leq\frac{1}{2}\Delta|u|^{2}=|du|^{2}-\frac{1}{2\epsilon^{2}}(1-|u|^{2})|u|^{2}=-\frac{1}{2\epsilon^{2}}(1-u^{2})u^{2},

which forces

and thus
u=1
everywhere, giving the contradiction
E_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla)=0
.
∎

Finally, we turn to the uniform upper bound.
In the next statement,
L\to M
is a Hermitian line bundle with a fixed Hermitian reference connection
\nabla_{0}
.
We identify any other Hermitian connection
\nabla
with the real one-form
A
such that
\nabla s=\nabla_{0}s-iA\otimes s
for all sections
s
.

Proposition 7.12
.

Given a smooth section
u:M\to L
, we can find a smooth couple
(u^{\prime},A^{\prime})
such that

(7.18)

\displaystyle E_{\epsilon}(u^{\prime},A^{\prime})\leq C\left(\epsilon^{-2}\operatorname{vol}\Big(\Big\{|u|\leq\frac{1}{2}\Big\}\Big)+(1+\epsilon^{2}\|\nabla_{0}u\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2})\int_{\{|u|\leq\frac{1}{2}\}}|\nabla_{0}u|^{2}+\epsilon^{2}\int_{M}|\omega_{0}|^{2}\right)

for a universal constant
C
.

Proof.

On
\{u\neq 0\}
we let

\displaystyle w:=\frac{u}{|u|},\qquad A\otimes iw:=\nabla_{0}w.

The compatibility of
\nabla_{0}
with the Hermitian metric on
L
forces
\langle\nabla_{0}w,w\rangle=0
, so that
A
is a real one-form. Equivalently, viewing
w
as a map from
M
to the circle bundle
U(L)
of
L
, which is a principal
S^{1}
-bundle with induced connection form
\varpi\in\Omega^{1}(U(L),\mathbb{R})
, we have
A=\varpi\circ dw
.

We fix a smooth function
\rho:[0,\infty]\to[0,1]
with

\displaystyle\rho(t)=0\text{ for }t\leq\frac{1}{4},\qquad\rho(t)=1\text{ for }t\geq\frac{1}{2}

and we set

\displaystyle(u^{\prime},A^{\prime}):=\rho(|u|)(w,A)

(the right-hand side is meant to be zero on
\{u=0\}
).
Writing
F_{\nabla_{0}}=-i\omega_{0}
, observe that

\displaystyle|F_{A}|=|dA+\omega_{0}|=0\quad\text{on }\{u\neq 0\},

so that
e_{\epsilon}(u^{\prime},A^{\prime})=0
on
\{|u|>\frac{1}{2}\}
. From the estimates
|A|=|\nabla_{0}w|\leq 2|u|^{-1}|\nabla_{0}u|
and
|d|u||\leq|\nabla_{0}u|
, it follows that also

\displaystyle|\nabla_{0}u^{\prime}|

\displaystyle\leq C|\nabla_{0}u|,

\displaystyle|A^{\prime}|

\displaystyle\leq C|\nabla_{0}u|,

\displaystyle|dA^{\prime}|

\displaystyle\leq|\rho^{\prime}(|u|)d|u|\wedge A|+|\omega_{0}|\leq C|\nabla_{0}u||d|u||+|\omega_{0}|,

and the statement follows immediately.
∎

Proof of (
7.1
).

The method used in
[
33
, Section 3]
gives a continuous map
H:\overline{D}\to W^{1,2}\cap C^{0}(M,\mathbb{C})

\displaystyle\|dH(y)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\epsilon^{-1},\quad\int_{\{|H(y)|\leq\frac{3}{4}\}}|dH(y)|^{2}\leq C,\quad\operatorname{vol}\Big(\Big\{|u|\leq\frac{1}{2}\Big\}\Big)\leq C\epsilon^{2}

(the full Dirichlet energy having a worse bound

To each section

7.2.
Minimizers for nontrivial line bundles

Suppose now that
L
is a nontrivial line bundle, equipped with a Hermitian metric. Fix a smooth Hermitian connection
\nabla_{0}
and identify any other Hermitian connection
\nabla
with the real one-form
A
such that

\displaystyle\nabla=\nabla_{0}-iA.

We can define
\widehat{X}
and
X
as in the previous subsection. With this notation, observe that the curvature of

\displaystyle F_{\nabla}=F_{\nabla_{0}}-idA.

Hence, writing

, we have

\displaystyle E_{\epsilon}(u,\nabla)=\int_{M}|\nabla_{0}u-A\otimes iu|^{2}+\epsilon^{-2}\int_{M}W(u)+\epsilon^{2}\int_{M}|\omega_{0}+dA|^{2}.

Definition 7.13
.

For a fixed
n<p<\infty
, we define
\widehat{X}
to be the Banach space of couples
(u,A)
, where

is an

section and

, both of class

, with the norm

\displaystyle\|(u,A)\|:=\|u\|_{L^{p}}+\|\nabla_{0}u\|_{L^{2}}+\|A\|_{L^{2}}+\|DA\|_{L^{2}}.

We let

.

The analogous statements to Remark
7.4
and Propositions
7.5
and
7.6
hold, with identical proofs (replacing

and

with
\nabla_{0}u
and

, respectively).

Arguing as in the proof of Proposition

, it is easy to see that a minimizing sequence of couples for

converges—in the appropriate Coulomb gauge—to a global minimizer

. We now show that the energy of these minimizers enjoys uniform upper and lower bounds as

.

Proof of (
7.1
).

The lower bound in (
7.1
) follows directly from Proposition
7.10
. In order to obtain the upper bound, pick a smooth section
s:M\to L
transverse to the zero section (see, e.g.,
[
24
, Theorem IV.2.1]
) and let
N:=\{s=0\}
, which is a smooth embedded

-submanifold of

.
Proposition

applied to

gives a couple

with

\displaystyle E_{\epsilon}(u_{\epsilon}^{\prime},A_{\epsilon}^{\prime})\leq C\epsilon^{-2}\operatorname{vol}\Big(\Big\{|\epsilon^{-1}s|\leq\frac{1}{2}\Big\}\Big)+C\epsilon^{2}\int_{M}|\omega_{0}|^{2}.

By transversality of

, the set

is contained in a

\displaystyle E_{\epsilon}(u_{\epsilon},A_{\epsilon})\leq E_{\epsilon}(u_{\epsilon}^{\prime},A_{\epsilon}^{\prime})\leq\epsilon^{-2}\operatorname{vol}\Big(\Big\{|s|\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2}\Big\}\Big)\leq C.\qed

Remark 7.14
.

N
can be oriented in such a way that

is Poincaré dual to the Euler class

. The fact that the energy of our competitors concentrates along

suggests that, given a sequence of global minimizers

, which also satisfies

.

Appendix. Interior regularity in the Coulomb gauge

In this short appendix, we describe the essential ingredients needed to establish local regularity in the Coulomb gauge for finite-energy critical points
(u,A)
of the (
\epsilon=1
) abelian Higgs energy
E(u,A)
, collecting some estimates which will be of use elsewhere in the paper.

Consider the manifold with boundary
(\overline{\Omega}^{n},g)
given by a smooth, contractible domain
\Omega^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
equipped with a
C^{2}
metric
g
, and let
L\cong\mathbb{C}\times\Omega
be the trivial line bundle over
\Omega
, with the standard Hermitian structure. With respect to the metric
g
, we then define the Yang–Mills–Higgs energies

E(u,A):=\int_{\Omega}e(u,A)=\int_{\Omega}|du-iA|^{2}+|dA|^{2}+W(u)

as in the preceding section. By Proposition
7.5
in the preceding section, it is easy to see that a pair
(u,A)
in
W^{1,2}
with

(A.1)

|u|\leq 1

is a critical point for
E
(with respect to smooth perturbations supported in
\Omega
) if and only if the equations

(A.2)

\displaystyle d^{*}dA

\displaystyle=\langle du-iAu,iu\rangle,

(A.3)

\displaystyle\Delta u

\displaystyle=2\langle idu,A\rangle+|A|^{2}u-\frac{1}{2}(1-|u|^{2})u-i(d^{*}A)u

are satisfied distributionally in
\Omega
, where all geometric quantities and operators are defined with respect to the metric
g
.

Now, given a pair
(u,A)
in
W^{1,2}
satisfying (
A.2
)–(
A.3
) and

(A.4)

E(u,A)\leq\Lambda<\infty,

we can select a
local Coulomb gauge
adapted to
A
as follows. Denote by
\theta\in W^{2,2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})
the unique solution of the Neumann problem

(A.5)

\Delta\theta=d^{*}A\text{ in }\Omega;\quad\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial\nu}=-A(\nu)\text{ on }\partial\Omega

with zero mean
\int_{\Omega}\theta=0
. Then the gauge-transformed pair

(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{A}):=(e^{i\theta}u,A+d\theta)

continues to satisfy (
A.2
)–(
A.3
), with

E(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{A})=E(u,A)\leq\Lambda,

but now with the additional constraints

(A.6)

d^{*}\widetilde{A}=0\text{ on }\Omega;\quad\widetilde{A}(\nu)=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega.

For the remainder of the section, we will assume that the pair
(u,A)
is already in the Coulomb gauge on
\Omega
, so that
A
satisfies (
A.6
). Note that the last term in the equation (
A.3
) then vanishes, so that we have

(A.7)

\Delta u=2\langle idu,A\rangle+|A|^{2}u-\frac{1}{2}(1-|u|^{2})u.

We now establish the local regularity for critical points
(u,A)
in the Coulomb gauge, giving in particular estimates for
(u,A)
in
W^{2,q}
norms.

Proposition A.1
.

Let
(u,A)
solve (
A.2
)–(
A.3
) in the Coulomb gauge (
A.6
) on
(\Omega,g)
, with
|u|\leq 1
. If

(A.8)

E(u,A;\Omega)\leq\Lambda

and

(A.9)

\|g\|_{C^{2}}+\|g^{-1}\|_{C^{2}}\leq\Lambda,

then for every compactly supported subdomain
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\subset\Omega
and
q\in(1,\infty)
there exists
C_{q}(\Lambda,\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})<\infty
such that

(A.10)

\|u\|_{W^{2,q}(\Omega^{\prime})}+\|A\|_{W^{2,q}(\Omega^{\prime})}\leq C_{q}.

Proof.

To begin, note that (
A.2
), (
A.6
), and standard Bochner–Weitzenböck identities give the (weak) subequation

\displaystyle\Delta\frac{1}{2}|A|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq-\langle\Delta_{H}A,A\rangle+|DA|^{2}+\operatorname{Ric}(A,A)

\displaystyle\geq-C(\Lambda)|A|^{2}-C|du-iAu|

for
|A|^{2}
.
On the other hand, as in Section
3
, we also obtain from (
A.3
) the relation

\Delta\frac{1}{2}|u|^{2}=|du-iAu|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}(1-|u|^{2})|u|^{2},

and combining the two (recalling also that
|u|\leq 1
), we find an estimate of the form

(A.11)

\frac{1}{2}\Delta(|A|^{2}+|u|^{2})\geq-C(|A|^{2}+|u|^{2})-C.

Applying Moser iteration to (
A.11
), we see in particular that

(A.12)

\|A\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{1})}^{2}\leq C(\Lambda,\Omega_{1},\Omega)(1+\|A\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}^{2})

for any
\Omega_{1}\subset\subset\Omega
. Moreover, by standard estimates for one-forms
A
satisfying (
A.6
) (see, e.g.,
[
20
]
), we have the global
L^{2}
bound

\|A\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}\leq\|dA\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\Lambda^{1/2},

which together with the preceding
L^{\infty}
estimate gives

(A.13)

\|A\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{1})}\leq C(\Lambda,\Omega_{1},\Omega)

for any subdomain
\Omega_{1}\subset\subset\Omega
.

Now, fixing some intermediate domain
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\subset\Omega_{1}\subset\subset\Omega
between
\Omega^{\prime}
and
\Omega
, the equation (
A.7
) together with the
L^{\infty}(\Omega_{1})
estimate for
A
give pointwise bounds of the form

(A.14)

|\Delta u|\leq C(\Omega_{1},\Omega,\Lambda)(|du|+1)\text{ in }\Omega_{1}.

And since

|du|\leq|du-iAu|+|A|\leq e(u,A)+C

in
\Omega_{1}
, we obtain from the energy bound
E(u,A)\leq\Lambda
and (
A.14
) the simple estimate

\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{1})}\leq C(\Omega_{1},\Omega,\Lambda),

and consequently

\|u\|_{W^{2,2}(\Omega_{2})}\leq C

for any
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\subset\Omega_{2}\subset\subset\Omega_{1}
. Returning to the pointwise bound (
A.14
), we can now employ a simple iteration argument—combining
L^{q}
regularity theory with the Sobolev embedding
W^{2,r}\hookrightarrow W^{1,\frac{rn}{n-r}}
—over successive domains between
\Omega^{\prime}
and
\Omega
, to arrive at the desired
W^{2,q}
estimates for
u
.

Returning finally to the equation (
A.2
) for
A
, in the Coulomb gauge, we see that

\Delta_{H}A=\langle du-iAu,iu\rangle,

and it therefore follows from the preceding estimates that

\|A\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega^{\prime\prime})}+\|\Delta A\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega^{\prime\prime})}\leq C(\Omega^{\prime\prime},\Omega,\Lambda)

for some intermediate domain
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\subset\Omega^{\prime\prime}\subset\subset\Omega
. In particular, this gives us upper bounds for
\|\Delta A\|_{L^{q}(\Omega^{\prime\prime})}
for every
q\in(1,\infty)
, and
L^{q}
regularity theory therefore gives us the desired estimates for
A
in
W^{2,q}(\Omega^{\prime})
.
∎

Finally, we remark that higher regularity of

and

in the Coulomb gauge follows in a standard way—e.g., via Schauder theory—from the

estimates obtained in the preceding proposition.

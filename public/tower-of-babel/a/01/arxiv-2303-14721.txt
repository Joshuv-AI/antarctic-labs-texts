Abstract
. The Geometrical Lemma is a classical result in the theory of (complex) smooth representations of p p -adic reductive groups, which helps to analyze the parabolic restriction of a parabolically induced representation by providing a filtration whose graded pieces are (smaller) parabolic inductions of parabolic restrictions. In this article, we establish the Geometrical Lemma for the derived category of smooth mod p p representations of a p p -adic reductive group. As an important application we compute higher extension groups between parabolically induced representations, which in a slightly different context had been achieved by Hauseux assuming a conjecture of Emerton concerning the higher ordinary parts functor. We also compute the (cohomology functors of the) left adjoint of derived parabolic induction on principal series and generalized Steinberg representations.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
History and motivation

Fix a finite extension
\mathfrak{F}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and let
G
be (the group of

\mathfrak{F}
-points of) a connected reductive
\mathfrak{F}
-group. In the theory of
complex smooth representations of
G
, the Geometrical Lemma, independently due
to Bernstein–Zelevinsky
[
BZ77
]
and Casselman

[
Cas95
]
, is one of the main tools in the classification of
irreducible smooth representations in terms of parabolically induced
representations. To state it, we fix a parabolic subgroup
P=M\ltimes U
of
G
with Levi quotient
M
and unipotent radical

U
. Denote by
\nInd_{P}^{G}\colon\Rep_{\mathbb{C}}(M)\to\Rep_{\mathbb{C}}(G)
the normalized parabolic induction functor on the categories of
smooth representations. Its left adjoint is the normalized parabolic restriction
functor
\nPRes^{G}_{P}
. If
Q=L\ltimes N
is another parabolic
subgroup with Levi quotient
L
and unipotent radical
N
such that
P\cap Q
contains a fixed minimal parabolic subgroup, then the Geometrical
Lemma states that the functor
\nPRes^{G}_{Q}\nInd_{P}^{G}
admits a filtration
by subfunctors such that the graded pieces are given by functors of the form

\nInd_{g^{-1}Pg\cap L}^{L}\circ g_{*}^{-1}\circ\nPRes^{M}_{M\cap gQg^{-1}},

where
g_{*}^{-1}\colon\Rep_{\mathbb{C}}\bigl(M\cap gLg^{-1}\bigr)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Rep_{\mathbb{C}}\bigl(g^{-1}Mg\cap L\bigr)
is the equivalence of categories induced
by conjugation with
g^{-1}
and
g
runs through a certain set of double coset
representatives of
P\backslash G/Q
. All known proofs rely on the use of Haar
measures and the exactness of
\nPRes^{G}_{P}
. They are easily adapted to prove
a Geometrical Lemma for smooth representations over a field
k
of
characteristic
\neq p
.

However, if
k
is a field of characteristic
p
, then the proofs break down
completely. Since there is no modulus character, one cannot talk about
normalization. We denote by
\pInd_{P}^{G}\colon\Rep_{k}(M)\to\Rep_{k}(G)
the
unnormalized parabolic induction functor and
\mathrm{L}^{0}(U,\raisebox{-2.0pt}{$-$})
its
left adjoint. In this context, Haar measures do not exist and

\mathrm{L}^{0}(U,\raisebox{-2.0pt}{$-$})
is not exact; the Geometrical Lemma takes on a
much simpler but also less satisfactory form: the functor
\mathrm{L}^{0}(N,\raisebox{-2.0pt}{$-$})\pInd_{P}^{G}
is isomorphic to
\pInd_{P\cap L}^{L}\mathrm{L}^{0}(M\cap N,\raisebox{-2.0pt}{$-$})
, see

[
AHV19
, Theorem 5.5]
; in other words, there is a filtration with only
one graded piece. The reason for this pathological behaviour comes down to the
fact that there exists no non-trivial Haar measure on
U
.

As it turns out, there is a full Geometrical Lemma once we pass to the derived
categories.

1.2.
Main results

From now on, let
k
be a field of characteristic
p
. For any
p
-adic Lie
group
H
we denote by
\mathrm{D}(H)
the unbounded derived category of the category

\Rep_{k}(H)
of smooth
k
-linear representations of
H
. With the notation from above the parabolic induction extends to a derived functor
\mathrm{R}\!\pInd_{P}^{G}\colon\mathrm{D}(M)\to\mathrm{D}(G)
.
By the main result of
[
Hey23
]
, there exists a left adjoint

\mathrm{L}(U,\raisebox{-2.0pt}{$-$})
. It should be noted that we need to assume that
\mathfrak{F}
has characteristic zero. If
\mathfrak{F}
has positive characteristic, then
G
is not a
p
-adic Lie group and the methods of
op. cit.
do not apply; in particular the existence of
\mathrm{L}(U,\raisebox{-2.0pt}{$-$})
remains unknown.

To state the
Geometrical Lemma, we fix a set
\mathcal{N}_{P,Q}
of double coset
representatives of
P\backslash G/Q
which normalizes a maximal
\mathfrak{F}
-split
torus (of
G
) that is contained in
P\cap Q
. For the notion of filtration on a
triangulated functor, we refer to Definition
3.2.3
. If
H
is a
p
-adic Lie group, we denote by
\dim H
its dimension as a
p
-adic manifold in the sense of
[
Sch11
]
.

A Theorem

(Corollary
3.3.4
)
.

The functor
\mathrm{L}(N,\raisebox{-2.0pt}{$-$})\circ\mathrm{R}\!\pInd_{P}^{G}\colon\mathrm{D}(M)\to\mathrm{D}(L)
admits a
filtration of length
\lvert\mathcal{N}_{P,Q}\rvert
with graded pieces of the
form

\mathrm{R}\!\pInd_{n^{-1}Pn\cap L}^{L}\circ(\omega_{n}\otimes_{k}\raisebox{-2.0pt}{$-$})\circ n_{*}^{-1}\circ\mathrm{L}(M\cap nNn^{-1},\raisebox{-2.0pt}{$-$}),

for
n\in\mathcal{N}_{P,Q}
, where
\omega_{n}\in\mathrm{D}(n^{-1}Mn\cap L)
is a
character in cohomological degree
-\dim(n^{-1}\overline{U}n\cap N)
and

\overline{U}
is the unipotent radical of the parabolic opposite
P
.

To fix ideas, we note that, if one chooses
\mathcal{N}_{P,Q}
carefully and if

G
is
\mathfrak{F}
-split, then
\omega_{n}
is concentrated in degree

-[\mathfrak{F}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]\ell(w)
, where
\ell(w)
denotes the length of the image
w
of

n
in the (finite) Weyl group. Thus,
\omega_{n}
contributes a cohomological
shift which is not detected on the abelian categories; this gives a conceptual
explanation of why there is no proper Geometrical Lemma for (underived) smooth
mod
p
representations. The twist by the character
\omega_{n}
is not surprising
as it occurs also in the classical context although it is hidden in the
normalization of the parabolic induction and restriction functors.

The proof of Theorem
A
follows the general strategy
employed by Bernstein–Zelevinsky and Casselman in that the problem is reduced
to checking certain compatibilities
between compact induction and (derived) coinvariants. Since we do not have Haar
measures at our disposal (which are the primary tool in the classical setting),
it is not possible to write down the required isomorphisms by hand. In comparison, our proof is more conceptual. As a non-formal input the proof uses that the derived inflation functor

\mathrm{R}\Inf^{M}_{P}\colon\mathrm{D}(M)\to\mathrm{D}(P)
is fully faithful, which follows from the fact
that
U
is a unipotent group, see
[
Hey23
, Example 3.4.24]
. Hence, the
same proof will apply in other contexts as well.

A general method to determine explicitly the characters
\omega_{n}
, for
n\in\mathcal{N}_{P,Q}
, is presented in Propositions
2.3.2

and
2.3.2
. This is applied in Lemma
4.1.3

to deduce a concrete description of the characters
\omega_{n}
.

As an application, we will compute several
\Ext
-groups between parabolically
induced representations. These are virtually identical with the main results of

[
Hau16
,
Hau18
]
; the important differences are that Hauseux
computes higher extensions in the category of locally admissible representations
and relies for the strongest of these results on an open conjecture of Emerton

[
Eme10
, Conjecture 3.7.2]
, which to our knowledge has only been resolved
for
\GL_{2}
. In contrast, the
\Ext
-groups in this paper are computed in the
category of all smooth representations and do not rely on conjectural
statements.

To state the results concerning
\Ext
-groups, we introduce some
notation. We fix a maximal
\mathfrak{F}
-split torus contained in a minimal parabolic
subgroup
B
. These choices come with a (relative) root system together with a
set of simple roots. Fix standard parabolic subgroups
P=M\ltimes U
and
Q=L\ltimes N
. We choose a distinguished set
\mathcal{N}_{P,Q}
of
double coset representatives of
P\backslash G/Q
, see
§
4.1.3
for more details. For each
n\in\mathcal{N}_{P,Q}

we consider the smooth character
\delta_{n}
of
n^{-1}Mn\cap L
given by

\omega_{n}=\delta_{n}[\dim(n^{-1}\overline{U}n\cap N)]
.

B Theorem

(Theorems
4.2.6
and
4.2.6
)
.

(a)

Assume
Q=B
. Let
\chi\colon M\to k^{\times}
be a smooth character, let

r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, and denote
Z(L)
the center of
L
.

(i)

Let
\chi^{\prime}\colon L\to k^{\times}
be a smooth character. If

\Ext_{G}^{r}\bigl(\pInd_{P}^{G}\chi,\pInd_{B}^{G}\chi^{\prime}\bigr)\neq 0,

then there exists
n\in\mathcal{N}_{P,B}
such that
\dim(n^{-1}\overline{U}n\cap N)\leq r
and
\chi^{\prime}\cong\delta_{n}\otimes_{k}n_{*}^{-1}\chi
after restriction to
Z(L)
.

(ii)

Assume
\delta_{n}\otimes_{k}n_{*}^{-1}\chi\not\cong\chi
after restriction to

Z(L)
, for all
n\in\mathcal{N}_{P,B}
. For each
n\in\mathcal{N}_{P,B}
with

\dim(n^{-1}\overline{U}n\cap N)\leq r
one has
k
-linear isomorphisms

\Ext_{G}^{r}\bigl(\pInd_{P}^{G}\chi,\pInd_{B}^{G}(\delta_{n}\otimes_{k}n_{*}^{-1}\chi)\bigr)\cong\Ext_{L}^{r-\dim(n^{-1}\overline{U}n\cap N)}(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1})\cong\mathrm{H}^{r-\dim(n^{-1}\overline{U}n\cap N)}(L,k),

where
\mathrm{H}^{*}(L,k)
denotes continuous group cohomology.

(b)

Let
V\in\Rep_{k}(M)
and
W\in\Rep_{k}(L)
.

(i)

Assume
P\nsubseteq Q
and
P\nsupseteq Q
, that
V
is left cuspidal and
that
W
is right cuspidal (see Definition
4.2.6
). Then

\Ext_{G}^{1}\bigl(\pInd_{P}^{G}V,\pInd_{Q}^{G}W\bigr)=0.

(ii)

Assume
P=Q
. For each
0\leq i<[\mathfrak{F}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]
the functor
\pInd_{P}^{G}

induces a
k
-linear isomorphism

\Ext^{i}_{M}(V,W)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Ext_{G}^{i}\bigl(\pInd_{P}^{G}V,\pInd_{P}^{G}W\bigr).

If moreover
V
is left cuspidal or
W
is right cuspidal, and

V
and
W
admit distinct central characters, then

\Ext_{G}^{[\mathfrak{F}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]}\bigl(\pInd_{P}^{G}V,\pInd_{P}^{G}W\bigr)\cong\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Delta_{M}^{\perp,1}}\Hom_{M}\bigl(\delta_{n_{\alpha}}\otimes_{k}n_{\alpha*}^{-1}V,W\bigr),

where
\Delta_{M}^{\perp,1}
denotes the set of simple roots
\alpha
of
G

which are orthogonal to all simple roots of
M
and such that the associated
root space has dimension
[\mathfrak{F}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]
as a
p
-adic manifold. Here,

n_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{N}_{P,P}
denotes the lift of the simple reflection
corresponding to
\alpha
.

(iii)

Assume
P\supsetneqq Q
and that
V
is left cuspidal. For all
0\leq i\leq[\mathfrak{F}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]
, the functor
\pInd_{P}^{G}
induces a
k
-linear isomorphism

\Ext_{M}^{i}\bigl(V,\pInd_{M\cap Q}^{M}W\bigr)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Ext_{G}^{i}\bigl(\pInd_{P}^{G}V,\pInd_{Q}^{G}W\bigr).

(iv)

Dually, assume
P\subsetneqq Q
and that
W
is right cuspidal. For all

0\leq i\leq[\mathfrak{F}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]
, the functor
\pInd_{Q}^{G}
induces a
k
-linear isomorphism

\Ext_{L}^{i}\bigl(\pInd_{P\cap L}^{L}V,W\bigr)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Ext_{G}^{i}\bigl(\pInd_{P}^{G}V,\pInd_{Q}^{G}W\bigr).

We further compute the representations
\mathrm{L}^{-j}(N,\Sp_{P}^{G})
for all
j\geq 0
,
where
\Sp_{P}^{G}
denotes the generalized Steinberg representation attached to

P
,
i.e.
, the unique irreducible quotient of
\pInd_{P}^{G}(\mathbf{1})
; see
Theorem
4.3.4
and
Corollary
4.3.4
. To our knowledge, this is the first
computation of this kind. This raises the question whether one can compute

\mathrm{L}^{-j}(N,V)
for all irreducible smooth representations
V
. However, for
supersingular
V
the answer seems to be out of reach with the current methods
available. A naive hope would be that
\mathrm{L}(N,V)=0
for all supersingular
V
,
but
[
HW22
, Theorem 10.37]
shows that already for
G=\GL_{2}(\mathfrak{F})
,
where
\mathfrak{F}\supsetneq\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is unramified, there exist a supersingular
representation
V
and a principal series
\pInd_{P}^{G}W
such that

\Ext_{G}^{1}(V,\pInd_{P}^{G}W)\neq 0
, and this implies
\mathrm{L}^{-1}(U,V)\neq 0
. In view of
this it is unclear what one should expect.

1.3.
Organization of the paper

The Geometrical Lemma is the content of Corollary
3.3.4
. The preparatory lemmas concerning compact induction and derived coinvariants are proved in §
2.2
; these in turn rely on the abstract results about functors in monoidal categories which are presented in appendix
A.1
. The various characters that implicitly appear in the Geometrical Lemma are completely determined in §
2.3
.

Regarding applications, we compute
\mathrm{L}^{-j}(N,V)
whenever
V
is a principal series representation (Example
4.2
) or a generalized Steinberg representation (Theorem
4.3.4
and Corollary
4.3.4
). Finally, we use the Geometrical Lemma to compute many Ext-groups between parabolically induced representations in Theorems
4.2.6
and
4.2.6
.

2.1.
Notation and conventions

We fix a finite extension
\mathfrak{F}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and a coefficient field
k
of characteristic
p
.
The mod
p
cyclotomic character of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times}
is given by the composition
\varepsilon\colon\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times}\to\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}\to\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}\subseteq k^{\times}
, where the first map is given by
x\mapsto xp^{-\val_{p}(x)}
for the usual
p
-adic valuation
\val_{p}
.
We put
\varepsilon_{\mathfrak{F}}\coloneqq\varepsilon\circ\mathrm{Nm}_{\mathfrak{F}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
, where
\mathrm{Nm}_{\mathfrak{F}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
is the norm of
\mathfrak{F}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.

If
\mathbf{H}
is an algebraic group defined over
\mathfrak{F}
, we denote its
group of
\mathfrak{F}
-points by the corresponding lightface letter, that is,

H=\mathbf{H}(\mathfrak{F})
.

We fix a field
k
of characteristic
p>0
.

For a
p
-adic Lie group
G
, we denote by
\dim(G)
its dimension as a
p
-adic manifold. We denote by
\Rep_{k}(G)
the Grothendieck abelian category of smooth
k
-linear
G
-representations.

The (unbounded) derived category of
\Rep_{k}(G)
is denoted by
\mathrm{D}(G)
; it is a
tensor triangulated category
, that is, a triangulated category which is symmetric monoidal and such that the functors
\raisebox{-2.0pt}{$-$}\otimes_{k}X
are triangulated for any
X
in
\mathrm{D}(G)
. The tensor unit is
\mathbf{1}=k[0]
. We denote the right adjoint of
\raisebox{-2.0pt}{$-$}\otimes_{k}X
by
\iHom(X,\raisebox{-2.0pt}{$-$})\colon\mathrm{D}(G)\to\mathrm{D}(G)
.
The
smooth dual
of
X
is denoted by
X^{\vee}\coloneqq\iHom(X,\mathbf{1})
.

The category
\mathrm{D}(G)
is naturally enriched over
\mathrm{D}(k)\coloneqq\mathrm{D}(\{1\})
, the unbounded derived category of the category of
k
-vector spaces; we denote by
\RHom_{G}(X,Y)\in\mathrm{D}(k)
the derived Hom-complex, for each
X,Y\in\mathrm{D}(G)
; it defines a triangulated functor in each variable.

The derived category
\mathrm{D}(G)
comes with a natural t-structure. For any integer
n\in\mathbb{Z}
we denote by
\mathrm{D}^{\leq n}(G)
(resp.
\mathrm{D}^{\geq n}(G)
) the full subcategory of objects
X
in
\mathrm{D}(G)
satisfying
\mathrm{H}^{i}(X)=0
for all
i>n
(resp.
i<n
). Denote by
\mathrm{H}^{i}\colon\mathrm{D}(G)\to\Rep_{k}(G)
the
i
-th cohomology functor. Note that
\RHom_{G}(X,Y)\in\mathrm{D}^{\geq 0}(k)
provided
X\in\mathrm{D}^{\leq 0}(G)
and
Y\in\mathrm{D}^{\geq 0}(G)
.

If
F,G\colon\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{D}
are two functors, we denote by
\Nat(F,G)
the class of natural transformations from
F
to
G
.

2.2.
Compact induction and derived coinvariants

Let
k
be a field of characteristic
p
.

Many arguments in this section rely crucially on the mate correspondence, which is recalled in §
A.1.1
.

2.2.1.

Let
G
be a
p
-adic Lie group and
H\leq G
a closed subgroup.
Given a smooth
H
-representation
V
, we denote by
\cInd_{H}^{G}V\in\Rep_{k}(G)

the space of all locally constant functions
f\colon G\to V
which satisfy

f(hg)=hf(g)
for all
h\in H
,
g\in G
, and have compact support in

H\backslash G
; note that
f
is fixed by an open
subgroup of
G
under the right translation action. The functor
V\mapsto\cInd_{H}^{G}V
is exact and hence extends to a triangulated functor on the
(unbounded) derived categories:

\cInd_{H}^{G}\colon\mathrm{D}(H)\longrightarrow\mathrm{D}(G).

As
\cInd_{H}^{G}
clearly commutes with direct sums, Brown representability shows
that it admits a right adjoint
\mathcal{R}^{G}_{H}
,
cf.
[
Hey23
, Corollary 2.3.10(a)]
. The functors
\cInd_{H}^{G}
and

\mathcal{R}^{G}_{H}
are transitive,
i.e.
, if
H\leq H^{\prime}\leq G
is a closed
intermediate group, then
\cInd_{H}^{G}\cong\cInd_{H^{\prime}}^{G}\cInd_{H}^{H^{\prime}}
and

\mathcal{R}^{G}_{H}\cong\mathcal{R}^{H^{\prime}}_{H}\mathcal{R}^{G}_{H^{\prime}}
.
If
K\leq G
is an open subgroup, we observe that
\mathcal{R}^{G}_{K}\cong\Res^{G}_{K}
by Frobenius reciprocity, where
\Res^{G}_{K}
is the restriction functor; in this case we prefer to write
\ind_{K}^{G}
instead of
\cInd_{K}^{G}
.

2.2.2.

With
H\leq G
as above, given a smooth
H
-representation
V
, the group

G
acts by right translation on the space of all functions
f\colon G\to V

which satisfy
f(hg)=hf(g)
for all
h\in H
,
g\in G
; we denote by
\Ind_{H}^{G}V\in\Rep_{k}(G)
the subspace of functions which are fixed by an open subgroup
of
G
. The functor
V\mapsto\Ind_{H}^{G}V
is left exact; if
H\backslash G
is
compact, then
\cInd_{H}^{G}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\Longrightarrow}}\Ind_{H}^{G}
is even exact. Taking the right
derived functor, we obtain a triangulated functor

\RInd_{H}^{G}\colon\mathrm{D}(H)\longrightarrow\mathrm{D}(G),

which is right adjoint to restriction
\Res^{G}_{H}\colon\mathrm{D}(G)\to\mathrm{D}(H)
by Frobenius reciprocity. By a slight abuse of notation we write
\Ind_{H}^{G}
for
\RInd_{H}^{G}
in case

H\backslash G
is compact.

The restriction functor

\Res^{G}_{H}\colon\mathrm{D}(G)\to\mathrm{D}(H)
satisfies the following compatibility with
compact induction.

2.2.3 Lemma

(Projection formula)
.

Let
H\leq G
be a closed subgroup. There exists an isomorphism

\cInd_{H}^{G}\bigl(X\otimes_{k}\Res^{G}_{H}Y\bigr)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\cInd_{H}^{G}(X)\otimes_{k}Y\qquad\text{in $\mathrm{D}(G)$}

which is natural in
X\in\mathrm{D}(H)
and
Y\in\mathrm{D}(G)
. Moreover, the natural map

\iHom\bigl(\cInd_{H}^{G}X,Y\bigr)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\RInd_{H}^{G}\iHom\bigl(X,\mathcal{R}^{G}_{H}Y\bigr)

is an isomorphism in
\mathrm{D}(G)
, for all
X\in\mathrm{D}(H)
and
Y\in\mathrm{D}(G)
.

Proof.

We describe the inverse map.
For any
V\in\Rep_{k}(H)
and
W\in\Rep_{k}(G)
, the morphism
\cInd_{H}^{G}(V)\otimes_{k}W\to\cInd_{H}^{G}(V\otimes_{k}\Res^{G}_{H}W)
given by
f\otimes w\mapsto[g\mapsto f(g)\otimes gw]
is a natural isomorphism,
cf.
[
AHV19
, Lemma 2.5]
. Since
\cInd_{H}^{G}
,
\Res^{G}_{H}
, and

\raisebox{-2.0pt}{$-$}\otimes_{k}\raisebox{-2.0pt}{$-$}
are exact functors, this isomorphism readily extends to
the derived categories.

The isomorphism
\cInd_{H}^{G}(X\otimes_{k}\raisebox{-2.0pt}{$-$})\Res^{G}_{H}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\Longrightarrow}}\cInd_{H}^{G}X\otimes_{k}\raisebox{-2.0pt}{$-$}
yields, by passing to the right adjoints, an
isomorphism
\iHom(\cInd_{H}^{G}X,\raisebox{-2.0pt}{$-$})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\Longrightarrow}}\RInd_{H}^{G}\iHom(X,\raisebox{-2.0pt}{$-$})\mathcal{R}^{G}_{H}
, which proves the last assertion.
∎

2.2.4.

Given a closed normal subgroup
N\trianglelefteq G
, the inflation

\Inf^{G/N}_{G}\colon\mathrm{D}(G/N)\to\mathrm{D}(G)
along the projection
G\to G/N
admits a
right adjoint
\mathrm{R}\mathrm{H}^{0}(N,\raisebox{-2.0pt}{$-$})
as well as a
left adjoint, see
[
Hey23
, Theorem 3.2.3]
, which we call the functor of

derived coinvariants
and denote

\mathrm{L}_{N}\colon\mathrm{D}(G)\longrightarrow\mathrm{D}(G/N).

Note that for any subgroup
N\leq H\leq G
there is an isomorphism
\Res^{G/N}_{H/N}\mathrm{L}_{N}\cong\mathrm{L}_{N}\Res^{G}_{H}
by
[
Hey23
, Proposition 3.2.19]
so that the notation should not lead to confusion.

If
G
is compact and torsion-free, then
\mathrm{R}\mathrm{H}^{0}(N,\raisebox{-2.0pt}{$-$})
admits a
right adjoint denoted
F^{G/N}_{G}
,
[
Hey23
, Lemma 3.1.3]
. In
this case, we call
\omega_{G}\coloneqq F^{G/N}_{G}(\mathbf{1})\in\mathrm{D}(G)
the

dualizing complex
; we remark that
\omega_{G}\cong k[\dim N]
,

[
Hey23
, Proposition 3.1.10]
.

2.2.5 Lemma
.

Let
G
be compact and torsion-free, and let
N\leq H\leq G
be closed
subgroups such that
N\trianglelefteq G

2.3.
The duality character

2.3.1.

We denote by
\varepsilon\colon\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times}\to k^{\times}
the mod
p
cyclotomic character given by sending

to the image of
xp^{-\val_{p}(x)}
in

. If

is a finite field extension, we put
\varepsilon_{\mathfrak{F}}\coloneqq\varepsilon\circ\mathrm{Nm}_{\mathfrak{F}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
, where
\mathrm{Nm}_{\mathfrak{F}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
denotes the norm map.

2.3.2.

The purpose of this section is to give an explicit description of the
character (

Let

be a
p
-adic Lie group of dimension
d
and denote by

its

\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-Lie algebra (cf.
[
Sch11
, p. 100]
). The determinant of the
adjoint action of

\mathfrak{g}
provides a character
\det\mathfrak{g}\colon G\to\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times}
.
Put

\mathfrak{d}_{G}\coloneqq\varepsilon\circ\det(\mathfrak{g})\colon G\longrightarrow k^{\times}.

Note that, if
G
is even a Lie group over a finite extension
\mathfrak{F}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
(in the sense of
[
Sch11
]
), then
\mathfrak{g}
is an
\mathfrak{F}
-vector space and
\mathfrak{d}_{G}=\varepsilon_{\mathfrak{F}}\circ\det_{\mathfrak{F}}(\mathfrak{g})
by
[
Bou07
, III, §9, no. 4, Proposition 6]

\det_{\mathfrak{F}}(\mathfrak{g})
denotes the determinant of the
\mathfrak{F}
-linear action of
G
on
\mathfrak{g}
.

2.3.3 Definition
.

Let
\Delta\colon G\to G\times G
,
g\mapsto(g,g)
be the diagonal and write

G_{1}\coloneqq G\times\{1\}
and
G_{2}\coloneqq\{1\}\times G
as subgroups of

G\times G
. The

of

is defined as

\omega_{G}\coloneqq\mathrm{L}_{G_{2}}\cInd_{\Delta(G)}^{G\times G}(\mathbf{1})\in\mathrm{D}(G),

where we identify

via the first projection.

We leave it as an easy exercise for the reader to check that the automorphism on

given by
(g,h)\mapsto(h,g)
induces an isomorphism
\omega_{G}\cong\mathrm{L}_{G_{1}}\cInd_{\Delta(G)}^{G\times G}(\mathbf{1})
.

2.3.4 Proposition
.

One has
\omega_{G}\cong\mathfrak{d}_{G}[d]
and
\omega_{G}^{\vee}\cong\mathcal{R}^{G\times G}_{\Delta(G)}(\mathbf{1})

Proof.

We compute

\displaystyle\iHom(\omega_{G},\mathbf{1})

\displaystyle\cong\mathrm{R}\mathrm{H}^{0}\bigl(G_{2},\iHom(\cInd_{\Delta(G)}^{G\times G}(\mathbf{1}),\mathbf{1})\bigr)

\displaystyle\cong\mathrm{R}\mathrm{H}^{0}\bigl(G_{2},\RInd_{\Delta(G)}^{G\times G}\iHom\bigl(\mathbf{1},\mathcal{R}^{G\times G}_{\Delta(G)}(\mathbf{1})\bigr)\bigr)

2.3.5 Lemma

Let

be a closed subgroup. Then

3.2.

3.2.1.

The group

The following lemma is well-known.

3.2.2 Lemma
.

For each

the subset

is the smallest open

-invariant subset in

containing

. In particular,

is open if

is
minimal and is closed if

Proof.

Note that

is

n^{\prime}\not\leq n
and
n^{\prime}\leq n^{\prime\prime}
implies
n^{\prime\prime}\not\leq n
. If

is
any open

-invariant subset containing
n
and if
n^{\prime}\leq n
, then

contains
n
. From the definition of topological closure and
the
P\times Q
-invariance of

it follows that
Pn^{\prime}Q\subseteq Y
. Hence

X_{n}\subseteq Y
. The last assertions are immediate.
∎

Remark
.

(a)

Every open

-invariant subset of

(b)

Let

-invariant subset. For any

3.2.4 Lemma

Let

-invariant subsets such that

Proof.

Choose a continuous section of the projection map

[
AHV19
, Lemma 2.3]

-invariant subset

and the second

f\otimes v\mapsto[Py\mapsto f(Py)v]

Under all these identifications the sequence in the lemma arises by applying

\raisebox{-2.0pt}{$-$}\otimes_{k}V

It therefore suffices to show that (

Let

Write

∎

3.2.7 Definition
.

Let
F\colon\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{D}
be a triangulated functor.
A
(ascending) filtration
of length
n
on

is a sequence of natural
transformations

(3.2.8)

F_{i}\xRightarrow{\lambda_{i}}F_{i+1}\xRightarrow{\mu_{i+1}}F_{i+1,i}\xRightarrow{\nu_{i}}F_{i}[1]

of triangulated functors
\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{D}
, for
i=0,1,\dotsc,n-1
, such
that:

–

F_{0}=0
and
F_{n}=F
;

–

evaluating (
3.2.8
) at any object of
\mathscr{C}
gives a
distinguished triangle in
\mathscr{D}
.

The functor
F_{i,i-1}
is called the
i
-th graded piece
of the
filtration; note that
\mu_{1}\colon F_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\Longrightarrow}}F_{1,0}
is an isomorphism.

3.2.9 Lemma
.

Let
Z\subseteq Z^{\prime}\subseteq G
be
P\times Q
-invariant subsets such that
Z

is open in
Z^{\prime}
. There
exists a natural transformation
\nu\colon\cInd_{P}^{Z^{\prime}\smallsetminus Z}\Rightarrow\cInd_{P}^{Z}[1]
of triangulated functors

such that for all

one has a distinguished triangle

\cInd_{P}^{Z}X\longrightarrow\cInd_{P}^{Z^{\prime}}X\longrightarrow\cInd_{P}^{Z^{\prime}\smallsetminus Z}X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\nu}}{{\longrightarrow}}\cInd_{P}^{Z}X[1].

Proof.

Denote by
\lambda\colon\cInd_{P}^{Z}\Rightarrow\cInd_{P}^{Z^{\prime}}
and
\mu\colon\cInd_{P}^{Z^{\prime}}\Rightarrow\cInd_{P}^{Z^{\prime}\smallsetminus Z}
the obvious maps.
In order to construct
\nu
let

be the cone of

\lambda
. More precisely, given a complex
(X,d)
in
\mathrm{C}(P)
, define

F(X)^{n}\coloneqq\cInd_{P}^{Z}X^{n+1}\oplus\cInd_{P}^{Z^{\prime}}X^{n}
with differential

given by the matrix
\begin{pmatrix}-\cInd_{P}^{Z}d^{n+1}&0\\
\lambda&\cInd_{P}^{Z^{\prime}}d^{n}\end{pmatrix}
. It is clear
that

is indeed a functor which comes with
natural
transformations
\nu_{1}\colon F\Rightarrow\cInd_{P}^{Z}[1]
and

given by

and

combination with Lemma
3.2.3
, the usual long exact
sequence argument and the five lemma show that
\nu_{2}(X)
is a
quasi-isomorphism for all

. Since

\cInd_{P}^{Z}
and
\cInd_{P}^{Z^{\prime}}
are exact and mapping cones are functorial in

\mathrm{C}(Q)

preserves quasi-isomorphisms.
Hence

descends to a functor
\mathrm{D}(P)\to\mathrm{D}(Q)
, and then
\nu\coloneqq\nu_{1}\circ\nu_{2}^{-1}

∎

4.2.5 Remark

In

-groups of representations which are
parabolically induced from a supersingular representation. This conjecture is
conditionally resolved in
[

, Corollary 5.2.9]

argument rely, however, on an open conjecture of Emerton,

[

[

In Theorems

and
4.2.6
below I give unconditional proofs of

4.2.7 Lemma
.

(a)

One has
\alpha_{w}=\rho-w^{-1}(\rho)

(b)

(c)

The assignment

(d)

(e)

Assume there exists a twisting element

\chi\star w=(\varepsilon_{\mathfrak{F}}\circ\theta)\otimes_{k}w^{-1}_{*}\bigl(\chi\otimes_{k}\varepsilon_{\mathfrak{F}}^{-1}\circ\theta\bigr)

and all smooth characters

Remark
.

The action

is reminiscent of the “dot action”
in the representation theory of semisimple Lie algebras.

Proof of Lemma
4.2.6
.

Note that
d_{\alpha}=d_{w(\alpha)}
for all
\alpha\in\Sigma
,
w\in\mathrm{W}
.
For
(a)
we compute

\displaystyle 2w^{-1}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in\Sigma^{+}}d_{\alpha}\cdot w^{-1}(\alpha)=\sum_{\alpha\in\Sigma^{+}\cap w(\Sigma^{+})}d_{w^{-1}(\alpha)}w^{-1}(\alpha)+\sum_{\alpha\in\Sigma^{+}\cap w(-\Sigma^{+})}d_{w^{-1}(\alpha)}w^{-1}(\alpha)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in w^{-1}(\Sigma^{+})\cap\Sigma^{+}}d_{\alpha}\alpha-\sum_{\alpha\in w^{-1}(-\Sigma^{+})\cap\Sigma^{+}}d_{\alpha}\alpha=2\rho-2\alpha_{w}.

The cocycle condition in

can be verified in

\mathrm{X}^{*}(Z)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}

(a)
.
If

are such that
\alpha_{v}=\alpha_{w}
, then
\alpha_{v}=\alpha_{vw^{-1}w}=\alpha_{w}+w^{-1}(\alpha_{vw^{-1}})
, and hence

\alpha_{vw^{-1}}=0
; but this necessitates
v=w
. Part
(c)

follows from
(b)
noting that also

is a cocycle. Let us prove

(d)
. For each
\alpha\in\Delta_{G}
we compute, using

(a)
,

\langle\rho,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle=\langle s_{\alpha}(\rho),s_{\alpha}(\alpha^{\vee})\rangle=\langle\rho-d_{\alpha}\alpha,-\alpha^{\vee}\rangle=-\langle\rho,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle+d_{\alpha}\langle\alpha,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle.

In combination with

\langle\rho,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle=d_{\alpha}
. Finally, we prove

.
Note that
\langle\theta-\rho,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle=0

4.2.8 Theorem
.

Let
\mathbf{P}=\mathbf{M}\ltimes\mathbf{U}
a standard parabolic subgroup of
\mathbf{G}

and write
\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{Z}\ltimes\mathbf{N}
. Denote by
C(Z)
the center of
Z
.
Let
\chi\colon M\to k^{\times}
be a smooth character and let
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.

(a)

Let
\chi^{\prime}\colon Z\to k^{\times}
be another smooth
character. If

\Ext_{G}^{r}\bigl(\pInd_{P}^{G}\chi,\pInd_{B}^{G}\chi^{\prime}\bigr)\neq 0,

then there exists
w\in D_{M,Z}
such that
\chi^{\prime}\cong\delta_{w}\otimes_{k}n_{w*}^{-1}\chi
as characters of
C(Z)
and with
[\mathfrak{F}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]d_{w}\leq r
.

(b)

Assume
\delta_{w}\otimes_{k}n_{w*}^{-1}\chi\not\cong\chi

as characters of
C(Z)
, for all
w\in D_{M,Z}
. For
each
w\in D_{M,Z}
with
[\mathfrak{F}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]d_{w}\leq r
one has
k
-linear
isomorphisms

\Ext^{r}_{G}\bigl(\pInd_{P}^{G}\chi,\pInd_{B}^{G}(\delta_{w}\otimes_{k}n_{w*}^{-1}\chi)\bigr)\cong\Ext_{Z}^{r-[\mathfrak{F}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]d_{w}}(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1})\cong\mathrm{H}^{r-[\mathfrak{F}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]d_{w}}(Z,k),

where
\mathrm{H}^{*}(Z,k)
denotes the continuous group cohomology of
Z
with
coefficients in
k
.

Proof.

By

[
Hey23
, Corollary 4.1.3]
, there is a spectral sequence

E_{2}^{i,j}=\Ext_{Z}^{i}\bigl(\mathrm{L}^{-j}(N,\pInd_{P}^{G}\chi),\chi^{\prime}\bigr)\implies\Ext_{G}^{i+j}\bigl(\pInd_{P}^{G}\chi,\pInd_{B}^{G}\chi^{\prime}\bigr).

Moreover, (
4.2.2
) reads
\mathrm{L}^{-j}(N,\pInd_{P}^{G}\chi)\cong\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in D_{M,Z}\\
[\mathfrak{F}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]d_{w}=j\end{subarray}}\delta_{w}\otimes_{k}n_{w*}^{-1}\chi
.
We prove
(a)
by showing the contrapositive. If
after restriction to
C(Z)
we
have
\chi^{\prime}\not\cong\delta_{w}\otimes_{k}n_{w*}^{-1}\chi
for all
w\in D_{M,Z}

with
[\mathfrak{F}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]d_{w}\leq r
, then
Lemma
4.2
shows that
E_{2}^{i,j}=0
for all
j\leq r
and all
i
.
But this implies
\Ext_{G}^{r}(\pInd_{P}^{G}\chi,\pInd_{B}^{G}\chi^{\prime})=0
.
We now prove
(b)
. Take
w\in D_{M,Z}
with
[\mathfrak{F}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]d_{w}\leq r

and put
\chi^{\prime}\coloneqq\delta_{w}\otimes_{k}n_{w*}^{-1}\chi
.
By the assumption and Lemma
4.2.6
.
(c)
we have

\chi^{\prime}\not\cong\delta_{v}\otimes_{k}n_{v*}^{-1}\chi

for all
v\neq w
(as characters of
C(Z)
). Together with Lemma
4.2

we deduce
E_{2}^{i,j}=0
whenever
j\neq[\mathfrak{F}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]d_{w}
, and

E_{2}^{i,[\mathfrak{F}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]d_{w}}\cong\Ext_{Z}^{i}\bigl(\chi^{\prime},\chi^{\prime}\bigr)\cong\Ext_{Z}^{i}(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1})\cong\mathrm{H}^{i}(Z,k),

where the third isomorphism follows from

[
Fus22
, Theorem 1.1]
. Hence, the spectral sequence collapses on the
second page and gives an isomorphism
\Ext_{Z}^{r-[\mathfrak{F}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]d_{w}}\bigl(\chi^{\prime},\chi^{\prime}\bigr)\cong\Ext_{G}^{r}\bigl(\pInd_{P}^{G}\chi,\pInd_{B}^{G}\chi^{\prime}\bigr)
.
∎

4.3.
Generalized Steinberg representations

We will need the following well-known result, which we will prove for the
convenience of the reader.

4.3.1 Lemma
.

Let
R
be a unital associative ring and
M
an
R
-module of finite length
whose constituents occur with multiplicity one. Denote by
\JH(M)
the set of
Jordan–Hölder factors of
M
.

(a)

For each
V\in\JH(M)
there exists a
unique minimal submodule
M_{V}\leq M
which has
V
as a quotient. The
cosocle of
M_{V}
is
V
.

(b)

One has
\JH(N_{1}\cap N_{2})=\JH(N_{1})\cap\JH(N_{2})
and
\JH(N_{1}+N_{2})=\JH(N_{1})\cup\JH(N_{2})
, for all submodules

N_{1},N_{2}\leq M
.

Proof.

We first show
(a)
. Let
V\in\JH(M)
and take any
submodule
N\leq M
minimal with the property that
V
is a quotient of

N
. If we had
\cosoc(N)\neq V
, then the preimage of
V
under the surjection

N\rightarrow\hskip-11.99998pt\rightarrow\hskip 1.99997pt\cosoc(N)
is strictly contained in
N
and has
V
as a
quotient, contradicting the minimality assumption on
N
.

Let now
N,N^{\prime}\leq M
be two minimal submodules having
V
as a quotient.
Consider the short exact sequence
0\to N^{\prime}\longrightarrow N+N^{\prime}\to N/(N\cap N^{\prime})\to 0
.
From the multiplicity one assumption, we deduce
V\notin\JH(N/(N\cap N^{\prime}))
.
Since
V\in\JH(N)
, it follows that
V\in\JH(N\cap N^{\prime})
. Hence, there exists

N^{\prime\prime}\leq N\cap N^{\prime}
having
V
as a quotient. By the minimality of
N
and

N^{\prime}
we conclude
N=N^{\prime\prime}=N^{\prime}
. This proves the uniqueness claim.

For
(b)
it is generally true that
\JH(N_{1}\cap N_{2})\subseteq\JH(N_{1})\cap\JH(N_{2})
and
\JH(N_{1}+N_{2})=\JH(N_{1})\cup\JH(N_{2})
. The
remaining assertion needs the multiplicity one condition and follows from

(a)
, since
V\in\JH(N_{1})\cap\JH(N_{2})
implies
M_{V}\subseteq N_{1}\cap N_{2}
and hence
V\in\JH(N_{1}\cap N_{2})
.
∎

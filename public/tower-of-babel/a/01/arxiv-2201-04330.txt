Also, if
H
and
G
be two graphs, where
G\ncong K_{d+1}
, and
|V(G)|=d+2
, then:

\chi_{G}(H)+\chi_{G}(\overline{H})\leq\lceil\frac{n}{d}\rceil+2.

Lemma 12
.

Let
H
and
G
be two graphs, where
\delta(G)=\delta
,
|V(H)|=n
,
G\ncong K_{d+1}
, and
H
is not a critical graph. Hence:

\chi_{G}(H)+\chi_{G}(\overline{H})\leq\lceil\frac{n}{\delta}\rceil+2.

Proof.

By induction on
n
, when
n\leq|G|-1
, the lemma holds. Suppose that
n\geq|V(G)|
,
\chi_{G}(H)=t_{1}
and
\chi_{G}(\overline{H})=t_{2}
. We shall show that
t_{1}+t_{2}\leq\lceil\frac{n}{\delta}\rceil+2
. By Lemma
9
,
H
has a
G
-free
t_{1}
-critical subgraph. Assume that
F
is a
t_{1}
-critical subgraph of
H
with
|V(F)|=n_{1}\leq n-1
. Let
V_{1}=V(H)\setminus V(F)
and
|V_{1}|=n_{2}
. W.l.g suppose that
V_{1}=\{v_{1},v_{2}\ldots,v_{n_{2}}\}
. As
F
is a
t_{1}
-critical, so by Theorem
11
,

(5)

\chi_{G}(F)+\chi_{G}(\overline{F})\leq\lceil\frac{n_{1}}{\delta}\rceil+1.

Since
F
is
G
-free
t_{1}
-critical subgraph of
H
, then
\chi_{G}(H)=\chi_{G}(F)=t_{1}
. Let
\chi_{G}(\overline{H}[V(F)])=t_{3}
. Hence:

(6)

\chi_{G}(F)+\chi_{G}(\overline{F})=\chi_{G}(H)+\chi_{G}(\overline{H}[V(F)])=t_{1}+t_{3}\leq\lceil\frac{n_{1}}{\delta}\rceil+1.

We note that
\chi_{G}(\overline{H})\leq\chi_{G}(\overline{H}[V(F)])+\chi_{G}(\overline{H}[V_{1}])
. Now we can partition the vertices of
V_{1}
into
m
sets with size at most
\delta+1
, say
W_{1},W_{2},\ldots,W_{m}
. Since
G\ncong K_{\delta+1}
, thus
|V(G)|\geq\delta+2
, that is
\overline{H}[W_{i}]
is
G
-free. So,
\chi_{G}(\overline{H}[V_{1}])\leq\lceil\frac{n_{2}}{\delta+1}\rceil\leq\lceil\frac{n_{2}}{\delta}\rceil
. Hence
\chi_{G}(\overline{H})\leq t_{3}+\chi_{G}(\overline{H}[V_{1}])
. That is:

\chi_{G}(H)+\chi_{G}(\overline{H})\leq\lceil\frac{n}{\delta}\rceil+2.

Which means that the proof is complete.
∎

In the next theorem, we determine an upper bound for
\chi_{G}(H)+\chi_{G}(\overline{H})
, where
G\cong K_{d+1}
, and
|V(H)|=kd(k,d\geq 3)
. Also, we characterize certain classes of graphs with better upper bound.

Theorem 13
.

Suppose that
H
and
G
be two graphs, where
G\cong K_{d+1}
, and
|V(H)|=kd(k,d\geq 3)
, then:

\chi_{G}(H)+\chi_{G}(\overline{H})\leq k+2.

And if:

(I):

Either,
H
or
\overline{H}
is
G
-free critical.

(II):

There exists a subset of
V(H)
say
S
, with size
2d
, such that
H[S]
and
\overline{H}[S]
is
G
-free.

(III):

There exists a subset of
V(H)
say
S
, with size
2d
, such that either
g(H[S])=2d
or
g(\overline{H}[S])=2d
.

Where,
g(H)
is the girth of
H
and is defined as the size of the smallest cycle in
H
. Then:

\chi_{G}(H)+\chi_{G}(\overline{H})\leq k+1.

Proof.

With an argument similar to Theorem
11
, one can say that:

\chi_{G}(H)+\chi_{G}(\overline{H})\leq k+2.

Now, to prove (I), w.l.g we may suppose that
H
is
G
-free critical, that is for each
v\in V(H)
,
\chi_{G}(H\setminus\{v\})\leq\chi_{G}(H)-1
. Let
v
be a vertex of
V(H)
. Hence, by Theorem
11
,

(7)

\chi_{G}(H\setminus\{v\})+\chi_{G}(\overline{H}\setminus\{v\})\leq\lceil\frac{n-1}{\delta}\rceil+1.

As,
H
is
G
-free critical,
\delta(H)\geq(\chi_{G}(H)-1)\delta
, it yields
\Delta(\overline{H})=n-1-\delta(H)\leq n-1-(\chi_{G}(H)-1)\delta
. Hence, by Corollary
7
,

\chi_{G}(\overline{H})\leq\lceil\frac{\Delta(\overline{H})+1}{\delta}\rceil\leq\lceil\frac{n-(\chi_{G}(H)-1)\delta}{\delta}\rceil=\lceil\frac{n}{\delta}\rceil-(\chi_{G}(H)-1).

Therefore,
\chi_{G}(H)+\chi_{G}(\overline{H})\leq\lceil\frac{n}{\delta}\rceil+1
and the proof of (I) is complete.

We prove (II) by induction on
k
. For
k=3
, it is clear that
\chi_{G}(H)+\chi_{G}(\overline{H})\leq k+1
. For
k=4
, as
H[S]
,
\overline{H}[S]
are
G
-free, and
|S|=2k
, so
\chi_{G}(H[S])+\chi_{G}(\overline{H})[S]=2
. Hence, with an argument similar to Claim 3, either
H[\overline{S}]
is
G
-free or
\overline{H}[\overline{S}]
is
G
-free. So,
\chi_{G}(H[\overline{S}])+\chi_{G}(\overline{H})[\overline{S}]\leq 3
and

\chi_{G}(H)+\chi_{G}(\overline{H})\leq 5=k+1
. Now, let for each
k^{\prime}\leq k-1
the statement holds, and suppose that
H
be a graph with
kd
vertices. W.l.g assume that
S
with size
2d
be a subset of
V(H)
, where
H[S]
and
\overline{H}[S]
are
G
-free. Set
H^{\prime}=H\setminus S
, Hence,
|H^{\prime}|=(k-2)d
, and by induction,
\chi_{G}(H^{\prime})+\chi_{G}(\overline{H^{\prime}})\leq k-1
. As,
H[S]
and
\overline{H}[S]
are
G
-free, thus
\chi_{G}(H)+\chi_{G}(\overline{H})\leq k+1
.

To prove (III), w.l.g we may suppose that
S
with size
2d
is a subset of
V(H)
, such that
g(H[S])=2d
. As,
g(H[S])=2d
,
d\geq 3
, and
G=K_{d+1}
, so
H[S]
is
G
-free. Since
g(H[S])=2d
, so
\overline{H}[S]\cong K_{2d}\setminus C_{2d}
, and
\omega(\overline{H}[S])=d
, that is
\overline{H}[S]
is
G
-free. Therefore, by proof of (II), the proof of (III) is complete.

Which implies that the proof is complete.
∎

Assume that
\C
is a family of all
2
-regular graphs. In the next theorem, we determine an upper bound for
\chi_{G}(H)+\chi_{G}(\overline{H})
, for each
G\in\C
.

Theorem 14
.

Let
H
and
G
be two graphs, where
G\in\C
, hence:

\chi_{G}(H)+\chi_{G}(\overline{H})\leq\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil+1.

And this bound is sharp.

Proof.

Case 1
:
G=K_{3}
. If
|V(H)\neq 2k
for each
k\geq 3
, then the proof is complete by Theorem
11
. So, suppose that
|V(H)=2k
for some
k\geq 1
. We prove by induction. For
k=1,2
it is clear that
\chi_{K_{3}}(H)+\chi_{K_{3}}(\overline{H})\leq k+1
. Suppose that
\chi_{K_{3}}(H)+\chi_{K_{3}}(\overline{H})\leq k^{\prime}+1
, for each
k^{\prime}\leq k-1
, and assume that
H
be a graph with
2k
vertices, where
k\geq 3
. Assume that
v,v^{\prime}
be two vertices of
H
, where
\deg_{H}(v)=\deg_{H}(v^{\prime})
. Set
H^{\prime}=H\setminus\{v,v^{\prime}\}
, as
|V(H^{\prime})|=2(k-1)
, by induction
\chi_{K_{3}}(H^{\prime})+\chi_{K_{3}}(\overline{H^{\prime}})\leq k

\chi_{K_{3}}(H^{\prime})+\chi_{K_{3}}(\overline{H^{\prime}})\leq k-1
, then the proof is complete. Hence, assume that
\chi_{K_{3}}(H^{\prime})+\chi_{K_{3}}(\overline{H^{\prime}})=k
. If either
\chi_{K_{3}}(H^{\prime})=\chi_{K_{3}}(H)
or
\chi_{K_{3}}(\overline{H^{\prime}})=\chi_{K_{3}}(\overline{H})
, then the proof is same. Now, suppose that
\chi_{K_{3}}(H)=\chi_{K_{3}}(H^{\prime})+1
and
\chi_{K_{3}}(\overline{H})=\chi_{K_{3}}(\overline{H^{\prime}})+1
. In other word, suppose that adding
x
to
H^{\prime}
and
x^{\prime}
to
\overline{H^{\prime}}
increases the
\chi_{K_{3}}(H^{\prime})
and
\chi_{K_{3}}(\overline{H^{\prime}})
by one, where

Claim 4
:
x\neq x^{\prime}
. By contrary, suppose that
x=x^{\prime}
,
\chi_{K_{3}}(H^{\prime})=t_{1}
and
\chi_{K_{3}}(\overline{H^{\prime}})=t_{2}
, where
t_{1}+t_{2}=k
. As
\chi_{K_{3}}(H^{\prime}\cup\{x\})=t_{1}+1
and
\chi_{K_{3}}(\overline{H^{\prime}}\cup\{x\})=t_{2}+1
, we can say that
|N_{H}(x)|\geq 2t_{1}
and
|N_{\overline{H}}(x)|\geq 2t_{2}
. Now,
2k-1=|N_{H}(x)|+|N_{\overline{H}}(x)|\geq 2(t_{1}+t_{2})
, that is
2k\leq 2k-1
, a contradiction.

So, suppose that
x=v
and
x=v^{\prime}
. Since
\chi_{K_{3}}(H^{\prime}\cup\{v\})=t_{1}+1
and
\chi_{K_{3}}(\overline{H^{\prime}}\cup\{v^{\prime}\})=t_{2}+1
, so
|N_{H}(v)|\geq 2t_{1}
and
|N_{\overline{H}}(v^{\prime})|\geq 2t_{2}
. As
\deg_{H}(v)=\deg_{H}(v^{\prime})
, thus
n-1=2k-1=|N_{H}(v)|+|N_{\overline{H}}(v^{\prime})|\geq 2(t_{1}+t_{2})
, that is

, a contradiction again. Hence, we can say that
\chi_{K_{3}}(H^{\prime})+\chi_{K_{3}}(\overline{H^{\prime}})\leq k-1
, or
\chi_{K_{3}}(H^{\prime})=\chi_{K_{3}}(H)
, or
\chi_{K_{3}}(\overline{H^{\prime}})=\chi_{K_{3}}(\overline{H})
, in any case,
\chi_{K_{3}}(H)+\chi_{K_{3}}(\overline{H})\leq k+1
.

Case 2
:
G=C_{m}
,
m\geq 4
. For the case that
|V(H)|=2k
, the proof is same as the Case 1. Hence assume that
|V(H)|=2k+1
for some
k\geq 2
. We prove by induction. For
k=1,2
it is clear that
\chi_{C_{m}}(H)+\chi_{C_{m}}(\overline{H})\leq k+1
. Suppose that
\chi_{C_{m}}(H)+\chi_{C_{m}}(\overline{H})\leq k^{\prime}+1
for each
k^{\prime}\leq k-1
, and assume that
H
be a graph with
2k+1
vertices, where
k\geq 3
. Assume that
V^{\prime}=\{v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}\}
be a subsets of
V(H)
. Set
H^{\prime}=H\setminus V^{\prime}
, as
|V(H^{\prime})|=2(k-1)
, by induction,
\chi_{C_{m}}(H^{\prime})+\chi_{C_{m}}(\overline{H^{\prime}})\leq k
. Also as
|V(G)|=m\geq
, so
H[V^{\prime}]
and
\overline{H}[V^{\prime}]
are
G
-free. Therefore,
\chi_{C_{m}}(H)+\chi_{C_{m}}(\overline{H})\leq\chi_{C_{m}}(H^{\prime})+1+\chi_{C_{m}}(\overline{H^{\prime}})+1\leq k+2=\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil+1
, and the proof of Case 2 is complete.

To prove the sharpness of the bound, set
H=K_{3}+3K_{1}

\overline{H}=K_{3}
. Hence,
\chi_{K_{3}}(H)=\chi_{K_{3}}(\overline{H})=2
. Therefore, as

\chi_{K_{3}}(H)+\chi_{K_{3}}(\overline{H})=4=\lceil\frac{6}{2}\rceil+1

Therefore by Theorems
11
,
13
, and
14
, we have a result as follow:

Theorem 15
.

Suppose that
\G

is a family of graphs with minimum degrees
\delta(\G)
, where
\delta(\G)=\min\{\delta(G):~G\in\G\}
. Also, let
H
be a graph with
n(H)
vertices. Then:

\chi_{\G}(H)+\chi_{\G}(\overline{H})\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\lceil\frac{n(H)}{\delta(\G)}\rceil+1&~~~~when~\G=\C~or~either,~~H~or~\overline{H}~is~\G-free~critical,\\
\lceil\frac{n(H)}{\delta(G)}\rceil+2&~~~~~~~~~~otherwise.\end{array}\right.

Proof.

Suppose that
G\in\G
, clearly
\chi_{\G}(H)\leq\max_{G\in\G}\chi_{G}(H)
. Assume that
\max_{G\in\G}\chi_{G}(H)=\chi_{G^{\prime}}(H)
, and
\delta^{\prime}=\delta(G^{\prime})
. Hence,
\chi_{\G}(H)\leq\chi_{G^{\prime}}(H)
. Since
\delta(\G)\leq\delta^{\prime}
and by Theorems
11
,
13
and
14
, one can check that
\chi_{\G}(H)\leq\chi_{G^{\prime}}(H)\leq\lceil\frac{n(H)}{\delta^{\prime}}\rceil+i\leq\lceil\frac{n(H)}{\delta(\G)}\rceil+i
, where
i\in\{1,2\}
. Which implies that the proof is complete.

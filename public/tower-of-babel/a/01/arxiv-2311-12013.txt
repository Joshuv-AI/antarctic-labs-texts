Weak existence for SDEs with singular drifts and fractional Brownian or Lévy noise beyond the subcritical regime
Oleg Butkovsky, Samuel Gallay

Abstract
We study a multidimensional stochastic differential equation with additive noise: d ​ X t = b ⁡ ( t , X t ) ​ d ​ t + d ​ ξ t , dX_{t}=b(t,X_{t})dt+d\xi_{t}, where the drift b b is integrable in space and time, and ξ \xi is either a fractional Brownian motion or an α \alpha -stable process. We show weak existence of solutions to this equation under the optimal condition on integrability indices of b b , going beyond the subcritical Krylov-Röckner (Prodi-Serrin-Ladyzhenskaya) regime. This extends the recent results of Krylov (2020) to the fractional Brownian and Lévy cases. We also construct a counterexample to demonstrate the optimality of this condition. Our methods are built upon a version of the stochastic sewing lemma of Lê and the John–Nirenberg inequality.



1
Introduction

We study the existence of weak solutions to stochastic differential equations (SDEs) driven by fractional Brownian or Lévy noise:

\displaystyle X_{t}

\displaystyle=x+\int_{0}^{t}b_{r}(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}r+W_{t}^{H},\quad\quad\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,t\in[0,T],

(1.1)

\displaystyle X_{t}

\displaystyle=x+\int_{0}^{t}b_{r}(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}r+L_{t},\quad\quad\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,t\in[0,T],

(1.2)

where
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
T>0
,
b\colon[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a function in the mixed Lebesgue space
L_{q}([0,T],L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
,
p,q\in[1,\infty]
,
W^{H}
is a fractional Brownian motion (fBm) of Hurst parameter
H\in(0,1)
and
L
is an
\alpha
-stable process,
\alpha\in(1,2)
. We show that if

\frac{1-H}{q}+\frac{Hd}{p}<1-H,

(1.3)

then equation (
1.1
) has a weak solution (
Theorem

2.1
). We also construct a counter-example showing optimality of this condition (
Theorem

2.2
). Similarly, we also show that if

\frac{\alpha-1}{q}+\frac{d}{p}<\alpha-1,

(1.4)

then equation (
1.2
) has a weak solution (
Theorem

2.3
). This extends the recent results of Krylov
[
Kry20
]
, where these statements were proven in the Brownian setting (
H=\frac{1}{2}
).

Let us recall that deterministic ordinary differential equations can be ill-posed, meaning that they may have multiple or no solutions, but by adding noise, they become well-posed. This phenomenon is called
regularization by noise
.

In the case where the noise is a standard Brownian motion, the problem has been widely studied. The first results in this direction are due to Zvonkin
[
Zvo74
]
and Veretennikov
[
Ver80
]
, who proved strong existence and uniqueness of solutions to SDE

dX_{t}=b(t,X_{t})dt+dW_{t},

(1.5)

when
b
is a bounded measurable function and
W
is a Brownian motion. Later Krylov and Röckner
[
KR05
]
and Zhang
[
Zha11
]
extended these results to the case where
b\in L_{q}([0,T],L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
,
p,q\in[1,\infty]
and

\frac{2}{q}+\frac{d}{p}<1.

(1.6)

This condition is often referred to in the literature as the Ladyzhenskaya-Prodi-Serrin condition (LPS). One can observe that if (
1.6
) is satisfied, then at small scales, noise dominates the drift. Furthermore, noise is less regular than the drift, which allows for regularization by noise to take place. This regime is often called subcritical. For a more detailed discussion, we refer to
[
GG23
, Section 1.1]
.

If, on the contrary,
\frac{2}{q}+\frac{d}{p}>1
(supercritical regime), then at small scales, the drift dominates, and as a result, regularization by noise is not guaranteed. In fact, for any values of
p,q,d
satisfying this inequality, there exists a drift
b\in L_{q}([0,T],L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
for which even weak uniqueness fails for SDE (
1.5
), see
[
GG23
, Section 1.3]
.

Weak existence is more nuanced. If time integrability
q=\infty
, then for any values of
p,d
in the supercritical regime, there exists a drift
b\in L_{\infty}([0,T],L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
for which weak existence
fails for SDE (
1.5
), see, e.g.,
[
Che02
, Example 2.1]
. Surprisingly enough, if
q\neq\infty
, then weak existence does hold for some supercritical values of
p,q,d
. Namely, it was discovered by Krylov
[
Kry20
]
(see
[
Gal23
]
for an alternative proof) that a weaker condition

\frac{1}{q}+\frac{d}{p}\leq 1

(1.7)

implies weak existence for SDE (
1.5
) with drift
b\in L_{q}([0,T],L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
for
p\geq q
, and
b\in L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},L_{q}([0,T]))
for
p\leq q
. It was also shown in
[
Kry20
]
that in the regime
p\leq qd
this condition is optimal: if
p,q,d
satisfy
\frac{1}{q}+\frac{d}{p}>1
and
p\leq qd
, then there exists a drift
b\in L_{q}([0,T],L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
for which no weak solution exists for SDE (
1.5
). Construction of a counter-example for the remaining values of parameters (that is,
p>qd
,
\frac{1}{q}+\frac{d}{p}>1
) was left in
[
Kry20
]
open.

Thus, if
q=\infty
, then Krylov’s condition (
1.7
) reduces to the LPS condition (
1.6
), and a weak solution exists only for values of
p,d
that are in the subcritical regime. If
q<\infty
, then (
1.7
) is weaker than (
1.6
), and a weak solution also exists for some supercritical
p,d
.
Note however, that under additional assumptions on the drift (e.g., that the drift is divergence-free) Krylov’s condition (
1.7
) can be further relaxed
[
ZZ21
]
.

Much less is known for the case where the noise is a fractional Brownian motion
W^{H}
. The main reason is that for
H\neq\frac{1}{2}
the fractional Brownian motion is neither a martingale nor a Markov process. Since the techniques used in
[
Zvo74
,
Ver80
,
KR05
,
Kry20
]
for both proving well-posedness and constructing counterexamples are based on Itô’s formula, they become inapplicable.

A breakthrough in understanding the well-posedness of SDEs driven by fBM was achieved in
[
CG16
]
and
[
Lê20
]
, where sewing and stochastic sewing techniques were developed. Extending their ideas,
[
GG23
]
showed (among many other interesting results) weak existence for SDE (
1.1
) if
b\in L_{q}([0,T],L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
,
p,q\in[1,\infty]
, and

\frac{1}{q}+\frac{Hd}{p}<1-H\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{2Hd}{p}<1-H.

(1.8)

When
H=\frac{1}{2}
, the first part of (
1.8
) reduces to the LPS condition (
1.6
), but the second part requires an additional condition,
2d<p
. Thus, one does not recover the optimal Krylov’s condition (
1.7
).

We improve this result in two directions. First, we show that the extra assumption
\frac{2Hd}{p}<1-H
can be dropped. Second, we demonstrate that the condition
\frac{1}{q}+\frac{Hd}{p}<1-H
can be relaxed to (
1.3
). We note that (
1.3
) can be viewed as an extended Krylov’s condition because it reduces to (
1.7
) when
H=\frac{1}{2}
.
This is the subject of
Theorem

2.1
.
Theorem

2.2
shows optimality of condition (
1.3
) by constructing the corresponding counter-example.
Theorem

2.2
seems to be new even in the Brownian case
H=1/2
as the case
p>qd
has not been covered in
[
Kry20
]
. Finally,
Theorem

2.3
establishes weak existence of SDEs with
\alpha
-stable noise under condition (
1.4
), which is an analogue of Krylov’s condition for Lévy processes. This improves
[
Zha13
, Theorem 4.1]
.

To obtain these results, we utilize stochastic sewing techniques, John–Nirenberg inequality, and taming singularities methods, building on
[
BLM23
]
, where these results were established for the time-independent case (
p=\infty
). We would like to point out that a direct application of the strategy in
[
BLM23
]
leads to a suboptimal condition,
\frac{1}{q}+\frac{Hd}{p}<1-H
. To derive a weaker condition (
1.3
), we had to conduct a much more intricate analysis and obtain improved integral bounds, see, in particular,
Lemma

3.11
.

The rest of the article is organized as follows: We present our main results in
Section

2
. The proofs are provided in
Section

3
, and all auxiliary technical results are presented in
Appendix

A
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\alpha,\beta>0

\gamma\in(0,1)

\alpha\gamma+\beta(1-\gamma)>1-\gamma.

Let
f\in\mathcal{C}^{\gamma}([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d})

X_{t}^{i}=-\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\sign(X_{s}^{i})|X_{s}|^{-\alpha}\mathbbm{1}_{0<|X_{s}|<1}s^{-\beta}+\mathbbm{1}_{|X_{s}|=0,s>0}s^{-2}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}s+f(t)

X\colon[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}

Assume the contrary. Let
X

With such choice of

|X_{t_{2}}|\leq d|X^{i}_{t_{2}}|

We see that
t_{1}

X_{0}^{i}=0
by assumption. Clearly,
X^{i}_{t_{2}}\neq 0

We get for

|X_{t_{i}}|^{-\alpha}\mathbbm{1}_{|X_{t_{i}}|>0}t_{i}^{-\beta}+\mathbbm{1}_{|X_{t_{i}}|=0}t_{i}^{-2}\leq t_{i}^{-1}

(3.7)

where we used again that
|X_{r}|<1
for
r\in[0,t_{2}]
.
If at one of such
t_{i}
,
X_{t_{i}}=0
, then (
3.7
) implies
t_{i}^{-2}\leq t_{i}^{-1}
which is impossible. Therefore
X_{t_{i}}\neq 0
and we get

|X_{t_{i}}|^{-\alpha}t_{i}^{-\beta}\leq t_{i}^{-1}\quad\text{so}\quad|X_{t_{i}}|\geq t_{i}^{\frac{1-\beta}{\alpha}}.

(3.8)

Combining (
3.6
) and (
3.8
) and taking
t_{i}\to 0
we get

\frac{1-\beta}{\alpha}\geq\frac{\beta\gamma}{1-\gamma-\alpha\gamma},\quad\text{which gives}\quad\beta(1-\gamma)+\alpha\gamma\leq 1-\gamma.

This contradicts (
3.4
).
∎

Proof of
Theorem

2.2
.

Because inequality (
2.5
) is strict there exist
\alpha,\beta>0
and

such that equation (

) is satisfied with

We define the function

We see that

.
A fractional Brownian motion

is a.s. in

.
Applying

3.1
with

) with this choice of

∎

3.2
Recap of sewing lemmas

The proofs of
Theorem

2.1
and
Theorem

2.3
rely on the stochastic sewing lemma introduced in
[
Lê20
]
. For the convenience of the reader, we recall the corresponding statement as well as its modified version from
[
BLM23
]
, which we also use.

Fix
0\leq S\leq T
and the filtration
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\in[S,T]}
.
Recall the notation (
3.3
). Let
(A_{s,t})_{(s,t)\in\Delta^{2}_{S,T}}

be a collection of random elements taking values in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, such that
A_{s,t}
is
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-measurable whenever
(s,t)\in\Delta^{2}_{S,T}
. For any
(s,u,t)\in\Delta^{3}_{S,T}
denote as usual

\delta A_{s,u,t}:=A_{s,t}-A_{s,u}-A_{u,t}.

Definition 3.2
.

A
control

w:\Delta_{S,T}^{2}\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is a measurable function that is subadditive, meaning that for any
(s,u,t)\in\Delta^{3}_{S,T}
it satisfies
w(s,u)+w(u,t)\leq w(s,t)
a.s.

Definition 3.3
.

We say that a random process
\mathcal{A}:\Omega\times[S,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a limit of Riemann sums of
\{A_{s,t},(s,t)\in\Delta_{S,T}^{2}\}
if, for every sequence of partitions
\Pi_{n}={\{s=t_{0}^{n},t_{1}^{n},\ldots,t_{k_{n}}^{n}=t\}}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, of
[s,t]\subseteq[S,T]
having a mesh size going to zero, we have

\sum_{i=0}^{k_{n}}A_{{t_{i}^{n}},{t_{i+1}^{n}}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{A}_{t}-\mathcal{A}_{s}\ \text{in probability as $n\to\infty$}.

(3.9)

We begin with the stochastic sewing lemma.

Proposition 3.4

(
[
Lê23
, Theorem 3.1 and Corollary 3.2]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}:\Omega\times[S,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a random process that is a limit of Riemann sums of
(A_{s,t})
as in
Definition

3.3
.
Suppose that there exist
m\geq 2
,
\varepsilon>0
and a
deterministic
control
\omega
such that for all
(s,u,t)\in\Delta^{3}_{S,T}

1.

\hskip 0.6458pt\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.43057pt^{s}\delta A_{s,u,t}=0
;

2.

\|A_{s,t}\|_{L_{m}(\Omega)}\leq\omega(s,t)^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}
.

Then there exists a constant
C=C(m,\varepsilon)
such that for every
(s,t)\in\Delta^{2}_{S,T}
,

\|\mathcal{A}_{t}-\mathcal{A}_{s}\|_{L_{m}(\Omega)}\leq C\omega(s,t)^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}.

The next statement is the Rosenthal-Burkholder stochastic sewing lemma. Its main advantage over the standard stochastic sewing lemma presented above is that in order to bound the
m
th moment of
\mathcal{A}_{t}
, one only has to provide a very loose bound on high moments of
A_{s,t}
. The price to pay is that, instead of controlling the second moments of
\delta A_{s,u,t}
, one now has to control second
conditional
moments of this quantity.

Proposition 3.5

(
[
BLM23
, Theorem 3.6]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}:\Omega\times[S,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a random process that is a limit of Riemann sums of
(A_{s,t})
as in
Definition

3.3
.
Suppose that there exist
\varepsilon_{0},\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}>0
and random controls
\omega_{1}
and
\omega_{2}
such that for all
(s,u,t)\in\Delta^{3}_{S,T}

1.

|\hskip 0.6458pt\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.43057pt^{u}\delta A_{s,u,t}|\leq\omega_{1}(s,t)(t-s)^{\varepsilon_{1}}
a.s.;

2.

\hskip 0.6458pt\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.43057pt^{u}[|\delta A_{s,u,t}|^{2}]^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\omega_{2}(s,t)^{\frac{1}{2}}(t-s)^{\varepsilon_{2}}
a.s.;

3.

for any
n\geq 1
there exists a constant
\Gamma=\Gamma_{n}
independent of
s,t
such that
\|A_{s,t}\|_{L_{n}(\Omega)}\leq\Gamma_{n}(t-s)^{\varepsilon_{0}}
.

Then for any
m\geq 2
there exist
\ell=\ell(m,\varepsilon_{0})
,
C=C(m,\varepsilon_{0},\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})
such that for every
(s,t)\in\Delta^{2}_{S,T}

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{A}_{t}-\mathcal{A}_{s}\|_{L_{m}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\Bigl(\Gamma_{\ell}(t-s)^{\varepsilon_{0}}+\|\omega_{1}(s,t)\|_{L_{m}(\Omega)}(t-s)^{\varepsilon_{1}}+\|\omega_{2}(s,t)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{L_{m}(\Omega)}(t-s)^{\varepsilon_{2}}\Bigr).

3.3
Main integral bound

This section is crucial for the overall proof of our main results. First, after a thorough preparation, we obtain a certain bound on additive functionals of the solutions to SDEs (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) in
Lemma

3.11
. Then, we use this bound to obtain a priori bounds on the
1
-variation of solutions to these SDEs. Since the driving noises of these SDEs share many similar features, to avoid unnecessary repetitions, we will work in the unified setting, which, as we demonstrate later, is applicable both to
W^{H}
and
L
.

Without loss of generality, assume that the time interval is
[0,1]
. Till the end of the section we
fix the filtration
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
,
H\in(0,1)
and a random process
Y:[0,1]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
adapted to
(\mathcal{F}_{t})
. Denote

Y_{s,t}:=Y_{t}-\hskip 0.6458pt\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.43057pt^{s}Y_{t},\quad(s,t)\in\Delta^{2}_{0,1}.

Assumption 3.6
.

Suppose the following condition holds for the process
Y
:

1.

for any
(s,t)\in\Delta^{2}_{0,1}
the random variable
Y_{s,t}
is independent of
\mathcal{F}_{s}
;

2.

for any
(s,u,t)\in\Delta^{3}_{0,1}
the random variable
\hskip 0.6458pt\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.43057pt^{u}Y_{s,t}
has a density
p_{s,u,t}
with respect to the Lebesgue measure and

\|p_{s,u,t}\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq(u-s)^{-Hd}+((t-s)^{2H}-(t-u)^{2H})^{-\frac{d}{2}};

3.

for any
\beta<0
,
\rho\in[1,\infty]
there exists a constant
C=C(H,d,\beta,\rho)
such that for any measurable function
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
(s,t)\in\Delta^{2}_{0,1}

\big\|\hskip 0.6458pt\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.43057ptf(Y_{s,t}+\cdot)\big\|_{L_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}_{\rho}^{\beta}}(t-s)^{\beta H}.

(3.10)

Throughout this section, we assume that the process
Y
and the filtration
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
satisfy
3.6
.

To simplify the notation we introduce the following convention. We put for
\rho\in[1,\infty]

\mathcal{D}^{\beta}_{\rho}:=\mathcal{B}^{\beta}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\,\,\text{if $\beta<0$;}\qquad\mathcal{D}^{0}_{\rho}:=L_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

(3.11)

This allows us to avoid additional complications with Besov space of zero regularity. We note in particular that by Minkowski’s inequality
\|\hskip 0.6458pt\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.43057ptf(Y_{s,t}+\cdot)\big\|_{L_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\|f\|_{L_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
, and thus (
3.10
) implies

\big\|\hskip 0.6458pt\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.43057ptf(Y_{s,t}+\cdot)\big\|_{L_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\rho}^{\beta}}(t-s)^{\beta H}.

(3.12)

for any
\beta\leq 0
,
\rho\in[1,\infty]
.

We begin with the following simple technical bounds.

Lemma 3.7
.

For any
\beta\leq 0
,
\rho\in[1,\infty)
there exists a constant
C=C(H,d,\beta,\rho)
such that for any
(s,u,t)\in\Delta^{3}_{0,1}
and any measurable function
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
we have

\|\hskip 0.6458pt\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.43057pt^{u}f(Y_{s,t})\|_{L_{\rho}(\Omega)}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\rho}^{\beta}}(t-u)^{\beta H}\bigl((u-s)^{-\frac{Hd}{\rho}}+\I_{H<\frac{1}{2}}(u-s)^{-\frac{d}{2\rho}}(t-u)^{\frac{d}{2\rho}-\frac{Hd}{\rho}}\bigr)

(3.13)

If, in addition,
f
is continuous, then for any
\lambda\in(0,1]
there exists
C=C(H,d,\lambda,\rho)
such that

\hskip 0.6458pt\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.43057pt|f(Y_{s,t}+\delta)-f(Y_{s,t})|\leq C|\delta|^{\lambda}\|f\|_{L_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}(t-s)^{-\lambda H-\frac{Hd}{\rho}}.

(3.14)

Proof.

It follows from the definition that
Y_{s,t}=Y_{u,t}+\hskip 0.6458pt\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.43057pt^{u}Y_{s,t}
and
Y_{u,t}
is independent of
\mathcal{F}_{u}
. Therefore, using
3.6
and (
3.12
), we deduce

\displaystyle\|\hskip 0.6458pt\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.43057pt^{u}f(Y_{s,t})\|_{L_{\rho}(\Omega)}^{\rho}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\hskip 0.6458pt\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.43057pt|f(Y_{u,t}+x)|^{\rho}p_{s,u,t}(x)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\bigl((u-s)^{-Hd}+((t-s)^{2H}-(t-u)^{2H})^{-\frac{d}{2}}\bigr)\|\hskip 0.6458pt\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.43057ptf(Y_{u,t}+\cdot)\|_{L_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\rho}

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\rho}^{\beta}}^{\rho}(t-u)^{\beta\rho H}\bigl((u-s)^{-Hd}+((t-s)^{2H}-(t-u)^{2H})^{-\frac{d}{2}}\bigr).

(3.15)

If
H\geq 1/2
, then
(t-s)^{2H}-(t-u)^{2H}\geq(u-s)^{2H}
. Substituting this into the above inequality we get the desired bound (
3.13
).

If
H<1/2
, then
(t-s)^{2H}-(t-u)^{2H}\geq(u-s)(t-s)^{2H-1}
. Therefore

((t-s)^{2H}-(t-u)^{2H})^{-\frac{d}{2}}\leq(u-s)^{-\frac{d}{2}}(t-s)^{\frac{d}{2}-Hd}\leq C(u-s)^{-Hd}+(u-s)^{-\frac{d}{2}}(t-u)^{\frac{d}{2}-Hd}.

Combining this with (
3.3
), we obtain (
3.13
).

Next, to prove (
3.14
), we fix
\lambda\in(0,1]
. We note that for any continuous
g\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
we have from (
3.12
)

|\hskip 0.6458pt\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.43057ptg(Y_{s,t})|\leq\|\hskip 0.6458pt\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.43057ptg(Y_{s,t}+\cdot)\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\|g\|_{\mathcal{B}^{-\lambda-\frac{d}{\rho}}_{\infty}}(t-s)^{-\lambda H-\frac{Hd}{\rho}}\leq C\|g\|_{\mathcal{B}^{-\lambda}_{\rho}}(t-s)^{-\lambda H-\frac{Hd}{\rho}},

where
C=C(H,d,\lambda,\rho)
and in the first inequality we used that the function
x\mapsto\hskip 0.6458pt\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.43057ptg(Y_{s,t}+x)
is continuous. Therefore, using (
3.1
), we deduce for any
\delta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

|\hskip 0.6458pt\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.43057ptf(Y_{s,t}+\delta)-f(Y_{s,t})|\leq C\|f-f(\delta+\cdot)\|_{\mathcal{B}^{-\lambda}_{\rho}}(t-s)^{-\lambda H-\frac{Hd}{\rho}}\leq C|\delta|^{\lambda}\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{0}_{\rho}}(t-s)^{-\lambda H-\frac{Hd}{\rho}},

for
C=C(H,d,\lambda,\rho)
. Since
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{B}^{0}_{\rho}}\leq C\|\cdot\|_{L_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
, this implies (
3.14
).
∎

Now we are ready to prove a preliminary bound on additive functional of
Y
. Recall the convention (
3.11
).

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
f:[0,1]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded function and suppose that
f\in L_{q}([0,1],\mathcal{D}_{\rho}^{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, where

q\in[1,\infty),\quad\rho\in[2,\infty),\quad\frac{1}{q}+\frac{Hd}{\rho}-H\beta<1,\quad\beta\in(-\frac{1}{2H},0].

(3.16)

Then there exists a constant
C=C(H,d,\beta,\rho,q)
such that for any
(s,t)\in\Delta^{2}_{[0,1]}

and any deterministic measurable function
x\colon[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
one has

\Big\|\int_{s}^{t}f_{r}(x_{r}+Y_{s,r})\,\mathrm{d}r\Big\|_{L_{\rho}(\Omega)}\leq C\|f\|_{L_{q}([s,t],\mathcal{D}_{\rho}^{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}(t-s)^{1-\frac{1}{q}-\frac{Hd}{\rho}+\beta H}.

(3.17)

Proof.

Fix
S_{0}\in[0,1]
. Let
(S,T)\in\Delta^{2}_{[S_{0},1]}
,
S\neq S_{0}
.
For
(s,t)\in\Delta^{2}_{[S,T]}
define

A_{s,t}:=\hskip 0.6458pt\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.43057pt^{s}\int_{s}^{t}f_{r}(x_{r}+Y_{S_{0},r})\,\mathrm{d}r\quad\quad\text{and}\quad\quad\mathcal{A}_{t}:=\int_{S_{0}}^{t}f_{r}(x_{r}+Y_{S_{0},r})\,\mathrm{d}r.

Let us apply stochastic sewing lemma (
Proposition

3.4
) to these random variables.

First, we verify the convergence hypothesis (
3.9
).
Let
\Pi_{N}:=\{s=t^{N}_{0},\dots,t^{N}_{k(N)}=t\}
be a sequence of partitions of
[s,t]\subseteq[S,T]
with
\lim_{N\to\infty}|\Pi_{N}|\to 0
.
Clearly,
A_{u,v}=\hskip 0.6458pt\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.43057pt^{u}[\mathcal{A}_{v}-\mathcal{A}_{u}]
so
R^{N}_{i+1}:=\mathcal{A}_{t_{i+1}^{N}}-\mathcal{A}_{t_{i}^{N}}-A_{t_{i}^{N},t_{i+1}^{N}}
is a
(\mathcal{F}_{t_{i+1}^{N}})_{i}
-martingale difference sequence. Therefore, we get

\displaystyle\Big\|\sum_{i=0}^{k(N)-1}A_{t^{N}_{i},t_{i+1}^{N}}-\mathcal{A}_{T}+\mathcal{A}_{S}\Big\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\Bigl\|\sum_{i=0}^{k(N)-1}R_{i+1}^{N}\Bigr\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{k(N)-1}\|R_{i+1}^{N}\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{L_{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\sum_{i=0}^{k(N)-1}(t_{i+1}^{N}-t_{i}^{N})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{L_{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}(T-S)|\Pi_{N}|\to 0

as
|\Pi_{N}|\to 0
. Hence, condition (
3.9
) holds.

Next, we verify assumption (a) of
Proposition

3.4
.
For
(s,u,t)\in\Delta^{3}_{[S,T]}

\delta A_{s,u,t}=A_{s,t}-A_{s,u}-A_{u,t}=\hskip 0.6458pt\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.43057pt^{s}[\mathcal{A}_{t}-\mathcal{A}_{u}]-\hskip 0.6458pt\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.43057pt^{u}[\mathcal{A}_{t}-\mathcal{A}_{u}].

Therefore
\hskip 0.6458pt\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.43057pt^{s}\delta A_{s,u,t}=0
and condition (a) holds.

Finally, let us check assumption (b) of
Proposition

3.4
.
We recall inequality (
3.13
) and derive

\displaystyle\|A_{s,t}\|_{L_{\rho}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\int_{s}^{t}\|\hskip 0.6458pt\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.43057pt^{s}f_{r}(x_{r}+Y_{S_{0},r})\|_{L_{\rho}(\Omega)}\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\quad\leq C\int_{s}^{t}\|f_{r}(x_{r}+\cdot)\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\rho}^{\beta}}\Bigl((s-S_{0})^{-\frac{Hd}{\rho}}(r-s)^{\beta H}+\mathbbm{1}_{H<\frac{1}{2}}(s-S_{0})^{-\frac{d}{2\rho}}(r-s)^{\frac{d}{2\rho}-\frac{Hd}{\rho}+\beta H}\Bigr)\,\mathrm{d}r

The function

3.4

3.4

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{A}_{T}-\mathcal{A}_{S}\|_{L_{\rho}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{L_{q}([S_{0},T],\mathcal{D}^{\beta}_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}(S-S_{0})^{-\frac{Hd}{\rho}}(T-S)^{1-\frac{1}{q}+\beta H}

(S,T)
were arbitrary elements of

,

.

,

\Big\|\int_{s}^{t}f_{r}(Y_{s,r}+x_{r}+\delta)-f_{r}(Y_{s,r}+x_{r})\,\mathrm{d}r\Big\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}\leq C|\delta|^{\lambda}\|f\|_{L_{q}([s,t],\mathcal{B}_{\rho}^{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}(t-s)^{1-\frac{1}{q}-\frac{Hd}{\rho}+\beta H-\lambda H}.

.

3.8
to the function

instead of

and

Our next step is to establish the same bound as in
Lemma

3.8
but for arbitrarily large moments of the integral. This is achieved using the John–Nirenberg inequality
[
Lê22
, Theorem 2.3]
,
[
Bas95
, Theorem I.6.10]
.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
m\geq 1
,
f:[0,1]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded function and suppose that
f\in L_{q}([0,1],\mathcal{D}_{\rho}^{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, where the parameters
q,\rho,H,d,\beta
satisfy (
3.16
). Let
\psi\colon[0,1]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an adapted measurable process.

Then there exists a constant
C=C(H,d,\beta,\rho,q,m)
such that for every
(s,t)\in\Delta^{2}_{0,1}

\Big\|\int_{s}^{t}f_{r}(Y_{r}+\psi_{s})\,\mathrm{d}r\Big\|_{L_{m}(\Omega)}\leq C\|f\|_{L_{q}([s,t],\mathcal{D}_{\rho}^{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}(t-s)^{1-\frac{1}{q}-\frac{Hd}{\rho}+\beta H}.

(3.19)

Proof.

Fix
(S,T)\in\Delta^{2}_{0,1}
. Define

\mathcal{A}_{t}:=\int_{S}^{t}f_{r}(Y_{r}+\psi_{S})\,\mathrm{d}r,\quad t\in[S,T].

Recall that for
0\leq s\leq r
we have
Y_{r}=Y_{s,r}+\hskip 0.6458pt\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.43057pt^{s}Y_{r}
and
Y_{s,r}
is independent of
\mathcal{F}_{s}
. Therefore, for any
(s,t)\in\Delta_{S,T}^{2}
we deduce

\hskip 0.6458pt\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.43057pt^{s}|\mathcal{A}_{t}-\mathcal{A}_{s}|=\hskip 0.6458pt\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.43057pt^{s}\Big|\int_{s}^{t}f_{r}(Y_{r}+\psi_{S})\,\mathrm{d}r\Big|=\hskip 0.6458pt\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.43057pt\Big|\int_{s}^{t}f_{r}(x_{r}+Y_{s,r})\,\mathrm{d}r\Big|\bigg\rvert_{x_{r}:=\hskip 0.45206pt\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.3014pt^{s}Y_{r}+\psi_{S}}.

Now using the fact that
\|\cdot\|_{L_{1}(\Omega)}\leq\|\cdot\|_{L_{\rho}(\Omega)}
and applying
Lemma

3.8
, we see that there exists
C=C(H,d,\beta,\rho,q)
such that for all
(s,t)\in\Delta_{S,T}^{2}
one has

\hskip 0.6458pt\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.43057pt^{s}|\mathcal{A}_{t}-\mathcal{A}_{s}|\leq C\|f\|_{L_{q}([S,T],\mathcal{D}_{\rho}^{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}(T-S)^{1-\frac{1}{q}-\frac{Hd}{\rho}+\beta H}.

We see that the process
\mathcal{A}
is continuous because
f
is bounded. Therefore, by John–Nirenberg’s theorem (
Proposition

A.2
) we get that there exists
C=C(H,d,\rho,q,m)
such that for all
m\geq 1
,

\Big\|\int_{S}^{T}f_{r}(Y_{r}+\psi_{S})\,\mathrm{d}r\Big\|_{L_{m}(\Omega)}=\|\mathcal{A}_{T}-\mathcal{A}_{S}\|_{L_{m}(\Omega)}\leq C\|f\|_{L_{q}([S,T],\mathcal{D}_{\rho}^{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}(T-S)^{1-\frac{1}{q}-\frac{Hd}{\rho}+\beta H}

which is (
3.19
).
∎

Now, we are ready to establish a bound on all moments of additive functionals of the noise
Y
perturbed by a drift of bounded variation. We will employ the Rosenthal–Burkholder stochastic sewing lemma
[
BLM23
, Theorem 3.6]
. To verify the assumptions of the lemma, we will use
Lemma

3.8
,
Corollary

3.9
, and
Lemma

3.10
. First, we assume additionally that
f
is bounded and Lipschitz, and later we will remove these conditions. Compared with
[
BLM23
, Lemma 4.6]
, we have here to treat more delicately the terms
|\hskip 0.6458pt\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.43057pt^{u}\delta A_{s,u,t}|
and
\hskip 0.6458pt\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.43057pt^{u}|\delta A_{s,u,t}|^{2}
in order to get the optimal condition and break the subcritical barrier.

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
m\geq 2
. Let
f:[0,1]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded function and suppose that
f\in L_{q}([0,1],L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, where
p
and
q
satisfy the main condition (
1.3
). Assume further that
f\in L_{\infty}([0,1],\mathcal{C}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
.
Let
\psi:[0,1]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an adapted measurable process such that

\big\|[\psi]_{\mathcal{C}^{1-\mathrm{var}}([0,1])}\big\|_{L_{m}(\Omega)}<\infty.

(3.20)

Then there exists a constant
C=C(H,d,p,q,m)
such that for any
(s,t)\in\Delta^{2}_{0,1}
one has

\displaystyle\Big\|\int_{s}^{t}f_{r}(Y_{r}+\psi_{r})\,\mathrm{d}r\Big\|_{L_{m}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad\leq C\|f\|_{L_{q}([s,t],L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}(t-s)^{1-\frac{1}{q}-\frac{Hd}{p}}\Bigl(1+\big\|[\psi]_{\mathcal{C}^{1-\mathrm{var}}([s,t])}\big\|_{L_{m}(\Omega)}^{1-\frac{1}{q}}(t-s)^{-H(1-\frac{1}{q})}\Bigr).

(3.21)

Proof.

We want to use
Proposition

3.5
with

A_{s,t}:=\int_{s}^{t}f_{r}(Y_{r}+\psi_{s})\,\mathrm{d}r\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{A}_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}f_{r}(Y_{r}+\psi_{r})\,\mathrm{d}r.

Step 1.

We prove convergence (
3.9
).
Let
\Pi_{N}:=\{S=s_{0}^{N}<\dots<s_{k(N)}^{N}=T\}
be a sequence of partitions of
[S,T]\subseteq[0,1]
such that
|\Pi_{N}|\to 0
when
N\to\infty
.
Using the triangle inequality and the fact that
f
is Lipschitz in space, we get

\displaystyle\Bigl|\mathcal{A}_{T}-\mathcal{A}_{S}-\sum_{i=0}^{k(N)-1}A_{s^{N}_{i},s_{i+1}^{N}}\Bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{k(N)-1}\int_{s_{i}^{N}}^{s_{i+1}^{N}}\bigl|f_{r}(Y_{r}+\psi_{r})-f_{r}(Y_{r}+\psi_{s_{i}^{N}})\bigr|\,\mathrm{d}r.

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{L_{\infty}([0,1],\mathcal{C}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\sum_{i=0}^{k(N)-1}\int_{s_{i}^{N}}^{s_{i+1}^{N}}[\psi]_{\mathcal{C}^{1-\mathrm{var}}([s_{i}^{N},s_{i+1}^{N}])}\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{L_{\infty}([0,1],\mathcal{C}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}[\psi]_{\mathcal{C}^{1-\mathrm{var}}([S,T])}|\Pi_{N}|.

Since by (
3.20
)
[\psi]_{\mathcal{C}^{1-\mathrm{var}}([S,T])}<\infty
a.s., we see that the left hand-side of the above inequality goes to
0
a.s. (and thus in probability) as
N\to\infty
. This proves condition (
3.9
).

Step 2.

We verify condition (a) of
Proposition

3.5
. Clearly, for
(s,u,t)\in\Delta^{3}_{[0,1]}
, we have

\delta A_{s,u,t}=\int_{u}^{t}f_{r}(Y_{r}+\psi_{s})-f_{r}(Y_{r}+\psi_{u})\,\mathrm{d}r.

Recall that
Y_{r}=\hskip 0.6458pt\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.43057pt^{u}Y_{r}+Y_{u,r}
. Since
\psi_{s}
,
\psi_{u}
,
\hskip 0.6458pt\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.43057pt^{u}Y_{r}
are
\mathcal{F}_{u}
-measurable, and
Y_{u,r}
is independent of
\mathcal{F}_{u}
by
3.6
, we get

|\hskip 0.6458pt\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.43057pt^{u}[\delta A_{s,u,t}]|\leq\int_{u}^{t}\big|\hskip 0.6458pt\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.43057pt\big[f_{r}(Y_{u,r}+x_{r}+\delta)-f_{r}(Y_{u,r}+x_{r})\big]\big|\,\mathrm{d}r\bigg\rvert_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{r}:=\hskip 0.45206pt\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.3014pt^{u}Y_{r}+\psi_{u}\\
\delta:=\psi_{u}-\psi_{s}\end{subarray}}.

(3.22)

Let
\lambda\in(0,1]
. Then by (
3.14
), we have

\big|\hskip 0.6458pt\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.43057pt\big[f_{r}(Y_{u,r}+x_{r}+\delta)-f_{r}(Y_{u,r}+x_{r})\big]\big|\leq C|\delta|^{\lambda}\|f_{r}\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}(r-u)^{-\frac{Hd}{p}-\lambda H}

for
C=C(H,d,\lambda,p)
. Substituting this back into (
3.22
) and applying the Hölder inequality, we get

\displaystyle|\hskip 0.6458pt\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.43057pt^{u}[\delta A_{s,u,t}]|

\displaystyle\leq C|\psi_{s}-\psi_{u}|^{\lambda}\int_{u}^{t}\|f_{r}\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}(r-u)^{-\frac{Hd}{p}-\lambda H}\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\leq C[\psi]_{\mathcal{C}^{1-\mathrm{var}}([s,t])}^{\lambda}\|f\|_{L_{q}([u,t],L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}(t-u)^{1-\frac{1}{q}-\frac{Hd}{p}-\lambda H},

(3.23)

provided that
1-\frac{1}{q}-\frac{Hd}{p}-\lambda H>0
.

Pick now
\lambda:=1-\frac{1}{q}
.
Then by main condition (
1.3
)

\quad\quad 1-\frac{1}{q}-\frac{Hd}{p}-\lambda H>0\quad\quad\text{and}\quad\quad 1-\frac{Hd}{p}+\lambda(1-H)>1.

(3.24)

The first part of (
3.24
) ensures that such a choice of
\lambda
is permissible for the validity of (
3.3
). Since
\|f\|^{q}_{L_{q}([s,t],L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}
,
[\psi]_{\mathcal{C}^{1-\mathrm{var}}([s,t])}
and
(t-s)
are three controls, the right-hand side of (
3.3
) is, by
[
FV10
, Exercise 1.10]
, a control to the power
1-\frac{Hd}{p}+\lambda(1-H)
. Therefore, condition (a) of
Proposition

3.5
is satisfied by the second part of (
3.24
) and (
3.3
).

Step 3.

We verify condition (b) of
Proposition

3.5
. Let
(s,u,t)\in\Delta^{3}_{[0,1]}
. Arguing as above, using
3.6
we get

\displaystyle\hskip 0.6458pt\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.43057pt^{u}[|\delta A_{s,u,t}|^{2}]=\hskip 0.6458pt\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.43057pt\Bigl[\int_{u}^{t}f_{r}(Y_{u,r}+x_{r}+\delta)-f_{r}(Y_{u,r}+x_{r})\,\mathrm{d}r\Bigr]^{2}\bigg\rvert_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{r}:=\hskip 0.45206pt\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.3014pt^{u}Y_{r}+\psi_{u}\\
\delta:=\psi_{u}-\psi_{s}\end{subarray}}.

Define

\rho:=2\vee p,\quad\quad\beta:=\frac{d}{\rho}-\frac{d}{p}\leq 0,\quad\quad\lambda:=\frac{1}{2}\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{q}\Bigr).

We verify the hypothesis of
Corollary

3.9
with these values of
\rho
,
\beta
and
\lambda
.
By the Besov embedding
f\in L_{q}([0,1],\mathcal{B}_{\rho}^{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
. We also have
\rho\geq 2
. Furthermore, using (
1.3
) we get

\frac{1}{q}+\frac{Hd}{\rho}+H(2\lambda-\beta)=\frac{1-H}{q}+H+\frac{Hd}{p}<1.

Since
\lambda\leq 2\lambda
, the third part of condition (
3.18
) is satisfied.
Finally, we note that by definition
\beta=\frac{d}{p}(\frac{p}{\rho}-1)
and
\rho\leq 2p
so
\beta\geq-\frac{d}{2p}
.
Using this fact and (
1.3
) we get

2H(\lambda-\beta)=H-\frac{H}{q}-2H\beta\leq H-\frac{H}{q}+\frac{Hd}{p}<1-\frac{1}{q}<1

so
\beta-\lambda>-1/(2H)
and the last part of (
3.18
) is satisfied.

Therefore applying
Corollary

3.9
and using the Besov embedding
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{B}_{\rho}^{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\|\cdot\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
, we get that there exists
C=C(H,d,p,q)
such that

\displaystyle\hskip 0.6458pt\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.43057pt^{u}[|\delta A_{s,u,t}|^{2}]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{L_{q}([s,t],L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}|\psi_{s}-\psi_{u}|^{\lambda}(t-s)^{1-\frac{1}{q}-\frac{Hd}{p}-\lambda H}

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{L_{q}([s,t],L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}[\psi]_{\mathcal{C}^{1-\mathrm{var}}([s,t])}^{\lambda}(t-s)^{1-\frac{1}{q}-\frac{Hd}{p}-\lambda H}.

As in the previous step, the right-hand side is a control to the power
1-\frac{Hd}{p}+(1-H)\lambda
and for condition (b) of
Proposition

3.5
to be verified we need this power to be bigger than
\frac{1}{2}
.
By (
1.3
),
\frac{Hd}{p}<2\lambda(1-H)
so
1-\frac{Hd}{p}+(1-H)\lambda>1-\frac{Hd}{2p}
.
But again by (
1.3
),
\frac{Hd}{p}<1
so
1-\frac{Hd}{p}+(1-H)\lambda>\frac{1}{2}
and condition (b) holds.

Step 4.

We verify condition (c) of
Proposition

3.5
.
We take

\rho:=2\vee p,\quad\quad\beta:=\frac{d}{\rho}-\frac{d}{p}.

and verify all the conditions of
Lemma

3.10
. Clearly,
\rho\geq 2
.
Assumption (
1.3
) yields

\frac{1}{q}+\frac{Hd}{\rho}-\beta H=\frac{1}{q}+\frac{Hd}{p}<1-H+\frac{H}{q}<1

so the third part of (
3.16
) is satisfied.
Finally,
\beta\geq-\frac{d}{2p}
and because
\frac{Hd}{p}<1
by (
1.3
) the last part of (
3.16
) is satisfied.

Thus, all the conditions of
Lemma

3.10
holds. Therefore we deduce that there exists a constant
C=C(H,d,p,q,m)
such that for all
m\geq 1
and all
(s,t)\in\Delta_{0,1}
,

\|A_{s,t}\|_{L_{m}(\Omega)}=\Bigl\|\int_{s}^{t}f_{r}(Y_{r}+\psi_{s})\,\mathrm{d}r\Bigr\|_{L_{m}(\Omega)}\leq C\|f\|_{L_{q}([s,t],L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}(t-s)^{1-\frac{1}{q}-\frac{Hd}{p}},

where we also used the Besov embedding
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\rho}^{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\|\cdot\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
(recall the convention (
3.11
)). Thus, condition (c) of
Proposition

3.5

holds.

Step 4.

Thus, all the assumptions of
Proposition

3.5
are satisfied. Therefore, there exists a constant
C=C(H,d,p,q,m)
such that for all
(s,t)\in\Delta_{0,1}

\displaystyle\Big\|\int_{s}^{t}f_{r}(Y_{r}+\psi_{r})\,\mathrm{d}r\Big\|_{L_{m}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\,\,\leq C\|f\|_{L_{q}([s,t],L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}(t-s)^{1-\frac{1}{q}-\frac{Hd}{p}}

\displaystyle\,\,\quad+C\|f\|_{L_{q}([s,t],L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\big\|[\psi]^{1-\frac{1}{q}}_{\mathcal{C}^{1-\mathrm{var}}([s,t])}\big\|_{L_{m}(\Omega)}(t-s)^{1-H-\frac{1-H}{q}-\frac{Hd}{p}}

\displaystyle\,\,\quad+C\|f\|_{L_{q}([s,t],L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\big\|[\psi]^{\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{1}{q})}_{\mathcal{C}^{1-\mathrm{var}}([s,t])}\big\|_{L_{m}(\Omega)}(t-s)^{1-\frac{H}{2}-\frac{2-H}{2q}-\frac{Hd}{p}}

\displaystyle\,\,\leq C\|f\|_{L_{q}([s,t],L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}(t-s)^{1-\frac{1}{q}-\frac{Hd}{p}}\Bigl(1+\frac{\big\|[\psi]_{\mathcal{C}^{1-\mathrm{var}}([s,t])}\big\|^{1-\frac{1}{q}}_{L_{m}(\Omega)}}{(t-s)^{H(1-\frac{1}{q})}}+\frac{\big\|[\psi]_{\mathcal{C}^{1-\mathrm{var}}([s,t])}\big\|^{\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{1}{q})}_{L_{m}(\Omega)}}{(t-s)^{\frac{H}{2}(1-\frac{1}{q})}}\Bigr).

Using inequality
1+x+\sqrt{x}\leq 2(1+x)
valid for all
x\geq 0
, we get the desired bound (
3.11
).
∎

Finally, let us remove the restriction in
Lemma

3.11
that
f
is a smooth function.

Corollary 3.12
.

Let
m\geq 2
. Let
f\in L_{q}([0,1],L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, where
p
and
q
satisfy (
1.3
).
Let
\psi:[0,1]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an adapted measurable process such that (
3.20
) holds. Then there exists a constant
C=C(H,d,p,q,m)
such that for any
(s,t)\in\Delta^{2}_{0,1}
bound (
3.11
) holds.

Proof.

Step 1.

Let
f\colon[0,1]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded continuous function. Then for any fixed
\varepsilon>0
the function
f_{\varepsilon}(t,x):=\int p_{\varepsilon}(y)f(t,x-y)\,dy
, where
p_{t}
denotes the Gaussian kernel, satisfies all the conditions of
Lemma

3.11
. Since
\|f_{\varepsilon}\|_{L_{q}([s,t],L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\leq\|f\|_{L_{q}([s,t],L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}
for any
\varepsilon>0
, the desired result follows from the pointwise convergence of
f_{\varepsilon}
to
f
as
\varepsilon\to 0
at any
t,x
and the Fatou’s lemma.

Step 2.

Now let’s prove the result for
f=\mathbbm{1}_{U}
, where
U
is an open set in
[0,1]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Then there exists a sequence of continuous functions
f_{n}
, such that
f_{n}\leq\I_{U}
everywhere and
f_{n}
converges to
\I_{U}
pointwise. Therefore, by Fatou’s lemma,

\Big\|\int_{s}^{t}\mathbbm{1}_{U}(r,Y_{r}+\psi_{r})\,\mathrm{d}r\Big\|_{L_{m}(\Omega)}\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\Big\|\int_{s}^{t}f_{n}(r,Y_{r}+\psi_{r})\,\mathrm{d}r\Big\|_{L_{m}(\Omega)}.

Now we can apply Step 1 of the argument. Since
0\leq f_{n}\leq\mathbbm{1}_{U}
we have
\|f_{n}\|_{L_{q}([s,t],L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\leq\|\mathbbm{1}_{U}\|_{L_{q}([s,t],L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}
. Therefore (
3.11
) holds for
f=\mathbbm{1}_{U}
.

Step 3.

Now let
f
be a bounded non-negative measurable function. Denote
N=\|f\|_{L_{\infty}([0,1]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}
.
Let
\varepsilon>0
.
By Lusin’s theorem combined with Tietze extension theorem there exists a non-negative continuous function
f_{\varepsilon}
such that
\text{Leb}(\{f_{\varepsilon}\neq f\})\leq\varepsilon
. By regularity of the Lebesgue measure, there exists an open set
U_{\varepsilon}
such that
\{f_{\varepsilon}\neq f\}\subset U_{\varepsilon}
and
\text{Leb}(U_{\varepsilon})\leq 2\varepsilon
.
Clearly, one has
0\leq f\leq f_{\varepsilon}+N\mathbbm{1}_{U_{\varepsilon}}
and therefore

\Big\|\int_{s}^{t}f(r,Y_{r}+\psi_{r})\,\mathrm{d}r\Big\|_{L_{m}(\Omega)}\leq\Big\|\int_{s}^{t}f_{\varepsilon}(r,Y_{r}+\psi_{r})\,\mathrm{d}r\Big\|_{L_{m}(\Omega)}+N\Big\|\int_{s}^{t}\mathbbm{1}_{U_{\varepsilon}}(r,Y_{r}+\psi_{r})\,\mathrm{d}r\Big\|_{L_{m}(\Omega)}.

We bound the first term using the first step and the second term using the second step.
We get

\Big\|\int_{s}^{t}f(r,Y_{s,r}+\psi_{r})\,\mathrm{d}r\Big\|_{L_{m}(\Omega)}\leq C\bigl(\|f_{\varepsilon}\|_{L_{q}([s,t],L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}+N\|\mathbbm{1}_{U_{\varepsilon}}\|_{L_{q}([s,t],L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\bigr)I_{s,t}

(3.25)

where

I_{s,t}:=(t-s)^{1-\frac{1}{q}-\frac{Hd}{p}}\bigl(1+\big\|[\psi]_{\mathcal{C}^{1-\mathrm{var}}([s,t])}\big\|_{L_{m}(\Omega)}^{1-\frac{1}{q}}(t-s)^{-H(1-\frac{1}{q})}\bigr)

is independent of
\varepsilon
. Since
p,q<\infty
and
\text{Leb}(U_{\varepsilon})\leq 2\varepsilon
, we have

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|\mathbbm{1}_{U_{\varepsilon}}\|_{L_{q}([s,t],L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\leq\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}Leb(U_{\varepsilon})^{\frac{1}{p\vee q}}=0.

Using again that
f_{\varepsilon}\leq f+N\mathbbm{1}_{U_{\varepsilon}}
, we see that
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|f_{\varepsilon}\|_{L_{q}([s,t],L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\leq\|f\|_{L_{q}([s,t],L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}
.
As a result, by passing in (
3.25
) to the limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
, we get (
3.11
) for any bounded measurable non-negative
f
.

Step 4.

Consider a non-negative measurable function
f\in L_{q}([s,t],L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
.
The function
f\wedge N
is non-negative, bounded, and measurable. Thus, we can apply the third step and get (
3.11
) for
f\wedge N
.
The function
f\wedge N
converges pointwise to
f
when
N\to\infty
and
\|f\wedge N\|_{L_{q}([s,t],L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\leq\|f\|_{L_{q}([s,t],L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}
. Therefore, by Fatou’s lemma we see that (
3.11
) holds for
f
.

Step 5.

Finally, let us consider any
f\in L_{q}([s,t],L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
.
We apply Step 4 separately to the positive and negative part of
f
. Using that
\|f\vee 0\|_{L_{q}([s,t],L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\leq\|f\|_{L_{q}([s,t],L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}
and
\|(-f)\vee 0\|_{L_{q}([s,t],L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\leq\|f\|_{L_{q}([s,t],L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}
,
we get the desired result.
∎

Finally, we are ready to provide a priori bounds to solutions to equation

\psi_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}f_{r}(x+\psi_{r}+Y_{r})\,\mathrm{d}r,\quad t\in[0,1],\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(Eq(
x;f;Y
))

where the integral is understood as a Lebesgue integral. This equation is closely related to our main SDEs (
1.1
) and (
1.2
), by taking there
Y=W^{H}
or
Y=L
and
X=Y+\psi+x
. We say that
\psi
solves

)
if
\mathsf{P}\bigl(\text{\hyperref@@ii[SDEfunc]{Eq($x;f;Y$)} holds for all $t\in[0,1]$}\bigr)=1
.

Corollary 3.13
.

Let
m\geq 2
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Let
f:[0,1]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded function and suppose that
f\in L_{q}([0,1],L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, where
p
and
q
satisfy the main condition (
1.3
).
Let
\psi:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a solution to
Eq(
x;f;Y
)
adapted to the filtration
(\mathcal{F}_{t})
.

Then there exists a constant
C=C(H,d,p,q,m)
such that for any
(s,t)\in\Delta^{2}_{0,1}
one has

\bigl\|[\psi]_{\mathcal{C}^{1-\mathrm{var}}([s,t])}\bigr\|_{L_{m}(\Omega)}\leq C\bigl(\|f\|_{L_{q}([s,t],L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}+\|f\|_{L_{q}([s,t],L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}^{q}\bigr)(t-s)^{1-H-\frac{Hd}{p}-\frac{1-H}{q}}.

(3.26)

Furthermore, there exist
\gamma>0
and
C=C(H,d,p,q,m)>0
such that

\mathsf{P}(\|\psi\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\gamma}([0,1])}\geq r)\leq C\bigl(1+\|f\|_{L_{q}([s,t],L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}^{q}\bigr)^{m}r^{-m},\quad r>0.

(3.27)

Remark 3.14
.

We note that the power of the factor
(t-s)
in the right-hand side of (
3.26
) is positive by condition (
1.3
).

Proof.

Fix
m\geq 2
.
We apply
Corollary

3.12
to the function
|f|
. We see that
|f|\in L_{q}([0,1],L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
.
Further, we clearly have from the definition of
\psi
in
Eq(
x;f;Y
)

[\psi]_{\mathcal{C}^{1-\mathrm{var}}([s,t])}=\int_{s}^{t}|f_{r}|(Y_{r}+\psi_{r}+x)\,\mathrm{d}r,\quad(s,t)\in\Delta^{2}_{[0,1]}.

Since
f
is bounded, we immediately get
\|[\psi]_{\mathcal{C}^{1-\mathrm{var}}([0,1])}\|_{L_{m}(\Omega)}\leq\|f\|_{L_{\infty}([0,1]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}<+\infty
.
This allow us to apply
Corollary

3.12
. We derive for any
(s,t)\in\Delta^{2}_{[0,1]}

\displaystyle\big\|[\psi]_{\mathcal{C}^{1-\mathrm{var}}([s,t])}\big\|_{L_{m}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\,\,=\Bigl\|\int_{s}^{t}|f_{r}|(Y_{r}+\psi_{r}+x)\,\mathrm{d}r\Bigr\|_{L_{m}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\,\,\leq C\|f\|_{L_{q}([s,t],L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}(t-s)^{1-\frac{1}{q}-\frac{Hd}{p}}\Bigl(1+\big\|[\psi]_{\mathcal{C}^{1-\mathrm{var}}([s,t])}\big\|_{L_{m}(\Omega)}^{1-\frac{1}{q}}(t-s)^{-H(1-\frac{1}{q})}\Bigr)

(3.28)

\displaystyle\,\,\leq C\|f\|_{L_{q}([s,t],L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\Bigl(1+\bigl\|[\psi]_{\mathcal{C}^{1-\mathrm{var}}([s,t])}\bigr\|^{1-\frac{1}{q}}_{L_{m}(\Omega)}\Bigr)

where
C=C(H,d,p,q,m)
and in the last inequality we used that
1-\frac{1}{q}-\frac{Hd}{p}-H(1-\frac{1}{q})>0
thanks to (
1.3
).
By
Lemma

A.4
, this implies

\bigl\|[\psi]_{\mathcal{C}^{1-\mathrm{var}}([s,t])}\bigr\|_{L_{m}(\Omega)}\leq C\bigl(\|f\|_{L_{q}([s,t],L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}+\|f\|_{L_{q}([s,t],L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}^{q}\bigr)\leq C\bigl(1+\|f\|_{L_{q}([s,t],L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}^{q}\bigr)

for a constant
C=C(H,d,p,q,m)
.
Reinserting this bound into (
3.3
), we deduce that there exists a constant
C=C(H,d,p,q,m)
such that for all
(s,t)\in\Delta^{2}_{[0,1]}
, one has

\bigl\|[\psi]_{\mathcal{C}^{1-\mathrm{var}}([s,t])}\bigr\|_{L_{m}(\Omega)}\leq C\bigl(\|f\|_{L_{q}([s,t],L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}+\|f\|_{L_{q}([s,t],L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}^{q}\bigr)(t-s)^{1-H-\frac{Hd}{p}-\frac{1-H}{q}},

which is (
3.26
).

Next, note that for any
m\geq 1
,
(s,t)\in\Delta^{2}_{[0,1]}
we have

\|\psi_{t}-\psi_{s}\|_{L_{m}(\Omega)}\leq\bigl\|[\psi]_{\mathcal{C}^{1-\mathrm{var}}([s,t])}\bigr\|_{L_{m}(\Omega)}\leq C\bigl(1+\|f\|_{L_{q}([s,t],L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}^{q}\bigr)(t-s)^{1-H-\frac{Hd}{p}-\frac{1-H}{q}},

for
C=C(H,d,p,q,m)
. Therefore, applying the quantitative version of Kolmogorov’s continuity theorem
[
FV10
, Theorem A.11]
and the Chebyshev inequality, we get (
3.27
) with
\gamma=\frac{1}{2}(1-H-\frac{Hd}{p}-\frac{1-H}{q})>0
.
∎

We also obtain a certain stability result for the solutions of SDE

)
.

Lemma 3.15
.

Assume that the parameters
p,q\in[1,\infty)
satisfy (
1.3
). Let
(b^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
be a sequence of bounded continuous functions
[0,1]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
converging to
b
in
L_{q}([0,1],L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
. Let
(x^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
be a sequence in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
converging to
x
. Let
Y^{n}
be a random element which has the same law as
Y
and which is adapted to the filtration
\mathbb{F}^{n}:=(\mathcal{F}^{n}_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
. Assume that
\psi^{n}
solves
Eq(
x^{n};b^{n};Y^{n}
)
and is also adapted to
\mathbb{F}^{n}
.

Suppose that there exists a measurable function
\psi\colon[0,1]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
adapted to the same filtration
\mathbb{F}:=(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
as in
3.6
and such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{r\in[0,1]}|\psi_{r}^{n}-\psi_{r}|=0,\,\,a.s;\qquad\lim_{n\to\infty}|Y_{t}^{n}-Y_{t}|=0,\,\,a.s\text{\,\, for any $t\in[0,1]$.}

(3.29)

Then
\psi
solves
Eq(
x;b;Y
)
.

Proof.

Define for
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}

X^{n}:=x^{n}+\psi^{n}+Y^{n},\quad X:=x+\psi+Y.

(3.30)

We begin by observing that by
Corollary

3.13
there exists a constant
C=C(H,d,p,q)>0
independent of
n
such that

\|\sup_{t\in[0,1]}|\psi^{n}_{t}|\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}\leq\|[\psi^{n}]_{\mathcal{C}^{1-\mathrm{var}}([0,1])}\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}\leq C,\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},

(3.31)

where we used that
\psi^{n}=0
by definition of solution to
Eq(
x^{n};b^{n};Y^{n}
)
.
By passing to the limit as
n\to\infty
, we get

\|\sup_{t\in[0,1]}|\psi_{t}|\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}\leq C.

(3.32)

Let
n,k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. Consider the following decomposition:

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\Big|\psi_{t}-\int_{0}^{t}b_{r}(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}r\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\Big|\psi_{t}-\int_{0}^{t}b^{k}_{r}(X^{k}_{r})\,\mathrm{d}r\Big|+\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\Big|\int_{0}^{t}(b^{k}_{r}-b^{n}_{r})(X^{k}_{r})\,\mathrm{d}r\Big|

\displaystyle\quad+\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\Big|\int_{0}^{t}b^{n}_{r}(X^{k}_{r})-b^{n}_{r}(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}r\Big|+\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\Big|\int_{0}^{t}(b^{n}_{r}-b_{r})(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}r\Big|

\displaystyle=:A_{1}(k)+A_{2}(n,k)+A_{3}(n,k)+A_{4}(n).

(3.33)

Let us bound all the terms in the right hand side of the above inequality.

By assumptions of the lemma,
\psi^{k}_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}b_{r}^{k}(X_{r}^{k})\,\mathrm{d}r
,
t\in[0,1]
, and
\psi^{k}
converges to
\psi
almost surely in
\mathcal{C}([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Therefore,

\lim_{k\to\infty}A_{1}(k)=0\quad a.s.

(3.34)

Next, applying
Lemma

3.11
to the function
|b^{k}-b^{n}|
and using bound (
3.31
), we get

\|A_{2}(n,k)\|_{L_{1}(\Omega)}\leq\Big\|\int_{0}^{1}|b^{k}_{r}-b^{n}_{r}|(Y^{k}_{r}+x^{k}+\psi^{k}_{r})\,\mathrm{d}r\Big\|_{L_{1}(\Omega)}\leq C\|b^{k}-b^{n}\|_{L_{q}([0,1],L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}

(3.35)

for
C=C(H,d,p,q)>0
.
By assumptions, for any
r\in[0,1]
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}

|b^{n}_{r}(X^{k}_{r})-b^{n}_{r}(X_{r})|=|b^{n}_{r}(x^{k}+\psi_{r}^{k}+Y_{r}^{k})-b^{n}_{r}(x+\psi_{r}+Y_{r})|\to 0\,\,a.s.\quad\text{as $k\to\infty$},

because the function
b^{n}
is continuous,
(\psi_{r}^{k},Y_{r}^{k})
converge a.s. to
(\psi_{r},Y_{r})
and
x^{k}
converges to
x
. Therefore, applying twice the dominated convergence theorem, which is applicable because
b^{n}
is bounded,
we get for any fixed
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}

\lim_{k\to\infty}\|A_{3}(n,k)\|_{L_{1}(\Omega)}\leq\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\|b^{n}_{r}(X^{k}_{r})-b^{n}_{r}(X_{r})\|_{L_{1}(\Omega)}\,\mathrm{d}r=0.

(3.36)

Finally, applying
Corollary

3.12
with
|b^{n}-b|
instead of
f
and using (
3.32
), we get

\|A_{4}(n)\|_{L_{1}(\Omega)}\leq\Big\|\int_{0}^{1}|b^{n}_{r}-b_{r}|(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}r\Big\|_{L_{1}(\Omega)}\leq C\|b^{n}-b\|_{L_{q}([0,1],L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}

(3.37)

for
C=C(H,d,p,q)>0
.
Now we combine (
3.34
)–(
3.37
), substitute them into (
3.3
), and pass to the limit for fixed
n
as
k\to\infty
. We get that for any
\varepsilon>0

\mathsf{P}\Bigl(\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\Big|\psi_{t}-\int_{0}^{t}b_{r}(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}r\Big|>\varepsilon\Bigr)\leq C\varepsilon^{-1}\|b-b^{n}\|_{L_{q}([0,1],L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}.

By passing in the above inequality to limit as

and then as

, we get that

\mathsf{P}\Bigl(\forall t\in[0,1]:\psi_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}b_{r}(x+\psi_{r}+Y_{r})\,\mathrm{d}r\Bigr)=1,

where we also use the definition of
X
in (
3.30
).
∎

3.4
Fractional and Lévy noises satisfy the main assumption

In this section, we prove that our main hypothesis on the noise,
3.6
, is satisfied for fractional Brownian and Lévy noises.

We begin with the fractional Brownian case.

Lemma 3.16
.

Let
\mathbb{F}=(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
be a complete filtration. Let
W^{H}
be a
d
-dimensional
\mathbb{F}
-fractional Brownian motion of Hurst parameter
H\in(0,1)
.
Then
W^{H}
satisfies
3.6
with this
H
and
\mathbb{F}
.

Proof.

By
Proposition

A.1
there exists a
d
-dimensional
\mathbb{F}
-Brownian motion
B
such that

W_{t}^{H}=\int_{0}^{t}K_{H}(t,r)\,\mathrm{d}B_{r},\quad t\in[0,1],

where the kernel
K_{H}
is defined in (
2.2
)–(
2.3
). Then we have for any
(s,t)\in\Delta^{2}_{0,1}

Y_{s,t}=W_{t}^{H}-\hskip 0.6458pt\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.43057pt^{s}W_{t}^{H}=\int_{s}^{t}K_{H}(t,r)\,\mathrm{d}B_{r}.

(3.38)

Since
B
is a
\mathbb{F}
-Brownian motion, (
3.38
) implies that
Y_{s,t}
is independent of
\mathcal{F}_{s}
for any
(s,t)\in\Delta^{2}_{0,1}
. This shows part (i) of
3.6
.

To show part (ii), we note that for any
(s,u,t)\in\Delta^{3}_{0,1}
we have

\hskip 0.6458pt\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.43057pt^{u}Y_{s,t}=\int_{s}^{u}K_{H}(t,r)\,\mathrm{d}B_{r}.

Hence
\hskip 0.6458pt\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.43057pt^{u}Y_{s,t}
is a vector of Gaussian independent random variables each having variance

\int_{s}^{u}K_{H}^{2}(t,r)\,\mathrm{d}r
. Therefore, denoting by
p_{s,u,t}
the density of
\hskip 0.6458pt\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.43057pt^{u}Y_{s,t}
, we get that there exists
C=C(H,d)
such that

\|p_{s,u,t}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=C\Bigl(\int_{s}^{u}K_{H}^{2}(t,r)\,\mathrm{d}r\Bigr)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\leq C((t-s)^{2H}-(t-u)^{2H})^{-\frac{d}{2}},

where in the inequality we used
Proposition

A.1
6
. This proves part (ii) of
3.6
.

Finally, let us verify part (iii) of
3.6
. Let
\beta<0
,
\rho\in[1,\infty]
and
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be a measurable function. By the definition of the Besov norm for
\beta<0

\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}_{\rho}^{\beta}}=\sup_{a\in(0,1]}a^{-\frac{\beta}{2}}\|p_{a}*f\|_{L_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\sup_{a>0}(a\wedge 1)^{-\frac{\beta}{2}}\|p_{a}*f\|_{L_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

where
p_{a}
stands for the
d
-dimensional Gaussian density of variance
diag(a,\ldots,a)
. Let now
(s,t)\in\Delta_{0,1}^{2}
. Denote
\sigma^{2}_{s,t}:=\int_{s}^{t}K_{H}^{2}(t,r)\,dr
. Then we have

\|\hskip 0.6458pt\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.43057ptf(Y_{s,t}+\cdot)\|_{L_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\|p_{\sigma^{2}_{s,t}}*f\|_{L_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}_{\rho}^{\beta}}(\sigma^{2}_{s,t}\wedge 1)^{\frac{\beta}{2}}.

Since, by
Proposition

A.1
6
,
\sigma^{2}_{s,t}\geq C(H,d)(t-s)^{2H}
and
t-s\leq 1
, we get

\|\hskip 0.6458pt\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.43057ptf(Y_{s,t}+\cdot)\|_{L_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}_{\rho}^{\beta}}(t-s)^{\beta H},

which is the desired bound (
3.13
). Thus, part (iii) of
3.6
holds.
∎

Similarly, it is easy to see that our assumptions are satisfied for the case of
\alpha
-stable noise.

Lemma 3.17
.

Let
\mathbb{F}=(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
be a complete filtration. Let
L
be a
d
-dimensional symmetric
\alpha
-stable
\mathbb{F}
-Lévy process having characteristic function (
2.6
) with
\alpha\in(1,2)
. Then
L
satisfies
3.6
with this
\mathbb{F}
and
H:=1/\alpha
.

Proof.

Let
p^{\alpha}_{t}
be the density of
L_{t}
,
t>0
. By the scaling property of
\alpha
-stable processes we have for any
t>0

p^{\alpha}_{t}(x)=t^{-\frac{d}{\alpha}}p^{\alpha}_{1}(t^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(3.39)

Since
L
is a Markov process,

Y_{s,t}=L_{t}-\hskip 0.6458pt\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.43057pt^{s}L_{t}=L_{t}-L_{s},\quad(s,t)\in\Delta^{2}_{0,1}.

(3.40)

Therefore,
Y_{s,t}
is independent of
\mathcal{F}_{s}
, which proves part (i) of
3.6
.

Let
(s,u,t)\in\Delta^{3}_{0,1}
. Then
\hskip 0.6458pt\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.43057pt^{u}Y_{s,t}=L_{u}-L_{s}
. Therefore, using (
3.39
) we derive

p_{s,u,t}(x)=(u-s)^{-\frac{d}{\alpha}}p_{1}^{\alpha}((u-s)^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}x),\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\quad\text{so}\quad\|p_{s,u,t}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=(u-s)^{-\frac{d}{\alpha}}\|p_{1}^{\alpha}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Because
L
is an
\alpha
-stable Lévy process,
p_{1}^{\alpha}
is bounded
[
KI99
, Example 28.2]
. Hence part (ii) of
3.6
holds.

Finally, to verify part (iii), we fix
\beta<0
,
\rho\in[1,\infty]
and
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
measurable. Recall relation (
3.40
). Using the embedding
L_{\rho}\subset\mathcal{B}^{1}_{\rho}
, Young’s convolution inequality for Besov spaces (
[
KS21
, Theorem 2.2]
) and scaling (
3.39
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|\hskip 0.6458pt\mathsf{E}\hskip 0.43057ptf(Y_{s,t}+\cdot)\|_{L_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle=\|f*p^{\alpha}_{t-s}\|_{L_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=(t-s)^{\frac{d}{\alpha\rho}}\|f((t-s)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\cdot)*p^{\alpha}_{1}\|_{L_{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq C(t-s)^{\frac{d}{\alpha\rho}}\|f((t-s)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\cdot)\|_{\mathcal{B}_{\rho}^{\beta}}\|p_{1}^{\alpha}\|_{\mathcal{B}_{1}^{1-\beta}}

\displaystyle\leq C(t-s)^{\frac{\beta}{\alpha}}\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}_{\rho}^{\beta}}\|p_{1}^{\alpha}\|_{\mathcal{B}_{1}^{1-\beta}},

where
C=C(H,d,\beta,\rho)>0
, and in the last inequality follows from homogeneity of the Besov norm (
3.2
) valid because
t-s\leq 1
. Since
\|p_{1}^{\alpha}\|_{\mathcal{B}_{1}^{1-\beta}}<\infty
by
Proposition

A.5
, part (iii) of
3.6
holds.
∎

3.5
Proofs of weak existence

Finally, we are ready to show weak existence of solutions to SDEs (
1.1
) and (
1.2
).

Proof of
Theorem

2.1
.

We first remark that (i) follows straightforwardly from (ii). Indeed, such a sequence of smooth functions
b^{n}\to b
exists by a density argument, and on any probability space containing a fractional Brownian motion
W^{H}
, we can construct, by Banach’s fixed-point theorem, a strong solution
X^{n}
to (
2.4
) that is adapted to the natural filtration of
W^{H}
.
Therefore, the conditions of (ii) are satisfied, which implies weak existence.

Thus, we concentrate on (ii).

Step 1: setup.

We are given a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathsf{P})
with a fractional Brownian motion
W^{H}
. We denote by
\mathbb{F}=(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
the natural filtration associated with
W^{H}
. By assumption, we have a sequence
\{b_{n},n\in\mathbb{N}\}
of smooth functions converging to
b
and strong solutions
X^{n}
to (
2.4
). The solutions
X^{n}
are therefore adapted to
\mathbb{F}
. Without loss of generality, we assume
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|b^{n}\|_{L_{q}([0,1],L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\leq 2\|b\|_{L_{q}([0,1],L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}
.

We define the drift
\psi^{n}
by

\psi^{n}_{t}:=\int_{0}^{t}b_{r}^{n}(X_{r}^{n})\,\mathrm{d}r,\quad t\in[0,1]

(3.41)

when equation (
2.4
) is satisfied and
0
otherwise.
Therefore by (
2.4
) a.s. we have

X^{n}_{t}=x^{n}+\psi_{t}^{n}+W_{t}^{H}\quad\text{for all $t$ in $[0,1]$}.

(3.42)

Hence
\psi^{n}
solves
Eq(
x^{n};b^{n};W^{H}
)
.

Step 2: tightness.

It follows from
Lemma

3.16
, that the process
W^{H}
and the filtration
\mathbb{F}
satisfies
3.6
. Hence, by
Corollary

3.13
there exists
\gamma,m>0
such that for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
N>0

\mathsf{P}(\|\psi^{n}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\gamma}}>N)\leq CN^{-m},

(3.43)

for some
C=C(H,d,p,q,m)
independent of
n,N
.
For
N>0
we define

H_{N}:=\{\varphi\in\mathcal{C}([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d})\ \text{such that}\|\psi^{n}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\gamma}}\leq N\}.

By the Arzelà–Ascoli theorem, the set
H_{N}
is compact in
\mathcal{C}([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Since by (
3.43
)

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathsf{P}(\psi^{n}\notin H_{N})=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathsf{P}(\|\psi^{n}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\gamma}}>N)\leq CN^{-m},

we see that the sequence
\{\law(\psi^{n}),\ n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is tight in
\mathcal{C}([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Note that the constant sequence
\{\law(W^{H}),n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is tight in
\mathcal{C}([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d})
because the space is Polish.
Take
\theta\in[0,\frac{1}{2})
such that
\theta>H-\frac{1}{2}
and define using
Proposition

A.1
the Brownian motion
B:=\Phi(W^{H})
.
The sequence
\{\law(B),n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is also tight in
\mathcal{C}^{\theta}([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d})
because
\theta<\frac{1}{2}
.
Combining the previous results, we see that the sequence

\{\law(\psi^{n},W^{H},B),n\in\mathbb{N}\}\ \text{is tight in}\ \mathcal{C}([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d})^{2}\times\mathcal{C}^{\theta}([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d}).

(3.44)

Step 3: convergence.

Because
\mathcal{C}([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d})^{2}\times\mathcal{C}^{\theta}([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a complete separable metric space there exists by Prokhorov’s theorem a subsequence of (
3.44
) that converges in law to a probability measure.
By Skorokhod’s representation theorem, there exists another probability space
(\widetilde{\Omega},\widetilde{\mathcal{F}},\widetilde{\mathsf{P}})
, random variables
\widetilde{\psi}
,
\widetilde{W}^{H}
,
\widetilde{B}
, and for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, random variables
\widetilde{\psi}^{n}
,
\widetilde{W}^{n,H}
,
\widetilde{B}^{n}
such that

\law(\widetilde{\psi}^{n},\widetilde{W}^{H,n},\widetilde{B}^{n})=\law(\psi^{n},W^{H},B),

(3.45)

and such that a subsequence of

(\widetilde{\psi}^{n},\widetilde{W}^{H,n},\widetilde{B}^{n})\ \text{converges a.s.
to}\ (\widetilde{\psi},\widetilde{W}^{H},\widetilde{B})\ \text{in}\ \mathcal{C}([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{2d})\times\mathcal{C}^{\theta}([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d}).

(3.46)

To avoid overburdening the proof with indices, we denote one of these subsequences again by
(\widetilde{\psi}^{n},\widetilde{W}^{H,n},\widetilde{B}^{n})
.
The limit
(\widetilde{X}:=x+\widetilde{\psi}+\widetilde{W}^{H},\widetilde{W}^{H})
is our candidate for a solution.

Step 4: Choice of a filtration and proof that
\widetilde{B}
is an
\widetilde{\mathbb{F}}
–Brownian motion.

Introduce filtrations
\widetilde{\mathbb{F}}=(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
,
\widetilde{\mathbb{F}}^{n}=(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}^{n}_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}

Distribution of rational points on toric varieties: All the heights
Arda Demirhan, Ramin Takloo-Bighash

Abstract




Abstract: In this paper we prove a formula for the number of rational points in a certain height box for a split toric variety over a number field.

1
Introduction

The purpose of this note is to provide an example where the role played by the exceptional set in Manin’s conjecture can be eliminated. In its original formulation Manin’s conjectures
[
9
,
1
]
dealt with a Fano variety over a number field and counted rational points with height relative to a metrized line bundle whose underlying line bundle was in the cone of effective divisors, and predicted an asymptotic formula for the number of rational points of bounded height on a small enough open set. One has to consider proper open sets as opposed to the whole variety because there are many examples where a proper Zariski closed subset of a variety can have more rational points than the rest of the variety. The easiest example one can construct is the blowup of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
at the origin,
[
18
]
, §2.12. As stated these conjectures are wrong
[
4
]
. This is due to the presence of dense thin sets with too many rational points. Recently, many works have explored the effects of thin sets on the distribution of rational points and their counting functions. In particular, Lehman, Sengupta, and Tanimoto
[
11
,
12
]
have formulated a deep conjecture about the distribution of rational points on any geometrically rationally connected and geometrically integral smooth projective variety with bounded height relative to a metrized line bundle which is big and nef (if
X
is Fano, it suffices to assume that the line bundle is big). In this conjecture, there is a careful geometric description of a set
Z
such that the number of rational points
x\in X(F)\setminus Z
of height less than
B
grows like

cB^{a}(\log B)^{b-1}

with
c,a,b
the expected constants,
[
13
,
6
]
, provided that
X(F)
is not thin. We refer the reader to
[
12
,
11
]
for the precise formulation of the conjecture, examples, and references to many works by other mathematicians that motivated the conjecture.

In the other direction, there have been attempts to formulate conjectures that do not require removing exceptional sets,
[
14
,
15
]
. In one approach by Peyre
[
15
, §4]
, the
all heights approach
, instead of considering a single height function, one considers the distribution of rational points relative to the height functions coming from
all
line bundles, or a family thereof. In the case where the cone of effective divisors is polyhedral, one could, for example, consider the distribution relative to the generators of the cone. Namely, suppose
X
is a Fano variety over a number field
F
and let
H_{1},\dots,H_{n}
be height functions associated to the generators of the cone of effective divisors of
X
. Fix positive real numbers
\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{n}
. For an open set
U
and
B>1
, we set

N(U,B)=\#\{x\in U(F)\mid H_{i}(x)\leq B^{\beta_{i}},1\leq i\leq n\}.

Then one might hope to prove an asymptotic formula
CB^{a}(\log B)^{b-1}
for
N(U,B)
with
a,b
independent of
U
. There are, however, families of varieties of Picard rank
1
with non-trivial exceptional sets. For some explicit constructions, see
[
12
, §10]
. This means that the
all height
approach relative to the generators of the cone of effective divisors for this family of example is not going to remove the effect of exceptional sets on the asymptotic formulae in this case, and one may have to consider larger families of line bundles. In general, it is not clear that the
all heights
approach for any family, and we would expect to need other notions, such as
freeness
, to formulate a Manin-type conjecture that does not require removing exceptional sets,
[
14
,
15
]
.

In this paper we consider the case of smooth projective toric varieties over a number field whose torus is split, the so-called
split toric varieties
, and we show that the all heights approach of Peyre works relative to the family of line bundles associated to all
T
-invariant divisors. Manin’s conjecture in its original form was proved for toric varieties by Batyrev and Tschinkel
[
2
,
3
,
5
]
using harmonic analysis on tori. Toric varieties are the perfect test ground for investigations of the sort we consider here because their geometry is well-understood, and the structure of the boundary divisors is given in terms of combinatorial data.

We now state our theorem. Let
X
be a split smooth projective toric variety defined over a number field
F
, and let
T
be the open torus in
X
. Let
\Delta_{1},\dots,\Delta_{n}
be the
T
-invariant divisors of
X
as in §
2.1.4
.

Theorem 1.1
.

With notations as above, there are metrizations of the line bundles corresponding to the divisors
\Delta_{1},\dots,\Delta_{n}
such that if
H_{1},\dots,H_{n}
are the associated height functions, then the following holds: Let
\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{n}
be fixed positive real numbers. Then there are constants
C>0
and
\epsilon>0
such that as
B\to\infty
,

\#\{x\in X(F)\mid H_{i}(x)\leq B^{\beta_{i}},1\leq i\leq n\}=CB^{\sum_{i}\beta_{i}}(1+O(B^{-\epsilon})).

(1)

This theorem is Corollary
4.3
below, stated in the notations of §
2.1.4
. The proof has two parts. In the first part we count rational points on the torus and show that Equation (
1
) holds. In the second part we show that the contribution of the boundary is less. This latter step relies on the fact that the boundary of a toric variety is a union of tori, and that we can use analogues of Equation (
1
) for these tori in an inductive fashion. In the case of toric varieties the exceptional set predicted by the conjecture of Lehman, Sengupta, and Tanimoto is contained in the boundary (Example 5.12 of
[
12
]
), and as indicated our proof shows that the boundary pieces contribute less than the open torus to the asymptotic formula. Unfortunately at present we do not know how to compute the constant
C
in Theorem
1.1
.

To count rational points on the torus, we use the methodology of Strauch and Tschinkel
[
19
]
using height zeta functions. The height zeta function considered by Strauch and Tschinkel is muti-dimensional, but they restrict the zeta function to a line and use a one dimensional Tauberian theorem. In our case, we do not restrict the zeta function to lines and apply a multi-dimensional Tauberian theorem, Theorem
4.1
. The required analytic properties needed for the Tauberian theorem are given in Proposition
3.9
.

The splitness assumption in Theorem
1.1
is made only to simplify notation, and to avoid dealing with Galois actions. In general, Equation (
1
) has the same shape, except that the sum
\sum_{i}\beta_{i}
has to be replaced by the similar sum over the Galois orbits of the boundary divisors. The proof of general case follows from the same argument and requires only minor modifications.

It might at first be surprising that there is no power of
\log
in the statement of Theorem
1.1
, given that most Manin-type results have a
\log
term. We give two heuristic reasons for this phenomenon. Consider, for example, the following situation: For a positive integer
n
, let
d(n)
be the number of divisors of
n
. Then it is a well-known result that

\sum_{n\leq X}d(n)\sim X\log X

(2)

as
X\to\infty
. Now compare this with the following trivial statement

\#\{(x,y)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\mid 1\leq x\leq X,1\leq y\leq Y\}=XY.

(3)

Dirichlet’s Hyperbola Method deduces the proof of Equation (
2
) to Equation (
3
). Theorem
1.1
is the analogue of Equation (
3
) in our context, and standard Manin type results should follow from it using general Dirichlet Hyperbola theorem type results. At present this is not possible as we do not have enough power saving in in the error term in Theorem
1.1
to implement such an idea.

Our next heuristic reasoning relies on the theory of
Universal Torsors
. For a split Fano toric variety over the field of rational numbers, there is a theory of universal torsors worked out by Salberger
[
17
]
. According to this theory in order to count rational points on a toric variety one needs to count integral points satisfying certain coprimality conditions on the universal torsor. The coordinates on the universal torsors are given, essentially, by the height functions
H_{1},\dots,H_{n}
. For that reason, the expression on the left hand side of Equation (
1
) counts integral points with certain coprimality conditions inside a box. Without the coprimality restrictions, the number of integral points in the box is approximately a constant multiple of
B
raised to
\sum_{i}\beta_{i}
. Imposing the coprimality conditions, via an analysis involving Peyre’s Möbius function
[
13
, 7.1.7]
as in
[
17
, §11]
, just modifies the constant in front of
B^{\sum_{i}\beta_{i}}
. Because of the existence of units, the theory of universal torsors is far more complicated over fields other than the rational numbers. Pieropan
[
16
]
has implemented Salberger’s program for the case of imaginary quadratic fields taking advantage of the fact that the group of units is finite. In this case too a heuristic reasoning as above recovers Equation (
1
). Our approach makes no assumption about the number field.

We conclude this introduction by formulating two conjectures. Our first conjecture, involving general height boxes, is motivated by the above heuristic reasoning using universal torsors.

Conjecture 1.2
.

Let the notation be as in Theorem
1.1
. There are metrizations of the line bundles corresponding to the divisors
\Delta_{1},\dots,\Delta_{n}
in such a way that if
H_{1},\dots,H_{n}
are the associated height functions, then the following holds: There are real numbers
C>0
and
\epsilon>0
such that for any fixed
a>1
, and any real numbers
B_{1},\dots,B_{n}>0
satisfying

\log B_{i}/\log B_{j}\in(a^{-1},a),\quad\text{ for all }i,j

and
\min_{i}B_{i}\to+\infty
we have

\#\{x\in X(F)\mid H_{i}(x)\leq B_{i},1\leq i\leq n\}=C(\prod_{i}B_{i})(1+O(\prod_{i}B_{i}^{-\epsilon})).

(4)

It would also be of interest to have a result similar to Theorem
1.1
for the generators of the cone of effective divisors. In order to state the conjecture we need a couple of notations. Let
X
,
T
, and
F
be as above. Let
\Lambda_{\rm eff}(X)\subset{\rm Pic}\,(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
be the cone of effective divisors defined in §
2.1.3
, and let
D_{1},\dots,D_{r}
be divisors whose classes form the extremal rays of the cone
\Lambda_{\rm eff}(X)
in
{\rm Pic}\,(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
. Let
K_{X}\in{\rm Pic}\,(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
be the canonical class. Write
-K_{X}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}r_{i}D_{i}
, for real numbers
r_{i}>0
. Then we have the following conjecture:

Conjecture 1.3
.

There are metrizations of the line bundles corresponding to the divisors
D_{1},\dots,D_{r}
in such a way that if
H_{D_{1}},\dots,H_{D_{r}}
are the associated height functions, then the following holds: Let
\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{r}
be fixed positive real numbers. Then there are constants
C^{\prime}>0
and
\epsilon>0
such that as
B\to\infty
,

\#\{x\in X(F)\mid H_{D_{i}}(x)\leq B^{\beta_{i}},1\leq i\leq r\}=C^{\prime}B^{\sum_{i}r_{i}\beta_{i}}(1+O(B^{-\epsilon})).

(5)

There should also be a statement similar to Conjecture
1.2
for general height boxes associated to the generators of the cone of effective divisors.

The proofs of these conjectures should follow an argument similar to that of Theorem
1.1
, in that one should first prove Formula (
5
) with
X(F)
replaced by
T(F)
, and then do induction. For Conjecture
1.2
the induction step is identical to the one in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, but the induction step for the proof of Conjecture
1.3
is rather subtle. Unfortunately, however, the Tauberian theorem we use for the proof of Theorem
1.1
, Theorem
4.1
, is not strong enough to accomplish Equation (
5
) for the torus
T
.

This paper is organized as follow. Section
2
gives the basic definitions regarding toric varieties and their associated height functions. Section
3
contains the analytic properties of the height zeta function. Section
4
has the proof of the main theorem.

Preliminiaries

The standard elementary introduction to the geometry of toric varieties is
[
10
]
. We use the notations and terminology of
[
19
]
. In order to make this paper as self-contained as possible we review the basic theory of split toric varieties and their height functions in this section.

2.1
Toric varieties

2.1.1

Let
T
be a split algebraic torus of dimension
d
over a number field
F
, and let
M=X^{*}(T),N={\rm Hom}(M,{\mathbb{Z}})
. We
let
\Sigma
be a complete regular
fan in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
, and we define
X=X_{\Sigma}
to be the smooth projective toric variety corresponding to
\Sigma
. The variety
X
is
obtained by patching together affine open sets

U_{\sigma}={\rm Spec}\,(F[M\cap\check{\sigma}])

for
\sigma\in\Sigma
, and for each
\sigma
,

\check{\sigma}=\{m\in M_{\mathbb{R}}\mid n(m)\geq 0,\forall n\in\sigma\}.

2.1.2

We define
PL(\Sigma)
be the group of functions
\varphi
on
N_{\mathbb{R}}
that satisfy the following two conditions: (i)
\varphi(N)\subset{\mathbb{Z}}
, (ii) for each
\sigma\in\Sigma
, the restriction of
\varphi
is equal to the restriction of a linear function on
N
to

\sigma
. We call
PL(\Sigma)
the group of
\Sigma
-piecewise linear integral functions on
N_{\mathbb{R}}
. For
\varphi\in PL(\Sigma)
and every
d
dimensional
\sigma\in\Sigma
, there is a unique
m_{\varphi,\sigma}\in M
such that

\varphi(n)=n(m_{\varphi,\sigma}).

For a general
\sigma\in\Sigma
, we pick a
d
dimensional
\sigma^{\prime}\in\Sigma
and we set

m_{\varphi,\sigma}=m_{\varphi,\sigma^{\prime}}.

To any
\varphi\in PL(\Sigma)
we associate a line bundle
L_{\varphi}
on
X
by setting

L_{\varphi}=\left(\coprod_{\sigma\in\Sigma}U_{\sigma}\times{\mathbb{A}}^{1}\right)/\sigma

where
(x,a)\in U_{\sigma}\times{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
and
(x^{\prime},a^{\prime})\in U_{\sigma^{\prime}}\times{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
are identified if
x=x^{\prime}\in U_{\sigma}\cap U_{\sigma^{\prime}}
and
a^{\prime}=\frac{m_{\varphi,\sigma^{\prime}}}{m_{\varphi,\sigma}}(x)\cdot a
. The class of
(x,a)
will be denoted by

[x,a]
. There is a canonical
T
action on
L_{\varphi}
given by

[x,a]\cdot t=[x\cdot t,m_{\varphi,\sigma}(t)\cdot a],

if
x\in U_{\sigma}
. This action identifies
PL(\Sigma)
with
T
-linearized line bundles on
X
. We have an exact
sequence

0\to M\to PL(\Sigma)\to{\rm Pic}\,X\to 0

(6)

where the map
\eta:PL(\Sigma)\to{\rm Pic}\,X
is given by
\varphi\mapsto L_{\varphi}
. We let
PL(\Sigma)_{\mathbb{R}}=PL(\Sigma)\otimes\mathbb{R}
,
{\rm Pic}\,(X)_{\mathbb{R}}={\rm Pic}\,(X)\otimes\mathbb{R}
. We extend
\eta
linearly to a map
\eta_{\mathbb{R}}:PL(\Sigma)_{\mathbb{R}}\to{\rm Pic}\,(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
.

2.1.3

We define
the cone of effective divisors

\Lambda_{\rm eff}(X)
to be the cone in
{\rm Pic}\,(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
generated by classes of effective divisors on
X
. Let us describe the cone
\Lambda_{\rm eff}(X)
. We define
\Sigma_{1}\subset N
to be the set of primitive integral generators of the one-dimensional cones in
\Sigma

and we set

PL(\Sigma)^{+}=\{\varphi\in PL(\Sigma)_{\mathbb{R}}\mid\varphi(e)>0,\forall e\in\Sigma_{1}\}.

It is known that

\Lambda_{\rm eff}(X)=\overline{\eta_{\mathbb{R}}(PL(\Sigma)^{+})}

(7)

where the closure is taken in
{\rm Pic}\,(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
. A fact of utmost importance is the statement that if we define
\varphi_{\Sigma}

to be the piecewise-linear function which is defined by
\varphi_{\Sigma}(e)=1
for all
e\in\Sigma_{1}
, then

L_{\varphi_{\Sigma}}
is isomorphic to the anti-canonical line bundle on
X
.

2.1.4

For each
e\in\Sigma_{1}
, let
T_{e}
be the
(d-1)
-dimensional torus orbit corresponding to the cone
{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}e\in\Sigma
. Define
D_{e}
to be the Zariski closure of
T_{e}
in
X
. Proposition 1.2.11 of
[
2
]

asserts that the images of
D_{e}
,
e\in\Sigma_{1}
, generate the cone
\Lambda_{\rm eff}(X)
. Each divisor
D_{e}
is a toric variety in its own right. Let
(N_{e})_{\mathbb{R}}=N_{\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{R}}e
, and let
\Sigma_{e}
be the cones in
\Sigma
that contain
{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}e
as a face. Then for each
\sigma\in\Sigma_{e}
, we let
\bar{\sigma}=\sigma+{\mathbb{R}}e/{\mathbb{R}}e
. The collection
\{\bar{\sigma},\sigma\in\Sigma_{e}\}
, again denoted
\Sigma_{e}
, is a fan in
(N_{e})_{{\mathbb{R}}}
. The toric variety corresponding to this fan is
D_{e}
.

2.2
Metrizations

Following
[
19
]
we introduce an adelic metric on
L_{\varphi}
. For
\sigma\in\Sigma

and
v\in{\rm Val}\,(F)
we set

{\mathbf{K}}_{\sigma,v}=\{x\in U_{\sigma}(F_{v})\mid|m(x)|_{v}\leq 1,\forall m\in\check{\sigma}\cap M\}.

If
x\in{\mathbf{K}}_{\sigma,v},a\in F_{v}
, then
[x,a]\in L_{\varphi}(F)
is
F_{v}
-rational, and we put

\|[x,a]\|_{x}=|a|_{v}.

This is well-defined. The family
\|\cdot\|_{v}=(\|\cdot\|_{x})_{x\in X(F_{v})}
is a
v
-adic metric on
L_{\varphi}
, and

\mathcal{L}_{\varphi}=(L_{\varphi},(\|\cdot\|_{v})_{v})
is a metrization of
L_{\varphi}
. Let
{\mathbf{K}}_{T,v}
be the maximal
compact subgroup. For
x\in T(F_{v})
, consider the map
M\to{\mathbb{Z}}
, for
v
non-archimedean, and
M\to{\mathbb{R}}
for
v

archimedean, given by

m\mapsto-\frac{\log(|m(x)|_{v})}{\log q_{v}}

where for
v
non-archimedean,
q_{v}
is the size of the residue field of
F_{v}
, and for
v
archimedean, we set

\log q_{v}=1
. This map provides an isomorphism
T(F_{v})/{\mathbf{K}}_{T,v}\to N
for
v
non-archimedean, and
N_{\mathbb{R}}

otherwise. Let
\bar{x}
be the image of
x\in T(F_{v})
in
N
, resp.
N_{\mathbb{R}}
for archimedean
v
. For
\varphi\in PL(\Sigma)_{\mathbb{C}}
define a function
H_{\Sigma,v}(\cdot,\varphi):T(F_{v})\to{\mathbb{C}}
by

H_{\Sigma,v}(x,\varphi)=e^{-\varphi(\bar{x})\log(q_{v})}.

The corresponding global height function
H_{\Sigma}(\cdot,\varphi):T({\mathbb{A}})\to{\mathbb{C}}
defined by

H_{\Sigma}(x,\varphi)=\prod_{v}H_{\Sigma,v}(x_{v},\varphi)

is well-defined.

2.3
\mathcal{A}_{T}
and
\mathcal{U}_{T}

Let
{\mathbf{K}}_{T}=\prod_{v}{\mathbf{K}}_{T,v}\subset T({\mathbb{A}})
. Define

\mathcal{A}_{T}=(T({\mathbb{A}})/T(F){\mathbf{K}}_{T})^{*}

to be the group of unitary characters of
T({\mathbb{A}})
which are trivial on
T(F){\mathbf{K}}_{T}
. For
m\in M
we
define the character

\chi^{m}(x)=e^{i\log(|m(x)|_{\mathbb{A}})}.

The map
m\mapsto\chi^{m}
provides us with an embedding
M_{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathcal{A}_{T}
. For any archimedean place

v
and
\chi\in\mathcal{A}_{T}
there is
m_{v}=m_{v}(\chi)\in M_{\mathbb{R}}
such that

\chi_{v}(x_{v})=e^{-i\bar{x}_{v}(m_{v})}

for
x_{v}\in T(F_{v})
. We then get a homomorphism

\mathcal{A}_{T}\to M_{{\mathbb{R}},\infty}=\oplus_{v\mid\infty}M_{\mathbb{R}},

given by sending
\chi
to the vector
m_{\infty}(\chi)=(m_{v}(\chi))_{v\mid\infty}
.
In the sequel, given a real vector space
W
, we set
W_{\infty}=\oplus_{v\mid\infty}W
.

We define
T({\mathbb{A}})^{1}
to be the common
kernel of the all maps
T({\mathbb{A}})\to{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
,
x\mapsto|m(x)|_{\mathbb{A}}
,
m\in M
. Set

\mathcal{U}_{T}=(T({\mathbb{A}})^{1}/T(F){\mathbf{K}}_{T})^{*}.

If we fix an isomorphism
T\tilde{\longrightarrow}\,\mathbb{G}_{m,F}^{d}
and a projection
\mathbb{G}_{m}({\mathbb{A}})\to\mathbb{G}_{m}({\mathbb{A}})^{1}
we obtain a splitting of the exact sequence

1\rightarrow T({\mathbb{A}})^{1}\rightarrow T({\mathbb{A}})\rightarrow T({\mathbb{A}})/T({\mathbb{A}})^{1}\to 1.

We then obtain a decomposition

\mathcal{A}_{T}=M_{\mathbb{R}}\oplus\mathcal{U}_{T}

and

M_{{\mathbb{R}},\infty}=M_{\mathbb{R}}\oplus M_{{\mathbb{R}},\infty}^{1}

(8)

with
M_{{\mathbb{R}},\infty}^{1}
being the minimal
\mathbb{R}
-subspace of
M_{{\mathbb{R}},\infty}
containing the image of
\mathcal{U}_{T}

under the map
\mathcal{A}_{T}\to M_{{\mathbb{R}},\infty}
defined above. For the remainder of this work we fix these splittings. Note
that by Dirichlet’s unit theorem, the image of
\mathcal{U}_{T}\to M_{{\mathbb{R}},\infty}^{1}
is a lattice of maximal rank.

We also fix measures as follows. For an archimedean place
v
, let
dx_{v}
be the Haar measure on
T(F_{v})

giving
{\mathbf{K}}_{T,v}
volume one. For archimedean
v
, we give
T(F_{v})/{\mathbf{K}}_{T,v}
the pullback of the Lebesque
measure on
N_{\mathbb{R}}
, giving volume
1
to
N_{{\mathbb{R}}}/N
. We then give
{\mathbf{K}}_{T,v}
the Haar measure with total
measure one. We then obtain an invariant measure on
T(F_{v})
. On
T({\mathbb{A}})
we have an adelic measure

dx=\prod_{v}dx_{v}
.

3
Height zeta function

In this section we establish the basic properties of the height zeta function.

3.1
Integrals of height functions

For a character
\chi:T(F_{v})\to S^{1}
define

\hat{H}_{\Sigma,v}(\chi,\varphi)=\int_{T(F_{v})}H_{\Sigma,v}(x_{v},\varphi)\chi(x_{v})dx_{v}.

Suppose the integral is convergent. Then, as
H
is invariant under
{\mathbf{K}}_{T,v}
, the value of the integral is zero unless
\chi
is trivial on
{\mathbf{K}}_{T,v}
. These integrals converge absolutely for
\varphi\in PL(\Sigma)^{+}
.

Let
v
be an archimedean place of
F
. Any
d
dimensional cone
\sigma\in\Sigma
is simplicial, generated by

\Sigma_{1}\cap\sigma
. Let
\chi
be unramified. This means
\chi(x)=e^{-\bar{x}(m)}
for some
m\in M_{\mathbb{R}}
.
Then

\hat{H}_{\Sigma,v}(\chi,\varphi)=\sum_{\dim\sigma=d}\prod_{e\in\sigma\cap\Sigma_{1}}\frac{1}{\varphi(e)+ie(m)}.

We also compute the integral at the non-archimedean places. We have a set of variables
\underline{u}=\{u_{e}\}_{e\in\Sigma_{1}}
. Set
for each
\sigma\in\Sigma

R_{\sigma}((u_{e})_{e})=\prod_{e\in\sigma\cap\Sigma_{1}}\frac{u_{e}}{1-u_{e}},

and define

R_{\Sigma}((u_{e})_{e})=\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma}R_{\sigma}((u_{e})_{e}),

and

Q_{\Sigma}((u_{e})_{e})=R_{\Sigma}((u_{e})_{e})\prod_{e\in\Sigma_{1}}(1-u_{e}).

Then if
\chi
is an unramified unitary character of
T(F_{v})
and if
\Re\phi\in PL(\Sigma)^{+}
, then

\hat{H}_{\Sigma,v}(\chi,\varphi)=Q_{\Sigma}((\chi(e)q_{v}^{-\varphi(e)})\prod_{e\in\Sigma_{1}}(1-\chi(e)q_{v}^{-\varphi(e)})^{-1}.

Any
e\in\Sigma_{1}
defines a homomorphism
F[M]\to F[{\mathbb{Z}}]
, and by duality, a morphism of algebraic tori

\mathbb{G}_{m}\to T
. For any character
\chi\in\mathcal{A}_{T}
, we let
\chi_{e}
be the Hecke character

{\mathbb{G}}({\mathbb{A}})\to T({\mathbb{A}})\rightarrow S^{1}.

Let

L_{f}(\chi_{e},s)=\prod_{v\nmid\infty}(1-\chi_{e}(\pi_{v})q_{v}^{-s})^{-1}.

The product converges for
\Re s>1
. A consequence of this is that the global Fourier transform

\hat{H}_{\Sigma}(\chi.\varphi)=\int_{T({\mathbb{A}})}H_{\Sigma}(x,\varphi)\chi(x)\,dx

converges absolutely if
\Re\varphi\in\varphi_{\Sigma}+PL(\Sigma)^{+}
.

3.2
An inequality

For
\chi\in\mathcal{A}_{T}

4.1
A theorem of de la Bretèche

In this section we recall the following theorem of de la Bretèche.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{S}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{S}_{m}
be discrete strictly increasing sequences of real numbers larger than or equal to
1
.
Suppose
f
is a positive function defined on
\mathcal{S}_{1}\times\cdots\times\mathcal{S}_{r}
, and let
F
be the associated multiple
Dirichlet series

F(\mathbf{s})=\sum_{d_{1}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}}\cdots\sum_{d_{r}\in\mathcal{S}_{r}}\frac{f(d_{1},\dots,d_{r})}{d_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots d_{r}^{s_{r}}}.

Suppose there is a vector
\mathbf{\alpha}\in(0,+\infty)^{r}
such that
F
satisfies the following conditions:

(P1)

The series
F(\mathbf{s})
is absolutely convergent for
\mathbf{s}
such that
\Re s_{j}>\alpha_{j}
.

(P2)

There are
n
linear forms
\mathcal{L}=\{\ell_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
on
{\mathbb{C}}^{r}
with the property that
\ell_{i}(e_{j})\in[0,+\infty)
such that the function
H
on
{\mathbb{C}}^{r}
defined by
H(\mathbf{s})=F(\mathbf{s}+\mathbf{\alpha})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\ell_{i}(\mathbf{s})
has an analytic continuation to a holomorphic function on the domain
\mathcal{D}(\delta_{1})=\{\mathbf{s}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{r}\mid\Re\ell_{i}(\mathbf{s})>-\delta_{1}\}
with
\delta_{1}>0
.

(P3)

There is
\delta_{2}>0
such that for each
\epsilon,\epsilon^{\prime}>0
, we have

|H(\mathbf{s})|\ll\prod_{i=1}^{n}(|\Im\ell_{i}(\mathbf{s})|+1)^{1-\delta_{2}\min(0,\Re\ell_{i}(\mathbf{s}))}(1+\|\Im\mathbf{s}\|_{1}^{\epsilon})

uniformly on the domain
\mathcal{D}(\delta_{1}-\epsilon^{\prime})
. Here for an
r
-tuple of real numbers
\mathbf{\tau}=(\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{r})
, we have set
\|\mathbf{\tau}\|_{1}=\sum_{i}|\tau_{i}|
.

Suppose
\mathbf{\beta}=(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{r})\in(0,+\infty)^{r}
. Then there is a polynomial
Q=Q_{\mathbf{\beta}}
with real coefficients of degree less than or equal to
n-\mathrm{rank}\,\mathcal{L}
and a positive real number
\vartheta
, depending on
\mathcal{L}
,
\delta_{1}
,
\delta_{2}
,
\mathbf{\alpha}
, and
\mathbf{\beta}
such that for
B\geq 1
we have

S_{\mathbf{\beta}}(B):=\sum_{1\leq d_{1}\leq B^{\beta_{1}}}\cdots\sum_{1\leq d_{r}\leq B^{\beta_{r}}}f(d_{1},\dots,d_{r})

=B^{\sigma}Q(\log B)+O(B^{\sigma-\vartheta}),

with
\sigma=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\alpha_{j}\beta_{j}
.

De la Bretèche
[
7
,
8
]
states this theorem only for
\mathcal{S}_{i}={\mathbb{N}}
,
1\leq i\leq r
, but the same argument works for the more general theorem stated here.

4.2
Application

For
e\in\Sigma_{1}
, we let
D_{e}
be the corresponding
T
-invariant divisor. Define a
\varphi_{e}\in PL(\Sigma)
by setting
\varphi_{e}(f)=\delta_{ef}
,
Kronecker’s delta, for

. The line bundle corresponding to

is

. We denote the metrized line bunde

by

. We choose the metrization so that for

,

H_{\mathcal{L}(e)}(x)=H_{\Sigma}(x,\varphi_{e})^{-1}.

Theorem 4.2
.

Fix positive real numbers
\beta_{e}
, for each

. For a positive real number
B>1
, set

N_{T}(B)=\#\{x\in T(F)\mid 1\leq H_{\mathcal{L}(e)}(x)\leq B^{\beta_{e}},\forall e\in\Sigma_{1}\}.

There is a constant
C>0
and

such that

N_{T}(B)=CB^{\sum_{e\in\Sigma_{1}}\beta_{e}}(1+O(B^{-\epsilon})

as
B\to\infty
.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.1
we need to understand the analytic properties of the zeta function

Z((s_{e})_{e\in\Sigma_{1}})=\sum_{x\in T(F)}\frac{1}{\prod_{e\in\Sigma_{1}}H_{\mathcal{L}(e)}(x)^{s_{e}}}.

We have

\prod_{e\in\Sigma_{1}}H_{\mathcal{L}(e)}(x)^{s_{e}}=\prod_{e\in\Sigma_{1}}H_{\Sigma}(x,\varphi_{e})^{-s_{e}}=H_{\Sigma}(x,\sum_{e\in\Sigma_{1}}s_{e}\varphi_{e})^{-1}.

As a result,

Z((s_{e})_{e\in\Sigma_{1}})=Z_{\Sigma}(\varphi)

with
\varphi=\sum_{e\in\Sigma_{1}}s_{e}\varphi_{e}
. That the conditions required by Theorem
4.1
are satisfied is the content of Proposition
3.9
. The only fact that requires verification is the statement that, in the notations of Theorem
4.1
,
\deg Q=0
. By Theorem
4.1
,
\deg Q=n-\mathrm{rank}\,\mathcal{L}
, but by Proposition
3.9
our linear forms are linearly independent and as a result
n=\mathrm{rank}\,\mathcal{L}
.
∎

Corollary 4.3
.

Fix positive real numbers
\beta_{e}
,
e\in\Sigma_{1}
. For a positive real number
B>1
, set

N_{X}(B)=\#\{x\in X(F)\mid 1\leq H_{\mathcal{L}(e)}(x)\leq B^{\beta_{e}},e\in\Sigma_{1}\}.

There is a constant
C>0
and
\epsilon^{\prime}>0
such that

N_{X}(B)=CB^{\sum_{e\in\Sigma_{1}}\beta_{e}}(1+O(B^{-\epsilon^{\prime}})

as

Proof.

We will do induction on

\beta_{e}
,

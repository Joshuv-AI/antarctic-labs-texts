A study of dissipative models based on Dirac matrices
Jyotsna Gidugu, Daniel P. Arovas

Abstract
We generalize the recent work of Shibata and Katsura Shibata2019 , who considered a S = 1 2 S={\textstyle{1\over 2}} chain with alternating X ​ X XX and Y ​ Y YY couplings in the presence of dephasing, the dynamics of which are described by the GKLS master equation. Their model is equivalent to a non-Hermitian system described by the Kitaev formulation Kitaev2006 in terms of a single Majorana species hopping on a two-leg ladder in the presence of a nondynamical Z 2 {\mathbb Z}^{\vphantom{*}}_{2} gauge field. Our generalization involves Dirac gamma matrix ‘spin’ operators on the square lattice, and maps onto a non-Hermitian square lattice bilayer which is also Kitaev-solvable. We describe the exponentially many non-equilibrium steady states in this model. We identify how the spin degrees of freedom can be accounted for in the 2d model in terms of the gauge-invariant quantities and then proceed to study the Liouvillian spectrum. We use a genetic algorithm to estimate the Liouvillian gap and the first decay modes for large system sizes. We observe a transition in the first decay modes, similar to that in ref. Shibata2019 . The results we obtain are consistent with a perturbative analysis for small and large values of the dissipation strength.

I
Introduction

Open quantum systems afford us the opportunity to study phenomena such as relaxational quantum dynamics
for systems coupled to a bath
Breuer2007
. Typically this involves ‘integrating out’ or eliminating
in some way the bath degrees of freedom, resulting in a dynamics for the system itself in terms of its reduced density matrix:
{\dot{\varrho}}={\cal L}\varrho
, where
{\cal L}
is the Liouvillian operator. At long times, the system
relaxes to a non-equilibrium steady state (NESS); the existence of a NESS is guaranteed by the dynamics,
but under special circumstances owing to, for example, extra conserved quantities, the NESS may not be unique.

For noninteracting systems, hybridization with the bath degrees of freedom still results in a solvable
(quadratic) model
Prosen2008
. For interacting systems, solvable models are rare, and numerical
approaches are challenging. This is especially true for density matrix evolution since one must keep track
of not just populations
{|\alpha\rangle}{\langle\alpha|}
but also the coherences
{|\alpha\rangle}{\langle\beta|}
with

\alpha\neq\beta
, effectively squaring the size of the problem
vis-a-vis
the system’s Hilbert
space dimension.

Recently, Shibata and Katsura
Shibata2019
(SK) described a model of open system dynamics based
on the GKLS master equation
ChruPa2017
which, though interacting, is solvable in the sense of
Kitaev’s celebrated honeycomb lattice Hamiltonian model
Kitaev2006
. That is to say, the evolution
of
\varrho(t)
under the Liouvillian
{\cal L}
is effectively described by a non-interacting dynamics in the
presence of a static
{\mathbb Z}^{\vphantom{*}}_{2}
gauge field. While in each gauge sector the evolution is described by
a quadratic, albeit non-Hermitian, Hamiltonian, there are exponentially many gauge sectors to evaluate
(which in general have no discrete space group symmetries), and in this sense the general problem is
intractable. For the Hermitian Kitaev model, oftentimes the ground state may be ascertained with help
from a remarkable theorem by Lieb
Lieb1989
, which provides valuable information regarding much of
the gauge-invariant content of the ground state,
i.e.
the
{\mathbb Z}^{\vphantom{*}}_{2}
plaquette fluxes. For the non-Hermitian
case, however, we know of no generalization of Lieb’s theorem which constrains the gauge-invariant content
of, say, the longest-lived decaying density matrix. Thus, in general one must resort to numerics if one is
interested in the complex spectrum of
{\cal L}
.

The Shibata-Katsura construction involves a
S={\textstyle{1\over 2}}
chain where each site is coupled to an environmental
bath. Within the GKLS formalism, this results in an effective two leg ladder system, where one leg
corresponds to the bra states and the other to the ket states of the density matrix, and the rungs of the
ladder contribute non-Hermitian terms which result from the effective elimination of the bath degrees of
freedom. The ladder is thus three-fold coordinated, and the model is constructed so that it satisfies
the Kitaev solvability criteria §
II.3
). Our main goal is to introduce and analyze a generalization
of the SK model to two space dimensions, based on a
4\times 4
gamma matrix generalization of the Hamiltonian
Kitaev model
YZK09
;
WAH09
. As the dissipative SK model is described by non-Hermitian Hamiltonian
evolution on the ladder, our model is described by such an evolution on a square lattice bilayer.
As we shall see, while our model is a direct analog of SK in some sense,
it also entails some important differences – in particular, an extensive number of conserved quantities
leading to exponentially many NESSes.

We first discuss various preliminaries, including the GLKS master equation, its vectorization and
description in terms of non-Hermitian Hamiltonian evolution on a product Hilbert space, the Shibata-Katsura
model, gamma matrix generalizations of the Kitaev honeycomb model, and finally our extension of SK to
a dissipative square lattice model involving
4\times 4
Dirac matrix ‘spin’ operators.

While this paper was in the final stages of preparation, two analyses of

Dai23
;
Scheurer23
.

II
Preliminaries

II.1
The GKLS master equation

An open quantum system
S
is one which unitarily co-evolves with an
environment
E
under a Hamiltonian
H=H^{\vphantom{*}}_{\textsf{S}}+H^{\vphantom{*}}_{\textsf{E}}+H^{\vphantom{*}}_{\rm int}
,
where
H^{\vphantom{*}}_{\rm int}
couples
S
and
E
.
The expectation of any operator
{\cal O}
restricted to
S
is given by

\langle{\cal O}(t)\rangle=\mathop{\textsf{Tr}}\big(\varrho^{\vphantom{*}}_{S}(t)\,{\cal O}\big)
, where
\varrho^{\vphantom{*}}_{\textsf{S}}(t)
is
the time-dependent reduced density matrix of
S
,
i.e.

\varrho^{\vphantom{*}}_{\textsf{S}}(t)=\mathop{\textsf{Tr}}^{\vphantom{*}}_{\textsf{E}}\,\varrho^{\vphantom{*}}_{\textsf{U}}(t)
, where
\varrho^{\vphantom{*}}_{\textsf{U}}(t)
is the full density matrix describing the ‘universe’
{\textsf{U}}={\textsf{S}}\cup{\textsf{E}}
. Under certain
assumptions, the dynamics of the system’s reduced density matrix is described
by the GKLS master equation
ChruPa2017
;
Breuer2007
,

{d\varrho\over dt}=-i\big[H,\varrho\big]+\sum_{a}\!\Big(L^{\vphantom{\dagger}}_{a}\,\varrho\,L^{\dagger}_{a}-{\textstyle{1\over 2}}L^{\dagger}_{a}L^{\vphantom{\dagger}}_{a}\,\varrho-{\textstyle{1\over 2}}\varrho\,L^{\dagger}_{a}L^{\vphantom{\dagger}}_{a}\Big)\quad,

(1)

Here and henceforth we drop the subscript
S
on
\varrho^{\vphantom{*}}_{\textsf{S}}
. The

\{L^{\vphantom{*}}_{a}\}
are the Lindblad jump operators, which describe the effects of
the system-environment coupling on
\varrho
after the environment is traced out.

H
is the ‘Lamb shift Hamiltonian’, which commutes with
H^{\vphantom{*}}_{\textsf{S}}
and includes
renormalizations of the system’s unperturbed energy levels resulting from the
environmental couplings. In the absence of all such couplings, we recover
the usual Liouville evolution
{\dot{\varrho}}=-i[H^{\vphantom{*}}_{\textsf{S}},\varrho]
.

The full GKLS evolution in eqn.
1
is of the form
{\dot{\varrho}}={\cal L}\varrho
.
Assuming
{\cal L}
is time-independent, one may formally write

\varrho(t)=\exp({\cal L}t)\varrho(0)
, which defines for each
t
a map
\Phi^{\vphantom{*}}_{t}\colon\varrho(0)\mapsto\varrho(t)
which possesses the following salient properties:
(i) linearity, (ii) trace-preserving, (iii) Hermiticity preserving, and
(iv) complete positivity
Breuer2007
. Writing
\varrho(t)=\sum_{j,k}\varrho^{\vphantom{*}}_{jk}(t)\,{|j\rangle}{\langle k|}
in terms of basis states, we may write

{\dot{\varrho}}^{\vphantom{*}}_{jk}={\cal L}^{\vphantom{*}}_{jk,lm}\,\varrho^{\vphantom{*}}_{lm}
, where
{\cal L}^{\vphantom{*}}_{jk,lm}

is a supermatrix of dimension
N^{2}
, where
N
is the dimension of the basis
and
(jk)/(lm)
are composite indices. Generically
{\cal L}
is not a normal matrix,

i.e.

[{\cal L},{\cal L}^{\dagger}]\neq 0
, and its eigenvalues
\Lambda^{\vphantom{*}}_{a}
may be complex.
However, since the evolution is trace-preserving, one has that
\delta^{\vphantom{*}}_{jk}
is a
left-eigenvector of
{\cal L}
with eigenvalue zero. The corresponding right
eigenvector is the NESS,
\varrho^{\scriptscriptstyle\textsf{NESS}}_{lm}
.
Under special circumstances there may be more than one NESS
Albert2018
.
Positivity entails that
\textsf{Re}\,\Lambda^{\vphantom{*}}_{a}\leq 0
for each eigenvalue of the
Liouvillian
{\cal L}
.

When each jump operator is Hermitian, then from eqn.
1
we have that the
infinite temperature state
\varrho\propto\mathbb{1}
is a valid NESS. Furthermore, if

H
as well as all the jump operators commute with a set of independent projectors

\{{\rm P}^{\vphantom{*}}_{s}\}
with
s\in\{1,\ldots,K\}
, then any density matrix of the form

\varrho=c^{\vphantom{*}}_{0}\,\mathbb{1}+\sum_{s=1}^{K}c^{\vphantom{*}}_{s}\,{\rm P}^{\vphantom{*}}_{s}

(2)

is also a valid NESS. This shall be the case for the model we investigate below.
Thus we shall describe a system where there is relaxation to a degenerate block
of NESSes. While such solutions to GKLS depend on the form of
H
and the jump operators

\{L^{\vphantom{*}}_{a}\}
, they are independent of the various coupling constants (so long as they
remain finite), and we shall consider them all to be infinite temperature states.

II.2
Equivalent non-Hermitian Hamiltonian

Any density matrix
\varrho=\sum_{m,n}\varrho^{\vphantom{*}}_{mn}\,{|\,m\,\rangle}\,{\langle\,n\,|}
may be represented in
vector form as

\varrho\longrightarrow{|\,\varrho\,\rangle}\equiv\sum_{m,n}\varrho^{\vphantom{*}}_{mn}\,{|\,m\,\rangle}\otimes{|\,n\,\rangle}\quad.

(3)

Thus, the bra vector
{\langle\,n\,|}
is replaced by the corresponding ket vector
{|\,n\,\rangle}
,
i.e.

{|\,m\,\rangle}{\langle\,n\,|}\to{|\,m\,\rangle}\otimes{|\,n\,\rangle}
.
If
B
is any operator, then under vectorization we have

\begin{split}{\langle\,n\,|}\,B&=\sum_{k}{\langle\,n\,|\,B\,|\,k\,\rangle}{\langle\,k\,|}\\
&\longrightarrow\sum_{k}{|\,k\,\rangle}{\langle\,k\,|\,B^{\scriptscriptstyle\textsf{T}}\,|\,n\,\rangle}=B^{\scriptscriptstyle\textsf{T}}\,{|\,n\,\rangle}\quad.\end{split}

(4)

The GKLS master equation eqn.
1
then takes the vectorized form

i\,{d\over dt}\,{|\,\varrho\,\rangle}={\cal W}\,{|\,\varrho\,\rangle}\quad,

(5)

where
Dzhioev_2012

\displaystyle{\cal W}

\displaystyle=H\otimes\mathbb{1}-\mathbb{1}\otimes H^{\scriptscriptstyle\textsf{T}}+

(6)

\displaystyle\quad i\sum_{r}\Big(L^{\vphantom{*}}_{r}\otimes L^{*}_{r}-{\textstyle{1\over 2}}\,L^{\dagger}_{r}\,L^{\vphantom{*}}_{r}\otimes\mathbb{1}-\mathbb{1}\otimes{\textstyle{1\over 2}}\,L^{\scriptscriptstyle\textsf{T}}_{r}\,L^{*}_{r}\Big)\quad.

Note that operators
{\cal O}
acting on the
{|\,n\,\rangle}
component of the product
{|\,m\,\rangle}\otimes{|\,n\,\rangle}
appear as transposes
{\cal O}^{\scriptscriptstyle\textsf{T}}
,
since they would normally act to the left on
{\langle\,n\,|}
. Eqn.
5
takes the form of an effective Schrödinger equation, with

{|\,\varrho(t)\,\rangle}
evolving according to the non-Hermitian effective Hamiltonian
{\cal W}
acting on a doubled Hilbert space.
For any operator
{\cal O}
, we may compute the trace in the vectorized representation according to

\mathop{\textsf{Tr}}({\cal O}\varrho)={\langle\,{\rm I}\,|\,{\cal O}\otimes\mathbb{1}\,|\,\varrho\,\rangle}\quad,

(7)

where
{\langle\,{\rm I}\,|}=\sum_{n}{\langle\,n\,|}\otimes{\langle\,n\,|}
. The eigenvalues of
{\cal W}
, which
we denote by
\{E^{\vphantom{*}}_{a}\}
, are related to those of the Liouvillian
by
E^{\vphantom{*}}_{a}=i\Lambda^{\vphantom{*}}_{a}
.

Shibata-Katsura Model

The Shibata-Katsura (SK) model
Shibata2019
describes a dissipative
S={\textstyle{1\over 2}}
chain. The Hamiltonian is

(8)

and the jump operators are
L^{\vphantom{*}}_{n}=\sqrt{\gamma}\,Z^{\vphantom{*}}_{n}
, with
\gamma>0
. Thus, we have

\displaystyle{\cal W}(\gamma)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{N^{\vphantom{*}}_{\rm c}}\Big(J^{\vphantom{*}}_{x}\,X^{\vphantom{*}}_{2n-1}X^{\vphantom{*}}_{2n}+J^{\vphantom{*}}_{y}\,Y^{\vphantom{*}}_{2n}Y^{\vphantom{*}}_{2n+1}-J^{\vphantom{*}}_{x}\,{\widetilde{X}}^{\vphantom{*}}_{2n-1}{\widetilde{X}}^{\vphantom{*}}_{2n}

\displaystyle\hskip 21.68121pt-J^{\vphantom{*}}_{y}\,{\widetilde{Y}}^{\vphantom{*}}_{2n}{\widetilde{Y}}^{\vphantom{*}}_{2n+1}\Big)+i\gamma\sum_{j=1}^{N}\Big(Z^{\vphantom{*}}_{j}{\widetilde{Z}}^{\vphantom{*}}_{j}-1\Big)\quad,

(9)

where the
(X,Y,Z)
operators act on the first Hilbert space and

The system is depicted in Fig.

Note that

, and that if we define

as the reflection operator mapping one leg into the other,

(X^{\vphantom{*}}_{j},Y^{\vphantom{*}}_{j},Z^{\vphantom{*}}_{j})\leftrightarrow({\widetilde{X}}^{\vphantom{*}}_{j},{\widetilde{Y}}^{\vphantom{*}}_{j},{\widetilde{Z}}^{\vphantom{*}}_{j})

This establishes that the eigenvalues of

Total positivity requires that
\textsf{Im}\,(\Gamma^{\vphantom{*}}_{a})\leq 0

III.1
Gamma matrix Kitaev models

A Clifford algebra is defined by the anticommutation relations,

\big\{\Gamma^{a}\,,\,\Gamma^{b}\big\}=2\delta^{ab}\qquad\mu,\nu\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\quad.

(15)

When
n=2k
, a representation of the algebra can be constructed by tensor products of
k
Pauli matrices,
viz.

\displaystyle\Gamma^{1}

\displaystyle=X\otimes\mathbb{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbb{1}

\displaystyle\Gamma^{2k-1}

\displaystyle=Z\otimes Z\otimes\cdots\otimes X

\displaystyle\Gamma^{2}

\displaystyle=Y\otimes\mathbb{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbb{1}

\displaystyle\Gamma^{2k}

\displaystyle=Z\otimes Z\otimes\cdots\otimes Y

(16)

\displaystyle\Gamma^{3}

\displaystyle=Z\otimes X\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbb{1}

\displaystyle\Gamma^{2k+1}

\displaystyle=Z\otimes Z\otimes\cdots\otimes Z

The gamma matrices defined above are all Hermitian. In even dimensions, we define

\Gamma^{2k+1}=(-i)^{k}\,\Gamma^{1}\,\Gamma^{2}\cdots\Gamma^{2k}\quad.

(17)

Introducing
2k+2
Majorana fermions
\theta^{a}
with indices
a\in\{0,\ldots,2k+1\}
satisfying
\big\{\theta^{a},\theta^{b}\big\}=2\delta^{ab}
, we
define
\Gamma^{\mu}=i\theta^{0}\theta^{\mu}
with
\mu>0
. Analogous to the constraint
\theta^{0}\theta^{1}\theta^{2}\theta^{3}=1

when
k=1
, we demand

\theta^{0}\,\theta^{1}\cdots\theta^{2k+1}=i^{k-1}\quad.

(18)

The case
k=1
yields the
2\times 2
Pauli matrices, with
\Gamma^{1}=X
,
\Gamma^{2}=Y
, and

\Gamma^{3}=-i\,\Gamma^{1}\Gamma^{2}=Z
. The case
k=2
yields the
4\times 4
Dirac matrices, with

\Gamma^{5}=-\Gamma^{1}\Gamma^{2}\Gamma^{3}\Gamma^{4}
. For general
k
this yields
2k+1
matrices of rank
2^{k}
.
One can then form
\Gamma^{\mu\nu}=i\Gamma^{\mu}\Gamma^{\nu}=i\theta^{\mu}\theta^{\nu}
of which there are
{2k+1\choose 2}
independent
representatives (take
\mu<\nu
), and next
\Gamma^{\mu\nu\rho}=-i\Gamma^{\mu}\Gamma^{\nu}\Gamma^{\rho}=\theta^{0}\theta^{\mu}\theta^{\nu}\theta^{\rho}
and
\Gamma^{\mu}\Gamma^{\nu}\Gamma^{\rho}\Gamma^{\sigma}=\theta^{\mu}\theta^{\nu}\theta^{\rho}\theta^{\sigma}
, which yield

{2k+1\choose 3}
and
{2k+1\choose 4}
independent terms, respectively,
etc.
Proceeding thusly one obtains at level

a basis of
4^{k}
Hermitian matrices of rank
2^{k}
.

Analogs of Kitaev’s honeycomb lattice model using these higher level Clifford algebras have been considered,
inter alia
in refs.
YZK09
;
WAH09
, with
interactions
\Gamma^{\mu}_{i}\Gamma^{\mu}_{j}
along the links. When the underlying
lattice is such that each site lies at the confluence of
2k+1
distinctly labeled

\mu
–links, the ‘spin’ Hamiltonian is again expressible as a single
species (
\theta^{0}
) Majorana fermion hopping in the presence of a static
{\mathbb Z}^{\vphantom{*}}_{2}
gauge field. Other generalizations, in which multiple
species of Majoranas hop in the same
{\mathbb Z}^{\vphantom{*}}_{2}
static gauge field and hybridize as well have also been constructed

;
CYF11
.

V
Conclusions

In this paper we have described a two-dimensional square lattice model of interacting
gamma matrix ‘spins’ coupled to a dissipative environment. The density matrix
evolution is described by the GKLS master equation
{\dot{\varrho}}={\cal L}\varrho
, where
{\cal L}

is the Liouvillian, which in general has complex eigenvalues
\Lambda^{\vphantom{*}}_{a}
.
This description is equivalent to Schrö dinger evolution under a non-Hermitian
Hamiltonian
{\cal W}
on a square lattice bilayer, whose eigenvalues are
E^{\vphantom{*}}_{a}=i\Lambda^{\vphantom{*}}_{a}
. Our model is inspired by, and a generalization of, the dissipative one-dimensional Pauli matrix spin model of Shibata and Katsura

Shibata2019
. It is in the ‘solvable’ class of models exemplified by Kitaev’s
celebrated honeycomb lattice model
Kitaev2006
, equivalent to a single species
of Majorana fermion hopping in a nondynamical
{\mathbb Z}^{\vphantom{*}}_{2}
background gauge field.
It is solvable in the sense that for any given configuration of the gauge-invariant
plaquette fluxes and Wilson phases, the non-Hermitian Hamiltonian
{\cal W}
is quadratic
and solvable by Prosen’s method
Prosen2008
. However, there are exponentially
many such configurations, and when the gauge field structure is not translationally
invariant, the Hamiltonian must be diagonalized numerically. Furthermore, there is
no analog of Lieb’s theorem
Lieb1989
to assist us in identifying the longest
lived decaying eigenmodes.

In the infinite time limit, the system approaches one of an exponentially large number
of nonequilibrium steady states, with a spectrum
\{-\textsf{Im}\,E^{\vphantom{*}}_{a}\}
of relaxation
rates. The minimum relaxation rate
g(\gamma)
is typically achieved for different

{\mathbb Z}^{\vphantom{*}}_{2}
flux configurations in the small and large
\gamma
limits, a feature also
observed by Shibata and Katsura.

We have not indicated in our plots the spectrum
\{\textsf{Re}\,E^{\vphantom{*}}_{a}\}
of the
real parts of the eigenvalues of
{\cal W}
. This is because in almost all cases
studied we have found the imaginary parts of the first decay mode eigenvalues to
be zero. The only exception we observed was in the
6\times 6
case with all

J
couplings equal and for
\gamma<\gamma_{\rm c}
, as shown in
fig.
20

3
3

3

Modes with equal and opposite nonzero values of
E^{\vphantom{*}}_{a}

are degenerate. We cannot rule out the possibility that there are degenerate lowest
decay modes for the
6\times 6
lattice with uniform
J^{\vphantom{*}}_{\delta}
with

\textsf{Re}\,E^{\vphantom{*}}_{a}=0
.
(When all
J
’s are different, we find
\textsf{Re}\,E^{\vphantom{*}}_{a}=0
for the lowest decay modes, for all
\gamma
and all sizes.)

Our model can further be generalized to other lattices. The Kitaev solvability of the
SK model is associated with the fact that their model is equivalent to non-Hermitian
Hamiltonian evolution on a two leg ladder, where each site lies at the confluence of
three distinct classes of links. For the dimension
k
Clifford algebra, we have

2k+1
gamma matrices of dimension
2^{k}
, and a Kitaev Hamiltonian (Hermitian or not)
can be constructed on any lattice where each site lies at the confluence of
(2k+1)

distinct classes of links
WAH09
. Thus, for
k=2
, our square lattice bilayer
is five-fold coordinated. A corresponding model could thus be constructed on
the kagome lattice, leading to a non-Hermitian Dirac matrix Hamiltonian
{\cal W}

on the kagome bilayer. (Further generalizations of this construction can result
in multiple species of hopping and hybridizing Majoranas in the presence of a
background nondynamical gauge field, as in refs.
YZK09
;
CYF11
.) Thus,
proceeding to
k=3
with its seven
8\times 8
gamma matrices, a corresponding model
can be constructed on a cubic lattice (bipartite NaCl structure) with

\Gamma^{\delta}_{\boldsymbol{R}}\,\Gamma^{\delta}_{{\boldsymbol{R}}+{\boldsymbol{\delta}}}
interactions on each class
\delta
link with
\delta\in\{1,\ldots,6\}
and Lindblad jump operators

\sqrt{\gamma}\,\Gamma^{7}_{\boldsymbol{r}}
at each site. Again, there will be an
exponentially large block of NESS density matrices owing to the conserved
plaquette fluxes.

Perturbation Theory for Large and Small values of
\gamma

Can
et al.

[
18

considered a model of a random Hermitian Hamiltonian

H
in the presence of a single Hermitian jump operator
\sqrt{\gamma}\,L
,
with both
H
and
L

They showed how the spectrum of relaxation times could be computed perturbatively
in the small and large
\gamma

{\cal L}^{\vphantom{*}}_{D}={\textstyle{1\over 2}}\gamma\big[\,L{\,,\,}[\,\bullet{\,,\,}L\,]\,\big]

{\cal L}^{\vphantom{*}}_{H}=-i\,[\,H{\,,\,}\bullet\,]

Thus, at small
\gamma
, the smallest nonzero relaxation rate
\tau^{-1}

is proportional to
\gamma
, while at large
\gamma
it is proportional to

\gamma^{-1}
. In this appendix we perform a related analysis for our model.

A.1
\gamma\ll 1

For
\gamma\ll 1
we write the GKLS equation as a classical master equation
for the projectors
{\rm P}^{\vphantom{*}}_{\alpha}={|\,\alpha\,\rangle}{\langle\,\alpha\,|}
, where
\alpha
is
an eigenstate of the Hamiltonian
H

[
18
]

is written

{d\,{\rm P}^{\vphantom{*}}_{\alpha}\over dt}=\gamma{\cal M}^{\vphantom{*}}_{\alpha\beta}\,{\rm P}^{\vphantom{*}}_{\beta}\quad,

(42)

with

{\cal M}^{\vphantom{*}}_{\alpha\beta}=\sum_{\boldsymbol{r}}\Big(\big|{\langle\,\alpha\,|\,\Gamma^{5}_{\boldsymbol{r}}\,|\,\beta\,\rangle}\big|^{2}-\delta^{\vphantom{*}}_{\alpha\beta}\Big)\quad.

(43)

\gamma

by starting with different randomly chosen

g

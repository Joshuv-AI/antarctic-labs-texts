Fourier–Mukai transforms and the decomposition theorem for integrable systems
Davesh Maulik, Junliang Shen, Qizheng Yin

Abstract
. We study the interplay between the Fourier–Mukai transform and the decomposition theorem for an integrable system π : M → B \pi:M\rightarrow B . Our main conjecture is that the Fourier–Mukai transform of sheaves of Kähler differentials, after restriction to the formal neighborhood of the zero section, are quantized by the Hodge modules arising in the decomposition theorem for π \pi . For an integrable system, our formulation unifies the Fourier–Mukai calculation of the structure sheaf by Arinkin–Fedorov, the theorem of the higher direct images by Matsushita, and the “perverse = Hodge” identity by the second and the third authors. As evidence, we show that these Fourier–Mukai images are Cohen–Macaulay sheaves with middle-dimensional support on the relative Picard space, with support governed by the higher discriminants of the integrable system. We also prove the conjecture for smooth integrable systems and certain 2-dimensional families with nodal singular fibers. Finally, we sketch the proof when cuspidal fibers appear.

0.1.
A Fourier–Mukai/Decomposition correspondence

The purpose of this paper is to formulate and explore a correspondence between two geometric structures associated with an integrable system — the Fourier–Mukai transform
[
26
]

[

Roughly speaking, a geometric model of an (algebraically completely) integrable system is a Lagrangian fibration

(1)

[
26
]

(2)

\phi_{\mathrm{FM}}(\Omega_{M}^{k})\in D^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(\check{M});

when

Lagrangian Cohen–Macaulay sheaves

Our first result is a consistency check for Conjecture

For the Hodge module

For any

0.5.3.
Hyper-Kähler mirror symmetry

The transform
\phi_{\mathrm{FM}}(\Omega_{M}^{k})
can also be studied from the perspective of hyper-Kähler mirror symmetry and

When

is sent to “BAA” branes on

We note that for Hitchin systems, recently Hausel and Hitchin

12
]
studied another interesting class of hyper-holomorphic bundles given by the universal family of the Higgs bundles, whose mirrors are supported on the upward flows of certain very stable Higgs bundles.

The following proposition is a consequence of the existence of a symplectic form.

Proposition 1.3

(Ngô; see
[
4
, Proposition 8.9]
)
.

For an integrable system
\pi_{M}:M\to B
with
A
the associated group scheme as in Proposition
1.2
, we have

\mathrm{codim}_{B}\{b\in B\,|\,\delta(A_{b})\geq i\}\geq i.

1.3.
Picard

We introduce here another group over
B
closely related to
A
, the Picard space, which will appear in the Fourier–Mukai transform.

The (relative) Picard stack represents the functor

\mathcal{P}ic(M/B):B\textrm{-schemes}\to\mathrm{groupoids}

sending a
B
-scheme
S
to the groupoid of line bundles over
M\times_{B}S
. The section
s_{B}:B\to M
trivializes the
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-gerbe:

\mathcal{P}ic(M/B)=\mathrm{Pic}(M/B)\times B{\mathbb{G}}_{m}.

Here
\mathrm{Pic}(M/B)
is a
B
-group algebraic space whose restriction to a geometric point
b\in B
recovers the Picard scheme
\mathrm{Pic}(M_{s})
. In particular, the algebraic space
\mathrm{Pic}(M/B)
carries a universal line bundle

(14)

{\mathcal{L}}\rightarrow M\times_{B}\mathrm{Pic}(M/B).

The identity component
P\subset\mathrm{Pic}(M/B)
is also a group space over
B
, and we obtain a universal line bundle
{\mathcal{L}}\to M\times_{B}P
by the restriction of (
14
), which satisfies

{\mathcal{L}}|_{M\times_{B}0_{B}(B)}\simeq{\mathcal{O}}_{M}.

By the choice of splitting, the universal bundle
{\mathcal{L}}
on
M\times_{B}P
is normalized to satisfy the condition

{\mathcal{L}}|_{s_{B}(B)\times_{B}P}\simeq{\mathcal{O}}_{P}.

We will always work with this normalized universal family
{\mathcal{L}}
.

The two groups
A
and
P
over
B
are closely related. If we choose a
\pi_{M}
-relatively ample line bundle

(15)

\Theta\in\mathrm{Pic}(M),

we obtain a Poincaré line bundle

{\mathcal{P}}_{\Theta}:=\mu^{*}\Theta\otimes\mathrm{pr}_{M}^{*}\Theta

over
A\times_{B}M
, which further induces a morphism

\kappa_{\Theta}:A\rightarrow\mathrm{Pic}(M/B).

By
[
4
, Corollary 7.7]
, the morphism
\kappa_{\Theta}
is étale. Since
A=M^{\mathrm{sm}}\subset M
is irreducible, the image of
\kappa_{\Theta}
has to lie in the identity component
P\subset\mathrm{Pic}(M/B)
. We thus obtain the étale homomorphism of
B
-groups

\kappa_{\Theta}:A\to P.

The following proposition serves as the foundation for Conjecture
0.1
which identifies the formal neighborhoods of
B
inside
P
and
T^{*}B
.

Proposition 1.4
.

The
\pi_{M}
-relatively ample line bundle
\Theta
in (
15
) induce an isomorphism

\hat{\kappa}_{\Theta}:\hat{B}_{P}\xrightarrow{\simeq}\hat{B}_{T^{*}B}.

Here
\hat{B}_{(-)}
stands for the formal neighborhood of
B
inside
(-)
.

Proof.

Since the formal neighborhood of the
0
-section in a commutative group scheme splits,
i.e.
, it is isomorphic by the logarithm to the formal completion of the
0
-section in its normal bundle, we have

\hat{B}_{P}=\hat{B}_{N_{B/P}}.

So it suffices to show that
\kappa_{\Theta}
(together with
\sigma
) identifies the normal bundle
N_{B/P}
and the cotangent bundle
T^{*}B
over
B
. This is achieved in two steps. First, the étale
B
-morphism
\kappa_{\Theta}
induces an isomorphism
N_{B/P}\simeq N_{B/A}
. Then the restriction of the symplectic form
\sigma
on
B
to

A=M^{\mathrm{sm}}\subset M

induces an isomorphism
N_{B/A}\simeq T^{*}B
. The composition of these two isomorphism gives the desired one.
∎

Now we may state our main conjecture precisely using Proposition
1.4
.

Conjecture 1.5
.

For an integrable system
\pi_{M}:(M,\sigma)\to B
, let
\Theta
be any
\pi_{M}
-relatively ample line bundle (
15
). Under the isomorphism
\hat{\kappa}_{\Theta}
between the formal neighborhoods of
B
in
P
and
T^{*}B
, there exists an isomorphism

\phi_{\mathrm{FM}}(\Omega_{M}^{k})|_{\hat{B}_{P}}\simeq\mathrm{gr}(P_{k})|_{\hat{B}_{T^{*}B}}\in\mathrm{Coh}(\hat{B}).

Remark 1.6
.

The statements of Conjecture
1.5
come in pairs. On one hand, the symplectic form
\sigma
induces an isomorphism
\Omega_{M}^{k}\simeq\Omega_{M}^{2n-k}
, where
2n=\dim M
. On the other hand, we have
\mathrm{gr}(P_{k})\simeq\mathrm{gr}(P_{2n-k})
by the relative Hard Lefschetz theorem.

2.
Singular supports

2.1.
Overview

In this section, we review the decomposition theorem of
\pi_{M}:M\to B
and describe the reduced support

\mathrm{supp}^{\mathrm{red}}(\mathrm{gr}(P_{k}))\subset T^{*}B

following Migliorini–Shende
[
25
]
using the group scheme
A
. As this coincides with the singular support of the underlying perverse sheaf, in this section we can view
P_{k}
merely as a perverse sheaf; in particular the filtration
F_{\bullet}P_{k}
provided by Saito’s theory does not play a role.

We conclude this section by introducing
\Lambda^{\prime}\subset P
which serves as the counter-part at the Fourier–Mukai side of the singular support associated with the decomposition theorem. A comparison between
\Lambda
and
\Lambda^{\prime}
is deduced in Corollary
2.5
.

2.2.
The decomposition theorem

As before, we assume that

\dim M=2\dim B=2n.

Since a Lagrangian fibration has equidimesional fibers of dimension
n

[
23
]
, the decomposition theorem
[
7
]
yields

R\pi_{M*}{\mathbb{Q}}_{M}[n]\simeq\bigoplus_{i=0}^{2n}P_{k}[-k],\quad P_{k}={{}^{\mathfrak{p}}{\mathcal{H}}}^{k}(R\pi_{*}{\mathbb{Q}}_{M}[n]).

Here each perverse sheaf
P_{k}
is semisimple.

We note that in the cases of Example
1.1
Ngô’s support theorem
[
27
]
implies that all
P_{k}
have full support. In particular, each
P_{k}
is given by the intermediate extension of the local systems obtained from the smooth locus of the integrable system
\pi_{M}:M\to B
. More refined information regarding the topology of
\pi_{M}
is encoded in the
singular supports
which are certain conical Lagrangians subvarieties of
T^{*}B
. We describe them in the following section for integrable systems.

2.3.
Singular supports

For any proper morphism
f:X\to Y
between nonsingular varieties, Migliorini and Shende
[
25
]
provide a concrete description of the singular supports

\mathrm{SS}(Rf_{*}{\mathbb{Q}}_{X})\subset T^{*}Y

associated with the decomposition theorem of
f
using higher discriminants.

Recall that for each
i\geq 0
, the higher discriminant
\Delta^{i}(f)
is formed by points
y\in Y
such that no
(i-1)
-dimensional subspace of the tangent space
T_{y}Y
at
y
is transverse to
f
. We obtain a stratification

Y=\Delta^{0}(f)\supset\Delta^{1}(f)\supset\Delta^{2}(f)\supset\dots,

where each
\Delta^{i}(f)\subset Y
is closed with

(16)

\mathrm{codim}_{Y}(\Delta^{i}(f))\geq i.

The closed subset
\Delta^{i}(f)
generalizes the discriminant
\Delta^{1}(f)
— the locus where the fiber is singular.

The following is the main result of
[
25
]
.

Theorem 2.1

(Migliorini–Shende)
.

The singular support of
Rf_{*}{\mathbb{Q}}_{X}
is contained in the union of the conormal varieties to
i
-codimensional components of
\Delta^{i}(f)
for all
i
.

In the case of an integrable system
\pi_{M}:M\to B
, the higher discriminants
\Delta^{i}(\pi_{M})
are more concretely given by the
\delta
-stratification associated with the group scheme
A

[
25
, Proposition 4.3]
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
A
be the
B
-group scheme associated with an integrable system
\pi_{M}:M\to B
. We have

\Delta^{i}(\pi_{M})=\{b\in B\,|\,\delta(b)\geq i\}.

Using Proposition
2.2
, we may reformulate Ngô’s
\delta
-regularity in Proposition
1.3
as

\mathrm{codim}_{B}\Delta^{i}(\pi_{M})\geq i,

which is an immediate consequence of (
16
).

2.4.
The subvarieties
\Lambda\subset T^{*}B
and
\Lambda^{\prime}\subset P

Recall from (
6
) the conical Lagrangian
\Lambda\subset T^{*}B
; the “support part” of Theorem
0.2
follows from Theorem

:

\mathrm{supp}^{\mathrm{red}}(\mathrm{gr}(P_{k}))=\mathrm{SS}(P_{k})\subset\mathrm{SS}(R{\pi_{M}}_{*}{\mathbb{Q}}_{M})\subset\Lambda.

Furthermore, in view of Proposition
2.2
the higher discriminants
\Delta^{i}(\pi_{M})
and the conical Lagrangian
\Lambda\subset T^{*}B
are governed by the
\delta
-stratification on the base
B
. In the following, we construct a closed subvariety
\Lambda^{\prime}\subset P
using also

, which serves as
the counter-part of
\Lambda\subset T^{*}B
for the Fourier–Mukai side.

For notational convenience, we denote by
G
a commutative group space over

which is either
A
or

. Since
\kappa_{\Theta}
preserves the affine parts, the

Let

1\to R_{b}\to G_{b}\to H_{b}\to 1

be the Chevalley decomposition for the group space

over a closed point

\Delta_{G}^{\mathrm{aff}}\subset G
to be the locus

such that

lies in the affine part
R_{\pi_{M}(g)}
over
\pi_{M}(g)\in B
.

The lemma below follows from Proposition
1.3
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Any irreducible component of
\Delta_{G}^{\mathrm{aff}}
has dimension
\leq n
.

We denote by

\Lambda^{\prime}_{G}\subset\Delta_{G}^{\mathrm{aff}}

the union of all
n
-dimensional irreducible components.

We now give a more concrete description of
\Lambda^{\prime}_{G}
in terms of
\Delta^{i}(\pi_{M})
parallel to that of
\Lambda
in (
6
). For a locally closed subset
Z\subset B
, there exists an open dense
V\subset Z
such that the Chevalley decomposition over

is of the form

1\to R_{V}\to G|_{V}\to H_{V}\to 1

with
R_{V}
affine and

c.f.
the paragraph before
[
8
, Section 2.3]
. We use
\overline{R_{Z}}
to denote the Zariski closure of

in
G
for any choice of

as above.

The following proposition can be compared with the definition (
6
) of
\Lambda\subset T^{*}B
.

Proposition 2.4
.

We have

\Lambda^{\prime}_{G}=\bigcup_{i}\bigcup_{Z_{i}}\overline{R_{Z_{i}}}\subset G.

Here
Z_{i}
runs through purely
i
-codimensional irreducible components of
\Delta^{i}(\pi_{M})
.

Proof.

Since specializations preserve affine parts of the groups, we have

\Lambda^{\prime}_{G}\supseteq\bigcup_{i}\bigcup_{Z_{i}}\overline{R_{Z_{i}}}.

To prove the other inclusion, we take
W
to be an irreducible component of
\Lambda_{G}^{\prime}
; by definition it is purely of dimension
n
. We consider
Z=\pi_{M}(W)\subset B
. Assume that a general point

satisfies

. Hence a general fiber of

has dimension

. This implies that

has codimension
i
since

On the other hand, we have

where the latter has codimension at least

is an
i

, and the irreducible component

∎

In the following corollary, we relate
\Lambda\subset T^{*}B
to

\Lambda^{\prime}_{A}\subset A,\quad\Lambda^{\prime}_{P}\subset P.

Corollary 2.5
.

(i)

For the identification
\hat{B}_{A}=\hat{B}_{T^{*}B}
of formal neighborhoods induced by the symplectic form
\sigma
, we have

\Lambda^{\prime}_{A}|_{\hat{B}_{A}}=\Lambda|_{\hat{B}_{T^{*}B}}.

(ii)

For any choice of
\Theta
in (
15

\hat{B}_{P}
and
\hat{B}_{T^{*}B}
as in Proposition

\Lambda_{P}^{\prime}|_{\hat{B}_{P}}=\Lambda|_{\hat{B}_{T^{*}B}}.

Proof.

The first part follows from Proposition

\overline{R_{Z_{i}}}|_{\hat{B}_{A}}=\overline{T^{*}_{Z_{i}}B}|_{\hat{B}_{T^{*}B}}.

The second part follows from the fact that the étale map between the groups

∎

From now on we set

\Lambda^{\prime}:=\Lambda^{\prime}_{P}\subset P.

3.
Fourier–Mukai I: the perverse–Hodge symmetry

3.1.
Overview

We review Arinkin–Fedorov’s Fourier–Mukai transform and deduce some of its basic properties. This recovers the border cases
k=0,2n
of Conjecture
1.5
; see Corollary
3.2
. Then we discuss a reformulation from the Fourier–Mukai viewpoint of Matsushita’s theorem
[
24
]
concerning the higher direct image of
{\mathcal{O}}_{M}
and its generalization — the perverse–Hodge symmetry (Conjecture
3.6
)
[
31
,
32
]
. We conclude this section by proving Proposition
0.6
.

3.2.
Fourier–Mukai functors

We start with a brief review of the classical Fourier–Mukai transform
[
26
]
.

Let
A
be an
n
-dimensional abelian variety with
P=\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(A)
its dual. Then a universal family induces a canonical (normalized) Poincaré line bundle
{\mathcal{P}}
on
A\times P
; it further induces two functors for the bounded derived categories of coherent sheaves:

\Phi_{\mathrm{FM}}:D^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(P)\to D^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(A),\quad{\mathcal{E}}\mapsto R{q_{A*}}(q_{P}^{*}{\mathcal{E}}\otimes{\mathcal{P}})

and

\phi_{\mathrm{FM}}:D^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(A)\to D^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(P),\quad{\mathcal{E}}\mapsto R{q_{P*}}(q_{A}^{*}{\mathcal{E}}\otimes{\mathcal{P}}^{\vee})[n].

Here
q_{A}
and
q_{P}
are the natural projections from
A\times P
to the corresponding factors. Both functors
\Phi_{\mathrm{FM}}
and
\phi_{\mathrm{FM}}
are equivalences of categories, and they are inverses of each other:

\phi_{\mathrm{FM}}\circ\Phi_{\mathrm{FM}}\simeq\mathrm{id}_{D^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(P)},\quad\Phi_{\mathrm{FM}}\circ\phi_{\mathrm{FM}}\simeq\mathrm{id}_{D^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(A)}.

Arinkin–Fedorov
[
4
]
generalizes the picture above and their construction works for a large class of degenerate abelian schemes including integrable systems; see also
[
1
,
2
]
for the case of compactified Jacobians. Here we focus on the case of the integrable system
\pi_{M}:M\to B
with

\dim M=2\dim B=2n.

Recall the relative Picard space
P
which is smooth over
B
, and the normalized universal line bundle
{\mathcal{L}}
over
M\times_{B}P
. Similarly, we have two functors

\Phi_{\mathrm{FM}}:D\mathrm{QCoh}(P)\to D\mathrm{QCoh}(M),\quad{\mathcal{E}}\mapsto R{q_{M*}}(q_{P}^{*}{\mathcal{E}}\otimes{\mathcal{L}})

and

\phi_{\mathrm{FM}}:D\mathrm{QCoh}(M)\to D\mathrm{QCoh}(P),\quad{\mathcal{E}}\mapsto R{q_{P*}}(q_{M}^{*}{\mathcal{E}}\otimes{\mathcal{L}}^{\vee})\otimes\pi_{P}^{*}\omega_{B}^{\vee}[n].

When
\pi_{M}
has singular fibers, the above two functors are no longer equivalences. Nevertheless, we still have by
[
4
]
that

(17)

\phi_{\mathrm{FM}}\circ\Phi_{\mathrm{FM}}\simeq\mathrm{id}_{D\mathrm{QCoh}(P)}:D\mathrm{QCoh}(P)\to D\mathrm{QCoh}(P),

and in particular
\Phi_{\mathrm{FM}}
is fully-faithful. We note that since
q_{M}:M\times_{B}P\to P
is proper, the functor
\phi_{\mathrm{FM}}
preserves bounded coherent complexes.

Proposition 3.1
.

For
{\mathcal{K}}\in D^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(B)
, we have

\Phi_{\mathrm{FM}}(0_{B*}{\mathcal{K}})\simeq\pi_{M}^{*}{\mathcal{K}},\quad\phi_{\mathrm{FM}}(\pi_{M}^{*}{\mathcal{K}})\simeq 0_{B*}{\mathcal{K}}.

Proof.

By (
17
) it suffices to prove the first statement. Since
{\mathcal{L}}
is trivialized along the
0
-section
0_{B}:B\rightarrow P
, we have

(18)

0_{B}^{*}{\mathcal{L}}\simeq 0_{B}^{*}{\mathcal{L}}^{\vee}\simeq{\mathcal{O}}_{M}.

We use
0_{M}:M\rightarrow M\times_{B}P
to denote the base change of the
0
-section
0_{B}:B\rightarrow P
. We have

\displaystyle\Phi_{\mathrm{FM}}(0_{B*}{\mathcal{K}})

\displaystyle\simeq Rq_{M*}(q_{P}^{*}0_{B*}{\mathcal{K}}\otimes{\mathcal{L}})

\displaystyle\simeq Rq_{M*}(0_{M*}\pi_{M}^{*}{\mathcal{K}}\otimes{\mathcal{L}})

\displaystyle\simeq Rq_{M*}0_{M*}(\pi_{M}^{*}{\mathcal{K}}\otimes 0_{M}^{*}{\mathcal{L}})

\displaystyle\simeq\pi_{M}^{*}{\mathcal{K}}.

Here the second isomorphism is the base change
q_{P}^{*}0_{B*}\simeq 0_{M*}\pi_{M}^{*}
, the third isomorphism is the projection formula, and the fourth isomorphism follows from
q_{M}\circ 0_{M}=\mathrm{id}_{M}
and (
18
).
∎

Applying Proposition
3.1
to
{\mathcal{K}}={\mathcal{O}}_{B}
, we can verify the border cases of Conjecture
1.5
.

Corollary 3.2
.

Conjecture
1.5
holds for
k=0,2n
.

Proof.

We have

\phi_{\mathrm{FM}}({\mathcal{O}}_{M})\simeq 0_{B*}{\mathcal{O}}_{B}\in\mathrm{Coh}(P),\quad\mathrm{gr}(P_{0})\simeq 0_{B*}{\mathcal{O}}_{B}\in\mathrm{Coh}(T^{*}B).

Both are structure sheaves of the
0
-sections; in particular, they are isomorphic in the formal neighborhoods of
B
.
∎

As in the abelian variety case, there is a (derived) Pontryagin product on
P
. Consider the addition map

m_{P}:P\times_{B}P\to P.

We define

\star^{R}:D\mathrm{QCoh}(P)\times D\mathrm{QCoh}(P)\to D\mathrm{QCoh}(P),\quad({\mathcal{E}}_{1},{\mathcal{E}}_{2})\mapsto Rm_{P*}(q_{1}^{*}{\mathcal{E}}_{1}\otimes^{L}q_{2}^{*}{\mathcal{E}}_{2})

where
q_{1}
and
q_{2}
are the two projections from
P\times_{B}P
.

Proposition 3.3
.

For
{\mathcal{E}}_{1},{\mathcal{E}}_{2}\in D\mathrm{QCoh}(P)
and
{\mathcal{K}}_{1},{\mathcal{K}}_{2}\in D^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(M)
, we have

\Phi_{\mathrm{FM}}({\mathcal{E}}_{1}\star^{R}{\mathcal{E}}_{2})\simeq\Phi_{\mathrm{FM}}({\mathcal{E}}_{1})\otimes^{L}\Phi_{\mathrm{FM}}({\mathcal{E}}_{2}),\quad\phi_{\mathrm{FM}}({\mathcal{K}}_{1}\otimes^{L}{\mathcal{K}}_{2})\simeq\phi_{\mathrm{FM}}({\mathcal{K}}_{1})\star^{R}\phi_{\mathrm{FM}}({\mathcal{K}}_{2}).

Proof.

The two statements are similar; we only prove the first. By the theorem of the cube applied to
M\times_{B}P\times_{B}P
, we have

(19)

(\mathrm{id}\times m_{P})^{*}{\mathcal{L}}\simeq q_{12}^{*}{\mathcal{L}}\otimes q_{13}^{*}{\mathcal{L}}

where the
q_{ij}
are the natural projections from
M\times_{B}P\times_{B}P
to the respective factors. Then

\displaystyle\Phi_{\mathrm{FM}}({\mathcal{E}}_{1}\star^{R}{\mathcal{E}}_{2})

\displaystyle\simeq Rq_{M*}(q_{P}^{*}Rm_{P*}(q_{1}^{*}{\mathcal{E}}_{1}\otimes^{L}q_{2}^{*}{\mathcal{E}}_{2})\otimes{\mathcal{L}})

\displaystyle\simeq Rq_{M*}(R(\mathrm{id}\times m_{P})_{*}q_{23}^{*}(q_{1}^{*}{\mathcal{E}}_{1}\otimes^{L}q_{2}^{*}{\mathcal{E}}_{2})\otimes{\mathcal{L}})

\displaystyle\simeq Rq_{M*}R(\mathrm{id}\times m_{P})_{*}(q_{12}^{*}q_{P}^{*}{\mathcal{E}}_{1}\otimes^{L}q_{13}^{*}q_{P}^{*}{\mathcal{E}}_{2}\otimes(\mathrm{id}\times m_{P})^{*}{\mathcal{L}})

\displaystyle\simeq Rq_{M*}Rq_{13*}(q_{12}^{*}q_{P}^{*}{\mathcal{E}}_{1}\otimes^{L}q_{13}^{*}q_{P}^{*}{\mathcal{E}}_{2}\otimes q_{12}^{*}{\mathcal{L}}\otimes q_{13}^{*}{\mathcal{L}})

\displaystyle\simeq Rq_{M*}(Rq_{13*}q_{12}^{*}(q_{P}^{*}{\mathcal{E}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{L}})\otimes^{L}(q_{P}^{*}{\mathcal{E}}_{2}\otimes{\mathcal{L}}))

\displaystyle\simeq Rq_{M*}(q_{M}^{*}Rq_{M*}(q_{P}^{*}{\mathcal{E}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{L}})\otimes^{L}(q_{P}^{*}{\mathcal{E}}_{2}\otimes{\mathcal{L}}))

\displaystyle\simeq\Phi_{\mathrm{FM}}({\mathcal{E}}_{1})\otimes^{L}\Phi_{\mathrm{FM}}({\mathcal{E}}_{2}).

Here the second isomorphism is the base change
q_{P}^{*}Rm_{P*}\simeq R(\mathrm{id}\times m_{P})_{*}q_{23}^{*}
, the third isomorphism is the projection formula, the fourth isomorphism follows from
q_{M}\circ(\mathrm{id}\times m_{P})=q_{M}\circ q_{13}
and (
19
), the fifth isomorphism is again the projection formula, the sixth isomorphism is the base change
Rq_{13*}q_{12}^{*}\simeq q_{M}^{*}Rq_{M*}
, and the last isomorphism follows from the projection formula and the definition of
\Phi_{\mathrm{FM}}
.
∎

3.3.
Pushforward and Fourier–Mukai

The Fourier–Mukai functors
\Phi_{\mathrm{FM}}
and
\phi_{\mathrm{FM}}
are also compatible with the pushforwards

R\pi_{P*}:D\mathrm{QCoh}(P)\to D\mathrm{QCoh}(B),\quad R\pi_{M*}:D^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(M)\rightarrow D^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(B).

Proposition 3.4
.

For
{\mathcal{E}}\in D\mathrm{QCoh}(P)
and
{\mathcal{K}}\in D^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(M)
, we have

Ls_{B}^{*}\,\Phi_{\mathrm{FM}}({\mathcal{E}})\simeq R\pi_{P*}{\mathcal{E}},\quad L0_{B}^{*}\,\phi_{\mathrm{FM}}({\mathcal{K}})\simeq R\pi_{M*}{\mathcal{K}}\otimes\omega_{B}^{\vee}[n].

Proof.

The two statements are similar; we only prove the second. We have

\displaystyle L0_{B}^{*}\,\phi_{\mathrm{FM}}({\mathcal{K}})

\displaystyle\simeq L0_{B}^{*}Rq_{P*}(q_{M}^{*}{\mathcal{K}}\otimes{\mathcal{L}}^{\vee})\otimes\omega_{B}^{\vee}[n]

\displaystyle\simeq R\pi_{M*}L0_{M}^{*}(q_{M}^{*}{\mathcal{K}}\otimes{\mathcal{L}}^{\vee})\otimes\omega_{B}^{\vee}[n]

\displaystyle\simeq R\pi_{M*}({\mathcal{K}}\otimes 0_{M}^{*}{\mathcal{L}}^{\vee})\otimes\omega_{B}^{\vee}[n]

\displaystyle\simeq R\pi_{M*}{\mathcal{K}}\otimes\omega_{B}^{\vee}[n].

Here the second isomorphism is the base change
L0_{B}^{*}Rq_{P*}\simeq R\pi_{M*}L0_{M}^{*}
, the third isomorphism is given by
q_{M}\circ 0_{M}=\mathrm{id}_{M}
, and the fourth isomorphism follows from (
18
).
∎

The following example explains a connection between the Fourier–Mukai transform and a theorem of Matsushita
[
24
]
. It can be thought of as a first example where the decomposition theorem and the Fourier–Mukai transform are related.

Example 3.5
.

Applying Proposition
3.4
to
{\mathcal{K}}={\mathcal{O}}_{M}
, we obtain from Proposition
3.1
that

R\pi_{M*}{\mathcal{O}}_{M}\simeq L0_{B}^{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{B}\otimes\omega_{B}[-n].

The object
L0_{B}^{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{B}
is the derived self-tensor of the structure sheaf
{\mathcal{O}}_{B}
of the
0
-section
B\subset P
. Using either the Koszul resolution or the derived self-intersection formula
[
3
]
, we have

(20)

L0_{B}^{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{B}\simeq\bigoplus_{k=0}^{n}\wedge^{k}N^{\vee}_{B/P}[k]\simeq\bigoplus_{k=0}^{n}T_{B}^{k}[k].

Therefore

(21)

R\pi_{M*}{\mathcal{O}}_{M}\simeq\bigoplus_{k=0}^{n}T_{B}^{k}\otimes\omega_{B}[k-n]\simeq\bigoplus_{k=0}^{n}\Omega_{B}^{k}[-k].

The equation (
21
) implies simultaneously that

(i)

the derived direct image admits a splitting into sheaves

(22)

R\pi_{M*}{\mathcal{O}}_{M}\simeq\bigoplus_{k=0}^{n}R^{k}\pi_{M*}{\mathcal{O}}_{M}[-k],

and

(ii)

each higher direct image is given by

R^{k}\pi_{M*}{\mathcal{O}}_{M}\simeq\Omega_{B}^{k}.

In fact, both statements are known to hold for any Lagrangian fibration
\pi_{M}:M\to B
beyond the case of integrable systems we consider here. The statement (i) was deduced from Saito’s enhancement of the decomposition theorem by passing to graded pieces. The statement (ii) is a theorem of Matsushita
[
24
]
which was proven for any Lagrangian fibration; Matsushita’s argument relies on the decomposition theorem (i) as well as Hodge-theoretic and birational-geometric techniques.

For an integrable system
\pi_{M}:M\to B
, the Fourier–Mukai transform provides an alternative argument, yielding both (i) and (ii) from a single calculation

Rq_{P*}{\mathcal{L}}\simeq 0_{B*}\omega_{B}[-n],

which is proven in
[
4
]
essentially from Ngô’s
\delta
-inequality (Proposition
1.3
in
[
4
]
). In particular, the Fourier–Mukai approach suggests that (i) and (ii) are both determined by the topology of the integrable system. ∎

3.4.
The perverse–Hodge symmetry

As illustrated in the example above, the object

is governed by the Fourier–Mukai identity

\phi_{\mathrm{FM}}({\mathcal{O}}_{M})\simeq 0_{B*}{\mathcal{O}}_{B}.

In this section we discuss a generalization (Conjecture

For our purpose here, we view

of the decomposition theorem a Hodge module on the base

Conjecture 3.6

(Categorified perverse–Hodge symmetry)

We have

R\pi_{M*}\Omega_{M}^{k}\simeq(L0_{B}^{*}\,\mathrm{gr}(P_{k}))^{\vee}\in D^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(B).

(i)

is self-dual. Consequently, Conjecture

(iii)

When

4.
Fourier–Mukai II: Lagrangian Cohen–Macaulay sheaves

4.1.
Overview

In this section, we prove Theorem
0.3
which shows that the object

\phi_{\mathrm{FM}}(\Omega_{M}^{k})|_{\hat{B}_{P}}

is a Cohen–Macaulay sheaf supported on the conical Lagrangian
\Lambda^{\prime}|_{\hat{B}_{P}}
. This is matched with the singular support of
P_{k}
in view of Theorem
0.2
and Corollary
2.5
.

Our method is built on the ideas of Arinkin
[
1
]
and Arinkin–Fedorov
[
4
]
.

4.2.
Cohen–Macaulay sheaves

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a coherent sheaf on
P
. We say that
{\mathcal{F}}
is
Cohen–Macaulay
if for any closed point
x\in P
, we have

\mathrm{depth}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{P,x}}({\mathcal{F}}_{x})=\dim\mathrm{supp}({\mathcal{F}}_{x}).

In particular,
{\mathcal{F}}
is Cohen–Macaulay of pure dimension
d
if and only if its Verdier dual

R{\mathcal{H}}\mathrm{om}({\mathcal{F}},\omega_{P})\in D^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(P)

is concentrated in degree
d
.

The following theorem is a Cohen–Macaulay criterion for the Fourier–Mukai transform of a locally free sheaf.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{K}}
be a locally free sheaf on
M
. Let
Z\subset P
be a closed subset for which each irreducible component has dimension
\leq n
. If

\mathrm{supp}^{\mathrm{red}}(\phi_{\mathrm{FM}}({\mathcal{K}}))\subset Z,

then
\phi_{\mathrm{FM}}({\mathcal{K}})
is a Cohen–Macaulay sheaf of pure dimension
n
concentrated in degree 0.

Proof.

By definition the object

\phi_{\mathrm{FM}}({\mathcal{K}})=R{q_{P*}}(q_{M}^{*}{\mathcal{K}}\otimes{\mathcal{L}}^{\vee})\otimes\pi_{P}^{*}\omega_{B}^{\vee}[n]\in D^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(P)

is concentrated in degrees
[-n,0]
. Concerning its Verdier dual, we have

\displaystyle R{\mathcal{H}}\mathrm{om}(\phi_{\mathrm{FM}}({\mathcal{K}}),\omega_{P})

\displaystyle\simeq R{\mathcal{H}}\mathrm{om}(Rq_{P*}(q_{M}^{*}{\mathcal{K}}\otimes{\mathcal{L}}^{\vee})\otimes\pi_{P}^{*}\omega_{B}^{\vee}[n],\omega_{P})

\displaystyle\simeq Rq_{P*}R{\mathcal{H}}\mathrm{om}(q_{M}^{*}{\mathcal{K}}\otimes{\mathcal{L}}^{\vee},\omega_{q_{P}})

\displaystyle\simeq Rq_{P*}(q_{M}^{*}{\mathcal{K}}^{\vee}\otimes{\mathcal{L}}\otimes\omega_{q_{P}}).

Here the second isomorphism follows from Grothendieck–Verdier duality. Since
{\mathcal{K}}
is locally free on
M
, we have that
q_{M}^{*}{\mathcal{K}}^{\vee}\otimes{\mathcal{L}}\otimes\omega_{q_{P}}
is a (locally free) sheaf on
M\times_{B}P
. Therefore the object

R{\mathcal{H}}\mathrm{om}(\phi_{\mathrm{FM}}({\mathcal{K}}),\omega_{P})\in D^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(P)

is concentrated in degrees
[0,n]
.

In conclusion, we know that:

(i)

\phi_{\mathrm{FM}}({\mathcal{K}})
is concentrated in
[-n,0]
,

(ii)

its Verdier dual
R{\mathcal{H}}\mathrm{om}(\phi_{\mathrm{FM}}({\mathcal{K}}),\omega_{P})
is concentrated in
[0,n]
, and

(iii)

both objects have supports of dimension
\leq n
.

The only way for this to happen is that
\phi_{\mathrm{FM}}({\mathcal{K}})
is a sheaf concentrated in degree 0 of pure dimension
n
, and its Verdier dual is concentrated in degree
n
(see
[
2
, Lemma 7.7]
); in other words,
\phi_{\mathrm{FM}}({\mathcal{K}})
is a Cohen–Macaulay sheaf of pure dimension
n
(concentrated in degree 0).
∎

4.3.
Proof of Theorem
0.3

Proposition 4.2
.

Assume that
{\mathcal{K}}\in D^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(M)
underlies an
A
-equivariant bounded complex on
M
. Then we have

(i)

the support of
\phi_{\mathrm{FM}}({\mathcal{K}})
has dimension
\leq n
, and

(ii)

its restriction to
\hat{B}\subset P
is contained in
\Delta_{P}^{\mathrm{aff}}
;
i.e.
,

\mathrm{supp}^{\mathrm{red}}(\phi_{\mathrm{FM}}({\mathcal{K}}))|_{\hat{B}}\subset\Delta_{P}^{\mathrm{aff}}|_{\hat{B}}.

Proof.

The proof essentially follows from the discussion in
[
4
, Sections 4 and 5]
; we present it here for the reader’s convenience.

Let
l\in P
be a line bundle on
M
with

l\in\mathrm{supp}^{\mathrm{red}}(\phi_{\mathrm{FM}}({\mathcal{K}}))\subset P.

We write
b:=\pi_{P}(l)\in B
, and denote by
A_{b},M_{b},P_{b}
the restrictions of
A,M,P
over the closed point
b
respectively. The (derived) restriction of the
A
-equivariant object
{\mathcal{K}}
on
M
gives an
A_{b}
-equivariant object
{\mathcal{K}}_{b}\in D^{b}\mathrm{Coh}(M_{b})
.

Step 1.
By base change, we know that

(27)

H^{j}(M_{b},{\mathcal{K}}_{b}\otimes l_{b}^{\vee})\neq 0

for some
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. The restriction of the line bundle
l_{b}^{\vee}
to
A_{b}\subset M_{b}
corresponds to a
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-torsor over
A_{b}
. By
[
4
, Proposition 5.6]
this
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-torsor, denoted by
\widetilde{A}_{b}
, is actually a commutative group scheme given by the extension

(28)

1\to{\mathbb{C}}^{*}\to\widetilde{A}_{b}\to A_{b}\to 1.

It acts on the total space of
l_{b}^{\vee}
such that the subtorus
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}\subset\widetilde{A}
acts tautologically by dilations. Since
{\mathcal{K}}_{b}
is
A_{b}
-equivariant, this gives rise to a natural
\widetilde{A}_{b}
-action on the vector space (
27
).

Step 2.
Since
\widetilde{A}_{b}
is commutative, we may find a 1-dimensional sub-representation

V\subset H^{j}(M_{b},{\mathcal{K}}_{b}\otimes l_{b}^{\vee}),

which induces a character

\chi:\widetilde{A}_{b}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}^{*},\quad\chi|_{{\mathbb{C}}^{*}\subset\widetilde{A}_{b}}=\mathrm{id}_{{\mathbb{C}}^{*}}.

It splits the extension (
28
). In particular, the restriction of
l_{b}^{\vee}
to
A_{b}\subset M_{b}
is a trivial line bundle; equivalently, the restriction of
l_{b}
to
A_{b}
is trivial.

Step 3.
Now we focus on the
cohomology support locus

K_{b}=\{l\in\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(M_{b})\,|\,l|_{A_{b}}\simeq{\mathcal{O}}_{A_{b}}\}\subset\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(M_{b});

it is a quasi-subgroup in the sense of
[
4
, Definition 4.11]
, whose identity component
K_{b}^{0}\subset K_{b}
is affine by
[
4
, Corollary 4.6]
. In particular, we have

\dim K_{b}\leq\delta(b).

By Steps 1 and 2, we know that

\mathrm{supp}^{\mathrm{red}}(\phi_{\mathrm{FM}}({\mathcal{K}}))\subset\bigcup_{b\in B}K_{b}

where the latter
K:=\bigcup_{b\in B}K_{b}
is a countable union of constructible sets satisfying:

\dim K\leq\dim\Delta_{P}^{\mathrm{aff}}\leq n,\quad K|_{\hat{B}}\subset\Delta_{P}^{\mathrm{aff}}|_{\hat{B}}.

The proposition is concluded.
∎

We complete in the following the proof of Theorem

. Note that in general the locally free sheaves

are not

Example 4.3
.

Let
\pi:M\to B
be a smooth elliptic fibration with a section, and let

is identical to

itself. If the tangent bundle tangent bundle

is

Therefore, we are not allowed to apply Proposition

directly. To solve this issue, we need the following lemma.

Let

with morphisms

{\mathcal{G}}_{i-1}\to{\mathcal{G}}_{i}\to\wedge^{k-i}{\mathcal{E}}_{1}\otimes^{L}\wedge^{i}{\mathcal{E}}_{3}\xrightarrow{+1}{\mathcal{G}}_{i-1}[1].

This follows from the proof of
[

via taking compatible locally free resolutions of the short exact sequence

Proof of Theorem
0.3
.

We first note that it suffices to prove

\mathrm{supp}^{\mathrm{red}}(\phi_{\mathrm{FM}}(\Omega_{M}^{k}))\subset K

Smooth fibrations

5.1.
Overview

In this section, we verify Conjecture
1.5
for smooth Lagrangian fibrations which proves Theorem
0.4
. Let
(M,\sigma)

The

(i)

Our strategy is similar to the proof of Theorem

Proposition 6.2
.

Assume

{\mathcal{A}}
and

and
\rho_{{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}}([{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}])
coincide:

\rho_{\mathcal{A}}([{\mathcal{A}}])=\rho_{{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}}([{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}])\in\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{T^{*}B}(0_{B*}\Omega_{B}^{1\vee},0_{B*}\Omega^{1}_{B}(D)).

Then we have

{\mathcal{A}}\simeq{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}.

Proof.

Applying
\mathrm{Hom}_{T^{*}B}(0_{B*}\Omega^{1\vee}_{B},-)
to (
57
), we obtain the long exact sequence:

\displaystyle\cdots\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}_{T^{*}B}(0_{B*}\Omega_{B}^{1\vee},0_{B*}\Omega^{1}_{B}(D))\xrightarrow{\textup{(i)}}\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}\mathrm{Ext}_{T^{*}B}^{1}(0_{B*}\Omega^{1\vee}_{B},{\mathcal{O}}_{F_{i}})

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\textup{(ii)}}\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{T^{*}B}(0_{B*}\Omega_{B}^{1\vee},{\mathcal{W}}_{0}^{\mathcal{A}})\xrightarrow{\rho_{{\mathcal{A}}}}\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{T^{*}B}(0_{B*}\Omega_{B}^{1\vee},0_{B*}\Omega^{1}_{B}(D)).

We would like to show in the following that, for a good admissible sheaf
{\mathcal{A}}
, the map (i) in the chain above is surjective, so that (ii) is 0. In particular, the extension class
[{\mathcal{A}}]
is completely characterized by its image
\rho_{\mathcal{A}}([{\mathcal{A}}])
and the proposition follows.

To prove the surjectivity of (i), we consider the residue exact sequence

0\to\Omega^{1}_{B}\to\Omega^{1}_{B}(D)\xrightarrow{\mathrm{res}}\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}{\mathbb{C}}_{p_{i}}\to 0.

Applying
\mathrm{Hom}_{B}(\Omega_{B}^{1\vee},-)

\cdots\to\mathrm{Hom}_{B}(\Omega_{B}^{1\vee},\Omega^{1}_{B}(D))\xrightarrow{\textup{(i)'}}{\mathbb{C}}^{\oplus m}\to\mathrm{Ext}_{B}^{1}(\Omega_{B}^{1\vee},\Omega^{1}_{B})\to\cdots

Using the fact that

is not proper, we obtain that

\mathrm{Ext}_{B}^{1}(\Omega_{B}^{1\vee},\Omega^{1}_{B})=0.

Therefore the map (i)’ above is surjective. On the other hand, the map (i)’ recovers (i):

\mathrm{Hom}_{T^{*}B}(0_{B*}\Omega_{B}^{1\vee},0_{B*}\Omega^{1}_{B}(D))=\mathrm{Hom}_{B}(\Omega_{B}^{1\vee},\Omega^{1}_{B}(D))\xrightarrow{\textup{(i)}=\textup{(i)'}}{\mathbb{C}}^{\oplus m}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}\mathrm{Ext}_{T^{*}B}^{1}(0_{B*}\Omega^{1\vee}_{B},{\mathcal{O}}_{F_{i}}).

Hence we conclude the surjectivity of (i), which completes the proof of the proposition.
∎

(i)

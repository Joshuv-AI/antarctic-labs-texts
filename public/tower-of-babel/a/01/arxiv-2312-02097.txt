Inapproximability of Maximum Diameter Clustering for Few Clusters
Henry Fleischmann, Kyrylo Karlov, Karthik C. S, Ashwin Padaki, Stepan Zharkov

Abstract
In the 𝖬𝖺𝗑 \mathsf{Max} - k k - 𝖣𝗂𝖺𝗆𝖾𝗍𝖾𝗋 \mathsf{Diameter} problem, we are given a set of points in a metric space, and the goal is to partition the input points into k k parts such that the maximum pairwise distance between points in the same part of the partition is minimized. The approximability of the 𝖬𝖺𝗑 \mathsf{Max} - k k - 𝖣𝗂𝖺𝗆𝖾𝗍𝖾𝗋 \mathsf{Diameter} problem was studied in the eighties, culminating in the work of Feder and Greene [STOC’88], wherein they showed it is 𝖭𝖯 \mathsf{NP} -hard to approximate within a factor better than 2 in the ℓ 1 \ell_{1} and ℓ ∞ \ell_{\infty} metrics, and 𝖭𝖯 \mathsf{NP} -hard to approximate within a factor better than 1.969 in the Euclidean metric. This complements the celebrated 2 factor polynomial time approximation algorithm for the problem in general metrics (Gonzalez [TCS’85]; Hochbaum and Shmoys [JACM’86]). Over the last couple of decades, there has been increased interest from the algorithmic community to study the approximability of various clustering objectives when the number of clusters is fixed. In this setting, the framework of coresets has yielded 𝖯𝖳𝖠𝖲 \mathsf{PTAS} for most popular clustering objectives, including k k - 𝗆𝖾𝖺𝗇𝗌 \mathsf{means} , k k - 𝗆𝖾𝖽𝗂𝖺𝗇 \mathsf{median} , k k - 𝖼𝖾𝗇𝗍𝖾𝗋 \mathsf{center} , k k - 𝗆𝗂𝗇𝗌𝗎𝗆 \mathsf{minsum} , and so on. In this paper, rather surprisingly, we prove that even when k = 3 k=3 , the 𝖬𝖺𝗑 \mathsf{Max} - k k - 𝖣𝗂𝖺𝗆𝖾𝗍𝖾𝗋 \mathsf{Diameter} problem is 𝖭𝖯 \mathsf{NP} -hard to approximate within a factor of 1.5 1.5 in the ℓ 1 \ell_{1} -metric (and Hamming metric) and 𝖭𝖯 \mathsf{NP} -hard to approximate within a factor of 1.304 1.304 in the Euclidean metric. Our main conceptual contribution is the introduction of a novel framework called cloud systems which embed hypergraphs into ℓ p \ell_{p} -metric spaces such that the chromatic number of the hypergraph is related to the quality of the 𝖬𝖺𝗑 \mathsf{Max} - k k - 𝖣𝗂𝖺𝗆𝖾𝗍𝖾𝗋 \mathsf{Diameter} clustering of the embedded pointset. Our main technical contributions are the constructions of nontrivial cloud systems in the Euclidean and ℓ 1 \ell_{1} -metrics using extremal geometric structures.

Introduction

The
\mathsf{Max}
-
k
-
\mathsf{Diameter}

k

2

This clustering objective was actively studied in the eighties under the lens of approximation. One of the main advantages of

By the early 1980s, the

-

-complete even in the Euclidean plane (implicit in
[

,

,

,

-hard to approximate to a factor of 1.732. This was improved by Feder and Greene

-

-
\mathsf{Diameter}

-hard to approximate within a factor better than 2 in both the

and
\ell_{\infty}
metrics. More importantly, all the results of Feder and Greene hold when the input is in two dimensions.

At first glance, it looks like we have hit a road block in understanding the approximability of the
\mathsf{Max}
-
k
-
\mathsf{Diameter}
problem. On the one hand, we have completely understood its approximability in the general metrics,
\ell_{1}
, and
\ell_{\infty}

-
k
-
\mathsf{Diameter}
problem is much like other Euclidean clustering problems (such as Euclidean
k
-
\mathsf{center}

[
FG88
,
Men88
]
), which have been open for decades with no real tools of attack to bridge the gap in approximability.

Over the last two-and-a-half decades, a lot of effort has been invested by the algorithmic community to understand the approximability of popular clustering objectives when the number of clusters is
fixed
(in
\ell_{p}
-metrics). In fact, a powerful paradigm has emerged to provide polynomial time approximation schemes (
\mathsf{PTAS}
) for all these problems:

[

,

]
. In the literature, when

-
\mathsf{center}

[

]
,

-
\mathsf{means}

[

]
,

\mathsf{median}

[

,
Che09
]
,

-
\mathsf{minsum}

]
, and other popular clustering objectives.

Does

-

-
\mathsf{Diameter}

Our Techniques

Clustering and coloring.

There is a natural connection between the geometric problem of
\mathsf{Max}
-
k
-
\mathsf{Diameter}
and the graph theoretic problem of
k
-coloring. Namely, there is a simple reduction from
\mathsf{Max}
-
k
-
\mathsf{Diameter}
to
k
-coloring where given a pointset
P
, we can construct a graph
G_{P}
where points in
P
correspond to vertices in
G_{P}
and edges are drawn between vertices if the distance between their corresponding points exceeds a threshold
\beta>0
. Then,
G_{P}
is
k
-colorable if and only if
P
can be partitioned into
k
clusters, each of which has diameter at most
\beta
.

A simple approach.

The above reduction from
\mathsf{Max}
-
k
-
\mathsf{Diameter}
to
k
-coloring also motivates a simple approach for reducing
k
-coloring to the approximation version of
\mathsf{Max}
-
k
-
\mathsf{Diameter}
. Let
G
be a graph, and suppose we can embed the vertices of
G
as a set of points
P_{G}
in a metric space, such that the following property holds: if two vertices are adjacent in
G
, the distance between their corresponding points in
P_{G}
is at least
r\beta
(for some
r>1
), and if the two vertices are nonadjacent, the distance between their corresponding points in
P_{G}
is at most
\beta
. Then, if
G
is
k
-colorable, there is a clustering of
P_{G}
with diameter at most
\beta
. However, if
G
is not
k
-colorable, then any
k
-clustering of
P_{G}
has diameter at least
r\beta
(i.e., any partition of
P_{G}
into
k
clusters contains at least one cluster of diameter at least
r\beta
). Therefore, approximating
\mathsf{Max}
-
k
-
\mathsf{Diameter}
within a factor strictly less than
r
is at least as hard as determining if
G
is
k
-colorable.

We formalize this technique as follows. For a metric space
(X,\textsf{dist})
, a graph
G=(V,E)
, and an approximation ratio
r>1
, we say that an
r
-embedding
of
G
is a map
\varphi\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}V\to X
such that, for some
\beta>0
,

1.

if
(u,v)\notin E
then
\textsf{dist}(\varphi(u),\varphi(v))\leq\beta
, and

2.

if
(u,v)\in E
then
\textsf{dist}(\varphi(u),\varphi(v))\geq r\beta
.

We refer to
\beta
as the
short distance
of the
r
-embedding.
The first property guarantees that if
G
is
k
-colorable, then the optimal
k
-clustering diameter of
\varphi(V)
is at most
\beta
. The second property guarantees that if
\varphi(V)
has a
k
-clustering with diameter less than
r\beta
, then
G
is
k
-colorable.

In particular, if
G
is a family of graphs on which
k
-coloring is
\mathsf{NP}
-hard, and
\varphi
is an efficiently computable
r
-embedding of
G
, then it is
\mathsf{NP}
-hard to approximate
\mathsf{Max}
-
k
-
\mathsf{Diameter}
within a factor better than
r
. We now present a proof-of-concept hardness of approximation result using this
r
-embedding framework.

Warm up in the
\ell_{\infty}
-metric.

We describe a simple
2
-embedding of general graphs into the
\ell_{\infty}
-metric, as given in
[
Meg90
]
.
Let
G=(V,E)
be an arbitrary graph with
V=[n]
. Define a mapping
\varphi\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}V\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
via
\varphi(u)=(a_{u,1},\ldots,a_{u,n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
given by:

a_{u,v}=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }(u,v)\in E\\
2&\text{if }u=v\\
1&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}.

It can be checked that
\varphi
is a
2
-embedding in the
\ell_{\infty}
-metric. Thus,
\mathsf{Max}
-
k
-
\mathsf{Diameter}
is hard to approximate within a factor of
2
in the
\ell_{\infty}
-metric for any
k\geq 3
, proving that the
2
-approximation algorithm is optimal in this setting.

We can further extend the above
\mathsf{NP}
-hardness to
\ell_{p}
-metrics in the following way. It is known that
3
-coloring remains
\mathsf{NP}
-hard even when restricted to
4
-regular graphs. When
\varphi
is considered in the
\ell_{p}
-metric, it is an
r
-embedding of
4
-regular graphs for

r=\mathinner{\left(\frac{2^{p+1}+6}{10}\right)}^{1/p}.

Thus,
\mathsf{Max}
-
k
-
\mathsf{Diameter}
(for
k\geq 3
) is hard to approximate within a factor of
\sqrt{7/5}
in the
\ell_{2}
-metric. However, in the
\ell_{1}
-metric, this approach does not give any nontrivial hardness, suggesting a need for different techniques.

Trevisan’s embedding in the
\ell_{1}
-metric.

Trevisan utilized Hadamard codes to prove inapproximability results for geometric problems
[
Tre00
]
. Recall that for
m
a power of
2
, a Hadamard code is a set of
m
elements in
\{0,1\}^{m}
whose pairwise
\ell_{1}
distances are all
m/2
. We outline a direct application of Trevisan’s embedding to obtain a nontrivial hardness of approximation result for
\mathsf{Max}
-
3
-
\mathsf{Diameter}
in the
\ell_{1}
-metric. In particular, we construct a general
5/4
-embedding of graphs which are
4
-regular and
4
-edge-colorable. It can be shown that
3
-coloring is
\mathsf{NP}
-hard on this family of graphs.

On an input graph
G=(V,E)
, let
m
be a power of
2
that is at least
|V|
. Since
G
is
4
-regular and
4
-edge colorable, we can decompose
E
as the disjoint union of matchings
M_{1},M_{2},M_{3},M_{4}\subseteq E
. Then, we embed each
v\in V
as the concatenation
\varphi(v)=(a_{v,1},a_{v,2},a_{v,3},a_{v,4})\in\{0,1\}^{4m}
, where the strings
a_{v,i}\in\{0,1\}^{m}
satisfy the following properties for each
i\in[4]
:

1.

If
(u,v)\in M_{i}
, then
a_{u,i}=\overline{a_{v,i}}
and thus
\left\lVert a_{u,i}-a_{v,i}\right\rVert_{1}=m
.

2.

If
(u,v)\notin M_{i}
, then
\left\lVert a_{u,i}-a_{v,i}\right\rVert_{1}=m/2
.

One can construct such an embedding by choosing each
a_{v,i}
to be either a Hadamard codeword or the complement of a Hadamard codeword. If
(u,v)\in E
, then
\left\lVert\varphi(u)-\varphi(v)\right\rVert_{1}=\frac{5m}{2}
, and otherwise,
\left\lVert\varphi(u)-\varphi(v)\right\rVert_{1}=2m
. Therefore,
\varphi
is a
5/4
-embedding of
G
, proving that
\mathsf{Max}
-
k
-
\mathsf{Diameter}
is hard to approximate within a factor of
5/4
in the
\ell_{1}
-metric for any
k\geq 3
.

As shown by these preliminary results, the
r
-embedding framework is a straightforward way to prove hardness of approximation results for
\mathsf{Max}
-
k
-
\mathsf{Diameter}
. However, it turns out that using this framework is restrictive, as it forces a one-to-one correspondence between vertices and points, and it forbids distances in the range
(\beta,r\beta)
. We can relax both of these restrictions using a different reduction from
k
-coloring to
\mathsf{Max}
-
k
-
\mathsf{Diameter}
. This novel framework, which we call an
r
-cloud system
, allows us to obtain significantly better inapproximability results in the
\ell_{1}
-metric and the
\ell_{2}
-metric.

The
r
-cloud system.

We now explain the intuition behind the
r
-cloud system for reductions from coloring to
\mathsf{Max}
-
k
-
\mathsf{Diameter}
. In the previous discussion, we reduced from the problem of
k
-coloring on graphs. For the
r
-cloud construction, a more natural problem to consider is panchromatic
k
-coloring on
k
-uniform hypergraphs.
2
2

2

In panchromatic
k
-coloring, we are given a
k
-uniform hypergraph, and the task is to color the vertices such that in each hyperedge, every vertex is assigned a distinct color.
A simple padding argument shows that this problem is at least as hard as
k
-coloring on graphs. Next, we explain the definition of
r
-cloud system as a natural framework for reducing this problem to the
\mathsf{Max}
-
k
-
\mathsf{Diameter}
problem. The formal definition and proof of the reduction are given in Section
3
.

Let
\mathcal{H}=(V,E)
be a
k
-uniform hypergraph and
(X,\textsf{dist})
be a metric
space. The goal is to produce a pointset
P\subset X
such that panchromatic
k
-coloring on
\mathcal{H}
reduces to approximating
\mathsf{Max}
-
k
-
\mathsf{Diameter}
on
P
within a factor of
r
. We start by associating each vertex
v\in V
with a set
P_{v}\subset X
, where
P\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\bigcup_{v\in V}P_{v}
and the sets
P_{v}
are pairwise disjoint. Note the contrast to the
r
-embedding framework, in which each vertex corresponded to only a single point in
X
. We will also define sets
P_{e}
for every edge
e\in E
, which we will motivate later. In the next couple of paragraphs, we will specify two conditions,
proximity
and
spread
, that we would like
P
to satisfy in order to achieve the completeness and soundness guarantees for the reduction from panchromatic
k
-coloring to
\mathsf{Max}
-
k
-
\mathsf{Diameter}
.

For the completeness guarantee of the reduction to hold, we must first show that if
\mathcal{H}
is panchromatic
k
-colorable then there is a
k
-clustering of
P
with low diameter, say at most some threshold
\beta
. Given a
k
-coloring of
\mathcal{H}
, we create a cluster for each of the
k
colors, and, for each
v\in V
, we place the entire set
P_{v}
in the cluster corresponding to the color of
v
. Since vertices in
\mathcal{H}
that are of the same color must be nonadjacent, and we would like every cluster to have diameter at most
\beta
, the proximity condition that
P
must satisfy is naturally defined as follows:

\text{For every nonadjacent }v,v^{\prime}\in V\text{ we have }\textsf{diam}(P_{v}\cup P_{v^{\prime}})\leq\beta.

(proximity)

Note that
v
and itself are also considered nonadjacent.

Second, for the soundness guarantee of the reduction to hold, we must show that if
\mathcal{H}
is not panchromatic
k
-colorable, then every
k
-clustering of
P
has large diameter—namely, at least
r\beta
. This can be enforced by showing that any
k
-clustering of
P
with diameter strictly less than
r\beta
corresponds to a panchromatic
k
-coloring of
\mathcal{H}
. A natural way of extracting a
k
-coloring of
\mathcal{H}
from a
k
-clustering of
P
is to identify each vertex
v\in V
with a designated point
\rho(v)\in P_{v}
. Given a
k
-clustering of
P
, we create a color for each of the
k
clusters, and we assign each
v\in V
the color associated with the cluster of
\rho(v)
. Then, provided that the clustering has diameter less than
r\beta
, we have the following condition: for each edge
e=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}\}\in E
, the points
\rho(v_{1}),\ldots,\rho(v_{k})
must all be in different clusters. To make the condition easier to verify, we localize it to each edge. In particular, we associate the edge
e
with a set
P_{e}\subset P
that contains the points
\rho(v_{1}),\ldots,\rho(v_{k})
. Then, the spread condition that
P
must satisfy is naturally defined as follows:

\begin{array}[]{c}\text{For every $k$-clustering of the set $P_{e}$ with diameter strictly less than $r\beta$,}\\
\text{the points $\rho(v_{1}),\ldots,\rho(v_{k})$ must all be in different clusters.}\end{array}

We say
P
is an
r
-cloud system of
\mathcal{H}
if it satisfies the above defined proximity and spread conditions. Importantly, neither condition places a restriction on individual distances within the pointset. Indeed, the final pointset
P
may contain distances in the range
(\beta,r\beta)
, allowing for more general constructions than the
r
-embedding framework.

For convenience, we further constrain the sets
P_{e}
without losing reasonable generality. First, it is most natural for
P_{e}
to be disjoint with all
P_{v}
for
v\not\in e
. To justify this, recall that whether or not an edge
e
is properly colored depends only on the colors of its constituent vertices
v\in e
. Equivalently, it is reasonable to assume
P_{e}\subseteq\bigcup_{v\in e}P_{v}
. Now, let
\tau(e,v)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=P_{e}\cap P_{v}
. It follows that
P_{e}
can be written as

P_{e}=\bigcup_{v\in e}P_{e}\cap P_{v}=\bigcup_{v\in e}\tau(e,v).

Similarly, it is reasonable to assume that
P_{v}\subseteq\bigcup_{e\in E,\,v\in e}P_{e}
. Thus,
P_{v}
can be written as

P_{v}=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}e\in E\\
v\in e\end{subarray}}P_{e}\cap P_{v}=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}e\in E\\
v\in e\end{subarray}}\tau(e,v).

Then, the entire
r
-cloud construction is characterized by the sets
\tau(e,v)
over all
e\in E
and
v\in e
. In other words, an
r
-cloud system is simply a pair of mappings
(\rho,\tau)
satisfying the proximity and spread conditions. This is the framing used in Definition
3.2
.

Since
r
-cloud systems can be used to reduce panchromatic
k
-coloring to approximate
\mathsf{Max}
-
k
-
\mathsf{Diameter}
, then by noting that the panchromatic
k
-coloring problem on
k
-uniform hypergraphs is
\mathsf{NP}
-hard for
k\geq 3
, we have the following result:

Theorem 1.3

(formalized in Theorem
3.1
)
.

For
k\geq 3
, suppose there is an efficiently computable
r
-cloud system in a metric space
(X,\textup{{dist}})
for the family of
k
-uniform hypergraphs. Then, for any
\varepsilon>0
, approximating
\mathsf{Max}
-
k
-
\mathsf{Diameter}
in
(X,\textup{{dist}})
to a factor
r-\varepsilon
is
\mathsf{NP}
-hard.

In Sections
4
and
5
, we construct a
3/2
-cloud system in the
\ell_{1}
-metric and a
1.304
-cloud system in the
\ell_{2}
-metric, both for
3
-uniform hypergraphs. This proves that for
k\geq 3
,
\mathsf{Max}
-
k
-
\mathsf{Diameter}
is hard to approximate within a factor of
3/2
in the
\ell_{1}
-metric and
1.304
in the
\ell_{2}
-metric.

Constructing a
3/2
-cloud system in the
\ell_{1}
-metric.

Let
\mathcal{H}=(V,E)
be a
3
-uniform hypergraph, and for simplicity let
m\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=|V|
be a power of
2
. We associate each vertex
v\in V
with a Hadamard codeword
\textbf{h}_{v}\in\{0,1\}^{m}
, and we define
\rho(v)=(\textbf{h}_{v},\textbf{h}_{v})\in\{0,1\}^{2m}
. For each edge
e=\{x,y,z\}
, the set
P_{e}
is then comprised of pairs of
\textbf{h}_{x},\textbf{h}_{y},\textbf{h}_{z}
and their complements. Specifically,
\tau(e,x)
consists of
5
out of the
9
pairs that can be formed using elements in
\mathinner{\left\{\textbf{h}_{x},\overline{\textbf{h}}_{y},\overline{\textbf{h}}_{z}\right\}}
, and
\tau(e,y),\tau(e,z)
are constructed symmetrically.

With this construction, it can be shown that the proximity condition holds with
\beta=m
. For any vertex
v\in V
, recall that
P_{v}
is the union of
\tau(e,v)
over all
e\in E
that contain
v
. This means that the only non-complemented codeword used by any point in
P_{v}
is
\textbf{h}_{v}
, and also that no point in
P_{v}
contains the complement
\overline{\textbf{h}}_{v}
. As a result, no two points in
P_{v}
can contain complementary codewords. By the distance property of the Hadamard code,
\textsf{diam}(P_{v})\leq m
. In the proof of Lemma
4.1
, we slightly extend this argument to show that also
\textsf{diam}(P_{v}\cup P_{v^{\prime}})\leq m
for nonadjacent
v,v^{\prime}\in V
, which is exactly the proximity condition.

Proving that our construction satisfies the spread condition is
more involved. We start by fixing an edge
e=\{x,y,z\}
. Then, we argue through casework that any
3
-clustering of
P_{\{x,y,z\}}
in which
(\textbf{h}_{x},\textbf{h}_{x})
and
(\textbf{h}_{y},\textbf{h}_{y})
are clustered together necessarily has diameter at least
3m/2
. Since our construction of
\tau(e,\cdot)
is symmetric in
(x,y,z)
, the spread property follows.

1.304
-cloud system in the
\ell_{2}
-metric.

Let
\mathcal{H}=(V,E)
be a

\tau(e,x)
to be a finite net of points on this region. The sets
\tau(e,y)
and

are formed symmetrically. An illustration of these regions is shown below in Figure

\textsf{diam}(P_{v})\leq\sqrt{2}

, we argue that
\textsf{diam}(P_{v}\cup P_{v^{\prime}})\leq\sqrt{2}

e=\{x,y,z\}
. Then, we show that any
3
-clustering of
P_{\{x,y,z\}}
in which
\textbf{e}_{x}
and
\textbf{e}_{y}
are clustered together must have diameter greater than
1.304\cdot\sqrt{2}
. Since the number of
3
-clusterings of
P_{e}
is large even for small nets
\tau(e,\cdot)
, we verify this property using a computer search.

We remark that no matter how dense of a net we choose, we cannot improve substantially on the factor
1.304
. This is because it is possible to cluster the set
P_{\{x,y,z\}}
with diameter at most
\sqrt{2+\sqrt{2}}\approx 1.307\cdot\sqrt{2}
such that
\textbf{e}_{x}
and
\textbf{e}_{y}
are in the same cluster. Geometrically, the length
\sqrt{2+\sqrt{2}}
is the distance between
\textbf{e}_{y}
and the midpoint of the arc between
\textbf{e}_{x}
and
-\textbf{e}_{y}
. Our hardness factor of
1.304
was obtained using a sufficiently dense net for which verifying the spread property was computationally tractable.

Barriers to proving hardness.

In Section
6
, we outline two barriers to showing improved hardness of approximation for
\mathsf{Max}
-
k
-
\mathsf{Diameter}
. Recall that one limitation of the
r
-embedding technique is that it forbids distances strictly between
\beta
and
r\beta
(for some
\beta>0
). Our first barrier result, formalized in Corollary
6.3
, shows that it is not possible to prove hardness above a factor of
5/3
in the
\ell_{1}
-metric with the
r
-embedding framework, or using any construction of pointsets with such a gap in the set of distances.

Our next barrier says that if a pointset
P
in the
\ell_{2}
-metric is not contained within a sphere of diameter
\Delta\cdot\sqrt{2}
, where
\Delta
is the optimal
3
-clustering diameter of
P
, then there is a polynomial time algorithm to approximate
\mathsf{Max}
-
3
-
\mathsf{Diameter}
on
P
within a factor of
\sqrt{2}-\varepsilon
. So, in a proof of hardness up to a factor of
\sqrt{2}
in the
\ell_{2}
-metric, the hard instance must effectively lie within a sphere of diameter
\Delta\cdot\sqrt{2}
. This has an interesting graph theoretic consequence: if
\mathsf{Max}
-
3
-
\mathsf{Diameter}
is hard to approximate within a factor of 2 in the
\ell_{1}
-metric or within a factor of
\sqrt{2}
in the
\ell_{2}
-metric, then the graphs associated with the hard instance must have unbounded odd girth. This is formalized in Theorems
6.5
and
6.6
.

1.3
Open Problems

A couple of open problems immediately stem from this work.

•

In this work, we proved strong inapproximability results for
\mathsf{Max}
-
k
-
\mathsf{Diameter}
(where
k
is fixed) in the Euclidean and
\ell_{1}
-metrics, but our understanding is far from tight in both these metrics, and it remains an important open problem to bridge the gap between the
\mathsf{NP}
-hardness and polynomial time approximability factors. In order to do so, some of the barriers that need to be overcome are detailed in Section
6
. However, an intriguing (intermediate) direction that is worth exploring is if we can obtain tight hardness of approximation factors for
\mathsf{Max}
-
k
-
\mathsf{Diameter}
in the Euclidean metric (or
\ell_{1}
-metric) when
k
is some large constant.

•

We believe that the techniques introduced in this paper, in particular the graph embedding to
\ell_{1}
-metric and Euclidean metric, might be useful to prove improved hardness of approximation results for other geometric problems as well. For example, in
[
CKL21
]
the authors gap reduce graph coloring problem to the continuous
k
-
\mathsf{means}
problem in high-dimensional

-metric, to obtain strong inapproximability results that are higher than the ones derived from covering problems in
[
CK19
,
CKL22
]
. We wonder if it is possible to use our embedding schemes to obtain strong hardness of approximation results for the high dimensional continuous Euclidean
k
-
\mathsf{means}

.

Preliminaries

This section contains definitions, remarks, and notation that will be used throughout this paper.

2.1
Diameter Objective

Definition 2.1

(diameter)
.

Given a metric space
(X,\textsf{dist})
and a set
C\subset X
, we say the diameter of
C
is

\textsf{diam}(C)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\max_{x,y\in C}\textsf{dist}(x,y).

For a collection of subsets
C_{1},\dots,C_{k}\subset X
, we say

\textsf{diam}(\{C_{1},\dots,C_{k}\})\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in[k]\\
x,y\in C_{i}\end{subarray}}\textsf{dist}(x,y)

is the diameter of the collection.

Definition 2.2

(
k
-clustering)
.

Given a set
P
, we say that a collection
\{C_{1},\dots C_{k}\}
is a
k
-clustering of
P
if
C_{1},\dots C_{k}
are pairwise disjoint and
C_{1}\cup\cdots\cup C_{k}=P
.

Definition 2.3

(
\mathsf{Max}
-
k
-
\mathsf{Diameter}
clustering problem)
.

Let
(X,\textsf{dist})
be a metric space and let
k
be a constant. Given as input a finite set
P\subset X
, find a
k
-clustering
\{C_{1},\dots,C_{k}\}
that minimizes
\textsf{diam}(\{C_{1},\dots,C_{k}\})
.

Definition 2.4

(
r
-approximate
\mathsf{Max}
-
k
-
\mathsf{Diameter}
clustering problem)
.

Let
(X,\textsf{dist})
be a metric space and let
k
be a constant. Given as input a finite set
P\subset X
, let

\Delta\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\min\textsf{diam}(\{C_{1},\dots,C_{k}\}),

where the minimum is taken over all possible
k
-clusterings of
P
.
Find a
k
-clustering
\{C_{1},\dots,C_{k}\}
with diameter at most
r\Delta
.

Remark 2.1
.

Let
P
be a pointset and let
p
be a point whose distance to
P
is more than
r\cdot\textsf{diam}(P)
. Then,
r
-approximate
\mathsf{Max}
-
k
-
\mathsf{Diameter}
on
P
reduces to
r
-approximate
\mathsf{Max}
-
(k+1)
-
\mathsf{Diameter}
on
P\cup\{p\}
, because any
(k+1)
-clustering whose diameter is at most
r\Delta
must have
\{p\}
as a standalone cluster. Thus, if
\mathsf{Max}
-
k
-
\mathsf{Diameter}
clustering is
\mathsf{NP}
-hard, then
\mathsf{Max}
-
(k+1)
-
\mathsf{Diameter}
is
\mathsf{NP}
-hard. In other words, if
\mathsf{Max}
-
3
-
\mathsf{Diameter}
is hard to approximate to some factor
r
, then so is
\mathsf{Max}
-
k
-
\mathsf{Diameter}
for any
k\geq 3
.

We now restate the definition of
r
-embedding mentioned in Section
1
.

Definition 2.5

(
r
-embedding)
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph and
(X,\textsf{dist})
a metric space. For
r>1
, we say a map
\varphi\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}V\to X
is an

-embedding of

with short distance

if the following two conditions hold for every

:

•

(u,v)\notin E\implies\textsf{dist}(\varphi(u),\varphi(v))\leq\beta

•

(u,v)\in E\implies\textsf{dist}(\varphi(u),\varphi(v))\geq r\beta

Definition 2.6
.

Let
k
be a constant,
r
a ratio, and
P
a pointset. Let
\Delta
be the optimal
k
-clustering diameter of
P
. Define
\Gamma_{k,r}(P)
to be the graph in which the points in

are vertices, with edges between any two points whose distance is greater than
r\Delta
.

2.2
Coloring Problems

Definition 2.7
.

A hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
consists of a set of vertices
V
and a set of hyperedges
E
, where each hyperedge
e\in E
is simply a subset
e\subseteq V
. We say that a hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
is
k
-uniform
if
|e|=k
for every
e\in E
.

We define the following coloring problem on
k
-uniform hypergraphs.

Definition 2.8

(panchromatic
k
-coloring problem)
.

Let
k
be a constant. Given a
k
-uniform hypergraph
\mathcal{H}=(V,E)
as input, find an assignment of
k
colors to the vertices of
G
such that for any hyperedge
e\in E
, all vertices in
e
are given distinct colors. Such an assignment is called a panchromatic

-coloring.

Remark 2.2
.

Note that
3
-regular graphs can be viewed as
3
-uniform
2
-regular hypergraphs: edges correspond to vertices, and triples of edges incident to the same vertex correspond to hyperedges. Since
3
-edge coloring is
\mathsf{NP}
-hard on
3
-regular graphs
[
Hol81
]
, we know that panchromatic
3
-coloring is
\mathsf{NP}
-hard on
3
-uniform hypergraphs.

2.3
Hadamard Codes

For a bit string
x\in\{0,1\}^{m}
, we denote its bitwise complement by
\overline{x}
.

Definition 2.9

(Hadamard code)
.

Let
m
be a power of 2. There exists a subset of
\{0,1\}^{m}
of size
m
such that the Hamming distance between distinct elements is exactly
m/2
. We call this a Hadamard code and denote it by
\text{Had}_{m}^{+}
. We also define
\text{Had}_{m}^{-}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\{\overline{\textbf{h}}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\textbf{h}\in\text{Had}_{m}^{+}\}
, which can alternatively be seen as an affine shift of
\text{Had}_{m}^{+}
by the vector
\textbf{1}=(1,1,\ldots,1)
in the vector space
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{m}
. Then, let

\widetilde{\text{Had}}_{m}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\text{Had}_{m}^{+}\cup\text{Had}_{m}^{-}.

Given
\textbf{h}_{i},\textbf{h}_{j}\in\widetilde{\text{Had}}_{m}
, we will denote the concatenation of
\textbf{h}_{i}
and
\textbf{h}_{j}
by
(\textbf{h}_{i},\textbf{h}_{j})\in\{0,1\}^{2m}
.

Remark 2.3
.

The set
\widetilde{\text{Had}}_{m}
has the following property: for every
\textbf{h}\in\widetilde{\text{Had}}_{m}
, there is exactly one element in
\widetilde{\text{Had}}_{m}
that is distance
m
away, namely
\overline{\textbf{h}}
. All other elements have distance
m/2
to
h
.

3
The
r
-cloud System

In this section, we introduce a geometric object that can be used to prove inapproximability results for the
\mathsf{Max}
-
k
-
\mathsf{Diameter}
problem.

Definition 3.1
.

For a hypergraph
\mathcal{H}=(V,E)
, let
I_{\mathcal{H}}\subset E\times V
denote its set of incidences:

I_{\mathcal{H}}=\{(e,v)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}e\in E,\,v\in e\}.

Definition 3.2

(
r
-cloud system)
.

Let
(X,\textsf{dist})
be a metric space and
U\subseteq X
be a finite set of points. Let
\mathcal{H}=(V,E)
be a
k
-uniform hypergraph. Suppose that
(\rho,\tau)
is a pair of mappings for which
\rho\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}V\to U
is injective, and
\tau\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}I_{\mathcal{H}}\to\mathcal{P}(U)
satisfies
\rho(v)\in\tau(e,v)
for all
(e,v)\in I_{\mathcal{H}}
. For
r>1
, we say that
(U,\rho,\tau)
is an
r
-cloud system of
\mathcal{H}
if the following properties hold for some
\beta>0
:

•

(Proximity)
For
v\in V
, define

P_{v}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}e\in E\\
(e,v)\in I_{\mathcal{H}}\end{subarray}}\tau(e,v).

For any nonadjacent
v,v^{\prime}\in V
, we have
\textsf{diam}(P_{v}\cup P_{v^{\prime}})\leq\beta
. Note that we consider
v
to be nonadjacent to itself.

•

(Spread)
For
e\in E
, define

P_{e}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\bigcup_{v\in e}\tau(e,v)

Let
e=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\}\in E
. For any
k
-clustering of
P_{e}
with diameter strictly less than
r\beta
, the points
\rho(v_{1}),\dots,\rho(v_{k})\in P_{e}
are all in different clusters.

We refer to
\beta
as the
short distance
of
(U,\rho,\tau)
. Furthermore, we say that an
r
-cloud system
(U,\rho,\tau)
is
efficiently computable
if there are
\poly(|V|)
-time algorithms to identify
U
and evaluate
\rho
and
\tau
.

The existence of an efficiently computable
r
-cloud system gives a polynomial time reduction from panchromatic
k
-coloring to
\mathsf{Max}
-
k
-
\mathsf{Diameter}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
be a constant and let
\mathcal{H}=(V,E)
be a
k
-uniform hypergraph. Suppose
\mathcal{H}
has an efficiently computable
r
-cloud system
(U,\rho,\tau)
with short distance
\beta
, where
U\subseteq X
for a metric space
(X,d)
. Let

P\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\bigcup_{(e,v)\in I_{\mathcal{H}}}\ \tau(e,v)=\bigcup_{v\in V}P_{v}=\bigcup_{e\in E}P_{e}.

Then, there is a polynomial time algorithm which takes as input
\mathcal{H}
and outputs
P
with the following guarantees:

•

Completeness:
If
\mathcal{H}
is panchromatic
k
-colorable, then there exists a
k
-clustering of
P
with diameter at most
\beta
.

•

Soundness:
If
\mathcal{H}
is not panchromatic
k
-colorable, then any
k
-clustering of
P
has diameter at least
r\beta
.

Proof.

We note that, since
|I_{\mathcal{H}}|\leq n^{k}
, the pointset
P
is computable in polynomial time.

We first prove completeness. Let
\mathcal{H}
be panchromatic
k
-colorable, and let
c\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}V\to[k]
be a panchromatic
k
-coloring of
\mathcal{H}
. We define a
k
-clustering
\{C_{1},\ldots,C_{k}\}
of
P
given by

C_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\bigcup_{v\>\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\>c(v)=i}P_{v}.

To show that this clustering has diameter at most
\beta
, it suffices to show that for any
i\in[k]
and
x,y\in C_{i}
, we have
d(x,y)\leq\beta
. This follows from the observation that
x\in P_{v}
and
y\in P_{v^{\prime}}
for some
v,v^{\prime}\in V
, and since
c(v)=c(v^{\prime})
, either
v,v^{\prime}
are nonadjacent or
v=v^{\prime}
. Applying the proximity property of Definition

gives

Now, we prove soundness. Suppose

-coloring

. Now, let

∎

By Remark

-

-
\mathsf{Diameter}
.

. Denote the clusters

and for

, let

if

deciding ties arbitrarily with a single exception: we force that

, which is possible since

. It can be checked that for any

has diameter at most

clustered together, this confirms that the spread property does not hold.

Definition 6.1

Let

It turns out that there is a connection between

Theorem 6.1

Let

6.2

In this section, we show a better than

-approximation algorithm for
\mathsf{Max}
-

-
\mathsf{Diameter}

We start by formally describing the

-

-

mentioned in Section

. The natural setting of this algorithm is the closely related

Definition 6.3

(

Let

be a metric space and

. Given as input a finite set

Theorem 6.4

(follows from
[

]
)
.

Let

and

-approximate

From

-approximation of Euclidean

. By Jung’s theorem

, any set of points with diameter

-

\mathsf{Diameter}
of the given set of points.
Applying Jung’s theorem, the optimal clustering for the

Therefore,

In the case of

.

. Importantly, Theorem

has implications for the graph

introduced in Definition

Definition 6.4
.

Given a graph

is the length of the smallest odd cycle. If there are no odd cycles, we say that the odd girth is infinite.

Theorem 6.6
.

For any

, there exists an

such that
(\sqrt{2}-\varepsilon)
-approximate
\mathsf{Max}
-

Proof.

By scaling, we may assume without loss of generality that the optimal

-clustering diameter of

is 1. We will prove that for small enough

is at most

So, suppose for contradiction that

is a pointset contained in a ball of diameter
\sqrt{2}+\varepsilon
and the odd girth of

is at most
g
. We can assume that this ball is centered at the origin. Since the optimal diameter is

, all edges in
\Gamma_{k,\sqrt{2}-\varepsilon}(P)
correspond to distances of at least

. Let

be arbitrary adjacent vertices and let

be their corresponding points. Note that

for small enough

By assumption, there must be an odd cycle

For small enough

Appendix A
Testing
r
-embeddability with a Linear Program

It turns out finding the largest ratio
r
for which a given graph
H
is
r
-embeddable into
(\{0,1\}^{m},\ell_{1})
can be framed as the solution to a linear program. To see this, let
H=(V,E)
be a graph on
n
vertices labeled
v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{n}
, and let
\varphi
be an
r
-embedding of
H
into the space
\{0,1\}^{m}
, with short distance
\beta
. For each
w\in\{0,1\}^{n}
, associate a nonnegative integer
x_{w}
indicating the number of indices
i\in[m]
such that
(\varphi(v_{j}))_{i}=w_{j}
holds for all
j\in[n]
. Then, distances in our embedded space can be written as the following linear combination:

\left\lVert\varphi(v_{a})-\varphi(v_{b})\right\rVert_{0}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in\{0,1\}^{n}\\
w_{a}\neq w_{b}\end{subarray}}x_{w}.

Then, for a given short distance parameter
\beta
, finding the largest possible embeddability ratio
r
is equivalent to solving the following optimization problem over nonnegative integer variables
\{x_{w}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}w\in\{0,1\}^{n}\}
and a rational variable
r
:

\begin{array}[]{ll@{}ll}\text{maximize}&r&\\
\text{subject to}&\begin{cases}\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in\{0,1\}^{n}\\
w_{a}\neq w_{b}\end{subarray}}x_{w}\geq r\beta&\text{if }(v_{a},v_{b})\in E\\
\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in\{0,1\}^{n}\\
w_{a}\neq w_{b}\end{subarray}}x_{w}\leq\beta&\text{if }(v_{a},v_{b})\notin E\end{cases}\end{array}

We can remove dependence on
s
by simply setting
\beta=1
and allowing each
x_{w}
to take any nonnegative rational value, obtaining the following linear program:

\begin{array}[]{ll@{}ll}\text{maximize}&r&\\
\text{subject to}&\begin{cases}\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in\{0,1\}^{n}\\
w_{a}\neq w_{b}\end{subarray}}x_{w}\geq r&\text{if }(v_{a},v_{b})\in E\\
\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in\{0,1\}^{n}\\
w_{a}\neq w_{b}\end{subarray}}x_{w}\leq 1&\text{if }(v_{a},v_{b})\notin E\end{cases}\end{array}

Moreover, since each of our constraints have integer coefficients, the solution vector to this linear program will be rational. Thus, we can scale each
x_{w}
to be an integer, thus obtaining an embedding into
\{0,1\}^{m}
that achieves the optimal ratio
r
, where
m=\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in\{0,1\}^{n}\end{subarray}}x_{w}\in\mathbb{N}
.

In particular, for a given graph
H
and parameter
r>1
, this gives us a computational way to check whether or not
H
is
r
-embeddable in the Hamming metric. That said, the number of constraints in the linear program is
\Omega(2^{n})
, meaning this method is only tractable when
n=|H|
is relatively small.

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{3\alpha^{2}}{4}+2\alpha\sqrt{2}+\frac{\alpha\varepsilon^{\prime}}{2}+2-\frac{{\varepsilon^{\prime}}^{2}}{4}}

Hence, it suffices to show that:

(\sqrt{2}-\varepsilon)^{2}=2-2\varepsilon\sqrt{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\geq\frac{3\alpha^{2}}{4}+\left(2\sqrt{2}+\frac{\varepsilon^{\prime}}{2}\right)\cdot\alpha+2-\frac{{\varepsilon^{\prime}}^{2}}{4}

From here, we provide a sequence of stronger inequalities, eventually showing that our setting of
\varepsilon^{\prime}=60\varepsilon^{1/4}
is satisfies the above inequality. First, recall that
\alpha\leq\sqrt{2\varepsilon\sqrt{2}-\varepsilon^{2}}
. Thus, it is sufficient to have

2-2\varepsilon\sqrt{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\geq\frac{6\varepsilon\sqrt{2}-3\varepsilon^{2}}{4}+\left(2\sqrt{2}+\frac{\varepsilon^{\prime}}{2}\right)\sqrt{2\varepsilon\sqrt{2}-\varepsilon^{2}}+2-\frac{{\varepsilon^{\prime}}^{2}}{4}

Subtracting
(2+\frac{6\varepsilon\sqrt{2}-3\varepsilon^{2}}{4})
from both sides, it is equivalent that

\frac{7}{4}(\varepsilon^{2}-2\varepsilon\sqrt{2})\geq\left(2\sqrt{2}+\frac{\varepsilon^{\prime}}{4}\right)\sqrt{2\varepsilon\sqrt{2}-\varepsilon^{2}}-\frac{{\varepsilon^{\prime}}^{2}}{4}

Applying the trivial bound
\varepsilon^{2}\geq 0
, it is sufficient that

-\frac{7\varepsilon}{\sqrt{2}}\geq\left(2\sqrt{2}+\frac{\varepsilon^{\prime}}{2}\right)\sqrt{2\varepsilon\sqrt{2}}-\frac{{\varepsilon^{\prime}}^{2}}{4}

For
\varepsilon^{\prime}\leq 1
, we have
\left(2\sqrt{2}+\frac{\varepsilon^{\prime}}{2}\right)\sqrt{2\sqrt{2}}<6
, so it would be sufficient if

-\frac{7\varepsilon}{\sqrt{2}}\geq 6\sqrt{\varepsilon}-\frac{{\varepsilon^{\prime}}^{2}}{4}.

Rearranging, we have:

{\varepsilon^{\prime}}^{2}\geq 24\sqrt{\varepsilon}+\frac{28\varepsilon}{\sqrt{2}}.

Finally, for
\varepsilon\leq 1
it is true that
\varepsilon\leq\sqrt{\varepsilon}
, and since
24+\frac{28}{\sqrt{2}}<45
, it is good enough to have

{\varepsilon^{\prime}}^{2}\geq 45\sqrt{\varepsilon}

This is satisfied when
\varepsilon^{\prime}=45\varepsilon^{1/4}.
Note that our argument relied on
\varepsilon\leq 1
,
\varepsilon^{\prime}\leq 1
and
\varepsilon^{\prime}\geq 2\varepsilon
all holding. One can verify that for
\varepsilon^{\prime}=45\varepsilon^{1/4}
, all of these bounds are true when
\varepsilon
is sufficiently small.

In both cases, then, we produced an algorithm which outputs a
3
-clustering with diameter at most
\sqrt{2}-\varepsilon
. Since the optimal
3
-clustering has diameter
\Delta=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{2}+\varepsilon}
, we achieve an approximation ratio of:

\frac{\sqrt{2}-\varepsilon}{\Delta}=\frac{(\sqrt{2}-\varepsilon)(\sqrt{2}+\varepsilon)}{\sqrt{2}}=\frac{2-\varepsilon^{2}}{\sqrt{2}}=\sqrt{2}-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{\sqrt{2}}.

We have demonstrated a
(\sqrt{2}-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{\sqrt{2}})
-approximation algorithm for pointsets not contained in a bounding sphere of diameter
\Delta\cdot\left(\sqrt{2}+45\varepsilon^{1/4}\right)
. By a change of variables
\varepsilon\mapsto 2^{1/4}\sqrt{\varepsilon}
, this gives a
(\sqrt{2}-\varepsilon)
-approximation for pointsets not contained in a bounding sphere with diameter

\Delta\cdot\left(\sqrt{2}+45\left(2^{1/4}\sqrt{\varepsilon}\right)^{1/4}\right)=\Delta\cdot\left(\sqrt{2}+45\cdot 2^{1/16}\varepsilon^{1/8}\right).

Since
45\cdot 2^{1/16}<50
, this algorithm also holds for pointsets not contained in a bounding sphere with diameter
\Delta\cdot\left(\sqrt{2}+50\varepsilon^{1/8}\right)
, which is what we wanted to show.

∎

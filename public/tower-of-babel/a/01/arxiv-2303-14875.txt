Regularity of pullback attractors for nonclassical diffusion equations with delay


Abstract
In this paper, we mainly study the regularity of pullback 𝒟 \mathcal{D} -attractors for a nonautonomous nonclassical diffusion equation with delay term b ⁡ ( t , u t ) b(t,u_{t}) which contains some hereditary characteristics. Under a critical nonlinearity f f , a time-dependent force g ⁡ ( t , x ) g(t,x) with exponential growth and a delayed force term b ⁡ ( t , u t ) b(t,u_{t}) , we prove that there exists a pullback 𝒟 \mathcal{D} -attractor 𝒜 = { A ⁡ ( t ) : t ∈ ℝ } \mathcal{A}=\{A(t):t\in\mathbb{R}\} in 𝕂 1 = H 0 1 ​ ( Ω ) × L 2 ​ ( ( − h , 0 ) , L 2 ​ ( Ω ) ) \mathbb{K}^{1}=H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times L^{2}((-h,0);L^{2}(\Omega)) to problem ( 1.1 ) and for each t ∈ ℝ t\in\mathbb{R} , A ⁡ ( t ) A(t) is bounded in 𝕂 2 = H 2 ​ ( Ω ) ∩ H 0 1 ​ ( Ω ) × L 2 ​ ( ( − h , 0 ) , L 2 ​ ( Ω ) ) \mathbb{K}^{2}=H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times L^{2}((-h,0);L^{2}(\Omega)) .

In this paper, we consider the following nonclassical diffusion equation with delay:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u(t,x)-\Delta\partial_{t}u(t,x)-\Delta u(t,x)+f(u(t,x))=b(t,u_{t})(x)+g(t,x)&in\ (\tau,\infty)\times\Omega,\\
u=0&on\ (\tau,\infty)\times\partial\Omega,\\
u(\tau,x)=u^{0}(x)&\tau\in\mathbb{R},x\in\Omega,\\
u(\tau+\theta,x)=\phi(\theta,x)&\theta\in(-h,0),x\in\Omega,\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
is a bounded domain with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
.
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
,
u^{0}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
is the initial condition at
\tau
and
\phi\in L^{2}(\left[-h,0\right];L^{2}(\Omega))
is also the initial condition in
\left[\tau-h,\tau\right]
,
h(>0)
is the length of the delay effects. Moreover, for each
t\geq\tau
, we denote by
u_{t}
the function defined in
\left[-h,0\right]
by
u_{t}(\theta)=u(t+\theta),\,\theta\in\left[-h,0\right]
. The nonlinearity
f
and the external forces
b
and
g
meet certain criteria later.

We assume the following conditions to hold (cf.
[
8
,
19
]
):

\left(\mathbf{H1}\right)
The nonlinear term
f\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
with
f(0)=0
, satisfies the following growth and dissipation conditions:

\left|f(u)-f(v)\right|\leq C\left|u-v\right|(1+\left|u\right|^{4}+\left|v\right|^{4}),

(1.2)

\lim_{\left|u\right|\to\infty}\inf\frac{f(u)}{u}>-\lambda_{1}.

(1.3)

According to (
1.2
) and (
1.3
), it can be inferred that
f(u)
satisfies the following inequalities:

f(u)u\geq-\mu u^{2}-C_{1},

(1.4)

f^{\prime}(u)\geq-l,

(1.5)

\left|f(u)\right|\leq C_{2}(1+\left|u\right|^{5}),

(1.6)

\lim_{\left|u\right|\to\infty}\inf\frac{uf(u)-kF(u)}{u^{2}}\geq 0,

(1.7)

\lim_{\left|u\right|\to\infty}\inf\frac{F(u)}{u^{2}}\geq 0,

(1.8)

where
\mu
,
l
,
k
,
C_{1}
,
C_{2}
are positive constants,
\lambda_{1}>0
is the first eigenvalue of
-\Delta
in
\Omega
with the homogeneous Dirichlet condition such that

.

Let us denote by

F(u):=\int_{0}^{u}f(s){\rm d}s.

We infer from equation (
1.7
) and (
1.8
) that for any
\delta>0
, there exist positive constants
C_{\delta 1},C_{\delta 2}
such that

uf(u)-kF(u)+\delta u^{2}+C_{\delta 1}\geq 0\quad\forall u\in\mathbb{R},

(1.9)

F(u)+\delta u^{2}+C_{\delta 2}\geq 0\quad\forall u\in\mathbb{R}.

(1.10)

\left(\mathbf{H2}\right)
In terms of the time-dependent external force term without delay, we assume that
L^{2}_{loc}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X})
is the space of functions
g(s)
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
with values in
\mathbb{X}
and locally square integrable in time, that is,

\int_{a}^{b}\left\|g(s)\right\|^{2}_{\mathbb{X}}ds<\infty,\quad\forall\left[a,b\right]\subset\mathbb{R}.

\left(\mathbf{H3}\right)
We assume that the operator
b(\cdot,\cdot)
is well-defined as
b:\mathbb{R}\times L^{2}((-h,0);L^{2}(\Omega))\to L^{2}(\Omega)
which is a time-dependent external force with delay, and it satisfies:

(I) for all
\phi\in L^{2}((-h,0);L^{2}(\Omega))
, the function

is measurable;

(II)
b(t,0)=0
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}

(III) there exists a constant

From above assumptions, for

Many scholars have studied the nonclassical reaction diffusion equation, which was developed as a model to describe physical phenomena such as fluid mechanics, solid mechanics, non-Newtonian flows, and heat conduction theory (
[
1
,
4
]
). This type of equation has been widely used in physics, chemistry, biology, and other fields in recent years. Meanwhile, the long-time behavior and well-posedness of the solutions of the nonclassical reaction diffusion equation have been investigated (
[
2
,
15
,
3
,
12
,
13
,
22
]
, etc.).

It is worth highlighting that the existence and regularity of attractors of related dynamic equations have been examined by numerous researcher under distinct settings. The authors in
[
18
,
21
,
11
]
have established the regularity of global attractors for the nonclassical diffusion equations. With reference to the pullback attractors, the existence as well as regularity for a wide class of non-autonomous, fractional, nonclassical diffusion equations with
(p,q)
-growth nonlinearities defined on unbounded domains have been obatained in
[
14
]
. The regularity for a nonautonomous nonclassical diffusion equation with critical nonlinearity have been proved in
[
19
]
and the existence pullback attractors for a nonautonomous nonclassical diffusion equation with delay have been proved in
[
8
]
. But, the regularity of pullback attractors for a nonautonomous nonclassical diffusion equation with delay was not studied in
[
8
]
and
[
19
]
. Thus it is natural to study this problem in this paper.

The main aim in this paper is to prove the regularity of pullback
\mathcal{D}
-attractors
\mathcal{A}=\{A(t):t\in\mathbb{R}\}
for problem (
1.1
). Particularly, we will prove that there exists a pullback
\mathcal{D}
-attractors
\mathcal{A}=\{A(t):t\in\mathbb{R}\}
in
\mathbb{K}^{1}=H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times L^{2}((-h,0);L^{2}(\Omega))
by weaks solutions of problem (
1.1
). More specifically,
A(t)
is bounded in
\mathbb{K}^{2}=H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times L^{2}((-h,0);L^{2}(\Omega))
for each
t\in\mathbb{R}
. In order to obatin the regularity of pullback arrtactors for problem (
1.1
), by extending the methods in
[
19
,
6
,
5
,
7
,
20
]
, we split the solution of the problem into two parts: the first part satisfies exponentially decay by employing priori estimation techniques, and the second part satisfies suitable asymptotic behavior in some phase space with higher regularity by giving the operator
-\Delta
a proper fractional power. Because of the term

, we know that if the intial datas belong to
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, the solution for problem (

and has no higher regularity, which is similar to the hyperbolic equation (
[
8
,
19
]
). Furthermore, the time-delay term forces us to consider the past history of the solution. To overcome these difficulties casued by the term

and time-delay term, by generalizing the methods in
[
8
]
and
[

4.2

\displaystyle\leq\left((\|w(\tau)\|^{2}_{1/3}+\|w(\tau)\|^{2}_{4/3})+\int_{\tau}^{t}C(Q(s,s-\tau)+1){\rm d}s\right.

\displaystyle\quad\left.+C\int_{\tau}^{t}\|g(s)\|^{2}_{-2/3}{\rm d}s+C\int_{\tau}^{t}\|b(s,w_{s})\|^{2}{\rm d}s\right)e^{\int_{\tau}^{t}(h(s,s-\tau)-\delta_{2}){\rm d}s}.

Due to (
4.8
),(II) and (IV), we observe that

e^{\int_{\tau}^{t}h(s,s-\tau){\rm d}s}\leq e^{(G^{4}_{-2/3}(t)+1)\int_{\tau}^{t}Ce^{-2\delta_{1}(t-\tau)}{\rm d}s}\leq e^{C(G^{4}_{-2/3}(t)+1)},

(4.14)

(4.15)

(4.16)

We now can find that

Substituting (

)-(
4.16
) into (

), we derive that

\displaystyle\|w(t)\|^{2}_{4/3}

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-\delta_{2}(t-\tau)}e^{C(G^{4}_{-2/3}(t)+1)}\|w(\tau)\|^{2}_{4/3}

\displaystyle\quad+C(1+Q(t,t-\tau)+G^{2}_{-2/3}(t))e^{C(G^{4}_{-2/3}(t)+1)}.

(4.17)

Combining (
4.8
) and (

) and using

\displaystyle\|P_{x_{\tau}}(t,\tau)z_{\tau}\|^{2}_{4/3}

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-\delta_{2}(t-\tau)}e^{C(G^{4}_{-2/3}(t)+1)}\|w(\tau)\|^{2}_{4/3}+C\|w(\tau)\|^{2}_{4/3}

\displaystyle+C(1+Q(t,t-\tau)+G^{2}_{-2/3}(t))e^{C(G^{4}_{-2/3}(t)+1)}+C\left(t-\tau+1\right)\left(G^{30}_{-1}(t)+1\right).

Then, we can conclude that

\|P_{x_{\tau}}(t,\tau)z_{\tau}\|_{4/3}\leq\beta(t,t-\tau)\|z_{\tau}\|_{4/3}+\alpha(t,t-\tau),

(4.18)

where

\alpha(t,t-\tau)=C(1+Q^{1/2}(t,t-\tau)+G_{-2/3}(t))e^{C(G^{4}_{-2/3}(t)+1)},

for some
C>0
large enough.

Since

and

, we know that
\beta(\cdot,\cdot)\in\widehat{\mathfrak{D}}
and
\alpha(\cdot,\cdot)\in\widehat{\mathfrak{I}}
. Hence, taking
\tau=t-r(r\geq 0)
and

in (
4.18
), it is easy to check that assumption
iii)
of Lemma
2.8
is satisfied.

Let
T_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
be arbitrary fixed. Recall that the family
\widehat{B}=\{B(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is pullback
\mathcal{D}
-abosorbing and
G_{-2/3}(\cdot)\in\mathfrak{I}
, we can choose

large enough such that

U(t,t-r)B(t-r)\subseteq B(t),\quad\forall r\geq r_{\ast},\ t\leq T_{0},

(4.19)

and the following estimates hold ture

\gamma(r_{\ast})=Ce^{-\frac{\delta_{1}r_{\ast}}{2}}<1,

(4.20)

\beta(t,r_{\ast})=Ce^{-\frac{\delta_{2}r_{\ast}}{2}}e^{C(G^{4}_{-2/3}(t)+1)}\leq\beta(T_{0},r_{\ast})=Ce^{-\frac{\delta_{2}r_{\ast}}{2}}e^{C(G^{4}_{-2/3}(T_{0})+1)}<1,\quad\forall t\leq T_{0},

(4.21)

\frac{1}{1-Ce^{-\frac{\delta_{2}r_{\ast}}{2}}e^{C(G^{4}_{-2/3}(t)+1)}}\leq 2,\quad\forall t\leq T_{0}.

(4.22)

From (
2.1
), setting

\displaystyle\rho_{r_{\ast}}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1+\beta(t,0)-\beta(t,r_{\ast})}{1-\beta(t,r_{\ast})}\alpha(t,r_{\ast})

\displaystyle=\frac{C\left(1+e^{C(G^{4}_{-2/3}(t)+1)}-e^{-\frac{\delta_{2}r_{\ast}}{2}}e^{C(G^{4}_{-2/3}(t)+1)}\right)}{1-Ce^{-\frac{\delta_{2}r_{\ast}}{2}}e^{C(G^{4}_{-2/3}(t)+1)}}\alpha(t,r_{\ast}),

) and

, it follows that

(4.23)

where

\hat{\rho}_{r_{\ast}}(t)=C(1+e^{C(G^{4}_{-2/3}(t)+1)})\alpha(t,r_{\ast}),

for some
C>0
large enough.

From the invariant property of the pullback
\mathcal{D}
-attractor
\mathcal{A}=\{A(t):t\in\mathbb{R}\}
and recall that

, it deduces that

A(t)=U(t,t-r)A(t-r)\subseteq U(t,t-r)B(t-r),\quad\forall t\in\mathbb{R},\ \forall r\geq 0.

(4.24)

Then, combining (
4.23
) and (
4.24
), Lemma
2.8
yields for each

,

\displaystyle dist_{\mathbb{K}^{1}}(A(t),\overline{\mathbf{B}}_{\mathbb{K}^{4/3}}(\hat{\rho}_{r_{\ast}}(t)))

\displaystyle\leq dist_{\mathbb{K}^{1}}(U(t,t-r)D(t-r),\overline{\mathbf{B}}_{\mathbb{K}^{4/3}}(\hat{\rho}_{r_{\ast}}(t)))

\displaystyle\leq Q_{r_{\ast}}(\gamma(r_{\ast}))^{\frac{r}{r_{\ast}}}R_{0}(t-r)

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-\frac{\delta_{2}(r-r_{\ast})}{2}}R_{0}(t-r)

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-\frac{\delta_{2}(r-r_{\ast})}{2}}(G_{-1}(t-r)+1)\to 0,\quad\text{as}\ r\to+\infty.

Thus

A(t)\subseteq\overline{\mathbf{B}}_{\mathbb{K}^{4/3}}(\hat{\rho}_{r_{\ast}}(t))\subseteq\overline{\mathbf{B}}_{\mathbb{K}^{4/3}}(\hat{\rho}_{r_{\ast}}(T_{0})),\quad\forall t\leq T_{0}.

The proof of the lemma is thus finished.
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

Let assumptions
\left(\mathbf{H1}\right)-\left(\mathbf{H3}\right)

If

(4.25)

(4.26)

Proof.

We split the solution

(4.29)

and

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}w+A\partial_{t}w+Aw=\Psi&in\ (\tau,\infty)\times\Omega,\\

w(\tau+\theta,x)=\phi(\theta,x)&\theta\in(-h,0),x\in\Omega,\end{cases}

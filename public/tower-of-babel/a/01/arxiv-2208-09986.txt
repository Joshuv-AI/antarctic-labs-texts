Non–regular McKean–Vlasov equations and calibration problem in local stochastic volatility models The author thanks Daniel Lacker and Nizar Touzi for their helpful suggestions and interesting discussions.
Mao Fabrice Djete

Abstract
In order to deal with the question of the existence of a calibrated local stochastic volatility model in finance, we investigate a class of McKean–Vlasov equations where a minimal continuity assumption is imposed on the coefficients. Namely, the drift coefficient and, in particular, the volatility coefficient are not necessarily continuous in the measure variable for the Wasserstein topology. In this paper, we provide an existence result and show an approximation by N N –particle system or propagation of chaos for this type of McKean–Vlasov equations. As a direct result, we are able to deduce the existence of a calibrated local stochastic volatility model for an appropriate choice of stochastic volatility parameters. The associated propagation of chaos result is also proved.

Reminder on functional spaces

We start by recalling some functional spaces and their properties. Let

The open ball of

with radius

(thus of diameter

. As already mentioned in the previous section, we use Einstein notation i.e. in expressions like

and

the standard summation rule with respect to repeated indices will be meant.

Given an open set

and a Banach space

, for a nonnegative measure

and

, the symbols

or

denote the space of equivalence classes of
\mu

such that the function

is
\mu
–integrable. This space is equipped with the standard norm

\displaystyle\|f\|_{q}:=\|f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega,\mu;E)}:=\Big(\int_{\Omega}|f(\boldsymbol{x})|^{q}_{E}\;\mu(\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x})\Big)^{1/q}.

We say

if for any compact

we have

is
\mu
–integrable. When

for

or when

is obvious, we will simply write

or

. Also, the notation

or

always refers to the situation where

is the classical Lebesgue measure. As usual, for
q\in[1,\infty]
we set

the conjugate of

by

q^{\prime}:=\frac{q}{q-1}.

We denote by

or

the Sobolev class of all functions

whose generalized partial derivatives
\partial_{x_{i}}f
are in

. A generalized (or Sobolev) derivative is defined by the equality (the integration by parts formula)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}f(\boldsymbol{x})\partial_{x_{i}}\varphi(\boldsymbol{x})\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}=-\int_{\Omega}\partial_{x_{i}}f(\boldsymbol{x})\varphi(\boldsymbol{x})\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x},\;\;\mbox{for all}\;\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega).

This space is equipped with the Sobolev norm

\displaystyle\|f\|_{q,1}:=\|f\|_{H^{q,1}(\Omega)}:=\Big(\|f\|^{q}_{q}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\|\partial_{x_{i}}f\|^{q}_{q}\Big)^{1/q}.

The class
W^{q,1}_{0}(\Omega)
is defined as the closure of
C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
in
W^{q,1}(\Omega).

Let
U_{R}
be an open ball of diameter
R
. The dual of the space
W^{q^{\prime},1}_{0}(U_{R})
is denoted
W^{q,-1}(U_{R})
or
H^{q,-1}(U_{R}).
When

is a map, we will say

whenever the linear map
W^{q^{\prime},1}_{0}(U_{R})\ni v\to\langle u,v\rangle\in\mathbb{R}
is well defined and continuous. We refer to
[
2
, Chapter 3]
for an overview on the dual of Sobolev spaces.

Let
J\subset[0,T]
be an interval (open or closed) and
U
be an open set in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Let
\mathbb{H}^{q,1}(J\times U)
denote the space of all measurable functions

on the set

such that

for almost all

and the norm

is finite. The space
\mathbb{H}^{q,1}_{0}(J\times U)
is defined similarly, but with
H^{q,1}_{0}(U)
in place of

, and
\mathbb{H}^{q^{\prime},-1}(J\times U)
denotes its dual.

This is enough to take
\varphi

We set

in the following way: for any

\displaystyle{\cal L}u(\varphi)

\displaystyle:=\int_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\partial_{t}u(t,\boldsymbol{x})\varphi(t,\boldsymbol{x})-a^{i,j}_{0}(t)\partial_{x_{i}}u(t,\boldsymbol{x})\partial_{x_{j}}\varphi(t,\boldsymbol{x})\;\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\;\mathrm{d}t,

is a Borel map satisfying

. The next result is essentially a reformulation of
[
17

(see also
[
5

for the dual norm indicated in the proposition below).

Proposition A.1
.

Let
\boldsymbol{f}:=(f^{i})_{1\leq i\leq d}:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
g:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be Borel maps. The map
u
is s.t.
u(0,\cdot)=0
and for any
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}([0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\displaystyle{\cal L}u(\varphi)=-\int_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}f^{i}(t,\boldsymbol{x})\partial_{x_{i}}\varphi(t,\boldsymbol{x})+g(t,\boldsymbol{x})\varphi(t,\boldsymbol{x})\;\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\;\mathrm{d}t.

Then, there exists a positive constant
N
depending only on
T
,
d
,
r
,
M
and
m
s.t.

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathbb{H}^{r,1}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq N\;\Big[\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{L^{r}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{r}+\|g\|_{L^{r}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{r}\Big]^{1/r}=N\;\|{\cal L}u\|_{\mathbb{H}^{r,-1}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Now, let

be a map s.t. there exists

satisfying

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u(t,\boldsymbol{x})|\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\leq G,\;\;\mbox{for all a.e.}\;t\in[0,T].

Proposition A.2
.

If
1\leq r<d
, one has

\displaystyle\|u\|^{\gamma}_{L^{\gamma}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C(d,r)^{r}G^{r/d}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{r}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{r}

where
\gamma:=r\frac{d+1}{d}
and
C(d,r)
is a positive constant appearing in the Sobolev embedding Theorem depending only on
r
and
d
.

Proof.

As
1\leq r<d
, by Gagliardo–Nirenberg–Sobolev inequality (Sobolev embedding Theorem), there exists
C(d,r)>0
s.t. for each
t\in[0,T],

\displaystyle\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{r^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C(d,r)\|\nabla u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\;\;\mbox{where}\;r^{*}:=\frac{dr}{d-r}.

Let
a>1
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u(t,\boldsymbol{x})|^{\ell}\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u(t,\boldsymbol{x})|^{\ell-1/a}|u(t,\boldsymbol{x})|^{1/a}\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\leq G^{1/a}\bigg[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u(t,\boldsymbol{x})|^{(\ell-1/a)a^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}\bigg]^{1/a^{\prime}}=G^{1/a}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{(\ell-1/a)a^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\frac{(\ell-1/a)a^{\prime}}{a^{\prime}}}.

We want

and
r=\gamma-1/a
. This leads to

and
\ell=r\frac{d+1}{d}
. Therefore

\displaystyle\|u\|^{\ell}_{L^{\ell}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\int_{0}^{T}\|u(t,\cdot)\|^{r}_{L^{r^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\mathrm{d}t\leq C(d,r)^{r}G^{p/r}\|\nabla u\|^{r}_{L^{r}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

∎

Proposition A.3
.

If
d<r
, for any

, one has

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{C^{0,1-d/r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{r}\mathrm{d}t\leq C(d,r)^{r}\|u\|^{r}_{\mathbb{H}^{r,1}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}

and

\displaystyle\|u\|^{\ell}_{L^{\ell}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq G^{1/s^{\prime}}C(d,r)^{r/s^{\prime}}\|u\|^{r/s}_{L^{r}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|u\|_{\mathbb{H}^{r,1}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{r/s^{\prime}}

Since

Let

\displaystyle\|u\|^{\ell}_{L^{\ell}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}

Proposition A.4

For any

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rm exp}^{\alpha|z_{0}|^{2}}q(0,z_{0})^{r}\mathrm{d}z_{0}<\infty

Topological entropy for countable Markov shifts and Exel–Laca algebras
Yuta Michimoto, Yushi Nakano, Hisayoshi Toyokawa, Keisuke Yoshida

Abstract
. We show that the (Gurevich) topological entropy for the countable Markov shift associated with an infinite transition matrix A A coincides with the non-commutative topological entropy for the Exel–Laca algebra associated with A A , under certain conditions on A A . An important example satisfying the conditions is the renewal shift, which is not locally finite. We also pose interesting questions for future research on non-commutative topological entropy for non-locally finite transition matrices.

Introduction

The topological entropy was first introduced in 1965 by Adler, Konheim and McAndrew
[
1

[

Hence, it was a natural hope to extend the concept of topological entropy to operator algebras.
Among several attempts, Voiculescu
[
33
]
in 1995 introduced his useful definition of non-commutative entropy for completely positive maps on nuclear C
∗
-algebras, and it was later extended to exact C
∗
-algebras by Brown
[
6

We refer to monographs

In

This was extended by Boca and Goldstein in
[

to finite Markov shifts and their associated Cuntz–Krieger algebras.
On the other hand, Matsumoto

In this paper, we consider another important generalization of finite Markov shifts, i.e. countable Markov shifts, and establish that its (Gurevich) topological entropy coincides with the non-commutative topological entropy for the associated Exel–Laca algebra, under certain conditions on the transition matrix and KMS states:

2.2.
Gurevich topological entropy

It is well known that the classical definition of topological entropy
h_{\mathrm{top}}(\sigma)
of a continuous map
\sigma:X\to X
on a topological space
X
(in the sense of the original definition by Adler, Konheim and McAndrew
[
1
]
) does not behave well if
X
is not compact.
In fact, it follows from Theorem 1.3 of
[
14
]
that if there is a point

whose orbit

does not have an accumulation point (e.g.
\sigma
is the renewal shift), then

.

A useful definition of topological entropy for countable Markov shifts was given by Gurevich
[
13
]
from the viewpoint of the variational principle, and its properties were later intensively studied by Sarig
[
29
]
:
If
\sigma_{A}:\Sigma_{A}\to\Sigma_{A}
is topologically mixing,
2
2

2

A continuous map
\sigma:X\to X
on a metric space is said to be

if for any nonempty open sets

one can find
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that

for any

.
then the following holds (cf.
[
31
]
).

•

The
Gurevich topological entropy

of

, given by

h_{\mathrm{G}}(\sigma_{A})\coloneqq\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\sum_{\sigma_{A}^{n}(x)=x}1_{[a]_{1}}(x)

for some

, exists and is independent of the choice of

.

•

The variational principle holds:

h_{G}(\sigma_{A})=\sup\left\{h_{\mu}(\sigma_{A})\mid\text{$\mu$: $\sigma_{A}$-invariant Borel probability measure}\right\},

where

is the metric entropy of

.

2.3.
Non-commutative topological entropy

Let
\mathcal{O}
be an exact C
∗
-algebra and
\pi:\mathcal{O}\to B(\mathcal{H})
a faithful
*
-representation of
\mathcal{O}
on the set
B(\mathcal{H})
of bounded linear operators on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
.
Given a finite subset
\omega\subset\mathcal{O}
and
\delta>0
, the completely positive
\delta
-rank
rcp(\omega;\delta)\equiv rcp_{\pi}(\omega;\delta)
of
\omega
is given by

rcp(\omega;\delta)=\min\Big\{N\in\mathbb{N}:\text{there exists $(\phi,\psi,\mathcal{A})\in CPA(\mathcal{O})$ such that}\\
\mathrm{rank}\,\mathcal{A}=N,\quad\|\psi\circ\phi(S)-\pi(S)\|<\delta\;\text{for all $S\in\omega$}\Big\},

where
CPA(\mathcal{O})
is the set of triples
(\phi,\psi,\mathcal{A})
of a finite-dimensional C
∗
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
and cp (i.e. completely positive) maps
\phi:\mathcal{O}\to\mathcal{A}
,
\psi:\mathcal{A}\to B(\mathcal{H})
,
\mathrm{rank}\,\mathcal{A}
is the dimension of a maximal abelian C
∗
-subalgebra of
\mathcal{A}
, and
\|\cdot\|
is the norm on
B(\mathcal{H})
.
Standard references for non-commutative entropy are e.g.
[
27
,
22
]
.
By Kirchberg–Wassermann’s nuclear embedding of exact C
∗
-algebra (cf.
[
35
]
), there exists a faithful
*
-representation
\pi:\mathcal{O}\to B(\mathcal{H})
such that
rcp_{\pi}(\omega;\delta)<\infty
for all finite subset
\omega\subset\mathcal{O}
and
\delta>0
.
Furthermore, it is observed by Brown
[
6
]
that
rcp(\omega;\delta)
is independent of the choice of
\pi
.

Let
\gamma:\mathcal{O}\to\mathcal{O}
be a cp map.
The non-commutative topological entropy
ht(\gamma)
of
\gamma
is given by

ht(\gamma)=\sup_{\omega}ht(\gamma,\omega),\qquad ht(\gamma,\omega)=\sup_{\delta>0}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log rcp\left(\bigcup_{j=0}^{n-1}\gamma^{j}(\omega);\delta\right),

where the supremum is taken over all finite subsets
\omega
of
\mathcal{O}
.
The basic properties of the non-commutative topological entropy are as follows, refer to
[
3
,
22
]
.

•

If
\mathcal{O}_{0}
is a subalgebra of
\mathcal{O}
such that
\gamma(\mathcal{O}_{0})\subset\mathcal{O}_{0}
, then
ht(\gamma|_{\mathcal{O}_{0}})\leq ht(\gamma)
.

•

If
\{\omega_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is an increasing sequence of finite subsets of
\mathcal{O}
(i.e.
\omega_{j}\subset\omega_{j+1}
for any
j\in\mathbb{N}
) and the norm closure of the linear span of
\bigcup_{j,n\in\mathbb{N}}\gamma^{n}(\omega_{j})
equals to
\mathcal{O}
, then
ht(\gamma)=\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}ht(\gamma,\omega_{j}).

•

If
\theta
is an automorphism of
\mathcal{O}
, then
ht(\theta\circ\gamma\circ\theta^{-1})=ht(\gamma)
.

•

Given a continuous map
\sigma:X\to X
on a compact metric space
X
, if
\mathcal{O}=C(X)
and
\gamma(f)=f\circ\sigma
for each
f\in\mathcal{O}
, then
ht(\gamma)=h_{\mathrm{top}}(\sigma)
.

2.4.
KMS state and GNS representation

Let
\mathcal{O}

Furthermore, given a group action

KMS states can be seen as a generalization of Gibbs states and played an important role in the context of quantum statistical mechanics, refer to e.g.
[

Furthermore, KMS states were recently used in

Given a state

The GNS representation of a KMS state is a fundamental (von Neumann algebraic) approach in operator algebras theory, refer to

2.5.

For a 0-1 matrix

(2.3)

\prod_{i\in X}S^{*}_{i}S_{i}\prod_{j\in Y}(1-S^{*}_{j}S_{j})=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}A(X,Y,k)S_{k}S^{*}_{k}

3.
Canonical ucp map on the Exel–Laca algebra

In this section, we define a ucp (i.e. unital cp) map

\sigma_{A}:\Sigma_{A}\to\Sigma_{A}

-

First, we aim to define

(3.1)

\gamma_{A}(S_{j}S^{*}_{j})\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}S_{i}S_{j}S^{*}_{j}S^{*}_{i}

To avoid treating infinite sums, the following condition plays an indispensable role.
For notational simplicity, we write

C_{j}\coloneqq\{i\in\mathbb{N}:A(i,j)=1\},\quad R_{j}\coloneqq\{i\in\mathbb{N}:A(j,i)=1\}.

Definition 3.1

We introduce the canonical gauge action

6.1.
Preliminary

Note that

The following estimate is important to prove Theorem

Proposition 6.1
.

Assume that
(SH)
,
(UCF)
and
(FS)
hold.
Then, for any sufficiently large
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

h_{G}(\sigma_{A})\leq\liminf_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{m}\log w(m,n)\leq\limsup_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{m}\log w(m,n)\leq h_{G}(\sigma_{A})+\mathcal{I}.

Proof.

We first observe that, for each
m\geq 2
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

(6.2)

\begin{split}w(m,n)&=\left|\left\{\{\alpha_{j}\}_{j=1}^{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{m}:\alpha_{m}\leq n,\prod_{k=1}^{m-1}A(\alpha_{k},\alpha_{k+1})=1\right\}\right|\\
&=\sum_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{m}\in\mathbb{N},i_{m}\leq n}\prod_{k=1}^{m-1}A(i_{k},i_{k+1})=\sum_{i,j\in\mathbb{N},\;j\leq n}A^{m-1}(i,j).\end{split}

Hence, due to the fact that
h_{G}(\sigma_{A})=\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{m}\log A^{m}(j,j)
for any
j\in\mathbb{N}
(cf.
[
31
]
), we get
h_{G}(\sigma_{A})\leq\liminf_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{m}\log w(m,n)
.
Below we will see the other inequality.

Notice also that
A
is not locally finite due to
(FS)
and the fact that
\mathcal{O}_{A}
is unital (refer to Section
2.5
).
So, there exists an
A
-infinite emitter, denoted by
J\in\mathbb{N}
(otherwise,
A
is row finite by
(FS)
, implying that
A
is locally finite due to
(UCF)
).
Let

N_{J}\coloneqq\min\{j_{0}\in\mathbb{N}:\forall j\geq j_{0},\;A(J,j)=1\},

which is well-defined (and finite) because
J
is an
A
-infinite emitter.

Fix
n\geq N_{J}
.
For
1\leq i,j\leq n
, we introduce

k_{j}\coloneqq\min\{l\in\mathbb{N}:A^{l}(j,J)\neq 0\},\quad k_{i,j}\coloneqq\min\{l\in\mathbb{N}:A^{l}(i,j)\neq 0\}.

By the mixing property in
(SH)
,

K\coloneqq\max\left\{\max_{1\leq j\leq n}(k_{j}+1),\max_{1\leq i,j\leq n}k_{i,j}\right\}\leq I(m,n).

Let
j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
When
i\geq n
,

\displaystyle A^{m+k_{j}+1}(j,j)

\displaystyle\geq A^{k_{j}}(j,J)A^{m+1}(J,j)

\displaystyle\geq A^{m+1}(J,j)

\displaystyle\geq A(J,i)A^{m}(i,j)=A^{m}(i,j).

On the other hand, when
i<n
,

\displaystyle A^{m+k_{i,j}}(j,j)\geq A^{k_{i,j}}(j,i)A^{m}(i,j)\geq A^{m}(i,j).

Therefore, we have

\sum_{i,j\in\mathbb{N},\;j\leq n}A^{m-1}(i,j)\leq n\cdot I(m,n)\cdot\max_{0\leq k\leq K,\;1\leq j\leq n}A^{m+k}(j,j).

By using again the fact that
h_{G}(\sigma_{A})=\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{m}\log A^{m}(j,j)
for any
j\in\mathbb{N}
, we get

\frac{1}{m}\log w(m,n)\leq\frac{1}{m}\left(\log n+\log I(m,n)\right)+\max_{0\leq k\leq K,\;1\leq j\leq n}\frac{1}{m}\log A^{m+k}(j,j)\\
\to h_{G}(\sigma_{A})+\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{m}\log I(m,n)

as
m\to\infty
.
This completes the proof.

Given a subshift

Here, a multiple

which satisfies

\Sigma=\{\{\lambda(e_{j})\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\,:\,e_{j}\in E_{j-1,j},\;t(e_{j})=s(e_{j+1})\},

The
Matsumoto algebra

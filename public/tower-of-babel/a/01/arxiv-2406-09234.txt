Abstract
. In this note, we study the distance from an arbitrary nonzero projection P P to the set of nilpotents in a factor ℳ \mathcal{M} equipped with a normal faithful tracial state τ \tau . We prove that the distance equals ( 2 ​ cos ⁡ τ ⁡ ( P ) ​ π 1 + 2 ​ τ ​ ( P ) ) − 1 (2\cos\frac{\tau(P)\pi}{1+2\tau(P)})^{-1} . This is new even in the case where ℳ \mathcal{M} is the matrix algebra. The special case settles a conjecture posed by Z. Cramer.

1.
Introduction

Let
{\mathcal{M}}
be a factor (a von Neumann algebra with trivial center) acting on a separable Hilbert space.
A
nilpotent
is an operator
X\in{\mathcal{M}}
satisfying
X^{n}=0
for some positive integer
n
.
For
X\in{\mathcal{M}}
, let
\nu_{\mathcal{M}}(X)
denote the distance from
X
to the set of nilpotents in
{\mathcal{M}}
.
In other words,

\nu_{\mathcal{M}}(X)=\inf\{\lVert X-N\rVert\mid N\text{ is a nilpotent in }{\mathcal{M}}\},

where
\lVert\cdot\rVert
denotes the operator norm.

In 1970, Paul Halmos proposed a list of 10 problems in operator theory
[
6
]
.
Problem 7 in the list asks the following.
Let
\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
denote the von Neumann algebra (type I
∞
factor) of all bounded linear operators on a separable infinite-dimensional complex Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
.
Let
X\in\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
be a
quasinilpotent
operator, that is, an operator whose spectrum
\sigma(X)
consists of only one point
0
. Is it true that
\nu_{\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})}(X)=0
?

The answer to this problem is affirmative.
In fact, the theorem below is given by Apostol, Foia

s

,

, Voiculescu in
[
1
]
, which completely characterizes the condition for an operator
X
to satisfy
\nu_{\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})}(X)=0
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
X\in\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
.
The equation
\nu_{\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})}(X)=0
holds if and only if the following three conditions hold.

•

The spectrum
\sigma(X)
of
X
is connected and
0\in\sigma(X)
.

•

The essential spectrum
\sigma_{e}(X)
of
X
is connected and
0\in\sigma_{e}(X)
.

•

If
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
and
\lambda I-X
is semi-Fredholm, then the Fredholm index of
\lambda I-X
equals
0
.

It is natural to get interested in the value
\nu_{\mathcal{M}}(X)
for a more general factor
{\mathcal{M}}
and an operator
X\in{\mathcal{M}}
.
In this note, we completely compute
\nu_{\mathcal{M}}(P)
for a projection
P
in a general factor
{\mathcal{M}}
.
Recall that a
projection
is an operator
P\in{\mathcal{M}}
satisfying
P=P^{2}=P^{*}
.
Let
{\mathcal{P}}({\mathcal{M}})
denote the set of projections in
{\mathcal{M}}
.
For
P\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathcal{M}})
, we use the symbol
P^{\perp}:=I-P
.
It is clear that
\nu_{\mathcal{M}}(0)=0
.

It appears that the oldest result on
\nu_{\mathcal{M}}(P)
for a projection
P
is given by Hedlund in 1972
[
7
]
.
Herrero
[
8
, Corollary 9]
completely computed
\nu_{\mathcal{M}}(P)
when
{\mathcal{M}}
is a type I
∞
factor.
From this result, it is not difficult to get the following.

Theorem 2

(
[
8
, Corollary 9]
, see also
[
16
, Section 8]
)
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}
be an infinite factor and
P\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathcal{M}})\setminus\{0\}
.

•

If
P^{\perp}\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathcal{M}})
is an infinite projection, then
\nu_{\mathcal{M}}(P)=1/2
.

•

If
P^{\perp}\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathcal{M}})
is a finite projection, then
\nu_{\mathcal{M}}(P)=1
.

Therefore, we restrict our attention to finite factors.
In the case of matrix algebras, one may find some research in the literature.
Let
n

\mathbb{M}_{n}
denote the algebra of

n

[

.
He conjectured that
\nu_{\mathbb{M}_{n}}(P)=(2\cos\frac{\pi}{\frac{n}{m}+2})^{-1}
for every projection
P\in{\mathcal{P}}(\mathbb{M}_{n})
of rank
m

1\leq m\leq n

[
3

.
The second author recently proved that
\nu_{\mathbb{M}_{n}}(P)\geq(2\cos\frac{\pi}{n+2})^{-1}
for every nonzero projection
P\in{\mathcal{P}}(\mathbb{M}_{n})

[
13
]
, settling a conjecture posed by MacDonald in
[
11
]
.
For more information concerning related topics, see
[

,
[

]
,
[

]
.
See also
[

]

]

In what follows, let
{\mathcal{M}}
be a finite factor.
Then
{\mathcal{M}}
is equipped with a unique normal faithful tracial state
\tau
.
As one may see from the preceding paragraph, the computation of the exact value of
\nu_{\mathcal{M}}(P)
was not complete even in the case of matrix algebras, and little was known about the case of type II
1

The goal of this note is to prove

Theorem 3
.

If
0\neq P\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathcal{M}})
, then
\nu_{\mathcal{M}}(P)=(2\cos\theta)^{-1}
, where
\theta=\displaystyle\frac{\tau(P)\pi}{1+2\tau(P)}
.

This completes the program of the determination of
\nu_{\mathcal{M}}(P)
for a general factor
{\mathcal{M}}
and a general projection
P\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathcal{M}})

Note that
\tau(X)=\mathrm{tr}\,X/n
when
{\mathcal{M}}=\mathbb{M}_{n}
and
X\in{\mathcal{M}}
.
It follows that Theorem
3

2.
Results

For
X\in{\mathcal{M}}
, the projection
E
onto the closure of the range of
X
belongs to
{\mathcal{P}}(M)
.
We call
E
the range projection of
X
.

Lemma 4
.

Let
n\geq 1
.
An operator
N\in{\mathcal{M}}
satisfies
N^{n}=0
if and only if there are projections
0=P_{0}\leq P_{1}\leq\cdots\leq P_{n}=I
in
{\mathcal{M}}
satisfying
P_{k-1}^{\perp}NP_{k}=0
for every
1\leq k\leq n
.

Proof.

If
N^{n}=0
, then define
P_{k}
to be the range projection of
N^{n-k}
.
The converse is easier.
∎

Lemma 5
.

Let
0=P_{0}\leq P_{1}\leq\cdots\leq P_{n}=I
be in
{\mathcal{P}}({\mathcal{M}})
and
X\in{\mathcal{M}}
.
Set
\mathcal{N}:=\{N\in{\mathcal{M}}\mid P_{k-1}^{\perp}NP_{k}=0\text{ for every }1\leq k\leq n\}
.
Then there is a nilpotent
N_{0}\in\mathcal{N}
satisfying

\lVert X-N_{0}\rVert=\inf_{N\in\mathcal{N}}\lVert X-N\rVert=\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\lVert P_{k-1}^{\perp}XP_{k}\rVert.

Proof.

This is a version of the so-called Arveson’s distance formula
[
2
]
. See
[
14
, Theorem 1]
and
[
15
]
.
∎

For
X\in{\mathcal{M}}
, let
\delta(X)
denote the trace of the range projection of
X
.

Lemma 6
.

If
X,Y\in{\mathcal{M}}
, then
\delta(XY),\delta(YX)\leq\delta(X)
and
\delta(X+Y)\leq\delta(X)+\delta(Y)
hold.

Proof.

It is clear that
\delta(XY)\leq\delta(X)
.
If
Z=V\lvert Z\rvert
is the polar decomposition of an operator
Z\in{\mathcal{M}}
,
then
VV^{*}
is the range projection of
Z
and
V^{*}V
is the range projection of
Z^{*}
, hence
\delta(Z)=\tau(VV^{*})=\tau(V^{*}V)=\delta(Z^{*})
.
Thus we have
\delta(YX)=\delta((YX)^{*})=\delta(X^{*}Y^{*})\leq\delta(X^{*})=\delta(X)
.

Let
E_{1},E_{2},E_{3}
denote the range projections of
X+Y,X,Y
, respectively.
Then we have
E_{1}\leq E_{2}\vee E_{3}
. Since
E_{2}\vee E_{3}-E_{2}
is Murray–von Neumann equivalent to
E_{3}-E_{2}\wedge E_{3}
, we have
\tau(E_{2}\vee E_{3}-E_{2})=\tau(E_{3}-E_{2}\wedge E_{3})
, which implies
\tau(E_{2}\vee E_{3})\leq\tau(E_{2})+\tau(E_{3})
. Thus
\delta(X+Y)\leq\delta(X)+\delta(Y)
holds.
∎

Lemma 7
.

Let
X\in{\mathcal{M}}
satisfy
X\geq 0
. Let
n\geq 1
and let
0=P_{0}\leq P_{1}\leq\cdots\leq P_{n}=I
be in
{\mathcal{P}}({\mathcal{M}})
.
For each
k
, put
A_{k}=X^{1/2}P_{k}X^{1/2}
.
Then

\lVert P_{k-1}^{\perp}XP_{k}\rVert=\lVert(X-A_{k-1})^{1/2}\cdot A_{k}^{1/2}\rVert.

Moreover,

(1)

0=A_{0}\leq A_{1}\leq\cdots\leq A_{n}=X\text{ and }\sum_{k=1}^{n}\delta(A_{k}-A_{k-1})\leq 1

hold.

Proof.

We have

\begin{split}\lVert P_{k-1}^{\perp}XP_{k}\rVert&=\lVert P_{k-1}^{\perp}X^{1/2}\cdot X^{1/2}P_{k}\rVert\\
&=\lVert(X^{1/2}P_{k-1}^{\perp}X^{1/2})^{1/2}\cdot(X^{1/2}P_{k}X^{1/2})^{1/2}\rVert\\
&=\lVert(X-X^{1/2}P_{k-1}X^{1/2})^{1/2}\cdot(X^{1/2}P_{k}X^{1/2})^{1/2}\rVert\\
&=\lVert(X-A_{k-1})^{1/2}\cdot A_{k}^{1/2}\rVert.\end{split}

It is clear that
0=A_{0}\leq A_{1}\leq\cdots\leq A_{n}=X
.
Since
\delta(A_{k}-A_{k-1})\leq\delta(P_{k}-P_{k-1})=\tau(P_{k}-P_{k-1})
, we get
\sum_{k=1}^{n}\delta(A_{k}-A_{k-1})\leq 1
.
∎

Conversely, the following holds.

Lemma 8
.

Let
X\in{\mathcal{M}}
satisfy
X\geq 0
.
Let
n\geq 1
and
A_{0},A_{1},\dots,A_{n}\in{\mathcal{M}}
satisfy (
1
).
Then there are projections
0=P_{0}\leq P_{1}\leq\cdots\leq P_{n}=I
in
{\mathcal{M}}
satisfying
X^{1/2}P_{k}X^{1/2}=A_{k}
for every
k
.

Proof.

Since
\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\delta(A_{k}-A_{k-1})\leq 1
, one may choose operators
Y_{1},Y_{2},\ldots,Y_{n}\in{\mathcal{M}}
, whose range projections
Q_{1},Q_{2},\ldots,Q_{n}
are pairwise orthogonal, with the property
Y_{k}^{*}Y_{k}=A_{k}-A_{k-1}
.
Then
Y:=Y_{1}+Y_{2}+\cdots+Y_{n}
satisfies
Y^{*}(Q_{1}+\cdots+Q_{k})Y=A_{k}
for every
k
.
Since
{\mathcal{M}}
is of finite type and
Y^{*}Y=X
, one may take a unitary operator
U\in{\mathcal{M}}
with
Y=UX^{1/2}
.
Thus
P_{k}=U^{*}(Q_{1}+\cdots Q_{k})U
,
k\leq n-1
, and
P_{n}=I
satisfy the desired property.
∎

Therefore, we get the following.

Proposition 9
.

Let
X\in{\mathcal{M}}
and
X\geq 0
.
Then

\nu_{\mathcal{M}}(X)=\inf\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\lVert(X-A_{k-1})^{1/2}\cdot A_{k}^{1/2}\rVert,

where the infimum in the right-hand side is taken over the collection of all
n\geq 1
and
(A_{0},A_{1},\dots,A_{n})\in{\mathcal{M}}^{n+1}
satisfying (
1
).

In what follows, we study the case
0\neq X=P\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathcal{M}})
.
Let
n\geq 1
and
(A_{0},A_{1},\dots,A_{n})\in{\mathcal{M}}^{n+1}
satisfy (
1
).
Set
\displaystyle\theta:=\frac{\tau(P)\pi}{1+2\tau(P)}
and
\varphi:=\theta/2
.
To get Theorem
3
, we need to compare
(2\cos\theta)^{-1}
with

\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\lVert(P-A_{k-1})^{1/2}A_{k}^{1/2}\rVert=\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\lVert(I-A_{k-1})^{1/2}A_{k}^{1/2}\rVert.

Observe that the mapping

t\mapsto\frac{1}{2\cos\theta}\cdot\frac{\sin t}{\sin(t+\theta)}

is a monotone increasing bijection from
[0,\pi-2\theta]
onto
[0,1]
.
Since
0\leq A_{k}\leq P
, by functional calculus we may uniquely take an operator
B_{k}\in{\mathcal{M}}
satisfying
0\leq B_{k}\leq(\pi-2\theta)P
and

(2)

A_{k}=\frac{1}{2\cos\theta}\cdot\frac{\sin B_{k}}{\sin(B_{k}+\theta I)}.

(Note that the right-hand side makes sense because
\sin(B_{k}+\theta I)
is invertible and the pair
\sin B_{k}
,
\sin(B_{k}+\theta I)
commutes. In what follows we frequently use such fractional expressions.)
Since
A_{0}\leq A_{1}\leq\cdots\leq A_{n}
, we have

\frac{\sin B_{0}}{\sin(B_{0}+\theta I)}\leq\frac{\sin B_{1}}{\sin(B_{1}+\theta I)}\leq\cdots\leq\frac{\sin B_{n}}{\sin(B_{n}+\theta I)}.

Lemma 10
.

Let
B,{B^{\prime}}\in{\mathcal{M}}
satisfy
0\leq B\leq(\pi-2\theta)I
,
0\leq{B^{\prime}}\leq(\pi-2\theta)I
.
Then

\displaystyle\frac{\sin B}{\sin(B+\theta I)}\leq\frac{\sin{B^{\prime}}}{\sin({B^{\prime}}+\theta I)}
holds
if and only if

\cot(B+{\varphi}I)\geq\cot({B^{\prime}}+{\varphi}I)
.
Moreover, we have

\delta\left(\frac{\sin B}{\sin(B+\theta I)}-\frac{\sin{B^{\prime}}}{\sin({B^{\prime}}+\theta I)}\right)=\delta(\cot(B+{\varphi}I)-\cot({B^{\prime}}+{\varphi}I)).

Proof.

By applying the formula
\sin(\alpha\pm\beta)=\sin\alpha\cos\beta\pm\cos\alpha\sin\beta
to mutually commuting operators, we get

\begin{split}\sin(B+{\varphi}I\pm{\varphi}I)&=\sin(B+{\varphi}I)\cos({\varphi}I)\pm\cos(B+{\varphi}I)\sin({\varphi}I)\\
&=\cos{\varphi}\sin(B+{\varphi}I)\pm\sin{\varphi}\cos(B+{\varphi}I).\end{split}

Thus

\frac{\sin B}{\sin(B+\theta I)}=\frac{\cos{\varphi}\sin(B+{\varphi}I)-\sin{\varphi}\cos(B+{\varphi}I)}{\cos{\varphi}\sin(B+{\varphi}I)+\sin{\varphi}\cos(B+{\varphi}I)}=\frac{2I}{I+\tan{\varphi}\cot(B+{\varphi}I)}-I.

We also get

\frac{\sin{B^{\prime}}}{\sin({B^{\prime}}+\theta I)}=\frac{2I}{I+\tan{\varphi}\cot({B^{\prime}}+{\varphi}I)}-I.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{\sin B}{\sin(B+\theta I)}\leq\frac{\sin{B^{\prime}}}{\sin({B^{\prime}}+\theta I)}
holds if and only if

\frac{2I}{I+\tan{\varphi}\cot(B+{\varphi}I)}\leq\frac{2I}{I+\tan{\varphi}\cot({B^{\prime}}+{\varphi}I)}.

Since
X\leq Y\iff X^{-1}\geq Y^{-1}
for positive invertible operators, we see that the above condition is equivalent to
\cot(B+{\varphi}I)\geq\cot({B^{\prime}}+{\varphi}I)
.

Using the identity
X^{-1}-Y^{-1}=X^{-1}(Y-X)Y^{-1}
for invertible operators, we also get the equality concerning
\delta
.
∎

Lemma 11
.

Let
B,{B^{\prime}}\in{\mathcal{M}}
satisfy
0\leq B\leq(\pi-2\theta)I
,
0\leq{B^{\prime}}\leq(\pi-2\theta)I
.
Put

A=\frac{1}{2\cos\theta}\cdot\frac{\sin B}{\sin(B+\theta I)},\quad A^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2\cos\theta}\cdot\frac{\sin{B^{\prime}}}{\sin({B^{\prime}}+\theta I)}.

Then
\displaystyle\lVert(I-A)^{1/2}(A^{\prime})^{1/2}\rVert\leq(2\cos\theta)^{-1}

if and only if

\cot({B^{\prime}}+{\varphi}I)\geq\cot(B+{3\varphi}I)
.

Proof.

Since

\sin B+\sin(B+2\theta I)=2\sin(B+\theta I)\cos(\theta I)=2\cos\theta\sin(B+\theta I),

we get

I-\frac{1}{2\cos\theta}\cdot\frac{\sin B}{\sin(B+\theta I)}=\frac{1}{2\cos\theta}\cdot\frac{\sin(B+2\theta I)}{\sin(B+\theta I)}.

It follows that

\lVert(I-A)^{1/2}(A^{\prime})^{1/2}\rVert\leq(2\cos\theta)^{-1}\iff\left\lVert\left(\frac{\sin(B+2\theta I)}{\sin(B+\theta I)}\right)^{1/2}\left(\frac{\sin{B^{\prime}}}{\sin({B^{\prime}}+\theta I)}\right)^{1/2}\right\rVert\leq 1.

Using the argument as in the proof of the preceding lemma, we get

\frac{\sin(B+2\theta I)}{\sin(B+\theta I)}=\frac{I+\tan{\varphi}\cot(B+{3\varphi}I)}{I-\tan{\varphi}\cot(B+{3\varphi}I)}=\frac{I+S}{I-S}

and

\frac{\sin{B^{\prime}}}{\sin({B^{\prime}}+\theta I)}=\frac{I-\tan{\varphi}\cot({B^{\prime}}+{\varphi}I)}{I+\tan{\varphi}\cot({B^{\prime}}+{\varphi}I)}=\frac{I-T}{I+T},

where
S=\tan{\varphi}\cot(B+{3\varphi}I)
and
T=\tan{\varphi}\cot({B^{\prime}}+{\varphi}I)
.
(Observe that
-I\leq S\leq I
and
-I\leq T\leq I
hold. Note that
I-S
and
I+T
are invertible, while
I+S
and
I-T
are not necessarily invertible.)
Hence

\begin{split}\lVert(I-A)^{1/2}(A^{\prime})^{1/2}\rVert\leq(2\cos\theta)^{-1}&\iff\left\lVert\left(\frac{I+S}{I-S}\right)^{1/2}\left(\frac{I-T}{I+T}\right)^{1/2}\right\rVert\leq 1\\
&\iff\left(\frac{I-T}{I+T}\right)^{1/2}\left(\frac{I+S}{I-S}\right)\left(\frac{I-T}{I+T}\right)^{1/2}\leq I\\
&\iff(I-T)^{1/2}\left(\frac{2I}{I-S}-I\right)(I-T)^{1/2}\leq I+T\\
&\iff 2(I-T)^{1/2}(I-S)^{-1}(I-T)^{1/2}\leq 2I\\
&\iff(I-T)^{1/2}(I-S)^{-1}(I-T)^{1/2}\leq I\\
&\iff\lVert(I-S)^{-1/2}(I-T)^{1/2}\rVert\leq 1\\
&\iff(I-S)^{-1/2}(I-T)(I-S)^{-1/2}\leq I\\
&\iff I-T\leq I-S\\
&\iff S\leq T\\
&\iff\cot(B+{3\varphi}I)\leq\cot({B^{\prime}}+{\varphi}I).\end{split}

∎

Lemma 12
.

Let
X,Y,Z\in{\mathcal{M}}
be self-adjoint operators satisfying
X\geq Y\geq Z
.
If
X-Z
is invertible, then there uniquely exists a self-adjoint operator
W\in{\mathcal{M}}
satisfying
X\geq Y\geq W\geq Z
,
\delta(X-Y)=\delta(X-W)
, and
\delta(X-W)+\delta(W-Z)=1
.

Proof.

One may assume
Z=0
without loss of generality.
Then
X\geq Y\geq 0
and
X
is invertible.
By multiplying
X^{-1/2}
from both sides of each operator, we may also assume
X=I
without loss of generality.
Then we have
I\geq Y\geq 0
.
Assume that a self-adjoint operator
W\in{\mathcal{M}}
satisfies the desired properties.
From
\delta(I-W)+\delta(W)=1
, we see that
W
is a projection.
This together with
I\geq Y\geq W\geq 0
,
\delta(I-Y)=\delta(I-W)
shows that
W=\chi_{\{1\}}(Y)
. Conversely, it is easy to see that
W=\chi_{\{1\}}(Y)
satisfies the desired properties.
∎

Lemma 13
.

Let
X,Y\in{\mathcal{M}}
be self-adjoint operators satisfying
X\leq Y
.
If
f\colon\sigma(X)\cup\sigma(Y)\to\mathbb{R}
is a monotone decreasing function, then
\tau(f(X))\geq\tau(f(Y))
.

Proof.

By considering the pair
X+\alpha I
,
Y+\alpha I
instead of
X
,
Y
for a suitable real number
\alpha
, one may assume
\sigma(X),\sigma(Y)\subset(0,\infty)
without loss of generality.
One may also assume that the range of
f
is contained in
(0,\infty)
by adding some constant function.
For a real number
t\in(0,1]
and a positive operator
Z\in{\mathcal{M}}
, we define

\mu_{t}(Z)=\inf\{s\geq 0\mid\tau(\chi_{(s,\infty)}(Z))\leq t\}.

By
[
5
, Lemma 2.5 (iii)]
, we have
\mu_{t}(X)\leq\mu_{t}(Y)
for every
t\in(0,1]
.
This together with
[
5
, Remark 3.3]
and the assumption on
f
implies

\tau(f(X))=\int_{0}^{1}f(\mu_{t}(X))\,dt\geq\int_{0}^{1}f(\mu_{t}(Y))\,dt=\tau(f(Y)).

∎

Lemma 14
.

Let
U,V\in{\mathcal{M}}
be unitaries, and
r,s
real numbers with
0\leq r\leq 1
,
0<s<2\pi
.
If
\delta(U-V)=r
and
\delta(U-e^{is}V)=1-r
, then there is a projection
Q\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathcal{M}})
satisfying
\tau(Q)=r
and
U=Ve^{isQ}
.

Proof.

It follows from
\delta(U-V)=r
and
\delta(U-e^{is}V)=1-r
that
\delta(V^{*}U-I)=r
and
\delta(V^{*}U-e^{is}I)=1-r
.
Considering the spectral distribution of the unitary
V^{*}U
, we see that there is a projection
Q\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathcal{M}})
satisfying
\tau(Q)=r
and
V^{*}U=e^{isQ}
.
∎

Lemma 15
.

Let
B,{B^{\prime}}\in{\mathcal{M}}
satisfy
0\leq B\leq(\pi-2\theta)I
,
0\leq{B^{\prime}}\leq(\pi-2\theta)I
, and

\cot(B+{\varphi}I)\geq\cot({B^{\prime}}+{\varphi}I)\geq\cot(B+{3\varphi}I).

Set

r=\delta(\cot(B+{\varphi}I)-\cot({B^{\prime}}+{\varphi}I))
.
Then
\tau({B^{\prime}})-\tau(B)\leq r\theta
.
Moreover,

\tau({B^{\prime}})-\tau(B)=r\theta

holds if and only if
\delta(\cot({B^{\prime}}+{\varphi}I)-\cot(B+{3\varphi}I))=1-r
.

Proof.

Since
\cot(B+{\varphi}I)-\cot(B+{3\varphi}I)
is invertible, by Lemma
12
we may find an operator
C\in{\mathcal{M}}
satisfying
\delta(\cot(B+{\varphi}I)-C)=r
,
\delta(C-\cot(B+{3\varphi}I))=1-r
, and

\cot(B+{\varphi}I)\geq\cot({B^{\prime}}+{\varphi}I)\geq C\geq\cot(B+{3\varphi}I).

One may take an operator
D\in{\mathcal{M}}
with

{\varphi}I\leq D\leq(\pi-{\varphi})I
and
C=\cot D
.
Then

\cot(B+{\varphi}I)\geq\cot({B^{\prime}}+{\varphi}I)\geq\cot D\geq\cot(B+{3\varphi}I).

Since the inverse function of
\cot
is monotone decreasing, Lemma
13
implies that

\tau(B+{\varphi}I)\leq\tau({B^{\prime}}+{\varphi}I)\leq\tau(D)\leq\tau(B+{3\varphi}I).

Hence

\tau({B^{\prime}})-\tau(B)=\tau({B^{\prime}}+{\varphi}I)-\tau(B+{\varphi}I)\leq\tau(D)-\tau(B+{\varphi}I).

Moreover, we see that
\tau({B^{\prime}}+{\varphi}I)=\tau(D)
holds if and only if

\cot({B^{\prime}}+{\varphi}I)=\cot D
, which is in turn equivalent to
\delta(\cot({B^{\prime}}+{\varphi}I)-\cot(B+{3\varphi}I))=1-r
.
Consequently, to get the desired conclusion, it suffices to show that

\tau(D)-\tau(B+{\varphi}I)=r\theta
.
By
\delta(\cot(B+{\varphi}I)-C)=r
and the equality

(3)

\begin{split}\cot(B+{\varphi}I)-C&=\cot(B+{\varphi}I)-\cot D\\
&=i\left(\frac{2I}{e^{2i(B+\varphi I)}-I}+I\right)-i\left(\frac{2I}{e^{2iD}-I}+I\right)\\
&=2i((e^{2i(B+\varphi I)}-I)^{-1}-(e^{2iD}-I)^{-1})\\
&=2i(e^{2i(B+\varphi I)}-I)^{-1}(e^{2iD}-e^{2i(B+\varphi I)})(e^{2iD}-I)^{-1},\end{split}

we get

(4)

\delta(e^{2iD}-e^{2i(B+\varphi I)})=r.

Similarly,
\delta(C-\cot(B+{3\varphi}I))=1-r
implies

(5)

\delta(e^{2iD}-e^{2i(B+3\varphi I)})=1-r.

The equations (
4
) and (
5
) and Lemma
14
imply that there is a projection
Q\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathcal{M}})
satisfying
\tau(Q)=r
and
e^{2iD}=e^{2i(B+\varphi I)}\cdot e^{4i\varphi Q}
.
Thus Lemma
18
below implies that
\tau(2D)=\tau(2(B+\varphi I))+\tau(4\varphi Q)
, hence

\tau(D)-\tau(B+\varphi I)=2\tau(Q)\varphi=r\theta
.
∎

Lemma 16

(Lemma 3 in Section I.6.11 of
[
4
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{U}\subset\mathbb{C}
be an open set and
f\colon\mathcal{U}\to\mathbb{C}
an analytic function.
Let
g\colon[0,1]\to\mathcal{M}
be a norm-differentiable function.
Assume that the spectra
\sigma(g(t))
,
t\in[0,1]
, are contained in a common compact subset of
\mathcal{U}
.
Then

\frac{d}{dt}\tau(f\circ g(t))=\tau(f^{\prime}(g(t))g^{\prime}(t))

holds for every
t\in[0,1]
.

Lemma 17
.

Let
X,Y\in{\mathcal{M}}
be positive operators.
Then

\sigma(e^{iX}e^{iY})\subset\{e^{i\alpha}\mid 0\leq\alpha\leq\lVert X\rVert+\lVert Y\rVert\}.

Proof.

This is trivial when
\lVert X\rVert+\lVert Y\rVert\geq 2\pi
(note that
e^{iX}e^{iY}
is unitary).
Assume that
\lVert X\rVert+\lVert Y\rVert<\alpha<2\pi
.
We need to show that
e^{iX}e^{iY}-e^{i\alpha}I
is invertible.
Observe that the convex hull of
\sigma(e^{iX})
and that of
\sigma(e^{i\alpha}e^{-iY})
do not intersect.
Let
\mathcal{H}
be the Hilbert space on which
{\mathcal{M}}
acts.
Observe that
\langle Zh,h\rangle
lies in the convex hull of
\sigma(Z)
for every normal operator
Z\in{\mathcal{M}}
and every unit vector
h
.
It follows that there is a positive real number
\varepsilon>0
satisfying
\lvert\langle(e^{iX}-e^{i\alpha}e^{-iY})h,h\rangle\rvert\geq\varepsilon
for every unit vector
h\in\mathcal{H}
.
This implies that
\lVert(e^{iX}-e^{i\alpha}e^{-iY})h\rVert\geq\varepsilon
and
\lVert(e^{iX}-e^{i\alpha}e^{-iY})^{*}h\rVert\geq\varepsilon
for every unit vector
h\in\mathcal{H}
.
Hence
e^{iX}-e^{i\alpha}e^{-iY}
is invertible, so
e^{iX}e^{iY}-e^{i\alpha}I
is also invertible.
∎

Lemma 18

(See also Lemma 4 in Section I.6.11 of
[
4
]
)
.

Let
X,Y,Z\in{\mathcal{M}}
be positive operators satisfying
\lVert X\rVert+\lVert Y\rVert<2\pi
,
\lVert Z\rVert<2\pi
, and
e^{iZ}=e^{iX}e^{iY}
. Then
\tau(Z)=\tau(X)+\tau(Y)
.

Proof.

Consider the mapping
g\colon[0,1]\to\mathcal{M}
defined by
g(t)=e^{iX}e^{itY}
.
By Lemma
17
, we get
\sigma(g(t))\subset\{e^{i\alpha}\mid 0\leq\alpha\leq\lVert X\rVert+\lVert Y\rVert\}
for every
t\in[0,1]
.
Fix
\beta\in(\lVert X\rVert+\lVert Y\rVert,2\pi)
.
Let
f\colon\{re^{i\alpha}\mid r>0,\,\beta-2\pi<\alpha<\beta\}\to\mathbb{C}
be the inverse mapping of the exponential function on
\{a+bi\mid a,b\in\mathbb{R},\ \beta-2\pi<b<\beta\}
.
Observe that
g^{\prime}(t)=e^{iX}e^{itY}\cdot iY=g(t)\cdot iY
and
f^{\prime}(z)=1/z
.
Thus Lemma
16
implies that

\frac{d}{dt}\tau(f\circ g(t))=\tau(g(t)^{-1}g^{\prime}(t))=i\tau(Y).

By integrating over
[0,1]
, we get

\tau(f\circ g(1))-\tau(f\circ g(0))=i\tau(Y).

Since
g(1)=e^{iX}e^{iY}=e^{iZ}
and
g(0)=e^{iX}
, we obtain
f\circ g(1)=iZ
and
f\circ g(0)=iX
. Thus the desired conclusion follows.
∎

Now we are ready to prove one-half of Theorem
3
.

Proof of
\nu_{\mathcal{M}}(P)\geq(2\cos\theta)^{-1}
.

Let
A_{k}
and
B_{k}
be as above.
Assume that

\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\lVert(P-A_{k-1})^{1/2}A_{k}^{1/2}\rVert<(2\cos\theta)^{-1}.

By Lemmas
10
,
11
,
15
, we have

\tau(B_{k})-\tau(B_{k-1})<\delta(A_{k}-A_{k-1})\theta
for every
k
.
Thus we obtain
\tau(B_{n})-\tau(B_{0})<\theta
.
Since
B_{0}=0
and
B_{n}=(\pi-2\theta)P
, we get
(\pi-2\theta)\tau(P)<\theta
, so
\displaystyle\theta>\frac{\tau(P)\pi}{1+2\tau(P)}
, a contradiction.
∎

We continue with the other half of the proof.
We first study the case where
{\mathcal{M}}=\mathbb{M}_{n}
is the
n\times n
matrix algebra and
\tau(X)=\mathrm{tr}\,X/n
,
X\in\mathbb{M}_{n}
.

Lemma 19
.

Let
X,Y,Z\in\mathbb{M}_{n}
be self-adjoint (
=
hermitian) matrices.
If
X-Z
is positive and invertible, and if
\mathrm{rank}\,(X-Y)+\mathrm{rank}\,(Y-Z)=n
, then
X\geq Y\geq Z
.

Proof.

As in the proof of Lemma
12
, one may assume
Z=0
and
X=I
without loss of generality.
In that case, we have
\mathrm{rank}\,(I-Y)+\mathrm{rank}\,Y=n
, which shows that
Y
is a projection, so
I\geq Y\geq 0
, as desired.
∎

Proposition 20
.

Let
0\neq P\in{\mathcal{P}}(\mathbb{M}_{n})
and
\displaystyle\theta:=\frac{\tau(P)\pi}{1+2\tau(P)}
.
Let
0=A_{0}\leq A_{1}\leq\cdots\leq A_{n}=P
be in
\mathbb{M}_{n}
satisfying
\mathrm{rank}(A_{k}-A_{k-1})=1
for every
k\in\{1,2,\dots,n\}
.
Take
B_{k}\in\mathbb{M}_{n}
satisfying
0\leq B_{k}\leq(\pi-2\theta)P
and the equation (
2
).
Then
\displaystyle\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\lVert(P-A_{k-1})^{1/2}A_{k}^{1/2}\rVert\leq(2\cos\theta)^{-1}
holds if and only if
\mathrm{tr}\,B_{k}=k\theta
holds for every
k\in\{1,2,\dots,n\}
.

Proof.

Assume that
\displaystyle\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\lVert(P-A_{k-1})^{1/2}A_{k}^{1/2}\rVert\leq(2\cos\theta)^{-1}
holds.
By Lemma
15
, we have
\tau(B_{k})-\tau(B_{k-1})\leq\theta/n
, or equivalently,
\mathrm{tr}\,(B_{k}-B_{k-1})\leq\theta
for each
k
. This together with
\mathrm{tr}\,B_{0}=0
and
\mathrm{tr}\,B_{n}=\mathrm{tr}\,((\pi-2\theta)P)=n\theta
shows that
\mathrm{tr}\,B_{k}=k\theta
.

Conversely, assume that
\mathrm{tr}\,B_{k}=k\theta
holds for every
k\in\{1,2,\dots,n\}
.
We show
\displaystyle\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\lVert(P-A_{k-1})^{1/2}A_{k}^{1/2}\rVert\leq(2\cos\theta)^{-1}
.
By Lemma
11
, it suffices to show that
\cot(B_{k}+{\varphi}I)\geq\cot(B_{k-1}+{3\varphi}I)
for every
k\in\{1,2,\dots,n\}
.
Let
k\in\{1,2,\dots,n\}
.
By Lemma
10
, we know that

(6)

\mathrm{rank}\,(\cot(B_{k-1}+{\varphi}I)-\cot(B_{k}+{\varphi}I))=\mathrm{rank}\,(A_{k-1}-A_{k})=1.

By imitating the calculation in (
3
), we get

\begin{split}&\quad\cot(B_{k-1}+{\varphi}I)-\cot(B_{k}+{\varphi}I)\\
&=2i(e^{2i(B_{k-1}+\varphi I)}-I)^{-1}(e^{2i(B_{k}+\varphi I)}-e^{2i(B_{k-1}+\varphi I)})(e^{2i(B_{k}+\varphi I)}-I)^{-1}\\
&=2ie^{2i\varphi}(e^{2i(B_{k-1}+\varphi I)}-I)^{-1}(e^{2iB_{k}}-e^{2iB_{k-1}})(e^{2i(B_{k}+\varphi I)}-I)^{-1},\end{split}

which implies
\mathrm{rank}\,(e^{2iB_{k}}-e^{2iB_{k-1}})=1
and thus
\mathrm{rank}\,(e^{-2iB_{k-1}}e^{2iB_{k}}-I)=1
.
On the other hand, we know
\mathrm{tr}\,(B_{k}-B_{k-1})=\theta
from the assumption.
Recall that
\det e^{X}=e^{\mathrm{tr}\,X}
holds for every
X\in\mathbb{M}_{n}
.
Thus we get

\det(e^{-2iB_{k-1}}e^{2iB_{k}})=e^{\mathrm{tr}\,(-2iB_{k-1})}\cdot e^{\mathrm{tr}\,(2iB_{k})}=e^{2i\theta}.

It follows that there is a projection
Q\in\mathbb{M}_{n}
of rank one satisfying
e^{-2iB_{k-1}}e^{2iB_{k}}=e^{2i\theta Q}
.
Then

\mathrm{rank}\,(e^{2iB_{k}}-e^{2i(B_{k-1}+\theta I)})=\mathrm{rank}\,(e^{2iB_{k-1}}(e^{2i\theta Q}-e^{2i\theta}I))=n-1.

By imitating the calculation in (
3
), we also get

\begin{split}&\quad\cot(B_{k-1}+{3\varphi}I)-\cot(B_{k}+{\varphi}I)\\
&=2i(e^{2i(B_{k-1}+3\varphi I)}-I)^{-1}(e^{2i(B_{k}+\varphi I)}-e^{2i(B_{k-1}+3\varphi I)})(e^{2i(B_{k}+\varphi I)}-I)^{-1}\\
&=2ie^{2i\varphi}(e^{2i(B_{k-1}+3\varphi I)}-I)^{-1}(e^{2iB_{k}}-e^{2i(B_{k-1}+\theta I)})(e^{2i(B_{k}+\varphi I)}-I)^{-1}.\end{split}

Thus we get

(7)

\mathrm{rank}\,(\cot(B_{k-1}+{3\varphi}I)-\cot(B_{k}+{\varphi}I))=n-1.

Since
\cot(B_{k-1}+{\varphi}I)-\cot(B_{k-1}+{3\varphi}I)
is positive and invertible, Lemma
19
with (
6
) and (
7
) shows that

\cot(B_{k-1}+{\varphi}I)\geq\cot(B_{k}+{\varphi}I)\geq\cot(B_{k-1}+{3\varphi}I),

which completes the proof.
∎

We will need the following well-known fact in matrix analysis.

Lemma 21

(See for example
[
10
, Theorem 4.3.26]
)
.

Let

\alpha_{1}^{\prime}\geq\alpha_{1}\geq\alpha_{2}^{\prime}\geq\alpha_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\alpha_{n}^{\prime}\geq\alpha_{n}

be real numbers.
Let
A\in\mathbb{M}_{n}
be a self-adjoint matrix whose eigenvalues are
\alpha_{1}\geq\alpha_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\alpha_{n}
.
Then there is a self-adjoint matrix
A^{\prime}\in\mathbb{M}_{n}
whose eigenvalues are
\alpha_{1}^{\prime}\geq\alpha_{2}^{\prime}\geq\cdots\geq\alpha_{n}^{\prime}
with
A\leq A^{\prime}
and
\mathrm{rank}\,(A^{\prime}-A)\leq 1
.

Lemma 22
.

Let
0\neq P\in{\mathcal{P}}(\mathbb{M}_{n})
and
\displaystyle\theta:=\frac{\tau(P)\pi}{1+2\tau(P)}
.
Then
\nu_{\mathbb{M}_{n}}(P)\leq(2\cos\theta)^{-1}
.

Proof.

Fix real numbers
\beta_{kl}\in[0,\pi-2\theta]
(
0\leq k\leq n
,
1\leq l\leq n
) satisfying the following conditions:

(a)

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{n}\beta_{kl}=k\theta
for every
k\in\{0,1,\dots,n\}
.

(b)

\beta_{k,1}\geq\beta_{k-1,1}\geq\beta_{k,2}\geq\beta_{k-1,2}\geq\cdots\geq\beta_{k,n}\geq\beta_{k-1,n}
for every
k\in\{1,2,\dots,n\}
.

(c)

\beta_{nl}=\pi-2\theta
for every
l\leq\mathrm{rank}\,P
, and
\beta_{nl}=0
for every
l>\mathrm{rank}\,P
.

For example, we may choose
\beta_{kl}=\max\{\min\{\pi-2\theta,k\theta-(l-1)(\pi-2\theta)\},0\}
.

We set

\alpha_{kl}=\frac{1}{2\cos\theta}\cdot\frac{\sin\beta_{kl}}{\sin(\beta_{kl}+\theta)}\in[0,1].

Since the mapping
t\mapsto\sin t/\sin(t+\theta)
is monotone on
[0,\pi-2\theta]
, we see from (b) that

\alpha_{k,1}\geq\alpha_{k-1,1}\geq\alpha_{k,2}\geq\alpha_{k-1,2}\geq\cdots\geq\alpha_{k,n}\geq\alpha_{k-1,n}

for every
k\in\{1,2,\dots,n\}
.
Therefore, Lemma
21
implies that there are
0=A_{0}\leq A_{1}\leq\cdots\leq A_{n}
in
\mathbb{M}_{n}
such that
\mathrm{rank}(A_{k}-A_{k-1})=1
and

\alpha_{k1}\geq\alpha_{k2}\geq\cdots\geq\alpha_{kn}
are the eigenvalues of
A_{k}
for every
k\in\{1,2,\dots,n\}
.
By (c),
A_{n}
is a projection that has the same rank as
P
.
Thus
A_{n}
is unitarily equivalent to
P
, which implies
\nu_{\mathbb{M}_{n}}(A_{n})=\nu_{\mathbb{M}_{n}}(P)
.
Take
B_{k}\in\mathbb{M}_{n}
satisfying
0\leq B_{k}\leq(\pi-2\theta)A_{n}
and the equation (
2
). Then (a) implies that
\mathrm{tr}\,B_{k}=k\theta
for every
k\in\{1,2,\dots,n\}
.
Therefore, by Propositions
9
and
20
, we obtain
\nu_{\mathbb{M}_{n}}(A_{n})\leq(2\cos\theta)^{-1}
.
∎

.

Proof of

{\mathcal{M}}

One may also take a system of matrix units

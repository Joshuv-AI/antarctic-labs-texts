Unitarity Bounds on the Massive Spin-2 Particle Explanation of Muon g − 2 g-2 Anomaly
Da Huang, Chao-Qiang Geng, Jiajun Wu

Abstract
Motivated by the long-standing discrepancy between the Standard Model prediction and the experimental measurement of the muon magnetic dipole moment, we have recently proposed to interpret this muon g − 2 g-2 anomaly in terms of the loop effect induced by a new massive spin-2 field G G . In the present paper, we investigate the unitarity bounds on this scenario. We calculate the s s -wave projected amplitudes for two-body elastic scatterings of charged leptons and photons mediated by G G at high energies for all possible initial and final helicity states. By imposing the condition of the perturbative unitarity, we obtain the analytic constraints on the charged-lepton- G G and photon- G G couplings. We then apply our results to constrain the parameter space relevant to the explanation of the muon g − 2 g-2 anomaly.

II
A Spin-2 Particle Model and its Explanation to Lepton
g-2

The Lagrangian for the spin-2 particle
G
explanation to the lepton
g-2
is given by
Han:1998sg

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{G}=-\frac{1}{\Lambda}G_{\mu\nu}\Big[c_{\gamma}T_{\gamma}^{\mu\nu}+\sum_{\ell=e,\mu,\tau}c_{\ell}T_{\ell}^{\mu\nu}\Big]\,,

(2)

where the stress-energy tensors,
T_{\ell}^{\mu\nu}
and
T_{\gamma}^{\mu\nu}
, of charged leptons and photons are defined by

\displaystyle T_{\ell}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{i}{4}\bar{\ell}(\gamma^{\mu}\partial^{\nu}+\gamma^{\nu}\partial^{\mu})\ell-\frac{i}{4}(\partial^{\mu}\bar{\ell}\gamma^{\nu}+\partial^{\nu}\bar{\ell}\gamma^{\mu})\ell

\displaystyle-i\eta^{\mu\nu}[\bar{\ell}\gamma^{\rho}\partial_{\rho}\ell+im_{\ell}\bar{\ell}\ell-\frac{1}{2}\partial^{\rho}(\bar{\ell}\gamma_{\rho}\ell)]\,,

\displaystyle T_{\gamma}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\eta^{\mu\nu}F^{\rho\sigma}F_{\rho\sigma}-F^{\mu\rho}F^{\nu}_{\rho}

(3)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\xi}\left[\eta^{\mu\nu}\left(\partial^{\rho}\partial^{\sigma}A_{\sigma}A_{\rho}+\frac{1}{2}(\partial^{\rho}A_{\rho})^{2}\right)-\left(\partial^{\mu}\partial^{\rho}A_{\rho}A^{\nu}+\partial^{\nu}\partial^{\rho}A_{\rho}A^{\mu}\right)\right]\,,

respectively, with
\xi
the gauge parameter for the photon field. This Lagrangian can be viewed as a part of the low-energy effective action in the generalized Randall-Sundrum model where the massive spin-2 particle is the first KK excitation of the conventional graviton
Randall:1999ee
;
Davoudiasl:1999tf
;
Pomarol:1999ad
;
Chang:1999nh
;
Davoudiasl:2000wi
;
Falkowski:2016glr
.

In the light of the effective interactions between
G
and charged leptons/photons in Eq. (
2
), we have drawn and calculated in Ref.
Huang:2022
the one-loop Feynman diagrams contributing to the charged lepton
g-2
, with the dominant contribution given by

\displaystyle\Delta a_{\ell}^{G}=\left(\frac{m_{\ell}^{2}}{\Lambda}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{\Lambda}{m_{G}}\right)^{4}\left(\frac{c_{\ell}^{2}}{48\pi^{2}}-\frac{c_{\ell}c_{\gamma}}{24\pi^{2}}\right)\,.

(4)

When deriving this leading-order result, we have applied the loop regularization
Wu:2002xa
;
Wu:2003dd
method to regularize the quartic divergences of loop integrals, which has been shown to maintain the gauge invariance of quantum electrodynamics and the correct divergence structure simultaneously.

Moreover, by requiring the validity of the perturbative expansion,
i.e.
, the one-loop contributions to the lepton
g-2
should dominate over the the two-loop ones, we obtain novel constraints from the perturbativity on our non-renormalizable spin-2 particle interactions as follows

\displaystyle|c_{\ell}|<4\pi\left(\frac{m_{G}}{\Lambda}\right)^{2}\,,\quad\quad|c_{\gamma}|<4\pi\left(\frac{m_{G}}{\Lambda}\right)^{2}\,,

(5)

which are obviously natural but non-trivial generalizations of perturbativity constraints on renormalizable operators
Nebot:2007bc
.

III
Unitarity Bounds

In this section, we
apply the tree-level unitarity bounds as our criterion to determine whether our perturbative calculations remain under control, which can give extra constraints to our spin-2 particle model. Concretely, the unitarity of the S-matrix imposes the following bound to the
s
-wave projected amplitude
a_{0}(\sqrt{s})

Lee:1977eg
;
Goodsell:2018tti
;
Banta:2021dek

\displaystyle{\rm Re}(a_{0})(\sqrt{s})\leq\frac{1}{2}\,,

(6)

where
a_{0}(\sqrt{s})
is defined as

\displaystyle a_{0}(\sqrt{s})=\sqrt{\frac{4|{\bf p}_{i}||{\bf p}_{f}|}{2^{\delta_{i}+\delta_{f}}s}}\frac{1}{32\pi}\int^{1}_{-1}d(\cos\theta){\cal M}(i\to f)=\sqrt{\frac{4|{\bf p}_{i}||{\bf p}_{f}|}{2^{\delta_{i}+\delta_{f}}s}}\frac{1}{16\pi}\int^{0}_{-s}\frac{dt}{s}{\cal M}(i\to f)\,,

(7)

in which the indices
\delta_{i,f}=1
if the two particles in the initial or final states are identical to each other, otherwise
\delta_{i,f}=0
.
In our model, we have two kinds of effective vertices: the photon and lepton couplings to the spin-2 particle
G
, so we need to compute the amplitudes for
\ell^{-}\ell^{+}\to\ell^{-}\ell^{+}
and
\gamma\gamma\to\gamma\gamma
of various helicity assignments to determine their respective bounds.

III.1
\ell^{-}\ell^{+}\to\ell^{-}\ell^{+}

We begin by considering the perturbative unitarity bounds for the lepton-
G
couplings
c_{\ell}
with
\ell=e
,
\mu
and
\tau
, which can be derived from the amplitudes of
\ell^{-}\ell^{+}\to\ell^{-}\ell^{+}
of different
\ell^{\pm}
helicity configurations. Given the Lagrangian in Eq. (
2
), there are two Feynman diagrams contributing to this process, which are shown in Fig.
1
with left and right panels corresponding to the
s
- and
t
-channels, respectively.

Figure 1:
Feynman diagrams which give rise to the scattering process
\ell^{-}\ell^{+}\to\ell^{-}\ell^{+}

Note that the lepton-
G
interaction in Eq. (
2
) is
P
-invariant,
i.e.
, if the two scatterings of different helicity configurations are related by the parity
{P}
transformation, then they would be equal in amplitude. For example, we have the following relations

\displaystyle{\cal M}(\ell_{R}^{-}\ell^{+}_{L}\to\ell^{-}_{R}\ell^{+}_{L})={\cal M}(\ell^{-}_{L}\ell^{+}_{R}\to\ell^{-}_{L}\ell^{+}_{R})\,,\quad{\cal M}(\ell_{R}^{-}\ell^{+}_{R}\to\ell^{-}_{R}\ell^{+}_{R})={\cal M}(\ell^{-}_{L}\ell^{+}_{L}\to\ell^{-}_{L}\ell^{+}_{L})\,.

(8)

Also, we will work in the high-energy limit in which the lepton masses can be ignored compared with the external particle momenta.
In what follows of this subsection, we shall present the detailed calculations of independent non-zero
\ell^{-}\ell^{+}\to\ell^{-}\ell^{+}
helicity amplitudes. By applying the unitarity bound on these amplitudes, we can obtain constraints on

III.1.1
\ell^{-}_{R}\ell^{+}_{L}\to\ell^{-}_{R}\ell^{+}_{L}

Let us begin by computing the amplitude of
\ell^{-}_{R}\ell^{+}_{L}\to\ell^{-}_{R}\ell^{+}_{L}
. In the massless limit, the momenta of external particles in the center-of-mass (com) frame are given by:

\displaystyle{\rm Incoming}:

\displaystyle\quad\ell^{-}_{R}:\quad p_{1}=(E,0,0,E)\,,\quad\quad\ell^{+}_{L}:\quad p_{2}=(E,0,0,-E)\,

(9)

\displaystyle{\rm Outgoing}:

\displaystyle\quad\ell^{-}_{R}:\quad k_{1}=(E,E\sin\theta,0,E\cos\theta)\,,\quad\quad\ell^{+}_{L}:\quad k_{2}=(E,-E\sin\theta,0,-E\cos\theta)\,,

while the polarization vectors are denoted as

\displaystyle{\rm Incoming}

\displaystyle:u_{R}(p_{1})=\sqrt{2E}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
0\\
1\\
0\end{array}\right)\,,\quad v_{L}(p_{2})=\sqrt{2E}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}0\\

-1\\
\end{array}\right)\,,

\displaystyle{\rm Outgoing}

\displaystyle:u_{R}(k_{1})=\sqrt{2E}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
0\\
\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\\
\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\end{array}\right)\,,\quad v_{L}(k_{2})=\sqrt{2E}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
0\\
\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\\
-\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\\
\end{array}\right)\,.

The total amplitude is composed of the
s
- and
t
-channel ones. The
s
-channel amplitude is given by

\displaystyle i{\cal M}_{s}(\ell_{R}^{-}\ell^{+}_{L}\to\ell^{-}_{R}\ell^{+}_{L})

\displaystyle=-\frac{ic_{\ell}^{2}}{32\Lambda^{2}}\bar{v}_{L}(p_{2})[\gamma^{\mu}(p_{1}^{\nu}-p_{2}^{\nu})+\gamma^{\nu}(p_{1}^{\mu}-p_{2}^{\mu})-2\eta^{\mu\nu}(\not{p}_{1}-\not{p}_{2}-2m_{\ell})]u_{R}(p_{1})

\displaystyle\bar{u}_{R}(k_{1})[\gamma^{\rho}(k_{1}^{\sigma}-k_{2}^{\sigma})+\gamma^{\sigma}(k_{1}^{\rho}-k_{2}^{\rho})-2\eta^{\rho\sigma}(\not{k}_{1}-\not{k}_{2}-2m_{\ell})]v_{L}(k_{2})\frac{B_{\mu\nu,\rho\sigma}}{Q^{2}-m_{G}^{2}}\,.

By using the external particles’ equations of motion
\not{p}_{1}u_{R}(p_{1})=m_{\ell}u_{R}(p_{1})\simeq 0
and
\not{p}_{2}v_{L}(p_{2})=-m_{\ell}v_{L}(p_{2})\simeq 0
where the symbol

represents the massless limit, the above

-channel amplitude can be simplified to

\displaystyle i{\cal M}_{s}(\ell_{R}^{-}\ell^{+}_{L}\to\ell^{-}_{R}\ell^{+}_{L})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{ic_{\ell}^{2}}{8\Lambda^{2}}\big\{(p_{1}-p_{2})(k_{1}-k_{2})[\bar{u}_{R}(k_{1})\gamma^{\mu}v_{L}(k_{2})][\bar{v}_{L}(p_{2})\gamma_{\mu}u_{R}(p_{1})]

(29)

\displaystyle+[\bar{u}_{R}(k_{1})(\not{p}_{1}-\not{p}_{2})v_{L}(k_{2})][\bar{v}_{L}(p_{2})(\not{k}_{1}-\not{k}_{2})u_{R}(p_{1})]\big\}\frac{1}{Q^{2}-m_{G}^{2}}\,.

Now we turn to compute the fermion current in this
s

\ell^{-}_{R}\ell^{+}_{L}\to\ell^{-}_{R}\ell^{+}_{L}
process. Note that

\displaystyle\gamma^{0}\gamma^{\mu}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\

(L,R)
and
(R,L)

\ell^{-}_{R}\ell^{+}_{L}

\displaystyle\bar{v}_{L}(p_{2})\gamma^{\mu}u_{R}(p_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(2E)(0,-1)\sigma^{\mu}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
0\end{array}\right)=(2E)(0,-1,-i,0)\,,

\displaystyle\bar{v}_{L}(k_{2})\gamma^{\mu}u_{R}(k_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(2E)(\sin\frac{\theta}{2},-\cos\frac{\theta}{2})\sigma^{\mu}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\\
\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\end{array}\right)=(2E)(0,-\cos\theta,-i,\sin\theta)\,,

so that

\displaystyle[\bar{u}_{R}(k_{1})\gamma^{\mu}v_{L}(k_{2})][\bar{v}_{L}(p_{2})\gamma_{\mu}u_{R}(p_{1})]=-(2E)^{2}(1+\cos\theta)=2u\,,

(43)

\displaystyle\bar{v}_{L}(p_{2})(\not{k}_{1}-\not{k}_{2})u_{R}(p_{1})=(2E)^{2}\sin\theta=s\sin\theta\,,

\displaystyle\bar{u}_{R}(k_{1})(\not{p}_{1}-\not{p}_{2})v_{L}(k_{2})=-(2E)^{2}\sin\theta=-s\sin\theta\,.

Consequently, the s-channel amplitude can be given by

\displaystyle i{\cal M}_{s}=-\frac{4ic_{\ell}^{2}}{32\Lambda^{2}}\frac{\left(2u(u-t)-s^{2}\sin^{2}\theta\right)}{s-m_{G}^{2}}=-\frac{ic_{\ell}^{2}}{4\Lambda^{2}}\frac{u(u-3t)}{s-m_{G}^{2}}=-\frac{ic_{\ell}^{2}}{4\Lambda^{2}}\frac{(s+t)(s+4t)}{s-m_{G}^{2}}\,,

(45)

where in the last equality we have used the following relations

\displaystyle s(1+\cos\theta)=-2u\,,\quad s(1-\cos\theta)=-2t,\quad 4ut=s^{2}\sin^{2}\theta\,.

(46)

Now we come to compute
t
-channel contribution, with the amplitude given by

\displaystyle i{\cal M}_{t}(\ell^{-}_{R}\ell^{+}_{L}\to\ell^{-}_{R}\ell^{+}_{L})

\displaystyle=-\frac{ic_{\ell}^{2}}{32\Lambda^{2}}\bar{v}_{L}(p_{2})[\gamma^{\mu}(-p_{2}^{\nu}-k_{2}^{\nu})+\gamma^{\nu}(-p_{2}^{\mu}-k_{2}^{\mu})-2\eta^{\mu\nu}(-\not{p}_{2}-\not{k}_{2}-2m_{\ell})]v_{L}(k_{2})

(47)

\displaystyle\bar{u}_{R}(k_{1})[\gamma^{\rho}(p_{1}^{\sigma}+k_{1}^{\sigma})+\gamma^{\sigma}(p_{1}^{\rho}+k_{1}^{\rho})-2\eta^{\rho\sigma}(\not{p}_{1}+\not{k}_{1}-2m_{\ell})]u_{R}(p_{1})\frac{B_{\mu\nu,\rho\sigma}}{q^{2}-m_{G}^{2}}\,.

By using the lepton on-shell conditions, the
t
-channel amplitude can be reduced into

\displaystyle i{\cal M}_{t}(\ell^{-}_{R}\ell^{+}_{L}\to\ell^{-}_{R}\ell^{+}_{L})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4ic_{\ell}^{2}}{32\Lambda^{2}}\frac{1}{q^{2}-m_{G}^{2}}\big\{(k_{1}+p_{1})(k_{2}+p_{2})[\bar{u}_{R}(k_{1})\gamma^{\mu}u_{R}(p_{1})][\bar{v}_{L}(p_{2})\gamma_{\mu}v_{L}(k_{2})]

(48)

\displaystyle+[\bar{u}_{R}(k_{1})(\not{k}_{2}+\not{p}_{2})u_{R}(p_{1})][\bar{v}_{L}(p_{2})(\not{k}_{1}+\not{p}_{1})v_{L}(k_{2})]\big\}\,.

In the center of mass frame, the t-channel amplitude can be further simplified to

\displaystyle i{\cal M}_{t}(\ell^{-}_{R}\ell^{+}_{L}\to\ell^{-}_{R}\ell^{+}_{L})=\frac{ic_{\ell}^{2}}{4\Lambda^{2}}\frac{u(u-3s)}{t-m_{G}^{2}}=\frac{ic_{\ell}^{2}}{4\Lambda^{2}}\frac{(t+s)(t+4s)}{t-m_{G}^{2}}\,.

(49)

Therefore, the total amplitude is given by summing over the
s
- and
t
-channel amplitudes:

\displaystyle i{\cal M}(\ell^{-}_{R}\ell^{+}_{L}\to\ell^{-}_{R}\ell^{+}_{L})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{ic_{\ell}^{2}}{4\Lambda^{2}}\left[\frac{u(u-3t)}{s-m_{G}^{2}}-\frac{u(u-3s)}{t-m_{G}^{2}}\right]

(50)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{ic_{\ell}^{2}}{4\Lambda^{2}}\left[\frac{(s+t)(s+4t)}{s-m_{G}^{2}}-\frac{(t+s)(t+4s)}{t-m_{G}^{2}}\right]\,.

Here the minus sign between the two terms in the bracket can be understood to come from the interchange the two external fermion particles of momenta
k_{1}
and
p_{2}

\displaystyle\bar{u}_{R}(k_{1})(\not{p}_{2}+\not{k}_{2})u_{R}(p_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2(2E)^{2}\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\,,

\displaystyle\bar{v}_{R}(p_{2})(\not{p}_{1}+\not{k}_{1})v_{R}(k_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2(2E)^{2}\cos\frac{\theta}{2}\,.

(81)

It turns out that the final expression for the amplitude of
\ell_{R}^{-}\ell_{R}^{+}\to\ell_{R}^{-}\ell_{R}^{+}
is given by

\displaystyle i{\cal M}(\ell_{R}^{-}\ell_{R}^{+}\to\ell_{R}^{-}\ell_{R}^{+})=i{\cal M}_{t}(\ell_{R}^{-}\ell_{R}^{+}\to\ell_{R}^{-}\ell_{R}^{+})=\frac{ic_{\ell}^{2}}{4\Lambda^{2}}\frac{s(s-3u)}{t-m_{G}^{2}}\,.

(82)

III.1.4
Unitarity Bounds for
\ell^{-}\ell^{+}\to\ell^{-}\ell^{+}

In the above discussion, we obtains all of the nonzero
\ell^{-}\ell^{+}\to\ell^{-}\ell^{+}
amplitudes of different helicity configurations, which are summarized again as follows

\displaystyle{\cal M}(\ell^{-}_{R}\ell^{+}_{L}\to\ell^{-}_{R}\ell^{+}_{L})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\cal M}(\ell^{-}_{L}\ell^{+}_{R}\to\ell^{-}_{L}\ell^{+}_{R})=-\frac{c_{\ell}^{2}}{4\Lambda^{2}}\left[\frac{u(u-3t)}{s-m_{G}^{2}}-\frac{u(u-3s)}{t-m_{G}^{2}}\right]\,,

\displaystyle{\cal M}(\ell^{-}_{R}\ell^{+}_{R}\to\ell^{-}_{R}\ell^{+}_{R})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\cal M}(\ell^{-}_{L}\ell^{+}_{L}\to\ell^{-}_{L}\ell^{+}_{L})=\frac{c_{\ell}^{2}}{4\Lambda^{2}}\frac{s(s-3u)}{t-m_{G}^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle{\cal M}(\ell^{-}_{R}\ell^{+}_{L}\to\ell^{-}_{L}\ell^{+}_{R})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\cal M}(\ell^{-}_{L}\ell^{+}_{R}\to\ell^{-}_{R}\ell^{+}_{L})=-\frac{c_{\ell}^{2}}{4\Lambda^{2}}\frac{t(t-3u)}{s-m_{G}^{2}}\,,

(83)

where the two amplitudes in a single line are
P
-symmetric to one another, while other helicity configurations would lead to vanishing amplitudes. By using the definition of
a_{0}(\sqrt{s})
in Eq. (
7
), we can obtain the
s
-wave projected amplitudes, given by

\displaystyle a_{0}(\ell^{-}_{R}\ell^{+}_{L}\to\ell^{-}_{R}\ell^{+}_{L})=a_{0}(\ell^{-}_{L}\ell^{+}_{R}\to\ell^{-}_{L}\ell^{+}_{R})

(84)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{16\pi s}\frac{c_{\ell}^{2}}{4\Lambda^{2}}\left[-\frac{s(28s^{2}-21m_{G}^{2}s-6m_{G}^{4})}{6(s-m_{G}^{2})}+(4s+m_{G}^{2})(s+m_{G}^{2})\ln\left(\frac{s+m_{G}^{2}}{m_{G}^{2}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{c_{\ell}^{2}}{16\pi}\frac{s}{4\Lambda^{2}}\left(\frac{14}{3}-4\ln\frac{s}{m_{G}^{2}}\right)\sim\frac{c_{\ell}^{2}}{16\pi}\left(\frac{14}{3}-4\ln\frac{4\Lambda^{2}}{m_{G}^{2}}\right)\,,

\displaystyle a_{0}(\ell^{-}_{R}\ell^{+}_{R}\to\ell^{-}_{R}\ell^{+}_{R})=a_{0}(\ell^{-}_{L}\ell^{+}_{L}\to\ell^{-}_{L}\ell^{+}_{L})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{16\pi}\frac{c_{\ell}^{2}}{4\Lambda^{2}}\left[3s-(4s+3m_{G}^{2})\ln\left(\frac{s+m_{G}^{2}}{m_{G}^{2}}\right)\right]\approx\frac{c_{\ell}^{2}}{16\pi}\frac{s}{4\Lambda^{2}}\left(3-4\ln\frac{s}{m_{G}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{c_{\ell}^{2}}{16\pi}\left(3-4\ln\frac{4\Lambda^{2}}{m_{G}^{2}}\right)\,,

\displaystyle a_{0}(\ell^{-}_{R}\ell^{+}_{L}\to\ell^{-}_{L}\ell^{+}_{R})=a_{0}(\ell^{-}_{L}\ell^{+}_{R}\to\ell^{-}_{R}\ell^{+}_{L})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{16\pi}\frac{c_{\ell}^{2}}{4\Lambda^{2}}\frac{s^{2}}{6(s-m_{G}^{2})}\approx\frac{1}{16\pi}\frac{c_{\ell}^{2}}{6}\frac{s}{4\Lambda^{2}}\sim\frac{c_{\ell}^{2}}{96\pi}\,.

where the symbol
\sim
denotes the high energy limit with
s\to 4\Lambda^{2}
. By requiring the
s
-wave projected amplitudes to satisfy the unitarity bound
{\rm Re}[a_{0}(\sqrt{s})]\leq 1/2
, we can obtain the following constraints on the Wilsonian coefficient
c_{\ell}

\displaystyle\ell^{-}_{R}\ell^{+}_{L}\to\ell^{-}_{R}\ell^{+}_{L}:\quad c_{\ell}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{8\pi}{\left|\frac{14}{3}-4\ln\frac{4\Lambda^{2}}{m_{G}^{2}}\right|}}\,,

(85)

\displaystyle\ell^{-}_{R}\ell^{+}_{L}\to\ell^{-}_{L}\ell^{+}_{R}:\quad c_{\ell}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{48\pi}\,,

(86)

\displaystyle\ell^{-}_{R}\ell^{+}_{R}\to\ell^{-}_{R}\ell^{+}_{R}:\quad c_{\ell}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{8\pi}{\left|3-4\ln\frac{4\Lambda^{2}}{m_{G}^{2}}\right|}}\,.

(87)

In Fig.

III.2
\gamma\gamma\to\gamma\gamma

In order to obtain the unitarity bounds on the photon-
G
coupling
c_{\gamma}
, we have to calculate the
\gamma\gamma\to\gamma\gamma
amplitudes of various polarization configurations. The relevant Feynman diagrams are shown in Fig.

, which correspond to the
s
-,

-, and
u
-channels, respectively. As the photon-

vertex in Eq. (
2
) preserves the spatial parity
P
symmetry, the amplitudes of different photon polarizations are the same to each other when they are related by parity transformation, which greatly reduces the number of independent photon 2-to-2 scattering amplitudes. In this subsection, we compute these independent nonzero photon scattering amplitudes, based on which we can yield the associated unitarity bounds on

.

III.2.1
\gamma_{R}\gamma_{R}\to\gamma_{R}\gamma_{R}

Firstly, we consider the amplitude of
\gamma_{R}\gamma_{R}\to\gamma_{R}\gamma_{R}
, where the four external photons are all right-handed.
In the com frame, the two incoming photons are specified by

\displaystyle p_{1}^{\mu}=(E,0,0,E)\,,

\displaystyle\epsilon^{\mu}_{R}(p_{1})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(0,-1,-i,0)\,,

\displaystyle p_{2}^{\nu}=(E,0,0,-E)\,,

\displaystyle\epsilon^{\nu}_{R}(p_{2})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(0,1,-i,0)\,,

(88)

while the two outgoing photons are represented by

\displaystyle k_{1}^{\rho}=(E,E\sin\theta,0,E\cos\theta)\,,

\displaystyle\epsilon^{*}_{R}(k_{1})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(0,-\cos\theta,i,\sin\theta)\,,

\displaystyle k_{2}^{\sigma}=(E,-E\sin\theta,0,-E\cos\theta)\,,

\displaystyle\epsilon^{*}_{R}(k_{2})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(0,\cos\theta,i,-\sin\theta)\,.

(89)

The amplitude constitutes three Feynman diagrams shown in Fig.
3
, corresponding to
s
-,
t
- and
u
-channels, respectively. In the following, we shall calculate them one by one. According to the Feynman rules, the
s
-channel amplitude is given by

\displaystyle i{\cal M}_{s}(\gamma_{R}\gamma_{R}\to\gamma_{R}\gamma_{R})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{ic_{\gamma}^{2}}{2\Lambda^{2}}\epsilon_{R}^{\mu}(p_{1})\epsilon_{R}^{\nu}(p_{2})\epsilon_{R}^{*}(k_{1})\epsilon^{*}_{R}(k_{2})[(p_{1}\cdot p_{2})C_{\alpha\beta,\mu\nu}+D_{\alpha\beta,\mu\nu}(p_{1},p_{2})]

(90)

\displaystyle\frac{B^{\alpha\beta,\lambda\delta}(Q)}{Q^{2}-m_{G}^{2}}[(k_{1}\cdot k_{2})C_{\lambda\delta,\rho\sigma}+D_{\lambda\delta,\rho\sigma}(k_{1},k_{2})]\,.

Note that, by using the relation
Q=p_{1}+p_{2}=k_{1}+k_{2}
, we find that terms proportional to four- and two-powers of
1/m_{G}
are canceled. By further making use of the specific momenta and polarization vectors of the four external particles in Eqs. (
III.2.1
) and (
III.2.1
), the
s
-channel amplitude vanishes, while the
t
- and
u
-channel amplitudes are given by

\displaystyle i{\cal M}_{t}(\gamma_{R}\gamma_{R}\to\gamma_{R}\gamma_{R})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{ic_{\gamma}^{2}}{2\Lambda^{2}}\epsilon_{R}^{\mu}(p_{1})\epsilon_{R}^{\nu}(p_{2})\epsilon_{R}^{*}(k_{1})\epsilon^{*}_{R}(k_{2})[(-p_{1}\cdot k_{1})C_{\alpha\beta,\mu\rho}+D_{\alpha\beta,\mu\rho}(p_{1},-k_{1})]

(91)

\displaystyle\frac{B^{\alpha\beta,\lambda\delta}(q)}{q^{2}-m_{G}^{2}}[(-p_{2}\cdot k_{2})C_{\lambda\delta,\nu\sigma}+D_{\lambda\delta,\nu\sigma}(p_{2},-k_{2})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{ic_{\gamma}^{2}}{2\Lambda^{2}}\frac{2s^{2}}{t-m_{G}^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle i{\cal M}_{u}(\gamma_{R}\gamma_{R}\to\gamma_{R}\gamma_{R})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{ic_{\gamma}^{2}}{2\Lambda^{2}}\epsilon_{R}^{\mu}(p_{1})\epsilon_{R}^{\nu}(p_{2})\epsilon_{R}^{*}(k_{1})\epsilon^{*}_{R}(k_{2})[(-p_{1}\cdot k_{2})c_{\alpha\beta,\mu\rho}+D_{\alpha\beta,\mu\rho}(p_{1},-k_{2})]

(92)

\displaystyle\frac{B^{\alpha\beta,\lambda\delta}(q^{\prime})}{q^{\prime\,2}-m_{G}^{2}}[(-p_{2}\cdot k_{1})C_{\lambda\delta,\nu\sigma}+D_{\lambda\delta,\nu\sigma}(p_{2},-k_{1})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{ic_{\gamma}^{2}}{2\Lambda^{2}}\frac{2s^{2}}{u-m_{G}^{2}}\,,

where
q\equiv p_{1}-k_{1}=k_{2}-p_{2}
and
q^{\prime}=p_{1}-k_{2}=k_{1}-p_{2}
, so that
q^{2}=t
and
q^{\prime\,2}=u
. Therefore, the total amplitude of
\gamma_{R}\gamma_{R}\to\gamma_{R}\gamma_{R}
is written as

\displaystyle i{\cal M}(\gamma_{R}\gamma_{R}\to\gamma_{R}\gamma_{R})=-\frac{iC_{\gamma}^{2}}{2\Lambda^{2}}\left(\frac{2s^{2}}{t-m_{G}^{2}}+\frac{2s^{2}}{u-m_{G}^{2}}\right)\,.

(93)

III.2.2
\gamma_{R}\gamma_{L}\to\gamma_{R}\gamma_{L}

We now turn to the process of
\gamma_{R}\gamma_{L}\to\gamma_{R}\gamma_{L}
. In the com frame, we can define the polarization vectors of the incoming particles as follows

\displaystyle\epsilon_{R}^{\mu}(p_{1})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(0,-1,-i,0)^{\mu}\,,\quad\epsilon_{L}^{\nu}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(0,-1,-i,0)^{\nu}\,,

(94)

while those of the outgoing particles are given by

\displaystyle\epsilon_{R}^{*}(k_{1})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(0,-\cos\theta,i,\sin\theta)\,,\quad\epsilon^{*}_{L}(k_{2})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(0,-\cos\theta,i,\sin\theta)\,.

(95)

By taking the above expressions of the polarization vectors and momenta of the external particles into formulas for the
s
-,
t
- and
u
-channels, we can easily obtain the following partial amplitudes

\displaystyle i{\cal M}_{s}(\gamma_{R}\gamma_{L}\to\gamma_{R}\gamma_{L})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{ic_{\gamma}^{2}}{2\Lambda^{2}}\frac{2u^{2}}{s-m_{G}^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle i{\cal M}_{t}(\gamma_{R}\gamma_{L}\to\gamma_{R}\gamma_{L})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{ic_{\gamma}^{2}}{2\Lambda^{2}}\frac{2u^{2}}{t-m_{G}^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle i{\cal M}_{u}(\gamma_{R}\gamma_{L}\to\gamma_{R}\gamma_{L})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\,.

(96)

Thus, the total amplitude is the summation over the
s
-,
t
- and
u
-channels

\displaystyle i{\cal M}(\gamma_{R}\gamma_{L}\to\gamma_{R}\gamma_{L})=-\frac{ic_{\gamma}^{2}}{2\Lambda^{2}}\left(\frac{2u^{2}}{s-m_{G}^{2}}+\frac{2u^{2}}{t-m_{G}^{2}}\right)\,.

(97)

III.2.3
\gamma_{R}\gamma_{L}\to\gamma_{L}\gamma_{R}

In the case of
\gamma_{R}\gamma_{L}\to\gamma_{L}\gamma_{R}
, the polarization vectors for the external photons in the com frame are given by

\displaystyle\epsilon_{R}(p_{1})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(0,-1,-i,0)\,,\quad\epsilon_{L}(p_{2})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(0,-1,-i,0)\,,

\displaystyle\epsilon^{*}_{L}(k_{1})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(0,\cos\theta,i-\sin\theta)\,,\quad\epsilon_{R}^{*}(k_{2})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(0,\cos\theta,i,-\sin\theta)\,.

(98)

Following exactly the same procedure in the previous subsections, we can derive the following amplitudes for the
s
-,
t
- and
u
-channels of
\gamma_{R}\gamma_{L}\to\gamma_{L}\gamma_{R}
:

\displaystyle i{\cal M}_{s}(\gamma_{R}\gamma_{L}\to\gamma_{L}\gamma_{R})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{ic_{\gamma}^{2}}{2\Lambda^{2}}\frac{2t^{2}}{s-m_{G}^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle i{\cal M}_{t}(\gamma_{R}\gamma_{L}\to\gamma_{L}\gamma_{R})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\,,

\displaystyle i{\cal M}_{u}(\gamma_{R}\gamma_{L}\to\gamma_{L}\gamma_{R})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{ic_{\gamma}^{2}}{2\Lambda^{2}}\frac{2t^{2}}{u-m_{G}^{2}}\,.

It turns out that the total amplitude of

\displaystyle i{\cal M}(\gamma_{R}\gamma_{L}\to\gamma_{L}\gamma_{R})=-\frac{ic_{\gamma}^{2}}{2\Lambda^{2}}\left(\frac{2t^{2}}{s-m_{G}^{2}}+\frac{2t^{2}}{u-m_{G}^{2}}\right)\,.

(100)

III.2.4
Unitarity Bounds for
\gamma\gamma\to\gamma\gamma

Here we summarize the nonzero independent
\gamma\gamma\to\gamma\gamma
amplitudes of different polarizations as follows

\displaystyle i{\cal M}(\gamma_{R}\gamma_{L}\to\gamma_{R}\gamma_{L})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i{\cal M}(\gamma_{L}\gamma_{R}\to\gamma_{L}\gamma_{R})=-\frac{ic_{\gamma}^{2}}{2\Lambda^{2}}\left(\frac{2u^{2}}{s-m_{G}^{2}}+\frac{2u^{2}}{t-m_{G}^{2}}\right)\,,

\displaystyle i{\cal M}(\gamma_{R}\gamma_{L}\to\gamma_{L}\gamma_{R})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i{\cal M}(\gamma_{L}\gamma_{R}\to\gamma_{R}\gamma_{L})=-\frac{ic_{\gamma}^{2}}{2\Lambda^{2}}\left(\frac{2t^{2}}{s-m_{G}^{2}}+\frac{2t^{2}}{u-m_{G}^{2}}\right)\,,

\displaystyle i{\cal M}(\gamma_{R}\gamma_{R}\to\gamma_{R}\gamma_{R})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i{\cal M}(\gamma_{L}\gamma_{L}\to\gamma_{L}\gamma_{L})=-\frac{ic_{\gamma}^{2}}{2\Lambda^{2}}\left(\frac{2s^{2}}{t-m_{G}^{2}}+\frac{2s^{2}}{u-m_{G}^{2}}\right)\,,

(101)

while all other polarization amplitudes vanish identically. According to the definition of
a_{0}(\sqrt{s})
in Eq. (
7
), the
s
-wave projected amplitudes for the nontrivial polarization assignments are given by

\displaystyle a_{0}(\gamma_{R}\gamma_{R}\to\gamma_{R}\gamma_{R})=a_{0}(\gamma_{L}\gamma_{L}\to\gamma_{L}\gamma_{L})

(102)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{c_{\gamma}^{2}}{4\pi}\frac{s}{4\Lambda^{2}}\ln\frac{s+m_{G}^{2}}{m_{G}^{2}}\approx\frac{c_{\gamma}^{2}}{4\pi}\frac{s}{4\Lambda^{2}}\ln\frac{s}{m_{G}^{2}}\sim\frac{c_{\gamma}^{2}}{4\pi}\ln\frac{4\Lambda^{2}}{m_{G}^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle a_{0}(\gamma_{R}\gamma_{L}\to\gamma_{R}\gamma_{L})=a_{0}(\gamma_{R}\gamma_{L}\to\gamma_{L}\gamma_{R})=a_{0}(\gamma_{L}\gamma_{R}\to\gamma_{L}\gamma_{R})=a_{0}(\gamma_{L}\gamma_{R}\to\gamma_{R}\gamma_{L})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{32\pi s}\frac{c_{\gamma}^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}\left[\frac{s(11s^{2}-3m_{G}^{2}s-6m_{G}^{4})}{6(s-m_{G}^{2})}-(s+m_{G}^{2})\ln\frac{s+m_{G}^{2}}{m_{G}^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle-\frac{c_{\gamma}^{2}}{8\pi}\frac{s}{4\Lambda^{2}}\left(\frac{11}{6}-\ln\frac{s}{m_{G}^{2}}\right)\sim-\frac{c_{\gamma}^{2}}{8\pi}\left(\frac{11}{6}-\ln\frac{4\Lambda^{2}}{m_{G}^{2}}\right)\,,

where we have taken the high-energy limit with
s\sim 4\Lambda^{2}\gg m_{G}^{2}
.
Therefore, the unitarity bounds for these channels are given by

\displaystyle\gamma_{R}\gamma_{R}\to\gamma_{R}\gamma_{R}:\quad c_{\gamma}\lesssim\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{\left|\ln\frac{4\Lambda^{2}}{m_{G}^{2}}\right|}}\,,

(103)

\displaystyle\gamma_{R}\gamma_{L}\to\gamma_{R}\gamma_{L}:\quad c_{\gamma}\lesssim\sqrt{\frac{4\pi}{\left|\frac{11}{6}-\ln\frac{4\Lambda^{2}}{m_{G}^{2}}\right|}}\,.

(104)

V
Conclusions

In Ref.
Huang:2022
, we explain the long-standing discrepancy between the experimental measurements and the SM prediction of the muon
g-2
in terms of a new massive spin-2 particle, which can be easily derived in the generalized RS models. Note that we have only considered the perturbativity constraints in Ref.
Huang:2022
on the model parameter space. In the present paper, we have further investigated unitarity bounds on this spin-2 particle model.
We have obtained the bounds by computing the
s
-wave projected amplitudes for two-body elastic scatterings of charged leptons and photons in the high-energy limit for all possible initial and final helicity states. As a result, we have found that the most stringent constraints on the lepton-
G
coupling
c_{\ell}
and on the photon-
G
coupling
c_{\gamma}
are yielded via the processes
\ell^{-}_{R}\ell^{+}_{R}\to\ell^{-}_{R}\ell^{+}_{R}
and
\gamma_{R}\gamma_{R}\to\gamma_{R}\gamma_{R}
, respectively. We have applies the obtained unitarity bounds to numerically explore the spin-2 particle parameter space again. It turns out that the tree-level
S
-matrix unitarity gives useful constraints on the muon
g-2
preferred parameter regions, which is complementary to the perturbativity.
In particular, it is seen that the unitarity bounds give the strongest limits on
c_{\ell}
and
c_{\gamma}
in the large
m_{G}
region. Nevertheless, despite the stringent constraints imposed by the perturbativity and unitarity, there is still a considerable portion of parameter spaces which can explain the muon
g-2
anomaly. By further extending this analysis to the electron
g-2
, we have shown that the LKB measurement of
\Delta a_{e}^{\rm LKB}
can be compatible with the latest
\Delta a_{\mu}
data, while
\Delta a_{e}^{\rm B}
obtained at Berkeley cannot be explained by the present spin-2 particle model due to the strong constraints from the perturbativity and unitarity.

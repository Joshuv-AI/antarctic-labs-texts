Regularity of minimal surfaces with capillary boundary conditions
Luigi De Masi, Nick Edelen, Carlo Gasparetto, Chao Li

Abstract
. We prove ε \varepsilon -regularity theorems for varifolds with capillary boundary condition in a Riemannian manifold. These varifolds were first introduced by Kagaya-Tonegawa [ 16 ] . We establish a uniform first variation control for all such varifolds (and free-boundary varifolds generally) satisfying a sharp density bound and prove that if a capillary varifold has bounded mean curvature and is close to a capillary half-plane with angle not equal to π 2 \tfrac{\pi}{2} , then it coincides with a C 1 , α C^{1,\alpha} properly embedded hypersurface. We apply our theorem to deduce regularity at a generic point along the boundary in the region where the density is strictly less than 1 1 .



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we are interested in the boundary regularity of capillary hypersurfaces, which are surfaces with prescribed mean curvature that meet the container boundary at a prescribed angle. Such surfaces arise as critical points of the Gauss’ free energy: given a Riemannian manifold with boundary
(X^{n+1},g)
, functions
h,\beta\in C^{1}(X)
, the Gauss’ free energy for an open subset
E\subset X
is defined as

(1.1)

\mathcal{A}(E)=\mathcal{H}^{n}(\partial E\cap\mathring{X})+\int_{\partial E\cap\partial X}\cos\beta(x)d\mathcal{H}^{n}+\int_{E}h(x)d\mathcal{H}^{n+1}.

When
E\subset X
is stationary for
\mathcal{A}
, or stationary among domains with a fixed volume, the interface
M=\partial E\cap\mathring{X}
is called a capillary (hyper)surface. Such surfaces are the mathematical model for interfaces of incompressible fluids in an equilibrium state. Formally (or when
M
is sufficiently regular), the Euler-Lagrange equation for
M
- also known as
Young’s law

[
31
]
- asserts that the mean curvature of
M
equals
h
(plus a constant, if volume-constrained), and that
M
meets
\partial X
at an angle
\beta
. The existence, regularity, and geometric properties of capillary surfaces have attracted active investigations for the past two centuries, see, e.g.
[
11
]
for a beautiful historical survey on the subject. There have also been interesting developments and geometric applications in recent years, see, e.g.
[
18
,
15
,
30
,
6
,
19
,
2
]
.
A non-local version of Gauss’ energy was introduced in
[
21
]
, see also the survey
[
27
]
and the references therein.
For a capillary problem involving the one-phase Bernoulli free boundary problem, see
[
10
]
.

The primary scope of this paper is to establish Allard type
[
1
]
,
\varepsilon
-regularity results for capillary hypersurfaces near their boundary. Such results are fundamental in understanding the regularity of stationary capillary hypersurfaces, which can arise not only in minimization problems but also in min-max constructions. Our weak notion of a hypersurface is a stationary varifold, and since our regularity results are local, we model a neighborhood of
p\in\partial X
with the half-ball
B_{1}\cap\{x_{1}\leq 0\}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
endowed with a Riemannian metric
g
.

We remark here that an
\varepsilon
-regularity theorem for
minimizing
capillary hypersurfaces was obtained by Taylor in
[
26

(when

), and by De Philippis-Maggi in
[
8
]
(in general dimensions). Let us also remark that in the special case when

[

Varifolds with prescribed contact angle arise naturally as critical points of the Gauss’ free energy (
1.1
).
Indeed, assume
E\subset\{x_{1}\leq 0\}
is a smooth, relatively open set such that the first variation of
\mathcal{A}
vanishes at

for every smooth compactly supported vector field

that is tangential along

(1.2)

\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}\mathcal{A}(\Phi_{t}(E))=0,

where
\Phi_{t}(x)=x+tX(x)
.
Then a simple computation (see section

(1.3)

in the form of

(1.4)

\displaystyle\delta_{\beta,g}V(X)

\displaystyle:=\int\mathrm{div}_{V,g}(X)d\mu_{V_{I}}+\int_{S}\mathrm{div}_{\mathbb{R}^{n},g}(X\cos\beta)d\mathcal{H}^{n}_{g}

(1.5)

\displaystyle=-\int g(H^{tan},X)d\mu_{V_{I}}

for all

\{x_{1}=0\}

Example 1.2
.

An archetype example, which serves the model for our regularity theorem, is the half-plane capillary varifold

(Swapping the wet region)

1.1.
First variation control

In light of these issues, we first establish a uniform first variation control for capillary varifolds, which asserts that under a necessary density bound, both
V_{I}
and
V_{B}
have uniform first variation bounds. Our result holds also for general free-boundary varifolds
V
in the sense of (
1.6
) without assuming that
V\llcorner\{x_{1}=0\}=0
- particularly, when
\beta
is constant, a
(\beta,S)
-capillary varifold
V
is free-boundary. As such, we anticipate that this should be widely applicable for the study of free-boundary minimal surfaces. See Theorems
3.1
and
3.16
for more detailed conclusions.

(First variation control)
.

Let

\displaystyle\Theta_{V}(0,1)+(\cos\beta(0))_{-}\leq 1-\alpha,

(1.8)

\displaystyle\max\{||H^{tan}_{V,g}||_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})},|g-g_{eucl}|_{C^{1}(B_{1})},|D\beta|_{C^{0}(B_{1})}\}\leq\delta,

Remark 1.7

It is instructive to keep in mind that when

1.2.

we cannot rule out there being many points whose tangent cones are

, but which themselves are limits of capillary points, i.e. we cannot rule out that
\overline{\partial^{*}S}\setminus\partial^{*}S
has positive
(n-1)
-measure in
\{\beta\neq\pi/2\}
.

The following examples illustrate this kind of subtlety. Define
\mathbf{Y}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
by

(1.18)

\displaystyle\mathbf{Y}=\Big(\{x_{2}=\sqrt{3}x_{1},x_{1}>0\}\cup\{x_{2}=-\sqrt{3}x_{1},x_{1}>0\}\cup\{x_{2}=0,x_{1}<0\}\Big)\times\mathbb{R}.

As a
2
-varifold in
(\mathbb{R}^{3},g_{eucl})
,
\mathbf{Y}
is stationary. Now let
A\subset\mathbb{R}
be a fat Cantor set, i.e. a closed set of positive
\mathcal{H}^{1}
-measure which is nowhere dense and Let
h:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth function which is positive on
\mathbb{R}\setminus A
and vanishes on
A
with small derivative. Let us define the diffeomorphism of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
given by
\Psi(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=(x_{1}+h(x_{3}),x_{2},x_{3})
: then
\Psi(\mathbf{Y})
is stationary in
(\mathbb{R}^{3},\Psi_{\sharp}g_{eucl})
and the varifold
V=V_{I}+\beta S
defined as

V_{I}=\Psi(\mathbf{Y})\cap\{x_{1}<0\},\quad S=\{(x_{1},x_{2},y):x_{1}=0,|x_{2}|>h(y)\sqrt{3}\}.

is a
(\beta,S)
-capillary varifold in
(\{x_{1}<0\},\Psi_{\sharp}g_{eucl})
, where
\beta
is small perturbation of the constant
\pi/6
, depending on how small the derivative of
h
is.
Moreover
\partial^{*}S
are all regular capillary points (with tangent cones being rotations of
V^{(\beta(x))}
), while
\overline{\partial^{*}S}\setminus\partial^{*}S=\{0^{2}\}\times A=\mathrm{sing}V_{I}\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}
has positive measure, and at every point
(0,y)\in\{0^{2}\}\times A
the tangent cone to
V
is
V^{(\pi/2)}+(\sqrt{3}/2)[\mathbb{R}^{2}]
.

Although the singularities of
V
have very “non-optimal” density
=1/2+\sqrt{3}/2
, these examples are somewhat devious because
V_{I}
can be made to look arbitrarily close to a free-boundary half-plane
[P_{\pi/2}]
with multiplicity-one. We expect this pathology cannot occur when
\Theta_{V}(0,1)<1
.

On the other hand the bound
\Theta_{V}(x)<1
is optimal, in that Theorem
1.19
can fail at points where
\Theta_{V}(x)\geq 1
. For an example, take any
\beta\in(0,\pi/2)
and consider the (conical, stationary)
(\beta,\mathbb{R}^{2})
-capillary varifold in
(\mathbb{R}^{3}\cap\{x_{1}<0\},g_{eucl})
given by

V=[\{x_{2}=(\cot\beta)x_{1},x_{1}<0\}]+[\{x_{2}=-(\cot\beta)x_{1},x_{1}<0\}].

Then
\Theta_{V}(0,1)=\Theta_{V}(0)=1
and
\{x_{1}=x_{2}=0\}=\mathrm{sing}V_{I}
.

1.3.
Idea of Proof and plan of the paper

Section
2
is dedicated to introducing some notation and the definition of capillary varifolds. We also recall some basic results (basic first variation control, monotonicity, Allard’s theorems on compactness and regularity) which apply to capillary varifolds after some simple adaptation of classical results for free-boundary varifolds.

First variation control.

The crux of the paper is to establish the first variation control of Theorem
1.6
.
The key step in the proof of Theorem
1.6
is the Ahlfors-regularity of the boundary measure
\sigma_{V,g}
which is deduced from a dichotomy result (Theorem
3.11
): roughly speaking, for a capillary varifold satisfying the assumptions of Theorem
1.6
, at any point
\xi\in\{x_{1}=0\}
, either
V_{I}
vanishes in a small ball
B_{cr}(\xi)
or
\sigma_{V,g}(B_{r}(\xi))\geq cr^{n-1}
.
The proof of this dichotomy rests on a boundary rigidity result (Theorem
3.7
), which can be seen as a
“baby version”
of Allard’s theorem: if a stationary varifold in
\{x_{1}<0\}
has density
<1
, and if the excess at scale
1
with respect to the plane
\{x_{1}=0\}
is small enough, then the varifold must vanish in a smaller ball.

We prove Theorem
1.6
in Section
3
. In particular, Subsection
3.1
is dedicated to proving the boundary rigidity result and in Subsection
3.2
we prove the dichotomy result.
Subsection
3.3
is dedicated to proving an improved compactness and convergence result for capillary varifolds with small enough total mass (Lemma
3.12
), while in Subsection
3.4
we prove a more refined structure for the boundary measure of capillary varifolds (Theorem
3.16
).

Regularity results.

The proof of Theorem
1.11
is based on an
L^{\infty}
-excess decay estimate using a viscosity approach modeled on
[
22
,
7
,
9
]
.
With techniques similar to ours (although without the complications arising from the lack of first variation control), the regularity for the one-phase free boundary problem with capillarity was addressed in the recent paper
[
10
]
.

In Section
4
we take the first step towards this result by proving a boundary maximum principle (Theorem
4.1
) for capillary varifolds.
Roughly speaking, we prove that any smooth surface touching the support of a capillary varifold at a boundary point must do so without violating the expected condition that the varifolds attaches to
\{x_{1}=0\}
with angle
\beta
.
However, the discussion after Definition
1.1
shows that, in general, one should not expect that a
(\beta,S)
capillary varifold induced by a smooth surface attaches to
\{x_{1}=0\}
with angle
\beta
(since, for instance,
[\{x_{n+1}=0,x_{1}\leq 0\}]
is a
(\beta,\emptyset)
-capillary varifold for any
\beta\in(0,\pi)
).
Despite this, by exploiting Theorem
3.1
, we may prove a soft classification of capillary tangent cones (Lemma
4.2
) that allows us to exclude any pathological behavior if the varifold is close enough to the model capillary cone
V^{(\theta)}
.

The key part of the proof of Theorem
1.11
is a weak decay of oscillations (Theorem
6.1
, which we prove in Section
5
), whose proof is based on ideas developed in
[
9
]
for the regularity of the one-phase free boundary. In some ways, capillary minimal surfaces behave more like solutions to the one-phase Bernoulli problem than minimal surfaces with free-boundary (compare also with
[
3
]
).

With the above result at hand, the proof of the
\varepsilon
-regularity results stated above are extensions of already existing results (see for instance
[
22
,
7
]
), which we carry out in Sections
6
and
7
.

|P-Q|\coloneqq|\pi_{P}-\pi_{Q}|

\eta_{x,r}(y):=(y-x)/r

Define

It will be useful to define the squashed balls

the reason being is that the (Euclidean) orthogonal projection

.

Given an
n
-plane

(2.1)

\mathrm{osc}_{P}(S,B_{r}(x))=\frac{1}{2}\sup\{|\pi_{P}^{\perp}(y-z)|:y,z\in S\cap B_{r}(x)\}.

If

is any invertible linear map, and

, we have

(2.2)

|\pi_{AP}^{\perp}(Az)|=|\pi_{AP}^{\perp}(Az^{\perp})|\leq|Az^{\perp}|\leq|A||\pi_{P}^{\perp}(z)|,

(where

(2.3)

\mathrm{osc}_{AP}(AS,B_{r})\leq|A|\mathrm{osc}_{P}(S,B_{|A^{-1}|r}).

We will often endow (subset of)

. In this case we write

for the

-geodesic ball of radius

for the

-dimensional Hausdorff measure w.r.t.

,
\pi_{P,g}
for the
g

,
\nabla^{g}
for the

-Levi-Civita connection, etc. However we emphasize that

is explicitly stated

We define the

pointing in the positive

(2.4)

\nu_{g}=\frac{\pi^{\perp}_{\mathbb{R}^{n},g}e_{1}}{|\pi^{\perp}_{\mathbb{R}^{n},g}e_{1}|}.

2.1.
Slanted graphs

For geometric convenience, we will be working with (small) graphs over
P_{\theta}
, but which push in the
e_{n+1}
direction and are defined as functions in
x^{\prime}\equiv(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in{P_{\pi/2}}
. Specifically, if
u(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}):U\subset{P_{\pi/2}}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies
|u|_{C^{2}}\leq 1
, then we will be considering the surface

G_{\theta}(u):=\{(x^{\prime},-\cot\theta x_{1}+u(x^{\prime})):x^{\prime}\in U\}.

It will also be convenient to consider
G_{\theta}(u)
as sitting inside
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
endowed with a general metric
g
.

By direct computation, the mean curvature scalar of
G_{\theta}(u)
with respect to an ambient metric
g
and in the
e_{n+1}
direction, as a function of
x^{\prime}\in U
, is

H_{\theta}(u,g)=\mathcal{L}_{\theta}(u)+R_{1}(\theta,u)+R_{2}(\theta,u,g),

where
\mathcal{L}_{\theta}
is the linear operator

\mathcal{L}_{\theta}(u)=\Delta u-\cos^{2}\theta D_{1}^{2}u,

R_{1}
is a smooth function in
\theta,Du,D^{2}u
which is quadratic in
Du,D^{2}u
, and
R_{2}
is a smooth function of
\theta,g,Dg,u,Du,D^{2}u
which vanishes when
g=g_{eucl}
.
In other words:

R_{1}=R_{1,1}\star Du\star Du+R_{1,2}\star Du\star D^{2}u,\quad R_{2}=R_{2,1}\star(g-g_{eucl})+R_{2,2}\star Dg

for smooth functions
R_{1,i}(\theta,Du,D^{2}u)
,
R_{2,i}(\theta,g,Dg,u,Du,D^{2}u)
.

A further computation shows that the unit normal
\nu_{\theta}(u,g)
of
G_{\theta}(u)
, with respect to an ambient metric
g
and in the
e_{n+1}
direction, satisfies

\nu_{\theta}(u,g)\cdot e_{1}=\cos\theta+D_{1}u(\cos^{2}\theta\sin\theta-1)+\tilde{R}_{1}(u)+\tilde{R}_{2}(u,g)

where
\tilde{R}_{1}
is a smooth function of
\theta,Du
which is quadratic in
Du
; and
\tilde{R}_{2}
is a smooth function of
\theta,g,u,Du
which vanishes when
g=g_{eucl}
:

\tilde{R}_{1}=\tilde{R}_{1,1}\star Du\star Du,\quad\tilde{R}_{2}=\tilde{R}_{2,1}\star(g-g_{eucl}),

for smooth functions
\tilde{R}_{1,1}(\theta,Du)
,
\tilde{R}_{2,1}(\theta,g,u,Du)
.

2.2.
(Signed) varifolds in an ambient metric

Let us recall that if
g
is a
C^{1}
metric on
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, then an
n
-rectifiable varifold
V
in
(U,g)
is a positive Radon measure on the Grassmannian bundle
U\times\mathrm{Gr}_{n}(n+1)
of the form

(2.5)

V(\phi(x,S))=\int_{M_{V}}\phi(x,T_{x}M_{V})\theta_{V,g}(x)d\mathcal{H}^{n}_{g}(x)\quad\forall\phi\in C^{0}_{c}(U\times\mathrm{Gr}_{n}(n+1)),

where:
M_{V}
is an
n
-rectifiable set in
U
,
\theta_{V,g}
is a non-negative
\mathcal{H}^{n}_{g}\llcorner M_{V}
-measurable function, and
\mathcal{H}^{n}_{g}
is the
n
-dimensional Hausdorff measure with respect to
g
.
We say
V
is
integral
if, in addition to being rectifiable,
\theta_{V,g}\in\mathbb{N}
at
\mu_{V}
-a.e. If
g=g_{eucl}
we will often write
\theta_{V}
in place of
\theta_{V,g_{eucl}}
.

Associated to
V
is the mass measure
\mu_{V}
, which is a Radon measure on
U
defined by
\mu_{V}:=\pi_{\sharp}V
, where
\pi:U\times\mathrm{Gr}_{n}(n+1)\to U
is the projection map. Equivalently,

(2.6)

\mu_{V}(\phi)=\int\phi(x)\theta_{V,g}(x)d\mathcal{H}^{n}_{g}(x)\quad\forall\phi\in C^{0}_{c}(U).

If
M
is a
n
-dimensional Lipschitz submanifold of
U
, we write
[M]_{g}=\mathcal{H}^{n}_{g}\llcorner M\oplus\delta_{T_{x}M}
for the (multiplicity-one) varifold obtained by integrating
M
against
\mathcal{H}^{n}_{g}
, and when
g=g_{eucl}
we will for shorthand write
[M]:=[M]_{g_{eucl}}\equiv\mathcal{H}^{n}\llcorner M\oplus\delta_{T_{x}M}
.

Given a proper
C^{1}
map
f:(U,g)\to(U^{\prime},g^{\prime})
, the pushforward
f_{\sharp}V
is defined by

(f_{\sharp}V)(\phi(x,S))=\int_{M_{V}}\phi(f(x),Df|_{x}(T_{x}M_{V}))Jf|_{(x,T_{x}M_{V})}\theta_{V,g}(x)d\mathcal{H}^{n}_{g}

where
Jf|_{x,S}=\det(g^{\prime}(Df|_{x}(e_{i}),Df|_{x}(e_{j})))^{1/2}
is the Jacobian of
f
, the
\{e_{i}\}
being a
g
-orthonormal basis of
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. The area formula implies

(f_{\sharp}V)(\phi(x,S))=\int_{f(M_{V})}\phi(x,T_{x}f(M_{V}))\theta_{V,g}(f^{-1}(x))d\mathcal{H}^{n}_{g^{\prime}}(x).

And we remark that if
f
is an isometry, then

\mu_{f_{\sharp}V}(A)=\mu_{V}(f^{-1}(A))\equiv(f_{\sharp}\mu_{V})(A)\quad\forall A\subset U^{\prime}.

Given an
n
-plane
P
and a varifold
V
, we define

(2.7)

\displaystyle\mathrm{osc}_{P}(V,B_{r}(x))

\displaystyle:=\mathrm{osc}_{P}(\mathrm{spt}V,B_{r}(x)),

where
\mathrm{spt}V\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is the support of the mass measure
\mu_{V}
.

It will useful to talk about varifolds with possibly negative density on some regions. Let us say
V
is a
signed
n
-rectifiable varifold in
(U,g)
if we can write
V=V_{+}-V_{-}
for
n
-rectifiable varifolds
V_{+},V_{-}
in
(U,g)
satisfying
V_{+}\perp V_{-}
. Equivalently,
V
can be thought of as a signed Radon measure on the Grassmannian bundle of the same form as (
2.5
), but now
\theta_{V,g}\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathcal{H}^{n}_{g}\llcorner M_{V})
is an
\mathbb{R}
-valued function.

Note that if
V
is a signed varifold, then the pushforward measure
\mu_{V}=\pi_{\sharp}V
is now a
signed
mass measure, having the same form as (
2.6
). Let us write
|\mu_{V}|
for the
unsigned
or total mass measure:

|\mu_{V}|(\phi)=\int_{M_{V}}\phi|\theta_{V,g}|d\mathcal{H}^{n}_{g}.

We can define the pushforward
f_{\sharp}V:=f_{\sharp}V_{+}-f_{\sharp}V_{-}
.

We say signed Radon measures
\mu_{i}\to\mu
in an open set
U
if
\mu_{i}(\phi)\to\mu(\phi)
for all
\phi\in C^{0}_{c}(U)
, and correspondingly we say signed varifolds

if they converge as signed Radon measures. Note that it it not necessarily the case that
V_{+,i}\to V_{+}
and
V_{-,i}\to V_{i}
.

For
V,W
signed
n
-varifolds in
B_{1}
, define the varifold metric

(2.8)

\mathcal{D}(V,W)=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}}2^{-\alpha}\frac{|V(\phi_{\alpha})-W(\phi_{\alpha})|}{1+|V(\phi_{\alpha})-W(\phi_{\alpha})|}

where
\{\phi_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha}
is a dense subset of
C^{0}_{c}(B_{1})\times\mathrm{Gr}_{n}(n+1)
. Let us further arrange the
\{\phi_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha}
to satisfy the two additional properties: for every
r=1-1/k
(for
k\in\mathbb{N}
), some subset of
\{\phi_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha}
forms a dense subset of
C^{0}_{c}(B_{r})\times\mathrm{Gr}_{n}(n+1)
; and for every such
r
there is a
\phi_{\alpha}\in C^{0}_{c}(B_{\sqrt{r}})
satisfying
\phi_{\alpha}\equiv 1
on
B_{r}
and
|\phi_{\alpha}|\leq 1
.

Then
\mathcal{D}
is a metric on the space of signed
n
-varifolds in
B_{1}
which is compatible with varifold convergence in the following sense: If
V_{i},V
are signed
n
-rectifiable varifolds with negative parts uniformly bounded by
\Lambda
for some
\Lambda\geq 0
, then
\mathcal{D}(V_{i},V)\to 0
if and only if
V_{i}\to V
as varifolds in
B_{1}
.

To see this, let
V_{i},V
be as above, and suppose
\mathcal{D}(V_{i},V)\to 0
. Fix
\phi\in C^{0}_{c}(B_{1})\times\mathrm{Gr}_{n}(n+1)
,
\varepsilon>0
, and choose an
r=1-1/k
(
k\in\mathbb{N}
) so that
\mathrm{spt}\phi\subset B_{r}
. First observe that, taking
\phi_{\alpha}\in C^{1}_{c}(B_{\sqrt{r}})
which
\equiv 1
on
B_{r}
we have

|\mu_{V_{i}}|(B_{r})\leq V_{i}(\phi_{\alpha})+\Lambda\leq 4^{\alpha}\mathcal{D}(V_{i},V)+V(\phi_{\alpha})+\Lambda\leq 1+2\Lambda+|\mu_{V}|(B_{\sqrt{r}}).

Now choose a
\phi_{\alpha}\in C^{0}_{c}(B_{r})
with
|\phi_{\alpha}-\phi|_{C^{0}}\leq\varepsilon
, and then get for
i\gg 1
:

(2.9)

\displaystyle|V_{i}(\phi)-V(\phi)|

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon|\mu_{V_{i}}|(B_{r})+\varepsilon|\mu_{V}|(B_{r})+|V_{i}(\phi_{\alpha})-V(\phi_{\alpha})|

(2.10)

\displaystyle\leq(1+3\Lambda+|\mu_{V}|(B_{\sqrt{r}}))\varepsilon+4^{\alpha}\mathcal{D}(V_{i},V)

(2.11)

\displaystyle\leq(2+3\Lambda+|\mu_{V}|(B_{\sqrt{r}}))\varepsilon.

This shows
V_{i}(\phi)\to V(\phi)
. On the other hand, if we assume
V_{i}\to V
, then it’s easy to see
\mathcal{D}(V_{i},V)\to 0
also.

If
V
is a signed
n
-rectifiable varifold in
(U,g)
, and

is a compactly supported

vector field in

, then
\{id+tX:(U,g)\to(U,g)\}_{t\in(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)}
is a family of diffeomorphisms of

into itself and we may define the

-first variation of

as

(2.12)

\displaystyle\delta_{g}V(X)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}\mu_{(id+tX)_{\sharp}V}(U)=\int_{M_{V}}\dive_{V,g}(X)\theta_{V,g}(x)d\mathcal{H}^{n}_{g}(x)

where
\dive_{V,g}(X)|_{x}=\sum_{i}g(e_{i},\nabla^{g}_{e_{i}}X)
is the tangential
g
-divergence of
X
, with
e_{i}
being a
g
-orthonormal basis of

and
\nabla^{g}
being the Levi-Civita connection with respect to

.
If

we will often simply write
\delta V\equiv\delta_{g_{eucl}}V
.
V
is said to have

if

|\delta_{g}V(X)|\leq C(W)|X|_{C^{0}(W)}\quad\forall X\in C^{1}_{c}(W,\mathbb{R}^{n+1}),\forall W\subset\subset U.

We say
V
is

\delta_{g}V(X)=0
for all
X\in C^{1}_{c}(U,\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
.

In general, if
V
has locally finite

-first-variation then

will not also have locally-finite
g_{eucl}
-first-variation. However, in many variational arguments it will be useful to treat

as a perturbation of

(2.13)

\displaystyle\big|\mathrm{div}_{V,g}(X)|_{x}-\mathrm{div}_{V,g_{eucl}}(X)|_{x}\big|

\displaystyle\qquad=\bigg|\sum_{i,j}g^{ij}g(\nabla^{g}_{\tau_{i}}X,\tau_{j})-\mathrm{div}_{V,g_{eucl}}(X)\bigg|

(2.14)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq c(n,|g|_{C^{0}})(|Dg||X|+|g-g_{eucl}||D_{V}X|)

where
\{\tau_{i}\}
is any
g_{eucl}
-orthonormal basis for
T_{x}M_{V}
,
g^{ij}
is the inverse of
g(\tau_{i},\tau_{j})
, and
|D_{V}X|^{2}:=\sum_{i}|D_{\tau_{i}}X|^{2}
.

By the Riesz representation theorem, if
V
has locally-finite
g
-first variation in
U
then
||\delta_{g}V||
is a Radon measure in the sense that

\delta_{g}V(X)=\int g(\nu_{V,g},X)d||\delta_{g}V||

for some
||\delta_{g}V||
-measurable,
g
-unit vector field
\nu_{V,g}
. By the Radon-Nikodyn theorem, we can further decompose
\delta_{g}V
by finding
H_{V,g}\in L^{1}_{loc}(U,\mathbb{R}^{n+1};|\mu_{V}|)
, a Radon measure
\sigma_{V,g}\perp|\mu_{V}|
, and a
\sigma_{V,g}
-measurable
g
-unit vector field
\eta_{V,g}
so that

3.
Capillary first variation control

This proves our claim.

\displaystyle V^{\prime}=V^{(\beta^{\prime})}\quad\text{ or }\quad V^{\prime}=V^{(\pi/2)}\quad\text{ or }\quad V^{\prime}=V^{(\pi/2)}+\cos\beta^{\prime}[\mathbb{R}^{n}].

Note that the nature of

-a.e.

is a multiple and rotation of

is

-rectifiable, and hence (by (

is
(n-1)
-rectifiable.

By standard density estimates (

), for

-a.e.

or

\sigma_{V,g}
-a.e.

For

\displaystyle\lambda(\overline{B_{R}})

\displaystyle=\lim_{i\to\infty}\sigma_{V_{I,i},g_{i}}(\overline{B_{R}})

-a.e.

From our characterization of tangent cone we deduce that

4.
Boundary maximum principle

Our goal of this section is to establish the following boundary maximum principle (Theorem
4.1
). Our strategy is to first prove the maximum principle at the level of the tangent cone (Lemma
4.5
), by showing that any blow-up cannot be contained in a wedge of too small or large an angle. To ensure the tangent cone has genuine capillary boundary data (as opposed to secretly being free-boundary), we employ an argument (Lemma

) which shows that closeness to the model cone

propagates to all scales on the boundary
provided

to rotate with the scale. The argument is very soft and only uses monotonicity and a fairly basic classification of cones (Lemma

In Sections

5

6

\in(0,\pi/2)

, we recall the notations

and

at

5.
Partial harnack

In the next two sections we will make the following running assumptions on a varifold
V

Assumption 5.1
.

(\beta,S)

For

\displaystyle\leq\cos\theta-\eta\bar{c}\sin\theta

with

Theorem 5.3

(Partial Harnack)
.

Given
\tau>0
, there are
c(n,\theta_{0})
,
\varepsilon(n,\theta_{0},\tau)
,
\alpha(n,\theta_{0})
so that the following holds. If

(5.10)

\displaystyle E:=\max\{\mathrm{osc}_{P_{\beta(0)}}(V_{I},B_{1}),||H^{tan}_{V,g}||_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})},|D\beta|_{C^{0}(B_{1})},|g-g_{eucl}|_{C^{1}(B_{1})}\}\leq\varepsilon,

(5.11)

\displaystyle|\beta(0)-\theta_{0}|\leq\varepsilon,

then for all

and all
\tau\leq r\leq 1/2

(5.12)

\displaystyle\mathrm{osc}_{P_{\beta(0)}}(V_{I},B_{r}(x))\leq cr^{\alpha}E.

Proof.

We use Theorem
5.2
to get Hölder decay centered at boundary points, and Allard’s theorem to get Hölder decay at interior points up until the radius is comparable to
|x_{1}|
. We combine the two decays to get decay for all radii
\geq\tau

\tau\leq 1/100
. For ease of notation write

First let
\gamma
be the constant from Theorem

, and choose

We claim that

(5.13)

To see this, note that for such

\displaystyle\max\{r||H^{tan}_{V,g}||_{L^{\infty}(B_{r}(x))},r|D\beta|_{C^{0}(B_{r}(x))},|g-g_{eucl}|_{C^{0}(B_{r}(x))},r|Dg|_{C^{0}(B_{r}(x))}\}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\gamma\mathrm{osc}_{P_{\beta(x)}}(V_{I},B_{r}(x))/r,

On the other hand, if (

\displaystyle F(\gamma r)

This proves (
5.13
).
By iterating (
5.13
), and ensuring

is small, we deduce that with the choice

for all
x\in\mathrm{spt}V_{I}\cap\{x_{1}=0\}\cap B_{1/2}
and all
\tau\leq r\leq 1/2
, provided we ensure
\varepsilon(n,\tau)
is small enough to guarantee

Second, since

as
\varepsilon\to 0
(by Lemma
3.15
) and
\theta\to\theta_{0}
, then by Allard’s theorem and provided

is sufficiently small we can write

\mathrm{spt}V_{I}\cap C^{\theta}_{9/10}\cap\{x_{1}<-\tau\}=G_{\theta}(w),

for

a

function satisfying the following estimate: for any

, we have

(5.16)

\sup_{B^{\theta}_{|x_{1}|/4}(x^{\prime})}\left(|x_{1}|^{-1}|w|+|Dw|\right)\\
\leq c\left(\frac{\mathrm{osc}_{P_{\theta}}(V_{I},B_{|x_{1}|}(x))}{|x_{1}|}+|x_{1}|||H_{V,g}^{tan}||_{L^{\infty}(B_{|x_{1}|}(x))}+|x_{1}||Dg|_{C^{0}(B_{|x_{1}|}(x))}\right)

for some

.
Therefore (using that

) we deduce that

(5.17)

, where

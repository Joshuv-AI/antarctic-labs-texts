Radha Kessar, Gunter Malle, Jason Semeraro

Abstract
. Let ℓ \ell be a prime. If 𝐆 \mathbf{G} is a compact connected Lie group, or a connected reductive algebraic group in characteristic different from ℓ \ell , and ℓ \ell is a good prime for 𝐆 \mathbf{G} , we show that the number of weights of the ℓ \ell -fusion system of 𝐆 \mathbf{G} is equal to the number of irreducible characters of its Weyl group. The proof relies on the classification of ℓ \ell -stubborn subgroups in compact Lie groups.

1.
Introduction

In this paper we prove the following identity, first conjectured in
[
15
]
:

Theorem 1
.

Suppose that
\mathbf{G}
is a compact connected Lie group. Let
\ell
be a prime
which is good for
\mathbf{G}
,
S
a Sylow
\ell
-subgroup of
\mathbf{G}
,

\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}_{S}(\mathbf{G})
the corresponding saturated fusion system and
W
the Weyl
group of
\mathbf{G}
. Then,

\mathbf{w}(\mathcal{F})=|{{\operatorname{Irr}}}(W)|.

Here
\mathbf{w}(\mathcal{F})
denotes the number of weights of the
\ell
-fusion system
attached to
\mathbf{G}
(see Section
2
for precise definitions) and
{{\operatorname{Irr}}}(W)
denotes the
set of ordinary irreducible characters of
W
. We recall the definition of good primes later in this introduction. While
the stated identity is rather easy to see for primes not dividing
|W|
, it
seems totally mysterious for the other primes.

The origin of our interest in this theorem is Alperin’s Weight Conjecture (AWC)
in finite group representation theory which relates the number
of simple modules of a block of a finite group algebra over a field of
characteristic
\ell
to the number of simple, projective modules of smaller
“local” groups. Indeed, via some deep representation theory, it can be shown that
for the principal block of the group algebra of a
finite group of Lie type whose defining characteristic is different
from
\ell
, and under some extra conditions, AWC is equivalent to an equation
of the above type (see
[
10
, Prop. 4.1]
). In
[
10
, Thm 1]
, we proved
this equality for finite groups of Lie type (under the relevant conditions) and made the surprising discovery
that it continues to hold in a broader setting than that of finite groups, in
particular for certain exotic fusion systems on finite
\ell
-groups arising
from homotopy fixed point spaces of connected
\ell
-compact groups. We view
Theorem
1
, where the underlying
\ell
-group is an infinite
group, as additional evidence for AWC and a further indication that it is not
restricted to the world of finite groups. We also note that
Theorem
1
yields the analogous identity for
\ell
-weights
of fusion systems of connected reductive algebraic groups in characteristic
different from
\ell
:

Theorem 2
.

Let
\ell
and
p
be distinct primes. Suppose that
\mathbf{G}
is a connected
reductive algebraic group over
\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{p}
such that
\ell
is good
for
\mathbf{G}
, let
S
be a Sylow
\ell
-subgroup of
\mathbf{G}
,
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}_{S}(\mathbf{G})

the corresponding saturated fusion system and
W
the Weyl group of
\mathbf{G}
.
Then,

\mathbf{w}(\mathcal{F})=|{{\operatorname{Irr}}}(W)|.

The proof of Theorem
1
proceeds by transferring from the
discrete setting to the continuous one in which the role of centric, radical
subgroups in fusion systems is taken over by
\ell
-stubborn subgroups (in the
sense of Jackowski–McClure–Oliver
[
8
]
) of compact Lie groups. This
leads to a straightforward reduction to the case that the underlying compact
group is simple, at which stage we are able to invoke known classifications of

\ell
-stubborn subgroups
[
13
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
16
]
. The proof for
the classical groups involves counting arguments similar to those used for

[
10
, Thm 1]
which originated in the work of Alperin–Fong
[
1
]
.

Recall that a prime
\ell
is
bad
for a root system
\Phi
if
{\mathbb{Z}}\Phi/{\mathbb{Z}}\Psi

has
\ell
-torsion for some closed subsystem
\Psi\subset\Phi
(see e.g.

[
12
, Def. B.24]
). Thus, it is bad for
\Phi
if it is so for one of its
indecomposable summands. The bad primes for indecomposable root systems are:
none for type
A_{n}
,
\ell=2
for
B_{n},C_{n}
and
D_{n}
,
\ell=2,3
for

G_{2},F_{4},E_{6},E_{7}
, and
\ell=2,3,5
for
E_{8}
(see
[
12
, Tab. 14.1]
). In
particular, all primes
\ell\geq 7
are good (that is, non bad)
for all types. In Proposition
4.1
we show that the conclusion of
Theorem
1
does not hold for the compact symplectic groups
at the bad prime
\ell=2
, and in Proposition
4.5
that it does not
hold for
\mathbf{G}=F_{4}
at the bad prime
\ell=3
.
Still in both cases we obtain
\mathbf{w}(\mathcal{F})\leq|{{\operatorname{Irr}}}(W)|
, as conjectured in

[
15
]
. In Example
4.3
we observe that for finite reductive
groups in bad characteristic we may have
\mathbf{w}(\mathcal{F})>|{{\operatorname{Irr}}}(W)|
.

Recall that by results of Friedlander (see
[
4
, Thm 3.1]
and
Section
2.3
), the
\ell
-completed classifying space (and
therefore the
\ell
-fusion system) of a
finite group of Lie type in non-describing characteristic can be recovered by
taking homotopy fixed points under suitable unstable Adams operations on the

\ell
-completed classifying space of the corresponding compact Lie group. It
would be desirable to obtain a direct connection between the weight equation
demonstrated in Theorems
1
and
2
and
that shown in
[
10
, Thm 1]
, but as yet we do not see such a connection
either via unstable Adams operations or via Frobenius morphisms on algebraic
groups. In particular, we do not see how to deduce the equation in the finite
setting from the infinite one or vice-versa. Note that our results show that
the number of weights for compact groups does not depend on the isogeny type,
while for example the two isogenous groups
\operatorname{SL}_{3}(4)
and
\operatorname{PGL}_{3}(4)
have
five respectively three 3-weights. In another direction, we believe
that the purview of Theorem
1
could be expanded to also
cover fusion systems of
\ell
-compact groups as defined in

[
5
, Sec. 10]
.

The paper is organised as follows. In Section
2
, we recall the
relevant background material. Section
3
contains the proofs of
Theorems
1
and
2
and in
Section
4
, we present our calculations for bad primes.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we recall some aspects of the theory of saturated fusion
systems associated to compact Lie groups as set up by Broto, Levi and Oliver
in
[
5
]
.
For subgroups
Q,R
of a group
G
,
{{\operatorname{Hom}}}_{G}(Q,R)
consists of the group
homomorphisms from
Q
to
R
induced by conjugation by elements of
G
,

{{\operatorname{Aut}}}_{G}(Q):={{\operatorname{Hom}}}_{G}(Q,R)\cong N_{G}(Q)/C_{G}(Q)
is the group of
G
-automorphisms
of
Q
, and
{{\operatorname{Out}}}_{G}(Q)={{\operatorname{Aut}}}_{G}(Q)/{{\operatorname{Inn}}}(Q)\cong N_{G}(Q)/QC_{G}(Q)
is the
corresponding group of outer automorphisms. For
H\leq G
denote by
\mathcal{F}_{H}(G)

the category with objects the subgroups of
H
, in which the
\mathcal{F}
-morphisms
from
Q
to
R
are the elements of
{{\operatorname{Hom}}}_{G}(Q,R)
for
Q,R\leq H
, and
composition of morphisms is the usual composition of maps. Let
\ell
be a
prime number.

2.1.
Fusion systems on discrete
\ell
-toral groups.

A
discrete
\ell
-toral group
is a group
P
with normal subgroup

P^{\circ}
such that
P^{\circ}
is isomorphic to a finite product of copies of

{\mathbb{Z}}/\ell^{\infty}
and
P/P^{\circ}
is a finite
\ell
-group. In particular, any
finite
\ell
-group is a discrete
\ell
-toral group. Here
P^{\circ}
is
characterised as the subset of infinitely divisible elements of
P
as well as
the minimal subgroup of finite index of
P
. In particular,
P^{\circ}
is
characteristic in
P
. It is called the
identity component of
P
and

P
is
connected
if
P=P^{\circ}
.

Let
S
be a discrete
\ell
-toral group and let
\mathcal{F}
be a saturated fusion
system on
S
as defined in
[
5
, Sec. 2]
. Following
[
15
]
, the
the number of weights of
\mathcal{F}
is defined by

\mathbf{w}(\mathcal{F}):=\sum_{Q}z({{\operatorname{Out}}}_{\mathcal{F}}(Q)),

where
Q
runs over a set of representatives of
\mathcal{F}
-conjugacy classes of

\mathcal{F}
-centric,
\mathcal{F}
-radical subgroups of
S
,

{{\operatorname{Out}}}_{\mathcal{F}}(Q)={{\operatorname{Aut}}}_{\mathcal{F}}(Q)/{{\operatorname{Inn}}}(Q)
is the group of
\mathcal{F}
-outer automorphisms
of
Q
, and
z({{\operatorname{Out}}}_{\mathcal{F}}(Q))
is the number of ordinary irreducible characters
of
{{\operatorname{Out}}}_{\mathcal{F}}(Q)
characters of zero
\ell
-defect. Note that by

[
5
, Cor. 3.5, Defn. 2.2]
,
S
has only finitely many classes of

\mathcal{F}
-centric,
\mathcal{F}
-radical subgroups and moreover, the
\mathcal{F}
-outer
automorphism group of any
\mathcal{F}
-centric,
\mathcal{F}
-radical subgroup of
S
is
finite. Thus,
\mathbf{w}(\mathcal{F})
is well defined. Also, note that if
G
is a finite
group,
S
is a Sylow
\ell
-subgroup of
G
and
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}_{S}(G)
is the
corresponding saturated fusion system, then
\mathbf{w}(\mathcal{F})
is the number of Alperin
weights associated to the principal block of
kG
,
k
an algebraically closed
field of characteristic
\ell
(see
[
11
, Thm 8.14.4]
).

Following
[
5
, Sec. 8]
, we say a group
G
“has Sylow

\ell
-subgroups” if there is a discrete
\ell
-toral subgroup
S\leq G
which
contains all discrete
\ell
-toral subgroups of
G
up to conjugacy.
Such a subgroup, if it exists, is called a
Sylow
\ell
-subgroup of
G
.
Note that the set of Sylow
\ell
-subgroups of
G
is a single

G
-conjugacy class. An
\ell
-centric subgroup
of
G
is a discrete

\ell
-toral subgroup
P\leq G
such that
C_{G}(P)
has a Sylow

\ell
-subgroup, which is
Z(P)
(and unique). Equivalently, a discrete

\ell
-toral
P\leq G
is
\ell
-centric in
G
if
C_{G}(P)/Z(P)
contains no
elements of order
\ell
.

2.2.
Fusion systems of compact Lie groups.

Let
\mathbf{G}
be a compact Lie group. The following definitions and results are
taken from
[
5
, Sec. 9]

•

An
\ell
-toral group
is a compact Lie group whose identity
component is a torus and whose group of components is an
\ell
-group. If
P

is a discrete
\ell
-toral subgroup of
\mathbf{G}
, then the closure
\bar{P}
of

-toral group.

•

For any

-toral group

\ell
-toral subgroups

of
\mathbf{P}

\mathbf{P}^{\circ}.P=\mathbf{P}
, where

\mathbf{P}^{\circ}
is the identity component of
\mathbf{P}
and
P
contains all

\ell
-power torsion of
\mathbf{P}
. The elements of
{{\operatorname{Syl}}}_{\ell}(\mathbf{P})
form a single

\mathbf{P}
-conjugacy class.

•

We denote by
\overline{{{\operatorname{Syl}}}}_{\ell}(\mathbf{G})
the set of all
\ell
-toral
subgroups
\mathbf{S}
of
\mathbf{G}
such that the identity component
\mathbf{S}^{\circ}
is a
maximal torus of
\mathbf{G}
and
\mathbf{S}/\mathbf{S}^{\circ}
is a Sylow
\ell
-subgroup of

N_{\mathbf{G}}(\mathbf{S}^{\circ})/\mathbf{S}^{\circ}

{{\operatorname{Syl}}}_{\ell}(\mathbf{G})
is the set of all discrete

\ell
-toral subgroups

of
\mathbf{G}
such that
\bar{P}\in\overline{{{\operatorname{Syl}}}}_{\ell}(\mathbf{G})
and

. Any two elements of

\overline{{{\operatorname{Syl}}}}_{\ell}(\mathbf{G})
are
\mathbf{G}
-conjugate and each
\ell
-toral subgroup
of

is contained in an element of
\overline{{{\operatorname{Syl}}}}_{\ell}(\mathbf{G})
. Any two

{{\operatorname{Syl}}}_{\ell}(\mathbf{G})
are
\mathbf{G}
-conjugate and each discrete

\ell
-toral subgroup of
\mathbf{G}
is contained in an element of
{{\operatorname{Syl}}}_{\ell}(\mathbf{G})

(see
[

). In particular, the elements of

If

.

•

An
\ell
-stubborn subgroup

\ell
-toral subgroup

\mathbf{G}

•

An
\ell
-toral subgroup

is called
\ell
-centric

if

.

•

A discrete
\ell
-toral subgroup

.

Proposition 4.1
.

Suppose that
\mathbf{G}={{\operatorname{Sp}}}(n)

\ell=2

\mathbf{w}(\mathcal{F})<|{{\operatorname{Irr}}}(W)|
.

Proof.

For
\gamma\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\mathfrak{c}\in\mathcal{F}
, let
\mathbf{S}_{\gamma,\mathfrak{c}}
(respectively

\bar{\mathbf{S}}_{\gamma,\mathfrak{c}}

\Gamma_{2^{\gamma}}^{{\operatorname{Sp}}}\wr E_{2^{c_{1}}}\wr\cdots\wr E_{2^{c_{t}}}
(respectively

\bar{\Gamma}_{2^{\gamma}}^{{\operatorname{Sp}}}\wr E_{2^{c_{1}}}\wr\cdots\wr E_{2^{c_{t}}}

{{\operatorname{Sp}}}(2^{\gamma+|\mathfrak{c}|})
defined in
[
13
, Def. 2]
. By
[
13
, Thms 6 and 8]
,
the
\mathbf{G}
-classes of
2
-stubborn subgroups of
\mathbf{G}
are in
bijection with the set of ordered pairs of functions
(f,f^{\prime})
where

f:{\mathbb{N}}\times\mathcal{C}\to{\mathbb{N}}\quad\text{and}\quad f^{\prime}:{\mathbb{N}}\times\mathcal{C}\to{\mathbb{N}}

\sum_{(\gamma,\mathfrak{c})}2^{\gamma+|\mathfrak{c}|}f({\gamma,\mathfrak{c}})+\sum_{(\gamma^{\prime},\mathfrak{c}^{\prime})}2^{\gamma^{\prime}+|\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}|}f^{\prime}({\gamma^{\prime},\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}})=n

and
f^{\prime}((0,()))\neq 2,4

\mathbf{P}\leq\mathbf{G}
is a
2
-stubborn subgroup
corresponding to
(f,f^{\prime})

\mathbf{P}\cong\prod_{(\gamma,\mathfrak{c})}\mathbf{S}_{\gamma,\mathfrak{c}}^{f(\gamma,\mathfrak{c})}\times\prod_{(\gamma^{\prime},\mathfrak{c}^{\prime})}\bar{\mathbf{S}}_{\gamma^{\prime},\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}}^{f(\gamma^{\prime},\mathfrak{c}^{\prime})}

Now
\operatorname{GO}_{2}^{-}(2)\cong\mathfrak{S}_{3}
has a unique irreducible character of
2
-defect

0
while
\operatorname{GO}_{2\gamma+2}^{-}(2)
has no irreducible characters of
2
-defect
0

\gamma\geq 1
so the base subgroup
\operatorname{GO}_{2\gamma+2}^{-}(2)\times G_{\mathfrak{c}}
has
a unique irreducible character of
2
-defect

\gamma\geq 1

{{\operatorname{Sp}}}_{2\gamma^{\prime}}(2)\times G_{\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}}

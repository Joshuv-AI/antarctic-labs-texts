Exploring Edge Probability Graph Models Beyond Edge Independency: Concepts, Analyses, and Algorithms
Fanchen Bu, Ruochen Yang, Paul Bogdan, Kijung Shin

Abstract
Desirable random graph models (RGMs) should (i) generate realistic structures such as high clustering (i.e., high subgraph densities), (ii) generate variable (i.e., not overly similar) graphs, and (iii) remain tractable to compute and control graph statistics. A common class of RGMs (e.g., Erdős-Rényi and stochastic Kronecker) outputs edge probabilities, and we need to realize (i.e., sample from) the edge probabilities to generate graphs. Typically, each edge’s existence is assumed to be determined independently for simplicity and tractability. However, with edge independency, RGMs theoretically cannot produce high subgraph densities and high output variability simultaneously. In this work, we explore realization beyond edge independence that can produce more realistic structures while maintaining high tractability and variability. Theoretically, we propose an edge-dependent realization framework called binding that provably preserves output variability, and derive closed-form tractability results on subgraph (e.g., triangle) densities in generated graphs. Practically, we propose algorithms for graph generation with binding and parameter fitting of binding. Our empirical results demonstrate that binding exhibits high tractability and generates realistic graphs with high clustering, significantly improving upon existing RGMs assuming edge independency.

Edge probability graph models: Concepts and basic properties

Given edge probabilities, EIGMs generate graphs assuming edge independency.
In contrast, we explore a broader class of edge probability graph models (EPGMs) going beyond edge independency.

Definition 4.1

(EPGMs)
.

Given edge probabilities
p\colon\binom{V}{2}\to[0,1]
, the set
\mathcal{F}(p)
of
edge probability graph models
(EPGMs) w.r.t.
p
consists of all the RGMs satisfying the given marginal edge probabilities, i.e.,

\mathcal{F}(p)\coloneqq\{f\colon\Pr_{f}[(u,v)]=p(u,v),\forall u,v\in V\}
.

The concept of EPGMs decomposes each RGM into two factors:

(F1)
the marginal probability of each edge and

(F2)
how the edge probabilities are realized (i.e., sampled), where (F2) has been overlooked by EIGMs and this decomposition introduces a novel way of imposing edge dependency.
Below, we show some basic properties of EPGMs and discuss their meanings and implications.

Property 4.2
.

EIGMs are special cases of EPGMs w.r.t. the same edge probabilities.

Proof.

See Appendix
B
for all the formal statements and proofs not covered in the main text.
∎

Property 4.3
.

Each RGM can be represented as an EPGM (w.r.t its marginal edge probabilities).

While
4.3
is an immediate result following the definition of EPGMs, it shows the
generality
of the concept of EPGMs, yet also implies the impossibility of exploring all possible EPGMs,
which motivates us to find
good subsets
of EPGMs.
Specifically,
4.3
tells us that each RGM can be represented as an EPGM w.r.t.
some
edge probabilities.
What can we obtain for
given
edge probabilities?
For this, we obtain the upper bounds of expected subgraph densities in the graphs generated by EPGMs with given edge probabilities.

Property 4.4

(Upper bound of edge-group probabilities in EPGMs)
.

For any
p\colon\binom{V}{2}\to[0,1]
and any
P\subseteq\binom{V}{2}
,

\Pr_{f}[P\subseteq E(G)]\leq\min_{(u,v)\in P}p(u,v),\forall f\in\mathcal{F}(p)
.

Remark 4.5
.

Later, we shall show that the upper bound in
4.4
is tight, i.e., we can find EPGMs achieving the upper bound (see

B.3
).

4.4
can be applied to obtain the upper bounds of the expected number of

Corollary 4.6

For any
p

\mathbb{E}_{f}[\triangle(G)]\leq\sum_{\{u,v,w\}\in\binom{V}{3}}\min(p(u,v),p(u,w),p(v,w)),\forall f\in\mathcal{F}(p)

Property 4.7

(EPGMs have constant expected degrees and overlap)

For any

Many EIGMs (e.g., Chung-Lu and Kronecker) can generate graphs with desirable degrees, and this property ensures that EPGMs can inherit such strengths (see

As discussed in

6
Experiments

In this section, we empirically evaluate EGPMs with our binding schemes and show the superiority of realization schemes beyond edge independency.
Specifically, we show the following two points:

•

(P1)
When we use our tractability results to fit the parameters of EPGMs, we improve upon EIGMs and reproduce high triangle densities, and thus produce high clustering which is a realistic pattern; this also validates the correctness of our tractability results and algorithms.

•

(P2)
We can reproduce other realistic patterns, e.g., power-law degrees and small diameters, especially when the corresponding EIGMs are able to do so; this shows that improving EIGMs w.r.t. clustering by binding does not harm the generation quality w.r.t. other realistic patterns.

Appendix B
Proofs

In this section, we show the proofs of our theoretical results.

B.1
EPGMs

Proposition
4.2

(EIGMs are special EPGMs)
.

For any
p
, the EIGM w.r.t.
p
is an EPGM w.r.t.
p
, i.e.,
f^{\text{EI}}_{p}\in\mathcal{F}(p)
.

Proof.

By the definition of EIGMs,

\displaystyle~\Pr\nolimits_{f^{\text{EI}}_{p}}[(u,v)]

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\sum_{G\in\mathcal{G}(V)}f^{\text{EI}}_{p}(G)\mathbf{1}[(u,v)\in G]

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\sum_{(u,v)\in G\in\mathcal{G}(V)}f^{\text{EI}}_{p}(G)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\sum_{(u,v)\in G\in\mathcal{G}(V)}p(u,v)\prod_{(u,v)\neq(u^{+},v^{+})\in G}p(u^{+},v^{+})\prod_{(u^{-},v^{-})\notin G}(1-p(u^{-},v^{-}))

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle p(u,v),\forall u,v,

completing the proof.
∎

Proposition
4.3

(EPGMs are general)
.

For any
f\colon\mathcal{G}(V)\to[0,1]
, there exists
p\colon\binom{V}{2}\to[0,1]
such that
f\in\mathcal{F}(p)
.

Proof.

Let
p\colon\binom{V}{2}\to[0,1]
be that
p(u,v)=\Pr_{f}[(u,v)],\forall u,v\in V
, then by
Definition

4.1
,
f\in\mathcal{F}(p)
.
∎

Proposition
4.4

(Upper bound of edge-group probabilities)
.

For any
p\colon\binom{V}{2}\to[0,1]
and any edge group
P\subseteq\binom{V}{2}
,

\Pr_{f}[P\subseteq E(G)]\leq\min_{(u,v)\in P}p(u,v),\forall f\in\mathcal{F}(p)
.

Proof.

By definition,
\Pr_{f}[(u,v)]=p(u,v),\forall(u,v)
.
Hence,

\displaystyle\Pr\nolimits_{f}[P\subseteq E(G)]

\displaystyle=\Pr\nolimits_{f}[\bigwedge_{(u,v)\in P}(u,v)\in G]

\displaystyle\leq\min_{(u,v)\in P}\Pr\nolimits_{f}[(u,v)]

\displaystyle=\min_{(u,v)\in P}p(u,v),

where we have used the fact that
\bigwedge_{(u,v)\in P}(u,v)\in G
is a subevent of
(u,v)\in G
for any
(u,v)\in P
.
∎

Proposition
4.7

(EPGMs have constant expected degrees and overlap)
.

For any
p\colon\binom{V}{2}\to[0,1]
, the expected degree of each node and the overlap of all the EPGMs w.r.t.
p
are constant.
Specifically,

\mathbb{E}_{f}[d(v;G)]=\sum_{u\in V}p(u,v)
and

\operatorname{Ov}(f)=\frac{\sum_{u,v\in V}p^{2}(u,v)}{\sum_{u,v\in V}p(u,v)},\forall f\in\mathcal{F}(p)
.

Proof.

By linearity of expectation,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{f}[d(v;G)]=\sum_{u\in V}\Pr[u\in N(v)]=\sum_{u\in V}\Pr[(u,v)\in G]=\sum_{u\in V}p(u,v),

which does not depend on anything else but
p
.

By
Definition

3.1
,

\displaystyle~\operatorname{Ov}(f)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{E}_{G^{\prime},G^{\prime\prime}\sim f}\left|E(G^{\prime})\cap E(G^{\prime\prime})\right|}{\mathbb{E}_{f}\left|E(G)\right|}

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\frac{\sum_{u,v}\Pr[(u,v)\in G^{\prime}\land(u,v)\in G^{\prime\prime}]}{\sum_{u,v}\Pr[(u,v)\in G]}

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\frac{\sum_{u,v}\Pr[(u,v)\in G^{\prime}]\Pr[(u,v)\in G^{\prime\prime}]}{\sum_{u,v}\Pr[(u,v)\in G]}

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\frac{\sum_{u,v\in V}p^{2}(u,v)}{\sum_{u,v\in V}p(u,v)},\forall f\in\mathcal{F}(p),

where we have used linearity of expectation and the independence between
G^{\prime}
and
G^{\prime\prime}
, completing the proof.
∎

Corollary
4.6
.

For any
p\colon\binom{V}{2}\to[0,1]
,

\mathbb{E}_{f}[\triangle(G)]\leq\sum_{\{u,v,w\}\in\binom{V}{3}}\min(p(u,v),p(u,w),p(v,w)),\forall f\in\mathcal{F}(p)
, where
\triangle(G)
is the number of triangles in
G
.

Proof.

By linearity of expectation and
4.4
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{f}[\triangle(G)]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\{u,v,w\}\in\binom{V}{3}}\Pr\nolimits_{f}[\{(u,v),(u,w),(v,w)\in E(G)\}]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\{u,v,w\}\in\binom{V}{3}}\min(p(u,v),p(u,w),p(v,w)).

∎

B.2
General binding

Definition B.1

(Formal definition of binding)
.

Given edge probabilities
p\colon\binom{V}{2}\to[0,1]
and a partition
\mathcal{P}
of node pairs, binding results the RGM
f^{\text{BD}}_{p;\mathcal{P}}
defined as follows:

•

For each
P_{i}\in\mathcal{P}
, write
P_{i}=\{(u_{i1},v_{i1}),\ldots,(u_{i|P_{i}|},v_{i|P_{i}|})\}
in the descending order w.r.t. edge probabilities, i.e.,
p(u_{i1},v_{i1})\geq\cdots\geq p(u_{i|P_{i}|},v_{i|P_{i}|})
.

•

Let
P_{i;k}:=\{(u_{i1},v_{i1}),\ldots,(u_{ik},v_{ik})\}
.

•

For
G
with edges
\bigcup_{i}P_{i;k_{i}}
for some
k_{i}
’s,
f_{p;\mathcal{P}}^{\text{BD}}(G)=\prod_{i}(p(u_{ik_{i}},v_{ik_{i}})-p(u_{i,k_{i}+1},v_{i,k_{i}+1}))
, where we take
p(u_{i,|P_{i}|+1},v_{i,|P_{i}|+1})=0
.

•

For any other
G
,
f^{\text{BD}}_{p;\mathcal{P}}(G)=0
.

Proposition
5.1

(Binding produces EPGMs)
.

For any
p\colon\binom{V}{2}\to[0,1]
and any pair partition
\mathcal{P}
,
f^{\text{BD}}_{p;\mathcal{P}}\in\mathcal{F}(p)
.

Proof.

For each pair
(u,v)
, the existence of the corresponding edge is determined in the “binding” procedure on the group
P
such that
(u,v)\in P
(
Algorithms

1
and
1
), where
(u,v)
is added into
\hat{E}
and thus
E
if and only if
s\leq\hat{p}(u,v)=p(u,v)
(
Algorithm

1
), which happens with probability
p(u,v)
since
s\sim\mathcal{U}(0,1)
.
∎

Proposition
5.2

(Binding produces higher edge-group probabilities)
.

For any
p\colon\binom{V}{2}\to[0,1]
, any pair partition
\mathcal{P}
, and any
P\subseteq\binom{V}{2}
,

\Pr_{f^{\text{BD}}_{p;\mathcal{P}}}[P\subseteq E(G)]\geq\Pr_{f^{\text{EI}}_{p}}[P\subseteq E(G)]
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
be a partition of
P
such that
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\coloneqq\{P_{0}\cap P\colon P_{0}\in\mathcal{P},P_{0}\cap P\neq\emptyset\}
.
Then

\displaystyle\Pr\nolimits_{f^{\text{BD}}_{p;\mathcal{P}}}[P\subseteq E(G)]

\displaystyle=\prod_{P^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}\min_{(u,v)\in P^{\prime}}p(u,v)

\displaystyle=\prod_{(u,v)\in P\colon\exists P^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime},(u,v)=\arg\min_{(u^{\prime},v^{\prime})\in P^{\prime}}p(u,v)}p(u,v)

\displaystyle\geq\prod_{(u,v)\in P}p(u,v),

since each
p(u,v)\leq 1
.
∎

B.3
Maximal binding

In order to prove
Lemma

B.3
, let us prove the following lemma first.

Lemma B.2

(The graph distribution with maximal binding)
.

For any
p\colon\binom{V}{2}\to[0,1]
,
we sort the pairs in
\binom{V}{2}=\{(u_{1},v_{1}),(u_{2},v_{2}),\ldots,(u_{M},v_{M})\}
with
M=\binom{\left|V\right|}{2}
such that
p(u_{1},v_{1})\geq p(u_{2},v_{2})\geq\cdots\geq p(u_{M},V_{M})
, then

f^{\text{BD}}_{p;\{\binom{V}{2}\}}(G=(V,\emptyset))=1-p(u_{1},v_{1})
,

f^{\text{BD}}_{p;\{\binom{V}{2}\}}(G=(V,\binom{V}{2}))=p(u_{M},v_{M})
,

f^{\text{BD}}_{p;\{\binom{V}{2}\}}(G=(V,\{(u_{j},v_{j})\colon 1\leq j\leq i\}))=p(u_{i},v_{i})-p(u_{i+1},v_{i+1}),\forall i\in[M-1]
,
and
f^{\text{BD}}_{p;\{\binom{V}{2}\}}(G)=0
for any other
G
.

Proof.

With
\mathcal{P}=\{\binom{V}{2}\}
, all the edge existences are determined by the same random variable
s
.
Hence, if a pair
(u,v)
exists, then all the pairs
(u^{\prime},v^{\prime})
with
p(u^{\prime},v^{\prime})\geq p(u,v)
must exist.
The possible outputs are either
G=(V,\emptyset)
or
G=(V,\{(u_{j},v_{j})\colon 1\leq j\leq i\})
for some
i\in[M]
.
The case
G=(V,\emptyset)
happens when
s>\max_{u,v\in V}p(u,v)=p(u_{1},v_{1})
with probability
1-p(u_{1},v_{1})
.
The case
G=(V,\binom{V}{2})
happens when
s\leq\min_{u,v\in V}p(u,v)=p(u_{M},v_{M})
with probability
p(P_{\binom{\left|V\right|}{2}})
.
For each remaining case
G=(V,\{(u_{j},v_{j})\colon 1\leq j\leq i\}
with
i\in[M-1]
, it happens when
p(u_{i+1},v_{i+1})<s\leq p(u_{i},v_{i})
with probability
p(u_{i},v_{i})-p(u_{i+1},v_{i+1})
.
∎

Lemma B.3

(Maximal binding achieves maximum edge-group probabilities)
.

For any
p\colon\binom{V}{2}\to[0,1]
and any edge-group
P\subseteq\binom{V}{2}
,

\Pr_{f^{\text{BD}}_{p;\{\binom{V}{2}\}}}[P\subseteq E(G)]=\min_{(u,v)\in P}p(u,v),\forall f\in\mathcal{F}(p)
, where
f^{\text{BD}}_{p;\mathcal{P}}
denotes the RGM defined by
f^{\text{BD}}_{p;\mathcal{P}}(G)=\Pr[\text{\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{alg:binding_general} outputs $G$ with inputs $p$ and $\mathcal{P}$}]
.

Proof.

By
Lemma

B.2
, in a graph
G
generated by
f^{\text{BD}}_{p;\{\binom{V}{2}\}}
,
P\subseteq E(G)
if and only if
\arg\min_{(u,v)\in P}p(u,v)\in G
., which happens with probability
\min_{(u,v)\in P}p(u,v)
.
∎

B.4
Local binding

Proposition
5.4

(Local binding produces EPGMs)
.

For any
p\colon\binom{V}{2}\to[0,1]
,
g\colon V\to[0,1]
and
R\in\mathbb{N}
,
f^{\text{LB}}_{p;g,R}\in\mathcal{F}(p)
.

Proof.

For each pair
(u,v)
,

\Pr_{f^{\text{LB}}_{p;g,R}}[(u,v)]=\sum_{\mathcal{P}}\Pr_{\mathcal{P}\sim g}[\mathcal{P}]\Pr_{f^{\text{BD}}_{p;\mathcal{P}}}[(u,v)]
.
By
Proposition

5.1
,
\Pr_{f^{\text{BD}}_{p;\mathcal{P}}}[(u,v)]=p(u,v),\forall\mathcal{P}
.
Hence,
\Pr_{f^{\text{LB}}_{p;g,R}}[(u,v)]=\sum_{\mathcal{P}}\Pr_{\mathcal{P}\sim g}[\mathcal{P}]p(u,v)=p(u,v)
.
∎

Theorem
5.6

(Time complexities of graph generation with local binding)
.

Given
p\colon\binom{V}{2}\to[0,1]
,
g\colon V\to[0,1]
, and
R\in\mathbb{N}
,

f^{\text{LB}}_{p;g,R}
generates a graph in
O(R\left(\sum_{v\in V}g(v)\right)^{2}+\left|V\right|^{2})
time with high probability, with the worst case
O(R\left|V\right|^{2})
.

Proof.

We have at most
R
rounds of sampling and binding, where each round samples at most
\left|V\right|
nodes and thus at most
\binom{\left|V\right|}{2}
pairs.
More specifically, the number of nodes sampled in each round is
\sum_{v\in V}g(v)
in expectation, and thus

O(\sum_{v\in V}g(v))
with high probability (e.g., you can use a Chernoff bound).
Hence, it takes
O(R\sum_{v\in V}g(v))
time with high probability, and
at most
O(\binom{\left|V\right|}{2}R)
time for the
R
rounds.
The number of remaining pairs is at most
\binom{\left|V\right|}{2}
so dealing with them takes
O(\binom{\left|V\right|}{2})
time.
For the generation, we need to enumerate all the node groups and each pair in each group.
Since the partition is disjoint, i.e., each pair is in exactly one group, each pair is visited exactly once, which takes
O(\binom{\left|V\right|}{2})
time.
In conclusion, generating a graph takes

O(R\sum_{v\in V}g(v)+\left|V\right|^{2})

Proposition B.4

(Parallel binding produces EPGMs)
.

For any
p\colon\binom{V}{2}\to[0,1]
,
g\colon V\to[0,1]
, and
R\in\mathbb{N}
,
f^{\text{PB}}_{p;g,R}\in\mathcal{F}(p)
.

Proof.

For each pair
(u,v)
,
if
\frac{1-\left(1-p(u,v)\right)^{1/R}}{g(u)g(v)}\leq 1
, i.e.,

p(u,v)\leq 1-(1-g(u)g(v))^{R}
,
then
p_{rem}(u,v)=0
and

\displaystyle\Pr\nolimits_{f^{\text{PB}}_{p;g,R}}[(u,v)]

\displaystyle=1-\Pr[\text{$(u,v)$ not inserted in the $R$ rounds}]\Pr[\text{$(u,v)$ not inserted when dealing with $p_{rem}$}]

\displaystyle=1-(1-g(u)g(v)r(u,v))^{R}(1-p_{rem})

\displaystyle=1-(1-p(u,v))

\displaystyle=p(u,v).

Otherwise, if
p(u,v)>1-(1-g(u)g(v))^{R}
, then

r(u,v)=1
and

\displaystyle\Pr\nolimits_{f^{\text{PB}}_{p;g,R}}[(u,v)]

\displaystyle=1-\Pr[\text{$(u,v)$ not inserted in the $R$ rounds}]\Pr[\text{$(u,v)$ not inserted when dealing with $p_{rem}$}]

\displaystyle=1-(1-g(u)g(v)r(u,v))^{R}(1-p_{rem})

\displaystyle=1-(1-g(u)g(v))^{R}\frac{1-p(u,v)}{(1-g(u)g(v))^{R}}

\displaystyle=1-(1-p(u,v))

\displaystyle=p(u,v).

Theorem B.5

(Time complexities of graph generation with parallel binding)
.

Given
p\colon\binom{V}{2}\to[0,1]
,
g\colon V\to[0,1]
, and
R\in\mathbb{N}
,

f^{\text{PB}}_{p;g,R}
generates a graph in
O(R\left(\sum_{v\in V}g(v)\right)^{2}+\left|V\right|^{2})
time with high probability, with the worst case
O(R\left|V\right|^{2})
.

Proof.

We have at most
R
rounds of sampling and binding, where each round samples at most
\left|V\right|
nodes and thus at most
\binom{\left|V\right|}{2}
pairs.
More specifically, the number of nodes sampled in each round is
\sum_{v\in V}g(v)
in expectation, and thus

O(\sum_{v\in V}g(v))
with high probability (e.g., one can use a Chernoff bound).
Hence, it takes
O(R\sum_{v\in V}g(v))
time with high probability, and
at most
O(\binom{\left|V\right|}{2}R)
time for the
R
rounds.
The number of pairs with
p_{rem}>0
is at most
\binom{\left|V\right|}{2}
so dealing with them takes
O(\binom{\left|V\right|}{2})
time.
In conclusion, generating a graph takes

O(R\sum_{v\in V}g(v)+\left|V\right|^{2})
with high probability, and

O(\binom{\left|V\right|}{2}R)
time in the worst case.
∎

Proof.

n\in\mathbb{N}
is a permutation matrix if exactly one entry in each row or column of
P
is
1

\sum_{k}P_{ik}=\sum_{k}P_{kj}=1,\forall i,j\in[n]

With binary node labels, given two nodes

Theorem C.1

(Tractable number of (non-)isolated nodes with parallel binding)
.

For any
p\colon\binom{V}{2}\to[0,1]
,
g\colon V\to[0,1]
,
R\in\mathbb{N}
,
we can compute the closed-form (w.r.t.
p
,
g
, and
R
)

\mathbb{E}_{f^{\text{PB}}_{p;g,R}}[\left|\{v\in G\colon d(v;G)\geq 1\}\right|]
.

Proof.

By the linearity of expectation,

\mathbb{E}_{f^{\text{PB}}_{p;g,R}}[\left|\{v\in G\colon d(v;G)\geq 1\}\right|]=\sum_{v\in V}\Pr\nolimits_{f^{\text{PB}}_{p;g,R}}[d(v;G)\geq 1].

Hence, we only need to compute the probability of each node
v
being (non-)isolated.
A node
v
is isolated if and only if no edge incident to
v
is inserted in each round.
In each round, when
v
is sampled, i.e.,
v\in V_{s}
, the probability that no edge incident to
v
is inserted is
1-\max_{u\in V_{s}}p(u,v)
.
Let
p_{iso}(v)
denote the aforementioned probability and sort

V\setminus\{v\}=\{u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{n-1}\}
with
n=\left|V\right|
and
p(u_{1},v)\geq p(u_{2},v)\geq\cdots\geq p(u_{n-1},v)
.
We have

p_{iso}(v)=(1-\Pr[v\in V_{s}])+\Pr[v\in V_{s}](1-\mathbb{E}_{f^{\text{PB}}_{p;g,R}}[\max_{u\in V_{s}}p(u,v)])=1-g(v)\mathbb{E}_{f^{\text{PB}}_{p;g,R}}[\max_{u\in V_{s}}p(u,v)],

where

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{f^{\text{PB}}_{p;g,R}}[\max_{u\in V_{s}}p(u,v)]

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\Pr[u_{1}\in V_{s}]p(u_{1},v)+\Pr[u_{1}\notin V_{s}\land u_{2}\in V_{s}]p(u_{2},v)+\cdots+\Pr[(\bigwedge_{i=1}^{n-2}u_{i}\notin V_{s})\land u_{n-1}\in V_{s}]p(u_{n-1},v)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle g(u_{1})p(u_{1},v)+(1-g(u_{1}))g(u_{2})p(u_{2},v)+\cdots+\prod_{i=1}^{n-2}(1-g(u_{i}))g(u_{n-1})p(u_{n-1},v).

Finally, the probability that
v
is isolated after
R
rounds and dealing with
p_{rem}
is

\tilde{p}_{iso}(v)=(p_{iso}(v))^{R}(1-p_{rem}(v)),

and thus the expected number of non-isolated nodes is

\mathbb{E}_{f^{\text{PB}}_{p;g,R}}[\left|\{v\in G\colon d(v;G)\geq 1\}\right|]=\sum_{v\in V}(1-\tilde{p}_{iso}(v)).

Theorem C.2

(Time complexity of computing the expected number of (non-)isolated nodes with parallel binding)
.

Given
p\colon\binom{V}{2}\to[0,1]
,
g\colon V\to[0,1]
, and
R\in\mathbb{N}
,

Proof.

v
, we need to first sort the other nodes

v

O(\log\left|V\right|)
times.
In conclusion, for all the nodes in
V

O(\left|V\right|\log\left|V\right|)
time in total.

D.2
Overlap-related triangle-density results

As mentioned in
Section

3.1
,

Chanpuriya et al. 2024

(
Chanpuriya et al. 2024
)
have recently extended their theoretical analysis to other categories of RGMs.
In addition to EIGMs, they further considered two other categories: node independent graph models (NIGMs) and fully dependent graph models (FDGMs).
Between the two, FDGMs means any distribution of graphs, i.e., any RGM, is allowed.

They only discussed general overlap-related triangle-density upper bounds in those categories of RGMs, without detailed tractability results for practical graph generations.
Specifically, their graph generation algorithm is based on maximal clique enumeration (MCE).
However, given a graph, MCE itself can take exponential time
(
Eblen et al. 2012
)
.

Also, what we focus on in this work, i.e., the category of binding-based EPGMs, is a subset of EPGMs and are not “fully general” as FDGMs.
On the other hand, NIGMs are associated with node embeddings, where we have a node embedding space (i.e., a distribution)
\mathcal{E}
and a symmetric function
e\colon\mathcal{E}\times\mathcal{E}\to[0,1]
, and
each node
i
has a node embedding
\bm{x}_{i}
sampled from
\mathcal{E}
i.i.d., and each edge
(i,j)
exists with probability
e(\bm{x}_{i},\bm{x}_{j})
independently.
Our binding-based EPGMs do not fall in this category either.

As mentioned in

, we use independent

Specifically, independent node sampling allows us to easily compute the marginal probability of each node binding sampled in each round, which is involved in the derivation of our tractability results.
Also, as shown in our experiments, with binding schemes using independent node sampling, we still achieve significant empirical improvement over EIGMs.
In the most general case, considering the sampling probabilities of all

;
Sutter et al. 2023
)
, and exploring more flexible node sampling schemes is an interesting future direction to be explored.

Definition E.1

(Clustering coefficients)
.

Given
G=(V,E)
, the number of wedges (i.e., open triangles) is
n_{w}(G)=\sum_{v\in V}\binom{d(v)}{2}
. The
global clustering coefficient
(GCC) of
G
is defined as

\text{GCC}(G)=\frac{3\triangle(G)}{n_{w}(G)},

where
\triangle(G)
is the number of triangles in
G
and it is multiplied by
3
because each triangle corresponds to three wedges (consider three different nodes as the center of the wedge).
The
average local clustering coefficient
(ALCC) of
G
is defined as

\text{ALCC}(G)=\sum_{v\colon d(v)\geq 2}\frac{\triangle(v;G)}{\binom{d(v)}{2}},

where
\triangle(v;G)
is the number of triangles involving
v
in
G
.

The Erdős-Rényi (ER) model outputs edge probabilities with two parameters:
n_{0}
and
p_{0}
,
and the output is
p^{ER}_{n_{0},p_{0}}
with
p^{ER}_{n_{0},p_{0}}(u,v)=p_{0},\forall u,v\in\binom{V}{2}
with
V=[n_{0}]
.
Given a graph
G=(V=[n],E)
, the standard fitting of ER gives
n_{0}=n
and
p_{0}=\frac{\left|E\right|}{\binom{\left|V\right|}{2}}
.

The Chung-Lu (CL) model outputs edge probabilities with a sequence of expected degrees
D=(d_{1},d_{2},\ldots,d_{n})
, and the output is
p^{CL}_{D}
with
p^{CL}_{D}(u,v)=\min(\frac{d_{u}d_{v}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}},1),\forall u,v\in\binom{V}{2}
with
V=[n]
.
Given a graph
G=(V=[n],E)
, the standard fitting of CL gives
d_{i}=d(i;G)
for each node
i\in V
.

The stochastic block (SB) model outputs edge probabilities with
(1) a partition of nodes which can be represented by an assignment function
f_{B}\colon[n_{0}]\to[c]
with
n_{0}
nodes and
c
blocks and
(2) the edge probability between each pair of blocks (including between two identical blocks), which can be represented by
p_{B}\colon[c]\times[c]\to[0,1]
,
and the output is
p^{SB}_{f_{B},p_{B}}
with

p^{SB}_{f_{B},p_{B}}(u,v)=p_{B}(f_{B}(u),f_{B}(v)),\forall u,v\in[n_{0}]
.
In our experiments, we use the Python library Graspologic
(
Chung et al. 2019
)
which contains a fitting algorithm for SB.
Specifically, it uses
spectral embedding
(
Von Luxburg 2007
;
Sussman et al. 2012
;
Rohe et al. 2011
)

and
a Gaussian mixture model
(
Reynolds et al. 2009
)
to obtain node partitions.

The stochastic Kronecker (KR) model outputs edge probabilities with a seed matrix
\theta\in[0,1]^{2\times 2}
and a Kronecker power
k_{KR}\in\mathbb{N}
, and the output is
p^{KR}_{\theta,k_{KR}}
with
p^{KR}_{\theta,k_{KR}}(u,v)=\theta^{(k_{KR})}_{uv},\forall u,v\in\binom{V}{2}
with
V=[2^{k_{KR}}]
, where
\theta^{(k_{KR})}\in[0,1]^{2^{k_{KR}}\times 2^{k_{KR}}}
is the
k_{KR}
-th Kronecker power of
\theta
.
In our experiments, we use
kronfit

(
Leskovec et al. 2010
)
proposed by the original authors of KR.

E.6
On high-overlap EIGMs, other edge-dependent RGMs, and more

As discussed in
Section

3.1
, there exist methods that shift edge probabilities by various mechanisms, while they are still essentially EIGMs.
Hence, by
Theorem

3.3
, they inevitably trade-off between variability and the ability to generate high-clustering graphs.
Such methods include Binning Chung Lu (BCL) proposed by
Mussmann et al. 2015
that uses accept-reject and Block Two-level Erdos-Renyi (BTER) proposed by
Kolda et al. 2014
that uses a mixture of different EIGMs (specifically, Erdos-Renyi and Chung-Lu).
Also, as discussed in
Section

3.2
, there are also existing methods that use additional mechanisms to improve upon existing EIGMs.
For example,
Pfeiffer et al. 2012
proposed Transitive Chung-Lu (TCL) that uses an additional mechanism to directly insert triangles on top of the original edge-independent Chung-Lu.

In this work, we aim to improve upon EIGMs by further exploring models without assuming edge independency.
The key point is to preserve individual edge probabilities and thus have high tractability, but the existing methods usually use mixed models and thus change the underlying edge probabilities.

We empirically tested three deep graph generative models:
CELL
(
Rendsburg et al. 2020
)
,
GraphVAE
(
Simonovsky & Komodakis 2018
)
, and
GrpahRNN
(
You et al. 2018
)
.

We summarize our empirical observations as follows:

•

CELL often fails to generate high clustering, and also generates high overlap (i.e., low variability).
CELL is essentially an EIGM. See also the discussions by
Chanpuriya et al. 2021
.

•

GraphVAE learns to duplicate the training graph (i.e., 100% overlap). This is likely because GraphVAE was designed to learn from a population of graphs instead of a single graph, as discussed above.

•

GraphRNN often generates graphs with far more edges but still low clustering.
This is likely because GraphRNN was designed mainly for relatively small graphs and cannot fit well to individual large graphs.

As discussed by
Chanpuriya et al. 2021
, several deep graph generative models also output edge probabilities (e.g., CELL), and this work provides a new perspective to potentially enhance them with edge dependency.

Abstract
. We establish Zaremba problem for Laplacian and p p -Laplacian with degenerate weights when the Dirichlet condition is only imposed in a set of positive weighted capacity. We prove weighted Sobolev-Poincaré inequality with sharp scaling-invariant constants involving weighted capacity. Then we show higher integrability of the gradient of the solution (Meyers estimate) with minimal conditions on the part of the boundary where the Dirichlet condition is assumed. Our results are new both for the linear p = 2 p=2 and nonlinear case and include problems with the weight not only as a measure but also as a multiplier of the gradient of the solution.

1.
Introduction

The purpose of this article is to obtain the small higher integrability (Meyers property) for the solution of mixed boundary value problem – Zaremba problem – for the second order elliptic equation with degenerate weights. The prototype equation is the weighted Laplacian of the form

(1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}-\operatorname{div}\left(\mathbb{A}(x)\nabla u\right)&=l\quad\text{in }\Omega,\\
u&=0\quad\text{on }\mathcal{D},\\
\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}&=0\quad\text{on }\mathcal{N},\end{split}

and weighted
p
-Laplacian of the form

(2)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} -\operatorname{div}(|\mathbb{M}(x)\nabla u|^{p-2}\mathbb{M}^{2}(x)\nabla u)&=l&\qquad&\text{in }\Omega,\\
u&=0&\qquad&\text{on }\mathcal{D},\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}&=0&\qquad&\text{on }\mathcal{N}.\end{aligned}

Here,
1<p<\infty
,
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a bounded Lipschitz domain with
n\geq 2
. The set
\mathcal{D}\subset\partial\Omega
is a closed set where the Dirichlet condition is assumed and
\mathcal{N}=\partial\Omega\setminus\mathcal{D}
is the part of the boundary where the Neumann condition is. We point out that the Dirichlet and Neumann conditions can irregularly be alternating on the boundary. The data
l
in the right-hand side is a linear functional for suitable function spaces. The coefficient functions
\mathbb{A},\mathbb{M}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\text{sym}}
are symmetric and positive definite matrix-valued weights which satisfy
\mathbb{M}^{2}(x)=\mathbb{A}(x)
and

(3)

\displaystyle{\lvert\mathbb{M}(x)\rvert}\,{\lvert\mathbb{M}^{-1}(x)\rvert}\leq\Lambda

for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with some constant
\Lambda\geq 1
, where by
{\lvert\cdot\rvert}
we denote the spectral norm of a matrix. Note that the condition (
3
) is equivalent to say that
{\mathbb{M}}
has a uniformly bounded condition number
\Lambda
. The typical example of such weight is
\mathbb{M}(x)=|x|^{\pm\varepsilon}\mathrm{Id}
for small
\varepsilon>0
. Note that when
p=2
, (
2
) becomes linear problem (
1
). To our knowledge no regularity results for Zaremba problem with possible degenerate weights are known both for the linear case (
1
) and nonlinear case (
2
).

Let us start with several results concerning local and global results regarding Dirichlet problem for the linear weighted equations (
1
). Fabes, Kenig and
Serapioni
[
FKS82
]
studied the case of Dirichlet problem for linear weighted equation with degenerate coefficients
\mathbb{A}(x):\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{{\mathrm{sym}}}
such that

(4)

\displaystyle\Lambda^{-1}\mu(x){\lvert\xi\rvert}^{2}\leq{\langle{{\mathbb{A}}(x)\xi},{\xi}\rangle}\leq\Lambda\mu(x){\lvert\xi\rvert}^{2}\quad(\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n})

for some non-negative weight
\mu
. We mention that (
4
) is equivalent to say

{\mathbb{A}}(x)
has a uniformly bounded condition number
\Lambda^{2}
. The authors proved boundedness and Hölder continuity of the solution if
\mu
belongs to the Muckenhoupt class
\mathcal{A}_{2}
and
l
is sufficiently regular. In
[
CMP18
]
, the authors showed
|F|^{q}\mu\in L^{1}\Rightarrow|\nabla u|^{q}\mu\in L^{1}
for all
q\in(1,\infty)
, where the conditions
\mu\in\mathcal{A}_{2}
and a smallness assumption on a weighted
{\mathrm{BMO}}
norm of
\mathbb{A}
were imposed, but still included the case
\mu(x)=|x|^{\pm\varepsilon}\mathrm{Id}
for small
\varepsilon>0
. See also
[
Pha20
,
CMP19
]
for the related results.

In the recent paper
[
BDGP22
]
, the authors proved a slightly different type of gradient estimates like
(|F|\omega)^{q}\in L^{1}_{{\mathrm{loc}}}\Rightarrow(|\nabla u|\omega)^{q}\in L^{1}_{{\mathrm{loc}}}
for each
q\in(1,\infty)
with the assumptions (
3
) and the smallness condition

\displaystyle[\log\mathbb{A}]_{{\mathrm{BMO}}_{{\mathrm{loc}}}(\Omega)}=\sup_{B\Subset\Omega}\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{B}\lvert\log\mathbb{A}(x)-\langle{\log\mathbb{A}}\rangle_{B}|\,dx\leq\frac{c}{q}

for some
c=c(n,p,\Lambda)
, where
B
denotes the ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\langle{f}\rangle_{B}=\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}f\,dx
for an integrable function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{{\mathrm{sym}}}
. Here, since
\mathbb{A}
is positive definite almost everywhere, the logarithm
\log\mathbb{A}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{{\mathrm{sym}}}
is well-defined. This
\log
–
{\mathrm{BMO}}
condition for
\mathbb{A}
also includes the case
\mathbb{A}(x)=|x|^{\pm\varepsilon}\mathrm{Id}
for small
\varepsilon>0
. Moreover, the condition is optimal for integrability exponent
q
. In
[
BBDL23
]
, Balci, Byun, Diening and Lee show the same result up to the boundary for the Dirichlet boundary value problem. In particular, that higher integrability of the gradients can be obtained by imposing a local small oscillation condition on the weight and a local small Lipschitz condition on the boundary of the domain.

In this paper we concentrate on Zaremba problems (
1
) and (
2
). Mixed boundary value problems admit less regularity properties for the general case of Zaremba problem. Without extra assumptions on the angles between the subsets of boundary where the Dirichlet condition and Neumann condition are imposed, the only available regularity results are small higher integrability of the gradient (Meyers property), which means that
\nabla u\in L^{p+\varepsilon}(\Omega)
for small
\varepsilon>0
in the nonlinear case, which is above the natural a-priori integrability. In
[
ACM22
]
Alkhutov, Chechkin and Maz’ya obtained Meyers estimate for solutions to the linear Zaremba problem with uniformly elliptic coefficients , i.e.

\lambda_{1}{\lvert\xi\rvert}^{2}\leq{\langle{{\mathbb{A}}(x)\xi},{\xi}\rangle}\leq\Lambda_{1}{\lvert\xi\rvert}^{2}

for all
x\in\Omega
with some
0<\lambda_{1}\leq\Lambda_{1}
, and in

[
AC22
]
Alkhutov and Chechkina obtained Meyers property for unweigthed
p
-Laplace equation. See also
[
ADKC23
]
,
[
AC23
]
, and
[
AC24
]
for the other contributions for different models including
p(\cdot)
-Laplacian. Due to the assumed uniform ellipticity in previous papers, the results above exclude the possibility of degenerate weights like

{\lvert x\rvert}^{\pm\varepsilon}\mathrm{Id}
. In the present paper, we study the linear and nonlinear problems with degenerate weights that belong to the natural Muckenhoupt classes
\mathcal{A}_{p}
under the setting of the Zaremba problem, and obtain Meyers property for the solution.

The method of the proof goes back to Maz’ya
[
Maz11
]
and
[
ACM22
]
. It is based on the several general results including Sobolev-Poincaré inequality with conditions formulated in terms of capacity. In our paper, assuming (
4
), we prove such results for our weighted problem with
\mu(x)\in\mathcal{A}_{p}
, and then we show existence and uniqueness of the weak solution to our problems with the assumption (
43
), where the weighted capacity is defined in (
12
). Then assuming scaling-invariant condition for capacity such as (
52
), which in principle implies that
\mathcal{D}
satisfies
\mu
-weighted essential subset of
Q_{r}
and
\mathcal{D}
is not negligible in the sense of
\mu
-weighted capacity, the following

(5)

\displaystyle{\lvert G\rvert}^{p+\delta}\mu\in L^{1}(\Omega)\quad\implies\quad{\lvert\nabla u\rvert}^{p+\delta}\mu\in L^{1}(\Omega)

is shown for any
\delta\in[0,\delta_{0}]
with small
\delta_{0}
, when the data in the right-hand side of the equation is expressed as
-\operatorname{div}(|G|^{p-2}\mathbb{A}(x)G)
. We also prove the analogous result to (
5
) for the problem (
2
) as follows:

(6)

\displaystyle{\lvert\mathbb{M}G\rvert}^{p+\delta}\in L^{1}(\Omega)\quad\implies\quad{\lvert\mathbb{M}\nabla u\rvert}^{p+\delta}\in L^{1}(\Omega)

when
l=-\operatorname{div}(|\mathbb{M}G|^{p-2}\mathbb{M}(x)^{2}G)
for any
\delta\in[0,\delta_{0}]
with small
\delta_{0}
.

The outline of this article is as follows. In Section

•

In Section

2.
preliminaries

We use
c
as generic constants which can vary from each line of the paper, and we clarify its dependence by using parenthesis, e.g.,
c=c(n,p)
means that
c
depends on
n
and
p
. For (real-valued) functions or numbers
f
and
g
, we say
f\lesssim g
when there exists a implicit constant
c
such that
f\leq cg
holds. We denote
f\eqsim g
when both
f\lesssim g
and
g\lesssim f
holds, i.e., there exists a implicit constant
c\geq 1
such that
\frac{1}{c}g\leq f\leq cg
holds.

For
r>0
and
x_{0}=(x_{0,1},\dots,x_{0,n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we define the cube

\displaystyle Q_{r}(x_{0})=\{x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x_{i}-x_{0,i}|<r\,\,\text{for each }i\}.

Also, let us denote by
Q^{+}_{r}(x_{0}):=Q_{r}(x_{0})\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
. We occasionally omit the center of the cube if it is not important in the context. We write the integral average of a measurable function
f:Q_{r}(x_{0})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
as

\displaystyle\langle{f}\rangle_{Q_{r}(x_{0})}=\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{Q_{r}(x_{0})}f\,dx=\dfrac{1}{|Q_{r}(x_{0})|}\int_{Q_{r}(x_{0})}f\,dx.

A function
\mu\in L^{1}_{{\mathrm{loc}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is called a weight if
\mu>0
almost everywhere in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For
1<p<\infty
and an open set
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, a weight
\mu\in L^{1}_{{\mathrm{loc}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is called an
\mathcal{A}_{p}(U)
-Muckenhoupt weight, i.e.,
\mu\in\mathcal{A}_{p}(U)
, if

\displaystyle[\mu]_{\mathcal{A}_{p}(U)}:=\sup_{Q_{r}(x_{0})\subset U}\left(\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{Q_{r}(x_{0})}\mu\,dx\right)\left(\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{Q_{r}(x_{0})}\mu^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\,dx\right)^{p-1}<\infty.

We remark that the
\mathcal{A}_{p}
-Muckenhoupt weight is originally defined with the ball
B_{r}(x_{0})
instead of
Q_{r}(x_{0})
, but since
B_{r}(x_{0})\subset Q_{r}(x_{0})\subset B_{\sqrt{2}r}(x_{0})
,
|B_{r}(x_{0})|\eqsim|Q_{r}(x_{0})|\eqsim|B_{\sqrt{2}r}(x_{0})|
and
\mu>0
a.e., our definition written with
Q_{r}(x_{0})
is equivalent to the original one. With
Q_{r}\subset U
, we write

\displaystyle\mu(Q_{r})=\int_{Q_{r}}\mu(x)\,dx.

Now we list some basic properties when
\mu
is an

-Muckenhoupt weight.

•

The maximal operator
M
is bounded on
L^{p}(U;\mu dx)
(see
[
Muc72
]
), where

is the completion of functions which are infinitely differentiable in

endowed with

\displaystyle{\lVert v\rVert}_{L^{p}(U;\mu dx)}=\left(\int_{U}|v|^{p}\mu\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

•

By
[
Gra14
, Proposition 7.2.8]
, for
U\subset Q
with any cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
we have

(7)

\displaystyle\dfrac{\mu(U)}{\mu(Q)}\leq c\left(\dfrac{|U|}{|Q|}\right)^{\delta}

for some
\delta=\delta(n,p,[\mu]_{\mathcal{A}_{p}})\in(0,1)
and
c=c(n,p,[\mu]_{\mathcal{A}_{p}})\geq 1
.

•

Moreover, by
[
MP12
, Lemma 3.5]
, there holds

(8)

\displaystyle\dfrac{\mu(Q)}{\mu(U)}\leq[\mu]_{\mathcal{A}_{p}}\left(\dfrac{|Q|}{|U|}\right)^{p}

for

with
|U|>0
.

•

By
[
MP12
, Lemma 3.6]
, there exists
\gamma_{1}=\gamma_{1}(n,p,[\mu]_{\mathcal{A}_{p}})\in(1,p)
such that

(9)

\displaystyle[\mu]_{\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}}\leq c(n,p,[\mu]_{\mathcal{A}_{p}})

for any
\gamma\in[\gamma_{1},p]
.

For the further studies of Muckenhoupt weight, we refer to
[
Gra14

Now, we introduce Lipschitz domain.

Definition 1
.

Let two positive parameters
L
and
R
be given. The domain
\Omega
is called
(L,R)
–Lipschitz if for any
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
, we can find a coordinate system
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
and Lipschitz map
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that
x_{0}
is the origin in this new coordinate system, and we have

(10)

\displaystyle\Omega\cap Q_{R}(x_{0})=\{x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in Q_{R}(x_{0}):x_{n}>\psi(x^{\prime})\}

together with

(11)

\displaystyle\|\nabla\psi\|_{\infty}\leq L.

One of the good property of an Lipschitz domain is that Lipschitz domain is an extension domain for not only Sobolev spaces but also weighted Sobolev spaces (See
[
Chu92
]
). This property is used for Corollary
7
and Corollary
14
, where derive the existence of the weak solution to our problem in consideration.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded
(L,R)
–Lipschitz domain with
n\geq 2
,
\mathcal{D}\subset\partial\Omega
be a closed set and
\mu\in\mathcal{A}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
We denote by
W^{1,p}(\Omega,\mathcal{D};\mu dx)
the completion of functions which are infinitely differentiable in
\overline{\Omega}
and equal to zero in a neighborhood of
\mathcal{D}
with the norm

\displaystyle{\lVert v\rVert}_{W^{1,p}(\Omega,\mathcal{D};\mu dx)}=\left(\int_{\Omega}|v|^{p}\mu\,dx+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{p}\mu\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Note that it is possible to define
W^{1,p}(\Omega,\mathcal{D};\mu dx)
in this way since smooth functions are dense in weighted Sobolev spaces with Muckenhoupt weights.

For an open set
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
K_{0}
being closed subset of
U
,
\mu\in\mathcal{A}_{q}
for
q\geq 1
, let us define the weighted capacity which will be frequently used throughout the paper as follows:

(12)

\displaystyle\textrm{Cap}_{q,\mu}(K_{0},U):=\inf\left\{\int_{U}|\nabla\varphi|^{q}\mu\,dx:\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(U),\,\,\varphi\geq 1\,\,\text{on}\,\,K_{0}\right\}.

Let us mention that there is a fundamental relation between the capacity of a set and validity of the Sobolev-Poincaré inequality. Here we record some results which go back to classic works by Maz’ya, see
[
Maz11
, Chapters 2,13,14]
and
[

]

We now give weighted Sobolev-Poincaré inequality with the appropriate dependency to the universal constants for the later use.

Lemma 2
.

For
1<p<\infty
and
\mu(\cdot)\in\mathcal{A}_{p}
, there exist
c=c(n,p,[\mu]_{\mathcal{A}_{p}})\geq 1
and
q_{0}=q_{0}(n,p,[\mu]_{\mathcal{A}_{p}})<p
such that

(13)

\displaystyle\left(\dfrac{1}{\mu(Q_{r})}\int_{Q_{r}}\left|\dfrac{u}{r}\right|^{p}\mu\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq c\left(\dfrac{1}{\mu(Q_{r})}\int_{Q_{r}}|\nabla u|^{q}\mu\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

for any
q\in[q_{0},p]
,
Q_{r}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
u\in W^{1,q}_{0,\mu}(Q_{r})
. Moreover, we have

(14)

\displaystyle\left(\dfrac{1}{\mu(Q_{r})}\int_{Q_{r}}\left|\dfrac{u-A}{r}\right|^{p}\mu\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq c\left(\dfrac{1}{\mu(Q_{r})}\int_{Q_{r}}|\nabla u|^{q}\mu\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},

where
A=\langle{u}\rangle_{Q_{r}}
or
A=\frac{1}{\mu(Q_{r})}\int_{Q_{r}}u(x)\mu\,dx
.

Proof.

The proof is based on
[
FKS82
]
together with
[
MW74
]
. One of the differences compared to the mentioned literatures is that we use the cube
Q_{r}
instead of the ball
B_{r}
, but in principle the proof will be same. First, similar to
[
MW74
, Theorem 1]
and
[
FKS82
, Page 84]
, we see that the following is true: For
1<p<\infty
and
\mu\in\mathcal{A}_{p}
, there exists a constant
c=c(n,p,[\mu]_{\mathcal{A}_{p}})>0
such that for all measurable
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,

(15)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\dfrac{f(y)}{|x-y|^{n-1}}\,dy\right|^{p}\mu(x)\,dx\leq c\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|Tf(x)|^{p}\mu(x)\,dx

holds, where

\displaystyle Tf(x)=\sup_{0<s<\infty}\dfrac{1}{s^{n-1}}\int_{Q_{s}(x)}|f(y)|\,dy.

Indeed, using (
7
) and following the argument of
[
MW74
, Page 263–265]
, we obtain (
15
).

On the other hand, similar to
[
FKS82
, Lemma 1.1]
, the following holds true: There exists
c=c(n,p,[\mu]_{\mathcal{A}_{p}})\geq 1
and
q_{1}=q_{1}(n,p,[\mu]_{\mathcal{A}_{p}})\in(1,p)
such that

(16)

\displaystyle\left(\dfrac{1}{\mu(Q_{r})}\int_{Q_{r}}(Tf)^{p}\mu(x)\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq cr\left(\dfrac{1}{\mu(Q_{r})}\int_{Q_{r}}|f|^{q}\mu(x)\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

for
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
with
f\in L^{q}_{\mu}(Q_{r})
supported in the ball
Q_{r}
and any
q\in[q_{1},p]
. Indeed, with
\gamma_{1}
in (
9
) we set

\displaystyle q_{0}=\max\left\{\frac{n-1}{n}p,\frac{p+\gamma_{1}}{2}\right\}\in(\gamma_{1},p).

Then following the same argument of the proof of
[
FKS82
, Lemma 1.1]
, together with the fact that

(17)

\displaystyle\dfrac{\mu(Q_{r})}{\mu(E)}\leq[\mu]_{\mathcal{A}_{q}}\left(\dfrac{|Q_{r}|}{|E|}\right)^{q}\leq[\mu]_{\mathcal{A}_{\gamma_{1}}}\left(\dfrac{|Q_{r}|}{|E|}\right)^{q}\leq c(n,p,[\mu]_{\mathcal{A}_{p}})\left(\dfrac{|Q_{r}|}{|E|}\right)^{q}

for any subset
E\subset Q_{r}
, we have (
16
).

Now note that for any
\gamma\in[\gamma_{1},p]
,
\mu\in\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}
implies
\mu^{-\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}\in\mathcal{A}_{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}}
and so by
[
Gra14
, Remark 7.2.3 and Proposition 7.2.8]
, we have

\displaystyle\dfrac{(\mu^{-\frac{1}{\gamma-1}})(E)}{(\mu^{-\frac{1}{\gamma-1}})(Q_{r})}\leq c(n,p,[\mu]_{\mathcal{A}_{p}})\left(\dfrac{|E|}{|Q_{r}|}\right)^{\delta}

with
E\subset Q_{r}
. Then following the argument of the proof of
[
FKS82
, Theorem 1.2]
along with the above inequality, (
15
) and (
16
), we obtain (
13
). Now (
14
) follows from the same argument of the proof of
[
FKS82
, Theorem 1.5]
.
∎

We also give weighted Sobolev-Poincaré inequality with the weight as a multiplier for appropriate dependency.

Lemma 3

for any
q\in[q_{1},p]
,
Q_{r}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
u\in W^{1,q}_{0,\mu}(Q_{r})
. Moreover, we have

(19)

\displaystyle\left(\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{Q_{r}}\left|\dfrac{u-\langle{u}\rangle_{Q_{r}}}{r}\right|^{p}\mu^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq c\left(\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{Q_{r}}|\nabla u|^{q}\mu^{q}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

3.
Zaremba problem with degenerate weights as measures

This section is devoted to establishing the weighted equation where the weights are considered as measures. First of all, we prove optimal Sobolev-Poincaré inequality where the relevant constant is proportional to a scaling-invariant quantity involving the weighted capacity as in (
22
) and (
24
). Then we introduce precise settings of our problem, and show the existence and uniqueness result by assuming that the portion of the boundary where the Dirichlet condition is imposed has positive weighted capacity.

Moreover, assuming the additional condition using the weighted capacity, a higher integrability result is proved. Finally, we give an nontrivial example of the set which satisfies our weighted capacity conditions.

3.1.
Weighted Sobolev-Poincaré inequality with optimal constants

In this subsection, we prove weighted Sobolev-Poincaré inequality for our problem, together with finding the scaling-invariant quantities comparable to the optimal constants of the inequality. We also show that the aforementioned comparability is unavoidable in general. To do this, we first define the following notion for subsets in
\overline{Q}_{r}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

Definition 4

be a closed subset of

,

Proposition 5

Let

We first prove (a). Let us prove (a) in the case of

. Note that from

such that

Step 1.
We first show that if

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\text{Cap}_{q,\mu}(K,Q_{2})\\
&\quad\eqsim\inf\left\{\sum^{1}_{m=0}{\lVert\nabla_{m}(1-u)\rVert}^{q}_{L^{q}(Q_{1};\mu dx)}:u\in C^{\infty}(\overline{Q}_{1}),\text{dist}(\text{supp}\,u,K)>0\right\}\end{split}

To show this, we recall that by
[

\displaystyle A:C^{k-1,1}(\bar{Q}_{1})\rightarrow C^{k-1,1}(\bar{Q}_{2})\quad\text{for}\,\,k=1,2,\dots

(ii)

To show
Step 1

with

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(K,Q_{2}):=\{f\in C^{\infty}_{0}(Q_{2}):f\equiv 1\,\,\text{in a neighborhood of }K\}.

Step 2.
Now we will show (a) when

where

•

for the first inequality we have used (

•

for the second one we employed (

) holds when

Corollary 7

Let
\Omega

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega}\left|f\right|^{p}\mu\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq c\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla f|^{p}\mu\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

Since Lipschitz domain is an extension domain for given parameters

3.2.
Main settings, existence and uniqueness

We are now ready to offer the rigorous settings of the problem in consideration. For
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded

-Lipschitz domain. The coefficient function
\mathbb{A}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\text{sym}}
is positive definite matrix-valued weight with

(45)

\displaystyle{\lvert\mathbb{A}(x)\rvert}\,{\lvert\mathbb{A}^{-1}(x)\rvert}\leq\Lambda

for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with some constant
\Lambda\geq 1
, where by
{\lvert\cdot\rvert}
we denote the spectral norm of a matrix, i.e.,

\displaystyle{\lvert\mathbb{A}(x)\rvert}=\sup_{{\lvert y\rvert}\leq 1}\dfrac{{\lvert\mathbb{A}(x)y\rvert}}{{\lvert y\rvert}}.

Let us define

\displaystyle\omega(x):={\lvert\mathbb{A}(x)\rvert}.

Then one can see that (
45
) is equivalent to say that

(46)

\displaystyle\Lambda^{-1}\omega(x){\lvert\xi\rvert}^{2}\leq{\langle{{\mathbb{A}}(x)\xi},{\xi}\rangle}\leq\Lambda\omega(x){\lvert\xi\rvert}^{2}\quad(\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}).

For
1<p<\infty
, assume
\omega\in\mathcal{A}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and let
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega,\mathcal{D};\omega dx)
be a weak solution of

(47)

\displaystyle\begin{split}-\operatorname{div}\left({\lvert\nabla u\rvert}^{p-2}\mathbb{A}(x)\nabla u\right)&=l\quad\text{in }\Omega,\\
u&=0\quad\text{on }\mathcal{D},\\
\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}&=0\quad\text{on }\mathcal{N},\end{split}

where
l
is a linear functional on
W^{1,p}(\Omega,\mathcal{D};\omega dx)
, and
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=\sum^{n}_{i=1}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\nu_{i}
is the outward conormal derivative of
u
. By Corollary
7
, if we assume that

\displaystyle\textrm{Cap}_{p,\omega}(\mathcal{D},Q_{2R})>0,

then the following weighted Poincaré inequality

(48)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega}\left|u\right|^{p}\omega\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq c\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}\omega\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

holds for some
c>0
, so that the norm of
W^{1,p}(\Omega,\mathcal{D};\omega dx)
is equivalent to the norm only associated with the gradient. Then the application of Hahn-Banach theorem yields that the functional
l
is written as follows:

\displaystyle l(\varphi)=-\sum^{n}_{i=1}\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{f}\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dx

for some
\mathbf{f}=(\mathbf{f}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{f}_{n}):\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
|\mathbf{f}|
belonging to the dual space of
L^{p}_{\omega}(\Omega)
, i.e.,
|\mathbf{f}|\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega;\omega^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\,dx)
.
By letting

\displaystyle G=(G_{1},\dots,G_{n})\quad\text{with}\quad G_{i}=\left|\mathbb{A}^{-1}(x)\mathbf{f}\right|^{\frac{2-p}{p-1}}\mathbb{A}^{-1}(x)\mathbf{f}_{i},

we have
|G|\in L^{p}(\Omega;\omega\,dx)
and
\mathbf{f}(\cdot)=|G(\cdot)|^{p-2}\mathbb{A}(x)G(\cdot)
. Therefore, under the assumption (
44
), (
47
) is equivalent to

(49)

\displaystyle\begin{split}-\operatorname{div}\left({\lvert\nabla u\rvert}^{p-2}\mathbb{A}(x)\nabla u\right)&=-\operatorname{div}\left({\lvert G\rvert}^{p-2}\mathbb{A}(x)G\right)\quad\text{in }\Omega,\\
u&=0\quad\text{on }\mathcal{D},\\
\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}&=0\quad\text{on }\mathcal{N}\end{split}

for
{\lvert G\rvert}\in L^{p}(\Omega;\omega dx)
.
Note that since
\mathbb{A}(x)
is symmetric and positive definite,
\mathbb{A}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x)
is well-defined, still symmetric and positive definite. Then the above equation is the Euler-Lagrange equation of the following functional:

\displaystyle v\mapsto\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{p-2}|\mathbb{A}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x)\nabla v|^{2}\,dx-\int_{\Omega}\left<|G|^{p-2}\mathbb{A}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x)G,\mathbb{A}^{\frac{1}{2}}(x)Dv\right>\,dx

with the admissible set
W^{1,p}(\Omega,\mathcal{D};\omega dx)
.

Now we are ready to give the existence and uniqueness of the weak solution of our problem with the help of the results in the previous section.

Theorem 8
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded
(L,R_{0})
-Lipschitz domain. Assume that
\mathbb{A}(x):\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{{\mathrm{sym}}}
satisfies
{\lvert\mathbb{A}(x)\rvert}>0
a.e., and
{\lvert\mathbb{A}(x)\rvert}{\lvert\mathbb{A}^{-1}(x)\rvert}\leq\Lambda
for some
\Lambda>0
. Suppose that for
1<p<\infty
,
{\lvert\mathbb{A}(x)\rvert}\in\mathcal{A}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

(50)

\displaystyle\textrm{Cap}_{p,\omega}(\mathcal{D},Q_{2R})>0

hold for
\Omega\subset Q_{R}
with
\omega(x)={\lvert\mathbb{A}(x)\rvert}
and
{\lvert G\rvert}\in L^{p}(\Omega;\omega dx)
. Then there exists a unique weak solution
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega,\mathcal{D};\omega dx)
of (
49
). Moreover, we have the estimate

(51)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}\omega\,dx\leq c\int_{\Omega}|G|^{p}\omega\,dx

with
c=c(n,p,\Lambda)
.

Proof.

By Corollary
7
, (
50
) implies (
48
). Then we obtain the existence by the standard theory of calculus of variations. The energy estimate (
51
) follows from testing
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega,\mathcal{D};\omega dx)
to (
49
) and using (
46
) together with Young’s inequality. Then from
(
46
) we have the monotonicity

\displaystyle{\langle{{\lvert\xi\rvert}^{p-2}\mathbb{A}(x)\xi-{\lvert\eta\rvert}^{p-2}\mathbb{A}(x)\eta},{\xi-\eta}\rangle}\gtrsim\omega(x){\lvert\xi-\eta\rvert}\quad(\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n})

and so the uniqueness follows in a standard way.
∎

We point out that the Poincaré inequality (
48
) is used not only modifying the problem (
47
) to (
49
), but also used for the proof of existence of weak solution of (
49
).

Remark 9
.

The existence result could be obtained under the different assumption on the weight:
\omega,\omega^{-1}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
using the results from Zhikov
[
Zhi11
, Section 1.5]
. This situation is more difficult since smooth functions are in general not dense in
W^{1,2}(\Omega,\omega dx)
and presence of Lavrentiev phenomenon. However, these challenges do not impede the validity of the existence theorem.

3.3.
Higher integrability of the gradient of the solution

Here, we impose an additional assumption to prove higher integrability of the gradient of the solution. By
[
MP12
, Lemma 3.6]
, there exists
q_{2}=q_{2}([\omega]_{\mathcal{A}_{p}})\in(1,p)
such that
\omega\in\mathcal{A}_{q_{2}}
. Let us define

\displaystyle q:=\begin{cases}\max\left\{q_{2},\frac{p+1}{2}\right\}&\quad\text{if }p\in\left(1,\frac{n}{n-1}\right],\\
\max\left\{q_{2},\frac{np}{n+p}\right\}&\quad\text{if }p\in\left(\frac{n}{n-1},\infty\right).\end{cases}

Then we consider the following assumption.

Assumption 10
.

Let
r_{0}>0
be given. for any
x_{0}\in\mathcal{D}
and
r\in(0,r_{0})
, we have

(52)

\displaystyle\dfrac{\textrm{Cap}_{q,\omega}(\mathcal{D}\cap Q_{r}(x_{0}),\mathbb{R}^{n})}{\omega(Q_{r}(x_{0}))}\geq c_{0}r^{-q}

for some
c_{0}>0
independent of
x_{0}
and
r
.

Now we give the following theorem.

Theorem 11

(Higher integrability)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded
(L,R_{0})
-Lipschitz domain,
1<p<\infty
,
\omega\in\mathcal{A}_{p}(\Omega)
, and assume (
52
). If
G\in L^{p+{\delta_{0}}}(\Omega;\omega dx)
with
\delta_{0}>0
, then there exist
\delta_{1}=\delta_{1}(n,\delta_{0})>0
such that the following holds: the weak solution
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega,\mathcal{D};\omega dx)
of (
49
) satisfies
{\lvert\nabla u\rvert}\in L^{p+\delta}(\Omega;\omega dx)
for any
\delta\leq\delta_{1}
. Moreover, the estimate

(53)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p+\delta}\omega(x)\,dx\leq c\int_{\Omega}|G|^{p+\delta}\omega(x)\,dx

holds for some
c=c(n,p,\Lambda,[\omega]_{\mathcal{A}_{p}},q,L,R_{0},|\Omega|,c_{0},r_{0})>0
.

Proof.

We first choose
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
. Then since
\Omega
is
(L,R)
–Lipschitz, there exists a coordinate system
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
and Lipschitz map
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that
x_{0}=0
in this coordinate system, and there holds

\displaystyle\Omega\cap Q_{R}(x_{0})=\{x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in Q_{R}(x_{0}):x_{n}>\psi(x^{\prime})\}

together with
\|\nabla\psi\|_{\infty}\leq L
. Let us write
Q_{R}=Q_{R}(x_{0})
and define
\Psi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
as

\displaystyle\Psi(x^{\prime},x_{n})=(x^{\prime},x_{n}-\psi(x^{\prime}))\,\,\text{for}\,\,(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Then
\Psi
is invertible, and we observe following facts from
[
ACM22
]
:

•

\Psi(\partial\Omega\cap Q_{R})\subset T_{R}:=\{y:|y_{i}|<R\,\,\text{for}\,\,i=1,\dots,n-1,y_{n}=0\}
,

•

\widetilde{Q}_{R}:=Q_{(1+\sqrt{n-1}L)^{-1}R}\subset\Psi(Q_{R})\subset Q_{(1+\sqrt{n-1}L)R}
, and

•

\widetilde{Q}_{R}^{+}:=Q^{+}_{(1+\sqrt{n-1}L)^{-1}R}\subset\Psi(\Omega\cap Q_{R})
.

Now by transformation
y=\Psi(x)
, the problem (
49
) locally takes of the form

(54)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&-\operatorname{div}\left((\nabla\Psi(y))^{t}\widetilde{\mathbb{A}}(y){\lvert\nabla\Psi(y)\nabla\widetilde{u}(y)\rvert}^{p-2}(\nabla\Psi(y)\nabla\widetilde{u}(y))\right)\\
&\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad=-\operatorname{div}\left((\nabla\Psi(y))^{t}\widetilde{\mathbb{A}}(y){\lvert\widetilde{G}(y)\rvert}^{p-2}\widetilde{G}(y)\right)\quad\text{in }\widetilde{Q}^{+}_{R}\end{split}

with

\displaystyle\begin{split}\widetilde{u}=0\quad\text{on }\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{R}\quad\text{and}\quad\dfrac{\partial\widetilde{u}}{\partial\widetilde{\nu}}=0\quad\text{on }\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{R}.\end{split}

Here, we list definitions of each quantities and its properties below:

•

We have
\widetilde{\mathbb{A}}(y):=(\mathbb{A}\circ\Psi^{-1})(y)
and
\widetilde{\omega}(y):={\lvert\widetilde{\mathbb{A}}(y)\rvert}\in\mathcal{A}_{p}
, since
\widetilde{Q}_{R}:=Q_{(1+\sqrt{n-1}L)^{-1}R}\subset\Psi(Q_{R})\subset Q_{(1+\sqrt{n-1}L)R}
and
\psi
is a Lipschitz map and so for any
Q_{R}
,

\displaystyle\left(\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{\widetilde{Q}_{R}}\widetilde{\omega}(y)\,dy\right)\left(\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{\widetilde{Q}_{R}}\widetilde{\omega}(y)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\,dy\right)^{p-1}

\displaystyle\quad=\left(\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{\Psi(Q_{R})}\omega(x)|\nabla\Psi(x)|\,dx\right)\left(\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{\Psi(Q_{R})}\omega(x)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}|\nabla\Psi(x)|\,dx\right)^{p-1}

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim\left(\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{Q_{(1+\sqrt{n-1}L)R}}\omega(x)\,dx\right)\left(\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{Q_{(1+\sqrt{n-1}L)R}}\omega(x)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\,dx\right)^{p-1}\leq[\omega]_{\mathcal{A}_{p}}

with the implicit constant
c=c(n,L)>0
.

•

We denote
\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{R}=\Psi(\mathcal{D}\cap Q_{R})\cap\widetilde{Q}_{R}
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{R}=\Psi(\mathcal{N}\cap Q_{R})\cap\widetilde{Q}_{R}
.

•

Note that
\widetilde{u}(y):=(u\circ\Psi^{-1})(y)\in W^{1,p}(\widetilde{Q}^{+}_{R},\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{R};\widetilde{\omega}dy)
, and
\widetilde{G}(y):=(G\circ\Psi^{-1})(y)\in L^{p}(\widetilde{Q}^{+}_{R};\widetilde{\omega}dy)
by the fact that
\psi
is a Lipschitz map.

•

The outer conormal derivative of
\widetilde{u}
is denoted by
\frac{\partial\widetilde{u}}{\partial{\tilde{\nu}}}
, and since
\Psi(\partial\Omega\cap Q_{R})\subset T_{R}
, we have
\frac{\partial\widetilde{u}}{\partial{\tilde{\nu}}}=\partial_{n}\widetilde{u}
.

We use the even extension for the weight
\widetilde{\omega}(y)
and
\widetilde{u}
with respect to
\{y:y_{n}=0\}
. Let us write the extended function of
\widetilde{\omega}
as
\widehat{\omega}
, and
\widetilde{u}
as
\widehat{u}
. Then
\widehat{\omega}(y)\in\mathcal{A}_{p}
and
\widehat{u}\in W^{1,p}(\widetilde{Q}_{R},\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{R};\widehat{\omega}dy)
satisfies the following equation:

(55)

\displaystyle\begin{split}-\operatorname{div}\left({\lvert\widehat{\nabla}\widehat{u}(y)\rvert}^{p-2}\widehat{\mathbb{A}}(y)\nabla\widehat{u}(y)\right)=-\operatorname{div}\left(\widehat{G}(y)\right)\quad\text{in }\widetilde{Q}_{R}\end{split}

with

\displaystyle\begin{split}\widehat{u}=0\quad\text{on }\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{R}.\end{split}

Here, let us list definitions of each quantities and properties; all of the extensions mentioned below are with respect to
\{y:y_{n}=0\}
.

•

We define
\widehat{\nabla}\widehat{u}(y)
as an odd extension of
\nabla\Psi(y)\nabla\widetilde{u}(y)
, and note that since
\psi
is Lipschitz,
|\widehat{\nabla}\widehat{u}(y)|\eqsim|\nabla\widetilde{u}(y)|
holds.

•

The coefficient matrix
A(y):=(\nabla\Psi(y))^{t}\widetilde{\mathbb{A}}(y)(\nabla\Psi(y))
is such that
A_{in}(y)=A_{ni}(y)
for
i\neq n
are odd extensions of
[(\nabla\Psi(y))^{t}\widetilde{\mathbb{A}}(y)(\nabla\Psi(y))]_{in}
, and all other elements of
A_{ij}(y)
are even extensions of
[(\nabla\Psi(y))^{t}\widetilde{\mathbb{A}}(y)(\nabla\Psi(y))]_{ij}
. Note that
A(y)
is uniformly elliptic.

•

We define
\widehat{G}(y)=(\widehat{G}_{1}(y),\dots,\widehat{G}_{n}(y))\in L^{p}(\widetilde{Q}^{+}_{R};\widetilde{\omega}dy)
as such that
\widehat{G}_{i}(y)
are even extensions of
((\nabla\Psi(y))^{t}|\widetilde{G}(y)|^{p-2}\widetilde{\mathbb{A}}(x)\widetilde{G}(y))_{i}
for
i\neq n
, and
\widehat{G}_{n}(y)
are the odd extension of
((\nabla\Psi(y))^{t}|\widetilde{G}(y)|^{p-2}\widetilde{\mathbb{A}}(x)\widetilde{G}(y))_{n}
. Note that

\displaystyle|\widehat{G}|\eqsim\omega|\widetilde{G}|^{p-1}.

Then one can see that
\widehat{u}\in W^{1,p}(\widetilde{Q}_{R},\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{R};\widehat{\omega}dy)
from (
55
) satisfies the following

(56)

\displaystyle\int_{\widetilde{Q}_{R}}\widehat{\omega}(y)A(y){\lvert\widehat{\nabla}\widehat{u}\rvert}^{p-2}\nabla\widehat{u}\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dy=\int_{\widetilde{Q}_{R}}\widehat{\omega}(y)\widehat{G}\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dy

for any test functions
\varphi\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\widetilde{Q}_{R},\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{R};\widehat{\omega}dy)
.

From now on, we suppose that

\displaystyle y_{0}\in\widetilde{Q}_{R/2}\setminus\partial\widetilde{Q}_{R/2},\quad\text{and }r\leq\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{dist}(y_{0},\partial\widetilde{Q}_{R/2}).

First, consider the case when
Q_{3r/2}(y_{0})\subset\widetilde{Q}_{R}\setminus\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{R}
and test
\varphi=(\widehat{u}-\lambda)\eta^{p}
in (
56
), where
\lambda=\langle{\widehat{u}}\rangle_{Q_{3r/2}(y_{0})}
, and the cutoff function
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{0}(Q_{3R/2}(y_{0}))
satisfies
0\leq\eta\leq 1
,
\eta\equiv 1
in
Q_{r}(y_{0})
and
|\nabla\eta|\leq c/r
. As a result, by (
56
) and Young’s inequality together with
|\widehat{G}|\eqsim|\widetilde{G}|^{p-1}
, we arrive at the Caccioppoli inequality

(57)

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{r}(y_{0})}|\nabla\widehat{u}|^{p}\widehat{\omega}\,dy\leq c(n,p,\Lambda)\left(\int_{Q_{3r/2}(y_{0})}\left|\frac{\widehat{u}-\lambda}{r}\right|^{p}\widehat{\omega}\,dy+\int_{Q_{3r/2}(y_{0})}|\widehat{G}|^{p}\widehat{\omega}\,dy\right).

Further, from Lemma
2
, together with
\widehat{\omega}(Q_{r})\eqsim\widehat{\omega}(Q_{3r/2})
from (
7
) and (
8
), we have

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{\widehat{\omega}(Q_{r})}\int_{Q_{3r/2}(y_{0})}\left|\frac{\widehat{u}-\lambda}{r}\right|^{p}\widehat{\omega}\,dx\right)^{1/{p}}\leq c(n)\left(\frac{1}{\widehat{\omega}(Q_{r})}\int_{Q_{3r/2}(y_{0})}|\nabla\widehat{u}|^{q}\widehat{\omega}\,dx\right)^{1/{q}}.

The above two inequalities yield that

(58)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left(\frac{1}{\widehat{\omega}(Q_{r})}\int_{Q_{r}(y_{0})}|\nabla\widehat{u}|^{p}\widehat{\omega}\,dy\right)^{1/p}\\
&\quad\leq c\left(\left(\frac{1}{\widehat{\omega}(Q_{r})}\int_{Q_{2r}(y_{0})}|\nabla\widehat{u}|^{q}\widehat{\omega}\,dy\right)^{1/q}+\left(\frac{1}{\widehat{\omega}(Q_{r})}\int_{Q_{2r}(y_{0})}|\widehat{G}|^{p}\widehat{\omega}\,dy\right)^{1/p}\right).\end{split}

Next we consider the case when
Q_{3r/2}(y_{0})\cap\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{R}\neq\emptyset
. Choosing a test function
\varphi=u\eta^{p}
in (
56
), we find the similar estimate to (
57
) as follows:

(59)

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{r}(y_{0})}|\nabla\widehat{u}|^{p}\widehat{\omega}\,dy\leq c(n,p,\Lambda)\left(\int_{Q_{2r}(y_{0})}\left|\frac{\widehat{u}}{r}\right|^{p}\widehat{\omega}\,dy+\int_{Q_{2r}(y_{0})}|\widehat{G}|^{p}\widehat{\omega}\,dy\right).

Now we apply the Sobolev-Poincaré inequality (Proposition
5
) with the assumption (
52
). Since
Q_{3r/2}(y_{0})\cap\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{R}\neq\emptyset
, there exists a point
z_{0}\in Q_{3r/2}(y_{0})\cap\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{R}
such that
\overline{Q}_{r/2}(z_{0})\subset\overline{Q}_{2r}(y_{0})
. Let
z=\Psi^{-1}(z_{0})\in\mathcal{D}\cap Q_{R}
. Note that there is a cube
Q_{cr}(z)
with some
c=c(L,n)>0
such that
Q_{cr}(z)\subset\Psi^{-1}(\overline{Q}_{r/2}(z_{0}))
. By (
52
),

(60)

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{\int_{Q_{cr}(z)}\omega\,dx}\textrm{Cap}_{q,\omega}(\mathcal{D}\cap Q_{cr}(z),\mathbb{R}^{n})\geq cr^{-q}

holds, where
c=c(n,L,c_{0})
. Then considering the transformation with respect to
\Psi
, it follows that

(61)

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{\int_{Q_{2r}}\omega\,dx}\textrm{Cap}_{q,\omega}(\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})\geq cr^{-q}.

Hence we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{\widehat{\omega}(Q_{r})}\int_{Q_{2r}(y_{0})}\left|\frac{\widehat{u}}{r}\right|^{p}\widehat{\omega}\,dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq c\left(\frac{1}{\widehat{\omega}(Q_{r})}\int_{Q_{2r}(y_{0})}|\nabla\widehat{u}|^{q}\widehat{\omega}\,dy\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Combining with (
59
), we again obtain (
58
). Now we use Gehring’s lemma
[
MM00
, Theorem 1]
with the weight
\widehat{\omega}
to obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left(\frac{1}{\widehat{\omega}(Q_{r})}\int_{Q_{r}(y_{0})}|\nabla\widehat{u}|^{p+\delta}\widehat{\omega}\,dy\right)^{1/(p+\delta)}\\
&\quad\leq c\left(\left(\frac{1}{\widehat{\omega}(Q_{r})}\int_{Q_{2r}(y_{0})}|\nabla\widehat{u}|^{p}\widehat{\omega}\,dy\right)^{1/p}+\left(\frac{1}{\widehat{\omega}(Q_{r})}\int_{Q_{2r}(y_{0})}|\widehat{G}|^{p+\delta}\widehat{\omega}\,dy\right)^{1/(p+\delta)}\right).\end{split}

Multiplying
\widehat{\omega}(Q_{r})^{1/p}
to both sides of the above estimate, we find that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left(\int_{Q_{r}(y_{0})}|\nabla\widehat{u}|^{p+\delta}\widehat{\omega}\,dy\right)^{1/(p+\delta)}\\
&\quad\leq\frac{c}{\widehat{\omega}(Q_{r})^{1/(p+\delta)-1/p}}\left(\int_{Q_{2r}(y_{0})}|\nabla\widehat{u}|^{p}\widehat{\omega}\,dy\right)^{1/p}+c\left(\int_{Q_{2r}(y_{0})}|\widehat{G}|^{p+\delta}\widehat{\omega}\,dy\right)^{1/(p+\delta)}.\end{split}

Since the function
\widehat{u}
was the even extension of
\widetilde{u}
with respect to
\{y:y_{n}=0\}
, together with using the construction of
\widehat{\omega}
and
\widehat{G}
from
\widetilde{\omega}
and
\widetilde{G}
respectively, the above estimate implies

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left(\int_{Q_{r}(y_{0})}|\nabla\widetilde{u}|^{p+\delta}\widetilde{\omega}\,dy\right)^{1/(p+\delta)}\\

, and
\widehat{G}_{n}(y)
are the odd extension of
[(\nabla\Psi(y))^{t}{\lvert\widetilde{\mathbb{M}}(y)\widetilde{G}(y)\rvert}^{p-2}\widetilde{\mathbb{M}}(y)^{2}\widetilde{G}(y)]_{n}(y)
. Note that

\displaystyle|\widehat{G}|\eqsim\widehat{\omega}^{p}|\widetilde{G}|^{p-1}.

Then
\widehat{u}\in W^{1,p}(\widetilde{Q}_{R},\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{R};\widehat{\omega}^{p}dy)
from (
82
) satisfies

(83)

\displaystyle\int_{\widetilde{Q}_{R}}{\lvert\widehat{\nabla}\widehat{u}\rvert}^{p-2}(\widehat{\mathbb{M}}\nabla\widehat{u})\cdot(\widehat{\mathbb{M}}\nabla\varphi)\,dy=\int_{\widetilde{Q}_{R}}\widehat{G}\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dy

for every
\varphi\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\widetilde{Q}_{R},\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{R};\widehat{\omega}^{p}dy)
.

From now on, we assume

\displaystyle y_{0}\in\widetilde{Q}_{R/2}\setminus\partial\widetilde{Q}_{R/2},\quad\text{and }r\leq\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{dist}(y_{0},\partial\widetilde{Q}_{R/2}).

First, consider the case of
Q_{3r/2}(y_{0})\subset\widetilde{Q}_{R}\setminus\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{R}
. Test
\varphi=(\widehat{u}-\lambda)\eta^{p}
in (
83
) for
\lambda=\langle{\widehat{u}}\rangle_{Q_{3r/2}(y_{0})}
, and the cutoff function
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{0}(Q_{3R/2}(y_{0}))
satisfies

,
\eta\equiv 1
in
Q_{r}(y_{0})
and
|\nabla\eta|\leq c/r
. Then (
83
), Young’s inequality, and
|\widehat{G}|\eqsim\widehat{\omega}^{p}|\widetilde{G}|^{p-1}
imply the following Caccioppoli inequality

(84)

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{r}(y_{0})}|\nabla\widehat{u}|^{p}\widehat{\omega}^{p}\,dy\leq c(n,p,\Lambda)\left(\int_{Q_{3r/2}(y_{0})}\left|\frac{\widehat{u}-\lambda}{r}\right|^{p}\widehat{\omega}^{p}\,dy+\int_{Q_{3r/2}(y_{0})}|\widehat{G}|^{p}\widehat{\omega}^{p}\,dy\right).

Moreover, from Lemma
3
,

\displaystyle\left(\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{Q_{3r/2}(y_{0})}\left|\frac{\widehat{u}-\lambda}{r}\right|^{p}\widehat{\omega}^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/{p}}\leq c\left(\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{Q_{3r/2}(y_{0})}|\nabla\widehat{u}|^{q}\widehat{\omega}^{q}\,dx\right)^{1/{q}}

and so we have

(85)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left(\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{Q_{r}(y_{0})}|\nabla\widehat{u}|^{p}\widehat{\omega}^{p}\,dy\right)^{1/p}\\
&\quad\leq c\left(\left(\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{Q_{2r}(y_{0})}|\nabla\widehat{u}|^{q}\widehat{\omega}^{q}\,dy\right)^{1/q}+\left(\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{Q_{2r}(y_{0})}|\widehat{G}|^{p}\widehat{\omega}^{p}\,dy\right)^{1/p}\right).\end{split}

In case of
Q_{3r/2}(y_{0})\cap\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{R}\neq\emptyset
, we choose a test function
\varphi=u\eta^{p}
in (

) and so we get

(86)

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{r}(y_{0})}|\nabla\widehat{u}|^{p}\widehat{\omega}^{p}\,dy\leq c(n,p,L)\left(\int_{Q_{2r}(y_{0})}\left|\frac{\widehat{u}}{r}\right|^{p}\widehat{\omega}^{p}\,dy+\int_{Q_{2r}(y_{0})}|\widehat{G}|^{p}\widehat{\omega}^{p}\,dy\right).

Now we apply Proposition
13
with the assumption (

). With the same argument for obtaining (

), Proposition

is applicable similarly and so it follows that

\displaystyle\left(\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{Q_{2r}(y_{0})}\left|\frac{\widehat{u}}{r}\right|^{p}\widehat{\omega}^{p}\,dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq c(L,n,c_{0})\left(\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{Q_{2r}(y_{0})}|\nabla\widehat{u}|^{q}\widehat{\omega}^{q}\,dy\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Combining with (

), we again obtain (

). Now we use Gehring’s lemma
[

]
to find

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left(\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{Q_{r}(y_{0})}|\nabla\widehat{u}|^{p+\delta}\widehat{\omega}^{p+\delta}\,dy\right)^{1/(p+\delta)}\\
&\quad\leq c\left(\left(\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{Q_{2r}(y_{0})}|\nabla\widehat{u}|^{p}\widehat{\omega}^{p}\,dy\right)^{1/p}+\left(\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{Q_{2r}(y_{0})}|\widehat{G}|^{p+\delta}\widehat{\omega}^{p+\delta}\,dy\right)^{1/(p+\delta)}\right).\end{split}

with

with respect to

and

from
\widetilde{\omega}
and
\widetilde{G}
, respectively. Now by the covering argument together with the interior estimate

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left(\int_{Q_{r}}|\nabla\widetilde{u}|^{p+\delta}\widetilde{\omega}^{p+\delta}\,dy\right)^{1/(p+\delta)}\\
&\quad\leq c\left(\int_{Q_{2r}}|\nabla\widetilde{u}|^{p}\widetilde{\omega}^{p}\,dy\right)^{1/p}+c\left(\int_{Q_{2r}}|\widetilde{G}|^{p+\delta}\widetilde{\omega}^{p+\delta}\,dy\right)^{1/(p+\delta)}\end{split}

with

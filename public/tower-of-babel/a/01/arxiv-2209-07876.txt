Abstract
. We study the pullback of the stable and augmented base locus under a finite surjective morphism between normal varieties over a perfect field.

1.
introduction

The
stable
and
augmented
base locus, denoted

\mathbb{B}(\cdot)
and
\mathbb{B}_{+}(\cdot)
,
are fundamental invariants in Birational Geometry and in Complex Geometry, particularly when
positivity and asymptotic behaviour of divisors, line bundles, or vector bundles
are discussed
[
Nak00
,
BDPP13
,
ELM+06
]
.

In this article, we study the behaviour of the stable and augmented
base locus under the pullback via a finite surjective morphism.
Previous results in this direction,
[
DCL21
]
work in the setting of complex manifolds.
They, however, rely on powerful analytic methods such as currents and Lelong numbers
[
Bou04
]
.
If one is willing to impose strong positivity assumptions on the divisors, using
[
DCDC19
]
and
[
Bir17
]
,
these results can be extended to projective schemes. Our main result, Theorem
1.1
,
generalises these results to normal varieties over a perfect field.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X
and
Y
be normal projective varieties over a perfect field
k
.
Let
f:X\longrightarrow Y
be a finite surjective morphism over
k
. Then the following hold:

(1.1.1)

For every
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
D
on
Y
, we have an equality of reduced schemes

\mathbb{B}(f^{*}D)=f^{-1}\mathbb{B}(D)
.

(1.1.2)

For every
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
\Delta
on
Y
, we have an equality of reduced schemes

\mathbb{B}_{+}(f^{*}\Delta)=f^{-1}\mathbb{B}_{+}(\Delta)
.

In Section
2
, we collect the definitions of
\mathbb{Q}
-divisors,
\mathbb{R}
-divisors,
the stable and augmented base locus.
In Section
3
, we prove Theorem
1.1
in the separable case by
passing to the Galois closure. There, we use generalised Reynolds operators to produce
Galois-invariant sections to study the stable base locus.
In Section
4
, we use foliations to tackle the inseparable case.

2.1.
Stable and augmented base locus

We will follow

]

Definition 2.3

(

-divisors,
\mathbb{R}
-divisors and
\mathbb{R}

.

Let

be a
k

(2.3.1)

A
\mathbb{Q}

(2.3.2)

An

(2.3.3)

Two

\Delta_{1}-\Delta_{2}=\sum a_{i}L_{i}

Definition 2.4

(Stable base locus)
.

Let
Y
be a
k
-variety.

(2.4.1)

For a Cartier divisor
L
, the stable base locus is defined as

\mathbb{B}(L):=\Bigg(\bigcap_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\Bs(mL)\Bigg)_{\red},

where
\Bs(mL)
is the base locus of the linear system
|mL|
.

(2.4.2)

For a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
D
and a positive integer

such that

is Cartier, the stable locus is defined as

\mathbb{B}^{b}(D):=\mathbb{B}(bD).

Remark 2.5
.

In the setting of Definition
(2.4.2)
, the stable base locus for
D
does
not depend on the choice of the integer
b
. This follows from the
observation, for any Cartier divisor
L
and any positive integer
m
,
that
\Bs(L)\supseteq\Bs(mL)
. This implies that
\mathbb{B}(L)\supseteq\mathbb{B}(mL)
and
by Definition
2.4
, we find
\mathbb{B}(L)\subseteq\mathbb{B}(mL)
. Hence we will simply denote
the stable base locus as
\mathbb{B}(D):=\mathbb{B}^{b}(D)
.

We will use the definition of the augmented base locus for
\mathbb{R}
-divisors from

[
Bir17
, Definition 1.2]
.

Definition 2.6

(Augmented base locus)
.

Let
\Delta
be an
\mathbb{R}
-divisor on a projective
k
-variety
Y
and
A
an ample Cartier divisor on
Y
.
Choose a representation
\Delta\sim_{\mathbb{R}}\sum t_{i}A_{i}
where

t_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
and

are very ample Cartier divisors. Then, the

is

\mathbb{B}^{A,\sum t_{i}A_{i}}_{+}(\Delta):=\Bigg(\bigcap_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{B}\bigl(\langle m\Delta\rangle-A\bigr)\Bigg)_{\red}\penalty\ ,

where
\langle m\Delta\rangle=\sum\lfloor mt_{i}\rfloor A_{i}
.

Remark 2.7
.

The augmented base locus does not depend on the choice of an ample Cartier divisor
A
,
or representation
\Delta\sim_{\mathbb{R}}\sum t_{i}A_{i}

[

reason we drop the superfluous superscripts to simply denote the augmented base locus

\mathbb{B}_{+}(\Delta):=\mathbb{B}^{A,\sum t_{i}A_{i}}_{+}(\Delta).

By
[

this definition is consistent with
the definitions found in
[

,
ELM+06
,
ELM+09
]
.

Notation 2.8

(Pullback of the stable and augmented base locus)

In the setting of Definition

2.2.
Foliation

We recall the definition of a foliation.

(Foliation)
.

Let

. A

\scr{T}_{X}:=\bigl(\Omega^{1}_{X}\bigr)^{\vee}

(

Let

-variety

. Suppose that the characteristic

\scr{F}
is

3.
The case of separable morphisms

This section is devoted to proving the following result:

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
f:X\longrightarrow Y
be a finite, surjective and separable
k
-morphism of normal projective

k
-varieties. Then for every
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
D
on
Y
, we have
\mathbb{B}(f^{*}D)=f^{-1}\mathbb{B}(D)
.

3.1.
Symmetric Sections

To prove Theorem
3.1
we will first study the partial case when
f:X\longrightarrow Y
is a finite
Galois

morphism, that is when
f
is isomorphic to a quotient of
X
by a finite group

G
. In this case we make use of the group action
G
to produce a plethora of
sections by generalising the classical Reynolds operator.

Suppose that
L
is a Cartier divisor on
Y
and
\sigma\in H^{0}\bigl(X,\scr{O}_{X}(f^{*}L)\bigr)
, we
can apply the classical Reynolds operator on
\sigma
to obtain a section,
which we denote by,
R_{1}(\sigma)\in H^{0}\bigl(Y,\scr{O}_{Y}(L)\bigr)
. Even if
\sigma(x)\neq 0

for some
x\in X
, there is no guarantee that
R_{1}\bigl(\sigma\bigr)(f(x))\neq 0
.
Hence it is hard to control the stable base locus with the Reynolds operator. The following
Construction
3.2
remedies this issue.

Construction 3.2
.

Let
X
be a projective
k
-variety and
G<\Aut_{k}(X)
be a non trivial finite group.
Denote by
f:X\longrightarrow Y
the quotient of
X
by
G
. Take a Cartier divisor
L
on
Y
. Write
G
as

G=\{g_{1},g_{2},\ldots,g_{N}\},

such that
g_{1}
is the identity element.
For each
1\leq i\leq N
there is a linear map

\displaystyle R_{i}:H^{0}\bigl(X,\scr{O}_{X}(f^{*}L)\bigr)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow H^{0}\bigl(X,\scr{O}_{X}(i\cdot f^{*}L)\bigr)=H^{0}\bigl(X,\Sym^{i}\scr{O}_{X}(f^{*}L)\bigr)

\displaystyle\sigma

\displaystyle\longmapsto\sum_{1\leq j_{1}<j_{2}<\cdots<j_{i}\leq N}g_{j_{1}}^{*}\sigma\otimes g_{j_{2}}^{*}\sigma\otimes\cdots\otimes g_{j_{i}}^{*}\sigma.

It should be clear that
R_{i}(\sigma)
are
G
-invariant by construction. It will
also be useful to define the following linear maps for
i=1,\ldots,N-1
:

\displaystyle\tau_{i}:H^{0}\bigl(X,\scr{O}_{X}(f^{*}L)\bigr)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow H^{0}\bigl(X,\scr{O}_{X}(i\cdot f^{*}L)\bigr)

\displaystyle\sigma

\displaystyle\longmapsto\sum_{1<j_{1}<j_{2}<\cdots<j_{i}\leq N}g_{j_{1}}^{*}\sigma\otimes g_{j_{2}}^{*}\sigma\otimes\cdots\otimes g_{j_{i}}^{*}\sigma

Remark 3.3
.

In Construction
3.2
, the definitions of
R_{i}(\sigma)
and
\tau_{i}(\sigma)

do not depend on the choice of ordering of
G
.

Lemma 3.4
.

In the setting of Construction
3.2
,
given a section
\sigma\in H^{0}\bigl(X,\scr{O}_{X}(f^{*}L)\bigr)
there are relations:

R_{i}(\sigma)=\begin{cases}\sigma+\tau_{1}(\sigma)&\textmd{ for $i=1$}\\
\sigma\otimes\tau_{i-1}(\sigma)+\tau_{i}(\sigma)&\textmd{ for $1<i<N$}\\
\sigma\otimes\tau_{N-1}(\sigma)&\textmd{ for $i=N$}.\end{cases}

Proof.

The cases
i=1
and
i=N
are clear. For
1<i<N
, we can split
R_{i}(\sigma)
as follows

\displaystyle R_{i}(\sigma)

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq j_{1}<j_{2}<\cdots<j_{i}\leq N}g_{j_{1}}^{*}\sigma\otimes g_{j_{2}}^{*}\sigma\otimes\cdots\otimes g_{j_{i}}^{*}\sigma

\displaystyle=\sum_{1=j_{1}<j_{2}<\cdots<j_{i}\leq N}g_{j_{1}}^{*}\sigma\otimes g_{j_{2}}^{*}\sigma\otimes\cdots\otimes g_{j_{i}}^{*}\sigma

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ +\sum_{1<j_{1}<\cdots<j_{i}\leq N}g_{j_{1}}^{*}\sigma\otimes g_{j_{2}}^{*}\sigma\otimes\cdots\otimes g_{j_{i}}^{*}\sigma

\displaystyle=\sigma\otimes\tau_{i-1}(\sigma)+\tau_{i}(\sigma).\qed

Lemma 3.5
.

In the setting of Construction
3.2
,
let
\sigma\in H^{0}\bigl(X,\scr{O}_{X}(f^{*}L)\bigr)
and
x\in X
be a scheme theoretic point such that

\sigma(x)\neq 0
. Then, there exists an
1\leq i\leq N
such that
R_{i}(\sigma)(x)\neq 0
.

Proof.

Suppose that
R_{i}(\sigma)(x)=0
for all
i=1,\ldots,N-1
.
Then by the relations of Lemma
3.4
, one finds that

R_{N}(\sigma)(x)=(-1)^{(N-1)}\sigma^{\otimes N}(x).

This implies that
R_{N}(\sigma)(x)
is non-zero as it is assumed that
\sigma(x)\neq 0
.
Hence
R_{i}(\sigma)(x)
cannot simultaneously vanish for all
1\leq i\leq N
.
∎

3.2.
Proof of Theorem
3.1

Lemma
2.12
allows us to pass to the Galois closure and, without loss of generality,
assume that
f:X\longrightarrow Y
is the quotient of
X
by a finite group
G
.

The inclusions
\mathbb{B}(f^{*}L)\subseteq f^{-1}\mathbb{B}(L)
follows from Lemma
2.11
.

Conversely, take
x\notin\mathbb{B}(f^{*}D)
. This means that there exists an
m\in\mathbb{N}

and a section
\sigma\in H^{0}\bigl(X,\scr{O}_{X}(m\cdot f^{*}D)\bigr)
such that
\sigma(x)\neq 0
.
To show that
x\notin f^{-1}\mathbb{B}(D)
, we need to produce a section in

H^{0}\bigl(Y,\scr{O}_{Y}(n\cdot D)\bigr)
, for some

, that does not vanish at

. By
Lemma
3.5
, we find that there exists an
i
, such that

R_{i}(\sigma)\in f^{*}H^{0}\bigl(Y,\scr{O}_{Y}(mi\cdot D)\bigr)
satisfying
R_{i}(\sigma)(x)\neq 0
. ∎

4.
The Case of inseparable morphisms

The goal of this section is to remove the separability hypothesis of Theorem
3.1
,
and hence proving Theorem
1.1
.
In characteristic
0
, every finite morphism is automatically separable. In positive
characteristic, a finite field extension splits into a separable extension and
a purely inseparable extension. Hence it enough to study purely inseparable
morphisms. In this case, the idea is to use Ekedahl’s theory of foliations and
purely inseparable morphisms to factorise a purely inseparable
k
-morphism
into purely inseparable morphisms of height one
[
Eke87
]
. We then study the
pullback of the stable and augmented base locus via a purely inseparable morphism
of height one.

Setting 4.1
.

Assume that the perfect field
k
has characteristic
p>0
.

4.1.
Ekedahl’s theory of foliations and purely inseparable morphisms

The original paper
[
Eke87
]
covers the setting of smooth
k
-varieties. However,
it is crucial to work in the setting of normal
k
-varieties. The excellent
[

]

gives a nice exposition of this more general setting in
[

which we follow closely.

Definition 4.2

(Absolute Frobenius)

In Setting

topological space and the morphism between the sheaves of rings,

is given by sending

In general, the absolute Frobenius morphism is not a

is isomorphic to

-module

\displaystyle\scr{O}_{X}\times\bigl(F_{X}\bigr)_{*}\scr{O}_{X}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\bigl(F_{X}\bigr)_{*}\scr{O}_{X}

\displaystyle(r,s)\quad\quad\ \

\displaystyle\longmapsto\ \ r^{p}\cdot s.

We will label

with this

-module structure

Construction 4.7

In Setting

Lemma 4.8
.

In Construction

, the sheaf
\scr{F}_{f}
is a

Proof.

To show that
\scr{F}_{f}
is closed under the Lie bracket, fix open sets

\partial,\partial^{\prime}\in\scr{F}_{f}(U)

[\partial,\partial^{\prime}](s)=\partial\bigl(\partial^{\prime}(s)\bigr)-\partial^{\prime}\bigl(\partial(s)\bigr)=0.

A similar computation shows that
\scr{F}_{f}
is

Now we show that
\scr{F}_{f}
is a saturated in
\scr{T}_{X}
. If
\scr{F}_{f}=\scr{T}_{X}
or
\scr{F}_{f}=0
, then
there is nothing to prove, so let us assume
\scr{F}_{f}
is a proper subsheaf. Fix an open set

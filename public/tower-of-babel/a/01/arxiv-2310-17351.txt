The recursive

\,\,{\rm tr}\,C^{k-1}&{\rm tr}\,C^{k-2}&&\cdots&1\\

4
Gram determinants and Gram matrices

Definition 4.1

Gram determinants were introduced in 1879 by J.P. Gram
[
4
]
.
For vectors
x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}
in some
Hilbert space
H
the Gram
matrix
\gamma(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n})
is defined by the formula (see also
[
3
]
, Chap IX, §5)

\gamma(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n})=(x_{k},x_{m})_{k,m=1}^{n}.

The determinant of this matrix is called the Gram determinant for
the vectors
x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n}
and is denoted by

\Gamma(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n})

\Gamma(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}):={\rm det}\,\gamma(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}).

(4.1)

Some authors use the notation
G(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n})
.

Remark 4.1

A Gram determinant is equal to the square of the
n-
dimensional volume of the parallelotope constructed on
x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n}
.

Fix some notations

X=X_{mn}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}x_{11}&x_{12}&...&x_{1n}\\
x_{21}&x_{22}&...&x_{2n}\\
...&...&...&...\\
x_{m1}&x_{m2}&...&x_{mn}\end{array}\right),

(4.2)

x_{k}=(x_{1k},x_{2k},\dots,x_{mk})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m},\quad y_{r}=(x_{r1},x_{r2},\dots,x_{rn})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.

(4.3)

Then, obviously, we get

X^{*}X=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}(x_{1},x_{1})&(x_{1},x_{2})&\dots&(x_{1},x_{n})\\
(x_{2},x_{1})&(x_{2},x_{2})&\dots&(x_{2},x_{n})\\
\dots&\dots&\dots&\dots\\
(x_{n},x_{1})&(x_{n},x_{2})&\dots&(x_{n},x_{n})\end{array}\right)=\gamma(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}),

(4.4)

XX^{*}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}(y_{1},y_{1})&(y_{1},y_{2})&\dots&(y_{1},y_{m})\\
(y_{2},y_{1})&(y_{2},y_{2})&\dots&(y_{2},y_{m})\\
\dots&\dots&\dots&\dots\\
(y_{m},y_{1})&(y_{m},y_{2})&\dots&(y_{m},y_{m})\end{array}\right)=\gamma(y_{1},y_{2},...,y_{m}).

(4.5)

Therefore, we have

\Gamma(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n})={\rm det}(X^{*}X)={\rm det}(XX^{*})=\Gamma(y_{1},y_{2},...,y_{m}).

(4.6)

We start with a classical result, see, e.g.,

[
3
]
.
Consider the hyperplane

(4.7)

Proof 1

We
follow closely the book by Axiezer and Glazman
[
1
]
.
Set

and

. Since

should be orthogonal to

we conclude that

for all

(4.8)

Set
A=\gamma(f_{1},f_{2},\dots,f_{n})
and
b=(f_{k},f_{0})_{k=1}^{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. By definition we have

d^{2}=\min_{f\in V_{n}}\|f-f_{0}\|^{2}=(At,t)-2(t,b)+(f_{0},f_{0}).

(4.9)

Since
d^{2}=(h,h)=(f_{0},h)
we conclude that
d^{2}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}t_{k}(f_{0},f_{k})-(f_{0},f_{0})
or

\sum_{k=1}^{n}t_{k}(f_{0},f_{k})=(f_{0},f_{0})-d^{2}.

(4.10)

So we have the system of equations:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}t_{1}(f_{1},f_{1})+t_{2}(f_{1},f_{2})+\dots+t_{n}(f_{1},f_{n})&=&(f_{1},f_{0})\\
t_{1}(f_{2},f_{1})+t_{2}(f_{2},f_{2})+\dots+t_{n}(f_{2},f_{n})&=&(f_{2},f_{0})\\
\dots&&\\
t_{1}(f_{n},f_{1})+t_{2}(f_{n},f_{2})+\dots+t_{n}(f_{n},f_{n})&=&(f_{n},f_{0})\\
t_{1}(f_{0},f_{1})+t_{2}(f_{0},f_{2})+\dots+t_{n}(f_{0},f_{n})&=&(f_{0},f_{0})-d^{2}\end{array}\right..

(4.11)

Excluding
t_{k}
from the system we get
d^{2}=\frac{\Gamma(f_{0},f_{1},f_{2},\dots,f_{n},)}{\Gamma(f_{1},f_{2},\dots,f_{n})}
.
Formula (
4.7
) also follows from Remark
4.1
.
∎

Remark 4.2

From the system (
4.11
) we conclude that

, where

, hence

.
By (

d^{2}=(f_{0},f_{0})-(A^{-1}b,b)=\frac{\Gamma(f_{0},f_{1},f_{2},\dots,f_{n})}{\Gamma(f_{1},f_{2},\dots,f_{n})}.

(4.12)

See also
[

7.2
The Ismagilov conjecture

To construct the regular representation for an infinite-dimensional
group
G
, first we should find some larger topological group
\widetilde{G}

and a measure
\mu
on
\widetilde{G}
such that
G
is a dense
subgroup in
\widetilde{G},

and the measure is right or left
G
-quasi-invariant, i.e.,

\mu^{R_{t}}\sim\mu
for all
t\in G,
(or
\mu^{L_{s}}\sim\mu
for all
s\in G
), here
\sim
means
equivalence
, for details see
[
9
]
.
We use notation
\mu^{f}(\Delta)=\mu\big(f^{-1}(\Delta)\big)
for
f:X\to X
, where
\Delta
is some measurable set in
X
.
Consider the right and the left actions
R_{t},L_{s}
of the group

G
on
\widetilde{G}
defined below:

R_{t}x=xt^{-1},\quad L_{s}x=sx,\quad t,s\in G,\,\,x\in\widetilde{G}.

Denote by
\mu^{R_{t}},\,\,\mu^{L_{s}}
the images of the measure

\mu
under the map
R_{t},L_{s}:\widetilde{G}\to\widetilde{G}
.
The right and left
representations
T^{R,\mu},T^{L,\mu}:G\rightarrow U(L^{2}(\widetilde{G},\mu))
are naturally defined in the Hilbert space
L^{2}(\widetilde{G},\mu)
by the following formulas:

(T^{R,\mu}_{t}f)(x)=(d\mu(xt)/d\mu(x))^{1/2}f(xt),

(7.6)

(T^{L,\mu}_{s}f)(x)=(d\mu(s^{-1}x)/d\mu(x))^{1/2}f(s^{-1}x).

(7.7)

The right regular representation of infinite-dimensional
groups can be irreducible if no left actions are
admissible
for
the measure
\mu
, i.e., when
\mu^{L_{t}}\perp\mu
for all
t\in G\backslash{\{e\}}
. In this case a von Neumann algebra

{\mathfrak{A}}^{T^{L,\mu}}
generated by the left regular
representation
T^{L,\mu}
is trivial. More precisely:

Conjecture 7.11
(Ismagilov, 1985)

The right regular
representation

T^{R,\mu}:G\rightarrow U(L^{2}(\widetilde{G},\mu))

is irreducible if and only if

1)
\mu^{L_{t}}\perp\mu\quad\text{for all}\quad t\in G\backslash{\{e\}},\,\,
(where
\perp
stands
for singular),

2) the measure
\mu
is
G
-ergodic.

Conditions 1) and 2) are the necessary conditions of the irreducibility. The problem is to prove that they are sufficient ones too.

Remark 7.2

This conjecture was expressed by Rais Salmanovich Ismagilov in his referee report of the author’s PhD Thesis, 1985. It was verified for a lot of particular cases. In the general case, it is an open problem.
In the case of a finite field

we need some additional conditions for the irreducibility

[
8
]
.

if and only if

S_{kn}^{L}(\mu_{b})=\sum_{m=k+1}^{\infty}\frac{b_{km}}{b_{nm}}=\infty\quad\text{for all}\quad k\!<\!n.

(7.13)

Idea of the proof of irreducibility, for details see

6

.
The conditions 1) and 2 are necessary conditions of the irreducibility of the representation
T^{R,\mu_{b}}
.
We show that they are also a sufficient ones. Let
\mathfrak{A}(B_{0}^{\mathbb{N}})
be a von Neumann algebra generated by the representation
T^{R,\mu_{b}}
:

\mathfrak{A}(B_{0}^{\mathbb{N}})=\Big(T^{R,\mu_{b}}_{t}\mid t\in B_{0}^{\mathbb{N}}\Big)^{\prime\prime}.

(7.14)

To prove the irreducibility, it is sufficient to show that

U_{kn}(t)\in\mathfrak{A}(B_{0}^{\mathbb{N}})

for all
k,n\in\mathbb{N},\,k<n
, where
U_{kn}(t)=e^{itx_{kn}}
. In this case we have

L^{\infty}(B^{\mathbb{N}},\mu_{b})\!\subset\!\mathfrak{A}(B_{0}^{\mathbb{N}})\quad\text{hence,}\quad\big(\mathfrak{A}(B_{0}^{\mathbb{N}})\big)^{\prime}\!\subset\!\Big(L^{\infty}(B^{\mathbb{N}},\mu_{b})\Big)^{\prime}=L^{\infty}(B^{\mathbb{N}},\mu_{b}),

(7.15)

since the algebra
L^{\infty}(B^{\mathbb{N}},\mu_{b})
is
maximal abelian
.
Let now some bounded operator
A
commute with the representation
[T^{R,\mu_{b}}_{t},A]=0
for all
t\in B_{0}^{\mathbb{N}}
. Then by (
7.15
),

A\in L^{\infty}(B^{\mathbb{N}},\mu_{b})
, i.e,
A
is a multiplication operator on some function
a\in L^{\infty}(B^{\mathbb{N}},\mu_{b})
. The commutation
[T^{R,\mu_{b}}_{t},a]=0
implies

a(xt)=a(x)
a.e. mod
\mu_{b}
. By ergodicity of the measure
\mu_{b}
on
B^{\mathbb{N}}
we conclude that
a(x)=const
hence
A=CI
, i.e, representation
T^{R,\mu_{b}}
is irreducible.

To illustrate the approximation we show here only that
e^{itx_{12}}\in\mathfrak{A}(B_{0}^{\mathbb{N}})

x_{12}\,\,\eta\,\,\mathfrak{A}(B_{0}^{\mathbb{N}})

\mathfrak{A}(B_{0}^{\mathbb{N}})

Definition 7.2

7.4
Koopman’s representation

Let
\alpha:G\rightarrow{\rm Aut}(X)
be a measurable action of a
group
G
on a measurable space
(X,\mu)
with

G
-quasi-invariant measure
\mu
, i.e,
\mu^{\alpha_{t}}\sim\mu

for all
t\in G
. With these date one can associate the
representation
\pi^{\alpha,\mu,X}:G\rightarrow U(L^{2}(X,\mu)),
by the following formula:

(\pi^{\alpha,\mu,X}_{t}f)(x)=(d\mu(\alpha_{t^{-1}}(x))/d\mu(x))^{1/2}f(\alpha_{t^{-1}}(x)),\quad f\in L^{2}(X,\mu).

(7.23)

In the case of an invariant measure this representation called
Koopman’s representation
.
We keep the same name for representation (
7.23
).

The following conjecture is a natural generalization of Ismagilov’s conjecture.

Conjecture 7.15

The representation (
7.23
)
is irreducible if and only if

1)
\mu^{g}\perp\mu\quad\text{for all}\quad g\in Z_{{\rm Aut}(X)}(\alpha(G))\backslash\{e\},\,\,

2) the measure
\mu
is
G
-ergodic.

Here
Z_{G}(H)
is a
centralizer
of the subgroup
H
in the group
G
:

Z_{G}(H)=\{g\in G\mid\{g,a\}=e\,\,\forall a\in H\},

where
\{g,a\}=gag^{-1}a^{-1}
.
In general, Conjecture
7.15
is false, our aim is to find when it holds, see the following section.

7.5
Group
{\rm GL}_{0}(2\infty,{\mathbb{R}})
acting on
m
infinite rows

Let us denote by
{\rm Mat}(2\infty,{\mathbb{R}})
the space of all
real matrices that are infinite in both directions:

{\rm Mat}(2\infty,{\mathbb{R}})=\Big\{x=\sum_{k,n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}x_{kn}E_{kn},\,\,x_{kn}\in{\mathbb{R}}\Big\}.

(7.24)

The group
G={\rm GL}_{0}(2\infty,{\mathbb{R}})=\varinjlim_{n,i^{s}}{\rm GL}(2n+1,{\mathbb{R}})
is defined as the inductive limit of the
general linear groups
G_{n}={\rm GL}(2n+1,{\mathbb{R}})
with
respect to the
symmetric embedding

i^{s}
:

G_{n}\ni x\mapsto i^{s}_{n+1}(x)=x+E_{-(n+1),-(n+1)}+E_{n+1,n+1}\in G_{n+1}.

(7.25)

For a fixed natural number
m
, consider a
G
-space
X_{m}
as the
following subspace of the space
{\rm Mat}(2\infty,{\mathbb{R}})
:

X_{m}=\Big\{x\in{\rm Mat}(2\infty,{\mathbb{R}})\mid x=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}x_{kn}E_{kn}\Big\}.

(7.26)

The group
{\rm GL}_{0}(2\infty,{\mathbb{R}})
acts from the right
on the space
X_{m}.
Namely, the right action of the group
{\rm GL}_{0}(2\infty,{\mathbb{R}})
is correctly defined on the space

X_{m}
by the formula
R_{t}(x)=xt^{-1},\,\,t\in G,\,\,x\in X_{m}
. We
define a Gaussian non-centered product measure

\mu:=\mu^{m}:=\mu_{(b,a)}^{m}

on the space
X_{m}:

\mu_{(b,a)}^{m}(x)=\otimes_{k=1}^{m}\otimes_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\mu_{(b_{kn},a_{kn})}(x_{kn}),

(7.27)

where

d\mu_{(b_{kn},a_{kn})}(x_{kn})=\sqrt{\frac{b_{kn}}{\pi}}e^{-b_{kn}(x_{kn}-a_{kn})^{2}}dx_{kn}

(7.28)

and
b=(b_{kn})_{k,n},\,\,b_{kn}>0,\,a=(a_{kn})_{k,n},\,a_{kn}\in{\mathbb{R}},\,1\leq k\leq m,\,n\in{\mathbb{Z}}.
Define the unitary
representation
T^{R,\mu,m}
of the group
{\rm GL}_{0}(2\infty,{\mathbb{R}})
on the space
L^{2}(X_{m},\mu^{m}_{(b,a)})

by the formula:

(T^{R,\mu,m}_{t}f)(x)=\big(d\mu_{(b,a)}^{m}(xt)/d\mu_{(b,a)}^{m}(x)\big)^{1/2}f(xt),\,\,f\in L^{2}(X_{m},\mu^{m}_{(b,a)}).

(7.29)

Obviously, the
centralizer

Z_{{\rm Aut}(X_{m})}(R(G))\subset{\rm Aut}(X_{m})
contains the group
L({\rm GL}(m,{\mathbb{R}}))
,
i.e., the image of the group
{\rm GL}(m,{\mathbb{R}})
with
respect to the left action
L:{\rm GL}(m,{\mathbb{R}})\rightarrow{\rm Aut}(X_{m}),\,L_{s}(x)\!=\!sx,\,s\in{\rm GL}(m,{\mathbb{R}}),\,x\in X_{m}.
We prove the following theorem.

Theorem 7.16

The representation
T^{R,\mu,m}\!:\!{\rm GL}_{0}(2\infty,{\mathbb{R}})\!\rightarrow\!U\Big(L^{2}(X_{m},\mu^{m}_{(b,a)})\Big)
is
irreducible
if and only if

\displaystyle(i)

\displaystyle(\mu^{m}_{(b,a)})^{L_{s}}\perp\mu^{m}_{(b,a)}\quad\text{for all}\quad s\in{\rm GL}(m,{\mathbb{R}})\backslash\{e\};

\displaystyle(ii)

\displaystyle\text{the measure}\quad\mu^{m}_{(b,a)}\quad\text{is $G$-{\rm ergodic}}.

In
[
9
,
10
]

this result was proved for
m\leq 2
. In
[
11
]
it was proved for
m=3
.
Note that conditions (i) and (ii) are necessary conditions for irreducibility.

Remark 7.3

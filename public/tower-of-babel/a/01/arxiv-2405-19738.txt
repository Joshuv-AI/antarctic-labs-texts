Abstract
. This paper is devoted to studying the weak Harnack inequalities for nonlocal double phase functionals by using expansion of positivity, whose prototype is ∬ ℝ n × ℝ n ( | u ⁡ ( x ) − u ⁡ ( y ) | p | x − y | n + s ​ p + a ⁡ ( x , y ) ​ | u ⁡ ( x ) − u ⁡ ( y ) | q | x − y | n + t ​ q ) ​ 𝑑 x ​ 𝑑 y \iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}+a(x,y)\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{q}}{|x-y|^{n+tq}}\right)\,dxdy with a ≥ 0 a\geq 0 and 0 < s ≤ t < 1 < p ≤ q 0<s\leq t<1<p\leq q . The core of our approach is to establish several measure theoretical estimates based on the nonlocal Caccioppoli-type inequality, where the challenges consist in controlling subtle interaction between the pointwise behaviour of modulating coefficient and the growth exponents. Meanwhile, a quantitative boundedness result on the minimizer of such functionals is also discussed.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we are interested in the weak Harnack inequalities for the minimizer of nonlocal double phase functionals, switching drastically between two diverse phases according to whether the coefficient
a
is zero or not, of the following form

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(u;\Omega):=\iint_{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}}\big[|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}K_{sp}(x,y)+a(x,y)|u(x)-u(y)|^{q}K_{tq}(x,y)\big]\,dxdy,

(1.1)

where
\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}=(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})\setminus((\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega)\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega))
,

0<s\leq t<1,\quad 1<p\leq q<\infty,

(1.2)

and
\Omega
is an open bounded subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(
n\geq 2
). Throughout this manuscript, the kernel functions
K_{sp},K_{tq}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
are supposed to satisfy

\frac{\Lambda^{-1}}{|x-y|^{sp}}\leq K_{sp}(x,y)\leq\frac{\Lambda}{|x-y|^{sp}},\quad\frac{\Lambda^{-1}}{|x-y|^{tq}}\leq K_{tq}(x,y)\leq\frac{\Lambda}{|x-y|^{tq}}

(1.3)

with a number
\Lambda\geq 1
, and the basic assumptions on the modulating coefficient
a:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
are measurable, symmetric and nonnegative:

0\leq a(x,y)=a(y,x)\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(1.4)

Additionally, when we investigate weak Harnack estimates, we also require

|a(x_{1},y_{1})-a(x_{2},y_{2})|\leq[a]_{\alpha}(|x_{1}-x_{2}|+|y_{1}-y_{2}|)^{\alpha}

(1.5)

with
\alpha\in(0,1]
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

The regularity theory on nonlocal problems has become an active realm of research in the past two decades. For the standard fractional Laplace equation, Caffarelli and Silvestre
[
9

[
8
]
for Hölder continuity of solutions to the parabolic version. Afterward, Di Castro, Kuusi and Palatucci
[
18
,
19
]
extended the De Giorgi-Nash-Moser technology to the nonlinear fractional
p
-Laplacian case, and obtained the local behaviors including Hölder, Harnack and weak Harnack estimates, where the key tool used is a logarithmic type inequality. One can refer to
[

,

,
27
,

,
30
,
14
,
32
,
35
]
for more topics, such as measure data problems, nonlocal Wiener criterion, equivalence of various solutions, non-homogeneous functionals with lower order terms, parabolic equations and so on.

In recent years, some regularity results mentioned above have been developed for the nonlocal functionals or equations exhibiting non-standard growth. For instance, Byun, Kim and Ok
[
3
]
proved the local boundedness and Hölder continuity for the nonlocal
G
-Laplace equations with Orlicz growth by exploiting the methods introduced by
[
19
]
; see also
[
10
]
for different approaches. On the other hand, for such an equation, Fang and Zhang
[
22
]
employed the De Giorgi iteration theory to establish the Harnack type inequality based on the weak Harnack estimate and boundedness of subsolutions. Subsequently, this result was improved by
[
11
,
4
]

. In addition, regarding the mixed local and nonlocal functionals with nonuniform growth, De Filippis and Mingione
[

studied the
C^{1,\alpha}_{\rm loc}
property through the perturbation argument, because the local
p
-Laplace operator refines the regularity directly.

For what concerns our main object in this work, the nonlocal double phase problem as a typical model with nonstandard growth has attracted increasing attention. De Filippis and Palatucci
[
17
]
first considered this kind of equation and explored the viscosity theory, and then a nonlocal self-improving attribute for bounded weak solutions was deduced in
[
36

[

[

, for minimizers/weak solutions gained the local Hölder regularity by considering carefully the interplay among growth powers, differentiability orders and coefficient
a

. Such results on the nonlocal functional (
1.1

\int_{\Omega}|Du|^{p}+a(x)|Du|^{q}\,dx,

[

proved the local gradient Hölder regularity for minimizers of nonuniformly elliptic integrals, that are not necessarily equipped with a Euler-Lagrange equation, which solved a longstanding and classical open problem in the regularity theory of variational integrals and elliptic equations. More investigations into local and nonlocal problems with nonstandard growth can be found in for example

The main goal of this article is to establish the weak Harnack inequalities for (
1.1
). To the best of our knowledge, the issues on Harnack type estimate or even on weak Harnack type inequality remain unsolved for the nonlocal double phase functionals/equations, although extensive research on such problems has been carried out up to now. To justify our results, we make use of the expansion of positivity method to derive some measure theoretical information just from the Caccioppoli estimate (Lemma
3.2
). Then weak inequalities follow from basic computations and without an application of Moser iteration that was however utilized to get such inequalities for the fractional
p
-Laplace equations in
[
18
, Theorem 1.2]
. Furthermore, these processes circumvent the complicated log-estimate paralleling
[
6

, and the functional (
1.1
) does not come into play at all except showing the energy inequality. Finally, let us comment that the approach developed for the nonlocal
p
-Laplacian scenario could not plainly work here because of the nonuniform ellipticity of (
1.1
). To overcome this difficulty, when inferring various measure estimates, we have to control the delicate interaction between the possibly degenerate part of the energy
a(x,y)|u(x)-u(y)|^{q}K_{tq}
and the non-degenerate one
|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}K_{sp}
, via applying a precise comparison scheme to distinguish two phases.

Before stating our main contributions, we introduce some terminologies as follows. For
k\in\mathbb{R}
, denote the functions

h_{r}(k):=\frac{k^{p-1}}{r^{sp}}+a^{-}_{r}\frac{k^{q-1}}{r^{tq}}

(1.7)

and

H_{r}(k)=\frac{k^{p}}{r^{sp}}+a^{-}_{r}\frac{k^{q}}{r^{tq}}

(1.8)

a^{-}_{r}:=\inf_{B_{r}(x_{0})\times B_{r}(x_{0})}a(x,y).

(1.9)

We use the symbols
H^{-1}_{r}
and
h^{-1}_{r}
to represent the inverses of
H_{r}
and
h_{r}

\mathrm{Tail}_{a}(u_{-};x_{0},r):=\sup_{y\in B_{r}(x_{0})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{r}(x_{0})}a(x,y)\frac{u_{-}^{q-1}(x)}{|x-x_{0}|^{n+tq}}\,dx.

If the function
a(\cdot,\cdot)\equiv 1
such tail becomes the usual tail, defined by

\mathrm{Tail}(u_{-};x_{0},r):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{r}(x_{0})}\frac{u_{-}^{p-1}(x)}{|x-x_{0}|^{n+sp}}\,dx.

Correspondingly, for the functional (

2.
Preliminaries

We in this part shall give some concepts and notations as well as auxiliary results. Throughout this manuscript, denote by
C
a positive constant that may vary from line to line. Relevant dependencies on parameters are emphasized by using parentheses, i.e.,
C(p,q,n)
means
C
depends on
p,q,n
. Let
B_{r}(x_{0}):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x-x_{0}|<r\}
represent a ball with radius

and center
x_{0}
. If not important or clear from the context, we will omit the center of the ball as
B_{r}=B_{r}(x_{0})
. To shorten the notations, for any subset

\mathbf{data}(D):=\begin{cases}n,p,q,s,t,\Lambda,\alpha,[a]_{\alpha},\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(D)},\quad&\text{if }sp\leq n,\\[5.69054pt]
n,p,q,s,t,\Lambda,\alpha,[a]_{\alpha},[u]_{W^{s,p}(D)},\quad&\text{if }sp>n.\end{cases}

For

, the fractional Sobolev space

is defined by

W^{s,p}(\Omega):=\left\{u\in L^{p}(\Omega)\Bigg|[u]_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}:=\left(\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,dxdy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty\right\},

equipped with the norm
\|u\|_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}=\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+[u]_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}

\varepsilon(u;\Omega):=\iint_{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}+a(x,y)\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{q}}{|x-y|^{n+tq}}\,dxdy,

\mathcal{A}(\Omega):=\left\{u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\,\Big|\,u|_{\Omega}\in L^{p}(\Omega)\ \text{and }\varepsilon(u;\Omega)<\infty\right\}.

Here it is easy to see via the previous definitions that
\mathcal{A}(\Omega)\subset W^{s,p}(\Omega)
. Moreover, due to fractional Sobolev embedding it holds that
\mathcal{A}(\Omega)\subset L^{q}(\Omega)
, if
p\leq q\leq\frac{np}{n-sp}
for

or
p\leq q<\infty
for
sp\geq n
. Now we state the definition of minimizer to the functional (
1.1
).

Definition 2.1
.

We call a function

a minimizer of (
1.1
) whenever

\mathcal{F}(u;\Omega)\leq\mathcal{F}(v;\Omega)

for each

with

\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
.

It is noteworthy that the existence of minimizer of (
1.1
) has been justified in
[
6
, Section 3]
. Taking the nonlocal characteristic of the functional (
1.1
) into account, we next introduce the tail space given as

L^{p-1}_{sp}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\left\{u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\,\Bigg|\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|u(x)|^{p-1}}{(1+|x|)^{n+sp}}\,dx<\infty\right\}.

Furthermore, owing to the

1.1
) perturbed by the modulating coefficient

, we have to consider a “tail space with weight” as follows,

L^{q-1}_{a,tq}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\left\{u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\,\Bigg|\,\sup_{x\in\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}a(x,y)\frac{|u(y)|^{p-1}}{(1+|y|)^{n+sp}}\,dy<\infty\right\}.

We can readily find the various tails defined in the Introduction are finite according to the definitions of tail spaces.

3.
Energy estimates

This section aims at establishing the Caccioppoli-type inequalities of the nonlocal version that contains all the information needed to conclude the weak Harnack estimates as well as boundedness. The first one is as follows.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
u\in\mathcal{A}(\Omega)\cap L^{p-1}_{sp}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{q-1}_{a,tq}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})

) and (

) with (

), we derive

\displaystyle\quad\int_{B_{2R}}\int_{B_{2R}}\frac{w_{-}(x)w_{+}^{p-1}(y)}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,dydx+a^{-}_{4R}\int_{B_{2R}}\int_{B_{2R}}\frac{w_{-}(x)w_{+}^{q-1}(y)}{|x-y|^{n+tq}}\,dydx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{2R}}w_{-}(x)\left(\int_{B_{2R}}\frac{w_{+}^{p-1}(y)}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}+a(x,y)\frac{w_{+}^{q-1}(y)}{|x-y|^{n+tq}}\,dy\right)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq CH_{4R}(l)|B_{4R}|.

(4.6)

In the sequel, we make two mutually exclusive alternatives:

\left(\frac{\delta\tau}{(4R)^{s}}\right)^{p}\geq a^{-}_{4R}\left(\frac{\delta\tau}{(4R)^{t}}\right)^{q}\quad\text{and}\quad\left(\frac{\delta\tau}{(4R)^{s}}\right)^{p}<a^{-}_{4R}\left(\frac{\delta\tau}{(4R)^{t}}\right)^{q}.

(4.7)

Case
\eqref{4-1-7}_{1}
. By letting

, we conclude from

, (

) and (

\displaystyle C\left(\frac{\delta\tau}{(4R)^{s}}\right)^{p}|B_{4R}|

\displaystyle\geq\int_{B_{2R}}\int_{B_{2R}}\frac{(u-4\delta\tau)_{-}(x)(u-4\delta\tau)_{+}^{p-1}(y)}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,dydx

\displaystyle\geq(4R)^{-n-sp}\int_{B_{2R}\cap\{u\geq\tau\}}(u-4\delta\tau)_{+}^{p-1}\,dy\cdot\int_{B_{2R}\cap\{u\leq 2\delta\tau\}}(u-4\delta\tau)_{-}\,dx

\displaystyle\geq(4R)^{-n-sp}[(1-4\delta)\tau]^{p-1}|B_{2R}\cap\{u\geq\tau\}|\cdot 2\delta\tau|B_{2R}\cap\{u\leq 2\delta\tau\}|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\nu\delta\tau^{p}}{2^{p-2}(4R)^{n+sp}}|B_{2R}||B_{2R}\cap\{u\leq 2\delta\tau\}|,

i.e.,

|B_{2R}\cap\{u\leq 2\delta\tau\}|\leq C\frac{\delta^{p-1}}{\nu}|B_{2R}|.

(4.8)

Case
\eqref{4-1-7}_{2}
. Notice
a^{-}_{4R}>0
at this time. Analogously, it yields that

\displaystyle Ca^{-}_{4R}\left(\frac{\delta\tau}{(4R)^{t}}\right)^{q}|B_{4R}|

\displaystyle\geq a^{-}_{4R}\int_{B_{2R}}\int_{B_{2R}}\frac{(u-4\delta\tau)_{-}(x)(u-4\delta\tau)_{+}^{q-1}(y)}{|x-y|^{n+tq}}\,dydx

\displaystyle\geq a^{-}_{4R}\frac{\nu\delta\tau^{q}}{2^{q-2}(4R)^{n+tq}}|B_{2R}||B_{2R}\cap\{u\leq 2\delta\tau\}|,

namely,

|B_{2R}\cap\{u\leq 2\delta\tau\}|\leq C\frac{\delta^{q-1}}{\nu}|B_{2R}|.

(4.9)

In summary, we deduce the desired conclusion from (
4.8
) and (
4.9
) due to

.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
u\in\mathcal{A}(\Omega)\cap L^{p-1}_{sp}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{q-1}_{a,tq}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

with

1.1
). For the case

, assume
u
is bounded in
B_{4R}
. Besides, suppose that

(4.10)

for
\tau\geq 0
and some
\nu\in(0,1]
. Then under the assumptions (
1.5
) and (
1.10
), there is a
\delta\in\left(0,\frac{1}{8}\right]
that depends on
\textbf{data}(B_{4R})
and
\nu
such that either

\mathrm{Tail}(u_{-};x_{0},4R)+\mathrm{Tail}_{a}(u_{-};x_{0},4R)\geq h_{4R}(\delta\tau)

(4.11)

or

u\geq\delta\tau\quad\text{in }B_{R}.

Proof.

If (
4.11
) is not true, we are going to prove

in
B_{R}
in the remaining contents. Here assume
\tau\leq\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}(B_{4R})
from (
4.10
) and the boundedness of
u
. Let
\delta\tau\leq l<k\leq 2\delta\tau
and
R\leq\rho<r\leq 2R
. Define

We pick

\beta:=\frac{p_{s}^{*}}{p}\leq\frac{q_{t}^{*}}{q}.

(4.12)

In fact, if

, then

via

; when

, then we take

sufficiently large so that (
4.12
) holds true.

In the scenario

, we can see that the minimizer

belongs to

so we can employ the fractional Sobolev embedding theorem and Hölder inequality to deduce

\displaystyle\quad\left[\left(\frac{k-l}{\rho^{s}}\right)^{p}+a^{-}_{4R}\left(\frac{k-l}{\rho^{t}}\right)^{q}\right]\left(\frac{E^{-}(l,\rho)}{|B_{\rho}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}

\displaystyle\leq\left({\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{B_{\rho}}\left[\left(\frac{(u-k)_{-}}{\rho^{s}}\right)^{p}+a^{-}_{4R}\left(\frac{(u-k)_{-}}{\rho^{t}}\right)^{q}\right]^{\beta}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}

\displaystyle\quad+C{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{B_{\rho}}\left(\frac{w_{-}}{\rho^{s}}\right)^{p}+a^{-}_{4R}\left(\frac{w_{-}}{\rho^{t}}\right)^{q}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{B_{\rho}}\int_{B_{\rho}}\frac{|w_{-}(x)-w_{-}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,dxdy+C{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{B_{\rho}}\int_{B_{\rho}}a^{-}_{4R}\frac{|w_{-}(x)-w_{-}(y)|^{q}}{|x-y|^{n+tq}}\,dxdy

\displaystyle\quad+CH_{4R}(k)\frac{|E^{-}(k,r)|}{|B_{r}|},

where
w_{-}:=(u-k)_{-}
, and in the last inequality the nonnegativity of
u
in
B_{4R}
and the relation
R\leq\rho<r\leq 2R
were used. Exploiting Lemma
3.2
in the display above and recalling the rang of
\rho
and

, we arrive at

\displaystyle\quad H_{4R}(k-l)\left(\frac{|E^{-}(l,\rho)|}{|B_{\rho}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{r}{r-\rho}\right)^{n+q}\Bigg[{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{B_{r}}\left(\frac{w_{-}}{\rho^{s}}\right)^{p}+a^{-}_{4R}\left(\frac{w_{-}}{\rho^{t}}\right)^{q}\,dx

\displaystyle\quad+{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{B_{r}}w_{-}(y)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{\rho}}\frac{w_{-}^{p-1}(x)}{|x-x_{0}|^{n+sp}}+a(x,y)\frac{w_{-}^{q-1}(x)}{|x-x_{0}|^{n+tq}}\,dx\right)dy\Bigg]+CH_{4R}(k)\frac{|E^{-}(k,r)|}{|B_{r}|}

\displaystyle\quad+C\left(\frac{r}{r-\rho}\right)^{n+q}{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{B_{r}}w_{-}(y)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{\rho}}\frac{w_{-}^{p-1}(x)}{|x-x_{0}|^{n+sp}}+a(x,y)\frac{w_{-}^{q-1}(x)}{|x-x_{0}|^{n+tq}}\,dx\right)dy,

\displaystyle\leq Ck|E^{-}(k,r)|\mathrm{Tail}_{a}(u_{-};x_{0},4R)+Ck^{q}|E^{-}(k,r)|(\rho^{\alpha-tq}+a^{-}_{4R}\rho^{-tq})

\displaystyle\leq C\left(H_{4R}(k)+k^{q-p}\frac{k^{p}}{(4R)^{sp}}+a^{-}_{4R}\frac{k^{q}}{(4R)^{tq}}\right)|E^{-}(k,r)|

Here we made use of

,
\rho\approx R\leq 1
and
k\leq\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{4R})}
. Merging the preceding three estimates infers

\frac{|E^{-}(l,\rho)|}{|B_{\rho}|}\leq C\left(\frac{r}{r-\rho}\right)^{(n+q)\beta}\left(\frac{H_{4R}(k)}{H_{4R}(k-l)}\right)^{\beta}\left(\frac{|E^{-}(k,r)|}{|B_{r}|}\right)^{\beta}

depending upon
\textbf{data}(B_{4R})

and

obtains

4.1
. We choose some

and
\nu

(4.15)

That is to say, we deduce from (

4.15
) and Lemma

namely,

u\geq\delta\tau\quad\text{in }B_{R}.

If

) as well.

Case
\eqref{4-3-4}_{1}
. When
|x-x_{0}|\leq 2R
and
|y-x_{0}|\geq 2R
, we have
|x-y|\leq 2|y-x_{0}|
. Due to (
4.23
), one obtains

\displaystyle C\frac{\tau^{p}}{(4R)^{sp}}|B_{4R}|

\displaystyle\geq CH_{4R}(\tau)|B_{4R}|

\displaystyle\geq\int_{B_{2R}}w_{-}(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{w_{+}^{p-1}(y)}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,dydx

\displaystyle\geq\int_{B_{2R}}w_{-}(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{2R}}\frac{\frac{1}{C}u_{+}^{p-1}(y)-\tau^{p-1}}{(2|y-x_{0}|)^{n+sp}}\,dydx

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{C}\int_{B_{2R}}w_{-}(x)\,dx\cdot\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{2R}}\frac{u_{+}^{p-1}(y)}{|y-x_{0}|^{n+sp}}\,dy-C\frac{\tau^{p}}{(4R)^{sp}}|B_{2R}|,

i.e.,

\mathrm{Tail}(u_{+};x_{0},2R)\int_{B_{2R}}w_{-}(x)\,dx\leq C\frac{\tau^{p}}{(4R)^{sp}}|B_{2R}|.

Finally, it follows from the inequality (
4.22
) that

|B_{2R}\cap\{u\leq\tau\}|\leq\frac{C\tau^{p-1}}{(2R)^{sp}\mathrm{Tail}(u_{+};x_{0},2R)}|B_{2R}|.

(4.24)

Case
\eqref{4-3-4}_{2}
. In an analogous manner to Case
\eqref{4-3-4}_{1}
in this proof, we evaluate

\displaystyle Ca^{-}_{4R}\frac{\tau^{q}}{(4R)^{tq}}|B_{4R}|

\displaystyle\geq\int_{B_{2R}}w_{-}(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}a(x,y)\frac{w_{+}^{q-1}(y)}{|x-y|^{n+tq}}\,dydx

\displaystyle\geq\int_{B_{2R}}w_{-}(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{2R}}a(x,y)\frac{\frac{1}{C}u_{+}^{q-1}(y)-\tau^{q-1}}{(2|y-x_{0}|)^{n+tq}}\,dydx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{C}\int_{B_{2R}}w_{-}(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{2R}}a(x,y)\frac{u_{+}^{q-1}(y)}{|y-x_{0}|^{n+tq}}\,dydx

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\tau^{q-1}}{C}\int_{B_{2R}}w_{-}(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{2R}}\frac{a(x,y)}{|y-x_{0}|^{n+tq}}\,dydx

\displaystyle=:I_{1}-I_{2}.

(4.25)

Since
a(x,y)\geq\lambda
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we readily discover

I_{1}\geq\frac{\lambda}{C}\int_{B_{2R}}w_{-}(x)\,dx\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{2R}}\frac{u_{+}^{q-1}(y)}{|y-x_{0}|^{n+tq}}\,dy.

(4.26)

Here, let us remark that if
a(x,y)\geq 0
instead of
a(x,y)\geq\lambda
, then we do not know how to find a proper nonlocal integral bounding
I_{1}
from below so that we can divide the integrals with respect to
x
and
y
.
Now we focus on dealing with
I_{2}
. For
x\in B_{2R}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{2R}
, by means of the Hölder continuity of
a(\cdot,\cdot)
, we can see

\displaystyle a(x,y)

\displaystyle=a(x,y)-a(x,x)+a(x,x)-a^{-}_{4R}+a^{-}_{4R}

\displaystyle\leq[a]_{\alpha}|x-y|^{\alpha}+a(x,x)-a^{-}_{4R}+a^{-}_{4R}

\displaystyle\leq 2[a]_{\alpha}|y-x_{0}|^{\alpha}+\sup_{B_{4R}\times B_{4R}}|a(x,y)-a(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})|+a^{-}_{4R}

\displaystyle\leq 2[a]_{\alpha}|y-x_{0}|^{\alpha}+C[a]_{\alpha}R^{\alpha}+a^{-}_{4R}.

Therefore, by virtue of
R\leq 1
and
\lambda\leq a^{-}_{4R}
,

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\tau^{q-1}}{C}\int_{B_{2R}}w_{-}(x)\,dx\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{2R}}\frac{2[a]_{\alpha}|y-x_{0}|^{\alpha}+C[a]_{\alpha}R^{\alpha}+a^{-}_{4R}}{|y-x_{0}|^{n+tq}}\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\tau^{q}}{C}(R^{\alpha-tq}+a^{-}_{4R}R^{-tq})|B_{2R}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{a^{-}_{4R}+1}{C}\frac{\tau^{q}}{R^{tq}}|B_{2R}|

\displaystyle\leq a^{-}_{4R}\frac{\lambda^{-1}+1}{C}\frac{\tau^{q}}{(4R)^{tq}}|B_{2R}|.

(4.27)

Combining (
4.26
) and (
4.27
) with (
4.25
) yields that

\int_{B_{2R}}w_{-}(x)\,dx\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{2R}}\frac{u_{+}^{q-1}(y)}{|y-x_{0}|^{n+tq}}\,dy\leq Ca^{-}_{4R}\frac{\tau^{q}}{(4R)^{tq}}|B_{2R}|.

Via (
4.22
), we arrive at

|\{u\leq\tau\}\cap B_{2R}|\leq Ca^{-}_{4R}\frac{\tau^{q-1}}{(2R)^{tq}\mathrm{Tail}_{q}(u_{+};x_{0},2R)}|B_{2R}|,

(4.28)

where the constant
C>1
depends on
\textbf{data}(B_{4R})
and
\lambda
. As a consequence, we immediately deduce the desired measure estimate from (
4.24
) and (
4.28
).
∎

5.
Proof of weak Harnack inequalities

In this section, we complete in turn the proof of three weak Harnack estimates, Theorems
1.1
–
1.4
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.
Let
B_{4R}(x_{0})\subset\Omega
with
R\leq 1
, and the minimizer
u
be nonnegative and bounded in
B_{4R}(x_{0})
. We will neglect the center
x_{0}
in this proof. We assume
u\not\equiv 0
in
B_{R}
or else there is nothing to prove. Let
\delta\in\left(0,\frac{1}{8}\right]
be the parameter provided by Corollary
4.4
with
\nu=\frac{1}{2}
. Set

\beta:=\frac{1}{2\log_{\frac{1}{2}}\delta}\in(0,1).

First, we claim that for
\tau\geq 0
there holds

\inf_{B_{R}(x_{0})}u+h_{4R}^{-1}\big(\mathrm{Tail}(u_{-};x_{0},4R)+\mathrm{Tail}_{a}(u_{-};x_{0},4R)\big)\geq\delta\left(\frac{|E^{+}(\tau,R)|}{|B_{R}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}\tau,

(5.1)

where the set
E^{+}(\tau,R):=B_{R}(x_{0})\cap\{u>\tau\}
. Owing to the definition of
E^{+}(\tau,R)
, the case
\tau\geq\sup_{B_{R}}u
is obvious since
u
is locally bounded in
\Omega
. Then we only need to show this assertion for
\tau\in\left[0,\sup_{B_{R}}u\right)
. Now given a
\tau\in\left[0,\sup_{B_{R}}u\right)
, let
k
be the smallest integer such that

|E^{+}(\tau,R)|\geq 2^{-k}|B_{R}|,

(5.2)

that is,

\log_{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{|E^{+}(\tau,R)|}{|B_{R}|}\leq k<1+\log_{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{|E^{+}(\tau,R)|}{|B_{R}|}.

We further have

\delta^{k}\geq\delta\left(\frac{|E^{+}(\tau,R)|}{|B_{R}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}.

(5.3)

Due to
u\geq 0
in
B_{4R}
, (
5.1
) directly holds true provided

\mathrm{Tail}(u_{-};x_{0},4R)+\mathrm{Tail}_{a}(u_{-};x_{0},4R)\geq h_{4R}(\delta^{k}\tau).

(5.4)

Otherwise, if (
5.4
) is false, then we combine (
5.2
) with the converse of (
5.4
) and employ Corollary
4.4
to deduce

u\geq\delta^{k}\tau\quad\text{in }B_{R}.

All in all, we have

\inf_{B_{R}}u+h_{4R}^{-1}\big(\mathrm{Tail}(u_{-};x_{0},4R)+\mathrm{Tail}_{a}(u_{-};x_{0},4R)\big)\geq\delta^{k}\tau,

and from (
5.3
) get the inequality (
5.1
).

Next, we denote

L:=\inf_{B_{R}}u+h_{4R}^{-1}\big(\mathrm{Tail}(u_{-};x_{0},4R)+\mathrm{Tail}_{a}(u_{-};x_{0},4R)\big)

and rearrange (
5.1
) as

\left(\frac{L}{\delta\tau}\right)^{\beta}\geq\frac{|E^{+}(\tau,R)|}{|B_{R}|}.

Using the last display and Cavalieri’s principle, we calculate

\displaystyle{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{B_{R}}u^{\frac{\beta}{2}}(x)\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta}{2}\int^{\infty}_{0}\tau^{\frac{\beta}{2}-1}\frac{|E^{+}(\tau,R)|}{|B_{R}|}\,d\tau

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\beta}{2}\left[\int^{L}_{0}\tau^{\frac{\beta}{2}-1}\,d\tau+\left(\frac{L}{\delta}\right)^{\beta}\int^{\infty}_{L}\tau^{\frac{\beta}{2}-1-\beta}\,d\tau\right]

\displaystyle\leq(1+\delta^{-\beta})L^{\frac{\beta}{2}}.

Therefore, we have finished the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
\Box

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.
Let
B_{4R}(x_{0})\subset\Omega
with
R\leq 1
, and the minimizer
u
be nonnegative and bounded in
B_{4R}(x_{0})
. We also omit the center
x_{0}
later. Let the parameter
\nu\in(0,1)
be from Corollary
4.3
. Moreover, according to Corollary
4.3
with the constant
C\geq 1
determined there, we select
\tau>0
to satisfy

C\left(\frac{\tau^{p-1}}{(u^{p-1})_{B_{2R}}}+\frac{\tau^{q-1}}{(u^{q-1})_{B_{2R}}}\right)\leq\nu.

Then we further choose such
\tau>0
that

\frac{\tau^{p-1}}{(u^{p-1})_{B_{2R}}}\leq\frac{\nu}{2C}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{\tau^{q-1}}{(u^{q-1})_{B_{2R}}}\leq\frac{\nu}{2C},

namely,

\tau\leq\left(\frac{\nu}{2C}(u^{p-1})_{B_{2R}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\quad\text{and}\quad\tau\leq\left(\frac{\nu}{2C}(u^{q-1})_{B_{2R}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q-1}}.

(5.5)

Thus we take

\displaystyle\tau=\left(\frac{\nu}{2C}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\min\left\{(u^{p-1})_{B_{2R}}^{\frac{1}{p-1}},(u^{q-1})_{B_{2R}}^{\frac{1}{q-1}}\right\}

). At this moment, through applying Corollary
4.3
, we draw a conclusion that either

\mathrm{Tail}(u_{-};x_{0},4R)+\mathrm{Tail}_{a}(u_{-};x_{0},4R)\geq h_{4R}(\tau)

or

u\geq\frac{1}{2}\tau\quad\text{in }B_{R}.

As a consequence, it holds that

\inf_{B_{R}}u+h_{4R}^{-1}\big(\mathrm{Tail}(u_{-};x_{0},4R)+\mathrm{Tail}_{a}(u_{-};x_{0},4R)\big)\geq\frac{3}{2}\tau.

Finally, the choice of
\tau
and this inequality directly imply the desired result.
\Box

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.
Let
B_{4R}(x_{0})\subset\Omega
with
R\leq 1
, and the minimizer
u
be nonnegative and bounded in

. Let the number

. Now pick a suitable

(5.6)

with the constant

.

We next select

Proof of Theorem

where we have used

Next, let the number

\displaystyle\quad{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{B_{i+1}}H_{r}(w_{i+1})\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\left({\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{B_{i+1}}H_{r}^{\beta}(w_{i+1})\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}\left(\frac{|E^{+}(k_{i+1},r_{i+1})|}{|B_{i+1}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq\left[\frac{1}{r^{sp}}\left({\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{B_{i+1}}w_{i+1}^{p\beta}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}+\frac{a^{-}_{r}}{r^{tq}}\left({\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{B_{i+1}}w_{i+1}^{q\beta}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}\right]\left(\frac{|E^{+}(k_{i+1},r_{i+1})|}{|B_{i+1}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\Bigg[{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{B_{i+1}}\int_{B_{i+1}}\frac{|w_{i+1}(x)-w_{i+1}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}+a_{r}^{-}\frac{|w_{i+1}(x)-w_{i+1}(y)|^{q}}{|x-y|^{n+tq}}\,dxdy

\displaystyle\quad+{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{B_{i+1}}\left(\frac{w_{i+1}^{p}}{r^{sp}}+a_{r}^{-}\frac{w_{i+1}^{q}}{r^{tq}}\right)dx\Bigg]\left(\frac{|E^{+}(k_{i+1},r_{i+1})|}{|B_{i+1}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta^{\prime}}}

(6.5)

with
E^{+}(k_{i+1},r_{i+1})=\{u>k_{i+1}\}\cap B_{i+1}
. Observe that

|E^{+}(k_{i+1},r_{i+1})|\leq\int_{B_{i}}\frac{H_{r}(w_{i})}{H_{r}(k_{i+1}-k_{i})}\,dx.

(6.6)

Thanks to the displays (
6.4
)–(
6.6
),

\displaystyle{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{B_{i+1}}H_{r}(w_{i+1})\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{2^{i(n+q)}}{H_{r}^{\frac{1}{\beta^{\prime}}}(k_{i+1}-k_{i})}\left(1+\frac{T\Big(u_{+};x_{0},\frac{r}{2}\Big)}{h_{r}(k_{i+1}-k_{i})}\right)\left({\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{B_{i}}H_{r}(w_{i})\,dx\right)^{1+\frac{1}{\beta^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{2^{i(n+3q)}}{H_{r}^{\frac{1}{\beta^{\prime}}}(\overline{k})}\left(1+\frac{T\Big(u_{+};x_{0},\frac{r}{2}\Big)}{h_{r}(\overline{k})}\right)\left({\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{B_{i}}H_{r}(w_{i})\,dx\right)^{1+\frac{1}{\beta^{\prime}}}.

Denote

Y_{i}={\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{B_{i}}H_{r}(w_{i})\,dx.

We now choose
\overline{k}>0
so large that

\frac{T\Big(u_{+};x_{0},\frac{r}{2}\Big)}{h_{r}(\overline{k}/\delta)}\leq\frac{\delta^{p}T\Big(u_{+};x_{0},\frac{r}{2}\Big)}{h_{r}(\overline{k})}\leq 1.

(6.7)

Then we will get

Y_{i+1}\leq\frac{C2^{i(n+3q)}}{\delta^{p}H_{r}^{\frac{1}{\beta^{\prime}}}(\overline{k})}Y_{i}^{1+\frac{1}{\beta^{\prime}}}.

Through employing Lemma
2.4
, we know that if

Y_{0}\leq\left(\frac{C}{\delta^{p}H_{r}^{\frac{1}{\beta^{\prime}}}(\overline{k})}\right)^{-\beta^{\prime}}2^{-(n+3q)\beta^{\prime 2}},

(6.8)

then it holds that
Y_{i}\rightarrow 0
as
i\rightarrow\infty
. Meanwhile, we get
u\leq 2\overline{k}
in
B_{\frac{r}{2}}
. The remaining work is to select further
\overline{k}>0
large enough such that (
6.8
) is true. Notice

Y_{0}={\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{B_{r}}H_{r}((u-\overline{k})_{+})\,dx\leq{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{B_{r}}H_{r}(u_{+})\,dx.

Hence we just need to require

{\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{B_{r}}H_{r}(u_{+})\,dx\leq\left(\frac{C}{\delta^{p}H_{r}^{\frac{1}{\beta^{\prime}}}(\overline{k})}\right)^{-\beta^{\prime}}2^{-(n+3q)\beta^{\prime 2}},

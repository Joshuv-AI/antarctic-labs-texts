On the persistent homology of almost surely C 0 ​ ( [ 0 , t ] , ℝ ) C^{0}([0,t],\mathbb{R}) stochastic processes
Daniel Perez

Abstract
This paper investigates the propreties of the persistence diagrams stemming from almost surely continuous random processes on [ 0 , t ] [0,t] . We focus our study on two variables which together characterize the barcode : the number of points of the persistence diagram inside a rectangle ] − ∞ , x ] × [ x + ε , ∞ [ ]\!-\!\infty,x]\times[x+\varepsilon,\infty[ , N x , x + ε N^{x,x+\varepsilon} and the number of bars of length ≥ ε \geq\varepsilon , N ε N^{\varepsilon} . For processes with the strong Markov property, we show both of these variables admit a moment generating function and in particular moments of every order. Switching our attention to semimartingales, we show the asymptotic behaviour of N ε N^{\varepsilon} and N x , x + ε N^{x,x+\varepsilon} as ε → 0 \varepsilon\to 0 and of N ε N^{\varepsilon} as ε → ∞ \varepsilon\to\infty . Finally, we study the repercussions of the classical stability theorem of barcodes and illustrate our results with some examples, most notably Brownian motion and empirical functions converging to the Brownian bridge.

Our contribution

Our contribution can be summarized along the following points.

Theorem 1

(Theorem

(Corollary

Under the same assumptions,

\mathbb{E}\!\left[N^{\varepsilon}_{X}\right]\sim\mathbb{P}(R_{t}\geq\varepsilon)\quad\text{as }\varepsilon\to\infty\,.

Definition/Proposition 2.4
.

The number of bars alive at
x
persisting through

N^{x,x+\varepsilon}_{f}:=\rk(H_{0}(\{f\geq x+\varepsilon\}\to\{f\geq x\}))=\#\{\tau\in T_{f}\,|\,f(\tau)=x\text{ and }h(\tau)\geq\varepsilon\}\,.

Proof.

By the results of

Remark 2.5

(Link with traditional persistence diagrams and sublevel set filtrations)
.

In the tree formalism, it is typical to consider
superlevel
filtrations as opposed to sublevel ones, as is typically done in traditional persistence theory. Considering one or the other poses no problem for us, as one can pass from one filtration to the other by switching
f
into
-f
. Diagrams (in the sense of collections of points
(b,d)
of moments of birth
b
and death
d
of bars of the barcode) of a filtration by superlevel sets lie
below
the diagonal, as the moment of birth occurs
higher
than the moment of death.
Given the diagram of
-f
as computed with the
superlevel
filtration (using for instance the tree), we can retrieve the diagram associated to
sublevels
of
f
by sending each point in the diagram (of the superlevel filtration of
-f
)

(b,d)\mapsto(-d,-b)\,.

(2.20)

For most results, this subtlety makes little to no difference, as most of the examples considered (typically Brownian motion
B
) are processes which are symmetric,
i.e.

B_{t}=-B_{t}
in distribution for all
t
. This is notably the case for the results obtained by Chazal and Divol
[
7
]
.

Finally, with respect to the superlevel (resp. sublevel) set filtration, we will henceforth
always
consider that the infinite bars of the barcode are capped at
\inf(f)
(resp.
\sup(f)
). Equivalently, in terms of barcodes we will always consider for any bar

b=b\cap[\inf f,\sup f]\,.

(2.21)

On a tree
T_{f}
, we can define a notion of integration by defining the unique atomless Borel measure
\lambda
which is characterized by the property that every geodesic segment on
T_{f}
has measure equal to its length. Formally, we can express
\lambda
in two ways
[
23
]

\lambda=\int_{\mathbb{R}}dx\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau\in T_{f}\\
f(\tau)=x\end{subarray}}\delta_{\tau}\quad\text{and}\quad\lambda=\int_{0}^{\infty}d\varepsilon\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau\in T_{f}\\
h(\tau)=\varepsilon\end{subarray}}\delta_{\tau}\,.

(2.22)

Proposition 2.6
.

\lambda(T_{f}^{\varepsilon})=\int_{\varepsilon}^{\infty}N^{a}_{f}\;da=\int_{\mathbb{R}}N^{x,x+\varepsilon}_{f}\;dx

(2.23)

Proof.

By using the second identity for
\lambda
,

\lambda(T_{f}^{\varepsilon})=\int_{\varepsilon}^{\infty}N^{a}_{f}\;da\,,

(2.24)

since every sum in the second expression is finite for all
\varepsilon>0
and has
N^{\varepsilon}
terms. Writing it using the first identity, we must restrict the sum in the identity to

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau\in T_{f}\\
f(\tau)=x\\
h(\tau)\geq\varepsilon\end{subarray}}\delta_{\tau}\,,

(2.25)

which is finite for all
\varepsilon>0
. There are exactly
N^{x,x+\varepsilon}_{f}
terms in this sum, therefore

\lambda(T_{f}^{\varepsilon})=\int_{\mathbb{R}}N^{x,x+\varepsilon}_{f}\;dx\,.

(2.26)

Consider now

Definition 2.7

The

)
preimage of

\overrightarrow{\tau}:=\sup\pi_{f}^{-1}(\tau)\quad\text{(resp. }\overleftarrow{\tau}:=\inf\pi_{f}^{-1}(\tau)\text{)}\,.

Remark 2.8

Notice we can have

Whenever

, we can compute

Definition 2.9

Setting

\displaystyle T_{i+1}^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle:=\inf\left\{\,s\geq S_{i}\;\Bigg|\;\sup_{[S_{i}^{\varepsilon},s]}f-f(s)>\varepsilon\right\}

\displaystyle S_{i+1}^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle:=\inf\left\{\,s\geq T_{i+1}\;\Bigg|\;f(s)-\inf_{[T_{i+1}^{\varepsilon},s]}f>\varepsilon\right\}

Lemma 2.10
.

If
k\geq 2
, and

is continuous,

N^{\varepsilon}_{f}\geq k\iff S_{k-1}^{\varepsilon}\leq t\,.

(2.28)

Proof.

(N^{\varepsilon}\geq k\implies S_{k-1}^{\varepsilon}\leq t)
: We start by noticing we can order the bars by the value of their preimages by virtue of the total order on
\mathbb{R}
. Since
N^{\varepsilon}\geq k
, there are at least
(k-1)
right preimages and left preimages by
\pi_{f}
of leaves of
T_{f}^{\varepsilon}
stemming from the first
(k-1)
bars, which we denote
\{(\overleftarrow{\tau}_{i},\overrightarrow{\tau}_{i})\}_{1\leq i\leq k-1}
satisfying

\overleftarrow{\tau}_{1}\leq\overrightarrow{\tau}_{1}=T^{\varepsilon}_{1}<S_{1}^{\varepsilon}\leq\overleftarrow{\tau}_{2}\leq\overrightarrow{\tau}_{2}=T^{\varepsilon}_{2}<\cdots\leq\overleftarrow{\tau}_{k-1}\leq\overrightarrow{\tau}_{k-1}=T^{\varepsilon}_{k-1}\,.

(2.29)

Note that
\overrightarrow{\tau}_{1}>0
as soon as
k\geq 2
and
\varepsilon>0
. But
N^{\varepsilon}\geq k
, therefore there must exist a preimage
\overleftarrow{\tau}_{k}\leq t
corresponding to the
k
th bar. But
S_{k-1}^{\varepsilon}\leq\overleftarrow{\tau}_{k}\leq t
by definition of
S_{k-1}^{\varepsilon}
.

(N^{\varepsilon}\geq k\Longleftarrow S_{k-1}^{\varepsilon}\leq t)
: Since
S_{k-1}^{\varepsilon}>T_{k-1}^{\varepsilon}=\overrightarrow{\tau}_{k-1}
, there are at least
(k-1)

.

Definition 2.11

Let
f:[0,t]\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function. Setting
US_{0}^{x,\varepsilon}=UT_{0}^{x,\varepsilon}=0
, we define a sequence of times recursively

\displaystyle UT_{i+1}^{x,\varepsilon}

\displaystyle:=\inf\left\{\,s\geq US_{i}^{x,\varepsilon}\;\Bigg|\;f(s)\leq x\wedge(x+\varepsilon)\right\}

\displaystyle US_{i+1}^{x,\varepsilon}

\displaystyle:=\inf\left\{\,s\geq UT_{i+1}^{x,\varepsilon}\;\Bigg|\;f(s)\geq x\vee(x+\varepsilon)\right\}\,.

The maximum
i
for which
US_{i}^{x,\varepsilon}\leq t
is called the
number of upcrossings by
f
from
x
to
x+\varepsilon
and we denote it
U^{x,x+\varepsilon}_{f}
. Similarly, setting
DS_{0}^{x,\varepsilon}=DT_{0}^{x,\varepsilon}=0
and defining

\displaystyle DT_{i+1}^{x,\varepsilon}

\displaystyle:=\inf\left\{\,s\geq DS_{i+1}^{x,\varepsilon}\;\Bigg|\;f(s)\leq x\wedge(x+\varepsilon)\right\}

\displaystyle DS_{i+1}^{x,\varepsilon}

\displaystyle:=\inf\left\{\,s\geq DT_{i}^{x,\varepsilon}\;\Bigg|\;f(s)\geq x\vee(x+\varepsilon)\right\}\,,

we can define the
number of downcrossings by
f
from
x
to
x+\varepsilon
and denote it
D^{x,x+\varepsilon}_{f}
as the maximum
i
for which
DT_{i}^{x,\varepsilon}\leq t
.

Proposition 2.12
.

Let
f:[0,t]\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function. Then,

N^{x,x+\varepsilon}_{f}=U_{f}^{x,x+\varepsilon}\vee D_{f}^{x,x+\varepsilon}\leq D^{x,x+\varepsilon}_{f}+1\,.

(2.30)

If
x\geq 0
, and
f(0)=0
,
N^{x,x+\varepsilon}_{f}=U_{f}^{x,x+\varepsilon}
.

Proof.

Each bar alive at
x+\varepsilon
persisting until
x
(seen as a branch of
T_{f}
) admits a left and right preimage (which can be sometimes equal), this shows
N^{x,x+\varepsilon}\geq U_{f}^{x,x+\varepsilon}\vee D_{f}^{x,x+\varepsilon}
. Conversely, every downcrossing (resp. upcrossing) generates a distinct homology class alive at
x+\varepsilon
persisting until
x
, which shows
U_{f}^{x,x+\varepsilon}\vee D_{f}^{x,x+\varepsilon}\geq N^{x,x+\varepsilon}
. The last inequality is a consequence of the fact that, by definition
\left\lvert U^{x,x+\varepsilon}_{f}-D^{x,x+\varepsilon}_{f}\right\rvert\leq 1
. Finally, if
x\geq 0
, and
f(0)=0
, by continuity of
f
, there is a bijective correspondence between upcrossings and the bars alive at
x+\varepsilon
persisting until
x
.
∎

Theorem 2.13
.

Let
X
be a non-constant stochastic process on
[0,t]
defined on the filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
allowing an almost surely continuous modification and satisfying the strong Markov property. Denote

R_{t}:=\sup_{[0,t]}X-\inf_{[0,t]}X\,,

(2.31)

Then,

\mathbb{P}(N^{\varepsilon}\geq k)\leq\mathbb{P}(R_{t}\geq\varepsilon)^{2(k-1)}\,.

(2.32)

Suppose further that
X
that there exists some
\varepsilon^{*}
such that for all
\varepsilon>\varepsilon^{*}
,
\mathbb{P}(R_{t}\geq\varepsilon^{*})<1
.
Then for every
\varepsilon>\varepsilon^{*}
and every
x\in\mathbb{R}
, all the moments of the random variables
N^{\varepsilon}
and
N^{x,x+\varepsilon}
are finite and their moment generating functions
M(\lambda)
converge uniformly and absolutely for every
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
such that
\Real(\lambda)>2\log(\mathbb{P}(R_{t}\geq\varepsilon))
.

Proof.

For simplicity, let us set
t=1
. The probability
\mathbb{P}(N^{\varepsilon}\geq k)
can be written in terms of the stopping times
T_{i}^{\varepsilon}
and
S_{i}^{\varepsilon}
and their increments by the (strong) Markov property of
X
. By lemma
2.10
,
\mathbb{P}(N^{\varepsilon}\geq k)=\mathbb{P}(S_{k-1}^{\varepsilon}\leq 1)
for
k\geq 2
, so

\mathbb{P}(N^{\varepsilon}\geq k)=\int_{\Sigma_{2k-2}}\mathbb{P}(T_{1}^{\varepsilon}=t_{1})\mathbb{P}(S_{1}=s_{1}\,|\,T_{1}=t_{1})\cdots\mathbb{P}(S_{k-1}=s_{k-1}\,|\,T_{k-1}=t_{k-1})\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}ds_{i}dt_{i}\,.

(2.33)

where
\Sigma_{2k-2}
denotes the simplex

\Sigma_{2k-2}:=\left\{(t_{1},s_{1},\cdots,s_{k-1})\in\mathbb{R}^{2k-1}\;\big|\;0\leq t_{1}\leq s_{2}\leq\cdots\leq s_{k-1}\leq 1\right\}\,.

(2.34)

By the definition of these stopping times we know that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(s\leq T_{i}^{\varepsilon}\leq t|S_{i-1}=s)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\!\left(\sup_{\tau\in[s,t]}\left[\sup_{[s,\tau]}X-X_{\tau}\right]\geq\varepsilon\right)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(t\leq S_{i}^{\varepsilon}\leq s|T_{i}=t)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\!\left(\sup_{\tau\in[t,s]}\left[X_{\tau}-\inf_{[t,\tau]}X\right]\geq\varepsilon\right)

Both of these expressions are dominated by
\mathbb{P}(R_{1}\geq\varepsilon)
. Indeed,

\mathbb{P}\!\left(\sup_{\tau\in[s,s^{\prime}]}\left[\sup_{[s,\tau]}X-X_{\tau}\right]\geq\varepsilon\right)\leq\mathbb{P}\!\left(\sup_{s\in[0,1]}\left[\sup_{[0,s]}X-X_{s}\right]\geq\varepsilon\right)

(2.35)

and the supremum on the right hand side is dominated by
R_{1}
. Thus,

\mathbb{P}\!\left(\sup_{\tau\in[s,s^{\prime}]}\left[\sup_{[s,\tau]}X-X_{\tau}\right]\geq\varepsilon\right)\leq\mathbb{P}(R_{1}\geq\varepsilon)\;.

(2.36)

Integrating the expression as a nested integral of
\mathbb{P}(N^{\varepsilon}\geq k)
, the variable
s_{k-1}
between
t_{k-1}
and
1

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(N^{\varepsilon}\geq k)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma_{2k-3}}\mathbb{P}(T_{1}^{\varepsilon}=t_{1})\mathbb{P}(S_{1}=s_{1}\,|\,T_{1}=t_{1})\cdots\mathbb{P}(t_{k-1}\leq S_{k-1}\leq 1\,|\,T_{k-1}=t_{k-1})\;dt_{k-1}\prod_{i=1}^{k-2}ds_{i}dt_{i}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}(R\geq\varepsilon)\int_{\Sigma_{2k-3}}\mathbb{P}(T_{1}^{\varepsilon}=t_{1})\mathbb{P}(S_{1}=s_{1}\,|\,T_{1}=t_{1})\cdots\mathbb{P}(T_{k-1}=t_{k-1}\,|\,S_{k-2}=s_{k-2})dt_{k-1}\prod_{i=1}^{k-2}ds_{i}dt_{i}

Carrying out the subsequent
2k-3
integrations and by repeated use of the inequality given in equation
2.36
, we obtain the result

\mathbb{P}(N^{\varepsilon}\geq k)\leq\mathbb{P}(R\geq\varepsilon)^{2k-2}\;.

(2.37)

By the hypothesis of the theorem, for all
\varepsilon\geq\varepsilon^{*}
,
\mathbb{P}(R\geq\varepsilon)<1
so the above condition guarantees the summability (and absolute and uniform convergence) of the series
\mathbb{E}\!\left[e^{\lambda N^{\varepsilon}}\right]
on the half plane
\Real(\lambda)>2\log(\mathbb{P}(R_{1}\geq\varepsilon))
. Moreover the same summability condition holds for
N^{x,x+\varepsilon}
, since the latter is dominated by
N^{\varepsilon}
.
∎

2.2
A priori
estimates on the asymptotic behaviour of small and large bars

If
f
is a continuous function, the asymptotic behaviour of
N^{\varepsilon}
is closely related to the regularity of
f
. For functions
f:[0,t]\to\mathbb{R}
, the correct notion of regularity to look at is the
p
-variation, of which we recall the definition.

Definition 2.14
.

Let
f:[0,t]\to\mathbb{R}
be a function. The
(true)
p
-variation of
f
is defined as

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{p\text{-var}}:=\sup_{\mathcal{P}}\left[\sum_{t_{k}\in\mathcal{P}}\left\lvert f(t_{k})-f(t_{k-1})\right\rvert^{p}\right]^{1/p}

(2.38)

where the supremum ranges over all finite partitions of
[0,t]
.

The
p
-variation can be used to infer something about the asymptotic behaviour of
N^{\varepsilon}_{f}
, as shown by the following theorem.

Theorem 2.15

(Picard, §3
[
23
]
)
.

Given a continuous function
f:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
,

\mathcal{V}(f)=\updim T_{f}=\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\log N^{\varepsilon}}{\log(1/\varepsilon)}\vee 1

(2.39)

where
\updim
denotes the upper-box dimension,
a\vee b=\max\{a,b\}
,

\mathcal{V}(f):=\inf\{p\geq 1\,|\,\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{p-\text{var}}<\infty\}\,.

(2.40)

Remark 2.16
.

A general version of this theorem, applicable on more general metric spaces exists
[
21
]
.

Corollary 2.17
.

For any deterministic function
f:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
and for every
\delta>0
,

N^{\varepsilon}_{f}=O(\varepsilon^{-\mathcal{V}(f)-\delta})\quad\text{as }\varepsilon\to 0\,.

(2.41)

Having characterized the behavious of
N^{\varepsilon}
for small
\varepsilon
, we can characterize the behaviour of large bars (in expectation) using theorem
2.13
.

Corollary 2.18
.

Let
X
be a non-constant stochastic process on
[0,t]
defined on the filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
allowing an almost surely continuous modification and satisfying the strong Markov property. Suppose also that there exists some
\varepsilon^{*}
such that for all
\varepsilon>\varepsilon^{*}
,
\mathbb{P}(R_{t}\geq\varepsilon^{*})<1
, then,

\mathbb{E}\!\left[N^{\varepsilon}\right]\sim\mathbb{P}(R_{t}\geq\varepsilon)\quad\text{as }\varepsilon\to\infty\,.

(2.42)

Proof.

Note
p_{\varepsilon}=\mathbb{P}(R_{t}\geq\varepsilon)
. From the theorem, we deduce,

p_{\varepsilon}\leq\mathbb{E}\!\left[N^{\varepsilon}\right]\leq p_{\varepsilon}+\frac{p_{\varepsilon}^{2}}{1-p_{\varepsilon}^{2}}\quad\implies\quad\mathbb{E}\!\left[N^{\varepsilon}\right]\sim p_{\varepsilon}\text{ as }\varepsilon\to\infty\,.

(2.43)

∎

3
Continuous semimartingales, local times and asymptotic behaviour of barcodes

In the previous section, we quantified the asymptotics of
N^{x,x+\varepsilon}
an
N^{\varepsilon}
solely based on the regularity of the functions considered. For stochastic processes on
[0,t]
, we can refine this analysis by focusing on (continuous) semimartingales. Semimartingales constitute the largest class of processes with respect to which the Itô and Stratonovich integrals can be defined. In other words, they are a class of processes rich enough to be worthy of particular attention. For a comprehensive introduction to these objects and the probabilistic concepts included in this section, we kindly refer the reader to classical references on stochastic calculus
[
16
,
24
]
.

Definition 3.1

(Local martingales and semimartingales)
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
be a filtered probability space and let
\mathcal{F}_{*}:=(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be the filtration of
\mathcal{F}
. An
\mathcal{F}_{*}
-adapted process
M:[0,\infty]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
is an
\mathcal{F}_{*}
-local martingale
if there exists a sequence of stopping times
\tau_{k}
such that

1.

The
\tau_{k}
are a.s. increasing,
i.e.

\mathbb{P}(\tau_{k}<\tau_{k+1})=1
;

2.

The
\tau_{k}
are a.s. divergent,
i.e.

\mathbb{P}(\lim_{k\to\infty}\tau_{k}=\infty)=1
;

3.

The process
M_{t\wedge\tau_{k}}
is an
\mathcal{F}_{*}
-martingale,
i.e.
for all
s<t
,

\mathbb{E}\!\left[M_{t\wedge\tau_{k}}\,|\,\mathcal{F}_{s}\right]=M_{s\wedge\tau_{k}}\,.

(3.44)

A process
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a
continuous semimartingale
if it can be written in the form

X_{t}=M_{t}+A_{t}

(3.45)

where
A_{t}
is a process of finite variation and
M_{t}
is a continuous local martingale.

Throughout this section, we will use two key concepts stemming from the theory of stochastic integration: the local time of a (continuous) semimartingale and the quadratic variation. The latter is the easiest to define, given our introduction of the
true
quadratic variation in the previous section.

3.1
Quadratic variation and
a priori
bounds

Definition 3.2

(Quadratic variation, Theorem 4.9
[
16
]
)
.

Suppose that
(X_{t})_{t}
is a
\mathbb{R}
-valued stochastic process indexed by
\mathbb{R}_{+}
. The
quadratic variation of
X
is the process defined as

[X]_{t}:=\lim_{\left\lVert\mathcal{P}\right\rVert\to 0}\sum_{k=1}^{n}(X_{t_{k}}-X_{t_{k-1}})^{2}\,,

(3.46)

where
\mathcal{P}
ranges over the set of finite partitions of the interval
[0,t]
and
\left\lVert\mathcal{P}\right\rVert
the mesh of the partition
\mathcal{P}
. If it exists, the limit is taken in the sense of convergence in probability. Whenever
X
is a continuous semimartingale, the quadratic variation always exists.

Remark 3.3
.

This definition is strictly weaker than the
true

2
-variation, which is defined as a supremum over the set of all finite partitions. In fact, for Brownian motion
B
, it is possible to show that
[B]_{t}=t
, but on the interval
[0,t]
,
\left\lVert B\right\rVert_{2-\text{var}}=\infty
almost surely.

Understanding the quadratic variation is in some sense enough to understand local martingales, as the following theorem shows.

Theorem 3.4

(Dambis-Dubins-Schwarz)
.

Let
M
be a continuous local martingale vanishing at
0
such that
[M]_{\infty}=\infty
, then there exists a Brownian motion
B
such that, a.s. for all
t\geq 0
,

M_{t}=B_{[M]_{t}}\,.

(3.47)

This theorem allows us to give
a priori
bounds on the small bar asymptotics of the barcode of a semimartingale. Indeed, since Brownian motion is a.s.
(\frac{1}{2}-\delta)
-Hölder continuous for every
\delta>0
, the
p
-variation of any semimartingale is almost surely finite as soon as
p>2
(since
p
-variation does not depend on parametrization). In particular we expect

N_{X}^{\varepsilon}=O(\varepsilon^{-2-\delta})\quad\text{as }\varepsilon\to 0\,.

(3.48)

for every
\delta>0
, by virtue of Picard’s theorem. We may further refine this result by introducing the local time.

3.2
The local time and sharp asymptotics

The local time of a continuous semimartingale on an interval
[0,t]
can be informally understood as the “time spent” by the process
X
around a level
x\in\mathbb{R}
, in an equation

L^{x}_{X}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\delta(x-X_{s})\;d[X]_{s}\,.

(3.49)

The above equation is informal and ill-defined, but gives an intuitive insight on what the local time represents and how it behaves. Formally, we define the local time as follows.

Definition/Proposition 3.5

(Proposition 9.2
[
16
]
)
.

Let
X
be a continuous semimartingale and
x\in\mathbb{R}
. There exists an increasing process
(L_{X}^{x}(t))_{t\geq 0}
such that the three following identities hold

\displaystyle\left\lvert X_{t}-x\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\left\lvert X_{0}-x\right\rvert+\int_{0}^{t}\sgn(X_{s}-x)\;dX_{s}+L^{x}_{X}(t)

\displaystyle(X_{t}-x)_{+}

\displaystyle=(X_{0}-x)_{+}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{1}_{X_{s}>x}\;dX_{s}+\frac{1}{2}L_{X}^{x}(t)

\displaystyle(X_{t}-x)_{-}

\displaystyle=(X_{0}-x)_{-}-\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{1}_{X_{s}\leq x}\;dX_{s}+\frac{1}{2}L_{X}^{x}(t)

The increasing process
(L^{x}_{X}(t))_{t\geq 0}
is called the
local time of
X
at level
x
. Furthermore, for every stopping time
T
, the local time at
x
of the stopped process
X
at
T
,
L_{X^{T}}^{x}(t)=L_{X}^{x}(t\wedge T)
.

The local time of a process is useful, as it allows us to exchange time and space in integrations. A first useful result in this direction, which we will later use, is the following proposition.

Proposition 3.6

(Density occupation formula, Corollary 9.7
[
16
]
)
.

Almost surely, for every non-negative, measurable function
\phi
on
\mathbb{R}
,

\int_{0}^{t}\phi(X_{s})\;d[X]_{s}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(a)L^{a}_{X}(t)\;da\,.

(3.50)

This result can be informally derived using the informal definition of the local time we gave earlier. For a rigorous proof, we refer the reader to the cited reference. From the informal description we gave about the local time as the time spent by the process around a level
x
, we could expect the local time to be related to
N^{x,x+\varepsilon}_{X}
for small
\varepsilon
. This turns out to be a well-known fact.

Proposition 3.7

(Approximation of the local time by downcrossings, §VI, Theorem 1.10
[
24
]
)
.

For every
t\geq 0
and
s\geq 1
, if
X=M+A
is a continuous semimartingale on
[0,t]
and suppose that for
s\geq 1

\mathbb{E}\!\left[[M]_{t}^{s/2}+\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left\lvert dA\right\rvert_{s}\right)^{s}\right]<\infty\,.

(3.51)

Then,

\varepsilon D_{X}^{x,x+\varepsilon}\xrightarrow[\varepsilon\to 0]{L^{s}}\frac{1}{2}L_{X}^{x}(t)\,.

(3.52)

Moreover, in
L^{s}(\Omega)
,

D_{X}^{x,x+\varepsilon}\sim\frac{L^{x}_{X}(t))}{2\varepsilon}+O(1)\quad\text{as }\varepsilon\to 0\,.

(3.53)

Remark 3.8
.

An analogous statement can be proven for upcrossings.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
X=M+A
be a continuous semimartingale on
[0,t]
and suppose that for
s\geq 1

\mathbb{E}\!\left[[M]_{t}^{s/2}+\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left\lvert dA\right\rvert_{s}\right)^{s}\right]<\infty\,.

(3.54)

Then in
L^{s}(\Omega)
,

\displaystyle N_{X}^{x,x+\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{L^{x}_{X}(t)}{2\varepsilon}+O(1)\quad\text{as }\varepsilon\to 0\,

\displaystyle N_{X}^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{[X]_{t}}{2\varepsilon^{2}}+O(\varepsilon^{-1})\,\quad\text{as }\varepsilon\to 0\,.

Proof.

Since
0\leq N^{x,x+\varepsilon}_{X}-D_{X}^{x,x+\varepsilon}\leq 1
, the statement of proposition
3.7
is applicable to
N^{x,x+\varepsilon}
yielding the first result. It follows that in
L^{s}(\Omega)
,

\left\lvert 2\varepsilon\lambda(T_{X}^{\varepsilon})-\int_{\mathbb{R}}L^{x}_{X}(t)\;dx\right\rvert=O(\varepsilon)\,.

(3.55)

Note that while the integral carries over
\mathbb{R}
, its support is contained within the compact
[\inf X,\sup X]
. We now use the density occupation formula for the local time where
\phi=1
, so

2\varepsilon\lambda(T_{X}^{\varepsilon})=[X]_{t}+O(\varepsilon)\quad\text{ as }\varepsilon\to 0\text{ in }L^{s}(\Omega)

(3.56)

We now use the fact that

\lambda(T_{X}^{\varepsilon})=\int_{\varepsilon}^{\infty}N^{a}\;da\,.

(3.57)

By monotonicity of
N^{\varepsilon}
, for every
\delta>0
small enough, almost surely,

N^{\varepsilon(1+\delta)}\leq\frac{\lambda(T_{X}^{\varepsilon})-\lambda(T_{X}^{\varepsilon(1+\delta)})}{\delta\varepsilon}\leq\frac{1}{\delta\varepsilon}\int_{\varepsilon}^{\varepsilon(1+\delta)}N^{a}\;da\leq N^{\varepsilon}\,.

(3.58)

It follows that in
L^{s}(\Omega)
, for every
\delta>0
small enough,

\frac{[X]_{t}}{1+\delta}+O(\varepsilon)\leq 2\varepsilon^{2}N^{\varepsilon}\leq\frac{[X]_{t}}{1-\delta}+O(\varepsilon)\quad\text{ as }\varepsilon\to 0\,.

(3.59)

Taking
\delta\to 0
, we obtain the desired result.
∎

4
Limiting processes

An interesting point of view relative to these stability results and the results of this paper is to consider these irregular processes as almost sure
C^{0}
-limits of smooth processes, which have traditionally been more difficult to study. In this way, we can make affirmations about the barcodes of smooth processes up to some (small) error. This way of thinking is inspired by the study of trees, ultralimits and asymptotic cones in geometric group theory
[
26
]
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
(M,d)
be a compact Polish metric space and let
X
be an almost surely continuous stochastic process on
M
, defined on a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
. Let
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be any sequence of continuous stochastic processes defined on the same probability space and suppose

\delta_{n}:=\left\lVert X-X_{n}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega,L^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R}))}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0\,.

(4.60)

If for all
\varepsilon>0
,
\mathbb{E}\!\left[N^{\varepsilon}_{X}\right]<\infty
and is continuous in
\varepsilon
, then for any
\varepsilon\geq 2\delta_{n}
,

N_{X_{n}}^{\varepsilon}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{L^{1}}N_{X}^{\varepsilon}\,,

Morever,

\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\lvert N_{X}^{\varepsilon}-N_{X_{n}}^{\varepsilon}\right\rvert\right]\leq\omega_{\varepsilon}(\delta_{n})\,,

(4.61)

where
\omega_{\varepsilon}(\delta):=\mathbb{E}\!\left[N_{X}^{\varepsilon-\delta}-N_{X}^{\varepsilon+\delta}\right]
. With analogous hypotheses, the same statement holds for
N^{x,x+\varepsilon}
.

Proof.

The
L^{\infty}
-stability of barcodes with respect to the
L^{\infty}
-distance tells us that
\delta_{n}
controls the bottleneck distance between the two barcodes
[
20
,
6
]
. This implies that there exists a
\delta_{n}
-matching between the barcodes of
X_{n}
and that of
X
, therefore:

•

If
\alpha\in\mathcal{B}(X)
has length
\left\lvert\alpha\right\rvert\geq 2\delta_{n}
, then
\exists!\,\beta\in\mathcal{B}(X_{n})
such that
(\alpha,\beta)
are matched and the difference
\left\lvert\left\lvert\alpha\right\rvert-\left\lvert\beta\right\rvert\right\rvert\leq 2\delta_{n}
;

•

If
\beta\in\mathcal{B}(X_{n})
has length
\left\lvert\beta\right\rvert\geq 2\delta_{n}
, then
\exists!\,\alpha\in\mathcal{B}(X)
such that
(\alpha,\beta)
are matched and the difference
\left\lvert\left\lvert\alpha\right\rvert-\left\lvert\beta\right\rvert\right\rvert\leq 2\delta_{n}
;

•

If
\alpha\in\mathcal{B}(X)
or
\beta\in\mathcal{B}(X_{n})
in unmatched, then they have length
\leq 2\delta_{n}
.

It follows that for
\varepsilon\geq 2\delta_{n}
we have inequalities

\displaystyle N_{X_{n}}^{\varepsilon+2\delta_{n}}

\displaystyle\leq N^{\varepsilon}_{X}

\displaystyle N_{X}^{\varepsilon+2\delta_{n}}

\displaystyle\leq N^{\varepsilon}_{X_{n}}\,,

from which we obtain bounds on
N_{X_{n}}^{\varepsilon}

N^{\varepsilon+2\delta_{n}}_{X}\leq N^{\varepsilon}_{X_{n}}\leq N^{\varepsilon-2\delta_{n}}_{X}\,.

(4.62)

These inequalities imply

\left\lvert N_{X_{n}}^{\varepsilon}-N_{X}^{\varepsilon}\right\rvert\leq\left\lvert N_{X}^{\varepsilon+2\delta_{n}}-N_{X}^{\varepsilon}\right\rvert\vee\left\lvert N_{X}^{\varepsilon-2\delta_{n}}-N_{X}^{\varepsilon}\right\rvert

Taking expectations of both sides, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\lvert N_{X_{n}}^{\varepsilon}-N_{X}^{\varepsilon}\right\rvert\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\lvert N_{X}^{\varepsilon+2\delta_{n}}-N_{X}^{\varepsilon}\right\rvert\vee\left\lvert N_{X}^{\varepsilon-2\delta_{n}}-N_{X}^{\varepsilon}\right\rvert\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\lvert N_{X}^{\varepsilon+2\delta_{n}}-N_{X}^{\varepsilon}\right\rvert\right]+\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\lvert N_{X}^{\varepsilon-2\delta_{n}}-N_{X}^{\varepsilon}\right\rvert\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\!\left[N_{X}^{\varepsilon}-N_{X}^{\varepsilon+2\delta_{n}}\right]+\mathbb{E}\!\left[N_{X}^{\varepsilon-2\delta_{n}}-N_{X}^{\varepsilon}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\omega_{\varepsilon}(2\delta_{n})

by monotonicity of
N_{X}^{\varepsilon}
. The right hand side of the inequality tends to
0
as
n\to\infty
by continuity of
\mathbb{E}\!\left[N^{\varepsilon}_{X}\right]
, so
N_{X_{n}}^{\varepsilon}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{L^{1}}N_{X}^{\varepsilon}
.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

The condition of uniform convergence over
\Omega
can be quite restrictive, but covers some cases of distributions with compact supports in
C^{0}(X,\mathbb{R})
. Moreover, it covers the case of processes derived from random point clouds
P
(the filtration function is given by
d(-,P)
) stemming from a distribution with compact support on any Polish metric space
M
.

We can adapt the proof of the above proposition to a setting relaxing the
L^{\infty}(\Omega,L^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R}))
convergence condition.

Proposition 4.3
.

Keeping the same notation as before, suppose there exists a
p\geq 1
such that

\delta_{n}:=\left\lVert X-X_{n}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega,L^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R}))}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0\,.

(4.63)

Then, with probability
\geq 1-\frac{1}{a^{p}}
, for every
\varepsilon\geq 2a\delta_{n}
,

\left\lvert N^{\varepsilon}_{X_{n}}-N^{\varepsilon}_{X}\right\rvert\leq N_{X}^{\varepsilon-2a\delta_{n}}-N_{X}^{\varepsilon+2a\delta_{n}}\,.

(4.64)

If
\mathbb{E}\!\left[N_{X}^{\varepsilon}\right]
is continuous in
\varepsilon
,

\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\lvert N_{X_{n}}^{\varepsilon}-N_{X}^{\varepsilon}\right\rvert\,\Big|\,\left\lVert X-X_{n}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq a\delta_{n}\right]\leq\omega_{\varepsilon}(2a\delta_{n})\,.

(4.65)

Moreover,

\mathbb{P}\!\left(\left\lvert N_{X_{n}}^{\varepsilon}-N_{X}^{\varepsilon}\right\rvert\geq k\right)\leq\frac{\omega_{\varepsilon}(2a\delta_{n})}{k}+\frac{1}{a^{p}}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{P}\!\left(\left\lvert N_{X_{n}}^{\varepsilon}-N_{X}^{\varepsilon}\right\rvert\geq k\;,\;\left\lVert X-X_{n}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq a\delta_{n}\right)\leq\frac{\omega_{\varepsilon}(2a\delta_{n})}{k}\,.

(4.66)

Proof.

The proof goes as the previous one for a few minor exceptions. To obtain the probable bound on
\left\lVert X-X_{n}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})}
, we apply the Markov inequality. The rest of the proof follows by noticing that the conditional expectation is a contractive projection on
L^{1}
and that

\mathbb{P}(A)=\mathbb{P}(A\cap B)+\mathbb{P}(A\cap B^{c})\leq\mathbb{P}(A\cap B)+\mathbb{P}(B^{c})\,.

(4.67)

∎

Remark 4.4
.

By an application of the Borel-Cantelli lemma, if
X_{n}
tends to
X
in
L^{p}(\Omega,L^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R}))
for
p>1
at a rate
O(r(n))
(where
r
is a function tending to
0
at infinity), then almost surely
X_{n}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{L^{\infty}}X
at a rate
O(r(n))
as well.

Applications

5.1
Brownian motion and local martingales with deterministic strictly increasing quadratic variation

For Brownian motion, it is possible to compute our quantities of interest exactly.

Theorem 5.1

(Perez, Proposition 4.4
[
22
]
)
.

For Brownian motion
B
on
[0,t]
,
\mathbb{E}\!\left[N^{\varepsilon}_{B}\right]
admits the following series representations which converge well for large and small
\varepsilon
respectively

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\!\left[N^{\varepsilon}_{B}\right]

\displaystyle=4\sum_{k\geq 1}(2k-1)\erfc\!\left(\frac{(2k-1)\varepsilon}{\sqrt{2t}}\right)-k\,\erfc\!\left(\frac{2k\varepsilon}{\sqrt{2t}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{t}{2\varepsilon^{2}}+\frac{2}{3}+2\sum_{k\geq 1}(2(-1)^{k}-1)\frac{e^{-\pi^{2}k^{2}t/2\varepsilon^{2}}t}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left[1+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{\pi^{2}k^{2}t}\right]\,.

Figure 4
:
The expected number of bars of length
\geq\varepsilon
,
\mathbb{E}\!\left[N^{\varepsilon}_{B}\right]
, as a function of
\varepsilon
.

Remark 5.2
.

In particular,
\mathbb{E}\!\left[N^{\varepsilon}_{B}\right]
is analytic in
\varepsilon
on an open wedge around the positive real axis in the complex plane, thereby confirming and extending Divol and Chazal’s results.

Proof.

We start by writing

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\!\left[N_{B}^{\varepsilon}\right]=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(N^{\varepsilon}\geq k)=\mathbb{P}(R_{t}\geq\varepsilon)+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(S_{k-1}^{\varepsilon}\leq t)

Using the scale invariance of Brownian motion,

\mathbb{E}\!\left[N_{B}^{\varepsilon}\right]=\mathbb{P}(R_{1}\geq t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\varepsilon)+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(S_{k-1}^{1}\leq t/\varepsilon^{2})

(5.68)

We now notice that the stopping times

S_{k-1}^{\varepsilon}=\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}(S_{i}^{\varepsilon}-T_{i}^{\varepsilon})+(T_{i}^{\varepsilon}-S_{i-1}^{\varepsilon})

(5.69)

and that the increments
(S_{i}^{\varepsilon}-T_{i}^{\varepsilon})
and
(T_{i}^{\varepsilon}-S_{i-1}^{\varepsilon})
are independent and identically distributed. Moreover, in distribution,

\sup_{[0,t]}B-B_{t}=\left\lvert B_{t}\right\rvert\,,

(5.70)

so that the
(S_{i}^{\varepsilon}-T_{i}^{\varepsilon})
and
(T_{i}^{\varepsilon}-S_{i-1}^{\varepsilon})
are distributed like the first hitting time of
\varepsilon
by
\left\lvert B_{t}\right\rvert
. It is a classical result
[
4
, p.641]
that this hitting time
H^{\varepsilon}
satisfies

\mathbb{E}\!\left[e^{\lambda H^{\varepsilon}}\right]=\sech(\varepsilon\sqrt{2\lambda})\,.

(5.71)

Similarly, it is also well-known that the range of Brownian motion satisfies

{\mathcal{L}}_{t}(\mathbb{P}(R_{t}\geq\varepsilon))(\lambda)=\frac{\sech^{2}(\varepsilon\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{2}})}{\lambda}

(5.72)

We now take the Laplace transform with respect to the time variable of
\mathbb{E}\!\left[N_{B}^{\varepsilon}\right]

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}_{t}(\mathbb{E}\!\left[N_{B}^{\varepsilon}\right])(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sech^{2}(\varepsilon\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{2}})}{\lambda}+\frac{1}{\lambda}\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\!\left[e^{-\lambda\varepsilon^{2}S^{1}_{k-1}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{\sech^{2}(\varepsilon\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{2}})}{\lambda}+\frac{1}{\lambda}\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\!\left[e^{-\lambda\varepsilon^{2}H^{1}}\right]^{2(k-1)}\,,

where the last equality holds by virtue of i.i.d. character of the increments
(S_{i}^{\varepsilon}-T_{i}^{\varepsilon})
and
(T_{i}^{\varepsilon}-S_{i-1}^{\varepsilon})
. Replacing the value of
\mathbb{E}\!\left[e^{\lambda H^{\varepsilon}}\right]
,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}_{t}(\mathbb{E}\!\left[N_{B}^{\varepsilon}\right])(\lambda)=\frac{\sech^{2}(\varepsilon\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{2}})+\csch^{2}(\varepsilon\sqrt{2\lambda})}{\lambda}

The result is obtained by taking the inverse Laplace transform.
∎

Persistent homology is invariant under reparametrization. In particular if
\lambda:[0,t]\to[0,\lambda(t)]
is an increasing bijection,
N_{f\circ\lambda}^{\varepsilon}
as calculated on
[0,\lambda(t)]
is exactly equal to
N_{f}^{\varepsilon}
as calculated on
[0,t]
. Invoking the Dambis-Dubins-Schwarz theorem (theorem
3.4
), for every continuous local martingale
M
(such that
M_{0}=0
) with deterministic and strictly increasing quadratic variation
[M]_{t}
, almost surely,

N_{M}^{\varepsilon}[0,t]=N_{B}^{\varepsilon}[0,[M]_{t}]\,.

(5.73)

Theorem 5.3
.

For any continuous local martingale
M
on
[0,t]
having deterministic and strictly increasing quadratic variation
[M]_{t}
such that
[M]_{\infty}=\infty
, the same formula holds by replacing
t
by
[M]_{t}
in the result of theorem
5.1
.

We can perform a similar calculation for
N^{x,x+\varepsilon}
when
x>0
,

Proposition 5.4

(Perez, Proposition 4.7
[
22
]
)
.

For Brownian motion on
[0,t]
and
x>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\!\left[N^{x,x+\varepsilon}_{B}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\text{erfc}\left(\frac{x+(2k-1)\varepsilon}{\sqrt{2t}}\right)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{t}\varphi(x,s)\;ds+\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{4(-2)^{k}\!\left(2^{2k+1}-1\right)\zeta(2k+2)}{\pi^{2k+2}}\left[\frac{\partial^{k}}{\partial t^{k}}\varphi(x,t)\right]\varepsilon^{2k+1}\;\text{as }\varepsilon\to 0\,,

where
\varphi(x,t)
is the density of a centered Gaussian random variable of variance
t
and
\zeta
denotes the Riemann zeta function.

Remark 5.5
.

In the appropriate limits, we retrieve the results established for semimartingales. By virtue of the Dambis-Dubins-Schwarz theorem and by the invariance under reparametrization of persistent homology, we may also immediately extend the results and formulæ of
[
22
]
to local martingales with deterministic and strictly increasing quadratic variation by replacing
t\mapsto[M]_{t}
.

5.2
Itô processes

Definition 5.6
.

An
Itô process
is the solution to a stochastic differential equation of the form

dX_{t}=\mu_{t}\,dt+\sigma_{t}\,dB_{t}

(5.74)

where
\mu_{t}
and
\sigma_{t}
are adapted processes.

Every Itô process of this form has the strong Markov property and moreover, it is a semimartingale. We deduce that

N_{X}^{\varepsilon}\sim\frac{[X]_{t}}{2\varepsilon^{2}}+O(\varepsilon^{-1})\text{ as }\varepsilon\to 0\,,

(5.75)

and an analogous expression for
N^{x,x+\varepsilon}
in terms of the local time of
X
. In expectation, we may always say something about
N^{x,x+\varepsilon}_{X}
by virtue of the following proposition.

Proposition 5.7
.

Let
X
be an Itô process with deterministic quadratic variation, then

\mathbb{E}\!\left[L_{X}^{x}(t)\right]=\int_{0}^{t}\varphi_{X}(x,s)\;d[X]_{s}\,,

(5.76)

where
\varphi_{X}(-,s)
is the density function of the random variable
X_{s}
(which itself is a solution of the Fokker-Planck PDE associated to the SDE).

Proof.

We take the expectation of both sides of the occupation density formula with
\phi(a)=e^{-i\lambda a}
.

\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\!\left[e^{-i\lambda X_{s}}\right]d[X]_{s}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-i\lambda a}\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[L^{a}_{X}(t)\right]\;da

(5.77)

The result follows from taking the inverse Fourier transform of both sides.
∎

Proposition 5.8

(Asymptotics of barcodes of Itô processes)
.

Let
X
be an Itô process with deterministic quadratic variation on
[0,t]
, then,

\mathbb{E}\!\left[N^{x,x+\varepsilon}\right]\sim\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{t}\varphi_{X}(x,s)\;d[X]_{s}+O(1)\quad\text{as }\varepsilon\to 0\,.

(5.78)

where
\varphi_{X}(-,s)
is the density function of the random variable
X_{s}
.

∎

As a Markov processes, Itô processes also satisfy

\mathbb{E}\!\left[N_{X}^{\varepsilon}\right]\sim\mathbb{P}(R_{t}\geq\varepsilon)\quad\text{as }\varepsilon\to\infty\,.

(5.79)

Finally, when
\mu_{t}=0
and
\sigma_{t}>0
for all
t>0
and is deterministic, an Itô process is a local martingale with quadratic variation

[X]_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}\sigma_{s}^{2}\;ds\,,

(5.80)

and, in particular, its barcode is then known completely.

5.3
Limiting processes

The Karhunen-Loève theorem
[
14
,
17
]
shows that many well-known
C^{0}
-processes can be seen as limits of smooth processes and that the logic of studying smooth objects with their more irregular
C^{0}
limits is a logic which can also be applicable in higher dimensions. Brownian motion itself can be seen as such a limit
[
16
,
18
]
. Indeed, Paul Lévy showed that if
(\xi_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of i.i.d. standard normal variables, the series

\xi_{0}t+\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\pi}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\xi_{k}\frac{\sin(\pi kt)}{k}

(5.81)

almost surely uniformly converges to the standard Brownian motion on
[0,1]
. Noting

S_{n}B:=\xi_{0}t+\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\pi}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\xi_{k}\frac{\sin(\pi kt)}{k}\,,

(5.82)

it can be shown (for instance using
[
13
, Chapter 15, Theorem 4]
) that for
p\geq 1
there exists a finite constant
C_{p}
such that

\left\lVert B-S_{n}B\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega,L^{\infty}([0,1],\mathbb{R}))}\leq C_{p}n^{-\frac{1}{2}}\log^{\frac{1}{2}}(n)\,.

(5.83)

Applying the results of proposition
4.3
and optimizing in
a
, we have

\mathbb{P}\!\left(\left\lvert N_{S_{n}B}^{\varepsilon}-N_{B}^{\varepsilon}\right\rvert\geq k\right)=O\!\left(\left[\frac{C_{p}\,n^{-\frac{1}{2}}\log^{\frac{1}{2}}(n)}{p\,\varepsilon^{3}k}\right]^{\frac{p}{p+1}}\right)\quad\text{as }\varepsilon\to 0.

(5.84)

In particular, we know that through this approximation yields a curve on the
\log
-chart which doesn’t stray far away from the Brownian motion’s for
\varepsilon\gtrsim(n^{-1}\log(n))^{\frac{1}{2}}
.
Brownian motion can also be approximated by random walks. The Komlós–Major–Tusnády (KMT) theorem provides a sharp estimate of the rate of convergence of these empirical processes to the Brownian bridge (which we will denote
W_{t}
).

Definition 5.9
.

Let
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
be a sequence of (reduced, centered) i.i.d random variables. The
empirical process
defined by
X
is the process

\alpha^{X}_{n}(t):=\left[\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbf{1}_{]-\infty,t]}(X_{k})\right]-t\,.

(5.85)

Theorem 5.10

(KMT Theorem,
[
15
]
)
.

Let
(U_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
be an i.i.d. sequence of uniform random variables on
[0,1]
. Then, there exists a Brownian bridge
(W_{t})_{1\geq t\geq 0}
such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and all
x>0

\mathbb{P}\!\left(\left\lVert\alpha_{n}^{U}(t)-W_{t}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}>n^{-\frac{1}{2}}(C\log(n)+x)\right)\leq Le^{-\lambda x}

(5.86)

for some universal positive constants
C
,
L
and
\lambda
which are explicitly known
[
5
]
.

Since
W
is a semimartingale with quadratic variation
[W]_{t}=t
on
[0,1]
, as it can be obtained as the solution to the SDE

dW_{t}=\frac{-W_{t}}{1-t}\;dt+dB_{t}\,.

(5.87)

We deduce that
N^{\varepsilon}_{W}\sim\frac{1}{2\varepsilon^{2}}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. The KMT theorem and the same reasoning behind proposition
4.3
imply

\mathbb{P}\!\left(\left\lvert N_{\alpha_{n}^{U}}^{\varepsilon}-N_{W}^{\varepsilon}\right\rvert\geq k\right)=O\left(\frac{n^{-\frac{1}{2}}\log(n)}{\varepsilon^{3}k}\right)\quad\text{as }\varepsilon\to 0\,.

(5.88)

In particular, this approximation yields the curve
N^{\varepsilon}_{\alpha_{n}^{U}}
on the
\log
-chart which doesn’t stray far away from
N^{\varepsilon}_{W}
’s for
\varepsilon\gtrsim n^{-\frac{1}{2}}\log(n)
.

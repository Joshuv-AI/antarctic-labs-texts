Regularized Newton Methods for Monotone Variational Inequalities with Hölder Continuous Jacobians
Chengchang Liu, Luo Luo

Abstract
This paper considers the problems of solving monotone variational inequalities with Hölder continuous Jacobians. By employing the knowledge of Hölder parameter ν \nu , we propose the ν \nu -regularized extra-Newton method within at most 𝒪 ( ϵ − 2 / ( 2 + ν ) ) {\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon^{-2/(2+\nu)}) iterations to obtain an ϵ \epsilon -accurate solution. In the case of ν \nu is unknown, we propose the universal regularized extra-Newton method within 𝒪 ( ϵ − 4 / ( 3 ( 1 + ν ) ) ) {\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon^{-4/(3(1+\nu))}) iteration complexity.

Introduction

We study the variational inequality problem:

\displaystyle\text{find}~~{\bf z}^{*}\in{\mathcal{Z}}~~~\text{such that}~~\langle F({\bf z}^{*}),{\bf z}-{\bf z}^{*}\rangle\geq 0,~~\text{for all}~~{\bf z}\in{\mathcal{Z}},

(1)

where
F:{\mathcal{Z}}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is monotone and
{\mathcal{Z}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is a closed and convex set.
We denote the solution set of problem (
1
) as

\displaystyle{\rm{VI}}_{{\mathcal{Z}}}(F({\bf z}))\overset{\text{def}}{=}\{{\bf z}^{*}:{\bf z}^{*}\in{\mathcal{Z}}~\text{such that}~\langle F({\bf z}^{*}),{\bf z}-{\bf z}^{*}\rangle\geq 0~~\text{for all}~~{\bf z}\in{\mathcal{Z}}\}.

Variational inequality problem is widely studied in economics, game theory
[
2
,
40
]
and machine learning applications such as adversarial learning
[
3
]
, GANs
[
13
]
and reinforcement learning
[
11
]
.
It is one of the most universal optimization formulation that contains minimization problem, saddle point problem and fixed point problem.
For more backgrounds and theories of variational inequality,
readers are referred to the literatures of
Facchinei and Pang 2003
and
Beznosikov et al. 2022
.

There are a great number of first-order optimization algorithms for the monotone variational inequality problem (
1
), including extragradient method
[
22
,
39
,
28
,
37
]
, hybrid proximal extragradient method
[

, optimistic gradient descent ascent
[

37

and dual extrapolation
[

.
For Lipschitz continuous operator
F

{\mathcal{O}}\left({\epsilon}^{-1}\right)
number of iterations to find an
\epsilon
-accurate solution
{\bf{z}}^{*}
such that

\displaystyle\max_{{\bf z}\in{\mathcal{Z}}}\langle F({\bf z}),{\bf{z}}^{*}-{\bf z}\rangle\leq\epsilon,

(2)

which matches the lower bound of first-order algorithms
[

]

The second-order optimization algorithms usually enjoy the superior convergence rate than first-order ones.
The ordinary Newton method for variational inequality problem (
1
) iterates with

\displaystyle{\bf z}_{k+1}={\rm{VI}}_{{\mathcal{Z}}}(F({\bf z}_{k})+\nabla F({\bf z}_{k})({\bf z}-{\bf z}_{k})),

which may not converge globally.

Nesterov 2006
,
Huang et al. 2020
proposed the cubic regularized Newton (CRN)
[
34
]
methods, which is based on solving the regularized sub-problem as follows

\displaystyle{\bf z}_{k+1}={\rm{VI}}_{{\mathcal{Z}}}\left(F({\bf z}_{k})+\nabla F({\bf z}_{k})({\bf z}-{\bf z}_{k})+\frac{L_{2}}{2}\|{\bf z}-{\bf z}_{k}\|({\bf z}-{\bf z}_{k})\right),

where
L_{2}\geq 0
is the Lipschitz continuous constant of the Jacobian
\nabla F(\cdot)
.
The CRN method requires
{\mathcal{O}}\left(\epsilon^{-1}\right)
iterations to achieve
\epsilon
-accurate solution.
Recently,
Huang and Zhang 2022
,
Lin et al. 2022
proposed a variant of CRN by introducing the extra-step, leading the iteration scheme

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle{{\bf z}_{k+0.5}={\rm{VI}}_{{\mathcal{Z}}}\left(F({\bf z}_{k})+\nabla F({\bf z}_{k})({\bf z}-{\bf z}_{k})+\frac{L_{2}}{2}\|{\bf z}-{\bf z}_{k}\|({\bf z}-{\bf z}_{k})\right)},\\[9.95863pt]
~~\displaystyle{{\bf z}_{k+1}=\argmin_{{\bf z}\in{\mathcal{Z}}}\langle F({\bf z}_{k+0.5}),{\bf z}-{\bf z}_{k+0.5}\rangle+\frac{L_{2}\|{\bf z}_{k+0.5}-{\bf z}_{k}\|}{4}\|{\bf z}-{\bf z}_{k+0.5}\|^{2}},\end{cases}

(3)

which requires the less iteration complexity of
{\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon^{-2/3})
.

All of the existing algorithms and theories for variational inequality problems are based on the assumption of the Lipschitz continuity for the operator or its derivatives,
while this is not the only possibility for measuring the smoothness.
A natural generalization of Lipschitz continuous assumption is the Hölder continuity.
In convex optimization,
Grapiglia and Nesterov 2017
,
Grapiglia and Nesterov 2019
proposed regularized Newton-type algorithms and establish the corresponding convergence analysis under the assumption of Hölder continuous Hessian.
However, these results cannot be used to variational inequality problems because they depend on the convexity of the objective function.

In this paper, we propose the regularized extra-Newton methods for solving monotone variational inequalities with
\nu
-Hölder continuous Jacobian.
We design the algorithms by integrating adaptively searching the regularization parameter with the extra steps
[

The adaptive algorithms for convex minimization typically rely on tracking the objective function value

However, for variational inequalities, the measure like the right-hand side of (

To address this issue, we establish the criterion on the norm of operator

to search the regularization parameter.
We summarize the main contributions of our paper below:

Preliminaries

We use
\left\|\cdot\right\|
to present spectral norm and Euclidean norm of matrix and vector respectively.
We let
\nabla F({\bf z})
be the Jacobian of
F(\cdot)
at
{\bf z}
.
We further denote

\displaystyle\bar{F}({\bf z}^{\prime};{\bf z})\overset{\text{def}}{=}F({\bf z})+\nabla F({\bf z})({\bf z}^{\prime}-{\bf z})\quad\text{and}\quad\bar{F}_{\nu,H}({\bf z}^{\prime};{\bf z})\overset{\text{def}}{=}F({\bf z})+\nabla F({\bf z})({\bf z}^{\prime}-{\bf z})+H\|{\bf z}^{\prime}-{\bf z}\|^{\nu}({\bf z}^{\prime}-{\bf z}).

We simplify the solution of the variational inequality problem as

\displaystyle{\rm{T}}({\bf z};\nu,H)\overset{\text{def}}{=}{\rm{VI}}_{{\mathcal{Z}}}(\bar{F}_{\nu,H}({\bf z}^{\prime};{\bf z}))

and define

\displaystyle\gamma({\bf z};\nu,H)\overset{\text{def}}{=}H\|{\rm{T}}({\bf z};\nu,H)-{\bf z}\|^{\nu}.

We introduce the potential function

\displaystyle m({\bf z})\overset{\text{def}}{=}\max_{{\bf z}^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{Z}}}\langle F({\bf z}^{\prime}),{\bf z}-{\bf z}^{\prime}\rangle

to characterize the convergence of the algorithm for variational inequality problem
[
18
]
.

We suppose the operator
F
satisfies the following assumptions.

Assumption 2.1
.

We assume the operator
F
is monotone. i.e., for all
{\bf z}
and
{\bf z}^{\prime}
, we have

\displaystyle\langle F({\bf z})-F({\bf z}^{\prime}),{\bf z}-{\bf z}^{\prime}\rangle\geq 0.

(4)

Assumption 2.2
.

We assume the Jacobian of operator
F
is
{\mathcal{H}}_{\nu}
-Hölder continuous, i.e., for all
{\bf z}
and
{\bf z}^{\prime}
, we have

\displaystyle\|\nabla F({\bf z})-\nabla F({\bf z}^{\prime})\|\leq{\mathcal{H}}_{\nu}\|{\bf z}-{\bf z}^{\prime}\|^{\nu},

(5)

for some
\nu\in[0,1]
.

Assumption 2.3
.

We assume there exists some
D>0
such that
\|{\bf z}-{\bf z}^{\prime}\|\leq D
for all
{\bf z}
,
{\bf z}^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{Z}}
.

Remark 2.4
.

The variational inequality problem with monotone operator includes the important optimization formulation such as convex minimization and convex-concave minimax problem
[
4
]
.
If we further suppose
\nu=1
, the Hölder continuity of Jacobian recovers the standard Lipschitz continuous assumption that is well studied in previous work
[
29
,
18
,
25
]
.

The Hölder continuous assumption on Jacobian leads to the following proposition.

Proposition 2.5
.

If
F(\cdot)
satisfies Assumption
2.2
, then it holds that

\displaystyle\left\|F({\bf z}^{\prime})-F({\bf z})-\nabla F({\bf z})({\bf z}^{\prime}-{\bf z})\right\|\leq\frac{{\mathcal{H}}_{\nu}\|{\bf z}-{\bf z}^{\prime}\|^{1+\nu}}{1+\nu},

(6)

for
\nu\in[0,1]
.

Proof.

Following the proof of
Grapiglia and Nesterov 2017
, we have

\displaystyle\|F({\bf z}^{\prime})-F({\bf z})-\nabla F({\bf z})({\bf z}^{\prime}-{\bf z})\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{t=0}^{1}\nabla F({\bf z}+t({\bf z}^{\prime}-{\bf z}))({\bf z}^{\prime}-{\bf z}){\rm d}t-\nabla F({\bf z})({\bf z}^{\prime}-{\bf z})\right\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{t=0}^{1}(\nabla F({\bf z}+t({\bf z}^{\prime}-{\bf z}))-\nabla F({\bf z}))({\bf z}^{\prime}-{\bf z}){\rm d}t\right\|

\displaystyle{\leq}

\displaystyle\int_{t=0}^{1}\left\|\nabla F({\bf z}+t({\bf z}^{\prime}-{\bf z}))-F({\bf z})\right\|^{\nu}\|{\bf z}-{\bf z}^{{}^{\prime}}\|{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:hesconti3}}{\leq}

\displaystyle\int_{t=0}^{1}{\mathcal{H}}_{\nu}\|{\bf z}-{\bf z}^{\prime}\|^{1+\nu}t^{\nu}{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{{\mathcal{H}}_{\nu}}{1+\nu}\|{\bf z}-{\bf z}^{\prime}\|^{1+\nu}.

∎

Proof.

According to the optimal condition finding

, we have

\displaystyle\langle\bar{F}({\bf z}_{k+0.5};{\bf z}_{k})+{\gamma({\bf z}_{k};\nu,2{\mathcal{H}}_{\nu})}({\bf z}_{k+0.5}-{\bf z}_{k}),{\bf z}-{\bf z}_{k+0.5}\rangle\geq 0,

\displaystyle\langle\bar{F}({\bf z}_{k+0.5};{\bf z}_{k}),{\bf z}_{k+0.5}-{\bf z}_{k+1}\rangle\leq-\frac{{\gamma({\bf z}_{k};\nu,2{\mathcal{H}}_{\nu})}}{2}\left(\|{\bf z}_{k+0.5}-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{2}+\|{\bf z}_{k+1}-{\bf z}_{k+0.5}\|^{2}-\|{\bf z}_{k+1}-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{2}\right).

\displaystyle\langle F({\bf z}_{k+0.5})+{\gamma({\bf z}_{k};\nu,2{\mathcal{H}}_{\nu})}({\bf z}_{k+1}-{\bf z}_{k}),{\bf z}-{\bf z}_{k+1}\rangle\geq 0

for all

According to the Proposition
2.5

\displaystyle\frac{\|F({\bf z}_{k+0.5})-\bar{F}({\bf z}_{k+0.5};z_{k})\|^{2}}{{\gamma({\bf z}_{k};\nu,2{\mathcal{H}}_{\nu})}}\leq\frac{\left(H_{\nu}\|{\bf z}_{k+0.5}-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{1+\nu}/(1+\nu)\right)^{2}}{2H_{\nu}\|{\bf z}_{k+0.5}-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{\nu}}=\frac{{\gamma({\bf z}_{k};\nu,2{\mathcal{H}}_{\nu})}}{4(1+\nu)^{2}}\|{\bf z}_{k+0.5}-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{2},

which implies that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\langle F({\bf z}_{k+0.5})-\bar{F}({\bf z}_{k+0.5};{\bf z}_{k}),{\bf z}_{k+0.5}-{\bf z}_{k+1}\rangle\\

&\leq{{\gamma({\bf z}_{k};\nu,2{\mathcal{H}}_{\nu})}}\left(\frac{1}{8(1+\nu)^{2}}\|{\bf z}_{k+0.5}-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|{\bf z}_{k+0.5}-z_{k+1}\|^{2}\right).\end{split}

(11)

Combining the above results, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{{\gamma({\bf z}_{k};\nu,2{\mathcal{H}}_{\nu})}}{2}\left(\|{\bf z}_{k+1}-{\bf z}\|^{2}-\|{\bf z}_{k}-{\bf z}\|^{2}+\|{\bf z}_{k+1}-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{2}\right)\\

\overset{\eqref{eq:fk0.5}}{\leq}&\langle F({\bf z}_{k+0.5}),{\bf z}-{\bf z}_{k+1}\rangle\\

\overset{\eqref{eq:innerbound1}}{\leq}&\langle F({\bf z}_{k+0.5}),{\bf z}-{\bf z}_{k+0.5}\rangle+{{\gamma({\bf z}_{k};\nu,2{\mathcal{H}}_{\nu})}}\left(\frac{1}{2(1+\nu)^{2}}\|{\bf z}_{k+0.5}-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|{\bf z}_{k+0.5}-z_{k+1}\|^{2}\right)\\
&+\langle\bar{F}({\bf z}_{k+0.5};{\bf z}_{k}),{\bf z}_{k+0.5}-{\bf z}_{k+1}\rangle\\
\overset{\eqref{eq:innerbound2}}{\leq}&\langle F({\bf z}_{k+0.5}),{\bf z}-{\bf z}_{k+0.5}\rangle+\frac{{\gamma({\bf z}_{k};\nu,2{\mathcal{H}}_{\nu})}}{2}\|{\bf z}_{k+1}-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{2}-\left(1-\frac{1}{8(1+\nu)^{2}}\right){\gamma({\bf z}_{k};\nu,2{\mathcal{H}}_{\nu})}\|{\bf z}_{k+0.5}-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{2}.\end{split}

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{1}{{\gamma({\bf z}_{k};\nu,2{\mathcal{H}}_{\nu})}}\langle F({\bf z}),{\bf z}_{k+0.5}-{\bf z}\rangle+C_{\nu}\|{\bf z}_{k+0.5}-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{2}\\

(16)

The line search steps in line 3 means

\displaystyle\frac{\|F({\bf z}_{k+0.5})-\bar{F}({\bf z}_{k+0.5};z_{k})\|^{2}}{{\gamma({\bf z}_{k};\nu,H_{k}2^{i_{k}})}}\leq\frac{(H_{k}2^{i_{k}-1}\|{\bf z}_{k+0.5}-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{1+\nu})^{2}}{H_{k}2^{i_{k}}\|{\bf z}_{k+0.5}-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{\nu}}=\frac{{\gamma({\bf z}_{k};\nu,H_{k}2^{i_{k}})}}{4}\|{\bf z}_{k+0.5}-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{2},

which results to

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\langle F({\bf z}_{k+0.5})-\bar{F}({\bf z}_{k+0.5};{\bf z}_{k}),{\bf z}_{k+0.5}-{\bf z}_{k+1}\rangle\\
\leq&\frac{\|F({\bf z}_{k+0.5})-\bar{F}({\bf z}_{k+0.5};{\bf z}_{k})\|^{2}}{2{\gamma({\bf z}_{k};\nu,H_{k}2^{i_{k}})}}+\frac{{\gamma({\bf z}_{k};\nu,H_{k}2^{i_{k}})}\|{\bf z}_{k+0.5}-{\bf z}_{k+1}\|^{2}}{2}\\
\leq&{{\gamma({\bf z}_{k};\nu,H_{k}2^{i_{k}})}}\left(\frac{1}{4}\|{\bf z}_{k+0.5}-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|{\bf z}_{k+0.5}-z_{k+1}\|^{2}\right).\end{split}

(17)

Together with above results, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{{\gamma({\bf z}_{k};\nu,H_{k}2^{i_{k}})}}{2}\left(\|{\bf z}_{k+1}-{\bf z}\|^{2}-\|{\bf z}_{k}-{\bf z}\|^{2}+\|{\bf z}_{k+1}-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{2}\right)\\
=\,&{\gamma({\bf z}_{k};\nu,H_{k}2^{i_{k}})}\langle{\bf z}_{k+1}-{\bf z}_{k},{\bf z}-{\bf z}_{k+1}\rangle\\
\overset{\eqref{eq:fk0.5-2}}{\leq}&\langle F({\bf z}_{k+0.5}),{\bf z}-{\bf z}_{k+1}\rangle\\
=\,&\langle F({\bf z}_{k+0.5}),{\bf z}-{\bf z}_{k+0.5}\rangle+\langle F({\bf z}_{k+0.5})-\bar{F}({\bf z}_{k+0.5};{\bf z}_{k}),{\bf z}_{k+0.5}-{\bf z}_{k+1}\rangle+\langle\bar{F}({\bf z}_{k+0.5};{\bf z}_{k}),{\bf z}_{k+0.5}-{\bf z}_{k+1}\rangle\\
\overset{\eqref{eq:innerbound1-2}}{\leq}&\langle F({\bf z}_{k+0.5}),{\bf z}-{\bf z}_{k+0.5}\rangle+{{\gamma({\bf z}_{k};\nu,H_{k}2^{i_{k}})}}\left(\frac{1}{4}\|{\bf z}_{k+0.5}-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|{\bf z}_{k+0.5}-z_{k+1}\|^{2}\right)\\
&~+\langle\bar{F}({\bf z}_{k+0.5};{\bf z}_{k}),{\bf z}_{k+0.5}-{\bf z}_{k+1}\rangle\\
\overset{\eqref{eq:innerbound2-2}}{\leq}&\langle F({\bf z}_{k+0.5}),{\bf z}-{\bf z}_{k+0.5}\rangle+\frac{{\gamma({\bf z}_{k};\nu,H_{k}2^{i_{k}})}}{2}\|{\bf z}_{k+1}-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{2}-\frac{{\gamma({\bf z}_{k};\nu,H_{k}2^{i_{k}})}}{4}\|{\bf z}_{k+0.5}-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{2}.\end{split}

(18)

We first use induction to show that
H_{k}\leq{2{\mathcal{H}}_{\nu}}/{(1+\nu)}
for all
k=0,1\cdots T
.
This statement holds obviously for
k=0
. Suppose we have
H_{k}\leq{2{\mathcal{H}}_{\nu}}/{(1+\nu)}
at the
k
-th iteration.
According to the condition of line-search, we have

\displaystyle H_{k}2^{i_{k}-2}\|{\rm{T}}({\bf z}_{k};\nu,H_{k}2^{i_{k}-1})-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{1+\nu}

\displaystyle\leq\|F({\rm{T}}({\bf z}_{k};\nu,H_{k}2^{i_{k}-1}))-\bar{F}({\rm{T}}({\bf z}_{k};\nu,H_{k}2^{i_{k}-1});{\bf z}_{k})\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{{\mathcal{H}}_{\nu}}{1+\nu}\|{\rm{T}}({\bf z}_{k};\nu,H_{k}2^{i_{k}-1})-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{1+\nu},

which means
2^{i_{k}}H_{k}\leq{4{\mathcal{H}}_{\nu}}/{(1+\nu)}
. Thus, we have

\displaystyle H_{k+1}=2^{i_{k}-1}H_{k}\leq\frac{2{\mathcal{H}}_{\nu}}{1+\nu}

and it finish the induction.

Now we can bound the sum of the regularization term by following the proof of Theorem
3.1
but take
C_{\nu}={1}/{4}
. We have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}{\gamma({\bf z}_{k};\nu,H_{k}2^{i_{k}})}^{-1}=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left(H_{k}2^{i_{k}}\right)^{-1}\|{\bf z}_{k+0.5}-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{-\nu}\geq\left(\frac{1}{4D^{2}}\right)^{\nu}\frac{1+\nu}{4{\mathcal{H}}_{\nu}}K^{\frac{2+\nu}{2}}.

We set

\displaystyle K:=\frac{4{\mathcal{H}}_{\nu}^{\frac{2}{2+\nu}}D^{2}}{\epsilon^{\frac{2}{2+\nu}}},

then

\displaystyle m(\bar{{\bf z}}_{K})\leq\frac{D^{2+\nu}{\mathcal{H}}_{\nu}}{\frac{1+\nu}{4^{(\nu/2+1)}}K^{\frac{2+\nu}{2}}}\leq\epsilon,

Finally, we bound the total number of oracle calls as

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}(i_{k}+1)=

\displaystyle K+\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left(\log_{2}\frac{H_{k+1}}{H_{k}}+1\right)=2K+\log_{2}H_{K+1}-\log_{2}H_{0}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2K+\log_{2}\frac{2{\mathcal{H}}_{\nu}}{1+\nu}-\log_{2}H_{0}={\mathcal{O}}\big(\epsilon^{-\frac{2}{2+\nu}}\big).

∎

4
The Universal Regularized Extra-Newton Method

In real problems, the Hölder continuous parameter
\nu
may be difficult to access, resulting the algorithms in Section
3
do not work since they depend on the terms
\|{\bf z}-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{\nu}({\bf z}-{\bf z}_{k})
and
\|{\rm{T}}({\bf z}_{k};\nu,H_{k}2^{i})-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{1+\nu}
.
We propose the universal regularized extra-Newton (UREN) method in Algorithm
3
, which does not require the exact values of
\nu
and
{\mathcal{H}}_{\nu}
.

We convergence analysis of UREN starts from the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
{\bf z}_{+}={\rm{T}}({\bf z};1,H)
for any
{{\bf z}}\in{\mathcal{Z}}
and
H>0
.
Suppose the operator
F
satisfies Assumption
2.1
,
2.2
and
2.3
.
If the conditions

\displaystyle\max_{{\bf z}\in{\mathcal{Z}}}\langle F({\bf z}_{+}),{\bf z}_{+}-{\bf z}\rangle\geq\delta\qquad\text{and}\qquad H\geq\left(3D\right)^{\frac{1-\nu}{1+\nu}}\left(\frac{{\mathcal{H}}_{\nu}}{1+\nu}\right)^{\frac{2}{1+\nu}}\left(\frac{1}{\delta}\right)^{\frac{1-\nu}{1+\nu}},

hold for some
\delta\geq 0
and
\nu\in[0,1]
,
we have

\displaystyle\|{\bf z}_{+}-{\bf z}\|^{1-\nu}\geq\frac{2{\mathcal{H}}_{\nu}}{H(1+\nu)}.

Proof.

Recall the definition of
{\bf z}_{+}=T_{1,H}({\bf z})
implies that

\displaystyle\langle\bar{F}({\bf z}_{+};{\bf z})+H\|{\bf z}_{+}-{\bf z}\|({\bf z}_{+}-{\bf z}),\bar{{\bf z}}-{\bf z}_{+}\rangle\geq 0~~~\text{for all}~~~\bar{{\bf z}}\in{\mathcal{Z}}.

(19)

We denote

\displaystyle{\bf z}^{\prime}=\argmax_{{\bf z}\in{\mathcal{Z}}}\langle F({\bf z}_{+}),{\bf z}_{+}-{\bf z}\rangle,

where we have
\|{\bf z}^{\prime}-{\bf z}_{+}\|\leq D
.
Substituting
\bar{{\bf z}}={\bf z}^{\prime}
into (
19
), we have

\displaystyle\langle\bar{F}({\bf z}_{+};{\bf z}),{\bf z}_{+}-{\bf z}^{\prime}\rangle\leq-H\|{\bf z}_{+}-{\bf z}\|\langle{\bf z}_{+}-{\bf z},{\bf z}_{+}-{\bf z}^{\prime}\rangle\leq HD\|{\bf z}_{+}-{\bf z}\|^{2}.

(20)

Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle\delta~~\leq~~

\displaystyle\langle F({\bf z}_{+}),{\bf z}_{+}-{\bf z}^{\prime}\rangle=\langle F({\bf z}_{+})-\bar{F}({\bf z}_{+};{\bf z}),{\bf z}_{+}-{\bf z}^{\prime}\rangle+\langle\bar{F}({\bf z}_{+};{\bf z}),{\bf z}_{+}-{\bf z}^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:unversalproper2},\eqref{eq:hesconti3}}{\leq}

\displaystyle\frac{{\mathcal{H}}_{\nu}}{1+\nu}D\|{\bf z}-{\bf z}_{+}\|^{1+\nu}+HD\|{\bf z}_{+}-{\bf z}\|^{2}

\displaystyle~~=~~

\displaystyle D\|{\bf z}-{\bf z}_{+}\|^{1+\nu}\left(\frac{{\mathcal{H}}_{\nu}}{1+\nu}+H\|{\bf z}_{+}-{\bf z}\|^{1-\nu}\right).

Suppose the result of this lemma does not hold, that is
H\|{\bf z}_{+}-{\bf z}\|^{1-\nu}<{2{\mathcal{H}}_{\nu}}/{(1+\nu)}
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\delta<\frac{3D{\mathcal{H}}_{\nu}}{1+\nu}\left(\frac{{\mathcal{H}}_{\nu}}{H(1+\nu)}\right)^{\frac{1+\nu}{1-\nu}},

which implies

\displaystyle H<\left(3D\right)^{\frac{1-\nu}{1+\nu}}\left(\frac{{\mathcal{H}}_{\nu}}{1+\nu}\right)^{\frac{2}{1+\nu}}\left(\frac{1}{\delta}\right)^{\frac{1-\nu}{1+\nu}}.

The above inequality contradicts to the second condition of this lemma.
∎

Algorithm 3

Universal Regularized Extra Newton (
\nu
and
{\mathcal{H}}_{\nu}
are
unknown)

1:

Input:

{\bf z}_{0}\in{\mathcal{Z}}
,
K
and
H_{0}\in\big(0,\inf_{\nu}H_{\nu}(\epsilon)\big]

2:

for

k=0,1,\dots K

3:

Find the smallest
i_{k}
such that
\|F({\rm{T}}({\bf z}_{k};1,H_{k}2^{i_{k}}))-\bar{F}({\rm{T}}({\bf z}_{k};1,H_{k}2^{i_{k}});{\bf z}_{k})\|\leq H_{k}2^{i_{k}-1}\|{\rm{T}}({\bf z}_{k};1,H_{k}2^{i_{k}})-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{2}

4:

{\bf z}_{k+0.5}={\rm{T}}({\bf z}_{k};1,H_{k}2^{i_{k}})

5:

{\bf z}_{k+1}=\mbox{arg}\min\limits_{z\in{\mathcal{Z}}}\,\langle F({\bf z}_{k+0.5}),{\bf z}-{\bf z}_{k}\rangle+\frac{{\gamma({\bf z}_{k};1,H_{k}2^{i_{k}})}}{2}\|{\bf z}-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{2}

6:

H_{k+1}=2^{i_{k}-1}H_{k}

7:

end for

Now, we present the main result for our universal algorithm.

Theorem 4.2
.

Suppose the operator
F
satisfies Assumption
2.1
,
2.2
and
2.3
and the sequence
\{{\bf z}_{k}\}_{k=0}^{K}
generated by Algorithm
3
satisfies

\displaystyle\max_{{\bf z}\in{\mathcal{Z}}}\langle F({\rm{T}}({\bf z}_{k},1,2^{i}H_{k})),{\rm{T}}({\bf z}_{k},1,2^{i}H_{k})-{\bf z}\rangle\geq\epsilon~~~\text{for}~~~i=0,\cdots,i_{k},k=0,\cdots,K.

(21)

Then we have

\displaystyle 2^{i_{k}-1}H_{k}\leq H_{\nu}(\epsilon):=\left(3D\right)^{\frac{1-\nu}{1+\nu}}\left(\frac{{\mathcal{H}}_{\nu}}{1+\nu}\right)^{\frac{2}{1+\nu}}\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)^{\frac{1-\nu}{1+\nu}}

for all
k=0,\cdots,T
. We also have
m(\bar{{\bf z}}_{K})\leq{\mathcal{O}}({K^{-{3(1+\nu)}/{4}}})
, where

\displaystyle\bar{{\bf z}}_{K}=\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{{\bf z}_{k+0.5}}{{\gamma({\bf z}_{k};1,H_{k}2^{i_{k}})}}}{\sum_{k=1}^{K}{\gamma({\bf z}_{k};1,H_{k}2^{i_{k}})}^{-1}}.

(22)

Besides, the total number of oracle calls to achieve an
\epsilon
-accurate solution is at most
{\mathcal{O}}({\epsilon^{-{4}/{(3(1+\nu))}}})
.

Proof.

We first show that the condition (
21
) means we can always find
i
such that

\displaystyle\|F({\rm{T}}({\bf z}_{k};1,H_{k}2^{i}))-\bar{F}({\rm{T}}({\bf z}_{k};1,H_{k}2^{i});{\bf z}_{k})\|\leq H_{k}2^{i-1}\|{\rm{T}}({\bf z}_{k};1,H_{k}2^{i}))-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{2}.

If it does not hold, the Hölder continuous assumption means

\displaystyle H_{k}2^{i-1}\|{\rm{T}}({\bf z}_{k};1,H_{k}2^{i_{k}})-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{2}<\frac{{\mathcal{H}}_{\nu}}{1+\nu}\|{\rm{T}}({\bf z}_{k};1,H_{k}2^{i_{k}})-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{1+\nu},

which implies

\displaystyle\|{\rm{T}}({\bf z}_{k};1,H_{k}2^{i_{k}})-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{1-\nu}<\frac{{\mathcal{H}}_{\nu}}{(1+\nu)H_{k}2^{i-1}}.

(23)

Since we have

\displaystyle\max_{{\bf z}\in{\mathcal{Z}}}\langle F({\rm{T}}({\bf z}_{k};1,H_{k}2^{i_{k}})),{\rm{T}}({\bf z}_{k};1,H_{k}2^{i_{k}})-{\bf z}\rangle\geq\epsilon,

Lemma
4.1
means choosing sufficient large
H_{k}2^{i}
such that

\displaystyle H_{k}2^{i}\geq\left(3D\right)^{\frac{1-\nu}{1+\nu}}\left(\frac{{\mathcal{H}}_{\nu}}{1+\nu}\right)^{\frac{2}{1+\nu}}\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)^{\frac{1-\nu}{1+\nu}},

leads to

\displaystyle\|{\rm{T}}({\bf z}_{k};1,H_{k}2^{i_{k}})-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{1-\nu}\geq\frac{{\mathcal{H}}_{\nu}}{(1+\nu)H_{k}2^{i-1}},

which contradicts to (
23
). This prove that our algorithm is well-defined.

Then we prove there is a unified upper bound for
H_{k}
. The steps of choosing
i_{k-1}
mean

\displaystyle H_{k}2^{i_{k}-2}\|{\rm{T}}({\bf z}_{k};1,H_{k}2^{i_{k}-1})-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{2}<\frac{{\mathcal{H}}_{\nu}}{1+\nu}\|{\rm{T}}({\bf z}_{k};1,H_{k}2^{i_{k}-1})-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{1+\nu},

which implies

\displaystyle\|{\rm{T}}({\bf z}_{k};1,H_{k}2^{i_{k-1}})-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{1-\nu}\leq\frac{{\mathcal{H}}_{\nu}}{(1+\nu)H_{k}2^{i_{k}-2}}.

Using Lemma
4.1
, we have

\displaystyle H_{k}2^{i_{k}-1}<\left(3D\right)^{\frac{1-\nu}{1+\nu}}\left(\frac{{\mathcal{H}}_{\nu}}{1+\nu}\right)^{\frac{2}{1+\nu}}\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)^{\frac{1-\nu}{1+\nu}},

which implies

\displaystyle H_{k+1}=2^{i_{k}-1}H_{k}\leq H_{\nu}\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)^{\frac{1-\nu}{1+\nu}}\overset{\text{def}}{=}H(\epsilon)

(24)

where
H_{\nu}:=\left(3D\right)^{{(1-\nu)}/{(1+\nu)}}\left({{\mathcal{H}}_{\nu}}/{(1+\nu)}\right)^{{2}/{(1+\nu)}}
.

Now, we can present the convergence results the algorithm. Following the proof of Theorem
3.2
, the statements (
15
), (
16
) and (
17
) still hold for
\nu=1
, that is

\displaystyle\langle\bar{F}({\bf z}_{k+0.5};{\bf z}_{k}),{\bf z}_{k+0.5}-{\bf z}_{k+1}\rangle\leq-\frac{{\gamma({\bf z}_{k};1,H_{k}2^{i_{k}})}}{2}\left(\|{\bf z}_{k+0.5}-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{2}+\|{\bf z}_{k+1}-{\bf z}_{k+0.5}\|^{2}-\|{\bf z}_{k+1}-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{2}\right),

(25)

\displaystyle\langle F({\bf z}_{k+0.5})+{\gamma({\bf z}_{k};1,H_{k}2^{i_{k}})}({\bf z}_{k+1}-{\bf z}_{k}),{\bf z}-{\bf z}_{k+1}\rangle\geq 0.

(26)

According to the line search step, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\|F({\bf z}_{k+0.5})-\bar{F}({\bf z}_{k+0.5};z_{k})\|^{2}}{{\gamma({\bf z}_{k};1,H_{k}2^{i_{k}})}}\leq\frac{(H_{k}2^{i_{k}-1}\|{\bf z}_{k+0.5}-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{2})^{2}}{H_{k}2^{i_{k}}\|{\bf z}_{k+0.5}-{\bf z}_{k}\|}=\frac{{\gamma({\bf z}_{k};1,H_{k}2^{i_{k}})}}{4}\|{\bf z}_{k+0.5}-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{2},

(27)

which results to

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\langle F({\bf z}_{k+0.5})-\bar{F}({\bf z}_{k+0.5};{\bf z}_{k}),{\bf z}_{k+0.5}-{\bf z}_{k+1}\rangle\\
&\leq\frac{\|F({\bf z}_{k+0.5})-\bar{F}({\bf z}_{k+0.5};{\bf z}_{k})\|^{2}}{2{\gamma({\bf z}_{k};1,H_{k}2^{i_{k}})}}+\frac{{\gamma({\bf z}_{k};1,H_{k}2^{i_{k}})}\|{\bf z}_{k+0.5}-{\bf z}_{k+1}\|^{2}}{2}\\
&\leq{{\gamma({\bf z}_{k};1,H_{k}2^{i_{k}})}}\left(\frac{1}{4}\|{\bf z}_{k+0.5}-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|{\bf z}_{k+0.5}-z_{k+1}\|^{2}\right).\end{split}

(28)

Following the proof of Theorem
2
by taking

\nu=1
and
C_{\nu}=1/4
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{{\gamma({\bf z}_{k};1,H_{k}2^{i_{k}})}}\langle F({\bf z}),{\bf z}_{k+0.5}-{\bf z}\rangle+\frac{1}{4}\|{\bf z}_{k+0.5}-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{2}{\leq}\frac{1}{2}\left(\|{\bf z}_{k}-{\bf z}\|^{2}-\|{\bf z}_{k+1}-{\bf z}\|^{2}\right),\end{split}

which means

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}\|{\bf z}_{k+0.5}-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{2}\leq 2\|{\bf z}^{\prime}-{\bf z}^{*}\|^{2}.

Using Hölder’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle k

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{K}1=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left(\|{\bf z}_{k+0.5}-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{-1}\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}\left(\|{\bf z}_{k+0.5}-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{k=1}^{K}\|{\bf z}_{k+0.5}-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{-1}\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{K}\|{\bf z}_{k+0.5}-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}},

which implies

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}{\gamma({\bf z}_{k};1,H_{k}2^{i_{k}})}^{-1}=\frac{1}{2H_{k+1}}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\|{\bf z}_{k+0.5}-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{-1}\geq\frac{1}{4DH(\epsilon)}K^{{3}/{2}}.

We set

\displaystyle K=\frac{6{\mathcal{H}}_{\nu}D^{\frac{8+4\nu}{3(1+\nu)}}}{\epsilon^{\frac{4}{3(1+\nu)}}},

then the point
\bar{{\bf z}}_{K}
defined in (
22
) holds that

\displaystyle m(\bar{\bf{z}}_{K})=\max_{{\bf z}\in{\mathcal{Z}}}\left\langle F({\bf z}),\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{{\bf z}_{k+0.5}-{\bf z}}{{\gamma({\bf z}_{k};1,H_{k}2^{i_{k}})}}/\sum_{k=1}^{K}{{\gamma({\bf z}_{k};1,H_{k}2^{i_{k}})}}^{-1}\right\rangle\leq\frac{2D^{3}H(\epsilon)}{K^{\frac{3}{2}}}\overset{\eqref{eq:Hepsilon}}{\leq}\epsilon.

Similar to the proof of Theorem
3.2
, we bound the total number of oracle calls as

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}(i_{k}+1)=2K+2+\log_{2}(H(\epsilon))-\log_{2}(H_{0})={\mathcal{O}}\left(\epsilon^{-\frac{4}{3(1+\nu)}}\right).

∎

) in Theorem

where

for some particular
k
and

, then we have

\displaystyle m(\xi)=\max_{{\bf z}\in{\mathcal{Z}}}\langle F({\bf z}),\xi-{\bf z}\rangle\overset{\eqref{eq:monotone}}{\leq}\max_{{\bf z}\in{\mathcal{Z}}}\langle F(\xi),\xi-{\bf z}\rangle\leq\epsilon,

which means we have already found an approximate solution with desired
\epsilon
-accuracy.

\nu=1
for

-AREN (Algorithm

3
) for the case that

However, the absence of exact information on

5
Discussion

We have proposed three algorithms for solving monotone variational inequality problem with
\nu
-Hölder continuous Jacobian.
The iteration complexities of
\nu
-REN (Algorithm
1
) and
\nu
-AREN (Algorithm
2
) are
{\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon^{-2/(2+\nu)})
, while the complexity of UREN (Algorithm
3
) is
{\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon^{-4/(3(1+\nu))})
.
All of our results match the complexity
{\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon^{-2/3})
of existing algorithms for the special case
\nu=1

[
18
,
25
]
.
Naturally, the convergence rate for the algorithms with known
\nu
is slightly better than the one without the knowledge of
\nu
.

Recall that the optimal first-order methods have the iteration complexity of
{\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon^{-1})
for solving monotone variational inequality problem with
L
-Lipschitz continuous operator
[
22
,
37
,
36
]
.
The following proposition states the
L
-Lipschitz continuity of
F
corresponds to Hölder continuity with
\nu=0
and
{\mathcal{H}}_{\nu}=2L
.

Proposition 5.1
.

If the operator
F(\cdot)
is
L
-Lipschitz continuous, it satisfies Assumption
2.2
with
\nu=0
and
{\mathcal{H}}_{0}=2L
.

Proof.

This proof is modified from
Nesterov 2018
.
We define
B_{\epsilon}({\bf z})=\{{\bf u}:\|{\bf z}-{\bf u}\|\leq\epsilon\}\subseteq{\mathcal{Z}}
for any
{\bf z}\in{\mathcal{Z}}
.
We have

\displaystyle\int_{t=0}^{1}\nabla F({\bf z}+t{\bf s}){\bf s}\,{\rm d}t=F({\bf z}+{\bf s})-F({\bf z}),

for any
{\bf z}
,
{\bf z}+{\bf s}\in B_{\epsilon}({\bf z})

which implies that

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{t=0}^{1}\nabla F({\bf z}+t{\bf s}){\rm d}t\right\|\leq\left\|\int_{t=0}^{1}\nabla F({\bf z}+t{\bf s}){\rm d}t\right\|\|{\bf s}\|\leq{\rm d}t\|F({\bf z}+{\bf s})-F({\bf z})\|\leq L\|{\bf s}\|.

Thus we have

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{t=0}^{1}\nabla F({\bf z}+t{\bf s}){\rm d}t\right\|\leq L.

Let
{\bf s}\to{\bf 0}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\nabla F({\bf z})\|=\lim_{{\bf s}\to 0}\left\|\int_{t=0}^{1}\nabla F({\bf z}+t{\bf s}){\rm d}t\right\|\leq L.

Then it holds that

\displaystyle\|\nabla F({\bf z})-\nabla F({\bf z}^{\prime})\|\leq 2L\|{\bf z}-{\bf z}^{\prime}\|^{0}:={\mathcal{H}}_{0}\|{\bf z}-{\bf z}^{\prime}\|^{0}

for all
{\bf z}
,
{\bf z}^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{Z}}
.
∎

We observe that the complexity of proposed
\nu
-REN and
\nu
-AREN matches the optimal first-order methods when
\nu=0
, while UREN has slightly worse complexity. This phenomenon is reasonable since it also appears in convex minimization.

However, when the Hölder continuous parameter satisfies
\nu\in\left[{1}/{3},1\right]
, we have

\displaystyle\epsilon^{-4/(3(1+\nu))}\leq\epsilon^{-1},

which implies UREN enjoys faster convergence than the optimal first order algorithms since it benefits from the addditional information of Jacobian.

Our methods also can be extended to higher-order optimization by the assumption of Hölder continuouity on the
p-1
-th order derivative of
F
.
The design and analysis for the high-order algorithms are quite similar to the second-order algorithms we have proposed and we provide them in appendix.

6
Conclusion

In this work, we propose regularized extra-Newton methods for solving variational inequalities with Hölders continuous Jacobian. We present algorithms for both of the cases that the Hölders parameter
\nu
is known and unknown.
For
\nu=1
, all of proposed methods match the optimal convergence rate for monotone variational inequality problem with Lipschitz continuous Jacobian.
For
\nu\in[0,1)
, our methods give the first universal method for variational inequalities without the knowledge of
\nu
and
{\mathcal{H}}_{\nu}
.
Besides, all of these results can be extended to design the high-order algorithms.
For the future work, it is interesting to apply the technique of gradient regularized Newton methods
[
26
,
8
,
10
]
and damped Newton methods
[
31
,
17
]
to design the more practical algorithms for solving variational inequality problem.

{\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon^{-2/(p+1)})
under the following assumptions.

Assumption A.1
.

We assume the
(p-1)
-th order derivative of operator
F
is
L_{p}
-Lipschitz continuous, i.e., for all
{\bf z}
and
{\bf z}^{\prime}
, we have

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{p-1}F({\bf z})-\nabla^{p-1}F({\bf z}^{\prime})\|\leq L_{p}\|{\bf z}-{\bf z}^{\prime}\|.

Following Assumption
A.1
, we introduce the Hölder-continuous assumption on
\nabla^{p-1}F(\cdot)
as follows.

Assumption A.2
.

We assume the
(p-1)
-th order derivative of operator
F
is
{\mathcal{H}}_{p,\nu}
-Hölder continuous, i.e., for all
{\bf z}
and
{\bf z}^{\prime}
, we have

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{p-1}F({\bf z})-\nabla^{p-1}F({\bf z}^{\prime})\|\leq{\mathcal{H}}_{p,\nu}\|{\bf z}-{\bf z}^{\prime}\|^{\nu}.

Similar to Section
2
, we introduce the following notations

\displaystyle\bar{F}_{p}({\bf z}^{\prime};{\bf z})

\displaystyle\overset{\text{def}}{=}\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\frac{1}{i!}\nabla^{i}F({\bf z}^{\prime})[{\bf z}-{\bf z}^{\prime}]^{i},

\displaystyle\bar{F}_{p,\nu,H}({\bf z}^{\prime};{\bf z})

\displaystyle\overset{\text{def}}{=}\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\frac{1}{i!}\nabla^{i}F({\bf z}^{\prime})[{\bf z}-{\bf z}^{\prime}]^{i}+H\|{\bf z}-{\bf z}^{\prime}\|^{p-2+\nu}({\bf z}-{\bf z}^{\prime}).

We denote the solution of variational inequalities as

\displaystyle T_{p}({\bf z};\nu,H)\overset{\text{def}}{=}{\rm{VI}}_{{\mathcal{Z}}}(\bar{F}_{p,\nu,H}({\bf z}^{\prime};{\bf z})).

and define

\displaystyle\gamma_{p}({\bf z};\nu,H)\overset{\text{def}}{=}H\|{\rm{T}}_{p}({\bf z};\nu,H)-{\bf z}\|^{p-2+\nu}.

According to Assumption
A.2
, we have

\displaystyle\|F({\bf z})-\bar{F}_{p}({\bf z}^{\prime};{\bf z})\|\leq C_{p,\nu}{\mathcal{H}}_{p,\nu}\|{\bf z}^{\prime}-{\bf z}\|^{p-1+\nu}.

(29)

Using the similar strategy in Section
3
and
4
, we propose adaptive
\nu
-Regularized Extra tensor (
\nu
-ARET) method in Algorithm
4
for known
\nu
and unknown
{\mathcal{H}}_{p,\nu}
and universal regularized extra tensor method (URET) in Algorithm
5
for unknown
\nu
and
{\mathcal{H}}_{p,\nu}
. We give the convergence analysis for these algorithms in the following theorems.

Algorithm 4

Adaptive
\nu
-Regularized Extra Tensor Method (
\nu
is known,
{\mathcal{H}}_{p,\nu}
is unknown)

1:

Input:

{\bf z}_{0}\in{\mathcal{Z}}
,
K
,
p
and
H_{0}\in\big(0,2C_{p,\nu}{\mathcal{H}}_{p,\nu}\big]

2:

for

k=0,1,\dots K

3:

Find the smallest
i_{k}
such that

4:

\left\|F({\rm{T}}_{p}({\bf z}_{k};\nu,H_{k}2^{i})))-\bar{F}({\rm{T}}_{p}({\bf z}_{k};\nu,H_{k}2^{i}));{\bf z}_{k})\right\|\leq H_{k}2^{i_{k}-1}\|{\rm{T}}_{p}({\bf z}_{k};\nu,H_{k}2^{i}))-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{p-1+\nu}

5:

{\bf z}_{k+0.5}=T_{p,\nu,H_{k}2^{i_{k}}}({\bf z}_{k})

6:

{\bf z}_{k+1}=\mbox{arg}\min\limits_{z\in{\mathcal{Z}}},\langle F({\bf z}_{k+0.5}),{\bf z}-{\bf z}_{k}\rangle+\frac{{\gamma_{p}({\bf z}_{k};\nu,H_{k}2^{i_{k}})}}{2}\|{\bf z}-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{2}

7:

H_{k+1}=2^{i_{k}-1}H_{k}

8:

end for

Algorithm 5

Universal Regularized Extra Tensor Method (
\nu
and
{\mathcal{H}}_{p,\nu}
are unknown)

1:

Input:

{\bf z}_{0}\in{\mathcal{Z}}
,
K
,
p
and

2:

for

k=0,1,\dots K

3:

Find the smallest
i_{k}
such that

4:

\|F({\rm{T}}_{p}({\bf z}_{k};1,H_{k}2^{i_{k}}))-\bar{F}({\rm{T}}_{p}({\bf z}_{k};1,H_{k}2^{i_{k}});{\bf z}_{k})\|\leq H_{k}2^{i_{k}-1}\|{\rm{T}}_{p}({\bf z}_{k};1,H_{k}2^{i_{k}})-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{p}

5:

{\bf z}_{k+0.5}={\rm{T}}_{p}({\bf z}_{k};1,H_{k}2^{i_{k}})

6:

{\bf z}_{k+1}=\mbox{arg}\min\limits_{z\in{\mathcal{Z}}}\,\langle F({\bf z}_{k+0.5}),{\bf z}-{\bf z}_{k}\rangle+\frac{{\gamma_{p}({\bf z}_{k};1,H_{k}2^{i_{k}})}}{2}\|{\bf z}-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{2}

H_{k+1}=2^{i_{k}-1}H_{k}

8:

Theorem A.3
.

Under Assumption
2.1
,
2.3
and
A.2
,
Algorithm
4
satisfies
m(\bar{{\bf z}}_{K})\leq{\mathcal{O}}(K^{-{p+\nu}/{2}})
,
where

\displaystyle\bar{{\bf z}}_{K}=\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{{\bf z}_{k+0.5}}{\gamma_{p}({\bf z}_{k};\nu,H_{k}2^{i_{k}})}}{\sum_{k=1}^{K}\gamma_{p}({\bf z}_{k};\nu,H_{k}2^{i_{k}})^{-1}},

Besides, the total number of oracle calls to achieve an
\epsilon
-accurate solution is at most
{\mathcal{O}}({\epsilon^{-2/(p+\nu)}})
.

Proof.

The proof of this theorem is similar to the one of Theorem
3.2
.
By replacing
{\gamma({\bf z}_{k};\nu,H_{k}2^{i_{k}})}
with
{\gamma_{p}({\bf z}_{k};\nu,H_{k}2^{i_{k}})}
in (
18
), we have

\displaystyle\left\langle F({\bf z}),\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{1}{{\gamma_{p}({\bf z}_{k};\nu,H_{k}2^{i_{k}})}}({\bf z}_{k+0.5}-{\bf z})\right\rangle+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\|{\bf z}_{k+0.5}-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|{\bf z}_{1}-{\bf z}\|^{2}.

We use induction to show
H_{k}\leq C_{p,\nu}{\mathcal{H}}_{p,\nu}
for all
k=0,1\cdots K
.
The result for
k=0
is obvious. Suppose we have
H_{k}\leq{2C_{p,\nu}{\mathcal{H}}_{\nu}}
at the
k
-th iteration.
According to the condition of line-search, we have

\displaystyle H_{k}2^{i_{k}-2}\|{\rm{T}}_{p}({\bf z}_{k};\nu,H_{k}2^{i_{k}-1})-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{p-1+\nu}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|F({\rm{T}}_{p}({\bf z}_{k};\nu,H_{k}2^{i_{k}-1}))-\bar{F}({\rm{T}}_{p}({\bf z}_{k};\nu,H_{k}2^{i_{k}-1});{\bf z}_{k})\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{p,\nu}{\mathcal{H}}_{p,\nu}\|{\rm{T}}_{p}({\bf z}_{k};\nu,H_{k}2^{i_{k}-1})-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{p-1+\nu},

which means
2^{i_{k}}H_{k}\leq 4C_{p,\nu}{\mathcal{H}}_{p,\nu}
. Hence, we have

\displaystyle H_{k+1}=2^{i_{k}-1}H_{k}\leq 2C_{p,\nu}{\mathcal{H}}_{p,\nu},

which finishes the induction.

Then we use Hölder’s inequality to obtain

\displaystyle K

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{K}1=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left(\|{\bf z}_{k+0.5}-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{-(p-2+\nu)}\right)^{\frac{2}{p+\nu}}\left(\|{\bf z}_{k+0.5}-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p-2+\nu}{p+\nu}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{k=1}^{K}\|{\bf z}_{k+0.5}-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{-(p-1)}\right)^{\frac{2}{p+\nu}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{K}\|{\bf z}_{k+0.5}-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p-2+\nu}{p+\nu}},

which implies

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}{\gamma_{p}({\bf z}_{k};1,H_{k}2^{i_{k}})}^{-1}=\frac{1}{2H_{k+1}}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\|{\bf z}_{k+0.5}-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{-1}\geq{\mathcal{O}}\big(K^{\frac{p+\nu}{2}}\big).

We set
K={\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon^{-{2}/{p+\nu}})
, then the point
\bar{{\bf z}}_{K}
satisfies
m(\bar{{\bf z}}_{K})\leq{\mathcal{O}}\left(\epsilon\right)
.

Finally, we bound the total number of oracle calls as

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}(i_{k}+1)=

\displaystyle K+\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left(\log_{2}\frac{H_{k+1}}{H_{k}}+1\right)=2K+\log_{2}H_{K+1}-\log_{2}H_{0}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2K+\log_{2}2C_{p,\nu}{\mathcal{H}}_{p,\nu}-\log_{2}H_{0}={\mathcal{O}}\big(\epsilon^{-\frac{2}{p+\nu}}\big).

∎

For Algorithm
5
, we first provide the following lemma to extend the result of Lemma
4.1
.

Lemma A.4
.

Let
{\bf z}_{+}={\rm{T}}_{p}({\bf z};1,H)
for some
{{\bf z}}\in{\mathcal{Z}}
and
H>0
. If

\displaystyle\max_{{\bf z}\in{\mathcal{Z}}}\langle F({\bf z}_{+}),{\bf z}_{+}-{\bf z}\rangle\geq\delta~~\text{and}~~~H\geq\left(3\cdot 2^{p-1}D\right)^{\frac{p-1+\nu}{1-\nu}}\left(C_{p,\nu}{\mathcal{H}}_{p,\nu}\right)^{\frac{p}{1-\nu}}\left(\frac{1}{\delta}\right)^{\frac{1-\nu}{p-1+\nu}}

holds for some
\delta\geq 0
,
\nu\in[0,1]
.
We have

\displaystyle\|{\bf z}_{+}-{\bf z}\|^{1-\nu}\geq\frac{2C_{p,\nu}{\mathcal{H}}_{p,\nu}}{H}.

Proof.

Recall the update of
{\bf z}_{+}=T_{p,1,H}({\bf z})
implies that

\displaystyle\langle\bar{F}_{p}({\bf z}_{+};{\bf z})+H\|{\bf z}_{+}-{\bf z}\|^{p-1}({\bf z}_{+}-{\bf z}),\bar{{\bf z}}-{\bf z}_{+}\rangle\geq 0~~~\text{for all}~~~\bar{{\bf z}}\in{\mathcal{Z}}.

(30)

Denote
{\bf z}^{\prime}
as

\displaystyle{\bf z}^{\prime}=\argmax_{{\bf z}\in{\mathcal{Z}}}\langle F({\bf z}_{+}),{\bf z}_{+}-{\bf z}\rangle,

where we have
\|{\bf z}^{\prime}-{\bf z}_{+}\|\leq D

and take
\bar{{\bf z}}={\bf z}^{\prime}
into (
30
), we have

\displaystyle\langle\bar{F}_{p}({\bf z}_{+};{\bf z}),{\bf z}_{+}-{\bf z}^{\prime}\rangle\leq-H\|{\bf z}_{+}-{\bf z}\|^{p-1}\langle{\bf z}_{+}-{\bf z},{\bf z}_{+}-{\bf z}^{\prime}\rangle\leq HD\|{\bf z}_{+}-{\bf z}\|^{p}.

(31)

Thus we have

\displaystyle\delta~~\leq\,~~

\displaystyle\langle F({\bf z}_{+}),{\bf z}_{+}-{\bf z}^{\prime}\rangle=\langle F({\bf z}_{+})-\bar{F}_{p}({\bf z}_{+};{\bf z}),{\bf z}_{+}-{\bf z}^{\prime}\rangle+\langle\bar{F}({\bf z}_{+};{\bf z}),{\bf z}_{+}-{\bf z}^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:punversalproper2},\eqref{eq:hespconti}}{\leq}

\displaystyle{{\mathcal{H}}_{p,\nu}}D\|{\bf z}-{\bf z}_{+}\|^{p-1+\nu}+HD\|{\bf z}_{+}-{\bf z}\|^{p}

\displaystyle~~=\,~~

\displaystyle D\|{\bf z}-{\bf z}_{+}\|^{p-1+\nu}\left(C_{p,\nu}{\mathcal{H}}_{p,\nu}+H\|{\bf z}_{+}-{\bf z}\|^{1-\nu}\right).

Assume
H\|{\bf z}_{+}-{\bf z}\|^{1-\nu}\leq 2C_{p,\nu}H_{\nu}
, then

\displaystyle\delta<3DC_{p,\nu}{\mathcal{H}}_{p,\nu}\left(\frac{2C_{p,\nu}{\mathcal{H}}_{p.\nu}}{H}\right)^{\frac{p-1+\nu}{1-\nu}},

which implies that

\displaystyle H<\left(3\cdot 2^{p-1}D\right)^{\frac{p-1+\nu}{1-\nu}}\left(C_{p,\nu}{\mathcal{H}}_{p,\nu}\right)^{\frac{p}{1-\nu}}\left(\frac{1}{\delta}\right)^{\frac{1-\nu}{p-1+\nu}}.

This contradicts the second condition.
∎

Then we provide the main result for Algorithm

.

Theorem A.5
.

Suppose the operator
F
satisfies Assumption
2.1
,
2.3
and
A.2
and the sequence
\{{\bf z}_{k}\}_{k=0}^{K}
generated by Algorithm

satisfies

\displaystyle\max_{{\bf z}\in{\mathcal{Z}}}\left\langle F({\rm{T}}_{p}({\bf z}_{k};1,2^{i})),{\rm{T}}_{p}({\bf z}_{k};1,2^{i})-{\bf z}\right\rangle\geq\epsilon~~~\text{for}~~~i=0,\cdots,i_{k},k=0,\cdots,K.

(32)

Then we have

\displaystyle 2^{i_{k}-1}H_{k}\leq H_{p,\nu}(\epsilon):=\left(3\cdot 2^{p-1}D\right)^{\frac{p-1+\nu}{1-\nu}}\left(C_{p,\nu}{\mathcal{H}}_{p,\nu}\right)^{\frac{p}{1-\nu}}\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)^{\frac{1-\nu}{p-1+\nu}}.

for all
k=0,\cdots,T
.
We also have
m(\bar{{\bf z}}_{K})\leq{\mathcal{O}}({K^{-{(p+1)(p-1+\nu)}/{2p}}})
, where

\displaystyle\bar{{\bf z}}_{K}=\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{{\bf z}_{k+0.5}}{{\gamma_{p}({\bf z}_{k};1,H_{k}2^{i_{k}})}}({\bf z}_{k})}{\sum_{k=1}^{K}{\gamma_{p}({\bf z}_{k};1,H_{k}2^{i_{k}})}({\bf z}_{k})^{-1}}.

(33)

Besides, the total number of oracle calls to achieve an
\epsilon
-accurate solution is at most

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}\left({\epsilon^{-\frac{2p}{(p+1)(p-1+\nu)}}}\right).

Proof.

We first show that the condition (
32
) means we can always find
i
such that

\displaystyle\|F({\rm{T}}_{p}({\bf z}_{k};1,H_{k}2^{i}))-\bar{F}_{p}\left({\rm{T}}_{p}({\bf z}_{k};1,H_{k}2^{i});{\bf z}_{k}\right)\|\leq H_{k}2^{i-1}\|{\rm{T}}_{p}({\bf z}_{k};1,H_{k}2^{i}))-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{p}.

If it does not hold, the Hölder continuous assumption means

\displaystyle H_{k}2^{i-1}\|{\rm{T}}_{p}({\bf z}_{k};1,H_{k}2^{i}))-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{p}<C_{p,\nu}{\mathcal{H}}_{p,\nu}\|{\rm{T}}_{p}({\bf z}_{k};1,H_{k}2^{i}))-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{p-1+\nu},

which implies

\displaystyle\|{\rm{T}}_{p}({\bf z}_{k};1,H_{k}2^{i}))-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{1-\nu}<\frac{C_{p,\nu}{\mathcal{H}}_{p,\nu}}{H_{k}2^{i-1}}.

(34)

Since we have

\displaystyle\max_{{\bf z}\in{\mathcal{Z}}}\langle F({\rm{T}}({\bf z}_{k};1,H_{k}2^{i_{k}})),{\rm{T}}({\bf z}_{k};1,H_{k}2^{i_{k}})-{\bf z}\rangle\geq\epsilon.

Lemma
A.4
means choosing large
H_{k}2^{i}
such that

\displaystyle H_{k}2^{i}>3\cdot 2^{p-1}\left(C_{p,\nu}{\mathcal{H}}_{p,\nu}\right)^{\frac{p}{1-\nu}}\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)^{\frac{1-\nu}{p-1+\nu}}.

leads to

\displaystyle\|T_{p,1,H_{k}2^{i}}({\bf z}_{k})-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{1-\nu}\geq\frac{C_{p,\nu}{\mathcal{H}}_{p,\nu}}{H_{k}2^{i-1}},

which contradict to (
34
). This prove that our algorithm is well-defined.

Then we prove there is a unified upper bound for
H_{k}
. The steps of choosing
i_{k-1}
means

\displaystyle H_{k}2^{i_{k}-2}\|{\rm{T}}_{p}({\bf z}_{k};1,H_{k}2^{i_{k}-1})-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{p}<C_{p,\nu}{{\mathcal{H}}_{p,\nu}}\|{\rm{T}}_{p}({\bf z}_{k};1,H_{k}2^{i_{k}-1})-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{p-1+\nu},

which implies

\displaystyle\|{\rm{T}}_{p}({\bf z}_{k};1,H_{k}2^{i_{k}-1})-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{1-\nu}\leq\frac{C_{p,\nu}{\mathcal{H}}_{p,\nu}}{H_{k}2^{i_{k}-2}}.

Using Lemma
A.4
, we have

\displaystyle 2^{i_{k}-1}H_{k}\leq\left(3\cdot 2^{p-1}D\right)^{\frac{p-1+\nu}{1-\nu}}\left(C_{p,\nu}{\mathcal{H}}_{p,\nu}\right)^{\frac{p}{1-\nu}}\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)^{\frac{1-\nu}{p-1+\nu}},

which implies

\displaystyle H_{k+1}=2^{i_{k}-1}H_{k}\leq H_{p,\nu}\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)^{\frac{1-\nu}{p-1+\nu}}\overset{\text{def}}{=}H(\epsilon),

(35)

where
H_{p,\nu}:=\left(3\cdot 2^{p-1}D\right)^{\frac{p-1+\nu}{1-\nu}}\left(C_{p,\nu}{\mathcal{H}}_{p,\nu}\right)^{\frac{p}{1-\nu}}
.

Following the proof of Theorem
4.2
by taking
\nu=1
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{{\gamma_{p}({\bf z}_{k};1,H_{k}2^{i_{k}})}}\langle F({\bf z}),{\bf z}_{k+0.5}-{\bf z}\rangle+\frac{1}{4}\|{\bf z}_{k+0.5}-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{2}{\leq}\frac{1}{2}\left(\|{\bf z}_{k}-{\bf z}\|^{2}-\|{\bf z}_{k+1}-{\bf z}\|^{2}\right),\end{split}

which means

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}\|{\bf z}_{k+0.5}-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{2}\leq 2\|{\bf z}^{\prime}-{\bf z}^{*}\|^{2}.

Using Hölder’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle K

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{K}1=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left(\|{\bf z}_{k+0.5}-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{-(p-1)}\right)^{\frac{2}{p+1}}\left(\|{\bf z}_{k+0.5}-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p+1}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{k=1}^{K}\|{\bf z}_{k+0.5}-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{-(p-1)}\right)^{\frac{2}{p+1}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{K}\|{\bf z}_{k+0.5}-{\bf z}_{k}\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p+1}},

Similar to the proof of Theorem

, we bound the total number of oracle calls as

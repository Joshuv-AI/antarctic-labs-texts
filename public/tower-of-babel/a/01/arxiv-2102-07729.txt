3.6
Summary

The level of separations demonstrated in the
RapidSim
simulations should
be representative, but
RapidSim
is not
a full LHCb detector simulation. Thus in data resolutions should not
be expected to be as good as shown here.
Mass, vertex, and other constraints on the
{\mathit{\chi_{c{\rm 1}}}(3872)}
exotic meson and
D
(and
B
,
where applicable) meson decays may
modify the level of separation. Also, real backgrounds are more complex
than those presented here, and of course real

{\mathit{\chi_{c{\rm 1}}}(3872)}\to{\mathit{\mathit{D}^{\mathrm{0}}}\mathit{\kern 2.04439pt\overline{\kern-2.04439ptD}{}^{\mathrm{0}}}\pi^{0}}
and
{\mathit{\chi_{c{\rm 1}}}(3872)}\to{\mathit{\mathit{D}^{\mathrm{0}}}\mathit{\kern 2.04439pt\overline{\kern-2.04439ptD}{}^{\mathrm{0}}}\gamma}
decays may not be perfectly described by the
decay models used to generate decays for this study.

Nevertheless,
the
variables
presented here have strong potential
to help distinguish between

{\mathit{\chi_{c{\rm 1}}}(3872)}\to{\mathit{\mathit{D}^{\mathrm{0}}}\mathit{\kern 2.04439pt\overline{\kern-2.04439ptD}{}^{\mathrm{0}}}\pi^{0}}
and
{\mathit{\chi_{c{\rm 1}}}(3872)}\to{\mathit{\mathit{D}^{\mathrm{0}}}\mathit{\kern 2.04439pt\overline{\kern-2.04439ptD}{}^{\mathrm{0}}}\gamma}
decays, in experiments without sufficient calorimeter
resolution to resolve the light neutral particle (of course, information from
the light neutral itself could only be helpful).
This step is
crucial to performing quantum-correlated analyses with the resulting

{\mathit{\mathit{D}^{\mathrm{0}}}\mathit{\kern 2.04439pt\overline{\kern-2.04439ptD}{}^{\mathrm{0}}}}
systems. Separation of these decays should also be able to help
considerably in other situations where separation is desired — for example,
the determination of
{\mathit{\chi_{c{\rm 1}}}(3872)}\to{\mathit{\mathit{D}^{\mathrm{0}}}\mathit{\kern 2.04439pt\overline{\kern-2.04439ptD}{}^{\mathrm{0}}}\pi^{0}}
and
{\mathit{\chi_{c{\rm 1}}}(3872)}\to{\mathit{\mathit{D}^{\mathrm{0}}}\mathit{\kern 2.04439pt\overline{\kern-2.04439ptD}{}^{\mathrm{0}}}\gamma}

B.1
Formalism

The probability distribution

{s_{z}}_{X}

\mathcal{I(\mathbf{x})}\propto\sum_{{s_{z}}_{X}}\left|A_{{\mathit{X}}}({\mathbf{x}},{s_{z}}_{X})\right|^{2},

(35)

where

consists of the coherent sum
over all intermediate state amplitudes
A_{i}({\mathbf{x}},{s_{z}}_{X})

representing the complete set of decay chains
\mathbb{P}

A_{{\mathit{X}}}({\mathbf{x}},{s_{z}}_{X})=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{P}}A_{i}({\mathbf{x}},{s_{z}}_{X})

The intermediate state

{B_{i}}_{L}
for the initial particle
{\mathit{X}}
and resonance
R

\displaystyle B_{0}(q)

\displaystyle B_{1}(q)

\displaystyle=1/\sqrt{{1+(q\,r_{\rm BW})^{2}}},

\displaystyle=1/\sqrt{9+3\,(q\,r_{\rm BW})^{2}+(q\,r_{\rm BW})^{4}}.

The spin projection operator
P^{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{S_{R}}\nu_{1}\dots\nu_{S_{R}}}_{(S_{R})}(p_{R})

\displaystyle P_{(0)}^{\mu\nu}(p_{R})

\displaystyle=1

\displaystyle P_{(1)}^{\mu\nu}(p_{R})

\displaystyle P_{(2)}^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}(p_{R})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\,\left[P_{(1)}^{\mu\alpha}(p_{R})\,P_{(1)}^{\nu\beta}(p_{R})+P_{(1)}^{\mu\beta}(p_{R})\,P_{(1)}^{\nu\alpha}(p_{R})\right]-\frac{1}{3}\,P_{(1)}^{\mu\nu}(p_{R})\,P_{(1)}^{\alpha\beta}(p_{R})\,,

Contracted with an arbitrary tensor, the projection operator selects

\displaystyle\epsilon_{(S_{R})}(R)

\displaystyle P_{(S_{R})}(R)

\displaystyle\equiv P_{(S_{R})}(p_{R}),

\displaystyle L_{(L_{AB})}(R)

\displaystyle\equiv L_{(L_{AB})}(p_{R},q_{R}).

\displaystyle\Phi_{AB}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=P_{(S_{AB})}(R)\,\Xi(S_{AB},S_{A},S_{B})\,\epsilon^{*}_{(S_{A})}(A)\,\epsilon^{*}_{(S_{B})}(B)\,,

\Xi(J_{1},J_{2},J_{3})=\begin{cases}1&\mbox{if }J_{1}+J_{2}+J_{3}\;{\rm even}\\

Here, a polarization vector is assigned to the decaying particle

and

Abstract
The production of prompt Λ c + \rm\Lambda_{\rm c}^{+} baryons at midrapidity ( | y | < 0.5 |y|<0.5 ) was measured in central (0–10%) and mid-central (30–50%) Pb–Pb collisions at the center-of-mass energy per nucleon–nucleon pair s NN = 5.02 \sqrt{s_{\mathrm{NN}}}=5.02 TeV with the ALICE detector. The results are more precise, more differential in centrality, and reach much lower transverse momentum ( p T = 1 p_{\rm T}=1 GeV / c /c ) with respect to previous measurements performed by the ALICE, STAR, and CMS Collaborations in nucleus–nucleus collisions, allowing for an extrapolation down to p T = 0 p_{\rm T}=0 . The p T p_{\rm T} -differential Λ c + / D 0 \rm\Lambda_{\rm c}^{+}/\rm D^{0} ratio is enhanced with respect to the pp measurement for 4 < p T < 8 4<p_{\rm T}<8 GeV / c /c by 3.7 standard deviations ( σ \sigma ), while the p T p_{\rm T} -integrated ratios are compatible within 1 σ \sigma . The observed trend is similar to that observed in the strange sector for the Λ / K S 0 \Lambda/{\rm K}^{0}_{\rm{S}} ratio. Model calculations including coalescence or statistical hadronization for charm-hadron formation are compared with the data.

Introduction

Heavy-ion collisions at LHC energies produce a phase of strongly-interacting matter, known as the quark–gluon plasma (QGP), in which quarks and gluons are deconfined
[
1
]
. The existing measurements indicate that the QGP behaves as a strongly-coupled low-viscosity liquid-like system
[
2
]
. Heavy quarks, produced at the start of the collision, experience the full evolution of the system and constitute a unique probe of the QGP properties
[
3
]
. The hadronization of heavy quarks into open heavy-flavor hadrons is expected to be influenced by the presence of a deconfined medium. Theoretical calculations that include modified hadronization via quark coalescence or via a resonance recombination approach
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
]
predict a significant enhancement of the
\rm\Lambda_{\rm c}^{+}/\rm D^{0}
yield ratio in heavy-ion collisions compared to the expected ratio in pp collisions. In addition, the collective radial expansion of the system determines a flow-velocity profile common to all thermalized particles, that could increase the
\rm\Lambda_{\rm c}^{+}/\rm D^{0}
ratio at intermediate transverse momentum, i.e.
2\lesssim p_{\rm T}\lesssim 8
GeV
/c

[
7
,
10
,
8
]
. The study of such a potential enhancement requires a good understanding of
\rm\Lambda_{\rm c}^{+}
production in smaller collision systems, which showed surprising features at LHC energies. The production of
\rm\Lambda_{\rm c}^{+}
baryons was measured at the LHC in pp collisions by the ALICE Collaboration at
\sqrt{s}=5.02
, 7, and 13 TeV
[
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
]
, by the CMS Collaboration at 5.02 TeV
[
15
]
, and by the LHCb Collaboration at 7 TeV
[
16
]
. At midrapidity, the ALICE and CMS results show a significant enhancement in the
\rm\Lambda_{\rm c}^{+}/\rm D^{0}
yield ratio (up to a factor 2–5 for
p_{\rm T}<8
GeV
/c
) compared to
\mathrm{e^{+}e^{-}}
and
\mathrm{e^{-}p}

[

and QCD-inspired theoretical predictions

\mathrm{e^{+}e^{-}}

\mathrm{e^{-}p}

2
Experimental apparatus and data sample

The ALICE apparatus is described in detail in
[
40
,
41
]
. The data were collected using triggers based on the signal in the V0 detectors
[
42
]
. A minimum bias trigger, which required coincident signals in both detecting components of the V0 detector along the beam axis on opposite sides of the interaction point, was exploited. In addition, and differently with respect to the previous
Pb–Pb
data taking period at the same
\sqrt{s_{\mathrm{NN}}}
, two new trigger selections were introduced to enrich the sample of central and mid-central collisions via an online event selection based on the V0-signal amplitude. Events were further selected offline using timing information from the V0 detectors and the neutron Zero Degree Calorimeters
[
43
]
to reject events due to the interaction of one of the beams with residual gas in the vacuum tube. Furthermore, only events with a primary vertex reconstructed within
\pm 10
cm from the center of the detector along the beam axis were considered in the analysis. Collisions were classified into centrality intervals, defined in terms of percentiles of the hadronic
Pb–Pb
cross section, using the V0-signal amplitudes
[
44
]
. The number of events in the centrality classes 0–10% and 30–50% considered for this analysis is about
100\times 10^{6}
and
85\times 10^{6}
, respectively, corresponding to a luminosity of
(130.5\pm 0.5)

\mu\rm b^{\rm-1}
and
(55.5\pm 0.2)

\mu\rm b^{\rm-1}

[
45
]
.

The Monte Carlo (MC) simulations utilized in this analysis were obtained using the HIJING 1.36 event generator
[
46
]
to simulate
Pb–Pb
collisions at
\sqrt{s_{\mathrm{NN}}}=5.02
TeV. In each simulated event,
\rm\Lambda_{\rm c}^{+}
signals were added by injecting
\rm c\overline{c}
or
\rm b\overline{b}
pairs generated with the PYTHIA 8.243 event generator
[
23
]
with the Monash tune
[
47
]
. The
\rm\Lambda_{\rm c}^{+}
baryons were forced to decay into the hadronic decay channel of interest,
\rm\Lambda_{\rm c}^{+}\to pK^{0}_{\rm S}
followed by
\rm K^{0}_{\rm S}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
, using PYTHIA. All generated particles were transported through the ALICE detector using the GEANT3 package
[
48
]
. The conditions of all the ALICE detectors in terms of active channels, gain, noise level, and alignment, and their evolution with time during the data taking, were taken into account in the simulations.

3
Data analysis

The
\rm\Lambda_{\rm c}^{+}
baryon and its charge conjugate were reconstructed by exploiting the topology of the hadronic decay channel
\rm\Lambda_{\rm c}^{+}\to pK^{0}_{\rm S}
(branching ratio
{\rm BR}=1.59\pm 0.08\%
), followed by the subsequent decay
\rm K^{0}_{\rm S}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
(
{\rm BR}=69.20\pm 0.05\%
)
[
28
]
. Charged-particle tracks used to define the
\rm\Lambda_{\rm c}^{+}
candidates are reconstructed using the Inner Tracking System (ITS)
[
49
]
and the Time Projection Chamber (TPC)
[
50
]
, located in a solenoid magnet that provides a
0.5
T field parallel to the beam direction. The
\rm\Lambda_{\rm c}^{+}\to pK^{0}_{\rm S}
candidates combine a proton-candidate track with a
\rm K^{0}_{\rm S}
-meson candidate, reconstructed in the
\rm K^{0}_{\rm S}\to\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
decay channel. Only proton (pion) tracks with
|\eta|<0.8
,
p_{\rm T}>0.4\ (0.1)
GeV
/c
, at least 70 out of 159 associated crossed TPC pad rows, a ratio of crossed rows to findable clusters in the TPC larger than 0.8, at least 50 clusters in the TPC available for particle identification (PID), and a
\chi^{2}/{\rm ndf}<1.25
in the TPC (where
\rm ndf
is the number of degrees of freedom involved in the track fit procedure) were considered for the analysis. Moreover, a minimum number of two hits (out of six) in the ITS, with at least one in the inner two layers, were required for the proton track. The selection of tracks with
|\eta|<0.8
limits the
\rm\Lambda_{\rm c}^{+}
acceptance in rapidity. For this reason a fiducial acceptance selection was applied on the rapidity of the
\rm\Lambda_{\rm c}^{+}
candidates,
|y_{\mathrm{lab}}|<y_{\mathrm{fid}}(p_{\rm T})
, where
y_{\mathrm{fid}}
increases from 0.6 to 0.8 in
1<p_{\rm T}<5
GeV
/c
, and
y_{\mathrm{fid}}=0.8
for
p_{\rm T}>5
GeV
/c
.

Unlike the previous analysis based on linear selections
[
33
]
, the
\rm\Lambda_{\rm c}^{+}
-candidate selection was performed using multivariate techniques based on the Boosted Decision Tree (BDT) algorithm provided by the XGBoost package
[
51
]
. Before the training, loose kinematic and topological selections were applied to the
{\rm K}^{0}_{\rm{S}}
-meson candidate together with the particle identification of the proton-candidate track. The PID was performed using the specific ionization energy loss
\textrm{d}E/\textrm{d}x
in the TPC gas and the time of flight from the interaction point to the Time-Of-Flight (TOF) detector
[
52
,
53
]
. The BDT training was performed considering as signal candidates prompt (not coming from beauty-hadron decays)
\rm\Lambda_{\rm c}^{+}
decays from MC simulations. Background candidates were taken from the sidebands of the invariant mass distribution in data (defined to be outside a 80 MeV
/c^{2}
window around the
\rm\Lambda_{\rm c}^{+}
mass value reported by the PDG
[
28
]
).

The variables that were most important in the training were the PID-related variables of the proton-candidate track, the displacement of the proton-candidate track from the primary vertex, the distance between the
{\rm K}^{0}_{\rm{S}}
-meson decay vertex and the primary vertex, and the cosine of the pointing angle between the
{\rm K}^{0}_{\rm{S}}
-meson candidate line of flight and its reconstructed momentum vector. Independent BDTs were trained for the different
p_{\rm T}
and centrality intervals.

The selection on the BDT output was tuned in each
p_{\rm T}
interval to maximize the expected statistical significance, which is estimated using i) the expected signal obtained from FONLL calculations
[
54
,
55
]
scaled by the corresponding
\langle T_{\rm AA}\rangle

[
45
]
and multiplied by the BDT selection efficiency and ii) the expected background estimated from an invariant mass sideband fit using a fraction of the data.

After applying selections on the BDT output, the yields of
\rm\Lambda_{\rm c}^{+}
baryons were extracted in each
p_{\rm T}
interval via binned maximum-likelihood fits to the candidate invariant mass distributions. The fitting function consisted of a Gaussian term to estimate the signal and a second-, third-, or fourth-order polynomial function (depending on the
p_{\rm T}
interval) to estimate the background. The default background fitting function was chosen after dedicated studies to obtain a good description of the invariant mass distribution in the sidebands. The other functions were considered for evaluating the systematic uncertainty.

The raw-yield extraction is challenging, especially at low
p_{\rm T}
with signal-to-background ratios below one per mille and relative statistical uncertainties on the extracted raw yield varying between 15–35%, as presented in Appendix
A
. Given the critical signal extraction due to the low signal-to-background ratios, the width of the Gaussian term for the signal was fixed to the value obtained from simulations. It was verified that the widths from the simulation were consistent within uncertainties to those extracted from fits to data without constraints on the width of the Gaussian (with a relative uncertainty of 1–2% in simulation and 20–30% in data). In addition, the stability of the signal extraction was further verified by i) fitting purely background candidates from simulations and ii) by repeating the fit after subtracting a background component estimated with an event-mixing technique. For the latter, the events were grouped in pools based on the primary-vertex position along
z
and the estimated centrality. For the first study, none of the invariant mass fits allowed to extract a signal in the
\rm\Lambda_{\rm c}^{+}
invariant mass region. For the second study, fits to the background-subtracted invariant mass distributions resulted in compatible
\rm\Lambda_{\rm c}^{+}
raw yields to the ones extracted from the default fits.

The corrected yields of prompt
\rm\Lambda_{\rm c}^{+}
baryons were obtained in each centrality interval as

\left.\frac{{\rm d}N^{\rm\Lambda_{\rm c}^{+}}}{{\rm d}p_{\rm T}}\right|_{|y|<0.5}=\frac{f_{\rm{prompt}}\times\frac{1}{2}\left.N^{\rm\rm\Lambda_{\rm c}^{\pm}}_{\rm raw}\right|_{|y|<y_{\rm fid}}}{\Delta p_{\rm T}\times c_{\Delta y}\times(A\times\epsilon)_{\rm prompt}\times{\rm BR}\times N_{\rm ev}}.

(1)

The raw yield values
N^{\rm\Lambda_{\rm c}^{\pm}}_{\rm raw}
, extracted in a given
p_{\rm T}
interval of width
\Delta p_{\rm T}
, were divided by a factor two and multiplied by the prompt fraction
f_{\rm prompt}
to obtain the charge-averaged yields of prompt
\rm\Lambda_{\rm c}^{+}
. Furthermore, they were divided by
c_{\Delta y}\times(A\times\epsilon)
, enclosing the rapidity coverage and the acceptance-times-efficiency, by the
\rm BR
of the decay channel, and by the number of analyzed events
N_{\rm ev}
.

The
(A\times\epsilon)
correction was determined from MC simulations, using samples not employed in the BDT training. The generated
p_{\rm T}
spectrum used to calculate the efficiencies was reweighted to reproduce the shape obtained from the
\rm D^{0}
measurement
[
56
]
multiplied by
\rm\Lambda_{\rm c}^{+}/\rm D^{0}
calculations from the TAMU model
[
8
]
in 0–10% and 30–50%
Pb–Pb
collisions at
\sqrt{s_{\mathrm{NN}}}=5.02
TeV. The
(A\times\epsilon)
increases from 1% (3%) at low
p_{\rm T}
to about 12% (16%) at high
p_{\rm T}
for central (mid-central) collisions. The correction factor for the rapidity acceptance,
c_{\Delta y}
, was computed as the ratio between the generated
\rm\Lambda_{\rm c}^{+}
-baryon yield in
\Delta y=2y_{\rm fid}(p_{\rm T})
and that in
|y|<0.5
using the reweighted
p_{\rm T}
shape and the rapidity distribution from PYTHIA 8 simulations
[
23
]
. It was verified in
[
56
]
that for
{\rm D}
mesons the calculation of
c_{\Delta y}
is only weakly sensitive to the rapidity distribution used for its calculation.

The
f_{\rm prompt}
fraction of the reconstructed signal was estimated using a similar strategy as described in
[
33
]
. In particular, the beauty-hadron production cross section was estimated with FONLL calculations
[
54
,
55
]
, the fraction of beauty quarks that fragment into
\rm\Lambda_{\rm b}^{0}
was estimated from the
\rm\Lambda_{\rm b}^{0}/(\rm B^{0}+\rm B^{+})
ratio measured by LHCb in pp collisions at
\sqrt{s}=13
TeV
[
57
]
following the same strategy as used in
[
11
]
, and the kinematics of the decay of beauty hadrons
H_{\rm b}\rightarrow\rm\Lambda_{\rm c}^{+}+X
simulated with PYTHIA 8
[
23
]
. The branching ratios were taken as implemented in PYTHIA 8.243, corresponding to approximately 82% for
\rm\Lambda_{\rm b}^{0}
baryons and 2% for either
\rm B^{0}
,
\rm B^{+}
, and
\rm B^{+}_{\rm s}
mesons. In addition, the
f_{\rm prompt}
fraction is modified to account for the nuclear modification factor of
\rm\Lambda_{\rm c}^{+}
baryons from beauty-hadron decays. The central correction is chosen such that
R^{\rm non\text{-}prompt}_{\mathrm{AA}}=2\times R^{\rm prompt}_{\mathrm{AA}}
as predicted by the “Catania” theoretical calculation
[
6
]
. The resulting
f_{\rm prompt}
fraction was found to be about 0.97 at low
p_{\rm T}
and about 0.81 at high
p_{\rm T}
.

The systematic uncertainty of the raw-yield extraction was estimated by repeating the invariant mass fits varying the lower and upper limits of the fit range, the functional form of the background fit function, and considering the Gaussian width (mean) as a free (fixed) parameter in the fit. In order to test the sensitivity to the line shape of the signal, a bin-counting method was used, in which the signal yield was obtained by integrating the invariant-mass distribution after subtracting the background estimated from the sideband fit, as well as by studying the signal shape in the MC simulations using multiple stacked Gaussian functions rather than a single one.
The procedure to estimate the systematic uncertainty of the track-reconstruction efficiency includes variations of the track-quality selection criteria for all decay tracks and studies on the probability to match TPC tracks to the ITS clusters in data and simulation for the proton-candidate track. The latter comparison was performed after weighting the relative abundances of primary and secondary particles in simulation to those in data
[
33
]
. The systematic uncertainty of the
\rm\Lambda_{\rm c}^{+}
selection efficiency was estimated by repeating the analysis with different selections on the BDT output, resulting in up to 50% lower and 20–50% higher efficiency values. Possible systematic effects due to the loose PID selection, applied prior to the BDT one, were investigated by comparing the PID-selection efficiencies in data and in simulations and found to be negligible. Both the tracking- and PID-efficiency studies were performed using pure samples of pions (from
{\rm K}^{0}_{\rm{S}}
decays) and protons (from
\Lambda
decays).
An additional contribution derives from the
p_{\rm T}
spectra of
\rm\Lambda_{\rm c}^{+}
generated in the simulation, which was estimated by using the
\rm\Lambda_{\rm c}^{+}/\rm D^{0}
predictions of the Catania model
[
7
]
and the SHMc
[
10
]
instead of the TAMU prediction
[
8
]
in the
p_{\rm T}
-shape reweighting procedure, as well as by an iterative method using a parametrization of the measured
p_{\rm T}
-differential production yields.
Finally, the systematic uncertainty of the feed-down subtraction was estimated by varying the FONLL parameters as prescribed in
[
55
]
and the function describing the
\rm\Lambda_{\rm b}^{0}
fragmentation fraction within the quoted experimental uncertainty as reported in
[
11
]
, as well as by varying the hypothesis on
R^{\rm non\text{-}prompt}_{\mathrm{AA}}
. For the latter, an interval
1/3<R^{\rm non\text{-}prompt}_{\mathrm{AA}}/R^{\rm prompt}_{\mathrm{AA}}<3
was considered, wider with respect to that used for non-strange D mesons
[
56
]
to cover possible yet unmeasured differences between the modification of charm- and beauty-baryon production in
Pb–Pb
collisions with respect to the one in pp collisions.

The sources of systematic uncertainty considered in this analysis are assumed to be uncorrelated among each other and the total systematic uncertainty in each
p_{\rm T}
and centrality interval is calculated as the quadratic sum of the individual uncertainties. For the
\rm\Lambda_{\rm c}^{+}/\rm D^{0}
ratio, the
\rm\Lambda_{\rm c}^{+}
and
\rm D^{0}
uncertainties were considered as uncorrelated except for the tracking efficiency and the feed-down contribution, which are assumed correlated and thus partially cancel in the ratio, and the systematic uncertainty of the centrality interval definition, which fully cancels. For the
R_{\mathrm{AA}}
, the pp and
Pb–Pb
uncertainties were considered as uncorrelated except for the branching ratio uncertainty and the feed-down contribution, which both partially cancel out (the former because the pp measurement considers additional decay modes). Finally, in case of the
p_{\rm T}
-integrated
\rm\Lambda_{\rm c}^{+}/\rm D^{0}
ratio, there is a correlation between the extrapolation uncertainty of the
\rm\Lambda_{\rm c}^{+}
baryon and the measured uncertainties of the
\rm\Lambda_{\rm c}^{+}
and
\rm D^{0}
hadrons. To treat this correlation, the extrapolation uncertainty is divided into a correlated part (estimated as the extrapolation uncertainty when considering only the shape predicted by TAMU) and an uncorrelated part (the total extrapolation uncertainty subtracting the correlated part) with respect to the measured uncertainties. The uncorrelated part is summed in quadrature with the measured uncertainties, while the correlated part is added linearly.

[

assumes that a QGP is formed in both pp and

Pb–Pb

Pb–Pb
collisions hadronization occurs either via coalescence, implemented through the Wigner formalism, or via fragmentation in case the quarks do not undergo coalescence. The TAMU model
[
8
]
describes charm-quark transport in an expanding medium with the Langevin equation and hadronization proceeds primarily via coalescence, implemented with a Resonance Recombination Model (RRM)
[
62
]

[
31

[
28
]
predicted by the Relativistic Quark Model (RQM)
[
63

[
31
]

[
10
]

The core contribution represents the central region of the colliding nuclei where charm quarks achieve local thermal equilibrium in a hydrodynamically expanding QGP. The charm-hadron spectra in the corona contribution are, instead, parameterized from measurements in pp collisions. The
p_{\rm T}
-spectra modification due to resonance decays is computed using the FastReso package
[
64
]
. The theoretical uncertainty bands shown in Fig.

5
Conclusions

In summary, the measurements of the production yield of prompt
\rm\Lambda_{\rm c}^{+}
baryons in central (0–10%) and mid-central (30–50%)
Pb–Pb
collisions at a center-of-mass energy per nucleon pair
\sqrt{s_{\mathrm{NN}}}=5.02
TeV were presented. The yield could be extrapolated to
p_{\rm T}=0
in the two centrality classes with significantly smaller uncertainties than the previous measurement by STAR in 10–80%
Au–Au
collisions at
\sqrt{s_{\mathrm{NN}}}=200
GeV, exploring not only a new energy regime but also higher multiplicities. The
p_{\rm T}
-differential
\rm\Lambda_{\rm c}^{+}/\rm D^{0}
ratios increase from pp to central
Pb–Pb
collisions for
4<p_{\rm T}<8
GeV
/c
with a significance of
3.7
standard deviations, while the
p_{\rm T}
-integrated ratios are compatible within one standard deviation. Both observations are in qualitative agreement with the baryon-to-meson ratio for strange hadrons. The measurements are described by theoretical calculations that include both coalescence and fragmentation processes when describing the hadronization of heavy flavors in the QGP. The upgraded ALICE detector for the LHC Runs 3 and 4 will increase its acquisition rate by up to a factor of about 50 in
Pb–Pb
collisions and the tracking precision by a factor 3–6, meaning future measurements of
\rm\Lambda_{\rm c}^{+}
-baryon production will allow for stronger constraints on the heavy-quark hadronization mechanisms in heavy-ion collisions
[
68
]
.

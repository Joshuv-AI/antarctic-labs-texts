Bounds on the minimum sound speed above neutron star densities
Dake Zhou

Abstract
We show that the existence of massive neutron stars and asymptotic freedom of QCD place robust upper bounds on the lowest sound speed of the ultra-dense matter unattainable in neutron stars. Centered on worst-case scenarios, our limits are the most conservative among physical equations of state in the density range ∼ 2 − 40 ​ n 0 \sim 2-40n_{0} . Discovery of ≳ 2.6 ​ M ⊙ \gtrsim 2.6M_{\odot} neutron stars, in combination with current multimessenger astrophysical constraints on the equation of state, would strongly support first-order phase transitions at high baryon densities.

Stronger limits on

Here, we extend these previously-known results to account for the effect of

Above an arbitrary point in the EOS,
the highest pressure at any density, or equivalently the lowest pressure at any chemical potential, is given by the maximally stiff EOS

5

The opposite limit, namely the lowest (highest) pressure at any density (chemical potential), follows from the so-called maximally soft EOS.
For the purpose of constraining the EOS between TOV and pQCD points,

C_{s}(n_{B})=\begin{cases}C_{s,\mathrm{min}},\quad n_{\text{TOV}}\leq n_{B}\leq n_{1};\\
C_{s,\mathrm{max}},\quad n_{1}<n_{B}\leq n_{\text{pQCD}},\end{cases}

(6)

1

Appendix A
A proof of the mean value bound
eq.

3

The speed of sound squared
C_{s}(n)
in thermodynamically stable matter may contain an arbitrary number of discontinuities or non-differentiable singularities, but must be an integrable function, whose integral yields the chemical potential. Therefore, by the fundamental theorem of calculus,

\displaystyle\log\mu(n_{H})-\log\mu(n_{L})

\displaystyle=\int_{\log n_{L}}^{\log n_{H}}C_{s}(n)\mathrm{d}\log n

(14)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\log n_{L}}^{\log n_{H}}\min\left\{C_{s}(n)\right\}\mathrm{d}\log n

\displaystyle=(\log n_{H}-\log n_{L})\min\left\{C_{s}(n)\right\},

from which the first half of
eq.

3
follows:

C_{s,\mathrm{min}}\equiv\min\left\{C_{s}(n)\right\}\leq\frac{\log\mu(n_{H})-\log\mu(n_{L})}{\log n_{H}-\log n_{L}}\equiv C_{s,\mathrm{mean}}.

(15)

The proof for the second inequality in
eq.

3
follows similarly.

Next we discuss the procedure to find

12

\beta=1/C_{s,\mathrm{min}}

As discussed in the main text,

Moduli of vector bundles on primitive multiple schemes
Jean–Marc Drézet

Abstract
Resume. A primitive multiple scheme is a Cohen-Macaulay scheme Y Y such that the associated reduced scheme X = Y r ​ e ​ d X=Y_{red} is smooth, irreducible, and that Y Y can be locally embedded in a smooth variety of dimension dim ( X ) + 1 \dim(X)+1 . If n n is the multiplicity of Y Y , there is a canonical filtration X = X 1 ⊂ X 2 ⊂ ⋯ ⊂ X n = Y X=X_{1}\subset X_{2}\subset\cdots\subset X_{n}=Y , such that X i X_{i} is a primitive multiple scheme of multiplicity i i . The simplest example is the trivial primitive multiple scheme of multiplicity n n associated to a line bundle L L on X X : it is the n n -th infinitesimal neighborhood of X X , embedded in the line bundle L ∗ L^{*} by the zero section. The main subject of this paper is the construction and properties of fine moduli spaces of vector bundles on primitive multiple schemes. Suppose that Y = X n Y=X_{n} is of multiplicity n n , and can be extended to X n + 1 X_{n+1} of multiplicity n + 1 n+1 , and let M n M_{n} be a fine moduli space of vector bundles on X n X_{n} . With suitable hypotheses, we construct a fine moduli space M n + 1 M_{n+1} for the vector bundles on X n + 1 X_{n+1} whose restriction to X n X_{n} belongs to M n M_{n} . It is an affine bundle over the subvariety N n ⊂ M n N_{n}\subset M_{n} of bundles that can be extended to X n + 1 X_{n+1} . In general this affine bundle is not banal. This applies in particular to Picard groups. We give also many new examples of primitive multiple schemes Y Y such that the dualizing sheaf ω Y \omega_{Y} is trivial.

3.

Theorem :
Suppose that

hyperelliptic,

.
Then
{\bf M}_{Y}(r,-1)
is not a vector bundle over

.

Proof.

Let
E
be a stable vector bundle on
C
of rank
r
and degree
0
and
x\in C
. Then for every non zero linear form
\varphi:E_{x}\to{\mathbb{C}}
, the kernel of
the induced morphism
\tilde{\varphi}:E\to{\mathcal{O}}_{x}

let
F\subset\ker(\tilde{\varphi})
be a subbundle such that

; then since
F\subset E
and
E
is stable, we have

\mathop{\rm deg}\nolimits(F)<0
. Hence
\displaystyle\frac{\mathop{\rm deg}\nolimits(F)}{\mathop{\rm rk}\nolimits(F)}<-\frac{1}{r}
.

Let
{\mathbb{P}}={\mathbb{P}}(E^{*})
and
\pi:{\mathbb{P}}\to C
,
p_{\mathbb{P}}:{\mathbb{P}}\times C\to C
,

p_{C}:{\mathbb{P}}\times C\to C
the projections. Let
\Delta\subset C\times C
be the diagonal. We have a canonical obvious surjective morphism on

{\mathbb{P}}\times C

{\bf p}:p_{C}^{*}(E)\otimes p_{\mathbb{P}}^{*}({\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}}(-1))\longrightarrow{\mathcal{O}}_{(\pi\times p_{C})^{-1}(\Delta)}\ .

x\in C

\varphi\in E_{x}^{*}
,
{\bf p}_{{\mathbb{C}}\varphi}

is the morphism
E\otimes{\mathbb{C}}\varphi\to{\mathcal{O}}_{x}
induced by
\varphi
. The kernel of

\bf p
is a vector bundle
{\mathbb{E}}
on
{\mathbb{P}}\times C
that is a family of stable
vector bundles on
C
, of rank
r
and degree
-1
, parametrized by
{\mathbb{P}}
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}
be a universal bundle on
M(r,-1)\times C
.
Since
r\geq 2
,
E^{*}
has a rank 1 subbundle
D
, inducing a section
s

of
\pi
. Then
{\mathbb{F}}=s^{*}({\mathbb{E}})
is a family of stable vector bundles on
C
, of
rank
r
and degree
-1
, parametrized by
C
. Hence there exists a morphism

\tau:C\to M(r,-1)
and a line bundle
H

C
such that
{\mathbb{F}}\simeq(\tau\times I_{C})^{*}({\mathcal{E}})\otimes q^{*}(H)

∎

Let

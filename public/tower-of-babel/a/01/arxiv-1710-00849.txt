An implicit algorithm for finding a fixed point of a Q Q -nonexpansive mapping in locally convex spaces
Ebrahim Soori, M. R. Omidi, Ali Farajzadeh, Yuanheng Wang

Abstract
. Suppose that Q Q is a family of seminorms on a locally convex space E E which determines the topology of E E . In this paper, first we define the notation of the q q -duality mappings in locally convex spaces. Then we introduce an implicit method for finding an element of the set of fixed points of a Q Q -nonexpansive mapping. Then we prove the convergence of the proposed implicit scheme to a fixed point of the Q Q -nonexpansive mapping in τ Q \tau_{Q} .

Lemma

and

.
Then

converges to a point

and for each

q(x_{n}-v)\leq\phi_{q}(x_{n})-\phi_{q}(v)

for all

n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

Proof.

Since
q(x_{n}-x_{n+1})\leq\phi_{q}(x_{n})-\phi_{q}(x_{n+1})
, for each

and

,
then we have
\{\phi_{q}(x_{n})\}
is a decreasing sequence for each

. Moreover, for

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{m}q(x_{n}-x_{n+1})

\displaystyle=q(x_{0}-x_{1})+q(x_{1}-x_{2})+\ldots+q(x_{m}-x_{m+1})

\displaystyle\leq\phi_{q}(x_{0})-\phi_{q}(x_{m+1})

\displaystyle\leq\phi_{q}(x_{0})-\inf_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\phi_{q}(x_{n}).

Letting

, we have

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}q(x_{n}-x_{n+1})<\infty,

for each

, then
\displaystyle\lim_{n}q(x_{n}-x_{n+1})=0
for each

. This implies that

is a left Cauchy sequence in

. Let

Theorem
3.2
.

(Banach
Q
-Contraction Principle)
Suppose that
Q
is a family of seminorms on a separated and complete locally
convex space
E
which determines the topology of
E

and
T:E\rightarrow E
a
Q
-contraction mapping with Lipschitz constant

k\in(0,1)
. Then we have the following:

(a)

There exists a unique fixed point
v\in E
.

(b)

For arbitrary
x_{0}\in E
, the Picard iteration process defined by

x_{n+1}=T({x_{n}}),n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

converges to
v
.

(c)

q(x_{n}-v)\leq(1-k)^{-1}k^{n}q(x_{0}-x_{1})
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
q\in Q
.

Proof.

(a) For each
q\in Q
, let
\phi_{q}:E\rightarrow\mathbb{R^{+}}
be a functions defined by

\displaystyle\phi_{q}(x)=(1-k)^{-1}q(x-Tx),

for each
x\in E
. From Theorem 1.4 in
[
3
]
, since each
q
is continuous,
\phi_{q}
is also a continuous function.
From the fact that
T
is a
Q
-contraction mapping, for each
q\in Q
we have

(3.1)

q(Tx-T^{2}x)\leq kq(x-Tx),x\in E,

which conclude that

\displaystyle q(x-Tx)-kq(x-Tx)\leq q(x-Tx)-q(Tx-T^{2}x).

Hence

\displaystyle q(x-Tx)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{1-k}[q(x-Tx)-q(Tx-T^{2}x)]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{1-k}[q(x-Tx)-q(Tx-T^{2}x)],

for each
q\in Q
, therefore

(3.2)

\displaystyle q(x-Tx)\leq\phi_{q}(x)-\phi_{q}(Tx).

Consider an arbitrary element
x
in
X
and define the sequence
{x_{n}}
in
E
by

x_{n}=T^{n}x,n\in\mathbb{N}
.
From (
3.2
), we have

q(x_{n}-x_{n+1})\leq\phi_{q}(x_{n})-\phi_{q}(x_{n+1}),n\in\mathbb{N},

for each
q\in Q
and since
E
is Hausdorff, it follows from Lemma
3.1
that there exists an element
v\in E
such that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}x_{n}=v,

and

(3.3)

q(x_{n}-v)\leq\phi_{q}(x_{n}),n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

for each
q\in Q
.
Since for example, from page 3 in
[
3
]
,
T
is continuous and
x_{n+1}=Tx_{n}
, it follows that

. Suppose that

is
another fixed point for

. If for each
q\in Q
we have
q(v-z)=0
, then from the fact that

is separated, we have

. On the other hands, let

0<q(v-z)=q(Tv-Tz)\leq kq(v-z)<q(v-z),

that is a contradiction. Hence

.

(b) This assertion follows from part (a).

(c) From (
3.1
) we have that
\phi_{q}(x_{n})\leq k^{n}\phi_{q}(x_{0})
for each
q\in Q
. This implies from (

) that

q(x_{n}-v)\leq k^{n}\phi_{q}(x_{0})
for each
q\in Q
.
∎

\displaystyle\frac{2}{1-\beta}\limsup_{n}\langle x-Px\,,\,J_{q}(z_{n}-Px)\rangle\leq 0,

for each
q\in Q
. That is
z_{n}\rightarrow Px
in
\tau_{Q}
.
∎

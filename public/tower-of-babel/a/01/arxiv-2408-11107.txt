Bounds on MLDR Codes Over ℤ p t \mathbb{Z}_{p^{t}}
Tim L. Alderson

Abstract
Upper bounds on the minimum Lee distance of codes that are linear over ℤ q \mathbb{Z}_{q} , q = p t q=p^{t} , p p prime are discussed. The bounds are Singleton like, depending on the length, rank, and alphabet size of the code. Codes meeting such bounds are referred to as Maximum Lee Distance with respect to Rank (MLDR) Codes. We present some new bounds on MLDR codes, using combinatorial arguments. In the context of MLDR codes, our work provides improvements over existing bounds in the literature. Introduction

The Lee metric, introduced by Lee in 1958 as an alternative to the Hamming metric
[
16
]
, was initially applied to various noisy communication channels, particularly those employing phase-shift keying modulation
[
18
]
. Over time, codes designed for the Lee metric have found applications in constrained and partial-response channels
[
7
]
, interleaving schemes
[
5
]
, orthogonal frequency-division multiplexing (OFDM)
[
20
]
, multidimensional burst-error correction
[
13
]
, and error correction for flash memories
[
3
]
. The study of optimal Lee codes has gained increasing attention, driven by these diverse applications.

As with the Hamming metric, a fundamental problem is determining upper bounds on
d_{L}({\mathcal{C}})
, the minimum Lee distance of a code
{\mathcal{C}}
with fixed size, length, and alphabet. One of the earliest such bounds was established by Wyner and Graham
[
25
]
. Later, Chiang and Wolf
[
9
]
provided a bound on free codes that, in many cases, improved upon that of Wyner and Graham. This bound was subsequently shown to hold for (not necessarily linear) codes of integral
p^{t}
-type in
[
1
]
. More recently, Byrne and Weger
[
8
]
extended the Chiang–Wolf bound to the setting of (not necessarily free) linear codes over the integer residue ring
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{t}}
, where
p
is prime.

In this work, we present new bounds on the minimum Lee distance of linear codes of fixed rank and length over
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{t}}
. These bounds not only generalize existing results but also provide improvements over each of the aforementioned bounds.

Preliminaries

2.1
MDS Codes and Singleton Defect

For
n\geq k
, an
(n,k,d)_{q}
code
{\mathcal{C}}
is a collection of
q^{k}

n
-tuples (or
codewords
) over an alphabet
A
of size
q

such that the minimum (Hamming) distance between any two codewords
of
{\mathcal{C}}
is
d
. In
the special case that
A=\mathbb{F}_{q}
(the finite field of order
q
) and

{\mathcal{C}}
is a vector space of dimension
k
,
{\mathcal{C}}
is a (classical)
linear

[n,k,d]_{q}
-code. Without reference to minimum distance, a
q
-nary linear code of length
n
and dimension
k
is denoted an
[n,k]_{q}
-code.

The Singleton bound
[
23
]
dictates that an
(n,k,d)_{q}
code
satisfies
d\leq n-k+1
. Codes meeting the Singleton bound are called maximum distance separable (MDS) codes. The maximum length of a linear
[n,k]_{q}
-MDS code is denoted
m(k,q)
.
In 1952, Bush
[
6
]
established that if
k\geq q
then
m(k,q)=k+1
. For
k<q
there is a long standing conjecture regarding linear MDS codes: Every linear
[n,k,n-k+1]_{q}
MDS code with
1<k<q
satisfies
n\leq q+1
, except when
q
is even and
k=3
or
k=q-1
in which case
n\leq q+2
. This conjecture is called the Main Conjecture on Linear MDS Codes, and has been shown to hold in many cases, (see e.g.
[
2
]
).

The
Singleton defect
of an

(n,k,d)_{q}
code is

\text{def}({\mathcal{C}})=n-k+1-d.

An
(n,k,d)_{q}
code
{\mathcal{C}}
with

\text{def}({\mathcal{C}})=0
is therefore an MDS code. Codes of Singleton defect
1
are called Almost-MDS
(AMDS) codes
[
10
]
, and more generally, those of Singleton defect
s
are denoted A
s
MDS codes.

A fundamental problem in coding theory is that of determining the
maximum length of a code with
k
,
q
, and
\text{def}({\mathcal{C}})
fixed.

The study of MDS codes, is a major research area. In the classical setting, several properties have been established. In particular, the recent work of Ball
[
2
]
established that for
p
prime, linear MDS codes of dimension
k<p
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
have length at most
p+1
, and attain this bound if and only if the code corresponds to a normal rational curve in
PG(k-1,q)
. Consequently, the bound is only attained by codes with a generator matrix equivalent to the matrix with columns

S=\{(1,t,t^{2},\ldots,t^{k-1})\mid t\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\}\cup\{(0,0,\ldots,0,1)\}.

More generally, if
{\mathcal{C}}
is a linear
[n,2]_{q}
-code with generator matrix
G
, then
{\mathcal{C}}
has a codeword of weight
n-\alpha
if and only if there exists a subset,
S
of
\alpha
columns of
G
that are pairwise linearly dependent, and
S
is maximal (in that the union of
S
with any further column of
G
no longer has this property). It follows that if def
({\mathcal{C}})=s
then
n\leq(q+1)(s+1)
. Inductively, for
k\geq 2
, a linear
[n,k,d]_{q}
code
{\mathcal{C}}
with
\text{def}({\mathcal{C}})=\delta
satisfies

k+\delta\leq n\leq(\delta+1)(q+1)+k-2.

(1)

In what follows we investigate optimality conditions analogous to the Singleton bound, in the setting of the Lee metric for linear codes over the integer residue ring
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
, where
q=p^{t}
for some prime
p
.

2.2
Linear Codes Over
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
,
q=p^{t}

Linear codes over
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
,
q=p^{t}
are sub-modules over the integer residue ring
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
.
A
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
module
{\mathcal{C}}
of
\left(\mathbb{Z}_{q}\right)^{n}
is called a linear code of length
n
.
An analogue of the (classical) dimension is the
p^{t}
-type of the code, defined by:

\kappa=\log_{p^{t}}(|{\mathcal{C}}|).

(2)

From the basic theory, any
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{t}}
module
{\mathcal{C}}
satisfies an isomorphism of the form

{\mathcal{C}}\cong\left(\mathbb{Z}_{p^{t}}\right)^{k_{1}}\times\left(\mathbb{Z}_{p^{t-1}}\right)^{k_{2}}\times\cdots\times(\mathbb{Z}_{p})^{k_{t}},

(3)

where the parameter
k_{1}
is called the free rank of the code and
K=\sum_{i=1}^{s}k_{i}
is called its rank. If
K=k_{1}
then the code is said to be free.
As in the classical case, if
{\mathcal{C}}\subseteq\left(\mathbb{Z}_{p^{t}}\right)^{n}
is a linear code then we call a matrix
G
a generator of
{\mathcal{C}}
if its row-span is
{\mathcal{C}}
.
It is helpful to consider these matrices in their systematic form.

If
{\mathcal{C}}\subseteq\left(\mathbb{Z}_{p^{t}}\right)^{n}
is a linear code as in (
3
), with rank
K
, then
{\mathcal{C}}
is equivalent (up to a permutation of coordinate positions) to a code having the following systematic generator matrix
G\in\left(\mathbb{Z}_{p^{t}}\right)^{K\times n}

G=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccc}I_{k_{1}}&A_{1,2}&A_{1,3}&\cdots&A_{1,t}&A_{1,t+1}\\
0&pI_{k_{2}}&pA_{2,3}&\cdots&pA_{2,t}&pA_{2,s+1}\\
0&0&p^{2}I_{k_{3}}&\cdots&p^{2}A_{3,t}&p^{2}A_{3,t+1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\cdots&p^{t-1}I_{k_{t}}&p^{t-1}A_{t,t+1}\end{array}\right),

where
A_{i,t+1}\in\left(\mathbb{Z}_{p^{t+1-i}}\right)^{k_{i}\times(n-K)},A_{i,j}\in\left(\mathbb{Z}_{p^{t+1-i}}\right)^{k_{i}\times k_{j}}
for
j\leq t
, and
I_{k_{\alpha}}
denotes the
k_{\alpha}\times k_{\alpha}
identity matrix.

The socle of a linear code is the sum of its minimal sub-modules; hence, in the current context, the socle of
{\mathcal{C}}
is
S({\mathcal{C}})=\left\langle p^{t-1}\right\rangle\cap{\mathcal{C}}
. In an unpublished manuscript
[
14
]
, Horimoto and Shiromoto establish that a linear code has the same length, rank, and minimum distance as its socle; the same result appears in more contemporary works (see e.g.
[
15
]
).

In the setting of linear codes over
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{t}}
we have the following analogue of the Singleton bound, (see e.g.
[
12
]
).

Lemma 1

(Singleton Bound for Linear Codes over
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{t}}
)
.

Let
p
be prime. If
{\mathcal{C}}\subseteq\left(\mathbb{Z}_{p^{t}}\right)^{n}
is a linear code of rank
K
and minimum Hamming distance
d_{H}(C)=d
, then

d\leq n-K+1.

2.3
Lee Weight

The
Lee weight
of an element
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}
is given by
w_{L}(a)=\min\{a,q-a\}
. Given an element
c=(c_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{n}
, the
Lee weight
of
c
, denoted
w_{L}(c)
is given by

w_{L}(c)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{L}(c_{i}).

For
c,c^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{n}
, the
Lee distance

d_{L}(c,c^{\prime})
between
c
and
c^{\prime}
is defined to be the Lee weight of their difference,

d_{L}(c,c^{\prime})=w_{L}(c-c^{\prime}).

The maximum Lee weight of any element in
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
shall be denoted by
M_{L}(q)
. In particular, we have

M_{L}(q)=\left\lfloor\frac{q}{2}\right\rfloor.

(4)

We shall also denote the average nonzero Lee weight of
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
by
\mu_{q}
. Simple counting shows

\mu_{q}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{q^{2}}{4(q-1)}&\text{if $q$ even}\\
\frac{q+1}{4}&\text{if $q$ odd.}\end{array}\right..

(5)

Given a linear
[n,k,d]_{q}
code over
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
,
d_{L}({\mathcal{C}})
shall denote the
minimum Lee distance
of
{\mathcal{C}}
, that is

\displaystyle d_{L}({\mathcal{C}})

\displaystyle=\min\{d_{L}(c,c^{\prime})\mid c,c^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{C}},c\neq c^{\prime}\}

\displaystyle=\min\{w_{L}(c)\mid c\in{\mathcal{C}},c\neq 0\}

Given a specific code
{\mathcal{C}}
, one can determine
d_{L}({\mathcal{C}})
directly, for instance, through brute-force enumeration. Alternatively, methods such as filtration
[
4
]
can be used to analyze the entries of a given generator matrix and derive upper bounds on
d_{L}({\mathcal{C}})
. In this work, however, we focus on bounds that depend only on the length
n
, rank
K
, and alphabet size
q=p^{t}
.

To frame our discussion, we introduce the following key parameter:

\Phi(n,K,p^{t})=\max\{d_{L}({\mathcal{C}})\mid{\mathcal{C}}\subseteq\left(\mathbb{Z}_{p^{t}}\right)^{n}\text{ is a linear code of rank }K\geq 1\}

(6)

When
t=1
, the rank of the code coincides with its (classical) dimension. To ensure clarity, in the sequel we will use
k
to denote dimension and
K
to denote rank. A code
{\mathcal{C}}\subseteq\left(\mathbb{Z}_{p^{t}}\right)^{n}
of rank
K
satisfying
d_{L}({\mathcal{C}})=\Phi(n,K,p^{t})
is called an MLDR (Maximum Lee Distance with respect to Rank) code
[
11
]
. We note that finding and classifying MLRD codes (not necessarily over
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{t}}
) is a challenging problem, even if one restricts to the case of rings of order
4

[
19
]
.

2.4
Some Established Bounds on
\Phi(n,K,p^{t})
,
p
Prime

One of the earliest bounds on the minimum Lee distance of codes was established in response to a conjecture of Lee
[
16
]
, proved in 1968 by Wyner and Graham
[
25
]
. Their result, when stated in the context of codes over
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{t}}
, is as follows:

Theorem 2

(
[
25
]
)
.

Let
p
be prime. If
{\mathcal{C}}\subseteq\left(\mathbb{Z}_{q}\right)^{n}
,
q=p^{t}
then

d_{L}({\mathcal{C}})\leq n\cdot\mu_{q}\cdot\frac{q-1}{q}\cdot\frac{|{\mathcal{C}}|}{|{\mathcal{C}}|-1}.

Remark 1
.

From equations (
2
) and (
3
), if a code
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\left(\mathbb{Z}_{p^{t}}\right)^{n}
has rank
K
, then its size satisfies
|{\mathcal{C}}|\geq p^{K}
(with the possibility of equality). Consequently, if
\kappa
denotes the
p^{t}
-type of
{\mathcal{C}}
, then we have the relation:

K\geq\kappa\geq\frac{K}{t}.

By applying Remark
1
to Theorem
2
, we obtain the following bound on MLDR codes:

Corollary 3
.

Let
p
be prime. If
q=p^{t}
, then

\Phi(n,K,p^{t})\leq n\cdot\mu_{q}\cdot\frac{q-1}{q}\cdot\frac{p^{K}}{p^{K}-1}.

Shiromoto and Yoshida
[
22
]
provided the following bound, which also follows readily from the Singleton Bound for linear codes over
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{t}}
.

Lemma 4

(
[
22
]
)
.

Let
p
be prime. If
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\left(\mathbb{Z}_{p^{t}}\right)^{n}
is a code of rank
K
then

d_{L}({\mathcal{C}})\leq M_{L}(q)\cdot(n-K+1)

Refining a bound on linear codes by Shiromoto
[
21
]
, Alderson and Huntemann
[
1
]
provide the following bound.

Theorem 5

(
[
1
]
)
.

Let
p
be prime. If
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\left(\mathbb{Z}_{p^{t}}\right)^{n}
,
p^{t}>3
, is a code of
p^{t}
-type
\kappa<n
then

d_{L}({\mathcal{C}})\leq M_{L}(q)\cdot\left(n-\lfloor\kappa\rfloor\right).

With reference to the Remark
1
, Theorem
5
provides the following bound pertaining to MLDR codes.

Corollary 6
.

Let
p
be prime. If
p^{t}>3
and
n>K
, then

\Phi(n,K,p^{t})\leq M_{L}(q)\cdot\left(n-\left\lfloor\frac{K}{t}\right\rfloor\right).

The following bound, established by Chiang and Wolf
[
9
]
, is particularly effective when applied to free codes.

Theorem 7

(
[
9
]
)
.

Let
p
be prime. If
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\left(\mathbb{Z}_{p^{t}}\right)^{n}
is a code with free rank
k_{1}
, then

d_{L}({\mathcal{C}})\leq\mu_{p^{t}}(n-k_{1}+1).

Since free rank and rank coincide when working over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, Theorem
7
provides the following bound on MLDR codes.

Corollary 8
.

If
p
is prime, then

\Phi(n,K,p)\leq\mu_{p}(n-K+1).

Alderson and Huntemann
[
1
]
later provided the following refinement of Theorem
7
, extending it to the setting of integral
p^{t}
-type codes.

Theorem 9

(
[
1
]
)
.

Let
p
be prime. If
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\left(\mathbb{Z}_{p^{t}}\right)^{n}
is a code of integral
p^{t}
-type
\kappa
, then

d_{L}({\mathcal{C}})\leq\mu_{p^{t}}(n-\kappa+1).

Using Remark
1
, we obtain the following corollary to Theorem
9
, which generalizes Corollary
8
.

Corollary 10
.

Let
p
be prime. If
q=p^{t}
, then

\Phi(n,K,q)\leq\mu_{q}\left(n-\left\lfloor\frac{K}{t}\right\rfloor+1\right).

More recently, additional bounds on
\Phi(n,K,p^{t})
have been developed. For instance, Bariffi and Weger
[
4
]
established the bound

\Phi(n,K,p^{t})\leq p^{t-1}\left\lfloor\frac{p}{2}\right\rfloor(n-K+1).

While this result improves upon some previously known bounds, it is itself subsumed by the following bound established by Byrne and Weger
[
8
]
.

Theorem 11

(
[
8
]
)
.

Let
p
be prime. For
q=p^{t}
,

\Phi(n,K,q)\leq p^{t-1}\mu_{p}(n-K+1).

It is worth noting that Theorem
11
corrects a minor discrepancy in the original result, which appears as Corollary 32 in
[
8
]
.

In the following sections, we present further refinements that, in many cases, improve upon the existing bounds on MLDR codes.

3
New Bounds

Before developing new bounds for codes over
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
, where
q=p^{t}
, we first restrict our discussion to the case
q=p
. As noted in Section
2.3
, we shall use the dimension parameter
k
to represent the rank when
t=1
, since in this case, rank and dimension coincide.

3.1
Bounds for Linear Codes Over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
,
p
Prime

Lemma 12
.

Let
p
be prime. If
{\mathcal{C}}
is a linear
[n,k,d]_{p}
code with
d_{L}({\mathcal{C}})>\left\lfloor\mu_{p}(n-k)\right\rfloor
, then

{\mathcal{C}}
is an MDS code, so
d=n-k+1
.

Proof.

If
p=2,3
, the result holds trivially, so assume
p\geq 5
. Suppose
x\in{\mathcal{C}}
satisfies
w_{H}(x)\leq n-k
, and consider the
(p-1)\times n
array whose rows consist of the nonzero elements of
\langle x\rangle
. In each column corresponding to a support position of
x
, each nonzero element of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
appears exactly once. Thus, the total Lee weight of the array is bounded above by

(n-k)\cdot\frac{p^{2}-1}{4}.

The result follows by averaging over the
p-1
rows.
∎

Lemma 13
.

Let
p
be prime. Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a linear
[n,k,d]_{p}
code and
s\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. If
d_{L}({\mathcal{C}})>\left\lfloor\mu_{p}(n-k-s)\right\rfloor
, then
d\geq n-k-s+1
.

Equivalently, if
d_{L}({\mathcal{C}})>\left\lfloor\mu_{p}(n-k-s)\right\rfloor
then
\operatorname{def}({\mathcal{C}})\leq s
.

Proof.

If
p=2,3
then the result holds trivially, so assume
p\geq 5
. By way of contradiction suppose
{\mathcal{C}}
has
d_{L}({\mathcal{C}})>\mu_{p}(n-k-s)
and
x\in{\mathcal{C}}
with
w_{H}(x)\leq n-k-s
. Consider the array whose rows are the nonzero members of
\left\langle{x}\right\rangle
. Counting and averaging the nonzero column weights over the rows, as in the proof of Lemma
12
gives the contradiction
d_{L}({\mathcal{C}})\leq\mu_{p}(n-k-s)
.
∎

As an immediate consequence, we have the following.

Corollary 14
.

Let
p
be prime. If
{\mathcal{C}}
is a linear
[n,k,d]_{p}
A
s
MDS code, then

d_{L}({\mathcal{C}})\leq\left\lfloor\mu_{p}(n-k+1-s)\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor d\cdot\mu_{p}\right\rfloor.

(7)

The following example demonstrates that equality can be attained in (
7
).

Example 15
.

For any prime
p
and
k\geq 1
, the code
{\mathcal{C}}=(\mathbb{Z}_{p})^{k}
is an
[k,k,1]_{p}
MDS code with
d_{L}({\mathcal{C}})=1
. If
p\in\{2,3\}
(when the Hamming and Lee metrics coincide), then
\mu_{p}=1
, and equality holds in (
7
).

Similarly, consider the MDS code
{\mathcal{C}}_{1}
over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
with generator matrix

G_{1}=[I_{k}\mid\bar{1}]:=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&\cdots&0&1\\
0&1&\cdots&0&1\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&1&1\end{bmatrix}.

In this case,
d_{L}({\mathcal{C}}_{1})=2
, and if
p\in\{2,3\}
, then equality holds in (

). Consequently, we obtain

\Phi(k+1,k,2)=\Phi(k+1,k,3)=2.

From the discussion in Section
2.1
, an
[n,k,d]_{p}
-MDS code with

satisfies

, and if

, then

(

]
,
[

]
). Consequently, Lemma

leads to the following result.

Corollary 16
.

Let
p
be prime. If
n>p+1
and
k\leq p
, or if
n>k+1
and
k\geq p
, then

\Phi(n,k,p)\leq\left\lfloor\mu_{p}(n-k)\right\rfloor.

The bound in Corollary
16
can be improved if the codes under consideration are relatively long, in that
n>2p+k
.

Corollary 17
.

Let
p
be prime. If
k\geq 2
, then

\Phi(n,k,p)\leq\mu_{p}\left(n-k+1-\left\lfloor\frac{n-k+1}{p+1}\right\rfloor\right).

Proof.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a linear
[n,k,d]_{p}
code with
k\geq 2
. Define
a=\left\lfloor\frac{n-k+1}{p+1}\right\rfloor
. Then we have
n-k+1\geq a(p+1)
.

If
\operatorname{def}({\mathcal{C}})\leq a-1
, then equation (
1
) leads to the contradiction
n\leq a(p+1)+k-2
. Hence, we must have
\operatorname{def}({\mathcal{C}})\geq a
, and the result follows from Corollary

.
∎

In the case where
\operatorname{def}({\mathcal{C}})=0
, the validity of the Main Conjecture on MDS Codes over prime fields
[
2
]
provides the bound
n\leq\max\{k+1,p+1\}
. Consequently, Corollary
17
offers no improvement over Theorem
11
(or Corollary
8
) when
{\mathcal{C}}
is MDS. However, we present the following refinement in the MDS case.

Theorem 18
.

Let
p
be prime. If
{\mathcal{C}}
is a linear
[n,k,d]_{p}
code with
k\geq 2
, then either:

1.

{\mathcal{C}}
is MDS, in which case

d_{L}({\mathcal{C}})\leq\left\lfloor\mu_{p}(n-k+1)\cdot\frac{p-1}{p}\right\rfloor+1,

(8)

2.

or
\operatorname{def}({\mathcal{C}})=s>0
, in which case

d_{L}({\mathcal{C}})\leq\mu_{p}(n-k+1-s)\leq\mu_{p}(n-k).

(9)

Proof.

If
{\mathcal{C}}
is not MDS, then the result follows from Corollary
14
. If
{\mathcal{C}}
is MDS, then consider the

array
S
, the rows of which comprise the codewords with the first

entries equal to

. It follows that no two rows of
S
have a common entry in the final
d=n-k+1
columns. If
p
is odd, then summing the Lee weights column-wise over the final

x

w_{L}(x)\leq\left\lfloor\frac{p^{2}-1}{4}\cdot d\cdot\frac{1}{p}\right\rfloor+1=\left\lfloor\mu_{p}(n-k+1)\cdot\frac{p-1}{p}\right\rfloor+1.

(10)

If
p=2
then
mutatis mutandis
,
S
provides a row,
x
, satisfying

w_{L}(x)\leq\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor+1\left(=\left\lfloor\mu_{p}(n-k+1)\cdot\frac{p-1}{p}\right\rfloor+1\right).

(11)

∎

Remark 2
.

In the present context of codes over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, the bounds of Chiang and Wolf (Corollary
8

We next explore some elementary properties of
\Phi(n,k,p)
. The following lemma will be useful.

Lemma 20
.

Let
k\geq 2
be an integer,
m>0
. If
A\subset(0,m]
with
|A|=k
, and
B=A\cup\{|a_{i}-a_{j}|\mid a_{i},a_{j}\in A\}
, then there exists
x\in B
with
0<x\leq\frac{m}{k}
.

Proof.

Assume for contradiction that
B\cap(0,\frac{m}{k}]=\emptyset
. The interval
(0,m]
is partitioned into
k
subintervals of the form
\left(\frac{m\cdot i}{k},\frac{m\cdot(i+1)}{k}\right],\quad 0\leq i\leq k-1.
By the pigeonhole principle, at least one of these subintervals must contain at least two elements of
A
, contradicting our assumption that
B\cap(0,\frac{m}{k}]=\emptyset
.
∎

Lemma 21
.

Some elementary properties of
\Phi(n,k,p)
,
p
prime.

1.

\Phi(k,k,p)=1

2.

\Phi(n,k,p)\leq\Phi(n+1,k,p)

3.

\Phi(n,k,p)\geq\Phi(n,k+1,p)

4.

If
p\in\{2,3\}
, or if
k\geq\frac{p-1}{2}
then
\Phi(k+1,k,p)=2
.

5.

If
p\geq 5
and
k<\frac{p-1}{2}
then
\Phi(k+1,k,p)\leq 2+\frac{p-3}{2k}
.

6.

\Phi(n,1,p)\leq n\mu_{p}
with equality only if
n=\alpha\cdot\frac{p-1}{2}
for some integer
\alpha>0
.

Proof.

1
: See Example
15
.

2
: If
{\mathcal{C}}
achieves
\Phi(n,k,p)
, then repeating any coordinate results in code
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
of length
n+1
, and
d_{L}({\mathcal{C}}^{\prime})\geq d_{L}({\mathcal{C}})
.

3
: Suppose
{\mathcal{C}}
achieves
\Phi(n,k+1,p)
and let
v
be a codeword of minimum Lee weight in
{\mathcal{C}}
. Any linearly independent
k
-subset
S^{\prime}
with
\{v\}\subseteq S^{\prime}\subseteq{\mathcal{C}}
spans a
k
-dimensional code
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
with
w_{L}({\mathcal{C}}^{\prime})\geq w_{L}(v)
.

4
: For
p\in\{2,3\}
see Example
15
. Assume
k\geq\frac{p-1}{2}
. If
{\mathcal{C}}
is not MDS, then
d_{H}({\mathcal{C}})=1=d_{L}({\mathcal{C}})
, so assume
{\mathcal{C}}
is MDS. The classical result of Bush
[
6
]
gives that
{\mathcal{C}}
is Lee-equivalent (up to positional permutations, and multiplication of a particular coordinate position by
\pm 1
) to a code with generator matrix
G=[I_{k}\mid\bar{v}]
, where
w_{H}(\bar{v})=k
. If
k=\frac{p-1}{2}
, then either some coordinate of
\bar{v}
has Lee weight
1
(and
d_{L}({\mathcal{C}})=2
), or at least two coordinates of
\bar{v}
have the same Lee weight. If
k>\frac{p-1}{2}
then it must be the case that two coordinates of
\bar{v}
have the same Lee weight. The difference or sum of the corresponding rows give
d_{L}({\mathcal{C}})\leq 2
, and observing that
d_{L}({\mathcal{C}})\geq d_{H}({\mathcal{C}})=2
gives the result.

5
: Arguing as in part
4
, we may assume
{\mathcal{C}}
is MDS, and by multiplying rows by
-1
as appropriate, we may assume
{\mathcal{C}}
has a generator matrix
G^{\prime}=[A\mid\bar{v}]
, where
A
is a diagonal matrix with each diagonal entry being
\pm 1
,
w_{H}(\bar{v})=k
, and the entries
v_{i}
of
\bar{v}
are mutually distinct, satisfying
2\leq v_{i}\leq\frac{p-1}{2}
(else
d_{L}({\mathcal{C}})=2
). From the Lemma
20
, among the row of
G^{\prime}
, or the pairwise differences of rows of
G^{\prime}
there exist a codeword of Lee weight at most

.

6
: Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be an
[n,1,d]_{p}
code. An averaging argument shows
d_{L}({\mathcal{C}})\leq n\mu_{p}
, with equality if and only if all nonzero codewords have constant Hamming weight
n
, and constant Lee weight
n\mu_{p}
. In
[
24
]
, Wood showed that a shortest length constant Lee-weight linear code over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
or
\mathbb{Z}_{2^{t}}
provides all constant Lee-weight codes in that all constant Lee-weight codes are
m
-fold replications of the shortest such code (up to Lee equivalence). In particular, he showed that any constant Lee weight code over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is an
m
-fold replication of
\left\langle{(1,2,\ldots,\frac{p-1}{2})}\right\rangle
up to multiplication of each coordinate by
\pm 1
, (and any constant Lee weight code over
\mathbb{Z}_{2^{t}}
is an
m
-fold replication of
\left\langle{(1,2,\ldots,2^{t}-1)}\right\rangle
up to multiplication of each coordinate by
\pm 1
). The result follows.
∎

Lemma 22
.

Let
p
be prime. If
{\mathcal{C}}
is an
[n,k,d]_{p}
code with
p>2
and
d_{L}({\mathcal{C}})=d\cdot\mu_{p}
, then
\frac{p-1}{2}
divides
d
.

In particular, if
p\equiv 3\mod 4
or if
d
is even, and
\frac{p-1}{2}
does not divide
d
, then

d_{L}({\mathcal{C}})\leq d\cdot\mu_{p}-1.

Proof.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be an
[n,k,d]_{p}
code. An averaging argument gives
w_{L}({\mathcal{C}})\leq d\mu_{p}
, with equality if and only if all minimum Hamming weight codewords have constant Lee weight
d\mu_{p}
. Using Wood’s result as in part
6
of Lemma
21
completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 23
.

Let
p
be prime. If
n\leq k+\frac{p-5}{2}
, then

\Phi(n,k,p)<\mu_{p}(n-k+1).

Proof.

Since
n\geq k
, we necessarily have
p\geq 5
. Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be an
[n,k,d]_{p}
code. If
d\leq n-k
, then the result follows from Corollary
14
. If
d=n-k+1
, then the assumption provides
d\leq\frac{p-3}{2}
, so the result follows from Lemma
22
.
∎

Having explored new bounds for codes over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, we now turn our attention to codes over
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
, where
q=p^{t}
.

3.2
Bounds for Linear Codes Over
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
,
q=p^{t}

The following lemma enables bounds established for codes over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
to be extended to bounds on codes over
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
,
q=p^{t}
.

Lemma 24
.

Let
p
be prime. If
t\geq 1
, then

\Phi(n,K,p^{t})\leq p^{t-1}\cdot\Phi(n,K,p).

(13)

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\left(\mathbb{Z}_{p^{t}}\right)^{n}
be an
[n,k,d]_{q}
code of rank
K
, and let
S({\mathcal{C}})
denote the socle of
{\mathcal{C}}
, so
S({\mathcal{C}})
is an
[n,K,d]_{p}
-code over the alphabet
\left\langle{p^{t-1}}\right\rangle
. It follows that

d_{L}({\mathcal{C}})\leq d_{L}(S({\mathcal{C}}))\leq p^{t-1}\Phi(n,K,p).

∎

The following examples show that equality in (
13
) is attainable.

Example 25
.

For fixed
K\geq 1
, consider the code
{\mathcal{C}}
over
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{t}}
with generator matrix
p^{t-1}I_{K}
.
Since, for any
1\leq\alpha\leq p-1
, we have
w_{L}(\alpha\cdot p^{t-1})\leq p^{t-1}
, it follows immediately that
d_{L}({\mathcal{C}})=p^{t-1}
.
With reference to part
1
of Lemma
21
, we obtain equality in (
13
), so

\Phi(K,K,p^{t})=p^{t-1}.

Example 26
.

With
G_{1}
as in Example
15
, the code
{\mathcal{C}}_{1}
over
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{t}}
with generator matrix
p^{t-1}G_{1}
satisfies
d_{L}({\mathcal{C}}_{1})=2\cdot p^{t-1}
, giving

\Phi(K+1,K,p^{t})\geq 2\cdot p^{t-1}.

Consequently, if
p\in\{2,3\}
, we have equality in (
13
), and

\Phi(K+1,K,p^{t})=2\cdot p^{t-1}.

We are now able to state our main result.

Theorem 27
.

Let
p
be prime. If
t\geq 1
, and
K\geq 2
, then the following bounds hold:

If
n\geq K
, then

\Phi(n,K,p^{t})\leq p^{t-1}\left\lfloor\mu_{p}\left(n-K+1-\left\lfloor\frac{n-K+1}{p+1}\right\rfloor\right)\right\rfloor.

(A)

If
K\geq p
and
n>K+1
, or if
2\leq K\leq p
and
n>p+1
, then

\Phi(n,K,p^{t})\leq p^{t-1}\left\lfloor\mu_{p}(n-K)\right\rfloor.

(B)

If
3\leq K+1\leq n\leq p+1
, then

\Phi(n,K,p^{t})\leq p^{t-1}\cdot\left\lfloor\mu_{p}(n-K+1)\cdot\frac{p-1}{p}+1\right\rfloor.

(C)

In the case
n=K
, we have

\Phi(K,K,p^{t})=p^{t-1}.

(D)

Proof.

Appealing to Lemma
24
, (
A
) follows from Corollary
17
, (
B
) follows from Corollary
16
, and (
C
) follow from Theorem
18
. For (
D

25
.
∎

In comparing the bounds in Theorem
27
, we observe that when the conditions for bound (
B
) are satisfied, it provides a stronger bound than (
A
) whenever
n-K+1<p+1
. Additionally, simple calculations show that when the conditions for bound (

Just as in the prime case, codes that are optimal with respect to the Lee metric must also be optimal with respect to the Hamming metric. The following result observed by Byrne and Weger
[
8
]
may be employed to establish this connection:

Lemma 28
.

Let
q=p^{t}
,
p
prime. A linear code over
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
is MDR if and only if its socle is classically equivalent to an
[n,K]_{p}
-MDS code.

An immediate consequence of this is the following

Lemma 29
.

Let
p
be prime. If
{\mathcal{C}}
is a linear
[n,K,d]_{q}
code,
q=p^{t}
, and
d_{L}({\mathcal{C}})>p^{t-1}\mu_{p}(n-K)
then
{\mathcal{C}}
is MDR. In particular, if
{\mathcal{C}}
is also a free code, then
{\mathcal{C}}
is MDS.

Proof.

Suppose
{\mathcal{C}}
is not MDR. Let
S({\mathcal{C}})
denote the socle of
{\mathcal{C}}
. By the Lemma
28
,
S({\mathcal{C}})
is not MDS. Let
x\in S({\mathcal{C}})
be a codeword of minimum Hamming weight, so in particular
w_{H}(x)\leq n-K
. Assume the nonzero entries are the first
w_{H}(x)
entries. Since
x\in S({\mathcal{C}})={\mathcal{C}}\cap\left\langle{p^{t-1}}\right\rangle
,
\left\langle{x}\right\rangle
holds
p-1
nonzero codewords all of which are nonzero in the first
d_{H}
coordinates, and no two agreeing in any of the first
w_{H}(x)
coordinates. Averaging as in Lemma
12
, and recognizing that each coordinate in
S({\mathcal{C}})
is an element of
\left\langle{p^{t-1}}\right\rangle
, we obtain

d_{L}({\mathcal{C}})\leq p^{t-1}\mu_{p}(n-K).

The result follows.
∎

3.3

\mathbb{Z}_{q}

It is well known (see e.g.
[

) that the only binary linear MDS codes are the trivial ones, namely the
[n,1,n]_{2}

[n,n,1]_{2}

[k+1,k,2]_{2}

Lemma 30
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a linear
[n,K,d]_{q}
MDR code,
q=2^{t}
. One of the following must hold:

•

K=1
and
d_{L}({\mathcal{C}})\leq n\cdot 2^{t-1}

•

n=K>1
and
d_{L}({\mathcal{C}})\leq 2^{t-1}

•

n=K+1
and
d_{L}({\mathcal{C}})\leq 2^{t}

Proof.

Since
{\mathcal{C}}
is MDR, the socle,
S({\mathcal{C}})
is a binary
[n,k,d]_{2}
-MDS code over the alphabet
\{2^{t-1},0\}
. All such codes are classified, giving the result.
∎

Corollary 31

If
n>K+1

Proof.

Follows from Lemma

5
Conclusion and Questions

For
p
a prime, we introduced the parameter
\Phi(n,K,p^{t})
, which denotes the maximum achievable minimum Lee distance over all linear
p^{t}
-nary codes of length
n
and rank
K
. Codes whose minimum Lee distance equal to
\Phi(n,K,p^{t})
are (by definition) MLDR codes.

We established several new upper bounds on
\Phi(n,k,p^{t})
. Based on analytical and numerical comparisons, the new bounds consistently yield lower values compared to the existing bounds, representing improved estimates of
\Phi(n,k,p)
. Additionally, we explored some elementary properties of this parameter in the case
t=1
(Lemma
21
).

While our results provide stronger upper bounds, an important open question remains: For
p>3
are there infinite families of MLDR codes that achieve these new bounds? Identifying such families or proving the tightness of these bounds in general remains an interesting challenge. Additionally, exploring conditions under which the inequalities in parts
2
and
3
of Lemma
21
are strict may yield further insights into the structure of MLDR codes.

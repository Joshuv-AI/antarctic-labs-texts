Optimal recovery of linear operators from information of random functions
K. Yu. Osipenko

Abstract
The paper concerns problems of the recovery of linear operators defined on sets of functions from information of these functions given with stochastic errors. The constructed optimal recovery methods, in general, do not use all the available information. As a consequence, optimal methods are obtained for recovering derivatives of functions from Sobolev classes by the information of their Fourier transforms given with stochastic errors. A similar problem is considered for solutions of the heat equation.

General setting

Denote by
\mathcal{W}
the set of functions
x(\cdot)\in L_{2}(\mathbb{R})
for which

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\nu(t)|x(t)|^{2}\,dt<\infty,

where
\nu(\cdot)
is continuous and positive almost everywhere. Put

W=\biggl\{\,x(\cdot)\in\mathcal{W}:\int_{\mathbb{R}}\nu(t)|x(t)|^{2}\,dt\leq 1\,\biggr\}.

Consider the problem of optimal recovery of the operator
\Lambda x(\cdot)=\mu(\cdot)x(\cdot)
on the class
W
by functions
x(\cdot)
given with random errors (we assume that
\mu(\cdot)
is continuous and such that
\Lambda
maps
\mathcal{W}
into
L_{2}(\mathbb{R})
). More precisely, for a fixed
\delta>0
and every
x(\cdot)\in W
we consider the set of random functions

Y_{\delta}(x(\cdot))=\{\,y_{\xi}(\cdot)\in L_{2}(\mathbb{R}):\mathbb{M}y_{\xi}(\cdot)=x(\cdot),\ \mathbb{D}y_{\xi}(\cdot)\leq\delta\,\}.

We will also assume that the set of these random functions and the corresponding probability measures are such that they allow for a change in integration so that the equalities are valid

\mathbb{M}\int_{\mathbb{R}}p(t)y_{\xi}(t)\,dt=\int_{\mathbb{R}}p(t)\mathbb{M}y_{\xi}(t)\,dt,\quad p(t)\in L_{2}(\mathbb{R}),

(1)

and

\mathbb{M}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|y_{\xi}(t)|^{2}\,dt=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{M}|y_{\xi}(t)|^{2}\,dt.

(2)

As recovery methods we consider all possible mappings
\varphi\colon L_{2}(\mathbb{R})\to L_{2}(\mathbb{R})
.
The error of a method
m
is defined as

e(\Lambda,W,\delta,\varphi)=\left(\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x(\cdot)\in W\\
y_{\xi}(\cdot)\in Y_{\delta}(x(\cdot))\end{subarray}}\mathbb{M}\left(\|\Lambda x(\cdot)-\varphi(y_{\xi}(\cdot))(\cdot)\|^{2}_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R})}\right)\right)^{1/2}.

The problem is to find the error of optimal recovery

E(\Lambda,W,\delta)=\inf_{\varphi\colon L_{2}(\mathbb{R})\to L_{2}(\mathbb{R})}e(\Lambda,W,\delta,\varphi)

(3)

and a method on which this infimum is attained which is called optimal.

We assume that
|\mu(\cdot)|
and
\nu(\cdot)
are even functions,
|\mu(t)|>0
almost everywhere, and
|\mu(\cdot)|/\sqrt{\nu(\cdot)}
is a monotonically decreasing function on
\mathbb{R}_{+}=[0,+\infty)
. Put

f(s)=\int_{|t|\leq s}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\nu(s)}}{|\mu(s)|}\frac{|\mu(t)|}{\sqrt{\nu(t)}}-1\right)\nu(t)\,dt.

(4)

It is easy to check that
f(\cdot)
is a monotonically increasing function. Assume that
f(s)\to+\infty
as
s\to+\infty
. Then for any
\delta>0
the equation
f(s)=\delta^{-2}
has a unique solution
t_{\delta}
.

Theorem 1
.

For all
\delta>0

E(\Lambda,W,\delta)=\delta\biggl(\int_{|t|\leq t_{\delta}}|\mu(t)|^{2}\biggl(1-\dfrac{\sqrt{\nu(t)}}{|\mu(t)|}\frac{|\mu(t_{\delta})|}{\sqrt{\nu(t_{\delta})}}\biggr)\,dt\biggr)^{1/2}.

(5)

Moreover, the method

\varphi(y_{\xi}(\cdot))(t)=\left(1-\dfrac{\sqrt{\nu(t)}}{|\mu(t)|}\frac{|\mu(t_{\delta})|}{\sqrt{\nu(t_{\delta})}}\right)_{+}\mu(t)y_{\xi}(t)

is optimal
(a_{+}=\max\{a,0\})
.

Proof.

1. The lower bound. Consider the segment
[-A,A]\subset\mathbb{R}

\dfrac{s_{1}(x)\tau_{1}}{1-p_{1}}e_{1}(\cdot)&\mbox{with probability }p_{2}-p_{1},\\
\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{2}\dfrac{s_{j}(x)\tau_{j}}{1-p_{j}}e_{j}(\cdot),&\mbox{with probability }p_{3}-p_{2},\\
\leaders{\hbox{.}}{\hfill}\kern 0.0pt&\leaders{\hbox{.}}{\hfill}\\
\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{2n-1}\dfrac{s_{j}(x)\tau_{j}}{1-p_{j}}e_{j}(\cdot),&\mbox{with probability }p_{2n}-p_{2n-1},\\
\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{2n}\dfrac{s_{j}(x)\tau_{j}}{1-p_{j}}e_{j}(\cdot),&\mbox{with probability }1-p_{2n}.\end{cases}

Thus, we have the following distribution for

It is easy to verify that
\mathbb{M}(\eta(x)(\cdot))(\cdot)=x(\cdot)

Consequently,

\geq\frac{1}{2^{2N}}\sum_{x(\cdot)\in B}\biggl(\sum_{j=1}^{2N+1}(p_{j}-p_{j-1})\biggl\|\Lambda x(\cdot)-\varphi\biggl(\sum_{k=1}^{j-1}\dfrac{s_{k}(x)\tau_{k}}{1-p_{k}}e_{k}(\cdot)\biggr)(\cdot)\biggr\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\biggr)\\

(6)

2. The upper bound. Let us find the error of the methods having the form

. We have

e^{2}(\Lambda,W,I,\delta,\varphi)=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x(\cdot)\in W\\
y_{\xi}(\cdot)\in Y_{\delta}(x(\cdot))\end{subarray}}\mathbb{M}\left(\|\Lambda x(\cdot)-\varphi(y_{\xi}(\cdot))(\cdot)\|^{2}_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R})}\right)\\
=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x(\cdot)\in W\\
y_{\xi}(\cdot)\in Y_{\delta}(x(\cdot))\end{subarray}}\mathbb{M}\left(\|\Lambda x(\cdot)-\varphi(x(\cdot))(\cdot)-\varphi(z_{\xi}(\cdot))(\cdot)\|^{2}_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R})}\right)\\
=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x(\cdot)\in W\\
y_{\xi}(\cdot)\in Y_{\delta}(x(\cdot))\end{subarray}}\left(\|\Lambda x(\cdot)-\varphi(x(\cdot))(\cdot)\|^{2}_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\mathbb{M}(\|\varphi(z_{\xi}(\cdot))(\cdot)\|^{2}_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R})})\right.\\
\left.-2\mathbb{M}\RE(\varphi(z_{\xi}(\cdot))(\cdot),\overline{\Lambda x(\cdot)-\varphi(x(\cdot))(\cdot)})\right);

here
(\cdot,\cdot)
is the standard scalar product in
L_{2}(\mathbb{R})
. It follows from the form of
\varphi
that

\mathbb{M}\RE(\varphi(z_{\xi}(\cdot))(\cdot),\Lambda x(\cdot)-\varphi(x(\cdot))(\cdot))\\
=\RE\mathbb{M}\left(\alpha(\cdot)\mu(\cdot)z_{\xi}(\cdot),\overline{\Lambda x(\cdot)-\varphi(x(\cdot))(\cdot)}\right)\\
=\RE\left(\alpha(\cdot)\mu(\cdot)\mathbb{M}z_{\xi}(\cdot),\overline{\Lambda x(\cdot)-\varphi(x(\cdot))(\cdot)}\right)=0.

Due to the fact that
\mathbb{M}(|z_{\xi}(\cdot)|^{2})=\mathbb{D}(y_{\xi}(\cdot))
, we have

e^{2}(\Lambda,W,I,\delta,\varphi)=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x(\cdot)\in W\\
y_{\xi}(\cdot)\in Y_{\delta}(x(\cdot))\end{subarray}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mu(t)|^{2}|1-\alpha(t)|^{2}|x(t)|^{2}\,dt\right.\\
\left.+\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mu(t)|^{2}|\alpha(t)|^{2}\mathbb{D}(y_{\xi}(t))\,dt\right)=\sup_{x(\cdot)\in W}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mu(t)|^{2}|1-\alpha(t)|^{2}|x(t)|^{2}\,dt\\
+\delta^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mu(t)|^{2}|\alpha(t)|^{2}\,dt

Since

\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mu(t)|^{2}|1-\alpha(t)|^{2}|x(t)|^{2}\,dt=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|\mu(t)|^{2}}{\nu(t)}|1-\alpha(t)|^{2}\nu(t)|x(t)|^{2}\,dt\\
\leq\vraisup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\left(\frac{|\mu(t)|^{2}}{\nu(t)}|1-\alpha(t)|^{2}\right),

we obtain

e^{2}(\Lambda,W,I,\delta,\varphi)\leq\vraisup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\left(\frac{|\mu(t)|^{2}}{\nu(t)}|1-\alpha(t)|^{2}\right)+\delta^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mu(t)|^{2}|\alpha(t)|^{2}\,dt.

Put

\alpha(t)=\left(1-\dfrac{\sqrt{\nu(t)}}{|\mu(t)|}\frac{|\mu(t_{\delta})|}{\sqrt{\nu(t_{\delta})}}\right)_{+}.

Due to the monotonous decreasing of the function
|\mu(\cdot)|/\sqrt{\nu(\cdot)}
we get

\vraisup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\left(\frac{|\mu(t)|^{2}}{\nu(t)}|1-\alpha(t)|^{2}\right)=\frac{|\mu(t_{\delta})|^{2}}{\nu(t_{\delta})}.

Consequently,

e^{2}(\Lambda,W,I,\delta,\varphi)\leq\frac{|\mu(t_{\delta})|^{2}}{\nu(t_{\delta})}\\
+\delta^{2}\int_{|t|\leq t_{\delta}}|\mu(t)|^{2}\biggl(1-\dfrac{\sqrt{\nu(t)}}{|\mu(t)|}\frac{|\mu(t_{\delta})|}{\sqrt{\nu(t_{\delta})}}\biggr)^{2}\,dt=\frac{|\mu(t_{\delta})|^{2}}{\nu(t_{\delta})}\\
+\delta^{2}\int_{|t|\leq t_{\delta}}|\mu(t)|^{2}\biggl(\biggl(1-\dfrac{\sqrt{\nu(t)}}{|\mu(t)|}\frac{|\mu(t_{\delta})|}{\sqrt{\nu(t_{\delta})}}\biggr)-\dfrac{\sqrt{\nu(t)}}{|\mu(t)|}\frac{|\mu(t_{\delta})|}{\sqrt{\nu(t_{\delta})}}\\
+\dfrac{\nu(t)}{|\mu(t)|^{2}}\frac{|\mu(t_{\delta})|^{2}}{\nu(t_{\delta})}\biggr)\,dt=\delta^{2}\int_{|t|\leq t_{\delta}}|\mu(t)|^{2}\biggl(1-\dfrac{\sqrt{\nu(t)}}{|\mu(t)|}\frac{|\mu(t_{\delta})|}{\sqrt{\nu(t_{\delta})}}\biggr)\,dt\\
+\frac{|\mu(t_{\delta})|}{\sqrt{\nu(t_{\delta})}}\biggl(\frac{|\mu(t_{\delta})|}{\sqrt{\nu(t_{\delta})}}\biggl(1+\delta^{2}\int_{|t|\leq t_{\delta}}\nu(t)\,dt\biggr)-\delta^{2}\int_{|t|\leq t_{\delta}}|\mu(t)|\sqrt{\nu(t)}\,dt\biggr).

It follows from the definition of
t_{\delta}
that

\frac{|\mu(t_{\delta})|}{\sqrt{\nu(t_{\delta})}}\biggl(1+\delta^{2}\int_{|t|\leq t_{\delta}}\nu(t)\,dt\biggr)-\delta^{2}\int_{|t|\leq t_{\delta}}|\mu(t)|\sqrt{\nu(t)}\,dt=0.

Thus,

e^{2}(\Lambda,W,I,\delta,\varphi)\leq\delta^{2}\int_{|t|\leq t_{\delta}}|\mu(t)|^{2}\biggl(1-\dfrac{\sqrt{\nu(t)}}{|\mu(t)|}\frac{|\mu(t_{\delta})|}{\sqrt{\nu(t_{\delta})}}\biggr)\,dt.

(16)

Consider the function

\widehat{x}(t)=\delta\biggl(\biggl(\frac{\sqrt{\nu(t_{\delta})}}{|\mu(t_{\delta})|}\frac{|\mu(t)|}{\sqrt{\nu(t)}}-1\biggr)_{+}\biggr)^{1/2}.

It is obvious that
\widehat{x}(\cdot)\in W_{0}
. Moreover,

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\nu(t)|\widehat{x}(t)|^{2}\,dt=\delta^{2}\int_{|t|\leq t_{\delta}}\nu(t)\biggl(\frac{\sqrt{\nu(t_{\delta})}}{|\mu(t_{\delta})|}\frac{|\mu(t)|}{\sqrt{\nu(t)}}-1\biggr)\,dt=1.

Taking into account (
16
), it follows from (
15
) that

E^{2}(\Lambda,W,I,\delta)\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\delta^{2}}{\delta^{2}+|\widehat{x}(t)|^{2}}|\mu(t)|^{2}|\widehat{x}(t)|^{2}\,dt\\
=\delta^{2}\int_{|t|\leq t_{\delta}}|\mu(t)|^{2}\biggl(1-\dfrac{\sqrt{\nu(t)}}{|\mu(t)|}\frac{|\mu(t_{\delta})|}{\sqrt{\nu(t_{\delta})}}\biggr)\,dt\\
\geq e^{2}(\Lambda,W,I,\delta,\varphi)\geq E^{2}(\Lambda,W,I,\delta).

This implies (
5
) and the optimality of the method
\varphi
.
∎

Now we consider some examples of the application of Theorem

.

3
Recovery of functions and their derivatives from the Fourier transform given with random error

Denote by
\mathcal{W}_{2}^{r}(\mathbb{R})
the set of functions
x(\cdot)\in L_{2}(\mathbb{R})
for which

x^{(r-1)}(\cdot)
is locally absolutely continuous and
x^{(r)}(\cdot)\in L_{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Put

W_{2}^{r}(\mathbb{R})=\{\,x(\cdot)\in\mathcal{W}_{2}^{r}(\mathbb{R}):\|x^{(r)}(\cdot)\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq 1\,\}.

Suppose that the Fourier transform
Fx(\cdot)
of the function
x(\cdot)\in W_{2}^{r}(\mathbb{R})
is given with a random error. We assume that instead of the function
Fx(\cdot)
we know a random function
y_{\xi}(\cdot)\in L_{2}(\mathbb{R})
such that
\mathbb{M}y_{\xi}(\cdot)=Fx(\cdot)
and
\mathbb{D}y_{\xi}(\cdot)\leq\delta
. Using this information, it is required to recover the function
x^{(k)}(\cdot)
,
0\leq k<r
, in the
L_{2}(\mathbb{R})
-metric.

The exact setting of the problem is as follows. For every
x(\cdot)\in W_{2}^{r}(\mathbb{R})
we consider the set of random functions

Y_{\delta}(x(\cdot))=\{\,y_{\xi}(\cdot)\in L_{2}(\mathbb{R}):\mathbb{M}y_{\xi}(\cdot)=Fx(\cdot),\ \mathbb{D}y_{\xi}(\cdot)\leq\delta\,\}.

(17)

This set requires additional conditions given in the general setting (the validity of the equalities (
1
) and (
2
)). Next, we define the error of the recovery method
\varphi\colon L_{2}(\mathbb{R})\to L_{2}(\mathbb{R})
as follows

e(D^{k},W_{2}^{r}(\mathbb{R}),\delta,\varphi)=\left(\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x(\cdot)\in W_{2}^{r}(\mathbb{R})\\
y_{\xi}(\cdot)\in Y_{\delta}(x(\cdot))\end{subarray}}\mathbb{M}\left(\|x^{(k)}(\cdot)-\varphi(y_{\xi}(\cdot))(\cdot)\|^{2}_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R})}\right)\right)^{1/2}.

The problem is to find the error of optimal recovery

E(D^{k},W_{2}^{r}(\mathbb{R}),\delta)=\inf_{\varphi\colon L_{2}(\mathbb{R})\to L_{2}(\mathbb{R})}e(D^{k},W_{2}^{r}(\mathbb{R}),\delta,\varphi)

and a method on which this infimum is attained.

It follows from the Parseval equality that

\displaystyle\|x^{(r)}(\cdot)\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|t|^{2r}|Fx(t)|^{2}\,dt,

\displaystyle\|x^{(k)}(\cdot)-\varphi(y_{\xi}(\cdot))(\cdot)\|^{2}_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R})}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|(it)^{k}F(t)-F\varphi(y_{\xi}(\cdot))(t)|^{2}\,dt.

Thus, the problem is reduced to problem (
3
) with
\nu(t)=t^{2r}
and
\mu(t)=(it)^{k}
.
The function
f(\cdot)
which was defined by (
4
), has the form

f(s)=\int_{|t|\leq s}\left(\frac{s^{r-k}}{|t|^{r-k}}-1\right)t^{2r}\,dt=2s^{2r+1}\frac{r-k}{(2r+1)(r+k+1)}.

The equation
f(s)=\delta^{-2}
has the unique solution

t_{\delta}=\left(\frac{(2r+1)(r+k+1)}{2\delta^{2}(r-k)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2r+1}}.

It follows from Theorem
1

Theorem 2
.

For all
\delta>0
and
0\leq k<r

E(D^{k},W_{2}^{r}(\mathbb{R}),\delta)=\frac{(2r+1)^{\frac{2k+1}{2(2r+1)}}}{\sqrt{2k+1}}\left(2\delta^{2}\frac{r-k}{r+k+1}\right)^{\frac{r-k}{2(2r+1)}}.

Moreover, the method

\varphi(y_{\xi}(\cdot))(t)=F^{-1}\left((it)^{k}\alpha(t)y_{\xi}(t)\right)(t),

where

\alpha(t)=\left(1-t^{r-k}\left(\frac{2\delta^{2}(r-k)}{(2r+1)(r+k+1)}\right)^{\frac{r-k}{2r+1}}\right)_{+},

is optimal.

Note that the optimal recovery method does not use all the information about random functions
y_{\xi}(\cdot)
, but only the information contained in the segment
[-t_{\delta},t_{\delta}]
. Moreover, the more accurate the measurements (the smaller the variance
\delta^{2}
), the larger this segment becomes.

The deterministic case of this problem was considered in
[
5
]
(see also
[
7
]
).

4
Recovery of the solution of the heat equation

The temperature distribution in an infinite rod is described by the equation

\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial t^{2}},

where
u(\cdot,\cdot)
is the function on
[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}
) with
a given initial temperature distribution

u(0,\cdot)=u_{0}(\cdot).

Consider the problem of recovering the temperature distribution at an instant of time
T
from information about the Fourier transform of the initial temperature distribution
u_{0}(\cdot)
, given with a random error. We assume that the functions
u_{0}(\cdot)
, given the initial temperature distribution, belong to the class
W_{2}^{r}(\mathbb{R})
. We are again considering a set of random functions similar to the set of
(
17
)

Y_{\delta}(u_{0}(\cdot))=\{\,y_{\xi}(\cdot)\in L_{2}(\mathbb{R}):\mathbb{M}y_{\xi}(\cdot)=Fu_{0}(\cdot),\ \mathbb{D}y_{\xi}(\cdot)\leq\delta\,\}

with the same additional conditions regarding the change in the order of integration.
We define the error of the recovery method
\varphi\colon L_{2}(\mathbb{R})\to L_{2}(\mathbb{R})
as follows

e(T,W_{2}^{r}(\mathbb{R}),\delta,\varphi)=\left(\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}u_{0}(\cdot)\in W_{2}^{r}(\mathbb{R})\\
y_{\xi}(\cdot)\in Y_{\delta}(u_{0}(\cdot))\end{subarray}}\mathbb{M}\left(\|u(T,\cdot)-\varphi(y_{\xi}(\cdot))(\cdot)\|^{2}_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R})}\right)\right)^{1/2}.

The problem is to find the error of optimal recovery

E(T,W_{2}^{r}(\mathbb{R}),\delta)=\inf_{\varphi\colon L_{2}(\mathbb{R})\to L_{2}(\mathbb{R})}e(T,W_{2}^{r}(\mathbb{R}),\delta,\varphi)

and a method on which this infimum is attained.

It is well known (see, for example,
[
3
]
) that for all
t\geq 0
the equality

F(u(t,\cdot))(\lambda)=e^{-\lambda^{2}t}Fu_{0}(\lambda)

holds. It follows from the Parseval equality that

\|u(T,\cdot)-\varphi(y_{\xi}(\cdot))(\cdot)\|^{2}_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R})}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|e^{-\lambda^{2}T}Fu_{0}(\lambda)-F\varphi(y_{\xi}(\cdot))(\lambda)|^{2}\,d\lambda.

Thus, the problem is reduced to problem (
3
) with
\nu(t)=t^{2r}
and
\mu(t)=e^{-t^{2}T}
.
The function
f(\cdot)
which was defined by (
4
), has the form

f(s)=\int_{|t|\leq s}\left(\frac{s^{r}e^{s^{2}T}}{t^{r}e^{t^{2}T}}-1\right)t^{2r}\,dt=2s^{r}e^{s^{2}T}\int_{0}^{s}t^{r}e^{-t^{2}T}\,dt-\frac{s^{2r+1}}{2r+1}.

It is easy to verify that
f(s)\to+\infty
as
s\to+\infty
(the monotonous increase of
f(\cdot)
was noted in the general case). Therefore, the equation
f(s)=\delta^{-2}
has a unique solution, which we denote by
t_{\delta}
.

From Theorem
1
we obtain the following result:

Theorem 3
.

Put

\alpha(t)=\left(1-\frac{t^{r}e^{t^{2}T}}{t_{\delta}^{r}e^{t_{\delta}^{2}T}}\right)_{+}.

Then the equality

E(T,W_{2}^{r}(\mathbb{R}),,\delta)=\delta\biggl(\int_{|t|\leq t_{\delta}}e^{-2t^{2}T}\alpha(t)\,dt\biggr)^{1/2}

holds. Moreover, the method

\varphi(y_{\xi}(\cdot))(t)=F^{-1}\left(e^{-t^{2}T}\alpha(t)y_{\xi}(t)\right)(t)

is optimal.

The deterministic case of this problem was considered in
[
6
]
.

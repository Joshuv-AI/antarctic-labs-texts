Supertraces on queerified algebras
Dimitry Leites a , Irina Shchepochkina b

Abstract
. We describe supertraces on “queerifications” (see arxiv:2203.06917) of the algebras of matrices of “complex size”, algebras of observables of Calogero-Moser model, Vasiliev higher spin algebras, and (super)algebras of pseudo-differential operators. In the latter case, the supertraces establish complete integrability of the analogs of Euler equations to be written.

Primary 17B20, 16W55 Secondary 81Q60, 17B70

The traces on Lie algebras, and even (not odd) supertraces on Lie superalgebras, are known to be very useful, for example, in representation theory, see, e.g.,
[
DSB
]
and references therein. The
odd

Inspired by

[

Preliminaries

1.1.
From associative to Lie

Let
\mathbb{K}
be an algebraically closed ground field of characteristic
p\neq 2
; unless otherwise stated, we consider
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{C}
.

Let
A
be any associative algebra, let
A^{L}
be the Lie algebra whose space is
A
but multiplication is given by the commutator
[a,b]:=ab-ba
for any
a,b\in A
.

Let
A
be a
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded algebra; let
p
denote the parity function. If
A
a
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded associative algebra, let
A^{S}
be the Lie superalgebra whose space is
A
but the multiplication is given by the supercommutator, which by the modern habitual abuse of notation is also denoted
[a,b]
although defined to be
[a,b]:=ab-(-1)^{p(a)p(b)}ba
. Let
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}:=[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
be the first derived Lie algebra (resp. Lie superalgebra), a.k.a. commutant (resp. supercommutant), of the Lie algebra (resp. Lie superalgebra)
\mathfrak{g}
.

Not every
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded algebra
\mathcal{A}
is called
superalgebra
: only if multiplication in
\mathcal{A}
or (if
\mathcal{A}
is associative) in
\mathcal{A}^{S}
depends on the parity.

Recall, that a
trace
(called
supertrace
in the super setting, for emphasis) on a given Liealgebra (resp. Lie superalgebra)
\mathfrak{g}
is a linear function that vanishes on its commutant (resp. supercommutant), so there are
\dim(\mathfrak{g}/[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}])
linearly independent traces (resp. supertraces) on
\mathfrak{g}
, some of the supertraces can even and some of them odd.

1.2.
Queerifications in characteristic
p\neq 2
(from
[
BLLS
]
)

Let
A
be an
associative algebra. The space of the associative algebra
\text{Q}(A)
— the
associative
queerification
of
A
— is
A\oplus\Pi(A)
, where

\Pi
is the change of parity functor, with the same multiplication in
A
and the adjoint action of
A
on
\Pi(A)
; let

\Pi(x)\Pi(y):=xy\text{~for any~~}x,y\in A.

Set
\text{Q}(n):=\text{Q}(\text{Mat}(n))
.

We will be mostly interested in the following
Lie
queerifications

\mathfrak{q}(A)
:

1) for
A
an associative algebra;

2) for
A
a
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded associative algebra (“Liefication” yields
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded Lie algebra
A^{L}
);

3) for
A
an associative superalgebra (this case differs from case 2) because passing to
A^{S}
the supercommutators instead of commutators are considered; “super Liefication” yields Lie superalgebra
A^{S}
which is
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded by parity.

In case 1), as spaces,
\mathfrak{q}(A):={A^{L}\oplus\Pi(A)}
, so
\mathfrak{q}(A)_{\bar{0}}=A^{L}
and
\mathfrak{q}(A)_{\bar{1}}=\Pi(A)
, with the bracket given
by the following expressions and super anti-symmetry, i.e., anti-symmetry amended by the Sign Rule:

(1)

{}[x,y]:=xy-yx;\quad[x,\Pi(y)]:=\Pi(xy-yx);\quad[\Pi(x),\Pi(y)]:=xy+yx\ \text{~for any~~}x,y\in A.

The term “queer”, now conventional, is taken after the Lie
superalgebra
\mathfrak{q}(n):=\mathfrak{q}(\text{Mat}(n))
. (The associative superalgebra
\text{Q}(n)
is an analog of
\text{Mat}(n)
; likewise, the Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{q}(n)
is an analog of
\mathfrak{gl}(n)
for several reasons, for example, due the role of these analogs in Schur’s Lemma and in the classification of central simple superalgebras, see
[
Lsos
, Ch.7]
.) We express the elements
of the Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{q}(n)
by means of a pair of matrices

(2)

(X,Y)\longleftrightarrow\begin{pmatrix}X&Y\\
Y&X\end{pmatrix}\in\mathfrak{gl}(n|n),\text{~~where $X,Y\in\text{Mat}(n)$}.

For any associative
A
, we will similarly denote the elements of
\mathfrak{q}(A)
by pairs
(X,Y)
, where
X,Y\in A
. The brackets between these elements are as follows:

(3)

\begin{array}[]{l}{}[(X_{1},0),(X_{2},0)]:=([X_{1},X_{2}],0),\quad[(X,0),(0,Y)]:=(0,[X,Y]),\\
{}[(0,Y_{1}),(0,Y_{2})]:=(Y_{1}Y_{2}+Y_{2}Y_{1},0).\end{array}

We define Lie queerifications in cases 2) and 3) in the next section.

2.
Traces and supertraces: generalities

Theorem
.

Let
A
be an associative algebra. Then, the estimate of the number of traces in the
3
cases of its Lie queerification are described below.

2.1.1.
Case
1

Let
A
be an associative algebra, then
Q(A):=A\oplus\Pi(A)
is the
associative queerification
of
A
; let
\mathfrak{g}:=A^{L}
be the corresponding Lie algebra and let the Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{q}\mathfrak{g}:=(Q(A))^{S}=\mathfrak{q}(A)
be the
Lie queerification
of
A
.
Clearly,
\mathfrak{q}\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}\oplus\Pi(\mathfrak{g})
as spaces.

Denote, for brevity,
\mathfrak{u}:=(\mathfrak{q}\mathfrak{g})^{\prime}
. By definition the supercommutant
\mathfrak{u}:=\mathfrak{u}_{\bar{0}}\oplus\mathfrak{u}_{\bar{1}}
is spanned by elements
[a,b]
for any
a,b\in\mathfrak{q}\mathfrak{g}
. In particular,
\mathfrak{u}_{\bar{1}}
is spanned by elements of the form
[x,\Pi(y)]=\Pi([x,y])
for any
x,y\in(\mathfrak{q}\mathfrak{g})_{\bar{0}}
, and hence
\mathfrak{u}_{\bar{1}}=\Pi([\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}])
. Therefore, there are as many odd supertraces on
\mathfrak{q}\mathfrak{g}
as there are traces on
\mathfrak{g}
.

Clearly,
\mathfrak{u}_{\bar{0}}
is the sum of two ideals of
\mathfrak{g}
:

\mathfrak{u}_{\bar{0}}=[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]+[\Pi(\mathfrak{g}),\Pi(\mathfrak{g})].

Observe that the second summand does not have to be contained in the first one. Therefore, there are fewer even supertraces on
\mathfrak{q}\mathfrak{g}
than there are traces on
\mathfrak{g}
.

In particular, if
A
has unit
{\mathbbm{1}}
,
then
\mathfrak{u}_{\bar{0}}=\mathfrak{g}
, because
[\Pi({\mathbbm{1}}),\Pi(x)]=2x
for any
x\in\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}
.

For example, if
A=\text{Mat}(n)
, then on
Q(A)
there is an odd trace, nowadays called
queertrace
; it was first defined in
[
BL
]
by the formula

\text{qtr}:\begin{pmatrix}X&Y\\
Y&X\end{pmatrix}\mapsto\text{tr}Y.

2.1.2.
Case
2

Let
A
be a
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded associative algebra,
\mathfrak{g}:=A^{L}
. Clearly,
\mathbb{Z}/2
-grading of

\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded Lie algebra.

Now, consider

\mathfrak{s}_{\bar{0}}=\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}

\mathfrak{s}_{\bar{1}}=\Pi(\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}})

\mathfrak{s}_{\bar{0}}=\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}

The supercommutants are as follows:

\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}=(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime})_{\bar{0}}\oplus(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime})_{\bar{1}}

(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime})_{\bar{0}}=\text{Span}([x,y]=xy-yx,[a,b]=ab-ba),\ (\mathfrak{g}^{\prime})_{\bar{1}}=\text{Span}([x,a]=xa-ax)

\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}=(\mathfrak{s}^{\prime})_{\bar{0}}\oplus(\mathfrak{s}^{\prime})_{\bar{1}}

(\mathfrak{s}^{\prime})_{\bar{0}}=\text{Span}([x,y]=xy-yx,[a,b]=ab+ba),\ (\mathfrak{s}^{\prime})_{\bar{1}}=\text{Span}([x,a]=xa-ax)
.

Therefore,
\mathfrak{s}^{\prime}_{\bar{1}}=\Pi((\mathfrak{g}^{\prime})_{\bar{1}})
, but
a priori
we can not say anything about relations between
(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime})_{\bar{0}}
and
(\mathfrak{s}^{\prime})_{\bar{0}}

2.1.3.
Case
3

Now, let
A=A_{\bar{0}}\oplus A_{\bar{1}}
be an associative superalgebra,

Let

It is also clear that the natural

Let us now compare the supercommutants of

\mathfrak{q}\mathfrak{g}

\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}

We have
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}=(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime})_{\bar{0}}\oplus(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime})_{\bar{1}}

The elements that span homogeneous components of
\mathfrak{q}\mathfrak{g}

\mathfrak{u}_{\bar{0},\bar{0}}

\mathfrak{u}_{\bar{0},\bar{1}}

\mathfrak{u}_{\bar{1},\bar{0}}

\mathfrak{u}_{\bar{1},\bar{1}}

[x,y]=xy-yx

[x,a]

[x,\Pi(a)]

[x,\Pi(y)]

[a,b]=ab+ba

=xa-ax

=\Pi(xa-ax)

=\Pi(xy-yx)

[\Pi(a),\Pi(b)]=ab-ba

[\Pi(a),\Pi(x)]

[a,\Pi(x)]

[a,\Pi(b)]

[\Pi(x),\Pi(y)]=xy+yx

=ax-xa

\mathfrak{u}_{\bar{1},\bar{0}}=(\mathfrak{q}\mathfrak{g})_{\bar{1},\bar{0}}=\Pi(\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}})

3.4.1.

Let, moreover,

Let

L
and
P

(5)

\dot{L}=[L,P],\text{~~where $L,P\in A$ and dot signifies derivative with respect to time $t$},

(\partial_{\tau}+\tau\partial_{t})L=[L,H],\text{~~where $L,H\in A$},

the functions

are integrals in involution with respect to the Poisson bracket defined on the space of functions on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
as follows, see, e.g.,
[
A
]
. We identify
\mathfrak{g}
with the linear functions on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
and for any functions

\{f,g\}(X):=X([df(X),dg(X)])\text{~~for any $X\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}$}.

It seems, nobody considered yet the Euler equations or Lax pairs (
5

A
on which there is an odd trace

, and hence an odd

On

5.1.
Two general facts (
[
S
]
)

1) If there is no (super)trace on an associative (super)algebra
A

Let us prove that

Clearly, it suffices to prove this for the case where

Case 1:

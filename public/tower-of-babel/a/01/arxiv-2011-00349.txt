Wonderful compactifications of Bruhat-Tits buildings in the non-split case
Dorian Chanfi

Abstract
Given an adjoint semisimple group G G over a local field k k , we prove that the maximal Satake-Berkovich compactification of the Bruhat-Tits building of G G can be identified with the one obtained by embedding the building into the Berkovich analytification of the wonderful compactification of G G , extending previous results of Rémy, Thuillier and Werner. In the process, we use the characterisation of the wonderful compactification in terms of Hilbert schemes given by Brion to extend the definition of the wonderful compactification to the case of a non-necessarily split adjoint semisimple group over an arbitrary field and investigate some of its properties pertaining to rational points on the boundary. Lastly, given a finite possibly ramified Galois extension k ′ / k k^{\prime}/k , we take a look at the action of the Galois group on the maximal compactification of the building of G G over k ′ k^{\prime} and check that the Galois-fixed points are precisely the limits of sequences of fixed points in the building over k ′ k^{\prime} , though they may not lie in the Satake-Berkovich compactification of G G over k k .



Introduction

In this article, we study compactifications of Bruhat-Tits buildings and relate them to compactifications of algebraic groups. Bruhat-Tits buildings were first introduced in the work of Iwahori and Matsumoto
[
IM65
]
in the late 1960s, then in a series of articles of Bruhat and Tits
[
BT72
]
,
[
BT84
]
with the aim of studying geometrically the structure of reductive groups over local, or more generally non-archimedean valued, fields. An affine building is a polysimplicial complex covered by subcomplexes called apartments, each of which being isomorphic to a common Euclidean tiling, subject to some very restrictive incidence relations
[
Bro89
, IV.1]
.
Bruhat-Tits theory associates to each reductive group over a local field an affine building on which the group of rational points acts strongly transitively in the sense of
[
Bro89
, V.1]
. This building plays the role of a coarse analogue to a symmetric space for reductive groups over valued fields: It is endowed with a nonpositively curved metric that is invariant under the group action and it provides a parameterisation for maximal compact subgroups through its vertices.

This guiding analogy motivates the search for compactifications of affine buildings, as compactifications of symmetric spaces have played an important role in group theory – as in the proof of Mostow’s strong rigidity theorem – and in number theory, for example via the study of locally symmetric spaces that appear naturally as moduli spaces of algebro-geometric objects of interest.
In
[
GR06
]
, the authors provide some group-theoretic payoff by establishing that the set of vertices of the so-called polyhedral compactification of the Bruhat-Tits building of a semisimple group
G
over a local field
k
parameterises closed amenable subgroups of
G(k)
with connected Zariski closure that are maximal for these properties.

This compactification is the main object of study of this article. First constructed by Landvogt
[
Lan96
]
by a glueing procedure similar to the construction of the building itself, it was incorporated by Werner
[
Wer07
]
into an ordered family of compactifications of which it is the maximal element. This family was recovered by Rémy, Thuillier and Werner in
[
RTW10
]
using Berkovich geometry and proved equivalent to the previous construction in
[
RTW12
]
. The construction proceeds first by embedding the building
\mathcal{B}(G,k)
into the Berkovich analytic space
G^{\an}
associated to
G
, then mapping the result to the analytic space
\Bor(G)^{\an}
associated to the maximal flag variety of
G
. The boundary of the compactification given by the closure of the image then decomposes as the union of the Bruhat-Tits buildings of the semisimple quotients of all the parabolic
k
-subgroups of
G
.

In
[
RTW17
]
, the authors establish that, if
G
is a split semisimple adjoint group defined over a local field
k
, then the polyhedral compactification
\overline{\mathcal{B}}(G,k)
– or maximal Satake-Berkovich compactification as it is called in that context – of the building
\mathcal{B}(G,k)
embeds naturally in the Berkovich analytification
\overline{G}^{\an}
of the wonderful compactification
\overline{G}
of
G
. They prove a compatibility result between the topological stratification of the polyhedral compactification into smaller affine buildings and the algebro-geometric stratification of
\overline{G}
into
(G\times G)
-orbits.

The wonderful compactification was introduced in
[
DCP83
]
for split semisimple adjoint groups defined over
\mathbb{C}
and extended to arbitrary algebraically closed fields in
[
Str87
]
and
[
DCS99
]
. Among its remarkable features, it admits a finite stratification into
(G\times G)
-orbits
X(\tau)
indexed by the types
\tau
of parabolic subgroups of
G
and these orbits fibre over products of flag varieties corresponding to opposite parabolic subgroups.

The upshot of
[
RTW17
]
is that boundary strata of
\overline{\mathcal{B}}(G,k)
corresponding to parabolic subgroups of type
\tau
are mapped to the analytification
X(\tau)^{\an}
of
\overline{G}
.

In this article, we extend this result to the case of a non-split group. Namely, we prove that the maximal Satake-Berkovich compactification of a semisimple adjoint group
G
over a local field
k
can be mapped homeomorphically to the analytification of the wonderful compactification
\overline{G}
of
G
and that the compatibility between boundaries is preserved.

The article splits in three largely independent sections, if one is ready to take the existence the wonderful compactification of a non-split group on faith.

In the first section, we provide a suitable extension of the definition of the wonderful compactification to the case of a general semisimple adjoint group. We prove that, given a semisimple adjoint group
G
over a field
k
and
l
a finite extension that splits
G
, the wonderful compactification of
G_{l}
is endowed with an action of
\Gal(l/k)
by automorphisms and therefore descends to
k
. The existence of this action follows from the description given by Brion
[
Bri03
]
of the wonderful compactification in terms of Hilbert schemes. We also study the
(G\times G)
-orbits and prove that the compactification has no rational points on the boundary if and only if
G
is anisotropic.

In the second section, we establish the following theorem.

Theorem
.

Let
G
be a semisimple adjoint group over a non-archimedean field
k
. Denote by
\overline{G}
its wonderful compactification, by
\mathcal{B}(G,k)
the Bruhat-Tits building of

over
k
and
\overline{\mathcal{B}}(G,k)
its maximal Satake-Berkovich compactification.

1.

There exists a continuous

-equivariant map
\overline{\Theta}:\mathcal{B}(G,k)\times\overline{\mathcal{B}}(G,k)\to\overline{G}^{\an}
such that, for each

\overline{\Theta}(x,\cdot):\overline{\mathcal{B}}(G,k)\to\overline{G}^{\an}

is a

2.

For each

and any parabolic subgroup

maps the boundary stratum

The proof follows the general template of

In the third section, that is essentially independent from the rest of the text, we prove the following result:

Theorem
.

If
k
is a local field and
k^{\prime}/k
is a finite Galois extension, then the set of fixed points of
\overline{\mathcal{B}}(G,k^{\prime})
under the Galois action is precisely the closure of the set of fixed points of the building
\mathcal{B}(G,k)
under the Galois action.

In other words, taking fixed points in the compactification yields no worse results than taking fixed points in the building. The main tool is the description of the compactification
\overline{\mathcal{B}}(G,k^{\prime})
as a union of Bruhat-Tits buildings. In the process, we make crucial use of metric properties of affine buildings. We also make some general reminders about ramification and fixed points under the Galois action in a Bruhat-Tits building and give some explicit examples of ”barbs”, that is points in the fixed locus of
\mathcal{B}(G,k^{\prime})
that do not belong to
\mathcal{B}(G,k)
. We prove by an example that the same phenomenon may occur at the boundary of the building.

1
Wonderful compactifications of semisimple groups

In this section, we summarise the facts that we shall need about wonderful compactifications of semisimple groups and extend known results about the
(G\times G)
-orbits of wonderful compactifications to the more general setting of an arbitrary adjoint (not necessarily split) semisimple group over a field

[
DCP83
]
and
[
Str87
]
in the split case and use the alternative, more intrinsic, construction given by Brion in
[
Bri03
]
to give a definition of the wonderful compactification
\overline{G}
in our more general setting.

1.1
The representation-theoretic construction

Let
k
be a field and
G
a
k
-split adjoint semisimple group. Fix a
k
-split maximal torus

and
B
a Borel subgroup containing

. Let
(V,\rho)
be an irreducible representation of

is the closure
\overline{G}
of the image of the embedding

\rho:\begin{array}[]{ccc}G&\to&\mathbb{P}(\End(V))\\
x&\mapsto&[\rho(x)]\end{array}.

The embedding is
(G\times G)
-equivariant if we let
G\times G
act on

via
(g,h)\cdot x=gxh^{-1}
, and on

(g,h)\cdot[\phi]=[\rho(g)\circ\phi\circ\rho(h)^{-1}]
. This makes

into a

-space. Moreover, it enjoys the following properties:

Theorem 1

(
[
DCS99
, 3.9-3.10]
,
[
Str87
, Theorem 2.1]
)
.

The wonderful compactification of
G
is a smooth projective
(G\times G)
-variety
X
such that

•

There exists a
(G\times G)
-equivariant open immersion
j:G\hookrightarrow Z
.

•

The boundary

is a normal crossings divisor. More precisely, it is the union of
r=\dim T

which intersect transversally. Moreover, for each

, the intersection

is the closure of a single
(G\times G)
-orbit. This correspondence establishes a bijection between subsets of
[\![1,r]\!]
and
(G\times G)
-orbits.

•

The intersection
\bigcap_{i\in I}D_{i}
is the unique closed
(G\times G)
-orbit of

.

In addition, these properties characterise

up to unique

-equivariant isomorphism.

In order to formulate our results, we need some additional information about the
(G\times G)
-orbits of

, namely the fact that they can be realised as fibrations over generalised flag varieties.
Following
[
Bri03

For each

\begin{array}[]{cc}\langle\alpha,\lambda_{\tau}\rangle=1&\text{if }\alpha\in{\tau},\\

Indeed, as

Then, by properness of

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\{\text{Subsets of }\Delta\}&\longleftrightarrow&\{(G\times G)\text{-orbits of }\overline{G}\}\\

Moreover, denoting by

the parabolic subgroup defined by

P_{\tau}=P_{G}(\lambda_{\tau})=\{g\in G,\lambda_{\tau}(t)g\lambda_{\tau}^{-1}(t)\text{ has a limit as }t\to 0\},

In particular, the orbit

A construction via Hilbert schemes

In

Bri03
, Section 2]

The embedding

Let

the variety of Borel subgroups of

(
[
SGA3
, XXII, 5.8.3]

Lemma 2

Denote by

-orbit of the diagonal
\Delta_{X}\in\Hilb(X\times X)(k)

\begin{array}[]{c c c}G\times G&\longrightarrow&Z\\
(g,h)&\longmapsto&(g,h)\cdot\Delta_{X}\end{array}

yields an open immersion

Proof.

To establish this, it is sufficient, by

∎

Precomposing with the

-equivariant isomorphism

-equivariant open immersion

.

Comparison in the split case

In

Consider the closure

Because

is a principal

1.3
The wonderful compactification of an arbitrary adjoint semisimple group

The above comparison theorem motivates the following definition.

Definition 4
.

Let
G
be a semisimple adjoint group over a field

and let
X=\Bor(G)
. We call wonderful compactification of

\overline{G}
the closure of the image of the
(G\times G)
-orbit of the diagonal of

\Hilb(X\times X)

In the first subsection, we gave a description of the boundary of the wonderful compactification of a split semisimple adjoint group. Here, we shall describe the boundary of the wonderful compactification of a general semisimple adjoint group, which will amount to an exercise in Galois descent.

Consider a semisimple adjoint group

[
SGA3
, XIV, 1.1]

. Let

be a finite Galois extension, with Galois group denoted by
\Gamma

such that

Galois action on the boundary orbits

We begin by proving that, under the identification of the
(G_{l}\times G_{l})
-orbits of

\Phi(G_{l},T_{l})

(T_{l},B)

\Gamma
on the
(G_{l}\times G_{l})
-orbits corresponds to the

-action of
\Gamma
on
\mathcal{P}(\Delta)
. Recall that the Galois group
\Gamma

in two ways. On the one hand, it has a natural action
(\gamma,\alpha)\mapsto\gamma(\alpha)
by base change. On the other hand, the basis

allows us to define another action of
\Gamma
– the so-called
\ast

\gamma\in\Gamma

\chi\in X_{l}^{*}(T)

\gamma\ast\chi=w_{\gamma}(\gamma(\chi))
, where
w_{\gamma}\in W(G,T)(l)
is the unique element of the Weyl group such that

\Gamma
on conjugacy classes of maximal proper parabolic subgroups
[

, 2.3]

[

, II.2.1]
.

We now have

Proposition 5
.

The bijection

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\{\text{Subsets of }\Delta\}&\longleftrightarrow&\{(G_{l}\times G_{l})\text{-orbits of }\overline{G}_{l}\}\\
\tau&\longmapsto&X(\tau)=(G_{l}\times G_{l})\cdot e_{(T_{l},B),\tau}.\end{array}

\Gamma
-equivariant for the
\ast
-action on
\Delta

Proof.

Let
{\tau}\subset\Delta
be a type of parabolic subgroups of
G_{l}
and let
\lambda_{\tau}
be the cocharacter associated to
{\tau}
as in 1.1. Let
\gamma\in\Gamma
. Then, we have, for each

\begin{array}[]{cc}\langle\alpha,\gamma(\lambda_{\tau})\rangle=1&\text{if }\alpha\in\gamma({\tau}),\\
\langle\alpha,\gamma(\lambda_{\tau})\rangle=0&\text{if }\alpha\in\gamma(\Delta)\setminus\gamma({\tau}),\end{array}

for the action of
\Gamma
by base change on

and

.
Let

be the element of the Weyl group such that

and

be a lift of

.
Then, similarly, we have :

\begin{array}[]{cc}\langle\alpha,w(\gamma(\lambda_{\tau}))\rangle=1&\text{if }\alpha\in w(\gamma({\tau}))=\gamma\ast{\tau},\\

In other words, we have

e_{(T,B),\gamma\ast{\tau}}=\lim_{t\to 0}w(\gamma(\lambda_{\tau}))(t)=\lim_{t\to 0}\dot{w}\gamma(\lambda_{\tau})(t)\dot{w}^{-1}=(\dot{w},\dot{w})\cdot\gamma(e_{(T_{l},B),\tau}).

{\tau}
to that corresponding to

∎

Because the actions of

\pi_{\tau}(\gamma(e_{(T_{l},B),\tau}))=\gamma(\pi_{\tau}(e_{(T_{l},B),\tau}))

2.1
A primer of Berkovich geometry

We begin by recalling some definitions from Berkovich geometry that will be necessary to formulate and understand the rest of the text.

We call non-archimedean field any field
k
that is equipped with a nontrivial non-archimedean absolute value
|\cdot|
with respect to which it is complete. We call
k
local if it is locally compact.

A non-archimedean extension
k^{\prime}/k
is an isometric extension of non-archimedean fields.

If
(A,||\cdot||)
is a Banach algebra over
k
, we denote by
\rho:f\mapsto\lim||f^{n}||^{1/n}=\inf||f^{n}||^{1/n}
its spectral seminorm. We then set

A^{\circ}=\{x\in A,\rho(f)\leq 1\}

its subalgebra of power-bounded elements and by

A^{\circ\circ}=\{x\in A,\rho(f)<1\}

the ideal of topologically nilpotent elements. We also denote by
\tilde{A}=A^{\circ}/A^{\circ\circ}
the residue ring.

In the following, we will mostly work with analytic spaces originating from varieties, therefore we will dispense with more general definitions. We denote by
k
a non-archimedean field.

•

If
X
is an affine scheme over
k
, then we denote by
X^{\an}
the set of non-archimedean multiplicative seminorms on
k[X]
. If
x\in X^{\an}
and
f\in k[X]
, we will write
|f|(x)
rather than
x(f)
. We endow
X^{\an}
with the coarsest topology that makes the maps
x\mapsto|f|(x)
continuous, for
f
ranging in
k[X]
.

This assignation is functorial and extends to a functor from the category of schemes over
k
to that of analytic spaces over
k
by glueing.

•

Given
r_{1},\dots,r_{n}>0
, we set

k\{r_{1}^{-1}x_{1},\dots,r_{n}^{-1}x_{n}\}=\left\{\sum_{\nu\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}a_{\nu}x^{\nu}\in k[[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]],|a_{\nu}|r_{1}^{\nu_{1}}\dots r_{n}^{\nu_{n}}\underset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0\right\}

and endow it with the norm
\left|\sum_{\nu\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}a_{\nu}x^{\nu}\right|=\max_{\nu\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}|a_{\nu}|r_{1}^{\nu_{1}}\dots r_{n}^{\nu_{n}}
.

•

A Banach algebra
(A,||\cdot||)
over
k
is called
k
-affinoid if there exist
r_{1},\dots,r_{n}>0
and a continuous epimorphism

\phi:k\{r_{1}^{-1}x_{1},\dots,r_{n}^{-1}x_{n}\}\twoheadrightarrow A

such that the induced morphism
k\{\underline{r}^{-1}\underline{x}\}/\ker\phi\to A
is an isometric isomorphism. It is called strictly
k
-affinoid if we may choose the
r_{i}
in the value group
|k^{\times}|
.
The space
\mathcal{M}(A)
of multiplicative seminorms on
A
that are bounded with respect to
||\cdot||
is then called a
k
-affinoid domain. It sits naturally in
\mathbb{A}^{n,\an}
as a compact subspace.

•

In
[
RTW10
, 1.2.4]
, the authors define a construction allowing one to associate to any finitely presented
k^{\circ}
-scheme
\mathcal{X}
a strictly
k
-affinoid domain
\mathcal{X}^{\an}
inside the analytification of the generic fibre
(X\otimes_{k^{\circ}}k)^{\an}
.

•

Given a commutative Banach
k
-algebra
A
, there exists a so-called reduction map

\rho:\mathcal{M}(A)\to\Spec(\tilde{A})

defined as follows: Given a point
x\in\mathcal{M}(A)
, denote by
\mathfrak{p}_{x}=\{f\in A,|f|(x)=0\}
and denote by
\kappa(x)
its completed residue field, ie.

\kappa(x)=\widehat{\mathrm{Frac}(A/\mathfrak{p}_{x})}

with the completion taken with respect to the seminorm given by
x
, which becomes a multiplicative norm on
A/\mathfrak{p}_{x}
. Then, the kernel of the canonical map

A^{\circ}\to\kappa(x)

is a prime ideal containing
A^{\circ\circ}
, which therefore defines a point
\rho(x)\in\Spec(\tilde{A})
.

Then, if
A
is strictly
k
-affinoid,
\rho
is surjective and the inverse image of the set of generic points of
\Spec(\tilde{A})
is the so-called
Shilov boundary

\Gamma(A)
of
\mathcal{M}(A)
, that is, it is a minimal closed subset of
\mathcal{M}(A)
such that, for any
f\in A
, we have

\max_{x\in\mathcal{M}(A)}|f|(x)=\max_{x\in\Gamma(A)}|f|(x).

2.2
Satake-Berkovich compactifications

We now remind the reader of the results of
[
RTW10
]
that we shall use in the following.

We begin with partial functoriality of Bruhat-Tits buildings with respect to field extensions – a property that will be ubiquitous in the following.

Under the assumptions on the non-archimedean field
k
brought forth in
[
RTW10
, 1.3.4]
, there exist

1.

A subcategory
\textbf{E}_{0}(k)
of the category of non-archimedean extensions of
k
that is cofinal, that is for any non-archimedean extension
k^{\prime}/k
, there exists an extension
K/k^{\prime}
such that
K\in\textbf{E}_{0}(k)

2.

A functor
\mathcal{B}(G,\cdot)
on the category
\textbf{E}_{0}(k)
that associates to each extension
K^{\prime}/K
in
\textbf{E}_{0}(k)
a
G(K)
-equivariant injection

\mathcal{B}(G,K)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{B}(G,K^{\prime}).

Moreover, if

is discretely valued with a perfect residue field (eg. if

), then we may take

to be the whole category of non-archimedean extensions of
k
.
In the following, all non-archimedean extensions will be assumed to lie in
\textbf{E}_{0}(k)
.

Finally, if
x\in\mathcal{B}(G,k)
and

, then we denote by
x_{K}
the image of

in
\mathcal{B}(G,K)
under the canonical embedding

\mathcal{B}(G,k)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{B}(G,K)

associated to the inclusion
k\subset K
.

Next, recall from
[
RTW10
]
that there exists a canonical embedding
\vartheta:\mathcal{B}(G,k)\to G^{\an}
such that, for each non-archimedean extension

, the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{B}(G,k^{\prime})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G_{k^{\prime}}^{\an}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{B}(G,k)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G^{\an}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\vartheta_{k^{\prime}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(pr_{k^{\prime}/k})^{\an}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\vartheta}

(1)

commutes, where the leftmost vertical arrow comes from partial functoriality with respect to field extensions.

The idea of the embedding can be understood from the following two observations:

1.

If
G
is split and
x
is a special vertex in
\mathcal{B}(G,k)
, then the parahoric subgroup
\mathfrak{G}_{x}^{\circ}
associated to

for each non-archimedean extensions

\{x_{K}\}=\mathcal{B}(G,K)^{\mathfrak{G}_{x}^{\circ}(K^{\circ})}

2.

Any point in the building can be made into a special vertex in the building of
G_{K}
for
K/k
a suitable non-archimedean extension such that

is split.
[
RTW10

We then associate to each point
x
of the building the strictly affinoid subgroup

G_{x}=\pr_{K/k}^{\an}(\mathfrak{G}_{x_{K}}^{\circ})^{\an}

of
G^{\an}
, where
\mathfrak{G}_{x_{K}}^{\circ}
is the connected parahoric subgroup associated to

. This analytic subgroup refines the parahoric group scheme associated to

K/k
, we have

G_{x}(K)=\Stab_{G(K)}(x_{K}).

The advantage of this construction over the algebraic one is that the subgroup
G_{x}
is uniquely associated to
x
, whereas the integral model of

associated to
x
by Bruhat-Tits theory depends only on the enclosure
\cl(x)
, that is the facet of the building that contains

. The subgroup

in turn happens to be the holomorphically convex hull of a single point in
G^{\an}
, its

, which we denote by
\vartheta(x)
.

From this construction is then deduced a family of
G(k)
-equivariant maps

(\vartheta_{t}:\mathcal{B}(G,k)\to\Par_{t}(G)^{\an})_{t}

indexed by
k
-rational types
t
of parabolic
k
-subgroups of
G
. More precisely, for each parabolic
k
-subgroup
P
, we define a map
\lambda_{P}:G\to\Par(G)
by mapping
g
to
gPg^{-1}
. Then, the composition
\lambda_{P}^{\an}\circ\vartheta
is easily proved
[
RTW10
, Lemma 2.14]
to be solely dependent on the type of

and denoted by
\vartheta_{t(P)}
. The results of
[
RTW10
, section 3.4]
then establish that, denoting the closure of their respective images by
\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{\tau}(G,k)
, the induced maps

\vartheta_{t}:\mathcal{B}(G,k)\to\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{t}(G,k)

are continuous, open, and injective if

is non-degenerate (if

is almost simple, it is equivalent to it being the type of a proper parabolic subgroup).

These constructions are referred to as
Berkovich
or
Satake-Berkovich
compactifications.

In the special case where

, the boundary admits the following description:

Theorem 9

(
[
RTW10
, Theorem 4.1]
)
.

For each parabolic subgroup
P
of
G
, there exists a canonical
P(k)
-equivariant embedding
\mathcal{B}(P_{\sss},k)\hookrightarrow\Bor(G)^{\an}
, where
P_{\sss}=P/R(P)
is the semisimple quotient of
P
.
Moreover, the buildings
\mathcal{B}(P_{\sss},k)
, as

runs over all parabolic subgroups of

, define a stratification of
\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{\varnothing}(G,k)
into locally closed subspaces:

\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{\varnothing}(G,k)=\bigsqcup_{P\in\Par(G)(k)}\mathcal{B}(P_{\sss},k).

For an arbitrary type

, the description is analogous but the range of parabolic subgroups involved must be restricted to the

-relevant parabolic subgroups
[
RTW10
, Definition 3.7]
.

2.3
Wonderful compactifications

In addition to defining the embedding
\vartheta
, the article
[
RTW10
]
also defines a ”relative position” map

\Theta:\mathcal{B}(G,k)\times\mathcal{B}(G,k)\to G^{\an}

using
\vartheta
. The idea is, as for
\vartheta
, to exploit the high transitivity properties and compatibility with field extensions earned by passing to Berkovich spaces. Specifically, crucial in both cases is the fact that, for any two points
x,y\in\mathcal{B}(G,k)
, there exist an affinoid field extension
K/k
and an element
g\in G(K)
such that
gx=y
. Here, it allows to set
\Theta(x,y)=\pr_{K/k}(g\vartheta_{K}(x))
, where
\vartheta_{K}:\mathcal{B}(G,K)\to G_{K}^{\an}
is the embedding over
K
. The commutativity of the diagram
1
from the previous section then allows one to prove the independence on
g
and
K
.

This map is extended in
[
RTW17
, Section 2]
, in the case where
G
is split over
k
, to a map

\overline{\Theta}:\mathcal{B}(G,k)\times\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{\varnothing}(G,k)\to\overline{G}^{\an}.

The construction, which spans the entirety of the second section proceeds by first extending the map to partial compactifications of apartments as follows.
Consider a Killing couple
(T,B)
and
x
a special point of the apartment
A=A(T,k)
associated to
T
in
\mathcal{B}(G,k)
. Denote by
\Phi^{+}=\Phi(B,T)
and
\Phi^{-}=-\Phi(B,T)
the sets of positive and negative roots corresponding to
B
. The point
x
defines a simplicial isomorphism between
A
and
X_{*}(T)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
or, equivalently,
\Hom_{\mathrm{Grp}}(X^{*}(T),\mathbb{R}_{>0})
. One then sets
\overline{A}^{B}=\Hom_{\Mon}(\langle\Phi^{-}\rangle,\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
, where
\langle\Phi^{-}\rangle
is the monoid generated by
\Phi^{-}
in
X^{*}(T)
, and defines the embedding
A\hookrightarrow\overline{A}^{B}
by restriction. Geometrically, this amounts to compactifying only the Weyl cone

\mathfrak{C}(B)=\{x\in A(T,k)\,|\,\forall\alpha\in\Phi^{+},\alpha(x)\geq 1\}

by allowing
\alpha(x)
to assume infinite values for positive
\alpha
.

The reason for introducing these partial compactifications is twofold. On the one hand,
\mathcal{B}(G,k)\times\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{\varnothing}(G,k)
is covered by the products
A\times\overline{A}^{B}
for all pairs
(T,B)
. On the other hand, the image of any point
(x,y)\in A\times\overline{A}^{B}
lies in the analytification of an affine scheme, namely the partial compactification of the open Bruhat cell of the wonderful completion of
G

[
RTW17
, Proposition 2.3]
, which makes these compactifications amenable to explicit computations.

The global construction is achieved by glueing the embeddings
\Theta_{(T,B)}:\mathcal{A}\times\overline{A}^{B}
for all pairs
(T,B)
.

The main result
[
RTW17
, Theorem 4.1]
of the article states that, if
G
is split, then, for any
x\in\mathcal{B}(G,k)
, the map
\overline{\Theta}(x,\cdot):\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{\varnothing}(G,k)\to\overline{G}^{\an}
is a
G(k)
-equivariant homeomorphism onto its image for the
G(k)
-action on
\overline{G}^{\an}
by left translation.

In the process,
[
RTW17
, Proposition 3.1]
shows a remarkable compatibility between the boundary structures of
\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{\varnothing}(G,k)
and that of
\overline{G}
. Namely:

•

For each proper parabolic subgroup
P
of
G
of type
\tau
, the map
\overline{\Theta}(x,\cdot)
maps the boundary stratum
\mathcal{B}(P_{\sss},k)
to the boundary stratum
X(\tau)^{\an}
in
\overline{G}^{\an}
.

•

Furthermore, letting
\pi_{\tau}^{\an}:X(\tau)^{\an}\to(\Par_{\tau}(G)\times\Par_{\tau^{\opp}}(G))^{\an}
be the analytification of the fibration defined in 1.1, we have for each
y\in\mathcal{B}(P_{\sss},k)
,

\pi_{\tau}^{\an}(\overline{\Theta}(x,y))=(P,\vartheta_{\tau}(x)).

In other words, the fibrations
\pi_{\tau}^{\an}
separate the boundary components associated to parabolic subgroups of a single type
\tau
.

2.4
Comparison of the compactifications in the general case

The extension of the above results to the case of an arbitrary reductive group follows from the split case, from functoriality of buildings with respect to field extensions, and from the descent of the fibrations constructed in section
1.3
. As in the previous case, the idea is to prove that each of the strata mentioned in Theorem
9
is mapped to a different fiber of the analytification of the fibration defined in
1.1
, and then prove that the restriction to each stratum is injective. The first part of this statement follows formally from the split case and the second part requires some explicit computations.

Definition of the embedding

Our first task is to define the extension

\overline{\Theta}:\mathcal{B}(G,k)\times\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{\varnothing}(G,k)\to\overline{G}^{\an}

of the map
\Theta
in our situation. Thankfully, the construction of this map, which occupies a large portion of
[
RTW17
]
can be deduced from the split case as follows:
Let
k^{\prime}/k
be a non-archimedean extension that splits
G
. Then, we let
\overline{\Theta}
be the only map that makes the following diagram commute:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{B}(G,k^{\prime})\times\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{\varnothing}(G,k^{\prime})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{G}_{k^{\prime}}^{\an}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{B}(G,k)\times\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{\varnothing}(G,k)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{G}^{\an}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{\Theta}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pr_{k^{\prime}/k}^{\an}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{\Theta}}

.

The commutativity of the diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{B}(G,k^{\prime\prime})\times\mathcal{B}(G,k^{\prime\prime})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G_{k^{\prime\prime}}^{\an}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{B}(G,k^{\prime})\times\mathcal{B}(G,k^{\prime})}

Reduction to a stratum

We first establish that, for each

Proof.

Let

1.

Let

Then, the images

The point

Note that, with these identifications, we have for any point

\langle\chi,p_{K}\rangle=\underbrace{\langle\chi_{|S},p\rangle}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{duality pairing between}\\

Assume that

Then, by the above identification, there must exist a root

|\phi|(\Theta(x,y_{n}))=\left(\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\Phi\\

∎

Putting all of the above facts together, we finally get point 1 of the theorem cited in the introduction:

For each

-equivariant embedding.

Let

and

of

∎

Moreover, in the case of a local field, this theorem does yield an identification between compactifications (point 3 in the theorem cited in the introduction).

Corollary 15

If

is a homeomorphism onto the closure of the image of

in
\overline{G}^{\an}
under
\overline{\Theta}(x,\cdot)

Assume that

3
Galois-fixed points in the compactification

Let
G
be a semisimple group over a local field
k
. In this section, given a Galois extension
k^{\prime}/k
, we examine the fixed points of
\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{\varnothing}(G,k^{\prime})
under the action of
\Gal(k^{\prime}/k)
. We check that the fixed points are precisely the limits of the fixed points in the building. In particular, in the case of a wildly ramified extension
k^{\prime}/k
, the fixed locus is larger than
\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{\varnothing}(G,k)
and contains undesirable components, the size of which is controlled by the wildness of the extension as follows from a result of Rousseau.

We begin with a refresher on fixed points in Bruhat-Tits theory.

3.1
Fixed points in the building

Unramified descent

In the case of an unramified extension
k^{\prime}/k
, the fixed point set
\mathcal{B}(G,k^{\prime})^{\Gal(k^{\prime}/k)}
is an affine building and
\iota_{k,k^{\prime}}
identifies it with the Bruhat-Tits building of
G

[
Pra20
, Section 3]
. Moreover, the simplicial structure of
\mathcal{B}(G,k)
is very easy to relate to that of
\mathcal{B}(G,k^{\prime})
: The simplices of
\mathcal{B}(G,k)
are precisely the intersections of simplices of
\mathcal{B}(G,k^{\prime})
with the fixed-point set.
[
Pra20
, 3.2]

Ramification and barbs

In the tamely ramified case, the fixed point set is still a building and identified to the Bruhat-Tits building of
G
(see
[
Rou77
]
and
[
Prant
]
). However, the simplicial structure of
\mathcal{B}(G,k)
does not relate to that of
\mathcal{B}(G,k^{\prime})
as neatly as in the unramified case.

In the general case, however, the fixed point set might not be a building. It contains the building of
G
over
k
but may contain some extra fixed points (”barbs”). The size of these extraneous components is bounded by the wildness of the extension. Recall that, if
k^{\prime}/k
is a finite Galois extension with residue characteristic
p
, then its wildness
s(k^{\prime}/k)
is the
p
-adic valuation of its ramification index. Then, we have the following statement:

Theorem 16

(
[
Rou77
, Prop 5.2.7]
)
.

There exists a constant
C
depending only on the absolute rank of
G
such that, for each non archimedean Galois extension
k^{\prime}/k
, and for each
x\in\mathcal{B}(G,l)^{\Gal(k^{\prime}/k)}
, we have

d(x,\mathcal{B}(G,k))\leq Cs(l/k).

Example 17
.

Consider the case when
k=\mathbb{Q}_{p}
,
k^{\prime}=\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\sqrt{p})
, and
G=\mathrm{SL}_{3,k}
. Let
\varpi=\sqrt{p}
. Then, the Galois group of the extension
k^{\prime}/k
is generated by the involution
\sigma:a+b\varpi\mapsto a-b\varpi
.

Note that
\mathcal{O}_{k}=\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and
\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}=\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\varpi]
.

Recall from eg.
[
Gar97
, 19.1]
that, in this case, the Bruhat-Tits building of
G
may be described as a simplicial complex of dimension 2 whose vertices are homothety classes of
\mathcal{O}_{k}
-submodules of
k^{3}
of maximal rank and where two vertices
[M_{1}]
and
[M_{2}]
are adjacent if and only if
M_{1}
and

may be chosen in such a way that
pM_{1}\subsetneq M_{2}\subsetneq M_{1}
.
Then, the inclusion map

may be described at the level of vertices as mapping the homothety class of an
\mathcal{O}_{k}
-lattice
M
of

to the homothety class of the
\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}
-lattice
M\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{k}}\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}
in
l^{3}
. Note that, because

is ramified, this map does not preserve adjacency.

Consider
e_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\\
0\end{pmatrix}
,
e_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
1\\
0\end{pmatrix}
and
e_{3}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
0\\

. Then, the subgroup
\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\
0&1&0\\
\varpi\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}&0&1\end{pmatrix}
acts transitively on the alcoves incident to the edge
\left[\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}e_{1}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}e_{2}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}\varpi e_{3}\right]
\bullet
\left[\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}e_{1}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}\varpi e_{2}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}\varpi e_{3}\right]
\bullet

aside from
\left[\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}e_{1}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}e_{2}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}\varpi e_{3}\right]
\bullet
\left[\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}e_{1}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}\varpi e_{2}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}\varpi e_{3}\right]
\bullet
\left[\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}e_{1}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}e_{2}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}e_{3}\right]
\bullet

An alcove containing these two vertices is completely determined by its third vertex, hence the alcoves incident to the edge
\left[\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}e_{1}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}e_{2}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}\varpi e_{3}\right]
\bullet
\left[\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}e_{1}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}\varpi e_{2}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}\varpi e_{3}\right]
\bullet

are precisely
\left[\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}e_{1}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}e_{2}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}\varpi e_{3}\right]
\bullet
\left[\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}e_{1}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}\varpi e_{2}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}\varpi e_{3}\right]
\bullet
\left[\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}e_{1}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}e_{2}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}e_{3}\right]
\bullet

and the
\left[\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}e_{1}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}e_{2}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}\varpi e_{3}\right]
\bullet
\left[\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}e_{1}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}\varpi e_{2}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}\varpi e_{3}\right]
\bullet
\left[\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}(\varpi^{-1}e_{1}+te_{3})\oplus\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}e_{2}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}\varpi e_{3}\right]
\bullet

for

. Because
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
and
e_{3}
are in

, the Galois involution fixes
[\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}e_{1}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}e_{2}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}e_{3}]
and
[\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}\varpi^{-1}e_{1}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}e_{2}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}\varpi e_{3}]
. Moreover, the quick computation below proves that there are no other fixed vertices if

, but that all the above vertices are fixed if

.

Indeed, the vertex
[\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}(\varpi^{-1}e_{1}+te_{3})\oplus\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}e_{2}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}e_{3}]
is fixed if and only if

-\varpi^{-1}e_{1}+\sigma(t)e_{3}=\alpha(\varpi^{-1}e_{1}+e_{3})+\beta e_{2}+\gamma\varpi e_{3}

for some
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in l
. We must have
\beta=0
and

and therefore

In other words, the point
[\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}e_{1}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}(\varpi^{-1}te_{1}+e_{2})]
is fixed if and only if

is not invertible. If

with

and

is invertible if and only if

is invertible in

, that is if and only if

.

If

, then the alcove
\left[\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}e_{1}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}e_{2}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}\varpi e_{3}\right]
\bullet
\left[\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}e_{1}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}\varpi e_{2}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}\varpi e_{3}\right]
\bullet
\left[\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}(\varpi^{-1}e_{1}+e_{3})\oplus\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}e_{2}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{k^{\prime}}\varpi e_{3}\right]
\bullet

is fixed under the action of the Galois group, whereas only the bottom edge actually lies in
\mathcal{B}(G,k)
.

3.2
Fixed points in the compactification

In this section, we prove that the situation for Galois-fixed points in the maximal Satake-Berkovich compactification of the building is no worse than that of the building, in the sense that Galois-fixed points in the compactification are limits of fixed points in the building. This is consistent with the observation that the boundary of the compactification is a union of Bruhat-Tits buildings of smaller rank.

Theorem 18
.

If
k^{\prime}/k
is a finite Galois extension with group
\Gamma
, we have:

(\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{\varnothing}(G,k^{\prime}))^{\Gamma}=\overline{\mathcal{B}(G,k^{\prime})^{\Gamma}},

with the closure in the right-hand side taken in
\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{\varnothing}(G,k^{\prime}).

Note that, because
\Gamma
acts continuously, we must have
\overline{\mathcal{B}(G,k^{\prime})^{\Gamma}}\subset(\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{\varnothing}(G,k^{\prime}))^{\Gamma}
.

The proof of the theorem therefore reduces to proving the reverse inclusion. That proof proceeds in three steps. A key observation is the fact that, if
P
is a parabolic
k
-subgroup of
G
,
M
a Levi factor of
P
and
S
a maximal split torus of
M_{k^{\prime}}
, then we may map the apartment
A(S,k^{\prime})
to the building
\mathcal{B}(P_{\sss},k^{\prime})
in two ways. One is given abstractly by Bruhat-Tits theory: There is a natural
M(k^{\prime})\rtimes\Gamma
-equivariant identification

\pi:\mathcal{B}^{e}(M,k^{\prime})\simeq\mathcal{B}(P_{\sss},k^{\prime})\times\Hom(Z(M),\mathbb{R}_{>0})

which restricts to a canonical projection
A(S,k^{\prime})\twoheadrightarrow A(\overline{S},k^{\prime})
, where

\overline{S}=S/Z(M)\subset M/Z(M)\simeq P_{\sss}.

On the other hand, given a cocharacter
\lambda\in X_{*}(S)
such that
P=P_{G}(\lambda)
, we prove below that, for each
x\in A(S,k^{\prime})
, the limit
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\lambda^{n}\cdot x
exists in
\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{\varnothing}(G,k^{\prime})
and lies in
A(\overline{S},k^{\prime})
, viewed as a subset of the boundary of
A(S,k^{\prime})
, which defines a second map
A(S,k^{\prime})\to A(\overline{S},k^{\prime})
. Moreover, under the above identifications, the two maps coincide.

The strategy is then the following: Given a Galois-fixed point
x\in\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{\varnothing}(G,k^{\prime})^{\Gamma}
, lying in an apartment
A(\overline{S},k^{\prime})
in the stratum
\mathcal{B}(P_{\sss},k^{\prime})
, we first establish that the parabolic subgroup
P
is defined over
k
. Next, if
M
is a Levi factor of
P
that is defined over
k
and
S
the inverse image of
\overline{S}
in
M
, we prove that there exists a Galois-fixed point
\tilde{x}_{0}\in A(S,k^{\prime})
such that
\pi(\tilde{x}_{0})=x
. Finally, we prove that if
\lambda\in X_{*}(S)
is a
k
-rational cocharacter such that
P=P_{G}(\lambda)
, then
\lambda^{n}\cdot\tilde{x}_{0}
is a sequence of Galois-fixed points that converges to
x
.

Proof.

Let
x\in(\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{\varnothing}(G,k^{\prime}))^{\Gamma}
. Assume that
x\not\in\mathcal{B}(G,k^{\prime})
. Then, by
[
RTW10
, Lemma 4.12]
, the Zariski closure of its stabiliser in
G(k^{\prime})
is a proper parabolic
k^{\prime}
-subgroup
P
of
G_{k^{\prime}}
and the boundary stratum of
x
in
\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{\varnothing}(G,k^{\prime})
identifies with
\mathcal{B}(P_{\sss},k^{\prime})
. Because
x
is
\Gamma
-invariant, so must be its stabiliser and the Zariski closure of its stabiliser. Hence,
P
is defined over
k
. Moreover, because the embedding
\mathcal{B}(P_{\sss},k^{\prime})\hookrightarrow\Bor(G_{k^{\prime}})^{\an}
is
\Gamma
-equivariant, as is clear on the definition
[
RTW10
, 4.1.1]
, the induced action of
\Gamma
on the boundary stratum of

coincides with the action of
\Gamma
on
\mathcal{B}(P_{\sss},k^{\prime})
given by Bruhat-Tits theory applied to
P_{\sss}
. It then follows that
x\in\mathcal{B}(P_{\sss},k^{\prime})^{\Gamma}
.
Let
\overline{S}\subset P_{\sss}
be a maximal
k^{\prime}
-split
k^{\prime}
-torus whose associated apartment
A(\overline{S},k^{\prime})
contains
x
.
Fix a Levi factor
M
of
P
that is defined over
k
and let
S
be the unique maximal
k^{\prime}
-split
k^{\prime}
-torus of
M
that lifts
\overline{S}
.

Then, we have a
\Gamma
-equivariant and
M(k^{\prime})
-equivariant identification

\mathcal{B}^{e}(M,k^{\prime})\simeq\mathcal{B}(P_{ss},k^{\prime})\times\Hom(Z(M),\mathbb{R}_{>0}).

Furthermore, the projection

\pi:\mathcal{B}^{e}(M,k^{\prime})\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{B}(P_{ss},k^{\prime})

is
\Gamma
-equivariant and we have
\pi^{-1}(A(\overline{S},k^{\prime}))=A(S,k^{\prime})
. Set
A=A(S,k^{\prime})
.
In particular, the fibre
F=\pi^{-1}(x)
is stable under the action of
\Gamma
and contained in
A
as an affine subspace.

Let
x_{0}\in\pi^{-1}(x)
and
\Gamma\cdot x_{0}
be its orbit under
\Gamma
. We claim that the

\tilde{x}_{0}
of
\Gamma\cdot x_{0}
, defined as the center of the unique ball in
\mathcal{B}^{e}(M,k^{\prime})
of minimal radius containing
\Gamma\cdot x_{0}
, is then fixed by
\Gamma
and lies in the fibre
\pi^{-1}(x)
. The existence and uniqueness of the circumcenter of a bounded subset is a consequence of the CAT(0) property of the metric on
\mathcal{B}^{e}(M,k^{\prime})
– see
[
Bro89
, Chapter VI, Theorem 2]
. The uniqueness property and the fact that
\Gamma
acts on
\mathcal{B}^{e}(M,k^{\prime})
by isometries immediately implies that
\tilde{x}_{0}
is fixed under
\Gamma
.

We prove that
\tilde{x}_{0}
lies in the fibre

in two steps: First, by proving that
\tilde{x}_{0}
lies in the apartment

, then by proving that it lies in the fibre

.

First, recall
[
Bro89
, 3A]
that there exists a

-Lipschitz retraction

\rho:\mathcal{B}(G,k^{\prime})\to A(S,k^{\prime}).

Let
r_{\min}
be such that
\overline{B}(\tilde{x}_{0},r_{\min})
is the unique ball of minimal radius containing
\Gamma\cdot x_{0}
. Then, because
\rho
leaves
\Gamma\cdot x_{0}
invariant and is
1
-Lipschitz, we must have
\Gamma\cdot x_{0}\subset\overline{B}(\rho(\tilde{x}_{0}),r_{\min})
. Because the ball of minimal radius containing the orbit is unique, we must have
\rho(\tilde{x}_{0})=\tilde{x}_{0}
or, in other words,
\tilde{x}_{0}\in A(S,k^{\prime})
. It follows that
\tilde{x}_{0}
is the center of the minimal bounding ball for
\Gamma\cdot x_{0}
, this time viewed as a subset of the affine space
A
. Denote by

of the minimal bounding ball
\overline{B}_{A}(\tilde{x}_{0},r_{\min})
is the ball
\overline{B}_{F}(p(\tilde{x}_{0}),r_{\min})
. That ball still contains

, hence the ball
\overline{B}_{A}(p(\tilde{x}_{0}),r_{\min})
is a minimal bounding ball for

. By uniqueness of the minimal bounding ball in Euclidean spaces, we now have

, and therefore

, ie.
\pi(x_{0})=x
.

Let
\lambda\in X_{*}(P)
be a
k
-rational
cocharacter such that

. Then, for all

, we have

\lambda^{n}\cdot\tilde{x}_{0}\in\mathcal{B}(G,k^{\prime})^{\Gamma}

and we claim that

\lambda^{n}\cdot\tilde{x}_{0}\underset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}x.

To prove this, we introduce some coordinates. The computation is ultimately simple but, unfortunately, requires a bit of work to set up.

The choice of a special point
o\in A
determines a simplicial isomorphism

A\simeq\Hom(X^{*}(S),\mathbb{R}_{>0}).

Recall from
[
RTW10
, Proposition 3.35]
that the closure of the apartment
A
in
\overline{\mathcal{B}}_{\varnothing}(G,k^{\prime})
is the compactification of

with respect to the Weyl fan, that is, under our identification, the fan consisting of the cones

\mathfrak{C}(P_{0})=\{\alpha\leq 1,\alpha\in-\Phi(P_{0},S)\},

with
P_{0}
ranging among the minimal parabolic subgroups of
G_{k^{\prime}}
containing
S
.

In our case, letting
P_{0}
be a minimal parabolic
k^{\prime}
-subgroup of
P
containing
S
, our initial point

actually lies in the partial compactification
\overline{A}^{P_{0}}
where only the cone
\mathfrak{C}(P_{0})
is compactified. Denote by
{}_{k^{\prime}}\Delta^{-}
the basis of corresponding to

and by
{}_{k^{\prime}}\Phi^{-}=-\Phi(P_{0},S)
the system of negative roots associated to

.
Moreover, under this identification, the intersection
\mathcal{B}(P_{ss},k^{\prime})\cap\overline{A}^{P_{0}}
corresponds to the locus of the points
z\in\overline{A}^{P_{0}}
where

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\langle\alpha,z\rangle=0&\text{if}&\alpha\in\Phi(R_{u}(P^{\opp}),S)\\
\langle\alpha,z\rangle>0&\text{if}&\alpha\in\Phi(M,S)\cap\,_{k^{\prime}}\Phi^{-}\end{array},

that is, the apartment
A(\overline{S},k^{\prime})\subset\mathcal{B}(P_{ss},k^{\prime})
.
Finally, under this identification, the map

\pi_{|A}:A(S,k^{\prime})\twoheadrightarrow A(\overline{S},k^{\prime})

given by Bruhat-Tits theory identifies with the projection

\Hom(X^{*}(S),\mathbb{R}_{>0})\twoheadrightarrow\Hom(X^{*}(\overline{S}),\mathbb{R}_{>0})

coming from the projection
S\twoheadrightarrow\overline{S}
that identifies
X^{*}(\overline{S})
with the subset

\{\lambda\}^{\perp}=\{\chi\in X^{*}(S),\langle\chi,\lambda\rangle=1\}.

Because the point
\tilde{x}_{0}
is a preimage of
x
under
\pi
, we have

\langle\alpha,\tilde{x}_{0}\rangle=\langle\alpha,x\rangle\text{ for all }\alpha\in\Phi(M,S)=\Phi(P_{ss},\overline{S}).

Moreover, because
P=P_{G}(\lambda)
, we have for each
\alpha\in\Phi(G,S)
(in multiplicative notation):

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\langle\alpha,\lambda\rangle=1&\text{ if }&\alpha\in\Phi(M,S)\\
\langle\alpha,\lambda\rangle>1&\text{ if }&\alpha\in\Phi(R_{u}(P),S)\end{array}.

Consequently, we have for any
\alpha\in\,_{k^{\prime}}\Phi^{-}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
:

\begin{array}[]{ccccc}\langle\alpha,\lambda^{n}\cdot\tilde{x}_{0}\rangle&=&\langle\alpha,x\rangle&\text{if}&\alpha\in\Phi(M,S)\\
\langle\alpha,\lambda^{n}\cdot\tilde{x}_{0}\rangle&=&\underbrace{\langle\alpha,\lambda\rangle^{n}}_{\underset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0}\langle\alpha,\tilde{x}_{0}\rangle&\text{if}&\alpha\in\Phi(R_{u}(P^{\opp}),S))\end{array}.

Whence, for each
\alpha\in\,_{k^{\prime}}\Phi^{-}

\langle\alpha,\lambda^{n}\cdot\tilde{x}_{0}\rangle\underset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\langle\alpha,x\rangle&\text{if }\alpha\in\Phi(M,S)\\
0=\langle\alpha,x\rangle&\text{if }\alpha\in\Phi(R_{u}(P^{\opp},S))\end{array}\right..

Because
(_{k^{\prime}}\Phi^{-}\cap\Phi(M,S))\cup\Phi(R_{u}(P^{\opp}),S)
spans
\langle_{k^{\prime}}\Phi^{-}\rangle
as a monoid, we deduce

\langle\alpha,\lambda^{n}\cdot\tilde{x}_{0}\rangle\underset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\langle\alpha,x\rangle

for each
\alpha\in\langle_{k^{\prime}}\Phi^{-}\rangle
and therefore

\lambda^{n}\cdot\tilde{x}_{0}\underset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}x.

∎

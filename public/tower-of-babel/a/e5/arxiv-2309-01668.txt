Nonequilibrium capillary self-assembly
S. J. Thomson 1 , 2 {}^{1},2 , J.-W. Barotta 2 , and Daniel M. Harris 2

Abstract




Macroscopic objects supported by surface tension at the fluid interface can self-assemble through the action of capillary forces arising from interfacial deformations. The resulting self-assembled structures are ordered but remain trapped in one of potentially many metastable states in the capillary energy landscape. This contrasts with microscopic colloidal self-assembly where thermal fluctuations excite transitions between geometrically distinct ground-state configurations. We herein utilize supercritical Faraday waves to drive structural rearrangements between metastable states of few-particle clusters of millimetric spheres bound by capillary attractions at the fluid interface. Using a combination of experiments and theoretical modelling, we demonstrate how the occupation probabilities of different cluster topologies and transition statistics are controlled by the level of the vibrational forcing and the spatial extent of long-range capillary forces. Our results demonstrate how self-assembly dynamics and statistics may be manipulated across scales by controlling the strength of fluctuations and by tuning the properties of the particle interaction-potential.

When combined with multi-particle interactions, this catalogue of techniques gives rise to potentially many metastable states in the capillary energy landscape that the assembling particles can occupy. However passive, non-Brownian particles are irreversibly locked-in to the resulting self-assembled structure in the absence of an external force or driving field. This lack of dynamic reconfigurability contrasts with the microscopic realm where thermal fluctuations are sufficient to excite transitions between geometrically distinct ground-state configurations of colloidal clusters held together by purely short-range depletion forces

;
Perry et al. 2015

We here substitute the role of a heat bath with a field of periodically forced supercritical Faraday waves to drive structural rearrangements in macroscopic clusters of spherical particles bound by capillary attractions (Fig.

We demonstrate experimentally how the cluster occupation probabilities and transition statistics are controlled by the strength of the vibrational forcing and use theoretical modelling to interrogate the role of long-range capillary forces on the assembly statistics. Our results shed light on how reconfigurable self-assembly may be manipulated across length-scales by exploiting fine control over the strength of fluctuations and properties of the particle interaction-potential.

In both thermal colloids held together by depletion forces
Perry et al. 2015
and acoustic assembly
Lim et al. 2019
, short-range (much less than a particle radius) interactions render the C, T and P cluster topologies energetically degenerate. In contrast the range of interactions is set by
l_{c}\sim R
in the present capillary system. Consequently,
U
varies along the entire reaction coordinate as the particles traverse transition pathways. To gain further insight into why C states are more propitious, we consider the one-dimensional energy landscape formed by a subset of possible transition paths wherein a single bond breaks in a C cluster and the particles go on to form a C, P or T configuration (Fig.
3
(d)). In addition to the C configuration being the true ground state of the system, we find that lower activation energies are required to excite hinge-like C-C transitions than any other pathway and hence these pathways will be most favored. Symmetry also plays a role: for example, C-P transitions are more favored than C-T transitions despite having slightly higher activation energy since there are 4 ways to enter into a C-P transition and only 1 for C-T transitions in this simplified landscape.

How does the interaction length influence the assembly statistics? We modify the attractive capillary force in our theoretical model (

We have studied the emergent assembly statistics for clusters of millimetric particles at the fluid interface driven by a field of supercritical Faraday waves. From an experimental perspective, it would be interesting to explore the implications of our results for colloidal assembly where fine control may be exerted over both the temperature (strength of fluctuations) and details of the interaction potential. Modern colloidal-synthesis techniques allow the properties of the interaction potential to be tuned
Gil-Villegas et al. 1997
;
Lu and Weitz 2013
;
Al Harraq et al. 2022
, for example by varying the depletant concentration
Edwards and Bevan 2012
or indeed by incorporating capillarity
Liu et al. 2018
, thereby offering control mechanisms over the assembly statistics. The results of such experiments could be readily corroborated using recently developed techniques from numerical continuation
Trubiano and Holmes-Cerfon 2020
. Finally, our results prompt investigation into how assembly statistics are affected when particles are submerged in a wider class of athermal environments, for example active bacterial baths
Heinonen et al. 2022
;
Grober et al. 2023
, or when the particles themselves are living or active
Ko et al. 2022
;
Protière 2023
;
Zöttl and Stark 2023
.

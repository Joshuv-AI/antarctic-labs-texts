Abstract
. Using the concept of prime submodule introduced by Raggi et.al. we extend the notion of reduced rank to the module-theoretic context. We study the quotient category of σ ⁡ [ M ] \sigma[M] modulo the hereditary torsion theory cogenerated by the M M -injective hull of M M when M M is a semiprime Goldie module. We prove that this quotient category is spectral. Later we consider the hereditary torsion theory in σ ⁡ [ M ] \sigma[M] cogenerated by the M M -injective hull of M / 𝔏 ⁡ ( M ) M/\mathfrak{L}(M) where 𝔏 ⁡ ( M ) \mathfrak{L}(M) is the prime radical of M M , and we characterize when the module of quotients of M M , respect to this torsion theory, has finite length in the quotient category. At the end we give conditions on a module M M with endomorphism ring S S to get that S S is an order in an Artininan ring, extending a remarkable Theorem of L.W. Small.

Introduction

The notion of reduced rank was introduced by Goldie in
[
8
]
. Over a semiprime left Goldie ring
R
with classical ring of quotients
Q
, the reduced rank of an
R
-module
M
is defined as the dimension of the semisimple
Q
-module
Q\otimes_{R}M
. Later, using that the prime radical of left Noetherian ring is nilpotent, the reduced rank is extended. The reduced rank of an
R
-module
M
over a left Noetherian ring
R
turned out to be a good invariant and it was used to characterize left orders in Artinian rings.

In 1982, the first author extends the notion of reduced rank to arbitrary rings
[
2
]
. For, he considers the hereditary torsion theory
\gamma
cogenerated by the injective hull of
R/N
where
N
is the prime radical of the ring
R
. It is said that the ring
R
has finite reduced rank if the quotient ring
\mathcal{Q}_{\gamma}(R)
has finite length in the quotient category
R
-Mod/
\gamma
. This definition agrees with that given by Goldie when
R
is a left Noetherian ring. Hence the class of rings with finite reduced rank contains properly all Noetherian rings and also it can be seen than includes all those rings with Krull dimension. It is showed that for a ring
R
with finite reduced rank, it happens that
R/N
is a Goldie ring where
N
is the prime radical of
R

[
2
, Theorem 1]
. Also, the reduced rank is used to characterize orders in Artinian rings extending Small’s Theorem
[
2
, Theorem 4]
(for Small’s Theorem see
[
10
, 4.1.4]
).

In this paper we are interested in a more general approach to the concept of reduced rank. In
[
12
]
, it was introduced a notion of prime submodule of a given module. Hence, it is natural to consider the prime radical of a module
M
, that is, the intersection of all prime submodules of
M
. In order to get a more general context, we will work on the category
\sigma[M]
. We will say that a module
M
has finite reduced rank in
\sigma[M]
if the module of quotients
\mathcal{Q}_{\gamma}(M)
has finite length in the quotient category
\sigma[M]/\gamma
where
\gamma
is the hereditary torsion theory in
\sigma[M]
cogenerated by the
M
-injective hull of
M/\mathfrak{L}(M)
where
\mathfrak{L}(M)
is the prime radical of
M
. As in the case of rings, we show that every module
M
with Krull dimension has finite reduced rank in
\sigma[M]
. Also, we explore when the finite reduced rank is preserved by submodules, taking factor modules or direct sums. We also prove that the finite reduced rank is preserved under equivalences between categories of the type
\sigma[M]
. This extends the fact that the finite reduced rank on rings is a Morita invariant property, and also we get as a corollary that the corner ring with a right semicentral idempotent of a ring with finite reduced rank inherits the property.

Given a module
M
with endomorphism ring
S
, we study when
S
has finite reduced rank in Mod-
S
. One of our goals is to extend the Small’s Theorem for
S
. For, we give a notion of the condition of regularity in
S
and we prove that
M
has finite reduced rank in
\sigma[M]
and satisfies that regularity condition if and only if
S
is a right order in an Artinian ring. Here, we must to say that this Artinian ring is the endomorphism ring of the module of quotients of
M
.

2.
Hereditary Torsion Theories in
\sigma[M]

Following Wisbauer
[
17
, 9.5]
, given a hereditary torsion theory
\tau=(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
in
\sigma[M]
, there exists an
M
-injective module
E
such that
\mathcal{T}=\{N\in\sigma[M]\mid\Hom_{R}(N,E)=0\}
. We claim that
\mathcal{F}=\{L\in\sigma[M]\mid L\hookrightarrow\prod_{I}^{[M]}E\}
where
\prod_{I}^{[M]}E
is the direct product of copies of
E
in the category
\sigma[M]
. For, let
N
in
\mathcal{T}
and
L\in\{L\in\sigma[M]\mid L\hookrightarrow\prod_{I}^{[M]}E\}
. Then there is a monomorphism
\alpha:L\hookrightarrow\prod_{I}^{[M]}E
for some index set
I
. Suppose
f:N\to L
is a nonzero homomorphism. It follows that there is an index
i\in I
such that
\pi_{i}\alpha f\neq 0
, where
\pi_{i}:\prod_{I}^{[M]}E\to E
is the canonical projection. This is a contradiction because
N
is in
\mathcal{T}
. On the other hand, let
L
be in
\mathcal{F}
. Then
\Hom_{R}(L,E)\neq 0
. Consider the canonical homomorphism
\alpha:L\to\prod_{\Hom_{R}(L,E)}^{[M]}E
. Therefore
\Ker\alpha=\bigcap_{f\in\Hom_{R}(L,E)}\Ker f
. Since
E
is injective in
\sigma[M]
,
\Hom_{R}(\Ker\alpha,E)=0
. This implies that
\Ker\alpha\in\mathcal{T}\cap\mathcal{F}=0
. Thus,
\alpha
is a monomorphism.

Hence, a hereditary torsion theory
\tau=(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
in
\sigma[M]
is cogenerated by an
M
-injective module
E
, that is,

(2.1)

\begin{split}\mathcal{T}&=\{N\in\sigma[M]\mid\Hom_{R}(N,E)=0\}\\
\mathcal{F}&=\{L\in\sigma[M]\mid L\hookrightarrow\prod_{I}^{[M]}E\;\text{for some index set}\;I\}\end{split}

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
M
be an
R
-module. Given a pair
\tau=(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
of classes of modules in
\sigma[M]
,
\tau
is a hereditary torsion theory in
\sigma[M]
if and only if there exists a hereditary torsion theory
(\mathfrak{T},\mathfrak{F})
in
R
-Mod such that
\mathcal{T}=\mathfrak{T}\cap\sigma[M]
and
\mathcal{F}=\mathfrak{F}\cap\sigma[M]
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\tau=(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
is a hereditary torsion theory in
\sigma[M]
. Then there is an
M
-injective module
E
satisfying (
2.1
). Let
Q
be the injective hull of
E
in
R
-Mod. Then
Q
cogenerates a hereditary torsion theory
(\mathfrak{T},\mathfrak{F})
in
R
-Mod. We claim that
\mathcal{T}=\mathfrak{T}\cap\sigma[M]
and
\mathcal{F}=\mathfrak{F}\cap\sigma[M]
. Since
E\leq Q
, it is clear that
\mathfrak{T}\cap\sigma[M]\subseteq\mathcal{T}
. Let
N\in\mathcal{T}
. Suppose that there is a homomorphism
f:N\to Q
. Since
N
is in
\sigma[M]
,
f(M)\in\sigma[M]
. This implies that
f(M)\subseteq E
and so
f=0
. Thus,
\mathcal{T}\subseteq\mathfrak{T}\cap\sigma[M]
. On the other hand, since
\prod_{I}^{[M]}E\leq Q^{I}
for any set
I

we have that
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathfrak{F}\cap\sigma[M]
. Now suppose
N
is in
\mathfrak{F}\cap\sigma[M]
. Then there is a monomorphism
\alpha:N\to Q^{I}
. For every index
i\in I
, consider
\pi_{i}\alpha:N\to Q
where
\pi_{i}:Q^{I}\to Q
is the canonical projection. Since
N
is in
\sigma[M]
and the largest submodule of
Q
in
\sigma[M]
is
E
, we have that
\pi_{i}\alpha(N)\subseteq E
for all
i\in I
. This implies that
\alpha(N)\subseteq E^{I}
. Since
\alpha(N)
is in
\sigma[M]
,
\alpha(N)
must be contained in
\prod_{I}^{[M]}E
. Thus
N
is in
\mathcal{F}
.

Conversely, let
(\mathfrak{T},\mathfrak{F})
be a hereditary torsion theory in
R
-Mod. Since
\sigma[M]
is a hereditary pretorsion class it follows easily that
(\mathfrak{T}\cap\sigma[M],\mathfrak{F}\cap\sigma[M])
is a hereditary torsion theory in
\sigma[M]
. ∎

Let
\tau
be a hereditary torsion theory in
\sigma[M]
and
N\in\sigma[M]
any module. Recall that the set of
\tau
-saturated submodules
of
N
is defined as

\Sat_{\tau}(N)=\{L\leq N\mid N/L\;\text{is $\tau$-torsionfree}\}.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\tau
be a hereditary torsion theory in
\sigma[M]
cogenerated by an
M
-injective module
E
and let
N\in\sigma[M]
. Then,

(1)

N
is
\tau
-torsionfree if and only if
\Ker f\in\Sat_{\tau}(N)
for all
f\in\End_{R}(N)
.

(2)

\Sat_{\tau}(N)=\{\bigcap_{f\in X}\Ker f\mid X\subseteq\Hom_{R}(N,E)\}
.

Proof.

(1) Suppose
N
is
\tau
-torsionfree and let
f:N\to N
be any endomorphism. Then
N/\Ker f\hookrightarrow N
. Since
N
is
\tau
-torsionfree,
\Ker f\in\Sat_{\tau}(N)
. The converse is obvious.

(2) It is clear that
\{\bigcap_{f\in X}\Ker f\mid X\subseteq\Hom_{R}(N,E)\}\subseteq\Sat_{\tau}(N)
. Let
L\in\Sat_{\tau}(N)
. Then there is a monomorphism
\alpha:N/L\to\prod_{I}^{[M]}E
for some index
I
. Let
\pi:N\to N/L
and
\rho_{i}:\prod_{I}^{[M]}E\to E
be the canonical projections. Then
\rho_{i}\alpha\pi\in\Hom_{R}(N,E)
for all
i\in I
and
L\subseteq\bigcap_{i\in I}\Ker\rho_{i}\alpha\pi
. Now, let
x\in\bigcap_{i\in I}\Ker\rho_{i}\alpha\pi
. Then
\rho_{i}(\alpha\pi(x))=0
for all
i\in I
. This implies that
\alpha(\pi(x))=0
. Since
\alpha
is a monomorphism,
\pi(x)=0
, that is,
x\in L
. Thus
L=\bigcap_{i\in I}\Ker\rho_{i}\alpha\pi
.
∎

.

Let
\tau
be a hereditary torsion theory in
\sigma[M]
. Suppose

\tau
-torsionfree and

-injective. Then, a submodule

-injective.

Proof.

Let
L\in\Sat_{\tau}(N)
and let
K\leq V\in\sigma[M]
such that

is
\tau
-torsion. Consider the following commutative diagram

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\Hom_{R}(V,L)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\lambda}
\textstyle{\Hom_{R}(V,N)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{\Hom_{R}(V,N/L)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{\Hom_{R}(K,N)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

Since

Reciprocally, sea
L\leq N

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\tau
be a hereditary torsion theory in
\sigma[M]
. Let
M
be
\tau
-torsionfree and let
f,g\in\End_{R}(M)
such that
f(M),g(M)\in\mathcal{L}(M,\tau)
. Then
f(g(M))\in\mathcal{L}(M,\tau)
.

Proof.

If
g(M)\subseteq\Ker f
, then
\Ker f\in\mathcal{L}(M,\tau)
. By Proposition
2.3
,
\Ker f\in\Sat_{\tau}(M)
. Therefore
\Ker f=M
and so
f=0
. Thus,
0=f(g(M))\in\mathcal{L}(M,\tau)
. Suppose
g(M)\nsubseteq\Ker f
. Note that
f
induces an epimorphism
M/g(M)\to f(M)/f(g(M))
. Since
M/g(M)
is
\tau
-torsion,
f(M)/f(g(M))
is
\tau
-torsion. There is an exact sequence

0\to f(M)/f(g(M))\to M/f(g(M))\to M/f(M)\to 0.

Since the
\tau
-torsion class is closed under extensions, we have that
M/f(g(M))
is
\tau
-torsion, that is,
f(g(M))\in\mathcal{L}(M,\tau)
.
∎

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
M
be projective in
\sigma[M]
and
\tau
-torsionfree. Suppose
\Sat_{\tau}(M)
satisfies the ascending chain condition (ACC). If
f\in\End_{R}(M)
is such that
f(M)\in\mathcal{L}(M,\tau)
then
f
is a monomorphism.

Proof.

Let
f\in\End_{R}(M)
such that
f(M)\in\mathcal{L}(M,\tau)
. Consider the chain

\Ker f\subseteq\Ker(f\circ f)\subseteq\Ker(f\circ f\circ f)\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\Ker(f^{n})\subseteq\cdots

Since
M
is
\tau
-torsionfree,
\Ker(f^{n})\in\Sat_{\tau}(M)
for all
n>0
. By hypothesis, there exists
k>0
such that
\Ker(f^{k})=\Ker(f^{k+i})
for all
i\geq 0
. By Lemma
2.5
,
f^{k}(M)\in\mathcal{L}(M,\tau)
. We have that there exists a submodule
T
of
M
such that
f^{k}(T)=\Ker f\cap f^{k}(M)
. Hence,

0=f(\Ker f\cap f^{k}(M))=ff^{k}(T)=f^{k+1}(T).

It follows that
T\subseteq\Ker f^{k+1}=\Ker f^{k}
and so
\Ker f\cap f^{k}(M)=f^{k}(T)=0
. This implies that
\Ker f
can be embedded into
M/f^{k}(M)
which is
\tau
-torsion because
f^{k}(M)\in\mathcal{L}(M,\tau)
. Thus,
\Ker f=0
and hence
f
is a monomorphism.
∎

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
\tau
be a hereditary torsion theory in
\sigma[M]
and let
N
be
\tau
-torsionfree. If
\Sat_{\tau}(N)
satisfies ACC, then
N
has finite uniform dimension.

Proof.

First let us see that the lattice
\Sat_{\tau}(N)
has uniform elements. If
M
is uniform, we are done. If not, there exist
K,L\in\Sat_{\tau}(N)
nonzero, such that
K\cap L=0
. This implies that there exists pseudocomplements in
\Sat_{\tau}(N)
. By hypothesis we can take
X
maximal among all the pseudocomplements in
\Sat_{\tau}(N)
. Suppose
X
is a pseudocomplement of
U
. We claim that
U
is uniform in
\Sat_{\tau}(N)
. If
U
is not uniform, there exists
A,B\in\Sat_{\tau}(N)
and
A,B\leq U
such that
A\cap B=0
. This implies that
(X+A)\cap B=0
. Hence it is possible to find a psudocomplement
V
in
\Sat_{\tau}(N)
of
B
containing
X+A
. By the maximality of
X
,
X=V
. Therefore
A=0
. Thus,
U
is uniform in
\Sat_{\tau}(N)
.

Second, let
A\in\Sat_{\tau}(N)
and suppose that for every
0\neq B\in\Sat_{\tau}(N)
,
A\cap B\neq 0
. We claim that
A\leq^{\text{ess}}N
. Let
K\leq N
such that
A\cap K=0
. Then,
0=\overline{A\cap K}=\overline{A}\cap\overline{K}=A\cap\overline{K}
. By the assumption on

5.
A generalization of the Small’s Theorem

Let
M
be a module and
N
a fully invariant submodule of
M
. From now on, let
S
denote the endomorphism ring of
M
. Given an endomorphism
f:M\to M
, since
N
is fully invariant,
f
induces an endomorphism
\overline{f}:M/N\to M/N
such that
\overline{f}\pi=\pi f
where
\pi:M\to M/N
is the canonical projection. We define the following subset
\mathcal{C}(N)=\{f\in S\mid\overline{f}\;\text{ is a monomorphism}\}
. It is clear that
\mathcal{C}(N)
is a multiplicative closed subset of
S
.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
M
be a module and
N
a fully invariant submodule of
M
. A module
L
in
\sigma[M]
is called
\mathcal{C}(N)
-torsion
if for every
f\in\Hom_{R}(M,L)
there exists
c\in\mathcal{C}(N)
such that
fc=0
. That is,
\Hom_{R}(M,L)
is a
\mathcal{C}(N)
-torsion right
S
-module.

Given a module
L\in\sigma[M]
, we can consider
\tau_{N}(L)
as the sum of all
\mathcal{C}(N)
-torsion submodules of
L
. The following lemma shows that
\tau_{N}
is a left exact radical.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
M
be projective in
\sigma[M]
and
N
a fully invariant submodule of
M
. Then
\tau_{N}
is a left exact radical.

Proof.

Let
K
and
L
be in
\sigma[M]
and
f:K\to L
any homomorphism. Suppose that
K^{\prime}\leq K
is a
\mathcal{C}(N)
-torsion submodule. Consider
g\in\Hom_{R}(M,f(K^{\prime}))
. Since
M
is projective in
\sigma[M]
, there exists
h:M\to K^{\prime}
such that
fh=g
. It follows that there exists
c\in\mathcal{C}(N)
such that
hc=0
. Therefore,
gc=fhc=0
. Thus,
f(K^{\prime})
is
\mathcal{C}(N)
-torsion and hence
f(\tau_{N}(K))\subseteq\tau_{N}(L)
.

Now suppose that
K/\tau_{N}(L)
is a
\mathcal{C}(N)
-torsion submodule of
L/\tau_{N}(L)
. Let
f:M\to K
be any homomorphism. Then there exists
c\in\mathcal{C}(N)
such that
\pi fc=0
where
\pi:L\to L/\tau_{N}(L)
is the canonical projection. Hence
fc(M)\subseteq\tau_{N}(L)
. Thus, there exists
d\in\mathcal{C}(N)
such that
fcd=0
. This implies that
K
is
\mathcal{C}(N)
-torsion and so
K/\tau_{N}(L)=0
.

It is clear that, if
K\leq L
, then
\tau_{N}(K)=\tau_{N}(L)\cap K
. That is,
\tau_{N}
is left exact.
∎

Remark 5.3

By Lemma

, for a fully invariant submodule

the radical

defines a hereditary torsion theory in

which will be also denoted

.

Proposition 5.4

such that

is
\gamma

\tau_{\mathfrak{L}(M)}(L)=0

\gamma
-torsion, so is

On the nonlinear Poincaré flow
Sylvain Crovisier, Dawei Yang

Abstract
We develop a tool in order to analyse the dynamics of differentiable flows with singularities. It provides an abstract model for the local dynamics that can be used in order to control the size of invariant manifolds. This work is the first part of the results announced in [ CY2 ] . 2000 Mathematics Subject Classification: 37C10, 37D30. Keywords: Vector field, Poincaré flow, dominated splitting, singularity.



1
Introduction

It is generally believed that the dynamics of vector fields is similar to the dynamics of diffeomorphisms.
Indeed if
X
is a vector field over a closed Riemannian manifold
M
which generates the flow
(\varphi_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
, and if there exists a global cross section
\Sigma
, then the first return map to
\Sigma

is a diffeomorphism whose dynamics reflects those of the flow. In general there does not exist a global cross section, but Poincaré used local cross sections in order to study the dynamics around periodic orbits.

When one considers a general compact invariant set
\Lambda
instead of a periodic orbit, Liao
[
Li1
]
extended the above ideas of Poincaré to the regular part of
\Lambda
and he defined the notion of
linear Poincaré flow
on the normal bundle. As summarized by Bonatti-da Luz
[
BdL
]
: “for flows, hyperbolic structures live on the normal bundle for the linear Poincaré flow, but not on the tangent bundle”. However, the linear Poincaré flow cannot be defined at singularities.

Singularities are special orbits of vector fields: these are those points
\sigma\in M
such that
X(\sigma)=0
. The other points are said to be
regular
points. Denote by
{\rm Sing}(X)
the set of singularities of
X
.
Singularities alone support very simple dynamics: they are in fact the fixed points of the flow. However, when one considers a general compact invariant set with singularities, the regular orbits may accumulate singularities.
This complicates the study of the dynamics on such invariant sets. The famous Lorenz attractor
[
Lo
]
is such an example.

The derivative with respect to the space variable
D\varphi_{t}:TM\to TM
is said to be the
tangent flow
of
X
.
For any regular point
x
, let
{\cal N}_{x}=\{v\in T_{x}M:~\left<v,X(x)\right>=0\}

be its normal tangent space.
This defines a normal bundle
{\cal N}_{M\setminus{\rm Sing}(X)}
on the regular set, which is a priori non-compact. A linear flow
\Psi_{t}:~{\cal N}_{M\setminus{\rm Sing}(X)}\to{\cal N}_{M\setminus{\rm Sing}(X)}
is then obtained in the following way: for any vector
v\in{\cal N}_{x}
with
x\in M\setminus{\rm Sing}(X)

\Psi_{t}\cdot v=D\varphi_{t}\cdot v-\frac{\left<X(\varphi_{t}(x)),D\varphi_{t}\cdot v\right>}{\left|X(\varphi_{t}(x))\right|^{2}}X(\varphi_{t}(x)).

Due to the existence of singularities, some compactness will be lost. In this paper, we use techniques for studying flows with singularities that may be useful for
other problems.

In order to analyze the tangent dynamics (for instance in order to prove the existence of a dominated splitting,
and then build invariant manifolds), one needs to analyze the local dynamics near
\Lambda
.
For a diffeomorphism
f
, one usually lifts the local dynamics to the tangent bundle:
for each
x\in M
, one defines a diffeomorphism
{\cal L}f_{x}\colon T_{x}M\to T_{f(x)}M
,
which preserves the
0
-section (i.e.
{\cal L}f_{x}(0_{x})=0_{f(x)}
) and is locally conjugated to
f

through the exponential map:

\exp_{f(x)}\circ{\cal L}f_{x}=f\circ\exp_{x}
near
0_{x}
in
T_{x}M
.
It defines in this way a local fibered system on the bundle
TM\to M
.
For flows one introduces a similar notion.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
(\varphi_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
be a continuous flow over a compact metric space
K
, and
{\cal N}\to K
be a continuous Riemannian vector bundle.
A
local
C^{k}
fibered flow

\psi
on
{\cal N}
is a continuous family of
C^{k}
diffeomorphisms
\psi_{t}\colon{\cal N}_{x}\to{\cal N}_{\varphi_{t}(x)}
, for
(x,t)\in K\times{\mathbb{R}}
, such that:

–

Each map
\psi_{t}
preserves the
0
-section of
{\cal N}
.

–

The flow property holds near the
0
-section:
there is
\beta_{0}>0
such that for each
x\in K
,
t_{1},t_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}
, and
u\in{\cal N}_{x}
satisfying

\big[\forall s\in[0,1],\max(\|\psi_{s.t_{1}}(u)\|,\|\psi_{s.t_{2}}(\psi_{t_{1}}(u))\|)\leq\beta_{0}\big]
,
then

\psi_{t_{1}+t_{2}}(u)=\psi_{t_{2}}\circ\psi_{t_{1}}(u).

(1)

Definition 1.2
.

A
C^{k}
identification

\mathfrak{h}
on an open set
U\subset K

is a continuous family of
C^{k}
diffeomorphisms
\mathfrak{h}_{y,x}\colon{\cal N}_{y}\to{\cal N}_{x}
indexed by
pairs of close points
x,y\in U
, satisfying for some
\beta_{0},r_{0}>0
the following: for any
x,y,z\in U
and
u\in{\cal N}_{y}

with
d(z,x),d(z,y)<r_{0}
and
\|u\|<\beta_{0}
,

\mathfrak{h}_{z,x}\circ\mathfrak{h}_{y,z}(u)=\mathfrak{h}_{y,x}(u).

We say that the identification
\mathfrak{h}
is
compatible with the local fibered flow

(\psi_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
, if it satisfies some commutation property near the
0
-section, see Section
7
for the formal definition.

For a vector field
X
, a natural way to lift the dynamics is to define a
Poincaré map
by projecting the normal spaces
{\cal N}_{x}
and

{\cal N}_{\varphi_{t}(x)}
above two points of a regular orbit by using the exponential map and the holonomy along the orbits of the flow.
One obtains a local diffeomorphism
\psi_{t}
from a neighborhood of
0_{x}
in
{\cal N}_{x}
to

{\cal N}_{\varphi_{t}(x)}
. The advantage of this construction is that the ambient dimension has been dropped by
1
. The linearization of the Poincaré map
\psi_{t}
at
0_{x}
coincides with the linear Poincaré flow
\Psi_{t}
.

A new difficulty appears: the domain of the Poincaré maps degenerate near the singularities.
For that reason one introduces the
rescaled Poincaré flow
:

\psi^{*}_{t}(u)=\|X(\varphi_{t}(x))\|^{-1}\cdot\psi_{t}(\|X(x)\|\cdot u).

We denote it by
\psi^{*}_{t,x}
if we want to indicate the base point
x
.

Our main result is that this family of maps can be compactified
as a local fibered flow, assuming that the singularities are not degenerate.

Main Theorem
.

Let
X
be a
C^{k}
vector field,
k\geq 1
, on a closed manifold
M
of dimension
d
and
\Lambda
be a compact set, invariant by the flow
(\varphi_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}

associated to
X
, such that
DX(\sigma)
is invertible for each
\sigma\in{\rm Sing}(X)\cap\Lambda
.
Let
(\psi^{*}_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
be the rescaled nonlinear Poincaré flow on
{\cal N}|_{\Lambda\setminus{\rm Sing}(X)}
.

Then, there exist a topological flow
\widehat{\varphi}
on a compact metric space
\widehat{\Lambda}
,
and a local
C^{k}
fibered flow
\widehat{\psi^{*}}
on a Riemannian vector bundle
\widehat{{\cal N}}\to\widehat{\Lambda}

whose fibers have dimension
d-1
such that:

–

there is an injective map
i\colon\Lambda\setminus{\rm Sing}(X)\to\widehat{\Lambda}

whose image is open in
\widehat{\Lambda}
, and which conjugates the restrictions
of
\varphi
to
\Lambda\setminus{\rm Sing}(X)
and of
\widehat{\varphi}

to
i(\Lambda\setminus{\rm Sing}(X))
;

–

there is an isometric bundle isomorphism
I\colon{\cal N}|_{\Lambda\setminus{\rm Sing}(X)}\to\widehat{\cal N}|_{i(\Lambda\setminus{\rm Sing}(X))}
fibered over
i
;

–

I
conjugates, near the
0
-section, the rescaled nonlinear Poincaré flow
\psi^{*}

over
\Lambda\setminus{\rm Sing}(X)
and the restriction of the local fibered flow
\widehat{\psi^{*}}

to
i(\Lambda\setminus{\rm Sing}(X))
. Locally:

\widehat{\psi^{*}}=I\circ\psi^{*}\circ I^{-1}
.

Moreover for any open set
\widehat{U}\subset\widehat{\Lambda}

whose closure is disjoint from

i(\Lambda\setminus{\rm Sing}(X))
, there exist
C>0
and a
C^{k}
identification
\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}
on
\widehat{U}

which is compatible with the flow
(\widehat{\psi^{*}_{C\cdot t}})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
.

Our main goal initially was to prove a conjecture by Palis for
3
-dimensional flows (see the announcement in
[
CY1
]
).
The present work is the first part of the proof of this conjecture (and is extracted from our previous unpublished preprint
[
CY2
]
); the other parts of the proof of the conjecture will appear in subsequent papers. The Main Theorem here is essential in order to prove the existence of stable manifolds that are weakly contracted, with uniform
C^{2}
topology, allowing to get distortion control.

As we mentioned the local fibered flow
(\psi_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
degenerates near the singularities.
The rescaling solves the degeneracy. From the proof of the Main Theorem, we get in particular the following property (see Theorem
6.6
):

In the setting of the Main Theorem, and given
t,\varepsilon>0
, there is
\delta,\beta>0
such that for any regular points
x,y\in M
, if
{\mathbb{R}}.X(x)
and
{\mathbb{R}}.X(y)
are
\delta
-close, then
\psi_{t,x}^{*}|_{B(0_{x},\beta)}
and
\psi_{t,y}^{*}|_{B(0_{y},\beta)}
are
\varepsilon
-close in the
C^{k}
topology
on the .

Our result extends previous constructions.
Liao first introduced the rescaling
[
Li2
]
of the linear Poincaré flow. For every regular point
x\in M
and every
v\in{\cal N}_{x}
, one defines

\Psi_{t}^{*}\cdot v=\frac{\|X(x)\|}{\|X(\varphi_{t}(x))\|}\Psi_{t}\cdot v=\frac{\Psi_{t}\cdot v}{\|D\varphi_{t}(x)|_{{\mathbb{R}}\cdot X(x)}\|}.

It is clear that the linearization of the rescaled Poincaré map
\psi^{*}_{t}
is
\Psi^{*}_{t}
.
Note that the hyperbolicity properties of
\Psi^{*}
allows to detect the existence of dominated splittings
for the tangent flow
D\varphi
:
a subbundle of
{\cal N}
is uniformly contracted by
\Psi^{*}
if and only if
the dynamics by
\Psi
is dominated by
D\varphi|_{{\mathbb{R}}\cdot X}

(we call this property: mixed dominated splitting).
Consequently,
if
{\cal N}
has a dominated splitting
{\cal N}={\cal E}\oplus{\cal F}
for
\Psi

(or equivalently for
\Psi^{*}
), and if
{\cal E}
is uniformly contracted by
\Psi^{*}
,
then
TM
has a dominated splitting
TM=E\oplus F
such that
\operatorname{dim}(E)=\operatorname{dim}({\cal E})

and
X
is tangent to
F
, see
[
LGW
, Lemma 5.5]
and
[
GY
, Lemma 2.13]
.

The introduction of the compactification of the rescaled non-linear Poincaré flow gives a general
framework in order to study locally flows with singularities.
Gan and Yang
[
GY
]
also considered previously the rescaled non-linear Poincaré flow and proved some uniform properties about the size of invariant manifolds in the
C^{1}
-topology. Our construction allows to recover their results in a different way
which avoids long computations and to extend it to the
C^{k}
-topologies. As an example of application, these techniques allow to prove that,

at points
x
satisfying some Pliss-hyperbolicity condition, there exist stable manifolds with size of order

d(x,\operatorname{Sing}(X))
, see
[
GY
, Lemma 2.18]
.

As another application, our compactification allows to obtain the proof of a property for vector fields
which generalizes a classical result for diffeomorphisms, see Section
9
below. Versions of this result have been proved
in
[
LY
]
for
C^{2}
vector fields and in
[
Y
, Lemma 2.20]
for
C^{1}
vector fields on surfaces:

let
X
be a
C^{1}
vector field on a closed manifold
M
such that
DX(\sigma)
is invertible at each singularity
\sigma
and let
\mu
be an ergodic non-atomic probability measure with
d-1
negative Lyapunov exponents. Then
\mu
is supported on an attracting periodic orbit.

2
Dynamics on Grassmann bundles

We associate to the flow
(\varphi_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
several
C^{k-1}
linear and projective flows.

2.1
The
tangent flow

(\Phi_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}=(D\varphi_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
on the tangent bundle
TM

The tangent flow is the flow on the tangent bundle

TM
which fibers over
(\varphi_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
through the projection

\pi\colon TM\to M
and is obtained by differentiation.

2.2
The
Grassmann flow

(\widetilde{\varphi}_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
on
PTM

We consider the projective tangent manifold
\widetilde{M}=PTM
, which is the Grassmannian space of

1
-dimensional linear spaces
L_{x}\subset T_{x}M
at points
x\in M

and let
\widetilde{p}\colon PTM\to M
denote the canonical projection.

The projective tangent flow on

PTM
is obtained from
(D\varphi_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
by taking the quotient:

\widetilde{\varphi}_{t}({\mathbb{R}}\cdot v)={\mathbb{R}}\cdot(D\varphi_{t}\cdot v)\text{ for non vanishing}v\in TM.

Sometimes we prefer to work with the unit tangent manifold
T^{1}M
which is a double cover of
PTM
. The tangent flow induces by normalization a flow on this space, that we also denote by
(\widetilde{\varphi}_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
for simplicity.

2.3
The linear flow
(\widetilde{\Phi}_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
on
\widetilde{TM}

The projection
\widetilde{p}\colon PTM\to M
allows
to pull back the tangent bundle
\pi\colon TM\to M
and induces
a linear bundle
\widetilde{\pi}\colon\widetilde{TM}\to PTM
.
It can be obtained as the restriction of the first projection

PTM\times TM\to PTM
to the set of pairs

(x,v)
such that
\widetilde{p}(x)=\pi(v)
.
It is naturally endowed with the pull back metric of
TM
and it is locally trivial in a neighborhood of preimages

\widetilde{p}^{-1}(z)
for
z\in M
.
The second projection
PTM\times TM\to PTM

induces a projection
\widetilde{P}\colon\widetilde{TM}\to TM
which is an isometry on each fiber.

By pulling back the tangent flow
D\varphi
, one defines a linear flow

(\widetilde{\Phi}_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
on
\widetilde{TM}
over
(\widetilde{\varphi}_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
.

2.4
The
normal flow

(\widetilde{\Psi}_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
on
\widetilde{{\cal N}}

We define the normal bundle
\widetilde{\cal N}\to PTM
:
any element of
PTM
is a one-dimensional line
L\subset T_{x}M

and its fiber in
\widetilde{\cal N}
is the orthogonal subspace of
\widetilde{TM}_{L}

to
L
:

\widetilde{\cal N}_{L}:=\{u\in\widetilde{TM}_{L}:\widetilde{P}(u)\perp L\}.

Let us consider the orthogonal quotient

\widetilde{Q}\colon\widetilde{TM}\to\widetilde{N}
. For any
u\in\widetilde{\cal N}_{L}
,

\widetilde{Q}(u):=u-\frac{<u,v>}{\|v\|^{2}}\cdot v,

(2)

where
v
is any non-zero vector in
\widetilde{TM}

such that
\widetilde{P}(v)\in L
.

The flow
(\widetilde{\Phi}_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
induces a linear flow
(\widetilde{\Psi}_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
on the quotient:

\widetilde{\Psi}_{t}\cdot u:=(\widetilde{p}\circ\widetilde{\Phi}_{t})\cdot u.

2.5
The
linear Poincaré flow

(\Psi_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
on the normal bundle
{\cal N}

The normal bundle
{\cal N}\to M\setminus{\rm Sing}(X)
over the space of non-singular points
x

is the union of the vector subspaces
{\cal N}_{x}=X(x)^{\perp}
,
which can be seen as the image of an orthogonal projection

q\colon TM\to{\cal N}
(defined in a similar way as in (
2
)).
Contrary to the previous bundles,
{\cal N}
depends on the vector field
X

and is as smooth as
X
.

Using the vector field one lifts
M\setminus{\rm Sing}(X)
to
PTM
by the map
(a local section of
\widetilde{p}
):

\widetilde{\text{\it\char 16\relax}}\colon x\mapsto(x,{\mathbb{R}}\cdot X(x)).

In this way the spaces
{\cal N}_{x}
can be identified with
\widetilde{\cal N}_{\widetilde{\text{\it\char 16\relax}}(x)}
by
\widetilde{P}
.
Let us denote
\widetilde{I}\colon{\cal N}_{x}\to\widetilde{\cal N}_{\widetilde{\text{\it\char 16\relax}}(x)}
the identification induced by
\widetilde{P}^{-1}
.

3
Blowup

We will consider compactifications of
M\setminus{\rm Sing}(X)
and of the tangent bundle

TM|_{M\setminus\text{Sing}(X)}
which allow to extend the line field
{\mathbb{R}}X
. This is given by the classical blowup.
In this section we assume that
DX(\sigma)
is invertible at each singularity
\sigma
. In particular
{\rm Sing}(X)

is a finite set.

3.1
The blownup manifold
\widehat{M}

We can blow up
M

at each singularity of
X
and get a new closed manifold
\widehat{M}

and a projection
p\colon\widehat{M}\to M
which is one-to-one above
M\setminus{\rm Sing}(X)
.
Each singularity
\sigma\in{\rm Sing}(X)
has been replaced by the projectivization

PT_{\sigma}M
. We denote
i\colon M\setminus{\rm Sing}(X)\to\widehat{M}

the inverse of
p
on the regular set.

More precisely, at each singularity
\sigma
,
one add
T^{1}_{\sigma}M
to
M\setminus\{\sigma\}
in order
to build a manifold with boundary. Locally,
it is defined by the chart

[0,\varepsilon)\times T^{1}_{\sigma}M\to(M\setminus\{\sigma\})\cup T^{1}_{\sigma}M

given by:

(s,u)\mapsto\begin{cases}&\exp(s\cdot u)\text{ if }s\neq 0,\\
&u\text{ if }s=0.\end{cases}

One then gets
\widehat{M}
by identifying points
(0,u)
and
(0,-u)

on the boundary.

It is sometimes convenient to
lift the dynamics on
M\setminus\{\sigma\}
near
\sigma

and work in the local coordinates

(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\times T^{1}_{\sigma}M
.
These coordinates define a double covering of an open subset of the blowup
\widehat{M}

and induce a chart from the quotient
(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\times T^{1}_{\sigma}M/_{(s,u)\sim(-s,-u)}

to a neighborhood of
p^{-1}(\sigma)
in
\widehat{M}
.

3.2
The
extended flow
(\widehat{\varphi}_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
on
\widehat{M}

The following result is proved in
[
T
, Section III]
.

Proposition 3.1
.

The flow
(\varphi_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
induces a
C^{k-1}
flow
(\widehat{\varphi}_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}

on
\widehat{M}
which is associated to a
C^{k-1}
vector field
\widehat{X}
.
For
\sigma\in{\rm Sing}(X)
, this flow preserves
PT_{\sigma}M
, and acts as:

\widehat{\varphi}_{t}(u)=\tfrac{D\varphi_{t}(0)\cdot u}{\|D\varphi_{t}(0)\cdot u\|}.

The vector field
\widehat{X}
coincides at
u\in PT_{\sigma}M
with
DX(\sigma).u

in
T_{u}(PT_{\sigma}M)\cong T_{\sigma}M/({\mathbb{R}}\cdot u)
.

In particular the tangent bundle
T\widehat{M}
extends
TM|_{M\setminus\text{Sing}(X)}
,
the linear flow
D\widehat{\varphi}
on
T\widehat{M}
extends the restriction of
D\varphi

to
TM|_{M\setminus\text{Sing}(X)}

and the vector field
\widehat{X}
extends
X
.
Note that each eigendirection
u
of
DX(\sigma)
at a singularity
\sigma

induces a singularity of
\widehat{X}
.

Remark
.

In
[
T
]
, the vector field and the flow are extended locally on the space
(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\times T^{1}_{\sigma}M
,
but the proof shows that these extensions are invariant under the map
(s,u)\mapsto(-s,-u)
, hence are also defined on
\widehat{M}
.

3.3
The
extended bundle

\widehat{TM}

and
extended tangent flow

(\widehat{\Phi}_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}

One associates a bundle
\widehat{\pi}\colon\widehat{TM}\to\widehat{M}

which is the pull-back of the bundle
\pi\colon TM\to M
over
M

by the map
p\colon\widehat{M}\to M
.
It can be obtained as the restriction of the first projection

\widehat{M}\times TM\to\widehat{M}
to the set of pairs

(x,v)
such that
p(x)=\pi(v)
.
It is naturally endowed with the pull back metric of
TM
and it is locally trivial in a neighborhood of preimages

p^{-1}(z)
,
z\in M
.
Note that the linear bundle
\widehat{TM}
is different from the tangent bundle

T\widehat{M}
.

The tangent flow
(D\varphi_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
can be pull back to
\widehat{TM}

as a
C^{k-1}
linear flow
(\widehat{\Phi}_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}

that we call
extended tangent flow
.

3.4
The
extended line field

\widehat{{\mathbb{R}}X}

The vector field
X
induces a line field
{{\mathbb{R}}X}
on
M\setminus\text{Sing}(X)

which admits an extension to
\widehat{TM}
. It is defined locally as follows.

Proposition 3.2
.

At each singularity
\sigma
, let
U
be a small neighborhood in
M

and
\widehat{U}=(U\setminus\{\sigma\})\cup PT_{\sigma}M
be a neighborhood of
PT_{\sigma}M

in
\widehat{M}
.
Then, the map
x\mapsto\frac{\exp_{\sigma}^{-1}(x)}{\|X(x)\|}
on
U\setminus\{\sigma\}
extends to
\widehat{U}
as a
C^{k-1}
-map which coincides at
u\in PT_{\sigma}M
with
\frac{u}{\|DX(\sigma)\cdot u\|}
, and
the map
x\mapsto\frac{\|X(x)\|}{\|\exp_{\sigma}^{-1}(x)\|}
on
U\setminus\{\sigma\}

extends to
\widehat{U}
as a
C^{k-1}
-map which coincides at
u\in PT_{\sigma}M

with
\|DX(\sigma)\cdot u\|
.

In the local coordinates
(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\times T^{1}_{\sigma}M
associated to
\sigma\in\text{Sing}(X)
,
the lift of the vector field
X_{1}:=X/{\|X\|}
on
M\setminus\text{Sing}(X)
extends as a (non-vanishing)
C^{k-1}
section

\widehat{X}_{1}\colon(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\times T^{1}_{\sigma}M\to\widehat{TM}
.
For each
x=(0,u)\in p^{-1}(\sigma)
,
one has

\widehat{X}_{1}(x)=\frac{DX(\sigma)\cdot u}{\|DX(\sigma)\cdot u\|}.

The proposition gives an extension of
X_{1}
to the local chart
(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\times T^{1}_{\sigma}M

which is a double covering of a neighborhood of
p^{-1}(\sigma)
in
\widehat{M}
.
A priori, the extension of
X_{1}
is not preserved by the symmetry
(-s,-u)\sim(s,u)

and does not define a map
\widehat{M}\to\widehat{TM}
.
However, the line field
{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{X}_{1}
is invariant by the local symmetry
(s,u)\mapsto(-s,-u)
,
hence induces a
C^{k-1}
-line field
\widehat{{\mathbb{R}}X}
on
\widehat{TM}
invariant by
(\widehat{D\varphi_{t}})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
.

Proof.

In a local chart near a singularity, we have

X(x)=\int_{0}^{1}(DX(r\cdot x)\cdot x)\;dr.

Working in the local coordinates
(s,u)\in(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\times T^{1}_{\sigma}M
, we get

X(x)=\bigg(\int_{0}^{1}DX(r\cdot s\cdot u)\;dr\bigg)\;.\;(s\cdot u).

This allows us to define a
C^{k-1}
section
in a neighborhood of
p^{-1}(\sigma)
defined by

\overline{X}\colon(s,u)\mapsto\bigg(\int_{0}^{1}DX(r\cdot s\cdot u)\;dr\bigg)\cdot u.

This section is
C^{k-1}
, is parallel to
X
(when
s\neq 0
)
and does not vanish.
Consequently
\frac{\overline{X}}{\|\overline{X}\|}
is
C^{k-1}
and extends the vector field

X_{1}:=X/{\|X\|}
as required.

Since
\overline{X}
extends as
DX(\sigma)\cdot u
at
u\in PT_{\sigma}M
,
then
X_{1}
extends as
DX(\sigma)\cdot u/\|DX(\sigma)\cdot u\|
.

Note also that for
s\neq 0
,

\|\overline{X}(s,u)\|
coincides with
\|X(x)\|/\|\exp_{\sigma}^{-1}(x)\|
where
x=\exp_{\sigma}(s\cdot u)
is a point
of
M\setminus\{\sigma\}
close to
\sigma
.
Since
\overline{X}
is
C^{k-1}
and does not vanish,

(s,u)\mapsto\|\overline{X}(s\cdot u)\|
extends as a
C^{k-1}
-function in the local coordinates

(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\times T^{1}_{\sigma}M
. It is invariant by the symmetry

(s,u)\sim(-s,-u)
, hence the maps
x\to\frac{\exp_{\sigma}^{-1}(x)}{\|X(x)\|}
and
x\mapsto\|X(x)\|/\|\exp_{\sigma}^{-1}(x)\|

for
x\in M\setminus\{\sigma\}
close to
\sigma
extends
as
C^{k-1}
maps on a neighborhood of
PT_{\sigma}M
in
\widehat{M}
.
∎

\widehat{{\cal N}}
and

(\widehat{\Psi}_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}

The orthogonal spaces to the lines of

by

3.6
Alternative construction

As we saw in Section
2.5
,
one can embed

in
PTM
by the map

\widetilde{\text{\it\char 16\relax}}\colon x\mapsto(x,{\mathbb{R}}\cdot X(x))

,

\widetilde{\varphi}
.
This way to compactify

depends on the vector field

and not only on the finite set

.
It is sometimes called

, see
[
No
]
.

When

is invertible at each singularity,

shows that the closure is homeomorphic to

.
Indeed the continuous map

(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\times T^{1}_{\sigma}M\to T^{1}M

defined by

is injective
and descends as an injective quotient map from a neighborhood of
p^{-1}(\sigma)

to
PTM
.

The restriction of the normal bundle
\widetilde{\cal N}\to PTM
to the closure of

in

gives the extended normal bundle
\widehat{{\cal N}}
.
The extended linear Poincaré flow
(\widehat{\Psi}_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
is the restriction of

(\widetilde{\Psi}_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
to the extended normal bundle
\widehat{{\cal N}}
.
This is the approach followed in
[
LGW
]
in order to compactify of the
linear Poincaré flow.

Remark
.

As said previously, the vector field
X
extends as a vector field
\widehat{X}

on
\widehat{M}
, and still admits singularities
\widehat{\sigma}

Assuming that
\widehat{\sigma}
is unique on a neighborhood
U
,
one may wonder if the line field
{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{X}
which is defined
on the tangent bundle to

extends to
U

since the limit set of
{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{X}
at
\widehat{\sigma}
may
take all the eigendirection of

.
This is the reason why, in order to extend

with the pull-back bundle
\widehat{TM}
and not the tangent bundle

.

4
Nonlinear local flows

We now define non-linear flows on neighborhoods of the
0
-section of
TM
and
{\cal N}
.

4.1
The
lifted flow

({\cal L}\varphi_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
on
TM

There exists
r_{0}>0
such that the ball
B(0,r_{0})
in
each tangent space
T_{x}M
projects in
M
diffeomorphically
by the exponential map.
For each
t\in[0,1]
and
x\in M
, the map

{\cal L}\varphi_{t}\colon y\mapsto\exp^{-1}_{\varphi_{t}(x)}\circ\varphi_{t}\circ\exp_{x}(y)

sends diffeomorphically a neighborhood of
0
in
T_{x}M
to a neighborhood of
0

in
T_{\varphi_{t}(x)}M
.
This extends to a local flow
({\cal L}\varphi_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
in a neighborhood of the
0
-section of
TM
, that is called
lifted flow
. It is tangent to
(D\varphi_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
at the
0
-section, i.e.
D({\cal L}\varphi_{t})(0_{x})=D\varphi_{t}(x)
.

4.2
The
fiber-preserving lifted flow

({\cal L}_{0}\varphi_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
on
TM

Sometimes we will need to preserve the base point and we introduce a
family of fiber-preserving maps
({\cal L}_{0}\varphi_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
, defined by:

{\cal L}_{0}\varphi_{t}(y)=\exp^{-1}_{x}\circ\varphi_{t}\circ\exp_{x}(y).

Since the
0
-section is not preserved, this is not a local flow and it will
be considered only for short times
t
. This family is called
fiber-preserving lifted flow
.

4.3
The
nonlinear Poincaré flow

(\psi_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
on
{\cal N}

Using the exponential map and the transversality between
{\cal N}_{x}
and
X(x)
,
one gets each
x\in M\setminus{\rm Sing}(X)
some number
r_{x}\in(0,r_{0})

such that for any
t\in[0,1]
, the holonomy map of the flow induces a local diffeomorphism

\psi_{t}
from
B(0,r_{x})\subset{\cal N}_{x}
to a neighborhood of
0
in
{\cal N}_{\varphi_{t}(x)}
.

This extends to a local flow
(\psi_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
in a neighborhood of the
0
-section
in
{\cal N}
, that is called
nonlinear Poincaré flow
. It is tangent to

(\Psi_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
at the
0
-section of
{\cal N}
, i.e.
D\psi_{t}(0_{x})=\Psi_{t}(x)
.

The following lemmas give estimates on the radius
r_{x}
.
They follow from the fact that the vector field
X
is almost constant in the
\beta\cdot\|X(y)\|
-neighborhood of
y
for
\beta>0
small enough. It is essentially contained in
[
GY
, Lemma 2.3]
and in the proof there.

Lemma 4.1
.

For any
\delta,t_{0}>0
, there exists
\beta>0
with the following property.

Let
x\in M\setminus{\operatorname{Sing}}(X)
and
y\in M
such that
d(x,y)\leq\beta\cdot\|X(x)\|
.
Then, there is a unique
t\in(-t_{0},t_{0})

such that
\varphi_{t}(y)
belongs to the image by
\exp_{x}
of
B(0_{x},\delta{\|X(x)\|})\subset{\cal N}_{x}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

For any
\rho>0
, there exists
\delta>0
with the following property.

Let
x\in M\setminus{\operatorname{Sing}}(X)
and

u\in B(0_{x},\delta\cdot\|X(x)\|)\subset{\cal N}_{x}
.
Then for any
t\in(0,1)
, there is a unique
t^{\prime}>0
such that

\varphi_{t^{\prime}}(\exp_{x}(u))=\exp_{\varphi_{t}(x)}\circ\psi_{t}(u)
.
Moreover
\max\big(\tfrac{t}{t^{\prime}},\tfrac{t^{\prime}}{t}\big)<1+\rho
.

5
Rescaled flows

In order to compensate the effect of the singularities on the transverse behavior of the flow,
one rescales the bundles.

5.1
Rescaled linear
and
nonlinear Poincaré flows

(\Psi^{*}_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
,
(\psi^{*}_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
on
{\cal N}

By Lemma
4.2
, there exists

\beta>0
such that
the radii
r_{x}
introduced in Section
4.3
to define
\psi
satisfies,
at any
x\in M\setminus{\rm Sing}(X)
,

r_{x}>\beta\|X(x)\|.

We can thus rescale the nonlinear Poincaré flow. We get for each
x\in M\setminus{\rm Sing}(X)
and
t\in[0,1]
a map
\psi^{*}_{t}
which sends
diffeomorphically
B(0,\beta)\subset{\cal N}_{x}
inside
{\cal N}_{\varphi_{t}(x)}
, defined by:

\psi^{*}_{t}(y)=\|X(\varphi_{t}(x))\|^{-1}\cdot\psi_{t}(\|X(x)\|\cdot y).

Again, this induces a local flow
(\psi_{t}^{*})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
in a neighborhood of the
0
-section
in
{\cal N}
, that is called
rescaled nonlinear Poincaré flow
.
Its tangent map at the
0
-section defines the
rescaled linear Poincaré flow

(\Psi_{t}^{*})
. Thus,

\Psi_{t}^{*}\cdot v=\frac{\|X(x)\|}{\|X(\varphi_{t}(x))\|}\Psi_{t}\cdot v.

5.2
Rescaled tangent flow

(\Phi_{t}^{*})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
and
rescaled lifted flow

({\cal L}\varphi_{t}^{*})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
on
TM

The
rescaled lifted flow
is defined on a neighborhood of the
0
-section
in
TM
by

{\cal L}\varphi^{*}_{t}(y)=\|X(\varphi_{t}(x))\|^{-1}\cdot{\cal L}\varphi_{t}(\|X(x)\|\cdot y).

Its tangent map at the
0
-section defines the
rescaled tangent flow

(\Phi_{t}^{*})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
:

\Phi_{t}^{*}\cdot v=\frac{\|X(x)\|}{\|X(\varphi_{t}(x))\|}D\varphi_{t}\cdot v.

5.3
The
rescaled fiber-preserving lifted flow

({\cal L}_{0}\varphi_{t}^{*})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
on
TM

The flow is defined similarly:

{\cal L}_{0}\varphi^{*}_{t}(y)=\|X(x)\|^{-1}\cdot{\cal L}_{0}\varphi_{t}(\|X(x)\|\cdot y).

6
Compactifications of nonlinear local fibered flows

The rescaled flows introduced above extend to the bundles
\widehat{TM}

or
\widehat{{\cal N}}
.
In the following, one will assume that
DX(\sigma)
is invertible at each singularity.

Related to the “local
C^{k}
fibered flow” (Definition
1.1
),
we will use the following notion.

Definition 6.1
.

Consider a continuous Riemannian vector bundle
{\cal N}
over a compact metric space

.
A map

is

fibered
, if it fibers over a homeomorphism
h
of
K

and if each induced map

is
C^{k}

and depends continuously on

for the
C^{k}
topology.

The image of

From the previous propositions the map

-topology and is defined at any

By the implicit function theorem and compactness, there exists

We now compute the rescaled linear Poincaré flow as the tangent map to the rescaled non-linear Poincaré flow along the

\begin{split}D\psi^{*}_{t}(0)&=\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial y^{\prime}}\bigg|_{y^{\prime}=0}{\cal L}_{0}\varphi^{*}_{0}(y^{\prime})\right)\circ\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\bigg|_{y=0}{\cal L}\varphi_{t}^{*}(y)\right)+\frac{\partial\tau}{\partial y}\bigg|_{y=0}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\bigg|_{\tau=0}{\cal L}_{0}\varphi^{*}_{\tau}(0_{x^{\prime}})\right)\\

7
Identifications

Identifications (see Definition
1.2
) provide a structure
on the local fibered flows which formalizes properties
satisfied by the rescaled non-linear Poincaré flow.
We introduce here an additional property (the compatibility) which will
be checked on the extended rescaled non-linear Poincaré flow in Section
8
.
We also discuss some consequences.

In the whole section we consider a
C^{k}
local fibered flow

(\psi_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
on a vector bundle
{\cal N}\to K

that fibers over a topological flow
(\varphi_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}

on the compact metric space
K
.

7.1
Compatible identifications

We have introduced in Definition
1.2

the notion of
C^{k}
identifications
\mathfrak{h}
on an open set
U\subset K
:
these are continuous families of
C^{k}
diffeomorphisms
\mathfrak{h}_{y,x}\colon{\cal N}_{y}\to{\cal N}_{x}
indexed by pairs of points
x,y\in K
, which satisfy
the relation (
1
) on balls
B(0_{x},\beta_{0})\subset{\cal N}_{x}

when
x,y,z
are
r_{0}
-close points in
U
.
In particular
\mathfrak{h}_{x,x}
coincides with the identity on the ball
B(0_{x},\beta_{0})\subset{\cal N}_{x}
.

Notations.
– In order to simplify the presentation, we will sometimes denote
\mathfrak{h}_{y,x}

by
\mathfrak{h}_{x}
. Also the projection
\mathfrak{h}_{y,x}(0)=\mathfrak{h}_{x}(0_{y})
of
0_{y}\in{\cal N}_{y}
on
{\cal N}_{x}
will be denoted
by
\mathfrak{h}_{x}(y)
.

We will denote by
{\rm Lip}
be the set of orientation-preserving bi-Lipschitz homeomorphisms
\theta
of
{\mathbb{R}}

(and by
{\rm Lip}_{1+\rho}
the set of maps in
{\rm Lip}
whose Lipschitz constant is smaller than
1+\rho
).

Definition 7.1
.

The identification
\mathfrak{h}
on
U
is
compatible
with the local fibered flow
(\psi_{t})
if:

–

No small period.

For any
\varkappa>0
, there is
r>0
such that for any
x\in\overline{U}

and
t\in[-2,2]
with
d(x,\varphi_{t}(x))<r
, then we have
|t|<\varkappa
.

–

Local injectivity.
For any
\delta>0
, there exists
\beta>0
such that for any
x,y\in U
:

if
d(x,y)<r_{0}
and
\|\mathfrak{h}_{x}(y)\|\leq\beta
, then
d(\varphi_{t}(y),x)\leq\delta
for some
t\in[-1/4,1/4]
.

–

Local invariance.
For any
x,y\in U

and
t\in[-2,2]
such that
y
and
\varphi_{t}(y)
are
r_{0}
-close to
x
,
and for any
u\in B(0,\beta_{0})\subset{\cal N}_{y}
, we have

\mathfrak{h}_{x}\circ\psi_{t}(u)=\mathfrak{h}_{x}(u).

–

Global invariance.

For any
\delta,\rho>0
, there exist
r,\beta>0
with the following properties:

1.
For any
y,y^{\prime}\in U
with
d(y,y^{\prime})<r
,
for any
u\in{\cal N}_{y}
,
u^{\prime}\in{\cal N}_{y^{\prime}}
with
\mathfrak{h}_{y}(u^{\prime})=u
,
and for any intervals
I,I^{\prime}\subset{\mathbb{R}}
containing
0
and satisfying

\|\psi_{t}(u)\|<\beta\text{ and }\|\psi_{t^{\prime}}(u^{\prime})\|<\beta\text{ for any }t\in I\text{ and any }t^{\prime}\in I^{\prime},

there is
\theta\in{\rm Lip}_{1+\rho}
such that
\theta(0)=0
and

d(\varphi_{t}(y),\varphi_{\theta(t)}(y^{\prime}))<\delta
for any
t\in I\cap\theta^{-1}(I^{\prime})
.

2.
In addition, if some
v\in{\cal N}_{y}
satisfies

[\forall t\in I\cap\theta^{-1}(I^{\prime}),\;\|\psi_{t}(v)\|<\beta]
, then:

2.i)

v^{\prime}:=\mathfrak{h}_{y^{\prime}}(v)
satisfies

[\forall t\in I\cap\theta^{-1}(I^{\prime}),\;\|\psi_{\theta(t)}(v^{\prime})\|<\delta]
;

2.ii)

if
\varphi_{t}(y)\in U

for some
t\in I\cap\theta^{-1}(I^{\prime})
, then
\mathfrak{h}_{\varphi_{t}(y)}\circ\psi_{\theta(t)}(v^{\prime})=\psi_{t}(v)
.

Remarks 7.2
.

a) These definitions are still satisfied if one reduces

r_{0}
or
\beta_{0}
. Their values may be reduced in the following sections in order to
satisfy additional properties.

b) One can rescale the time and keep a compatible identification: the flow
s\mapsto\varphi_{C\cdot s}
for
C>1

still satisfies the definitions above, maybe after reducing the constant
r_{0}
.

The main point to check is that the time in the Local injectivity can still
be chosen in
[-1/4,1/4]
. Indeed, this is ensured by the “No small period” Property applied with
\varkappa=1/(4C)
: if
r_{0}
is chosen smaller
and if

are both less than

for

, then
|t|
is smaller than
\varkappa
.
Now the time in the Local injectivity property belongs to
[-\varkappa,\varkappa]
for the initial flow, hence to
[-1/4,1/4]
for the time-rescaled flow.

c) Applying a rescaling as discussed in the item (b),
one can replace the interval
[-2,2]
in the “No small period” Property and in the Local invariance
by any larger interval
[-L,L]
; one can replace the interval
[-1/4,1/4]
in the local injectivity
by any small interval
[-\eta,\eta]
.

d) The “No small period” Property (which does not involve the projections
\mathfrak{h}_{x}
) is equivalent to the non-existence of periodic orbits of period
\leq 2

which intersect
\overline{U}
.
In particular, by reducing
r_{0}
, one can assume the following property:

For any
x\in U
and any
t\in[1,2]
, we have
d(x,\varphi_{t}(x))\geq r_{0}
.

e) For
x\in U
, the Local injectivity prevents the existence of
y\in U
that is
r_{0}
-close to
x
,
is different from
\varphi_{t}(x)
for any
t\in[-1/4,1/4]
, and
such that
\mathfrak{h}_{x}(y)=0_{x}
.
In particular:

If
x,\varphi_{t}(x)\in U
satisfy
d(x,\varphi_{t}(x))<r_{0}
,
t\not\in(-1/2,1/2)
and

\mathfrak{h}_{x}(\varphi_{t}(x))=0_{x}
, then
x
is periodic.

f) The Global invariance says that when two orbits
(\psi_{t}(u))_{t\in I}
and

(\psi_{t}(u^{\prime}))_{t\in I^{\prime}}
of the local fibered flow are close to
the
0
section of
{\cal N}
and have two points which are identified by
\mathfrak{h}
,
then they are associated to orbits of the flow
(\varphi)_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
that are close
(up to a reparametrization
\theta
).
In this case, pieces of orbit of
(\psi_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
close to the zero-section
above the first
\varphi
-orbit can be projected to an orbit of
(\psi_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
above the second
\varphi
-orbit.

g) The Global invariance can be applied to pairs of points
y,y^{\prime}
where the condition

d(y,y^{\prime})<r
has been replaced by a weaker one
d(y,y^{\prime})<r_{0}
. More precisely, the following holds:

For any
\delta,\rho>0
, there is
\beta>0
as follows:
for any
y,y^{\prime}\in U
with
d(y,y^{\prime})<r_{0}
, for any
u\in{\cal N}_{y}
,
u^{\prime}\in{\cal N}_{y^{\prime}}
with
\mathfrak{h}_{y}(u^{\prime})=u

and for any the intervals
I,I^{\prime}\subset{\mathbb{R}}
containing
0
and satisfying:

\|\psi_{t}(u)\|<\beta\text{ and }\|\psi_{t^{\prime}}(u^{\prime})\|<\beta\text{ for any }t\in I\text{ and any }s^{\prime}\in I^{\prime},

then there is
\theta\in{\rm Lip}_{1+\rho}
such that
|\theta(0)|\leq 1/4
and
d(\varphi_{t}(y),\varphi_{\theta(t)}(y^{\prime}))<\delta
for any
t\in I\cap\theta^{-1}(I^{\prime})
.

Indeed provided that
\beta>0
has been chosen small enough,
one can apply the Local injectivity and the Local invariance
in order to replace
y^{\prime}
and
u^{\prime}
by
y^{\prime\prime}=\varphi_{s}(y^{\prime})
and
u^{\prime\prime}=\psi_{s}(u^{\prime})

for some
s\in[-1/4,1/4]
such that

.
The assumptions for the Global invariance then are satisfied by

and

.
It gives a

7.2
No shear inside orbits

The next property states that along reparametrizations of orbits of the flow
(\varphi_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
,
the time cannot fluctuate widely. This will be useful for applications announced in
[

]
.

Proposition 7.3
.

Let us assume that there exists a
C^{k}
identification on an open set
U\subset K

which is compatible with the local fibered flow
(\psi_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}

and that the set
\Delta:=\{x|\forall|t|\leq\tfrac{1}{2},\;\varphi_{t}(x)\notin U\}

is disjoint from
\overline{U}
. If
r_{0}>0
is small, then
for any
x\in U
, any increasing homeomorphism
\theta
of
{\mathbb{R}}
, any closed interval
I
containing
0
satisfying
[d(\varphi_{t}(x),\varphi_{\theta(t)}(x))\leq r_{0},\;\forall t\in I]
and
\varphi_{\theta(0)}(x)\in U
,
the following holds:

–

If
\theta(0)>1/2
, then
\theta(t)>t+2
,
\forall t\in I
when
\varphi_{t}(x),\varphi_{\theta(t)}(x)\in U
;

–

If
\theta(0)\in[-2,2]
, then
\theta(t)\in[t-1/2,t+1/2]
,
\forall t\in I
when
\varphi_{t}(x),\varphi_{\theta(t)}(x)\in U
;

–

If
\theta(0)<-1/2
, then
\theta(t)<t-2
,
\forall t\in I
when
\varphi_{t}(x),\varphi_{\theta(t)}(x)\in U
.

Proof.

Let us take
r_{0},\varepsilon>0
small so that
\varphi_{s}(\overline{U})
and
\Delta
are at distance larger than
r_{0}
when
|s|\leq\varepsilon
. One also assume that:

a)

any piece of orbit

\{\varphi_{s}(y),s\in[0,b]\}\subset K\setminus U
, with
y,\varphi_{b}(y)
in the
r_{0}
-neighborhood of
\Delta
and

, is disjoint from the
r_{0}
-neighborhood of
\overline{U}
;

b)

if
\varphi_{s}(y)
is
r_{0}
-close to
y\in\overline{U}
for
|s|\leq 2
, then
|s|\leq\varepsilon
.

The first condition is satisfied by small

since
otherwise letting

one would construct

and
\varphi_{s}(y)\in\overline{U}
where

, contradicting the definition of
\Delta
.
The second condition is a consequence of the “No small period” Property.

Lemma 7.4
.

(i)

If
\theta(0)\geq-2
then
\theta(t)\geq t-1/2
for any
t\in I
satisfying
\varphi_{t}(x),\varphi_{\theta(t)}(x)\in U
.

(ii)

If
\theta(0)\leq 2
then
\theta(t)\leq t+1/2
for any
t\in I
satisfying
\varphi_{t}(x),\varphi_{\theta(t)}(x)\in U
.

Proof.

The case
t=0
is a consequence of the “No small period” Property.
We now deal with the assertion (i) in the case that
t
is positive.

Assume by contradiction that there is
t_{0}>0
in
I
such that
\varphi_{t_{0}}(x)\in U
, but
\theta(t_{0})<t_{0}-1/2
.
Since
\theta(0)\geq-1/2
, one can consider the maximal interval
J
containing
0

of parameters
t\in I
satisfying either
\varphi_{t}(x)\not\in\overline{U}
,
or
\theta(t)\geq t-1/2
.

Let
t_{1}
be the largest time in
[0,t_{0}]\cap J

satisfying
\varphi_{t_{1}}(x)\in\overline{U}
. It exists since

x=\varphi_{0}(x)\in\overline{U}
and
if
(t_{k})
is an increasing sequence in
[0,t_{0}]\cap J
satisfying

\varphi_{t_{k}}(x)\in\overline{U}
, then we have
\theta(t_{k})\geq t_{k}-1/2
.
So the limit
\overline{t}
satisfies
\theta(\overline{t})\geq\overline{t}-1/2
(and belongs to
J
)
and
\varphi_{\overline{t}}(x)\in\overline{U}
.

By construction
\theta(t_{1})\geq t_{1}-1/2
, hence
t_{1}<t_{0}
.
We claim that
\theta(t_{1})\geq t_{1}-\varepsilon
.
Indeed, one can reduce to consider the case
\theta(t_{1})\leq t_{1}+2

(otherwise the property holds trivially). Then, Property (b) gives
|\theta(t_{1})-t_{1}|\leq\varepsilon

which implies the claim.

For
s>t_{1}
close to
t_{1}
, we thus have
s\in J
. Since
t_{1}
is maximal

\varphi_{s}(x)\not\in\overline{U}
. Since
\varphi_{t_{0}}(x)\in U
,
there exists a minimal

\varphi_{t_{2}}(x)
belongs to the boundary of
U
.
In particular
\varphi_{\theta(t_{2})}(x)
is
r_{0}
-close to the boundary of
U
.
Note that
[t_{1},t_{2})\subset J
. By maximality of
t_{1}
one has
\theta(t_{2})<t_{2}-1/2
.

The “No small period” Property implies
\theta(t_{2})<t_{2}-2
.
In particular,

t_{2}>\theta(t_{2})+2>\theta(t_{1})+2\geq t_{1}+2-\varepsilon.

Hence the interval
[t_{1}+1/2,t_{2}-1/2]
is non-empty. By construction
any time

\varphi_{s+t}(x)\notin U
for
all

. One deduces
\varphi_{s}(x)\in\Delta
for all

Since
\varphi_{\theta(t_{1}+1/2)}(x)
is

-close to

,
and
\varphi_{t_{1}}(x)\in\overline{U}

(by our choice of

Since
\theta(t_{1})\geq t_{1}-\varepsilon
, this gives

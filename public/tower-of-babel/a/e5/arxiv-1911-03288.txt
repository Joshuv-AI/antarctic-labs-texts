Torus-Equivariant Chow Rings of Quiver Moduli
Hans FRANZEN

Abstract
We compute rational equivariant Chow rings with respect to a torus of quiver moduli spaces. We derive a presentation in terms of generators and relations, use torus localization to identify it as a subring of the Chow ring of the fixed point locus, and we compare the two descriptions.

Introduction

In this paper we study actions of tori on moduli spaces of quiver representations and determine their rational equivariant Chow rings as introduced by Edidin and Graham in
[
5
]
. More precisely, given a quiver
Q
, a dimension vector
d
, and a stability condition
\theta
, we look at the action of
T=\mathbb{G}_{m}^{Q_{1}}
which acts on
M^{\theta-\st}(Q,d)
by scaling along the arrows of
Q
. This action was introduced by Weist in
[
19
]
and has since been used successfully, for instance in
[
16
,
17
,
18
]
. The main objective of this paper is to determine the pull-back
i^{*}\colon A_{T}^{*}\big(M^{\theta-\st}(Q,d)\big)_{\mathbb{Q}}\to A_{T}^{*}\big(M^{\theta-\st}(Q,d)^{T}\big)_{\mathbb{Q}}
of the embedding
i\colon M^{\theta-\st}(Q,d)^{T}\to M^{\theta-\st}(Q,d)
of the fixed point locus explicitly and hence to find the
T
-equivariant Chow ring of
M^{\theta-\st}(Q,d)
as a subring of
A^{*}\big(M^{\theta-\st}(Q,d)^{T}\big)_{\mathbb{Q}}\otimes A_{T}^{*}(\pt)_{\mathbb{Q}}
. This is called torus localization and it is an idea that goes back to Goresky–Kottwitz–MacPherson’s paper
[
8
]
and even further to Chang–Skjelbred
[
4
]
, where equivariant cohomology rings of equivariantly formal spaces with respect to a compact torus are studied. Brion developed in
[
3
]
the algebraic counterpart of this theory. He gives descriptions of the equivariant Chow ring for an action of an algebraic torus on a variety. Both articles provide results of various generalities depending on the nature of the action of the torus. The most explicit description is possible if there are only finitely many fixed points and finitely many one-dimensional orbits. For quiver moduli however, this is hardly ever fulfilled. The fixed points of the aforementioned torus action are usually not isolated and even if they are, there might be infinitely many one-dimensional orbits. Still there is one particularly easy class of quiver moduli in which this condition holds. If we assume that the entries of
d
are all
1
then the moduli space is toric. This toric variety is well understood, see
[
1
]
. We study this case separately.

One of the main results of
[
19
]
asserts that each connected component of the locus of
T
-fixed points of
M^{\theta-\st}(Q,d)
is isomorphic to a stable moduli space of the universal abelian covering quiver
\smash{\widehat{Q}}
of
Q
. This result is the key ingredient to determine the localization map. We use that
M^{\theta-\st}(Q,d)
is an algebraic quotient
R(Q,d)^{\theta-\st}/PG_{d}
by a reductive group to identify the
T
-equivariant Chow ring of the quotient with the
PG_{d}\times T
-equivariant Chow ring of the total space. We can adapt a method of
[
6
]
to obtain a presentation of this ring in terms of Chern roots of certain
PG_{d}\times T
-equivariant vector bundles. This is Theorem
2
. We then exhibit in Theorem
5
the images of these generators under the localization map
i^{*}
using Weist’s characterization of the fixed points. This description is most useful if the torus acts with finitely many fixed points. We illustrate this result in an example where there are finitely many fixed points but infinitely many one-dimensional orbits. After that we turn to the case where
d=\mathbf{1}
, i.e.,
d
consists of ones entirely. In this case,
PG_{\mathbf{1}}
is itself a torus which embeds into
T
. We study the action of the cokernel
T_{0}
on the moduli space. We determine the fixed points and the one-dimensional orbits. Our description of the image of the localization map then follows from a result of Brion
[
3
]
.

2
Generalities on quiver moduli

Fix an algebraically closed field
k
. Let
Q
be a quiver. We denote its set of vertices with
Q_{0}
and its set of arrows with
Q_{1}
. With
s(\alpha)
and
t(\alpha)
we denote the source and target of an arrow
\alpha
. We assume throughout that
Q
is connected, which means that in the underlying unoriented graph, all vertices are connected by a path. Furthermore, we suppose that for every two vertices
i,j\in Q_{0}
there are just finitely many
\alpha\in Q_{1}
such that
s(\alpha)=i
and
t(\alpha)=j
. If
Q_{0}
(and hence also
Q_{1}
) is finite, then we say that
Q
is a finite quiver.

We assume that the reader is familiar with the basics of representation theory of quivers. For an introduction to the subject we refer to
[
2
, Section II.1]
.

Let
\Lambda(Q)
be the set of vectors
d\in\smash{\mathbb{Z}^{Q_{0}}}
such that
d_{i}=0
for all but finitely many
i\in Q_{0}
.
For
d\in\Lambda_{+}(Q):=\Lambda(Q)\cap\smash{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{Q_{0}}}
we fix
k
-vector spaces
V_{i}
of dimension
d_{i}
and we consider the finite-dimensional vector space

R(Q,d)=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in Q_{1}}\Hom(V_{s(\alpha)},V_{t(\alpha)}).

Its elements are representations of
Q
of dimension vector
d
. On
R(Q,d)
we have an action of the group
G_{d}=\prod_{i\in Q_{0}}\GL_{d_{i}}(k)
by

g\cdot M=\big(g_{t(\alpha)}M_{\alpha}g_{s(\alpha)}^{-1}\big)_{\alpha\in Q_{1}}.

Two elements of
R(Q,d)
, considered as representations of
Q
, are isomorphic if and only if they lie in the same
G_{d}
-orbit. Note that the image
\Delta\subseteq G_{d}
of the diagonal embedding of
\mathbb{G}_{m}
acts trivially on
R(Q,d)
so the action of
G_{d}
descends to an action of
PG_{d}=G_{d}/\Delta
.

A
\mathbb{Z}
-linear map
\theta\colon\Lambda(Q)\to\mathbb{Z}
is called a stability condition. For a vector
d\in\Lambda_{+}(Q)-\{0\}
we define the slope of
d
as

\mu(d)=\frac{\theta(d)}{\sum_{i}d_{i}}.

Define
\mu(M):=\mu(\dimvect M)
.
A representation
M
of
Q
is called
\theta
-semi-stable (
\theta
-stable) if
\mu(M^{\prime})\leq\mu(M)
(resp.
\mu(M^{\prime})<\mu(M)
) for every non-zero proper subrepresentation
M^{\prime}
of
M
.

We regard the vector space
R(Q,d)
as a variety and
G_{d}
as a linear algebraic group. The sets
R(Q,d)^{\theta-\sst}
and
R(Q,d)^{\theta-\st}
of semi-stable resp. stable points of
R(Q,d)
are Zariski open (but they might be empty). Obviously

R(Q,d)^{\theta-\st}\subseteq R(Q,d)^{\theta-\sst}\subseteq R(Q,d).

King shows in
[
9
, Proposition 3.1]
that the set of semi-stable points of
R(Q,d)
agrees with the set of semi-stable points with respect to the
G_{d}
-linearization of the (trivial) line bundle on
R(Q,d)
which is given by the character

\chi_{\theta}(g)=\prod_{i\in Q_{0}}\det(g_{i})^{\theta(d)-\theta_{i}\sum_{j}d_{j}}.

Note that
\chi_{\theta}
is trivial on
\Delta
whence it descends to a character of
PG_{d}
. The twist in the exponent in the definition of the character
\chi_{\theta}
was introduced in
[
15
, Section 3.4]
to get rid of the requirement
\theta(d)=0
. The set
R(Q,d)^{\theta-\st}
is the set of properly stable points (in the sense of Mumford
[
13
, Defenition 1.8]
) with respect to the aforementioned linearized line bundle. This holds as the isotropy group of a stable representation is
\Delta
by Schur’s lemma.

We define
M^{\theta-\sst}(Q,d)=R(Q,d)^{\theta-\sst}/\!\!/PG_{d}
and
M^{\theta-\st}(Q,d)=R(Q,d)^{\theta-\st}/PG_{d}
. The quotient map
R(Q,d)^{\theta-\st}\to M^{\theta-\st}(Q,d)
is a
PG_{d}
-torsor in the étale topology (see
[
13
, Proposition 0.9]
) with a smooth total space, so we conclude that
M^{\theta-\st}(Q,d)
is smooth. The induced morphism
M^{\theta-\sst}(Q,d)\to M^{0-\sst}(Q,d)=R(Q,d)/\!\!/PG_{d}
is projective. A result of Le Bruyn and Procesi
[
11
, Theorem 1]
states that the ring of invariants is generated by traces along oriented cycles in
Q
. If
Q
has no oriented cycles, in which case we call
Q
acyclic, then
M^{\theta-\sst}(Q,d)
is projective. If
R(Q,d)^{\theta-\sst}=R(Q,d)^{\theta-\st}
we call
\theta
generic for
d
and we write
R(Q,d)^{\theta}
for the (semi-)stable locus and
M^{\theta}(Q,d)
for the quotient.

Let
\mathcal{V}_{i}
be the (trivial) vector bundle on
R(Q,d)
with fiber
V_{i}
equipped with the
G_{d}
-linearization given by

g(M,v)=(g\cdot M,g_{i}v).

Note that
\Delta
does not act trivially on the fibers and hence
\mathcal{V}_{i}
does not descend along the geometric quotient
R(Q,d)^{\theta-\st}\to M^{\theta-\st}(Q,d)
. However, the bundles
\mathcal{V}_{i}^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{V}_{j}
do.

3
Torus actions on quiver moduli

Fix a finite quiver
Q
and a dimension vector
d
. Let
T=\mathbb{G}_{m}^{Q_{1}}
act on
R(Q,d)
as follows: an element
t=(t_{\alpha})_{\alpha}\in T
acts on
M=(M_{\alpha})_{\alpha}\in R(Q,d)
by
t.M=(t_{\alpha}M_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
. As it commutes with the
PG_{d}
-action on
R(Q,d)
, the action of
T
descends to an action on the geometric quotient
M^{\theta-\st}(Q,d)
.

The locus of fixed points
M^{\theta-\st}(Q,d)^{T}
can be described in terms of the universal abelian covering quiver
\smash{\widehat{Q}}
. The (infinite) quiver
\smash{\widehat{Q}}
is given by
\smash{\widehat{Q}_{0}}=Q_{0}\times\mathbb{Z}^{Q_{1}}
,
\smash{\widehat{Q}_{1}}=Q_{1}\times\mathbb{Z}^{Q_{1}}
and for
\alpha\in Q_{1}
and
\chi\in\mathbb{Z}^{Q_{1}}
the source and target of the arrow
(\alpha,\chi)
of
\smash{\widehat{Q}}
are

\displaystyle s(\alpha,\chi)=(s(\alpha),\chi),\qquad t(\alpha,\chi)=(t(\alpha),\chi+x_{\alpha}).

The character
x_{\alpha}\in X(T)
in the right-hand expression above is defined as
x_{\alpha}(t)=t_{\alpha}
.

We say that a dimension vector
\beta\in\smash{\Lambda_{+}\big(\widehat{Q}\big)}
covers
d
if
\smash{\sum_{\chi}}\beta_{i,\chi}=d_{i}
for every vertex
i
. There is an action of
\mathbb{Z}^{Q_{1}}
on
\smash{\Lambda_{+}\big(\widehat{Q}\big)}
given by
\xi\cdot\beta=(\beta_{i,\chi+\xi})_{(i,\chi)}
. Two dimension vectors in the same orbit will be called translates of one another. Translates clearly cover the same dimension vector of
Q
. We define a stability condition
\smash{\widehat{\theta}}
for
\widehat{Q}
by
\smash{\widehat{\theta}}_{(i,\chi)}=\theta_{i}
. The following result is due to Weist:

Theorem 1

(
[
19
, Theorem 3.8]
)
.

The fixed point locus
M^{\theta-\st}(Q,d)^{T}
decomposes as

M^{\theta-\st}(Q,d)^{T}=\bigsqcup_{\beta}X_{\beta},

a disjoint union of irreducible components
X_{\beta}
, where the union ranges over all
\beta\in\Lambda_{+}\big(\widehat{Q}\big)
up to translation which cover
d
. Each
X_{\beta}
is isomorphic to

X_{\beta}\cong M^{\widehat{\theta}-\st}\big(\widehat{Q},\beta\big).

4
Torus-equivariant tautological relations

Again let
Q
be a finite quiver, let
d
be a dimension vector for
Q
and let
\theta
be a stability condition. We compute a presentation for the rational equivariant Chow ring
A_{T}^{*}\big(M^{\theta-\st}(Q,d)\big)_{\mathbb{Q}}
. For the definition and general results on equivariant Chow rings we refer to
[
5
]
.
We will work with Chow rings with rational coefficients throughout.

The
T
-equivariant Chow ring
A_{T}^{*}\big(M^{\theta-\st}(Q,d)\big)_{\mathbb{Q}}
coincides with the
PG_{d}\times T
-equivariant Chow ring
A_{PG_{d}\times T}^{*}(R(Q,d)^{\theta-\st})_{\mathbb{Q}}
by
[
12
, Lemma 2.1]
. This is not far away from the
G_{d}\times T
-equivariant Chow ring. So we would first like to compute the rings

\displaystyle A_{G_{d}\times T}^{*}\big(R(Q,d)^{\theta-\st}\big)_{\mathbb{Q}},\qquad A_{G_{d}\times T}^{*}\big(R(Q,d)^{\theta-\sst}\big)_{\mathbb{Q}}.

We will derive a presentation of these rings.

Choose a basis for each of the
V_{i}
. Let
T_{d}
be the maximal torus of
G_{d}
of diagonal matrices. Let us recap the definitions of the groups that we are using:

\displaystyle G_{d}=\prod_{i\in Q_{0}}\GL(V_{i})\cong\prod_{i\in Q_{0}}\GL_{d_{i}}(k),

\displaystyle T_{d}\subseteq G_{d}\text{ maximal torus of invertible diagonal matrices},

\displaystyle\Delta=\{(z\id_{V_{i}})_{i}\,|\,z\in k^{\times}\}\subseteq G_{d},

\displaystyle PG_{d}=G_{d}/\Delta,

\displaystyle PT_{d}=T_{d}/\Delta,

\displaystyle T=\mathbb{G}_{m}^{Q_{1}}.

Let
\xi_{i,r}\in X(T_{d})
be the character that selects in the matrix corresponding to the vertex
i
the
r
th
diagonal entry. Recall that
x_{\alpha}\in X(T)
is the character that selects the entry which corresponds to
\alpha
. For a character
\chi\in X(T_{d}\times T)=X(T_{d})\oplus X(T)
let
L(\chi)
be the trivial line bundle on
R(Q,d)
equipped with the
T_{d}\times T
-linearization induced by
\chi
. So
\smash{c_{1}^{T_{d}\times T}(L(\chi))}=\chi
.
As
G_{d}\times T
acts linearly on the vector space
R(Q,d)
, we obtain

\displaystyle A_{G_{d}\times T}^{*}(R(Q,d))_{\mathbb{Q}}\cong A_{G_{d}\times T}^{*}(\pt)_{\mathbb{Q}}\cong\bigg(\bigotimes_{i\in Q_{0}}\mathbb{Q}[\xi_{i,1},\dots,\xi_{i,d_{i}}]^{S_{d_{i}}}\bigg)\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}[x_{\alpha}]_{\alpha\in Q_{1}}

\displaystyle\hphantom{A_{G_d \times T}^*(R(Q,d))_\mathbb{Q}}{}=\bigg(\bigotimes_{i\in Q_{0}}\mathbb{Q}[x_{i,1},\dots,x_{i,d_{i}}]\bigg)\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}[x_{\alpha}]_{\alpha\in Q_{1}}.

In the above equation
x_{i,r}
is the
r
th
elementary symmetric function in the variables
\xi_{i,1},\dots,\xi_{i,d_{i}}
. Note that
x_{i,r}=c_{r}^{G_{d}\times T}(\mathcal{V}_{i})
.
Let
j_{1}\colon R(Q,d)^{\theta-\sst}\to R(Q,d)
and
j_{2}\colon R(Q,d)^{\theta-\st}\to R(Q,d)
be the open embeddings.
The pull-backs of
j_{1}
and
j_{2}
induce surjective homomorphisms of graded rings

Let
i_{1}
and
i_{2}
be the closed embeddings of
R(Q,d)-R(Q,d)^{\theta-\sst}
and
R(Q,d)-R(Q,d)^{\theta-\st}
, respectively.
The push-forwards of
i_{1}
and
i_{2}
give surjections onto the kernels of
j_{1}^{*}
resp.
j_{2}^{*}
. The same arguments as in
[
6

show that the images of
i_{1}^{*}
and
i_{2}^{*}
can be re-written in terms of the
T
-equivariant CoHA multiplication. More precisely, we consider the correspondence diagram

R(Q,d^{\prime})\times R(Q,d^{\prime\prime})\leftarrow\begin{pmatrix}R(Q,d^{\prime})&*\\
0&R(Q,d^{\prime\prime})\end{pmatrix}\to R(Q,d),

where
d^{\prime}
and
d^{\prime\prime}
are dimension vectors of
Q
such that
d^{\prime}+d^{\prime\prime}=d
.
The left-hand map is the projection and the right-hand map is the inclusion as a linear subspace. These maps are equivariant with respect to

G_{d^{\prime}}\times G_{d^{\prime\prime}}\times T\leftarrow\begin{pmatrix}G_{d^{\prime}}&*\\
0&G_{d^{\prime\prime}}\end{pmatrix}\times T\to G_{d}\times T.

Passing to equivariant Chow groups we obtain a map

A_{G_{d^{\prime}}\times T}^{*}(R(Q,d^{\prime}))_{\mathbb{Q}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}A_{G_{d^{\prime\prime}}\times T}^{*}(R(Q,d^{\prime\prime}))_{\mathbb{Q}}\to A_{G_{d}\times T}^{*}(R(Q,d))_{\mathbb{Q}},

which sends
f\otimes g
to
f*g
which is given by

f*g=\sum_{\pi}f(\xi_{i,\pi^{i}(r)},x_{\alpha})_{r=1,\dots,d^{\prime}_{i}}\cdot g(\xi_{i,\pi^{i}(r)},x_{\alpha})_{r=d^{\prime}_{i}+1,\dots,d_{i}}\cdot\frac{\Delta_{1}(\xi_{i,\pi^{i}(r)},x_{\alpha})}{\Delta_{0}(\xi_{i,\pi^{i}(r)})}.

In the above formula
\pi=(\pi^{i})_{i\in Q_{0}}
ranges over all elements of
\prod_{i\in Q_{0}}S_{d_{i}}
for which each
\pi^{i}

\displaystyle\Delta_{0}=\prod_{i\in Q_{0}}\prod_{r=1}^{d^{\prime}_{i}}\prod_{s=d^{\prime}_{i}+1}^{d_{i}}(\xi_{i,s}-\xi_{i,r}),

\displaystyle\Delta_{1}=\prod_{\alpha:i\to j}\prod_{r=1}^{d^{\prime}_{i}}\prod_{s=d^{\prime}_{j}+1}^{d_{j}}(\xi_{j,s}-\xi_{i,r}+x_{\alpha}).

Recall that being a
(d^{\prime}_{i},d^{\prime\prime}_{i})
-shuffle permutation means
\pi^{i}(1)<\dots<\pi^{i}(d^{\prime}_{i})

\pi^{i}(d^{\prime}_{i}+1)<\dots<\pi^{i}(d_{i})
.

Now to the
T
-equivariant Chow ring of
M^{\theta-\st}(Q,d)
. The maximal torus
T_{d}
of
G_{d}
contains
\Delta
. The quotient
PT_{d}:=T_{d}/\Delta
is a maximal torus of
PG_{d}
. Its character lattice is given by

X(PT_{d})=\left\{\eta=\sum_{i\in Q_{0}}\sum_{r=1}^{d_{i}}b_{i,r}\xi_{i,r}\,|\,\sum_{i\in Q_{0}}\sum_{r=1}^{d_{i}}b_{i,r}=0\right\}\subseteq X(T_{d}).

The Weyl group of
PT_{d}
inside

is also
W_{d}:=\prod_{i\in Q_{0}}S_{d_{i}}
. Therefore

A_{PG_{d}\times T}^{*}(R(Q,d))_{\mathbb{Q}}\cong S(X(PT_{d}))_{\mathbb{Q}}^{W_{d}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}[x_{\alpha}]_{\alpha\in Q_{1}}

\smash{A_{PG_{d}\times T}^{*}}(R(Q,d))_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\smash{A_{G_{d}\times T}^{*}}(R(Q,d))_{\mathbb{Q}}

G_{d}\to PG_{d}
corresponds to the homomorphism that is induced by the inclusion
X(PT_{d})\hookrightarrow X(T_{d})
. We can give generators for
\smash{S(X(PT_{d}))_{\mathbb{Q}}^{W_{d}}}
. Fix an order on
Q_{0}
, say
Q_{0}=\{1,\dots,n\}
. Then consider the lattice
Z_{d}=\smash{\bigoplus_{1\leq i<j\leq n}}\mathbb{Z}^{d_{i}}\otimes\mathbb{Z}^{d_{j}}
. Let
\smash{\zeta_{r,s}^{i,j}}
be the pure tensor of unit vectors

embedded into the

Z_{d}
. On

there is an action of
W_{d}
in the obvious way. The map
Z_{d}\to X(PT_{d})
which sends
\smash{\zeta_{r,t}^{i,j}}
to

is well-defined,

z_{k,l}^{i,j}:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq r_{1}<\dots<r_{k}\leq d_{i}\\

Remark 3
.

Suppose that
k=\mathbb{C}
. The same arguments as in
[
6
, Theorem 5.1]
show that the
G_{d}\times T
-equivariant and also the
PG_{d}\times T
-equivariant cohomology of
R(Q,d)^{\theta-\sst}

H^{\prime}
defined by

and
H^{\prime}_{1}=H_{1}-\{\gamma\}
. Let

. Then

. By induction assumption there exists

such that
\smash{g^{\prime}_{t(\alpha)}{g^{\prime}}_{s(\alpha)}^{-1}}
for all

is an inverse image of

∎

We hence may identify for a
\theta
-stable spanning tree

the equivariant Chow ring
\smash{A_{T_{0}}^{*}}\!(\{[M_{H}]\})_{\mathbb{Q}}
of the (isolated) fixed point

with

The pull-back of the inclusion

of the fixed point then corresponds to the map

i_{H}^{*}\colon\ A_{T}^{*}\big(R(Q,\mathbf{1})^{\theta-\st}\big)_{\mathbb{Q}}\to A_{T_{H^{c}}}^{*}(\pt)_{\mathbb{Q}}=\mathbb{Q}[x_{\alpha}]_{\alpha\in H^{c}},

i_{H}^{*}(x_{\alpha})=\begin{cases}x_{\alpha},&\alpha\in H^{c},\\

We now want to describe the image of the pull-back of the inclusion of the fixed point locus. We use the following result of Brion. It is the algebraic analog of
[

Theorem 12

(
[
3
, Theorem 3.4]
)
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety on which a torus

acts with finitely many fixed points
x_{1},\dots,x_{N}
and with finitely many one-dimensional orbits. Then the image of the localization map

i^{*}\colon\ A_{T}^{*}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}\to S(X(T))_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\oplus N}

consists of all tuples
(f_{1},\dots,f_{N})
such that
f_{i}\equiv f_{j}
modulo
\chi
whenever there exists a one-dimensional
T
-orbit on which

acts with the character
\chi
and whose closure contains
x_{i}
and
x_{j}
.

We need to require

to be acyclic and
\theta
to be generic for
\mathbf{1}
in order to ensure the moduli space is smooth and projective. The action of
T_{0}
on
M^{\theta}(Q,\mathbf{1})
possesses just finitely many one-dimensional orbits. To describe them we introduce the notion of a spanning almost tree. A subset
\Omega\subseteq Q_{1}
is called a spanning almost tree if it is not a spanning tree but there exists an arrow
\alpha\in\Omega
such that
\Omega-\{\alpha\}
is a spanning tree. Note that this forces
(Q_{0},\Omega)
to be connected. Given a spanning almost tree we again define a representation
M_{\Omega}\in R(Q,\mathbf{1})
by assigning
M_{\alpha}=1
for
\alpha\in\Omega
and
M_{\alpha}=0
otherwise. We say
\Omega
is
\theta
-stable if
M_{\Omega}
is
\theta
-stable.

Lemma 13
.

Let
M\in R(Q,\mathbf{1})^{\theta}
. The orbit of
[M]
under
T_{0}
is one-dimensional if and only if
\operatorname{supp}(M)
is a
\theta
-stable spanning almost tree.

Proof.

Let
M
be a
\theta
-stable representation with a one-dimensional orbit. Put
\Omega:=\operatorname{supp}(M)
. Note that stability of
M
implies connectedness of
(Q_{0},\Omega)
. Assume that
\Omega
is not a
\theta
-stable spanning almost tree. As
M_{\Omega}
is contained in the
T
-orbit of
M
we may assume
M=M_{\Omega}
. Let
B=\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r}\}
be a maximal subset of
\Omega
such that
(Q_{0},\Omega-B)
is connected. Then
r\geq 2
as
\Omega
is neither a spanning almost tree nor a spanning tree. Moreover,
H:=\Omega-B
is a spanning tree (which is not necessarily
\theta
-stable). Let
(t_{1},\dots,t_{r})\in(k^{\times})^{r}
. Define the representation
M_{t}
by

(M_{t})_{\alpha}=\begin{cases}t_{k},&\alpha=\alpha_{k},\\
1,&\alpha\in H,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Clearly
M_{t}
is in the
T
-orbit of
M
. Moreover two such representations
M_{t}
and
M_{s}
are not isomorphic as a
g\in G_{1}
with
g\cdot M_{t}=M_{s}
would need to satisfy
g\cdot M_{H}=M_{H}
which implies

by Lemma
10
(stability is not necessary for this argument to hold). This shows that the orbit of
[M]
would be (at least)

-dimensional which is a contradiction.
The converse direction is obvious.
∎

The closure of such a one-dimensional orbit is isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
and hence contains precisely two fixed points. This means that from a
\theta
-stable spanning almost tree there are precisely two ways to remove an arrow and obtain a
\theta
-stable spanning tree. This does not seem to be obvious from the combinatorics of stable spanning almost trees. Let these arrows be
\alpha_{0}
and
\alpha_{\infty}
. Let
\Omega_{0}=\Omega-\{\alpha_{0}\}
and
\Omega_{\infty}=\Omega-\{\alpha_{\infty}\}
. These are the two
\theta
-stable spanning trees which represent the two fixed points in the closure of the orbit of
[M_{\Omega}]
. The inclusions
\Omega_{0}^{c}\supseteq\Omega^{c}\subseteq\Omega_{\infty}^{c}
give rise to the maps

A_{T_{\Omega_{0}^{c}}}^{*}(\pt)\xrightarrow{r_{\Omega,0}}A_{T_{\Omega^{c}}}^{*}(\pt)\xleftarrow{r_{\Omega,\infty}}A_{T_{\Omega_{\infty}^{c}}}^{*}(\pt),

which send
r_{\Omega,0}(x_{\alpha_{0}})=0
and
r_{\Omega,\infty}(x_{\alpha_{\infty}})=0
and act as the identity on the other variables. Let us write

for the image of a function
f\in\smash{A_{T_{\Omega_{0}^{c}}}^{*}(\pt)_{\mathbb{Q}}}=\smash{\mathbb{Q}[x_{\alpha}]_{\alpha\in\Omega_{0}^{c}}}
or
f\in\smash{A_{T_{\Omega_{\infty}^{c}}}^{*}(\pt)_{\mathbb{Q}}}=\smash{\mathbb{Q}[x_{\alpha}]_{\alpha\in\Omega_{\infty}^{c}}}
under the maps
r_{\Omega,0}
or

, respectively.

Theorem 14
.

The pull-back of the embedding of the fixed point locus
i\colon M^{\theta}(Q,\mathbf{1})^{T_{0}}\hookrightarrow M^{\theta}(Q,\mathbf{1})
is the map

i^{*}\colon\ A_{T}^{*}\big(R(Q,\mathbf{1})^{\theta}\big)_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\bigoplus_{H}\mathbb{Q}[x_{\alpha}]_{\alpha\in H^{c}},

which sends
f
to
(i_{H}^{*}(f))_{H}
. It is injective and its image consists precisely of those tuples
(f_{H})_{H}
for which
f_{\Omega_{0}}|_{\Omega^{c}}=f_{\Omega_{\infty}}|_{\Omega^{c}}
for every
\theta
-stable spanning almost tree
\Omega
.

The direct sum in the theorem ranges over all
\theta
-stable spanning trees
H
of
Q
.

Proof.

This is an application of Theorem
12
. Let us show how the statement of our theorem follows from it. Let
\Omega
be a
\theta
-stable spanning almost tree. Let
C
be the closure of the
T_{0}
-orbit of
[M_{\Omega}]
in
M^{\theta}(Q,\mathbf{1})
. Let
t\in T_{\Omega_{0}^{c}}
. We obtain

(t.M)_{\alpha}=\begin{cases}t_{\alpha_{0}},&\alpha=\alpha_{0},\\
1,&\alpha\in\Omega_{0},\\
0,&\alpha\in\Omega^{c}.\end{cases}

We identify the
\smash{T_{\Omega_{0}^{c}}}
-orbit of
[M_{\Omega}]
with
\mathbb{G}_{m}
by the entry which corresponds to the arrow
\alpha_{0}
. Then
T_{\Omega_{0}^{c}}
acts on
\mathbb{G}_{m}
by the character
x_{\alpha_{0}}
. Take the resulting identification of
C
with
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. The limit for
t_{\alpha_{0}}\to 0
is
[M_{\Omega_{0}}]
and the limit for
t_{\alpha_{0}}\to\infty
must hence be
[M_{\Omega_{\infty}}]
. Consider the composition of the isomorphisms of tori
\smash{T_{\Omega_{0}^{c}}}\to T_{0}\to\smash{T_{\Omega_{\infty}^{c}}}
from Lemma
10
. Call it
\varphi
. To determine
\varphi
, let
t\in\smash{T_{\Omega_{0}^{c}}}
and find the unique

for which there exists a
g\in G_{\mathbf{1}}
such that
g_{t(\alpha)}t_{\alpha}g_{s(\alpha)}^{-1}=s_{\alpha}
for all
\alpha\in Q_{1}
. The element
g
is uniquely determined up to scaling. We get four equations:

\displaystyle g_{t(\alpha)}g_{s(\alpha)}^{-1}=1,\qquad\alpha\in\Omega-\{\alpha_{0},\alpha_{\infty}\},

\displaystyle g_{t(\alpha)}t_{\alpha}g_{s(\alpha)}^{-1}=s_{\alpha}\qquad\alpha\in\Omega^{c},

\displaystyle g_{t(\alpha_{0})}t_{\alpha_{0}}g_{s(\alpha_{0})}^{-1}=1,

\displaystyle g_{t(\alpha_{\infty})}g_{s(\alpha_{\infty})}^{-1}=s_{\alpha_{\infty}}.

As
\Omega_{\infty}
is a spanning tree,
(Q_{0},\Omega-\{\alpha_{0},\alpha_{\infty}\})
is not connected. Let
C_{1},C_{2}\subseteq Q_{0}
be its connected components. The vertices
i_{0}:=s(\alpha_{0})
and
j_{0}:=t(\alpha_{0})
lie in different components, so we may assume
i_{0}\in C_{1}
and
j_{0}\in C_{2}
. As
\Omega_{0}
is connected,
i_{\infty}:=s(\alpha_{\infty})
and
j_{\infty}:=t(\alpha_{\infty})
cannot be contained in the same component. We first consider the case where
i_{\infty}\in C_{1}
and
j_{\infty}\in C_{2}
. We get
g_{i}
is constant on
C_{1}
and on
C_{2}
. Let its value on
C_{1}
be
g_{1}
and its value on
C_{2}
be
g_{2}
. Now we obtain

\displaystyle g_{2}t_{\alpha_{0}}g_{1}^{-1}=1,\qquad g_{2}g_{1}^{-1}=s_{\alpha_{\infty}}.

This implies
s_{\alpha_{\infty}}=t_{\alpha_{0}}^{-1}
and
g_{2}g_{1}^{-1}=t_{\alpha_{0}}^{-1}
. For
\alpha\colon i\to j
in
\Omega^{c}
we again have to distinguish three cases. If

and
j
are in the same component then
s_{\alpha}=t_{\alpha}
. If
i\in C_{1}
and
j\in C_{2}
then we get
s_{\alpha}=g_{2}t_{\alpha}g_{1}^{-1}=t_{\alpha_{0}}^{-1}t_{\alpha}
. Finally if

and

then we obtain
s_{\alpha}=g_{1}t_{\alpha}g_{2}^{-1}=t_{\alpha_{0}}t_{\alpha}
. We define

\delta_{\alpha}:=\begin{cases}\hphantom{-}0,&i,j\in C_{1}\text{ or }i,j\in C_{2},\\
-1,&i\in C_{1},\ j\in C_{2},\\
+1,&i\in C_{2},\ j\in C_{1}.\end{cases}

We get

\varphi(t)_{\alpha}=\begin{cases}t_{\alpha_{0}}^{-1},&\alpha=\alpha_{\infty},\\
t_{\alpha_{0}}^{\delta_{\alpha}}t_{\alpha},&\alpha\in\Omega^{c},\\
1,&\alpha\in\Omega_{\infty}.\end{cases}

We now deal with the second case where
i_{\infty}\in C_{2}
and
j_{\infty}\in C_{1}
. Then
\varphi
looks the same except for
\varphi(t)_{\alpha_{\infty}}=t_{\alpha_{0}}
. The torus
\smash{T_{\Omega_{\infty}^{c}}}
acts on

by the character
x_{\alpha_{\infty}}
. But as
\lim\limits_{t_{\alpha_{0}}\to\infty}\varphi(t).[M_{\Omega}]=\lim\limits_{t_{\alpha_{0}}\to\infty}t.[M_{\Omega}]=[M_{\Omega_{\infty}}]
we see that
\varphi(t)_{\alpha_{\infty}}=t_{\alpha_{0}}^{-1}
. So the second case will not occur.

The induced map by
\varphi
on the character lattices is
\varphi^{*}\colon X(\smash{T_{\Omega_{\infty}^{c}}})\to X(\smash{T_{\Omega_{0}^{c}}})
which is given by

\displaystyle\varphi^{*}(x_{\alpha_{\infty}})=-x_{\alpha_{0}},\qquad\varphi^{*}(x_{\alpha})=x_{\alpha}+\delta_{\alpha}x_{\alpha_{0}}

for

. Brion’s theorem now tells us that the image of

consists precisely of those tuples

for which

f_{\Omega_{0}}(x_{\alpha_{0}},x_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\Omega^{c}}\equiv f_{\Omega_{\infty}}(-x_{\alpha_{0}},x_{\alpha}+\delta_{\alpha}x_{\alpha_{0}})_{\alpha\in\Omega^{c}}

modulo

. This is the same as to say
f_{\Omega_{0}}|_{\Omega^{c}}=f_{\Omega_{\infty}}|_{\Omega^{c}}
.
∎

Remark 15
.

In
[
14
]
Pabiniak and Sabatini find a basis for the
T
-equivariant cohomology ring
H_{T}^{*}(M)
regarded as a module over
S(X(T))
for a compact symplectic toric manifold
M
with torus
T
. Let us briefly recap their result. They fix a moment map and a generic component
\mu
of it. A result of Kirwan states that for every
T
-fixed point
p\in M^{T}
there exists a class
\nu_{p}\in H^{2\lambda_{p}}(M)
such that its restriction
\nu_{p}|p\in\smash{H^{2\lambda_{p}}}(\{p\})
agrees with the equivariant Euler class of the negative normal bundle
N_{p}^{-}
of
\mu
at
p
, while
\nu_{p}|q=0
for every
q\in M^{T}
with
\mu(q)<\mu(p)
. Such a class
\nu_{p}
is called a Kirwan class. Any collection of Kirwan classes
\{\nu_{p}\}_{p\in M^{T}}
is a basis of
H_{T}^{*}(M)
over
S(X(T))
. Kirwan classes, however, are not unique. In
[
14
, Definition 4.5]
a special type of Kirwan class is defined, the so-called i-canonical class. This is a Kirwan class
\tau_{p}
such that the local index of
\tau_{p}
at
p
is 1, whereas the local index at every other fixed point vanishes. In
[
14
, Theorem 1.2]
it is shown that a set
\{\tau_{p}\}_{p\in M^{T}}
of i-canonical classes exists and is unique. It would be interesting to determine the i-canonical classes in the toric case and compare them to our result.

We end by illustrating this result in two examples.

Example 16
.

Let
Q
be the
n+1
-Kronecker quiver,

and

as in Example

. We have already identified

with

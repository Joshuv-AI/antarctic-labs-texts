On Σ \Sigma -skew reflexive-nilpotents-property for rings Thanks: The authors were supported by the research fund of Department of Mathematics, Faculty of Science, Universidad Nacional de Colombia - Sede Bogotá, Colombia, HERMES CODE 52464.
Héctor Suárez, Sebastián Higuera, Armando Reyes

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the reflexive-nilpotents-property (briefly, RNP) for Ore extensions of injective type, and more generally, skew PBW extensions. With this aim, we introduce the notions of Σ \Sigma -skew CN rings and Σ \Sigma -skew reflexive (RNP) rings, for Σ \Sigma a finite family of ring endomorphisms of a ring R R . Under certain conditions of compatibility, we study the transfer of the Σ \Sigma -skew RNP property from a ring of coefficients to an Ore extension or skew PBW extension over this ring. We also consider this property for localizations of these noncommutative rings. Our results extend those corresponding presented by Bhattacharjee [ 9 ] .

Throughout the paper,

is its upper nilradical (i.e. the sum of all nil ideals). It is well-known that

, and if the equality

[
52

forms an ideal if and only if
R/N^{*}(R)

[
30

is an NI ring of bounded index nilpotency, then

[
32

Following Cohn

14

43

[
2

, and
[
53

). We know that every reduced ring is symmetric

66

, but the converse does not hold
[
2
, Example II.5]
. Bell

Insertion-of-Factors-Property

In addition to reversible rings and their aforementioned generalizations of reduced rings, we also have the notion of reflexive ring. Mason
[
54

introduced the reflexive property for right ideals by defining a right ideal

They established a method by which a reflexive ring, which is not semiprime, can always be constructed from any semiprime ring, and showed that the reflexive property is Morita invariant. Kim

Preliminaries

Definition 2.1
.

(
[
17
, Definition 1]
)
Let
R
and
A
be rings. We say that
A
is a
skew PBW extension over

R
(the ring of coefficients), denoted
A=\sigma(R)\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\rangle
, if the following conditions hold:

(1)

R
is a subring of
A
sharing the same identity element.

(2)

there exist finitely many elements
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in A
such that
A
is a left free
R
-module, with basis the
set of standard monomials

{\rm Mon}(A):=\{x^{\alpha}:=x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}\mid\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}\}
.

Moreover,
x^{0}_{1}\cdots x^{0}_{n}:=1\in{\rm Mon}(A)
.

(3)

For each
1\leq i\leq n
and any
r\in R\ \backslash\ \{0\}
, there exists an
element
c_{i,r}\in R\ \backslash\ \{0\}
such that
x_{i}r-c_{i,r}x_{i}\in R
.

(4)

For
1\leq i,j\leq n
, there exists
d_{i,j}\in R\ \backslash\ \{0\}
such that

x_{j}x_{i}-d_{i,j}x_{i}x_{j}\in R+Rx_{1}+\cdots+Rx_{n},

i.e. there exist elements
r_{0}^{(i,j)},r_{1}^{(i,j)},\dotsc,r_{n}^{(i,j)}\in R
with
x_{j}x_{i}-d_{i,j}x_{i}x_{j}=r_{0}^{(i,j)}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}r_{k}^{(i,j)}x_{k}
.

From the definition it follows that every non-zero element
f\in A
can be uniquely expressed as
f=a_{0}+a_{1}X_{1}+\cdots+a_{m}X_{m}
, with
a_{i}\in R
and
X_{i}\in\text{Mon}(A)
, for
0\leq i\leq m

[
17
, Remark 2]
. For
X=x^{\alpha}=x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}\in\text{Mon}(A)

\text{deg}(X)=|\alpha|:=\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{n}
.

Remark 2.4

Some relationships between skew polynomial rings and skew PBW extensions are the following:

•

[

If

[

If

\sigma_{i}(r)

Skew PBW extensions of endomorphism type are more general than iterated skew polynomial rings of endomorphism type. With the aim of illustrating the differences between these structures, we consider the situations with two and three indeterminates. To finish this section, we recall the notion of Armendariz ring in the setting of commutative and noncommutative rings. Following Rege and Chhawchharia
[
60
]
, a ring

is called

if for elements

(where

is the commutative polynomial ring with an indeterminate
x
over
R
),
f(x)g(x)=0
implies
a_{i}b_{j}=0
, for all
i,j
. The importance of this notion lies in its natural and useful role in understanding the relation between the annihilators of the ring
R
and the annihilators of the polynomial ring
R[x]
. For example, Armendariz
[
4
, Lemma 1]
, showed that a reduced ring always satisfies this condition. For skew polynomial rings, the notion of Armendariz has been also studied by several authors (see
[
1
,
4
,
29
,
31
,
37
,
44
,
60
]
). Hirano
[
27
]
generalized this notion in the following way: a ring
R
is called

if for
f(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{m}a_{i}x^{i},\ g(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{l}b_{j}x^{j}
in
R[x]
,
f(x)R[x]g(x)=0
implies
a_{i}Rb_{j}=0
, for all
i,j
. It is well known that reduced rings are Armendariz, and Armendariz rings are quasi-Armendariz, but the converse are not true in general
[
13
, Proposition 2.1]
.

In the noncommutative setting, Hashemi et al.
[
25
]
investigated a generalization of
\sigma
-rigid rings by introducing the (SQA1) condition which is a version of quasi-Armendariz rings for skew polynomial rings. Let

be an Ore extension over

.

is said to satisfy the (

)

if whenever
f(x)R[x;\sigma,\delta]g(x)=0
for

and

, then

for all

. Later, Reyes and Suárez
[

considered this condition for skew PBW extensions. For

a skew PBW extension over

,

is said to satisfy the (

)

if whenever
fAg=0
for

and
g=b_{0}+b_{1}Y_{1}+\dotsb+b_{t}Y_{t}
elements of
A
, then
a_{i}Rb_{j}=0
, for every
i,j
. Relationships between the notions of compatibility, (SQA1), and Armendariz rings, in the context of skew PBW extensions, can be found in
[

,
65
]
. All of them will be useful in the next sections.

Definition 3.1

Let

\Sigma

(resp.,

)

\Sigma
-

Reflexive rings are
\Sigma
-skew reflexive. Since reduced rings and reversible rings are reflexive, then both rings are
\Sigma

Right (resp., left)
\sigma
-skew reflexive rings are right (resp., left)
\Sigma
-skew reflexive.

Example 3.2
.

Given a ring
R
and
M
an
(R,R)
-bimodule,
the trival extension of R by M
is the ring

with the usual addition and the multiplication defined as
(r_{1},m_{1})(r_{2},m_{2}):=(r_{1}r_{2},r_{1}m_{2}+m_{1}r_{2})

and

is isomorphic to the matrix ring (with the usual matrix operations) of the form
\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}r&m\\

, where

and

the ring of matrices isomorphic to

Consider the ring
S_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
given by

S_{2}(\mathbb{Z})=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
0&a\end{pmatrix}\mid a,b\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}

Let
\sigma_{1}={\rm id}_{S_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}
be the identity endomorphism of
S_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, and let
\sigma_{2}
and
\sigma_{3}
be the two endomorphisms of

defined by

\sigma_{2}\left(\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
0&a\end{pmatrix}\right)=\begin{pmatrix}a&-b\\
0&a\end{pmatrix},\ \ \ \ \ \ \sigma_{3}\left(\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
0&a\end{pmatrix}\right)=\begin{pmatrix}a&0\\
0&a\end{pmatrix}
.

Consider
ARB=0
, for all
R\in S_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, where

A=\begin{pmatrix}a&a^{\prime}\\
0&a\end{pmatrix},\ \ B=\begin{pmatrix}b&b^{\prime}\\
0&b\end{pmatrix}\ \ \text{and}\ \ R=\begin{pmatrix}r&r^{\prime}\\
0&r\end{pmatrix}
.

We have
arb=0
and
arb^{\prime}+ar^{\prime}b+bra^{\prime}=0
, for every

, whence
a=0
or
b=0
. If
a=0
, then
bra^{\prime}=0
, for every

, hence
b=0
or
a^{\prime}=0
. If
b=0

arb^{\prime}=0
, for each

, hence
a=0
or
b^{\prime}=0
. First, we consider the case
BR\sigma_{1}(A)=BRA

BR\sigma_{1}(A)=\begin{pmatrix}bra&bra^{\prime}+br^{\prime}a+b^{\prime}ra\\
0&bra\end{pmatrix}
.

From the previous observation, it follows that
BR\sigma_{1}(A)=0
. Now, consider the case
BR\sigma_{2}(A)
. A calculation shows us that

BR\sigma_{2}(A)=\begin{pmatrix}bra&-bra^{\prime}+br^{\prime}a+b^{\prime}ra\\
0&bra\end{pmatrix}
.

Again, making use of the initial observation, we can see that
BR\sigma_{2}(A)=0
.
S_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
is right
\sigma_{2}
-skew reflexive
[

, Proposition 2.11]
. Finally, we present the case

:

BR\sigma_{3}(A)=\begin{pmatrix}bra&br^{\prime}a+b^{\prime}ra\\

.

We obtain that

, since

. If

, then

. We can also observe that

The following result shows that under conditions of compatibility, the composition of left (resp. right) skew reflexive endomorphisms is left (resp. right) skew reflexive endomorphism.

Proposition 3.3
.

Composition of compatible left (resp. right) skew reflexive endomorphisms is left (resp. right) skew reflexive.

Proof.

Let
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
be compatible endomorphisms of a ring
R
and consider
a,b\in R
. Suppose that
aRb=0
, i.e.
arb=0
, for every
r\in R
. If
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
are left skew reflexive, then
\sigma_{1}(b)ra=\sigma_{2}(b)ra=0
. Hence
\sigma_{1}(\sigma_{2}(b)ra)=\sigma_{1}(\sigma_{2}(b))\sigma_{1}(ra)=\sigma_{1}(\sigma_{2}(b))ra=0
, since
\sigma_{1}
is compatible. So,
\sigma_{1}\circ\sigma_{2}
is left skew reflexive. Now, if
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
are right skew
reflexive endomorphisms, then
arb=0
implies
br\sigma_{1}(a)=br\sigma_{2}(a)=0
, whence
\sigma_{1}(br\sigma_{2}(a))=\sigma_{1}(br)\sigma_{1}(\sigma_{2}(a))=0
. Since
\sigma_{1}
is compatible,
\sigma_{1}(br)\sigma_{1}(\sigma_{2}(a))=0
implies
br\sigma_{1}(\sigma_{2}(a))=0
, and therefore
\sigma_{1}\circ\sigma_{2}
is a right skew reflexive endomorphism.
∎

we deduce the following results.

Corollary 3.4
.

Composition of a finite family of compatible left (resp. right) skew reflexive endomorphisms is a left (resp. right) skew reflexive endomorphism. If
R
is
\sigma
-compatible then

is right (resp., left)
\sigma
-skew reflexive if and only if it is right (resp., left)
\sigma^{k}
-skew reflexive, for all

. Thus, if
R
is
\sigma
-compatible then
R
is
\sigma
-skew reflexive if and only if it is
\sigma^{k}
-skew reflexive, for all
k\geq 1
.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
\Sigma=\{\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n}\}
be a compatible family of endomorphisms of

. Then
\Sigma
is a right (left) skew
reflexive family if and only if each

(left) skew reflexive endomorphism.

Proof.

Let

\sigma^{\alpha}=\sigma_{k}
, for
\alpha_{i}=0
if

and
\alpha_{k}=1

\Sigma
is a right (resp., left) skew reflexive family of endomorphisms, then for

(resp.,

is a right (resp., left) skew reflexive endomorphism. On the other hand, if each
\sigma_{k}\in\Sigma

is right (resp., left) skew reflexive, for every

(resp.,
\sigma^{\alpha}(b)Ra=0
), for every

Let

Proof.

Let

and

a_{i}Rb_{j}=0
, for each

. Since

is
\Sigma

b_{j}R\sigma^{\alpha}(a_{i})=\sigma^{\alpha}(b_{j})Ra_{i}=0

,

and every

, where

Definition 4.1

\Sigma
-skew RNP, reduced rings are

-skew RNP, for any finite family of endomorphisms

\Sigma
-skew RNP considering

Remark 4.2
.

Note that for
\Sigma=\{\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n}\}

\sigma^{\alpha}(N(R))\subseteq N(R)

Theorem 4.22
.

Let
R
be a ring with a finite family of automorphism
\Sigma
and

a multiplicatively closed subset of

consisting of central regular elements such that
\sigma^{\alpha}(S)\subseteq S
, for every
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
. Then,

is right
\Sigma
-skew RNP if and only if
S^{-1}R
is right
\Sigma_{S}
-skew RNP.

Proof.

Suppose that
R
is a right
\Sigma
-skew RNP and let
s_{1}^{-1}a,s_{2}^{-1}b\in N(S^{-1}R)
such that
(s_{1}^{-1}a)S^{-1}R(s_{2}^{-1}b)=0
, that is,
(s_{1}^{-1}a)s^{-1}r(s_{2}^{-1}b)=0
, for all
s^{-1}r\in S^{-1}R
. Notice that
N(S^{-1}R)=S^{-1}N(R)
, which implies that
a,b\in N(R)
. In addition,
(s_{1}ss_{2})^{-1}(arb)=(s_{1}^{-1}a)s^{-1}r(s_{2}^{-1}b)=0
. By definition of
S^{-1}R
, there is
c\in S
such that
c
is a central element of

and
0=(arb)c=a(rc)b
. Since
R
is right
\Sigma
-skew RNP, it follows that
b(rc)\sigma^{\alpha}(a)=0
, for all nonzero
\alpha
in
\mathbb{N}^{n}
. Therefore, we have that
(s_{2}^{-1}b)(s^{-1}r)\overline{\sigma}^{\alpha}(s_{1}^{-1}a)=(s_{2}^{-1}b)(s^{-1}r)\sigma^{\alpha}(s_{1})^{-1}\sigma^{\alpha}(a)=(s_{2}s\sigma^{\alpha}(s_{1}))^{-1}(br\sigma^{\alpha}(a))=0
, since
b(r)\sigma^{\alpha}(a)c=0
, for each nonzero
\alpha
in
\mathbb{N}^{n}
. Therefore,
S^{-1}R
is right
\Sigma_{S}
-skew RNP. Reciprocally, let
a,b\in N(R)
such that
aRb=0
, that is,
arb=0
, for all
r\in R
. We have that
(1^{-1})(arb)=(1^{-1}a)(1^{-1}r)(1^{-1}b)=0
. If
S^{-1}R
is right
\Sigma_{S}
-skew RNP, we have

. Thus,
br\sigma^{\alpha}(a)=0
, that is,
R
is right
\Sigma
-skew RNP.
∎

Weak solutions to Monge-Ampère type equations on compact Hermitian manifold with boundary
Sławomir Kołodziej and Ngoc Cuong Nguyen

Abstract
. We prove the bounded subsolution theorem for the complex Monge-Ampère type equation, with the right hand side being a positive Radon measure, on a compact Hermitian manifold with boundary.



Dedicated to the memory of Nessim Sibony

1.
Introduction

Let
(\overline{M},\omega)
be a smooth compact
n
-dimensional Hermitian manifold with the non-empty boundary
\partial M
. In
[
KN21
]
we studied weak quasi-plurisubharmonic solutions of the Dirichlet problem for the complex Monge-Ampère equation. In this paper we extend those results to the complex Monge-Ampère type equation where the right hand side depends also on the solution.

Let
\mu
be a positive Radon measure on
M=\overline{M}\setminus\partial M
. Suppose that
F(u,z):\mathbb{R}\times M\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
is a non-negative function.
Let
\varphi\in C^{0}(\partial M)
. We consider the Dirichlet problem

(1.1)

\begin{cases}u\in PSH(M,\omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\overline{M}),\\
(\omega+dd^{c}u)^{n}=F(u,z)\mu,\\
u=\varphi\quad\text{on }\partial M.\end{cases}

When
M
is a bounded strictly pseudoconvex domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
the problem for plurisubharmonic functions (
\omega
is just the zero form) was studied by Bedford and Taylor
[
BT77
]
for
\mu=dV_{2n}
the Lebesgue measure,
F\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R}\times\overline{M})
and
F^{1/n}
convex and nondecreasing in
u
. Generalizations for weak solutions were done by Cegrell and Kołodziej (
[
Ce84
]
,
[
Ko00
]
,
[
CK06
]
) with more general function
F(u,z)
and a positive Radon measures
\mu
. Classical smooth solutions were obtained in
[
CKNS85
]
.
On the product of a compact Kähler manifold and an annulus this problem, with
F\equiv 0
, is the geodesic equation on the space of Kähler potentials of the manifold. For
F(u,z)=e^{\lambda u}
with
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
one obtains the Kähler-Einstein metric equations as in Cheng and Yau
[
CY80
]
, where the authors
obtained complete Kähler-Einstein metrics, but then the potentials tend to infinity when the argument approaches the boundary.
Berman
[
Be19
]
discovered that one may use the solution of a family of Monge-Ampère type equation to approximate various envelopes of quasi-plurisubharmonic functions. This approximation process is very useful in studying regularity of global envelopes (see
[
CZ19
]
,
[
GLZ19
]
,
[
To19
]
).

On a compact Hermitian manifold with boundary, if
F(u,z)=1
and
\mu
is a positive Radon measure we obtained weak solutions of the problem under the hypothesis that a subsolutions exists,
[
KN21
]
. To deal with a general (non-Kähler) Hermitian metric
\omega
we needed to adapt a suitable comparison principle from
[
KN15
]
. Furthermore, we can no longer rely on assumption that the boundary is pseudoconvex. We bypassed this by employing the Perron envelope and the existence of a bounded subsolution. Here we use similar strategy to obtain the following result.

Theorem.
(c.f. Theorem
3.2
)
Assume that
F(u,z)
is a bounded nonnegative function which is continuous and non-decreasing in the first variable and
\mu
-measurable in the other one. Let
\mu
be a positive Radon measure which is locally dominated by Monge-Ampère measures of bounded plurisubharmonic functions. Then the Dirichlet problem (
1.1
) has a solution if and only if there is a bounded subsolution
\underline{u}\in PSH(M,\omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\overline{M})
satisfying
\lim_{z\to x}u(z)=\varphi(x)
for every
x\in\partial M
and

(\omega+dd^{c}\underline{u})^{n}\geq F(\underline{u},z)\mu\quad\text{on }M.

In many cases of interest the extra assumption on
\mu
(the local domination by Monge-Ampère measures of bounded plurisubharmonic functions) is always satisfied once the subsolution
\underline{u}
exists, for example
F(t,z)=e^{\lambda t}
with
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
or
\mu=\omega^{n}
. If
F(t,z)=e^{\lambda t}
with
\lambda>0
, then we also have the uniqueness of the solution (Corollary
3.3
).

Organization.

In Section
2
we study the problem (
1.1
) in a bounded strictly pseudoconvex domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. We give a partial generalization of a result in
[
CK06
]
and study the convergence in capacity in dimension
n=2
. In Section
3
we prove the main theorem. We also give a sketch of the proof of
Hölder continuity of solutions of the Laplace equation on compact Hermitian manifold with boundary in the appendix.

2.
Monge-Ampère type equations and stability of solutions

In this section we first study the problem (
1.1
) in the special case
M\equiv\Omega
a bounded strictly pseudoconvex domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. This will provide the construction of the lift of quasi-plurisubharmonic function in the Perron envelope method for the manifold case.

Let us recall the following version of the comparison principle for a background Hermitian metric, see
[
KN15
, Theorem 3.1]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded open set in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Fix
0<\theta<1
. Let
u,v\in PSH(\Omega,\omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
be such that
\liminf_{z\to\partial\Omega}(u-v)\geq 0
. Suppose that
-s_{0}=\sup_{\Omega}(v-u)>0
and
\omega+dd^{c}v\geq\theta\omega
in
\Omega
. Then, for any
0<s<\theta_{0}:=\min\{\frac{\theta^{n}}{16{\bf B}},|s_{0}|\}
,

\int_{\{u<v+s_{0}+s\}}(\omega+dd^{c}v)^{n}\leq\left(1+\frac{s{\bf B}}{\theta^{n}}C_{n}\right)\int_{\{u<v+s_{0}+s\}}(\omega+dd^{c}v)^{n},

where
C_{n}
is a dimensional constant and
{\bf B}>0
is a constant such that on
\overline{\Omega}
,

-{\bf B}\omega^{2}\leq 2ndd^{c}\omega\leq{\bf B}\omega^{2},\quad-{\bf B}\omega^{3}\leq 4n^{2}d\omega\wedge d^{c}\omega\leq{\bf B}\omega^{3}.

It gives a useful comparison principle for solutions of Monge-Ampère type equations. We shall use it frequently in the paper.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded open set in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Let
u,v\in PSH(\Omega,\omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
be such that
\liminf_{z\to\partial\Omega}(u-v)(z)\geq 0
. Let
F(t,z):\mathbb{R}\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
be a non-negative function which is non-decreasing in
t
and
d\mu
-measurable in
z
. Suppose
\mu\leq\nu
as measures and

(\omega+dd^{c}u)^{n}=F(u,z)\mu,\quad(\omega+dd^{c}v)^{n}=F(v,z)\nu

in
\Omega
. Then,
u\geq v
on
\Omega
.

Proof.

We may additionally assume that
\liminf_{z\to\partial\Omega}(u-v)\geq 2a>0
. The general case follows after replacing
u
by
u+2a
and letting
a\to 0
. Thus
\{u<v\}\subset\Omega^{\prime}\subset\subset\Omega
for some open set
\Omega^{\prime}
. By subtracting from
u,v
a constant
C
and replacing
F(t,z)
by
F(t+C,z)
we may also assume that
u,v\leq 0
. Arguing by contradiction, suppose that
\{u<v\}
was non-empty. Since
\Omega
is bounded, there exists a bounded, strictly plurisubharmonic function
\rho\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
such that
-C\leq\rho\leq 0
. Then one can first multiply
\rho
by small positive constant and then fix small positive constants
\theta,\tau>0
such that
the set
\{u<(1+\tau)^{\frac{1}{n}}v+\rho\}\subset\subset\Omega
is nonempty and

dd^{c}\rho\geq 2\theta\omega,\quad 1+\theta\geq(1+\tau)^{\frac{1}{n}}.

Put
\widehat{v}=(1+\tau)^{\frac{1}{n}}v+\rho
. Then,

(2.1)

\displaystyle\omega_{\widehat{v}}^{n}

\displaystyle=\left(\omega+dd^{c}\rho+dd^{c}(1+\tau)^{\frac{1}{n}}v\right)^{n}

\displaystyle\geq\left[(1+2\theta)\omega+dd^{c}(1+\tau)^{\frac{1}{n}}v\right]^{n}

\displaystyle\geq(1+\tau)\omega_{v}^{n}.

Denote by
U(s)
the set
\{u<\widehat{v}+s_{0}+s\}
where
-s_{0}=\sup_{\Omega}(\widehat{v}-u)>0
. Then, for
0<s<|s_{0}|
,

U(s)\subset\subset\Omega,\quad\sup_{U(s)}\{\widehat{v}+s_{0}+s-u\}=s.

It follows from Lemma
2.1
that for every
0<s<\min\{\frac{\theta^{n}}{16{\bf B}},|s_{0}|\}
,

0<\int_{U(s)}\omega_{\widehat{v}}^{n}\leq\left(1+\frac{s{\bf B}}{\theta^{n}}C_{n}\right)\int_{U(s)}\omega_{u}^{n},

where the first inequality holds because
\omega+dd^{c}\widehat{v}\geq\theta\omega
.
Note that
u<\widehat{v}
on
U(s)
. Hence, the monotonicity of
F
implies

\omega_{u}^{n}=F(u,z)\mu\leq F(u,z)\nu\leq F(\widehat{v},z)\nu\leq F(v,z)\nu=\omega_{v}^{n}.

Combining this and (
2.1
) we get

(1+\tau)\int_{U(s)}\omega_{v}^{n}\leq\left(1+\frac{s{\bf B}}{\theta^{n}}C_{n}\right)\int_{U(s)}\omega_{v}^{n}.

Therefore,
0<\tau\leq s{\bf B}C_{n}/\theta^{n}
, which is impossible for
s>0
small enough. Hence,
u\geq v
on
\Omega
and the proof is completed.
∎

Let
\Omega
be a bounded strictly pseudoconvex domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Let
\varphi\in C^{0}(\partial\Omega)
and
\mu
be a positive Radon measure in
\Omega
. Suppose that
F(u,z):\mathbb{R}\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
is non-negative function which is continuous and non-decreasing in the first variable and
\mu
-measurable in the other one. Consider the following Dirichlet problem

(2.2)

\begin{cases}u\in PSH(\Omega,\omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega}),\\
(\omega+dd^{c}u)^{n}=F(u,z)\mu,\\
\lim_{z\to x}u(z)=\varphi(x)\quad\text{for }x\in\Omega.\end{cases}

By Proposition
2.2
there is at most one solution to the problem. Furthermore, in the special case
\mu\equiv 0
, by
[
KN15
, Theorem 4.2]
there is a unique continuous (maximal)
\omega
-plurisubharmonic (
\omega
-psh for short) function
h
solving

(2.3)

(\omega+dd^{c}h)^{n}\equiv 0,\quad h=\varphi\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega.

We now study the existence of the solution. The Cegrell class
\mathcal{E}_{0}(\Omega)
is the set of all functions
v\in PSH(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
satisfying

\lim_{z\to\partial\Omega}v(z)=0\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{\Omega}(dd^{c}v)^{n}<+\infty.

The following generalizes
[
Ko00
, Theorem 1.1]
, where the case
\omega=0
was treated.

Theorem 2.3
.

Suppose that
F(t,z)
is a bounded non-negative function which is continuous and non-decreasing in the first variable and
\mu
-measurable in the second one. Suppose that
d\mu\leq(dd^{c}v)^{n}
for a function
v\in\mathcal{E}_{0}(\Omega)
. Then, there exists a unique bounded
\omega
-psh function in
\Omega
solving

\displaystyle(\omega+dd^{c}u)^{n}=F(u,z)d\mu,

\displaystyle\lim_{z\to x}u(z)=\varphi(x)\quad\text{for }x\in\partial\Omega.

Proof.

The proof follows the lines of
[
Ko00
, Theorem 1.1]
(see also
[
Ce84
]
), which applied Schauder’s fixed theorem. In the presence of the Hermitian background form
we need results from
[
KN21
]
to verify the hypothesis of that theorem.

We start with the following simple observation.

Lemma 2.4
.

We may assume that
\mu
has compact support in
\Omega
.

Proof.

Suppose that the problem is solvable for compactly supported measures. Let
\{\Omega_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
be an increasing exhausting sequence of open sets that are relatively compact in
\Omega
. Then we can find a sequence of functions:
u_{j}\in PSH(\Omega,\omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
with
\lim_{z\to x}u_{j}(z)=\varphi(x)
solving

(\omega+dd^{c}u_{j})^{n}=F(u_{j},z){\bf 1}_{\Omega_{j}}d\mu.

By Proposition
2.2
this is a decreasing sequence and

v+h\leq u_{j}\leq h\quad\text{on }\overline{\Omega},

where
h
is the maximal function defined in (
2.3
).
Put
u=\lim u_{j}
. By monotone convergence theorems
[
DK12
]
(see also
[
BT82
]
) we have that
\omega_{u_{j}}^{n}
converges weakly to
\omega_{u}^{n}
. Also
F(u_{j},z){\bf 1}_{\Omega_{j}}d\mu
converges weakly to
F(u,z)d\mu
when
j\to\infty
. Thus
u
satisfies the equation
\omega_{u}^{n}=F(u,z)d\mu
.
∎

By Lemma
2.4
we assume that
\mbox{supp }\mu
is compact in
\Omega
.
Since
F
is bounded, without loss of generality we may assume that
0\leq F\leq 1
, as we can rescale
\mu
and
v
by a positive constant. Let
h
be a maximal
\omega
-psh in
\Omega
as in (
2.3
). Then, the set

\mathcal{A}=\{u\in PSH(\Omega,\omega):v+h\leq u\leq h\}

is a convex and bounded set in
L^{1}(\Omega)
with respect to
L^{1}-
topology. Thus, it is a compact set. We define the map
T:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}
, where
T(u)=w
is the solution of

w\in\mathcal{A},\quad(\omega+dd^{c}w)^{n}=F(u,z)d\mu,

where this solution is uniquely determined by
[
KN21
, Theorem 3.1]
.

Next, we shall verify that
T
is continuous. Let
\{u_{j}\}
be a sequence in
\mathcal{A}
such that
u_{j}\to u
in
L^{1}(\Omega)
. Set
w=T(u)
and
w_{j}=T(u_{j})
. The continuity of
T
will follow if we have

(2.4)

\limsup w_{j}\leq w\leq\liminf w_{j}.

Since
\mathcal{A}
is compact in
L^{1}(\Omega)
we may assume that
w_{j}
converges to
w
in
L^{1}(\Omega).
In the arguments we often pass to a subsequence which does not affect the proof. We also skip the renumbering of indices of those subsequences.

We need the following result which is essentially due to Cegrell
[
Ce98
]
.

Lemma 2.5
.

There is a subsequence of
\{F(u_{j},z)\}_{j\geq 1}
that converges to
F(u,z)
in
L^{1}(d\mu)
.

Proof of Lemma
2.5
.

Since
u_{j}
are uniformly bounded we may subtract a constant and assume that
u_{j},u\leq 0
for all
j\geq 1
. Then it follows from
[
KN21
, Lemma 2.1]
that
\lim\int_{\Omega}u_{j}d\mu=\int_{\Omega}ud\mu
. Since
\mu
is dominated by capacity, it follows from
[
KN21
, Corollary 2.2]
that
u_{j}\to u
in
L^{1}(d\mu)
. Passing to a subsequence we have that
u_{j}
converges to
u
almost everywhere in
d\mu
. Since
F(t,z)
is continuous in
t
, the sequence
F(u_{j},z)
converges
\mu
-a.e to
F(u,z)
. By the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem we get the conclusion.
∎

After passing to a subsequence we may also assume that
u_{j}
converges to
u
almost everywhere in
d\mu
. Let us check the first inequality in (
2.4
). Define
\widehat{u}_{k}=\inf_{j\leq k}u_{j}
and denote by
\widehat{w}_{k}\in\mathcal{A}
the sequence of solutions to

(\omega+dd^{c}\widehat{w}_{k})^{n}=F(\widehat{u}_{k},z)d\mu.

Since
(\omega+dd^{c}\widehat{w}_{k})^{n}
is increasing, by the comparison principle
[
KN15
, Corollary 3.4]
,
\widehat{w}_{k}
decreases to
\widehat{w}\in\mathcal{A}
. As
u_{j}
converges to
u
, we have that
\widehat{u}_{k}
increases to
u
(almost everywhere in
d\mu
). Therefore,

(\omega+dd^{c}\widehat{w})^{n}=\lim_{k\to+\infty}(\omega+dd^{c}\widehat{w}_{k})^{n}=\lim_{k\to+\infty}F(\widehat{u}_{k},z)d\mu=F(u,z)d\mu,

where the first equality holds by the convergence theorem in
[
DK12
]
(see also
[
BT82
]
) and the last equality follows from Lemma
2.5
and the Lebesgue monotone convergence theorem.
By the uniqueness of the solution
w=\widehat{w}
. Note that
\widehat{w}_{k}\geq w_{k}
because
(\omega+dd^{c}w_{k})^{n}\geq(\omega+dd^{c}\widehat{w}_{k})^{n}
. Therefore,

w=\lim\widehat{w}_{k}\geq\limsup w_{k}.

Next we prove the second inequality in (
2.4
). By Hartogs’ lemma for
\omega
-psh functions,
u=(\limsup_{j\to+\infty}u_{j})^{*}
. Define
\widetilde{u}_{k}=(\sup_{j\geq k}u_{j})^{*}
and
\widetilde{w}_{k}=T(\widetilde{u}_{k})
. Since
(\omega+dd^{c}\widetilde{w}_{k})^{n}=F(\widetilde{u}_{k},z)d\mu
is decreasing, again by the comparison principle
[
KN15
]
one gets that the sequence
\widetilde{w}_{k}
is increasing to some
\widetilde{w}\in\mathcal{A}
. Note also that

(\omega+dd^{c}w_{k})^{n}=F(u_{k},z)d\mu\leq F(\widetilde{u}_{k},z)d\mu=(\omega+dd^{c}\widetilde{w}_{k})^{n}.

Hence,
\widetilde{w}_{k}\leq w_{k}
.
Furthermore,
\widetilde{u}_{k}\downarrow u
(almost everywhere in
d\mu
). By the convergence theorem in [DK12] and Lemma
2.5
we infer

(\omega+dd^{c}\widetilde{w})^{n}=\lim_{k\to+\infty}F(\widetilde{u}_{k},z)d\mu=F(u,z)d\mu.

As above by the uniqueness
w=\widetilde{w}
. Then,
w=\lim\widetilde{w}_{k}\leq\liminf w_{k}.

Thus, we conclude the continuity of
T
. The Schauder theorem says that
T
has a fixed point
T(u)=u
. This gives the existence of a solution to the Dirichlet problem.
∎

Remark 2.6
.

Thanks to the first lemma of the above proof the theorem can be extended to the case of unbounded
F
, e.g.
F(u,z)=|u|^{-\alpha}
with
\alpha>0
, in the Dirichlet problem:

\begin{cases}u\in PSH(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega}),\\
(dd^{c}u)^{n}=|u|^{-\alpha}d\mu,\\
u=0\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

Indeed, suppose that there exists a subslution
\underline{u}\in PSH(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
such that
\underline{u}=0
on
\partial\Omega
and

(dd^{c}\underline{u})^{n}\geq|\underline{u}|^{-\alpha}d\mu.
The proof above shows that for the sequence
\Omega_{j}\uparrow\Omega
in Lemma
2.4
we can find
u_{j}\in PSH(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
such that

(dd^{c}u_{j})^{n}=|u_{j}|^{-\alpha}{\bf 1}_{\Omega_{j}}d\mu,\quad u_{j}=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega.

Since
|u_{j}|^{\alpha}(dd^{c}u_{j})^{n}\leq|\underline{u}|^{\alpha}(dd^{c}\underline{u})^{n}
, by the comparison principle
\underline{u}\leq u_{j}\leq 0
. Similarly,
u_{j}
is a uniformly bounded and decreasing sequence. Therefore,
u=\lim_{j\to\infty}u_{j}
is the unique solution to the Dirichlet problem.

Furthermore one can obtain better regularity of the solution under stronger assumptions on
\mu
. Let us consider
\mu=f(z)dV_{2n}
with
f\in L^{1}(\Omega)
and let
\rho
be the strictly pluriharmonic defining function for
\Omega
. Assume that
|\rho|^{-\alpha}f\in L^{p}(\Omega)
for some
p>1
(e.g., when
0<\alpha<1
and
f\in L^{p}(\Omega)
is bounded near the boundary
\partial\Omega
.) Then, we have

|\rho|^{-\alpha}fdV_{2n}=(dd^{c}w)^{n},\quad w=0\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega

for some Hölder continuous plurisubharmonic function
w
(see
[
GKZ08
]
). Therefore,

fdV_{2n}=|\rho|^{\alpha}(dd^{c}w)^{n}\leq|w+\rho|^{\alpha}[dd^{c}(w+\rho)]^{n}.

Therefore,
\rho+w
is a Hölder continuous subsolution to the Dirichlet problem. So there is a unique bounded solution
u
. Furthermore,

|u|^{-\alpha}fdV_{2n}=(|\rho|/|u|)^{\alpha}|\rho|^{-\alpha}fdV_{2n}.

A simple use of Hölder inequality shows that the measure on the right hand side is well dominated by capacity on every compact set of
\Omega
. Thanks to
[
KN21
, Remark 4.5]
we get that
u
is continuous. Note that the Hölder continuity of
u
is proved in
[
HQ19
]
.

The above problem appeared in
[
BT77
]
and was more recently studied in
[
Cz10
]
and
[
HQ19
,
HQ21
]
.

It is well known that the convergence of
\omega
-psh functions in
L^{p}(\Omega)
does not imply the weak convergence of their associated Monge-Ampère operators.
However, under the assumption of a uniform bound of the Monge-Amère measures this is the case, see
[
CK06
, Theorem 2.2]
.
We give a (partial) corresponding stability result for Hermitian background metrics.

Theorem 2.7
.

Suppose
d\mu=(dd^{c}v)^{n}
for some
v\in\mathcal{E}_{0}(\Omega)
. Let
0\leq f_{j}\leq 1
be a sequence of
d\mu-
measurable functions such that
f_{j}d\mu
converges weakly to
fd\mu
as measures. Suppose that
u_{j}
solves

\begin{cases}u_{j}\in PSH(\Omega,\omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega}),\\
(\omega+dd^{c}u_{j})^{n}=f_{j}d\mu,\\
\lim_{z\to x}u_{j}(z)=\varphi(x)\quad\text{for }x\in\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

for a continuous function
\varphi
.

Then, for
u=\lim u_{j}
in
L^{1}(\Omega)
we have
(\omega+dd^{c}u)^{n}=fd\mu
.

Proof.

Let
h
be the maximal function defined in (
2.3
). Then by the comparison principle
[
KN15
, Corollary 3.4]
we have
h+v\leq u_{j}\leq h
. Thus the sequence
\{u_{j}\}
is uniformly bounded and this allows to apply
[
KN21
, Lemma 3.5 c)]
and conclude that there is a subsequence
\{u_{j_{s}}\}
such that

(2.5)

\lim_{j_{s}\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}|u_{j_{s}}-u|(\omega+dd^{c}u_{j_{s}})^{n}=0,

and moreover
\omega_{u_{j_{s}}}^{n}
converges weakly to
\omega_{u}^{n}=fd\mu
, see
[
KN21
, Lemma 3.6]
.
This gives the result.
∎

In this statement we would like to have also that a subsequence of
u_{j}
converges to
u
in capacity. Near the end of this section (Corollary
2.10
) we are able to do this for
n=2.

Recall that the Bedford-Taylor capacity for a Borel set
E\subset\Omega
is defined by

(2.6)

cap(E)=cap(E,\Omega)=\sup\left\{\int_{E}(dd^{c}v)^{n}:v\in PSH(\Omega),-1\leq v\leq 0\right\}.

Note that there is another (natural) capacity associated with the metric
\omega
which is equivalent to the one above (see
[
KN21
, Lemma 5.6]
).

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
\{u_{j}\}
be the sequence in Theorem
2.7
in the case of smooth
\varphi
. Then,
u_{j}
converges to
u
in capacity if and only if

(2.7)

\lim_{j\to+\infty}\int_{\Omega}|u_{j}-u|\omega_{u_{j}}^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}=0,\quad k=0,...,n.

Proof.

Suppose that
u_{j}\to u
in capacity, i.e., for a fixed
\varepsilon>0
, we have

\lim_{j\to+\infty}cap(|u_{j}-u|>\varepsilon)=0.

Let
g
be a strictly plurisubharmonic function in a neighborhood of
\overline{\Omega}
such that
dd^{c}g\geq\omega
and
g=-\varphi
on
\partial\Omega
. Denoting
\widehat{u}_{j}=u_{j}+g
, we have
\widehat{u}_{j}\to\widehat{u}=u+g
in capacity. Let
\rho
be a strictly plurisubharmonic defining function of
\Omega
such that
dd^{c}\rho\geq\omega
on
\overline{\Omega}
. By
[
KN21
, Lemma 3.3, Corollary 3.4]
,

(2.8)

\sup_{j\geq 1}\int_{\Omega}(dd^{c}\widehat{u}_{j})^{k}\wedge(dd^{c}\rho)^{n-k}\leq C

for some
C
. We can repeat the argument of
[
KN21
, Lemma 2.3]
, with the sequence
\{w_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
in the place of
\{\widehat{u}_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
, to obtain

\lim_{j\to+\infty}\int|u_{j}-u|(dd^{c}\widehat{u}_{j})^{k}\wedge(dd^{c}\rho)^{n-k}=0,

This gives the proof the necessary condition.

It remains to prove the other implication. Suppose that we have the limit (
2.7
) for all
k=0,...,n
. Note that

cap(|u_{j}-u|>\varepsilon)=cap(|\widehat{u}_{j}-\widehat{u}|>\varepsilon)\leq cap(\widehat{u}_{j}-\widehat{u}>\varepsilon)+cap(\widehat{u}-\widehat{u}_{j}>\varepsilon).

By Hartogs’ Lemma it is easy to see that
\max\{\widehat{u}_{j},\widehat{u}\}\to\widehat{u}
in capacity. Hence,

\lim_{j\to+\infty}cap(\widehat{u}_{j}-\widehat{u}>\varepsilon)=0.

The remaining term is estimated as in
[
CK06
, page 718]
via the comparison principle for plurisubharmonic functions:

\displaystyle cap(\widehat{u}_{j}<\widehat{u}-\varepsilon)\leq\left(2/\varepsilon\right)^{n}\int_{\{u_{j}<u-\varepsilon/2\}}(dd^{c}\widehat{u}_{j})^{n}

\displaystyle\leq(2/\varepsilon)^{n+1}\int_{\Omega}|u_{j}-u|(dd^{c}\widehat{u}_{j})^{n}.

Denote
\tau=dd^{c}g-\omega\leq C\omega
. Then,
dd^{c}\widehat{u}_{j}=\omega_{u_{j}}+\tau
. It follows that

\int_{\Omega}|u_{j}-u|(dd^{c}\widehat{u}_{j})^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\int_{\Omega}|u_{j}-u|\omega_{u_{j}}^{k}\wedge\tau^{n-k}

which goes to zero by the assumption. Thus, the convergence in capacity is proved by the previous inequality.
∎

Lemma 2.9
.

Suppose
n=2
. Let
u,v\in PSH(\Omega,\omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
be such that
u\leq v
in
\Omega
and
u=v
near
\partial\Omega
. Let
-1\leq\rho\leq 0
be a plurisubharmonic function in
\Omega
. Then,

\int_{\Omega}(v-u)^{3}(dd^{c}\rho)^{2}\leq 6\int_{\Omega}(v-u)\omega_{u}^{2}+C\int_{\Omega}(v-u)^{2}\omega^{2}+CE,

where
C
is a uniform constant depending only on
\Omega
and
\omega
and

E=\left(\int_{\Omega}(v-u)\omega_{u}\wedge\omega\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\Omega}(v-u)^{2}\,\omega^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Proof.

By quasi-continuity of plurisubharmonic and
\omega
-psh functions and the convergence theorems in
[
BT82
]
and
[
DK12
]
we may assume that all functions are smooth. Let us denote
h=v-u\geq 0
. Then,
dd^{c}h=\omega_{v}-\omega_{u}
and
dh\wedge d^{c}h
is a positive (1,1)-current. By integration by parts

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}h^{3}(dd^{c}\rho)^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\rho dd^{c}h^{3}\wedge dd^{c}\rho

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\rho(3h^{2}dd^{c}h+6hdh\wedge d^{c}h)\wedge dd^{c}\rho

\displaystyle\leq 3\|\rho\|_{\infty}\int_{\Omega}h^{2}\omega_{u}\wedge dd^{c}\rho.

Using the integration by parts again

\int_{\Omega}h^{2}\omega_{u}\wedge dd^{c}\rho=\int_{\Omega}\rho dd^{c}(h^{2}\omega_{u}).

Here,

\displaystyle\rho dd^{c}(h^{2}\omega_{u})

\displaystyle=\rho\left[dd^{c}h^{2}\wedge\omega_{u}+2dh^{2}\wedge d^{c}\omega+h^{2}dd^{c}\omega\right]

\displaystyle=\rho\left[2hdd^{c}h\wedge\omega_{u}+2dh\wedge d^{c}h\wedge\omega_{u}+2dh^{2}\wedge d^{c}\omega+h^{2}dd^{c}\omega\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2|\rho|h\,\omega_{u}^{2}+4\rho h\,dh\wedge d^{c}\omega+\rho h^{2}dd^{c}\omega.

because
\rho
is negative. Using
-C\omega^{2}\leq dd^{c}\omega\leq C\omega^{2}
and
-1\leq\rho\leq 0
, we have

\int_{\Omega}h^{2}\omega_{u}\wedge dd^{c}\rho\leq 2\int_{\Omega}h\omega_{u}^{2}+4\left|\int_{\Omega}\rho h\,dh\wedge d^{c}\omega\right|+C\int_{\Omega}h^{2}\omega^{2}.

Thus, to complete the proof we need to estimate the middle integral on the right hand side. In fact, using
[
Ng16
, Proposition 1.4]
we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega}\rho h\,dh\wedge d^{c}\omega\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{\Omega}dh\wedge d^{c}h\wedge\omega\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\rho|^{2}h^{2}\;\omega^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{\Omega}dh\wedge d^{c}h\wedge\omega\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\Omega}h^{2}\,\omega^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Note that
2dh\wedge d^{c}h=dd^{c}h^{2}-2hdd^{c}h\leq dd^{c}h^{2}+2h\omega_{u}
. Therefore,

2\int_{\Omega}dh\wedge d^{c}h\wedge\omega\leq\int_{\Omega}(dd^{c}h^{2}+2h\omega_{u})\wedge\omega.

By integration by parts

\int_{\Omega}dd^{c}h^{2}\wedge\omega=\int_{\Omega}h^{2}dd^{c}\omega^{2}\leq C\int_{\Omega}h^{2}\omega^{2}.

Combining these inequalities we get

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega}\rho h\,dh\wedge d^{c}\omega\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{\Omega}h^{2}\omega^{2}+h\omega_{u}\wedge\omega\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\Omega}h^{2}\,\omega^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\Omega}h^{2}\,\omega^{2}+C\left(\int_{\Omega}h\omega_{u}\wedge\omega\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\Omega}h^{2}\,\omega^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where we used an elementary inequality
(x+y)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq x^{\frac{1}{2}}+y^{\frac{1}{2}}
with
x,y\geq 0
in the second inequality.
This completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 2.10
.

If
n=2
, then there is a subsequence
\{u_{j_{s}}\}
of
\{u_{j}\}
in Theorem
2.7
that converges to
u
in capacity.

Proof.

Fix
a>0
. Let us denote
w_{s}=\max\{u_{j_{s}},u-1/s\}
. By Hartogs’ Lemma
w_{s}\to u
in capacity. Moreover,
\{|u-u_{j}|>2a\}\subset\{|u-w_{s}|>a\}\cup\{|w_{s}-u_{j_{s}}|>a\}
. Therefore, it is enough to show that

cap(|w_{s}-u_{j_{s}}|>a)\to 0

as
s\to+\infty.

By definition we have
w_{s}=\max\{u_{j_{s}},u-1/s\}\geq u_{j_{s}}
. Then, we need to show

cap(\{u_{j_{s}}<w_{s}-a\})\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad s\to+\infty.

To this end, by Lemma
2.9
,

\left(\frac{a}{2}\right)^{3}cap(\{u_{j_{s}}<w_{s}-a\})\leq\int_{\Omega}(w_{s}-u_{j_{s}})\omega_{u_{j_{s}}}^{2}+C\int_{\Omega}(w_{s}-u_{j_{s}})^{2}\omega^{2}+CE_{s},

where

E_{s}=\left(\int_{\Omega}(w_{s}-u_{j_{s}})\omega_{u_{j_{s}}}\wedge\omega\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\Omega}(w_{s}-u_{j_{s}})^{2}\,\omega^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

By the assumption
u_{j}\to u
in
L^{1}(\Omega)
(they are uniformly bounded) and (
2.5
), the first and second integrals on the right hand side go to zero as
s
goes to infinity.
Note that the first factor of
E_{s}
is uniformly bounded by (
2.8
). Hence, the last term
E_{s}
also goes to zero as
s\to+\infty
.
Therefore, the conclusion follows.
∎

Proposition
2.8
and Corollary
2.10
have their analogues on a compact Hermitian manifold without boundary. They allow, for example, to provide another proof of the following result of Guedj and Lu
[
GL21
, Proposition 3.4]
.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
(X,\omega)
be a compact
n
-dimensional Hermitian manifold (without boundary). Then for any
A>0
,

\inf\left\{\int_{X}(\omega+dd^{c}v)^{n}:v\in PSH(X,\omega),-A\leq v\leq 0\right\}>0.

Proof.

By replacing
\omega
with
\omega/A
and
v
with
v/A
we may assume that
A=1
. We argue by contradiction. Suppose that there was a sequence
\{u_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}\subset PSH(X,\omega)
such that
-1\leq u_{j}\leq 0
and

\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{X}\omega_{u_{j}}^{n}=0,\quad\sup_{X}u_{j}=0.

By passing to a subsequence we assume that
u_{j}\to u
in
L^{1}(X)
and
u_{j}\to u
a.e, where

u=(\limsup_{j\to\infty}u_{j})^{*}=\lim_{j\to\infty}(\sup_{\ell\geq j}u_{\ell})^{*}.

Then,
-1\leq u\leq 0
and
u\in PSH(X,\omega)
. We will show that there exists a subsequence
\{u_{j_{s}}\}
of
\{u_{j}\}
such that
\omega_{u_{j_{s}}}^{n}
converges weakly to
\omega_{u}^{n}
. Indeed, set

w_{j}=\{u_{j},u-1/j\}.

By the Hartogs lemma
w_{j}
converges to
u
in capacity. Therefore, by the convergence theorem in [BT82] and [DK12]

\lim_{j\to\infty}\omega_{w_{j}}^{n}=\omega_{u}^{n}.

Next, we will show that

\int_{X}|u_{j}-u|(\omega+dd^{c}u_{j})^{n}\to 0\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{X}|u_{j}-u|(\omega+dd^{c}w_{j})^{n}\to 0

as
j\to+\infty
.
The first statment holds because
\int_{X}|u_{j}-u|\omega_{u_{j}}^{n}\leq 2\int_{X}\omega_{u_{j}}^{n}\to 0
by the assumption. The second convergence also holds because
w_{j}\to u
in capacity and so the proofs of Lemma 2.1, Corollary 2.2, Lemma 2.3 in
[
KN21
]
can be applied because all considered functions are uniformly bounded and
X
is compact.

Thus, again by
[
KN21
, Lemma 3.6]
there exists a subsequence
u_{j_{s}}
such that
\omega_{u_{j_{s}}}^{n}
converges weakly to
\omega_{u}^{n}
. In particular,
\int_{X}\omega_{u}^{n}=\lim_{j_{s}\to+\infty}\int_{X}\omega_{u_{j_{s}}}^{n}=0
. Hence,
\omega_{u}^{n}\equiv 0
on
X
. This is a contradiction with the fact that the Monge-Ampère mass of a bounded
\omega
-psh function is always positive
[
KN15
, Remark 5.7]
.
∎

3.
The Dirichlet problem

In this section we solve the Dirichlet problem for Monge-Ampère type equation under the existence of a bounded subsolution.
Let
\mu
be a positive Radon measure on
M=\overline{M}\setminus\partial M
. Let
\varphi\in C^{0}(\partial M)
. Let
F(t,z):\mathbb{R}\times M\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
be a non-negative function which is non-decreasing in
t
and
d\mu
-measurable in
z
. We consider the Dirichlet problem

(3.1)

\begin{cases}u\in PSH(M,\omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\overline{M}),\\
(\omega+dd^{c}u)^{n}=F(u,z)\mu,\\
\lim_{z\to x}u(z)=\varphi(x)\quad\text{for }x\in\partial M.\end{cases}

A necessary condition to solve the Dirichlet problem is the existence of a subsolution, i.e., a function
\underline{u}\in PSH(M,\omega)
satisfying
\lim_{z\to x}u(z)=\varphi(x)
for
x\in\partial M
and

(3.2)

(\omega+dd^{c}\underline{u})^{n}\geq F(\underline{u},z)\mu.

We say that
\mu
is locally dominated by Monge-Ampère measures of bounded plurisubharmonic functions if for each
p\in M
, there exists a coordinate ball
B\subset\subset M
centered at
p
and
v\in PSH(B)\cap L^{\infty}(B)
such that

(3.3)

\mu_{|_{B}}\leq(dd^{c}v)^{n}.

By the subsolution theorem from
[
Ko95
]
on a smaller ball
B^{\prime}\subset\subset B
we can choose
v
in the Cegrell class
\mathcal{E}_{0}(B^{\prime})
, i.e.,
\lim_{z\to\partial B^{\prime}}v(z)=0
and
\int_{B^{\prime}}(dd^{c}v)^{n}<+\infty
.

Remark 3.1
.

The latter property often follows from the existence of a subsolution
\underline{u}
. For example in geometrically interesting cases: if either
F(u,z)=e^{\lambda u}
with
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, or if
\mu
is the volume form.

Our main result is as follows.

Theorem 3.2
.

Suppose that
F(t,z)
is a bounded non-negative function which is continuous and non-decreasing in the first variable and
\mu
-measurable in the second one. Let
\mu
be a positive Radon measure satisfying (
3.3
). Suppose that there exists a bounded subsolution
\underline{u}
as in (
3.2
). Then there exists a bounded solution to the Dirichlet problem (
3.1
).

Proof.

We use the Perron envelope method as in
[
KN21
, Theorem 1.2]
. Let

\mathcal{B}(\varphi,\mu)
be the set

(3.4)

\left\{w\in PSH(M,\omega)\cap L^{\infty}(M):(\omega+dd^{c}w)^{n}\geq F(w,z)\mu,w^{*}_{|_{\partial M}}\leq\varphi\right\},

where
w^{*}(x)=\limsup_{M\ni z\to x}w(z)
for every
x\in\partial M
.
Then,
\mathcal{B}(\varphi,\mu)
is non-empty as it contains
\underline{u}
. Let
u_{0}\in C^{0}(\overline{M})
be a
\omega
-subharmonic solution to

(\omega+dd^{c}u_{0})\wedge\omega^{n-1}=0,\quad u_{0}=\varphi\quad\text{on }\partial M

(see e.g. Corollary
4.1
).
By the comparison principle for
\omega
-subharmonic functions we have

(3.5)

v\leq u_{0}\quad\text{for every }v\in\mathcal{B}(\varphi,\mu).

Thus, the function

u(z)=\sup\{v(z):v\in\mathcal{B}(\varphi,\mu)\}

is well-defined.
We know that
u^{*}\in PSH(M,\omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\overline{M})
and
u=u^{*}
almost everywhere, outside a pluripolar set.
Moreover, if
v_{1},v_{2}\in\mathcal{B}(\varphi,\mu)
, then so is
\max\{v_{1},v_{2}\}
. Indeed, by an inequality of Demailly
[
De85
]
we have

\displaystyle(\omega+dd^{c}\max\{v_{1},v_{2}\})^{n}

\displaystyle\geq{\bf 1}_{\{v_{1}>v_{2}\}}(\omega+dd^{c}v_{1})^{n}+{\bf 1}_{\{v_{1}\leq v_{2}\}}(\omega+dd^{c}v_{2})^{n}

\displaystyle\geq{\bf 1}_{\{v_{1}>v_{2}\}}F(v_{1},z)\mu+{\bf 1}_{\{v_{1}\leq v_{2}\}}F(v_{2},z)\mu

\displaystyle=F(\max\{v_{1},v_{2}\},z)\mu.

By this property and Choquet’s lemma we can write
u=\lim_{j\to+\infty}u_{j}
, where
\{u_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}\subset\mathcal{B}(\varphi,\mu)
is an increasing sequence. Therefore,

\displaystyle(\omega+dd^{c}u^{*})^{n}

\displaystyle=\lim_{j\to+\infty}(\omega+dd^{c}u_{j})^{n},

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{j\to+\infty}F(u_{j},z)\mu

\displaystyle=F(u,z)\mu=F(u^{*},z)\mu,

where the last equality follows the fact that
\mu
does not charge pluripolar sets.
Thus,
u=u^{*}\in\mathcal{B}(\varphi,\mu)
is
\omega
-psh in
M
. It also follows from the definition and (
3.5
) that
\underline{u}\leq u\leq u_{0}
. Hence,
u=\varphi
is continuous on
\partial M
.

It remains to show that
\omega_{u}^{n}=F(u,z)\mu
in
M
. To see this, let
B\subset\subset M
be a coordinate ball in
M
. Following the same argument as in
[
KN21
, Lemma 3.7]
, given the local solvability of the Dirichlet problem (Theorem
2.3
),
there exists
\widetilde{u}\in\mathcal{B}(\varphi,\mu)
such that
u\leq\widetilde{u}
and
(\omega+dd^{c}\widetilde{u})^{n}=F(\widetilde{u},z)\mu
in this small coordinate ball.
By definition of
u
we must have
\widetilde{u}\leq u
. So,
\widetilde{u}=u
in
B
. In other words
\omega_{u}^{n}=F(u,z)\mu
. Since
B
is arbitrary, this proves our claim.
∎

For
F(t,z)=e^{\lambda t}
with
\lambda>0
we obtain a stronger statement.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
\lambda>0
. There exists a unique solution to the Dirichlet problem

\begin{cases}u\in PSH(M,\omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\overline{M}),\\
\omega_{u}^{n}=e^{\lambda u}\mu,\\
\lim_{z\to x}u(z)=\varphi(x)\quad\text{for }x\in\partial M.\end{cases}

if and only if there exists a bounded subsolution.

Moreover, if the subsolution is Hölder continuous, so is the solution.

Proof.

The uniqueness of the solution under the hypothesis of the existence of a subsolution follows by the same arguments as in
[
Ng16
, Lemma 2.3]
.
Next, to prove the equivalence, we only need to verify the local domination by Monge-Ampère measures of bounded plurisubharmonic functions for
\mu
(see condition (
3.3
)). This is straightforward.

For the second conclusion we first observe as in
[
KN21
, Lemma 6.5]
that
u
is Hölder continuous on the boundary
\partial M
. Since
u
is bounded,

\omega_{u}^{n}=e^{\lambda u}\mu\leq C(\omega+dd^{c}\underline{u})^{n}.

Thus, the Hölder continuity of
u
follows from the the proof of
[
KN21
, Theorem 1.4]
.
∎

Thanks to this we can also obtain the solution of the Monge-Ampère equation as the limit of the solutions of the Monge-Ampère type equations.

Corollary 3.4
.

Suppose that there exists a function
\underline{u}\in PSH(M,\omega)\cap L^{\infty}(M)
which satisfies:
\lim_{z\to x}\underline{u}(z)=\varphi(x)
for
x\in\partial M
and
(\omega+dd^{c}\underline{u})^{n}\geq\mu
in
M
. Then, the sequence of solutions

(\omega+dd^{c}u_{\lambda})^{n}=e^{\lambda u}\mu\quad\text{with }\quad\lambda>0

converges to a solution
u
of
\omega_{u}^{n}=\mu
and
u=\varphi-\sup_{M}\underline{u}
on
\partial M
as
\lambda\to 0
.

Proof.

Let
b=\sup_{M}\underline{u}
. Then,
v:=\underline{u}-b\leq 0
on
M
. For every
\lambda>0
, the function
v
satisfies
v=\varphi-b
on
\partial M
and

(\omega+dd^{c}v)^{n}\geq e^{\lambda v}\mu
in
M
.
Applying Theorem
3.2
we obtain the family of solutions
\{u_{\lambda}\}_{0<\lambda\leq 1}
of

(\omega+dd^{c}u_{\lambda})^{n}=e^{\lambda u}\mu,\quad u_{\lambda}=\varphi-b\quad\text{on }\partial M.

By the domination principle the family is increasing in
\lambda>0
and
u_{\lambda}\geq v
for every
0<\lambda\leq 1
. Set
u=\lim_{\lambda\to 0}u_{\lambda}.
Then,
u+b
is a solution to
\omega_{u}^{n}=\mu
in
M
and
u=\varphi
on
\partial M
.
∎

4.
Appendix

In the proofs in this paper as well as in
[
KN21
]
we use the existence and regularity of solutions of the linear elliptic equation
on a manifold with boundary. Those statements are known, for instance as consequences of general results for harmonic maps
[
JY93
, Theorem 6]
(see also
[
SY97
, Theorem 5.3]
). However, since the case of Hölder continuous boundary data seems not to be available in literature we include the proof here for the sake of completness.

Let
(\overline{M},\omega)
be a compact
n
-dimensional Hermitian manifold with nonempty boundary
\partial M
. Then
\overline{M}=M\cup\partial M
, where
M
is a (open) Hermitian manifold. Suppose that in local coordinate we have

\omega=\sqrt{-1}g_{i\bar{j}}(z)dz^{i}\wedge d\bar{z}^{j}.

Define
\Delta_{g}=g^{\bar{j}i}\partial_{i}\partial_{\bar{j}}
be the Laplace operator associated to
\omega
and denote by
{\rm dist}(z,w)
the distance function induced by
\omega
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\varphi\in C^{0}(\partial M)
. Then, there exists a unique continuous solution to

(\omega+dd^{c}u)\wedge\omega^{n-1}=0\quad\text{in }M,\quad u=\varphi\quad\text{on }\partial M.

Moreover, if
\varphi
is Hölder continuous on
\partial M
, then so is the solution
u
.

Since

\Delta_{g}u={\rm tr}_{\omega}u=\frac{ndd^{c}u\wedge\omega^{n-1}}{\omega^{n}},

we can separate the equation into two problems

\Delta_{g}u_{1}=0
in
M
with
u_{1}=\varphi
on
\partial M
and
\Delta_{g}u_{2}=-n
in
M
with
u_{2}=0
on
\partial M
. The latter solution
u_{2}
is smooth by the classical PDEs.

We are thus reduced to proving the following.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\varphi\in C^{0}(\partial M)
. Then, there exists a unique continuous solution to

(4.1)

\Delta_{g}u=0\quad\text{in M},\quad u=\varphi\quad\text{on }\partial M.

Moreover, if
\varphi
is Hölder continuous on
\partial M
, then so is
u
.

The existence of continuous solutions follows exactly as in
[
GT
, page 24-25]
by using the Perron envelope

\mathcal{S}_{\varphi}=\{v\in SH_{\omega}(M)\cap C^{0}(\overline{M}):v_{|_{\partial M}}\leq\varphi\},

where
SH_{\omega}(M)
is the set of all
\Delta_{g}
-subharmonic functions in
M
. The function

u(x)=\sup_{v\in S_{\varphi}}v(x)\quad\text{for }x\in\overline{M}

is the solution to (
4.1
) by the Perron method using harmonic liftings:

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
v\in S_{\varphi}
. Then there exists a function
\widetilde{v}
called a lift of
v
in
B
such that
\widetilde{v}\geq v
on
\overline{M}
and satisfying
\Delta_{g}\widetilde{v}=0
in
B
and
\widetilde{v}=v
on
\partial B
.

The boundary condition is satisfied since using the regularity of the boundary of the domain one can easily construct, as in
[
GT
]
, first the local barriers, and then the global one.

We now assume further that
\varphi
belongs to
C^{0,\alpha}(\overline{M})
with
0<\alpha<1
. Then, we wish to show that the solution also belongs to a Hölder space.

First, we will construct a Hölder continuous local barrier similarly as in
[
BT76
, Theorem 6.2]
on the coordinate half-ball at each boundary point.

Lemma 4.4
.

Suppose the origin
0\in\partial M\cap B(0,R)
,
\rho
is the defining function of
\partial M\cap B(0,R)
in the coordinate ball
B(0,R)
and

U_{R}=\{z\in B(0,R):\rho(z)\leq 0\}
is the coordinate half-ball centered at
0
. Denote
\|\varphi\|_{\alpha}=c_{1}
for the Hölder norm of
\varphi
on
\partial M
. Let
0<\tau\leq\alpha<1
. Then, there exists a constant
k=k(\varphi,U_{R})
and a neighborhood
W
of
0
such that the function

v(z)=k|\rho|^{\tau}(z)+c_{1}|z|^{\alpha}+\varphi(0)

is
\Delta_{g}
-superharmonic in
W\cap U_{R}
. Moreover,

v(0)=\varphi(0),\quad v(x)\geq\varphi(x)\quad\text{for every }x\in\partial M\cap B(0,R).

Proof.

We compute in
B(0,R)
,

dd^{c}|\rho|^{\tau}=-\tau|\rho|^{\tau-1}dd^{c}\rho-\tau(1-\tau)|\rho|^{\tau-2}d\rho\wedge d^{c}\rho,

and for
\alpha^{\prime}=\alpha/2
,

dd^{c}|z|^{2\alpha^{\prime}}=\alpha^{\prime}|z|^{2(\alpha^{\prime}-1)}dd^{c}|z|^{2}-\alpha^{\prime}(\alpha^{\prime}-1)|z|^{2(\alpha^{\prime}-2)}d|z|^{2}\wedge d^{c}|z|^{2}.

Hence,

dd^{c}v(z)\wedge\omega^{n-1}/\omega^{n}\leq-k\tau(1-\tau)|\rho|^{\tau-2}|\nabla\rho|_{g}^{2}+\frac{c_{1}\alpha}{2}|z|^{\alpha-2}.

Furthermore,
|\rho(z)|=|\rho(z)-\rho(0)|\leq c_{2}|z|
for every
z\in\overline{B(0,R)}
. Since
\tau-2<0
, it implies that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\Delta_{g}v(z)

\displaystyle\leq-c_{3}k|z|^{\tau-2}|\nabla\rho|_{g}^{2}+\frac{c_{1}\alpha}{2}|z|^{\alpha-2}

\displaystyle=|z|^{\alpha-2}\left(c_{1}\alpha/2-c_{3}k|z|^{\tau-\alpha}|\nabla\rho|_{g}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq|z|^{\alpha-2}\left(c_{1}\alpha-c_{3}kR^{\tau-\alpha}|\nabla\rho|_{g}^{2}\right),

where
c_{3}=\tau(1-\tau)c_{2}^{\tau-2}
. We used the fact
\tau\leq\alpha
for the last inequality. Since
\rho
is the defining function for
\partial M\cap B(0,R)
, it follows that

|\nabla\rho|_{g}>\varepsilon_{0}>0
in a neighborhood
W
of
0
. Then, we can choose large
k>0
, independent of the boundary point
0
, so that
\Delta_{g}v\leq 0
. Finally,
the remaining properties of
v
hold because
\rho(x)=0
for every
x\in\partial M\cap B(0,R)
.
∎

Proposition 4.5
.

There exists a function
\overline{u}(x)\in C^{0,\alpha}(\overline{M})
that is
\Delta_{g}
-superharmonic in
M
and
\overline{u}(\xi)=\varphi(\xi)
for
\xi\in\partial M
.

Proof.

We first show that at each point
\xi\in\partial M
there exist a
\Delta_{g}
-superhamonic function
v_{\xi}
in
M
such that
v_{\xi}\in C^{0,\alpha}(\overline{M})
and

(4.2)

\displaystyle v_{\xi}(x)\geq\varphi(x)\quad\text{on }\partial M,

(4.3)

\displaystyle v_{\xi}(\xi)=\varphi(\xi),

(4.4)

\displaystyle\|v_{\xi}\|_{\alpha}\leq C\|\varphi\|_{2\alpha},

where
\|\cdot\|_{\alpha}
denotes the
\alpha
-Hölder norm of the function and the constant
C
depends only on
\overline{M}
and the metric
\omega
.

Without loss of generality we may assume
\xi
is the origin and
\varphi(0)=0
.
By Lemma
4.4
for the boundary point
0\in\partial M
there exists a function
v=k|\rho|^{\alpha}+c_{1}|z|^{\alpha}
and a neighborhood
W
of
0
such that
v
satisfies (
4.2
), (
4.3
) and (
4.4
) on
W\cap U_{R}
. Note that the constants
k,c_{1}
are independent of the boundary points. We can extend this function to a global one as follows. Set
k_{1}=\sup_{\partial M}\varphi+1
. Then, for large
k_{2}\geq 1
(depending on
k_{1}
and
R
, but independent of the boundary point), the function

\widehat{v}=\min\{k_{1},k_{2}v\}

is
\Delta_{g}
-superharmonic on
M
and satisfies the list of required properties.

Now let us define

\overline{u}=\inf\{v_{\xi}:\xi\in\partial M\}.

From
|v_{\xi}(z)-v_{\xi}(w)|\leq C[{\rm dist}(z,w)]^{\alpha}
we deduce that
|\overline{u}(z)-\overline{u}(w)|\leq C[{\rm dist}(z,w)]^{\alpha}
. Thus,
\overline{u}
is Hölder continuous
\Delta_{g}
-superharmonic, and clearly
\overline{u}(\xi)=\varphi(\xi)
for every
\xi\in\partial M
. This completes the proof.
∎

By the similar argument we can find a global Hölder continuous
\Delta_{g}
-subharmonic barrier
\underline{u}\in C^{0,\alpha}(\overline{M})
such that

(4.5)

\underline{u}(\xi)=\varphi(\xi)\quad\text{for }\xi\in\partial M.

Hence, by the maximum principle,

\underline{u}\leq u\leq\overline{u}
on
\overline{M}.

Consequently, we get

(4.6)

|u(x)-u(\xi)|\leq C[{\rm dist}(x,\xi)]^{\alpha}\quad\text{for every }x\in\overline{M}\text{ and }\xi\in\partial M.

Now we are going to show the global Hölder continuity of the solution.

Lemma 4.6
.

There exists a constant
C=C(\varphi,M,\omega)
such that

(4.7)

|u(x)-u(y)|\leq C[{\rm dist}(x,y)]^{\alpha}\quad\text{for every }x,y\in\overline{M}.

Proof.

By maximum principle we get
\inf_{\partial M}\varphi\leq u\leq\sup_{\partial M}\varphi
. Denote
d_{x}={\rm dist}(x,\partial M)
and
d_{y}={\rm dist}(y,\partial M)
. Suppose that
d_{y}\leq d_{x}
, and take
x_{0},y_{0}\in\partial M
such that
{\rm dist}(x,x_{0})=d_{x}
and
{\rm dist}(y,y_{0})=d_{y}
.

Case 1:

{\rm dist}(x,y)\leq d_{x}/2
. Since
\Delta_{g}
is uniformly elliptic we have the interior Hölder estimates (see Corollary 9.24 and Lemma 8.23 in
[
GT
]
)

\|u\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(\overline{B}_{R/2})}\leq CR^{-\alpha}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})},

where
B_{R}\subset M
is a coordinate ball of small radius
0<R<1
.
Since
y\in\overline{B}_{d_{x}/2}(x)\subset B_{d_{x}}(x)\subset M
. Applying the interior inequality to
u-u(x_{0})
in
B_{d_{x}}(x)
we get

d_{x}^{\alpha}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{[{\rm dist}(x,y)]^{\alpha}}\leq C\|u-u(x_{0})\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{d_{x}}(x))}.

By (
4.6
) the right hand side is less than
Cd_{x}^{\alpha}
. It follows that

|u(x)-u(y)|\leq C[{\rm dist}(x,y)]^{\alpha}.

Case 2:

d_{y}\leq d_{x}\leq 2\,{\rm dist}(x,y)
. Then,

\displaystyle|u(x)-u(y)|

\displaystyle\leq|u(x)-u(x_{0})|+|u(x_{0})-u(y_{0})|+|u(y)-u(y_{0})|

\displaystyle\leq C(d_{x}^{\alpha}+[{\rm dist}(x_{0},y_{0})]^{\alpha}+d_{y}^{\alpha})

\displaystyle\leq C[{\rm dist}(x,y)]^{\alpha}

since
{\rm dist}(x_{0},y_{0})\leq d_{x}+{\rm dist}(x,y)+d_{y}\leq 5\,{\rm dist}(x,y)
.
∎

Abstract
For short-packet, low-latency communications over random access channels, piloting overhead significantly reduces spectral efficiency. Therefore, pilotless systems recently gained attraction. While blind phase estimation algorithms such as VVPE ( VVPE ) can correct a phase offset using only payload symbols, a phase ambiguity remains. We first show that the remaining phase rotations in a polar coded QAM ( QAM ) transmission with gray labeling are combinations of bit-flips and automorphisms. Therefore, the decoder is equivariant to such phase rotations and, by smartly selecting the frozen bits, one can jointly decode and resolve the phase ambiguity, without the need for pilot symbols or an outer code. Our proposed system outperforms pilot-assisted transmissions by up to 0.8 dB and 2 dB for QPSK ( QPSK ) and 16- QAM , respectively.

I
Introduction

Recent trends in wireless networks, such as
URLLC
(
URLLC
) and
mMTC
(
mMTC
), create an urgent need for the robust and efficient transmission of short packets. These scenarios typically involve a random access channel and due to the sporadic transmissions the receiver must re-estimate the channel for every transmission. Conventional systems rely on dedicated pilot symbols in the packet for this purpose, which is called
PAT
(
PAT
). However, pilot symbols attribute a non-negligible overhead in the short-package systems, reducing the spectral efficiency.

Consequently, systems with smaller or no piloting overhead (also called “non-coherent communication”) received increased attention.
A common approach is a two-stage estimation process.
The first stage is a
blind
channel estimator that operates on all received symbols and is not aware which data symbols have been transmitted.
The second stage is the resolution of the remaining phase ambiguity using a few pilot symbols or the error-correcting code.
For instance, in
[
1
]
, an
LDPC
(
LDPC
) code is used to resolve the phase ambiguities. In
[
2
]
, the authors propose to use polar codes with an outer
CRC
(
CRC
) code for this purpose. Similarly, in
[
3
]
, a polar codes were proposed for joint decoding and channel estimation of a fading channel.

In this work, we consider joint decoding and phase estimation by exploiting the symmetries of polar codes. In contrast to
[
2
]
, our approach poses little constraints on the polar code and does not require an outer code. Moreover, any polar decoder can be used, e.g.,
a single low-complexity
SC
(
SC
) decoder. However, a list decoder together with a
CRC
can still be used to enhance the performance.

Polar code symmetries, namely their affine automorphism group
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
have created interest due to applications in cryptoanalysis
[
7
]
and low-latency permutation-aided decoder architectures
[
5
,
8
]
. Moreover, in
[
9
]
, an automorphism corresponding to a quasi-cyclic shift by
N/4
has been proposed to simplify the detection in a broadcast scenario.

II-B

Polar Codes

Polar codes are based on the principle of channel polarization
[
10
]
. For a polar code of length
N=2^{n}
, the Plotkin transformation is applied
n
times to obtain
N
polarized
synthetic
channels.
K
polarized “reliable” channels are designated as the information set
\mathcal{I}
carrying the information, while the remaining
N-K
“unreliable” channels are frozen bits
\mathcal{F}
and fixed to 0. Hence, the generator matrix is
\mathbf{G}
are those rows of
\mathbf{G}_{N}=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right]^{\otimes n}
corresponding to
\mathcal{I}
, where
(\cdot)^{\otimes n}
denotes the
n
-th Kronecker power.
A partial order on the reliability of the synthetic channels induces the affine automorphism group of the code
[
4
,
11
]
, i.e., the set of codeword bit permutations that map the code onto itself.
A common decoder for polar codes is the
SC
decoder
[
10
]
, where the information bits are decoded sequentially in natural order. An extension is
SCL
(
SCL
) decoding that keeps a list of the
L
most promising decoding paths
[
12
]
.

II-C

Blind Phase Estimation

II-C
1
Maximum Likelihood

Assuming
N_{\mathrm{c}}
independent, uniformly distributed transmitted symbols, the log-likelihood function
[
13
]
of the phase angle
\phi
is

\ell(\phi)=\sum_{i=0}^{N_{\mathrm{c}}-1}\mathop{\mathrm{ln}}\left(\sum_{x\in\mathcal{K}}\exp{\left(-\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}}{|y_{i}-x\mathrm{e}^{j\phi}|}^{2}\right)}\right).

(3)

Consequently, the
ML
(
ML
) estimate of
\phi
is

\hat{\phi}=\arg\max_{\phi\in[0,2\pi)}\ell(\phi).

(4)

If the constellation exhibits a rotational symmetry, the estimation can only be unique up to this symmetry.
In case of the fourfold-symmetric
QPSK
and 16-
QAM
, the phase decomposes into
\phi=\phi_{\mathrm{i}}+\phi_{\mathrm{f}}
with the integer part
\phi_{\mathrm{i}}=m\frac{\pi}{2},\,m\in\{0,1,2,3\}
and the fractional (fine) phase shift
\phi_{\mathrm{f}}\in[0,\nicefrac{{\pi}}{{2}})
. Only
\phi_{\mathrm{f}}
can be estimated blindly, while
\phi_{\mathrm{i}}
has to be recovered by different methods.

II-C
2
Viterbi–Viterbi Algorithm

In
[
14
]
, the calculation of a phase offset of an
M
-
PSK
was presented.
The
VVPE
takes advantage of the inherent symmetry of the
PSK
constellation.
Independent of the transmitted symbols, multiplying the phase angle by
M
rotates all symbols onto the same point, directly yielding an estimate of
M\phi
.
The blind fine estimation can be calculated to be

\hat{\phi}_{\mathrm{f}}=\frac{1}{M}\operatorname{arctan}\left(\frac{\sum_{i=0}^{N_{\mathrm{c}}-1}\operatorname{Im}\left(\frac{y_{i}^{M}}{\lvert y_{i}\rvert^{M-1}}\right)}{\sum_{i=0}^{N_{\mathrm{c}}-1}\operatorname{Re}\left(\frac{y_{i}^{M}}{\lvert y_{i}\rvert^{M-1}}\right)}\right).

(5)

However, multiplying the phase by
M
, along with the final operation of dividing the
\arctan
function by
M
, gives rise to an
M
-fold ambiguity in the phase estimate
[
14
]
.

II-C
3
Rice–Rice–Cowley Algorithm

To estimate the phase offset of higher
QAM
modulations, the blind estimation scheme needs to be adapted.
In
[
15
]
, the authors treat an 16-
QAM
as a combination of three individual
QPSK
in order to perform the phase estimation.
We denote this estimation scheme as
RRC
(
RRC
) estimation. In a first step, the received symbols are classified into the three rings by their amplitude.
The phases of the eight symbols on the innermost and outermost ring are jointly estimated, as they stem from the same (scaled)
QPSK
constellation with the same phase offset.
Using this first estimate, the remaining eight symbols on the middle circle are partitioned into two sub-constellations whose offsets are again estimated by
VVPE
. With this additional information, the initial phase estimation can be improved.

III
Phase-Equivariant Polar Coded Modulation

III-A

Symmetries of Polar-Coded Modulation

Lemma 1 (2-Cyclic Polar Codes):

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a polar code with information set
\mathcal{I}
such that
i|1\in\mathcal{I}\;\forall i\in\mathcal{I}
, where “
|
” denotes the
bitwise or
operation. Then for all
\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}
, also
\mathbf{c}^{\prime}=[c_{(i+1)\operatorname{mod}2}]_{i}\in\mathcal{C}
. In other words, if for each even information bit index
i
also
i+1
corresponds to an information bit, then the code is quasi-cyclic with block-size 2, i.e., the permutation

\tau=(1,0,3,2,5,4,\dots,N-1,N-2)

(6)

is an automorphism of
\mathcal{C}
. We call such a polar code
2-cyclic
. Note that all polar codes constructed for
DMC
(
DMC
) exhibit this property, as the synthetic channel
i+1
is always more reliable than
i
, for even
i

[
10
]
.

Proof.
The statement is a relaxed version of
[
4
, Thm. 2]
for weakly decreasing codes. Intuitively,
\tau
affects solely the very first stage of the polar factor graph, so only the
N=2
component codes have to be regarded. The swap of the two coded bits is an automorphism, if each component code is either rate-0, rate-1 or a repetition code, i.e.,
\mathcal{I}_{N-2}\in\{\varnothing,\{1\},\{0,1\}\}
.
∎

Furthermore, we know that an automorphism on the codeword bits (represented by the
N\times N
permutation matrix
\mathbf{T}
) corresponds to a linear transformation
\mathbf{S}
of the message bits
[
16
, Ch. 8, §5, Lemma 12]

\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{G}=\mathbf{c}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{S}\cdot\mathbf{G}=\mathbf{c}\cdot\mathbf{T}.

(7)

For polar codes and the 2-cyclic shift
\tau
, we find this transformation matrix as
[
17
]

\tilde{\mathbf{S}}=\mathbf{G}_{N}\mathbf{T}\mathbf{G}_{N}=\left[\begin{matrix}1&1\\
0&1\end{matrix}\right]\otimes\mathbf{I}_{N/2}.

(8)

Note that in this case
\tilde{\mathbf{S}}
is also of dimension
N\times N
and includes also the frozen bits.
\mathbf{S}
is obtained by selecting only the rows and columns corresponding to
\mathcal{I}
.

Theorem 1 (Rotational Symmetry):
Let
\mathcal{C}
be a 2-cyclic polar code with
N\geq 4
and information set
\mathcal{I}
. Furthermore, let
\mathcal{X}=\{\mathbf{x}=\operatorname{map}(\mathbf{c}),\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}\}
be the set of all complex transmit signals under one of the mappings in Fig.
1
and let
\mathcal{R}=\{N-2,N-1\}
for
QPSK
and
\mathcal{R}=\{N-4,N-3\}
for 16-
QAM
. Then

\mathbf{x}\mathrm{e}^{-j\frac{\pi}{2}}\in\mathcal{X}\quad\forall\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X},

(9)

In other words, a mapped codeword is transformed to another mapped codeword under the rotation
\mathrm{e}^{-j\frac{\pi}{2}}
.

Proof.

For
QPSK
, the rotation of
\pi/2
corresponds to swapping adjacent bits and then flipping every other bit, i.e.,

(c_{0},c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},\dots)\mapsto(\bar{c}_{1},c_{0},\bar{c}_{3},c_{2},\dots).

(10)

This is a combination of the addition of row
N-2
of
\mathbf{G}_{N}
and the 2-cyclic automorphism
\tau
from Lemma 1. From the requirements of Lemma 1, also
N-1
must be in
\mathcal{I}
.
For 16-
QAM
with the labeling from Fig.
1(b)
, the
\pi/2
-rotation implies

(c_{0},c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},\dots)\mapsto(\bar{c}_{1},c_{0},c_{3},c_{2},\dots),

(11)

corresponding to
\tau
from Lemma 1 followed by the addition of row
N-4
of
\mathbf{G}_{N}
. Again, then also
N-3
must be in
\mathcal{I}
.
∎

Remark: A similar statement holds for the rotation by
\pi
in case of the
BPSK
(
BPSK
). Here, observe that this corresponds to flipping all bits, i.e., the addition of the all-one codeword. This codeword is the last row of
\mathbf{G}_{N}
and thus part of the code if
N-1\in\mathcal{I}
.

V
Conclusion

In this paper, we presented a joint estimation and decoding framework for channels with phase offset. The system works by exploiting phase-equivariant symmetries of polar-coded transmission with
QPSK
and 16-
QAM
. An extension for list and ensemble decoding has been proposed, significantly outperforming
PAT
in
BLER
performance.

While this work considered only single-shot processing, extensions to iterative estimation and decoding (e.g., using soft-in/soft-out decoders) may also profit from the phase-equivariant properties. Additionally, future work may focus on other channels, such as block fading channels or multipath channels. Lastly, different outer codes can be considered that respect the 2-cyclic polar code automorphism (similar to
[
23
]
) and thus, may be checked before resolving the phase ambiguities to reduce implementation complexity.

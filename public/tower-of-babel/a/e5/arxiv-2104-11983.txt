Let

2.
Preliminaries

Next we are going to give the different definitions of solutions that we will use throughout the paper. For the definitions, with
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we consider a function
F:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathcal{S}(n)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, where
\mathcal{S}(n)
denotes the set of
n\times n
real symmetric matrices, and the equation

(8)

F(x,u(x),Du,D^{2}u)=f(x),\quad\mbox{ in }\Omega.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
n\leq q
and
f\in L_{loc}^{q}(\Omega)
. A function
u\in C(\Omega)
is an
L^{q}
-viscosity subsolution (supersolution) of (
8
) if for all
\varphi\in W_{loc}^{2,q}(\Omega)
, and
\hat{x}\in\Omega
at which
u-\varphi
has a local maximum (respectively minimum) we have

\begin{cases}\mathrm{ess}\,\displaystyle\liminf_{x\rightarrow\hat{x}}(F(x,u(x),D\varphi(x),D^{2}\varphi(x))-f(x))\leq 0\\
(\mathrm{ess}\,\displaystyle\limsup_{x\rightarrow\hat{x}}(F(x,u(x),D\varphi(x),D^{2}\varphi(x))-f(x))\geq 0).\end{cases}

In addition,
u
is an
L^{q}
-viscosity solution of (
8
) if it is both an
L^{q}
-viscosity subsolution and an
L^{q}
-viscosity supersolution.

This notion of solution was studied by Caffarelli, Crandall, Kocan and Swiech in
[
13
]
, and rightly related with the notion of
L^{q}
-strong solutions. The use of
L^{q}-
viscosity solutions falls in that not differentiability is required for the test functions, either the continuity of
F
and
f
at
x
, therefore, pointwise relations are not required. If the functions
F
and
f
are continuous, and the test function
\varphi
is twice continuously differentiable we employ the notion of classical viscosity solution that we define next.

Definition 2.2
.

A function
u\in C(\Omega)
is a viscosity subsolution (supersolution) of (
8
) if, for any function
\varphi\in C(\Omega)\cap C^{2}(\Omega)
, if
\hat{x}
is a global maximum (minimum) point of
u-\varphi
, then

\begin{cases}F(\hat{x},u(\hat{x}),D\varphi(\hat{x}),D^{2}\varphi(\hat{x}))\leq f(\hat{x})\\
(F(\hat{x},u(\hat{x}),D\varphi(\hat{x}),D^{2}\varphi(\hat{x}))\geq f(\hat{x})).\end{cases}

In addition,
u
is a viscosity solution of (
8
) if it is both a viscosity subsolution and a viscosity supersolution.

The use of these types of solutions allows establish results for uniqueness, existence and stability for a wide class of equations. The notion of viscosity solutions was introduced by Crandall and Lions in
[
16
]
for first-order equations of Hamilton-Jacobi type. A second-order extension was introduced by Lions in
[
27
]
for Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman equations, where he showed uniqueness results for viscosity solutions of these type of problems.

We use that the Pucci operators are extremal, and can be written by

(9)

\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+}(N)&=\displaystyle\sup_{A\in\mathcal{A}_{\lambda,\Lambda}}tr(AN),\\
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{-}(N)&=\displaystyle\inf_{A\in\mathcal{A}_{\lambda,\Lambda}}tr(AN),\end{array}

where
\mathcal{A}_{\lambda,\Lambda}
denotes the set of all symmetric matrix whose eigenvalues lie in the interval
[\lambda,\Lambda]
. With these definitions, it is easy to see that the Pucci operators
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{\pm}
have the following properties: for any
\alpha\geq 0
and for any
n\times n
symmetric matrices
M,N
we have

(10)

\begin{cases}\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{\pm}(\alpha M)=\alpha\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{\pm}(M),\\
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{-}(M)\leq\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{\pm}(M+N)-\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{\pm}(N)\leq\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+}(M).\end{cases}

(For these and other properties of Pucci operators see
[
12
]
).

3.
Proof of main results and classification solution

In this section we prove our main results.
We first start this section with the classification results that can be deduce from our main theorems.

As a consequence of Theorem

we have that any solution of (

) is given by

i)

For

,

. If the operator is
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{-}
, the constant
\Lambda
in the solution must be replaced by

.

ii)

For

.

As a consequence of Theorem

, in this case we have that any solution of (

\displaystyle+\dfrac{\beta(1+\beta)\xi^{\beta}}{d(x)}\sqrt{1+t}\,\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+}(-\sqrt{1+t}C-(\sqrt{1+t}-2)C^{T})

\displaystyle+\dfrac{\gamma(L+(1+\beta)\xi^{\beta})}{d(y)}(\sqrt{1+t}-1)\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+}(-D-D^{T})

\displaystyle+\dfrac{\beta(1+\beta)\xi^{\beta+1}}{d(x)}(1+t)\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+}(Dd(x)\otimes Dd(x))

\displaystyle+\dfrac{\gamma(\gamma+1)|x-y|}{d(y)^{2}}(L+\xi^{\beta})\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+}(Dd(y)\otimes Dd(y))

\displaystyle+\dfrac{\beta\gamma\xi^{\beta+1}}{d(y)}\sqrt{1+t}\left[\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+}(Dd(x)\otimes Dd(y))+\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+}(Dd(y)\otimes Dd(x))\right]

B,T,C
and

,

and
D=\widehat{x-y}\otimes Dd(y)
, with
\otimes
the Kronecker product between two vectors. Using the fact that for any vectors

the only non trivial eigenvalue from the matrix

is
v_{1}\cdot v_{2}
and property in (

), the next estimation is valid:

\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+}(-\sqrt{1+t}C-(\sqrt{1+t}-2)C^{T})=-2(\sqrt{1+t}-1)\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{-}(C),

and since
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{-}(C)=\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{-}(\widehat{x-y}\otimes Dd(x))=C_{\lambda,\Lambda}(\widehat{x-y})\cdot Dd(x)
, we have

\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+}(-\sqrt{1+t}C-(\sqrt{1+t}-1)C^{T})\leq-2C_{\lambda,\Lambda}(\sqrt{1+t}-1)(\widehat{x-y})\cdot Dd(x).

On the other hand:

\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+}(-D-D^{T})\leq-\left[\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{-}(D)+\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{-}(D^{T})\right]=-2\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{-}(D),

and since
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{-}(D)=\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{-}(\widehat{x-y}\otimes Dd(y))=C_{\lambda,\Lambda}(\widehat{x-y})\cdot Dd(y)
we have

\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+}(-D-D^{T})\leq-2C_{\lambda,\Lambda}(\widehat{x-y})\cdot Dd(y).

Now observe that for all
t>0
we have

\begin{cases}(2-2\sqrt{1+t}+t)\leq t^{2}\leq t^{2}+|x-y|^{2},\\
2\sqrt{1+t}(\sqrt{1+t}-1)\leq 2t\leq 2t+|x-y|,\end{cases}

and we obtain then,

(51)

\displaystyle\quad\operatorname{tr}(\mathbf{A_{t}}D^{2}\Phi)

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{ck}{d(y)^{\gamma}}\left\{\dfrac{(L+\xi^{\beta})}{|x-y|}(t^{2}+|x-y|^{2})+\left(\dfrac{\xi^{\beta}}{d(x)}+\dfrac{1+\xi^{\beta}}{d(y)}\right)(t+|x-y|)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\xi^{\beta+1}\left(\dfrac{1}{d(x)}+\dfrac{1}{d(y)}\right)t+\left(\dfrac{\xi^{\beta+1}}{d(x)}+\dfrac{\xi^{\beta+1}}{d(y)}+\dfrac{(1+\xi^{\beta})|x-y|}{d(y)^{2}}\right)\right\}.

We also have the estimation:

\dfrac{\xi^{\beta}}{d(x)}\leq 2\dfrac{(1+\xi^{\beta+1})}{d(y)},

and with this, we obtain

\dfrac{\xi^{\beta+1}}{d(x)}+\dfrac{\xi^{\beta+1}}{d(y)}+\dfrac{(1+\xi^{\beta})|x-y|}{d(y)^{2}}\leq c|x-y|\dfrac{(1+\xi^{\beta+2})}{d(y)^{2}},

and

\left(\dfrac{\xi^{\beta}}{d(x)}+\dfrac{1+\xi^{\beta}}{d(y)}\right)(t+|x-y|)+\xi^{\beta+1}\left(\dfrac{1}{d(x)}+\dfrac{1}{d(y)}\right)t\leq c\dfrac{(1+\xi^{\beta+1})}{d(y)}|x-y|+ct\dfrac{(1+\xi^{\beta+2})}{d(y)}.

Using this in (
51
) and by (

\displaystyle\gamma_{0}t|D_{x}\Phi|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq g(-D_{y}\Phi)-g(D_{x}\Phi)+(1+t)f(x)-f(y)+c\dfrac{k}{d(y)^{\gamma}}\left[\dfrac{(L+\xi^{\beta})}{|x-y|}(t^{2}+|x-y|^{2})\right.

\displaystyle\left.+t\dfrac{(1+\xi^{\beta+2})}{d(y)}+\dfrac{(1+\xi^{\beta+1})}{d(y)}|x-y|+\dfrac{(1+\xi^{\beta+2})}{d(y)^{2}}|x-y|\right].

Considering now

and

we have
\gamma p\geq\gamma+1
and
(\beta+1)p\geq\beta+2
, and by estimation of

):

|D_{x}\Phi|^{p}\geq ck^{p}\dfrac{(1+\xi^{\beta+1})^{p}}{d(y)^{\gamma p}}\geq ck^{p}\dfrac{(1+\xi^{\beta+2})}{d(y)^{\gamma+1}}.

Since
p>1

ctk\dfrac{(1+\xi^{\beta+2})}{d(y)^{\gamma+1}}\leq ctk^{p}\dfrac{(1+\xi^{\beta+2})}{d(y)^{\gamma+1}}\leq\dfrac{t}{2}|D_{x}\Phi|^{p},

and therefore:

\displaystyle\dfrac{t}{2}|D_{x}\Phi|^{p}-ckt^{2}\dfrac{(L+\xi^{\beta})}{|x-y|d(y)^{\gamma}}

\displaystyle\leq tf(x)+g(-D_{y}\Phi)-g(D_{x}\Phi)+f(x)-f(y)

\displaystyle+ck\dfrac{|x-y|}{d(y)^{\gamma}}\left[(1+\xi^{\beta})+\dfrac{1+\xi^{\beta+1}}{d(y)}+\dfrac{1+\xi^{\beta+2}}{d(y)^{2}}\right].

Now we choose optimal

to maximize the left side:

(52)

t=c\dfrac{|D_{x}\Phi|^{p}}{k\frac{(L+\xi^{\beta})}{|x-y|d(y)^{\gamma}}}=c\dfrac{|D_{x}\Phi|^{p}}{k}\cdot\dfrac{|x-y|}{\varphi}.

Then

\displaystyle\dfrac{|D_{x}\Phi|^{2p}|x-y|}{k\varphi}

\displaystyle\leq c\left\{\dfrac{|D_{x}\Phi|^{p}}{k}\cdot\dfrac{|x-y|}{\varphi}\|f\|+g(-D_{y}\Phi)-g(D_{x}\Phi)+f(x)-f(y)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+k|x-y|\left[\varphi+\dfrac{1+\xi^{\beta+1}}{d(y)^{\gamma+1}}+\dfrac{1+\xi^{\beta+2}}{d(y)^{\gamma+2}}\right]\right\},

and therefore:

(53)

|D_{x}\Phi|^{2p}\leq c\left\{|D_{x}\Phi|^{p}\|f\|+k\dfrac{\varphi}{|x-y|}\left[g(-D_{y}\Phi)-g(D_{x}\Phi)+f(x)-f(y)\right]+k^{2}\varphi\left[\varphi+\dfrac{1+\xi^{\beta+2}}{d(y)^{\gamma+2}}\right]\right\}.

On the estimation of the term

, using hypothesis in (

Using this in (

\displaystyle|D_{x}\Phi|^{2p}

\displaystyle\leq c\left\{|D_{x}\Phi|^{p}\|f\|+k\varphi+k^{2}\varphi^{2}+\dfrac{|D_{x}\Phi|^{2p}}{k^{p-1}}+\dfrac{|D_{x}\Phi|^{2p}}{k^{2(p-1)}}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq c\left\{|D_{x}\Phi|^{p}+|D_{x}\Phi|+|D_{x}\Phi|^{2}+\dfrac{|D_{x}\Phi|^{2p}}{k^{p-1}}+\dfrac{|D_{x}\Phi|^{2p}}{k^{2(p-1)}}\right\},

Second case:
In order to have a Bernstein estimation of the solution in
\Omega
, we do the following rescale. Let

a differentiability point for

, and let

, we define

For this function we have

and by choose from

|Dv(x)|\leq\dfrac{C}{d_{\partial B}(x)^{\frac{p}{p-1}}},

and evaluating at

|Du(x_{0})|\leq\dfrac{C}{r^{\frac{1}{p-1}}}=\dfrac{C}{d_{\partial\Omega}(x_{0})^{\frac{1}{p-1}}},

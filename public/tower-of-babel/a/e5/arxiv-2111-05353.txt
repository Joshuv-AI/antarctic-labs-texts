On the validity of the guiding-center approximation in a magnetic dipole field
Alain J. Brizard 1,a and Danielle G. Markowski 1,2

Abstract
The problem of the charged-particle motion in an axisymmetric magnetic-dipole geometry is used to assess the validity of Hamiltonian guiding-center theory, which includes higher-order corrections associated with guiding-center polarization induced by magnetic-field nonuniformity. When a magnetically-confined charged-particle orbit is regular (i.e., its guiding-center magnetic moment is adiabatically invariant), the guiding-center approximation, which conserves both energy and azimuthal canonical angular momentum, is shown to be faithful to the particle orbit when guiding-center polarization effects are taken into account.



I
Introduction

Hamiltonian guiding-center theory
Littlejohn_1983
;
Cary_Brizard_2009
yields a reduced dynamical description in which the fast gyromotion of a charged particle about a local magnetic field line is asymptotically decoupled from the slow bounce and drift motions along and across magnetic-field lines. In particular, higher-order guiding-center corrections
Burby_2013
;
Tronko_Brizard_2015
have been shown to play an important role in the faithful representation of regular charged-particle orbits in nonuniform magnetic fields. For example, the validity of the guiding-center approximation was recently explored in a straight magnetic field with constant gradient
Brizard_2017
. There, it was shown that the guiding-center approximation remains valid even in the presence of strong gradients. See Ref.
Kabin_2021
for a recent discussion of the adiabatic invariance of the magnetic moment in a straight magnetic field with constant gradient.

Here, we investigate how a truncated guiding-center expression for an exact particle dynamical invariant can accurately represent this particle invariant. In previous work, the higher-order guiding-center corrections have been shown to be relevant for magnetically-confined energetic charged-particle orbits in realistic axisymmetric magnetic-field geometries
Belova_2003
, where the faithful guiding-center representation of the toroidal angular momentum for these energetic particles required higher-order corrections (up to first order in magnetic-field nonuniformity).

The purpose of our present work is to explore how higher-order Hamiltonian guiding-center theory is faithful to charged single-particle dynamics in an axisymmetric magnetic dipole field, in which the azimuthal canonical angular momentum is an exact invariant. In particular, we compare the faithfulness of the truncated expressions of the guiding-center azimuthal angular momentum derived in the original work of Littlejohn
Littlejohn_1983
and the recent work of Tronko and Brizard
Tronko_Brizard_2015
, which includes effects due to guiding-center polarization
Kaufman_1986
;
Brizard_2013
.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. In Sec.
II
, we present a brief description of the generic axisymmetric magnetic-dipole geometry considered in this work. While the magnetic dipole field considered here is a generic dipole field, we use the example of the Earth’s geomagnetic dipole field
Parks_2003
whenever we need explicit field parameters. In Sec.
III
, we present the numerical solutions of the equations of motion for a charged particle (with mass
m
and charge
q
) moving in an axisymmetric magnetic dipole field. These solutions satisfy the conservation laws of energy and azimuthal angular canonical momentum exactly. In addition, when the magnetic moment
\mu
is a valid adiabatic invariant, the particle orbits are labeled by the invariants
({\cal E},P_{\varphi},\mu)
and they exhibit the expected three orbital time scales involving the fast gyromotion, the intermediate bounce motion, and the slow azimuthal drift-precession motion. In Sec.
IV
, we present the guiding-center equations of motion expressed as Euler-Lagrange equations derived from a Lagrangian that contains higher-order guiding-center corrections. These equations conserve guiding-center energy and guiding-center azimuthal angular canonical momentum exactly.

In Sec.
V
, the validity of the guiding-center approximation is explored in axisymmetric magnetic dipole geometry. In particular, we compare the standard guiding-center correction
\mu\,({\bf R}+\frac{1}{2}\,\tau\,\widehat{{\sf b}})
, first derived by Littlejohn
Littlejohn_1983
, where
{\bf R}
denotes the gyrogauge vector field and the magnetic twist
\tau\equiv\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}
is defined in terms of the magnetic-field unit vector field
\widehat{{\sf b}}
, with the guiding-center correction
\mu\,({\bf R}+\frac{1}{2}\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}})
, which was derived more recently
Tronko_Brizard_2015
by requiring that the guiding-center transformation yield the correct expression for the guiding-center polarization
Kaufman_1986
. It will be shown that, in magnetic-dipole geometry (where the magnetic twist vanishes,
\tau\equiv 0
), the guiding-center correction
\mu\,({\bf R}+\frac{1}{2}\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}})
yields a guiding-center description that is faithful to the exact particle dynamics.

II
Magnetic Dipole Geometry

We begin by considering the problem of charged-particle motion in axisymmetric magnetic dipole geometry, where the magnetic field is represented by the generic expression

{\bf B}\;=\;\frac{B_{\rm e}\,r_{\rm e}^{3}}{r^{3}}\left(2\,\sin\lambda\;\widehat{r}\;-\;\cos\lambda\,\widehat{\lambda}\right).

(1)

Here,
B_{\rm e}
denotes the strength of the equatorial magnetic field at
(r,\lambda)=(r_{\rm e},0)
, and modified spherical coordinates
(r,\varphi,\lambda)
are used (with the latitude angle
-\pi/2\leq\lambda\leq\pi/2
used instead of the polar angle
\theta=\pi/2-\lambda
), with the unit vectors
(\widehat{r},\widehat{\lambda}=\partial\widehat{r}/\partial\lambda\equiv\widehat{r}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{\varphi})
on the poloidal plane:

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\widehat{r}&=&\cos\lambda\left(\cos\varphi\;\widehat{\sf x}+\sin\varphi\;\widehat{\sf y}\right)\;+\;\sin\lambda\;\widehat{\sf z},\\
\widehat{\lambda}&=&-\;\sin\lambda\left(\cos\varphi\;\widehat{\sf x}+\sin\varphi\;\widehat{\sf y}\right)\;+\;\cos\lambda\;\widehat{\sf z}.\end{array}

(2)

The magnitude of the magnetic field (
1
) is expressed as

B(r,\lambda)\;=\;\frac{B_{\rm e}\,r_{\rm e}^{3}}{r^{3}}\;\sqrt{1+3\,\sin^{2}\lambda}\;\equiv\;B_{\rm e}\,\overline{B},

(3)

while the unit vector along the magnetic field is

\widehat{{\sf b}}(\lambda,\varphi)\;=\;\frac{2\,\sin\lambda\;\widehat{r}\;-\;\cos\lambda\,\widehat{\lambda}}{\sqrt{1+3\,\sin^{2}\lambda}}.

(4)

In the present work, dimensionless variables and fields are normalized to their equatorial values and are represented with an overbar (e.g.,
\overline{r}=r/r_{\rm e}
). In addition, we will occasionally use the parameters associated with the geomagnetic dipole field
Parks_2003
, where
B_{\rm e}=B_{\rm E}\,(r_{\rm E}/r_{\rm e})^{3}
, with
B_{\rm E}\simeq 3.12\times 10^{-5}
Tesla and
r_{\rm E}=6378
km, whenever explicit numerical values are needed.

The magnetic dipole field (
1
) may also be written in terms of the magnetic vector potential
{\bf A}=\psi\,\nabla\varphi
as

{\bf B}\;=\;\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf A}\;=\;\nabla\psi\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\nabla\varphi,

(5)

which guarantees that the magnetic dipole field is divergenceless:
\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf B}=0
, where the dipole magnetic flux is

\psi(r,\lambda)\;=\;\psi_{\rm e}\,(r_{\rm e}/r)\;\cos^{2}\lambda\;\equiv\;\psi_{\rm e}\,\overline{\psi},

(6)

with
\psi_{\rm e}=B_{\rm e}\,r_{\rm e}^{2}
.

Since the magnetic dipole field satisfies the condition
{\bf B}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\psi=0
, the magnetic field lines (
1
) lie on a constant-
\psi
surface:
\psi=\psi_{\rm e}
(i.e.,
r=r_{\rm e}\,\cos^{2}\lambda
). On this constant-
\psi
surface, the magnitude (
3
) of the magnetic dipole field is a function of latitude
\lambda
alone:
B|_{\psi}(\lambda)=B_{\rm e}\,(1+3\,\sin^{2}\lambda)^{\frac{1}{2}}/\cos^{6}\lambda
, which becomes infinite as
\lambda\rightarrow\pm\,\pi/2
.

Next, we note that, in the absence of current sources, the curl of the magnetic field (
1
) vanishes:
\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf B}=0
, which implies that

\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\nabla\ln B\;=\;\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\;=\;\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,(\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\widehat{{\sf b}})\;=\;K(\lambda)\;\nabla\varphi,

(7)

where the magnetic curvature
\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\widehat{{\sf b}}=K\,\nabla\varphi\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}
is expressed in terms of the dimensionless latitude-dependent magnetic-curvature function

K(\lambda)\;=\;\frac{3\;(1-\sin^{4}\lambda)}{(1+3\,\sin^{2}\lambda)^{\frac{3}{2}}}.

(8)

This magnetic-curvature function, which is a monotonic function of latitude
\lambda
:
0\leq K\leq 3
, will play a key role in our discussion of the faithfulness of the guiding-center approximation.

IV
Guiding-center Phase-space Lagrangian Dynamics

In order to remove the fast gyromotion time scale from the equations of motion (
10
)-(
12
), as seen in Figs.
1
-
4
, and extract slower orbital bounce and azimuthal drift-precession time scales, we proceed with the guiding-center representation for charged-particle motion in a nonuniform magnetic field (see Refs.
Littlejohn_1983
;
Cary_Brizard_2009
;
Tronko_Brizard_2015
).

Using the guiding-center coordinates
({\bf X},P_{\|},\mu,\zeta)
, the phase-space Lagrangian (
9
) is transformed into the guiding-center phase-space Lagrangian
Tronko_Brizard_2015

L_{\rm gc}\;=\;\left(\frac{q}{c}\,{\bf A}+P_{\|}\;\widehat{{\sf b}}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\dot{{\bf X}}+J\,\left(\dot{\zeta}-{\bf R}^{*}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\dot{{\bf X}}\right)-H_{\rm gc},

(19)

where
{\bf X}=(R,\Phi,\Lambda)
denotes the guiding-center position,
P_{\|}
denotes the parallel guiding-center momentum, and the guiding-center gyroaction
J=\mu\,B_{\rm e}/\Omega_{\rm e}=\mu\,(mc/q)
is canonically conjugate to the guiding-center gyroangle
\zeta
, and the guiding-center Hamiltonian is
H_{\rm gc}=P_{\|}^{2}/2m+\mu\,B
. The guiding-center position
{\bf X}({\bf x},{\bf v})={\bf x}-\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}-\cdots
is defined with the help of the (lowest-order) gyroradius vector (
16
), so that the guiding-center spherical coordinates are

\left.\begin{array}[]{rcl}R&=&r\;-\;\epsilon\,\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}}_{0}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\overline{\nabla}r\;+\;\cdots\\
\Lambda&=&\lambda\;-\;\epsilon\,\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}}_{0}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\overline{\nabla}\lambda\;+\;\cdots\\
\Phi&=&\varphi\;-\;\epsilon\,\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}}_{0}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\overline{\nabla}\varphi\;+\;\cdots\end{array}\right\},

(20)

where we used the ordering
\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla=\epsilon\,\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}}_{0}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\overline{\nabla}
and higher-order corrections depend on the magnetic-field nonuniformity. The guiding-center transformation from the local momentum coordinates
(p_{\|}=\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf p},J_{0}=\mu_{0}\,B_{\rm e}/\Omega_{\rm e},\zeta_{0})
to the guiding-center momentum coordinates
(P_{\|},J\equiv\mu\,B_{\rm e}/\Omega_{\rm e},\zeta)
, on the other hand, depends on magnetic-field nonuniformity
Cary_Brizard_2009
. In the present work, the first-order correction
\mu_{1}
, which is derived in the Appendix, will be needed in order to verify the validity of the guiding-center approximation.

The vector functions
{\bf A}=\Psi\,\nabla\Phi
and
{\bf B}=B\,\widehat{{\sf b}}
in Eq. (
19
) are evaluated at the guiding-center position
{\bf X}
. We note that, as a result of the guiding-center transformation, the magnetic moment
\mu
is an invariant of the guiding-center dynamics, while it is not an invariant of the particle dynamics (the breakdown of the adiabatic invariance of the magnetic moment is briefly discussed in App.
A
). In Eq. (
19

&\\
\frac{1}{2}\,(\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}})\;\widehat{{\sf b}}\;\equiv\;0&({\sf B})\end{array}\right.

(21)

defined in terms of the gyrogauge vector field
Littlejohn_1983

{\bf R}=\nabla\widehat{1}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\widehat{2}
, which is expressed in terms of the orthogonal unit vector fields

IV.1
Guiding-center Euler-Lagrange equations

The guiding-center Euler-Lagrange equations associated with variations of the guiding-center Lagrangian (
19
) in
({\bf X},P_{\|},\mu,\zeta)
yield, respectively,

\displaystyle\dot{P}_{\|}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}-\frac{q}{c}\,\dot{\bf X}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf B}^{*}-\dot{J}\,{\bf R}^{*}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,\nabla H_{\rm gc}=-\,\mu\nabla B,

(23)

\displaystyle\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\dot{\bf X}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial H_{\rm gc}/\partial P_{\|}=P_{\|}/m,

(24)

\displaystyle\dot{\zeta}\;-\;\dot{\bf X}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf R}^{*}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial H_{\rm gc}/\partial J=\Omega,

\displaystyle\dot{J}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\;\partial H_{\rm gc}/\partial\zeta=0,

\displaystyle{\bf B}^{*}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\left[{\bf A}\;+\;(c/q)\left(P_{\|}\;\widehat{{\sf b}}\;-\frac{}{}\epsilon\,J\,{\bf R}^{*}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\nabla\Psi^{*}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\nabla\Phi\;+\;(c/q)\;P_{\|}K(\Lambda)\,\nabla\Phi

\displaystyle\frac{c}{qB_{\|}^{*}}\left(\mu\,B+\frac{P_{\|}^{2}}{m}\right)K\,\nabla\Phi\;+\;\frac{P_{\|}}{mB_{\|}^{*}}\,\nabla\Psi^{*}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\nabla\Phi,

\displaystyle-\;\frac{{\bf B}^{*}}{B_{\|}^{*}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mu\nabla B\;=\;-\;\frac{1}{B_{\|}^{*}}\;\nabla\Psi^{*}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\nabla\Phi\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mu\nabla B,

). We note that
P_{\|}=m\dot{\bf X}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}
is indeed the guiding-center parallel momentum and the fast gyromotion, now described by Eq. (
25
), is decoupled from the slow guiding-center dynamics (
29
)-(
30
).

In addition, we note that, while the guiding-center energy
{\cal E}=P_{\|}^{2}/2m+\mu\,B
is an exact guiding-center invariant:

\dot{\cal E}\;=\;P_{\|}\,\dot{P}_{\|}/m\;+\;\mu\,\dot{\bf X}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla B\;=\;0,

the guiding-center azimuthal canonical angular momentum

P_{{\rm gc}\Phi}\;=\;(q/c)\,\Psi^{*}\;=\;(q/c)\,\Psi\;-\;J\;b_{z}^{*}

(32)

is also an exact guiding-center invariant, since

\dot{\Psi}^{*}\;=\;\dot{\bf X}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\Psi^{*}\;=\;\dot{R}\;\frac{\partial\Psi^{*}}{\partial R}\;+\;\dot{\Lambda}\;\frac{\partial\Psi^{*}}{\partial\Lambda}\;=\;0,

(33)

where, from Eq. (
29
), we used

\displaystyle\dot{R}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dot{\bf X}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla R\;=\;\frac{P_{\|}}{mB_{\|}^{*}}\,\nabla\Psi^{*}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\nabla\Phi\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla R

(34)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\;\frac{P_{\|}}{m{\cal J}B_{\|}^{*}}\,\frac{\partial\Psi^{*}}{\partial\Lambda},

\displaystyle\dot{\Lambda}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dot{\bf X}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\Lambda\;=\;\frac{P_{\|}}{mB_{\|}^{*}}\,\nabla\Psi^{*}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\nabla\Phi\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\Lambda

(35)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{P_{\|}}{m{\cal J}B_{\|}^{*}}\,\frac{\partial\Psi^{*}}{\partial R}.

Lastly, the guiding-center azimuthal angular velocity

\dot{\Phi}\;=\;\dot{\bf X}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\Phi\;=\;\frac{c}{qB_{\|}^{*}}\left(\mu\,B+\frac{P_{\|}^{2}}{m}\right)K\,|\nabla\Phi|^{2}

(36)

is entirely driven by magnetic curvature, while the guiding-center parallel force equation (
30
) is driven by the magnetic-field gradient.

IV.2
Normalized guiding-center equations

In order to extract explicit orbital time scales from the guiding-center equations of motion (
30
) and (
34
)-(
36
), we now introduce the following normalizations. First, we introduce
B=B_{\rm e}\,\overline{B}
,

R=r_{\rm e}\,\rho
, and
\Psi=B_{\rm e}r_{\rm e}^{2}\,\overline{\Psi}
, so that

\Psi^{*}\;=\;B_{\rm e}r_{\rm e}^{2}\left(\overline{\Psi}\;-\;\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2}\;\overline{\mu}\,b_{z}^{*}(\Lambda)\right)\;\equiv\;B_{\rm e}r_{\rm e}^{2}\,\overline{\Psi}^{*},

(37)

where
\overline{B}=\rho^{-3}(1+3\sin^{2}\Lambda)^{\frac{1}{2}}
and
\overline{\Psi}=\rho^{-1}\cos^{2}\Lambda
. Next, the normalized guiding-center motion in the poloidal
(\rho,\Lambda)
-plane is expressed as

\left(\dot{\rho},\;\dot{\Lambda}\right)\;=\;\frac{\nu_{\rm e}\,\overline{P}_{\|}}{\overline{\cal J}\overline{B}_{\|}^{*}}\,\left(-\;\frac{\partial\overline{\Psi}^{*}}{\partial\Lambda},\frac{\partial\overline{\Psi}^{*}}{\partial\rho}\right),

(38)

where we introduced the energy-dependent bounce frequency

\nu_{\rm e}\;\equiv\;\sqrt{\frac{2\,\mu B_{\rm e}}{mr_{\rm e}^{2}}}\;\equiv\;\epsilon\,\Omega_{\rm e}\,\overline{\mu}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(39)

Here, we introduced the normalized guiding-center parallel momentum
\overline{P}_{\|}=P_{\|}/(mr_{\rm e}\nu_{\rm e})
, which satisfies the normalized guiding-center parallel-force equation

\dot{\overline{P}}_{\|}\;=\;-\;\frac{\nu_{\rm e}}{2\overline{\cal J}\overline{B}_{\|}^{*}}\left(\frac{\partial\overline{\Psi}^{*}}{\partial\rho}\,\frac{\partial\overline{B}}{\partial\Lambda}\;-\;\frac{\partial\overline{\Psi}^{*}}{\partial\Lambda}\,\frac{\partial\overline{B}}{\partial\rho}\right),

(40)

so that the total guiding-center kinetic energy becomes

{\cal E}\;=\;\mu\,B_{\rm e}\left(\overline{B}\;+\;\overline{P}_{\|}^{2}\right)\;=\;\mu\,B_{\rm t}\;\equiv\;\mu B_{\rm e}\;\overline{B}_{\rm t},

(41)

where
B_{\rm t}
denotes the magnetic field at which the parallel guiding-center momentum vanishes. We easily see that
|\overline{P}_{\|}|=\sqrt{\overline{B}_{t}-\overline{B}}
(which has a maximum at the equator
|\overline{P}_{\|{\rm e}}|=\sqrt{\overline{B}_{\rm t}-1}
), where
1\leq\overline{B}\leq\overline{B}_{\rm t}={\cal E}/(\mu B_{\rm e})
, and the poloidal guiding-center motion (
38
) also comes to a stop at a turning point. For this reason, the equations (
38
) represent the periodic bounce motion between turning points located symmetrically in the northern and southern hemispheres. In an axisymmetric magnetic dipole field
Parks_2003
;
Duthoit_2010

\pm\,\Lambda_{\rm b}
are determined from the roots of the equation

\cos^{6}\Lambda_{\rm b}\;-\;\sin^{2}\alpha_{0}\,\sqrt{1+3\,\sin^{2}\Lambda_{\rm b}}\;=\;0,

(42)

where
\alpha_{0}
denotes the initial pitch angle. The normalized guiding-center azimuthal motion is expressed as

\dot{\Phi}\;=\;\frac{\nu_{\rm e}^{2}}{2\,\Omega_{\rm e}\,\overline{B}_{\|}^{*}}\left(\overline{B}\;+\;2\,\overline{P}_{\|}^{2}\right)\,K\,|\overline{\nabla}\Phi|^{2},

(43)

which does not stop at a turning point since
\overline{B}+2\,\overline{P}_{\|}^{2}=2\,\overline{B}_{\rm t}-\overline{B}\geq\overline{B}_{\rm t}
does not vanish.

We conclude this Section by noting that Eqs. (
38

43

\overline{t}\equiv\nu_{\rm e}t
, so that we obtain the normalized guiding-center equations of motion

\displaystyle\left(\rho^{\prime}(\overline{t}),\;\Lambda^{\prime}(\overline{t})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\overline{P}_{\|}}{\overline{\cal J}\overline{B}_{\|}^{*}}\,\left(-\;\frac{\partial\overline{\Psi}^{*}}{\partial\Lambda},\frac{\partial\overline{\Psi}^{*}}{\partial\rho}\right),

(44)

\displaystyle\Phi^{\prime}(\overline{t})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\nu_{\rm e}}{2\,\Omega_{\rm e}\,\overline{B}_{\|}^{*}}\left(\overline{B}\;+\;2\,\overline{P}_{\|}^{2}\right)\,K\,|\overline{\nabla}\Phi|^{2},

(45)

\displaystyle\overline{P}_{\|}^{\prime}(\overline{t})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-1}{2\overline{\cal J}\overline{B}_{\|}^{*}}\left(\frac{\partial\overline{\Psi}^{*}}{\partial\rho}\,\frac{\partial\overline{B}}{\partial\Lambda}-\frac{\partial\overline{\Psi}^{*}}{\partial\Lambda}\,\frac{\partial\overline{B}}{\partial\rho}\right),

where the slow drift-precession time scale is clearly seen in Eq. (

VI
Summary

In previous work
Brizard_2017
, we showed that the guiding-center approximation was valid in a straight magnetic field with constant perpendicular magnetic gradient, even in the presence of strong gradients. In addition, based on the existence of an exact analytical solution for the particle orbits, this work also confirmed that the guiding-center polarization corresponded exactly with an orbit-averaged particle displacement.

In the present work, we extended our investigation of the validity of the guiding-center approximation in describing charged single-particle motion in a nonuniform magnetic field. Here, we considered regular particle orbits in azimuthally symmetric magnetic dipole geometry, in which the azimuthal angular canonical momentum is conserved and the guiding-center magnetic moment is an adiabatic invariant. We successfully validated the guiding-center approximation in describing particle motion in an azimuthally symmetric magnetic dipole field provided higher-order guiding-center corrections are taken into account, which had already been noted for the case of an axisymmetric tokamak magnetic field
Belova_2003
. In particular, the guiding-center polarization correction in the guiding-center azimuthal angular canonical momentum, not taken into account in the standard guiding-center approximation
Littlejohn_1983
, proved crucial in establishing a faithful guiding-center representation for regular particle orbits in magnetic dipole geometry.

Appendix A
Higher-order Guiding-center Approximation

In this Appendix, we will calculate explicit guiding-center corrections that arise at first-order in the magnetic-field nonuniformity associated with magnetic-field gradients
(\nabla\ln B)
and magnetic-field curvature
(\mbox{\boldmath$\kappa$}=\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\widehat{{\sf b}})
.

First, we explore the adiabatic invariance of the lowest-order magnetic moment

\mu_{0}=\frac{m|{\bf v}_{\bot}|^{2}}{2B}=\left(\epsilon^{2}mr_{\rm e}^{2}\Omega_{\rm e}^{2}/2B_{\rm e}\right)\;\frac{|\overline{\bf v}_{\bot}|^{2}}{\overline{B}}.

(51)

First, we find that
\dot{\mu}_{0}=-\,\mu_{0}\,{\bf v}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\ln B+m\dot{\bf v}_{\bot}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v}_{\bot}/B
, which can ultimately be expressed as

\displaystyle\dot{\mu}_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,\mu_{0}\,{\bf v}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\ln B+\frac{m}{B}\left(\dot{\bf v}-\dot{v}_{\|}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}-v_{\|}\,{\bf v}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\widehat{{\sf b}}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v}_{\bot}

(52)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,\mu_{0}\,{\bf v}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\ln B-\frac{mv_{\|}}{B}\;{\bf v}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v}_{\bot}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,\frac{v_{\|}}{B}\left(\mu_{0}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla B\;+\frac{}{}m\,{\bf v}_{\bot}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v}_{\bot}\right)

\displaystyle-\;\frac{{\bf v}_{\bot}}{B}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\mu_{0}\,\nabla B\;+\;m\,v_{\|}^{2}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\widehat{{\sf b}}\right).

Although
\dot{\mu}_{0}\neq 0
for general magnetic fields, its average over the fast gyromotion time scale yields

\langle\dot{\mu}_{0}\rangle\;=\;-\,\mu_{0}\,v_{\|}\left(\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\ln B\;+\frac{}{}\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\right)\;=\;-\,\frac{\mu_{0}\,v_{\|}}{B}\;(\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf B}),

where
\langle{\bf v}_{\bot}\rangle=0
and
\langle{\bf v}_{\bot}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v}_{\bot}\rangle=(\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}})\;|{\bf v}_{\bot}|^{2}/2
. Since magnetic fields are divergenceless, we immediately find that

\langle\dot{\mu}_{0}\rangle=0
, i.e.,
\mu_{0}
is an invariant over time scales that are long compared to the fast gyromotion time scale.

A.1
First-order correction to
\mu_{0}

The normalized guiding-center magnetic moment can be constructed
\overline{\mu}=\overline{\mu}_{0}+\epsilon\,\overline{\mu}_{1}+\cdots
as an asymptotic expansion in powers of
\epsilon
, where
\overline{\mu}_{1}
is a correction that involves the nonuniformity of the magnetic field. For a general magnetic field, the first-order correction to the magnetic moment is
Tronko_Brizard_2015

\displaystyle\mu_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\mu_{0}\nabla_{\bot}\ln B+\frac{p_{\|}^{2}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\kappa$}}{m\,B}\right)\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}-\mu_{0}\varrho_{\|}\left(\tau\;+\frac{}{}\alpha_{1}\right)

(53)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle(\epsilon^{2}mr_{\rm e}^{2}\Omega_{\rm e}^{2}/2B_{\rm e})\,\epsilon\,\overline{\mu}_{1},

where
\alpha_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\,\tau-\widehat{\bot}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\widehat{\rho}
. For magnetic dipole geometry, where
\tau=\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}=0
and
\nabla_{\bot}\ln B=\mbox{\boldmath$\kappa$}
, we find

-\,\mu_{0}\varrho_{\|}\alpha_{1}\;=\;\frac{p_{\|}}{2B}\;\left(\frac{d\widehat{{\sf b}}}{dt}\;-\;v_{\|}\;\mbox{\boldmath$\kappa$}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0},

so that Eq. (
53
) becomes

\mu_{1}\;=\;\left(\mu_{0}\;+\;\frac{p_{\|}^{2}}{2m\,B}\right)\mbox{\boldmath$\kappa$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\;+\;\frac{p_{\|}}{2B}\;\frac{d\widehat{{\sf b}}}{dt}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0},

(54)

where
\mbox{\boldmath$\kappa$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}=-\;\epsilon\,\varphi^{\prime}K/\overline{B}
. Hence, the normalized magnetic-moment correction
\overline{\mu}_{1}
in magnetic dipole geometry is expressed as

\overline{\mu}_{1}\;=\;-\,\left(\overline{\mu}_{0}\;+\;\frac{\overline{p}_{\|}^{2}}{\overline{B}}\right)\frac{\varphi^{\prime}}{\overline{B}}\;K\;+\;\frac{\overline{p}_{\|}}{\overline{B}}\;\widehat{{\sf b}}^{\prime}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}}_{0}.

(55)

Figure
9
shows how important the first-order correction (
54
) is in establishing the adiabatic invariance of magnetic moment. Indeed, using
\dot{\mu}_{1}=\Omega\,\partial\mu_{1}/\partial\zeta
to lowest order and
\dot{\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}}_{0}=\Omega\,\partial\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}/\partial\zeta={\bf v}_{\bot}
, we see that

\displaystyle\dot{\mu}_{0}+\dot{\mu}_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,\frac{v_{\|}}{B}\left(\mu_{0}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla B\;+\frac{}{}m\,{\bf v}_{\bot}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v}_{\bot}\right)\;-\;\frac{{\bf v}_{\bot}}{B}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\mu_{0}\,\nabla_{\bot}B\;+\;m\,v_{\|}^{2}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\widehat{{\sf b}}\right)

(56)

\displaystyle+\;\left(\mu_{0}\;\nabla_{\bot}\ln B\;+\;\frac{p_{\|}^{2}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\kappa$}}{m\,B}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v}_{\bot}\;+\;\frac{p_{\|}}{2B}\;\left({\bf v}_{\bot}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v}_{\bot}-\frac{\partial{\bf v}_{\bot}}{\partial\zeta}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial{\bf v}_{\bot}}{\partial\zeta}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,\frac{v_{\|}}{B}\;\mu_{0}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla B\;-\;\frac{p_{\|}}{2B}\;\left({\bf v}_{\bot}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v}_{\bot}+\frac{\partial{\bf v}_{\bot}}{\partial\zeta}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial{\bf v}_{\bot}}{\partial\zeta}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,\frac{\mu_{0}\,v_{\|}}{B}\left(\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla B\;+\frac{}{}B\;\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\right)\;=\;-\,\frac{\mu_{0}\,v_{\|}}{B}\;(\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf B})\;=\;0,

and, thus, the guiding-center magnetic moment
\mu
is conserved up to first-order effects in magnetic-field nonunifornity, without the need of gyroangle-averaging.

A.2
First-order correction to
\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}

When FLR corrections (
20
) need to be calculated up to first order in magnetic-field nonuniformity, we must evaluate the pull-back of the guiding-center position up to second order in
\epsilon
(now appearing as an ordering parameter associated with the rescaling
q\rightarrow q/\epsilon
):

\displaystyle{\sf T}_{\rm gc}{\bf X}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\bf x}+\epsilon\,G_{1}^{\bf x}+\epsilon^{2}\left(G_{2}^{\bf x}\;+\;\frac{1}{2}\,{\sf G}_{1}\cdot{\sf d}G_{1}^{\bf x}\right)+\cdots

(57)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle{\bf x}-\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}\;=\;{\bf x}-\epsilon\,\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\;-\;\epsilon^{2}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{1}+\cdots

where, using the components
(G_{2}^{\bf x},G_{1}^{\mu},G_{1}^{\zeta})
calculated in Ref.
Tronko_Brizard_2015
, we obtain the first-order gyroradius

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,G_{2}^{\bf x}\;-\;\frac{1}{2}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\;+\;\frac{1}{2}\,\left(G_{1}^{\mu}\,\frac{\partial\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}}{\partial\mu}+G_{1}^{\zeta}\,\frac{\partial\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}}{\partial\zeta}\right)

(58)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\rho_{1\|}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\;+\;\frac{1}{2}\,\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\ln B\right)\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\;-\;\frac{\mu B}{2m\Omega^{2}}\;\mbox{\boldmath$\kappa$}.

Here,
\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\ln B=-\,\dot{\varphi}\,K/\Omega
and the parallel component is defined as

\rho_{1\|}=\frac{5}{4}\left[\frac{\mu B}{2m\Omega^{2}}\left(\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\right)-\frac{v_{\|}}{\Omega}\,K\,\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\varphi\right]-\frac{3}{4\Omega^{2}}\;\frac{d\widehat{{\sf b}}}{dt}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf v}.

This first-order gyroradius correction can then be used in the guiding-center pull-back (
48
).

We now calculate the guiding-center gyroradius
\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{\rm gc}\equiv{\sf T}_{\rm gc}^{-1}{\bf x}-{\bf X}={\sf T}_{\rm gc}^{-1}\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}
, which is defined in terms of the guiding-center push-forward of the particle gyroradius into guiding-center phase space, where
\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{{\rm gc}0}=\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}
and
\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{{\rm gc}1}=\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{1}-{\sf G}_{1}\cdot{\sf d}\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}
. In Ref.
Tronko_Brizard_2015
, it was shown that the guiding-center polarization can be constructed explicitly from the guiding-center transformation, which is defined as the gyroangle-averaged expression
Brizard_2013

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}_{\rm gc}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle q\,\langle\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{{\rm gc}1}\rangle\;-\;\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left\langle\frac{q}{2}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\mbox{\boldmath$\rho$}_{0}\right\rangle

(59)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\;\frac{q}{m\Omega^{2}}\left(\mu\,B\;\nabla_{\bot}\ln B\;+\;\frac{p_{\|}^{2}}{m}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\kappa$}\right)\;=\;\frac{q\widehat{{\sf b}}}{\Omega}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{d{\bf X}}{dt},

thereby recovering the standard expression derived by Kaufman
Kaufman_1986
. The higher-order guiding-center corrections
Tronko_Brizard_2015
leading to Eq. (
59
) also yield the correction
b_{z}^{*}
in Eq. (
49
), which ensures a faithful guiding-center representation for regular charged-particle orbits in an axisymmetric magnetic dipole field.

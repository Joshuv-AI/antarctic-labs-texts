B-twisted Gaiotto–Witten theory and topological quantum field theory
Niklas Garner, Nathan Geer, Matthew B. Young

Abstract
. We develop representation theoretic techniques to construct three dimensional non-semisimple topological quantum field theories which model homologically truncated topological B-twists of abelian Gaiotto–Witten theory with linear matter. Our constructions are based on relative modular structures on the category of weight modules over an unrolled quantization of a Lie superalgebra. The Lie superalgebra, originally defined by Gaiotto and Witten, is associated to a complex symplectic representation of a metric abelian Lie algebra. The physical theories we model admit alternative realizations as Chern–Simons-Rozansky–Witten theories and supergroup Chern–Simons theories and include as particular examples global forms of g ​ l ​ ( 1 | 1 ) \mathfrak{gl}(1|1) -Chern–Simons theory and toral Chern–Simons theory. Fundamental to our approach is the systematic incorporation of non-genuine line operators which source flat connections for the topological flavour symmetry of the theory.

Introduction

We construct three dimensional non-semisimple topological quantum field theories (TQFTs) which model topological B-twists of Gaiotto–Witten theories with abelian gauge group and linear hypermultiplet matter. Our constructions are based on the representation theory of an unrolled quantization of a Lie superalgebra associated to a symplectic representation of a metric abelian Lie algebra.

Background and motivation

The notion of a topological twist is a tool used by physicists to extract TQFTs from supersymmetric quantum field theories. Much like the more familiar two dimensional quantum field theories with
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
supersymmetry and the resulting topological A- and B-models
[
Wit88b
,
Wit92
]
, three dimensional quantum field theories with
\mathcal{N}=4
supersymmetry admit two topological twists. The topological A-twist is a dimensional reduction of the four dimensional twist giving rise to Donaldson–Witten theory
[
Wit88a
]
and for gauge theories with Yang–Mills kinetic term leads to the Coulomb branch constructions of Braverman, Finkelberg and Nakajima
[
Nak16
,
BFN18
]
. The topological B-twist, as we describe in more detail below, gives rise to Rozansky–Witten theory
[
RW97
]
.

By a mechanism originally discovered by Gaiotto and Witten in the study of four dimensional
\mathcal{N}=4
supersymmetric Yang–Mills theory with a spatially varying
\theta
-angle
[
GW10
]
, it is possible to construct Chern–Simons-matter theories with
\mathcal{N}=4
supersymmetry, and hence admitting topological twists. The input data for the simplest Gaiotto–Witten theories includes a gauge group
G
and a holomorphic Hamiltonian
G
-manifold
X
satisfying some additional conditions. The A-twists of these theories were formulated in
[
KLL09
]
but are still not well understood. Their B-twists were first described by Kapustin and Saulina
[
KS09
]
and naturally interpolate between two particularly important three dimensional quantum field theories: they become Chern–Simons theories when
X
is a point and Rozansky–Witten theories when
G
is a point. Correspondingly, B-twists of these Gaiotto–Witten theories are known as
Chern–Simons-Rozansky–Witten theories
.

Chern–Simons theory is a quantum gauge theory defined by its compact gauge Lie group
G
and level
\kappa\in H^{4}(BG;\mathbb{Z})

[
Wit89
]
. At a formal level, partition functions in Chern–Simons theory are invariants of closed oriented
3
-manifolds. More generally, the inclusion of Wilson loops leads to invariants of
3
-manifolds containing a link coloured by representations of
G
. As such, Chern–Simons theory is a natural physical setting for many constructions in quantum topology, including the Jones, Kauffman and HOMFLYPT polynomials. A key feature of Chern–Simons theory is the finite semisimplicity of its category of (Wilson) line operators. This allows for a physical understanding of Chern–Simons theory via rational conformal field theory
[
Wit89
,
EMSS89
]
, namely, chiral Wess–Zumino–Witten theory, and underlies its mathematical construction, in the form of an Atiyah–Segal-style TQFT, as the Reshetikhin–Turaev theory of a modular tensor category associated to the pair
(G,\kappa)

[
Tur94
]
. See
[
DW90
,
FQ93
,
Fre94
]
,
[
RT91
,
AP95
]
and
[
JS93
,
BM05
,
Sti08
]
when
G
finite, semisimple and toral, respectively, and
[
FHLT10
,
Hen17
]
for general proposals. When
G
is simple simply connected, the relevant modular tensor category is the semisimplified category of modules over the small quantum group of
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}
at a
\kappa
-dependent root of unity.

Much less understood is Rozansky–Witten theory, a topological B-twist of a
\mathcal{N}=4
supersymmetric
\sigma
-model with target a holomorphic symplectic manifold
(X,\omega)

[
RW97
]
. In view of its central role in three dimensional mirror symmetry
[
IS96
]
and related mathematics
[
Tel14
,
BF19
]
, it is an important open problem to construct Rozansky–Witten theory as a TQFT. A significant obstacle to doing so is that Rozansky–Witten theory is neither finite nor semisimple: its category of line operators is expected to be the
2
-periodic derived category of coherent sheaves on
X

[
KRS09
]
. On the other hand, the lack of finiteness and semisimplicity leads to rich mathematical structures in the theory. Derived Lie theoretic aspects of Rozansky–Witten theory were studied in
[
Kap99
,
Kon99
]
, formalizing the intuition that Rozansky–Witten theory is a fermionic analogue of Chern–Simons theory, with
X
and
\omega
playing the roles of (the classifying stack of)
G
and
\kappa
, respectively. Perturbative studies of Rozansky–Witten theory are found in
[
QZ09
,
QZ10
,
RW10
,
CLL17
]
. The structure of topological boundary conditions and defects in Rozansky–Witten theory was explored in
[
KRS09
,
KR10
,
Tel20
,
OR23
]
and led, via the Cobordism Hypothesis
[
Lur09
]
, to the construction of partially defined TQFTs when
X
a cotangent bundle using derived algebraic geometry
[
BFN10
]
and
X=T^{\vee}\mathbb{C}^{n}
using categories of matrix factorizations
[
BCR23
]
.

The Chern–Simons-Rozansky–Witten theories of
[
KS09
]
are an amalgam of these two theories. We assume that
(X,\omega)
admits an action of a compact Lie group
G
by holomorphic Hamiltonian automorphisms. As described in
[
GW10
]
, it is only possible couple Chern–Simons gauge fields to this flavour symmetry
G
when it satisfies the
Fundamental Identity
, discussed below. When the theory has a linear target space, that is,
X
is a complex symplectic representation of
G
, the Fundamental Identity allows for the construction of complex Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}_{X}
from the complexified Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}
of
G
and
X
. The resulting Chern–Simons-Rozansky–Witten theory is equivalent to a supergroup Chern–Simons theory based on a global form of
\mathfrak{g}_{X}
. Perturbative aspects of Chern–Simons-Rozansky–Witten theory were studied in
[
KQZ13
]
, where it was reformulated as an AKSZ theory
[
AKSZ97
]
. In view of the utility of the boundary Wess–Zumino–Witten models in ordinary Chern–Simons theories, the first author recently proposed boundary logarithmic vertex operator algebras whose modules model the categories of line operators in A- and B-twisted Gaiotto–Witten theories
[
Gar23
]
, building on earlier work in the setting of three dimensional
\mathcal{N}=4
gauge theories with Yang–Mills kinetic term
[
CG19
,
CCG19
]
.

Main results

We construct three dimensional non-semisimple TQFTs which model B-twisted Gaiotto–Witten theory with the following input data:

•

Gauge group
G=T
, a non-trivial connected abelian Lie group with metric
\kappa
.

•

Hypermultiplet matter representation
X=T^{\vee}R\simeq R\oplus R^{\vee}
, a linear holomorphic symplectic manifold, where
R
is a complex representation of
T
.

Since the gauge group is abelian and the target is linear, we call these theories linear abelian Gaiotto–Witten theories. The input data is required to satisfy the Fundamental Identity along with further technical conditions (Assumptions
A
,
B
and
C
in the body of the paper). As indicated above, the theory we construct can alternatively be seen as the Chern–Simons-Rozansky–Witten theory of the Hamiltonian
T
-manifold
T^{\vee}R
at level
\kappa
or the Chern–Simons theory whose gauge Lie supergroup has complexified Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{t}_{R}
, defined below, and bosonic subgroup
T
.

Our approach to non-semisimple TQFT is via the theory of relative modular tensor categories
[
CGPM14
,
DR22
]
. As reviewed in Section
1
, this theory yields a non-finite, non-semisimple generalization of the Reshetikhin–Turaev construction of a TQFT from a modular tensor category
[
Tur94
]
. Relative modular categories have been used successfully to model topological A-twists of certain three dimensional
\mathcal{N}=4
Chern–Simons-matter theories
[
BCGPM16
,
CDGG21
]
and
U(1|1)
-Chern–Simons theory
[
GY22
]
; the Rozansky–Witten-theoretic analysis of
[
GHN+21
]
is expected to be related to the construction of
[
CDGG21
]
by three dimensional mirror symmetry. The fundamental new idea in physical realizations of relative modular categories is the introduction of background fields coupling to flavour symmetries of the underlying physical theory
[
Gai19
]
. In the present context, this involves considering non-genuine line operators which source non-trivial background flat connections for topological flavour symmetry.

To construct relative modular categories relevant to B-twisted Gaiotto–Witten theories, following Kapustin and Saulina
[
KS09
]
and Gaiotto and Witten
[
GW10
]
, in Section
2
we recall that associated to the input data is a metric Lie superalgebra

\mathfrak{t}_{R}=\mathfrak{t}\oplus\Pi T^{\vee}R.

Here
\mathfrak{t}
is the complexified Lie algebra of
T
and
\Pi T^{\vee}R
is the representation
T^{\vee}R
placed in odd degree. The Lie bracket of two even elements is zero, of one even with one odd element is determined by the representation
T^{\vee}R
and of two odd elements is determined by the metric
\kappa
and the holomorphic moment map. The Fundamental Identity is precisely the statement that the bracket satisfies the super Jacobi identity for three odd elements. Under Assumption
A
, which is a mild integrality condition on the pair
(\kappa,R)
, we introduce in Definition
2.9
the primary algebraic object of this paper: an unrolled quantization
U_{q}^{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{t}_{R})
of
\mathfrak{t}_{R}
at parameter
q\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}\setminus\{\pm 1\}
. The infinite dimensional Hopf superalgebra
U_{q}^{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{t}_{R})
is a semidirect product of a standard quantization of
\mathfrak{t}_{R}
with the enveloping algebra of
\mathfrak{t}
. Let
\mathcal{C}_{R}
be the category of finite dimensional
U_{q}^{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{t}_{R})
-modules on which
\mathfrak{t}
acts semisimply and the actions of
Z\in\mathfrak{t}
and its corresponding grouplike element
K(Z)\in U_{q}^{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{t}_{R})
satisfy
K(Z)=q^{Z}
. The category
\mathcal{C}_{R}
has infinitely many isomorphism classes of simple objects and, unless
R
is the zero representation, is non-semisimple.

In Section
3
we develop the representation theory of
U_{q}^{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{t}_{R})
. After establishing various structural properties of Verma modules and classifying the simple objects of
\mathcal{C}_{R}
via highest
\mathfrak{t}
-weights (Theorem
3.12
), we prove our first main result.

Theorem 1

(Theorem
3.16
)
.

The category
\mathcal{C}_{R}
is
\mathbb{C}
-linear, locally finite, abelian and ribbon and has enough projectives and injectives.

The proof of Theorem
1
begins with a direct construction of a braiding on
\mathcal{C}_{R}
; see Proposition
3.14
. The exponential factor of the braiding is determined by the metric
\kappa
. A key technical result, Proposition
3.15
, asserts that the category
\mathcal{C}_{R}
is naturally graded by characters of certain distinguished central elements of
U_{q}^{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{t}_{R})
in such a way that generic homogenous subcategories of
\mathcal{C}_{R}
are semisimple (although non-finite). This relies on Assumption
B
, which states that the trivial representation does not appear in
R
. (Assumption
B
can effectively be removed; see Section
5.3
.) With these preliminaries, Theorem
1
is proved by verifying that the candidate ribbon structure on
\mathcal{C}_{R}
, given by the partial trace of the braiding with respect to a carefully chosen pivotal structure, is balanced on generic homogeneous subcategories of
\mathcal{C}_{R}
. By a general argument of
[
GPM18
]
, this suffices to ensure balancing globally.

The ribbon category
\mathcal{C}_{R}
or better, its derived category, is a model for the category of line operators in the perturbative Gaiotto–Witten theory associated to
(T,\kappa,R)
. As an indication of its perturbative nature, note that
\mathcal{C}_{R}
depends on
T
only through its Lie algebra. To model the non-perturbative theory, including the global form of the gauge group, we equip
\mathcal{C}_{R}
with the additional data of a relative modular structure. This structure includes a grading of
\mathcal{C}_{R}
by an abelian group
\mathsf{G}
for which generic homogenous subcategories are semisimple, a modified trace on the ideal of projective objects of
\mathcal{C}_{R}
and a monoidal functor from an abelian group
\mathsf{Z}
into the degree
0\in\mathsf{G}
subcategory
\mathcal{C}_{R,0}
such that generic homogeneous subcategories of
\mathcal{C}_{R}
have only finitely many
\mathsf{Z}
-orbits of simple objects. By work of De Renzi
[
DR22
]
, a relative modular structure on
\mathcal{C}_{R}
defines a decorated
\mathsf{Z}
-graded TQFT, that is, a symmetric monoidal functor

\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{C}_{R}}:\mathsf{C}\mathsf{ob}^{\textnormal{ad}}_{\mathcal{C}_{R}}\rightarrow\mathsf{V}\mathsf{ect}^{\mathsf{Z}}_{\mathbb{C}}.

The codomain is the category of
\mathsf{Z}
-graded vector spaces with symmetric braiding determined by the relative modular data. The domain is the category of decorated closed surfaces and their admissible bordisms. The decorations include
\mathcal{C}_{R}
-coloured ribbon graphs and flat
\mathsf{G}
-connections. Admissibility can be seen as a genericity condition. Unlike the Atiyah–Segal framework, the category
\mathsf{C}\mathsf{ob}^{\textnormal{ad}}_{\mathcal{C}_{R}}
is usually not rigid.

For concreteness, in the introduction we focus on a particular class of relative modular structures on
\mathcal{C}_{R}
. Other classes are constructed Section
5
. Recall that in compact Chern–Simons theory, the level
\kappa
receives quantum corrections, resulting in the shift of
\kappa
by the dual Coxeter number
[
Wit89
]
. There is a similar mechanism in Gaiotto–Witten theory which leads to an effective, or quantum-corrected, metric
\kappa_{\textup{\text{eff}}}
which differs from
\kappa
by the quadratic Casimir of
R
.

Theorem 2

(Theorem
4.6
)
.

The choice of an even integral lattice
\Gamma\subset(\mathfrak{t},\kappa_{\textup{\text{eff}}})
of full rank which contains the weights of the representation
R
determines a relative modular structure on
\mathcal{C}_{R}
. The resulting TQFT
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{C}_{R}}
has the property that each of its state spaces is finite dimensional.

The relative modular structure of Theorem
2
is constructed as follows. Tracking
\mathfrak{t}
-weights modulo the dual lattice
\Gamma^{\vee}
grades
\mathcal{C}_{R}
by the torus
\mathsf{G}=\mathfrak{t}^{\vee}/\penalty\Gamma^{\vee}
which is Langlands dual to
T_{\Gamma}=(\Gamma\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C})/\penalty\Gamma
. In particular, the subcategory
\mathcal{C}_{R,\bar{0}}
labels
U_{q}^{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{t}_{R})
-modules which deform representations of the complex Lie supergroup with Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{t}_{R}
and bosonic subgroup
T_{\Gamma}
. Assumption
C
, which is a mild genericity condition on
R
, ensures that
\mathcal{C}_{R}
is unimoduar. Since
\mathcal{C}_{R}
is generically semisimple and ribbon, this guarantees the existence of a modified trace on
\mathcal{C}_{R}
; see Lemma
3.19
and Proposition
3.21
. We take for
\mathsf{Z}
the group of one dimensional
U_{q}^{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{t}_{R})
-modules whose weights lie in the image of the adjoint map
\kappa^{\flat}:\Gamma\rightarrow\Gamma^{\vee}
.

In physical terms, the TQFT
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{C}_{R}}
models the homological truncation of the B-twisted Gaiotto–Witten theory with compact gauge group
T_{c,\Gamma}=(\Gamma\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R})/\penalty\Gamma
at level
\kappa
and hypermultiplet matter in the representation
T^{\vee}R
. In Section
4.2
, we outline various consistency checks of this model. From the physical perspective, the ribbon category
\mathcal{C}_{R}
can be thought of as the category of line operators in the perturbative theory or, equivalently, the theory with simply connected gauge group of type
\mathfrak{t}_{R}
. The grading group
\mathsf{G}
agrees with the topological flavour symmetry group of the physical theory. Objects in the homogeneous category
\mathcal{C}_{R,\bar{\lambda}}
,
\bar{\lambda}\in\mathsf{G}
, correspond to non-genuine line operators which couple to flat
\mathsf{G}
-connection with holonomy
\bar{\lambda}
around the support of the operator. Objects of the group
\mathsf{Z}
label gauge vortex lines and are a key non-perturbative ingredient. Much like in
T_{c,\Gamma}
-Chern–Simons theory, one role of gauge vortices is to screen line operators, resulting in a truncation of the spectrum of line operators. Said differently, line operators whose labels differ by the action of
\mathsf{Z}
are physically equivalent. From the perspective of vertex operator algebras of boundary local operators, these gauge vortex lines end on boundary monopole operators and are entirely analogous to the modules that extend the perturbative Heisenberg vertex operator algebra to the full, non-perturbative lattice vertex operator algebra which models
T_{c,\Gamma}
-Chern–Simons theory
[
BN22
,
BCDN23
,
GN23
]
.

In Theorem
4.7
we prove a Verlinde formula for the TQFT
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{C}_{R}}
, relating the partition function of a trivial circle bundle over a genus
g
surface
\Sigma_{g}
to the graded dimensions of the spate space
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{C}_{R}}(\Sigma_{g})
. As an application, we compute the Euler characteristic of the underlying super vector space of
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{C}_{R}}(\Sigma_{g})
:

\chi(\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{C}_{R}}(\Sigma_{g}))=|D|^{g-1}\sumop\displaylimits_{k\in D}\prodop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{\dim_{\mathbb{C}}R}\Big(2\sin\big(\kappa^{\vee}(Q_{i},k)\pi\big)\Big)^{2g-2}.

Here
D
is the discriminant group of the metric
\kappa
and
Q_{i}\in\mathfrak{t}^{\vee}
are the weights of
R
. As we explain in Remark
4.9
, the above expression for
\chi(\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{C}_{R}}(\Sigma_{g}))
is in agreement with physical computations by way of supersymmetric localization.

As already mentioned, we expect the TQFTs of this paper to model homological truncations of particular physical theories. Indeed, the theories we consider arise as topological twists of supersymmetric quantum field theories. On general grounds, the category of line operators in such a theory is expected to form a differential graded category. It is an interesting mathematical problem to construct from such a differential category a TQFT.

Traditional representation theoretic approaches to compact Chern–Simons theory encode the level
\kappa
in a quantum group through the quantum parameter
q
. This approach applies most naturally when there is a basic level, such as the Killing form on a simple Lie algebra, to which all other levels are related by scaling. Since there is no such basic level in our setting, we instead encode the level in the relative modular structure on a
q
-independent category
\mathcal{C}_{R}
. In other words, we aim to find a suitable specialization of
q
which works for all levels
\kappa
. However, in some cases, the natural specialization is
q=-1
, at which
U_{q}^{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{t}_{R})
is not defined. This leads us to consider
\widetilde{U}_{q}^{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{t}_{R})
, an unrolled quantization of a non-standard presentation of
\mathfrak{t}_{R}
, whose specialization
\widetilde{U}_{\sqrt{-1}}^{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{t}_{R})
serves as a replacement for the desired
q=-1
specialization of
U_{q}^{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{t}_{R})
. By working with the category
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{R}
of weight
\widetilde{U}_{\sqrt{-1}}^{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{t}_{R})
-modules we are able to treat all levels uniformly. The TQFT constructions of the paper are therefore in terms of
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{R}
. Nevertheless, to ease the comparison with the mathematics literature, we develop the general theory for
U_{q}^{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{t}_{R})
and mention the modifications required for
\widetilde{U}_{q}^{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{t}_{R})
.

We end this introduction by mentioning some natural extensions of the present work which seem to be accessible with existing techniques.

•

\mathfrak{t}^{\vee}
, the matrix of
\kappa^{\vee}
is the inverse
(\kappa^{ab})
of the matrix
(\kappa_{ab})
. Write
(Q_{1i},\dots,Q_{ri})\in\mathbb{C}^{r}
for the image of
Q_{i}
under the isomorphism
\mathfrak{t}^{\vee}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbb{C}^{r}
and organize the roots of
R
into an
r\times n
matrix
Q=(Q_{ai})
. In the physics literature, the transpose of
Q
is referred to as the
charge matrix
of the theory. With this notation, the Fundamental Identity (
5
) reads

(6)

\sumop\displaylimits_{a,b=1}^{r}\kappa^{ab}Q_{ai}Q_{bj}=0,\qquad 1\leq i,j\leq n.

We can now give a presentation of
\mathfrak{t}_{H}
.

Proposition 2.1
.

The Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{t}_{H}
is isomorphic to the Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{t}_{R}
with even generators
\{Z_{a}\mid 1\leq a\leq r\}
, odd generators
\{E_{i},F_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq n\}
and defining relations

(7)

[Z_{a},Z_{b}]=0,

(8)

[Z_{a},E_{i}]=Q_{ai}E_{i},\qquad[Z_{a},F_{i}]=-Q_{ai}F_{i},

(9)

[E_{i},E_{j}]=[F_{i},F_{j}]=0,

(10)

[E_{i},F_{j}]=\delta_{ij}\sumop\displaylimits_{a,b=1}^{r}\kappa^{ab}Q_{ai}Z_{b}.

Proof.

Only relations (
9
) and (
10
) are non-obvious. These follow from the observation that, in the chosen Darboux basis, a moment map
\mu:H\rightarrow\mathfrak{t}^{\vee}
is given by

\mu=\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{n}\sumop\displaylimits_{a=1}^{r}Q_{ak}E^{\vee}_{k}F^{\vee}_{k}Z^{\vee}_{a},

where
E^{\vee}_{k}
and
F^{\vee}_{k}
are seen as coordinates on
H
and
Z^{\vee}_{a}
as an element of
\mathfrak{t}^{\vee}
.
∎

As a consequence of Proposition
2.1
, if
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
are two polarizations of
H
, then
\mathfrak{t}_{R_{1}}\simeq\mathfrak{t}_{R_{2}}
as Lie superalgebras.

We begin with two degenerate examples.

Example 2.2
.

If
n=0
, so that
R
is the zero representation, then
\mathfrak{t}_{R}=\mathfrak{t}
is simply a metric abelian Lie algebra.
∎

Example 2.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{psl}(1|1)
be the projective special linear Lie superalgebra of
\mathbb{C}^{1|1}
, equivalently, the purely odd (abelian) Lie superalgebra of dimension two. If
r=0
, so that
\mathfrak{t}
is the zero Lie algebra, then
\mathfrak{t}_{R}
is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{psl}(1|1)^{\oplus n}
with its standard symplectic form.
∎

Example 2.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)
be the super vector space
\textup{\text{End}}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{C}^{1|1})
with supercommutator as the Lie bracket. Let
r=2
and
n=1
with

\kappa=\left(\begin{matrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{matrix}\right),\qquad Q=\left(\begin{matrix}1\\
0\end{matrix}\right).

The assignments

N=\left(\begin{matrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{matrix}\right)\mapsto Z_{1},\qquad E=\left(\begin{matrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{matrix}\right)\mapsto Z_{2},\qquad\psi^{+}=\left(\begin{matrix}0&1\\
0&0\end{matrix}\right)\mapsto E_{1},\qquad\psi^{-}=\left(\begin{matrix}0&0\\
1&0\end{matrix}\right)\mapsto F_{1}

extend to a Lie superalgebra isomorphism
\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathfrak{t}_{R}
. The induced bilinear form
(-,-)
on
\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)
is that associated to
\textup{\text{str}}_{\mathbb{C}^{1|1}}
, the supertrace in the fundamental representation, and is determined by

(N,E)=(E,N)=(\psi^{+},\psi^{-})=-(\psi^{-},\psi^{+})=1,

with all other pairings of generators vanishing.
∎

Example 2.5
.

Let
\mathfrak{s}
be a complex abelian Lie algebra of dimension
s
and
R
a representation of
\mathfrak{s}
of dimension
n
with weights
Q^{(\mathfrak{s})}_{1},\dots,Q^{(\mathfrak{s})}_{n}\in\mathfrak{s}^{\vee}
. Let
\mathfrak{t}
be the abelian Lie algebra
\mathfrak{s}\oplus\mathfrak{s}^{\vee}
with metric
\kappa=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&\textup{\text{id}}_{\mathfrak{s}^{\vee}}\\
\operatorname{can}_{\mathfrak{s}}&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)
. View
T^{\vee}R
as a symplectic representation of
\mathfrak{t}
on which
\mathfrak{s}^{\vee}
acts trivially. The root matrix of
R
is
Q=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}Q^{(\mathfrak{s})}\\
0\end{smallmatrix}\right)
. Since
\kappa
is hyperbolic and
Q
is concentrated in the top block, equation (
5
) imposes no constraints on
R
. With the additional assumption that
Q^{(\mathfrak{s})}
has integer entries, the Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{t}_{R}
appears in the context of three dimensional
\mathcal{N}=4
abelian gauged hypermultiplets
[
BCDN23
, §4.2]
. When
s=n=1
and
Q^{(\mathfrak{s})}=(1)
, we again recover
\mathfrak{t}_{R}\simeq\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)
with its standard metric.
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

For each
1\leq i\leq n
, the element
\mathcal{Z}_{i}:=\sumop\displaylimits_{a,b=1}^{r}\kappa^{ab}Q_{ai}Z_{b}\in\mathfrak{t}_{R}
is central.

Proof.

We have

[\mathcal{Z}_{i},E_{j}]=\sumop\displaylimits_{a,b=1}^{r}\kappa^{ab}Q_{ai}Q_{bj}E_{j},

which vanishes due to equation (
6
). The bracket
[\mathcal{Z}_{i},F_{j}]
vanishes for the same reason.
∎

Lemma 2.7
.

Suppose that
R\simeq R^{\prime}\oplus\mathbb{C}
, where
\mathbb{C}
is the trivial representation of
\mathfrak{t}
. Then
\mathfrak{t}_{R}\simeq\mathfrak{t}_{R^{\prime}}\oplus\mathfrak{psl}(1|1)
as metric Lie superalgebras.

Proof.

The assumption
R\simeq R^{\prime}\oplus\mathbb{C}
translates to the vanishing
Q_{i}=0
for some
1\leq i\leq n
. With this observation, the lemma follows from Proposition
2.1
and Example
2.3
.
∎

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
1\leq a\leq r
and
1\leq i\leq n
be such that
Q_{ai}\neq 0
. Define

Z_{a}^{\prime}=\frac{1}{Q_{ai}}Z_{a}-\frac{\kappa_{aa}}{2Q_{ai}^{2}}\mathcal{Z}_{i}.

Keeping the notation of Example
2.4
, the assignment
\{N,E,\psi^{+},\psi^{-}\}\mapsto\{Z_{a}^{\prime},\mathcal{Z}_{i},E_{i},F_{i}\}
extends to a Lie superalgebra isometry

\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)\simeq\text{{span}}_{\mathbb{C}}\{Z_{a}^{\prime},\mathcal{Z}_{i},E_{i},F_{i}\}\subset\mathfrak{t}_{R},

where
\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)
is given the metric
\textup{\text{str}}_{\mathbb{C}^{1|1}}
.

Proof.

The lemma follows from the equalities

\kappa(\frac{1}{Q_{ai}}Z_{a},\frac{1}{Q_{ai}}Z_{a})=\frac{\kappa_{aa}}{Q_{ai}^{2}},\qquad\kappa(\frac{1}{Q_{ai}}Z_{a},\mathcal{Z}_{i})=1,\qquad\kappa(\mathcal{Z}_{i},\mathcal{Z}_{j})=0,

the last of which follows from equation (
6
). In particular, the second equality implies that
\mathcal{Z}_{i}
is non-zero.
∎

2.4.
Effective metrics

Recall that in compact Chern–Simons theory, the level
\kappa
receives quantum corrections, leading to the famous dual Coxeter shift
[
Wit89
, §2]
. Similarly, the level
\kappa
receives one-loop quantum corrections in B-twisted Gaiotto–Witten theory
[
CDG23
, §7]
,
[
Gar23
, §3]
. The resulting effective metric
\kappa_{\textup{\text{eff}}}
will play an important role in the construction of relative modular categories in Section
4
.

The effective, or quantum corrected, metric on
\mathfrak{t}
is defined by

\kappa_{\textup{\text{eff}}}=\kappa+\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}Q_{i}\otimes Q_{i}.

Using equation (
5
), we verify that
\kappa_{\textup{\text{eff}}}
is indeed non-degenerate. It follows that
\kappa_{\textup{\text{eff}}}
induces a metric
\kappa_{\textup{\text{eff}}}^{\vee}
on
\mathfrak{t}^{\vee}
. Explicitly, we have

\kappa_{\textup{\text{eff}}}^{\vee}=\kappa^{\vee}-\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}\kappa^{\sharp}(Q_{i})\otimes\kappa^{\sharp}(Q_{i}).

Given
\lambda\in\mathfrak{t}^{\vee}
and
1\leq i\leq n
, let

\chi_{i}(\lambda)=\kappa^{\vee}(Q_{i},\lambda).

Equation (
5
) implies that
\kappa_{\textup{\text{eff}}}^{\vee}(Q_{i},\lambda)=\chi_{i}(\lambda)
. With this notation, we have

\kappa_{\textup{\text{eff}}}^{\vee}(\lambda,\mu)=\kappa^{\vee}(\lambda,\mu)-\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}\chi_{i}(\lambda)\chi_{i}(\mu),\qquad\lambda,\mu\in\mathfrak{t}^{\vee}.

2.5.
Unrolled quantum abelian Gaiotto–Witten Lie superalgebras

Motivated by earlier unrolled quantizations of Lie (super)algebras
[
CGP15
, §2]
,
[
GPM18
, §3]
,
[
GY22
, §2]
, in this section we define an unrolled quantization of
\mathfrak{t}_{R}
.

Fix a parameter
\hbar\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\pi\sqrt{-1}\mathbb{Z}
and set
q=e^{\hbar}\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}\setminus\{\pm 1\}
. For
x\in\mathbb{C}
, define

q^{x}=e^{\hbar x},\qquad[x]_{q}=\frac{q^{x}-q^{-x}}{q-q^{-1}}.

In order to quantize
\mathfrak{t}_{R}
, we henceforth make the following assumption on the input data from Section
2.3
.

Assumption A
.

For each
1\leq a\leq r
and
1\leq i\leq n
, the quantity
\sumop\displaylimits_{b=1}^{r}\kappa^{ab}Q_{bi}
is an integer.

Definition 2.9
.

The
unrolled quantum group

U_{q}^{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{t}_{R})
is the unital complex superalgebra with even generators
\{Z_{a},K_{a}^{\pm 1}\mid 1\leq a\leq r\}
, odd generators
\{E_{i},F_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq n\}
and defining relations

(11)

K_{a}K_{a}^{-1}=K_{a}^{-1}K_{a}=1,

(12)

[Z_{a},Z_{b}]=[Z_{a},K_{b}]=[K_{a},K_{b}]=0,

(13)

[Z_{a},E_{i}]=Q_{ai}E_{i},\qquad[Z_{a},F_{i}]=-Q_{ai}F_{i},

(14)

K_{a}E_{i}=q^{Q_{ai}}E_{i}K_{a},\qquad K_{a}F_{i}=q^{-Q_{ai}}F_{i}K_{a},

(15)

[E_{i},E_{j}]=[F_{i},F_{j}]=0,

(16)

[E_{i},F_{j}]=\delta_{ij}\frac{\prodop\displaylimits_{a=1}^{r}K_{a}^{\sumop\displaylimits_{b=1}^{r}\kappa^{ab}Q_{bi}}-\prodop\displaylimits_{a=1}^{r}K_{a}^{-\sumop\displaylimits_{b=1}^{r}\kappa^{ab}Q_{bi}}}{q-q^{-1}}.

For each
1\leq i\leq n
, define

(17)

\mathcal{K}_{i}=\prodop\displaylimits_{a=1}^{r}K_{a}^{\sumop\displaylimits_{b=1}^{r}\kappa^{ab}Q_{bi}}\in U_{q}^{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{t}_{R}).

With this notation, relation (
16
) becomes
[E_{i},F_{j}]=\delta_{ij}\frac{\mathcal{K}_{i}-\mathcal{K}_{i}^{-1}}{q-q^{-1}}
. Note also that relations (
15
) imply the nilpotency of
E_{i}
and
F_{i}
:

E_{i}^{2}=F_{i}^{2}=0,\qquad 1\leq i\leq n.

Remark 2.10
.

Suppose that
n=0
. In this case, relations (
13
)-(
16
) are not present and we may take
\hbar\in\pi\sqrt{-1}\mathbb{Z}
, and hence
q=-1
, in Definition
2.9
.
∎

Lemma 2.11
.

For each
1\leq i\leq n
, the elements
\mathcal{Z}_{i}
and
\mathcal{K}_{i}
are central in
U_{q}^{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{t}_{R})
.

Proof.

This follows from equation (
6
), as in the proof of Lemma
2.6
.
∎

View
U_{q}^{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{t}_{R})\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}U_{q}^{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{t}_{R})
as a superalgebra via the bilinear extension of the product

(a_{1}\otimes b_{1})\cdot(a_{2}\otimes b_{2})=(-1)^{\bar{b}_{1}\bar{a}_{2}}a_{1}a_{2}\otimes b_{1}b_{2},

where
a_{i},b_{i}\in U_{q}^{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{t}_{R})
are homogeneous.

Lemma 2.12
.

The assignments

\epsilon(Z_{a})=0,\qquad\epsilon(K_{a}^{\pm 1})=1,\qquad\epsilon(E_{i})=0,\qquad\epsilon(F_{i})=0,

\Delta Z_{a}=Z_{a}\otimes 1+1\otimes Z_{a},\qquad\Delta(K_{a}^{\pm 1})=K_{a}^{\pm 1}\otimes K_{a}^{\pm 1},

\Delta E_{i}=E_{i}\otimes\mathcal{K}_{i}^{-1}+1\otimes E_{i},\qquad\Delta F_{i}=F_{i}\otimes 1+\mathcal{K}_{i}\otimes F_{i},

S(Z_{a})=-Z_{a},\qquad S(K_{a}^{\pm 1})=K_{a}^{\mp 1},\qquad S(E_{i})=-\mathcal{K}_{i}E_{i},\qquad S(F_{i})=-F_{i}\mathcal{K}_{i}^{-1}

extend to a unique Hopf superalgebra structure on
U_{q}^{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{t}_{R})
with counit
\epsilon
, coproduct
\Delta
and antipode
S
.

Proof.

We need to prove that the defining relations of
U_{q}^{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{t}_{R})
are preserved by
\epsilon
,
\Delta
and
S
. We only treat the most involved case, relation (
16
). The verification for the counit is immediate. For the coproduct, note that multiplicativity of
\Delta
implies that
\Delta(\mathcal{K}_{i}^{\pm 1})=\mathcal{K}_{i}^{\pm 1}\otimes\mathcal{K}_{i}^{\pm 1}
. Using this, we compute

\Delta(E_{i}F_{i})=E_{i}F_{i}\otimes\mathcal{K}_{i}^{-1}+\mathcal{K}_{i}E_{i}\otimes\mathcal{K}_{i}^{-1}F_{i}-F_{i}\otimes E_{i}+\mathcal{K}_{i}\otimes E_{i}F_{i}

and

\Delta(F_{i}E_{i})=F_{i}E_{i}\otimes\mathcal{K}_{i}^{-1}+F_{i}\otimes E_{i}-\mathcal{K}_{i}E_{i}\otimes\mathcal{K}_{i}^{-1}F_{i}+\mathcal{K}_{i}\otimes F_{i}E_{i}

so that

\Delta\big([E_{i},F_{i}]-\frac{\mathcal{K}_{i}-\mathcal{K}_{i}^{-1}}{q-q^{-1}}\big)=\big([E_{i},F_{i}]+\frac{\mathcal{K}_{i}^{-1}}{q-q^{-1}}\big)\otimes\mathcal{K}_{i}^{-1}+\mathcal{K}_{i}\otimes\big([E_{i},F_{i}]-\frac{\mathcal{K}_{i}}{q-q^{-1}}\big).

Subtract
\frac{\mathcal{K}_{i}\otimes\mathcal{K}_{i}^{-1}}{q-q^{-1}}
from the first term and add it to the second to get

\Delta\big([E_{i},F_{i}]-\frac{\mathcal{K}_{i}-\mathcal{K}_{i}^{-1}}{q-q^{-1}}\big)=\big([E_{i},F_{i}]-\frac{\mathcal{K}_{i}-\mathcal{K}_{i}^{-1}}{q-q^{-1}}\big)\otimes\mathcal{K}_{i}+\mathcal{K}_{i}\otimes\big([E_{i},F_{i}]-\frac{\mathcal{K}_{i}-\mathcal{K}_{i}^{-1}}{q-q^{-1}}\big),

as required. For the antipode, after noting that the anti-homomorphism property of
S
implies
S(\mathcal{K}^{\pm 1}_{i})=\mathcal{K}_{i}^{\mp 1}
, we compute

\displaystyle S([E_{i},F_{i}]-\frac{\mathcal{K}_{i}-\mathcal{K}_{i}^{-1}}{q-q^{-1}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-S(F_{i})S(E_{i})-S(E_{i})S(F_{i})-\frac{S(\mathcal{K}_{i})-S(\mathcal{K}_{i}^{-1})}{q-q^{-1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\big([E_{i},F_{i}]-\frac{\mathcal{K}_{i}-\mathcal{K}_{i}^{-1}}{q-q^{-1}}\big).

That the above data satisfies the Hopf condition can also be verified directly. For example, writing
m
and
\iota
for multiplication and the unit of
U_{q}^{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{t}_{R})
, respectively, we have

(m\circ(\textup{\text{id}}\otimes S)\circ\Delta)(K_{a})=m(K_{a}\otimes K_{a}^{-1})=1,

which is equal to
\iota(\epsilon(K_{a}))
, and

(m\circ(\textup{\text{id}}\otimes S)\circ\Delta)(E_{i})=m(E_{i}\otimes\mathcal{K}_{i}-1\otimes\mathcal{K}_{i}E_{i})=E_{i}\mathcal{K}_{i}-\mathcal{K}_{i}E_{i}=0,

the last equality following from Lemma
2.11
, which is equal to
\iota(\epsilon(E_{i}))
.
∎

Let
I
and
J
be disjoint subsets of
\{1,\dots,n\}
. Denote by
\textup{\text{sgn}}(I,J)\in\{\pm 1\}
the sign of the
(|I|,|J|)
-shuffle associated to the decomposition
I\sqcup J
. The characteristic function of
I
is

\delta_{l,I}=\begin{cases}1&\mbox{if }l\in I,\\
0&\mbox{if }l\notin I,\end{cases}\qquad 1\leq l\leq n.

Writing
I=\{i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\}
, set
E_{I}=E_{i_{1}}\cdots E_{i_{k}}\in U_{q}^{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{t}_{R})
and, analogously,
F_{I}
and
\mathcal{K}_{I}
.

Lemma 2.13
.

Let
M\subset\{1,\dots,n\}
and
1\leq l\leq n
. The following equalities hold:

(18)

\Delta(E_{M})=\sumop\displaylimits_{\begin{subarray}{c}I,J\subset\{1,\dots,n\}\\
I\sqcup J=M\end{subarray}}\textup{\text{sgn}}(I,J)E_{I}\otimes E_{J}\mathcal{K}^{-1}_{I}.

(19)

\Delta(F_{M})=\sumop\displaylimits_{\begin{subarray}{c}I,J\subset\{1,\dots,n\}\\
I\sqcup J=M\end{subarray}}\textup{\text{sgn}}(I,J)\mathcal{K}_{J}F_{I}\otimes F_{J}.

(20)

E_{l}E_{M}=(1-\delta_{l,M})\textup{\text{sgn}}(\{l\},M)E_{M\sqcup l}.

(21)

[E_{l},F_{M}]=\delta_{l,M}\textup{\text{sgn}}(\{l\},M\setminus\{l\})\frac{\mathcal{K}_{l}-\mathcal{K}_{l}^{-1}}{q-q^{-1}}F_{M\setminus l}.

In equations (
18
) and (
19
), the sums are over disjoint subsets of
I,J\subset\{1,\dots,n\}
whose union is
M
.

Proof.

Equations (
18
) and (
19
) follow from repeated use of the definitions of
\Delta E_{i}
and
\Delta F_{i}
and centrality of
\mathcal{K}_{i}
(Lemma
2.11
). Equations (
20
) and (
21
) follow from repeated application of relations (
15
) and (
16
), respectively.
∎

There are obvious analogues of equations (
20
) and (
21
) with the roles of
E_{i}
and
F_{i}
reversed.

The following quantum counterpart of Lemma
2.7
can be proved directly from Definition
2.9
.

Lemma 2.14
.

Suppose that
R\simeq R^{\prime}\oplus\mathbb{C}
, where
\mathbb{C}
is the trivial representation of
\mathfrak{t}
. Then

U_{q}^{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{t}_{R})\simeq U^{\mathfrak{t}}_{q}(\mathfrak{t}_{R^{\prime}})\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}U(\mathfrak{psl}(1|1))

as Hopf superalgebras.

The quantum counterpart of Lemma
2.8
is compatible with the Hopf structure of

. Indeed, under the same assumptions, the assignment of Lemma
2.8
, supplemented by

, extends to a morphism of Hopf superalgebras

which is an isomorphism onto its image. Here
U_{q}^{H}(\mathfrak{gl}(1|1))
denotes the unrolled quantum group of
\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)
, as defined in
[
GY22
, §2.2]
, with

the grouplike element associated to the central generator

.

2.6.
Modified unrolled quantum groups

We introduce a modified version of the unrolled quantum group of Definition

. We continue to fix

and set

.

Definition 2.15
.

The
modified unrolled quantum group

\widetilde{U}_{q}^{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{t}_{R})
is the unital complex superalgebra with even generators
\{Z_{a},K_{a}^{\pm 1}\mid 1\leq a\leq r\}
, odd generators
\{E_{i},F_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq n\}
and defining relations (
11
)-(

), (

) and (

) and (replacing relations (

))

Continuing to define

\widetilde{U}_{q}^{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{t}_{R})

Proposition 2.16
.

Assume that

extend to a Hopf superalgebra isomorphism

Proof.

This is a direct calculation.
∎

In particular,

for some

precisely when

to be the unital topological

3.
The category of weight
U_{q}^{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{t}_{R})
-modules

We develop the representation theory of
U_{q}^{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{t}_{R})
and its modified variant
\widetilde{U}_{q}^{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{t}_{R})
. Since all results for

3.1.
Weight
U_{q}^{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{t}_{R})
-modules

Let

be a
U_{q}^{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{t}_{R})
-module. A vector

weight vector of weight
\lambda\in\mathfrak{t}^{\vee}
if
Zv=\lambda(Z)v
for all
Z\in\mathfrak{t}
. With respect to the fixed basis
\{Z_{a}\mid 1\leq a\leq r\}
of
\mathfrak{t}

as

,
1\leq a\leq r

Definition 3.1
.

A
weight
U_{q}^{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{t}_{R})
-module
is a finite dimensional
U_{q}^{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{t}_{R})
-module
V
on which
\mathfrak{t}
acts semisimply and
K_{a}v=q^{\lambda_{a}}v
for all weight vectors
v
of weight
\lambda
.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{R}
be the category of weight
U_{q}^{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{t}_{R})
-modules and their
U_{q}^{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{t}_{R})
-linear maps of degree
\bar{0}

\bar{0}

in Section

. The category

is
\mathbb{C}
-linear, abelian and locally finite. The superbialgebra structure of

gives
\mathcal{C}_{R}
a monoidal structure with monoidal unit

the trivial module.

Given

and

of weight

, relations (

and

Call

(resp.

(resp.

) for each

Lemma 3.2
.

Every non-zero object of
\mathcal{C}_{R}
has a homogeneous highest weight vector.

Proof.

Let
V\in\mathcal{C}_{R}
be non-zero and

a non-zero homogeneous vector. Because of the relations (
15
), there exists a unique maximal subset
I\subset\{1,\dots,n\}
with the property that

. By maximality of

is highest weight.
∎

Given

, define
V^{\vee}\in\mathcal{C}_{R}
to be the super vector space
\textup{\text{Hom}}_{\mathbb{C}}(V,\mathbb{C})
with
U_{q}^{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{t}_{R})
-module structure

(x\cdot f)(v)=(-1)^{\bar{f}\bar{x}}f(S(x)v),\qquad v\in V,\;f\in V^{\vee},\;x\in U_{q}^{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{t}_{R}).

Let
\{v_{i}\}_{i}
be a homogeneous basis of

with dual basis
\{v_{i}^{\vee}\}_{i}
. Define

\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\longleftarrow}}{{\operatorname{ev}}}_{V}(f\otimes v)=f(v),\qquad\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\longleftarrow}}{{\operatorname{coev}}}_{V}(1)=\sumop\displaylimits_{i}v_{i}\otimes v_{i}^{\vee}

and

(25)

\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\longrightarrow}}{{\operatorname{ev}}}_{V}(v\otimes f)=(-1)^{\bar{f}\bar{v}}f(\mathcal{K}v),\qquad\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\longrightarrow}}{{\operatorname{coev}}}_{V}(1)=\sumop\displaylimits_{i}(-1)^{\bar{v}_{i}}v_{i}^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{K}^{-1}v_{i},

where we have written

Lemma 3.3
.

The maps (

) and (

) define a pivotal structure on

.

Proof.

We verify

and hence

. Using that

is central (Lemma

), we compute

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\longrightarrow}}{{\operatorname{ev}}}_{V}(E_{i}\cdot(v\otimes f))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\longrightarrow}}{{\operatorname{ev}}}_{V}(E_{i}v\otimes\mathcal{K}_{i}^{-1}f+(-1)^{\bar{v}}v\otimes E_{i}f)

\displaystyle(-1)^{(\bar{v}+1)\bar{f}}(\mathcal{K}_{i}^{-1}f)(\mathcal{K}E_{i}v)+(-1)^{\bar{v}+\bar{v}(\bar{f}+1)}(E_{i}f)(\mathcal{K}v)

3.2.
Verma and simple modules

Given a subset
I\subset\{1,\dots,n\}
, write
Q_{I}\in\mathfrak{t}^{\vee}
for the sum
\sumop\displaylimits_{i\in I}Q_{i}
.

Let
U_{q}^{\mathfrak{t},\geq 0}(\mathfrak{t}_{R})
be the subalgebra of
U_{q}^{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{t}_{R})
generated by
\{Z_{a},K_{a}^{\pm 1}\mid 1\leq a\leq r\}
and
\{E_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq n\}
. For each
(\lambda,\bar{p})\in\mathfrak{t}^{\vee}\times\mathbb{Z}/\penalty 2\mathbb{Z}
, let
\mathbb{C}_{(\lambda,\bar{p})}
be the vector space
\mathbb{C}
concentrated in degree
\bar{p}
with
U_{q}^{\mathfrak{t},\geq 0}(\mathfrak{t}_{R})
-module structure

Z_{a}\cdot 1=\lambda_{a},\qquad K_{a}\cdot 1=q^{\lambda_{a}},\qquad E_{i}\cdot 1=0.

Definition 3.4
.

The
Verma module of highest weight
(\lambda,\bar{p})\in\mathfrak{t}^{\vee}\times\mathbb{Z}/\penalty 2\mathbb{Z}
is

V_{(\lambda,\bar{p})}:=U_{q}^{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{t}_{R})\otimes_{U_{q}^{\mathfrak{t},\geq 0}(\mathfrak{t}_{R})}\mathbb{C}_{(\lambda,\bar{p})}.

The module
V_{(\lambda,\bar{p})}
is generated by the highest weight vector
v_{\varnothing}=1\otimes 1
. The set
\{v_{I}:=F_{I}v_{\varnothing}\mid I\subset\{1,\dots,n\}\}
is a weight basis of
V_{(\lambda,\bar{p})}
with
v_{I}
having weight
(\lambda-Q_{I},\bar{p}+\bar{I})
, where we have written
\bar{I}
for
\overline{|I|}
. Using this basis, we verify that the quantum dimension of a Verma module vanishes:

\textup{\text{qdim}}\,V_{(\lambda,\bar{p})}=\sumop\displaylimits_{I}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\longrightarrow}}{{\operatorname{ev}}}_{V_{(\lambda,\bar{p})}}(v_{I}\otimes v_{I}^{\vee})\\
=(-1)^{\bar{p}}q^{\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}\chi_{i}(\lambda)}\sumop\displaylimits_{I}(-1)^{\bar{I}}=0.

Here and below,
\sumop\displaylimits_{I}
indicates a sum over the power set of
\{1,\dots,n\}
. We will see in Section
3.7
below that, in many cases, there exists a modified trace on
\mathcal{C}_{R}
such that the modified dimension of a generic Verma module is non-zero.

Denote by
\lambda^{\vee}\in\mathfrak{t}^{\vee}
the weight
-\lambda+Q_{\{1,\dots,n\}}
.

Lemma 3.5
.

There is an isomorphism
V^{\vee}_{(\lambda,\bar{p})}\simeq V_{(\lambda^{\vee},\bar{p}+\bar{n})}
in
\mathcal{C}_{R}
.

Proof.

Note that
v^{\vee}_{I}\in V^{\vee}_{(\lambda,\bar{p})}
is of weight
(-\lambda+Q_{I},\bar{p}+\bar{I})
. In particular,
v^{\vee}_{\{1,\dots,n\}}
has weight
(\lambda^{\vee},\bar{p}+\bar{n})
and is of highest weight. It follows that the assignment
v^{\vee}_{\{1,\dots,n\}}\mapsto v_{\varnothing}
extends to an isomorphism
V^{\vee}_{(\lambda,\bar{p})}\xrightarrow{\sim}V_{(\lambda^{\vee},\bar{p}+\bar{n})}
.
∎

Proposition 3.6
.

The module
V_{(\lambda,\bar{p})}
is simple if and only if
\prodop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}[\chi_{i}(\lambda)]_{q}\neq 0
.

Proof.

Let
M\subset V_{(\lambda,\bar{p})}
be a non-zero submodule. By Lemma
3.2
, there exists a highest weight vector
v\in M
. Writing
v=\sumop\displaylimits_{I}c_{I}v_{I}
in the standard weight basis of
V_{(\lambda,\bar{p})}
, we use equation (
21
) to compute

0=E_{l}v=\sumop\displaylimits_{I}\delta_{l,I}\textup{\text{sgn}}(\{l\},I\setminus l)[\chi_{l}(\lambda)]_{q}c_{I}v_{I\setminus l},\qquad 1\leq l\leq n.

It follows that if
c_{I}\neq 0
, then
[\chi_{i}(\lambda)]_{q}=0
for all
i\in I
.

We can now prove the proposition. If
\prodop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}[\chi_{i}(\lambda)]_{q}\neq 0
, then
v
is a non-zero multiple of
v_{\varnothing}
and
M=V_{(\lambda,\bar{p})}
, proving simplicity of
V_{(\lambda,\bar{p})}
. Conversely, if
[\chi_{i}(\lambda)]_{q}=0
for some
1\leq i\leq n
, then
v_{\{i\}}
is a homogeneous highest weight vector which generates a submodule of
V_{(\lambda,\bar{p})}
which is strict, since it does not contain
v_{\varnothing}
.
∎

Definition 3.7
.

A weight
\lambda\in\mathfrak{t}^{\vee}
is called
typical
if

\prodop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}[\chi_{i}(\lambda)]_{q}\neq 0.

Otherwise,
\lambda
is called
atypical
.

In terms of the parameter
\hbar
, atypicality of
\lambda
is the existence of an index
1\leq i\leq n
such that
\chi_{i}(\lambda)\in\frac{\pi\sqrt{-1}}{\hbar}\mathbb{Z}
.

Proposition 3.8
.

There exists a typical weight if and only if the representation
R
has no trivial summands.

Proof.

Note that
R
has a trivial summand if and only if
Q_{i}=0
for some
1\leq i\leq n
. Recall that
\chi_{i}=\kappa^{\vee}(Q_{i},-)
. If
Q_{i}
is zero, then
\chi_{i}(\lambda)=0
for all
\lambda\in\mathfrak{t}^{\vee}
and all weights are atypical. The converse follows from the proceeding lemma applied to the linear functionals
\frac{\hbar}{\pi\sqrt{-1}}\chi_{i}:\mathfrak{t}^{\vee}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
,
1\leq i\leq n
.
∎

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
W
be a finite dimensional complex vector space and
\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{n}\in W^{\vee}
non-zero linear functionals. There exists a vector
w\in W
such that
\ell_{i}(w)\notin\mathbb{Z}
for all
1\leq i\leq n
.

Proof.

Note that
\ell_{i}^{-1}(\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z})=W\setminus\ell_{i}^{-1}(\mathbb{Z})
with
\ell_{i}^{-1}(\mathbb{Z})
a countable union of affine hyperplanes. It follows that

\bigcapop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}\ell_{i}^{-1}(\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z})=W\setminus\bigcupop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}\ell_{i}^{-1}(\mathbb{Z})

is the complement of countably many affine hyperplanes and thus non-empty.
∎

Motivated by Proposition
3.8
, we henceforth work under the following assumption.

Assumption B
.

The representation
R
has no trivial summands.

In terms of roots, Assumption
B
is the statement that
Q_{1},\dots,Q_{n}
are each non-zero. If Assumption
B
does not hold, then, in view of Lemma
2.14
, the study of
U_{q}^{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{t}_{R})
reduces to that of
U_{q}^{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{t}_{R^{\prime}})
and
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\textup{\text{Hom}}_{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathbb{C},R)
copies of
U(\mathfrak{psl}(1|1))
, where
R^{\prime}
is the quotient
R/\penalty\textup{\text{Hom}}_{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathbb{C},R)
. Relative modular categories arising from the representation theory of
\mathfrak{psl}(1|1)
are treated in Section
5.3
. In particular, we will still be able to model Gaiotto–Witten theories which do not satisfy Assumption
B
.

Lemma 3.10
.

Every simple object of
\mathcal{C}_{R}
is a quotient of a Verma module.

Proof.

Let
V\in\mathcal{C}_{R}
be simple and
v\in V
a homogeneous highest weight vector of weight
(\lambda,\bar{p})
. The assignment
v_{\varnothing}\mapsto v
extends to a morphism
V_{(\lambda,\bar{p})}\rightarrow V
in
\mathcal{C}_{R}
which, since
V
is simple, is necessarily an epimorphism.
∎

For each
1\leq i\leq n
, let
\langle v_{\{i\}}\rangle\subset V_{(\lambda,\bar{p})}
be the submodule generated by
v_{\{i\}}
.

Proposition 3.11
.

The module
V_{(\lambda,\bar{p})}
has a unique simple quotient

0\rightarrow\sumop\displaylimits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq n\\
[\chi_{i}(\lambda)]_{q}=0\end{subarray}}\langle v_{i}\rangle\rightarrow V_{(\lambda,\bar{p})}\rightarrow S_{(\lambda,\bar{p})}\rightarrow 0.

Moreover, the dimension of
S_{(\lambda,\bar{p})}
is
2^{n-k}
, where
k
is the number of indices
1\leq i\leq n
such that
[\chi_{i}(\lambda)]_{q}=0
.

Proof.

Observe that the submodule
M
of
V_{(\lambda,\bar{p})}
appearing in the statement of the proposition has weight basis

\{v_{I}\mid[\chi_{i}(\lambda)]_{q}=0\mbox{ for some }i\in I\}.

Let
0\neq N\subsetneq V_{(\lambda,\bar{p})}
be a submodule and
n\in N
a homogeneous highest weight vector. As in the proof of Proposition
3.6
, we have
n=\sumop\displaylimits_{I}c_{I}v_{I}
, where the sum is over those subsets
I\subset\{1,\dots,n\}
which contain at least one index
i
such that
[\chi_{i}(\lambda)]_{q}=0
. Then
N\subset M
and the first statement of the lemma follows. The second statement follows from the equalities
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}V_{(\lambda,\bar{p})}=2^{n}
and
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}M=2^{n-k}(2^{k}-1)
, the latter of which follows from the stated basis of
M
.
∎

Note that
S_{(\lambda,\bar{p})}=V_{(\lambda,\bar{p})}
when
\lambda
is typical.

The above results immediately lead to the following highest weight classification of simple objects of
\mathcal{C}_{R}
.

Theorem 3.12
.

A simple object of
\mathcal{C}_{R}
is isomorphic to exactly one of the following modules:

(1)

V_{(\lambda,\bar{p})}
where
\lambda\in\mathfrak{t}^{\vee}
is typical and
\bar{p}\in\mathbb{Z}/\penalty 2\mathbb{Z}
.

(2)

S_{(\lambda,\bar{p})}
, where
\lambda\in\mathfrak{t}^{\vee}
is atypical and
\bar{p}\in\mathbb{Z}/\penalty 2\mathbb{Z}
.

Lemma 3.13
.

Simple Verma modules are projective and injective objects of
\mathcal{C}_{R}
.

Proof.

Let
f:V\rightarrow W
be an epimorphism and
g:V_{(\lambda,\bar{p})}\rightarrow W
a non-zero morphism. The map
g
is determined by the highest weight vector
g(v_{\varnothing})=w\in W
of weight
(\lambda,\bar{p})
. Surjectivity of
f
implies that
w
has a preimage under
f
, say
v
, which is of weight
(\lambda,\bar{p})
and satisfies
E_{l}v\in\ker f
for each
1\leq l\leq n
. For any constants
c_{I}\in\mathbb{C}
,
I\neq\varnothing
, the vector

v^{\prime}=v+\sumop\displaylimits_{I\neq\varnothing}c_{I}F_{I}E_{I}v\in V

is of weight
(\lambda,\bar{p})
and satisfies
f(v^{\prime})=w
. Using equations (
20
) and (
21
), we compute

E_{l}v^{\prime}=E_{l}v+\sumop\displaylimits_{I\neq\varnothing}c_{I}\big((-1)^{\bar{I}}(1-\delta_{l,I})\textup{\text{sgn}}(\{l\},I)F_{I}E_{I\sqcup l}v+\textup{\text{sgn}}(\{l\},I\setminus\{l\})\delta_{l,I}[\chi_{l}(\lambda)l]_{q}F_{I\setminus l}E_{I}v\big).

We conclude that
E_{l}v^{\prime}=0
if and only if

(-1)^{\bar{I}}\textup{\text{sgn}}(\{l\},I)c_{I}+\textup{\text{sgn}}(\{l\},I)[\chi_{l}(\lambda)]_{q}c_{I\sqcup l}=0

for all subsets
I
which do not contain

. The equations have the unique solution

c_{I}=\frac{(-1)^{\lfloor\frac{|I|}{2}\rfloor}}{\prodop\displaylimits_{i\in I}[\chi_{i}(\lambda)]_{q}},\qquad I\subset\{1,\dots,n\}.

Note that typicality of
\lambda
ensures the non-vanishing of the denominator of each

. A lift of
g
to
V_{(\lambda,\bar{p})}\rightarrow V
is then determined by the assignment
v_{\varnothing}\mapsto v^{\prime}
. It follows that
V_{(\lambda,\bar{p})}
is projective.

Since
\mathcal{C}_{R}
satisfies the assumptions of
[

, Proposition 6.1.3]
, projective and injective objects coincide.
∎

3.3.
Braiding

Motivated by the connection between the Lie superalgebras
\mathfrak{t}_{R}
and
\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)
, as in Lemma
2.8
, and known universal
R
-matrices for
\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)

[
Kul89
,
KT91
]
, we construct in this section a braiding on the category
\mathcal{C}_{R}
.

Let
V,W\in\mathcal{C}_{R}
. Define
\Upsilon_{V,W}\in\textup{\text{End}}_{\mathbb{C}}(V\otimes W)
by

(26)

\Upsilon_{V,W}(v\otimes w)=q^{-\kappa^{\vee}(\lambda_{v},\lambda_{w})}v\otimes w,

where
v\in V
and
w\in W
are of weight
\lambda_{v}
and
\lambda_{w}
, respectively. Let

\tilde{R}=\exp\left((q-q^{-1})\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}E_{i}\mathcal{K}_{i}\otimes F_{i}\mathcal{K}_{i}^{-1}\right).

Because of the relations (
15
), the element
\tilde{R}
is a finite sum and so a well-defined element of
U_{q}^{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{t}_{R})\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}U_{q}^{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{t}_{R})
. Using centrality of
\mathcal{K}_{i}
, we find

\tilde{R}=\sumop\displaylimits_{I}(-1)^{{\bar{I}\choose 2}}(q-q^{-1})^{\bar{I}}E_{I}\mathcal{K}_{I}\otimes F_{I}\mathcal{K}_{I}^{-1},

where
{\bar{I}\choose 2}=\frac{|I|(|I|-1)}{2}
. Finally, let
c_{V,W}\in\textup{\text{Hom}}_{\mathbb{C}}(V\otimes W,W\otimes V)
be the composition
\tau_{V,W}\circ\tilde{R}_{V,W}\circ\Upsilon_{V,W}
, where
\tau
is the standard symmetric braiding on the category of super vector spaces which incorporates the Koszul sign rule and
\tilde{R}_{V,W}
is multiplication by
\tilde{R}
on
V\otimes W
.

Proposition 3.14
.

The morphisms
\{c_{V,W}\mid V,W\in\mathcal{C}_{R}\}
define a braiding on
\mathcal{C}_{R}
.

Proof.

Naturality of
c_{V,W}
is clear, keeping in mind that morphisms of
\mathcal{C}_{R}
are of degree
\bar{0}
; allowing morphisms of degree
\bar{1}
would introduce signs which would invalidate naturality. Invertibility of
c_{V,W}
follows from the observation that
\Upsilon_{V,W}
is invertible and the direct verification that
\tilde{R}
is a unit with inverse

\sumop\displaylimits_{I}(-1)^{{\bar{I}\choose 2}+\bar{I}}(q-q^{-1})^{\bar{I}}E_{I}\mathcal{K}_{I}\otimes F_{I}\mathcal{K}_{I}^{-1}.

To prove
U_{q}^{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{t}_{R})
-linearity of
c_{V,W}
, it suffices to verify that
c_{V,W}
commutes with the action of the generators of
U_{q}^{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{t}_{R})
. That
c_{V,W}
commutes with
Z_{a}
, and hence
K_{a}
, follows from the fact that
c_{V,W}
is a composition of operators of weight zero. We verify that
c_{V,W}
commutes with the action of
E_{l}
; the verification for
F_{l}
is similar. Let
v\in V
and
w\in W
be of weight
(\lambda_{v},\bar{v})
and
(\lambda_{w},\bar{w})
, respectively. Applying the definitions, we find that
E_{l}\cdot c_{V,W}(v\otimes w)
is equal to

\sumop\displaylimits_{I}(-1)^{{\bar{I}\choose 2}+\bar{v}\bar{I}+(\bar{v}+\bar{I})(\bar{w}+\bar{I})}(q-q^{-1})^{\bar{I}}q^{-\kappa^{\vee}(\lambda_{v},\lambda_{w})+\sumop\displaylimits_{i\in I}\chi_{i}(\lambda_{v}-\lambda_{w})-\chi_{l}(\lambda_{v})}E_{l}F_{I}w\otimes E_{I}v+\\
\sumop\displaylimits_{I}(-1)^{{\bar{I}\choose 2}+\bar{v}\bar{I}+(\bar{v}+\bar{I})(\bar{w}+\bar{I})+(\bar{w}+\bar{I})}(q-q^{-1})^{\bar{I}}q^{-\kappa^{\vee}(\lambda_{v},\lambda_{w})+\sumop\displaylimits_{i\in I}\chi_{i}(\lambda_{v}-\lambda_{w})}F_{I}w\otimes E_{l}E_{I}v

and
c_{V,W}(E_{l}\cdot(v\otimes w))
is equal to

\sumop\displaylimits_{I}(-1)^{{\bar{I}\choose 2}+(\bar{v}+1)\bar{I}+(\bar{I}+1+\bar{v})(\bar{I}+\bar{w})}(q-q^{-1})^{\bar{I}}q^{-\chi_{l}(\lambda_{w})+\sumop\displaylimits_{i\in I}\chi_{i}(\lambda_{v}+Q_{l}-\lambda_{w})-\kappa^{\vee}(\lambda_{v}+Q_{I},\lambda_{w})}F_{I}w\otimes E_{I}E_{l}v+\\
\sumop\displaylimits_{I}(-1)^{\bar{v}+{\bar{I}\choose 2}+\bar{v}\bar{I}+(\bar{I}+\bar{v})(\bar{I}+1+\bar{w})}(q-q^{-1})^{\bar{I}}q^{\sumop\displaylimits_{i\in I}\chi_{i}(\lambda_{w}-\lambda_{w}-Q_{l})-\kappa^{\vee}(\lambda_{v},\lambda_{w}+Q_{I})}F_{I}E_{l}w\otimes E_{I}v.

Using equation (
21
), we see that the coefficients of
F_{I}E_{l}w\otimes E_{I}v
in
E_{l}\cdot c_{V,W}(v\otimes w)
and
c_{V,W}(E_{l}\cdot(v\otimes w))
are

(-1)^{{\bar{I}\choose 2}+\bar{v}\bar{I}+(\bar{v}+\bar{I})(\bar{w}+\bar{I})+\bar{I}}(q-q^{-1})^{\bar{I}}q^{-\kappa^{\vee}(\lambda_{v},\lambda_{w})+\sumop\displaylimits_{i\in I}\chi_{i}(\lambda_{v}-\lambda_{w})-\chi_{l}(\lambda_{v})}

and

3.4.
Generic semisimplicity

Let
\chi:\mathfrak{t}^{\vee}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{n}
be the linear map whose
i
th
component is
\chi_{i}(\lambda)
. Viewing the root matrix

as a linear map, equation (

Let
\mathsf{H}
be the image of
\chi
. For each

, let
\mathcal{C}_{R,\xi}\subset\mathcal{C}_{R}
be the full subcategory of modules on which the central element
\mathcal{Z}_{i}
, defined in Lemma

, acts by
\xi_{i}
for each

\mathsf{Y}=\{(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n})\in\mathsf{H}\mid\prodop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}[\xi_{i}]_{q}=0\}.

If

, then Assumption
B
ensures that
\mathsf{H}
is non-trivial. In this case

is a small symmetric subset of
\mathsf{H}
.

Proposition 3.15
.

If
n=0
, then the category
\mathcal{C}_{R}
is semisimple. If
n\geq 1
, then the
\mathsf{H}
-graded category

\mathcal{C}_{R}\simeq\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{\xi\in\mathsf{H}}\mathcal{C}_{R,\xi}

is generically semisimple with small symmetric subset
\mathsf{Y}
. Moreover, for
\xi\in\mathsf{H}\setminus\mathsf{Y}
, a completely reduced dominating set of
\mathcal{C}_{R,\xi}
is

\{V_{(\lambda,\bar{p})}\mid\chi(\lambda)=\xi,\;\bar{p}\in\mathbb{Z}/\penalty 2\mathbb{Z}\}.

Proof.

When
n=0
the category
\mathcal{C}_{R}
is semisimple by construction.

Assume then that
n\geq 1
. If
V\in\mathcal{C}_{R}
is indecomposable, then its weights lie in a single congruence class of
\Lambda_{R}
in
\mathfrak{t}^{\vee}
. Equation (
6
) implies that
\Lambda_{R}\subset\ker\chi
. It follows from this and the definitions of the coproduct and antipode of
U_{q}^{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{t}_{R})
that
\mathcal{C}_{R}\simeq\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{\xi\in\mathsf{H}}\mathcal{C}_{R,\xi}
is an
\mathsf{H}
-grading. Let
\xi\in\mathsf{H}\setminus\mathsf{Y}
and
V\in\mathcal{C}_{R,\xi}
non-zero. By Lemmas
3.2
and
3.10
, the submodule generated by a homogeneous highest weight vector of

is isomorphic to a quotient of
V_{(\lambda,\bar{p})}
for some
\lambda\in\mathfrak{t}^{\vee}
with
\chi(\lambda)=\xi
. Since
\xi\notin\mathsf{Y}
, Proposition
3.6
implies that this submodule is simple and so isomorphic to

. By Lemma
3.13
,

is injective, whence there exists a splitting

with

∎

3.5.
Ribbon structure

Recall that the category
\mathcal{C}_{R}
is pivotal (Lemma
3.3
) and braided (Proposition
3.14
).

Theorem 3.16
.

The natural automorphism of the identity functor
\theta:\textup{\text{id}}_{\mathcal{C}_{R}}\Rightarrow\textup{\text{id}}_{\mathcal{C}_{R}}
whose components are the right partial traces of the braiding,

\theta_{V}=\operatorname{ptr}_{R}(c_{V,V}),\qquad V\in\mathcal{C}_{R}

gives
\mathcal{C}_{R}
the structure of a
\mathbb{C}
-linear ribbon category.

Proof.

The definition of
\theta
as the right partial trace of the braiding ensures that it satisfies the balancing conditions.

Assume first that
n\geq 1
. Give
\mathcal{C}_{R}
the generically semisimple structure of Proposition
3.15
. We are therefore in the setting of
[
GPM18
, Theorem 2]
, which asserts that if
\theta_{V^{\vee}}=\theta_{V}^{\vee}
for all generic simple objects
V\in\mathcal{C}_{R}
, then
\theta
is a ribbon structure on
\mathcal{C}_{R}
. By Theorem
3.12
and the definition of
\mathsf{Y}
, generic simple objects are typical Verma modules. Consider then a (not necessarily simple) Verma module

. Since
\textup{\text{End}}_{\mathcal{C}_{R}}(V_{(\lambda,\bar{p})})\simeq\mathbb{C}
, the morphism
\theta_{V_{(\lambda,\bar{p})}}
is determined by the scalar by which it acts on the highest weight vector

. Using that the off-diagonal terms of the braiding do not contribute to
\theta_{V_{(\lambda,\bar{p})}}v_{\varnothing}
, we compute

\theta_{V_{(\lambda,\bar{p})}}=q^{-\kappa^{\vee}(\lambda,\lambda)+\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}\chi_{i}(\lambda)}\textup{\text{id}}_{V_{(\lambda,\bar{p})}}.

Lemma
3.5
and repeated use of equation (
5
) then gives
\theta_{V_{(\lambda,\bar{p})}^{\vee}}=\theta^{\vee}_{V_{(\lambda,\bar{p})}}
.

When
n=0
, the calculation of the previous paragraph shows that
\theta_{V^{\vee}}=\theta_{V}^{\vee}
for all simple objects
V
. (We may take
\mathsf{X}=\varnothing
, so that all objects are generic.) Semisimplicity of
\mathcal{C}_{R}
then implies that
\theta_{V^{\vee}}=\theta_{V}^{\vee}
for all
V\in\mathcal{C}_{R}
.
∎

3.7.
Unimodularity and modified traces

We henceforth add to Assumptions
A
and
B
the following assumption.

Assumption C
.

The trivial representation appears in
\Lambda^{\bullet}R\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\Lambda^{\bullet}R
with multiplicity one.

In terms of the roots
Q_{1},\dots,Q_{n}
of
R
, Assumption
C
is the statement that the sum
Q_{I}+Q_{J}\in\mathfrak{t}^{\vee}
vanishes if and only if
I=J=\varnothing
.

The proof of the next result is a modification of that of
[
AGP21
, Theorem 3.31]
.

Lemma 3.19
.

If the representation
R
satisfies Assumption
C
, then
\mathcal{C}_{R}
is unimodular.

Proof.

Since projectivity and injectivity coincide in
\mathcal{C}_{R}
, it suffices to prove that the injective hull of the trivial module
\mathbb{C}
is self-dual.

Let
\lambda\in\mathfrak{t}^{\vee}
be typical. Then
V_{(\lambda,\bar{0})}
is projective and
V_{(\lambda,\bar{0})}\otimes V_{(\lambda,\bar{0})}^{\vee}
is a direct sum of projective indecomposables, say
\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{i=0}^{m}P_{i}
. Adjunction and simplicity of
V_{(\lambda,\bar{0})}
give isomorphisms

\textup{\text{Hom}}_{\mathcal{C}_{R}}(\mathbb{C},V_{(\lambda,\bar{0})}\otimes V_{(\lambda,\bar{0})}^{\vee})\simeq\textup{\text{End}}_{\mathcal{C}_{R}}(V_{(\lambda,\bar{0})})\simeq\mathbb{C}.

The injective hull of
\mathbb{C}
therefore appears in
V_{(\lambda,\bar{0})}\otimes V_{(\lambda,\bar{0})}^{\vee}
with multiplicity one; relabeling if necessary, denote this summand by
P_{0}
.

By Lemma
3.5
, the weight of
v_{I}\otimes v^{\vee}_{J}\in V_{(\lambda,\bar{0})}\otimes V_{(\lambda,\bar{0})}^{\vee}
is
-Q_{I}+Q_{J}
. In particular, the vectors

v_{+}=v_{\varnothing}\otimes v_{\{1,\dots,n\}}^{\vee},\qquad v_{-}=v_{\{1,\dots,n\}}\otimes v_{\varnothing}^{\vee}

have weights
Q_{\{1,\dots,n\}}
and
-Q_{\{1,\dots,n\}}
, respectively. Noting that
-Q_{I}+Q_{J}=Q_{\{1,\dots,n\}}
if and only if
Q_{I}+Q_{\{1,\dots,n\}\setminus J}=0
and
-Q_{I}+Q_{J}=-Q_{\{1,\dots,n\}}
if and only if
Q_{\{1,\dots,n\}\setminus I}+Q_{J}=0
, we see that Assumption
C
implies that
v_{\pm}
spans the weight space of weight
\pm Q_{\{1,\dots,n\}}
. Hence,
v_{+}\in P_{i}
and
v_{-}\in P_{j}
for some
i
and
j
. Self-duality of
V_{(\lambda,\bar{0})}\otimes V_{(\lambda,\bar{0})}^{\vee}
and the definitions of
v_{+}
and
v_{-}
then imply that
P_{i}^{\vee}\simeq P_{j}
. On the other hand, equation (
21
) and its counterpart with the roles of
E
and
F
reversed can be used to verify that
F_{\{1,\dots,n\}}v_{+}
and
E_{\{1,\dots,n\}}v_{-}
are
U_{q}^{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{t}_{R})
-invariant vectors of
V_{(\lambda,\bar{0})}\otimes V_{(\lambda,\bar{0})}^{\vee}
. Moreover,

is non-zero; the coefficient of
v_{\{1,\dots,n\}}\otimes v_{\{1,\dots,n\}}^{\vee}
in
F_{\{1,\dots,n\}}v_{+}
is
1
. Similarly,
E_{\{1,\dots,n\}}v_{-}
is non-zero; using equation (
21

v_{\varnothing}\otimes v_{\varnothing}^{\vee}
in

is seen to be

(-1)^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}q^{\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}\chi_{i}(\lambda)}\prodop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}[\chi_{i}(\lambda)]_{q},

which is non-zero by typicality of
\lambda

F_{\{1,\dots,n\}}v_{+}
and
E_{\{1,\dots,n\}}v_{-}
are non-zero elements of
P_{0}

i=j
and
P_{0}
is self-dual.
∎

x
and hence measures the Chern number of the gauge bundle sourced by
\mathcal{O}(x)
. In other words, operators charged under this symmetry are magnetic monopoles. The weights for the topological flavour symmetry group, that is, the allowed Chern numbers of gauge bundles on
S^{2}
, are identified with the cocharacter lattice
\Gamma\simeq\pi_{1}(T_{c,\Gamma})
of
T_{c,\Gamma}
and hence the topological flavour symmetry group is identified with Pontryagin dual
\hat{\Gamma}\simeq\mathfrak{t}^{\vee}/\Gamma^{\vee}\simeq\mathsf{G}
, as claimed

Finite symmetry transformations are implemented by the topological surface operators given by exponentiated integrals of the curvature,

\mathcal{U}_{\bar{g}}[\Sigma]=\exp\bigg(\sqrt{-1}\xi\intop\nolimits_{\Sigma}F\bigg)\,,

where
\Sigma
is a surface in
3
-dimensional spacetime and
\bar{g}=\xi+\Gamma^{\vee}\in\mathsf{G}
. Importantly, if
\Sigma
has non-empty boundary, then the surface operator is bounded by an improperly-quantized Wilson line
[
ALMRP92
,
GKSW15
]
; schematically, this is due to the relation

\mathcal{U}_{\bar{g}}[\Sigma]=\exp\bigg(\sqrt{-1}\xi\intop\nolimits_{\Sigma}F\bigg)=\exp\bigg(\sqrt{-1}\xi\ointop\nolimits_{\partial\Sigma}A\bigg)\,.

The way to interpret this formula is that a Wilson line labelled by the infinitesimal weight
\xi
, that is, an object of the subcategory
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{R,\Gamma,\xi}
, is not gauge invariant on its own unless
\xi\in\Gamma^{\vee}
, that is,
\xi
is a weight of
T_{c,\Gamma}
. If
\xi\notin\Gamma^{\vee}
, then the Wilson line must be attached to the corresponding magnetic surface operator
\mathcal{U}_{\bar{g}}
, whose support
\Sigma
is extra data necessary to define the Wilson line. The attached surface
\Sigma
can be thought of as the worldsheet of the electric analogue of a Dirac string
[
ALMRP92
]
, which is visible to magnetically charged local operators unless the Wilson line is properly quantized.

Line operators that are well-defined without the additional data of a topological surface defect are called
genuine
while those that require this additional data are
non-genuine

[
KS14
, §2]
. A consistent network of such topological surface defects precisely encodes (the holonomies of) a flat
\mathsf{G}
-connection on the complement of the support of these line operators or, equivalently, the Poincaré dual of this network realizes a
\mathsf{G}
-valued cohomology class
\omega
. This is the physical origin of the cohomology classes which decorate objects and morphisms of
\mathsf{C}\mathsf{ob}^{\textnormal{ad}}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{R,\Gamma}}
.

4.2.2.
Screening by gauge vortex lines

To describe the full, non-perturbative, theory the effects of screening by gauge vortex lines must be included. Namely, the physically inequivalent line operators in the non-perturbative theory are identified with
\mathsf{Z}
-orbits of objects in
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{R,\Gamma}
. In particular, simple line operators are labelled by their weights modulo screening, that is, elements of
\mathfrak{t}^{\vee}/\mathsf{Z}_{0}
. We note that all of these simple Wilson lines introduce a monodromy in the bosonic gauge fields, a phenomenon familiar to compact Chern–Simons theories
[
Wit89
, §3.3]
. The monodromy induced by simple line operators is controlled by the map
\mathfrak{t}^{\vee}/\mathsf{Z}_{0}\to T_{\Gamma}
induced by
\kappa^{\sharp}:\mathfrak{t}^{\vee}\to\mathfrak{t}
. The simple line operators in degree
\bar{0}
, that is, those line operators which source a flat connection for the topological flavour symmetry with trivial holonomy, are thus genuine and labelled by elements of the discriminant group
D\simeq\Gamma^{\vee}/\mathsf{Z}_{0}
, as expected.

4.2.3.
Orientations and spin structures

The boundary vertex operator algebras which generalize those of
[
GN23
]
to the present setting have boundary monopole operators with half-integral conformal weights/spins if and only if the lattice
(\Gamma,\kappa_{\textup{\text{eff}}})
is not even. In particular, if
(\Gamma,\kappa_{\textup{\text{eff}}})
is even, then the physical theory is defined on oriented
3
-manifolds; other wise a spin structures is required. Our evenness assumption in Proposition
4.1
therefore matches physics predictions. When the lattice
(\Gamma,\kappa_{\textup{\text{eff}}})
is odd, we expect a spin analogue of Theorem
4.6
and the resulting TQFT
\mathcal{Z}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{R,\Gamma}}
, along the lines of
[
BCGPM14
]
.

Remark 4.9

4.4.

Consider the degenerate setting of Section

Since

.

Appendix A
Reminders on lattices

Let
\kappa
be a non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form on
\mathbb{R}^{r}
. Let
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{r}
be a full rank integral lattice with basis
\{\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{r}\}
. The
r\times r
Gram matrix of
(\Gamma,\kappa)
is
B=(B_{ij})=(\kappa(\gamma_{i},\gamma_{j}))
with inverse
B^{-1}=(B^{ij})
. Integrality of the lattice is the statement that
B
has integer entries. If the diagonals of
B
are even integers, then
\Gamma
is called even. The dual lattice
\Gamma^{\vee}
has basis
\{\gamma^{\vee}_{1},\dots,\gamma^{\vee}_{r}\}
. There is an isomorphism

(34)

\Gamma^{\vee}\rightarrow\{v\in\Gamma\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}\mid\kappa(v,\gamma)\in\mathbb{Z}\;\,\forall\gamma\in\Gamma\},\qquad\gamma_{i}^{\vee}\mapsto\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{r}B^{ij}\gamma_{j}.

Let
B=X\tilde{B}Y
be the Smith normal form of
B
, so that
\tilde{B}=\mbox{diag}(d_{1},\dots,d_{r})
is diagonal with entries satisfying
d_{1}|d_{2}|\cdots|d_{r}
and the left and right Smith multipliers
X
and
Y
, respectively, are in
GL(r,\mathbb{Z})
. The isomorphism (
34
) gives

\sumop\displaylimits_{k=1}^{r}X^{ij}\gamma_{k}\mapsto d_{i}\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{r}Y_{ij}\gamma_{j}^{\vee},

where
X^{-1}=(X^{ij})
. It follows that
\delta_{i}:=\sumop\displaylimits_{j=1}^{r}Y_{ij}\gamma_{j}^{\vee}
generates the
i
th
factor of the discriminant group

D=\operatorname{coker}(\kappa^{\flat}:\Gamma\rightarrow\Gamma^{\vee})\simeq\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{r}\mathbb{Z}/\penalty d_{i}\mathbb{Z}.

Introduction

Throughout this paper, we assume that
R=k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
is the polynomial ring in
n
variables over a field
k
,
\mathfrak{m}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
the unique homogeneous maximal ideal of
R
and
I
a monomial ideal of
R
. We denote, as usual, by
G(I)
the unique minimal set of monomial generators of
I
. If
I
is generated in a single degree, then
I
is said to be
polymatroidal
if for any two elements
u,v\in G(I)
such that
\operatorname{deg}_{x_{i}}(v)<\operatorname{deg}_{x_{i}}(u)
there exists an index
j
with
\operatorname{deg}_{x_{j}}(u)<\operatorname{deg}_{x_{j}}(v)
such that
x_{j}(u/x_{i})\in G(I)
. The polymatroidal ideal
I
is called
matroidal
if
I
is generated by square-free monomials (see
[
6
]
or
[
8
]
). One of the most distinguished polymatroidal ideals is the ideal of
Veronese type
and the other is
transversal
polymatroidal ideals. Consider the fixed positive integers
d
and
1\leq a_{n}\leq\ldots\leq a_{1}\leq d
. The ideal of Veronese type of
R
indexed by
d
and
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
is the ideal
I_{(d;a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})}
which is generated by those monomials
u=x_{1}^{i_{1}}\ldots x_{n}^{i_{n}}
of
R
of degree
d
with
i_{j}\leq a_{j}
for each
1\leq j\leq n
. Let
F
be a non-empty subset of
[n]
and
P_{F}=({x_{i}|i\in F})
is the monomial prime ideal. A transversal polymatroidal ideal is an ideal
I
of the form
I=P_{F_{1}}P_{F_{2}}\ldots P_{F_{r}}
, where
F_{1},\ldots,F_{r}
is a collection of non-empty subsets of
[n]
with
r\geq 1
(see
[
9
]
).

Herzog and Hibi
[
7
]
proved that a polymatroidal ideal
I
is Cohen-Macaulay (i.e. CM) if and only if
I
is a principal ideal, a Veronese ideal, or a square-free Veronese ideal. Note that
I
is CM whenever
R/I
is a CM ring. Vladoiu in
[
21
]
proved that a Veronese type ideal
I
is CM if and only if
\operatorname{Ass}(I)=\operatorname{Min}(I)
. We say that the monomial ideal
I
is almost Cohen-Macaulay (i.e. aCM) when
\operatorname{depth}R/I\geq\dim R/I-1
. It is clear that all CM monomial ideals are aCM. Several authors studied almost Cohen-Macaulay modules (see for example
[
2
,
5
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
16
,
17
,
18
]
).

For a square-free monomial ideal
I
of
R
, we may consider the simplicial complex
\Delta
for which
I=I_{\Delta}
is the Stanley-Reisner ideal of
\Delta
and
K[\Delta]=R/I_{\Delta}
is the Stanley-Reisner ring. Eagon and Reiner
[
3
]
proved that
I
is CM if and only if the square-free Alexander dual
I^{\vee}
has linear resolution.

In this paper we are interested in studying the aCM simplicial complex. Also, we characterize the aCM polymatroidal Veronese type and transversal polymatroidal ideals and we give some examples. For any unexplained notion or terminology, we refer the reader to

[
8
,
20
]
. Several explicit examples were performed with help of the computer algebra system Macaulay2
[
4
]
.

1.
Preliminaries

\Delta

\Delta

\Delta

\Delta^{\vee}=\{V\setminus F\penalty\ |\penalty\ F\notin\Delta\}

I
. The ideal

which is minimally generated by the monomial
u_{i}=\prod_{x_{j}\in\mathfrak{p_{i}}}x_{j}
is called the

of

. For the simplicial complex
\Delta

\operatorname{reg}M:=\operatorname{max}\{j\penalty\ |\penalty\ \beta_{i,i+j}(M)\neq 0\}.

Terai
[

\operatorname{indeg}M:=\operatorname{min}\{j\penalty\ |\penalty\ M_{j}\neq 0\}=\operatorname{min}\{j\penalty\ |\penalty\ \beta_{0,j}(M)\neq 0\}.

It is clear that
\operatorname{reg}M\geq\operatorname{indeg}M
, with equality if and only if

has linear resolution.
Also, Terai proved the following interesting results:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

\operatorname{reg}(I_{\Delta})-\operatorname{indeg}(I_{\Delta})=\dim k[\Delta^{\vee}]-\operatorname{depth}k[\Delta^{\vee}].

In particular

\operatorname{indeg}I_{\Delta}=\mathrm{e}\mathrm{m}\mathrm{b}\dim k[\Delta^{\vee}]-\dim k[\Delta^{\vee}]

(

Let

2.
The aCM polymatroidal ideal

We start this section by the following definition:

Definition 2.1
.

Let
I
be a square-free monomial ideal in
R
. We say that
I
has almost linear resolution precisely when
\operatorname{reg}(I)\leq\operatorname{indeg}(I)+1
.

Remark 2.2
.

Let
I
be a square-free monomial ideal in
R
and
I^{\vee}
be the Alexander dual of
I
. Then, by Theorem
1.1
, we have

\dim(R/I)-\operatorname{depth}(R/I)=\operatorname{reg}(I^{\vee})-\operatorname{indeg}(I^{\vee}).

Hence
I
is aCM if and only if
I^{\vee}
has almost linear resolution.

By the Auslander-Buchsbaum formula, it is known that the monomial ideal
I
is CM if and only if
\operatorname{ht}(I)=\operatorname{pd}(R/I)
. We extend this result:

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
I
be a monomial ideal of
R
. Then
I
is aCM if and only if
\operatorname{ht}(I)\geq\operatorname{pd}(R/I)-1
.

Proof.

Suppose that
I
is aCM. Then
\operatorname{depth}R/I=\dim R/I
or
\operatorname{depth}R/I=\dim R/I-1
. If
\operatorname{depth}R/I=\dim R/I
, then
I
is CM and so
\operatorname{ht}(I)=\operatorname{pd}(R/I)
. Thus we have the result in this case. Let
\operatorname{depth}R/I=\dim R/I-1
. Then by the Auslander-Buchsbaum formula, we have
\operatorname{pd}(R/I)=n-\operatorname{depth}R/I=n-\dim R/I+1=\operatorname{ht}(I)+1
. Therefore the result follows.
Conversely, let
\operatorname{ht}(I)\geq\operatorname{pd}(R/I)-1
. Then
\dim R/I+\operatorname{ht}(I)\geq\dim R/I+\operatorname{pd}(R/I)-1
and so
n-\operatorname{pd}(R/I)\geq\dim R/I-1
. Again by the Auslander-Buchsbaum formula
\operatorname{depth}R/I\geq\dim R/I-1
. Thus
I
is aCM, as required.

∎

Let
I
be a monomial ideal of
R
. Then the big height of
I
, denoted by
\operatorname{bight}(I)
, is
\operatorname{max}\{\operatorname{height}(\mathfrak{p})\penalty\ |\penalty\ \mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I)\}
.

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
I
be an aCM monomial ideal of
R
. Then
\operatorname{bight}(I)-\operatorname{ht}(I)\leq 1
.

Proof.

It is known that
\operatorname{bight}(I)\leq\operatorname{pd}(R/I)
. Thus by using Proposition
2.3
, we have
\operatorname{bight}(I)-\operatorname{ht}(I)\leq\operatorname{pd}(R/I)-\operatorname{ht}(I)\leq 1
.

∎

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
I
be an ideal of Veronese type. Then the following statements are equivalent:

I
is almost Cohen-Macaulay

\operatorname{bight}(I)-\operatorname{ht}(I)\leq 1

Proof.

(i)\Rightarrow(ii):
This is obvious by Corollary
2.4
.

(ii)\Rightarrow(i):
Since
I
is a Veronese type ideal, by Theorem
1.7
, we have

P_{A}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I)\Longleftrightarrow\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}-d+1\geq|A|,\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \sum_{i\notin A}a_{i}\leq d-1.

If
\operatorname{bight}(I)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}-d+1
, then
\operatorname{ht}(I)\geq\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}-d
. By
[
9
, Corollary 5.7]
, we have
\operatorname{depth}(R/I)=\operatorname{max}\{0,d+n-1-\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\}
and so
\operatorname{depth}(R/I)=0
or
\operatorname{depth}(R/I)\neq 0
.
If
\operatorname{depth}(R/I)\neq 0
, then
\dim(R/I)=n-\operatorname{ht}(I)\leq n-\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}+d=\operatorname{depth}(R/I)+1
. Thus I is aCM in this case. If
\operatorname{depth}(R/I)=0
, then
\mathfrak{m}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I)
and so
\operatorname{ht}(I)\geq n-1
. Thus
\dim R/I\leq 1
. Hence
I
is aCM. Now, suppose that
\operatorname{bight}(I)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}-d
. Assume
P_{B}=(x_{i_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{r}})\in\operatorname{Ass}(I)
such that
\operatorname{bight}(I)=\operatorname{ht}(P_{B})
. Then
P_{C}=(x_{i_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{r}},x_{i_{r+1}})
satisfies the conditions in Theorem
1.7
, since
\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}-d+1\geq|C|
and
\sum_{i\notin C}a_{i}\leq\sum_{i\notin B}a_{i}\leq d-1
. Therefore
P_{C}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I)
and this is contrary to
\operatorname{bight}(I)=\operatorname{ht}(P_{B})
. This completes the proof.

∎

Corollary 2.6
.

(Compare with
[
21
, Theorem 3.4]
)
Let
I
be an ideal of Veronese type. Then
I
is Cohen-Macaulay if and only if
\operatorname{bight}(I)=\operatorname{ht}(I)
.

Proof.

If
I
is CM, then it is clear that
\operatorname{bight}(I)=\operatorname{ht}(I)
.
Conversely, suppose that
\operatorname{bight}(I)=\operatorname{ht}(I)
. By
[
9
, Corollary 5.7]
, we have
\operatorname{depth}(R/I)=\operatorname{max}\{0,d+n-1-\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\}
. If
\operatorname{depth}(R/I)=0
, then
\mathfrak{m}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I)
and so
I=\mathfrak{m}^{t}
for some
t\in\mathbb{N}
.
Thus we have the result in this case. Let
\operatorname{depth}R/I\neq 0
and so
\operatorname{depth}(R/I)=d+n-1-\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}
. On the other hand, by using the proof of Theorem
2.5
we conclude that
\operatorname{bight}(I)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}-d+1
and so
\dim R/I=n-\operatorname{ht}(I)=n-\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}+d-1=\operatorname{depth}(R/I)
. Therefore
I
is CM. This completes the proof.

∎

The following example shows that the above theorem is not true for all square-free transversal polymatroidal ideals.

Example 2.7
.

Let
I=(x_{1}x_{4},x_{1}x_{5},x_{1}x_{6},x_{1}x_{7},x_{2}x_{4},x_{2}x_{5},x_{2}x_{6},x_{2}x_{7},x_{3}x_{4},x_{3}x_{5},x_{3}x_{6},x_{3}x_{7})
be a monomial ideal of
R=k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{7}]
. Then
I
is matroidal ideal and has the following primary decomposition,

I=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})(x_{4},x_{5},x_{6},x_{7}).

Therefore
\operatorname{bight}(I)-\operatorname{ht}(I)\leq 1
, but
I
is not aCM.

In the following result we use the ideal
P_{i}=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,\widehat{{x_{i}}},\ldots,x_{n})
, where
x_{i}
in the generated of
P_{i}
is omitted.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
I
be an aCM polymatroidal ideal of degree
d
such that
\mathfrak{m}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I)
. Then
I
is a Veronese type ideal.

Proof.

Since
\mathfrak{m}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I)
, then
\operatorname{depth}(R/I)=0
. Since
I
is aCM, it follows that
\dim(R/I)=1
. Therefore
\operatorname{ht}(I)=n-1
. Since
I
is a polymatroidal ideal,
I
has the following presentation,
I=P_{1}^{d_{1}}\cap P_{2}^{d_{2}}\cap\ldots\cap P_{r}^{d_{r}}\cap\mathfrak{m}^{d}
where
P_{i}=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,\widehat{{x_{i}}},\ldots,x_{n})
and
d_{i}=\operatorname{reg}(I_{P_{i}})
for all
i=1,2,\ldots,r
(see
[
10
, Theorems 2.4, 2.6]
). Since
I(P_{\{i\}})=I:x_{i}^{a_{i}}=P_{i}^{d_{i}}\cap\mathfrak{m}^{d-a_{i}}=P_{i}^{d-a_{i}}
for large
a_{i}
, this implies that
I
is a Veronese type ideal by using
[
15
, Proposition 1.10]
.

∎

The following example says that the assumption of
\mathfrak{m}\in\operatorname{Ass}(I)
in the above proposition is essential.

Example 2.9

Let

is aCM but

In the following example

is aCM, but

Example 2.10

Let

For a monomial ideal

Theorem 2.11
.

Let

(i)

(ii)

(iv)

such that
a_{1}=\dots=a_{n-r}=d
and
a_{n-r+1}=\ldots=a_{n}=d-1
, where

First of all we relabel the non-empty subsets

(\Longleftarrow)
The case

aCM in this case.

If
|F_{1}|=2
, then
c(G_{I})\geq 2c(G_{I})-2
. Therefore
c(G_{I})\leq 2
and
n\leq 4
. If
c(G_{I})=1
, then the result follows as above. If
c(G_{I})=2
, then
n=4
. Since
|F_{1}|=2
, we have
I=P_{F_{1}}P_{F_{2}}
such that
|F_{1}|=|F_{2}|=2
and
F_{1}\cap F_{2}=\emptyset
.

If
|F_{1}|\geq 3
, then
c(G_{I})=1
. Hence the result follows as above in this case.

∎

3.
The aCM simplicial complex

We say that a simplicial complex
\Delta
is almost pure if for every two facets
F_{i}
and
F_{j}
belonging to
\Delta
, one has
||F_{i}|-|F_{j}||\leq 1
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\Delta
be an aCM simplicial complex. Then it is almost pure.

Proof.

Set
\Delta=\langle G_{1},G_{2},\ldots,G_{m}\rangle
, where
G_{1},\ldots,G_{m}
are facets of
\Delta
. Recall that the Stanley-Reisner ideal of
\Delta
has the presentation
I=I_{\Delta}=P_{F_{1}}\cap P_{F_{2}}\cap\ldots\cap P_{F_{m}}
such that
F_{i}=\bar{G_{i}}
, by
[
8
, Lemma 1.5.4]
. The minimal prime ideals of
I_{\Delta}
correspond to the facets
of
\Delta
. Hence
\Delta
is almost pure if and only if
\operatorname{bight}(I)-\operatorname{ht}(I)\leq 1
. Since
\Delta
is aCM, we have the result by Corollary
2.4
.

∎

Let
\Delta
be a simplicial complex on
[n]
of dimension
d-1
. Recall that, for each
0\leq i\leq d-1
, the simplicial complex
\Delta^{(i)}:=\{F\in\Delta|\penalty\ |F|\leq i+1\}
is called the
i
-th skeleton of
\Delta
. In addition, for each
0\leq i\leq d-1
, the pure
i
-th skeleton of
\Delta
is defined to be the pure subcomplex
\Delta(i)
of
\Delta
whose facets are those faces
F
of
\Delta
with
|F|=i+1
(see
[
8
, Section 8.2.6]
).

The simplicial complex
\Delta
is said to be connected if there exists a sequence of facets
F=F_{0},F_{1},\ldots,F_{n-1},F_{n}=E
such that
F_{i}\cap F_{i+1}\neq\emptyset
(see
[
8
, Section 1.5.1]
).

In the following we use
\tilde{H}_{i}(\Delta;k)
which is the ith reduced simplicial homology group of
\Delta
with coefficients in
k
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\Delta
be
(d-1)
-dimensional (
d\geq 3
) simplicial complex such that for all faces
F\in\Delta
and
i<\dim lk_{\Delta}F-1
, one has
\tilde{H}_{i}(lk_{\Delta}F;k)=0
. Then
\Delta
is connected.

Proof.

It is clear that
\tilde{H}_{i}(lk_{\Delta}{\emptyset};k)=0
for
i<\dim lk_{\Delta}{\emptyset}-1
, as
\emptyset
is a face of
\Delta
. Therefore
\tilde{H}_{i}({\Delta};k)=0
for
i<\dim{\Delta}-1
.
Since
\dim{\Delta}\geq 2
, one has
\tilde{H}_{0}({\Delta};k)=0
. Now by applying
[
20
, Proposition 6.2.3]
, we have
\Delta
is connected.

∎

Example 3.3
.

Let
\Delta=\langle\{1,2\},\{4,5\},\{3\}\rangle
be
1
-dimensional simplicial complex on
[5]
. Then
I_{\Delta}=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\cap(x_{3},x_{4},x_{5})\cap(x_{1},x_{2},x_{4},x_{5})
.
Set
I=I_{\Delta}
. Then

I=(x_{1}x_{3},x_{1}x_{4},x_{1}x_{5},x_{2}x_{3},x_{2}x_{4},x_{2}x_{5},x_{3}x_{4},x_{3}x_{5})

is aCM but
\Delta
is not connected.

Theorem 3.4
.

A simplicial complex
\Delta
of dimension
d-1
is aCM if and only if

\tilde{H}_{i}(lk_{\Delta}F;k)=0
, for all
F\in\Delta\setminus\Delta(d-1)
,
i<\dim lk_{\Delta}F
and

\tilde{H}_{i}(lk_{\Delta}F;k)=0
, for all
F\in\Delta(d-1)
,
i<\dim lk_{\Delta}F-1
.

Proof.

Let
\Delta
be aCM, by Hochster’s formula
\tilde{H}_{i-|F|-1}(lk_{\Delta}F;k)=0
for all
F\in\Delta
and
i<d-1
. If
F\in\Delta
, then there is a face
F_{1}
of dimension
d-1
or
d-2
containing
F
such that
\dim lk_{\Delta}F=|F_{1}\setminus F|-1
, since
F_{1}\setminus F\in lk_{\Delta}F
and
\Delta
is almost pure by Lemma
3.1
. Therefore
\dim lk_{\Delta}F=d-|F|-1
for
F\in\Delta(d-1)
and
\dim lk_{\Delta}F=d-|F|-2
for
F\in\Delta\setminus\Delta(d-1)
. Hence
\tilde{H}_{i}(lk_{\Delta}F;k)=0
, for all
F\in\Delta\setminus\Delta(d-1)
,
i<\dim lk_{\Delta}F
and for all
F\in\Delta(d-1)
,
i<\dim lk_{\Delta}F-1
.

Conversely, it is enough to show that
\Delta
is almost pure simplicial complex. Indeed, if
\Delta
be almost pure, then
\dim lk_{\Delta}F=d-|F|-1
or
\dim lk_{\Delta}F=d-|F|-2
for all
F\in\Delta
. Therefore
\tilde{H}_{i-|F|-1}(lk_{\Delta}F;k)=0
for
F\in\Delta
and
i<d-1
.
We proceed by induction on the dimension of
\Delta
. If
\dim(\Delta)=1
, then
\Delta
is almost pure. Assume
d\geq 3
and
F=\{x_{l}\}
be a face of
\Delta
such that
x_{l}
is a vertex. If
F\in\Delta(d-1)
, then by induction hypothesis
lk_{\Delta}F
is almost pure, i.e, if
G
be a facet of
lk_{\Delta}F
, then
|G|=d-2
or
|G|=d-3
, since
\dim lk_{\Delta}F=d-2
. Set
\Gamma:=lk_{\Delta}F
. If
F\in\Delta\setminus\Delta(d-1)
, then by hypothesis for all
G\in\Gamma
and for all
i<\dim lk_{\Gamma}G
,
\tilde{H}_{i}(lk_{\Gamma}G;k)=0
, since
G\cup F\in\Delta\setminus\Delta(d-1)
and
lk_{\Gamma}G=lk_{\Delta}F\cup G
. Hence by
[
20
, Theorem 6.3.12]
,
lk_{\Delta}F
is CM. Thus if
F_{i}
and
F_{j}
be a facets of
\Delta
such that have a vertex
F=\{x_{l}\}
in common , then cardinality of
F_{i}
and
F_{j}
is equal
d
or
d-1
for
F\in\Delta(d-1)
and
|F_{i}|=|F_{j}|
for
F\in\Delta\setminus\Delta(d-1)
. let
H
and
E
be two facets of
\Delta
. Since
\Delta
is connected, there exist facets
F_{1},...,F_{r}
with
H=F_{1}
and
E=F_{r}
such that
F_{i}\cap F_{i+1}\neq\emptyset
for
i=1,...,r
. Since for each
i
,
F_{i}
and
F_{i+1}
have a vertex in common, it follows that the cardinality of
F_{i}
and
F_{j}
is equal to
d
or
d-1
. In particular
|H|
and
|K|
is equal to
d
or
d-1
. Hence
\Delta
is almost pure.

∎

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
\Delta
be
2
-dimensional simplicial complex. Then
\Delta

\Delta
be aCM.

Proof.

(\Leftarrow)
This follows by Lemma
3.2
and Theorem
3.4

(\Rightarrow)
Let
\Delta

. If

. Since

\tilde{H}_{0}({lk_{\Delta}}\emptyset;k)=0

∎

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
\Delta
be an aCM simplicial complex and F be a face of
\Delta
. Then

Set

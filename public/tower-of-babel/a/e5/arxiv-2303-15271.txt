On the Positivity of the Discrete Green’s Function for Unstructured Finite Element Discretizations in Three Dimensions.
A. Miller

Abstract
. The aim of this paper is twofold. First, we prove L p L^{p} estimates for a regularized Green’s function in three dimensions. We then establish new estimates for the discrete Green’s function and obtain some positivity results. In particular, we prove that the discrete Green’s functions with singularity in the interior of the domain cannot be bounded uniformly with respect of the mesh parameter h h . Actually, we show that at the singularity the discrete Green’s function is of order h − 1 h^{-1} , which is consistent with the behavior of the continuous Green’s function. In addition, we also show that the discrete Green’s function is positive and decays exponentially away from the singularity. We also provide numerically persistent negative values of the discrete Green’s function on Delaunay meshes which then implies a discrete Harnack inequality cannot be established for unstructured finite element discretizations.



1.
Introduction

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N=3
, be a convex bounded domain with sufficiently smooth boundary. We consider the following Laplace equation;

(1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}-\Delta u=0,&\quad\text{ in }\Omega\\
u=b,&\quad\text{ on }\partial\Omega.\end{split}

Here we assume the boundary data
b\in C(\partial\Omega)
and
b\geq 0
.
Harmonic functions in the classical sense have been well studied across mathematics, mathematical physics, and stochastic processes. Many results are established for harmonic functions, including: regularity results, Liouville’s Theorem, weak and strong maximum principles, as well as a mean value property for example. An important and well known result for non-negative harmonic functions is the Harnack inequality
[
9
]
. The theorem essentially states that any two values of a non-negative harmonic function are comparable with a constant independent of the particular function itself. The Harnack inequality has been useful in the analysis of fully non-linear elliptic problems. This problem was then extended by Moser in 1961
[
15
]
for uniformly elliptic equations in divergence form with bounded measurable coefficients under the assumption that the eigenvalues of the matrix operator are bounded from above and below. Later in 1980, Krylov and Safonov extended the result to elliptic equations in non-divergence form with bounded measurable coefficients
[
11
]
. In addition there is literature on the Harnack inequality outside of the Euclidean setting on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
including, probability
[
12
]
, graph theory
[
5
]
, and infinite dimensional operators
[
2
]
.

The same cannot be said about the discrete setting, especially when the discretization is less structured. To the author’s knowledge there are only two existing papers on the Harnack inequality in the finite element literature. The first is a paper by Aguilera and Caffarelli
[
1
]
where they adapted the continuous De Giorgi-Nash-Moser iteration technique. However, this technique required the discrete maximum principle as well as other additional geometric constraints on the discretization. In 2014, Leykekhman and Pruitt established a discrete Harnack inequality
[
13
]
on general quasi-uniform meshes in two dimensions only requiring a mesh condition near the boundary of the domain. Their technique involved using a discrete Green’s representation for discrete harmonic functions, upper and lower bounds on both the continuous Green’s function and discrete Green’s functions, as well as applying analytical tools from Sobolev space theory and finite element theory.

Motivated by the Harnack inequality, in this paper we will work towards establishing criteria for the non-negativity of the discrete Green’s function including our main result, showing that the discrete Green’s function cannot be uniformly bounded at the singularity. We will also prove pointwise upper bounds and establish some
L^{p}
estimates for the discrete Green’s function. We will adapt techniques used by Leykekhman and Pruitt in two dimensions, however, some two dimensional techniques do not hold in three dimensions. In this case we introduce new analytical techniques while working to keep the discretization of the original space as general as possible. For example, requiring the stiffness matrix to be a
M
-matrix would give positivity of the discrete Green’s function, but with significant constraints on the triangulation that many mesh refinement schemes don’t follow.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In section
2
we introduce the problem as well as any relative definitions and preliminaries from finite elements and Green’s functions. Section
3
will concern estimates for the regularized Green’s function which have been an important tool for optimal control problems. In section
4
we present some new estimates for the discrete Green’s function including an interesting result that shows the function decays exponentially towards the boundary, analogous to the behavior of the classical Green’s function. Section
5
will present our results on the positivity of the discrete Green’s function including a new analytical technique used at the “singularity”. Finally, in
6
we will also establish numerically persistent negative values for the discrete Green’s function on a polyhedral domain, showing an inconsistency in the finite element approximation of the classical Green’s function, suggesting a discrete Harnack inequality cannot be established for unstructured meshes.

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout the paper we make use of standard Sobolev and finite element notation. Let
0<h<1
and
\{\mathcal{T}_{h}\}
be quasi-uniform shape regular family of triangulations of size
h
for the computational domain
\Omega_{h}\subset\Omega
. We define
V_{h}(\Omega_{h})
(resp.
V_{h}^{0}(\Omega_{h})
for zero boundary condition) the set of all continuous functions on
\Omega_{h}
that are linear (affine) when restricted to each tetrahedra in
\mathcal{T}
. Let
\{\phi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n+m}
denote the standard nodal basis function for
V_{h}(\Omega_{h})
and let
x_{i}
for
i\in\{1,...,n\}
denote interior nodes and
x_{j}
for
j\in\{n+1,...,n+m\}
denote boundary nodes (those that lie on the boundary).

We then define
u_{h}\in V_{h}
to be the solution of the problem,

(2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}(\nabla u_{h},\nabla v_{h})_{\Omega_{h}}=0,&\quad\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}^{0}(\Omega_{h}),\\
u_{h}=I_{h}b,&\quad\text{on}\;\;\partial\Omega_{h}.\end{split}

Where
I_{h}b
is an interpolant of the form

I_{h}b=\sum_{k=n+1}^{n+m}b_{k}\phi_{k}.

Definition 2.1
.

A function
u_{h}
satisfying (
2
) is said to be discrete harmonic on
\Omega_{h}
.

We then have the
classical version of the Green’s function
for problem (
1
).

Definition 2.2
.

Define
G^{z}(x)
to be the solution to the following problem;

(3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}-\Delta G^{z}(x)=\delta^{z}(x),&\quad\text{ in }\Omega\\
G^{z}(x)=0,&\quad\text{ on }\partial\Omega.\end{split}

Here
z\in\Omega
is a fixed point and
\delta^{z}
is the delta distribution.

It is well known that a solution
u
to Laplace’s problem can be represented by Green’s function representation. We will then require upper and lower bounds on the Green’s function. The proof of the following result for general second order elliptic equations can be found in
[
10
]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
G^{z}(x)
denote the Green’s function of the Laplace equation on
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N\geq 3
. Then the following estimates hold,

\displaystyle|G^{z}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\begin{array}[]{ll}C|x-z|^{2-N},&\text{}\end{array}

(4b)

\displaystyle|\nabla^{\alpha}_{x}\nabla^{\beta}_{z}G^{z}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq{C|x-z|^{2-N-|\alpha|-|\beta|}},\quad|\alpha|+|\beta|\geq 1.

There also exists a lower bound for the Green’s function which can be found in section 7 of
[
14
]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
G^{z}(x)
denote the Green’s function of the Laplace equation on
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N\geq 3
. Then the following estimate holds for some
C>0
,

(5)

C|x-z|^{2-N}\leq G^{z}(x).

We note that when
N=3
we have the following estimates on
G^{z}(x)
,

(6)

C_{1}|x-z|^{-1}\leq G^{z}(x)\leq C_{2}|x-z|^{-1}.

The
discrete Green’s function
,
G_{h}^{z}(x)
, is simply the finite element approximation to
G^{z}(x)
and can be defined in the following way.

Definition 2.5
.

The discrete Green’s function with singularity at
z
is the function
G_{h}^{z}(x)\in V_{h}^{0}(\Omega_{h})
satisfying

(7)

(\nabla G_{h}^{z},\nabla v_{h})_{\Omega}=v_{h}(z),\;\;\;\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}^{0}(\Omega_{h}).

From this definition it is clear to see that
G_{h}^{z}(x)
is also symmetric by substituting
v_{h}
with
G_{h}^{x}(z)
as is
G^{z}(x)
.

The Ritz projection of a function
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
onto
V_{h}^{0}(\Omega_{h})
defined by

(8)

(\nabla R_{h}u,\nabla v_{h})_{\Omega_{h}}=(\nabla u,\nabla v_{h})_{\Omega_{h}},\quad\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}^{0}(\Omega_{h}).

We will also make use of the discrete Laplace operator.

Definition 2.6
.

The discrete Laplace operator,
\Delta_{h}:V_{h}^{0}(\Omega_{h})\rightarrow V_{h}^{0}(\Omega_{h})
, is defined by

(9)

(-\Delta_{h}v_{h},\chi)_{\Omega}=(\nabla v_{h},\nabla\chi)_{\Omega},\quad\forall\chi\in V_{h}^{0}(\Omega_{h}).

In the analysis we will also need a
regularized Green’s function
. To define this we first need a
regularized delta function
.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
\tilde{\delta}^{z}(x)\geq 0
denote a regularized delta function supported in an element
\tau_{0}
containing
z
defined by,

(10)

(\tilde{\delta}^{z},v_{h})_{\Omega}=(\tilde{\delta}^{z},v_{h})_{\tau_{0}}=v_{h}(z),\;\;\;\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}(\Omega_{h}).

For an explicit construction of
\tilde{\delta}^{z}
see
[
3
]
.
In addition we also have the following estimates for
\tilde{\delta}^{z}
. There exists a constant
C
independent of
z
such that,

(11)

\|\tilde{\delta}^{z}\|_{W^{s}_{p}(\tau_{0})}\leq Ch^{-s-N(1-\frac{1}{p})},\;\;\;1\leq p\leq\infty,\;\;\;s=0,1.

Therefore we get in particular;
\|\tilde{\delta}^{z}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C
,
\|\tilde{\delta}^{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq Ch^{-\frac{3}{2}}
, and
\|\tilde{\delta}^{z}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq Ch^{-3}
.

Remark 2.8
.

Due to the construction of
\tilde{\delta}^{z}
we have
\tilde{\delta}^{z}>0
which implies
\int_{\tau_{0}}\tilde{\delta}^{z}\text{ dx}=1
. We will use this observation periodically throughout the analysis. See
[
3
]
.

Additionally, defining the
L^{2}

projection
of a function
u\in L^{2}(\Omega)
onto
V_{h}^{0}(\Omega_{h})
by,

(12)

(P_{h}u,v_{h})_{\Omega}=(u,v_{h})_{\Omega},\quad\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}^{0}(\Omega_{h}).

Then it has been shown that the
L^{2}
projection of
\tilde{\delta}^{z}
has exponential decay away from
z
. The following is Lemma 7.2 in
[
19
]
.

Lemma 2.9
.

Given a quasi-uniform triangulation
\Omega_{h}
there exists constants
C
and
c
such that

(13)

|P_{h}\tilde{\delta}^{z}(x)|\leq Ch^{-N}e^{-\frac{c|x-z|}{h}}.

Using
\tilde{\delta}^{z}
, we are able to define a regularized Green’s function.

Definition 2.10
.

The regularized Green’s function,
\widetilde{G}^{z}(x)
, is defined by

(14)

\displaystyle\begin{split}-\Delta\widetilde{G}^{z}&=\tilde{\delta}^{z},\quad\text{in }\Omega\\
\widetilde{G}^{z}&=0,\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{split}

Integration by parts gives us an alternative definition for
\widetilde{G}^{z}
.

(15)

(\nabla\widetilde{G}^{z},\nabla v_{h})_{\Omega}=(\tilde{\delta}^{z},v_{h})_{\Omega}=v_{h}(z),\quad\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}^{0}.

We note that
G_{h}^{z}=R_{h}\widetilde{G}^{z}=R_{h}G^{z}
. This implies that
G_{h}^{z}(x)
is the finite element approximation of both
G^{z}(x)
and
\widetilde{G}^{z}(x)
.

3.
Estimates for the Regularized Green’s Function

In this section we shall present some results concerning the regularized Green’s function. It is important to note that these results are valid for
N=3
only. Some of the results are used in the analysis of the discrete Green’s function while others are of independent interest.

Our first lemma shows that
\widetilde{G}^{z}
cannot be uniformly bounded in
h
.

Lemma 3.1
.

There exists a constant
C
independent of
h
and
z
such that

(16)

(\widetilde{G}^{z},\tilde{\delta}^{z})_{\Omega}\geq Ch^{-1}.

Proof.

Let
\tau_{0}
be the element such that
\tilde{\delta}^{z}
is supported in. Then by Green’s representation, for
x\in B_{z}(ch)
,
c>1
such that
B_{z}(ch)\subset\Omega
,

\displaystyle\widetilde{G}^{z}(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{\tau_{0}}G(z,x)\tilde{\delta}^{z}(x)\text{ dx}

\displaystyle\geq C\int_{\tau_{0}}|x-z|^{-1}\tilde{\delta}^{z}(x)\text{ dx}

\displaystyle\geq Ch^{-1}\int_{\tau_{0}}\tilde{\delta}^{z}(x)\text{ dx}=Ch^{-1}.

Therefore we get,

(\widetilde{G}^{z},\tilde{\delta}^{z})_{\Omega}=\int_{\tau_{0}}\widetilde{G}^{z}\tilde{\delta}^{z}\text{ dx}\geq Ch^{-1}\int_{\tau_{0}}\tilde{\delta}^{z}\text{ dx}=Ch^{-1}.

∎

We now show an upper bound, independent of
h
, on the
L^{2}
inner product of
\tilde{\delta}^{z}
and
\widetilde{G}^{z}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

There exists a constant
C
independent of
h
and
z
such that

(17)

(\widetilde{G}^{z},\tilde{\delta}^{z})_{\Omega}\leq Ch^{-1}.

Proof.

Let
\tau_{0}
be the element such that
\tilde{\delta}^{z}
is supported in. Then by Green’s representation we have,

\displaystyle\widetilde{G}^{z}(y)=\int_{\tau_{0}}G(y,x)\tilde{\delta}^{z}(x)\text{ dx}

\displaystyle\leq C_{G}\int_{\tau_{0}}|x-y|^{-1}\tilde{\delta}^{z}(x)\text{ dx}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\tilde{\delta}^{z}\|_{L^{\infty}(\tau_{0})}\cdot\int_{\tau_{0}}|x-y|^{-1}\text{ dx}.

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{-3}\int_{\tau_{0}}|x-y|^{-1}\text{ dx}\text{ by }\eqref{eq: regdelta estimates}.

Now we shall convert to spherical coordinates with
\rho=|x-y|
and choose
c>1
such that
\tau_{0}\subset B_{ch}(z)
to get,

\displaystyle Ch^{-3}\int_{B_{ch}(z)}|x-y|^{-1}\text{ dx}

\displaystyle=Ch^{-3}\int_{0}^{ch}\rho^{-1}\rho^{2}\;d\rho

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{-1}.

Therefore
\widetilde{G}^{z}(y)\leq Ch^{-1}
for
|x-y|\leq ch
. Thus,

(\widetilde{G}^{z},\tilde{\delta}^{z})_{\Omega}=\int_{\tau_{0}}\widetilde{G}^{z}\tilde{\delta}^{z}\text{ dx}\leq Ch^{-1}\underbrace{\int_{\tau_{0}}\tilde{\delta}^{z}\text{ dx}}_{=1}=Ch^{-1}.

∎

As a result of Lemmas
3.1
and
3.2
we get the following corollary.

Corollary 3.3
.

There exist constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
independent of
h
and
z
with
x\in B_{ch}(z)
,
c>1
such that
B_{z}(ch)\subset\Omega
, we have

(18)

C_{1}h^{-1}\leq\widetilde{G}^{z}(x)\leq C_{2}h^{-1},

and in particular we see,

(19)

C_{1}h^{-1}\leq\widetilde{G}^{z}(z)\leq C_{2}h^{-1},

The next lemma concerns an upper bound for
\nabla\widetilde{G}^{z}
in the
L^{\infty}
norm.

Lemma 3.4
.

There exists a constant
C
independent of
z
and
h
such that

(20)

\|\nabla\widetilde{G}^{z}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq Ch^{-2}.

Proof.

Let
y\in\Omega_{h}
. We shall observe two cases.

Case 1:
Let
c>1
and
y\in\Omega_{h}\backslash B_{ch}(z)
.

We appeal to a Green’s function representation;

\displaystyle|\nabla\widetilde{G}^{z}(y)|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\int_{\tau_{0}}\nabla_{y}G(y,x)\tilde{\delta}^{z}(x)\text{ dx}\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\tau_{0}}|\nabla_{y}G(y,x)||\tilde{\delta}^{z}(x)|\text{ dx}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\tau_{0}}|x-y|^{-2}|\tilde{\delta}^{z}(x)|\text{ dx}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{-2}\|\tilde{\delta}^{z}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{h})},\;\;\;\text{since }|x-y|>ch,

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{-2}.

Where the last line follows from estimate (
11
). We shall now proceed to case 2.

Case 2:
Let
c>1
and
y\in B_{ch}(z)
.

We again appeal to Green’s representation as in case 1 in which we still have,

|\nabla\widetilde{G}^{z}(y)|\leq C\int_{\tau_{0}}|x-y|^{-2}|\tilde{\delta}^{z}(x)|\text{ dx}.

Now we will apply the
L^{\infty}
estimate (
11
) and the Hölder ’s inequality to get,

\int_{\tau_{0}}|x-y|^{-2}|\tilde{\delta}^{z}(x)|\text{ dx}\leq Ch^{-3}\int_{\tau_{0}}|x-y|^{-2}\text{ dx}.

We shall now apply spherical coordinates with
|x-y|=\rho
to get,

\displaystyle Ch^{-3}\int_{\tau_{0}}|x-y|^{-2}\text{ dx}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{-3}\int_{B_{ch}(z)}|x-y|^{-2}\text{ dx}

\displaystyle=Ch^{-3}\int_{0}^{ch}\rho^{-2}\rho^{2}\;d\rho

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{-3}h=Ch^{-2}.

Since
y
is arbitrary, the result follows.

∎

Our next result concerns the
L^{1}
norm of the regularized Green’s function which requires a different proof technique. The result may be of independent interest, potentially in showing best approximation results.

Lemma 3.5
.

There exists a constant
C
independent of
h
and
z
such that

(21)

\|\nabla\widetilde{G}^{z}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{h})}\leq C.

Proof.

We shall proceed using a dyadic decomposition of
\Omega_{h}
. With out loss of generality, assume
diam(\Omega_{h})\leq 1
. Then let
d_{j}=2^{-j}
and

\Omega_{h}=\Omega_{*}\cup\bigcup\limits_{j=0}^{J}\Omega_{j},

where

\displaystyle\Omega_{*}

\displaystyle=\{x\in\Omega_{h}\mid|x-z|\leq Kh\},

\displaystyle\Omega_{j}

\displaystyle=\{x\in\Omega_{h}\mid d_{j+1}\leq|x-z|\leq d_{j}\},

with
K
possibly chosen later and
J
such that
2^{-J}\leq Kh\leq 2^{-J+1}
. This then gives

\|\nabla\widetilde{G}^{z}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{h})}\leq\underbrace{\sum_{j=0}^{J}\|\nabla\widetilde{G}^{z}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{j})}}_{(1)}+\underbrace{\|\nabla\widetilde{G}^{z}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{*})}}_{(2)}.

We will now estimate
(1)
and
(2)
individually. For
(1)
applying the Hölder ’s inequality first,

\displaystyle\|\nabla\widetilde{G}^{z}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{j})}

\displaystyle\leq d_{j}^{3}\|\nabla\widetilde{G}^{z}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{j})}

\displaystyle\leq d_{j}^{3}\int_{\tau_{0}}C|x-z|^{-2}|\tilde{\delta}^{z}|\text{ dx}

\displaystyle\leq Cd_{j}^{3}d_{j+1}^{-2}\int_{\tau_{0}}|\tilde{\delta}^{z}|\text{ dx}

\displaystyle\leq Cd_{j}^{3}d_{j}^{-2}\leq Cd_{j}.

Where the second line follows from Lemma
3.4
. Therefore,

\sum_{j=0}^{J}\|\nabla\widetilde{G}^{z}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{j})}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{J}Cd_{j}\leq C.

To estimate
(2)
we again appeal to the Hölder ’s inequality and Lemma
3.4
to get,

\displaystyle\|\nabla\widetilde{G}^{z}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{*})}

\displaystyle\leq K^{3}h^{3}\|\nabla\widetilde{G}^{z}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{*})}

\displaystyle\leq CK^{3}h^{3}h^{-2}

\displaystyle\leq Ch.

In fact, as
h\rightarrow 0
, we see that
\|\nabla\widetilde{G}^{z}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{*})}\rightarrow 0
.

∎

4.
Estimates for the Discrete Green’s Function

In the analysis we will require the following inverse estimate from Theorem (4.5.11) in
[
4
]
, for
v_{h}\in V_{h}
, there exists
C>0
such that

(22)

\|\nabla v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq Ch^{-1}\|v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Using the regularized Green’s function we shall show an estimate for
G_{h}^{z}(z)
and then give a resulting corollary.

Lemma 4.1
.

There exists a constant
C
independent of
h
and
z
such that

(23)

G_{h}^{z}(z)\leq Ch^{-1}.

Proof.

Indeed,

\displaystyle G_{h}^{z}(z)

\displaystyle=(G_{h}^{z},\tilde{\delta}^{z})_{\Omega_{h}}

\displaystyle=(\nabla G_{h}^{z},\nabla G_{h}^{z})_{\Omega_{h}}

\displaystyle=\|\nabla\widetilde{G}^{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}^{2}-\|\nabla\widetilde{G}^{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}^{2}+\|\nabla G_{h}^{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}^{2}

\displaystyle=(\widetilde{G}^{z},\tilde{\delta}^{z})_{\Omega_{h}}+\int_{\Omega_{h}}(|\nabla G_{h}^{z}|^{2}-|\nabla\widetilde{G}^{z}|^{2})\text{ dx}

\displaystyle=(\widetilde{G}^{z},\tilde{\delta}^{z})_{\Omega_{h}}-\int_{\Omega_{h}}(|\nabla\widetilde{G}^{z}|^{2}-|\nabla G_{h}^{z}|^{2})\text{ dx}

\displaystyle=(\widetilde{G}^{z},\tilde{\delta}^{z})_{\Omega_{h}}-(\nabla(\widetilde{G}^{z}-G_{h}^{z}),\nabla(\widetilde{G}^{z}+G_{h}^{z}))_{\Omega_{h}}

\displaystyle=(\widetilde{G}^{z},\tilde{\delta}^{z})_{\Omega_{h}}-(\nabla(\widetilde{G}^{z}-G_{h}^{z}),\nabla\widetilde{G}^{z})_{\Omega_{h}}

\displaystyle=(\widetilde{G}^{z},\tilde{\delta}^{z})_{\Omega_{h}}-(\nabla(\widetilde{G}^{z}-G_{h}^{z}),\nabla(\widetilde{G}^{z}-G_{h}^{z}))_{\Omega_{h}}

\displaystyle=(\widetilde{G}^{z},\tilde{\delta}^{z})_{\Omega_{h}}-\|\nabla(\widetilde{G}^{z}-G_{h}^{z})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}

\displaystyle\leq(\widetilde{G}^{z},\tilde{\delta}^{z})_{\Omega_{h}}=(\widetilde{G}^{z},\tilde{\delta}^{z})_{\tau_{0}}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{-1}.

We have used the Galerkin orthogonality and Lemma
3.2
.
∎

We then have a resulting corollary.

Corollary 4.2
.

There exists a constant
C
such that,

(24)

\|\nabla G_{h}^{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}\leq\|\nabla\widetilde{G}^{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}\leq Ch^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

Proof.

This follows from showing, by Galerkin orthogonality,

\int_{\Omega_{h}}|\nabla G_{h}^{z}|^{2}-|\nabla\widetilde{G}^{z}|^{2}\text{ dx}=-\|\nabla(\widetilde{G}^{z}-G_{h}^{z})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}\leq 0.

To show that,

(25)

\|\nabla\widetilde{G}^{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}\leq Ch^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

Observe,

\displaystyle\|\nabla\widetilde{G}^{z}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{h}}\nabla\widetilde{G}^{z}\cdot\nabla\widetilde{G}^{z}\text{ dx}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{h}}\widetilde{G}^{z}\cdot\tilde{\delta}^{z}\text{ dx},\;\;\;\text{by }\eqref{eq: variational reggreen},

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{-1},\;\;\;\text{by }\eqref{eq: L2 inner reggreen and regdelta}.

Therefore the result follows.
∎

Next we shall show the discrete Green’s function decays exponentially for values away from the singularity.

Lemma 4.3
.

For
x\in\Omega
there exists constants
C
and
c
independent of
z
and
h
such that

(26)

G_{h}^{z}(x)\leq Ch^{-5}e^{-\frac{c|x-z|}{h}}.

Proof.

We shall use the fact that the discrete Laplace operator is bounded as well as estimate (
13
). First note that by (
7
), (
10
), (
12
), and (
9
) we have,

(27)

-\Delta_{h}G_{h}^{z}=P_{h}\tilde{\delta}^{z}.

Therefore let
x\in\Omega
be fixed and arbitrary with associated regularized delta function
\tilde{\delta}^{x}
, then

\displaystyle G_{h}^{z}(x)=(\tilde{\delta}^{x},G_{h}^{z})_{\tau_{x}}

\displaystyle=(-\Delta_{h}\tilde{\delta}^{x},P_{h}\tilde{\delta}^{z})_{\tau_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\tilde{\delta}^{x}\|_{L^{1}(\tau_{x})}\|\Delta_{h}P_{h}\tilde{\delta}^{z}\|_{L^{\infty}(\tau_{x})}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{-2}\|P_{h}\tilde{\delta}^{z}\|_{L^{\infty}(\tau_{x})}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{-5}e^{-\frac{|x-z|}{h}}.

∎

We first note that this result does not give us insight to the positivity of
G_{h}^{z}(x)
. However, in contrast to Lemma
4.1
we gain a significantly sharper bound on the discrete Green’s function for
x
outside of a ball around the singularity.

Next, we present an upper bound for the discrete Green’s function in the
W^{1}_{\infty}(\Omega)
norm.

Lemma 4.4
.

There exists a constant
C
independent of
h
such that

(28)

\|G_{h}^{z}\|_{W^{1}_{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq Ch^{-2}.

Proof.

We first note that by Theorem 4.5.11 in
[
4
]
we have the following,

\|G_{h}^{z}\|_{W^{1}_{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq Ch^{-\frac{3}{2}}\|G_{h}^{z}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}.

Then since
G_{h}^{z}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
we have,

\|G_{h}^{z}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C|G_{h}^{z}|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}.

Therefore applying Corollary
4.2
we see,

\displaystyle Ch^{-\frac{3}{2}}|G_{h}^{z}|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{-\frac{3}{2}}\|\nabla\widetilde{G}^{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{h})}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{-2},

and we arrive at the desired result.

∎

This result is most notable close to the singularity as it may suggest, due to Lemma
4.1
, that the discrete Green’s function will have a persistent negative value. Otherwise, this result along with Lemma
4.1
can be thought of as an analog to the upper bounds of the continuous elliptic Green’s function since this result implies,

\|\nabla G_{h}^{z}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq Ch^{-2}.

Additionally, since Corollary
4.2
and Lemma
4.4
give,

\displaystyle\|\nabla G_{h}^{z}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{-\frac{1}{2}}\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle\|\nabla G_{h}^{z}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{-2}.

We can apply the interpolation inequality found in
[
8
]
to get the following Corollary.

Corollary 4.5
.

There exists a constant
C
independent of
h
and
z
such that

(29)

\|\nabla G_{h}^{z}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{h})}\leq Ch^{\frac{3}{p}-2},\quad\text{for}\;\;2\leq p\leq\infty.

5.
On the Positivity of the Discrete Green’s Function in 3D

It is well known that the classical Green’s function for (
4
) is positive. However, the discrete Green’s function arising from the finite element method is not as straightforward. For
N=2
, Drǎgǎnescu, et al.
[
7
]
, provided a counterexample showing on general meshes the discrete Green’s function may have a persistent negative value. For these meshes, the negative value and the singularity were both an
O(h)
away from the boundary of the mesh. Later for
N=2
, Leykekhman and Pruitt
[
13
]
, showed on a quasi-uniform, shape regular mesh of a polygonal domain, that if the singularity is of
O(1)
away from the boundary the discrete Green’s function must eventually be non-negative. With the help of a solution representation using the discrete Green’s function they were then able to show the non-negativity of discrete harmonic functions which then led to the establishment of a discrete Harnack type inequality. In this section we will work towards showing the positivity of the discrete Green’s function in three dimensions.

Positivity at the singularity is a simple consequence of Definition
2.5
. By choosing
v_{h}=G_{h}^{z}
we see that,

G_{h}^{z}(z)=(\nabla G_{h}^{z},\nabla G_{h}^{z})_{\Omega}=|G_{h}^{z}|^{2}_{H^{1}(\Omega)}>0.

Considering the estimate (
6
) for the classical Green’s function one would expect that
G_{h}^{z}(z)\rightarrow\infty
as
h\rightarrow 0
. In fact, in Section 5 of
[
7
]
, it was conjectured that,

G_{h}^{z}(z)=O(h^{2-N})\quad\forall z\;\text{(up to the boundary)}.

We shall show that this holds for
N=3
, however, the proof can be easily adapted for
N>3
.

Theorem 5.1
.

There exists a constant C independent of
h
and
z
such that

(30)

G_{h}^{z}(z)\geq Ch^{-1}.

Proof.

We again note that by (
7
), (
10
), (
12
), and (
9
) we have,

(31)

-\Delta_{h}G_{h}^{z}=P_{h}\tilde{\delta}^{z},

where
-\Delta_{h}
is the discrete Laplace operator. Since for all
v_{h}\in V_{h}^{0}(\Omega_{h})
we have,

(-\Delta_{h}G_{h}^{z},v_{h})_{\Omega}=\underbrace{(\nabla G_{h}^{z},\nabla v_{h})_{\Omega}=(\tilde{\delta}^{z},v_{h})_{\Omega}}_{=v_{h}(z)}=(P_{h}\tilde{\delta}^{z},v_{h})_{\Omega}.

Now, for all
v_{h}\in V_{h}^{0}
we have by the Poincaré’s inequality,

(32)

C_{1}(v_{h},v_{h})_{\Omega}\leq(\nabla v_{h},\nabla v_{h})_{\Omega}=(-\Delta_{h}v_{h},v_{h})_{\Omega}.

In addition, the inverse estimate (
22
) gives,

(33)

(-\Delta_{h}v_{h},v_{h})_{\Omega}=(\nabla v_{h},\nabla v_{h})_{\Omega}\leq C_{2}h^{-2}(v_{h},v_{h})_{\Omega}.

Combining (
32
) and (
33
) we see that,

C_{1}\leq\dfrac{(v_{h},-\Delta_{h}v_{h})_{\Omega}}{(v_{h},v_{h})_{\Omega}}\leq C_{2}h^{-2}.

This then implies the following estimate which holds by Lemma
A.1
, given in the appendix with proof,

(34)

C_{2}^{-1}h^{2}\leq\dfrac{(v_{h},(-\Delta_{h})^{-1}v_{h})_{\Omega}}{(v_{h},v_{h})_{\Omega}}\leq C_{1}^{-1}.

Now by (
31
) and (
34
) we have,

\displaystyle G_{h}^{z}(z)

\displaystyle=(\tilde{\delta}^{z},G_{h}^{z})_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=(P_{h}\tilde{\delta}^{z},G_{h}^{z})_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=(P_{h}\tilde{\delta}^{z},(-\Delta_{h})^{-1}P_{h}\tilde{\delta}^{z})_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\geq Ch^{2}\|P_{h}\tilde{\delta}^{z}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Here the key step is to apply the
L^{2}
-projection to
\tilde{\delta}^{z}
in order to project our regularized delta function onto
V_{h}^{0}(\Omega)
.

Therefore it remains to show
\|P_{h}\tilde{\delta}^{z}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\geq Ch^{-3}
. Indeed, by (
12
),

\|P_{h}\tilde{\delta}^{z}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=(P_{h}\tilde{\delta}^{z},P_{h}\tilde{\delta}^{z})_{\Omega}=(P_{h}\tilde{\delta}^{z},\tilde{\delta}^{z})_{\Omega}=P_{h}\tilde{\delta}^{z}(z),

where
P_{h}\tilde{\delta}^{z}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\gamma_{i}\phi_{i}(x)
by definition of
P_{h}\tilde{\delta}^{z}\in V_{h}^{0}(\Omega_{h})
using the nodal basis.
Now for
z=x_{k}
,
x_{k}
an interior node, we get,

P_{h}\tilde{\delta}^{x_{k}}(x_{k})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\gamma_{i}\phi_{i}(x_{k})=\gamma_{k}
. In addition, for
j=1,...,n
we have,

(P_{h}\tilde{\delta}^{x_{k}},\phi_{j})_{\Omega}=(\tilde{\delta}^{x_{k}},\phi_{j})_{\Omega}=\phi_{j}(x_{k})=\delta_{kj},

and therefore we get,

(P_{h}\tilde{\delta}^{x_{k}},\phi_{j})_{\Omega}=(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\gamma_{i}\phi_{i}(x),\phi_{j})_{\Omega}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\gamma_{i}(\phi_{i},\phi_{j})_{\Omega}=\delta_{kj},\quad j=1,...,n.

Interpreting the system as a matrix equation,

M\vec{\gamma}=\vec{e_{k}},

where
M
is the
Mass matrix
with entries
M_{ij}=(\phi_{i},\phi_{j})_{\Omega}
,
\vec{\gamma}^{\top}=(\gamma_{1},...,\gamma_{n})^{\top}
, and
\vec{e_{k}}^{\top}=(0,...,0,1,0,...,0)^{\top}
with
1
in the
k^{th}
position. Which then implies,

(35)

\gamma_{k}=\vec{e_{k}}^{\top}\vec{\gamma}=\vec{e_{k}}^{\top}M^{-1}\vec{e_{k}}.

Now by properties of the Mass matrix for finite elements in three dimensions, see for example, Rannacher
[
16
]
(page 121), there exists constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
independent of
h
such that for any
\vec{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{\vec{0}\}
,

c_{1}h^{3}\leq\dfrac{\vec{v}^{\top}M\vec{v}}{\vec{v}^{\top}\vec{v}}\leq c_{2}h^{3}.

Therefore by Lemma
A.1
we see,

(36)

c_{2}^{-1}h^{-3}\leq\dfrac{\vec{v}^{\top}M^{-1}\vec{v}}{\vec{v}^{\top}\vec{v}}\leq c_{1}^{-1}h^{-3}.

Thus, choosing
\vec{v}=\vec{e_{k}}
and substituting (
35
) into (
36
) we have shown
\|P_{h}\tilde{\delta}^{z}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\geq Ch^{-3}
and the result follows.

∎

We shall now show that the discrete Green’s function is positive away from the singularity. The proof technique used will give us insight into the scaling of
G_{h}^{z}
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Assume for some constant
K
that
|x-z|\geq Kh^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Then
G_{h}^{z}(x)>0
.

Proof.

We know using (
10
) that,

\displaystyle G_{h}^{z}(x)

\displaystyle=(G_{h}^{z},\tilde{\delta}^{x})_{\tau_{x}}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{(G_{h}^{z}-G^{z},\tilde{\delta}^{x})_{\tau_{x}}}_{I_{1}}+\underbrace{(G^{z},\tilde{\delta}^{x})_{\tau_{x}}}_{I_{2}},

where
\tau_{x}
is the element containing the support of
\tilde{\delta}^{x}
. Clearly
I_{2}
is positive due to the properties of
G^{z}
and
\tilde{\delta}^{x}
, however, we can find a lower bound for
I_{2}
. Observe,

\displaystyle(G^{z},\tilde{\delta}^{x})_{\tau_{x}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\tau_{x}}G^{z}(y)\cdot\tilde{\delta}^{x}(y)\text{ dy}

\displaystyle\geq C\int_{\tau_{x}}|x-y|^{-1}\cdot\tilde{\delta}^{x}(y)\text{ dy},\quad\text{by }\eqref{eq: lower bound greens}

\displaystyle\geq C(|z-x|+h)^{-1}\int_{\tau_{x}}\tilde{\delta}^{x}(y)\text{ dy},\quad\text{since }|x-y|\leq ch

\displaystyle\geq C|z-x|^{-1}>0.

Now we shall show that
I_{1}\rightarrow 0
as
h\rightarrow 0
, therefore showing
G_{h}^{z}(x)\approx C|z-x|^{-1}
when
|x-z|\geq Kh^{\frac{1}{2}}
, showing that the behavior of
G_{h}^{z}(x)
is consistent with the classical Green’s function. Indeed, we will use Theorem 6.1 where
K
is the constant
C_{7}
in estimate (6.3) in
[
18
]
which states: if
|x-z|\geq Kh
, then

(37)

|G_{h}^{z}(x)-G^{z}(x)|\leq\dfrac{Ch^{2}\ln\big(\frac{|x-z|}{h}\big)}{|x-z|^{3}}.

Adopting the notation
\omega=\frac{|x-z|}{h}
and using the assumption (
37
) becomes,

(38)

\displaystyle\begin{split}|G_{h}^{z}(x)-G^{z}(x)|&\leq\dfrac{C\ln(\omega)\omega^{-2}}{|x-z|}\\
&\leq\dfrac{C\ln(\omega)\omega^{-2}}{Kh^{\frac{1}{2}}}.\end{split}

Additionally
\omega\geq Kh^{-\frac{1}{2}}
and observe that
\ln(\omega)\omega^{-2}
is decreasing for
\omega\geq\sqrt{e}
. Therefore it’s maximum must occur at the left endpoint of
\omega\geq Kh^{-\frac{1}{2}}
for
h
small enough. Now (
38
) becomes,

\displaystyle|G_{h}^{z}(x)-G^{z}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{C\ln(\omega)\omega^{-2}}{Kh^{\frac{1}{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{C\ln(Kh^{-\frac{1}{2}})K^{-2}h}{Kh^{\frac{1}{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\ln(Kh^{-\frac{1}{2}})h^{\frac{1}{2}},

where
C\ln(Kh^{-\frac{1}{2}})h^{\frac{1}{2}}\rightarrow 0
as
h\rightarrow 0
.
Thus,
G_{h}^{z}(x)>0
.

∎

7.
Conclusions

In this paper, we have established some sharp pointwise bounds on the discrete Green’s function. In particular we showed that in three dimensions the discrete Green’s function cannot be uniformly bounded in
h
at the singularity. Furthermore, we show that away from the singularity the discrete Green’s function is positive and decays exponentially to the boundary. We also prove analogous results for the discrete Green’s function showing similarities to the continuous Green’s function. Numerically, we show that on an a convex domain with sufficiently smooth boundary on an unstructured mesh the discrete Green’s function has persistent negative values. The results are summarized in the following table.

Additionally we establish
L^{p}
estimates for the gradient of both the discrete and regularized Green’s function for
2\leq p\leq\infty
. Furthermore, we prove a
L^{1}
estimate for
\nabla\widetilde{G}^{z}
. At this time we suspect a similar result can be shown for
G_{h}^{z}
. Additionally, establishing a lower bound for
\nabla G_{h}^{z}
close to the singularity is an immediate priority. We believe these results could be used in establishing best approximation results in optimal control problems.

Going forward our goal is to theoretically establish the existence of a persistent negative value for the discrete Green’s function in three dimensions on unstructured meshes. We are also interested in providing a constructive mesh for which negativity occurs as well as detailing weaker mesh restrictions that give positivity. Finally, another direction for investigation may include the extension of the Harnack inequality to the inhomogeneous case and to parabolic or more general elliptic equations on non-smooth domains.

Appendix A
Miscellaneous Proofs

In the paper
Variational Bounds on the Entries of a Inverse Matrix

[
17
]
the authors conclude the following result, which was critical in the proof of Theorem
5.1
so we felt as though it were important to include a proof of the result.

Lemma A.1
.

Assume
\vec{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{\vec{0}\}
and the matrix
A
is symmetric. Then the following inequalities are equivalent.

(39)

\beta\leq\dfrac{\vec{v}^{\top}A\vec{v}}{\vec{v}^{\top}\vec{v}}\leq\alpha\;\;\iff\;\;\frac{1}{\alpha}\leq\dfrac{\vec{v}^{\top}A^{-1}\vec{v}}{\vec{v}^{\top}\vec{v}}\leq\frac{1}{\beta}.

Proof.

We shall start with the left hand result and show it is equivalent to the right hand side. The reverse implication follows immediately. That is, assume

\beta\leq\dfrac{\vec{v}^{\top}A\vec{v}}{\vec{v}^{\top}\vec{v}}\leq\alpha,

holds for all
\vec{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{\vec{0}\}
. Then the result also holds for
A^{-\frac{1}{2}}\vec{v}
. Therefore by the symmetry of
A
we have,

\displaystyle\beta\leq\dfrac{(A^{-\frac{1}{2}}\vec{v})^{\top}AA^{-\frac{1}{2}}\vec{v}}{(A^{-\frac{1}{2}}\vec{v})^{\top}A^{-\frac{1}{2}}\vec{v}}\leq\alpha

\displaystyle\implies\beta\leq\dfrac{\vec{v}^{\top}A^{-\frac{1}{2}}A^{\frac{1}{2}}\vec{v}}{(A^{-\frac{1}{2}}\vec{v})^{\top}A^{-\frac{1}{2}}\vec{v}}\leq\alpha

\displaystyle\implies\beta\leq\dfrac{\vec{v}^{\top}A^{-\frac{1}{2}}A^{\frac{1}{2}}v}{\vec{v}^{\top}A^{-\frac{1}{2}}A^{-\frac{1}{2}}\vec{v}}\leq\alpha

\displaystyle\implies\beta\leq\dfrac{\vec{v}^{\top}\vec{v}}{\vec{v}^{\top}A^{-1}\vec{v}}\leq\alpha

\displaystyle\implies\frac{1}{\alpha}\leq\dfrac{\vec{v}^{\top}A^{-1}\vec{v}}{\vec{v}^{\top}\vec{v}}\leq\frac{1}{\beta}.

∎

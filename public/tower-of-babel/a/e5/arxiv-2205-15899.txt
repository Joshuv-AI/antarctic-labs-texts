The continuity of p p -rationality and a lower bound for p ′ p^{\prime} -degree characters of finite groups Thanks: We thank Attila Maróti, Gabriel Navarro, and Jay Taylor for useful conversations on topics related to this work. Special thanks to Gunter Malle for his many helpful comments and corrections that have greatly improved the exposition in the paper. Finally, we thank the referee for careful reading and helpful comments on the paper.
Nguyen Ngoc Hung

Abstract
. Let p p be a prime and G G a finite group. We propose a strong bound for the number of p ′ p^{\prime} -degree irreducible characters of G G in terms of the commutator factor group of a Sylow p p -subgroup of G G . The bound arises from a recent conjecture of Navarro and Tiep [ NT21 ] on fields of character values and a phenomenon called the continuity of p p -rationality level of p ′ p^{\prime} -degree characters. This continuity property in turn is predicted by the celebrated McKay-Navarro conjecture [ Nav04 ] . We achieve both the bound and the continuity property for p = 2 p=2 .

1.
Introduction

For a character
\chi
of a finite group
G
, let
{\mathbb{Q}}(\chi)
denote
the smallest field containing all the values of
\chi
, which is
often referred to as the
field of values
of
\chi
. The
smallest positive integer
f
such that

{\mathbb{Q}}(\chi)\subseteq{\mathbb{Q}}_{f}:={\mathbb{Q}}(e^{2\pi i/f})
is called the

conductor
of
\chi
and denoted by
c(\chi)
. (This
c(\chi)

used to be called the Feit number of
\chi

[
Nav18
, p. 52]
,
but the name conductor appears more in recent literature.) To
measure how
p
-rational a character
\chi
is, one considers

c(\chi)_{p}
– the largest
p
-power divisor of
c(\chi)
. The
nonnegative integer

\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\chi)=\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}_{p}(\chi):=\log_{p}(c(\chi)_{p})

is called the
p
-
rationality level
of
\chi
. In a certain
sense, a character is more
p
-irrational if it has higher

p
-rationality level. For instance, the usual
p
-rational
characters (
[
Isa76
, Definition 6.29]
) are precisely
characters of level
0
, and the so-called almost
p
-rational
characters are the ones of level at most
1
(see

[
INS12
,
HMM22
]
).

The
p
-rationality level arises naturally in the context of the
celebrated McKay-Navarro (MN) conjecture
[
Nav04
]
(see
Section
2
for more details). Let
{\mathrm{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(G)
and

{\mathrm{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}({\mathbf{N}}_{G}(P))
respectively denote the set of
p^{\prime}
-degree
irreducible characters of
G
and of the normalizer
{\mathbf{N}}_{G}(P)
of a
Sylow
p
-subgroup
P
of
G
. The MN conjecture implies that the
number of characters in
{\mathrm{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(G)
and
{\mathrm{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}({\mathbf{N}}_{G}(P))
at
each
p
-rationality level is the same, therefore refining the
well-known McKay conjecture
[
McK72
]
, which asserts that

|{\mathrm{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(G)|=|{\mathrm{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}({\mathbf{N}}_{G}(P))|
.

This paper has two objectives that do not appear at first sight to
be connected. The first is to propose a
p
-local lower bound for
the number
|{\mathrm{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(G)|
and the second is to present a
phenomenon that we call the
continuity of
p
-rationality
of
irreducible characters of
p^{\prime}
-degree. The second is predicted by
the MN conjecture (see Theorem
2.5
) and the
first is implicitly suggested by the second (see Theorem

8.3
). At the end, we prove both for the prime
p=2
.

Conjecture 1.1
.

Let
p
be a prime,
G
a finite group and
\alpha\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{\geq 2}
.
If
G
has an irreducible
p^{\prime}
-degree character of
p
-rationality
level
\alpha
, then
G
has irreducible
p^{\prime}
-degree characters of
every level from
2
to
\alpha
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Conjecture
1.1
holds true for
p=2
.

The works of G. Navarro and P. H. Tiep
[
NT19
]
and of
G. Malle
[
Mal19
]
already show that, when the commutator
factor group of a Sylow
2
-subgroup
P
of
G
has exponent at
least 4, the largest
2
-rationality level of an odd-degree
irreducible character of
G
is precisely
\log_{2}(\exp(P/P^{\prime}))
(see
Theorem
2.2
). Therefore, Theorem

1.2
can be reformulated as:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
p=2
,
G
a finite group and
P\in{\mathrm{Syl}}_{p}(G)
. Suppose that

\log_{p}(\exp(P/P^{\prime}))=\alpha\geq 2
. Then
G
has irreducible

p^{\prime}
-degree characters of every
p
-rationality level from
2
to

\alpha
(and none of level higher than
\alpha
).

Conjecture
1.1
together with the recent works

[
NT21
]
on fields of values of
p^{\prime}
-degree characters
and
[
HMM22
]
on bounding the number of almost

p
-rational
p^{\prime}
-degree characters imply the following lower bound
for the number of
p^{\prime}
-degree irreducible characters in a finite
group.

Conjecture 1.4
.

Let
p
be a prime,
G
a finite group of order divisible by
p
,
and
P\in{\mathrm{Syl}}_{p}(G)
. Then

|{\mathrm{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(G)|\geq\frac{\exp(P/P^{\prime})-1}{p-1}+2\sqrt{p-1}-1.

Thanks to Theorem
1.3
, we have the following.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
G
be finite group and
P\in{\mathrm{Syl}}_{2}(G)
. Then

|{\mathrm{Irr}}_{2^{\prime}}(G)|\geq\exp(P/P^{\prime}).

Moreover, the equality occurs if
and only if
P
is cyclic and self-normalizing.

Remark 1.6
.

In general the continuity property fails at level
1
. On one hand,
as
{\mathbb{Q}}_{n}={\mathbb{Q}}_{2n}
for any odd positive integer
n
, there is no
character with
2
-rationality level
1
. On the other hand, for odd

p
, the simple group
PSL_{2}(8)
, for instance, has three
irreducible characters of
3
-rationality level
2
but none of
level
1
. The existence of level

when
p>2
depends on the
action of
{\mathbf{N}}_{G}(P)/P
on
P/P^{\prime}
and the precise connection is not
known to us at the time of this writing. Other than this exception,
Conjecture
1.1
and Isaacs-Navarro’s Conjecture

2.1
would provide complete control of the

p
-rationality level of
p^{\prime}
-degree characters of a finite group,
solely in terms of a Sylow
p
-subgroup.

Remark 1.7
.

The bound
|{\mathrm{Irr}}_{2^{\prime}}(G)|\geq\exp(P/P^{\prime})
can be refined by partitioning
the set
{\mathrm{Irr}}_{2^{\prime}}(G)
according to the
2
-rationality level, and
this indeed is how we came up with the bound in Conjecture

. When
\exp(P/P^{\prime})=2^{\alpha}\geq p^{2}
, as shown
in Section
8
,
G
will have at least two

2
-rational odd-degree irreducible characters and, for each

, at least
2^{\beta-1}
odd-degree irreducible
characters of
2
-rationality level
\beta
.

Remark 1.8
.

Given that the McKay conjecture is now known for
p=2
(thanks to
the work of G. Malle and B. Späth
[
MS16
]
), Theorem

of course can also be argued by assuming that

P\trianglelefteq\,G
, and so it is reduced to showing that the conjugacy class
number
k(AM)
of the semidirect product of an odd-order group

acting on an abelian
2
-group

is at least
\exp(A)
. However,
even in this much simpler situation, we are not aware of any proof
that does not use the idea of
2
-rationality level outlined in the
previous remark.

Remark 1.9
.

The bound proposed in Conjecture
1.4

substantially improves the known bound

established by G. Malle and A. Maróti in

[

]
. In fact, Malle-Maróti’s bound was improved
in
[

]
where the same bound is obtained but for
the number of almost
p
-rational

-degree characters instead.
Taking this into account, Conjecture

is
reduced to showing that, when
|G|
is divisible by
p
, the number
of
p^{\prime}
-degree irreducible characters of
G
of level 2 or higher is
at least
\frac{\exp(P/P^{\prime})-1}{p-1}-1
.

Remark 1.10
.

We reduce Conjecture
1.1
to a
problem on almost simple groups (Theorem
5.3
), and
then make use of the classification of finite simple groups to
achieve the results for

. Along the way, it is shown that the

-rationality level of a

-degree irreducible character

of a finite group of Lie type in characteristic not equal to

determined solely by the

-part of a semisimple element
s
(in

, under

, see Theorem

. This may be of independent interest and will be

-rationality of character values. In

]
to confirm a conjecture on

-degree characters

2.
The McKay-Navarro conjecture and
p
-rationality

In this section we explain that the continuity of
p
-rationality of

p^{\prime}
-degree characters indeed follows from the McKay-Navarro
conjecture.

Our notation is fairly standard, following
[
Isa76
,
Nav18
]

on character theory and
[
Car85
,
DM91
]
on
representation theory of finite groups of Lie type.

Let
p
be a prime and
G
a finite group. As usual let
{\mathrm{Irr}}(G)

denote the set of all irreducible ordinary characters of
G
, and
recall that

{\mathrm{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(G):=\{\chi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(G):p\nmid\chi(1)\}.

The well-known
McKay conjecture
[
McK72
]
asserts that the number of irreducible

p^{\prime}
-degree characters of
G
equals to that of the
p
-local
subgroup
{\mathbf{N}}_{G}(P)
, which is the normalizer of some

P\in{\mathrm{Syl}}_{p}(G)
; that is,

|{\mathrm{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(G)|=|{\mathrm{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}({\mathbf{N}}_{G}(P))|.

In
[
Nav04
]
, G. Navarro brought Galois automorphisms into
the McKay conjecture by proposing that there exists a bijection from

{\mathrm{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(G)
to
{\mathrm{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}({\mathbf{N}}_{G}(P))
that commutes with the
action of a certain subgroup
\mathcal{H}
(depending on
p
) of the
Galois group
{\it Gal}({\mathbb{Q}}_{|G|}/{\mathbb{Q}})
. The subgroup
\mathcal{H}

consists of automorphisms that send every root of unity

\zeta\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{|G|}
of order not divisible by
p
to
\zeta^{q}
,
where
q
is a certain fixed power of
p
, see
[
Nav04
]
and

[
Nav18
, Conjecture 9.8]
. This refinement of the McKay
conjecture has now become the McKay-Navarro conjecture, also known
as the Galois-McKay conjecture.

Assume the validity of the MN conjecture. We then have

|\{\chi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(G):\chi^{H}=\chi\}|=|\{\chi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}({\mathbf{N}}_{G}(P)):\chi^{H}=\chi\}|

for every subgroup
H
of
\mathcal{H}
. Since

{\it Gal}({\mathbb{Q}}_{|G|}/{\mathbb{Q}}_{p^{\alpha}|G|_{p^{\prime}}})
is contained in

\mathcal{H}
for every nonnegative integer
\alpha\leq\log_{p}|G|_{p}
,
the conjecture implies that the number of characters at every

p
-rationality level
\alpha
in
{\mathrm{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(G)
and

{\mathrm{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}({\mathbf{N}}_{G}(P))
is the same:

|\{\chi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(G):\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\chi)=\alpha\}|=|\{\psi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}({\mathbf{N}}_{G}(P)):\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\psi)=\alpha\}|.

Following
[
IN01
]
, for each
\alpha\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
, we
denote by
\sigma_{\alpha}
the automorphism in
Gal({\mathbb{Q}}^{ab}/{\mathbb{Q}})

that fixes roots of unity of order not divisible by
p
and maps
every
p
-power root of unity
\xi
to
\xi^{1+p^{\alpha}}
. Abusing
notation, when working with a specific group
G
, we also use

\sigma_{\alpha}
for the restriction of this automorphism to

{\mathbb{Q}}_{|G|}
.

Let
|G|=p^{a}m
with
(p,m)=1
and
a\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
. Note that

\sigma_{\alpha}
is a generator for the cyclic group

Gal({\mathbb{Q}}_{p^{a}m}/{\mathbb{Q}}_{p^{\alpha}m})
when
\alpha\geq 2
or
p>2
.
Note also that there does not exist
\chi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}_{2^{\prime}}(G)
that is
not
2
-rational but is
\sigma_{1}
-invariant. (Assume so. When

p=2
, the fixed field of
\sigma_{1}
in
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p^{a}m}
is
{\mathbb{Q}}_{m}
when

a\leq 2
and is
{\mathbb{Q}}_{m}(\sqrt{2}i)
when
a\geq 3
. In any case,
we have
{\mathbb{Q}}(\chi)\subseteq{\mathbb{Q}}_{m}(\sqrt{2}i)
. This contradicts
the main result of
[
ILNT19
]
stating that the field of values of
a non-2-rational odd-degree irreducible character always contains
the imaginary unit
i
.) We have shown that, if

\chi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(G)
is not
p
-rational, then

\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\chi)
is precisely the smallest positive integer

\alpha
such that
\chi
is
\sigma_{\alpha}
-invariant.

The relevance of
p
-rationality level was noticed even before the
birth of the MN conjecture. In 2001, motivated by Brauer’s Problem
12
[
Bra63
]
, M. Isaacs and G. Navarro
[
IN01
]

proposed the following, but in the language of the Galois
automorphism
\sigma_{\alpha}
.

Conjecture 2.1

(Isaacs-Navarro)
.

Let
p
be a prime,
G
a finite group and
P\in{\mathrm{Syl}}_{p}(G)
. Let

\alpha\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
. Then
\exp(P/P^{\prime})\leq p^{\alpha}
if and only if
the
p
-rationality level of every
p^{\prime}
-degree irreducible
characters of
G
is at most
\alpha
.

This has been confirmed for
p=2

[
NT19
,
Mal19
]
. In fact, the ‘if’ direction of the
conjecture has been confirmed for all
p

[
NT19
, Theorem
B]
and the ‘only if’ direction has been reduced to
almost quasisimple groups
[
NT19
, Theorem C]
.

Theorem 2.2

(Malle-Navarro-Tiep)
.

Let
p=2
,
G
a finite group, and
P\in{\mathrm{Syl}}_{p}(G)
. Suppose that

\exp(P/P^{\prime})=p^{\alpha}
. Then

(i)

If
\alpha=1
then every
p^{\prime}
-degree irreducible characters of

G
has
p
-rationality level at most
1
.

(ii)

If
\alpha\geq 2
then the maximal level of an irreducible
p^{\prime}
-degree character of

G
is
\alpha
.

The following simple statement is equivalent to the combination of
Conjectures
1.1
and
2.1
, as
shown below.

Conjecture 2.3
.

Let
p
be a prime,
G
a finite group and
\alpha\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{\geq 2}
.
Let
M
be a subgroup of
G
of
p^{\prime}
-index. Then
G
has an
irreducible
p^{\prime}
-degree characters of
p
-rationality level
\alpha

if and only if
M
does.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
p
be a prime. Let
G
be a finite group and
N\trianglelefteq\,G
. Let

\theta\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(N)
and
\chi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(G)
such that
\theta
is a
constituent of
\chi_{N}
.

(i)

Assume that
[G:N]
is not divisible by
p
and
\log_{p}|G|_{p}\geq\alpha\geq 1
. Then
\chi
is
\sigma_{\alpha}
-invariant if and only if
\theta

is. Furthermore,
\chi
is
\sigma
-invariant for every
\sigma\in{\it Gal}({\mathbb{Q}}_{|G|}/{\mathbb{Q}}_{p^{\alpha}}|G|_{p^{\prime}})
if and only if
\theta
is.

(ii)

If
[G:N]
is not divisible by
p
and
\max\{\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\theta),\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\chi)\}\geq 2
,
then
\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\theta)=\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\chi)
.

(iii)

If
p\nmid\chi(1)
and
\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\chi)\geq 1
, then
\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\theta)\leq\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\chi)
.

Proof.

The first statement of part (i) is
[
NT21
, Lemma 4.2]
and the second statement is proved similarly.
For parts (ii) and (iii), set
\alpha:=\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\chi)
and

\beta:=\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\theta)
.

We now prove (ii). First suppose that
\alpha\geq 2
. Then
\beta\leq\alpha
by part (i). Assume by contradiction that
\beta<\alpha
.
Then
\theta
would be
\sigma
-invariant for every
\sigma\in{\it Gal}({\mathbb{Q}}_{|G|}/{\mathbb{Q}}_{p^{\alpha-1}|G|_{p^{\prime}}})
, and thus so is
\chi

by again (i). It would follow that
\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\chi)\leq\alpha-1
, a
contradiction. The case
\beta\geq 2
is similar.

For part (iii) we note that the Galois group

{\it Gal}({\mathbb{Q}}_{|G|}/{\mathbb{Q}}_{p^{\alpha}|G|_{p^{\prime}}})
permutes the

G
-conjugates of
\theta
. Since
p\nmid\chi(1)
, the number of
those conjugates is coprime to
p
. However, the order of

{\it Gal}({\mathbb{Q}}_{|G|}/{\mathbb{Q}}_{p^{\alpha}|G|_{p^{\prime}}})
is a
p
-power, and it
follows that some conjugate of
\theta
is point-wise fixed by

{\it Gal}({\mathbb{Q}}_{|G|}/{\mathbb{Q}}_{p^{\alpha}|G|_{p^{\prime}}})
, which implies that

\theta
is point-wise fixed by

{\it Gal}({\mathbb{Q}}_{|G|}/{\mathbb{Q}}_{p^{\alpha}|G|_{p^{\prime}}})
as well.
∎

Theorem 2.5
.

The following hold:

(i)

Conjectures
1.1
and

2.1
imply Conjecture
2.3
.

(ii)

Conjecture
2.3
implies Conjecture

1.1
.

(iii)

The only if direction of Conjecture
2.3
implies Conjecture

2.1
.

(iv)

Conjectures
1.1
,
2.1
, and
2.3
are all consequences
of the MN conjecture.

Proof.

We begin with proving (i). Assume that
G
has an irreducible
p^{\prime}
-degree
character of
p
-rationality level
\alpha\geq 2
and
M\leq G
is
of
p^{\prime}
-index. Let
P\in{\mathrm{Syl}}_{p}(M)\subseteq{\mathrm{Syl}}_{p}(G)
. Then

\beta:=\log_{p}(\exp(P/P^{\prime}))\geq\alpha
by Conjecture

2.1
. By Conjecture
1.1
, it
follows that
M
possesses irreducible
p^{\prime}
-degree characters of
every level from
2
to
\beta
, implying that
M
has one of level

\alpha
. The if direction of Conjecture
2.3
is argued
similarly, with
G
and
M
interchanged.

To prove the remaining statements, we first claim that Conjecture

1.1
holds true when
G
is a
p
-group. Assume
that
G
has an irreducible
p^{\prime}
-degree characters of

p
-rationality level
\alpha\geq 2
. By the fundamental theorem of
finite Abelian groups,
G/G^{\prime}
is isomorphic to a direct product of
cyclic groups. The fact that
G/G^{\prime}
has an irreducible linear
character of level
\alpha\geq 2
implies that at least one of those
cyclic direct factors, say
C=\langle x\rangle
, has order at least

p^{\alpha}
:

|C|=p^{e}\geq p^{\alpha}.

For every
2\leq\beta\leq\alpha
, the

of
C
, where

\zeta
is a primitive
p^{e}
th root of unity, will have

p
-rationality level precisely equal to
\beta
. We deduce that

G/G^{\prime}
has irreducible
p^{\prime}
-degree characters of every

p
-rationality level from
2
to
\alpha
, as desired.

Now (ii) immediately follows from the claim. Also, (iii) follows
from the claim and
[
NT19
, Theorem B]
.

The fact that Conjecture
2.1
follows from the
MN conjecture was already observed before. Therefore, to see (iv),
it is enough to show that Conjecture
1.1
is a
consequence of the MN conjecture. For this, as remarked at the
beginning of this section, if the MN conjecture holds true then we
may assume that
P\in{\mathrm{Syl}}_{p}(G)
is normal in
G
. Using
Lemma
2.4
, we may further assume that
G=P
is a
p
-group,
but Conjecture
1.1
in this case is precisely the
claim above.
∎

3.
p
-Rationality level of character values

In this section we relate the
p
-rationality level of a character
with the level of the values of the character. We also prove a key
lemma that will be needed in the proof the main results.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
z
be a complex number belonging the smallest field
containing all roots of unity. The
p
-rationality level of
z
is
defined as

\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(z)=\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}_{p}(z):=\log_{p}(c({\mathbb{Q}}(z))),

where
c({\mathbb{Q}}(z))
is the smallest positive integer
f
such that

z\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{f}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\chi
be a character of a finite group
G
. Then

\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\chi)=\max_{g\in G}\{\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\chi(g))\}.

Proof.

It is clear that
\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\chi(g))\leq\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\chi)
for
every
g\in G
. On the other hand, as
{\mathbb{Q}}(\chi)\subseteq{\mathbb{Q}}_{{\mathrm{lcm}}(c({\mathbb{Q}}(\chi(g))):g\in G)}
, we have

\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\chi)\leq\max_{g\in G}\{\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\chi(g))\}
, and the result
follows.
∎

In view of Lemma
3.2
, we will say that the

p
-rationality level of
\chi
is
achieved
at
g\in G
if

\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\chi)=\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\chi(g))
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\varphi,\psi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(G)
and
\chi:=\varphi\psi
such
that
\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\varphi)\leq\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\chi)
. If the
p
-rationality level of

\psi
is achieved at a
p
-element, then

(i)

\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\psi)\leq\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\chi)
, and

(ii)

either
\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\varphi)=\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\chi)
or
\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\psi)=\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\chi)
.

In particular, if
G/\operatorname{Ker}(\psi)
is a
p
-group, then the
same conclusions hold.

Proof.

Suppose that the
p
-rationality level of
\psi
is achieved at some

p
-element
g\in G
. Since irreducible
p^{\prime}
-degree characters are
nonzero at
p
-elements (see
[
DPSS09
, Remark 4.1]
), we have

\chi(g)=\varphi(g)\psi(g)\neq 0,

so that
\psi(g)=\chi(g)\varphi(g)^{-1}
. Therefore,

\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\psi)=\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\psi(g))\leq\max\{\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\chi(g)),\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\varphi(g))\}\leq\max\{\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\chi),\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\varphi)\}=\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\chi),

proving part (i).

For (ii) we note that, if both
\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\varphi)
and
\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\psi)
are
smaller than
\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\chi)
, then

{\mathbb{Q}}(\chi)={\mathbb{Q}}(\varphi\psi)\subseteq{\mathbb{Q}}_{{\mathrm{lcm}}(c(\varphi),c(\psi))}={\mathbb{Q}}_{p^{a}m}

for some
a<\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\chi)

and
p\nmid m
, which is a contradiction.

Finally, assume that
G/\operatorname{Ker}(\psi)
is a
p
-group and again the
level of
\psi
is achieved at
g\in G
. Since
g\operatorname{Ker}(\psi)
is a

p
-element (of
G/\operatorname{Ker}(\psi)
), we have

g\operatorname{Ker}(\psi)=g_{p}\operatorname{Ker}(\psi)
. Hence,
\psi(g)=\psi(g_{p})
, and it
follows that the level of
\psi
is achieved at
g_{p}
, a
p
-element
of
G
.
∎

It is possible that the assumption on the
p
-rationality level of

\psi
being achieved at a
p
-element in Lemma
3.3
is superfluous. Suppose that

\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\psi)=\alpha\geq 2
.
[
NT21
, Conjecture C]

asserts that

the degree of the extension

{\mathbb{Q}}_{p^{\alpha}}/{\mathbb{Q}}(\psi_{P})
, where
P\in{\mathrm{Syl}}_{p}(G)
, is not
divisible by
p
.

This is equivalent to

\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}({\mathbb{Q}}(\psi_{P}))=\alpha
, which in turn is equivalent to that
there exists some
g\in P
such that
\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\psi(g))=\alpha
. In
other words, the level of
\psi
is achieved at the
p
-element
g
.
We rephrase
[
NT21
, Conjecture C]
.

Conjecture 3.4

(Navarro-Tiep)
.

The
p
-rationality level higher than 1 of a
p^{\prime}
-degree irreducible
character is always achieved at a
p
-element.

Let us mention that this rephrasing together with Theorem

5.6
have allowed us to confirm the conjecture for
linear and unitary groups
G
. We in fact have shown that if the
level of
\psi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(G)
is achieved at some
g\in G
, then it
is also achieved at
g_{p}
, where
g_{p}
is the
p
-part of

(work
in progress
[
Hun
]
).

In this section we prove Theorems

We begin with some preliminary results.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
N
be a normal subgroup of
G
and
p
a prime. Let
P_{N},P_{G}
,

P_{G/N}
be respectively Sylow
p
-subgroups of
N,G
, and
G/N
.
Then

(i)

\exp(P_{G/N}/P^{\prime}_{G/N})\leq\exp(P_{G}/P^{\prime}_{G})
.

(ii)

\exp(P_{G}/P^{\prime}_{G})\leq\exp(P_{N}/P^{\prime}_{N})\cdot\exp(P_{G/N}/P^{\prime}_{G/N})
.

Proof.

We may assume without loss that
P_{N}\leq P_{G}
and

P_{G/N}=P_{G}/P_{N}
. We then have
P_{G/N}/P^{\prime}_{G/N}\cong P_{G}/P^{\prime}_{G}P_{N}

and (i) is clear. To see (ii), note that if
X\trianglelefteq\,Y
then

\exp(Y)\leq\exp(X)\cdot\exp(Y/X)
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\exp(P_{G}/P^{\prime}_{G})

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(\frac{P_{G}/P^{\prime}_{G}}{P_{N}P^{\prime}_{G}/P^{\prime}_{G}}\right)\cdot\exp(P_{N}P^{\prime}_{G}/P^{\prime}_{G})

\displaystyle=\exp(P_{G}/P^{\prime}_{G}P_{N})\cdot\exp(P_{N}/(P^{\prime}_{G}\cap P_{N}))

\displaystyle\leq\exp(P_{G/N}/P^{\prime}_{G/N})\cdot\exp(P_{N}/P^{\prime}_{N}),

as desired.
∎

Recall that, for a character

of

of

is the order of

in the group of
linear characters of

Lemma 4.2
.

Let

This is Corollaries 6.2 and 6.4 of

Lemma 4.3
.

Let

Proof.

By Lemma

has a unique

\widehat{\theta}\in{\mathrm{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(NP)

.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let

.

We can now prove Theorem

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
p=2
,
G
a finite group and
P\in{\mathrm{Syl}}_{p}(G)
. Suppose that

\log_{p}(\exp(P/P^{\prime}))=\alpha\geq 2
. Then

has irreducible

-degree characters of every

-rationality level from

to

\alpha
.

Proof.

Let
N
be a minimal normal subgroup of

. We may assume that
p

divides
|N|
because otherwise we are done by induction on
|G|
.

Suppose first that
N
is abelian. Then in fact
N\trianglelefteq\,P
, so that

P/N\in{\mathrm{Syl}}_{p}(G/N)
. If
\exp(P/P^{\prime})=\exp((P/N)/(P/N)^{\prime})
then we are
done again, and therefore, by Lemma
4.1
(i), we may assume
that
\exp(P/P^{\prime})>\exp((P/N)/(P/N)^{\prime})
; in particular,

. Since
\exp(N)=p
, using Lemma
4.1
(ii), we have

\exp(P/P^{\prime})=p\cdot\exp((P/N)/(P/N)^{\prime}).

By induction, it is sufficient to show that
G
has an irreducible

p^{\prime}
-degree character of
p
-rationality level precisely equal to

\log_{p}(\exp(P/P^{\prime}))=\alpha
. This is in fact a consequence of
Theorem
2.2
.

We may now suppose that
N
is nonabelian of order divisible by
p
.

We claim that if

has a
p^{\prime}
-degree irreducible character of

\beta\geq 2
, then so does

. Let
H:=NP
and

\varphi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(H)
such that

\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\varphi)=\beta.

We have

\theta:=\varphi_{N}
is irreducible by
[
Isa76
, Corollary
11.29]
, and therefore is
H
-invariant. Note that
[H:N]

is a
p
-power and, as

is perfect, the determinantal order of

\theta
is trivial. It follows from Lemma
4.2
that
\theta
has a unique extension to
H
, say

\widehat{\theta}
, whose determinantal order is coprime to

.
Furthermore, we have
{\mathbb{Q}}(\widehat{\theta})={\mathbb{Q}}(\theta)
, and in
particular,

\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\widehat{\theta})=\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\theta).

Using Gallagher’s lemma, we have
\varphi=\widehat{\theta}\rho
for
some linear character
\rho
of the
p
-group
H/N
. By Lemma

2.4
(iii), we know that
\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\theta)\leq\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\varphi)=\beta
, and whence
\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\widehat{\theta})\leq\beta
.
Using Lemma
3.3
, we deduce that

\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\rho)\leq\beta
and

\text{either }\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\widehat{\theta})=\beta\text{ or }\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\rho)=\beta.

In the former case, we have

therefore guarantees that

has a
p^{\prime}
-degree
irreducible character of level
\beta
. Suppose the latter case that

\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\rho)=\beta

\beta\leq\log_{p}(\exp((H/N)/(H/N)^{\prime}))=:\gamma.

Note that

.
So

has
p^{\prime}
-degree irreducible characters of all levels from

2
to
\gamma
by induction, implying in particular that

, and
therefore

, has a
p^{\prime}
-degree irreducible character of level

\beta

It is now sufficient to show that

has irreducible
p^{\prime}
-degree
characters of every

-rationality level from
2
to
\alpha
.
Replacing
N
by the direct product of the simple factors in an

on the factors of
N
and repeating the
above arguments if necessary, we may furthermore assume that
P

transitively permutes the factors of
N
.

Let
S
be a simple factor of
N
. Let

and

C:={\mathbf{C}}_{H}(S)
. Then
K/C
is an almost simple group with socle

. Moreover, since

,

is a

p

We know from Theorem
2.2
that

has a

p^{\prime}
-degree irreducible character of level

, say
\chi
. As above we have

\eta:=\chi_{N}
is irreducible and
\chi=\widehat{\eta}\sigma
, where

\widehat{\eta}\in{\mathrm{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(H)
is the extension of

to

\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\eta)=\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\widehat{\eta})

is a linear

If

implying that

So we may assume that

is

We now can apply Theorem

We partly follow the setup in the proof of

Proposition 5.4

Proof.

The statement is trivial when all the irreducible

-degree

The focus of Conjecture
5.1
is therefore
on simple groups of Lie type in non-defining characteristic. To
continue, we need to setup a general framework for these groups via
algebraic groups and the Deligne-Lusztig theory on which we refer
the reader to
[

,

]
for some background.

Let
{\mathbf{G}}
be a connected reductive group defined over an
algebraically closed field of characteristic
r>0
and

F:{\mathbf{G}}\rightarrow{\mathbf{G}}
is a Frobenius endomorphism that defines an

\mathbb{F}_{q}
-rational structure
G={\mathbf{G}}^{F}
, where
q
is a power of

r
. Suppose that
({\mathbf{G}}^{\ast},F^{\ast})
is dual to
({\mathbf{G}},F)
and let

G^{\ast}:={{\mathbf{G}}^{\ast}}^{F^{\ast}}
.

The set
{\mathrm{Irr}}(G)
is naturally partitioned into the (rational)
Lusztig series
\mathcal{E}(G,s)
associated to various
G^{\ast}
-conjugacy
classes of semisimple elements
s\in G^{\ast}
. The series
\mathcal{E}(G,s)

is defined to be the set of irreducible characters of
{\mathbf{G}}^{F}

occurring in some Deligne-Lusztig character
R_{{\mathbf{T}}}^{{\mathbf{G}}}\theta
,
where
{\mathbf{T}}
is an
F
-stable maximal torus of
{\mathbf{G}}
and

\theta\in{\mathrm{Irr}}({\mathbf{T}}^{F})
such that the geometric conjugacy class of

({\mathbf{T}},\theta)
corresponds to the
G^{\ast}
-conjugacy class
containing
s
(see
[
Car85
, Theorem 4.4.6]
for this
correspondence). For later use, we also note that, when
{\mathbf{Z}}({\mathbf{G}})

is connected, there is a natural bijection
\pi_{s}
from
\mathcal{E}(G,s)

to
\mathcal{E}({\mathbf{C}}_{G^{\ast}}(s),1)
– the set of unipotent characters of

{\mathbf{C}}_{G^{\ast}}(s)
(see
[
GM20
, Theorem 4.7.1]
for the
description of
\pi_{s}
). If
\chi\in\mathcal{E}(G,s)
corresponds to

\psi\in\mathcal{E}({\mathbf{C}}_{G^{\ast}}(s),1)
under this bijection, we will say
that
(s,\psi)
is the Jordan decomposition of
\chi
.

Though the actions of
\sigma_{\alpha}
and
{\mathrm{Aut}}(G)
on
{\mathrm{Irr}}(G)
are
generally difficult to control, it turns out that their effect on
Lusztig series
\mathcal{E}(G,s)
is nicely behaved. (Recall that

\sigma_{\alpha}\in Gal({\mathbb{Q}}^{ab}/{\mathbb{Q}})
fixes roots of unity of order
not divisible by
p
and maps every
p
-power root of unity

to

\xi^{1+p^{\alpha}}
.) In fact, by a result of A. A. Schaeffer Fry
and J. Taylor
[
ST18
, Lemma 3.4]
, we have

(5.1)

\mathcal{E}(G,s)^{\sigma_{\alpha}}=\mathcal{E}(G,s_{p}^{1+p^{\alpha}}\cdot s_{p^{\prime}}),

where
s_{p}
and
s_{p^{\prime}}
are respectively the
p
- and
p^{\prime}
-parts of

s
.

To describe the action of
{\mathrm{Aut}}(G)
on Lusztig series, we need some
more notation. Let
\varphi\in{\mathrm{Aut}}(G)
. It is well-known that

\varphi
is then the restriction of some bijective morphism
\tau

of
{\mathbf{G}}
that commutes with
F
. This
\tau
induces a bijective
morphism
\tau^{\ast}
on
{\mathbf{G}}^{\ast}
that commutes with
F^{\ast}
,
which implies that the restriction

\varphi^{\ast}:=\tau^{\ast}\hskip-5.0pt\downarrow_{G^{\ast}}
is an
automorphism of
G^{\ast}
. We call
\varphi^{\ast}
an automorphism
dual to
\varphi
. By
[
NTT07
, Corollary 2.4]

(this is due to C. Bonnafé and a more detailed proof was later
given by J. Taylor in
[

\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\widetilde{\chi})=\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\chi)=\alpha.

By Lemma
6.3
, we have

\widetilde{\chi}=S(s_{1},\lambda_{1})\circ S(s_{2},\lambda_{2})\circ\cdots\circ S(s_{k},\lambda_{k}),

where

n=n_{1}+n_{2}+\cdots+n_{k}
is a proper decomposition of
n
and, for

1\leq i\leq k
,
s_{i}\in C_{q-\epsilon}
are pairwise different and

\lambda_{i}
is a partition of
n_{i}
.

We now consider

\widetilde{\chi}_{1}:=S(s_{1}^{2},(n_{1}))\circ S(s_{2}^{2},(n_{2}))\circ\cdots\circ S(s^{2}_{k},(n_{k}))

and

\chi_{1}:=\widetilde{\chi}_{1}\hskip-5.0pt\downarrow_{G}.

We claim that
\chi_{1}
is
odd-degree, irreducible, and trivial on
{\mathbf{Z}}(G)
, and thus can be
viewed as a member of
{\mathrm{Irr}}_{2^{\prime}}(S)
; furthermore,
\chi_{1}
has

2
-rationality level
\alpha-1
and is
P
-invariant.

(a) We note that the Jordan decomposition of
\widetilde{\chi}
is
simply the pair
(s,\psi)
where

s={\mathrm{diag}}(\underbrace{s_{1},...s_{1}}_{n_{1}\text{ times}},\underbrace{s_{2},...,s_{2}}_{n_{2}\text{ times}},...,\underbrace{s_{k},...,s_{k}}_{n_{k}\text{ times}})

and

\psi=\varphi^{\lambda_{1}}\otimes\varphi^{\lambda_{2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\varphi^{\lambda_{k}},

a
unipotent character of the centralizer

{\mathbf{C}}_{GL_{n}^{\epsilon}(q)}(s)=GL_{n_{1}}^{\epsilon}(q)\times GL_{n_{2}}^{\epsilon}(q)\times\cdots\times GL_{n_{k}}^{\epsilon}(q).

Since
\widetilde{\chi}(1)
is odd, the semisimple element
s
is

2
-central. It follows that
s^{2}
is
2
-central, and so

\widetilde{\chi}_{1}(1)
is odd, implying that
\chi_{1}
is
irreducible (by Lemma
6.4
) and odd-degree.

Next, we note that
\chi
, being the restriction of

\widetilde{\chi}
to
G
, must belong to the Lusztig series of
G

associated to
s^{\ast}\in G^{\ast}:=PGL_{n}^{\epsilon}(q)
, which is the
image of
s
under the natural projection from
\widetilde{G}
onto

G^{\ast}
. Since
\chi
is trivial on
{\mathbf{Z}}(G)
, Lemma

5.8
implies that
s^{\ast}\in[G^{\ast},G^{\ast}]=S
. Therefore
(s^{2})^{\ast}
, the image of
s^{2}
under
the same projection, also belongs to
[G^{\ast},G^{\ast}]
. It follows
that,
\chi_{1}
, a member of the series
\mathcal{E}(G,(s^{2})^{\ast})
, is
trivial on
{\mathbf{Z}}(G)
, again by Lemma
5.8
.

(b) Next we show that
\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\chi_{1})=\alpha-1
. We identify each

t=\tau I_{n}\in{\mathbf{Z}}(\widetilde{G})
with
\tau\in C_{q-\epsilon}
.
Then

{\mathrm{ord}}(ts)={\mathrm{lcm}}({\mathrm{ord}}(\tau s_{1}),{\mathrm{ord}}(\tau s_{2}),...,{\mathrm{ord}}(\tau s_{k})),

and so

{\mathrm{ord}}(ts)_{2}=\max_{1\leq i\leq k}\{{\mathrm{ord}}(\tau s_{i})_{2}\}.

Therefore, by Proposition
6.2
,

\alpha=\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\chi)=\log_{2}\min_{\tau\in C_{q-\epsilon}}\{\max_{1\leq i\leq k}\{{\mathrm{ord}}(\tau s_{i})_{2}\}\}.

Note that
\alpha=\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\widetilde{\chi})=\log_{2}(\max_{1\leq i\leq k}\{{\mathrm{ord}}(s_{i})_{2}\})
. We deduce that

\max_{1\leq i\leq k}\{{\mathrm{ord}}(s_{i})_{2}\}\leq\max_{1\leq i\leq k}\{{\mathrm{ord}}(\tau s_{i})_{2}\}

for every
\tau\in C_{q-\epsilon}
. This implies that

\max_{1\leq i\leq k}\{{\mathrm{ord}}(s_{i}^{2})_{2}\}\leq\max_{1\leq i\leq k}\{{\mathrm{ord}}(\tau s_{i}^{2})_{2}\}

for every
\tau\in C_{q-\epsilon}
. (The inequality is certainly
true if
\tau
is a square in
C_{q-\epsilon}
. Otherwise, we have

{\mathrm{ord}}(\tau)_{2}=(q-\epsilon)_{2}
, and so
{\mathrm{ord}}(\tau s_{i}^{2})_{2}=(q-\epsilon)_{2}
and the inequality is still satisfied.)
Using Proposition
6.2
again, we now have

\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\widetilde{\chi}_{1})\leq\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\chi_{1}).

On the other hand, we have
\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\widetilde{\chi}_{1})\geq\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\chi_{1})
by Lemma
2.4
(iii) and

\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\widetilde{\chi}_{1})=\log_{2}({\mathrm{ord}}(s^{2})_{2})=\alpha-1

by
Proposition
6.1
and a note that
\alpha\geq 3
. We
conclude that
\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\chi_{1})=\alpha-1
.

(c) Finally we argue that
\chi_{1}
is
\varphi
-invariant for every
automorphism
\varphi\in X
. We will identify
\varphi
as an
automorphism of
G
via the natural identification
{\mathrm{Aut}}(S)\cong{\mathrm{Aut}}(G)
(see
[
GLS94
, Corollary 5.1.4]
). Recall that

\chi
is
\varphi
-invariant and, when viewed as a character of

G
, belongs to
\mathcal{E}(G,s^{\ast})
, where
s^{\ast}
is the image of
s

under the projection
\widetilde{G}\rightarrow G^{\ast}
. We have that

\mathcal{E}(G,s^{\ast})
is
\varphi
-invariant, and therefore the

G^{\ast}
-conjugacy class of
s^{\ast}
is
\varphi^{\ast}
-invariant,
where
\varphi^{\ast}
is the dual automorphism of
\varphi
defined
just before (
5.2
). It follows that the
G^{\ast}
-conjugacy
class of
(s^{2})^{\ast}
is also
\varphi^{\ast}
-invariant, which in
turn implies that the series
\mathcal{E}(G,(s^{2})^{\ast})
is

\varphi
-invariant.

Recall that
\iota:SL_{n}(\overline{{\mathbb{F}}_{r}})\hookrightarrow GL_{n}(\overline{{\mathbb{F}}_{r}})
, where
r
is the (only) prime divisor of

q
, is a regular embedding, in the sense of
[
GM20
, §1.7]
.
Also,
\chi_{1}
is the unique irreducible constituent of the
restriction of the semisimple character

\widetilde{\chi}_{1}\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(\widetilde{G})
to
G
. It follows from

[
GM20
, Corollary 2.6.18]
that
\chi_{1}
is the unique semisimple
character in
\mathcal{E}(G,(s^{2})^{\ast})
, and hence must be

\varphi
-invariant.

We have produced the character
\chi_{1}\in{\mathrm{Irr}}_{2^{\prime}}(S)
of level

\alpha-1
that satisfies all the required conditions, provided that

\alpha\geq 3
. Repeating the process, one can show that
S

possesses similar characters of every level from
2
to
\alpha
.
∎

In this section we prove Theorem

for

with

for the groups in question. The

Proposition 7.1
.

Let

odd and

Proof.

We reuse some notation in Section

. In particular, we view

as

(of type
E_{6}

on

({\mathbf{G}}^{\ast},F^{\ast})

G^{\ast}={{\mathbf{G}}^{\ast}}^{F^{\ast}}

We first note that odd-degree unipotent characters of

lie in the

-rational, as mentioned in the proof of

. Therefore we may assume that

is

, the

is

-stable Levi subgroup of

{\mathbf{L}}^{\ast}

.) The centralizer

8.
A
p
-local lower bound for

|{\mathrm{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(G)|

In this section we discuss Conjecture
1.4
and
prove it for
p=2
.

As already mentioned in the introduction, Conjecture

1.4
arises from three different pieces: the
continuity property Conjecture
1.1
,
Navarro-Tiep’s conjecture on fields of values of
p^{\prime}
-degree
characters, and Hung-Malle-Maróti’s bound for the number of
almost
p
-rational irreducible characters. For reader’s
convenience, we recall the latter two here.

Conjecture 8.1

(
[
NT21
]
)
.

Let
\chi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(G)
with
\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}_{p}(\chi)=\alpha\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{\geq 0}
.
Then
p
does not divide
[{\mathbb{Q}}_{p^{\alpha}}:({\mathbb{Q}}(\chi)\cap{\mathbb{Q}}_{p^{\alpha}})]
.

We note that Conjecture
8.1
is trivial when

\alpha\leq 1
. Also, when
\alpha\geq 1
, its conclusion implies
that
[{\mathbb{Q}}(\chi):{\mathbb{Q}}]
is divisible by
p^{\alpha-1}
, which in turn
implies that
G
has at least
p^{\alpha-1}
different irreducible
characters of the same degree and field of values as
\chi
. This
bound is significant when
\alpha\geq 2
but not so when
\alpha=1
.
The following provides a bound for characters of levels
0
and
1
.

Theorem 8.2

(
[
HMM22
]
)
.

Let
G
be a finite group,
p
a prime dividing the order of
G
and
P
a
Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
. Then the number of almost

p
-rational irreducible characters of
p^{\prime}
-degree of
G
is at
least
2\sqrt{p-1}
.
Moreover, the following are equivalent:

(i)

the number of almost

p
-rational irreducible characters of
p^{\prime}
-degree of
G
is

2\sqrt{p-1}
;

(ii)

the number of almost

p
-rational irreducible characters of
p^{\prime}
-degree of
{\mathbf{N}}_{G}(P)
is

2\sqrt{p-1}
;

(iii)

P
is cyclic and
{\mathbf{N}}_{G}(P)
is isomorphic to the Frobenius
group
P\rtimes C_{\sqrt{p-1}}
.

Theorem 8.3
.

Conjecture
1.4
follows from Conjectures

1.1
and
8.1
.

Proof.

Assume that Conjectures
1.1
and

8.1
hold true for
G
and
p
. We aim to
establish the inequality

|{\mathrm{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(G)|\geq\frac{\exp(P/P^{\prime})-1}{p-1}+2\sqrt{p-1}-1,

provided that
|G|
is
divisible by
p
. As explained in Remark
1.9
, by Theorem

8.2
, this is reduced to showing

|\{\chi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(G):\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\chi)\geq 2\}|\geq\frac{\exp(P/P^{\prime})-1}{p-1}-1.

We may and will assume that
\exp(P/P^{\prime})\geq p^{2}
. The wanted
inequality is further reduced to

|\{\chi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(G):\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\chi)=\beta\}|\geq p^{\beta-1},

for every
2\leq\beta\leq\log_{p}(\exp(P/P^{\prime}))
.

By
[
NT19
, Theorem B]
, there exists a
p^{\prime}
-degree
irreducible character of
G
of
p
-rationality level at least

\log_{p}(\exp(P/P^{\prime})\geq 2
. The continuity property from Conjecture

1.1
then implies that
G
possesses
p^{\prime}
-degree
irreducible characters of all levels from
2
to

\log_{p}(\exp(P/P^{\prime}))
.

Let
\chi\in{\mathrm{Irr}}_{p^{\prime}}(G)
with
\mathrm{\mathbf{lev}}(\chi)=\beta
. By Conjecture

8.1
,
p
does not divide

[{\mathbb{Q}}_{p^{\beta}}:({\mathbb{Q}}(\chi)\cap{\mathbb{Q}}_{p^{\beta}})]
, implying that

[{\mathbb{Q}}(\chi):{\mathbb{Q}}]
is divisible by
p^{\beta-1}
. The number of
Galois conjugates of
\chi
is
[{\mathbb{Q}}(\chi):{\mathbb{Q}}]
, by
[
Nav18
, Theorem
3.1]
, and therefore is at least
p^{\beta-1}
. Also, each
Galois conjugate of
\chi
has the same degree and field of values
as
\chi
. We deduce that the number of
p^{\prime}
-degree irreducible
characters of
p
-rationality level
\beta
is at least

p^{\beta-1}
, as desired.
∎

Theorem 8.4
.

Let
G
be finite group and
P\in{\mathrm{Syl}}_{2}(G)
. Then

|{\mathrm{Irr}}_{2^{\prime}}(G)|\geq\exp(P/P^{\prime}).

Moreover, the equality occurs if
and only if
P
is cyclic and self-normalizing.

Proof.

Conjecture
8.1
has been verified for
p=2
in

[
NT21
, Theorem A1]
. Therefore, the inequality follows
from Theorem
1.3
and Theorem
8.3
.

The if direction of the second statement of the theorem is clear, by
using the cyclic-Sylow case of the McKay conjecture
[
Dad96
]
.
So assume that
|{\mathrm{Irr}}_{2^{\prime}}(G)|=\exp(P/P^{\prime})
. Following the proof of
Theorem
8.3
, we see that
G
then has precisely two
almost
2
-rational odd-degree irreducible characters. By Theorem

8.2
, this occurs if and only if
P
is cyclic
and
{\mathbf{N}}_{G}(P)=P
, as stated.
∎

We conclude by a remark that, as the McKay-Navarro conjecture admits
a block-wise version (see the discussion after Conjecture 9.13 in

[
Nav18
]
), Conjectures
1.1
and

1.4
are expected to hold for the principal block
(and perhaps for all blocks of maximal defect as well). That is, if
the principal

-block

of a finite group

-rationality level

\alpha\geq 2
, then

contains similar characters of all levels

to

. Moreover, the number of height-zero characters

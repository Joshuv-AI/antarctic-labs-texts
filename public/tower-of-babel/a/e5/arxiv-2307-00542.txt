On noncommutative ergodic theorems for semigroup and free group actions
Panchugopal Bikram, Diptesh Saha

Abstract
. In this article, we consider actions of ℤ + d \mathbb{Z}_{+}^{d} , ℝ + d \mathbb{R}_{+}^{d} and finitely generated free groups on a von Neumann algebras M M and prove a version of maximal ergodic inequality. Additionally, we establish non-commutative analogues of pointwise ergodic theorems for associated actions in the predual when M is finite.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary: 46L53, 46L55; Secondary: 37A55, 46L51.

1.
Introduction

Dynamical systems studied in the context of measure preserving action have a long history starting from Birkhoff and von Neumann around 1930. Since then, the subject has been studied in numerous directions. Among them, the study of actions by multiparameter semigroups and free groups
has attracted particular attention. This article is devoted to study ergodic theorem for such actions.

On the other hand, studying such ergodic theorems is also of paramount interest in the non-commutative probability theory and harmonic analysis. The groundbreaking work of Lance
[
Lan76
]
in 1976 yielded the first results in this direction. In this article, the author established a pointwise ergodic theorem for a state-preserving automorphism on a von Neumann algebra. Later, these results were extended to the case of a weak
∗
-continuous positive operator on von Neumann algebra by Kummerer in
[
K7̈8
]
and to the case of one parameter semigroups of positive contractions by Conze and Dang-Ngoc in
[
CDN78
]
.

In
[
Yea77
]
and
[
Yea80
]
, the author considered a positive linear transformation
\alpha
on
L^{1}(M,\tau)
(here
M
is a von Neumann algebra with f.n.s trace
\tau
) satisfying
0\leq\alpha(X)\leq 1
and a sub traciality condition (i.e,
\tau(\alpha(X))\leq\tau(X)
) for all
X\in L^{1}(M,\tau)\cap M
and
0\leq X\leq 1
. Under these conditions, for all
X\in L^{1}(M,\tau)
the author proved bilateral almost uniform convergence of the averages
A_{n}(X):=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\alpha^{k}(X)
, which is the non commutative analogue of almost everywhere pointwise convergence for measure space. In the process, he also established the maximal ergodic theorem in this context. In
[
JX07
]
, Junge and Xu further extended the pointwise and maximal ergodic theorems for the action of
\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
on
L^{p}
spaces for
1<p<\infty
.

Later on, in
[
HLW21
]
, the authors considered a strongly continuous action
\alpha
of G (of polynomial growth and having symmetric, compact generating set) of
\tau
-preserving automorphisms on
M
, where
\tau
is a f.n.s trace on
M
. Then they extended this action to
L^{1}(M,\tau)

and generalized the results of
[
Yea77
]
by proving the almost uniform convergence of the averages of the form
A_{n}(X):=\frac{1}{m(V^{n})}\int_{V^{n}}\alpha_{g}(X)dm(g)
, for
X\in L^{1}(M,\tau)
.
They obtained maximal ergodic theorem and various other individual ergodic theorems on tracial non-commutative
L^{p}
-spaces for the actions of locally compact groups with polynomial growth.

Very recently in
[
BS23
]
, we studied pointwise convergence of ergodic averages for action of a group
G
with polynomial growth. In this article, we considered an invariant state on a dynamical system
(M,G,\alpha)
, where
\alpha
is sub-unital and then proved the pointwise ergodic theorem for the associated predual action on
L^{1}(M,\tau)
, where
\tau
is a tracial state.

In the first part of the present article, we confine ourself to the case of multiparameter ergodic theorems, that is, the action of
\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{d}
or
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
for an integer
d>1
. In
[
JX07
]
, the authors also studied a maximal ergodic theorem and associated pointwise convergence of ergodic averages in noncommutative
L^{p}
spaces for the action of
\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{d}
or
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
, where the action is assumed to be trace preserving.

In the second part of the paper we study ergodic convergence of spherical averages associated to a sequence of
w
-continuous maps
\sigma:=\{\sigma_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
on a von Neumann algebra
M
. This was inspired by the study of the convergence of spherical averages’ associated to a free group action. To be precise let
\mathbb{F}_{r}
denote the free group generated by
r
elements and its inverses. Let
\phi
be a homomorphism from
\mathbb{F}_{r}
to
Aut(M)
and consider the spherical averages

\displaystyle\sigma_{n}(x):=\frac{1}{\left|W_{n}\right|}\sum_{a\in W_{n}}\phi(a)(x),~n\in\mathbb{N},

where
W_{n}
is the set of elements of
\mathbb{F}_{r}
of length
n
. Then it is interesting to determine the convergence of the sequence
S_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{0}^{n}\sigma_{k}(x)
.

Study of ergodic theorems for actions of groups which are not amenable were first initiated in classical setting by Arnold and Krylov in
[
AK63
]
. After that, it was carried out in great detail by many authors. We refer to
[
Gri86
]
,
[
Gui69
]
,
[
Nev94
]
,
[
NS94
]
and references therein. The free group action in the noncommutative setting was first considered by Walker in
[
Wal97
]
. He proved that if the homomorphism
\phi
is invariant under a faithful, normal state
\rho
, then for all
x\in M
the sequence
S_{n}(x)
will converge almost uniformly to an element
\hat{x}
in
M
. In fact, Walker dealt with more general maps on
M
. In particular, he considered a sequence
\{\sigma_{n}\}
of completely positive maps on
M
which preserves a faithful, normal state
\rho
and satisfies
\sigma_{1}\sigma_{n}=w\sigma_{n+1}+(1-w)\sigma_{n-1}
for some
w\in(1/2,1]
with
\sigma_{0}(x)=x
. Then he proved the almost uniform convergence of
\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{0}^{n}\sigma_{k}
under some natural spectral condition on
\sigma_{1}
. In
[
CLS05
]
, Walker’s result was extended to
L^{1}(M,\tau)
, where
\tau
is a faithful, normal, semifinite trace such that
\tau(\sigma_{1}(x))\leq\tau(x)
for all
x\in M\cap L^{1}(M,\tau)
with
0\leq x\leq 1
.

Later on, inspired by the seminal work of Junge and Xu (
[
JX07
]
), in
[
Hu08
]
, Hu obtained a maximal ergodic theorem and associated individual ergodic theorem in
L^{p}(M,\tau)
corresponding to
\{\sigma_{n}\}
. In particular, he considered the sequence
\{\sigma_{n}\}
as described above on
(M,\varphi)
, where
\varphi
is a faithful, normal state, together with
\varphi\circ\sigma_{n}\leq\varphi
and
\sigma_{1}\circ\sigma_{t}^{\varphi}=\sigma_{t}^{\varphi}\circ\sigma_{1}
. Under these assumptions, Hu proved maximal and individual ergodic theorems associated with
\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{0}^{n}\sigma_{k}
in Haagerup non-commutative
L^{p}
spaces for
1<p<\infty
. Furthermore, if
M
is assumed to be semifinite with faithful, normal, semifinite trace
\tau
(that is it is assumed that
\tau(\sigma_{1}(x))\leq\tau(x)
for all
x\in M\cap L^{1}(M,\tau)
with
0\leq x\leq 1
), then Hu recovered the result in
[
CLS05
]
as mentioned above.

In both cases, the individual ergodic theorems for state preserving maps on
L^{1}
spaces are not known. In this article, we address this situation and prove individual ergodic theorems for the state preserving maps on
L^{1}
spaces.
We like to point out that
the techniques that are used in proving our result are independent of the techniques that are used in
[
JX07
]
or
[
Hu08
]
, and it reflects in the fact that trace preserving condition is not necessary to prove the Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.4
.

Now we highlight some of our important results. This article deals with ergodic averages corresponding to actions of
\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{d}
,
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
, and finitely generated free groups. In these situations, we derive a suitable maximal ergodic inequality and use it to prove individual ergodic theorems. We first assume that
G
is either
\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{d}
or
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
, where
d>1
. If
G=\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{d}
, we first consider
d
many
w
-continuous commuting operators
T_{1},\ldots,T_{d}
on
M
and then the associated action of
G
is naturally defined as
T_{(i_{1},\cdots,i_{d})}(\cdot)=T_{1}^{i_{1}}T_{2}^{i_{2}}\cdots T_{d}^{i_{d}}(\cdot)
for
(i_{1},\cdots,i_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{d}
. If
G=\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
, then we consider a continuous action
T=\{T_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}}
of
G
on
M
. We will refer
(M,G,T)
as a noncommutative dynamical system and we study the following averages.

\displaystyle M_{a}(\cdot):=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{a^{d}}\sum_{0\leq i_{1}<a}\cdots\sum_{0\leq i_{d}<a}T_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots T_{d}^{i_{d}}(\cdot)&\text{ when }G=\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{d},~a\in\mathbb{N},\\
\\
\frac{1}{a^{d}}\int_{Q_{a}}T_{t}(\cdot)dt&\text{ when }G=\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d},~a\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.\end{cases}

Furthermore, if there exists a faithful normal state
\rho
on
M
such that
\rho\circ T_{g}=\rho
, for all
g\in G
, then the quadruple
(G,M,T,\rho)
is called kernel. Now for the induced predual maps we obtain the following maximal inequality.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G
be either
\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{d}
or
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
and
(M,G,T,\rho)
be a kernel. Let
\mu\in M_{*s}
and
\epsilon>0
, then for any
N\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a projection
e\in M
such that
\rho(1-e)<\frac{\chi_{d}\left\|\mu\right\|}{\epsilon}
and

\displaystyle\left|M_{a}(\mu)(x)\right|\leq\epsilon\rho(x)\text{ for all }x\in(eMe)_{+}\text{ and }1\leq a\leq N.

We then use Theorem
1.1
to deduce the following individual ergodic theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
M
be a finite von Neumann algebra with a faithful normal trace
\tau
and
(M,G,T,\rho)
be a kernel. Then for all
Y\in L^{1}(M,\tau)
, there exists
\overline{Y}\in L^{1}(M,\tau)
such that
M_{a}(Y)
converges to
\overline{Y}
bilaterally almost uniformly.

Next we consider a generalized noncommutative dynamical system
(M,\sigma)
where
\sigma=\{\sigma_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is a sequence of
w
-continuous maps on
M
satisfying

A_{1}
.

\sigma_{1}
is completely positive,
\sigma_{1}(1)\leq 1
and
\sigma_{n}
is positive.

A_{2}
.

\sigma_{1}\circ\sigma_{n}=w\sigma_{n+1}+(1-w)\sigma_{n-1}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\sigma_{0}(x)=x
, where
1/2<w<1
,

and consider the following averages.

\displaystyle S_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{0}^{n}\sigma_{k}(x),~n\in\mathbb{N}.

We further define a generalized kernel as a triple
(M,\sigma,\rho)
, where
\rho
is a faithful, normal state satisfying
\rho\circ\sigma_{1}=\sigma_{1}\text{ and }\rho(y\sigma_{1}(x))=\rho(\sigma_{1}(y)x)\text{for all }x,y\in M
. Such maps naturally arise to study the convergence of spherical averages associated to actions of finitely generated free groups.

Then again for the induced predual maps we obtain the following maximal inequality and individual ergodic theorem.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(M,\sigma)
be a generalized noncommutative dynamical system and

\rho
be a f.n. state on
M
such that
\rho\circ\sigma_{1}=\rho
. Further assume that
\mu\in M_{*s}
and
\epsilon>0
. Then for any
N\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a projection
e\in M
such that
\rho(1-e)<\frac{C_{w}\left\|\mu\right\|}{\epsilon}
and

\displaystyle\left|S_{n}(\mu)(x)\right|\leq\epsilon\rho(x)\text{ for all }x\in(eMe)_{+}\text{ and }1\leq n\leq N.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
M
be a finite von Neumann algebra with f.n trace
\tau
and
let
(M,\sigma,\rho)
be a generalized kernel. Then for all
Y\in L^{1}(M,\tau)
, there exists
\overline{Y}\in L^{1}(M,\tau)
such that
S_{n}(Y)
converges to
\overline{Y}
bilaterally almost uniformly.

For the definition of convergence in bilaterally almost uniformly of a sequence see Definition
2.3
.

Now we describe the layout of this article. In §2, we collect necessary definitions of non-commutative
L^{p}
spaces, bilateral almost uniform convergence and the actions of semigroups and finitely generated free groups which will be essential for the subsequent sections. In sections §3, §4 and §5, we establish mean, maximal and individual ergodic theorems, for the actions of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{+}
and
\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
. Finally, in §6, we study the similar theorems associated with a sequence of
w
-continuous positive contractions on a von Neumann algebra. This sequence of maps resonates with the spherical averages corresponding to a finitely generated free group.

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout this article,
M
will denote a von Neumann algebra acting on a separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. We will write
M_{s}
and
M_{+}
to designate the collection of self-adjoint and positive elements of
M
, respectively. The projection lattice of
M
will be denoted by
\mathcal{P}(M)
.

M_{*}
will represent the predual of
M
. It is a norm closed subspace of Banach space dual of
M
. Similar to above, the self-adjoint and positive elements of
M_{*}
will be denoted by
M_{*s}
and
M_{*+}
, respectively.

Let
\tau
be a faithful, normal tracial weight on
M
. A closed densely defined operator
X:\mathcal{D}(X)\subseteq\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
is called affiliated to
M
if
u^{\prime}X=Xu^{\prime}
for all unitary
u^{\prime}
in
M^{\prime}
, where
M^{\prime}
is the commutant of
M
in
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
. If
X
is affiliated to M, we will denote it by
X\eta M
. Now an operator
X\eta M
is called
\tau
-measurable if for every
\epsilon>0
, there exists
e\in\mathcal{P}(M)
such that
e\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathcal{D}(X)
and
\tau(1-e)<\epsilon
. The set of all
\tau
-measurable operators on
M
is denoted by
L^{0}(M,\tau)
. The set of positive operators in
L^{0}(M,\tau)
will be denoted by
L^{0}(M,\tau)_{+}
.

The trace
\tau
primarily defined on
M_{+}
can be extended to
L^{0}(M,\tau)_{+}
as an additive, positive homogeneous
(\text{ i.e, }\tau(\alpha A+B)=\alpha\tau(A)+\tau(B)\text{ for }A,B\in L^{0}(M,\tau)_{+}\text{ and }\alpha>0)
function by the following formula.

For
X\in L^{0}(M,\tau)_{+}
,

\displaystyle\tau(X):=\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda d\tau(e_{\lambda}),

where
X=\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda d(e_{\lambda})
is the spectral decomposition.

Definition 2.1
.

For
0<p\leq\infty
, the non-commutative tracial
L^{p}
-space on
(M,\tau)
is defined by

L^{p}(M,\tau):=\begin{cases}\{X\in L^{0}(M,\tau):\left\|X\right\|_{p}:=\tau(\left|X\right|^{p})^{1/p}<\infty\}&\text{ for }p\neq\infty,\\
(M,\left\|\cdot\right\|)&\text{ for }p=\infty\end{cases}

With the definition above,
L^{p}(M,\tau)
satisfies many properties similar to
L^{p}
spaces defined on a measure space. In this article, we confine ourself to the non-commutative
L^{1}
spaces. The following proposition will play important role in the sequel.

Proposition 2.2
.

The map
\Psi:L^{1}(M,\tau)\to M_{*}
defined by
\Psi(X)(a)=\tau(Xa)
for
X\in L^{1}(M,\tau),a\in M
is a surjective linear isometry. Furthermore,
X
is positive if and only if
\Psi(X)
is positive.

Now we define bilateral almost uniform convergence of a sequence of measurable operators. This type of convergence is one of the key components of this article. Due to Egorov’s theorem, this definition of convergence is actually refers to the almost everywhere convergence in classical measure space. In sections 4 and 5, we shall demonstrate this form of convergence of various sequences.

Definition 2.3
.

A sequence of operators
\{X_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq L^{0}(M,\tau)
converges bilaterally almost uniformly (b.a.u) to
X\in L^{0}(M,\tau)
if for every
\epsilon>0
there exists
e\in\mathcal{P}(M)
with
\tau(1-e)<\epsilon
and

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\|e(X_{n}-X)e\right\|=0.

We put down the following proposition for our future reference. The proof is simple, and hence we omit it.

Proposition 2.4
.

Suppose
\{X_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\{Y_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
are two sequences in
L^{0}(M,\tau)
such that
\{X_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges in measure (resp. b.a.u) to
X
and
\{Y_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges in measure (resp. b.a.u) to
Y
. Then, for all
c\in\mathbb{C}
,
\{cX_{n}+Y_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges in measure (resp. b.a.u) to
cX+Y
.

Throughout this article, unless otherwise mentioned,
G
will denote a semigroup and
m
be a
\sigma
-finite measure on
G
, which is both left and right invariant (i.e.
m(uB)=m(B)
and
m(Bu)=m(B)
for all
u\in G
). In the sequel
\mathbb{K}
will always denote either
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
or
\mathbb{R}_{+}
. We consider a collection
\{I_{l}\}_{l\in\mathbb{K}}
of measurable subsets of
G
having the following properties.

(P1)

0<m(I_{l})<\infty
for all
l\in\mathbb{K}
.

(P2)

\lim_{l\to\infty}\frac{m(I_{l}\Delta I_{l}u)}{m(I_{l})}=0
and
\lim_{l\to\infty}\frac{m(I_{l}\Delta uI_{l})}{m(I_{l})}=0
for all
u\in G
.

Recall that a real Banach space
E
paired with a closed convex subset
K
satisfying
K\cap-K=\{0\}
and
\lambda K\subseteq K
for
\lambda\geq 0
induces a partial order on
E
given by
x\leq y
if and only if
y-x\in K
where
x,y\in E
. With such a partial order,
E
will be referred to as an ordered Banach space. In our context, it is easy to verify that
M,M^{*},M_{*}
,
L^{p}(M,\tau)
for
1\leq p\leq\infty
become ordered Banach spaces with respect to the natural order. Let us now define an action of
G
on ordered Banach spaces.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
E
be an ordered Banach space. A map
\Lambda
defined by

\displaystyle G\ni g\xrightarrow{\Lambda}\Lambda_{g}\in\mathcal{B}(E)

is called an action if
\Lambda_{g}\circ\Lambda_{h}=\Lambda_{gh}
for all
g,h\in G
. It is called anti-action if
\Lambda_{g}\circ\Lambda_{h}=\Lambda_{hg}
for all
g,h\in G
. In this article, we consider both actions and anti-actions
\Lambda=\{\Lambda_{g}\}_{g\in G}
which satisfy the following conditions.

(C)

For all
x\in E
, the map
g\rightarrow\Lambda_{g}(x)
from
G
to
E
is continuous. Here we take
w^{*}
-topology when
E=M
and norm topology otherwise.

(UB)

\sup_{g\in G}\left\|\Lambda_{g}\right\|<\infty
.

(P)

For all
g\in G
and
x\in E
with
x\geq 0
,
\Lambda_{g}(x)\geq 0
.

We refer the triple
(E,G,\Lambda)
as a non-commutative dynamical system.

Let us consider a non-commutative dynamical system
(L^{1}(M,\tau),G,\alpha)
. Note that for each
g\in G
,
\alpha_{g}:L^{1}(M,\tau)\rightarrow L^{1}(M,\tau)
is a bounded operator.
We recall the dual of
\alpha_{g}
, denoted by
\alpha_{g}^{*}
, defined on
M={L^{1}(M,\tau)}^{*}
and it is determined by the following equation;

(2.1)

\displaystyle\tau(\alpha^{*}_{g}(x)Y)=\tau(x\alpha_{g}(Y))\text{ for all }x\in M\text{ and }Y\in L^{1}(M,\tau).

On the other hand if
\beta
is an action of
G
on
M
, then for all
g\in G
, the predual transformation of
\beta_{g}
, denoted by
\hat{\beta_{g}}
, defined on
L^{1}(M,\tau)
and it is determined by the following equation;

(2.2)

\displaystyle\tau(\beta_{g}(x)Y)=\tau(x\hat{\beta}_{g}(Y))\text{ for all }x\in M\text{ and }Y\in L^{1}(M,\tau).

Further, we have
{\hat{(\beta_{g})}}^{*}=\beta_{g}
. This identification will be used in the sequel.

3.
Mean convergence for semigroup action

The following known result provides a norm dense subset of
N_{*+}
associated with a f.n state
\varphi
on
N
and reader may find a proof in
[
Hia20
, lemma 3.19]
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
N
be a von Neumann algebra with a f.n state
\varphi
. Then
\{\psi\in N_{*+}:\psi\leq k\varphi\text{ for some }k>0\}
is a norm dense subset of
N_{*+}
.

Following is the Radon Nykodym type result, which will be used in the sequel and a proof can be found in
[
SZ79
]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
M\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be a von Neumann algebra and
\omega
be a positive linear functional on
M
. Suppose there exists a
C>0
and
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
such that
\omega(x)\leq C\left\langle x\xi,\xi\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}
for all
x\in M_{+}
, then there exists a
x^{\prime}\in M^{\prime}_{+}
such that
x^{\prime}\leq C
and
\omega(x)=\left\langle x^{\prime}x\xi,\xi\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}
for all
x\in M
. Moreover, if
\xi
is a cyclic vector for
M
in
\mathcal{H}
, then
x^{\prime}
is unique.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
(M,G,T)
be a non-commutative dynamical system and
\rho
be a
G
-invariant f.n state on
M
.
Then there exists a non-commutative dynamical system
(M^{\prime},G,T^{\prime})

such that

\displaystyle\langle T_{g}^{\prime}(y^{\prime})x\Omega_{\rho},\Omega_{\rho}\rangle_{\rho}=\langle y^{\prime}T_{g}(x)\Omega_{\rho},\Omega_{\rho}\rangle_{\rho}\text{ for all }x\in M,y^{\prime}\in M^{\prime},g\in G.

Proof.

For
y^{\prime}\in M_{+}^{\prime}
and
g\in G
, consider the linear functional
\nu_{y^{\prime}}^{g}:M\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
defined by

\displaystyle\nu_{y^{\prime}}^{g}(x)=\langle y^{\prime}T_{g}(x)\Omega_{\rho},\Omega_{\rho}\rangle_{\rho}.

For
x\in M_{+}
, note that

\displaystyle\nu_{y^{\prime}}^{g}(x)

\displaystyle=\langle y^{\prime}T_{g}(x)\Omega_{\rho},\Omega_{\rho}\rangle_{\rho}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|y^{\prime}\right\|_{\infty}\langle T_{g}(x)\Omega_{\rho},\Omega_{\rho}\rangle_{\rho}

\displaystyle=\left\|y^{\prime}\right\|_{\infty}\rho(T_{g}(x))

\displaystyle=\left\|y^{\prime}\right\|_{\infty}\rho(x)

\displaystyle=\left\|y^{\prime}\right\|_{\infty}\langle x\Omega_{\rho},\Omega_{\rho}\rangle_{\rho}.

Hence, by Lemma
3.2
there exists a unique
z^{\prime}\in M^{\prime}
such that
\nu_{y^{\prime}}^{g}(x)=\langle z^{\prime}x\Omega_{\rho},\Omega_{\rho}\rangle_{\rho}
. Write
z^{\prime}=T_{g}^{\prime}(y^{\prime})
and then we have
\langle y^{\prime}T_{g}(x)\Omega_{\rho},\Omega_{\rho}\rangle_{\rho}=\langle T_{g}^{\prime}(y^{\prime})x\Omega_{\rho},\Omega_{\rho}\rangle_{\rho}
.

\Box

The following is the mean ergodic type theorem for the action of semigroup. This will be useful for the subsequent results.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
(M,G,T)
be a non-commutative dynamical system.
Suppose there exists a f.n state
\rho
satisfying
\rho(T_{g}(x)^{2})\leq\rho(x^{2})
for all
x\in M_{s}
and
g\in G
. Then for all
\mu\in M_{*}
, there exists a
\bar{\mu}\in M_{*}
such that

\displaystyle\bar{\mu}=\left\|\cdot\right\|_{1}-\lim_{l\to\infty}B_{l}(\mu),

where for
l\in\mathbb{K}
,
B_{l}(\mu):=\frac{1}{m(I_{l})}\int_{I_{l}}\beta_{g}(\mu)dm(g)
and
\beta_{g}(\mu)=\mu\circ T_{g}
for all
g\in G
and
\mu\in M_{*}
.

Proof.

Let
L^{2}(M_{s},\rho)
be the closure of
M_{s}
with respect to the norm induced from the inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\rho}
.
Then we can define the following contractions on the Hilbert space
L^{2}(M_{s},\rho)
.

\displaystyle u_{g}(x\Omega_{\rho})=T_{g}(x)\Omega_{\rho},x\in M_{s},g\in G.

For
l\in\mathbb{K}
, consider
T_{l}:=\frac{1}{m(I_{l})}\int_{I_{l}}u_{g}^{*}\ dm(g)
. Then by von Neumann mean ergodic theorem, it follows that for all
\xi\in L^{2}(M_{s},\rho)
,
T_{l}(\xi)
converges to
P\xi
strongly, where
P
is the orthogonal projection of
L^{2}(M_{s},\rho)
onto the subspace
\{\xi\in L^{2}(M_{s},\rho):u_{g}^{*}\xi=\xi\text{ for all }g\in G\}
.

Now let
x\in M
. Further write
x
as
x_{1}+ix_{2}
, where
x_{1},x_{2}\in M_{s}
and
then by the previous argument, it follows that
T_{l}(x\Omega):=T_{l}(x_{1}\Omega_{\rho})+iT_{l}(x_{2}\Omega_{\rho})
converges in
L^{2}(M,\rho)
.

Let
y_{1},y_{2}\in M^{\prime}(\sigma)
and define
\psi_{y_{1},y_{2}}(x)=\left\langle xy_{1}\Omega_{\rho},y_{2}\Omega_{\rho}\right\rangle
for all
x\in M
. Then there exists a
z\in M
such that
y_{1}^{*}y_{2}\Omega_{\rho}=z\Omega_{\rho}
, where
z=J\sigma_{i/2}(y_{2}^{*}y_{1})J
. Consequently for
x\in M
,

\displaystyle B_{l}(\psi_{y_{1},y_{2}})(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m(I_{l})}\int_{I_{l}}\left\langle T_{g}(x)y_{1}\Omega_{\rho},y_{2}\Omega_{\rho}\right\rangle_{\rho}dm(g)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m(I_{l})}\int_{I_{l}}\left\langle T_{g}(x)\Omega_{\rho},y_{1}^{*}y_{2}\Omega_{\rho}\right\rangle_{\rho}dm(g)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m(I_{l})}\int_{I_{l}}\left\langle T_{g}(x)\Omega_{\rho},z\Omega_{\rho}\right\rangle_{\rho}dm(g)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m(I_{l})}\int_{I_{l}}\left\langle x\Omega_{\rho},u_{g}^{*}(z\Omega_{\rho})\right\rangle_{\rho}dm(g)

\displaystyle=\left\langle x\Omega_{\rho},T_{l}(z\Omega_{\rho})\right\rangle_{\rho}.

Hence, for all
x\in M
,
B_{l}(\psi_{y_{1},y_{2}})(x)\rightarrow\left\langle x\Omega_{\rho},\eta\right\rangle_{\rho}
, where
\eta=\lim_{l\to\infty}T_{l}(z\Omega_{\rho})
. Consider
\overline{\psi}_{y_{1},y_{2}}\in M_{*}
, defined by

\displaystyle\overline{\psi}_{y_{1},y_{2}}(x)=\left\langle x\Omega_{\rho},\eta\right\rangle_{\rho},~~x\in M.

Then by standard argument it follows that
\overline{\psi}_{y_{1},y_{2}}\circ T_{g}=\overline{\psi}_{y_{1},y_{2}}
for all
g\in G
and

\displaystyle\overline{\psi}_{y_{1},y_{2}}=\left\|\cdot\right\|_{1}-\lim_{l\to\infty}B_{l}(\psi_{y_{1},y_{2}}).

Hence the result follows since the set
\{\psi_{y_{1},y_{2}}:y_{1},y_{2}\in M^{\prime}(\sigma)\}
is total in
M_{*}
.

\Box

4.
Maximal Inequality for the actions of
\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{d}
and
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}

In this section we consider state preserving actions of
\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{d}
and
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
on a von Neumann algebra
M
. Then prove a version of maximal inequality for the induced action on the predual von Neumann algebra
M_{*}
. We start with the following useful lemma which is well known in the literature. For a proof we refer to
[
BS23
, Lemma 4.1]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
M\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be a von Neumann algebra and
c\in M
such that
0\leq c\leq 1
. Suppose
x\in M
such that
0\leq x\leq s(c)
, then

x\in\overline{\{y\in M:0\leq y\leq nc\text{ for some }n\in\mathbb{N}\}}^{w\text{-topology}}.

For a semigroup action on a von Neumann algebra, to prove a maximal inequality for the induced action of semigroups on the predual, we need an auxiliary maximal inequality type theorem for the single induced map
T^{*}
on
M_{*}
, where
T
is a positive, sub-unital and state preserving map on
M
.
Such maximal inequality is already established in
[
BS23
]
, following similar ideas as in
[
Yea77
]
, but in a different context. We include the statement and for
the completeness of this article, we write a proof.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
M
be a von Neumann algebra with a f.n state
\rho
on
M
. Assume that
T:M\to M
is a sub-unital,
\rho
-preserving and
w
-continuous positive linear map. Let
\mu\in M_{*s}
and
\epsilon>0
. Then for any
N\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
e\in\mathcal{P}(M)
such that
\rho(1-e)<\left\|\mu\right\|/\epsilon
and

\displaystyle\left|S_{n}(\mu)(x)\right|\leq\epsilon\rho(x)\text{ for all }x\in(eMe)_{+}\text{ and }n\in\{1,\ldots,N\}.

Proof.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
and consider the
w
-compact subset
\mathcal{L}
of the von Neumann algebra
R:=\bigoplus_{n=1}^{2N}M
defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}:=\Big\{(\underline{x},\underline{y}):=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N},y_{1},\ldots,y_{N})\in R:x_{i},y_{i}\geq 0\ \text{ and }\sum_{i=1}^{N}(x_{i}+y_{i})\leq 1\Big\}.

Now consider the
w
-continuous linear functional
\kappa
on
R
defined by

\displaystyle\kappa((\underline{x},\underline{y})):=\sum_{n=1}^{N}n\Big[S_{n}(\mu^{\prime})(x_{n})-\rho(x_{n})-S_{n}(\mu^{\prime})(y_{n})-\rho(y_{n})\Big],\text{ for all }(\underline{x},\underline{y})\in R,

where,
\mu^{\prime}:=\mu/\epsilon
. Since
\mathcal{L}
is
w
-compact, the supremum value of the function
\kappa
on the set
\mathcal{L}
will be attained. Let
(\underline{x},\underline{y})\in\mathcal{L}
be such that
\kappa((\underline{x},\underline{y}))\geq\kappa((\underline{a},\underline{b}))
for all
(\underline{a},\underline{b})\in\mathcal{L}
.
We define
c:=1-\sum_{n=1}^{N}(x_{i}+y_{i})
. Note that
c\geq 0
. Let
c^{\prime}
be an element in
M
such that
0\leq c^{\prime}\leq c
. For
m\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
, take
\underline{x}^{\prime}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}+c^{\prime},\ldots,x_{N})
. We consider the point
(\underline{x}^{\prime},\underline{y})\in\mathcal{L}
, i.e,

\displaystyle(\underline{x}^{\prime},\underline{y})=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}+c^{\prime},\ldots,x_{N},y_{1},\ldots,y_{N}).

Then we have,

\displaystyle\kappa((\underline{x},\underline{y}))\geq\kappa((\underline{x}^{\prime},\underline{y})).

Therefore, we deduce the following

\displaystyle\kappa((\underline{x},\underline{y}))-\kappa((\underline{x}^{\prime},\underline{y}))\geq 0

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\qquad

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N}n\Big[S_{n}(\mu^{\prime})(x_{n})-\rho(x_{n})-S_{n}(\mu^{\prime})(y_{n})-\rho(y_{n})\Big]

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N}n\Big[S_{n}(\mu^{\prime})(x_{n})-\rho(x_{n})-S_{n}(\mu^{\prime})(y_{n})-\rho(y_{n})\Big]-m\Big[S_{m}(\mu^{\prime})(c^{\prime})-\rho(c^{\prime})\Big]\geq 0

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\qquad

\displaystyle-m\Big[S_{m}(\mu^{\prime})(c^{\prime})-\rho(c^{\prime})\Big]\geq 0

(4.1)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\qquad

\displaystyle S_{m}(\mu^{\prime})(c^{\prime})\leq\rho(c^{\prime}).

We again consider the following point in
\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle(\underline{x},\underline{y}^{\prime}):=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N},y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}+c^{\prime},\dots,y_{N}).

Then we have

\displaystyle\kappa((\underline{x},\underline{y}))\geq\kappa((\underline{x},\underline{y}^{\prime})).

Likewise as eq.
4
, we deduce

(4.2)

\displaystyle-S_{m}(\mu^{\prime})(c^{\prime})\leq\rho(c^{\prime}).

Hence by eq.
4
and eq.
4.2
, we have

(4.3)

\displaystyle\left|S_{m}(\mu)(c^{\prime})\right|\leq\epsilon\rho(c^{\prime})\text{ for all }c^{\prime}\in M\text{ such that }0\leq c^{\prime}\leq c.

Now if
x\in M
with
0\leq x\leq 1
and
x\leq nc
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
, consider
c^{\prime}:=\frac{x}{n}
. As,
c^{\prime}\leq c
, hence by eq.
4.3
we have

\displaystyle\left|S_{m}(\mu)(x)\right|\leq\epsilon\rho(x).

Let
e=s(c)
and
x\in eMe
with
0\leq x\leq e
. Since
\left|S_{m}(\mu)(\cdot)\right|,~\rho
are w-continuous, so by Lemma
4.1
, we have

\displaystyle\left|S_{m}(\mu)(x)\right|\leq\epsilon\rho(x).

And consequently we achieve

\displaystyle\left|S_{n}(\mu)(x)\right|\leq\epsilon\rho(x)\text{ for all }x\in(eMe)_{+}\text{ and }n\in\{1,\ldots,N\}.

We claim that
\rho(1-e)<\left\|\mu\right\|/\epsilon
.
Consider the point
(Tx_{2},\ldots,Tx_{N},0,Ty_{2},\ldots,Ty_{N},0)\in R
. Since
T(1)\leq 1
, we note that
(Tx_{2},\ldots,Tx_{N},0,Ty_{2},\ldots,Ty_{N},0)\in\mathcal{L}
.
Hence, we have

\displaystyle\kappa((\underline{x},\underline{y}))\geq\kappa((Tx_{2},\ldots,Tx_{N},0,Ty_{2},\ldots,Ty_{N},0)).

From this we deduce the following

\displaystyle\kappa((\underline{x},\underline{y}))\geq\kappa((Tx_{2},\ldots,Tx_{N},0,Ty_{2},\ldots,Ty_{N},0))

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\qquad

\displaystyle\mu^{\prime}(x_{1})-\rho(x_{1})+\cdots+\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\mu^{\prime}(T^{k}(x_{N}))-N\rho(x_{N})

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\mu^{\prime}(y_{1})-\rho(y_{1})-\cdots-\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\mu^{\prime}(T^{k}(y_{N}))-N\rho(y_{N})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mu^{\prime}(Tx_{2})-\rho(Tx_{2})+\cdots+\sum_{k=0}^{N-2}\mu^{\prime}(T^{k+1}x_{N})-(N-1)\rho(Tx_{N})

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\mu^{\prime}(Ty_{2})-\rho(Ty_{2})-\cdots-\sum_{k=0}^{N-2}\mu^{\prime}(T^{k+1}y_{N})-(N-1)\rho(Ty_{N})

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\qquad

\displaystyle\mu^{\prime}(x_{1})+\sum_{k=0}^{1}\mu^{\prime}(T^{k}(x_{2}))+\cdots+\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\mu^{\prime}(T^{k}(x_{N}))-\sum_{k=1}^{N}k\rho(x_{k})

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\mu^{\prime}(Tx_{2})-\sum_{k=0}^{1}\mu^{\prime}(T^{k+1}x_{k})-\cdots-\sum_{k=0}^{N-2}\mu^{\prime}(T^{k+1}x_{N})+\sum_{k=1}^{N}(k-1)\rho(x_{k})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mu^{\prime}(y_{1})+\sum_{k=0}^{1}\mu^{\prime}(T^{k}(y_{2}))+\cdots+\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\mu^{\prime}(T^{k}(y_{N}))+\sum_{k=1}^{k}k\rho(y_{k})

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\mu^{\prime}(Ty_{2})-\sum_{k=0}^{1}\mu^{\prime}(T^{k+1}y_{3})-\cdots-\sum_{k=0}^{N-2}\mu^{\prime}(T^{k+1}y_{N})-\sum_{k=1}^{N}(k-1)\rho(y_{k})

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\qquad

\displaystyle\mu^{\prime}(\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_{k})-\rho(\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_{k})\geq\mu^{\prime}(\sum_{k=1}^{N}y_{k})+\rho(\sum_{k=1}^{N}y_{k})

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\qquad

\displaystyle\mu^{\prime}(\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_{k}-\sum_{k=1}^{N}y_{k})\geq\rho(\sum_{k=1}^{N}x_{k}+\sum_{k=1}^{N}y_{k})

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\qquad

\displaystyle\left\|\mu\right\|/\epsilon\geq\rho(\sum_{i=1}^{N}(x_{i}+y_{i}))

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\qquad

\displaystyle\rho(c)\geq 1-\left\|\mu\right\|/\epsilon,\quad(\text{since }c=1-\sum_{i=1}^{N}(x_{i}+y_{i}))

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\qquad

\displaystyle\rho(e)\geq 1-\left\|\mu\right\|/\epsilon,\quad(\text{since }e=s(c)).

This completes the proof.

\Box

Definition 4.3
.

Let
(M,G,T)
be a non-commutative dynamical system and
\rho
be a f.n. state on
M
. Then
(M,G,T,\rho)
is called kernel if

(1)

\rho
is
G
-invariant and

(2)

T
is sub-unital, i.e,
T_{g}(1)\leq 1
for all
g\in G
.

Remark 4.4
.

Let
(M,G,T,\rho)
be a kernel, then observe the following.

(1)

By Kadison’s inequality
[
Kad52
]
, for all
x\in M_{s}
we have
T_{g}(x)^{2}\leq T_{g}(x^{2})
, and further as
\rho
is
G
-invariant, it follows that

\rho(T_{g}(x)^{2})\leq\rho(x^{2})\text{ for all }x\in M_{s}\text{ and }g\in G.

(2)

As
T_{g}
is a positive map on
M
, so by Russo-Dye theorem
[
Pau02
, Corollary 2.9]

\left\|T_{g}\right\|=\left\|T_{g}(1)\right\|\leq 1
. Thus, we have
\text{sup}_{g\in G}\left\|T_{g}\right\|\leq 1
.

4.1.
Action of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{+}

In this subsection, we consider
(M,\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{+},T)
, the non-commutative dynamical system asociated to
\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{+}
actions. Then note that there exists positive
d
-commuting maps
T_{1},T_{2},\cdots,T_{d}
on
M
such that
T_{(i_{1},\cdots,i_{d})}(\cdot)=T_{1}^{i_{1}}T_{2}^{i_{2}}\cdots T_{d}^{i_{d}}(\cdot)
for
(i_{1},\cdots,i_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{d}
. Then we prove a maximal inequality for the induced action on
M_{*}
.
An analogue of the following result is proved in
[
Kre85
, Theorem 3.4, pp-213]
for the case of classical
L^{1}
spaces. In our setup we require the exact same result in a general ordered Banach space
E
. Although the proof is similar, we write it for the sake of completeness.
We recall the following known result which is the main ingredient in this context.

Lemma 4.5
.

[
Kre85
]

Let
\xi(x)=1-\sqrt{1-x}
for
0\leq x\leq 1
. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, write
[\xi(x)]^{n}=\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}\alpha_{p}^{(n)}x^{p}
. Then

(i)

\alpha_{p}^{(n)}=\begin{cases}0&\text{ for }p<n\\
\frac{n}{2p}2^{n+1-2p}{2p-n-1\choose p-1}&\text{ for }p\geq n.\end{cases}

(ii)

If
\varphi(n)
denotes the greatest integer less than or equal to
\sqrt{n}+1
, then there exists
c>0
such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varphi(n)}\sum_{0\leq j<\varphi(n)}\alpha_{v+j}^{(j)}\alpha_{w+j}^{(j)}\geq\frac{c}{n^{2}}\text{ holds for all }0\leq v,w<n.

First we fix the following notation. For for an integer
d>1
notice that there is an unique
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
2^{m-1}<d\leq 2^{m}
. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
d>1
we fix
n_{d}:=\varphi^{m}(n)
, where
\varphi^{m}:=\varphi\circ\cdots\circ\varphi
(
m
times).

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
E
be an ordered Banach space and
d>1
be an integer. Then there exists
\chi_{d}>0
and a family
\{a(u):u=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{d}\}
of strictly positive numbers summing to
1
such that the following holds: If
T_{1},\ldots,T_{d}
are commuting positive contractions of
E
, then the operator

\displaystyle U=\sum_{u\in Z_{+}^{d}}a(u)T_{1}^{u_{1}}\cdots T_{d}^{u_{d}}

satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n^{d}}\sum_{0\leq i_{1}<n}\cdots\sum_{0\leq i_{d}<n}T_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots T_{d}^{i_{d}}f\leq\frac{\chi_{d}}{n_{d}}\sum_{j=0}^{n_{d}-1}U^{j}f

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
f\in E_{+}
.

Proof.

Enough to consider
d=2^{m}
for one can put
T_{j}=I
for all
d<j\leq 2^{m}
if
d<2^{m}
.
Let us denote by
\overline{\xi}(x)=\frac{\xi(x)}{x}
for
0<x\leq 1
. Then, by (i) of Lemma
4.5
we have

\displaystyle\overline{\xi}(x)=\sum_{\lambda=0}^{\infty}\alpha_{\lambda+1}^{(1)}x^{\lambda}.

Now if
T
is any contraction, then
\overline{\xi}(T)=\sum_{\lambda=0}^{\infty}\alpha_{\lambda+1}^{(1)}T^{\lambda}
is again a contraction. Consequently, since
\alpha_{\lambda}^{(j)}=0
for all
\lambda<j
we have

\displaystyle(\overline{\xi}(T))^{j}=\sum_{\lambda=0}^{\infty}\alpha_{j+\lambda}^{(j)}T^{\lambda}

for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
. When
d=2
we first consider the contraction

\displaystyle U=\overline{\xi}(T_{1})\circ\overline{\xi}(T_{2}).

Note that since
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
commutes we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varphi(n)}\sum_{j<\varphi(n)}U^{j}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\varphi(n)}\sum_{0\leq i_{1},i_{2}<n}\sum_{j<\varphi(n)}\alpha_{j+i_{1}}^{(j)}\alpha_{j+i_{1}}^{(j)}T_{1}^{i_{1}}T_{2}^{i_{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{c}{n^{2}}\sum_{0\leq i_{1},i_{2}<n}T_{1}^{i_{1}}T_{2}^{i_{2}}~~(\text{by Lemma }\ref{Krengel1985}~(ii)).

Therefore, the result holds for
d=2
with
\chi_{d}=1/c
. Next, assume that
d=4
. In this case consider the contraction

\displaystyle U=\overline{\xi}(U_{1})\circ\overline{\xi}(U_{2}),

where
U_{1}=\overline{\xi}(T_{1})\circ\overline{\xi}(T_{2})
and
U_{2}=\overline{\xi}(T_{3})\circ\overline{\xi}(T_{4})
. Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varphi(\varphi(n))}\sum_{0\leq j<n_{4}}U^{j}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\varphi(\varphi(n))}\sum_{0\leq j<\varphi(n)}U^{j}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\varphi(\varphi(n))}\sum_{0\leq i_{1},i_{2}<\varphi(n)}\sum_{j<\varphi(\varphi(n))}\alpha_{j+i_{1}}^{(j)}\alpha_{j+i_{1}}^{(j)}U_{1}^{i_{1}}U_{2}^{i_{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{c}{\varphi(n)^{2}}\sum_{0\leq i_{1},i_{2}<\varphi(n)}U_{1}^{i_{1}}U_{2}^{i_{2}}~~(\text{by Lemma }\ref{Krengel1985}~(ii))

\displaystyle\geq c\Big(\frac{1}{\varphi(n)}\sum_{0\leq i_{1}<\varphi(n)}U_{1}^{i_{1}}\Big)\circ\Big(\frac{1}{\varphi(n)}\sum_{0\leq i_{2}<\varphi(n)}U_{2}^{i_{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{c^{3}}{n^{4}}\sum_{0\leq i_{1},i_{2},i_{3},i_{4}<n}T_{1}^{i_{1}}T_{2}^{i_{2}}T_{3}^{i_{3}}T_{4}^{i_{4}}.

Therefore, the result holds for
d=3,4
with
\chi_{3}=\frac{1}{c^{3}}
and
\chi_{4}=\frac{1}{c^{4}}
respectively.

Now if for
d>1
the contractions
\{T_{1},\ldots,T_{2d}\}
are given, then following the method described above, first construct a contraction
U_{1}
using
\{T_{1},\ldots,T_{d}\}
, and then construct another contraction
U_{2}
using
\{T_{d+1},\ldots,T_{2d}\}
. Finally consider
U:=\overline{\xi}(U_{1})\circ\overline{\xi}(U_{2})
.

Moreover, observe that it follows from the construction that

U=\sum_{u\in Z_{+}^{d}}a(u)T_{1}^{u_{1}}\cdots T_{d}^{u_{d}},

where
a(u)>0
for all
u\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{d}
and
\sum_{u\in Z_{+}^{d}}a(u)=1
.

\Box

.

For

.

Definition 4.9
.

Remark 4.10
.

In particular, when
d>1
be an integer and
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, note that

\displaystyle M_{n}(f):=\frac{1}{n^{d}}\sum_{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{+}\cap Q_{n}}\int_{Q_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{d}}}T_{t}(f)dt,

Let
(M,G,T,\rho)
be a kernel. Then by Remark
4.4
and Theorem
3.4

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
(M,G,T,\rho)
be a kernel. Consider the following set

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{1}:=\{\nu-B_{k}(\nu)+\bar{\nu}:\quad k\in\mathbb{K},\ \nu\in M_{*+}\text{ with }\nu\leq\lambda\rho\text{ for some }\lambda>0\}.

(i)

Write
\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{W}_{1}-\mathcal{W}_{1}
, then
\mathcal{W}
is dense in

and

(ii)

for all

(5.1)

\displaystyle\lim_{l\to\infty}\sup_{x\in M_{+},x\neq 0}\left|(B_{l}(\nu)-\bar{\nu})(x)\right|/\rho(x)=0.

Proof.

(i):
Let
\mu\in M_{*+}
and
\epsilon>0
. From Theorem
3.1
, find a
\nu\in M_{*+}
with
\nu\leq\lambda\rho
for some
\lambda>0
, such that
\left\|\mu-\nu\right\|<\epsilon/2
. Further, by Theorem
3.4
we know that
B_{l}(\nu)
is convergent, and write
\bar{\nu}=\lim_{l\to\infty}B_{l}(\nu)
. So there exists a
l_{0}\in\mathbb{K}
such that
\left\|\bar{\nu}-B_{l_{0}}(\nu)\right\|\leq\epsilon/2
. Therefore by triangle inequality, we have

\left\|\mu-(\nu-B_{l_{0}}(\nu)+\bar{\nu})\right\|\leq\left\|\mu-\nu\right\|+\left\|B_{l_{0}}(\nu)-\bar{\nu}\right\|<\epsilon
.

Now for
\mu\in M_{*s}
, we write
\mu=\mu_{+}-\mu_{-}
, where
\mu_{+},\mu_{-}
are normal positive linear functional. Thus, it follows that
\mathcal{W}
is dense in
M_{*s}
.

(ii):
Fix
k\in\mathbb{K}
and consider
\nu_{k}:=\nu-B_{k}(\nu)+\bar{\nu}
and it is enough to prove eq.
5.1
for
\nu_{k}
. First we claim that
\bar{\nu}_{k}=\bar{\nu}
.

Since
\nu\leq\lambda\rho
, so by Lemma
3.2
there exists a unique
y_{1}^{\prime}\in M_{+}^{\prime}
with
y_{1}^{\prime}\leq\lambda
such that

\displaystyle\nu(x)=\langle y_{1}^{\prime}x\Omega_{\rho},\Omega_{\rho}\rangle_{\rho}\text{ for all }x\in M.

Let
y^{\prime}\in M^{\prime}
, write
B_{l}^{\prime}(y^{\prime}):=\frac{1}{m(I_{l})}\int_{I_{l}}T_{g}^{\prime}(y^{\prime})dm(g)
and by Lemma
3.3
, we have

\displaystyle\langle B_{l}^{\prime}(y^{\prime})x\Omega_{\rho},\Omega_{\rho}\rangle_{\rho}=\langle y^{\prime}B_{l}(x)\Omega_{\rho},\Omega_{\rho}\rangle_{\rho},\quad x\in M,y^{\prime}\in M^{\prime}.

Now for all
l\in\mathbb{K}
and
x\in M_{+}
, we note that

\displaystyle\left|(B_{l}(\nu_{k})-\bar{\nu})(x)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|(\nu_{k}-\bar{\nu})(B_{l}(x))\right|

\displaystyle=\left|(\nu-B_{k}(\nu))(B_{l}(x))\right|~~\text{ (since }\nu_{k}:=\nu-B_{k}(\nu)+\bar{\nu})

\displaystyle=\left|\nu(B_{l}(x))-\nu(B_{k}(B_{l}(x)))\right|~~\text{ (as }B_{k}(\nu)(\cdot)=\nu(B_{k}(\cdot)))

\displaystyle=\left|\left\langle y_{1}^{\prime}B_{l}(x)\Omega_{\rho},\Omega_{\rho}\right\rangle_{\rho}-\left\langle y_{1}^{\prime}B_{k}(B_{l}(x))\Omega_{\rho},\Omega_{\rho}\right\rangle_{\rho}\right|~~\text{ (as }\nu(\cdot)=\langle y_{1}^{\prime}(\cdot)\Omega_{\rho},\Omega_{\rho}\rangle_{\rho})

\displaystyle=\left|\left\langle y_{1}^{\prime}B_{l}(x)\Omega_{\rho},\Omega_{\rho}\right\rangle_{\rho}-\left\langle B_{k}^{\prime}(y_{1}^{\prime})(B_{l}(x))\Omega_{\rho},\Omega_{\rho}\right\rangle_{\rho}\right|,\text{ (by Lemma }\ref{au conv on dense set-1})

\displaystyle=\left|\left\langle(y_{1}^{\prime}-B_{k}^{\prime}(y_{1}^{\prime}))B_{l}(x)\Omega_{\rho},\Omega_{\rho}\right\rangle_{\rho}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\left\langle B_{l}^{\prime}(y_{1}^{\prime}-B_{k}^{\prime}(y_{1}^{\prime}))x\Omega_{\rho},\Omega_{\rho}\right\rangle_{\rho}\right|,\text{ (by Lemma }\ref{au conv on dense set-1})

\displaystyle\leq\left\|B_{l}^{\prime}(y_{1}^{\prime}-B_{k}^{\prime}(y_{1}^{\prime})\right\|\rho(x).

Further, for all
n\in\mathbb{K}
, note that

\displaystyle B_{l}^{\prime}(y_{1}^{\prime}-B_{k}^{\prime}(y_{1}^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m(I_{l})}\int_{I_{l}}\Big[T_{g}^{\prime}(y_{1}^{\prime})-T_{g}^{\prime}(B^{\prime}_{k}(y_{1}^{\prime}))\Big]dm(g)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m(I_{l})}\int_{I_{l}}\Big[T_{g}^{\prime}(y_{1}^{\prime})-T_{g}^{\prime}\Big(\frac{1}{m(I_{k})}\int_{I_{k}}T_{h}^{\prime}(y_{1}^{\prime})dm(h)\Big)\Big]dm(g)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m(I_{k})}\frac{1}{m(I_{l})}\int_{I_{k}}\int_{I_{l}}\Big(T_{g}^{\prime}(y_{1}^{\prime})-T^{\prime}_{hg}(y_{1}^{\prime})\Big)dm(g)dm(h)

\displaystyle\left\|B_{l}^{\prime}(y_{1}^{\prime}-B_{k}^{\prime}(y_{1}^{\prime}))\right\|\leq\frac{1}{m(I_{k})}\int_{I_{k}}\frac{m(I_{l}\Delta hI_{l})}{m(I_{l})}dm(h).

l\in\mathbb{K}

and bounded by

. Thus, applying DCT we get

(5.2)

\displaystyle\lim_{l\to\infty}\left\|B_{l}^{\prime}(y_{1}^{\prime}-B_{k}^{\prime}(y_{1}^{\prime}))\right\|\leq\frac{1}{m(I_{k})}\int_{I_{k}}\lim_{l\to\infty}\frac{m(I_{l}\Delta hI_{l})}{m(I_{l})}dm(h)=0.

Hence, we also obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{l\to\infty}\left\|B_{l}(\nu_{k})-\overline{\nu}\right\|=0.

\displaystyle=\lim_{l\to\infty}\sup_{x\in M_{+},x\neq 0}\left|(B_{l}(\nu_{k})-\bar{\nu})(x)\right|/\rho(x)=0.

This completes the proof.

1/2<w<1
.

We remark that such examples naturally arises to study ergodic converges of spherical averages of actions of free group action.
Let
G
be free group with generators
\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{r},a_{r}^{-1},\ldots,a_{r}^{-1}\}
. Let
\phi:G\to Aut(M)
denote a group homomorphism. Define,

\displaystyle\sigma_{n}:=\frac{1}{\left|W_{n}\right|}\sum_{a\in W_{n}}\phi(a),~n\in\mathbb{N},

where,
W_{n}
denotes the set of words of length
n
. It is well known that
\sigma_{m}\circ\sigma_{n}=\sigma_{m}\circ\sigma_{n}
for all
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
. Furthermore,

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}\circ\sigma_{n}=w\sigma_{n+1}+(1-w)\sigma_{n-1},~n\in\mathbb{N},

and
\sigma_{0}(x)=x
for all
x\in M
, where
1/2<w<1
is a fixed number.

Given a noncommutative dynamical system
(M,\sigma)
, we consider the following average

\displaystyle S_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{0}^{n}\sigma_{k}(x),~n\in\mathbb{N}.

Although, it is referred as spherical average in the context of free group action, we freely use the same terminology in our context as well.

6.1.
Mean convergence

In this subsection we prove mean convergence of spherical averages associated to a generalized kernel.

Definition 6.2
.

Consider a sequence of
w
-continuous maps
\sigma:=\{\sigma_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
and a f.n state
\rho
on
M
. Then
(M,\sigma,\rho)
is call called generalized kernel if it satisfies the following

(1)

\sigma_{1}
is completely positive,
\sigma_{1}(1)\leq 1
and
\sigma_{n}
is positive.

(2)

\sigma_{1}\circ\sigma_{n}=w\sigma_{n+1}+(1-w)\sigma_{n-1}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\sigma_{0}(x)=x
, where
1/2<w\leq 1
.

(3)

\rho\circ\sigma_{1}=\sigma_{1}\text{ and }\rho(y\sigma_{1}(x))=\rho(\sigma_{1}(y)x)\text{for all }x,y\in M
.

For given a
(M,\sigma,\rho)
, there exists a sequence of operators
\{u_{n}\}
on
L^{2}(M,\rho)
such that

\displaystyle u_{n}(x\Omega)=\sigma_{n}(x)\Omega,~~x\in M.

Then immediately observe that

(1)

u_{n}
is self adjoint and

u_{m}\circ u_{n}=u_{n}\circ u_{m}
for all
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
.

(2)

There exists
1/2<w\leq 1
such that
u_{1}\circ u_{n}=wu_{n+1}+(1-w)u_{n-1},~n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Remark 6.3
.

Consider the following set;

\displaystyle D_{w}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\left|\sqrt{z+4w-4w^{2}}+\sqrt{z-4w+4w^{2}}\right|\leq 2\sqrt{w}~\text{and}~

\displaystyle\left|\sqrt{z+4w-4w^{2}}-\sqrt{z-4w+4w^{2}}\right|\leq 2\sqrt{w}\}.

Then by
[
Wal97
]
, we note that
\sigma(u_{1})\subseteq D_{w}
.

Then we recall the following mean ergodic type theorem in this context, the proof follows from Remark
6.3
and
[
Wal97
]
.

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
(M,\sigma,\rho)
be a kernel, then the ergodic averages
\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{0}^{n}u_{n}
converges strongly on
L^{2}(M,\rho)
to a projection onto the space
\{\eta\in L^{2}(M,\rho):v_{1}\eta=\eta\}
.

Remark 6.5
.

We note that the above situation is automatic for free group action. Indeed, let
\rho
be a f.n state on
M
such that
\rho\circ\phi(a)(x)=\rho(x)
for all
x\in M
and for all
a\in G
, Then consider the following spherical
average we can define self adjoints
\{t_{a}\}_{a\in G}
on
L^{2}(M,\rho)
by

\displaystyle\sigma_{n}=\frac{1}{\left|W_{n}\right|}\sum_{a\in W_{n}}\phi_{a}.

Then
(M,\sigma,\rho)
becomes a kernel.

Theorem 6.6
.

Let
(M,\sigma,\rho)
be a kernel. For all
\mu\in M_{*}
, there exists a
\bar{\mu}\in M_{*}
such that

\displaystyle\bar{\mu}=\left\|\cdot\right\|_{1}-\lim_{n\to\infty}S_{n}(\mu),

where, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\sigma_{n}(\mu)=\mu\circ\sigma_{n}
,
\mu\in M_{*}
.

Proof.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
u_{n}
be the associated self adjoint operator defined by
u_{n}(x\Omega_{\rho})=\sigma_{n}(x)\Omega_{\rho}
for
x\in M
. consider
T_{n}=\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{0}^{n}u_{k}
. Let
y_{1},y_{2}\in M^{\prime}(\sigma)
and define
\psi_{y_{1},y_{2}}(x)=\left\langle xy_{1}\Omega_{\rho},y_{2}\Omega_{\rho}\right\rangle
for all
x\in M
. Then there exists a
z\in M
such that
y_{1}^{*}y_{2}\Omega_{\rho}=z\Omega_{\rho}
, where
z=J\sigma_{i/2}(y_{2}^{*}y_{1})J
. Consequently, for
x\in M

\displaystyle S_{n}(\psi_{y_{1},y_{2}})(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{0}^{n}\left\langle\sigma_{k}(x)y_{1}\Omega,y_{2}\Omega\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{0}^{n}\left\langle u_{k}(x\Omega),z\Omega\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{0}^{n}\left\langle x\Omega,u_{k}(z\Omega)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle x\Omega,\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{0}^{n}u_{k}(z\Omega)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle x\Omega,T_{n}(z\Omega)\right\rangle

Therefore, by Theorem
6.4
we obtain that for all
x\in M
,
S_{n}(\psi_{y_{1},y_{2}})(x)\rightarrow\left\langle x\Omega,\eta\right\rangle
, where
\eta=\lim_{n\to\infty}T_{n}(z\Omega)
. Observe that
u_{1}\eta=\eta
. By induction argument
u_{n}\eta=\eta
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Now let
x\in M
. Further write
x
as
x_{1}+ix_{2}
, where
x_{1},x_{2}\in M_{s}
and
then by the previous argument, it follows that
T_{n}(x\Omega):=T_{n}(x_{1}\Omega_{\rho})+iT_{n}(x_{2}\Omega_{\rho})
converges in
L^{2}(M,\rho)
.

Now consider
\overline{\psi}_{y_{1},y_{2}}\in M_{*}
, defined by

\displaystyle\overline{\psi}_{y_{1},y_{2}}(x)=\left\langle x\Omega_{\rho},\eta\right\rangle_{\rho},~~x\in M.

Then by a standard argument it follows that
\overline{\psi}_{y_{1},y_{2}}\circ\sigma_{n}=\overline{\psi}_{y_{1},y_{2}}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and

\displaystyle\overline{\psi}_{y_{1},y_{2}}=\left\|\cdot\right\|_{1}-\lim_{n\to\infty}S_{n}(\psi_{y_{1},y_{2}}).

Hence the result follows since the set
\{\psi_{y_{1},y_{2}}:y_{1},y_{2}\in M^{\prime}(\sigma)\}
is total in
M_{*}
.

Theorem 6.7
.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{l=0}^{n}\kappa_{l}(x)\leq C_{w}\frac{1}{3n+1}\sum_{l=0}^{3n}\kappa_{1}^{l}(x).

Observe that a simple calculation implies that the composition power

\kappa_{l}

Now a calculation similar to the proof of
[

estimates the coefficients

and the results follows.

Lemma 6.9
.

Let
(M,\sigma)
be a generalized noncommutative dynamical system and

\rho
be a f.n. state on
M
such that
\rho\circ\sigma_{1}=\rho
. Then there exists a sequence of maps
\{\sigma_{n}^{\prime}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
on
M^{\prime}
such that

(1)

\langle\sigma_{n}^{\prime}(y^{\prime})x\Omega_{\rho},\Omega_{\rho}\rangle_{\rho}=\langle y^{\prime}\sigma_{n}(x)\Omega_{\rho},\Omega_{\rho}\rangle_{\rho}\text{ for all }x\in M,y^{\prime}\in M^{\prime},n\in\mathbb{N}.

\sigma^{\prime}_{m}\circ\sigma^{\prime}_{n}=\sigma^{\prime}_{n}\circ\sigma^{\prime}_{m}
for all

(3)

\sigma^{\prime}_{1}\circ\sigma^{\prime}_{n}=w\sigma^{\prime}_{n+1}+(1-w)\sigma^{\prime}_{n-1},~n\in\mathbb{N}

(M^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})

For
(1)
let
y^{\prime}\in M_{+}^{\prime}
and

, consider the linear functional
\nu_{y^{\prime}}^{n}:M\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
defined by

\displaystyle\nu_{y^{\prime}}^{n}(x)=\langle y^{\prime}\sigma_{n}(x)\Omega_{\rho},\Omega_{\rho}\rangle_{\rho}.

For

, note that

\displaystyle\nu_{y^{\prime}}^{n}(x)

\displaystyle=\langle y^{\prime}\sigma_{n}(x)\Omega_{\rho},\Omega_{\rho}\rangle_{\rho}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|y^{\prime}\right\|\langle\sigma_{n}(x)\Omega_{\rho},\Omega_{\rho}\rangle_{\rho}

\displaystyle=\left\|y^{\prime}\right\|\rho(\sigma_{n}(x))

\displaystyle=\left\|y^{\prime}\right\|\rho(x)

\displaystyle=\left\|y^{\prime}\right\|\langle x\Omega_{\rho},\Omega_{\rho}\rangle_{\rho}.

Hence, by Lemma
3.2
there exists a unique
z^{\prime}\in M^{\prime}
such that
\nu_{y^{\prime}}^{n}(x)=\langle z^{\prime}x\Omega_{\rho},\Omega_{\rho}\rangle_{\rho}

z^{\prime}=\sigma_{n}^{\prime}(y^{\prime})
and then we have
\langle y^{\prime}\sigma_{n}(x)\Omega_{\rho},\Omega_{\rho}\rangle_{\rho}=\langle\sigma_{n}^{\prime}(y^{\prime})x\Omega_{\rho},\Omega_{\rho}\rangle_{\rho}
. Note that
(2)
and
(3)
follows immediately from
(1)
.

Proposition 6.10
.

With the above notation, for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
we have,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\|S_{n}^{\prime}(y^{\prime}-\sigma_{k}^{\prime}(y^{\prime}))\right\|=0.

Proof.

First, we note that for all
n,k\in\mathbb{N}
and
y^{\prime}\in M^{\prime}
, we have

\displaystyle S_{n}^{\prime}(y^{\prime}-\sigma_{k+1}^{\prime}(y^{\prime}))

(6.1)

\displaystyle\quad=(1-\frac{1}{w})S_{n}^{\prime}(y^{\prime}-\sigma_{k-1}^{\prime}(y^{\prime}))-\frac{1}{w}\sigma^{\prime}_{1}(S_{n}^{\prime}(\sigma^{\prime}_{k}(y^{\prime})-y^{\prime}))-\frac{1}{w}S_{n}^{\prime}(\sigma^{\prime}_{1}(y^{\prime})-y^{\prime}).

Indeed, For all
n,k\in\mathbb{N}
we note that

\displaystyle S_{n}^{\prime}(y^{\prime}-\sigma_{k+1}^{\prime}(y^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=S_{n}^{\prime}\Big[y^{\prime}-\frac{1}{w}\Big(\sigma^{\prime}_{1}\circ\sigma^{\prime}_{k}(y^{\prime})-(1-w)\sigma_{k-1}^{\prime}(y^{\prime})\Big)\Big]

\displaystyle=S_{n}^{\prime}\Big(y^{\prime}-\sigma_{k-1}^{\prime}(y^{\prime})\Big)-\frac{1}{w}S_{n}^{\prime}\Big(\sigma^{\prime}_{1}\circ\sigma^{\prime}_{k}(y^{\prime})-\sigma_{k-1}^{\prime}(y^{\prime})\Big).

Also we have,

\displaystyle S_{n}^{\prime}\Big(\sigma^{\prime}_{1}\circ\sigma^{\prime}_{k}(y^{\prime})-\sigma_{k-1}^{\prime}(y^{\prime})\Big)

\displaystyle=S_{n}^{\prime}\Big(\sigma^{\prime}_{1}\circ\sigma^{\prime}_{k}(y^{\prime})-\sigma^{\prime}_{1}(y^{\prime})+\sigma^{\prime}_{1}(y^{\prime})-y^{\prime}+y^{\prime}-\sigma_{k-1}^{\prime}(y^{\prime})\Big)

\displaystyle=\sigma^{\prime}_{1}(S_{n}^{\prime}(\sigma^{\prime}_{k}(y^{\prime})-y^{\prime}))+S_{n}^{\prime}(\sigma^{\prime}_{1}(y^{\prime})-y^{\prime})+S_{n}^{\prime}(y^{\prime}-\sigma_{k-1}^{\prime}(y^{\prime})).

Therefore, combining the above two equality we obtain eq.
6.3
.

For
k=0
, the result follows immediately. For
k=1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|S_{n}^{\prime}(y^{\prime}-\sigma_{1}^{\prime}(y^{\prime}))\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{l=0}^{n}\sigma_{l}^{\prime}(y^{\prime}-\sigma_{1}^{\prime}(y^{\prime}))\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\frac{w}{n+1}(y^{\prime}-\sigma_{n+1}^{\prime}(y^{\prime}))+\frac{1-w}{n+1}\sigma_{n}^{\prime}(y^{\prime})\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1+w}{n+1}\left\|y^{\prime}\right\|

Therefore, the result is true for
k=1
. Also when
k=2
, then we have

\displaystyle\left\|S_{n}^{\prime}(y^{\prime}-\sigma_{2}^{\prime}(y^{\prime}))\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{l=0}^{n}\sigma_{l}^{\prime}(y^{\prime}-\sigma_{2}^{\prime}(y^{\prime}))\right\|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{w}\left\|\sigma^{\prime}_{1}(S_{n}^{\prime}(y^{\prime}-\sigma^{\prime}_{1}(y^{\prime})))+S_{n}^{\prime}(y^{\prime}-\sigma^{\prime}_{1}(y^{\prime}))\right\|~(\text{by eq. }\ref{double sum-free group})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{w}\left\|S_{n}^{\prime}(y^{\prime}-\sigma^{\prime}_{1}(y^{\prime}))\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2(1+w)}{w(n+1)}\left\|y^{\prime}\right\|~\text{(from the case $k=1$)}

Hence the result is true for
k=2
. Now let us assume that the result is true for
\{3,4,\ldots,k\}
where
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Now, by eq.
6.3
it follows that

\displaystyle\left\|S_{n}^{\prime}(y^{\prime}-\sigma_{k+1}^{\prime}(y^{\prime}))\right\|

\displaystyle\leq(1-\frac{1}{w})\left\|S_{n}^{\prime}(y^{\prime}-\sigma_{k-1}^{\prime}(y^{\prime}))\right\|+\frac{1}{w}\left\|\sigma^{\prime}_{1}(S_{n}^{\prime}(\sigma^{\prime}_{k}(y^{\prime})-y^{\prime}))\right\|+\frac{1}{w}\left\|S_{n}^{\prime}(\sigma^{\prime}_{1}(y^{\prime})-y^{\prime})\right\|

\displaystyle\leq(1-\frac{1}{w})\left\|S_{n}^{\prime}(y^{\prime}-\sigma_{k-1}^{\prime}(y^{\prime}))\right\|+\frac{1}{w}\left\|S_{n}^{\prime}(\sigma^{\prime}_{k}(y^{\prime})-y^{\prime})\right\|+\frac{1}{w}\left\|S_{n}^{\prime}(\sigma^{\prime}_{1}(y^{\prime})-y^{\prime})\right\|.

Hence by induction hypothesis, the result follows for
k+1
. Therefore, the results holds for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Lemma 6.11
.

Let
(M,\sigma,\rho)
be a generalized kernel.
Consider the following set

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{1}:=\{\nu-S_{k}(\nu)+\bar{\nu}:\quad k\in\mathbb{N},\ \nu\in M_{*+}\text{ with }\nu\leq\lambda\rho\text{ for some }\lambda>0\}.

Abstract
. Representation theory of Lie (super)algebras has garnered significant research attention over many years, specially due to its applications in theoretical physics; in this regard, representation theory of affine Lie (super)algebras is of great importance. To characterize simple modules over affine Lie (super)algebras, we need to address the cases of nonzero and critical level separately. Although a vast amount of research exists on the representation theory of affine Lie (super)algebras, investigations regarding general modules at the critical level are few. In the current paper, we focus on twisted affine Lie superalgebras ℒ \mathcal{L} ( ℒ 1 ≠ { 0 } \mathcal{L}_{1}\neq\{0\} ). The even part of almost all such ℒ \mathcal{L} ’s contains two affine Lie algebras and in turn, the behavior of the restricted modules corresponding to these affine components and their interactions play important roles in characterizing simple ℒ \mathcal{L} -modules. In this paper, we deal with ℒ \mathcal{L} -modules, at the critical level, for which only one of the restricted modules corresponding to the affine components of ℒ 0 \mathcal{L}_{0} , is integrable. The results are important from two perspectives: first, we provide a characterization of a class of modules at the critical level and second, we will use this to characterize general modules at the critical level.

1.
Introduction

The study of Lie (super)algebras and their representations
have attracted significant research attention, specially because of their applications in various domains of mathematics and physics; (see for instance
[
8
]
and
[
12
]
). For example, affine Lie (super)algebras are important tools in number theory, group theory, knot theory, and quantum field theory; see e.g.,
[
4
,
10
,
11
]
. Affine Lie (super)algebras were introduced and classified by V. Kac and R. Moody in non-super case and by J.W. Van de Leur in super case; according to their classification, affine Lie (super)algebras are divided into untwisted and twisted types, see
[
9
,
15
]
(see also
[
18
, Appendix A]
).

An affine Lie (super)algebra
\mathcal{L}
contains a Cartan subalgebra
{\mathcal{H}}
with respect to which
\mathcal{L}
has a root space decomposition. The first step toward the classification of
\mathcal{L}
-modules is dealing with finite weight
\mathcal{L}
-modules, i.e.,
\mathcal{L}
-modules having weight space decomposition with respect to
{\mathcal{H}}
with finite weight multiplicities. An affine Lie (super)algebra contains a canonical central element acting on a simple finite weight module
as a scalar called the
level
of the module.

There has been a vast amount of research on module theory of affine Lie (super)algebras and in most of them, the characterization is reduced to the characterization of modules over finite dimensional Lie (super)algebras using a kind of induction called the parabolic induction:
A parabolic subset of the root system
\Phi
of a Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{k}
, having root space decomposition, is a subset
P\subseteq\Phi
satisfying

{{\Phi}=P\cup-P\quad\hbox{and}\quad(P+P)\cap{\Phi}\subseteq P}.

This gives a decomposition
\Phi=(P\setminus-P)\cup(P\cap-P)\cup(-P\setminus P)
for
\Phi
and respectively a decomposition

\mathfrak{k}=\mathfrak{k}^{+}_{{}_{P}}\oplus\mathfrak{k}^{\circ}_{{}_{P}}\oplus\mathfrak{k}^{-}_{{}_{P}}

for
\mathfrak{k}.
A parabolically induced
\mathfrak{k}
-module
V
is a module induced from a module
\Omega
over
\mathfrak{k}^{\circ}_{{}_{P}}
, in a prescribed way including
\mathfrak{k}^{+}_{{}_{P}}\Omega=\{0\}
, for some parabolic subset
P
. The most tricky part to prove that a simple finite weight
\mathfrak{k}
-module is parabolically induced,
is finding an appropriate parabolic subset
P
for which the set of vectors vanishing by root vectors corresponding to
\mathfrak{k}^{+}_{{}_{P}}
is nonzero. Using this nonzero subset, it is not difficult to find a simple module over
\mathfrak{k}^{\circ}_{{}_{P}}
.

Parabolic induction has been used in different contexts; S.L. Fernando showed that a finite weight module over a finite dimensional reductive Lie algebra
is parabolically induced from a module on which all nonzero root vectors act injectively. Then, Mathieu
[
14
]
continued Fernando’s work by classifying these mentioned modules. The study of simple finite weight modules over finite dimensional Lie superalgebras was started by I. Dimitrov and his colleagues in
[
5
]
; according to their results, the characterization of these modules is reduced to the study of so-called cuspidal modules over certain finite dimensional Lie superalgebras.

Switching from finite dimensional Lie (super)algebras to affine Lie (super)algebras has naturally some complexity
due to the existence of imaginary roots i.e., roots which are orthogonal to all other roots. Moreover, in super case, we have some roots which are self-orthogonal but not orthogonal to the entire root system; this in turn makes the study more complicated.

The key point in module theory of affine Lie (super)algebras is the behavior of the actions of real root vectors. More precisely, real root vectors act on a simple module either injevtively or locally nilpotently. This, in turn, helps us to divide the category
\mathscr{C}
of finite weight
\mathcal{L}
-modules into several subcategories. The nature of simple objects in each subcategory, offers an exclusive approach to get a characterization.
The first subcategory which one can think of is the subcategory consisting of integrable modules, i.e., those modules on which real root vectors act nilpotently.
The study of integrable modules, in the non-super case, was initiated by V. Chari and A. Pressley
[
3
,
2
]
; the focus of their papers
is the study of simple integrable finite weight modules over affine Lie algebras.
They show that these modules are either highest-lowest weight modules or so-called loop modules.

In the super case, V.G. Kac and M. Wakimoto
[
11
]
noticed that for almost all untwisted affine Lie superalgebras
\mathcal{L}
, there is no nonzero level integrable module.
Then they defined so-called weakly-integrable
\mathcal{L}
-modules and classified weakly-integrable highest weight simple
\mathcal{L}
-modules. It was shown in

[
6
]
that all simple nonzero level weakly-integrable finite weight
\mathcal{L}
-modules are highest weight modules. One of the properties of a weakly integrable
\mathcal{L}
-module is that its restriction to one of the affine components of the zero part of
\mathcal{L}
is integrable. In
[
16
]
, quasi-integrable modules were defined for twisted Affine Lie superalgebras
\mathcal{L}
. They are also modules whose restrictions to one of the affine components of the zero part of
\mathcal{L}
is integrable but against nonzero level simple weakly-integrable finite weight modules, quasi-integrable modules are not necessary highest weight modules.
It was shown that each nonzero level quasi-integrable module over a twisted affine Lie superalgebra is parabolically induced from a module studied in
[
5
]
.

The focus of the present paper is on the subcategory of
\mathscr{C},
whose objects are critical level quasi-integrable
\mathcal{L}
-modules (
\mathcal{L}
is a twisted affine Lie superalgebr whose zero part has two affine components) but for our further use, we will consider a more general case; more precisely, we will characterize simple quasi-integrable modules not only over
\mathcal{L}
but over subalgebras of
\mathcal{L}.

We notice that a parabolic induction based on a parabolic subset
P
, of the root system of
\mathcal{L},
is useful only if we know the structure and the characterization of
\mathcal{L}^{\circ}_{{}_{P}}
-modules. In this regard, we know from one of our preprints that results we are presenting here
will also provide us with the information we need to complete the characterization of simple objects of the category
\mathscr{C}.

Here is the outline of the paper. In Section 2, we gather some information we need for our main theorem and Section 3 is devoted to our main result.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Generic Information

In this short subsection, to unify the notations, we gather some generic definitions and
notions. The underlying field, for all vector (super)spaces, is the field of complex numbers

Suppose that

(2)

\mathfrak{k}_{i}{V}_{j}\subseteq{V}_{i+j}

(3)

{V}=\oplus_{\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{\ast}}{V}^{\lambda}

{V}^{\lambda}:=\{v\in{V}\mid hv=\lambda(h)v\;\;(h\in\mathfrak{h})\}
for each
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{\ast}(\hbox{\rm dual space of $\mathfrak{h}$}).

In this setting, an element
\lambda
of the

\hbox{\rm supp}({V}):=\{\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}\mid{V}^{\lambda}\neq\{0\}\},

is called a

of

. If all weight spaces are finite dimensional, then the module

is called a

If

is an
\mathfrak{h}
-weight module via the adjoint representation, we say that

has a root space decomposition with respect to
\mathfrak{h}
; the set of weights of

is said to be the

and weights, weight vectors and weight spaces are termed respectively

,

and

Next, assume that

Since the form on

Now, we assume

\begin{array}[]{ll}\hbox{\footnotesize$S_{re}=\{\alpha\in S\mid(\alpha,\alpha)\neq 0\},$}&\hbox{\footnotesize$S_{im}=\{\alpha\in S\mid(\alpha,\Phi)=\{0\}\}$},\\
\hbox{\footnotesize$S_{ns}=\{\alpha\in S\setminus S_{im}\mid(\alpha,\alpha)=0\},$}&\hbox{\footnotesize$S^{\times}=S\setminus{S_{im}}.$}\end{array}

Elements of

Next assume

we denote by

-weight

-module

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{g},\phi
and
\Delta
be as above and define a functional

\bm{\zeta}:\hbox{\rm span}_{\mathbb{R}}\Delta(=\hbox{\rm span}_{\mathbb{R}}\dot{\phi})\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}

.
Then, there is a nonzero weight vector

such that

\mathfrak{k}^{\dot{\alpha}+n\delta}v=\{0\}

and
\dot{\alpha}\in\dot{\phi}
with
\bm{\zeta}(\dot{\alpha})>0
.

Proof.

Set

r:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&X=Y^{(1)},\\
3&X=D_{4}^{(3)},\\
2&\hbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Using (
2.6
) and (
2.7
) as well as the same argument as in

{\mu+\dot{\gamma}+rn\delta\not\in\hbox{\rm supp}(V)\quad\hbox{and}\quad 2(\mu,\dot{\gamma})/(\dot{\gamma},\dot{\gamma})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{\geq 0}\quad(\dot{\gamma}\in{\dot{\phi}_{ind}}^{+}(\Delta),n\in{\mathbb{Z}}).}

(2.9)

\mathfrak{k}^{\dot{\alpha}+n\delta}v=\{0\}\quad(v\in V^{\mu},~\dot{\alpha}\in{\dot{\phi}_{ind}}^{+}(\Delta),~n\in r{\mathbb{Z}}).

Next pick

If

then, we are done. So we assume
\mathfrak{k}^{\dot{\alpha}+n\delta}\mathfrak{k}^{\dot{\alpha}_{*}+m_{*}\delta}v\neq\{0\}
and show that it leads to a contradiction. We have
\mu+(\dot{\alpha}+\dot{\alpha}_{*})+(m_{*}+n)\delta\in\hbox{\rm supp}(V).

Hence, we can contemplate the following scenarios.

(i)

If
\dot{\alpha},\dot{\alpha}_{*}\in\dot{\phi}_{ind},
then since either
2\frac{(\dot{\alpha}_{*}+\dot{\alpha},\dot{\alpha})}{(\dot{\alpha},\dot{\alpha})}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{>0}
or
2\frac{(\dot{\alpha}_{*}+\dot{\alpha},\dot{\alpha}_{*})}{(\dot{\alpha}_{*},\dot{\alpha}_{*})}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{>0}
, we get using (
2.6
), (
2.8
) and (
2.11
) that
\mu+\dot{\alpha}\in\hbox{\rm supp}(V)
or
\mu+\dot{\alpha}_{*}\in\hbox{\rm supp}(V)
contradicting (
2.8
).

(ii)

If exactly one of
\dot{\alpha},\dot{\alpha}_{*}
belongs to
\dot{\phi}_{ind},
then, without loss of generality, we assume
\dot{\alpha}=2\dot{\beta}
for some short root
\dot{\beta}
and
\dot{\alpha}_{*}\in\dot{\phi}_{ind}.
We have
2\frac{(\mu+2\dot{\beta}+\dot{\alpha}_{*},\dot{\beta})}{(\dot{\beta},\dot{\beta})}>0
. Using (
2.6
), this implies that
\mu+\dot{\beta}+\dot{\alpha}_{*}+(m_{*}+n)\delta\in\hbox{\rm supp}(V)
. We get a contradiction as in the previous case.

(iii)

If
\dot{\alpha},\dot{\alpha}_{*}\in\dot{\phi}_{ex},
then, we have
\dot{\alpha}=2\dot{\beta}
and
\dot{\alpha}_{*}=2\dot{\beta}^{\prime}
for some

\dot{\beta},\dot{\beta}^{\prime}\in{\dot{\phi}}_{sh}.

In particular, we have

(2.12)

\dot{\beta}^{\prime}+(m_{*}+n)\delta\in\phi\quad\hbox{and}\quad 2(\dot{\beta}+\dot{\beta}^{\prime},\dot{\beta}^{\prime})/(\dot{\beta}^{\prime},\dot{\beta}^{\prime})>0.

This together with (
2.6
)
implies that

\mu+2\dot{\beta}+\dot{\beta}^{\prime}=\mu+2(\dot{\beta}+\dot{\beta}^{\prime})+(m_{*}+n)\delta-(\dot{\beta}^{\prime}+(m_{*}+n)\delta)\in\hbox{\rm supp}(V).

Similarly, we get

\mu+\dot{\beta}+\dot{\beta}^{\prime}=\mu+2\dot{\beta}+\dot{\beta}^{\prime}-\dot{\beta}\in\hbox{\rm supp}(V),

and finally, as
2(\mu+\dot{\beta}+\dot{\beta}^{\prime},\dot{\beta}^{\prime})/(\dot{\beta}^{\prime},\dot{\beta}^{\prime})>0,

we have

\mu+\dot{\beta}=\mu+\dot{\beta}+\dot{\beta}^{\prime}-\dot{\beta}^{\prime}\in\hbox{\rm supp}(V)

which is a contradiction due to (
2.8
).

The proof is now complete.
∎

\upsilon_{n}:=\mu_{{}_{\kappa n_{0}+\kappa n}}=\mu_{\kappa n_{0}}+\kappa n\dot{\alpha}_{*}+(m_{\kappa n_{0}}+\cdots+m_{\kappa n-1})\delta\in\hbox{\rm supp}(V)\quad(n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{>0}).

Since real roots of
T(2)
are up-nilpotent hybrid and all real roots of
T(1)
are locally nilpotent, recalling (
3.4
)(1), for a large enough
m^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{>0},
we have

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\kappa\dot{\zeta}_{*}+k_{\kappa\dot{\zeta}_{*}}\delta+r_{\kappa\dot{\zeta}_{*}}m^{\prime}\delta\in T^{ln}(V),~~-(\kappa\dot{\zeta}_{*}+k_{\kappa\dot{\zeta}_{*}}\delta+r_{\kappa\dot{\zeta}_{*}}m^{\prime}\delta)\in T^{in}(V)&\hbox{if $\kappa\dot{\zeta}_{*}\in\dot{T}(2)$}\\
\kappa\dot{\zeta}_{*}+k_{\kappa\dot{\zeta}_{*}}\delta+r_{\kappa\dot{\zeta}_{*}}m^{\prime}\delta\in T^{ln}(V),~~-(\kappa\dot{\zeta}_{*}+k_{\kappa\dot{\zeta}_{*}}\delta+r_{\kappa\dot{\zeta}_{*}}m^{\prime}\delta)\in T^{ln}(V)&\hbox{if $\kappa\dot{\zeta}_{*}\in\dot{T}(1).$}\end{array}\right.

This, in particular, together with (
3.15
) and (
3.8
) implies that

\displaystyle\upsilon_{n}-i(\kappa\dot{\zeta}_{*}+k_{\kappa\dot{\zeta}_{*}}\delta+r_{\kappa\dot{\zeta}_{*}}m^{\prime}\delta)\in\hbox{\rm supp}(V)\quad(n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{>0},~1\leq i\leq n),

in particular, for

p_{n}:=(m_{\kappa n_{0}}+\cdots+m_{\kappa n-1})-n(k_{\kappa\dot{\zeta}_{*}}+r_{\kappa\dot{\zeta}_{*}}m^{\prime})\quad\quad(n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{>0}),

we have

\displaystyle\mu_{\kappa n_{0}}+n(\kappa\dot{\xi}_{*})+p_{n}\delta=

\displaystyle\mu_{\kappa n_{0}}+n(\kappa\dot{\xi}_{*})+(m_{\kappa n_{0}}+\cdots+m_{\kappa n-1})\delta-n(k_{\kappa\dot{\zeta}_{*}}+r_{\kappa\dot{\zeta}_{*}}m^{\prime})\delta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\upsilon_{n}-n(\kappa\dot{\zeta}_{*}+k_{\kappa\dot{\zeta}_{*}}\delta+r_{\kappa\dot{\zeta}_{*}}m^{\prime}\delta)\in\hbox{\rm supp}(V)\quad(n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{>0}).

Therefore, we have

(3.16)

\mu_{\kappa n_{0}}+n(\kappa\dot{\xi}_{*}+k_{\kappa\dot{\xi}_{*}}\delta)+(p_{n}-nk_{\kappa\dot{\xi}_{*}})\delta=\mu_{\kappa n_{0}}+n(\kappa\dot{\xi}_{*})+p_{n}\delta\in\hbox{\rm supp}(V)

for
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{>0}.

We next note that for each
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{>0},
using the quotient algorithm, we get
q_{n}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
0\leq s_{n}<r_{\kappa\dot{\xi}_{*}}
such that

p_{n}-nk_{\kappa\dot{\xi}_{*}}=q_{n}r_{\kappa\dot{\xi}_{*}}+s_{n}.

In particular, (
3.16
) gives that

(3.17)

\displaystyle(\mu_{\kappa n_{0}}+s_{n}\delta)+n(\kappa\dot{\xi}_{*}+k_{\kappa\dot{\xi}_{*}}\delta)+q_{n}r_{\kappa\dot{\xi}_{*}}\delta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{\kappa n_{0}}+n(\kappa\dot{\xi}_{*}+k_{\kappa\dot{\xi}_{*}}\delta)+(q_{n}r_{\kappa\dot{\xi}_{*}}+s_{n})\delta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{\kappa n_{0}}+n(\kappa\dot{\xi}_{*}+k_{\kappa\dot{\xi}_{*}}\delta)+(p_{n}-nk_{\kappa\dot{\xi}_{*}})\delta\in\hbox{\rm supp}(V).

Since
T(1)_{re}\subseteq R^{ln}(V),
using this together with (
3.8
) and (
3.15
) repeatedly, we get

\displaystyle\mu_{\kappa n_{0}}+s_{n}\delta\in\hbox{\rm supp}(V)\quad(n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{>0}).

Since
s_{n}
’s are equal for infinitely many
n
, we get positive integers
n_{1},n_{2},\ldots
with
s:=s_{n_{1}}=s_{n_{2}}=\cdots,
and so contemplating (
3.17
), we have

\displaystyle(\mu_{\kappa n_{0}}+s\delta)+n_{i}(\kappa\dot{\xi}_{*}+k_{\kappa\dot{\xi}_{*}}\delta)+(q_{n_{0}}r_{\kappa\dot{\xi}_{*}})\delta\in\hbox{\rm supp}(V)\quad(i>0).

Using (
3.8
), we get that

\displaystyle(\mu_{\kappa n_{0}}+s\delta)+(n_{i}-1)(\kappa\dot{\xi}_{*}+k_{\kappa\dot{\xi}_{*}}\delta)\in\hbox{\rm supp}(V)\quad(i>0)

which is a contradiction as
\kappa\dot{\xi}_{*}+k_{\kappa\dot{\xi}_{*}}\delta\in T(1)_{re}\subseteq R^{ln}(V).
This completes the proof of Claim 4.

Proof of Claim 5.
Using Claim 4, we get that
U
is nonzero. Suppose that
v\in U
,
\dot{\beta}\in\dot{T}(2)
and
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
with
\dot{\beta}+n\delta\in R.
For
\dot{\alpha}\in\dot{T}\setminus\dot{T}(2)
with

\bm{\zeta}(\dot{\alpha})>0,

m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
\dot{\alpha}+m\delta\in R
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{\dot{\alpha}+m\delta}\mathcal{L}^{\dot{\beta}+n\delta}v\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{\dot{\beta}+n\delta}\underbrace{\mathcal{L}^{\dot{\alpha}+m\delta}v}_{=\{0\}}+[\mathcal{L}^{\dot{\alpha}+m\delta},\mathcal{L}^{\dot{\beta}+n\delta}]v\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{\dot{\alpha}+\dot{\beta}+(n+m)\delta}v.

It is enough to show that
\mathcal{L}^{\dot{\alpha}+\dot{\beta}+(n+m)\delta}v=\{0\}
.
If
\dot{\alpha}+\dot{\beta}+(n+m)\delta
is not a root, then, we are done. So, assume
\dot{\alpha}+\dot{\beta}+(n+m)\delta\in R
(and so
\in T
). As in (
3.12
), we have
\dot{\alpha}=\dot{\gamma}+\dot{\eta}
in which
0\neq\dot{\gamma}\in\hbox{\rm span}_{\mathbb{R}}\dot{T}(1)
and
\dot{\eta}\in\hbox{\rm span}_{\mathbb{R}}\dot{T}(2).
Now, we have the following cases:

•

\dot{\alpha}+\dot{\beta}=\dot{\gamma}+\dot{\eta}+\dot{\beta}\in\hbox{\rm span}_{\mathbb{R}}\dot{T}(2)_{re}
. Then,
\dot{\gamma}\in\hbox{\rm span}_{\mathbb{R}}\dot{T}(1)_{re}\cap\hbox{\rm span}_{\mathbb{R}}\dot{T}(2)_{re}
which implies that
\dot{\gamma}=0
. This is a contradiction.

•

\dot{\alpha}+\dot{\beta}=\dot{\gamma}+\dot{\eta}+\dot{\beta}\in\hbox{\rm span}_{\mathbb{R}}\dot{T}(1)_{re}
. Then
\dot{\eta}+\dot{\beta}\in\hbox{\rm span}_{\mathbb{R}}\dot{T}(1)_{re}\cap\hbox{\rm span}_{\mathbb{R}}\dot{T}(2)_{re}.
This implies that
\dot{\eta}+\dot{\beta}=0.

Therefore, we have
\dot{\alpha}+\dot{\beta}+(m+n)\delta=\dot{\gamma}+(m+n)\delta
and so
\mathcal{L}^{\dot{\alpha}+\dot{\beta}+(n+m)\delta}v=\{0\}
as
\bm{\zeta}(\dot{\gamma})=\bm{\zeta_{1}}(\dot{\gamma})>0

3.7
).

•

\dot{\alpha}+\dot{\beta}\not\in\hbox{\rm span}_{\mathbb{R}}\dot{T}(1)_{re}\cup\hbox{\rm span}_{\mathbb{R}}\dot{T}(2)_{re}
. Then,

\dot{\alpha}+\dot{\beta}\in\dot{T}_{ns}\setminus(\dot{T}(1)_{ns}\cup\dot{T}(2)_{ns}).
Therefore, there are

such that

This implies that

Since
\bm{\zeta_{1}}(\dot{\zeta})=\bm{\zeta_{1}}(\dot{\gamma})>0.
Therefore by (

), we have

and so

\mathcal{L}^{\dot{\alpha}+\dot{\beta}+(n+m)\delta}v=\mathcal{L}^{\dot{\zeta}+\dot{\xi}+(n+m)\delta}v=\{0\}
, as desired.

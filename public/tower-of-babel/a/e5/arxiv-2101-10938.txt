The Ax–Schanuel conjecture for variations of mixed Hodge structures
Ziyang Gao, Bruno Klingler

Abstract
. We prove in this paper the Ax–Schanuel conjecture for all admissible variations of mixed Hodge structures.



1.
Introduction

In this paper we prove the Ax–Schanuel conjecture for all admissible, graded-polarized, integral variation of mixed Hodge structures over a smooth complex
quasi-projective variety
S
.

Let
(\mathbb{V}_{\mathbb{Z}},W_{\bullet},\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})\rightarrow S^{\textnormal{an}}
be an admissible, graded-polarized, integral
variation of mixed Hodge structures on the complex manifold

S^{\textnormal{an}}
associated to
S
. Let
[\Phi]\colon S^{\textnormal{an}}\rightarrow\Gamma\backslash\mathcal{M}
be the associated complex analytic period map, where
\mathcal{M}

denotes the period domain classifying graded polarized mixed Hodge
structures of the relevant type and
\Gamma
is an
arithmetic subgroup in the group of automorphisms of
\mathcal{M}
. The
classifying space
\mathcal{M}

admits a natural realization as a real semi-algebraic subset, open in the usual
topology, of a complex algebraic variety
\mathcal{M}^{\vee}
. The Ax–Schanuel
conjecture is a functional transcendence statement
comparing the algebraic structure on
\mathcal{M}^{\vee}

and the algebraic structure on
S

[\Phi]
and

. Consider the commutative diagram

We prove the following result, conjectured in
[
Kli17
, Conj. 7.5]
(we refer to
2.5
for the
definition of weak Mumford–Tate subdomains of
\mathcal{M}
):

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
{Z}
be a complex analytic irreducible subset of

S^{\textnormal{an}}\times_{\Gamma\backslash\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{M}
. Then

(1.1)

\dim{Z}^{\mathrm{Zar}}-\dim{Z}\geq\dim p_{\mathcal{M}}({Z})^{\mathrm{ws}},

where
{Z}^{\mathrm{Zar}}
denotes the Zariski closure of
{Z}

in
S\times\mathcal{M}^{\vee}
, and
p_{\mathcal{M}}({Z})^{\mathrm{ws}}
is
the smallest weak Mumford–Tate subdomain of
\mathcal{M}
containing
p_{\mathcal{M}}({Z})
.

In the course of the proof, we also explain how to construct
p_{\mathcal{M}}({Z})^{\mathrm{ws}}
. Let
S^{\prime}
be the Zariski closure of
p_{S}({Z})
. Let
N
be the connected algebraic monodromy group of
(\mathbb{V}_{\mathbb{Z}},W_{\bullet},\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})|_{S^{\prime}}\rightarrow S^{\prime\textnormal{an}}
. Then
p_{\mathcal{M}}({Z})^{\mathrm{ws}}
is the
N(\mathbb{R})^{+}\mathcal{R}_{u}(N)(\mathbb{C})
-orbit of any point
\widetilde{z}\in p_{\mathcal{M}}({Z})
, where
\mathcal{R}_{u}(N)
is the unipotent radical of
N
; see
7.3
.

The idea of functional
transcendence statements related to Hodge theory first appeared in the
context of Shimura varieties, where
[\Phi]
is the identity. Motivated by Pila’s pioneer work
[
Pil11
]
on
the André–Oort
conjecture for copies of moduli curves, the Ax–Lindemann conjecture (a
special case of the Ax–Schanuel conjecture) was proved for various cases in
[
PT13
,
UY14
,
PT14
]
and ultimately for all pure Shimura varieties in

[
KUY16
]
; this was extended to mixed Shimura varieties in
[
Gao17
]
.
After the proof of the André–Oort
conjecture
[
Tsi18
]
(see
[
Gao16
]
for mixed Shimura varieties), and in order to attack the more general Zilber–Pink
conjecture,
1.1
was proved for copies of moduli curves in
[
PT16
]
and for any pure Shimura
variety in
[
MPT19
]
; this was extended
to mixed Shimura varieties of Kuga type in
[
Gao20b
]
. In

[
Kli17
, Conj. 7.5]
the second author suggested that
these functional transcendence statements should hold much more
generally for all admissible, graded
polarizable, integral variation of mixed Hodge structures over a smooth complex
quasi-projective variety
S
and formulated
1.1
; this was proved in
[
BT19
]
if the variation of Hodge structures in question is pure.

All these works have been important ingredients in the proofs
of various diophantine results: the André–Oort conjecture for
mixed Shimura varieties, results in the direction of the more general Zilber–Pink conjecture
[
DR18
]
,
use of
[
MPT19
]
to prove the submersivity of the Betti map in
[
ACZ20
]
,
use of

[
BT19
]
for Shafarevich type results in

[
LV20
,
LS20
]
, use of
[
Gao20b
]
to fully study the Betti rank in
[
Gao20a
]
which eventually was applied to prove a rather uniform bound on the number of rational points on curves
[
DGH21
]
. Hast
[
Has21
]
recently proved a transcendence property of the unipotent Albanese map assuming
1.1
.
We expect
1.1
to have more
applications in diophantine geometry, for instance in direction of the general
Hodge-theoretical atypical intersection conjecture
[
Kli17
, Conj.
1.9]
and its special case
[
Kli17
, Conj.
5.2]
.

The strategy for proving
1.1
is similar in spirit to previous works, in particular
[
BT19
]
,

[
MPT19
]
and
[
Gao20b
]
. However its implementation in the mixed non-Shimura
case contains serious new difficulties.

For readers’ convenience, we start the paper by recalling basic knowledge on variations of mixed Hodge structures and mixed Mumford–Tate domains in
Section

2

Section

3
,

4
and

. Unlike for the pure or the Shimura case, references to some of the results recalled hereby are not easy to find. We also give proofs in these sections and

.

. In fact, with these preparations, we can prove a particular case of

Another formalism we do for our strategy is the fibered structure of mixed Mumford–Tate domains. We also need to discuss the real points of mixed Mumford–Tate domains; they correspond to mixed Hodge structures split over
\mathbb{R}
. This is done in
Section

6
.

Then we move on to prove
1.1
. We start by some dévissages
in

8

in

is an irreducible

see

, we also replace the classifying space

by its refinement

of the variation

.

The first step in the proof of
1.1
consists of proving that the inequality (
1.1
)
holds true if the
\mathbb{Q}
-stabilizer of
{Z}^{\mathrm{Zar}}

(for the
action of

on the second factor of

),
denoted by

. To do so we use

[
BBKT20
]

generalizing
[
BKT20
]
saying that mixed period maps are definable in

More precisely, take a suitable semi-algebraic fundamental set

for

. As in all proofs of Ax–Schanuel type transcendence results via o-minimality, we start by constructing a definable subset

of

which contains all integer elements

such that
\gamma(S\times\mathfrak{F})\cap{Z}\not=\emptyset
. We wish to prove that
\Theta
contains semi-algebraic curves with arbitrarily many integer elements; this will yield the non-triviality of

unless (

) already holds true by induction. The Pila-Wilkie theorem then reduces the question to showing that the number of elements in

The first main new difficulty lies in the proof of this counting result. It occupies the full section

9

quite technical. While in the pure case it follows from an explicit

for

]

of mixed Mumford–Tate domains
associated to the weight filtration of the variation of Hodge

- we initiate the process at the

The second step in the proof of

2.
Mixed Hodge structures, classifying space, and Mumford–Tate
domains

2.1.
Mixed Hodge structure

In this subsection we recall some definitions and properties of
\mathbb{Q}
-mixed Hodge structures.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
V
be a finite dimensional
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space and
V_{\mathbb{C}}:=V\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{C}
its complexification.

(i)

A
\mathbb{Q}
-pure Hodge structure on
V
of weight
n
is a decreasing filtration
F^{\bullet}
(the
Hodge filtration
) on
V_{\mathbb{C}}
such that
V_{\mathbb{C}}=F^{p}V_{\mathbb{C}}\oplus\overline{F^{n+1-p}V_{\mathbb{C}}}
for all
p\in\mathbb{Z}
.

(ii)

A
\mathbb{Q}
-mixed Hodge structure on
V
consists of two filtrations, an increasing filtration
W_{\bullet}
on
V
(the
weight filtration
) and a decreasing filtration
F^{\bullet}
on
V_{\mathbb{C}}
(the
Hodge filtration
) such that for each
k\in\mathbb{Z}
the
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space
\mathrm{Gr}_{k}^{W}V=W_{k}/W_{k-1}
is a pure Hodge structure of weight
k
for the filtration on
\mathrm{Gr}_{k}^{W}V\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{C}
deduced from
F^{\bullet}
.

The numbers

h^{p,q}(V)=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}F^{p}\mathrm{Gr}^{W}_{p+q}(V_{\mathbb{C}})/F^{p+1}\mathrm{Gr}^{W}_{p+q}(V_{\mathbb{C}})

are called the
Hodge numbers
of
(V,W_{\bullet},F^{\bullet})
.

\mathbb{Q}
-mixed Hodge structures, defined in terms of two filtrations, can be equivalently described in terms of
bigradings
. This is classical in the pure case, where a weight
n

\mathbb{Q}
-pure Hodge structure on
V
is equivalently given by a direct sum decomposition
V_{\mathbb{C}}=\oplus_{p+q=n}V^{p,q}
(the
Hodge decomposition
) into
\mathbb{C}
-vector spaces, such that the complex conjugate
\overline{V^{q,p}}
coincides with
V^{p,q}
for all
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}
with
p+q=n
. The relation between the Hodge filtration and the Hodge decomposition is given by
F^{p}V_{\mathbb{C}}=\oplus_{p^{\prime}\geq p}V^{p^{\prime},n-p^{\prime}}
. In the general mixed case Deligne
[
Del71
, 1.2.8]

proved the following:

Proposition 2.2
.

A
\mathbb{Q}
-mixed Hodge structure on
V
is the datum of a bigrading

(2.1)

V_{\mathbb{C}}=\bigoplus_{p,q\in\mathbb{Z}}I^{p,q}

satisfying that each complex vector subspace
W_{k}V_{\mathbb{C}}=\bigoplus_{p+q\leq k}I^{p,q}
of
V_{\mathbb{C}}
is defined over
\mathbb{Q}
and

(2.2)

I^{p,q}\equiv\overline{I^{q,p}}\bmod\bigoplus_{r<p,s<q}I^{r,s}.

The Hodge filtration is then defined by
F^{p}V_{\mathbb{C}}=\bigoplus_{r\geq p}I^{r,q}
.

We will use a third, more group-theoretic, point of view on
\mathbb{Q}
-mixed Hodge structures. Let
\mathbb{S}=\mathrm{Res}_{\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m},\mathbb{C}}
be the Deligne torus, this is the real algebraic group such that
\mathbb{S}(\mathbb{R})=\mathbb{C}^{*}
and
\mathbb{S}(\mathbb{C})=\mathbb{C}^{*}\times\mathbb{C}^{*}
, with the action of the complex conjugation twisted by the automorphism that interchanges the two factors. The character group of
\mathbb{S}
, denoted by
X_{*}(\mathbb{S})
, identifies with
\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}
under

\begin{array}[]{lll}\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}&\xrightarrow{\sim}&X_{*}(\mathbb{S})\\
(p,q)&\mapsto&\big(z\in\mathbb{S}(\mathbb{R})=\mathbb{C}^{*}\mapsto z^{-p}\overline{z}^{-q}\in\mathbb{C}^{*}\big).\end{array}

Given a
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space
V
a bigrading
V_{\mathbb{C}}=\oplus_{p,q\in\mathbb{Z}}I^{p,q}
is thus equivalent to a homomorphism
h\colon\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}(V_{\mathbb{C}})
. In particular we deduce from the paragraph above that any mixed Hodge structure on
V
defines a homomorphism
h\colon\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}(V_{\mathbb{C}})
. In
[
Pin89
]
Pink identified the conditions such a homomorphism has to satisfy to define a mixed Hodge structure on

Proposition 2.3
.

[
Pin89
, 1.4 and 1.5]

Let
V
be a finite dimensional
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space. A morphism

defines a MHS on
V
if and only if there exists a connected
\mathbb{Q}
-algebraic subgroup
P\subset\mathrm{GL}(V)
such that
h
factors through
P_{\mathbb{C}}
and which satisfies the following conditions:

(i)

The composite
\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{C}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle h}}{{\to}}P_{\mathbb{C}}\to(P/W_{-1})_{\mathbb{C}}
is
defined over
\mathbb{R}
, where
W_{-1}
denotes the unipotent radical of
P
. Call this composite
\overline{h}
.

(ii)

The composite
\mathbb{G}_{m,\mathbb{R}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w}}{{\to}}\mathbb{S}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\overline{h}}}{{\to}}(P/W_{-1})_{\mathbb{R}}
is a cocharacter of the center of
(P/W_{-1})_{\mathbb{R}}
defined over
\mathbb{Q}
.

(iii)

The weight filtration on

\operatorname{Lie}P
defined by
\textnormal{Ad}_{P}\circ h
satisfies
W_{0}\operatorname{Lie}P=\operatorname{Lie}P
and
W_{-1}(\operatorname{Lie}P)=\operatorname{Lie}W_{-1}
.

If

let us define the

\operatorname{MT}(h)
of the
\mathbb{Q}
-mixed Hodge structure
(M,h)
as the smallest
\mathbb{Q}
-subgroup of
\operatorname{GL}(V)
whose complexification contains
h(\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{C}})
. One easily checks that the groups

satisfying the conditions of

are precisely the ones containing
\operatorname{MT}(h)
.

We finish this subsection by recalling the definition of polarizations.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
(V,W_{\bullet},F^{\bullet})
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-mixed Hodge structure. A
(graded) polarization
is a collection of non-degenerate
(-1)^{k}
-symmetric bilinear forms

Q_{k}\colon\mathrm{Gr}_{k}^{W}(V)\otimes\mathrm{Gr}_{k}^{W}(V)\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}

such that

(i)

Q_{k}(F^{p}\mathrm{Gr}^{W}_{k}V_{\mathbb{C}},F^{k-p+1}\mathrm{Gr}^{W}_{k}V_{\mathbb{C}})=0
for each
k
(first Riemann bilinear relation);

(ii)

the Hermitian form on
\mathrm{Gr}_{k}^{W}(V)_{\mathbb{C}}
given by
Q_{k}(Cu,\overline{v})
is positive-definite, where
C
is the Weil operator (
C|_{I^{p,q}}=i^{p-q}
for all
p,q
).

One easily checks that the Mumford-Tate group of a polarizable pure
\mathbb{Q}
-Hodge structure is reductive.

2.2.
Classifying space

In this subsection, we discuss the classifying space of all
\mathbb{Q}
-mixed Hodge structures with given weight filtration, graded polarization and Hodge numbers.

Let
V
be a finite dimensional
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space, endowed with the following additional data:

(i)

a finite increasing filtration
W_{\bullet}
of
V
;

(ii)

a collection of non-degenerate
(-1)^{k}
-symmetric bilinear forms

Q_{k}\colon\mathrm{Gr}_{k}^{W}(V)\otimes\mathrm{Gr}_{k}^{W}(V)\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}\;\;;

(iii)

a partition
\{h^{p,q}\}_{p,q\in\mathbb{Z}}
of
\dim V_{\mathbb{C}}
into non-negative integers.

Given these data, one forms the classifying space
\mathcal{M}
parametrizing
\mathbb{Q}
-mixed Hodge structures
(V,W_{\bullet},F^{\bullet})
with the following properties:

(1)

the
(p,q)
-constituent
V^{p,q}:=\operatorname{Gr}^{p}_{F}\operatorname{Gr}^{W}_{p+q}V_{\mathbb{C}}
has complex dimension
h^{p,q}
;

(2)

Q_{k}(F^{p}\mathrm{Gr}^{W}_{k}V_{\mathbb{C}},F^{k-p+1}\mathrm{Gr}^{W}_{k}V_{\mathbb{C}})=0
for each
k
(first Riemann bilinear relation);

(3)

(V,W_{\bullet},F^{\bullet})
is graded-polarized by
Q_{k}
.

Let us summarize the construction and basic properties of
\mathcal{M}
; see
[
Kap95
]
,
[
Pea00
, below (3.7) to Lemma 3.9]
for more details.
First one defines the complex algebraic variety
\mathcal{M}^{\vee}
parametrizing mixed Hodge structures satisfying only the conditions (1) and (2) above (see
[
Pea00
, Lem. 3.8]
). This is a homogeneous space under
P^{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbb{C})
, where
P^{\mathcal{M}}
is the
\mathbb{Q}
-algebraic group defined as follows: for any
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra
R
,

(2.3)

P^{\mathcal{M}}(R):=\{g\in\mathrm{GL}(V_{R}):g(W_{k})\subseteq W_{k}\text{ and }\mathrm{Gr}^{W}_{k}(g)\in\mathrm{Aut}_{R}(Q_{k})\;\text{for all}\,k\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

The classifying space
\mathcal{M}
is defined as the real semi-algebraic open subset of
\mathcal{M}^{\vee}
consisting of mixed Hodge structures which satisfy moreover condition (3) above (see
[
Pea00
, Lem. 3.9 and above]
). The fact that
\mathcal{M}
is open in
\mathcal{M}^{\vee}
endows
\mathcal{M}
with a natural complex analytic structure. The real semi-algebraic group

(2.4)

\{g\in P^{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbb{C}):\mathrm{Gr}^{W}_{k}(g)\in\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathbb{R}}(Q_{k})\;\text{for all }k\in\mathbb{Z}\}

identifies with
P^{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbb{R})^{+}W^{\mathcal{M}}_{-1}(\mathbb{C})
, where
W^{\mathcal{M}}_{-1}
is the unipotent radical of
P^{\mathcal{M}}
, see
[
Pea00
, Remark below Lem. 3.9]
. It acts transitively on
\mathcal{M}
.

2.3.
Adjoint Hodge structure

For each
h\in\mathcal{M}

2.3
defines a natural
\mathbb{Q}
-mixed Hodge structure on
\operatorname{Lie}P^{\mathcal{M}}
via
\mathrm{Ad}^{\mathcal{M}}\circ h\colon\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow P^{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}(\operatorname{Lie}P^{\mathcal{M}})_{\mathbb{C}}
: the
adjoint Hodge structure
associated with

. One easily checks that the corresponding weight filtration and graded polarization are independent of

. Indeed the weight filtration
W_{\bullet}
on
\operatorname{Lie}P^{\mathcal{M}}\subseteq\mathrm{End}(V)=V\otimes V^{\vee}
is the one deduced from the weight filtration
W_{\bullet}
on

. Similarly for the graded-polarization.

2.4.
(Weak) Mumford–Tate domains

2.3
suggests to attack the problem of classifying mixed Hodge structures by rather considering mixed Hodge structures with prescribed Mumford-Tate group. This leads abstractly to the notion of mixed Hodge data, see
Section

4.1
; and geometrically to the notion of (weak) Mumford-Tate domain refining the classifying space
\mathcal{M}
.

Definition 2.5
.

(i)

A subset
\mathcal{D}
of the classifying space
\mathcal{M}
is called a Mumford–Tate domain if there exists an element

such that
\mathcal{D}=P(\mathbb{R})^{+}W_{-1}(\mathbb{C})h
, where
P=\operatorname{MT}(h)
and
W_{-1}=\mathcal{R}_{u}(P)
is the unipotent radical of
P
.

(ii)

A subset
\mathcal{D}
of the classifying space
\mathcal{M}
is called a weak Mumford–Tate domain if there exist an element
h\in\mathcal{D}
and a normal subgroup

of

such that
\mathcal{D}=N(\mathbb{R})^{+}\mathcal{R}_{u}(N)(\mathbb{C})h
, where
\mathcal{R}_{u}(N)
is the unipotent radical of

.

In the definition, as
N\lhd P
, we have
\mathcal{R}_{u}(N)=W_{-1}\cap N
.
One easily checks that
\mathcal{M}
is a Mumford-Tate domain in itself, for

. A closer look at the geometry of general Mumford–Tate domains is given in
Section

A.1
. In particular we will prove the following results (well-known in the pure case):

Proposition 2.6
.

Every weak Mumford–Tate domain in
\mathcal{M}
is a complex analytic subspace of
\mathcal{M}
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}
be Mumford–Tate domains in
\mathcal{M}
. Then every irreducible component of
\mathcal{D}_{1}\cap\mathcal{D}_{2}
is again a Mumford–Tate domain in
\mathcal{M}
.

This lemma has the following immediate corollary.

Corollary 2.8
.

Let
\mathcal{Z}
be a complex analytic irreducible subset of
\mathcal{M}
. Then there exists a smallest Mumford–Tate domain, denoted by
\mathcal{Z}^{\mathrm{sp}}
and called the special closure of
\mathcal{Z}
, which contains
\mathcal{Z}
.

We close this subsection with some discussion on the
generic Mumford–Tate group
of a complex analytic irreducible subvariety of
\mathcal{M}
. In particular the discussion applies to weak Mumford–Tate domains.
The trivial local system
\mathbb{V}=\mathcal{M}\times V
underlies a natural family of mixed Hodge structures: for each

the triple
(V,(W_{\bullet})_{h},(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})_{h})
is a mixed
\mathbb{Q}
-Hodge structure. For any complex analytic irreducible subset
\mathcal{Z}
of
\mathcal{M}
, the first part of the proof of
[
And92
,
\mathsection
4, Lemma 4]
applies: for a very general element

, the Mumford–Tate group

does not depend on
h
. Such an

is said to be
Hodge–generic
in
\mathcal{Z}
and its Mumford-Tate group is called the
generic Mumford–Tate group
of
\mathcal{Z}
. We write
\mathrm{MT}(\mathcal{Z})
to denote the generic Mumford–Tate group of
\mathcal{Z}
. It satisfies the following property:
\mathrm{MT}(h^{\prime})<\mathrm{MT}(\mathcal{Z})
for any

.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
\mathcal{D}=P(\mathbb{R})^{+}W_{-1}(\mathbb{C})h
be a Mumford-Tate domain in

(thus

,

and

is the unipotent radical of

). Then

.

Proof.

By definition of
\operatorname{MT}(\mathcal{D})
the group

is a subgroup of
\operatorname{MT}(\mathcal{D})
. Thus we are reduced to proving the converse inclusion.

Each

is of the form

for some

, and hence the homomorphism

factors through

. This implies that
\mathrm{MT}(h^{\prime})<P
for all

. Looking at a Hodge generic point

∎

The following lemma, whose proof is given
Section

A.1
, is useful to determine when an orbit is a Mumford–Tate domain.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let

be a
\mathbb{Q}
-subgroup of

with

and let

be a

.

3.
Variation of mixed Hodge structures

Let
f\colon X\to S
be a morphism of algebraic varieties. If
f
satisfies a sharp notion of topological local constancy (suffice it to say here it is automatically satisfied if
f
is proper smooth, and is true over a Zariski-open subset of
S
for any morphism of varieties), then

gives rise to a family of mixed Hodge structures (pure when
f
is proper smooth) on

, as
s
varies over
S^{\textnormal{an}}
, subject to certain rules. This leads to the notion of a (graded-polarizable) variation of mixed Hodge structures, which we now recall:

Definition 3.1
.

Let
S
be a connected complex manifold. A
variation of mixed Hodge structures
(abbreviated VMHS) on
S
is a triple
(\mathbb{V}_{\mathbb{Z}},W_{\bullet},\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
consisting of:

(i)

a local system
\mathbb{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}
of free
\mathbb{Z}
-modules of finite rank on
S
;

(ii)

a finite increasing filtration
W_{\bullet}
of the local system
\mathbb{V}:=\mathbb{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{S}}\mathbb{Q}_{S}
by local subsystems (weight filtration);

(iii)

a finite decreasing filtration
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
of the holomorphic vector bundle
\mathcal{V}:=\mathbb{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{O}_{S}
by holomorphic subbundles (Hodge filtration),

satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

for each
s\in S
, the triple
(\mathbb{V}_{s},W_{\bullet}(s),\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}(s))
is a mixed Hodge structure;

(2)

the connection
\nabla\colon\mathcal{V}\rightarrow\mathcal{V}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{S}}\Omega_{S}^{1}
whose sheaf of horizontal sections is
\mathbb{V}_{\mathbb{C}}:=\mathbb{V}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{C}
satisfies the Griffiths’ transversality condition

\nabla(\mathcal{F}^{p})\subseteq\mathcal{F}^{p-1}\otimes\Omega_{S}^{1}.

Definition 3.2
.

A VMHS
(\mathbb{V}_{\mathbb{Z}},W_{\bullet},\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
on
S
is called
graded-polarizable
if the induced variations of pure
\mathbb{Q}
-Hodge structures (VHS)
\mathrm{Gr}^{W}_{k}\mathbb{V}
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, are all polarizable,
i.e.
for each
k\in\mathbb{Z}
there exists a morphism of local systems

\mathcal{Q}_{k}\colon\mathrm{Gr}^{W}_{k}\mathbb{V}\otimes\mathrm{Gr}^{W}_{k}\mathbb{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}_{S}

inducing on each fiber a polarization of the corresponding
\mathbb{Q}
-Hodge structure of weight
k
.

From now on all VMHS are assumed to be graded-polarizable.

3.1.
Mumford-Tate group and monodromy group

Let
S
be a connected complex manifold and
(\mathbb{V}_{\mathbb{Z}},W_{\bullet},\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
a VMHS on
S
. The pull-back

For

-algebraic subgroup of

By definition the algebraic monodromy group

.
By
[

,
\mathsection
4, Lemma 4]
, following
[

,
\mathsection

in the pure case, the Mumford-Tate group

is locally constant on

denotes a meager subset of

; and

is a subgroup of

for all

(P^{\mathcal{M}},\mathcal{X}^{\mathcal{M}})
and classifying map

\varphi^{\mathcal{M}}\colon\mathcal{X}^{\mathcal{M}}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}
as in
Lemma

4.3
.

(i)

For each Mumford–Tate domain
\mathcal{D}
in
\mathcal{M}
, there exists
a sub-mixed Hodge datum
(\mathrm{MT}(\mathcal{D}),\mathcal{X})
of

(P^{\mathcal{M}},\mathcal{X}^{\mathcal{M}})
such that
\varphi^{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{X})=\mathcal{D}
. Moreover

\varphi:=\varphi^{\mathcal{M}}|_{\mathcal{X}}\colon\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
is
precisely the classifying map (
4.1
) for
(\mathrm{MT}(\mathcal{D}),\mathcal{X})
.

(ii)

Conversely for any sub-mixed Hodge datum
(P,\mathcal{X})
of
(P^{\mathcal{M}},\mathcal{X}^{\mathcal{M}})
, the image
\varphi^{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{X})
is a Mumford–Tate domain in
\mathcal{M}
(whose generic Mumford–Tate group is a normal subgroup of
P
).

Proof.

For (i): for simplicity we write
P
for

\mathrm{MT}(\mathcal{D})
and
W_{-1}
for
\mathcal{R}_{u}(P)
. Take a point
x\in\mathcal{D}
; it gives rise to a homomorphism
h_{x}\colon\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow P_{\mathbb{C}}
. View
h_{x}\in\mathcal{X}^{\mathcal{M}}
, then

\varphi^{\mathcal{M}}(h_{x})\in\mathcal{D}
by definition of
\varphi^{\mathcal{M}}
. Let

\mathcal{X}=P(\mathbb{R})^{+}W_{-1}(\mathbb{C})h_{x}\subset\mathcal{M}
. As
\varphi^{\mathcal{M}}
is

P^{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbb{R})^{+}W_{-1}^{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbb{C})
-equivariant, we have

\varphi^{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{X})=P(\mathbb{R})^{+}W_{-1}(\mathbb{C})\varphi^{\mathcal{M}}(h_{x})=P(\mathbb{R})^{+}W_{-1}(\mathbb{C})x=\mathcal{D}
. By
2.3
the pair
(P,\mathcal{X})
is
a mixed Hodge datum and by construction
\varphi=\varphi^{\mathcal{M}}|_{\mathcal{X}}
is precisely the map in (
4.1
).

For (ii): Denote by
\mathcal{D}=\varphi^{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{X})
. Then
\mathcal{D}
is a
P(\mathbb{R})^{+}W_{-1}(\mathbb{C})
-orbit because the map
\varphi^{\mathcal{M}}
is
P^{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbb{R})^{+}W_{-1}^{\mathcal{M}}(\mathbb{C})
-equivariant. Moreover for any
x\in\mathcal{D}
, the corresponding homomorphism
h_{x}\colon\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}(V_{\mathbb{C}})
factors through
P_{\mathbb{C}}
by definition of mixed Hodge data. Thus
\mathcal{D}
is a Mumford–Tate domain and
\mathrm{MT}(\mathcal{D})\lhd P
by
Lemma

2.10
.
∎

5.
Quotients

5.1.
Quotient of mixed Hodge datum

Given a connected mixed Hodge datum
(P,\mathcal{X})
and a normal subgroup
N\lhd P
, the
quotient mixed Hodge datum

(5.1)

q_{N}\colon(P,\mathcal{X})\rightarrow(P,\mathcal{X})/N

is defined as follows. Given
h\in\mathcal{X}\subseteq\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{C}},P_{\mathbb{C}})
we denote by
\overline{h}\in\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{C}},(P/N)_{\mathbb{C}})
the homomorphism
\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{C}}\xrightarrow{h}P_{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow(P/N)_{\mathbb{C}}
. Note that
\mathcal{R}_{u}(P/N)=W_{-1}/(W_{-1}\cap N)
.
Denote by
\mathcal{X}/N=(P/N)(\mathbb{R})^{+}(W_{-1}/W_{-1}\cap N)(\mathbb{C})\overline{h}\subseteq\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{C}},(P/N)_{\mathbb{C}})
. One easily checks that
(P,\mathcal{X})/N\colon=(P/N,\mathcal{X}/N)
is a connected mixed Hodge datum, independent of the choice of
h\in\mathcal{X}
. The morphism
q_{N}\colon(P,\mathcal{X})\rightarrow(P/N,\mathcal{X}/N)
is what we desire. Moreover
q_{N}\colon\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}/N
is clearly real algebraic.

5.2.
Quotient of Mumford–Tate domains

Next we prove that Mumford–Tate domains are stable under taking quotients. This operation is important to understand the structure of Mumford–Tate domains.

Let
V_{\mathbb{Z}}
be a free finite rank
\mathbb{Z}
-module and
V:=V_{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
be the associated
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space. Let
\mathcal{M}
be the classifying space of certain polarized mixed Hodge structures and let
P^{\mathcal{M}}
be the
\mathbb{Q}
-group, both from
Section

2.2
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a Mumford–Tate domain in
\mathcal{M}
with
P=\mathrm{MT}(\mathcal{D})
, and let
(P,\mathcal{X})
and
\varphi\colon\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
be as in (
4.4
)(i). Let
N
be a normal subgroup of
P
. Then there exists a quotient
p_{N}\colon\mathcal{D}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}/N
, in the category of complex varieties, such that

(i)

\mathcal{D}/N
is a Mumford–Tate domain in some classifying space of mixed Hodge structures, and
\mathrm{MT}(\mathcal{D}/N)=P/N
.

(ii)

Each fiber of
p_{N}
is an
N(\mathbb{R})^{+}(W_{-1}\cap N)(\mathbb{C})
-orbit, where
W_{-1}=\mathcal{R}_{u}(P)
.

(iii)

For the quotient mixed Hodge datum
q_{N}\colon(P,\mathcal{X})\rightarrow(P/N,\mathcal{X}/N)
defined in (
5.1
), the classifying map (
4.1
) for
(P/N,\mathcal{X}/N)
has image
\mathcal{D}/N
, thus defining
\varphi_{/N}\colon\mathcal{X}/N\rightarrow\mathcal{D}/N
.

(iv)

The following commutative diagram commutes

(5.2)

\textstyle{\mathcal{X}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varphi}
\scriptstyle{q_{N}}
\textstyle{\mathcal{X}/N\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varphi_{/N}}
\textstyle{\mathcal{D}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{p_{N}}
\textstyle{\mathcal{D}/N.}

Proof.

Consider the quotient mixed Hodge datum
q_{N}\colon(P,\mathcal{X})\rightarrow(P/N,\mathcal{X}/N)
defined in (
5.1
). Any
\overline{h}\in\mathcal{X}/N\subseteq\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{C}},(P/N)_{\mathbb{C}})
induces a
\mathbb{Q}
-mixed Hodge structure on
\operatorname{Lie}(P/N)
, via
\mathrm{Ad}_{P/N}\circ\overline{h}\colon\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow(P/N)_{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}(\operatorname{Lie}(P/N))_{\mathbb{C}}
, which satisfies the three properties listed in
4.1
with
P
replaced by
P/N
and
h
replaced by
\overline{h}
.

Fix a faithful representation
\overline{\rho}\colon P/N\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}(V^{\prime})
defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. Then the morphism
\overline{\rho}\circ\overline{h}
induces a
\mathbb{Q}
-mixed Hodge structure on
V^{\prime}
by
2.3
for each
\overline{h}\in\mathcal{X}/N
, and the weight filtration and the Hodge numbers do not depend on the choice of
\overline{h}\in\mathcal{X}/N
. Thus we obtain a map

\varphi_{/N}\colon\mathcal{X}/N\rightarrow\{\text{mixed Hodge structures on }V^{\prime}\}.

Set
\mathcal{D}/N=\varphi_{/N}(\mathcal{X}/N)
. Then we get
\varphi_{/N}\colon\mathcal{X}/N\rightarrow\mathcal{D}/N
, which by
[
Pin89
, 1.7]
is
(P/N)(\mathbb{R})^{+}(W_{-1}/(W_{-1}\cap N))(\mathbb{C})
-equivariant (here
W_{-1}=\mathcal{R}_{u}(P)
and hence
\mathcal{R}_{u}(P/N)=W_{-1}/(W_{-1}\cap N)
). This establishes (iii) for the space
\mathcal{D}/N
.

By
[
Pin89
, 1.12]
the
\mathbb{Q}
-mixed Hodge structures on
V^{\prime}
thus obtained are graded-polarized by some collection of non-degenerate bilinear forms (same for all
\overline{h}
). So
\mathcal{D}/N
is a contained in some classifying space
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
. This establishes (i).

Now let us construct the map
p_{N}\colon\mathcal{D}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}/N
and prove properties (ii) and (iv). Take
x\in\mathcal{D}
, and take any
h_{x}\in\varphi^{-1}(x)
. Then
\varphi^{-1}(x)=\exp(F^{0}_{x}(\operatorname{Lie}W_{-1})_{\mathbb{C}})h_{x}
by
Lemma

4.2
.
Note that
\exp(F^{0}_{x}(\operatorname{Lie}W_{-1})_{\mathbb{C}})
is a
subgroup of
P_{\mathbb{C}}
. Then
q_{N}(\varphi^{-1}(x))=q_{N}(\exp(F^{0}_{x}(\operatorname{Lie}W_{-1})_{\mathbb{C}})h_{x})=\frac{\exp(F^{0}_{x}(\operatorname{Lie}W_{-1})_{\mathbb{C}})}{N(\mathbb{C})\cap\exp(F^{0}_{x}(\operatorname{Lie}W_{-1})_{\mathbb{C}})}q_{N}(h_{x})
.

On the other hand define
\overline{x}:=\varphi_{/N}(q_{N}(h_{x}))
. Then
\varphi_{/N}^{-1}(\overline{x})=\exp(F^{0}_{\overline{x}}(\operatorname{Lie}W_{-1}/(W_{-1}\cap N))_{\mathbb{C}})q_{N}(h_{x})
again by
Lemma

4.2
.

We claim that
\frac{\exp(F^{0}_{x}(\operatorname{Lie}W_{-1})_{\mathbb{C}})}{N(\mathbb{C})\cap\exp(F^{0}_{x}(\operatorname{Lie}W_{-1})_{\mathbb{C}})}=\exp(F^{0}_{\overline{x}}(\operatorname{Lie}W_{-1}/(W_{-1}\cap N))_{\mathbb{C}})
. Indeed it suffices to check for Lie algebras,
i.e.
it suffices to prove
F^{0}_{x}(\operatorname{Lie}W_{-1})_{\mathbb{C}}/(\operatorname{Lie}N_{\mathbb{C}}\cap F^{0}_{x}(\operatorname{Lie}W_{-1})_{\mathbb{C}})\simeq F^{0}_{\overline{x}}(\operatorname{Lie}W_{-1}/(W_{-1}\cap N))_{\mathbb{C}}
canonically. As
N\lhd P
, we have
\mathrm{Ad}_{P}(\operatorname{Lie}N)\subseteq\operatorname{Lie}N
. So
\operatorname{Lie}N
is a sub-mixed Hodge structure of the adjoint Hodge structure on
\operatorname{Lie}P
. Thus
\operatorname{Lie}N_{\mathbb{C}}\cap F^{0}_{x}(\operatorname{Lie}W_{-1})_{\mathbb{C}}=F^{0}_{x}(\operatorname{Lie}W_{-1}\cap N)_{\mathbb{C}}
. Thus we proved the desired claim.

By the last three paragraphs, we have
q_{N}(\varphi^{-1}(x))=\varphi_{/N}^{-1}(\overline{x})
. So the map
\mathcal{D}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}/N
,
x\mapsto\overline{x}:=\varphi_{/N}(q_{N}(h_{x}))
is well-defined. Call this map
p_{N}
. Then property (iv) holds true by construction of
p_{N}
. Property (ii) then is not hard to check.

Now the map is complex analytic by property (ii).
∎

.

Proof.

We have the moduli interpretation of Mumford–Tate domains above. On the other hand, every complex analytic variety has at most countably many irreducible components, and by definition there are countably many Hodge tensors. Hence there are at most countably many Mumford–Tate domains contained in
\mathcal{M}
.
∎

This allows to prove a stronger version of
2.8
.

Lemma A.8
.

Let
\mathcal{Z}
be a complex analytic irreducible subvariety of
\mathcal{M}
. Let
P=\mathrm{MT}(\mathcal{Z})
be the generic Mumford–Tate group of
\mathcal{Z}
. Then
\mathcal{Z}^{\mathrm{sp}}
, the smallest Mumford–Tate domain which contains
\mathcal{Z}
, is precisely
P(\mathbb{R})^{+}W_{-1}(\mathbb{C})h
for some
h\in\mathcal{Z}
, where
W_{-1}=\mathcal{R}_{u}(P)
.

Proof.

Denote by
\mathcal{Z}^{o}
the set of Hodge generic points in
\mathcal{Z}
. Then
\mathcal{Z}^{o}
is the complement of the union of countably many proper complex analytic irreducible subvarieties of
\mathcal{Z}
. In particular,
\mathcal{Z}^{o}
is irreducible since
\mathcal{Z}
is.

It is clearly true that
\mathcal{Z}^{o}\subseteq\bigcup_{h\in\mathcal{Z}^{o}}P(\mathbb{R})^{+}W_{-1}(\mathbb{C})h
. Each member in the union is by definition a Mumford–Tate domain, and hence the union is at most a countable union by
A.7
. Moreover two
P(\mathbb{R})^{+}W_{-1}(\mathbb{C})
-orbits either coincide or are disjoint. So
\mathcal{Z}^{o}
is contained in a countable disjoint union of some
P(\mathbb{R})^{+}W_{-1}(\mathbb{C})
-orbits. But
\mathcal{Z}^{o}
is irreducible, so it is contained some member in the union. Thus
\mathcal{Z}^{o}\subseteq P(\mathbb{R})^{+}W_{-1}(\mathbb{C})h
for some
h\in\mathcal{Z}^{o}
. But then
\mathcal{Z}\subseteq P(\mathbb{R})^{+}W_{-1}(\mathbb{C})h
. Hence we are done.
∎

Now we are ready to prove
Lemma

2.10
.

Proof of
Lemma

2.10
.

By assumption
\mathcal{D}=P(\mathbb{R})^{+}W_{-1}(\mathbb{C})h
. From now on we fix
h^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}
Hodge generic, namely
\mathrm{MT}(h^{\prime})=\mathrm{MT}(\mathcal{D})
.

By
Lemma

A.8
we have

(A.7)

P(\mathbb{R})^{+}W_{-1}(\mathbb{C})h^{\prime}=\mathcal{D}\subseteq\mathrm{MT}(\mathcal{D})(\mathbb{R})^{+}\mathcal{R}_{u}(\mathrm{MT}(\mathcal{D}))(\mathbb{C})h^{\prime}.

Let us prove
\mathrm{MT}(\mathcal{D})<P
. Indeed, each point
h^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}
is of the form
g\cdot h
for some
g\in P(\mathbb{R})^{+}W_{-1}(\mathbb{C})
. The homomorphism
h^{\prime}=g\cdot h\colon\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}(V_{\mathbb{C}})
factors through
gh(\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{C}})g^{-1}\subseteq gP_{\mathbb{C}}g^{-1}=P_{\mathbb{C}}
. Hence
\mathrm{MT}(h^{\prime})<P
for all
h^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}
. So
\mathrm{MT}(\mathcal{D})<P
.

Next we show that
\mathrm{MT}(\mathcal{D})
is normal in
P
. Indeed for any
g\in P(\mathbb{Q})
, we have

\mathrm{MT}(\mathcal{D})\supseteq\mathrm{MT}(g\cdot h^{\prime})=g\mathrm{MT}(h^{\prime})g^{-1}=g\mathrm{MT}(\mathcal{D})g^{-1}.

By comparing dimensions, we have
\mathrm{MT}(\mathcal{D})=g\mathrm{MT}(\mathcal{D})g^{-1}
. By letting
g
run over elements in
P(\mathbb{Q})
, we get
\mathrm{MT}(\mathcal{D})\lhd P
. In particular
\mathcal{R}_{u}(\mathrm{MT}(\mathcal{D}))=W_{-1}\cap\mathrm{MT}(\mathcal{D})
.

This implies

(A.8)

\mathrm{MT}(\mathcal{D})(\mathbb{R})^{+}\mathcal{R}_{u}(\mathrm{MT}(\mathcal{D}))(\mathbb{C})h^{\prime}\subseteq P(\mathbb{R})^{+}W_{-1}(\mathbb{C})h^{\prime}.

Thus
\mathcal{D}=\mathrm{MT}(h^{\prime})(\mathbb{R})^{+}\mathcal{R}_{u}(\mathrm{MT}(h^{\prime}))(\mathbb{C})h^{\prime}
by (
A.7
) and (
A.8
). So
\mathcal{D}
is a Mumford–Tate domain.
∎

Appendix B
Underlying group

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a Mumford–Tate domain in some classifying space
\mathcal{M}

with
P=\mathrm{MT}(\mathcal{D})
. Each
h\in\mathcal{D}

defines an adjoint Hodge structure on
\operatorname{Lie}P
. Write
W_{\bullet}
for
the weight filtration. By property (iii) of
2.3

W_{\bullet}
does not depend on the choice of
h\in\mathcal{D}
and satisfies

W_{0}(\operatorname{Lie}P)=\operatorname{Lie}P
and
W_{-1}=\mathcal{R}_{u}(P)
.

The weight filtration
0=W_{-m-1}(\operatorname{Lie}P)\subseteq W_{-m}(\operatorname{Lie}P)\subseteq\cdots\subseteq W_{-1}(\operatorname{Lie}P)
defines a sequence of
connected subgroups

(B.1)

0=W_{-(m+1)}\subseteq W_{-m}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq W_{-1}

of
P
. Each
W_{-k}
,
k\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
, is a normal
unipotent subgroup of
P
.

Write as before
G=P/W_{-1}
the reductive part of
P
. We wish to
reconstruct
P
from
G
and the
W_{-k}
’s.

Let us start with the
unipotent radical
W_{-1}
.

Lemma B.1
.

(a)

For each
k\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
,
W_{-k}/W_{-(k+1)}
is a vector
group.

(b)

There is an isomorphism of
\mathbb{Q}
-algebraic varieties

(B.2)

\begin{array}[]{lll}W_{-1}&\to&(W_{-1}/W_{-2})\times\cdots\times(W_{-(m-1)}/W_{-m})\times W_{-m}\\
\mathbf{w}&\mapsto&(w_{1},\cdots,w_{m-1},w_{m})\;\;.\end{array}

Proof.

We first prove (a).
For each
k\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
, the algebraic group
W_{-k}/W_{-(k+1)}
is
unipotent since
W_{-k}
is unipotent. On the other hand
[\operatorname{Lie}W_{-k},\operatorname{Lie}W_{-k}]\subseteq W_{-2k}
by reason of weight, and

W_{-2k}\subseteq W_{-(k+1)}
as
k\geq 1
. Thus
\operatorname{Lie}W_{-k}/W_{-(k+1)}
is a commutative Lie algebra, hence

W_{-k}/W_{-(k+1)}
is an abelian algebraic group. Finally the
algebraic group
W_{-k}/W_{-(k+1)}

is a vector group as it is abelian and unipotent.

We now turn to the description of the isomorphism (
B.2
).
As
W_{-1}
is unipotent, the exponential map
\exp\colon\operatorname{Lie}W_{-1}\rightarrow W_{-1}
is an
isomorphism of
\mathbb{Q}
-algebraic varieties.

Fix an isomorphism of
\mathbb{Q}
-vector spaces
\operatorname{Lie}W_{-1}\simeq\bigoplus_{j=1}^{m}\operatorname{Lie}W_{-j}/W_{-(j+1)}
. As part (a) provides a canonical
identification of
\mathbb{Q}
-algebraic varieties
\operatorname{Lie}W_{-k}/W_{-(k+1)}=W_{-k}/W_{-(k+1)}
between a vector group and its
Lie algebra, we get the desired the isomorphism (
B.2
) by

W_{-1}\xleftarrow[\sim]{\exp}\operatorname{Lie}W_{-1}=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{m}\operatorname{Lie}(W_{-j}/W_{-(j+1)})=\prod_{j=1}^{m}W_{-j}/W_{-(j+1)}.\qed

Notice that this isomorphism (
B.2
) is not
canonical. In this paper, we fix such an isomorphism once and for all.

Next we give the formula for the group law on
W_{-1}
under
this identification given by (
B.2
).

Definition B.2
.

For
k\in\{1,\cdots,m\}
we define the
k
-truncation
\mathbf{w}_{k}\in W_{-1}/W_{-k-1}\simeq\prod_{j=1}^{k}W_{-j}/W_{-(j+1)}
of an element

\mathbf{w}\in W_{-1}
as follows. If
\mathbf{w}=(w_{1},\cdots,w_{m-1},w_{m})
under the
identification (
B.2
), then
\mathbf{w}_{k}=(w_{1},\cdots,w_{k})
.

Lemma B.3
.

For each
k\geq 2
, there exists a polynomial map

\mathrm{calb}_{k}\colon W_{-1}/W_{-k-1}\times W_{-1}/W_{-k-1}\to W_{-k}/W_{-k-1}

of degree at most
k-1
and constant term
0

such that for any
\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\in W_{-1}
, their product
is given under the identification (
B.2
) by

(B.3)

\mathbf{w}\cdot\mathbf{w}^{\prime}=(w_{1}+w^{\prime}_{1},w_{2}+w^{\prime}_{2}+\mathrm{calb}_{2}(\mathbf{w}_{1},\mathbf{w}_{1}^{\prime}),\ldots,w_{m}+w^{\prime}_{m}+\mathrm{calb}_{m}(\mathbf{w}_{m-1},\mathbf{w}^{\prime}_{m-1})).

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{w}=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{m})
and
\mathbf{w}^{\prime}=(w^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,w^{\prime}_{m})
under (
B.2
). The Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff formula gives:

(B.4)

\mathbf{w}\cdot\mathbf{w}^{\prime}=\exp\left((w_{1},\ldots,w_{m})+(w^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,w^{\prime}_{m})+\frac{1}{2}[(w_{1},\ldots,w_{m}),(w^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,w^{\prime}_{m})]+\ldots\right),

where all operations in the exponential are taken in
\operatorname{Lie}W_{-1}
,
and the sum is finite as
\operatorname{Lie}W_{-1}
is nilpotent.
Noticing that

[W_{-k}/W_{-(k+1)},W_{-k^{\prime}}/W_{-(k^{\prime}+1)}]\subseteq W_{-(k+k^{\prime})}/W_{-(k+k^{\prime}+1)},

one can rewrite (
B.4
) as

\mathbf{w}\cdot\mathbf{w}^{\prime}=\exp\left((w_{1}+w^{\prime}_{1},w_{2}+w^{\prime}_{2}+\mathrm{calb}_{2}(\mathbf{w}_{1},\mathbf{w}_{1}^{\prime}),\ldots,w_{m}+w^{\prime}_{m}+\mathrm{calb}_{m}(\mathbf{w}_{m-1},\mathbf{w}^{\prime}_{m-1}))\right),

with polynomials
\mathrm{calb}_{k}
for each
k\geq 2
as required by the lemma.
∎

The next lemma explains how
G=P/W_{-1}
acts on
W_{-1}=\mathcal{R}_{u}(P)

under the identification (
B.2
).

Lemma B.4
.

For each
k\geq 1
,
W_{-k}/W_{-(k+1)}
is a
G
-module. Moreover this
G
-module structure is induced by the action of
G
on
W_{-1}
.

As a consequence, for each
g_{0}\in G
and
\mathbf{w}=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{m})\in W_{-1}
under (
B.2
), we have

(B.5)

g_{0}\cdot\mathbf{w}=(g_{0}w_{1},\ldots,g_{0}w_{m}).

Proof.

As
\mathrm{Gr}_{0}^{W_{\bullet}}(\operatorname{Lie}P)=\operatorname{Lie}G
and
W_{-k}(\operatorname{Lie}P)=\operatorname{Lie}W_{-k}
for each
k\geq 1
, we have
[\operatorname{Lie}G,\operatorname{Lie}W_{-k}]\subseteq\operatorname{Lie}W_{-k}
. Hence the action of
G
on
W_{-1}
preserves
W_{-k}
for each
k\geq 1
, and hence furthermore induces an action on
W_{-k}/W_{-(k+1)}
which is a
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space. This concludes the lemma.
∎

We are now ready to state the result to reconstruct
P
from
G
and the
W_{-k}
’s. First let us fix a Levi decomposition
P=W_{-1}\rtimes G
.

Proposition B.5
.

The fixed Levi decomposition
P=W_{-1}\rtimes G
and the fixed isomorphism (
B.2
) together induce an isomorphism as algebraic varieties defined over
\mathbb{Q}

(B.6)

P\simeq G\times(W_{-1}/W_{-2})\times\cdots(W_{-(m-1)}/W_{-m})\times W_{-m}.

The group law on the right hand side of (
B.6
) is given as follows. Let
(g_{0},w_{1},\ldots,w_{m})
and
(g_{0}^{\prime},w_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,w_{m}^{\prime})
be two elements in
P
under the identification (
B.6
). Denote by
\mathbf{w}=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{m})
and
\mathbf{w}^{\prime}=(w_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,w_{m}^{\prime})
. Then

(B.7)

(g_{0},\mathbf{w})\cdot(g_{0}^{\prime},\mathbf{w}^{\prime})=(g_{0}g_{0}^{\prime},w_{1}+g_{0}w_{1}^{\prime},w_{2}+g_{0}w_{2}^{\prime}+\mathrm{calb}_{2}(w_{1},g_{0}w_{1}^{\prime}),\ldots,w_{m}+g_{0}w_{m}^{\prime}+\mathrm{calb}_{m}(\mathbf{w}_{m-1},g_{0}\mathbf{w}^{\prime}_{m-1}))

where
\mathrm{calb}_{2},\ldots,\mathrm{calb}_{m}
are the
\mathbb{Q}
-polynomials from
Lemma

B.3
,
\mathbf{w}_{k}
(resp.
\mathbf{w}^{\prime}_{k}
) is the
k
-th truncation as in
Lemma

B.3
, and
g_{0}\mathbf{w}_{k}^{\prime}=(g_{0}w_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,g_{0}w_{k}^{\prime})
for each
k\geq 1
.

Proof.

(
B.6
) follows directly from the fixed Levi decomposition and (
B.2
).

To prove (
B.7
), first note that
(g_{0},\mathbf{w})=(1,\mathbf{w})\cdot(g_{0},0)
for
P=W_{-1}\rtimes G
. Similarly
(g_{0}^{\prime},\mathbf{w}^{\prime})=(1,\mathbf{w}^{\prime})\cdot(g_{0}^{\prime},0)
. So

\displaystyle(g_{0},\mathbf{w})\cdot(g_{0}^{\prime},\mathbf{w}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=(1,\mathbf{w})\cdot\left((g_{0},0)\cdot(1,\mathbf{w}^{\prime})\right)\cdot(g_{0}^{\prime},0)

\displaystyle=(1,\mathbf{w})\cdot(g_{0},g_{0}\cdot\mathbf{w}^{\prime})\cdot(g_{0}^{\prime},0)

\displaystyle=(1,\mathbf{w})\cdot\left((1,g_{0}\cdot\mathbf{w}^{\prime})\cdot(g_{0},0)\right)\cdot(g_{0}^{\prime},0)

\displaystyle=(1,w_{1},\ldots,w_{m})\cdot(1,g_{0}w_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,g_{0}w_{m}^{\prime})\cdot(g_{0},0)\cdot(g_{0}^{\prime},0)\quad\text{by \eqref{EqActionOfGonW}}

\displaystyle=(1,w_{1}+g_{0}w_{1}^{\prime},w_{2}+g_{0}w_{2}^{\prime}+\mathrm{calb}_{2}(w_{1},g_{0}w_{1}^{\prime}),\ldots,

\displaystyle\qquad\quad w_{m}+g_{0}w_{m}^{\prime}+\mathrm{calb}_{m}(\mathbf{w}_{m_{1}},g_{0}\mathbf{w}^{\prime}_{m-1}))\cdot(g_{0}g_{0}^{\prime},0)\quad\text{by \eqref{EqGroupLawUnipotent}}

\displaystyle=(g_{0}g_{0}^{\prime},w_{1}+g_{0}w_{1}^{\prime},w_{2}+g_{0}w_{2}^{\prime}+\mathrm{calb}_{2}(w_{1},g_{0}w_{1}^{\prime}),\ldots,

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad w_{m}+g_{0}w_{m}^{\prime}+\mathrm{calb}_{m}(\mathbf{w}_{m_{1}},g_{0}\mathbf{w}^{\prime}_{m-1})).\qed

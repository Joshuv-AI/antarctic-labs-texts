Abstract




Abstract

We continue our study on the global solution to the
two-dimensional Prandtl’s system for unsteady boundary layers in
the class considered by Oleinik provided that the pressure is
favorable. First, by using a different method from [13], we gave a
direct proof of existence of a global weak solution by a direct
BV estimate. Then we prove the uniqueness and continuous dependence on data of such a weak solution to the
initial-boundary value problem. Finally, we
show the smoothness of the weak solutions and then the global existence of smooth solutions.

§1
Introduction

In this paper, we continue our study on the initial-boundary value
problem for the two-dimensional unsteady Prandtl’s system:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}u+u\partial_{x}u+\upsilon\partial_{y}u+\partial_{x}P=\nu\partial^{2}_{x}u,\qquad 0<x<L,\qquad y>0\\
\partial_{x}u+\partial_{y}\upsilon=0\\
u|_{t=0}=u_{0}(x,y),\qquad u|_{y=0}=0\\
\upsilon|_{y=0}=\upsilon_{0}(x,t),\qquad u|_{x=0}=u_{1}(y,t)\\
u(x,y,t)\rightarrow U(x,t),\qquad y\rightarrow+\infty\end{array}\right.

with
\nu
being a fixed positive constant, and the pressure
p

determined by the Bernoulli’s law:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}U+U\partial_{x}U+\partial_{x}P=0.

(1.7)

The physical situation described by problem (1.1) with (1.2)
corresponds to a plane unsteady flow of viscous incompressible
fluid in the presence of an arbitrary injection and removal of
fluid across the boundaries. Thus, one may assume that

\displaystyle U(x,t)>0,\quad u_{0}(x,t)>0,\quad u_{1}(y,t)>0,\quad\text{and}\quad v_{0}(x,t)\leq 0

(1.8)

As in Oleinik [8, 9], we assume that the data are in the monotone
class in the sense that

\displaystyle\partial_{y}u_{0}(x,y)>0,\quad\partial_{y}u_{1}(t,y)>0

(1.9)

Under the further assumption that the pressure is favorable, i.e.,

\displaystyle\partial_{x}P(x,t)\leq 0\quad{\rm for}\quad t>0,\quad 0<x\leq L,

(1.10)

we have shown the existence of a global weak solution to the
Cauchy problem (1.1) by a splitting method in [13]. The main
purpose in this paper is to show that such a weak solution is in
fact unique and depend continuously on the data. Furthermore,
such a solution in fact is a classical solution in the sense that it is smooth in the interior.

To formulate the problem, as in [8], we introduce the following
Crocco transformation:

\displaystyle\tau=t,\quad\xi=x,\quad\eta=\frac{u(x,y,t)}{U(x,t)},\quad w(\tau,\xi,\eta)=\frac{\partial_{y}u(x,y,t)}{U(x,t)}\,.

(1.11)

Then the initial-boundary value problem (1.1) is transformed into
the following initial-boundary value problem:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{\tau}w^{-1}+\eta U\partial_{\xi}w^{-1}+A\partial_{\eta}w^{-1}-Bw^{-1}=-\nu\partial^{2}_{\eta}w\\
\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad{\rm on}\quad Q=\{(\xi,\eta,\tau),\quad 0<\tau<\infty,\quad 0<\xi<L,\quad 0<\eta<1\}\\
\displaystyle w|_{\tau=0}=\frac{\partial_{y}u_{0}}{U}\equiv w_{0},\quad w|_{\eta=1}=0\\
\displaystyle w|_{\xi=0}=w_{1}\equiv\frac{\partial_{y}u_{1}(y,\tau)}{U(0,\tau)},\quad(\nu w\partial_{\eta}w-v_{0}w)|_{\eta=0}=\frac{\partial_{x}P}{U},\end{array}\right.

where

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle A=(1-\eta^{2})\partial_{x}U+(1-\eta)\frac{\partial_{t}U}{U},&{\rm and}\\
\displaystyle B=\eta\partial_{x}U+\frac{\partial_{t}U}{U}.\end{array}\right.

Set

\displaystyle Q_{T}=\{(\xi,\eta,\tau)|\quad 0<\tau<T,\quad 0<\xi<L,\quad 0<\eta<1\}

A weak solution to the initial-boundary value
problem (1.7) can be defined as follows:

Definition 1.1

A function
w\in BV(Q_{T})\cap L^{\infty}(Q_{T})
is said to
be a weak solution to the problem (1.7) if the following
conditions are satisfied:

(i)

There exists a positive constant
C=C(T)
such that

\displaystyle C^{-1}(1-\eta)\leq w(\tau,\xi,\eta)\leq C(1-\eta),\quad\forall(\xi,\eta,\tau)\in Q_{T},\quad{\rm and}

(1.20)

\displaystyle(1-\eta)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\partial_{\eta}w\in L^{2}(Q_{T}),

(1.21)

(ii)

w_{\eta\eta}
is a locally bounded measure in

Q_{T}
, and

\displaystyle\int\!\!\!\int_{Q_{T}}(1-\eta)^{2}d|w_{\eta\eta}|<\infty,

(1.22)

where
d|w_{\eta\eta}|
denotes the variation of
w_{\eta\eta}
.

(iii)

The boundary conditions in (1.7) are satisfied in
the sense of trace, i.e.,

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\gamma w(\xi,\eta,\tau)|_{\tau=0}=w_{0}(\xi,\eta)&{\rm a.e.\ on}&\bar{Q}_{T}\cap\{\tau=0\},\\
\gamma w(\xi,\eta,\tau)|_{\eta=1}=0&{\rm a.e.\ on}&\bar{Q}_{T}\cap\{\eta=1\},\\
\gamma w(\xi,\eta,\tau)|_{\xi=0}=w_{1}(\eta,\tau)&{\rm a.e.\ on}&\bar{Q}_{T}\cap\{\xi=0\},\\
\displaystyle\gamma w_{\eta}(\xi,\eta,\tau)|_{\eta=0}=w_{0}(\xi,\tau)+\left.\frac{\partial_{x}P}{U}\frac{1}{\gamma w}\right|_{\eta=0}&{\rm a.e.\ on}&\bar{Q}_{T}\cap\{\eta=0\},\\
\end{array}\right.

where
\gamma w
and
\gamma w_{\eta}
are the traces of
w
and

w_{\eta}
on the corresponding boundaries respectively.

(iv)

For any
\varphi\in C^{1}(\bar{Q}_{T})
with
\varphi|_{\tau=0}=\varphi|_{\xi=0}=\varphi|_{\xi=L}=0
, the following identity holds:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}&\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}w^{-1}\varphi(1-\eta)^{2}\,d\xi\,d\eta|_{\tau=\tau}+\int\!\!\!\int_{Q_{T}}w^{-1}(1-\eta)^{2}\,\partial_{\tau}\varphi\,d\xi\,d\eta\,d\tau\\
+&\displaystyle\nu\int\!\!\!\int_{Q_{T}}\left((1-\eta)^{2}\varphi\right)_{\eta}w_{\eta}\,d\xi\,d\eta\,d\tau+\int\!\!\!\int_{Q_{T}}(\eta U\varphi)_{\xi}(1-\eta)^{2}\,w^{-1}\,d\xi\,d\eta\,d\tau\\
+&\displaystyle\int\!\!\!\int_{Q_{T}}\left((1-\eta)^{2}A\varphi\right)_{\eta}w^{-1}\,d\xi\,d\eta\,d\tau+\int\!\!\!\int_{Q_{T}}(1-\eta)^{2}\varphi Bw^{-1}\,d\xi\,d\eta\,d\tau\\
+&\displaystyle\int^{\tau}_{0}\!\!\!\!\int^{L}_{0}\upsilon_{0}(\xi,\tau)\varphi(\xi,0,\tau)d\xi\,d\tau=0\qquad\qquad\forall\,\tau\in(0,T)\,.\end{array}

The main results in this paper can be summarized in the following
theorem:

Theorem 1.2

Assume that the data satisfy condition (1.3) and (1.4), and
that the pressure is favorable, i.e., (1.5) holds. Then,

(i)

There exists a weak solution
w\in BV(Q_{T})\cap L^{\infty}(Q_{T})
in the sense of Definition 1.1;

(ii)

Such a weak solution is unique and depends
continuously on the initial and boundary data in
L^{1}
-norms;

(iii)

Such a weak solution is smooth in
Q_{T}
for any
T>0
.

Remark:
It should be remarked that the existence of a weak solution has
been proved by the first two authors in [13], in this paper, we
give a different proof by a direct BV estimate, which yields also some additional estimates (1.10) and (1.11) that are important for the structure of such weak solutions. The key new ingredients of the current paper are the uniqueness and the regularity of a weak solution.
Some new ideas are required due to the strong degeneracy of the
equation in (1.7). Moreover, the well-posedness we proved in the BV class in Proposition 3.5, in particular the estimate (3.35) is of independent interests and optimal, and the Poincaré type inequality we introduced for the proof of Hölder regularity of weak solutions is also of independent interests.

Together with Proposition 3.5, we have

Corollary 1.3

Assume that there are two initial datum
u_{10}(x,y)
,
u_{20}(x,y)
, boundary datum
u_{11}(y,t)
,
u_{21}(y,t)
and
v_{10}(x,t)
,
v_{20}(x,t)
satisfying condition (1.3) and (1.4) respectively, with the same
U(x,t)
as
y\rightarrow\infty
and
that the pressure is favorable, namely, (1.5) holds. Let
u_{1}(t,x,y)
and
u_{2}(t,x,y)
be the corresponding solution to the problem (1.1). Then, there exists a constant
C=C(T,L)
such that

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lcl}&&\displaystyle\int^{L}_{0}\!\!\!\int^{\infty}_{0}|\partial_{y}u_{1}(x,y,t)-\partial_{y}u_{2}(x,\tilde{y},t)|\frac{\partial_{y}u_{1}(x,y,t)}{U^{2}(x,t)}dy\,dx\\
&\leq&\displaystyle C\left\{\int^{\infty}_{0}\!\!\!\int^{L}_{0}|\partial_{y}u_{10}(x,y)-\partial_{y}u_{20}(x,\tilde{y})|\frac{\partial_{y}u_{10}(x,y)}{U^{2}(x,0)}dx\,dy+\int^{t}_{0}\!\!\!\int^{L}_{0}|\upsilon_{10}(x,s)-\upsilon_{20}(x,s)|dx\,ds\right.\\
&&\displaystyle+\left.\int^{t}_{0}\!\!\!\int^{\infty}_{0}|\partial_{y}u_{11}(y,s)-\partial_{y}u_{21}(\tilde{y},s)|\frac{\partial_{y}u_{11}(y,s)}{U^{2}(x,s)}dy\,ds\right\}\,,\end{array}

where
\tilde{y}
is given by
u_{1}(x,y,t)=u_{2}(x,\tilde{y},t)
which is uniquely determined.

Finally, we mention that there have been increasing activities in recent years on the studies of the unsteady Prandtl’s system in both two and three dimensions with substantial results for general initial data, ill-posedness, instability and different methods, we refer to [22-32] and references therein for these further developments.

§2
Existence

In this section, we will give an alternative proof of existence of
weak solutions to the initial-boundary value problem (1.7) by a
vanishing viscosity method, which is somewhat simpler than the
approach given by the first two authors in [13].

For the convenience of presentation, we may rewrite the problem
(1.7) as (we use notations independent of those in §1).

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}u-u^{2}\,\partial^{2}_{y}\,u+a\,\partial_{x}\,u+b\,\partial_{y}\,u+cu=0\\
u(x,y,t)|_{t=0}=u_{0}(x,y)\\
u(x,y,t)|_{x=0}=u_{1}(y,t)\\
\displaystyle u(x,y,t)|_{y=1}=0,\quad u\,\partial_{y}\,u|_{y=0}=\left.\left(\upsilon_{0}\,u+\frac{\partial_{x}P}{U}\right)\right|_{y=0}\end{array}\right.

with

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle a(x,y,t)=yU(x,t),\quad b(x,y,t)=A=(1-y^{2})\partial_{x}U(x,t)+(1-y)\frac{\partial_{t}U}{U}\,,\\
\displaystyle c=c(x,y,t)=B=(1-y)\partial_{x}U(x,t)-\frac{\partial_{x}P}{U}\,.\end{array}\right.

We also use the notations

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ccl}Q_{T}&=&\{(x,y,t)|0<x<L,\quad 0<y<1,\quad 0<t<T\},\\
\Omega&=&\{(x,y)|0<x<L,\quad 0<y<1\}.\end{array}

Then our main results in this section are:

Theorem 2.1

Assume that the pressure is favorable, i.e., (1.9) holds, and
there exists a positive constant
C_{0}
such that for all

0<x<L
,
0<y<1
,
0<t<T
, the initial and boundary condition

\displaystyle C^{-1}_{0}(1-y)<u_{0}(x,y)<C_{0}(1-y),\quad C^{-1}_{0}(1-y)<u_{1}(y,t)<C_{0}(1-y).

(2.10)

Then there exists a weak solution to the initial-boundary value
problem (2.1) in the sense given in Definition 1.1, i.e., there
exists a
u\in BV(Q_{T})\cap L^{\infty}(Q_{T})
with the properties:

(i)

It holds that

\displaystyle(1-y)^{\frac{\alpha-1}{2}}\,\partial_{y}\,u\in L^{2}(Q_{T}),\quad{\rm and}

(2.11)

\displaystyle C^{-1}_{1}(1-y)\leq u(x,y,t)\leq C_{1}(1-y),\quad(x,y,t)\in\,Q_{T}

(2.12)

for some positive constants
C_{1}=C_{1}(T,\Omega)
and
\alpha>0
.

(ii)

u_{yy}
is a locally bounded measure in
Q_{T}
in
the sense that

\displaystyle\int\!\!\!\int_{Q_{T}}(1-y)^{\alpha}\,d|u_{yy}|<+\infty,

(2.13)

where
d|u_{yy}|
denotes the variation of
u_{yy}
, and
\alpha>0
is a constant.

(iii)

And

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\gamma u(x,y,t)|_{t=0}=u_{0}(x,y)&{\rm a.e.\ on}&\Omega,\\
\gamma u(x,y,t)|_{y=1}=0&{\rm a.e.\ on}&\bar{Q}_{T}\cap\{y=1\},\\
\gamma u(x,y,t)|_{x=0}=u_{1}(y,t)&{\rm a.e.\ on}&\bar{Q}_{T}\cap\{x=0\},\\
\displaystyle\gamma u_{y}(x,y,t)|_{y=0}=\left.\left(\upsilon_{0}+\frac{\partial_{x}P}{U}\frac{1}{\gamma u}\right)\right|_{y=0}&{\rm a.e.\ on}&\bar{Q}_{T}\cap\{y=0\},\\
\end{array}\right.

where
\gamma u
and
\gamma u_{y}
are the traces of
u
and
\partial_{y}\,u
on the corresponding boundaries respectively.

(iv)

For any
\varphi\in C^{1}(\bar{Q}_{T})
with
\varphi|_{t=0}=\varphi|_{x=0}=\varphi|_{x=L}=0
, the following identity holds:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}&\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}u^{-1}\varphi(1-y)^{2}dx\,dy|_{t=t}+\int\!\!\!\int_{Q_{T}}[u^{-1}(1-y)^{2}\,\partial_{t}\varphi+\left((1-y)^{2}\varphi\right)_{y}\,u_{y}]\,dx\,dy\,dt\\
+&\displaystyle\int\!\!\!\int_{Q_{T}}[(a\varphi)_{x}(1-y)^{2}\frac{1}{u}\,+\left((1-y)^{2}b\varphi\right)_{y}\,\frac{1}{u}\,+(1-y)^{2}\,\varphi c\,\frac{1}{u}]\,dx\,dy\,dt\\
+&\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}\!\!\int^{L}_{0}\upsilon_{0}(x,t)\,\varphi(x,y,t)dx\,dt|_{y=0}=0\qquad\qquad\qquad{\rm for\ any}\qquad t\in(0,T)\,.\end{array}

We will prove the above theorem by studying the following
regularized problem:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}\,u-(u+\varepsilon)^{2}\,\partial^{2}_{y}\,u+(a+\varepsilon)\partial_{x}\,u+b\,\partial_{y}\,u+cu=0\\
u|_{t=0}=u^{\varepsilon}_{0},\quad u|_{x=0}=u^{\varepsilon}_{1}\\
\displaystyle u^{\varepsilon}|_{y=1}=0,\quad(u+\varepsilon)\partial_{y}u|_{y=0}=\left.\left(\upsilon^{\varepsilon}_{0}(u+\varepsilon)+\frac{\partial_{x}P}{U}\right)\right|_{y=0}\end{array}\right.

where
u^{\varepsilon}_{0}
,
u^{\varepsilon}_{1}
, and
\upsilon^{\varepsilon}_{0}
are some suitable
regularization of
u_{0}
,
u_{1}
, and
\upsilon_{0}
respectively, and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
is a constant.

It follows from the standard theory for initial-boundary value
problems for ultra-parabolic equations that for each fixed
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a unique classical solution
u^{\varepsilon}
to the
problem (2.9). Our main strategy is to pass the limit as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}
in (2.9) to obtain a solution to the problem (2.1). To
this end, some a priori estimates are needed. We start with the
super-norm estimates.

Lemma 2.2

Assume that (1.5) holds and that there exists a positive
constant
C_{0}
such that the initial and boundary data

\displaystyle C^{-1}_{0}(1-y)\leq u^{\varepsilon}_{i}\leq C_{0}(1-y),\quad{\rm on}\quad Q_{T},\quad i=0,1\,.

(2.27)

Then there exists a positive constant
C_{1}=C_{1}(Q_{T})
such that the solution

\displaystyle C^{-1}_{1}(1-y)\leq u^{\varepsilon}(x,y,t)\leq C_{1}(1-y),\quad\forall(x,y,t)\,\in\,Q_{T}\,.

(2.28)

Proof:

First, it follows from the assumptions (1.3), (1.5), (2.10), and
the standard maximum principle argument that

\displaystyle u^{\varepsilon}(x,y,t)\geq 0\qquad\qquad{\rm on}\qquad Q_{T}\,.

(2.29)

Next, we estimate the upper bound on
u^{\varepsilon}
. For any given
\delta>0
, consider

\theta(x,y,t)=\frac{u^{\varepsilon}}{1-y+\delta}

Then
\theta(x,y,t)
solves the following initial-boundary value
problem:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\partial_{t}\theta-(u^{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon)\theta_{yy}+(a+\varepsilon)\partial_{x}\theta+\left(b-\frac{2(u^{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon)^{2}}{1-y+\delta}\right)\theta_{y}+\left(c-\frac{b}{1-y+\delta}\right)\theta=0\\
\displaystyle\theta|_{t=0}=\frac{u^{\varepsilon}_{0}}{1-y+\delta}\,,\quad\theta|_{x=0}=\frac{u^{\varepsilon}_{1}}{1-y+\delta}\,,\quad\theta|_{y=1}=0\\
\displaystyle(u^{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon)\left.\left[\partial_{y}\theta-\frac{\theta}{1-y+\delta}\right]\right|_{y=0}=\left.\left[\upsilon_{0}\,\frac{u^{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon}{1-y+\delta}+\frac{\partial_{x}P}{(1-y+\delta)U}\right]\right|_{y=0}\end{array}\right.

Due to the specific structure of
b
and
c
((2.2)), the
coefficient
\displaystyle c(x,y,t)-\frac{b(x,y,t)}{1-y+\delta}
admits a
uniform bounded,

\left|c(x,y,t)-\frac{b(x,y,t)}{1-y+\delta}\right|\leq\tilde{c}_{2}

where
\tilde{c}_{2}
is independent of
\delta
and
\varepsilon
. Thus,
standard maximum principle argument leads to

\displaystyle 0\leq\theta(x,y,t)\leq e^{\tilde{c}_{2}T}\,\max\{\theta|_{t=0},\theta|_{x=0},\theta|_{y=0},\theta|_{y=1}\}\,.

(2.34)

Since
\theta|_{t=0}\leq C_{0}
,
\theta|_{x=0}\leq C_{0}
, and

\theta|_{y=1}=0
, it suffices to estimate the
\displaystyle\max_{y=0}\,\theta(x,y,t)
. At the maximum,
\partial_{y}\theta\leq 0
, so the boundary
condition at
y=0
yields

\frac{u^{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon}{1+\delta}\theta\leq|\upsilon_{0}|\frac{u^{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon}{1+\delta}+\frac{|\partial_{x}P|}{(1+\delta)|U|}\,,

which implies

\theta^{2}\leq|\upsilon_{0}|(1+\theta)+\frac{|\partial_{x}P|}{|U|}\,.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\max_{y=0}\,\theta(x,y,t)\leq\tilde{c}_{3}

where
\tilde{c}_{3}
is a positive constant independent of
\varepsilon

and
\delta
. Thus (2.14) shows that

0\leq\theta(x,y,t)\leq e^{\tilde{c}_{2}T}\,\max(c_{0},\tilde{c}_{3})=\tilde{c}_{4}\,,

and so

0\leq u^{\varepsilon}(x,y,t)\leq\tilde{c}_{4}(1-y+\delta)\,.

Since
\delta
is arbitrary, the desired upper bound in
(2.11) follows. Finally, we obtain the lower bound estimate. Set

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\theta(x,y,t)&=&e^{\alpha t}\,u^{\varepsilon}-\mu_{0}\,\varphi(y)e^{-\beta t},\quad{\rm with}\\
\varphi(y)&=&e^{-\gamma(1-y)^{2}}(1-y)\end{array}\right.

where
\alpha
,
\beta
,
\gamma
are large and
\mu_{0}
is small to be
determined. Define also operator

\mathcal{L}=\partial_{t}-(u^{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon)^{2}\partial^{2}_{y}+(a+\varepsilon)\partial_{x}+b\,\partial_{y}+c\,.

Then direct calculations yield

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rcl}\mathcal{L}\theta&=&\alpha\theta+\mu_{0}\,\varphi(y)e^{-\beta t}\left[\alpha+\beta-(u^{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon)(6\gamma+4\gamma(1-y)^{2})-b\frac{(2\gamma(y-1)^{2}-1)}{(1-y)}+c\right]\,,\\
\theta|_{t=0}&=&u^{\varepsilon}_{0}-\mu_{0}\,\varphi(y),\quad\theta|_{y=1}=0\,,\\
\theta|_{x=0}&=&u^{\varepsilon}_{1}\,e^{\alpha t}-\mu_{0}\,\varphi(y)e^{-\beta t}=e^{\alpha t}\left[u^{\varepsilon}_{1}-\mu_{0}\,e^{-(\alpha+\beta)t}\,\varphi(y)\right]\,,\\
\displaystyle\partial_{y}\theta|_{y=0}&=&\displaystyle e^{\alpha t}\left.\left(\upsilon_{0}+\frac{\partial_{x}P}{(u^{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon)U}\right)\right|_{y=0}-\mu_{0}\,e^{-\gamma}(2\gamma-1)e^{-\beta t}\,.\end{array}

We now fix
\gamma
so that
\gamma>\frac{1}{2}
. Thus,
\partial_{y}\,\theta|_{y=0}<0
.
Then one can choose
\mu_{0}
so small such that
\theta|_{t=0}\geq 0
, and

\theta|_{x=0}\geq e^{\alpha t}[u^{\varepsilon}_{1}-\mu_{0}\,\phi(y)]\geq 0

by the assumption (2.10).

Due to the upper bound estimate on
u^{\varepsilon}
, one can choose

\alpha
and
\beta
big enough so that

\alpha+\beta-(u^{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon)(6\gamma+4\gamma(1-y)^{2})-b\frac{(2\gamma(y-1)^{2}-1)}{(1-y)}+c>0\,.

Hence

\mathcal{L}\theta-\alpha\theta>0\qquad\qquad{\rm in}\qquad Q_{T}\,.

It follows by the maximum principle that
\theta\geq 0
on
Q_{T}
. As
a consequence, we have shown that

u^{\varepsilon}(x,y,t)\geq C^{-1}_{1}(1-y)

where
C_{1}=C_{1}(Q_{T})
is a positive constant independent of
\varepsilon
.
The proof of Lemma 2.2 is completed.

\square

Next, we derive the uniform total variation estimate on the
approximation solutions. For any given scalar function
\psi
, we set

\displaystyle\nabla\psi={\rm grad\ }\psi=(\partial_{x}\psi,\partial_{y}\psi,\partial_{t}\psi)^{t}\,,\qquad{\rm and}

(2.39)

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}\psi=(\partial_{x}\psi,\partial_{t}\psi)^{t}\,.\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\quad

(2.40)

Then, our next key uniform estimate is the following total
variation estimate on
u^{\varepsilon}
.

Proposition 2.3

There exists a positive constant
C_{2}=C_{2}(Q_{T})
independent
of
\varepsilon
, such that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{Q_{T}}|\nabla u^{\varepsilon}(x,y,t)|dx\,dy\,dt\leq C_{2}\,.

(2.41)

This proposition will follow from the following two lemmas
directly.

Lemma 2.4

For any given constant
\alpha>-1
, there exists a positive
constant
C_{3}=C_{3}(\alpha)
such that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{Q_{T}}(1-y)^{\alpha}|\partial_{y}u^{\varepsilon}|dx\,dy\,dt\leq C_{3}\,,\quad{\rm and}

(2.42)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{Q_{T}}(1-y)^{\alpha}|\partial_{y}u^{\varepsilon}|^{2}dx\,dy\,dt\leq C_{3}\,.\quad\quad\,

(2.43)

Proof:

Note that (2.19) follows immediately
from (2.20) by the Hölder inequality. So it suffices to
show that (2.20) holds. Setting
g(u)=ln(u+\varepsilon)+\varepsilon(u+\varepsilon)^{-1}
, multiplying the equation in (2.9) by

(1-y)^{\alpha}g^{\prime}(u^{\varepsilon})
, and integrating the resulting equation over

Q_{T}
, one can obtain after integration by parts that

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}&\displaystyle\left.\int_{\Omega}g(u^{\varepsilon})(1-y)^{\alpha}\right|^{T}_{0}dx\,dy\\
=&\displaystyle-\int_{Q_{T}}(1-y)^{\alpha}(\partial_{y}u^{\varepsilon})^{2}dx\,dy\,dt+\alpha\int_{Q_{T}}(1-y)^{\alpha-1}\,u^{\varepsilon}\,\partial_{y}u^{\varepsilon}dx\,dy\,dt\\
&\displaystyle+\int_{Q_{T}}g(u^{\varepsilon})\partial_{y}\,((1-y)^{\alpha}\,b)\,dx\,dy\,dt+\int_{Q_{T}}(1-y)^{\alpha}\,\partial_{x}a\,g(u^{\varepsilon})dx\,dy\,dt\\
&\displaystyle-\int_{Q_{T}}c(1-y)^{\alpha}\frac{(u^{\varepsilon})^{2}}{(u^{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon)^{2}}dx\,dy\,dt+\left.\int^{T}_{0}\!\!\!\int^{L}_{0}(1-y)^{\alpha}\,u^{\varepsilon}\partial_{y}u^{\varepsilon}dx\,dt\right|^{1}_{0}\\
&\displaystyle-\left.\int^{T}_{0}\!\!\!\int^{L}_{0}(1-y)^{\alpha}\,b\,g(u^{\varepsilon})dx\,dt\right|^{1}_{0}-\left.\int^{T}_{0}\!\!\!\int^{1}_{0}(1-y)^{\alpha}(a+\varepsilon)\,g(u^{\varepsilon})dy\,dt\right|^{L}_{0}\end{array}

First, it holds that

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}&\displaystyle\left|\alpha\int_{Q_{T}}(1-y)^{\alpha-1}\,u^{\varepsilon}\,\partial_{y}u^{\varepsilon}dx\,dy\,dt\right|\\
\leq&\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{Q_{T}}(1-y)^{\alpha}(\partial_{y}u^{\varepsilon})^{2}dx\,dy\,dt+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}\int_{Q_{T}}(1-y)^{\alpha-2}\,(u^{\varepsilon})^{2}dx\,dy\,dt\end{array}

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rcl}|I_{11}|&\leq&\displaystyle\int_{B^{-}_{r/\theta}}|\partial^{2}_{\eta}\phi(\zeta)|w(\zeta)\Gamma_{0}(z,\zeta)d\zeta\\
&\leq&\displaystyle ln\,h^{-\frac{1}{8}}\int^{0}_{-r^{2}}\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\partial^{2}_{\eta}\phi(\zeta)|\Gamma_{0}(z,\zeta)d\zeta\\
&\leq&\displaystyle ln\,h^{-\frac{1}{8}}r^{2}\,\sup_{\text{supp}\phi}|\partial^{2}_{\eta}\phi(\zeta)|,\end{array}

where one has used (4.32) and the fact

B^{-}_{r/\theta}\cap\,\text{supp}\phi\subset Q^{-}_{\theta}

due to Lemma 4.4. On the other hand, it follows from (4.36)-(4.37), (4.34), and direct computatons that

|\partial^{2}_{\eta}\phi(\zeta)|\leq c\theta^{2}r^{-2}.

This, together with (4.55), yields

\displaystyle|I_{11}|\leq c\theta^{2}ln(h^{-\frac{1}{8}}).

(4.85)

We now turn to the estimate
I_{12}=\int_{B^{-}_{r/\theta}}\Gamma_{0}(z,\zeta)(\partial_{\tau}\phi(\zeta)+\eta\partial_{\xi}\phi(\zeta))w(\zeta)d\zeta
.

First, note that for suitably small
\theta>0
,
z\in B^{-}_{r/\theta}
,
\phi(z)=1
. Thus for
z\in B^{-}_{r/\theta}
,

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rcl}1&=&\displaystyle\int_{B^{-}_{r/\theta}}\Gamma_{0}(z,\zeta)(\partial_{\tau}+\eta\partial_{\xi}-\partial^{2}_{\eta})\phi(\zeta)d\zeta\\
&=&\displaystyle\int_{B^{-}_{r/\theta}}\phi_{1}\Gamma_{0}(z,\zeta)(\partial_{\tau}\phi_{0}+\eta\partial_{\xi}\phi_{0})d\zeta+\int_{B^{-}_{r/\theta}}\Gamma_{0}(z,\zeta)(-\partial^{2}_{\eta}\phi(\zeta))d\zeta.\end{array}

This and the arguments for (4.55)-(4.56) show that

\displaystyle\int_{B^{-}_{r/\theta}}\phi_{1}(\zeta)\Gamma_{0}(z,\zeta)(\partial_{\tau}\phi_{0}(\zeta)+\eta\partial_{\xi}\phi_{0}(\zeta))d\zeta=1+O(1)\theta^{2}.

(4.87)

Next, let
\alpha_{1}
,
\beta
and
h_{1}
be given in Proposition 4.2 and set

\displaystyle S=\left\{\zeta=(\xi,\eta,\tau)|-\alpha_{1}r^{2}\leq\tau\leq-\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2}r^{2},\quad(\xi,\eta)\in C_{\beta r},\quad w(\zeta)=0\right\}

(4.88)

Note that if
\zeta
is such that
u(\zeta)\geq h
, then
w(\zeta)=0
, and
u
is assumed to satisfy the assumptions in Proposition 4.2. Thus it follows from Proposition 4.2 and the definition of
S
that there exists a positive constant
c(\alpha_{1},\beta)>0
such that

\displaystyle\text{mes}\,S\geq c(\alpha_{1},\beta)r^{6}\quad\text{for}\quad h\leq h_{1}

(4.89)

Furthermore, it follows from the construction of
\chi
((4.34)) and Lemma 4.4 that for
\theta
suitably small and any
\zeta\in S
,
\phi_{1}(\zeta)=1
,
\phi_{0}(\zeta)>0
,
\theta^{\frac{1}{6}}r<(\theta^{2}|\xi|^{2}-6\tau\,r^{4})^{\frac{1}{6}}<r
,
6r^{4}-2\eta\xi\theta^{2}>3r^{4}
, and

|\chi^{\prime}([\theta^{2}|\xi|^{2}-6\tau r^{4}]^{\frac{1}{6}})|\geq\frac{c(\alpha_{1},\theta)}{r}>0

with a uniform constant
c(\alpha_{1},\theta)
independent of
r
. Hence

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cl}&\displaystyle\int_{S}\Gamma_{0}(z,\zeta)(\partial_{\tau}\phi_{0}(\zeta)+\eta\partial_{\xi}\phi_{0}(\zeta))\phi_{1}(\zeta)d\zeta\\
=&\displaystyle\int_{S}\Gamma_{0}(z,\zeta)|\chi^{\prime}([\theta^{2}|\xi|^{2}-6\tau r^{4}]^{\frac{1}{6}})\,\frac{1}{6}[\theta^{2}\xi^{2}-6\tau r^{4}]^{\frac{5}{6}}[6r^{4}-2\eta\xi\theta^{2}]d\zeta\\
\geq&\displaystyle c(\alpha_{1},\theta)\int_{S}r^{-2}\Gamma_{0}(z,\zeta)\alpha\zeta\geq\underline{c}>0,\end{array}

where
\underline{c}=c(\alpha_{1},\beta,\theta)>0
, and one has used (4.59) and the fact that

\Gamma_{0}(z,\zeta)\geq cr^{-4}\quad\text{for}\quad\tau\leq-\frac{\alpha_{1}}{2}r^{2},\quad z\in B^{-}_{\theta r}

if
\theta^{2}\leq\frac{1}{4}\alpha_{1}
. We are now ready to estimate
I_{12}
. It follows from (4.58) and Lemma 4.4 that

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rcl}\displaystyle|I_{12}|&=&\displaystyle\left|\int_{B^{-}_{r/\theta}}\Gamma_{0}(z,\zeta)(\partial_{\tau}\phi(\zeta)+\eta\partial_{\xi}\phi(\zeta))w(\zeta)d\zeta\right|\\
&=&\displaystyle\int_{B^{-}_{r/\theta}\setminus S}\Gamma_{0}(z,\zeta)(\partial_{\tau}\phi(\zeta)+\eta\partial_{\xi}\phi(\zeta))w(\zeta)d\zeta\\
&\leq&\displaystyle ln\,h^{-\frac{1}{8}}\int_{B^{-}_{r/\theta}\setminus S}\Gamma_{0}(z,\zeta)(\partial_{\tau}\phi(\zeta)+\eta\partial_{\xi}\phi(\zeta))d\zeta\\
&=&\displaystyle ln\,h^{-\frac{1}{8}}\left(\int_{B^{-}_{r/\theta}}\Gamma_{0}(z,\zeta)(\partial_{\tau}\phi(\zeta)+\eta\partial_{\xi}\phi(\zeta))d\zeta-\int_{S}\Gamma_{0}(z,\zeta)(\partial_{\tau}\phi(\zeta)+\eta\partial_{\xi}\phi(\zeta))d\zeta\right)\\
&=&\displaystyle ln\,h^{-\frac{1}{8}}\left(1+O(1)\theta^{2}-\int_{S}\Gamma_{0}(z,\zeta)(\partial_{\tau}\phi(\zeta)+\eta\partial_{3}\phi(\zeta))d\zeta\right)\\
&\leq&(1-\underline{c}+O(1)\theta^{2})ln\,h^{-\frac{1}{8}},\\
\end{array}

where one has used (4.57) and (4.60).

Consequently, it holds that

I_{0}=\sup_{B^{-}_{\theta r}}I_{1}(z)\leq(1-\underline{c}+O(1)\theta^{2})ln\,h^{-\frac{1}{8}}

for any
r<\theta
,
h\leq h_{1}
, and
\theta
being suitably small. Set
\lambda_{0}=1-\underline{c}+O(1)\theta^{2}
. Then the conclusion in Lemma 4.2 holds. This completes the proof of Lemma 4.6.
\square

We are now ready to give the main step in the oscillation estimates.

Lemma 4.7

Let
u
be a non-negative weak solution to (4.15) in
B^{-}_{1}
satisfying the assumption in Proposition 4.2. Then there exist positive constants
\bar{h}
and
\bar{\theta}
in (0,1), which depend only on
\lambda_{0}
and
\Lambda
, such that

\displaystyle u(z)\geq\bar{h}>0\quad\text{on}\quad B^{-}_{\bar{\theta}r}.

(4.101)

Proof
: Let
h_{0}
and
\theta
be fixed such that the conclusions in Proposition 4.5 and Lemma 4.6 hold. Set

w(z)=ln^{+}\left(\frac{h}{u+h^{\frac{9}{8}}}\right),\quad 0<h\leq h_{0}

It then follows from Proposition 4.5 that

\displaystyle f_{B^{-}_{\theta r}}((w-I_{0})^{+})^{2}dz\leq c\frac{\theta^{2}r^{2}}{|B^{-}_{\theta r}|}\int_{B^{-}_{r/\theta}}|\partial_{y}w(z)|^{2}dz.

(4.102)

Where
f_{\Omega}f\,dz
denotes the average of
f
on
\Omega
. To estimate the integral on the right hand side of (4.63), we note that
w(z)=ln^{+}(\frac{1}{\tilde{u}+h^{\frac{1}{8}}})
with
\tilde{u}=\frac{u}{h}
being a non-negative solution to (4.15). One then can follow the argument for (4.22) in the proof of Proposition 4.2 that for
\tau<t
in
(-(\tilde{r})^{2},0)
with
\tilde{r}=\frac{r}{\theta}<1
, it holds that

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cl}&\displaystyle\int_{c^{-}_{\tilde{r}}}w(z)dxdy+\int^{t}_{\tau}\!\!\int_{c^{-}_{(1+\delta)\tilde{r}}}\chi^{2}_{\delta}a|\partial_{y}\,w|^{2}dz\\
&\displaystyle\int_{c^{-}_{(1+\delta)\tilde{r}}}\chi^{2}_{\delta}w(l,y,\tau)dxdy-\int^{t}_{\tau}\!\!\int_{c^{-}_{(1+\delta)\tilde{r}}}2\chi_{\delta}(\partial y\chi_{\delta})a\partial_{y}w\,dz-\int^{t}_{\tau}\!\!\int_{c^{-}_{(1+\delta)\tilde{r}}}\chi^{2}_{\delta}\,y\,\partial_{x}\,w\,dz\end{array}

where
\delta\in(0,1)
so that
(1+\delta)\tilde{r}<1
, and
\chi_{\delta}=\chi_{\delta}(|y|)
and
\chi_{\delta}\in c^{\infty}[0,\infty)
,
0<\chi_{\delta}(s)\leq 1
,
\chi_{\delta}(s)=1
for
0\leq s\leq\tilde{r}
,
\chi_{\delta}(s)=0
for
s>(1+\delta)\tilde{r}
, and

\displaystyle|\chi^{\prime}_{\delta}(s)|\leq\frac{2}{\delta\tilde{r}}\quad\text{for all}\quad s\geq 0

(4.106)

As in the derivation of (4.23), one has from (4.65) that

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rcl}\displaystyle\int^{t}_{\tau}\!\!\int_{c^{-}_{(1+\delta)\tilde{r}}}2\chi_{\delta}\partial_{y}\chi_{\delta}a\partial_{y}wdz&\leq&\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int^{t}_{\tau}\!\!\int_{c^{-}_{(1+\delta)\tilde{r}}}a\chi^{2}_{\delta}(\partial_{y}w|^{2}dz+\frac{16\Lambda}{\delta^{2}\tilde{r}^{2}}(t-\tau)|c^{-}_{(1+\delta)\tilde{r}}|\\
&\leq&\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int^{t}_{\tau}\!\!\int_{c^{-}_{(1+\delta)\tilde{r}}}a\chi^{2}_{\delta}|\partial_{y}w|^{2}dz+\frac{16\Lambda}{\delta^{2}}(1+\delta)^{4}\tilde{r}^{4},\\
\displaystyle-\int^{t}_{\tau}\!\!\int_{c^{-}_{(1+\delta)\tilde{r}}}\chi^{2}_{\delta}y\partial_{x}w\,dz&=&\displaystyle-\int^{t}_{\tau}\!\!\int^{(1+\delta)\tilde{r}}_{-(1+\delta)\tilde{r}}\chi^{2}_{\delta}yw\bigg|^{((1+\delta)\tilde{r})^{3}}_{-((1+\delta)\tilde{r})^{3}}dyds\\
&\leq&\displaystyle(t-\tau)(1+\delta)^{2}\tilde{r}^{2}lnh^{-\frac{1}{8}}\leq(1+\delta)^{2}\tilde{r}^{4}ln\,h^{-\frac{1}{8}},\end{array}

and

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rcl}\displaystyle\int_{c^{-}_{(1+\delta)\tilde{r}}}\chi^{2}_{\delta}w(x,y,\tau)dxdy&=&\displaystyle\int_{c^{-}_{(1+\delta)\tilde{r}}\cap\{\tilde{u}\leq 1\}}\chi^{2}_{\delta}w(x,y,\tau)dxdy\\
&\leq&\displaystyle ln\,h^{-\frac{1}{8}}\,\text{mes}\,c^{-}_{(1+\delta)\tilde{r}}=(1+\delta)^{4}\tilde{r}^{4}ln\,h^{-\frac{1}{8}}.\end{array}

These and (4.64) yield that for
\tau
and
t
,
\tau<t
in
(-\tilde{r},0)
,

\int^{t}_{\tau}\!\!\int_{c^{-}_{(1+\delta)\tilde{r}}}\chi^{2}_{\delta}a|\partial_{y}w(z)|^{2}dz\leq((1+\delta)^{4}(1+\frac{16\Lambda}{\delta^{2}}))\tilde{r}^{4}ln\,h^{-\frac{1}{8}}.

This, together with the construction of
\chi_{\delta}
and (4.16), implies that

\displaystyle\int_{B^{-}_{\tilde{r}}}|\partial_{y}w(z)|^{2}dz\leq C(\Lambda,\delta)\tilde{r}^{4}ln\,h^{-\frac{1}{8}}

(4.109)

It follows from (4.66) and (4.63) that there exists a positive constant
C=C(\Lambda,\theta,\delta)>0
such that

\displaystyle f_{B^{-}}((w-I_{0})^{+})^{2}dz\leq C\frac{\theta^{2}\,r^{2}}{|B^{-}_{\theta r}|}\int_{B^{-}_{\tilde{r}}}|\partial_{y}w|^{2}dz\leq C\,ln\,h^{-\frac{1}{8}}.

(4.110)

Based on (4.67), one can modify the Moser’s iteration method for
(w-I_{0})^{+}
as in [10] to show that there exists a uniform constant
k\in(0,1)
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{B^{-}_{k\theta r}}((w-I_{0})^{+})^{2}\leq C\,ln\,h^{-\frac{1}{8}}.

(4.111)

Set
\bar{\theta}=k\theta
. Then (4.68) and Lemma 4.6 imply that for all
z\in B^{-}_{\bar{\theta}r}
,

\displaystyle w(z)\leq I_{0}+C(ln\,h^{-\frac{1}{8}})^{2}\leq\lambda_{0}\,ln\,h^{-\frac{1}{8}}+C(ln\,h^{-\frac{1}{8}})^{2}.

(4.112)

Since
\displaystyle\lim_{h\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\lambda_{1}\,ln\,h^{-\frac{1}{8}}+C(ln\,h^{-\frac{1}{8}})^{\frac{1}{2}}}{ln(\frac{1}{2h^{\frac{1}{8}}})}=\lambda_{0}<1
due to Lemma 4.6, thus there exists a constant
h_{2}\leq\min(h_{0},2^{-8})
such that

\lambda_{0}\,ln\,h^{-\frac{1}{8}}_{2}+C(ln\,h_{2}^{-\frac{1}{8}})^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq ln(\frac{1}{2h^{\frac{1}{8}}_{0}}).

Consequently, it holds that

\displaystyle\max_{B^{-}_{\bar{\theta}r}}ln^{+}(\frac{h_{2}}{u+h_{2}^{\frac{9}{8}}})\leq ln(\frac{1}{2h^{\frac{1}{8}}_{2}}).

(4.113)

It follows from (4.70) and the choice of
h_{2}
that

\displaystyle\min_{B^{-}_{\bar{\theta}r}}\,u\geq h^{\frac{9}{8}}_{2}

(4.114)

which yields the desired estimate (4.62). Hence the proof of Lemma 4.7 is completed.

As an immediately consequence of Lemma 4.7, the following desired oscillation estimate holds.

Proposition 4.8

Let
u
be a weak solution of (4.15) in
B^{-}_{1}
and
\bar{\theta}
and
r
be given as in Lemma 4.7. Then there exists an uniform constant
\bar{\beta}
,
0<\bar{\beta}<1
, such that

\displaystyle\text{Osc}_{B^{-}_{\bar{\theta}r}}\,u\leq\bar{\beta}\,\text{Osc}_{B^{-}_{r}}\,u

(4.115)

where
\text{Osc}_{Q}\,f
denotes the oscillation of
f
over
Q
for any domain
\Omega
.

Proof
: Since
u
is bounded, one can assume that
M\equiv\max_{B^{-}_{r}}\,u=-m\equiv-\min_{B^{-}_{r}}\,u
without loss of generality since otherwise, one may consider
u-\frac{1}{2}(M+m)
. Thus both
1+\frac{u}{M}
and
1-\frac{u}{M}
are non-negative weak solutions to (4.15), and at least one of them satisfies the main assumption (4.17). We treat the case that
1-\frac{u}{M}
satisfies (4.17). Then Lemma 4.7 can be applied to
(1-\frac{u}{M})
to get

(1-\frac{u}{M})(z)\geq\bar{h}\quad\text{on}\quad B^{-}_{\bar{\theta}r},

where
\bar{h}\in(0,1)
is a constant given in Lemma 4.7. Thus one gets that
\max_{B^{-}_{\bar{\theta}r}}\,u\leq(1-\bar{h})M
. Consequently,

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rcl}\displaystyle\text{Osc}_{B^{-}_{\bar{\theta}r}}\,u&=&\displaystyle\max_{B^{-}_{\bar{\theta}r}}\,u-\min_{B^{-}_{\bar{\theta}r}}\,u\leq\max_{B^{-}_{\bar{\theta}r}}\,u+M\\
&\leq&\displaystyle(1-\bar{h})M+M=(1-\frac{\bar{h}}{2})2M=(1-\frac{\bar{h}}{2})\text{Osc}_{B^{-}_{r}}\,u,\end{array}

which yields the desired estimate (4.72) with
\bar{\beta}=(1-\frac{\bar{h}}{2})
. Hence the proof of Proposition (4.72) is completed.
\square

It follows from the oscillation estimate (4.72) in Proposition 4.8 and the standard regularity arguments [16] that the following statement holds.

Proposition 4.9

Let
u
be a weak solution to (4.15) in
B^{-}_{1}
. Then there exists a positive constant
\delta>0
such that
u
is Hölder continuous on
B^{-}_{\delta}
which is a small neighborhood of
z=0
.

Now, Proposition 4.9 shows that the weak solution
u
to (4.2) is Hölder continuous in an interior point in
Q_{T}
. Then the standard regularity theory for ultra-parabolic equation [1, 14].
u
is in fact
C^{\infty}
smooth in
Q_{T}
. Thus the proof of Theorem 4.1 is completed.

Abstract
The theory of affine processes has been recently extended to the framework of stochastic Volterra equations with continuous trajectories. These so–called affine Volterra processes overcome modeling shortcomings of affine processes because they can have trajectories whose regularity is different from the regularity of the paths of Brownian motion. More specifically, singular kernels yield rough affine processes. This paper extends the theory by considering affine stochastic Volterra equations with jumps. This extension is not straightforward because the jump structure together with possible singularities of the kernel may induce explosions of the trajectories. Keywords: affine processes, affine Volterra processes, stochastic Volterra equations, Riccati–Volterra equations, rough volatility. MSC2020 classifications: 60H20 (primary), 60G22, 45D05, 60G17 (secondary).

Introduction

Affine stochastic processes constitute unquestionably the most popular multi–factor framework to model rich and flexible stochastic dependence structures.
Semi–explicit formulas for the Fourier–Laplace transform of affine processes make them numerically tractable since Fourier transform–based methods can be used to perform fast calculations.

We recall that a square–integrable conservative regular affine process
X
with state space
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a special semimartingale whose semimartingale characteristics
(B,C,\nu)
, with respect to the “truncation function”

B_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}b(X_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s,\quad C_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}a(X_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s,\quad\nu(\mathrm{d}t,\mathrm{d}\xi)=\eta(X_{t},\mathrm{d}\xi)\,\mathrm{d}t

(1)

where

b(x)=b_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{m}x_{k}b_{k},\quad a(x)=A_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{m}x_{k}A_{k},\quad\eta(x,\mathrm{d}\xi)=\nu_{0}(\mathrm{d}\xi)+\sum_{k=1}^{m}x_{k}\nu_{k}(\mathrm{d}\xi),\quad x\in E.

(2)

In (
2

A_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}
,
b_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, and
\nu_{k}(\mathrm{d}\xi)
signed measures on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
such that

and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}|\xi|^{2}|\nu_{k}|(\mathrm{d}\xi)<\infty

b_{k}
and
\nu_{k}

[

&\leq\left\lVert F\right\rVert_{L^{1}\left(\left[0,T\right];\mathbb{R}^{l\times d}\right)}\left[\left|b_{0}\right|T+n\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left|b_{k}\right|\right)\right]<\infty,\quad T>0.\end{split}

This shows that there exists a

null set

such that the next expression is well–defined

1_{\left\{t\leq\tau_{n}\left(\omega\right)\right\}}\int_{0}^{t}F\left(t-s\right)\left(b_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{m}b_{k}X_{k,s}\left(\omega\right)\right)\mathrm{d}s,\quad n\in\mathbb{N},\,\left(\omega,t\right)\in\left(\Omega\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\right)\setminus N_{1}.

\mathbb{P}-

(18)

2.1
Stochastic convolution for processes with jumps

The goal of this subsection is to develop technical results concerning the stochastic convolution. In particular, we aim to make Lemma

and Lemma

in
[

]
feasible in our context, where we are dealing with discontinuities for

Such changes are important from a conceptual point of view and after every result we add a remark showing the parallel with the setting in

We start with a preliminary claim.

Lemma 2
.

Fix
p\in\mathbb{N}
. Let

(20)

In particular,

F\ast\mathrm{d}Z=G\ast\mathrm{d}Z,\quad\mathbb{P}\otimes\mathrm{d}t-\text{a.e.}

(21)

Proof.

It is sufficient to prove (
20
) replacing
Z
with
\widetilde{Z}
, because trivially

\mathbb{P}\otimes\mathrm{d}t-
a.e. on
\Omega\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Moreover, we only work with the stochastic integral in
\mathrm{d}M^{c}
, as by (
15

\mathrm{d}M^{d}

The argument above in the section implies the existence of a
\mathrm{d}t-

\int_{0}^{T}1_{S}\left(s\right)G\left(T-s\right)\mathrm{d}M^{c}_{s}-\int_{0}^{T}F\left(T-s\right)\mathrm{d}M^{c}_{s}=\int_{0}^{T}\left(1_{S}\left(s\right)G\left(T-s\right)-F\left(T-s\right)\right)\mathrm{d}M^{c}_{s},\quad\mathbb{P}-\text{a.s.}

Consider the square–integrable,

Since

) follows.

Regarding (

), it is an immediate consequence of (

) with

and the joint measurability of the stochastic convolutions, which allows to state an equality holding true
\mathbb{P}\otimes\mathrm{d}t-

∎

Remark 2
.

In
[
5
]
, the authors consider the stochastic convolution of a function
F\in L^{2}_{\emph{loc}}\left(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathbb{R}^{p\times d}\right)
with respect to a continuous local martingale
M
with predictable quadratic covariation

, where
\left(a_{t}\right)
is an adapted, locally bounded process. These assumptions allow to define
\left(F\ast\mathrm{d}M\right)_{t}
for every

. In particular, two jointly measurable versions of the stochastic convolution are equal
\mathbb{P}-
a.s., for every

\mathbb{P}\otimes\mathrm{d}t-
uniqueness that we have in our framework. As for (

, in the continuous case it can be stated as follows: for every
F,G\in L^{2}_{\emph{loc}}\left(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathbb{R}^{p\times d}\right)
, with

a.e. in
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, one has

\left(F\ast\mathrm{d}M\right)_{t}=\left(G\ast\mathrm{d}M\right)_{t},\quad\mathbb{P}-\text{a.s., }t\geq 0.

Now we state a result concerning the associativity of the stochastic convolution.

Lemma 3
.

Fix
p,q\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
\rho\in L^{1}_{\emph{loc}}\left(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathbb{R}^{q\times p}\right)
and
F\in L^{2}_{\emph{loc}}\left(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathbb{R}^{p\times d}\right)
. Then

\left(\left(\rho\ast F\right)\ast\mathrm{d}Z\right)_{t}=\left(\rho\ast\left(F\ast\mathrm{d}Z\right)\right)\left(t\right),\quad\mathbb{P}-\text{a.s., for a.e. }t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.

(23)

Proof.

Also in this case we just need to show the statement with
\mathrm{d}\widetilde{Z}
in place of
\mathrm{d}Z
, because an application of Fubini’s theorem provides

\left(\rho\ast F\right)\ast b\left(X\right)=\rho\ast\left(F\ast b\left(X\right)\right),\,\mathbb{P}\otimes\mathrm{d}t-
a.e. on
\Omega\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
.
In addition it is sufficient to focus only on the stochastic convolutions in
\mathrm{d}M^{c}
, as discussed in the preceding proof. By linearity we can assume
d=p=q=1
without loss of generality, and we consider
\rho\geq 0
to keep the notation simple, otherwise we should split it into positive and negative part.

First note that the function
\rho\ast F\in L^{2}_{\text{loc}}\left(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathbb{R}\right)
, hence for every
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\setminus N_{1}
, being
N_{1}
a
\mathrm{d}t-
null set, we have

\left(\left(\rho\ast F\right)\ast\mathrm{d}M^{c}\right)_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}\left(1_{\left\{\left(t-u\right)\in S\right\}}\int_{0}^{t-u}F\left(t-u-s\right)\rho\left(s\right)\mathrm{d}s\right)\mathrm{d}M^{c}_{u}\\
=\int_{0}^{t}\left(\int_{0}^{t}1_{\left\{\left(t-u\right)\in S\right\}}1_{\left\{s\leq t-u\right\}}F\left(t-u-s\right)\rho\left(s\right)\mathrm{d}s\right)\mathrm{d}M^{c}_{u},\quad\mathbb{P}-\text{a.s},

(24)

where
S\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
is such that
\int_{0}^{t}F\left(t-s\right)\rho\left(s\right)\mathrm{d}s,\,t\in S,
is well–defined. In particular,
\mathbb{R}_{+}\setminus S
is a
\mathrm{d}t-
null set.
Our goal is to apply the stochastic Fubini’s theorem (see, e.g.,
[
22
, Theorem
65
, Chapter IV]
), but before we can do that we need a preliminary step. For every
T>0
, a change of variables, sequential applications of Tonelli’s theorem and Young’s inequality yield (in the whole
\Omega
)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{T}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\left(\int_{0}^{t}1_{\left\{\left(t-u\right)\in S\right\}}1_{\left\{s\leq t-u\right\}}\left|F\left(t-s-u\right)\right|^{2}\rho\left(s\right)\mathrm{d}s\right)\left|X_{u}\right|\,\mathrm{d}u\right]\mathrm{d}t\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{T}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\left(\int_{0}^{t-s}\!\left|F\left(t-s-u\right)\right|^{2}\left|X_{u}\right|\,\mathrm{d}u\right)\rho\left(s\right)\mathrm{d}s\right]\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{T}\left[\int_{s}^{T}\left(\left|F\right|^{2}\ast\left|X\right|\right)\left(t-s\right)\mathrm{d}t\right]\rho\left(s\right)\mathrm{d}s\\
&=\int_{0}^{T}\left[\int_{0}^{T-s}\left(\left|F\right|^{2}\ast\left|X\right|\right)\left(t\right)\mathrm{d}t\right]\rho\left(s\right)\mathrm{d}s\leq\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{1}\left(\left[0,T\right]\right)}\left\lVert F\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}\left(\left[0,T\right]\right)}\left\lVert X\right\rVert_{L^{1}\left(\left[0,T\right]\right)}.\end{split}

Taking expectation and recalling (
19
) we have

\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\left(\int_{0}^{t}1_{\left\{\left(t-u\right)\in S\right\}}1_{\left\{s\leq t-u\right\}}\left|F\left(t-s-u\right)\right|^{2}\rho\left(s\right)\mathrm{d}s\right)\left|X_{u}\right|\mathrm{d}u\right]\mathrm{d}t\\
\leq\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{L^{1}\left(\left[0,T\right]\right)}\left\lVert F\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}\left(\left[0,T\right]\right)}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\lVert X\right\rVert_{L^{1}\left(\left[0,T\right]\right)}\right]<\infty.

This proves that there exists

such that

\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\left(\int_{0}^{t}1_{\left\{\left(t-u\right)\in S\right\}}1_{\left\{s\leq t-u\right\}}\left|F\left(t-s-u\right)\right|^{2}\rho\left(s\right)\mathrm{d}s\right)X_{u}\,\mathrm{d}u\right]<\infty,\quad t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\setminus N_{2}.

(25)

Taking

, thanks to (
25
) and Lemma
2
(see (
20
)) we can apply the stochastic Fubini’s theorem in (

\displaystyle\left(\left(\rho\ast F\right)\ast\mathrm{d}M^{c}\right)_{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\!\left(\int_{0}^{t}1_{\left\{\left(t-u\right)\in S\right\}}1_{\left\{s\leq t-u\right\}}F\left(t-u-s\right)\rho\left(s\right)\mathrm{d}s\right)\mathrm{d}M^{c}_{u}\!=\!\int_{0}^{t}\left(\int_{0}^{t-s}\!1_{\left\{\left(t-u\right)\in S\right\}}F\left(t-s-u\right)\mathrm{d}M^{c}_{u}\right)\!\rho\left(s\right)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\left(F\ast\mathrm{d}M^{c}\right)_{t-s}\rho\left(s\right)\mathrm{d}s=\left(\rho\ast\left(F\ast\mathrm{d}M^{c}\right)\right)\left(t\right),\quad\mathbb{P}-\text{a.s.},

and the proof is complete.
∎

Proposition 4

Assume that
m=d,
and that the kernel

admits a resolvent of the first kind
L
4
4

4

\left(F\ast\mathrm{d}Z\right)_{t}=\left(F\ast L\right)\left(0\right)\left(X-g_{0}\right)\left(t\right)+\left(\left(F\ast L\right)^{\prime}\ast\left(X-g_{0}\right)\right)\left(t\right),\quad\text{for a.e. }t\in\mathbb{R}_{+},\,\mathbb{P}-\text{a.s.}

By Lebesgue’s fundamental theorem of calculus we can write (denoting by

We first introduce some notation. For a

\displaystyle\left\langle\eta\left(x,\mathrm{d}\xi\right),g\left(\xi\right)\right\rangle

Condition 1

Lemma 9

The convolution equation (

\left(0,T\right),T>0

\left(\int_{0}^{T}\left|X_{t}\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}t\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq 2\bigg(2+\left\lVert g_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{2}\left(\left[0,T\right]\right)}+\left|b\right|\left\lVert K\ast X\right\rVert_{L^{2}\left(\left[0,T\right]\right)}\\
+\int_{0}^{T}\left|\left(K\ast\mathrm{d}M^{c}\right)_{t}\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\left|\left(K\ast\mathrm{d}M^{d}\right)_{t}\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}t\bigg),\quad\mathbb{P}-\text{a.s.,}

where we also use that

By
[

(i), Chapter

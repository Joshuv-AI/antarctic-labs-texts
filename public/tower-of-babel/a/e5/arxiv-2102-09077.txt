Abstract
. We prove that the number of irreducible ordinary characters in the principal p p -block of a finite group G G of order divisible by p p is always at least 2 ​ p − 1 2\sqrt{p-1} . This confirms a conjecture of Héthelyi and Külshammer [ HK00 ] for principal blocks and provides an affirmative answer to Brauer’s Problem 21 [ Bra63 ] for principal blocks of bounded defect. Our proof relies on recent works of Maróti [ Mar16 ] and Malle-Maróti [ MM16 ] on bounding the conjugacy class number and the number of p ′ p^{\prime} -degree irreducible characters of finite groups, earlier works of Broué-Malle-Michel [ BMM93 ] and Cabanes-Enguehard [ CE94 , CE04 ] on the distribution of characters into unipotent blocks and e e -Harish-Chandra series of finite reductive groups, and known cases of the Alperin-McKay conjecture.

1.
Introduction

Bounding the number
k(G)
of conjugacy classes of a finite group

G
in terms of a certain invariant associated to
G
is a
fundamental problem in group representation theory. Let
p
be a
prime dividing the order of
G
. As observed by Pyber, a result of
Brauer
[
Bra42
]
on groups
G
of order divisible by
p
but
not by
p^{2}
implies that
k(G)\geq 2\sqrt{p-1}
for those
groups, and the bound was later conjectured to be true
for all finite groups. After several earlier partial results

[
HK00
,
HK03
,
Mal06
,
Kel09
,
HHKM11
]
, the
conjecture was finally proved by Maróti
[
Mar16
]
.

An equally important problem in modular representation theory is to
bound the number
k(B)
of ordinary irreducible characters in a
block. It is not surprising that this problem is closely related to
the previous one on bounding
k(G)
; for instance, the
p
-solvable
case of the celebrated Brauer’s
k(B)
-conjecture
[
Bra63
, Problem
20]
, which asserts that
k(B)
is bounded
above
by
the order of a defect group for
B
, was known to be equivalent to
the coprime
k(GV)
-problem, which in turn was eventually solved in
2004
[
GMRS04
,
Sch07
]
. While there have been a number of
results on upper bounds for
k(B)

[
BF59
,
Rob04
,
Sam17
,
Mal17
]
, not much has been
done on lower bounds.

In the proof of
k(G)\geq 2\sqrt{p-1}
for solvable groups,
Héthelyi and Külshammer

[
HK00
, Remark (ii)]
speculated that
“
perhaps it is even true that
k(B)\geq 2\sqrt{p-1}
for
every
p
-block
B
of positive defect, where
k(B)
denotes the
number of irreducible ordinary characters in
B
”. Of course,
Héthelyi and Külshammer were aware of blocks of defect zero,
which have a unique irreducible ordinary character (whose degree has
the same
p
-part as the order of the group) and a unique
irreducible Brauer character as well, see
[
Nav98
, Theorem
3.18]
.

The main aim of this paper is to confirm
Héthelyi-Külshammer’s conjecture for principal blocks.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
p
a prime such that
p\mid|G|
. Let

B_{0}(G)
denote the principal
p
-block of
G
. Then
k(B_{0}(G))\geq 2\sqrt{p-1}
.

Problem 21 in Brauer’s famous list
[
Bra63
]
asks whether
there exists a function
f(q)
on prime powers
q
such that

f(q)\rightarrow\infty
for
q\rightarrow\infty
and that

k(B)\geq f(p^{d(B)})
for every
p
-block
B
of defect
d(B)>0
.
Our Theorem
1.1
provides an affirmative answer to
this question for principal blocks of
bounded
defect.

Building upon the ideas in
[
Mar16
]
and the subsequent paper

[
MM16
]
of Malle and Maróti on bounding the number
of
p^{\prime}
-degree irreducible characters in a finite group, we observe
that Héthelyi-Külshammer’s conjecture for principal blocks
is essentially a problem on bounding the number of irreducible
ordinary characters in principal blocks of almost simple groups, as
well as bounding the number of orbits of irreducible characters in
principal blocks of simple groups under the action of their
automorphism groups.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
S
be a non-abelian simple group and
p
a
prime such that
p\mid|S|
. Let
G
be an almost simple group with
socle
S
such that
p\nmid|G/S|
. Then

(i)

k(B_{0}(G))\geq 2\sqrt{p-1}
. Moreover,
k(B_{0}(G))>2\sqrt{p-1}
if
S
does not have cyclic Sylow
p
-subgroups.

(ii)

Assume furthermore that
p\geq 11
and
S
does not have cyclic Sylow
p
-subgroups. Then
the number of
{\mathrm{Aut}}(S)
-orbits on
{\mathrm{Irr}}(B_{0}(S))
is at least

2(p-1)^{1/4}
.

As we will explain in the next section, Theorem
1.1

is a consequence of
[
Mar16
]
and the well-known Alperin-McKay
conjecture, which asserts that the number of irreducible characters
of height 0 in a block
B
of a finite group
G
coincides with the
number of irreducible characters of height 0 in the Brauer
correspondent of
B
of the normalizer of a defect subgroup for
B

in
G
. We take advantage of the recent advances on the conjecture
in the proof of our results, particularly the fact that Späth’s
inductive Alperin-McKay conditions hold for all
p
-blocks with
cyclic defect groups
[
Spa13
,
KSp16
]
. This explains why simple
groups with cyclic Sylow
p
-subgroups are excluded in
Theorem
1.2
(ii). Additionally, we take
advantage of recent results on the possible structure of defect
groups of principal blocks with few ordinary characters

[
KS20
,
RSV21
]
, and this explains why the smaller values
of
p
are excluded in Theorem
1.2
(ii).

Theorem
1.2
turns out to be straightforward for
alternating groups or groups of Lie type in characteristic
p
, but
highly nontrivial for groups of Lie type in characteristic not equal
to
p
. We make use of Cabanes-Enguehard’s results on unipotent
blocks
[
CE04
]
to prove that the so-called semisimple
characters of
S
all fall into the principal
p
-block
B_{0}(S)
in
a certain nice situation. This and results of Broué-Malle-Michel

[
BMM93
]
and Cabanes-Enguehard

[
CE94
]
on the compatibility between the
distributions of unipotent characters into unipotent blocks and

e
-Harish-Chandra series allow us to obtain a general bound for the
number of
{\mathrm{Aut}}(S)
-orbits of characters in
{\mathrm{Irr}}(B_{0}(S))
in terms
of certain data associated to
S
, for
S
a simple group of
Lie type, see Theorem
8.3
. We hope this result will
be useful in other purposes.

The next result classifies groups for which
k(B_{0}(G))
is minimal
in the sense of Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
p
a prime. Let
P
be a Sylow

p
-subgroup of
G
and
B_{0}
denote the principal
p
-block of
G
.
Then
k(B_{0})=2\sqrt{p-1}
if and only if
\sqrt{p-1}\in{\mathbb{N}}
and

{\mathbf{N}}_{G}(P)/{\mathbf{O}}_{p^{\prime}}({\mathbf{N}}_{G}(P))
is isomorphic to the Frobenius group

C_{p}\rtimes C_{\sqrt{p-1}}
.

We remark that, in the situation of Theorem
1.3
,
the number of
p^{\prime}
-degree irreducible characters in
B_{0}(G)
is also
equal to
2\sqrt{p-1}
. In general, if a
p
-block
B
of a finite
group has an abelian defect group, then every ordinary irreducible
character of
B
has height zero. This is the ‘if direction’ of
Brauer’s height-zero conjecture, which is now known to be true,
thanks to the work of Kessar and Malle
[
KM13
]
.
Theorem
1.1
therefore implies that if
P\in{\mathrm{Syl}}_{p}(G)

is abelian and nontrivial then
k_{0}(B_{0}(G))\geq 2\sqrt{p-1}
, where

k_{0}(B)
denotes the number of height zero ordinary irreducible
characters of a block
B
.

Theorems
1.1
and
1.3
are useful in
the study of principal blocks with few height zero ordinary
irreducible characters. In fact, using them, we are able to show in
a forthcoming paper
[
HSF21
]
that
k_{0}(B_{0}(G))=3
if and
only if
P\cong C_{3}
, and that
k_{0}(B_{0}(G))=4
if and only if

|P/P^{\prime}|=4
or
P\cong C_{5}
and
{\mathbf{N}}_{G}(P)/{\mathbf{O}}_{p^{\prime}}({\mathbf{N}}_{G}(P))
is
isomorphic to the dihedral group
D_{10}
. These results have been
known only in the case
p\leq 3
, see
[
NST18
, Theorems A and
C]
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
,
we recall some known results on the Alperin-Mckay conjecture and
prove that our results follow when all the non-abelian composition
factors of
G
have cyclic Sylow
p
-subgroups. We also prove
Theorem
1.2
for the sporadic simple groups and
groups of Lie type defined in characteristic
p
in
Section
2
. The alternating groups are treated in
Section
3
. Section
4
takes care of
the case when the Sylow
p
-subgroups of
S
are non-abelian.
Sections
6
,
7
, and

9
are devoted to proving
Theorem
1.2
for simple groups of Lie type defined in characteristics different from
p
. To do
so, in Section
5
, we provide some background on
semisimple characters of finite reductive groups and prove that
those associated to conjugacy classes of
p
-elements belong to the
principal
p
-block in a certain situation, and in
Section
8
we obtain a bound for the number of

{\mathrm{Aut}}(S)
-orbits of characters in
{\mathrm{Irr}}(B_{0}(S))
. Finally, we finish
the proofs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.3

in Section
10
.

2.
Some first observations

In this section we make some observations toward the proofs of the
main results.

2.1.
The Alperin-McKay conjecture

The well-known Alperin-McKay (AM) conjecture predicts that the
number of irreducible characters of height zero in a block
B
of a
finite group
G
coincides with the number of irreducible characters
of height zero in the Brauer correspondent of
B
of the normalizer of
a defect subgroup of
B
in
G
. For the principal blocks, the
conjecture is equivalent to

k_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(G))=k_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}({\mathbf{N}}_{G}(P))),

where
P
is a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
and
k_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}(G))

denotes the number of
p^{\prime}
-degree irreducible ordinary characters in

B_{0}(G)
.

On the other hand, if
p\mid|G|
, we have

\displaystyle k_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}({\mathbf{N}}_{G}(P)))

\displaystyle\geq k_{p^{\prime}}(B_{0}({\mathbf{N}}_{G}(P)/P^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=k\left(B_{0}({\mathbf{N}}_{G}(P)/P^{\prime})\right)

\displaystyle=k\left(B_{0}\left(({\mathbf{N}}_{G}(P)/P^{\prime})/\mathbf{O}_{p^{\prime}}({\mathbf{N}}_{G}(P)/P^{\prime})\right)\right)

\displaystyle=k\left(({\mathbf{N}}_{G}(P)/P^{\prime})/\mathbf{O}_{p^{\prime}}({\mathbf{N}}_{G}(P)/P^{\prime})\right)

\displaystyle\geq 2\sqrt{p-1},

where the first inequality follows from
[
Nav98
, p. 137]
, the
first equality follows from the fact that every irreducible ordinary
character of
{\mathbf{N}}_{G}(P)/P^{\prime}
has
p^{\prime}
-degree, the last two equalities
follow from
[
Nav98
, Theorem 9.9]
and Fong’s theorem (see

[
Nav98
, Theorem 10.20]
), and the last inequality follows from

[
Mar16
]
. Therefore, if the AM conjecture holds for
G
and

p
, then the number of
p^{\prime}
-degree irreducible ordinary characters
in
B_{0}(G)
is bounded by
2\sqrt{p-1}
.

From this, we see that Theorems
1.1
and

1.2
(i) hold if the AM conjecture holds for

(G,p)
. We now prove that the same is true for
Theorem
1.3
. Note that the “if” implication of this
theorem is clear. Assume that the AM conjecture holds for
B_{0}(G)

and
k(B_{0}(G))=2\sqrt{p-1}
for some prime
p
such that

\sqrt{p-1}\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Then, as seen above, we have

2\sqrt{p-1}=k(B_{0}(G))\geq k(({\mathbf{N}}_{G}(P)/P^{\prime})/\mathbf{O}_{p^{\prime}}({\mathbf{N}}_{G}(P)/P^{\prime}))\geq 2\sqrt{p-1},

implying

k(({\mathbf{N}}_{G}(P)/P^{\prime})/\mathbf{O}_{p^{\prime}}({\mathbf{N}}_{G}(P)/P^{\prime}))=2\sqrt{p-1},

and thus
({\mathbf{N}}_{G}(P)/P^{\prime})/\mathbf{O}_{p^{\prime}}({\mathbf{N}}_{G}(P)/P^{\prime})
is isomorphic to
the Frobenius group
C_{p}\rtimes C_{\sqrt{p-1}}
, by
[
Mar16
, Theorem
1]
. In particular,
P/P^{\prime}\cong C_{p}
, implying that
P\cong C_{p}
, and hence it follows that
{\mathbf{N}}_{G}(P)/{\mathbf{O}}_{p^{\prime}}({\mathbf{N}}_{G}(P))
is
isomorphic to the Frobenius group
C_{p}\rtimes C_{\sqrt{p-1}}
, as
wanted.

The AM conjecture is known to be true when
G
has a cyclic Sylow

p
-subgroup by Dade’s theory
[
Dad66
]
. In fact, by

[
Spa13
,
KSp16
]
, the so-called
inductive Alperin-McKay
conditions
are satisfied for all blocks with cyclic defect groups.
Therefore, we have:

Lemma 2.1

(Koshitani-Späth)
.

Let
p
be a prime. Assume that all the non-abelian composition
factors of a finite group
G
have cyclic Sylow
p
-subgroups. Then
the Alperin-Mckay conjecture holds for
G
and
p
, and thus
Theorems
1.1
,
1.2
(i), and

1.3
hold for
G
and
p
.

Note that the linear groups
{\mathrm{PSL}}_{2}(q)
, the Suzuki groups
\hskip 1.42262pt{}^{2}\hskip-0.56905ptB_{2}(2^{2f+1})
and the Ree groups
\hskip 1.42262pt{}^{2}\hskip-0.56905ptG_{2}(3^{2f+1})
all have
cyclic Sylow
p
-subgroups for
p
different from the defining
characteristic of the group. So Theorem
1.2

automatically follows from Lemma
2.1
for these groups in
characteristic not equal to
p
.

2.2.
Small blocks

Blocks with a small number of ordinary characters have
been studied significantly in the literature. In particular, the
possible structure of defect groups of principal blocks with at most
5 ordinary irreducible characters are now known, see

[
Bra82
,
Bel90
,
KS20
,
RSV21
]
. Using these results,
we can easily confirm our results for
p\leq 7
. For instance, to
prove Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
for

p=7
it is enough to assume that
k(B_{0}(G))\leq 4
, but by going
through the list of possible defect groups of
B_{0}(G)
, we then have

{\mathrm{Syl}}_{p}(G)\in\{1,C_{2},C_{3},C_{2}\times C_{2},C_{4},C_{5}\}
, which cannot
happen. To prove Theorem
1.3
for
p<7
we
note that if
p=5
and
k(B_{0}(G))=4
then
P=C_{5}
; and if
p=2
and

k(B_{0}(G))=2
then
P=C_{2}
, both of which cases
P
is cyclic, and
thus the result of Subsection
2.1
applies.

Therefore we will assume from now on that
p\geq 11
, unless stated
otherwise.

2.3.
Sporadic and the Tits groups

We remark that Theorem

1.2
can be confirmed directly using

[
Atl85
,
Atl95
]
or
[
GAP
]
for sporadic simple groups and
the Tits group. Therefore, we are left with the alternating groups
and groups of Lie type, which will be treated in the subsequent
sections.

2.4.
Groups of Lie type in characteristic
p

Let
S
be a
simple group of Lie type defined over the field of
q=p^{f}
elements.
According to results of Dagger and Humphreys on defect groups of
finite reductive groups in defining characteristic, see

[
Cab18
, Proposition 1.18 and Theorem 3.3]
for instance,
S

has only two
p
-blocks. Namely, the only non-principal block is a defect-zero block containing only the Steinberg character of
S
. Therefore,

k(B_{0}(S))=k(S)-1.

Let
{\mathbf{G}}
be a simple algebraic group of simply connected type and let
F
be a Steinberg endomorphism on
{\mathbf{G}}
such that
S=X/{\mathbf{Z}}(X)
, where
X={\mathbf{G}}^{F}
. Assume that the rank of
{\mathbf{G}}
is
r
. By a result of
Steinberg (see
[
FG12
, Theorem 3.1]
),
X
has at
least
q^{r}
semisimple conjugacy classes, and thus
k(X)>q^{r}
. It
follows that

k(B_{0}(S))\geq\left[\frac{q^{r}}{|{\mathbf{Z}}(X)|}-1\right],

which yields
k(B_{0}(S))\geq q^{r}/|{\mathbf{Z}}(X)|
. Using the values of

|{\mathbf{Z}}(X)|
and
|{\mathrm{Out}}(S)|
available in
[
Atl85
, p. xvi]
, it is
straightforward to check that
q^{r}/|{\mathbf{Z}}(X)|\geq 2\sqrt{p-1}|{\mathrm{Out}}(S)|
, proving Theorem
1.2
for the
relevant
S
and
p
.

3.
Alternating groups

In this section we prove Theorem
1.2
for the
alternating groups. The background on block theory of symmetric and
alternating groups can be found in
[
Ols93
]
for instance.

The ordinary irreducible characters of
\textup{{S}}_{n}
are naturally labeled
by partitions of
n
. Two characters are in the same
p
-block if
and only if their corresponding partitions have the same
p
-cores,
which are obtained from the partitions by successive removals of rim

p
-hooks until no
p
-hook is left. Therefore,
p
-blocks of

\textup{{S}}_{n}
are in one-to-one correspondence with
p
-cores of
partitions of
n
.

Let
B
be a
p
-block of
\textup{{S}}_{n}
. The number
k(B)
of ordinary
irreducible characters in
B
turns out to depend only on
p

and the so-called
weight
of
B
, which is defined to be

w(B):=(n-|\mu|)/p
, where
\mu
is the
p
-core corresponding to

B
under the aforementioned correspondence. In fact,

k(B)=k(p,w(B)):=\Sigma_{(w_{0},w_{1}...,w_{p-1})}\pi(w_{0})\pi(w_{1})\cdots\pi(w_{p-1}),

where
(w_{0},w_{1}...,w_{p-1})
runs through all
p
-tuples of
non-negative integers such that
w(B)=\Sigma_{i=0}^{p-1}w_{i}
and

\pi(x)
is the number of partitions of
x
, see
[
Ols93
, Proposition
11.4]
. Note that
k(p,w(B))
is precisely the number of

p
-tuples of partitions of
w(B)
.

For the principal block
B_{0}(\textup{{S}}_{n})
of
\textup{{S}}_{n}
, we have

w(B_{0}(\textup{{S}}_{n}))=[n/p]
, which is at least 1 by the assumption
p\mid|S|
. It follows that

k(B_{0}(\textup{{S}}_{n}))\geq k(p,1)=p\geq 2\sqrt{p-1}.

Moreover, according to
[
Ols92
, Proposition 2.8]
, when
p
is
odd and
\widetilde{B}
is a block of
\textup{{A}}_{n}
covered by
B
, then

B
and
\widetilde{B}
have the same number of irreducible ordinary
characters (and indeed the same number of irreducible Brauer
characters as well). In particular, when p is odd, we have

k(B_{0}(\textup{{A}}_{n}))=k(B_{0}(\textup{{S}}_{n}))\geq 2\sqrt{p-1}
, which proves
Theorem
1.2
(i) for the alternating groups.

For part (ii) of Theorem
1.2
, recall that
p\geq 11
, and thus
n\geq 11
and
{\mathrm{Aut}}(S)=\textup{{S}}_{n}
. The
number of
\textup{{S}}_{n}
-orbits on
{\mathrm{Irr}}(B_{0}(\textup{{A}}_{n}))
is at least

k(B_{0}(\textup{{A}}_{n}))/2
, which in turn is at least

1+(p-1)/2=(p+1)/2>2(p-1)^{1/4}
, and this proves
Theorem
1.2
(ii) for the alternating groups.

4.
Groups of Lie type: the non-abelian
p
-Sylow case

In this section, we let
{\mathbf{G}}
be a simple
algebraic group of adjoint type and
F
a Steinberg endomorphism on

{\mathbf{G}}
such that
S\cong[{\mathbb{G}},{\mathbb{G}}]
where
{\mathbb{G}}:={\mathbf{G}}^{F}
. Let

q=\ell^{f}
with
\ell\neq p
be the absolute value of all
eigenvalues of
F
on the character group of an
F
-stable maximal
torus of
G
. Recall that we are assuming
p\geq 11
.

In this section we prove Theorem
1.2
for those
S

of Lie type in characteristic different from
p
such that the Sylow

p
-subgroups of
{\mathbb{G}}
are non-abelian.

In that case, there are then more than one
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that

p\mid\Phi_{d}(q)
with
\Phi_{d}
dividing the order polynomial of

({\mathbf{G}},F)
. Here, as usual,
\Phi_{d}
denotes the
d
th cyclotomic
polynomial. (In fact, if there a unique such
d
, then a Sylow

p
-subgroup of
{\mathbb{G}}
is contained in a Sylow
d
-torus of
{\mathbb{G}}
,
and hence is abelian, see
[
MT11
, Theorem 25.14]
.)

Let
e_{p}(q)
denote the multiplicative order of
q
modulo
p
. Note
that, by
[
MT11
, Lemma 25.13]
,
p\mid\Phi_{d}(q)
if
and only if
d=e_{p}(q)p^{i}
for some
i\geq 0
. Therefore, as there is
more than one
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
p\mid\Phi_{d}(q)
, we must have

p\mid d
for some
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
\Phi_{d}
divides the order
polynomial of
({\mathbf{G}},F)
. The fact that
p\geq 11
then rules out the
cases when
{\mathbf{G}}
is of exceptional type and thus we are left with
only the classical types. That is,
{\mathbb{G}}={\mathrm{PGL}}_{n}(q)
,
{\mathrm{PGU}}_{n}(q)
,

{\mathrm{SO}}_{2n+1}(q)
,
{\mathrm{PCSp}}_{2n}(q)
, or

\mathrm{P}(\mathrm{CO}^{\pm}_{2n}(q))^{0}
.

For
{\mathbb{G}}={\mathrm{PGL}}_{n}(q)
or
{\mathrm{PGU}}_{n}(q)
, we define
e
to be the smallest
positive integer such that
p\mid(q^{e}-(\epsilon)^{e})
(
\epsilon=1

for linear groups and
\epsilon=-1
for unitary groups), so that

e=e_{p}(q)
when
{\mathbb{G}}={\mathrm{PGL}}_{n}(q)
or
{\mathbb{G}}={\mathrm{PGU}}_{n}(q)
and
4\mid e_{p}(q)
,
e=e_{p}(q)/2
when
{\mathbb{G}}={\mathrm{PGU}}_{n}(q)
and
2\mid e_{p}(q)
but

4\nmid e_{p}(q)
, and
e=2e_{p}(q)
when
{\mathbb{G}}={\mathrm{PGU}}_{n}(q)
and
2\nmid e_{p}(q)
. For
{\mathbb{G}}={\mathrm{SO}}_{2n+1}(q)
,
{\mathrm{PCSp}}_{2n}(q)
, or

\mathrm{P}(\mathrm{CO}^{\pm}_{2n}(q))^{0}
, we define
e
to be the
smallest positive integer such that
p\mid(q^{e}\pm 1)
, so that

e=e_{p}(q)
when
e_{p}(q)
is odd and
e=e_{p}(q)/2
when
e_{p}(q)
is
even.

Let
n=we+m
where
0\leq m<e
. We claim that
p\leq w
. To see
this, first assume that
{\mathbb{G}}={\mathrm{PGL}}_{n}(q)
. Then, as mentioned above,

ep\leq n
, which implies that
ep<(w+1)e
, and thus
p\leq w
.
Next, assume that
{\mathbb{G}}={\mathrm{SO}}_{2n+1}(q)
,
{\mathrm{PCSp}}_{2n}(q)
, or

\mathrm{P}(\mathrm{CO}^{\pm}_{2n}(q))^{0}
. If
e=e_{p}(q)
is odd,
then since
p\mid(q^{e}-1)
and
\gcd(q^{e}-1,q^{i}+1)\leq 2
for every

i\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we have
p\mid(q^{j}-1)
for some
e<j\leq n
, and it
follows that
ep\leq n
, implying
p\leq w
. On the other hand, if

2e=e_{p}(q)
is even then
2ep=e_{p}(q)p\leq 2n<2(w+1)e
, which also
implies that
p\leq w
. Finally, assume
{\mathbb{G}}={\mathrm{PGU}}_{n}(q)
. The case

4\mid e_{p}(q)
is argued as in the case
S={\mathrm{PGL}}_{n}(q)
; the case

2\mid e_{p}(q)
but
4\nmid e_{p}(q)
is argued as in the case

S={\mathrm{SO}}_{2n+1}(q)
and
2\mid e_{p}(q)
. For the last case
2\nmid e_{p}(q)
, we have
ep/2=e_{p}(q)p
, and in order for
\Phi_{e_{p}(q)p}

dividing the generic order of
|{\mathrm{PGU}}_{n}(q)|
,
e_{p}(q)p\leq n/2
, and
hence it follows that
ep\leq n
, which also implies that
p\leq w
.
The claim is fully proved.

By
[
BMM93
, Theorem 3.2]
and
[
CE94
, Main
Theorem]
, the number of unipotent characters of

{\mathbb{G}}
in the principal block
B_{0}({\mathbb{G}})
is equal to
k(W_{e})
- the
number of irreducible complex characters of the relative Weyl group

W_{e}
of a Sylow
e_{p}(q)
-torus of
{\mathbb{G}}
. This
W_{e}
is the wreath
product
C_{e}\wr\textup{{S}}_{w}
when
{\mathbf{G}}
is of type
A
and is a subgroup
of index 1 or 2 of
C_{2e}\wr\textup{{S}}_{w}
when
{\mathbf{G}}
is of type
B
,
C
,
or
D
, see
[
MM16
, Proposition 5.5 and its proof]
. In
any case we have that the number of unipotent characters in

{\mathrm{Irr}}(B_{0}({\mathbb{G}}))
is at least
k(\textup{{S}}_{w})/2=\pi(w)/2
, which in turns
is at least
\pi(p)/2
as
p\leq w
. Since every unipotent character
of
{\mathbb{G}}
restricts irreducibly to
S
, it follows that the number of
unipotent characters in
{\mathrm{Irr}}(B_{0}(S))
is at least
\pi(p)/2
.

By a result of Lusztig (see
[
Mal08
, Theorem 2.5]
), every
unipotent character of a simple group of Lie type lies in a

{\mathrm{Aut}}(S)
-orbit of length at most 3. (In fact, every
{\mathrm{Aut}}(S)
-orbit
on unipotent characters of
S
has length 1 or 2, except when

S=P\Omega_{8}^{+}(q)
whose the graph automorphism of order 3 produces
two orbits of length 3.) Therefore, together with the conclusion of
the previous paragraph, we deduce that the number of

{\mathrm{Aut}}(S)
-orbits on
{\mathrm{Irr}}(B_{0}(S))
is at least
\pi(p)/6
. This
bound is greater than
2\sqrt{p-1}
when
p\geq 11
, as required.

5.
Semisimple characters and principal
blocks

Before continuing with our proof of Theorem
1.2

for groups of Lie type, we recall some background on certain
characters known as
semisimple characters
and show how they
fall into the principal block in a certain situation. Background on
character theory of finite reductive groups can be found in

[
Car85
,
CE04
,
DM91
]
. Let
{\mathbf{G}}
be a
connected reductive group defined over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
and
F
an associated
Frobenius endomorphism on
{\mathbf{G}}
. Let
{\mathbf{G}}^{\ast}
be an algebraic
group with a Frobenius endomorphism which, for simplicity, we denote
by the same
F
, such that
({\mathbf{G}},F)
is in duality to

({\mathbf{G}}^{\ast},F)
.

Let
t
be a semisimple element of
({\mathbf{G}}^{\ast})^{F}
. The rational
Lusztig series
\mathcal{E}({\mathbf{G}}^{F},(t))
associated to the

({\mathbf{G}}^{\ast})^{F}
-conjugacy class
(t)
of
t
is defined to be the set
of irreducible characters of
{\mathbf{G}}^{F}
occurring in some
Deligne-Lusztig character
R_{{\mathbf{T}}}^{{\mathbf{G}}}\theta
, where
{\mathbf{T}}
is an

F
-stable maximal torus of
{\mathbf{G}}
and
\theta\in{\mathrm{Irr}}({\mathbf{T}}^{F})
such
that
({\mathbf{T}},\theta)
corresponds in duality to a pair
({\mathbf{T}}^{\ast},s)

with
s\in{\mathbf{T}}^{\ast}\cap(t)
. Here we recall that there is a
one-to-one duality correspondence between
{\mathbf{G}}^{F}
-conjugacy classes
of pairs
({\mathbf{T}},\theta)
, where
{\mathbf{T}}
is an
F
-stable maximal torus
of
{\mathbf{G}}
and
\theta\in{\mathrm{Irr}}({\mathbf{T}}^{F})
, and the

({\mathbf{G}}^{\ast})^{F}
-conjugacy classes of pairs
({\mathbf{T}}^{\ast},s)
, where

{\mathbf{T}}^{\ast}
is dual to
{\mathbf{T}}
and
s\in({\mathbf{T}}^{\ast})^{F}
.

We continue to let
t
be a semisimple element of
({\mathbf{G}}^{\ast})^{F}
and
assume furthermore that
{\mathbf{C}}_{{\mathbf{G}}^{\ast}}(t)
is a Levi subgroup of

{\mathbf{G}}^{\ast}
. Let
{\mathbf{G}}(t)
be a Levi subgroup of
{\mathbf{G}}
in duality with

{\mathbf{C}}_{{\mathbf{G}}^{\ast}}(t)
and
\mathbf{P}
be a parabolic subgroup of

{\mathbf{G}}
for which
{\mathbf{G}}(t)
is the Levi complement. The twisted
induction
R_{{\mathbf{G}}(t)\subseteq P}^{\mathbf{G}}
and the multiplication by

\widehat{t}
, a certain linear character of
{\mathrm{Irr}}({\mathbf{G}}(t)^{F})

naturally defined by
t
(see
[
CE04
, (8.19)]
), then
induce a bijection between the Lusztig series

\mathcal{E}({\mathbf{G}}(t)^{F},1)
and
\mathcal{E}({\mathbf{G}}^{F},(t))
, see

[
CE04
, Proposition 8.26 and Theorem 8.27]
or

[
DM91
, Theorem 13.25 and Proposition 13.30]
. In fact,
for each
\lambda\in\mathcal{E}({\mathbf{G}}(t)^{F},1)
, one has

\varepsilon_{{\mathbf{G}}}\varepsilon_{{\mathbf{G}}(t)}R_{{\mathbf{G}}(t)\subseteq P}^{\mathbf{G}}(\widehat{t}\lambda)\in\mathcal{E}({\mathbf{G}}^{F},(t)),

where

\varepsilon_{{\mathbf{G}}}:=(-1)^{\sigma({\mathbf{G}})}
with
\sigma({\mathbf{G}})
the

\mathbb{F}_{q}
-rank of
{\mathbf{G}}
. Taking
\lambda
to be trivial, we
have the character

\chi_{(t)}:=\varepsilon_{{\mathbf{G}}}\varepsilon_{{\mathbf{G}}(t)}R_{{\mathbf{G}}(t)\subseteq P}^{\mathbf{G}}(\widehat{t}\mathbf{1}_{{\mathbf{G}}(t)^{F}})\in\mathcal{E}({\mathbf{G}}^{F},(t)),

which is often referred to as a semisimple character of
{\mathbf{G}}^{F}
, of
degree

\chi_{(t)}(1)=|({\mathbf{G}}^{\ast})^{F}:{\mathbf{C}}_{{{\mathbf{G}}^{\ast}}^{F}}(t)|_{\ell^{\prime}},

where

\ell
is the defining characteristic of
{\mathbf{G}}
, see
[
DM91
, Theorem
13.23]
.

By
[
CE04
, Theorem 9.12]
, every element of
B_{0}({\mathbf{G}}^{F})

lies in a Lusztig series
\mathcal{E}({\mathbf{G}}^{F},(t))
where
t
is a

p
-element of
{{\mathbf{G}}^{\ast}}^{F}
. Hence one might ask which such
t

indeed produce semisimple characters that contribute to the
principal block. We will see in the following theorem that in a
certain nice situation which is indeed enough for our purpose, the
centralizer
{\mathbf{C}}_{{\mathbf{G}}^{\ast}}(t)
is a Levi subgroup of
{\mathbf{G}}^{\ast}
,
and thus the semisimple character
\chi_{(t)}
associated to
(t)

is well-defined and belongs to
B_{0}({\mathbf{G}}^{F})
.

In the following, we recall that a prime
p
is good for
{\mathbf{G}}
if it
does not divide the coefficients of the highest root of the root
system associated to
{\mathbf{G}}
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
({\mathbf{G}},F)
be a connected reductive group defined
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. Let
p
be a good prime for
{\mathbf{G}}
and not dividing

q
. Let
t
be a
p
-element of
{{\mathbf{G}}^{\ast}}^{F}
. If

{\mathbf{C}}_{{\mathbf{G}}^{\ast}}(t)
is connected, then the semisimple character

\chi_{(t)}\in{\mathrm{Irr}}({\mathbf{G}}^{F})
belongs to the principal
p
-block of

{\mathbf{G}}^{F}
. In particular, if
{\mathbf{Z}}({\mathbf{G}})
is connected, the character

\chi_{(t)}
belongs to the principal block of
{\mathbf{G}}^{F}
for every

p
-element
t\in{{\mathbf{G}}^{\ast}}^{F}
.

Proof.

Assume that
{\mathbf{C}}_{{\mathbf{G}}^{\ast}}(t)
is connected. Since
p
is good for

{\mathbf{G}}
,
{\mathbf{C}}_{{\mathbf{G}}^{\ast}}(t)
is then a Levi subgroup of
{\mathbf{G}}^{\ast}
,
see
[
CE04
, Proposition 13.16]
. Define a Levi subgroup

{\mathbf{G}}(t)
and parabolic subgroup
\mathbf{P}
of
{\mathbf{G}}
as above.
Since

\chi_{(t)}=\varepsilon_{{\mathbf{G}}}\varepsilon_{{\mathbf{G}}(t)}R_{{\mathbf{G}}(t)\subseteq\mathbf{P}}^{\mathbf{G}}\left(\widehat{t}\mathbf{1}_{{\mathbf{G}}(t)^{F}}\right)
,

[
CE04
, Theorem 21.13]
implies that all the irreducible
constituents of
R_{{\mathbf{G}}(t)\subseteq\mathbf{P}}^{\mathbf{G}}(\mathbf{1}_{{\mathbf{G}}(t)^{F}})
are unipotent characters of

{\mathbf{G}}^{F}
which are in the same
p
-block as
\chi_{(t)}
. As the
trivial character is obviously a constituent of
R_{{\mathbf{G}}(t)\subseteq\mathbf{P}}^{\mathbf{G}}(\mathbf{1}_{{\mathbf{G}}(t)^{F}})
, we deduce that

\chi_{(t)}\in B_{0}({\mathbf{G}}^{F})
.

The second statement of the theorem immediately follows from the
first, as if
{\mathbf{Z}}({\mathbf{G}})
is connected then the centralizer of every
semisimple element of
{\mathbf{G}}^{\ast}
is connected, see
[
DM91
, Lemma
13.14]
.
∎

6.
Linear and Unitary Groups

In this section, we let
S={\mathrm{PSL}}_{n}^{\epsilon}(q)
, where
p\nmid q
and

\epsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
. Here
{\mathrm{PSL}}_{n}^{\epsilon}(q):={\mathrm{PSL}}_{n}(q)
in the
case
\epsilon=1
and
{\mathrm{PSU}}_{n}(q)
in the case
\epsilon=-1
, and
analogous for
{\mathrm{SL}}_{n}^{\epsilon}(q)
,
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}^{\epsilon}(q)
, and

{\mathrm{PGL}}_{n}^{\epsilon}(q)
. We further let
\overline{q}:=q
if

\epsilon=1
and
\overline{q}:=q^{2}
if
\epsilon=-1
. Note that
with our notation,
{\mathrm{SL}}^{\epsilon}_{n}(q)
and
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}^{\epsilon}(q)
are
naturally subgroups of
{\mathrm{SL}}_{n}(\overline{q})
and

{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(\overline{q})
, respectively.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
S={\mathrm{PSL}}^{\epsilon}_{n}(q)
and let
p\nmid q
be a prime. Then Theorem
1.2
holds for any almost simple group
A
with socle
S
and
p\nmid|A/S|
.

Proof.

With the results of the previous sections, we may assume
n\geq 3
,

p\geq 11
, and
q
is a power of some prime different from
p
.

Write
S={\mathrm{PSL}}^{\epsilon}_{n}(q)
,
{\mathbb{G}}={\mathrm{PGL}}^{\epsilon}_{n}(q)
,

\widetilde{G}={\mathrm{GL}}^{\epsilon}_{n}(q)
, and
G={\mathrm{SL}}^{\epsilon}_{n}(q)
. Then we have

G=[\widetilde{G},\widetilde{G}]
,
S=G/{\mathbf{Z}}(G)
, and
{\mathbb{G}}=\widetilde{G}/{\mathbf{Z}}(\widetilde{G})
.
From Section
4
, we may assume that Sylow

p
-subgroups of
{\mathbb{G}}
are abelian, which implies that there is a
unique
e
such that
p\mid\Phi_{e}({q})
and
\Phi_{e}
divides the
generic order polynomial of
{\mathbb{G}}
. Here
e
must be
e_{p}({q})
, the
multiplicative order of
{q}
modulo
p
. Note that this also
forces
p\nmid n
by again appealing to
[
MT11
, Lemma
25.13]
.

We will further define
\overline{e}:=e_{p}(\overline{q})
and
e^{\prime}
as
follows:

e^{\prime}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\overline{e}&\hbox{ if $\epsilon=1$ or if $\epsilon=-1$ and $p\mid q^{\overline{e}}-(-1)^{\overline{e}}$}\\
2\overline{e}&\hbox{ if $\epsilon=-1$ and $p\mid q^{\overline{e}}+(-1)^{\overline{e}}$}.\\
\end{array}\right.

To prove Theorem
1.2
, our aim is to show that when
a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
S
is not cyclic, then the number of

{\mathrm{Aut}}(S)
-orbits on
{\mathrm{Irr}}(B_{0}(S))
is larger than
2\sqrt{p-1}
.

Note that since
p\nmid\gcd(n,q-\epsilon)=|{\mathbf{Z}}(G)|
, the
irreducible characters in the principal block of
S
are the same as
that of
G
, under inflation (see
[
Nav98
, Theorem 9.9]
).
Similarly, if
e^{\prime}>1
, then
p\nmid(q-\epsilon)=|{\mathbf{Z}}(\widetilde{G})|
and
an analogous statement holds for
{\mathbb{G}}
and
\widetilde{G}
. Hence, we begin
by studying
B_{0}(\widetilde{G})
, which will be sufficient for our purposes
in the case
e^{\prime}>1
.

Let
n=we^{\prime}+m
with
0\leq m<e^{\prime}
. Set

p^{a}:=(\overline{q}^{\overline{e}}-1)_{p}\geq p
. The case
p\leq w

was treated in Section
4
, so we assume that

p>w
. Note that by
[
MO83
, Theorem 1.9]
,

B_{0}(\widetilde{G})
and
B_{0}({\mathrm{GL}}^{\epsilon}_{we^{\prime}}(q))
have the same number
of ordinary irreducible characters, so we may assume that
n=we^{\prime}
. (Note that the action of
{\mathrm{Aut}}(S)
is analogous as well.)

Let
\mathcal{F}(p,a)
denote the set of monic polynomials over

\mathbb{F}_{\overline{q}}
in the set
\mathscr{F}
defined in

[
FS82
]
whose roots have
p
-power order in

\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{{q}}^{\times}
at most
p^{a}
. Note that

|\mathcal{F}(p,a)|=1+(p^{a}-1)/e^{\prime}
, see
[
MO83
, p.
211]
.

The conjugacy classes
(t):=t^{\widetilde{G}}
of
p
-elements in
\widetilde{G}

are parameterized by
p
-weight vectors of
w
, which are functions

\mathbf{w}:=\mathbf{w}_{(t)}:\mathcal{F}(p,a)\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
such that
w=\sum_{g\in\mathcal{F}(p,a)}\mathbf{w}(g)
. The characteristic polynomial of elements in
(t)

is

(x-1)^{e^{\prime}\mathbf{w}(x-1)}\prod_{x-1\neq g\in\mathcal{F}(p,a)}g^{\mathbf{w}(g)},

and the centralizer of
t
is

{\mathbf{C}}_{\widetilde{G}}(t)={\mathrm{GL}}^{\epsilon}_{e^{\prime}\mathbf{w}(x-1)}(q)\times\prod_{x-1\neq g\in\mathcal{F}(p,a)}{\mathrm{GL}}^{\eta}_{\mathbf{w}(g)}(q^{{e^{\prime}}})

where
\eta=\epsilon
unless
\epsilon=-1
and
e^{\prime}=2\overline{e}
, in which case
\eta=1
.

Each character in the Lusztig series
\mathcal{E}(\widetilde{G},t)
is
labeled by
\chi_{t,\psi}
where
\psi
is a unipotent character of

{\mathbf{C}}_{\widetilde{G}}(t)
. So
\psi=\prod_{g\in\mathcal{F}(p,a)}\psi_{g}

where
\psi_{g}
is a unipotent character of

{\mathrm{GL}}^{\eta}_{\mathbf{w}(g)}(q^{{e^{\prime}}})
if
g\neq x-1
and of

{\mathrm{GL}}_{e^{\prime}\mathbf{w}(x-1)}(q)
if
g=x-1
. Note that there is a
canonical correspondence between unipotent characters of

{\mathrm{GL}}^{\pm}_{x}(q)
and partitions of
x
, so we may view
\psi_{g}
as a
partition of
\mathbf{w}(g)
when
g\neq x-1
and of

e^{\prime}\mathbf{w}(x-1)
when
g=x-1
.
Further, by
[
FS82
, Theorem (7A)]
, the characters of
B_{0}(\widetilde{G})
are exactly those
\chi_{t,\psi}

satisfying
t
is a
p
-element and the partition
\psi_{x-1}
has
trivial
e^{\prime}
-core.

By
[
Ols84
, Proposition 6]
,

k(B_{0}(\widetilde{G}))=k\left(e^{\prime}+\frac{p^{a}-1}{e^{\prime}},w\right),

where
k(x,y)
is as defined in Section
3
above. This
number is at least

(6.1)

e^{\prime}+\frac{p^{a}-1}{e^{\prime}}\geq 2\sqrt{p^{a}-1}\geq 2\sqrt{p-1}.

But, recall that we wish to show that there are at least
2\sqrt{p-1}
orbits on

{\mathrm{Irr}}(B_{0}(S))
under
{\mathrm{Aut}}(S)
.

Now, by taking
t=1
, the number of unipotent characters in

B_{0}(\widetilde{G})
is precisely
k(e^{\prime},w)
. Note that
k(e^{\prime},w)\geq k(e^{\prime},1)=e^{\prime}
, and that further
k(e^{\prime},w)\geq 2e^{\prime}
if
w\geq 2
with strict inequality for
(e^{\prime},w)\neq(1,2)
, and each unipotent character is
{\mathrm{Aut}}(S)
-invariant. So
we have at least
e^{\prime}

{\mathrm{Aut}}(S)
-orbits of unipotent characters in

B_{0}({\mathbb{G}})
, and hence of
B_{0}(S)
, since restriction yields a
bijection between unipotent characters of
S
and
{\mathbb{G}}
.

Let
\widetilde{{\mathbf{G}}}:={\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
so that

\widetilde{G}=\widetilde{{\mathbf{G}}}^{F}
. Since
{\mathbf{Z}}(\widetilde{{\mathbf{G}}})
is connected,

[
CS13
, Theorem 3.1]
yields that the “Jordan decomposition”

\psi_{t,\psi}\leftrightarrow(t,\psi)
can be chosen to be

{\mathrm{Aut}}(S)
-equivariant. Since
\psi
is a unipotent character of a
product of groups of the form
{\mathrm{GL}}^{\pm}_{x}(q^{d})
, which are invariant
under automorphisms as discussed above, it follows that the orbit of

\chi_{t,\psi}
is completely determined by the action of
{\mathrm{Aut}}(S)

on the class
(t)
.

Now, recall that the
\widetilde{G}
-class of
t
is completely determined
by its eigenvalues. Let
|t|=p^{c}
and note that
c\leq a
. By
viewing
t
as an element
1\times\prod_{x-1\neq g\in\mathcal{F}(p,a)}\zeta_{g}
of

{\mathbf{Z}}({\mathbf{C}}_{\widetilde{G}}(t))\cong C_{q-\epsilon}\times\prod_{x-1\neq g\in\mathcal{F}(p,a)}C_{q^{e^{\prime}}-\eta}

we see that for
\alpha\in{\mathrm{Aut}}(S)
, the eigenvalues of
t^{\alpha}
are those of
t
raised to some power
\eta q_{0}^{e}
for some
\eta\in\{\pm 1\}
and some
q_{0}
such that
\overline{q}
is a power of
q_{0}
.
This implies that the
{\mathrm{Aut}}(S)
-orbit of
(t)
has size at most
\frac{p^{c}-1}{e^{\prime}}\leq\frac{p^{a}-1}{e^{\prime}}
.

Now, the Sylow
p
-subgroup
P
of
\widetilde{G}
is of the form

C_{p^{a}}^{w}\leq(\mathbb{F}_{\overline{q}^{\overline{e}}}^{\times})^{w}
.
Then if
w=1
,
P
is cyclic, and hence we may assume that
w\geq 2
. In this case, we have at least

\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{p^{a}-1}{e^{\prime}}\right)^{2}
choices for
(t)\neq(1)
, and hence at least

\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{p^{a}-1}{e^{\prime}}\right)^{2}
non-unipotent
characters in
B_{0}(\widetilde{G})
by taking
\psi_{x-1}
to be trivial.
This gives at least
\frac{p^{a}-1}{2e^{\prime}}
distinct orbits of
non-unipotent characters, and hence more than
2\sqrt{p-1}
orbits
of characters in
B_{0}({\mathbb{G}})
under
{\mathrm{Aut}}(S)
when
e^{\prime}>1
, by (
6.1
) with
2e^{\prime}
rather than
e^{\prime}
.
This completes the proof of Theorem
1.2
for
S
in
the case
e^{\prime}>1
by the discussion at the beginning of the section.

Finally suppose
e^{\prime}=1
, so
w=n\geq 3
and we may continue to assume

p>w
. Consider the elements
t
of
\widetilde{G}
whose eigenvalues are
of the form
\{\zeta,\xi,(\zeta\xi)^{-1},1,\ldots,1\}
with

\zeta
and
\xi

p
-elements of

C_{q-\epsilon}\leq\mathbb{F}_{\overline{q}}^{\times}
. Note that
each member of
\mathcal{E}(\widetilde{G},t)
lies in the principal block
of
\widetilde{G}
and that
t
lies in
G=[\widetilde{G},\widetilde{G}]
. Further,
t

cannot be conjugate to
tz
for any nontrivial
z\in{\mathbf{Z}}(\widetilde{G})
,
since such a
z
would have determinant 1 and
p
-power order,
contradicting
p>n
. Then using
[
RSV21
, Lemma 1.4]
and

[
SFT21
, Proposition 2.6]
, each character in such a

\mathcal{E}(\widetilde{G},t)
is irreducible on restriction to
G
,
yielding at least
\frac{(p^{a}-1)^{2}}{2}
non-unipotent members of
B_{0}(G)
.
Since the
{\mathrm{Aut}}(S)
-orbits of such characters are again of size at
most
p^{a}-1
, this yields at least
2+\frac{p^{a}-1}{2}
distinct orbits, which is
larger than
2\sqrt{p-1}
. This completes the proof
of Theorem
1.2
in the case that

S={\mathrm{PSL}}^{\epsilon}_{n}(q)
.
∎

7.
Symplectic and Orthogonal Groups

In this section, we consider the simple groups coming from
orthogonal and symplectic groups. That is, simple groups of Lie
type
B_{n},C_{n},D_{n}
, and
{}^{2}D_{n}
. We let

\epsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
, and let
{\mathrm{P\Omega}}_{2n}^{\epsilon}(q)
denote the
simple group of Lie type
D_{n}(q)
for
\epsilon=1
and of type

{}^{2}D_{n}(q)
for
\epsilon=-1
.

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
q
be a power of a prime different from
p
and let
S={\mathrm{PSp}}_{2n}(q)
with
n\geq 2
,
{\mathrm{P\Omega}}_{2n+1}(q)
with
n\geq 3
, or
{\mathrm{P\Omega}}^{\epsilon}_{2n}(q)

. Then Theorem

holds for any almost simple group

with socle

Proof.

With the results of the previous sections, we may again assume that

and that a Sylow

-subgroup of

is abelian, but not

Let

be the corresponding symplectic or special orthogonal group

{\mathrm{Sp}}_{2n}(q)
,
{\mathrm{SO}}_{2n+1}(q)
, or
{\mathrm{SO}}_{2n}^{\epsilon}(q)
and let

({\mathbf{H}},F)
be the corresponding simple algebraic group and Frobenius
endomorphism so that
H={\mathbf{H}}^{F}
. Let
G={\mathbf{G}}^{F}
be the corresponding group
of simply connected type, so that

in the symplectic case or

is the appropriate spin group in the orthogonal cases. Further,
let
({\mathbf{H}}^{\ast},F)
and
({\mathbf{G}}^{\ast},F)
be dual to
({\mathbf{H}},F)
and

({\mathbf{G}},F)
, respectively, and
H^{\ast}={{\mathbf{H}}^{\ast}}^{F}
and

Define
\bar{H}
to be the group
{\mathrm{GO}}^{\epsilon}_{2n}(q)
in the case

, and

otherwise. We also

be the unique subgroup of index

in

Now, let

and write

. From Section

. To obtain our result, we will rely on the case

Namely,

. In most cases, we will again show that the number of
{\mathrm{Aut}}(S)

2\sqrt{p-1}
.

If

, a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
\Omega
,
G
,
H
, or
\bar{H}

(recall

) is cyclic, so we may assume by Lemma

2.1
that

. Note that the unipotent characters of

\Omega
. Arguing as in the

[

, if

{\mathrm{Aut}}(S)
-orbits of unipotent characters in

, and hence

, is

. Note that

since

.

The characters in
B_{0}(H)
and
B_{0}(G)
lie in Lusztig series
indexed by
p

of
H^{\ast}
, respectively

, by

[
CE04
, Theorem 9.12]
. Note that centralizers of

[

, Exercise (20.16)]
) and
that every odd

{\mathbf{H}}
and
{\mathbf{G}}
, so that
\chi_{(t)}

lies in

, respectively

, for every
p
-element

of
H^{\ast}
, respectively

, by Theorem
5.1
.
Further, note that the action on
\chi_{(t)}
under a graph-field

[
NTT08
, Corollary

. (See also (

Now let
{\mathbf{G}}\hookrightarrow\widetilde{{\mathbf{G}}}
be a regular embedding as in

[
CE04
, 15.1]
and let
\widetilde{G}:=\widetilde{{\mathbf{G}}}^{F}
. Then the

on

induces all diagonal automorphisms of

.
Now, since

is connected for any

-element

, we have every character in
\mathcal{E}(G,(t))
extends to
a character in
\widetilde{G}
. (Indeed, since

is abelian and

\widetilde{G}
to

of characters lying below a given

is the

\beta\in{\mathrm{Irr}}(\widetilde{G}/G)
such that

\widetilde{\chi}\beta=\widetilde{\chi}

[
RSV21
, Lemma 1.4]
.
Hence
[

and
[

, Proposition

yields the claim.) Therefore, each member of

is

First consider the case

or
{\mathrm{Sp}}_{2n}(q)
, so

or
{\mathrm{SO}}_{2n+1}(q)
, respectively. Note that

{\mathrm{Aut}}(S)/S
in this case is generated by field automorphisms, which
also act on

2
.

If

above and noting

must have the same multiplicity as its

\frac{p^{a}-1}{4e}
distinct orbits of

under the field automorphisms,
and hence at least
\frac{p^{a}-1}{4e}

under

{\mathrm{Aut}}(S)
. This gives more than

, which proves Theorem

1.2
in this case using (
6.1
).

If

, by
[
GH91
, Theorem 4.2]
, there is a
bijection between classes of
p
-elements of
H
and

, and
we note that field automorphisms act analogously on the

-elements
of

and

. Then the above again yields the result in this

with

odd.

If

with

odd, then we must have

and

. Here

, Theorem 2.5]
tells us that there is a
pair of unipotent characters permuted by the exceptional graph

under

. In this case, arguing as before and considering the action of the graph automorphism gives at least

orbits in

under
{\mathrm{Aut}}(S)
, which is at least
2(p-1)^{1/4}
. Hence part (ii) of Theorem
1.2
holds. So let
S\leq A\leq{\mathrm{Aut}}(S)
, and we wish to show that

contains more than

characters. Note that in this case,
{\mathrm{Aut}}(S)/S
is cyclic. Let

for

. Then

is cyclic, say of size

, and

is the unique block covering

by
[

, (9.19) and (9.20)]
. Note that since at least
3
of the unipotent characters of

are

3b
characters in

lying above unipotent characters. Further, since the automorphisms corresponding to those in

stabilize

-classes in

, the arguments above give at least
\frac{1}{2}\cdot\left(\frac{p^{a}-1}{2}\right)^{2}
members of

lying above semisimple characters of

, and hence there are at least
\frac{(p^{a}-1)^{2}}{8b}
members of
B_{0}(A)
lying above semisimple characters of
S
. Note then that the size of

is at least

, which is larger than

, completing the proof in this case.

Now, suppose we are in the case that
\bar{H}={\mathrm{GO}}_{2n}^{\epsilon}(q)
. Note that the action of
\bar{H}/H
induces a graph automorphism of order 2 in the case
\epsilon=1
, and that
{\mathrm{Aut}}(S)/S
is generated by a group of diagonal automorphisms of size at most 4, along with graph and field automorphisms. Further, note that the action of
H
on
\Omega
induces a diagonal automorphism of order
2
on
S
.
We may embed
\bar{H}
in
{\mathrm{SO}}_{2n+1}(q)
, and by
[

, Proof of Proposition 5.5]
, the classes of
p
-elements
t
with Lusztig series
contributing to

are parametrised exactly as in the case of
{\mathrm{SO}}_{2n+1}(q)
above.

Assume that
(n,\epsilon)\neq(4,1)
. By again considering semisimple characters
\chi_{(t)}
of
H
with
t\in H^{\ast}

p
-elements, we may conclude that the number of orbits of non-unipotent characters in
B_{0}(S)
under the
{\mathrm{Aut}}(S)
is at least
\frac{p^{a}-1}{4e}
. This yields at least
k(2e,w)+\frac{p^{a}-1}{4e}
orbits in
{\mathrm{Irr}}(B_{0}(S))
under
{\mathrm{Aut}}(S)
. Hence we have the number of
{\mathrm{Aut}}(S)
orbits in
B_{0}(S)
is strictly larger than
4e+\frac{p^{a}-1}{4e}
, completing Theorem
1.2
again in this case using (
6.1
).

Finally, suppose
S=D_{4}(q)={\mathrm{P\Omega}}_{8}^{+}(q)
so
\bar{H}={\mathrm{GO}}_{8}^{+}(q)
.
In this case, the graph automorphisms generate a group of size 6,
and a triality graph automorphism of order 3 permutes two triples of

[
Mal08
, Theorem 2.5]
).
Since
w\geq 2
, we have
(e,w)\in\{(1,4),(2,2)\}
. The arguments
above give at least
k(2e,w)-4+\frac{p^{a}-1}{12e}
distinct

{\mathrm{Aut}}(S)
-orbits in
{\mathrm{Irr}}(B_{0}(S))
. Since
k(2,4)=20
, by again
applying (
6.1
), we may assume

. In this case, we
have

, so Theorem

1.2
(ii) is proved in this case.

Now, let
S\leq A\leq{\mathrm{Aut}}(S)
, let
\Gamma
be the subgroup of
{\mathrm{Aut}}(S)
generated by inner, diagonal, and graph automorphisms, and let

. Then

is cyclic, and by
[
Nav98
, (9.19) and (9.20)]
,

is the unique block covering

. Let

.
Now, the arguments above give at least
\frac{1}{3}\cdot\frac{1}{2}\cdot\left(\frac{p-1}{4}\right)^{2}

lying above semisimple characters of

, since members of

correspond to automorphisms stabilizing classes of

-elements of

, and hence there are at least
\frac{(p-1)^{2}}{96b}
members of

lying above semisimple characters of

. Further, there are at least 10 characters in
B_{0}(X)
lying above unipotent characters in

. Since unipotent characters extend to their inertia groups and are invariant under field automorphisms (see
[
Mal08
, Theorems 2.4 and 2.5]
), this gives at least
10b
elements of
B_{0}(A)
lying above unipotent characters of
S
. Together, this gives

, proving part (i) of Theorem
1.2
.
∎

8.
A general bound for the number of
{\mathrm{Aut}}(S)
-orbits on

{\mathrm{Irr}}(B_{0}(S))

The aim of this section is to obtain a general bound for the number
of
{\mathrm{Aut}}(S)
-orbits on irreducible ordinary characters in the
principal block of
S
, for
S
a simple group of Lie type.

Building off of Theorem
5.1
, we will show that the principal block of
S
contains
many

irreducible semisimple characters. By controlling the length of

{\mathrm{Aut}}(S)
-orbits on these characters, we are able to bound below the
number of
{\mathrm{Aut}}(S)
-orbits on
{\mathrm{Irr}}(B_{0}(S))
. The bound turns out to
be enough to prove Theorem
1.2
for groups of
exceptional types, at least in the case when the Sylow
p
-subgroups
of the group of inner and diagonal automorphisms of
S
are abelian
but non-cyclic, which is precisely the case we need after
Sections
2.1
and
4
.

8.1.
Specific setup for our purpose

From now on we will work with the following setup:
{\mathbf{G}}
is a simple
algebraic group of adjoint type defined over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
and
F
a
Frobenius endomorphism on
{\mathbf{G}}
such that
S=[{\mathbb{G}},{\mathbb{G}}]
with

{\mathbb{G}}={\mathbf{G}}^{F}
. Let
({\mathbf{G}}^{\ast},F^{\ast})
be the dual pair of
({\mathbf{G}},F)

and for simplicity we will use the same notation
F
for
F^{\ast}
,
and thus
{\mathbf{G}}^{\ast}
is a simple algebraic group of simply connected
type and
S={\mathbb{G}}^{\ast}/{\mathbf{Z}}({\mathbb{G}}^{\ast})
, where
{\mathbb{G}}^{\ast}:=({\mathbf{G}}^{\ast})^{F}
.

Theorem
5.1
has the following consequence.

Lemma 8.1
.

Assume the above notation.
Let
p
be a good prime for
{\mathbf{G}}

not dividing
q
. For every
p
-element
t
of
{{\mathbb{G}}^{\ast}}
, the
semisimple character
\chi_{(t)}\in\mathcal{E}({\mathbb{G}},(t))
belongs to
the principal block of
{\mathbb{G}}
.

Proof.

Since
{\mathbf{G}}^{\ast}
is of simply connected type, the centralizer

{\mathbf{C}}_{{\mathbf{G}}^{\ast}}(t)
is connected for every semisimple element

t\in{\mathbb{G}}^{\ast}
, by
[
MT11
, Exercise 20.16]
. The lemma
follows from Theorem
5.1
.
∎

8.2.
Orbits of semisimple characters

Knowing that the semisimple characters

\chi_{(t)}\in{\mathrm{Irr}}({\mathbb{G}})
associated to
{\mathbb{G}}^{\ast}
-conjugacy classes
of
p
-elements all belong to
B_{0}({\mathbb{G}})
, we now wish to control the
number of orbits of the action of the automorphism group
{\mathrm{Aut}}(S)

on these characters. By a result of Bonnafé

[
NTT08
, §2]
, this action turns out to be
well-behaved.

Let
\alpha\in{\mathrm{Aut}}({\mathbb{G}})
, which in our situation will be a product
of a field automorphism and a graph automorphism. It is easy to see
that
\alpha
then can be extended to a bijective morphism

{\overline{\alpha}}:{\mathbf{G}}\rightarrow{\mathbf{G}}
such that

{\overline{\alpha}}
commutes with
F
. This
{\overline{\alpha}}

induces a bijective morphism

\overline{\alpha}^{\ast}:{\mathbf{G}}^{\ast}\rightarrow{\mathbf{G}}^{\ast}
which commutes
with the dual of
F
. The restriction of
\overline{\alpha}^{\ast}
to

{\mathbb{G}}^{\ast}
, which we denote by
\alpha^{\ast}
, is now an automorphism
of
{\mathbb{G}}^{\ast}
. Recall that
\alpha\in{\mathrm{Aut}}({\mathbb{G}})
induces a natural
action on
{\mathrm{Irr}}({\mathbb{G}})
by
\chi^{\alpha}:=\chi\circ\alpha^{-1}
. By

[
NTT08
, §2]
,
\alpha
maps the Lusztig series

\mathcal{E}({\mathbb{G}},(t))
of
{\mathbb{G}}
associated to
(t)
to the series

\mathcal{E}({\mathbb{G}},(\alpha^{\ast}(t)))
associated to

(\alpha^{\ast}(t))
. Consequently, if
{\mathbf{C}}_{{\mathbf{G}}^{\ast}}(t)
is
connected, then
{\mathbf{C}}_{{\mathbf{G}}^{\ast}}(\alpha^{\ast}(t))
is also connected,
and

(8.1)

{\chi_{(t)}}^{\alpha}=\chi_{(\alpha^{\ast}(t))},

which means that an automorphism of
{\mathbb{G}}
maps a semisimple
character associated to a conjugacy class
(t)
(of
{\mathbb{G}}^{\ast}
) to
the semisimple character associated to
(\alpha^{\ast}(t))
.

Due to Section
4
and Subsection
2.1
, we
may assume that the Sylow
p
-subgroups of
{\mathbb{G}}
are abelian but not
cyclic. Therefore,
{\mathbb{G}}
is not of type
\hskip 1.42262pt{}^{2}\hskip-0.56905ptB_{2}
or
\hskip 1.42262pt{}^{2}\hskip-0.56905ptG_{2}
.
Assume for a moment that
{\mathbb{G}}
is not of type
\hskip 1.42262pt{}^{2}\hskip-0.56905ptF_{4}
as well.
Then there is a unique positive integer
e
such that
p\mid\Phi_{e}(q)
and
\Phi_{e}
divides the generic order of
{\mathbb{G}}
. (Recall
that
\Phi_{e}
denotes the
e
th cyclotomic polynomial.) This
e

then must be the multiplicative order of
q
modulo
p
, which means
that
p\mid(q^{e}-1)
but
p\nmid(q^{i}-1)
for every
0<i<e
. In the
case
{\mathbb{G}}
is of type
\hskip 1.42262pt{}^{2}\hskip-0.56905ptF_{4}
, we use
\Phi_{4^{\pm}}(q):=q\pm\sqrt{2q}+1
, and what we discuss below still holds with slight
modification.

Let
\Phi_{e}(q)=p^{a}m
where
\gcd(p,m)=1
and
\Phi_{e}^{k_{e}}
the
precise power of
\Phi_{e}
dividing the generic order of
{\mathbb{G}}
. We
will use
k
for
k_{e}
for convenience if
e
is not specified. A
Sylow
e
-torus of
{\mathbb{G}}^{\ast}
has order
\Phi_{e}(q)^{k}
and contains a
Sylow
p
-subgroup of
{\mathbb{G}}^{\ast}
. Sylow
p
-subgroups of
{\mathbb{G}}^{\ast}

(and
{\mathbb{G}}
) are then isomorphic to

\underbrace{C_{p^{a}}\times C_{p^{a}}\times\cdots\times C_{p^{a}}}_{k\text{ times}}.

Assume that
q=\ell^{f}
where
\ell
is the defining characteristic
of
S
.

Lemma 8.2
.

Assume the above notation. Let
\alpha
be a field
automorphism of
{\mathbb{G}}
of order
f
, and thus
\langle\alpha\rangle

is the group of field automorphisms of
{\mathbb{G}}
. Each
\alpha
-orbit on
semisimple characters
\chi_{(t)}\in{\mathrm{Irr}}({\mathbb{G}})
associated to
conjugacy classes of
p
-elements (
p\neq\ell
) has length at most

\min\{f,p^{a}-p^{a-1}\}
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha^{\ast}
be an automorphism of
{\mathbb{G}}^{\ast}

constructed from
\alpha
by the process described above. For
simplicity we use
\alpha
for
\alpha^{\ast}
. By
(
8.1
) and since
|\alpha|=f
, it is enough to show
that each
\alpha
-orbit on
{\mathbb{G}}^{\ast}
-conjugacy classes of
(semisimple)
p
-elements of
{\mathbb{G}}^{\ast}
is at most
p^{a}-p^{a-1}
.

Let
t\in{\mathbb{G}}^{\ast}
be a
p
-element. Note that each element in

{\mathbb{G}}^{\ast}
conjugate to
t
under
{\mathbf{G}}^{\ast}
is automatically
conjugate to
t
under
{\mathbb{G}}^{\ast}
, by
[
DM91
, (3.25)]

and the fact that
{\mathbf{C}}_{{\mathbf{G}}^{\ast}}(t)
is connected. Let
t
be
conjugate to
h_{\alpha_{1}}(\lambda_{1})\cdots h_{\alpha_{n}}(\lambda_{n})
, where the
h_{\alpha_{i}}
are the coroots
corresponding to a set of fundamental roots with respect to a
maximal torus
{\mathbf{T}}^{\ast}
of
{\mathbf{G}}^{\ast}
and
n
is the rank of

{\mathbf{G}}^{\ast}
. Since
{\mathbf{G}}^{\ast}
is simply connected, note that
(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})\mapsto h_{\alpha_{1}}(t_{1})\cdots h_{\alpha_{n}}(t_{n})
is an
isomorphism from
(\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{q}^{\times})^{n}
to
{\mathbf{T}}^{\ast}
(see

[
GLS94
, Theorem 1.12.5]
).

Now, if
\lambda=\lambda_{i}
for some
1\leq i\leq n
, then

\lambda^{p^{a}}=1
, since
|t|\mid p^{a}
. Recall that
\ell\neq p
,
and thus
\ell^{p^{a}-p^{a-1}}\equiv 1(\bmod\,p^{a})
by Euler’s
totient theorem. It follows that

\lambda^{\ell^{p^{a}-p^{a-1}}}=\lambda
, which yields that the

\alpha
-orbit on
(t)
is contained in

\{(t),(\alpha(t)),...,(\alpha^{p^{a}-p^{a-1}-1}(t))\}
, as desired.
∎

8.3.
A bound for the number of
{\mathrm{Aut}}(S)
-orbits on

{\mathrm{Irr}}(B_{0}(S))

Let
{\mathbf{T}}_{e}
be an
F
-stable maximal torus containing a Sylow

e
-torus of
{\mathbf{G}}^{\ast}
, and thus
{\mathbf{T}}_{e}
contains a Sylow

p
-subgroup
P
of
{\mathbb{G}}^{\ast}
. Let

W({\mathbf{T}}_{e}):={\mathbf{N}}_{{\mathbb{G}}^{\ast}}({\mathbf{T}}_{e})/{\mathbf{T}}_{e}^{F}
be the relative Weyl group
of
{\mathbf{T}}_{e}
. It is well-known that fusion of semisimple elements in a
maximal torus is controlled by its relative Weyl group (see

[
MT11
, Exercise 20.12]
or
[
MM16
, p. 6]
).
Therefore, the number of conjugacy classes of (nontrivial)

p
-elements of
{\mathbb{G}}^{\ast}
is at least

\frac{|P|-1}{|W({\mathbf{T}}_{e})|}=\frac{p^{ak}-1}{|W({\mathbf{T}}_{e})|}.

Note that
\chi_{(t)}
belongs to the Lusztig series

\mathcal{E}({\mathbb{G}},(t))
defined by the conjugacy class
(t)
and the
Lusztig series are disjoint, and so two semisimple characters

\chi_{(t)}
and
\chi_{(t_{1})}
are equal if and only if
t
and

t_{1}
are conjugate in
{\mathbb{G}}^{\ast}
. Therefore, using
Lemma
8.1
, we deduce that, when
p
is
a good prime for
{\mathbf{G}}
and not dividing
q
,

(8.2)

|{\mathrm{Irr}}_{ss}(B_{0}({\mathbb{G}}))|\geq\frac{p^{ak}-1}{|W({\mathbf{T}}_{e})|},

where
{\mathrm{Irr}}_{ss}(B_{0}({\mathbb{G}}))
denotes the set of (nontrivial)
semisimple characters (associated to
p
-elements of
{\mathbb{G}}^{\ast}
) in

B_{0}({\mathbb{G}})
. Let
n(X,Y)
denote the number of
X
-orbits on a set

Y
. Using Lemma
8.2
, we then have

\displaystyle n({\mathrm{Aut}}(S),{\mathrm{Irr}}_{ss}(B_{0}({\mathbb{G}})))

\displaystyle\geq\frac{p^{ak}-1}{g\min\{f,p^{a}-p^{a-1}\}|W({\mathbf{T}}_{e})|}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{p^{k}-1}{g(p-1)|W({\mathbf{T}}_{e})|},

where
g
is the order of the group of graph automorphisms of
S
.
Let
d:=|{\mathbb{G}}/S|
– the order of the group of diagonal automorphisms
of
S
and viewing the irreducible constituents of the restrictions
of semisimple characters of
{\mathbb{G}}
to
S
as semisimple characters of

S
, we now have

(8.3)

n({\mathrm{Aut}}(S),{\mathrm{Irr}}_{ss}(B_{0}(S)))\geq\frac{p^{k}-1}{dg(p-1)|W({\mathbf{T}}_{e})|}.

We note that values of
d,f
, and
g
for various families of simple
groups are known, see
[
Atl85
, p. xvi]
for instance.

We now turn to unipotent characters in the principal block
B_{0}(S)
.
Broué, Malle, and Michel
[
BMM93
, Theorem 3.2]

partitioned the set
\mathcal{E}({\mathbb{G}}^{\ast},1)
of unipotent
characters of
{\mathbb{G}}^{\ast}
into
e
-Harish-Chandra series associated
to
e
-cuspidal pairs of
{\mathbf{G}}^{\ast}
, and furthermore obtained
one-to-one correspondences between
e
-Harish-Chandra series and the
irreducible characters of the relative Weyl groups of the

e
-cuspidal pairs defining these series. Cabanes and Enguehard

[
CE04
, Theorem 21.7]
then proved the compatibility
between Broué-Malle-Michel’s partition of unipotent characters
of
{\mathbb{G}}^{\ast}
by
e
-Harish-Chandra series and the partition of
unipotent characters by unipotent blocks. These results imply that
the number of unipotent characters in
B_{0}(S)
(and
B_{0}({\mathbb{G}}^{\ast})

as well) is the same as the number of conjugacy classes of the
relative Weyl group
W({\mathbf{L}}_{e})
of the centralizer

{\mathbf{L}}_{e}:={\mathbf{C}}_{{\mathbf{G}}^{\ast}}({\mathbf{S}}_{e})
of a Sylow
e
-torus
{\mathbf{S}}_{e}
of

{\mathbf{G}}^{\ast}
. Here we note that
{\mathbf{L}}_{e}
is a minimal
e
-split Levi
subgroup of
{\mathbf{G}}^{\ast}
, and
{\mathbf{L}}_{e}={\mathbf{S}}_{e}
if
{\mathbf{S}}_{e}
happens to be a
maximal torus of
{\mathbf{G}}^{\ast}
, since every maximal torus is equal to
its centralizer in a connected reductive group.

By the aforementioned result of Lusztig (
[
Mal08
, Theorem
2.5]
), every unipotent character of a simple group of Lie
type lies in a
{\mathrm{Aut}}(S)
-orbit of length at most 3. In fact, every
unipotent character of
S
is
{\mathrm{Aut}}(S)
-invariant, except in the
following cases:

(1)

S=P\Omega_{2n}^{+}(q)
(
n
even), the graph automorphism of order 2
has
o_{2}(S)
orbits of length 2, where
o_{2}(S)
is the number of
degenerate symbols of defect 0 and rank
n
parameterizing unipotent
characters of
S
(see
[
Car85
, p. 471]
).

(2)

S=P\Omega^{+}_{8}(q)
, the graph automorphism of order 3 has

o_{3}(S)=2
orbits of length 3, each of which contains one pair of
characters parameterized by one degenerate symbol of defect 0 and
rank
2
in (1).

(3)

S={\mathrm{Sp}}_{4}(2^{f})
(
f
odd), the graph automorphism of order 2
has
o_{2}(S)=1
orbit of length 2.

(4)

S=G_{2}(3^{f})
(
f
odd), the graph automorphism of order 2 has

o_{2}(S)=1
orbit of length 2 on unipotent characters.

(5)

S=F_{4}(2^{f})
(
f
odd), the graph automorphism of order
2 has
o_{2}(S)=8
orbits of length 2 on unipotent characters.

Combining this with the bound (
8.3
), we obtain:

Theorem 8.3
.

Let
S
be a simple group of Lie type. Let
p
be a good prime for

S
and different from the defining characteristic of
S
. Assume
that Sylow
p
-subgroups of the group of inner and diagonal
automorphisms of
S
are abelian. Let
k,d,f,g
,
{\mathbf{T}}_{e}
, and

{\mathbf{L}}_{e}
as above. Let
n(S)
denote the number of
{\mathrm{Aut}}(S)
-orbits
on irreducible ordinary characters in
B_{0}(S)
. Then

\displaystyle n(S)

\displaystyle\geq k(W({\mathbf{L}}_{e}))+\frac{p^{k}-1}{dg(p-1)|W({\mathbf{T}}_{e})|},

except possibly the above cases
(1),(3),(4)
, and
(5)
in which
the bound is lower by the number
o_{2}(S)
of orbits of length
2
on
unipotent characters and case
(2)
in which the bound is lower by

4
.

We remark that when
e
is regular for
{\mathbf{G}}^{\ast}
, which means that
the centralizer
{\mathbf{C}}_{{\mathbf{G}}^{\ast}}({\mathbf{S}}_{e})
of the Sylow
e
-torus

{\mathbf{S}}_{e}
is a maximal torus of
{\mathbf{G}}^{\ast}
, the maximal torus
{\mathbf{T}}_{e}

containing
{\mathbf{S}}_{e}
can be chosen to be precisely

{\mathbf{L}}_{e}={\mathbf{C}}_{{\mathbf{G}}^{\ast}}({\mathbf{S}}_{e})
. When
e
is not regular,
W({\mathbf{T}}_{e})

is always bigger than
W({\mathbf{L}}_{e})
. However, for exceptional types,

e
being non-regular happens only when
{\mathbf{G}}
is of type
E_{7}
and

e\in\{4,5,8,10,12\}
, see
[
BMM93
, Table 3]
.
We thank G. Malle for pointing out these facts to us.

We also remark that when the Sylow
p
-subgroups of the group of
inner and diagonal automorphisms of
S
are furthermore noncyclic,
then
k\geq 2
, and, away from those exceptions, we have a rougher
bound

(8.4)

n(S)\geq k(W({\mathbf{L}}_{e}))+\frac{p+1}{dg|W({\mathbf{T}}_{e})|},

but turns out to be sufficient for our purpose in most cases.

Proof of Theorems
1.1
and

First we remark that the ‘if’ implication of

1.3

Let

Assume first that

.
This in fact also yields that

is a unique minimal normal
subgroup of
G

With Theorem
1.1
in mind, it follows that for any

p
-block
B
for a finite group such that
B
shares its invariants
with the the principal block of some finite group
H
(or even just
satisfying
k(B)=k(B_{0}(H))
), we have
k(B)\geq 2\sqrt{p-1}
. In
particular, we may record the following:

Corollary 10.1
.

Let
G
be one of the classical groups
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(q)
,
{\mathrm{GU}}_{n}(q)
,

{\mathrm{Sp}}_{2n}(q)
,
{\mathrm{SO}}_{2n+1}(q)
, or
{\mathrm{GO}}_{2n}^{\pm}(q)
. Let
p
be a prime dividing
|G|
and not dividing
q
. Then for any

p
-block
B
of
G
with positive defect, we have
k(B)\geq 2\sqrt{p-1}
.

Proof.

If
p=2
, then the statement is clear, so we assume
p
is odd. First, if
G={\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(q)
or
{\mathrm{GU}}_{n}(q)
, the statement follows immediately from
Theorem
1.1
and
[
MO83
, Theorem (1.9)]
,
which states that
B
has the same block invariants as the principal
block of a product of lower-rank general linear and unitary groups.

Now suppose that
G
is
{\mathrm{Sp}}_{2n}(q)
,
{\mathrm{SO}}_{2n+1}(q)
, or

{\mathrm{GO}}_{2n}^{\pm}(q)
. If
B
is a unipotent block, then by

[
Mal17
, Proposition 5.4 and 5.5]
,
B
has the same block
invariants as an appropriate general linear group. (In the case

{\mathrm{GO}}_{2n}^{\pm}(q)
, we define a unipotent block to be one lying above
a unipotent block of
{\mathrm{SO}}_{2n}^{\pm}(q)
.) Hence the statement holds
if
B
is a unipotent block.

Now, the block
B
determines a class of semisimple
p^{\prime}
-elements

(s)
of the dual group
G^{\ast}
(see
[
CE04
, Theorem
9.12]
) such that
B
contains some member of

\mathcal{E}(G,(s))
. By
[
E08
, Théorème 1.6]
,
there exists a group
G(s)
dual to
{\mathbf{C}}_{G^{\ast}}(s)
such that

k(B)=k(b)
for some unipotent block
b
of
G(s)
. Now, in the
cases under consideration,
{\mathbf{C}}_{G^{\ast}}(s)
and
G(s)
are direct
products of lower-rank classical groups of the types being
considered here, completing the proof.
∎

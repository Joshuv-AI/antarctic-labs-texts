Building models of quarks and gluons with an arbitrary number of colors using Cartan-Polyakov loops
Renan Câmara Pereira, Pedro Costa

Abstract
In this work we introduce the concept of Cartan-Polyakov loops, a special subset of Polyakov loops in the fundamental and antifundamental representation of the SU ⁡ ( N c ) \mathrm{SU}(N_{c}) group, ℓ F , k \ell_{\mathrm{F},k} and ℓ ¯ F , k \overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},k} respectively, with charges k = 1 , … , ( N c − 1 ) / 2 k=1,\ldots,(N_{c}-1)/2 . It constitutes a sufficient set of independent degrees of freedom and it is used to parametrize the thermal Wilson line. Polyakov loops not contained in this set are classified as non-Cartan-Polyakov loops. Using properties of the characteristic polynomial of the thermal Wilson line, we write a non-Cartan-Polyakov loop charge decomposition formula. This formalism allows one to readily build effective models of quarks and gluons with an arbitrary number of colors. We apply it to the Polyakov − - Nambu − - Jona-Lasinio model and to an effective glue model, in the mean field approximation, showing how to directly extend these models to higher values of N c N_{c} .



I
Introduction

Gauge theories with the symmetry group
\mathrm{SU}(N)
, are fundamental building blocks to our current understanding of the laws of physics at the microscopic level. The theory of strong interactions, Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD), is a perfect example of a gauge theory based on the local invariance under transformations of the
\mathrm{SU}(N_{c})
symmetry group, with
N_{c}
being the number of colors. This theory has several important properties like scaling, asymptotic freedom, chiral symmetry breaking and confinement
Skands 2013
.

The interplay of the properties of QCD alongside its non-perturbative nature at low energies, leads the way to an extremely rich phase structure for strongly interacting matter, also known as the QCD phase diagram
Halasz et al. 1998
. Originally, only confined and deconfined phases were displayed in the diagram however, since then, other features have been discovered or proposed, such as, the existence of the quark-gluon plasma at high temperatures, the existence of the conjectured critical endpoint at finite temperature and baryon density, the possible diquark condensation in high density baryon matter or the confined chirally symmetric phase
Hansen et al. 2020
, to name a few.

However, in order to study QCD in these energy regimes we are limited in options due to the non-perturbative behavior of the theory. From a theoretical point of view, one can use lattice QCD calculations, Dyson
-
Schwinger equations and effective models. Lattice methods are the main tool to understand strongly interacting matter from first principles, at least for finite temperature and zero chemical potential. At finite density, lattice QCD suffers from the infamous sign problem which renders the importance sampling, necessary in Monte Carlo simulations, ineffective
Schmidt and Sharma 2017
.

Deviating from the real world of three colors and studying systems with increasing
N_{c}
, or considering the large
N_{c}
limit (also known as ’t Hooft limit, originally introduced in Ref.
’t Hooft 1974
), has been demonstrated to be a really good tool to better understand the behavior of the QCD properties
Witten 1979
;
Jenkins 1998
;
Bonanno and Giacosa 2011
;
Lucini and Panero 2014
;
Richardson et al. 2021
;
Lucha et al. 2021
;
Flores-Mendieta et al. 2021
, allowing for the exploration of the non-perturbative behavior of QCD in a systematic way
Witten 1979
;
Jenkins 1998
;
Lucini and Panero 2014
. The large
N_{c}
limit is taken by considering a large number of colors, while fixing
\lambda=g^{2}N_{c}
, with
g
the strong coupling and
\lambda
the so-called ’t Hooft coupling
’t Hooft 1974
.

Indeed, the large
N_{c}
limit of the theory should still exhibit confinement and chiral symmetry breaking since the theory remains asymptotically free, with a growing coupling at low energies
Jenkins 1998
;
Hernández and Romero-López 2021
. Not only that, some lattice studies, the phenomenological OZI (Okubo
-
Zweig
-
Iizuka) rule and the reproduction of ratios of low-lying meson masses
Karthik and Narayanan 2022
, also seem to point in the direction of a large
N_{c}
limit of QCD resembling the real world case of
N_{c}=3

Teper 1997
;
Teper 1999
;
Di Vecchia 1999
;
DeGrand and Liu 2016
;
García Pérez 2020
;
Hernández and Romero-López 2021
. Although QCD in the large
N_{c}
limit is a simpler theory, even in such approximation it has not yet to been solved
Polchinski 1992
;
Makeenko 2010
. Non-abelian gauge theories, with the gauge group
\mathrm{SU}(N)
, in the large
N
limit are also very important in the study of string theories, in particular, in studies involving the conjectured AdS/CFT correspondence, i.e., the duality between gauge theories and theories of gravity
Maldacena 1998
;
Aharony et al. 2000
;
Lucini and Panero 2014
. Indeed, one can also use this correspondence to study the QCD phase diagram
Yang and Yuan 2022
.

In the spirit of the large
N_{c}
limit, Pisarski et al. proposed the existence of a new phase of matter in the phase diagram of strongly interacting matter, the so-called quarkyonic phase, expected when the number of colors,
N_{c}
, is very large
McLerran and Pisarski 2007
. It was conjectured that, in this limit, the QCD phase diagram can be separated in three distinct phases, two at lower temperatures and one at high temperatures. The latter is the usual quark-gluon plasma, constituting the deconfined phase of matter. At low temperatures there would be two distinct phases: a mesonic phase, described by confined matter with zero baryon number density and, at higher chemical potentials, the so-called quarkyonic phase, described by confined matter with finite baryon number density
McLerran and Pisarski 2007
;
McLerran et al. 2009
. The conjectured quarkyonic phase of matter was discussed and studied in Refs.
McLerran et al. 2009
;
Torrieri and Mishustin 2010
;
Buisseret and Lacroix 2012
;
Yang and Yuan 2022
;
Kovács et al. 2022
, being also explored in the context of neutron stars in Refs.
Giacosa and Pagliara 2017
;
Margueron et al. 2021
.

From the phenomenological point of view, several effective models of QCD have been built in order to study the properties of the conjectured quarkyonic phase. Some of these models were based on the Polyakov
-
Nambu
-
Jona-Lasinio (PNJL) model, in which quark-quark interactions are point-like, with a static gluon field in the temporal direction acting as a background field. These models possess all the global symmetries of QCD, including the confinement/deconfinement transition in the sense of the breaking of the
\mathrm{Z}(N_{c})
symmetry and which can be measured by the expectation value of the Polyakov loop. This quantity is an exact order parameter in the pure glue theory but only approximate when quarks are introduced in the Lagrangian. The Polyakov loop is extremely used as an order parameter to study the deconfinement transition in both ab-initio calculations like lattice QCD, in Dyson-Schwinger equations, and in phenomenological models like the Polyakov-quark-meson model or the PNJL model.

In this work we show how to build models of quarks and gluons with an arbitrary number of colors, in a systematic way. More precisely, we will build a version of the Polyakov
-
Nambu
-
Jona-Lasinio model, in the mean field approximation and consider a glue effective potential, both for any number of colors. Being able to study such models systematically can be quite useful in order to better understand the deconfinement phase transition, the conjectured quarkyonic phase and the properties of quarks and gluons.

II
Center symmetry and the Polyakov loop

In the imaginary time formalism, often used to study quantum field theory at finite temperature, boson and fermion fields must respect periodic and antiperiodic boundary conditions, respectively. Using this prescription, it can be shown that, at the Lagrangian level, the boson fields of the Yang-Mills theory have symmetric boundary conditions with respect to transformations of the discrete group
\mathrm{Z}(N_{c})
, the center of the
\mathrm{SU}(N_{c})
. The introduction of fermion fields, on the other hand, explicitly breaks such invariance. Hence, Yang-Mills theory at finite temperature have boundary conditions that are invariant under
\mathrm{Z}(N_{c})
transformations while QCD does not share the same property, due to the presence of the quark fields. However, even in the pure glue theory, such symmetry can be spontaneously broken.

The Polyakov loop,
\Phi
, is a gauge invariant quantity, that can be used as an exact order parameter for the spontaneous symmetry breaking of the
\mathrm{Z}(N_{c})
symmetry in the Yang-Mills theory
Pisarski 2002
: if the Polyakov loop is zero, the
\mathrm{Z}(N_{c})
symmetry is preserved while, if it is non-zero, the symmetry is broken.

One can check the gauge invariance of the Polyakov loop by considering the gauge transformation of the thermal Wilson loop,
L\quantity( \vec{x} )
, from which one can define the Polyakov loop. The Wilson line can be defined along a path in spacetime, from an initial position (
x_{i}
) to a final position (
x_{f}
), as follows:

\displaystyle W(x_{i},x_{f})=\mathcal{P}\exp\quantity[ ig \int_{x_i}^{x_f} \dd{z^\mu} A_\mu(z) ].

(1)

Here,
\mathcal{P}
is the path-ordering operator,
g
is the strong interaction coupling and
A_{\mu}
is the gauge field
1
1

1

Under a gauge transformation, the gauge field
A_{\mu}(x)
transforms as:

\displaystyle A_{\mu}(x)\xrightarrow{\text{g.t.}}A_{\mu}^{\prime}(x)=\Omega(x)\quantity( A_\mu+ \frac{1}{ig} \partial_\mu)\Omega^{\dagger}(x).

(2)

. The Wilson line of the gauge transformed field is given by:

\displaystyle W(x_{i},x_{f})\xrightarrow{\text{g.t.}}W^{\prime}(x_{i},x_{f})=\Omega(x_{f})W(x_{i},x_{f})\Omega^{\dagger}(x_{i}).

By considering a closed path (
x_{i}=x_{f}=x
), one gets the so-called Wilson loop, which is a gauge invariant quantity due to the cyclic property of the trace:

\displaystyle\tr\quantity[ W(x_i,x_f) ]\xrightarrow{\text{g.t.}}\tr\quantity[ W^\p(x_i,x_f) ]

\displaystyle=\tr\quantity[
\Omega(x_f) W(x_i,x_f) \Omega^\dagger(x_i)
]

\displaystyle=\tr\quantity[ W(x_i,x_f) ].

(3)

Considering the Wilson loop wrapped in imaginary time (i.e., at finite temperature with periodic boundary conditions) one defines the thermal Wilson line,

\displaystyle L\quantity( \vec{x} )=\mathcal{P}\exp\quantity[ i \int_0^\beta\dd{\tau} A_4 (\vec{x},\tau) ].

(4)

Here,
A_{4}=ig{A}_{\mu}^{a}T_{a}\delta_{0}^{\mu}
, is the gluon field in the temporal direction (
T_{a}
are the
\mathrm{SU}(N_{c})
generators in the fundamental representation,
{A}_{\mu}^{a}
are the gluon fields in the adjoint representation and the index

runs from
0
to
N_{c}^{2}-1
). This quantity is also gauge invariant. Finally, one can define the Polyakov loop on a spatial coordinate
\bm{x}
, in the fundamental representation of the
\mathrm{SU}(N_{c})
group,
\Phi(\bm{x})
, as the trace of the thermal Wilson loop with the field
A_{4}
written in the fundamental representation

;
Fukushima 2004
:

\displaystyle\Phi(\bm{x})=\frac{1}{N_{c}}\tr\quantity[
L_{\Fund} \qty( \vec{x} ) ].

(5)

Here, the trace is made over color space and the index
\mathrm{F}
indicates that the matrix
A_{4}
is written in the fundamental representation of the
\mathrm{SU}(N_{c})
group. The Polyakov loop transforms akin to the fermion fields and only the identity element of the
\mathrm{Z}(N_{c})
group maintains the symmetry indeed, under a global
\mathrm{Z}(N_{c})
transformation, it transforms as a field with charge one
Pisarski 2002
;
Fukushima and Skokov 2017
.

The physical interpretation behind the two previously discussed phases, corresponding to zero and non-zero Polyakov loop, can be understood by evaluating the Helmholtz free energy of a single quark in a background of gluons. To this end, one can solve the static Dirac equation coupled to a static background field and use it to calculate the Helmholtz free energy of a single quark in a background of gluons
McLerran and Svetitsky 1981
;
Pisarski 2002
;
Ratti
. It yields that, if the Polyakov loop is finite, a single quark can be created in the gluonic background with a finite amount of energy while, if the Polyakov loop is zero, an infinite amount of energy is required
Pisarski 2002
;
Ratti
. Hence, a finite Polyakov loop would correspond to a deconfined phase of matter while, a zero Polyakov loop to a confined phase.

As already pointed out, the Polyakov loop is often incorporated in phenomenological models of QCD, like the PNJL or quark-meson models, in order to study the properties of the phase diagram of strongly interacting matter and explore the relation between the restoration of chiral symmetry and the deconfinement transition. In the following sections we will show how to build both a PNJL model and an effective glue potential, with an arbitrary number of colors, which can be used to explore the properties of strongly interacting matter.

III
Cartan-Polyakov loops

In this section, we will introduce the concept of Cartan-Polyakov loops which will enable one to readily derive quark and gluon models with an arbitrary number of colors.

Consider the thermal Wilson line defined in Eq. (
4
),
L
. Allying the unity determinant property of this quantity (it is an element of the
\mathrm{SU}(N_{c})
group) with the fact that, at every spacetime point, this matrix can be diagonalized with a gauge transformation
Pisarski 2002
, one can conclude that this quantity has a total of
N_{c}-1
degrees of freedom, the same number as the rank of the
\mathrm{SU}(N_{c})
group
Zee 2016
. Knowing the value of
N_{c}-1
traces of different powers of this matrix, allows one to build a system of
N_{c}-1
equations to the same number of unknown matrix entries. Hence, the traces can be used to parametrize the thermal Wilson line
Hidaka and Pisarski 2010
.

As introduced earlier, the Wilson line in the fundamental representation transforms as a field with charge
+1
, under a
\mathrm{Z}(N_{c})
symmetry transformation. The antifundamental representation transforms as a field with charge
-1
. As pointed out in Ref.
Dumitru et al. 2004
, the charges are defined modulo
N_{c}
, meaning that the antifundamental charge,
-1
, is equivalent to the charge
N_{c}-1
.

One can build Polyakov loops in higher dimensional representations, using combinations of fundamental and antifundamental Wilson lines. Examples include Wilson lines in the adjoint representation of
\mathrm{SU}(N_{c})
, two-index symmetric, antisymmetric and other representations, with higher index counts. These different possible representations transform under
\mathrm{Z}(N_{c})
as fields with different charges. For more information on different representations of the Wilson line, see
Dumitru et al. 2004
;
Gupta et al. 2008
;
Abuki and Fukushima 2009
. In particular, in Ref.
Dumitru et al. 2004
, it was shown how the classification procedure can be made in the large
N_{c}
limit.

In
\mathrm{SU}(3)
, the fundamental and antifundamental Polyakov loops constitute a sufficient set from which one can build any other higher representation Polyakov loop. For larger values of
N_{c}
this is not the case
Ayyar et al. 2018
and a higher number of degrees of freedom must be considered. In this work we will use Polyakov loops with different charges, in the fundamental representation, in order to easily build effective models containing both gluon and quark degrees of freedom, for an arbitrary
N_{c}
. More specifically, we will show how to build the Polyakov
-
Nambu
-
Jona-Lasinio model, in the mean field approximation and an effective gluon potential, for increasing values of
N_{c}
. Such constructions can be very helpful in better understanding the thermodynamics of QCD at large
N_{c}
, as well as, studying the properties of the conjectured quarkyonic phase in the QCD phase diagram or determining the behavior of the critical endpoint for increasing
N_{c}

Kovács et al. 2022
.

We define the Polyakov loop with charge
k
, as the trace of the
k-
power of the thermal Wilson line, in a particular representation,
\mathcal{R}
, of the
\mathrm{SU}(N_{c})
group. Polyakov loops with negative charge, are defined analogously, as the trace of the
k-
power of the adjoint thermal Wilson line
Fukushima and Skokov 2017
. Formally, we write:

\displaystyle\ell_{\mathcal{R},k}\quantity( \vec{x} )=\frac{1}{N_{\mathcal{R}}}\tr[L_{\mathcal{R}}(\bm{x})^{k}\big],

(6)

\displaystyle\overline{\ell}_{\mathcal{R},k}\quantity( \vec{x} )=\frac{1}{N_{\mathcal{R}}}\tr[L_{\mathcal{R}}^{\dagger}(\bm{x})^{k}\big].

(7)

Here,
N_{\mathcal{R}}
is the dimension of the representation. In the literature, the Polyakov loop variable in the fundamental representation is denoted by
\Phi
and
\overline{\Phi}
, see Eq.(
5
). Thus, in our notation, the following equivalence holds:
\Phi=\ell_{\mathrm{F},1}
and
\overline{\Phi}=\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},1}
. The zero charge Polyakov loop is trivially defined by the normalized trace of the
N_{\mathcal{R}}\times N_{\mathcal{R}}
identity matrix,
\ell_{\mathcal{R},0}=\overline{\ell}_{\mathcal{R},0}=1
. In other words, the Polyakov loop with charge
k
is the normalized trace of a thermal Wilson line wrapped
k
times around the compact imaginary time dimension
Polchinski 1992
:

\displaystyle\ell_{\mathcal{R},k}\quantity( \vec{x} )=\frac{1}{N_{\mathcal{R}}}\tr\mathcal{P}\exp\quantity[ i \int_0^{k\beta} \dd{\tau} A_4 (\vec{x},\tau) ].

(8)

Interestingly, for a given number of colors, these objects are not independent of each other. Indeed, a given Polyakov loop with charge
k
can be written as a function of other Polyakov loops, with different charges. This is connected to a previously mentioned property of the thermal Wilson line: it has a total of
N_{c}-1
independent degrees of freedom. As discussed earlier, one can parametrize the thermal Wilson line by calculating the trace of
N_{c}-1
powers of this matrix. Since a Polyakov loop with charge
k
is simply the trace of the
k-
power of the Wilson thermal line, there is a total of
N_{c}-1
independent charges. This immediately poses the question about which charges should be chosen as degrees of freedom to parametrize a phenomenological model.

This motivates the introduction of a special subset of Polyakov loops, which we denote by Cartan-Polyakov loops. For a specific number of colors,
N_{c}
, we denote the subset of
N_{c}-1
Polyakov loops, with charges chosen to be independent of each other, by Cartan-Polyakov loops and the remaining ones, by non-Cartan-Polyakov loops. In this work we define the Cartan-Polyakov loop set to be constituted by Polyakov loops in the fundamental and antifundamental representations of the
\mathrm{SU}(N_{c})
group,
\ell_{\mathrm{F},k}
and
\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},k}
, with charges
k=1,\ldots,(N_{c}-1)/2
. Hence, the Cartan-Polyakov loop set,
\mathcal{C}_{N_{c}}
, is given by:

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{N_{c}}=\quantity{
\Plrep{\Fund}{1},\ldots,\Plrep{\Fund}{(N_c-1)/2},
\Plbarrep{\Fund}{1},\ldots,\Plbarrep{\Fund}{(N_c-1)/2}
}.

(9)

Although this choice is made
a priori
and may seem arbitrary, it is guided by simplicity: although other choices exist
2
2

2

For instance, one could choose
\ell_{\mathrm{F},k}
and
\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},k}
with
k=(N_{c}-1)/2,\ldots,(N_{c}-1)
or, only consider the fundamental representation,
\ell_{\mathrm{F},k}
with
k=1,\ldots,(N_{c}-1)
.
, this particular one produces straightforward one-to-one mappings from the Cartan loops to non-Cartan loops while, for other choices, this may not be the case. For
N_{c}=3
, for example, the Cartan loops, are given by the set
\mathcal{C}_{3}=\quantity{ \Plrep{\Fund}{1}, \Plbarrep{\Fund}{1} }
. For the cases in which
N_{c}
is an even number, we opted to remove the element
\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},(N_{c}-1)/2}
from the set defined in Eq. (
9
). The reason for this is based on the fact that, for an even number of colors, there is freedom to choose between
\ell_{\mathrm{F},(N_{c}-1)/2}
and
\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},(N_{c}-1)/2}
. In other words, picking one of them to include in the Cartan set allows one to write the other in terms of the other loops in the Cartan set. In this work, for simplicity, we mainly focus on results for odd
N_{c}
: for such case there is a symmetry between the number of
\ell_{\mathrm{F},k}
and
\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},k}
contained in the Cartan set.

In the following, we will write recurrence relations, which will allow us to, within a fixed representation of the
\mathrm{SU}(N_{c})
group, decompose a non-Cartan-Polyakov loop with charge
k
, in terms of Polyakov loops with smaller charges,
k-1,k-2,\ldots
The idea is to use such recurrence relation in order to build effective models of quarks and gluons whose only degrees of freedom are the Cartan-Polyakov loops. For example, when building a model with
N_{c}=5
, the Cartan loops, are given by the set
\mathcal{C}_{5}=\quantity{ \Plrep{\Fund}{1}, \Plrep{\Fund}{2}, \Plbarrep{\Fund}{1},\Plbarrep{\Fund}{2} }
. However, in the construction process, non-Cartan-Polyakov loops such as,
\ell_{\mathrm{F},3}
,
\ell_{\mathrm{F},4}
,
\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},3}
,
\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},4}
, will arise. Using the recurrence relation that we will derive in the next section, enables one to write these non-Cartan-Polyakov loops as functions of loops with smaller charge, more specifically the ones present in the set
\mathcal{C}_{5}
.

We note that it may not be always possible to write effective models in terms of Cartan-Polyakov loops. Indeed in some models arises the necessity to involve elements of the thermal Wilson line matrix
Abuki and Fukushima 2009
. The mapping between the matrix elements of
L_{\mathcal{R}}
and the Cartan-Polyakov loops is not straightforward.

, can be readily calculated by the product of the diagonal entries since the matrix
J_{\mathcal{R},k}^{(k,1)}
is of the lower triangular type. It yields:

\displaystyle\det\quantity[J_{\Rep,k}^{(k,1)}]=1\times 2\times 3\times\ldots\times(k-1)=\frac{k!}{k}.

(15)

The determinant of the matrix
J_{\mathcal{R},k}
can then be written as:

\displaystyle\det\quantity[J_{\Rep,k}]

\displaystyle=\quantity(-1)^{k+1}N_{\mathcal{R}}\frac{k!}{k}\ell_{\mathcal{R},k}+N_{\mathcal{R}}\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\quantity(-1)^{i+1}\ell_{\mathcal{R},i}\det\quantity[J_{\Rep,k}^{(i,1)}].

(16)

Using the coefficients of the characteristic polynomial of the thermal Wilson line, defined in Eq. (
11
), we can write the determinant in the left-hand side of the above equation as:

\displaystyle(-1)^{-k}k!c_{k}(L_{\mathcal{R}})

\displaystyle=\quantity(-1)^{k+1}N_{\mathcal{R}}\frac{k!}{k}\ell_{\mathcal{R},k}+N_{\mathcal{R}}\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\quantity(-1)^{i+1}\ell_{\mathcal{R},i}\det\quantity[J_{\Rep,k}^{(i,1)}].

(17)

At this point we still cannot solve the equation for
\ell_{\mathcal{R},k}
since the coefficients,
c_{k}(L_{\mathcal{R}})
, still strongly depend on
\ell_{\mathcal{R},k}
. So, we make use of a special property of these coefficients. If an
N-
dimensional square matrix
M
is unitary, then the following holds:

\displaystyle c_{j}(M)

\displaystyle=\quantity(-1)^{N}\det\quantity[ M ]c_{N-j}(M^{\dagger}),

(18)

with
j=0,1,2,3,\dots,N-1,N
. Here,
c_{j}(M)
and
c_{j}(M^{\dagger})
are the
j-
coefficients of the characteristic polynomial of the matrices
M
and
M^{\dagger}
, respectively. This property is shown in Appendix
A
. For our purposes, it will allow us to write
c_{k}(L_{\mathcal{R}})
in terms of
c_{N_{\mathcal{R}}-k}(L_{\mathcal{R}}^{\dagger})
. This might not sound as much progress however, the highest charge Polyakov loop present in the coefficient
c_{N_{\mathcal{R}}-k}(L_{\mathcal{R}}^{\dagger})
is
\overline{\ell}_{\mathcal{R},N_{\mathcal{R}}-k}
. As long as
N_{\mathcal{R}}-k>0
, all the Polyakov loops present in
c_{N_{\mathcal{R}}-k}(L_{\mathcal{R}}^{\dagger})
will have a smaller charge than
\ell_{\mathcal{R},k}
. Hence, applying the identity written in Eq. (
18
) to the thermal Wilson line,
L_{\mathcal{R}}
, we can write Eq. (
17
) as:

\displaystyle(-1)^{N_{\mathcal{R}}-k}k!c_{N_{\mathcal{R}}-k}(L_{\mathcal{R}}^{\dagger})

\displaystyle=\quantity(-1)^{k+1}N_{\mathcal{R}}\frac{k!}{k}\ell_{\mathcal{R},k}+N_{\mathcal{R}}\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\quantity(-1)^{i+1}\ell_{\mathcal{R},i}\det\quantity[J_{\Rep,k}^{(i,1)}].

(19)

Additionally, we have used the fact that the thermal Wilson line is an special unitary matrix,
\det\quantity[ L_\Rep]=1
. Finally, we can solve this equation for
\ell_{\mathcal{R},k}
to yield the generating formula to decompose a non-Cartan-Polyakov loop into combinations of loops with smaller charges:

\displaystyle\ell_{\mathcal{R},k}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k+1}\frac{k}{N_{\mathcal{R}}\quantity(N_\Rep-k)!}\det\quantity[ \bar{J}_{\Rep,N_\Rep-k} ]-\frac{k}{k!}\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\quantity(-1)^{i-k}\ell_{\mathcal{R},i}\det\quantity[J_{\Rep,k}^{(i,1)}].

(20)

In the above, we used Eq. (
11
) to write,
c_{N_{\mathcal{R}}-k}(L_{\mathcal{R}}^{\dagger})=\nicefrac{{(-1)^{N_{\mathcal{R}}-k}}}{{\quantity(N_\Rep-k)!}}\det\quantity[ \bar{J}_{\Rep,N_\Rep-k} ]
and applied the identity,
(-1)^{2N_{\mathcal{R}}-3k-1}=(-1)^{k+1}
. The most important feature of this equation is that the right-hand side does not depend on the non-Cartan-Polyakov loop of charge
k
. Indeed, both
\det\quantity[ \bar{J}_{\Rep,N_\Rep-k} ]
and
\det\quantity[J_{\Rep,k}^{(i,1)}]
, depend only on Polyakov loops with charge smaller than
k
.

One can also derive a very similar formula using the characteristic polynomial of
-L_{\mathcal{R}}
, instead of
L_{\mathcal{R}}
i.e.,
p\quantity( -L_\Rep,\lambda)
. This polynomial can be written as:

\displaystyle p\quantity( -L_\Rep,\lambda)

\displaystyle=\det\quantity[ -L_\Rep- \lambda I ]

\displaystyle=\quantity(-1)^{N_{\mathcal{R}}}\quantity(
\lambda^{N_\Rep}
+
\sum_{i=1}^{{N_\Rep}-1}
c_i ( -L_\Rep) \lambda^{{N_\Rep}-i}
+
\qty(-1)^{N_\Rep} \det\qty[ -L_\Rep]
).

(21)

Similar to the previous characteristic polynomial, the coefficients are given by:

\displaystyle c_{k}(-L_{\mathcal{R}})

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}\det\quantity[ R_{\Rep,k} ].

(22)

The
k\times k
matrix
R_{\mathcal{R},k}
used above, is defined by:

\displaystyle R_{\mathcal{R},k}

\displaystyle=N_{\mathcal{R}}\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix
-\Plrep{\Rep}{1} & \frac{1}{N_\Rep} & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\
\Plrep{\Rep}{2} & -\Plrep{\Rep}{1} & \frac{2}{N_\Rep} & 0 & 0 & 0 \\
- \Plrep{\Rep}{3} & \Plrep{\Rep}{2} & -\Plrep{\Rep}{1} & \frac{3}{N_\Rep} & 0 & 0 \\
\vdots& \vdots& \vdots& \vdots& \ddots& 0 \\
(-1)^{k-1} \Plrep{\Rep}{k-1} & (-1)^{k-2} \Plrep{\Rep}{k-2} & (-1)^{k-3} \Plrep{\Rep}{k-3} & (-1)^{k-4} \Plrep{\Rep}{k-4} & \dots& \frac{ k-1 }{N_\Rep} \\
(-1)^{k} \Plrep{\Rep}{k} & (-1)^{k-1} \Plrep{\Rep}{k-1} & (-1)^{k-2} \Plrep{\Rep}{k-2} & (-1)^{k-3} \Plrep{\Rep}{k-3} & \dots& - \Plrep{\Rep}{1}
\endlx@physics@matrix).

(23)

The matrix elements are given by:

\displaystyle(R_{\mathcal{R},k})_{i,j}=(-1)^{i-j+1}N_{\mathcal{R}}\ell_{\mathcal{R},i-j+1}H\quantity(i-j)+i\delta_{i,j-1}.

(24)

Following the same steps as before, one can arrive at the following decomposition formula:

\displaystyle\ell_{\mathcal{R},k}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{N_{\mathcal{R}}+1}\frac{k}{N_{\mathcal{R}}(N_{\mathcal{R}}-k)!}\det\quantity[ \bar{R}_{\Rep,N_\Rep-k} ]-\frac{k}{k!}\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\ell_{\mathcal{R},i}\det\quantity[R_{\Rep,k}^{(i,1)}].

(25)

Once again, the matrix
\overline{R}_{\mathcal{R},k}
, has the same structure as the matrix defined in Eq. (
23
) with the replacement:
\ell_{\mathcal{R},k}\to\overline{\ell}_{\mathcal{R},k}
.

We highlight that both formulas, given in Eqs. (
20
) and (
25
), are only valid for
k<N_{\mathcal{R}}
. In order to decompose non-Cartan loops with charge
k\geq N_{\mathcal{R}}
, one can use the Cayley-Hamilton theorem. Using this theorem, we can write:

\displaystyle L_{\mathcal{R}}^{N_{\mathcal{R}}}+\sum_{i=1}^{{N_{\mathcal{R}}}-1}c_{i}(L_{\mathcal{R}})L_{\mathcal{R}}^{{N_{\mathcal{R}}}-i}+\quantity(-1)^{N_{\mathcal{R}}}=0.

(26)

Multiplying by
p-
powers of the thermal Wilson line, dividing by the dimension of the representation and taking the trace, yields the following equality:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N_{\mathcal{R}}}\tr[L_{\mathcal{R}}^{N_{\mathcal{R}}+p}]=-\frac{1}{N_{\mathcal{R}}}\sum_{i=1}^{{N_{\mathcal{R}}}-1}c_{i}(L_{\mathcal{R}})\tr[L_{\mathcal{R}}^{{N_{\mathcal{R}}}+p-i}]+\quantity(-1)^{N_{\mathcal{R}}+1}\frac{1}{N_{\mathcal{R}}}\tr[L_{\mathcal{R}}^{p}].

(27)

Hence, recognizing the different Polyakov loops, for
k\geq N_{\mathcal{R}}+p
, we can write:

\displaystyle\ell_{\mathcal{R},k}=\quantity(-1)^{N_{\mathcal{R}}+1}\ell_{\mathcal{R},k-N_{\mathcal{R}}}-\sum_{i=1}^{{N_{\mathcal{R}}}-1}c_{i}(L_{\mathcal{R}})\ell_{\mathcal{R},k-i}.

(28)

This non-Cartan-Polyakov loop decomposition equation is valid for
k\geq N_{\mathcal{R}}
. One can also use the identity given in Eq. (
18
) in order to introduce the adjoint Polyakov loops.

In order to show some examples in which Eqs. (

). It yields:

\displaystyle\ell_{\mathrm{F},2}

\displaystyle=3\ell_{\mathrm{F},1}^{2}-2\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},1}.

(29)

For the higher charges, we use Eq. (
28

\displaystyle\ell_{\mathrm{F},3}

\displaystyle=1-\frac{9}{2}\ell_{\mathrm{F},1}^{3}+\frac{9}{2}\ell_{\mathrm{F},1}\ell_{\mathrm{F},2}

\displaystyle=1-9\ell_{\mathrm{F},1}\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},1}+9\ell_{\mathrm{F},1}^{3},

(30)

(31)

\displaystyle\ell_{\mathrm{F},5}

\displaystyle=\ell_{\mathrm{F},2}-\frac{9}{2}\ell_{\mathrm{F},1}^{2}\ell_{\mathrm{F},3}+\frac{3}{2}\ell_{\mathrm{F},2}\ell_{\mathrm{F},3}+3\ell_{\mathrm{F},1}\ell_{\mathrm{F},4}

\displaystyle=15\ell_{\mathrm{F},1}^{2}+81\ell_{\mathrm{F},1}^{5}-135\ell_{\mathrm{F},1}^{3}\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},1}+45\ell_{\mathrm{F},1}\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},1}^{2}-5\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},1},

(32)

\displaystyle\;\;\vdots

Lo et al. 2021

applies. Thus, in this particular gauge, Polyakov loops of different charges can be written as:

\displaystyle\ell_{\mathrm{F},k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N_{c}}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{c}}\mathrm{e}^{ikq_{j}}=\frac{1}{N_{c}}\quantity(
\sum_{j=1}^{N_c-1} \e^{ i k q_{j} }

\e^{ \sum_{j=1}^{N_c-1} i k q_{j} }
).

Using these gauge fixed Polyakov loops in the fundamental representation, one can check the results coming from the non-Cartan-Polyakov decomposition formulas.

IV
Applications

In this section we show two examples of how to construct models of quarks and gluons with any number of colors. First, we consider the PNJL model in the mean field approximation. Secondly, we consider a glue effective potential.

IV.1
The mean-field Polyakov
-
Nambu
-
Jona-Lasinio model with an arbitrary
N_{c}

The NJL model is widely used as an effective model of QCD due to its simplicity, symmetry properties and ability to display dynamical chiral symmetry breaking in the vacuum and restoration at high temperatures/densities. Some applications of this model include the study of in-medium meson behavior, transport coefficients of quark matter, the equation of state of hybrid neutron stars and the phase diagram of strongly interacting matter with great emphasis on the location of the conjectured critical endpoint. This model however, lacks the ability to describe the confinement-deconfinement transition. In the Polyakov version of the model, a static gluonic background field is coupled to the quarks sector, allowing for the description of both the chiral and deconfinement transitions
Fukushima 2004
;
Mocsy et al. 2004
;
Ratti et al. 2006
;
Hansen et al. 2007
.

The Polyakov loop can be included in the model by minimally coupling a background gluon field in the time direction,
A_{0}=-iA_{4}

Ratti et al. 2006
, to the Lagrangian density via the covariant derivative. It can be defined as:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\quantity( \psi, \bar{\psi}, A_4 , T )

\displaystyle=\overline{\psi}\quantity(i\slashed{D}-\hat{m})\psi+\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{int}}\quantity( \psi, \bar{\psi} )-\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{eff}}\quantity( A_4 , T ).

(39)

Here,
\psi
is the quark field,
D^{\mu}=\partial^{\mu}-i\delta_{0}^{\mu}A^{0}
is the covariant derivative and
\hat{m}=\diag\quantity{ m_1, m_2, \ldots, m_{N_f} }
is the quark current mass matrix (with
N_{f}
the number of quark flavors). The different quark-quark interactions are contained in the term
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{int}}\quantity( \psi, \bar{\psi} )
and, for our purposes, its exact definite form is not important. This interaction term not only includes the dynamical chiral symmetry breaking 4-quark scalar-pseudoscalar interaction,
\mathcal{L}\supset(\overline{\psi}\lambda_{a}\psi)^{2}+(\overline{\psi}i\gamma_{5}\lambda_{a}\psi)^{2}
(
\lambda_{a}
are the generators of the
\mathrm{U}(N_{f})
algebra), but it also includes other multi-quark interactions, like the ’t Hooft determinant, eight quark-quark interactions, explicit chiral symmetry breaking interactions, vector interactions, etc
Moreira et al. 2018
;
Câmara Pereira et al. 2020
;
Pereira 2021
;
Ferreira et al. 2021
. The effective potential,
\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{eff}}\quantity( A_4 , T )
, incorporates the spontaneous breaking of the
\mathrm{Z}(N_{c})
symmetry at some finite temperature. This potential will be discussed in much detail in later sections where we will define a version of it for any number of colors.

The Euclidean generating functional for the PNJL model, can be written as:

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}\quantity[ \eta, \bar{\eta} , j_4 ]\propto\int\mathcal{D}A_{4}\mathcal{D}\overline{\psi}\mathcal{D}\psi\exp\quantity[
- \mathcal{S}_E \qty[ \bar{\psi},\psi, A_4 ]
+ \overline{\psi} \eta+ \bar{\eta} \psi+A_4 j_4
].

(40)

Here,
\mathcal{S}_{E}
is the Euclidean action of the PNJL model. The path integral over the temporal gluon field
A_{4}
is to be made over all of its
N_{c}-1
components and
j_{4}
is a source for the temporal gluon field.

To deal with the path integral over the quark fields, we apply the mean field approximation. In this scheme, the multi-quark interactions present in
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{int}}
, can be written as the product of quark bilinear operators with the introduction of mean fields,
\expectationvalue{\phi_i}
. After the linearization of the product between these operators, a quadratic Lagrangian in the quark fields is obtained and the quark fields can be integrated out from the generating function
Câmara Pereira et al. 2020
. The generating functional becomes:

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}\quantity[ \expval{\phi_i}, j_4 ]\propto\int\mathcal{D}A_{4}\exp\quantity[
- \mathcal{S}_E \qty[ \expval{\phi_i}, A_4 ]
+ A_4 j_4
].

(41)

The only thing left to do is to solve the path integral over the temporal gluon field. Different approaches to deal with the path integral over the
A_{4}
field, can be found in the literature, ranging from using the Functional Renormalization Group, a Gaussian model or using the Weiss mean field approximation, see
Abuki and Fukushima 2009
;
Haas et al. 2013
;
Lo et al. 2018
. One can parametrize the
\mathrm{SU}(N_{c})
group volume in terms of the diagonal elements of the
\mathrm{SU}(N_{c})
algebra, i.e., the Cartan elements of the algebra
Rößner 2006
;
Hell et al. 2010
;
Hell 2010
;
Sasaki and Redlich 2012
. Hence, we can separate the integration over
A_{4}
in Eq. (
41
) in two contributions, one made over the diagonal generators of the
\mathrm{SU}(N_{c})
algebra and another over the non-diagonal generators. One can integrate over the non-diagonal components to yield the so-called Haar measure of the
\mathrm{SU}(N_{c})
group,
H

Hell et al. 2009
;
Hell et al. 2010
;
Drouffe and Zuber 1983
:

\displaystyle H(A_{4}^{\diag})=\frac{1}{V_{\mathrm{SU}(N_{c})}}\int\prod_{i\notin\diag}\differential{A_4^{(i)}}.

(42)

Here,
A_{4}^{\mathrm{diag}}
is the set of diagonal components of
A_{4}
. In the already introduced Polyakov gauge,
A_{4}
is diagonal and traceless, meaning it can be parametrized in terms of the Cartan elements,
A_{4}=\sum_{i\in\diag}A_{4}^{(i)}t_{i}
with
t_{i}
the diagonal generators of the
\mathrm{SU}(N_{c})
algebra i.e., the Cartan subalgebra. For
N_{c}=3
, for instance, we can write
A_{4}=A_{4}^{(3)}\lambda_{3}+A_{4}^{(8)}\lambda_{8}
, with
\lambda_{3}
and
\lambda_{8}
the diagonal Gell-Mann matrices. Thus, in Eq. (
41
) one can integrate out the non-diagonal elements of
A_{4}
leaving only the path integral over the diagonal components, which contains all the physics of the model
Hell et al. 2009
;
Hell et al. 2010
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}\quantity[ \expval{\phi_i}, j_4 ]\propto\int\prod_{j\in\diag}\mathcal{D}{A_{4}^{(j)}}\exp\quantity[
- \mathcal{S}_E \qty[ \expval{\phi_i}, A_4 ]
+ A_4 j_4
+ \kappa\ln\qty[ H (A_4^{\diag}) ]
].

(43)

Here,
\kappa
is some proportionality constant and
H[A_{4}^{\diag}]
is the Haar measure, which results from integrating the non-diagonal components of
A_{4}
. The Haar measure can be absorbed on the definition of the Polyakov effective potential,
\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{eff}}
. As a matter of fact, the Haar measure has been understood to be a crucial part of the potential by favoring the confined phase
Fukushima and Skokov 2017
.

At this point one needs to solve the path integral over the diagonal components of the field
A_{4}
. The standard approach is to consider that the functional integration is dominated by the stationary point: quantum fluctuations are neglected and only the classical configuration contributes to the path integral
Roessner et al. 2007
;
Pereira 2021
. Thus, the functional integration can be dropped and the diagonal components of the
A_{4}
field can be treated as mean fields. The values of these fields are calculated by requiring the effective action to be stationary,
\derivative*{ \mathcal{S}_E }{ A_4^{(i)} }=0
, with
A_{4}^{(i)}
the Cartan elements.

Usually, for
N_{c}=3
, the requirements of stationarity of the effective action with respect to the diagonal components of the
A_{4}
field are replaced by stationarity with respect to the values of the charge one Polyakov loop and its adjoint in the fundamental representation,
\Phi
and
\overline{\Phi}

Roessner et al. 2007
. Since these quantities are functions of
A_{4}^{\diag}
,
\Phi=\Phi[A_{4}^{\diag}]
and
\overline{\Phi}=\overline{\Phi}[A_{4}^{\diag}]
, changing variables is allowed as long as the Jacobian of the transformation is non-singular
Haag 1958
;
Chisholm 1961
;
Kamefuchi et al. 1961
. For other values of
N_{c}
,
\ell_{\mathrm{F},1}=\Phi
and
\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},1}=\overline{\Phi}
are not enough degrees of freedom to express the Haar measure. Thus, for other values of
N_{c}
, we will consider that these change of variables are allowed and replace the variables of the model: instead of using the set of Cartan elements of the
A_{4}
field, we will use the Cartan-Polyakov loop set defined in Eq. (
9
).

Within the mean field approximation, the Lagrangian density of the PNJL model can be written as the one of fermionic quasiparticles with an effective mass,
M
, chemical potential,
\mu
, and effective mean field potential,
U
. Using the Matsubara formalism, one can calculate the thermodynamic potential of the model at finite temperature and chemical potential,
\Omega\quantity(N_c, T,\mu)

Fukushima 2004
;
Ratti et al. 2006
. It is given by:

\displaystyle\Omega(N_{c},T,\mu)

\displaystyle=U(\expectationvalue{\phi_i})+\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{eff}}(A_{4}^{\diag},T)

\displaystyle-2T\tr\int_{\mathrm{reg}}\frac{\differential[3]{p}}{\quantity(2 \pi)^{3}}\quantity{
\beta N_c E
+
\ln\det\qty[ 1 + L_\Fund\e^{ -\beta\qty( E - \mu) } ]
+
\ln\det\qty[ 1 + L_\Fund^\dagger\e^{ -\beta\qty( E + \mu) } ]
}.

(44)

Here,
E=\sqrt{p^{2}+M^{2}}
and
\beta
is the inverse temperature,
\beta={T}^{-1}
. The potential
U(\expectationvalue{\phi_i})
contains the contribution coming from the mean fields,
\expectationvalue{\phi_i}
. The trace must be calculated over flavor indexes while the determinant is calculated over color space. The mean fields are fixed by requiring for thermodynamic consistency,
\derivative*{\Omega}{ \expval{\phi_i} }=0

Until now, we have not yet specified the number of colors. In order to obtain the thermodynamic potential for a particular number of colors, one must write the effective Polyakov loop potential and calculate the determinant inside the logarithms, for the chosen
N_{c}

IV.1.1
The fermionic determinant with an arbitrary
N_{c}

Lets start by evaluating the fermionic contribution for a given number of colors. The fermionic determinants are given by:

\displaystyle\det\quantity[ 1 + L_\Fund\e^{ -\beta\qty( E - \mu) } ]=h_{-}^{N_{c}}\det\quantity[ h_-^{-1} I + L_\Fund],

(45)

\displaystyle\det\quantity[ 1 + L_\Fund^\dagger\e^{ -\beta\qty( E + \mu) } ]=h_{+}^{N_{c}}\det\quantity[ h_+^{-1} I + L_\Fund^\dagger].

(46)

Here, we have defined the non-zero quantities:

\displaystyle h_{\pm}=\mathrm{e}^{-\beta\quantity( E \pm{\mu} )}.

(47)

The goal is to calculate the determinants for an arbitrary
N_{c}
using only the corresponding set of Cartan-Polyakov loops. To this end, we will write the determinants in Eqs. (
45
) and (
46

(N_{c}-1)

c_{k}(-M)

\displaystyle h_{-}^{N_{c}}\det\quantity[ L_\Fund+ h_-^{-1} I ]

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{k=1}^{{N_{c}}-1}c_{k}(-L_{\mathrm{F}})h_{-}^{k}+h_{-}^{N_{c}}.

(48)

We used the fact the dimension of the fundamental representation is,
N_{\mathrm{F}}=N_{c}
and that
\det\quantity[ -L_\Fund]=\quantity(-1)^{N_{c}}

In the sum, the difficulty of calculating the determinant increases with the number of colors. For a fixed number of colors, calculating the
k-
th coefficient in the sum, amounts to solving the determinant of the
R_{\mathcal{R},k}
matrix, see Eq. (
22

k-
th coefficient in the sum will necessarily contain Polyakov loops, of charge
\quantity[1,\ldots,k]
. This can be seen by observing the structure of the matrix
R_{\mathcal{R},k}
, defined in Eq. (
23

\quantity[1,\ldots,(N_c-1)/2]
. Hence, the coefficients in the sum, with
k=(N_{c}-1)/2+1,\ldots,N_{c}-1
, will unavoidably contain non-Cartan-Polyakov loops. Since the goal is to write the model exclusively as a function of Cartan-Polyakov loops, this feature poses the difficult task of expanding the determinant of Eq. (
48

20

In order to solve this issue, we will use a trick to simplify the practical calculations while, at the same time, automatically re-write the sum as a function of Cartan-Polyakov loops only. For simplicity, in the following discussion, we will focus on the case in which the number of colors is odd. The even number of colors case is a straightforward extension.

Consider the identity given in Eq. (
18
), which states that we can relate the coefficient
c_{k}(-L_{\mathrm{F}})
, with the coefficient
c_{N_{c}-k}(-L_{\mathrm{F}}^{\dagger})
. If we employ this identity, we can break the original sum in Eq. (
48

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{{N_{c}}-1}c_{k}(-L_{\mathrm{F}})h_{-}^{k}=\sum_{k=1}^{(N_{c}-1)/2}c_{k}(-L_{\mathrm{F}})h_{-}^{k}+\sum_{k=(N_{c}+1)/2}^{{N_{c}}-1}c_{N_{c}-k}(-L_{\mathrm{F}}^{\dagger})h_{-}^{k},

(49)

where we have used
c_{j}(-L_{\mathrm{F}})=c_{N-j}(-L_{\mathrm{F}}^{\dagger})
. One can check that both the first and second sums, on the right-hand side of this equation, contains coefficients that will only produce Polyakov loops with charges in the interval
\quantity[1,\ldots,(N_c-1)/2]
. This range of charges is the same as the one which defines the Cartan-Polyakov loop set. Hence, the determinant can be written only as a function of Cartan-Polyakov loops.

From an analytically point of view, another nice feature of this trick is that by employing it, one only needs to calculate half of the coefficients. At first glance, one has to calculate
N_{c}-1

\ell_{\mathrm{F},k}\to\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},k}
.

Thus, for an odd number of colors, we can re-write the sum using Eq. (
49

\displaystyle\det\quantity[ 1 + L_\Fund\e^{ -\beta\qty( E - \mu) } ]

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{k=1}^{(N_{c}-1)/2}c_{k}(-L_{\mathrm{F}})h_{-}^{k}+\sum_{k=(N_{c}+1)/2}^{{N_{c}}-1}c_{N_{c}-k}(-L_{\mathrm{F}}^{\dagger})h_{-}^{k}+h_{-}^{N_{c}}.

(50)

For the case in which
N_{c}
is an even number, the idea is the same with the small caveat that one of the sums will have an additional term.

The calculation of the determinant, defined in Eq. (
46

50
) with the following replacements:
-\to+
,
L_{\mathrm{F}}\to L_{\mathrm{F}}^{\dagger}
and

The explicit calculation of the coefficients follows from Eq. (

IV.1.2
The Polyakov loop effective potential with an arbitrary
N_{c}

We now turn our attention to the effective Polyakov loop potential,
\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{eff}}
, which can be written using the Ginzburg
-
Landau theory of phase transitions. Within this approach, the potential must respect the symmetry properties of the original theory.

For
N_{c}=3
, one can build this potential using the charge one Polyakov loop in the fundamental representation (and its conjugate),
\ell_{\mathrm{F},1}=\Phi
and
\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},1}=\overline{\Phi}
. Different phenomenological potentials were introduced in the literature including the logarithmic, polynomial and polynomial-logarithmic. They all share a kinetic term, proportional to
\Phi\overline{\Phi}
and higher order terms that are essential to build a bounded potential, with the correct symmetries (the kinetic term introduces an additional
\mathrm{U}(1)
symmetry which must be removed by other terms
Rößner 2006
;
Hell 2010
;
Stiele 2014
), which reproduces a first-order phase transition at some finite temperature.

In the logarithm potential, the non-quadratic term is given by the logarithm of the Haar measure. As already seen, this contribution naturally appears when one integrates out the non-diagonal components of the
A_{4}
field
Fukushima 2004
;
Hell et al. 2009
. This potential, for
N_{c}=3
, is widely defined as
Fukushima 2004
;
Roessner et al. 2007
;
Costa et al. 2010
:

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{eff}}\quantity( \Phi, \bar{\Phi} , T )}{T^{4}}=-\frac{1}{2}a\quantity( T )\overline{\Phi}\Phi+b\quantity( T )\ln\quantity[ 1 - 6 \bar{\Phi} \Phi+ 4 \qty( \bar{\Phi}^3 + \Phi^3 ) - 3 \qty( \bar{\Phi} \Phi)^2 ].

(56)

The quantity inside the logarithm is the Haar measure for
N_{c}=3
. The temperature dependent parameters
Roessner et al. 2007
;
Costa et al. 2010
are:

\displaystyle a\quantity( T )

\displaystyle=a_{0}+a_{1}\quantity( \frac{T_0}{T} )+a_{2}\quantity( \frac{T_0}{T} )^{2},

(57)

\displaystyle b\quantity( T )

\displaystyle=b_{3}\quantity( \frac{T_0}{T} )^{3}.

(58)

The parameters
T_{0}
,
a_{0}
,
a_{1}
,
a_{2}
and
a_{3}
are fixed by reproducing lattice QCD results
Boyd et al. 1996
;
Kaczmarek et al. 2002
with
T_{0}
fixing the temperature of the phase transition
Ratti et al. 2006
. The parameter
a_{0}
is fixed by requiring the Steffan-Boltzmann limit when
T\to\infty
while the parameter
b_{3}
is fixed by requiring the second-order phase transition to occur at
T=T_{0}

Ratti et al. 2006
;
Roessner et al. 2007
. The gluon contribution to the Steffan-Boltzmann pressure is
\nicefrac{{P_{\mathrm{SB}}}}{{T^{4}}}=\nicefrac{{2(N_{c}^{2}-1)\pi^{2}}}{{90}}
. Hence, the
a_{0}
parameter can be readily defined at diverging temperatures,
T\to\infty
,
a_{0}=\nicefrac{{4(N_{c}^{2}-1)\pi^{2}}}{{90}}
. Thus, one can conclude that this parameter is
N_{c}
dependent. Since
b_{3}
can be calculated from the remaining parameters, including
a_{0}
, it is also
N_{c}
dependent. When building an effective Polyakov loop potential for any number of colors, this has to be taken into account.

For
N_{c}=3
, following lattice QCD calculations at zero chemical potential, the
T_{0}
parameter is usually fixed to
270
MeV
Karsch 2002
;
Borsanyi et al. 2012
. Different approaches have been used in the literature to fix the value of
T_{0}
, specially when studying systems at finite chemical potential. As a matter or fact, in Ref.
Schaefer et al. 2007
, the parameter
T_{0}
was given an explicit dependence on the number of flavors of quarks considered, due to renormalization group arguments.

The polynomial potential is very similar with the major difference that higher powers of
\ell_{\mathrm{F},1}=\Phi
and
\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},1}=\overline{\Phi}
are incorporated in the potential
Pisarski 2000
;
Ratti et al. 2006
. The polynomial-logarithmic potential, as stated in the name, is a mixture between the logarithmic and polynomial potentials
Lo et al. 2013a
. Another important property of the logarithmic potential is its diverging nature in the limit
\ell_{\mathrm{F},1}=\Phi\to 1
and
\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},1}=\overline{\Phi}\to 1
. This ensures that the Polyakov loops do not exceed unity
Roessner et al. 2007
.

The question that now arises is the following: how to define an effective Polyakov loop potential for any given value of the number of colors? The studies performed in Refs.
McLerran et al. 2009
;
Buisseret and Lacroix 2012
, built effective Polyakov loop potentials for any number of colors based on the polynomial potential in order to study the phase diagram at large
N_{c}
. However, such potentials were written only as a function of
\ell_{\mathrm{F},1}=\Phi
and
\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},1}=\overline{\Phi}
and Polyakov loops with larger charges were neglected. At large
N_{c}
, the fundamental loop with charge one may indeed be the most important degree of freedom however, in this work, we want to write a general expression for any
N_{c}
, without any
a priori
approximations (apart from the mean field one). After building such model, then one may perform a large
N_{c}
approximation.

In this work, we propose to simply extend the logarithmic potential to any value of
N_{c}
. Using the polynomial potential, would mean having to consider different interaction terms between different Cartan-Polyakov loops in such a way to build
\mathrm{Z}(N_{c})
invariant and
\mathrm{U}(1)
breaking interactions. The Haar measure automatically gives interaction terms which respect the symmetries of the model, yielding an appropriate effective potential at any number of
N_{c}
. To this end we consider that the potential can be written solely in terms of the previously introduced Cartan-Polyakov loops. Hence, for the kinetic part of the potential we simply consider a sum over quadratic terms of the Cartan-Polyakov loop set:

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\frac{N_{c}-1}{2}}\ell_{\mathrm{F},i}\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},i}.

(59)

This term is invariant under
\mathrm{Z}(N_{c})
and under an unwanted
\mathrm{U}(1)
symmetry which must be removed by the remaining contributions. The non-quadratic part of the potential will be given by the Haar measure, for a given
N_{c}
, written exclusively in terms of Cartan-Polyakov loops. In a later discussion, we will cover the details of how one can accomplish this task. Hence, the effective Polyakov loop potential, with an arbitrary
N_{c}
, can be expressed as:

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{eff}}\quantity( \mathcal{C}_{N_c} , T )}{T^{4}}=-\frac{1}{2}A\quantity(N_c, T )\sum_{i=1}^{\frac{N_{c}-1}{2}}\ell_{\mathrm{F},i}\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},i}+B\quantity( N_c, T )\ln\quantity[ H( \mathcal{C}_{N_c} ) ].

(60)

Here, we define the temperature and color dependent parameters
A\quantity(N_c, T )
and
B\quantity( N_c, T )
, in the same way as it was done for the temperature parameters
a(T)
and
b(T)
in Eqs. (
57
) and (
58
):

\displaystyle A\quantity(N_c, T )

\displaystyle=A_{0}(N_{c})+A_{1}(N_{c})\quantity[ \frac{T_0}{T} ]+A_{2}(N_{c})\quantity[ \frac{T_0}{T} ]^{2},

(61)

\displaystyle B\quantity( N_c, T )

\displaystyle=B_{3}(N_{c})\quantity[ \frac{T_0}{T} ]^{3}.

(62)

As before, the
A_{0}(N_{c})
parameter have to be fixed in such a way to recover the Steffan-Boltzmann limit for a gas of gluons with
\mathrm{SU}(N_{c})
symmetry. The remaining parameters must ensure that a second-order phase transition occurs at
T_{0}
at least for
N_{c}=3
. At higher values of
N_{c}
other behaviors might be expected
Lucini and Panero 2014
. We did not introduce a color dependence in the

parameter. Some lattice results, seem to indicate that

very weakly depends on the number of colors
Lucini et al. 2002
;
Lucini et al. 2004
;

;
Lucini and Panero 2014
. Indeed, in such studies, the deconfinement temperature

, as a function of the number of colors, was found to be,

\displaystyle T_{0}(N_{c})

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\sigma}\quantity(
\alpha_1 + \frac{ \alpha_2 }{ N_c^2 }
),

(63)

where,

is the

Lucini et al. 2002
;
Lucini et al. 2004

\alpha_{1}=0.582(15)
and
\alpha_{2}=0.43(13)

Liddle and Teper 2008

The exact functional dependencies, on the number of colors, of the different parameters present in the effective Polyakov loop potential, is beyond the scope of the present work. In order to find such dependencies, one can perform phenomenological studies involving results coming from lattice QCD with

We now turn our attention to evaluating the argument inside the logarithm of Eq. (

In the following, we show how one can write the argument of the logarithm in Eq. (

\displaystyle H(q_{1},q_{2},\ldots,q_{N_{c}})=\prod_{1\leq i<j}^{N_{c}}\absolutevalue{ \e^{ i q_i } - \e^{ i q_j } }^{2}.

\displaystyle=N_{c}\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix
1 & \Plrep{\Fund}{1} & \Plrep{\Fund}{2} & \dots& \Plrep{\Fund}{N_c-1} \\
\Plbarrep{\Fund}{1} & 1 & \Plrep{\Fund}{1} & \dots& \Plrep{\Fund}{N_c-2}\\
\Plbarrep{\Fund}{2} & \Plbarrep{\Fund}{1} & 1 & \dots& \Plrep{\Fund}{N_c-3}\\
\vdots& \vdots& \vdots& \ddots&\vdots\\
\Plbarrep{\Fund}{N_c-1} & \Plbarrep{\Fund}{N_c-2} & \Plbarrep{\Fund}{N_c-3} & \dots& 1
\endlx@physics@matrix).

(70)

Thus, the Haar measure is exclusively written in terms of Polyakov loops of different charges (and its adjoints), in the fundamental representation. It is given by:

\displaystyle H(\ell_{\mathrm{F},1},\ldots,\ell_{\mathrm{F},N_{c}-1},\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},1},\ldots,\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},N_{c}-1})

\displaystyle=\det\quantity[ W ].

(71)

Using the recursion formula in Eq. (
20
), it is possible to write the Haar measure above using only the set of Cartan-Polyakov loops,
\mathcal{C}_{N_{c}}
. Hence,
H(\ell_{\mathrm{F},1},\ldots,\ell_{\mathrm{F},N_{c}-1},\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},1},\ldots,\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},N_{c}-1})=H(\mathcal{C}_{N_{c}})
. For
N_{c}=3
, for example, we can write the Haar measure as:

\displaystyle H(\ell_{\mathrm{F},1},\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},1})

\displaystyle=27\matrixquantity|\lx@physics@matrix
1 & \Plrep{\Fund}{1} & \Plrep{\Fund}{2} \\
\Plbarrep{\Fund}{1} & 1 & \Plrep{\Fund}{1} \\
\Plbarrep{\Fund}{2} & \Plbarrep{\Fund}{1} & 1 \\
\endlx@physics@matrix|.

(72)

Using Eq. (
29
), we can write
\ell_{\mathrm{F},2}=\ell_{\mathrm{F},2}(\ell_{\mathrm{F},1},\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},1})
,
\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},2}=\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},2}(\ell_{\mathrm{F},1},\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},1})
and, after the evaluation of the determinant, obtain the same expression as the one inside the logarithm in Eq. (
56
). For our purposes, we can drop the overall constant
N_{c}^{N_{c}}
that arise when calculating the determinant of the matrix
W
. Since we are interested in the logarithm of the Haar measure, we can write:
\ln\quantity[N_c^{N_c} \qty(\ldots) ]=N_{c}\ln\quantity[N_c ]+\ln\quantity[ \ldots]
. The term
N_{c}\ln\quantity[N_c ]
can be absorbed in the overall normalization constant of the generating functional, leaving only the logarithm of the Haar measure, see Eq. (
43
). From this definition one can expect the complexity of the calculation to largely increase for increasing values of
N_{c}
, leading to polynomials with several interaction terms. For instance, for
N_{c}=5
, the Haar measure is given by:

\displaystyle H\quantity(
\Plrep{\Fund}{1},\Plrep{\Fund}{2}, \Plbarrep{\Fund}{1} , \Plbarrep{\Fund}{2}
)

\displaystyle=5^{5}\matrixquantity|\lx@physics@matrix
1 & \Plrep{\Fund}{1} & \Plrep{\Fund}{2} & \Plrep{\Fund}{3}( \mathcal{C}_{5} ) & \Plrep{\Fund}{4}( \mathcal{C}_{5} )
\\
\Plbarrep{\Fund}{1} & 1 & \Plrep{\Fund}{1} & \Plrep{\Fund}{2} & \Plrep{\Fund}{3}( \mathcal{C}_{5} )
\\
\Plbarrep{\Fund}{2} & \Plbarrep{\Fund}{1} & 1 & \Plrep{\Fund}{1} & \Plrep{\Fund}{2}
\\
\Plbarrep{\Fund}{3} ( \mathcal{C}_{5} ) & \Plbarrep{\Fund}{2} & \Plbarrep{\Fund}{1} & 1 & \Plrep{\Fund}{1}
\\
\Plbarrep{\Fund}{4} ( \mathcal{C}_{5} ) & \Plbarrep{\Fund}{3} ( \mathcal{C}_{5} ) & \Plbarrep{\Fund}{2} & \Plbarrep{\Fund}{1} & 1
\endlx@physics@matrix|.

(73)

Here,
\ell_{\mathrm{F},3}
,
\ell_{\mathrm{F},4}
,
\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},3}
and
\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},4}
, are non-Cartan-Polyakov loops that must be decomposed in terms of Cartan loops using the previously derived decomposition formulas, in particular, Eqs. (
33
) and (
34
). Written in terms of the Cartan-Polyakov loop set, the Haar measure for
N_{c}=5
has 106 different symmetry conserving interaction terms. For
N_{c}=7
and
N_{c}=9
this number increases to 3589 and 164856, respectively. The reason for this rapid increase in the number of terms can be traced back to combinatorics: increasing
N_{c}
increases the number of elements inside the Cartan set, which in turn, boosts the number of possible
\mathrm{Z}(N_{c})
invariant combinations of Cartan-Polyakov loops.

IV.1.3
The thermodynamic potential with an arbitrary
N_{c}

Using the results obtained so far, we are finally able to write a thermodynamic potential for the PNJL model, in the mean field approximation, for an arbitrary number of colors:

\displaystyle\Omega

\displaystyle\quantity(N_c,T,\mu)=U\quantity(N_c, \expval{\phi_i})+\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{eff}}\quantity( \mathcal{C}_{N_c} , T )-2N_{c}\tr\int_{\mathrm{reg}}\frac{\differential[3]{p}}{\quantity(2 \pi)^{3}}E

\displaystyle-2T\tr\int_{\mathrm{reg}}\frac{\differential[3]{p}}{\quantity(2 \pi)^{3}}\ln\quantity[
1
+
\sum_{k=1}^{\frac{N_c-1}{2}}
c_k ( -L_\Fund) \e^{ -k \beta\qty( E - {\mu} ) }
+
\sum_{k=\frac{N_c+1}{2}}^{{N_c}-1}
c_{N_c-k} ( -L_\Fund^\dagger) \e^{ -k\beta\qty( E - {\mu} ) }
+
\e^{ -N_c \beta\qty( E - {\mu} ) }
]

\displaystyle-2T\tr\int_{\mathrm{reg}}\frac{\differential[3]{p}}{\quantity(2 \pi)^{3}}\ln\quantity[
1
+
\sum_{k=1}^{\frac{N_c-1}{2}}
c_k ( -L_\Fund^\dagger) \e^{ -k \beta\qty( E + {\mu} ) }
+
\sum_{k=\frac{N_c+1}{2}}^{{N_c}-1}
c_{N_c-k} ( -L_\Fund) \e^{ -k\beta\qty( E + {\mu} ) }
+
\e^{ - N_c \beta\qty( E + {\mu} ) }
].

(74)

As already stated,
\mathcal{C}_{N_{c}}
, is the set of Cartan-Polyakov loops, the coefficients
c_{k}(-L_{\mathrm{F}})
and
c_{k}(-L_{\mathrm{F}}^{\dagger})
can be calculated with Eq. (
22
) and the effective Polyakov loop potential is given by Eq. (
60
). The mean field potential,
U\quantity(N_c, \expval{\phi_i})
, can also be
N_{c}
dependent. For instance, in the NJL model, the coupling of the four scalar-pseudoscalar quark-quark interaction can be considered as being inversely proportional to
N_{c}
due to the QCD counting rules
Ripka 1997
. As already discussed, the values of the mean fields,
\expectationvalue{\phi_i}
, and of the Cartan-Polyakov loops (elements of the
\mathcal{C}_{N_{c}}
set) can be obtained by deriving the gap equations of the model:
\derivative*{\Omega}{\expval{\phi_i}}=\derivative*{\Omega}{\mathcal{C}_{N_c}^i}=0
, where
\mathcal{C}_{N_{c}}^{i}
is the
i-
th element of the Cartan set.

It is important to note that the fermionic part of the thermodynamic potential written above, by construction, only depends on Cartan-Polyakov loops. However, the effective Polyakov loops potential,
\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{eff}}\quantity( \mathcal{C}_{N_c} , T )
, contains the Haar measure which depends explicitly on non-Cartan-Polyakov loops, in the fundamental representation, with charges up to
N_{c}-1
(see Eq. (
71
)). Of course, as already discussed, one can make use of the decomposition formula given in Eq. (
20
) (or Eq. (
25
)) in order to write the Haar measure in terms Cartan-Polyakov loops only. However, when dealing with the mean field approximation, the values of the Cartan-Polyakov loops are fixed by solving the gap equations,
\derivative*{\Omega}{\mathcal{C}_{N_c}^i}=0
. The derivative of the Haar measure with respect to an element of the Cartan-Polyakov loop set,
\ell_{\mathrm{F},k}
(the same can be applied to derivatives with respect to
\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},k}
), is:

\displaystyle\derivative{H}{ \Plrep{\Fund}{k} }=H\,\tr\quantity[
W
\dv{W}{ \Plrep{\Fund}{k} }
].

(75)

Here, we used Eq. (
71
) without the
N_{c}^{N_{c}}
coefficient and
\derivative*{W}{ \Plrep{\Fund}{k} }
is a tangent matrix, whose matrix elements are derivatives of Polyakov loops, in the fundamental representation, with charges
1,\ldots,N_{c}-1
, with respect to
\ell_{\mathrm{F},k}
(or
\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},k}
). Explicitly, it is given by:

\displaystyle\derivative{W}{ \Plrep{\Fund}{k} }=\matrixquantity(\lx@physics@matrix
0 & \dv{\Plrep{\Fund}{1}}{ \Plrep{\Fund}{k} } & \dv{\Plrep{\Fund}{2}}{ \Plrep{\Fund}{k} } & \dots& \dv{\Plrep{\Fund}{N_c-1}}{ \Plrep{\Fund}{k} } \\
\dv{\Plbarrep{\Fund}{1}}{ \Plrep{\Fund}{k} } & 0 & \dv{\Plrep{\Fund}{1}}{ \Plrep{\Fund}{k} } & \dots& \dv{\Plrep{\Fund}{N_c-2}}{ \Plrep{\Fund}{k} }\\
\dv{\Plbarrep{\Fund}{2}}{ \Plrep{\Fund}{k} } & \dv{\Plbarrep{\Fund}{1}}{ \Plrep{\Fund}{k} } & 0 & \dots& \dv{\Plrep{\Fund}{N_c-3}}{ \Plrep{\Fund}{k} }\\
\vdots& \vdots& \vdots& \ddots&\vdots\\
\dv{\Plbarrep{\Fund}{N_c-1}}{ \Plrep{\Fund}{k} } & \dv{\Plbarrep{\Fund}{N_c-2}}{ \Plrep{\Fund}{k} } & \dv{\Plbarrep{\Fund}{N_c-3}}{ \Plrep{\Fund}{k} } & \dots& 0
\endlx@physics@matrix).

(76)

When differentiating a Cartan-Polyakov loop, the result is a delta function. However, the matrix above contains non-Cartan-Polyakov loops with charges in the interval
(N_{c}+1)/2,\ldots,N_{c}-1
. For these cases one can calculate these derivatives using:

\displaystyle\derivative{\Plrep{\Fund}{a}}{ \Plrep{\Fund}{b} }

\displaystyle=\partialderivative{\Plrep{\Fund}{a}}{ \Plrep{\Fund}{b} }+\sum_{i=\frac{N_{c}+1}{2}}^{a-1}\quantity(
\pdv{\Plrep{\Fund}{a}}{ \Plrep{\Fund}{i} }
)\quantity(
\dv{\Plrep{\Fund}{i}}{ \Plrep{\Fund}{b} }
),

(77)

\displaystyle\derivative{\Plrep{\Fund}{a}}{ \Plbarrep{\Fund}{b} }

\displaystyle=\partialderivative{\Plrep{\Fund}{a}}{ \Plbarrep{\Fund}{b} }+\sum_{i=\frac{N_{c}+1}{2}}^{a-1}\quantity(
\pdv{\Plrep{\Fund}{a}}{ \Plrep{\Fund}{i} }
)\quantity(
\dv{\Plrep{\Fund}{i}}{ \Plbarrep{\Fund}{b} }
),

(78)

\displaystyle\derivative{\Plbarrep{\Fund}{a}}{ \Plrep{\Fund}{b} }

\displaystyle=\partialderivative{\Plbarrep{\Fund}{a}}{ \Plrep{\Fund}{b} }+\sum_{i=\frac{N_{c}+1}{2}}^{a-1}\quantity(
\pdv{\Plbarrep{\Fund}{a}}{ \Plbarrep{\Fund}{i} }
)\quantity(
\dv{\Plbarrep{\Fund}{i}}{ \Plrep{\Fund}{b} }
),

(79)

\displaystyle\derivative{\Plbarrep{\Fund}{a}}{ \Plbarrep{\Fund}{b} }

\displaystyle=\partialderivative{\Plbarrep{\Fund}{a}}{ \Plbarrep{\Fund}{b} }+\sum_{i=\frac{N_{c}+1}{2}}^{a-1}\quantity(
\pdv{\Plbarrep{\Fund}{a}}{ \Plbarrep{\Fund}{i} }
)\quantity(
\dv{\Plbarrep{\Fund}{i}}{ \Plbarrep{\Fund}{b} }
).

(80)

For more details see Appendix
B
where all the partial derivatives that are necessary in the above equations are calculated, for any representation,
\mathcal{R}
. With this result one can write the gap equations of the PNJL model for any value of
N_{c}
.

This model can be used, as is, to study the phase diagram of the PNJL model for increasing values of
N_{c}
. Numerically one can define all the necessary quantities, impose the gap equations and calculate all thermodynamics functions of interest for a given value of
N_{c}
. Of course, apart from the increase in the number of degrees of freedom, increasing
N_{c}
means that the largest matrix in
c_{k}(-L_{\mathrm{F}})
or
c_{k}(-L_{\mathrm{F}}^{\dagger})
, has dimensions
(N_{c}-1)/2\times(N_{c}-1)/2
. Thus, naturally, the complexity of the numerical calculation, using the thermodynamic potential of Eq. (
74
), gets larger for larger values of
N_{c}
. Also, Eq. (
74
) can be a good starting point to write down a thermodynamic potential for the large
N_{c}
limit. Such potential could then be used to study the phase diagram of strongly interacting matter at large
N_{c}
, and the quarkyonic phase of matter. We leave both aforementioned studies, the numerical analysis and the large
-N_{c}
model, as future research endeavors.

We would like to highlight that the formalism employed here, to define the PNJL for different values of
N_{c}
, can also be applied to other fermionic models as long as the thermodynamic potential can be written in a similar way to the one given in Eq. (
44
).

IV.2
Glue effective potential with an arbitrary
N_{c}

In this section we apply the concept of Cartan-Polyakov loops to extend the one-loop gluon effective potential to arbitrary values of
N_{c}
. The starting point is the usual one-loop contribution to an effective potential of gluons with a spatially uniform Polyakov loop
Meisinger and Ogilvie 2002
;
Meisinger et al. 2002
;
Sasaki and Redlich 2012
;
Megias et al. 2014
:

\displaystyle\Omega_{g}(N_{c},T)=2T\int_{\mathrm{reg}}\frac{\differential[3]{p}}{\quantity(2 \pi)^{3}}\quantity{
\beta N_A E_g
+
\ln\det\qty[
1 - L_\Adj\e^{ -\beta E_g }
]
}.

(81)

Here,
N_{\mathrm{A}}=N_{c}^{2}-1
, is the dimension of the adjoint representation,
E_{g}=\sqrt{p^{2}+M_{g}^{2}}
(
M_{g}
is a phenomenological gluon effective mass
Sasaki and Redlich 2012
;
Megias et al. 2014
) and,
L_{A}
is the thermal Wilson line in the adjoint representation of the
\mathrm{SU}(N_{c})
group (see Eq. (
4
)). This contribution to the effective glue potential drives the system to a state of spontaneously broken
\mathrm{Z}(N_{c})
symmetry
Lo et al. 2021
. In order to study the transition from a
\mathrm{Z}(N_{c})
symmetric state to the broken state, one can consider a confining contribution to the potential in the form of the Haar measure
Lo et al. 2021
which, as previously discussed, can be written in terms of the Cartan-Polyakov loop set,
\mathcal{C}_{N_{c}}
. Hence, the total effective glue potential,
\Omega(N_{c},T)
, is given by:

\displaystyle\Omega(N_{c},T)=k(N_{c},T)\ln\quantity[ H( \mathcal{C}_{N_c} ) ]+\Omega_{g}(N_{c},T).

(82)

Here,
H(\mathcal{C}_{N_{c}})
is the Haar measure and
k(N_{c},T)
is a phenomenological parameter, similar to the one introduced for the PNJL model with an arbitrary
N_{c}
(see the discussion in Section
IV.1.2
). The effective glue potential defined above was also derived, for the
N_{c}=3
case, in Ref.
Sasaki and Redlich 2012
where the background field approximation was applied to the generating functional of the
\mathrm{SU}(3)
Yang-Mills theory, in the presence of a uniform gluon field. This model was also considered in Ref.
Lo et al. 2021
, in order to study fluctuations of the order parameter in an
\mathrm{SU}(N_{c})
effective model.

The strategy to extend this model will be the same as the one used for the PNJL model. The confining part of the potential, written in Eq. (
82
), was previously defined for an arbitrary number of colors (see Eqs. (
60
) and (
71
)). Indeed, in Section
IV.1.2
we discussed how to write the Haar measure using exclusively the elements which constitute the Cartan-Polyakov loop set. The final step is to evaluate the gluonic determinant, present in Eq. (
81
), for an arbitrary number of colors. The gluonic determinant is defined by:

\displaystyle\det\quantity[ 1 - L_\Adj\e^{ -\beta E_g } ]=h_{g}^{N_{\mathrm{A}}}\det\quantity[ h_g^{-1} I - L_\Adj].

(83)

Like in the case of the fermionic determinant, in the above, we defined the non-zero quantity,
h_{g}
, as:

\displaystyle h_{g}=\mathrm{e}^{-\beta E_{g}}.

(84)

As done for the fermionic determinant, the goal is to calculate the gluonic determinant for an arbitrary
N_{c}
using only the corresponding Cartan-Polyakov loop set. To this end, consider the characteristic polynomial
p\quantity( L_\Adj, h_g^{-1} )
(see Eq. (
10
)). We can write the gluonic determinant as:

\displaystyle h_{g}^{N_{\mathrm{A}}}\det\quantity[ h_g^{-1} I - L_\Adj]

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{k=1}^{N_{c}^{2}-2}c_{k}(L_{\mathrm{A}})h_{g}^{k}+\quantity(-1)^{(N_{c}^{2}-1)}h_{g}^{(N_{c}^{2}-1)}.

(85)

Where we used the fact that
\det\quantity[L_\Adj]=1
. The coefficients,
c_{k}(L_{\mathrm{A}})
, which arise in the sum, are calculated using Eq. (
11
) with the appropriate choice of representation. Observing the structure of the matrix
J_{\mathrm{A},k}
, defined in Eq. (
12
), one can conclude that the
k
coefficient will depend on the non-Cartan-Polyakov loops
\ell_{\mathrm{A},k}
, with charges
1,\ldots,k
. Since we want to write the model in terms of Cartan-Polyakov loops, we must find a way to write the non-Cartan-Polyakov loop of charges
k
, in the adjoint representation,
\ell_{\mathrm{A},k}
, in terms of Cartan-Polyakov loops. In order to accomplish this one can use some results from group theory.

A known result from representation theory allows for the decomposition of the product between the fundamental and the conjugate representations of the
\mathrm{SU}(N_{c})
group (
\mathrm{F}
and
\overline{\mathrm{F}}
, respectively) with its adjoint and the singlet representations
Elvang et al. 2003
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{F}\otimes\overline{\mathrm{F}}=\mathrm{A}\oplus 1.

(86)

This result can be proven using Young tableaux or by sandwiching the fundamental and anti-fundamental thermal Wilson lines between two
\mathrm{SU}(N_{c})
generators in order to get an object with adjoint indexes
Dumitru et al. 2004
. Since powers of the thermal Wilson line,
L^{k}
, are elements of the
\mathrm{SU}(N_{c})
group, we can write the following identities using character theory
Megias et al. 2014
:

\displaystyle\tr\quantity[ L_{\rho\oplus\sigma}^k ]

\displaystyle=\tr\quantity[ L_{\rho}^k ]+\tr\quantity[ L_{\sigma}^k ],

(87)

\displaystyle\tr\quantity[ L_{\rho\otimes\sigma}^k ]

\displaystyle=\tr\quantity[ L_{\rho}^k ]\tr\quantity[ L_{\sigma}^k ].

(88)

Where
\rho
and
\sigma
are different representations of the
\mathrm{SU}(N_{c})
group. Using these properties, alongside the decomposition made in Eq. (
86
), one can write:

\displaystyle\tr[L_{\mathrm{F}\otimes\overline{\mathrm{F}}}^{k}\big]

\displaystyle=\tr\quantity[ L_{ \Adj\oplus 1 }^k ]\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\tr[L_{\mathrm{F}}^{k}\big]\tr[{L_{\mathrm{F}}^{\dagger}}^{k}\big]

\displaystyle=\tr[L_{\mathrm{A}}^{k}\big]+1\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle N_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\ell_{\mathrm{F},k}\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},k}

\displaystyle=N_{\mathrm{A}}\ell_{\mathrm{A},k}+1.

(89)

Thus, one can write the Polyakov loop with charge
k
, in the adjoint representation, in terms of Polyakov loops with charge
k
, in the fundamental and antifundamental representations. Manipulating the equation above yields
Dumitru et al. 2004
;
Fukushima and Skokov 2017
:

\displaystyle\ell_{\mathrm{A},k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-1/N_{c}^{2}}\ell_{\mathrm{F},k}\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},k}-\frac{1}{N_{c}^{2}-1}.

(90)

As mentioned for the non-Cartan loops in the fundamental representation, this relation can also be checked by hand, by considering a particular gauge choice, e.g., using the already mentioned Polyakov loop gauge
Sasaki and Redlich 2012
.

With this result, one can readily write the gluonic determinant as a function of fundamental and anti-fundamental Polyakov loops of different charges. Moreover, recursively, one can use the non-Cartan-Polyakov loop decomposition formula of Eq. (
20
) for loops with charge
k<N_{c}
and Eq. (
28
) for loops with charge
k\geq N_{c}
, in order to write the gluonic determinant of Eq. (
85
) in terms of Cartan-Polyakov loops only. We point out that, just like for the derivation of the non-Cartan decomposition formulas, or the calculation of fermionic determinant, one can make use of the identity between the characteristic polynomial coefficients of unitary matrices, written in Eq. (
18
) and shown in Appendix
A
. Making use of this identity implies that only
(N_{c}^{2}-1)/2
different coefficients have to be calculated, with the remaining ones arising automatically. For
N_{c}=3
, there are a total of seven coefficients, but only four have to be calculated. Explicitly, for
N_{c}=3
, one gets:

\displaystyle c_{1}(L_{\mathrm{A}})=c_{7}(L_{\mathrm{A}})

\displaystyle=-8\ell_{\mathrm{A},1}

\displaystyle=1-9\ell_{\mathrm{F},1}\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},1},

(91)

\displaystyle c_{2}(L_{\mathrm{A}})=c_{6}(L_{\mathrm{A}})

\displaystyle=32\ell_{\mathrm{A},1}^{2}-4\ell_{\mathrm{A},2}

\displaystyle=1-27(\ell_{\mathrm{F},1}\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},1}-\ell_{\mathrm{F},1}^{3}-\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},1}^{3}),

(92)

\displaystyle c_{3}(L_{\mathrm{A}})=c_{5}(L_{\mathrm{A}})

\displaystyle=-\frac{8}{3}\quantity(
32 \Plrep{\Adj}{1}^3
- 12 \Plrep{\Adj}{1} \Plrep{\Adj}{2} + \Plrep{\Adj}{3}
)

\displaystyle=-81\ell_{\mathrm{F},1}^{2}\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},1}^{2}+27\ell_{\mathrm{F},1}\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},1}-2,

(93)

\displaystyle c_{4}(L_{\mathrm{A}})

\displaystyle=\frac{8}{3}\quantity(64 \Plrep{\Adj}{1}^4 - 48 \Plrep{\Adj}{2} \Plrep{\Adj}{1}^2 + 8 \Plrep{\Adj}{1} \Plrep{\Adj}{3} + 3 \Plrep{\Adj}{2}^2
)-2\ell_{\mathrm{A},4}

\displaystyle=162\ell_{\mathrm{F},1}^{2}\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},1}^{2}+18\ell_{\mathrm{F},1}\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},1}-54\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},1}^{3}-54\ell_{\mathrm{F},1}^{3}-2.

(94)

Here, we have written the coefficients first in terms of charged Polyakov loops in the adjoint representation and then used Eq. (
90
) alongside the non-Cartan decomposition formulas, in order to write the coefficients in terms of Cartan-Polyakov loops. These results agree with the ones present in the literature, see Ref.
Sasaki and Redlich 2012
. For
N_{c}=5
, one has to calculate twelve coefficients, the first three are given by:

\displaystyle c_{1}(L_{\mathrm{A}})=c_{23}(L_{\mathrm{A}})

\displaystyle=-24\ell_{\mathrm{A},1}

\displaystyle=1-25\ell_{\mathrm{F},1}\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},1},

(95)

\displaystyle c_{2}(L_{\mathrm{A}})=c_{22}(L_{\mathrm{A}})

\displaystyle=288\ell_{\mathrm{A},1}^{2}-12\ell_{\mathrm{A},2}

\displaystyle=1+\frac{625}{2}\ell_{\mathrm{F},1}^{2}\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},1}^{2}-25\ell_{\mathrm{F},1}\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},1}-\frac{25}{2}\ell_{\mathrm{F},2}\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},2},

(96)

\displaystyle c_{3}(L_{\mathrm{A}})=c_{21}(L_{\mathrm{A}})

\displaystyle=-8\quantity(
288 \Plrep{\Adj}{1}^3 - 36 \Plrep{\Adj}{1} \Plrep{\Adj}{2} + \Plrep{\Adj}{3}
)

\displaystyle=1-\frac{15625}{4}\ell_{\mathrm{F},1}^{3}\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},1}^{3}+\frac{3125}{4}\ell_{\mathrm{F},1}^{3}\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},1}\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},2}-\frac{625}{4}\ell_{\mathrm{F},1}^{2}\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},1}^{2}+\frac{375}{4}\ell_{\mathrm{F},1}^{2}\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{3125}{4}\ell_{\mathrm{F},1}\ell_{\mathrm{F},2}\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},1}^{3}-25\ell_{\mathrm{F},1}\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},1}-\frac{625}{4}\ell_{\mathrm{F},1}\ell_{\mathrm{F},2}\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},1}\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},2}+\frac{375}{4}\ell_{\mathrm{F},2}\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},1}^{2}+\frac{375}{4}\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},1}\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},2}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{3125}{4}\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},1}^{5}-625\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},1}^{3}\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},2}-\frac{125}{4}\ell_{\mathrm{F},2}\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},2}+\frac{3125}{4}\ell_{\mathrm{F},1}^{5}-625\ell_{\mathrm{F},1}^{3}\ell_{\mathrm{F},2}+\frac{375}{4}\ell_{\mathrm{F},1}\ell_{\mathrm{F},2}^{2}.

(97)

One can immediately observe that the complexity of the coefficients, as expressions written in terms of Cartan-Polyakov loops, rapidly increases for larger numbers of colors, as well as, for higher order coefficients for a fixed
N_{c}
. Indeed, the
c_{12}(L_{\mathrm{A}})
coefficient, for example, has 104 different terms when written in terms of the Cartan-Polyakov loop set. In practice, for numerical calculations, the overall size of the analytical expressions for a given coefficient at fixed
N_{c}
is not important. In such approach, using as variables the Cartan-Polyakov loops, one can build all non-Cartan-Polyakov loops using the decomposition formulas, make use of Eq. (
90
) to directly obtain different charged Polyakov loops in the adjoint representation and then get the coefficients.

We point out that, since we are dealing with a gluonic theory, the coefficients only have terms which preserve
\mathrm{Z}(N_{c})
symmetry. Naturally, the opposite was found for the fermionic coefficients where the terms explicitly broke the
\mathrm{Z}(N_{c})
symmetry.

Finally, one can write the glue effective potential defined in Eq. (
81
), for any value of
N_{c}
, using only Cartan-Polyakov loops. It is explicitly given by:

\displaystyle\Omega(N_{c},T)

\displaystyle=k(N_{c},T)\ln\quantity[ H( \mathcal{C}_{N_c} ) ]+2\quantity(N_c^2-1)\int_{\mathrm{reg}}\frac{\differential[3]{p}}{\quantity(2 \pi)^{3}}E_{g}

\displaystyle+2T\int_{\mathrm{reg}}\frac{\differential[3]{p}}{\quantity(2 \pi)^{3}}\ln\quantity[
1
+
\sum_{k=1}^{ N_c^2-2 }
c_k ( L_\Adj)
\e^{ - k \beta E_g }
+
\qty(-1)^{ (N_c^2-1) }
\e^{ -(N_c^2-1) \beta E_g }
].

(98)

It is known that the mean field approximation is not suitable to study gluon effective potentials written in terms of fundamental and anti-fundamental Polyakov loops
Zhang et al. 2010
. The reason for such can be understood in the basis of the relation between the adjoint and the fundamental loops, written in Eq. (
90
). In the naive mean field approximation, considered in this work, one can see that in the confined phase, where the expectation values of charged Polyakov loops in the fundamental representation are zero, the expectation values of charged adjoint Polyakov loops are negative
Zhang et al. 2010
. As pointed out in Ref.
Fukushima and Skokov 2017
, this implies that charged Polyakov loops in the adjoint representation,
\ell_{\mathrm{A},k}
, cannot be used as an order parameters for gluon confinement. Also, the thermodynamic potential obtained in Eq. (
98
) leads to a negative pressure, negative entropy density and thus, to thermodynamic instability
Zhang et al. 2010
;
Sasaki and Redlich 2012
. This was discussed in Ref.
Sasaki and Redlich 2012
, using the glue model of Eq. (
81
) for
N_{c}=3
, by considering the low temperature expansion of the effective action. In such limit, the last term inside the logarithm,
\quantity(-1)^{(N_{c}^{2}-1)}\exp\quantity[ -(N_c^2-1) \beta E_g ]
, prevails and the incorrect sign for Bose statistics in front of the exponential is obtained for
N_{c}=3

Sasaki and Redlich 2012
. To overcome this feature of the glue effective potential for
N_{c}=3
, an hybrid model of glueballs in the confined phase and Polyakov loops in the deconfined phase was suggested, with the transition between models occurring at the transition temperature. For further details see Ref.
Sasaki and Redlich 2012
. We point out an interesting feature of the arbitrary
N_{c}
effective potential written in Eq. (
98
): the incorrect Bose statistics sign is obtained for and odd number of colors while, for an even number of colors, the sign is the one expected for a system of bosons.

Although one can use the approach used here to extended glue effective potentials to arbitrary values of
N_{c}
, as discussed above, the simple mean field approach might not be sufficient and corrections coming from connected diagrams in Eq. (
90
), might be necessary
Fukushima and Skokov 2017
. However, at large
N_{c}
, these corrections become negligible since expectation values factorize
Dumitru et al. 2004
. Thus, in this limit, the relation between the adjoint loops of charge
k
and the fundamental and anti-fundamental loops of charge
k
become much simpler:

\displaystyle\ell_{\mathrm{A},k}

\displaystyle\sim\ell_{\mathrm{F},k}\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},k}+O\quantity( \frac{1}{N_c} ).

(99)

In fact, at large
N_{c}
, the expectation value of any Polyakov loop can be written as powers of the expectation value of the fundamental and anti-fundamental Polyakov loops
Dumitru et al. 2004
. Hence, at large
N_{c}
, this model is a proper tool to study the behavior of the deconfining transition of strongly interacting matter.

As a final note, we point out that this model can be used to extend the PNJL model with an arbitrary
N_{c}
obtained earlier in this work. For instance, instead of using the Haar measure and the kinetic term proposed in Eq. (
59
) for the effective Polyakov loop potential in the PNJL model, one could use the above effective glue potential. Indeed, one can show that, for
N_{c}=3
, under certain assumptions, it is possible to recover from Eq. (
98
) the
-\overline{\Phi}\Phi
used in the usual
N_{c}=3
PNJL model, which is essential in order to get a first-order phase transition
Sasaki and Redlich 2012
. Not only that, using this effective glue potential, for the gluonic sector of PNJL model, would remove the necessity of defining so many
N_{c}-
dependent phenomenological parameters present in the effective Polyakov loop potential (see Eq. (
60
)). In such case, the only parameter coming from the gluonic sector would be the one in front of the Haar measure
Sasaki and Redlich 2012
. Of course, the problem of the negative contribution to the pressure in the confined phase, for
\ell_{\mathrm{F},k}=0
, would have to be addressed
Sasaki and Redlich 2012
;
Lo et al. 2021
. This issue is partially resolved in Ref.
Lo et al. 2021
by incorporating, in the thermodynamic potential, a contribution coming from the ghost fields. Such contribution reinforces the confining part of the potential, opposing the gluonic (deconfining) part and it might be able to overturn the negative tendency of the gluonic pressure in the confined phase
Lo et al. 2021
. In the same work, it is discussed a phenomenological solution to this problem for
N_{c}=3
: subtract, from the glue effective potential, its contribution at
\ell_{\mathrm{F},1}=\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},1}=0
. Employing this recipe does not change the results for the Polyakov loops, but requires one to correct the number of gluonic degrees of freedom at large temperatures. Naturally, for other values of
N_{c}
, this phenomenological approach would also work, albeit one would have to subtract the potential at
\ell_{\mathrm{F},k}=\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},k}=0
, with
k=1,\ldots,(N_{c}-1)/2
, i.e., considering all elements of the Cartan-Polyakov loop set to vanish. To explore additional works that address and offer solutions to this problem, beyond the ones already mentioned, see Refs.
Tsai and Muller 2009
;
Ruggieri et al. 2012
;
Alba et al. 2014
;
Islam et al. 2022
.

V
Conclusions

In this work we defined Cartan-Polyakov loops, a special subset of Polyakov loops in the fundamental representation of the
\mathrm{SU}(N_{c})
group which, under
\mathrm{Z}(N_{c})
transformations, behave like fields with charges
k=1,\ldots,(N_{c}-1)/2
. This set can be used as independent degrees of freedom to parametrize effective models of quarks and gluons with any number of colors. When building such phenomenological models, non-Cartan-Polyakov loops, Polyakov loops with charges higher than
(N_{c}-1)/2
, naturally arise. In order to write these models as functions of the loops contained in the Cartan set, we derived a decomposition formula for non-Cartan-Polyakov loops. Such formula was derived using properties of the characteristic polynomial of unitary matrices and the Cayley-Hamilton theorem. Fixing some values of
N_{c}
, we consistently decomposed some non-Cartan loops until they were only functions of Cartan-Polyakov loops. Finally, we showed how to build two distinct effective models for any
N_{c}
, including a version of the PNJL model and an effective glue potential. These models were built using the naive mean field approximation commonly used when dealing with the PNJL model: the Cartan-Polyakov loops were directly substituted by their respective expectation values and the expectation values of products between them factorized. We highlight that the potentials presented in this work, were derived using a particular gauge choice and/or required some phenomenological parameters (see Eq. (
74
) for the thermodynamic potential of the PNJL model and Eq. (
98
) for the glue effective potential, both written for an arbitrary
N_{c}
). For instance, the number of colors and temperature dependent prefactors
B\quantity( N_c, T )
and
k(N_{c},T)
, in Eqs. (
60
) and (
82
) respectively, were included because of phenomenological considerations. They were not derived from pure gauge theory.

The formalism developed in this work, allows for a consistent study on the effect of increasing the number of colors in effective models of quarks and gluons. Numerically, one can build tools that, for a given
N_{c}
, automatically builds the Cartan-Polyakov loop set and calculate all the necessary non-Cartan-Polyakov loops from it. In the case of the PNJL model, for example, one can build numerical tools in order to solve the model, within the mean field approximation, for a fixed value of
N_{c}
. Having the solution of such model, one can analyze the phase diagram of the model for that particular value of
N_{c}
, especially the behavior of the critical endpoint and the interplay between the chiral transition and the deconfinement transition. One can then repeat the calculation to increasing values of
N_{c}
and study the behavior of the critical endpoint for large values of
N_{c}
, as well as study the conjectured quarkyonic phase of matter at high chemical potentials and moderate temperatures. Indeed, in Ref.
Kovács et al. 2022
, the fate of the critical endpoint at large
N_{c}
was studied using a Polyakov loop quark-meson model by varying the number of colors. Within this work it was also confirmed the existence of the quarkyonic phase, in which chiral symmetry is restored but quarks are still confined
Kovács et al. 2022
. It would be very interesting to see if similar features were obtained within the model proposed in this work. In the case of the glue effective model defined in this work, it can be used to study the fluctuations of the confined-deconfined order parameters, given by different susceptibilities of the Polyakov loops, and the curvature masses associated with the Cartan-Polyakov loop set, for any value of
N_{c}
. These observables can be extremely useful tools to study the deconfinement phase transition in the Yang-Mills theory
Lo et al. 2013b
, providing useful information about the link between the structure of the vacuum and gluon properties
Lo et al. 2021
. For example, in Ref.
Lo et al. 2013b
, the susceptibilities of the modulus, the real part and the imaginary part of the Polyakov loop, in the fundamental representation, were calculated in
\mathrm{SU}(3)
lattice gauge theory (for studies including quark fields see Refs.
Lo et al. 2013a
;
Bazavov et al. 2016
;
Clarke et al. 2020
). In that work the authors were able to show that different ratios of these susceptibilities are independent of the renormalization of the Polyakov and that these ratios depend weakly on the system size. Additionally, from the point of view of the phase transition, in the same work, it was shown that these ratios are discontinuous at the phase transition point and that above and below this point they are almost temperature independent. For other studies involving the susceptibilities of the Polyakov loop, see Refs.
Lo et al. 2013a
;
Lo et al. 2014
;
Miura et al. 2017
. A similar study carried out for larger values of
N_{c}
, using
\mathrm{SU}(N_{c})
lattice gauge theory, might be extremely useful in order to verify the relevance of the Cartan-Polyakov loop set. In such study one can evaluate different observables in the form of different ratios of different elements of the Cartan-Polyakov loop set. For instance, for
N_{c}=5
, the Cartan-Polyakov set is
\mathcal{C}_{5}=\quantity{ \Plrep{\Fund}{1}, \Plrep{\Fund}{2}, \Plbarrep{\Fund}{1}, \Plbarrep{\Fund}{2} }
and the ratio of the modulus, and real and imaginary parts of distinct elements of the set, as a function of temperature, can be analyzed.

The Cartan-Polyakov loop set can also be relevant to study the so-called Casimir scaling hypothesis. For Polyakov loops, this hypothesis states that there is a scaling relation between the Polyakov loops in different representations of the
\mathrm{SU}(N_{c})
group
Gupta et al. 2008
;
Abuki and Fukushima 2009
;
Megias et al. 2014
. The Casimir hypothesis is not only applicable to Polyakov loops but also to other observables
Mykkanen et al. 2012
. For values of
N_{c}>3
, it can be insightful to understand if the hypothesis holds for all elements of the Cartan-Polyakov loop set, besides the
\ell_{\mathrm{F},1}
and
\overline{\ell}_{\mathrm{F},1}
. For some works where the Casimir scaling hypothesis is discussed and studied, see Refs.
Bali 2000
;
Gupta et al. 2008
;
Abuki and Fukushima 2009
;
Megias et al. 2014
;
Petreczky and Schadler 2015
;
Petreczky and Schadler 2015
;
Mykkanen et al. 2012
.

As future work, we plan to use the formalism developed in this paper to build numerical tools which are prepared to solve the PNJL model for any given
N_{c}
, with the goal of studying the behavior of the critical endpoint with increasing
N_{c}
. As pointed out, this can be accomplished recursively without further approximations, except the ones introduced in this work. At the same time, we will build an effective PNJL model at large
N_{c}
. In this limit, we plan to study the phase diagram of the model and the properties of the expected quarkyonic phase at large baryon densities. Having a numerical calculation together with a large
N_{c}
approximation will allow us to compare both approaches and better understand the behavior of strongly interacting matter at large
N_{c}
.

\qty[ J_{\Rep,a} ]^{-1}
\pdv{ J_{\Rep,a} }{ \Plrep{\Rep}{b} }
].

(116)

Likewise, one can calculate the other partial derivatives. Summarizing, one gets:

\displaystyle\left.\partialderivative{\Plbarrep{\Rep}{a}}{ \Plbarrep{\Rep}{b} }\right|_{a<N_{\mathcal{R}}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{a}{a!}\quantity(
\qty(-1)^{b-a}
\det\qty[ \bar{J}_{\Rep,a}^{(b,1)}]
+
\sum_{i=1}^{a-1}
\qty(-1)^{i-a}
\Plbarrep{\Rep}{i}
\det\qty[ \bar{J}_{\Rep,a}^{(i,1)}]
\tr\qty[
\qty[ \bar{J}_{\Rep,a}^{(i,1)} ]^{-1}
\pdv{ \bar{J}_{\Rep,a}^{(i,1)} }{ \Plbarrep{\Rep}{b} }
]
),

(117)

\displaystyle\left.\partialderivative{\Plbarrep{\Rep}{a}}{ \Plbarrep{\Rep}{b} }\right|_{a\geq N_{\mathcal{R}}}

\displaystyle=\quantity(-1)^{N_{\mathcal{R}}+1}\delta_{b,a-N_{\mathcal{R}}}-\sum_{i=1}^{{N_{\mathcal{R}}}-1}\delta_{b,a-i}c_{i}(L_{\mathcal{R}}^{\dagger})-\sum_{i=1}^{{N_{\mathcal{R}}}-1}c_{i}(L_{\mathcal{R}}^{\dagger})\overline{\ell}_{\mathcal{R},a-i}\tr\quantity[
\qty[ \bar{J}_{\Rep,a} ]^{-1}
\pdv{ \bar{J}_{\Rep,a} }{ \Plbarrep{\Rep}{b} }
],

(118)

\displaystyle\left.\partialderivative{\Plbarrep{\Rep}{a}}{ \Plrep{\Rep}{b} }\right|_{a<N_{\mathcal{R}}}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{a+1}\frac{a}{N_{\mathcal{R}}\quantity(N_\Rep-a)!}\det\quantity[ J_{\Rep,N_\Rep-a} ]\tr\quantity[
\qty[ J_{\Rep,N_\Rep-a} ]^{-1}
\pdv{ J_{\Rep,N_\Rep-a} }{ \Plrep{\Rep}{b} }
],

(119)

\displaystyle\left.\partialderivative{\Plbarrep{\Rep}{a}}{ \Plrep{\Rep}{b} }\right|_{a\geq N_{\mathcal{R}}}

\displaystyle=0,

(120)

\displaystyle\left.\partialderivative{\Plrep{\Rep}{a}}{ \Plbarrep{\Rep}{b} }\right|_{a<N_{\mathcal{R}}}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{a+1}\frac{a}{N_{\mathcal{R}}\quantity(N_\Rep-a)!}\det\quantity[ \bar{J}_{\Rep,N_\Rep-a} ]\tr\quantity[
\qty[ \bar{J}_{\Rep,N_\Rep-a} ]^{-1}
\pdv{ \bar{J}_{\Rep,N_\Rep-a} }{ \Plbarrep{\Rep}{b} }
],

(121)

\displaystyle\left.\partialderivative{\Plrep{\Rep}{a}}{ \Plbarrep{\Rep}{b} }\right|_{a\geq N_{\mathcal{R}}}

\displaystyle=0.

(122)

With these tools one is able to evaluate the total derivatives of quantities which depend on non-Cartan-Polyakov loops, as is the case with the Haar measure (see Eq. (
71
)). So, one can readily evaluate the derivatives of the Haar measure with respect to the elements of the Cartan-Polyakov loop set and obtain the gap equations of the PNJL model for any value of
N_{c}
.

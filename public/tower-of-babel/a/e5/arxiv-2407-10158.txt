Formulas for the h h -mass on 1 1 -currents with coefficients in ℝ m \mathbb{R}^{m}
Julius Lohmann, Bernhard Schmitzer, Benedikt Wirth

Abstract
We consider the minimization of the h h -mass over normal 1 1 -currents in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} with coefficients in ℝ m \mathbb{R}^{m} and prescribed boundary. This optimization is known as multi-material transport problem and used in the context of logistics of multiple commodities, but also as a relaxation of nonconvex optimal transport tasks such as so-called branched transport problems. The h h -mass with norm h h can be defined in different ways, resulting in three functionals ℳ h , | ⋅ | H \mathcal{M}_{h},|\cdot|_{H} , and 𝕄 h \mathbb{M}_{h} , whose equality is the main result of this article: ℳ h \mathcal{M}_{h} is a functional on 1 1 -currents in the spirit of Federer and Fleming, norm | ⋅ | H |\cdot|_{H} denotes the total variation of a Radon measure with respect to H H induced by h h , and 𝕄 h \mathbb{M}_{h} is a mass on flat 1 1 -chains in the sense of Whitney. On top we introduce a new and improved notion of calibrations for the multi-material transport problem: w e identify calibrations with (weak) Jacobians of optimizers of the associated convex dual problem, which yields their existence and natural regularity.

1.1
Multi-material transport

The multi-material transport problem was introduced (with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}^{m}
) in
[
20
]
and generalized (to coefficients in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
) in
[
19

It is the

m

These distributions must of course admit a connecting flux.

Definition 1.1

(Source and sink distribution)
.

An admissible
source and sink distribution
is a compactly supported
\mathbb{R}^{m}
-valued Radon measure
F_{0}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}

such that
F_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=0
or equivalently there exists some normal
1
-current
F_{1}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with coefficients in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
\partial F_{1}=F_{0}
.

A source and sink distribution
F_{0}
can be written
F_{0}=\mu_{-}-\mu_{+}
(Hahn decomposition theorem), where
\mu_{+}
and
\mu_{-}
can be interpreted as the source and sink configurations of the material bundles
\theta{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}
(with non-negative components and satisfying
\mu_{+}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\mu_{-}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

Optimality is with respect to an overall transporation cost,
which in turn depends on the cost
h(\theta)
to transport a material bundle
\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
per unit distance.
In this article,

Definition 1.2

(Multi-material transport cost)
.

A

is a norm
h:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to[0,\infty)
.

Norms and their properties appear quite naturally as cost in this context.
The subadditivity leads to the appearance of branched structures in the support of optimal multi-material mass fluxes:
t

h(\theta+\bar{\theta})
for transporting material bundles
\theta
and
\bar{\theta}
together
is strictly smaller than the cost
h(\theta)+h(\bar{\theta})
for transporting them separately
so that the overall cost can be reduced by merging multiple initially separate mass fluxes.
The absolute homogeneity induces a scaling property: if

is a minimizer of the
h
-mass subject to
\partial F=F_{0}
, then
\lambda F_{\textup{opt}}
is a minimizer under
\partial F=\lambda F_{0}
for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}

A multi-material transport cost
h
induces a second norm
H
that becomes relevant when viewing multi-material mass fluxes
F
as
\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
-valued Radon measures (each row describing the mass flux of one of the material types). This norm is applicable to densities of
F
(e.g. the Radon–Nikodym derivative with respect to the total variation measure
|F|
).

Definition 1.3

(Generated norm)

The norm

\partial H(0)=\left\{M\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}\>\middle|\>MB_{1}(0)\subset\partial h(0)\right\}.

Above,

This definition is motivated by the following simple fact, proven in

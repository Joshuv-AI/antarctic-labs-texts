Bijection between Increasing Binary Trees and Rook Placements on Double Staircases
Bishal Deb

Abstract
In this paper, we shall construct a bijection between rook placements on double staircases (introduced by Josuat-Vergès in 2017) and increasing binary trees. We introduce two subclasses of rook placements on double staircases, which we call left and right-aligned rook placements. We show that their enumeration, while keeping track of a certain statistic, gives the γ \gamma -vectors of the Eulerian polynomials. We conclude with a discussion on a different bijection that fits in very well with our main bijection, and another discussion on generalising our main bijection. Our main bijection is a special case of a bijection due to Tewari (2019).

S_{n}(x)\coloneqq\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}x^{\mathrm{des}(\sigma)}.

It is not difficult to see that the number descents of a permutation is equal to the number of ascents of its reversal. Thus, we also have that,

S_{n}(x)=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}x^{\mathrm{asc}(\sigma)}.

Notice that the
(n+1)^{\mathrm{st}}
Eulerian polynomial is of degree
n
and hence,
S_{n+1}(x)\in\mathbb{C}\langle 1,x,\ldots,x^{n}\rangle
, the vector space over
\mathbb{C}
consisting of polynomials of degree less than or equal to
n
. If we write
S_{n+1}(x)
in the basis
\{(x+1)^{n},x(x+1)^{n-2},x^{2}(x+1)^{n-4},\ldots\}
then we obtain coefficients
\gamma_{n,0},\gamma_{n,1},\gamma_{n,2},\ldots
such that

S_{n+1}(x)=\gamma_{n,0}(x+1)^{n}+\gamma_{n,1}x(x+1)^{n-2}+\gamma_{n,3}x^{2}(x+1)^{n-4}+\ldots.

(7)

Foata and Schützenberger
[
1
]
showed that the Eulerian polynomials are
\gamma
-positive, i.e., the coefficients
\gamma_{n,0},\gamma_{n,1},\gamma_{n,2},\ldots
are all non-negative. Furthermore, they also showed that
\gamma_{n,k}
is the number of permutations on
n+1
letters with
k
descents such that every descent is a peak which is the same as the number of permutations on
n+1
letters with
k
ascents such that every ascent is a valley. See
[
10
, Chapter 4]
for more on
\gamma
-vectors of Eulerian polynomials.

4.1

4.1.1
Chains of Dyck Shapes and Rook Placements on Double Staircases

Chains of Dyck shapes were introduced by Josuat-Vergés in

where a bijection to

was also constructed
[
6

of Françon-Viennot

(also see

Before proceeding further, let us set some notation on Dyck paths and words that encode Dyck paths.

Definition 4.1
.

A

of size

such that

•

\nearrow

\searrow

•

\nearrow

A

\nearrow

\searrow

(1,-1)

2\delta_{n}
, we define

to be the word of length

•

the first letter is
\nearrow
, the last letter is
\searrow

•

if

, the

\nearrow
if the

\searrow

Josuat-Vergés showed that

is a Dyck word (
[

).

Let us define the large profile of a permutation. Let

were instead

for a double rise, and

To prove this lemma, we need to observe how we obtain

Case 1

\sigma

i

\sigma^{\prime}

\searrow

A spinorial quasilocal mass
John Lott Department of Mathematics University of California, Berkeley Berkeley, CA 94720-3840 USA

Abstract
. We define a quasilocal energy of a compact manifold-with-boundary, relative to a background manifold. The construction uses spinors on one manifold and the pullback of dual spinors from the other manifold. We prove positivity results for the quasilocal energy, in both the Riemannian and Lorentzian settings.

1.
Introduction

The mass of an asymptotically flat initial data set is a quantity computed from the asymptotic geometry. It has the physical interpretation of measuring the gravitational energy within the
space. The positive mass theorem says that under an appropriate positivity assumption on the curvature,
the mass is positive
[
15
,
19
]
.

It is an old problem to localize the measurement of gravitational energy to a compact region, i.e. to
give a good notion of a quasilocal mass. Some survey articles are

[
16
,
17
]
.
In most of this paper we will refer more precisely to a quasilocal energy rather than a quasilocal mass.

In view of Witten’s proof of the positive energy theorem for spin manifolds
[
19
]
,
an attractive approach to define a quasilocal energy
is to use spinors. This idea has been
considered by various people, notably Dougan-Mason
[
5
]
and Zhang

[
20
,
21
]
. There are two basic issues with such an approach. First,
the quasilocal energy is always defined relative to some background space. For example, in the positive
energy theorem, the background space is Euclidean space. It is not clear how to incorporate the
background space into the definition of the quasilocal energy. The second issue is what boundary
conditions to impose on the spinor fields. Witten’s proof used spinor fields that asymptotically
approach a constant spinor with respect to the model flat space. It is not evident what the analog
should be for a compact manifold-with-boundary.

We show that both of these issues can be handled by the technique of
pulling back (dual) spinors from the background
space. This technique has some history in the mathematical literature and
has been applied to prove sharp comparison results about scalar curvature by
Llarull
[
10
,
11
]
, Goette-Semmelmann
[
8
]
,
the author
[
14
]
and Wang-Xie-Yu
[
18
]
. One advantage
of the spinorial approach to the quasilocal energy is that the nonnegativity of the energy, under
some curvature conditions, falls out immediately.

In Section
2
we define a quasilocal energy in the Riemannian setting.
Given compact
connected
n
-dimensional
Riemannian manifolds-with-boundary
(N,\partial N)
and
(M,\partial M)
, suppose that

f:(N,\partial N)\rightarrow(M,\partial M)
is a spin map that is an isometry on

\partial N
. We think of
M
as the background
space and
N
as the manifold whose quasilocal energy we want to define, relative to
M
. There is
a corresponding Clifford module
E
on
N
. For simplicity, suppose that

is even dimensional
and that
N
and
M
are spin, in

is the twisted
spinor bundle
S_{N}\otimes f^{*}S_{M}^{*}
. Let
D^{N}
be the corresponding Dirac-type operator on
C^{\infty}(N;E)
.

On
\partial N
, we have the identifications

E\Big|_{\partial N}\cong(S_{N}\otimes(\partial f)^{*}S_{M}^{*})\Big|_{\partial N}\cong(S_{N}\otimes S_{N}^{*})\Big|_{\partial N}\cong\Lambda^{*}T^{*}N\Big|_{\partial N}\cong\Lambda^{*}T^{*}\partial N\oplus\Lambda^{*}T^{*}\partial N,

(1.1)

where the last isomorphism is a separation into tangential forms and forms with a normal
component. Let
\pi_{+}:\Lambda^{*}T^{*}N\Big|_{\partial N}\rightarrow\Lambda^{*}T^{*}\partial N
be projection onto the tangential component.
There is a natural “Dirichlet” boundary condition for sections
\psi
of
E
given by

\pi_{+}\left(\psi\Big|_{\partial N}\right)=0
; let
{\mathcal{D}}

denote the ensuing self-adjoint operator. For simplicity, in this introduction we mostly consider the
case when
\operatorname{Ker}({\mathcal{D}})=0
.

To set up a boundary value problem, there is a
canonical choice of section of
\Lambda^{*}T^{*}\partial N
, namely the constant function
1
.
Let
e_{n}
be the inward-pointing unit normal on
\partial N
.

Definition 1.2
.

If
C
is a
nonempty union of connected components of
\partial N
, let
\psi\in C^{\infty}(N;E)
be
the (unique) solution to
D^{N}\psi=0
with
\pi_{+}\left(\psi\Big|_{\partial N}\right)=1_{C}
, the characteristic

.
The quasilocal energy associated to the boundary subset

{\mathcal{E}}_{C}=-\int_{\partial N}\langle\psi,\nabla^{N}_{e_{n}}\psi\rangle\>\operatorname{dvol}_{\partial N}.

(1.3)

Here are some basic properties of the quasilocal energy. Let
R_{N}
and
R_{M}
denote the scalar
curvatures of
N
and
M
, respectively. Define

, the distortion of

2
-forms, to be
the pointwise norm
\sup_{v\wedge w\neq 0}\frac{|df(v)\wedge df(w)|}{|v\wedge w|}
.

We recall that the boundary map
\partial f
is an isometry on
\partial N
.

Theorem 1.4
.

1. If
f
is an isometric diffeomorphism then
{\mathcal{E}}_{\partial N}=0
.

2. If
M
has nonnegative curvature operator and
R_{N}\geq|\Lambda^{2}df|(f^{*}R_{M})
then

{\mathcal{E}}_{C}\geq 0
.

Part 1 of Theorem
1.4
is consistent with the interpretation of
{\mathcal{E}}
as being a
relative energy between
N
and
M
. Part 2 of Theorem
1.4
is an immediate consequence
of a Bochner-type formula. In particular, if
M
is flat and

then
{\mathcal{E}}_{C}\geq 0
.

Although (

) is an integral over the boundary of
N
, the solution
\psi
to the boundary

a priori
on the interior geometry of
N
and the map
f
.
When
M
is a domain in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, the quasilocal energy only depends on

\partial f
.

We give a formula for

indicating that in the weak field limit, i.e. when

a perturbation of

energy
[
3
]
.
We expect, but do not prove, that if one takes an
appropriate exhaustion of an asymptotically flat manifold by compact domains then their
quasilocal energies will approach a normalization constant times the ADM mass.
We compute
{\mathcal{E}}_{\partial N}
when
N
is conformally
related to
M
and find that it is exactly the Brown-York energy.
We find a similar statement when
N
is rotationally symmetric.

In Section
3
we consider a Lorentzian quasilocal energy. That is, we have
Lorentzian
(n+1)

manifolds
\overline{N}
and
\overline{M}
, along with
compact connected spatial hypersurfaces-with-boundary
N\subset\overline{N}
and

M\subset\overline{M}
. Again, we use a comparison map
f:(N,\partial N)\rightarrow(M,\partial M)
that is an isometry on
\partial N

acting on sections of the
twisted spinor bundle

. There is a new feature that, in general,

need not be formally self-adjoint on the interior of
N
. We first look at the
case when
M
is a totally geodesic hypersurface in
\overline{M}
. Then there is no
problem with self-adjointness and results from Section
2
extend. The

in part 2 of Theorem
1.4
gets replaced by

, where

is the Einstein tensor of
\overline{N}
.

We next look at the case when
\overline{M}
is the flat Minkowski space

{\mathbb{R}}^{n,1}
, but the hypersurface
M
need not be totally geodesic. It turns out that
D^{N}
is formally
self-adjoint on the interior of
N
if one uses an appropriate weighted

L^{2}
-space. Regarding boundary conditions, there are two natural choices.
The first one does not give a self-adjoint boundary value problem but nevertheless
one can use it to define a quasilocal energy
{\mathcal{E}}_{C}
. The second one does
give a self-adjoint boundary value problem and gives rise to a quasilocal energy

{\mathcal{E}}^{sa}_{C}
. They have the following properties.

Theorem 1.5
.

1. If
f
is an isometric diffeomorphism then
{\mathcal{E}}_{\partial N}=0
.

2. If
\overline{N}
satisfies the dominant energy condition

T_{00}\geq\sqrt{-\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n}T_{0\alpha}T^{0\alpha}}
then

{\mathcal{E}}_{C}\geq 0
and
{\mathcal{E}}^{sa}_{C}\geq 0
.

Comparing
{\mathcal{E}}_{C}
and
{\mathcal{E}}^{sa}_{C}
, the first one
{\mathcal{E}}_{C}

has the advantage, from part 1 of Theorem
1.5
, of vanishing when
C=\partial N
and
f
is an isometric
diffeomorphism. On the other hand,
{\mathcal{E}}^{sa}_{C}
has the advantage of coming
from a self-adjoint boundary value problem.

Finally, we extend the results to the case when

\overline{M}
is a more general Lorentzian manifold than Minkowski space. It turns
out that it is enough for
\overline{M}
to be a product spacetime
{\mathbb{R}}\times X
for some
Riemannian manifold
X
. This can be compared with Chen-Wang-Wang-Yau’s construction of a
quasilocal energy when the background space is in a static spacetime

[
4
]
.

Regarding earlier work about a spinorial approach to quasilocal energy, Dougan and Mason
used the complex structure on an oriented surface to make an interesting choice of boundary condition

[
5
]
.
Their approach is clearly restricted to

define a quasilocal energy when the background space

is a domain in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{3,1}

[

]
. His boundary condition came from using constant spinors
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{3,1}
; compare with Proposition
3.20
of the present paper. He imposed
geometric restrictions to ensure that the Dirac operator is invertible; compare with
Proposition
3.19
of the present paper. In
[
21

he also allowed

to be a domain in a hyperbolic submanifold of
{\mathbb{R}}^{3,1}
.

2.2.
Definition of the quasilocal energy

Let
\epsilon
be the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-grading operator on
S_{N}
. Put

\gamma^{0}=i\epsilon
. Let
T

be the involution on
S_{N}\Big|_{\partial N}
given by

T\eta=\gamma^{0}\gamma^{n}\eta.

(2.10)

Identifying

f^{*}S^{*}_{M}\Big|_{\partial N}
with
S^{*}_{N}\Big|_{\partial N}
, there is an induced involution
T
on

\Lambda^{*}T^{*}N\Big|_{\partial N}\cong\operatorname{End}(S_{N})\Big|_{\partial N}\cong(S_{N}\otimes S_{N}^{*})\Big|_{\partial N}\cong S_{N}\Big|_{\partial N}\otimes f^{*}S^{*}_{M}\Big|_{\partial M}.

(2.11)

We recall that an element of
\Lambda^{*}T^{*}N
acts on
S_{N}
by Clifford multiplication,
realizing the isomorphism
\Lambda^{*}T^{*}N\cong\operatorname{End}(S_{N})
. Then
for
\omega\in\Lambda^{*}T^{*}N\Big|_{\partial N}
and
\eta\in S_{N}\Big|_{\partial N}
, we have
T(\omega\cdot\eta)=T(\omega)\cdot T(\eta)
.
In terms of the orthonormal frame,
T
acts on
T^{*}N\Big|_{\partial N}
as

\gamma^{0}\gamma^{n}\mathaccent 866{\gamma}^{\mathaccent 866{0}}\mathaccent 866{\gamma}^{\mathaccent 866{n}}

Let
\tau^{n}
be the
dual covector to

Lemma 2.12
.

Given
\omega\in\Lambda^{*}T^{*}N\Big|_{\partial N}
, write

\omega=\omega_{+}+\tau^{n}\wedge\omega_{-}
for

\omega_{\pm}\in\Lambda^{*}T^{*}\partial N
. Then

T\omega=\omega_{+}-\tau^{n}\wedge\omega_{-}
.

Proof.

Given an increasing multi-index
I
with entries between

and

, we have

T(\gamma^{I}\psi)=(\gamma^{0}\gamma^{n})\gamma^{I}\psi=\gamma^{I}(\gamma^{0}\gamma^{n})\psi=\gamma^{I}T(\psi)

(2.13)

and

T(\gamma^{n}\gamma^{I}\psi)=(\gamma^{0}\gamma^{n})\gamma^{n}\gamma^{I}\psi=-\gamma^{n}\gamma^{I}(\gamma^{0}\gamma^{n})\psi=-\gamma^{n}\gamma^{I}T(\psi)

(2.14)

Using the isomorphism
\Lambda^{*}T^{*}N\Big|_{\partial N}\cong\operatorname{End}(S_{N})\Big|_{\partial N}
,
the lemma follows.

•

•

Suppose that

From (

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle\int_{N}|\nabla^{N}\psi|^{2}\>\operatorname{dvol}_{N}+\frac{1}{4}\int_{N}R_{N}|\psi|^{2}\>\operatorname{dvol}_{N}-\frac{1}{32}\int_{N}\mathaccent 866{R}_{\mathaccent 866{\alpha}\mathaccent 866{\beta}\sigma\tau}\langle\psi,[\gamma^{\sigma},\gamma^{\tau}][\mathaccent 866{\gamma}^{\mathaccent 866{\alpha}},\mathaccent 866{\gamma}^{\mathaccent 866{\beta}}]\psi\rangle+

(2.16)

As

. From

, Lemma 2.1]
,

\langle\psi,\gamma^{n}\gamma^{i}\mathaccent 866{\gamma}^{n}\mathaccent 866{\gamma}^{\mathaccent 866{j}}\mathaccent 866{A}_{\mathaccent 866{j}i}\psi\rangle\leq(\partial f)^{*}H_{\partial M}|\psi|^{2}
.
Then (
2.8
) and (

) imply that
\nabla^{N}\psi=0
. Since

is unitary,
it follows that
|\psi|^{2}
is constant on the connected manifold

. Then
(

|\psi|^{2}=0
.
∎

Remark 2.18
.

If the Euler characteristic of
M
is nonzero and the degree of

\operatorname{Ker}({\mathcal{D}})\neq 0

[

.

We now discuss the boundary value problem.
Given
\sigma\in\Omega^{*}(\partial N)
with
\pi_{+}(\sigma)=\sigma
,

\psi\in C^{\infty}(N;E)

then there is some

so that

and
\pi_{+}\left(\psi\Big|_{\partial N}\right)=\sigma

[
7

. The section

is unique up to addition by elements of
\operatorname{Ker}({\mathcal{D}})
.

Let
C
be a nonempty union of connected components of
\partial N
.
To define the quasilocal energy, we would like to take

to be the characteristic function

.
To be sure that
\psi

have to slightly modify this choice. Let

P_{\gamma^{n}\operatorname{Ker}({\mathcal{D}})\Big|_{\partial N}}
be

from

to
\gamma^{n}\operatorname{Ker}({\mathcal{D}})\Big|_{\partial N}

We put
\overline{1}_{C}=1_{C}-P_{\gamma^{n}\operatorname{Ker}({\mathcal{D}})\Big|_{\partial N}}1_{C}
.
We then take

accordingly.

Definition 2.19
.

Put

{\mathcal{C}}_{C}=\left\{\psi\in C^{\infty}(N;E)\>:\>D^{N}\psi=0,\pi_{+}\left(\psi\Big|_{\partial N}\right)=\overline{1}_{C}\right\}.

(2.20)

The quasilocal energy associated to the boundary subset

is

{\mathcal{E}}_{C}=\inf_{\psi\in{\mathcal{C}}_{C}}\left(-\int_{\partial N}\langle\psi,\nabla^{N}_{e_{n}}\psi\rangle\>\operatorname{dvol}_{\partial N}\right).

(2.21)

Note that
{\mathcal{E}}_{C}
could be

.

Lemma 2.22
.

If
\operatorname{Ker}({\mathcal{D}})=0
then
{\mathcal{E}}_{C}>-\infty
. In general,
if
{\mathcal{E}}_{C}>-\infty
then it is realized by some

\psi_{min}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{C}
.

Proof.

We know that
{\mathcal{C}}_{C}
is a finite dimensional affine space modelled on
\operatorname{Ker}({\mathcal{D}})
. If
\operatorname{Ker}({\mathcal{D}})=0
then
{\mathcal{C}}_{C}
has a single element and

{\mathcal{E}}_{C}>-\infty
.
If
\operatorname{Ker}({\mathcal{D}})\neq 0
then as

-\int_{\partial N}\langle\psi,\nabla^{N}_{e_{n}}\psi\rangle\>\operatorname{dvol}_{\partial N}
is a quadratic function on

{\mathcal{C}}_{C}
, the lemma follows.
∎

In some statements that follow we may implicitly assume that
{\mathcal{E}}_{C}>-\infty
. We will see relevant examples where this is the case
in Proposition

, Proposition
2.27
, Lemma
2.43
and
Proposition
2.55
.

2.3.
Properties of the quasilocal energy

Proposition 2.23
.

If
f:N\rightarrow M
is an isometric diffeomorphism then

{\mathcal{E}}_{\partial N}=0
.

Proof.

If
f
is an isometric diffeomorphism then we can take

E\cong\Lambda^{*}T^{*}N
and identify
D^{N}
with
d+d^{*}
.
Letting
*
denote the Hodge duality operator, if
\psi\in C^{\infty}(N;E)
then

\displaystyle\int_{N}\langle\left(D^{N}\right)^{2}\psi,\psi\rangle\>\operatorname{dvol}_{N}

\displaystyle=\int_{N}\langle(dd^{*}+d^{*}d)\psi,\psi\rangle\>\operatorname{dvol}_{N}

(2.24)

\displaystyle=\int_{N}\left(dd^{*}\psi\wedge*\psi+d^{*}d\psi\wedge*\psi\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{N}\left(dd^{*}\psi\wedge*\psi+*d^{*}d\psi\wedge*(*\psi)\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{N}\left(dd^{*}\psi\wedge*\psi+dd^{*}*\psi\wedge*(*\psi)\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{N}\left(|d^{*}\psi|^{2}+|d\psi|^{2}\right)\>\operatorname{dvol}_{N}+\int_{\partial N}\left(d^{*}\psi\wedge*\psi+d^{*}*\psi\wedge*(*\psi)\right).

Comparing with equation (
2.4
) shows

-\int_{\partial N}\langle\psi,\nabla^{N}_{e_{n}}\psi\rangle\>\operatorname{dvol}_{\partial N}=-\int_{\partial N}\left(d^{*}\psi\wedge*\psi+d^{*}*\psi\wedge*(*\psi)\right),

(2.25)

There is a
solution
\psi
of
D^{N}\psi=0

given by
\psi=1
, with
\pi_{+}(1)=1
.
It follows that
1
is orthogonal to

\gamma^{n}\operatorname{Ker}({\mathcal{D}})\Big|_{\partial N}
, so

\overline{1}_{\partial N}=1
.

If
i\>:\>\partial N\rightarrow N
is the boundary inclusion then
an element of
\operatorname{Ker}({\mathcal{D}})
is a differential
form
\rho\in\Omega^{*}(N)

satisfying
(d+d^{*})\rho=0
and the relative (Dirichlet) boundary condition

i^{*}\rho=0
. Applying
i^{*}
, it follows that
i^{*}(d^{*}\rho)=0
.
Squaring
d+d^{*}
, it follows that
(dd^{*}+d^{*}d)\rho=0
. Hence

\rho
is a
harmonic form
\alpha
on
N
satisfying relative (Dirichlet) boundary
conditions. In particular,
d\rho=d^{*}\rho=0
.

Hence elements
\psi

of
{\mathcal{C}}_{\partial N}
are of the form
1+\rho
for such
\rho
.
As

d\psi=d^{*}\psi=0
,
equation (
2.25
) gives
-\int_{\partial N}\langle\psi,\nabla^{N}_{e_{n}}\psi\rangle\>\operatorname{dvol}_{\partial N}=0
, which proves the lemma.
∎

We now give the basic positivity property of the quasilocal energy in the
physically relevant case.

Proposition 2.26
.

Suppose that
M
has nonnegative curvature operator.
If
R_{N}\geq|\Lambda^{2}df|(f^{*}R_{M})
then
{\mathcal{E}}_{C}\geq 0
. If

R_{N}\geq|\Lambda^{2}df|(f^{*}R_{M})
and
{\mathcal{E}}_{C}=0
then

R_{N}=|\Lambda^{2}df|(f^{*}R_{M})
.

Proof.

This follows from (
2.4
) and (
2.8
).
∎

Proposition 2.27
.

If
M
is flat and
R_{N}\geq 0
then
{\mathcal{E}}_{C}\geq 0
. If

M
is flat,
R_{N}\geq 0
and
{\mathcal{E}}_{C}=0
then
N
is Ricci flat.

Proof.

The first statement follows from Proposition
2.26
. The second
statement follows as in
[
14
, Proof of Proposition 2.3]
.
∎

We now write
{\mathcal{E}}_{C}
more explicitly as a boundary integral. From (
2.5
), we have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}_{C}=

\displaystyle-\int_{\partial N}\langle\psi_{min},D^{\partial N}\psi_{min}\rangle\>\operatorname{dvol}_{\partial N}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial N}H_{\partial N}|\psi_{min}|^{2}\>\operatorname{dvol}_{\partial N}+

(2.28)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial N}\langle\psi_{min},\gamma^{n}\gamma^{i}\mathaccent 866{\gamma}^{n}\mathaccent 866{\gamma}^{\mathaccent 866{j}}\mathaccent 866{A}_{\mathaccent 866{j}i}\psi_{min}\rangle\>\operatorname{dvol}_{\partial N}.

We write
\psi_{min}\Big|_{\partial N}=\overline{1}_{C}+\tau^{n}\wedge\phi
for some

\phi\in\Omega^{*}(\partial N)
. Let
E^{i}
denote exterior multiplication
by
\tau^{i}
and
I^{i}
denote interior multiplication by

e_{i}
, when
acting on
\Lambda^{*}T^{*}\partial N+\tau^{n}\wedge\Lambda^{*}T^{*}\partial N
.

Proposition 2.29
.

If
\overline{1}_{C}=1_{C}
then

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}_{C}=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{C}H_{\partial N}\>\operatorname{dvol}_{\partial N}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{C}(\partial f)^{*}H_{\partial M}\>\operatorname{dvol}_{\partial N}-

(2.30)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial N}H_{\partial N}|\phi|^{2}\>\operatorname{dvol}_{\partial N}+\int_{\partial N}\frac{1}{2}\mathaccent 866{A}_{ji}\langle\phi,(E^{i}-I^{i})(E^{j}+I^{j})\phi\rangle\>\operatorname{dvol}_{\partial N}.

Proof.

On
\partial N
, we can represent
\gamma^{i}
by

\sqrt{-1}(E^{i}-I^{i})
,
\gamma^{n}
by

\sqrt{-1}(E^{n}-I^{n})
,
\mathaccent 866{\gamma}^{i}
by
E^{i}+I^{i}
and

\mathaccent 866{\gamma}^{n}
by

E^{n}+I^{n}
.

Lemma 2.31
.

We have

\int_{\partial N}\langle\psi_{min},D^{\partial N}\psi_{min}\rangle\>\operatorname{dvol}_{\partial N}=0.

(2.32)

Proof.

As
D^{\partial N}
anticommutes with
T
, it follows that

\int_{\partial N}\langle 1_{C},D^{\partial N}1_{C}\rangle\>\operatorname{dvol}_{\partial N}=\int_{\partial N}\langle\tau^{n}\wedge\phi,D^{\partial N}(\tau^{n}\wedge\phi)\rangle\>\operatorname{dvol}_{\partial N}=0.

(2.33)

Next, since
\nabla^{\partial N}
restricts to the Riemannian connection on

\Lambda^{*}T^{*}\partial N
, we know that

\nabla^{\partial N}1_{C}=0
, so

\int_{\partial N}\langle\tau^{n}\wedge\phi,D^{\partial N}1_{C}\rangle\>\operatorname{dvol}_{\partial N}=0

(2.34)

and

\int_{\partial N}\langle 1_{C},D^{\partial N}\tau^{n}\wedge\phi\rangle\>\operatorname{dvol}_{\partial N}=\int_{\partial N}\langle D^{\partial N}1_{C},\tau^{n}\wedge\phi\rangle\>\operatorname{dvol}_{\partial N}=0

(2.35)

The lemma follows.
∎

Also

\int_{\partial N}H_{\partial N}|\psi_{min}|^{2}\>\operatorname{dvol}_{\partial N}=\int_{C}H_{\partial N}\>\operatorname{dvol}_{\partial N}+\int_{\partial N}H_{\partial N}|\phi|^{2}\>\operatorname{dvol}_{\partial N}.

(2.36)

Next, one can check that

\langle\psi_{min},\gamma^{n}\gamma^{i}\mathaccent 866{\gamma}^{n}\mathaccent 866{\gamma}^{j}\psi_{min}\rangle=\delta^{ij}1_{C}+\langle\phi,(E^{i}-I^{i})(E^{j}+I^{j})\phi\rangle.

(2.37)

Hence

\langle\psi_{min},\gamma^{n}\gamma^{i}\mathaccent 866{\gamma}^{n}\mathaccent 866{\gamma}^{j}\mathaccent 866{A}_{ji}\psi_{min}\rangle=(\partial f)^{*}H_{\partial M}1_{C}+\mathaccent 866{A}_{ji}\langle\phi,(E^{i}-I^{i})(E^{j}+I^{j})\phi\rangle.

(2.38)

This proves the proposition.
∎

The first two terms on the right-hand side of (
2.30
) give the Brown-York energy. (We have taken a particular normalization of the Brown-York energy.)
In the case when
N
is a small perturbation of
M
, the function
\phi
will also be small.
Hence Proposition
2.29
shows that in this weak field limit, to leading order
the quasilocal energy

{\mathcal{E}}_{C}
is the Brown-York energy.

Remark 2.39
.

With reference to Proposition
2.29
, in the asymptotically flat case the geometry on most of a large domain
N
will be a small perturbation of the Euclidean metric. Furthermore, it’s known that the Brown-York energy of large spheres approaches the ADM mass
(
[
6
]
and references therein). This makes it plausible that under an appropriate exhaustion of an asymptotically flat manifold, the quasilocal mass will approach the ADM mass. That this is true in the rotationally symmetric case follows from Proposition
2.55
.

2.4.
Conformal deformations

In this section we use the conformal covariance of the Dirac operator to
say something about the quasilocal energy; c.f.
[
20
, Section 4]
.

Put
(N^{\prime},g^{\prime})=(N,e^{2\phi}g)
, where

\phi\Big|_{\partial N}
is locally constant and

\phi\Big|_{C}=0
.
Let
f:N\rightarrow M
be as before. Suppose that
\operatorname{Ker}({\mathcal{D}})=0
.
Then
\overline{1}_{C}=1_{C}
.
The pure Dirac operator
Dirac^{N^{\prime}}
is related to the pure Dirac operator

Dirac^{N}
by
Dirac^{N^{\prime}}=e^{-\frac{n+1}{2}\phi}Dirac^{N}e^{\frac{n-1}{2}\phi}

[
12
, Section II]
.
Thinking of
S_{N}\otimes f^{*}S_{M}^{*}
as
\operatorname{Hom}(f^{*}S_{M},S_{N})
, the same argument gives
D^{N^{\prime}}=e^{-\frac{n+1}{2}\phi}D^{N}e^{\frac{n-1}{2}\phi}
.

Let
\psi
be a
minimizer for the quasilocal energy of
N
relative to
M
, with

\pi_{+}\left(\psi\Big|_{\partial N}\right)=1_{C}
. Putting
\psi^{\prime}=e^{-\frac{n-1}{2}\phi}\psi
, it satisfies
D^{N^{\prime}}\psi^{\prime}=0

with
\pi_{+}\left(\psi^{\prime}\Big|_{\partial N^{\prime}}\right)=1_{C}
. As

\operatorname{Ker}({\mathcal{D}}^{\prime})=0
, it follows that

{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}_{C}=-\int_{\partial N^{\prime}}\langle\psi^{\prime},\nabla^{N^{\prime}}_{e_{n}^{\prime}}\psi^{\prime}\rangle\operatorname{dvol}_{g^{\prime}}.

(2.40)

From
[
12
, Section II]
,

\nabla^{N^{\prime}}_{e_{n}^{\prime}}=e^{-\phi}\left(\nabla^{N}_{e_{n}}+\frac{1}{4}(e_{\alpha}\phi)[\gamma^{n},\gamma^{\alpha}]\right).

(2.41)

Applying this to (
2.40
), and using the fact that
\phi\Big|_{\partial N}

is locally constant,
one finds

{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}_{C}-{\mathcal{E}}_{C}=\frac{n-1}{2}\int_{\partial N}(e_{n}\phi)|\psi|^{2}\>\operatorname{dvol}_{\partial N}.

(2.42)

We now specialize to the case when
N=M
,
f=\operatorname{Id}
and
C=\partial N
. We no longer have
\operatorname{Ker}({\mathcal{D}})=0
.

Lemma 2.43
.

If
e_{n}\phi\geq 0
then

{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}_{\partial N^{\prime}}=\frac{n-1}{2}\int_{\partial N}(e_{n}\phi)\>\operatorname{dvol}_{\partial N}
. If
e_{n}\phi<0
then
{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}_{\partial N^{\prime}}=-\infty
.

Proof.

Note that
\phi\Big|_{\partial N}=0
.
Given
\psi^{\prime}\in C^{\infty}(N^{\prime};E^{\prime})

satisfying
D^{N^{\prime}}\psi^{\prime}=0
and
\pi_{+}\left(\psi^{\prime}\Big|_{\partial N}\right)=\overline{1}^{\prime}_{\partial N}
, put

\psi=e^{\frac{n-1}{2}\phi}\psi^{\prime}
. Then

D^{N}\psi=0
and
\pi_{+}\left(\psi\Big|_{\partial N}\right)=\overline{1}_{\partial N}
. The proof of Proposition
2.23

implies that
\psi=1+\rho
for some harmonic form
\rho
satisfying relative boundary conditions. As
in the proof of Proposition
2.23
, we know that

\int_{\partial N}\langle\psi,\nabla^{N}_{e_{n}}\psi\rangle\operatorname{dvol}_{g}=0
.
Since

-\int_{\partial N^{\prime}}\langle\psi^{\prime},\nabla^{N^{\prime}}_{e_{n}^{\prime}}\psi^{\prime}\rangle\operatorname{dvol}_{g^{\prime}}+\int_{\partial N}\langle\psi,\nabla^{N}_{e_{n}}\psi\rangle\operatorname{dvol}_{g}=\frac{n-1}{2}\int_{\partial N}(e_{n}\phi)|\psi|^{2}\>\operatorname{dvol}_{\partial N},

(2.44)

if
e_{n}\phi\geq 0
then we minimize
\frac{n-1}{2}\int_{\partial N}(e_{n}\phi)|\psi|^{2}\>\operatorname{dvol}_{\partial N}
by taking
\psi=1
. As
\operatorname{dvol}_{N}\in\operatorname{Ker}({\mathcal{D}})
, if

e_{n}\phi<0
then we can make
\frac{n-1}{2}\int_{\partial N}(e_{n}\phi)|\psi|^{2}\>\operatorname{dvol}_{\partial N}
arbitrarily negative by taking
\psi=1+s\operatorname{dvol}_{N}
with
s
large.
∎

One can check that the mean curvatures of
\partial N^{\prime}
and

\partial N
are related by

H_{\partial N^{\prime}}=H_{\partial N}-(n-1)e_{n}\phi.

(2.45)

Hence if
e_{n}\phi\geq 0
then

{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}_{\partial N^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial N}\left(H_{\partial N}-H_{\partial N^{\prime}}\right)\>\operatorname{dvol}_{\partial N}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial N^{\prime}}\left(H_{\partial N}-H_{\partial N^{\prime}}\right)\>\operatorname{dvol}_{\partial N^{\prime}},

(2.46)

showing that the quasilocal energy equals the
Brown-York energy.

In this conformal setting, we can also express the quasilocal energy as an interior integral.
If
n>2
then

\displaystyle\frac{n-1}{2}\int_{\partial N}(e_{n}\phi)\>\operatorname{dvol}_{\partial N}

\displaystyle=\frac{n-1}{n-2}\int_{\partial N}\left(e_{n}e^{\frac{(n-2)\phi}{2}}\right)\>\operatorname{dvol}_{\partial N}

(2.47)

\displaystyle=-\frac{n-1}{n-2}\int_{N}\triangle e^{\frac{(n-2)\phi}{2}}\>\operatorname{dvol}_{N}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\int_{N}e^{(\frac{n}{2}+1)\phi}\left(R_{g^{\prime}}-e^{-2\phi}R_{g}\right)\operatorname{dvol}_{N}.

Hence if
e_{n}\phi\geq 0
then

{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}_{\partial N^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{4}\int_{N}e^{(\frac{n}{2}+1)\phi}\left(R_{g^{\prime}}-e^{-2\phi}R_{g}\right)\operatorname{dvol}_{N}

(2.48)

One can check that this is also true when
n=2
.

|\Lambda^{2}(d\operatorname{Id})|=e^{-2\phi}
, for

, equation (

) is consistent with Proposition
2.26
.

As a special case, let
(M,g_{Eucl})
be a compact connected
codimension-zero submanifold of

{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
with nonempty boundary. If

vanishes on

\partial M
and satisfies

.

(2.49)

2.5.

In this section we make the quasilocal energy more explicit when the

From Corollary

.

If

Proposition 2.51

\operatorname{Ker}({\mathcal{D}})=0

(2.52)

In particular, this is true under the hypotheses of Proposition
2.15

Proof.

Let
\epsilon_{a}^{*}\in S_{M}^{*}

\epsilon_{a}

\epsilon_{a}^{\prime,*}

Under the isomorphism

\sum_{a}\left(\epsilon_{a}\otimes\epsilon_{a}^{*}+\epsilon^{\prime}_{a}\otimes\epsilon_{a}^{\prime,*}\right)

On the other hand, if
\eta_{a}\in\operatorname{Ker}(\gamma^{0}\gamma^{n}+I)

, and
similarly for an element of the form

Abstract
Ruskey and Savage in 1993 asked whether every matching in a hypercube can be extended to a Hamiltonian cycle. A positive answer is known for perfect matchings, but the general case has been resolved only for matchings of linear size. In this paper we show that there is a quadratic function q ⁡ ( n ) q(n) such that every matching in the n n -dimensional hypercube of size at most q ⁡ ( n ) q(n) may be extended to a cycle which covers at least 3 4 \frac{3}{4} of the vertices.

Introduction

Frank Ruskey and Carla Savage in 1993 formulated the following problem
[
11
]
: Does every matching in a hypercube extend to a Hamiltonian cycle? More than two decades have passed and the question still remains open.
It may be of interest that a complementary problem on the existence of a Hamiltonian cycle avoiding a given matching in a hypercube has been already resolved
[
4
]
.

An important step towards a solution to the Ruskey-Savage problem was made in 2007 by Fink who answered the question affirmatively for every perfect matching
[
7
]
.
Note that this implies a positive solution for every matching that extends to a perfect one, which includes e.g. every induced matching
[
12
]
. However, this result does not immediately provide a complete answer to the original question, as hypercubes contain matchings that are maximal with respect to inclusion, but still not perfect. Actually, to determine the minimum size of such a matching is another long-standing problem
[
9
]
. The simplicity and elegance of Fink’s method inspired several generalizations
[
1
,
6
,
10
]
, but none of them addresses the extendability of imperfect matchings.

As far as arbitrary matchings are concerned, there are only partial results that deal with matchings of linear size.
The author of the current paper showed that a set
\mathcal{P}
of at most
2n-3
edges of the
n
-dimensional hypercube extends to a Hamiltonian cycle iff
\mathcal{P}
induces a linear forest
[
5
]
. This bound is sharp, as for every
n\geq 3
there is a non-extendable linear forest of
2n-2
edges
[
3
]
. Of course, these edges do not form a matching, so this does not imply a negative answer to the Ruskey-Savage problem. In the case when
\mathcal{P}
is a matching, the bound on
|\mathcal{P}|
was improved to
3n-10
by Wang and Zhang
[
14
]
.

The purpose of this paper is to derive a quadratic upper bound on the size of a matching that extends to a cycle which covers at least
\frac{3}{4}
of the vertices of the hypercube.
Our result is based on an inductive construction which combines a refinement of Fink’s method for perfect matchings
[
8
]
with a lemma on hypercube partitioning due to Wiener
[
15
]
.

The graph-theoretic terms used in this paper but not defined below may be found e.g. in

]
.
Throughout the paper,

stands for the set

Vertex and edge sets of a graph G are denoted by

A sequence

Let

denotes the path between

and

with

A

In this paper we deal with the

-dimensional hypercube

which is a graph with all

-bit strings as vertices, an edge joining two vertices whenever they differ in a single bit.
Given a string

to denote the string

Given a set

induced by the vertex set

Subcubes

Note that

•

,

•

if

are adjacent subcubes, then an arbitrary

Given a set

To engineer our inductive construction, we employ the following result on hypercube partitioning due to Wiener

Theorem 1

(
[
15
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a set of vertices of
Q_{n}
of size
s

with
s\geq 2n
and
d=\lceil\frac{n^{2}}{2s-n-2}\rceil
. Then there is a
set
D\subseteq[n]
such that
|D|=d
and
|\mathcal{S}_{D}(u)|\leq d+1

for every

.

Let

denote the complete graph on the set of vertices of
Q_{n}
. The set
\dim(uv)
of

of an edge

,
u=u_{1}\dots u_{n},v=v_{1}\dots v_{n}
, is defined by
\dim(uv)=\{i\in[n]\mid u_{i}\neq v_{i}\}
. An edge

is called

if

and

otherwise.
For a vertex

let
v^{d}
denote the vertex of
Q_{n}
such that

is a short edge of dimension
d
.

A

is a set of pairwise non-adjacent edges. Given a matching

to denote the set of all vertices incident with an edge of
M
.
We say that a matching

in

is

if every short edge of dimension

not in

is adjacent to some edge of

.

Note that removing all edges of some fixed dimension

splits

into two
(n-1)
-dimensional subcubes
Q^{0}=Q_{\mkern 5.0mu\overline{\mkern-5.0mu\{d\}\mkern-5.0mu}\mkern 5.0mu}(0)
and
Q^{1}=Q_{\mkern 5.0mu\overline{\mkern-5.0mu\{d\}\mkern-5.0mu}\mkern 5.0mu}(1)
. We use
M^{i}_{d}
to denote
M\cap E(K(Q^{i}))
for both
i\in\{0,1\}
and
M^{2}_{d}=M\setminus(M^{0}_{d}\cup M^{1}_{d})
.

Observation 2
.

Let

and

be a matching in

containing
s
short edges of dimension
d
. If
2|M|-s<2^{n-1}
, then

is not

-saturated.

Proof.

Put
Q^{0}=Q_{\mkern 5.0mu\overline{\mkern-5.0mu\{d\}\mkern-5.0mu}\mkern 5.0mu}(0)
.
Since
|\{u\in V(Q^{0})\mid\{u,u^{d}\}\cap\bigcup M\neq\emptyset\}|=2|M|-s<2^{n-1}=|V(Q^{0})|

such that
\{u,u^{d}\}\cap\bigcup M=\emptyset
. Hence
M
is not
d
-saturated.
∎

3
Construction

The following construction is a refinement of Fink’s method
[
8
, Proof of Theorem 3]
which was originally devised to extend perfect matchings in hypercubes to Hamiltonian cycles.

Construction 4
.

Let
M
be a matching in
K(Q_{n})
,
n\geq 2
,
d\in[n]
.

1.

Split
Q_{n}
into subcubes
Q^{0}=Q_{\mkern 5.0mu\overline{\mkern-5.0mu\{d\}\mkern-5.0mu}\mkern 5.0mu}(0)
and
Q^{1}=Q_{\mkern 5.0mu\overline{\mkern-5.0mu\{d\}\mkern-5.0mu}\mkern 5.0mu}(1)
.

2.

If
|M^{2}_{d}|
is odd, select an edge of
E(Q_{n})\setminus M
of dimension
d
which is not adjacent to any edge of
M
and add it to
M^{2}_{d}
.

3.

If
M^{2}_{d}
is empty, select two edges of
E(Q_{n})\setminus M
of dimension
d
which are not adjacent to any edge of
M
and add them to
M^{2}_{d}
.

4.

Let
M^{2}_{d}=\{u_{1}v_{1},u_{2}v_{2},\dots,u_{k}v_{k}\}
with
u_{i}\in V(Q^{0})
and
v_{i}\in V(Q^{1})
for all
i\in[k]
.

Select a perfect matching
P^{0}
in the subgraph of
K(Q^{0})
induced by
u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{k}
.

Let
C^{0}
be a cycle in
K(Q^{0})
such that
E(C^{0})=M^{0}_{d}\cup P^{0}\cup S^{0}
where
S^{0}\subseteq E(Q^{0})
.

5.

The removal of edges of
P^{0}
breaks
C^{0}
into pairwise disjoint paths, and we can without loss of generality assume that these are paths
P_{u_{1}u_{2}},P_{u_{3}u_{4}},\dots,P_{u_{k-1}u_{k}}
.

Put
P^{1}=\{v_{1}v_{2},v_{3}v_{4},\dots,v_{k-1}v_{k}\}
.

Let
C^{1}
be a cycle in
K(Q^{1})
such that
E(C^{1})=M^{1}_{d}\cup P^{1}\cup S^{1}
where
S^{1}\subseteq E(Q^{1})
.

Note that Construction
4
is non-deterministic in the sense that edges selected in steps
2
and
3
as well as a matching
P^{0}
and cycles
C^{0}
and
C^{1}
formed in steps
4
and
5
may not be unique or may not even exist. If
C^{0}
and
C^{1}
are cycles of
K(Q^{0})
and
K(Q^{1})
, respectively, defined in steps
4
and
5
, where
M^{i}_{d}
,
P^{i}
,
S^{i}
,
i\in\{0,1\}
used in their definition are obtained by some execution of Construction
4
, then the pair
(C^{0},C^{1})
is called a
d
-extension
of the matching
M
in
K(Q_{n})
.

Observation 5
.

If
(C^{0},C^{1})
is a
d
-extension of a matching
M
in
K(Q_{n})
, then there is a cycle
C
in
K(Q_{n})
such that
E(C)=M\cup S
where
S\subseteq E(Q_{n})
and
|V(C)|=|V(C^{0})|+|V(C^{1})|
.

Proof.

Referring to the notation of Construction
4
, replace each edge
v_{i}v_{j}\in P^{1}
in
C^{1}
with the path
v_{i}+P_{u_{i}u_{j}}+v_{j}
. This extends
C^{1}
to a cycle
C
in
K(Q_{n})
such that
E(C)=M\cup S^{0}\cup S^{1}\cup S^{2}
where
S^{2}
consists of edges added in Step
2
or
3
. Then
S^{0}\cup S^{1}\cup S^{2}\subseteq E(Q_{n})
and
|V(C)|=|V(C^{0})\,\mathbin{\dot{\cup}}\,V(C^{1})|=|V(C^{0})|+|V(C^{1})|
.
∎

4
Initial step

The following application of Construction
4
shall be useful as an initial step for the inductive proof of our main result.

Lemma 6
.

Every matching
M
in
K(Q_{n})
,
|M|\leq n\geq 2
, may be extended by short edges to a cycle of length at least
\frac{3}{4}|V(Q_{n})|
.

Proof.

We argue by induction on
n
. The case
n=2
may be verified by a direct inspection.
Next assume that
n>2
and that
M\neq\emptyset
, for otherwise we may use an arbitrary Hamiltonian cycle of
Q_{n}
.

Case A
.

n=3
and for every
d\in[3]
,
either
|M^{2}_{d}|=\emptyset
,
or
|M^{2}_{d}|
is odd and
M
is
d
-saturated.

Recall that if
M
is
d
-saturated, Observation
2
says that
|M|\geq 2+s/2
where
s
is the number of short edges of dimension
d
. Consequently,
|M|=2
would mean that
M
consists of two long edges, which together with

implies that
|M^{2}_{d}|=2
for some

, contrary to our assumption. Hence it must be the case that
|M|=3
. If all these three edges are short,

extends to a Hamiltonian cycle by part (
3
) of Proposition
3
. If at least one edge is long, then there is

such that

and therefore, by our assumption,

.

Hence we can assume that
M=M^{2}_{d}=\{u_{i}v_{i}\mid i\in[3]\}
where
u_{i}
lies in
Q^{0}=Q_{\mkern 5.0mu\overline{\mkern-5.0mu\{d\}\mkern-5.0mu}\mkern 5.0mu}(0)
for all

. Since
M
is
d
-saturated, one of these edges, say

, must have the property that

is not incident with any edge of

. Then
P^{0}=\{u_{1}v_{1}^{d},u_{2}u_{3}
} forms a perfect matching of

and therefore, by the induction hypothesis, it may be extended by short edges to a Hamiltonian cycle
C^{0}
of
Q^{0}
. Since
Q^{1}=Q_{\mkern 5.0mu\overline{\mkern-5.0mu\{d\}\mkern-5.0mu}\mkern 5.0mu}(1)
is a cycle, there exists a path
P_{u_{2}^{d}u_{3}^{d}}
in

avoiding

.
Replacing edges

and

of

and

to a cycle

7>\frac{3}{4}|V(Q_{4})|
such that

where

as required.

Case B
.

n=4
and for every
d\in[4]
,
either
|M^{2}_{d}|=\emptyset
,
or
|M^{2}_{d}|
is odd and
M
is
d
-saturated.

Recall that

such that

is

-saturated. By Observation

,

where

is the number of short edges of

. Since we assume that

, it follows that

Hence we can without loss of generality assume that

while
u_{i}v_{i}\in M^{2}_{d}
where
u_{i}
lies in
Q^{0}=Q_{\mkern 5.0mu\overline{\mkern-5.0mu\{d\}\mkern-5.0mu}\mkern 5.0mu}(0)
for all

.
Since

is
d
-saturated,
P^{0}=\{u_{1}v_{1}^{d},u_{2}v_{2}^{d},u_{3}v_{3}^{d},u_{4}v_{4}

. By part (

) of Proposition

,

of

. Since

, there is an edge

for any

. Replacing edges

of

with the paths

and

, respectively, we extend

to a cycle

such that

where

as required.

Case C
.

Either

, or
n\in\{3,4\}
and there is

such that

and if

is

-saturated then

, let

5
Results

We start with another application of Construction
4
. This time, the assumption on matching size is replaced with a requirement of even distribution of its edges among 4-dimensional subcubes.

Lemma 7
.

Let
M
be a matching in
K(Q_{n})
,
n\geq 4
, and
D\subseteq[n]
such that
|D|=4
and
|\bigcup M_{D}(u)|\leq 7
for every
u\in\{0,1\}^{n-4}
. Moreover, there are at most two distinct

such that

, and if they both exist, then the subcubes
Q_{D}(u)
and
Q_{D}(v)
are adjacent.
Then there is a set
S\subseteq E(Q_{n})
such that
M\cup S
forms a cycle in
K(Q_{n})
of length at least
\frac{3}{4}|V(Q_{n})|
.

We argue by induction on
n
. For
n=4
we have
|\bigcup M|=|\bigcup M_{D}(u)|\leq 7
, which means that
|M|\leq 3
and the statement follows from Lemma
6
. For
n>4
we show that there is a
d
-extension of
M
for certain

. To that end, consider four cases.

Case A
.

n>5
and there are
u\neq v\in\{0,1\}^{n-4}

.

Case B
.

n>5
and there is exactly one

such that
|\bigcup M_{D}(u)|\in\{6,7\}
.

Case C
.

n>5
and
|\bigcup M_{D}(u)|\leq 5
for every
u\in\{0,1\}^{n-4}
.

Case D
.

n=5
.

Note that in Case
A
, the subcubes

and
Q_{D}(v)
are adjacent by our assumption, and hence we can select
d
as the dimension of the edge

. Otherwise

may be an arbitrary element of

(in Case

Now go through the steps of Construction
4
, using the notation introduced there.
To perform Step
2
or
3
, we need to find one or two non-adjacent short edges of dimension
d
, not intersecting any edge of
M
. To that end, select

such that

•

in Case
A
, subcube

is adjacent to

but different from
Q_{D}(v)
,

•

in Case
B
,
Q_{D}(w)
is adjacent to

The Cauchy-Dirichlet problem for parabolic deformed Hermitian-Yang-Mills equation
Liding Huang, Jiaogen Zhang

Abstract
. The purpose of this paper is to investigate the parabolic deformed Hermitian-Yang-Mills equation with hypercritical phase in a smooth domain Ω ⊂ ℂ n \Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n} . By using J J -functional, we are able to prove the convergence of solutions. As an application, we give an alternative proof of the Dirichlet problem for deformed Hermitian-Yang-Mills equation.

58J35, 35J60, 53C07, 35B45

1.
Introduction

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a smooth domain with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
. For any
u\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
, we let
\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u=\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}u
be the complex Hessian of
u
and
\lambda=\lambda(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u)
be the eigenvalues of
\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u
. Throughout the paper, for each
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})
and
\lambda(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u)=(\lambda_{1}(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u),\cdots,\lambda_{n}(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u))
, we denote

\Theta(\lambda)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{arccot}\,\lambda_{i},\qquad\Theta(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u)=\Theta(\lambda(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u))

when no confusion will occur.

The Dirichlet problem for the deformed Hermitian-Yang-Mills (dHYM in short) equation can be written in the following form

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Theta(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u)=\hat{\vartheta}&\mathrm{in}~{\Omega},\\[2.84526pt]
u=\varphi&\mathrm{on}~\partial{\Omega}.\\[2.84526pt]
\end{array}\right.

Here
\varphi
is a smooth functions on
\partial\Omega
. First, we may extend
\varphi
to a smooth function (still denoted by
\varphi
for convenience) such that

(1.2)

0<\Theta(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,\varphi)<\frac{\pi}{2},\quad\mathrm{on\,}\ \overline{\Omega}.

We say the (
1.1
) is
supercritical
if
\hat{\vartheta}\in(0,\pi)
, and of
hypercritical
if
\hat{\vartheta}\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
. In the sequel we are interested in the later case.

The Dirichlet problem (
1.1
) was first posed by Harvey-Lawson
[
11
]
. Under a certain assumption on the geometry of
\partial\Omega
, Caffarelli-Nirenberg-Spruck
[
1
]
established the existence of solutions. Viscosity solutions were also considered by Harvey-Lawson
[
12
]
. Collins-Picard-Wu
[
5
]
proved the existence of classical solutions provided there exists a subsolution on
\Omega
. For more related works, we refer to
[
2
,
8
,
9
,
19
]
and references therein.

The equation (
1.1
) is a local version of the dHYM equation over closed manifolds, which has also been extensively researched motivated by mirror symmetry.
Jacob-Yau
[
14
]
initially studied the dHYM equation and it was solved by Collins-Jacob-Yau
[
4
]
under the existence of a notion of subsolution in the case of supercritical phase. Recently, Collins-Yau
[
6
]
introduced an infinite dimensional GIT approach to the dHYM equation. One of the important roles in this theory is the so-called geodesic equation which can be reduced to a Dirichlet problem for the degenerate dHYM equation.

On the other hand, to study the dHYM equation, the theory of geometric flows have been well developed at the same time. A line bundle version of the Lagrangian mean curvature flow was introduced by Jacob-Yau
[
6
]
. Takahashi
[
18
]
defined the tangent Lagrangian phase flow, which is expected to be effective in the GIT framework. Recently Fu-Yau-Zhang
[
7
]
considered a new geometric flow which resolved the dHYM equation on compact Kähler manifolds. The twisted version of Fu-Yau-Zhang’s flow can be also used to prove the equivalence between coerciveness of the
J
-functional and the existence of solutions to the dHYM equations (see Chu-Lee
[
3
]
).

Before stating our main results it is necessary for us to collect some notations. For each
T>0
,

\mathcal{Q}_{T}=\Omega\times(0,T],\,\,\mathcal{B}=\Omega\times\{0\},\,\,\mathcal{S}=\partial\Omega\times[0,T],\,\,\partial_{p}\mathcal{Q}_{T}=\mathcal{B}\cup\mathcal{S}.

Here,
\mathcal{B}
is the
bottom
of
\mathcal{Q}_{T}
,
\mathcal{S}
is the
side
of
\mathcal{Q}_{T}
and
\partial_{p}\mathcal{Q}_{T}
is the
parabolic

boundary
of
\mathcal{Q}_{T}
.

Inspired by the work in
[
7
]
, in this paper we shall study the Cauchy-Dirichlet problem about parabolic dHYM equation

(1.3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}u=\cot\Theta(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u)-\cot\hat{\vartheta}&\mathrm{in}~{\mathcal{Q}_{T}},\\[2.84526pt]
u=\psi&\mathrm{on}~\partial_{p}\mathcal{Q}_{T},\\[2.84526pt]
\end{array}\right.

where
\partial_{t}u=\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}
,
\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u
denotes the spatial Hessian of
u
,
\hat{\vartheta}
is a given constant, and
\psi=\psi(x,t)
is a smooth function on
\partial_{p}{\mathcal{Q}_{T}}
satisfies

C1)

\psi(\cdot,0)=\varphi
on
\overline{\Omega}
,

C2)

0<\vartheta_{0}<\Theta(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,\psi)<\frac{\pi}{2}-\vartheta_{0}
on
\mathcal{S}
with
\vartheta_{0}\in(0,\,\frac{\pi}{4})
is a given constant for any
T>0
.

We remark that such function
\psi
is always exists, for instance, we can choose
\psi(\cdot,t)=\varphi(\cdot)
be a constant valued function in
[0,T]
.

The main result concerns the problems of the long time existence of flow (
1.3
) and the solvability of (
1.1
). The statement is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose there exists a parabolic subsolution
\underline{u}\in C^{2,1}(\overline{\Omega}\times[0,\,\infty))
for the flow (
1.3
), i.e. for every
T>0
,

(1.4)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}\underline{u}\leq\cot\Theta(\mathrm{Hess}\,\underline{u})-\cot\hat{\vartheta}&\mathrm{in}~{\mathcal{Q}_{T}},\\[2.84526pt]
\underline{u}\leq\varphi\quad\mathrm{on}~\mathcal{B}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\underline{u}=\psi&\mathrm{on}~\mathcal{S}.\\[2.84526pt]
\end{array}\right.

Assume that
\Theta(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,\underline{u})\in(\vartheta_{0},\frac{\pi}{2}-\vartheta_{0})
.
Then the solution
u
of flow (
1.3
) exists for all time. Furthermore, as
t\rightarrow+\infty
,
u(\cdot,t)
approaches to the unique solution
u_{\infty}
for (
1.1
) in
C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
.

Remark 1.2
.

Here we consider the hypercritical case since we need this assumption in the proof of
C^{0}
estimates (see Proposition
2.6
).

The paper is organized as follows. In section 2, we first recall the definition and some properties of parabolic subsolutions which play important roles in the proof of global
C^{2}
estimates. Secondly, using the maximum principle, we prove the
C^{0}
and the
C^{1}
estimates. Finally, the main challenge is to prove the boundary second order estimates. It can be solved by employing the barrier function in
[
9
]
and the idea of Trudinger
[
19
]
. In section 3 we generalize the definitions of Calabi-Yau functional and
J
-functional over smooth domains in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Using the monotonicity of
J
-functional along (
1.3
), we obtain the convergence of flow (
1.3
) via the arguments of
[
10
,
15
]
.

2.
A priori estimates

By a simple computation, we get

\cot\Theta(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u)=\frac{\textrm{Re}\det(\sqrt{-1}\delta_{kl}+u_{k\bar{l}})}{\textrm{Im}\det(\sqrt{-1}\delta_{kl}+u_{k\bar{l}})}.

Let us denote

F^{i\bar{j}}(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u)=\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{i\bar{j}}}\cot\Theta(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u),

which determines a positive definite Hermitian form. Then

Lu=\partial_{t}u-F^{i\bar{j}}u_{i\bar{j}}

is a parabolic linearized operator of (
1.3
).
We assume that there exist solutions of the flow (
1.3
) on
\Omega\times[0,\,T^{\prime})
.
In this section, we will prove the estimates of solutions on
\mathcal{Q}_{T}
, where
T<T^{\prime}
,

2.1.
Subsolutions

Let us
denote
\Gamma_{n}
be the positive orthant in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and

\Gamma=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\ 0<\Theta(\lambda)<\frac{\pi}{2}\},\qquad\Gamma^{\sigma}=\{\lambda\in\Gamma:0<\Theta(\lambda)<\sigma\},

where
\sigma\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
is a constant.

Definition 2.1
.

[
17
]

We say that a smooth function
\underline{u}:\overline{\Omega}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a
\mathcal{C}
-subsolution of (
1.1
) if at each point
x\in\overline{\Omega}
, the set

\left\{\lambda\in\Gamma:\Theta(\lambda)=\hat{\vartheta},\text{\ and\ }\lambda-\lambda(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,\underline{u})\in\Gamma_{n}\right\}

is bounded.

For the (
1.1
), Collins-Jacob-Yau
[
4
]
(see also Collins-Picard-Wu
[
5
]
) provided an explicit description of subsolutions.

Lemma 2.2
.

A smooth function
\underline{u}:\overline{\Omega}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a subsolution of (
1.1
) if and only if at each point
x\in\overline{\Omega}
,

\max_{1\leq j\leq n}\sum_{i\neq j}\mathrm{arccot}\,\lambda_{i}(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,\underline{u})(x)<\hat{\vartheta}.

We shall recall the terminology of parabolic subsolutions for the parabolic dHYM equations, as already defined in
[
16
, Definition 1]
.

Definition 2.3
.

We say a function
\underline{u}_{p}\in C^{2,1}(\mathcal{Q}_{T})
is a parabolic
\mathcal{C}
-subsolution for (
1.3
), if there exist uniform constants
\delta_{0},R_{0}>0
, such that for any
(x,t)\in\mathcal{Q}_{T}
,

(2.1)

\cot\Theta(\lambda(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,\underline{u}_{p})+\mu)-\partial_{t}\underline{u}_{p}+\tau=\cot\hat{\vartheta},~\mu+\delta_{0}I\in\Gamma_{n}~\textrm{and}~\tau>-\delta_{0}

implies that
|\mu|+|\tau|<R_{0}
. Here
I
denotes the vector
(1,\cdots,1)
of
n
-tuples.

The following proposition is principle, which is inspired by the recent work of Phong-Tô
[
16
]
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\delta_{0},R_{0}>0
be uniform constants as in Definition
2.3
and
\underline{u}
be a parabolic subsolution of (
1.3
). Then there exists a constant
\kappa=\kappa(\delta_{0},R_{0})
such that if
|\lambda(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u)|\geq R_{0}
we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{either}

\displaystyle L(u-\underline{u})\geq\kappa\mathcal{F}\,,

\displaystyle\mathrm{or}

\displaystyle F^{k\bar{k}}(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u)\geq\kappa\mathcal{F}\,,\qquad\text{for all }k=1,\dots,n\,.

Here and hereafter we denote
\mathcal{F}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}F^{i\bar{i}}(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u)
.

Proof.

Using the argument of Phong-Tô
[
16
, Lemma 3]
, we shall prove
that
\underline{u}
is also a parabolic
\mathcal{C}
-subsolution. As pointed in
[
16
, Lemma 8]
, it suffices to show there exists a uniform constant
\varepsilon>0
such that for any
i=1,2,\cdots,n
,

(2.2)

\lim_{s\rightarrow\infty}\cot\Theta(\underline{\lambda}+s\,\textbf{e}_{i})-\partial_{t}\underline{u}>\varepsilon+\cot\hat{\vartheta},

where we have used the simplified notation
\lambda_{j}(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,\underline{u})=\underline{\lambda}_{j}
. It is clear that
\lim_{s\rightarrow\infty}\Theta(\underline{\lambda}+s\textbf{e}_{i})=\sum_{j\neq i}\mathrm{arccot}\,\underline{\lambda}_{j}
. Therefore (
2.2
) is equivalent to

(2.3)

\cot\Big(\sum_{j\neq i}\mathrm{arccot}\,\underline{\lambda}_{j}\Big)-(\partial_{t}\underline{u}+\cot\hat{\vartheta})>\varepsilon,

which can be verified if we set
\varepsilon=\frac{1}{2}\min_{1\leq i\leq n}\inf_{\overline{\Omega}\times[0,\infty)}\mathrm{arccot}\,\underline{\lambda}_{i}.

Indeed, using the concavity of
\lambda\mapsto\cot\Theta(\lambda)
, we get

\begin{split}\mathrm{arccot}\,\underline{\lambda}_{i}=&\Theta(\underline{\lambda})-\sum_{j\neq i}\mathrm{arccot}\,\underline{\lambda}_{j}\\
\leq&\cot\Big(\sum_{j\neq i}\mathrm{arccot}\,\underline{\lambda}_{j}\Big)-\cot\Theta(\underline{\lambda})\\
\leq&\cot\Big(\sum_{j\neq i}\mathrm{arccot}\,\underline{\lambda}_{j}\Big)-(\partial_{t}\underline{u}+\cot\hat{\vartheta}).\end{split}

Here, in the last inequality we have used (
1.4
). By the definition of
\varepsilon
, then this proves (
2.3
).
∎

2.2.
The
\partial_{t}u
estimate.

Proposition 2.5
.

On
\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{T}
, there exists a constant
\delta_{1}\in(0,\frac{\pi}{4})
depending on
\psi
,
\vartheta_{0}
and
\hat{\vartheta}
such that

(a)

\frac{\pi}{2}-\delta_{1}>\Theta(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u)>\delta_{1}.

(b)

|\partial_{t}u|\leq\frac{1}{\delta_{1}}.

(c)

\mathcal{F}>\delta_{1}.

Proof.

Differentiating (
1.3
) in
t
variable we get
L(\partial_{t}u)=0.

By maximum principle, we get
\inf_{\partial_{p}\mathcal{Q}_{T}}\partial_{t}u\leq\partial_{t}u\leq\sup_{\partial_{p}\mathcal{Q}_{T}}\partial_{t}u
and hence

\inf_{\partial_{p}\mathcal{Q}_{T}}\Theta(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,\psi)\leq\Theta(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u)\leq\sup_{\partial_{p}\mathcal{Q}_{T}}\Theta(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,\psi).

Note that

\sup_{\partial_{p}\mathcal{Q}_{T}}\Theta(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,\psi)=\max\{\sup_{\partial\Omega}\Theta(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,\varphi),\sup_{\mathcal{S}}\Theta(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,\psi)\}\leq\frac{\pi}{2}-\vartheta_{0}

by using (
1.2
) and C2). Similarly, we also get
\inf_{\partial_{p}\mathcal{Q}_{T}}\Theta(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,\psi)>0
. These complete the proof of (a) and (b).

We remark that, as in
[
16
]
, there exists a constant
\delta>0
depending on
\|\partial_{t}u\|_{C^{0}}
such that
\mathcal{F}>\delta
. This proves (c).
∎

2.3.
C^{0}
estimate

Proposition 2.6
.

We have on
\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{T}
,

(2.4)

\underline{u}\leq u\leq\sup_{\mathcal{S}}\underline{u}.

Therefore, there exists a uniform constant
C_{1}
such that
|u|\leq C_{1}
on
\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{T}
.

Proof.

The first inequality in (
2.4
) is follows from the comparison principle. If we order that
\lambda_{1}(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u)\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n}(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u)
in
\mathcal{Q}_{T}
, then by Proposition (
2.5
) we immediately have
\lambda_{n}(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u)\geq\tan(\delta_{1})
. This implies
u(\cdot,t)
is plurisubharmonic in
\Omega
. For any
t\in[0,T]
, we see that
u(\cdot,t)
achieves its maximum on
\partial\Omega
, i.e.

u(\cdot,t)\leq\sup_{\partial\Omega}u(\cdot,t)=\sup_{\partial\Omega}\underline{u}(\cdot,t)

for all
t\in[0,T]
, and then the second inequality follows.
∎

2.4.
Gradient estimate

Extend
\psi
to a spatial harmonic function
\overline{u}
which satisfies
\Delta\overline{u}(\cdot,t)=0
in
\Omega
and
\overline{u}(\cdot,t)=\psi(\cdot,t)
in
\overline{\Omega}
, for any
t\in[0,T]
. By maximum principle we conclude
\underline{u}\leq u\leq\overline{u}
on
\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{T}
and
\underline{u}=u=\overline{u}
on
\mathcal{S}
.
This also gives us

(2.5)

|Du|\leq|D\underline{u}|+|D\overline{u}|\leq C_{2}\quad\mathrm{on}\ \mathcal{S}

for a uniform constant
C_{2}
.

In the rest of this section, we shall give the interior gradient estimate.

Proposition 2.7
.

There exists a uniform constant
C_{3}
such that

|Du|\leq C_{3}\quad\mathrm{on}\ \overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{T}.

Proof.

For any
k=1,\cdots n
, let us consider the quantity

v=\pm u_{x_{k}}+|z|^{2}\quad\mathrm{on}\ \overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{T}.

Suppose that
v
attains its maximum at an interior point
p_{0}=(z_{0},t_{0})
of
\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{T}
, otherwise we are done.
By an orthogonal transformation, we may further assume the complex Hessian
(u_{i\bar{j}})
is diagonal at
p_{0}
. It follows that
F^{i\bar{j}}
is also diagonal at
p_{0}
and

F^{i\bar{j}}=\frac{1+\cot^{2}\Theta(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u)}{1+\lambda^{2}_{j}(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u)}\delta_{ij}.

It follows from Proposition
2.5
and the maximal principle that at
p_{0}
,

\begin{split}Lv=&v_{t}-F^{i\bar{j}}v_{i\bar{j}}=\pm\big((\partial_{t}u)_{x_{k}}-F^{i\bar{j}}u_{i\bar{j}x_{k}}\big)-\mathcal{F}\leq-\delta_{1}<0,\end{split}

which is absurd. This implies
v
must achieve its maximum on
\mathcal{S}
. Together with (
2.5
), we obtain the global gradient estimate.
∎

2.5.
Interior second order estimate

Proposition 2.8
.

There exists a uniform constant
C_{4}
such that

(2.6)

|D^{2}u|\leq C_{4}\Big(1+\max_{\mathcal{S}}|D^{2}u|\Big)\quad\mathrm{on}\ \overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{T}.

Proof.

Similar to the gradient estimate, let us consider a quantity

w=\Delta u+|z|^{2}\quad\mathrm{on}\ \overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{T}.

Claim 2.1
.

w
cannot attain its maximum at an interior point of
\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{T}
.

Given this, as well as
u(\cdot,t)
is plurisubharmonic in
\overline{\Omega}
, the inequality (
2.6
) follows.

Proof of Claim
2.1
.

We fix an arbitrary interior point
q_{0}=(z_{0},t_{0})
of
\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{T}
.
By an orthogonal transformation, we may further assume the complex Hessian
(u_{i\bar{j}})
is diagonal at
q_{0}
. Using the concavity of
F
,

\begin{split}Lw=&\partial_{t}w-F^{i\bar{j}}w_{i\bar{j}}=\Delta(\partial_{t}u)-F^{i\bar{j}}(\Delta u)_{i\bar{j}}-\mathcal{F}\leq-\delta_{1}<0,\end{split}

which is impossible. By arbitrariness of
q_{0}
, the claim follows.
∎

Consequently, we have finished the proof of Proposition
2.8
. ∎

2.6.
Boundary second order estimates

In this subsection we shall prove

\sup_{\mathcal{S}}|D^{2}u|\leq C_{5}

for a uniform constant
C_{5}.
We need the following lemma.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
\delta_{0},R_{0}>0
be uniform constants as in Definition
2.3
and
\underline{u}
be a subsolution of (
1.1
). There exists a constant
\delta_{3}=\delta_{3}(\delta_{0},R_{0},\psi,\underline{u},\vartheta_{0},\hat{\vartheta})
such that if
|\lambda(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u)|\geq R_{0}
, one of following statements holds:

(2.7)

\displaystyle\mathrm{either}\,

\displaystyle L(u-\underline{u})\geq\delta_{3}\,,

(2.8)

\displaystyle\mathrm{or}\,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\delta_{3}}\geq\lambda_{1}(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u)\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n}(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u)\geq\tan(\delta_{1})\,\ \mathrm{on}\ \overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{T}.

Here we have ordered
\lambda_{1}(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u)\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n}(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u)
.

Proof.

Using Lemma
2.4
, there exists a constant
\kappa=\kappa(\delta_{0},R_{0})
such that one of the following inequalities holds:

(a)

L(u-\underline{u})\geq\kappa\mathcal{F}
.

(b)

F^{k\bar{k}}(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u)\geq\kappa\mathcal{F}
for any
k=1,2,\cdots,n
.

If (a) holds, we immediately obtain (
2.7
).
Now we assume (b) holds. Then
F^{1\bar{1}}\geq\kappa F^{n\bar{n}}
and hence
\lambda_{n}(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u)\geq\sqrt{\kappa(\lambda^{2}_{1}(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u)-1)}
. It follows from
Propsition
2.5
(a) that
\tan(\delta_{1})\leq\lambda_{n}(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u)\leq\cot(\frac{\delta_{1}}{n})
.
These give a uniform upper bound of
\lambda_{1}(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u)
.

∎

Remark 2.10
.

We remind that (
2.8
) gives us the global second order estimates. In the sequel, we always assume (
2.7
) holds.

Let us fix a point
p_{0}=(z,t_{0})\in\mathcal{S}
. Choose coordinates
z_{1},z_{2},\cdots,z_{n}
with
z_{i}=x_{i}+\sqrt{-1}y_{i}
for
\Omega
. After a translation we may assume
z=0
and the positive
x_{n}
axis is the direction of interior normal of
\partial\Omega
at 0. We denote

s_{2k-1}=x_{k},\,s_{2k}=y_{k},\,1\leq k\leq n-1;\,s_{2n-1}=y_{n},\,s_{2n}=x_{n}

and
s^{\prime}=(s_{1},\cdots,s_{2n-1})
. Near the origin, we may
assume
\partial\Omega
is represented as a graph

x_{n}=\rho(s^{\prime})=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1}^{2n-1}\rho_{\alpha\beta}(0)s_{\alpha}s_{\beta}+O(|s^{\prime}|^{3}).

2.6.1.
Pure tangential estimate

Since
(u-\underline{u})(s^{\prime},\rho(s^{\prime}),t)=0
, one derives

(2.9)

(u-\underline{u})_{s_{\alpha}s_{\beta}}(p_{0})=-(u-\underline{u})_{x_{n}}(p_{0})\rho_{\alpha\beta}(0),\qquad\alpha,\beta<2n.

This gives the desired estimate.

2.6.2.
Mixed direction estimate

Next we proceed to prove

(2.10)

|u_{x_{n}s_{\alpha}}|(p_{0})\leq C_{5},\qquad\alpha<2n.

For a small constant
\varepsilon>0
and set

\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon,p_{0}}=(\Omega\cap B_{\varepsilon}(0))\times(0,t_{0}).

Let
d(z)=\text{dist}(z,\partial\Omega)
be distance function from
\partial\Omega
.
The following lemma plays a key role in our proof.

Lemma 2.11
.

There exist some uniform positive constants
s
and
\varepsilon
small and
N
large such that the function

v=(u-\underline{u})+sd-Nd^{2}

satisfies

(2.11)

\begin{cases}v\geq\frac{s}{2}d\quad&\mathrm{on}\,(B_{\varepsilon}(0)\cap\Omega)\times\{0\}.\\[2.84526pt]
v\geq 0\quad&\mathrm{on}\,\partial(\Omega\cap B_{\delta}(0))\times(0,t_{0}].\\[2.84526pt]
L(v)\geq\varepsilon_{0}\quad&\mathrm{in}\,\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon,p_{0}},\end{cases}

where
\varepsilon_{0}>0
is a constant depending on
\varphi,\,\underline{u},\,\vartheta_{0},\,\hat{\vartheta}
.

Proof.

Since
\underline{u}\leq u
by (
2.4
), one derives
v\geq 0
on
\partial(\Omega\cap B_{\varepsilon}(0))\times(0,t_{0})
if we further require
\varepsilon\ll s\ll 1
such that
N\varepsilon<\frac{s}{2}
. In this case we also have the first inequality in (
2.11
). Now we divide the proof of last inequality in (
2.11
) into two possibilities:

(a)

Assume
\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i\bar{i}}\leq R_{0}
for the constant
R_{0}
in Definition

\begin{split}L(v)&=\partial_{t}u-F^{i\bar{j}}(u_{i\bar{j}}-\underline{u}_{i\bar{j}})-(s-Nd)F^{i\bar{j}}d_{i\bar{j}}+NF^{i\bar{j}}d_{i}d_{\bar{j}}\\

for a uniform constant
\varepsilon_{0}>0

(b)

Suppose now that

In this case, we also have

∎

For each integer

(2.12)

\begin{cases}\lim\sup_{(s^{\prime},x_{n},t)\rightarrow(s^{\prime},\rho(s^{\prime}),t)}|T_{\alpha}(u-\underline{u})|&\leq C|s^{\prime}|^{2}.\\[2.84526pt]

Here the constant

Let us employ the barrier function

It follows from (

Lw>0\quad\text{in}\,\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon,p_{0}},\qquad\underset{x\rightarrow\partial_{p}\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon,p_{0}}}{\lim\inf}w(x)\geq 0.

Let

In fact, if (

\begin{split}\cot\vartheta\leq\cot\Theta(\lambda^{\prime}(\tilde{U}),D_{1})-\frac{c_{0}}{2}\\

If we set

\mathrm{arccot}\lambda_{n}\geq\mathrm{arccot}D_{1}+\mathrm{arccot}\Theta(\lambda^{\prime}(\tilde{U}))-\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\mathrm{arccot}\lambda_{i}+\frac{c_{0}}{2}\sin^{2}\Theta(\lambda^{\prime}(\tilde{U}),D_{1})

Here

Note that

It follows

Suppose that
\tilde{m}<\infty

\tilde{m}

\mathrm{Re}\,e_{n}=\partial_{x_{n}}
is the

Let us denote

\widetilde{F}[E]=\lim_{D\rightarrow\infty}\cot\Theta(\lambda^{\prime}(E),D),\qquad\widetilde{F}_{0}^{\alpha\bar{\beta}}=\frac{\partial\widetilde{F}}{\partial E_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}}[\tilde{U}(p_{0})]

Hermitian matrices with

Hence,

Similar to (

Insert it into (

\begin{split}(u-\underline{u})_{x_{n}}(p_{0})\widetilde{F}_{0}^{\alpha\bar{\beta}}\rho_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}(p_{0})\geq&\widetilde{F}[\tilde{\underline{U}}(p_{0})]-\widetilde{F}[\tilde{U}(p_{0})]\\

Here the constant

Now we divide the proof of (

Case 1.
Assume

Then (

Case 2.
Suppose now that

(u-\underline{u})_{x_{n}}(p_{0})\widetilde{F}_{0}^{\alpha\bar{\beta}}\rho_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}(0)\geq\frac{\tilde{c}}{2}.

Let us denote

Note that

\eta(p_{0})\geq\frac{\tilde{c}}{2(u-\underline{u})_{x_{n}}(p_{0})}\geq 2\varepsilon_{1}\tilde{c}

\varepsilon_{1}>0

\eta\geq\varepsilon_{1}\tilde{c}

\mathcal{Q}_{\varepsilon,p_{0}}

\varepsilon

\Phi

\begin{split}\Phi=&-\eta(u-\underline{u})_{x_{n}}+\widetilde{F}_{0}^{\alpha\bar{\beta}}\big(\tilde{\underline{U}}_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}-\tilde{U}_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}(p_{0})\big)-\partial_{t}u+\partial_{t}u(p_{0}).\\
\end{split}

3.

Consider the potential space

\mathcal{H}=\{v\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)\ |\ \Theta(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,v)\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2}),\,v|_{\partial\Omega}=\varphi\}.

3.1.
Calabi-Yau functional

Fix
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}
and for each
\psi\in\mathcal{H}
, we define

(3.1)

\begin{split}CY_{\varphi}(\psi)=&\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{s}v\Big[(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,v+\sqrt{-1}\omega_{0})^{n}-(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,\varphi+\sqrt{-1}\omega_{0})^{n}\Big]ds\\
&+\int_{\Omega}\psi(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,\varphi+\sqrt{-1}\omega_{0})^{n},\end{split}

where
v(\cdot,s)
is a path in
\mathcal{H}
which connects
\varphi
and
\psi
, and
\omega_{0}=\sqrt{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}dz_{i}\wedge d\bar{z}_{i}
is the standard metric in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

Proposition 3.1
.

CY_{\varphi}(\psi)
is well-defined and satisfies

(3.2)

\delta CY_{\varphi}(\psi)=\int_{\Omega}(\delta\psi)(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,\psi+\sqrt{-1}\omega_{0})^{n}.

Proof.

We need to show that the integration defined in (
3.1
) is independent of the choice of path
v(\cdot,s)
in
\mathcal{H}
.

Let
v(\cdot,s,r)
be a family of paths in
\mathcal{H}
satisfying
v(\cdot,0,r)=\varphi
and
v(\cdot,1,r)=\psi
with
s,r\in[0,1]
.
Let
w(\cdot,\cdot,r)=\partial_{r}v(\cdot,\cdot,r)
satisfying

w(\cdot,\cdot,1)=w(\cdot,\cdot,0)=0\text{\ \ and\ \ }w(z,\cdot,\cdot)=0\text{\, for\, }z\in\partial\Omega.

Then, using
w(\cdot,\cdot,1)=0
,

\begin{split}\partial_{r}CY_{\varphi}(\psi)=&\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{s}w\Big[(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,v+\sqrt{-1}\omega_{0})^{n}-(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,\varphi+\sqrt{-1}\omega_{0})^{n}\Big]ds\\
&+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{s}v\cdot n\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,w\wedge(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,v+\sqrt{-1}\omega_{0})^{n-1}ds\\
=&I_{1}+I_{2}.\end{split}

For the first term
I_{1}
, using integration by parts with respect to
s
and
z
respectively, we have

\begin{split}I_{1}&=-\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega}w\cdot n\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}(\partial_{s}v)\wedge(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u+\sqrt{-1}\omega_{0})^{n-1}ds\\
&=-\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{s}v\cdot n\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,w\wedge(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u+\sqrt{-1}\omega_{0})^{n-1}ds.\end{split}

Therefore
\partial_{r}CY_{\varphi}(\psi)=0
. It follows that
CY_{\varphi}(\psi)
is well-defined. In a similar way one can also prove (
3.2
).
∎

3.2.
J
-functional

Let
u(\cdot,s)
be a path in
\mathcal{H}
from
u(\cdot,0)=\varphi
to
u(\cdot,1)=\psi
.
Now, we introduce the so-called
J
-functional by

(3.3)

J(u)=\mathrm{Im}(e^{-\sqrt{-1}\hat{\vartheta}}CY(u)).

Claim 3.1
.

For each
u\in\mathcal{H}
,

\begin{split}\mathrm{Im}\Big(e^{-\sqrt{-1}\hat{\vartheta}}&(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u+\sqrt{-1}\omega_{0})^{n}\Big)\\
=&-\sin(\hat{\vartheta})\Big[\cot\Theta(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u)-\cot\hat{\vartheta}\Big]\mathrm{Im}(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u+\sqrt{-1}\omega_{0})^{n}.\end{split}

Proof of Claim
3.1
.

By a direct calculation,

\begin{split}\mathrm{Im}&\Big(e^{-\sqrt{-1}\hat{\vartheta}}(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u+\sqrt{-1}\omega_{0})^{n}\Big)\\
=&\mathrm{Im}\Big((\cos(\hat{\vartheta})-\sqrt{-1}\sin(\hat{\vartheta}))\big(\mathrm{Re}(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u+\sqrt{-1}\omega_{0})^{n}+\sqrt{-1}\mathrm{Im}(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u+\sqrt{-1}\omega_{0})^{n}\big)\Big)\\
=&\cos(\hat{\vartheta})\mathrm{Im}(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u+\sqrt{-1}\omega_{0})^{n}-\sin(\hat{\vartheta})\mathrm{Re}(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u+\sqrt{-1}\omega_{0})^{n}\\
=&-\sin(\hat{\vartheta})\Big[\frac{\mathrm{Re}(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u+\sqrt{-1}\omega_{0})^{n}}{\mathrm{Im}(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u+\sqrt{-1}\omega_{0})^{n}}-\cot\hat{\vartheta}\Big]\mathrm{Im}(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u+\sqrt{-1}\omega_{0})^{n}\\
=&-\sin(\hat{\vartheta})\Big[\cot\Theta(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u)-\cot\hat{\vartheta}\Big]\mathrm{Im}(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u+\sqrt{-1}\omega_{0})^{n}.\end{split}

This completes the proof.
∎

By Claim
3.1
, we immediately see that the variation of
J
is given by

\delta J(u)=-\int_{\Omega}(\delta u)\cdot\sin(\hat{\vartheta})\Big[\cot\Theta(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u)-\cot\hat{\vartheta}\Big]\mathrm{Im}(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u+\sqrt{-1}\omega_{0})^{n}.

Then the (negative) gradient flow of
J
-functional is exactly the heat flow (
1.3
), i.e.

\frac{d}{dt}J(u)=-\int_{\Omega}(\partial_{t}u)^{2}\cdot\sin(\hat{\vartheta})\mathrm{Im}(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u+\sqrt{-1}\omega_{0})^{n}\leq 0.

Thus
J(u(\cdot,t))
is monotonic decreasing as
t\rightarrow+\infty
.

Combining with (
3.3
),
J(u)
is also bounded. Therefore,

(3.4)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}(\partial_{t}u)^{2}\cdot\sin(\hat{\vartheta})\mathrm{Im}(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u+\sqrt{-1}\omega_{0})^{n}dt<+\infty.

We set

S(t)=\int_{\Omega}(\partial_{t}u)^{2}\cdot\sin(\hat{\vartheta})\mathrm{Im}(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u+\sqrt{-1}\omega_{0})^{n}\geq 0.

Thus (
3.4
) implies

(3.5)

\int_{0}^{\infty}S(t)dt<\infty.

Claim 3.2
.

S(t)\rightarrow 0
, as
t\rightarrow+\infty
.

Proof of Claim
3.2
.

Differentiating (
1.3
) in t, we have

\begin{split}\partial^{2}_{t}u=&\frac{n\text{Re}(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u\wedge(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u+\sqrt{-1}\omega_{0})^{n-1})}{\text{Im}(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u+\sqrt{-1}\omega_{0})^{n})}\\
&-\cot\Theta(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u)\frac{n\text{Im}(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u\wedge(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u+\sqrt{-1}\omega_{0})^{n-1})}{\text{Im}(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u+\sqrt{-1}\omega_{0})^{n})}.\end{split}

Based on the a priori estimates in Section 2, it follows that

\begin{split}S^{\prime}=\int_{\Omega}&2n\partial_{t}u\sin(\hat{\vartheta})\mathrm{Re}(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u\wedge(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u+\sqrt{-1}\omega_{0})^{n-1})\\
&-\int_{\Omega}2n\partial_{t}u\cos(\hat{\vartheta})\mathrm{Im}(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u\wedge(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u+\sqrt{-1}\omega_{0})^{n-1})\\
&+\int_{\Omega}n(\partial_{t}u)^{2}\sin(\hat{\vartheta})\mathrm{Im}(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u\wedge(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u+\sqrt{-1}\omega_{0})^{n-1})\\
\leq C\int_{\Omega}&\big((\partial_{t}u)^{2}+|\partial_{t}u|\big)\omega_{0}^{n}\leq C(S+\sqrt{S}),\end{split}

where in the last inequality we have used Proposition
2.5
to derive
\mathrm{Im}(\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathbb{C}}\,u+\sqrt{-1}\omega_{0})^{n}\geq\frac{1}{C}\omega_{0}^{n}
. By solving the above ODE problem, we get

(3.6)

\sqrt{S(t)}+1\leq e^{C(t-s)}(\sqrt{S(s)}+1),\qquad\forall\,t>s.

Using (
3.5
), we have for any positive constant
\varepsilon
, there exists
t_{0}=t_{0}(\varepsilon)
such that

|\{t>t_{0}:\,S(t)>\varepsilon\}|\leq\varepsilon.

Therefore,
for any
t\geq t_{0}
, we can find a
t^{\prime}\in\{t>t_{0}:\,S(t)\leq\varepsilon\}
with
0<t-t^{\prime}\leq\varepsilon
. Thanks to (

), it follows that

\sqrt{S(t)}\leq\sqrt{S(t^{\prime})}e^{C\varepsilon}+e^{C\varepsilon}-1\leq\sqrt{\varepsilon}e^{C\varepsilon}+C\varepsilon e^{C\varepsilon}.

By arbitrariness of
\varepsilon
, the Claim
3.2
follows.
∎

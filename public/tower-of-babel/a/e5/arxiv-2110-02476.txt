Abstract
Let 𝒞 \mathscr{C} be an n n -exangulated category. In this note, we show that if 𝒞 \mathscr{C} is locally finite, then 𝒞 \mathscr{C} has Auslander-Reiten n n -exangles. This unifies and extends results of Xiao–Zhu, Zhu–Zhuang, Zhou and Xie–Lu–Wang for triangulated, extriangulated, ( n + 2 ) (n+2) -angulated and n n -abelian categories, respectively. Keywords: n n -exangulated categories; Auslander-Reiten n n -exangles; locally finite; extriangulated categories; triangulated categories; n n -abelian categories 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 18G80; 18E10; 18G50

Introduction

The notion of extriangulated categories was introduced by Nakaoka–Palu in
[
17
]
, which can be viewed
as a simultaneous generalization of exact categories and triangulated categories.
The data of such a category is a triplet
(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
, where
\mathscr{C}
is an additive category,
\mathbb{E}:\mathscr{C}^{\rm op}\times\mathscr{C}\rightarrow\mathsf{Ab}
is an additive bifunctor and
\mathfrak{s}
assigns to each
\delta\in\mathbb{E}(C,A)
a class of
3
-term sequences with end terms
A
and
C
such that certain axioms hold. Recently, Herschend–Liu–Nakaoka
[
8
]

introduced the notion of
n
-exangulated categories for any positive integer
n
. It is not only a higher dimensional analogue of extriangulated categories,
but also gives a common generalization of
n
-exact categories in the sense of
Jasso
[
13
]
and
(n+2)
-angulated in the sense of Geiss–Keller–Oppermann
[
6
]
.
However,
there are some other examples of
n
-exangulated categories which are neither
n
-exact nor
(n+2)
-angulated, see
[
8
,
16
,
9
]
.

Auslander-Reiten theory was introduced by Auslander and Reiten in
[
1
,
2
]
. Since its introduction, Auslander-Reiten theory has become a fundamental tool for
studying the representation theory of Artin algebras.
Later it has been generalized to these situation of exact categories
[
12
]
, triangulated categories
[
7
,
18
]
and its subcategories
[
3
,
14
]

and some certain additive categories
[
15
,
14
,
19
]
by many authors.
Iyama, Nakaoka and Palu
[
11
]
developed Auslander–Reiten theory for extriangulated categories.
This unifies Auslander–Reiten theories in exact categories and triangulated categories independently.
Xiao and Zhu
[
21
,
22
]
showed that if a triangulated category
\mathscr{C}
is locally finite, then
\mathscr{C}
has Auslander-Reiten triangles. Recently, Zhu and Zhuang
[
25
]
proved that if an extriangulated category
\mathscr{C}
is locally finite, then
\mathscr{C}
has Auslander-Reiten
\mathbb{E}
-triangles.
Later, Zhou
[
24
]
extended Xiao-Zhu’s result into
(n+2)
-angulated categories. Namely, Zhou proved that if an
(n+2)
-angulated category
\mathscr{C}
is locally finite, then
\mathscr{C}
has Auslander-Reiten
(n+2)
-angules. Subsequently, Xie-Lu-Wang
[
20
]
proved a similar result to Zhou. More precisely, they showed that if an
n
-abelian category
\mathscr{C}
is locally finite, then
\mathscr{C}
has
n
-Auslander-Reiten sequences. Based on this idea, we have a natural question of whether their results of Zhou
[
24
]
and Xie-Lu-Wang
[
20
]
can be unified under the framework of
n
-exangulated categories or whether the result of Zhu-Zhuang
[
25
]
has a higher counterpart. In this article, we give an affirmative answer.

Our main result is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

(see Theorem
3.10
for details)

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a locally finite
n
-exangulated category. If
X\in\mathsf{ind}\hskip 0.72229pt(\mathscr{C})
is a non-projective object, then there exists an Auslander-Reiten
n
-exangle ending at
X
, and if
Y\in\mathsf{ind}\hskip 0.72229pt(\mathscr{C})
is a non-injective object, then there exists an Auslander-Reiten
n
-exangle starting at
Y
.
In this case, we say that
\mathscr{C}
has Auslander-Reiten
n
-exangles.

This article is organized as follows: In Section 2, we recall the definition of
n
-exangulated category and review some results. In Section 3, we show our main result.

Definition 2.1
.

[
8
, Definition 2.7]

Let
\bf{C}_{\mathscr{C}}
be the category of complexes in
\mathscr{C}
. As its full subcategory, define
{\bf{C}}^{n+2}_{\mathscr{C}}
to be the category of complexes in
\mathscr{C}
whose components are zero in the degrees outside of

. Namely, an object in

We write a morphism

simply
f_{\bullet}=(f_{0},f_{1},\ldots,f_{n+1})
, only indicating the terms of degrees

.

Definition 2.2
.

[
8
, Definition 2.11]

By Yoneda lemma, any extension
\delta\in\mathbb{E}(C,A)
induces natural transformations

\delta_{\sharp}\colon\mathscr{C}(-,C)\Rightarrow\mathbb{E}(-,A)\ \ \text{and}\ \ \delta^{\sharp}\colon\mathscr{C}(A,-)\Rightarrow\mathbb{E}(C,-).

For any

, these

and

are given as follows.

(1)

(\delta_{\sharp})_{X}\colon\mathscr{C}(X,C)\to\mathbb{E}(X,A)\ :\ f\mapsto f^{\ast}\delta
.

(2)

\delta^{\sharp}_{X}\colon\mathscr{C}(A,X)\to\mathbb{E}(C,X)\ :\ g\mapsto g_{\ast}\delta
.

We simply denote
(\delta_{\sharp})_{X}(f)
and
\delta^{\sharp}_{X}(g)
by
\delta_{\sharp}(f)
and
\delta^{\sharp}(g)
, respectively.

Definition 2.4

[

An

(1)

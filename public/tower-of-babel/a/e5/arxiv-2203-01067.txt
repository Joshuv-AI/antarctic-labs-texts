A new approach to projectivity in the categories of complexes, II
Driss Bennis, Juan Ramón García Rozas

Abstract




Abstract.
It is now very known how the subprojectivity of modules provides a fruitful new unified framework of the classical projectivity and flatness. In this paper, we extend this fact to the category of complexes by generalizing and unifying several known classical results. We further provide various examples to illustrate the scopes and limits of the established results.

This paper is a continuation of a recent work in which it was shown among other several things that the subprojectivity of complexes can be characterized in terms of morphisms in the homotopy category.

Key Words.
Subprojectivity domain of a complex; Subprojectivity domain of a class complexes; projective complex; flat complex

.

Let

be a complex and

be a family of modules. Then, we get that

∎

.

Recall that the subprojectivity domain of the class of all finitely presented modules is the class of all flat modules (see

Corollary 2.7

The subprojectivity domain of a bounded complex below of finitely presented modules contains the class of all flat complexes.

Corollary 2.8
.

The subprojectivity domain of a bounded complex below of projective modules contains the class of all exact complexes.

3
Subprojectivity domains of classes of complexes

In this section, we deal with the subprojectivity domain of classes of complexes. We will answer questions raised at the end of the introduction. The first main result, which answers the first question, is given as follows:

.

\mathcal{L}
and

\mathcal{L}

.

). We use

. The authors in

, that is the class

(see

, Proposition 3.4 and Remark 3.3]
). Here, we show when these two classes coincide.

Proposition 3.3
.

For any ring,
\mathcal{GP}_{{\mathscr{C}}(R)}^{\perp}=\mathcal{\widetilde{GP_{R-{\rm Mod}}^{\perp}}}
if and only if
{\rm Hom}_{{\mathscr{K}}(R)}(M,N)=0
for any Gorenstein projective complex
M
and any complex
N\in\mathcal{\widetilde{GP_{R-{\rm Mod}}^{\perp}}}
.

Proof.

We have
{{\underline{\mathfrak{Pr}}}_{R-{\rm Mod}}}^{-1}(\mathcal{GP_{R-{\rm Mod}}})=\mathcal{GP}_{R-{\rm Mod}}^{\perp}
by
[
1
, Corollary 2.28]
, then, by Theorem
3.2
,
{{\underline{\mathfrak{Pr}}}_{{\mathscr{C}}(R)}}^{-1}(\#\mathcal{GP_{R-{\rm Mod}}})=\mathcal{\widetilde{GP_{R-{\rm Mod}}^{\perp}}}
if and only if
{\rm Hom}_{{\mathscr{K}}(R)}(M,N)=0
for any
M\in\#\mathcal{{GP_{R-{\rm Mod}}}}
and
N\in\mathcal{\widetilde{GP_{R-{\rm Mod}}^{\perp}}}
. But
\#\mathcal{GP_{R-{\rm Mod}}}=\mathcal{GP}_{{\mathscr{C}}(R)}
, by
[
10
, Theorem 2.2]
. Then,
{{\underline{\mathfrak{Pr}}}_{{\mathscr{C}}(R)}}^{-1}(\#\mathcal{GP_{R-{\rm Mod}}})={{\underline{\mathfrak{Pr}}}_{{\mathscr{C}}(R)}}^{-1}(\mathcal{GP_{{\mathscr{C}}(R)}})=\mathcal{GP}_{{\mathscr{C}}(R)}^{\perp}
(see
[
1
, Corollary 2.28]
). Therefore,
\mathcal{GP}_{{\mathscr{C}}(R)}^{\perp}=\mathcal{\widetilde{GP_{R-{\rm Mod}}^{\perp}}}
if and only if
{\rm Hom}_{{\mathscr{K}}(R)}(M,N)=0
for any
M
Gorenstein projective complex and any
N\in\mathcal{\widetilde{GP_{R-{\rm Mod}}^{\perp}}}
.
∎

Now, we turn our attention to the third question. In the study of this question, a new type of classes appears naturally which are defined as follows: First, we fix some notations and recall some facts: For complexes
X
and
Y
, we let
{\rm Hom}^{\bullet}(X,Y)
denote the complex of abelian groups with

{\rm Hom}^{\bullet}(X,Y)_{n}=\prod_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}{\rm Hom}_{R}(X_{i},Y_{i+n})

and

d_{n}^{{\rm Hom}^{\bullet}(X,Y)}(\psi)=(d^{Y}_{i+n}\psi_{i}-(-1)^{n}\psi_{i-1}d_{i}^{X})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}.

Note that for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,

Z_{n}({\rm Hom}^{\bullet}(X,Y))={\rm Hom}_{{\mathscr{C}}(R)}(X[n],Y)={\rm Hom}_{{\mathscr{C}}(R)}(X,Y[-n])

and

H_{n}({\rm Hom}^{\bullet}(X,Y))={\rm Hom}_{{\mathscr{K}}(R)}(X[n],Y)={\rm Hom}_{{\mathscr{K}}(R)}(X,Y[-n]).

For every complex
X
,
{\rm Hom}^{\bullet}(X,-)
is a left exact functor from the category of complexes of modules to the category of complexes of abelian groups.

Definition 3.4
.

Given a class of modules
\mathcal{L}
, a complex
X
is said to be a
dg\mathcal{L}
complex, if
X_{n}\in\mathcal{L}
, for each
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, and
{\rm Hom}^{\bullet}(X,G)
is exact whenever
G
is an exact complex with cycles in
{{\underline{\mathfrak{Pr}}}_{R-{\rm Mod}}}^{-1}(\mathcal{L})
. We denote the class of
dg\mathcal{L}
complexes by
dg\mathcal{\widetilde{L}}
.

The terminology used in this definition is inspired from
dg\mathcal{L}
complexes introduced by Gillespie
[
5
]
based on the fact that, when
(L,G)
is the classical cotorsion pair
(Proj,R-Mod)
,
dg\mathcal{\widetilde{L}}
is nothing but the classical DG-projective complexes.

Lemma 3.5
.

If
\mathcal{L}
is a class which contains
0
, then
{\mathscr{C}}^{-}(\mathcal{L})\subseteq dg\mathcal{\widetilde{L}}
.

Proof.

Set
\mathcal{G}={{\underline{\mathfrak{Pr}}}_{R-{\rm Mod}}}^{-1}(\mathcal{L})
and let
f:X\to N
be a morphism of complexes with
N\in\mathcal{\widetilde{G}}
.
N
is exact and
Z_{n}(N)\in{{\underline{\mathfrak{Pr}}}_{R-{\rm Mod}}}^{-1}(\mathcal{L})
, for each
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, hence
N
is
{\rm Hom}_{R}(Proj,-)
-exact and every morphism
X_{n}\to Z_{n}(N)
factors through a projective module. Therefore
f:X\to N
is null-homotopic, by Lemma
2.5
. Thus,
{\rm Hom}^{\bullet}(X,N)
is exact whenever
N
is a
\mathcal{\widetilde{G}}
complex.
∎

Now, our third main result of this section is given as follows.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{G}
be two classes of modules such that
0,R\in\mathcal{L}
. Then, the following conditions are equivalent.

1.

{{\underline{\mathfrak{Pr}}}_{R-{\rm Mod}}}^{-1}(\mathcal{L})=\mathcal{G}
.

2.

{{\underline{\mathfrak{Pr}}}_{{\mathscr{C}}(R)}}^{-1}({\mathscr{C}}^{b}(\mathcal{L}))=\mathcal{\widetilde{G}}.

3.

{{\underline{\mathfrak{Pr}}}_{{\mathscr{C}}(R)}}^{-1}({\mathscr{C}}^{-}(\mathcal{L}))=\mathcal{\widetilde{G}}.

4.

{{\underline{\mathfrak{Pr}}}_{{\mathscr{C}}(R)}}^{-1}(dg\mathcal{\widetilde{L}})=\mathcal{\widetilde{G}}.

Proof.

1.\Rightarrow 4.
Let us prove that
{{\underline{\mathfrak{Pr}}}_{{\mathscr{C}}(R)}}^{-1}(dg\mathcal{\widetilde{L}})\subseteq\mathcal{\widetilde{G}}
. For let
N\in{{\underline{\mathfrak{Pr}}}_{{\mathscr{C}}(R)}}^{-1}(dg\mathcal{\widetilde{L}})
, then
N\in{{\underline{\mathfrak{Pr}}}_{{\mathscr{C}}(R)}}^{-1}(\underline{R}[n])
for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, by Lemma
3.5
, so
N
is exact by
[
2
, Corollary 3.17]
. Now, let
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
L\in\mathcal{L}
, then
N\in{{\underline{\mathfrak{Pr}}}_{{\mathscr{C}}(R)}}^{-1}(\underline{L}[n])
for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, by Lemma
3.5
. Then, by
[
2
, Lemma 3.6]
,
Z_{n}(N)\in{{\underline{\mathfrak{Pr}}}_{R-{\rm Mod}}}^{-1}(L)
for every
L\in\mathcal{L}
. Then,
Z_{n}(N)\in{{\underline{\mathfrak{Pr}}}_{R-{\rm Mod}}}^{-1}(\mathcal{L})=\mathcal{G}
for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Thus,
N\in\mathcal{\widetilde{G}}
. Now, let us prove that
\mathcal{\widetilde{G}}\subseteq{{\underline{\mathfrak{Pr}}}_{{\mathscr{C}}(R)}}^{-1}(dg\mathcal{\widetilde{L}})
. Let
N\in\mathcal{\widetilde{G}}
and
M\in dg\mathcal{\widetilde{L}}
, then
{\rm Hom}_{{\mathscr{K}}(R)}(M,N)=0
since
{\rm Hom}^{\bullet}(X,N)
is exact (see
[
5
, Lemma 2.1]
). For every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
Z_{n}(N)\in\mathcal{G}={{\underline{\mathfrak{Pr}}}_{R-{\rm Mod}}}^{-1}(\mathcal{L})
, hence
N_{n}\in{{\underline{\mathfrak{Pr}}}_{R-{\rm Mod}}}^{-1}(\mathcal{L})
since
{{\underline{\mathfrak{Pr}}}_{R-{\rm Mod}}}^{-1}(\mathcal{L})
is closed under extensions. Then,
N\in{{\underline{\mathfrak{Pr}}}_{{\mathscr{C}}(R)}}^{-1}(dg\mathcal{\widetilde{L}})
by
[
2
, Theorem 3.11]
. Thus,
N\in{{\underline{\mathfrak{Pr}}}_{{\mathscr{C}}(R)}}^{-1}(dg\mathcal{\widetilde{L}})
.

4.\Rightarrow 1.
Let
N\in{{\underline{\mathfrak{Pr}}}_{R-{\rm Mod}}}^{-1}(\mathcal{L})
, then for every
M\in dg\mathcal{\widetilde{L}}
,
N\in{{\underline{\mathfrak{Pr}}}_{R-{\rm Mod}}}^{-1}(M_{0})
since
M_{0}\in\mathcal{L}
. Then, for every
M\in dg\mathcal{\widetilde{L}}
,
\overline{N}\in{{\underline{\mathfrak{Pr}}}_{{\mathscr{C}}(R)}}^{-1}(M)
(see
[
2
, Lemma 3.7]
). Therefore,
\overline{N}\in{{\underline{\mathfrak{Pr}}}_{{\mathscr{C}}(R)}}^{-1}(dg\mathcal{\widetilde{L}})=\mathcal{\widetilde{G}}
, hence
N\in\mathcal{G}
. Conversely, let
N\in\mathcal{G}
and
M\in\mathcal{L}
then,
\overline{N}\in\mathcal{\widetilde{L}}
and
\overline{M}\in dg\mathcal{\widetilde{L}}
(see Lemma
3.5
). Then,
\overline{N}\in{{\underline{\mathfrak{Pr}}}_{{\mathscr{C}}(R)}}^{-1}(\overline{M})
by assumption. Then,
N\in{{\underline{\mathfrak{Pr}}}_{R-{\rm Mod}}}^{-1}(M)
by
[
2
, Lemma 3.7]
. Then,
N\in{{\underline{\mathfrak{Pr}}}_{R-{\rm Mod}}}^{-1}(\mathcal{L})

(4.\Leftrightarrow 1.)\Rightarrow 2.
It is clear that
\mathcal{\widetilde{G}}\subseteq{{\underline{\mathfrak{Pr}}}_{{\mathscr{C}}(R)}}^{-1}({\mathscr{C}}^{b}(\mathcal{L}))
since
{\mathscr{C}}^{-}(\mathcal{L})\subseteq dg\mathcal{\widetilde{L}}
by Lemma
3.5
. Conversely, let
N\in{{\underline{\mathfrak{Pr}}}_{{\mathscr{C}}(R)}}^{-1}({\mathscr{C}}^{-}(\mathcal{L}))
then
N\in{{\underline{\mathfrak{Pr}}}_{{\mathscr{C}}(R)}}^{-1}(\underline{R}[n])
for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, so
N
is exact by
[
2
, Corollary 3.17]
. Now, let
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
L\in\mathcal{L}
, then
N\in{{\underline{\mathfrak{Pr}}}_{{\mathscr{C}}(R)}}^{-1}(\underline{L}[n])
for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then, by
[
2
, Lemma 3.6]
,
Z_{n}(N)\in{{\underline{\mathfrak{Pr}}}_{R-{\rm Mod}}}^{-1}(L)
for every
L\in\mathcal{L}
. Then,
Z_{n}(N)\in{{\underline{\mathfrak{Pr}}}_{R-{\rm Mod}}}^{-1}(\mathcal{L})=\mathcal{G}
for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Thus,
N\in\mathcal{\widetilde{G}}
.

For
2.\Rightarrow 1.
and
3.\Rightarrow 1.
we use the same arguments of
4.\Rightarrow 1.

(2.\Leftrightarrow 4.)\Rightarrow 3.
is clear since
{\mathscr{C}}^{b}(\mathcal{L})\subseteq{\mathscr{C}}^{-}(\mathcal{L})\subseteq dg\mathcal{\widetilde{L}}
(by Lemma
3.5
).

∎

Apllying Theorem
3.6
to the class of finitely presented modules, we get the following characterization of flat complexes.

Corollary 3.7
.

The following conditions are equivalent for a complex
F
.

1.

F
is exact and every cycle is a flat module.

2.

Every morphism
X\to F
, with
X
is a finitely presented complex, factors through a projective complex.

3.

Every morphism
X\to F
, with
X
is a bounded below complex of finitely presented modules, factors through a projective complex.

4.

Every morphism
X\to F
, with
X
is a
dg\mathcal{\widetilde{FP}}
complex, factors through a projective complex.

Apllying Theorem
3.6
, to
{{\underline{\mathfrak{Pr}}}_{R-{\rm Mod}}}^{-1}(Proj)=R-{\rm Mod}
, we get the following characterization of exact complexes.

Corollary 3.8
.

The following conditions are equivalent for a complex
F
.

1.

F
is exact.

2.

Every morphism
X\to F
, with
X
is a bounded complex of projective modules, factors through a projective complex.

3.

Every morphism
X\to F
, with
X
is a bounded complex below of projective modules, factors through a projective complex.

4.

Every morphism
X\to F
,with
X
is a
DG
-projective complex, factors through a projective complex.

Finally, applying Theorem
3.6
to
{{\underline{\mathfrak{Pr}}}_{R-{\rm Mod}}}^{-1}(R-{\rm Mod})=Proj
, we get the following characterization of projective complexes.

Corollary 3.9
.

The following conditions are equivalent for a complex
F

F

, with

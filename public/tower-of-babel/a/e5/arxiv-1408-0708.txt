Steady-state bifurcation analysis of a strong nonlinear atmospheric vorticity equation Journal: JMAA 431(2015) 1-21
Zhi-Min Chen

Abstract
The atmospheric vorticity equation studied in the present paper is a simplified form of the atmospheric circulation model introduced by Charney and DeVore [J. Atmos. Sci. 36(1979), 1205–1216] on the existence of multiple steady states to the understanding of the persistence of atmospheric blocking. The fluid motion defined by the equation is driven by a zonal thermal forcing and an Ekman friction forcing measured by κ \kappa . It is proved that the steady-state solution is globally unique for large κ \kappa values while multiple steady-state solutions branch off the basic steady-state solution for κ < κ crit \kappa<\kappa_{\rm{crit}} where the critical value κ crit \kappa_{\rm{crit}} is less than one. Without involvement of viscosity, the equation has strong non-linear property as its non-linear part contains the highest order derivative term. Steady-state bifurcation analysis is essentially based on the compactness, which can be simply obtained for semilinear equations such as the Navier–Stokes equations but is not available for the strong nonlinear vorticity equation in the Euler formulation. Therefore the Lagrangian formulation of the equation is employed to gain the required compactness. Keywords: atmospheric vorticity equation, steady-state bifurcation, Lagrange formulation, strong non-linear equation Mathematics Subject Classification: 35B32, 35B35, 35Q35, 86A10, 76B03

\kappa

\displaystyle\kappa\Delta\psi^{*}=-\kappa\cos x_{2}\,\,\mbox{ with }\psi^{*}=\cos x_{2},

The main result of the present paper reads as follows:

Theorem 1.1

For

,
the equations (
1

4

\displaystyle\kappa_{a}<a\sqrt{\frac{1-a^{2}}{2(1+a^{2})}}

The

with the semi-norm

[\phi]_{C^{\alpha}}=\sup_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in{\Omega_{a}},\,\mathbf{x}\neq\mathbf{y}}\frac{|\phi(\mathbf{x})-\phi(\mathbf{y})|}{|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}|^{\alpha}}.

We use the function space

C^{k+\alpha}_{\rm{per}}({\Omega_{a}})=\left\{\phi\in C^{k+\alpha}({\Omega_{a}});\,\,\mbox{ $\phi$ satisfies the conditions (\ref{nb2}) and (\ref{nbb3})}\right\}.

2
Lagrangian formulation of the fluid motion

For the velocity
\mathbf{u}=(u_{1},u_{2})=\nabla\times\psi
of the fluid flow in the domain
\Omega_{a}
and a trajectory
\mathbf{y}=(y_{1},y_{2})
initiating from a particle

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rll}-\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\mathbf{y}(\mathbf{x},t)&=\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{y}(\mathbf{x},t)),&t>0,\\
\mathbf{y}(\mathbf{x},0)&=\mathbf{x}\in{\Omega_{a}}.&\end{array}\right.

(6)

\displaystyle\nabla\mathbf{y}\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{y}(\mathbf{x},t)),\,\,\,t>0,\vskip 5.69054pt

\displaystyle\nabla\mathbf{y}(\mathbf{x},0)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}|\partial_{x_{i}}y_{j}|\leq|\partial_{x_{i}}y_{1}|\,|\partial_{y_{1}}u_{j}(\mathbf{y})|+|\partial_{x_{i}}y_{2}|\,|\partial_{y_{2}}u_{j}(\mathbf{y})|,\,\,\,i,\,j=1,\,2.

or the solution family of (
61
) branches off
(0,\kappa_{\rm{crit}})

when
\kappa
varies across the critical value
\kappa_{\rm{crit}}
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.
From Lemmas
2.1
and
2.2
we see that a solution
\psi
bifurcating from
\psi^{*}
is regular
whenever
\psi\in C^{2+\alpha}_{\rm{per}}({\Omega_{a}})
. Thus it suffices to seek bifurcating solutions in the function space
\hat{C}^{2+\alpha}_{\rm{per}}({\Omega_{a}})
for
0<\alpha<1
. Recall
\psi^{*}=\cos x_{2}
,
the operator

\displaystyle F(\psi,\kappa)=\psi+\kappa\Delta^{-1}[\kappa+(\nabla\times\psi)\cdot\nabla]^{-1}\psi^{*},

set in the previous section,
the operator
L_{\kappa}
defined by (
30
) and the critical number
\kappa_{a}
in Theorem
3.1
. For a constant
\epsilon
such that
0<\epsilon<\kappa_{a}
, we introduce the
symbols

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}X=\hat{C}^{2+\alpha}_{\rm{per}}({\Omega_{a}}),\\
\mathcal{D}=\left\{\psi\in\hat{C}^{2+\alpha}_{\rm{per}}({\Omega_{a}});\,\|\nabla^{2}\psi-\nabla^{2}\psi^{*}\|_{C^{\alpha}}<\frac{(\kappa_{a}-\epsilon)^{2}}{4}\right\}\times(\kappa_{a}-\epsilon,\kappa_{a}+\epsilon),\\
M_{k}\psi=\frac{1}{\kappa}(L_{k}\psi-\psi)=\frac{1}{\kappa}\Delta^{-1}[\kappa+(\nabla\times\psi^{*})\cdot\nabla]^{-1}(\nabla\times\psi^{*})\cdot\nabla\psi,\\
N(\psi,\kappa)=F(\psi,\kappa)-\left[\psi-\psi^{*}+kM_{k}(\psi-\psi^{*})\right].\end{array}\right.

To verify the bifurcation assertion now remains to demonstrate the validity of
the assumptions of Krasnolselskii’s
theorem.

Firstly
, we verify the assumptions (i, ii) of Theorem
4.1
.
For the even function property of
F(\psi,\kappa)(\mathbf{x})
with
(\psi,\kappa)\in\mathcal{D}
, we
see that the even function
\psi
implies
\nabla\times\psi
to be an odd function and so
\mathbf{y}
. This observation
implies that

\psi^{*}(\mathbf{y}(-\mathbf{x},s))=\cos(-y_{2}(\mathbf{x},s))=\psi^{*}(\mathbf{y}(\mathbf{x},s)),

and hence
F(\psi,\kappa)(\mathbf{x})
is an even function of
\mathbf{x}\in{\Omega_{a}}
.

To show the continuity of
F
, for
(\psi,\kappa),\,(\psi^{\prime},\kappa^{\prime})\in\mathcal{D}
, we note that

\displaystyle|\Delta F(\psi^{\prime},\kappa^{\prime})-\Delta F(\psi,\kappa)-(\Delta\psi^{\prime}-\Delta\psi)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|\kappa^{\prime}[\kappa^{\prime}+(\nabla\times\psi^{\prime})\cdot\nabla]^{-1}\psi^{*}-\kappa^{\prime}[\kappa+(\nabla\times\psi)\cdot\nabla]^{-1}\psi^{*}\right|

\displaystyle+\left|\kappa^{\prime}[\kappa+(\nabla\times\psi)\cdot\nabla]^{-1}\psi^{*}-\kappa[\kappa+(\nabla\times\psi)\cdot\nabla]^{-1}\psi^{*}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\kappa^{\prime}|[\kappa^{\prime}+(\nabla\times\psi^{\prime})\cdot\nabla]^{-1}(\kappa-\kappa^{\prime}+[\nabla\times\psi-\nabla\times\psi^{\prime}]\cdot\nabla)[\kappa+(\nabla\times\psi)\cdot\nabla]^{-1}\psi^{*}|

\displaystyle+|\kappa-\kappa^{\prime}|\,|[\kappa+(\nabla\times\psi)\cdot\nabla]^{-1}\psi^{*}|.

By the Lagrangian formulation

\displaystyle[\kappa+(\nabla\times\psi)\cdot\nabla]^{-1}f(\mathbf{x})=\int^{\infty}_{0}\mathrm{e}^{-\kappa s}f(\mathbf{y}(\mathbf{x},s))ds,

(62)

we have

\displaystyle\|\Delta F(\psi^{\prime},\kappa^{\prime})-\Delta F(\psi,\kappa)-(\Delta\psi^{\prime}-\Delta\psi)\|_{C^{0}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|(\kappa-\kappa^{\prime}+[\nabla\times\psi-\nabla\times\psi^{\prime}]\cdot\nabla)[\kappa+(\nabla\times\psi)\cdot\nabla]^{-1}\psi^{*}\|_{C^{0}}+\frac{|\kappa-\kappa^{\prime}|}{\kappa}\|\psi^{*}\|_{C^{0}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\kappa}|\kappa-\kappa^{\prime}|+\|\nabla\times\psi-\nabla\times\psi^{\prime}\|_{C^{0}}\,\int^{\infty}_{0}e^{-\kappa s}\|\nabla\mathbf{y}\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}\psi^{*}(\mathbf{y})\|_{C^{0}}ds,

which is bounded by, using (
12
) and
\sqrt{\|\nabla^{2}\psi-\nabla^{2}\psi^{*}\|_{C^{0}}}\leq(\kappa_{a}-\epsilon)/2
,

\displaystyle\frac{2|\kappa-\kappa^{\prime}|}{\kappa}+\frac{\left(\sqrt{2}(\kappa-\kappa_{a}+\epsilon)+\sqrt{5}\right)\|\nabla\psi^{*}\|_{C^{0}}\|\nabla\psi-\nabla\psi^{\prime}\|_{C^{0}}}{(\kappa-\kappa_{a}+\epsilon)^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2|\kappa-\kappa^{\prime}|}{\kappa}+\frac{\left(\sqrt{2}(\kappa-\kappa_{a}+\epsilon)+\sqrt{5}\right)\|\nabla\psi-\nabla\psi^{\prime}\|_{C^{0}}}{(\kappa-\kappa_{a}+\epsilon)^{2}}.

Additionally, by Lemma
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle\|\Delta F(\psi,\kappa)-\Delta\psi\|_{C^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\kappa\|[\kappa+(\nabla\times\psi)\cdot\nabla]^{-1}\psi^{*}\|_{C^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 1+\frac{\sqrt{2}\kappa(\kappa-2\sqrt{\|\nabla^{2}\psi-\nabla^{2}\psi^{*}\|_{C^{0}}})+\sqrt{5}\kappa}{(\kappa-2\sqrt{\|\nabla^{2}\psi-\nabla^{2}\psi^{*}\|_{C^{0}}})^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 1+\frac{\sqrt{2}(\kappa-\kappa_{a}+\epsilon)\kappa+\sqrt{5}\kappa}{(\kappa-\kappa_{a}+\epsilon)^{2}}.

With the use of the above
C^{0}
and
C^{1}
estimates, the required continuity of the operator
F
in the intermediate
Hölder space is thus derived from the interpolation inequality

\displaystyle\|\Delta f\|_{C^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\|\Delta f\|_{C^{0}}^{1-\alpha}\|\Delta f\|_{C^{1}}^{\alpha}

and the Hölder inequality of the Laplace operator

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{2}f\|_{C^{\alpha}}\leq c\|\Delta f\|_{C^{\alpha}}.

For the assumption (ii) of Theorem

4.1
, we rewrite the operator
M_{\kappa}
as

\displaystyle M_{k}\psi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\kappa}\Delta^{-1}[\kappa+(\nabla\times\psi^{*})\cdot\nabla]^{-1}(\nabla\times\psi^{*})\cdot\nabla\psi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\kappa}\Delta^{-1}\psi-\Delta^{-1}[\kappa+(\nabla\times\psi^{*})\cdot\nabla]^{-1}\psi.

This formulation enables us to apply the argument on the continuity of the operator
F(\psi,\kappa)
to obtain the continuity of the operator
M_{\kappa}:\mathcal{D}\mapsto X
and result of

M_{\kappa}\psi\in C^{2+\delta}({\Omega_{a}})
for any
\alpha<\delta<1
. The compactness of the operator
M_{\kappa}
is due to the compact imbedding of

C^{2+\delta}({\Omega_{a}})
into
C^{2+\alpha}({\Omega_{a}})
.

Next
, to verify the assumptions (iii, iv), we notice that

\displaystyle N(\psi,\kappa)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\kappa\Delta^{-1}[\kappa+(\nabla\times\psi)\cdot\nabla]^{-1}\psi^{*}+\psi^{*}-\kappa M_{\kappa}(\psi-\psi^{*}).

Therefore the compactness of the operator
N
is implied in the proof of the continuity of
F
and the compactness of the operator
M_{\kappa}
. To prove the non-linear assertion, we transform the operator
N
into an explicit quadratic form. That is, by the solution property of
\psi^{*}
satisfying (
29
),

\displaystyle N

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F(\psi,\kappa)-\psi+\psi^{*}-\kappa M_{\kappa}(\psi-\psi^{*})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\kappa\Delta^{-1}[\kappa+(\nabla\times\psi)\cdot\nabla]^{-1}\psi^{*}

\displaystyle+\psi^{*}-\Delta^{-1}[\kappa+(\nabla\times\psi^{*})\cdot\nabla]^{-1}(\nabla\times\psi^{*})\cdot\nabla(\psi-\psi^{*})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\kappa\Delta^{-1}[\kappa+(\nabla\times\psi)\cdot\nabla]^{-1}\psi^{*}-\kappa\Delta^{-1}[\kappa+(\nabla\times\psi^{*})\cdot\nabla]^{-1}\psi^{*}

\displaystyle+\Delta^{-1}[\kappa+(\nabla\times\psi^{*})\cdot\nabla]^{-1}(\nabla\times\psi-\nabla\times\psi^{*})\cdot\nabla\psi^{*},

since

(\nabla\times\psi^{*})\cdot\nabla(\psi-\psi^{*})=-(\nabla\times\psi-\nabla\times\psi^{*})\cdot\nabla\psi^{*}.

By elementary manipulations, we have

\displaystyle N

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\kappa\Delta^{-1}[\kappa+(\nabla\times\psi)\cdot\nabla]^{-1}(\nabla\times\psi-\nabla\times\psi^{*})\cdot\nabla(\kappa+(\nabla\times\psi^{*})\cdot\nabla)^{-1}\psi^{*}

\displaystyle+\Delta^{-1}[\kappa+(\nabla\times\psi^{*})\cdot\nabla]^{-1}(\nabla\times\psi-\nabla\times\psi^{*})\cdot\nabla\psi^{*}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\Delta^{-1}[\kappa+(\nabla\times\psi)\cdot\nabla]^{-1}(\nabla\times\psi-\nabla\times\psi^{*})\cdot\nabla\psi^{*}

\displaystyle+\Delta^{-1}[\kappa+(\nabla\times\psi^{*})\cdot\nabla]^{-1}(\nabla\times\psi-\nabla\times\psi^{*})\cdot\nabla\psi^{*}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\Delta^{-1}\left[(\kappa+(\nabla\times\psi)\cdot\nabla)^{-1}\!-\!(\kappa+(\nabla\times\psi^{*})\cdot\nabla)^{-1}\right](\nabla\times\psi\!-\!\nabla\times\psi^{*})\!\cdot\!\nabla\psi^{*}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta^{-1}[\kappa+(\nabla\times\psi)\cdot\nabla]^{-1}(\nabla\times\psi-\nabla\times\psi^{*})

\displaystyle\cdot\nabla[\kappa+(\nabla\times\psi^{*})\cdot\nabla]^{-1}(\nabla\times\psi-\nabla\times\psi^{*})\cdot\nabla\psi^{*}.

With the use of this quadratic form and (
62
), we have

\displaystyle\|\Delta N(\psi,\kappa)\|_{C^{0}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\kappa}\|(\nabla\times\psi-\nabla\times\psi^{*})\cdot\nabla[\kappa+(\nabla\times\psi^{*})\cdot\nabla]^{-1}(\nabla\times\psi-\nabla\times\psi^{*})\cdot\nabla\psi^{*}\|_{C^{0}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\kappa}\|\nabla\psi-\nabla\psi^{*}\|_{C^{0}}\,\|\nabla[\kappa+(\nabla\times\psi^{*})\cdot\nabla]^{-1}(\nabla\times\psi-\nabla\times\psi^{*})\cdot\nabla\psi^{*}\|_{C^{0}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\kappa}\|\nabla\psi-\nabla\psi^{*}\|_{C^{0}}\,\|\nabla[(\nabla\times\psi-\nabla\times\psi^{*})\cdot\nabla\psi^{*}]\|_{C^{0}}\int^{\infty}_{0}\mathrm{e}^{-\kappa s}\|\nabla\mathbf{y}^{*}\|_{C^{0}}ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\kappa}\|\nabla\psi-\nabla\psi^{*}\|_{C^{0}}(\|\nabla^{2}\psi-\nabla^{2}\psi^{*}\|_{C^{0}}+\|\nabla\psi-\nabla\psi^{*}\|_{C^{0}})\int^{\infty}_{0}\mathrm{e}^{-\kappa s}(2+s)ds,

where the flow trajectory
\mathbf{y}^{*}
is defined by the velocity
\nabla\times\psi^{*}=(-\sin x_{2},0)
and is in the following form

\displaystyle\mathbf{y}^{*}(\mathbf{x},t)=\mathbf{x}+t(\sin x_{2},\,0).

(63)

Hence

\|\Delta N(\psi,\kappa)\|_{C^{0}}\leq\frac{2\kappa+1}{\kappa^{3}}\|\psi-\psi^{*}\|_{C^{2}}^{2}.

For the estimate of the operator
N
in the Hölder semi-norm,
we employ (
12
), (
62
) and (
63
) to produce the estimates

\displaystyle[(\kappa+(\nabla\times\psi)\cdot\nabla)^{-1}f]_{C^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle[f]_{C^{\alpha}}\int^{\infty}_{0}\mathrm{e}^{-\kappa s}\|\nabla\mathbf{y}\|_{C^{0}}^{\alpha}ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle[f]_{C^{\alpha}}\frac{\sqrt{2}(\kappa-2\alpha\sqrt{\|\nabla^{2}\psi-\nabla^{2}\psi^{*}\|_{C^{0}}})+\sqrt{5}}{(\kappa-2\alpha\sqrt{\|\nabla^{2}\psi-\nabla^{2}\psi^{*}\|_{C^{0}}})^{2}}

and

\displaystyle[\nabla(\kappa+(\nabla\times\psi^{*})\cdot\nabla)^{-1}f]_{C^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int^{\infty}_{0}\mathrm{e}^{-\kappa s}[\nabla\mathbf{y}^{*}\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf{y}^{*}}f(\mathbf{y}^{*}(\cdot,s)]_{C^{\alpha}}ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\nabla f\|_{C^{0}}\int^{\infty}_{0}\mathrm{e}^{-\kappa s}[\nabla\mathbf{y}^{*}]_{C^{\alpha}}ds+[\nabla f]_{C^{\alpha}}\int^{\infty}_{0}\mathrm{e}^{-\kappa s}\|\nabla\mathbf{y}^{*}\|_{C^{0}}^{1+\alpha}ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\nabla f\|_{C^{0}}\int^{\infty}_{0}\mathrm{e}^{-\kappa s}2sds+[\nabla f]_{C^{\alpha}}\int^{\infty}_{0}\mathrm{e}^{-\kappa s}(2+s)^{1+\alpha}ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\kappa^{2}}\|\nabla f\|_{C^{0}}+\frac{4\kappa^{2}+4\kappa+2}{\kappa^{3}}[\nabla f]_{C^{\alpha}}.

Let
c
be a constant independent of
\psi
and
\kappa
close to
\kappa_{a}
and the constant may change from line to line. Hence for

\displaystyle\mathbf{w}=\nabla\times\psi-\nabla\times\psi^{*},

the Hölder semi-norm of the operator
N
is estimated as

\displaystyle[\Delta N(\psi,\kappa)]_{C^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[(\kappa+(\nabla\times\psi)\cdot\nabla)^{-1}\mathbf{w}\cdot\nabla(\kappa+(\nabla\times\psi^{*})\cdot\nabla)^{-1}\mathbf{w}\cdot\nabla\psi^{*}]_{C^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c[\mathbf{w}\cdot\nabla(\kappa+(\nabla\times\psi^{*})\cdot\nabla)^{-1}\mathbf{w}\cdot\nabla\psi^{*}]_{C^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\|\mathbf{w}\|_{C^{0}}[\nabla(\kappa+(\nabla\times\psi^{*})\cdot\nabla)^{-1}\mathbf{w}\cdot\nabla\psi^{*}]_{C^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle+c[\mathbf{w}]_{C^{\alpha}}\|\nabla(\kappa+(\nabla\times\psi^{*})\cdot\nabla)^{-1}\mathbf{w}\cdot\nabla\psi^{*}\|_{C^{0}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\left(\|\mathbf{w}\|_{C^{0}}\|\nabla(\mathbf{w}\cdot\nabla\psi^{*})\|_{C^{\alpha}}+[\mathbf{w}]_{C^{\alpha}}\|\nabla(\mathbf{w}\cdot\nabla\psi^{*})\|_{C^{0}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\|\nabla\psi-\nabla\psi^{*}\|_{C^{1+\alpha}}^{2}.

This shows that the assumptions (iii,iv) of Theorem
4.1
hold true.

Finally,
for the verification of the spectral condition, we apply Theorem

3.1
to obtain the existence of critical value
\kappa_{a}
satisfying the simplicity condition

\displaystyle\dim\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}\left\{\psi=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}b_{n}\cos(ax_{1}+nx_{2})\in\hat{C}^{2+\alpha}_{\rm{per}}({\Omega_{a}});\,\,L_{\kappa_{a}}^{i}\psi=0\right\}=1.

Therefore, this together with (
33
) for
m\neq 1
produces the validity of the assumption (v) of Theorem
4.1
:

\displaystyle\dim\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}\left\{\psi\in\hat{C}^{2+\alpha}_{\rm{per}}({\Omega_{a}});\,\,L_{\kappa_{a}}^{i}\psi=0\right\}=1.

(64)

The bifurcation assertion of Theorem
1.1
is thus follows from Theorem
4.1
and the proof of Theorem
1.1
is completed.

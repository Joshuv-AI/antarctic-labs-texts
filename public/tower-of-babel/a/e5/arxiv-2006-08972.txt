Valuation rings of dimension one as limits of smooth algebras
Dorin Popescu

Abstract
. As in Zariski’s Uniformization Theorem we show that a valuation ring V V of characteristic p > 0 p>0 of dimension one is a filtered direct limit of smooth 𝐅 p {\bf F}_{p} -algebras under some conditions of transcendence degree. Under mild conditions, the algebraic immediate extensions of valuation rings are dense if they are filtered direct limit of smooth morphisms. Key words : Immediate extensions of valuations rings, Pseudo convergent sequences, Pseudo limits, smooth morphisms, Henselian rings. 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification: Primary 13F30, Secondary 13A18,13L05,13B40.



Introduction

Zariski predicted, and proved in characteristic
0
in
[
20
]
, that any integral algebraic variety X
equipped with a dominant morphism
v:Spec(V)\rightarrow X
from a valuation ring
V
can be desingularized
along
V
: there should exist a proper birational map
{\tilde{X}}\rightarrow X
for which the lift
{\tilde{v}}:Spec(V)\rightarrow{\tilde{X}}

of
v
supplied by the valuative criterion of properness would factor through the regular locus of
\tilde{X}
.
This local form of resolution of singularities remains open in positive and mixed characteristic, and
implies that every valuation ring V should be a filtered direct limit of regular rings. A filtered direct limit (in other words a filtered colimit) is a limit indexed by a small category that is filtered (see
[
16
, 002V]
or
[
16
, 04AX]
). A filtered union is a filtered direct limit in which all objects are subobjects of the final colimit, so that in particular all the transition arrows are monomorphisms. There exists several nice extensions of Zariski’s Uniformization Theorem as for example recently the result of B. Antieau, R. Datta
[
1
, Theorem 4.1.1]
, which says that every perfect valuation ring of characteristic
p>0
is a filtered union of its smooth
{\bf F}_{p}
-subalgebras. This result is an application of
[
18
, Theorem 1.2.5]
which relies on some results from
[
3
]
. Also E. Elmanto and M. Hoyois proved that an absolute integrally closed valuation ring of residue field of characteristic
p>0
is a filtered union of its regular finitely generated
{\bf Z}
-subalgebras (see
[
1
, Corollary 4.2.4]
).

The goal
of this paper is to establish
a kind of Zariski’s Uniformization Theorem in characteristic
p>0
and dimension one.

1.
The Defect

Let
V
be a valuation ring with value group
\Gamma
,
K
its fraction field and its valuation
\mathrm{val}:K^{*}=K\setminus\{0\}\rightarrow\Gamma
. Let
F
be a finite field extension of
K
,
v_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq r
the valuations of
F
extending
\mathrm{val}
and
V_{i}
be the valuation rings of
F
defined by
v_{i}
. Let
e_{i},f_{i}
be the ramification index respectively the degree of the residue field extension of
V_{i}
over
V
. It is well known that

[F:K]\geq\sum_{i=1}^{r}e_{i}f_{i}.

If this inequality is equality for all finite (resp. finite separable) field extensions
F
of
V
we say that
V
is
defectless
(resp.
separably defectless
, see
[
7
]
for details and examples).

We remind the following important result for the existence of defectless valuation rings.

Theorem 3
.

(
[
2
, Theorem 2, VI, (8.5)]
) Let
F
be a finite field extension of
K
and
B
be the integral closure of
V
in
F
. Then the above inequality is equality if and only if
B
is a free
V
-module.

Lemma 4
.

Let
V
be a valuation ring and
S
a multiplicative closed system from
V
. If
V
is defectless (resp. separably defectless) then the valuation ring
S^{-1}V
is defectless (resp. separably defectless) too.

Proof.

Assume
V
is defectless (the proof in the separably defectless case is similar). Let
F
be a finite field extension of the fraction field
K
of
V
(and of
S^{-1}V
too) and
B
,
B^{\prime}
be the integral closures of
V
, respectively
S^{-1}V
in
F
.
By Theorem
3
we have to show that
B^{\prime}
is a free
S^{-1}V
-module. As
V
is defectless by the quoted theorem we see that
B
is a free
V
-module and so
S^{-1}B
is a free
S^{-1}V
-module.

We claim that
B^{\prime}\cong S^{-1}B
which is enough. Indeed, let
z\in F
be integral over
S^{-1}V
. We have

z^{e}+\sum_{i=0}^{e-1}(a_{i}/s)z^{i}=0

for some
e\in{\bf N}
,
a_{i}\in V
and

. Then

(sz)^{e}+\sum_{i=0}^{e-1}s^{e-i-1}a_{i}(sz)^{i}=0

and so

, which shows our claim.

An inclusion
V\subset V^{\prime}
of valuation rings is an
immediate extension
if it is local as a map of local rings and induces equalities between the value groups and the residue fields of
V
and
V^{\prime}
. It is dense if for any
x^{\prime}\in V^{\prime}
and
\gamma\in\Gamma
there exists
x\in V
such that
\mathrm{val}(x-x^{\prime})>\gamma
.

Let
\lambda
be a fixed limit ordinal and
v=\{v_{i}\}_{i<\lambda}
a sequence of elements in
V
indexed by the ordinals
i
less than
\lambda
. Then
v
is
pseudo convergent
if

\mathrm{val}(v_{i}-v_{i^{\prime\prime}})<\mathrm{val}(v_{i^{\prime}}-v_{i^{\prime\prime}})\ \ \mbox{for}\ \ i<i^{\prime}<i^{\prime\prime}<\lambda

(see
[
4
]
,
[
15
]
).
A
pseudo limit
of
v
is an element
x\in V
if

\mathrm{val}(x-v_{i})=\mathrm{val}(v_{i}-v_{i^{\prime}}))\ \ \mbox{for}\ \ i<i^{\prime}<\lambda
. We say that
v
is

(1)

algebraic
if some
f\in V[T]
satisfies
\mathrm{val}(f(v_{i}))<\mathrm{val}(f(v_{i^{\prime}}))
for large enough
i<i^{\prime}<\lambda
;

(2)

transcendental
if each
f\in V[T]
satisfies
\mathrm{val}(f(v_{i}))=\mathrm{val}(f(v_{i^{\prime}}))
for large enough
i<i^{\prime}<\lambda
.

When for any
\gamma\in\Gamma
it holds
\mathrm{val}(v_{i}-v_{i^{\prime}})>\gamma
for
i<i^{\prime}<\lambda

large we call
v

fundamental
.

Lemma 8
.

Let
V\subset V^{\prime}
be an immediate extension of one dimensional valuation rings and
K\subset K^{\prime}
their fraction field extension. If
V
is separably defectless and
K^{\prime}/K
is separable and algebraic then
K^{\prime}/K
is dense. Moreover, if
K^{\prime}/K
is not separable but
V
is defectless then
K^{\prime}/K
is still dense.

Proof.

We may reduce to the case when
K^{\prime}/K
is finite separable. Let
K^{h}
,
K^{\prime h}
be the Henselizations of
K
respectively
K^{\prime}
and
L=K^{h}(K^{\prime})\subset K^{\prime h}
. By
[
7
, Theorem 2.3]
we have
K^{h}
separably defectless and so we have
[L:K^{h}]=e_{L/K^{h}}f_{L/K^{h}}=1
because there exists an unique extension of the valuation of
V
to
K^{h}
. Thus
L\subset K^{h}
and
K\subset K^{h}
is dense because
\dim K=1
. The second statement goes similarly.

Let

z
is standard over
F
, which is necessary later to apply Lemma
15
.

Set
E^{\prime}=E\otimes_{F}W
and let
w^{\prime}:E^{\prime}\rightarrow V
be the map induced by
w
. We have
w^{\prime}(H_{E^{\prime}/W})\supset zV
and note that

w^{\prime}
factors modulo
z^{3}
through the smooth
W/z^{3}W
-algebra
W/z^{3}W\cong V/z^{3}V
since
W\subset V
is dense. Then
using Lemma
15
we see that
w^{\prime}
factors through a smooth
W
-algebra. Since
W
is a filtered direct limit of smooth
k
-algebra as in Corollary
14
we see that
w
factors through a smooth
k
-algebra even
F
-algebra. Actually, we may apply as in Proposition
13
a variant of Néron-Schappacher Theorem to get
V
as a filtered union of its smooth
W
-subalgebras and we are done.

∎

Corollary 17
.

Let
V
be a one dimensional valuation ring containing a perfect field
F
of characteristic
p>0
,
\Gamma
its value group,
K
its fraction field and
k
its residue field. Assume
\Gamma
is free of rank
r
,
k/F
is a field extension of finite type and
\operatorname{tr\,deg}_{F}K=\operatorname{tr\,deg}_{F}k+r
.
Then
V
is a filtered union of its smooth
F
-subalgebras, in particular of its smooth
{\bf F}_{p}
-algebras.

Proof.

Let
E\subset V
be a finitely generated
F
-subalgebra and
w:E\rightarrow V
its inclusion. Using
[
17
, Lemma 1.5]
it is enough to show that
w
factors through a smooth
F
-algebra. Using Lemma
15
we may replace
V
by its completion as in
[
14
, Proposition 9]
. So we may assume
V
is Henselian. A lifting of
k
to
V
could be done when
V
is Henselian and char
k=p=0
(see
[
19
, Theorem 2.9]
) but the proof goes in the same way when
p>0
and
k
is separably generated over
F
. In particular the lifting could be done when
k/F
is of finite type because
F
is perfect. Thus we may assume
k\subset V
and
\operatorname{tr\,deg}_{k}K=r
. Choose some elements
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})\in V^{r}
such that
\Gamma=\oplus_{i=1}^{r}{\bf Z}\mathrm{val}(x_{i})
. Then
W=V\cap k(x)\subset V
is algebraic and we may proceed as in Corollary
16
.

∎

3.
Algebraic pseudo convergent sequences

Let
V^{\prime}
be an immediate extension of a valuation ring
V
,
K\subset K^{\prime}
their fraction field extension,
v=(v_{j})_{j<\lambda}
a pseudo-convergent sequence in
V
which is not fundamental and has a pseudo limit
x
in
V^{\prime}
, but having no pseudo limit in
K
. Suppose that
K^{\prime}=K(x)
. We need the following result
[
8
, Theorem 1.8]
.

Theorem 18
.

(Kuhlmann-Ćmiel) Assume that
x
is not a root of an irreducible polynomial
f\in K[X]
and either
\dim V=1
, or
V
is Henselian. Then
for all
z\in V
there is a
\nu<\lambda
such that
\mathrm{val}(f(z))<\mathrm{val}(f(v_{j}))
for all
\nu<j<\lambda
.

Theorem 19
.

Assume
x
is transcendental over
K
and either
\dim V=1
, or
V
is Henselian.
Then the following statements are equivalent:

(1)

for every polynomial
f\in V[X]
with
f(x)\not=0
there exists a
y\in V
such that
\mathrm{val}(f(y))=\mathrm{val}(f(x))
,

(2)

for every polynomial
f\in V[X]
with
f(x)\not=0
there exists a
y\in V
such that
\mathrm{val}(f(y))\geq\mathrm{val}(f(x))

(3)

v
is transcendental,

(4)

V^{\prime}
is a filtered union of its localizations of polynomial
V
-subalgebras in one variable.

Proof.

First assume that (4) holds and let
f
be as in (1). Let
d\in V
,
z\in V^{\prime}
be such that
f(x)=dz
and
zz^{\prime}=1
for some
z^{\prime}\in V^{\prime}
. Then the solution
x,z,z^{\prime}
in
V^{\prime}
of the polynomials
F_{1}=f-dZ,F_{2}=ZZ^{\prime}-1\in V[X,Z,Z^{\prime}]
must be contained in a localization of a polynomial
V
-subalgebra
C=V[u^{\prime}]_{P(u^{\prime})}
of
V^{\prime}
, where
P\in V[U]
and
u^{\prime}\in V^{\prime}
is transcendental over
V
. Choose a
u\in V
such that
u\equiv u^{\prime}
modulo
{\mathfrak{m}}^{\prime}
the maximal ideal of
V^{\prime}
. Then
P(u)
is a unit in
V
and the map
\rho:C\rightarrow V
given by
u^{\prime}\rightarrow u
is a retraction of
V\subset C
and
y=\rho(x),\rho(z),\rho(z^{\prime})
is a solution of
F_{1},F_{2}
in
V
, and so
\mathrm{val}(f(y))=\mathrm{val}(d)=\mathrm{val}(f(x))
.

Clearly (2) follows from (1) and assume that (2) holds but
v
is algebraic. This is not possible as F. V. Kuhlmann said, the proof being done in Theorem
18
(see also
[
9
, Lemma 5.4]
). Let
h\in V[T]
be a polynomial with minimal degree among the polynomials
f\in V[T]
such that

\mathrm{val}(f(v_{i}))<\mathrm{val}(f(v_{j}))
for large
i<j<\lambda
. Then
h
is irreducible (see the proof of
[
15
, (II,4), Lemma 12]
). Note that
\mathrm{val}(h(x)-h(v_{j}))\geq\mathrm{val}(x-v_{j})=\mathrm{val}(v_{j+1}-v_{j})
. As the last term is increasing we must have
\mathrm{val}(h(x))\geq\mathrm{val}(h(v_{j}))
for all
j
. By Theorem
18
for every
z\in V
we have
\mathrm{val}(h(z))<\mathrm{val}(h(v_{j}))
for
j
large. Thus

h
fails the condition (2) and so
v
cannot be algebraic.

If
v
is transcendental then
V^{\prime}
is a filtered union of its localizations of polynomial
V
-subalgebras in one variable by
[
11
, Lemma 3.2]
(see also
[
14
, Lemma 15]
).

∎

Corollary 20
.

In the assumptions of the above theorem, if
v
is algebraic then
V^{\prime}
is not a filtered union of its localizations of polynomial
V
-subalgebras in one variable.

Lemma 21
.

In the assumptions of the above theorem, assume that
V
is Henselian and there exists an immediate extension of valuation rings
V^{\prime}\subset V_{1}
such that
V_{1}
is a filtered direct limit of smooth
V
-algebras. Then for every polynomial
f\in V[X]
such that
f(x)\not=0
there exists a
y\in V
such that
\mathrm{val}(f(y))=\mathrm{val}(f(x))
.

Proof.

Fix
f\in V[X]
. Let
d\in V
,
z\in V^{\prime}
be such that
f(x)=dz
and
zz^{\prime}=1
for some
z^{\prime}\in V^{\prime}
. Since
V_{1}
is a filtered direct limit of smooth
V
-algebras, the solution
x,z^{\prime},z^{\prime}
in
V^{\prime}
of the polynomials
F_{1}=f-dZ,F_{2}=ZZ^{\prime}-1\in V[X,Z,Z^{\prime}]
must come from a solution
\bar{x}
,
\bar{z}
,
{\bar{z}}^{\prime}
in a smooth
V
-algebra
C
. But
V
is Henselian and so there exists a retraction
\rho:C\rightarrow V
of
V\subset C
. Thus
y=\rho({\bar{x}}),\rho({\bar{z}}),\rho({\bar{z}}^{\prime})
is a solution of
F_{1},F_{2}
in
V
, and so
\mathrm{val}(f(y))=\mathrm{val}(d)=\mathrm{val}(f(x))
.

∎

Lemma 22
.

In the assumptions of the above theorem, assume that
\dim V=1
and there exists an immediate extension of valuation rings
V^{\prime}\subset V_{1}
such that
V_{1}
is a filtered direct limit of smooth
V
-algebras. Let
\hat{V}
be the completion of
V
and
K
,
\hat{K}
the fraction fields of
V
,
\hat{V}
. Suppose that
{\hat{K}}/K
is separable. Then for every polynomial
f\in V[X]
with
f(x)\not=0
there exists a
y\in V
such that
\mathrm{val}(f(y))=\mathrm{val}(f(x))
,

Proof.

Let
{\hat{V}}_{1}
be the completion of
V_{1}
and
K_{1}
the fraction field of
V_{1}
. Then
{\hat{V}}_{1}\cap{\hat{K}}={\hat{V}}
. Note that
K_{2}={\hat{K}}(V_{1})
is a localization of

since

and so

is a filtered direct limit of smooth
\hat{V}
-algebras. As

is Henselian we get
\mathrm{val}(f(z))=\mathrm{val}(f(x))

∎

Theorem 23
.

Let
V^{\prime}
be an immediate algebraic extension of a valuation ring

,

\hat{V}
the completion of
V
and

,
{\hat{K}}
the fraction fields of
V
,
{\hat{V}}
. Assume that

Assume that

Let

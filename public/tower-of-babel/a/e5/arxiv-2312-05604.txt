Abstract
. We present a general framework for constructing examples on Lavrentiev energy gap for nonlocal problems and apply it to several nonlocal and mixed models of double-phase type.

1.
Introduction

The Lavrentiev gap is known to appear in the study of local problems of variational type. The minimum of the integral functional taken over smooth functions may differ from the one taken over the associated energy space. This leads to significant challenges such as special kind of non-uniqueness for such problems, non-density of smooth functions, lack of regularity. Within this article we introduce the procedure to construct examples of an energy gap for nonlocal and mixed problems.
In particular we concentrate on several nonlocal and mixed models of double-phase type. The energy is defined as the sum of two parts (”phases”)
\mathcal{J}^{s}_{p}
and
\mathcal{J}^{t}_{q,a}
which are connected through a possible degenerate coefficient
a(\cdot,\cdot)
in the energy
\mathcal{J}^{t}_{q,a}
. The strong part
\mathcal{J}^{t}_{q,a}
is only activated at the part where the weight
a(\cdot,\cdot)
does not vanish.
Depending on the concrete structure of the phases we distinguish the following cases

Local model
(I)
:

\displaystyle\quad\mathcal{J}(v)=\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{p}{\lvert\nabla v\rvert}^{p}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{q}a(x){\lvert\nabla v\rvert}^{q}\,dx,

Nonlocal-local model
(II)
:

\displaystyle\quad\mathcal{J}(v)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{p}\frac{{\lvert v(x)-v(y)\rvert}^{p}}{{\lvert x-y\rvert}^{d+sp}}dxdy+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{q}a(x){\lvert\nabla v\rvert}^{q}\,dx,

Local-nonlocal model
(III)
:

\displaystyle\quad\mathcal{J}(v)=\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{p}{\lvert\nabla v\rvert}^{p}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{q}a(x,y)\frac{{\lvert v(x)-v(y)\rvert}^{q}}{{\lvert x-y\rvert}^{d+tq}}dxdy,

\displaystyle\text{Nonlocal model}\textbf{ (IV)}:

\displaystyle\quad\mathcal{J}(v)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{p}\frac{{\lvert v(x)-v(y)\rvert}^{p}}{{\lvert x-y\rvert}^{d+sp}}dxdy+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}\frac{1}{q}a(x,y)\frac{{\lvert v(x)-v(y)\rvert}^{q}}{{\lvert x-y\rvert}^{d+tq}}dxdy.

Here
0<s\leq t<1
,
p,q\in(1,\infty)
and
a:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is a nonnegative coefficient. When
a(x,y)=0
, the double phase models reduce to the
p
-Laplacian for the models
I
and
III
, and to the
s
-fractional
p
-Laplacian for the models
II
and
IV
.

1.1.
Known models

Most classical and well-studied is the local model
I
. The integrand

\varPhi(x,t)=\frac{1}{p}{\lvert t\rvert}^{p}+a(x)\frac{1}{q}{\lvert t\rvert}^{q}

defines the corresponding generalized Sobolev-Orlicz spaces
W^{1,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega)
(the natural energy space for
\mathcal{J}
for model
I
) . The Lavrentiev gap in this case is the inequality

(1)

\inf\mathcal{J}(W^{1,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega))<\inf\mathcal{J}(C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)).

A similar phenomenon for boundary value problems can be expressed as the inequality

(2)

\inf\mathcal{J}\big((u_{D}+W^{1,\varPhi(\cdot)}_{0}(\Omega)\big)<\inf\mathcal{J}\big(u_{D}+C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)\big)

for some
u_{D}\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
.

A closely related problem is density of smooth functions in the natural energy space of the functional. Denote the closure of smooth forms from
W^{1,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega)
in this space by
H^{1,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega)
. If any function from the domain of
\mathcal{J}
can be approximated by smooth functions with energy convergence (equivalently, if
H^{1,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega)=W^{1,\varPhi(\cdot)}(\Omega)
, which is abbreviated to
H=W
) then the Lavrentiev gap is obviously absent. In the autonomous case, when the integrand
\varPhi=\varPhi(t)
is an Orlicz function independent of
x
, the Lavrentiev phenomenon is absent (
H=W
). When
\varPhi:=\varPhi(x,t)
and
d=1
it was first observed in
[
Lav27
]
; for more general integrands see also recent results in
[
Mar23
]
.

In 1995 Zhikov
[
Zhi95
, Example 3.1]
considered local model
I
in the context of Lavrentiev phenomenon. He constructed with checkerboard
setup a weight
a\in C^{0,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
with

\alpha=1
,
\Omega=(-1,1)^{2}
and
p<2<2+\alpha=3<q
such that
the Lavrentiev gap occurs. This example was generalized by

[
ELM04
]
to the case of higher dimensions and less
regular weights, i.e.
\alpha\in(0,1]
. In particular,
for
\Omega=(-1,1)^{d}
, they constructed a
weight
a\in C^{0,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
and exponents

1<p<d<d+\alpha<q
such that the Lavrentiev gap occurs. In both examples by Zhikov and
Esposito-Leonetti-Mingione the two exponents
p
and
q
cross the
dimensional threshold
d
.
[
FMM04
]
studied fractal singular sets for such problems. The general procedure to construct examples on Lavrentiev gap for the local case without any dimensional restrictions was presented by
[
BDS20
]
and was based on fractal contact sets. For the so-called borderline case of double-phase the results on Lavrentiev gap are obtained by
[
BS21
]
. Recently this procedure was generalised to the case of the variational problems with differential forms by
[
BS23
]
.

Let us mention that Lavrentiev gap could appear for vectorial autonomous problems, where integrand only depends on the gradient of the function. Notable examples of such scenarios emerge in the field of nonlinear elasticity and were constructed by
[
FHM03
]
and more very recently modified by
[
AKM23
]
.

Challenges also arise in the numerical approximation of problems exhibiting the Lavrentiev phenomenon. Discussions on these challenges for non-autonomous functionals can be found in the works of
[
BOS22
]
and
[
BK23
]
.

Lavrentiev gap for the double phase potential can also be seen as a
lack of higher regularity, see
[
Mar89
]
for the first
example in this direction. In fact, local minimizers of
\mathcal{F}

need not be

-functions unless

The different question is related to the density of smooth functions in the corresponding generalized Sobolev-Orlicz spaces. The sufficient conditions for density for double phase local model were formulated in
[

]
[Lemma 2.1] for the case of Lipschitz continuous weight

The questions of density of smooth functions was recently studied by

In a nonlocal context,
[

employed a class of symmetric jump processes to create bounded harmonic functions that lack continuity. A careful analysis of the geometrical configuration of this example shows that it closely resembles the construction of a one-saddle point. In this particular model, the generative integrand is linear; however, the presence of a singular kernel introduces an intriguing phenomenon. This phenomenon prevents the Harnack inequality from conferring regularity properties. The study of non-local nonlinear models goes back to

Mixed problems of

1.2.
New models and main results

In this paper we show that nonlocal and mixed problems of the type
I
-
IV
admit energy gaps. We present the general method of constructing such examples for mixed and nonlocal models. We introduce the mixed model
III
and present the sharp bounds for the energy gap to occur. Our method extends and strengthens our previous local approach for the model
I
from
[
BDS20
]
and
[
BS21
]
.
This contains a new approach based on estimates for operator of Riesz potential type for nonlocal problems, which makes it possible to avoid the duality theory, which in the general case is not available for the nonlocal case.

Now we state the main results of this paper. We work with three models: nonlocal model
IV
, mixed models
II
-
III
. In each of these scenarios, we construct illustrative examples of the Lavrentiev gap. Importantly, the methodology we propose is not confined solely to these models and can be extended to a wider range of problem types. The main result if formulated in the following theorem.

Theorem 1

(Energy gap)
.

Let
\Omega=(-1,1)^{d}
with
d\geq 2
, be such that
t-\frac{q}{d}>s-\frac{p}{d}
and
t>s
. Then there exists a weight
a(\cdot,\cdot):\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,\infty)
which is measurable, symmetric, bounded and Hölder continuous function in
C^{\alpha}\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
and the boundary value
u_{D}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
such that

\displaystyle\inf\mathcal{J}\big(W_{u_{D}}(\Omega)\big)<\inf\mathcal{J}\big(H_{u_{D}}(\Omega)\big)

holds.

Function spaces
W_{u_{D}}(\Omega)
and
H_{u_{D}}(\Omega)
are defined later in Section
2
.

Remark 2
.

Let us mention, that the Theorem
1
gives the example of the energy gap for the case
q<p
for the model
IV
. In the purely local context this effect does not appear, since if
q<p
the situation is similar to the
p
-Dirichlet energy, where Lavrentiev gap does not occur.

Note, that the condition for the supercritical case and local model
I
here are written in the form to be easy compared with mixed and nonlocal case. This condition the same as the original one for the local double phase, formulated in
[
BDS20
]
[Section 4.2] as

q>p+\alpha\frac{p-1}{d-1}.

Let us mention that for the supercritical case no higher regularity results are knows till now neither for the nonlocal no for mixed problems. Nevertheless our conditions for the supercritical case indicate the possible bounds for the model parameters in future investigations, particularly in the density of smooth functions and regularity aspects.

For both subcritical and supercritical scenarios in the mixed and nonlocal models
II
-
IV
, the conditions we derive for the Lavrentiev gap
do not necessitate

q>p
. Conversely, in the local case of model
I
, such a condition becomes imperative to substantiate the ”double-phase” structure and the existence of the Lavrentiev gap. Despite the absence of regularity theory for the case
q<p
, the condition

t-\frac{d}{q}>s-\frac{d}{p}

ensures the embedding
W^{t,q}\hookrightarrow W^{s,p}
, thereby preserving the double-phase structure.

The proof of Theorem
1
relies on the construction and analysis of a special function, which is referred to as the ”competitor” and denoted by
u_{C}=u(x,y)
. This function is designed to possess certain properties and belongs to the energy space of the double-phase problem, which ensures that it has finite energy. However, for the subcritical case
\mathrm{index}(W^{s,p})<0
the competitor
u_{C}
has discontinuities across the fractal contact set (we denote the smooth version of it as
u_{D}
to be a boundary value). The transition weight function
a(x,y)
is constructed in a specific manner to suit the requirements of the problem and its parameters. In particular it behaves like
{\lvert x_{d}\rvert}^{\alpha}+{\lvert y_{d}\rvert}^{\alpha}
in the upper and lower regions of the domain
\Omega
, and it vanishing in the other regions close to the fractal contact set. In addition we construct a function
u_{D}
that agrees with the competitor
u_{C}
on
\Omega^{\complement}
but is
C^{\infty}
. This function
u_{D}
will be used later as the boundary value.

The overall strategy of the proof involves showing, under certain additional assumptions on the parameters of the model, that are different for the cases where
\mathrm{index}(W^{s,p})>0
and
\mathrm{index}(W^{s,p})<0
, that the following desired properties are achieved:

(a)

\mathcal{J}^{s}_{p}(u_{c})<\infty
and
J_{q,a}^{t}(u_{C})=0
(the competitor does not see the
J_{q,a}^{t}
phase).

(b)

for any smooth function
v\in C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})
with
v=u_{D}
on
\Omega^{\complement}
the second phase
J_{q,a}^{t}
is bounded from below:

J_{q,a}^{t}(v)\geq{\mathrm{const}}>0.

Combination of these properties gives an example of energy gap.

3.
Cantor barriers

We start with the one dimensional generalized Cantor
set
{\mathfrak{C}}_{\lambda}
with
\lambda\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
, which is also
known as the (1-2
\lambda
)-middle Cantor set. We begin with the
interval
{\mathfrak{C}}_{\lambda,0}:=(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})
. Then we
define
{\mathfrak{C}}_{\lambda,k+1}
inductively by removing the
middle
1-2\lambda
parts from
{\mathfrak{C}}_{\lambda,k}
. In particular, we
define
{\mathfrak{C}}_{\lambda}:=\cap_{k\geq 1}{\mathfrak{C}}_{\lambda,k}
. The
corresponding Cantor measure
\mu_{\lambda}
(also Cantor distribution)
is then defined as the weak limit of the
measures
\mu_{\lambda,k}:=(2\lambda)^{-k}\mathbbm{1}_{{\mathfrak{C}}_{\lambda,k}}\,dx
. The factor
(2\lambda)^{-k}
is chosen such
that
\mu_{\lambda,k}([-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}])=1=\mu([-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}])
. Thus,
\mu_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R})=1
and

\operatorname{supp}\mu_{\lambda}={\mathfrak{C}}_{\lambda}
. The fractal dimension
of
{\mathfrak{C}}_{\lambda}
is

\dim({\mathfrak{C}}_{\lambda})=\log(2)/\log(1/\lambda)\in(0,1)
,
i.e.
\lambda=2^{-{\mathfrak{D}}}
.

We will also need the
m
-dimensional Cantor sets
{\mathfrak{C}}_{\lambda}^{m}
and
its distribution
\mu^{m}_{\lambda}
, which are just the Cartesian
products of
{\mathfrak{C}}_{\lambda}
and
\mu_{\lambda}
. Its fractal dimension
is
\dim{\mathfrak{C}}_{\lambda}^{m}=m\dim{\mathfrak{C}}_{\lambda}=m\log(2)/\log(1/\lambda)\in(0,m)
. Note that

{\mathfrak{C}}_{\lambda}^{m}=\cap_{k\geq 1}{\mathfrak{C}}_{\lambda,k}^{m}
.

Our construction is based on the following ”upper” and ”lower” sets:

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\tau}^{+}

\displaystyle:={{\{{z\in\Omega,z_{d}>0:\dis(\overline{z},{\mathfrak{C}}^{m}_{\lambda})\leq\tau{\lvert z_{d}\rvert}}\}}},

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\tau}^{-}

\displaystyle:={{\{{z\in\Omega,z_{d}<0:\dis(\overline{z},{\mathfrak{C}}^{m}_{\lambda})\leq\tau{\lvert z_{d}\rvert}}\}}},

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\tau}

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{M}_{\tau}^{+}\cup\mathcal{M}_{\tau}^{-}.

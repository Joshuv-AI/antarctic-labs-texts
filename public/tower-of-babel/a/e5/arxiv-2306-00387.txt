1.
Introduction

Let
X
be a complex Banach space, and
\mathcal{B}(X)
the algebra of bounded linear operators on
X
. For
T\in\mathcal{B}(X)
,
let
\sigma(T)
be the spectrum of
T
.
Say
T
is
quasinilpotent
if
\sigma(T)=\{0\}
.
The hyperinvariant subspace problem asks:
Does every bounded operator
T
on an infinite dimensional Hilbert space have a non-trivial closed hyperinvariant (i.e., invariant for all the operators commuting with
T
) subspace?
This question is still open, especially for quasinilpotent operators.
We refer the readers to
[
4
,
5
,
11
,
12
,
13
]
and the references therein for more information along this line.

The
power set
\Lambda(T)
of a quasinilpotent operator
T
was introduced by Douglas and Yang (see
[
1
,
2
]
).
For reader’s convenience, we recall it below.

Definition 1.1
.

Suppose that
T\in\mathcal{B}(X)
is quasinilpotent and
x\in X\setminus\{0\}
. Let

k_{(T,x)}=\limsup\limits_{z\rightarrow 0}\frac{\ln\|(z-T)^{-1}x\|}{\ln\|(z-T)^{-1}\|}.

Usually, we briefly write
k_{x}
instead of
k_{(T,x)}
if there is no confusion possible.
Set
\Lambda(T)=\{k_{x}:x\neq 0\}
, and call it the
power set
of
T
.

Remark 1.2
.

For
\Lambda(T)
, there are two basic and often-used facts.

(1)

From

\frac{\|x\|}{|z|+\|T\|}\leq\|(z-T)^{-1}x\|\leq\|(z-T)^{-1}\|\|x\|,~~\forall z\neq 0,

it follows that
\Lambda(T)\subset[0,1]
.

(2)

Since
\lim\limits_{z\rightarrow 0}\|(z-T)^{-1}\|=+\infty
when
\sigma(T)=\{0\}
,
it holds that
k_{(\alpha x)}=k_{x}
for all
\alpha\neq 0
and
x\neq 0
.

Proposition 1.3

(see
[
2
]
)
.

Suppose that
T\in{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})}
, if
T^{n}=0
and
T^{n-1}\neq 0
, then
\Lambda(T)=\{\frac{j}{n}:j=1,2,\cdots,n\}
.

Following from that proposition above, when
T
is Jordan block of order
n
, it is obvious that
\operatorname{Lat}T
is order isomorphic
\{0\}\bigcup\Lambda(T)
.
Therefore, what instinct information about
\operatorname{Lat}T
can be seen from
\Lambda(T)
?
Douglas and Yang established a link between the power set and the hyperinvariant subspace problem as follows.

Proposition 1.4

(see
[
2
, Proposition 7.1, Corollary 7.2]
)
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{B}(X)
be a quasinilpotent operator.
For
\tau\in[0,1]
, write
M_{\tau}=\{x:k_{x}\leq\tau\}\bigcup\{0\}
.
Then,
M_{\tau}
is a linear subspace of
X
, and
A(M_{\tau})\subset M_{\tau}
for every
A
commuting with
T
.

In particular, when
\Lambda(T)
contains two different points
\tau
with closed
M_{\tau}
,

T
has a nontrivial closed hyperinvariant subspace.

In fact,
M_{\tau}
is not always closed.
In
[
8
]
,
Ji and Liu constructed a quasinilpotent operator
T\in{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})}
with power set
[0,1]
,
for which
M_{\tau}
is not closed for each
\tau\in[0,1)
.
But they proved that there exists a subset
\mathcal{N}
of
\operatorname{Lat}T
,
which is order isomorphic to
\Lambda(T)
.
Therefore, the following question which appears in
[
8
]
, comes as most natural.

Question 1
.

For any quasinilpotent operator
T\in\mathcal{B}(X)
, can one find a subset
\mathcal{N}
of
\operatorname{Lat}T
which is order isomorphic to
\Lambda(T)
?

Furthermore, we can consider more questions about the power set.
In
[
7
,
8
]
, we observe that the subsets
\{1\}
and
[0,1]
can be the power set of some quasinilpotent operator.
Can we find other subsets of
[0,1]
that correspond to power set of some quasinilpotent operator?
Thus, we may consider the following question.

Question 2
.

Which subsets of
[0,1]
could be the power set of a quasinilpotent operator
T\in\mathcal{B}(X)
?

Given a Banach space
X
, we let
X^{\prime}
denote its dual space. Suppose that
T\in\mathcal{B}(X)
, we denote by
T^{\prime}
the adjoint of acting on
X^{\prime}
. Comparing with the fact that
\sigma(T)=\sigma(T^{\prime})
, one may naturally consider the following question.

Question 3
.

Are
\Lambda(T)
and
\Lambda(T^{\prime})
intrinsically linked?

In light of these questions, this paper is devoted to the calculations of the power set of quasinilpotent weighted shifts on
\ell^{p}
.
To proceed, we recall some terminology and notation.

For
1\leq p<\infty
, let
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})
be the Banach space of all two-sided
p
-summable sequences of complex numbers, and
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N})
be the Banach space of all sequences

\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})
for which
x_{n}=0
for all
n\leq 0
.
Let
\{e_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{K}}
be the canonical basis for
l^{p}(\mathbb{K})
, where the set
\mathbb{K}
will be the set
\mathbb{N}
or
\mathbb{Z}
.

Say operator
T
on
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N})
is a
forward unilateral weighted shift
with weight sequence
\{w_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
if
T\in\mathcal{B}(\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N}))
and if

Te_{n}=w_{n}e_{n+1},\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N},

i.e.,
T
has the following matrix representation

\begin{bmatrix}0\\
w_{0}&0\\
&w_{1}&0\\
&&w_{2}&0\\
&&&\ddots&\ddots\end{bmatrix}\begin{matrix}e_{0}\\
e_{1}\\
e_{2}\\
e_{3}\\
\vdots\end{matrix}.

Say operator
T
on
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N})
is a
backward unilateral weighted shift
with weight sequence
\{w_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
if
T\in\mathcal{B}(\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N}))
and if

Te_{0}=0,\quad Te_{n}=w_{n-1}e_{n-1},\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N},

i.e.,
T
has the following matrix representation

\begin{bmatrix}0&w_{0}\\
&0&w_{1}\\
&&0&w_{2}\\
&&&\ddots&\ddots\end{bmatrix}\begin{matrix}e_{0}\\
e_{1}\\
e_{2}\\
\vdots\end{matrix}.

Say operator
T
on
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N})
is a
bilateral weighted shift
with weight sequence
\{w_{n}\}_{n=-\infty}^{+\infty}
if
T\in\mathcal{B}(\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}))
and if

Te_{n}=w_{n}e_{n+1},\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{Z},

i.e.,
T
has the following matrix representation

\begin{bmatrix}\ddots\\
\ddots&0&\\
&w_{-2}&0\\
&&w_{-1}&0\\
&&&w_{0}&0\\
&&&&w_{1}&0\\
&&&&&\ddots&\ddots\\
\end{bmatrix}\begin{matrix}\vdots\\
e_{-2}\\
e_{-1}\\
e_{0}\\
e_{1}\\
e_{2}\\
\vdots\end{matrix}.

Now we review some known results about the calculations of the power set of quasinilpotent operators.
Let
V
be the Volterra operator on the Hardy space
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
, defined by
(Vf)(z)=\int_{0}^{z}f(\xi)d\xi,f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
.
Liang and Yang
[
10
]
first observed that
\Lambda(V)=\{1\}
.
Actually,
V
is a strongly strictly cyclic forward unilateral weighted shift on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
with weight sequence
\{\frac{1}{n+1}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
.
In
[
8
]
, Ji and Liu showed that if
T
is a strongly strictly cyclic quasinilpotent forward unilateral weighted shift on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N})
, then
\Lambda(T)=\{1\}.

Later,
He and Zhu generalized Ji and Liu’s above mentioned result by showing that
if
T
is either a strictly cyclic quasinilpotent forward unilateral weighted shift
or a strongly strictly cyclic quasinilpotent operator

l^{2}(\mathbb{N})
, then
\Lambda(T)=\{1\}

[
7
]
.

Given a weight sequence
\{w_{n}\}_{n}
, for
1\leq p<\infty
,
let
T_{p}
be the weighted shift with weight sequence
\{w_{n}\}_{n}
on
\ell^{p}
.
A natural question is:

Question 4
.

Is the power set
\Lambda(T_{p})
independent of
p
?

In Section
2
, we prove that if
T_{p}
is a quasinilpotent forward unilateral weighted shift on
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N})
,
then
\Lambda(T_{p})=\{1\}
when
k_{(T_{p},e_{0})}=1
.
As an application, we show that if
T_{p}
is a strictly cyclic quasinilpotent forward unilateral weighted shift on
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N})
, then
\Lambda(T_{p})
are the same for different
p
and
\Lambda(T_{p})=\{1\}
.
This also gives a partial answer to Question
4
.

Next, we consider the backward weighted shift on
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N})
.
Indeed, given a forward weighted shift
A
on
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N})
with weight sequence
\{w_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
,
it is well-known that
A^{\prime}
is the backward weighted shift on
(l^{p}(\mathbb{N}))^{\prime}

with weight sequence
\{w_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
.
Now we suppose that
T
is a quasinilpotent backward unilateral weighted shift on
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N})
.
Since
\|(z-T)^{-1}e_{n}\|_{p}^{p}
is a polynomial in
\frac{1}{z}
with degree
n+1
, it is not difficult to check that
k_{(T,e_{n})}=0
for each
n\geq 0
.
Moreover, one can also check that
k_{(T,x)}=0
when
x\in\operatorname{span}\{e_{n}:n\geq 0\}
.
Thus, it is interesting to consider the following question.

Question 5
.

For any quasinilpotent backward unilateral weighted shift
T
on
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N})
, is it true that
\Lambda(T)=\{0\}
?

In Section
3
, we first prove that the power set of a class of backward unilateral weighted shifts
can contain
1
. That provides a negative answer to Question
5
.
Moreover, we show that the power set of some backward unilateral weighted shifts is
[0,1]
.
In particular, they include the backward unilateral weighted shift
T
with weight sequence
\{\frac{1}{n+1}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
.
In fact,
T
is unicellular and has only countable many invariant subspaces (see
[
14
]
).
So one can not find a subset
\mathcal{N}
of
\operatorname{Lat}T
which is order isomorphic to
\Lambda(T)
.
That also provides a negative answer to Question
1
.
In addition, in view of these results, it also provides some information for Question
3
.

In Section
4
, we construct a bilateral weighted shift on
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})
for
1<p<\infty
,
which power set is
[\frac{1}{2},1]
.
This provides a new method to construct a quasinilpotent operator whose power set is an interval segment on
[0,1]
, and brings a new insight into the Question
2
.

2.
Power set of forward unilateral weighted shifts

In this section, we aim to investigate the power set of forward unilateral weighted shifts on
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N})
for
1\leq p<\infty
.
Before proceeding, let us recall some basic properties for weighted shifts.

Given a weighted shift
T
with the weight sequence
\{w_{n}\}_{n}
, there exists a surjective isometry
U
on
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N})
such that
U^{-1}TU
is the weighted shift with weight sequence
\{|w_{n}|\}_{n}
(see
[
14
]
). From this, we say
T
have circular symmetry. Moreover, if
w_{n}\neq 0
for each
n
, then say that
T
is injective.
Thus, without loss of generality, we may assume that
w_{n}>0
for all
n
.
In order to prove the main theorem, we first give the following lemmas.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
T
be a quasinilpotent forward unilateral weighted shift on
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N})
.
Then, for any polynomial
\varphi
,

\lim\limits_{z\rightarrow 0}\frac{\ln|\varphi(|z|)|}{\ln\|(z-T)^{-1}e_{0}\|_{p}}=0.

Proof.
.

One can refer to the proof in
[
8
, Lemma 3.6]
.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
T
be a quasinilpotent forward unilateral weighted shift on
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N})
.
Suppose that
x\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N})
and
k_{x}\neq 0
, then
k_{(T^{n}x)}=k_{x}
for all
n\geq 0
.

Proof.
.

By Lemma
2.1
, it is easy to check that

\lim\limits_{z\rightarrow 0}\frac{\ln\frac{1}{|z|}}{\ln\|(z-T)^{-1}\|_{p}}=0.

For
z\neq 0
, we have

\displaystyle(z-T)^{-1}x

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{z}+\frac{1}{z^{2}}Tx+\frac{1}{z^{3}}T^{2}x+\frac{1}{z^{4}}T^{3}x+\cdots

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{z}+\frac{1}{z}(\frac{1}{z}Tx+\frac{1}{z^{2}}T^{2}x+\frac{1}{z^{3}}T^{3}x+\cdots)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{z}+\frac{1}{z}(z-T)^{-1}Tx.

Therefore,

\frac{1}{|z|}\|(z-T)^{-1}Tx\|_{p}=\|(z-T)^{-1}x-\frac{1}{z}\|_{p}
and

\ln\frac{1}{|z|}+\ln\|(z-T)^{-1}Tx\|_{p}=\ln\|(z-T)^{-1}x-\frac{1}{z}\|_{p}.

Since
\lim\limits_{z\rightarrow 0}\|(z-T)^{-1}\|=+\infty
and
k_{x}\neq 0
, we can obtain that

k_{(Tx)}=k_{x}
. Assume that
n=k
, have
k_{(T^{k}x)}=k_{x}
, we next prove that
k_{(T^{k+1}x)}=k_{x}
.
For
z\neq 0
, we also have

\displaystyle(z-T)^{-1}T^{k}x

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{z}T^{k}x+\frac{1}{z^{2}}T^{k+1}x+\frac{1}{z^{3}}T^{k+2}x+\frac{1}{z^{4}}T^{k+3}x+\cdots

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{z}T^{k}x+\frac{1}{z}(\frac{1}{z}T^{k+1}x+\frac{1}{z^{2}}T^{k+2}x+\frac{1}{z^{3}}T^{k+3}x+\cdots)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{z}T^{k}x+\frac{1}{z}(z-T)^{-1}T^{k+1}x.

Thus,

\frac{1}{|z|}\|(z-T)^{-1}T^{k+1}x\|_{p}=\|(z-T)^{-1}x-\frac{1}{z}T^{k}x\|_{p}
and

\ln\frac{1}{|z|}+\ln\|(z-T)^{-1}T^{k+1}x\|_{p}=\ln\|(z-T)^{-1}T^{k}x-\frac{1}{z}T^{k}x\|_{p}.

It follows that
k_{(T^{k+1}x)}=k_{x}
.
So,
k_{(T^{n}x)}=k_{x}
for all
n\geq 0
.
∎

For
k\geq 0
, set
f_{k}(x)=x_{k}
,
\forall x=\{x_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}\in l^{p}(\mathbb{N})
. Then
f_{k}\in(l^{p}(\mathbb{N}))^{\prime}
, the dual space of
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N})
, and
\|f_{k}\|=1
.
If
1<p<\infty
, for
f\in(l^{p}(\mathbb{N}))^{\prime}
, there is unique
\xi=\{\xi_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}\in l^{q}(\mathbb{N})
such that
f=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\xi_{k}f_{k}
, where
q=\frac{p}{p-1}
. In this case,
\|f\|=(\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}|\xi_{k}|^{q})^{\frac{1}{q}}
.
If
p=1
, for
f\in(l^{p}(\mathbb{N}))^{\prime}
, there is unique
\xi=\{\xi_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}\in l^{\infty}(\mathbb{N})
such that
f=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\xi_{k}f_{k}
in weak-star topology, and
\|f\|=\sup\limits_{k}|\xi_{k}|
.

For convenience, we write

\|f\|_{q}=\begin{cases}\big(\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}|\xi_{k}|^{q}\big)^{\frac{1}{q}}&\textrm{ if }q\neq\infty;\\
\sup\limits_{k}|\xi_{k}|&\textrm{ if }q=\infty.\end{cases}

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
T
be a quasinilpotent forward unilateral weighted shift on
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N})

with weight sequence
\{w_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
.
If
k_{e_{0}}\neq 0
,
then

k_{x}\geq k_{e_{0}}
for any non-zero
x\in l^{p}(\mathbb{N})
.

Proof.
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle(z-T)^{-1}e_{n}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{z^{k+1}}T^{k}e_{n}=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{w_{n}w_{n+1}\cdots w_{n+k-1}}{z^{k+1}}e_{n+k}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\beta_{n+k}}{\beta_{n}z^{k+1}}e_{n+k}=\beta_{n}^{-1}z^{n-1}\sum\limits_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{\beta_{k}}{z^{k}}e_{k}.

Suppose that
x=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\hat{x}_{n}e_{n}\in l^{p}(\mathbb{N})
and
x\neq 0
, then there exists a non-negative integer
n_{0}

such that
\hat{x}_{n_{0}}\neq 0
and
\hat{x}_{j}=0,\forall j<n_{0}
. Thus,

\displaystyle{(z-T)^{-1}x}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\hat{x}_{n}(z-T)^{-1}e_{n}=\sum\limits_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}\hat{x}_{n}(z-T)^{-1}e_{n}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}\hat{x}_{n}\beta_{n}^{-1}z^{n-1}\sum\limits_{k=n}^{\infty}\frac{\beta_{k}}{z^{k}}e_{k}=\sum\limits_{k=n_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{\beta_{k}}{z^{k}}(\sum\limits_{n=n_{0}}^{k}\hat{x}_{n}\beta_{n}^{-1}z^{n-1})e_{k}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\|(z-T)^{-1}x\|_{p}

\displaystyle=\sup\Big\{|f((z-T)^{-1}x)|:f\in(l^{p}(\mathbb{N}))^{\prime},\|f\|=1\Big\}

\displaystyle=\sup\Big\{\sum\limits_{k=n_{0}}^{\infty}\big|\frac{\xi_{k}\beta_{k}}{z^{k}}\big|\big|\sum\limits_{n=n_{0}}^{k}\hat{x}_{n}\beta_{n}^{-1}z^{n-1}\big|:f=\{\xi_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}\in l^{q}(\mathbb{N}),\|f\|_{q}=1\Big\}

\displaystyle\geq\sum\limits_{k=n_{0}}^{\infty}\big|\frac{\xi_{k}\beta_{k}}{z^{k}}\big|\big|\sum\limits_{n=n_{0}}^{k}\hat{x}_{n}\beta_{n}^{-1}z^{n-1}\big|.

We next compute the integral mean value of
\|(z-T)^{-1}x\|_{p}
.
Let
z=re^{i\theta}
with
r=|z|
. Then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\|(re^{i\theta}-T)^{-1}x\|_{p}d\theta

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\sum\limits_{k=n_{0}}^{\infty}\big|\frac{\xi_{k}\beta_{k}}{(re^{i\theta})^{k}}\big|\big|\sum\limits_{n=n_{0}}^{k}\hat{x}_{n}\beta_{n}^{-1}(re^{i\theta})^{n-1}\big|d\theta

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=n_{0}}^{\infty}\big|\frac{\xi_{k}\beta_{k}}{r^{k}}\big|\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\big|\sum\limits_{n=n_{0}}^{k}\hat{x}_{n}\beta_{n}^{-1}r^{n-1}e^{i(n-n_{0})\theta}\big|d\theta

\displaystyle\geq\sum\limits_{k=n_{0}}^{\infty}\big|\frac{\xi_{k}\beta_{k}}{r^{k}}\big|\frac{1}{2\pi}\big|\int_{0}^{2\pi}\sum\limits_{n=n_{0}}^{k}\hat{x}_{n}\beta_{n}^{-1}r^{n-1}e^{i(n-n_{0})\theta}d\theta\big|

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=n_{0}}^{\infty}\big|\frac{\xi_{k}\beta_{k}}{r^{k}}\big|\big|\hat{x}_{n_{0}}\beta_{n_{0}}^{-1}r^{n_{0}-1}\big|

\displaystyle\geq\big|\hat{x}_{n_{0}}\big|\big|\sum\limits_{k=n_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{\xi_{k}\beta_{k}}{r^{k}}\beta_{n_{0}}^{-1}r^{n_{0}-1}\big|

\displaystyle=\big|\hat{x}_{n_{0}}\big|\big|f((z-T)^{-1}e_{{n_{0}}})\big|,

where
f=\{\xi_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}\in(l^{p}(\mathbb{N}))^{\prime}
and
\|f\|_{q}=1
. Thus for any
|z|=r
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{\theta\in[0,2\pi]}\|(re^{i\theta}-T)^{-1}x\|_{p}

\displaystyle\geq{\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\|(re^{i\theta}-T)^{-1}x\|_{p}d\theta}

\displaystyle\geq|x_{n_{0}}|\|(z-T)^{-1}e_{n_{0}}\|_{p}.

Pick
z=re^{i\theta}
so that

\|(z-T)^{-1}x\|_{p}=\sup_{\theta\in[0,2\pi]}\|(re^{i\theta}-T)^{-1}x\|_{p}.

Thereby,

(2.1)

\|(re^{i\theta}-T)^{-1}x\|_{p}\geq|\hat{x}_{n_{0}}|\|(re^{i\theta}-T)^{-1}e_{n_{0}}\|_{p}.

Note that for
z\neq 0
,

\|(z-T)^{-1}e_{n_{0}}\|_{p}=\|(|z|-T)^{-1}e_{n_{0}}\|_{p}.

And since
T
have circular symmetry, we have that

\|(z-T)^{-1}\|=\|(|z|-T)^{-1}\|,~~\forall z\neq 0.

So

\displaystyle k_{e_{n_{0}}}=\limsup\limits_{|z|\rightarrow 0}\frac{\ln\|(|z|-T)^{-1}e_{n_{0}}\|_{p}}{\ln\|(|z|-T)^{-1}\|}.

We can choose a sequence
\{r_{j}\}_{j=0}^{\infty}\subset[0,+\infty)
with
\lim\limits_{j\rightarrow\infty}r_{j}=0
such that

(2.2)

k_{e_{n_{0}}}=\lim\limits_{j\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\ln\|(r_{j}-T)^{-1}e_{n_{0}}\|_{p}}{\ln\|(r_{j}-T)^{-1}\|}.

By (
2.1
), one can pick a sequence
\{z_{j}\}_{j=0}^{\infty}\subset\mathbb{C}
with
|z_{j}|=r_{j}
such that

(2.3)

\liminf_{j\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\ln\|(z_{j}-T)^{-1}x\|_{p}}{\ln\|(z_{j}-T)^{-1}e_{n_{0}}\|_{p}}\geq 1.

Combining (
2.2
) and (
2.3
), we can deduce that

\displaystyle{k_{x}}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{z\rightarrow 0}\frac{\ln\|(z-T)^{-1}x\|_{p}}{\ln\|(z-T)^{-1}\|}

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{j\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\ln\|(z_{j}-T)^{-1}x\|_{p}}{\ln\|(z_{j}-T)^{-1}\|}

\displaystyle=\liminf_{j\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\ln\|(z_{j}-T)^{-1}x\|_{p}}{\ln\|(z_{j}-T)^{-1}e_{n_{0}}\|_{p}}\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\ln\|(z_{j}-T)^{-1}e_{n_{0}}\|_{p}}{\ln\|(z_{j}-T)^{-1}\|}

\displaystyle=\liminf_{j\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\ln\|(z_{j}-T)^{-1}x\|_{p}}{\ln\|(z_{j}-T)^{-1}e_{n_{0}}\|_{p}}\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\ln\|(r_{j}-T)^{-1}e_{n_{0}}\|_{p}}{\ln\|(r_{j}-T)^{-1}\|}

\displaystyle\geq k_{e_{n_{0}}}.

Moreover, since
T^{n_{0}}e_{0}=\prod\limits_{j=0}^{n_{0}-1}w_{j}e_{n_{0}}
and by Lemma
2.2
,
we have
k_{e_{n_{0}}}=k_{e_{0}}
.
Thus, it holds that
k_{x}\geq k_{e_{0}}
for any non-zero
x\in l^{p}(\mathbb{N})
.
∎

From the above lemma, we can obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.4
.

Suppose that
T
is a quasinilpotent forward unilateral weighted shift on
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N})
.
If
k_{e_{0}}=1
, then
\Lambda(T)=\{1\}
.

Proof.
.

For any non-zero
x\in l^{p}(\mathbb{N})
, by Lemma
2.3
and the fact
0\leq k_{x}\leq 1
, we have

k_{x}=1
.
Hence,
\Lambda(T)=\{1\}
.
∎

Next, we apply Theorem
2.4
to give the following two corollaries.

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
T
is a forward unilateral weighted shift on
l^{1}(\mathbb{N})
with weight sequence
\{w_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
. If
\{w_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is decreasing to
0
, then
\Lambda(T)=\{1\}
.

Proof.
.

It is easy to check that

\|T^{n}\|=\|T^{n}e_{0}\|=\prod\limits_{j=0}^{n-1}w_{j}

and
\sigma(T)=\{0\}
.
For
z\neq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\|(z-T)^{-1}\|=\big\|\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{T^{n}}{z^{n+1}}\big\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\|T^{n}\|}{|z|^{n+1}}=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{|z|^{n+1}}\prod\limits_{j=0}^{n-1}w_{j}

and

\|(z-T)^{-1}e_{0}\|_{1}\leq\|(z-T)^{-1}\|
.
Thus,
\|(z-T)^{-1}e_{0}\|_{1}=\|(z-T)^{-1}\|

and

k_{e_{0}}=\limsup_{z\rightarrow 0}\frac{\ln\|(z-T)^{-1}e_{0}\|_{1}}{\ln\|(z-T)^{-1}\|}=\limsup_{z\rightarrow 0}\frac{\ln\|(z-T)^{-1}\|}{\ln\|(z-T)^{-1}\|}=1.

By Theorem
2.4
, we have
\Lambda{(T)}=\{1\}
.
∎

Remark 2.6
.

In
[
3
]
, Embry showed that if
T
is a forward unilateral weighted shift on
l^{1}(\mathbb{N})
with weight sequence decreasing to
0
, then
T
is strictly cyclic.
However, this is not valid for
p>1
.
For counterexample, we can see
[
6
]
.
Next, we will prove that if
T
is strictly cyclic on
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N})
for
1\leq p<\infty
, then
\Lambda(T)=\{1\}
.

Recall that
T\in\mathcal{B}(X)
is called strictly cyclic if there exists a vector
x_{0}\in X
such that
\{Ax_{0}:A\in\mathcal{A}(T)\}=X
,
where
\mathcal{A}(T)
denote the closed subalgebra generated by the identity
I
and
T
in the weak operator topology.
Such vector
x_{0}
is called a strictly cyclic vector for
T
(see
[
14
]
).

Corollary 2.7
.

Let
T
be a strictly cyclic quasinilpotent forward unilateral weighted shift on
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N})
for
1\leq p<\infty
, then
\Lambda(T)=\{1\}
.

Proof.
.

Obviously,
e_{0}
is a cyclic vector for
T
. Let
\Phi
be the map from
\mathcal{A}(T)
to
X
such that

\Phi(A)=Ae_{0}
for
A\in\mathcal{A}(T)
.
Then
\Phi
is invertible (see
[
14
, Page 93]
).
So, there exists a constant
c>0
such that

c\|A\|\leq\|Ae_{0}\|\leq\|A\|,\quad\forall A\in\mathcal{A}(T).

Since
T
quasinilpotent, for
z\neq 0
,
(z-T)^{-1}\in\mathcal{A}(T)
.
Thus

\displaystyle\frac{\ln\|(z-T)^{-1}e_{0}\|_{p}}{\ln\|(z-T)^{-1}\|}\geq\frac{\ln C\|(z-T)^{-1}\|}{\ln\|(z-T)^{-1}\|}=\frac{\ln C}{\ln\|(z-T)^{-1}\|}+1

and
\liminf\limits_{z\rightarrow 0}\frac{\ln\|(z-T)^{-1}e_{0}\|_{p}}{\ln\|(z-T)^{-1}\|}\geq 1
. It follows from
0\leq k_{e_{0}}\leq 1
that

\displaystyle k_{e_{0}}=\lim\limits_{z\rightarrow 0}\frac{\ln\|(z-T)^{-1}e_{0}\|_{p}}{\ln\|(z-T)^{-1}\|}=1.

By Theorem
2.4
, we have
\Lambda(T)=\{1\}
.
∎

3.
Some backward weighted shifts with power set
[0,1]

For
1\leq p<\infty
, let
T
be a quasinilpotent backward unilateral weighted shift on
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N})
with weight sequence
\{w_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
.
Given
m\geq 1
, for
z\neq 0
,

\displaystyle\|(z-T)^{-1}e_{m}\|_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle=\|\sum\limits_{i=0}^{m}\frac{1}{z^{i+1}}T^{i}e_{m}\|_{p}^{p}=\|\frac{e_{m}}{z}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\big(\frac{1}{z^{i+1}}\prod\limits_{j=0}^{i-1}w_{m-j}\big)e_{m-i}\|_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|z|^{p}}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\Big|\frac{1}{z^{i+1}}\prod\limits_{j=0}^{i-1}w_{m-j}\Big|^{p}.

Since
\|(z-T)^{-1}e_{N}\|_{p}\leq\|(z-T)^{-1}\|
for any
N\in\mathbb{N}
, it follows that

\displaystyle k_{e_{m}}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{z\rightarrow 0}\frac{\ln\|(z-T)^{-1}e_{m}\|_{p}}{\ln\|(z-T)^{-1}\|}

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{z\rightarrow 0}\frac{\ln\|(z-T)^{-1}e_{m}\|_{p}}{\ln\|(z-T)^{-1}e_{N}\|_{p}}=\frac{m+1}{N+1}.

Thus, we have
k_{e_{m}}=0
, because the above formula holds for any
N\in\mathbb{N}
.
Further, it is obvious that
k_{e_{0}}=0
.
Therefore, we conclude that
k_{e_{m}}=0
for every
m\geq 0
.
From this, it is easy to check that
k_{x}=0
when
x\in\operatorname{span}\{e_{n}:n\geq 0\}
.
So, this gives people a false impression that
\Lambda(T)=\{0\}
.
Actually, that is not the case.
In this section, we will prove that the power set of a class of backward unilateral weighted shifts is
[0,1]
.

Throughout this section, for
k\geq 0
, we set
f_{k}(x)=x_{k}
for any
x=\{x_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}\in l^{p}(\mathbb{N})
.
We first give the following Theorem.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
T
be a backward unilateral weighted shift on
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N})

with decreasing weight sequence
\{w_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
.
If
\{w_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is
p^{\prime}
-summable for some positive number
p^{\prime}
,
then
1\in\Lambda(T)
.

Proof.
.

Obviously,
T
is a quasinilpotent operator. Since
\{w_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is
p^{\prime}
-summable for some positive number
p^{\prime}
,
one can pick
m\geq 1
so that the sequence
\{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m}w_{n+j}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is summable.
Set
x=\{\alpha_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
, where
\alpha_{n}=\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m}w_{n+j}
.
Then
x\in l^{p}(\mathbb{N})
for any
p\in[1,+\infty)
.
We can first observe that

\|(z-T)^{-1}x\|_{p}\geq\big|f_{0}\big((z-T)^{-1}x\big)\big|,\quad\forall z\neq 0,

and

\displaystyle k_{x}=\limsup_{z\rightarrow 0}\frac{\ln\|(z-T)^{-1}x\|_{p}}{\ln\|(z-T)^{-1}\|}\geq\limsup_{z\rightarrow 0}\frac{\ln\big|f_{0}\big((z-T)^{-1}x\big)\big|}{\ln\|(z-T)^{-1}\|}.

Next, we compute the value of
f_{0}\big((z-T)^{-1}x\big)
.
For
z\neq 0
,

(3.1)

\displaystyle f_{0}\big((z-T)^{-1}x\big)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{f_{0}(T^{n}x)}{z^{n+1}}=\frac{\alpha_{0}}{z}+\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}}{z^{n+1}}\alpha_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha_{0}}{z}+\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n+m}w_{j}}{z^{n+1}}=\frac{\alpha_{0}}{z}+z^{m}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n+m}w_{j}}{z^{n+m+1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha_{0}}{z}+z^{m}\sum\limits_{k=m+1}^{\infty}\frac{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{k}w_{j}}{z^{k+1}}=\frac{\alpha_{0}}{z}+z^{m}\big(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{k}w_{j}}{z^{k+1}}-\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}\frac{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{k}w_{j}}{z^{k+1}}\big).

Furthermore, since
\{w_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is decreasing, it follows that
\|T^{k}\|=\prod\limits_{j=1}^{k}w_{j}
and

(3.2)

\|(z-T)^{-1}\|\leq\frac{1}{|z|}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\|T^{k}\|}{|z|^{k+1}}=\frac{1}{|z|}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{k}w_{j}}{|z|^{k+1}}.

Therefore, combining (
3.1
) and (
3.2
), we can deduce that

(3.3)

\displaystyle k_{x}

\displaystyle\geq\limsup_{z\rightarrow 0}\frac{\ln|f_{0}\big((z-T)^{-1}x\big)|}{\ln\|(z-T)^{-1}\|}

\displaystyle\geq\limsup_{z\rightarrow 0}\frac{\ln\big|\frac{\alpha_{0}}{z}+z^{m}\big(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{k}w_{j}}{z^{k+1}}-\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}\frac{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{k}w_{j}}{z^{k+1}}\big)\big|}{\ln\Big(\frac{1}{|z|}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{k}w_{j}}{|z|^{k+1}}\Big)}

\displaystyle\geq\limsup_{|z|\rightarrow 0}\frac{\ln\Big(\frac{\alpha_{0}}{|z|}+|z|^{m}\big(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{k}w_{j}}{|z|^{k+1}}-\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}\frac{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{k}w_{j}}{|z|^{k+1}}\big)\Big)}{\ln\Big(\frac{1}{|z|}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{k}w_{j}}{|z|^{k+1}}\Big)}=1.

By the fact
0\leq k_{x}\leq 1
, we have
k_{x}=1
. Hence
1\in\Lambda(T)
.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

By Theorem
3.1
, one can see that the power set of a backward weighted shift can also contain
1
.
Therefore, it will be very interesting to investigate its power set.
Next, we will show that the power set of a class of backward weighted shifts is
[0,1]
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
T
be a quasinilpotent backward unilateral weighted shift on
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N})
with weight sequence
\{w_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
.
If there is a natural number
m
such that

x=\{\alpha_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
in
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N})
, where
\alpha_{0}=1
and
\alpha_{n}=\big((n+m)!\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}\big)^{-1}
for
n\geq 1
,
then
[0,k_{x}]\subset\Lambda(T)
.

Proof.
.

For
0<r\leq 1
, let
x_{r}=\big\{a_{n}\big\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
, where
a_{0}=1
and
a_{n}=r^{n}\big((n+m)!\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}\big)^{-1}
for
n\geq 1
.
Then
x_{1}=x
and
x_{r}\in l^{p}(\mathbb{N})
.
For
z\neq 0
,

(3.4)

\displaystyle f_{0}\big((|z|-T)^{-1}x_{r}\big)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{f_{0}(T^{k}x_{r})}{|z|^{k+1}}=\frac{1}{|z|}\Big(1+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(k+m)!}\frac{r^{k}}{|z|^{k}}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|z|}\Big(1+\frac{|z|^{m}}{r^{m}}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(k+m)!}\frac{r^{k+m}}{|z|^{k+m}}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|z|}+\frac{|z|^{m-1}}{r^{m}}\sum\limits_{n=m+1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}\frac{r^{n}}{|z|^{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|z|}+\frac{|z|^{m-1}}{r^{m}}\Big(\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}\frac{r^{n}}{|z|^{n}}-\sum\limits_{n=1}^{m}\frac{1}{n!}\frac{r^{n}}{|z|^{n}}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|z|}+\frac{|z|^{m-1}}{r^{m}}\Big(e^{\frac{r}{|z|}}-\sum\limits_{n=0}^{m}\frac{1}{n!}\frac{r^{n}}{|z|^{n}}\Big).

It follows that

(3.5)

\displaystyle\lim_{z\rightarrow 0}\frac{\ln\big|f_{0}\big((|z|-T)^{-1}x_{r}\big)\big|}{\ln\big|f_{0}\big((|z|-T)^{-1}x\big)\big|}=\lim_{|z|\rightarrow 0}\frac{\ln\Big(\frac{1}{|z|}+\frac{|z|^{m-1}}{r^{m}}\big(e^{\frac{r}{|z|}}-\sum\limits_{n=0}^{m}\frac{1}{n!}\frac{r^{n}}{|z|^{n}}\big)\Big)}{\ln\Big(\frac{1}{|z|}+|z|^{m-1}\big(e^{\frac{1}{|z|}}-\sum\limits_{n=0}^{m}\frac{1}{n!}\frac{1}{|z|^{n}}\big)\Big)}=r.

Moreover, for any
n\geq 1
we have

\displaystyle f_{n}\big((z-T

\displaystyle)^{-1}x_{r}\big)=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{f_{n}(T^{k}x_{r})}{z^{k+1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(n+m)!\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}}\frac{r^{n}}{z}+\frac{w_{n+1}}{(n+m+1)!\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n+1}w_{j}}\frac{r^{n+1}}{z^{2}}+\frac{w_{n+1}w_{n+2}}{(n+m+2)!\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n+2}w_{j}}\frac{r^{n+2}}{z^{3}}+\cdots

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(n+m)!\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}}\frac{r^{n}}{z}\Big(1+\frac{1}{n+m+1}\frac{r}{z}+\frac{1}{(n+m+1)(n+m+2)}\frac{r^{2}}{z^{2}}+\cdots\Big)

and

\displaystyle|f_{n}\big((z-T)^{-1}x_{r}\big)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(n+m)!\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}}\frac{r^{n}}{|z|}\Big(1+\frac{1}{n+m+1}\frac{r}{|z|}+\frac{1}{(n+m+1)(n+m+2)}\frac{r^{2}}{|z|^{2}}+\cdots\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(n+m)!\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}}\frac{r^{n}}{|z|}\Big(1+\frac{1}{(m+1)}\frac{r}{|z|}+\frac{1}{(m+1)(m+2)}\frac{r^{2}}{|z|^{2}}+\cdots\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{r^{n}}{(n+m)!\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}}|f_{0}\big((|z|-T)^{-1}x_{r}\big)|.

It follows that

(3.6)

\displaystyle\|(z-T)^{-1}x_{r}\|_{p}

\displaystyle=\Big(\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\big|f_{n}\big((z-T)^{-1}x_{r}\big)\big|^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\big|f_{0}\big((|z|-T)^{-1}x_{r}\big)\big|\bigg(1+\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{r^{n}}{(n+m)!\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}}\Big)^{p}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=\big|f_{0}\big((|z|-T)^{-1}x_{r}\big)\big|\|x_{r}\|_{p}.

Moreover, it is obvious that
\|(z-T)^{-1}x_{r}\|_{p}\geq\big|f_{0}\big((z-T)^{-1}x_{r}\big)\big|
,
\forall z\neq 0
. So

\displaystyle\frac{\ln\big|f_{0}\big((|z|-T)^{-1}x_{r}\big)\big|}{\ln\|(z-T)^{-1}\|}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\ln\|(z-T)^{-1}x_{r}\|_{p}}{\ln\|(z-T)^{-1}\|}\leq\frac{\ln\big|f_{0}\big((|z|-T)^{-1}x_{r}\big)\big|\|x_{r}\|_{p}}{\ln\|(z-T)^{-1}\|}.

On the other hand, since
T
have circular symmetry, we have

\|(z-T)^{-1}\|=\|(|z|-T)^{-1}\|,~~\forall z\neq 0.

Then, we can conclude that

(3.7)

\displaystyle k_{x_{r}}=\limsup\limits_{|z|\rightarrow 0}\frac{\ln\big|f_{0}\big((|z|-T)^{-1}x_{r}\big)\big|}{\ln\|(|z|-T)^{-1}\|},~~\forall r\in(0,1].

Combining
(\ref{fr})
and
(\ref{r01})
, we can deduce that

\displaystyle k_{x_{r}}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{z\rightarrow 0}\frac{\ln\|(z-T)^{-1}x_{r}\|_{p}}{\ln\|(z-T)^{-1}\|}

\displaystyle=\lim_{|z|\rightarrow 0}\frac{\ln\big|f_{0}\big((|z|-T)^{-1}x_{r}\big)\big|}{\ln\big|f_{0}\big((|z|-T)^{-1}x\big)\big|}\limsup_{|z|\rightarrow 0}\frac{\ln\big|f_{0}\big((|z|-T)^{-1}x\big)\big|}{\ln\|(|z|-T)^{-1}\|}

\displaystyle=r\cdot\limsup_{|z|\rightarrow 0}\frac{\ln\big|f_{0}\big((|z|-T)^{-1}x\big)\big|}{\ln\|(|z|-T)^{-1}\|}=rk_{x}.

Since
0<r\leq 1
and
0\in\Lambda(T)
, it holds that
[0,k_{x}]\subset\Lambda(T)
.
∎

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
T
be a backward unilateral weighted shift on
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N})
with weight sequence
\{w_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
.
If there is a positive number
m_{0}
such that
\frac{1}{n+2m_{0}}\leq w_{n+m_{0}}\leq\frac{1}{n}
for
n\geq 1
, then
\Lambda(T)=[0,1]
.

Proof.
.

Pick a positive number
m>2m_{0}+2
, then for
n>m_{0}
we have

\displaystyle\big((n+m)!\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}\big)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\big((n+m)!\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m_{0}}w_{j}\prod\limits_{j=m_{0}+1}^{n}w_{j}\big)^{-1}

\displaystyle\leq(\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m_{0}}w_{j})^{-1}\frac{(n+2m_{0})!}{(n+m)!}\leq(\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m_{0}}w_{j})^{-1}\frac{1}{(n+m)(n+m+2)}.

From this, we have

(3.8)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\big((n+m)!\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}\big)^{-1}\leq\sum\limits_{n=1}^{m_{0}}\big((n+m)!\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}\big)^{-1}+\sum\limits_{n=m_{0}+1}^{\infty}\frac{(\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m_{0}}w_{j})^{-1}}{(n+m)(n+m+2)}<\infty.

Let
x=\{\alpha_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
, where
\alpha_{0}=1
and
\alpha_{n}=\big((n+m)!\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}\big)^{-1}
for
n\geq 1
.
Then
x\in l^{p}(\mathbb{N})
for any
p\in[1,+\infty)
.
By Lemma
3.3
, have
[0,k_{x}]\subset\Lambda(T)
.
Next, we prove
k_{x}=1
.
From
w_{n+m_{0}}\leq\frac{1}{n}
, we can deduce that

\displaystyle\|(z-T)^{-1}\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\|T^{k}\|}{|z|^{k+1}}=\frac{1}{|z|}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{k}w_{j}}{|z|^{k+1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|z|}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m_{0}}\frac{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{k}w_{j}}{|z|^{k+1}}+\sum\limits_{k=m_{0}+1}^{\infty}\frac{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{k}w_{j}}{|z|^{k+1}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|z|}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m_{0}}\frac{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{k}w_{j}}{|z|^{k+1}}+\sum\limits_{k=m_{0}+1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!|z|^{k+1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|z|}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m_{0}}\frac{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{k}w_{j}}{|z|^{k+1}}+\frac{1}{|z|}\big(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!|z|^{k}}-\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m_{0}}\frac{1}{k!|z|^{k}}\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|z|}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m_{0}}\frac{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{k}w_{j}}{|z|^{k+1}}+\frac{1}{|z|}\big(e^{\frac{1}{|z|}}-\sum\limits_{k=0}^{m_{0}}\frac{1}{k!|z|^{k}}\big).

Combining (
3.4
), we can obtain that

\displaystyle k_{x}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{z\rightarrow 0}\frac{\ln\|(z-T)^{-1}x\|_{p}}{\ln\|(z-T)^{-1}\|}

\displaystyle\geq\limsup_{z\rightarrow 0}\frac{\ln|f_{0}\big((z-T)^{-1}x\big)|}{\ln\|(z-T)^{-1}\|}

\displaystyle\geq\limsup_{|z|\rightarrow 0}\frac{\ln\Big(\frac{1}{|z|}+|z|^{m-1}\big(e^{\frac{1}{|z|}}-\sum\limits_{n=0}^{m}\frac{1}{n!}\frac{1}{|z|^{n}}\big)\Big)}{\ln\Big(\frac{1}{|z|}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m_{0}}\frac{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{k}w_{j}}{|z|^{k+1}}+\frac{1}{|z|}\big(e^{\frac{1}{|z|}}-\sum\limits_{k=0}^{m_{0}}\frac{1}{k!|z|^{k}}\big)\Big)}=1.

Since
0\leq k_{x}\leq 1
, it yields that
k_{x}=1
. Hence
\Lambda(T)=[0,1]
.
∎

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
T
be the backward unilateral weighted shift with weight sequence
\{\frac{1}{n+1}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
on
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N})
for
1\leq p<\infty
. Then
\Lambda(T)=[0,1]
.

Proof.
.

It follows immediately from Theorem
3.4
.
∎

4.
A bilateral weighted shift with power set
[\frac{1}{2},1]

For
1<p<\infty
, let
A
be an injective forward unilateral weighted shift on
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N})
with weight sequence
\{w_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
.
If
\{w_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is monotone decreasing and
p^{\prime}
-summable for some positive number
p^{\prime}
, then
A
is strictly cyclic (see
[
9
, Corollary 3.5]
).
So, by Corollary
2.7
, we have
\Lambda(A)=\{1\}
.
From this, the aim of this section is to construct a bilateral weighted shift with power set
[\frac{1}{2},1]
on
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})
for
1<p<\infty
.

For
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, set
f_{k}(x)=x_{k}
,
\forall x=\{x_{n}\}_{n=-\infty}^{+\infty}\in l^{p}(\mathbb{Z})
.
Let
e_{1}\otimes f_{0}
denote the rank-one operator on
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})
defined as
(e_{1}\otimes f_{0})(x)=f_{0}(x)e_{1},\forall x\in l^{p}(\mathbb{Z})
.
Now, we defined a bilateral weighted shift on
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})
as follows:

(4.1)

T=\begin{bmatrix}B&0\\
e_{1}\otimes f_{0}&A\end{bmatrix}

where

A=\begin{bmatrix}0\\
w_{1}&0\\
&w_{2}&0\\
&&w_{3}&0\\
&&&\ddots&\ddots\end{bmatrix}\begin{matrix}e_{1}\\
e_{2}\\
e_{3}\\
e_{4}\\
\vdots\end{matrix},\ \ \ \ B=\begin{bmatrix}0&w_{1}\\
&0&w_{2}\\
&&0&w_{3}\\
&&&0&w_{4}\\
&&&&\ddots&\ddots\end{bmatrix}\begin{matrix}e_{0}\\
e_{-1}\\
e_{-2}\\
e_{-3}\\
\vdots\end{matrix}.

Note that
A=B^{tr}
, where
B^{tr}
is the transpose of
B
. And it is not difficult to prove that
\|(z-A)^{-1}\|=\|(z-B)^{-1}\|
for
z\neq 0
.
Then we have the following lemmas.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
T
be as in
(\ref{bmT})
above. If
\{w_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is monotone decreasing and
p^{\prime}
-summable for some positive number
p^{\prime}
, then
\Lambda(T)\subset[\frac{1}{2},1]
.

Proof.
.

We first compute the value of
\|(z-T)^{-1}\|
.
For
z\neq 0
,

(4.2)

(z-T)^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}(z-B)^{-1}&0\\
-(z-A)^{-1}e_{1}\otimes(z-B^{\prime})^{-1}f_{0}&(z-A)^{-1}\end{bmatrix},

where
B^{\prime}
is the Banach conjugate of
B
.
It follows that

\|(z-T)^{-1}\|\leq\|(z-A)^{-1}\|+\|(z-B)^{-1}\|+\|(z-A)^{-1}e_{1}\|_{p}\|(z-B^{\prime})^{-1}f_{0}\|_{q}

and

\|(z-A)^{-1}e_{1}\|_{p}\|(z-B^{\prime})^{-1}f_{0}\|_{q}\leq\|(z-T)^{-1}\|,

where
q=\frac{p}{p-1}
.
Noting that
e_{1}
is a strictly cyclic vector of
A
. So, there exists a constant
c>0
such that

c\|(z-A)^{-1}\|\leq\|(z-A)^{-1}e_{1}\|_{p}\leq\|(z-A)^{-1}\|.

From this, we have

(4.3)

k_{(A,e_{1})}=\lim\limits_{z\rightarrow 0}\frac{\ln\|(z-A)^{-1}e_{1}\|_{p}}{\ln\|(z-A)^{-1}\|}=1.

For convenience, given two functions
X(z),Y(z):\mathbb{C}\backslash{\{0\}}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
, we write
X(z)\approx Y(z)
if
\lim\limits_{z\rightarrow 0}\frac{X(z)}{Y(z)}=1
.
Hence, when
z
tends to
0
, we have that

\ln\|(z-A)^{-1}\|\approx\ln\|(z-A)^{-1}e_{1}\|_{p}.

Similarly, we can also obtain

\ln\|(z-A)^{-1}\|=\ln\|(z-B)^{-1}\|=\ln\|(z-B^{\prime})^{-1}\|\approx\ln\|(z-B^{\prime})^{-1}f_{0}\|_{q}.

Therefore, as
z
tends to
0
, we have

(4.4)

\displaystyle\ln\|(z-T)^{-1}\|

\displaystyle\approx\ln\|(z-A)^{-1}e_{1}\|_{p}\|(z-B^{\prime})^{-1}f_{0}\|_{q}

\displaystyle\approx\ln\|(z-A)^{-1}\|^{2}.

Next, we prove that
k_{(T,x)}\geq\frac{1}{2}
holds for any non-zero
x\in l^{p}(\mathbb{Z})
.
Let

x=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}x_{1}\\
x_{2}\end{array}\right]\in l^{p}(\mathbb{Z})
and
x\neq 0
. Then

(4.5)

(z-T)^{-1}x=\begin{bmatrix}(z-B)^{-1}x_{1}\\
-\big((z-B^{\prime})^{-1}f_{0}\big)(x_{1})(z-A)^{-1}e_{1}+(z-A)^{-1}x_{2}\end{bmatrix}.

The rest proof is divided into three cases.

Case 1.

x_{1}=0,x_{2}\neq 0
.

It is clear that
\|(z-T)^{-1}x\|_{p}=\|(z-A)^{-1}x_{2}\|_{p}
.
Since
k_{(A,x_{2})}=1
, it follows that

\displaystyle k_{(T,x)}=\limsup_{z\rightarrow 0}\frac{\ln\|(z-T)^{-1}x\|_{p}}{\ln\|(z-T)^{-1}\|}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{z\rightarrow 0}\frac{\ln\|(z-A)^{-1}x_{2}\|_{p}}{\ln\|(z-T)^{-1}\|}

\displaystyle\approx\limsup_{z\rightarrow 0}\frac{\ln\|(z-A)^{-1}x_{2}\|_{p}}{\ln\|(z-A)^{-1}\|^{2}}=\frac{1}{2}.

Case 2.

x_{1}\neq 0,x_{2}=0
.

In this case, we can observe that

\|(z-T)^{-1}x\|_{p}\geq\big|\big((z-B^{\prime})^{-1}f_{0}\big)(x_{1})\big|\|(z-A)^{-1}e_{1}\|_{p}.

Notice that

\displaystyle\big((z-B^{\prime})^{-1}f_{0}\big)(x_{1})=f_{0}\big((z-B)^{-1}x_{1}\big)=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{f_{0}(B^{k}x_{1})}{z^{k+1}}.

Since
x_{1}\neq 0
, we have that

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{z\rightarrow 0}\big|\big((z-B^{\prime})^{-1}f_{0}\big)(x_{1})\big|=+\infty.

We can pick a sequence
\{z_{j}\}_{j=0}^{\infty}\subset\mathbb{C}
with
\lim\limits_{j\rightarrow\infty}|z_{j}|=0
such that

(4.6)

|\big((z_{j}-B^{\prime})^{-1}f_{0}\big)(x_{1})|>1,\quad\forall j\geq 0.

Combining (
4.3
) and (
4.6
), we can deduce that

\displaystyle k_{(T,x)}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{z\rightarrow 0}\frac{\ln\|(z-T)^{-1}x\|_{p}}{\ln\|(z-T)^{-1}\|}

\displaystyle\approx\limsup_{z\rightarrow 0}\frac{\ln\|(z-T)^{-1}x\|_{p}}{\ln\|(z-A)^{-1}\|^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\limsup_{z\rightarrow 0}\frac{\ln\big|\big((z-B^{\prime})^{-1}f_{0}\big)(x_{1})\big|+\ln\|(z-A)^{-1}e_{1}\|_{p}}{\ln\|(z-A)^{-1}\|^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\limsup_{j\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\ln\big|\big((z_{j}-B^{\prime})^{-1}f_{0}\big)(x_{1})\big|+\ln\|(z_{j}-A)^{-1}e_{1}\|_{p}}{\ln\|(z_{j}-A)^{-1}\|^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{2}.

Case 3.

x_{i}\neq 0,i=1,2
.

Let
x_{2}=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}e_{n}

and
\widetilde{x}_{2}=\sum\limits_{n=2}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}e_{n}
.
We have

\displaystyle-\big((z-B^{\prime})^{-1}f_{0}\big)(x_{1})(z-A)^{-1}e_{1}+(z-A)^{-1}x_{2}

\displaystyle=\big(-\big((z-B^{\prime})^{-1}f_{0}\big)(x_{1})+\alpha_{1}\big)(z-A)^{-1}e_{1}+(z-A)^{-1}\widetilde{x}_{2}

and

\displaystyle\|(z-T)^{-1}x\|_{p}

\displaystyle\geq\big\|\big(\alpha_{1}-\big((z-B^{\prime})^{-1}f_{0}\big)(x_{1})\big)(z-A)^{-1}e_{1}+(z-A)^{-1}\widetilde{x}_{2}\big\|_{p}

\displaystyle\geq\big|\big(\alpha_{1}-\big((z-B^{\prime})^{-1}f_{0}\big)(x_{1})\big)\big|\|(z-A)^{-1}e_{1}\|_{p}-\|(z-A)^{-1}\widetilde{x}_{2}\|_{p}.

Notice that

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{z\rightarrow 0}\big|\big(\alpha_{1}-\big((z-B^{\prime})^{-1}f_{0}\big)(x_{1})\big)\big|=+\infty.

We can pick a sequence
\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j=0}^{\infty}\subset\mathbb{C}
with
\lim\limits_{j\rightarrow\infty}|\lambda_{j}|=0
such that

(4.7)

\big|\big(\alpha_{1}-((\lambda_{j}-B^{\prime})^{-1}f_{0})(x_{1})\big)\big|>1,\quad\forall j\geq 0.

Moreover, since
k_{(A,\widetilde{x}_{2})}=1
and
k_{(A,e_{1})}=1
, it follows that

\displaystyle\ln\Big(\big|

\displaystyle\big(\alpha_{1}-\big((z-B^{\prime})^{-1}f_{0}\big)(x_{1})\big)\big|\|(z-A)^{-1}e_{1}\|_{p}-\|(z-A)^{-1}\widetilde{x}_{2}\|_{p}\Big)

\displaystyle\approx\ln\Big(\big|\big(\alpha_{1}-\big((z-B^{\prime})^{-1}f_{0}\big)(x_{1})\big)\big|\|(z-A)^{-1}e_{1}\|_{p}\Big)

when
z
tends to
0
. Then combining (
4.3
) and (
4.7
), we can deduce that

\displaystyle k_{(T,x)}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{z\rightarrow 0}\frac{\ln\|(z-T)^{-1}x\|_{p}}{\ln\|(z-T)^{-1}\|}

\displaystyle\approx\limsup_{z\rightarrow 0}\frac{\ln\|(z-T)^{-1}x\|_{p}}{\ln\|(z-A)^{-1}\|^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\limsup_{z\rightarrow 0}\frac{\ln\big|\big(\alpha_{1}-\big((z-B^{\prime})^{-1}f_{0}\big)(x_{1})\big)\big|\|(z-A)^{-1}e_{1}\|_{p}}{\ln\|(z-A)\|^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\limsup_{j\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\ln\big|\big(\alpha_{1}-\big((\lambda_{j}-B^{\prime})^{-1}f_{0}\big)(x_{1})\big)\big|\|(\lambda_{j}-A)^{-1}e_{1}\|_{p}}{\ln\|(\lambda_{j}-A)^{-1}\|^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{2}.

In summary,
k_{(T,x)}\geq\frac{1}{2}
for all non-zero
x\in l^{p}(\mathbb{Z})
.
Hence,
\Lambda(T)\subset[\frac{1}{2},1]
.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
T
be as in
(\ref{bmT})
above. If
\{w_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is monotone decreasing and
p^{\prime}
-summable for some positive number
p^{\prime}
, then
1\in\Lambda(T)
.

Proof.
.

Pick
m\geq 1
such that the sequence
\{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m}w_{n+j}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is summable.
Write
\alpha_{n}=\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m}w_{n+j}
for
n\geq 0
. And
let

y=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\xi_{1}\\
\xi_{2}\end{array}\right]
,
where

\xi_{1}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}\alpha_{0}\\
\alpha_{1}\\
\alpha_{2}\\
\vdots\end{array}\right]\begin{matrix}e_{0}\\
e_{-1}\\
e_{-2}\\
\vdots\end{matrix},\ \ \xi_{2}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}0\\
0\\
0\\
\vdots\end{array}\right]\begin{matrix}e_{1}\\
e_{2}\\
e_{3}\\
\vdots\end{matrix}.

Then
y\in l^{p}(\mathbb{Z})
for any
p\in[1,+\infty)
and

(z-T)^{-1}y=\begin{bmatrix}(z-B)^{-1}\xi_{1}\\
-\big((z-B^{\prime})^{-1}f_{0}\big)(\xi_{1})(z-A)^{-1}e_{1}+(z-A)^{-1}\xi_{2}\end{bmatrix}.

It follows that

\|(z-T)^{-1}y\|_{p}\geq\big|\big((z-B^{\prime})^{-1}f_{0}\big)(\xi_{1})\big|\|(z-A)^{-1}e_{1}\|_{p}.

Notice that
\big|\big((z-B^{\prime})^{-1}f_{0}\big)(\xi_{1})\big|=\big|f_{0}\big((z-B)^{-1}\xi_{1}\big)\big|
.
By (
3.3
), one can observe that

\displaystyle\limsup_{z\rightarrow 0}\frac{\ln\big|\big((z-B^{\prime})^{-1}f_{0}\big)(\xi_{1})\big|}{\ln\|(z-B^{\prime})^{-1}\|}\geq 1.

Since
\|(z-A)^{-1}\|=\|(z-B)^{-1}\|=\|(z-B^{\prime})^{-1}\|
, we have

\limsup_{z\rightarrow 0}\frac{\ln\big|\big((z-B^{\prime})^{-1}f_{0}\big)(\xi_{1})\big|}{\ln\|(z-B^{\prime})^{-1}\|}=\limsup_{z\rightarrow 0}\frac{\ln\big|\big((z-B^{\prime})^{-1}f_{0}\big)(\xi_{1})\big|}{\ln\|(z-A)^{-1}\|}\geq 1.

Combining (
4.3
), we can obtain that

\displaystyle k_{(T,y)}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{z\rightarrow 0}\frac{\ln\|(z-T)^{-1}y\|_{p}}{\ln\|(z-T)^{-1}\|}

\displaystyle\approx\limsup_{z\rightarrow 0}\frac{\ln\|(z-T)^{-1}y\|_{p}}{\ln\|(z-A)^{-1}\|^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\limsup_{z\rightarrow 0}\frac{\ln\big|\big((z-B^{\prime})^{-1}f_{0}\big)(\xi_{1})\big|+\ln\|(z-A)^{-1}e_{1}\|_{p}}{\ln\|(z-A)^{-1}\|^{2}}\geq 1.

By the fact
0\leq k_{y}\leq 1
, it holds that
k_{y}=1
. Hence
1\in\Lambda(T)
.
∎

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
T
be as in
(\ref{bmT})
above.
If there is a positive number
m_{0}
such that
\frac{1}{n+2m_{0}}\leq w_{n+m_{0}}\leq\frac{1}{n}
for
n\geq 1
,
then
\Lambda(T)=[\frac{1}{2},1]
.

Proof.
.

By Lemmas
4.1
and
4.2
, we have
\Lambda(T)\subset[\frac{1}{2},1]
and
1\in\Lambda(T)
.
Next, we need only prove
[\frac{1}{2},1)\subset\Lambda(T)
.

Pick a natural number
m>2m_{0}+2
, for
0<r\leq 1
, set

x_{r}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\xi_{r}\\
\xi_{0}\end{array}\right]
,
where

\xi_{r}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}1\\
r\big((1+m)!w_{1}\big)^{-1}\\
r^{2}\big((2+m)!\prod\limits_{j=1}^{2}w_{j}\big)^{-1}\\
\vdots\end{array}\right]\begin{matrix}e_{0}\\
e_{-1}\\
e_{-2}\\
\vdots\end{matrix},\ \ \xi_{0}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}0\\
0\\
0\\
\vdots\end{array}\right]\begin{matrix}e_{1}\\
e_{2}\\
e_{3}\\
\vdots\end{matrix}.

By (
3.8
), we have
x_{r}\in l^{p}(\mathbb{Z})
for any
p\in[1,+\infty)
. Then

(z-T)^{-1}x_{r}=\begin{bmatrix}(z-B)^{-1}\xi_{r}\\
-\big((z-B^{\prime})^{-1}f_{0}\big)(\xi_{r})(z-A)^{-1}e_{1}+(z-A)^{-1}\xi_{0}\end{bmatrix}.

It follows that

\|(z-T)^{-1}x_{r}\|_{p}=\|(z-B)^{-1}\xi_{r}\|_{p}+\big|\big((z-B^{\prime})^{-1}f_{0}\big)(\xi_{r})\big|\|(z-A)^{-1}e_{1}\|_{p}.

For
\|(z-B)^{-1}\xi_{r}\|_{p}
, by (
3.6
), one can observe that

\displaystyle\big|f_{0}\big((z-B)^{-1}\xi_{r}\big)\big|\leq\|(z-B)^{-1}\xi_{r}\|_{p}\leq\big|f_{0}\big((z-B)^{-1}\xi_{r}\big)\big|\|\xi_{r}\|_{p}.

So, when
z
tends to
0
, we have

\ln\|(z-B)^{-1}\xi_{r}\|_{p}\approx\ln\big|f_{0}\big((z-B)^{-1}\xi_{r}\big)\big|.

For
\|(z-A)^{-1}e_{1}\|_{p}
, by (
4.3
), we have that

\ln\|(z-A)^{-1}e_{1}\|_{p}\approx\ln\|(z-A)^{-1}\|.

For
\big|\big((z-B^{\prime})^{-1}f_{0}\big)(\xi_{r})\big|
, by
(\ref{fxr})
, it follows that

\displaystyle\big|\big((z-B^{\prime})^{-1}f_{0}\big)(\xi_{r})\big|

\displaystyle=\big|f_{0}\big((z-B)^{-1}\xi_{r}\big)\big|=\big|\frac{1}{z}+\frac{z^{m-1}}{r^{m}}\big(e^{\frac{r}{z}}-\sum\limits_{n=0}^{m}\frac{1}{n!}\frac{r^{n}}{z^{n}}\big)\big|.

Hence, as
z
tends to
0
, we have

\displaystyle\ln\|(z-T)^{-1}x_{r}\|_{p}

\displaystyle\approx\ln\|(z-A)^{-1}e_{1}\|_{p}\big|\big((z-B^{\prime})^{-1}f_{0}\big)(\xi_{r})\big|.

Notice that

\displaystyle\|(z-A)^{-1}\|

\displaystyle=\|(z-B)^{-1}\|\geq\frac{1}{\|\xi_{1}\|_{p}}\|(z-B)^{-1}\xi_{1}\|_{p}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\|\xi_{1}\|_{p}}|f_{0}((z-B)^{-1}\xi_{1})|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\|\xi_{1}\|_{p}}\big|\frac{1}{z}+z^{m-1}\big(e^{\frac{1}{z}}-\sum\limits_{n=0}^{m}\frac{1}{n!}\frac{1}{z^{n}}\big)\big|

and

\displaystyle\|(z-A)^{-1}\|\leq\frac{1}{|z|}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m_{0}}\frac{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{k}w_{j}}{|z|^{k+1}}+\frac{1}{|z|}\big(e^{\frac{1}{|z|}}-\sum\limits_{k=0}^{m_{0}}\frac{1}{k!|z|^{k}}\big).

So, as
z
tends to
0
, we have

\ln\|(z-A)^{-1}\|\approx\ln e^{\frac{1}{|z|}}
and

\displaystyle\ln\|(z-T)^{-1}x_{r}\|_{p}

\displaystyle\approx\ln e^{\frac{1}{|z|}}+\ln\big|\frac{1}{z}+\frac{z^{m-1}}{r^{m}}\big(e^{\frac{r}{z}}-\sum\limits_{n=0}^{m}\frac{1}{n!}\frac{r^{n}}{z^{n}}\big)\big|.

It follows that

\displaystyle k_{(T,x_{r})}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{z\rightarrow 0}\frac{\ln\|(z-T)^{-1}x_{r}\|_{p}}{\ln\|(z-T)^{-1}\|}

\displaystyle\approx\limsup_{z\rightarrow 0}\frac{\ln\|(z-A)^{-1}e_{1}\|_{p}\big|\big((z-B^{\prime})^{-1}f_{0}\big)(\xi_{r})\big|}{\ln\|(z-A)^{-1}\|^{2}}

\displaystyle\approx\limsup_{z\rightarrow 0}\frac{\ln e^{\frac{1}{|z|}}+\ln\big|\frac{1}{z}+\frac{z^{m-1}}{r^{m}}\big(e^{\frac{r}{z}}-\sum\limits_{n=0}^{m}\frac{1}{n!}\frac{r^{n}}{z^{n}}\big)\big|}{\ln(e^{\frac{1}{|z|}})^{2}}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{|z|\rightarrow 0}\frac{\ln e^{\frac{1}{|z|}}+\ln\Big(\frac{1}{|z|}+\frac{|z|^{m-1}}{r^{m}}\big(e^{\frac{r}{|z|}}-\sum\limits_{n=0}^{m}\frac{1}{n!}\frac{r^{n}}{|z|^{n}}\big)\Big)}{\ln(e^{\frac{1}{|z|}})^{2}}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{|z|\rightarrow 0}\frac{\ln e^{\frac{1}{|z|}}+\ln e^{\frac{r}{|z|}}\Big(\frac{(e^{\frac{r}{|z|}})^{-1}}{|z|}+\frac{|z|^{m-1}}{r^{m}}\big(1-(e^{\frac{r}{|z|}})^{-1}\sum\limits_{n=0}^{m}\frac{1}{n!}\frac{r^{n}}{|z|^{n}}\big)\Big)}{\ln(e^{\frac{1}{|z|}})^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1+r}{2}.

Since
0<r\leq 1
, it yields that
(\frac{1}{2},1]\subset\Lambda(T)
.

Finally, we prove that
\frac{1}{2}\in\Lambda(T)
.
Suppose that

\widetilde{x}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\beta_{1}\\
\beta_{2}\end{array}\right]
,
where

\beta_{1}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}1\\
0\\
0\\
\vdots\end{array}\right]\begin{matrix}e_{0}\\
e_{-1}\\
e_{-2}\\
\vdots\end{matrix},\ \ \beta_{2}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}0\\
0\\
0\\
\vdots\end{array}\right]\begin{matrix}e_{1}\\
e_{2}\\
e_{3}\\
\vdots\end{matrix}.

Then
\widetilde{x}\in l^{p}(\mathbb{Z})
and

(z-T)^{-1}\widetilde{x}=\begin{bmatrix}(z-B)^{-1}\beta_{1}\\
-\big((z-B^{\prime})^{-1}f_{0}\big)(\beta_{1})(z-A)^{-1}e_{1}+(z-A)^{-1}\beta_{2}\\
\end{bmatrix}.

It follows that

\|(z-T)^{-1}\widetilde{x}\|_{p}=\|(z-B)^{-1}\beta_{1}\|_{p}+\big|\big((z-B^{\prime})^{-1}f_{0}\big)(\beta_{1})\big|\|(z-A)^{-1}e_{1}\|_{p}.

Notice that

\big|\big((z-B^{\prime})^{-1}f_{0}\big)(\beta_{1})\big|=\big|f_{0}((z-B)^{-1}\beta_{1})\big|=\frac{1}{|z|}

and
\|(z-B)^{-1}\beta_{1}\|_{p}=\frac{1}{|z|^{p}}
.
So, when
z
tends to
0
, we have

\displaystyle\ln\|(z-T)^{-1}\widetilde{x}\|_{p}

\displaystyle\approx\ln\big|\big((z-B^{\prime})^{-1}f_{0}\big)(\beta_{1})\big|\|(z-A)^{-1}e_{1}\|_{p}

\displaystyle\approx\ln\frac{1}{|z|}+\ln\|(z-A)^{-1}\|.

Then

\displaystyle k_{(T,\widetilde{x})}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{z\rightarrow 0}\frac{\ln\|(z-T)^{-1}\widetilde{x}\|_{p}}{\ln\|(z-T)^{-1}\|}

\displaystyle\approx\limsup_{z\rightarrow 0}\frac{\ln\frac{1}{|z|}+\ln\|(z-A)^{-1}\|}{\ln\|(z-A)^{-1}\|^{2}}=\frac{1}{2}.

Therefore,
\frac{1}{2}\in\Lambda(T)
. In summary
\Lambda(T)=[\frac{1}{2},1]
, completing the proof.
∎

We close this paper with an interesting question.

Question 6
.

Given a weighted shift
T
on
l^{\infty}
, is there the same power set as
T
on
\ell^{p}
for
p<\infty
?

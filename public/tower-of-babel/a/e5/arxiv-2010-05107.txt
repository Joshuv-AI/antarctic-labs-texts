Kolmogorov widths of Besov classes B 1 , θ 1 B^{1}_{1,\theta} and products of octahedra
Yuri Malykhin

Abstract
In this paper we find the orders of decay for Kolmogorov widths of some Besov classes related to W 1 1 W^{1}_{1} (the behaviour of the widths for W 1 1 W^{1}_{1} remains unknown): d_n(B^1_1, θ [0,1],L_q[0,1])≍n^-1/2log^max( 1 2 ,1- 1 θ )n, 2¡q¡∞. The proof relies on the lower bound for widths of product of octahedra in a special norm (maximum of two weighted ℓ q \ell_{q} norms). This bound generalizes the theorem of B.S. Kashin on widths of octahedra in ℓ q N \ell_{q}^{N} .



1
Introduction

Let us recall the definition of widths.
Let
X
be a normed space and
K\subset X
. The Kolmogorov
n
-width of
K
in

X
is the following quantity

d_{n}(K,X):=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q_{n}\subset X\\
\dim Q_{n}\leqslant n\end{subarray}}\sup_{x\in K}\inf_{y\in Q}\|x-y\|_{X},

where
\inf_{Q_{n}}
is taken over all linear subspaces in
X
having dimension at
most
n
.

The main problem of the widths theory is to find the order of decay of

d_{n}(W,X)
at
n\to\infty
for functional classes and finite-dimensional sets.
One of interesting cases is the family of Sobolev spaces
\mathcal{W}^{r}_{p}
consisting of
functions
f
with a finite norm
\|f\|_{p}+\|f^{(r)}\|_{p}
and Sobolev classes

W^{r}_{p}
(unit balls in corresponding spaces).
The order of
d_{n}(W^{r}_{p}[0,1],L_{q}[0,1])
is known for all

r\in\mathbb{N}
and
1\leqslant p,q\leqslant\infty
(V.M. Tikhomirov, B.S. Kashin,
E.D. Gluskin, R.S. Ismagilov — see
[
10
]
, Chapters 13, 14, and
the survey
[
8
]
, §4.3), except the case
r=1
,
p=1
,

q\in(2,\infty)
. There is a logarithmic gap
[
9
,
18
]
:

c(q)n^{-1/2}\log^{1/2}n\leqslant d_{n}(W^{1}_{1}[0,1],L_{q}[0,1])\leqslant C(q)n^{-1/2}\log n.

The order of approximation is known only for special approximation subspaces

Q_{n}=\mathrm{span}\{\exp(im_{j}x)\}_{j=1}^{n}
, see.
[
12
]
:

d_{n}^{T}(W^{1}_{1}[0,1],L_{q}[0,1])\asymp n^{-1/2}\log n
.

Sobolev spaces are related to Besov spaces. Let us give the definition,
following
[
6
]
, Chapter 2, §10.
Given parameters
r>0
,
p,\theta\in[1,\infty]
and a (finite or infinite)
segment
I\subset\mathbb{R}
, we define the Besov space

\mathcal{B}^{r}_{p,\theta}(I)
as the space of functions

f\in L_{p}(I)
having a finite semi-norm (with
s=1+\lfloor r\rfloor
)

|f|_{B^{r}_{p,\theta}(I)}:=\begin{cases}\int_{0}^{\infty}(t^{-r}\omega_{s}(f,t)_{p}^{\theta})^{1/\theta}\frac{dt}{t},&\quad 1\leqslant\theta<\infty,\\
\sup_{t>0}t^{-r}\omega_{s}(f,t)_{p},&\quad\theta=\infty.\end{cases}

Here
\omega_{s}(f,t)_{p}:=\sup_{0<h\leqslant t}\|\Delta_{h}^{s}(f,\cdot)\|_{L_{p}(I\cap(I-sh))}
is the usual modulus of
smoothness. The norm in the Besov space is defined as

\|f\|_{L_{p}(I)}+|f|_{B^{r}_{p,\theta}(I)}
.
The Besov class
B^{r}_{p,\theta}(I)
is the unit ball in the corresponding space.

Remark
.

One can define Besov space on domains
I\neq\mathbb{R}
as the restriction of
functions
f\in B^{r}_{p,\theta}(\mathbb{R})
to
I
. Then the norm of
g

is defined as

\inf\{\|f\|\colon\left.f\right|_{I}=g\}
. For segments
I\subset\mathbb{R}
these
definitions (and corresponding norms) are equivalent, see, e.g.,
[
4
]
, §1.11, Theorem 1.118.

There are embeddings
\mathcal{B}^{r}_{p,\min(p,2)}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{W}^{r}_{p}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{B}^{r}_{p,\max(p,2)}
for

1<p<\infty
and
\mathcal{B}^{r}_{p,1}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{W}^{r}_{p}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{B}^{r}_{p,\infty}
for
p=1
,
p=\infty
. As a corollary,
we have
c_{1}B^{1}_{1,1}[0,1]\subset W^{1}_{1}[0,1]\subset c_{2}B^{1}_{1,\infty}[0,1]
. This gives us motivation to study widths of

B^{1}_{1,\theta}
.

Of course, widths of Besov classes are interesting by themselves.
Let us mention the papers of Ding Dung
[
14
]
, A.S. Romanyuk
[
15
]
,
E.M. Galeev
[
16
]
; see also the survey
[
8
]
, §4.3.
The case
B^{1}_{1,\theta}
in
L_{q}
,
q>2
, as far as author knows, was not
studied before.

Theorem 1
.

For any
q\in(2,\infty)
there exist
c_{q}>0
and
C_{q}>0
, such that
for all
1\leqslant\theta\leqslant\infty
and
n=2,3,\ldots
the inequalities hold:

c_{q}n^{-1/2}\log^{1/2}n\leqslant d_{n}(B^{1}_{1,\theta}[0,1],L_{q}[0,1])\leqslant C_{q}n^{-1/2}\log^{1/2}n,\quad 1\leqslant\theta\leqslant 2,

c_{q}n^{-1/2}\log^{1-1/\theta}n\leqslant d_{n}(B^{1}_{1,\theta}[0,1],L_{q}[0,1])\leqslant C_{q}n^{-1/2}\log^{1-1/\theta}n,\quad 2\leqslant\theta\leqslant\infty.

Unfortunately, this gives us no new information about
W^{1}_{1}
.

The proof is based on the following finite-dimensional result.
Consider a weighted norm in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
:

\|x\|_{\ell_{q,w}^{N}}:=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{N}w_{i}|x_{i}|^{q}\right\}^{1/q}.

Suppose that the set of coordinates is divided into
m
blocks:

\{1,\ldots,N\}=\sqcup_{s=1}^{m}\Delta_{s}
,
|\Delta_{s}|=N_{s}
.
Let us take the octahedron
B_{1}^{N_{s}}
in each block, and consider their
Cartesian product

\prod_{s=1}^{m}B_{1}^{N_{s}}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\colon\sum_{i\in\Delta_{s}}|x_{i}|\leqslant 1,\;s=1,\ldots,m\}.

Theorem 2
.

Let there are weights
w_{1},\ldots,w_{N}\geqslant 0
, division into
m
blocks

\{1,\ldots,N\}=\sqcup_{s=1}^{m}\Delta_{s}
,
N_{s}=|\Delta_{s}|
, and a number

n\in\mathbb{N}
, such that the following conditions hold:

1)

the weights are sufficiently small:
\max\limits_{1\leqslant i\leqslant N}w_{i}\leqslant(4n)^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N}w_{i}
;

2)

the weighted ratio of each block is not small:

\nu_{s}:=\frac{\sum_{i\in\Delta_{s}}w_{i}}{\sum_{i=1}^{N}w_{i}}\geqslant\frac{C\log 2m}{n},\quad s=1,\ldots,m.

where
C
is enough large absolute constant.

Suppose that for
q\in[2,\infty)
and
h>0
the norm in the space

X=(\mathbb{R}^{N},\|\cdot\|)
satisfies

\|x\|_{X}\geqslant\max(\|x\|_{\ell_{{q},w}^{N}},h\|x\|_{\infty}),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Then the Kolmogorov width of the product of octahedra in
X
satisfies the
inequality

d_{n}(\prod_{s=1}^{m}B_{1}^{N_{s}},X)\geqslant\min(c_{q}\,(n\max_{1\leqslant s\leqslant m}\nu_{s})^{-1/2}(\sum_{i=1}^{N}w_{i})^{1/q},h/2).

We note that the condition 2) implies that there are not so many blocks:

Cm\log(2m)\leqslant n
.

Let us mention some corollaries of the Theorem 2. Here and after by intersection

X\cap Y
of two normed spaces we mean the space with the norm

\|x\|_{X\cap Y}:=\max(\|x\|_{X},\|x\|_{Y})
.
If we take
q_{2}\geqslant 1
, then
\|x\|_{\ell_{{q_{2}},w}^{N}}\geqslant\min_{i}w_{i}^{1/q_{2}}\|x\|_{\infty}
.
Suppose that the sum of weights in each block is constant
(hence
\nu_{s}=m^{-1}
), then the bound in the theorem becomes (where we put

q_{1}:=q
for more symmetry):

d_{n}(\prod_{s=1}^{m}B_{1}^{N_{s}},\ell_{{q_{1}},w}^{N}\cap\ell_{{q_{2}},w}^{N})\geqslant\min(c_{q_{1}}\sqrt{\frac{m}{n}}(\sum_{i=1}^{N}w_{i})^{1/q_{1}},\frac{1}{2}\min w_{i}^{1/q_{2}}).

(1)

We will use the bound in the form (
1
) to prove Theorem 1; we shall
take
q_{1}=2
and
q_{2}
equal to
q
from Theorem 1.

If
q_{1}=q_{2}=q\in[2,\infty)
in (
1
) then we get lower bound
for the width of product of octahedra in
\ell_{q,w}^{N}
. Moreover, if all blocks are equal
and
w_{i}=1
, then we get

d_{n}(\prod_{s=1}^{m}B_{1}^{N/m},\ell_{q}^{N})\geqslant\min(c_{q}\sqrt{\frac{m}{n}}N^{1/q},\frac{1}{2}),\quad\mbox{if $Cm\log(2m)\leqslant n\leqslant N/4$}.

For
m=1
this was proven in the paper of B.S. Kashin
[
1
]
, there was also
noted that the bound is sharp:

\frac{1}{4}\min(\frac{N^{1/q}}{\sqrt{n}},1)\leqslant d_{n}(B_{1}^{N},\ell_{q}^{N})\leqslant\min(c_{q}\frac{N^{1/q}}{\sqrt{n}},1),\quad n<\frac{N}{2},\;q\in(2,\infty).

(2)

Our proof of
2
is the development of a method from
[
1
]
.

The product of octahedra had already appeared in the context of widths of
functional classes, see, e.g.
[
13
]
,
[
19
]
.

2
Proofs

We will prove Theorem
2
and then derive Theorem
1
.

Proof.

W.l.o.g.
\|x\|_{X}=\max(\|x\|_{\ell_{{q},w}^{N}},h\|x\|_{\infty})
.
We denote
B:=\prod_{s=1}^{m}B_{1}^{N_{s}}
,

d_{n}:=d_{n}(B,X)
, and let
L_{n}
be the extremal subspace for that width.
If
d_{n}\geqslant h/2
, the proof is done, so we will assume that
d_{n}<h/2
.

We are interested in the approximation of the extreme points of
B
; it is easy to see that

\extr B=\{\sum_{s=1}^{m}\pm e_{i_{s}}\colon i_{s}\in\Delta_{s}\}
. Let us show that
for any
u\in\extr B
there is
v=v(u)\in L_{n}
such that

\|u-v\|_{X}\leqslant 2d_{n},

(3)

u_{i}=v_{i}\quad\forall\,i\in\supp u.

(4)

Fix
u\in\extr B
and take
v_{0}\in L_{n}
, such that
\|u-v_{0}\|_{X}\leqslant d_{n}
.
Write the error
u-v_{0}
as
u_{1}+\beta_{1}
, where

\supp u_{1}\subset\supp u
,
\supp\beta_{1}\cap\supp u=\varnothing
.
We have
\|\beta_{1}\|\leqslant d_{n}
,

\|u_{1}\|_{\infty}\leqslant h^{-1}\|u_{1}\|_{X}\leqslant d_{n}/h\leqslant 1/2,

and the condition on
\supp u_{1}
implies that
u_{1}\in\frac{1}{2}B
. Hence, the
vector
u_{1}
may be approximated by
v_{1}\in L_{n}
with an error
\leqslant\frac{1}{2}d_{n}
.
Further, we write
u_{1}-v_{1}=u_{2}+\beta_{2}
,
\supp u_{2}\subset\supp u
and approximate

u_{2}
. If we repeat this process, we will get the approximation of
u
by the sum

v_{0}+v_{1}+\ldots\in L_{n}
with the error
\|\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}+\ldots\|\leqslant 2d_{n}
.

Let us fix a mapping
v(\cdot)\colon\extr B\to L_{n}
,
which satisfies (
3
) and (
4
).

We can normalize the weights by
\sum_{i=1}^{N}w_{i}=1
.
Let
p_{i}:=2nw_{i}
. Let
\mathsf{\Omega}\subset\{1,\ldots,N\}
be a random subset, such
that any point
i
gets in
\mathsf{\Omega}
independently of others with
probability
p_{i}
. Condition 1) of the Theorem gives us

p_{i}\in[0,1]
(even
p_{i}\leqslant 1/2
; we will use that later). The choice of
the probabilites proportional to the weights allows us to write weighted the norm as the
expectation:

\mathsf{E}\sum_{i\in\mathsf{\Omega}}|x_{i}|^{q}=2n\|x\|_{\ell_{{q},w}^{N}}^{q}.

Also,
\mathsf{E}|\mathsf{\Omega}|=\sum p_{i}=2n
and

\mathsf{E}|\mathsf{\Omega}\cap\Delta_{s}|=2n\nu_{s}
.

Let
\mathsf{u}
be a random point in
\extr B
(equiprobable) independent
of
\mathsf{\Omega}
. In other words, in each block
\Delta_{s}
one
random coordinate of
\mathsf{u}
equals
\pm 1
and others are zero.
Put
\mathsf{v}=v(\mathsf{u})
.
Consider the random variable

\xi:=\sum_{i\in\mathsf{\Omega}}|\mathsf{u}_{i}-\mathsf{v}_{i}|^{q}\cdot\mathbf{1}\{\supp\mathsf{u}\subset\mathsf{\Omega}\}

and the conditional expectation

K:=\mathsf{E}(\sum_{i\in\mathsf{\Omega}}|\mathsf{u}_{i}-\mathsf{v}_{i}|^{q}\,|\,\supp\mathsf{u}\subset\mathsf{\Omega})=\frac{\mathsf{E}\xi}{\mathsf{P}(\supp\mathsf{u}\subset\mathsf{\Omega})}.

The meaning of the condition

\{\supp\mathsf{u}\subset\mathsf{\Omega}\}
is that it allows us to come to a
low-dimensional case:
|\mathsf{\Omega}|\approx\mathsf{E}|\mathsf{\Omega}|=2n
.

We will bound
K
in two ways.
Fix
u^{\circ}
and average over
\mathsf{\Omega}
:

\mathsf{E}(\xi|\mathsf{u}=u^{\circ})=\sum_{i=1}^{N}|u^{\circ}_{i}-v^{\circ}_{i}|^{q}\cdot\mathsf{P}(\supp u^{\circ}\subset\mathsf{\Omega},\;i\in\mathsf{\Omega}).

If
i\in\supp u^{\circ}
, then by (
4
) the corresponding term is zero.
Otherwise, by the independence,

\frac{\mathsf{P}(\supp u^{\circ}\subset\mathsf{\Omega},\;i\in\mathsf{\Omega})}{\mathsf{P}(\supp u^{\circ}\subset\mathsf{\Omega})}=P(i\in\mathsf{\Omega})=2nw_{i}.

We sum over
i
and obtain

\frac{\mathsf{E}(\xi|\mathsf{u}=u^{\circ})}{\mathsf{P}(\supp u^{\circ}\subset\mathsf{\Omega})}=2n\sum_{i=1}^{N}w_{i}|u^{\circ}_{i}-v^{\circ}_{i}|^{q}=2n\|u^{\circ}-v^{\circ}\|_{\ell_{{q},w}^{N}}^{q}\ll nd_{n}^{q}.

Then we multiply by the probability, average over
u^{\circ}
and obtain

\mathsf{E}\xi\ll nd_{n}^{q}\cdot\mathsf{P}(\supp\mathsf{u}\subset\mathsf{\Omega}),\quad K\ll nd_{n}^{q}.

Now we fix
\Omega^{\circ}
and average over
u
:

\mathsf{E}(\xi|\mathsf{\Omega}=\Omega^{\circ})=\mathsf{E}\left(\left.\sum_{i\in\Omega^{\circ}}|\mathsf{u}_{i}-\mathsf{v}_{i}|^{q}\;\right|\supp\mathsf{u}\subset\Omega^{\circ}\right)\cdot\mathsf{P}(\supp\mathsf{u}\subset\Omega^{\circ}).

Let us call a set
\Omega

regular
if the cardinalities of the sets

\Omega_{s}:=\Omega\cap\Delta_{s}
satisfy

\frac{3}{2}n\nu_{s}\leqslant|\Omega_{s}|\leqslant An\nu_{s}
for all
s
; the constant
A

is large enough (see later).

Suppose that
\Omega^{\circ}
is regular. Obviously,
\frac{3}{2}n\leqslant|\Omega^{\circ}|\leqslant An
.
By
\widetilde{\mathsf{u}}
we denote the random vector with the conditional
distribution

\mathsf{Law}(\widetilde{\mathsf{u}})=\mathsf{Law}(\mathsf{u}|\supp\mathsf{u}\subset\Omega^{\circ})
. Let

\widetilde{\mathsf{v}}=v(\widetilde{\mathsf{u}})
. We have

\mathsf{E}\left(\left.\sum_{i\in\Omega^{\circ}}|\mathsf{u}_{i}-\mathsf{v}_{i}|^{q}\;\right|\supp\mathsf{u}\subset\Omega^{\circ}\right)=\mathsf{E}\|\widetilde{\mathsf{u}}-\widetilde{\mathsf{v}}\|_{\ell_{q}(\Omega^{\circ})}^{q}.

We can consider
\widetilde{\mathsf{u}}
as a random vector in

\mathbb{R}^{\Omega^{\circ}}
.
It is easy to see that
\widetilde{\mathsf{u}}
takes values (equiprobable)
in the set
\extr\prod_{s=1}^{m}B_{1}^{|\Omega_{s}^{\circ}|}
; in other words, in each set
\Omega^{\circ}_{s}
one random
coordinate is
\pm 1
and others are zero.
We can pass from
q
-mean to
2
-mean and from

\ell_{q}(\Omega^{\circ})
metric to
\ell_{2}(\Omega^{\circ})

(here we use that
q\geqslant 2
):

(\mathsf{E}\|\widetilde{\mathsf{u}}-\widetilde{\mathsf{v}}\|_{q}^{q})^{1/{q}}\geqslant(\mathsf{E}\|\widetilde{\mathsf{u}}-\widetilde{\mathsf{v}}\|_{q}^{2})^{1/{2}}\gg n^{1/{q}-1/2}(\mathsf{E}\|\widetilde{\mathsf{u}}-\widetilde{\mathsf{v}}\|_{2}^{2})^{1/2}.

The estimate for the approximation in Euclid metric is standard (a variation of
Eckart-Young theorem).

Lemma 1

(See, e.g.,
[
11
]
)
.

Let
\mathsf{z}
be a random vector in Hilbert space
H
with

\mathsf{E}\|z\|_{H}^{2}<\infty
and in some orthonormal basis

\{\varphi_{i}\}
we have
\mathsf{E}\langle\mathsf{z},\varphi_{i}\rangle\langle\mathsf{z},\varphi_{j}\rangle=0
for
i\neq j
.
Let
\sigma_{i}:=(\mathsf{E}\langle\mathsf{z},\varphi_{i}\rangle^{2})^{1/2}
and

\sigma_{1}^{*}\geqslant\sigma_{2}^{*}\geqslant\ldots
be the non-increasing permutation of

(\sigma_{i})
.
Then for any
n
-dimensional subspace
L_{n}\subset H
the inequality holds

\mathsf{E}\rho(\mathsf{z},L_{n})_{H}^{2}\geqslant\sum_{k>n}(\sigma_{k}^{*})^{2},\quad\mbox{where $\rho(x,L_{n})_{H}:=\inf_{y\in L}\|x-y\|_{H}$}.

In our case one can take the standard basis
\{e_{i}\}
, because

\mathsf{E}\widetilde{\mathsf{u}}_{i}\widetilde{\mathsf{u}}_{j}=0
;

\sigma_{i}^{2}=\mathsf{E}\widetilde{\mathsf{u}}_{i}^{2}=|\Omega_{s}^{\circ}|^{-1}\asymp(n\nu_{s})^{-1}
,

i\in\Delta_{s}
. Denote
\nu:=\max\nu_{s}
, then

\mathsf{E}\|\widetilde{\mathsf{u}}-\widetilde{\mathsf{v}}\|_{2}^{2}\geqslant\mathsf{E}\rho(\widetilde{\mathsf{u}},L_{n})_{2}^{2}\geqslant\sum_{k=n+1}^{3n/2}(\sigma_{k}^{*})^{2}\gg\nu^{-1}.

Therefore, for regular
\Omega^{\circ}
we have

\mathsf{E}(\xi|\mathsf{\Omega}=\Omega^{\circ})\gg\mathsf{P}(\supp\mathsf{u}\subset\Omega^{\circ})\cdot n^{1-q/2}\cdot\nu^{-q/2}.

Averaging over
\Omega^{\circ}
we obtain

\mathsf{E}\xi\gg n^{1-q/2}\nu^{-q/2}\cdot\mathsf{P}(\supp\mathsf{u}\subset\mathsf{\Omega},\;\mathsf{\Omega}\mbox{ is regular}).

Later we will show that one can separate conditions in
\mathsf{P}(\cdot)
:

\mathsf{P}(\supp\mathsf{u}\subset\mathsf{\Omega},\;\mathsf{\Omega}\mbox{ is regular})\geqslant\mathsf{P}(\supp\mathsf{u}\subset\mathsf{\Omega})\mathsf{P}(\mathsf{\Omega}\mbox{ is regular}).

(5)

It is easy to check that the probability that
\mathsf{\Omega}
is regular is

\geqslant 1/2
. To do this we apply Bernstein’s inequality:

\mathsf{P}(|\sum_{i=1}^{k}X_{i}|>t)\leqslant 2\exp(-\frac{t^{2}}{2(\sigma^{2}+Mt/3)}),

where
X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}
are independent r.v. with zero average,

M:=\max_{i}\|X_{i}\|_{\infty}
,
\sigma^{2}:=\sum_{i}\mathsf{E}X_{i}^{2}
. Fix
s
,
put

X_{i}=\mathbf{1}\{i\in\mathsf{\Omega}_{s}\}-p_{i}
,
i\in\Delta_{s}
(recall that

\mathsf{\Omega}_{s}:=\mathsf{\Omega}\cap\Delta_{s}
), use that
M\leqslant 1
,
\sigma^{2}\leqslant\sum_{i\in\Delta_{s}}p_{i}=2n\nu_{s}
to obtain

\mathsf{P}(||\mathsf{\Omega}_{s}|-2n\nu_{s}|>n\nu_{s}/2)\leqslant 2\exp(-\frac{(n\nu_{s}/2)^{2}}{2(2n\nu_{s}+n\nu_{s}/6)})\leqslant 2\exp(-cn\nu_{s}).

The condition 2) of the Theorem implies that this probability is less than
\frac{1}{2m}
.
Hence, the probability of irregularity of
\mathsf{\Omega}
is less than
1/2
.
So,

\mathsf{E}\xi\gg n^{1-q/2}\nu^{-q/2}\mathsf{P}(\supp\mathsf{u}\subset\mathsf{\Omega}),\quad K\gg n^{1-q/2}\nu^{-q/2}.

Finally, we compare upper and lower bounds on
K
and obtain the required
bound for
d_{n}
.

It remains to prove (
5
). We will use one technical lemma.
Here we write
\#\Omega
instead of
|\Omega|
for convenience.

Lemma 2
.

Let
\delta\in(0,1)
,
\mathsf{\Omega}
is a random subset of
\{1,\ldots,N\}
, and
each point
i
gets in
\mathsf{\Omega}
independently of others with probability
p_{i}\leqslant 1/2
. If
\omega:=\mathsf{E}\#\mathsf{\Omega}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}p_{i}

is large enough (
\omega\geqslant c(\delta)
), then

\mathsf{E}(\#\mathsf{\Omega}|\mathcal{R})\geqslant\mathsf{E}\#\mathsf{\Omega},\quad\mbox{where $\mathcal{R}:=\{(1-\delta)\omega\leqslant\#\mathsf{\Omega}\leqslant 5\omega\}$}.

Let us derive (
5
) from lemma. It is enough to prove the inequality for each
block (because of the independence):

\mathsf{P}(\supp\mathsf{u}\cap\Delta_{s}\subset\mathsf{\Omega}\cap\Delta_{s}\big|3n\nu_{s}/2\leqslant|\mathsf{\Omega}_{s}|\leqslant 5n\nu_{s})\geqslant\mathsf{P}(\supp\mathsf{u}\cap\Delta_{s}\subset\mathsf{\Omega}\cap\Delta_{s}).

The right side of the inequality is
N_{s}^{-1}\mathsf{E}|\mathsf{\Omega}\cap\Delta_{s}|
, and left
side is equal to the corresponding conditional expectation. It remains to apply the Lemma for

\mathsf{\Omega}\cap\Delta_{s}
.

Let us prove this lemma. The probability space is a union of non-intersecting
events
\mathcal{R}
,
\mathcal{C}_{-}:=\{\#\mathsf{\Omega}|<(1-\delta)\omega\}
and

\mathcal{C}_{+}:=\{\#\mathsf{\Omega}>5\omega\}
.
Denote
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}_{-}\cup\mathcal{C}_{+}
. We have to prove that

\mathsf{E}(\#\mathsf{\Omega}|\mathcal{R})\geqslant\mathsf{E}(\#\mathsf{\Omega}|\mathcal{C})
.
Using Bernstein’s inequality we get

\mathsf{P}(\#\mathsf{\Omega}\in[(1-\delta/3)\omega,(1+\delta/3)\omega])\geqslant 1-2\exp(-c_{\delta}\omega)>9/10

for large
\omega
. This is also true conditioning on
\mathcal{R}
, so

\mathsf{E}(\#\mathsf{\Omega}|\mathcal{R})\geqslant\frac{1}{10}(1-\delta)\omega+\frac{9}{10}(1-\delta/3)\omega>(1-\delta/2)\omega.

Let us bound
\mathsf{E}(\#\mathsf{\Omega}|\mathcal{C})=\mathsf{E}(\#\mathsf{\Omega}|\mathcal{C}_{-})\mathsf{P}(\mathcal{C}_{-}|\mathcal{C})+\mathsf{E}(\#\mathsf{\Omega}|\mathcal{C}_{+})\mathsf{P}(\mathcal{C}_{+}|\mathcal{C})
.

The first term is not more than
\mathsf{E}(\#\mathsf{\Omega}|\mathcal{C}_{-})\leqslant(1-\delta)\omega
.
The second term is equal to

\mathsf{E}(\#\mathsf{\Omega}|\mathcal{C}_{+})\mathsf{P}(\mathcal{C}_{+}|\mathcal{C})=\frac{\mathsf{E}\#\mathsf{\Omega}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{C}_{+}}}{\mathsf{P}(\mathcal{C})}=\frac{1}{\mathsf{P}(\mathcal{C})}\sum_{k>5\omega}k\mathsf{P}(\#\mathsf{\Omega}=k).

Note that
\mathsf{P}(\mathcal{C})\geqslant\mathsf{P}(\mathsf{\Omega}=\varnothing)=\prod(1-p_{i})\geqslant\prod 4^{-p_{i}}=4^{-\omega}
(we use that
p_{i}\leqslant 1/2
), hence the second term is less or equal than

4^{\omega}\sum_{k>5\omega}k\mathsf{P}(\#\mathsf{\Omega}=k)\leqslant 4^{\omega}\sum_{k>5\omega}k\exp(-ck)\leqslant C\omega e^{-\omega}<\frac{\delta}{2}\omega

for large
\omega
.
Summing up, we obtain
\mathsf{E}(\#\mathsf{\Omega}|\mathcal{C})\leqslant(1-\delta/2)\omega<\mathsf{E}(\#\mathsf{\Omega}|\mathcal{R})
.
The lemma and the Theorem are proven.
∎

Before we proceed to the proof of Theorem 1, we will formulate some useful
statements.

It is well known that Besov spaces are isomorphic to some sequence spaces
(Lizorkin, Triebel, Meyer). Let us describe this isomorphism in the case of
spaces on
\mathbb{R}
. Let

\psi
,
\varphi
be a wavelet function and a scaling function, correspondingly.
We will make use of regular Daubechies wavelets with compact support
(see.
[
3
]
, §1.2):
\psi,\varphi\in C^{r_{0}}(\mathbb{R})
and
\int\psi(x)x^{j}\,dx=0
,
0\leqslant j<r_{0}
; with some fixed

r_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
.

Denote
\psi_{k,j}(x):=2^{k/2}\psi(2^{k}x-j)
,

\varphi_{0,j}(x):=\varphi(x-j)
,
k,j\in\mathbb{Z}
.

We consider the space
\ell^{\sigma}_{p,\theta}
of sequences

(\lambda_{k,j})_{k\geqslant 0,j\in\mathbb{Z}}
with the norm

\|(\lambda_{k,j})_{k\geqslant 0,j\in\mathbb{Z}}\|_{\ell^{\sigma}_{p,\theta}}=\begin{cases}\left(\sum_{k\geqslant 0}2^{k\sigma\theta}\|(\lambda_{k,j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\|_{\ell_{p}}^{\theta}\right)^{1/\theta},&\quad\theta<\infty,\\
\sup_{k\geqslant 0}2^{k\sigma}\|(\lambda_{k,j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\|_{\ell_{p}},&\quad\theta=\infty.\end{cases}

Theorem A

(See.
[
3
]
, §1.2, Theorem 1.20)
.

Suppose
0<r<r_{0}
;
p,\theta\in[1,\infty]
. Then
f\in L_{p}(\mathbb{R})
lies in

\mathcal{B}^{r}_{p,\theta}(\mathbb{R})
if and only if the following norm is finite:

\|f\|:=\|(\langle f,\varphi_{0,j}\rangle)_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\|_{\ell_{p}}+\|(\langle f,\psi_{k,j}\rangle)_{k\geqslant 0,j\in\mathbb{Z}}\|_{\ell^{\sigma}_{p,\theta}},\;\sigma:=r+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}.

(6)

This norm is equivalent to the norm of
\mathcal{B}^{r}_{p,\theta}(\mathbb{R})
.

See also
[
7
]
, Th.1.13.

We will use later that Besov classes grow as
\theta
increases
(
B^{1}_{1,\theta_{1}}
is embedded in
B^{1}_{1,\theta_{2}}
as
\theta_{1}<\theta_{2}
), it is easily
seen from this theorem.

We should discretize the norm, too.
Consider the subspace in
L_{p}
:

L_{p}\langle\Psi_{+}\rangle:=\overline{\mathrm{span}}\,\Psi_{+},\quad\Psi_{+}:=\{\psi_{k,j}\colon k\geqslant 0,\,j\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

(the closure is in
L_{p}(\mathbb{R})
).

Lemma 3
.

Let
f\in L_{p}\langle\Psi_{+}\rangle
. Then for
2\leqslant p<\infty
we have

\|(\langle f,\psi_{k,j}\rangle)_{k\geqslant 0,j\in\mathbb{Z}}\|_{\ell^{1/2-1/p}_{p,p}}\ll\|f\|_{p}\ll\|(\langle f,\psi_{k,j}\rangle)_{k\geqslant 0,j\in\mathbb{Z}}\|_{\ell^{1/2-1/p}_{p,2}}.

(7)

We provide the proof for completeness; it is possible that such bounds are
known. Similar inequalities had appeared in the context of Kolmogorov widths
in, e.g.,
[
9
]
for Haar wavelets.

Proof.

We will make use of the following facts about wavelets.

(i)

Wavelets
\{\psi_{k,j}\}
are an unconditional basis
in
L_{p}(\mathbb{R})
, see.
[
2
]
, §8.2;
hence, using
[
2
]
, §7.3, Corrolary 7.11, we have

\|f\|_{p}\asymp\|(\sum_{k,j}\langle f,\psi_{k,j}\rangle^{2}|\psi_{k,j}|^{2})^{1/2}\|_{p}.

(ii)

For fixed
k
, inequality
[
2
]
, §8.1,
Proposition 8.3 gives

\|\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle f,\psi_{k,j}\rangle\psi_{k,j}\|_{p}\asymp 2^{k(1/2-1/p)}\|(\langle f,\psi_{k,j}\rangle)_{j}\|_{\ell_{p}}.

Use (i), the inequality
(a+b)^{r}\geqslant a^{r}+b^{r}
for
r\geqslant 1
,

a,b\geqslant 0
, and the relation
\|\psi_{k,j}\|_{p}\asymp 2^{k(1/2-1/p)}
:

\|f\|_{p}^{p}\asymp\int|\sum_{k\geqslant 0,j\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle f,\psi_{k,j}\rangle^{2}\psi_{k,j}(t)^{2}|^{p/2}\,dt\geqslant\sum_{k\geqslant 0,j\in\mathbb{Z}}\|\langle f,\psi_{k,j}\rangle\psi_{k,j}\|_{p}^{p}\asymp\\
\asymp\sum_{k\geqslant 0}2^{kp(1/2-1/p)}\|(\langle f,\psi_{k,j}\rangle)_{j}\|_{\ell_{p}}^{p}.

It gives us the required lower bound.

For the upper bound we use (i), put
g_{k}=(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle f,\psi_{k,j}\rangle^{2}\psi_{k,j}^{2})^{1/2}
and obtain

\|f\|_{p}^{2}\asymp\|(\sum_{k\geqslant 0}g_{k}^{2})^{1/2}\|_{p}^{2}=\|\sum_{k\geqslant 0}g_{k}^{2}\|_{p/2}\leqslant\sum_{k\geqslant 0}\|g_{k}^{2}\|_{p/2}=\sum_{k\geqslant 0}\|g_{k}\|_{p}^{2}.

Now, using (i) and then (ii),

\|g_{k}\|_{p}=\|(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle f,\psi_{k,j}\rangle^{2}\psi_{k,j}^{2})^{1/2}\|_{p}\asymp\\
\asymp\|\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle f,\psi_{k,j}\rangle\psi_{k,j}\|_{p}\asymp 2^{k(1/2-1/p)}\|(\langle f,\psi_{k,j}\rangle)_{j}\|_{\ell_{p}}.

Therefore,

\|f\|_{p}^{2}\ll\sum_{k\geqslant 0}2^{2k(1/2-1/p)}\|(\langle f,\psi_{k,j}\rangle)_{j}\|_{\ell_{p}}^{2},

as required. Lemma is proved.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1
.

We will use Theorem A and Lemma 3
to discretize our problem and then we will bound widths using Theorem 2.

Discretization.

It is convenient to change coordinates (to get octahedra):

x_{k,j}:=2^{k/2}\langle f,\psi_{k,j}\rangle.

Moreover, as we work with function classes on the segment, we may restrict indices to
the set
T:=\{(k,j)\colon k\geqslant 0,\,j=1,\ldots,2^{k}\}
.
Consider the subspace
L_{q}\langle\Psi_{T}\rangle
, spanned by

\Psi_{T}:=\{\psi_{k,j}\colon(k,j)\in T\}
.
The support of functions from
\Psi_{T}
is contained in some segment

[-a,a]
; this is also true for
L_{q}\langle\Psi_{T}\rangle
.
The natural projector

L_{q}(\mathbb{R})\to L_{q}\langle\Psi_{T}\rangle
is bounded (because wavelets
form a basis), hence

d_{n}(B^{1}_{1,\theta}[-a,a],L_{q}[-a,a])\gg d_{n}(B^{1}_{1,\theta}(\mathbb{R})\cap L_{q}\langle\Psi_{T}\rangle,L_{q}\langle\Psi_{T}\rangle).

(8)

Remark
.

One could use periodic wavelets in
\mathcal{B}^{r}_{p,\theta}(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z})
instead, see
[
3
]
, §1.3, Theorem 1.37; however,
this also brings technical complications.
The most convenient for us would be Haar wavelets, but they do not form an
unconditional basis for
\mathcal{B}^{1}_{1,\theta}
, see
[
17
]
.

We denote by
\ell^{\sigma}_{p,\theta}(T)
the analog of the space

\ell^{\sigma}_{p,\theta}
for vectors
x\in\mathbb{R}^{T}
:

\|x\|_{\ell^{\sigma}_{p,\theta}(T)}:=\begin{cases}\left\{\sum_{k\geqslant 0}2^{k\sigma\theta}\|(x_{k,j})_{1\leqslant j\leqslant 2^{k}}\|_{\ell_{p}}^{\theta}\right\}^{1/\theta},&\quad\theta<\infty,\\
\sup_{k\geqslant 0}2^{k\sigma}\|(x_{k,j})_{1\leqslant j\leqslant 2^{k}}\|_{\ell_{p}},&\quad\theta=\infty,\end{cases}

b^{\sigma}_{p,\theta}(T)
is the unit ball of this space.

Theorem A gives that for
f\in L_{q}\langle\Psi_{T}\rangle
we have

\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{1}_{1,\theta}}\asymp\|\langle f,\psi_{k,j}\rangle\|_{\ell^{1/2}_{1,\theta}(T)}=\|x\|_{\ell^{0}_{1,\theta}(T)},\quad x_{k,j}=2^{k/2}\langle f,\psi_{k,j}\rangle.

(9)

Indeed, the first term in (
6
) is zero as
\psi_{k,j}\perp\varphi_{0,i}
,
k\geqslant 0

(this follows from the definition of wavelets and scaling functions).
So, we have proved that the set
b^{0}_{1,\theta}(T)
is the discretization of

B^{1}_{1,\theta}\cap L_{q}\langle\Psi_{T}\rangle
.
The set
b^{0}_{1,\theta}(T)
is the infinite-dimensional octahedron for

\theta=1
and the cartesian product of octahedra of dimensions

1,2,\ldots,2^{k},\ldots
for
\theta=\infty
.

Let us write the lower bound from (
7
) for
p=q
,
f\in L_{q}\langle\Psi_{T}\rangle
, in the coordinates
x_{k,j}
:

\|f\|_{q}^{q}\gg\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{-k}\sum_{j=1}^{2^{k}}|x_{k,j}|^{q}=\|x\|^{q}_{\ell^{-1/q}_{q,q}(T)}.

(10)

It is important to note that as
q>2
, we have
\|f\|_{q}\geqslant\|f\|_{2}
and hence
\|f\|_{q}\geqslant\|x\|_{\ell^{-1/2}_{2,2}(T)}
.
Using (
8
), (
10
) and (
9
),
we obtain the “lower” discretization:

d_{n}(B^{1}_{1,\theta}[-a,a],L_{q}[-a,a])\gg d_{n}(b^{0}_{1,\theta}(T),\ell^{-1/q}_{q,q}(T)\cap\ell^{-1/2}_{2,2}(T)).

(11)

(The change of segment from
[0,1]
to
[-a,a]
makes no difference.)

The “upper” discretization is analogous.
Pick
f\in B^{1}_{1,\theta}[0,1]
. We can extend
f
from the segment to the
whole line and control it’s norm and support.
To do this, one can extend
f
to

\mathbb{R}
(see the Remark after Besov space definition) and then multiply it by some
“cut function”, see
[
5
]
, Lemma 3.
We obtain
f\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
with support
[-b,b]
. Only finite number of
functions
\varphi_{0,j}
have support intersecting
[-b,b]
.
We subtract from
f
the linear combination of such functions (it is an element
of
n_{0}
-dimensional space for some fixed
n_{0}
) and get a function with
support in larger segment
[-B,B]
, but orthogonal to
\psi_{k,j}
,
k<0

(orthogonality follows from the wavelet definition):

\widetilde{f}:=f-\sum\langle\varphi_{0,j},f\rangle\varphi_{0,j}\in L_{q}\langle\Psi_{+}\rangle,\;\supp\widetilde{f}\subset[-B,B],\;\|\widetilde{f}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{1}_{1,\theta}(\mathbb{R})}\ll\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{1}_{1,\theta}[0,1]}.

Denote

T^{\prime}:=\{(k,j)\colon k\geqslant 0,\,\supp\psi_{k,j}\cap[-B,B]\neq\varnothing\},\quad\Psi_{T^{\prime}}:=\{\psi_{k,j}\colon(k,j)\in T^{\prime}\},

then
\widetilde{f}\in L_{q}\langle\Psi_{T^{\prime}}\rangle
.
Therefore

d_{n+n_{0}}(B^{1}_{1,\theta}[0,1],L_{q}[0,1])\ll d_{n}(B^{1}_{1,\theta}(\mathbb{R})\cap L_{q}\langle\Psi_{T^{\prime}}\rangle,L_{q}).

The inequality (
7
) in the coordinates
x_{k,j}
becomes

\|f\|_{q}^{2}\ll\sum_{k\geqslant 0}\left(2^{-k}\sum_{j=1}^{2^{k}}|x_{k,j}|^{q}\right)^{2/q}=\|x\|^{2}_{\ell^{-1/q}_{q,2}(T)},

and, finally,

d_{n+n_{0}}(B^{1}_{1,\theta}[0,1],L_{q}[0,1])\ll d_{n}(b^{0}_{1,\theta}(T^{\prime}),\ell^{-1/q}_{q,2}(T^{\prime}))\asymp d_{n}(b^{0}_{1,\theta}(T),\ell^{-1/q}_{q,2}(T)).

(12)

Lower bound.

We will bound (

).
Obviously,
\ell^{-1/q}_{q,q}(T)
and

\ell^{-1/2}_{2,2}(T)
are weighted norms with weights

Let us start with the case
\theta=1
; the set
b^{0}_{1,1}(T)

We denote by
k_{0}
and
k_{1}
the minimal and maximal

4n<2^{k}<n^{\tilde{q}/2}

and (
1

•

from

;

k_{0}\leqslant k\leqslant k_{1}

•

Let us check that the conditions of Theorem

The case

In the case

d_{n}(b^{0}_{1,\infty}(T_{m}),\ell^{-1/q}_{q,q}(T_{m})\cap\ell^{-1/2}_{2,2}(T_{m}))\gg n^{-1/2}\log n,\quad m\asymp\log n.

Finally,
\theta\in(2,\infty)

Upper bound.
First we prove that in (
12
) it is enough to
consider
m\asymp\log n
blocks. Denote

. If
x\in b^{0}_{1,\theta}(T)
,
then
\|x[k]\|_{1}\leqslant 1
for all
k
; hence,
\|x[k]\|_{q}\leqslant 1
,

\sum_{k\geqslant m}(2^{-k}\|x[k]\|_{q}^{q})^{2/q}\ll 2^{-2m/q}.

For
m\geqslant C\log n
this “tail” is less than
n^{-C_{1}}
; it has no
effect on the width for large
C_{1}
. So, it is enought to bound

d_{n}(b^{0}_{1,\theta}(T_{m}),\ell^{-1/q}_{q,2}(T_{m}))

m\asymp\log n

We start with the case
\theta=2

\leqslant n/2

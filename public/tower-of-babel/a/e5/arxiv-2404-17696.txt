New second-order optimality conditions for directional optimality of a general set-constrained optimization problem Thanks: The alphabetical order of the paper indicates the equal contribution to the paper.
Wei Ouyang, Jane J. Ye, Binbin Zhang

Abstract
In this paper we derive new second-order optimality conditions for a very general set-constrained optimization problem where the underlying set may be nononvex. We consider local optimality in specific directions in pursuit of developing these new optimality conditions. Utilizing the classical and/or the lower generalized support function, we obtain new second-order necessary and sufficient conditions for local optimality of general nonconvex constrained optimization problems in given directions via both the corresponding asymptotic second-order tangent cone and outer second-order tangent set. Our results do not require convexity and/or nonemptiness of the outer second-order tangent set. This is an important improvement to other results in the literature since the outer second-order tangent set can be nonconvex and empty even when the set is convex. Key words. first-order and second-order optimality condition, directional limiting normal cone, outer second-order tangent set, asymptotic second-order tangent cone, support function, lower generalized support function AMS subject classification: 90C26, 90C46, 49J52, 49J53.

Introduction

In this paper, we consider the general set-constrained optimization problem in the form

\min f(x)\,\,\,{\rm s.t.}\,\,\,g(x)\in K,

(1)

where
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
K
is a closed subset of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Throughout the paper we assume that
f,g
are twice continuously differentiable unless otherwise specified. For convenience, we denote by
C:=g^{-1}(K)
the feasible region of problem (
1
),
T_{K}(\cdot)
the tangent cone to
K
and
C(\bar{x}):=\{d\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\nabla g(\bar{x})d\in T_{K}(g(\bar{x})),\nabla f(\bar{x})d\leq 0\}
the critical cone at a feasible solution
\bar{x}
. In fact, the results of this paper can be suitably modified to include the more general case where the image space is a finite dimensional Hilbert space as in Guo et al.
[
16
]
.

When
K
is convex, problem (
1
) includes the classical optimization problem classes such as the nonlinear program, the nonlinear conic program (when
K
is a convex cone), the second-order cone program and the semidefinite cone program as special cases. The possibility of
K
being nonconvex in (
1
) extends the problem (
1
) to include much more general problems and provides a generic framework for a number of important optimization problem classes such as the mathematical program with equilibrium constraints
[
18
]
, the mathematical program with second-order cone complementarity constraints (Ye and Zhou
[
25
,
26
]
) and the mathematical program with semidefinite cone complementarity constraints (Ding et al.
[
6
]
).

In the case of a nonlinear program (e.g. when
K=\mathbb{R}_{-}^{m}
), recall that if
\bar{x}
is a local optimal solution of problem (
1
), then under certain constraint qualification (e.g. Mangasarian-Fromovitz constraint qualification), there is a multiplier
\lambda
fullfilling the first order necessary optimality condition such that the second order necessary optimality condition holds:

\nabla^{2}_{xx}L(\bar{x},\lambda)(d,d)\geq 0\qquad\forall d\in C(\bar{x}),

(2)

where
L(x,\lambda):=f(x)+\langle\lambda,g(x)\rangle
denotes the Lagrangian. Conversely, if (
2
) holds with strict inequality “
>
” for all nonzero critical direction
d\in C(\bar{x})\setminus\{0\}
, for certain feasible solution
\bar{x}
, a certain multiplier
\lambda
, then
\bar{x}
must be a locally optimal solution. This kind of second-order necessary and sufficient optimality condition is of “no gap” meaning that the only difference between the sufficent and the necessary optimality condition is that
the inequality “
\geq
” in the necessary optimality condition is changed to the strict inequality in the sufficient condition.

Recently, second-order variational analysis and optimality conditions for problem in the form (
1
) have been developed rapidly; see
[
2
,
3
,
7
,
11
,
12
,
14
]
and the references therein.
For problem (
1
) with

being convex, there is complete theory of optimality conditions in the existing literature (see Bonnans et al.
[
4
]
and the monograph of Bonnans and Shapiro
[
5
]
). The second-order necessary optimality conditions are derived within the framework of convex analysis with the following form (cf.
[
5
, Theorem 3.45]
): If the Robinson constraint qualification (Robinson CQ) holds at a local minimizer
\bar{x}
, then for every critical direction
d\in C(\bar{x})
and every convex subset
K(d)
of the second-order tangent set
T_{K}^{2}(g(\bar{x});\nabla g(\bar{x})d)
, there is a multiplier
\lambda
fulfilling the first-order necessary optimality condition such that

\nabla^{2}_{xx}L(\bar{x},\lambda)(d,d)-\sigma_{K(d)}(\lambda)\geq 0,

(3)

where
\sigma_{K(d)}(\cdot)
represents the classic support function to set
{K(d)}
.
In particular if
K
and
T_{K}^{2}(g(\bar{x});\nabla g(\bar{x})d)
are both convex, then
K(d)
in (
3
) can be taken as
T_{K}^{2}(g(\bar{x});\nabla g(\bar{x})d)
and (
3
) becomes the following condition

\nabla^{2}_{xx}L(\bar{x},\lambda)(d,d)-\sigma_{T_{K}^{2}(g(\bar{x});\nabla g(\bar{x})d)}(\lambda)\geq 0.

(4)

Unfortunately, however, the second-order tangent set of a convex set is generally not convex and hence even when
K
is convex, (
4
) may not hold as a second-order necessary optimality condition.

For the general case where
K
may be nonconvex, Gfrerer et al.
[
14
, Theorem 2]
has obtained the following second-order necessary optimality condition: Let
\bar{x}
be a local minimum. If the directional metric subregularity constraint qualification (MSCQ), which is weaker than Robinson CQ, holds at
\bar{x}
in direction
d
with

d\in C(\bar{x})
and
T_{K}^{2}(g(\bar{x});\nabla g(\bar{x})d)\not=\emptyset
, then there exists a Mordukhovich (M-) multiplier
\lambda
in direction
d
such that

\nabla^{2}_{xx}L(\bar{x},\lambda)(d,d)-\hat{\sigma}_{T_{K}^{2}(g(\bar{x});\nabla g(\bar{x})d)}(\lambda)\geq 0,

(5)

where
\hat{\sigma}_{C}(\cdot)
is the so-called lower generalized support function (see Definition
4.5
). The function
\hat{\sigma}_{C}
is indeed an extension of the classic support function and it is “lower” than the support function since it has property that

\hat{\sigma}_{C}\leq\sigma_{C}
, and the equality holds when

is closed and conex. Hence (
5
) is a weaker condition than (
4
). In general if
K
is nonconvex we can only obtain the weaker condition (
5
). However it was shown in
[
5

and Gfrerer et al.
[
14

that the stronger condition (
4

K
is nonconvex if the multipliers considered are unique.
Under the assumption that the set
K
is outer second-order regular and the second-order tangent set

T_{K}^{2}(g(\bar{x});\nabla g(\bar{x})d)
is convex, by the classical result
[
5

and
[
14

, the second order necessary optimality condition (
4
) becomes the following “no gap” sufficient optimality condition: Let
\bar{x}
be a feasible solution of problem (
1
). If for each nonzero critical direction
d
there is
\lambda
fullfiling the first order necessary optimality condition such that

\nabla_{xx}^{2}L(\bar{x},\lambda)(d,d)-\sigma_{T^{2}_{K}(g(\bar{x});\nabla g({\bar{x}})d)}(\lambda)>0,

(6)

then
\bar{x}
is a local optimal solution of problem (
1
). Recently sufficient optimality conditions with the second-order subderivative of the indicator function
\delta_{K}
are developed first in Mohammadi et al.
[
19
, Proposition 7.3]
, under the convexity and the parabolical derivability of set
K
and with these assumptions removed in
[
2
, Theorem 3.3]
from which we obtain a second-order sufficient optimality condition as follows: Let
\bar{x}
be a feasible solution. Suppose that for every nonzero critical direction
d
there exists
\lambda
fullfiling the first order necessary optimality condition such that

\nabla^{2}_{xx}L(\bar{x},\lambda)(d,d)+d^{2}\delta_{K}(g(\bar{x});\lambda)(\nabla g(\bar{x})d)>0.

(7)

Then
\bar{x}
is a local minimizer.

From our discussions so far, we see that the second-order optimality conditions rely on the three second-order objects
\hat{\sigma}_{T^{2}_{K}(g(\bar{x});\nabla g(\bar{x})d))}(\lambda)
,
\sigma_{T^{2}_{K}(g(\bar{x});\nabla g(\bar{x})d))}(\lambda)
and
{\rm d}^{2}\delta_{K}(g(\bar{x});\lambda)(\nabla g(\bar{x})d))
, each of them describing in some way the curvature of the set
K
. It is known by
[
3
, Proposition 2.18]
that these three second-order objectives are linked together by the inequalities

{{\rm d}^{2}\delta_{K}(g(\bar{x});\lambda)(\nabla g(\bar{x})d)\leq-\sigma_{T^{2}_{K}(g(\bar{x});\nabla g(\bar{x})d)}(\lambda)\leq-\hat{\sigma}_{T^{2}_{K}(g(\bar{x});\nabla g(\bar{x})d)}(\lambda).}

(8)

Since the equalities in general do not hold, (
7
) cannot be a “no gap” sufficient condition of the necessary condition (
5
).

For an empty set, by convention
\sigma_{\emptyset}(\lambda):=-\infty
and by definition
\hat{\sigma}_{\emptyset}(\lambda):=-\infty
as well. Hence when

T_{K}^{2}(g(\bar{x});\nabla g(\bar{x})d)=\emptyset
, conditions
(
3
), (
4
) and (
5
) hold trivially. This means that if
T_{K}^{2}(g(\bar{x});\nabla g(\bar{x})d)=\emptyset
, conditions (
3
),
(
4
) and (
5
) do not provide any useful information. Moreover the outer second-order regularity of
K
ensures that nonemptiness of the set
T_{K}^{2}(g(\bar{x});\nabla g(\bar{x})d)
(see
[
5
, Page 202]
).

In this paper we aim at deriving second-order optimality conditions which do not require convexity of set
K
, the outer second-order regularity of set
K
and the nonemptiness of the second-order tangent set

T_{K}^{2}(g(\bar{x});\nabla g(\bar{x})d)
. Moreover we wish to derive second-order necessary conditions that are as close to the corresponding second-order sufficient condition as possible. In
[
22
]
, Penot introduced the notion of asymptotic second-order tangent cone
T_{K}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(g(\bar{x});\nabla g(\bar{x})d)
and used it in the study of second-order optimality conditions for optimization problems with an abstract set constraint (i.e., the case when the constraint is
x\in K
). He observed the following important fact. The set of nonzero elements in asymptotic second-order tangent cone
T_{K}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(g(\bar{x});\nabla g(\bar{x})d)
and the second-order tangent set
T_{K}^{2}(g(\bar{x});\nabla g(\bar{x})d)
cannot be empty simultaneously and had used both these sets in the second-order optimality conditions. Taking further Penot’s idea, in this paper we obtain second-order optimality conditions using both the second-order tangent set and the asymptotic second-order tangent cone and hence no longer require the nonemptiness of the set

T_{K}^{2}(g(\bar{x});\nabla g(\bar{x})d)
in our optimality conditions.

We observe that the following relationships always hold:

\displaystyle\Omega

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\nabla g(\bar{x})(T_{C}^{2}(\bar{x};d))+\nabla^{2}g(\bar{x})(d,d)\subseteq T_{K}^{2}(g(\bar{x});\nabla g(\bar{x})d),

(9)

\displaystyle\Theta

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\nabla g(\bar{x})(T_{C}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\bar{x};d))\subseteq T_{K}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(g(\bar{x});\nabla g(\bar{x})d),

(10)

and the strict inclusions may hold (see Proposition
2.7
and Example
4.3
).
We succeed in showing that (see Theorem
4.2
), if problem (
1
) attains its local minimum at
\bar{x}

and there is no descent direction, then for any
\lambda
with
\nabla_{x}L(\bar{x},\lambda)=0
and any critical direction
d
, the following conditions hold:

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}_{xx}L(\bar{x},\lambda)(d,d)-\sigma_{\Omega}(\lambda)\geq 0\mbox{ and }\sigma_{\Theta}(\lambda)\leq 0.

(11)

Moreover by Theorem
4.14
, we can obtain the following “no gap” sufficient optimality condition of the necessary optimality condition (
11
). Let
\bar{x}
be a feasible solution. Suppose there is
\lambda
with
\nabla_{x}L(\bar{x},\lambda)=0
and there is no descent direction. Suppose for all nonzero critical directions
d
the following conditions hold:

\nabla^{2}_{xx}L(\bar{x},\lambda)(d,d)-\sigma_{\Omega}(\lambda)>0\ \ \text{and}\ \ \sigma_{\Theta\backslash\{0\}}(\lambda)<0.

(12)

Then
\bar{x}
is a local optimal solution.

Since the objects
\Omega
and
\Theta
are implicit, conditions (
11
) and (
12
) themselves are of limited practical use.
However, by Proposition
4.6
, if
\bar{x}
is a local minimum of problem (
1
) and the directional MSCQ holds at
\bar{x}
in a critical direction
d
, then we have the following relationships:

(i)

There exists an M-multiplier
\lambda
in direction
d
such that

\hat{\sigma}_{{T^{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{K}(g(\bar{x});\nabla g(\bar{x})d)}}(\lambda)\leq\sigma_{{\Theta}}(\lambda).

(ii)

If
T^{2}_{K}(g(\bar{x});\nabla g(\bar{x})d)\not=\emptyset
, then there exists an M-multiplier
\lambda
in direction

\hat{\sigma}_{{T^{2}_{K}(g(\bar{x});\nabla g(\bar{x})d)}}(\lambda)\leq\sigma_{\Omega}(\lambda).

These relationships together with conditions (

.
Indeed we have improved Gfrerer et al.

There exists an M-multiplier

is a local optimal solution of problem (

).
Note that unlike in the classical result
[

and
[

, in our new second-order sufficient optimality condition,

is not required to be outer second-order regular and

is not required to be convex.

In practical applications, it may be useful to study local minima or maxima in certain directions.
For example in the exact line search of a gradient method, to find a step size, one may minimize the objective function in a certain descent direction. Sometimes, information on a local minimum along a certain direction may have information related to the non-directional local minimum. For example, if we know a point is not a local minimum along a non-descent direction then it cannot be a local minimum in the classic sense as well. To this end, we introduce in this paper the notion of local minima in a certain direction. For a given reference point
\bar{x}
, if a local minimum value is obtained in each critical direction, then it reduces to the notion of local minimum in the classic sense.

Unlike the directional necessary and/or sufficient conditions obtained for local optimality (along all directions of a reference point) in the existing literature, in this paper, we develop new optimality conditions for problem (
1
) to obtain a local minimum value in a certain direction.

The main contributions of this paper are as follows.

•

We introduce the concept of a local optimal solution in a given direction and derive first-order and second-order optimality conditions for a directional local optimum. This provides a more flexible framework since one can derive the corresponding first-order and second-order optimality conditions for a standard local optimum from a directional local optimum if the given directions are the set of all critical directions.

•

Unlike classical second-order optimality conditions, our new second-order optimality condition for local optimality of nonconvex problem (
1
) in given directions exhibits the exact correspondence between the direction of local optimality and the direction in optimality conditions.

•

We obtain second-order optimality conditions without additional restrictions (such as convexity of set
K
, outer second-order regularity on the set
K
, nonemptiness and/or of the second-order tangent set

T_{K}^{2}(g(\bar{x});\nabla g(\bar{x})d)
etc.). Moreover our new second-order sufficient optimality condition (
12
) is of “no gap” to its corresponding second-order necessary optimality condition (
11
).

We organize our paper as follows. Section 2 contains the basic notations and preliminary results.
In Section 3, we derive new first- and second-order necessary optimality conditions for nonconvex optimization problem with an abstract set constraint in given directions. In Section 4, we develop second-order optimality conditions for general nonconvex optimization problem (
1
) in given directions.

2.1
Tangent and Normal Cones

For a closed subset
S
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a point
\bar{x}\in S
, the regular/Clarke and Bouligand-Severi tangent/contingent cone to
S
at
\bar{x}
is defined, respectively, by

\hat{T}_{S}(\bar{x}):=\liminf\limits_{t\downarrow 0,x\xrightarrow{S}\bar{x}}\frac{S-x}{t}=\{d\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\forall t_{k}\downarrow 0,x_{k}\xrightarrow{S}\bar{x},\exists d_{k}\rightarrow d\,\,{\rm with}\,\,x_{k}+t_{k}d_{k}\in S\}

and

T_{S}(\bar{x}):=\limsup\limits_{t\downarrow 0}\frac{S-\bar{x}}{t}=\{d\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\exists t_{k}\downarrow 0,d_{k}\rightarrow d\,\,{\rm with}\,\,\bar{x}+t_{k}d_{k}\in S\},

where
x\xrightarrow{S}\bar{x}
represents the convergence of
x
to
\bar{x}
with
x\in S
.
The Fréchet normal cone of
S
to
\bar{x}
is defined by

\hat{N}_{S}(\bar{x}):=\left\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\limsup_{x\xrightarrow{S}\bar{x}}\frac{\langle v,x-\bar{x}\rangle}{\|x-\bar{x}\|}\leq 0\right\}.

It is well known that
\hat{N}_{S}(\bar{x})=(T_{S}(\bar{x}))^{\circ}
.

Let
N_{S}(\bar{x})
denote the limiting/Mordukhovich/basic normal cone of
S
at
\bar{x}
, that is,

N_{S}(\bar{x}):=\limsup_{x\xrightarrow{S}\bar{x}}{\hat{N}_{S}(x)}.

By convention for any
\bar{x}\not\in S
,
N_{S}(\bar{x})
is defined as the empty set.
The limiting normal cone is, in general, nonconvex, whereas the Fréchet normal cone is always convex (see,
e.g., Mordukhovich
[
20
, Definition 1.1]
and Rockafellar and Wets
[
24
, Proposition 6.5]
). In the case of
S
being convex, the Fréchet and limiting normal cones coincide with the normal cone in the sense of convex analysis, that is,

N_{S}(\bar{x}):=\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\langle v,x-\bar{x}\rangle\leq 0\ \text{for\ all}\ x\in S\}.

The directional limiting normal cone was introduced in Ginchev and Mordukhovich
[
15
]
and was extended to general Banach spaces in Gfrerer
[
8
]
. For a given direction
d\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the limiting normal cone to
S
in direction
d
at
\bar{x}
is defined by

\displaystyle{N_{S}(\bar{x};d):=\limsup_{t\downarrow 0,d^{\prime}\rightarrow d}\hat{N}_{S}(\bar{x}+td^{\prime}).}

From the definition, it is easy to observe that
N_{S}(\bar{x};d)=\emptyset
if
d\not\in T_{S}(\bar{x})
,
N_{S}(\bar{x};d)\subseteq N_{S}(\bar{x})
and
N_{S}(\bar{x};0)=N_{S}(\bar{x})
. When
S
is convex and
d\in T_{S}(\bar{x})
, we have the following relationship for directional normal cone:

N_{S}(\bar{x};d)=N_{S}(\bar{x})\cap\{d\}^{\bot}=N_{T_{S}(\bar{x})}(d).

(13)

For more details, please refer to Gfrerer
[
9
, Lemma 2.1]
.

Recently, the directional regular/Clarke tangent cone and directional Clarke normal cone were introduced by Gfrerer et al.
[
14
]
. Let
\hat{T}_{S}(\bar{x};d)
denote the directional regular/Clarke tangent cone of
S
at
\bar{x}
in direction
d
, that is,

\displaystyle\hat{T}_{S}(\bar{x};d)

\displaystyle:=\liminf_{t\downarrow 0,d^{\prime}\rightarrow d,\bar{x}+td^{\prime}\in S}T_{S}(\bar{x}+td^{\prime}).

Let
N^{c}_{S}(\bar{x};d):={\rm cl}\ {\rm co}\ (N_{S}(\bar{x};d))
denote the directional Clarke normal cone of
S
at
\bar{x}
in direction
d
.
It is easy to see from the definition that, for a closed set S, the directional version of the regular tangent cone
contains the nondirectional one, and it coincides with the nondirectional one when the direction is equal to zero,
that is,
\hat{T}_{S}(\bar{x})\subset\hat{T}_{S}(\bar{x};d)
and
\hat{T}_{S}(\bar{x})=\hat{T}_{S}(\bar{x};0)
. It is shown in
[
14
, Proposition 3]
that,
\hat{T}_{S}(\bar{x};d)
is closed and convex and
(\hat{T}_{S}(\bar{x};d))^{\circ}=N^{c}_{S}(\bar{x};d)
.

For a proper lower semi-continuous function
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
, let
{\rm dom}(\varphi)
and
{\rm epi}(\varphi)
denote the domain and the epigraph of
\varphi
, respectively, that is,

{\rm dom}(\varphi):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\,\,\varphi(x)<+\infty\}\,\,{\rm and}\,\,{\rm epi}(\varphi):=\{(x,r)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}\mid\varphi(x)\leq r\}.

Let
\hat{\partial}\varphi(x)
and
\partial\varphi(x)
denote the Fréchet and the limiting/Mordukhovich subdifferential of
\varphi
at
x
, respectively, that is

\hat{\partial}\varphi(x):=\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid(v,-1)\in\hat{N}_{{\rm epi}(\varphi)}(x,\varphi(x))\},\quad\forall x\in{\rm dom}(\varphi)

and

\partial\varphi(x):=\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid(v,-1)\in N_{{\rm epi}(\varphi)}(x,\varphi(x))\},\quad\forall x\in{\rm dom}(\varphi).

Recall that the indicator function of
S
is defined as

\delta_{S}(x):=\left\{\begin{matrix}0,&{if\,x\in S},\\
+\infty,&{otherwise}.\end{matrix}\right.

It is well known that

\hat{N}_{S}(x)=\hat{\partial}\delta_{S}(x)\,\,{\rm and}\,\,N_{S}(x)=\partial\delta_{S}(x),\quad\forall x\in S.

Now we are ready to review two kinds of second-order tangent sets which play a fundamental role in the second-order analysis later on.

Definition 2.1

(Second-Order Tangent Sets
[
5
,
22
]
)
.

Let
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\bar{x}\in S
and
d\in T_{S}(\bar{x})
be given.

(i) The (outer) second-order tangent set to
S
at
\bar{x}
in direction
d
is defined by

T_{S}^{2}(\bar{x};d):=\{w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\exists t_{k}\downarrow 0,w_{k}\rightarrow w\,\,{\rm such\,that}\,\,\bar{x}+t_{k}d+\frac{1}{2}t_{k}^{2}w_{k}\in S\}.

(ii) The asymptotic second-order tangent cone to
S
at
\bar{x}
in direction
d
is defined by

\displaystyle T_{S}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\bar{x};d):=

\displaystyle\{w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\exists(t_{k},r_{k})\downarrow(0,0),w_{k}\rightarrow w\,\,{\rm such\,that}

\displaystyle t_{k}/r_{k}\rightarrow 0,\bar{x}+t_{k}d+\frac{1}{2}t_{k}r_{k}w_{k}\in S\}.

It is clear from the above definitions that both sets
T_{S}^{2}(\bar{x};d)
and
T_{S}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\bar{x};d)
are closed. The asymptotic second-order tangent cone was first introduced by Penot
[
22
]
in the study of optimality conditions for scalar optimization, the original definition of which is slightly different while being equivalent to the one in Definition(
2.1
)(ii). It is known that unlike the first-order tangent cone which is always a nonempty cone, the second-order tangent set may not be a cone and it may be empty (see, e.g., Bonnans and Shapiro
[
5
, Example 3.29]
). However it can be easily verified that
T_{S}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\bar{x};d)
is indeed a cone.

In general, both
T_{S}^{2}(\bar{x};d)
and
T_{S}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\bar{x};d)
are contained in

{\rm cl}([{\rm cone}[{\rm cone}(S-\bar{x})-d]])
and when
S
is convex,
T_{S}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\bar{x};d)={{\rm cl}([{\rm cone}[{\rm cone}(S-\bar{x})-d]])}
and
T_{S}^{2}(\bar{x};d)\subseteq T_{S}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\bar{x};d)
, which indicates that the asymptotic second-order tangent cone may be larger than the second-order tangent set. For more details, please refer to
[
17
]
and the references therein.

In
[
22
, Proposition 2.1]
, it was stated that for any
d\in T_{S}(\bar{x})
, the sets
T_{S}^{2}(\bar{x};d)
and
T_{S}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\bar{x};d)
cannot be empty simultaneously. However, the set
T_{S}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\bar{x};d)
is a cone and, hence, it
cannot be empty since
0\in T_{S}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\bar{x};d)
always. Indeed, a slight modification of the proof of
[
22
, Proposition 2.1]
shows that the sets
T_{S}^{2}(\bar{x};d)
and
T_{S}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\bar{x};d)\setminus\{0\}
cannot be empty simultaneously. For completeness we revise the statements in
[
22
, Proposition 2.1]
and provide a proof here.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a closed set,
\bar{x}\in S
and
d\in T_{S}(\bar{x})
. Then,

T_{S}^{2}(\bar{x};d)\cup(T_{S}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\bar{x};d)\backslash\{0\})\neq\emptyset.

Proof.
If
d=0
, one has
T_{S}^{2}(\bar{x};d)=T_{S}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\bar{x};d)=T_{S}(\bar{x})
and the statement is obviously true. We now assume
d\not=0
and

T_{S}^{2}(\bar{x};d)=\emptyset
. Since
d\in T_{S}(\bar{x})
, there exist
t_{k}\downarrow 0,d_{k}\rightarrow d
such that

\bar{x}+t_{k}d_{k}\in S
.
Let
x_{k}:=\bar{x}+t_{k}d_{k}
,
\tilde{t}_{k}:=\frac{t_{k}\|d_{k}\|}{\|d\|}
and
v_{k}:=\frac{x_{k}-\bar{x}-\tilde{t}_{k}d}{\frac{1}{2}\tilde{t}_{k}^{2}}
. Clearly,
\tilde{t}_{k}\downarrow 0
.
If
\{v_{k}\}
is bounded, without loss of generality, we may assume that
v_{k}\rightarrow v
.
Since
x_{k}=\bar{x}+\tilde{t}_{k}d+\frac{1}{2}\tilde{t}_{k}^{2}v_{k}\in S
, we have
v\in T_{S}^{2}(\bar{x};d)
, which is a contradiction to our assumption that
T_{S}^{2}(\bar{x};d)=\emptyset
.
Therefore,
\{v_{k}\}
is unbounded.
Let
w_{k}:=\frac{v_{k}}{\|v_{k}\|}
. Without loss of generality, assume that
w_{k}\rightarrow w
, where
\|w\|=1
.
Let
r_{k}:=\tilde{t}_{k}\|v_{k}\|
. Then one has
\frac{\tilde{t}_{k}}{r_{k}}=\frac{1}{\|v_{k}\|}\rightarrow 0
,
r_{k}\downarrow 0

(according to the definition of
v_{k}
and
\frac{x_{k}-\bar{x}}{\tilde{t}_{k}}\rightarrow d
),
and

x_{k}=\bar{x}+\tilde{t}_{k}d+\frac{1}{2}\tilde{t}_{k}^{2}v_{k}=\bar{x}+\tilde{t}_{k}d+\frac{1}{2}\tilde{t}_{k}r_{k}w_{k}\in S.

This show that
w\in T_{S}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\bar{x};d)
. Since
w\not=0
, the proof is completed.

It is easy to obtain the following results from definition.
Given
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n},\bar{x}\in S
and
d\in T_{S}(\bar{x})
. Then we have

T_{S}^{2}(\bar{x};td)=t^{2}T_{S}^{2}(\bar{x};d)\ \ \text{and}\ \ T_{S}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\bar{x};td)={T_{S}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\bar{x};d)}

(14)

hold for all positive
t
.

We now derive some properties of first- and second-order tangent sets, which will be useful later on.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a closed set,
\bar{x}\in S
and
d\in T_{S}(\bar{x})
. Then,

T_{S}^{2}(\bar{x};d)+\hat{T}_{S}(\bar{x};d)=T_{S}^{2}(\bar{x};d)\ \ \text{and}\ \ T_{S}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\bar{x};d)+\hat{T}_{S}(\bar{x};d)=T_{S}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\bar{x};d).

Proof.
The first equality is clear from
[
14
, Proposition 1]
. It suffices to show that the second equality holds. The inclusion
\supseteq
in second equation is trivial because
0\in\hat{T}_{S}(\bar{x};d)
. For the converse inclusion
\subseteq
, consider
w\in T_{S}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\bar{x};d)
and
v\in\hat{T}_{S}(\bar{x};d)
, and we prove that
w+v\in T_{S}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\bar{x};d)
by contradiction. Assume to the contrary that
w+v\not\in T_{S}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\bar{x};d)
.
By definition, there exists
\epsilon>0
and sequences
(t_{k},r_{k})\downarrow(0,0),w_{k}\rightarrow w
such that

t_{k}/r_{k}\rightarrow 0
with
\bar{x}+t_{k}d+\frac{1}{2}t_{k}r_{k}w_{k}\in S

and

d\left(\bar{x}+t_{k}d+\frac{1}{2}t_{k}r_{k}(w_{k}+v),S\right)>\frac{1}{2}t_{k}r_{k}\epsilon

for sufficiently large
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Denote by
x_{k}:=\bar{x}+t_{k}d+\frac{1}{2}t_{k}r_{k}w_{k}
and
\tau_{k}:=\frac{1}{2}t_{k}r_{k}
, the preceding inequality is equivalent to saying that

(x_{k}+\tau_{k}B_{\epsilon}(v))\cap S=\emptyset
. Then following the lines of the arguments in
[
14
, Proposition 1]
, we deduce that
v\not\in\hat{T}_{S}(\bar{x};d)
, which is a contradiction.

The following estimation on limiting normal cone to second-order tangent sets will be used in our development of directional second-order optimality conditions.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
S
be a closed subset in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\bar{x}\in S
and
d\in T_{S}(\bar{x})
. Then,

N_{T_{S}^{2}(\bar{x};d)}(w)\subseteq N_{S}(\bar{x};d),\ \forall w\in T_{S}^{2}(\bar{x};d),\mbox{ and }N_{T_{S}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\bar{x};d)}(w)\subseteq N_{S}(\bar{x};d),\ \forall w\in T_{S}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\bar{x};d).

Proof.
The first inclusion follows from
[
14
, Lemma 3]
. It suffices to show that the second inclusion holds. If
T_{S}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\bar{x};d)=\emptyset
, the assertion is trivial. Otherwise, we first claim that

\hat{N}_{T_{S}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\bar{x};d)}(w)\subseteq N_{S}(\bar{x};d),\quad\forall w\in T_{S}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\bar{x};d).

(15)

To this end, we pick any
w\in T_{S}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\bar{x};d)
, which indicates the existence of sequences
(t_{k},r_{k})\downarrow(0,0),w_{k}\rightarrow w
such that

t_{k}/r_{k}\rightarrow 0
with
\bar{x}+t_{k}d+\frac{1}{2}t_{k}r_{k}w_{k}\in S
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let
T_{k}:=\{w^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\bar{x}+t_{k}d+\frac{1}{2}t_{k}r_{k}w^{\prime}\in S\}
. It is easy to observe that
T_{k}
is closed,
w_{k}\in T_{k}
and
w\in\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}T_{k}\subset T_{S}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\bar{x};d)
. Then,

\hat{N}_{T_{S}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\bar{x};d)}(w)\subseteq\hat{N}_{\limsup\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}T_{k}}(w)\subseteq N_{\limsup\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}T_{k}}(w).

For any
v\in\hat{N}_{T_{S}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\bar{x};d)}(w)
, one has
v\in N_{\limsup\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}T_{k}}(w)
.
It follows from Rockafellar and Wets
[
24
, Exercise 6.18]
that there exist
w_{k}^{\prime}\in T_{k}
and
v_{k}\in\hat{N}_{T_{k}}(w_{k}^{\prime})
, such that

w_{k}^{\prime}\rightarrow w
and
v_{k}\rightarrow v
(taking subsequences if necessary). Since
v_{k}\in\hat{N}_{T_{k}}(w_{k}^{\prime})=\hat{N}_{S}(\bar{x}+t_{k}d+\frac{1}{2}t_{k}r_{k}w_{k}^{\prime})
,
we have
v\in N_{S}(\bar{x};d)
. Hence (
15
) holds.
Since
N_{S}(\bar{x};d)
is closed, it follows from (
15
) that
N_{T_{S}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\bar{x};d)}(w)=\limsup\limits_{w^{\prime}\rightarrow w}\hat{N}_{T_{S}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\bar{x};d)}(w^{\prime})\subseteq N_{S}(\bar{x};d)
.

Note that both inclusions in Lemma
2.4
may be strict, see
[
14
, Example 1]
and the following example.

Example 2.5
.

Consider
S=\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:x_{1}^{2}-x_{2}^{3}=0\}
and
\bar{x}=(0,0)
. It is easy to calculate that
T_{S}(\bar{x})=\mathbb{R}_{+}(0,1)
, where
\mathbb{R}_{+}(0,1)
represents the cone generated by the vector

. Then we have
T_{S}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\bar{x};d)=\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
T_{S}^{2}(\bar{x};d)=\emptyset
.
Hence
N_{T_{S}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\bar{x};d)}(w)=\{(0,0)\}
for all

, while

, which implies that

is a proper subset of

.

2.2
Second-Order Tangent Set and Asymptotic Second-Order Tangent Cone to Feasible Set under Directional MSCQ

In this subsection, we provide some auxiliary facts regarding the second-order tangent set and the asymptotic second-order tangent cone to the feasible set
C:=g^{-1}(K)
. The preliminary results are obtained under the validity of directional MSCQ of the constraint system
g(x)\in K
.

To introduce the property of directional MSCQ, we need to review the concept of directional neighborhood.
Let
d\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a given direction and
\rho,\delta>0
, the directional neighborhood of direction
d
is defined as follows:

\displaystyle V_{\rho,\delta}(d):

\displaystyle=\left\{w\in\delta B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mid\left\|\|d\|w-\|w\|d\right\|\leq\rho\|w\|\|d\|\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{matrix}\delta B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}},&{if\,d=0},\\
\left\{w\in\delta B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\backslash\{0\}\mid\left\|\frac{w}{\|w\|}-\frac{d}{\|d\|}\right\|\leq\rho\right\}\cup\{0\},&{if\,d\not=0}.\end{matrix}\right.

Note that the directional neighborhood is not a ball except for the case of
d=0
. In general, when
d\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\}
, the directional neighborhood is a section of the classical neighborhood. Furthermore, for
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, if there are sequences
\{x_{k}\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\{t_{k}\}\downarrow 0
such that
\frac{x_{k}-\bar{x}}{t_{k}}\rightarrow d
, then

x_{k}\in\bar{x}+V_{\rho,\delta}(d),\,{\rm for\,sufficiently\,large}\,\,k.

Indeed, the aforementioned relation holds due to the fact that
\left\|\frac{x_{k}-\bar{x}}{t_{k}}\right\|\rightarrow\|d\|

and

\frac{x_{k}-\bar{x}}{\|x_{k}-\bar{x}\|}=\frac{x_{k}-\bar{x}}{t_{k}}\frac{t_{k}}{\|x_{k}-\bar{x}\|}\rightarrow\frac{d}{\|d\|}.

(16)

Let
M:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a given multifunction and
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in{\rm gph}M
.
M
is said to be metrically subregular at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
in direction
d\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
if there are positive numbers
\rho,\delta,\kappa>0
such that

d(x,M^{-1}(\bar{y}))\leq\kappa d(\bar{y},M(x)),\quad\forall x\in\bar{x}+V_{\rho,\delta}(d).

The infimum of
\kappa
over all the combinations
\rho,\delta
and
\kappa
satisfying the above relation is called the modulus of directional metric subregularity. In the case of
d=0
, we simply say that
M
is metrically subregular at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
.

For the constraint system
g(x)\in K
, we say that the metric subregularity constraint qualification (MSCQ) holds at
\bar{x}
in direction
d
(with modulus
\kappa
) if the set-valued mapping
M(x):=g(x)-K
is metrically subregular at
(\bar{x},0)
in direction
d
(with modulus
\kappa
).

The following inclusion provides an upper estimate for the directional normals to the feasible set
C:=g^{-1}(K)
, which reduces to
[
13
, Theorem 3]
when direction
d=0
is considered.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\bar{x}\in C:=g^{-1}(K)
and assume that MSCQ holds at
\bar{x}
in direction
d\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
for the constraint system

with modulus

N_{g^{-1}(K)}(\bar{x};d)\subseteq\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\exists\lambda\in N_{K}(g(\bar{x});\nabla g(\bar{x})d)\cap\kappa\|v\|B_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\,\,{\rm with}\,\,v=\nabla g(\bar{x})^{T}\lambda\}.

(17)

Note that
g^{-1}(K)=M^{-1}(0)
with

, by
applying
[

Because

N_{{\rm gph}(M)}((\bar{x},0);(d,0))=\{(\nabla g(\bar{x})^{T}\lambda,-\lambda)\mid\lambda\in N_{K}(g(\bar{x});\nabla g(\bar{x})d)\},

which yields (

Now we are ready to present some basic facts about the second-order tangent set

\displaystyle d(\tilde{x}_{k},C)

\displaystyle\leq\kappa^{\prime}d(g(\tilde{x}_{k}),K)

\displaystyle\leq\kappa^{\prime}\|g(\tilde{x}_{k})-g(\bar{x})-t_{k}\nabla g(\bar{x})d-\frac{1}{2}t_{k}r_{k}u_{k}\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\kappa^{\prime}t_{k}r_{k}}{2}\left\|\nabla g(\bar{x})w+\frac{t_{k}}{r_{k}}\nabla^{2}g(\bar{x})(d,d)-u_{k}\right\|+o(t_{k}^{2}).

Then

d\left(w,\frac{C-\bar{x}-t_{k}d}{\frac{1}{2}t_{k}r_{k}}\right)\leq\kappa^{\prime}\left\|\nabla g(\bar{x})w+\frac{t_{k}}{r_{k}}\nabla^{2}g(\bar{x})(d,d)-u_{k}\right\|+\frac{o(t_{k}^{2})}{\frac{1}{2}t_{k}r_{k}},

which implies that there exists
x_{k}^{\prime}\in C
such that

\|w-w_{k}\|\leq\kappa^{\prime}\left\|\nabla g(\bar{x})w+\frac{t_{k}}{r_{k}}\nabla^{2}g(\bar{x})(d,d)-u_{k}\right\|+4\frac{o(t_{k}^{2})}{t_{k}r_{k}},

(23)

where
w_{k}:=\frac{x_{k}^{\prime}-\bar{x}-t_{k}d}{\frac{1}{2}t_{k}r_{k}}
. Since the right-hand side of (
23
) converges to
\kappa^{\prime}\left\|\nabla g(\bar{x})w-u\right\|
, we know that
\{w_{k}\}
is bounded. Without loss of generality, we assume that
w_{k}\rightarrow w^{\prime}
, then
w^{\prime}\in T_{C}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\bar{x};d)
.
Passing to the limit in (
23
), we arrive at

d(w,T_{C}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\bar{x};d))\leq\|w-w^{\prime}\|\leq\kappa^{\prime}\left\|\nabla g(\bar{x})w-u\right\|\leq\kappa^{\prime}d(\nabla g(\bar{x})w,T_{K}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(g(\bar{x});\nabla g(\bar{x})d))+{\kappa^{\prime}}\varepsilon.

Because
\kappa^{\prime}
can be chosen arbitrarily close to
\kappa
and
\varepsilon
can be chosen arbitrarily small, we know that (
21
) holds true.
From (
21
), we may conclude that

T_{C}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\bar{x};d)\supseteq\{w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\nabla g(\bar{x})w\in T_{K}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(g(\bar{x});\nabla g(\bar{x})d)\},

which indicates that inclusion (
19
) holds as equality.

In the following proposition, we provide upper estimations for limiting normals to second order tangent set and asymptotic second-order tangent cone under directional MSCQ, respectively.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
\bar{x}\in C:=g^{-1}(K)
and
d\in T_{C}(\bar{x})
be given. Assume that MSCQ holds at
\bar{x}
in direction
d\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
for the constraint system
g(x)\in K
with modulus
\kappa
. Then,

\displaystyle N_{T_{C}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\bar{x};d)}(v)\subseteq\{z

\displaystyle\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\exists\lambda\in N_{T_{K}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(g(\bar{x});\nabla g(\bar{x})d)}(\nabla g(\bar{x})v)\cap\kappa\|z\|B_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}

\displaystyle\rm{\displaystyle with}\,\,z=\nabla g(\bar{x})^{T}\lambda\},\quad\forall v\in T_{C}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\bar{x},d)

and

\displaystyle N_{T_{C}^{2}(\bar{x};d)}(w)\subseteq\{z

\displaystyle\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\exists\lambda\in N_{T_{K}^{2}(g(\bar{x});\nabla g(\bar{x})d)}(\nabla g(\bar{x})w+\nabla^{2}g(\bar{x})(d,d))\cap\kappa\|z\|B_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}

\displaystyle\rm{\displaystyle with}\,\,z=\nabla g(\bar{x})^{T}\lambda\},\quad\forall w\in T_{C}^{2}(\bar{x};d).

Proof.
According to the assumption, for any
v\in T_{C}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\bar{x},d)
, it follows from Proposition
2.7
that the mapping
w\mapsto\nabla g(\bar{x})w-T_{K}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(g(\bar{x});\nabla g(\bar{x})d)
is metrically subregular at
(v,0)
with modulus
\kappa
.
Then the first inclusion follows directly from
[
13
, Theorem 3]
(it can also be obtained from
[
12
, Proposition 4.1]
by choosing the zero direction). The second inclusion can be obtained similarly.

3
Optimality Conditions for Nonconvex Optimization Problem with an Abstract Set Constraint

In this section we consider the following nonconvex optimization problem with an abstract set constraint:

\min f(x)\quad\text{s.t.}\quad x\in C,

(24)

where
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is twice continuously differentiable and
C
is a closed subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Definition 3.1

(Directional Local Optimality)
.

A point
\bar{x}\in C
is said to be a local optimal solution of (
24
) in direction
d\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, if there exist positive numbers
\rho,\delta>0
such that

f(x)\geq f(\bar{x}),\,\,\,\forall x\in C\cap(\bar{x}+V_{\rho,\delta}(d)).

(25)

Note that the directional local optimal solution is only well-defined for directions
d\in T_{C}(\bar{x})
. In fact, we claim that for an arbitary given
d\not\in T_{C}(\bar{x})
, there exist
\rho,\delta>0
such that
C\cap(\bar{x}+V_{\rho,\delta}(d))=\{\bar{x}\}
. If this is not the case, then
for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
x_{k}\in C\cap(\bar{x}+V_{1/k,1/k}(d))\backslash\{\bar{x}\}
, that is,
x_{k}\in C,\|x_{k}-\bar{x}\|\leq\frac{1}{k}\rightarrow 0
with
\left\|\frac{x_{k}-\bar{x}}{\|x_{k}-\bar{x}\|}-\frac{d}{\|d\|}\right\|\leq\frac{1}{k}\rightarrow 0
. We arrive at
\frac{d}{\|d\|}\in T_{C}(\bar{x})
, which is a contradiction to our choice of
d\not\in T_{C}(\bar{x})
.
Hence,
\bar{x}
is always a local optimal solution of (
24
) in each direction outside of
T_{C}(\bar{x})
.
It worth to note that, if
\bar{x}
is a local optimal solution of (
24
) in all directions
d\in T_{C}(\bar{x})
, then
\bar{x}
is a local optimal solution of (
24
).

For optimization problem with an abstract set constraint, there are well-developed theoretical results which reveals essential connections between variational geometry and optimality conditions. First-order necessary conditions for local optimality are provided in
[
24
, Theorem 6.12]
in both primal and dual forms. In the following proposition, we improved the aforementioned results to the directional case which sharpened the necessary condition while weakened the optimality assumption. The obtained results are useful in developing our main results in Section
4
and have its own independent interest.

Proposition 3.2

(First-Order Necessary Optimality Condition)
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be continuously differentiable and
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be closed. Suppose that
\bar{x}\in C,d\in T_{C}(\bar{x})
and
\bar{x}
is a local optimal solution of (
24
) in direction
d
.
Then,

(i)

\nabla f(\bar{x})d\geq 0
;

(ii)

if
\nabla f(\bar{x})d=0
, then
0\in\nabla f(\bar{x})+N_{C}(\bar{x};d)
.

Proof.
According to the assumptions, there exist
\rho,\delta>0
such that (
25
) holds.
Since
d\in T_{C}(\bar{x})
, there exist
t_{k}\downarrow 0
and
d_{k}\rightarrow d
such that

x_{k}:=\bar{x}+t_{k}d_{k}\in C
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Notice that
x_{k}\in C\cap(\bar{x}+V_{\rho,\delta}(d))
for sufficiently large
k
thanks to (
16
), it then follows from (
25
) that

f(x_{k})=f(\bar{x})+t_{k}\nabla f(\bar{x})d_{k}+o(t_{k})\geq f(\bar{x}).

(26)

Dividing both sides of the above inequality by
t_{k}
and passing to the limit, we conclude that (i) holds.

It remains to show the validity of (ii). Since
\frac{d_{k}}{\|d_{k}\|}-\frac{d}{\|d\|}\rightarrow 0
and
x_{k}-\bar{x}=t_{k}d_{k}\rightarrow 0
, without loss of generality, we may assume that

\left\|\frac{d_{k}}{\|d_{k}\|}-\frac{d}{\|d\|}\right\|<\frac{\rho}{2}
and
\|x_{k}-\bar{x}\|<\frac{\delta}{2}
, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. For each
k
, let
r_{k}:=\frac{1}{4}\rho t_{k}\|d_{k}\|
, then, for any
x\in B_{r_{k}}(x_{k})
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{x-\bar{x}}{\|x-\bar{x}\|}-\frac{d}{\|d\|}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\frac{x-\bar{x}}{\|x-\bar{x}\|}-\frac{x_{k}-\bar{x}}{\|x_{k}-\bar{x}\|}\right\|+\left\|\frac{d_{k}}{\|d_{k}\|}-\frac{d}{\|d\|}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\|x-x_{k}\|}{\|x_{k}-\bar{x}\|}+\frac{\rho}{2}\leq\rho,

where the second inequality holds due to the fact that
\left\|\frac{u}{\|u\|}-\frac{v}{\|v\|}\right\|\leq\frac{2\|u-v\|}{\|u\|}
for all
u,v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\}
.
This shows that
B_{r_{k}}(x_{k})\subset\bar{x}+V_{\rho,\delta}(d)
.
Again, by (
25
), we have

f(x)\geq f(\bar{x})\quad\forall x\in C\cap B_{r_{k}}(x_{k}).

Since
f
is continuously differentiable, we can find
\varepsilon_{k}\in(0,1)
such that
\varepsilon_{k}\rightarrow 0
and

f(\bar{x})\leq f(x_{k})+\nabla f(x_{k})(x-x_{k})+\varepsilon_{k}\|x-x_{k}\|\quad\forall x\in C\cap B_{r_{k}}(x_{k}).

(27)

Note that
\nabla f(\bar{x})d=0
, by (
26
), we have

f(x_{k})-f(\bar{x})=t_{k}\nabla f(\bar{x})d_{k}+o(t_{k})=o(t_{k}).

Without loss of generality, we assume that

f(x_{k})-f(\bar{x})\leq\varepsilon_{k}t_{k}\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{N}.

(28)

Define
\varphi_{k}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
as follows:

\varphi_{k}(x):=f(x_{k})-f(\bar{x})+\nabla f(x_{k})(x-x_{k})+\varepsilon_{k}\|x-x_{k}\|+\delta_{C\cap B_{r_{k}}(x_{k})}(x)\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

It follows from (
27
) that
\varphi_{k}(x_{k})\leq\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varphi_{k}(x)+f(x_{k})-f(\bar{x})
.
By Ekeland’s variational principle, there exists
u_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

\|u_{k}-x_{k}\|\leq\sqrt{\varepsilon_{k}}r_{k}/4
,
\varphi_{k}(u_{k})\leq\varphi_{k}(x_{k})<+\infty
and

\varphi_{k}(u_{k})\leq\varphi_{k}(x)+\frac{4}{\sqrt{\varepsilon_{k}}r_{k}}(f(x_{k})-f(\bar{x}))\|x-u_{k}\|\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(29)

Then
u_{k}\in C\cap B_{r_{k}}(x_{k})
.
By (
29
), according to the first-order optimality conditions, we have

0\in\hat{\partial}(\varphi_{k}+\frac{4}{\sqrt{\varepsilon_{k}}r_{k}}(f(x_{k})-f(\bar{x}))\|\cdot-u_{k}\|)(u_{k})
.
Then, by fuzzy sum rule of the subdifferential, there exists
u_{k}^{\prime}\in B_{\sqrt{\varepsilon_{k}}r_{k}/4}(u_{k})
such that

0\in\nabla f(x_{k})+\left(\varepsilon_{k}+\frac{4}{\sqrt{\varepsilon_{k}}r_{k}}(f(x_{k})-f(\bar{x}))+\frac{\sqrt{\varepsilon_{k}}r_{k}}{4}\right)B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}+\hat{N}_{C\cap B_{r_{k}}(x_{k})}(u_{k}^{\prime}).

(30)

It is easy to see that

\|u_{k}^{\prime}-x_{k}\|\leq\|u_{k}^{\prime}-u_{k}\|+\|u_{k}-x_{k}\|\leq\sqrt{\varepsilon_{k}}r_{k}/2<r_{k}

and

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{u_{k}^{\prime}-\bar{x}}{\|u_{k}^{\prime}-\bar{x}\|}-\frac{d}{\|d\|}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\frac{u_{k}^{\prime}-\bar{x}}{\|u_{k}^{\prime}-\bar{x}\|}-\frac{x_{k}-\bar{x}}{\|x_{k}-\bar{x}\|}\right\|+\left\|\frac{d_{k}}{\|d_{k}\|}-\frac{d}{\|d\|}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\|u_{k}^{\prime}-x_{k}\|}{\|x_{k}-\bar{x}\|}+\left\|\frac{d_{k}}{\|d_{k}\|}-\frac{d}{\|d\|}\right\|\rightarrow 0.

This shows that
u_{k}^{\prime}\in{\rm int}(B_{r_{k}}(x_{k}))
and
d_{k}^{\prime}:=\frac{u_{k}^{\prime}-\bar{x}}{t_{k}^{\prime}}\rightarrow d
, where
t_{k}^{\prime}:=\|d\|^{-1}\|u_{k}^{\prime}-\bar{x}\|
.
Then
\hat{N}_{C\cap B_{r_{k}}(x_{k})}(u_{k}^{\prime})=\hat{N}_{C\cap B_{r_{k}}(x_{k})}(\bar{x}+t_{k}^{\prime}d_{k}^{\prime})=\hat{N}_{C}(\bar{x}+t_{k}^{\prime}d_{k}^{\prime})
.
It follows from (
30
) that, there exists
b_{k}\in B_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
such that

-\nabla f(x_{k})+\left(\varepsilon_{k}+\frac{4}{\sqrt{\varepsilon_{k}}r_{k}}(f(x_{k})-f(\bar{x}))+\frac{\sqrt{\varepsilon_{k}}r_{k}}{4}\right)b_{k}\in\hat{N}_{C}(\bar{x}+t_{k}^{\prime}d_{k}^{\prime}).

By (
28
), we have

0\leq\frac{4}{\sqrt{\varepsilon_{k}}r_{k}}(f(x_{k})-f(\bar{x}))\leq\frac{16\varepsilon_{k}t_{k}}{\sqrt{\varepsilon_{k}}\rho t_{k}\|d_{k}\|}\rightarrow 0.

Since
\|b_{k}\|\leq 1,\nabla f(x_{k})\rightarrow\nabla f(\bar{x})
and
\varepsilon_{k}\rightarrow 0
,
we conclude that (ii) holds.

When the direction
d\neq 0
, the directional normal cone is smaller than the limiting normal cone. Hence Proposition
3.2
obtains a stronger necessary optimality condition under weaker assumptions than the classical result
[
24
, Theorem 6.12]
. Note that along with the condition
d\in T_{C}(\bar{x})
and Proposition
3.2
(i), the condition of
\nabla f(\bar{x})d=0
in Proposition
3.2
(ii) means that
d
must be a critical direction. The following example illustrates that, the condition of
\nabla f(\bar{x})d=0
in Proposition
3.2
(ii) cannot be dropped, even in the case when
C
is polyhedral. It is worth to note that, when
C
is convex, it follows from (
13
) that
N_{C}(\bar{x};d)=N_{C}(\bar{x})\cap\{d\}^{\bot}
. Therefore, if
-\nabla f(\bar{x})\in N_{C}(\bar{x};d)
, we must have
\nabla f(\bar{x})d=0
.

Example 3.3
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be defined as
f(x_{1},x_{2})=(x_{1}+1)^{2}
for all
(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Consider
C=\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+},\bar{x}=(0,0)
and
d=(1,1)
. Then
\bar{x}
is a local optimal solution of (
24
) in direction
d
. But, in this case,

\nabla f(\bar{x})d\not=0
and
-\nabla f(\bar{x})\not\in N_{C}(\bar{x};d)=\{0\}
.

Since the asymptotic second-order tangent cone
T_{C}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\bar{x};d)
and the outer second-order tangent set
T_{C}^{2}(\bar{x};d)
cannot be empty simultateously, it is more appropriate to consider second-order optimality conditions that involves both sets. The following proposition is an enhancement to
[
21
, Corollary 1.3]
and
[
22
, Corollary 3.2]
in which the author provided second-order necessary conditions for problem (
24
) to obtain a local minimum with no direction specified. It establishes exact correspondence between the direction along which problem (
24
) obtains a local solution and the direction involved in the presentation of the optimality conditions.

Proposition 3.4

(Primary Second-Order Necessary Optimality Condition)
.

Let
d\in T_{C}(\bar{x})
and
\bar{x}
be a local optimal solution of (
24
) in direction
d\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\nabla f(\bar{x})d=0
.
Then,

(i)

\nabla f(\bar{x})v\geq 0,\quad\forall v\in T_{C}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\bar{x},d)
;

(ii)

\nabla f(\bar{x})(w)+\nabla^{2}f(\bar{x})(d,d)\geq 0,\quad\forall w\in T_{C}^{2}(\bar{x};d)
.

Proof.
Since
\bar{x}
is a local optimal solution of (
24
) in direction
d\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, there exist
\rho,\delta>0
such that (
25
) holds.
Pick any
v\in T_{C}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\bar{x};d)
, then there exist
(t_{k},r_{k})\downarrow(0,0)
and
v_{k}\rightarrow v
such that

t_{k}/r_{k}\rightarrow 0
and
x_{k}:=\bar{x}+t_{k}d+\frac{1}{2}t_{k}r_{k}v_{k}\in C
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Since
\frac{x_{k}-\bar{x}}{t_{k}}=d+r_{k}v_{k}\rightarrow d
, then we have (
16
) holds.
It follows from (
25
) that, for sufficiently large
k
,

f(x_{k})=f(\bar{x})+t_{k}\nabla f(\bar{x})d+\frac{1}{2}t_{k}r_{k}\nabla f(\bar{x})v_{k}+\frac{1}{2}t_{k}^{2}\nabla^{2}f(\bar{x})\left(d,d\right)+o(t_{k}^{2})\geq f(\bar{x}).

Note that
\nabla f(\bar{x})d=0
, one has

\nabla f(\bar{x})v_{k}+\frac{t_{k}}{r_{k}}\nabla^{2}f(\bar{x})\left(d,d\right)+\frac{o(t_{k}^{2})}{\frac{1}{2}t_{k}r_{k}}\geq 0.

Since
t_{k}/r_{k}\rightarrow 0
, passing to the limit in the above inequality, we arrive at (i).

Pick any
w\in T_{C}^{2}(\bar{x};d)
. Then there exist
t_{k}\downarrow 0
and
w_{k}\rightarrow w
such that

\tilde{x}_{k}:=\bar{x}+t_{k}d+\frac{1}{2}t_{k}^{2}w_{k}\in C
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Similarly, we know that for sufficiently large
k
,
\tilde{x}_{k}\in C\cap(\bar{x}+V_{\rho,\delta}(d))
and

f(x_{k})=f(\bar{x})+t_{k}\nabla f(\bar{x})d+\frac{1}{2}t_{k}^{2}(\nabla f(\bar{x})w_{k}+\nabla^{2}f(\bar{x})(d,d))+o(t_{k}^{2})\geq f(\bar{x}).

Dividing both sides of the above inequality by
\frac{1}{2}t_{k}^{2}
and passing to the limit, we have (ii) holds.

Assertion (ii) in Proposition
3.4
shows that
\inf_{w\in T_{C}^{2}(\bar{x};d)}(\nabla f(\bar{x})(w)+\nabla^{2}f(\bar{x})(d,d))

\geq 0
when
\bar{x}
is a local optimal solution of (
24
) in direction
d
. In the following example, we illustrate that this infimum can be strictly positive.

Example 3.5
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be defined as
f(x_{1},x_{2})=x_{2}^{2}
for all
(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Consider
C=\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+},\bar{x}=(0,0)
and
d=(0,1)
, then
\bar{x}
is a local optimal solution of (
24
) in direction
d
. It is easy to see that

\nabla f(\bar{x})=(0,0),\nabla^{2}f(\bar{x})=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&0\\
0&2\\
\end{array}\right)
and
T_{C}^{2}(\bar{x};d)=\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}
. Hence, we have
\inf_{w\in T_{C}^{2}(\bar{x};d)}(\nabla f(\bar{x})(w)+\nabla^{2}f(\bar{x})(d,d))=2>0
.

4
Optimality Conditions for Problem (
1
)

In this section, we aim at developing new necessary and sufficient optimality conditions for problem (
1
) to characterize a local optimal solution in direction
d
.
Throughout this section we denote by
C:=g^{-1}(K)
,

\Theta:=\nabla g(\bar{x})(T_{C}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\bar{x};d))
and
\Omega:=\nabla g(\bar{x})(T_{C}^{2}(\bar{x};d))+\nabla^{2}g(\bar{x})(d,d)
.

Since problem (
1
) is problem (
24
) with
C:=g^{-1}(K)
, in the following proposition, by Proposition
3.2
, we provide the first-order necessary optimality condition for problem (
1
) at a local directional minimizer under the directional MSCQ for the constraint system
g(x)\in K
. Note that one always has

d\in T_{C}(\bar{x})\Longrightarrow\nabla g(\bar{x})d\in T_{K}(g(\bar{x}))

and the equivalence
\Leftrightarrow
holds if the directional MSCQ holds in direction
d
.

Proposition 4.1

(First-Order Necessary Optimality Condition)
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
be continuously differentiable,
d\in T_{C}(\bar{x})
and
\bar{x}
be a local optimal solution of problem (
1
) in direction
d
. Suppose that
\nabla f(\bar{x})d=0
and MSCQ holds at
\bar{x}
in direction
d
for the constraint system
g(x)\in K
with modulus
\kappa
. Then
there exists
\lambda\in N_{K}(g(\bar{x});\nabla g(\bar{x})d)
such that

\|\lambda\|\leq\kappa\|\nabla f(\bar{x})\|\,\,{\rm and}\,\,\nabla_{x}L(\bar{x},\lambda)=0.

(31)

Proof.
By Proposition
3.2
, we have
-\nabla f(\bar{x})\in N_{C}(\bar{x};d)
. At the hand of directional MSCQ, it follows from Lemma
2.6
that

\displaystyle-\nabla f(\bar{x})\in N_{C}(\bar{x};d)\subseteq\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid

\displaystyle\exists\lambda\in N_{K}(g(\bar{x});\nabla g(\bar{x})d)\cap\kappa\|v\|B_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}

\displaystyle{\rm with}\,\,v=\nabla g(\bar{x})^{T}\lambda\}.

This implies that there exists
\lambda\in N_{K}(g(\bar{x});\nabla g(\bar{x})d)
such that
-\nabla f(\bar{x})=\nabla g(\bar{x})^{T}\lambda
, i.e.,
\nabla_{x}L(\bar{x},\lambda)=0
, and

\|\lambda\|\leq\kappa\|\nabla g(\bar{x})^{T}\lambda\|=\kappa\|\nabla f(\bar{x})\|
.

For convenience, for each feasible solution
\bar{x}
and each
d\in T_{C}(\bar{x})
, define the M-multiplier set in direction
d
as

\Lambda(\bar{x};d):=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\lambda\in N_{K}(g(\bar{x});\nabla g(\bar{x})d)\,\,{\rm and}\,\,\nabla_{x}L(\bar{x},\lambda)=0\}.

By Proposition
4.1
, the directional MSCQ ensures that
\Lambda(\bar{x};d)
for a local optimal solution
\bar{x}
and a direction
d\in T_{C}(\bar{x})\cap\{\nabla f(\bar{x})\}^{\perp}
is nonempty.

We now prove a new second-order necessary optimality condition.

Theorem 4.2

(Second-Order Necessary Optimality Condition)
.

Let
d\in T_{C}(\bar{x})
and
\bar{x}
be a local optimal solution of problem (
1
) in direction
d
with
\nabla f(\bar{x})d=0
.
Then, for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
with
\nabla_{x}L(\bar{x},\lambda)=0
, one has

(i)

\sigma_{\Theta}(\lambda)\leq 0
;

(ii)

\nabla_{xx}^{2}L(\bar{x},\lambda)(d,d)-\sigma_{\Omega}(\lambda)=\alpha\geq 0
, where

\alpha:=\inf_{w\in T_{C}^{2}(\bar{x};d)}(\nabla f(\bar{x})(w)+\nabla^{2}f(\bar{x})(d,d)).

Proof.
Since
\bar{x}
is a local optimal solution of (
1
) in direction
d\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, it follows from Proposition
3.4
that

\alpha\geq 0
and

\nabla f(\bar{x})u\geq 0,\quad\forall u\in T_{C}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\bar{x};d).

(32)

Pick any
\lambda
such that
\nabla_{x}L(\bar{x},\lambda)=0
. For any
v\in\nabla g(\bar{x})(T_{C}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\bar{x};d))
, there exists
u\in T_{C}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\bar{x};d)
such that

.
It follows from (

\langle\lambda,v\rangle=\langle\nabla g(\bar{x})^{T}\lambda,u\rangle=-\nabla f(\bar{x})u\leq 0,

which shows that (i) holds. We have

\displaystyle\sigma_{\Omega}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\sup_{u\in\Omega}\langle\lambda,u\rangle

\displaystyle=\sup_{w\in T_{C}^{2}(\bar{x};d)}\langle\lambda,\nabla g(\bar{x})w+\nabla^{2}g(\bar{x})(d,d)\rangle

\displaystyle=\sup_{w\in T_{C}^{2}(\bar{x};d)}\langle\nabla g(\bar{x})^{T}\lambda,w\rangle+\langle\lambda,\nabla^{2}g(\bar{x})(d,d)\rangle

\displaystyle=\sup_{w\in T_{C}^{2}(\bar{x};d)}-(\nabla f(\bar{x})w+\nabla^{2}f(\bar{x})(d,d))+\nabla^{2}f(\bar{x})(d,d)+\langle\lambda,\nabla^{2}g(\bar{x})(d,d)\rangle

\displaystyle=-\inf_{w\in T_{C}^{2}(\bar{x};d)}(\nabla f(\bar{x})w+\nabla^{2}f(\bar{x})(d,d))+\nabla^{2}_{xx}L(\bar{x},\lambda)(d,d)

\displaystyle=-\alpha+\nabla^{2}_{xx}L(\bar{x},\lambda)(d,d),

which implies that (ii) holds.

Definition 4.5

[

Let

be a nonempty closed set. For every subset

if for all

close to

.

Remark 4.8
.

In addition to the blanket assumption that
\bar{x}
is a local optimal solution of problem (

, Theorem

[

Recall that the directional Clarke multiplier set:

which is stronger than the directional first-order sufficient condition for metric subregularity (FOSCMS)(

10

replaced by the directional limiting normal cone

, we integrate the approach of convex duality theory employed in the recent work of Gfrerer, Ye and Zhou
[
14

to derive the anticipated new second-order necessary optimality condition for problem (

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
d\in T_{C}(\bar{x})
and
\bar{x}
be a local optimal solution of problem (
1
) in direction
d
with
\nabla f(\bar{x})d=0
. Suppose that DirRCQ holds in direction

.
Then, the following three statements are equivalent:

(i)

The primal second-order necessary condition

\nabla f(\bar{x})v\geq 0,\quad\forall v\in T_{C}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\bar{x},d)

of Proposition
3.4
holds;

(ii)

For every

\lambda_{u}\in\Lambda^{c}(\bar{x};d)
such that

\langle\lambda_{u},u\rangle\leq 0

(iii)

For every nonempty convex subset

, there exists
\lambda\in\Lambda^{c}(\bar{x};d)
such that

\sigma_{K(d)}(\lambda)\leq 0
.

Proof.
Since DirRCQ implies that MSCQ holds in direction
d
for the constraint system
g(x)\in K
, it follows from Proposition
2.7
that

T_{C}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\bar{x};d)=\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\nabla g(\bar{x})v\in T_{K}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(g(\bar{x});\nabla g(\bar{x})d)\}.

First, we show that “(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii)”. Take arbitary
u\in T_{K}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(g(\bar{x});\nabla g(\bar{x})d)

Assumption (i) ensures that the following conic linear program

\min_{v}\nabla f(\bar{x})v\,\,\,{\rm s.t.}\,\,\,\nabla g(\bar{x})v\in u+\hat{T}_{K}(g(\bar{x});\nabla g(\bar{x})d)

(38)

has nonnegative optimal value. The dual program of the conic linear program (
38
) (c.f.
[
5
, Section 2.5.6]

\max_{\lambda\in(\hat{T}_{K}(g(\bar{x});\nabla g(\bar{x})d))^{\circ}}-\lambda^{T}u\,\,\quad{\rm s.t.}\quad\,\,\nabla g(\bar{x})^{T}\lambda+\nabla f(\bar{x})=0.

(39)

Since
(\hat{T}_{K}(g(\bar{x});\nabla g(\bar{x})d))^{\circ}=N^{c}_{K}(g(\bar{x});\nabla g(\bar{x})d)
, the preceding dual problem can be equivalently written as

\max_{\lambda\in\Lambda^{c}(\bar{x};d)}-\lambda^{T}u.

By
[
14
, Lemma 6]
, the DirRCQ implies that

0\in{\rm int}(\nabla g(\bar{x})\mathbb{R}^{n}-u-\hat{T}_{K}(g(\bar{x});\nabla g(\bar{x})d)),

which indicates that there is no dual between problems (
38
) and (
39
) according to
[
5
, Theorem 2.187]
. Hence, the dual program has an optimal solution
\lambda_{u}
such that

-\langle\lambda_{u},u\rangle=\max_{\lambda\in\Lambda^{c}(\bar{x};d)}-\lambda^{T}u\geq 0,

which establishes the validity of (ii).

Conversely, take
v\in T_{C}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\bar{x};d)
and set
u:=\nabla g(\bar{x})v
. Then
u\in T_{K}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(g(\bar{x});\nabla g(\bar{x})d)
, and it follows from (ii) that there exists
\lambda_{u}\in\Lambda^{c}(\bar{x};d)
satisfying

\langle\lambda_{u},u\rangle\leq 0
. Therefore

\nabla f(\bar{x})v=-\langle g(\bar{x})^{T}\lambda_{u},v\rangle=-\langle\lambda_{u},u\rangle\geq 0.

which shows that “(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i)”.

To show that “(ii)
\Leftrightarrow
(iii)”, it suffices to establish that “(ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii)” since the converse implication holds trivially. To this end, pick any nonempty convex subset

{K(d)}\subseteq T^{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{K}(g(\bar{x});\nabla g(\bar{x})d)

is closed since
\sigma_{K(d)}=\sigma_{{\rm cl}(K(d))}

is nonempty, and then we conclude from
[
14

that
\Lambda^{c}(\bar{x};d)
is

[
23

that

\inf_{u\in{K(d)}}\sup_{\lambda\in\Lambda^{c}(\bar{x};d)}-\lambda^{T}u=\sup_{\lambda\in\Lambda^{c}(\bar{x};d)}\inf_{u\in{K(d)}}-\lambda^{T}u=\sup_{\lambda\in\Lambda^{c}(\bar{x};d)}-\sigma_{{K(d)}}(\lambda).

Recall that the left-hand side of the above equality has nonnegative value according to (ii), and the supremum of the right-hand side of the above equality is attained at some
\lambda
, we conclude that (iii) holds true.

(ii)

\nabla_{xx}^{2}L(\bar{x},\lambda)(d,d)-\sigma_{K(d)}(\lambda)\geq 0

Recall that according to

\nabla g(\bar{x})^{T}\lambda=0,\lambda\in{\rm span}(N_{K}(g(\bar{x});\nabla g(\bar{x})d))\Rightarrow\lambda=0

(40)

Corollary 4.11
.

Let

and
\bar{x}
be a local optimal solution of problem (
1
) in direction
d
with
\nabla f(\bar{x})d=0
. Suppose that the directional nondegeneracy condition (

(i)

\sigma_{T^{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{K}(g(\bar{x});\nabla g(\bar{x})d)}(\lambda){\leq}0

(ii)

If

\nabla_{xx}^{2}L(\bar{x},\lambda)(d,d)-\sigma_{T^{2}_{K}(g(\bar{x});\nabla g(\bar{x})d)}(\lambda)\geq 0

Lemma 4.13

Theorem 4.14

(Second-Order Sufficient Optimality Condition)

Let

(ii)

\nabla_{xx}^{2}L(\bar{x},\lambda)(d,d)-\sigma_{\nabla g(\bar{x})(T_{C}^{2}(\bar{x};d)\cap\{d\}^{\bot})+\nabla^{2}g(\bar{x})(d,d)}(\lambda)>0

Proof.
We argue by contradiction. Assume to the contrary that the second-order growth condition in direction

\bar{x}

\{x_{k}\}\subseteq\bar{x}+V_{1/k,1/k}(d)

f(x_{k})<f(\bar{x})+\frac{1}{k}\|x_{k}-\bar{x}\|^{2}.

(42)

According to the assumptions, let
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
with
\nabla_{x}L(\bar{x},\lambda)=0
be such that (i) and (ii) hold. Then, we have

\nabla f(\bar{x})+\nabla g(\bar{x})^{T}\lambda=0
.
Let
t_{k}=\|x_{k}-\bar{x}\|

converges to some vector

or

(b) there exists a sequence
r_{k}\downarrow 0
such that
t_{k}/r_{k}\rightarrow 0
and
\tilde{w}_{k}:=\frac{x_{k}-\bar{x}-t_{k}\frac{d}{\|d\|}}{\frac{1}{2}t_{k}r_{k}}

\tilde{w}\in T_{C}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\bar{x};\frac{d}{\|d\|})\cap\left(\{d\}^{\bot}\backslash\{0\}\right)

If condition (a) holds, in this case, we have
x_{k}=\bar{x}+t_{k}\frac{d}{\|d\|}+\frac{1}{2}t_{k}^{2}w_{k}

Note that
\nabla f(\bar{x})d=0

\frac{1}{k}t_{k}^{2}>f(x_{k})-f(\bar{x})=t_{k}\nabla f(\bar{x})\frac{d}{\|d\|}+\frac{1}{2}t_{k}^{2}\left(\nabla f(\bar{x})w_{k}+\nabla^{2}f(\bar{x})\left(\frac{d}{\|d\|},\frac{d}{\|d\|}\right)\right)+o(t_{k}^{2}).

Dividing both sides of above inequality by

and passing to the limit, we have

\nabla f(\bar{x})w+\nabla^{2}f(\bar{x})\left(\frac{d}{\|d\|},\frac{d}{\|d\|}\right)\leq 0.

(43)

For convenience, let
\Lambda:=\nabla g(\bar{x})(T_{C}^{2}(\bar{x};d)\cap\{d\}^{\bot})+\nabla^{2}g(\bar{x})(d,d)

\displaystyle\nabla g(\bar{x})w+\nabla^{2}g(\bar{x})\left(\frac{d}{\|d\|},\frac{d}{\|d\|}\right)

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle\nabla g(\bar{x})\left(T_{C}^{2}\left(\bar{x};\frac{d}{\|d\|}\right)\cap\{d\}^{\bot}\right)+\frac{1}{\|d\|^{2}}\nabla^{2}g(\bar{x})(d,d)

Adding (

\displaystyle\nabla f(\bar{x})w+\langle\nabla g(\bar{x})^{T}\lambda,w\rangle+\nabla^{2}f(\bar{x})\left(\frac{d}{\|d\|},\frac{d}{\|d\|}\right)+\left\langle\lambda,\nabla^{2}g(\bar{x})\left(\frac{d}{\|d\|},\frac{d}{\|d\|}\right)\right\rangle

If condition (b) holds, then we have
x_{k}=\bar{x}+t_{k}\frac{d}{\|d\|}+\frac{1}{2}t_{k}r_{k}\tilde{w}_{k}
, and
{\tilde{w}}\in T^{\prime\prime}_{C}(\bar{x},d)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k}t_{k}^{2}

\displaystyle>

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we consider second-order optimality conditions in specific directions for a generalized set-constrained optimization problem. By utilizing both the outer second-order tangent set and the asymptotic second-order tangent cone, we establish new directional necessary and “no gap” sufficient second-order optimality conditions which do not require convexity or outer second-order regularity of the underlying set or nonemptiness of the outer second-order tangent set. The obtained theoretical results are improvement of the existing ones. In our future work, we may consider extending our results to problems without second-order differentiability in functions
f
and/or
g
. We also look forward to apply our results in numerical algorithms.

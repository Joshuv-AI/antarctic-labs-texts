A conforming discontinuous Galerkin finite element method
Xiu Ye, Shangyou Zhang

Abstract
A new finite element method with discontinuous approximation is introduced for solving second order elliptic problem. Since this method combines the features of both conforming finite element method and discontinuous Galerkin (DG) method, we call it conforming DG method. While using DG finite element space, this conforming DG method maintains the features of the conforming finite element method such as simple formulation and strong enforcement of boundary condition. Therefore, this finite element method has the flexibility of using discontinuous approximation and simplicity in formulation of the conforming finite element method. Error estimates of optimal order are established for the corresponding discontinuous finite element approximation in both a discrete H 1 H^{1} norm and the L 2 L^{2} norm. Numerical results are presented to confirm the theory.



keywords

weak Galerkin, discontinuous Galerkin, finite element methods, second order elliptic problem

1
Introduction

For the sake of clear presentation, we consider Poisson equation with Dirichlet boundary condition in two dimension as our model problem. This conforming DG method can be extended to solve other elliptic problems.
The Poisson problem seeks an unknown function
u
satisfying

(1)

\displaystyle-\Delta u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f,\quad\mbox{in}\;\Omega,

(2)

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g,\quad\mbox{on}\;\partial\Omega,

where
\Omega
is a polytopal domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Researchers started to use discontinuous approximation in finite element procedure in the early 1970s
[
2
,
7
,
12
,
17
]
. Local discontinuous Galerkin methods were introduced in
[
6
]
. Then a paper
[
1
]
in 2002 provides a unified analysis of discontinuous Galerkin (DG) finite element methods for Poisson equation.
Since then, many new finite element methods with discontinuous approximations have been developed such as
hybridizable discontinuous Galerkin (HDG) method
[
5
]
, mimetic finite differences method
[
10
]
,
hybrid high-order (HHO) method

[
11
]
, virtual element (VE) method
[
13
]
, weak Galerkin (WG) method
[
14
]
and references therein.

The weak form of the problem (
1
)-(
2
) is given as follows: find
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)

such that
u=g
on
\partial\Omega
and

(3)

\displaystyle(\nabla u,\nabla v)=(f,v)\quad\forall v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega).

The conforming finite element method for the problem (
1
)-(
2
) keeps the same simple form as in (
3
). However,
when discontinuous approximation is used, finite element formulations tend to be more complex than (
3
) to ensure connection of discontinuous function across element boundary. For example, the following is the formulation for the symmetric interior penalty discontinuous Galerkin (IPDG) method for the Poisson equation (
1
) with homogeneous boundary condition: find
u_{h}\in V_{h}
such that for all
v_{h}\in V_{h}
,

\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}(\nabla u_{h},\nabla v_{h})_{T}-\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}}\int_{e}\Big(\{\nabla u_{h}\}[v_{h}]+\{\nabla v_{h}\}[u_{h}]-\alpha h_{e}^{-1}[u_{h}][v_{h}]\Big)=(f,v_{h}),

where
\alpha
is called a penalty parameter that needs to be tuned.

A first order weakly over-penalized symmetric interior penalty method is proposed in
[
3
]
aiming for
simplifying the above IPDG formulation by eliminating the two nonsymmetric middle terms:
find
u_{h}\in V_{h}
such that for all
v_{h}\in V_{h}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}(\nabla u_{h},\nabla v_{h})_{T}+\alpha\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}}h_{e}^{-3}(\Pi_{0}[u_{h}],\;\Pi_{0}[v_{h}])_{e}=(f,v_{h}),

where
\Pi_{0}
is the
L^{2}
projection to the constant space
and
\alpha
is a positive number. The price paid for a simpler formulation is a worse condition number for the
resulting system of linear equations.

In this paper, we propose a new conforming DG method using the same finite element space
used in the IPDG method for any polynomial degree
k\geq 1

but having a simple symmetric and positive definite system:
find
u_{h}\in V_{h}
satisfying
u_{h}=I_{h}g
on
\partial\Omega
and

(4)

(\nabla_{w}u_{h},\nabla_{w}v_{h})=(f,v_{h})\quad\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}^{0},

where
\nabla_{w}
is called weak gradient introduced in the weak Galerkin finite element method
[
14
,
15
]
. It follows from (
4
) that the conforming DG method can be obtained from the conforming formulation simply by replacing
\nabla
by
\nabla_{w}
and enforcing the boundary condition strongly. The simplicity of the conforming DG formulation will ease the complexity for implementation of DG methods. The computation of weak gradient
\nabla_{w}v
is totally local. Optimal convergence rates for the conforming DG approximation are obtained in a
discrete
H^{1}
norm and in the
L^{2}
norm.
This new conforming DG method is tested numerically for
k=1,2,3,4
and
5
, and the results confirm the theory.

2
Finite Element Method

In this section, we will introduce the conforming DG method.
For any given polygon
D\subseteq\Omega
, we use the standard
definition of Sobolev spaces
H^{s}(D)
with
s\geq 0
. The associated inner product,
norm, and semi-norms in
H^{s}(D)
are denoted by

(\cdot,\cdot)_{s,D}
,
\|\cdot\|_{s,D}
, and
|\cdot|_{s,D}
, respectively. When
s=0
,
H^{0}(D)
coincides with the space
of square integrable functions
L^{2}(D)
. In this case, the subscript

s
is suppressed from the notation of norm, semi-norm, and inner
products. Furthermore, the subscript
D
is also suppressed when

D=\Omega
.

Let
{\cal T}_{h}
be a triangulation of the domain
\Omega
with mesh
size
h
that consists of triangles. Denote by

{\cal E}_{h}
the set of all edges in
{\cal T}_{h}
, and let

{\cal E}_{h}^{0}={\cal E}_{h}\backslash\partial\Omega
be the set of all
interior edges.

We define the average and the
jump on edges for a scalar-valued function
v
. For an
interior edge
e\in{\cal E}_{h}^{0}
, let
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
be two triangles
sharing
e
.
Let
{\bf n}_{1}
and
{\bf n}_{2}
be the two unit outward normal
vectors on
e
, associated with
{T_{1}}
and
{T_{2}}
, respectively.
Define the average
\{\cdot\}
and the jump
[\cdot]
on
e
by

(5)

\{v\}=\frac{1}{2}(v|_{T_{1}}+v|_{T_{2}})\quad\hbox{and \ }[v]=v|_{T_{1}}{\bf n}_{1}+v|_{T_{2}}{\bf n}_{2},

respectively.
If
e
is a boundary edge, then

(6)

\{v\}=v,\quad[v]=v{\bf n}.

For simplicity, we adopt the following notations,

\displaystyle(v,w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(v,w)_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}=\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}(v,w)_{T}=\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\int_{T}vwd{\bf x},

\displaystyle{\langle}v,w{\rangle}_{\partial{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}{\langle}v,w{\rangle}_{\partial T}=\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\int_{\partial T}vwds.

First we define two discontinuous finite element spaces for
k\geq 1
,

(7)

V_{h}=\left\{v\in L^{2}(\Omega):\ v|_{T}\in P_{k}(T),\;\;T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}\right\},

and

(8)

V_{h}^{0}=\left\{v\in V_{h}:\ v=0\;{\rm on}\;\partial\Omega\right\}.

Algorithm 1
.

A conforming DG finite element method for the problem (
1
)-(
2
)
seeks
u_{h}\in V_{h}
satisfying
u_{h}=I_{h}g
on
\partial\Omega
and

(9)

\displaystyle(\nabla_{w}u_{h},\nabla_{w}v)_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(f,\;v)\quad\forall v\in V_{h}^{0},

where
I_{h}
is the
k
th order Lagrange interpolation.

Next we will discuss how to compute weak gradient
\nabla_{w}u_{h}
and
\nabla_{w}v
in (
9
). The concept of weak gradient
\nabla_{w}
was first introduced in
[
14
,
15
]
for weak functions in WG methods and was modified in
[
16
,
8
]
for the functions in
V_{h}
in (
7
) as follows.
For a given
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
and a function
v\in V_{h}
, the weak gradient
\nabla_{w}v\in RT_{k}(T)
on
T
is the unique solution of the following equation,

(10)

(\nabla_{w}v,\tau)_{T}=-(v,\nabla\cdot\tau)_{T}+\langle\{v\},\tau\cdot{\bf n}\rangle_{\partial T},\qquad\forall\tau\in RT_{k}(T),

where
RT_{k}(T)=[P_{k}(T)]^{2}+{\bf x}P_{k}(T)
and
\{v\}
is defined in (
5
) and (
6
). The weak gradient
\nabla_{w}
is a local operator computed at each element.

3
Well Posedness

We start this section by introducing two semi-norms
{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}v{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}
and
\|v\|_{1,h}

for any
v\in V_{h}
as follows:

(11)

\displaystyle{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}v{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}(\nabla_{w}v,\nabla_{w}v)_{T},

(12)

\displaystyle\|v\|_{1,h}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\|\nabla v\|_{T}^{2}+\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{0}}h_{e}^{-1}\|[v]\|_{e}^{2}.

The following norm equivalences is proved in Lemma 3.2
[
9
]
with
v_{0}=v
and
v_{b}=\{v\}
that there exist two constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
independent of
h
such that

(13)

C_{1}\|v\|_{1,h}\leq{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}v{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}\leq C_{2}\|v\|_{1,h},\quad\forall v\in V_{h}^{0}.

Lemma 1
.

The semi-norm
{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}\cdot{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}
defined in (
11
) is a norm in
V_{h}^{0}
.

Proof.

We only need to prove
v=0
if
{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}v{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}=0
for all
v\in V_{h}^{0}
. Let
v\in V_{h}^{0}
and
{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}v{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}=0
. By (
13
), we have
\|v\|_{1,h}=0
which implies that
\nabla v=0
in each
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
and
[v]=0
on
e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{0}
.
\nabla v=0
on
T
implies that
v
is a constant on each
T
.
[v]=0
on
e
means that
v
is continuous.
Thus
v
is a global constant on the whole domain.
With
v=0
on
\partial\Omega
, we conclude
v=0
. This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

The well posedness of the conforming DG method (
9
) follows immediately from the above lemma.

4
Error Equation

In this section, we will derive an error equation which will be used in the convergence analysis. First we define

H({\rm div};\Omega)
space as the set of vector-valued
functions on
\Omega
which, together with their divergence, are
square integrable; i.e.,

H({\rm div};\Omega)=\left\{{\bf v}:\ {\bf v}\in[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{2},\nabla\cdot{\bf v}\in L^{2}(\Omega)\right\}.

Define an interpolation operator
\mathbb{Q}_{h}
for
\tau\in H({\rm div},\Omega)
(see
[
4
]
) such that

\mathbb{Q}_{h}\tau\in H({\rm div},\Omega)
,
\mathbb{Q}_{h}\tau\in RT_{k}(T)
on each
T\in{\cal T}_{h}
, and satisfies:

(14)

(\nabla\cdot\tau,\;v)_{T}=(\nabla\cdot\mathbb{Q}_{h}\tau,\;v)_{T}\qquad\forall v\in P_{k}(T).

Lemma 2
.

For any
\tau\in H({\rm div},\Omega)
,

(15)

-(\nabla\cdot\tau,\;v)_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}=(\mathbb{Q}_{h}\tau,\;\nabla_{w}v)_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\quad\forall v\in V_{h}^{0}.

Proof.

Since
\{v\}=v=0
on
\partial\Omega
and
\mathbb{Q}_{h}\tau\in H({\rm div},\Omega)
, then

(16)

\langle\mathbb{Q}_{h}\tau\cdot{\bf n},\{v\}\rangle_{\partial{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}=0.

It follows from (
14
), (
10
) and (
16
) that

\displaystyle-(\nabla\cdot\tau,\;v)_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(\nabla\cdot\mathbb{Q}_{h}\tau,\;v)_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(\nabla\cdot\mathbb{Q}_{h}\tau,\;v)_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}+{\langle}\{v\},\mathbb{Q}_{h}\tau\cdot{\bf n}{\rangle}_{\partial{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathbb{Q}_{h}\tau,\;\nabla_{w}v)_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}},

which proves the lemma.
∎

Define a continuous finite element space
\tilde{V}_{h}
, a subspace of
V_{h}
, by

(17)

\displaystyle\tilde{V}_{h}=\{v\in H^{1}(\Omega):\;v|_{T}\in P_{k}(T),\;\forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\}.

Lemma 3
.

For any
v\in\tilde{V}_{h}
,

\displaystyle\nabla_{w}v=\nabla v.

Proof.

By the definition of the weak gradient (
10
) and integration by parts, we have for any
\tau\in RT_{k}(T)
,

\displaystyle(\nabla_{w}v,\tau)_{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(v,\nabla\cdot\tau)_{T}+\langle\{v\},\tau\cdot{\bf n}\rangle_{\partial T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(v,\nabla\cdot\tau)_{T}+\langle v,\tau\cdot{\bf n}\rangle_{\partial T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\nabla v,\tau)_{T},

which implies

\displaystyle(\nabla_{w}v-\nabla v,\tau)_{T}=0,\quad\forall\tau\in RT_{k}(T).

Since
\nabla_{w}v-\nabla v\in RT_{k}(T)
, letting
\tau=\nabla_{w}v-\nabla v
in the above equation gives

\|\nabla_{w}v-\nabla v\|_{T}^{2}=0,

which proves the lemma.
∎

Let
e_{h}=I_{h}u-u_{h}
. Obviously,
e_{h}\in V_{h}^{0}
. Recall that
I_{h}u
is the
k
th order Lagrange interpolation of
u
and then
I_{h}u\in\tilde{V}_{h}
. By Lemma
3
, we have

(18)

\displaystyle\nabla_{w}I_{h}u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla I_{h}u.

Lemma 4
.

Let
e_{h}=I_{h}u-u_{h}
be the error of the finite element solution arising from (
9
). Then we have

(19)

\displaystyle(\nabla_{w}e_{h},\;\nabla_{w}v)_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle l(u,v),\quad\forall v\in V_{h}^{0},

where

(20)

\displaystyle l(u,v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\nabla I_{h}u-\mathbb{Q}_{h}\nabla u,\;\nabla_{w}v)_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}.

Proof.

Testing the equation (
1
) by
v\in V_{h}^{0}
gives

(21)

-(\nabla\cdot\nabla u,v)=(f,v).

It follows from (
15
) that

(22)

\displaystyle(\mathbb{Q}_{h}\nabla u,\nabla_{w}v)_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(f,v).

Adding
(\nabla_{w}I_{h}u,\nabla_{w}v)_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}
to the both sides of the equation (
22
) and using (
18
) yield

(23)

\displaystyle(\nabla_{w}I_{h}u,\nabla_{w}v)_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(f,v)+(\nabla I_{h}u-\mathbb{Q}_{h}\nabla u,\nabla_{w}v)_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}.

The difference of (
23
) and (
9
) gives (
19
).
We have proved the lemma.
∎

5
Error Estimates

In this section, we shall establish optimal order error estimates
for
u_{h}
in a discrete
H^{1}
norm and the
L^{2}
norm.

5.1
An Estimate in a Discrete
H^{1}
Norm

We start this subsection by bounding the term
l(u,v)
defined in (
20
).

Lemma 5
.

Let
u\in H^{k+1}(\Omega)
and

v\in V_{h}^{0}
. Then, the following estimate holds,

(24)

\displaystyle|l(u,v)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ch^{k}|u|_{k+1}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}v{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}.

Proof.

Using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the definition of
I_{h}
and
\mathbb{Q}_{h}
, we have

\displaystyle l(u,v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\nabla I_{h}u-\mathbb{Q}_{h}(\nabla u),\nabla_{w}v)_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\|\nabla I_{h}u-\mathbb{Q}_{h}(\nabla u)\|_{T}\|\nabla_{w}v\|_{T}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\|\nabla I_{h}u-\mathbb{Q}_{h}(\nabla u)\|_{T}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\|\nabla_{w}v\|_{T}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\|\nabla I_{h}u-\nabla u\|_{T}^{2}+\|\nabla u-\mathbb{Q}_{h}(\nabla u)\|_{T}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}v{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ch^{k}|u|_{k+1}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}v{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|},

which proves the lemma.
∎

Theorem 6
.

Let
u_{h}\in V_{h}
be the finite element solution of (
9
). Assume the exact solution
u\in H^{k+1}(\Omega)
. Then,
there exists a constant
C
such that

(25)

{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}u_{h}-I_{h}u{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}\leq Ch^{k}|u|_{k+1}.

Proof.

Letting
v=e_{h}
in (
19
) gives

(26)

\displaystyle{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}e_{h}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle l(u,e_{h}).

Using (
24
), we arrive

{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}e_{h}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}^{2}\leq Ch^{k}|u|_{k+1}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}e_{h}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|},

which completes the proof.
∎

5.2
An Estimate in the
L^{2}
Norm

In this subsection, we will derive the error estimate for
u_{h}
in the
L^{2}
norm.
First we define
\tilde{V}_{h}^{0}
a subspace of
\tilde{V}_{h}
in (
17
) as

(27)

\displaystyle\tilde{V}_{h}^{0}=\{v\in\tilde{V}_{h}:v|_{\partial\Omega}=0\}.

Let
\tilde{u}_{h}\in\tilde{V}_{h}
be the conforming finite element solution such that

\tilde{u}_{h}=I_{h}g
on
\partial\Omega
and satisfies

(28)

\displaystyle(\nabla\tilde{u}_{h},\nabla v)=(f,v)\quad\forall v\in\tilde{V}_{h}^{0}.

Since
\tilde{V}_{h}^{0}\subset V_{h}^{0}
, by Lemma
3
, (
9
) and (
28
), we have

(29)

\displaystyle(\nabla_{w}u_{h}-\nabla\tilde{u}_{h},\nabla v)=0,\quad\forall v\in\tilde{V}_{h}^{0}.

Consider the dual problem: seek
\Phi\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
satisfying

(30)

\displaystyle-\nabla\cdot(\nabla\Phi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u_{h}-\tilde{u}_{h}\quad\mbox{in}\;\Omega.

Assume that the following
H^{2}
-regularity holds

(31)

\|\Phi\|_{2}\leq C\|u_{h}-\tilde{u}_{h}\|.

Now we are ready to derive the
L^{2}
error estimate.

Theorem 7
.

Let
u_{h}\in V_{h}
be the finite element solution of (
9
). Assume that the
exact solution
u\in H^{k+1}(\Omega)
and that (
31
) holds true.
Then, there exists a constant
C
such that

(32)

\|u-u_{h}\|\leq Ch^{k+1}|u|_{k+1}.

Proof.

By the triangle inequality, we have

(33)

\|u-u_{h}\|\leq\|u-\tilde{u}_{h}\|+\|u_{h}-\tilde{u}_{h}\|.

The definition of
\tilde{u}_{h}
implies

(34)

\|u-\tilde{u}_{h}\|\leq Ch^{k+1}|u|_{k+1}.

Next we will estimate
\|u_{h}-\tilde{u}_{h}\|
.
Let
\Phi_{h}\in V_{h}^{0}
be the conforming DG approximation to the problem (
30
) satisfying

(35)

(\nabla_{w}\Phi_{h},\nabla_{w}v)=(u_{h}-\tilde{u}_{h},v),\quad\forall v\in V_{h}^{0}.

Letting
v=u_{h}-\tilde{u}_{h}\in V^{0}_{h}
in (
35
) and using Lemma
3
and (
29
), we have,

\displaystyle\|u_{h}-\tilde{u}_{h}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=(\nabla_{w}\Phi_{h},\nabla_{w}(u_{h}-\tilde{u}_{h}))_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}=(\nabla_{w}\Phi_{h},\nabla_{w}u_{h}-\nabla\tilde{u}_{h})_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}

\displaystyle=(\nabla_{w}(\Phi_{h}-I_{h}\Phi),\nabla_{w}u_{h}-\nabla\tilde{u}_{h})_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}.

By the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality, (
25
) and (
31
), then

\displaystyle\|u_{h}-\tilde{u}_{h}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}\Phi_{h}-I_{h}\Phi{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}\;({|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}u_{h}-I_{h}u{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}+\|\nabla(I_{h}u-\tilde{u}_{h})\|)

\displaystyle\leq Ch|\Phi|_{2}h^{k}|u|_{k+1}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{k+1}|u|_{k+1}\|u_{h}-\tilde{u}_{h}\|,

which implies

(36)

\|u_{h}-\tilde{u}_{h}\|\leq Ch^{k+1}|u|_{k+1}.

Combining (
34
) and (
36
) with (
33
), we have proved the theorem.
∎

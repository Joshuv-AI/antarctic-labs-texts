Sech-squared Pockels solitons in the microresonator parametric down-conversion Journal: oe Publication type: Research Article
Dmitry V. Skryabin \authormark 1,2,*

Abstract
We present an explicit sech-squared-soliton solution associated with the optical Pockels effect, achieved through the generation of the frequency combs via parametric down-conversion in optical microresonators with quadratic nonlinearity. This soliton contrasts the parametric sech-soliton describing the half-harmonic field in the limit of the large index mismatch, and associated with the cascaded-Kerr effect. We predict differences in the spectral profiles and powers of the Pockels and cascaded-Kerr solitons, and report that the pump power threshold of the former agree with the recent experimental observations.

Introduction

Nonlinear and quantum optics in ring microresonators have been attracting a great deal of attention over the past decades
[
2
]
. In particular, the frequency comb generation and soliton photonics in microresonators are reaching unprecedented levels of practical relevance
[
3

.
The most ubiquitous microresonator solitons remain the dissipative version of the Kerr solitons, which, thanks to the small losses of modern microresonators, remain well approximated by the fundamental bright solitons of the Nonlinear Schrödinger (NLS) equation

.

The second-order,
\chi^{(2)}
, nonlinearity has always been a viable alternative to the Kerr one.
The
\chi^{(2)}
microresonators have the potential to extend comb generation to new wavelengths, elevate dispersion constraints, and reduce power requirements
[
6
]
.
Using of the
\chi^{(2)}
response comes, however, with the caveats of
the need to care about the phase and group velocity matching to take full advantage of it.
One of the recent highlights in this area was the result by Bruch and colleagues
[
7
]
demonstrating
the solitons due to parametric down-conversion in an integrated
\chi^{(2)}
microresonator.
A sufficiently comprehensive list of references on the solitons in
\chi^{(2)}

resonators can be found in
[
8
]
.
For further recent and historic theoretical contributions on the bright and dark
solitons due to parametric-down conversion
see, e.g., Refs.
[

,
10
,

,
12
,
13
,
14
,
18
,
15
,
16
,
17
]
.

While Ref.
[
7
]
has used, or perhaps even coined, the term ’Pockels solitons’,
it is also known that the impact of the
\chi^{(2)}
nonlinearity on the refractive
index change depends on how well the index matching between the pump and either half- or second-harmonic is arranged. In the mismatched limit, the
\chi^{(2)}
susceptibility starts mimicking the Kerr effect, which could be called the cascaded-Kerr nonlinearity, see, e.g.,
[
8
]
. Thus, the transition between the
Pockels and the cascaded-Kerr solitons is a problem that requires further considerations.
Our focus here is on the bright soliton pulses.
It is known that, in the phase-mismatched cascaded-Kerr limit, the
\chi^{(2)}
microresonator model can be reduced to the parametric NLS equation
[
9
,
10
]
, which has the sech-soliton solution
[
9
,
10
,
19
,
20
,
21
,
18
]
. One outstanding problem would be finding the
solution for the bright parametric solitons in the practically important case of the phase-matched resonators.

Any intense intra-resonator waveform produces the nonlinear shift of the resonance frequencies. For the Kerr or Kerr-like solitons, this shift is naturally expected to be proportional to the power.
This work aims to demonstrate that, in the phase-matched microresonator parametric-down conversion, there exists an explicit bright soliton solution producing the nonlinear resonance shift proportional to the field amplitude, i.e., like should be expected in the optical Pockels effect.
The spatial profile of this soliton is given by the sech-squared function, unlike the sech-profile of the Kerr soliton.
We note before proceeding, that the optical Pockels effect discussed below should be distinguished from the voltage controlled one, which is broadly used in Pockels cells, and as a frequency comb generation tool
[
22
,
23
]
.

2
Model

Our model of the multi-mode half-harmonic generation in a microresonator
follows the approach of Ref.
[
8
]
. The fundamental (pump)
field, ’f’, is spectrally centred around the pump laser frequency,

\omega_{p}
, and the half-harmonic field, ’h’, is around
\omega_{p}/2
. The fields and
detunings are defined as

\begin{split}&\text{fundamental/pump:}~~~~\psi_{\text{f}}(\vartheta,t)\times\exp\left\{i2\vartheta J-it\omega_{p}\right\}+c.c.,\\
&\text{half-harmonic:}~~~~~~~~~~~\psi_{\text{h}}(\vartheta,t)\times\exp\left\{i\vartheta J-\frac{it\omega_{p}}{2}\right\}+c.c.,\\
&\text{half-harmonic detuning:}~~\omega_{0\text{h}}-\frac{\omega_{p}}{2}=\delta,\\
&\text{pump detuning:}~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\omega_{0\text{f}}-\omega_{p}=2\delta-\varepsilon.\end{split}

(1)

Here,
\psi_{\text{f}}
and
\psi_{\text{h}}
are the envelope functions,

\vartheta\in[0,2\pi)
is the angular coordinate along the ring, and

J
and
2J
are the resonator mode numbers with the frequencies
\omega_{0\text{h}}
and
\omega_{0\text{f}}
,
respectively.

\varepsilon
is the frequency mismatch parameter,

\varepsilon=2\omega_{0\text{h}}-\omega_{0\text{f}}=2J\frac{c}{R}\left[\frac{1}{n_{J}}-\frac{1}{n_{2J}}\right].

(2)

Here,
n_{J}
is the effective refractive index felt by the mode
J
,

c
is the vacuum speed of light, and
R
is the resonator radius.
Requiring
\varepsilon=0
,
yields the anticipated index matching condition,
n_{J}=n_{2J}
.
Refractive index, and hence
\varepsilon
, can be fine-tuned by, e.g., temperature
or electro-optic controls. Small nonlinear shifts of the refractive index proportional to either power
(optical Kerr effect) or the field amplitude (optical Pockels effect)
translate, via the standard Taylor series, to the respective shifts of the resonance frequencies.

Dispersion engineering is important for any type of modelocking,
including the soliton modelocking. We set

D_{1\text{f},1\text{h}}/2\pi
as the repetition-rate (free-spectral-ranges, FSR),
and
D_{2\text{f},2\text{h}}
as the dispersion parameters having units of Hz
[
8
]
.
The FSR difference,
D_{1\text{f}}-D_{1\text{h}}
, scales inversely with
R
, while

D_{2\text{f},2\text{h}}\sim 1/R^{2}

[
24
]
.
Therefore going from the mm to 10’s of micron radii leads to the relative reduction of the impact of the FSR difference on the dynamics,
|D_{1\text{f}}-D_{1\text{h}}|/|D_{2\text{f}}|\sim R
.
The group-velocity matching can be implemented either across the zero-dispersion point for the modes of the same family or, for the same signs of
D_{2\text{f},2\text{h}}
, using the different mode families and the avoided mode-crossings, see, e.g.,
[
25
,
26
,
27
,
28
]
. Applications of the quasi-phase matching
[
29
,
30
]
and optical fibres
[
31
,
32
,
33
]

offer further avenues for dispersion engineering in
\chi^{(2)}
resonators.
In order to derive transparent analytical results for Pockels solitons, we consider the case of the FSR, i.e., group velocity, matching,
D_{1\text{f}}=D_{1\text{h}}=D_{1}
, for the same dispersion signs, i.e.,
D_{2\text{f}}/D_{2\text{h}}>0
. The case of the opposite dispersion signs,
D_{2\text{f}}/D_{2\text{h}}<0
, is also proceedable analytically, but for the reasons of brevity we prefer to keep this work focused on the former.

Transforming to the rotating frame of reference,

\theta=\vartheta-D_{1}t
, the equations for
\psi_{\text{f},\text{h}}
are
[
8
]

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}\psi_{\text{h}}

\displaystyle=\delta\psi_{\text{h}}-\tfrac{1}{2}D_{2\text{h}}\partial^{2}_{\theta}\psi_{\text{h}}-\gamma_{\text{h}}\psi_{\text{f}}\psi_{\text{h}}^{*}-i\tfrac{1}{2}\kappa_{\text{h}}\psi_{\text{h}},

(3a)

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}\psi_{\text{f}}

\displaystyle=(2\delta-\varepsilon)\psi_{\text{f}}-\tfrac{1}{2}D_{2\text{f}}\partial^{2}_{\theta}\psi_{\text{f}}-\gamma_{\text{f}}\psi_{\text{h}}^{2}-i\tfrac{1}{2}\kappa_{\text{f}}\big(\psi_{\text{f}}-{\cal H}\big),

(3b)

where
\kappa_{\text{f},\text{h}}
are the linewidths, and

{\cal H}
is the pump parameter defined as

{\cal H}=\sqrt{\frac{\eta{\cal F}{\cal W}}{\pi}}=\frac{1}{\kappa_{\text{f}}}\sqrt{\frac{\widehat{\kappa}_{\text{f}}D_{1\text{f}}{\cal W}}{\pi}}.

(4)

Here,
\eta=\widehat{\kappa}_{\text{f}}/\kappa_{\text{f}}<1
is the coupling coefficient,
\widehat{\kappa}_{\text{f}}
is the intrinsic resonator linewidth, and
{\cal F}=D_{1\text{f}}/\kappa_{\text{f}}
is finesse.
{\cal W}
is the ’on-chip’ laser power.

|\psi_{\text{f},\text{h}}|^{2}
have units of Watts, and the
nonlinear parameters
\gamma_{\text{f},\text{h}}/2\pi\sim\chi^{(2)}

are measured in Hz
/\sqrt{\text{W}}

[
8
]
.
Note, that
\kappa_{\text{f}}{\cal H}
does not depend on
\kappa_{\text{f}}
.

While the results derived below could be a guideline for a range of devices, our
choice of parameters for the numerical estimates
is geared towards the integrated resonators as used in Ref.
[
7
]
.
Namely, we assume
\kappa_{\text{f},\text{h}}/2\pi=100
MHz,
D_{1\text{f},1\text{h}}/2\pi=300
GHz,

D_{2\text{f},2\text{h}}/2\pi=30
MHz,
\gamma_{\text{f},\text{h}}/2\pi=500
MHz/
\sqrt{\text{W}}
, and
\eta=1/2
.
The linewidth here is two orders of magnitudes larger than in the high-Q bulk resonator samples
[
2
]
,
this explains relatively high pump power,
{\cal W}=80
mW, required to generate solitons in Ref.
[
7
]
.
The sech-squared soliton derived below fits well into the above range of the parameter values,
while the sech one requires even higher input powers
{\cal W}\sim 1
W. This is because the Pockels nonlinear response is achieved under the phase matching conditions, i.e.,
|\varepsilon|/\kappa_{\text{h}}\sim 1
, while the sech-soliton is associated with the cascaded-Kerr nonlinear response triggered for
|\varepsilon|/\kappa_{\text{h}}\sim 10^{2}
. The large
|\varepsilon|
lead to the inefficient conversion and push the soliton thresholds up.

3
Sech-squared soliton: Pockels regime

To find the Pockels soliton we first neglect the loss terms by assuming
|\delta|\gg\kappa_{\text{h}}
,
|2\delta-\varepsilon|\gg\kappa_{\text{f}}
. Practically, it suffices to take
|\delta|/\kappa_{\text{h}}\sim 10
and

|\varepsilon|/\kappa_{\text{h}}\lesssim 1
. Then, Eq. (
3
) become

\displaystyle\delta\psi_{\text{h}}

\displaystyle-\tfrac{1}{2}D_{2\text{h}}\partial^{2}_{\theta}\psi_{\text{h}}-\gamma_{\text{h}}\psi_{\text{f}}\psi_{\text{h}}^{*}=0,

(5a)

\displaystyle(2\delta-\varepsilon)\psi_{\text{f}}

\displaystyle-\tfrac{1}{2}D_{2\text{f}}\partial^{2}_{\theta}\psi_{\text{f}}-\gamma_{\text{f}}\psi_{\text{h}}^{2}=i\tfrac{1}{2}\kappa_{\text{f}}{\cal H}.

(5b)

The soliton solution is sought in the form

\displaystyle\psi_{\text{h}}=B~\psi(\theta)e^{i\phi/2},

(6a)

\displaystyle\psi_{\text{f}}=\psi(\theta)e^{i\phi}-iH,

(6b)

so that the half-harmonic pulse has the vanishing background and the fundamental has the finite one.
Here, the soliton profile
\psi(\theta)
, the phase
\phi
, the dimensionless constant
B
, and
the background field
H
, should be determined. We note that the existence of the zero solution (vanishing background) for
\psi_{\text{h}}
also holds for the full model, see Eq. (
3
).

We substitute Eq. (
6
) in Eqs. (
5
), and
separate the real and imaginary parts.
The first outcomes of this procedure are the explicit expressions for the soliton background

H=\frac{\kappa_{\text{f}}{\cal H}}{2(2\delta-\varepsilon)},

(7)

and for the phase,
\phi=\pm\pi/2
. The
\phi=-\pi/2
makes the soliton part of
\psi_{\text{f}}
in Eq. (
6b
) to be in-phase with the background field, which is known to correspond to the always unstable soliton family. The
\phi=\pi/2
solution is
\pi
-out-of-phase with the background and is a largely stable one
[
16
,
20
]
.

The further result of the substitution is a pair of the differential equations for
\psi
,

\displaystyle\delta\psi-

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}D_{2\text{h}}\partial^{2}_{\theta}\psi-\gamma_{\text{h}}\psi^{2}=0,

(8a)

\displaystyle(2\delta-\varepsilon)\psi-

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}D_{2\text{f}}\partial^{2}_{\theta}\psi-\gamma_{\text{f}}B^{2}\psi^{2}=0.

(8b)

The solution of Eq. (
8a
) is defined here as the sech-squared Pockels soliton,

\psi=\frac{3\delta}{2\gamma_{\text{h}}}\text{sech}^{2}\left(\theta\sqrt{\frac{\delta}{2D_{2\text{h}}}}\right),~\delta D_{2\text{h}}>0.

(9)

The validity of Eq. (
9
) can be verified by substitution.
Thus, if dispersion is normal, then the sech-squared solitons exist for

\delta<0
and, for
\delta>0
, if dispersion is anomalous.
The sech-squared soliton can be found among the in-line equations in the
paper by Karamzin and Sukhorukov on the beam diffraction in a

\chi^{(2)}
crystal
[
34
]
, and it also
has been later found and elaborated on by others, see, e.g.,
[
35
,
36
]
. The results derived by us here
are a generalization of Ref.
[
34
]
that incorporates the pump term (this section) and the linewidth (section
4
), and thereby shows how the sech-squared solitons become relevant in the resonators, in general, and microresonators, in particular.

\psi
in Eq. (
9
) must simultaneously solve Eqs. (
8b
) and (
8a
), which
requires acknowledging the following conditions

\frac{\delta}{D_{2\text{h}}}=\frac{2\delta-\varepsilon}{D_{2\text{f}}},~\frac{\gamma_{\text{h}}}{D_{2\text{h}}}=\frac{\gamma_{\text{f}}B^{2}}{D_{2\text{f}}}.

(10)

The latter of the above fixes the, so far free, parameter
B
, see Eq. (
6
),
and implies that the dispersions
are either both normal or both anomalous, i.e.,
D_{2\text{h}}/D_{2\text{f}}>0
.
The first of the conditions in Eq. (
10
) restricts the combination of the detuning, frequency mismatch, and dispersions necessary for the analytic solution to exist.

We first look into how the peak power of the sech-squared soliton scales with the detuning by taking
the important practical case of the exact phase matching,
\varepsilon=0
.
Fixing dispersions to comply with Eq. (
10
),
D_{2\text{f}}/D_{2\text{h}}=2
,
is a soft assumption to make for the sake of dealing with a transparent analytical solution.
For
\delta/\kappa_{\text{h}}=10
, the peak soliton power
in the fundamental and half-harmonic fields are

\displaystyle\max|\psi_{\text{f}}|^{2}=\frac{9\delta^{2}}{4\gamma_{\text{h}}^{2}}\approx 9\text{W},

(11a)

\displaystyle\max|\psi_{\text{h}}|^{2}=\frac{9\delta^{2}}{2\gamma_{\text{h}}^{2}}\approx 18\text{W}.

(11b)

For
{\cal W}=80
mW, we find that
{\cal H}^{2}\approx 40
W, and
the power of the soliton background is

\kappa_{\text{f}}^{2}{\cal H}^{2}/16\delta^{2}=25
mW, see Eq. (
7
).
We note that the peak powers
of the half-harmonic pulse are proportional to
\delta^{2}
, hence,
\max|\psi_{\text{h}}|\sim\delta=\omega_{0\text{h}}-\omega_{p}/2
, and therefore the shift
\omega_{0\text{h}}
and the effective index change, see Eq. (
2
), induced by the soliton are proportional to the field amplitude, so that, we are indeed dealing with the Pockels regime of the resonator operation.

The total power and the spectrum of the half-harmonic component of the sech-squared Pockels soliton are

\displaystyle{\cal P}=B^{2}\int^{2\pi}_{0}|\psi_{\text{h}}|^{2}d\theta

\displaystyle={\cal P}_{\text{Pock}}~\left[\frac{|\delta|}{\kappa_{\text{h}}}\right]^{3/2},

(12a)

\displaystyle{\cal P}_{\text{Pock}}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{6\kappa_{\text{h}}\sqrt{2\kappa_{\text{h}}D_{2\text{h}}}}{\gamma_{\text{h}}^{2}}\approx 0.2\text{W},

(12b)

and

S_{\mu}\sim\Big|\int_{0}^{2\pi}\psi_{\text{h}}e^{i\mu\theta}\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}\Big|^{2}\sim\left(\frac{\mu/\mu_{0}}{\text{sinh}~\mu/\mu_{0}}\right)^{2},~~~\mu_{0}=\frac{1}{\pi}\sqrt{\frac{\delta}{2D_{2\text{h}}}},

(13)

respectively. The integrals above have been calculated by extending the zero-
2\pi
interval
to the infinity and by applying the table integrals found in Ref.
[
37
]
. The difference of
S_{\mu}
in Eq. (
13
) with
the triangular spectrum of the sech-soliton is illustrated in Fig.
1
, and
the comparative discussion is included in Section
5
.

4
Limits of existence of sech-squared solitons

Accepting a small level of complication of the algebra as a necessity lets us to trace the effects of the so far neglected loss terms. This needs to
be limited by the half-harmonic loss,
\kappa_{\text{h}}
,
which, however, reveals the net effect well, and allows
to remain within the comfortably transparent analytical considerations.
Eqs. (
5
) are now replaced with

\displaystyle\delta\psi_{\text{h}}

\displaystyle-\tfrac{1}{2}D_{2\text{h}}\partial^{2}_{\theta}\psi_{\text{h}}-\gamma_{\text{h}}\psi_{\text{f}}\psi_{\text{h}}^{*}=i\tfrac{1}{2}\kappa_{\text{h}}\psi_{\text{h}},

(14a)

\displaystyle(2\delta-\varepsilon)\psi_{\text{f}}

\displaystyle-\tfrac{1}{2}D_{2\text{f}}\partial^{2}_{\theta}\psi_{\text{f}}-\gamma_{\text{f}}\psi_{\text{h}}^{2}=i\tfrac{1}{2}\kappa_{\text{f}}{\cal H}.

(14b)

The substitution is again as in Eq. (
6
) and the background amplitude as in Eq. (
7
).
The equations for the phase become nontrivial

\cos\phi=\frac{\kappa_{\text{h}}(2\delta-\varepsilon)}{\kappa_{\text{f}}\gamma_{\text{h}}{\cal H}},~\sin\phi=\pm\sqrt{1-\cos^{2}\phi}

(15)

In the limit
\kappa_{\text{h}}\to 0
considered in the previous section, the
\sin\phi=-\sqrt{~}
state corresponds to
\phi\to-\pi/2
, i.e., the soliton is in-phase with the background field, which is
the always unstable soliton family. The
\sin\phi=+\sqrt{~}
,
\phi\to\pi/2
soliton
is
\pi
-out-of-phase with the background and can be stable
[
16
,
20
]
,
see similar comments after Eq. (
7
).

Eqs. (
6
), (
14
), (
15
) lead
to the refreshed system for
\psi

\displaystyle(2\delta-\varepsilon)\psi

\displaystyle-\tfrac{1}{2}D_{2\text{f}}\partial^{2}_{\theta}\psi-\gamma_{\text{f}}B^{2}\psi^{2}=0,

(16a)

\displaystyle\left(\delta+\tfrac{1}{2}\kappa_{\text{h}}\tan\phi\right)\psi

\displaystyle-\tfrac{1}{2}D_{2\text{h}}\partial^{2}_{\theta}\psi-\gamma_{\text{h}}\psi^{2}=0.

(16b)

The soliton solution of Eq. (

) is then,

\psi=\frac{3\bar{\delta}}{2\gamma_{\text{h}}}\text{sech}^{2}\left(\theta\sqrt{\frac{\bar{\delta}}{2D_{2\text{h}}}}\right),~~\bar{\delta}=\delta+\frac{\kappa_{\text{h}}}{2}\tan\phi,

(17)

where
\bar{\delta}
takes two values, see Eq. (
15
),

\bar{\delta}_{\pm}=\delta\pm\frac{\kappa_{\text{h}}}{2}\sqrt{\frac{\kappa_{\text{f}}^{2}\gamma_{\text{h}}^{2}{\cal H}^{2}}{\kappa_{\text{h}}^{2}(2\delta-\varepsilon)^{2}}-1}.

(18)

\bar{\delta}_{+}=\bar{\delta}_{-}=\delta_{\lim}
when the square root becomes zero,

(19)

which sets the existence limit for the sech-squared solitons.
If, e.g.,

(anomalous dispersion) and

,
then one should choose plus in the above. The compatibility of Eqs. (

) and (

),
requires replacing the first condition in Eq. (

) with

\bar{\delta}/D_{2\text{h}}=(2\delta-\varepsilon)/D_{2\text{f}}
.

\kappa_{\text{h}}
, are shown in Fig.

(a).
The input power is chosen to be

mW as in Ref.
[

]
, which also
provides the soliton existence around
\delta/\kappa_{\text{h}}\approx 10
fitting with our assumptions.

5
Sech soliton: cascaded-Kerr regime

The parametric sech-solitons in resonators
[
18
,
9
]
are like the ones previously found in
a few other physical contexts, see, e.g., Refs.
[
19
,
20
,
21
,
18
]
. They
can be derived via the reduction of Eq. (
3
) to the parametric NLS equation.
The reduction starts from the assumption that the resonator is pumped well away from the resonance,
|\varepsilon|\gg|\delta|
, while the half-harmonic is generated close to the resonance.
This practically inefficient phase-mismatched arrangement allows to approximately resolve Eq. (
3b
) with

\psi_{\text{f}}\approx\frac{\gamma_{\text{f}}\psi_{\text{h}}^{2}-i\tfrac{1}{2}\kappa_{\text{f}}{\cal H}}{-\varepsilon},

(20)

so that
Eq. (
3a
) becomes

i\partial_{t}\psi_{\text{h}}=\delta\psi_{\text{h}}-\frac{1}{2}D_{2\text{h}}\partial^{2}_{\theta}\psi_{\text{h}}+\frac{\gamma_{\text{f}}\gamma_{\text{h}}}{\varepsilon}|\psi_{\text{h}}|^{2}\psi_{\text{h}}-i\frac{\kappa_{\text{f}}\gamma_{\text{h}}{\cal H}}{2\varepsilon}\psi_{\text{h}}^{*}-i\frac{1}{2}\kappa_{\text{h}}\psi_{\text{h}}.

(21)

Now, the half-harmonic experiences the effective Kerr, i.e.,
cascaded-Kerr, nonlinearity. For
\varepsilon<0
,
the cascaded-Kerr effect is positive, i.e., focusing, and for
\varepsilon>0
it is negative.
To be consistent with Section
3
, we first neglect the
\kappa_{\text{f},\text{h}}
terms, which works well if

|\varepsilon|\gg|\delta|\gg\kappa_{\text{f},\text{h}}
, e.g., for
\varepsilon/\kappa_{\text{h}}=-10^{2}
and
\delta/\kappa_{\text{h}}=10
.
Then, the exact soliton solution of Eq. (
21
) is

\psi_{\text{h}}=\sqrt{\frac{-2\delta\varepsilon}{\gamma_{\text{f}}\gamma_{\text{h}}}}\times\text{sech}\left(\theta\sqrt{\frac{2\delta}{D_{2\text{h}}}}\right)e^{\pm i\pi/4},~\delta\varepsilon<0,~\delta D_{2\text{h}}>0.

(22)

For the sech solitons to exist, the signs of dispersion, detuning, and of the mismatch parameters must be correlated as indicated above. The peak powers of the cascaded-Kerr soliton are

\displaystyle\max|\psi_{\text{f}}|^{2}=\frac{4\delta^{2}}{\gamma_{\text{h}}^{2}}\approx 16\text{W},

(23a)

\displaystyle\max|\psi_{\text{h}}|^{2}=\frac{2|\delta||\varepsilon|}{\gamma_{\text{f}}\gamma_{\text{h}}}\approx 80\text{W}.

(23b)

Now, the peak power of the half-harmonic,
\max|\psi_{\text{h}}|^{2}
, is growing proportionally to
\delta
, cf., Eq. (
11
). This is the same as for the non-parametric NLS solitons in Kerr microresonators
[
3
,
4
]
,
and is consistent with the change of the effective index,
n_{J}
, being proportional
to the pump power as it should be expected in the optical Kerr effect. The intrinsic

Summary

We have elaborated the explicit sech-squared Pockels solitons describing the soliton regime of the phase and group velocity matched parametric down-conversion in microresonators with either normal or anomalous dispersion. We have compared these solitons with the cascaded-Kerr sech-solitons existing away from the phase-matching condition and revealed the
differences in (i) the values of the threshold powers, see Eqs. (
26
), (
27
),
(ii) scaling of the soliton power with the detuning parameter, see Fig.
2
, and
(iii) how the Pockels-soliton spectra deviate from the triangular spectra of the sech-solitons, see Fig.
1
and, cf., Eqs. (
13
) and (
25
).
The sech and sech-squared shapes of the half-harmonic field are
embedded, as the two limit cases, inside the wider and numerically accessible family of the bright soliton solutions, see, e.g.,
[

,

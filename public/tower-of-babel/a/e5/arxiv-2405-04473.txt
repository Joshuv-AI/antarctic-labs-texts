Abstract
. We prove nonlinear Landau damping in optimal weighted Gevrey-3 spaces for solutions of the confined Vlasov-Poisson system on 𝕋 d × ℝ d \mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d} which are small perturbations of homogeneous Penrose-stable equilibria. We also prove the existence of nonlinear scattering operators associated to the confined Vlasov-Poisson evolution, as well as suitable injectivity properties and Lipschitz estimates (also in weighted Gevrey-3 spaces) on these operators. Our results give definitive answers to two well-known open problems in the field, both of them stated in the recent review of Bedrossian [ 4 , Section 6] .



1.
Introduction

In this article we investigate asymptotic properties of solutions of the classical Vlasov-Poisson system. In the confined case the electron distribution is modeled by a function
F=F(t,x,v):I\times\mathbb{T}^{d}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}_{v}\to\mathbb{R}
, where
I\subseteq\mathbb{R}
is an interval,
\mathbb{T}:=\mathbb{R}/(2\pi\mathbb{Z})
, and
d\geq 1
, evolving according to the nondimensionalized Vlasov-Poisson system

(1.1)

\begin{split}\left(\partial_{t}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}\right)F+\nabla_{x}\phi\cdot\nabla_{v}F=0,\qquad\Delta_{x}\phi=\rho=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}Fdv-1.\end{split}

Our goal is to investigate the global nonlinear stability properties (Landau damping) of solutions around a
spatially homogeneous equilibrium

M_{0}=M_{0}(v)
normalized such that

(1.2)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}M_{0}(v)\,dv=1.

Looking for solutions of (
1.1
) of the form
F=M_{0}+f
, we obtain the system

(1.3)

\begin{split}&\left(\partial_{t}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}\right)f+E\cdot\nabla_{v}f+E\cdot\nabla_{v}M_{0}=0,\\
&E:=\nabla_{x}\Delta_{x}^{-1}\rho,\qquad\rho(t,x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(t,x,v)\,dv.\end{split}

1.1.
The main theorems

The free transport equation
\partial_{t}f+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f=0
exhibits phase mixing, which leads to time decay of the spatial density
\rho
. It was a fundamental observation of Landau
[
36
]
(see also
[
37
, Chapter 3]
) that an interesting mechanism of decay exists also in the linearized Vlasov-Poisson equations near homogeneous equilibria satisfying certain conditions (nowadays called ”Penrose criterion”
[
45
]
, see (
1.7
) below).

The classical mechanism for nonlinear stability involves trading regularity for decay. In order to state our main theorems we need some definitions. For any
\lambda,s\in(0,1]
we define the Gevrey spaces
\mathcal{G}^{\lambda,s}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
induced by the norms

(1.4)

\|g\|_{\mathcal{G}^{\lambda,s}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}:=\big\|\widehat{g}(k,\xi)e^{\lambda\langle k,\xi\rangle^{s}}\big\|_{L^{2}_{k,\xi}},

where
\langle k,\xi\rangle:=\sqrt{1+|k|^{2}+|\xi|^{2}}
and
\widehat{g}:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
denotes the Fourier transform of the function
g
,

\widehat{g}(k,\xi):=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(x,v)e^{-ik\cdot x}e^{-i\xi\cdot v}\,dxdv.

For
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
we define also the weighted Gevrey spaces
\mathcal{G}^{\lambda,s}_{n}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
induced by the norms

(1.5)

\|g(x,v)\|_{\mathcal{G}^{\lambda,s}_{n}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}:=\sum_{|a|\leq n}\|D^{a}_{\xi}\widehat{g}(k,\xi)e^{\lambda\langle k,\xi\rangle^{s}}\|_{\mathcal{G}^{\lambda,s}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

We also define the spaces
\mathcal{G}^{\lambda,s}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
,
\mathcal{G}^{\lambda,s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and
\mathcal{G}^{\lambda,s}_{n}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
in a similar fashion. For simplicity of notation, we sometimes let
\mathcal{G}^{\lambda,s}_{n}:=\mathcal{G}^{\lambda,s}_{n}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

We assume that there are constants
\lambda_{0},\vartheta\in(0,1]
such that the homogenous equilibrium
M_{0}
satisfies the smoothness and decay bounds

(1.6)

\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{\lambda_{0}\langle\xi\rangle^{1/3}}\big\{\big|\widehat{M_{0}}(\xi)\big|+|\xi|\,\big|\nabla_{\xi}\widehat{M}_{0}(\xi)\big|\big\}+\|\nabla_{v}M_{0}\|_{\mathcal{G}^{\lambda_{0},1/3}_{d^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\vartheta^{-1},

where
d^{\prime}:=\lfloor d/2+1\rfloor
, and the
Penrose criterion

(1.7)

\inf_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\},\,\tau\in\mathbb{C},\,\Im(\tau)\leq 0}\Big|1+\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-i\tau s}s\widehat{M_{0}}(sk)\,ds\Big|\geq\vartheta>0.

To state our main results it is convenient to define the kinetic profile

(1.8)

g(t,x,v):=f(t,x+tv,v).

In terms of the profile
g
, the Vlasov-Poisson system (
1.3
) becomes

(1.9)

\begin{split}&\partial_{t}g(t,x,v)+E(t,x+tv)\cdot\nabla_{v}M_{0}(v)+E(t,x+tv)\cdot(\nabla_{v}-t\nabla_{x})g(t,x,v)=0,\\
&E:=\nabla_{x}\Delta_{x}^{-1}\rho,\qquad\rho(t,x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(t,x-tv,v)\,dv.\end{split}

Our first main theorem concerns the global nonlinear stability of solutions of the Vlasov-Poisson system (
1.3
).

Theorem 1.1
.

(Nonlinear Landau damping) Assume
d\geq 1
,
\lambda_{0},\vartheta\in(0,1]
, and
M_{0}
is a homogeneous equilibrium satisfying the bounds (
1.6
)–(
1.7
).

(i) There is a constant
\overline{\kappa}=\overline{\kappa}(d,\lambda_{0},\vartheta)>0
with the property that if
f_{0}
is a Gevrey-smooth, small, and neutral perturbation satisfying

(1.10)

\|f_{0}\|_{\mathcal{G}_{d^{\prime}}^{\lambda_{0},1/3}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\kappa_{0}\leq\overline{\kappa},\qquad\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{0}(x,v)\,dxdv=0

then there is a unique global solution
g\in C([0,\infty):\mathcal{G}_{d^{\prime}}^{3\lambda_{0}/4,1/3})
of the equation (
1.3
) with initial data
f(0)=f_{0}
. Moreover
\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(t,x,v)\,dxdv=0
for any
t\in[0,\infty)
, and there is a function
g_{\infty}\in\mathcal{G}_{d^{\prime}}^{3\lambda_{0}/4,1/3}
(the final state) such that

(1.11)

\|\rho(t)\|_{\mathcal{G}^{\lambda_{0}/2,1/3}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}+\|g(t)-g_{\infty}\|_{\mathcal{G}^{\lambda_{0}/2,1/3}_{d^{\prime}}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq A\kappa_{0}e^{-\lambda_{0}\langle t\rangle^{1/3}/4}

for any
t\in[0,\infty)
, where
A=A(d,\lambda_{0},\vartheta)\in[1,\overline{\kappa}^{-1}]
is a constant,
\rho
denotes the macroscopic density defined in (
1.3
), and
g
denotes the profile defined in (
1.8
).

(ii) The solution map
f_{0}\to g
is a continuous map from
B_{\overline{\kappa}}(\mathcal{G}_{d^{\prime}}^{\lambda_{0},1/3})
to
C\big([0,\infty):\mathcal{G}_{d^{\prime}}^{3\lambda_{0}/4,1/3}\big)
. In fact, for any
f_{01},f_{02}\in B_{\overline{\kappa}}(\mathcal{G}_{d^{\prime}}^{\lambda_{0},1/3})
and
t\in[0,\infty)
we have the Lipschitz estimates

(1.12)

A^{-1}\|f_{01}-f_{02}\|_{\mathcal{G}_{d^{\prime}}^{\lambda_{0}/2,1/3}}\leq\|(g_{1}-g_{2})(t)\|_{\mathcal{G}_{d^{\prime}}^{3\lambda_{0}/4,1/3}}\leq A\|f_{01}-f_{02}\|_{\mathcal{G}_{d^{\prime}}^{\lambda_{0},1/3}}.

In particular, the final state operator
\mathcal{S}_{+}(f_{0}):=g_{\infty}
is a Lipschitz continuous and injective map from
B_{\overline{\kappa}}(\mathcal{G}_{d^{\prime}}^{\lambda_{0},1/3})
to
\mathcal{G}_{d^{\prime}}^{3\lambda_{0}/4,1/3}
.

Our second main theorem concerns the solvability of the final state problem, and the construction of injective wave operators satisfying Lipschitz bounds.

Theorem 1.2
.

(The final state problem) Assume
d
,
\lambda_{0}
,
\vartheta
, and
M_{0}
are as in Theorem
1.1
. Then there is a constant
\overline{\kappa}^{\prime}=\overline{\kappa}^{\prime}(d,\lambda_{0},\vartheta)>0
with the property that if
g_{\infty}
satisfies

(1.13)

\|g_{\infty}\|_{\mathcal{G}_{d^{\prime}}^{\lambda_{0},1/3}}\leq\overline{\kappa}^{\prime},\qquad\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}g_{\infty}(x,v)\,dxdv=0

then there is a unique solution
g\in C([0,\infty):\mathcal{G}_{d^{\prime}}^{3\lambda_{0}/4,1/3})
of the system (
1.9
) satisfying

(1.14)

\lim_{t\to\infty}\|g(t)-g_{\infty}\|_{\mathcal{G}_{d^{\prime}}^{3\lambda_{0}/4,1/3}}=0,\qquad\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(t,x,v)\,dxdv=0.

Moreover, for any
g_{\infty 1},g_{\infty 2}\in B_{\overline{\kappa}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{G}_{d^{\prime}}^{\lambda_{0},1/3})
and
t\in[0,\infty)
we have the bi-Lipschitz estimates

(1.15)

(A^{\prime})^{-1}\|g_{\infty 1}-g_{\infty 2}\|_{\mathcal{G}_{d^{\prime}}^{\lambda_{0}/2,1/3}}\leq\|(g_{1}-g_{2})(t)\|_{\mathcal{G}_{d^{\prime}}^{3\lambda_{0}/4,1/3}}\leq A^{\prime}\|g_{\infty 1}-g_{\infty 2}\|_{\mathcal{G}_{d^{\prime}}^{\lambda_{0},1/3}},

for some constant
A^{\prime}=A^{\prime}(d,\lambda_{0},\vartheta)\in[1,1/\overline{\kappa}^{\prime}]
. In particular, the wave operator
\mathcal{W}_{+}(g_{\infty}):=g(0)
is a Lipschitz continuous and injective map from
B_{\overline{\kappa}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{G}_{d^{\prime}}^{\lambda_{0},1/3})
to
\mathcal{G}_{d^{\prime}}^{3\lambda_{0}/4,1/3}
.

Finally, we can combine Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
to prove the existence of nonlinear scattering operators associated to the confined Vlasov-Poisson system. More precisely:

Theorem 1.3
.

(The scattering operator) Assume
d
,
\lambda_{0}
,
\vartheta
, and
M_{0}
are as in Theorem
1.1
. Then there is a constant
\overline{\kappa}^{\prime\prime}=\overline{\kappa}^{\prime\prime}(d,\lambda_{0},\vartheta)>0
with the property that if
g_{-\infty}
satisfies

(1.16)

\|g_{-\infty}\|_{\mathcal{G}_{d^{\prime}}^{\lambda_{0},1/3}}\leq\overline{\kappa}^{\prime\prime},\qquad\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}g_{-\infty}(x,v)\,dxdv=0

then there is a unique neutral solution
g\in C(\mathbb{R}:\mathcal{G}_{d^{\prime}}^{\lambda_{0}/2,1/3})
of the system (
1.9
) satisfying

(1.17)

\lim_{t\to-\infty}g(t)=g_{-\infty}\text{ in }\mathcal{G}_{d^{\prime}}^{\lambda_{0}/2,1/3}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\lim_{t\to\infty}g(t)=g_{\infty}\text{ in }\mathcal{G}_{d^{\prime}}^{\lambda_{0}/2,1/3}.

Moreover the scattering operator
\mathcal{S}(g_{-\infty}):=g_{\infty}
is a Lipschitz continuous and injective map from
B_{\overline{\kappa}^{\prime\prime}}(\mathcal{G}_{d^{\prime}}^{\lambda_{0},1/3})
to
\mathcal{G}_{d^{\prime}}^{\lambda_{0}/2,1/3}
. In fact, for any
g_{-\infty 1},g_{-\infty 2}\in B_{\overline{\kappa}^{\prime\prime}}(\mathcal{G}_{d^{\prime}}^{\lambda_{0},1/3})
we have

(1.18)

(A^{\prime\prime})^{-1}\|g_{-\infty 1}-g_{-\infty 2}\|_{\mathcal{G}_{d^{\prime}}^{\lambda_{0}/4,1/3}}\leq\|g_{\infty 1}-g_{\infty 2}\|_{\mathcal{G}_{d^{\prime}}^{\lambda_{0}/2,1/3}}\leq A^{\prime\prime}\|g_{-\infty 1}-g_{-\infty 2}\|_{\mathcal{G}_{d^{\prime}}^{\lambda_{0},1/3}},

for some constant
A^{\prime\prime}=A^{\prime\prime}(d,\lambda_{0},\vartheta)\in[1,1/\overline{\kappa}^{\prime\prime}]
.

We conclude this subsection with some remarks:

Remark 1.4
.

Our main theorems provide definitive answers to two of the open problems stated in the recent review article of Bedrossian
[
4
, Section 6]
:

(a) nonlinear Landau damping for initial perturbations in sharp weighted Gevrey-3 spaces, which was already conjectured by Mouhot-Villani
[
41
, Section 13]
;

(b) injectivity and Lipschitz bounds on the scattering operator
g_{-\infty}\mapsto g_{\infty}
(and similar properties for the final state operator
f_{0}\mapsto g_{\infty}
and the wave operator
g_{\infty}\mapsto g(0)
).

Remark 1.5
.

We also allow a larger class of equilibria
M_{0}
than in the earlier work, in the same weighted Gevrey-3 space as the initial perturbation. For comparison, all the earlier proofs of nonlinear Landau damping in the confined case (in
[
41
,
5
,
22
]
) require analytic equilibria. Our assumptions (
1.6
) also allow certain slowly decreasing equilibria, like the Poisson equilibrium corresponding to
\widehat{M_{0}}(\xi)=e^{-|\xi|}
in all dimensions
d\geq 1
, which were not included in the earlier nonlinear theorems. The main point is that we can still prove sufficiently strong bounds on the associated Green’s function (as stated in (
2.11
) below) under the weaker and more natural assumptions (
1.6
) on
M_{0}
. We provide all the details in section
6
.

We notice also that we could allow non-neutral perturbations in all the theorems, by modifying suitably the homogeneous equilibrium
M_{0}
around which we perturb.

Remark 1.6
.

We note that there is exponential loss of derivative during the nonlinear flow. For example, in Theorem
1.1
the initial data is assumed to be in the stronger Gevrey space
\mathcal{G}_{d^{\prime}}^{\lambda_{0},1/3}
, while the bounds on the solution are stated in the weaker Gevrey space
\mathcal{G}_{d^{\prime}}^{\lambda_{0}/2,1/3}
. The precise amount of derivative loss can be reduced (for example from
\mathcal{G}_{d^{\prime}}^{\lambda_{0},1/3}
to
\mathcal{G}_{d^{\prime}}^{0.99\lambda_{0},1/3}
, with small adjustments in the proof), but some loss is necessary to get time decay of macroscopic quantities. This is an important conceptual feature of Landau damping.

Remark 1.7
.

The global well-posedness theory of the final state problem and the construction of scattering operators (Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
) are new in the context of asymptotic stability of the Vlasov-Poisson system near homogeneous equilibria. To summarize, we prove that 3 nonlinear operators, the final state operator
f_{0}\mapsto g_{\infty}
, the wave operator
g_{\infty}\mapsto g(0)
, and the scattering operator
g_{-\infty}\mapsto g_{\infty}
are well defined, Lipschitz continuous (between suitable spaces, with derivative loss, see (
1.12
), (
1.15
), (
1.18
)) and injective (in the quantitative sense given by the lower bounds in these inequalities).

1.2.
Linear and nonlinear Landau damping

Landau damping is one of the most fundamental phenomena in Plasma Physics, and goes back to foundational work of Landau
[
36
]
in 1946 on the linearized Vlasov-Poisson equations on
\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
near Maxwellian equilibria. Nowadays, Landau damping is a term that is broadly used to denote a mechanism of decay of certain averaged quantities, as well as asymptotic stability. It took more than 60 years to develop a first understanding of Landau damping at the nonlinear level, for the full Vlasov-Poisson system (
1.3
). This concerns the
confined
periodic setting
x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
, where the decay of macroscopic quantities (such as the density
\rho
and the electric field
E
) occurs due to phase mixing and relies mainly on the high regularity of the initial data (see also
[
39
]
). After some preliminary works
[
11
,
30
]
, nonlinear Landau damping was proved in pioneering work by Mouhot–Villani
[
41
]
, who showed that small and analytic perturbations of homogeneous equilibria
M_{0}
that satisfy the Penrose criterion (
1.7
) lead to global solutions which scatter linearly, with density functions that decay exponentially in time. The theory has been refined significantly by Bedrossian-Masmoudi-Mouhot
[
5
]
and then Grenier-Nguyen-Rodnianski
[
22
]
, who expanded the class of acceptable perturbations to the more flexible class of Gevrey spaces
\mathcal{G}_{d^{\prime}}^{\lambda_{0},s}
, in the almost optimal range
s>1/3
, and simplified the argument substantially by using energy estimates with suitably defined weights instead of a global Newton scheme.

Our Theorem
1.1
above represents a sharp endpoint result for this classical line of research on Landau damping in the confined case. Stability in Gevrey-3 spaces was already conjectured in
[
41
, Section 13]
, and it is known by now that these spaces are best possible, due to the recent results in
[
3
,
4
,
52
]
. The theorem also applies to the vacuum case
M_{0}\equiv 0
; the normalization (
1.2
) is only needed to pass from (
1.1
) to (
1.3
).

A second contribution of this paper is the development of a new and complete scattering theory for the Vlasov-Poisson system in the confined case around homogeneous equilibria, in Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
, also in optimal weighted Gevrey-3 spaces. The existence of the scattering operator (or even just the injectivity of the final state operator
f_{0}\mapsto g_{\infty}
proved in Theorem
1.1
) has a natural physical interpretation: despite its name, “Landau damping” is different from other types of damping (for example damping of solutions of parabolic equations) in the sense that there is no loss of information during the nonlinear evolution in passing from the initial data
f_{0}
to the final state
g_{\infty}
. The “damping” takes place only at the level of macroscopic quantities, like the density, consistent with the time-reversibility of the system.

In view of these results Landau damping is well understood by now in the confined case. We remark, however, that nonlinear Landau damping around homogeneous equilibria remains a major open problem in the
unconfined case
, particularly in 3 dimensions
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, both for the Vlasov-Poisson system and for the related relativistic Vlasov-Maxwell system. A key difference is that the Penrose condition (
1.7
) cannot hold uniformly as the frequency
\tau
goes to
0
; as a result the Green’s function cannot have fast decay and macroscopic quantities like the density and the electric field can only decay slowly, at dimension-dependent polynomial rates in
\langle t\rangle^{-1}
, which are not sufficient in dimension
d=3
. Linear stability results are known in these cases (see, for example,
[
26
,
7
,
34
,
27
]
for recent work in this direction), indicating the possibility of nonlinear stability, but the only nonlinear result so far was proved recently by the authors in
[
33
]
for the Poisson equilibrium.

1.3.
Main ideas

Our proofs are based on what we call
the
Z
-norm method
. This is a general method that has been used extensively by the authors to prove global stability of various quasi-linear dispersive and hyperbolic equations, such as water waves, plasma models, and the Einstein equations of General Relativity. At the abstract level, it consists of proving simultaneous bootstrap control of two types of quantities:

(1) Energy functionals at the highest order of derivatives, sometimes including vector-fields that commute with the linearized equation;

(2) A special
Z
-norm (usually not based on
L^{2}
spaces) which provides stronger control of the solutions, including time decay, but at a lower level of derivatives.

In our specific case, to prove Theorem
1.1
we use energy functionals defined in the Fourier space, of the form

(1.19)

\mathcal{E}^{0}_{g}(t):=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}A(t,k,\xi)^{2}\Big\{\sum_{|a|\leq d^{\prime}}\big|D^{a}_{\xi}\widehat{g}(t,k,\xi)\big|^{2}\Big\}\,d\xi,

where
g
is the profile of the solution and
A:[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[1,\infty)
is a family of suitable weights. The precise choice of the weights
A(t,k,\xi)=e^{\lambda(t,|k,\xi|)}
is very important, see the discussion in subsection
1.4
below. These weights are decreasing in time, to reflect the ”sliding regularity” basic mechanism of the proof, and increasing in frequency at a rate consistent with Gevrey-3 spaces,
\lambda(t,r)\approx\langle r\rangle^{1/3}
.

We then define a matching
Z
-norm, which controls the macroscopic density, by the formula

(1.20)

\mathcal{Z}^{0}_{g}(t):=\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\}}A(t,k,tk)\big|\widehat{g}(t,k,tk)\big||k|^{-1/2}=\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\}}A(t,k,tk)\big|\widehat{\rho}(t,k)\big||k|^{-1/2}.

This type of
Z
-norms (weighted
L^{\infty}
norms in the Fourier space) have been used extensively by the authors, starting with the work
[
35
]
on gravity water waves in 2D. A similar norm was also used in
[
22
]
, with a different weight
A
.

The basic idea is to prove uniform bootstrap control of the form

(1.21)

\mathcal{E}^{0}_{g}(t)+\big[\mathcal{Z}_{g}^{0}(t)\langle t\rangle^{6d}\big]^{2}\lesssim\kappa_{0}^{2},

for solutions
g
of the system (
1.9
), where
\kappa_{0}
denotes the size of the initial data. Notice the basic interplay of the proof: the energy norm is strong, but does not decay in time, while the
Z
-norm is comparatively weaker in terms of regularity (due to the factor of
|k|^{-1/2}
in the definition), but is able to capture the time decay of the macroscopic density. The precise powers
1/2
and
6d
are related in the proof, but there is some flexibility in their choice.

The proof of the bootstrap estimates (
1.21
) consists of two main steps. First we use the density equation

(1.22)

\widehat{\rho}(t,k)+\int_{0}^{t}\widehat{\rho}(s,k)\widehat{M_{0}}((t-s)k)(t-s)\,ds=\widehat{\mathcal{N}}(t,k),

where
\mathcal{N}
is a suitable nonlinearity, together with bounds on Green’s function associated with the equilibrium
M_{0}
, to control the
Z
-norm of the solution. Then we use the basic equation (
1.9
) for
g
, in the Fourier space form (
2.1
), to prove uniform control on the functional
\mathcal{E}^{0}_{g}
.

1.4.
New weights

The main novelty in our construction, which allows us to reach the sharp Gevrey-3 regularity, is the choice of the weights
A
. The standard choice used in
[
41
,
5
,
22
]
involves weights having favorable product structure of the form

(1.23)

A(t,k,\xi)=e^{\lambda(t,|k,\xi|)},\qquad\lambda(t,r)=\lambda_{1}(t)\lambda_{2}(r),

for suitable functions
\lambda_{1}
(decreasing) and
\lambda_{2}
(increasing). This product structure easily leads to bilinear estimates of the form

(1.24)

A(t,k_{1}+k_{2},\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})\lesssim A(t,k_{1},\xi_{1})A(t,k_{2},\xi_{2})\{(1+|k,\xi|)^{-4}+(1+|k^{\prime},\xi^{\prime}|)^{-4}\},

for any
t,k,k^{\prime},\xi,\xi^{\prime}
, which play a crucial role in nonlinear analysis. Weights of this type have been used in
[
5
]
and
[
22
]
(in the context of so-called ”generator functions”) to prove Landau damping in Gevrey spaces
\mathcal{G}_{d^{\prime}}^{\lambda_{0},s}
in the full subcritical range
s>1/3
, but appear to be insufficient to deal with the critical case
s=1/3
.

Our main new idea is to work with a more general class of weights, of the form

(1.25)

A(t,k,\xi)=e^{\lambda(t,|k,\xi|)},\qquad\lambda(t,r)=[\lambda_{1}+\delta(1+t)^{-\delta}]\langle r\rangle^{1/3}+\delta\big(1+t\langle r\rangle^{-2/3}\big)^{-\delta}\langle r\rangle^{1/3},

where
\lambda_{1}\in[0.6\lambda_{0},0.9\lambda_{0}]
and
\delta=\lambda_{0}/200
. These weights do not have the basic product structure (
1.23
), due to the last term, but we can still prove favorable bilinear estimates like (
1.24
) (in the stronger form given in (
2.32
)), as well as suitable commutator estimates like (
2.33
), which are needed to prevent derivative loss. The key point is that we can choose the last term in the definition (
1.25
) in a way that improves the analysis of the density equation; for example we are able to prove uniform estimates like

(1.26)

\sum_{a\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},\,|a|\leq|k|/8}\int_{0.99t}^{t}\frac{|t-s|A(t,k,tk)}{A(s,k,tk)}e^{-\delta(|a|^{1/3}+|sa+(t-s)k|^{1/3})}\,ds\lesssim 1,

for any
t\in[10,\infty)
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
(see (
3.15
)), which ultimately allow us to close the main bootstrap argument at the level of the sharp Gevrey-3 spaces.

1.5.
The final state problem

At the quantitative level, the estimates we need to prove Theorem
1.2
are similar to the estimates in the initial data problem, using a combination of an energy functional and a
Z
-norm. However we need a new family of weights
A^{\sharp}(t,k,\xi)=e^{\lambda^{\sharp}(t,|k,\xi|)}:[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[1,\infty)
, which are now increasing in time to indicate that the solution comes from infinity and loses regularity as
t
decreases. We also need a new density equation, of the form

(1.27)

\widehat{\rho}(t,k)-\int_{t}^{\infty}\widehat{\rho}(s,k)\widehat{M_{0}}((t-s)k)(t-s)\,ds=\widehat{\mathcal{N}^{\prime}}(t,k),

for a suitable nonlinearity
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
, which replaces the equation (
1.22
) and gives us a basic formula for the density
\rho
at time
t
in terms of
\rho
at later times.

To construct the solution itself we construct a family of solutions
g_{n}
of the system (
1.9
) on the time interval
[0,n]
, with data
g_{n}(n)=g_{\infty}
. Then we prove estimates on the differences of these solutions, and find the full solution
g
as the limit as
n\to\infty
of
g_{n}
. This construction is possible as long as we allow partial loss of regularity, as claimed in Theorem
1.2
, and provided that we control very well differences of solutions.

1.6.
Organization

The rest of the paper is organized as follows: in section
2
we derive our main equations for the profile
g
and the density
\rho
, define our two families of weights and prove some of their basic properties, and state our two main bootstrap propositions, Propositions
2.2
and
2.3
. Then we prove these propositions in sections
3
and
4
respectively. In section 5 we show how to use these bootstrap propositions to complete the proofs of the main Theorems
1.1
,
1.2
, and
1.3
. Finally, in section 6 we prove Lemma
2.1
, which provides suitable bounds on the Green’s function associated to the equilibrium
M_{0}
.

2.
The main equations and the bootstrap proposition

Recall the main equations (
1.9
) for the kinetic profile
g
. We assume that
T_{1}\leq T_{2}\in[0,\infty)
and
g\in C([T_{1},T_{2}]:\mathcal{G}^{\lambda_{0}/2,1/3}_{d^{\prime}}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}))
is a solution of the system (
1.9
), satisfying the neutrality condition
\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(t,x,v)\,dxdv=0
. Taking the Fourier transform in both
x
and
v
variables we derive the equations

(2.1)

\begin{split}&\partial_{t}\widehat{g}(t,k,\xi)+\widehat{\rho}(t,k)\widehat{M_{0}}(\xi-tk)\frac{k\cdot(\xi-tk)}{|k|^{2}}+\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\widehat{\rho}(t,l)\widehat{g}(t,k-l,\xi-tl)\frac{l\cdot(\xi-tk)}{|l|^{2}}=0,\\
&\widehat{\rho}(t,k):=\widehat{g}(t,k,tk).\end{split}

2.1.
The initial data problem

It follows from (
2.1
) that

\begin{split}\widehat{g}(t,k,\xi)-\widehat{g}(T_{1},k,\xi)&+\int_{T_{1}}^{t}\widehat{\rho}(s,k)\widehat{M_{0}}(\xi-sk)\frac{k\cdot(\xi-sk)}{|k|^{2}}\,ds\\
&+\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\int_{T_{1}}^{t}\widehat{\rho}(s,l)\widehat{g}(s,k-l,\xi-sl)\frac{l\cdot(\xi-sk)}{|l|^{2}}\,ds=0.\end{split}

Letting
\xi=tk
it follows that

(2.2)

\begin{split}&\widehat{\rho}(t,k)+\int_{T_{1}}^{t}\widehat{\rho}(s,k)\widehat{M_{0}}((t-s)k)(t-s)\,ds=\widehat{\mathcal{N}}(t,k),\\
&\widehat{\mathcal{N}}(t,k):=\widehat{g}(T_{1},k,tk)-\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\}}\int_{T_{1}}^{t}\widehat{\rho}(s,l)\widehat{g}(s,k-l,tk-sl)\frac{(t-s)l\cdot k}{|l|^{2}}\,ds.\end{split}

We would like to use the Volterra equation (
2.2
) and the main assumptions (
1.6
)–(
1.7
) to prove an important formula for
\rho
. We extend
\widehat{\rho}
to a function on
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{Z}^{d}
supported in
[T_{1},T_{2}]\times\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, and extend the function
\widehat{\mathcal{N}}
according to the identity in the first line of (
2.2
) (clearly
\widehat{\mathcal{N}}(t,k)=0
if
t<T_{1}
). Then we define, for
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,

(2.3)

\widetilde{\rho}(\tau,k):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{\rho}(t,k)e^{-it\tau}\,dt,\qquad\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}(\tau,k):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{\mathcal{N}}(t,k)e^{-it\tau}\,dt.

For
\tau\in\mathbb{H}_{-}:=\{\tau\in\mathbb{C}:\,\Im\tau\leq 0\}
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
let

(2.4)

L(\tau,k):=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-i\tau s}s\widehat{M_{0}}(sk)\,ds.

In view of (
1.6
), for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
the function
L(.,k)
is well-defined and continuous in
\mathbb{H}_{-}
, and analytic in the open half-plane
\mathbb{H}^{\circ}_{-}:=\{\tau\in\mathbb{C}:\,\Im\tau<0\}
.

It follows from the identity in the first line of (
2.2
) that

(2.5)

\widetilde{\rho}(\tau,k)\big(1+L(\tau,k)\big)=\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}(\tau,k),\qquad\tau\in\mathbb{R},\,k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\},

where the function
L
is defined as in (
2.4
). For
\tau\in\mathbb{H}_{-}
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
let

(2.6)

L^{\prime}(\tau,k):=\frac{L(\tau,k)}{1+L(\tau,k)}.

In view of the Penrose condition (
1.7
), the function
L^{\prime}:\mathbb{H}_{-}\times(\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\})\to\mathbb{C}
is well-defined and continuous in
\mathbb{H}_{-}
, and analytic in
\mathbb{H}^{\circ}_{-}
. Moreover, using (
2.5
),

(2.7)

\widetilde{\rho}(\tau,k)=\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}(\tau,k)-\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}(\tau,k)L^{\prime}(\tau,k),\qquad\tau\in\mathbb{R},\,k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\}.

We take the inverse Fourier transform in
\tau
to conclude that

(2.8)

\begin{split}&\widehat{\rho}(t,k)=\widehat{\mathcal{N}}(t,k)-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{\mathcal{N}}(t-s,k)\widehat{G}(s,k)\,ds\quad\text{ where }\quad\widehat{G}(s,k):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{is\tau}L^{\prime}(\tau,k)\,d\tau.\end{split}

Our next lemma provides estimates on the functions
L,L^{\prime}
and the Green’s function
\widehat{G}
, which show in particular that the formal calculations above are justified.

Lemma 2.1
.

(i) The functions
L,L^{\prime}:\mathbb{H}_{-}\times(\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\})\to\mathbb{C}
defined in (
2.4
) and (
2.6
) are well-defined and continuous in
\mathbb{H}_{-}
, analytic in
\mathbb{H}^{\circ}_{-}
, and satisfy the bounds

(2.9)

\begin{split}\big\|\langle\alpha\rangle L(\alpha+i\gamma,k)\big\|_{L^{2}_{\alpha}}+\big\|\langle\alpha\rangle L^{\prime}(\alpha+i\gamma,k)\big\|_{L^{2}_{\alpha}}&\leq C_{0}^{\ast}|k|^{-1/2},\\
\big\|\langle\alpha\rangle D^{a}_{\alpha}L(\alpha+i\gamma,k)\big\|_{L^{\infty}_{\alpha}}+\big\|\langle\alpha\rangle D^{a}_{\alpha}L^{\prime}(\alpha+i\gamma,k)\big\|_{L^{\infty}_{\alpha}}&\leq C_{0}^{\ast}\frac{(3a)!(1.01)^{a}}{(|k|\lambda_{0}^{3})^{a+1}},\end{split}

for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
,
\gamma\in(-\infty,0]
, and
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. The constant
C_{0}^{\ast}
may depend only on the parameters
d
,
\lambda_{0}
, and
\vartheta
.

(ii) With
\widehat{\rho}
and
\widehat{\mathcal{N}}
as in (
2.2
), for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
we have

(2.10)

\widehat{\rho}(t,k)=\widehat{\mathcal{N}}(t,k)-\int_{T_{1}}^{t}\widehat{\mathcal{N}}(s,k)\widehat{G}(t-s,k)\,ds

where the Green’s function
\widehat{G}:\mathbb{R}\times(\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\})\to\mathbb{C}
defined as in (
2.8
) is supported in
[0,\infty)\times(\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\})
and satisfies the uniform bounds

(2.11)

\big|\widehat{G}(s,k)\big|\leq C_{0}^{\ast}e^{-0.95\lambda_{0}|sk|^{1/3}}.

We provide a self-contained proof of this lemma in section
6
.

2.1.1.
Energy functionals and the first bootstrap proposition

The main idea is to estimate the increment of suitable functionals, which are defined using special weights. For
t,r\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
k,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we define

(2.12)

\begin{split}&\lambda(t,r):=\lambda_{1}\langle r\rangle^{1/3}+\delta(1+t)^{-\delta}\langle r\rangle^{1/3}+\delta\big(1+t\langle r\rangle^{-2/3}\big)^{-\delta}\langle r\rangle^{1/3},\\
&A(t,k,\xi):=e^{\lambda(t,|k,\xi|)},\end{split}

where
\lambda_{1}\in[0.5\lambda_{0},0.9\lambda_{0}]
,
\delta:=\lambda_{0}/200
and
\langle w\rangle:=(1+|w|^{2})^{1/2}
,
w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

The precise definition of the weight
A
is important; some properties are proved in section
2.3
below. Notice that the weights
\lambda
and
A
are decreasing in
t
.

Our first bootstrap argument is based on controlling simultaneously an energy functional for the profile
g
and a decaying norm for the density
\rho
. Let
d^{\prime}=\lfloor d/2+1\rfloor
and define the spaces

(2.13)

\begin{split}&X_{[T_{1},T_{2}]}:=\Big\{h\in C([T_{1},T_{2}]:\mathcal{G}^{\lambda_{0}/2,1/3}_{d^{\prime}}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})):\\
&\quad\|h\|_{X_{[T_{1},T_{2}]}}:=\sup_{t\in[T_{1},T_{2}]}\Big\{\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}A(t,k,\xi)^{2}\Big[\sum_{|a|\leq d^{\prime}}\big|D^{a}_{\xi}\widehat{h}(t,k,\xi)\big|^{2}\Big]\,d\xi\Big\}^{1/2}<\infty\Big\},\end{split}

for
T_{1}\leq T_{2}\in[0,\infty)
. For any
h\in X_{[T_{1},T_{2}]}
and
t\in[T_{1},T_{2}]
,
\beta\in[0,1]
, and
p\in[0,2]
we define

(2.14)

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}^{p}_{h}(t)&:=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\langle k,\xi\rangle^{-2p}A(t,k,\xi)^{2}\Big\{\sum_{|a|\leq d^{\prime}}\big|D^{a}_{\xi}\widehat{h}(t,k,\xi)\big|^{2}\Big\}\,d\xi,\\
\mathcal{Z}^{p}_{h}(t)&:=\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\}}\langle k,tk\rangle^{-p}A(t,k,tk)\big|\widehat{h}(t,k,tk)\big||k|^{-\beta}.\end{split}

There are two parameters in these definitions: the parameter
\beta
is taken
\beta=1/2
in the proof, and it indicates the fact that the
Z
-norms of solutions are measured at lower order of differentiability than the energy norms. The parameter
p
is taken either
p=0
to estimate the solutions themselves or
p=2
to estimate the difference of solutions (due to some loss of symmetry, the difference of solutions can only be estimated in slightly weaker norms).

Our first main bootstrap proposition is the following:

Proposition 2.2
.

Assume
T_{1}\leq T_{2}\in[0,\infty)
and
g_{1},g_{2}\in X_{[T_{1},T_{2}]}
are real-valued solutions of the system (
1.9
) satisfying
\widehat{g_{i}}(t,0,0)=0
for any
t\in[T_{1},T_{2}]
and
i\in\{1,2\}
. Assume that
\epsilon_{0}\leq\overline{\epsilon}\ll 1
,
\lambda_{1}\in[0.5\lambda_{0},0.9\lambda_{0}]
, and

(2.15)

\|g_{i}(T_{1})\|_{{\mathcal{G}_{d^{\prime}}^{\lambda_{1}+4\delta,1/3}}}\leq\overline{\epsilon},\qquad\|(g_{1}-g_{2})(T_{1})\|_{\mathcal{G}_{d^{\prime}}^{\lambda_{1}+4\delta,1/3}}\leq\epsilon_{0},

for
i\in\{1,2\}
. Let
\beta=1/2
and assume that for any
t\in[T_{1},T_{2}]
and
i\in\{1,2\}

(2.16)

\begin{split}&\mathcal{E}^{0}_{g_{i}}(t)+\big[\mathcal{Z}_{g_{i}}^{0}(t)\langle t\rangle^{6d}\big]^{2}\leq 4\overline{C}^{2}\overline{\epsilon}^{2},\\
&\mathcal{E}^{2}_{g_{1}-g_{2}}(t)+\big[\mathcal{Z}_{g_{1}-g_{2}}^{2}(t)\langle t\rangle^{6d}\big]^{2}\leq 4\overline{C}^{2}\epsilon_{0}^{2},\end{split}

where
\overline{C}\leq\overline{\epsilon}^{-1/8}
is a sufficiently large constant that depends only on the structural constants
d,\lambda_{0},\vartheta
. Then for any
t\in[T_{1},T_{2}]
and
i\in\{1,2\}
we have the improved bounds

(2.17)

\begin{split}&\mathcal{E}^{0}_{g_{i}}(t)+\big[\mathcal{Z}_{g_{i}}^{0}(t)\langle t\rangle^{6d}\big]^{2}\leq\overline{C}^{2}\overline{\epsilon}^{2},\\
&\mathcal{E}_{g_{1}-g_{2}}^{2}(t)+\big[\mathcal{Z}_{g_{1}-g_{2}}^{2}(t)\langle t\rangle^{6d}\big]^{2}\leq\overline{C}^{2}\epsilon_{0}^{2}.\end{split}

This proposition plays a key role in the proof of Theorem
1.1
(i); we will prove it in section
3
below. We note that the constant
\overline{C}
will be taken much larger than other structural constants such as the constants
C_{0}^{\ast}
in Lemma
2.1
,
C_{0}
in Lemma
2.5
,
C_{1}
in Lemma
3.1
, and
C_{2}
in Proposition
3.2
.

2.2.
The final state problem

We also prove quantitative estimates going backwards in time. Assume that
T_{1}\leq T_{2}\in[0,\infty)
and
g\in C([T_{1},T_{2}]:\mathcal{G}_{d^{\prime}}^{\lambda_{0}/2,1/3}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}))
is a solution of the system (
1.9
). For any
t\in[T_{1},T_{2}]
we use (
2.1
) and integrate for
s\in[t,T_{2}]
, thus

\begin{split}-\widehat{g}(t,k,\xi)+\widehat{g}(T_{2},k,\xi)&+\int_{t}^{T_{2}}\widehat{\rho}(s,k)\widehat{M_{0}}(\xi-sk)\frac{k\cdot(\xi-sk)}{|k|^{2}}\,ds\\
&+\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\int_{t}^{T_{2}}\widehat{\rho}(s,l)\widehat{g}(s,k-l,\xi-sl)\frac{l\cdot(\xi-sk)}{|l|^{2}}\,ds=0.\end{split}

Letting
\xi=tk
it follows that

(2.18)

\begin{split}&\widehat{\rho}(t,k)-\int_{t}^{T_{2}}\widehat{\rho}(s,k)\widehat{M_{0}}((t-s)k)(t-s)\,ds=\widehat{\mathcal{N}^{\prime}}(t,k),\\
&\widehat{\mathcal{N}^{\prime}}(t,k):=\widehat{g}(T_{2},k,tk)+\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\}}\int_{t}^{T_{2}}\widehat{\rho}(s,l)\widehat{g}(s,k-l,tk-sl)\frac{(t-s)l\cdot k}{|l|^{2}}\,ds.\end{split}

We would like to find an integral formula for
\rho
in terms of
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
. For this we define
\rho_{1}(t)=\rho(t)
for
t\in[T_{1},T_{2}]
and
\rho_{1}(t)=0
if
t\notin[T_{1},T_{2}]
, and then we define
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}_{1}
by

(2.19)

\widehat{\mathcal{N}^{\prime}_{1}}(t,k):=\widehat{\rho_{1}}(t,k)-\int_{t}^{\infty}\widehat{\rho_{1}}(s,k)\widehat{M_{0}}((t-s)k)(t-s)\,ds.

For
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
we define the time Fourier transforms

\widetilde{\rho_{1}}(\tau,k):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{\rho_{1}}(t,k)e^{-it\tau}\,dt,\qquad\widetilde{\mathcal{N}^{\prime}_{1}}(\tau,k):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{\mathcal{N}^{\prime}_{1}}(t,k)e^{-it\tau}\,dt.

It follows from (
2.19
) that for any
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
we have

\widetilde{\mathcal{N}^{\prime}_{1}}(\tau,k)=\widetilde{\rho_{1}}(\tau,k)(1+L(-\tau,-k)),

where
L
is defined as in (
2.4
). Therefore

\widetilde{\rho_{1}}(\tau,k)=\widetilde{\mathcal{N}^{\prime}_{1}}(\tau,k)-\widetilde{\mathcal{N}^{\prime}_{1}}(\tau,k)L^{\prime}(-\tau,-k),\qquad\tau\in\mathbb{R},\,k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\},

where
L^{\prime}
is defined as in (
2.6
). We take the inverse Fourier transform and recall the definition (
2.8
) of the Green’s functions
\widehat{G}
, so

\widehat{\rho_{1}}(t,k)=\widehat{\mathcal{N}^{\prime}_{1}}(t,k)-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{\mathcal{N}^{\prime}_{1}}(s,k)\widehat{G}(s-t,-k)\,ds.

Recall that
\widehat{G}
is supported in
[0,\infty)\times(\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\})
(see Lemma
2.1
),
\rho_{1}(t)=\rho(t)
,
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}_{1}(t)=\mathcal{N}^{\prime}(t)
for
t\in[T_{1},T_{2}]
, and
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}_{1}(t)=0
for
t>T_{2}
. We can thus derive our main identity

(2.20)

\widehat{\rho}(t,k)=\widehat{\mathcal{N}^{\prime}}(t,k)-\int_{t}^{T_{2}}\widehat{\mathcal{N}^{\prime}}(s,k)\widehat{G}(s-t,-k)\,ds\qquad\text{ for }t\in[T_{1},T_{2}]\text{ and }k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\}.

This formula serves as the main substitute of the identity (
2.10
) in the analysis of the final state problem.

2.2.1.
The second bootstrap proposition

To understand the evolution backwards in time we need to work with a new set of weights, which are increasing in time. We define these weights as in (
2.12
): for
t\in[0,\infty)
,
r\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
k,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we define

(2.21)

\begin{split}&\lambda^{\sharp}(t,r):=\lambda_{1}\langle r\rangle^{1/3}-\delta(1+t)^{-\delta}\langle r\rangle^{1/3}-\delta\big(1+t\langle r\rangle^{-2/3}\big)^{-\delta}\langle r\rangle^{1/3},\\
&A^{\sharp}(t,k,\xi):=e^{\lambda^{\sharp}(t,|k,\xi|)},\end{split}

where
\lambda_{1}\in[0.5\lambda_{0},0.9\lambda_{0}]
and
\delta=\lambda_{0}/200
. We will use these weights for certain values of
\lambda_{1}
.

Our second bootstrap argument is also based on controlling simultaneously an energy functional for
g
and a decaying norm for
\rho
. As in subsection

we define

(2.22)

\begin{split}&X^{\sharp}_{[T_{1},T_{2}]}:=\Big\{h\in C([T_{1},T_{2}]:\mathcal{G}^{\lambda_{0}/2,1/3}_{d^{\prime}}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})):\\
&\qquad\|h\|_{X^{\sharp}_{[T_{1},T_{2}]}}:=\sup_{t\in[T_{1},T_{2}]]}\Big\{\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}A^{\sharp}(t,k,\xi)^{2}\Big[\sum_{|a|\leq d^{\prime}}\big|D^{a}_{\xi}\widehat{h}(t,k,\xi)\big|^{2}\Big]\,d\xi\Big\}^{1/2}<\infty\Big\}.\end{split}

For any
h\in X^{\sharp}_{[T_{1},T_{2}]}
,
t\in[T_{1},T_{2}]
, and

we define

(2.23)

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}^{\sharp,p}_{h}(t)&:=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\langle k,\xi\rangle^{-2p}A^{\sharp}(t,k,\xi)^{2}\Big\{\sum_{|a|\leq d^{\prime}}\big|D^{a}_{\xi}\widehat{h}(t,k,\xi)\big|^{2}\Big\}\,d\xi,\\

Our second main bootstrap proposition is the following:

Proposition 2.3
.

Assume
T_{1}\leq T_{2}\in[0,\infty)
and
g_{1},g_{2}\in X^{\sharp}_{[T_{1},T_{2}]}
are real-valued solutions of the system (
1.9
) satisfying
\widehat{g_{i}}(t,0,0)=0
for any
t\in[T_{1},T_{2}]
and
i\in\{1,2\}
. Assume that
\theta_{0}\leq\overline{\theta}\ll 1
and

(2.24)

\|g_{i}(T_{2})\|_{{\mathcal{G}_{d^{\prime}}^{\lambda_{1}+4\delta,1/3}}}\leq\overline{\theta},\qquad\|(g_{1}-g_{2})(T_{2})\|_{\mathcal{G}_{d^{\prime}}^{\lambda_{1}+4\delta,1/3}}\leq\theta_{0}.

for
i\in\{1,2\}
, where
\lambda_{1}\in[0.5\lambda_{0},0.9\lambda_{0}]
. Assume that

(2.25)

\begin{split}&\mathcal{E}^{\sharp,0}_{g_{i}}(t)+\big[\mathcal{Z}_{g_{i}}^{\sharp,0}(t)\langle t\rangle^{6d}\big]^{2}\leq 4\overline{B}^{2}\overline{\theta}^{2},\\
&\mathcal{E}^{\sharp,2}_{g_{1}-g_{2}}(t)+\big[\mathcal{Z}_{g_{1}-g_{2}}^{\sharp,2}(t)\langle t\rangle^{6d}\big]^{2}\leq 4\overline{B}^{2}\theta_{0}^{2},\end{split}

for any
t\in[T_{1},T_{2}]
and
i\in\{1,2\}
, where
\overline{B}\leq\overline{\theta}^{-1/8}
is a sufficiently large constant. Then for any
t\in[T_{1},T_{2}]
and
i\in\{1,2\}
we have the improved bounds

(2.26)

\begin{split}&\mathcal{E}^{\sharp,0}_{g_{i}}(t)+\big[\mathcal{Z}_{g_{i}}^{\sharp,0}(t)\langle t\rangle^{6d}\big]^{2}\leq\overline{B}^{2}\overline{\theta}^{2},\\
&\mathcal{E}^{\sharp,2}_{g_{1}-g_{2}}(t)+\big[\mathcal{Z}_{g_{1}-g_{2}}^{\sharp,2}(t)\langle t\rangle^{6d}\big]^{2}\leq\overline{B}^{2}\theta_{0}^{2}.\end{split}

2.3.
Some lemmas

We prove first some properties of the main weights
A
and
A^{\sharp}
.

Lemma 2.4
.

(i) Assume that
\lambda_{1}\in[\lambda_{0}/2,\lambda_{0}]

\begin{split}\partial_{r}\lambda(t,r)&=\big[\lambda_{1}+\delta(1+t)^{-\delta}\big](r/3)\langle r\rangle^{-5/3}\\

\end{split}

and

\begin{split}&\lambda(a,r)-\lambda(b,r)=\lambda^{\sharp}(b,r)-\lambda^{\sharp}(a,r)\\

(ii) If

(ii) In view of (

Lemma 2.5

(2.36)

\begin{split}H_{p}(h_{1})(t,k)&:=\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big|\langle k,\xi\rangle^{-p}A(t,k,\xi)\widehat{h_{1}}(t,k,\xi)\big|,\\

\begin{split}|H_{p}(h_{1})(t,k)|^{2}&\leq C_{0}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\langle k,\xi\rangle^{-2p}A(t,k,\xi)^{2}\Big\{\sum_{|a|\leq d^{\prime}}\big|D^{a}_{\xi}\widehat{h_{1}}(t,k,\xi)\big|^{2}\Big\}\,d\xi,\\

The bounds in the first line of (

&K_{a}(t,k,s):=|t-s|e^{-\delta^{4}|t-s|t^{-1/3}|k|^{-1/3}(1+t|k|^{-2})^{-1/3-\delta}}e^{-\delta|s|a|-(t-s)|k||^{1/3}}e^{-\delta|a|^{1/3}/2}.\end{split}

The bounds (

. On the other hand, if

\begin{split}|K_{a}(t,k,s)|&\lesssim t|k|^{-1}e^{-\delta^{4}(t|k|^{-2})^{2/3}(1+t|k|^{-2})^{-1/3-\delta}/4}e^{-\delta|s|a|-(t-s)|k||^{1/3}}e^{-\delta|a|^{1/3}/4}\\

if

We can now prove our main estimates on the function

\begin{split}\Re\Big\{&\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\langle k,\xi\rangle^{-2}A_{L}(t,k,\xi)\cdot\langle k-l,\xi-tl\rangle^{-2}A_{L}(t,k-l,\xi-tl)\\
&\qquad\qquad\times\Big[\,\overline{D^{a}_{\xi}\widehat{\delta g}(t,k,\xi)}\widehat{\rho_{1}}(t,l)D^{a}_{\xi}\widehat{\delta g}(t,k-l,\xi-tl)\frac{l\cdot(\xi-tk)}{|l|^{2}}\Big]\,d\xi\Big\}=0,\end{split}

for any

and any multi-index

satisfying

. This follows easily by expanding

and observing that
\overline{\widehat{\rho_{1}}(t,l)}=\widehat{\rho_{1}}(t,-l)
since

is real-valued. Therefore

(3.46)

|\delta III(s)|\lesssim\delta III_{1}(s)+\delta III_{2}(s)

where

(3.47)

\begin{split}\delta III_{1}(s):=\sum_{|a|\leq d^{\prime}}&\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\langle k,\xi\rangle^{-2}A_{L}(s,k,\xi)\Big|\frac{A_{L}(s,k,\xi)}{\langle k,\xi\rangle^{2}}-\frac{A_{L}(s,k-l,\xi-sl)}{\langle k-l,\xi-sl\rangle^{2}}\Big|\\

(3.48)

\begin{split}\delta III_{2}(s):=\sum_{|a|\leq d^{\prime}}&\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\langle k,\xi\rangle^{-4}A_{L}(s,k,\xi)^{2}\\
&\times\big|D^{a}_{\xi}\widehat{\delta g}(s,k,\xi)\big||\widehat{\delta\rho}(s,l)|\,\big|D^{a}_{\xi}\widehat{g_{2}}(s,k-l,\xi-sl)\big|\frac{|\xi-sk|}{|l|}\,d\xi.\end{split}

Step 1.
We bound first the contribution of the term
\delta III_{1}
. Recalling the definitions (
3.2

(3.49)

\begin{split}\delta III_{1}(s)\lesssim&\sum_{|a|\leq d^{\prime}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|\widehat{\mathcal{A}_{a,L}\delta g}(s,k,\xi)|}{\langle k,\xi\rangle^{2}}\frac{|\widehat{\mathcal{A}_{a,L}\delta g}(s,k-l,\xi-sl)|}{\langle k-l,\xi-sl\rangle^{2}}\frac{|\widehat{\mathcal{A}\rho_{1}}(s,l)|}{|l|}\\

10|l,sl|\geq|k-l,\xi-sl|

) to estimate

\begin{split}\Big|\frac{A_{L}(s,k,\xi)}{\langle k,\xi\rangle^{2}}-\frac{A_{L}(s,k-l,\xi-sl)}{\langle k-l,\xi-sl\rangle^{2}}\Big|\frac{\langle k-l,\xi-sl\rangle^{2}|\xi-sk|}{A(s,l,sl)A_{L}(s,k-l,\xi-sl)}&\lesssim|\xi-sk|e^{-2\delta|k-l,\xi-sl|^{1/3}}\\

On the other hand, if

\begin{split}\Big|\frac{A_{L}(s,k,\xi)}{\langle k,\xi\rangle^{2}}-\frac{A_{L}(s,k-l,\xi-sl)}{\langle k-l,\xi-sl\rangle^{2}}\Big|\frac{\langle k-l,\xi-sl\rangle^{2}|\xi-sk|}{A(s,l,sl)A_{L}(s,k-l,\xi-sl)}&\lesssim|\xi-sk|e^{-2\delta\langle l,sl\rangle^{1/3}}\langle k,\xi\rangle^{-2/3}\\

, using (

Step 2.

Lemma 4.1
.

Assume that

(4.1)

\begin{split}\sup_{t\in[T_{1},T_{2}],\,k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\}}A^{\sharp}(t,k,tk)|\widehat{\mathcal{N}^{\prime}_{i}}(t,k)||k|^{-1/2}\langle t\rangle^{6d}&\leq B_{1}\overline{\theta},\\
\sup_{t\in[T_{1},T_{2}],\,k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\}}\langle k,tk\rangle^{-2}A^{\sharp}(t,k,tk)|(\widehat{\mathcal{N}^{\prime}_{1}}-\widehat{\mathcal{N}^{\prime}_{2}})(t,k)||k|^{-1/2}\langle t\rangle^{6d}&\leq B_{1}\theta_{0},\end{split}

Proof.

This is similar to the proof of Lemma

3.6

(4.2)

\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\}}\int_{t}^{\infty}\frac{|t-s||k|^{1/2}\langle t\rangle^{6d}}{|l|^{1/2}\langle s\rangle^{6d}}\frac{A^{\sharp}(t,k,tk)}{A^{\sharp}(s,k,tk)}\big[e^{-8\delta\langle l,sl\rangle^{1/3}}+e^{-8\delta\langle k-l,tk-sl\rangle^{1/3}}\big]\,ds\lesssim 1.

As before, we prove these bounds in three steps.

Step 1.
We show first that

(4.3)

\begin{split}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\}}\int_{t}^{\infty}\frac{|t-s||k|^{1/2}\langle t\rangle^{6d}}{|l|^{1/2}\langle s\rangle^{6d}}\frac{A^{\sharp}(t,k,tk)}{A^{\sharp}(s,k,tk)}e^{-\delta(s^{1/3}+|l|^{1/3})}\,ds\lesssim 1.\end{split}

This is similar to the proof of (
3.7
). The definition (
2.21

(4.4)

\lambda^{\sharp}(s,r)-\lambda^{\sharp}(t,r)=\delta^{2}\int_{t}^{s}\big[(1+u)^{-\delta-1}\langle r\rangle^{1/3}+\big(1+u\langle r\rangle^{-2/3}\big)^{-\delta-1}\langle r\rangle^{-1/3}\big]\,du,

. Therefore

(4.5)

\frac{A^{\sharp}(t,k,tk)}{A^{\sharp}(s,k,tk)}=e^{\lambda^{\ast}(t,|k,tk|)-\lambda^{\ast}(s,|k.tk|)}\leq e^{-\delta^{2}|t-s|(1+s)^{-\delta-1}|k|^{1/3}\langle t\rangle^{1/3}}.

Therefore, if
k,l\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
, and

then

\begin{split}\frac{|t-s||k|^{1/2}\langle t\rangle^{6d}}{|l|^{1/2}\langle s\rangle^{6d}}&\frac{A^{\sharp}(t,k,tk)}{A^{\sharp}(s,k,tk)}e^{-\delta(s^{1/3}+|l|^{1/3})}\\

&\lesssim e^{-\delta|l|^{1/3}}e^{-\delta s^{1/3}/2}|t-s||k|^{1/2}(1+|t-s||k|^{1/3})^{-4}.\end{split}

Step 2.
We show now that for any

\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\}}\int_{t}^{\infty}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{R}^{\prime}}(l,s)\frac{|t-s||k|^{1/2}\langle t\rangle^{6d}}{|l|^{1/2}\langle s\rangle^{6d}}\frac{A^{\sharp}(t,k,tk)}{A^{\sharp}(s,k,tk)}e^{-\delta(|k-l|^{1/3}+|tk-sl|^{1/3})}\,ds\lesssim 1,

where

Recall the identity (
4.4
). Therefore, if

and

(4.8)

\frac{|t-s|\langle t\rangle^{6d}}{\langle s\rangle^{6d}}\frac{A^{\sharp}(t,k,tk)}{A^{\sharp}(s,k,tk)}e^{-\delta|tk-sl|^{1/3}}\lesssim\frac{|t-s|\langle t\rangle^{6d}}{\langle s\rangle^{6d}}e^{-\delta^{4}(1+t)^{1/4}}.

Moreover, if

Step 3.
Finally, we show that

\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\}}\int_{t}^{\infty}(1-\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{R}^{\prime}}(l,s))\frac{|t-s||k|^{1/2}\langle t\rangle^{6d}}{|l|^{1/2}\langle s\rangle^{6d}}\frac{A^{\sharp}(t,k,tk)}{A^{\sharp}(s,k,tk)}e^{-\delta(|k-l|^{1/3}+|tk-sl|^{1/3})}\,ds\lesssim 1,

where

is as in (

). We make the change of variables

and

then

(4.10)

\begin{split}\sum_{a\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},\,|a|\leq|k|/8}\int_{t}^{1.01t}\frac{|t-s|A^{\sharp}(t,k,tk)}{A^{\sharp}(s,k,tk)}e^{-\delta(|a|^{1/3}+|sa+(t-s)k|^{1/3})}\,ds\lesssim 1.\end{split}

We examine the identity (

\begin{split}\lambda^{\sharp}(s,|k,tk|)-\lambda^{\sharp}(t,|k,tk|)&\geq\delta^{2}|t-s|(1+s\langle k,tk\rangle^{-2/3})^{-1-\delta}\langle k,tk\rangle^{-1/3}\\

if

,

, and

Similarly, we calculate

(4.22)

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}^{\sharp,2,L}_{g_{1}-g_{2}}(t)=\delta I^{\sharp}(t)+\delta II^{\sharp}(t)+\delta III^{\sharp}(t)+\delta IV^{\sharp}(t),

where

(4.23)

\delta I^{\sharp}(t):=2\sum_{|a|\leq d^{\prime}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\langle k,\xi\rangle^{-4}A_{L}^{\sharp}(t,k,\xi)\dot{A_{L}^{\sharp}}(t,k,\xi)\big|D^{a}_{\xi}(\widehat{g_{1}}-\widehat{g_{2}})(t,k,\xi)\big|^{2}\,d\xi,

(4.24)

As before, we notice that

(4.27)

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}^{\sharp,2,L}_{g_{1}-g_{2}}(t)&\leq\mathcal{E}^{\sharp,2}_{g_{1}-g_{2}}(T_{2})+\int_{t}^{T_{2}}|\delta II^{\sharp}(s)|\,ds+\int_{t}^{T_{2}}|\delta IV^{\sharp}(s)|\,ds\\
&+\int_{t}^{T_{2}}\big[|\delta III^{\sharp}(s)|-|\delta I^{\sharp}(s)|\big]\,ds.\end{split}

As in the proof of Lemma
3.4
, we can use the bounds (
3.37
) and (
4.14
) to show that

(4.28)

\int_{t}^{T_{2}}\big|II^{\sharp}_{i}(s)\big|\,ds\leq\overline{B}^{2}\overline{\theta}^{2}/8\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\int_{t}^{T_{2}}\big|\delta II^{\sharp}(s)\big|\,ds\leq\overline{B}^{2}\theta_{0}^{2}/8,

for any

and

and
3.6

2.32

2.33

and

\int_{t}^{T_{2}}\big|IV^{\sharp}_{i}(s)\big|\,ds\leq\overline{\theta}^{2}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\int_{t}^{T_{2}}\big|\delta IV^{\sharp}(s)\big|\,ds\leq\theta_{0}^{2},

The desired bounds (

5.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

We use the following simple continuation criterion:

Lemma 5.1
.

Assume
T_{1}\leq T_{2}\in[0,\infty)
,
\lambda_{1}\in[0.6\lambda_{0},0.9\lambda_{0}]
, and
g\in X_{[T_{1},T_{2}]}
is a real-valued solution of the system (
1.9
) satisfying
\widehat{g}(t,0,0)=0
for any
t\in[T_{1},T_{2}]
. Assume that

(5.1)

\sup_{t\in[T_{1},T_{2}]}\mathcal{E}^{0}_{g}(t)\leq\rho_{0}^{2},

where
\rho_{0}=\rho_{0}(d,\lambda_{0},\vartheta)
is sufficiently small. Then
g
can be extended to a real-valued solution
g^{\prime}\in X_{[T_{1},T^{\prime}_{2}]}
of the system (
1.9
), for
T^{\prime}_{2}=T_{2}+c_{d,\lambda_{0},\vartheta}(1+T_{2})^{-2}
, satisfying

(5.2)

\sup_{t\in[T_{1},T^{\prime}_{2}]}\mathcal{E}^{0}_{g}(t)\leq 2\rho_{0}^{2}.

Proof.

This is a local well-posedness result and we can use a standard parabolic regularization argument to prove it. We provide some details for the sake of completeness.

For
\varepsilon>0
we are looking for solutions
g^{\varepsilon}\in X_{[T_{2},T_{2}^{\varepsilon}]}
of the equation (in the Fourier space)

(5.3)

\begin{split}&\partial_{t}\widehat{g^{\varepsilon}}(t,k,\xi)+\varepsilon(|k|^{2}+|\xi|^{2})\widehat{g^{\varepsilon}}(t,k,\xi)+\widehat{g^{\varepsilon}}(t,k,tk)\widehat{M_{0}}(\xi-tk)\frac{k\cdot(\xi-tk)}{|k|^{2}}\\
&\qquad+\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\}}\widehat{g^{\varepsilon}}(t,l,tl)\widehat{g^{\varepsilon}}(t,k-l,\xi-tl)\frac{l\cdot(\xi-tk)}{|l|^{2}}=0,\end{split}

with initial data
\widehat{g^{\varepsilon}}(T_{2})=\widehat{g}(T_{2})
(compare with (
2.1
)). Such solutions can be constructed by a fixed-point argument in the space
X_{[T_{2},T_{2}^{\varepsilon}]}
, using the identity

(5.4)

\widehat{g^{\varepsilon}}(t,k,\xi)=e^{-\varepsilon(|k|^{2}+|\xi|^{2})(t-T_{2})}\widehat{g}(T_{2},k,\xi)-\sum_{i\in\{1,2\}}\int_{T_{2}}^{t}e^{-\varepsilon(|k|^{2}+|\xi|^{2})(t-s)}\mathcal{M}_{i}(\widehat{g^{\varepsilon}})(s,k,\xi)\,ds,

where
T_{2}^{\varepsilon}-T_{2}
is sufficiently small (depending on
\varepsilon,T_{2},d,\lambda_{0},\vartheta
) and

(5.5)

\begin{split}&\mathcal{M}_{1}(f)(t,k,\xi):=f(t,k,tk)\widehat{M_{0}}(\xi-tk)\frac{k\cdot(\xi-tk)}{|k|^{2}},\\
&\mathcal{M}_{2}(f)(t,k,\xi):=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\}}f(t,l,tl)f(t,k-l,\xi-tl)\frac{l\cdot(\xi-tk)}{|l|^{2}}.\end{split}

We may assume that the solutions
g^{\varepsilon}
are real-valued and
\mathcal{E}^{0}_{g^{\varepsilon}}(t)\leq 1.5\rho_{0}^{2}
for any
t\in[T_{2},T_{2}^{\varepsilon}]
. As in the proof of Proposition
3.3
, we can estimate the energy increment by

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}_{g^{\varepsilon}}^{0}(t)\lesssim_{d,\lambda_{0},\vartheta}(1+T_{2})^{2}\mathcal{E}_{g^{\varepsilon}}^{0}(t).

Therefore the solution
g^{\varepsilon}
of (
5.3
) can be extended on the time interval
[T_{2},T^{\prime}_{2}]
,
T^{\prime}_{2}:=T_{2}+c_{d,\lambda_{0},\vartheta}(1+T_{2})^{-2}
, satisfying the uniform bounds
\mathcal{E}^{0}_{g^{\varepsilon}}(t)\leq 1.5\rho_{0}^{2}
for any
t\in[T_{2},T^{\prime}_{2}]
.

We can now let
\varepsilon\to 0
to construct the desired extension
g
. Indeed, let

\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}}(t):=\sum_{|a|\leq d^{\prime}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big|D^{a}_{\xi}\widehat{(g^{\varepsilon}-g^{\varepsilon^{\prime}})}(t,k,\xi)\big|^{2}\,d\xi.

As in the proof of Proposition
3.3
, we can estimate

|\partial_{t}\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}}(t)|\lesssim_{d,\lambda_{0},\vartheta}(\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{\prime})[\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}}(t)]^{1/2}+\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}}(t),

for any
t\in[T_{2},T^{\prime}_{2}]
. Since
\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}}(T_{2})=0
it follows that
\lim_{\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}\to 0}\|\mathcal{L}_{\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}}=0
. Therefore the sequence
g^{\varepsilon}
converges to a solution
g\in X_{[T_{1},T^{\prime}_{2}]}
of the equation (
2.1
), which satisfies the uniform bounds (
5.2
), as desired.
∎

Proof of Theorem

First, we combine Lemma

(both with

(i) Using the definition (

\big\|\langle\alpha\rangle D^{a}_{\alpha}L(\alpha+i\beta,k)\big\|_{L^{\infty}_{\alpha}}\lesssim\frac{(3a+5)!}{(|k|\lambda_{0}^{3})^{a+1}}.

It remains to prove pointwise estimates on higher order derivatives of

, of the form

(6.4)

\big\|\langle\alpha\rangle D^{a}_{\alpha}L^{\prime}(\alpha+i\beta,k)\big\|_{L^{\infty}_{\alpha}}\lesssim\frac{(3a)!(1.01)^{a}}{(|k|\lambda_{0}^{3})^{a+1}},

for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
,
\beta\in(-\infty,0]
, and
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. We start from the Faá di Bruno’s formula

(6.5)

D^{n}(g\circ f)(x)=\sum_{\ast}\frac{n!}{m_{1}!(1!)^{m_{1}}\cdot\ldots\cdot m_{n}!(n!)^{m_{n}}}D^{m_{1}+\ldots+m_{n}}g(f(x))\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{n}(D^{j}f(x))^{m_{j}},

for any
n\geq 1
and any sufficiently smooth functions
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
and
g:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
, where
\sum\limits_{\ast}
denotes the sum over all
n
-tuples of non-negative integers
m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}
satisfying

(6.6)

1\cdot m_{1}+\ldots+n\cdot m_{n}=n.

In our case, we fix
\beta\in(-\infty,0]
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
, and
g(y)=y/(y+1)
. Clearly
|D^{b}g(y)|\leq b!|y+1|^{-b-1}
for any integer
b\geq 0
, thus

|D^{n}_{\alpha}L^{\prime}(\alpha+i\beta,k)|\leq\sum_{\ast}\frac{n!(m_{1}+\ldots+m_{n})!}{m_{1}!\cdot\ldots\cdot m_{n}!\vartheta^{m_{1}+\ldots+m_{n}+1}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Big[\frac{D^{j}L(\alpha+i\beta,k)}{j!}\Big]^{m_{j}},

for any integer
n\geq 1
. It follows from (
6.3
) that

(6.7)

\langle\alpha\rangle|D^{n}_{\alpha}L^{\prime}(\alpha+i\beta,k)|\leq\sum_{\ast}[C_{1}^{\ast}]^{m_{1}+\ldots+m_{n}}\frac{n!(m_{1}+\ldots+m_{n})!}{m_{1}!\cdot\ldots\cdot m_{n}!}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Big[\frac{(3j+5)!}{j!(|k|\lambda_{0}^{3})^{j+1}}\Big]^{m_{j}},

for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{\ast}
, where
C_{1}^{\ast}=C_{1}^{\ast}(d,\lambda_{0},\vartheta)
is a constant. Thus

(6.8)

\begin{split}&\frac{\langle\alpha\rangle|D^{n}_{\alpha}L^{\prime}(\alpha+i\beta,k)|(|k|\lambda_{0}^{3})^{n+1}}{(3n)!}\leq\sum_{\ast}\frac{[C_{2}^{\ast}]^{m_{1}+\ldots+m_{n}}}{m_{1}!\cdot\ldots\cdot m_{n}!}\exp(H_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}}),\\
&H_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}}:=[\ln(n!)-\ln((3n)!)]+\sum_{j=1}^{n}m_{j}[\ln((3j+5)!)-\ln(j!)]+\big[\ln((m_{1}+\ldots+m_{n})!)\big],\end{split}

where
C_{2}^{\ast}=C_{2}^{\ast}(d,\lambda_{0},\vartheta)
is a constant.

To estimate the sum in the right-hand side of (
6.8
) we notice that

\Big|\ln 1+\ldots+\ln m-\int_{1/2}^{m+1/2}\ln x\,dx\Big|\leq C\qquad\text{ for any }m\geq 1,

where
C
is an absolute constant. Therefore

(6.9)

\big|\ln(m!)-(m+1/2)\ln(m/e)\big|\leq C^{(1)}\qquad\text{ for any }m\geq 1,

where
C^{(1)}\geq 1
is a absolute constant. Thus, for
n\geq 1
,

\ln(n!)-\ln((3n)!)\leq-2n\ln(n/e)-3n\ln 3+2C^{(1)}

and

\begin{split}\sum_{j=1}^{n}m_{j}[\ln((3j+5)!)-\ln(j!)]&\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}m_{j}[2C^{(1)}+(2j+5)\ln(j/e)+(3j+5.5)\ln((3j+5)/j)]\\
&\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}m_{j}[C^{(2)}+(2j+5)\ln(j/e)+3j\ln 3],\end{split}

where
C^{(2)}\geq 2C^{(1)}
is an absolute constant. In view of (
6.6
) we have
n=\sum_{j=1}^{n}jm_{j}
, thus

(6.10)

\begin{split}H_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}}&\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}m_{j}[C^{\prime\prime\prime}+(2j+5)\ln(j/e)-2j\ln(n/e)+\ln(m/e)]\\
&\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}m_{j}[C^{(3)}+7\ln(j/e)-(2j-1)\ln(n/j)],\end{split}

where
C^{(3)}\geq C^{(2)}
is an absolute constant and
m:=m_{1}+\ldots+m_{n}\leq n
. It is easy to see that

\sum_{j\in[1,2n/3]\cap\mathbb{Z}}m_{j}[C^{(3)}+7\ln(j/e)-(2j-1)\ln(n/j)]\leq\sum_{j\in[1,2n/3]\cap\mathbb{Z}}C^{(4)}m_{j}

for some constant
C^{(4)}\geq C^{(3)}
. Also

\sum_{j\in(2n/3,n]\cap\mathbb{Z}}m_{j}[C^{(3)}+7\ln(j/e)-(2j-1)\ln(n/j)]\leq C^{(3)}+7\ln n,

since
\sum_{j\in(2n/3,n]\cap\mathbb{Z}}m_{j}\leq 1
(due to the restriction (
6.6
)). Therefore it follows from (
6.8
) and (
6.10
) that

(6.11)

\frac{\langle\alpha\rangle|D^{n}_{\alpha}L^{\prime}(\alpha+i\beta,k)|(|k|\lambda_{0}^{3})^{n+1}}{(3n)!}\leq n^{7}\sum_{\ast}\frac{[C_{3}^{\ast}]^{m_{1}+\ldots+m_{n}}}{m_{1}!\cdot\ldots\cdot m_{n}!}

for any
n\geq 1
, where
C_{3}^{\ast}=C_{3}^{\ast}(d,\lambda_{0},\vartheta)
is a constant.

Let
C_{4}^{\ast}:=\sum_{m\geq 0}(C_{3}^{\ast})^{m}/(m!)
, let
\rho\in(0,1/2]
be such that
(C_{4}^{\ast})^{\rho}=1.005
, and let
J
denote the largest integer
\leq\rho n
. Notice that if the
n
-tuple
(m_{1},\ldots,m_{n})
satisfies (
6.6
) then

m_{J+1}+\ldots+m_{n}\leq 1/\rho.

In particular, the number of possible choices of the integers
m_{J+1},\ldots,m_{n}
is
\lesssim n^{1/\rho}
. Thus

\sum_{\ast}\frac{[C_{3}^{\ast}]^{m_{1}+\ldots+m_{n}}}{m_{1}!\cdot\ldots\cdot m_{n}!}\leq\sum_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{J}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\sum_{m_{J+1}+\ldots+m_{n}\leq 1/\rho}\frac{[C_{3}^{\ast}]^{m_{1}+\ldots+m_{n}}}{m_{1}!\cdot\ldots\cdot m_{n}!}\lesssim(C_{4}^{\ast})^{J}n^{1/\rho}.

The desired bounds (
6.4
) follow from (
6.11
).

(ii) We can complete the proof of the lemma. In view of the analyticity of
L^{\prime}
, we can write

\widehat{G}(s,k)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}L^{\prime}(\alpha+i\beta,k)e^{i(\alpha+i\beta)s}\,d\alpha=\frac{e^{-\beta s}}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}L^{\prime}(\alpha+i\beta,k)e^{i\alpha s}\,d\alpha.

We use the uniform bounds (
6.4
) and let
\beta\to-\infty
to conclude that
\widehat{G}(s,k)=0
if
s<0
.

To prove the main estimates (
2.11
) we may assume
|sk|\geq 1
, use the formula above with
\beta=0
, and integrate by parts
a\geq 1
times when
|\alpha|\leq e^{s|k|}
. Therefore for any
s\geq 1/|k|

\begin{split}2\pi|\widehat{G}(s,k)|&\leq\int_{|\alpha|\geq e^{s|k|}}|L^{\prime}(\alpha,k)|\,d\alpha+|s|^{-a}\Big|\int_{|\alpha|\leq e^{s|k|}}e^{is\alpha}(D^{a}_{\alpha}L^{\prime})(\alpha,k)\,d\alpha\Big|\\
&+\sum_{a^{\prime}=0}^{a-1}|s|^{-a^{\prime}-1}\big[|D^{a^{\prime}}_{\alpha}L^{\prime}(e^{s|k|},k)|+|D^{a^{\prime}}_{\alpha}L^{\prime}(-e^{s|k|},k)|\big].\end{split}

We estimate the first term using the
L^{2}
bounds (
2.9
) and the other two terms using the
L^{\infty}
bounds (
6.4
). Therefore for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
,
s\geq 1/|k|
, and
a\geq 1
we have

\begin{split}|\widehat{G}(s,k)|\lesssim e^{-s|k|/2}+|sk||s|^{-a}\frac{(3a)!(1.01)^{a}}{(|k|\lambda_{0}^{3})^{a+1}}+e^{-s|k|}\sum_{a^{\prime}=0}^{a-1}|s|^{-a^{\prime}-1}\frac{(3a^{\prime})!(1.01)^{a^{\prime}}}{(|k|\lambda_{0}^{3})^{a^{\prime}+1}}.\end{split}

Therefore for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
,
s\geq 1/|k|
, and
a\geq 1

(6.12)

\begin{split}|\widehat{G}(s,k)|\lesssim e^{-s|k|/2}+|sk|\frac{(3a)!(1.01)^{a}}{(s|k|\lambda_{0}^{3})^{a}}+e^{-s|k|}\sum_{a^{\prime}=0}^{a-1}\frac{(3a^{\prime})!(1.01)^{a^{\prime}}}{(s|k|\lambda_{0}^{3})^{a^{\prime}}}.\end{split}

We let now
a
denote the smallest integer
\geq\lambda_{0}(s|k|)^{1/3}/3
. Using the bounds (
6.9
) below,

\begin{split}\frac{(3a^{\prime})!(1.01)^{a^{\prime}}}{(s|k|\lambda_{0}^{3})^{a^{\prime}}}&\lesssim\exp\big[(3a^{\prime}+1/2)(\ln(3a^{\prime}+1)-1)-3a^{\prime}\ln(\lambda_{0}(s|k|)^{1/3})+a^{\prime}\ln(1.01)\big]\\
&\lesssim\exp\big[-3a^{\prime}+(1/2)\ln(3a^{\prime}+1)+a^{\prime}\ln(1.01)\big]\end{split}

for any integer
a^{\prime}\in[0,a]
. The desired bounds (
2.11
) follow from (
6.12
).

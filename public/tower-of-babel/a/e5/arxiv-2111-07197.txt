Regularity Results for an Optimal Design Problem with lower order terms
L. Esposito, L. Lamberti

Abstract
. We study the regularity of the interface for optimal energy configurations of functionals involving bulk energies with an additional perimeter penalization of the interface. It is allowed the dependence on ( x , u ) (x,u) for the bulk energy. For a minimal configuration ( E , u ) (E,u) , the Hölder continuity of u u is well known. We give an estimate for the singular set of the boundary ∂ E \partial E . Namely we show that the Hausdorff dimension of the singular set is strictly smaller than n − 1 n-1 .

Introduction and statements

In this paper we will deal with energy functionals of the type

{\mathcal{F}}(E,u;\Omega)=\int_{\Omega}\ \bigl[F(x,u,\nabla u)+\mathbbm{1}_{E}G(x,u,\nabla u)\bigr]\,\,dx+P(E,\Omega)\,,

(1)

where
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
and
\mathbbm{1}_{E}
denotes the characteristic function of a set
E\subset\Omega
with finite perimeter in
\Omega
, denoted as
P(E,\Omega)
.
In mathematical and physical literature, the problem of finding the minimal energy configuration of a mixture of two materials in a bounded connected open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
has been widely investigated (see for instance
[
1
]
,
[
2
]

[
10
]

[
13
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
17

elasticity, material sciences and image segmentations in the computer vision. A relevant case deeply studied by several authors is the following model functional,

\int_{\Omega}\sigma_{E}(x)|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx+P(E,\Omega),

(2)

where
u=u_{0}
is prescribed on
\partial\Omega
and
\sigma_{E}(x)=\beta\mathbbm{1}_{E}+\alpha\mathbbm{1}_{\Omega\setminus E}
, with
0<\alpha<\beta
given constant.

In

L. Ambrosio and G. Buttazzo in
[
1

proved that, if

is a minimizer of the functional
(

is locally Hölder continuous in
\Omega
and
E
is relatively open in
\Omega
. In the same year F.H. Lin proved the regularity of the interface (see
[

), that is
\partial E
is regular outside a relatively closed set of vanishing

To be more precise, we define the set of regular points of

\mbox{Reg}(E):=\left\{x\in\partial E\cap\Omega:\ \partial E\mbox{ is a }C^{1,\gamma}\mbox{ hypersurface in some }I(x)\right\}

(3)

denotes a neighborhood of

\Sigma(E):=(\partial E\cap\Omega)\setminus\mbox{Reg}(E).

(4)

In the paper by F.H. Lin (see
[

(

In 1999 F.H. Lin and R.V. Kohn in [LK] treated a more general quadratic bulk energy of the type

actually proving the same regularity they proved for the model case

In this paper we address the issue of improving the dimensional estimate for the singular part

Our path to prove the aforementioned result basically follows the same strategy used in [FJ]. As we will point out later in more detail, our technique relies on the linearity of the Euler equation of the functional

1

(6)

\displaystyle\nu|\xi|^{2}\leq a_{ij}(x,s)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\leq N|\xi|^{2},\;\;\;\nu|\xi|^{2}\leq b_{ij}(x,s)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\leq N|\xi|^{2}

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}|a_{i}(x,s)|+\sum_{i=1}^{n}|b_{i}(x,s)|+|a(x,s)|+|b(x,s)|\leq L

[

]
in the context of the Mumford and Shah functional and recently used in a paper by E. Mukoseeva and G. Vescovo,

6.

6.1.
Excess and height bound

Now we introduce the usual quantities involved in regularity theory. Given
x\in\partial E
, a scale
r>0
and a direction
\nu\in\mathbbm{S}^{n-1}
we define the
spherical excess

{\mathbf{e}}(x,r,\nu):=\frac{1}{r^{n-1}}\int_{\partial E\cap B_{r}(x)}\frac{|\nu_{E}(y)-\nu|^{2}}{2}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y),

and

{\mathbf{e}}(x,r):=\min_{\nu\in\mathbbm{S}^{n-1}}{\mathbf{e}}(x,r,\nu).

In addition we define the
rescaled Dirichlet integral
of

\mathcal{D}(x,r):=\frac{1}{r^{n-1}}\int_{B_{r}(x)}|\nabla u|^{2}dy.

The following height bound lemma is a standard step in the proof of regularity.

Lemma 6.1

(Height bound)
.

Let
(u,E)
be a
\Lambda
-minimizer of
\mathcal{F}
in

and
\varepsilon

\left\lVert\nabla u\right\rVert_{L^{2}(B_{r}(x_{0}))}

and

\sup_{y\in\partial E\cap B_{r/2}(x_{0})}\frac{|\langle\nu,y-x_{0}\rangle|}{r}\leq C{\mathbf{e}}(x,r,\nu)^{\frac{1}{2(n-1)}}

Proof.

The proof of this lemma is almost equal to the one of
[
20
, Theorem 22.8]
. As a matter of fact it follows from the density lower bound (see Theorem
4.2
), the relative isoperimetric inequality and the compactness result proved above.
∎

[

]
we give the following Lipschitz approximation lemma which is a consequence of the height bound lemma. Its proof follows exactly as in
[

, Theorem 23.7]

\begin{split}&\mathcal{F}_{r}(w,D):=\int_{B_{1}}\big[F_{r}(x,u,\nabla u)+\mathbbm{1}_{D}G_{r}(x,u,\nabla u)\big]\,dx\\

The argument used to prove the next result is similar to the one in

Theorem 6.4

(Weak Euler-Lagrange equation)

Let
(u,E)
be a
\Lambda r

\begin{split}\int_{\partial E}\textnormal{div}_{\tau}X\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\leq\overline{C}\int_{B_{1}}\big(|\nabla u|^{2}+r\big)\,dx+\Lambda r\int_{\partial E}|X|\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1},\end{split}

(76)

where

(77)

In order to obtain (
76

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{1}{t}[P(E_{t},B_{1})-P(E,B_{1})]=\int_{\partial E}\textnormal{div}_{\tau}X\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.

(78)

(79)

Thus we gain

\begin{split}&\int_{B_{1}}\big[F_{r}(y,u_{t},\nabla u_{t})+\mathbbm{1}_{E_{t}}(y)G_{r}(y,u_{t},\nabla u_{t})\big]\,dy\\
&=\int_{B_{1}}\big[F_{r}(\Phi_{t}(x),u,\nabla u)+\mathbbm{1}_{E}(x)G_{r}(\Phi_{t}(x),u,\nabla u)\big](1+t\textnormal{div}X)\,dx\\
&-t\int_{B_{1}}\big[2\big\langle C_{1}\nabla u\nabla X,\nabla u\big\rangle+\sqrt{r}\big\langle C_{2},\nabla u\nabla X\big\rangle\big]\,dx+o(t),\end{split}

where we set

C_{i}:=\tilde{A}_{ir}+\mathbbm{1}_{E}\tilde{B}_{ir}=A_{ir}(\Phi_{t}(x),u)+\mathbbm{1}_{E}(x)B_{ir}(\Phi_{t}(x),u),

for
i=1,2,3
. By simple calculations we obtain

\begin{split}&\int_{B_{1}}\big[F_{r}(y,u_{t},\nabla u_{t})+\mathbbm{1}_{E_{t}}(y)G_{r}(y,u_{t},\nabla u_{t})\big]\,dy\\
&-\int_{B_{1}}\big[F_{r}(x,u,\nabla u)+\mathbbm{1}_{E}(x)G_{r}(x,u,\nabla u)\big]\,dx\\
&=\int_{B_{1}}\big\{F_{r}(\Phi_{t}(x),u,\nabla u)+\mathbbm{1}_{E}(x)G_{r}(\Phi_{t}(x),u,\nabla u)-[F_{r}(x,u,\nabla u)+\mathbbm{1}_{E}(x)G_{r}(x,u,\nabla u)]\big\}\,dx\\
&+t\bigg[\int_{B_{1}}\big[F_{r}(\Phi_{t}(x),u,\nabla u)+\mathbbm{1}_{E}(x)G_{r}(\Phi_{t}(x),u,\nabla u)\big]\textnormal{div}X\,dx\\
&-\int_{B_{1}}\big[2\big\langle C_{1}\nabla u\nabla X,\nabla u\big\rangle+\sqrt{r}\big\langle C_{2},\nabla u\nabla X\big\rangle\big]\,dx\bigg]+o(t).\end{split}

(80)

Let us estimate the first of the three terms. By Lipschitz continuity and Young’s inequality we get

\begin{split}&\int_{B_{1}}\Big\{\big\langle F_{r}(\Phi_{t}(x),u,\nabla u)+\mathbbm{1}_{E}(x)G_{r}(\Phi_{t}(x),u,\nabla u)-[F_{r}(x,u,\nabla u)+\mathbbm{1}_{E}(x)G_{r}(x,u,\nabla u)]\Big\}\,dx\\
&\leq c(L_{D})t\int_{B_{1}}|X|[|\nabla u|^{2}+\sqrt{r}|\nabla u|+r]\,dx\leq c(L_{D})t\int_{B_{1}}|X|[|\nabla u|^{2}+r]\,dx.\end{split}

Finally, dividing by
t
and passing to the upper limit as
t\rightarrow 0^{+}
we infer

\begin{split}&\limsup_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{1}{t}\bigg[\int_{B_{1}}[F_{r}(y,u_{t},\nabla u_{t})+\mathbbm{1}_{E_{t}}(y)G_{r}(y,u_{t},\nabla u_{t})]\,dy\\
&-\int_{B_{1}}[F_{r}(x,u,\nabla u)+\mathbbm{1}_{E}G_{r}(x,u,\nabla u)]\,dx\bigg]\\
&\leq c(L_{D})\int_{B_{1}}|X|[|\nabla u|^{2}+r]\,dx+\int_{B_{1}}[F_{r}(x,u,\nabla u)+\mathbbm{1}_{E}G_{r}(x,u,\nabla u)]\textnormal{div}X\,dx\\
&-\int_{B_{1}}\big[2\big\langle(A_{1r}+\mathbbm{1}_{E}B_{1r})\nabla u\nabla X,\nabla u\big\rangle+\sqrt{r}\big\langle(A_{2r}+\mathbbm{1}_{E}B_{2r}),\nabla u\nabla X\big\rangle\big]\,dx.\end{split}

(81)

Passing to the upper limit as
t\rightarrow 0^{+}
and putting (
78
), (
79
), (
81
) together we get

\begin{split}&\int_{\partial E}\textnormal{div}_{\tau}X\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\\
&\leq c(L_{D})\int_{B_{1}}|X|[|\nabla u|^{2}+r]\,dx+\bigg|\int_{B_{1}}[F_{r}(x,u,\nabla u)+\mathbbm{1}_{E}G_{r}(x,u,\nabla u)]\textnormal{div}X\,dx\bigg|\\
&+\int_{B_{1}}\big|2\big\langle(A_{1r}+\mathbbm{1}_{E}B_{1r})\nabla u\nabla X,\nabla u\big\rangle+\sqrt{r}\big\langle(A_{2r}+\mathbbm{1}_{E}B_{2r}),\nabla u\nabla X\big\rangle\big|\,dx+\Lambda r\int_{\partial E}\lvert X\rvert\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\\
&\leq\overline{C}\int_{B_{1}}\big(|\nabla u|^{2}+r\big)\,dx+\Lambda r\int_{\partial E}|X|\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1},\end{split}

where
\overline{C}=\overline{C}(N,L_{D},\sup|X|,\sup|\nabla X|)
.

∎

7.
Excess improvement

Theorem 7.1

(Excess improvement)
.

For every
\tau\in\big(0,\frac{1}{2}\big)
and
M>0
there exists a constant
\varepsilon_{5}=\varepsilon_{5}(\tau,M)\in(0,1)
such that if
(u,E)
is a
\Lambda
-minimizer of
\mathcal{F}
in
B_{r}(x_{0})
with
x_{0}\in\partial E
and

{\mathbf{e}}(x_{0},r)\leq\varepsilon_{5}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{D}(x_{0},r)+r\leq M{\mathbf{e}}(x_{0},r)

(82)

then there exists a positive constant
C_{5}
, depending on
\left\lVert\nabla u\right\rVert_{L^{2}(B_{r}(x_{0}))}
, such that

{\mathbf{e}}(x_{0},\tau r)\leq C_{5}(\tau^{2}{\mathbf{e}}(x_{0},r)+\mathcal{D}(x_{0},2\tau r)+\tau r).

Proof.

Without loss of generality we may assume that
\tau<\frac{1}{8}
. Let us rescale and assume by contradiction that there exists an infinitesimal sequence
\{\varepsilon_{h}\}_{h\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{+}
, a sequence
\{r_{h}\}_{h\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{+}
and a sequence
\{(u_{h},E_{h})\}_{h\in\mathbb{N}}
of
\Lambda r_{h}
-minimizers of
\mathcal{F}_{r_{h}}
in
B_{1}
, with equibounded energies, such that, denoting by
{\mathbf{e}}_{h}
the excess of
E_{h}
and by
\mathcal{D}_{h}
the rescaled Dirichlet integral of
u_{h}
, we have

{\mathbf{e}}_{h}(0,1)=\varepsilon_{h},\quad\mathcal{D}_{h}(0,1)+r_{h}\leq M\varepsilon_{h}

and

{\mathbf{e}}_{h}(0,\tau)>C_{5}(\tau^{2}{\mathbf{e}}(0,1)+\mathcal{D}(0,2\tau)+\tau r_{h}),

with some positive constant
C_{5}
to be chosen.
Up to rotating each
E_{h}
we may also assume that for all
h\in\mathbb{N}

{\mathbf{e}}_{h}(0,1)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial E_{h}\cap B_{1}}\lvert\nu_{E_{h}}-e_{n}\rvert^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.

Step 1.
Thanks to the Lipschitz approximation theorem, for
h
sufficiently large, there exists a 1-Lipschitz function
f_{h}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that

\sup_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\lvert f_{h}\rvert\leq C_{3}\varepsilon_{h}^{\frac{1}{2(n-1)}},\quad\mathcal{H}^{n-1}((\partial E_{h}\Delta\Gamma_{f_{h}})\cap B_{\frac{1}{2}})\leq C_{3}\varepsilon_{h},\quad\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}^{n-1}}\lvert\nabla^{\prime}f_{h}\rvert^{2}\,dx^{\prime}\leq C_{3}\varepsilon_{h}.

(83)

We define

g_{h}(x^{\prime}):=\frac{f_{h}(x^{\prime})-a_{h}}{\sqrt{\varepsilon_{h}}},\quad\text{where}\quad a_{h}=\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}^{n-1}}f_{h}\,dx^{\prime}

and we assume up to a subsequence that
\{g_{h}\}_{h\in\mathbb{N}}
converges weakly in
H^{1}(B_{\frac{1}{2}}^{n-1})
and strongly in
L^{2}(B_{\frac{1}{2}}^{n-1})
to a function
g
.

We prove that
g
is harmonic in
B_{\frac{1}{2}}^{n-1}
. It’s enough show that

\lim_{h\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon_{h}}}\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}^{n-1}}\frac{\langle\nabla^{\prime}f_{h},\nabla^{\prime}\phi\rangle}{\sqrt{1+\lvert\nabla^{\prime}f_{h}\rvert^{2}}}\,dx^{\prime}=0,

(84)

for all
\phi\in C_{0}^{1}(B_{\frac{1}{2}}^{n-1})
; indeed, if
\phi\in C_{0}^{1}(B_{\frac{1}{2}}^{n-1})
, by weak convergence we have

\begin{split}&\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}^{n-1}}\langle\nabla^{\prime}g,\nabla^{\prime}\phi\rangle\,dx^{\prime}=\lim_{h\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon_{h}}}\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}^{n-1}}\langle\nabla^{\prime}f_{h},\nabla^{\prime}\phi\rangle\,dx^{\prime}\\
&=\lim_{h\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon_{h}}}\bigg\{\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}^{n-1}}\frac{\langle\nabla^{\prime}f_{h},\nabla^{\prime}\phi\rangle}{\sqrt{1+\lvert\nabla^{\prime}f_{h}\rvert^{2}}}\,dx^{\prime}+\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}^{n-1}}\bigg[\langle\nabla^{\prime}f_{h},\nabla^{\prime}\phi\rangle-\frac{\langle\nabla^{\prime}f_{h},\nabla^{\prime}\phi\rangle}{\sqrt{1+\lvert\nabla^{\prime}f_{h}\rvert^{2}}}\bigg]\,dx^{\prime}\bigg\}.\end{split}

Using the lipschitz-continuity of
f_{h}
and the third equation in (
83
) we infer that the second term in the previous equality is infinitesimal:

\begin{split}&\limsup_{h\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon_{h}}}\bigg|\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}^{n-1}}\bigg[\langle\nabla^{\prime}f_{h},\nabla^{\prime}\phi\rangle-\frac{\langle\nabla^{\prime}f_{h},\nabla^{\prime}\phi\rangle}{\sqrt{1+\lvert\nabla^{\prime}f_{h}\rvert^{2}}}\bigg]\,dx^{\prime}\bigg|\\
&\leq\limsup_{h\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon_{h}}}\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}^{n-1}}\lvert\nabla^{\prime}f_{h}\rvert\lvert\nabla^{\prime}\phi\rvert\frac{\sqrt{1+\lvert\nabla^{\prime}f_{h}\rvert^{2}}-1}{\sqrt{1+\lvert\nabla^{\prime}f_{h}\rvert^{2}}}\,dx^{\prime}\\
&\leq\limsup_{h\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\varepsilon_{h}}}\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}^{n-1}}\lvert\nabla^{\prime}\phi\rvert\lvert\nabla^{\prime}f_{h}\rvert^{2}\,dx^{\prime}\leq\lim_{h\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{C_{3}\left\lVert\nabla^{\prime}\phi\right\rVert_{\infty}\sqrt{\varepsilon_{h}}}{2}=0.\end{split}

Therefore, we can prove (
84
). We fix
\delta>0
so that spt
\phi\times[-2\delta,2\delta]\subseteq B_{\frac{1}{2}}
, choose a cut-off function
\psi\colon\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[0,1]
with spt
\psi\subseteq(-2\delta,2\delta)
,
\psi=1
in
(-\delta,\delta)
and apply to
E_{h}
the weak Euler-Lagrange equation with
X=\phi\psi e_{n}
. By the height bound, for
h
sufficiently large it holds that
\partial E_{h}\cap B_{\frac{1}{2}}\subseteq B_{\frac{1}{2}}^{n-1}\times(-\delta,\delta)
. Plugging
X
in the weak Euler-Lagrange equation and using the assumption in (
82
), we have

\begin{split}&-\gamma\int_{\partial E_{h}\cap B_{\frac{1}{2}}}\langle\nu_{E_{h}},e_{n}\rangle\langle\nabla^{\prime}\phi,\nu_{E_{h}}^{\prime}\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\\
&\leq c(N,L_{D},\phi,\psi)\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}}\big(|\nabla u_{h}|^{2}+r_{h}\big)\,dx+\Lambda r_{h}\int_{\partial E_{h}\cap B_{\frac{1}{2}}}\lvert\phi\psi\rvert\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\\
&\leq c(n,N,\Lambda,L_{D},\phi,\psi)\varepsilon_{h}.\end{split}

Therefore, if we replace
\phi
by
-\phi
, we infer

\lim_{h\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon_{h}}}\int_{\partial E_{h}\cap B_{\frac{1}{2}}}\langle\nu_{E_{h}},e_{n}\rangle\langle\nabla^{\prime}\phi,\nu_{E_{h}}^{\prime}\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=0.

(85)

Decomposing
\partial E_{h}\cap B_{\frac{1}{2}}=\big([\Gamma_{f_{h}}\cup(\partial E_{h}\setminus\Gamma_{f_{h}})]\setminus(\Gamma_{f_{h}}\setminus\partial E_{h})\big)\cap B_{\frac{1}{2}}
, we deduce

\begin{split}&-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon_{h}}}\int_{\partial E_{h}\cap B_{\frac{1}{2}}}\langle\nu_{E_{h}},e_{n}\rangle\langle\nabla^{\prime}\phi,\nu_{E_{h}}^{\prime}\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon_{h}}}\bigg[-\int_{\Gamma_{f_{h}}\cap B_{\frac{1}{2}}}\langle\nu_{E_{h}},e_{n}\rangle\langle\nabla^{\prime}\phi,\nu_{E_{h}}^{\prime}\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\\
&-\int_{(\partial E_{h}\setminus\Gamma_{f_{h}})\cap B_{\frac{1}{2}}}\langle\nu_{E_{h}},e_{n}\rangle\langle\nabla^{\prime}\phi,\nu_{E_{h}}^{\prime}\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+\int_{(\Gamma_{f_{h}}\setminus\partial E_{h})\cap B_{\frac{1}{2}}}\langle\nu_{E_{h}},e_{n}\rangle\langle\nabla^{\prime}\phi,\nu_{E_{h}}^{\prime}\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\bigg].\end{split}

(86)

Since by the second inequality in (
83
) we have

\bigg|\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon_{h}}}\int_{(\partial E_{h}\setminus\Gamma_{f_{h}})\cap B_{\frac{1}{2}}}\langle\nu_{E_{h}},e_{n}\rangle\langle\nabla^{\prime}\phi,\nu_{E_{h}}^{\prime}\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\bigg|\leq C_{3}\sqrt{\varepsilon_{h}}\sup_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\lvert\nabla^{\prime}\phi\rvert,

\bigg|\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon_{h}}}\int_{(\Gamma_{f_{h}}\setminus\partial E_{h})\cap B_{\frac{1}{2}}}\langle\nu_{E_{h}},e_{n}\rangle\langle\nabla^{\prime}\phi,\nu_{E_{h}}^{\prime}\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\bigg|\leq C_{3}\sqrt{\varepsilon_{h}}\sup_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\lvert\nabla^{\prime}\phi\rvert,

then by (
85
) and the area formula, we infer

0=\lim_{h\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{-1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon_{h}}}\int_{\Gamma_{f_{h}}\cap B_{\frac{1}{2}}}\langle\nu_{E_{h}},e_{n}\rangle\langle\nabla^{\prime}\phi,\nu_{E_{h}}^{\prime}\rangle\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=\lim_{h\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon_{h}}}\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}^{n-1}}\frac{\langle\nabla^{\prime}f_{h},\nabla^{\prime}\phi\rangle}{\sqrt{1+\lvert\nabla^{\prime}f_{h}\rvert^{2}}}\,dx^{\prime}.

This proves that
g
is harmonic.

Step 2.
The proof of this step now follows exactly as in [FJ] using the eight bound lemma and the revers Poincaré inequality. We give it here to be thorough. By the mean value property of harmonic functions, Lemma 25.1 in
[
20
]
, Jensen inequality, semicontinuity and the third inequality in (
83
) we deduce that

\begin{split}&\lim_{h\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{h}}\int_{B_{2\tau}^{n-1}}\lvert f_{h}(x^{\prime})-(f_{h})_{2\tau}-\langle(\nabla^{\prime}f_{h})_{2\tau},x^{\prime}\rangle\rvert^{2}\,dx^{\prime}\\
&=\int_{B_{2\tau}^{n-1}}\lvert g(x^{\prime})-(g)_{2\tau}-\langle(\nabla^{\prime}g)_{2\tau},x^{\prime}\rangle\rvert^{2}\,dx^{\prime}\\
&=\int_{B_{2\tau}^{n-1}}\lvert g(x^{\prime})-g(0)-\langle\nabla^{\prime}g(0),x^{\prime}\rangle\rvert^{2}\,dx^{\prime}\\
&\leq c(n)\tau^{n-1}\sup_{x^{\prime}\in B_{2\tau}^{n-1}}\lvert g(x^{\prime})-g(0)-\langle\nabla^{\prime}g(0),x^{\prime}\rangle\rvert^{2}\\
&\leq c(n)\tau^{n+3}\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}^{n-1}}\lvert\nabla^{\prime}g\rvert^{2}\,dx^{\prime}\leq c(n)\tau^{n+3}\liminf_{h\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}^{n-1}}\lvert\nabla^{\prime}g_{h}\rvert^{2}\,dx^{\prime}\\
&\leq\tilde{C}(n,C_{3})\tau^{n+3}.\end{split}

On one hand, using the area formula, the mean value property, the previous inequality and setting

c_{h}:=\frac{(f_{h})_{2\tau}}{\sqrt{1+\lvert(\nabla^{\prime}f_{h})_{2\tau}\rvert^{2}}},\quad\nu_{h}:=\frac{(-(\nabla^{\prime}f_{h})_{2\tau},1)}{\sqrt{1+\lvert(\nabla^{\prime}f_{h})_{2\tau}\rvert^{2}}},

we have

\begin{split}&\limsup_{h\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{h}}\int_{\partial E_{h}\cap\Gamma_{f_{h}}\cap B_{2\tau}}\lvert\langle\nu_{h},x\rangle-c_{h}\rvert^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\\
&=\limsup_{h\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{h}}\int_{\partial E_{h}\cap\Gamma_{f_{h}}\cap B_{2\tau}}\frac{\lvert\langle-(\nabla^{\prime}f_{h})_{2\tau},x^{\prime}\rangle+f_{h}(x^{\prime})-(f_{h})_{2\tau}\rvert}{1+\lvert(\nabla^{\prime}f_{h})_{2\tau}\rvert^{2}}^{2}\sqrt{1+\lvert\nabla^{\prime}f_{h}(x^{\prime})\rvert^{2}}\,dx^{\prime}\\
&\leq\lim_{h\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{h}}\int_{B_{2\tau}^{n-1}}\lvert f_{h}(x^{\prime})-(f_{h})_{2\tau}-\langle(\nabla^{\prime}f_{h})_{2\tau},x^{\prime}\rangle\rvert^{2}\,dx^{\prime}\leq\tilde{C}(n,C_{3})\tau^{n+3}.\end{split}

On the other hand, arguing as in Step 1, we immediately get from the height bound and the first two inequalities in (
83
) that

\lim_{h\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{h}}\int_{(\partial E_{h}\setminus\Gamma_{f_{h}})\cap B_{2\tau}}\lvert\langle\nu_{h},x\rangle-c_{h}\rvert^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=0.

Hence we conclude that

\limsup_{h\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{h}}\int_{\partial E_{h}\cap B_{2\tau}}\lvert\langle\nu_{h},x\rangle-c_{h}\rvert^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\leq\tilde{C}(n,C_{3})\tau^{n+3}.

(87)

We claim that the sequence
\{{\mathbf{e}}_{h}(0,2\tau,\nu_{h})\}_{h\in\mathbb{N}}
is infinitesimal; indeed, by the definition of excess, Jensen’2 inequality and the third inequality in (
83
) we have

\begin{split}&\limsup_{h\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{\partial E_{h}\cap B_{2\tau}}\lvert\nu_{E_{h}}-\nu_{h}\rvert^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\\
&\leq\limsup_{h\rightarrow+\infty}\bigg[2\int_{\partial E_{h}\cap B_{2\tau}}\lvert\nu_{E_{h}}-e_{n}\rvert^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+2\lvert e_{n}-\nu_{h}\rvert^{2}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial E_{h}\cap B_{2\tau})\bigg]\\
&\leq\limsup_{h\rightarrow+\infty}\bigg[4\varepsilon_{h}+2\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(B_{2\tau})\frac{\lvert((\nabla^{\prime}f_{h})_{2\tau},\sqrt{1+\lvert(\nabla^{\prime}f_{h})_{2\tau}\rvert^{2}}-1)\rvert^{2}}{1+\lvert(\nabla^{\prime}f_{h})_{2\tau}\rvert^{2}}\bigg]\\
&\leq\limsup_{h\rightarrow+\infty}\big[4\varepsilon_{h}+4\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(B_{2\tau})\lvert(\nabla^{\prime}f_{h})_{2\tau}\rvert^{2}\big]\\
&\leq\limsup_{h\rightarrow+\infty}\bigg[4\varepsilon_{h}+4\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}^{n-1}}\lvert\nabla^{\prime}f_{h}\rvert^{2}\,dx^{\prime}\bigg]\leq\lim_{h\rightarrow+\infty}[4\varepsilon_{h}+4C_{3}\varepsilon_{h}]=0.\end{split}

Therefore applying the reverse Poicaré’s inequality and (
87
) we have for
h
large that

\begin{split}{\mathbf{e}}_{h}(0,\tau)\leq{\mathbf{e}}_{h}(0,\tau,\nu_{h})\leq C_{4}(\tilde{C}\tau^{2}{\mathbf{e}}_{h}(0,1)+\mathcal{D}(0,2\tau)+2\tau r_{h}),\end{split}

which is a contradiction if we choose
C_{5}>C_{4}\max\{\tilde{C},2\}
.
∎

For every

\varepsilon_{6}=\varepsilon_{6}(\tau)>0

3.10

\tau\in(0,1)

\varepsilon_{h}

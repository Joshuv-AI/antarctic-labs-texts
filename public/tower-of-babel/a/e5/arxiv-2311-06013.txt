Spanners under the Hausdorff and Fréchet Distances
Tsuri Farhana, Matthew J. Katz

Abstract
We initiate the study of spanners under the Hausdorff and Fréchet distances. We show that any t t -spanner of a planar point-set S S is a t 2 − 1 2 \frac{\sqrt{t^{2}-1}}{2} -Hausdorff-spanner and a min ⁡ { t 2 , t 2 − t 2 } \min\{\frac{t}{2},\frac{\sqrt{t^{2}-t}}{\sqrt{2}}\} -Fréchet spanner. We also prove that for any t > 1 t>1 , there exist a set of points S S and an ε 1 {\varepsilon}_{1} -Hausdorff-spanner of S S and an ε 2 {\varepsilon}_{2} -Fréchet-spanner of S S , where ε 1 {\varepsilon}_{1} and ε 2 {\varepsilon}_{2} are constants, such that neither of them is a t t -spanner.

Introduction

Let
S
be a set of points in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. The
Euclidean graph
over
S
, denoted
G_{S}
, is the complete graph over
S
, in which the weight of an edge
(u,v)
is the Euclidean distance between its endpoints, denoted
d(u,v)
. A subgraph
H
of
G_{S}
is a
t
-spanner
of
S
, for a real number
t\geq 1
, if it is a
t
-spanner of
G_{S}
, that is, if for any pair of points
u,v\in S
, the length of the shortest path between
u
and
v
in
H
is at most
t\cdot d(u,v)
. In general, a path in
G_{S}
between
u
and
v
whose length is at most
t\cdot d(u,v)
, is called a
t
-path
.

Geometric spanners, i.e.,
t
-spanners of
G_{S}
, have been studied extensively over the years (see
[
5
]
), where the goal is often to construct a
t
-spanner, for a given
t>1
, with some desirable properties, such as, small number of edges, small weight, small degree, and small diameter (i.e., the maximum number of edges in a minimum-hop
t
-path).

In this paper we initiate the study of Hausdorff-spanners and Fréchet-spanners.
Before defining these spanners, let us recall the definitions of the corresponding distances, i.e., Hausdorff
[
3
]
(denoted
d_{H}
) and Fréchet
[
2
]
(denoted
d_{F}
), adapted to our specific setting. The
Hausdorff
distance between a segment
\overline{uv}
and a polygonal path
P(u,v)
between
u
and
v
is the maximum distance between a point
p
on
P(u,v)
and its closest point on
\overline{uv}
, that is,
d_{H}(P(u,v),\overline{uv})=\max\{d(p,\overline{uv})\,|\,p\in P(u,v)\}
. The
Fréchet
distance between
P(u,v)
and
\overline{uv}
is the minimum leash length, such that, a dog and its owner, both initially located at
u
, can walk along
P(u,v)
and
\overline{uv}
, respectively, from
u
to
v
, without backtracking.

We now define the notions of Hausdorff-spanner and Fréchet-spanner. Let
{\varepsilon}
be a non-negative real number.
A path
P(u,v)
in
G_{S}
between
u
and
v
is an
{\varepsilon}
-Hausdorff-path
(
{\varepsilon}
-Fréchet-path
), if
d_{H}(P(u,v),\overline{uv})\leq{\varepsilon}\cdot d(u,v)

(
d_{F}(P(u,v),\overline{uv})\leq{\varepsilon}\cdot d(u,v)
).
Thus, a sub-graph
H
of
G_{S}
is an
{\varepsilon}
-Hausdorff-spanner
(
{\varepsilon}
-Fréchet-spanner
), if
there exists in
H
an
{\varepsilon}
-Hausdorff-path (
{\varepsilon}
-Fréchet-path) between any two points
u
and
v
.

Let
S
be a set of points in the plane. We show that a
t
-spanner is an
{\varepsilon}_{1}
-Hausdorff-spanner, for
{\varepsilon}_{1}=f(t)
, and an
{\varepsilon}_{2}
-Fréchet-spanner, for
{\varepsilon}_{2}=f(t)
. We also prove that for any
t>1
, there exist an
{\varepsilon}_{1}
-Hausdorff-spanner
H_{1}
and an
{\varepsilon}_{2}
-Fréchet-spanner
H_{2}
, where
{\varepsilon}_{1}
and
{\varepsilon}_{2}
are constants, such that both
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
are
not
a
t
-spanner.

Finally, we show that a WSPD-based
t
-spanner is an
{\varepsilon}
-Fréchet-spanner, for
{\varepsilon}=\frac{t-1}{2t+2}
. This bound is significantly better than the general one (mentioned above) when
{\varepsilon}
is close to zero. Since, to construct an
{\varepsilon}
-Fréchet-spanner, using an arbitrary
t
-spanner and thus the general bound, we need to set
t
to
\frac{1+\sqrt{1+8{\varepsilon}^{2}}}{2}
, while to do so using a WSPD-based
t
-spanner, we need to set
t
to
\frac{1+2{\varepsilon}}{1-2{\varepsilon}}
. Notice that when
{\varepsilon}
is close to zero, the latter expression is much larger than the former one, which may be an advantage, since some of the spanner’s properties, such as its number of edges, depend on
t
.

Let

. We assume, without loss of generality, that

be a

Observation 2.1

Let

be the ellipse with foci points at

Lemma 1

P(u,v)
is a
\frac{\sqrt{t^{2}-1}}{2}
-Hausdorff-path.

Proof

Since

On the one hand, by Observation

is at most

We have shown that any

-path in

-Hausdorff-path, and therefore

\frac{\sqrt{t^{2}-1}}{2}
-Hausdorff-spanner. We conclude that

Corollary 1

Any t-spanner is an
{\varepsilon}
-Hausdorff-spanner, for
{\varepsilon}=\frac{\sqrt{t^{2}-1}}{2}
.

2.2
{\varepsilon}
-Hausdorff-spanners are not necessarily
t
-spanners

In this section we show that not every Hausdorff spanner is a Euclidean spanner. More precisely, we present an infinite sequence of graphs, such that all of them are
c
-Hausdorff spanners, for some fixed constant
c>0
, but for any
t\geq 1
, there exists a graph in the sequence that is not a
t
-spanner. Formally, we prove the following theorem.

Theorem 2.2

There exists a constant
c>0
, such that for any
t\geq 1
, one can construct a graph that is a
c
-Hausdorff-spanner and is not a
t
-spanner.

To prove the theorem, we define a sequence of graphs
F_{0},F_{1},F_{2}\ldots
as follows:
Let
F_{0}
be the unit line segment with endpoints
(0,0)
and
(1,0)
, that is, the endpoints are the vertices of
F_{0}
and the segment is its single edge. Now, for any
n>0
, we construct
F_{n}
from
F_{n-1}
, by considering each segment (i.e., edge)
s
of
F_{n-1}
and (i) partitioning
s
into three subsegments of equal length by adding two new vertices, (ii) forming an equilateral triangle with the middle subsegment as its base by adding a vertex on the outer side of the middle subsegment and connecting it to the endpoints of the middle subsegmet, and (iii) removing the middle subsegment. That is, the edge
s
is replaced by four edges obtained by adding three new vertices; see Figure
2
.

The curve that is obtained by applying this construction indefinitely is the fractal known as the Koch curve
[
4
]
; it is one of the three curves forming the Koch snowflake. It is well known that the length of the Koch curve is unbounded, that is, for every
l>0
, there exists an integer
n
, such that the length of
F_{n}
, i.e., the sum of its edge lengths, is greater than

.

The graphs
F_{0}
,
F_{1}
,
F_{2}
and
F_{3}
.

Since the length of the path between the extreme vertices of

, i.e., between its vertices at

and

, can be made arbitrarily long, we conclude that for any

, there exists an integer

, such that

is not a

-spanner.

We next show that there exists a constant

is a

3.1

.

Let
H
be a

-spanner,

, and let

. We assume, with loss of generality, that

and that

and

. Let

be a

. Then,

’s length is at most

We first prove that

. This bound is useful when

Bound 1 — The bound for large
t

Lemma 4

P(u,v)

\frac{t}{2}
-Fréchet-path.

Proof

Let

, that is, the distance from

) is equal to the distance from

Consider a dog and its owner, both walking from

and the owner is walking along
\overline{uv}
. Their hike consists of three stages. In the first, the owner is at
u
and the dog advances to
p
; in the second, the owner advances from
u
to
v
, while the dog stays at
p
; and in the third, the owner is at
v
and the dog advances from
p
to
v
.

We show that at any point along their hike, the distance between the dog and its owner does not exceed
t/2
. Indeed, let
p^{-}
be a point on
P(u,v)
, anywhere between
u
and
p
. Then, the distance from
u
to
p^{-}
(through
P(u,v)
) is at most
t/2
, and therefore
d(p^{-},u)\leq t/2
. Similarly, let
p^{+}
be a point on
P(u,v)
, anywhere between
p
and
v
. Then, the distance from
p^{+}
to
v
(through
P(u,v)
) is at most
t/2
, and therefore
d(p^{+},v)\leq t/2
. Finally, let
q
be a point on
\overline{uv}
. We need to show that
d(p,q)\leq t/2
. Indeed, if
q_{x}\leq p_{x}
, then
d(p,q)\leq d(p,u)\leq t/2
,
and if
q_{x}\geq p_{x}
, then
d(p,q)\leq d(p,v)\leq t/2
.

Corollary 2

Any
t
-spanner is an
{\varepsilon}
-Fréchet-spanner, for
{\varepsilon}=\frac{t}{2}
.

Bound 2 — The bound for small
t

Recalling Observation
2.1
, we observe that

Observation 3.1

Let
p
be any point on
P(u,v)
, then
(i)
-\frac{t-1}{2}\leq p_{x}\leq\frac{t-1}{2}
and
(ii)
-\frac{\sqrt{t^{2}-1}}{2}\leq p_{y}\leq\frac{\sqrt{t^{2}-1}}{2}
.

Lemma 5

P(u,v) is a
\frac{\sqrt{t^{2}-t}}{\sqrt{2}}
-Fréchet-path.

Proof

Consider a dog and its owner, both walking from
u
to
v
, where the dog is walking along
P(u,v)
and the owner is walking along
\overline{uv}
. We denote the location of the dog at time
0\leq\tau\leq 1
by
P(\tau)
, where
P(0)=u
,
P(1)=v
, and, for any
\tau_{1}<\tau_{2}
, the point
P(\tau_{2})
does not precede the point
P(\tau_{1})
(on
P(u,v)
). Moreover, we denote the
x
-coordinate of the rightmost point visited by the dog by time
\tau
by
x_{\tau}
, that is,
x_{\tau}=\max\{P(\tau^{\prime})_{x}\mid 0\leq\tau^{\prime}\leq\tau\}
.

The location of the person is determined by the location of the dog. More precisely, at time
\tau
the person is at
(\max\{x_{\tau}-\frac{t-1}{2},0\},0)
. Finally, if at time
\tau=1
the person is not yet at
v
, then she advances directly to
v
. Clearly, the person never moves backwards, since the function
x_{\tau}
is non-decreasing.

We now prove that the distance between the dog and its owner never exceeds
\frac{\sqrt{t^{2}-t}}{\sqrt{2}}
.
Let
0\leq\tau\leq 1
. We distinguish between three cases.

Case 1:
\max\left\{x_{\tau}-\frac{t-1}{2},0\right\}\leq P(\tau)_{x}
.

\begin{split}d(P(\tau),&(\max\{x_{\tau}-\frac{t-1}{2},0\},0))\leq\sqrt{(P(\tau)_{x}-(x_{\tau}-\frac{t-1}{2}))^{2}+P(\tau)_{y}^{2}}\\
&\leq\sqrt{(x_{\tau}-(x_{\tau}-\frac{t-1}{2}))^{2}+P(\tau)_{y}^{2}}\leq\sqrt{\left(\frac{t-1}{2}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{\sqrt{t^{2}-1}}{2}\right)^{2}}\\
&=\sqrt{\frac{t^{2}-2t+1}{4}+\frac{t^{2}-1}{4}}=\sqrt{\frac{2t^{2}-2t}{4}}=\frac{\sqrt{t^{2}-t}}{\sqrt{2}}\,,\end{split}

where the first inequality is true, since, if
\max\{x_{\tau}-\frac{t-1}{2},0\}\neq x_{\tau}-\frac{t-1}{2}
, then
x_{\tau}-\frac{t-1}{2}<0
and both
P(\tau)_{x}
and
(P(\tau)_{x}-(x_{\tau}-\frac{t-1}{2}))
are non-negative where the latter is greater than the former.

Case 2:
x_{\tau}-\frac{t-1}{2}>0
and
P(\tau)_{x}<x_{\tau}-\frac{t-1}{2}
.

We first observe that
P(\tau)_{x}\geq x_{\tau}-(t-1)
, since
t\geq x_{\tau}+(x_{\tau}-P(\tau)_{x})+(1-P(\tau)_{x})\geq x_{\tau}+(1-P(\tau)_{x})
. Now,

\begin{split}d(P(\tau),&(\max\{x_{\tau}-\frac{t-1}{2},0\},0))=d(P(\tau),(x_{\tau}-\frac{t-1}{2},0))\\
&=\sqrt{(P(\tau)_{x}-(x_{\tau}-\frac{t-1}{2}))^{2}+P(\tau)_{y}^{2}}=\sqrt{((x_{\tau}-\frac{t-1}{2})-P(\tau)_{x})^{2}+P(\tau)_{y}^{2}}\\
&\leq\sqrt{((x_{\tau}-\frac{t-1}{2})-(x_{\tau}-(t-1)))^{2}+P(\tau)_{y}^{2}}\\
&\leq\sqrt{\left(\frac{t-1}{2}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{\sqrt{t^{2}-1}}{2}\right)^{2}}=\frac{\sqrt{t^{2}-t}}{\sqrt{2}}\,.\end{split}

Case 3:
x_{\tau}-\frac{t-1}{2}\leq 0
and
P(\tau)_{x}<0
.

By Observations
3.1
, we have
-\frac{t-1}{2}\leq P(\tau)_{x}<0
.

\begin{split}d(P(\tau),&(\max\{x_{\tau}-\frac{t-1}{2},0\},0))=d(P(\tau),(0,0))=\sqrt{P(\tau)_{x}^{2}+P(\tau)_{y}^{2}}\\
&\leq\sqrt{\left(-\frac{t-1}{2}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{\sqrt{t^{2}-1}}{2}\right)^{2}}=\frac{\sqrt{t^{2}-t}}{\sqrt{2}}\,.\end{split}

We have shown that in all cases the distance between the dog and its owner is at most
\frac{\sqrt{t^{2}-t}}{\sqrt{2}}
. Moreover, if the dog reaches
v
first, then during the last part of the person’s hike, this distance only decreases.
We thus conclude that P(u,v) is a
\frac{\sqrt{t^{2}-t}}{\sqrt{2}}
-Fréchet-path.

Corollary 3

Any
t
-spanner is an
{\varepsilon}
-Fréchet-spanner, for
{\varepsilon}=\frac{\sqrt{t^{2}-t}}{\sqrt{2}}
.

Consider the graph

. One can prove that

-Fréchet-spanner, for

, in essentially the same way as we proved that it is a

4
The bound for WSPD-based
t
-spanners

We proved (Corollaries
2
and
3
) that
any

t
-spanner of a planar point-set
S
is an
{\varepsilon}
-Fréchet-spanner for
{\varepsilon}=\min\{\frac{t}{2},\frac{\sqrt{t^{2}-t}}{\sqrt{2}}\}
. In other words, if we wish to construct an
{\varepsilon}
-Fréchet-spanner of
S
for some
{\varepsilon}>0
, it is sufficient to construct a
t
-spanner of
S
for an appropriate value of
t
. The resulting spanner will have some desirable properties, depending on
t
and on the specific algorithm chosen to construct it. In general, it is beneficial to set
t
to the largest value for which our bounds guarantee that we get an
{\varepsilon}
-Fréchet-spanner. However, with our current general bounds, this value is very small, when
{\varepsilon}
is close to zero. For example, to obtain an
{\varepsilon}
-Fréchet-spanner for
{\varepsilon}=0.01
, we need to set
t
to
\frac{1+\sqrt{1+8{\varepsilon}^{2}}}{2}
, which is approximately
1.0002
.

It is likely that by considering a specific spanner-construction algorithm, one can get better bounds. Indeed, we derive below a better bound for the WSPD-based spanner, where the WSPD is computed using a split tree. Specifically, we show that any WSPD-based
t
-spanner is an
{\varepsilon}
-Fréchet-spanner for
{\varepsilon}=\frac{t-1}{2t+2}
. Thus, in the example above, we may set
t
to approximately
1.04
.

In more detail, given a separation factor
s>0
, we show that the WSPD-based spanner (using
s
as the separation factor) is a
\frac{2}{s}
-Fréchet-spanner. But, to ensure that the spanner is a
t
-spanner, we need to set
s=\frac{4t+4}{t-1}

[
5
]
, yielding the claimed bound, i.e.,
\frac{t-1}{2t+2}
.

We now sketch the proof of the claim that the WSPD-based spanner (using
s
as the separation factor) is a
\frac{2}{s}
-Fréchet-spanner. We begin by recalling a few well-known definitions.

Given a real number
s>0
, a
well-separated pair
with respect to
s
is a pair of two finite point-sets
A,B\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, such that there exist two disjoint
d
-dimensional balls
C_{A}
and
C_{B}
satisfying the following conditions: (i)
C_{A}
and
C_{B}
have the same radius
r
, (ii)
C_{A}
contains the bounding box
R(A)
of
A
, and
C_{B}
contains the bounding box
R(B)
of
B
, and (iii) The distance between
C_{A}
and
C_{B}
is at least
sr
.

Given a set
S\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
of
n
points and
s>0
, a
well-separated pair decomposition
(WSPD) of
S
with respect to
s

[
1
]
, is a sequence
\{A_{1},B_{1}\},\ldots,\{A_{m},B_{m}\}
of pairs of non-empty subsets of
S
, for some integer
m
, such that
(i)
A_{i}
and
B_{i}
are well-separated with respect to
s
, for
i=1,\ldots,m
, and
(ii) for any pair of points
p,q\in S
, there is exactly one pair
\{A_{i},B_{i}\}
, such that either
p\in A_{i}
and
q\in B_{i}
, or
q\in A_{i}
and
p\in B_{i}
. See Section
4.1
below for the definition of a split tree of
S
and for the algorithm for computing a WSPD of
S
using it.

Given a WSPD of
S
with separation
s
, a
WSPD-based
spanner has
S
as its vertex set and
E=\{(a_{1},b_{1}),\ldots,(a_{m},b_{m})\}
as its edge set, where
a_{i}
and
b_{i}
are arbitrary representatives of
A_{i}
and
B_{i}
, respectively.

We need to show that a WSPD-spanner,
G
, is a
\frac{2}{s}
-Fréchet-spanner, assuming the WSPD is computed using a
split tree
. That is, given a pair of points
u,v\in S
,
u\neq v
, we need to show that there exists a path
P(u,v)
in
G
between
u
and
v
, such
d_{F}(P(u,v),\overline{uv})\leq\frac{2}{s}\cdot d(u,v)
. Indeed, assume
u\in A_{i}
and
v\in B_{i}
. We first observe that
d_{F}(\overline{a_{i}b_{i}},\overline{uv})\leq\frac{2}{s}\cdot d(u,v)
. This is obvious, since
d(a_{i},u)\leq 2r
and
d(b_{i},v)\leq 2r
, and so
d_{F}(\overline{a_{i}b_{i}},\overline{uv})\leq 2r=\frac{2}{s}\cdot sr\leq\frac{2}{s}\cdot d(u,v)
. Now, since the WSPD is computed using a split tree, there exists a path
P(u,a_{i})
in
G
that passes only through points of
A_{i}
and is therefore contained in
C_{A_{i}}
, and there exists a path
P(b_{i},v)
in
G
that passes only through points of
B_{i}
and is therefore contained in
C_{B_{i}}
. (For completeness, we include a proof of the latter assertion, see Lemma
6
below, but we believe that it may be already known.) We consider the path
P(u,v)
that is obtained by concatenating the paths
P(u,a_{i})
and
P(b_{i},v)
, and assume the dog is walking along
P(u,v)
and its owner along
\overline{uv}
. The owner waits for the dog at
u
until it reaches
a_{i}
, then they walk simultaneously along their respective segments, i.e.,
\overline{a_{i}b_{i}}
and
\overline{uv}
, and finally, while the owner is at
v
, the dog walks from
b_{i}
to
v
. Clearly,
d_{F}(P(u,v),\overline{uv})\leq 2r=\frac{2}{s}\cdot sr\leq\frac{2}{s}\cdot d(u,v)
.

.

For a finite set of point

[

]
.
If

consists of single node that stores that point. Assume that

and

that are contained in these two new boxes. The split tree of

and

A WSPD of

(with separation

) is computed by calling ComputeWSPD(

,

), which calls FindPairs(

), for each internal node

Lemma 6

The spanner
G
has the following property. For any subset
C
participating in the WSPD of
S
(i.e.,
C=A_{i}
or
C=B_{i}
, for some
1\leq i\leq m
), and for any two points

, there exists a path
P
in
G
between
p
and
q
that passes only through points in
C
.

Proof

The proof is by induction on the size of
C
. If
|C|=1
, then the lemma is trivially true. Assume it is true for any subset
C
of size at most
n
, and consider a subset
C
of size
n+1
and two points

. Let
u
be the node of
T
, such that
C=S_{u}
.

If there exists a node
u^{\prime}
of
T
, such that

and

, then from

’s definition,

is a descendant of

and

.
From the Induction hypothesis, we get that there exists a path

in
G
from

to

that passes only through points in

.

Assume now that there is no node

as above. Then, either

and

, or

and

, and assume, without loss of generality, that

and

. The WSPD construction algorithm, ComputeWSPD(

,
s
), issues the call FindPairs(

,

), which returns a collection of well-separated pairs, including a unique pair

(

and

), such that

and

and

be the representatives of

and

in

between

Harnack inequality for the nonlocal equations with general growth Thanks: ∗ Corresponding author.
Yuzhou Fang and Chao Zhang ∗

Abstract
. We consider a class of generalized nonlocal p p -Laplacian equations. We find some proper structural conditions to establish a version of nonlocal Harnack inequalities of weak solutions to such nonlocal problems by using the expansion of positivity and energy estimates.

1.
Introduction

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\,(n\geq 2)
be a bounded domain. In this paper, we are interested in the following class of integro-differential equations with general growth

\mathcal{L}u=0\quad\text{in }\Omega

(1.1)

with

\mathcal{L}u(x):=\mathrm{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g\left(\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|u(x)-u(y)|}\frac{K(x,y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\,dy,

where the symbol
\mathrm{P.V.}
represents “in the principal value sense”,
s\in(0,1)
and the function
K(x,y):\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow(0,\infty]
is a symmetric measurable kernel such that

\frac{\Lambda^{-1}}{|x-y|^{n}}\leq K(x,y)\leq\frac{\Lambda}{|x-y|^{n}},\quad\Lambda\geq 1.

(1.2)

Particularly when
\Lambda=1
, Eq. (
1.1
) is called
s
-fractional
G
-Laplace equation. The function
g:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
is continuous and strictly increasing fulfilling
g(0)=0
,
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}g(t)=\infty
and

1<p\leq\frac{tg(t)}{G(t)}\leq q<\infty\quad\text{ with }G(t)=\int_{0}^{t}g(\tau)d\tau,

(1.3)

where
G(\cdot)
is an
N
-function possessing the
\Delta_{2}
and
\nabla_{2}
conditions (see Section
2
).

In recent years, a great attention has been concentrated on the nonlocal
p
-Laplacian problems, which is the special case that
g(t)=t^{p-1}
. For the regularity theory on this kind of problems, Kassmann
[
26
]
proved the nonlocal Harnack inequality with tail-term for the fractional Laplacian. Di Castro-Kuusi-Palatucci
[
10
]
further investigated the local behaviour of weak solutions incorporating boundedness and Hölder continuity in the spirit of De Giorgi-Nash-Moser iteration; see also
[
11
]
for the nonlocal Harnack inequalities. The Hölder regularity up to the boundary was whereafter showed by Iannizzotto-Mosconi-Squassina
[
25
]
. We also refer the readers to
[
1
]
for higher Sobolev regularity,
[
31
]
for self-improving properties,
[
28
,
30
]
for the viscosity and potential theory,
[
3
,
18
]
for fractional
p
-eigenvalue problems. When it comes to the parabolic counterpart, several features of solutions have already been studied, such as the local regularity
[
2
,
13
,
40
]
and the well-posedness
[
35
,
41
]
. For more results on the nonlocal nonlinear problems of the
p
-Laplacian type, one can see for instance
[
29
,
32
,
38
,
6
,
15
,
42
]
.

When
g(\cdot)
carries a more general structure, Eq. (
1.1
) can be viewed naturally as the nonlocal analogue of the
G
-Laplace equation whose classical model is

-\operatorname{div}\left(g(|\nabla u|)\frac{\nabla u}{|\nabla u|}\right)=0\quad\text{with }g(t)=G^{\prime}(t).

(1.4)

The so-called
G
-Laplace equations have been extensively studied over the past years. The regularity theory, especially for the scenario that
g(t)\approx t^{p}+t^{q}
, is initially explored by the celebrated papers of Marcellini
[
33
,
34
]
. More results on the generalized
p
-Laplace equations can be found in
[
12
,
24
,
39
]
. On the other hand, Fernández Bonder-Salort-Vivas
[
16
]
established the Hölder continuity for weak solutions to the fractional
g
-Laplacian with Dirichlet boundary values; see also
[
17
]
for the global regularity of eigenfunctions. Chaker-Kim-Weidner
[
8
]
proved, via De Giorgi classes, the interior regularity properties for the nonlocal functionals with
(p,q)
-growth and related equations. More recently, the weak solutions to (
1.1
) were proved to be locally bounded and Hölder continuous in
[
4
]
under the assumption (
1.3
). Regarding further studies of the nonlocal problems possessing non-standard growth, including also double phase equations and equations with variable exponents, one can refer to
[
9
,
14
,
5
,
7
,
37
,
23
,
19
,
20
]
and references therein.

Although pretty abundant research results have been obtained for the nonlocal problems with non-standard growth, to the best of our knowledge, there are few results regarding the pointwise estimates such as the Harnack inequalities. To this end, our aim of this manuscript is to investigate Harnack estimate for Eq. (
1.1
), which can be regarded as a natural outgrowth of the result in
[
11
]
. Due to the possibly inhomogeneous growth of the function
G
, we have to explore the suitable conditions on
G
in order to infer the desired result. Additionally, we need require that the Young functions
G
satisfies the following conditions:

G(t\tau)\leq c_{0}G(t)G(\tau)

(1.5)

and

\min\{t^{p},t^{q}\}\leq G(t)\leq c_{1}\max\{t^{p},t^{q}\}

(1.6)

for any
t,\tau\geq 0
and
c_{0},c_{1}
being two positive constants. Examples of
G
satisfying the requirements (
1.3
), (
1.5
) and (
1.6
) include

•

G(t)=t^{p}
,
t\geq 0
,
p>1
;

•

G(t)=\max\{t^{p},t^{q}\}
,
t\geq 0
,
1<p\leq q<\infty
;

•

G(t)=t^{p}+a_{0}t^{q}\text{ with }a_{0}>0
,
t\geq 0
,
1<p\leq q<\infty
;

•

G(t)=t^{p}\log(e+t)
,
t\geq 0
,
p>1
.

Before giving our main result, we introduce the so-called “tail space”,

L^{g}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\left\{u\text{ is measurable function in }\mathbb{R}^{n}:\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g\left(\frac{|u(x)|}{(1+|x|)^{s}}\right)\,\frac{dx}{(1+|x|)^{n+s}}<\infty\right\}.

The corresponding nonlocal tail of
u
is given by

\mathrm{Tail}(u;x_{0},R)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}(x_{0})}g\left(\frac{|u(x)|}{|x-x_{0}|^{s}}\right)\,\frac{dx}{|x-x_{0}|^{n+s}}.

(1.7)

Notice that
u\in L^{g}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if and only if
\mathrm{Tail}(u;x_{0},R)
is finite for any
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
R>0
. The details can be found in
[
4
, Subsection 2.3]
.

Now we are in a position to state the main result as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
s\in(0,1)
and the assumptions (
1.3
), (
1.5
) and (
1.6
) are in force. Let
u\in\mathbb{W}^{s,G}(\Omega)\cap L^{g}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a weak solution of Eq. (
1.1
) such that
u\geq 0
in
B_{R}:=B_{R}(x_{0})\subset\Omega
. Then, for every
B_{r}:=B_{r}(x_{0})\subset B_{\frac{R}{2}}(x_{0})
, we have the following nonlocal Harnack inequality

\displaystyle\sup_{B_{r}}u

\displaystyle\leq Cr^{s(1-\frac{q}{p})\frac{q}{\epsilon}}\max_{\iota\in\{1,\frac{q}{p},\frac{p}{q}\}}\left\{\left(\inf_{B_{r}}u+r^{s}g^{-1}(r^{s}\mathrm{Tail}(u_{-};x_{0},R))\right)^{\iota}\right\}

\displaystyle\quad+Cr^{s}g^{-1}(r^{s}\mathrm{Tail}(u_{-};x_{0},R)),

where
Tail
(\cdot)
is defined in (
1.7
),
u_{-}:=\max\{-u,0\}
, the positive constant
C
depends on
n,p,q,s,\Lambda
as well as the structural constants
c_{0},c_{1}
given by (
1.5
) and (
1.6
), and the absolute constant
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, coming from Lemma
3.3
below, is a priori determined by
n,p,q,s,\Lambda
.

Remark 1.2
.

Let us point out that the extra hypotheses (
1.5
) and (
1.6
) are only exploited in the proof of Theorem
1.1
below. The reason why we impose the additional strong conditions on
G
is that we need split the term
G(u)
into
G(u^{1-\varepsilon})G(u^{\varepsilon})
with
\varepsilon
being an arbitrary number in
(0,1)
, and then get the integral of
u^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}

(\varepsilon^{\prime}\in(0,1))
as the integrand, which enables us to apply Lemma
3.3
. Observe that, if
g(t)=t^{p-1}
, then
q=p
and

\displaystyle r^{s}g^{-1}(r^{s}\mathrm{Tail}(u_{-};x_{0},R))

\displaystyle=r^{s}\left(r^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}(x_{0})}\frac{u_{-}^{p-1}(x)}{|x-x_{0}|^{s(p-1)}}\frac{dx}{|x-x_{0}|^{n+s}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{\frac{sp}{p-1}}\left(R^{sp}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{R}(x_{0})}\frac{u_{-}^{p-1}(x)}{|x-x_{0}|^{n+sp}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}.

Hence, our result is reduced to the Harnack inequality obtained in
[
11
, Theorem 1.1]
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2

is devoted to deducing infimum estimates for weak supersolutions by employing the expansion of positivity. Finally, we prove the Harnack inequality in Section

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we shall give some basic inequalities, state notions of some functional spaces and weak solutions, and then provide a covering lemma.

In what follows, we denote by
C
a generic positive constant which may change from line to line. Relevant dependencies on parameters will be illustrated utilizing parentheses, i.e.,
C\equiv C(n,p,q)
means that
C
depends on
n,p,q
. Let
B_{r}(x_{0}):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x-x_{0}|<r\}

stand for the open ball with center
x_{0}
and radius

. If not important, or clear from the context, we do not denote the center as follows:
B_{r}:=B_{r}(x_{0})
. If

and

is a measurable subset with positive measure

(f)_{A}:={\int\hskip-10.81218pt-}_{A}f(x)\,dx=\frac{1}{|A|}\int_{A}f(x)\,dx.

The function

is an
N

G(0)=0,\quad\lim_{t\rightarrow 0+}\frac{G(t)}{t}=0\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{G(t)}{t}=\infty.

The conjugate function of
N
-function

is denoted by

G^{*}(t)=\sup_{\tau\geq 0}\{\tau t-G(\tau)\}.

From the relation (
1.3
), we now give several inequalities to be utilized later:

(

)

for

\begin{cases}a^{q}G(t)\leq G(at)\leq a^{p}G(t)&\text{{if }}a\in(0,1),\\[5.69054pt]
a^{p}G(t)\leq G(at)\leq a^{q}G(t)&\text{{if }}a\in(1,\infty)\end{cases}

(2.1)

and

\begin{cases}a^{p^{\prime}}G^{*}(t)\leq G^{*}(at)\leq a^{q^{\prime}}G^{*}(t)&\text{{if }}a\in(0,1),\\[5.69054pt]
a^{q^{\prime}}G^{*}(t)\leq G^{*}(at)\leq a^{p^{\prime}}G^{*}(t)&\text{{if }}a\in(1,\infty),\end{cases}

(2.2)

where

separately are the Hölder conjugates of

.

(

)

Young’s inequality with
\epsilon\in(0,1]

t\tau\leq\epsilon^{1-q}G(t)+\epsilon G^{*}(\tau),\quad t,\tau\geq 0.

(2.3)

(

)

for
t,\tau\geq 0
,

G^{*}(g(t))\leq(q-1)G(t),

(2.4)

and

2^{-1}(G(t)+G(\tau))\leq G(t+\tau)\leq 2^{q-1}(G(t)+G(\tau)).

(2.5)

Moreover, the function
G
fulfills the following
\Delta_{2}
and
\nabla_{2}
conditions (see
[
36
, Proposition 2.3]

(
\Delta_{2}
)

there is a constant
\mu>1
such that
G(2t)\leq\mu G(t)
for
t\geq 0
;

(
\nabla_{2}
)

there is a constant

such that
G(t)\leq\frac{1}{2\nu}G(\nu t)
for

,

where

depend on

.

We next introduce the notion of Orlicz-Sobolev spaces. For an
N
-function
G
with the
\Delta_{2}
and
\nabla_{2}
conditions, the Orlicz space
L^{G}(\Omega)
is defined as

L^{G}(\Omega)=\left\{u\text{ is measurable function in }\Omega:\int_{\Omega}G(|u(x)|)\,dx<\infty\right\}

equipped with the Luxemburg norm

\|u\|_{L^{G}(\Omega)}=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\int_{\Omega}G\left(\frac{|u(x)|}{\lambda}\right)\,dx\leq 1\right\}.

The fractional Orlicz-Sobolev space
W^{s,G}(\Omega)
(
s\in(0,1)
) is given by

W^{s,G}(\Omega)=\left\{u\in L^{G}(\Omega):\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}G\left(\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\,\frac{dxdy}{|x-y|^{n}}<\infty\right\}

endowed with the norm

\|u\|_{W^{s,G}(\Omega)}=\|u\|_{L^{G}(\Omega)}+[u]_{s,G,\Omega},

where
[u]_{s,G,\Omega}
is the Gagliardo semi-norm defined as

[u]_{s,G,\Omega}=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}G\left(\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{\lambda|x-y|^{s}}\right)\,\frac{dxdy}{|x-y|^{n}}\leq 1\right\}.

Let
C_{\Omega}\equiv(\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n})\cup(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Omega)
. For measurable function
u
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we define

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}^{s,G}(\Omega)=\left\{u\big|_{\Omega}\in L^{G}(\Omega):\iint_{C_{\Omega}}G\left(\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\,\frac{dxdy}{|x-y|^{n}}<\infty\right\},

which is the space weak solutions of (
1.1
) belong to.

Now we give the definition of weak solutions to (
1.1
).

Definition 2.1
.

We call
u\in\mathbb{W}^{s,G}(\Omega)
a weak supersolution of Eq. (
1.1
) if

\iint_{C_{\Omega}}g\left(\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|u(x)-u(y)|}(\psi(x)-\psi(y))\frac{K(x,y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\,dxdy\geq 0

(2.6)

for each nonnegative function
\psi\in\mathbb{W}^{s,G}(\Omega)
with compact support in
\Omega
. For weak subsolution, the above inequality is reversed.
u\in\mathbb{W}^{s,G}(\Omega)
is a weak solution to (
1.1
) if and only if it is both a weak supersolution and a weak subsolution.

We conclude this section with presenting the Krylov-Sofonov covering lemma (see for instance
[
27
]
) playing an important role in proving Lemma
3.3
below.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\overline{\delta}\in(0,1)
and
E\subset B_{r}(x_{0})
be a measurable set. Denote

[E]_{\overline{\delta}}=\bigcup_{\rho>0}\{B_{3\rho}(x)\cap B_{r}(x_{0}),x\in B_{r}(x_{0}):|E\cap B_{3\rho}(x)|>\overline{\delta}|B_{\rho}(x)|\}.

Then one of the following must hold:

(i)

|[E]_{\overline{\delta}}|\geq\frac{c(n)}{\overline{\delta}}|E|;

(ii)

[E]_{\overline{\delta}}=B_{r}(x_{0}).

3.
Expansion of positivity

This section is devoted to deriving the infimum estimates on the weak supersolutions of (

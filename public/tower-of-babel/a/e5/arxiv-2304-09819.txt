Old and new motivic cycles on Abelian surfaces
Ramesh Sreekantan

Abstract
Collino [ Col02 ] discovered indecomposable motivic cycles in the group H ℳ 2 ​ g − 1 ​ ( J ⁡ ( C ) , ℤ ⁡ ( g ) ) H^{2g-1}_{{\mathcal{M}}}(J(C),{\mathds{Z}}(g)) . In [ Sre22 ] we described the construction of some new motivic cycles which can be viewed as a generalization of Collino’s cycle when g = 2 g=2 . In this paper we show that our new cycles are in fact related to Collino’s cycles of higher genus. On one hand this suggests that new cycles are hard to find. On the other, it suggests that the tools developed to study Collino’s cycle can be applied to our cycles.



1
Introduction

1.1
Algebraic Cycles

Let
(C,P
) be a pointed curve of genus
g\geq 3
which is not hyperelliptic. A very important cycle, at least from the point of view of algebraic cycles, is the
Ceresa cycle
in the Jacobian of
non-hyperelliptic curves
of genus
g>3
given by

Z_{P}=C_{P}-(-1)^{*}(C_{P})

where
C_{P}
is the image of
C
under the map
\iota_{P}(x)=x-P
. This is a null homologous cycle lying in the group
CH^{g-1}_{hom}(J(C))
. In general it is
not
algebraically equivalent to
0
and is hence a non-trivial element of the Griffiths group of
J(C)

[
Cer83
]
.

Collino
[
Col97
]
discovered a higher Chow cycle
Z_{P_{1},P_{2},\tilde{R}}
in the group
CH^{g}(J(C),1)
where
J(C)
is the Jacobian of a
hyperelliptic curve
. This can be viewed as a degeneration of the Ceresa cycle and depends on the curve
C
along with a function with divisor supported on two Weierstrass points
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
. To determine the function precisely we have to choose a third point
\tilde{R}
where the function takes the value
1
for which it is convenient to choose another Weierstrass point. Analogous to Ceresa’s result, it provides an example of a generically
indecomposable cycle
, which roughly means that is not the product of cycles in other Chow groups.

In the case of genus
2
, when all curves are hyperelliptic, Collino’s element is defined in fibres over the complement of the moduli of products of elliptic curves in the moduli of Abelian surfaces. In
[
Sre22
]
we generalised Collino’s cycles to cycles defined in the fibres over the complement of certain
Noether-Lefschetz divisors
, which are surfaces on the modular threefold where the Neron-Severi group of the corresponding Abelian surface has an element which is not a multiple of the class of principal polarization. These divisors can also be understood as components of the image of the moduli of Abelian surfaces with real multiplication or moduli of products of elliptic curves. These are also called
Humbert surfaces
or
Heegner divisors
. Collino’s cycle is defined in the complement of the Humbert surface of invariant
1
.

There is a classical result of Kummer which gives a bijective correspondence between Abelian surfaces on the one hand and configurations of six lines in
{\mathds{P}}^{2}
tangent to a conic on the other. Humbert, and more recently Birkenhake-Wilhelm, related the existence of rational curves which have exceptional intersection with this configuration of lines to the corresponding Abelian surface having exceptional divisors, or equivalently, the moduli point lying on a Noether-Lefschetz divisor.

In this paper we recall the construction of the new motivic cycles which are defined in the complement of such Noether-Lefschetz divisors. The main theorem states that in fact these cycles are related to Collino’s cycles in higher genus hyperelliptic curves.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{Q}}_{0}
be a rational curve in
{\mathds{P}}^{2}
which determines a certain Noether-Lefschetz divisor as in Secton
3
. Let
A
be an Abelian surface whose moduli point does not lie on that divisor. Let
{\mathcal{Z}}_{{\mathcal{Q}}_{0},P,R}
be the cycle in
H^{3}_{{\mathcal{M}}}(A,{\mathds{Q}}(2))
determined by
{\mathcal{Q}}_{0}
, a node
P
and an auxilliary point
R
as in Theorem
2.1
. Then there is a hyperellipic curve
{\tilde{D}}
mapping to a curve
D
on
A
and points
P_{1},P_{2}
and
\tilde{R}
on
{\tilde{D}}
such that under the induced map from the Jacobian

\pi:J({\tilde{D}})\rightarrow A

\pi_{*}(Z_{P_{1},P_{2},\tilde{R}})=2{\mathcal{Z}}_{{\mathcal{Q}}_{0},P,R}

where
Z_{P_{1},P_{2},\tilde{R}}
is the cycle constructed by Collino in
CH^{g}(J({\tilde{D}}),1)=H^{2g-1}_{{\mathcal{M}}}(J({\tilde{D}}),{\mathds{Z}}(g))
as in section
2.2
.

On one hand, the theorem shows that perhaps there are not too many ways to construct algebraic cycles. Two seemingly unrelated constructions lead to the essentially the same cycle. On the other hand it allows one to relate the new cycle to certain extensions of the mixed Hodge structure on the fundamental group of a curve. It is known that Abel Jacobi image of the Ceresa cycle
[
Hai87
,
Pul88
,
Kae01
,
Rab01
]
and the regulator of the Collino cycle
[
Col02
,
SS20
]
are related to explicit extensions of the mixed Hodge structure on the fundamental group of the curve. So one can similarly relate the regulator of the new cycles to extensions of the the fundamental group.

2
Motivic Cycles

In this section we introduce the background on motivic cycles and Collino’s cycle.

2.1
Motivic Cohomology Cycles

Let
X
be a smooth projective algebraic variety of dimension
g
defined over
{\mathds{C}}
. The motivic cohomology group
H^{2g-1}_{{\mathcal{M}}}(X,{\mathds{Z}}(g))
has the following presentation: Generators are represented by finite sums

Z=\sum_{i}(C_{i},f_{i})

where
C_{i}
are curves on
X
and
f_{i}:C_{i}\longrightarrow{\mathds{P}}^{1}
are functions on them subject to the co-cycle condition

\sum_{i}\operatorname{div}(f_{i})=0

Relations in this group are defined as follows. If
Y
is a surface on
X
and
f
and
g
are functions on
Y
, one has the Steinberg element
\{f,g\}
in
K_{2}({\mathds{C}}(Y))
, where
{\mathds{C}}(Y)
is the function field of
Y
. To such an element one can consider the sum, called the tame symbol of
\{f,g\}
,

\tau(\{f,g\})=\sum_{W\in Y^{(1)}}(W,(-1)^{\operatorname{ord}_{W}(f)\operatorname{ord}_{W}(g)}\frac{f^{\operatorname{ord}_{W}(g)}}{g^{\operatorname{ord}_{W}(f)}}).

where
Y^{(1)}
is the collection of curves on
Y
. This is a finite sum and satisfies the co-cycle condition, hence lies in the above group. An element is said to be
0
in
H^{2g-1}_{{\mathcal{M}}}(X,{\mathds{Z}}(g))
if it lies in the image of the free abelian group generated by the tame symbols of elements of
K_{2}({\mathds{C}}(Y))
for some surface
Y\subset X
. The group
H^{2g-1}_{{\mathcal{M}}}(X,{\mathds{Z}}(g))
is the same as the higher Chow group
CH^{g}(X,1)
.

In the group
H^{2g-1}_{{\mathcal{M}}}(X,{\mathds{Z}}(g))
there are certain
decomposable
cycles coming from the product

H^{2g-1}_{{\mathcal{M}}}(X,{\mathds{Z}}(g))_{dec}=\operatorname{Im}\left(H^{2g-2}_{{\mathcal{M}}}(X,{\mathds{Z}}(g-1)\otimes H^{1}_{{\mathcal{M}}}(X,{\mathds{Z}}(1)))\longrightarrow H^{2g-1}_{{\mathcal{M}}}(X,{\mathds{Z}}(g))\right).

This is simply the image of
CH^{g-1}(X)\otimes{\mathds{C}}^{*}
- namely elements of the form
(C,a)
where
C
is a curve and
a
is a constant function.

The group of
indecomposable
cycles is defined as the quotient

H^{2g-1}_{{\mathcal{M}}}(X,{\mathds{Z}}(g))_{ind}=H^{2g-1}_{{\mathcal{M}}}(X,{\mathds{Z}}(g))/H^{2g-1}_{{\mathcal{M}}}(X,{\mathds{Z}}(g))_{dec}.

In general it is not easy to find non trivial elements in this group.

If
X
is a surface, one way of constructing a possibly non-trivial cycle is the following. Since we will use it later we label it a proposition.

Proposition 2.1
.

Suppose
C
is a
nodal rational
curve on a surface
X
with node
P
. Let
\pi:\tilde{X}\rightarrow X
denote the blow up of
X
at
P
such that the strict transform
\tilde{C}
of
C
no longer has a node at
P
. Let
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
be the two points lying over
P
on
\tilde{C}
. If
E_{P}
is the exceptional fibre it is a rational curve and it meets the strict transform at the points
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
. Let
f
be a function with
\operatorname{div}(f)=P_{1}-P_{2}
on
\tilde{C}
and let
g
be a function on
E_{P}
with divisor
\operatorname{div}(g)=P_{2}-P_{1}
. Then

\pi_{*}((\tilde{C},f)+(E_{P},g))

is a cycle in
H^{3}_{{\mathcal{M}}}(X,{\mathds{Z}}(2))
.

2.2
Old cycles: Collino’s cycle

Let
C
be a hyperelliptic curve of genus
g
and
J(C)
its Jacobian. Collino
[
Col97
]
constructed cycle in the group
H^{2g-1}_{{\mathcal{M}}}(J(C),{\mathds{Z}}(g))=CH^{g}(J(C),1)
as follows:

Let
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
be two Weierstrass points or, equivalently, two of the ramification points of the map to
{\mathds{P}}^{1}
. Let
{\tilde{R}}
be a third point on
C
distinct from
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
. There exists a function
f
on
C
with

\operatorname{div}(f)=2P_{1}-2P_{2}

we further assume, though not strictly necessary for the purpose of constructing the cycle, that
{\tilde{R}}
is also a Weierstrass point and that
f({\tilde{R}})=1
.

Let
\iota_{P_{i}}:C\rightarrow J(C)
be the map

\iota_{P_{i}}(x)=x-P_{i}

Let
C_{P_{i}}=\iota_{P_{i}}(C)
. Since
P_{1}-P_{2}
is a two torsion point on
J(C)
,
P_{1}-P_{2}=P_{2}-P_{1}
. One has
C_{P_{1}}\cap C_{P_{2}}=\{O,P_{1}-P_{2}\}
where
O
is the identity in
J(C)
. Let
f_{P_{i}}
be the function
f
considered as a function on
C_{P_{i}}
. Then

\operatorname{div}(f_{P_{1}})=2(0)-2(P_{2}-P_{1})\text{ and }\operatorname{div}(f_{P_{2}})=2(P_{1}-P_{2})-2(O)

Hence
Z_{P_{1},P_{2},R}=(C_{P_{1}},f_{P_{1}})+(C_{P_{2}},f_{P_{2}})
satisfies the cocycle condition and determines an element of the motivic cohomology group
H^{2g-1}_{{\mathcal{M}}}(J(C),{\mathds{Z}}(g)
.

This cycle is defined as long as the hyperelliptic curve is irreducible. Collino
[
Col97
]
shows futher that it is generically indecomposable. In the case of genus
2
the cycle is defined on the complement of a component of the moduli of products of elliptic curves.

3
New cycles on the moduli of Abelian sufaces

In this section we describe the construction of new cycles in the generic fibre of the universal Abelian surface over the Siegel modular threefold with full level
2
structure. The idea is to use Proposition
2.1
– but we do not have any rational curves on an Abelian variety. Hence we have to go to an auxilliary variety, the Kummer
K3
surface. This construction is done in
[
Sre22
]
but we will recall it here.

3.1
Humbert Surfaces

Over the Siegel modular threefold the Picard number of the generic Abelian surface is
1
. There are certain divisors corresponding to the moduli of those Abelian surfaces where the Picard number is at least
2
. These are called
Noether-Lefshetz
divisors, or
Heegner
divisors, or
Humbert
surfaces as they were first studied by Humbert.

Let
A=A_{z}
be an Abelian surface corresponding to a point
z
on the Siegel modular threefold. Let
\theta
be its principal polarisation, so
\theta^{2}=2
. If
D
is in
NS(A)
define its
Humbert invariant

\Delta(D)=(D.\theta)^{2}-2D^{2}

This is essentially the negative of the intersection pairing on the orthogonal complement of the class of
\theta
in the Picard lattice
NS(A_{z})
. In particular it is non-negative and is non-zero if and only if
D\notin{\mathds{Z}}\cdot\theta
.

Let
H_{\Delta}
be the closure of the moduli of those Abelian surfaces in whose Picard lattice there is an element of Humbert invariant
\Delta
.
H_{\Delta}
is called the
Humbert Surface of invariant
\Delta
. These is the same as Heegner divisors
H(\gamma,N)
or Noether-Lefschetz divisors
D_{M,r}
for some choices of
N,\gamma,M
and
r

[
Pet15
]
. Note that
H_{1}
is the moduli of products of elliptic curves.

3.2
The Theorem of Birkenhake and Wilhelm

Birkenhake and Wilhelm
[
BW03
]
determine conditions under which the moduli of an Abelian surface lies on
H_{\Delta}
for certain
\Delta
. This is a generalisation of an old theorem of Humbert
[
Hum01
]
for
\Delta=5
. The idea is the following: Given an Abelian surface
A=J(H)
for some genus
2
curve
H
, the square of the principal polarization determines a map
\phi:A\rightarrow{\mathds{P}}^{3}
. This is a double cover ramified at the
16
two torsion points. The image
K_{A}
is called the
Kummer surface
and is isomorphic to
A/\pm 1
. It is a quartic hypersurface in
{\mathds{P}}^{3}
. Projecting from the image of
0
gives a map
\psi:K_{A}\rightarrow{\mathds{P}}^{2}
and it turns out that this is a double cover ramified at six lines tangent to a conic. Let
{\mathds{P}}^{2}_{A}=({\mathds{P}}^{2},l_{1},\dots,l_{6})
be the collection of
{\mathds{P}}^{2}
with the six lines. Let
S_{A}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{6}l_{i}
. This is a degenerate sextic with fifteen nodes corresponding to the images of the fifteen non-zero two torsion points. One has maps

A\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi}}{{\longrightarrow}}K_{A}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\psi}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\mathds{P}}^{2}_{A}.

If one blows up
K_{A}
at the sixteen points then one obtains a
K3
surface
{\tilde{K}}_{A}
which we will refer to as the
Kummer
K3
surface
.

Hence to an Abelian surface, one can canonically associate a configuration of six lines in
{\mathds{P}}^{2}
tangent to a conic. Conversely, a degenerate sextic
S_{A}\subset{\mathds{P}}^{2}
tangent to a conic determines a principally polarised Abelian surface.

Given this correspondence one can ask what conditions on
{\mathds{P}}^{2}_{A}
determines if the moduli point of the corresponding Abelian surface lies on
H_{\Delta}
for some
\Delta
?. Humbert
[
Hum01
]
proved the following theorem.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
A
be an Abelian surface and
{\mathds{P}}^{2}_{A}
the corresponding configulation of
{\mathds{P}}^{2}
with six lines
l_{i}
tangent to a conic. Let
q_{ij}=l_{i}\cap l_{j}
denote the fifteen nodal points of the degenerate sextic
S_{A}
. Then
A
corresponds to a point on
H_{5}
if and only if there exist a conic
{\mathcal{Q}}
passing through five of the points
q_{ij}
and tangent to the remaining line.

Birkenhake and Wilhelm
[
BW03
]
generalised this to other
\Delta
. For certain classes of
\Delta=\Delta(d,k)
determined by integers
k>2
and
d>0
, they show that the moduli point of an Abelian surface lies on
H_{\Delta}
implies that there is a rational curve
{\mathcal{Q}}
of degree
d
meeting the sextic
S_{A}
at
k
of the points
q_{ij}
and the remaining lines at points of even multiplicity. Conversely, if there is such a rational curve, then it corresponds to some
\Delta^{\prime}\leq\Delta
.

To recover a divisor
D
of invariant
\Delta>0
from the rational curve, we observe the following.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{Q}}
be a rational curve on
{\mathds{P}}^{2}_{A}
which meets the sextic
S_{A}
only at points of even multiplicity or at the double points
q_{ij}
as in the theorems of Birkenhake and Wilhelm. Let
\psi:{\mathcal{C}}\longrightarrow Q
be the double cover. Then
{\mathcal{C}}
is
not
irreducible and is the union of two rational curves
{\mathcal{C}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{C}}_{2}
.

Proof.

The map
{\mathcal{C}}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{Q}}
is ramified at the points of
{\mathcal{Q}}\cap S_{A}
which are all double points. Hence the ramification points are nodes of
{\mathcal{C}}
. Let
\psi:\tilde{{\mathcal{C}}}\longrightarrow\tilde{{\mathcal{Q}}}
be the maps between the normalizations induced by
\psi
. As all the ramification points of
{\mathcal{C}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{Q}}
are singular points,
\tilde{{\mathcal{C}}}
is an
unramified
double cover of a
{\mathds{P}}^{1}
. Since the fundamental group of
{\mathds{P}}^{1}
is trivial, there are no
irreducible
unramified double covers. Hence
\tilde{{\mathcal{C}}}
is the union of two rational curves meeting at a point and
{\mathcal{C}}
is the union of two curves
{\mathcal{C}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{C}}_{2}
which meet at the points lying over the ramification points.

∎

Let
{\mathcal{D}}_{i}=\phi^{*}({\mathcal{C}}_{i})
. Then
{\mathcal{D}}_{1}
(or
{\mathcal{D}}_{2}
) will not be a multiple of the
\theta
. Let
D={\mathcal{D}}_{1}
. Since
{\mathcal{D}}_{1}+{\mathcal{D}}_{2}=\phi^{*}(\psi^{*}({\mathcal{Q}}))
is multiple of
\theta
it does not matter which one we choose.

3.3
A Theorem in Enumerative Geometry

We would like to deform the rational curve
{\mathcal{Q}}
that meets the configuration of six lines at double points to a rational curve that meets the configuration at fewer double points - but with the advantage that it always exists. A classical theorem in enumerative geometry states that there exists a unique conic passing through
5
points in general position in
{\mathds{P}}^{2}
. This has the following well known generalization.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
n_{d}
be the number of rational curves of degree
d
passing through
3d-1
points in general position. Then
n_{d}
is finite and non-zero.

Proof.

[
MS12
]
, Prop. 7.4.8, page 230. ∎

The exact number
n_{d}
was computed by Kontsevich-Manin
[
KM94
]
and Ruan-Tian
[
RT95
]
in the early 90s. Classically it was known that
n_{1}=1
,
n_{2}=1
and
n_{3}=12
. More generally one has

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
0\leq k\leq 3d-1
. Let
n_{d,k}
be the number of rational curves passing through
k
points and meeting
(3d-1-k)
lines tangentially. Then
n_{d,k}
is finite and non-zero.

For instance,
n_{2,0}=n_{2,5}=1,n_{2,1}=n_{2,4}=2,n_{2,3}=n_{2,2}=4,n_{3,7}=36
. The number
n_{d,k}
is called a
characteristic number
and the analogue of the theorem of Kontsevich and Manin can be found in Pandharipande
[
pand
]
.

We have the following theorem.

Theorem 3.5

(Graber,S.)
.

Consider pairs
(S,{\mathcal{Q}})
where
S=\bigcup_{i=1}^{6}L_{i}
is a degenerate sextic in
{\mathds{P}}^{2}
given by a product of six lines and
{\mathcal{Q}}
is a rational curve of degree
d
. In general they will meet at
6d
points. Suppose there exists a degenerate sextic
S_{0}=\bigcup_{i}L_{0,i}
and a rational curve
{\mathcal{Q}}_{0}
of degree
d
such that they meet at
\leq 3d+1
points of the following type

•

k
nodes of the form
q_{0,ij}=L_{0,i}\cap L_{0,j}
of
S_{0}

•

3d-1-k
other points tangent to the lines
L_{0,i}
(which could coincide to be of even multiplicity).

•

2
other points (which could coincide and be of one of the above types).

Then, for
any
degenerate sextic
S=\bigcup_{i}L_{i}
the number
N_{d,k}
of rational curves
{\mathcal{Q}}
of degree
d
passing through the
k
nodes and meeting the lines
L_{i}
tangentially at
3d-1-k
points is finite and non-zero.

Proof.

[
Sre22
]
. ∎

In fact, more generally if one has a family of sextics with
k
sections corresponding to
k
nodes one has a similar theorem. In particular, for smooth sextics if for some sextic there exists a rational curve of degree
d
meeting it tangentially at
3d-1
points then for any sextic there exists such a curve.

Its an interesting question to count the exact number. For small
d
the numbers are essentially charcteristic numbers. For instance, if
d=2
and
k=5
this is simply
n_{2}=1
. When
d
gets larger though we have to use our theorem as the number of lines is fixed.

3.4
The motivic cycle

In this section we show that there exists a motivic cycles in the group
H^{3}_{{\mathcal{M}}}(A_{\eta},{\mathds{Q}}(2))
where
A_{\eta}
is the generic Abelian surface. These cycles
Z_{\Delta}
are defined in the fibres in the complement of components of
H_{\Delta}
. Further, they are
indecomposable
. Finally, like Collino’s element the boundary of the cycles in the localization sequence is a multiple of the cycle
{\mathcal{D}}_{1}-{\mathcal{D}}_{2}
. where
{\mathcal{D}}_{1}
is an element of invariant
\Delta
. We sketch the argument here. The details can be found in
[
Sre22
]
.

Let
A_{z}
be an Abelian surface corresponding to a point
z
on the moduli. This determines the lines
l_{i}(z)
, points
q_{ij}(z)
and the degenerate sextic
S_{A}(z)=\bigcup l_{i}(z)
in
{\mathds{P}}^{2}
all of which vary smoothly in
z
. Suppose for some
z_{0}
, one has a rational curve
{\mathcal{Q}}_{0}
of degree
d
as in Proposition
3.2
. From Theorem of Birkenhake and Wilhem
[
BW03
]
such
{\mathcal{Q}}_{0}
exists for infinitely many
(d,k)
.

A curve of degree
d
meets a sextic at
6d
points - however, since
{\mathcal{Q}}_{0}
meets
S(z_{0})
only at double points, there are at most
3d
points. Let
T(z_{0}):=S(z_{0})\cap{\mathcal{Q}}_{0}
. In
T(z_{0})
the points are of two types – either points of the form
q_{ij}(z_{0})
or points
P_{i}(z_{0})
where the line
l_{i}(z_{0})
and
{\mathcal{Q}}_{0}
meet with even multiplicity
m_{P_{i}}
. Let there be
k
points of the type
q_{ij}(z_{0})
. The set of all such
z_{0}
is component
P_{\Delta}
of a Humbert surface
H_{\Delta}
for some
\Delta
determined by
{\mathcal{Q}}_{0}
.

From Theorems 7.1,7.2,7.3 and 7.4 of Birkenhake and Wilhelm
[
BW03
]
at least
3
points of
T(z_{0})
are of the type
q_{ij}(z_{0})
. Let
q=q_{i_{0},j_{0}}(z_{0})
be one such point in
T(z_{0})
. For any
z
there are the points
q_{ij}(z)=l_{i}(z)\cap l_{j}(z)
. From Theorem
3.5
there exists a rational curve
{\mathcal{Q}}(z)
such that

•

{\mathcal{Q}}(z)
is of degree
d
.

•

{\mathcal{Q}}(z)
passes though points of the form
q_{ij}(z)
such that
q_{ij}(z_{0})\in T(z_{0})
except
q
.

•

{\mathcal{Q}}(z)
meets the lines
l_{i}(z)
with multplicity
m_{P_{i}}
.

•

{\mathcal{Q}}(z)
is such that
{\mathcal{Q}}(z_{0})={\mathcal{Q}}_{0}
.

We have two cases:

Case 1:
If
z
is not on
P_{\Delta}
, the curve
{\mathcal{Q}}(z)
will meet
S(z)
at two other points, say
s_{i_{0}}(z)
and
s_{j_{0}}(z)
on
l_{i_{0}}(z)
and
l_{j_{0}}(z)
respectively.

T(z)={\mathcal{Q}}(z)\cap S(z)
has
3d-1
points which are of even multiplicity and
2
points of multiplicity one.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}(z)\rightarrow{\mathcal{Q}}(z)
be the double cover induced by the map
\psi:K_{A_{z}}\rightarrow{\mathds{P}}^{2}
. The normalization
\tilde{{\mathcal{C}}}(z)
is a double cover of
{\mathds{P}}^{1}
which is ramified at the two points
s_{i_{0}}(z)
and
s_{j_{0}}(z)
and is hence an
irreducible smooth rational curve
and
{\mathcal{C}}(z)
is a
nodal rational curve
. The nodes are at the points lying over
T(z)
.

Case 2:
If
z
lies on
P_{\Delta}
the points
s_{i_{0}}(z)
and
s_{j_{0}}(z)
coincide at the point

3.4.1
An Example: Humbert’s theorem

As an example of the above theorem one can consider the following situation. Let

has six components in the moduli of Abelian surfaces with level

4
Old and new cycles

In this section we relate the Collino cycle to our new cycle. We first need some generalities.

4.1
The Universal Property of the Jacobian

If
D
is a curve, its Jacobian
J(D)
satisfies the following universal property.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
D
be a curve of genus
g
and
P
a point on
D
. Let
\eta:D\rightarrow A
be a map from
D
to an Abelian variety
A
such that
\eta(P)=0
. Then there is a unique
homomorphism

\tilde{\eta}:J(D)\longrightarrow A
such that

\eta=\tilde{\eta}\circ\iota_{P}

where
\iota_{P}:D\longrightarrow J(C)
is the map

\iota_{P}(x)=x-P

Proof.

(Sketch). Define
\tilde{\eta}:D^{g}\longrightarrow A
by

\tilde{\eta}(Q_{1},\dots,Q_{g})=\sum\eta(Q_{i})

This is clearly invariant under the symmetric group and hence descends to a rational map, which we also call
\tilde{\eta}:J(D)\longrightarrow A
. Further, this can be seen to be a morphism which sends
0
to
0
and is hence a homomorphism. The map
\iota_{P}:D\longrightarrow J(D)
is the composite of the maps
D\rightarrow D^{g}\rightarrow J(D)
given by
Q\rightarrow(Q,P,\dots,P)
and
(Q_{1},\dots,Q_{g})\rightarrow\sum_{i}\iota_{P}(Q_{i})
. Hence
\eta=\tilde{\eta}\circ\iota_{P}
.
∎

We have following corollary which drops the assumption that
\eta(P)=0
. The result is that one does not have a homomorphism from
J(C)\rightarrow A
, only a morphism.

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
D
be a curve of genus
g
and
\mu:D\rightarrow A
be a map such that
\mu(D)=Q
for some point
Q
on
A
. Then there is a morphism
\tilde{\mu}:J(C)\rightarrow A
such that

\mu=\tilde{\mu}\circ\iota_{P}

Further, if
P^{\prime}
is another point such that
\mu(P^{\prime})=Q
, then
\mu=\tilde{\mu}\circ\iota_{P^{\prime}}
as well.

Proof.

Let
\eta=T_{-Q}\circ\mu
where
T_{*}:A\rightarrow A
is the translation map
T_{*}(x)=x+*
. Then
\eta:C\rightarrow A
satisfies
\eta(P)=0
and we can apply Proposition
4.1
. Hence there is a homomorphism
\tilde{\eta}:J(C)\rightarrow A
such that
T_{-Q}\circ\mu=\eta=\tilde{\mu}\circ\iota_{P}
. Let
\tilde{\mu}=T_{Q}\circ\tilde{\eta}
. One then has

\mu=T_{Q}\circ\eta=T_{Q}\circ\tilde{\mu}\circ\iota_{P}=\tilde{\mu}\circ\iota_{P}

Since the construction of
\tilde{\eta}
involves only
\eta
and not
P
it does not depend on the choice of
P
in
\mu^{-1}(Q)
.

∎

Let
{\mathcal{Q}}_{0}
be fixed and
z
in the complement of the corresponding
P_{\Delta}
. Consider the cycle
\Xi=\Xi_{{\mathcal{Q}}_{0}}(z)=\phi^{*}(\pi_{*}({\mathcal{Z}}_{{\mathcal{Q}}_{0}}(z)))
which is the pull-back of the cycle
{\mathcal{Z}}_{{\mathcal{Q}}_{0}}(z)
to the Abelian surface
A_{z}
. This is an element of
H^{3}_{{\mathcal{M}}}(A_{z},{\mathds{Q}}(2))
. In what follows since we have fixed
z
we will drop it from our notation.

4.1.1
Nodal hyperelliptic curves

The cycle
\Xi
is of the form
({\mathcal{D}},h_{P})
where
{\mathcal{D}}
is a
nodal hyperelliptic curve
, namely a double cover of the nodal rational curve
{\mathcal{C}}
on
K_{A}
. The point

is a node as well as a ramification point of the double cover.

Let
\nu:\tilde{{\mathcal{D}}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{D}}
denote the normalization of

. Since
{\mathcal{D}}
is a singular hyperelliptic curve,
\tilde{{\mathcal{D}}}
is a
smooth hyperelliptic curve
. Let
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}
be the normalization of

. From the universal property of the normalization there is a unique map
\tilde{\phi}:\tilde{{\mathcal{D}}}\longrightarrow\tilde{\mathcal{C}}
such that the following diagram commutes

\begin{CD}\tilde{{\mathcal{D}}}@>{\tilde{\phi}}>{}>\tilde{\mathcal{C}}\\
@V{}V{\nu}V@V{}V{\nu}V\\
{\mathcal{D}}@>{\phi}>{}>{\mathcal{C}}\end{CD}

\tilde{{\mathcal{D}}}
is the double cover of the normalization of
{\mathcal{C}}
. Recall that we have the points

and

lying over the node

in

Lemma 4.3
.

The points lying over the node
P
under the map
\tilde{{\mathcal{D}}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{D}}
are ramification points of the map
\tilde{\phi}:{\tilde{{\mathcal{D}}}}\rightarrow\tilde{\mathcal{C}}
. To reduce notation, we will continue to call them

.

Proof.

One has an involution
i
on
A
induced by the double cover
\phi:A\rightarrow K_{A}
which restricts to give the hyperelliptic involution on
{\mathcal{D}}
. The point
P
on

is fixed by

at

every point in the exceptional fibre
E_{P}
is fixed by

and hence so are the points
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
. Hence they are ramification points of the hyperelliptic curve
{\tilde{{\mathcal{D}}}}\rightarrow\tilde{\mathcal{C}}
.

∎

For the same reason, the points

and

\operatorname{div}(f_{P_{1}P_{2}})=2P_{1}-2P_{2}\text{ and }f_{P_{1}P_{2}}(R_{1})=1

Collino’s cycle revisited

Theorem 4.4
.

Let

and

, where

is a Weierstrass point mapping to the point

where

Proof.

From Corollary
4.2
The map
\mu:{\tilde{{\mathcal{D}}}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{D}}
determines a map
\mu:{\tilde{{\mathcal{D}}}}\rightarrow A
which takes

to

and detemines a morphism

such that

\mu=\tilde{\mu}\circ\iota_{P_{1}}

Howvever, the map also takes

({\tilde{{\mathcal{D}}}}_{P_{2}},f_{P_{2}})

This shows that in fact the cycle we constuct in
[
Sre22
]
is not entirely a new cycle but a version of Collino’s cycle for a different hyperelliptic curve, and in a sense goes even further back to the work of Bloch
[

where he constructed a cycle on

which maps to the Collino cycle under the map
X_{0}(37)\times X_{0}(37)\rightarrow J(X_{0}(37)

This result suggests that it is not so easy to find new motivic cycles - all constructions seem to be a variation on the original construction of Bloch. In
[
Sre22
]
we speculated on a relationship between weakly holomorphic modular forms and motivic cycles on Abelian surfaces and it would be interesting to see if there is some fundamental modular form such that all modular forms are derived from it in some way.

A positive consequence of this result is that it is known that the Collino cycle is natural in the following sense. From generalities one expects that motivic cycles can be understood as certain extensions in the category of mixed motives - thought this is still conjectural. However, the regulator of this cycle can be understood as a extension in the category of Mixed Hodge structures. In Colombo
[
Col02
]
and more generally in Sarkar-Sreekantan
[
SS20
]
it was shown that the regulator of Collino’s cycle or Bloch’s cycle can be realised in terms of natural extensions of mixed Hodge structures coming from the
fundamental group
of the curve.

Thanks to this connection with the Collino cycle we can obtain the extension class associated to our new motivic cycle.

Recall that there is an extension class

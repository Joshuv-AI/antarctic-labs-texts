Hausdorff dimension of the parameters for ( α , β ) (\alpha,\beta) -transformations with the specification property
Mai Oguchi, Mao Shinoda

Abstract
. In this paper we consider the specification property for ( α , β ) (\alpha,\beta) -shifts. When α = 0 \alpha=0 , Schmeling shows that the set of β > 1 \beta>1 for which the β \beta -shift has the specification property has the Lebesgue measure zero but has the full Hausdorff dimension [ 14 ] . So it is natural to ask what happens when α > 0 \alpha>0 . Buzzi shows that for fixed α \alpha the set of β > 1 \beta>1 for which the ( α , β ) (\alpha,\beta) -shift has the specification properyt has Lebesgue measure zero. Hence we consider the Hausdorff dimension of the parametar space of ( α , β ) (\alpha,\beta) -shifts.

1.
Introduction

In this paper we consider the specification property for
(\alpha,\beta)
-shifts, which is the coding spaces of
(\alpha,\beta)
-transformations.
More precisely, for
0\leq\alpha<1
and
\beta>1
the
(\alpha,\beta)
-transformation
T_{\alpha,\beta}:[0,1)\rightarrow[0,1)
is defined by

(1)

T_{\alpha,\beta}(x)=\beta x+\alpha-\lfloor\beta x+\alpha\rfloor

for all
x\in(0,1]
where
\lfloor\xi\rfloor
denotes the largest integer no more than
\xi

and the
(\alpha,\beta)
-shift
\Sigma_{\alpha,\beta}
is a subshift consisting of all
(\alpha,\beta)
-expansions induced by the
(\alpha,\beta)
-transformation.
After the pioneering work of Renyi
[
13
]
and Parry
[
10
,
11
]
dynamical properties of
T_{\alpha,\beta}
and
\Sigma_{\alpha,\beta}
have been investigated from view points of ergodic theory and number theory for over half a century
[
3
,
6
,
12
,
8
,
4
]
.

The specification property, which is introduced by Bowen
[
1
]
, is very important since it allows the study of generic properties of invariant measures.
For example, it implies the uniqueness of equilibrium measure for any smooth potential with the additional hypothesis of expansiveness.

In the case of
\alpha=0
, the shift is known as the
\beta
-shift
\Sigma_{\beta}:=\Sigma_{0,\beta}
and the parameter
\beta>1
for which
\Sigma_{\beta}
has the specification property is studied: the set of such parameters has the Lebesgue measure
0
but has the full Hausdorff dimension
[
14
]
.
For a fixed
\alpha\in[0,1)
it is also known that
\beta>1
for which
\Sigma_{\alpha,\beta}
has the specification property has the Lebesgue measure
0

[
2
]
.
Hence it is natural to ask the Hausdorff dimension in the case of
\alpha\in(0,1)
.

It is well known that the
(\alpha,\beta)
-shift consists of the
(\alpha,\beta)
-expansions in the interval with regard to the lexicographical order
\preceq
whose end points are
(\alpha,\beta)
-expansions of
0
and
1
(See for more details §
2
).
Let
\mathbb{N}_{0}
be the set of non negative integers.
Fixing the expansion of
0
, we show that the set of the parameters for which the
(\alpha,\beta)
-shift has the specification property has the full Hausdorff dimension:

Theorem 1
.

Let
\underline{u}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
satisfy

u_{0}=0
,
\underline{u}\preceq\sigma^{n}\underline{u}
for all
n\geq 0
and
\max_{i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}u_{i}=:K<\infty
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\dim_{H}\{\beta>K+2:\Sigma_{\alpha(\beta),\beta}\ \mbox{has the specification property}\}=1

where
\displaystyle\alpha(\beta)=(\beta-1)\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{u_{j}}{\beta^{j+1}}
and
\dim_{H}E
is the Hausdorff dimension of
E
.

Our proof is based on the work by Hu et.al
[
9
]
, which shows the set of
\beta>1
for which the
(-\beta)
-shift has the specification property has the full Hausdorff dimension.
The main tool is the well know theorem on the Hausdorff dimension of the attractor for an interacted function system.

The rest of this paper organized as follows.
In §2, we establish our definitions and prepare several facts.
In §3 we prove our main theorem.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Symbolic dynamics

Let
A
be a finite set and
A^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
the set of all one-sided infinite sequences on the alphabet
A

with the standard metric
d(x,y)=2^{-t(x,y)}
, where
t(x,y)=\min\{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:x_{k}\not=y_{k}\}
.
Then the shift map
\sigma\colon A^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}\to A^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
defined by

\sigma(x)_{i}=x_{i+1}
is a continuous map. If a compact subset
X\subset A^{\mathbb{N}}

satisfies
\sigma(X)\subset X
, then
X
is called a
subshift
.
For a finite sequence
w\in\bigcup_{n\geq 1}A^{n}
, denote by
w^{\infty}:=ww\cdots\in A^{\mathbb{N}}

the periodic infinite sequence and by

[w]:=\{x\in X:x_{1}\cdots x_{|w|}=w\}
the
cylinder set
for
w
, where
|w|
denotes the length of
w
.
The
language

\mathcal{L}(X)
is defined by

\mathcal{L}(X):=\left\{w\in\bigcup_{n\geq 1}A^{n}:[w]\not=\emptyset\right\}.

The topological entropy of
X
is defined by

\displaystyle h_{{\rm top}}(X)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\#\mathcal{L}(X).

Definition 2.1

(Specification)
.

Let
\mathcal{L}(X)
be the language of a subshift
X
.
We say that
X
has
specification
with gap size
t\in\mathbb{N}
if

•

for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
w^{1},\ldots,w^{m}\in\mathcal{L}(X)
,
there exist
v^{1},\ldots,v^{m-1}\in\mathcal{L}(X)
such that

x:=w^{1}v^{1}w^{2}v^{2}\cdots v^{m-1}w^{m}\in\mathcal{L}(X)

and
|v^{i}|=t
for all
i
and

•

the cylinder
[x]
contains a periodic point of period exactly
|x|+t
.

2.2.
(\alpha,\beta)
-shifts

Let
\alpha\in[0,1)
and
\beta>1
and
T_{\alpha,\beta}
be the
(\alpha,\beta)
-transformation defined by (
1
).
Let
k=\lceil\alpha+\beta\rceil
and
A=\{0,1,\ldots,k-1\}
.
Set
I_{0}=\left[0,\frac{1-\alpha}{\beta}\right)
,
I_{j}=\left[\frac{j-\alpha}{\beta},\frac{j+1-\alpha}{\beta}\right)
for
1\leq j\leq k-2
and
I_{k-1}=\left[\frac{k-1-\alpha}{\beta}\right)
.
Define the coding map
i_{\alpha,\beta}:[0,1)\rightarrow A^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
by
i_{\alpha,\beta}(x)_{n}=j
iff
T^{n}_{\alpha,\beta}(x)\in I_{j}

for all
x\in[0,1)
and
n\geq 0
.
Then we have

\displaystyle x=\frac{i_{\alpha,\beta}(x)_{0}-\alpha}{\beta}+\frac{i_{\alpha,\beta}(x)_{1}-\alpha}{\beta^{2}}+\cdots=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{i_{\alpha,\beta}(x)_{n}-\alpha}{\beta^{n}}.

We call
i_{\alpha,\beta}(x)
a
(\alpha,\beta)
-expansion of
x
.
Now we define

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\alpha,\beta}:={\rm cl}(\{i_{\alpha,\beta}(x)\in A^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}:x\in(0,1]\})

where
{\rm cl}(X)
is the clusure of
X
in
A^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
.
Then it is easy to see that
\Sigma_{\alpha,\beta}
is a subshift of
A^{\mathbb{N}}
.
We call
(\Sigma_{\alpha,\beta},\sigma)
the
(\alpha,\beta)
-shift.
It is well known that the
(\alpha,\beta)
-shift are characterized by the lexicographical order and the expansions of the end points:

(2)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\alpha,\beta}=\{\underline{x}\in A^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}:\underline{u}^{\alpha,\beta}\preceq\sigma^{n}\underline{x}\preceq\underline{v}^{\alpha,\beta}\ \mbox{for all}\ n\geq 0\}

where
\underline{u}^{\alpha,\beta}=i_{\alpha,\beta}(0)
and
\displaystyle\underline{v}^{\alpha,\beta}=\lim_{x\uparrow 1}i_{\alpha,\beta}(x)
.
Moreover we have

\displaystyle\underline{u}^{\alpha,\beta}\preceq\sigma^{n}\underline{u}^{\alpha,\beta}\preceq\underline{v}^{\alpha,\beta}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\underline{u}^{\alpha,\beta}\preceq\sigma^{n}\underline{v}^{\alpha,\beta}\preceq\underline{v}^{\alpha,\beta}

for all
n\geq 0
.
See
[
7
]
and references therein for more details.

For
(\alpha,\beta)
-shifts the specification property is characterized by the expansions
\underline{u}^{\alpha,\beta}
and
\underline{v}^{\alpha,\beta}
.
For
\underline{u}^{\alpha,\beta},\underline{v}^{\alpha,\beta}
define

\displaystyle D(\underline{u}^{\alpha,\beta})=\{n\in\mathbb{N}\;:\;(v^{\alpha,\beta}_{0},\dots,v^{\alpha,\beta}_{n})=(u^{\alpha,\beta}_{j},\dots,u^{\alpha,\beta}_{j+n})\;\text{for some}\;j\in\mathbb{N}\}

\displaystyle D(\underline{v}^{\alpha,\beta})=\{n\in\mathbb{N}\;:\;(u^{\alpha,\beta}_{0},\dots,u^{\alpha,\beta}_{n})=(v^{\alpha,\beta}_{j},\dots,v^{\alpha,\beta}_{j+n})\;\text{for some}\;j\in\mathbb{N}\}.

Theorem 2.2
.

[
3
, Theorem 1.5]

Let
0\leq\alpha<1
and
\beta>2
.
Then
\Sigma_{\alpha,\beta}
has the specification property if and only if both
D(\underline{u}^{\alpha,\beta})
and
D(\underline{v}^{\alpha,\beta})
are bounded.

3.
Proof of the main theorem

Our proof of Theorem
1
is based on the idea by Hu et.al
[
9
]
, which proves that the set of
\beta>1
for which the
(-\beta)
-shift has the specification property has full Hausdorff dimension.

Let
\underline{u}\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
satisfy the following:

U1:

u_{0}=0

U2:

\underline{u}\prec\sigma^{n}\underline{u}
for every
n\geq 1
and

U3:

\displaystyle\max_{i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}u_{i}=:K<\infty

Since the case
\underline{u}=0^{\infty}
corresponding to the case
\alpha=0
is proved by Schmeling
[
14
]
, we may assume
\underline{u}\neq 0^{\infty}
.
In the lest of this paper we assume
N\geq K+3
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\underline{v}\in\{0,1,\ldots,N-1\}^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
such that

v_{0}=N-1
and
1\leq v_{i}\leq N-2
for all
i\geq 1
.
Then there exist
(\alpha,\beta)\in(0,1)\times(1,\infty)
satisfying

\displaystyle 0=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{u_{i}-\alpha}{\beta^{i+1}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad 1=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{v_{i}-\alpha}{\beta^{i+1}}.

Moreover we have
\underline{u}^{\alpha,\beta}=\underline{u}
and
\underline{v}^{\alpha,\beta}=\underline{v}
.

Proof.

Since
u_{0}=0
,
v_{0}=N-1
and
1\leq v_{i}\leq N-2
for all
i\geq 1
, it is easy to see that

(3)

\displaystyle\underline{u}\preceq\sigma^{n}\underline{u}\preceq\underline{v}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\underline{u}\preceq\sigma^{n}\underline{v}\preceq\underline{v}\quad^{\forall}n\geq 0

holds.
Set
\hat{\underline{u}}=\max\{\sigma^{n}\underline{u}:n\geq 0\}
and

\displaystyle\Sigma(\underline{u},\hat{\underline{u}})=\{\underline{x}\in\{0,1,\ldots,N-1\}^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}:\underline{u}\preceq\sigma^{n}\underline{x}\preceq\hat{\underline{u}}\ \mbox{for all}\ n\geq 0\}.

By U3 we have
\Sigma(\underline{u},\hat{\underline{u}})\subset\{0,1,\ldots,K\}^{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
and
h_{top}(\Sigma(\underline{u},\hat{\underline{u}}))\leq\log(K+1)
.
By the choice of
N
, we have
\log v_{0}=\log K+2>\log(K+1)=h_{{\rm top}}(\Sigma(\underline{u},\hat{\underline{u}}))
.
Then by Theorem 6.20 in
[
7
]
there exist
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\beta>1
such that
\underline{u}=\underline{u}^{\alpha,\beta}
and
\underline{v}=\underline{v}^{\alpha,\beta}
.
∎

Let

\displaystyle E_{N}=\{\beta>N-2:v_{0}^{\alpha(\beta),\beta}=N-1,1\leq v_{i}^{\alpha(\beta),\beta}\leq N-2\ \mbox{for all}\ i\geq 1\ \}

where
\displaystyle\alpha(\beta)=(\beta-1)\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{u_{j}}{\beta^{j+1}}
.
For
\beta\in E_{N}
we have
D(\underline{u}^{\alpha(\beta),\beta})=D(\underline{v}^{\alpha(\beta),\beta})=\emptyset

since
v_{0}^{\alpha(\beta),\beta}=N-1\neq u_{i}
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
u_{0}^{\alpha(\beta),\beta}=0\neq v_{i}
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
By Theorem
2.2
the shift
\Sigma_{\alpha(\beta),\beta}
satisfies the specification property.

For
\beta>2
we have

\displaystyle\frac{d\alpha(\beta)}{d\beta}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(-j+\frac{1}{\beta}(j+1)\right)<0,

which implies
\alpha(\beta)
is strictly decreasing.
By Lemma
3.1
for
\beta\in E_{N}
we have
\beta\leq N
and

\displaystyle\alpha(\beta)\leq\alpha(N-2)\leq\frac{K}{N-2}=:\alpha_{N}<1.

Hence we have
E_{N}\subset(N-1-\alpha_{N},N]
.

Define a map
\varphi:E_{N}\rightarrow[0,1]
by

\displaystyle\varphi(\beta)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{v_{k}^{\alpha,\beta}}{N^{k}}.

For
i=1,\ldots,N-2
set
\displaystyle\psi_{i}(x)=\frac{x+i}{N}
.
Then by Lemma
3.1
it is easy to see
\varphi(E_{N})
satisfies

\displaystyle\varphi(E_{N})=\bigcup_{i=1}^{N-2}\psi_{i}(\varphi(E_{N}))

and it is the attractor of the interacted function system
\Psi=\{\psi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N-2}
.
By
[
5
, Theorem 9.3]

\dim_{H}\varphi(E_{N})=s
, where
s
is given by
\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N-2}\left(\frac{1}{N}\right)^{s}=1
.
Hence we have

\displaystyle\dim_{H}\varphi(E_{N})=\frac{\log(N-3)}{\log N}.

Lemma 3.2
.

There exists
C_{N}>0
such that for every
\beta,\beta^{\prime}\in E_{N}
we have

\displaystyle|\varphi(\beta)-\varphi(\beta^{\prime})|\leq C_{N}|\beta-\beta^{\prime}|.

We will prove this lemma later.
By using this lemma and
[
5
]
[Proposition 3.3] we have

\displaystyle\dim_{H}E_{N}\geq\dim_{H}\varphi(E_{N})=\frac{\log(N-3)}{\log N}.

Since this holds for ever
N\geq K+3
, Theorem
1
is proved.

In the rest of this paper, we prove Lemma
3.2
.
Let
\beta,\beta^{\prime}\in E_{N}
and
n\geq 1
satisfy

\displaystyle v_{i}^{\alpha(\beta),\beta}=v_{i}^{\alpha(\beta^{\prime}),\beta^{\prime}}\quad(0\leq i\leq n),\quad v_{n+1}^{\alpha(\beta),\beta}\neq v_{n+1}^{\alpha(\beta^{\prime}),\beta^{\prime}}.

By the definition of
\varphi
it is easy to see that

(4)

\displaystyle|\varphi(\beta)-\varphi(\beta^{\prime})|\leq N^{-n}.

Without loss of generality we may assume
\beta<\beta^{\prime}
.
For
0\leq i\leq n
set
v_{i}:=v_{i}^{\alpha(\beta),\beta}=v_{i}^{\alpha(\beta^{\prime}),\beta^{\prime}}
.
Define polynomials by
P_{0}(x)=x+\alpha(x)-v_{0}
and

\displaystyle P_{k}(x)=xP_{k-1}(x)-\alpha(x)-v_{k}

for
1\leq k\leq n
.
Then for
0\leq k\leq n
we have
T^{k}_{\alpha(\beta),\beta}(1)=P_{k}(\beta)
and
T^{k}_{\alpha(\beta^{\prime}),\beta^{\prime}}(1)=P_{k}(\beta^{\prime})
.
Since

\displaystyle P^{\prime}_{0}(x)=1+\alpha^{\prime}(x),\quad P^{\prime}_{k}(x)=P_{k-1}(x)+xP^{\prime}_{k-1}(x)+\alpha^{\prime}(x)\ (1\leq k\leq n),

we have

(5)

\displaystyle P^{\prime}_{n}(x)=x^{n-1}\left((1+\alpha^{\prime}(x))x+\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{P_{j}(x)+\alpha^{\prime}(x)}{x^{j}}\right).

By the mean value theorem there exists
\xi\in(\beta,\beta^{\prime})\subset(N-1-\alpha_{N},N]
such that

\displaystyle\left|T_{\alpha(\beta),\beta}^{n}(\beta)-T_{\alpha(\beta^{\prime}),\beta^{\prime}}^{n}(1)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|P_{n}(\beta)-P_{n}(\beta^{\prime})\right|

\displaystyle=|\beta-\beta^{\prime}|P^{\prime}_{n}(\xi).

By (
5
) and
P_{j}\in[0,1]
for
0\leq j\leq n-1
we have

\displaystyle\left|T_{\alpha(\beta),\beta}^{n}(\beta)-T_{\alpha(\beta^{\prime}),\beta^{\prime}}^{n}(1)\right|

\displaystyle=|\beta-\beta^{\prime}|\left((1+\alpha^{\prime}(\xi))\xi^{n}+\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{P_{j}(\xi)+\alpha^{\prime}(\xi)}{\xi^{j}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq|\beta-\beta^{\prime}|\left((1+\alpha^{\prime}(\xi))\xi^{n}+\left(1+\alpha^{\prime}\left(\zeta\right)\right)\frac{1-\frac{1}{\zeta^{n}}}{1-\frac{1}{\zeta}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq|\beta-\beta^{\prime}|\frac{\zeta^{2n}+\zeta^{n}-|\alpha^{\prime}(\zeta)|(-\zeta^{2n-1}-1)}{\zeta^{n}-\zeta^{n-1}}

\displaystyle\leq|\beta-\beta^{\prime}|\frac{2\zeta^{2n}+2|\alpha^{\prime}(\zeta)|\zeta^{2n}}{\zeta^{n}-\zeta^{n-1}}

\displaystyle\leq|\beta-\beta^{\prime}|\zeta^{n+1}(2+2|\alpha^{\prime}(\zeta)|)

\displaystyle<|\beta-\beta^{\prime}|N^{n+1}(2+2|\alpha^{\prime}(\zeta)|).

Therefore we have

(6)

\displaystyle|\beta-\beta^{\prime}|>\left|T_{\alpha(\beta),\beta}^{n}(\beta)-T_{\alpha(\beta^{\prime}),\beta^{\prime}}^{n}(1)\right|\frac{1}{N^{n+1}(2+2|\alpha^{\prime}(\zeta)|)}.

For
x,y\in(N-1-\alpha_{N},N]
define

\displaystyle\Delta_{N}(x,y)=\min\left\{\left(\frac{2-\alpha(x)}{x}+\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{k=2}\frac{1-\alpha(x)}{x^{k}}\right)-\left(\frac{1-\alpha(y)}{y}+\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{k=2}\frac{N-2-\alpha(y)}{y^{k}}\right),\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left(\frac{N-2-\alpha(y)}{y}+\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{k=2}\frac{1-\alpha(y)}{y^{k}}\right)-\left(\frac{N-3-\alpha(x)}{x}+\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{k=2}\frac{N-2-\alpha(x)}{x^{k}}\right)\right\}.

Then we have

\Delta_{N}(x,x)=\frac{1}{x}-\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{k=2}\frac{N-3}{x^{k}}=\frac{1}{x}\left(1-\frac{N-3}{x-1}\right)>\frac{1}{N}\left(1-\frac{N-3}{N-2-\alpha_{N}}\right)=:C_{N}>0.

Since
\Delta_{N}
is continuous, there exists
\delta(N)>0
such that for
x,y>2
with
0\leq|x-y|\leq\delta(N)
implies
\Delta(x,y)\geq C(N)>0
.

By the definition of the
(\alpha,\beta)
-transformation we have

T^{n}_{\alpha(\beta),\beta}(1)=\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{i=0}\frac{v_{n+i}^{\alpha(\beta),\beta}-\alpha(\beta)}{\beta^{i+1}}\;\;,\;\;T^{n}_{\alpha(\beta^{\prime}),\beta^{\prime}}(1)=\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{i=0}\frac{v_{n+i}^{\alpha(\beta^{\prime}),\beta^{\prime}}-\alpha(\beta^{\prime})}{\beta^{\prime i+1}}

and by the definition of
\Delta_{N}
we have

(7)

|T^{n}_{\alpha(\beta),\beta}(1)-T^{n}_{\alpha(\beta^{\prime}),\beta^{\prime}}(1)|\geq\Delta_{N}(\beta,\beta^{\prime}).

By (
4
),(
6
) and (
7
) we have

\displaystyle|\beta-\beta^{\prime}|\geq C_{1}(N)N^{-n}\geq C_{1}(N)|\varphi(\beta)-\varphi(\beta^{\prime})|

where
C_{1}(N)=C_{N}(N(2+2|\alpha^{\prime}(\zeta)|))^{-1}
, which completes the proof.

Remarks on the ℤ / p \mathbb{Z}/p Dijkgraaf-Witten invariants of 3D mapping tori Thanks: A. Kontorovich supported under NSF grants: DMS-1802119 and BSF 2020119. Thanks: S. Sikander supported under US DOE grant: DOE-SC0010008 to Rutgers
William Chen, Alex Kontorovich, Shehryar Sikander

Abstract
. We make some remarks on the ℤ / p \mathbb{Z}/p Dijkgraaf-Witten invariants of 3D mapping tori and determine the asymptotic behavior of their sum over all diffeomorphism classes of 3D mapping tori of genus one.



1.
Introduction

Starting with the Euclidean gravitational path integral defined by Hartle and Hawking, sums over diffeomorphism classes of D dimensional manifolds are by now ubiquitous in quantum gravity. Recently, Banerjee and Moore
[
1
]
considered sums of TQFT invariants over equivalence classes of one and two dimensional cobordisms. In this note we study sums of
\mathbb{Z}/p
Dijkgraaf-Witten invariants over diffeomorphism classes of 3D mapping tori.

Let
G
be a finite group. Using gauge theory with gauge group
G
, Dijkgraaf and Witten
[
3
]
constructed invariants of oriented three manifolds which depend on a choice of a class in
H^{3}(BG,U(1))
. Let
S
be a closed, connected, and oriented surface of genus bigger than zero, let
\Gamma(S)
denote its mapping class group, and let
[\Gamma(S)]
be the set of conjugacy classes of
\Gamma(S)
. For
f
in
\Gamma(S)
let
M(f)
denote the three dimensional mapping torus of
f
. Since conjugate mapping classes give diffeomorphic mapping tori, the Dijkgraaf-Witten invariant only depends on the conjugacy class
[f]
in
[\Gamma(S)]
. Let
Z(M([f]),G,\omega)
denote the Dijkgraaf-Witten invariant for some
\omega
in
H^{3}(BG,U(1))
, and let
H:[\Gamma(S)]\to\mathbb{R}
be a suitable ordering function. We seek the asymptotics of the sum

(1)

\displaystyle\sum_{[f]\in[\Gamma(S)],\atop H([f])<T}Z(M([f],G,\omega)

as
T\to\infty
. Let
S
be of genus one, then
\Gamma(S)
is isomorphic to
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
and the absolute value of the trace provides a natural ordering function. Let
p
be an odd prime and for a conjugacy class
[A]
in
[\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})]
let
Z(M([A]),\mathbb{Z}/p)
denote the
\mathbb{Z}/p
Dijkgraaf-Witten invariant, with
\omega
the identity class
in
H^{3}(B(\mathbb{Z}/p),U(1))
, of the mapping torus
M([A])
. Using the formula for
Z(M([A],\mathbb{Z}/p)
derived in Corollary
3.3
and Sarnak’s Chebotarev density theorem for prime geodesics, we prove the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
p
be a fixed odd prime. Then the
\mathbb{Z}/p
Dijkgraaf-Witten invariants of genus one mapping tori satisfy

(2)

\displaystyle\sum_{[A]\in[\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})],\atop|Tr([A])|<T}Z(M([A]),\mathbb{Z}/p)=\frac{2p^{3}-2p+1}{p^{3}-p}\operatorname{li}(T^{2})+O(T^{3/2}(\log T)^{2}),

as
T\to\infty
. Here

(3)

\displaystyle\operatorname{li}(x)=\int_{2}^{x}\frac{dt}{\log t},

is the logarithmic integral function (as in the prime number theorem).

In the case
p=2
, similar asymptotics can be derived
using results of Section
5
. In Section
5
, we also prove that the numbers
Z(M([A]),\mathbb{Z}/2)+2
are Fourier coefficients of a modular form of weight one, see Theorem
5.3
for details.

Let
S
be of arbitrary genus
g
and for a mapping class
f
in
\Gamma(S)
denote by
\hat{f}
the matrix in
\mathrm{Sp}(2g,\mathbb{Z})
which gives the action of
f
on
H_{1}(S,\mathbb{Z})
, with respect to a fixed basis of
H_{1}(S,\mathbb{Z})
. It is classical that the Smith normal form of the matrix
(\hat{f}-\Id)
determines
H_{1}(M([f]),\mathbb{Z})
, and hence the invariants
Z([f],\mathbb{Z}/p,\omega)
when
\omega
is the identity class, completely, see (
23
), (
25
), and (
28
). The duality between the 3D Dijkgraaf-Witten theory and 2D rational conformal field theory (holomorphic orbifold models in particular), allows one to use methods of rational conformal field theory to study Smith normal forms of matrices
(\hat{f}-\Id)
where
\hat{f}
is in the symplectic group. As an application of these 2D methods and Sarnak’s Chebotarev density theorem, we prove the following refinements of Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.2
.

For
[A]
in
[\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})]
, let
A(1)
and
A(2)
denote the diagonal entries of the Smith normal form of the matrix
(A-\Id)
, see (
60
). Let
p
be an odd prime. Then

(4)

\displaystyle\sum_{[A]\in[\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})],|Tr([A])|<T,\atop p|A(1)\,\text{and}\,p|A(2)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p^{3}-p}\operatorname{li}(T^{2})+O(T^{3/2}(\log T)^{2})

(5)

\displaystyle\sum_{[A]\in[\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})],|Tr([A])|<T,\atop p\nmid A(1)\,\text{and}\,p|A(2)}

\displaystyle=\frac{p^{2}-1}{p^{3}-p}\operatorname{li}(T^{2})+O(T^{3/2}(\log T)^{2})

(6)

\displaystyle\sum_{[A]\in[\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})],|Tr([A])|<T,\atop p\nmid A(1)\,\text{and}\,p\nmid A(2)}

\displaystyle=\frac{p^{3}-p^{2}-p-1}{p^{3}-p}\operatorname{li}(T^{2})+O(T^{3/2}(\log T)^{2})

as
T\to\infty
.

These asymptotics should be compared with the nice results in
[
10
]
. For an application of these 2D methods in genus greater than one, see (
57
). Perhaps these 2D methods can be leveraged to give faster than the state of the art algorithms for computing the Smith normal forms of
(\hat{f}-\Id)
; one wonders if and how the topological quantum computers based on 2D rational conformal fields theories can calculate Smith normal forms faster than classical computers.

2.
Preliminaries:
\mathbb{Z}/p
Dijkgraaf-Witten invariants of 3D mapping tori and the Smith normal form

Let
G
be a finite group and let
M
be a closed, connected, and oriented three manifold. Dijkgraaf and Witten introduced an invariant of
M
, which depends on the choice of a class in
H^{3}(G,U(1))
, as follows:
Any
\rho
in

\Hom(\pi_{1}(M),G)
determines a principal
G
-bundle
E_{\rho}\to M
as the quotient

(7)

\displaystyle\tilde{M}\times_{\pi_{1}(M)}G:=(\tilde{M}\times G)/\pi_{1}(M)

where
\tilde{M}
is the universal cover of
M
and
\pi_{1}(X)
acts on
\tilde{M}
as the deck group and acts on
G
by left multiplication via
\rho
. The existence of the universal
G
-bundle
EG\to BG
on the classifying space
BG
of the group
G
implies that there exists a unique (up to homotopy) continuous map

(8)

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}:M\to BG

such that

(9)

\displaystyle E_{\rho}\cong\hat{\rho}^{*}(EG)

While isomorphism classes of
G
-bundles are parametrized by the set
\Hom(\pi_{1}(M),G)/G
, the set
\Hom(\pi_{1}(M),G)
parametrizes homotopy classes of pointed maps; if one chooses a point in
M
and a point in
BG
, then there is an equivalence between the set
\Hom(\pi_{1}(M),G)
, where
\pi_{1}(M)
and
\pi_{1}(BG)\cong G

are defined with respect to the chosen point in
M
and
BG
, and the set of homotopy equivalence classes of continuous maps from
M
to
BG
which map the chosen point in
M
to the chosen point in
BG
.

Let
\omega
be a class in
H^{3}(BG,U(1))
and let
\rho
be a homomorphism in
\Hom(\pi_{1}(M),G)
. Using the continuous map
\hat{\rho}
from (
8
) associated with
\rho
, one pulls back the class
\omega
to obtain the class

(10)

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}^{*}(\omega)\in H^{3}(M,U(1))

on the three manifold
M
. The pulled back class (
10
) only depends on the homotopy class of
\hat{\rho}
and in this way one associates a well defined cohomology class in
H^{3}(M,U(1))
with every
\rho
in
\Hom(\pi_{1}(M),G)
. The top degree homology group
H_{3}(M,\mathbb{Z})
is infinite cyclic and the orientation of
M
determines a generator of this group. Denote this generator by
[M]
which is also called the fundamental class of
M
. One can now evaluate the pull-back (
10
) on the fundamental class, i.e.

(11)

\displaystyle\langle\hat{\rho}^{*}(\omega),[M]\rangle=\int_{M}\hat{\rho}^{*}(\omega)\in U(1)

The Dijkgraaf-Witten invariant of
M
with respect to
\omega
in
H^{3}(BG,U(1))
is defined as

(12)

\displaystyle Z(M,G,\omega):=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{\rho\in\Hom(\pi_{1}(M),G)}\langle\hat{\rho}^{*}(\omega),[M]\rangle

In case one chooses
\omega
to be the identity element of the group
H^{3}(M,U(1))
, the formula (
12
) specializes to

(13)

\displaystyle Z(M,G):=\frac{|\Hom(\pi_{1}(M),G)|}{|G|}

and will be refered to as the untwisted Dijkgraaf-Witten invariant.

Example 2.1
.

This example is taken from
[
6
]
and will be used later. Let
G=\mathbb{Z}/2
, its classifying space is the infinite dimensional real projective space

(14)

\displaystyle BG=\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{\infty}

The authors work with cohomology of
BG
with
\mathbb{Z}/2
coeffecients rather than with
U(1)
coefficients. We have that

H^{3}(\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{\infty},\mathbb{Z}/2)=\mathbb{Z}/2

Let
\alpha
denote the non-trivial element of
H^{3}(\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{\infty},\mathbb{Z}/2)
. For any
\rho
in
\Hom(\pi_{1}(M),G)
the evaluation

(15)

\displaystyle\langle\hat{\rho}^{*}(\alpha),[M]\rangle\in\mathbb{Z}/2

so that (
12
) specializes to

(16)

\displaystyle Z(M,\mathbb{Z}/2,\alpha):=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\rho\in\Hom(\pi_{1}(M),\mathbb{Z}/2)}(-1)^{\langle\hat{\rho}^{*}(\alpha),[M]\rangle}

If
H^{1}(M,\mathbb{Z}/2)=0
then
Z(M,\mathbb{Z}/2,\alpha)=\frac{1}{2}
, otherwise
Z(M,\mathbb{Z}/2,\alpha)
is an integer since an even number of terms are involved in the sum.

One can extend the definiton of these invariants to manifolds with boundary and obtain a 3d TQFT where the invariant
Z(M,G,\omega)
is the partition function of the theory evaluated on
M
.

Let
S
be a closed, connected, and oriented surface of genus
g>0
. Let
\text{Diff}(S)^{+}
be the group of orientation preserving diffeomorphisms of
S
and let
\text{Diff}(S)_{0}^{+}\subset\text{Diff}(S)^{+}
be the connected component of the identity, with respect to the compact open topology. The mapping class group of
S
is

(17)

\displaystyle\Gamma(S):=\text{Diff}(S)^{+}/\text{Diff}(S)_{0}^{+}

It is the group of isotopy classes of orientation preserving diffeomorphims of
S
. For
f
in
\Gamma(S)
, the mapping torus of
f
is the three fold

(18)

\displaystyle M(f):=S\times[0,1]/\sim

where the equivalence
\sim
is given by

(19)

\displaystyle(x,0)\sim(f(x),1)

The three fold
M(f)
is closed, connected, and orientable
and its diffeomorphism class does not depend on the choice of the representative of the mapping class
f
. It is the total space of a fiber bundle over the circle

(20)

\displaystyle\pi:M(f)\to S^{1}

such that for all
x
in
S^{1}
the fiber
\pi^{-1}(x)
is diffeomorphic to the surface
S
and the image of the monodromy map
\pi_{1}(S^{1})\to\Gamma(S)
is generated by
f
. Two mapping tori
M(f)
and
M(g)
are diffeomorphic to each other as fibre bundles if and only if
f
is conjugate to
g
.
We note that there exist examples of two mapping tori that are diffeomorphic to each other as three manifolds, but not diffeomorphic as fiber bundles.

Let
N(S)\subset M(f)
be the collar neighbourhood of some fiber of (
20
) and let
W=M(f)-\text{Interior}(N(S))
. Since
W
is diffeomorphic to
S\times I
, the Mayer-Vietoris sequence associated with the decomposition
M(f)=W\cup N(S)
reduces to

(21)

\displaystyle\cdots\to H_{1}(S,\mathbb{Z})\xrightarrow{\hat{f}-\Id}H_{1}(S,\mathbb{Z})\to H_{1}(M(f),\mathbb{Z})\to\mathbb{Z}\to 0

where

(22)

\displaystyle\hat{f}:H_{1}(S,\mathbb{Z})\to H_{1}(S,\mathbb{Z})

is the induced action of
f
on the homology of
S
, see
[
7
, Fact 2.1]
. The sequence (
21
) implies

(23)

\displaystyle H_{1}(M(f),\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}\oplus Coker(\hat{f}-\Id)

There exist two matrices
P
and
Q
in
\mathrm{GL}(2g,\mathbb{Z})
such that

(24)

\displaystyle P.(\hat{f}-\Id).Q=\begin{bmatrix}A(1)&&&&&&\\
&A(2)&&&&&\\
&&\ddots&&&&\\
&&&A(N)&&&\\
&&&&0&&\\
&&&&&\ddots&&\\
&&&&&&&0\end{bmatrix}

where for all
1\leqslant i\leqslant N
the diagonal entries
A(i)
are positive integers such that for all
i>1
,
A(i-1)
divides
A(i)
. The diagonal matrix on the RHS is called the Smith normal form of
(\hat{f}-\Id)
and will be denoted as
SNF(\hat{f}-\Id)
. The
SNF(\hat{f}-\Id)
does not depend on the pair
P,Q
and is an invariant of the conjugacy class of
\hat{f}
in
\mathrm{Sp}(2g,\mathbb{Z})
. In particular,

(25)

\displaystyle Coker(\hat{f}-\Id)=\mathbb{Z}^{2g-N}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/A(1)\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb{Z}/A(N)

The diagonal entries
A(i)
of
SNF(\hat{f}-\Id)
can be computed from the the entries of the matrix
(\hat{f}-\Id)
as follows: Let
D(i)
be the greatest common divisor of all the determinants of
i\times i
minors of
(\hat{f}-\Id)
, and let
D(0):=0
, then

(26)

\displaystyle A(i)=\frac{D(i)}{D(i-1)}

The fastest known algorithm for computing these integers
A(i)
has runtime complexity

(27)

\displaystyle O(\lVert\hat{f}-\Id\rVert\log\lVert\hat{f}-\Id\rVert 16g^{4}\log 2g)

Let
G=\mathbb{Z}/p
for
p
a prime. From (
23
) and (
25
) it follows that the untwisted Dijkgraaf-Witten invariant is

(28)

\displaystyle Z(M(f),\mathbb{Z}/p)

\displaystyle=\frac{|\Hom(H_{1}(M(f),\mathbb{Z}),\mathbb{Z}/p)|}{|\mathbb{Z}/p|}

(29)

\displaystyle=p^{\big|\{A(i),\,1\leqslant i\leqslant N\,|\,\text{p divides A(i)}\}\big|+(2g-N)}

3.
\mathbb{Z}/p
Dijkgraaf-Witten invariants of 3D mapping tori of genus one

Let
S
be a closed, connected, and oriented surface of genus
g=1
. The mapping class group
\Gamma(S)
is isomorphic to
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
. If
A=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}
in
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
, then the entries of the Smith normal form

(60)

\displaystyle SNF(A-\Id)=\begin{pmatrix}A(1)&0\\
0&A(2)\end{pmatrix}

can be calculated using (
28
), and are given as

(61)

\displaystyle A(1)=\mathrm{GCD}(a-1,b,c,d-1)\quad\quad\text{and}\quad\quad A(2)=\frac{|\operatorname{Tr}(A)-2|}{A(1)}

Since
2-\operatorname{Tr}(A)=\operatorname{Det}(A-\Id)
, it follows that
|\operatorname{Tr}(A)-2|
is divisible by
A(1)^{2}
, confirming that
A(2)
is an integer which is divisible by
A(1)
. Denoting by
M(A)
the mapping torus of
A
, formula (
61
) along with (
23
) and (
25
) implies the following trichotomy;

(62)

\displaystyle H_{1}(M(A),\mathbb{Z})=\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/A(1)\oplus\mathbb{Z}/A(2)&\text{if $\operatorname{Tr}(A)\neq$ }2\\
\mathbb{Z}^{2}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/A(1)&\text{if $\operatorname{Tr}(A)=2$ and $A\neq\Id$ }\\
\mathbb{Z}^{3}&\text{if $A=\Id$}\end{cases}

The following proposition gives a formula for
Z(M(A),\mathbb{Z}/p)
, the untwisted Dijkgraaf-Witten invariant, defined in (
13
).

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
p
be a prime and let
A
be in
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
, then

(63)

\displaystyle Z(M(A),\mathbb{Z}/p)=\begin{cases}p^{2}&\text{if $A\equiv\Id\mod p$}\\
p&\text{if $A\nequiv\Id\mod p$ and $\operatorname{Tr}(A)\equiv 2\mod p$}\\
1&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

Proof.

Assume that
\operatorname{Tr}(A)\neq 2
. Then both
A(1)
and
A(2)
are non-zero and

(64)

\displaystyle\hash\{\Hom(\mathbb{Z}/A(1)\oplus\mathbb{Z}/A(2),\mathbb{Z}/p)\}=\begin{cases}p^{2}&\text{if $p$ divides both $A(1)$ and $A(2)$}\\
p&\text{if $p$ divides $A(1)$ or $A(2)$ }\\
1&\text{if $p$ does not divide $A(1)$ and $A(2)$}\end{cases}

If
A\equiv\Id\mod p
then
p
divides both
A(1)
and
A(2)
. This follows since if
A\equiv\Id\mod p
then all four integers
a-1,b,c,d-1
are divisible by
p
. Since A(1) is the GCD of these four integers it follows that
A(1)
is divisible by
p
. Since
A(2)
is always divisible by
A(1)
, it follows that
A(2)
is also divisible by
p
.

If
A\nequiv\Id\mod p
and
\operatorname{Tr}(A)\equiv 2\mod p
then
p
divides
A(2)
but does not divide
A(1)
. This follows since if
A\nequiv\Id\mod p
then
p
does not divide
A(1)
. Since
p
does not divide
A(1)
and
\operatorname{Tr}(A)\equiv 2\mod p
then from definition of A(2) in (
61

6.
Further remarks:
\mathrm{SU}(2)
Chern-Simons-Witten invariants of mapping tori of genus one

Let
A
be in
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
such that
|\operatorname{Tr}(A)|>2
. One maps
A
to an indefinite binary quadratic form of discriminant
\operatorname{Tr}(A)^{2}-4
as follows

(135)

\displaystyle A=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\mapsto bx^{2}+(a-d)xy-cy^{2}=:Q_{A}(x,y)

This map is equivariant with respect to the
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
action on the left by conjugation and on the right by the standard action on binary quardatic forms, and one gets an isomorphism between conjugacy classes of hyperbolic elements of
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
with trace
t
and isomorphism classes of indefinite binary quadratic forms of discriminant
t^{2}-4
. Under this isomorphism, if
mx^{2}+lxy+ky^{2}
is a BQF of discriminant
t^{2}-4
, then its inverse is

(136)

mx^{2}+lxy+ky^{2}\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}\frac{t-l}{2}&k\\
-m&\frac{t+l}{2}\end{pmatrix}

which has trace equal to
t
.
Let
[A]
be in
[\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})]
such that
|\operatorname{Tr}([A])|>2
and
let
k
be a positive integer. Define

(137)

\displaystyle Z(M([A]),\mathrm{SU}(2),k):=\frac{\text{sign}(\operatorname{Tr}(A))}{2}\Bigg(

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\operatorname{Tr}(A)-2|}}\sum_{\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}/(A-\Id)\mathbb{Z}^{2}\}}e^{2\pi ik\frac{Q_{A}(x,y)}{\operatorname{Tr}(A)-2}}\,\,+\,\,

(138)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\operatorname{Tr}(A)+2|}}\sum_{\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}/(-A-\Id)\mathbb{Z}^{2}\}}e^{2\pi ik\frac{Q_{A}(x,y)}{\operatorname{Tr}(A)+2}}\Bigg)

This is the
\mathrm{SU}(2)
Chern-Simons-Witten invariant of
M([A])
at level
k
, see
[
4
, Equation (5.20)]
. Let
N(k):=8(k+2)
, the invariant
Z(M([A]),\mathrm{SU}(2),k)
only depends on the conjugacy class of
[A]\bmod N(k)
in
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/N(k))
. This follows from the duality between 3D Chern-Simons-Witten theory and 2D rational conformal field theory which constructs a representation

(139)

\displaystyle\Phi^{(k)}:\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})\to\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{C}^{k+1})

which factors through
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/N(k))
and has the property that

(140)

\displaystyle Trace(\Phi^{(k)}(A))=Z(M([A]),\mathrm{SU}(2),k)

For a fixed positive integer
k
one can thus apply Sarnak’s Chebotarev density theorem to obtain asymptotics of sums of (
137
) over all hyperbolic conjugacy classes
[A]
in
[\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})]
. It would be interesting to also consider the asymptotics of these sums as

Introduction

Cross-wired lamplighter groups were introduced in
[
1
]
as a generalization of lamplighter groups. Traditional lamplighters are a heavily studied object, it is defined as the restricted wreath product
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\wr\mathbb{Z}=\left(\bigoplus_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\right)\rtimes\mathbb{Z}
where the action of
\mathbb{Z}
on
\bigoplus_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
is given by shifting the coordinates in the direct sum. Geometrically, the lamplighter group can be thought of as an infinite street with an infinite number of lamps, and a person to light the lamps, called the lamplighter. The lamplighter can move left and right, turning the lamps on or off, while only a finite number of lamps are on. An element in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\wr\mathbb{Z}
corresponds to the positions of the on lamps, and the integer
n\in\mathbb{Z}
is the position of the lamplighter. More generally, for any finite group
F
, the lamplighter group of
F
is the wreath product
F\wr\mathbb{Z}=\left(\bigoplus_{\mathbb{Z}}F\right)\rtimes\mathbb{Z}
. This group is step-2 solvable and it has exponential growth. Grigorchuk and Zuk showed that
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\wr\mathbb{Z}
can be constructed as the automata groupof a 2-state automaton. Yang further to show that many automata groups arising from the Cayley machines defined by Steinberg and Silva,
[
2
]
, are also cross-wired lamplighters,
[
3
]

For one particular choice of generators the Cayley graph of a lamplighter group, is a Diestel-Leader graph
[
4
]
. For two positive integers,
m,n\geq 2
, the vertex set of a Diestel-Leader graph is defined as

DL(m,n)=\left\{(x,y)\in T_{m}\times T_{n}\mid b(x)+b^{\prime}(y)=0\right\}

where
T_{m}
and
T_{n}
are the regular infinite tree of degrees
m
and
n
respectively, and
b,b^{\prime}
are a Busemann functions on
T_{m}
and
T_{n}
respectively. Two vertices
(x,y)
,
(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
are connected by an edge if
x
and
x^{\prime}
are connected by an edge in
T_{m}
and
y
and
y^{\prime}
are connected by an edge in
T_{n}
. Eskin, Fisher, and Whyte proved that a finitely generated group is quasi-isometric to a lamplighter group
F\wr\mathbb{Z}
if and only if it acts properly and cocompactly by isometries on a Diestel-Leader graph
DL(n,n)

[
5
]
. Moreover, they showed that if
m\neq n
, then
DL(m,n)
is not quasi-isometric to the Cayely graph of any finitely generated group, proving a conjecture of Diestel and Leader,
[
6
]
.

In
[
1
]
, Cornulier, Fisher, and Kashyap study the quasi-isometric rigidity of lamplighter groups. They do this by studying the cocompact lattices in the isometry group of a Diestel-Leader graph,
{\rm Isom}({\rm DL}(n,n))
, and closed, cocompact subgroups of
{\rm Isom}({\rm DL}(m,n))
when
m\neq n
. These lattices turn out to not be lamplighter groups in general, and instead are called
cross-wired lamplighters
. The name is due to the fact that we can view them as an infinite street with an infinite number of lamps and a lamplighter. The difference is that when the lamplighter changes the state of one lamp, it might change the state of other lamps as well.

In this paper we answer question 1.4(iii) posed in
[
1
]
. They ask, if given
\Gamma
, a closed cocompact subgroup of
{\rm Isom}({\rm DL}(n,n))
not contained in
{\rm Isom}^{+}({\rm DL}(n,n))
, does the extension

1\to H\to\Gamma\to D_{\infty}\to 1

necessarily split? Here
H
is the unique open normal subgroup such that
\Gamma/H\cong D_{\infty}
, and
D_{\infty}
is the infinite dihedral group. We prove that the answer is yes by giving an isomorphism between the groups
(U\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z})\rtimes_{\psi}Z/2\mathbb{Z}
and
U\rtimes_{\phi}(\mathbb{Z}\rtimes_{\psi}\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
. At the end we make a couple of comments on question 1.4(i) posed in
[
1
]
. They ask if there exists a cross-wired lamplighter group that is not virtually symmetric. Where a cross-wired lamplighter
\Gamma
is symmetric if it admits an automorphism
\alpha
such that
\pi\circ\alpha=-\pi
where
\pi
is the projection from
\Gamma
to
\mathbb{Z}
. A cross-wired lamplighter is virtually symmetric if it has a finite index symmetric subgroup. While we do not prove the statement either way we demonstrate that the example given in
[
1
]

of
\Gamma=\mathbb{H}(\mathbb{F}_{q}[X,X^{-1}]
is symmetric, where
\Gamma
is the Heisenberg group of upper triangular matrices over
\mathbb{F}_{q}[X,X^{-1}]
.

Proof of the Main Theorem

Now, we look at the action in the group
U\rtimes_{\phi}(\mathbb{Z}\rtimes_{\psi}\mathbb{Z}/2/\mathbb{Z})
. We first see that the action of
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
on
\mathbb{Z}
is defined in the only possible way, by sending
\epsilon
to the automorphism
(-1)^{\epsilon}\cdot n
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

We define the action of
U\rtimes_{\phi}(\mathbb{Z}\rtimes_{\psi}\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
by the homomorphism,
\phi\colon\mathbb{Z}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\to{\rm Aut}(U)
given by
\phi(a,\epsilon).=\phi_{(a,\epsilon)}(u_{0}.u_{1})=(\phi^{a}u_{0+\epsilon}\phi^{-a},\phi^{-a}u_{1+\epsilon}\phi^{a})
for
(a,\epsilon)\in\mathbb{Z}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
. We first check that it is a homomorphism:

\displaystyle\phi(a,\epsilon)\phi(b,\delta)(u_{0},u_{1})=

\displaystyle\;\phi(a,\epsilon)(\phi^{b}u_{0+\delta}\phi^{-b},\phi^{-b}u_{1+\delta}\phi^{b})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;(\phi^{a}\phi^{(-1)^{\epsilon}b}u_{0+\epsilon+\delta}\phi^{(-1)^{\epsilon+1}b}\phi^{-a},\phi^{-a}\phi^{(-1)^{\epsilon+1}b}u_{1+\epsilon+\delta}\phi^{(-1)^{\epsilon}b}\phi^{a})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;(\phi^{1+(-1)^{\epsilon}b}u_{0+\epsilon+\delta}\phi^{-a-(-1)^{\epsilon}b},\phi^{-a-(-1)^{\epsilon}b}u_{1+\epsilon+\delta}\phi^{1+(-1)^{\epsilon}b})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\phi(a+(-1)^{\epsilon}b,\epsilon+\delta)=\phi((a,\epsilon)(b,\delta))

Next, we check that it is an automorphism. Let
(u_{0},u_{1}),(v_{0},v_{1})\in U
and
(a,\epsilon)\in\mathbb{Z}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
. Then

\displaystyle\phi_{(a,\epsilon)}(u_{0},u_{1})\phi_{(a,\epsilon)}(v_{0},v_{1})=

\displaystyle\;(\phi^{a}u_{0+\epsilon}\phi^{-a},\phi^{-a}u_{1+\epsilon}\phi^{a})(\phi^{a}v_{0+\epsilon}\phi^{-a},\phi^{-a}v_{1+\epsilon}\phi^{a})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;(\phi^{a}u_{0+\epsilon}v_{0+\epsilon}\phi^{-a},\phi^{-a}u_{1+\epsilon}v_{1+\epsilon}\phi^{a})

and

\phi_{(a,\epsilon)}(u_{0}v_{0},u_{1}v_{1})=(\phi^{a}u_{0+\epsilon}v_{0+\epsilon}\phi^{-a},\phi^{-a}u_{1+\epsilon}v_{1+\epsilon}\phi^{a})

It is also clear that this map is bijective,and thus an isomorphism.

Then looking at the multiplication in the group we have

((u_{0},u_{1}),(a,\epsilon))\cdot((v_{0},v_{1}),(b,\delta))=((u_{0}v_{0+\epsilon}\phi^{a},u_{1}v_{1+\epsilon}\phi^{-a}),(a+(-1)^{\epsilon}\cdot b,\epsilon+\delta)).

Since the action gives us the same triple of elements after multiplication, we can conclude they are isomorphic by sending
(((u_{0},u_{1}),a),\epsilon)
and
((u_{0},u_{1}),(a,\epsilon))
to
((u_{0},u_{1}),a,\epsilon)
.

Combining all of the above we get the following lemma.

Lemma 8
.

(U\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z})\rtimes_{\psi}\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\cong U\rtimes_{\phi}(\mathbb{Z}\rtimes_{\psi}\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
.

Now we turn to proving the main theorem we are interested in by first stating a lemma that is an extension of corollary 2.3 from
[
1
]
.

Lemma 9
.

Let
\Gamma\leq{\rm Isom}({\rm DL}(n,n))
be a closed, cocompact subgroup that is not contained in
{\rm Isom}^{+}({\rm DL}(n,n))
. Then there is an element

such that
\Gamma=((\Gamma\cap U)\rtimes\langle t\rangle)\rtimes_{\psi}\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

First take
\Gamma^{\prime}=\Gamma\cap{\rm Isom}^{+}({\rm DL}(n,n))
, then by corollary 2.3 from
[
1
]
, there is an element

such that
\Gamma^{\prime}=(\Gamma^{\prime}\cap U)\rtimes\left<t\right>
. Finally, since
{\rm Isom}^{+}({\rm DL}(n,n))
is an index 2 subgroup in
{\rm Isom}({\rm DL}(n,n))
, we see that
\Gamma^{\prime}
is an index 2 subgroup in
\Gamma
, and thus
\Gamma=\Gamma^{\prime}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}=(\Gamma^{\prime}\cap U)\rtimes\left<t\right>
. But
\Gamma^{\prime}\cap U=\Gamma\cap U
, giving us the result.
∎

Following the proof of the theorem 1.1
[
1
]
, we let

. Then
\Gamma/H\cong D_{\infty}
. For clarity, we will write

as

However, by lemma

\pi\circ\alpha=-\pi
where
\pi\colon\Gamma=H\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}
is the projection onto the second coordinate. We say that
\Gamma
is
virtually symmetric
if
\Gamma
has a symmetric subgroup of finite index.

In
[
1
]
, Cournulier, Fisher and Kashyap ask whether or not there exists a non virtually-symmetric cross-wired lamplighter.

We provide an example showing that the cross-wired lamplighter group
\mathbb{H}(\mathbb{F}_{q}[X,X^{-1}])\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z}
is symmetric where
\mathbb{H}(\cdot)
is the Heisenberg group of upper triangular matrices over a ring.

Proposition 11
.

The group
\mathbb{H}(\mathbb{F}_{q}[X,X^{-1}])\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z}
is symmetric.

Proof.

Let
\\
sigma\colon\Gamma\to\Gamma
be given by

\sigma\left(\begin{pmatrix}1&f(X,X^{-1})&h(X,X^{-1})\\
0&1&g(X,X^{-1})\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix},a\right)=\left(\begin{pmatrix}1&f(X^{-1},X)&h(X^{-1},X)\\
0&1&g(X^{-1},X)\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix},-a\right).

So
\sigma
restricts to an automorphism of
\mathbb{F}_{q}[X,X^{-1}]
that sends
X
to
X^{-1}
. It is clear the
\sigma
is bijective. We check that it is a homomorphism:

\displaystyle\sigma\left(\begin{pmatrix}1&f(X,X^{-1})&h(X,X^{-1})\\
0&1&g(X,X^{-1})\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix},a\right)\sigma\left(\begin{pmatrix}1&i(X,X^{-1})&k(X,X^{-1})\\
0&1&j(X,X^{-1})\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix},b\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\left(\begin{pmatrix}1&f(X^{-1},X)&h(X^{-1},X)\\
0&1&g(X^{-1},X)\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix},-a\right)\left(\begin{pmatrix}1&i(X^{-1},X)&k(X^{-1},X)\\
0&1&j(X^{-1},X)\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix},-b\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\left(\begin{pmatrix}1&f(X^{-1}X)+X^{-a}i(X^{-1},X)&X^{-2a}h(X^{-1},X)+X^{-a}f(X^{-1},X)j(X^{-1},X)+h(X^{-1},X)\\
0&1&g(X^{-1},X)+X^{-a}j(X^{-1},X)\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix},-a-b\right)

and

\displaystyle\sigma\left(\left(\begin{pmatrix}1&f(X,X^{-1})&h(X,X^{-1})\\
0&1&g(X,X^{-1})\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix},a\right)\left(\begin{pmatrix}1&i(X,X^{-1})&k(X,X^{-1})\\
0&1&j(X,X^{-1})\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix},b\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma\left(\begin{pmatrix}1&f(X,X^{-1})+X^{a}i(X,X^{-1})&x^{2a}k(X,X^{-1})+X^{a}f(X,X^{-1})j(X,X^{-1})+h(X,X^{-1})\\
0&1&g(X,X^{-1})+X^{a}j(X,X^{-1})\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix},a+b\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\left(\begin{pmatrix}1&f(X^{-1}X)+X^{-a}i(X^{-1},X)&X^{-2a}h(X^{-1},X)+X^{-a}f(X^{-1},X)j(X^{-1},X)+h(X^{-1},X)\\
0&1&g(X^{-1},X)+X^{-a}j(X^{-1},X)\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix}\right)

Thus
\sigma
is an automorphism and
\Gamma
is symmetric.
∎

We end by stating a few open questions about cross-wired lamplighters.

1.

Is there a cross-wired lamplighter that is not virtually symmetrc?

2.

Given a cross-wired lamplighter, is the pair
\left\{L,L^{\prime}\right\}
unique up to commensurability and automorphisms? Suppose that there are two embeddings of
\Gamma
as a cross-wired lamplighter group
(L_{1},L_{1}^{\prime})
and
(L_{2},L_{2}^{\prime})
. Is there an automorphism
\beta
of
\Gamma
such that
\beta(L_{1})
is commensurable to
L_{1}^{\prime}
and
\beta(L_{2})
is commensurable to
L_{2}^{\prime}
. This is question 1.4(ii) from
[
1
]
.

3.

Is there a non-linear cross-wired lamplighter? All of the example of cross-wired lamplighters from
[
1
]
, and
[
3
]
are linear, so finding a non-linear one or proving that they are all linear will give great insight into their structure.

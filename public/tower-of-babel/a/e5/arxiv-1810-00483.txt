Mean values of arithmetic functions in short intervals and in arithmetic progressions in the large-degree limit
Ofir Gorodetsky

Abstract
A classical problem in number theory is showing that the mean value of an arithmetic function is asymptotic to its mean value over a short interval or over an arithmetic progression, with the interval as short as possible or the modulus as large as possible. We study this problem in the function field setting, and prove for a wide class of arithmetic functions (namely factorization functions), that such an asymptotic result holds, allowing the size of the short interval to be as small as a square-root of the size of the full interval, and analogously for arithmetic progressions. For instance, our results apply for the indicator function of polynomials with a divisor of given degree, and are much stronger than those known for the analogous function over the integers. As opposed to many previous works, our results apply in the large-degree limit , where the base field 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} is fixed. Our proofs are based on relationships between certain character sums and symmetric functions, and in particular we use results from symmetric function theory due to Eğecioğlu and Remmel. We also use recent bounds of Bhowmick, Lê and Liu on character sums, which are in the spirit of the Drinfeld–Vlăduţ bound.



1
Introduction

An important and challenging class of problems in analytic number theory is showing that the mean value of an arithmetic function in a short interval
[x,x+h]\cap\mathbb{Z}
is asymptotic to its mean over a long interval. Concretely, this often takes the following form: proving for various
\alpha\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{C}
that

\frac{\sum_{x\leq n\leq x+x^{\varepsilon}}\alpha(n)}{x^{\varepsilon}}=\frac{\sum_{n\leq x}\alpha(n)}{x}(1+o(1)),\qquad x\to\infty

(1.1)

for
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
as small as possible. If the mean value of
\alpha
tends to
0
and does not have a main term, as happens, for example, for the Möbius function, one should be more careful and instead ask for

\frac{\sum_{x\leq n\leq x+x^{\varepsilon}}\alpha(n)}{x^{\varepsilon}}=o(1),\qquad x\to\infty

(1.2)

to hold for
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
as small as possible. A related class of problems is showing that the mean value of
\alpha\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{C}
over integers
1\leq n\leq x
which lie in the arithmetic progression
a\bmod q
(
\gcd(a,q)=1
) is close to the mean value of
\alpha
over integers
1\leq n\leq x
which are coprime to
q
. Formally, this often means proving

\frac{\sum_{n\leq x,\,n\equiv a\bmod q}\alpha(n)}{x/\phi(q)}=\frac{\sum_{n\leq x,\,(n,q)=1}\alpha(n)}{x(\phi(q)/q)}(1+o(1)),\qquad x\to\infty

(1.3)

or

\frac{\sum_{n\leq x,\,n\equiv a\bmod q}\alpha(n)}{x/\phi(q)}=o(1),\qquad x\to\infty

(1.4)

uniformly in
q<x^{1-\varepsilon}
for
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
as small as possible.

Conditionally on the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis (GRH), it is a classical result that in the range
\varepsilon>1/2
, (
1.1
) and (
1.3
) hold for the von Mangoldt function
\Lambda
and (
1.2
) and (
1.4
) hold for the Möbius function
\mu
. Ramachandra
[
17
]
showed that RH implies (
1.1
) for
\varepsilon>1/2
and
\alpha=d_{k}
, the
k
th divisor function (
k
not necessarily an integer) and some other functions. Kátai
[
12
]
, building on ideas of Ramachandra, proved that RH also implies (
1.1
) for
\varepsilon>1/2
and
\alpha
the indicator function of
k
-almost primes. Some other examples exist
[
3
,
6
,
13
]
.

There are close analogues in the function field setting of these results. Because GRH is known in that setting (a theorem due to Weil), they are unconditional. However, both in the number field setting and the function field setting, these results are proven by
ad hoc
arguments, each using GRH in a different way, and in particular the results always require the associated Dirichlet series
\sum\alpha(n)/n^{s}
to possess certain structure. In this paper, we prove analogues of (
1.1
)–(
1.4
) in the function field setting for
\varepsilon\in(1/2,1)
for a wide class of arithmetic functions defined over the polynomial ring
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
, the so-called “factorization functions.” As in previous works, our main tool is GRH for
\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)
, but we are able to treat all sensible arithmetic functions at once. This leads to interesting applications which are currently out of reach in the number field setting; these are described in §
1.3
.

1.1
The function field setting

Throughout we fix a prime power
q
. Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
be the polynomial ring in
T
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
,
\mathcal{M}_{n}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
be the subset of monic polynomials of degree
n
,
\mathcal{P}_{n}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{n}
be the subset of monic irreducible polynomials of degree
n
,
\mathcal{M}=\cup_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{M}_{n}
be the set of all monic polynomials and
\mathcal{P}=\cup_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{P}_{n}
be the set of all monic irreducible polynomials.

To any
f\in\mathcal{M}
with prime factorization
f=\prod_{i=1}^{k}P_{i}^{e_{i}}
(
P_{i}\in\mathcal{P}
distinct,
e_{i}\geq 1
), we can associate the following multiset, named the
extended factorization type of
f
:

\omega_{f}:=\{(\deg(P_{i}),e_{i}):1\leq i\leq k\}.

(1.5)

(We often omit the word “extended.”) Following Rodgers
[
19
]
, an arithmetic function
\alpha\colon\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{C}
is called a
factorization function
if
\alpha(f)
depends only on
\omega_{f}
. Some of the most commonly studied arithmetic functions in number theory, when considered in the function field setting, are instances of factorization functions: the von Mangoldt function

\Lambda(f)=\begin{cases}\deg(P)&\mbox{if $f=P^{k}$ for $P\in\mathcal{P}$ and $k\geq 1$},\\
0&\mbox{otherwise},\end{cases}

(1.6)

the Möbius function

\mu(f)=\begin{cases}(-1)^{k}&\mbox{if $f=P_{1}P_{2}\cdots P_{k}$ for distinct $P_{i}\in\mathcal{P}$,}\\
0&\mbox{otherwise},\end{cases}

(1.7)

the indicator of squarefrees

\mu^{2}(f)=\begin{cases}1&\mbox{if $f$ is squarefree,}\\
0&\mbox{otherwise},\end{cases}

(1.8)

the divisor functions, and many more.

A short interval of size
q^{h+1}
around
f_{0}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
is the subset

I(f_{0},h):=\{f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]:\|f-f_{0}\|\leq q^{h+1}\}=\{f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]:\deg(f-f_{0})\leq h\},

(1.9)

where
\|f\|:=|\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]/(f)|=q^{\deg(f)}
for
f\neq 0
and
\|0\|:=0
. In addition to short intervals in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
, we also recall the definition of arithmetic progressions in this setting. Consider a non-zero polynomial
M\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
and a polynomial
f_{0}\in\mathcal{M}_{n}
coprime to
M
. We define the arithmetic progression of monic polynomials of degree
n
in the residue class
f_{0}\bmod M
to be

\mathrm{AP}(f_{0},M):=\{f\in\mathcal{M}_{\deg(f_{0})}:f\equiv f_{0}\bmod M\},

(1.10)

which is a subset of

\mathcal{M}_{n;M}:=\{f\in\mathcal{M}_{n}:\gcd(f,M)=1\}.

(1.11)

If
n\geq\deg(M)
, then
\left|\mathcal{M}_{n;M}\right|=q^{n-\deg(M)}\phi(M)
, where
\phi(M)
is Euler’s totient function.

1.2
Main results

For a given arithmetic function
\alpha\colon\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{C}
, let
\langle\alpha\rangle_{S}
be the mean value of
\alpha
over a non-empty finite subset
S\subseteq\mathcal{M}
:

\langle\alpha\rangle_{S}:=\frac{1}{|S|}\sum_{f\in S}\alpha(S).

(1.12)

We prove the following analogue of (
1.1
) and (
1.2
).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\alpha\colon\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{C}
be a factorization function. Let
0\leq h\leq n-1
and
f_{0}\in\mathcal{M}_{n}
. Then

\left|\langle\alpha\rangle_{I(f_{0},h)}-\langle\alpha\rangle_{\mathcal{M}_{n}}\right|\leq\max_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{n}}\left|\alpha(f)\right|q^{\frac{n}{2}-h-1}e^{O_{q}(\frac{n\log\log(n+2)}{\log(n+2)})}.

(1.13)

The analogous result for arithmetic progressions is the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\alpha\colon\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{C}
be a factorization function. Let
M\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
be a non-zero polynomial and let
f_{0}\in\mathcal{M}_{n}
be a polynomial coprime to
M
. Then whenever
n\geq\deg(M)
,

\left|\langle\alpha\rangle_{\mathrm{AP}(f_{0},M)}-\langle\alpha\rangle_{\mathcal{M}_{n;M}}\right|\leq\max_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{n;M}}\left|\alpha(f)\right|q^{\deg(M)-\frac{n}{2}}e^{O_{q}(\frac{n\log\log(n+2)}{\log(n+2)})}.

(1.14)

As long as
\max_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{n}}\left|\alpha(f)\right|
grows subexponentially in
n
, Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
give a non-trivial result in the range
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{h+1}{n}>1/2
and
\limsup_{n\to\infty}(1-\frac{\deg(M)}{n})>1/2
, respectively. These limsups are the function field analogue of the quantity
\varepsilon
discussed before.

We also prove the following results on the variance of the sums
\sum_{f\in I(f_{0},h)}\alpha(f)
and
\sum_{f\in\mathrm{AP}(f_{0},M)}\alpha(f)
as
f_{0}
varies. First, we define the short-interval variance

\mathrm{Var}(\alpha;n,h):=\frac{1}{\left|\mathcal{M}_{n}\right|}\sum_{f_{0}\in\mathcal{M}_{n}}\left|\sum_{f\in I(f_{0},h)}\alpha(f)-q^{h+1}\langle\alpha\rangle_{\mathcal{M}_{n}}\right|^{2},

(1.15)

and the arithmetic progression variance

\mathrm{Var}_{M}(\alpha;n):=\frac{1}{\left|\mathcal{M}_{n;M}\right|}\sum_{f_{0}\in\mathcal{M}_{n;M}}\left|\sum_{f\in\mathrm{AP}(f_{0},M)}\alpha(f)-q^{n-\deg(M)}\langle\alpha\rangle_{\mathcal{M}_{n;M}}\right|^{2}.

(1.16)

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\alpha\colon\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{C}
be a factorization function. For any
0\leq h\leq n-1
, we have

\mathrm{Var}(\alpha;n,h)\leq\max_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{n}}\left|\alpha(f)\right|^{2}q^{h+1}e^{O_{q}(\frac{n\log\log(n+2)}{\log(n+2)})}.

(1.17)

For any non-zero polynomial
M
with
\deg(M)\leq n
, we have

\mathrm{Var}_{M}(\alpha;n)\leq\max_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{n;M}}\left|\alpha(f)\right|^{2}\frac{q^{n}}{\phi(M)}e^{O_{q}(\frac{n\log\log(n+2)}{\log(n+2)})}.

(1.18)

The trivial bound for
\mathrm{Var}(\alpha;n,h)
is
q^{2(h+1)}\max_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{n}}|\alpha(f)|^{2}
, and we beat it as long as
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{h+1}{n}
is positive, which corresponds to
\varepsilon>0
in the number field setting. Similarly, (
1.18
) beats the trivial bound as long as
\limsup_{n\to\infty}(1-\frac{\deg(M)}{n})>0
.

1.3
Applications

Let
H(x,y,2y)
be number of positive integers up to
x
which have a divisor in
(y,2y]
. Ford
[
7
,
8
]
showed that

H(x,y,2y)\asymp\frac{x}{(\log y)^{\delta}(\log\log y)^{3/2}},\qquad 3\leq y\leq\sqrt{x},

(1.19)

where
\delta=1-\frac{1+\log\log 2}{\log 2}
. Here
A\asymp B
means
cB\leq A\leq CB
for absolute positive constants
c
and
C
. Ford has a short interval version of this result
[
7
, Theorem 2]
:

H(x,y,2y)-H(x-\Delta,y,2y)\asymp\frac{\Delta}{x}H(x,y,2y),\qquad y_{0}\leq y\leq\sqrt{x},\ \frac{x}{\log^{10}x}\leq\Delta\leq x,

(1.20)

where
y_{0}
is a sufficiently large real number. Let
H_{q}(n,d)
be the function field analogue of
H(x,y,2y)
, counting monic polynomials of degree
n
which have a divisor of degree
d
. Meisner
[
16
, Theorem 1.2]
has recently shown that

H_{q}(n,d)\asymp\frac{q^{n}}{d^{\delta}(1+\log d)^{3/2}},\qquad 1\leq d\leq\frac{n}{2}.

(1.21)

Applying Theorem
1.1
with

\alpha_{d}\colon\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{C},\qquad\alpha_{d}(f)=\begin{cases}1&\mbox{if $f$ has a divisor of degree $d$},\\
0&\mbox{otherwise,}\end{cases}

(1.22)

we obtain the following

Corollary 1.4
.

For any
n\geq 1
, let
f_{0}\in\mathcal{M}_{n}
and
1\leq d\leq n
. As
n\to\infty
, we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in I(f_{0},h)\\
f\text{ has a divisor of degree }d\end{subarray}}1\sim q^{h+1}\frac{H_{q}(n,d)}{q^{n}}

(1.23)

uniformly in
d
, as long as
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{h}{n}>1/2
.

Not only is this corollary valid in a much wider range than (
1.20
), it is an
asymptotic result
and not an estimate. In the integer setting, RH might help in improving the range where (
1.20
) holds, but it is not clear how to use it to establish an asymptotic formula. We raise the following

Question
.

Assume
RH
. Do we have

H(x,y,2y)-H(x-\Delta,y,2y)=\frac{\Delta}{x}H(x,y,2y)(1+o(1)),\qquad y_{0}\leq y\leq\sqrt{x},\ \frac{x}{\log^{10}x}\leq\Delta\leq x,

(1.24)

as
x
tends to infinity?

Our second example is Hooley’s Delta function. In his study of Waring’s problem, diophantine approximation and other problems
[
11
]
, Hooley introduced the function

\Delta(n):=\max_{u>0}\sum_{d\mid n,\,d\in(u,eu]}1,

(1.25)

and obtained upper and lower bounds on its mean value. Hall and Tenenbaum
[
9
]
showed that

\frac{1}{x}\sum_{n\leq x}\Delta(n)=O(\log^{A_{0}}x\sqrt{\log\log x})

(1.26)

for an explicit (optimal) constant
A_{0}
, and

\frac{1}{x}\sum_{n\leq x}\Delta(n)=\Omega(\log\log x).

(1.27)

As far as the author knows,
\Delta
was not studied in short intervals or in arithmetic progressions. The function field analogue of
\Delta
is

\Delta_{q}\colon\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{C},\qquad\Delta_{q}(f):=\max_{0\leq i\leq\deg(f)}\sum_{d\mid f,\,\deg(d)=i}1.

(1.28)

Let
d_{2}(f)
be the usual divisor function, which satisfies
\langle d_{2}\rangle_{\mathcal{M}_{n}}=n+1

[
20
, Proposition 2.5]
. It is evident that
\max_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{n}}\Delta_{q}(f)\leq\max_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{n}}d_{2}(f)
, which is known to grow slower than any power of
q^{n}
as
n
tends to infinity. Also,
\langle\Delta_{q}\rangle_{\mathcal{M}_{n}},\,\langle\Delta_{q}\rangle_{\mathcal{M}_{n;M}}\geq 1
, since
\Delta_{q}\geq 1
. Applying Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
with
\alpha=\Delta_{q}
, we obtain the following

Corollary 1.5
.

For any
n\geq 1
, let
f_{0}\in\mathcal{M}_{n}
. As
n\to\infty
, we have

\langle\Delta_{q}\rangle_{I(f_{0},h)}\sim\langle\Delta_{q}\rangle_{\mathcal{M}_{n}}

(1.29)

as long as
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{h}{n}>1/2
, and

\langle\Delta_{q}\rangle_{\mathrm{AP}(f_{0},M)}\sim\langle\Delta_{q}\rangle_{\mathcal{M}_{n;M}}

(1.30)

as long as
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\deg(M)}{n}<1/2
.

Previous works

1.4.1
Mean values

In the large-
q
limit, where the degree
n
is fixed and the size of the base field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
tends to infinity, see Bank
et al.

[
1
]
for a general result on mean values, where completely different methods are employed and do not give a result for growing
n
.

For some specific functions, one can go beyond some of our mean-value results, see the works of Sawin
[
21
]
and Sawin and Shusterman
[
22
]
.

1.4.2
Variance

In the large-
q
limit, see Rodgers
[
19
]
for a general result on the variance of arithmetic functions. Again, the methods are different than ours and do not allow one to vary
n
and fix
q
.

For some specific functions, the work of Ramachandra in the integer setting
[
17
]
gives non-trivial mean-value results and variance results, which, although conditional on RH, are much weaker in comparison to ours. For instance, in
[
17
, Equation (11)]
, an upper bound of the form
O(H^{2}X^{-o(1)})
is obtained for the variance of
\mu
in short intervals (and some other functions), while our upper bound should be compared with
O(HX^{o(1)})
, which is better whenever
X^{\varepsilon}<H<X^{1-\varepsilon}
. Recently, Matomäki and Radziwiłł
[
15
]
proved unconditionally that

\frac{1}{X}\int_{X}^{2X}(\sum_{n\in[x,x+H]}\mu(n))^{2}dx=o(H^{2})

(1.31)

which goes beyond Ramachandra’s result for
\mu
if
H
is very small. The methods of
[
15
]
apply in general to bounded multiplicative functions, and so they cannot handle e.g. divisor functions or the von Mangoldt function. Our upper bound is qualitatively better in the range
X^{\varepsilon}<H<X^{1-\varepsilon}
.

2
Results on character sums

Theorems
1.1
–
1.3
rest on the character sums estimate given in Theorem
2.1
below. The theorem involves Hayes characters, which are a function field analogue of Dirichlet characters defined in §
3
. Informally, a function
\chi\colon\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{C}
is called a Hayes character modulo
R_{\ell,M}
(
\ell
a non-negative integer,
M
a non-zero polynomial) if the following conditions hold: (1)
\chi(fg)=\chi(f)\chi(g)
for every
f,g\in\mathcal{M}_{q}
, (2)
\chi(f)
depends only on the residue of
f
modulo
M
and on the first
\ell
next-to-leading coefficients of
f
and (3)
\chi(f)=0
if and only if
\gcd(f,M)\neq 1
. Such a
\chi
is called
trivial
if it only assumes the values
0
and
1
. For instance, the notion of a Dirichlet character modulo
M
coincides with that of a Hayes character modulo
R_{0,M}

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\alpha\colon\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{C}
be a factorization function, and let
\chi
be a non-trivial Hayes character modulo
R_{\ell,M}
. We have

\left|\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{n}}\alpha(f)\chi(f)\right|\leq 7\max_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{n;M}}\left|\alpha(f)\right|q^{\frac{n}{2}}\binom{20(\ell+\deg(M)+1)+n-1}{n}.

(2.1)

If
\ell+\deg(M)=O(n)
, then

\left|\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{n}}\alpha(f)\chi(f)\right|\leq\max_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{n;M}}\left|\alpha(f)\right|q^{\frac{n}{2}}e^{O_{q}(\frac{n\log\log(n+2)}{\log(n+2)})}.

(2.2)

Remark 1
.

If
\ell+\deg(M)
is small compared to
n
, say
\ell+\deg(M)=o(n/\ln n)
, then (
2.1
) is superior to (
2.2
). For
\ell+\deg(M)
which is proportional to
n
, (
2.2
) is better than (
2.1
).

We manage to prove such a theorem, which works for any
\alpha
, by reducing it to estimating a single character sum, which we now describe.

Let
\Omega
be the set of finite multisets of elements from
\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}
, so that
\omega_{f}
, the factorization type of a polynomial
f
, is an element of
\Omega
. For an element
\omega=\{(d_{i},e_{i}):1\leq i\leq k\}\in\Omega
, we define its size to be
|\omega|:=\sum_{i=1}^{k}d_{i}e_{i}
and its length to be
\ell(\omega):=k
. For a factorization function
\alpha
and a factorization type
\omega\in\Omega
, we denote by
\alpha(\omega)
the value of
\alpha
on a polynomial
f
with
\omega_{f}=\omega
if such a polynomial exists, and otherwise set
\alpha(\omega)=0
. We have, by the triangle inequality,

\left|\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{n}}\alpha(f)\chi(f)\right|=\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\omega\in\Omega\\
|\omega|=n\end{subarray}}\alpha(\omega)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{M}_{n}\\
\omega_{f}=\omega\end{subarray}}\chi(f)\right|\leq\max_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{n;M}}\left|\alpha(f)\right|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\omega\in\Omega\\
|\omega|=n\end{subarray}}\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{M}_{n}\\
\omega_{f}=\omega\end{subarray}}\chi(f)\right|.

(2.3)

Thus, it suffices to bound the sum on the right-hand side of (
2.3
). In order to bound the character sums

\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{n}:\,\omega_{f}=\omega}\chi(f),

(2.4)

we relate them to products of
monomial symmetric polynomials
evaluated at
\chi(P)
where
P
runs over irreducible polynomials of certain degrees, see Lemmas
5.1
and
5.2
. We use tools from symmetric function theory in order to be able to use RH efficiently in bounding these evaluations of symmetric polynomials.

Apart from symmetric function theory and RH, we use an additional idea, which first appeared in
[
2
]
. Namely, we use the trivial bound in place of RH in some cases, as the trivial bound is superior when the degrees considered are small. This is in the spirit of the Drinfeld–Vlăduţ bound, where one bounds the number of points on curves over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
when
q
is fixed and the genus varies.

3
Hayes characters

Here we review the function field analogue of Dirichlet characters, first introduced by Hayes in the paper
[
10
]
which is based on his thesis. We call these characters “Hayes characters,” or sometimes “generalized arithmetic progression characters.” Unless otherwise stated, the proofs of the statements in this section appear in Hayes’ original paper. The main difference between Hayes characters and Dirichlet characters is that in the function field setting we can also consider characters modulo the prime at infinity.

3.1
Equivalence relation

Let
\ell
be a non-negative integer and
M
be a non-zero polynomial in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
. We define an equivalence relation
R_{\ell,M}
on
\mathcal{M}
by saying that
A\equiv B\bmod R_{\ell,M}
if and only if
A
and
B
have the same first
\ell
next-to-leading coefficients and
A\equiv B\bmod M
. We adopt throughout the following convention: the
j
th next-to-leading coefficient of a polynomial
f(T)\in\mathcal{M}
with
j>\deg(f)
is considered to be
0
.
It may be shown that there is a well-defined quotient monoid
\mathcal{M}/R_{\ell,M}
, where multiplication is the usual polynomial multiplication. An element of
\mathcal{M}
is invertible modulo
R_{\ell,M}
if and only if it is coprime to
M
. The units of
\mathcal{M}/R_{\ell,M}
form an abelian group, having as identity element the equivalence class of the polynomial
1
. We denote this unit group by
\left(\mathcal{M}/R_{\ell,M}\right)^{\times}
. It may be shown that

\left|\left(\mathcal{M}/R_{\ell,M}\right)^{\times}\right|=q^{\ell}\phi(M).

(3.1)

3.1.1
Characters

For every character
\chi
of the finite abelian group
\left(\mathcal{M}/R_{\ell,M}\right)^{\times}
, we define
\chi^{\dagger}
with domain
\mathcal{M}
as follows. If
A
is invertible modulo
R_{\ell,M}
and if
\mathfrak{c}
is the equivalence class of
A
, then
\chi^{\dagger}(A)=\chi(\mathfrak{c})
. If
A
is not invertible, then
\chi^{\dagger}(A)=0
.

The set of functions
\chi^{\dagger}
defined in this way are called the characters of the relation
R_{\ell,M}
, or sometimes “characters modulo
R_{\ell,M}
”. We shall abuse language somewhat and write
\chi
instead of
\chi^{\dagger}
to indicate a character of the relation
R_{\ell,M}
derived from the character
\chi
of the group
\left(\mathcal{M}/R_{\ell,M}\right)^{\times}
. Thus we write
\chi_{0}
for the character of
R_{\ell,M}
which has the value
1
when
A
is invertible and the value
0
otherwise. We denote by
G(R_{\ell,M})
the set
\{\chi^{\dagger}:\chi\in\widehat{\left(\mathcal{M}/R_{\ell,M}\right)^{\times}}\}
.

A set of polynomials in
\mathcal{M}
is called a representative set modulo
R_{\ell,M}
if the set contains one and only one polynomial from each equivalence class of
R_{\ell,M}
. If
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}\in G(R_{\ell,M})
, then

\frac{1}{q^{\ell}\phi(M)}\sum_{F}\chi_{1}(F)\overline{\chi_{2}}(F)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }\chi_{1}\neq\chi_{2},\\
1&\text{if }\chi_{1}=\chi_{2},\end{cases}

(3.2)

F
running through a representative set modulo
R_{\ell,M}
. If
n\geq\ell+\deg(M)
, then
\mathcal{M}_{n}
is a disjoint union of
q^{n-\ell-\deg(M)}
representative sets, and a set of polynomials on which
\chi\in G(R_{\ell,M})
vanishes. Thus, applying (
3.2
) with
\chi_{2}=\chi_{0}
, we obtain that for all
n\geq\ell+\deg(M)
,

\frac{1}{q^{n-\deg(M)}\phi(M)}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{M}_{n}}\chi(F)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }\chi\neq\chi_{0},\\
1&\text{if }\chi=\chi_{0}.\end{cases}

(3.3)

We also have, for all
A,B\in\mathcal{M}
coprime to
M
,

\frac{1}{q^{\ell}\phi(M)}\sum_{\chi\in G(R_{\ell,M})}\chi(A)\overline{\chi}(B)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }A\equiv B\bmod R_{\ell,M},\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(3.4)

We also call the elements of
G(R_{\ell,M})
“generalized arithmetic progression characters,” because for any
A\in\mathcal{M}_{n}
,
\chi\in G(R_{\ell,M})
is constant on the set

\{f\in\mathcal{M}_{n}:f\equiv A\bmod R_{\ell,M}\}=\{f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]:f\equiv A\bmod M\}\cap\{f\in\mathcal{M}_{n}:\deg(f-A)\leq n-\ell-1\}

which is an intersection of an arithmetic progression and a short interval. We set, for future use,

\mathrm{GAP}(A;\ell,M):=\{f\in\mathcal{M}_{\deg(A)}:f\equiv A\bmod R_{\ell,M}\}.

If
\chi\in G(R_{\ell,1})
, we say that
\chi
is a short interval character of
\ell
coefficients, and if
\chi\in G(R_{0,M})
, we say that
\chi
is a Dirichlet character modulo
M
. Every element of
G(R_{\ell,M})
is a product of an element from
G(R_{\ell,1})
with an element from
G(R_{0,M})
.

3.1.2
L
-Functions

Let
\chi\in G(R_{\ell,M})
. The
L
-function of
\chi
is the following series in
u
:

L(u,\chi)=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}}\chi(f)u^{\deg(f)},

(3.5)

which also admits the Euler product

L(u,\chi)=\prod_{P\in\mathcal{P}}(1-\chi(P)u^{\deg(P)})^{-1}.

(3.6)

If
\chi
is the trivial character
\chi_{0}
of
G(R_{\ell,M})
, then

L(u,\chi)=\frac{\prod_{P\mid M}(1-u^{\deg(P)})}{1-qu}.

(3.7)

Otherwise, the orthogonality relation (
3.3
) implies that
L(u,\chi)
is a polynomial in
u
of degree at most

\deg L(u,\chi)\leq\ell+\deg(M)-1.

(3.8)

The first one to realize that Weil’s proof of the Riemann Hypothesis for Function Fields
[
24
, Theorem 6, p. 134]
implies the Riemann Hypothesis for the
L
-functions of
\chi\in G(R_{\ell,M})
was Rhin
[
18
, Chapitre 2]
in his thesis (cf.
[
4
, Theorem 5.6]
and the discussion following it). Hence we know that if we factor
L(u,\chi)
as

L(u,\chi)=\prod_{i=1}^{\deg L(u,\chi)}(1-\gamma_{i}(\chi)u),

(3.9)

then for any
i
,

\left|\gamma_{i}(\chi)\right|\in\{1,\sqrt{q}\}.

(3.10)

The following consequence of (
3.10
) is a basic ingredient in our proofs.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
M
be a non-zero polynomial in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
and
\ell
a non-negative integer. Let
\chi\in G(R_{\ell,M})
and
n\geq 1
. Then

\left|\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{n}}\chi(P)\right|\leq\begin{cases}\min\{\frac{q^{\frac{n}{2}}}{n}(\ell+\deg(M)+1),\frac{q^{n}}{n}\}&\mbox{if $\chi\neq\chi_{0}$,}\\
\frac{q^{n}}{n}&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{cases}

(3.11)

Proof.

The bound
\left|\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{n}}\chi(P)\right|\leq q^{n}/n
follows from the bound
|\mathcal{P}_{n}|\leq q^{n}/n

[
20
, Proposition 2.1]
. For
\chi\neq\chi_{0}
, we obtain the additional bound as follows. We equate (
3.6
) with (
3.9
) and take logarithmetic derivatives to obtain

|\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{n}}\Lambda(f)\chi(f)|=|\sum_{i=1}^{\deg L(u,\chi)}\gamma_{i}(\chi)^{n}|\leq(\ell+\deg(M)-1)q^{\frac{n}{2}},

(3.12)

where the last passage used (
3.8
) and (
3.10
). We can split (
3.12
) into the contribution of primes of degree
n
and proper prime powers:

\Big|n\sum_{f\in\mathcal{P}_{n}}\chi(f)+\sum_{d\mid n,\,d\neq 1}\frac{n}{d}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{P}_{\frac{n}{d}}}\chi^{d}(f)\Big|\leq(\ell+\deg(M)-1)q^{\frac{n}{2}}.

(3.13)

As

\begin{split}\left|\sum_{d\mid n,\,d\neq 1}\frac{n}{d}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{P}_{\frac{n}{d}}}\chi^{d}(f)\right|&\leq\sum_{d\mid n,\,d\neq 1}\frac{n}{d}|\mathcal{P}_{n/d}|\leq\sum_{d\mid n,\,d\neq 1}q^{n/d}\leq 2q^{\frac{n}{2}},\end{split}

(3.14)

we obtain from (
3.13
) and the triangle inequality that

|n\sum_{f\in\mathcal{P}_{n}}\chi(f)|\leq(\ell+\deg(M)+1)q^{\frac{n}{2}}.

(3.15)

After dividing by
n
, the lemma is established.
∎

3.2
Sums over generalized arithmetic progressions and their variance

For an arithmetic function
\alpha\colon\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{C}
and
n\geq 1
, define

S(n,\alpha):=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{n}}\alpha(f).

(3.16)

The following lemma expresses sums over generalized arithmetic progressions, and the variance of such sums, as sums over characters in
G(R_{\ell,M})
. Special cases of this lemma appeared in a paper of Keating and Rudnick
[
14
]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\ell
be a non-negative integer and
M\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
be a non-zero polynomial. Let
n\geq\ell+\deg(M)
. Let
A\in(\mathcal{M}/R_{\ell,M})^{\times}
and define
f_{A}\in\mathcal{M}
to be some polynomial of degree
n
in the equivalence class of
A
modulo
R_{\ell,M}
. Then the following hold.

1.

We have, for all
g\in\mathcal{M}_{n;M}
,

\mathbf{1}_{g\in\mathrm{GAP}(f_{A};\ell,M)}=\frac{\sum_{\chi\in G(R_{\ell,M})}\overline{\chi}(A)\chi(g)}{\phi(M)q^{\ell}}.

(3.17)

2.

For any arithmetic function
\alpha\colon\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{C}
, we have

\begin{split}\sum_{g\in\mathrm{GAP}(f_{A};\ell,M)}\alpha(g)&=\frac{\sum_{\chi\in G(R_{\ell,M})}\overline{\chi}(A)S(n,\alpha\cdot\chi)}{\phi(M)q^{\ell}}\\
&=q^{n-\ell-\deg(M)}\langle\alpha\rangle_{\mathcal{M}_{n;M}}+\frac{\sum_{\chi_{0}\neq\chi\in G(R_{\ell,M})}\overline{\chi}(A)S(n,\alpha\cdot\chi)}{q^{\ell}\phi(M)}.\end{split}

(3.18)

3.

For any arithmetic function
\alpha\colon\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{C}
, the variance of
\{\sum_{g\in GAP(f;n,\ell;M)}\alpha(g)\}_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{n;M}}
, defined as

\mathrm{Var}_{M}(\alpha;n,n-\ell-1):=\frac{1}{q^{n-\deg(M)}\phi(M)}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{n;M}}\left|\sum_{g\in\mathrm{GAP}(f;\ell,M)}\alpha(g)-q^{n-\ell-\deg(M)}\langle\alpha\rangle_{\mathcal{M}_{n;M}}\right|^{2},

(3.19)

may be expressed as

\mathrm{Var}_{M}(\alpha;n,n-\ell-1)=\frac{\sum_{\chi_{0}\neq\chi\in G(R_{\ell,M})}\left|S(n,\alpha\cdot\chi)\right|^{2}}{q^{2\ell}\phi^{2}(M)}.

(3.20)

Proof.

The first part of the lemma is a restatement of the orthogonality relation (
3.4
). For the second part of the lemma, we observe that
\sum_{g\in\mathrm{GAP}(f_{A};\ell,M)}\alpha(g)=\sum_{g\in\mathcal{M}_{n;M}}\alpha(g)\cdot\mathbf{1}_{g\in\mathrm{GAP}(f_{A};\ell,M)}
, and now we apply the first part of the lemma and interchange the order of summation. Note that

\frac{S(n,\alpha\cdot\chi_{0})}{\phi(M)q^{\ell}}=q^{n-\ell-\deg(M)}\langle\alpha\rangle_{\mathcal{M}_{n;M}}.

(3.21)

To prove the last part of the lemma, we use (
3.21
) and (
3.18
) as follows:

\begin{split}\mathrm{Var}_{M}(\alpha;n,n-\ell-1)&=\frac{1}{q^{\ell}\phi(M)}\sum_{(A\in\mathcal{M}/R_{\ell,M})^{\times}}\left|\sum_{g\in\mathrm{GAP}(f_{A};\ell,M)}\alpha(g)-\frac{S(n,\alpha\cdot\chi_{0})}{\phi(M)q^{\ell}}\right|^{2}\\
&=\frac{1}{q^{\ell}\phi(M)}\sum_{(A\in\mathcal{M}/R_{\ell,M})^{\times}}\left|\frac{\sum_{\chi_{0}\neq\chi\in G(R_{\ell,M})}\overline{\chi}(A)S(n,\alpha\cdot\chi)}{\phi(M)q^{\ell}}\right|^{2}\\
&=\frac{1}{q^{3\ell}\phi^{3}(M)}\sum_{(A\in\mathcal{M}/R_{\ell,M})^{\times}}\sum_{\chi_{1},\chi_{2}\in G(R_{\ell,M})\setminus\{\chi_{0}\}}\overline{\chi_{1}}(A)S(n,\alpha\cdot\chi_{1})\chi_{2}(A)\overline{S(n,\alpha\cdot\chi_{2})}.\end{split}

(3.22)

Interchanging the order of summation and applying (
3.2
), we conclude the proof.
∎

Here and in subsequent sections, we shall use the notation

5.1
Multiplicativity of character sums

Given
d\geq 1
and a factorization type

\omega=\{(d_{i},e_{i}):1\leq i\leq k\}\in\Omega,

(5.1)

we denote by
\omega(d)\subseteq\omega
the factorization type
\{(d_{i},e_{i}):1\leq i\leq k,\,d_{i}=d\}
. By definition,
\omega
is the disjoint of union of the
\omega(d)
-s. Let
\mathbf{1}_{\omega}
be the indicator function of polynomials
f
with
\omega_{f}=\omega
. The following lemma shows that the character sums
S(n,\chi\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\omega})
(recall (
3.16
)) enjoy a multiplicative property.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\omega\in\Omega
with
|\omega|=n
. Let
\chi
be a Hayes character. Then

S(n,\chi\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\omega})=\prod_{d=1}^{n}S(|\omega(d)|,\chi\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\omega(d)}).

(5.2)

Proof.

Each
f
with
\omega_{f}=\omega
can be written uniquely as
f=\prod_{d=1}^{n}f_{d}
where
f_{d}
is divisible only by primes of degree
d
. We then have
\omega_{f_{d}}=\omega(d)
, and the lemma follows by expanding the right-hand side of (
5.2
).
∎

5.2
Symmetric function theory

A partition of size
n
is a finite (possibly empty) non-increasing sequence of positive integers that sum to
n
. The length of a partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{k})
is the number of its elements and is denoted by
\ell(\lambda):=k
. We write
\lambda\vdash n
to indicate that
\lambda
sums to
n
. The empty partition is of size and length
0
. We denote by
\mathbb{Y}
the set of all partitions.

An important class of symmetric polynomials is the
monomial symmetric polynomials
. Given a partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{k})
and variables
X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}
, the monomial symmetric polynomial
m_{\lambda}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{k})
is the symmetric polynomial

m_{\lambda}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}):=\sum_{\exists\pi\in S_{k}:(\lambda^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,\lambda^{\prime}_{k})=(\lambda_{\pi(1)},\ldots,\lambda_{\pi(k)})}\prod_{i=1}^{k}X^{\lambda^{\prime}_{i}}_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}],

(5.3)

where the sum is over the
distinct
permutations of
\lambda
. It is useful to extend
m_{\lambda}
to the case of a general number of variables
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
. If
n<k
, we define
m_{\lambda}(X_{i}:1\leq i\leq n)
to be zero. If
n>k
, we set
\lambda_{j}=0
for
j=k+1,\ldots,n
and define

m_{\lambda}(X_{i}:1\leq i\leq n):=\sum_{\exists\pi\in S_{n}:(\lambda^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,\lambda^{\prime}_{n})=(\lambda_{\pi(1)},\ldots,\lambda_{\pi(n)})}\prod_{i=1}^{n}X^{\lambda^{\prime}_{i}}_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}],

(5.4)

where the sum is over the
distinct
permutations of
\lambda
followed by
n-k
zeros. In particular,
m_{\lambda}
is the elementary symmetric polynomial
e_{k}
if
\lambda=(1,1,\ldots,1)
with
k
ones.

The following lemma expresses character sums, of the form appearing in the right-hand side of (
5.2
), as an evaluation of a monomial symmetric polynomial.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\omega=\{(d,e_{i}):1\leq i\leq k\}\in\Omega
. Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{Y}
be the partition whose parts are
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
in non-increasing order. Let
\chi
be a Hayes character. We have

S(|\omega|,\chi\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\omega})=m_{\lambda}(\chi(P):P\in\mathcal{P}_{d}).

(5.5)

Proof.

A polynomial
f
with
\omega_{f}=\omega
is necessarily given by a product
\prod_{i=1}^{k}P_{i}^{e_{i}}
where
P_{i}
are distinct elements from
\mathcal{P}_{d}
. Equivalently,
f
may be expressed as
\prod_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{d}}P^{e(P)}
where the multiset
\{e(P):P\in\mathcal{P}_{d}\}
is equal to

E:=\{e_{i}:1\leq i\leq k\}\cup\{0:1\leq i\leq|\mathcal{P}_{d}|-k\}.

(5.6)

Moreover, by unique factorization, this form is unique. Thus,

\begin{split}S(|\omega|,\chi\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\omega})&=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{M}_{|\omega|}:\omega_{f}=\omega}\chi(f)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e\colon\mathcal{P}_{d}\to\mathbb{N}_{\geq 0}\\
\{e(P):P\in\mathcal{P}_{d}\}=E\end{subarray}}\chi\left(\prod_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{d}}P^{e(P)}\right)\\
&=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}e\colon\mathcal{P}_{d}\to\mathbb{N}_{\geq 0}\\
\{e(P):P\in\mathcal{P}_{d}\}=E\end{subarray}}\prod_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{d}}\big(\chi(P)\big)^{e(P)},\end{split}

(5.7)

which is just
m_{\lambda}
evaluated at
\{\chi(P):P\in\mathcal{P}_{d}\}
, as needed.
∎

Another class of symmetric polynomials is the
power sum symmetric polynomials
. Given a positive integer
r
, the power sum symmetric polynomial
p_{r}(X_{i}:1\leq i\leq n)
is the symmetric polynomial

p_{r}(X_{i}:1\leq i\leq n):=\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}^{r}\in\mathbb{Z}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}].

(5.8)

More generally, given a partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{k})
, then the power sum symmetric polynomial
p_{\lambda}(X_{i}:1\leq i\leq n)
is the symmetric polynomial

p_{\lambda}(X_{i}:1\leq i\leq n):=\prod_{i=1}^{k}p_{\lambda_{i}}(X_{i}:1\leq i\leq n).

(5.9)

A basic result in symmetric function theory says that whenever
m\geq n
,
\{m_{\lambda}(X_{i}:1\leq i\leq m)\}_{\lambda\vdash n}
and
\{p_{\lambda}(X_{i}:1\leq i\leq m)\}_{\lambda\vdash n}
are both bases for homogeneous symmetric polynomials of degree
n
with rational coefficients. In particular,
m_{\lambda}(X_{i}:1\leq i\leq m)
can be expressed uniquely as a linear combination of the symmetric polynomials
p_{\mu}
for partitions
\mu
of size
n
, that is, there are unique coefficients
c_{\lambda,\mu}\in\mathbb{Q}
such that

m_{\lambda}(X_{i}:1\leq i\leq m)=\sum_{\mu\vdash n}c_{\lambda,\mu}p_{\mu}(X_{i}:1\leq i\leq m)

(5.10)

for all
m\geq n
(
c_{\lambda,\mu}
are independent of
m
). Eğecioğlu and Remmel
[
5
, pp. 107–111]
gave a combinatorial interpretation of
c_{\lambda,\mu}
which we now describe. We begin with their definition of
\lambda
-brick tabloids.

Lemma 5.3

\ell(\lambda)=t\end{subarray}}w(B_{\lambda,\mu})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b_{1},\ldots,b_{r}\geq 1\\

5.3
Permutation statistics

We denote the expectation of a function
f\colon S_{n}\to\mathbb{R}
with respect to the uniform probability measure on
S_{n}
by
\mathrm{E}_{\pi\in S_{n}}f(\pi)
. We denote by
\ell(\pi)
the number of cycles in a permutation
\pi
.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
n\geq 1
,
m\geq 2
be positive integers. Let
z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
. Define the following function on
S_{n}
:

f(\pi)=\prod_{C\in\pi,\,m\nmid\left|C\right|}z_{1}\prod_{C\in\pi,\,m\mid\left|C\right|}z_{2}

(5.24)

where the product is over the disjoint cycles of
\pi
. Then

\mathrm{E}_{\pi\in S_{n}}f(\pi)=[u^{n}](1-u)^{-z_{1}}(1-u^{m})^{(-z_{2}+z_{1})/m}.

(5.25)

Proof.

The exponential formula for permutations
[
23
, Corollary 5.1.9]
states the following. Given a function
g\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{C}
, we construct a corresponding function on permutations (on arbitrary number of elements) as follows:

G(\pi)=\prod_{C\in\pi}g(|C|),

(5.26)

where the product is over the disjoint cycles of
\pi
. We then have the following identity of formal power series:

1+\sum_{i\geq 1}\left(\mathrm{E}_{\pi\in S_{i}}G(\pi)\right)u^{i}=\exp(\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{g(j)}{j}u^{j}).

(5.27)

Applying the identity with

g(j)=\begin{cases}z_{1}&\mbox{if $m\nmid j$,}\\
z_{2}&\mbox{otherwise,}\end{cases}

(5.28)

we find that
G(\pi)=f(\pi)
for every
\pi\in S_{n}
, and the lemma follows by a short computation.
∎

) with
m:=\mathrm{ord}(\chi)
and

z_{1}=z_{1,d}:=\min\{\frac{q^{\frac{d}{2}}}{d}(\ell+\deg(M)+1),\frac{q^{d}}{d}\},\qquad z_{2}=z_{2,d}:=\frac{q^{d}}{d},

(6.9)

and
\mu_{\pi}
is the partition of
n
whose parts are the cycle sizes of
\pi
. By Lemma
5.3
and (
6.8
), the coefficients of
F_{d}
are bounded from above by the coefficients of

G_{d}(u):=1+\sum_{n\geq 1}\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{|\mathcal{P}_{d}|}\binom{n}{i}\Big)\mathrm{E}_{\pi\in S_{n}}f(\pi)u^{n}.

(6.10)

If
q\geq 4
, we do the following. Replacing
\sum_{i=0}^{|\mathcal{P}_{q}|}\binom{n}{i}
with
2^{n}
, we use Lemma
5.4
to bound the coefficients of
G_{d}
from above by the coefficients of

H_{d}(u):=(1-2u)^{-z_{1,d}}(1-(2u)^{\mathrm{ord}(\chi)})^{(-z_{2,d}+z_{1,d})/\mathrm{ord}(\chi)},

(6.11)

and so the coefficients of
F_{\chi}
are bounded from above by the coefficients of

H_{\chi}(u):=\prod_{d\geq 1}H_{d}(u^{d}).

(6.12)

Summarizing,

\begin{split}\sum_{\omega\in\Omega,\,|\omega|=n}|S(n,\chi\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\omega})|&=[u^{n}]F_{\chi}(u)\leq[u^{n}]H_{\chi}(u)\\
&=[u^{n}]\prod_{d\geq 1}(1-2u^{d})^{-z_{1,d}}(1-2^{\mathrm{ord}(\chi)}u^{d\,\mathrm{ord}(\chi)})^{(-z_{2,d}+z_{1,d})/\mathrm{ord}(\chi)}.\end{split}

(6.13)

The logarithm of the power series
H_{\chi}
is given by

\log H_{\chi}(u)=\sum_{i,d\geq 1}\frac{2^{i}u^{di}}{i}z_{1,d}+\sum_{i,d\geq 1}\frac{2^{i\,\mathrm{ord}(\chi)}u^{di\,\mathrm{ord}(\chi)}}{i}\frac{z_{2,d}-z_{1,d}}{\mathrm{ord}(\chi)},

(6.14)

so that

[u^{k}]\log H_{\chi}(u)=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{di=k}2^{i}(z_{1,d}d)+\frac{\mathbf{1}_{\mathrm{ord}(\chi)\mid k}}{k}\cdot\sum_{di=\frac{k}{\mathrm{ord}(\chi)}}2^{i\,\mathrm{ord}(\chi)}(z_{2,d}-z_{1,d})d.

(6.15)

Set

L:=\lfloor 2\log_{q}(\ell+\deg(M)+1)\rfloor.

(6.16)

For
d\leq L
, we have
z_{1,d}=z_{2,d}=q^{d}/d
, so that by the first part of Lemma
5.5
with
x=q
,

[u^{k}]\log H_{\chi}(u)\leq\frac{1}{k}\sum_{di=k}2^{i}q^{d}\leq\frac{8q^{k}}{k}.

(6.17)

for all
1\leq k\leq L
. As
z_{2,d}-z_{1,d}\leq q^{d}/d
and
z_{1,d}\leq q^{\frac{d}{2}}(\ell+\deg(M)+1)/d
, we also have, by the first part of Lemma
5.5
with
x=\sqrt{q}
and the second part of the lemma with
x=q
, that

\begin{split}[u^{k}]\log H_{\chi}(u)&\leq\frac{(\ell+\deg(M)+1)}{k}\sum_{di=k}2^{i}q^{\frac{d}{2}}+\frac{\mathbf{1}_{\mathrm{ord}(\chi)\mid k}}{k}\cdot\sum_{di=\frac{k}{\mathrm{ord}(\chi)}}2^{i\,\mathrm{ord}(\chi)}q^{d}\\
&\leq 8(\ell+\deg(M)+1)\frac{q^{\frac{k}{2}}}{k}+10\frac{q^{\frac{k}{2}}}{k}\leq 10(\ell+\deg(M)+2)\frac{q^{\frac{k}{2}}}{k}\end{split}

(6.18)

for all
k\geq 1
. From (
6.13
) and (
6.18
), we have

\sum_{\omega\in\Omega,\,|\omega|=n}|S(n,\chi\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\omega})|\leq[u^{n}]\exp(\sum_{1\leq k\leq L}\frac{10q^{k}}{k}u^{k}+\sum_{k>L}10(\ell+\deg(M)+2)\frac{q^{\frac{k}{2}}}{k}u^{k}),

(6.19)

and by Lemma
5.8
with
t=q
,
r=\ell+\deg(M)+1
, we have the estimates

\sum_{\omega\in\Omega,\,|\omega|=n}|S(n,\chi\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\omega})|\leq q^{\frac{n}{2}}\binom{20(\ell+\deg(M)+2)+n-1}{n}

(6.20)

and, if
\ell+\deg(M)=O(n)
,

\sum_{\omega\in\Omega,\,|\omega|=n}|S(n,\chi\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\omega})|\leq q^{\frac{n}{2}}e^{O_{q}(\frac{n\log\log(n+2)}{\log(n+2)})}.

(6.21)

The theorem is established for
q\geq 4
. We now suppose
q\in\{2,3\}
. We define
\widetilde{H_{d}}:=H_{d}
for
d\geq 2
, while for
d=1

\widetilde{H_{1}}(u):=1+\sum_{n\geq 1}1.4^{n}\mathrm{E}_{\pi\in S_{n}}f(\pi)u^{n}=(1-1.4u)^{-z_{1,1}}(1-(1.4u)^{\mathrm{ord}(\chi)})^{(-z_{2,1}+z_{1,1})/\mathrm{ord}(\chi)},

(6.22)

where in the last passage we used Lemma
5.4
. As
|\mathcal{P}_{1}|=q\leq 3
for
q\in\{2,3\}
, it follows that
\sum_{i=0}^{|\mathcal{P}_{1}|}\binom{n}{i}\leq\sum_{i=0}^{3}\binom{n}{i}
, which is at most
7\cdot 1.4^{n}
by Lemma
5.7
. Thus the coefficients of
G_{1}
are bounded from above by those of
\widetilde{H_{1}}
times
7
, and so the coefficients of
F_{\chi}
are bounded from above by those of

\widetilde{H_{\chi}}(u):=\prod_{d\geq 1}\widetilde{H_{d}}(u^{d}),

(6.23)

times
7
. As in the case
q\geq 4
, we proceed to upper bound the coefficients of
\log\widetilde{H_{\chi}}
. For
d\leq L
, we have
z_{1,d}=z_{2,d}=q^{d}/d
, so that by Lemma
5.6
with
x=q
,

[u^{k}]\log\widetilde{H_{\chi}}(u)\leq\frac{1}{k}\Big(1.4^{k}q+\sum_{di=k,\,d\neq 1}2^{i}q^{d}\Big)\leq 10\frac{q^{k}}{k},

(6.24)

for all
1\leq k\leq L
, where we used
1.4<q\leq 3
. For any
k\geq 1
, we have by Lemma
5.6
that

\begin{split}[u^{k}]\log\widetilde{H_{\chi}}(u)&\leq\frac{(\ell+\deg(M)+1)}{k}\Big(1.4^{k}q^{\frac{1}{2}}+\sum_{di=k,\,d\neq 1}2^{i}q^{\frac{d}{2}}\Big)+\frac{\mathbf{1}_{\mathrm{ord}(\chi)\mid k}}{k}\big(\sum_{di=\frac{k}{\mathrm{ord}(\chi)},\,d\neq 1}2^{i\,\mathrm{ord}(\chi)}q^{d}+1.4^{k}q\big)\\
&\leq\frac{(\ell+\deg(M)+1)}{k}\Big(2\cdot 1.4^{k}+7q^{\frac{k}{2}}\Big)+14\frac{q^{\frac{k}{2}}}{k}\leq 20(\ell+\deg(M)+2)\frac{q^{\frac{k}{2}}}{k}.\end{split}

(6.25)

From this point we continue as in the case
q\geq 4
and conclude the proof of the theorem.

On the action of the Steenrod-Milnor operations on the invariants of the general linear groups
Nguyen Thai Hoa, Pham Thi Kim Minh, Nguyen Sum

Abstract




1.
Introduction

Let
p
be an odd prime number. Denote by
GL_{n}=GL(n,\mathbb{F}_{p})
the general linear group over the prime field
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Each subgroup of
GL_{n}
acts on the algebra
P_{n}=E(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})
in the usual manner. Here and in what follows,
E(.,\ldots,.)
and
\mathbb{F}_{p}(.,\ldots,.)
are the exterior and polynomial algebras over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
generated by the indicated variables. We grade
P_{n}
by assigning
\dim x_{i}=1
and
\dim y_{i}=2.

Dickson showed in
[
1
]
that the invariant algebra
\mathbb{F}_{p}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})^{GL_{n}}
is a polynomial algebra generated by the Dickson invariants
Q_{n,s},\ 0\leq s<n
. Huỳnh Mùi
[
5
,
6
]
computed the invariant algebras
P_{n}^{GL_{n}}
. He proved that
P_{n}^{GL_{n}}
is generated by
Q_{n,s},\ 0\leq s<n,\ R_{n,s_{1},\ldots,s_{k}},\ 0\leq s_{1}<\ldots<s_{k}<n.
Here
R_{n,s_{1},\ldots,s_{k}}
are Mùi invariants and
Q_{n,s}
are Dickson invariants (see Section 2).

Let
\mathcal{A}_{p}
be the mod
p
Steenrod algebra and let

\tau_{s},\xi_{i}
be the Milnor elements of dimensions
2p^{s}-1,\ 2p^{i}-2
respectively in the dual algebra
\mathcal{A}_{p}^{*}
of
\mathcal{A}_{p}
.
In
[
4
]
, Milnor showed that as an algebra,

\mathcal{A}_{p}^{*}=E(\tau_{0},\tau_{1},\ldots)\ \otimes\ \mathbb{F}_{p}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\ldots).

Then
\mathcal{A}_{p}^{*}
has a basis consisting of all monomials

\tau_{S}\xi^{R}\ =\ \tau_{s_{1}}\ldots\tau_{s_{k}}\xi_{1}^{r_{1}}\ldots\xi_{m}^{r_{m}},

with
S=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{k}),\ 0\leq s_{1}<\ldots<s_{k},R=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{m}),\ r_{i}\geq 0
. Let
St^{S,R}\in\mathcal{A}_{p}

denote the dual of
\tau_{S}\xi^{R}
with respect to that basis.
Then
\mathcal{A}_{p}
has a basis consisting of all operations
St^{S,R}
. For
S=\emptyset,R=(r)
,
St^{\emptyset,(r)}
is nothing but the Steenrod operation
P^{r}
. So, we call
St^{S,R}
the Steenrod-Milnor operation of type
(S,R)
.

We have the Cartan formula

St^{S,R}(uv)=\sum_{\overset{\scriptstyle{S_{1}\cup S_{2}=S}}{R_{1}+R_{2}=R}}St^{S_{1},R_{1}}(u)St^{S_{2},R_{2}}(v),

where
R_{1}=(r_{1i}),\ R_{2}=(r_{2i}),\ R_{1}+R_{2}=(r_{1i}+r_{2i}),S_{1}\cap S_{2}=\emptyset,u,v\in P_{n}
(see Mùi
[
6
]
).

We denote
St_{u}=St^{(u),(0)},\ St^{\Delta_{i}}=St^{\emptyset,\Delta_{i}}
, where
\Delta_{i}=(0,\ldots,

1,\ldots,0)
with 1 at the
i
-th place. In
[
6
]
, Huỳnh Mùi proved that as a coalgebra,

\mathcal{A}_{p}=\Lambda(St_{0},St_{1},\ldots)\otimes\Gamma(St^{\Delta_{1}},St^{\Delta_{1}},\ldots).

Here,
\Lambda(St_{0},St_{1},\ldots)
(resp.
\Gamma(St^{\Delta_{1}},St^{\Delta_{2}},\ldots)
) denotes the exterior (resp. polynomial) Hopf algebra with divided powers generated by the primitive Steenrod-Milnor operations
St_{0},\ \!St_{1},\ldots
(resp.
St^{\Delta_{1}},St^{\Delta_{2}},\ldots)
.

The Steenrod algebra
\mathcal{A}_{p}
acts on
P_{n}
by means of the Cartan formula together with the relations

\displaystyle St^{S,R}x_{k}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}x_{k},&S=\emptyset,\ R=(0),\\
y_{k}^{p^{u}},&S=(u),\ R=(0),\\
0,&otherwise,\end{cases}

\displaystyle St^{S,R}y_{k}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}y_{k},&S=\emptyset,\ R=(0),\\
y_{k}^{p^{i}},&S=\emptyset,\ R=\Delta_{i},\\
0,&otherwise,\end{cases}

for
k=1,\ \!2,\ldots,n
(see Steenrod-Epstein
[
8
]
, Sum
[
11
]
).
Since this action commutes with the action of
GL_{n}
, it induces an actions of
\mathcal{A}_{p}
on
P_{n}^{GL_{n}}
.

The action of
St^{S,R}
on the modular invariants of subgroups of general linear group has partially been studied by many authors. This action for
S=\emptyset,\ \!R=(r)
was explicitly determined by Smith-Switzer
[
7
]
, Hung-Minh
[
2
]
, Kechagias
[
3
]
, Sum
[
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
]
. Smith-Switzer
[
7
]
, Wilkerson
[
15
]
have studied the action of
St^{\Delta_{i}}
on the Dickson invariants.

The purpose of the paper is to compute the action of the Steenrod-Milnor operations on the generators of
P_{2}^{GL_{2}}
. More precisely, we explicitly determine the action of
St^{(i,j)}
on the Dickson invariants
Q_{2,0}
and
Q_{2,1}
.

The analogous results for
p=2
have been anounced in
[
9
]
.

The rest of the paper contains two sections. In Section 2, we recall some needed information on the invariant theory. In Section 3, we compute the action of the Steenrod-Milnor operations on the Dickson invariants.

2.
Preliminaries

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(e_{k+1},\ldots,e_{n}),\ 0\leq k<n
, be a sequence of non-negative integers. Following Dickson
[
1
]
, Mùi
[
5
]
, we define

[k;e_{k+1},\ldots,e_{n}]=\frac{1}{k!}\begin{vmatrix}x_{1}&\cdots&x_{n}\\
\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
x_{1}&\cdots&x_{n}\\
y_{1}^{p^{e_{k+1}}}&\cdots&y_{m}^{p^{e_{k+1}}}\\
\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
y_{1}^{p^{e_{n}}}&\cdots&y_{m}^{p^{e_{n}}}\end{vmatrix},

in which there are exactly
k
rows of
(x_{1}\ldots x_{n})
. (See the accurate meaning of the
determinants in a commutative graded algebra in Mùi n
[
5
]
). For
k=0
, we write

[0;e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}]=[e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}]=\det(y_{i}^{p^{e_{j}}}).

In particular, we set

\displaystyle L_{n,s}

\displaystyle=[0,1,\ldots,\hat{s},\ldots,n],\ \!0\leq s\leq n,

\displaystyle L_{n}

\displaystyle=L_{n,n}=[0,1,\ldots,n-1].

Each
[k;e_{k+1},\ldots,e_{n}]
is an invariant of the special linear group
SL_{n}
and
[e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}]
is divisible by
L_{n}
. Then, Dickson invariants
Q_{n,s},\ 0\leq s<n
, are defined by

Q_{n,s}=L_{n,s}/L_{n}.

Here, by convention,
L_{0}=[\emptyset]=1.

3.
Main Results

First of all, we recall the following.

Proposition 3.1

(Hung-Minh
[
2
]
, Sum
[
11
]
)
.

Let
i
be a nonnegative integer. We have

\displaystyle St^{(i,0)}Q_{2,0}=\begin{cases}Q_{2,0}^{p},&i=p^{2}-1,\\
(-1)^{k}\binom{k}{r}Q_{2,0}^{r+1}Q_{2,1}^{k-r},&i=kp+r,\ 0\leq r\leq k<p,\\
0,&\text{ otherwise},\end{cases}

\displaystyle St^{(i,0)}Q_{2,1}=\begin{cases}Q_{2,1}^{p},&i=p^{2}-p,\\
(-1)^{k}\binom{k+1}{r}Q_{2,0}^{r}Q_{2,1}^{k+1-r},&i=kp+r,\ r\leq k+1\leq p,\\
0,&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

Theorem 3.2

(Sum
[
12
]
)
.

Let
j
be a nonnegative integer and
s=0,1
. Then we have

St^{(0,j)}Q_{2,s}=\begin{cases}Q_{2,0}^{r}Q_{2,s}\sum_{i=0}^{k}(-1)^{i}\binom{k+s}{i+s}\binom{r+i}{i}Q_{2,0}^{(k-i)p}Q_{2,1}^{i(p+1)},\\
\hskip 62.59596pt\text{ if }j=kp+r\text{ with }0\leq k,\ r<p,\\
0,\hskip 56.9055pt\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Our new results are the actions of
St^{(i,j)}
on
Q_{2,0}
and
Q_{2,1}
with
i>0.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
i,j
be nonnegative integers. We have

(i)
If
0\leq k<i<p
and
0\leq r<p
then

St^{(i,kp+r)}Q_{2,0}=0.

(ii)
If
0\leq i<p
then

St^{(i,ip)}Q_{2,0}=(-1)^{i}Q_{2,0}^{i+1}Q_{2,1}^{ip}.

(iii)

St^{(1,j)}Q_{2,0}=\begin{cases}-Q_{2,0}^{r+2}Q_{2,1}^{p}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}(-1)^{i}\big[k\binom{k-1}{i}\binom{r+i+1}{i+1}\\
\hskip 71.13188pt-\binom{k-2}{i-1}\binom{r+i}{i+1}\big]Q_{2,0}^{(k-1-i)p}Q_{2,1}^{i(p+1)},\\
\hskip 62.59596pt\text{ if }j=kp+r\text{ with }0\leq k,r<p,\\
0,\hskip 56.9055pt\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(iv)
If
k+1<i<p
and
0\leq r<p
then

St^{(i,kp+r)}Q_{2,1}=0.

(v)
If
0\leq k<p
then

St^{(k+1,kp)}Q_{2,1}=(-1)^{k}Q_{2,0}^{k+1}Q_{2,1}^{kp}.

(vi)

St^{(1,j)}Q_{2,1}=\begin{cases}(k+1)Q_{2,0}^{r+1}\sum_{i=0}^{k}(-1)^{i}\binom{k}{i}\binom{r+i}{i}Q_{2,0}^{(k-i)p}Q_{2,1}^{i(p+1)}\\
\hskip 65.44142pt\text{if $j=kp+r$ with $0\leq k,\ r<p$,}\\
0,\hskip 56.9055pt\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

We prove i) by induction on
k
. Let
k=0
. For
r=0
, using Cartan formula we have

St^{(i,0)}Q_{2,0}=0.
For
r>0
, applying the Cartan formula and the inductive hypothesis one gets

St^{(i,r)}Q_{2,0}=Q_{2,0}St^{(i,r-1)}Q_{2,0}=0.

Part i) of the theorem holds for
k=0
. Suppose it is true for
0\leq k<p-1
. For
i>k+1
, we have

\displaystyle St^{(i,(k+1)p)}Q_{2,0}

\displaystyle=Q_{2,0}St^{(i,kp+p-1)}Q_{2,0}+(Q_{2,0}^{p}-Q_{2,1}^{p+1})St^{(i,kp)}Q_{2,0}

\displaystyle\qquad-Q_{2,0}^{p+1}St^{(i,(k-1)p+p-1)}Q_{2,0}-Q_{2,0}Q_{2,1}^{p}St^{(i-1,kp)}Q_{2,0}

\displaystyle=0.

Part i) of the theorem holds for
i>k+1
and
r=0
. Suppose it is true for
i>k+1
and
0\leq r<p-1
. Then using the inductive hypothesis we have

\displaystyle St^{(i,(k+1)p+r+1)}Q_{2,0}

\displaystyle=Q_{2,0}St^{(i,(k+1)p+r)}Q_{2,0}+(Q_{2,0}^{p}-Q_{2,1}^{p+1})St^{(i,kp+r+1)}Q_{2,0}

\displaystyle\qquad-Q_{2,0}^{p+1}St^{(i,kp+r)}Q_{2,0}-Q_{2,0}Q_{2,1}^{p}St^{(i-1,kp+r+1)}Q_{2,0}

\displaystyle=0.

Part i) of the theorem is proved.

Now we prove Part ii). Obviously, it is true for
i=0
. Suppose
i>0
and Part ii) holds for
i-1
. Using the Cartan formula and the inductive hypothesis, we have

\displaystyle St^{(i,ip)}Q_{2,0}

\displaystyle=-Q_{2,0}Q_{2,1}^{p}St^{(i-1,(i-1)p)}Q_{2,0}

\displaystyle=-(-1)^{i-1}Q_{2,0}Q_{2,1}^{p}Q_{2,0}^{i}Q_{2,1}^{(i-1)p}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{i}Q_{2,0}^{i+1}Q_{2,1}^{ip}.

The proof of Part iii). It is well known that the Steenrod-Milnor operations are stable. That means
St^{(1,j)}x=0
for any homogeneous
x\in P_{2}
with
\deg x<j
(see Mùi
[
5
]
). Since
\deg Q_{2,0}=p^{2}-1<p^{2}
, we have
St^{(1,j)}Q_{2,0}=0
for
j\geq p^{2}
. Suppose that
j<p^{2}
. Then using the
p
-adic expansion of
j
, we have

j=kp+r\ \text{ for }0\leq k,r<p.

Now we prove the theorem by double induction on
(k,r)
.

If
k=0
, then using Part i) of Theorem
3.3

we get
St^{(1,r)}Q_{2,0}=0
, so the theorem is true for
k=0
.
If
r=p-1
, then
\binom{r+i+1}{i+1}=\binom{r+i}{i+1}=0

in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, so we obtain

St^{(1,kp+p-1)}Q_{2,0}=0.

If
r=0
, then the formula in the theorem becomes

St^{(1,kp)}Q_{2,0}=-kQ_{2,0}^{2}Q_{2,1}^{p}(Q_{2,0}^{p}-Q_{2,1}^{p+1})^{k-1}.

We prove this formula by induction on
k
. Suppose this formula holds for
0\leq k<p-1
. For
r=0
, we have

\displaystyle St^{(1,(k+1)p)}Q_{2,0}

\displaystyle=(Q_{2,0}^{p}-Q_{2,1}^{p+1})St^{(1,kp)}Q_{2,0}-Q_{2,0}Q_{2,1}^{p}St^{(0,kp)}Q_{2,0}

\displaystyle=-kQ_{2,0}^{2}Q_{2,1}^{p}(Q_{2,0}^{p}-Q_{2,1}^{p+1})^{k}-Q_{2,0}^{2}Q_{2,1}^{p}(Q_{2,0}^{p}-Q_{2,1}^{p+1})^{k}

\displaystyle=-(k+1)Q_{2,0}^{2}Q_{2,1}^{p}(Q_{2,0}^{p}-Q_{2,1}^{p+1})^{k}.

So the formula holds for
k+1
and
r=0
. Suppose
0\leq r<p-1
and the formula holds for
j=(k+1)p+r
. Applying the Cartan formula and the inductive hypothesis, one gets

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle t^{(1,(k+1)p+r+1)}Q_{2,0}=Q_{2,0}St^{(1,(k+1)p+r)}Q_{2,0}+(Q_{2,0}^{p}-Q_{2,1}^{p+1})St^{(1,kp+r+1)}Q_{2,0}

\displaystyle\quad-Q_{2,0}^{p+1}St^{(1,kp+r)}Q_{2,0}-Q_{2,0}Q_{2,1}^{p}St^{(0,kp+r+1)}Q_{2,0}

\displaystyle=-Q_{2,0}^{r+3}Q_{2,1}^{p}\Big(k\sum_{i=0}^{k}(-1)^{i}\big[\binom{k}{i}\binom{r+i+1}{i+1}-\binom{k-1}{i}\binom{r+i+2}{i+1}

\displaystyle\quad+\binom{k-1}{i-1}\binom{r+i+1}{i}-\binom{k-1}{i}\binom{r+i+1}{i+1}\big]Q_{2,0}^{(k-i)p}Q_{2,1}^{i(p+1)}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{i=0}^{k}(-1)^{i}\binom{k}{i}\big[\binom{r+i+1}{i+1}-\binom{r+i+1}{i}\big]Q_{2,0}^{(k-i)p}Q_{2,1}^{i(p+1)}

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{i=0}^{k}(-1)^{i}\big[\binom{k-1}{i-1}\binom{r+i}{i+1}+\binom{k-2}{i-1}\binom{r+i+1}{i+1}

\displaystyle\quad+\binom{k-2}{i-2}\binom{r+i}{i}-\binom{k-2}{i-1}\binom{r+i}{i+1}\big]Q_{2,0}^{(k-i)p}Q_{2,1}^{i(p+1)}\Big)

\displaystyle=-Q_{2,0}^{r+3}Q_{2,1}^{p}\sum_{i=0}^{k}(-1)^{i}\big[(k+1)\binom{k}{i}\binom{r+i+2}{i+1}

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt-\binom{k-1}{i-1}\binom{r+i+1}{i+1}\big]Q_{2,0}^{(k-i)p}Q_{2,1}^{i(p+1)}.

Parts iv), v) and vi) are proved by the same argument as the previous one.

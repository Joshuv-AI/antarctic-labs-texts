Spectral gap and character limits in arithmetic groups
Arie Levit, Raz Slutsky and Itamar Vigdorovich

Abstract
. We establish vanishing results for limits of characters in various discrete groups, most notably irreducible lattices in higher rank semisimple Lie groups. As an application, we show that any sequence of finite-dimensional representations converges to the regular representation in the Fell topology. We achieve this by studying the geometry of the simplex of traces of discrete groups having Kazhdan’s property (T) or its relative generalizations.

if and only if the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
is finite-dimensional.

Let
\pi
be a unitary representation acting on the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. Then the unitary representation
\pi\otimes\pi^{*}
acts on the tensor product Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}\otimes\overline{\mathcal{H}}
, and this action is equivalent to the conjugation action on
\mathrm{HS}(\mathcal{H})
.
An invariant vector
T\in\mathrm{HS}(\mathcal{H})
for the unitary representation
\pi\otimes\pi^{*}
is an operator satisfying
\pi(\gamma)T=T\pi(\gamma)
for all elements
\gamma\in\Gamma
. In other words, such an invariant vector
T
is an intertwining operator for the representation
\pi
.

A trace
\varphi\in\mathrm{Tr}\left(\Gamma\right)
is said to be
finite-dimensional
if its GNS unitary representation
\pi_{\varphi}
is finite-dimensional.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\varphi\in\mathrm{Tr}\left(\Gamma\right)
be a trace. The unitary representation
\pi_{\varphi}\otimes\pi_{\varphi}^{*}
has invariant vectors if and only if
\psi\leq\varphi
for some finite-dimensional character
\psi\in\mathrm{Ch}\left(\Gamma\right)
.

Proof.

Assume that there is a finite-dimensional character
\psi\in\mathrm{Ch}\left(\Gamma\right)
given by
\psi=\varphi_{T}
for some non-zero operator
T\in\mathrm{Z}(M_{\varphi})
satisfying
0\leq T\leq 1
.
Consider the closed subspace
\mathcal{L}
of the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\varphi}
given by

(2.4)

\mathcal{L}=\overline{\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{\pi_{\varphi}(\gamma)v_{\psi}\>:\>\gamma\in\Gamma\}}

where

v_{\psi}=T^{\frac{1}{2}}v_{\varphi}
. The GNS data associated to the character
\psi
is uniquely determined up to an isomorphism. This implies that the subspace
\mathcal{L}
is isomorphic to the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\psi}
. As such, the closed subspace
\mathcal{L}
is finite-dimensional. Therefore the orthogonal projection operator
\mathrm{P}
to the subspace
\mathcal{L}
belongs to
\mathrm{HS}(\mathcal{H}_{\varphi})
. Moreover
\mathrm{P}
is an invariant vector for the unitary representation
\pi_{\varphi}\otimes\pi_{\varphi}^{*}
as the subspace
\mathcal{L}
is
\pi_{\varphi}(\Gamma)
-invariant.

Conversely, suppose that the unitary representation
\pi_{\varphi}\otimes\pi^{*}_{\varphi}
admits invariant vectors. It follows that the unitary representation
\pi_{\varphi}
admits a finite-dimensional sub-representation
\pi_{0}

[
BdLHV08
, A.1.12]
.
Let
\mathrm{P}
be the orthogonal projection to the sum of all the sub-representations of
\pi_{\varphi}
isomorphic to
\pi_{0}
. Then
\mathrm{P}\in\pi_{\varphi}(\Gamma)^{\prime}
. In addition
\mathrm{P}\in\pi_{\varphi}(\Gamma)^{\prime\prime}
as the von Neumann algebra generated by a direct sum of disjoint representations is the direct sum of the corresponding von Neumann algebras
[
BdlH20
, Proposition 6.B.4]
.
It follows that
\mathrm{P}\in\mathrm{Z}(M_{\varphi})
. Denote
\psi=\varphi_{\mathrm{P}}
so that
\psi\leq\varphi
. Note that the trace
\psi
is finite-dimensional.
∎

Restricting once again to the case where
\varphi
is a character, we get the following useful characterization.

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
\varphi\in\mathrm{Ch}\left(\Gamma\right)
be a character. Then
\varphi
is finite-dimensional if and only if the representation
\pi_{\varphi}\otimes\pi^{*}_{\varphi}
has invariant vectors.

Amenable traces

A trace
\varphi\in\mathrm{Tr}\left(\Gamma\right)
is called
amenable
if the corresponding unitary representation
\pi_{\varphi}
is amenable in the sense of Bekka
[
Bek90
]
, namely the tensor product representation
\pi_{\varphi}\otimes\pi^{*}_{\varphi}
admits almost-invariant vectors. This is equivalent to saying that the norm of the positive contracting operator
(\pi_{\varphi}\otimes\pi_{\varphi}^{*})(a)
is
1
for some choice of an element
a\in\ell^{1}(\Gamma)
such that the support of the element
a^{*}a
generates the group
\Gamma

[
BdLHV08
, G.4.2]
. A non-amenable trace cannot dominate any finite-dimensional trace, see Lemma
2.3
. In particular, a non-amenable character is infinite-dimensional.

3.
The conjugation representation

In this section, we turn our attention to the conjugation representation associated to a trace. This representation will be helpful in determining whether the trace in question is in fact a character.

Let
\Gamma
be a countable discrete group and let
\varphi\in\mathrm{Tr}\left(\Gamma\right)
be a trace on
\Gamma
.
Consider the unitary representation
c_{\varphi}:\Gamma\to\mathrm{U}(\mathcal{H}_{\varphi})
given by

(3.1)

c_{\varphi}(\gamma)=\pi_{\varphi}(\gamma)\rho_{\varphi}(\gamma)=\rho_{\varphi}(\gamma)\pi_{\varphi}(\gamma)\quad\forall\gamma\in\Gamma.

Note that the representation
c_{\varphi}
is the restriction of the representation

(3.2)

\pi_{\varphi}\times\rho_{\varphi}:\Gamma\times\Gamma\to\mathrm{U}(\mathcal{H}_{\varphi})

to the diagonal subgroup
\Gamma\cong\Delta\Gamma\leq\Gamma\times\Gamma
.

Let
M_{\varphi}
be the von Neumann algebra associated to the trace
\varphi
. Consider the linear map

(3.3)

\iota_{\varphi}:M_{\varphi}\to\mathcal{H}_{\varphi},\quad\iota:x\mapsto xv_{\varphi}\quad\forall x\in M_{\varphi}.

The fact that the vector
v_{\varphi}
is cyclic for the representation
\pi_{\varphi}
implies that the image of
\iota_{\varphi}
is dense. Likewise, the fact that the vector
v_{\varphi}
is cyclic for the representation
\rho_{\varphi}
combined with the fact that
\rho_{\varphi}(\Gamma)\subset M^{\prime}_{\varphi}
implies that
\iota_{\varphi}
is injective. The map

\iota_{\varphi}
intertwines the adjoint representation of the group
\Gamma
on the algebra
M_{\varphi}
with the representation
c_{\varphi}
on the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\varphi}
, namely

(3.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\iota_{\varphi}(\mathrm{Ad}_{\gamma}(x))&=\mathrm{Ad}_{\gamma}(x)v_{\varphi}=\left(\pi_{\varphi}(\gamma)x\pi_{\varphi}(\gamma^{-1})\right)v_{\varphi}=\pi_{\varphi}(\gamma)x\rho_{\varphi}(\gamma)v_{\varphi}=\\
&=\pi_{\varphi}(\gamma)\rho_{\varphi}(\gamma)xv_{\varphi}=c_{\varphi}(\gamma)xv_{\varphi}=c_{\varphi}(\gamma)\iota_{\varphi}(x)\end{split}

for all group elements
\gamma\in\Gamma
and all
x\in M_{\varphi}
.
If follows that the image of
\iota_{\varphi}
is a
c_{\varphi}
-invariant subspace of
\mathcal{H}_{\varphi}
and that the center
\mathrm{Z}(M_{\varphi})
is mapped injectively into the subspace of
c_{\varphi}
-invariant vectors
\mathcal{H}_{\varphi}^{c_{\varphi}}
.

The main goal of the current section is the following result providing information about the image of
the linear map
\iota_{\varphi}
.

Proposition 3.1
.

The embedding
\iota_{\varphi}:\mathrm{Z}(M_{\varphi})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{H}_{\varphi}^{c_{\varphi}}
has dense image.

The proof of Proposition
3.1
will be given below. As was already mentioned, the trace
\varphi
is a character if and only if the center
\mathrm{Z}(M_{\varphi})
of the von Neumann algebra
M_{\varphi}
is one-dimensional. Therefore Proposition
3.1
immediately implies the following fact. While this is known to experts, we are not aware of any straightforward reference.

Corollary 3.2
.

The trace
\varphi
is a character if and only if
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{H}_{\varphi}^{c_{\varphi}}=1
.

The condition
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{H}_{\varphi}^{c_{\varphi}}=1
is equivalent to saying that
\mathcal{H}_{\varphi}^{c_{\varphi}}=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{C}}v_{\varphi}
. We generalize this statement to the relative setting in §
7
.

Dense linear subspaces of Hilbert spaces

The proof of Proposition
3.1
will rely on the following general lemma concerning Hilbert spaces.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
V
be a Hilbert space. Let
C
be a closed convex balanced
8
8

8

Recall that a subset
C
of a complex vector space is called
balanced
if
aC\subset C
for all scalars
a
with
|a|\leq 1
.
subset of
V
such that the subspace
A=\bigcup_{n}nC
is dense in
V
. Denote by
\Pi
the nearest point projection from
V
to
C
. Then
A\cap W
is dense in
W
for any closed subspace
W\leq V
satisfying
\Pi(W)\subset W
.

Proof.

Let
W
be any closed subspace of
V
satisfying
\Pi(W)\subset W
.
Consider any vector
v\in W\cap(A\cap W)^{\perp}
. We will show that necessarily
v=0
.

We start by showing that
\Pi(v)=0
. Note that the nearest point projection
\Pi(v)
satisfies

(3.5)

\Pi(v)\in C\cap W\subset A\cap W.

The properties of the nearest point projection
\Pi(v)
imply that for any other vector
w\in C
the angle at the point
\Pi(v)
between the two line segments
\left[\Pi(v),v\right]
and
\left[\Pi(v),w\right]
cannot be acute. In terms of the inner product this means that

(3.6)

\Re\left<v-\Pi(v),w-\Pi(v)\right>\leq 0\quad\forall w\in C.

Substituting
w=0
and recalling that
v\in(A\cap W)^{\perp}
while
\Pi(v)\in A\cap W
gives
\Re\left<\Pi(v),\Pi(v)\right>\leq 0
. Therefore
\Pi(v)=0
. As Hilbert spaces are strictly convex, we conclude that

(3.7)

\|v\|=\|v-\Pi(v)\|<\|v-w\|

for all vectors
w\in C
with
w\neq\Pi(v)=0
.

The linear subspace
A
is dense in the Hilbert space
V
by assumption. Therefore there is a sequence of vectors
u_{n}\in nC
converging to
v
. Denote
x_{n}=\frac{1}{n}u_{n}
so that
x_{n}\in C
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
x_{n}\to 0
.
As
u_{n}\to v
we have that
\left<u_{n},v\right>\to\left<v,v\right>
as well as
\left<u_{n},u_{n}\right>\to\left<v,v\right>
. Assume towards contradiction that
v\neq 0
. In that case

(3.8)

\frac{2}{n}\Re\left<u_{n},v\right>>\frac{1}{n^{2}}\left<u_{n},u_{n}\right>

for all
n
sufficiently large.
On the other hand, for all
n
we have that

(3.9)

\left<v-x_{n},v-x_{n}\right>=\left<v,v\right>-\frac{2}{n}\Re\left<u_{n},v\right>+\frac{1}{n^{2}}\left<u_{n},u_{n}\right>.

Putting together the two Equations (
3.8
) and (
3.9
) implies that

\|v\|>\|v-x_{n}\|
for all
n
sufficiently large.
It follows from the discussion involving Equation (
3.7
) that
x_{n}=u_{n}=0
for all
n
sufficiently large. As
u_{n}\to v
we arrive at a contradiction to the assumption
v\neq 0
and deduce that
v=0
as required.
∎

(M_{\varphi})_{1}

.

. We let

4.
Traces and spectral gap

Let
\Gamma
be a countable discrete group and
\varphi\in\mathrm{Tr}\left(\Gamma\right)
be a fixed trace with associated GNS data
(\pi_{\varphi},\rho_{\varphi},\mathcal{H}_{\varphi},v_{\varphi})
. In addition, consider the unitary conjugation representation
c_{\varphi}:\Gamma\to\mathrm{U}(\mathcal{H}_{\varphi})
introduced in §
3
.
In this key section, we show that spectral gap for any of the representations
\pi_{\varphi},\pi_{\varphi}\otimes\pi^{*}_{\varphi}
and
c_{\varphi}
can be expressed in terms of inequalities involving elements of the group algebra
\mathbb{C}\left[\Gamma\right]
. The exact formulas will not be crucial. Rather, their existence demonstrates that spectral gap associated to traces is preserved when taking limits. This concept resonates with Kesten’s theorem, though our analysis demands a more detailed approach.

Fix an arbitrary element
a\in\ell^{1}(\Gamma)
. Typically the element
a
will be chosen so that its support generates the group
\Gamma
, but this is not necessary at this point. Set
b=a^{*}a\in\ell^{1}(\Gamma)
and denote

(4.1)

b=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}b_{\gamma}\gamma

with the coefficients
b_{\gamma}\in\mathbb{C}
for each
\gamma\in\Gamma
.
We will use these notations throughout this section without further mention.

In what follows we will repeatedly make use of the formula

(4.2)

\|\pi_{\varphi}(x)v_{\varphi}\|^{2}=\left<\pi_{\varphi}(x)v_{\varphi},\pi_{\varphi}(x)v_{\varphi}\right>=\left<\pi_{\varphi}(x^{*}x)v_{\varphi},v_{\varphi}\right>=\varphi(x^{*}x)

holding true for all elements
x\in\ell^{1}(\Gamma)
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Fix
\beta\geq 0
. Then

\|\pi_{\varphi}(a)\|\leq\beta^{\frac{1}{2}}
if and only if

(4.3)

\varphi(x^{*}(b-\beta)x)\leq 0

for every element
x\in\mathbb{C}\left[\Gamma\right]
.

Proof.

Since the collection of vectors
\pi_{\varphi}(x)v_{\varphi}
with
x\in\mathbb{C}\left[\Gamma\right]
is dense in the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\varphi}
, it suffices to estimate the norm of the operator
\pi_{\varphi}(a)
on this subset. In particular, the estimate
\|\pi_{\varphi}(a)\|\leq\beta^{\frac{1}{2}}
is equivalent to the inequality

(4.4)

\|\pi_{\varphi}(a)\pi_{\varphi}(x)v_{\varphi}\|\leq\beta^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\pi_{\varphi}(x)v_{\varphi}\|

holding true for every
x\in\mathbb{C}\left[\Gamma\right]
. The formula in Equation (
4.2
) shows that Equation (
4.4
) is equivalent for each individual element
x\in\mathbb{C}\left[\Gamma\right]
to the inequality

(4.5)

\varphi(x^{*}bx)=\varphi((ax)^{*}(ax))\leq\beta\varphi(x^{*}x).

The desired formula in Equation (
4.3
) follows by rearranging Equation (
4.5
).
∎

The next estimate is concerned with the conjugation representation
c_{\varphi}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Fix
\beta\geq 0
. Then

(4.6)

\|c_{\varphi}(a)_{|\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{C}}(v_{\varphi})^{\perp}}\|\leq\beta^{\frac{1}{2}}

if and only if the inequality

(4.7)

\varphi\left(\sum_{\gamma}b_{\gamma}\gamma^{*}x^{*}\gamma x-\beta x^{*}x\right)\leq(1-\beta)|\varphi(x)|^{2}

holds true for every element
x\in\mathbb{C}\left[\Gamma\right]

Proof.

The collection of vectors of the form
\pi_{\varphi}(x)v_{\varphi}
for some element
x\in\mathbb{C}\left[\Gamma\right]
forms a dense subset of the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\varphi}
. Therefore, to estimate the norm of the operator
c_{\varphi}(a)
it suffices to restrict attention to this subset. The operator norm bound
\|c_{\varphi}(a)_{|\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{C}}(v_{\varphi})^{\perp}}\|\leq\beta^{\frac{1}{2}}
is equivalent to the inequality

(4.8)

\beta\|w\|^{2}-\|c_{\varphi}(a)w\|^{2}\geq(\beta-1)|\left<w,v_{\varphi}\right>|^{2}

for all vectors of the form
w=\pi_{\varphi}(x)v_{\varphi}
for some
x\in\mathbb{C}\left[\Gamma\right]
, see the auxiliary Lemma
4.5
. Let us estimate each one of the three terms appearing in Equation (
4.8
). First

(4.9)

\|w\|^{2}=\|\pi_{\varphi}(x)v_{\varphi}\|^{2}=\varphi(x^{*}x)

according to Equation (
4.2
). Next we compute
\|c_{\varphi}(a)w\|^{2}
. Observe that

(4.10)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\langle c_{\varphi}(\gamma)w,w\right\rangle&=\left\langle c_{\varphi}(\gamma)\pi_{\varphi}(x)v_{\varphi},\pi_{\varphi}(x)v_{\varphi}\right\rangle=\left\langle\pi_{\varphi}(\gamma)\rho_{\varphi}(\gamma)\pi_{\varphi}(x)v_{\varphi},\pi_{\varphi}(x)v_{\varphi}\right\rangle=\\
&=\left\langle\pi_{\varphi}(x^{*})\pi_{\varphi}(\gamma)\pi_{\varphi}(x)\rho_{\varphi}(\gamma)v_{\varphi},v_{\varphi}\right\rangle=\varphi\left(\gamma^{*}x^{*}\gamma x\right)\end{split}

for all elements
\gamma\in\Gamma
.
We have used the formula
\rho_{\varphi}(z)v_{\varphi}=\pi_{\varphi}(z^{*})v_{\varphi}
as well as the trace property
\tau(zz^{\prime})=\tau(z^{\prime}z)
which hold true for all elements
z,z^{\prime}\in M_{\varphi}
. Therefore we get

(4.11)

\|c_{\varphi}(a)w\|^{2}=\left\langle c_{\varphi}(b)w,w\right\rangle=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\left\langle b_{\gamma}c_{\varphi}(\gamma)w,w\right\rangle=\varphi\left(\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}b_{\gamma}\gamma^{*}x^{*}\gamma x\right).

Lastly the inner product
\left<w,v_{\varphi}\right>
is simply given by

(4.12)

\left<w,v_{\varphi}\right>=\left<\pi_{\varphi}(x)v_{\varphi},v_{\varphi}\right>=\varphi(x).

The desired formula in Equation (
4.7
) follows by combining these three computations.
∎

The following two results are extensions of Lemma
4.1
to the tensor product representation
\pi_{\varphi}\otimes\pi^{*}_{\varphi}
. We identify
\pi_{\varphi}^{*}
with the unitary representation associated to the conjugate trace
\overline{\varphi}\in\mathrm{Tr}\left(\Gamma\right)
admitting the GNS data
(\pi_{\varphi}^{*},\rho_{\varphi}^{*},\mathcal{H}^{*}_{\varphi},v^{*}_{\varphi})
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Assume that the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\varphi}
is infinite-dimensional. Fix
\beta\geq 0
. Then

(4.13)

\|(\pi_{\varphi}\otimes\pi_{\varphi}^{*})(a)\|\leq\beta^{\frac{1}{2}}

if and only if

(4.14)

\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}\left(\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}b_{\gamma}\varphi(x_{j}^{*}\gamma x_{i})\overline{\varphi}\left(y_{j}^{*}\gamma y_{i}\right)-\beta\varphi(x_{j}^{*}x_{i})\overline{\varphi}(y_{j}^{*}y_{i})\right)\leq 0

for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
and every
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\in\mathbb{C}\left[\Gamma\right]
and
y_{1},\ldots,y_{k}\in\mathbb{C}\left[\Gamma\right]
.

Proof.

The collection of all the vectors of the form

(4.15)

w=w^{k}_{x_{i},y_{i}}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\pi_{\varphi}(x_{i})v_{\varphi}\otimes\pi^{*}_{\varphi}(y_{i})v^{*}_{\varphi}

for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
and some
x_{i},y_{i}\in\mathbb{C}\left[\Gamma\right]
is a dense subset of the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\varphi}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\varphi}^{*}\cong\mathrm{HS}(\mathcal{H}_{\varphi})
. Therefore, the operator norm bound
\|(\pi_{\varphi}\otimes\pi_{\varphi}^{*})(a)\|^{2}\leq\beta
is equivalent to the inequality

(4.16)

\|(\pi_{\varphi}\otimes\pi_{\varphi}^{*})(a)w\|^{2}\leq\beta\|w\|^{2}

holding true for every vector
w=w^{k}_{x_{i},y_{i}}
as in Equation (
4.15
) above.
It is possible to rewrite the norm of the vector
w
in terms of the trace
\varphi
, namely

(4.17)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|w\|^{2}=\langle w,w\rangle&=\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}\left<\pi_{\varphi}(x_{i})v_{\varphi}\otimes\pi^{*}_{\varphi}(y_{i})v^{*}_{\varphi},\pi_{\varphi}(x_{j})v_{\varphi}\otimes\pi^{*}_{\varphi}(y_{j})v^{*}_{\varphi}\right>\\
&=\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}\left<\pi_{\varphi}(x_{i})v_{\varphi},\pi_{\varphi}(x_{j})v_{\varphi}\right>\cdot\left<\pi^{*}_{\varphi}(y_{i})v^{*}_{\varphi},\pi^{*}_{\varphi}(y_{j})v^{*}_{\varphi}\right>\\
&=\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}\varphi(x_{j}^{*}x_{i})\overline{\varphi}(y_{j}^{*}y_{i}).\end{split}

The next step is to estimate the norm
\|(\pi_{\varphi}\otimes\pi_{\varphi}^{*})(a)w\|^{2}
. First, note that for each element
\gamma\in\Gamma
individually we have

(4.18)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\langle\pi_{\varphi}\otimes\pi_{\varphi}^{*}(\gamma)w,w\right\rangle&=\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}\left\langle\pi_{\varphi}(\gamma)\pi_{\varphi}(x_{i})v_{\varphi},\pi_{\varphi}(x_{j})v_{\varphi}\right\rangle\cdot\left\langle\pi_{\varphi}^{*}(\gamma)\pi_{\varphi}^{*}\left(y_{i}\right)v_{\varphi}^{*},\pi_{\varphi}^{*}(y_{j})v_{\varphi}^{*}\right\rangle\\
&=\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}\left\langle\pi_{\varphi}(x_{j}^{*}\gamma x_{i})v_{\varphi},v_{\varphi}\right\rangle\cdot\left\langle\pi_{\varphi}^{*}\left(y_{j}^{*}\gamma y_{i}\right)v_{\varphi}^{*},v_{\varphi}^{*}\right\rangle\\
&=\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}\varphi(x_{j}^{*}\gamma x_{i})\bar{\varphi}\left(y_{j}^{*}\gamma y_{i}\right).\end{split}

Using the fact that
b=a^{*}a
and summing Equation (
4.18
) over all
\gamma
’s we get

(4.19)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\pi_{\varphi}\otimes\pi_{\varphi}^{*}(a)w\|^{2}=\left\langle\pi_{\varphi}\otimes\pi_{\varphi}^{*}(b)w,w\right\rangle&=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}b_{\gamma}\left\langle\pi_{\varphi}\otimes\pi_{\varphi}^{*}(\gamma)w,w\right\rangle\\
&=\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}b_{\gamma}\varphi(x_{j}^{*}\gamma x_{i})\overline{\varphi}\left(y_{j}^{*}\gamma y_{i}\right).\end{split}

The desired Equation (
4.16
) follows from the two Equations (
4.18
) and (
4.19
) by rearranging the terms.
∎

We now consider the situation when unitary representation
\pi_{\varphi}
is finite-dimensional. In this case, we only obtain a one-way implication.

Lemma 4.4
.

Assume that the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\varphi}
is
finite-dimensional so that
\mathrm{Id}\in\mathrm{HS}(\mathcal{H}_{\varphi})
. Fix
\beta\geq 0
. If

(4.20)

\|(\pi_{\varphi}\otimes\pi_{\varphi}^{*})(a)_{|\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathrm{Id})^{\perp}}\|\leq\beta^{\frac{1}{2}}

then

(4.21)

\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}b_{\gamma}\varphi(x_{j}^{*}\gamma x_{i})\overline{\varphi}\left(y_{j}^{*}\gamma y_{i}\right)-\beta\varphi(x_{j}^{*}x_{i})\overline{\varphi}(y_{j}^{*}y_{i})\leq(1-\beta)\frac{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\|x_{i}\|_{1}\|y_{i}\|_{1}\right)^{2}}{(\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{H}_{\varphi})^{3}}

for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
and every
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\in\mathbb{C}\left[\Gamma\right]
and
y_{1},\ldots,y_{k}\in\mathbb{C}\left[\Gamma\right]
.

Proof.

The operator norm bound in Equation (
4.20
) is equivalent to the inequality

(4.22)

\|(\pi_{\varphi}\otimes\pi_{\varphi}^{*})(a)w\|^{2}-\beta\|w\|^{2}\leq\frac{(1-\beta)}{\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{H}_{\varphi}}|\left<w,\mathrm{Id}\right>|^{2}

holding true for any vector
w\in\mathrm{HS}(\mathcal{H}_{\varphi})
of the form defined in Equation (
4.15
), see the auxiliary Lemma
4.5
below. We have obtained formulas for the norms
\|w\|^{2}
as well as
\|(\pi_{\varphi}\otimes\pi^{*}_{\varphi})(a)w\|^{2}
in terms of the trace
\varphi
, see Equations (
4.17
) and (
4.18
).

To conclude the proof it remains to estimate the inner product
\left<w,\mathrm{Id}\right>
.
Recall that the inner product on the Hilbert–Schmidt space
\mathrm{HS}(\mathcal{H}_{\varphi})
is given explicitly by

(4.23)

\langle T,S\rangle=\frac{1}{\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{H}_{\varphi}}\mathrm{tr}(S^{*}T)

for each pair of operators
T,S\in\mathrm{HS}(\mathcal{H}_{\varphi})
. In particular
\|\mathrm{Id}\|^{2}=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{H}_{\varphi}
.

First consider the special case
k=1,x=\delta_{\gamma_{1}}
and
y=\delta_{\gamma_{2}}
where
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\Gamma
are arbitrary elements so that
w=\pi_{\varphi}(\gamma_{1})v_{\varphi}\otimes\pi^{*}_{\varphi}(\gamma_{2})v^{*}_{\varphi}
has rank
1
as an operator on
\mathcal{H}_{\varphi}
. In that case

(4.24)

|\left<w,\mathrm{Id}\right>|=|\left<\pi_{\varphi}(\gamma_{1})v_{\varphi}\otimes\pi^{*}_{\varphi}(\gamma_{2})v^{*}_{\varphi},\mathrm{Id}\right>|\leq\frac{1}{\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{H}_{\varphi}}.

In the general case, relying on the triangle inequality we get

(4.25)

|\left<w,\mathrm{Id}\right>|\leq\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{k}\|x_{i}\|_{1}\|y_{i}\|_{1}}{\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{H}_{\varphi}}.

Substituting this Equation (
4.25
) in the previous Equation (
4.22
), we get the desired inequality in Equation (

).
∎

The following auxiliary computation was used twice in the above proofs.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
A
be a bounded linear operator acting on the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. Let
v\in\mathcal{H}
be an
A
-invariant
unit
vector. Fix some
0<\beta<1
. Then

(4.26)

\|A_{|\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{C}}(v)^{\perp}}\|\leq\beta^{\frac{1}{2}}

if and only if

(4.27)

\beta\|w\|^{2}-\|Aw\|^{2}\geq(\beta-1)|\left<w,v\right>|^{2}

for all vectors
w\in\mathcal{H}
.

Proof.

Denote
\mathcal{H}_{0}=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{v\}
. Consider the
A
-invariant direct sum decomposition
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{0}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{1}
where
\mathcal{H}_{1}=\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\perp}
. Any vector

can be written uniquely as
w=w_{0}+w_{1}
with
w_{0}=\left<w,v\right>v\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
and
w_{1}=w-w_{0}\in\mathcal{H}_{1}
.

To begin with, assume that Equation (
4.27
) holds for all vectors
w\in\mathcal{H}
.
Note that every vector
w\in\mathcal{H}_{1}
satisfies
\left<w,v\right>=0
. In particular Equation (
4.27
) reads
\|Aw\|^{2}\leq\beta\|w\|^{2}
with respect to any such vector, as required.

Conversely, assume that
\|A_{|\mathcal{H}_{1}}\|\leq\beta^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Therefore any vector

satisfies

(4.28)

\|Aw\|^{2}=\|Aw_{0}\|^{2}+\|Aw_{1}\|^{2}\leq\|w_{0}\|^{2}+\beta\|w_{1}\|^{2}=(1-\beta)\|w_{0}\|^{2}+\beta\|w\|^{2}.

The desired Equation (
4.27
) follows from Equation (

) noting that
\|w_{0}\|=|\langle w,v\rangle|
.
∎

5.
Spectral gap and convergence of traces

Let
\Gamma
be a countable discrete group.
We study the behaviour of various spectral gap properties with respect to taking limits of sequences of traces on the group
\Gamma
.

Let
\mathcal{R}
be a set of equivalence classes of unitary representations of
\Gamma
. For each unitary representation
\pi\in\mathcal{R}
let
\pi_{0}
denote its sub-representation obtained by taking the orthogonal complement to the subspace of invariant vectors.

Definition
.

The group
\Gamma
has
property
\mathrm{T}(\mathcal{R})
if the trivial representation
1\in\widehat{\Gamma}
is an isolated point of the set

(5.1)

\{\pi_{0}\>:\>\pi\in\mathcal{R}\}\cup\{1\}

with respect to the Fell topology.

Property
\mathrm{T}(\mathcal{R})
for the group
\Gamma
is equivalent to the following condition:

•

If
\rho
is a unitary representation with
1\leq\rho
then
\rho\notin\overline{\{\pi_{0}\>:\>\pi\in\mathcal{R}\}}
with respect to the Fell topology.

In the special case where every representation

is irreducible, i.e. the set of representations

is contained in the unitary dual
\widehat{\Gamma}
, the above definition of property
\mathrm{T}(\mathcal{R})
coincides with the one given by Lubotzky and Zimmer in
[
LZ89
, Definition 1.1]
. For our purposes it will be more convenient to allow for reducible representations as well.

Here are some well known examples of property
\mathrm{T}(\mathcal{R})
.

•

Let
\mathrm{Rep}_{\aleph_{0}}(\Gamma)
be the set of equivalence classes of all separable unitary representations of the countable group
\Gamma
. Property
\mathrm{T}(\mathrm{Rep}_{\aleph_{0}}(\Gamma))
is the same as Kazhdan’s property (T).

•

Let
\mathrm{FD}
be the family of all equivalence classes of finite-dimensional unitary representations of the group
\Gamma
. Property
\mathrm{T}(\mathrm{FD})
has been considered in the literature
[
LZ89
]
.

•

Property
(\tau)
can be regarded as a special case of property
\mathrm{T}(\mathcal{R})
for a particular choice of the set
\mathcal{R}
. Indeed, given a family
\mathcal{N}
of normal subgroups of the group
\Gamma
we denote by
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{N})
the set of all unitary representation factoring through some normal subgroup in
\mathcal{N}
.
Property
(\tau)
is the same as property
\mathrm{T}(\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{F}))
where
\mathcal{F}
is the family of all finite index normal subgroups of the group
\Gamma
. See
[
LZ05
]
for more information.

•

In the special case where
\Gamma
is an arithmetic group,
Selberg’s property
is the same as property
\mathrm{T}(\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{C}))
where
\mathcal{C}
is the family of all congruence subgroups
[
Clo03
]
.

Definition
.

The set
\mathcal{R}
of equivalence classes of unitary representations is called
hereditary
if

(1)

\mathcal{R}
is
subrepresentation-closed
: if
\rho\in\mathcal{R}
and
\pi\leq\rho
then
\pi\in\mathcal{R}
,

(2)

\mathcal{R}
is
dual-closed
: if
\rho\in\mathcal{R}
then the dual representation satisfies

,

(3)

\mathcal{R}
is
diagonal-closed
: Let
\Pi
be a representation of the product group

whose restriction to each factor is contained in

. Then the restriction of
\Pi
to the diagonal embedding of
\Gamma
is also contained in
\mathcal{R}
.

The set of finite-dimensional representations

is hereditary. The set of unitary representations

associated to the family

of normal subgroups is hereditary provided that

is closed under taking finite intersections.

Convergence of traces

Let
\mathcal{R}
be a fixed
hereditary
set of equivalence classes of unitary representations of the group
\Gamma
. Denote

(5.2)

\mathrm{Ch}\left(\Gamma;\mathcal{R}\right)=\{\varphi\in\mathrm{Ch}\left(\Gamma\right)\>:\>\pi_{\varphi}\in\mathcal{R}\}.

Note that the dual-closed and diagonal-closed conditions (which are part of the notion of a hereditary set) imply that any character
\varphi\in\mathrm{Ch}\left(\Gamma;\mathcal{R}\right)
has in addition
\pi_{\varphi}\otimes\pi_{\varphi}^{*}\in\mathcal{R}
as well as
c_{\varphi}\in\mathcal{R}
.

Fix an arbitrary element
a\in\ell^{1}(\Gamma)
satisfying
\|a\|_{1}=1
and such that the support of
a
generates the group
\Gamma
. Note that the group
\Gamma
has property
\mathrm{T}(\mathcal{R})
if and only if there is some
0<\beta<1
such that
\|\pi_{0}(a)\|\leq\beta
for all
\pi\in\mathcal{R}
.

Proposition 5.1
.

If the group
\Gamma
has property
\mathrm{T}(\mathcal{R})
then
\overline{\mathrm{Ch}\left(\Gamma;\mathcal{R}\right)}\subset\mathrm{Ch}\left(\Gamma\right)
.

Proof.

Consider the family of the conjugation representations
c_{\varphi}
corresponding to the characters
\varphi\in\mathrm{Ch}\left(\Gamma;\mathcal{R}\right)
. The subspace of
c_{\varphi}
-invariant vectors for each such representation
c_{\varphi}
is one-dimensional and is spanned by the cyclic vector
v_{\varphi}\in\mathcal{H}_{\varphi}
, see Corollary
3.2
. The property
\mathrm{T}(\mathcal{R})
assumption implies that there is a fixed constant
0<\beta<1
(depending on the chosen element
a\in\ell^{1}(\Gamma)
) such that operator norm bound
\|c_{\varphi}(a)_{|\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{C}}(v_{\varphi})^{\perp}}\|\leq\beta
holds true for all characters
\varphi\in\mathrm{Ch}\left(\Gamma;\mathcal{R}\right)
. The same operator norm bound holds true for any given limiting trace
\psi\in\overline{\mathrm{Ch}\left(\Gamma;\mathcal{R}\right)}
by Proposition
4.2
. In particular, the subspace of invariant vectors in the unitary representation
c_{\psi}
corresponding to the trace
\psi
is spanned by the cyclic vector
v_{\psi}
. We conclude that
\psi\in\mathrm{Ch}\left(\Gamma\right)
by the converse direction of Corollary
3.2
.
∎

Proposition 5.2
.

If the group
\Gamma
has property
\mathrm{T}(\mathcal{R})
then the trivial character
1\in\mathrm{Ch}\left(\Gamma\right)
is an isolated point of the set
\mathrm{Ch}\left(\Gamma;\mathcal{R}\right)\cup\{1\}
.

Proof.

Consider the family of the unitary
representations
\pi_{\varphi}
corresponding to the characters
\varphi\in\mathrm{Ch}\left(\Gamma;\mathcal{R}\right)\setminus\{1\}
. The representations
\pi_{\varphi}
admit no invariant vectors, see Corollary
2.2
. The property
\mathrm{T}(\mathcal{R})
assumption implies that there is a fixed constant
0<\beta<1
(depending on the chosen element
a\in\ell^{1}(\Gamma)
) such that the operator norm bound
\|\pi_{\varphi}(a)\|\leq\beta
holds true for all characters
\varphi\in\mathrm{Ch}\left(\Gamma;\mathcal{R}\right)\setminus\{1\}
. The same operator norm bound holds true for any given limiting trace
\psi\in\overline{\mathrm{Ch}\left(\Gamma;\mathcal{R}\right)\setminus\{1\}}
by Lemma
4.1
. In particular, the unitary representation
\pi_{\psi}
corresponding to the trace
\psi
admits no invariant vectors. Moreover
\psi\in\mathrm{Ch}\left(\Gamma\right)

. We conclude that

by the converse direction of Corollary
2.2
.
∎

Recall the notion of
amenable characters
defined in the last paragraph of §
2
.

Proposition 5.3
.

Assume that the group
\Gamma
has property
\mathrm{T}(\mathcal{R})
. Let
\varphi_{n}\in\mathrm{Ch}\left(\Gamma;\mathcal{R}\right)
be any sequence of characters converging to some
\varphi\in\mathrm{Tr}\left(\Gamma\right)
. Assume that the characters
\varphi_{n}
are either finite-dimensional and satisfy
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{H}_{\varphi_{n}}\to\infty
or are infinite-dimensional. Then the character

is non-amenable.

Proof.

First, note that
\varphi\in\mathrm{Ch}\left(\Gamma\right)
by Proposition
5.1
. Second, the representation
\pi_{\varphi}
must be infinite dimensional by Proposition
5.7
below. The property
\mathrm{T}(\mathcal{R})
assumption implies that there is a fixed constant
0<\beta<1
(depending on the chosen element

(1)

if the characters

are infinite-dimensional then the operator norm bound
\|\pi_{\varphi_{n}}\otimes\pi_{\varphi_{n}}^{*}(a)\|\leq\beta
holds true for each

, and

(2)

if the characters
\varphi_{n}
are finite-dimensional then the operator norm bound

We may apply Lemma

in the first case, or Lemma
4.3
in tandem with Lemma

in the second case, to deduce that the operator norm bound passes to the limiting character

Faces and Bauer simplices

Assume that the countable group
\Gamma
has property
\mathrm{T}(\mathcal{R})
. Then
\overline{\mathrm{Ch}\left(\Gamma;\mathcal{R}\right)}\subset\mathrm{Ch}\left(\Gamma\right)
by Proposition
5.1
. To complete the proof of Theorem
1.5
of the introduction we will require the following abstract observation.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
S
be a metrizable Choquet simplex.
If
X
is a compact subset of the set of extreme points
\partial S
then
\overline{\mathrm{conv}\left(X\right)}

is a face of the simplex
S
and is a Bauer simplex.

Proof.

Denote
C=\overline{\mathrm{conv}\left(X\right)}
so that
C
is closed and convex. Note that

(5.3)

X\subseteq C\cap\partial S\subseteq\partial C\subseteq\overline{X}.

The rightmost inclusion in Equation (
5.3
) follows from Milman’s converse to the Krein–Milman theorem
[
Mil47
]
.
Since
X
is compact
X=\overline{X}
and therefore equality must hold throughout. In particular
\partial C=X
.

We claim that the subset
C
is a face of the simplex
S
. Suppose that
\mu
is a probability
measure with
\mathrm{supp}(\mu)\subset\partial S
such that its barycenter
\mathrm{bar}(\mu)=c
satisfies
c\in C
. By Choquet’s
theorem applied to the convex set
C
there exists a probability measure
\nu
with
\mathrm{supp}(\nu)\subset\partial C=X
whose barycenter is
\mathrm{bar}(\nu)=c
. Since
S
is a Choquet simplex it follows that
\mu=\nu
. In particular the measure
\mu
is supported on
\partial C
so that
C
is a face by definition.

To see that the convex set
C
is a Choquet simplex note that as
\partial C\subset\partial S
any two probability measures
supported on
\partial C
are in particular
supported on
\partial S
. Therefore if these two measures have the same barycenter in
C
they must coincide. Finally the simplex
C
is Bauer since
\partial C=X
is compact.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

We have already established that
\overline{\mathrm{Ch}\left(\Gamma;\mathcal{R}\right)}\subset\mathrm{Ch}\left(\Gamma\right)
in Proposition

. The second part of the theorem saying that

is a closed face of the simplex

and is a Bauer simplex follows abstractly by applying Lemma

with respect to the subset

of the Choquet simplex

Finite-dimensional representations

Recall that
\Gamma
is a countable group and
\mathcal{R}
is a hereditary set of equivalence classes of unitary representations of the group
\Gamma
.
We establish a finiteness result for finite-dimensional characters in the presence of property
\mathrm{T}(\mathcal{R})
, see Corollary
5.8
.

Lemma 5.5
.

Assume that the countable group
\Gamma
has property
\mathrm{T}(\mathcal{R})
. Let
\rho\in\overline{\mathcal{R}}
be a finite-dimensional unitary representation of the group
\Gamma
. Then

(1)

\rho\in\overline{\{\pi\in\mathcal{R}\>:\>\text{$\pi$ has a non-trivial finite-dimensional subrepresentation}\}}
.

(2)

If the representation
\rho
is irreducible and
\rho\in\overline{\mathcal{R}\cap\widehat{\Gamma}}
then
\rho\in\mathcal{R}
and
\rho
is an isolated point of the subset
\overline{\mathcal{R}\cap\widehat{\Gamma}}
in the Fell topology.

Proof.

Let
\rho\in\overline{\mathcal{R}}
be a finite-dimensional unitary representation. Consider a sequence
\pi_{n}\in\mathcal{R}
of unitary representations such that
\pi_{n}\to\rho
in the Fell topology. Therefore
\rho\otimes\pi_{n}^{*}\to\rho\otimes\rho^{*}
in the Fell topology.
As the unitary representation
\rho
is finite-dimensional it satisfies
1\leq\rho\otimes\rho^{*}
. In particular
\rho\otimes\pi_{n}^{*}\to 1
in the Fell topology. It follows that there is a subsequence
m_{n}
such that
\pi_{m_{n}}\otimes\pi^{*}_{n}\to 1
in the Fell topology. Note that
\pi_{m_{n}}\otimes\pi_{n}^{*}\in\mathcal{R}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
as the family
\mathcal{R}
is diagonal-closed. Property
\mathrm{T}(\mathcal{R})
implies that
1\leq\pi_{m_{n}}\otimes\pi^{*}_{n}
for all
n
sufficiently large.
We conclude that the unitary representation
\pi_{n}
admits a non-trivial finite-dimensional sub-representation for all
n
sufficiently large
[
BdLHV08
, Proposition A.1.12]
. Item (1) follows.

Assume in addition that the unitary representations
\pi_{n}
are all irreducible. This additional assumption implies by Schur’s lemma that
\pi_{n}
is finite-dimensional and that
\pi_{n}=\pi_{m_{n}}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. However, we may repeat the argument of the previous paragraph with respect to the subsequence
m^{\prime}_{n}=m_{n}+1
. This implies by Schur’s lemma that the sequence of unitary representations
\pi_{n}
stabilizes, namely there is some unitary representation
\pi\in\mathcal{R}
with
\pi_{n}=\pi
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
sufficiently large. The fact that the sequence
\pi_{n}
converges to the representation
\rho
in the Fell topology is therefore equivalent to saying that
\rho\prec\pi
.
Since the representation
\pi
is finite-dimensional and the representation
\rho
is irreducible, weak containment implies containment
[
BdLHV08
, Corollary F.2.9]
. Therefore
\rho\leq\pi
. As the representation
\pi
is irreducible we conclude that
\rho=\pi\in\mathcal{R}
and that
\rho
is an isolated point of the subset
\overline{\mathcal{R}\cap\widehat{\Gamma}}
in the Fell topology. Item (2) follows.
∎

Character limits of groups with property

We study groups enjoying various flavours of property

The information gathered in the previous section §

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a countable group and
N\lhd\Gamma
a normal subgroup. Let
\mathcal{R}
be a hereditary set
of equivalence classes of unitary representations of
\Gamma
. Assume

(1)

(spectral gap)
the group
\Gamma
has property
\mathrm{T}(\mathcal{R})
, and

(2)

(character dichotomy)
any character of the group
\Gamma

Let
\varphi_{n}\in\mathrm{Ch}\left(\Gamma;\mathcal{R}\right)
by any sequence of pairwise distinct characters. Then any accumulation point of the sequence
\varphi_{n}
is a character supported on the subgroup
N
. Namely

(6.1)

\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}\varphi_{n}(\gamma)=0\quad\forall\gamma\in\Gamma\setminus N.

Proof.

Recall that the
space
\mathrm{Tr}\left(\Gamma\right)
is compact. Let
\varphi\in\mathrm{Tr}\left(\Gamma\right)
be any accumulation point of the sequence
\varphi_{n}
. We know from Proposition
5.1
that
\overline{\mathrm{Ch}\left(\Gamma;\mathcal{R}\right)}\subset\mathrm{Ch}\left(\Gamma\right)
. In particular the accumulation point
\varphi
is a
character
of the group
\Gamma
.

The subset
\mathrm{Ch}\left(\Gamma;\mathcal{R}\right)
contains only finitely many distinct characters in each dimension, see Corollary
5.8
. We may assume up to passing to a subsequence either that the characters
\varphi_{n}

Convergence of representations

We prove the results stated in the introduction concerning convergence of representations in the Fell topology.

Definition
.

Let

•

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Let

[

Consider the situation where the group

The main point of the remainder of this section will be to obtain a similar conclusion assuming that the group

\alpha_{\varphi}
.

Proposition 7.2
.

Assume that
\mathrm{Inn}(\Gamma)\leq\Lambda
. Let
\varphi\in\mathrm{Tr}_{\Lambda}\left(\Gamma\right)
be a relative trace. Then
\varphi\in\mathrm{Ch}_{\Lambda}\left(\Gamma\right)
if and only if
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{H}_{\varphi}^{\alpha_{\varphi}}=1
.

Proof.

The same proof as that of Proposition
3.1
relying on Lemma
3.3
shows that the commutant
N_{\varphi}^{\prime}
is dense in the
Hilbert subspace
\mathcal{H}_{\varphi}^{\alpha_{\varphi}}
.
Note that any element
\lambda\in\Lambda
which acts on the group
\Gamma
via an inner automorphism of the form
\mathrm{Inn}(\gamma)
for some element
\gamma\in\Gamma
satisfies
\alpha_{\varphi}(\lambda)=c_{\varphi}(\gamma)
where
c_{\varphi}:\Gamma\to\mathrm{U}(\mathcal{H}_{\varphi})
is the conjugation representation studied in §
3
. The desired conclusion follows by using the aforementioned fact that the relative trace
\varphi
is a relative character if and only if
N^{\prime}_{\varphi}=\mathbb{C}\cdot\mathrm{Id}
.
∎

Assume that
\lambda\in\Lambda
is an element satisfying
\theta(\lambda)=\mathrm{Inn}(g)
for some element
\gamma\in\Gamma
. In this case we have already seen that
U_{\varphi}(\lambda)=\pi_{\varphi}(\gamma)
. As a result
\alpha_{\varphi}(\lambda)=c_{\varphi}(\gamma)
holds true.
Therefore Proposition
7.2
can be seen as a generalization to the relative setting of the statements in §
3
dealing with the conjugation representation.

Spectral gap for the representation
\alpha_{\varphi}

The action by automorphisms of the group
\Lambda
on the group
\Gamma
induces an action on its group ring
\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]
. We denote this action by
x\mapsto x^{\lambda}
for any element
\lambda\in\Lambda
and
x\in\mathbb{C}\left[\Gamma\right]
.

Fix an arbitrary element
a\in\ell^{1}(\Lambda)
with
\|a\|_{1}=1
whose support generates the group
\Lambda
.
Denote
b=a^{*}a
and write
b=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}b_{\lambda}\lambda
with coefficients
b_{\lambda}\in\mathbb{C}
.
We extend Lemma
4.2
to the relative setting.

Lemma 7.3
.

Fix
0<\beta<1
. Let
\varphi\in\mathrm{Tr}_{\Lambda}\left(\Gamma\right)
be a relative trace. Then

(7.9)

\|\alpha_{\varphi}(a)_{|\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{C}}(v_{\varphi})^{\perp}}\|\leq\beta^{\frac{1}{2}}

if and only if the inequality

(7.10)

\varphi\left(\sum_{\lambda}b_{\lambda}x^{*}x^{\lambda}-\beta x^{*}x\right)\leq(1-\beta)|\varphi(x)|^{2}

holds true for every element
x\in\mathbb{C}\left[\Gamma\right]
.

Proof.

Let
(\pi_{\varphi},\rho_{\varphi},\mathcal{H}_{\varphi},v_{\varphi})
be the GNS data associated to the relative trace
\varphi
. Consider a vector
w\in\mathcal{H}_{\varphi}
of the form
w=\pi_{\varphi}(x)v_{\varphi}
for some element
x\in\mathbb{C}\left[\Gamma\right]
. We obtain the estimate

(7.11)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\langle\alpha_{\varphi}(\lambda)w,w\right\rangle&=\left\langle\alpha_{\varphi}(\lambda)\pi_{\varphi}(x)v_{\varphi},\pi_{\varphi}(x)v_{\varphi}\right\rangle=\left\langle U_{\varphi}(\lambda)\pi_{\varphi}(x)U_{\lambda}^{-1}v_{\varphi},\pi_{\varphi}(x)v_{\varphi}\right\rangle=\\
&=\left\langle\pi_{\varphi}(x^{\lambda})v_{\varphi},\pi_{\varphi}(x)v_{\varphi}\right\rangle=\varphi\left(x^{*}x^{\lambda}\right)\end{split}

for every element
\lambda\in\Lambda
.
Note that the estimate in Equation
(
7.11
) is analogous to that of Equation (
4.10
) with the difference of using the unitary representation
\alpha_{\varphi}
of the group
\Lambda
instead of the conjugation representation
c_{\varphi}
of the group
\Gamma
.
In particular

(7.12)

\|\alpha_{\varphi}(a)w\|^{2}=\left\langle\alpha_{\varphi}(b)w,w\right\rangle=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\left\langle b_{\lambda}\alpha_{\varphi}(\lambda)w,w\right\rangle=\varphi\left(\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}b_{\lambda}x^{*}x^{\lambda}\right).

Once again, note that Equation (
7.12
) is analogous to Equation
4.11
up to replacing the conjugation representation
c_{\varphi}
with the representation
\alpha_{\varphi}
.
From this point onward the proof follows in exactly the same way as that of Lemma
4.2
.
∎

Remark 7.4
.

The only property of the conjugation representation
c_{\varphi}
and the representation
\alpha_{\varphi}
, which was used in Lemmas
4.2
and
7.3
respectively, to express the spectral gap via formulas of traces is the fact that the representation under consideration preserves the group algebra
\mathbb{C}[\Gamma]
sitting inside the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\varphi}
.

Finite-dimensional relative characters

The correspondence taking a relative trace
\varphi\in\mathrm{Tr}_{\Lambda}\left(\Gamma\right)
to the unitary representation
U_{\varphi}:\Lambda\to\mathrm{U}(\mathcal{H}_{\varphi})
is continuous with respect to the pointwise convergence topology on traces and the Fell topology on unitary representations. The proof of this fact is mutatis mutandis the same as that of Lemma
5.6
. Indeed as
\mathrm{Inn}(\Gamma)\leq\theta(\Lambda)
we get
\pi_{\varphi}(\Gamma)\subseteq U_{\varphi}(\Lambda)
so that the vector
v_{\varphi}\in\mathcal{H}_{\varphi}
is cyclic for the representation
U_{\varphi}
as well.

Proposition 7.5
.

Let
\Lambda
be a countable group admitting an action
\theta:\Lambda\to\mathrm{Aut}\left(\Gamma\right)
on the countable group
\Gamma
. Assume that
\Lambda
has Kazhdan’s property (T) and that
\mathrm{Inn}(\Gamma)\leq\theta(\Lambda)
. Then any finite-dimensional relative character
\varphi\in\mathrm{Ch}_{\Lambda}\left(\Gamma\right)
is an isolated point of the set
\mathrm{Ch}_{\Lambda}\left(\Gamma\right)
.

Proof.

Consider a sequence of relative characters
\varphi_{n}\in\mathrm{Ch}_{\Lambda}\left(\Gamma\right)
converging pointwise to a finite-dimensional relative character
\varphi\in\mathrm{Ch}_{\Lambda}\left(\Gamma\right)
.
As
\mathrm{Inn}(\Gamma)\leq\theta(\Lambda)
and taking into account Proposition
7.1
we get

N_{\varphi}^{\prime}=U_{\varphi}(\Lambda)^{\prime}
. On the other hand, since
\varphi
is a relative character we have
N_{\varphi}^{\prime}=\mathbb{C}\cdot\mathrm{Id}
. Therefore
U_{\varphi}(\Lambda)^{\prime}=\mathbb{C}\cdot\mathrm{Id}
which means that
U_{\varphi}(\Lambda)
is an irreducible representation. The same argument applied with respect to each relative character
\varphi_{n}
shows that each representation
U_{\varphi_{n}}
is irreducible.

The paragraph preceding this proposition shows that the unitary representations corresponding to the relative characters
\varphi_{n}
and
\varphi
satisfy
U_{\varphi_{n}}\to U_{\varphi}
in the Fell topology.
The assumption that the relative character
\varphi
is finite-dimensional means that the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\varphi}
is finite-dimensional. In particular, the representation
U_{\varphi}
is finite-dimensional.
Recall that for Kazhdan groups, finite-dimensional representations are isolated points of the unitary dual in the Fell topology
[
BdLHV08
, Theorem 1.2.5]
. It follows that
U_{\varphi_{n}}=U_{\varphi}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
sufficiently large. In particular
\pi_{\varphi_{n}}=\pi_{\varphi}
so that
\varphi_{n}=\varphi
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
sufficiently large.
∎

The simplex of relative traces

Assume that the group
\Lambda
has property (T) and that
\mathrm{Inn}(\Gamma)\leq\theta(\Lambda)
. The fact that the space of relative traces
\mathrm{Tr}_{\Lambda}\left(\Gamma\right)
is a Bauer simplex follows along the same lines as the proof of Theorem
1.6
.
Let us provide a bit more detail.

Proof of Theorem
1.9
.

Consider a sequence of relative characters
\varphi_{n}\in\mathrm{Ch}_{\Lambda}\left(\Gamma\right)
converging pointwise to some relative trace
\varphi\in\mathrm{Tr}_{\Lambda}\left(\Gamma\right)
.
For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
the subspace of the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\varphi_{n}}
consisting of
{\alpha_{\varphi_{n}}}
-invariant vectors is one-dimensional and is spanned by the cyclic vector
v_{\varphi_{n}}
, see
Proposition
7.2
. Since the group
\Lambda
has Kazhdan’s property (T) and according to Lemma
7.3
there is some constant
0<\beta<1
(depending on the chosen element
a\in\ell^{1}(\Lambda)
) such that Equation (
7.10
) holds true with respect to all the relative characters
\varphi_{n}
. The validity of this equation passes to the limiting trace
\varphi
with the same value of
\beta
. Therefore the relative trace
\varphi
is a relative character by the converse direction of Lemma
7.3
and of Proposition
7.2
.
The second part of the statement concerning finite-dimensional relative characters has already been established in Proposition
7.5
.
∎

The last part of our work deals with relative characters of the abelian group
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.

Corollary 7.6
.

Assume that
d\geq 3
. Any sequence of pairwise distinct relative characters
\varphi_{n}\in\mathrm{Ch}_{\mathrm{SL}_{d}(\mathbb{Z})}\left(\mathbb{Z}^{d}\right)
converges pointwise to the Dirac trace
\delta_{e}
on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.

The Pontryagin dual of the free abelian group
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is the torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}
. Bochner’s theorem sets up an
\mathrm{SL}_{d}(\mathbb{Z})
-equivariant affine homeomorphism
\mathrm{Tr}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\cong\mathrm{Prob}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
of compact convex sets, where the space of probability measures
\mathrm{Prob}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
is equipped with the weak-
*
topology. Therefore Corollary
7.6
is equivalent to the following dynamical statement:

Let
\mu_{n}
be any sequence of pairwise distinct ergodic
\mathrm{SL}_{d}(\mathbb{Z})
-invariant Borel probability measures on the torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}
. Then the sequence
\mu_{n}
converges in the weak-
*
topology to the Haar measure on the torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}
.
This statement is certainly well known to experts and is valid for all
d\geq 2
(however we could not locate a reference in the literature).

Proof of Corollary
7.6
.

Let
\varphi_{n}\in\mathrm{Ch}_{\mathrm{SL}_{d}(\mathbb{Z})}\left(\mathbb{Z}^{d}\right)
be any sequence of pairwise distinct relative characters. Consider an accumulation point
\varphi\in\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathrm{SL}_{d}(\mathbb{Z})}\left(\mathbb{Z}^{d}\right)
so that
\varphi
is a relative trace. Theorem
1.9
says that
\varphi
is in fact an infinite-dimensional relative character. In particular, the corresponding ergodic
\mathrm{SL}_{d}(\mathbb{Z})
-invariant Borel probability measure
\widehat{\varphi}\in\mathrm{Prob}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
is non-atomic. However the only non-atomic
\mathrm{SL}_{d}(\mathbb{Z})
-invariant Borel probability measures on the torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}
is the Haar measure
[
Bur91
, Proposition 9]
. In other words, the character
\varphi
must be the Dirac trace
\delta_{e}
.
∎

We conclude with a vanishing result for the semidirect product
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\ltimes\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.

Theorem 7.7
.

Let
\varphi_{n}\in\mathrm{Ch}\left(\mathrm{GL}_{d}(\mathbb{Z})\ltimes\mathbb{Z}^{d}\right)
be any sequence of characters such that the restrictions
\varphi_{n\,|\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
are pairwise distinct. Then the characters
\varphi_{n}
converge pointwise to the Dirac character
\delta_{e}\in\mathrm{Ch}\left(\mathrm{GL}_{d}(\mathbb{Z})\ltimes\mathbb{Z}^{d}\right)
.

Proof.

Assume that
d\geq 3
. Consider the semidirect product
\Gamma=\mathrm{GL}_{d}(\mathbb{Z})\ltimes\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Let
\varphi_{n}\in\mathrm{Ch}\left(\Gamma\right)
be any sequence of characters whose restrictions to the subgroup
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
are pairwise distinct. Let
\varphi\in\mathrm{Tr}\left(\Gamma\right)
be any accumulation point of the sequence
\varphi_{n}
. We know that
\varphi(\gamma)=0
for any element
\gamma\in\Gamma\setminus\mathrm{Rad}\left(\Gamma\right)
where
\mathrm{Rad}\left(\Gamma\right)
is the amenable radical given by
\mathrm{Rad}\left(\Gamma\right)=\mathrm{Z}(\mathrm{GL}_{d}(\mathbb{Z}))\ltimes\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, see Theorem
6.6
. In addition, the fact that
\varphi(\gamma)=0
for any element
\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{e\}
follows from Corollary
7.6
.

It remains to show that
\varphi(\gamma)=0
for all elements
\gamma\in\mathrm{Rad}\left(\Gamma\right)\setminus\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.
Note that any given element
\gamma\in\mathrm{Rad}\left(\Gamma\right)\setminus\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is of the form
\gamma=(-\mathrm{Id},u)
for some
u\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Take any sequence of pairwise distinct elements
v_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. The commutators given by

(7.13)

\left[\gamma,v_{n}\right]=\left[(-\mathrm{Id},u),(\mathrm{Id},v_{n})\right]=(\mathrm{Id},2(v_{n}-u))

are pairwise distinct as well. Since the trace
\varphi
vanishes on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{e\}
we conclude that
\varphi(\gamma)=0
using
[
LL23
, Lemma 4.13]
.
∎

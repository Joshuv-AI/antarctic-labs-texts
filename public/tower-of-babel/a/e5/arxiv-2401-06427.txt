Abstract
. For every simple Hermitian Lie group G G , we consider a certain maximal parabolic subgroup whose unipotent radical N N is either abelian (if G G is of tube type) or two-step nilpotent (if G G is of non-tube type). By the generalized Whittaker Plancherel formula we mean the Plancherel decomposition of L 2 ​ ( G / N , ω ) L^{2}(G/N,\omega) , the space of square-integrable sections of the homogeneous vector bundle over G / N G/N associated with an irreducible unitary representation ω \omega of N N . Assuming that the central character of ω \omega is contained in a certain cone, we construct embeddings of all holomorphic discrete series representations of G G into L 2 ​ ( G / N , ω ) L^{2}(G/N,\omega) and show that the multiplicities are equal to the dimensions of the lowest K K -types. The construction is in terms of a kernel function which can be explicitly defined using certain projections inside a complexification of G G . This kernel function carries all information about the holomorphic discrete series embedding, the lowest K K -type as functions on G / N G/N , as well as the associated Whittaker vectors.

Introduction

Gerrit van Dijk worked on several aspects of representation theory and harmonic analysis, in particular using special functions and distributions on Lie groups. With his younger colleagues he developed the notion of canonical representations due to Gelfand et al. as concrete models of unitary representations; giving the explicit decomposition of these into irreducibles amounts in effect to calculating tensor products of unitary irreducible representations or the restriction of such to symmetric subgroups. The groups in question include the classical rank one semisimple Lie groups, such as
G=\SU(n,1)
with the corresponding Riemannian symmetric space
\mathcal{D}=G/K
of Hermitian type, namely the open unit ball in complex Euclidian
n
-space; here the boundary sphere is also the Shilov boundary and of the form
G/P
for a parabolic subgroup
P
with nilradical
N
a Heisenberg group if
n>1
.

In the present paper we shall consider similar groups of Hermitian type, non-tube type, and various models of unitary representations. Even though unitary representations are often considered up to unitary equivalence, for certain problems it is convenient to work with concrete models; this is the case for the above mentioned problems studied by Gerrit van Dijk, namely branching laws, i.e. finding the explicit decomposition of a given irreducible representation restricted to a subgroup. We shall also use some of the tools in his study, namely reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces, interwining operators given as kernel operators, and explicit distribution vectors in unitary representations.

With notation as in the example above, we shall find explicit irreducible invariant subspaces of
L^{2}(G/N,\omega)
where
\omega
is an infinite-dimensional irreducible unitary representation of
N
defining a homogeneous vector bundle over
X=G/N
; these will be equivalent to holomorphic discrete series representations of
G
. In effect this gives a so-called Whittaker model of these representations corresponding to the Siegel parabolic subgroup
P
of potential use in the study of automorphic representations and number theory. Thus, our aim in this paper is to

•

Find explicit Whittaker distribution vectors in the holomorphic discrete series for
G
with values in
\omega
,

•

Find explicit intertwining kernel operators between the standard model on
\mathcal{D}=G/K
and the Whittaker model on
X=G/N
for these representations,

•

Find an explicit expression for the lowest
K
-types on
X
,

and to understand links between these problems.

Let us state our results in more detail.

The inclusion
G\subseteq P^{+}K_{\mathbb{C}}N_{\mathbb{C}}

Let
G
be a simple Lie group of Hermitian type, i.e. the associated Riemannian symmetric space
\mathcal{D}=G/K
carries a
G
-invariant complex structure. Denote by
\mathfrak{p}^{+}
the holomorphic tangent space at the base point
o=eK\in\mathcal{D}
and by
\mathfrak{p}^{-}
the antiholomorphic tangent space. We assume that
G
is contained as a real form inside a simply-connected complex Lie group
G_{\mathbb{C}}
. Let
P^{\pm}=\exp\mathfrak{p}^{\pm}
denote the connected subgroups of
G_{\mathbb{C}}
with Lie algebras
\mathfrak{p}^{\pm}
and write
K_{\mathbb{C}}
for the complexification of
K
in
G_{\mathbb{C}}
.

For groups of tube type, we constructed in our previous work
[
2
]
the holomorphic discrete series contribution to the Plancherel decomposition of
L^{2}(G/N,\psi)
, where
N
is the unipotent radical of some maximal parabolic subgroup and
\psi
a certain non-degenerate character on
N
. One approach to this problem was to show the inclusion
G\subseteq P^{+}K_{\mathbb{C}}N_{\mathbb{C}}
, where
N_{\mathbb{C}}
is the connected complexification of
N
inside
G_{\mathbb{C}}
. In order to extend these results to non-tube type groups, we first investigate for which maximal parabolic subgroups
P=MAN
this inclusion holds.

Theorem A

(see Theorem
2.3
)
.

For every simple Hermitian Lie group
G
there exists a unique standard maximal parabolic subgroup
P=MAN
such that
G\subseteq P^{+}K_{\mathbb{C}}N_{\mathbb{C}}
.

Discrete spectrum in the generalized Whittaker Plancherel formula

We fix the parabolic subgroup
P=MAN
from Theorem
A
. The unipotent radical
N
is either abelian (if
\mathcal{D}
is of tube type) or two-step nilpotent (if
\mathcal{D}
is of non-tube type). We fix an irreducible unitary representation
\omega
of
N
whose central character is contained in a certain cone in the Lie algebra of the center of
N
(see Section
4
for details). Note that
\omega
is a character if
N
is abelian and infinite-dimensional otherwise. By the generalized Whittaker Plancherel formula we mean the Plancherel formula for the induced representation
\Ind_{N}^{G}(\omega)
which can be realized on
L^{2}(G/N,\omega)
, the
L^{2}
-sections of the homogeneous vector bundle over
G/N
associated with
\omega
. Our main result is the following:

Theorem B

(see Corollary
5.9
)
.

Every holomorphic discrete series representation of
G
occurs discretely in
L^{2}(G/N,\omega)
with multiplicity equal to the dimension of its highest weight space.

Here, the highest weight space of an irreducible unitary representation
(U,\mathcal{H})
is the subspace
\mathcal{H}^{\mathfrak{p}^{+}}
annihilated by
\mathfrak{p}^{+}
. It forms an irreducible representation
(\pi,\mathcal{V}_{\pi})
of
K
which determines
(U,\mathcal{H})
uniquely. We therefore write
(U_{\pi},\mathcal{H}_{\pi})
for the holomorphic discrete series representation with highest weight
\pi
, and we realize it on a space
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
of holomorphic
\mathcal{V}_{\pi}
-valued functions on
\mathcal{D}
(see Section
3
for details). Note that
\mathcal{V}_{\pi}
is also the lowest
K
-type of
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
.

Lowest
K
-types and Whittaker vectors via kernel functions

The proof of Theorem
B
is by constructing explicit embeddings

\mathcal{H}_{\pi}\otimes\overline{\mathcal{V}_{\pi}}\to L^{2}(G/N,\omega),\quad F\otimes\overline{\eta}\mapsto T_{\pi,\eta}F.

The maps
T_{\pi,\eta}:\mathcal{H}_{\pi}\to L^{2}(G/N,\omega)
(
\eta\in\mathcal{V}_{\pi}
) are given in terms of their integral kernels.

In view of Theorem
A
, every element
g\in G
decomposes into
g=p^{+}_{N}(g)k_{\mathbb{C},N}^{+}(g)n_{\mathbb{C}}^{+}(g)\in P^{+}K_{\mathbb{C}}N_{\mathbb{C}}
(not uniquely, but all formulas will be independent of the choice of decomposition). Using this decomposition, we define for every
\eta\in\mathcal{V}_{\pi}
a function
\Psi_{\pi,\eta}:G\times\mathcal{D}\to\Hom(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{V}_{\pi})
by its adjoint:

\Psi_{\pi,\eta}(x,g\cdot o)^{*}:\mathcal{V}_{\pi}\to\mathcal{F},\\
\xi\mapsto\omega(n_{\mathbb{C}}^{+}(g^{-1}x))^{-1}m\Big(\pi(k_{\mathbb{C},N}^{+}(g^{-1}x))^{-1}\circ j_{\pi}(g,o)^{-*}\xi\otimes\overline{\eta}\Big)\qquad(x,g\in G,\xi\in\mathcal{V}_{\pi}),

where
j_{\pi}(g,z)\in\GL(\mathcal{V}_{\pi})
(
g\in G
,
z\in\mathcal{D}
) is the cocycle used to define the holomorphic discrete series action on
\mathcal{D}
(see (
3.2
)) and
m:\mathcal{V}_{\pi}\otimes\overline{\mathcal{V}}_{\pi}\to\mathcal{F}
is a certain matrix coefficient map (see (
5.4
)). This construction is discussed in detail in Section
5.4
, but the main point is that the definition of
\Psi
is completely explicit when using the embedding
G\subseteq P^{+}K_{\mathbb{C}}N_{\mathbb{C}}
.

The operator
T_{\pi,\eta}:\mathcal{H}_{\pi}\to L^{2}(G/N,\omega)
(
\eta\in\mathcal{V}_{\pi}
) is most easily characterized by its adjoint
T_{\pi,\eta}^{*}:L^{2}(G/N,\omega)\to\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
which has
\Psi(x,z)
as integral kernel:

T_{\pi,\eta}^{*}f(z)=\int_{G/N}\Psi_{\pi,\eta}(x,z)f(x)\,d(xN)\qquad(f\in L^{2}(G/N,\omega),z\in\mathcal{D}).

The function
\Psi
carries all information about the embedding of the associated holomorphic discrete series representation into
L^{2}(G/N,\omega)
in the following sense:

Theorem C

(see Proposition
5.5
, Lemma
5.7
and Proposition
5.10
)
.

(1)

The map

\mathcal{H}_{\pi}\otimes\overline{\mathcal{V}_{\pi}}\to L^{2}(G/N,\omega),\quad F\otimes\overline{\eta}\mapsto T_{\pi,\eta}F

constructs the holomorphic discrete series contribution in Theorem
B
.

(2)

The embedding
T_{\pi,\eta}
restricted to the lowest
K
-type
\mathcal{V}_{\pi}
is given by

T_{\pi,\eta}\xi(x)=\Psi_{\pi,\eta}(x,o)^{*}\xi\qquad(\xi\in\mathcal{V}_{\pi},x\in G).

(3)

Identifying distribution vectors on
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
with antiholomorphic
\mathcal{V}_{\pi}^{\vee}
-valued functions on
\mathcal{D}
(see Section
5.5
for details), the Whittaker vector
W\in\Hom_{N}(U_{\pi}^{\infty}|_{N},\omega)
associated with the embedding
T_{\pi,\eta}\in\Hom_{G}(U_{\pi},\Ind_{N}^{G}(\omega))
by Frobenius reciprocity is given by the
\mathcal{V}_{\pi}^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{F}=\Hom(\mathcal{V}_{\pi},\mathcal{F})
-valued antiholomorphic function

W(z)=\Psi_{\pi,\eta}(e,z)^{*}\qquad(z\in\mathcal{D}).

Items (
2
) and (
3
) show that the kernel
\Psi_{\pi,\eta}
contains both information about the Whittaker vector (when restricted to
\{e\}\times\mathcal{D}
) and about the lowest
K
-type embedding (when restricted to
G\times\{o\}
).

Relation to other work

This paper extends parts of the results in our previous work
[
2
]
to the case of non-tube type groups. In

]
we also give explicit formulas for Whittaker vectors in different models of the holomorphic discrete series. This should also be possible for non-tube type groups using the

Structure of the paper

1.1.
The Harish-Chandra realization of bounded domains

In this article we assume that
G
is a connected simple Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. We assume that
G
is
contained as a closed subgroup in a simply connected complex Lie group
G_{\mathbb{C}}
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}
. Let

\sigma_{\mathfrak{g}}:\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}\to\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}
be the conjugation with respect to
\mathfrak{g}
,
\sigma_{\mathfrak{g}}(X+iY)=X-iY
for
X,Y\in\mathfrak{g}
.
Then
\sigma_{\mathfrak{g}}
can be lifted to a homomorphism
\sigma_{G}:G_{\mathbb{C}}\to G_{\mathbb{C}}
such that
G\subseteq G_{\mathbb{C}}^{\sigma_{G}}
is open.

Let
\theta_{\mathfrak{g}}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
be a Cartan involution which we extend to a complex-linear involution
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}\to\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}
. It lifts to an involution
\theta_{G}:G_{\mathbb{C}}\to G_{\mathbb{C}}
such that
K=G^{\theta_{G}}
is a maximal compact subgroup of
G
with complexification
K_{\mathbb{C}}=G_{\mathbb{C}}^{\theta_{G}}
. If the role of
G
or
\mathfrak{g}
is clear from the context, we simply write
\theta
for both involutions.

Denote by
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{p}
the corresponding Cartan decomposition of
\mathfrak{g}
. We assume that
G/K
is a bounded symmetric domain. This happens if and only if the center
\mathfrak{z}
of
\mathfrak{k}
is non-zero, and in that case it is one-dimensional. There exists an element
z_{0}\in\mathfrak{z}
, unique up to sign, such
that
\ad(z_{0})
has eigenvalues
0
,
\pm i
on
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}
. The zero-eigenspace is
\mathfrak{k}_{\mathbb{C}}
. We write

\mathfrak{p}^{\pm}=\{X\in\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}\,:\,[z_{0},X]=\pm iX\}.

Then
\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbb{C}}=\mathfrak{p}^{+}\oplus\mathfrak{p}^{-}
and
\mathfrak{p}^{\pm}
are abelian Lie algebras and the corresponding analytic subgroups
P^{\pm}=\exp\mathfrak{p}^{\pm}\subseteq G_{\mathbb{C}}
are normalized by
K_{\mathbb{C}}
. The
set
P^{+}K_{\mathbb{C}}P^{-}
is open and dense in
G_{\mathbb{C}}
and the multiplication
map

P^{+}\times K_{\mathbb{C}}\times P^{-}\to G_{\mathbb{C}},\quad(p^{+},k,p^{-})\mapsto p^{+}kp^{-}

is a holomorphic diffeomorphism into its image.
For
g\in P^{+}K_{\mathbb{C}}P^{-}
we write
g=p^{+}(g)k_{\mathbb{C}}(g)p^{-}(g)
.
We have
G\subseteq P^{+}K_{\mathbb{C}}P^{-}
,
G\cap K_{\mathbb{C}}P^{-}=K
and the map

\iota:G/K\to\mathfrak{p}^{+},\quad gK\mapsto\log_{\mathfrak{p}^{+}}(p^{+}(g))

is a well defined
G
-equivariant embedding. Its image
\mathcal{D}=\iota(G/K)
is a bounded circular domain in the complex vector space
\mathfrak{p}^{+}
, the Harish-Chandra realization of
G/K
, and we write

g\cdot Z=\iota(g\exp Z)\qquad(g\in G,Z\in\mathcal{D})

for the corresponding action of
G
on
\mathcal{D}
.

Embedding
\mathfrak{p}^{+}
into
G_{\mathbb{C}}/K_{\mathbb{C}}P^{-}
by
Z\mapsto\exp(Z)K_{\mathbb{C}}P^{-}
, we can consider the action of
G_{\mathbb{C}}
on
\mathcal{D}
and
\mathfrak{p}^{+}
. Clearly,
P^{+}
acts on
\mathfrak{p}^{+}
by translation
\exp(X)\cdot Z=Z+X
and
K_{\mathbb{C}}
acts linearly by
k\cdot Z=\Ad(k)Z
.

1.2.
Strongly orthogonal roots

Let
\mathfrak{t}
be a Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
containing
z_{0}
, then
\mathfrak{t}\subseteq\mathfrak{k}
. Let
\Delta(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{C}})
be the roots of
\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{C}}
in
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}
and for
\alpha\in\Delta(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{C}})
write
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}^{\alpha}
for the corresponding root space. We define subsets

\displaystyle\Delta_{c}

\displaystyle=\{\alpha\in\Delta(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{C}})\,:\,\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}^{\alpha}\subseteq\mathfrak{k}_{\mathbb{C}}\}=\{\alpha\in\Delta(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{C}})\,:\,\alpha(z_{0})=0\}

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}

\displaystyle=\{\alpha\in\Delta(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{C}})\,:\,\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}^{\alpha}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbb{C}}\}=\{\alpha\in\Delta(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{C}})\,:\,\alpha(z_{0})\not=0\}

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}^{+}

\displaystyle=\{\alpha\in\Delta(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{C}})\,:\,\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}^{\alpha}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}^{+}\}=\{\alpha\in\Delta_{n}\,:\,\alpha(z_{0})=i\}

and call the roots in
\Delta_{c}
compact and the roots in
\Delta_{n}
non-compact. Choose a positive system of roots
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{C}})
such that
\Delta_{n}^{+}\subseteq\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{C}})
. Then
\Delta_{c}^{+}:=\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{C}})\cap\Delta_{c}
is a positive system in

\Delta_{c}
and
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{C}})=\Delta_{n}^{+}\cup\Delta_{c}^{+}
.

Let
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{r}
be a maximal set of (long) strongly orthogonal roots in
\Delta_{n}^{+}
constructed in the
usual way:
\gamma_{1}
is the maximal root in
\Delta_{n}^{+}
and
\gamma_{j+1}
the maximal root in
\Delta_{n}^{+}
strongly
orthogonal to
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{j}
. Let
h_{j}\in i\mathfrak{t}
be the corresponding
co-root vector. In particular,
\gamma_{i}(h_{j})=2\delta_{ij}
. Let
\mathfrak{c}=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\mathbb{R}ih_{j}\subseteq\mathfrak{t}
and

\mathfrak{c}_{\mathbb{C}}
its complexification. Abusing notation, we also write
\gamma_{i}
for its restriction to
\mathfrak{c}_{\mathbb{C}}
. Moore’s Theorem describes the roots of
\mathfrak{c}_{\mathbb{C}}
in
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}
, i.e. the restrictions of roots in
\Delta(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{C}})
to
\mathfrak{c}_{\mathbb{C}}
. Here, we call a restricted root compact resp. non-compact if it is the restriction of a compact resp. non-compact root.

Theorem 1.1

(Moore’s Theorem, first version)
.

For the set
\Delta(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{c}_{\mathbb{C}})
of restricted roots there are two possibilities:

(1)

\mathcal{D}
is of tube type. In this case

\qquad\displaystyle\Delta(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{c}_{\mathbb{C}})=\{\pm\gamma_{i}:1\leq i\leq r\}\cup\{\tfrac{1}{2}(\pm\gamma_{i}\pm\gamma_{j})\,:\,1\leq i<j\leq r\},

(2)

\mathcal{D}

\qquad\quad\displaystyle\Delta(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{c}_{\mathbb{C}})=\{\pm\tfrac{1}{2}\gamma_{i},\pm\gamma_{i}:1\leq i\leq r\}\cup\{\tfrac{1}{2}(\pm\gamma_{i}\pm\gamma_{j})\,:\,1\leq i<j\leq r\},

the roots
\pm\gamma_{i}
,
\pm\tfrac{1}{2}\gamma_{i}
and
\pm\frac{1}{2}(\gamma_{i}+\gamma_{j})

\pm\tfrac{1}{2}\gamma_{i}
and
\pm\frac{1}{2}(\gamma_{i}-\gamma_{j})

1.3.
The
\su(1,1)
-embeddings

Let for the moment

G=\SU(1,1)=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
\bar{b}&\bar{a}\end{pmatrix}\,:\,|a|^{2}-|b|^{2}=1\right\}\subseteq\SL(2,\mathbb{C})

with Lie algebra

\mathfrak{g}=\su(1,1)=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}ix&z\\
\bar{z}&-ix\end{pmatrix}\,:\,x\in\mathbb{R},z\in\mathbb{C}\right\}\subseteq\sl(2,\mathbb{C}).

We have
\sigma_{G}(g)=I_{1,1}g^{-*}I_{1,1}
and

\sigma_{\mathfrak{g}}(X)=-I_{1,1}X^{*}I_{1,1}
, with the notation
I_{1,1}=\diag(1,-1)
and
X^{*}=\overline{X}^{\top}
. We choose
\theta_{G}(g)=I_{1,1}gI_{1,1}
as a Cartan involution, then
\theta_{\mathfrak{g}}(X)=I_{1,1}XI_{1,1}
,
\mathfrak{k}=\{\diag(ix,-ix)\,:\,x\in\mathbb{R}\}
and
K=\{\diag(\gamma,\gamma^{-1})\,:\,|\gamma|=1\}
.

Let

z_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{i}{2}&0\\
0&-\frac{i}{2}\end{pmatrix},\quad e_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad f_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},

then

\mathfrak{k}_{\mathbb{C}}=\mathbb{C}z_{0},\quad\mathfrak{p}^{+}=\mathbb{C}e_{0},\quad\mathfrak{p}^{-}=\mathbb{C}f_{0}

and

h_{0}=[e_{0},f_{0}]=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}=-2iz_{0}.

We have

P^{+}K_{\mathbb{C}}P^{-}=\left\{g=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}:ad-bc=1,d\not=0\right\}

Identifying
K_{\mathbb{C}}\simeq\mathbb{C}^{*}
and
\mathfrak{p}^{+}=\mathbb{C}e_{0}\simeq\mathbb{C}
we have for a matrix
g
as above

k_{\mathbb{C}}(g)=\frac{1}{d}\quad\text{and}\quad p^{+}(g)=\frac{b}{d}.

(1.1)

Therefore, the Harish-Chandra embedding is given by

\iota:G/K\to\mathfrak{p}^{+},\quad\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
\bar{b}&\bar{a}\end{pmatrix}K\mapsto\frac{b}{\bar{a}}\in\mathcal{D}_{0}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\,:\,|z|<1\}

Lemma 1.2
.

The subspace
\mathfrak{a}=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{r}\mathbb{R}x_{j}

For
\SU(1,1)

c_{0}=\exp\left(-\frac{\pi}{4}(e_{0}-f_{0})\right)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\

The holomorphic discrete series

In this section we recall the realization of holomorphic discrete series representations on spaces of holomorphic functions on the bounded domain
\mathcal{D}
. We refer to
[

11

We start by defining the universal

, see
[
9
, pp 64]

and

(J)

(

(K)

(

)

The following relations are easy to verify:

Lemma 3.1
.

For
k\in K_{\mathbb{C}}
define
k^{*}=\sigma_{G}(k)^{-1}
. Then the following holds true for

,

and

(1)

J(k,z)=k
,

(2)

J(ab,z)=J(a,b\cdot z)J(b,z)

(3)

(4)

Moreover, if

(5)

J(a,w)^{*}K(a\cdot z,a\cdot w)J(a,z)=K(z,w)

(6)

K(a\cdot o,a\cdot o)=J(a,o)^{-*}J(a,o)^{-1}

Now fix a finite-dimensional holomorphic representation

of
K_{\mathbb{C}}

\mathcal{V}_{\pi}
with an inner product that makes

unitary, so that

\pi(k)^{*}=\pi(k^{*})\qquad(k\in K_{\mathbb{C}}).

(3.1)

Let

j_{\pi}(g,z)=\pi(J(g,z))^{-1}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad K_{\pi}(z,w)=\pi(K(z,w))^{-1},

(3.2)

whenever

and

. Then by Lemma
3.1
(
2

U_{\pi}(g)F(z)=j_{\pi}(g^{-1},z)F(g^{-1}z)\qquad(g\in G,F\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{V}_{\pi}),z\in\mathcal{D})

defines a representation of
G
on
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{V}_{\pi})
, the space of
\mathcal{V}_{\pi}
-valued holomorphic functions on
\mathcal{D}
. By Lemma
3.1
(
5
) it preserves the inner product

\langle F_{1},F_{2}\rangle_{\pi}=\int_{\mathcal{D}}\langle K_{\pi}(z,z)^{-1}F_{1}(z),F_{2}(z)\rangle\,d^{*}z,

where

denotes a
G
-invariant measure on
\mathcal{D}
. This defines a unitary representation
(U_{\pi},\mathcal{H}_{\pi})
, but
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
could be zero. A necessary and sufficient condition for

can be given in terms of the highest weight
\mu_{\pi}\in\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
of
\pi
:

Theorem 3.2

(Harish-Chandra)
.

Let
\rho=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{C}})}\alpha
. Then
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}\neq\{0\}
if and only if

\langle\mu+\rho,\alpha\rangle<0,\quad\text{for all }\alpha\in\Delta_{n}^{+}.

In this case,
(U_{\pi},\mathcal{H}_{\pi})
is irreducible and belongs to the discrete series.

are called

. Moreover, the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
is a reproducing kernel Hilbert space with reproducing kernel
K_{\pi}(z,w)
(after suitable normalization of the measure

).

Note that the condition (

) only depends on the restriction of
\mu_{\pi}
to
\mathfrak{c}_{\mathbb{C}}
. Writing
\mu_{\pi}|_{\mathfrak{c}_{\mathbb{C}}}=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{r}\mu_{\pi,j}\gamma_{j}
and

, we find that (

\mu_{\pi,j}\geq\rho_{j}\qquad\mbox{for all }1\leq j\leq r.

(3.4)

In this notation, the action of the torus
\exp\mathfrak{c}_{\mathbb{C}}
on a highest weight vector

is given by

\exp\left(\sum_{j=1}^{r}t_{j}h_{j}\right)\xi=\left(\prod_{j=1}^{r}e^{-\mu_{\pi,j}t_{j}}\right)\xi.

(3.5)

Example 3.3

(The case of

)
.

We identify
K_{\mathbb{C}}
with
\mathbb{C}^{*}
by

\mathfrak{p}^{+}
with

4.
Fock space representations

We recall the construction of the irreducible unitary representations of the nilpotent group

is abelian if

is of tube-type and two-step nilpotent otherwise. In particular,
\mathfrak{n}_{1}
is the center of
\mathfrak{n}
. It follows that for every non-trivial central character
\psi:N\to S^{1}
there exists a unique irreducible unitary representation
\omega_{\psi}
of
N
such that
\omega_{\psi}(n)=\psi(n)\cdot\id
for all
n\in\exp(\mathfrak{n}_{1})
. If
\mathfrak{n}
is abelian, then
\omega_{\psi}=\psi
is simply a unitary character of
N
, and if
\mathfrak{n}
is two-step nilpotent the representation
\omega_{\psi}
is infinite-dimensional.

We realize
\omega_{\psi}
explicitly on a Fock space
\mathcal{F}
of holomorphic functions on
\mathfrak{n}_{\frac{1}{2}}
, with the convention that
\mathcal{F}=\mathbb{C}
if
\mathfrak{n}_{\frac{1}{2}}=\{0\}
. More precisely, let
J
be the complex structure on
\mathfrak{n}_{\frac{1}{2}}
given by Lemma
1.5
. The Killing form
\kappa
on
\mathfrak{g}
gives rise to an inner product on the real vector space
\mathfrak{n}
:

(z|w)=\kappa(z,\theta w)\qquad(z,w\in\mathfrak{n}).

Using the complex structure
J
, we define a Hermitian form on
\mathfrak{n}_{\frac{1}{2}}
:

\langle z,w\rangle=\kappa(z,\theta w)-i\kappa(Jz,\theta w)\qquad(z,w\in\mathfrak{n}_{\frac{1}{2}}).

It is easy to see that this form is indeed Hermitian with respect to the complex structure induced by
J
. The Fock space
\mathcal{F}
is defined by

\mathcal{F}=\left\{\zeta\in\mathcal{O}(\mathfrak{n}_{\frac{1}{2}}):\int_{\mathfrak{n}_{\frac{1}{2}}}|\zeta(w)|^{2}e^{-|w|^{2}}\,dw<\infty\right\}

with the obvious norm, where

denotes Lebesgue measure on
\mathfrak{n}_{\frac{1}{2}}
normalized such that

is a probability measure.
\mathcal{F}
is a reproducing kernel Hilbert space with reproducing kernel given by

K_{\mathcal{F}}(z,w)=e^{\langle z,w\rangle}\qquad(z,w\in\mathfrak{n}_{\frac{1}{2}}).

The group
N
acts on
\mathcal{F}
by

\displaystyle\omega(\exp(z))\zeta(w)

\displaystyle=e^{\langle w,z\rangle-\frac{1}{2}|z|^{2}}\zeta(w-z)

\displaystyle(z\in\mathfrak{n}_{\frac{1}{2}}),

(4.2)

\displaystyle\omega(\exp(x))\zeta(w)

\displaystyle=e^{2i(x|E)}\zeta(w)

\displaystyle(x\in\mathfrak{n}_{1}),

(4.3)

where
\zeta\in\mathcal{F}
and
w\in\mathfrak{n}_{\frac{1}{2}}
and
E
as in (
1.2
). Here, we normalize the Killing form such that
(E|E)=r
. Note that
K\cap L
leaves

invariant.

On the dense subspace
\mathcal{F}_{0}
spanned by functions of the form

where

is a polynomial and

, this representation extends to the complexification

of

. Recall that

\mathfrak{n}_{\frac{1}{2},\mathbb{C}}=\mathfrak{n}_{\frac{1}{2},\mathbb{C}}^{+}\oplus\mathfrak{n}_{\frac{1}{2},\mathbb{C}}^{-}\qquad\mbox{with }\mathfrak{n}_{\frac{1}{2},\mathbb{C}}^{\pm}=\{\tfrac{1}{2}(z\mp iJz):z\in\mathfrak{n}_{\frac{1}{2}}\}

(4.4)

It follows that for

and

:

\displaystyle\omega(n_{z}^{+})\zeta(w)

\displaystyle=\zeta(w-z),

(4.5)

\displaystyle\omega(n_{z}^{-})\zeta(w)

\displaystyle=e^{\langle w,z\rangle}\zeta(w).

(4.6)

In particular,

\omega(n_{z}^{+})^{*}=\omega(n_{z}^{-})^{-1}\qquad(z\in\mathfrak{n}_{\frac{1}{2}}).

(4.7)

The compact group

acts on

and

5.

We fix the irreducible unitary representation
(\omega,\mathcal{F})
of
N
constructed in the previous section. For a holomorphic discrete series representation
(U_{\pi},\mathcal{H}_{\pi})
we study three types of objects:

•

(Whittaker vectors)

•

(Intertwining operators)
T\in\Hom_{G}(\mathcal{H}_{\pi}^{\infty},C^{\infty}(G/N,\omega))

•

(Kernels)
\Psi:G\times\mathcal{D}\to\Hom(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{V}_{\pi})

Using explicit kernels, we embed holomorphic discrete series representations
(U_{\pi},\mathcal{H}_{\pi})
into

, the space of

-sections of the homogeneous vector bundle
G\times_{N}\mathcal{F}
over
G/N
induced from
(\omega,\mathcal{F})
.

5.1.
Whittaker vectors vs. intertwining operators

Let
W\in\Hom_{N}(\mathcal{H}_{\pi}^{\infty},\mathcal{F})
be a Whittaker functional. Then we obtain a continuous
G
-equivariant embedding

\displaystyle T:\mathcal{H}_{\pi}^{\infty}\to C^{\infty}(G/N,\omega)=\{f\in C^{\infty}(G,\mathcal{F}):f(gn)=\omega(n)^{-1}f(g)\mbox{ for }g\in G,n\in N\},

\displaystyle T(F)(x)=W(U_{\pi}(x)^{-1}F)\qquad(F\in\mathcal{H}_{\pi}^{\infty},x\in G).

On the other hand, every continuous
G
-equivariant linear operator
T:\mathcal{H}_{\pi}^{\infty}\to C^{\infty}(G/N,\omega)
gives rise to a Whittaker functional
W\in\Hom_{N}(\mathcal{H}_{\pi}^{\infty},\mathcal{F})
by

W(F)=T(F)(e)\qquad(F\in\mathcal{H}_{\pi}^{\infty}).

(5.1)

This gives the Frobenius reciprocity isomorphism

\Hom_{N}(\mathcal{H}_{\pi}^{\infty},\mathcal{F})\simeq\Hom_{G}(\mathcal{H}_{\pi}^{\infty},C^{\infty}(G/N,\omega)),\quad W\mapsto T.

5.2.
From intertwining operators to kernels

To every
T\in\Hom_{G}(\mathcal{H}_{\pi}^{\infty},C^{\infty}(G/N,\omega))
we can now associate a kernel function
\Psi:G\times\mathcal{D}\to\Hom(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{V}_{\pi})
in the following way. For every
z\in\mathcal{D}
and
\xi\in\mathcal{V}_{\pi}
, the reproducing kernel
K_{z}\xi
is contained in
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}^{\infty}
, and we can put

\Psi^{*}(x,z)\xi=T(K_{z,\xi})(x)=T(U_{\pi}(x)^{*}K_{z,\xi})(e)\qquad(x\in G,z\in\mathcal{D},\xi\in\mathcal{V}_{\pi}).

This defines a map
\Psi^{*}:G\times\mathcal{D}\to\Hom(\mathcal{V}_{\pi},\mathcal{F})
. Since
K_{z,\xi}
is antiholomorphic in
z\in\mathcal{D}
, the function
\Psi^{*}(x,z)
is continuous in
x\in G
and antiholomorphic in
z\in\mathcal{D}
. Taking the adjoint of
\Psi^{*}(x,z)
, we obtain

\Psi:G\times\mathcal{D}\to\Hom(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{V}_{\pi}),\quad\Psi(x,z):=\Psi^{*}(x,z)^{*}.

Lemma 5.1
.

The function
\Psi(x,z)
is continuous in
x\in G
and holomorphic in
z\in\mathcal{D}
. Moreover, it has the following invariance:

(1)

\Psi(xn,z)=\Psi(x,z)\circ\omega(n)
for
n\in N
.

(2)

\Psi(gx,z)=j_{\pi}(g^{-1},z)\circ\Psi(x,g^{-1}z)
for all
g,x\in G
and
z\in\mathcal{D}
.

Proof.

The proof is the same as in
[
2
, Lemma 4.4]
.
∎

By the previous lemma, the kernel
\Psi(x,z)
is uniquely determined by its values at
(x,o)
for
x\in G
. We therefore let

\widetilde{\Psi}:G\to\Hom(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{V}_{\pi}),\quad\widetilde{\Psi}(x)=\Psi(x,o),

noting that

\Psi(x,g\cdot o)=j_{\pi}(g,o)^{-1}\circ\widetilde{\Psi}(g^{-1}x)\qquad(x,g\in G).

(5.2)

Using the equivariance properties from before, we can express the values of
\widetilde{\Psi}
in terms of the decomposition
G\subseteq P^{-}K_{\mathbb{C}}N_{\mathbb{C}}
(see Remark
2.4
).

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
g\in G
and write
g=p^{-}kn\in P^{-}K_{\mathbb{C}}N_{\mathbb{C}}
. Then the following identity holds in
\Hom(\mathcal{F}_{0},\mathcal{V}_{\pi})
with
\mathcal{F}_{0}
as in Section
4
:

\widetilde{\Psi}(g)=\pi(k)\circ\widetilde{\Psi}(e)\circ\omega(n).

Proof.

The same as in
[
2
, Remark 4.5 (2)]
.
∎

It follows that
\widetilde{\Psi}
is uniquely determined by
\widetilde{\Psi}(e)=\Psi(e,o)\in\Hom(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{V}_{\pi})
. The latter space is infinite-dimensional in the case where
\mathcal{D}
is of non-tube type. However, by Lemma
5.2
there is some restriction on
\widetilde{\Psi}(e)
: for
n\in N_{\mathbb{C}}\cap(K_{\mathbb{C}}P^{-})
we have

\widetilde{\Psi}(e)\circ\omega(n)=\widetilde{\Psi}(n)=\pi(n)\circ\widetilde{\Psi}(e),

where we view
(\pi,\mathcal{V}_{\pi})
as a representation of
K_{\mathbb{C}}P^{-}
by extending it trivially to
P^{-}
. It follows that
A=\widetilde{\Psi}(e)
belongs to
\Hom_{N_{\mathbb{C}}\cap(K_{\mathbb{C}}P^{-})}(\mathcal{F}_{0},\mathcal{V}_{\pi})
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Every
A\in\Hom_{N_{\mathbb{C}}\cap(K_{\mathbb{C}}P^{-})}(\mathcal{F}_{0},\mathcal{V}_{\pi})
is of the form

A\zeta=\int_{\mathfrak{n}_{\frac{1}{2}}}\zeta(w)\pi(n_{w}^{-})\eta\cdot e^{-|w|^{2}}\,dw\qquad(\zeta\in\mathcal{F})

for some
\eta\in\mathcal{V}_{\pi}
.

Proof.

Taking adjoints, we have

\omega(n)^{*}\circ A^{*}=A^{*}\circ\pi(n)^{*}\qquad(n\in N_{\mathbb{C}}\cap(K_{\mathbb{C}}P^{-})).

Since
N_{\mathbb{C}}\cap K_{\mathbb{C}}P^{-}=\{n_{z}^{-}:z\in\mathfrak{n}_{\frac{1}{2}}\}
(see Lemma
1.5
and (
4.4
)), using (
4.1
) and (
4.5
) we find

\displaystyle A^{*}\xi(z)

\displaystyle=[\omega(n_{z}^{+})^{-1}\circ A^{*}\xi](0)=[\omega(n_{z}^{-})^{*}\circ A^{*}\xi](0)

\displaystyle=[A^{*}\circ\pi(n_{z}^{-})^{*}\xi](0)=\langle A^{*}\circ\pi(n_{z}^{-})^{*}\xi,\mathbf{1}\rangle=\langle\xi,\pi(n_{z}^{-})\circ A\mathbf{1}\rangle.

Writing
\eta=A\mathbf{1}
it follows that

\langle A\zeta,\xi\rangle=\langle\zeta,A^{*}\xi\rangle=\int_{\mathfrak{n}_{\frac{1}{2}}}\zeta(w)\langle\pi(n_{z}^{-})\eta,\xi\rangle e^{-|w|^{2}}\,dw

and the claim follows.
∎

Corollary 5.4
.

\widetilde{\Psi}(e)\in\Hom(\mathcal{F}_{0},\mathcal{V}_{\pi})
is given by

\widetilde{\Psi}(e)\zeta=\int_{\mathfrak{n}_{\frac{1}{2}}}\zeta(w)\pi(n_{w}^{-})\eta\cdot e^{-|w|^{2}}\,dw\qquad(\zeta\in\mathcal{F})

for some
\eta\in\mathcal{V}_{\pi}
. In particular, the space
\Hom_{N}(\mathcal{H}_{\pi}^{\infty},\mathcal{F})
of Whittaker vectors on
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
is of dimension at most
\dim\mathcal{V}_{\pi}
.

To show that the dimension is actually equal to
\dim\mathcal{V}_{\pi}
, we construct in Section
5.4
kernel functions
\Psi
explicitly and show that they give rise to Whittaker vectors.

5.3.
The case of unitary intertwining operators

We assume for a moment that
T
extends to an embedding
T:\mathcal{H}_{\pi}\to L^{2}(G/N,\omega)
. Then its adjoint
T^{*}:L^{2}(G/N,\omega)\to\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
is an integral operator with integral kernel
\Psi(x,z)
:

Proposition 5.5
.

If
T:\mathcal{H}_{\pi}\to L^{2}(G/N,\omega)
is a
G
-equivariant continuous linear map and
\Psi
as in Section
5.2
, then its adjoint
T^{*}:L^{2}(G/N,\omega)\to\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
is given by

T^{*}f(z)=\int_{G/N}\Psi(x,z)f(x)\,d(xN)\qquad(f\in L^{2}(G/N,\omega),z\in\mathcal{D}).

Proof.

For
f\in L^{2}(G/N,\omega)
we have

\displaystyle\langle T^{*}f(z),\xi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle T^{*}f,K_{z,\xi}\rangle=\langle f,T(K_{z,\xi})\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{G/N}\langle f(x),\Psi(x,z)^{*}\xi\rangle\,d(xN)

\displaystyle=\int_{G/N}\langle\Psi(x,z)f(x),\xi\rangle\,d(xN).\qed

Corollary 5.6
.

If
T:\mathcal{H}_{\pi}\to L^{2}(G/N,\omega)
is a
G
-equivariant continuous linear map and
\Psi
as in Section
5.2
, then

TF(x)=\int_{\mathcal{D}}\Psi(x,z)^{*}K_{\pi}(z,z)^{-1}F(z)\,d^{*}z\qquad(F\in\mathcal{H}_{\pi},x\in G).

Proof.

For
f\in L^{2}(G/N,\omega)
we have, in view of the previous proposition:

\displaystyle\langle TF,f\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle F,T^{*}f\rangle=\int_{\mathcal{D}}\langle K_{\pi}(z,z)^{-1}F(z),T^{*}f(z)\rangle\,d^{*}z

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{D}}\int_{G/N}\langle K_{\pi}(z,z)^{-1}F(z),\Psi(x,z)f(x)\rangle\,d(xN)\,d^{*}z

\displaystyle=\int_{G/N}\left\langle\int_{\mathcal{D}}\Psi(x,z)^{*}K_{\pi}(z,z)^{-1}F(z)d^{*}z,f(x)\right\rangle\,d(xN).\qed

5.4.
From kernels to unitary intertwining operators

Now let
\eta\in\mathcal{V}_{\pi}
and define
A_{\eta}\in\Hom(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{V}_{\pi})
by

A_{\eta}\zeta=\int_{\mathfrak{n}_{\frac{1}{2}}}\zeta(w)\pi(n_{w}^{-})\eta\cdot e^{-|w|^{2}}\,dw\qquad(\zeta\in\mathcal{F}).

Note that since
\pi(n_{w}^{-})\eta
is an antiholomorphic polynomial in
w
, the integral converges for all
\zeta\in\mathcal{F}
. In view of the proof of Lemma
5.3
, the adjoint
A_{\eta}^{*}\in\Hom(\mathcal{V}_{\pi},\mathcal{F})
is given by

A_{\eta}^{*}\xi(z)=\langle\xi,\pi(n_{z}^{-})\circ\eta\rangle=\langle\pi(n_{z}^{+})^{-1}\xi,\eta\rangle\qquad(\xi\in\mathcal{V}_{\pi},z\in\mathfrak{n}_{\frac{1}{2}}).

We define
\widetilde{\Psi}_{\pi,\eta}:G\to\Hom(\mathcal{F}_{0},\mathcal{V}_{\pi})
at
s=p^{-}_{N}(s)k_{\mathbb{C},N}^{-}(s)n_{\mathbb{C}}^{-}(s)\in P^{-}K_{\mathbb{C}}N_{\mathbb{C}}
by

\widetilde{\Psi}_{\pi,\eta}(s)=\pi(k_{\mathbb{C},N}^{-}(s))\circ A_{\eta}\circ\omega(n_{\mathbb{C}}^{-}(s)).

By the
(N_{\mathbb{C}}\cap K_{\mathbb{C}}P^{-})
-equivariance of
A_{\eta}
, this is well-defined (cf. Remark
2.6
) and gives rise to a kernel function
\Psi_{\pi,\eta}:G\times\mathcal{D}\to\Hom(\mathcal{F}_{0},\mathcal{V}_{\pi})
by (
5.2
). For later purpose, we note the following formula for the adjoint
\Psi_{\pi,\eta}^{*}:G\times\mathcal{D}\to\Hom(\mathcal{V}_{\pi},\mathcal{F})
which follows from (
2.1
), (
3.1
) and (
4.7
):

\displaystyle\Psi_{\pi,\eta}(x,g\cdot o)^{*}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\Psi}_{\pi,\eta}(g^{-1}x)^{*}\circ j_{\pi}(g,o)^{-*}

\displaystyle=\omega(n_{\mathbb{C}}^{-}(g^{-1}x))^{*}\circ A_{\eta}^{*}\circ\pi(k_{\mathbb{C},N}^{-}(g^{-1}x))^{*}\circ j_{\pi}(g,o)^{-*}

\displaystyle=\omega(n_{\mathbb{C}}^{+}(g^{-1}x))^{-1}\circ A_{\eta}^{*}\circ\pi(k_{\mathbb{C},N}^{+}(g^{-1}x))^{-1}\circ j_{\pi}(g,o)^{-*}.

(5.3)

Using the notation

m:\mathcal{V}_{\pi}\otimes\overline{\mathcal{V}}_{\pi}\to\mathcal{F},\quad m(\xi\otimes\overline{\eta})(z)=\langle\pi(n_{z}^{+})^{-1}\xi,\eta\rangle\qquad(\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{V}_{\pi},z\in\mathfrak{n}_{\frac{1}{2}}),

(5.4)

so that
m(\xi\otimes\overline{\eta})
is a polynomial and hence contained in
\mathcal{F}_{0}
, this can be written as

\Psi_{\pi.,\eta}(x,g\cdot o)^{*}\xi=\omega(n_{\mathbb{C}}^{+}(g^{-1}x))^{-1}m\Big(\pi(k_{\mathbb{C},N}^{+}(g^{-1}x))^{-1}\circ j_{\pi}(g,o)^{-*}\xi\otimes\overline{\eta}\Big).

(5.5)

In particular,
\Psi_{\pi,\eta}(x,z)^{*}\in\Hom(\mathcal{V}_{\pi},\mathcal{F})
so
\Psi_{\pi,\eta}(x,z)\in\Hom(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{V}_{\pi})
instead of
\Hom(\mathcal{F}_{0},\mathcal{V}_{\pi})
.

In view of Corollary
5.6
, we define (whenever the integral converges)

T_{\pi,\eta}:\mathcal{H}_{\pi}^{\infty}\to C^{\infty}(G/N,\omega),\quad T_{\pi,\eta}F(x)=\int_{\mathcal{D}}\Psi_{\pi,\eta}(x,z)^{*}K_{\pi}(z,z)^{-1}F(z)\,d^{*}z.

In order to show that
T_{\pi,\eta}
defines an intertwining operator
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}\to L^{2}(G/N,\omega)
, it suffices to show that
T_{\pi,\eta}F\in L^{2}(G/N,\omega)
for
F
contained in the lowest
K
-type. Recall that the lowest
K
-type of
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
is spanned by the functions
z\mapsto K_{\pi}(z,o)\xi=\xi
for
\xi\in\mathcal{V}_{\pi}
.

Lemma 5.7
.

Identifying
\xi\in\mathcal{V}_{\pi}
with the constant function
z\mapsto\xi
, the restriction of
T_{\pi,\eta}
to
\mathcal{V}_{\pi}
is given by

T_{\pi,\eta}\xi(x)=\Psi_{\pi,\eta}(x,o)^{*}\xi=\omega(n_{\mathbb{C}}^{+}(x))^{-1}\circ A_{\eta}^{*}\circ\pi(k_{\mathbb{C},N}^{+}(x))^{-1}\xi\qquad(\xi\in\mathcal{V}_{\pi},x\in G).

Proof.

The first identity follows from the reproducing kernel property of
K_{\pi}
and the second identity is a special case of (
5.3
).
∎

In particular, for
x=k_{1}ak_{2}
with
k_{1}\in K
,
a\in A
and
k_{2}\in K\cap L
we find

T_{\pi,\eta}\xi(x)=\omega(k_{2}^{-1}n_{\mathbb{C}}^{+}(a)k_{2})^{-1}\circ A_{\eta}^{*}\circ\pi(k_{1}k_{\mathbb{C},N}^{+}(a)k_{2})^{-1}\xi.

By
\SU(1,1)
-reduction,
n_{\mathbb{C}}^{-}(a)
is contained in the center of
N_{\mathbb{C}}
for
a\in A
. Since
K\cap L
leaves
E\in\mathfrak{n}_{1}
invariant, it follows from (
4.3
) that
\omega(k_{2}^{-1}n_{\mathbb{C}}^{+}(a)k_{2})=\omega(n_{\mathbb{C}}^{+}(a))
. This implies:

T_{\pi,\eta}\xi(x)=\omega(n_{\mathbb{C}}^{+}(a))^{-1}\circ A_{\eta}^{*}\circ\pi(k_{2})^{-1}\circ\pi(k_{\mathbb{C},N}^{+}(a))^{-1}\circ\pi(k_{1})^{-1}\xi.

Theorem 5.8
.

T_{\pi,\eta}\xi\in L^{2}(G/N,\omega)
for some resp. all
\xi\in\mathcal{V}_{\pi}
if and only if
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
belongs to the holomorphic discrete series.

Proof.

We use the integral formulas

\int_{G/N}\varphi(x)\,d(xN)=\int_{K}\int_{L}\varphi(kl)l^{2\rho_{\mathfrak{n}}}\,dl\,dk

and

\int_{L}\varphi(l)\,dl=\int_{K\cap L}\int_{K\cap L}\int_{A}\varphi(k_{1}ak_{2})w(a)\,da\,dk_{1}\,dk_{2},

where

w(\exp H)=\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{a})}(\sinh\alpha(H))^{\dim\mathfrak{l}^{\alpha}}.

With this, we find

\int_{G/N}\|T_{\pi,\eta}\xi\|^{2}\,d(gN)=\int_{K}\int_{A}\int_{K\cap L}\|\omega(n_{\mathbb{C}}^{+}(a))^{-1}\circ\\
A_{\eta}^{*}\circ\pi(k_{2})^{-1}\circ\pi(k_{\mathbb{C},N}^{+}(a))^{-1}\circ\pi(k_{1})^{-1}\xi\|^{2}\,a^{2\rho_{n}}w(a)\,dk_{2}\,da\,dk_{1}.

By Schur’s orthogonality relations this equals up to a constant

\int_{A}\|\omega(n_{\mathbb{C}}^{+}(a))^{-1}\circ A_{\eta}^{*}\circ\pi(k_{\mathbb{C},N}^{+}(a))^{-1}\xi\|^{2}a^{2\rho_{\mathfrak{n}}}w(a)\,da.

(5.6)

Now, by the
\SU(1,1)
-reduction in Corollary
2.7
we find for
a=\exp\sum_{j=1}^{r}t_{j}x_{j}
:

\displaystyle k_{\mathbb{C},N}^{+}(a)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-\sum_{j=1}^{r}t_{j}h_{j}\right),

\displaystyle n_{\mathbb{C}}^{+}(a)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\frac{1}{2i}\sum_{j=1}^{r}(1-e^{-2t_{j}})\varphi_{j}\begin{pmatrix}i&-i\\
i&-i\end{pmatrix}\right).

By (
4.3
) we have

\omega(n_{\mathbb{C}}^{+}(a))^{-1}=\exp\left(-\sum_{j=1}^{r}(1-e^{-2t_{j}})\right).

Moreover, if
\xi\in\mathcal{V}_{\pi}
is a vector in the highest restricted weight space, then by (
3.5
):

\pi(k_{\mathbb{C},N}^{+}(a))^{-1}\xi=\left(\prod_{j=1}^{r}e^{-\mu_{\pi,j}t_{j}}\right)\xi.

It follows that (
5.6
) equals

\int_{t_{1}>\cdots>t_{r}}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\exp\left(-2(1-e^{-2t_{j}})-2\mu_{\pi,j}t_{j}+2\rho_{\mathfrak{n},j}t_{j}\right)\cdot w\left(\exp\sum_{j=1}^{r}t_{j}x_{j}\right)\,dt_{1}\cdots dt_{r}.

Because of the superexponential decay as
t_{j}\to-\infty
, it is sufficient to study the integral over
t_{1}>\cdots>t_{r}>0
. In this region, we can estimate
w(a)\sim a^{2\rho_{\mathfrak{l}}}
, so the integral under consideration becomes

\int_{t_{1}>\cdots>t_{r}>0}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\exp\left(-2(1-e^{-2t_{j}})-2\mu_{\pi,j}t_{j}+2\rho_{\mathfrak{n},j}t_{j}+2\rho_{\mathfrak{l},j}t_{j}\right)\,dt_{1}\cdots dt_{r}.

This integral clearly converges if and only if
\mu_{\pi,j}>\rho_{\mathfrak{n},j}+\rho_{\mathfrak{l},j}=\rho_{j}
for all
1\leq j\leq r
, which is precisely condition (
3.4
). Finally,
T_{\pi,\eta}\xi\in L^{2}(G/N,\omega)
holds for
\xi
in the highest restricted weight space if and only if it holds for all
\xi\in\mathcal{V}_{\pi}
by irreducibility.
∎

This shows that every
T_{\pi,\eta}
extends to
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}\to L^{2}(G/N,\omega)
. Because
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
is irreducible and unitary, this extension must be unitary (up to a scalar). As shown in Section
5.3
, every unitary intertwining operator
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}\to L^{2}(G/N,\omega)
must be of this form, so we have shown:

Corollary 5.9
.

For every
\eta\in\mathcal{V}_{\pi}
the operator
T_{\pi,\eta}
extends to a
G
-equivariant unitary (up to a scalar) operator

T_{\pi,\eta}:\mathcal{H}_{\pi}\to L^{2}(G/N,\omega).

Moreover, the map

\bigoplus_{\pi}\mathcal{H}_{\pi}\otimes\overline{\mathcal{V}_{\pi}}\to L^{2}(G/N,\omega),\quad\mathcal{H}_{\pi}\otimes\overline{\mathcal{V}_{\pi}}\ni F\otimes\overline{\eta}\mapsto T_{\pi,\eta}F

is an embedding onto the holomorphic discrete series contribution to the Plancherel decomposition of
L^{2}(G/N,\omega)
.

5.5.
Back to Whittaker vectors

Every unitary intertwining operator
T_{\pi,\eta}:\mathcal{H}_{\pi}\to L^{2}(G/N,\omega)
constructed in the previous section restricts to an operator
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}^{\infty}\to C^{\infty}(G/N,\omega)
, so the inequality in Corollary
5.4
is actually an equality. We now identify the corresponding Whittaker vectors with
\mathcal{F}
-valued antiholomorphic functions on
\mathcal{D}
.

As in
[
2
, Lemma 2.4]
, we identify a Whittaker vector
W\in\Hom_{N}(\mathcal{H}_{\pi}^{\infty},\mathcal{F})
with an antiholomorphic function
\Pi:\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{V}_{\pi}^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{F}
in the sense that

W(F)=\int_{\mathcal{D}}\Pi(z)\left(K_{\pi}(z,z)^{-1}F(z)\right)\,d^{*}z\qquad(F\in\mathcal{H}_{\pi}^{\infty}).

Let
\Pi_{\pi,\eta}
denote the function associated with the Whittaker vector which arises from the intertwiner
T_{\pi,\eta}
(in the sense of Section
5.1
), i.e.

T_{\pi,\eta}(F)(e)=\int_{\mathcal{D}}\Pi_{\pi,\eta}(z)\left(K_{\pi}(z,z)^{-1}F(z)\right)\,d^{*}z\qquad(F\in\mathcal{H}_{\pi}^{\infty}).

(5.7)

Proposition 5.10
.

As an element of
\Hom(\mathcal{V}_{\pi},\mathcal{F})\simeq\mathcal{V}_{\pi}^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{F}
, the following identity holds, where
z=g\cdot o
:

\Pi_{\pi,\eta}(z)=\Psi(e,z)^{*}=\omega(n_{\mathbb{C}}^{+}(g^{-1}))^{-1}\circ A_{\eta}^{*}\circ\pi(k_{\mathbb{C},N}^{+}(g^{-1}))^{-1}\circ j_{\pi}(g,o)^{-*}.

Proof.

The first identity is immediately clear from (
5.1
), Corollary
5.6
and (
5.7
). The second one is a special case of (
5.3
).
∎

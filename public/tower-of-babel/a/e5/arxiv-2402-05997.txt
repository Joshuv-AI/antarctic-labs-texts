I – Love – ⟨ c s 2 ⟩ \langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\ : Approximately universal relations for the average neutron star stiffness
Jayana A. Saes, Raissa F. P. Mendes, Nicolás Yunes

Abstract
The accurate observations of neutron stars have deepened our knowledge of both general relativity and the properties of nuclear physics at large densities. Relating observations to the microphysics that govern these stars can sometimes be aided by approximate universal relations. One such relation connects the ratio of the central pressure to the central energy density and the compactness of the star, and it has been found to be insensitive to realistic models for the equation of state to a ∼ 10 % \sim 10\% level. In this paper, we clarify the meaning of the microscopic quantity appearing in this relation, which is reinterpreted as the average of the speed of sound squared in the interior of a star, ⟨ c s 2 ⟩ \langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\ . The physical origin of the quasi-universality of the ⟨ c s 2 ⟩ − C \langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\ -C relation is then investigated. Using post-Minkowskian expansions, we find it to be linked to the Newtonian limit of the structure equations and the fact that the equations of state that describe NSs are relatively stiff. The same post-Minkowskian approach is also applied to the relations between ⟨ c s 2 ⟩ \langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\ , the moment of inertia, and the tidal deformability of a neutron star, in which cases the same degree of universality is found across post-Minkowskian orders.

Introduction

Neutron stars (NSs) are highly compact astrophysical objects, in which matter exists at extremely dense levels, with their maximum density possibly reaching up to
\sim
10 times the saturation density
\rho_{sat}=2.7\times 10^{14}
g/cm
3
. This unique environment renders a NS an ideal laboratory for fundamental physics, encompassing tests of general relativity (GR) and investigations into the equation of state (EoS) of supra-nuclear matter.
To realize this potential, accurate measurements of NS observables are needed.
Recent gravitational wave (GW) detections of binary NS mergers
Abbott et al. 2017
;
Abbott et al. 2018

Kramer et al. 2006

Weisberg et al. 2010

Next-generation GW observatories such as the Einstein Telescope
Maggiore et al. 2020

Evans et al. 2021

An important tool for the interpretation of NS observational data and its linkage to the microphysics governing the interior of these stars lies in approximate universal relations, i.e., EoS-insensitive relations between NS properties. One example is the I-Love-Q relations

II
p_{c}/\epsilon_{c}
as a measure of the average NS stiffness

Let us begin by providing a new interpretation for the ratio
p_{c}/\epsilon_{c}
between the central pressure and central energy density.
As argued in
Saes and Mendes 2022
, this ratio can be physically interpreted as a measure of the mean stiffness of nuclear matter inside a NS, indicative of the global growth of pressure with respect to the energy density.
Indeed, let us assume that the energy density is zero when the pressure is null, i.e.,
\epsilon(p=0)=0
, so that then

\displaystyle\frac{p_{c}}{\epsilon_{c}}=\frac{1}{\epsilon_{c}}\int_{0}^{\epsilon_{c}}\frac{dp}{d\epsilon}d\epsilon=\frac{1}{c^{2}\epsilon_{c}}\int_{0}^{\epsilon_{c}}c_{s}^{2}d\epsilon=:\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\ \,.

(1)

The quantity
\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\
is the dimensionless speed of sound squared averaged over the range of energy densities present inside the NS. This expression reinforces the interpretation provided in
Saes and Mendes 2022
since the speed of sound is a typical indicator of the stiffness of an EoS.

A visual representation of this average is shown in Fig.
2
, where we present the function
c_{s}^{2}(\epsilon)
for two representative EoSs. The filled circles in this figure indicate the central energy density and the maximum speed of sound inside a star with
C=0.2
. The speed of sound squared, averaged over the energy density, is then nothing but the “area under the curve” (i.e. the shaded regions) divided by the central energy density (because the energy density at the surface is zero).

The approximate universality between
\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\
and
C
is then connected to the fact that two NSs with different EoSs but the same compactness will typically have different central energy densities. As we can see from Fig.
2
, as one considers an EoS with a smaller stiffness, one naturally decreases the rate at which the speed of sound increases as a function of energy density in the NS interior. This, then, implies that, to obtain the same compactness as a NS with a stiffer EoS, the central energy density must be higher. This naturally increases the area under the
c_{s}^{2}
-
\epsilon
curve, in such a way that the ratio of this new area to the new central energy density is roughly unchanged. To beyond these qualitative considerations, in the remainder of this paper, we analyze the
\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\
–
C/\Lambda/\bar{I}
relations in a post-Minkowksian framework.

III
\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\
–
C
relation

To explore the underlying causes behind the quasi-universality of the
\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\
–
C
relation, in this section, we first assess its presence within analytic solutions for the TOV equation
Oppenheimer and Volkoff 1939
;
Tolman 1939
, namely, the incompressible, Tolman VII and Buchdahl solutions.
The common Newtonian limit of the
\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\
–

Finally, we contrast these simple models with realistic EoSs and discuss in which regime the results obtained for the former can be informative of the latter.

III.1
Exact Solutions in GR

While several known analytic solutions to the TOV equations exist, not all of them yield relevant descriptions for NSs. Here, as in
Lattimer and Prakash 2001
, we shall present the
\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\
–
C
relation for three exact solutions of the TOV equations: the incompressible fluid, a Tolman VII fluid, and a Buchdahl fluid. For concreteness, and to further establish notation, we review the TOV equations in GR in the appendix
A.1
. Detailed formulae for the models we consider can be found in appendix
A.3
.

III.1.1
Incompressible fluid

We first examine the incompressible case, which describes a fluid with constant density. The EoS is given by

\displaystyle\epsilon(p)

\displaystyle=\epsilon_{c}=\text{constant},

(2)

which leads to the simple analytic relation
1
1

1

Although an incompressible fluid does not satisfy the condition
\epsilon(p=0)=0
required for Eq. (
1
) to hold, and has a diverging speed of sound, throughout the paper we adhere to the definition
\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\ \equiv p_{c}/\epsilon_{c}
.
(cf. appendix
A.3
)

\displaystyle\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\ =\frac{p_{c}}{\epsilon_{c}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1-\sqrt{1-2C}}{3\sqrt{1-2C}-1}.

(3)

This can be expanded for small values of compactness to find

\displaystyle\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\ \approx\frac{C}{2}+C^{2}+\mathcal{O}(C^{3}).

(4)

Relation (
3
) presents some interesting, albeit well-known behavior. First, the averaged speed of sound diverges when
C\to 4/9
, which corresponds to the Buchdahl limit, i.e. an equilibrium sequence of incompressible solutions to the TOV equations ceases to exist at this value of compactness.
Second, the average speed of sound squared exceeds unity when
C>3/8
. For extremely non-relativistic stars, the average speed of sound tends to zero, and, for weakly-gravitating stars, it scales with compactness. The radius of convergence of the post-Minkowskian expansion (
4
) is simply the Buchdahl limit, as one can confirm by inspection or through the ratio test.

III.1.2
Tolman VII fluid

The Tolman VII solution, introduced in
Tolman 1939
, is characterized by the density profile

\displaystyle\epsilon(r)

\displaystyle=\epsilon_{c}\left[1-\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{2}\right].

(5)

Again, the averaged speed of sound squared can be computed analytically,

\displaystyle\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\ =\frac{1}{15}\left(\frac{2\sqrt{3}\tan\phi_{c}}{\sqrt{C}}-5\right),

(6)

where
\phi_{c}=\phi_{c}(C)
is given by Eq. (
84
).
This expression can be Taylor expanded in small
C
to obtain

\displaystyle\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\ \approx\frac{C}{2}+\frac{133C^{2}}{120}+\mathcal{O}(C^{3}).

(7)

From Eq. (
6
) we see that the central pressure, and consequently, the averaged squared speed of sound becomes infinite when the compactness reaches the point where
\phi_{c}(C)=\pi/2
, which corresponds to
C\approx 0.386
. This value of the compactness is lower than the Buchdahl limit of incompressible fluids, and it defines the end of the equilibrium branch of the Tolman VII solution. However, before diverging,
\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\
reaches and subsequently exceeds unity at
C\approx 0.335
, which sets the limit beyond which the fluid becomes superluminal, on average. The Taylor series (
7
) for this analytic relation converges for
C\lesssim 0.386
, as one can check with the ratio test, which coincides with the value beyond which the central pressure diverges.

III.1.3
Buchdahl fluid

The Buchdahl EoS
Buchdahl 1967
is given by

\displaystyle\epsilon(p)=12(p_{*}p)^{1/2}-5p,

(8)

where
p_{*}
is a constant. This equation of state reduces, in the Newtonian limit, to a
n=1
polytrope,
p\propto\epsilon^{2}
.

As shown in appendix
A.3
, the averaged speed of sound squared can be cast in this case as

\displaystyle\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\ =\frac{C}{2-5C},

(9)

and for small values of compactness:

\displaystyle\left<c_{s}^{2}\right>\approx\frac{C}{2}+\frac{5C^{2}}{4}+\mathcal{O}(C^{3}).

(10)

From Eq. (
9
) one sees that
\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\
diverges when
C=2/5
. Contrary to the previous cases, this is not due to the divergence of the central pressure [which is given by the regular expression (
91
)], but to the fact that the central energy density becomes null when
C=2/5
. This will be the limiting factor that marks the end of the equilibrium sequence for the Buchdahl solution.
Before that value, when
C=1/3
, the average squared speed of sound becomes greater than one, and the fluid becomes superluminal, on average. The expansion for low compactness values, Eq. (
10
), has a convergence radius of
C=2/5
, as expected from the analytic expression, and as can also be found via the ratio test.

As a final remark, notice that, for the Buchdahl solution, the relation between
\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\
and
C
, given in Eq. (
9
), is exactly the ratio of two linear polynomials, which succinctly captures both the
\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\ \to C/2
limit as
C\to 0
and the pole at
C=2/5
. This same structure, but suitably generalized, could provide useful fitting formulae for the case of realistic EoSs.

III.1.4
Comparing solutions

From the different
\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\
–
C
analytic relations found above, perhaps the most noteworthy feature is their common Newtonian limit.
The Taylor expansions of the analytic expressions shown above imply that
\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\ =C/2+{\cal{O}}(C^{2})
, and the coefficient of the
C^{2}
term is of order unity. This observation leads us to speculate that the emergence of universality in this limit may be attributed to the predominance of Newtonian effects over relativistic effects, at least for EoSs close to those considered here. To further explore this point, in the next subsection, we study the
\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\
–
C
relation in the Newtonian limit for two families of one-parameter EoSs, which interpolate between the models considered in this subsection. We will later perform a post-Minkowskian expansion to elucidate the role of GR in the quasi-universality.

III.2
One-Parameter Families of EoSs:

Newtonian Limit

Given the common Newtonian limit of the averaged speed of sound squared, regardless of the EoS considered above, it is of interest to study the Newtonian limit directly for other EoSs.
By “Newtonian limit,” we here mean the leading-order expansion of the equations of structure when
c\to\infty
, or equivalently, when
G\to 0
(see appendix
A.2
). Naturally, it is impractical to fully cover the space of EoS, as an infinite number of parameters would be needed. Instead, in what follows we present the
\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\
–
C
relation for 2 one-parameter families of EoS, namely, a polytropic EoS and a generalized version of the Tolman VII solution. As pictorially illustrated in Fig.
3

III.1

III.1

III.2.1
Polytropic EoSs

A polytropic EoS is defined as

\displaystyle p=K\,\rho^{1+\frac{1}{n}},

(11)

where
K
is the polytropic constant, and
n
is the polytropic index. For Newtonian polytropes, it is customary to define the dimensionless variables

\displaystyle\xi\equiv r/r_{0},\qquad\mu^{(N,P)}\equiv m/m_{0}^{(N,P)},

(12)

where

\displaystyle r_{0}^{2}\equiv\frac{(n+1)p_{c}}{4\pi G\rho_{c}^{2}},\qquad m_{0}^{(N,P)}=4\pi\rho_{c}r_{0}^{3},

(13)

are given in terms of the central rest mass density
\rho_{c}=\rho(r=0)
and the superscript
(N,P)
stands for “Newtonian polytrope.” The stellar radius is then given by
R=r_{0}\xi_{1}
and is determined by finding the value of the dimensionless variable
\xi
at which
p(\xi=\xi_{1})=0
. Similarly, the stellar mass is given by
M=m_{0}\mu_{1}^{(N,P)}
, where
\mu_{1}^{(N,P)}=\mu^{(N,P)}(\xi=\xi_{1})
.

With this in hand, we can now compute the averaged speed of sound squared. From Eq. (
13
) it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{Gm_{0}}{r_{0}c^{2}}=(n+1)\frac{p_{c}}{\rho_{c}c^{2}}=(n+1)\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\ ,

(14)

so that the stellar compactness is linearly related to
\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\
via

\displaystyle\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\ =C\left[(n+1)\frac{\mu^{(N,P)}_{1}}{\xi_{1}}\right]^{-1}.

(15)

In this expression, it is evident that both
(n+1)
and
\mu_{1}^{(N,P)}/\xi_{1}
are highly dependent of the EoS parameter
n
. Consequently, any universality must arise from the combination of both terms.

The polytropic index values that suitably approximate the behavior of realistic EoSs for NSs are limited to
n\in[0,1]
. For the boundaries,
n=0
(the incompressible limit) and
n=1
(the Newtonian limit of a Buchdahl fluid), the equations of hydrostatic equilibrium can be analytically solved to find

\displaystyle n=0\longrightarrow

\displaystyle\quad p(\xi)=p_{c}\left(1-\frac{\xi^{2}}{6}\right),\quad\mu^{(N,P)}(\xi)=\frac{\xi^{3}}{3},

\displaystyle\quad\xi_{1}=\sqrt{6},\quad\mu_{1}^{(N,P)}=2\sqrt{6}.

(16)

\displaystyle n=1\longrightarrow

\displaystyle\quad p(\xi)=p_{c}\left(\frac{\sin\xi}{\xi}\right)^{2},\quad\mu^{(N,P)}(\xi)=\sin\xi-\xi\cos\xi,

\displaystyle\quad\xi_{1}=\pi,\quad\mu_{1}^{(N,P)}=\pi.

(17)

Thus, one sees that in both cases

\displaystyle\left<c_{s}^{2}\right>=\frac{C}{2}\qquad(n=0\;{\textrm{or}}\;1).

(18)

In fact, by solving the Lane-Emden equation (i.e. the Newtonian version of the TOV equation in the reduced variables defined above) numerically for values of
n\in[0,1]
, we find that the maximum fractional difference in
\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\ \!/C
with respect to a fiducial EoS with
n=0.5
(the EoS median) is
\sim 3\%
.
This can be seen in Fig.
4
, where we show the ratio
\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\ \!/C
for the extended interval
n\in[0,2]
. This ratio is notably flat for
n\in[0,1]
, with a stationary point at
n\approx 0.454
, but rapidly increases for higher values of
n
, eventually diverging as
n\to 5
. It thus becomes clear that the universality of the
\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\
–
C
relation in the Newtonian limit is restricted to relatively stiff EoS (i.e., to relatively low values of
n
).

III.2.2
Generalized Tolman VII EoS

Let us now consider fixing the density profile through a generalized Tolman VII EoS, which we define as

\displaystyle\rho(r)=\rho_{c}\left[1-\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{k}\right]\,.

(19)

The constant
k
must here satisfy
k>1

k=2

5

k\to\infty

2

19

Let us define the dimensionless variables

\displaystyle x\equiv r/R,\qquad\mu^{(N,T)}\equiv\frac{m}{m_{0}^{(N,T)}},

(20)

where
R
is the stellar radius, and the superscript
(N,T)

\displaystyle m_{0}^{(N,T)}\equiv 4\pi\rho_{c}R^{3}.

(21)

Then, for arbitrary values of
k
, the equation of hydrostatic equilibrium and the equation for the enclosed mass can be solved analytically to find

p(x)=p_{c}\left[1-\frac{\beta}{\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\ }\left(\frac{x^{2}}{6}-\frac{x^{k+2}(k+6)}{3(k+2)(k+3)}+\frac{x^{2k+2}}{2(k+1)(k+3)}\right)\right],

(22)

\mu^{(N,T)}(x)=\frac{x^{3}}{3}-\frac{x^{k+3}}{k+3},

(23)

where anticipating the notation we will use in our post-Minkowskian calculations, we have defined
\beta\equiv Gm_{0}/Rc^{2}
.

With this in hand, we can now compute the average speed of sound squared. From the fact that
p(x=1)=0
, and considering that

\displaystyle C=\frac{GM}{Rc^{2}}=\beta\mu^{(N,T)}(1)=\beta\left(\frac{1}{3}-\frac{1}{k+3}\right),

(24)

one finds the simple relation

\displaystyle\left<c_{s}^{2}\right>=\left(2+\frac{1}{k}-\frac{3}{k+4}\right)^{-1}C,

(25)

from where we see that

\displaystyle\left<c_{s}^{2}\right>=\frac{C}{2}\quad\textrm{for }k=2\textrm{ and }k\to\infty.

(26)

For
k\in[2,\infty)
in Eq. (
25
), the maximum fractional difference in
\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\ \!/C
with respect to the EoS median is
\sim 6\%
, which characterizes the dependence of the relation with respect to
k
in such interval. Here and below, when referring to the generalized Tolman VII model, the median is defined using a uniform probability distribution in the variable
q=2/k\in[0,1]
, i.e., it refers to a fiducial model with
q=0.5
(
k=4
). The ratio
\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\ \!/C
is shown in Fig.
4
for the full range
k\in(1,\infty)
. Similarly to the polytropic case,
\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\ \!/C
is relatively flat over the range
k\in[2,\infty)
, displaying a stationary point between the incompressible and the Tolman VII solutions, when
k=2(1+\sqrt{3})\approx 5.46
, or equivalently,
q\approx 0.37
.

III.3
One-Parameter Families of EoSs:

Post-Minkowskian Expansion

In the previous subsections, we observed that the emergence of the quasi-universality of the
\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\
–
C
relation is rooted in the Newtonian limit and restricted to relatively stiff EoS. Furthermore, upon examining the exact solutions in GR, we noted that the contribution of higher-order terms becomes increasingly dependent on the EoS. To more precisely quantify the impact of GR on this dependence, we perform a post-Minkowskian expansion for the aforementioned one-parameter families of EoSs, which allows us to systematically study and analyze such effects semi-analytically.

III.3.1
Polytropic EoSs

The relativistic generalization of the polytropic family of EoS is

\displaystyle p(\rho)=K\rho^{1+\frac{1}{n}},\quad\text{with }\quad\epsilon=\rho c^{2}+np.

(27)

We will once more use the dimensionless quantities defined in Eq. (
12
),
\xi
and
\mu^{(P)}
here, where the superscript
(P)
indicates that we are referring to the polytropic EoS. Additionally, we use the definitions
\bar{p}\equiv p/p_{c}
,
\bar{\rho}\equiv\rho/\rho_{c}
,
\bar{\epsilon}\equiv\epsilon/(\rho_{c}c^{2})
, where the subscript
c
stands for the central value of a particular variable. The relativistic structure equations can then be written as

\displaystyle\frac{d\mu^{(P)}}{d\xi}=\xi^{2}\bar{\epsilon},\qquad\frac{d\bar{p}}{d\xi}=-(n+1)\frac{(\bar{\epsilon}+\sigma\bar{p})(\mu^{(P)}+\sigma\xi^{3}\bar{p})}{\xi^{2}(1-2(n+1)\sigma\mu^{(P)}/\xi)}.

(28)

The dimensionless parameter
\sigma\equiv p_{c}/(\rho_{c}c^{2})
, is related to the average speed of sound squared via

\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\ =\frac{p_{c}}{\rho_{c}c^{2}+np_{c}}=\frac{\sigma}{1+n\sigma}.

(29)

In the Newtonian limit, we have that
\sigma\ll 1
, and therefore
\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\ \sim\sigma\ll 1
which then implies that all three quantities (
\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\
,
\sigma
, and
C
) are natural expansion parameters in the post-Minkowskian approximation.

By considering
\sigma
as the small parameter, we can search for power series solutions of the form

\mu^{(P)}(\xi;\sigma)=\sum_{i=0}^{N}\mu_{(i)}^{(P)}(\xi)\sigma^{i}+\mathcal{O}(\sigma^{N+1}),

(30)

\bar{p}(\xi;\sigma)=\sum_{i=0}^{N}p_{(i)}(\xi)\sigma^{i}+\mathcal{O}(\sigma^{N+1}).

(31)

Additionally, the dimensionless radius of the star and dimensionless mass of the star can also be expanded in the form

\xi_{1}(\sigma)=\sum_{i=0}^{N}\xi_{1,i}\sigma^{i},\qquad\mu_{1}^{(P)}(\sigma)=\sum_{i=0}^{N}\mu_{1,i}^{(P)}\sigma^{i},

(32)

and can be obtained by solving the resulting equations perturbatively (see Appendix
B
for details).

The averaged speed of sound squared as a function of the compactness can now be found as follows. The compactness is obtained by evaluating

C=(n+1)\sigma\frac{\mu_{1}^{(P)}(\sigma)}{\xi_{1}(\sigma)}

(33)

as a power series in
\sigma
. This series can be rewritten in terms of
\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\
, and can subsequently be inverted into a power series in
C
. For example, for an
n=1
polytrope, the next-to-leading-order term in the
\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\
–
C
relation can be obtained analytically, and the subsequent terms can be obtained numerically, namely

\displaystyle\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\ \approx

\displaystyle\frac{C}{2}+\frac{C^{2}}{16}\left[8+9\text{Ci}(\pi)-9\text{Ci}(3\pi)+9\ln 3\right]+2.97C^{3}+\mathcal{O}(C^{4})

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{C}{2}+1.15C^{2}+2.97C^{3}+\mathcal{O}(C^{4}).

(34)

Observe that, similarly to the analytic solutions, the coefficient of the
C^{2}
term is of order unity.

The post-Minkowskian expansion of the averaged speed of sound with
n\in[0,1]
is shown in Fig.
5
, which demonstrates the effects of higher-order terms on the
\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\
–
C
relation. A significant observation derived from this plot is that incorporating the
C^{2}
and
C^{3}
terms results in an increased sensitivity to the EoS in the
\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\
–
C
relation. For a fiducial compactness of
C=0.2
, the maximal fractional deviation with respect to the EoS median, for polytropes with
n\in[0,1]
, raises from
\sim 3\%
at
\mathcal{O}(C)
(Newtonian limit) to
\sim 7\%
at
\mathcal{O}(C^{2})
,
\sim 9\%
at
\mathcal{O}(C^{3})
, and
\sim 17\%
in full GR. This observation is consistent with the notion that the insensitivity to the EoS (for
n\in[0,1]
), which stems from the Newtonian limit, deteriorates (i.e. the
\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\
–
C
relation becomes more sensitive to the EoS) as GR contributions are included.

III.3.2
Generalized Tolman VII

Let us now consider the case of the generalized Tolman VII density profile, defined in the relativistic regime as

\epsilon(r)=\epsilon_{c}\left[1-\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{k}\right],

(35)

where
k>1
. This family of solutions interpolates between the Tolman VII solution, for
k=2

k\to\infty
. Taking the Newtonian limit,
\epsilon=\rho c^{2}

20

21

m_{0}^{(T)}\equiv 4\pi\epsilon_{c}R^{3}.

(36)

We use the same definition of
\beta

\bar{p}=p/p_{c}

\bar{\epsilon}=\epsilon/\epsilon_{c}

\mu^{(T)}(x)=\frac{x^{3}}{3}-\frac{x^{k+3}}{k+3},

(37)

where the superscript
{(T)}
indicates the generalized Tolman VII solution and where once again the compactness and the parameter
\beta
are linearly related by

C=\frac{GM}{Rc^{2}}=\beta\mu^{(T)}(1)=\beta\left(\frac{1}{3}-\frac{1}{k+3}\right).

(38)

The dimensionless pressure then obeys the differential equation

\frac{d\bar{p}}{dx}=-\frac{\beta}{\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\ }\frac{(\bar{\epsilon}+\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\ \bar{p})(\mu^{(T)}+\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\ x^{3}\bar{p})}{x^{2}(1-2\beta\mu^{(T)}/x)}.

(39)

\bar{p}(x;C)=\sum_{i=0}^{N}p_{i}(x)C^{i},\qquad\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\ (C)=\sum_{i=0}^{N}d_{i+1}C^{i+1},

where we write the relevant boundary condition as
\bar{p}(0;C)=1
, while the requirement that
\bar{p}(1;C)=0
determines the coefficients in the expansion of
\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\ (C)
. For the generalized Tolman VII models, the structure equations can be solved analytically, order by order, for any

N

III.4
Comparison to Realistic EoSs

To conclude this section, let us assess how useful the results obtained above (for simple, one-parameter families of EoSs) can be for understanding the
\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\ \!-C
relation when using realistic EoSs. To address this question, we consider a set of 15 tabulated EoSs, which is the same as that employed in Fig.
1
. In particular, we consider the KDE0v, KDE0v1, Rs, SK255, SK272, SKI2-6, SkMp, SkOp, SLY2, SLY230a, and SLY9 EoSs, with tables obtained from the CompOSE repository.

Realistic EoSs encode the rich behavior of nuclear matter from the outer crust to the inner core. The adiabatic index,
\Gamma=d(\log p)/d(\log\rho)
predicted by these EoSs typically spans a large interval, starting from
\sim 1.33
at the outer crust, decreasing after neutron drip, and then increasing considerably (typically above 3) after the crust-core interface
Douchin and Haensel 2001
.
Naturally, a single polytrope is not capable of capturing the full behavior of the nuclear EoS, nor is the density profile fully described by a simple analytic function as in Eq. (
35
).
Still, it will be useful to construct effective polytropic and generalized Tolman VII models to NSs described by our set of realistic EoSs, in order to verify to which extent (or in which domain) our results for the former models can be used to build intuition for the latter.

From the many possible definitions of an effective polytropic index of an EoS inside a NS, we select the simplest one, consisting of

n_{\text{eff}}=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\rho_{c})–1}\,,

(42)

and thus,
n_{\text{eff}}
is fully determined by the adiabatic index at the center of the star. Additionally, we define an effective generalized-Tolman-VII exponent,
k_{\text{eff}}
, by fitting an expression of the form of Eq. (
35
) to the energy-density profile of a given NS. To assess the adequacy of these effective descriptions, we introduce the following error measure,

E=\sqrt{\left(\frac{\Delta\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\ }{\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\ }\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{\Delta C}{C}\right)^{2},}

(43)

where
\Delta
denotes the difference between the quantities predicted by the effective model (the polytropic or generalized Tolman VII mimickers) and the original tabulated EoSs, which is then divided by the quantity predicted by the original EoS.

as a function of the stellar compactness, for our set of tabulated EoSs, while the bottom panel shows the error

as defined in Eq. (
43
). In the range of compactness considered here,
n_{\text{eff}}\in(0,1)
for a polytropic description of the EoS, but it fails to reasonably describe the NS properties for
C\lesssim 0.1
, as the error
E
rapidly increases. Physically, this is due to the fact that the effective polytrope underestimates the stellar radius for low compactness NSs, for which the crust occupies a larger portion of the stellar volume. This can be visualized in Fig.

, where the energy density profile is represented for very low (
C=0.05
), medium (
C=0.2
), and high-compactness (
C=0.28
) NSs.

Analogously, an effective generalized Tolman VII model also fails to describe low compactness stars, with
k_{\text{eff}}
eventually dropping below 1, which is physically unacceptable. By construction, these models preserve the radius of the star but may fail to reasonably predict its mass (cf. Fig.

), giving rise to large errors for small values of
C
.
For
C\gtrsim 0.1
,
E\lesssim 0.1
and our results for these simple one-parameter families of EoSs can be extended to interpret the case of realistic EoSs.

IV
I – Love –
\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\

Alongside the near EoS-insensitive relation between
\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\
and
C
,
Saes and Mendes 2022
presented a similar relation between the average speed of sound squared and both the dimensionless moment of inertia
\bar{I}
and the dimensionless tidal deformability
\Lambda
, which is insensitive to the EoS at the
\sim 10\%
level for realistic EoSs. Having explored the origins of the quasi-universality of the
\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\
–
C
relation, and arguing that, for relevant models that describe the NS interior, this quasi-universality emerges from the Newtonian limit and subsequently decays with increasing contributions of GR, we will now investigate the
\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\
–
\bar{I}/\Lambda
relations. Due to the lack of analytic solutions in GR for both these quantities, we start by analyzing the relations in the Newtonian regime and proceed to study their corresponding post-Minkowskian corrections.

Before we begin, let us define the dimensionless quantities that shall be used throughout this section. The dimensionless moment of inertia and the dimensionless tidal deformability are respectively defined as

\displaystyle\bar{I}\equiv\frac{c^{4}I}{G^{2}M^{3}},\qquad\Lambda=\frac{2k_{2}}{3C^{5}},

(44)

where
I
is the moment of inertia and
k_{2}
is the
l=2
tidal Love number.

IV.1
Newtonian Limit

To delve into the question of the role of the Newtonian limit in the
\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\
–
\bar{I}/\Lambda
relations, we study them in this limit and compare any universality, to that present in the
\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\
–
C
relation, or lack thereof.

The moment of inertia in the Newtonian regime, which we shall name
I^{(N)}
, is defined as

I^{(N)}=\frac{8\pi}{3}\int_{0}^{R}r^{4}\rho(r)dr.

(45)

The tidal deformability is found through the perturbation of a non-rotating equilibrium state. If a static quadrupolar tidal field perturbs such state, the Eulerian change
h_{2}(r)
to the gravitational potential can be found through the Newtonian limit of Eq. (15) in
Hinderer 2008
:

\frac{d^{2}h_{2}}{dr^{2}}+\frac{2}{r}\frac{dh_{2}}{dr}-\left[\frac{6}{r^{2}}-4\pi G\rho\frac{d\rho}{dp}\right]h_{2}=0,

(46)

and noticing that regularity at
r=0
implies that
h_{2}(r)\sim r^{2}
as
r\to 0
. Defining
y\equiv Rh_{2}^{\prime}(R)/h_{2}(R)
, the Newtonian
l=2
Love number can be computed from

k_{2}^{(N)}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{2-y}{y+3}\right),

(47)

which is then related to the dimensionless tidal deformability via Eq. (
44
).

IV.1.3
Comparing solutions

The
\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\ \!-\bar{I}/\Lambda

\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\ \!-C
relation, at least in the Newtonian regime. The variation in the

The parallels between the
\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\ \!-\bar{I}

\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\ \!-\Lambda

IV.2
Post-Minkowskian Expansion

To assess whether increasing GR contributions will lead to a reduction in the universality for both
\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\ \!-\bar{I}/\Lambda
relations, we perform a post-Minkowskian expansion. This enables us to derive GR contributions in an order-by-order manner, facilitating a more precise assessment of the impact of each contribution. In this section, this will be done for the generalized Tolman VII models alone, defined in Eq. (
35
).

The relativistic moment of inertia can be derived under the context of slow rotation, as originally outlined by Hartle
Hartle 1967
:

I=\frac{8\pi R^{5}}{3}\int_{0}^{1}dx\,x^{4}\frac{(\epsilon+p)}{c^{2}}e^{(\lambda-\nu)/2}\bar{\omega}.

(62)

Here,
g_{tt}=-e^{\nu}
and
g_{rr}=e^{\lambda}
are metric coefficients describing the unperturbed (i.e., non-rotating) spacetime, while
g_{t\phi}=-\Omega(1-\bar{\omega})r^{2}\sin^{2}\theta
describes the perturbation due to rotation (to first order in the angular velocity
\Omega
). The function
\bar{\omega}(x)
, with
x\equiv r/R
, obeys

\frac{1}{x^{4}}\frac{d}{dx}\left(\,x^{4}j\frac{d\bar{\omega}}{dx}\right)+\frac{4}{x}\frac{dj}{dx}\bar{\omega}=0,

(63)

with
j\equiv e^{-(\nu+\lambda)/2}
, along with the boundary conditions
d\bar{\omega}/dx|_{x=0}=0
and
\lim_{x\to\infty}\bar{\omega}(x)=1
.

The function
\lambda(x)
, for the generalized Tolman VII models, can be directly derived from Eq. (
37
) as
\lambda(x)=-\log(1-\beta\mu/x)
. For the metric potentials
\nu
and
\bar{\omega}
, we employ a power series approximation analogous to Eq. (
40
):

\nu(x;C)=\sum_{i=0}^{N}\nu_{i}(x)C^{i},\qquad\bar{\omega}(x;C)=\sum_{i=0}^{N}\bar{\omega}_{i}(x)C^{i}.

(64)

We can now solve Eq. (
63
) and the corresponding equation for
\nu
,

\frac{d\nu}{dx}=-\frac{2\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\ }{\bar{\epsilon}+\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\ \bar{p}}\frac{d\bar{p}}{dx}\,,

(65)

analytically, order by order in
C
, which allows the relativistic moment of inertia (
62
) to be computed in successive post-Minkowskian approximations. In the Newtonian limit (i.e. when
N=0
), we recover Eq. (
IV.1.2
) for the dimensionless quantity
\bar{I}

The relation
\bar{I}-\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\

\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\

\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\

approaching infinity, or, equivalently,

V
Conclusions

We have here explored the underlying physics responsible for the approximately universal relations between the ratio of the central pressure to the central energy density,
p_{c}/\epsilon_{c}
, and dimensionless astrophysical observables, namely, the compactness
C
, the tidal deformability
\Lambda
and the moment of inertia
\bar{I}
of NSs
Saes and Mendes 2022
.
The quantity
p_{c}/\epsilon_{c}
was reinterpreted as the squared speed of sound averaged over the range of energy densities present inside the star,
\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\ \!
.
The averaged speed of sound squared has a natural interpretation as a measure of the mean stiffness of the EoS up to the central energy density of a given NS.

We first studied the
\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\ \!-C
relation using analytic solutions of the TOV equation for an incompressible fluid, a Tolman VII profile, and the Buchdahl fluid. We found that, albeit quite different from each other, these EoSs led to
\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\ \!-C
relations with the same Newtonian limit,
\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\ =C/2
. This prompted further study of the Newtonian limit and its relativistic corrections, which we performed for 2 one-parameter families of EoSs that interpolate between the (Newtonian limit of the) models considered previously: polytropic EoSs and a generalized Tolman VII profile. For the range of parameters most relevant for the description of NSs, the
\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\ \!-C
relation was shown to be remarkably flat (with respect to EoS variation) in the Newtonian limit. This insensitivity to the EoS then deteriorated as relativistic corrections were included. For instance, for polytropic EoSs with
n\in[0,1]
, the maximal fractional difference of the
\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\ \!-C
relation with respect to the EoS median increased from
3\%
at
\mathcal{O}(C)
(Newtonian limit), to
9\%
at
\mathcal{O}(C^{3})
and 17% in full GR.

When contrasting realistic EoSs to the one-parameter families considered previously, we found that the latter provides reasonable approximants to the former, as long as the compactness of the NS is sufficiently large (
C\gtrsim 0.1
). Thus, the EoS insensitivity of the
\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\ \!-C
relation, for realistic EoSs, can be linked to EoSs being relatively stiff throughout a large portion of the NS for sufficiently large values of the stellar compactness.

In contrast to the case of the
\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\ \!-C
relations, we found that the approximate universality of the
\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\ \!-\bar{I}/\Lambda
relations is not enhanced in the Newtonian limit. Indeed, for the generalized Tolman VII profile with
k\in[2,\infty)
, we showed that the EoS dependence remains at the
10-11\%
level to all orders in compactness. This is also the case when using realistic EoSs, as long as the NS is sufficiently compact.

Our work suggests a few avenues for future work. One possibility would be to study potential approximately universal relations between
\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\
and the quadrupole and higher-order multipole moments of rotating NSs. This could be done by considering the slow-rotation expansion
Hartle 1967
, and constructing equilibrium sequences of stars with fixed rotational periods. Such a study would also benefit from the post-Minkowskian analysis we laid out in this work.

Appendix A
Equations of structure and analytical solutions

A.1
General Relativity

The relativistic equation of hydrostatic equilibrium is

\displaystyle\frac{dp}{dr}=-\frac{G}{c^{2}}(\epsilon+p)\frac{m+4\pi r^{3}p/c^{2}}{r\left(r-\frac{2Gm}{rc^{2}}\right)}\,,

(68)

where
m
is the enclosed mass,
p
is the internal pressure,
\epsilon
is the energy density, and
r
is the radial coordinate. The enclosed mass satisfies the equation:

\displaystyle\frac{dm}{dr}=4\pi r^{2}\frac{\epsilon}{c^{2}}\,.

(69)

These two equations form a closed system once an EoS is prescribed and their solution requires boundary conditions. As usual, we impose regularity at the center of the star, enforcing
m(r=0)=0
, while
p(r=0)=p_{c}
, where
p_{c}
is the central pressure. With these conditions, the radius of the star
R
is defined as the radial coordinate at which the pressure vanishes,
p(r=R)=0
, while the stellar mass is the enclosed mass evaluated at this radial coordinate
M=m(r=R)
.

A.2
Newtonian Limit

Using that in the Newtonian limit
\epsilon=\rho c^{2}
, where
\rho
is the rest mass density, Eqs. (
68
) and (
69
) reduce to

\displaystyle\frac{dp}{dr}=-\rho\frac{m}{r^{2}},\qquad\frac{dm}{dr}=\rho\frac{Gm}{r^{2}}.

(70)

Just as in the relativistic case, these equations must be closed by choosing an EoS and prescribing boundary conditions.

A.3
Analytical solutions to the TOV equations

In this section we derive expressions (
3
), (
6
), and (
9
) for the
\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\
–
C
relation for an incompressible fluid, a Tolman VII density profile and the Buchdahl EoS, respectively.

A.3.1
Incompressible fluid

A constant-density profile

\displaystyle\epsilon=\epsilon_{c}=\text{constant}

(71)

results in the following analytic solutions to the TOV (
68
) and the enclosed mass (
69
) equations

\displaystyle p(r)

\displaystyle=\frac{\epsilon_{c}\left(R\sqrt{1-2C}-\sqrt{R^{2}-2Cr^{2}}\right)}{\sqrt{R^{2}-2Cr^{2}}-3R\sqrt{1-2C}},\quad m(r)=\frac{4}{3}\pi r^{3}\frac{\epsilon_{c}}{c^{2}},

(72)

where the central energy density
\epsilon_{c}
can be expressed in terms of the stellar mass and radius by

\displaystyle\epsilon_{c}=\frac{3Mc^{2}}{4\pi R^{3}}.

(73)

Evaluating
p(r=0)
, we obtain

\displaystyle p_{c}=\epsilon_{c}\frac{1-\sqrt{1-2C}}{3\sqrt{1-2C}-1},

(74)

which leads to the simple analytic relation

\displaystyle\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\ =\frac{p_{c}}{\epsilon_{c}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1-\sqrt{1-2C}}{3\sqrt{1-2C}-1}.

(75)

A.3.2
Tolman VII fluid

The Tolman VII solution
Tolman 1939
is characterized by the density profile

\displaystyle\epsilon(r)

\displaystyle=\epsilon_{c}\left[1-\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{2}\right].

(76)

To simplify the resulting analytic expressions presented in
Jiang and Yagi 2020
, we introduce the dimensionless variable
x=r/R
. The mass aspect function and pressure can be expressed as follows:

\displaystyle m(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{4\pi\epsilon_{c}}{R^{3}}\left(\frac{x^{3}}{3}-\frac{x^{5}}{5}\right),

(77)

\displaystyle p(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{c^{4}}{4\pi GR^{2}}\left[\sqrt{3Ce^{-\lambda}}\tan\phi-\frac{C}{2}(5-3x^{2})\right],

(78)

where
\epsilon_{c}
can be expressed in terms of
R
and the stellar mass
M
, or, alternatively, the compactness
C
, as

\displaystyle\epsilon_{c}=\frac{15Mc^{2}}{8\pi R^{3}}=\frac{15c^{4}C}{8\pi GR^{2}}.

(79)

The functions
\lambda(x)
and
\phi(x)
are given by

\displaystyle e^{-\lambda(x)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-Cx^{2}\left(5-3x^{2}\right),

(80)

\displaystyle\phi(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k-\frac{1}{2}\log\left(x^{2}-\frac{5}{6}+\sqrt{\frac{e^{-\lambda}}{3C}}\right),

(81)

where

\displaystyle k=\arctan\sqrt{\frac{C}{3(1-2C)}}+\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\frac{1}{6}+\sqrt{\frac{1-2C}{3C}}\right).

(82)

At the center of the star (
x=0
), we have that
\lambda=0
and

\displaystyle p_{c}=\frac{\epsilon_{c}}{15}\left(\frac{2\sqrt{3}\tan\phi_{c}}{\sqrt{C}}-5\right),

(83)

where

\phi_{c}=\phi(0)=k-\frac{1}{2}\log\left(1/\sqrt{3C}-5/6\right).

(84)

All of the above allows us to easily calculate the averaged speed of sound squared analytically, namely

\displaystyle\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\ =\frac{1}{15}\left(\frac{2\sqrt{3}\tan\phi_{c}}{\sqrt{C}}-5\right).

(85)

A.3.3
Buchdahl fluid

The Buchdahl EoS
Buchdahl 1967
is given by

\displaystyle\epsilon(p)=12(p_{*}p)^{1/2}-5p,

(86)

where
p_{*}
is a constant. With this EoS, one can find an analytic solution to the TOV equation, which, as presented in
Lattimer and Prakash 2001
, is

\displaystyle p(r)=\frac{c^{4}}{8\pi G}A^{2}u^{2}(1-2C)(1-C+u)^{-2},

(87)

where

\displaystyle r

\displaystyle=r^{\prime}(1-C+u)(1-2C)^{-1},

(88)

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=C(Ar^{\prime})^{-1}\sin Ar^{\prime},

(89)

\displaystyle A^{2}

\displaystyle=288\pi p_{*}Gc^{-4}(1-2C)^{-1}.

(90)

At the center of the star,
r=r^{\prime}=0
and
u=C
; thus, the central pressure can be expressed as

\displaystyle p_{c}=36p_{*}C^{2},

(91)

and, from Eq. (
8
), the central energy density is

\displaystyle\epsilon_{c}=72p_{*}C\left(1-\frac{5C}{2}\right).

(92)

With all of the above, we can now compute the averaged speed of sound square as

\displaystyle\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\ =\frac{C}{2-5C}.

(93)

Appendix B
Post-Minkowskian expansion of
C-\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\
for polytropic EoSs

Differently from the generalized Tolman VII density profile, for which the post-Minkowskian expansion of the
\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\ \!-C
relation can be found analytically, in the case of a generic polytropic EoS, the
\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\ \!-C
relation must be obtained numerically. In this Appendix, we sketch the numerical procedure and discuss certain subtleties that render the straightforward computation of high post-Minkowskian coefficients of that expansion numerically challenging.

The compactness
C
is related to
\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\
by

C=(n+1)\sigma\frac{\mu_{1}^{(P)}(\sigma)}{\xi_{1}(\sigma)},

(94)

where
\sigma=(1/\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\ -n)^{-1}
[cf. Eq. (
29
)], which can be expanded to any desired order. Here,
\mu_{1}^{(P)}(\sigma)=\mu^{(P)}(\xi_{1}(\sigma))
, and the dimensionless stellar radius,
\xi=\xi_{1}
, is determined by

\bar{p}(\xi_{1}(\sigma);\sigma)=0.

(95)

Inserting the Taylor expansion of Eq. (
32
) for
\xi_{1}(\sigma)
in the above equation, we can solve the latter order by order in a post-Minkowskian expansion. For example, at Newtonian order, one obtains

p_{(0)}(\xi_{1,0})=0,

(96)

which implicitly defines
\xi_{1,0}
. The coefficient
\xi_{1,i}
with
i\geq 1
then depends on the respective pressure coefficient
p_{(i)}
, as well as on derivatives of the lower-order pressure coefficients (
p_{(j)}
,
j<i
), evaluated at
\xi_{1,0}
. Explicitly, for
i=1,2
, one has

\displaystyle\xi_{1,1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{p_{(1)}(\xi_{1,0})}{p^{\prime}_{(0)}(\xi_{1,0})},

(97)

\displaystyle\xi_{1,2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{p_{(2)}(\xi_{1,0})+\xi_{1,1}p^{\prime}_{(1)}(\xi_{1,0})+\frac{1}{2}\xi_{1,1}^{2}p^{\prime\prime}_{(0)}(\xi_{1,0})}{p^{\prime}_{(0)}(\xi_{1,0})}.

(98)

The numerical procedure then consists of the following steps:

(i)

numerically solving the Newtonian equations for
p_{(0)}
and
\mu_{(0)}^{(P)}
, and extracting the value of
\xi_{1,0}
such that Eq. (
96
) holds, as well as
\mu_{1,0}^{(P)}
;

(ii)

numerically solving the equations for
p_{(i)}
,
\mu_{(i)}^{(P)}
, for
i\geq 1
up to the desired order in the fixed domain
\xi\in[0,\xi_{1,0}]
, and computing the coefficients
\xi_{1,i}
and
\mu_{1,i}^{(P)}
;

(iii)

computing the
C-\langle c_{s}^{2}\rangle\penalty\
relation from the Taylor-expanded version of Eq. (
94
).

Now, it can be shown that, around
\xi=\xi_{1,0}
, the
i
-th pressure coefficient has the leading behavior
p_{(i)}\propto(\xi-\xi_{1,0})^{n-i+1}
. In the range of interest
0\leq n\leq 1
, Eq. (
97
) presents a
0/0
indeterminacy, and must be computed by taking the limit of that expression as
\xi\to\xi_{1,0}
. This limit is expected to be finite and is easily handled numerically. However, in the same range
0\leq n\leq 1
, Eq. (
98
) displays an apparent divergence in the limit
\xi\to\xi_{1,0}
, since the numerator goes as
(\xi-\xi_{1,0})^{n-1}
and the denominator goes as
(\xi-\xi_{1,0})^{n}
. A careful analysis of the differential equation for
p_{(2)}
shows, however, that the coefficient of the dominant term in the numerator of Eq. (
98
), i.e.,
\propto(\xi-\xi_{1,0})^{n-1}
, vanishes exactly, so that the true leading-order behavior of both the numerator and denominator is
(\xi-\xi_{1,0})^{n}
, yielding a finite value for the ratio in the limit
\xi\to\xi_{1,0}
. A similar situation is expected for higher-order coefficients (
\xi_{1,i}
,
i>2
).
However, numerically the cancellation of diverging terms described above is not guaranteed due to the inevitable presence of numerical errors, and a higher-precision calculation may be required.

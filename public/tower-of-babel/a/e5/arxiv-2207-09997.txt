Some identities on degenerate r r -Stirling numbers via boson operators
Taekyun Kim, DAE SAN KIM

Abstract
. Broder introduced the r r -Stirling numbers of the first kind and of the second kind which enumerate restricted permutations and respectively restricted partitions, the restriction being that the first r r elements must be in distinct cycles and respectively in distinct subsets. Kim-Kim-Lee-Park constructed the degenerate r r -Stirling numbers of both kinds as degenerate versions of them. The aim of this paper is to derive some identities and recurrence relations for the degenerate r r -Stirling numbers of the first kind and of the second kind via boson operators. In particular, we obtain the normal ordering of a degenerate integral power of the number operator multiplied by an integral power of the creation boson operator in terms of boson operators where the degenerate r r -Stirling numbers of the second kind appear as the coefficients.

1.
Introduction

The
r
-Stirling numbers of the first kind and of the second kind enumerate restricted permutations and respectively restricted partitions, the restriction being that the first
r
elements must be in distinct cycles and respectively in distinct subsets (see [2]).

Carlitz initiated a study of degenerate versions of some special numbers and polynomials (see [3]), where the degenerate Bernoulli and Euler numbers were investigated. It is remarkable that in recent years intensive studies have been done for degenerate version of quite a few special polynomials and numbers and have yielded many interesting results (see [7-15]).

The normal ordering of an integral power of the number operator
a^{+}a
in terms of boson operators
a
and
a^{+}
can be written in the form

(a^{+}a)^{k}=\sum_{l=0}^{k}S_{2}(k,l)(a^{+})^{l}a^{l},

where
S_{2}(k,l)
are the Stirling numbers of the second kind (see (
2
)).
The normal ordering of the degenerate
k
th power of the number operator
a^{+}a
, namely
(a^{+}a)_{k,\lambda}
, in terms of boson operators
a
and
a^{+}
can be written in the form

(a^{+}a)_{k,\lambda}=\sum_{l=0}^{k}S_{2,\lambda}(k,l)(a^{+})^{l}a^{l},

where
S_{2,\lambda}(k,l)
are the degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind (see (
5
)) and the generalized falling factorials
(x)_{n,\lambda}
are given by (
3
).

The aim of this paper is to derive some identities and recurrence relations for the degenerate
r
-Stirling numbers of the first kind and of the second kind via boson operators. In particular, we obtain the normal ordering of a degenerate integral power of the number operator multiplied by an integral power of the creation boson operator in trems of boson operators where the degenerate
r
-Stirling numbers of the second kind appear as the coefficients. In addition, we derive recurrence relations for the degenerate
r
-Stirling numbers of both kinds from certain normal orderings which generalize aforementioned ones.

The outline of this paper is as follows. In Section 1, we recall the Stirling numbers of both kinds, the unsigned Stirling numbers of the first kind, the degenerate Stirling numbers of both kinds, and the unsigned degenerate Stirling numbers of the first kind. We remind the reader of the normal ordering of an integral power of the number operator in terms of boson operators and its degenerate version, namely the normal ordering of a degenerate integral power of the number operator. We also recall the degenerate
r
-Stirling numbers of both kinds and the unsigned degenerate
r
-Stirling numbers of the first kind. Section 2 is the main result of this paper. Let
D=\frac{d}{dx}

applied to the formal power series

with the coefficients given by the degenarate

(a^{+}a)_{n,\lambda}(a^{+})^{r}=\sum_{l=0}^{n}{n+r\brace l+r}_{r,\lambda}(a^{+})^{l+r}a^{l},

where

In Theorem 3, we find that the differential operator

r

It is well known that the Stirling numbers of the first kind are defined by

(1)

(x)_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{1}(n,k)x^{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [4-17]),

where
(x)_{0}=1,\ (x)_{n}=x(x-1)\cdots(x-n+1),\ (n\geq 1)
.

As the inversion formula of (
1
), the Stirling numbers of the second kind are given by

(2)

x^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2}(n,k)(x)_{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [4,5,17]).

The unsigned Stirling number of the first kind is defined by

{n\brack k}=(-1)^{n-k}S_{1}(n,k),\quad(n,k\geq 0).

For any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, the degenerate factorial sequence is defined by

(3)

(x)_{0,\lambda}=1,\quad(x)_{n,\lambda}=x(x-\lambda)(x-2\lambda)\cdots(x-(n-1)\lambda),\quad(n\geq 1),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [8,10]).

In [8], the degenerate Stirling numbers of the first kind are defined by

(4)

(x)_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{1,\lambda}(n,k)(x)_{k,\lambda},\quad(n\geq 0).

In view of (
2
), the degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind are given by

(5)

(x)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{2,\lambda}(n,k)(x)_{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [8]).

The unsigned degenerate Stirling numbers of the first kind are given by

(6)

\langle x\rangle_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brack k}_{\lambda}\langle x\rangle_{k,\lambda},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [13-15]),

where

\langle x\rangle_{n}=(-1)^{n}(-x)_{n}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\langle x\rangle_{n,\lambda}=(-1)^{n}(-x)_{n,\lambda},\quad(n\geq 0).

Note that

{n\brack k}_{\lambda}=(-1)^{n-k}S_{1,\lambda}(n,k).

We note that the boson annihilation and creation operators
a
and
a^{+}
satisfy the commutation relation given by

(7)

[a,a^{+}]=aa^{+}-a^{+}a=1,\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [1,6,9,11]).

In the corresponding Fock space, we have

(8)

a^{+}|k\rangle=\sqrt{k+1}|k+1\rangle,\quad a|k\rangle=\sqrt{k}|k-1\rangle,\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [1,12]).

Now, the number operator
\hat{n}
is defined by

(9)

\hat{n}|k\rangle=k|k\rangle.

By (
8
), we get
a^{+}a|k\rangle=k|k\rangle
, that is
\hat{n}=a^{+}a
. Thus, we note that

[a,\hat{n}]=a\hat{n}-\hat{n}a=a,\quad[\hat{n},a^{+}]=\hat{n}a^{+}-a^{+}\hat{n}=a^{+}.

The normal ordering of an integral power of the number operator
\hat{n}=a^{+}a
in terms of boson operators
a
and
a^{+}
can be written in the form

(10)

(a^{+}a)^{k}=\sum_{l=0}^{k}S_{2}(k,l)(a^{+})^{l}a^{l},\quad(k\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [1,6,16]).

Recently, the normal ordering of the degenerate
k
-th power of the number operator
a^{+}a
, namely
(a^{+}a)_{k,\lambda}
, in terms of boson operators
a
and
a^{+}
can be written in the form

(11)

(a^{+}a)_{k,\lambda}=\sum_{l=0}^{k}S_{2,\lambda}(k,l)(a^{+})^{l}a^{l},\quad(k\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [12]).

For
r\geq 0
, the unsigned
r
-Stirling numbers are defined by

(12)

\langle x+r\rangle_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n+r\brack k+r}_{r}x^{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [13-15]),

where

\langle x\rangle_{n}=x(x+1)\cdots(x+n-1),\quad(n\geq 1),\quad\langle x\rangle_{0}=1.

In view of (
2
), the
r
-Stirling numbers of the second kind are given by

(13)

(x+r)^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n+r\brace k+r}_{r}(x)_{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [14,15]).

Recently, the authors introduced the unsigned degenerate
r
-Stirling numbers of the first kind, which are given by

(14)

\langle x+r\rangle_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n+r\brack k+r}_{r,\lambda}\langle x\rangle_{k,\lambda},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [13-15]),

In view of (
5
), the degenerate
r
-Stirling numbers of the second kind are defined by the authors, which are given by

(15)

(x+r)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n+r\brace k+r}_{r,\lambda}(x)_{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [13-15]).

In addition, the degenerate
r
-Stirling numbers of the first kind are given by

(16)

(x-r)_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{1,\lambda}^{(r)}(n+r,k+r)(x)_{k,\lambda},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [13,15]).

Note that

{n+r\brack k+r}_{r,\lambda}=(-1)^{n-k}S_{1,\lambda}^{(r)}(n+r,k+r),\quad(n\geq 0).

In this paper, we study the degenerate
r
-Stirling numbers associated with the number operator
\hat{n}=a^{+}a
in terms of boson operators
a
and
a^{+}
that satisfy
[a,a^{+}]=aa^{+}-a^{+}a=1
.

That is, we derive some identities involving the degenerate
r
-Stirling numbers arising from the normal ordering of degenerate integral powers of number operator.
\hat{n}=a^{+}a
in terms of boson operators
a
and
a^{+}
, which are given by

(a^{+}a+r)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n+r\brace k+r}_{r,\lambda}(a^{+})^{k}a^{k}.

2.
Some identities on degenerate
r
-Stirling numbers via boson operators

Let
f(x)
be a formal power series in
x
with coefficients in
\mathbb{C}
, given by
f(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}x^{n}
. For
r\geq 0
, we observe that

(17)

\displaystyle(xD)_{m,\lambda}x^{r}f(x)

\displaystyle=(xD)(xD-\lambda)\cdots(xD-(m-1)\lambda)x^{r}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}x^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}(k+r)_{m,\lambda}x^{k+r}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}\sum_{l=0}^{m}{m+r\brace l+r}_{r,\lambda}(k)_{l}x^{k+r}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{m}{m+r\brace l+r}_{r,\lambda}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}(k)_{l}x^{k}x^{r}=\sum_{l=0}^{m}{m+r\brace l+r}_{r,\lambda}x^{l+r}f^{(l)}(x),

where

f^{(l)}(x)=\bigg(\frac{d}{dx}\bigg)^{l}f(x),\quad D=\frac{d}{dx}.

From now on throughout this paper,
D
will denote the operator
D=\frac{d}{dx}
.

Now, by (
17
), we get

(18)

(xD)_{m,\lambda}x^{r}f(x)=\sum_{l=0}^{m}{m+r\brace l+r}_{r,\lambda}\Big(D^{l}f(x)\Big)x^{l+r}.

In particular, for
m,r\geq 0
with
m\geq r
, we have

(19)

(xD)_{m-r,\lambda}x^{r}f(x)=\sum_{l=r}^{m}{m\brace l}_{r,\lambda}\Big(D^{l-r}f(x)\Big)x^{l}.

Replacing
f(x)
by
f^{(r)}(x)
, we get

(20)

(xD)_{m-r,\lambda}x^{r}D^{r}f(x)=\sum_{l=r}^{m}{m\brace l}_{r,\lambda}x^{l}D^{l}f(x).

Therefore, by (
18
) and (
20
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 1
.

For
m,r\in\mathbb{Z}
with
m,r\geq 0
, we have

(xD)_{m,\lambda}x^{r}f(x)=\sum_{l=0}^{m}{m+r\brace l+r}_{r,\lambda}x^{l+r}D^{l}f(x).

In addition, for
m,r\in\mathbb{Z}
with
m\geq r\geq 0
, we have

(xD)_{m-r,\lambda}x^{r}D^{r}f(x)=\sum_{l=r}^{m}{m\brace l}_{r,\lambda}x^{l}D^{l}f(x).

Note that
Dx-xD=1
. We recall that the bosonic commutation relation
[a,a^{+}]=aa^{+}-a^{+}a=1
can be realized formally in a suitable space of functions by letting
a=\frac{d}{dx}
and
a^{+}=x
.
Therefore we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 2
.

For
m,r\in\mathbb{Z}
with
m,r\geq 0
, we have

(a^{+}a)_{m,\lambda}(a^{+})^{r}=\sum_{l=0}^{m}{m+r\brace l+r}_{r,\lambda}(a^{+})^{l+r}a^{l}.

In addition, for
m,r\in\mathbb{Z}
with
m\geq r\geq 0
, we have

(a^{+}a)_{m-r,\lambda}(a^{+})^{r}a^{r}=\sum_{l=r}^{m}{m\brace l}_{r,\lambda}(a^{+})^{l}a^{l}.

By (
15
), we get

(21)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}{n+r+1\brace k+r}_{r,\lambda}(x)_{k}

\displaystyle=(x+r)_{n+1,\lambda}=(x+r)_{n,\lambda}(x+r-n\lambda)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n+r\brace k+r}_{r,\lambda}(x)_{k}(x-k+k+r-n\lambda)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n+r\brace k+r}_{r,\lambda}(x)_{k+1}+\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n+r\brace k+r}_{r,\lambda}(k+r-n\lambda)(x)_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}\bigg({n+r\brace k+r-1}_{r,\lambda}+(k+r-n\lambda){n+r\brace k+r}_{r,\lambda}\bigg)(x)_{k}.

Comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
21
), we have

{n+r+1\brace k+r}_{r,\lambda}={n+r\brace k+r-1}_{r,\lambda}+(k+r-n\lambda){n+r\brace k+r}_{r,\lambda},

where
n,k\in\mathbb{Z}
with
n+1\geq k\geq 0
.

Now, we observe that

(22)

\displaystyle x^{k}D^{k}f(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}(n)_{k}x^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}\bigg(\sum_{m=0}^{k}S_{1,\lambda}^{(r)}(k+r,m+r)(n+r)_{m,\lambda}\bigg)x^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{k}S_{1,\lambda}^{(r)}(k+r,m+r)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}(n+r)_{m,\lambda}x^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{k}S_{1,\lambda}^{(r)}(k+r,m+r)\bigg(x\frac{d}{dx}+r\bigg)_{m,\lambda}f(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{k}(-1)^{k-m}{k+r\brack m+r}_{r,\lambda}\bigg(x\frac{d}{dx}+r\bigg)_{m,\lambda}f(x).

Therefore, by (
22
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 3
.

For
k\geq 0
, we have

(23)

x^{k}D^{k}f(x)=\sum_{m=0}^{k}(-1)^{k-m}{k+r\brack m+r}_{r,\lambda}(xD+r)_{m,\lambda}f(x).

In (
23
), the normal ordering of degenerate integral power of the number operator
\hat{n}a^{+}a
in terms of boson operators
a^{+}
and
a
can be presented in the form

(24)

\displaystyle(a^{+})^{k}a^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{k}(-1)^{k-m}{k+r\brack m+r}_{r,\lambda}(a^{+}a+r)_{m,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{k}(-1)^{k-m}{k+r\brack m+r}_{r,\lambda}(\hat{n}+r)_{m,\lambda}.

For
k,r\in\mathbb{Z}
with
k\geq r\geq 0
, by (
24
), we get

(25)

\displaystyle(a^{+})^{k-r}a^{k-r}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=r}^{k}(-1)^{k-m}{k\brack m}_{r,\lambda}(a^{+}a+r)_{m-r,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=r}^{k}(-1)^{k-m}{k\brack m}_{r,\lambda}(\hat{n}+r)_{m-r,\lambda}.

Therefore, by (
24
) and (
25
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 4
.

For
k,r\in\mathbb{Z}
with
k\geq r\geq 0
, we have

(a^{+})^{k}a^{k}=\sum_{m=0}^{k}(-1)^{k-m}{k+r\brack m+r}_{r,\lambda}(\hat{n}+r)_{m,\lambda},

and

(a^{+})^{k-r}a^{k-r}=\sum_{m=r}^{k}(-1)^{k-m}{k\brack m}_{r,\lambda}(\hat{n}+r)_{m-r,\lambda}.

As the inversion formula of (
23
), we consider the following differential equation:

(26)

\displaystyle\bigg(x\frac{d}{dx}+r\bigg)_{n,\lambda}f(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{m}\bigg(x\frac{d}{dx}+r\bigg)_{n,\lambda}x^{m}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{m}(m+r)_{n,\lambda}x^{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{m}\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n+r\brace k+r}_{r,\lambda}(m)_{k}x^{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n+r\brace k+r}_{r,\lambda}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{m}(m)_{k}x^{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n+r\brace k+r}_{r,\lambda}x^{k}\bigg(\frac{d}{dx}\bigg)^{k}f(x).

Therefore, by (
26
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 5
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

(27)

(xD+r)_{n,\lambda}f(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n+r\brace k+r}_{r,\lambda}x^{k}D^{k}f(x).

From Theorems 1 and 5, we note that

(28)

x^{r}(xD+r)_{n,\lambda}=(xD)_{n,\lambda}x^{r},\quad(n\geq 0).

Thus, by (
28
), we get

(29)

(a^{+})^{r}(a^{+}a+r)_{n,\lambda}=(a^{+}a)_{n,\lambda}(a^{+})^{r},\quad(n\geq 0).

From (
27
), we have

(30)

(a^{+}a+r)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n+r\brace k+r}_{r,\lambda}(a^{+})^{k}a^{k}.

Therefore, by (
29
) and (
30
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 6
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

(a^{+})^{r}(a^{+}a+r)_{n,\lambda}=(a^{+}a)_{n,\lambda}(a^{+})^{r},

and

(31)

(a^{+}a+r)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n+r\brace k+r}_{r,\lambda}(a^{+})^{k}a^{k}.

By (
31
), we get

(32)

\displaystyle a^{+}(\hat{n}+r+1-\lambda)_{k,\lambda}a

\displaystyle=a^{+}\sum_{l=0}^{k}\binom{k}{l}(\hat{n}+r)_{l,\lambda}(1-\lambda)_{k-l,\lambda}a

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{k}\binom{k}{l}(1-\lambda)_{k-l,\lambda}\sum_{m=0}^{l}{l+r\brace m+r}_{r,\lambda}(a^{+})^{m+1}a^{m+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{k}\sum_{l=m}^{k}\binom{k}{l}(1-\lambda)_{k-l,\lambda}{l+r\brace m+r}_{r,\lambda}(a^{+})^{m+1}a^{m+1}.

On the other hand, by (
30
) and (
32
), we get

(33)

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{k+1}{k+1+r\brace m+r}_{r,\lambda}(a^{+})^{m}a^{m}=(\hat{n}+r)_{k+1,\lambda}=(\hat{n}+r)(\hat{n}+r-\lambda)_{k,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\hat{n}(\hat{n}+r-\lambda)_{k,\lambda}+r(\hat{n}+r-\lambda)_{k,\lambda}=a^{+}(\hat{n}+r+1-\lambda)_{k,\lambda}a+r(\hat{n}+r-\lambda)_{k,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{k}\sum_{l=m}^{k}\binom{k}{l}(1-\lambda)_{k-l,\lambda}{l+r\brace m+r}_{r,\lambda}(a^{+})^{m+1}a^{m+1}+r\sum_{l=0}^{k}\binom{k}{l}(-\lambda)_{k-l,\lambda}(\hat{n}+r)_{l,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{k+1}\sum_{l=m-1}^{k}\binom{k}{l}(1-\lambda)_{k-l,\lambda}{l+r\brace m-1+r}_{r,\lambda}(a^{+})^{m}a^{m}+r\sum_{l=0}^{k}\binom{k}{l}(-\lambda)_{k-l,\lambda}\sum_{m=0}^{l}{l+r\brace m+r}_{r,\lambda}(a^{+})^{m}a^{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{k+1}\sum_{l=m-1}^{k}\binom{k}{l}(1-\lambda)_{k-l,\lambda}{l+r\brace m+r-1}_{r,\lambda}(a^{+})^{m}a^{m}+\sum_{m=0}^{k}\bigg(r\sum_{l=m}^{k}\binom{k}{l}(-\lambda)_{k-l,\lambda}{l+r\brace m+r}_{r,\lambda}\bigg)(a^{+})^{m}a^{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{k+1}\bigg(\sum_{l=m-1}^{k}\binom{k}{l}(1-\lambda)_{k-l,\lambda}{l+r\brace m+r-1}_{r,\lambda}+r\sum_{l=m}^{k}\binom{k}{l}(-\lambda)_{k-l,\lambda}{l+r\brace m+r}_{r,\lambda}\bigg)(a^{+})^{m}a^{m}.

Therefore, by comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
33
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 7
.

For
m,k\in\mathbb{Z}
with
k\geq m\geq 0
, we have

{k+1+r\brace m+r}_{r,\lambda}=\sum_{l=m-1}^{k}\binom{k}{l}(1-\lambda)_{k-l,\lambda}{l+r\brace m+r-1}_{r,\lambda}+r\sum_{l=m}^{k}\binom{k}{l}(-\lambda)_{k-l,\lambda}{l+r\brace m+r}_{r,\lambda}.

We observe that

(34)

\displaystyle\hat{n}(\hat{n}+r-1)_{k,\lambda}

\displaystyle=a^{+}a(a^{+}a+r-1)_{k,\lambda}

\displaystyle=a^{+}a(a^{+}a+r-1)(a^{+}a+r-1-\lambda)\cdots(a^{+}a+r-1-(k-1)\lambda)

\displaystyle=a^{+}(aa^{+}+r-1)a(a^{+}a+r-1-\lambda)(a^{+}a+r-1-2\lambda)\cdots(a^{+}a+r-1-(k-1)\lambda)

\displaystyle=a^{+}(aa^{+}+r-1)(aa^{+}+r-1-\lambda)a(a^{+}a+r-1-2\lambda)\cdots(a^{+}a+r-1-(k-1)\lambda)

\displaystyle=\cdots

\displaystyle=a^{+}(aa^{+}+r-1)(aa^{+}+r-1-\lambda)(aa^{+}+r-1-2\lambda)\cdots(aa^{+}+r-1-(k-1)\lambda)a

\displaystyle=a^{+}(a^{+}a+r)(a^{+}a+r-\lambda)(a^{+}a+r-2\lambda)\cdots(a^{+}a+r-(k-1)\lambda)a

\displaystyle=a^{+}(a^{+}a+r)_{k,\lambda}a=a^{+}(\hat{n}+r)_{k,\lambda}a.

Therefore, by (
34
), we obtain the following lemma.

Lemma 8
.

For
k\geq 0
, we have

(35)

\hat{n}(\hat{n}+r-1)_{k,\lambda}=a^{+}(\hat{n}+r)_{k,\lambda}a.

From (
35
) and Theorem 6, we note that

(36)

\displaystyle a^{+}(\hat{n}+r)_{k,\lambda}a=\hat{n}(\hat{n}+r-1)_{k,\lambda}=\sum_{m=0}^{k}\binom{k}{m}(-1)_{k-m,\lambda}\hat{n}(\hat{n}+r)_{m,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{k}\binom{k}{m}(-1)^{k-m}\langle 1\rangle_{k-m,\lambda}(\hat{n}+r-m\lambda+m\lambda-r)(\hat{n}+r)_{m,\lambda}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{k}\binom{k}{m}(-1)^{k-m}\langle 1\rangle_{k-m,\lambda}\Big((\hat{n}+r)_{m+1,\lambda}+(m\lambda-r)(\hat{n}+r)_{m,\lambda}\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{k}\binom{k}{m}(-1)^{k-m}\langle 1\rangle_{k-m,\lambda}(\hat{n}+r)_{m+1,\lambda}

\displaystyle\qquad+\lambda\sum_{m=0}^{k}\binom{k}{m+1}(-1)^{k-m-1}\langle 1\rangle_{k-m-1,\lambda}(m+1)(\hat{n}+r)_{m+1,\lambda}

\displaystyle\qquad-r\sum_{m=0}^{k}\binom{k}{m}(-1)^{k-m}\langle 1\rangle_{k-m,\lambda}(\hat{n}+r)_{m,\lambda}

The Stirling numbers of the second kind appear as the coefficients in the normal ordering of an integral power of the number operator in terms of the boson operators. Recently, it was shown that the degenrate Stirling numbers of the second kind appear as the coefficients in the normal ordering of a degenerate integral power of the number operator in terms of boson operators.

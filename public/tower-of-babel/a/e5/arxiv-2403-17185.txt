Magnon-microwave backaction noise evasion in cavity magnomechanics Preprint: APS/123-QED
V.A.S.V. Bittencourt, C.A. Potts, J. P. Davis, A. Metelmann

Abstract
In cavity magnomechanical systems, magnetic excitations couple simultaneously with mechanical vibrations and microwaves, combining the tunability of the magnetization, the long lifetimes of mechanical modes and the whole measurement toolbox of microwave systems. Such hybrid systems have been proposed for applications ranging from thermometry to entanglement generation. However, backaction noise can hinder the measurement of the mechanical vibrations, potentially rendering such applications infeasible. In this paper, we investigate the noise introduced in a mechanical mode of a cavity magnomechanical system in a one-tone drive scheme and propose a scheme for realizing backaction evasion measurements of the mechanical vibrations. Our proposal consists of driving the microwave cavity with two tones separated by twice the phonon frequency and with amplitudes balanced to generate equal numbers of coherent magnons. We demonstrate that different configurations of such a scheme are possible and show that drives centered around the lower frequency magnon-microwave polariton in a triple resonance scheme add the minimum imprecision noise in the measurement, even though such configuration is not the most robust to imperfections.



I
Introduction

One of the most iconic features of quantum mechanics is the disturbance of a system due to measurements
Braginsky et al. 1980
. The random nature of quantum measurements performed in a system of interest via an apparatus implies the addition of measurement noise to the system to be probed
Clerk et al. 2010
, which can hinder applications such as sensing. Backaction evasion (BAE) schemes often rely on engineering a Hamiltonian, which is quantum non-demolition (QND) in an observable of the system to be measured. This means that the measurement of such an observable does not disturb its evolution. The price to be paid is strong noise contamination in non-commuting observables. Such engineered interaction can be realized in hybrid systems architecture, a prominent example being the BAE scheme for measuring mechanical vibration in optomechanical systems
Clerk et al. 2008
;
Woolley and Clerk 2013
;
Aspelmeyer et al. 2014
;
Yanay and Clerk 2017
;
Hauer et al. 2018
;
Shomroni et al. 2019
. Other examples include electromechanical systems
Hertzberg et al. 2010
;
Lecocq et al. 2015
;
Ockeloen-Korppi et al. 2016
, atomic ensembles coupled to mechanical resonators
Møller et al. 2017
, Bose-Einstein condensates
Altuntaş and Spielman 2023
, and superconducting qubits coupled to microwave cavities
Gambetta et al. 2006
.

Cavity magnomechanical systems have recently emerged as a promising platform for quantum technologies
Zhang et al. 2016
;
Rameshti et al. 2022
. In such systems, a magnetic element, usually made of yttrium iron garnet, is loaded into a microwave cavity, as we depict in Fig.
1
. The magnetic excitation (magnons) couple simultaneously to the microwaves (via magnetic dipole coupling) and to the elastic vibrations of the material (via magnetoelastic effects)
LeCraw et al. 1961
;
Spencer and LeCraw 1958
;
Zhang et al. 2016
;
Gonzalez-Ballestero et al. 2020
. Such a system allows the drive and measurement of phonons via the microwave resonator while retaining the tunability of the magnons. Among the potential applications proposed for such systems are the generation of entangled states
Li et al. 2018
;
Li and Gröblacher 2021
;
Fan et al. 2022
, the generation of squeezing of magnons and phonons via magnon nonlinearities
Li et al. 2022
;
Ding et al. 2022
, noise-based thermometry
Potts et al. 2020
and microwave-to-optical frequency conversion
Engelhardt et al. 2022
.

Due to a frequency mismatch, the coupling between magnons and phonons in cavity magnomechanical systems resembles that of an optomechanical system
Zhang et al. 2016
;
Gonzalez-Ballestero et al. 2020
. Consequently, the mechanical oscillator experiences
dynamical backaction

Potts et al. 2021
;
Zhang et al. 2016
, namely, a frequency shift and modified decay due to its interaction with the magnons. Different from its optomechanical counterpart, magnons hybridize with microwaves, changing some characteristics of dynamical backaction. Recently, it was experimentally shown
Potts et al. 2023
that (classical) dynamical backaction can be evaded in such systems by judiciously choosing the microwave drive frequency. Nevertheless, up to date, there is no (quantum) BAE scheme tailored for cavity magnomechanical systems, a feature that could allow further applications of such systems at the quantum level, for instance, the measurement of entangled states that can be engineered in such systems
Amazioug et al. 2023
;
Li et al. 2018
.

In this paper, we propose a scheme for evading quantum backaction in a cavity magnomechanical system. We first characterize the magnomechanical noise introduced in the phonon mode in a one-tone setup, in correspondence with the recent experiments in which
dynamical backaction
is evaded. We then propose a scheme inspired by BAE schemes in opto and electromechanics
Clerk et al. 2008
;
Woolley and Clerk 2013
;
Hertzberg et al. 2010
in which a two-tone drive applied to the microwave cavity realizes a QND Hamiltonian for a phonon quadrature. Our scheme requires that the tone frequencies are separated by twice the phonon frequency, as shown in Fig.
1
(b), and the amplitudes of the drives have to be balanced to generate the same number of coherent magnons. The tone frequency separation provides a modulation of the magnomechanical force, which in turn yields a quantum non-demolition Hamiltonian involving one quadrature of the phonon mode
Braginsky et al. 1980
. We study the robustness of the system to imperfections of the parameters and quantify the noise added to the measurement of the mechanics. We show that for drives centered around the hybrid magnon-microwave modes frequency, the imprecision noise can go below the standard quantum limit. The drawback is that the BAE is less robust to imperfections, for example, slight deviations in the drive frequencies, for such configurations. Two-tone drive schemes have been proposed for magnomechanical systems to generate squeezing
Zhang et al. 2021
;
Qian et al. 2024
and induce magnonic frequency combs
Liu et al. 2023
, and our scheme might allow future applications of cavity magnomechanical systems that rely on quantum features of the phonon mode.

This paper is structured as follows. In Section
II
we present a brief description of a cavity magnomechanical Hamiltonian, and calculate the backaction noise acting on a quadrature of the phonon mode for a single tone drive. We show in this section that the dynamical backaction evasion point demonstrated in
Potts et al. 2023
does not correspond to a quantum BAE point. In Section
III
we present our BAE scheme, and quantify the noise that acts on the quadrature conjugated to the BAE quadrature. In section
IV
, we compute the imprecision noise added to the measurement of the BAE quadrature via the output of the microwave mode. Finally, we present our conclusions and outline of the work in section
V
. We present full formulas and some detailed derivations in the appendix.

III
Scheme for backaction evasion

The backaction noise can be eliminated from a quadrature of the phonon mode by considering a microwave drive containing two tones. In such a scheme the amplitude quadrature
\hat{x}(t)=(e^{-i\omega_{b}t}\hat{b}+e^{i\omega_{b}t}\hat{b})/\sqrt{2}
of the phonon field will be a BAE quadrature, completely free of noise due to the coupling to magnons at all times. The drive term to be included in the magnomechanical Hamiltonian is

\frac{\hat{H}_{\rm{drive}}}{\hbar}=i\sqrt{\kappa_{e}}(\epsilon_{-}e^{-i\delta t}+\epsilon_{+}e^{i\delta t})e^{i\omega_{d}t}\hat{c}+\rm{H.c.}

(28)

This term describes two coherent tones applied to the microwave mode with frequencies
\omega_{d}\pm\delta
. To linearize the corresponding equations of motion, we again move to a rotating frame at the central drive frequency
\omega_{d}
and make the ansatz
\hat{c}=\hat{c}_{0}+\hat{c}_{+}e^{-i\delta t}+\hat{c}_{-}e^{i\delta t}
and
\hat{m}=\hat{m}_{0}+\hat{m}_{+}e^{-i\delta t}+\hat{m}_{-}e^{i\delta t}
. We show the full set of equations for the expectation values
\langle\hat{o}_{i}\rangle=o_{i}
(
o=m,

c
, and
i=0,\pm
) in the appendix. The steady-state solutions
\bar{o}_{i}
are

\displaystyle\bar{c}_{0}

\displaystyle=\bar{m}_{0}=0,

(29)

\displaystyle\bar{c}_{\pm}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{\kappa_{e}}\epsilon_{\pm}\left(i(\Delta_{m}\pm\delta)-\frac{\kappa_{m}}{2}\right)}{\left(i(\Delta_{m}\pm\delta)-\frac{\kappa_{m}}{2}\right)\left(i(\Delta_{c}\pm\delta)-\frac{\kappa_{c}}{2}\right)+g_{mc}^{2}},

\displaystyle\bar{m}_{\pm}

\displaystyle=\frac{ig_{mc}\sqrt{\kappa_{e}}\epsilon_{\pm}}{\left(i(\Delta_{m}\pm\delta)-\frac{\kappa_{m}}{2}\right)\left(i(\Delta_{c}\pm\delta)-\frac{\kappa_{c}}{2}\right)+g_{mc}^{2}}.

We have assumed that the single-magnon magnomechanical coupling is small, so we can ignore it in obtaining the mean-field steady-state solutions. The detunings with respect to the central drive frequency are given by
\Delta_{c,m}=\omega_{d}-\omega_{c,m}
, which we call from now on just detunings.

We linearize the two-tone drive Hamiltonian by considering fluctuations around the steady-state given by Eqs. (
29
):
\hat{\alpha}=\delta\hat{\alpha}+\bar{\alpha}_{+}e^{-i\delta t}+\bar{\alpha}_{-}e^{i\delta t}
for
\alpha=c,m
. We drop the deltas indicating the fluctuations and keep only the quadratic terms of the Hamiltonian. In an interacting frame with respect to
\omega_{b}\hat{b}^{\dagger}\hat{b}
, the Hamiltonian reads

\displaystyle\frac{\hat{H}_{L}^{(2)}}{\hbar}

\displaystyle=-\Delta_{c}\hat{c}^{\dagger}\hat{c}-\Delta_{m}\hat{m}^{\dagger}\hat{m}+g_{mc}\left(\hat{m}^{\dagger}\hat{c}+\hat{m}\hat{c}^{\dagger}\right)

(30)

\displaystyle+e^{i\delta t}\left(g_{-}\hat{m}^{\dagger}+g_{+}^{*}\hat{m}\right)\left(\hat{b}e^{-i\Omega_{b}t}+\hat{b}^{\dagger}e^{i\Omega_{b}t}\right)

\displaystyle+e^{-i\delta t}\left(g_{+}\hat{m}^{\dagger}+g_{-}^{*}\hat{m}\right)\left(\hat{b}e^{-i\Omega_{b}t}+\hat{b}^{\dagger}e^{i\Omega_{b}t}\right),

where we have defined
g_{\pm}=g_{mb}^{0}\bar{m}_{\pm}
.

Figure 6:

Since
\delta
is positive, the interacting terms are resonant provided that
\delta=\omega_{b}
, in other words, if the two tones are separated by twice the phonon frequency as we schematically shown in Fig.
6
.
With this choice, a QND Hamiltonian for a phonon quadrature can be obtained by an appropriate choice of the magnomechanical coupling rates
g_{\pm}=|g_{\pm}|e^{i\phi_{\pm}}
. If coupling rates have the same modulus,

|g_{-}|=|g_{+}|=G,

(31)

we get after some algebraic manipulations

\displaystyle\frac{\hat{H}_{\rm{QND}}}{\hbar}

\displaystyle=-\Delta_{c}\hat{c}^{\dagger}\hat{c}-\Delta_{m}\hat{m}^{\dagger}\hat{m}+g_{mc}\left(\hat{m}^{\dagger}\hat{c}+\hat{m}\hat{c}^{\dagger}\right)

(32)

\displaystyle+G\left(e^{i\varphi}\hat{m}^{\dagger}+e^{-i\varphi}\hat{m}\right)\left(e^{i\psi}\hat{b}^{\dagger}+e^{-i\psi}\hat{b}\right),

where
\varphi=(\phi_{+}+\phi_{-})/2
and
\psi=(\phi_{+}-\phi_{-})/2
. The above Hamiltonian is QND with respect to the phonon quadrature

\hat{x}_{b,\psi}=\frac{e^{i\psi}\hat{b}^{\dagger}+e^{-i\psi}\hat{b}}{\sqrt{2}},

(33)

meaning that the above quadrature is completely unaffected by the coupling to the magnons and by any measurement process done in the magnon-microwave part of the system. This quadrature is thus a BAE quadrature.

The requirement set in Eq. (
31
) corresponds to
|\bar{m}_{+}|=|\bar{m}_{-}|
; in other words, the drives induce the same amount of coherent magnons. Such a requirement implies the following relation between the drive amplitudes

\frac{|\epsilon_{+}|}{|\epsilon_{-}|}=\frac{|\left(i(\Delta_{m}+\omega_{b})-\frac{\kappa_{m}}{2}\right)\left(i(\Delta_{c}+\omega_{b})-\frac{\kappa_{c}}{2}\right)+g_{mc}^{2}|}{|\left(i(\Delta_{m}-\omega_{b})-\frac{\kappa_{m}}{2}\right)\left(i(\Delta_{c}-\omega_{b})-\frac{\kappa_{c}}{2}\right)+g_{mc}^{2}|}.

(34)

We note that only at zero detuning
\Delta_{m}=\Delta_{c}=0
, the above equation implies an equal amplitude of both drives
|\epsilon_{-}|=|\epsilon_{+}|
. The phase
\psi
is given by the relative phase of the magnon steady-state solutions

\psi=\frac{{\rm{arg}}\left[\bar{m}_{+}\right]-{\rm{arg}}\left[\bar{m}_{-}\right]}{2},

(35)

which can be tuned by, for example, a relative phase between the drive amplitudes
\epsilon_{\pm}
.

Unlike dynamical backaction evasion, the quantum nondemolition Hamiltonian does not require the detunings to vanish. Nevertheless, to obtain Eq. (
32
) we have adopted a RWA for discarding terms rotating at twice the phonon frequency. In this case, even at zero detuning, this RWA is not exact, and we discuss and evaluate the effects of the counter-rotating terms at zero detuning in the appendix. We show that, for the parameters considered here, the corrections that the counter-rotating terms introduce in the integral of the mechanics noise spectrum is
\sim 10^{-4}
, and therefore constitute a small correction that can be safely ignored. At finite detunings, the analysis is more involved, but we expect that the same conclusion still holds.

III.1
BAE phonon noise spectrum: orthogonal quadrature

In the last section, we have shown that a careful tuning of the two tones frequencies and amplitudes yields a QND Hamiltonian for the phonon quadrature (
33
). In turn, the conjugated quadrature

\hat{p}_{b,\psi}=\frac{i\left(e^{i\psi}\hat{b}^{\dagger}-e^{-i\psi}\hat{b}\right)}{\sqrt{2}},

(36)

will be driven by the magnon-microwave noise. To quantify how much noise is dumped into
\hat{p}_{b,\psi}
, we calculate its noise spectrum. We start with the Heisenberg-Langevin equations for the phonon operator in the frequency domain, which reads

\displaystyle\left(\chi_{b}^{-1}[\omega]-i\Sigma_{b}[\omega]\right)e^{-i\psi}\hat{b}[\omega]=ie^{i\psi}\Sigma_{b}[\omega]\hat{b}^{\dagger}[\omega]

(37)

\displaystyle-iGe^{i\varphi}\hat{\xi}_{{\rm{BA}}}^{\dagger}[\omega]-iGe^{-i\varphi}\hat{\xi}_{{\rm{BA}}}[\omega]+\sqrt{\gamma_{b}}\hat{\xi}_{b}[\omega],

where, because now we are in an interacting frame of the phonon mode, the susceptibility
\chi_{b}
is given by

\displaystyle\chi_{b}[\omega]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{-i\omega+\frac{\gamma_{b}}{2}}.

(38)

The phonon self-energy
\Sigma_{b}
is given by Eq. (
11
) with the substitution
g_{mb}\rightarrow G
, and the backaction noise operator
\hat{\xi}_{\rm{BA}}
is given by Eq. (
16
). We then get the following equations for the BAE quadrature and its canonical conjugated quadrature

\displaystyle\chi_{b}^{-1}[\omega]\hat{x}_{b,\psi}[\omega]

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\gamma_{b}}\hat{\xi}_{x_{b,\psi}}[\omega],

(39)

\displaystyle\chi_{b}^{-1}[\omega]\hat{p}_{b,\psi}[\omega]

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\gamma_{b}}\hat{\xi}_{p_{b,\psi}}[\omega]-2G\hat{\xi}_{{\rm{BA}},x_{\varphi}}[\omega]

\displaystyle+2\Sigma_{b}[\omega]\chi_{b}[\omega]\hat{\xi}_{x_{b,\psi}}[\omega].

The noise quadratures are defined as the other quadratures. As expected from the Hamiltonian in Eq. (
32
), the equation for
\hat{x}_{b,\psi}
has no magnon-microwave noise. Otherwise, the non-BAE quadrature
\hat{p}_{b,\psi}[\omega]
has two contributions in addition to thermal noise: the backaction noise
G\hat{\xi}_{{\rm{BA}},x_{\varphi}}[\omega]
and the “dynamical backaction” noise
\Sigma_{b}[\omega]\chi_{b}[\omega]\hat{\xi}_{x_{b,\psi}}[\omega]
. In the case where both tones are perfectly centered around the magnon/microwave frequency, the last term vanishes.

The BAE quadrature noise spectrum is just the spectrum of an uncoupled oscillator at thermal equilibrium

\displaystyle S_{xx}[\omega]

\displaystyle=\int\frac{d\omega^{\prime}}{2\pi}\langle\hat{x}_{b,\psi}[\omega]\hat{x}_{b,\psi}[\omega^{\prime}]\rangle

(40)

\displaystyle=S^{(0)}[\omega]=\frac{\gamma_{b}(n_{b}+\frac{1}{2})}{\omega^{2}+\frac{\gamma_{b}^{2}}{4}},

while, at zero detuning (
\Delta_{m}=\Delta_{c}=0
) the non-BAE quadrature noise spectrum is

\displaystyle S_{pp}[\omega]

\displaystyle=\int\frac{d\omega^{\prime}}{2\pi}\langle\hat{p}_{b,\psi}[\omega]\hat{p}_{b,\psi}[\omega^{\prime}]\rangle

(41)

\displaystyle=S^{(0)}[\omega]+S_{pp}^{(\rm{BA})}[\omega].

On top of the thermal noise
S^{(0)}[\omega]
the quadrature is subjected to backaction noise
S_{pp}^{(\rm{BA})}[\omega]
given by

\displaystyle S_{pp}^{(\rm{BA})}[\omega]

\displaystyle=4\frac{G^{2}|\Xi_{m}[\omega]|^{2}}{\omega^{2}+\frac{\gamma_{b}^{2}}{4}}\Big[\kappa_{m}\left(n_{m}+\frac{1}{2}\right)

(42)

\displaystyle+g_{mc}^{2}\kappa_{c}|\chi_{c}[\omega]|^{2}\left(n_{c}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\Big].

For
\Delta_{m}\neq\Delta_{c}\neq 0
, the non-BAE noise spectrum has further contributions due to the self-energy term, the last term in Eq. (
39
). We show the full formula for
S_{pp}[\omega]
in the appendix. To compute the effects of backaction noise in the non-BAE quadrature of the phonon mode, we compute the integral of the noise spectrum

I_{pp}=\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}S_{pp}[\omega].

(43)

We take the value for an uncoupled oscillator of the same frequency at thermal equilibrium value as a reference

I_{pp}^{(0)}=\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}S^{(0)}[\omega]=n_{b}+\frac{1}{2}.

(44)

IV
Microwave output spectrum

We now turn our attention to the measurement of the BAE quadrature via the output microwave signal. For this, we consider the standard input-output relation

\hat{c}_{\rm{out}}[\omega]=\hat{\xi}_{c}[\omega]-\sqrt{\kappa_{c}}\hat{c}[\omega],

(45)

where we have assumed that the only source of input noise is thermal. We then consider the noise spectrum of the output quadrature

\hat{x}_{\rm{out},\theta}[\omega]=\frac{e^{i\theta}\hat{c}_{\rm{out}}^{\dagger}[\omega]+e^{-i\theta}\hat{c}_{\rm{out}}[\omega]}{\sqrt{2}},

(46)

given by

S_{\theta\theta}[\omega]=\int\frac{d\omega^{\prime}}{2\pi}\langle\hat{x}_{\rm{out},\theta}[\omega]\hat{x}_{\rm{out},\theta}[\omega^{\prime}]\rangle.

(47)

The symmetrized version of (
47
) consists of dips associated with the hybrid modes response. On top of such spectrum, there will be a Lorentzian centered around
\omega=0
, which is the signal of the BAE quadrature. In fact, for frequencies in an interval close to zero, we can write

\displaystyle S_{\theta\theta}[\omega]

\displaystyle=|\mathcal{G}_{x,\theta}[\omega]|^{2}\left[S^{(0)}[\omega]+\frac{4n_{\rm{imp},\theta}}{\gamma_{b}}\right],

(48)

where
n_{\rm{imp},\theta}
is an effective number of quanta added to the measurement of the mechanical spectrum due to the imprecision noise. Since the scheme is BAE, there is no noise added by backaction. The explicit expression for the coefficient
\mathcal{G}_{x,\theta}[\omega]
, representing the measurement gain, and the added number of quanta
n_{\rm{imp},\theta}
is given in the appendix.

Under the assumption that the counter-rotating terms appearing in the solutions for
\hat{x}_{\psi,b}[\omega]
can be discarded and for perfect detection efficiency, the imprecision noise
n_{\rm{imp}}
can be made arbitrarily small by increasing the driving amplitude, which is limited by the system stability conditions. In such a perfect scenario, the added quanta to the measurement can go below the standard quantum limit (SQL),
n_{\rm{imp}}^{\rm{SQL}}=1/2
, for drives centered around the hybrid modes. The imprecision added noise, in this case, is mainly determined by the thermal occupation of the magnon and microwave baths and by the drives amplitudes, as such,
n_{\rm{imp}}
can be below the SQL even at moderately high temperatures. The RWA performed should hold in the ‘good cavity’ regime
\kappa_{c,m}<\omega_{b}
and for weak magnomechanical couplings but, analogously to standard optomechanical system
Clerk et al. 2008
, we expect that such a scheme can cope with small deviations from the perfect BAE measurement and still be able to beat the standard quantum limit.

Finally, the added imprecision quanta depends on the magnon/microwave bath occupancy and on the drive power (see the Appendix). For the parameters we considered here, the scheme is able to beat the SQL for magnon/microwave bath occupancies up to a few hundred quanta, while at weaker drive powers the bath temperature has to be smaller. We notice, however, that for magnon/microwave modes with
\sim 10
GHz frequencies, a negligible occupancy can be attained at a few hundreds of mK, which is routinely achieved in cavity magnonic experiments
Huebl et al. 2013
;
Lachance-Quirion et al. 2019
.

V
Conclusion

To summarize, we have characterized the quantum noise added to mechanical vibrations in cavity magnomechanical systems and proposed a two-tone drive scheme to implement a backaction evasion measurement of a mechanical quadrature. Different schemes for backaction evasion are possible, and we have studied their robustness to imperfections of the BAE requirements, as well as the amount of noise added in relevant schemes on the measurement via the output of the microwave mode. The effectiveness of BAE in our proposal is more robust to imperfections for a vanishing microwave detuning (both tones centered at the microwave frequency). Nevertheless, this case corresponds to the maximum imprecision noise added to the measurement of the BAE phonon quadrature. Otherwise, by centering the tones around the hybrid magnon-microwave modes, even though the scheme is less robust to imperfections, less imprecision noise is added to the measurement. In fact, in this case, it is possible to go below the standard quantum limit.

Our results provide a potential route for measuring phonons in cavity magnomechanical systems. For instance, such a quantum backaction evasion scheme can be used to perform quantum tomography of phonons and validate the creation of entangled states proposed in the literature. A more complete model should include imperfect measurement efficiency as well as other intrinsic features of cavity magnomechanical systems, in particular magnetic nonlinearities and the unavoidable coupling to high order Walker modes
Bittencourt et al. 2023
. As we have shown previously, both nonlinearities and the coupling to several magnon modes change dynamical backaction, and the same should be true for the quantum backaction studied here. Still, a double-drive BAE scheme should be possible, with small modifications due to the change in the magnon and phonon frequencies stemming from nonlinearities and the coupling to multiple magnon modes, an analysis that we postpone to future work.

\displaystyle=\langle\hat{\xi}_{\rm{BA}}^{\dagger}[\omega]\hat{\xi}_{\rm{BA}}^{\dagger}[\omega^{\prime}]\rangle=0

(2)

\displaystyle\langle\hat{\xi}_{\rm{BA}}[\omega]\hat{\xi}^{\dagger}_{\rm{BA}}[\omega^{\prime}]\rangle

\displaystyle=2\pi\Xi_{m}[\omega]\Xi_{m}^{*}[-\omega^{\prime}]\left(\kappa_{m}(n_{m}+1)+g_{mc}^{2}\kappa_{c}\chi_{c}[\omega]\chi_{c}^{*}[-\omega^{\prime}](n_{c}+1)\right)\delta(\omega+\omega^{\prime})

\displaystyle\langle\hat{\xi}_{\rm{BA}}^{\dagger}[\omega]\hat{\xi}_{\rm{BA}}[\omega^{\prime}]\rangle

\displaystyle=2\pi\Xi_{m}^{*}[-\omega]\Xi_{m}[\omega^{\prime}]\left(\kappa_{m}n_{m}+g_{mc}^{2}\kappa_{c}\chi_{c}^{*}[-\omega]\chi_{c}[\omega^{\prime}]n_{c}\right)\delta(\omega+\omega^{\prime})

V.2
Effects of the counter-rotating term on the mechanics noise spectrum for a single tone drive

In section
II
, we have discarded a counter-rotating term in the solution for the phonon operator in frequency space. We now asses the impact of this discarded term in the results presented in the main text.

The solution for the phonon operator obtained from the linearized Heisenberg-Langevin equation in frequency space, Eq. (
9
), reads

\tilde{\chi}_{b}^{-1}[\omega]\hat{b}[\omega]=-i\hat{f}_{\rm{BA}}[\omega]+\sqrt{\gamma_{b}}\hat{\xi}_{b}[\omega]+i\Sigma_{b}[\omega]\hat{b}^{\dagger}[\omega].

(3)

The counter-rotating term induces squeezing which depends on the “bare“ self-energy
\Sigma_{b}[\omega]
. For the dynamical backaction evasion scheme, i.e., for
g_{mc}=\omega_{b}
and
\Delta_{c}=0
, such counter-rotating term has no effect since in such parameter regime
\Sigma_{b}[\omega]=0
. In any case, the full solution for the phonon operator in frequency domain is given by

\chi_{b,{\rm{eff}}}^{-1}[\omega]\hat{b}[\omega]=-iF_{\rm{BA}}[\omega]\hat{f}_{\rm{BA}}[\omega]+\sqrt{\gamma_{b}}\hat{\xi}_{b}[\omega]+i\sqrt{\gamma_{b}}\Lambda[\omega]\hat{\xi}_{b}^{\dagger}[\omega].

(4)

The effective phonon susceptibility including the counter-rotating term is

\chi_{b,{\rm{eff}}}^{-1}[\omega]=\chi_{b}^{-1}[\omega]-i\Sigma_{b}[\omega]\left(1-\frac{i\Sigma_{b}[\omega]}{\chi_{b}^{-1,*}[\omega]+i\Sigma_{b}[\omega]}\right).

(5)

The pre-factor of the backaction noise term
\hat{f}_{\rm{BA}}
is given by

\displaystyle F_{\rm{BA}}[\omega]

\displaystyle=1-i\Lambda[\omega]

(6)

\displaystyle\Lambda[\omega]

\displaystyle=\frac{\Sigma_{b}[\omega]}{\chi_{b}^{-1,*}[\omega]+i\Sigma_{b}[\omega]}.

With those modifications of the phonon susceptibility and the pre-factors of the noise terms, the position noise spectrum reads

\displaystyle S_{xx}[\omega]=S_{xx}^{(\rm{D})}[\omega]+S_{xx}^{(\rm{BA})}[\omega],

(7)

where now the dynamical backaction term
S_{xx}^{(\rm{D})}[\omega]
reads

\displaystyle S_{xx}^{(\rm{D})}[\omega]

\displaystyle=\gamma_{b}\left[(n_{b}+1)|F_{b}[\omega]|^{2}+n_{b}|F_{b}[-\omega]|^{2}\right]

(8)

\displaystyle F_{b}[\omega]

\displaystyle=\chi_{b,{\rm{eff}}}[\omega]-\chi_{b,{\rm{eff}}}^{*}[-\omega]\Lambda^{*}[-\omega].

Compared with the contribution without the counter-rotating term, given in Eq. (
24
), the effective susceptibility is substituted by the new effective susceptibility plus a term related to the squeezing induced by the counter-rotating term. The backaction noise contribution reads

\displaystyle S_{xx}^{(\rm{BA})}[\omega]

\displaystyle=\kappa_{c}g_{mc}^{2}|g_{mb}\mathcal{F}_{\rm{BA}}[\omega]|^{2}|\chi_{c}[\omega]|^{2}\Big[|\Xi_{m}[-\omega]|^{2}n_{c}+|\Xi_{m}[\omega]|^{2}(n_{c}+1)\Big]

(9)

\displaystyle+\gamma_{m}|g_{mb}\mathcal{F}_{\rm{BA}}[\omega]|^{2}\Big[|\Xi_{m}[-\omega]|^{2}n_{m}+|\Xi_{m}[\omega]|^{2}(n_{m}+1)\Big],

where now the function
\mathcal{F}_{\rm{BA}}[\omega]
reads

\mathcal{F}_{\rm{BA}}[\omega]=F_{\rm{BA}}[\omega]\chi_{b,\rm{eff}}[\omega]-F_{\rm{BA}}^{*}[-\omega]\chi_{b,\rm{eff}}^{*}[-\omega].

(10)

Similar to the dynamical backaction term, the difference is the modification of the effective phonon susceptibility plus a term related to squeezing.

All the corrections due to the counter-rotating term appearing in equation (
9
) have the phonon self-energy
\Sigma_{b}[\omega]
as a pre-factor. For the case where magnons and microwaves are in resonance and a zero detuned drive
\Delta_{c}=0
, the phonon self-energy vanishes and the full formulas shown here coincide with the simplifyied versions given in the main text. In the case where dynamical backaction is present, the counter-rotating terms induce small corrections in the quantities that we have studied in the main text.

In Fig.
1
, we show the difference between the full symmetrized noise spectrum and the noise spectrum shown in the main text, for the triple resonance and the dynamical backaction evasion schemes and drive detunings
\Delta_{c}=\pm g_{mc}
, corresponding to the situations studied in the main text in Fig.

. For a red detuned drive and both schemes, the maximum difference between the noise spectra is
\sim 10^{-6}
the reference value
S_{xx}^{(0)}[\omega_{b}]
, 5 orders of magnitude smaller than the difference between the noise spectrum computed in the main text and the uncoupled position noise spectrum. Otherwise, the counter-rotating terms introduce a difference
\sim 10\%
with respect to the reference value for a blue detuned drive in the triple resonance scheme, nevertheless, the difference itself is 4 order of magnitude smaller than the peaks. Moreover, such a difference is relevant in a frequency range narrower than the other cases.

V.3
Heisenberg-Langevin equations for two-tones drive

The cavity magnomechanics Hamiltonian with the two-tones drive given in Eq. (
28
) reads:

\displaystyle\frac{\hat{H}}{\hbar}

\displaystyle=\omega_{c}\hat{c}^{\dagger}\hat{c}+\omega_{m}\hat{m}^{\dagger}\hat{m}+\omega_{b}\hat{b}^{\dagger}\hat{b}

(11)

\displaystyle+g_{mc}\left(\hat{m}^{\dagger}\hat{c}+\hat{m}\hat{c}^{\dagger}\right)+g_{mb}^{0}\hat{m}^{\dagger}\hat{m}\left(\hat{b}^{\dagger}+\hat{b}\right)

\displaystyle+i\sqrt{\kappa_{e}}(\epsilon_{-}e^{-i\delta t}+\epsilon_{+}e^{i\delta t})e^{i\omega_{d}t}\hat{c}+\rm{H.c.}.

To obtain the semiclassical steady-state we move to an interacting frame rotating with
\omega_{d}\hat{c}^{\dagger}\hat{c}+\omega_{d}\hat{m}^{\dagger}\hat{m}
and make the ansatz
\hat{c}=\hat{c}_{0}+\hat{c}_{+}e^{-i\delta t}+\hat{c}_{-}e^{i\delta t}
and
\hat{m}=\hat{m}_{0}+\hat{m}_{+}e^{-i\delta t}+\hat{m}_{-}e^{i\delta t}
. Within a mean field approximation, the equations for the expectation values of each bosonic operator reads

\displaystyle\dot{c}_{0}

\displaystyle=\left(i\Delta_{c}-\frac{\kappa}{2}\right)c_{0}-ig_{mc}m_{0},

(12)

\displaystyle\dot{c}_{\pm}

\displaystyle=\left(i(\Delta_{c}\pm\delta)-\frac{\kappa}{2}\right)c_{\pm}-ig_{mc}m_{\pm}-\sqrt{\kappa_{e}}\epsilon_{\pm},

\displaystyle\dot{m}_{0}

\displaystyle=\left(i\Delta_{m}-\frac{\gamma}{2}\right)m_{0}-ig_{mc}c_{0}-ig_{mb}^{0}(m_{-}b+m_{+}b^{*}),

\displaystyle\dot{m}_{+}

\displaystyle=\left(i(\Delta_{m}+\delta)-\frac{\gamma}{2}\right)m_{+}-ig_{mc}c_{+}-ig_{mb}^{0}m_{0}b,

\displaystyle\dot{m}_{-}

\displaystyle=\left(i(\Delta_{m}-\delta)-\frac{\gamma}{2}\right)m_{-}-ig_{mc}c_{-}-ig_{mb}^{0}m_{0}b^{*},

\displaystyle\dot{b}

\displaystyle=\left(i\Delta_{b}-\frac{\Gamma}{2}\right)b-ig_{mb}^{0}(m_{0}^{*}m_{+}+m_{-}^{*}m_{0}).

We have discarded all terms
\propto e^{\pm 2i\delta t}
. The steady-state is obtained by setting all time derivatives to zero and solving the system of equations. In doing so, we assume the bare magnomechanical coupling
g_{mc}^{0}
small enough to be ignored in the dynamics of the magnon and microwave amplitudes. Such a procedure gives Eqs. (
29
).

V.4
Solution for the phonon operator in the linearized two-tone driving scheme

We define
\delta_{b}=\omega_{b}-\delta
and
\sigma_{b}=\omega_{b}+\delta
. In an interacting frame rotating with
\omega_{b}\hat{b}^{\dagger}\hat{b}
, we have the following equation for
\hat{m}[\omega]
as a function of phonon operators

\displaystyle\hat{m}[\omega]=-ig_{-}\Xi[\omega]\left(\hat{b}[\omega-\delta_{b}]+\hat{b}^{\dagger}[\omega+\sigma_{b}]\right)-ig_{+}\Xi[\omega](\hat{b}[\omega-\sigma_{b}]+\hat{b}^{\dagger}[\omega+\delta_{b}])+\hat{\xi}_{\rm{BA}}[\omega],

(13)

where the modified magnon susceptibility
\Xi[\omega]
and the backaction noise
\hat{\xi}_{\rm{BA}}[\omega]
have the same formula as in the single drive case. The equation for the phonon operator reads

\chi_{b}^{-1}[\omega]\hat{b}[\omega]=-ig_{-}\hat{m}^{\dagger}[\omega+\sigma_{b}]-ig_{+}\hat{m}^{\dagger}[\omega+\delta_{b}]-ig_{-}^{*}\hat{m}[\omega+\delta_{b}]-ig_{+}^{*}\hat{m}[\omega+\sigma_{b}]+\sqrt{\gamma_{b}}\hat{\xi}_{b}[\omega].

(14)

Notice that in this frame
\chi_{b}[\omega]=(-i\omega+\gamma_{b}/2)^{-1}
.

Eliminating the magnon operator in favor of the phonon operators gives

\displaystyle\chi^{-1}_{b}[\omega]\hat{b}[\omega]

\displaystyle=i\Sigma_{b,{\rm{T}}}[\omega]\hat{b}[\omega]+i\Sigma_{b,{\rm{T}}}[\omega]\hat{b}^{\dagger}[\omega+2\omega_{b}]+f_{1}[\omega]\hat{b}[\omega+2\delta]+f_{1}[\omega]\hat{b}^{\dagger}[\omega+2\sigma_{b}]

(15)

\displaystyle+f_{2}[\omega]\hat{b}[\omega-2\delta]+f_{2}[\omega]\hat{b}^{\dagger}[\omega+2\delta_{b}]-ig_{+}\hat{\xi}_{\rm{BA}}^{\dagger}[\omega+\delta_{b}]-ig_{-}^{*}\hat{\xi}_{\rm{BA}}[\omega+\delta_{b}]

\displaystyle-ig_{-}\hat{\xi}_{\rm{BA}}^{\dagger}[\omega+\sigma_{b}]-ig_{+}^{*}\hat{\xi}_{\rm{BA}}[\omega+\sigma_{b}]+\sqrt{\gamma_{b}}\hat{\xi}_{b}[\omega],

In the above expression, the total phonon self-energy
\Sigma_{b,{\rm{T}}}[\omega]
is given by

\Sigma_{b,{\rm{T}}}[\omega]=\Sigma_{b}[\omega]+\Sigma_{b,{\rm{CRT}}}[\omega],

(16)

where the co-rotating contribution
\Sigma_{b}[\omega]
is given by

\Sigma_{b}[\omega]=i|g_{-}|^{2}\Xi_{m}[\omega+\delta_{b}]-i|g_{+}|^{2}\Xi_{m}^{*}[-\omega-\delta_{b}],

(17)

which is similar to that obtained for the one tone drive case, while the
counter-rotating
contribution is

\Sigma_{b,{\rm{CRT}}}[\omega]=i|g_{+}|^{2}\Xi_{m}[\omega+\sigma_{b}]-i|g_{-}|^{2}\Xi_{m}^{*}[-\omega-\sigma_{b}].

(18)

The pre-factor functions
f_{1,2}[\omega]
are given by

\displaystyle f_{1}[\omega]

\displaystyle=g_{-}g_{+}^{*}\left(\Xi_{m}^{*}[-\omega-\sigma_{b}]-\Xi_{m}[\omega+\sigma_{b}]\right),

(19)

\displaystyle f_{2}[\omega]

\displaystyle=g_{-}^{*}g_{+}\left(\Xi_{m}^{*}[-\omega-\delta_{b}]-\Xi_{m}[\omega+\delta_{b}]\right).

V.4.1
Mechanics noise spectrum in the BAE scheme

The BAE scheme requires that
\delta_{b}=0
and
|g_{+}|=|g_{-}|=G
. Using the same notation of the main text
g_{\pm}=Ge^{i\pm\varphi_{\pm}}
, we obtain for the pre-factors

\displaystyle\Sigma_{b,{\rm{T}}}[\omega]

\displaystyle=\Sigma_{b}[\omega]+\Sigma_{b,{\rm{CRT}}}[\omega],

(20)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{b}[\omega]

\displaystyle=iG^{2}\left(\Xi_{m}[\omega]-\Xi_{m}^{*}[-\omega]\right),

\displaystyle\Sigma_{b,{\rm{CRT}}}[\omega]

\displaystyle=iG^{2}\left(\Xi_{m}[\omega+2\omega_{b}]-\Xi_{m}^{*}[-\omega-2\omega_{b}]\right),

\displaystyle f_{1}[\omega]

\displaystyle=-G^{2}e^{-i(\varphi_{+}-\varphi_{-})}\left(\Xi_{m}[\omega+2\omega_{b}]-\Xi_{m}^{*}[-\omega-2\omega_{b}]\right)=ie^{-i(\varphi_{+}-\varphi_{-})}\Sigma_{b,{\rm{CRT}}}[\omega]

\displaystyle f_{2}[\omega]

\displaystyle=-G^{2}e^{i(\varphi_{+}-\varphi_{-})}\left(\Xi_{m}[\omega]-\Xi_{m}^{*}[-\omega]\right)=ie^{i(\varphi_{+}-\varphi_{-})}\Sigma_{b}[\omega].

and the phonon operator is given by

\displaystyle\chi^{-1}_{b}[\omega]\hat{b}[\omega]

\displaystyle=i\Sigma_{b}[\omega]\hat{b}[\omega]+i\Sigma_{b,\rm{CRT}}[\omega]\hat{b}[\omega]+i\Sigma_{b,{\rm{T}}}[\omega]\hat{b}^{\dagger}[\omega+2\omega_{b}]

(21)

\displaystyle+ie^{-2i\psi}\Sigma_{b,{\rm{CRT}}}[\omega]\left(\hat{b}[\omega+2\omega_{b}]+\hat{b}^{\dagger}[\omega+4\omega_{b}]\right)

\displaystyle+ie^{2i\psi}\Sigma_{b}[\omega]\left(\hat{b}^{\dagger}[\omega]+\hat{b}[\omega-2\omega_{b}]\right)

\displaystyle-iGe^{i(\psi+\varphi)}\hat{\xi}_{\rm{BA}}^{\dagger}[\omega]-iGe^{i(\psi-\varphi)}\hat{\xi}_{\rm{BA}}[\omega]

\displaystyle-iGe^{-i(\psi-\varphi)}\hat{\xi}_{\rm{BA}}^{\dagger}[\omega+2\omega_{b}]-iGe^{-i(\psi+\varphi)}\hat{\xi}_{\rm{BA}}[\omega+2\omega_{b}]+\sqrt{\gamma_{b}}\hat{\xi}_{b}[\omega],

where we have explicitly separated rotating and counter-rotating contributions for the self-energy in the first term and we have used the definitions. We have defined the phases
\psi
and
\phi
as

\displaystyle\psi=\frac{\varphi_{+}-\varphi_{-}}{2},

(22)

\displaystyle\varphi=\frac{\varphi_{+}+\varphi_{-}}{2}.

After some algebraic manipulations, from eq. (
21
) we obtain the following equation for the quadrature
\hat{x}_{b,\psi}
defined in eq. (
33
)

\displaystyle\sqrt{2}\chi^{-1}_{b}[\omega]\hat{x}_{b,\psi}[\omega]

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2\gamma_{b}}\hat{\xi}_{x_{b,\psi}}[\omega]

(23)

\displaystyle+i\Sigma_{b,\rm{CRT}}[\omega]e^{-i\psi}\hat{b}[\omega]-i\Sigma_{b,\rm{CRT}}^{*}[-\omega]e^{i\psi}\hat{b}^{\dagger}[\omega]

\displaystyle+ie^{-3i\psi}\Sigma_{b,{\rm{CRT}}}[\omega]\left(\hat{b}[\omega+2\omega_{b}]+\hat{b}^{\dagger}[\omega+4\omega_{b}]\right)-ie^{3i\psi}\Sigma_{b,{\rm{CRT}}}^{*}[-\omega]\left(\hat{b}^{\dagger}[\omega-2\omega_{b}]+\hat{b}[\omega-4\omega_{b}]\right)

\displaystyle+i\Sigma_{b}[\omega]\left(e^{i\psi}\hat{b}[\omega-2\omega_{b}]-e^{-i\psi}\hat{b}^{\dagger}[\omega+2\omega_{b}]\right)

\displaystyle-iGe^{-i(2\psi-\varphi)}\hat{\xi}_{\rm{BA}}^{\dagger}[\omega+2\omega_{b}]+iGe^{i(2\psi-\varphi)}\hat{\xi}_{\rm{BA}}[\omega-2\omega_{b}]

\displaystyle-iGe^{-i(2\psi+\varphi)}\hat{\xi}_{\rm{BA}}[\omega+2\omega_{b}]+iGe^{i(2\psi+\varphi)}\hat{\xi}_{\rm{BA}}^{\dagger}[\omega-2\omega_{b}].

All the terms on the right-hand side of the above equation besides the first one, are counter-rotating terms, either becasue they are multiplied by
\Sigma_{b,{\rm{CRT}}}
or because they are evaluated at twice a phonon frequency apart from the frequency where the equation is evaluated. In general, one can further write an infinite set of linear for this quadrature and its canonical conjugated
\hat{p}_{b,\psi}
, evaluated at frequencies
\omega\pm 2n\omega_{b}
, where
n
is a positive integer.

If in addition to the other requirements
we further impose that both drives are centered around the magnon/microwave frequency, i.e., if the drive-detuning is zero, then as in the one-tone drive case
\Sigma_{b}[\omega]=\Sigma_{b,\rm{CRT}}[\omega]=0
. In this particular case:

\displaystyle\sqrt{2}\chi^{-1}_{b}[\omega]\hat{x}_{b,\psi}[\omega]

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2\gamma_{b}}\hat{\xi}_{x_{b,\psi}}[\omega]

(24)

\displaystyle-iGe^{-i(2\psi-\varphi)}\hat{\xi}_{\rm{BA}}^{\dagger}[\omega+2\omega_{b}]+iGe^{i(2\psi-\varphi)}\hat{\xi}_{\rm{BA}}[\omega-2\omega_{b}]

\displaystyle-iGe^{-i(2\psi+\varphi)}\hat{\xi}_{\rm{BA}}[\omega+2\omega_{b}]+iGe^{i(2\psi+\varphi)}\hat{\xi}_{\rm{BA}}^{\dagger}[\omega-2\omega_{b}].

Since in this situation microwaves and magnons are at resonance,
n_{m}=n_{c}
. The noise spectrum of such quadrature is then given by

S_{xx,\psi}[\omega]=\int\frac{d\omega^{\prime}}{2\pi}\langle\hat{x}_{b,\psi}[\omega]\hat{x}_{b,\psi}[\omega^{\prime}]\rangle=S_{xx,\psi}^{(0)}[\omega]+S_{xx,\psi}^{(\rm{CRT})}[\omega],

(25)

where

S_{xx,\psi}^{(0)}[\omega]=\gamma_{b}|\chi_{b}[\omega]|^{2}\left(n_{b}+\frac{1}{2}\right)=\frac{\gamma_{b}\left(n_{b}+\frac{1}{2}\right)}{\omega^{2}+\frac{\gamma_{b}^{2}}{4}},

(26)

is the uncoupled noise spectrum, and

\displaystyle S_{xx,\psi}^{(\rm{CRT})}[\omega]=|\chi_{b}[\omega]|^{2}G^{2}\left(n_{m}+\frac{1}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\Bigg[\mathcal{A}[\omega-2\omega_{b}]\left(1-\frac{\chi_{b}^{*}[\omega-4\omega_{b}]e^{4i\psi}}{\chi_{b}^{*}[\omega]}\right)

(27)

\displaystyle+\mathcal{A}[\omega+2\omega_{b}]\left(1-\frac{\chi_{b}^{*}[\omega+4\omega_{b}]e^{-4i\psi}}{\chi_{b}^{*}[\omega]}\right)\Bigg],

where

\mathcal{A}[\omega]=|\Xi_{m}[\omega]|^{2}(\kappa_{m}+g_{mc}^{2}\kappa_{c}|\chi_{c}[\omega]|^{2}).

(28)

Notice that since the drive detuning
\Delta_{c}=\Delta_{m}=0
then
\mathcal{A}[\omega]=\mathcal{A}[-\omega]
. Furthermore
\chi_{b}[\omega]=\chi_{b}^{*}[-\omega]
.

V.4.3
Position noise spectrum: deviations from the BAE setup

Starting with the full solution for
\hat{b}[\omega]
given in Eq. (
15
), we drop any term that has an argument containing
\sigma_{b}
and solve the set of equations for
\hat{b}[\omega]
and
\hat{b}^{\dagger}[\omega]
. This yields

\displaystyle\chi_{b,{\rm{eff}}}^{-1}[\omega]\hat{b}[\omega]

\displaystyle=ig_{+}^{*}\mathcal{B}[\omega]\hat{\xi}_{\rm{BA}}[\omega]-ig_{-}^{*}\hat{\xi}_{\rm{BA}}[\omega+\delta_{b}]+ig_{-}\mathcal{B}[\omega]\hat{\xi}_{\rm{BA}}^{\dagger}[\omega]

(32)

\displaystyle-ig_{+}\hat{\xi}_{\rm{BA}}^{\dagger}[\omega+\delta_{b}]+\sqrt{\gamma_{b}}\hat{\xi}_{b}[\omega]+\sqrt{\gamma_{b}}\mathcal{B}[\omega]\hat{\xi}_{b}^{\dagger}[\omega+2\delta_{b}],

\mathcal{B}[\omega]=\frac{f_{2}[\omega]}{\chi_{b}^{-1}[\omega+2\delta_{b}]+i\Sigma_{b}^{*}[-\omega-2\delta_{b}]}.

(33)

The effective phonon susceptibility
\chi_{b,{\rm{eff}}}^{-1}[\omega]

\chi_{b,{\rm{eff}}}^{-1}[\omega]=\chi_{b}^{-1}[\omega]-i\Sigma_{b}[\omega]-f_{2}^{*}[-\omega-2\delta_{b}]\mathcal{B}[\omega].

(34)

The self-energy term
\Sigma_{b}[\omega]
is given in Eq. (
17
), while the function
f_{2}[\omega]
was defined in Eq. (
19
).

Before proceeding, we define the following functions

\displaystyle\chi_{\rm{BA}}[\omega]

\displaystyle=g_{-}\chi_{b,{\rm{eff}}}[\omega]\mathcal{B}[\omega]e^{-i\psi}-g_{+}\chi_{b,{\rm{eff}}}^{*}[-\omega]\mathcal{B}^{*}[-\omega]e^{i\psi},

(35)

\displaystyle\chi_{+}[\omega]

\displaystyle=g_{+}\chi_{b,{\rm{eff}}}[\omega],

\displaystyle\chi_{-}[\omega]

\displaystyle=g_{-}^{*}\chi_{b,{\rm{eff}}}[\omega],

\displaystyle\chi_{\mathcal{B}}[\omega]

\displaystyle=\mathcal{B}[\omega]\chi_{b,{\rm{eff}}}[\omega],

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{(1)}[\omega]

\displaystyle=|\Xi_{m}[\omega]|^{2}\left[\kappa_{m}(n_{m}+1)+g_{mc}^{2}\kappa_{c}|\chi_{c}[\omega]|^{2}(n_{c}+1)\right],

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{(2)}[\omega]

\displaystyle=|\Xi_{m}[-\omega]|^{2}\left[\kappa_{m}n_{m}+g_{mc}^{2}\kappa_{c}|\chi_{c}[-\omega]|^{2}n_{c}\right].

The phase
\psi

\psi=\frac{{\rm{arg}}[g_{+}]-{\rm{arg}}[g_{-}]}{2}.

(36)

We can then calculate the position noise spectrum

(37)

As in the previously studied cases, the first term contains contributions due to dynamical backaction, and is given by

\displaystyle S_{xx}^{(\rm{D})}[\omega]

\displaystyle=\left[|\chi_{b,{\rm{eff}}}[\omega]|^{2}+|\chi_{\mathcal{B}}[-\omega]|^{2}+\chi_{b,{\rm{eff}}}[\omega]\chi_{\mathcal{B}}[-\omega-2\delta_{b}]e^{-2i\psi}+\chi_{b,{\rm{eff}}}^{*}[\omega-2\delta_{b}]\chi_{\mathcal{B}}^{*}[-\omega]e^{2i\psi}\right]\frac{\gamma_{b}(n_{b}+1)}{2}

\displaystyle S_{xx}^{(\rm{BA})}[\omega]

\displaystyle=\left[|\chi_{\rm{BA}}[-\omega]|^{2}+\chi_{\rm{BA}}^{*}[-\omega]\chi_{-}^{*}[\omega-\delta_{b}]e^{i\psi}-\chi_{\rm{BA}}^{*}[-\omega]\chi_{+}[-\omega-\delta_{b}]e^{-i\psi}\right]\frac{\mathcal{C}^{(1)}[\omega]}{2}

(39)

\displaystyle+\left[|\chi_{\rm{BA}}[\omega]|^{2}+\chi_{\rm{BA}}[\omega]\chi_{-}[-\omega-\delta_{b}]e^{-i\psi}-\chi_{\rm{BA}}[\omega]\chi_{+}^{*}[\omega-\delta_{b}]e^{i\psi}\right]\frac{\mathcal{C}^{(2)}[\omega]}{2}

\displaystyle+\left[|\chi_{-}[\omega]|^{2}+\chi_{-}[\omega]\chi_{\rm{BA}}[-\omega-\delta_{b}]e^{-i\psi}-\chi_{-}[\omega]\chi_{+}[-\omega-2\delta_{b}]e^{-2i\psi}\right]\frac{\mathcal{C}^{(1)}(\omega+\delta_{b})}{2}

\displaystyle+\left[|\chi_{-}[-\omega]|^{2}+\chi_{-}^{*}[-\omega]\chi_{\rm{BA}}^{*}[\omega-\delta_{b}]e^{i\psi}-\chi_{-}^{*}[-\omega]\chi_{+}^{*}[\omega-2\delta_{b}]e^{2i\psi}\right]\frac{\mathcal{C}^{(2)}[\omega-\delta_{b}]}{2}

\displaystyle+\left[|\chi_{+}[-\omega]|^{2}-\chi_{+}^{*}[-\omega]\chi_{\rm{BA}}[-\omega+\delta_{b}]e^{i\psi}-\chi_{+}^{*}[-\omega]\chi_{-}^{*}[\omega-2\delta_{b}]e^{2i\psi}\right]\frac{\mathcal{C}^{(1)}[\omega-\delta_{b}]}{2}

\displaystyle+\left[|\chi_{+}[\omega]|^{2}-\chi_{+}[\omega]\chi_{\rm{BA}}^{*}[\omega+\delta_{b}]e^{-i\psi}-\chi_{+}[\omega]\chi_{-}[-\omega-2\delta_{b}]e^{-2i\psi}\right]\frac{\mathcal{C}^{(2)}[\omega+\delta_{b}]}{2}.

One can check that under the BAE conditions
\delta_{b}=0
and
|g_{+}|=|g_{-}|=G
, each term in brackets in the above expression vanishes, and those in Eq. (
38
) simplify to
|\chi_{b}[\omega]|^{2}
.

V.5
Output microwave spectrum

Here we show the formulas not displayed in the main text for the output microwave spectrum. We consider exclusively the case where the BAE conditions are met. From the Heisenberg-Langevin equations, we obtain the following solution for the microwave operator as a function of the noises and the BAE phonon quadrature

\displaystyle\chi_{c}^{-1}[\omega]\hat{c}[\omega]=-\sqrt{2}Gg_{mc}\Xi_{m}[\omega]e^{i\phi}\hat{x}_{b,\psi}[\omega]-ig_{mc}\hat{\xi}_{\rm{BA}}[\omega]+\sqrt{\kappa_{c}}\hat{\xi}_{c}[\omega].

(40)

Recall that the “backaction“ noise term
\hat{\xi}_{\rm{BA}}[\omega]
has a component
\hat{\xi}_{c}[\omega]
, see Eq. (
16
). We now consider the standard input-output relation for the reflection of the microwave mode and assume that the only source of input noise is thermal, such that

\hat{c}_{\rm{out}}[\omega]=\hat{\xi}_{c}[\omega]-\sqrt{\kappa_{c}}\hat{c}[\omega].

(41)

We then compute the output quadrature

\displaystyle\hat{x}_{\rm{out,\theta}}[\omega]

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{i\theta}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{\rm{out}}[\omega]+e^{-i\theta}\hat{c}_{\rm{out}}[\omega]}{\sqrt{2}}

(42)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{G}_{x,\theta}[\omega]\hat{x}_{b,\psi}[\omega]+\mathbb{A}_{m;\theta}[\omega]\hat{\xi}_{m}[\omega]+\mathbb{A}_{c;\theta}[\omega]\hat{\xi}_{c}[\omega]

\displaystyle+\mathbb{A}_{m;\theta}^{*}[-\omega]\hat{\xi}_{m}^{\dagger}[\omega]+\mathbb{A}_{c;\theta}^{*}[-\omega]\hat{\xi}_{c}^{\dagger}[\omega].

The coefficients appearing in the above expression are

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{x,\theta}[\omega]

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\kappa_{c}}Gg_{mc}\left[\chi_{c}[\omega]\Xi_{m}[\omega]e^{i(\phi-\theta)}+\chi_{c}^{*}[-\omega]\Xi_{m}^{*}[-\omega]e^{-i(\phi-\theta)}\right],

(43)

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}_{m;\theta}[\omega]

\displaystyle=ig_{mc}\sqrt{\frac{\kappa_{c}\kappa_{m}}{2}}\chi_{c}[\omega]\Xi_{m}[\omega]e^{-i\theta},

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}_{c;\theta}[\omega]

\displaystyle=\left(1-\kappa_{c}\frac{\chi_{c}[\omega]}{\chi_{m}[\omega]}\Xi_{m}[\omega]\right)\frac{e^{-i\theta}}{\sqrt{2}}.

The correlation noise spectrum between two of such quadratures is given by

S_{\theta\theta^{\prime}}[\omega]=\int\frac{d\omega^{\prime}}{2\pi}\langle\hat{x}_{\rm{out},\theta}[\omega]\hat{x}_{\rm{out},\theta^{\prime}}[\omega^{\prime}]\rangle,

(44)

and then the self-correlation, obtained for
\theta=\theta^{\prime}
reads explicitly

\displaystyle S_{\theta\theta}[\omega]

\displaystyle=|\mathcal{G}_{x,\theta}[\omega]|^{2}S^{(0)}[\omega]

(45)

\displaystyle+|\mathbb{A}_{m,\theta}[\omega]|^{2}(n_{m}+1)+|\mathbb{A}_{m,\theta}[-\omega]|^{2}n_{m}

\displaystyle+|\mathbb{A}_{c,\theta}[\omega]|^{2}(n_{c}+1)+|\mathbb{A}_{c,\theta}[-\omega]|^{2}n_{c}.

In order to obtain the noise added to the measurement of
S^{(0)}[\omega]
we write

\displaystyle S_{\theta\theta}[\omega]

\displaystyle=|\mathcal{G}_{x,\theta}[\omega]|^{2}\left[S^{(0)}[\omega]+S_{\rm{imp}}[\omega]\right],

(46)

where

S_{\rm{imp}}[\omega]=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}_{x,\theta}[\omega]|^{2}}\left[|\mathbb{A}_{m,\theta}[\omega]|^{2}(n_{m}+1)+|\mathbb{A}_{m,\theta}[-\omega]|^{2}n_{m}+|\mathbb{A}_{c,\theta}[\omega]|^{2}(n_{c}+1)+|\mathbb{A}_{c,\theta}[-\omega]|^{2}n_{c}\right].

(47)

We notice now that, at
\omega=0

\displaystyle S_{\theta\theta}(0)

\displaystyle=|\mathcal{G}_{x,\theta}(0)|^{2}\left[S^{(0)}(0)+S_{\rm{imp}}(0)\right]

(48)

\displaystyle=|\mathbb{A}_{x,\theta}(0)|^{2}\frac{2}{\gamma_{b}}\left[2n_{b}+1+\frac{\gamma_{b}}{2}S^{(0)}_{\rm{imp}}(0)\right].

We then define the added quanta to the measurement due to the imprecision noise as

n_{{\rm{imp}},\theta}=\frac{\gamma_{b}}{4}S_{{\rm{imp}},\theta}(0).

(49)

Since for frequencies
\omega
close to zero we have
S_{\rm{imp}}[\omega]\approx S_{\rm{imp}}(0)
, we can write

S_{\theta\theta}[\omega]=|\mathcal{G}_{x,\theta}[\omega]|^{2}\left[S^{(0)}[\omega]+\frac{4n_{{\rm{imp}},\theta}}{\gamma_{b}}\right].

(50)

We note that, for schemes that break the BAE conditions, the phonon quadrature will include a backaction noise term, which then correlates with the imprecision noise.

The imprecision noise for a given angle
\theta
is below the standard quantum limit provided that
n_{{\rm{imp}},\theta}<1/2
. For magnons at resonance with microwaves, the bath occupancy of such modes
n_{m,c}
has to satisfy

n_{m,c}<\frac{|\mathcal{G}_{x,\theta}[0]|^{2}}{\gamma_{b}(|\mathbb{A}_{m,\theta}[0]|^{2})+|\mathbb{A}_{c,\theta}[0]|^{2})}-\frac{1}{2}.

(51)

The maximum occupancy of the magnon/microwave baths for beating the SQL depends on the gain factor
|\mathcal{G}_{x,\theta}[0]|^{2}
, which scales linearly with the drive power. In general, strong drive powers allow to beat the SQL at higher bath occupancies, while smaller phonon decays also allow higher
n_{c,m}
. We should nevertheless notice that the typical temperature required for having a negligible occupancy of the magnon/microwave bath is of a few hundreds of mK, a condition attainable routinely in cavity magnonics experiments.

V.5.1
Brief comparison with the single tone drive case

We can obtain the output noise spectrum for the case of a single tone drive by following the same procedure presented above for the single-tone drive case using the frequency domain Heisenberg-Langevin equation shown in Sec.
II
. The result for the output noise spectrum
S_{\theta\theta}[\omega]
, define in the same way as in the backaction evasion framework, is given in this case by

S_{\theta\theta}[\omega]=|\mathcal{G}_{x,\theta}[\omega]|^{2}\left[S_{xx}^{(\rm{D})}[\omega]+S_{xx}^{(\rm{BA})}[\omega]+S_{\rm{imp}}[\omega]+S_{\rm{corr}}[\omega]\right].

(52)

In the above expression
S_{xx}^{(\rm{D})}
and
S_{xx}^{(\rm{BA})}
are given by Eqs. (
24
) and (
25
) respectively. The gain term
\mathcal{G}_{x,\theta}[\omega]
is given as in Eq. (
43
) with
G\rightarrow|g_{mb}|
and
\phi={\rm{arg}}[g_{mb}]
. The imprecision noise
S_{\rm{imp}}[\omega]
is given by (
47
). Finally the correlation noise
S_{\rm{corr}}[\omega]
comes from correlations between the backaction noise terms appearing in the solution for
\hat{x}_{b}[\omega]
and the imprecision noise terms.

Comparing this expression with what was obtained in the backaction evasion case we notice the presence of bakcation and correlation noises as well as a change in the bare phonon noise due to dynamical backaction, as described by
S_{xx}^{(\rm{D})}
. In analogy with the previous case, we have the number of added quanta to the measurement given by

n_{\rm{add}}=n_{\rm{imp}}+n_{\rm{BA}}+n_{\rm{corr}},

(53)

where

\displaystyle n_{\rm{imp}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\tilde{\gamma}_{b}}{4}S_{\rm{imp}}[\omega_{b}],

(54)

\displaystyle n_{\rm{BA}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\tilde{\gamma}_{b}}{4}S_{xx}^{(\rm{BA})}[\omega_{b}],

\displaystyle n_{\rm{corr}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\tilde{\gamma}_{b}}{4}S_{\rm{corr}}[\omega_{b}].

As in typical linear systems, backaction noise prevents the measurement to go bellow the standard quantum limit
n_{\rm{add}}=1/2

Clerk et al. 2010
.

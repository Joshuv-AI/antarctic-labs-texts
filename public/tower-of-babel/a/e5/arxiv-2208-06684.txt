Extension domains for Hardy spaces
Shahaboddin Shaabani

Abstract
. We show that a proper open subset Ω ⊂ ℝ n \Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n} is an extension domain for H p H^{p} ( 0 < p ≤ 1 0<p\leq 1 ), if and only if it satisfies a certain geometric condition. When n ⁡ ( 1 p − 1 ) ∈ ℕ n(\frac{1}{p}-1)\in\mathbb{N} this condition is equivalent to the global Markov condition for Ω c \Omega^{c} , for p = 1 p=1 it is stronger, and when n ⁡ ( 1 p − 1 ) ∉ ℕ ∪ { 0 } n(\frac{1}{p}-1)\notin\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\} every proper open subset is an extension domain for H p H^{p} . It is shown that in each case a linear extension operator exists. We apply our results to study some complemented subspaces of B ​ M ​ O ​ ( ℝ n ) {BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}) .

1.
Introduction

In general for a given function space
X
, the extension problem is to characterize open subsets
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, such that there exists a bounded (linear or nonlinear) operator, extending every element of
X(\Omega)
to an element of
X(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Such domains are called extension domains for
X
.

This problem has been studied for several classes of functions like Sobolev spaces
W^{k,p}

[
8
]
, functions of bounded mean oscillation
BMO

[
7
]
,
VMO
[
2
]
, etc. Here, we’re going to give an answer to the extension problem for Hardy spaces
H^{p}
when
0<p\leq 1
. As it is well known, this scale of spaces serves as a good substitute for
L^{p}
when
0<p\leq 1

[
12
]
. Unlike
L^{p}
, being in
H^{p}
is not just a matter of “size” but also a matter of “cancellation”. Indeed, these distributions must have vanishing moments up to order
n(\frac{1}{p}-1)
, and this makes the extension problem a bit nontrivial. In Theorem
3.2
, which is the main result of this paper, we give a characterization of these domains, and below we explain the idea behind the proof.

By Miyachi’s atomic decomposition theorem, the extension problem is reduced to extending certain elements of
{H}^{p}(\Omega)
, namely
(p,\Omega)
-atoms. These are atoms without any cancellations and are supported on Whitney cubes of
\Omega
. Then since our characterization requires
\Omega^{c}
to be thick in some sense, in dilated Whitney cubes, we are able to create the required cancellation. When
p=1
, this can be done by putting a proper constant on
\Omega^{c}
in that dilated cube, and for
p=\frac{n}{n+k}
, adding a suitable linear combination of derivatives of Dirac masses supported on
\Omega^{c}
will do the job. The latter case involves interpolating data with polynomials of several variables, and we show that with the condition mentioned in Theorem
3.2
, it is always possible to do that. Also in the absence of these thickness conditions, we are able to construct some functions in the dual of
{H}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, namely
{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\dot{\Lambda}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, which are supported on
\Omega
and have small norms yet large averages. This prevents any bounded extension of
(p,\Omega)
-atoms. Finally, when
n(\frac{1}{p}-1)
is not an integer, it can be shown that every open set is an extension domain.

Definition 2.1

Theorem 2.2

(Miyachi)
.

Let
\Omega

to

.

We continue this section by recalling definitions of two types of atoms.
The first kind are the usual atoms appearing in the atomic decomposition of
{H}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Definition 2.3
.

A bounded function

is called a
p
-atom, if there exists a ball
B
(the supporting ball of

) such that
\text{supp}(f)\subset B
,
\|f\|_{{L}^{\infty}}\leq|B|^{-\frac{1}{p}}
(size condition), and
\left\langle f,P\right\rangle=0
for all polynomials with degree no more than

(cancellation condition).

However, there’s another type of atom which appears in the atomic decomposition of
{H}^{p}(\Omega)
. These atoms do not need to have any kind of cancellations and are called
(p,\Omega)
-atoms.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\Omega
be a proper open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We say that a bounded function

is a
(p,\Omega)
-atom if it is supported on some Whitney cube

of
\Omega
, and
\|g\|_{{L}^{\infty}}\leq|Q|^{-\frac{1}{p}}
.

In
[
11
]
, it’s shown that such atoms are elements of
{H}^{p}(\Omega)
and are uniformly bounded in this space. After fixing these definitions let us recall the atomic decomposition theorem for Hardy spaces on domains.

Theorem 2.5

(Miyachi)
.

Let
\Omega
be a proper open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
0<p\leq 1
and
f\in{H}^{p}(\Omega)
, then there exist sequences of nonnegative numbers
\{\lambda_{i}\},\{\mu_{j}\}
, a sequence of
p
-atoms
\{f_{i}\}
, and a sequence of

(p,\Omega)
-atoms
\{g_{j}\}
, such that the latter is depended linearly on

, each

is supported in
\Omega
, and

(1)

f=\sum\lambda_{i}f_{i}+\sum\mu_{j}g_{j}\quad\text{with}\quad\|f\|_{{H}^{p}(\Omega)}\approx\left(\sum\lambda^{p}_{i}+\sum\mu^{p}_{j}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

In the above, first a suitable Whitney decomposition of the domain is fixed and then roughly speaking,
g_{j}
s are obtained by projecting

onto the space of polynomials localized on Whitney cubes of
\Omega
. This is why
g_{j}
depends linearly on
f
, but this is not the case for

s. Also, it follows from the construction that if
f
is compactly supported in
\Omega
, then the support of all
f_{j}s
and
g_{j}s
are contained in a probably larger compact subset of
\Omega

[
11
]
. It should be noted here that even if
f
is a
(p,\Omega)-
atom, the above decomposition gives us some

-atoms and
(p,\Omega)
-atoms which are probably different from

In
[
11
]
, by using this powerful theorem, the extension problem for distributions in
{H}^{p}(\Omega)
was reduced to the especial case of extending
(p,\Omega)
-atoms. Although the following lemma is used there, but it’s not stated, so we decided to state it here as a lemma.

Lemma 2.6
.

For an open set
\Omega
to be an
H^{p}
extension domain it is necessary and sufficient that for every
(p,\Omega)
-atom
g
there exists an extension
\tilde{g}\in{H}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\|\tilde{g}\|_{{H}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim 1
. Moreover, if this extension is linear in terms of
g
then there is a bounded linear extension operator on
{H}^{p}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

The necessity of the above condition follows directly from the definition of extension domains and the fact that
(p,\Omega)
-atoms have bounded norms.

For the sufficiency, take a distribution
f\in{H}^{p}(\Omega)
, decompose it into atoms as in Theorem
2.5
and extend it in the following way:

\tilde{f}=\sum\lambda_{i}\tilde{f_{i}}+\sum\mu_{j}\tilde{g_{j}},

where
\tilde{f_{i}}
is the extension of
f_{i}
by considering it to be zero outside of
\Omega
. Since
\text{supp}(f_{i})\subset\Omega
,
\tilde{f}
is a well-defined distribution which belong to
{H}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and its norm is bounded by

\|\tilde{f}\|_{{H}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\leq\sum\lambda^{p}_{i}\|\tilde{f_{i}}\|_{{H}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}+\sum\mu^{p}_{j}\|\tilde{g_{j}}\|_{{H}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{p}\lesssim\sum\lambda^{p}_{i}+\sum\mu^{p}_{j}\lesssim\|f\|_{{H}^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}.

So this defines a bounded extension operator on
{H}^{p}(\Omega)
. Whenever the extension of
(p,\Omega)
-atoms is linear, the above operator is also linear on compactly supported distributions in
{H}^{p}(\Omega)
. To see this, we note that
\sum\mu_{j}\tilde{g_{j}}
is linear in terms of
f
and
\sum\lambda_{i}\tilde{f_{i}}=(f-\sum\mu_{j}g_{j})\chi_{\Omega}
, which is true because the support of
f
,
f_{i}
s and
g_{j}
s all are contained in a compact subset of
\Omega
. This implies that
\sum\lambda_{i}\tilde{f_{i}}
is also linear in
f
. Now, the operator
f\to\tilde{f}
is densely defined and bounded, so it has a unique extension to
{H}^{p}(\Omega)
, which is the desired linear extension operator.

∎

Main result

Definition 3.1
.

Let

Theorem 3.2
.

Let

•

The condition (

is equivalent to the geometric condition that characterizes “sets preserving the global Markov inequality”. A closed set

is said to preserve the global Markov inequality if for every positive integer

with

(4)

\|\nabla P\|_{L^{\infty}(F\cap B_{r}(y))}\leq c(F,k)r^{-1}\|P\|_{L^{\infty}(F\cap B_{r}(y))},\quad P\in\mathbb{P}_{k},

where

is a constant depending only on

and

preserves the global Markov inequality if and only if there exists a constant

such that for all

(5)

\frac{w(F\cap B_{r}(y))}{r}>\varepsilon

(see

Proposition 3.3

A proper open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfies condition (

preserves the global Markov inequality.

Proof.

First suppose

and

and some

\nu=\frac{z-y}{|z-y|}
we have

|(y-y_{z})\cdot\nu|\geq|y-z|-|y_{z}-z|>\frac{r}{2(1+a)}.

This shows that
w(\Omega^{c}\cap B(y,r))\geq\frac{r}{2(1+a)}
and hence (
5
) holds with
\varepsilon=\frac{1}{2(1+a)}
. Finally, when
\varepsilon_{0}\geq\frac{r}{2(1+a)}
there is
x\in S(y,\frac{r}{1+a})\cap\Omega
such that
\frac{r}{2(1+a)}\leq d(x)\leq\frac{r}{1+a}
. Noting that
B(x,ad(x))\subset B(y,r)
together with (
3
) gives us

w(\Omega^{c}\cap B(y,r))\geq w(\Omega^{c}\cap B(x,ad(x)))>\delta d(x)\geq\delta\frac{r}{2(1+a)},

which shows that (
5
) holds with
\varepsilon=\frac{\delta}{2(1+a)}
. So in any case the condition (
5
) holds with
\varepsilon=\frac{\min(1,\delta)}{2(1+a)}
, and this completes the proof.

∎

We break the proof of Theorem
3.2
into two parts, the sufficiency and the necessity. In the next section, we will see it is easy to show that (
2
) is enough for extending each
(1,\Omega)
-atom to some 1-atom in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. To prove that (
3
) is sufficient, we need to show that it allows us to choose an interpolation problem and solve it properly, which is the content of Lemma
4.4
. This minor difficulty comes from the fact that when
n(\frac{1}{p}-1)\in\mathbb{N}
, the order of distributions in
H^{p}
can not exceed than
n(\frac{1}{p}-1)-1
, while they must have vanishing moments up to order
n(\frac{1}{p}-1)
. This becomes more clear when it is compared to the case
n(\frac{1}{p}-1)\notin\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
.

4.
Proof of sufficiency

We begin with the following lemma, which looks like a reverse Markov’s inequality (
4
) and is already known. Nevertheless, for the convenience of the reader we give a proof of this here (see
[
9
]
Remark 1 p. 35, and
[
1
]
Theorem 9.21).

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose
E
is a subset of the unit ball
B
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then for any
k=1,2,..
, we have

(6)

{\|P\|}_{C^{k-1}(E)}>c(n,k)w(E){\|P\|}_{L^{\infty}(B)},\quad P\in\mathbb{P}_{k}.

Proof.

Suppose
P\in\mathbb{P}_{k}
with
{\|P\|}_{L^{\infty}(B)}=1
but
{\|P\|}_{C^{k-1}(E)}\leq cw(E)
. We will show that if
c=c(n,k)
is chosen sufficiently small, the set
E
lies entirely between two parallel hyperplanes at distance less than
\frac{1}{2}w(E)
, which is a contradiction. Take
x_{0}\in E
and write the Taylor expansion of
P
around this point then we have

P(x)=\sum\limits_{|\alpha|\leq k-1}\frac{\partial^{\alpha}P(x_{0})}{\alpha!}(x-x_{0})^{\alpha}+\sum\limits_{|\alpha|=k}\frac{\partial^{\alpha}P(x_{0})}{\alpha!}(x-x_{0})^{\alpha},

which implies the following inequality:

{\|P\|}_{L^{\infty}(B)}\leq c^{\prime}(n,k)cw(E)+c^{\prime}(n,k)\max\limits_{|\alpha|=k}|\partial^{\alpha}P(x_{0})|.

Noting that
w(E)\leq 2
, and for the moment choosing
c\leq\frac{1}{4c^{\prime}}
, we get
|\partial^{\alpha}P(x_{0})|\geq\frac{1}{2c^{\prime}}
for some
\alpha
with
|\alpha|=k
. Now suppose
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{n})
with
\alpha_{i}=\max\limits_{1\leq j\leq n}\alpha_{j}
and let
\alpha^{\prime}=(\alpha_{1},..,\alpha_{i}-1..,\alpha_{n})
. Then we note that in the derivative
\partial^{\alpha^{\prime}}P
,
x_{i}
appears in one and only one term with the coefficient
\partial^{\alpha}P(x_{0})
. So the polynomial
\partial^{\alpha^{\prime}}P(x)
has the form

\partial^{\alpha^{\prime}}P(x)=a\cdot x+b,\quad|a|\geq\frac{1}{2c^{\prime}},

and if we normalize it by setting
\nu=\frac{a}{|a|}
and
b^{\prime}=\frac{b}{|a|}
, for all
x\in E
we get

|\nu\cdot x+b^{\prime}|\leq 2c^{\prime}cw(E),

which means that
w(E,\nu)\leq 4c^{\prime}cw(E)
. So if we take
c(n,k)=\frac{1}{8c^{\prime}}
we get the desired contradiction and this finishes the proof.
∎

In the next two lemmas we show that how (
6
) can be relaxed so that we can replace
\|P\|_{C^{k-1}(E)}
by a smaller quantity
\max\limits_{(x,\beta)\in F}|\partial^{\beta}P(x)|
, where
F
is a set of pairs of points and indices with
\#F=\text{dim}(\mathbb{P}_{k})
. The first one is a general geometric result bounding the width of a compact set in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with the width of its finite subsets with
d+1
points.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
E
be a bounded subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\varepsilon>0
such that for each
d
points
x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{d}
in
E
, there exists a unit vector
\nu
satisfying
|\nu\cdot x_{i}|\leq\varepsilon
for
1\leq i\leq d
. Then there is a unit vector
\nu_{0}
such that
|\nu_{0}\cdot x|\leq 2d\varepsilon
for every
x\in E
.

Proof.

First we note that the assumption on
E
holds for
\bar{E}
too, which allows us to assume
E
is compact. This implies that there are
x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{d-1}\in E
such that the
(d-1)
-dimensional area of the simplex
\Delta(0,x_{1},..x_{d-1})
is maximal. Let
A=|\Delta(0,x_{1},..x_{d-1})|
and note that if
A=0
,
E
entirely lies in a
d-2
dimensional subspace and
\nu_{0}
can be taken as a unit vector perpendicular to that. Then suppose
A>0
and let
\nu_{0}
be the normal vector to
H=span(x_{1},...,x_{d-1})
. We prove this vector has the desired property, which means that for any
x\in E
we have to show that
|\nu_{0}\cdot x|\leq 2d\varepsilon
or equivalently
d(x,H)\leq 2d\varepsilon
.

Now from the assumption there is a unit vector
\nu
such that
|\nu\cdot x_{i}|\leq\varepsilon
,
|\nu\cdot x|\leq\varepsilon
, which implies that the
d
dimensional simplex
\Delta(0,x,x_{1},...,x_{d-1})
lies between two parallel hyperplane of distance
2\varepsilon
from each other. Consequently, the length of one of the altitudes of this simplex must be no more than
2\varepsilon
. Let us call the length of this altitude
h
and the area of its corresponding face
A^{\prime}
. There are two cases, either
0
is one of the vertices of this face or not. In the former case
A^{\prime}\leq A
, by maximality of
A
, and in the latter,
A^{\prime}\leq dA
. The reason for this is the fact that the area of each face of a simplex is no more than the sum of areas of other faces, and all the other faces have
0
as a vertex. Now that we know
A^{\prime}\leq dA
, we calculate the
d
dimensional volume of the simplex
\Delta(0,x,x_{1},...,x_{d-1})
in two ways with different altitudes and faces, and we get
d(x,H)A=hA^{\prime}\leq 2\varepsilon.dA
. Hence
d(x,H)\leq 2d\varepsilon
and for each
x\in E
,
|\nu_{0}\cdot x|\leq 2d\varepsilon
.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
k
be a positive integer,
B
the unit ball of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
E\subset B
satisfying

(7)

{\|P\|}_{C^{k-1}(E)}>\delta{\|P\|}_{L^{\infty}(B)},\quad P\in\mathbb{P}_{k}.

Then there exists a set
F\subset\left\{(x,\beta)|x\in E,|\beta|\leq k-1\right\}
with
\#F=\text{dim}(\mathbb{P}_{k})
such that

(8)

\max\limits_{(x,\beta)\in F}|\partial^{\beta}P(x)|>c(n,k)\delta{\|P\|}_{L^{\infty}(B)},\quad P\in\mathbb{P}_{k}.

Proof.

For
P\in\mathbb{P}_{k}
with
P(y)=\sum\limits_{|\alpha|\leq k}c_{\alpha}y^{\alpha}
let
\hat{P}=(c_{\alpha})_{|\alpha|\leq k}
. Here,
\hat{P}
is a
d
-dimensional vector with
d=\text{dim}\mathbb{P}_{k}
, and we note that this defines an isomorphism between
\mathbb{P}_{k}
and
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then from the finiteness of the dimension we have
\|\hat{P}\|\approx{\|P\|}_{L^{\infty}(B)}
, where
\|\hat{P}\|
is the Euclidean norm in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. So in (
7
) we may replace
{\|P\|}_{L^{\infty}(B)}
by
\|\hat{P}\|
, and
\delta
by
c^{\prime}(n,k)\delta
. Then for each
x\in E
and
|\beta|\leq k-1
we set
\hat{(x,\beta)}=(b_{\alpha})_{|\alpha|\leq k}
, where this vector is defined by

b_{\alpha}=\begin{cases}\frac{\alpha!}{(\alpha-\beta)!}x^{\alpha-\beta}&\beta\leq\alpha\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases},

and it satisfies

\partial^{\beta}P(x)=\left\langle\hat{P},\hat{(x,\beta)}\right\rangle,\quad P\in\mathbb{P}_{k}.

Now consider the set

\hat{E}=\left\{\hat{(x,\beta)}|x\in E,|\beta|\leq k-1\right\},

which is bounded in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and note that if for every
d
points like
Q_{1},..,Q_{d}
in
\hat{E}
there exists a unit vector
\hat{P}
with
|\left\langle\hat{P},Q_{i}\right\rangle|\leq\frac{c^{\prime}(k,n)}{2d}\delta
, then according to Lemma
4.2
there is a unit vector
\hat{P}
such that
|\left\langle\hat{P},Q\right\rangle|\leq c^{\prime}(k,n)\delta
for each
Q\in\hat{E}
. This means that
\|P\|_{C^{k-1}(E)}\leq c^{\prime}(k,n)\delta
, which is a contradiction to (
7
). So there exists
d
points
Q_{1},..,Q_{d}
in
\hat{E}
such that

\inf_{\|\hat{P}\|=1}\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq d}|\left\langle\hat{P},Q_{i}\right\rangle|>\frac{c^{\prime}(k,n)}{2d}\delta,

and this means that there is a set
F\subset\left\{(x,\beta)|x\in E,|\beta|\leq k-1\right\}
with
\#F=\text{dim}(\mathbb{P}_{k})
such that

\max\limits_{(x,\beta)\in F}|\partial^{\beta}P(x)|>\frac{c^{\prime}(k,n)}{2d}\delta{\|\hat{P}\|},\quad P\in\mathbb{P}_{k}.

Now it’s enough to replace
\|\hat{P}\|
by
\|P\|_{L^{\infty}(B)}
and get (
8
).
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
E
be a subset of
B
, the unit ball of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, with
w(E)>0
. Then there exists a set
F\subset\left\{(x,\beta)|x\in E,|\beta|\leq k-1\right\}
with
\#F=\text{dim}(\mathbb{P}_{k})
, and a basis of polynomials
\left\{P_{(x,\beta)}\right\}
for
\mathbb{P}_{k}
such that for all
(x^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})\in F
we have

(9)

\partial^{\beta^{\prime}}(P_{(x,\beta)})(x^{\prime})=\begin{cases}1&(x^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})=(x,\beta)\\
0&(x^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})\neq(x,\beta)\end{cases}

Moreover,

(10)

\|P_{(x,\beta)}\|_{L^{\infty}(B)}\lesssim w(E)^{-1}\quad(x,\beta)\in F.

Proof.

From Lemmas
4.1
and
4.3
it follows that there exists a set
F
with
\#F=\text{dim}(\mathbb{P}_{k})
such that for every
P\in\mathbb{P}_{k}
we have

{\|P\|}_{L^{\infty}(B)}\lesssim w(E)^{-1}\max\limits_{(x,\beta)\in F}|\partial^{\beta}P(x)|.

This implies that the mapping
P\longrightarrow(\partial^{\beta}P(x))_{(x,\beta)\in F}
is an isomorphism from
\mathbb{P}_{k}
to
\mathbb{R}^{\text{dim}(\mathbb{P}_{k})}
, therefore for each
(x,\beta)\in F
, (
9
) has a unique solution
P_{x,\beta}
. These polynomials satisfy (
10
), and since
\#F=\text{dim}(\mathbb{P}_{k})
, they form a basis for
\mathbb{P}_{k}
, and this finishes the proof. ∎

Our last lemma in this section is a simple generalization of boundedness of norms of atoms in
{H}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
(see
[
12
]
Ch.3 Further results 5.5).

Lemma 4.5
.

Suppose
B
is the unit ball of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
f
a distribution supported in
B
and of the form
f=g+\sum\limits_{(x,\beta)\in F}c_{x,\beta}\partial^{\beta}\delta_{x}
with
\|g\|_{{L}^{\infty}}\leq 1
,
|c_{x,\beta}|\leq 1
, and
F
a nonempty finite set of pairs
(x,\beta)
such that
x\in B
and
|\beta|<n(1/p-1)
. Moreover, suppose all moments of
f
vanishes up to order
N_{p}=[n(1/p-1)]
. Then
f\in{H}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\|f\|_{{H}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\#F
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B)
,
0\leq\varphi\leq 1
with
\int\varphi=1
. We estimate
M_{\varphi}(f)
by following the usual local-nonlocal argument. For
x_{0}\in 3B
we have

\varphi_{t}*f(x_{0})=\varphi_{t}*g(x_{0})+\sum\limits_{(x,\beta)\in F}(-1)^{\beta}c_{x,\beta}t^{-n-|\beta|}(\partial^{\beta}\varphi)(\frac{x_{0}-x}{t})=\textrm{I}+\textrm{II}.

Now for
t>0
,
|\textrm{I}|\leq 1
, and we note that in the second term
(\partial^{\beta}\varphi)(\frac{x_{0}-x}{t})\neq 0
only if
t>|x-x_{0}|
, so this term is bounded by

|\textrm{II}|\lesssim\sum\limits_{(x,\beta)\in F}|x-x_{0}|^{-n-|\beta|}.

This gives us an estimate for the local part and it remains to estimate
M_{\varphi}(f)
on
(3B)^{c}
, where we have to use cancellation properties of
f
. So this time assume
x_{0}\notin 3B
and note that since
\varphi_{t}(x_{0}-\cdot)
is supported in
B_{t}(x_{0})
,
f*\varphi_{t}(x_{0})\neq 0
only if
t>|x_{0}|/2
. To use the cancellation properties of
f
, let
P
be the Taylor polynomial of
\varphi_{t}(x_{0}-\cdot)
around
0
and of order
N_{p}
. Then we have

\displaystyle f*\varphi_{t}(x_{0})=\left\langle f,\varphi_{t}(x_{0}-\cdot)\right\rangle=\left\langle f,\varphi_{t}(x_{0}-\cdot)-P\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\int g(y)(\varphi_{t}(x_{0}-y)-P(y))dy+\sum\limits_{(x,\beta)\in F}(-1)^{\beta}c_{x,\beta}\partial^{\beta}(\varphi_{t}(x_{0}-y)-P(y))|_{y=x}

\displaystyle=\textrm{I}+\textrm{II}.

Now from the Taylor remainder theorem it follows that for
y\in B
we have

|\varphi_{t}(x_{0}-y)-P(y)|\leq\|\nabla^{N_{p}+1}(\varphi_{t})\|_{{L}^{\infty}}\lesssim t^{-(N_{p}+1+n)},

therefore
|\textrm{I}|\lesssim|x_{0}|^{-(N_{p}+1+n)}
. To estimate the second term we note that
\partial^{\beta}P
is the
(N_{p}-|\beta|)
-th order Taylor polynomial of
\partial^{\beta}(\varphi_{t}(x_{0}-\cdot))
around
0
, and this gives us

|\partial^{\beta}(\varphi_{t}(x_{0}-y)-P(y))|\lesssim\|\nabla^{N_{p}-|\beta|+1}(\partial^{\beta}(\varphi_{t}))\|_{{L}^{\infty}}\lesssim t^{-(N_{p}+1+n)}\lesssim|x_{0}|^{-(N_{p}+1+n)},

so we have
|\textrm{II}|\lesssim|x_{0}|^{-(N_{p}+1+n)}
. Next, by bringing the estimates for the local and nonlocal parts together we obtain

M_{\varphi}(f)(x_{0})\lesssim\left(1+\sum\limits_{(x,\beta)\in F}|x-x_{0}|^{-n-|\beta|}\right)\mathbbm{1}_{3B}(x_{0})+|x_{0}|^{-(N_{p}+1+n)}\mathbbm{1}_{(3B)^{c}}(x_{0}).

Now using the above estimate and our assumptions on
\beta
and
N_{p}
gives us

\|f\|_{{H}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\|M_{\varphi}(f)\|_{{L}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\#F,

which completes the proof.
∎

With the above lemmas in hand we are ready to prove the sufficiency part of the Theorem
3.2
.

Proof of sufficiency.

According to Lemma
2.6
it is enough to extend each
(p,\Omega)
-atom to an element of
{H}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with a uniform bound on its norm.

Case
p=1
. Take a
(1,\Omega)-
atom
g
supported on a ball
B_{r}(x_{0})
, with
r\approx d(x_{0})
. Now, from the assumption there are positive constants
a
and
\delta
such that (
2
) holds. Then we extend
g
by putting a constant on
\Omega^{c}\cap B_{ar}(x_{0})
, to create the required cancellation. So let

\tilde{g}=g-c.\mathbbm{1}_{\Omega^{c}\cap B_{ar}(x_{0})},\quad c=\frac{\int g}{|\Omega^{c}\cap B_{ar}(x_{0})|}.

Here, we note that
\text{supp}(\tilde{g})\subset B_{ar}(x_{0})
,
\int\tilde{g}=0
and this function is bounded by

\|\tilde{g}\|_{{L}^{\infty}}\leq\|g\|_{{L}^{\infty}}\left(1+a^{-n}\frac{|B_{ar}(x_{0})|}{|\Omega^{c}\cap B_{ar}(x_{0})|}\right)\lesssim\|g\|_{{L}^{\infty}}\left(1+a^{-n}\delta^{-1}\right)\lesssim_{a,\delta}|B_{ar}(x_{0})|^{-1}.

Hence
\tilde{g}
is a constant multiple of a 1-atom so
\|\tilde{g}\|_{{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq c(a,\delta,n)
, and this finishes the proof of this case.

Case
p=\frac{n}{k+n}
,
k=1,2,...
. Let
g
be a
(p,\Omega)
-atom similar to the previous case, and let
a>1
,
\delta>0
be the constants that satisfy (
3
). By a translation and dilation, we bring everything to the unit ball and set

E=\frac{\left(\Omega^{c}\cap\bar{B}_{ar}\right)-x_{0}}{ar},\quad g^{\prime}=g(x_{0}+arx).

Now
E
is a subset of the unit ball
B
with
w(E)>a^{-1}\delta
, so let
F
be a set provided by Lemma
4.4
. Then we extend
g^{\prime}
by

\widetilde{g^{\prime}}=g^{\prime}+\sum\limits_{(x,\beta)\in F}c_{x,\beta}\partial^{\beta}\delta_{x},

where the coefficient
c_{x,\beta}
are chosen in way that
\widetilde{g^{\prime}}
has vanishing moments up to order
k
. To see this can be done, recall that from Lemma
4.4
we may choose a basis of polynomials for
\mathbb{P}_{k}

\left\{P_{x,\beta}\right\}
, such that
\partial^{\beta}(P_{x,\beta})(x)=1
and
\partial^{\beta^{\prime}}(P_{x,\beta})(x^{\prime})=0
when
(x,\beta)\neq(x^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})
. Then the cancellation condition for
\widetilde{g^{\prime}}
is equivalent to

\left\langle\widetilde{g^{\prime}},P_{x,\beta}\right\rangle=0\quad(x,\beta)\in F,

which gives us the following equation for the coefficients
c_{x,\beta}
:

(11)

\int g^{\prime}(y)P_{x,\beta}(y)dy+\sum\limits_{(x^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})\in F}c_{x^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}}\left\langle\partial^{\beta^{\prime}}\delta_{x^{\prime}},P_{x,\beta}\right\rangle=0\quad(x,\beta)\in F.

Now we note that

(12)

\left\langle\partial^{\beta^{\prime}}\delta_{x^{\prime}},P_{x,\beta}\right\rangle=(-1)^{|\beta^{\prime}|}\partial^{\beta^{\prime}}(P_{x,\beta})(x^{\prime}),

so from (
11
), (
12
) and Lemma
4.4
we have

(13)

c_{x,\beta}=(-1)^{|\beta|+1}\int g^{\prime}(y)P_{x,\beta}(y)dy.

This shows that
\widetilde{g^{\prime}}
depends linearly on
g
,
\left\langle\tilde{g^{\prime}},P\right\rangle=0
for every
P\in\mathbb{P}_{k}
, and moreover

\displaystyle|c_{x,\beta}|\leq|B|\|g^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(B)}\|P_{x,\beta}\|_{L^{\infty}(B)}\lesssim\|g^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(B)}w(E)^{-1}\lesssim a\delta^{-1}\|g^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(B)}.

Now
\widetilde{g^{\prime}}/\|g^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(B)}
satisfies all the required conditions of Lemma
4.5
and by applying that we get

\|\tilde{g^{\prime}}\|_{{H}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|g^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(B)}\lesssim_{a,\delta}|B_{ar}(x_{0})|^{\frac{-1}{p}}.

Finally, let
\tilde{g}=\widetilde{g^{\prime}}(\frac{x-x_{0}}{ar})
which is an extension of
g
with
\|\tilde{g}\|_{{H}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq c(n,k,a,\delta)
, and this completes the proof of the second case.

Case
p\neq\frac{n}{n+k}
,
k=0,1,2,...
. This case is similar the previous one and even simpler. This time we can take
a
to be any number such that
E={\Omega}^{c}\cap B_{ar}\neq\emptyset
and after a dilation and translation, we pick a point
x\in E
and extend
g^{\prime}
by

\widetilde{g^{\prime}}=g^{\prime}+\sum\limits_{|\beta|\leq[n(1/p-1)]}c_{\beta}\partial^{\beta}\delta_{x},\quad c_{\beta}=(-1)^{|\beta|+1}\int g^{\prime}(y)\frac{(y-x)^{\beta}}{\beta!}dy.

The rest is just like the previous case.

In all the above cases, the method of extension is linear in terms of
g
so from Lemma
2.6
, there is a linear extension operator if the conditions of Theorem
3.2
hold.
∎

5.
Proof of necessity

Now that we’ve proved the conditions of Theorem
3.2
are sufficient, we have to show they are necessary as well. To do this, we need to construct some functions in the dual of
{H}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to prevent any bounded extension when those conditions are not satisfied. We need some lemmas and our first one is a simple consequence of a theorem proved by Uchiyama. We give it below and its proof is found in
[
13
,
4
]
.

Theorem

(Uchiyama)
.

Let
\lambda>0
and
E
,
B
be two subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that for any cube
Q
we have

(14)

\min\left\{\frac{|Q\cap E|}{|Q|},\frac{|Q\cap B|}{|Q|}\right\}\leq 2^{-2n\lambda}.

Then there exists a function
f\in{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
f=1
on
B
and
f=0
on
E
with
\|f\|_{{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\frac{1}{\lambda}
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
0<\varepsilon\leq\frac{1}{2}
,
a>3
and
B
the unit ball of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then for every set
E\subset(2B)^{c}
such that
|E\cap aB|<\varepsilon
, there exists a function
f\in{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with the following properties:

•

f=0
on
E

•

f=1
on
B

•

\|f\|_{{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\frac{1}{\min\left\{\log a,\log\varepsilon^{-1}\right\}}

Proof.

Let us check the condition of the above theorem for
B
and
E
. If a cube
Q
doesn’t intersect one of the sets (
14
) holds for any
\lambda>0
, so assume it intersects both of them. Then there are two possibilities either
Q\cap(aB)^{c}\neq\emptyset
or
Q\subset aB
. In the first case
l(Q)\geq\frac{(a-2)}{\sqrt{n}}
which implies
\frac{|Q\cap B|}{|Q|}\lesssim a^{-n}
, and in the second one we have
l(Q)\geq 1
, which gives us
\frac{|Q\cap A|}{|Q|}=\frac{|Q\cap E\cap aB|}{|Q|}\leq\varepsilon
. So for
\lambda\approx\min\left\{\log a,\log\varepsilon^{-1}\right\}
(
14
) holds and the claim follows from the theorem.
∎

To construct similar functions of the above lemma in
\dot{\Lambda}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we need a couple of facts which are well known and we bring here as lemmas. Here it’s convenient to write
\text{BMO}(\mathbb{R})=\dot{\Lambda}^{0}(\mathbb{R})
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
f\in\dot{\Lambda}^{k}(\mathbb{R})
and
F(x)=\int_{0}^{x}f(t)dt
, Then
F\in\dot{\Lambda}^{k+1}(\mathbb{R})
with
\|F\|_{\dot{\Lambda}^{k+1}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\dot{\Lambda}^{k}(\mathbb{R})}
for
k=0,1,2,...
.

Proof.

For
k=0
we have

|\Delta^{2}_{h}F(x)/h|=\left|1/h\int_{x+h}^{x+2h}f-1/h\int_{x}^{x+h}f\right|\lesssim\|f\|_{\text{BMO}(\mathbb{R})},

and for
k\geq 1
we have

\Delta^{k+2}_{h}F(x)=\Delta^{k+1}_{h}(\Delta^{1}_{h}F)(x)=\Delta^{k+1}_{h}\int_{0}^{h}f(x+t)dt=\int_{0}^{h}(\Delta^{k+1}_{h}f)(x+t)dt,

which implies

|\frac{\Delta^{k+2}_{h}F(x)}{h^{k+1}}|\leq\frac{1}{|h|}\int_{0}^{h}|\frac{\Delta^{k+1}_{h}}{h^{k}}|(x+t)dt\leq\|f\|_{\dot{\Lambda}^{k}(\mathbb{R})}.

So in any case we get
\|F\|_{\dot{\Lambda}^{k+1}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\dot{\Lambda}^{k}(\mathbb{R})}
.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
k
be a positive integer,
f\in\dot{\Lambda}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
g\in C^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then
fg\in\dot{\Lambda}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\|fg\|_{\dot{\Lambda}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\sum_{l=1}^{k}\|f\|_{\dot{\Lambda}^{l}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|\nabla^{k-l}(g)\|_{{L}^{\infty}}+\|f\|_{{L}^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{k}(g)\|_{{L}^{\infty}}
.

Proof.

Let
\tau^{h}g(x)=g(x+h)
, so for
\Delta^{1}_{h}(fg)
we have

(15)

\Delta^{1}_{h}(fg)=\Delta^{1}_{h}(f)\tau^{h}g+f\Delta^{1}_{h}(g).

By taking the difference operator
k+1
times and applying (
15
) each time we can see that
\Delta^{k+1}_{h}(fg)
has the form

(16)

\Delta^{k+1}_{h}(fg)=\sum\limits_{-1\leq l\leq k}\sum\limits_{0\leq m,s\leq k+1}c_{l,k,m,s}\tau^{mh}\Delta^{l+1}_{h}(f)\tau^{sh}\Delta^{k-l}_{h}(g),

where in this formula
\Delta^{0}_{h}(f)=f
and
c_{l,k,m,s}
are some integers. Now for
1\leq l\leq k
we have

|\Delta^{l+1}_{h}(f)|\leq\|f\|_{\dot{\Lambda}^{l}(\mathbb{R})}|h|^{l},\quad|\Delta^{k-l}_{h}(g)|\lesssim\|\nabla^{k-l}(g)\|_{{L}^{\infty}}|h|^{k-l},

where
\|\nabla^{0}(g)\|_{{L}^{\infty}}=\|g\|_{{L}^{\infty}}
,
and for
l=-1,0
we have

|\Delta^{l+1}_{h}(f)|\lesssim\|f\|_{{L}^{\infty}},\quad|\Delta^{k-l}_{h}(g)|\lesssim\|\nabla^{k}(g)\|_{{L}^{\infty}}|h|^{k}.

Putting these estimates in (
16
) completes the proof.

∎

One more property of
\dot{\Lambda}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
that is needed here is the inequality

(17)

\|f\|_{\dot{\Lambda}^{l}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|f\|_{{L}^{\infty}}^{1-\frac{l}{k}}\|f\|_{\dot{\Lambda}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\frac{l}{k}}\quad 1\leq l\leq k,

which is a consequence of the fact that
\dot{\Lambda}^{l}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the interpolation space between
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\dot{\Lambda}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
(see
[
10
]
Theorem 17.30).

Lemma 5.4
.

Suppose
0<\varepsilon<1/4
,
a>4
and let
I\subset[-a,a]
be an interval with
|I|<2\varepsilon
. Then for each
k=1,2,3,...
there exists a function
f_{k}\in\dot{\Lambda}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{1})
with the following properties:

•

f_{k}=0
on
I\cup[-a,a]^{c}

•

|f_{k}|\gtrsim\min\{\log a,\log\varepsilon^{-1}\}
on
J=[x_{0}-1/2,x_{0}+1/2]^{c}\cap[-1,1]

•

\|f_{k}\|_{{L}^{\infty}}\lesssim a^{k}

•

\|f_{k}\|_{\dot{\Lambda}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{1})}\lesssim 1

Proof.

Suppose
x_{0}
is the mid point of
I
and consider the function

g(t)=\min\left\{\log^{+}|\frac{t-x_{0}}{\varepsilon}|,\log^{+}|\frac{a}{t}|\right\},

which is nonnegative, vanishes on
I\cup[-a,a]^{c}
,
g\geq\min\{\log a,\log\frac{\varepsilon^{-1}}{4}\}
on the interval
[x_{0}-1/4,x_{0}+1/4]^{c}\cap[-1,1]
and also
\|g\|_{\text{BMO}}\lesssim 1
. Now let
F_{1}(x)=\int_{x_{0}}^{x}g(t)dt
which vanishes on
I
and
|F_{1}|\gtrsim\min\{\log a,\log\varepsilon^{-1}\}
on
J=[x_{0}-1/2,x_{0}+1/2]^{c}\cap[-1,1]
. Also, according to Lemma
5.2
we have
\|F_{1}\|_{\dot{\Lambda}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{1})}\lesssim 1
and we note that

|F_{1}(x)|\leq\int_{-a}^{a}g(t)dt\leq\int_{-a}^{a}\log^{+}|\frac{a}{t}|dt\lesssim a.

Now let
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}([-1,1])
such that
0\leq\varphi\leq 1
,
\varphi=1
on
[-1/2,1/2]
and set
\varphi_{a}(x)=\varphi(x/a)
. Then the function
f_{1}(x)=F_{1}(x)\varphi_{a}(x)
has the following properties:

•

f_{1}=0
on
I\cup[-a,a]^{c}

•

f_{1}
is positive on the right hand side of
x_{0}
and negative on the other side with
|f_{1}|\gtrsim\min\{\log a,\log\varepsilon^{-1}\}
on
J

•

\|f_{1}\|_{{L}^{\infty}}\lesssim a

•

\|f_{1}\|_{\dot{\Lambda}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{1})}\lesssim 1

For the last property it’s enough to note that

\|F_{1}\|_{\dot{\Lambda}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{1})}\lesssim 1,\quad\|F_{1}\|_{{L}^{\infty}}\lesssim a,\quad\|\varphi_{a}\|_{{L}^{\infty}}\leq 1,\quad\|\varphi_{a}^{\prime}\|_{{L}^{\infty}}\lesssim a^{-1},

and therefore Lemma
5.3
implies
\|f_{1}\|_{\dot{\Lambda}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{1})}\lesssim 1
. We continue this process, meaning each time we integrate the function of the previous step from
x_{0}
and with
\varphi_{a}
cut it off the interval
[-a,a]
then at k-th step we have
F_{k}(x)=\int_{x_{0}}^{x}f_{k-1}(t)dt
and
f_{k}=F_{k}\varphi_{a}
.

The function
F_{k}
vanishes on
I
, is positive on the right hand side of
x_{0}
and negative or positive (depending on
k
) on the other side. Also, it follows from Lemma
5.2
that

(18)

\|F_{k}\|_{\dot{\Lambda}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{1})}\lesssim 1,

and moreover

(19)

\|F_{k}\|_{{L}^{\infty}}\leq\int_{-a}^{a}|f_{k-1}|\lesssim a^{k}.

Now from (
17
-
19
) we get
\|F_{k}\|_{\dot{\Lambda}^{l}(\mathbb{R}^{1})}\lesssim a^{k-l}
for
1\leq l\leq k
. We also have the bound
\|\varphi_{a}^{(k-l)}\|_{{L}^{\infty}}\lesssim a^{l-k}
for
0\leq l\leq k
. Now these facts combined with Lemma
5.3
shows that
\|f_{k}\|_{\dot{\Lambda}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{1})}\lesssim 1
.
Finally we observe that at the previous step,
f_{k-1}
changes sign only at
x_{0}
, which implies that by integrating it from
x_{0}
no cancellation happens and then
|f_{k}|\gtrsim\min\{\log a,\log\varepsilon^{-1}\}
on
J
. The proof is now complete.
∎

The following is an analog of Lemma
5.1
in the
\dot{\Lambda}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
setting.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
0<\varepsilon<1/4
,
a>4
and
B
the unit ball of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then for every set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
w(E\cap aB)<\varepsilon
, there exists a function
f\in\dot{\Lambda}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that

•

f=0
on
E

•

\int_{B}|f|\approx 1

•

\|f\|_{\dot{\Lambda}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\frac{1}{\min\left\{\log a,\log\varepsilon^{-1}\right\}}

Proof.

Let
\nu
be a direction for which
w(E\cap aB,\nu)\leq\varepsilon
and choose
c
such that
|\nu\cdot x+c|\leq\varepsilon
for
x\in E\cap aB
. Also let
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B)
with
0\leq\varphi\leq 1
and
\varphi=1
on
\frac{1}{2}B
. Now take the function
f_{k}\in\dot{\Lambda}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{1})
constructed in the previous lemma with
I=\left[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon\right]
and set

f(x)=\frac{1}{\min\left\{\log a,\log\varepsilon^{-1}\right\}}f_{k}(\nu\cdot x+c)\varphi(x/a).

This function has the desired properties. In fact the first two properties of
f
follows directly from properties of
f_{k}
, and for the third one we note that
\|f_{k}(\nu\cdot+c)\|_{\dot{\Lambda}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\|f_{k}\|_{\dot{\Lambda}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{1})}\lesssim 1
, so an application of Lemma
5.3
gives us
\|f\|_{\dot{\Lambda}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\frac{1}{\min\left\{\log a,\log\varepsilon^{-1}\right\}}
.
∎

Now that we have constructed our functions in the dual of Hardy spaces we can prove the necessity part of Theorem
3.2
.

Proof of necessity.

We have two cases:

Case
p=1
. Suppose
\Omega
doesn’t satisfy (
2
) then for
\delta_{j}=2^{-j}
and
a_{j}=j
there’s a point
x_{j}
such that (
2
) doesn’t hold. Let us denote
B_{j}=B(x_{j},\frac{1}{2}d(x_{j}))
and
r_{j}=\frac{1}{2}d(x_{j})
.

Now suppose
\Omega
still is an extension domain and take a sequence of
(1,\Omega)
-atoms
g_{j}
with supporting ball
B_{j}
and denote their extensions by
\tilde{g_{j}}
. Then we must have
\|\tilde{g_{j}}\|_{{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim 1
. Now by a translation and dilation we move every thing to the unit ball and set

E_{j}=\frac{\Omega^{c}-x_{j}}{r_{j}},\quad g^{\prime}_{j}(x)=r_{j}^{n}g_{j}(r_{j}x+x_{j}),\quad\tilde{g_{j}}^{\prime}(x)=r_{j}^{n}\tilde{g_{j}}(r_{j}x+x_{j}).

Here we note that
E_{j}
satisfies the condition in Lemma
5.1
with
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{j}\approx\delta_{j}a_{j}^{n}
and
a=a_{j}
. So there exists a function
f_{j}\in{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with properties of Lemma
5.1
. Now from duality and the fact that
\|\tilde{g_{j}}^{\prime}\|_{{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim 1

|\left\langle\tilde{g_{j}}^{\prime},f_{j}\right\rangle|\lesssim\|\tilde{g_{j}}^{\prime}\|_{{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|f_{j}\|_{{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\frac{1}{\min\left\{\log a_{j},\log\varepsilon_{j}^{-1}\right\}}

and on the other hand since
f_{j}=0
on
E_{j}
we have

(20)

\left\langle\tilde{g_{j}}^{\prime},f_{j}\right\rangle=\int_{B_{1}}g_{j}^{\prime}f_{j},

which implies the following contradiction if we choose
g_{j}=\text{sgn}(f_{j}).|B_{j}|^{-1}

1\approx\int_{B_{1}}|f_{j}|\approx|\left\langle\tilde{g_{j}}^{\prime},f_{j}\right\rangle|\lesssim\frac{1}{\min\left\{\log a_{j},\log\varepsilon_{j}^{-1}\right\}}\longrightarrow 0.

The proof of this case is now complete.

Case
p\neq 1
. The above argument holds word by word if instead of Lemma
5.1
we use Lemma
5.5
and note that the functions
f_{j}
vanishes in a neighborhood of
E_{j}
so (

) holds.

This finishes the proof of necessity.
∎

Corollary 6.1

When
\Omega

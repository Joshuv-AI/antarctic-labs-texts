Abstract
The Continuity Problem is the question whether effective operators are continuous, where an effective operator F F is a function on a space of constructively given objects x x , defined by mapping construction instructions for x x to instructions for F ⁡ ( x ) F(x) in a computable way. In the present paper the problem is dealt with in a bi-topological setting. To this end the topological setting developed by the author [ 22 ] is extended to the bi-topological case. Under very natural conditions it is shown that an effective operator F F between bi-topological spaces 𝒯 = ( T , τ , σ ) \mathcal{T}=(T,\tau,\sigma) and 𝒯 ′ = ( T ′ , τ ′ , σ ′ ) \mathcal{T}^{\prime}=(T^{\prime},\tau^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}) is (effectively) continuous, if τ ′ \tau^{\prime} is (effectively) regular with respect to σ ′ \sigma^{\prime} . A central requirement on 𝒯 ′ \mathcal{T}^{\prime} is that bases of the neighbourhood filters of the points in T ′ T^{\prime} can computably be enumerated in a uniform way, not only with respect to topology τ ′ \tau^{\prime} , but also with respect to σ ′ \sigma^{\prime} . As follows from an example by Friedberg, the last condition is indispensable. Conversely, it is proved that (effectively) bi-continuous operators are effective. Prominent examples of bi-topological spaces are quasi-metric spaces. Under a very reasonable computability requirement on the quasi-metric it is shown that all effectivity assumptions made in the general results are satisfied in the quasi-metric case.

Introduction

Whereas in classical mathematics, one can prove the existence of a mathematical object without “finding” the object explicitly, by assuming its non-existence and then deriving a contradiction, in a constructive approach a
construction
of the object has to be presented and it has to be shown that the object thus constructed has the property initially stated. But what is a construction? In the Russian school of constructive mathematics (founded by A.A. Markov, Jr. in the late 1940s-early 1950s) a construction is an
algorithm
. For the latter notion many equivalent formalisations are known.

Let us consider the real numbers as an example. In classical real analysis, one way to define a real number is to establish an equivalence class of Cauchy sequences of rational numbers. In Markov-style constructive mathematics accordingly a real number is constructed by presenting a program
P
that takes a positive integer
n
and outputs a rational
a_{n}
, together with a program
Q
that takes a positive integer
n
and outputs a positive integer
N_{n}
such that

(\forall n)(\forall i,j\geq N_{n})\,|a_{i}-a_{j}|\leq 2^{-n}.

We can use
P
and
Q
together to compute as close a rational approximation as we like to the real number they represent.

We see that in Markov-style constructive analysis real numbers can be represented by pairs of programs. Note that programs are just pieces of text. An
effective operator

F
from Markov reals to Markov reals (or, in general, between sets of constructive objects) is then a map
F
for which there is a program translating programs
A_{x}
representing objects
x
in the domain of
F
into programs
B_{F(x)}
representing
F(x)
.

The
continuity problem
is the question whether effective operators are continuous.

For several classes of effectively presented spaces positive answers have been given: Classes of continuous domains (directed-complete partial orders) have been studied in
[
7
,
8
,
16
,
18
,
24
]
and of metric spaces in
[
5
,
13
,
14
,
15
]
. In
[
22
]
the present author studied the problem in a general topological setting. A natural condition was presented forcing effective operators between
T_{0}
spaces to be continuous. The condition is satisfied in the cases just mentioned, i.e., continuous domains and metric spaces. Moreover, in the latter case the result could be extended to regular spaces.

In the present paper we will push the limit even further and show that effective operators between pairwise regular bi-topological spaces are bi-continuous.

The generalisation to bi-topological spaces
(T,\tau,\sigma)
is a natural step. As is well known from
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
]
, the continuity of an effective operator
F\colon T\rightarrow T^{\prime}
cannot be derived in a constructive setting without
Markov’s principle
, which says for a computable map
\alpha
,

\neg\neg(\exists x\,\alpha\,x=0)\Rightarrow(\exists x\,\alpha\,x=0).

Thus, for any
y\in T
, we have to assume that
F
is not continuous at
y
. That is, for
V\in\tau^{\prime}
with
F(y)\in V
, we assume that

(\forall U\in\tau)[y\in U\Rightarrow F[U]\not\subseteq V].

In order to derive a contradiction, we need
F[U]\not\subseteq V
to hold constructively, which means we can effectively find
z\in U
so that
F(z)\notin V
. Regularity of
(T^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})
allows to find some
\widehat{V}\in\tau^{\prime}
with
F(y)\in\widehat{V}\subseteq\mathop{\mathstrut\rm cl}\nolimits(\widehat{V})\subseteq V
, where
\mathop{\mathstrut\rm cl}\nolimits(\widehat{V})
is the closure of
\widehat{V}
. Then
F[U]
hits the exterior
\mathop{\mathstrut\rm ext}\nolimits(\widehat{V})
of
\widehat{V}
. A central assumption for a space to be effectively presented is that for any (basic) open set its elements are effectively listable. So, by running through the listings for
U
and
\widehat{V}
we can effectively find a
z\in U
with
F(z)\in\mathop{\mathstrut\rm ext}\nolimits(\widehat{V})
.

If we assume that
\tau^{\prime}
is regular with respect to
\sigma^{\prime}
then we can proceed in a similar way. The only difference is that now we have that
\widehat{V}\in\sigma^{\prime}
with
\widehat{V}\supseteq T\setminus V
.

The paper is organised as follows: In Section
2
bi-topological spaces and their regularity are discussed. As a prominent example, quasi-pseudo-metric spaces are considered in Section
3
. In Section
4
effectivity requirements for topological spaces are recalled from
[
22
]
and extended to the bi-topological case. Computable quasi-metric spaces are studied in Section
5
. The general continuity results are derived in Section
6
and in Section
7

Bi-topological spaces

As is well known, on every topological space

Example 2.1

A typical example of a bi-topological space is the space

\mathcal{U}=\mbox{$\{\,(a,\infty)\mid a\in\mathbb{R}\,\}$}\cup\{\mathbb{R},\emptyset\}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{L}=\mbox{$\{\,(-\infty,a)\mid a\in\mathbb{R}\,\}$}\cup\{\mathbb{R},\emptyset\}.

.
The usual Euclidean topology on

For two topologies

[

]

(

Lemma 2.4
.

Let

Next, assume (

Quasi-pseudo-metric spaces

In this section we will consider an important subclass of bi-topological spaces.

Definition 3.1
.

A
quasi-pseudo-metric
on
X
is a function
\delta\colon X\times X\to[0,\infty)
such that for all
x,y,z\in X
,

1.

\delta(x,x)=0
,

2.

\delta(x,z)\leq\delta(x,y)+\delta(y,z)
.

If
\delta
satisfies the stronger condition

(
1
’)

\delta(x,y)=\delta(y,x)=0
only if
x=y
,

then
\delta
is a
quasi-metric
.

Every quasi-pseudo-metric defines a pre-order
\sqsubseteq_{\delta}
on
X
:
y\sqsubseteq_{\delta}z
, if
\delta(y,z)=0
.

Let
\delta
be a quasi-pseudo-metric on
X
. Then
\mathcal{X}=(X,\delta)
is called
quasi-pseudo-metric space
. Define
\delta^{c}
by
\delta^{c}(x,y)=\delta(y,x)
. As is easily verified,
\delta^{c}
is a quasi-pseudo-metric as well, called the
conjugate
quasi-pseudo-metric of
\delta
. Moreover,
\mathcal{X}^{c}=(X,\delta^{c})
is said to be the
dual
of
\mathcal{X}
.

Example 3.2
.

For real numbers
y,z
set

\delta_{L}(y,z)=\max\{z-y,0\}\quad\text{and}\quad\delta_{U}(y,z)=-\min\{z-y,0\}.

Then
\delta_{L}
and
\delta_{U}
are both quasi-metrics such that
\delta_{L}
is conjugate to
\delta_{U}
. Moreover,
y\sqsubseteq_{\delta_{L}}z
exactly if
y\geq z
, and
y\sqsubseteq_{\delta_{U}}z
exactly if
y\leq z
.

For
x\in X
and
r\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, let
\mathrm{B}_{\delta}(x,r)=\mbox{$\{\,y\in X\mid\delta(x,y)<r\,\}$}
be the ball of radius
r
about
x
, and
\mathrm{B}^{\leq}_{\delta}(x,r)=\mbox{$\{\,y\in X\mid\delta(x,y)\leq r\,\}$}
. As in the classical case of metric spaces, the collection of all balls forms a basis for a topology
\tau_{\delta}
, called the

of
\delta
. Its specialisation pre-order
\leq_{\tau_{\delta}}
coincides with the canonical pre-order
\sqsubseteq_{\delta}
associated with
\delta
. In Example
2.1
,
\tau_{\delta_{L}}=\mathcal{L}
and
\tau_{\delta_{U}}=\mathcal{U}
.

Similarly,
\delta^{c}
determines a topology
\tau_{\delta^{c}}
for

. Thus the natural topological structure associated with a quasi-pseudo-metric on a set

is that of set

with two topologies
\tau_{\delta}
and
\tau_{\delta^{c}}

[

, for every fixed

\delta(x,y)
is a
\tau_{\delta}
-upper semi-continuous and
\tau_{\delta^{c}}
-lower semi-continuous function of

Set
\delta^{*}(x,y)=\max\{\delta(x,y),\delta^{c}(x,y)\}
. Then
\delta^{*}
is a pseudo-metric on

, called the

of
\delta
. The topology
\tau_{\delta^{*}}
generated by

is equivalent to the symmetrisation topology of
\tau_{\delta}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{Q}
be a dense subset of
X
with respect to
\tau_{\delta^{*}}
and
\mathbb{D}=\mbox{$\{\,2^{-e}\mid e\in\mathbb{N}\,\}$}

\{\,\mathrm{B}_{\delta}(u,p)\mid u\in\mathcal{Q}\wedge p\in\mathbb{D}\,\}

is a basis of
\tau_{\delta}
.

Proof.

By definition,
\tau_{\delta}
is generated by the balls
\mathrm{B}_{\delta}(x,r)
with
x\in X
and
r\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Let
y\in\mathrm{B}_{\delta}(x,r)
. Then
\delta(x,y)<r
. Let

with
a_{y}\leq r-\delta(x,y)
and choose
u_{y}\in\mathrm{B}_{\delta}(y,a_{y}/2)\cap\mathrm{B}_{\delta^{c}}(y,a_{y}/2)\cap\mathcal{Q}
. We show that

1.

y\in\mathrm{B}_{\delta}(u_{y},a_{y}/2)
,

2.

\mathrm{B}_{\delta}(u_{y},a_{y}/2)\subseteq\mathrm{B}_{\delta}(y,a_{y})
,

3.

\mathrm{B}_{\delta}(y,a_{y})\subseteq\mathrm{B}_{\delta}(x,r)

Then

\mathrm{B}_{\delta}(x,r)=\bigcup\mbox{$\{\,\mathrm{B}_{\delta}(u_{y},a_{y}/2)\mid y\in\mathrm{B}_{\delta}(x,r)\wedge a_{y}\in\mathbb{D}\wedge a_{y}\leq r-\delta(x,y)\,\}$},

from which the statement follows.

(

(

) Let

(

\delta(x,z)\leq\delta(x,y)+\delta(y,z)<\delta(x,y)+a_{y}\leq r.\qed

Note that in (

The proposition that we are going to derive next is a special case of

Effective spaces

In the remainder of this paper we will concentrate on spaces the elements of which are constructive. In our context this means that they are constructed by a computation process. Hence, they can be represented by a program. Note, however, that not every program needs to determine a construction process. A program is a finite piece of text and can therefore be coded into a natural number.

Let
\langle\;,\;\rangle:\mathbb{N}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
be a
computable pairing function with corresponding projections
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
such that
\pi_{i}(\langle a_{1},a_{2}\rangle)=a_{i}
. We inductively extend the pairing function to an
n
-tuple encoding for

\langle a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\rangle=\langle\langle a_{1},\ldots,a_{n-1}\rangle,a_{n}\rangle

Let

denote the set of all
n
-ary partial (total) computable functions, and let

be the domain of the
i
th partial computable function
\varphi_{i}
with respect
to some Gödel numbering
\varphi
. Moreover, we let
\varphi_{i}(a)\mathclose{\downarrow}
mean that the computation of
\varphi_{i}(a)
stops, and
\varphi_{i}(a)\mathclose{\downarrow}\in C
that it stops with value in
C
.

Let
S
be a non-empty set. A
(partial) numbering

\nu

\nu\colon\mathbb{N}\rightharpoonup S
(onto) with domain
\mathop{\mathstrut\rm dom}(\nu)
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\nu

\nu\leq\kappa
, read

is

to

, for all

.

2.

\nu\equiv\kappa
, read
\nu

\nu\leq\kappa
and

.

Lemma 4.2

For the numberings

Now, let
\mathcal{T}=(T,\tau)
be a countable topological

-space with a countable basis

.
Moreover, let

be a numbering of

mind the basic open sets can be described in a finite way. The
indexing

is then obtained by an encoding of the finite
descriptions. Moreover, in these cases there is a canonical relation
between the (code numbers of the) descriptions which is stronger than
the usual set inclusion between the described sets. This relation is
computably enumerable, which in general is not true for set inclusion. One has to use
this stronger relation when introducing the notion of an effective space.

Definition 4.3

Let

be a transitive binary relation on

1.

is a strong

If one considers basic open sets as vague descriptions, then strong
inclusion relations can be considered as ‘definite refinement’
relations

]

Strong inclusion relations that satisfy much stronger
requirements (in particular, they are extensional) appear very
naturally in the study of quasi-proximities
[

]
.
Moreover,
such relations have been used in Czászár’s approach to general
topology
[

]
and in Smyth’s work on topological foundations
of programming language semantics
[

. Compared with
the conditions used in these papers, the above requirements seem to
be rather weak, but as we go along, we shall meet a further
requirement, and it is this condition which in applications prevents
us from choosing

to be (the index set of) ordinary set
inclusion. For what follows we assume that
\prec_{B}
is a strong
inclusion with respect to which
\mathcal{B}
is a strong basis.

Definition 4.4

Let

and the property of being a strong basis holds effectively, which

Note that very often the totality of

If

Definition 4.6

An enumeration

6
The continuity theorem

As already said, in Markov-style constructive mathematics objects are constructed by algorithmic instructions. So, in order to define a function on such objects one has to say how it algorithmically transforms the algorithms underlying the objects. Formalised algorithms are pieces of text and can therefore coded by natural numbers.

Let
{\mathcal{T}}^{\prime}=(T^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})
be a further countable topological space with a countable basis
{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}
, and let
x^{\prime}
and
B^{\prime}
, respectively, be numberings of
T^{\prime}
and
{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}

Definition 6.1
.

A map

is an
effective operator
, if there is a function

such that
f(i)\mathclose{\downarrow}\in\mathop{\mathstrut\rm dom}(x^{\prime})
and

, for all
i\in\mathop{\mathstrut\rm dom}(x)
.

The
continuity problem
is the question whether effective operators are (effectively) continuous.

Definition 6.2
.

A map
F\colon T\rightarrow T^{\prime}
is said to be

1.

effectively pointwise continuous
, if there is a function
h\in P^{(2)}
such that
for all

and

with

,

,

x_{i}\in B_{h(i,n)}
, and

2.

effectively continuous
, if there is a
function
g\in P^{(1)}
such that for all
n\in\mathop{\mathstrut\rm dom}(B^{\prime})
,

g(n)\mathclose{\downarrow}\in\mathop{\mathstrut\rm dom}(L^{\tau})
and
F^{-1}[B_{n}^{\prime}]=L_{g(n)}^{\tau}
.

Lemma 6.3

(
[
22
]
)
.

1.

Let

be computable. If

is effectively
continuous, then it is also effectively pointwise continuous.

2.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be effective and computably
separable,

be acceptable,

be computable, and

Friedberg’s counterexample

Friedberg
[
10
]
has presented an effective operator

that is not continuous. Here,
M
is the set of computable elements of a computable metric space and
\mathcal{S}=(S,\sigma)
with
S=\{\bot,\top\}
and
\sigma=\{\emptyset,\{\top\},S\}
is
Sierpinski space
. Define

\delta(y,z)=\begin{cases}0&y=z\vee(y=\bot\wedge z=\top)\\
1&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Then
\delta
is a quasi-metric on
S
that generates the given topology
\sigma
.
S
is dense in itself with respect to
\tau_{\delta}
as well as
\tau_{\delta^{c}}
. Let

\beta_{i}=\begin{cases}\bot&i=0\\
\top&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

be an indexing of the dense base. As is easily verified, the quasi-metric space
(S,\delta)
is computable. Moreover, the elements of
S
are computable as well. Note further that

B^{\delta}_{\langle a,e\rangle}=\mathrm{B}_{\delta}(\beta_{a},2^{-e})=\begin{cases}S&\text{if $a=0$,}\\
\{\top\}&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Hence,
\langle a,e\rangle\prec_{B^{\delta}}\langle a^{\prime},e^{\prime}\rangle
, exactly if
a\not=0
and
a^{\prime}=0
. It follows that
\prec_{B^{\delta}}
is computably enumerable.

In Friedberg’s example,
M
is indexed by an acceptable numbering. Moreover,
S
is indexed by a numbering
x^{S}
that is acceptable with respect to
\sigma
, i.e.
\tau_{\delta}
.
Then the set
\{\bot\}
is not completely enumerable. Otherwise, it would be upwards closed under
\prec_{\sigma}
, by Proposition
4.10
. Since
\prec_{\sigma}=\prec_{\tau_{\delta}}=\sqsubseteq_{\delta}
, it would follow that

, which is not the case.

Assume now that

is also computable with respect to
\tau_{\delta^{c}}
. As
\{\bot\}
is basic open with respect to
\tau_{\delta^{c}}
, it follows that
\{\bot\}
is completely enumerable with respect to

, a contradiction.

In Theorem

8
Conclusion

Bi-topological spaces are not just an extension of topological spaces by adding a further topology on the underlying set. Many important notions and results naturally extend to the bi-topological case. Moreover, spaces of this kind have turned out to allow for interesting representations of symmetric (i.e. Hausdorff) topologies by two asymmetric ones.

In the present paper the framework of effective spaces developed in
[
22
]
was extended to the bi-topological case and the question of whether effective operators are effectively continuous was considered. It was shown that under the effectivity conditions that are always satisfied in applications, an effective operator
F\colon\mathcal{T}\rightarrow\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
, where
\mathcal{T}=(T,\tau)
is an effective topological space and
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}=(T^{\prime},\tau^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})
a bi-topological space, is effectively continuous with respect to
\tau
and
\tau^{\prime}
, if
\tau^{\prime}
is effectively regular with respect to
\sigma^{\prime}
.

Interestingly, it turned out that though the continuity is stated for
F
as a map from
(T,\tau)
to
(T^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})
and topology
\sigma^{\prime}
is more or less considered as an auxiliary topology, one nevertheless has to require each of the general effectivity conditions simultaneously for both topologies,
\tau^{\prime}
and
\sigma^{\prime}
. It thus follows that if also
\mathcal{T}=(T,\tau,\sigma)
is a bi-topological space and
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
is effectively pairwise regular, then
F
is even effectively bi-continuous. Conversely, it was shown that effectively bi-continuous operators are also effective, independently of any regularity requirement.

As an important example, computable quasi-metric spaces were studied. The topologies induced by the given quasi-metric and its conjugate are considered in this case. All general effectivity requirements that had to be made in the general study are now satisfied. Moreover, such spaces are effectively pairwise regular.

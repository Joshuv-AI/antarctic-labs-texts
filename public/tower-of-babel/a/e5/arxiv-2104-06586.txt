Survey on homological flips and homological flops
Wai-Kit Yeung

Abstract
. We give a survey for the results in [ 13 , 14 , 15 ] , which attempts to relate the derived categories under general classes of flips and flops. We indicate how the approach fails because of what appears to be a formal problem. We give some ideas, and record some failed attempts, to fix this problem. We also present some new examples.

In this article, we give a survey for an approach developed in
[

For the sake of clarity, we will recall the notions of flips and flops. In this section, we will start with the notion of a log flip (which generalizes both flips and flops), and we describe an algebraic setup associated to a log flip.
In the next section, we will define flips and flops, and elaborate more on this algebraic setup.

We do not assume knowledge of birational geometry from the reader. Most notions from birational geometry that we use will be reminded. We will also add several “checkpoints” in this article that summarize some essential information of the situation, so that readers less familiar with some parts of birational geometry or homological algebra will be able to read the rest of the article even if (s)he does not follow the arguments that lead to that description.

\,&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(*)}}{{=}}\,{\bm{R}}\underline{{\rm Hom}}_{A}(M,{\bm{R}}\Gamma_{I^{-}}(A))\\
\,&\cong\,\mathbb{D}_{Y}(M\otimes_{A}^{{\bm{L}}}{\bm{R}}\Gamma_{I^{+}}(A))(-a)[-1]\end{split}

where
(*)
holds because
{\bm{R}}\Gamma_{I^{-}}(A)
has finite Tor dimension and
M\in\mathcal{D}^{-}_{{\rm coh}}({\rm Gr}(A))
.
∎

By the results of the last section, we may assume that
A
is Gorenstein, so that
\mathbb{D}_{A}
is in some sense well-behaved. Thus, if (
3.9
)(1) holds in some good context, we would have obtained a relation between the derived categories of
\mathfrak{X}^{+}
and
\mathfrak{X}^{-}
similar to the expected relation (
3.1
).
While this picture of using
\mathbb{D}_{A}
to relate the two windows was the first argument along this line that the author discovered (and is still very attractive to the author), it seems to be not the most efficient one. We now seek to reformulate it without using the duality
\mathbb{D}_{A}
.

In our above argument, the functor that we want to use to relate the derived categories of
\mathfrak{X}^{-}
and
\mathfrak{X}^{+}
can be written as follows:

(3.12)

\mathcal{D}^{-}_{{\rm coh}}(\mathfrak{X}^{+})\,\xleftarrow[\simeq]{(j^{+})^{*}}\,\mathcal{D}^{-}_{{\rm coh},[\geq w],(<w)}({\rm Gr}(A))\,\xrightarrow[(?)]{\mathbb{D}_{A}}\,\mathcal{D}^{+}_{{\rm coh},[\leq-w],(>-w)}({\rm Gr}(A))^{{\rm op}}\,\xrightarrow{(j^{-})^{*}}\,\mathcal{D}^{+}_{{\rm coh}}(\mathfrak{X}^{-})^{{\rm op}}

Here, we have marked the middle arrow with a question mark to indicate that it is not clear whether the functor
\mathbb{D}_{A}
really sends
\mathcal{D}^{-}_{{\rm coh},[\geq w],(<w)}({\rm Gr}(A))
to
\mathcal{D}^{+}_{{\rm coh},[\leq-w],(>-w)}({\rm Gr}(A))
. Thus, the question mark in (
3.12
) is a version of (
3.8
) where we restrict to
\mathcal{D}^{-}_{{\rm coh}}
. If it holds, then we see that (
3.12
) is a composition of three fully faithful functors, and is therefore fully faithful, giving a result along the lines of (
3.1
).

Whether or not the question mark in (
3.12
) holds, the functor (
3.12
) makes sense if we consider

\mathcal{D}^{-}_{{\rm coh}}(\mathfrak{X}^{+})\,\xleftarrow[\simeq]{(j^{+})^{*}}\,\mathcal{D}^{-}_{{\rm coh},[\geq w],(<w)}({\rm Gr}(A))\,\xrightarrow{(j^{-})^{*}\circ\mathbb{D}_{A}}\,\mathcal{D}^{+}_{{\rm coh}}(\mathfrak{X}^{-})^{{\rm op}}

Since duality is local on
\mathcal{D}^{-}_{{\rm coh}}(\mathfrak{X})
, the last functor may be rewritten as
(j^{-})^{*}\circ\mathbb{D}_{A}=\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{X}^{-}}\circ(j^{-})^{*}
.
But
\mathfrak{X}^{-}
is Gorenstein, so that
\mathbb{D}_{\mathfrak{X}^{-}}
is a contravariant equivalence on
\mathcal{D}_{{\rm coh}}(\mathfrak{X}^{-})
, and we may skip that functor.
In other words, the desired functor is in fact

(3.13)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{D}^{-}_{{\rm coh}}(\mathfrak{X}^{+})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{D}^{-}_{{\rm coh},[\geq w],(<w)}({\rm Gr}(A))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{D}^{-}_{{\rm coh}}(\mathfrak{X}^{-})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{L}_{[\geq w]}\circ{\bm{R}}j^{+}_{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(j^{+})^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(j^{-})^{*}}

Since the duality
\mathbb{D}_{A}
is not involved, we expect that our argument will not need to use the Gorenstein property. In fact, we will show that the duality
{\bm{R}}\Gamma_{I^{+}}(A)(a)[1]\cong\mathbb{D}_{Y}({\bm{R}}\Gamma_{I^{-}}(A))
“almost implies” that this functor is fully faithful, except that a similar formal problem arise.
To describe the problem, we start with the following straightforward

Lemma 3.14
.

Given a full triangulated subcategory
\mathcal{E}\subset\mathcal{D}({\rm Gr}(A))
, then the functor
\check{\mathcal{C}}_{I^{-}}:\mathcal{E}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}_{I^{-}\text{-triv}}({\rm Gr}(A))

is fully faithful if and only if

{\bm{R}}{\rm Hom}_{A}(M,{\bm{R}}\Gamma_{I^{-}}(N)):={\bm{R}}\underline{{\rm Hom}}_{A}(M,{\bm{R}}\Gamma_{I^{-}}(N))_{0}\simeq 0\qquad\text{ for all }M,N\in\mathcal{E}

We wish to show that the second functor in (
3.13
) is fully faithful by verifying this condition.
Thus, let
M\in\mathcal{D}^{-}_{{\rm coh},(<w)}({\rm Gr}(A))
and
N\in\mathcal{D}^{-}_{{\rm coh},[\geq w]}({\rm Gr}(A))
, we compute

{\bm{R}}\underline{{\rm Hom}}_{A}(M,{\bm{R}}\Gamma_{I^{-}}(N))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(?)}}{{\simeq}}{\bm{R}}\underline{{\rm Hom}}_{A}(M,{\bm{R}}\Gamma_{I^{-}}(A))\otimes_{A}^{{\bm{L}}}N\simeq\mathbb{D}_{Y}({\bm{R}}\Gamma_{I^{+}}(M))(-a)[-1]\otimes_{A}^{{\bm{L}}}N

Notice that
\mathbb{D}_{Y}({\bm{R}}\Gamma_{I^{+}}(M))\otimes_{A}^{{\bm{L}}}N
is in
\mathcal{D}_{>-w}\otimes_{A}^{{\bm{L}}}\mathcal{D}_{[\geq w]}\subset\mathcal{D}_{>0}
. Therefore, its shift by
a\geq 0
is zero at weight zero.

We see once again that the argument falls short because of what seems like a formal problem (more precisely, the equality marked with (?) may not hold).
However, notice that our arguments in either the formulations (
3.8
) or (
3.13
) work if the
3
-term SOD restricts to
\mathcal{D}_{{\rm perf}}({\rm Gr}(A))
. In the next section, we will see two examples in which it holds.

Remark 3.15
.

One can “solve” the problem (
3.9
)(1) by formulating the duality
\mathbb{D}_{A}
in terms of a perfect pairing of DG categories

\mathcal{D}_{[\geq w]}({\rm Gr}(A))\times\mathcal{D}_{[\leq-w]}({\rm Gr}(A))\,\rightarrow\,\mathcal{D}(k)\,,\qquad(M,N)\mapsto(M\otimes^{{\bm{L}}}_{A}N)_{0}

which then restricts to a pairing on the window subcategories
\mathcal{D}_{[\geq w],(<w)}({\rm Gr}(A))
and
\mathcal{D}_{[\leq-w],(>-w)}({\rm Gr}(A))
. One can then try to show that it is a perfect pairing.
Unravelling the definition (at least in the way the author formulated it), it corresponds to the functor (
3.13
), except that we do not restrict to
\mathcal{D}^{-}_{{\rm coh}}
.

Remark 3.16
.

The functor (
3.13
) is
\mathcal{O}_{Y}
-linear.

Remark 3.17
.

By a result
[
8
]
of Kawamata, any two birational projective Calabi-Yau varieties with at most
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial terminal singularities are connected by a finite number of flops, each of which is obviously a Gorenstein flop, and hence falls into our present setting.

Remark 3.18
.

One might try to repeat the arguments in this section for a different version of
\mathcal{D}({\rm Gr}(A))
. Some potential candidates are: (1)
{\rm Ind}\,\mathcal{D}^{b}_{{\rm coh}}({\rm Gr}(A))
, (2) co-derived or contra-derived categories, (3) homotopy category of (unbounded) complexes of finitely generated projectives.
For example, if we work with (3), then problems such as (
3.10
) seem to not arise (although other problems might arise then).
Accordingly, the theory of relative homological algebra (
e.g.

[
4
]
) might be relevant here, although the author is not very familiar with this part of the literature.
If one changes the setting, one should also guarantee that results of Section
2
can be carried over.
It is not clear to us whether endeavors in these formal directions will be useful (the author has spent a lot of time trying out various formal modifications, but such effort has not been fruitful so far).

4.
Some examples

We will present two examples in which the
3
-term SOD (
3.6
) restricts to
\mathcal{D}_{{\rm perf}}({\rm Gr}(A))
, so that our argument does work. For this to hold, it is certainly necessary that the functor
\mathcal{L}_{[\geq w]}
in (
3.4
) preserves
\mathcal{D}_{{\rm perf}}({\rm Gr}(A))
.
The following result says that this condition is also sufficient. A proof may be found in
[
14
]
(a revised version is under preparation, for which the arguments will be simplified).

Proposition 4.1
.

Suppose that the functor
\mathcal{L}_{[\geq w]}
in (
3.4
) preserves
\mathcal{D}_{{\rm perf}}({\rm Gr}(A))
, then the the
3
-term SOD (
3.6
) restricts to
\mathcal{D}_{{\rm perf}}({\rm Gr}(A))
.

In this case, the functor (
3.13
) restricts to

(4.2)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{D}_{{\rm perf}}(\mathfrak{X}^{+})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{D}_{{\rm perf},[\geq w],(<w)}({\rm Gr}(A))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{D}_{{\rm perf}}(\mathfrak{X}^{-})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{L}_{[\geq w]}\circ{\bm{R}}j^{+}_{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(j^{+})^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(j^{-})^{*}}

Moreover, the arguments in the previous section then guarantees that, if the duality
\mathbb{D}_{Y}({\bm{R}}\Gamma_{I^{-}}(A))\cong{\bm{R}}\Gamma_{I^{+}}(A)(a)[1]
holds for some
a\geq 0
, then (
4.2
) is fully faithful.

In general, it seems quite difficult to describe
\mathcal{L}_{[\geq w]}
explicitly, as it is defined in terms of the Neeman-Brown representability theorem. The closest to an explicit formula that we managed to get is the following tensor form

(4.3)

\mathcal{L}_{[\geq w]}(M)\,=\,M_{\geq w}\otimes^{{\bm{L}}}_{\mathcal{F}_{\geq w}}\mathcal{F}

Here,
\mathcal{F}
is the small pre-additive (
i.e.,

{\rm Ab}
-enriched) category given by
\rm{Ob}(\mathcal{F})=\mathbb{Z}
and
\mathcal{F}(i,j)=A_{i-j}
. Then a graded
A
-module is the same as a right module over
\mathcal{F}
(recall that a right module over a small pre-additive category
\mathcal{C}
means an additive functor
\mathcal{C}^{{\rm op}}\rightarrow{\rm Ab}
). Let
\mathcal{F}_{\geq w}\subset\mathcal{F}
be the full subcategory on the object set
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq w}\subset\mathbb{Z}
. Then the inclusion functor
\mathcal{F}_{\geq w}\rightarrow\mathcal{F}
gives rise to restriction and induction functors between the module categories. In particular, for any
M\in{\rm Mod}(\mathcal{F})
, we may restrict it to
\mathcal{F}_{\geq w}
to obtain
M_{\geq w}\in{\rm Mod}(\mathcal{F}_{\geq w})
, and then tensor it back to
\mathcal{F}
to obtain
M_{\geq w}\otimes_{\mathcal{F}_{\geq w}}\mathcal{F}\in{\rm Mod}(\mathcal{F})
. The right hand side of (
4.3
) is its derived functor.

The description (
4.3
) seems to be of little use in the two examples that we present below. In both cases, the functor
\mathcal{L}_{[\geq w]}
is described by some ad-hoc arguments specific to the form of the graded ring
A
in question.

The first example is the polynomial ring

(4.4)

A=k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{p},y_{1},\ldots,y_{q}]\,,\qquad\deg(x_{i})>0,\,\,\deg(y_{j})<0

Write
A=A^{+}\otimes A^{-}
, where
A^{+}=k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{p}]
and
A^{-}=k[y_{1},\ldots,y_{q}]
.
Then we have

\mathcal{D}_{[\geq w]}({\rm Gr}(A^{+}))\otimes A^{-}\subset\mathcal{D}_{[\geq w]}({\rm Gr}(A))\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{D}_{<w}({\rm Gr}(A^{+}))\otimes A^{-}\subset\mathcal{D}_{<w}({\rm Gr}(A))

This allows us to compute the weight truncation of objects of the form
M=M^{+}\otimes A^{-}
for
M^{+}\in\mathcal{D}({\rm Gr}(A^{+}))
. Namely, we have

\mathcal{L}_{[\geq w]}^{A}(M^{+}\otimes A^{-})\,=\,\mathcal{L}_{[\geq w]}^{A^{+}}(M^{+})\otimes A^{-}

To show that
\mathcal{L}_{[\geq w]}^{A}
preserves
\mathcal{D}_{{\rm perf}}({\rm Gr}(A))
, it suffices to verify it on the split-generating objects
\{A(-i)\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
, each of which is of the form
M^{+}\otimes A^{-}
for
M^{+}=A^{+}(-i)
. Hence it suffices to compute
\mathcal{L}_{[\geq w]}^{A^{+}}(A^{+}(-i))
.
Since
A^{+}
is non-negatively graded, we in fact have
\mathcal{D}_{[\geq w]}({\rm Gr}(A^{+}))=\mathcal{D}_{\geq w}({\rm Gr}(A^{+}))
, so that the weight truncation is easy to describe:

Lemma 4.5
.

For any
M\in{\rm Gr}(A^{+})
, we have

\mathcal{L}_{[\geq w]}^{A^{+}}(M)=M_{\geq w}\,,\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{L}_{<w}^{A^{+}}(M)=M/M_{\geq w}

As a result, we have

\mathcal{L}_{[\geq w]}^{A}(A(-i))=((A^{+})_{\geq w-i}\otimes A^{-})(-i)

Since
A^{+}
is smooth, we have
(A^{+})_{\geq w-i}\in\mathcal{D}_{{\rm perf}}({\rm Gr}(A^{+}))
. This shows the following

Proposition 4.6
.

For the polynomial ring (
4.4
), weight truncation
\mathcal{L}_{[\geq w]}
preserves
\mathcal{D}_{{\rm perf}}({\rm Gr}(A))
.

Remark 4.7
.

Our argument here works whenever a graded ring can be written as
A=A^{+}\otimes A^{-}
where
A^{+}
is non-negatively graded and
A^{-}
is non-positively graded. In this case, if
A^{+}
is smooth, then
\mathcal{L}_{[\geq w]}
preserves
\mathcal{D}_{{\rm perf}}({\rm Gr}(A))
. Interestingly, if
A^{-}
is smooth, then by the arguments of
[
6
]
, one can show that
\mathcal{L}_{[\geq w]}
preserves
\mathcal{D}^{b}_{{\rm coh}}({\rm Gr}(A))
.

Proposition
4.6
shows that our approach in the previous section works perfectly for the polynomial ring (
4.4
) (notice that we have established the duality
\mathbb{D}_{Y}({\bm{R}}\Gamma_{I^{-}}(A))\cong{\bm{R}}\Gamma_{I^{+}}(A)(a)[1]
directly in Remark
2.20
without applying the main Theorems of Section

).
We will now compute the corresponding functor (
4.2
). For simplicity, we will now assume
w=0
.
Let
\eta^{+}=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\deg(x_{i})
and
\eta^{-}=-\sum_{j=1}^{p}\deg(y_{j})
, then
a=\eta^{-}-\eta^{+}
.

By the computation in Remark
2.20
, we see that

{\bm{R}}\Gamma_{I^{+}}(A)\in\mathcal{D}_{\leq-\eta^{+}}({\rm Gr}(A))\qquad\text{and}\qquad{\bm{R}}\Gamma_{I^{-}}(A)\in\mathcal{D}_{\geq\eta^{-}}({\rm Gr}(A))

Thus, in particular, for
0\leq i<\eta^{+}
, the objects
A(-i)
are in the window subcategory

.
This implies that

(4.8)

For

, the functor (
4.2
) for

\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}^{+}}(-i)

\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}^{-}}(-i)
.

In fact, this uniquely characterize the functor (

Namely, the arguments of
[

\{\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}^{+}}(-i)\}_{0\leq i<\eta^{+}}
split generates

.

Proposition 4.9
.

For the polynomial ring (
4.4
), we have
\mathcal{D}_{{\rm perf},(<w)}({\rm Gr}(A))=\mathcal{D}_{{\rm perf},[<w+\eta^{+}]}({\rm Gr}(A))
. As a result, the subcategory
\mathcal{D}_{{\rm perf},[\geq 0],(<0)}({\rm Gr}(A))
is split generated by
\{A,A(-1),\ldots,A(-\eta^{+}+1)\}
.

Similarly, we have
\mathcal{D}_{{\rm perf},(>-w)}({\rm Gr}(A))=\mathcal{D}_{{\rm perf},[>-w-\eta^{-}]}({\rm Gr}(A))
, so that
\mathcal{D}_{{\rm perf},[\leq 0],(>0)}({\rm Gr}(A))
is split generated by
\{A,A(1),\ldots,A(\eta^{-}-1)\}
.

By our above description of
\mathcal{L}_{[\geq 0]}
in Lemma
4.5
, we also have

(4.10)

For
i>0
, the functor (
4.2
) for
w=0
sends
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}^{+}}(i)
to the associated sheaf on
\mathfrak{X}^{-}
of the graded module
((A^{+})_{\geq i}\otimes A^{-})(i)

The image of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{X}^{+}}(i)
for

seems to be more difficult to explicate, since
\mathcal{L}_{[\geq 0]}({\bm{R}}\Gamma_{I^{+}}(A(i)))
does not vanish in this case, so the answer invovles a certain cone. We will skip the details.

Remark 4.11
.

The case
\deg(x_{i})=1
and
\deg(y_{j})=-1
corresponds to a standard flip/flop (we then have

,

with respect to the standard span

Fix positive integers

with

We have renamed the generators of

A Noetherian graded algebra

if there exists a map

from a polynomial graded algebra

Proposition 4.16
.

If

\mathcal{L}_{[\geq w]}
preserves
\mathcal{D}_{{\rm perf}}({\rm Gr}(A))
.

Proof.

In general, we have
\mathcal{D}_{[\geq w]}({\rm Gr}(C))\otimes^{{\bm{L}}}_{C}A\subset\mathcal{D}_{[\geq w]}({\rm Gr}(A))
. The condition on non-positive presentation also guarantees that
\mathcal{D}_{<w}({\rm Gr}(C))\otimes^{{\bm{L}}}_{C}A\subset\mathcal{D}_{<w}({\rm Gr}(A))

\mathcal{L}_{[\geq w]}^{A}(M\otimes^{{\bm{L}}}_{C}A)\cong\mathcal{L}_{[\geq w]}^{C}(M)\otimes^{{\bm{L}}}_{C}A
for all
M\in\mathcal{D}({\rm Gr}(C))
. Apply this to

for
i\in\mathbb{Z}
to conclude the proof.
∎

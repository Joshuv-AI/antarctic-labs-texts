Absence of Replica Symmetry Breaking in the Random Field Mixed-Spin Ginzburg-Landau Model
R. Vila

Abstract
. In this paper, an extension of the random field Ginzburg-Landau model on the hypercubic lattice is considered by adding p p -spin ( p ⩾ 2 p\geqslant 2 ) interactions coupled to general disorders. This new model is called the random field mixed-spin Ginzburg-Landau model. We proved that, in the infinite volume limit of this model, the variance of spin overlap vanishes.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
MSC 82B20, MSC 82B44, MSC 60K35.

1.
Introduction

A long-standing debate on the presence of elusive spin glass phase (i.e., when the ferromagnetic susceptibility is finite, while the spin glass susceptibility is infinite) in several systems has been the subject of research for many years. For example, in
[
7
,
8
]
, among other things, the authors argued that there is no spin glass phase at equilibrium in the random field Ising model, the random field Ginzburg-Landau model and the random temperature Ginzburg-Landau model, for non-negative interactions and arbitrary disorders on an arbitrary lattice.
In 2015, Chatterjee
[
2
]
provided a rigorous
mathematical proof supporting part (absence of replica symmetry breaking) of the findings claimed in
[
7
,
8
]
for the random field Ising model, in the case of Gaussian disorders. In 2019, Itoi and Utsunomiya
[
6
]
extended the Chatterjee results for a disordered spin model (the random field Ginzburg-Landau model) in which the disorder is Gaussian and the single-spins are not limited.
More recently, Roldan and Vila (2020)
[
13
]
have extended the Chatterjee results for the random field Ising model in weak disorders.

The objective of this paper is to prove the absence of replica symmetry breaking in the random field Ginzburg-Landau model allowing
p
-spin interactions which are coupled to disorders not necessarily Gaussian. This new model is called the random field mixed-spin Ginzburg-Landau model. Results involving absence of replica symmetry breaking in the random field Ginzburg-Landau model and related ones can be found in
[
2
,
5
,
6
,
13
]
. Roughly speaking, by adding asymptotically vanishing perturbations to the Hamiltonian corresponding to Gibbs measure of the random field Ginzburg-Landau model, we prove that, in the thermodynamic limit of this model, the degree to which the values of a spin overlap differ from its expectation value vanishes. So, in our paper, as the ones in
[
2
,
6
]
, we give a support in favor of the absence of the spin glass phase, but for general disorders.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we present the random field mixed-spin Ginzburg-Landau model, and some definitions related to this model.
In Section 3 we state the main result of paper. In Section 4, we present the proof of the main result in details.

2.
The model and some definitions

The model is defined as follows.
Given
n\geqslant 1
, let

V_{n}=\mathbb{Z}^{d}\cap[1,n]^{d}
,
d\geqslant 1
, be a finite subset of vertices of

d
-dimensional hypercubic lattice
with cardinality denoted by
|V_{n}|
.
The
Hamiltonian of the random field mixed-spin Ginzburg-Landau model on the set of spins configurations
\mathbb{R}^{V_{n}}
reads

(1)

\displaystyle H_{n;\bm{\alpha}}(\phi)=H_{n}(\phi;g)+u\sum_{x\in V_{n}}\phi_{x}^{4}-r\sum_{x\in V_{n}}\phi_{x}^{2}-H^{\rm per}_{n;\bm{\alpha}}(\phi;\xi),

where the Hamiltonian
H_{n}(\phi;g)

is
given by

(2)

\displaystyle H_{n}(\phi;g)=-\beta\sum_{\langle xy\rangle}\phi_{x}\phi_{y}-h\sum_{x\in V_{n}}g_{x}\phi_{x}.

Here,
\langle xy\rangle
denotes the set of ordered pairs in
V_{n}
of nearest neighbors,
\beta>0
and
h>0
are called inverse temperature and field strength, respectively. Furthermore,

u>0
,
r\in\mathbb{R}
and
\phi=(\phi_{x})\in\mathbb{R}^{V_{n}}
is a spin configuration.
In this paper, the random external magnetic field

g=(g_{x})
(called disorder)
consists of independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) standard Gaussian random variables. Following
[
13
]
, it is not trivial to remove the Gaussian hypothesis on
g
because a generalization of the Gaussian integration by parts for not limited spins is needed which is not always possible to obtain.

The perturbing Hamiltonian
H^{\rm per}_{n;\bm{\alpha}}(\phi;\xi)
in (
1
) is defined in function of a pure
p
-spin Hamiltonian
H_{n;p}(\phi;\xi)
, for
p\geqslant 2
, as follows

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lllll}&\displaystyle H^{\rm per}_{n;\bm{\alpha}}(\phi;\xi)=i(1-\delta_{\bm{\alpha},\bm{0}})c_{n}\sum_{p=2}^{\infty}\alpha_{p}2^{-p}H_{n;p}(\phi;\xi);\\[14.22636pt]
&\displaystyle H_{n;p}(\phi;\xi)={1\over|V_{n}|^{(p-1)/2}}\sum_{{\bf x}\in\otimes_{l=2}^{p}V_{n}}\xi_{\bf x}\phi_{\bf x};\end{array}

where
\delta
is the Kronecker delta function,
i
is the imaginary unit, and

\displaystyle\phi_{\bf x}=\phi_{x_{1}}\cdots\phi_{x_{p}},

for
{\bf x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{p})
.
Here in (
2
) and throughout this paper,
the sequences of real numbers
(c_{n})
and

\bm{\alpha}=(\alpha_{p})
satisfy
c_{n}\mbox{$\;\longrightarrow_{\hskip-11.9501pt{\small n}}\;$~}0
and
|\alpha_{p}|\leqslant 1
, respectively. In order that the Hamiltonian

H_{n;\bm{\alpha}}(\phi)
in (
1
) to be well-defined almost surely (see Proposition
4.3
), we assume the following regularity condition:

(5)

\displaystyle\sum_{p=2}^{\infty}\alpha_{p}2^{-p}C_{2p}^{1/2}<\infty,

where the sequence
(C_{k})
is formed by upper bounds of expectation of the function of a spin variable whose existence is guaranteed by Lemma II.1 of
[
6
]
(see Proposition
4.1
).
The random sequence
\xi=(\xi_{\bf x})

consists of i.i.d. real-valued random variables
\xi_{\bf x}
with zero-mean and unit-variance.
Moreover,
the disorders
g=(g_{x})
and
\xi=(\xi_{\bf x})
are assumed to be independent of each other.

The Gibbs measure of the random field mixed-spin Ginzburg-Landau model on

\mathbb{R}^{V_{n}}
is given by

(6)

\displaystyle{G}_{n;\bm{\alpha}}(\{\phi\})={1\over Z_{n;\bm{\alpha}}}\exp\big(-H_{n;\bm{\alpha}}(\phi)\big),\quad\phi=(\phi_{x})\in\mathbb{R}^{V_{n}},

where
H_{n;\bm{\alpha}}(\phi)
is as in (
1
).
Note that the classical random field Ginzburg-Landau
model
is recovered
when

\bm{\alpha}=\bm{0}
.
Denoting
{\rm D}\phi=\prod_{x\in V_{n}}\exp(-u\phi_{x}^{4}+r\phi_{x}^{2})\,{\rm d}\phi_{x}
,
the constant
Z_{n;\bm{\alpha}}
is the partition function and it is given by

(7)

\displaystyle Z_{n;\bm{\alpha}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{|V_{n}|}}\exp\big(-H_{n}(\phi;g)+H^{\rm per}_{n;\bm{\alpha}}(\phi;\xi)\big)\,{\rm D}\phi.

For a function
f:(\mathbb{R}^{V_{n}})^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
,
m\geqslant 1
, we define

\displaystyle\langle\,f\,\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\dfrac{1}{Z_{n;\bm{\alpha}}^{m}}\int f(\phi^{1},\ldots,\phi^{m})\exp\left(\sum_{s=1}^{m}\biggl(-H_{n}(\phi^{s};g)+H^{\rm per}_{n;\bm{\alpha}}(\phi^{s};\xi)\biggr)\right){\text{D}\phi^{1}}\cdots{\text{D}\phi^{m}}.

Following the notation of Talagrand
[
15
]
, we write

\mathbb{E}\langle\,f\,\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}

to denote the average of the Gibbs expectation
\langle\,f\,\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}
over all the realizations of the disorder
(g_{x},\xi_{\bf x})
, for
p\geqslant 2
.

3.
The main result

If
\phi^{1},\phi^{2},\ldots
are i.i.d
configurations under (random) Gibbs measure (
6
), known as replicas, the spin overlap
between two replicas
\phi^{\ell}
,
\phi^{\ell^{\prime}}
is defined as

(8)

\displaystyle R_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}\coloneqq{1\over|V_{n}|}\sum_{x\in V_{n}}\phi^{\ell}_{x}\phi^{\ell^{\prime}}_{x},\quad\forall\ell,\ell^{\prime}.

Note that
R_{\ell,\ell}\neq 1
.

The system is said to exhibit replica symmetry breaking (as stated by Chatterjee
in the random field Ising model
[
2
]
) when a finite variance calculated in the replica symmetric expectation, in the infinite volume limit, is observed

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\big\langle(R_{1,2}-\mathbb{E}\langle R_{1,2}\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}})^{2}\big\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}>0.

In the present paper,
the next main theorem shows that
this does not happen in the random field mixed-spin Ginzburg-Landau model.

Theorem 1
.

For almost all coupling constants
(\beta,h,u,r)\in(0,\infty)^{3}\times\mathbb{R}
, in the
random field mixed-spin Ginzburg-Landau model, the following variance vanishes

\mathbb{E}\big\langle(R_{1,2}-\mathbb{E}\langle R_{1,2}\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}})^{2}\big\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\xrightarrow{\hskip 25.6073pt}}0

in the infinite volume limit.

The rest of this paper is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1
.

4.
Proof

Before beginning the proof details of the main theorem of this paper,
we will enunciate and prove some results that will
facilitate the presentation of paper.

4.1.
Upper bound for the expectation of the function of a spin variable

Our first tool will be the following proposition. Its proof appears in Itoi and Utsunomiya (2019)
[
6
]
, Lemma II.1.

Proposition 4.1
.

There exists some positive constant
C_{k}=C_{k}(h)
independent of the system size
n
so that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\langle\phi_{x}^{k}\rangle_{\bm{0}}\leqslant C_{k}\ \quad\text{for all}\ {x}\in V_{n},

where
k
is an arbitrary even integer.

Proposition 4.2
.

The expectation of the function of a pure
p
-spin Hamiltonian
H_{n;p}(\phi;\xi)
has an upper bound

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big\langle[H_{n;p}(\phi;\xi)]^{2}\big\rangle_{\bm{0}}\leqslant|V_{n}|C_{2p},\quad p\geqslant 2.

Proof.

Let
\widehat{\mathbb{E}}
be the expectation over
\xi_{\bf x}
,
p\geqslant 2
. By Fubini’s theorem we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big\langle[H_{n;p}(\phi;\xi)]^{2}\big\rangle_{\bm{0}}=\mathbb{E}\big\langle\widehat{\mathbb{E}}[H_{n;p}(\phi;\xi)]^{2}\big\rangle_{\bm{0}}=\mathbb{E}\biggl\langle{1\over|V_{n}|^{p-1}}\sum_{{\bf x}}\phi_{\bf x}^{2}\biggr\rangle_{\bm{0}},

where in the last equality we used that
\xi=(\xi_{\bf x})

consists of i.i.d. real-valued random variables with
\widehat{\mathbb{E}}\xi_{\bf x}=0
and
\widehat{\mathbb{E}}\xi_{\bf x}^{2}=1
. By using Hölder’s inequality and Proposition
4.1
, the above expression is at most

\displaystyle{1\over|V_{n}|^{p-1}}\sum_{{\bf x}}\prod_{j=1}^{p}\mathbb{E}^{1/p}\langle\phi_{x_{j}}^{2p}\,\rangle_{\bm{0}}\leqslant|V_{n}|C_{2p}.

Then the proof follows.
∎

Our second tool will be the following lemma.
This result plays a fundamental role in our work, because it allows us to relate the averages of both Gibbs expectations corresponding to (classic) random field Ginzburg-Landau model (when
\bm{\alpha}=\bm{0}
) and to our model (
6
).

Lemma 1
.

Let
F(\phi):\mathbb{R}^{V_{n}}\to\mathbb{R}
be a non-negative Borel function. There is a positive constant
\widetilde{C}=\widetilde{C}(n)
, with
\widetilde{C}\mbox{$\;\longrightarrow_{\hskip-11.9501pt{\small n}}\;$~}1
as
n\to\infty
, so that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\langle F(\phi)\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}\leqslant\widetilde{C}\,\mathbb{E}^{1/2}\langle F^{2}(\phi)\,\rangle_{\bm{0}}.

Proof.

A straightforward computation shows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\langle F(\phi)\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\,{Z_{n;\bm{0}}\over Z_{n;\bm{\alpha}}}\,\big\langle F(\phi)\,{\exp}\big({H^{\rm per}_{n;\bm{\alpha}}(\phi;\xi)}\big)\big\rangle_{\bm{0}}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\,{\big\langle F(\phi)\,{\exp}\big({H^{\rm per}_{n;\bm{\alpha}}(\phi;\xi)}\big)\big\rangle_{\bm{0}}\over\big\langle{\exp}\big({H^{\rm per}_{n;\bm{\alpha}}(\phi;\xi)}\big)\big\rangle_{\bm{0}}}.

By using Jensen and Cauchy-Schwarz inequalities, the above expression is

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{E}\,{\big\langle F(\phi)\,{\exp}\big({H^{\rm per}_{n;\bm{\alpha}}(\phi;\xi)}\big)\big\rangle_{\bm{0}}\over{\exp}\big\langle\big({H^{\rm per}_{n;\bm{\alpha}}(\phi;\xi)}\big)\big\rangle_{\bm{0}}}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\,\Big\langle F(\phi)\,{\exp}\Big({H^{\rm per}_{n;\bm{\alpha}}(\phi;\xi)}-\big\langle{H^{\rm per}_{n;\bm{\alpha}}(\phi;\xi)}\big\rangle_{\bm{0}}\Big)\Big\rangle_{\bm{0}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{E}^{1/2}\langle F^{2}(\phi)\,\rangle_{\bm{0}}\,\mathbb{E}^{1/2}\Big\langle{\exp}\Big(2\big[{H^{\rm per}_{n;\bm{\alpha}}(\phi;\xi)}-\big\langle{H^{\rm per}_{n;\bm{\alpha}}(\phi;\xi)}\big\rangle_{\bm{0}}\big]\Big)\Big\rangle_{\bm{0}}.

Therefore,

(9)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\langle F(\phi)\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}\leqslant\mathbb{E}^{1/2}\langle F^{2}(\phi)\,\rangle_{\bm{0}}\,\mathbb{E}^{1/2}\Big\langle{\exp}\Big(2\big[{H^{\rm per}_{n;\bm{\alpha}}(\phi;\xi)}-\big\langle{H^{\rm per}_{n;\bm{\alpha}}(\phi;\xi)}\big\rangle_{\bm{0}}\big]\Big)\Big\rangle_{\bm{0}}.

On the other hand, let

(10)

\displaystyle X_{n}\coloneqq(1-\delta_{\bm{\alpha},\bm{0}})\sum_{p=2}^{\infty}\alpha_{p}2^{-p}H_{n;p}(\phi;\xi).

Note that,
{H^{\rm per}_{n;\bm{\alpha}}(\phi;\xi)}=ic_{n}X_{n}
.
By triangle inequality,

\mathbb{E}\big\langle\big|X_{n}-\langle X_{n}\rangle_{\bm{0}}\big|\big\rangle_{\bm{0}}\leqslant 2\mathbb{E}\big\langle|X_{n}|\big\rangle_{\bm{0}},

with

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big\langle|X_{n}|\big\rangle_{\bm{0}}

\displaystyle\leqslant(1-\delta_{\bm{\alpha},\bm{0}})\sum_{p=2}^{\infty}\alpha_{p}2^{-p}\mathbb{E}\big\langle|H_{n;p}(\phi;\xi)|\big\rangle_{\bm{0}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{p=2}^{\infty}\alpha_{p}2^{-p}\mathbb{E}^{1/2}\big\langle[H_{n;p}(\phi;\xi)]^{2}\big\rangle_{\bm{0}}

(11)

\displaystyle\leqslant\sqrt{|V_{n}|}\sum_{p=2}^{\infty}\alpha_{p}2^{-p}C_{2p}^{1/2}<\infty,

where in the second inequality we use Lyapunov’s inequality and in the third one the Proposition
4.2
with the regularity condition (
5
).

Consequently, since
\mathbb{E}\big\langle\big|X_{n}-\langle X_{n}\rangle_{\bm{0}}\big|\big\rangle_{\bm{0}}<\infty
, it follows that, for
n\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big\langle{\exp}\Big(2\big[{H^{\rm per}_{n;\bm{\alpha}}(\phi;\xi)}-\big\langle{H^{\rm per}_{n;\bm{\alpha}}(\phi;\xi)}\big\rangle_{\bm{0}}\big]\Big)\Big\rangle_{\bm{0}}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\big\langle{\exp}\big(2ic_{n}[X_{n}-\langle X_{n}\rangle_{\bm{0}}]\big)\big\rangle_{\bm{0}}

\displaystyle=1+2ic_{n}\mathbb{E}\langle X_{n}-\langle X_{n}\rangle_{\bm{0}}\rangle_{\bm{0}}+2ic_{n}o(1)

(12)

\displaystyle=1+2ic_{n}o(1).

By combining (
9
) with (
4.1
), the proof follows by taking
\widetilde{C}\coloneqq 1+2ic_{n}o(1)
.
∎

The next result is an extension of Proposition
4.1
when asymptotically vanishing perturbations are added to the random field Ginzburg-Landau model.

Lemma 2
.

The expectation of the functions of the spin variables
\phi_{x}
and
\phi_{\bf x}
have the following upper bounds:

(i)

\mathbb{E}\langle\phi_{x}^{k}\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}\leqslant\widetilde{C}C_{2k}^{1/2}
,

for all

{x}\in V_{n}
;

(ii)

\mathbb{E}\langle\phi_{\bf x}^{k}\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}\leqslant\widetilde{C}C_{2kp}^{1/2}

and

\mathbb{E}\langle\phi_{\bf x}^{2k}\rangle_{\bm{0}}\leqslant C_{2kp},

for all

{\bf x}\in\otimes_{l=2}^{p}V_{n}
,
p\geqslant 2
;

where
k
is an arbitrary even integer.

Proof.

Taking
F(\phi)=\phi_{x}^{k}
in Lemma
1
, we have

\mathbb{E}\langle\phi_{x}^{k}\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}\leqslant\widetilde{C}\,\mathbb{E}^{1/2}\langle\phi_{x}^{2k}\,\rangle_{\bm{0}}.

By combining this inequality with Proposition
4.1
, the proof of Item (i) follows.

On the other hand, taking
F(\phi)=\phi_{\bf x}^{k}
in Lemma
1
, by Hölder’s inequality, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\langle\phi_{\bf x}^{k}\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}\leqslant\widetilde{C}\,\mathbb{E}^{1/2}\langle\phi_{\bf x}^{2k}\,\rangle_{\bm{0}}\leqslant\widetilde{C}\,\prod_{j=1}^{p}\mathbb{E}^{1/2p}\langle\phi_{x_{j}}^{2kp}\,\rangle_{\bm{0}}.

By combining the above inequalities with Proposition
4.1
, the proof of Item (ii) follows.
∎

The next result guarantees that the Hamiltonian

H_{n;\bm{\alpha}}(\phi)
in (
1
) is well-defined almost surely.

Proposition 4.3
.

The perturbing Hamiltonian
H^{\rm per}_{n;\bm{\alpha}}(\phi;\xi)
in (
2
)
converges almost surely.

Proof.

By Lemma 3.6
′′
of
[
9
]
and by definition of
H^{\rm per}_{n;\bm{\alpha}}(\phi;\xi)
, it is enough to verify that

(13)

\displaystyle\sum_{p=2}^{\infty}\alpha_{p}2^{-p}\mathbb{E}\langle|H_{n;p}(\phi;\xi)|\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}<\infty.

Indeed, taking
F(\phi)=|H_{n;p}(\phi;\xi)|
in Lemma
1
, by Proposition
4.2
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\langle|H_{n;p}(\phi;\xi)|\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}\leqslant\widetilde{C}\,\mathbb{E}^{1/2}\langle[H_{n;p}(\phi;\xi)]^{2}\,\rangle_{\bm{0}}\leqslant\widetilde{C}\sqrt{|V_{n}|}C_{2p}^{1/2}.

Multiplying by
\alpha_{p}2^{-p}
and then summing on
p\geqslant 2
in the above inequalities, by regularity condition (
5
), the statement in (
13
) follows.

We thus complete the proof.
∎

4.2.
Properties of the Free energy

For each
(\beta,h,u,r)\in(0,\infty)^{3}\times\mathbb{R}
, let

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lll}\psi_{n;\bm{\alpha}}=\psi_{n;\bm{\alpha}}(\beta,h,u,r)\coloneqq\dfrac{\log Z_{n;\bm{\alpha}}}{|V_{n}|};&&p_{n;\bm{\alpha}}=p_{n;\bm{\alpha}}(\beta,h,u,r)\coloneqq\mathbb{E}\psi_{n;\bm{\alpha}};\end{array}

be the free energy and the average free energy, respectively.

The following result states that
the perturbation term has a small influence in the computation of the expectation of the free energy
\psi_{n;\bm{\alpha}}
.

Lemma 3
.

We have

(15)

\displaystyle p_{n;\bm{0}}\leqslant p_{n;\bm{\alpha}}\leqslant p_{n;\bm{0}}+o(1),

where
p_{n;\bm{\alpha}}
is the average free energy given in (
4.2
).

Proof.

A simple computation shows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\log Z_{n;\bm{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{partition-function}}}{{=}}\mathbb{E}\log\int_{\mathbb{R}^{|V_{n}|}}\exp\big(-H_{n}(\phi;g)+H^{\rm per}_{n;\bm{\alpha}}(\phi;\xi)\big)\,{\rm D}\phi

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\log Z_{n;\bm{0}}\big\langle{\exp}\big({H^{\rm per}_{n;\bm{\alpha}}(\phi;\xi)}\big)\big\rangle_{\bm{0}}

(16)

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{E}\log Z_{n;\bm{0}}+\mathbb{E}\big\langle{\exp}\big({H^{\rm per}_{n;\bm{\alpha}}(\phi;\xi)}\big)\big\rangle_{\bm{0}}-1,

where in the last line we used the inequality
\log(x)\leqslant x-1
for
x>0
.

Let
X_{n}
be as in (
10
) and let
\widehat{\mathbb{E}}
be the expectation over
\xi_{\bf x}
,
p\geqslant 2
. Note that,
{H^{\rm per}_{n;\bm{\alpha}}(\phi;\xi)}=ic_{n}X_{n}
, and by (
4.1
), that

\mathbb{E}\langle|X_{n}|\rangle_{\bm{0}}<\infty.

Consequently, for
n\to\infty
, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big\langle{\exp}\big({H^{\rm per}_{n;\bm{\alpha}}(\phi;\xi)}\big)\big\rangle_{\bm{0}}=\mathbb{E}\big\langle{\exp}(ic_{n}X_{n})\big\rangle_{\bm{0}}

\displaystyle=1+ic_{n}\mathbb{E}\langle X_{n}\rangle_{\bm{0}}+ic_{n}o(1)

\displaystyle=1+\mathbb{E}\langle{H^{\rm per}_{n;\bm{\alpha}}(\phi;\xi)}\rangle_{\bm{0}}+ic_{n}o(1)

\displaystyle=1+\mathbb{E}\big\langle{\widehat{\mathbb{E}}H^{\rm per}_{n;\bm{\alpha}}(\phi;\xi)}\big\rangle_{\bm{0}}+ic_{n}o(1)

(17)

\displaystyle=1+ic_{n}o(1),

where in the third equality we use Fubini’s theorem to justify the change in order of expectations and in the last one that
\widehat{\mathbb{E}}H^{\rm per}_{n;\bm{\alpha}}(\phi;\xi)=0
.

By (
4.2
) and (
4.2
), we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\log Z_{n;\bm{\alpha}}\leqslant\mathbb{E}\log Z_{n;\bm{0}}+ic_{n}o(1).

Dividing the above inequality by
|V_{n}|
and using the definition in (
4.2
) of
p_{n;\bm{\alpha}}
, the proof of the upper bound (
15
) of the average free energy
p_{n;\bm{\alpha}}
follows.

Now we get the lower bound of
p_{n;\bm{\alpha}}
in (
15
). Indeed, a simple computation shows that

\displaystyle{1\over|V_{n}|}\,\log Z_{n;\bm{\alpha}}-{1\over|V_{n}|}\,\log Z_{n;\bm{0}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{partition-function}}}{{=}}{1\over|V_{n}|}\,\log{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{|V_{n}|}}\exp\big(-H_{n}(\phi;g)+H^{\rm per}_{n;\bm{\alpha}}(\phi;\xi)\big)\,{\rm D}\phi\over\int_{\mathbb{R}^{|V_{n}|}}\exp\big(-H_{n}(\phi;g)\big)\,{\rm D}\phi}

\displaystyle={1\over|V_{n}|}\,\log\big\langle\exp\big(H^{\rm per}_{n;\bm{\alpha}}(\phi;\xi)\big)\big\rangle_{\bm{0}}

\displaystyle\geqslant{1\over|V_{n}|}\,\big\langle{H^{\rm per}_{n;\bm{\alpha}}(\phi;\xi)}\big\rangle_{\bm{0}},

where in the above inequality we used Jensen’s inequality.
Taking the expectation from both sides of the above inequality,
we obtain

\displaystyle p_{n;\bm{\alpha}}-p_{n;\bm{0}}\geqslant{1\over|V_{n}|}\,\mathbb{E}\big\langle{H^{\rm per}_{n;\bm{\alpha}}(\phi;\xi)}\big\rangle_{\bm{0}}={1\over|V_{n}|}\,\mathbb{E}\big\langle{\widehat{\mathbb{E}}H^{\rm per}_{n;\bm{\alpha}}(\phi;\xi)}\big\rangle_{\bm{0}}=0,

because
\widehat{\mathbb{E}}H^{\rm per}_{n;\bm{\alpha}}(\phi;\xi)=0
. Thus, we have complete the proof of lemma.
∎

Lemma 4
.

For every
(\beta,h,u,r)\in(0,\infty)^{3}\times\mathbb{R}
, the limit, defined in (
4.2
),

\displaystyle p_{\bm{\alpha}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}p_{n;\bm{\alpha}}(\beta,h,u,r)

exists and is finite.

Proof.

From Lemma
3
,
p_{\bm{\alpha}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}p_{n;\bm{0}}(\beta,h,u,r)
. But, it is known that
\lim_{n\to\infty}p_{n;\bm{0}}
exists and is finite for every
(\beta,h,u,r)\in(0,\infty)^{3}\times\mathbb{R}
; see, e.g., Lemma II.2 of
[
6
]
. This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 5
.

The limit
p_{\bm{\alpha}}
is a convex function
of
h
for every fixed
\beta
,
u
and
r
. The same statement is true for
\psi_{n;\bm{\alpha}}
and
p_{n;\bm{\alpha}}
.

Proof.

The proof is trivial and omitted.
∎

4.3.
Upper bound of Variance of Free energy

Let
g=(g_{x})
and
g^{\prime}=(g_{x}^{\prime})
be two disorders consisting of i.i.d. standard Gaussian random variables.
For each
s\in[0,1]
, we define a new random field
G=(G_{x})
as follows:

\displaystyle G_{x}\coloneqq\sqrt{s}\,g_{x}+\sqrt{1-s}\,g_{x}^{\prime},\quad x\in V_{n}.

Additionally, similarly to
[
6
]
, we consider the following generating function:

\displaystyle\gamma_{n;\bm{\alpha}}(s)\coloneqq\mathbb{E}\big[\mathbb{E}^{\prime}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}\psi_{n;\bm{\alpha}}(G)\big]^{2}=\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}^{\prime}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}{1\over|V_{n}|}\,\log Z_{n;\bm{\alpha}}(G)\right]^{2},

with

\displaystyle Z_{n;\bm{\alpha}}(G)\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{|V_{n}|}}\exp\big(-H_{n}(\phi;G)+H^{\rm per}_{n;\bm{\alpha}}(\phi;\xi)\big)\,{\rm D}\phi,

where

H_{n}(\phi;G)
and
H^{\rm per}_{n;\bm{\alpha}}(\phi;\xi)
are as in (

) and (

), respectively. Here,

\mathbb{E}
and
\mathbb{E}^{\prime}
denote expectation over
g
and
g^{\prime}
, respectively, and
\widehat{\mathbb{E}}
is the expectation over
\xi_{\bf x}
,
p\geqslant 2
.

Proposition 4.4
.

For any
(\beta,h,u,r)\in(0,\infty)^{3}\times\mathbb{R}
, any
k\geqslant 1
integer and any

Furthermore, for any

\displaystyle\gamma^{(k)}_{n;\bm{\alpha}}(s_{0})\leqslant{(k-1)!\over(1-s_{0})^{k-1}}\,{h^{2}\widetilde{C}C_{4}^{1/2}\over|V_{n}|};

\displaystyle\gamma^{\prime}_{n;\bm{\alpha}}(1)\leqslant{h^{2}\widetilde{C}C_{4}^{1/2}\over|V_{n}|}.

4.4.
Properties of the Energy function due to disorder

For any
n\geqslant 1
, let us define

(18)

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}={1\over|V_{n}|}\,\sum_{x\in V_{n}}g_{x}\phi_{x}

the part of the energy function (
1
) due to the first disorder
g=(g_{x})
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be the countable set of all
(\beta,h,u,r)\in(0,\infty)^{3}\times\mathbb{R}

such that

\textstyle{\partial p_{\bm{\alpha}}\over\partial h^{-}}(\beta,h,u,r)\neq{\partial p_{\bm{\alpha}}\over\partial h^{+}}(\beta,h,u,r)
and

\textstyle{\partial p_{\bm{\alpha}}\over\partial r^{-}}(\beta,h,u,r)\neq{\partial p_{\bm{\alpha}}\over\partial r^{+}}(\beta,h,u,r)
.

Proposition 4.5
.

For any
(\beta,h,u,r)\in{\mathcal{A}}^{c}
,

1)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\langle\Delta_{n}\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\xrightarrow{\hskip 25.6073pt}}{\partial p_{\bm{\alpha}}\over\partial h}(\beta,h,u,r)
and

\mathbb{E}\big|\langle\Delta_{n}\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}-\mathbb{E}\langle\Delta_{n}\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}\big|\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\xrightarrow{\hskip 25.6073pt}}0;

2)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\langle R_{1,1}\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\xrightarrow{\hskip 25.6073pt}}{\partial p_{\bm{\alpha}}\over\partial r}(\beta,h,u,r)
and

\mathbb{E}\big|\langle R_{1,1}\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}-\mathbb{E}\langle R_{1,1}\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}\big|\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\xrightarrow{\hskip 25.6073pt}}0.

Proof.

The proof of this proposition is quite standard when we have the validity of the following three fundamental ingredients; see, e.g., Lemma 2.7 of
[
2
]
:

•

the convexity of
\psi_{n;\bm{\alpha}}
, which is guaranteed by Lemma
5
;

•

the variance of
\log Z_{n;\bm{\alpha}}
doesn’t grow faster than
|V_{n}|
, which is guaranteed by Lemma
6
; and that

•

the limit
p_{\bm{\alpha}}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}p_{n;\bm{\alpha}}
exists and is finite, which is guaranteed by Lemma
4
.

Therefore, the proof follows.
∎

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
F(\phi):\mathbb{R}^{V_{n}}\to\mathbb{R}
be a Borel function. If the following hold:

(a)

\mathbb{E}(\langle F^{2}(\phi)\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}-\langle F(\phi)\rangle^{2}_{\bm{\alpha}})=\mathbb{E}(\langle F^{2}(\phi)\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}-\mathbb{E}\langle F(\phi)\rangle^{2}_{\bm{\alpha}})\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\xrightarrow{\hskip 25.6073pt}}0
;

(b)

\mathbb{E}|\langle F(\phi)\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}-\mathbb{E}\langle F(\phi)\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}|\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\xrightarrow{\hskip 25.6073pt}}0
.

Then

\mathbb{E}\big\langle|F(\phi)-\mathbb{E}\langle F(\phi)\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}|\big\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\xrightarrow{\hskip 25.6073pt}}0.

Proof.

The proof is immediate since, by triangle and Lyapunov inequalities,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big\langle|F(\phi)-\mathbb{E}\langle F(\phi)\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}|\big\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{E}\langle|F(\phi)-\langle F(\phi)\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}|\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}+\mathbb{E}\big|\langle F(\phi)\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}-\mathbb{E}\langle F(\phi)\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}\big|

\displaystyle\leqslant\sqrt{\mathbb{E}(\langle F^{2}(\phi)\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}-\langle F(\phi)\rangle^{2}_{\bm{\alpha}})}+\mathbb{E}\big|\langle F(\phi)\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}-\mathbb{E}\langle F(\phi)\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}\big|.

∎

Lemma 7
.

For any
(\beta,h,u,r)\in{\mathcal{A}}^{c}
,

\mathbb{E}\big\langle|\Delta_{n}-\mathbb{E}\langle\Delta_{n}\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}|\big\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\xrightarrow{\hskip 25.6073pt}}0.

Proof.

Note that, by combining Proposition
4.5
-Item 1 and Proposition
4.6
, with
F(\phi)=\Delta_{n}
, it is sufficient to verify that

(19)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big(\langle\Delta_{n}^{2}\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}-\langle\Delta_{n}\rangle^{2}_{\bm{\alpha}}\big)\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\xrightarrow{\hskip 25.6073pt}}0.

Indeed, as sub-product of proof of Lemma II.7 in
[
6
]
we have the following inequality:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big(\langle\Delta_{n}^{2}\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}-\langle\Delta_{n}\rangle^{2}_{\bm{\alpha}}\big)

\displaystyle\leqslant{1\over h^{2}}\,\Biggl(\,\sum_{x,y\in V_{n}}\biggl(\mathbb{E}{\partial^{4}\psi_{n;\bm{\alpha}}\over\partial g_{x}^{2}\partial g_{y}^{2}}\biggr)^{2}\Biggr)^{1/2}+{1\over|V_{n}|^{2}}\,\sum_{x\in V_{n}}\mathbb{E}\big(\langle\phi_{x}^{2}\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}-\langle\phi_{x}\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}^{2}\big).

Using Proposition (
4.4
), with
k=4
,
x_{1}=x_{2}=x
and
x_{3}=x_{4}=y
:

\displaystyle\sum_{x,y\in V_{n}}\biggl(\mathbb{E}{\partial^{4}\psi_{n;\bm{\alpha}}\over\partial g_{x}^{2}\partial g_{y}^{2}}\biggr)^{2}=\gamma^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}_{n;\bm{\alpha}}(0),

we written the last inequality as follows

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big(\langle\Delta_{n}^{2}\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}-\langle\Delta_{n}\rangle^{2}_{\bm{\alpha}}\big)

\displaystyle\leqslant{1\over h^{2}}\,\big[\gamma^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}_{n;\bm{\alpha}}(0)\big]^{1/2}+{1\over|V_{n}|^{2}}\,\sum_{x\in V_{n}}\mathbb{E}\big(\langle\phi_{x}^{2}\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}-\langle\phi_{x}\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}^{2}\big).

By Proposition
4.4
and Lemma
2
-Item (i), the right-hand expression is at most

\displaystyle{1\over h}\,{\sqrt{6\widetilde{C}C_{4}^{1/2}}\over\sqrt{|V_{n}|}}+{\widetilde{C}C_{4}^{1/2}\over|V_{n}|}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big(\langle\Delta_{n}^{2}\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}-\langle\Delta_{n}\rangle^{2}_{\bm{\alpha}}\big)\leqslant{1\over h}\,{\sqrt{6\widetilde{C}C_{4}^{1/2}}\over\sqrt{|V_{n}|}}+{\widetilde{C}C_{4}^{1/2}\over|V_{n}|}.

Letting

in the above inequality, (
19
) follows.
∎

4.5.
The Ghirlanda-Guerra identities

Lemmas
7
and
8
enable us to derive the Ghirlanda-Guerra identities
[
3
,
4
,
10
,

,
12
,

]
(also sometimes called the Aizenman-Contucci identities
[

]
) at almost all
(\beta,h,u,r)
for the random mixed-spin Ginzburg-Landau model.
As usual, details are presented below for
the sake of completeness.

Take any integer
m\geqslant 2
and let
\phi^{1},\ldots,\phi^{m},\phi^{m+1}

denote
m+1
spin configurations drawn independently from the Gibbs measure.
Let
R_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}
be the overlap between
\phi^{\ell}
and
\phi^{\ell^{\prime}}
defined in (
8
),
with
\ell,\ell^{\prime}=1,\ldots,m+1
.
Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{m(m-1)/2}\to[-1,1]
be a bounded measurable function
of these overlaps that not change with
n
.

Lemma 9
.

Consider the random field mixed-spin Ginzburg-Landau model defined by the Gibbs measure in (
6
).
Then, if
f
is as above, the following identity

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\langle fR_{1,m+1}\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}-\frac{1}{m}\,\mathbb{E}\langle f\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}\mathbb{E}\langle R_{1,2}\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}-\frac{1}{m}\,\sum_{s=2}^{m}\mathbb{E}\langle fR_{1,s}\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\xrightarrow{\hskip 25.6073pt}}0

is satisfied at almost all
(\beta,h,u,r)
.

Proof.

Since
\|f\|_{\infty}\leqslant 1
, from Lemmas
7
and
8
, it follows that

(21)

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}\big\langle\Delta_{n}(\phi^{1})f\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}-\mathbb{E}\big\langle\Delta_{n}(\phi^{1})\big\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}\mathbb{E}\big\langle f\big\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}\right|\leqslant\mathbb{E}\big\langle|\Delta_{n}-\mathbb{E}\langle\Delta_{n}\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}|\big\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\xrightarrow{\hskip 25.6073pt}}0;

(22)

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}\big\langle R_{1,1}f\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}-\mathbb{E}\big\langle R_{1,1}\big\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}\mathbb{E}\big\langle f\big\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}\right|\leqslant\mathbb{E}\big\langle|R_{1,1}-\mathbb{E}\langle R_{1,1}\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}|\big\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\xrightarrow{\hskip 25.6073pt}}0;

where
\Delta_{n}
is as in (
18
).

Let
\widehat{\mathbb{E}}
be the expectation over
\xi_{\bf x}
,
p\geqslant 2
. Integration by parts gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big\langle\Delta_{n}(\phi^{1})f\big\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}

\displaystyle={1\over|V_{n}|}\,\sum_{x\in V_{n}}\mathbb{E}(g_{x}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}\langle\phi_{x}^{1}f\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}})

\displaystyle={1\over|V_{n}|}\,\sum_{x\in V_{n}}\mathbb{E}\biggl({\partial\widehat{\mathbb{E}}\langle\phi_{x}^{1}f\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}\over\partial g_{x}}\biggr)

(23)

\displaystyle=h\mathbb{E}\biggl\langle\Big(\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}R_{1,\ell}-mR_{1,m+1}\Big)f\biggr\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}.

In the particular case where

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big\langle\Delta_{n}(\phi^{1})\big\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}=h\mathbb{E}\langle R_{1,1}-R_{1,2}\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}.

By combining the above identity with (
21
) and (
4.5

\displaystyle\sup_{\|f\|_{\infty}\leqslant 1}\biggr|\mathbb{E}\langle R_{1,1}-R_{1,2}\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}\mathbb{E}\langle f\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}-\mathbb{E}\biggl\langle\Big(\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}R_{1,\ell}-mR_{1,m+1}\Big)f\biggr\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}\biggr|\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\xrightarrow{\hskip 25.6073pt}}0.

Finally, by applying (

∎

4.6.
The self-averaging of the spin overlap

Proposition 4.7
.

For any
(\beta,h,u,r)\in(0,\infty)^{3}\times\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{x,y\in V_{n}}(\mathbb{E}\langle\phi_{x};\phi_{y}\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}})^{2}\leqslant{\widetilde{C}C_{4}^{1/2}\over h^{2}}\,|V_{n}|,

where
\langle\phi_{x};\phi_{y}\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}
is the truncated two-point correlation.

Proof.

It is well-known that

\displaystyle{\partial^{2}\psi_{n;\bm{\alpha}}\over\partial g_{x}\partial g_{y}}={h^{2}\over|V_{n}|}\,\langle\phi_{x};\phi_{y}\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\sum_{x,y\in V_{n}}(\mathbb{E}\langle\phi_{x};\phi_{y}\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}})^{2}={|V_{n}|^{2}\over h^{4}}\,\sum_{x,y\in V_{n}}\biggl(\mathbb{E}{\partial^{2}\psi_{n;\bm{\alpha}}\over\partial g_{x}\partial g_{y}}\biggr)^{2}={|V_{n}|^{2}\over h^{4}}\,\gamma^{\prime\prime}_{n;\bm{\alpha}}(0).

Then the proof follows by applying Proposition
4.4
.
∎

Lemma 10

(Self-averaging of the overlap)
.

For any
(\beta,h,u,r)\in(0,\infty)^{3}\times\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big(\langle R_{1,2}^{2}\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}-\langle R_{1,2}\rangle^{2}_{\bm{\alpha}}\big)=\mathbb{E}\big\langle R_{1,2}-\langle R_{1,2}\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}\big\rangle^{2}_{\bm{\alpha}}\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\xrightarrow{\hskip 25.6073pt}}0.

Proof.

As sub-product of proof of Lemma II.12 in
[
6
]
, the following inequality remain valid:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big(\langle R_{1,2}^{2}\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}-\langle R_{1,2}\rangle^{2}_{\bm{\alpha}}\big)\leqslant{2\over|V_{n}|^{2}}\,\Biggl(\,\sum_{x,y\in V_{n}}\mathbb{E}\langle\phi_{x};\phi_{y}\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}^{2}\sum_{x,y\in V_{n}}\mathbb{E}^{1/2}\langle\phi_{x}^{4}\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}\,\mathbb{E}^{1/2}\langle\phi_{y}^{4}\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}\Biggr)^{1/2},

where the FKG inequality plays an important role in achieving this one.
By Lemma
2
-Item (i), the right-hand expression is at most

\displaystyle 2\sqrt{\widetilde{C}C_{8}^{1/2}}\Biggl({1\over|V_{n}|^{2}}\sum_{x,y\in V_{n}}\mathbb{E}\langle\phi_{x};\phi_{y}\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}^{2}\Biggr)^{1/2}.

Therefore,

(24)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big(\langle R_{1,2}^{2}\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}-\langle R_{1,2}\rangle^{2}_{\bm{\alpha}}\big)\leqslant 2\sqrt{\widetilde{C}C_{8}^{1/2}}\Biggl({1\over|V_{n}|^{2}}\sum_{x,y\in V_{n}}\mathbb{E}\langle\phi_{x};\phi_{y}\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}^{2}\Biggr)^{1/2}.

On the other hand, again, as sub-product of proof of Lemma II.11 in
[
6
]
, we have

\displaystyle{1\over|V_{n}|^{2}}\,\sum_{x,y\in V_{n}}\mathbb{E}\langle\phi_{x};\phi_{y}\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant{C^{\prime}\over|V_{n}|^{2}}\,\Biggl(\,\sum_{x,y\in V_{n}}(\mathbb{E}\langle\phi_{x};\phi_{y}\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}})^{2}|V_{n}|^{2}\Biggr)^{1/2}

\displaystyle+4\Biggl({1\over C^{\prime}}\,\sup_{x\in V_{n}}\mathbb{E}\langle\phi_{x}^{8}\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}\ {1\over|V_{n}|^{2}}\,\biggl(\,\sum_{x,y\in V_{n}}(\mathbb{E}\langle\phi_{x};\phi_{y}\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}})^{2}|V_{n}|^{2}\biggr)^{1/2}\,\Biggr)^{1/2},

where
C^{\prime}
is a positive constant. By using Proposition
4.7
and Lemma
2
-Item (i), the above inequality is

\displaystyle\leqslant{C^{\prime}\over h}\,{\sqrt{\widetilde{C}C_{4}^{1/2}}\over\sqrt{|V_{n}|}}+4\ \sqrt{\widetilde{C}C_{16}^{1/2}\over C^{\prime}h}\,{\sqrt[4]{\widetilde{C}C_{4}^{1/2}}\over\sqrt[4]{|V_{n}|}}.

Hence,

\displaystyle{1\over|V_{n}|^{2}}\,\sum_{x,y\in V_{n}}\mathbb{E}\langle\phi_{x};\phi_{y}\rangle_{\bm{\alpha}}^{2}\leqslant{C^{\prime}\over h}\,{\sqrt{\widetilde{C}C_{4}^{1/2}}\over\sqrt{|V_{n}|}}+4\ \sqrt{\widetilde{C}C_{16}^{1/2}\over C^{\prime}h}\,{\sqrt[4]{\widetilde{C}C_{4}^{1/2}}\over\sqrt[4]{|V_{n}|}}.

By combining the above inequality with (
24
) and then letting

∎

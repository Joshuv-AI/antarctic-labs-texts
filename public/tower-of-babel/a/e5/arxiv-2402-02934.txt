Quantum loop corrections in the modified gravity model of
Starobinsky inflation with primordial black hole production

Abstract




September 2024 IPMU24-0002

Abstract

A modified gravity model of Starobinsky inflation and primordial black hole production was proposed in good (within
1\sigma
) agreement with current measurements of the cosmic microwave background radiation. The model is an extension of the singularity-free Appleby-Battye-Starobinsky model by the
R^{4}
-term with different values of the parameters whose fine-tuning leads to efficient production of primordial black holes on smaller scales with the asteroid-size masses between
10^{16}
g and
10^{20}
g. Those primordial black holes may be part (or the whole) of the current dark matter, while the proposed model can be confirmed or falsified by detection or absence of the induced gravitational waves with the frequencies in the
10^{-2}
Hz range. The relative size of quantum (loop) corrections to the power spectrum of scalar perturbations in the model is found to be of the order
10^{-3}
or less, so that the model is not ruled out by the quantum corrections.

The model

The phenomenological model
[
8
]
of inflation and PBH production has the
F(R)

S={{1}\over{2}}\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\,F(R)~~,

(1)

F(R)=(1+g\tanh b)R+gE_{AB}\ln\left[{{\cosh\left({\textstyle{R\over\vphantom{2}\smash{\raise 0.60275pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{E_{AB}}$}}}}-b\right)}\over{\cosh(b)}}\right]+{{R^{2}}\over{6M^{2}}}-\delta{{R^{4}}\over{48M^{6}}}~~,

(2)

where the first three terms are known in the literature as the Appleby-Battye-Starobinsky (ABS) model
[
20
]
with
the Starobinsky mass
M\approx 1.3\times 10^{-5}
defining the scale of the first SR phase of inflation.The ABS parameter

E_{AB}={{R_{0}}\over{2g\ln(1+e^{2b})}}

(3)

has the new scale

2

The other parameters

To produce PBH, one needs a large enhancement of the power spectrum of scalar perturbations by seven orders of magnitude against the CMB spectrum. Then, as was shown in Ref.
[

R_{0}\approx 3.00M^{2}~,\quad g\approx 2.25\quad{\rm and}\quad b\approx 2.89~.

PBH-induced GW and PBH-DM density fraction

PBH production in the early universe leads to stochastic gravitational waves (GW) different from primordial GW caused by inflation. The current energy density fraction of those PBH-induced GW can be computed in the second order with respect to perturbations as
[
24
,
25
]

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mathrm{GW}}(k)=

\displaystyle{{c_{g}\Omega_{r,0}}\over{36}}\int^{{{1}\over{\sqrt{3}}}}_{0}\,\mathrm{d}d\int^{\infty}_{{{1}\over{\sqrt{3}}}}\,\mathrm{d}s\left[{{(d^{2}-1/3)(s^{2}-1/3)}\over{s^{2}-d^{2}}}\right]^{2}\times

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\zeta}\left({{k\sqrt{3}}\over{2}}(s+d)\right)\mathcal{P}_{\zeta}\left({{k\sqrt{3}}\over{2}}(s-d)\right)\left[\mathcal{I}_{c}(d,s)^{2}+\mathcal{I}_{s}(d,s)^{2}\right]~,

(12)

where the functions
\mathcal{I}_{c}(d,s)
and
\mathcal{I}_{s}(d,s)
are

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{c}(d,s)

\displaystyle=-36\pi{{(s^{2}+d^{2}-2)^{2}}\over{(s^{2}-d^{2})^{3}}}\theta(s-1)~,

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{s}(d,s)

\displaystyle=-36{{s^{2}+d^{2}-2}\over{(s^{2}-d^{2})^{2}}}\left[{{s^{2}+d^{2}-2}\over{(s^{2}-d^{2})}}\mathrm{ln}\left|{{d^{2}-1}\over{s^{2}-1}}\right|+2\right]~,

(13)

4
Loop corrections

In the
\delta N
formalism
[
19
]
for single-field inflation, a scalar (comoving curvature) perturbation

\delta N\equiv\zeta
is a function of variation
\delta\phi
of inflaton
\phi_{i}
at its initial value,

\delta N=N^{\prime}(\phi)\delta\phi+{{N^{\prime\prime}(\phi)}\over{2!}}\delta\phi^{2}+{{N^{\prime\prime\prime}(\phi)}\over{3!}}\delta\phi^{3}+\ldots~,

(20)

where perturbations are not assumed to be small. The power spectrum of scalar perturbations is defined by a two-point function of Fourier components
\zeta_{\bf p}
as

\langle\zeta_{\bf p_{1}}\zeta_{\bf p_{2}}\rangle=(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{3}({\bf{p_{1}}}+{\bf p_{2}})P(p)~,\quad P_{\zeta}(p)={{k^{3}}\over{2\pi^{2}}}P(p)~,

(21)

where

\zeta_{\bf p}=N^{\prime}\delta\phi_{\bf p}+{{N^{\prime\prime}}\over{2!}}\int{{d^{3}q}\over{(2\pi)^{3}}}\delta\phi_{\bf q}\delta\phi_{\bf p-q}+{{N^{\prime\prime\prime}}\over{3!}}\int{{d^{3}q_{1}}\over{(2\pi)^{3}}}{{d^{3}q_{2}}\over{(2\pi)^{3}}}\delta\phi_{\bf q_{1}}\delta\phi_{\bf q_{2}}\delta\phi_{\bf p-q_{1}-q_{2}}+\ldots

(22)

in terms of external 3D momenta
{\bf p}
and loop momenta
{\bf q}
. Substituting Eq. (
22
) into Eq. (
21
) yields the loop expansion of the power spectrum
P_{\zeta}(p)
. In order to apply that to a particular model, one has to know the function
N(\phi)
explicitly. It was derived in Ref.
[
16
]
,

N_{\rm total}(\phi_{i})\simeq{{1}\over{\eta_{\rm sr}}}\ln\Big[1+{{\eta_{\rm sr}}\over{\sqrt{2\epsilon_{\rm sr}}}}(\phi_{i}-\phi_{s})\Big]-{{1}\over{3}}\ln\Big[1+{{3(\phi_{s}-\phi_{e})}\over{\pi_{s}}}\Big]+{{1}\over{\eta_{\rm V}}}\ln[-2\eta_{\rm V}\pi_{e}-6\sqrt{2\epsilon_{\rm V}}]~,

(23)

where the first term refers to the SR(I) phase with the initial value
\phi_{i}
and the end value
\phi_{s}
, the second term refers to the USR phase with the initial momentum value
\pi_{s}
and the end momentum value
\pi_{e}
, and the third terms refers to the SR(II) phase with the slow-roll parameters
\epsilon_{\rm V}
and
\eta_{\rm V}
. The subscripts
(s,e)
refer to values of any quantity at the start and end of the USR phase, respectively. The leading contribution comes from the first term in Eq. (
23
).

To compute loop corrections, one has to calculate the derivatives
N^{(n)}(\phi)
. The first three derivatives can be estimated as follows:

N^{\prime}\approx{{1}\over{\sqrt{2\epsilon_{s}}}}\approx{{e^{-3\Delta N_{\rm USR}}}\over{\sqrt{2\epsilon_{e}}}}~,\quad N^{\prime\prime}\approx-{{\eta_{\rm sr}}\over{2\epsilon_{s}}}~,\quad N^{\prime\prime\prime}\approx{{2\eta_{\rm sr}^{2}}\over{(2\epsilon_{s})^{3/2}}}~~,

(24)

where
\Delta N_{\rm USR}
is the duration of the USR phase and
\eta_{\rm sr}\equiv\eta_{\rm sr}(\phi_{s})
.

To compute loop corrections to the amplitude of the power spectrum, we considered the effective action up to the third order with respect to
\delta\phi(\bm{x},t)
on the background
\phi(t)
,

S_{\delta\phi}=S^{(2)}_{\delta\phi}+S^{(3)}_{\delta\phi}=\hbox{\large{${{\textstyle{{1}\over\vphantom{2}\smash{\raise 0.72331pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{{2}}$}}}}}$}}\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\Big(g_{\mu\nu}\partial^{\mu}\delta\phi\partial^{\nu}\delta\phi-V_{,\phi\phi}\delta\phi^{2}\Big)+\hbox{\large{${{\textstyle{{1}\over\vphantom{2}\smash{\raise 0.72331pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{{2}}$}}}}}$}}\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\Big(-\hbox{\large{${{\textstyle{{1}\over\vphantom{2}\smash{\raise 0.72331pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{{3}}$}}}}}$}}V_{,\phi\phi\phi}\delta\phi^{3}\Big).

(25)

A comparable contribution of the quartic coupling
V_{,\phi\phi\phi\phi}
was investigated in Ref.
[
45
]
. When using the FLRW background, the effective action reads

S_{\delta\phi}=\hbox{\large{${{\textstyle{{1}\over\vphantom{2}\smash{\raise 0.72331pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{{2}}$}}}}}$}}\int d^{4}x\,a^{3}(t)\Big[\dot{\delta\phi}^{2}-a^{-2}(t)(\partial\delta\phi)^{2}-V_{,\phi\phi}\delta\phi^{2}\Big]+\int d^{4}x\Big[-\hbox{\large{${{\textstyle{{a^{3}(t)}\over\vphantom{2}\smash{\raise 0.72331pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{{3!}}$}}}}}$}}V_{,\phi\phi\phi}\delta\phi^{3}\Big]~,

(26)

where
\partial\equiv\sum_{i}\partial_{i}
is a sum of spatial derivatives. The mode functions arising in the solutions to classical equations of motion from the action (
26
)
with Bunch-Davies initial conditions,

u_{k}(\tau)={{H}\over{\sqrt{2k^{3}}}}(1+ik\tau)e^{-ik\tau}~,

(27)

are written down in terms of the conformal time
d\tau=\hbox{\large{${{\textstyle{{dt}\over\vphantom{2}\smash{\raise 0.72331pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{{a}}$}}}}}$}}
, where
-\infty<\tau\leq 0
. The CMB modes that left the horizon during inflation are given by
u_{k}(0)={{H}\over{\sqrt{2k^{3}}}}
.

Canonical quantization implies a decomposition into positive and negative parts, as well as the commutation relations (in the
interaction picture)

\begin{split}&\delta\phi^{I}_{\bf k}(\tau)=\delta\phi^{+}_{\bf k}(\tau)+\delta\phi^{-}_{\bf k}(\tau)=u_{k}(\tau)a_{\bf k}+u_{k}^{*}(\tau)a^{\dagger}_{\bf-k}~,\\
&[a_{\bf p},a^{\dagger}_{\bf q}]=(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{3}(\bm{p}-\bm{q}),~~[a_{\bf p},a_{\bf q}]=[a^{\dagger}_{\bf p},a^{\dagger}_{\bf q}]=0~.\end{split}

(28)

To get the 1-loop correction according to Eqs. (
21
) and (
22
), one has to evaluate the three-point correlation function. For this purpose we applied the
in-in
formalism that gives

\langle\delta\phi_{\bf p}\delta\phi_{\bf q}\delta\phi_{\bf-p-q}\rangle=\langle\overline{T}e^{i\int_{t_{0}}^{t}d\widetilde{t}H_{\rm int}}~\delta\phi^{I}_{\bf p}\delta\phi^{I}_{\bf q}\delta\phi^{I}_{\bf-p-q}~Te^{-i\int_{t_{0}}^{t}d\widetilde{t}H_{\rm int}}\rangle~,

(29)

where
T
and
\overline{T}
stand for the time ordering and anti-time ordering respectively,
t_{0}
and
t
are the times associated with the subhorizon and superhorizon scales, respectively, and
H_{\rm int}(\tilde{t})
is the interaction Hamiltonian in the 3rd order,

H_{\rm int}(\tilde{t})=\hbox{\large{${{\textstyle{{1}\over\vphantom{2}\smash{\raise 0.72331pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{{3!}}$}}}}}$}}a^{3}(\tilde{t})V_{,\phi\phi\phi}\int d^{3}x(\delta\phi^{I}(\bm{x},\tilde{t}))^{3}={{1}\over{3!(2\pi)^{6}}}a^{3}(\tilde{t})V_{,\phi\phi\phi}\int d^{3}kd^{3}\lambda\delta\phi^{I}_{\bf k}(\tilde{t})\delta\phi^{I}_{\bm{\lambda}}(\tilde{t})\delta\phi^{I}_{\bf-k-\bm{\lambda}}(\tilde{t})~.

(30)

The standard (Friedmann and Klein-Gordon) equations of motion yield the following asymptotic approximation for the third derivative of the potential in terms of the Hubble flow parameters (
11
):

V_{,\phi\phi\phi}\simeq-{{3H\dot{\eta_{H}}}\over{2\sqrt{2\epsilon_{H}}}}~~.

(31)

Expanding the T-exponentials in Eq. (
29
) to the first order with respect to
H_{\rm int}
, we find

\begin{split}&\langle\delta\phi_{\bf p}(t)\delta\phi_{\bf q}(t)\delta\phi_{\bf-p-q}(t)\rangle\approx-i\int_{t_{0}}^{t}d\widetilde{t}\langle[\delta\phi^{I}_{\bf p}(t)\delta\phi^{I}_{\bf q}(t)\delta\phi^{I}_{\bf-p-q}(t),H^{int}(\tilde{t})]\rangle\\
&=2{\rm Im}\Bigg(\int_{t_{0}}^{t}d\widetilde{t}\langle\delta\phi^{I}_{\bf p}(t)\delta\phi^{I}_{\bf q}(t)\delta\phi^{I}_{\bf-p-q}(t)H^{int}(\tilde{t})\rangle\Bigg)~.\end{split}

(32)

After substituting Eqs. (
27
), (
28
), (
30
) and (
31
) into Eq. (
32
) and using Wick’s theorem, we derived the three-point correlator as follows:

\begin{split}&\langle\delta\phi_{\bf p}\delta\phi_{\bf q}\delta\phi_{\bf-p-q}\rangle=\\

(33)

where the

5
Conclusion

The main new results of this paper are given by Fig.
6
, Eqs. (
19
) and (
35
).
It follows that the modified gravity model
[
8
]
of Starobinsky inflation with PBH production may generate a significant part (or the whole) of dark matter from PBH, while it is not ruled out by quantum loop corrections because the latter are relatively small by the factor of
10^{-3}
against the tree-level (classical) contribution. The key role in the last conclusion was played by the derivatives of the function
N(\phi)
during the USR phase,
describing superhorizon curvature perturbations in the
\delta N
formalism, which led to the suppression of loop contributions. It is worth mentioning that our results only apply to the particular phenomenological model of PBH production related to Starobinsky inflation.

The predicted frequency
f_{p}\approx 2.55\cdot 10^{-2}
Hz of the PBH-production-induced stochastic GW is in the range between
10^{-3}
Hz and
10^{-1}
Hz of the frequencies that are expected to be sensitive to the future space-based gravitational interferometers.

As was recently pointed out in the literature
[
50
,
51
,
52
,
53
]
, the standard result for the primordial black hole survival at present, based on the Hawking semiclassical evaporation formula, may be relaxed below
10^{15}
g, when going beyond the semiclassical approximation. Should this be the case, fine-tuning of the parameters in our model for efficient PBH production (needed for DM) may be significantly relaxed.

Localization for CS manifolds and volume of homogeneous superspaces
Vera Serganova, Dmitry Vaintrob

Abstract
We prove the Schwarz-Zaboronsky localization theorem for CS manifolds and use this to give a volume calculation for homogeneous superspaces for super-Lie groups that lack a real form.



1
Introduction

In the paper
[
5
]
, Schwarz and Zaboronsky prove a powerful localization result in supergeometry which in particular implies the Duistermaat-Heckman formula for hamiltonian actions. Namely, they show that if
X
is a super-manifold with an odd vector field
Q
, such that
Q^{2}
has a certain compactness property and
Q
has well-behaved fixed points, then any
Q
-equivariant Berezinian integral localizes to an integral over the fixed point submanifold
X^{Q}.

Their proof is in the context of (real) super-manifolds; in this paper we re-interpret it slightly in a way that applies to CS super-manifolds in the case of isolated fixed points.

We apply this theorem to the representation theory of quasireductive algebraic
supergroups, i.e., supergroups whose reduced group is reductive. In

[
4
]
the first author and A. Sherman introduced
the notion of a splitting subgroup. If
K\subset G
is a pair of
quasireductive groups then
K
is splitting in
G
if the trivial

G
-submodule
\mathbb{C}\subset\operatorname{Ind}^{G}_{K}\mathbb{C}

splits as a
G
-module. The splitting condition is equivalent to
relative reductivity: any short exact sequence of
G
-modules splits
if and only if it splits over
K
. The analogous notion is trivial in the theory of compact Lie groups,
where any inclusion
K\subset G
is splitting (since the representation theory is semisimple),
but has an analogue in modular representation theory. Namely, working
over a base field of characteristic
p
, it turns out that an inclusion of finite groups

K\subset G
is splitting if and only if
K
contain a
Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G
. Many properties of representations of
G
can be deduced from
those of
K
. Splitting subgroups should be useful for giving a super-Lie group generalisation of
the Green correspondence theorem from modular representation
theory of finite groups. They are also important in the theory of support
varieties in the super Lie group setting.

In
[
4
]
small splitting subgroups
are constructed for
G=GL(m|n)
,
G=Q(n)
and all Kac-Moody supergroups
of defect
1
. For a general Kac-Moody supergroup
G
, the authors of that paper define a
defect subgroup
D
of
G
isomorphic to a direct product of several
copies of
SL(1|1)
and conjecture that
D
is minimal splitting. They were
able to prove that
D
is splitting for
GL(m|n)
by passing to the
compact real form
X
of the homogeneous superspace
G/K
where
K
is the connected
component of the centralizer of
\underline{D}
. After they show that
X
has
nonzero volume the result follows by classical unitary trick. For
other Kac-Moody supergroups they encounter an obstacle, as
G/K
does not
have a compact real form. In the present paper we are able to obtain a simple uniform proof
of the fact that
D
is splitting for all Kac-Moody supergroups

G
using the CS structure on
G/K
and the new CS localization formula.
A nice corollary of this fact is a ”projectivity criterion” for all Kac-Moody
supergroups (see
[
1
]
for the history of this notion). Using the same method we
obtain small splitting subgroups of periplectic groups. This fully resolves the question of
constructing small splitting subgroups (i.e., splitting subgroups whose only simple
subfactors are abelian supergroups and
SL_{2}
) for all simple and almost simple
quasireductive groups
G
which appear in the Kac classification,
[
2
]
.

1.1
Notation and conventions

In this paper we work with
C^{\infty}
CS manifolds, i.e., ringed spaces of
\mathbb{C}
-super-algebras which are locally isomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with ring of functions
C^{\infty}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{R}^{m\mid n}).
All ringed spaces and all instances of the sheaf
C^{\infty}
will by convention be taken with complex coefficients. Given a CS manifold
X
, write
\underline{X}
for the underlying topological space and
X^{red}
for the purely even supermanifold
\big(\underline{X},(C^{\infty}_{X})^{red}\big)
with reduced sheaf of functions, which is canonically isomorphic as a ringed space to the sheaf of complex-valued
C^{\infty}
functions on the underlying manifold
\underline{X}.

All algebra in this paper is super, so “derivation” means super-derivation, “commutator” means super-commutator, etc. All manifolds are super-ringed spaces with a suitable local model.

We write
CS vector space
for the notion of a complex supervector space
V=V_{0}\oplus\Pi V_{1}
with a choice of real structure
(V_{0})_{\mathbb{R}}\subset V_{0}
on the even part. Associated to such a space, we have a “linear” CS manifold
V_{CS}
with underlying space
(V_{0})_{\mathbb{R}}
and sheaf of functions

C^{\infty}(V_{CS}):=C^{\infty}((V_{0})_{\mathbb{R}})\otimes S^{*}\Pi(V_{1}^{*}).

It is clear that any (even) linear map of CS spaces
L:V\to W
which takes
(V_{0})_{\mathbb{R}}
to
(W_{0})_{\mathbb{R}}
induces a map
L_{CS}:V_{CS}\to W_{CS}
in a functorial way. We say that an
orientation
on a CS vector space
V
is a choice of orientation on the real vector space
(V_{0})_{\mathbb{R}},
equivalently a choice of orientation on the CS manifold
V_{CS}.

A vector field on a CS supermanifold is a derivation of the sheaf of functions, equivalently a section of the sheaf
T_{X}
(defined as usual, as the normal bundle of the diagonal in
X^{2}
). The reduced part (a.k.a. “body”) of a vector field
\xi
is the restriction
\xi\mid_{X^{red}},
a section of

T^{red}X:=TX\mid_{X^{red}}.

Note that we have canonically
TX\mid_{X^{red}}\cong T(X^{red})\oplus\Pi N_{X/X^{red}},
where
N_{X/X^{red}}
is the normal bundle of odd directions. In particular, note that the complex bundle
T_{0}^{red}:=T^{red}(X)_{0}
is isomorphic (as a bundle over
X^{red}
) to the tangent bundle
T(X^{red})
of the even manifold, and in particular has canonical real structure
T^{red}_{0,\mathbb{R}}\subset T^{red}_{0}
inherited from the canonical real structure on the even manifold
X^{red}.
The odd part
T^{red}_{1}\cong N_{X/X^{red}}
(a bundle on
X^{red}
) will not in general have canonical real structure, and indeed can have obstructions to any choice of real structure (e.g. a nonzero first chern class). Note that an even vector field
\xi
can be (locally) integrated to positive time if and only if
\xi^{red}
is real, and this gives an alternative intrinsic characterization of real structure on
T^{red}_{0}.

Given a vector field
Q
(either even or odd), we define the invariant vanishing ideal sheaf

I_{Q}(X):=C^{\infty}\cdot Q(C^{\infty})

(following the algebraic geometry definition). It is obvious that if
\xi
is a vector field (either even or odd), then the restriction
I_{Q}(X)\mid X^{red}
is precisely the ideal of vanishing of
\xi^{red}.

Given a super-vector space
V
, respectively, a super-line bundle
V
over a base CS manifold
X
, its (super-)determinant
\operatorname{sDet}(V)
is understood in the super-sense as a
(1,0)
-dimensional space, resp., as a line bundle over
X
(so if
V
is a vector space, resp., a super-vector bundle over an even base, we have canonically
\operatorname{sDet}(V)=\operatorname{Det}(V_{0})\otimes\operatorname{Det}(V_{1})^{-1}
). We view
\operatorname{Det}
as a functor on the category of super-vector vector spaces with isomorhpisms, resp., on the category of super-bundles with isomorhpism. In particular, given an automorphism
V\to V
of a vector space its determinant give an automorphism
\text{Aut}(\operatorname{sDet}(V))
of a one-dimensional space, which is canonically identified with
\mathbb{C}^{*}.
We define the Berezinian line bundle
\operatorname{Ber}_{X}:=\operatorname{sDet}(T^{*}_{X}).
Sections of a Berezinian on an oriented CS manifold can be integrated in the same way (and using the same formulas) as on a (real) supermanifold: see e.g.
[
7
]
.

1.2
The CS localization theorem

In the Schwarz-Zaboronsky paper
[
5
]
, they prove a localization result for a manifold with a choice of vector field
Q
that has a so-called
compactness
condition. This condition is equivalent to the existence of a certain compact super-Lie group
{G_{Q}}
which acts on
X,
such that
Q
is the vector field associated to the action of some odd Lie algebra element
Q_{lie}\in\mathfrak{g}_{1}
for
\mathfrak{g}
the Lie algebra associated to
{G_{Q}}
. (Note that in the sequel, we will abuse notation and denote both the lie algebra element
Q_{lie}\in\mathfrak{g}_{1}
and the vector field
Q
given by its action by the same letter.) In the CS case we will also work with a group
{G_{Q}}
with a distinguished odd Lie algebra element, which we consisder fixed throughout the paper.

Definition 1
.

A CS
Q
-group is a
compact
CS super-Lie group
{G_{Q}}
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and a choice of odd element
Q\in\mathfrak{g}_{1},
such that
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
is commutative and central and
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
is one-dimensional and generated by
Q.

Note that this implies that
{G_{Q}}^{red}
is a real torus, and we will write by convention

T:={G_{Q}}^{red}.

It is clear that a
Q
-group
({G_{Q}},Q)
is determined up to isomorphism by the torus
T:={G_{Q}}^{red}
and the element
Q^{2}\in\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{C}}
in the
complex
Lie algebra of
T.

Given a
Q
-group
{G_{Q}}
, we will be studying
{G_{Q}}
-equivariant manifolds
X
, with additional
{G_{Q}}
-equivariant (or sometimes, only
T
-equivariant) structures.

We will prove a localization theorem under an additional two conditions on the
{G_{Q}}
-action, namely:

Definition 2
.

A (reduced) fixed point
x\in\underline{X}^{Q}
in a
Q
-manifold is nondegenerate if, for every
Q
-fixed point
x\in\underline{X}^{Q},
the action of
Q
on the tangent bundle
T_{x}X
is an odd automorphism.

Note that nondegeneracy of a fixed point
x\in\underline{X}
means that
x
is a “scheme-theoretically” isolated fixed point, i.e., that in an open neighborhood of
x
we have an equality of sheaves of ideals
I_{Q}=m_{x}
between
I_{Q}
and the maximal ideal at the point. Suppose
{G_{Q}}
is a
Q
-group and
X
is a
{G_{Q}}
-manifold with discrete
Q
-fixed points which are nondegenerate. Let
i:X^{Q}\to X
be the embedding of the fixed points. In this context we prove the following result.

Theorem 1

(Localization)
.

There exists an isomorphism of line bundles on
X^{Q}

\operatorname{Loc}:i^{*}\operatorname{Ber}_{X}\to C_{X^{Q}},

such that,
for any compactly supported and
Q
-equivariant Berezinian form

\omega\in\Gamma(\underline{X},\operatorname{Ber}_{X}),
we have the equality

\int_{X}\omega=\sum_{X^{Q}}\operatorname{Loc}\omega.

Note that since
X^{Q}
is discrete, line bundles on it are simply collections of one-dimensional vector spaces indexed by fixed points (points of
X^{Q}
). The map
\operatorname{Loc}
is thus equivalent to choosing a parametrization of the Berezinian fiber
\operatorname{Ber}_{X}\mid_{x}
with
\mathbb{C}
for each point
x\in X^{Q}.
We give an explicit formula for this identification in definition
4
, and deduce a precise (and computable) version localization formula in Theorem
7
.

This is a generalization to the CS context of one of the main results (Lemma 2) in
[
5
]
. Note that the main result of
[
5
]
, their Lemma 3, implies that an analogous localization result also holds (in the real case) when the fixed points are reduced and nondegenerate but not necessarily discrete. We expect such a generalization to hold also in the CS case, though we do not prove it here.

Along the way we also prove the following
Q
-acycicity
lemma.

Lemma 2

(
Q
-acyclicity)
.

Suppose
X
is a
{G_{Q}}
-equivariant CS manifold and
Q
is everywhere nonvanishing. Then there exists an odd function
\beta\in C^{\infty}(X)_{1}
such that
Q\beta=1.

This is a CS version of a secondary result, Theorem
1
, of
[
5
]
. Note that this lemma implies the main theorem in the case where
Q
has no fixed points: indeed, then the right hand side of the localization formula is an empty sum, hence
0
, whereas for any
\omega
with
Q\omega=0
we can write the left hand integral
\int\omega=\int Q(\beta\omega),
which is the integral of a total derivative of a compactly supported Berezinian — hence equal to zero.

1.3
Comparison with Schwarz-Zaboronsky

Recall that a (real) supermanifold is the same thing as a CS manifold with a choice of real structure
C^{\infty}_{\mathbb{R}}\subset C^{\infty}
on functions. Thus any suitably functorial result about CS manifolds implies a corresponding result for real supermanifolds. We can thus define a
real
Q
-group

{G_{Q}}
to be a real Lie group with a choice of real vector
Q\in\mathfrak{g}_{1},
such that the data
({G_{Q}},Q)
forms a CS
Q
-Lie group after forgetting the real structure.

Since
Q
is real,
Q^{2}\in\mathfrak{g}_{0,\mathbb{R}}
is real, and so we can define exponents
\exp(tQ^{2})\in{G_{Q}}^{red}=T
for any real number
t\in\mathbb{R}.
We say a real
Q
-Lie group
({G_{Q}},Q)
is
minimal
if
\exp(\mathbb{R}Q^{2})
is dense in
{G_{Q}}^{red}.
Note that in this case, the action of
{G_{Q}}
on a manifold or a vector space is determined (by continuity) by the action of
Q
, and a function or vector is
{G_{Q}}
-invariant if and only if it is
Q
-invariant.

Remark 1
.

It is, furthermore, easy to see that any real
Q
-Lie group is a product of a minimal
Q
-Lie group with an even torus.

In our language, Schwarz and Zaboronsky prove the following theorem in the case of vector fields with isolated fixed points.

Theorem

(Schwarz-Zaboronsky,
[
5
]
, Section 4)
.

Suppose given a (real) minimal super
Q
-Lie group
{G_{Q}}
and a
{G_{Q}}
-equivariant space
X
, such that
Q
has isolated and nondegenerate fixed points on
X
. Then the integral

\int\omega

of a compactly supported
{G_{Q}}
-equivariant Berezinian
\omega\in\Gamma_{comp}(X,\operatorname{Ber}_{X})
only depends on the vectors

\omega\mid_{x_{i}}\in\operatorname{Ber}_{X}\mid_{x_{i}},

for
x_{i}
running through the set of fixed poins
X^{Q}.

Moreover,
[
5
]
gives a formula for the contribution at each fixed point in terms of local coordinates and an auxiliary choice of a metric on its tangent space.

Note that the minimality condition can be easily removed here by Remark
1
, and the Schwarz-Zaboronsky result holds for arbitrary real
Q
-groups
{G_{Q}}
acting on
X
with nondegenerate fixed points. The result of the present paper replaces both the manifold
X
and also the group
{G_{Q}}
by CS versions. Note that (the CS group underlying) a real
Q
-Lie group
{G_{Q}}
can act on a CS manifold, and we already obtain a new result in this case. Our result is also more general in allowing the group
{G_{Q}}
to be itself CS (and the element
Q
to be non-real). In this context, when
Q^{2}\in\mathfrak{g}_{0}
is not a real even Lie algebra element, there seems to be no good notion of minimality for a Lie group
{G_{Q}}
associated to
Q
(and certainly it is unreasonable to expect that the action of
{G_{Q}}
is determined by the vector field associated to
Q
). Thus the decision of carrying the full
Q
-Lie group
{G_{Q}}
(rather than just the vector field given by the action of
Q\in\mathfrak{g}_{1}
) is necessary in this context.

1.4
Idea of proof of localization

The key idea of our proof is to refine a statement about scalars to a statement about
distributions
, i.e., possibly singular integral forms on the manifold
X
. Indeed, on the one hand, any Berezinian
\omega
on
X
is a non-singular distribution. On the other hand, if
X
is a CS manifold and
x\in X
is a reduced point, we have a
delta distribution

\delta_{x},
which is an even distribution supported at
x
and uniquely determined by the property

\int_{X}\delta_{x}\cdot f=f(x)

for any even function
f
on the CS manifold. In particular, since
\int_{X}\delta_{x}=1
for any
x\in X,
the localization formula is equivalent to the statement

\int_{X}\omega=\int_{X}\sum_{x\in X^{Q}}(\operatorname{Loc}_{x}\omega)\cdot\delta_{x}

(for
\omega
a compactly supported and
{G_{Q}}
-invariant Berezinian), where
\operatorname{Loc}_{x}\omega
are the scalar local contributions. Our proof proceeds by showing that the difference between these two distributions is a total derivative, i.e., we find an odd compactly supported distribution
D
such tha

QD=\omega-\sum_{x\in X^{Q}}(\operatorname{Loc}_{x}\omega)\cdot\delta_{x}.

Since the integral of a total derivative is zero, the localization theorem follows. We construct such a distribution-valued enhancement of the localization result for certain “nice”
{G_{Q}}
-manifolds (namely linear
{G_{Q}}
-manifolds and
{G_{Q}}
-manifolds with no
Q
-fixed points), and show that any manifold
X
with nondegenerate
Q
-fixed points can be glued out of such models.

We note that, at first glance, our proof is quite different from Schwarz and Zaboronsky’s argument
[
5
]
(beyond the fact that it applies in the more general CS context). Indeed, they use limits of oscillating integrals instead of the theory of distributions. However their result can be re-interpreted (by viewing regularized limits of oscillating wavefunctions as generalized functions) as also constructing a distribution
D_{SZ}
such that, once again,

QD_{SZ}=\omega-\sum_{x\in X^{Q}}(\operatorname{Loc}_{x}\omega)\cdot\delta_{x}.

Interestingly, the distribution one obtains by interpreting their result in this language is different from ours. For example in the lowest-dimensional nontrivial example, which is
(2,2)
-dimensional case, our “antiderivative” distribution
D
has singularities of the form
\frac{1}{x+iy}
where
x,y
are the even coordinates, while
D_{SZ}
would have regularized singularities of the form
\frac{1}{x^{2}+y^{2}}.

1.5
Splitting subgroups and CS volumes of homogeneous superspaces

In the last part of the paper we consider the case when
X=G/K
where
G
is a quasireductive complex algebraic supergroup and
K
is a connected
quasireductive subsupergroup.
In this case
X
is a smooth affine algebraic supervariety and also analytic complex supermanifold. In many cases the underlying manifold
\underline{X}

has a compact
real form
\underline{X}_{\mathbb{R}}
and then
X
has a canonical structure of CS manifold, see
[
6
]
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{k}
denote the Lie superalgebras of
G
and
K
respectively, and
p\in X
be the point corresponding to the coset
eK
.
Then
T_{p}X
can be identified with
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{k}
as a
K
-module. Let us assume that
K\subset SL(\mathfrak{p})
. This happens
for example if
\mathfrak{p}
is a self-dual
K
-module or if
\mathfrak{k}=[\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{k}]
. Then we can fix a
K
-invariant volume form on

T_{p}X
and using left translations extend it to a
G
-invariant volume form
\omega
on
X
. In this case the CS integral

I(f)=\int_{X}f\omega
defines a
G
-equivariant map
\mathbb{C}[X]\to\mathbb{C}
. If the CS volume
I(1)
is not zero then
K
is a splitting subgroup
of
G
.

We consider several examples of
X=G/K
such that
\underline{X}_{\mathbb{R}}
is a (partial) flag manifold for
\underline{G}
and
T_{p}X
is a self-dual
K
-module.
To compute the CS volume of
X
we choose an odd
element
u\in\mathfrak{k}_{1}
such that
[u,u]\in\operatorname{Lie}\underline{K}
and consider the corresponding odd vector field
Q=L_{u}
on
X
. If
u
is generic
we can show that
X^{Q}
is finite and
\operatorname{Loc}_{x}\omega
is the same at all points
x\in X^{Q}
. Now using our main Theorem
1
we obtain that
CS volume of
X
is not zero and hence
K
is splitting.

If
\mathfrak{g}
is a finite-dimensional Kac-Moody superalgebra one can choose
K
such that the defect subgroup
D
is a splitting subgroup in
K
.
By transitivity of the splitting property we obtain that
D
is splitting in
G
.

1.7
Structure of the paper

We first prove the localization theorem for a
linear

{G_{Q}}
-equivariant CS manifold
X
, i.e.,
X=V_{CS}
for
V
an oriented CS linear space with
{G_{Q}}
-action. In this case, as fixed points form a linear subspace, the condition of isolated fixed points implies that
X^{Q}
is supported at the point
\mathbf{0}\in V_{CS},
and the condition of nondegeneracy is equivalent to the condition that the lie algebra element
Q\in\mathfrak{g}
acts invertibly on
V
. In section
2
, we classify such representations of
{G_{Q}}
, which we call
nondegenerate
. We also write down the linear “localization functional”
\operatorname{Loc}:\operatorname{sDet}(V^{*})\to\mathbb{C}
(which depends only on the
\mathfrak{g}
-action and the orientation), and using this functional we write down a precise formula for the local contributions in the localization theorem associated to fixed points on a more general manifold. This allows us to write down a precise localization theorem, Theorem
7
. Note that throughout this section we study (for convenience of notation) the linear
{G_{Q}}
-representation
W
consisting of
linear functions
on our linear space
X
, so that
X
is the CS space underlying the dual vector space
W^{*}
and not
W
itself.

Then, in section
3.3
, we prove the precise theorem, Theorem
7
, for
X
a linear
{G_{Q}}
-equivariant CS space. Since the
{G_{Q}}
-action on
X
has a single fixed point, the localization theorem has only one local contribution and states that for any
{G_{Q}}
-equivariant form
\omega,
we have

\int_{X}\omega=\operatorname{Loc}_{W}(\omega\mid_{\mathbf{0}}),

where we view
\omega\mid_{\mathbf{0}}
as an element of
\operatorname{Ber}_{X}\mid_{\mathbb{0}}=\operatorname{sDet}(T^{*}_{X}\mid_{\mathbf{0}})=\operatorname{sDet}(V^{*}),
and
\operatorname{Loc}_{W}:\operatorname{sDet}(V^{*})\to\mathbb{C}
is the localization functional defined in section
2
.

The proof proceeds by induction on dimension, and using the theory of distributions and generalized functions on CS manifolds. We develop this theory in Section
3.1
. This theory is almost fully analogous to the theory in the even case, with one key difference: namely, that the delta distribution at the origin on a purely odd space
\mathbb{R}^{0,n}
(always viewed as a CS space) is actually a smooth distribution, given by the Berezinian,

\delta_{0}=\theta_{1}\cdots\theta_{n}\{\mid d\theta_{1}\ldots\theta_{n}\}.

Via this formalism we show (by induction on dimension) that for any
{G_{Q}}
-equivariant distribution
\omega
on
V_{CS},
the difference of distributions

\omega-\operatorname{Loc}_{W}(\omega\mid_{\mathbf{0}})\cdot\delta_{\mathbf{0}}

is a total derivative on
X
of another distribution, and hence has integral zero. Since
\int_{X}\delta_{\mathbf{0}}=1,
this implies the localization theorem in this case.

Finally, in section
4
we finish the proof of the localization theorem by reducing the localization result for general CS manifold
X
to the case of a linear
{G_{Q}}
-representation. This consists of two steps. First, in
4.1
, we prove the
acyclicity lemma
, a result of independent interest, which states that if
X
is a manifold with
{G_{Q}}
-action and
X^{Q}
is empty (i.e., the field
Q
is everywhere nondegenerate), then the result holds. Second, we show that in the neighborhood of any isolated nondegenerate fixed point
x\in X^{Q},
there is an isomorphism of
{G_{Q}}
-equivariant spaces between a neighborhood of
X
and a nondegenerate CS
{G_{Q}}
-representation taking
x
to
\mathbf{0}.
Using these arguments, we can decompose any
X
as a union of
{G_{Q}}
-equivariant opens which are equivariantly isomorphic to linear representations (on which the localization theorem follows from section
3.3
) and opens on which
{G_{Q}}
acts without fixed points (on which the localization theorem follows from the acyclicity lemma). We interpolate between these two using a bump function, and deduce the precise localization result,
7
.

In section
5
, we apply this theorem to suitable odd vector fields on homogeneous superspaces to deduce splitting results for super-Lie groups.

2
Linear algebra

Suppose
{G_{Q}}
is a
Q
-group and
V
is a representation of
{G_{Q}}
, always assumed finite-dimensional in this section. We say that
V
is
nondegenerate
if
Q
acts invertibly.

Since
Q
is odd, any
m\mid n
-dimensional nondegenerate
{G_{Q}}
-representation
V
must have equal even and odd dimensions,

n=m.

In particular, the minimal dimension for a nonzero nondegenerate
{G_{Q}}
-representation is
(1,1).

For any character
\chi
of
T,
we define a
(1,1)
-dimensional representation
V_{\chi}=\langle z,\theta\rangle
with scalar
T
-action given by
\chi
and

Qz=\theta,\qquad Q\theta=\chi(Q^{2})z.

It is clear that any
(1,1)
-dimensional
{G_{Q}}
-representation is isomorphic to some
V_{\chi}
. Moreover, nondegenerate
{G_{Q}}
-representations are completely reducible into a direct sum of copies of
V_{\chi}.

Lemma 3

(structure theorem for nondegenerate
{G_{Q}}
-representations)
.

Any nondegenerate
{G_{Q}}
-representation is isomorphic to

\bigoplus_{i}V_{\chi_{i}}

for some finite collection of characters
\chi_{i}.

Proof.

Choose a
T
-eigenbasis
z_{1},z_{1},\dots,z_{n}
of
V_{0}
with eigencharacters
\chi_{1},\dots,\chi_{n}.
Write
\theta_{i}=Q(z_{i}).
Since
Q
is an isomorphism,
\theta_{i}
are a basis of
V_{1}.
Then
\phi_{i}:z\mapsto z_{i},\theta\mapsto\theta_{i}
gives a map
V_{\chi_{i}}\to V
and
V=\sum_{i}\phi_{i}(V_{\chi_{i}}).
∎

Recall that a
CS structure
on a
{G_{Q}}
-representation
V
is a real structure

V_{0,\mathbb{R}}\subset V_{0}

which is closed under the action of
T={G_{Q}}^{red}.
Note that while
Q^{2}\in\text{Lie}_{T},
it is an element of the complexified Lie algebra and need not preserve
V_{0,\mathbb{R}}.
Evidently, if
X
is a
G
-manifold and
x\in X^{Q},
then
T_{x}X
(as well as its dual) has a structure of a CS
{G_{Q}}
-representation. Conversely, if
V
is a CS
{G_{Q}}
-representation, we can view
V
as a CS
{G_{Q}}
-manifold with underlying topological space
V_{0,\mathbb{R}}.

Given a
{G_{Q}}
-representation
V
with CS structure, recall that an
orientation
on
V
is an orientation on
V_{0,\mathbb{R}}
. If
X
is an oriented CS manifold with
{G_{Q}}
action and
x
is a
{G_{Q}}
-fixed point, then
T_{x}X
inherits the structure of an
oriented
CS
{G_{Q}}
-representation.

We define
W_{\chi}^{or}
to be the oriented CS
{G_{Q}}
-representation with underlying CS space
W_{\chi}
and the unique orientation such that the map
z:(W_{\chi})_{0,\mathbb{R}}\to\mathbb{C}
is orientation-preserving (where we view
\mathbb{C}=\mathbb{R}\oplus i\mathbb{R}
as a real space with standard orientation). When clear from context, we write
W_{\chi}
for the oriented space
W_{\chi}^{or}.

Note that the dual to an oriented CS
{G_{Q}}
-representation is again an oriented CS
{G_{Q}}
-representation.

Suppose
V
is a nondegenerate CS
{G_{Q}}
-representation and
v\in V_{0}
is a (complex) eigenvector for the
T
-action with eigenvalue
\chi.
Then
Q^{2}v=\chi(Q^{2})v,
where we view
Q^{2}\in\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{C}}
as a Lie algebra element and
\chi\in\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{C}}^{\vee}
as a vector in the dual lattice. Since
Q
is invertible, we must have
\chi\neq 0.
Now
\overline{v}
is an eigenvector with eigenvalue
\overline{\chi}=-\chi
(since the
T
-action is compatible with real structure), so
v,\overline{v}
are linearly independent, and thus a nontrivial CS
{G_{Q}}
-vector space must be at least
(2,2)
-dimensional. Write

W_{\chi}:=V_{\chi}\oplus V_{-\chi}.

Choose a basis vector
z
of
V_{\chi,0}\subset W
and
\overline{z}
of
V_{-\chi,0}\subset W.
Then the complex conjugation
z\mapsto\overline{z}
on
W_{\chi,0}
turns
W_{\chi}
into a CS
{G_{Q}}
-representation.

Lemma 4
.

1.

Every nondegenerate CS
{G_{Q}}
-representation
W
is a direct sum of copies of
W_{\chi},
and two such sums are isomorphic if and only if they differ only by replacing copies of
W_{\chi}
by
W_{-\chi}.

2.

Every oriented nondegenerate CS
{G_{Q}}
-representation
W
is a direct sum of copies of
W_{\chi}^{or},
and two such sums are isomorphic if and only if they differ only by an even number of replacements of
W_{\chi}^{or}
by
W_{-\chi}^{or}.

Proof.

Choose a partition
\Lambda_{T}\setminus\{0\}=\Lambda_{+}\sqcup-\Lambda_{+}
of the character lattice of
T
into positive and negative characters. Let
W_{0,+}
be the eigenspace of
W
corresponding to
\Lambda_{+},
and let
z_{1},\dots,z_{n/2}
be a basis. Then (since
\overline{\chi}=-\chi
for any nonzero character), the elements
z_{1},\dots,z_{n/2},\overline{z}_{1},\dots,\overline{z}_{n/2}
form a basis of
W
. The subspace spanned by
z_{i},\overline{z}_{i},Q(z_{i}),Q(\overline{z}_{i})
is a subrepresentation (since
Q^{2}
acts by scalars in a single eigenspace) isomorphic to
W_{\chi_{i}},
and
W
is a sum of such. Any other sum decomposition must be isomorphic as a
T
-representation, hence must have the same characters
\chi_{i}
up to sign. And if we are in the oriented context, switching a
W_{\chi}^{or}
to a
W_{-\chi}^{or}
flips the orientation, hence there must be an even number of sign changes between the two sets of characters.
∎

In the remainder of the section, we will write down a certain linear-algebraic invariant of an oriented CS
G
-representation
W
which will be responsible for the local contribution in the localization formula for
W=T^{*}_{x,X}
for
x\in X
a
Q
-fixed point. This linear contribution depends on a notion of “Pfaffian,” a scalar invariant of an oriented CS
{G_{Q}}
-representation, which is a square root of
Q^{2}\mid_{W_{0}}
(up to some factors of
i
). We begin by writing down down this scalar invariant.

Definition 3
.

Suppose
W
is an oriented nondegenerate CS
{G_{Q}}
-representation, such that

W\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}W_{\chi_{i}}.

Write

\mathrm{Pf}(W):=\prod_{i}\chi_{i}(Q^{2}).

Note that by part
2
of Lemma
4
, any oriented nondegenerate CS representation
W
has such a decomposition and two different decompositions differ by an even number of signs, thus
\mathrm{Pf}(W)
is well defined. Note also that because of nondegeneracy,
\chi(Q^{2})\neq 0
for any torus character
\chi
appearing in
W
, so
\mathrm{Pf}(W)\neq 0.

The reason we call this expression the
Pfaffian
is the following.

Proposition 5
.

Assume
Q^{2}\mid{W_{0,\mathbb{R}}}
is real, and we specify a choice of metric on
W_{0,\mathbb{R}}
compatible with the
T
-action, such that
Q^{2}\mid_{W_{0,\mathbb{R}}}
is skew-symmetric. Then
\mathrm{Pf}(W)
is isomorphic to the ordinary Pfaffian of the matrix
Q^{2}\mid_{W_{0,\mathbb{R}}}.

Proof.

Since both functionals are multiplicative under direct sum and
W
is a direct sum of copies of
W_{\chi}
, it suffices to check that this fact holds for
W_{\chi},
where it is obvious.
∎

Now recall that the local contribution in the integral formula is a linear functional on the fibre of the Berezinian bundle
\operatorname{sDet}(T^{*}_{x,X})
for
X
a fixed point. So, for
W=T^{*}_{x,X}
a nondegenerate oriented
{G_{Q}}
-representation with CS structure, we must produce a class
\operatorname{Loc}_{W}\in\operatorname{sDet}(W)^{-1}=\det(W_{0})^{-1}\otimes\det(W_{1})=\operatorname{Hom}(\det(W_{0}),\det(W_{1})).
The determinants of the two isomorphisms
\alpha:=Q\mid_{W_{0}}:W_{0}\to W_{1}
and
\beta=(Q\mid_{W_{1}})^{-1}:W_{0}\to W_{1}
give two different candidates for such a class, which differ by the factor
\det(\beta^{-1}\alpha)=\det(Q^{2}\mid_{W_{0}}).
The local contribution
\operatorname{Loc}_{W}
turns out to be (up to a fixed factor) a geometric mean between these two natural classes. To write down such a geometric mean, we need a root of the determinant, which is provided by the Pfaffian. We are now ready to define the localization form.

Definition 4
.

We define the localization functional of
W
to be

\displaystyle\operatorname{Loc}_{W}:=(2\pi)^{n/2}\mathrm{Pf}(W)^{-1}\det(Q\mid_{W_{0}})\in\det(W_{0})^{-1}\det(W_{1})=\operatorname{sDet}(W^{*}).

(1)

Given an element
\omega\in\operatorname{sDet}(W),
we write
\operatorname{Loc}_{W}(\omega)
for the pairing between
\operatorname{Loc}_{W}
and
\omega
. In the localization formula,
W=T^{*}_{X,x}
and
\omega
is the fiber of a volume form.

We record here the following obvious proposition. Note that if

0\to W\to W^{\prime}\to W^{\prime\prime}\to 0

is a short exact sequence of super-vector spaces, then we have canonically
\operatorname{sDet}(W)=\operatorname{sDet}(W^{\prime})\otimes\operatorname{sDet}(W^{\prime\prime}).
Via this identification, we recurd the following obvious proposition.

Proposition 6
.

If
0\to W\to W^{\prime}\to W^{\prime\prime}\to 0
is a short exact sequence of nondegenerate and oriented CS
{G_{Q}}
-representations, then
\operatorname{Loc}(W)=\operatorname{Loc}(W^{\prime})\operatorname{Loc}(W^{\prime\prime}).

Proof.

Note that by our complete reducibility result, we can choose a splitting
W\cong W^{\prime}\oplus W^{\prime\prime}.
Both the Pfaffian and
\det(Q\mid_{W_{0}})
are then obviously multiplicative under direct sum.
∎

Remark 2
.

While our notion of a
Q
-group
{G_{Q}}
has a fixed choice of
Q\in\mathfrak{g},
the localization formula only depends on a
{G_{Q}}
-invariant volume form. As such it should only depend on
Q
up to a scalar. And indeed, note that if we replace
Q
by
Q^{\prime}=cQ
for
c\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
a nonzero scalar, we get
\mathrm{Pf}_{Q^{\prime}}(W)=(2\pi)^{n/2}\mathrm{Pf}(W^{\prime})^{-1}\det(Q^{\prime}\mid_{W_{1}})\in\det(W_{0})\det(W_{1})^{-1}\in\operatorname{sDet}(W).
Now
\mathrm{Pf}(W^{\prime})
is a Pfaffian invariant of
(Q^{\prime})^{2}=c^{2}Q^{2},
so
\mathrm{Pf}(W^{\prime})=c^{2n/2}\mathrm{Pf}(W)=c^{n}\mathrm{Pf}(W).
We also have
\det(Q^{\prime})_{\mid W_{1}}=c^{n}\det(Q)_{\mid W_{1}},
and these two factors cancel.

We are now ready to give a precise statement of the localization theorem.

Theorem 7
.

[Precise form of Theorem
1
]
Suppose
{G_{Q}}
is a
Q
-group,
X
is a CS
\mathcal{G}/q
-manifold that satisfies the conditions of Theorem
1
(i.e., such that the action has isolated and nondegenerate fixed points) and
\omega
is a
{G_{Q}}
-equivariant form. Then

\int\omega=\sum_{x\in X^{Q}}\operatorname{Loc}_{T_{x}^{*}X}\omega\mid_{\{x\}}.

Here
T_{x}^{*}X
has CS
{G_{Q}}
-representation structure induced from the action of
{G_{Q}}
on
X
and this structure is nondegenerate because of the nondegeneracy, hence we are in a situation where
\operatorname{Loc}_{W}
is well-defined.

2.1
(2,2)
-dimensional case, in coordinates

Suppose
{G_{Q}}
is a
Q
-group,
\chi
is a
T
-character and
W=W_{\chi}
is a two-dimensional CS
{G_{Q}}
-representation. Let
z
be a torus
\chi
-eigenvector and
\overline{z}
its conjugate. We can assume WLOG (up to switching
z
and
\overline{z}
) that
z
gives an oriented map
W_{\chi,0}\to\mathbb{C}.
Then the
\mathfrak{t}
-action is given by
t(z)=i\chi(t)z,t(\overline{z})=-i\chi(t)
for
t\in\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{R}}.
Write
\lambda:=\chi(Q^{2}),
which is nonzero by nondegeneracy but not necessarily real. Write
\theta=Qz,\overline{\theta}=Q\overline{z}.
Note that there is no sense in which
\theta
and
\overline{\theta}
are conjugate since the space of odd linear functions
W_{1}^{*}
has no distinguished real structure (rather, we use the bar notation for consistency with
z,\overline{z}
). However, if we had chosen a real structure on
W
for which
Q\in\mathfrak{g}
is real, then
\theta
and
\overline{\theta}
would be conjugate.

For the reader’s convenience, we record the full
\mathfrak{g}
-action in this basis.

\displaystyle t(z)=i\chi(t)z,\quad t(\theta)=i\chi(t)\theta,\quad t(\overline{z})=-i\chi(t)z,t(\overline{\theta})=-i\chi(t)\theta

(2)

\displaystyle Q(z)=\theta\quad Q(\overline{z})=\overline{\theta}

(3)

\displaystyle Q(\theta)=i\lambda z\quad Q(\overline{\theta})=-i\lambda\overline{z}.

(4)

Note that in this basis,

Q^{2}=\begin{pmatrix}i\lambda&0\\
0&-i\lambda\end{pmatrix}.

We compute the Pfaffian, using
W_{0,+}=\langle z\rangle
.
The orientation assumption on
z
then gives

\mathrm{Pf}_{W}=\det\left(\frac{Q^{2}\mid_{W_{0,+}}}{i}\right)=\lambda.

The Berezinian term in the integral formula is
\det(Q\mid_{W_{0}})\in\operatorname{sDet}(W)^{-1}=\Lambda^{2}W_{1}\Lambda^{2}W_{0}^{*}.
Recall that the determinant of an isomorphism
M:V\to W
of even spaces is given by the wedge product of a basis
x_{i}
of
V
divided by the wedge product of
M(x_{i}),
giving
\det(Q\mid_{W_{0}})=\frac{\theta\wedge\overline{\theta}}{z\wedge\overline{z}}.
Finally, the integral form is

\operatorname{Loc}_{W}=2\pi\frac{\theta\wedge\overline{\theta}}{\lambda z\wedge\overline{z}}.

Remark 3

(Real basis in real case)
.

Note that we can always choose a real basis of
W_{0}
given by
x=\frac{z+\overline{z}}{2},y=\frac{z-\overline{z}}{2i},
though it is less convenient for calculations.
If all of
W
has a distinguished real structure compatible with
{G_{Q}}
-action, and
Q
is real, then we can also choose the real basis on
W_{1}
given by
\theta_{x}:=\frac{\theta+\overline{\theta}}{2},\theta_{y}:=\frac{\theta-\overline{\theta}}{2i}.
In this basis, we have

Q^{2}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\lambda\\
-\lambda&0\end{pmatrix}.

The localization form in this basis is

\operatorname{Loc}_{W}=2\pi\frac{\theta_{x}\wedge\theta_{y}}{\lambda x\wedge y}.

Note that if
W=V\oplus V^{*}
then
W
has a canonical volume form
\omega
which pairs
\operatorname{sDet}V
and
\operatorname{sDet}V^{*}
. If
u_{1},\dots,u_{n}
is basis
in
V_{0}
and
v_{1},\dots,v_{n}
is a basis in
V_{1}
, and
u_{1}^{\vee},\dots,u_{n}^{\vee},v^{\vee}_{1},\dots,v^{\vee}_{n}
is the dual basis in
V^{*}

then

\omega=\frac{u_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge u_{n}\wedge u_{1}^{\vee}\wedge\dots\wedge u_{n}^{\vee}}{v_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge v_{n}\wedge v^{\vee}_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge v^{\vee}_{n}}.

The volume form
\omega
does not depend on the choice of a basis.

Lemma 8
.

Let
W=V\oplus V^{*}
and
\dim V=(n|n)
. Assume that one can choose an
u^{2}
eigenbasis
z_{1},\dots,z_{n}
in
V_{0}
such that
\overline{z}_{i}=z_{i}^{\vee}
.
Then

\operatorname{Loc}_{W}\omega=\left(\frac{2\pi}{\mathbf{i}}\right)^{n}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{i}\lambda_{1},\dots,\mathbf{i}\lambda_{n}
be the corresponding eigenvalues.
Set
\theta_{i}=Qz_{i}
. Then

\langle Q\overline{z}_{i},\theta_{j}\rangle=-\langle\overline{z}_{i},Q\theta_{j}\rangle=\langle z_{i},\mathbf{i}\lambda_{j}z_{j}\rangle=\mathbf{i}\lambda_{i}\delta_{i,j}.

Therefore
\overline{\theta}_{i}=\mathbf{i}\lambda_{i}\theta_{i}^{\vee}
. By Proposition
6
we have

\operatorname{Loc}_{W}=\frac{(2\pi)^{n}}{\prod\lambda_{j}}\frac{\theta_{1}\wedge\dots\theta_{n}\wedge\overline{\theta}_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\overline{\theta}_{n}}{z_{1}\wedge\dots z_{n}\wedge\overline{z}_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\overline{z}_{n}}=\left(\frac{2\pi}{\mathbf{i}}\right)^{n}\frac{\theta_{1}\wedge\dots\theta_{n}\wedge\theta_{1}^{\vee}\wedge\dots\wedge\theta_{n}^{\vee}}{z_{1}\wedge\dots z_{n}\wedge z^{\vee}_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge z^{\vee}_{n}}.

This implies the statement.
∎

Remark 4
.

The assumption of Lemma
8
holds if
V=\bigoplus V_{\chi_{j}}
if
\dim V_{\chi_{j}}=(1|1)
for every

and
\chi_{i}\neq\chi_{j}
for

.

3
Calculation on linear space.

In this section we prove the main theorem for a linear local model.

Theorem 9
.

Suppose
{G_{Q}}
is a
Q
-group and
W
is an oriented
(n,n)
-dimensional nondegenerate CS
{G_{Q}}
-representation. Then Theorem
7
holds for the linear manifold
X=W^{*}.

In the remainder of this section we prove this local theorem. For
v\in W_{0},
we write
\mathbf{v}
for
v
considered as a point of the linear space
X
(with underlying manifold
\underline{X}=W_{0,\mathbb{R}}
). In this case, there is a unique
Q
-fixed point
\mathbf{0},
which obviously satisfies the conditions of Theorem
1
. We prove the theorem by induction on
n
. Recall that the CS and nondegeneracy conditions imply that
n
must be even. So suppose Theorem
7
holds for
(n-2,n-2)
-dimensional manifolds. We argue using the theory of distributions.

3.1
Distributions and generalized functions

Recall that a function on a super-ringed space
X=(\underline{X},C^{\infty}_{X})
is
compactly supported
in
\underline{U}\subset\underline{X}
if the restriction of
f
is zero on the complement of a compact subset of
\underline{U}
, and write
C^{\infty}_{c}(\underline{U})
for the space of compactly supported functions.

Definition 5
.

A
distribution

\xi
on
U
is a functional on compactly supported functions
\xi:f\mapsto\langle\xi,f\rangle
i.e., an element of
C^{\infty}_{c}(\underline{U})^{\vee}.

When
X
admits partitions of unity, distributions on
X
form a sheaf; it is
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded and pre-composition with multiplication by functions makes it into a
C^{\infty}_{X}
-module. We will also need a dual notion, of
generalized functions
.

Definition 6
.

For
X
a supermanifold, we define the sheaf of
generalized functions

C^{-\infty}_{X}:=\text{Distr}_{X}\otimes_{C^{\infty}_{X}}\operatorname{Ber}_{X}^{-1}

(equivalently, this is the dual sheaf to compactly supported Berezinian sections).

Any Berezinian can be viewed as a distribution (
\omega
goes to the functional
f\mapsto\int f\omega
), giving a map of
C^{\infty}
-module sheaves
\operatorname{Ber}_{X}\to\text{Distr}_{X},
thus also
C^{\infty}_{X}\to C^{-\infty}_{X}.
This is the super-analogue of the inclusion of smooth functions into generalized functions (indeed, in the super case it is also true that
C^{-\infty}
is a completion of
C^{\infty}
in a suitable topology). Note that for the purely odd linear CS vector space
Y=\Pi W_{1},
all functions are compactly supported so we have
\text{Distr}_{Y}=C^{\infty}(Y).
The integral pairing
\operatorname{Ber}_{Y}\otimes\mathbb{O}_{Y}\to\mathbb{C}
is a perfect pairing of finite-dimensional vector spaces, so we have canonical isomorphisms
\text{Distr}_{Y}=\operatorname{Ber}_{Y},C^{-\infty}_{Y}=C^{\infty}_{Y}.
Applying this argument fiberwise on the family
X\to X^{red}
(with fibers
W_{1}
), we see that we have canonical isomorphisms of sheaves of vector spaces over
\underline{X}=V_{0,\mathbb{R}},
and
C^{-\infty}_{W}\cong C^{-\infty}_{W_{0}}\otimes C^{\infty}\Pi V_{1}.

Note that since differentiation preserves the class of compactly supported functions, any derivation
Q
on
X
induces a derivation on
Distr
by
\langle Q\xi,f\rangle:=(-1)^{|Q||f|}\langle\xi,Qf\rangle.
This is a right action of the Lie algebra,
TX
, or equivalently, an action of
TX^{op}
. We get a (left) action of
TX
on
C^{-\infty}
in a similar way.

Note that if a distribution
\xi
is compactly supported, we can choose a compactly supported bump function
\tau
equal to
1
on the support of
\xi,
and define

\langle\xi,f\rangle:=\langle\xi,\tau f\rangle

for
any
, not necessarily compactly supported function
f
. This is obviously independent of the bump function
\tau,
and we obtain a canonical pairing between compactly supported distributions and
all
functions. In particular we can define the
integral
of a compactly supported distribution
\xi
on
X
by

\int_{X}\xi:=\langle\xi,1\rangle.

We then have the familiar identity that a compaclty supported distribution which is a “total derivative” of another distribution has zero integral. In other words, if
\omega
is a compactly supported distribution and
Q
is a vector field (even or odd), then we have

\int_{X}Q(\xi)=\pm\langle\xi,Q(1)\rangle=0.

We will use the fact that (as in even geometry), generalized functions pull back along smooth maps. Namely, if
\pi:X\to Y
is a smooth submersion of CS supermanifolds and
f
is a generalized function on
Y,
then we define a generalized function
\pi^{*}f
on
X
as follows. Recall that for any compactly supported volume form
\omega
on
X
, we can integrate it along the fibers of
\pi
to produce a new volume form
\pi_{!}\omega\in\Gamma(\operatorname{Ber}_{Y}).
We then define
\pi^{*}f
to be the form characterized by
\int_{X}\pi^{*}f\cdot\omega:=\int_{Y}f\cdot\pi_{!}\omega.

The pullback map is compatible with differentiation, in the sense that if
Q
is a differential on
Y
and
\widetilde{Q}
is a lift of
Q
to
X
then
\widetilde{Q}\pi^{*}f=\pi^{*}(Qf)
.

3.2
\delta
distributions and
\delta
functions

Let
V
be any CS vector space and let
X=V_{CS}
be the corresponding linear CS manifold. We have a canonically defined delta distribution
\delta_{\mathbf{0}}
defined by the formula

\langle\delta_{\mathbf{0}},f\rangle=f(0).

If
\omega\in\operatorname{sDet}(V^{*})
is a constant Berezinian form, we have a generalized delta function
\delta_{\mathbf{0}}/\omega
(unlike the delta distribution, the delta function is only canonical up to scalar). If
V=\mathbb{R}^{0,n}
is purely odd, then
C^{-\infty}(V_{CS})=C^{\infty}(V_{CS}),
so the delta function is an ordinary function. Choosing coordinates functions
\theta_{i},
we have the following important (and obvious) identity:

\displaystyle\frac{\delta_{\mathbf{0}}}{\{\mid d\theta_{1}\cdots d\theta_{n}\}}=\theta_{1}\cdots\theta_{n}.

(5)

In other words, when working with delta functions we can “move factors of
\{:\theta_{i}\}
in the denominator to factors of
\theta_{i}
in the numerator”. More generally, suppose
V=V^{\prime}\oplus V^{\prime\prime}
is a direct sum decomposition. Write
X=V_{CS}^{*},B={V^{\prime}}_{CS}^{*},Y={V^{\prime\prime}}_{CS}^{*}.
Let
i:Y\to X
be the inclusion and
\pi:X\to F
be the quotient (so
Y\to X\to B
is a fibration sequence). Note that elements of
\operatorname{sDet}(V),\operatorname{sDet}(V^{\prime}),\operatorname{sDet}(V^{\prime\prime})
can be viewed as constant Berezinians on the corresponding spaces. We have a canonical isomorphism
\operatorname{sDet}(V)=\operatorname{sDet}(V^{\prime})\otimes\operatorname{sDet}(V^{\prime\prime})
. Now, given any Berezinian section
\omega^{\prime\prime}\in\operatorname{Ber}(Y),
we can define a distribution

\delta_{Y}\cdot\omega^{\prime\prime}\in\text{Distr}_{X}

with
\langle\delta_{Y}\cdot\omega^{\prime\prime},f\rangle:=\int_{Y}(f\mid_{Y})\cdot\omega^{\prime\prime}.
We can view the symbol

\delta_{Y}\in\Gamma(Y,\operatorname{sDet}(N_{Y\subset X}^{*}))

formally as a “relative Berezinian” on
Y
, i.e., as a section of the determinant of the conormal bundle
N_{Y\subset X}^{*}
of
Y
in
X.
Via this point of view, given an element
\kappa\in\operatorname{sDet}(V^{\prime})^{-1},
we can define the generalized function
\kappa\cdot\delta_{Y\subset X}\in C^{-\infty}(X)
by
\langle\kappa,\omega\rangle:=\int_{Y}\omega\otimes\kappa
for any compactly supported Berezinian section
\omega\in\Gamma_{cs}(X,\operatorname{Ber})
where we view
\omega\otimes\kappa\in\Gamma_{cs}(\underline{Y},\operatorname{Ber}(Y))
via the natural isomorphism

\operatorname{Ber}(X)\mid_{Y}\otimes\operatorname{sDet}(V^{\prime})^{-1}\cong C^{\infty}(X)\otimes\operatorname{sDet}(V)\otimes\operatorname{sDet}(V^{\prime})^{-1}\cong\operatorname{Ber}(Y).

3.3
The interpolating distribution

4
Reduction to linear case

In this section we reduce the general case of Theorem

4.1
Proof of the acyclicity lemma

We give a reminder of the statement of the lemma.

Lemma

(
Q
-acyclicity, Lemma
2
)
.

Suppose
X
is a
{G_{Q}}
-equivariant CS manifold and
Q
is everywhere nonvanishing. Then there exists an odd function
\beta\in C^{\infty}(X)_{1}
such that
Q\beta=1.

Proof.

Our proof closely follows the proof in
[
5
]
(with the exception that we replace an argument involving a choice of metric by Lemma
11
below ). We first construct an odd function
\alpha
on
X
which satisfies the condition up to nilpotents,
(Q\alpha)^{red}=1.

Let
T^{red}_{1}
be the odd component of
T^{red},
i.e., the normal bundle to
X^{red}
in
X
.

Let
\Omega^{red}
be the restriction of the cotangent bundle
\Omega
to
X^{red}
and let
\Omega^{red}_{1}
be its odd part, which is canonically isomorphic to the conormal bundle
N_{X/X^{red}}^{*}
. Let
\rho:\Gamma(\underline{X},\Omega)_{1}\to\Gamma(\underline{X},\Omega^{red}_{1})
be the restriction map, and let

d^{red}_{1}:=\rho\circ d:\Gamma(\underline{X},C^{\infty}_{X})\to\Gamma(\underline{X},\Omega^{red}_{1})

be the composition, that takes an odd function to its differential in the normal directions.

Lemma 11
.

The restriction map
d^{red}_{1}:\Gamma(\underline{X},C^{\infty}_{X})_{1}\to\Gamma(\underline{X},\Omega^{red}_{1})
is surjective.

Proof.

This result is obvious for
X=\mathbb{R}^{m\mid n},
hence true locally. Let
\{\underline{U}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be a Čech cover of
\underline{X}
such that the lemma is true on each
\underline{U}_{i}
(any Čech refinement of a cover by
\mathbb{R}^{m\mid n}
will do). Let
\{\tau_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be a partition of unity with
\tau_{i}
supported on
U_{i}.
For
\phi\in\Gamma(\underline{X},\Omega^{red}_{1}),
let
f_{i}\in\Gamma(\underline{U}_{i},C^{\infty}_{X})
be odd functions with
d^{red}_{1}f_{i}=\phi\mid_{U_{i}}.
Then

f:=\sum\tau_{i}f_{i}

is a locally finite sum of compactly supported functions, hence defines a global odd function on
X
. Since
d^{red}_{1}
commutes with multiplication by even functions, we have
d^{red}f=\phi.

∎

Remark 5
.

This lemma implies that any CS manifold is (non-canonically) isomorphic to the total space of the bundle
T^{red}_{1}
on
X^{red},
a well-known result in the real case.

Now convolving with
Q^{red}\in T^{red}_{1}
gives a linear map of vector bundles
*_{Q}^{red}:\Omega^{red}_{1}\to C^{\infty}_{X^{red}}
on the even manifold
X^{red},
which is surjective since
Q
was assumed nowhere vanishing. Surjective maps of
C^{\infty}
vector bundles split, so we can choose a preimage
\phi
with
*_{Q}^{red}(\phi)=1.
Using Lemma
11
, we choose a lift
\alpha\in C^{\infty}(X)_{1}
with
d^{red}\alpha=\phi.
By construction, we then have

(Q\alpha)^{red}=Q^{red}*d^{red}\alpha=1.

Now write
[\alpha]
for the
T
-average of
\alpha.
Then
[\alpha]
is a
T
-equivariant function, and since
Q
is
T
-equivariant we have
Q[\alpha]^{red}=[1]=1.
In particular, we see that
Q[\alpha]
is invertible. Write

\beta:=\frac{[\alpha]}{Q[\alpha]}.

We compute:
Q\beta=\frac{Q[\alpha]}{Q[\alpha]}+\frac{Q^{2}[\alpha]}{\ldots}=1,
since
Q^{2}[\alpha]=0
by
T
-equivariance of the averaged function
[\alpha].

∎

4.2
Reduction to a neigbhorhood

We write down the following corollary, which implies that the localization theorem reduces to a neighborhood of the fixed points.

Corollary 12
.

Suppose
X
is a
{G_{Q}}
-manifold with
X^{Q}
supported at the dimension-zero submanifold
\underline{Y}\subset\underline{X},
and suppose that the localization theorem, Theorem
1
holds for
U=\underline{X},C^{\infty}_{X}\mid_{\underline{U}}
for
\underline{U}
some open
T
-equivariant neighborhood
\underline{U}\supset\underline{Y}
of
\underline{Y}
in
\underline{X}
(note that since
{G_{Q}}
is a nilpotent thickening of
T
and
U
is open,
{G_{Q}}
automatically acts on
U
). Then the localization theorem holds for
X
.

Proof.

Let
\tau
be a “bump function,” such that
\tau
has support inside
\underline{U}
and is equal to
1
in a smaller neighborhood
\underline{V}\supset\underline{U}
of
\underline{Y}
(it is standard that such exist). By taking the
T
-average, we can assume WLOG that
\tau
is
T
-equivariant (hence also
Q^{2}
-equivariant). Let
\underline{V}^{\prime}
be a
T
-equivariant neighborhood such that
\underline{V}\cup\underline{V}^{\prime}=\underline{X}
but
\underline{V}^{\prime}
does not intersect
\underline{Y}.
By the
Q
-acyclicity theorem, there is a function
\beta_{V^{\prime}}
on
V^{\prime}
with
Q\beta_{V^{\prime}}=1
on
V^{\prime}.
Write
\beta^{\prime}:=\beta_{V^{\prime}}\cdot\tau^{\prime}.
This function is zero on
\underline{V}\cap\underline{V}^{\prime},
hence can be extended (by zero) to a function on all of
X=\underline{V}\cup\underline{V}^{\prime},
which we also call
\beta^{\prime}.
Now outside
\underline{U},
we have
\tau=0
so
\tau^{\prime}=1,
and thus
\beta^{\prime}\mid_{\underline{X}\setminus\underline{U}}=\beta\mid_{\underline{X}\setminus\underline{U}}
. Now suppose
\omega
is a
{G_{Q}}
-equivariant Berezinian. Write
\omega_{\underline{U}}:=\omega-Q(\omega\beta^{\prime}).
Since they differ by a total derivative,

\int\omega_{\underline{U}}=\int\omega.

Now in the complement of
\underline{U},
we have
Q\beta^{\prime}=1,
so

\omega_{\underline{U}}\mid_{\underline{X}\setminus\underline{U}}=0.

On the other hand, inside
\underline{V},
we have
\tau^{\prime}=0,
so
\beta^{\prime}=0
and

\omega_{\underline{U}}\mid_{\underline{V}}=\omega\mid_{\underline{V}}.

Since
\underline{V}
contains the fixed point locus
\underline{Y}
, this implies that the local contributions

\sum_{x\in X^{Q}}\operatorname{Loc}_{T_{x}^{*}X}\omega\mid_{\{x\}}=\sum_{x\in X^{Q}}\operatorname{Loc}_{T_{x}^{*}X}\omega_{\underline{U}}\mid_{\{x\}}

of
\omega
and
\omega_{\underline{U}}
are equal. Now by construction,
\omega_{\underline{U}}
is supported on
\underline{U}
and
{G_{Q}}
-equivariant (this because
\omega-\omega_{\underline{U}}=Q(\omega\beta^{\prime})
is the
Q
-derivative of something
T
-equivariant, thus is both
T
-equivariant and
Q
-equivariant). Thus if the localization theorem holds for the manifold
U
, we must have

\sum_{x\in X^{Q}}\operatorname{Loc}_{T_{x}^{*}X}\omega\mid_{\{x\}}=\int\omega_{\underline{U}},

and we deduce the same result holds for
\omega.

∎

4.3
Local linearity

With Corollary
12
and Theorem
9
in hand, it remains only to prove that every
Q
-fixed point of
X
(the
{G_{Q}}
-manifold in Theorem
1
) has a neighborhood isomorphic as a CS
{G_{Q}}
-manifold to a neighborhood of
\mathbf{0}
in a linear CS
{G_{Q}}
-representation.

We give a brief reminder of the implicit function theorem for CS manifolds. Suppose
X
is a CS manifold and
\mathbf{x}
is a point. Let
W
be a CS super-vector space with
W^{*}_{CS}
the CS space associated to the dual vector space, with underlying manifold
W^{*}_{0,\mathbb{R}}
. Let
f:X\to W^{*}_{CS}
be a map of CS vector spaces such that
\mathbf{x}\mapsto 0
. Then pullbacks of linear functions on
W^{*}_{CS}
give a map

L=f^{*}\mid_{W\subset C^{*}(W^{*})}:W\to C^{\infty}X.

Note that
L
encodes all the information about the map. In particular, the map on underlying topological spaces is given by
x\mapsto L_{x}
for
L_{x}\in W^{*}
the functional
L_{x}:w\mapsto L(w)(x).
Since odd functions vanish at points,
L_{x}\in W_{0}^{*}
is even and furthermore
L_{x}\in W_{0,\mathbb{R}},
as
L_{x}
must correspond to a topological point of the underlying manifold
\underline{W}^{*}=\underline{W}_{0,\mathbb{R}}^{*}.
We show in the following proposition that, conversely, a linear function
W\to C^{\infty}X
that takes
W_{0,\mathbb{R}}
to functions with real values at all points uniquely determines a map
X\to W^{*}_{CS},
and that moreover, this correspondence satisfies an implicit function theorem.

Proposition 13
.

Suppose
L:W\to C^{\infty}(X)
is a linear map such that
L(w)(\mathbf{x})=0
vanishes on the marked point for all
w
and such that
\pi_{red}\circ L\mid_{W_{0}}:W_{0}\to C^{\infty}(X)^{red}
is compatible with real structure (for
\pi_{red}
the projection map to the reduced part).

1.

There is a unique map of CS manifolds
f:X\to W^{*}_{CS}
with
f^{*}(w)=L(w)
for any
w\in W
viewed as a linear function on
W^{*}_{CS}
.

2.

Let
d_{x}\circ L:W\to\Omega^{1}_{X,\mathbf{x}}
be the composition of
W
with the differential at
\mathbf{x}.
Then
f
is a local isomorphism near
\mathbf{x}
if and only if
d_{x}\circ L
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

Part
1
follows immediately from the interpretation of CS manifolds in terms of charts. Alternatively, let
L_{red,\mathbb{R}}:W_{0}\to C^{\infty}(X)_{red}
be the map on even, reduced functions. This is a map in the world of (real) differential geometry, and since it is compatible with real structures, it corresponds to a unique map of real manifolds
f^{red}:X^{red}\to W^{*}_{0,\mathbb{R}}
(since in the world of real manifolds,
C^{\infty}_{\mathbb{R}}
classifies smooth maps to
\mathbb{R}^{1}
). Now the proposition follows from the product identity
W^{*}_{CS}=W^{*}_{0,CS}\times\Pi(W^{*}_{1})_{CS}
in the category of CS manifolds.

For part
2
, let
f:X\to W^{*}_{CS}
be the map of CS manifolds corresponding to the linear map
L:W\to C^{\infty}(X)
. Let
df:T^{1}_{\mathbf{x}}X\to T^{1}_{\mathbf{0}}W^{*}_{CS}
be the induced map on tangent spaces. The map
df
is evidently adjoint to
d_{x}\circ L,
and so one is an isomorphism if and only if the other is. In particular if
f
is a local isomorphism at
\mathbf{x}
, then the induced map on tangent spaces
df\mid_{\mathbf{x}}
must be an isomorphism as well. It remains to show the converse. Suppose
df
is an isomorphism at
\mathbf{x}
. Then it must be an isomorphism in a neigborhood of
\mathbf{x},
so since we are working locally, we can assume WLOG that it is an isomorhpism on all
X
. Then ordinary differential geometry implies that
f^{red}:X^{red}\to W^{*}_{0,\mathbb{R}}
is a local isomorphism. The induced map on the normal bundles of
X^{red}\subset X
is now also an isomorphism. If we filter both the sheaves
C^{\infty}(X)
and
C^{\infty}(W^{*}_{CS})
by powers of the nilpotent ideal, the map of ringed spaces is compatible with these filtrations and induces an isomorphism of associated graded components (which are powers of the induced map on normal bundles). Thus
f
is a local isomorphism.
∎

Now suppose that
\mathbf{x}\in\underline{X}
is an isolated fixed point of a
{G_{Q}}
-action on
X
with nondegenerate action.

Lemma 14
.

There is a
{G_{Q}}
-equivariant map of CS manifolds

sending
\mathbf{x}
to

Proof.

Let

W:=T^{*}_{\mathbf{x},X}

with induced CS structure, and let

be the ideal of functions on

that vanish on
\mathbf{x}.
We have a map

(differentiating at

) which is
{G_{Q}}
-equivariant and surjective. Let

be its even component, which is surjective and equivariant with respect to the even part

. Furthermore,

factors through the reduced restriction

Choose an arbitrary

then average with respect to the compact group

to get a

and

Now by nondegeneracy, the action of

Write

∎

Proof.

.

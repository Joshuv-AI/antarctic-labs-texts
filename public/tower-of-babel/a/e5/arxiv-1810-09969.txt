Abstract




Abstract
: The aim of this paper is to introduce the notion of a Suzuki-Gerghaty type contractive mapping via simulation function along with
\mathcal{C}
-class functions and prove the existence of fixed point result. An example is given to show the validity of our results given herein. Moreover, we prove the existence of solution of nonlinear Hammerstein integral equation.

: 54H25, 47H10

\mathcal{C}

We begin with the following notion:

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space and
T:X\rightarrow X
be a mapping.

\alpha:X\times X\rightarrow[0,\infty)

\frac{1}{2}d(x,Tx)<d(x,y)~\text{implies}~\zeta(\alpha(x,y)d(Tx,Ty),\beta(M(x,y))M(x,y))\geq\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}},

(5)

By definition of

\frac{1}{2}d(x,Tx)<d(x,y)~\text{implies}~\alpha(x,y)d(Tx,Ty)<\beta(M(x,y))M(x,y)).

(6)

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
T:X\rightarrow X

\alpha
-admissible map. Assume that

\alpha(x_{1},Tx_{1})\geq 1

To prove the existence of fixed point result, we need the following lemmas:

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(X,d)
be a complete metric space,
\alpha:X\times X\rightarrow[0,\infty)

\alpha(y,z)\geq 1

Proof.

Suppose that
x,y\in Fix(T)

x\neq y

0=\frac{1}{2}d(x,Tx)<d(x,y),

then by (
5

\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}\leq\zeta(\alpha(x,y)d(Tx,Ty),\beta(M(x,y))M(x,y)),

where

\displaystyle M(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\{d(x,y),d(x,Tx),d(y,Ty)\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d(x,y).

This implies

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\zeta(\alpha(x,y)d(x,y),\beta(d(x,y))d(x,y))

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(\beta(d(x,y))d(x,y)),\alpha(x,y)d(Tx,Ty)),

by definition of
\mathcal{G}

\displaystyle\alpha(x,y)d(x,y)<\beta(d(x,y))d(x,y).

Theorem 2.1
.

(X,d)
be a complete metric space,
\alpha:X\times X\rightarrow[0,\infty)
and
T:X\rightarrow X
be two functions. Suppose that the
following conditions are satisfied:

(1)

T
is Suzuki type
\mathcal{Z}_{(\alpha,\mathcal{G})}
-Geraghty contraction ;

(2)

T
is triangular
\alpha
-admissible;

(3)

there exists
x_{1}\in X
such that
\alpha(x_{1},Tx_{1})\geq 1
.

Then
T
has a fixed point.

Proof.

From Lemma
2.5
,
\{x_{n}\}
is a Cauchy sequence and by the completeness of
X

x\in X
such that

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}x_{n}=x.

(13)

Now, we show that
x
is a fixed point of
T
. We claim that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}d(x_{n},Tx_{n})<d(x_{n},x)~~or~~\frac{1}{2}d(x_{n+1},Tx_{n+1})<d(x_{n+1},x),~~for~~all~~n\in\mathbb{N}.

That is,

\displaystyle(I):=~\frac{1}{2}d(x_{n},Tx_{n})<d(x_{n},x)~~\text{or}~~(II):=~\frac{1}{2}d(Tx_{n},T^{2}x_{n})<d(Tx_{n},x),~~for~~all~~n\in\mathbb{N}.

(14)

Assume that there exists
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}d(x_{m},Tx_{m})\geq d(x_{m},x)~~or~~\frac{1}{2}d(Tx_{m},T^{2}x_{m})\geq d(Tx_{m},x).

(15)

Hence,

2d(x_{m},x)\leq d(x_{m},Tx_{m})\leq d(x_{m},x)+d(x,Tx_{m})

\displaystyle d(x_{m},x)\leq d(x,Tx_{m}).

(16)

From (
9
)and (
16
), we have

\displaystyle d(Tx_{m},T^{2}x_{m})<d(x_{m},Tx_{m})\leq d(x_{m},x)+d(x,Tx_{m})<2d(x,Tx_{m}).

15
) and (

), that

, which is a contradiction. Thus (
14
) holds. If

14
) is true, by definition of
T
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\zeta(\alpha(x_{n},x)d(Tx_{n},Tx),\beta(M(x_{n},x))M(x_{n},x))

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(\beta(M(x_{n},x))M(x_{n},x),\alpha(x_{n},x)d(Tx_{n},Tx)).

\mathcal{G}
, we have

\displaystyle\alpha(x_{n},x)d(Tx_{n},Tx)<\beta(M(x_{n},x))M(x_{n},x),

by Lemma
2.1
and the fact that
\beta\in\mathcal{F}
, we have

\displaystyle d(Tx_{n},Tx)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\alpha(x_{n},x)d(Tx_{n},Tx)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\beta(M(x_{n},x))M(x_{n},x)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle M(x_{n},x).

\displaystyle d(Tx_{n},Tx)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle M(x_{n},x)=\max\{d(x_{n},x),d(x_{n},Tx_{n}),d(x,Tx)\}.

13

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}M(x_{n},x)=d(x,Tx).

(18)

Now,

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\zeta(\alpha(x_{n},x)d(Tx_{n},Tx),\beta(M(x_{n},x))M(x_{n},x))

Now,

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\zeta(\alpha(Tx_{n},x)d(T^{2}x_{n},Tx),\beta(M(Tx_{n},x))M(Tx_{n},x))

3
Application

In this section, we present an application of Theorem
2.1
to guarantee the
existence and uniqueness of solution of the following non linear
Hammerstein integral equation:

x(t)=f(t)+\int_{0}^{t}K(t,s)h(s,x(s))ds,

(20)

where the unknown function
x(t)
takes real values. Let
X=C([0,1])
be
the space of all real continuous functions defined on
[0,1]
. It is well
known that
C[0,1]
endowed with the metric

d(x,y)=\|x-y\|=\max_{t\in[0,1]}|x(t)-y(t)|.

(21)

So,
(X,d)
is a complete metric space. Define a mapping
T:X\rightarrow X

by

T(x)(t)=f(t)+\int_{0}^{t}K(t,s)h(s,x(s))ds,

(22)

for all
t\in[0,1]
.

Suppose that the following conditions are satisfied:

(1)

f\in C([0,1])\times(-\infty,+\infty),f\in X
and
K\in C([0,1]\times[0,1])
such that
K(t,s)\geq 0
;

(2)

h(t,s):(-\infty,+\infty))\rightarrow(-\infty,+\infty))
is increasing for all
t\in[0,1]
, such that

\frac{1}{2}d(x,F(x))<d(x,y)

implies
|h(t,x)-h(t,y)|<M(x,y)
, for all distinct
x,y\in X,t\in[0,1]
, where

M(x,y)=\max\{|x-y|,|x-Tx|,|y-Ty|\};

(3)

for all
t,s\in[0,1]
,

\int_{0}^{t}|K(t,s)|ds\leq\frac{1}{1+M(x,y)}.

Theorem 3.1
.

Under the assumptions (1)-(3), the integral equation (
20
) has a unique solution in
X=C([0,1])
.

Proof.

Define a mapping
\alpha:C[0,1]\times C[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
by

\displaystyle\alpha(x,y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}1&\hbox{if}~~x(s),y(s)\in[0,\infty)~\text{for all}~~s\in[0,1]\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Notice that the existence of solution to (
20
) is equivalent to the existence of fixed point of
T
. Now, we will show that all hypothesis of Theorem
2.1
are satisfied.
We first show that
T
is triangular
\alpha
-admissible mapping. Indeed, for
x,y,z\in C[0,1]
such that
\alpha(x(s),y(s))\geq 1
and
\alpha(y(s),z(s))\geq 1
, we have
x(s),y(s),z(s)\geq 0
for all
s\in[0,1]
. Therefore
\alpha(x(s),z(s))\geq 1
and hence
T
is triangular
\alpha
-admissible mapping. Also there is
x_{1}(s)\geq 0
such that
Tx_{1}(s)\geq 0
, then
\alpha(x_{1}(s),Tx_{1}(s))\geq 1
.

Now, we claim that the mapping
T:X\rightarrow X
define by (
22
) is
a Suzuki type
\mathcal{Z}_{(\alpha,\mathcal{G})}
Geraghty contraction.

From condition (2) and (3), for all distinct
x,y\in C([0,1]),t\in[0,1]
,
we have

\displaystyle\alpha(x,y)|Tx(t)-Ty(t)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|Tx(t)-Ty(t)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Bigg|\int_{0}^{t}K(t,s)(h(s,x(s))-h(s,y(s)))ds\Bigg|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}|K(t,s)||h(s,x(s))-h(s,y(s))|ds

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}|K(t,s)|M(x(s),y(s))ds,

where

\displaystyle M(x(s),y(s))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\{|x(s)-y(s)|,|x(s)-Tx(s)|,|y(s)-Ty(s)|\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max\{d(x,y),d(x,Tx),d(y,Ty)\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle M(x,y).

This implies,

\displaystyle\alpha(x,y)|Tx(t)-Ty(t)|

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle M(x,y)\int_{0}^{t}|K(t,s)|ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{M(x,y)}{1+M(x,y)}.

Hence, the mapping

is a Suzuki type
\mathcal{Z}_{(\alpha,\mathcal{G})}

Geraghty contraction with
\zeta(t,s)=\mathcal{G}(s,t)=s-t
and
\beta(t)=\frac{1}{1+t}
. Thus we can apply Theorem
2.1
, which guarantee
the existence of a unique fixed point
w\in X
. That is,

is the unique
solution of non linear Hammerstein integral equation (
20
).
∎

Schur rings over Sp ⁡ ( 𝐧 , 𝟐 ) {\bf{\rm Sp}(n,2)} and multiplicity one subgroups
Stephen Humphries

Abstract
. We study commutative Schur rings over the symplectic groups Sp ( n , 2 ) (n,2) containing the class 𝒞 \mathcal{C} of symplectic transvections. We find the possible partitions of 𝒞 \mathcal{C} determined by the Schur ring. We show how this restricts the possibilities for multiplicity one subgroups of Sp ( n , 2 ) (n,2) . Keywords : Symplectic group; characteristic 2 2 ; Schur ring; Orthogonal group; symmetric group; 3 3 -transposition group.

1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a finite group. For
X\subseteq G
we let
\overline{X}=\sum_{x\in X}x\in\mathbb{Q}G
and
X^{(-1)}=\{x^{-1}:x\in X\}
. Then a
Schur ring

[
21
,
24
,
8
,
20
]
over
G
is a subalgebra
\mathfrak{S}
of
\mathbb{Q}G
determined by a partition
C_{1}=\{1\},C_{2},\cdots,C_{r}
of

where
(i) for
1\leq i\leq r
there is
1\leq i^{\prime}\leq r
with
C_{i}^{(-1)}=C_{i^{\prime}}
;
(ii) there are
\lambda_{ijk}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\geq 0}
with
\overline{C_{i}}\,\overline{C_{j}}=\sum_{k=1}^{r}\lambda_{ijk}\overline{C_{k}}.

The

are called

(or

-set

is to take a subgroup

and let the

be the
H

.

With this definition we can propose the following:

that is generated by a conjugacy class
\mathcal{C}
. Determine all partitions of
\mathcal{C}
that come from a commutative Schur ring
\mathfrak{S}
over
G
that contains
{\mathcal{C}}
as an
\mathfrak{S}
-set.

This problem is particularly relevant for groups containing a canonical class
\mathcal{C}
of generators, and more specifically when those generators are involutions.
Some examples of such a class of groups are the 3-transposition groups: a group
G
that has a conjugacy class
\mathcal{C}
of involutary generators satisfying: for

the order of

is
1,2
or
3

[

,
4
,
15
]
. The most-studied class of 3-transposition groups are the symmetric groups

where

is the set of transpositions. Other examples of 3-transposition groups are the symplectic groups Sp
(n,2)
and the orthogonal groups

[

we addressed the above problem for

More information about the partition of

2.
Definitions and Preliminary results

If
V=\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
is a non-degenerate symplectic space with symplectic form
\cdot
then there is a (standard) symplectic basis
e_{1},\cdots,e_{n}
, where

e_{i}\cdot e_{j}=1
if and only if
i+j=n+1
.
We will also sometimes write a symplectic basis as
a_{1}=e_{1},b_{1}=e_{n},a_{2}=e_{2},b_{2}=e_{n-1},\cdots,a_{n/2}=e_{n/2},b_{n/2}=e_{n/2+1}
.
We let
V^{\sharp}:=V\setminus\{0\}
. Then
G_{n}={\rm Sp}(n,2)
is the group of linear maps of
V
that preserve this form
[
3
,
10
]
.

To each
v\in V^{\sharp}
we associate the
symplectic transvection

t_{v}\in G_{n}
, where

(w)t_{v}=w+(v\cdot w)v\text{ for }w\in V.

If
v=0\in V
, then we naturally define
t_{0}=id_{G}
. Let
\mathcal{T}=\mathcal{T}_{n}
denote the set of symplectic transvections in
G_{n}
. Then
\mathcal{T}
is a conjugacy class of involutions in
G
.
For
1\leq i,j\leq n
we let
t_{i}=t_{e_{i}},t_{i,j}=t_{e_{i}+e_{j}}
etc.

In what follows we will identify
v\in V^{\sharp}
with
t_{v}\in\mathcal{T}
and so identify any
U\subseteq V^{\sharp}
with

\mathcal{T}(U):=\{t_{u}:u\in U\}
.

We recall that a subset
X\subset V
is
isotropic
if
u\cdot v=0
for all
u,v\in X.

For
X\subseteq V
we let
X^{\perp}=\{u\in V:u\cdot x=0\text{ for all }x\in X\}
.
For
X,Y\subseteq V
we write
X\perp Y
if
x\cdot y=0
for all
x\in X,y\in Y
.

We will have need to use the
Schur-Wielandt principle
: if
\alpha=\sum_{g\in G}f(g)g\in\mathfrak{S},
where
f:G\to\mathbb{R}
is the coefficient function, then for each

\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
we have
\sum_{g\in G:f(g)=\lambda}g\in\mathfrak{S}
.
We will also use the following without reference
[
3
,
10
]
:

Theorem 2.1

(Witt’s Theorem)
.

Let
U,W
be subspaces of
V
and let
\varphi:U\to W
be an isometry. Then
\varphi
can be extended to an isometry
\psi:V\to V.

We have the following fundamental properties of transvections::

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
t_{a},t_{b}\in\mathcal{T}
. Then

(i)
t_{a}=t_{b}
if and only if
a=b
if and only if the order of
t_{a}t_{b}
is
1
.

(ii) For
a\neq b
we have:
a\cdot b=0
if and only if the order of
t_{a}t_{b}
is
2
if and only if
t_{a}
and
t_{b}
commute.

(iii)
a\cdot b=1
if and only if the order of
t_{a}t_{b}
is
3
if and only if
t_{a}
and
t_{b}
do not commute if and only if
t_{a}^{t_{b}}=t_{a+b}=t_{b}^{t_{a}}
.

(iv) For
\alpha\in G,\alpha\in V^{\sharp},
we have
\alpha^{-1}t_{a}\alpha=t_{(a)\alpha}
.

(v) For pairs
t_{a},t_{b}\in\mathcal{T},a\neq b,
and
t_{c},t_{d}\in\mathcal{T},c\neq d,
the elements
t_{a}t_{b}
and
t_{c}t_{d}
are conjugate if and only if
a\cdot b=c\cdot d
.

(vi) If
\alpha=t_{v_{1}}t_{v_{2}}\cdots t_{v_{s}},v\in V
, then
(v)\alpha=v+w
, where
w\in{\rm Span}(v_{1},\cdots,v_{s})
.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
a,b\in V^{\sharp}
with
a\cdot b=1.

If
t_{c},t_{d}\in\mathcal{T}
, then
t_{c}t_{d}=t_{a}t_{b}
, if and only if we have one of:

(i)
c=a,d=b
;

(ii)
c=b,d=a+b
; or

(iii)
c=a+b,d=a
.

Here the pair
[t_{c},t_{d}]
has the form
[t_{a}^{g^{i}},t_{b}^{g^{i}}],
for some
i=0,1,2,
where
g=t_{a}t_{b}
.

Proof

Note that
t_{a}^{t_{b}}=t_{a+b}
. For
c,d
as in (i)-(iii) above, one checks that
t_{c}t_{d}=t_{a}t_{b}
.

Conversely: assume
t_{c}t_{d}=t_{a}t_{b}
. If
c\cdot d=0
, then
t_{a}t_{b}
and
t_{c}t_{d}
are not conjugate and so not equal.
Thus
c\cdot d=1
,
and from
(x)t_{a}t_{b}=(x)t_{c}t_{d},x\in V,
we get

(2.1)

\displaystyle(b\cdot x)b+(a\cdot x)[a+b]=(d\cdot x)d+(c\cdot x)[c+d].

Putting
x=a
and
x=b
in Eq. (
2.1
) we get

b,a+b\in{\rm{\rm Span}}(c,d)
(respectively),
so that
{\rm Span}(a,b)
= Span
(c,d)
and we write:

c=\alpha a+\beta b,\qquad d=\gamma a+\delta b\text{ where }\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta\in\mathbb{F}_{2}.

Putting
x=a
in Eq. (
2.1
) we get

b=\delta(\gamma a+\delta b)+\beta(\alpha a+\beta b+\gamma a+\delta b)

Since
a\cdot b=1
,
a,b
are linearly independent, and equating coefficients of
a,b
we get

(2.2)

\displaystyle 0=\delta\gamma+\beta\alpha+\beta\gamma,\qquad 1=\delta+\gamma+\delta\gamma.

Similarly, putting
x=b
in Eq. (
2.1
), we get

(2.3)

\displaystyle 1=\gamma+\alpha+\alpha\gamma,\qquad 1=\gamma\delta+\alpha\beta+\alpha\delta.

Solving Eqs (
2.2
), (
2.3
) over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
gives

[c,d]\in\{[a,b],[b,a+b],[a+b,a]\}
. ∎

Lemma 2.4
.

(i) If
a,b\in V^{\sharp}
with
a\cdot b=0,a\neq b
, then the only
c,d\in V^{\sharp}
with
t_{c}t_{d}=t_{a}t_{b}
are where
[c,d]\in\{[a,b],[b,a]\}
.

(ii) For
a,b,c\in V^{\sharp}
we cannot have
t_{a}t_{b}=t_{c}
.

Proof
(i) This is similar to the proof of Lemma
2.3
.
(ii) If
a=b
, then
t_{c}=1
, a contradiction. If
a\neq b
, then one shows that the image of
t_{a}t_{b}-1
has dimension
2
, a contradiction, since the image of
t_{c}-1
has dimension
1
.
∎

The next result tells us when
\overline{C}
and
\overline{D}
commute, for disjoint
C,D\subseteq\mathcal{T}
.

Lemma 2.5
.

For disjoint and non-empty
C,D\subseteq\mathcal{T}
we have:
\overline{C},\overline{D}
commute if and only if
for all
t_{a}\in C,t_{b}\in D
with
a\cdot b=1
we have
t_{a+b}\in C\cup D
.

Proof
First note that if
C\perp D
, then
\overline{C},\overline{D}
commute, so we may now assume that

\overline{C},\overline{D}
commute and there are

t_{a}\in C,t_{b}\in D
with
a\cdot b=1
. Then
t_{a}t_{b}\in\overline{C}\cdot\overline{D}
, and so there are
t_{c}\in D,t_{d}\in C
with

t_{c}t_{d}=t_{a}t_{b}
. By Lemma
2.3
we have one of:

(i)
c=a,d=b
;

(ii)
c=b,d=a+b
; or

(iii)
c=a+b,d=a
.

If (i), then
t_{a}\in C\cap D=\emptyset
, a contradiction; if (ii) or (iii), then
t_{a+b}\in C\cup D
.

For the converse: assume that if
t_{a}\in C,t_{b}\in D,a\cdot b=1
, then
t_{a+b}=t_{a}^{t_{b}}=t_{b}^{t_{a}}\in C\cup D
.
We show that
\overline{C},\overline{D}
commute.
Let
t_{a}\in C,t_{b}\in D
so that
t_{a}t_{b}\in\overline{C}\,\overline{D}
. We first show that
t_{a}t_{b}
is a summand of
\overline{D}\,\overline{C}
.
If
a\cdot b=0
, then
t_{a}t_{b}=t_{b}t_{a}\in\overline{D}\cdot\overline{C}
and we are done.
So assume
a\cdot b=1
.
Then
t_{a}^{t_{b}}=t_{a+b}\in C\cup D
. We have two cases:

(a) If
t_{a}^{t_{b}}\in C
, then
\overline{D}\,\overline{C}
contains
t_{b}t_{a}^{t_{b}}=t_{a}t_{b}
as a summand, and we are done.

(b) If
t_{a}^{t_{b}}\in D
, then
\overline{D}\,\overline{C}
contains
t_{a}^{t_{b}}t_{a}=t_{b}^{t_{a}}t_{a}=t_{a}t_{b}
, as required.

From Lemma
2.3
, Lemma
2.4
and the above enumeration of cases we see that, for fixed
t_{a}\in C,t_{b}\in D,
there is only one way to find
t_{c}\in D,t_{d}\in C
with
t_{a}t_{b}=t_{c}t_{d}
. Thus the coefficient of
t_{a}t_{b}
in
\overline{C}\,\overline{D}
and in
\overline{D}\,\overline{C}
is
1
, and so
\overline{C}\,\overline{D}=\overline{D}\,\overline{C}
.∎

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
C,D\subset\mathcal{T}
be disjoint and assume that
\overline{C},\,\overline{D}
commute. Let
W\leq V
be a subspace. Then

\overline{C\cap W},\overline{D\cap W}

Proof
We use Lemma
2.5
: assume that
t_{a}\in C\cap\mathcal{T}(W),t_{b}\in D\cap\mathcal{T}(W),a\cdot b=1.
Then

a,b\in W

t_{a}\in C,t_{b}\in D

t_{a+b}\in C\cup D
.
But
W

t_{a+b}\in C\cap\mathcal{T}(W)
or
t_{a+b}\in D\cap\mathcal{T}(W)

∎

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
C\subseteq\mathcal{T}
. Choose
a,b\in V^{\sharp},a\cdot b=1
.
Then

By Lemma

\{t_{a},t_{a+b}\},\{t_{b},t_{a+b}\},\{t_{a},t_{b},t_{a+b}\}

∎

.

(i) Let
a,b\in V^{\sharp},a\neq b,a\cdot b=0

.

(ii) Let

\overline{\mathcal{T}}^{2}=|\mathcal{T}|+2(t_{1}t_{2})^{G}+3(t_{1}t_{n})^{G}

Let

(i)
\sum_{T\in{\rm Tr}^{2}(C)}T^{*}\in\mathfrak{S}

(ii)

Proof
By Corollary
2.8

(t_{1}t_{n})^{G}\in\mathfrak{S}

By Lemma

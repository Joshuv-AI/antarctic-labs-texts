Second-Scale Be + 9 {}^{9}\text{Be}^{+} Spin Coherence in a Compact Penning Trap
Brian J. McMahon, Brian C. Sawyer

Abstract
We report microwave spectroscopy of co-trapped Be + 9 {}^{9}\text{Be}^{+} and Ca + 40 {}^{40}\text{Ca}^{+} within a compact permanent-magnet-based Penning ion trap. The trap is constructed with a reconfigurable array of NdFeB rings providing a 0.654 T magnetic field that is near the 0.6774-T magnetic-field-insensitive hyperfine transition in Be + 9 {}^{9}\text{Be}^{+} . Performing Ramsey spectroscopy on this hyperfine transition, we demonstrate nuclear spin coherence with a contrast decay time of > 1 ​ s >1~\text{s} . The Be + 9 {}^{9}\text{Be}^{+} is sympathetically cooled by a Coulomb crystal of Ca + 40 {}^{40}\text{Ca}^{+} , which minimizes Be + 9 {}^{9}\text{Be}^{+} illumination and thus mitigates reactive loss. Introducing a unique high-magnetic-field optical detection scheme for Ca + 40 {}^{40}\text{Ca}^{+} , we perform spin state readout without a 729 nm shelving laser. We record a fractional trap magnetic field instability below 20 ​ ppb 20~\text{ppb} ( < 13 <13 nT) at 43 s of averaging time with no magnetic shielding and only passive thermal isolation. We discuss potential applications of this compact, reconfigurable Penning trap.

Introduction

Cold, trapped atomic ions have long served as platforms for precision measurements. In contrast with neutral atoms and molecules, the ion charge provides a mechanism for stable, long-term confinement that is insensitive to internal state dynamics.

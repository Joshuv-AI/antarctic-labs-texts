Model-free optimal controller for discrete-time Markovian jump linear systems: A Q-learning approach
Ehsan Badfar, Babak Tavassoli

Abstract
This research paper introduces a model-free optimal controller for discrete-time Markovian jump linear systems (MJLSs), employing principles from the methodology of reinforcement learning (RL). While Q-learning methods have demonstrated efficacy in determining optimal controller gains for deterministic systems, their application to systems with Markovian switching remains unexplored. To address this research gap, we propose a Q-function involving the Markovian mode. Subsequently, a Q-learning algorithm is proposed to learn the unknown kernel matrix using raw input-state information from the system. Notably, the study proves the convergence of the proposed Q-learning optimal controller gains to the model-based optimal controller gains after proving the convergence of a value iteration algorithm as the first step. Addition of excitation noise to input which is required to ensure the leaning performance does not lead to any bias. Unlike the conventional optimal controller, the proposed method does not require any knowledge on system dynamics and eliminates the need for solving coupled algebraic Riccati equations arising in optimal control of MJLSs. Finally, the efficiency of the proposed method is demonstrated through a simulation study. Keywords: Markovian Jump Systems; Optimal Control; Reinforcement Learning; Q-Learning.



1
Introduction

Markovian systems constitute a practically significant category within stochastic switching systems. They can be employed to model dynamics that switch abruptly across a finite set of linear or nonlinear models, guided by a finite Markov chain
(
Shi and Li,, 2015
;
Zhao et al.,, 2019
)
. They are widely used in various domains, encompassing modeling, stability analysis, and control within real-world applications, such as networked control systems
(
Gupta and Chow,, 2009
;
Zhang et al.,, 2015
)
, power systems
(
Sanjari and Gooi,, 2016
;
Corrêa and Vieira,, 2021
)
, and cyber-attacks
(
Wu et al.,, 2016
;
Cao et al.,, 2019
)
.

During the past decade, several efforts have been directed towards the advancement of control methodologies for MJLS. For instance, in
Tzortzis et al., (2020)
, the authors designed a Linear Quadratic Regulator (LQR) for MJLS with uncertain transition probabilities through a min-max optimization approach. In
Han et al., (2021)
, the authors introduced a finite horizon optimal controller for MJLS with input delay with stability guarantee in mean-square sense. In
Tang et al., (2024)
, the authors proposed a novel augmentation technique to develop criteria for the finite-time controllability of time-varying delayed Markovian jump control networks. Additionally, other approaches such as sliding mode control
(
Qi et al.,, 2019
)
, model predictive control
(
Zhang and Song,, 2022
)
, event-triggered control
(
Zhou et al.,, 2024
)
, and fuzzy control
(
Tan et al.,, 2024
)
have been successfully applied to control MJLS.

All the previously mentioned research works rely on the model of system to achieve the controller gain for a Markovian jump linear system (MJLS). In other words, it is essential to have complete knowledge of system dynamics to derive the control input signal. Since the dynamics of the system might be unavailable in many practical applications, the design of model-free controllers can be regarded as an important challenge for the control engineering community
(
Annaswamy et al.,, 2023
;
Dörfler,, 2023
)
.

An innovative approach to solve this challenging problem lies in the adoption of machine learning techniques, particularly RL
(
Bertsekas,, 2019
)
. In RL, an intelligent agent exchanges data with an unknown environment. The intelligent agent mainly aims to learn the policies by observing the response from the unknown environment. Recently, RL has demonstrated outstanding results across diverse application domains, including robotics
(
Polydoros and Nalpantidis,, 2017
;
Singh et al.,, 2022
)
, transportation
(
Cao et al.,, 2020
;
Wei et al.,, 2021
)
, and economics
(
Charpentier et al.,, 2021
;
Hambly et al.,, 2023
)
. From a control engineering perspective, RL has a high potential to bridge the gap between conventional optimal control and data-driven control
(
Kiumarsi et al.,, 2017
;
Buşoniu et al.,, 2018
;
Annaswamy,, 2023
)
. To mention some of the approaches to applying RL methods in various control problems,
Rizvi and Lin, (2018)
formulates a model-free
H_{\infty}
controller for a class of linear discrete-time system based on the input-output information from the system. In
Fan et al., (2019)
, the authors develop a model-free tracking controller for discrete-time linear systems through an off-policy method.
Farjadnasab and Babazadeh, (2022)
employs RL techniques to develop LQR for a decentralized system.
Jiang et al., (2023)
uses RL to design a cooperative
H_{\infty}
output regulation for multi-agent systems.
Huang et al., (2024)
utilizes RL methods to develop zero tracking error optimal controller for linear discrete-time systems with user-specified convergence rate.

In contrast to the recent advances of RL in developing model-free optimal controllers for deterministic systems, the problem of designing model-free optimal controllers for an MJLS with RL has not yet been investigated. The primary objective of this research is to employ RL methods, specifically Q-learning, to develop a model-free controller for discrete-time MJLS. This controller must converge to the conventional optimal controller for MJLS without the need for prior knowledge of system dynamics or the offline solution of the Algebraic Riccati equation backward in time. Accordingly, the contributions of this research paper are summarized as follows. We introduce a model-free optimal controller for a linear discrete-time system with Markovian transitions. For this purpose, a novel formulation of value function and Q-function are suggested. It is demonstrated that the excitation noise during the learning process does not introduce any bias in the estimation of the kernel matrix. Additionally, the algorithm proposed in this research does not require an initial stabilizing control policy. Finally, it is proven that the proposed model-free controller converges to the model-based optimal controller, which is guaranteed to be mean-square stable.

The rest of this research paper is organized as follows. Section

briefly presents the MJLS and the corresponding conventional optimal controller. In Section
3
, the value iteration algorithm for MJLS is developed. Section
4
formulates Q-learning method and algorithm for MJLS using the least squares (LS) technique to learn the unknown kernel matrix of the Q-function. Furthermore, the convergence of the proposed method to the conventional optimal controller is proved. Then, a simulation study is presented in Section
5
to demonstrate the efficacy of the proposed controller. Finally, the conclusions are provided in Section
6
.

2
Background and problem statement

In this section, we first introduce the Markovian system under study, together with the stability notation. Then, we briefly present the model-based optimal controller for MJLS.

2.1
System description

The discrete-time MJLS in the state-space form is considered as

\mathcal{G}:\begin{cases}x_{k+1}=A_{\theta_{k}}x_{k}+B_{\theta_{k}}u_{k}\\
y_{k}=C_{\theta_{k}}x_{k}\end{cases}

(1)

where
x_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
y_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{q}
, and
u_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
are the state, output, and control input vectors, respectively. The system matrices are
N
-matrices given as
A=(A_{1},\dots,A_{N})
,
B=(B_{1},\dots,B_{N})
, and
C=(C_{1},\dots,C_{N})
with compatible dimensions.
The Markovian jump parameter
\theta_{k}
takes values from the finite set
\Theta=\{1,2,\dots,N\}
with transition probabilities

p_{ij}=\mathbb{P}[\theta_{k+1}=j\mid\theta_{k}=i],

(2)

where
p_{ij}\in[0,1]
for all
i,j\in\Theta
and
\sum_{j=1}^{N}p_{ij}=1
for all
i\in\Theta
.
The probability that the Markovian mode is
i\in\Theta
at time step
k
is denoted as

\pi_{k,i}=\mathbb{P}[\theta_{k}=i].

(3)

which satisfies
\sum_{i=1}^{N}\pi_{k,i}=1
for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{0}^{+}
.
The probability distribution of
\theta_{k}
is given by the vector
\pi_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
defined such that
[\pi_{k}]_{i}=\pi_{k,i}
. The transition probability matrix is obtained as
[\Phi]_{ij}=p_{ij}
for
i,j\in\Theta
. Then, one can use (
2
) to write

\pi_{k+1}^{T}=\pi_{k}^{T}\Phi\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{Z}_{0}^{+}.

(4)

If the limit probability distribution
\pi_{\infty}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\pi_{k}
exists, then the Markov chain
\{\theta_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
is called ergodic
(
Costa et al.,, 2006
)
.

The overall state of MJLS at time step
k
is composed of the pair of system state and Markovian mode which is denoted as

I_{k}=(x_{k},\theta_{k}).

(5)

The stability of system
\mathcal{G}
is studied in the context of mean-square stability in
Costa et al., (2006)
, which can be represented as the following definition for the system
\mathcal{G}
in (
1
).

Definition 1
.

The system
\mathcal{G}
is mean-square stable if the following relation holds for all
I_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Theta
.

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|x_{k}\right\|^{2}\mid I_{0}\right]<\infty.

(6)

Definition 2

(
Zhao, 2008
)
.

The pair
(A,B)
is mean-square stabilizable if there exists a control law in the form of
u_{k}=-K_{\theta_{k}}x_{k}
with
K=(K_{1},\dots,K_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
, such that the closed-loop system is mean-square stable.

2.2
Model-based Optimal Controller

In the conventional optimal control design for MJLS, it is desired to find a stabilizing control law that minimizes an infinite horizon quadratic cost function

\mathbb{J}\left(x_{0},\theta_{0}\right)=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}x_{k}^{T}Q_{\theta_{k}}x_{k}+u_{k}^{T}R_{\theta_{k}}u_{k}\mid I_{0}\right]

(7)

for the initial state
I_{0}=(x_{0},\theta_{0})
. The weighting matrices
Q=(Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{N})
and
R=(R_{1},\ldots,R_{N})
are
N
-matrices with appropriate dimensions such that
R_{i}>0
and
Q_{i}\geq 0
for
i\in\Theta
.

To facilitate the control design it is common to make the following reasonable assumptions
(
Costa et al.,, 2006
)
.

Assumption 1
.

It is assumed that the state variables and Markovian modes of the system
\mathcal{G}
in (
1
) are conditionally independent random variables such that

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}[f(x_{k+1})g(\theta_{k+1})\mid I_{k}]\\
&=\mathbb{E}[f(x_{k+1})\mid I_{k}]\mathbb{E}[g(\theta_{k+1})\mid I_{k}].\end{split}

(8)

Assumption 2
.

It is assumed that the Markovian mode of the system
\mathcal{G}
in (
1
) is ergodic such that for all
i,j\in\Theta
, the mode transition from
i
to
j
within a finite number of steps has a non-zero probability.

Assumption 3
.

It is assumed that the system
\mathcal{G}
is mean-square stabilizable.

Then, the following theorem from
(
Costa et al.,, 2006
, Theorem 4.5)
provides the model-based optimal controller for the MJLS
\mathcal{G}
through solution of some coupled algebraic Riccati equations (CARE).

Theorem 1

(
Costa et al., 2006
)
.

If the system
\mathcal{G}
in (
1
) satisfies the assumptions
1
,
2
, and
3
, then, the optimal value of the cost function (
7
) is achieved by applying the control policy given by

u^{*}_{k}=-K_{\theta_{k}}x_{k}.

(9)

with the control gains for
i\in\Theta
defined as

K_{i}=\left(R_{i}+B_{i}^{T}\mathcal{E}_{i}(P)B_{i}\right)^{-1}B_{i}^{T}\mathcal{E}_{i}(P)A_{i}

(10)

where the
N
-matrix
P=(P_{1},\ldots,P_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n\times N}
has positive definite elements that satisfy

\begin{split}P_{i}=&Q_{i}+A_{i}^{T}\mathcal{E}_{i}(P)A_{i}\\
-&A_{i}^{T}\mathcal{E}_{i}(P)B_{i}\left(R_{i}+B_{i}^{T}\mathcal{E}_{i}(P)B_{i}\right)^{-1}B_{i}^{T}\mathcal{E}_{i}(P)A_{i}\end{split}

(11)

for
i\in\Theta
with the operator
\mathcal{E}_{i}(P)
defined as

\mathcal{E}_{i}(P)=\mathbb{E}\left[P_{\theta_{k+1}}\mid\theta_{k}=i\right]=\sum_{j=1}^{N}p_{ij}P_{j}.

(12)

Moreover, the optimal cost function denoted by
\mathbb{J}^{*}
is

\mathbb{J}^{*}(x_{0},\theta_{0})=x_{0}^{T}P_{\theta_{0}}x_{0}.

(13)

2.3
Problem statement

The controller gains in (
10
) are computed based on the information of the MJLS model in (
1
).
The objective of this work is to compute the same set of controller gains only based on the measured system variables data and without need to use equation (
10
).
In other words, no knowledge of the coefficients in (
1
) or solution of the CARE in (
11
) are required.
The following additional assumptions are made.

Assumption 4
.

It is assumed that the information set
\mathcal{I}_{k}=\{I_{0},I_{1},\cdots,I_{k}\}
on the system state variables is available to the controller at every time step
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{0}^{+}
.

Assumption 5
.

It is assumed that the transition probabilities in (
2
) for the Markovian mode of
\mathcal{G}
in (
1
) are known and available.

It is noteworthy that Assumption
5
is not restrictive, because the transition probabilities can be easily estimated empirically considering that the Markovian mode is measurable according to Assumption
4
.

3
Value iteration for MJLS

In this section, we employ the value iteration method to determine the optimal controller gain without the need to solve the CARE in (
11
). Subsequently, we prove that the value function converges to the optimal value function along with the algorithm iterations.

3.1
Value iteration algorithm

In this subsection, the Bellman principle of optimality is employed to introduce the value iteration algorithm for MJLS. The proposed method obtains the optimal control policies iteratively.
As the first step, the quadratic value function for the MJLS with a given controller is written as

\mathbb{J}(x_{k},\theta_{k})=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i=k}^{\infty}x_{i}^{T}Q_{\theta_{i}}x_{i}+u_{i}^{T}R_{\theta_{i}}u_{i}\mid I_{k}\right].

(14)

The cost function (
7
) is the above value function for
k=0
.
According to Equation (
13
) in Theorem
1
, the optimal value function
\mathbb{J}^{*}
which is achieved by using the control gains (
10
) also takes the quadratic form

\mathbb{J}^{*}(x_{k},\theta_{k})=x_{k}^{T}P_{\theta_{k}}x_{k}.

(15)

Using the iterated conditional mathematical expectation
(
Papoulis,, 2002
)
, the value function (
14
) can be decomposed as

\begin{split}&\mathbb{J}\left(x_{k},\theta_{k}\right)=\mathbb{E}\bigg[x_{k}^{T}Q_{\theta_{k}}x_{k}+u_{k}^{T}R_{\theta_{k}}u_{k}+\\
&\qquad\mathbb{E}\Big[\sum_{i=k+1}^{\infty}x_{i}^{T}Q_{\theta_{i}}x_{i}+u_{i}^{T}R_{\theta_{i}}u_{i}\mid I_{k+1}\Big]\mid I_{k}\bigg]\text{.}\end{split}

(16)

By the definition of the value function (
14
), the above equation can be expressed as

\begin{split}\mathbb{J}(x_{k},\theta_{k})=x_{k}^{T}Q_{\theta_{k}}x_{k}&+u_{k}^{T}R_{\theta_{k}}u_{k}\\[3.0pt]
&+\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{J}(x_{k+1},\theta_{k+1})|I_{k}].\end{split}

(17)

The above equation is recognized as the Bellman equation for system
\mathcal{G}
. Based on the Bellman principle of optimality, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{J}^{*}(x_{k},\theta_{k})=

\displaystyle\min_{u_{k}}\Big[x_{k}^{T}Q_{\theta_{k}}x_{k}+u_{k}^{T}R_{\theta_{k}}u_{k}

(18)

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\big[J^{*}(x_{k+1},\theta_{k+1})\mid I_{k}\big]\Big].

This equation is a representation of the Hamiltonian-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) equation for MJLS. According to the above equation, the optimal control policy is calculated as

\displaystyle u^{*}_{k}=\text{arg}\min_{u}\big[

\displaystyle x_{k}^{T}Q_{\theta_{k}}x_{k}+u_{k}^{T}R_{\theta_{k}}u_{k}

(19)

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{J}^{*}(x_{k+1},\theta_{k+1})|I_{k}]\,\big].

The value iteration algorithm computes sequences of control laws and value functions according to

\begin{split}u^{j}(x_{k},\theta_{k})=\text{arg}\min_{u_{k}}\big[&x_{k}^{T}Q_{\theta_{k}}x_{k}+u_{k}^{T}R_{\theta_{k}}u_{k}\\
&+\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{J}^{j}(x_{k+1},\theta_{k+1})|I_{k}]\,\big]\end{split}

(20)

\begin{split}\mathbb{J}^{j+1}(x_{k},\theta_{k})=&x_{k}^{T}Q_{\theta_{k}}x_{k}+u^{j^{T}}(x_{k},\theta_{k})R_{\theta_{k}}u^{j}(x_{k},\theta_{k})\\
&+\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{J}^{j}(x_{k+1},\theta_{k+1})\mid I_{k}]\end{split}

(21)

in which
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{0}^{+}
is the iteration index and the initialization for
i=0
is made as
\mathbb{J}^{0}(x_{k},\theta_{k})=0
.
The two equations (
20
) and (
21
) can be combined as

\begin{split}\mathbb{J}^{j+1}(x_{k},\theta_{k})=x_{k}^{T}Q_{\theta_{k}}&x_{k}+\min_{u_{k}}~\big[~u_{k}^{T}R_{\theta_{k}}u_{k}\\
&+\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{J}^{j}(x_{k+1},\theta_{k+1})\mid I_{k}]\,\big]\end{split}

(22)

3.2
Convergence proof of value iteration algorithm

In this subsection, it is proved that the sequence of value functions given by (
21
) converges to the optimal value function, i.e.,
\mathbb{J}^{j}(x_{k},\theta_{k})\rightarrow\mathbb{J}^{*}(x_{k},\theta_{k})
, as
i\rightarrow\infty
. Let
\mu^{j}(x_{k},\theta_{k})
be an arbitrary sequence of control policies, and a sequence
\Lambda^{j}(x_{k},\theta_{k})
, with the initial condition
\Lambda^{0}(x_{k},\theta_{k})=0
, be recursively defined as

\displaystyle\Lambda^{j+1}(x_{k},\theta_{k})

\displaystyle=x_{k}^{T}Q_{\theta_{k}}x_{k}+\mu^{j^{T}}(x_{k},\theta_{k})R_{\theta_{k}}\mu^{j}(x_{k},\theta_{k})

(23)

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}[\Lambda^{j}(x_{k+1},\theta_{k+1})\mid I_{k}].

The following lemmas are necessary to prove the convergence of the value iteration algorithm for MJLS.

Lemma 1
.

Let the value functions
\mathbb{J}^{j}(x_{k},\theta_{k})
and
\Lambda^{j}(x_{k},\theta_{k})
for the
j
-th iteration step be updated as in (
21
) and (
23
) with
\mathbb{J}^{0}(x_{0},\theta_{0})=\Lambda^{0}(x_{0},\theta_{0})=0
. Then, the following inequality holds for all
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{0}^{+}
.

\mathbb{J}^{j}(x_{k},\theta_{k})\leq\Lambda^{j}(x_{k},\theta_{k}).

(24)

Proof.

The proof begins by setting the initial conditions
\mathbb{J}^{0}(x_{k},\theta_{k})=\Lambda^{0}(x_{k},\theta_{k})=0
. This establishes that the inequality (
24
) holds at the initial step. Now, it is assumed that the inequality holds for some arbitrary step
j
, i.e.,
\mathbb{J}^{j}(x_{k},\theta_{k})\leq\Lambda^{j}(x_{k},\theta_{k})
. It is necessary to show that this implies the inequality also holds for the next iteration step
i+1
. Based on the definitions of value functions,
\mathbb{J}^{j+1}(x_{k},\theta_{k})
is obtained by minimizing the right-hand side of (
21
) with respect to the optimal control policy, while
\Lambda^{j+1}(x_{k},\theta_{k})
is the result of an arbitrary control policy. Therefore, it is concluded that

\mathbb{J}^{j+1}(x_{k},\theta_{k})\leq\Lambda^{j+1}(x_{k},\theta_{k}).

(25)

Hence, mathematical induction ensures that the inequality (
24
) holds for all iteration steps. The proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 2
.

Consider the sequence of value functions
\mathbb{J}^{j}(x_{k},\theta_{k})
given by (
21
). If there exists a control policy
\mu(x_{k},\theta_{k})
that stabilizes the system
\mathcal{G}
in the mean-square sense, then there exists an upper bound
Y(x_{k},\theta_{k})
such that
0\leq\mathbb{J}^{j}(x_{k},\theta_{k})\leq\mathbb{J}^{*}(x_{k},\theta_{k})\leq Y(x_{k},\theta_{k})
for all
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{0}^{+}
.

Proof.

Let
\mu(x_{k},\theta_{k})
be any stabilizing control policy, and set
\mu^{j}(x_{k},\theta_{k})
in (
23
) to
\mu(x_{k},\theta_{k})
for
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{0}^{+}
. The difference between two consecutive iteration steps of
\Lambda^{j}(x_{k},\theta_{k})
can be written as

\displaystyle\Lambda^{j+1}(x_{k},\theta_{k})-\Lambda^{j}(x_{k},\theta_{k})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\Lambda^{j}(x_{k+1},\theta_{k+1})\mid I_{k}]-\mathbb{E}[\Lambda^{j-1}(x_{k+1},\theta_{k+1})\mid I_{k}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\Lambda^{j-1}(x_{k+2},\theta_{k+2})\mid I_{k}]-\mathbb{E}[\Lambda^{j-2}(x_{k+2},\theta_{k+2})\mid I_{k}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\Lambda^{j-2}(x_{k+3},\theta_{k+3})\mid I_{k}]-\mathbb{E}[\Lambda^{j-3}(x_{k+3},\theta_{k+3})\mid I_{k}]

\displaystyle\vdots

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\Lambda^{1}(x_{k+i},\theta_{k+i})\mid I_{k}]-\mathbb{E}[\Lambda^{0}(x_{k+i},\theta_{k+i})\mid I_{k}].

Considering that
\Lambda^{0}
is zero, the result is

\Lambda^{j+1}(x_{k},\theta_{k})-\Lambda^{j}(x_{k},\theta_{k})=\mathbb{E}[\Lambda^{1}(x_{k+i},\theta_{k+i})\mid I_{k}].

Summing up the above equation, we obtain

\displaystyle\Lambda^{j+1}(x_{k},\theta_{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{i}\mathbb{E}[\Lambda^{1}(x_{k+n},\theta_{k+n})|I_{k}].

(26)

By the definition (
23
), we have

\Lambda^{1}(x,\theta)=x^{T}Q_{\theta}x+\mu^{T}(x,\theta)R_{\theta}\mu(x,\theta).

(27)

By defining

\displaystyle Y(x_{k},

\displaystyle\theta_{k})=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}[x_{k+i}^{T}Q_{\theta_{k+i}}x_{k+i}+

\displaystyle\mu^{T}(x_{k+i},\theta_{k+i})R_{\theta_{k+i}}\mu(x_{k+i},\theta_{k+i})|I_{k}]

(28)

one can deduce from (
26
) that

\Lambda^{j}(x_{k},\theta_{k})\leq Y(x_{k},\theta_{k})\qquad\forall j\in\mathbb{Z}_{0}^{+}

(29)

Since the control policy
\mu(x_{k},\theta_{k})
stabilizes the system
\mathcal{G}
it is also concluded that

Y(x_{k},\theta_{k})<\infty.

(30)

Using Lemma
1
, we have

\mathbb{J}^{j}(x_{k},\theta_{k})\leq Y(x_{k},\theta_{k}).

(31)

According to (
28
) and (
14
), we have
Y(x_{k},\theta_{k})=\mathbb{J}(x_{k},\theta_{k})
if we set
u_{k}=\mu(x_{k},\theta_{k})
.
Hence, in the special case
\mu(x_{k},\theta_{k})=u^{*}_{k}
the value of
Y
is minimized and becomes equal to
\mathbb{J}^{*}
which implies

\mathbb{J}^{*}(x_{k},\theta_{k})\leq Y(x_{k},\theta_{k}).

(32)

Since
Y
is equal to
\mathbb{J}^{*}
in the special case
\mu(x_{k},\theta_{k})=u^{*}_{k}
, the inequality (
31
) implies

\mathbb{J}^{j}(x_{k},\theta_{k})\leq\mathbb{J}^{*}(x_{k},\theta_{k}).

(33)

It is concluded recursively from (
21
) that
\mathbb{J}^{j}
is non-negative for every
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{0}^{+}
which together with (
30
), (
32
) and (
33
) completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3
.

Consider the sequence of value functions
\mathbb{J}^{j}(x_{k},\theta_{k})
in (
21
) with the control policies in (
20
) and
\mathbb{J}^{0}(x_{k},\theta_{k})=0
. Then, the sequence of value functions is non-decreasing, such that
\mathbb{J}^{j+1}(x_{k},\theta_{k})\geq\mathbb{J}^{j}(x_{k},\theta_{k})

4
Q-learning

In this section, we first construct the Q-function for model-free optimal control of MJLS. Then, we develop a mechanism for estimating the kernel matrix using the LS method.
Unlike the model-based LQR and value iteration algorithm, the resulting Q-learning based MJLS optimal control design procedure depends solely on the input-state information of the system.
Finally, we prove that the proposed model-free controller converges to the model-based controller and we show that the estimator is unbiased against excitation noise.

4.1
Q-function for optimal control

The stochastic Q-function represents the value associated with taking an action
u_{k}
in a specific state
x_{k}
, mode
\theta_{k}
, and following the optimal policy after the time step
k
. It is defined as follows

\begin{split}\mathbb{Q}(x_{k},u_{k},\theta_{k})&=x_{k}^{T}Q_{\theta_{k}}x_{k}+u_{k}^{T}R_{\theta_{k}}u_{k}\\
&\qquad+\mathbb{E}\left[\ \mathbb{J}^{*}(x_{k+1},\theta_{k+1})\ \,\middle|\,I_{k}\right].\end{split}

(42)

The stochastic Q-function proposed above is closely related to the value function for system
\mathcal{G}
. This relationship can be formulated by representing the HJB equation (
18
) in terms of the Q-function as

\mathbb{J}^{*}(x_{k},\theta_{k})=\min_{u_{k}}\mathbb{Q}(x_{k},u_{k},\theta_{k}).

(43)

Subsequently, we have

u_{k}^{*}=\arg\min_{u_{k}}~\mathbb{Q}(x_{k},u_{k},\theta_{k}).

(44)

It is usual and useful to define a stage cost function such as below to write down the calculations more compactly.

r(x,u,\theta)=x^{T}Q_{\theta}x+u^{T}R_{\theta}u.

(45)

Substitution of (
15
) and (
1
) into (
42
) while using the above notation results in

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}(x_{k},u_{k},\theta_{k})

\displaystyle=r(x_{k},u_{k},\theta_{k})+\left(A_{\theta_{k}}x_{k}+B_{\theta_{k}}u_{k}\right)^{T}

(46)

\displaystyle\times\mathbb{E}\left[P_{\theta_{k+1}}\,\middle|\,I_{k}\right]\left(A_{\theta_{k}}x_{k}+B_{\theta_{k}}u_{k}\right).

Expanding the products and using (
12
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}(

\displaystyle x_{k},u_{k},\theta_{k})=r(x_{k},u_{k},\theta_{k})

(47)

\displaystyle+x_{k}^{T}A_{\theta_{k}}^{T}\mathcal{E}_{\theta_{k}}(P)A_{\theta_{k}}x_{k}+u_{k}^{T}B_{\theta_{k}}^{T}\mathcal{E}_{\theta_{k}}(P)B_{\theta_{k}}u_{k}

\displaystyle+x_{k}^{T}A_{\theta_{k}}^{T}\mathcal{E}_{\theta_{k}}(P)B_{\theta_{k}}u_{k}+u_{k}^{T}B_{\theta_{k}}^{T}\mathcal{E}_{\theta_{k}}(P)A_{\theta_{k}}x_{k}.

The Q-function can be written in terms of the augmented vector
z_{k}=[x_{k}^{T}~~u_{k}^{T}]^{T}
as

\mathbb{Q}(x_{k},u_{k},\theta_{k})=z_{k}^{T}H_{\theta_{k}}z_{k}.

(48)

where

,
l=n+m
is denoted as the kernel matrix of the Q-function. Based on
(45)
and
(46)
, the kernel matrix is decomposed as

\displaystyle H_{\theta_{k}}=

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}H_{\theta_{k}}^{xx}&H_{\theta_{k}}^{xu}\\
H_{\theta_{k}}^{ux}&H_{\theta_{k}}^{uu}\end{bmatrix},

(49a)

\displaystyle H_{\theta_{k}}^{xx}

\displaystyle=Q_{\theta_{k}}+A_{\theta_{k}}^{T}\mathcal{E}_{\theta_{k}}(P)A_{\theta_{k}},

(49b)

\displaystyle H_{\theta_{k}}^{xu}

\displaystyle=A_{\theta_{k}}^{T}\mathcal{E}_{\theta_{k}}(P)B_{\theta_{k}},

(49c)

\displaystyle H_{\theta_{k}}^{ux}

\displaystyle=B_{\theta_{k}}^{T}\mathcal{E}_{\theta_{k}}(P)A_{\theta_{k}},

(49d)

\displaystyle H_{\theta_{k}}^{uu}

\displaystyle=R_{\theta_{k}}+B_{\theta_{k}}^{T}\mathcal{E}_{\theta_{k}}(P)B_{\theta_{k}}.

It is straightforward to use the equations (

P_{i}=\begin{bmatrix}I&K_{i}^{T}\end{bmatrix}H_{i}\begin{bmatrix}I\\

(50)

We can now formulate the optimal controller using the proposed Q-function.
According to (

which is solved based on (

In contrast to the well-established conventional approaches, if the information of Q-function is available, the the proposed method can be used without the need for any prior knowledge of the system dynamics. Furthermore, the optimal control policy is achieved without the necessity to solve nonlinear CARE (

6
Conclusion

In this research, we use RL concepts to develop a model-free controller for discrete-time MJLS based on the data exchanged between the unknown plant and intelligent agent. It is proved that the model-free controller converges to the model-based optimal controller. It is also shown that inclusion of excitation noise does not lead to any bias during the learning process. Therefore, the stability of closed-loop MJLS under the proposed model-free controller is guaranteed. Finally, simulation study is carried out to evaluate the performance of the proposed controller. This research can be extended in several ways, including model-free controller design for MJLS with partially measured state or mode variables.

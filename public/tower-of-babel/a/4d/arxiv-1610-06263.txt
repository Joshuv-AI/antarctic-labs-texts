Cousin groups and Hodge structures
Rodion N. Déev

Abstract
We consider the geometric properties of Hodge Cousin groups, introduced in an unpublished paper [ OVV ] , emphasizing the case of Hodge Cousin groups corresponding to polarized ℚ \mathbb{Q} -Hodge structures. Basing on this consideration, we introduce the class of abelian Cousin groups and prove an analogue of Poincaré complete reducibility theorem for them.



1
Introduction

From now onwards all the Lie groups are assumed to be connected.

Definition 1
.

A complex Lie group
G
is called
Cousin group
if any holomorphic function on it is constant.

This concept goes back to the 1910 Cousin’s paper
[
C
]
. The name is due to Huckleberry and Margulis
[
HM
]
. The definition of Cousin groups appeared in the paper of Morimoto
[
M
]
under the name of
H.C groups
and in the paper of Kopfermann
[
K
]
under the name of
toroidal groups
. For a rather complete account on Cousin groups, see
[
AK
]
.

Proposition 1.1
.

Any representation of a Cousin group
G
in a Banach space
V
is trivial.

Proof.

A representation of
G
in a Banach space
V
is a map
G\to\End(V)
. Pullback of any linear functional on
\End(V)
to
G
is constant, so, by Hahn–Banach theorem, image of
G
is a single point.
∎

Proposition 1.2
.

Finite-dimensional (Banach) Cousin groups are commutative. Moreover, they are quotients of a complex vector (Banach) space by a discrete subgroup.

Proof.

By Proposition
1.1
, the adjoint representation of a Cousin group
G
in its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is trivial. That means that
G
is commutative. Since the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is abelian, the exponential map
\mathfrak{g}\to G
is a homomorphism. Its kernel is discrete since the quotient is a manifold of the same dimension.
∎

Of course, complex tori are Cousin groups. Nevertheless, Cousin groups need not to be compact. For example, a quotient of
\mathbb{C}^{2}=\mathbb{C}\langle v,w\rangle
by a rank
3
lattice which spans the real subspace
\{(v,w)\mid\mathrm{Im}\penalty\ w=0\}
but intersects the complex subspace
\{w=0\}
only by
\{0\}
, is a Cousin group.

Proposition 1.3

(K. Kopfermann, 1964
[
K
]
)
.

Any commutative complex Lie group is a product of Cousin group, several copies of
\mathbb{C}
and several copies of
\GL(1,\mathbb{C})
.

Proposition 1.4
.

Commutative complex Lie group is Cousin if and only if it does not have nontrivial characters.

Proof.

One direction follows from Proposition
1.1
. The other way around, by Kopfermann’s theorem, any commutative complex Lie group
G
is isomorphic to the product of a Cousin group and a group
\GL(1,\mathbb{C})^{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{m}
, which admits a homomorphism to
\GL(1,\mathbb{C})^{n+m}
via exponent provided
n+m>0
(that is,
G
is not Cousin).
∎

2
Complex tori and Hodge structures

This chapter is intended to be an exposition of the well-known correspondence between the complex tori and Hodge structures.

Definition 2
.

Let
k\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be a subring. A
k
-Hodge structure of weight
n
is a pair
(V_{k},\{V^{p,q}\}_{p+q=n}^{p,q\geq 0})
of a finite generated
k
-module
V_{k}
and a splitting
V_{k}\otimes_{k}\mathbb{C}=\oplus_{p+q=n}V^{p,q}
such that
\overline{V^{p,q}}=V^{q,p}
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
k\subseteq k^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be subrings. The category of
k
-Hodge structures is equivalent to category of
k^{\prime}
-Hodge structures on modules of form
V_{k}\otimes k^{\prime}
with
k
-linear maps, where
V_{k}
are
k
-modules.

Proof.

Follows easily from the above Definition.
∎

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
V_{\mathbb{Z}}
be a
\mathbb{Z}
-Hodge structure of weight
1
. Denote by
\pi
the projection of
V_{\mathbb{C}}
onto
V^{1,0}
along
V^{0,1}
. Then the quotient
V^{1,0}/\pi(V_{\mathbb{Z}})
is a complex torus.

Proof.

Of course
\pi\colon V_{\mathbb{R}}\to V^{1,0}
is an isomorphism of
\mathbb{R}
-vector spaces. One has
\mathbb{R}\langle\pi(V_{\mathbb{Z}})\rangle=\pi(\mathbb{R}\langle V_{\mathbb{Z}}\rangle)=\pi(V_{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes\mathbb{R})=\pi(V_{\mathbb{R}})=V^{1,0}
. So
\pi(V_{\mathbb{Z}})
has full rank inside
V^{1,0}
considered as an
\mathbb{R}
-vector space, and the quotient is a compact torus. It inherits complex structure from
V^{1,0}
.
∎

Proposition 2.3
.

The category of complex vector spaces is equivalent to the category of
\mathbb{R}
-Hodge structures of weight
1
.

Proof.

An
\mathbb{R}
-Hodge structure of weight
1
is an
\mathbb{R}
-vector space
V_{\mathbb{R}}
together with a splitting
V_{\mathbb{C}}=V_{\mathbb{R}}\otimes\mathbb{C}=V^{1,0}\oplus V^{0,1}
such that
V^{0,1}=\overline{V^{1,0}}
. Define
I\in\End(V_{\mathbb{C}})
as
\sqrt{-1}\id_{V^{1,0}}\oplus(-\sqrt{-1})\id_{V^{0,1}}
. This operator is real, that is, preserves
V_{\mathbb{R}}=V_{\mathbb{C}}^{\Gal(\mathbb{C}:\mathbb{R})}
, and
I^{2}=-\id
. The other way around, any
I\in\End(V_{\mathbb{R}})
with
I^{2}=-\id
determines an
\mathbb{R}
-Hodge structure on
V_{\mathbb{R}}
by setting
V^{1,0}
to be the
\sqrt{-1}
-eigenspace of
I
.

It can be easily verified that maps of Hodge structure give rise to maps of vector spaces compatible with the complex structures operator, and vice versa.
∎

Proposition 2.4
.

Suppose
V_{\mathbb{R}}
is an
\mathbb{R}
-Hodge structure of weight
1
. Then the projection
V_{\mathbb{R}}=V_{\mathbb{C}}^{\Gal(\mathbb{C}:\mathbb{R})}\to V^{1,0}
is complex linear w. r. t. the complex structure on
V_{\mathbb{R}}
corresponding to the Hodge structure.

Proof.

The complex structure operator on
V_{\mathbb{R}}=\{v+\overline{v}\mid v\in V^{1,0}\}
is given by
I(v+\overline{v})=\sqrt{-1}(v-\overline{v})=\sqrt{-1}v+\overline{\sqrt{-1}v}
. Of course the projection maps
I(v+\overline{v})
to
\sqrt{-1}v
.
∎

Proposition 2.5
.

The category of complex tori is equivalent to the category of
\mathbb{Z}
-Hodge structures of weight
1
.

Proof.

The correspondence on objects in one direction is given by Proposition
2.2
. Let us construct it in the backward direction.

Let
X
be a complex torus. Then its universal cover
\widetilde{X}
is a complex vector space, and the kernel of the projection
\ker(\widetilde{X}\to X)
is a lattice which is of full rank if one consider
\widetilde{X}
as an
\mathbb{R}
-vector space, that is, a
\mathbb{Z}
-module
V_{\mathbb{Z}}
with complex structure operator on
V_{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes\mathbb{R}
. By Propositions
2.3
and
2.1
it is the same as
\mathbb{Z}
-Hodge structure of weight
1
. It follows from the Proposition
2.4
that these correspondences are mutually inverse.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a map of complex tori. Its lift to the universal cover
\widetilde{f}\colon\widetilde{X}\to\widetilde{Y}
is a
\mathbb{C}
-linear map, mapping the lattice
\ker(\widetilde{X}\to X)
to the lattice
\ker(\widetilde{Y}\to Y)
. By Propositions
2.3
and
2.1
this is a map of
\mathbb{Z}
-Hodge structures of weight
1
.
∎

Another, in fact equivalent way to obtain a weight
1

\mathbb{Z}
-Hodge structure from the complex torus, is to take its first homology with integral coefficients.

Definition 3
.

A surjective morphism of complex tori with finite kernel is called an
isogeny
.

Definition 4
.

For a category
C
and a class of morphisms
W
the
localization

C[W^{-1}]
is the category with the same class of objects and

\Hom_{C[W^{-1}]}(X,Y)=\{X\xleftarrow{f_{1}}Z_{1}\to Y_{1}\xleftarrow{f_{2}}Z_{2}\to\dots\xleftarrow{f_{n}}Z_{n}\to Y\mid f_{1},f_{2}\dots,f_{n}\in W\}

up to suitable equivalence with obvious composition.

Essential details of the definition of localization can be found in any text on categorical algebra, e. g.
[
KSch
]
. However, they are insufficient for our exposition.

Definition 5
.

Localization of the category of complex tori by isogenies is called the
category of complex tori up to isogeny
.

Proposition 2.6
.

The category of complex tori up to isogeny is equivalent to the category of
\mathbb{Q}
-Hodge structures of weight
1
.

Proof.

Any
\mathbb{Q}
-Hodge structure may be made
\mathbb{Z}
-Hodge structure by taking a base
\{v_{i}\}
of
V_{\mathbb{Q}}
and setting
V_{\mathbb{Z}}=\mathbb{Z}\langle\{v_{i}\}\rangle
. Hence the objects of this category can be identified with complex tori by Proposition
2.5
, although in a non-unique way. However, the isogeny class of a torus corresponding to
V_{\mathbb{Z}}
is independent on choise of the base. Indeed, tori corresponging to bases
\{v_{i}\}
and
\{Nv_{i}\}
, where
N
is a nonzero integer, are isogenous by construction, and any two bases of a
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space can be made to span the same lattice by multiplying them by appropriate integers. Moreover, any morphism of
\mathbb{Q}
-Hodge structures may be made a morphism of
\mathbb{Z}
-Hodge structures in this way.
∎

3
Hodge structures from CR geometric viewpoint

3.1
Reminder of CR and co-CR vector spaces

We start this section with a reminder of definitions in CR geometry. For a more detailed exposition, see e. g.
[
MOP
]
.

Definition 6
.

A
CR vector space

(V,H,I)
is an
\mathbb{R}
-vector space
V
together with a subspace
H\subseteq V
(called
distinguished complex subspace
) and an operator
I\colon H\to H
such that
I^{2}=-1
(called
CR structure operator
). A
co-CR vector space

(U,F,J)
is an
\mathbb{R}
-vector space with a subspace
F\subseteq U
(called
distinguished cocomplex subspace
) and an operator
J\colon U/F\to U/V
such that
J^{2}=-1
(called
co-CR structure operator
). The
(co-)CR linear map
of (co-)CR vector spaces is a linear map of underlying
\mathbb{R}
-vector spaces mapping distinguished (co)complex subspace to distinguished (co)complex subspace and whose restriction to distinguished complex subspace (map induced on quotients by distinguished cocomplex subspaces) is complex linear w. r. t. the (co-)CR structures operators.

Proposition 3.1
.

Suppose that
W
is a
\mathbb{C}
-vector space, and
V\subseteq W
is an
\mathbb{R}
-vector subspace. Then
V
carries a natural CR structure, and
W/V
carries a natural co-CR structure.

Proof.

The subspace
H=V\cap\sqrt{-1}V
is invariant under multiplication by
\sqrt{-1}
and is hence a
\mathbb{C}
-vector subspace in the space
V
. The pair
(V,H)
is a CR vector space.

Denote the projection of the space
\sqrt{-1}V
to
W/V
by
F
. Then the quotient
(W/V)/F
is isomorphic to
W/\mathbb{C}V
, hence carries a natural complex structure. The pair
(W/V,F)
is a co-CR vector space.
∎

The complex structure operator is characterized by its
\sqrt{-1}
-eigensubspace, which leads to another

Definition 7
.

A
CR vector space

(V,H^{1,0})
is an
\mathbb{R}
-vector space
V
together with a subspace
H^{1,0}\subseteq V\otimes\mathbb{C}
such that
H^{1,0}\cap\overline{H^{1,0}}=\{0\}
. A
co-CR vector space

(U,F^{1,0})
is an
\mathbb{R}
-vector space
U
together with a subspace
F^{1,0}\subseteq U\otimes\mathbb{C}
such that
\langle F^{1,0},\overline{F^{1,0}}\rangle=U\otimes\mathbb{C}
.

In the notation of the previous Definition,
H=(H^{1,0}\oplus\overline{H^{1,0}})^{\Gal(\mathbb{C}:\mathbb{R})}
and
F=F^{1,0}\cup\overline{F^{1,0}}
. These two definitions of CR structures are obviously equivalent; to see that the definitions of co-CR structures are equivalent, note that
F^{1,0}
may be reconstructed as a preimage of the subspace
(U/F)^{1,0}\subseteq(U/F)\otimes\mathbb{C}
.

We may see from this Definition that any CR or co-CR structure is isomorphic to an example from Proposition
3.1
, however, strictly speaking, in a non-unique way.

3.2
Bi-CR structures and Hodge structures of weight two

Of course, if
(V,V^{1,0})
is both CR and co-CR structure, then
V\otimes\mathbb{C}=V^{1,0}\oplus\overline{V^{1,0}}
, and this is just a complex structure. But there exists a more appropriate notion of compatibility of a CR and a co-CR structure on the same
\mathbb{R}
-vector space.

Definition 8
.

We call by a
bi-CR vector space

(V,H,F,I,J)
a pair of CR structure
(V,H,I)
and co-CR structure
(V,F,J)
such that
H\oplus F=V
is a direct sum decomposition and the projection
V/F\to H
is complex linear w. r. t. the complex structure
J
on the left and
I
on the right. Alternatively, a
bi-CR vector space

(V,H^{1,0},F^{1,0})
is a pair of CR structure
(V,H^{1,0})
and co-CR structure
(V,F^{1,0})
such that
H^{1,0}\subseteq F^{1,0}
and
F^{1,0}\cap\overline{H^{1,0}}=0
.

Proposition 3.2
.

The category of bi-CR vector spaces is equivalent to the category of
\mathbb{R}
-Hodge structure of weight
2
.

Proof.

Let
V
be an
\mathbb{R}
-Hodge structure of weight 2:
V\otimes\mathbb{C}=V^{2,0}\oplus V^{1,1}\oplus V^{0,2}
. Then
(V,V^{2,0},V^{2,0}\oplus V^{1,1})
is a bi-CR structure. The other way around, if
(V,H,F)
is a bi-CR structure, then
V\otimes\mathbb{C}=H^{1,0}\oplus(F\otimes\mathbb{C})\oplus\overline{H^{1,0}}
is an
\mathbb{R}
-Hodge structure of weight 2. It can be easily seen that these correspondences are compatible with maps.
∎

Similarly, to a
\mathbb{Z}
-Hodge structure of weight 2
V_{\mathbb{Z}}
one can associate a torus with left invariant bi-CR structure
A_{V_{\mathbb{Z}}}=V_{\mathbb{R}}/V_{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Definition 9
.

Let
V_{\mathbb{Z}}
be a
\mathbb{Z}
-Hodge structure of weight 2. We call the quotient
A_{V_{\mathbb{Z}}}=V_{\mathbb{R}}/V_{\mathbb{Z}}
with canonical left invariant bi-CR structure an
Abel torus
of
V_{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Proposition 3.3
.

The category of bi-CR tori is equivalent to the category of
\mathbb{Z}
-Hodge structures of weight 2.

Proof.

Much like the proof of the Proposition
2.5
, this proof follows from Propositions
3.2
and
2.1
.
∎

3.3
The canonical embedding

Definition 10
.

Let
V_{\mathbb{R}}
,
V_{\mathbb{R}}\otimes\mathbb{C}=V^{2,0}\oplus V^{1,1}\oplus V^{0,2}
be an
\mathbb{R}
-Hodge structure of weight 2. Denote by
\varpi
the projection
V^{2,0}\oplus V^{0,2}\to V^{2,0}
, and by
\imath
the embedding
(V^{1,1})^{\Gal(\mathbb{C}:\mathbb{R})}\to V^{1,1}
. We call the map

\gamma=\varpi|_{(V^{2,0}\oplus V^{0,2})^{\Gal(\mathbb{C}:\mathbb{R})}}\oplus\imath\colon(V^{2,0}\oplus V^{0,2})^{\Gal(\mathbb{C}:\mathbb{R})}\oplus(V^{1,1})^{\Gal(\mathbb{C}:\mathbb{R})}=V_{\mathbb{R}}\to V^{2,0}\oplus V^{1,1}

the
canonical embedding
.

Proposition 3.4
.

The canonical embedding is CR linear w. r. t. the usual complex structure on
V^{2,0}\oplus V^{1,1}
considered as CR structure.

Proof.

The proof is more or less straightforward. The distinguished complex subspace of the CR space
V_{\mathbb{R}}
is the subspace
(V^{2,0}\oplus V^{0,2})^{\Gal(\mathbb{C}:\mathbb{R})}
and consists of vectors
v+\overline{v}
for
v\in V^{2,0}
(moreover, each vector in the distinguished complex subspace of
V_{\mathbb{R}}
can be written in such way uniquely). The CR structure operator is given by
I(v+\overline{v})=\sqrt{-1}(v-\overline{v})
. The projection
\varpi
maps
v+\overline{v}
to
v\in V^{2,0}
, and
Iv
to
\sqrt{-1}v
. Hence the sum
\varpi\oplus\imath
is CR linear.
∎

Proposition 3.5
.

Consider the induced CR structure on the space
\gamma(V_{\mathbb{R}})\subseteq V^{2,0}\oplus V^{1,1}
. W. r. t. this CR structure on the image, the canonical embedding is a CR isomorphism.

Proof.

One has
\gamma(V_{\mathbb{R}})\cap\sqrt{-1}\gamma(V_{\mathbb{R}})=(V^{2,0}\oplus(V^{1,1})^{\Gal(\mathbb{C}:\mathbb{R})})\cap(\sqrt{-1}V^{2,0}\oplus\sqrt{-1}(V^{1,1})^{\Gal(\mathbb{C}:\mathbb{R})})=(V^{2,0}\cap\sqrt{-1}V^{2,0})\oplus(V^{2,0}\cap\sqrt{-1}(V^{1,1})^{\Gal(\mathbb{C}:\mathbb{R})})\oplus((V^{1,1})^{\Gal(\mathbb{C}:\mathbb{R})}\cap\sqrt{-1}V^{2,0})\oplus((V^{1,1})^{\Gal(\mathbb{C}:\mathbb{R})}\cap\sqrt{-1}(V^{1,1})^{\Gal(\mathbb{C}:\mathbb{R})})
. The first summand equals
V^{2,0}
as it is complex vector subspace. The second and third summand vanish, because
V^{2,0}\cap V^{1,1}=\{0\}
by definition of Hodge structure. The fourth summand vanishes because for any vector
u
in this summand one has
u=-\sqrt{-1}\sqrt{-1}u=-\sqrt{-1}\overline{\sqrt{-1}u}=-\sqrt{-1}(-\sqrt{-1})\overline{u}=-\overline{u}=-u
. Therefore the distinguished complex subspace in
\gamma(V_{\mathbb{R}})
in induced CR structure equals
V^{2,0}
. However, the image of distinguished complex subspace in
V_{\mathbb{R}}
is precisely
V^{2,0}
by the previous Proposition.
∎

4
Hodge Cousin groups

The Abel tori, defined in the previous Section, are generalizations of Abel–Jacobi varieties for
\mathbb{Z}
-Hodge structures of weight 1 to
\mathbb{Z}
-Hodge structures of weight 2. In the present section we propose another generalization of Abel–Jacobi varieties, which works for
\mathbb{Z}
-Hodge structures of any weight.

Definition 11
.

Let
V_{\mathbb{Z}}
be a
\mathbb{Z}
-Hodge structure of weight
n
. We call
Jacobi group
the complex Lie group

J_{V_{\mathbb{Z}}}=\frac{\oplus_{p\leq q}V^{p,q}}{\pi(V_{\mathbb{Z}})},

where
\pi
denotes the projection of
V_{\mathbb{C}}
onto
\oplus_{p\leq q}V^{p,q}
along the subspace
\oplus_{p>q}V^{p,q}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
W_{\mathbb{Z}}
be a weight 0
\mathbb{Z}
-Hodge structure. Then
J_{W_{\mathbb{Z}}}
is isomorphic to a product of several copies of
\GL(1,\mathbb{C})
.

Proof.

One has
W^{0,0}=W_{\mathbb{C}}=W_{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes\mathbb{C}
. Pick up an integral basis
w_{i}
for the lattice
W_{\mathbb{Z}}
, it determines a decomposition
W^{0,0}=\oplus_{i}\mathbb{C}w_{i}
. Now one has
J_{W_{\mathbb{Z}}}=\frac{\oplus_{p\leq q}W^{p,q}}{\pi(W_{\mathbb{Z}})}=\frac{W^{0,0}}{W_{\mathbb{Z}}}=\frac{\oplus_{i}\mathbb{C}w_{i}}{\oplus_{i}\mathbb{Z}w_{i}}=\oplus_{i}(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z})=\oplus_{i}\GL(1,\mathbb{C})
.
∎

Definition 12

(L. Ornea, M. Verbitsky, V. Vuletescu
[
OVV
]
)
.

The Cousin group which is of the form
J_{V_{\mathbb{Z}}}
for some
\mathbb{Z}
-Hodge structure
V_{\mathbb{Z}}
is called
Hodge Cousin group
.

Proposition 4.2
.

The complex Lie group
J_{V_{\mathbb{Z}}}
is Cousin group if and only if the Hodge structure
V_{\mathbb{Z}}
does not admit a nontrivial quotient Hodge structure of weight
0
.

Proof.

Suppose that
J_{V_{\mathbb{Z}}}
is a Cousin group. Let
V_{\mathbb{Z}}\to W_{\mathbb{Z}}
be a surjective map onto a
\mathbb{Z}
-Hodge structure of weight
0
. Then the induced homomorphism of complex Lie groups
J_{V_{\mathbb{Z}}}\to J_{W_{\mathbb{Z}}}=\oplus_{i}\GL(1,\mathbb{C})
, determines, by Proposition
4.1
, a collection of characters of
J_{V_{\mathbb{Z}}}
. By Propostion
1.1
all representations of the Cousin group
J_{V_{\mathbb{Z}}}
are trivial, hence the
\mathbb{Z}
-Hodge structure
W_{\mathbb{Z}}
is trivial.

Suppose that
J_{V_{\mathbb{Z}}}
is not a Cousin group. Since it is commutative, by Proposition
1.4
, it admits a nontrivial character
J_{V_{\mathbb{Z}}}\to\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}
. The lift of this map to the universal cover is a surjective map
V_{\mathbb{C}}\to\mathbb{C}
. Its restriction to
V_{\mathbb{Z}}\subset V_{\mathbb{C}}
is a surjective map of
V_{\mathbb{Z}}
to
\mathbb{Z}\subset\mathbb{C}
a
\mathbb{Z}
-Hodge structure of weight
0
.
∎

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
V_{\mathbb{Z}}
be a
\mathbb{Z}
-Hodge structure of weight 2. Then the canonical embedding
\gamma
descends to an embedding of the Abel torus
A_{V_{\mathbb{Z}}}
to the Jacobi group
J_{V_{\mathbb{Z}}}
.

Proof.

Let
v\in V_{\mathbb{Z}}
be a vector considered as a vector in
V_{\mathbb{R}}
. It can be uniquely written as a sum
v^{\prime}+w
, where
v^{\prime}\in(V^{2,0}\oplus V^{0,2})^{\Gal(\mathbb{C}:\mathbb{R})}
and
w\in(V^{1,1})^{\Gal(\mathbb{C}:\mathbb{R})}
. It can be further written as
u+\overline{u}+w
, where
u\in V^{2,0}
. One has
\gamma(v)=u+w
. On the other hand,
u+w
is precisely the
\left((2,0)+(1,1)\right)
-component of the vector
v
considered as a vector in
V_{\mathbb{C}}
, that is,
\pi(v)
. Hence
\gamma(V_{\mathbb{Z}})=\pi(V_{\mathbb{Z}})
, and the map
\gamma
descends to the quotients by
V_{\mathbb{Z}}
and
\pi(V_{\mathbb{Z}})
, respectively.
∎

Proposition 4.4
.

The image
\gamma(A_{V_{\mathbb{Z}}})
is the maximal compact subgroup of
J_{V_{\mathbb{Z}}}
.

Proof.

The real dimension of maximal compact subgroup in
J_{V_{\mathbb{Z}}}
equals to
\dim V_{\mathbb{R}}
, just like the dimension of
A_{V_{\mathbb{Z}}}=\gamma(A_{V_{\mathbb{Z}}})
. On the other hand,
\gamma(A_{V_{\mathbb{Z}}})
is compact.
∎

Proposition 4.5
.

The maximal compact subgroup of the complex Lie group
J_{V_{\mathbb{Z}}}
with the induced CR structure on it is CR isomorphic to the bi-CR group
A_{V_{\mathbb{Z}}}
.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.4
, the maximal compact subgroup of
J_{V_{\mathbb{Z}}}
is the subgroup
\gamma(A_{V_{\mathbb{Z}}})
. The induced CR structure on it is isomorphic (via
\gamma
) to the CR structure on
A_{V_{\mathbb{Z}}}
by Proposition
3.5
.
∎

Suppose that
G
is a Cousin group. If it is Hodge Cousin group of some
\mathbb{Z}
-Hodge structure of weight 2
V_{\mathbb{Z}}
, then the summands
V^{2,0}
and
V^{0,2}
can be reconstructed from its complex structure alone: namely, the maximal compact subgroup
G_{c}\subseteq G
carries a CR structure, which rises to a (linear) CR structure on its universal cover
\widetilde{G_{c}}
. If
H\subseteq\widetilde{G_{c}}
is its distinguished complex subspace, then there exists an isomorphism
\widetilde{G_{c}}\otimes\mathbb{C}\to V_{\mathbb{C}}
which identifies
H^{1,0}
with
V^{2,0}
. This observation leads to the following

Problem
.

Describe the Hodge Cousin groups among the Cousin groups in terms of their complex Lie group structure.

5.1
Reminder on abelian varieties

Definition 13
.

A
polarization
on the
k
-Hodge structure of weight
n

V
is a
k
-valued bilinear form
Q
on
V_{k}
such that

(i)

Q(v,u)=(-1)^{n}Q(u,v)
,

(ii)

Q(u,v)=0
for
u\in V^{p,q}
,
v\in V^{r,s}
with
p\neq s
,
q\neq r
,

(iii)

\sqrt{-1}^{p-q}Q(u,\overline{u})>0
for nonzero
u\in V^{p,q}
.

Maps between polarized Hodge structures are orthogonal maps of underlying Hodge structures.

Let me remind some well-known facts.

Proposition 5.1
.

The category of polarized
\mathbb{R}
-Hodge structures of weight
1
is equivalent to the category of Hermitian vector spaces.

Proof.

For an
\mathbb{R}
-Hodge structure of weight 1
V
(which is by Proposition
2.3
the same as a complex structure operator on
V_{\mathbb{R}}
) a polarization
Q
is skew-symmetric. If
u,v\in V^{1,0}
, then one has
Q(I(u+\overline{u}),I(v+\overline{v}))=Q(\sqrt{-1}(u-\overline{u}),\sqrt{-1}(v-\overline{v}))=-Q(u-\overline{u},v-\overline{v})=Q(u,\overline{v})+Q(\overline{u},v)=Q(u+\overline{u},v+\overline{v})
, that is,
I
is orthogonal w. r. t.
Q
. If we define
g(x,y)=Q(Ix,y)
for
x,y\in V_{\mathbb{R}}
, then
g(y,x)=Q(Iy,x)=-Q(x,Iy)=-Q(Ix,I^{2}y)=-Q(Ix,-y)=Q(Ix,y)=g(x,y)
, go
g
is symmetric. Moreover, for
0\neq x=u+\overline{u}
,
u\in V^{1,0}
one has
g(x,x)=Q(I(u+\overline{u}),u+\overline{u})=Q(\sqrt{-1}(u-\overline{u}),u+\overline{u})=\sqrt{-1}(Q(u,\overline{u})-Q(\overline{u},u))=2\sqrt{-1}Q(u,\overline{u})>0
, so the form
g
is positive definite, and the form
h=g+\sqrt{-1}Q
is Hermitian.

The other way around, imaginary part of any Hermitian form gives rise to a polarization on the correspondent weight
1
Hodge structure.
∎

Definition 14
.

A complex torus with a left invariant Kähler form
\omega
is called an
abelian variety
, if
[\omega]
is an integral cohomology class.

By Kodaira’s embedding theorem, it is the same that a projective complex torus.

Proposition 5.2
.

The category of polarized
\mathbb{Z}
-Hodge structures of weight
1
is equivalent to the category of abelian varieties.

Proof.

By Propositions
2.5
and
5.1
, the category of polarized
\mathbb{Z}
-Hodge structures of weight
1
is equivalent to the category of complex tori with left-invariant Hermitian metric satisfying certain integrality conditions.

Consider a Hermitian torus
(X,\omega)
which is correspondent to a polarized
\mathbb{Z}
-Hodge structure of weight
2

V
. The
2
-form
\omega
is parallel w. r. t. Levi-Civita connection and is hence closed, so
(X,\omega)
a Kähler torus. We need to show that the Kähler class
[\omega]
is integral. For any two vectors
u,v\in V_{\mathbb{Z}}
consider an oriented parallelogram spanned by them in
V_{\mathbb{R}}
, and denote by
c_{u,v}
the
2
-cycle in
X
, which is the projection of this parallelogram into
X=V_{\mathbb{R}}/V_{\mathbb{Z}}
. Homology classes of such cycles
[c_{u,v}]
generate the group
H_{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
. On the other hand, it is easy to see that
\int_{c_{u,v}}\omega=Q(u,v)
. As the form
Q
is integral, the class
[\omega]
is also integral, and
(X,\omega)
is an abelian variety.
∎

Proposition 5.3
.

The category of polarized
\mathbb{Q}
-Hodge structures of weight
1
is equivalent to the category of abelian varieties up to isogeny.

Proof.

This is a combination of Proposition
2.6
and
5.2
.
∎

Proposition 5.4

(H. Poincaré, 1886
[
P
]
)
.

The category of abelian varieties up to isogeny is semisimple.

Proof.

The category of
\mathbb{Q}
-Hodge structures of weight
1
is semisimple because one may take orthogonals w. r. t.
Q
. However, by Proposition
5.3
this category is equivalent to the category of abelian varieties up to isogeny.
∎

This Poincaré complete reducibility theorem, stated in less fancy terms, says that for any abelian subvariety
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
inside the abelian variety
A
there exists an abelian variety
A^{\prime\prime}\subseteq A
such that
A
is isogenous to
A^{\prime}\times A^{\prime\prime}
.

5.2
Abelian Cousin groups

Now we want to generalize these facts to the Hodge structures of weight
2
.

Definition 15
.

We call a pair
((V,H,F,I,J),g)
consisting of a bi-CR vector space
(V,H,F,I,J)
and a pseudo-Euclidean metric
g
on
V

CR Hermitian
, if

(i)

g|_{H}
is negative definite and
I
-invariant,

(ii)

g|_{F}
is positive definite;

(iii)

H\perp^{g}F
.

Proposition 5.5
.

The category of polarized
\mathbb{R}
-Hodge structure of weight
2
is equivalent to the category of CR Hermitian vector spaces.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.2
, the bi-CR vector spaces and
\mathbb{R}
-Hodge structures of weight
2
are the same. Let us examine what new structure comes up on bi-CR vector space
(V,H,F)
along with polarization
Q
on the corresponding Hodge structure
V_{\mathbb{C}}=V^{2,0}\oplus V^{1,1}\oplus V^{0,2}
. This is certainly some bilinear form on the underlying
\mathbb{R}
-vector space
V=V_{\mathbb{R}}
, so let us denote by the same letter
Q
.

First,
Q
is symmetric since
Q(x,y)=(-1)^{2}Q(y,x)
.

Second, for
x,y\in H
, where
x=u+\overline{u}
,
y=v+\overline{v}
for
u,v\in V^{2,0}
, one has
Q(Ix,Iy)=Q(\sqrt{-1}(u-\overline{u}),\sqrt{-1}(v-\overline{v}))=-Q(u-\overline{u},v-\overline{v})=-(-Q(u,\overline{v})-Q(\overline{u},v))=Q(u,\overline{v})+Q(\overline{u},v)=Q(u+\overline{u},v+\overline{v})=Q(x,y)
. If
x\neq 0
, then one has
Q(x,x)=Q(u+\overline{u},u+\overline{u})=Q(u,\overline{u})+Q(\overline{u},u)=2Q(u,\overline{u})<0
. If
0\neq x\in F=(V^{1,1})^{\Gal(\mathbb{C}:\mathbb{R})}
, then
Q(x,x)=\sqrt{-1}^{1-1}Q(x,x)>0
.

Finally,
H
and
F
are perpendicular because
V^{2,0}\oplus V^{0,2}
and
V^{1,1}
are.
∎

As we want to speak about pseudo-Riemannian manifolds, we need to introduce an invariant which would replace the usual notion of length of the curve.

Definition 16
.

Let
(X,g)
be a pseudo-Riemannian manifold and
\gamma\colon[0;1]\to X
be a curve. Then we call the value
\int_{0}^{1}g(d\gamma(\partial_{t}),d\gamma(\partial_{t}))dt
as
action
of
\gamma
.

This terminology is borrowed from classical mechanics, see e. g.
[
B
]
.

Definition 17
.

Let
(X,g)
be a pseudo-Riemannian manifold. We call it
polarized
, if the action of any closed geodesic on it is a rational number.

For bi-CR tori an equivalent definition of polarization was given in the paper
[
CT
]
by P. Caressa and A. Tomassini.

Proposition 5.6
.

The category of polarized
\mathbb{Q}
-Hodge structures of weight
2
is equivalent to the category of left-invariant polarized CR Hermitian tori up to isogeny.

Proof.

By Propositions
5.5
,
3.3
and
2.6
the category of polarized
\mathbb{Q}
-Hodge structures of weight
2
is equivalent to the category of left-invariant CR Hermitian tori up to isogeny with some integrality conditions satisfied. Let us figure out what these conditions are.

Given a polarized
\mathbb{Q}
-Hodge structure of weight
2

V_{\mathbb{Q}}
, fix some polarized
\mathbb{Z}
-Hodge structure of weight
2

V_{\mathbb{Z}}
such that
V_{\mathbb{Q}}=V_{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes\mathbb{Q}
. Denote the corresponding pseudo-Riemannian metric on the torus
A_{V_{\mathbb{Z}}}
by
g
. The value of the symmetric form
Q
is determined by the pseudo-norm of vectors on the lattice
V_{\mathbb{Z}}
w. r. t.
Q
, and
Q
is rational if and only if all the values
Q(v,v)
are. The projection of a segment
[0,v]\subset V_{\mathbb{R}}
to
A_{V_{\mathbb{Z}}}=V_{\mathbb{R}}/V_{\mathbb{Z}}
is a closed geodesic, and square of its length w. r. t. the pseudo-Riemannian metric
g
equals
Q(v,v)
. Hence the desired integrality condition is the rationality of squares of lengths of all closed geodesics.
∎

This Proposition may be used for a nice derivation of description of the moduli space of polarized CR Hermitian tori given in
[
CT
]
: indeed, it is the same as the well-known moduli space of polarized Hodge structures.

CR vector spaces arise as real subspaces in complex, and the following Definition asserts that CR Hermitian vector spaces arise as real subspaces in Hermitian.

Definition 18
.

For an
\mathbb{R}
-vector subspace
V\subseteq U
of a Hermitian vector space
(U,h)
we call the CR Hermitian structure
g
defined by
g|_{V\cap\sqrt{-1}V}=-\mathrm{Re}\penalty\ h|_{V\cap\sqrt{-1}V}
and
g|_{(V\cap\sqrt{-1}V)^{\perp}}=\mathrm{Re}\penalty\ h|_{(V\cap\sqrt{-1}V)^{\perp}}
as the
induced CR Hermitian structure
. If
Y\subseteq X
is a
C^{\infty}
-submanifold in an almost Hermitian manifold
(X,h)
, then we also call the almost CR Hermitian structure on
Y
given at each point
y\in Y
as the induced CR Hermitian structure on
T_{y}Y\subseteq T_{y}X
as the
induced almost CR Hermitian structure
.

Proposition 5.7
.

Let
V\subseteq U
be an
\mathbb{R}
-vector subspace of a
\mathbb{C}
-vector space
U
, and
H
be the induced CR structure. Then for any CR Hermitian structure
(V,H,F,I,J,g)
extending the CR structure
(V,H)
there exists a Hermitian structure on
U
which induces this CR Hermitian structure on
V
.

Proof.

By definition of a bi-CR structure,
\sqrt{-1}F\subset U
is an
\mathbb{R}
-complement to
V
inside
U
. Then we can define a symmetric
\mathbb{R}
-bilinear form
\widetilde{g}
on
U
by
\widetilde{g}|_{H}=-g|_{H}
,
\widetilde{g}|_{F}=g|_{F}
and for
v\in\sqrt{-1}F
set
\widetilde{g}(v)=g(-\sqrt{-1}v)
. This is a real part of a Hermitian structure which obviously induces on
V
the CR Hermitian structure
g
.
∎

Proposition 5.8
.

For a Hodge Cousin group
J_{V_{\mathbb{Z}}}
of a polarized
\mathbb{Z}
-Hodge structure of weight
2

V_{\mathbb{Z}}
there exist a Hermitian metric on
J_{V_{\mathbb{Z}}}
such that the induced CR Hermitian metric on the maximal compact subgroup
A_{V_{\mathbb{Z}}}\subseteq J_{V_{\mathbb{Z}}}
coinicides with the structure described by Proposition
5.6
.

Proof.

The wanted Hermitian metric on the tangent space
T_{e}J_{V_{\mathbb{Z}}}
is given by Proposition
5.7
. Then it can be extended to all the group
J_{V_{\mathbb{Z}}}
via the right shifts.
∎

Of course in this case actions of closed geodesics w. r. t. induced CR Hermitian metric on the subgroup
A_{V_{\mathbb{Z}}}\subseteq J_{V_{\mathbb{Z}}}
are rational. This motivates us to give the following

Definition 19
.

We call an
abelian Cousin group
a Cousin group
G
with left invariant Hermitian metric such that the maximal compact subgroup
G_{c}
with its induced CR Hermitian structure is a polarized pseudo-Riemannian manifold.

Thus we have a particular answer to the Question from the Section 4 of the present paper.

Proposition 5.9
.

Any abelian Cousin group is Hodge Cousin group of a certain
\mathbb{Z}
-Hodge structure of weight
2
.

Proof.

First reconstruct the
\mathbb{R}
-Hodge structure from the Cousin group
G
. As it was already stated in the Section 4, one needs to take
V_{\mathbb{R}}=T_{e}G_{c}
, where
G_{c}\subseteq G
is the maximal compact subgroup,
V^{2,0}=H^{1,0}
, where
H\subseteq T_{e}G_{c}
is the distinguished complex subspace for a CR strcuture on
G_{c}\subseteq G
, and
V^{0,2}=\overline{V^{2,0}}
. The subspace
V^{1,1}
can be reconstructed as the complexification of the orthogonal to
H\subseteq T_{e}G_{c}
w. r. t. the pseudo-Riemannian metric on
G_{c}
.

Now note that
V_{\mathbb{R}}
is the universal cover of
G_{c}
, and its kernel is the lattice. Call it
V_{\mathbb{Z}}
. Then
(V_{\mathbb{Z}},Q,V_{\mathbb{C}}=V^{2,0}\oplus V^{1,1}\oplus V^{0,2})
is a polarized
\mathbb{Z}
-Hodge structure of weight
2
. Denote the restriction of the pseudo-Euclidean form on
T_{e}G_{c}
to
V_{\mathbb{Z}}
by
Q
. The integrality condition on the Hermitian form on
G
implies that
Q
is an integral form, and defines a polarization on the Hodge structure
V_{\mathbb{Z}}
.

One can easily check that
A_{V_{\mathbb{Z}}}=G_{c}
and
J_{V_{\mathbb{Z}}}=G
.
∎

Note that by Proposition
4.2
the constructed Hodge structure
V_{\mathbb{Z}}
does not admit a quotient of weight
0
.

Proposition 5.10
.

The category of polarized
\mathbb{Q}
-Hodge structures of weight
2
without quotients of weight
0
is equivalent to the category of abelian Cousin groups up to isogeny.

Proof.

This is a combination of Propositions
5.9
and
4.2
.
∎

Proposition 5.11

(Poincaré complete reducibility theorem for Cousin groups)
.

The category of abelian Cousin groups up to isogeny is semisimple.

Proof.

By Proposition
5.10
, it is equivalent to a full subcategory of
\mathbb{Q}
-Hodge structures category consisting of polarized
\mathbb{Q}
-Hodge structures of weight
2
which do not admit a quotient of weight
0
. The category of polarized
\mathbb{Q}
-Hodge structures is itself semisimple, so one needs to check that sum of polarized
\mathbb{Q}
-Hodge structures of weight
2
without weight
0
quotients do not admit weight
0
quotients itself. Indeed, if
W
is a weight
0
Hodge structure and
0\neq\Hom(U\oplus V,W)=\Hom(U,W)\oplus\Hom(V,W)
, then either
\Hom(U,W)
or
\Hom(V,W)
needs to be nonzero.
∎

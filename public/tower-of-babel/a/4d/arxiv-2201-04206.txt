0.1
Introduction

Measurements of the
\PW^{\pm}
and
\PZ
boson production at hadron colliders are important tests of the higher order perturbative quantum chromodynamics (QCD) and electroweak radiative corrections. Precise determination of the total production cross sections and their ratios provides constraints to parton distribution functions (PDFs) of the proton. Theoretical predictions of the
\PW^{\pm}
and
\PZ
boson production total cross sections are available at next-to-next-to-leading order (NNLO) accuracy in QCD
[
1
,
2
]
. The electroweak corrections are available at next-to-leading-order accuracy (NLO)
[
3
,
4
,
5
]
.

This paper describes the measurements of the inclusive
\PW^{\pm}
and
\PZ
boson total production cross section times leptonic branching ratios at the CERN LHC with CMS detector
[
6
]
. The gauge bosons are observed via their decays to electrons and muons with a clean experimental signature. The
\cPZ/\gamma^{*}\to\ell^{+}\ell^{-}
process, where
\ell
is a muon or an electron, is referred to as the
\cPZ
boson process in this note.

The measurements are performed in proton-proton (
\Pp\Pp
) collisions at
\sqrt{s}=5.02
and 13
\TeV
collected at the end of 2017 and correspond to an integrated luminosities of
291\pbinv
and
200\pbinv
, respectively. The peak delivered instantaneous luminosities were
1.3\times 10^{33}
cm
-2
s
-1
and
2.5\times 10^{33}
cm
-2
s
-1
with an average of 2 and 3
\Pp\Pp
interactions per bunch crossing at
\sqrt{s}=5.02
and 13
\TeV
, respectively. The measurements of ratios of
\PW^{\pm}
to
\PZ
and
\PW^{+}
to
\PW^{-}
production cross sections as well as double ratios at different
\sqrt{s}
are performed.

Inclusive
\PW^{\pm}
and
\PZ
boson production total and fiducial cross sections and their ratios were previously measured by the by the ATLAS, CMS, and
LHCb Collaborations in
\Pp\Pp
collisions at
\sqrt{s}=2.76
,
5.02
,
7
,
8
,
and 13
\TeV
at the LHC

0.2
The CMS detector

The central feature of the CMS apparatus is a superconducting solenoid
of 6
\unit
m internal diameter, providing a magnetic field of
3.8
\unit
T. Within the solenoid volume there are a silicon pixel and strip
tracker, a lead tungstate crystal electromagnetic calorimeter (ECAL),
and a brass and scintillator hadron calorimeter, each composed
of a barrel and two endcap sections. Forward calorimeters extend the
\eta

coverage provided by the barrel and endcap detectors. Muons are detected in gas-ionization
detectors embedded in the steel flux-return yoke outside the solenoid. A more detailed
description of the CMS detector, together with a definition of the coordinate system used
and the relevant kinematic variables, can be found in Ref.
[
6
]
.

The first level of the CMS trigger system, composed of custom hardware processors, uses
information from the calorimeters and muon detectors to select events of interest in a
fixed time interval of less than 4
\mus
. The second level, known as the high-level trigger,
consists of a farm of processors running a version of the full event reconstruction
software optimized for fast processing, and reduces the event rate to
\mathcal{O}
(1
\unit
kHz)
before data storage
[
19
]
.

0.3
Event simulation

The
\PW^{\pm}
and
\PZ
signal samples at 5.02 and 13
\TeV
are simulated using
\MADGRAPH
5_a
\MCATNLO
2.3.3
[
20
]
at next-to-leading order (NLO) with up to two outgoing partons at Born level merged with the parton shower description using the
FxFx
scheme
[
21
]
. The signal samples are also generated at NLO with
\POWHEG
2.0
[
22
,
23
,
24
,
25
]
, where the
MiNLO
procedure is used for the
\PZ
sample
[
26
]
. The background samples are simulated with
\POWHEG
2.0.

The
\PYTHIA
8.230
[
27
]
package is used for parton showering, hadronization, and the underlying event simulation, with tune CP5
[
28
,
29
]
. The NNPDF 3.1
[
30
]
set is used as the default set of parton distribution functions (PDFs). The PDFs are calculated to the same order in QCD as the hard process.

The detector response is simulated using a detailed description of the CMS detector based on the
\GEANTfour
package
[
31
]
, and event reconstruction is performed with the same algorithms used for data. Additional
\Pp\Pp
interactions (pileup) occurring in the same beam crossing as the event of interest are included in the simulation.

0.4
Event reconstruction

The particle-flow algorithm
[
32
]
reconstructs and identifies each individual particle in an event, with an optimized combination of all subdetector information. The individual particles are identified as charged and neutral hadrons, leptons, and photons. The missing transverse momentum,
\ptvecmiss
, is defined as the magnitude of the negative vector
\pt
sum of all reconstructed particles in the event. Its magnitude is denoted by
\ptmiss
.

The reconstructed vertex with the largest value of summed physics-object
\pt^{2}
is the primary
\Pp\Pp
interaction vertex. The physics objects are the jets, clustered using the jet-finding algorithm
[
33
,
34
]
with the tracks assigned to the vertex as inputs, and the associated missing transverse momentum, the negative vector sum of the
\pt
of those jets.

Muons are reconstructed by associating a track reconstructed in the inner silicon detectors with a track in the muon system. Selected muon candidates are required to satisfy a set of quality requirements based on the number of spatial measurements in the silicon tracker and the muon system, as well as the fit quality of the combined muon track
[
35
,
36
]
.

Electrons are reconstructed by associating a track reconstructed in the inner silicon detectors with a cluster of energy in ECAL
[
37
]
. The selected electron candidates cannot originate from photon conversions in the inner silicon tracker material and must satisfy a set of quality requirements based on the shower shape of the energy deposit in the ECAL.

The lepton (electrons or muons) candidate tracks must be consistent with the primary vertex of the event
[
38
]
to suppress electron candidates from photon conversions and lepton candidates originating from decays of heavy quarks. The lepton candidates must be isolated from other particles in the event. The relative isolation for the lepton candidates with transverse momentum
\pt^{\ell}
is defined as

R_{\text{iso}}=\bigg[\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{charged}\\
\text{hadrons}\end{subarray}}\!\!\pt\,+\,\max\big(0,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{neutral}\\
\text{hadrons}\end{subarray}}\!\!\pt\,+\,\sum_{\text{photons}}\!\!\pt\,-\,\pt^{\mathrm{PU}}\big)\bigg]\bigg/\pt^{\ell},

(1)

where the sums run over the charged and neutral hadrons and photons in a
cone defined by

\Delta R\equiv\sqrt{\smash[b]{\left(\Delta\eta\right)^{2}+\left(\Delta\phi\right)^{2}}}=0.4
(0.3)
around the muon (electron) trajectory, and
\pt^{\mathrm{PU}}
denotes the contribution of neutral particles from pileup
[
35
,
37
]
. Only charged hadrons originating from the primary vertex are included in the first sum. Muon candidates with
R_{\mathrm{iso}}<0.15
are considered isolated. Electron candidates with
R_{\mathrm{iso}}<0.05+0.51/\pt
(
0.07+0.96/\pt
) in the ECAL barrel (endcap) region are considered isolated.

0.5
Event selection

Leptonic
\PW
boson decays are characterized by a prompt, energetic, and
isolated charged lepton and a neutrino giving rise to significant
\ptmiss
. The
\cPZ
boson decays to leptons are selected by requiring two energetic and isolated leptons of the same flavor and opposite charge.

The events are triggered by the presence of at least one muon with
\pt>17\GeV
and
|\eta|<2.4
, or at least one electron with transverse energy
\ET>17\GeV
(
\ET>20\GeV
) and
|\eta|<2.5
at
5.02\TeV
(
13\TeV
). The triggered electron candidates satisfy less restrictive isolation and quality requirements than the offline selection criteria.

The fiducial region for the
\PZ
boson kinematic acceptance is defined by a common set of kinematic selections applied to both the
\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
and
\Pe^{+}\Pe^{-}
final states at generator level, emulating the selection performed at the reconstruction level. Leptons are required to have
\pt>25\GeV
and
\abs{\eta}<2.4
, and a dilepton invariant mass
60<m_{\ell\ell}<120\GeV
.

The
\PW^{\pm}
boson fiducial region is defined by the charged lepton kinematic requirements with
\pt>25\GeV
and
\abs{\eta}<2.4
and a requirement on the
\PW^{\pm}
boson transverse mass
m_{\mathrm{T}}>40
\GeV
, defined as
m_{\mathrm{T}}=\sqrt{\smash[b]{2\pt^{\ell}\pt^{\nu}(1-\cos(\Delta\phi))}}
, where
\pt^{\nu}
is the transverse momentum of the neutrino and
\Delta\phi
is the azimuthal angle between the lepton and the neutrino directions. The
\ptmiss
is associated with the undetected neutrino in the reconstruction selection requirements. Events with additional muons or electron with
\pt>10\GeV
and satisfying less restrictive selection requirements than the signal lepton candidate selection, and with average selection efficiencies above 95% are rejected
[
36
,
37
]
. This requirement reduces the contamination from events with a
\PZ
boson.

The total production cross section for the
\PW^{\pm}
and
\PZ
bosons times the leptonic branching ratio can be expressed as:

\sigma_{\mathrm{tot}}=\frac{N_{\mathrm{sig}}}{A\cdot\epsilon\cdot\mathrm{L}},

(2)

where
N_{\mathrm{sig}}
is the number of signal events,
A
is the kinematic acceptance,
\epsilon
is the efficiency of the signal events within the fiducial region satisfying the event selection requirements, and
\mathrm{L}
is the integrated luminosity. The fiducial cross section
\sigma_{\mathrm{fid}}
is defined as
\sigma_{\mathrm{tot}}\cdot A
. Electrons and muons produced in the decay of a
\tau
lepton are not included in the definition of the fiducial region. The leptons at generator level are defined at so called dressed level by combining the four-momentum of each lepton after the final-state photon radiation with that of photons found within a cone of
\Delta R=0.1
around the lepton.

The efficiencies for the reconstruction, identification, and isolation
requirements on the leptons are obtained in bins of
\pt
and
\eta
using the
“tag-and-probe” techniques with Drell–Yan events that provide an unbiased data sample with high purity
[
10
]
. The muon (electron) candidates have an average combined reconstruction, identification, and isolation efficiency of 90 (70)%. Momentum scale corrections are applied to the muons and electrons in both data and simulated events
[
39
]
.

0.6
Signal extraction and background estimation

Statistical analysis of the event yields is performed with a simultaneous fit to the dilepton mass
m_{\ell\ell}
and
m_{\mathrm{T}}
distributions in the
\PZ
and
\PW^{\pm}
signal regions. The systematic uncertainties are treated as nuisance parameters in the fit and profiled. A combination of data-driven methods and detailed simulated studies to estimate background contributions is used. In all cases where simulation is used, events are reweighted to correct for the lepton and trigger efficiencies to agree with the data distribution.

The Drell–Yan lepton pair production constitutes a background in the
\PW
boson signal selection where one of the two leptons does not enter the fiducial region or is not reconstructed. The
\PW\to\tau\nu
and
\PZ\to\tau^{+}\tau^{-}
processes, where one or both
\tau
leptons decays leptonically, are considered as background processes. The contribution of these background processes as well as the contribution of the top quark and boson pairs (
\PW\PW
,
\PW\PZ
, and
\PZ\PZ
) is estimated from the simulation. The contribution of the Drell-Yan events not entering the fiducial region at the generator level in the
\PZ
boson selection is normalized to the
\PZ
signal yield in the simultaneous fit. Similarly. the
\PW\to\tau\nu
background contribution in the
\PW
boson selection is normalized to the
\PW
signal yield in the simultaneous fit. The background contributions from Drell–Yan,
\PW\to\tau\nu
,
\PZ\to\tau^{+}\tau^{-}
, and diboson processes is denoted as the EW background.

The isolation requirements reduce the contribution of the QCD multijet events. The remaining QCD multijet background contributions is estimated from a background enriched region using nonisolated leptons. Events for this QCD multijet enriched region share the same event selection and lepton identification requirements, with the exception of the
R_{\mathrm{iso}}
requirement, for which the selection criteria is inverted. The contribution of the EW background processes is subtracted. It is assumed that the shape of the
m_{\mathrm{T}}
distribution of the QCD multijet events in the simultaneous fit has the same shape in both the signal and the QCD multijet enriched control region. The normalizations of the QCD multijet background contributions in the
\PW^{+}
and
\PW^{-}
signal regions are then freely floated in the simultaneous fit for the cross sections.

Various isolation regions are considered to derive the shape of the QCD mutlijet background contribution. The shape of the QCD multijet background is found to be stable in different isolation regions. The isolation region
0.30<R_{\mathrm{iso}}<0.45
is used to derive the QCD multijet background shape and has an expected signal contamination of about 5% subtracted. The statistical uncertainties due to the limited number of selected events in the control region dominate and are included in the likelihood fit as nuisance parameters for each bin of the
m_{\mathrm{T}}
distribution. In addition, the relative shape difference between the
0.30<R_{\mathrm{iso}}<0.45
and
0.45<R_{\mathrm{iso}}<0.55
isolation regions are included as an additional shape uncertainty in the maximum likelihood fit.

An accurate simulation of the
\ptmiss
is essential to distinguish the
\PW
boson signal from the QCD multijet backgrounds. The missing transverse energy response and resolution is derived from a
\cPZ
boson sample. The recoil in these events is studied in data and the measured performance is applied to the simulation as a function of the
\pt
of the generated
\PW
boson. The recoil in simulation is corrected to data for the
\PW
signal and Drell–Yan,
\PW\to\tau\nu
, and
\PZ\to\tau^{+}\tau^{-}
background processes.

0.7
Systematic uncertainties

The sources of systematic uncertainty in the measurement include the
uncertainties in the integrated luminosity, lepton efficiencies
(reconstruction, identification, and trigger), lepton momentum scale and resolution, background estimation,
\ptmiss
modeling, and the theoretical uncertainty in the kinematic acceptance. Different sources of systematic uncertainties can affect the rates and shapes of the
m_{\ell\ell}
and
m_{\mathrm{T}}
distributions for the signal and background processes in the simultaneous fit.

The largest source of uncertainty in the inclusive total cross section
measurements comes from the measurement of the integrated luminosity and
amounts to 1.7% and 4.8% for the 13 and 5
\TeV
data samples, respectively.
[
40
]
. The uncertainty in the integrated luminosity cancels in the measurements of the cross section ratios.

A potential bias in the measurement of the reconstruction, identification, and
isolation efficiencies is estimated by studying the modeling of the background and signal parameterization in the dilepton invariant mass fit in the tag-and-probe technique. The uncertainty in the modeling of the electromagnetic
final state radiation (FSR) in the tag-and-probe fits is obtained by weighting the simulation to reflect the differences between
\PYTHIA
and PHOTOS 3.56
[
41
]
modeling of the FSR with the exponentiation mode. An additional uncertainty is considered by varying the tag selection requirements in the efficiency measurement to estimate the bias coming from the tag selection in the tag-and-probe technique. The statistical uncertainties in the efficiency measurement are considered as uncorrelated between the lepton
\pt
and
\eta
bins used for the determination of the lepton efficiencies. They are also considered as uncorrelated in the cross section ratios:
\PW^{+}/\PW^{-}
,
\PW^{+}/\PZ
, and
\PW^{-}/\PZ
.

The systematic uncertainties affecting the shape of the
m_{\mathrm{T}}
distributions are considered with alternative shapes in the maximum-likelihood fit. These include uncertainties in modeling the lepton momentum scale and resolution and also in the
\ptmiss
modeling.

A normalization uncertainty of
6\%
is assigned to the top and diboson background processes. The systematic uncertainty in the data driven QCD multijet background estimation was described in Section
0.6
. The statistical uncertainties due to the finite size of simulated samples and nonisolated control regions are also included
[
42
]
.

The theoretical uncertainties in the kinematic acceptance are estimated by varying the renormalization (
\mu_{\mathrm{R}}
) and factorization (
\mu_{\mathrm{F}}
) scales independently up and down by a factor of two from their nominal values (excluding the two extreme variations) and taking the largest acceptance variation as the uncertainty. The uncertainties from the NNPDF 3.1 PDF set and
\alpS
are evaluated according to the procedure described in Ref.
[
43
]
. The fixed-order calculations are unreliable at low
\pt
due to soft and collinear gluon radiation, resulting in large logarithmic
corrections
[
44
]
. Fixed-order perturbative calculations combined with parton shower models
[
27
,
45
,
46
]
provide fully exclusive predictions
[
47
,
48
,
25
,
20
]
with only leading logarithmic formal accuracy. Resummation of the logarithmically divergent terms at next-to-next-to-leading logarithmic (NNLL) accuracy has been matched with the fixed-order predictions to achieve accurate predictions for the entire boson
\pt
range
[
49
,
50
]
. The differences in the kinematic acceptance between the nominal
\MGvATNLO
and resummed
DYTURBO

[
51
]
predictions are taken as a systematic uncertainty. Finally, the uncertainty in the modeling of the FSR is obtained by comparing the acceptance differences between
\PYTHIA
and PHOTOS 3.5.6 predictions (
\POWHEG
2.0 is used for the matrix element).

Results

The results for the electron and muon final states are presented separately. Assuming lepton universality, we combine our measurements in the different lepton decay modes. The combination is performed with a simultaneous fit in the electron and muon final states. The uncertainties in the integrated luminosity, background estimation, and modeling uncertainties are treated as correlated parameters. The other experimental uncertainties are considered as uncorrelated. The PDF and QCD modeling uncertainties in the kinematic acceptance are assumed to be correlated in the combination. The FSR modeling uncertainties are considered as uncorrelated.

\PW
and
\PZ
boson production cross sections in proton-proton collisions at
5.02\TeV
and
13\TeV
are presented. Electron and muon final states are studied in data samples collected with the CMS detector corresponding to integrated luminosities of
201\pm 3\pbinv
at
13\TeV
and
296\pm 10\pbinv
at
5.02\TeV
. The measured total inclusive cross sections times branching fractions are
\sigma(\Pp\Pp\to\PW^{+}{}+X)\times\mathcal{B}(\PW^{+}\to\ell^{+}\nu)=xx\pm xx\mathrm{(stat)}\pm xx\mathrm{(syst)}\pm xx\mathrm{(lumi)}
nb,
\sigma(\Pp\Pp\to\PW^{-}X)\times\mathcal{B}(\PW^{-}\to\ell^{-}\bar{\nu})=xx\pm xx\mathrm{(stat)}\pm xx\mathrm{(syst)}\pm xx\mathrm{(lumi)}
nb, and
\sigma(\\

nb for the dilepton mass in the range of 60 to 120
\GeV

Abstract
. In this paper we study the nuclearity and weak containment property of reduced cross-sectional C ∗ C^{*} -algebras of Fell bundles over inverse semigroups. In order to develop the theory, we first prove an analogue of Fell’s absorption principle in the context of Fell bundles over inverse semigroups. In parallel, the approximation property of Exel can be reformulated in this context, and Fell’s absorption principle can be used to prove that the approximation property, as defined here, implies that the full and reduced cross-sectional C ∗ C^{*} -algebras are isomorphic via the left regular representation, i.e., the Fell bundle has the weak containment property. We then use this machinery to prove that a Fell bundle with the approximation property and nuclear unit fiber has a nuclear cross-sectional C ∗ C^{*} -algebra. This result gives nuclearity of a large class of C ∗ C^{*} -algebras as, remarkably, all the machinery in this paper works for étale non-Hausdorff groupoids just as well.

1.
Introduction

There is an abundant number of examples of
C^{*}
-algebras that have been studied in the almost 80 years of
C^{*}
-literature, along with numerous different properties. Of these properties, one of the most ubiquitous ones is
nuclearity
. In general, a
C^{*}
-algebra

is

if there is exactly one
C^{*}
-norm on
A\odot B
, the algebraic tensor product of
A
and
B
, for any
C^{*}
-algebra
B
(see
[
8
]
for many other equivalent characterizations). Since it was first introduced, nuclearity has played a crucial role in the field, for it provides a natural finite-dimensional approximation of
A
in terms of finite-dimensional
C^{*}
-algebras (see, e.g.,
[
8
]
*Section 2.3). Thus, as the class of
C^{*}
-algebras is vast, criteria that guarantees when a
C^{*}
-algebra is nuclear is desirable, as it allows to tackle many different problems by various means. The present paper has the goal of giving sufficient criteria for the nuclearity of a large class of
C^{*}
-algebras, namely those arising as
cross-sectional

C^{*}
-algebras from a
Fell bundle
over an
inverse semigroup
.

Inverse semigroups
were introduced independently by Wagner
[
42
,
43
]
and Preston
[
38
,
37
,
39
]
in the 50’s. If a (discrete) group can be understood as the set of
global
symmetries of a space, then an
inverse semigroup
can be understood as an algebraic structure that captures the
local
symmetries of a space
[
33
]
. Following this idea, one can also define
actions
of inverse semigroups on locally compact and Hausdorff spaces, which generalize group
partial
actions
[
23
,
33
]
. In this setting, such an action gives rise to
full
and
reduced
crossed product
C^{*}
-algebras. One may even
twist
the action, in the sense of
[
19
]
, and construct
full
and
reduced twisted
crossed products. This class of
C^{*}
-algebras is, already, quite large, as it contains every
classifiable

C^{*}
-algebra
[
32
]
, among many others. In this paper we shall go one step further, and study cross-sectional
C^{*}
-algebras arising from
Fell bundles
over inverse semigroups.

A
Fell bundle over an inverse semigroup

S
is a bundle
\mathcal{A}=(A_{s})_{s\in S}
of Banach spaces
A_{s}
endowed with associative multiplication maps
A_{s}\times A_{t}\to A_{st}
, involution maps
A_{s}\to A_{s^{*}}
and inclusion maps
A_{s}\hookrightarrow A_{t}
for
s\leq t
in

satisfying certain natural axioms that are reminiscent of
C^{*}
-algebraic structures (see
Definition

2.4
below). These objects were first introduced by Sieben in the unpublished paper
[
40
]
, and first formally introduced in
[
21
]
by Exel. In fact, Exel introduced them in order to deal with
noncommutative Cartan subalgebras
(see
[
21
]
for precise definitions). Indeed, the Fell bundle data generalizes both twisted actions on spaces and actions on noncommutative
C^{*}
-algebras, and hence it is the right setting where one may study noncommutative Cartan subalgebras. We shall usually assume that the inverse semigroup
S
has a unit
1\in S
, as this amounts to no loss in generality. In this case
A_{1}
is a distinguished
C^{*}
-algebra, and the fibres
A_{s}
are Hilbert bimodules over
A_{1}
with respect to the Fell bundle operations. In this setting, the Fell bundle
\mathcal{A}
can be viewed as a (generalized) action of
S
on
A_{1}
by Hilbert bimodules; this is the point of view taken in
[
13
]
and in the sequel.

As is well known
[
31
,
14
]
, inverse semigroups are strongly connected to
étale groupoids
. In particular, every action of an inverse semigroup gives rise to an étale groupoid that may be non-Hausdorff (see
Section

4.1
below). Likewise, Fell bundles over inverse semigroups are strongly related to Fell bundles over étale groupoids, as defined by Kumjian in
[
26
]
. The connection between these structures is, in fact, so tight that Fell bundles over inverse semigroups may be interpreted as
(twisted) étale groupoids with noncommutative unit spaces
, as is done in
[
11
]
. This relationship is further detailed in
Section

4.1
, particularly in
Theorems

4.16
and
4.25
.

Ever since it was known that amenable groups have nuclear reduced
C^{*}
-algebras,
amenability
of different types of structures has received considerable attention from the community (see
[
9
,
16
,
25
,
34
,
35
]
and references therein for examples of
amenable
actions). Amenability of Fell bundles has, in particular, also received attention, starting from the work of Exel
[
18
]
and Exel-Ng
[
16
]
where the notion of
approximation property
for a Fell bundle
\mathcal{A}=(A_{g})_{g\in G}
over a group

was first introduced. This should be viewed as amenability of the (generalized) action of
G
on
A_{1}
implemented by
\mathcal{A}
; indeed, whenever
\mathcal{A}
comes from an ordinary action
\alpha
of
G
on
A_{1}
the approximation property of
\mathcal{A}
turns out to be equivalent to the amenability of
\alpha
as defined by Anantharaman-Delaroche in
[
4
]
. Although Exel’s approximation property and Anantharaman-Delaroche’s amenability have been around for quite some time (around 35 years), only recently have they been proved to be equivalent for Fell bundles associated with ordinary group actions; see the recent papers
[
9
,
1
]
for further discussions on this.

Exel’s approach in
[
18
]
, in fact, shows that the approximation property of
\mathcal{A}
, together with the nuclearity of the unit fiber

, imply that
C_{\text{max}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\cong C_{\text{red}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
is nuclear. Note that

needs to be nuclear if
C_{\text{red}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
is to be nuclear as well, since there always is a canonical conditional expectation

. Moreover, it was already known for some time, and proved in
[

]
*Theorem 6.4, that if

is nuclear then

, that is, the weak containment property for

holds. Only recently, see
[
9
,
1
]
, it was proved that the nuclearity of
C_{\text{red}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
(or equivalently of
C_{\text{max}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
) is equivalent to nuclearity of
A_{1}
plus the approximation property of
\mathcal{A}
. It is, however, not yet known whether the weak containment property for
\mathcal{A}
implies its approximation property.
1
1

1

This is known to be false in the more general setting of locally compact groups, as there are examples of locally compact groups
G
and actions on noncommutative
C^{*}
-algebras for which the weak containment holds but the underlying Fell bundle does not have the approximation property (i.e., the action is not amenable), see
[
9
]
.
Lastly, Kranz recently
[
25
]
generalized the notion of approximation property so as to cover Fell bundles over second countable locally compact Hausdorff étale groupoids, and derived some of the expected results.

Our main goal in this paper is to extend the notion of
approximation property
from Fell bundles over groups to Fell bundles over inverse semigroups. This is not trivial due to the subtlety implicitly contained in these objects. In fact, the most direct translation from groups or groupoids to inverse semigroups does not capture the correct properties. Nevertheless, our notion of approximation property (see
Definition

4.4
) does extend that of groups and groupoids (see
Theorem

4.16
). It is worth noting that our techniques actually allow us to generalize some of the main theorems of
[
25
]
, as we do not require the groupoids to be Hausdorff or second-countable, nor do we need the Fell bundles to be saturated or separable (see
Corollary

6.9
below). In fact, we generalize the main result of
[
18
,
25
]
in the following way.

Theorem 1

(cf.
Theorem

6.7
)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(A_{s})_{s\in S}
be a Fell bundle over an inverse semigroup with unit
1\in S
. Suppose that
\mathcal{A}
has the approximation property, as in
Definition

4.4
, and that
A_{1}
is nuclear. Then
C_{\text{max}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\cong C_{\text{red}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
is nuclear.

In order to prove
1
we, in fact, need to prove a version of Fell’s absorption principle (see
[
22
]
*Proposition 18.4) for Fell bundles over inverse semigroups (see
2

\pi
of
\mathcal{A}
, once tensored with the (left) regular representation
\lambda
of the underlying group, yields a new representation
\pi\otimes\lambda
whose extension to
C_{\text{max}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
factors through
C_{\text{red}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
. Again, for inverse semigroups this theorem is much more subtle, since the simple-minded tensor product
\pi\otimes\lambda
does not necessarily yield a representation of
\mathcal{A}
, due to the relations one has to impose on those representations coming from the inclusions
A_{s}\hookrightarrow A_{t}
for
s\leq t
. We are going to construct instead a different representation
\pi^{\Lambda}
that will play the role of
\pi\otimes\lambda
. It will act on a Hilbert space
\ell^{2}_{\pi}(S,\mathcal{H})
that is a Hausdorff completion of a certain space of finitely supported sections
S\to\mathcal{H}
. Our version of Fell’s absorption principle then reads as follows.

Theorem 2

(cf.
Theorems

3.20
and
3.23
)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(A_{s})_{s\in S}
be a Fell bundle over an inverse semigroup
S
, and let
\pi=(\pi_{s})_{s\in S}
be a representation of
\mathcal{A}
. Then
\pi^{\Lambda}
is unitarily equivalent to the representation
\Ind\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}=\Lambda\otimes_{\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}}\id\colon\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime})\otimes_{\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}}\mathcal{H})
induced from the regular representation
\Lambda\colon\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime}))
. In particular
\pi^{\Lambda}
extends to a representation of
C_{\text{red}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
. Moreover, if
\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}
is faithful as a representation of
A_{1}^{\prime\prime}
, then
\pi^{\Lambda}
yields a faithful representation

\pi^{\Lambda}\colon C_{\text{red}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2}_{\pi}(S,\mathcal{H})).

2
above will allow us to describe induced representations from the regular representation, and hence is the main technical tool in order to prove
1
. We now briefly mention that in
Corollary

6.11
we also prove that the approximation property of
\mathcal{A}
implies that the
essential
C^{*}
-algebra

is nuclear (see
[

]
*Theorem 4.11 or

below). In fact, in an upcoming paper, the authors will use similar methods to those in this text in order to introduce an

, proving the same results we now show but in the context of

instead.

2.1.
Inverse semigroups and groupoids

Recall that a
semigroup
is a set
S
equipped with a binary associative operation
S\times S\to S
, where
(s,t)\mapsto st
. As expected, the main difference between
semigroups
and
groups
is the lack of the inverse
s^{-1}
of some elements, which means that the maps given by
\lambda_{s}\colon S\to S
, where
t\mapsto st
, may be non-injective. In particular, the map
\lambda_{s}
may not define a
bounded
operator on
\ell^{2}(S)
. The following notion, introduced independently by Wagner
[
42
,
43
]
and Preston
[
38
,
37
,
39
]
, will fix this caveat, albeit only
locally
(see
Remark

2.2
).

Definition 2.1
.

A semigroup
S
is called an
inverse semigroup
if for all
s\in S
there is a unique
s^{*}\in S
such that
ss^{*}s=s
and
s^{*}ss^{*}=s^{*}
.

Henceforth
S
will denote a discrete inverse semigroup. We now briefly recall the inner structure of a general inverse semigroup, and set some notation. We refer the reader to
[
30
]
for a more comprenhensive introduction to inverse semigroups. The set of
idempotents of
S
, denoted by
E
, is the set
E\coloneqq\{e\in S\mid e^{2}=e\}
. It is well known (see
[
30
]
*Theorem 3) that
E\subseteq S
forms a
commutative
sub-semigroup, and hence we may define a partial order on
E
by stating that
e\leq f
if, and only if,
ef=e
. This endows
E
with a meet semi-lattice structure. In fact, the partial order
\leq
can be extended to the whole semigroup
S
by stating that
s\leq t
if, and only if,
te=s
for some idempotent
e\in E
. It is also well known (and follows from the uniqueness of
s^{*}
in
Definition

2.1
, see
[
30
]
) that
E
is precisely the set
\{s^{*}s\mid s\in S\}
, and hence every idempotent
e\in E
is self-adjoint. We say that an element
z\in S
is
a zero
if
zt=tz=z
for every
t\in S
. If there is a zero element then it is clear it must be unique, and in such case we will denote it by
0\in E\subseteq S
. Likewise, a
unit
, or an
identity of
S
, is an element
u\in S
such that
ut=tu=t
for every
t\in S
. Again, if there is a unit in
S
then it must be unique, and we shall henceforth denote it by
1\in E\subseteq S
. In fact, a unit may be always adjoined to
S
and for convenience we shall assume
S
has a unit throughout.

Remark 2.2
.

We briefly highlight that, even though the map
\lambda_{s}\colon S\to S
defined above is still not injective even if
S
is an inverse semigroup, it will be injective when restricted to
\lambda_{s}\colon s^{*}s\cdot S\to ss^{*}\cdot S
. This observation leads to a theory of
actions
of inverse semigroups (see, e.g.,
[
6
]
and references therein), and will permeate throughout the present paper, although not explicitly. In fact, the canonical action given by
\lambda_{s}\colon s^{*}s\cdot S\to ss^{*}\cdot S
is known as the
Wagner-Preston representation
. Likewise, inverse semigroups are then realized by partial isometries of Hilbert spaces with commuting domains and ranges.

We may now shift our point of view, and discuss
groupoids
. However, observe that the main objects of study of the text (see
Definitions

2.4
and
4.4
) revolve around inverse semigroups, and so we will keep the discussions about groupoids brief. We refer the reader to
[
41
]
and references therein for a more in-depth discussion, as well as natural examples of groupoids arising in the literature.

Definition 2.3
.

A
groupoid

G
is a small category with inverses, that is, it is a set
G
together with a distinguished set
G^{(2)}\subseteq G\times G
and maps
\cdot\colon G^{(2)}\to G
and
{}^{-1}\colon G\to G
such that

(1)

(g^{-1})^{-1}=g
;

(2)

if
(g,h),(h,k)\in G^{(2)}
then
(g,hk),(gh,k)\in G^{(2)}
and
(gh)k=g(hk)
;

(3)

(g,g^{-1})\in G^{(2)}
, and whenever
(g,h)\in G^{(2)}
(resp.
(h,g)\in G^{(2)}
) we have
g^{-1}gh=h
(resp.
h=hgg^{-1}
),

for all
g,h,k\in G
.

We usually think of elements in a groupoid as
arrows between units
. Observe that every groupoid has a distinguished subset
G^{0}\subseteq G
, whose elements are called
units
, which are precisely those elements that, as arrows, form loops. In fact, the rest of the elements of the groupoid can be thought of as arrows
g\colon g^{-1}g\to gg^{-1}
. This way we may define maps
r,s\colon G\to G^{0}
by
s(g)\coloneqq g^{-1}g
and
r(g)\coloneqq gg^{-1}
, which we call the
range
and
source
map respectively. Henceforth, every groupoid here considered will be endowed with a topology for which the multiplication and inverse maps are continuous. Hence, we say
G
is a
locally compact groupoid
if it is locally compact as a topological space. Moreover, the notion of
étale
groupoid will be discussed in
Section

4.1
, and thus we will not discuss it here any further.

It is by now well known that
étale groupoids
and
inverse semigroups
are dual to each other (see
[
31
]
for precise statements). On one hand, given an inverse semigroup
S
one may construct the
universal
groupoid associated to
S
(see
[
36
]
*Chapter 4). As is expected from the name, this groupoid encapsulates the representation theory of
S
, and is
universal
in some suitable sense
[
36
]
*Theorems 4.4.1 and 4.4.2. However, we will not use this construction in the present paper, and hence we will refrain from discussing it further.

On the other hand, given a locally compact étale groupoid
G
, one may look at the set of open
bisections

\Bis(G)
, which carries a natural inverse semigroup structure (see
Section

4.1
below, particularly
Definition

4.11
). We may then recover the groupoid
G
as the
groupoid of germs
G^{0}\rtimes\Bis(G)
, where
\Bis(G)
acts on
G^{0}
canonically (see
Proposition

4.12
). All in all, elements in an inverse semigroup may be seen as appropriately chosen open sets of a groupoid, and arrows in a groupoid may be seen as germs of actions of inverse semigroups on spaces.

Fell bundles

The following remarks are in order.

Remark 2.5
.

Historically, the notion of Fell bundles has first been defined by Sieben in an unpublished manuscript
[
40
]
. This definition was then published and used in the paper
[
21
]
by Exel. We use the above notion from
[
13
]
by the first-named author and Meyer, where it is shown that both definitions are equivalent. In
[
13
]
, in fact, a Fell bundle over
S
with unit fibre

is viewed as a (generalized)

on

by Hilbert bimodules
.

Remark 2.6
.

The notion in

2.4
is actually what is known as a

Fell bundle in the literature, since the maps

are assumed to be only embeddings of Hilbert

-

-bimodules.
Saturated
Fell bundles, on the other hand, assume that
\mu_{s,t}
is, actually, an isomorphism. It is well known (see
[
12
]
) that given a non-saturated Fell bundle
\mathcal{A}=(A_{s})_{s\in S}
one can enlarge
S
to an inverse semigroup

(with the same unit, the so-called
prefix expansion of
S
) and endow
\mathcal{A}
with a natural saturated Fell bundle structure over
T
in such a way that the full and reduced
C^{*}
-algebras of
\mathcal{A}
over
S
and
T
are canonically isomorphic (see
[
12
]
*Theorem 7.2).

Remark 2.7
.

We restrict ourselves to the case where

has a unit for convenience of both the readers and the authors, but this is not necessary. In general, if

not

and

, we shall usually just say that

is a

The following theorem was proved in
[

canonical conditional expectation
of
\mathcal{A}
.

Since
C_{c}(\mathcal{A})
and
\mathbb{C}_{\text{alg}}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
are only
∗
-algebras, it might be not clear what we mean by a (weak) conditional expectation on these algebras. This only means
P
is a linear map which is positive in the sense that
P(x^{*}x)\geq 0
for all
x\in C_{c}(\mathcal{A})
; it preserves adjoints, meaning that
P(x^{*})=P(x)^{*}
, and that
P
restricts to the identity on
A_{1}
, that is,
P(a\delta_{1})=a
for all
a\in A_{1}
. Similar properties hold for
\tilde{P}
. And the fact that
\tilde{P}
is faithful means that
P(x^{*}x)=0
happens only if

for any
x\in\mathbb{C}_{\text{alg}}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
. This is proved in
[
10
]
*Proposition 3.6. The fact that
\tilde{P}
is faithful also implies that
P
is symmetric in the sense that

P(x^{*}x)=0
if and only if
P(xx^{*})=0
, for all
x\in C_{c}(\mathcal{A})
. This means that

(2.26)

\mathcal{N}_{P}\coloneqq\left\{x\in C_{c}\left(\mathcal{A}\right):P\left(x^{*}x\right)=0\right\}

is a
∗
-ideal of
C_{c}(\mathcal{A})
. In fact, since
\tilde{P}
is faithful, it also follows that

\mathcal{N}_{P}
coincides with the kernel of the quotient homomorphism
C_{c}(\mathcal{A})\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{C}_{\text{alg}}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
, that is,

\mathcal{N}_{P}=\mathcal{J}_{\mathcal{A}}=\spn\left\{\theta_{u,t}(a)\delta_{u}-a\delta_{t}\colon a\in I_{u,t}\right\}.

The following result from
[
10
]
indicates that
\mathbb{C}_{\text{alg}}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
yields the “correct” definition of the algebraic crossed product of a Fell bundle.

Proposition 2.27
.

[
10
]
*Proposition 4.3
Given a Fell bundle
\mathcal{A}=(A_{s})_{s\in S}
, the
∗
-algebra
\mathbb{C}_{\text{alg}}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)

embeds densely in
C_{\text{max}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
.

For the following lemma recall that we canonically embed
A_{s}
into
C_{c}(\mathcal{A})
as the set
A_{s}\delta_{s}
of sections
a_{s}\delta_{s}
taking value
a_{s}
at

and
0
otherwise.

Lemma 2.28
.

Given a Fell bundle
\mathcal{A}=(A_{s})_{s\in S}
,
A_{s}\delta_{s}\cap\mathcal{N}_{P}=0
for every
s\in S
.

Proof.

For any
a_{s}\delta_{s}\in A_{s}\delta_{s}
we have

P\left(\left(a_{s}\delta_{s}\right)^{*}\left(a_{s}\delta_{s}\right)\right)=P\left(a_{s}^{*}a_{s}\delta_{s^{*}s}\right)=\theta_{s^{*}s,1}^{\prime\prime}\left(a_{s}^{*}a_{s}1_{s^{*}s}\right)=a_{s}^{*}a_{s}1_{s^{*}s}

since
s^{*}s\leq 1
. Therefore if
a_{s}\delta_{s}\in\mathcal{N}_{P}
then
a_{s}=0
, as claimed.
∎

Observe that, in general, the target of the canonical conditional expectation
P
in
Proposition

2.24
is only the von Neumann algebra
A_{1}^{\prime\prime}
, as opposed to
A_{1}
itself. We end the section with a condition that guarantees that the image of

lands in
A_{1}
.

Corollary 2.29
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(A_{s})_{s\in S}
be a Fell bundle. Then
P(x)\in A_{1}
for all
x\in C_{c}(\mathcal{A})
if and only if
I_{s,1}
is complemented in
\cspn A_{s}^{*}A_{s}\subseteq A_{1}
for all
s\in S
.

Proof.

The proof is given in
[
10
]
*Proposition 6.3, and it does not assume that the Fell bundle is saturated. However, we now give a sketch of the proof of the forward implication and refer the reader to
[
10
]
*Proposition 6.3 for the (more complicated) proof of the converse. Observe that if
I_{s,1}
is a complemented ideal of

then
A_{s}
decomposes as
A_{s}=A_{s}\cdot I_{s,1}\oplus A_{s}\cdot I_{s,1}^{\perp}
. And in this case

acts on
A_{s}
by projecting first to

and then composes this with the isomorphism
\theta_{s,1}\colon A_{s}\cdot I_{s,1}\xrightarrow{\sim}I_{s,1}\subseteq A_{1}
.
Hence

for all

and therefore also

.
∎

The following definition is from
[

. We will use it in the sequel, albeit not predominantly.

Definition 2.30
.

We say a Fell bundle
\mathcal{A}=(A_{s})_{s\in S}

.

Remark 2.31
.

Observe that for

to be closed it is

be complemented or even unital. Indeed, this is not even true in the basic case of a Fell bundle coming from just an ordinary inverse semigroup. For instance, take

is a unit,

,

for all

2.4.
Representations of Fell bundles

In this section we introduce the central notion of a
representation of a Fell bundle
. As expected, it preserves all the inherent structure of the bundle.

Definition 2.32
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(A_{s})_{s\in S}
be a Fell bundle over an inverse semigroup
S
.

(i)

A
representation of
\mathcal{A}
on the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
is a family
\pi=(\pi_{s})_{s\in S}
of linear maps
\pi_{s}\colon A_{s}\to\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
such that

\pi_{s}\left(a_{s}\right)\pi_{t}\left(b_{t}\right)=\pi_{st}\left(a_{s}b_{t}\right),\;\;\pi_{s}\left(a_{s}\right)^{*}=\pi_{s^{*}}\left(a_{s}^{*}\right)\;\;\text{and}\;\;\pi_{s}\left(a_{s}\right)=\pi_{u}\left(a_{s}\right),

whenever
s,t,u\in S
with
s\leq u
and
a_{s}\in A_{s}
and
b_{t}\in A_{t}
.

(ii)

We say the representation
\pi=(\pi_{s})_{s\in S}
is
nondegenerate
if
\pi_{1}
is a nondegenerate representation of
A=A_{1}
.

By definition a representation of
\mathcal{A}
always respects the inclusion maps
\iota_{t,s}\colon A_{s}\hookrightarrow A_{t}
for every
s\leq t
in
S
. It turns out that they also automatically respect the extended inclusion maps
\theta_{t,u}\colon A_{u}\cdot I_{t,u}\hookrightarrow A_{t}\cdot I_{t,u}
for every
t,u\in S
, as the following result shows. Actually, the following was only proved in the saturated case, but the same ideas work in the non-saturated setting as well (recall
Remark

2.6
).

Proposition 2.33

(
[
10
]
*Proposition 2.9)
.

Every representation
\pi
of
\mathcal{A}
satisfies

\pi_{t}\circ\theta_{t,u}\left(a_{u}\right)=\pi_{u}\left(a_{u}\right)

for all
t,u\in S
and
a_{u}\in A_{u}\cdot I_{t,u}
.

The following notation will be useful throughout the paper, particularly when discussing Fell’s absorption principle in the context of Fell bundles over inverse semigroups.

Definition 2.34
.

Given a Fell bundle
\mathcal{A}=(A_{s})_{s\in S}
and a representation
\pi=(\pi_{s})_{s\in S}
, we define
\mathcal{H}_{s}:=\cspn\pi_{s}(A_{s})\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
.

Observe that, by
[
10
]
*Lemma 2.8, we have
\mathcal{H}_{s}=\mathcal{H}_{ss^{*}}=\pi_{1}(A_{ss^{*}})\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
for all
s\in S
. Likewise, notice that
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{1}
if and only if the representation
\pi
is nondegenerate.

Remark 2.35
.

Notice that every representation
\pi
of
\mathcal{A}
extends uniquely to a weakly continuous representation
\pi^{\prime\prime}=(\pi_{s}^{\prime\prime})_{s\in S}
of its von Neumann enveloping Fell bundle
\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime}
. Moreover, since
A_{s}
is weakly dense in
A_{s}^{\prime\prime}
, for all
s\in S
we have

\mathcal{H}_{s}=\cspn\pi_{s}\left(A_{s}\right)\mathcal{H}=\cspn\pi_{s}^{\prime\prime}\left(A_{s}^{\prime\prime}\right)\mathcal{H}=\cspn\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}\left(A_{ss^{*}}^{\prime\prime}\right)\mathcal{H}.

We end this section with the expected universal characterization of the full cross sectional
C^{*}
-algebra.

Proposition 2.36
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(A_{s})_{s\in S}
be a Fell bundle over the inverse semigroup
S
. Then
C_{\text{max}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
is universal for representations of
\mathcal{A}
, that is, given any representation
\pi=(\pi_{s})_{s\in S}
of
\mathcal{A}
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, there is a (unique)
*
-homomorphism
\rho\colon C_{\text{max}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
such that
\rho(a_{s}\delta_{s})=\pi_{s}(a_{s})
for every
a_{s}\in A_{s}
and
s\in S
.

Regular representation and Fell’s absorption principle

Before going into the

(see

3.1.
The regular representation

In this section we introduce the
left regular representation
of a Fell bundle, and prove some structural results about it. Note that this representation will be key in order to construct the
reduced cross-sectional
C^{*}
-algebra
of a Fell bundle (see
Definition

3.3
). We refer the reader to
[
10
]
for a more comprehensive discussion.

We first need to discuss a canonical inner product on
C_{c}(\mathcal{A})
. Recall we naturally endow
C_{c}(\mathcal{A})
with a
∗
-algebra structure (see
Section

2.3
). Likewise, recall the definition of the ideal
\mathcal{N}_{P}\subseteq C_{c}(\mathcal{A})
from
Equation

2.26
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(A_{s})_{s\in S}
be a Fell bundle. Consider

\langle\xi\!\mid\!\eta\rangle_{A_{1}^{\prime\prime}}\coloneqq P\left(\xi^{*}\cdot\eta\right),\;\;\text{where}\;\,\xi,\eta\in C_{c}\left(\mathcal{A}\right).

Then
\langle\cdot\!\mid\!\cdot\rangle_{A_{1}^{\prime\prime}}
is a
semi-inner product
with values in
A_{1}^{\prime\prime}
whose kernel is exactly
\mathcal{N}_{P}
, that is,
\langle\xi\!\mid\!\xi\rangle_{A_{1}^{\prime\prime}}=0
if and only if
\xi\in\mathcal{N}_{P}
. Thus, it factors through an
A_{1}^{\prime\prime}
-valued inner product on
\mathbb{C}_{\text{alg}}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
.

Proof.

The proof follows from the properties of
P
showed in
[
10
]
*Proposition 3.6 and Remark 4.7. Recall that
\langle\cdot\!\mid\!\cdot\rangle_{A_{1}^{\prime\prime}}
being a semi-inner product with values in
A_{1}^{\prime\prime}
means that it is a sesquilinear form,
A_{1}
-linear in the second variable, and is positive meaning that
\langle\xi\!\mid\!\xi\rangle_{A_{1}^{\prime\prime}}\geq 0
in the
C^{*}
-algebra
A_{1}^{\prime\prime}
for all
\xi\in C_{c}(\mathcal{A})
.
∎

We could now proceed and take the completion of
\mathbb{C}_{\text{alg}}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
with the respect to the norm induced from the above inner product.
But there is a technical complication here as this completion does not necessarily carry a (right)
A_{1}^{\prime\prime}
-module structure.
The easy way out of this is to go directly to the von Neumann enveloping Fell bundle
\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime}
and consider the similar completion there.
This is the way it is done in
[
10
]
.

Definition 3.2
.

Given a Fell bundle
\mathcal{A}=(A_{s})_{s\in S}
we denote by
\ell^{2}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime})
the completion of
\mathbb{C}_{\text{alg}}\left(\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime}\right)
with respect to the norm induced from the inner product
\langle\cdot\!\mid\!\cdot\rangle_{A_{1}^{\prime\prime}}
given by
Proposition

3.1
for
\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime}
.

Note that
\ell^{2}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime})
is, in particular, a Hilbert module over
A_{1}^{\prime\prime}
. We can now introduce the left regular representation of a Fell bundle.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(A_{s})_{s\in S}
be a Fell bundle over an inverse semigroup
S
.

(i)

The
(left) regular representation
of
\mathcal{A}
is the canonical
∗
-homomorphism
\Lambda\colon\mathbb{C}_{\text{alg}}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\to\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime}))
defined by (left) multiplication, that is,

\Lambda\left(a\right)\left(f\right)\coloneqq af

for every
f\in\ell^{2}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime})
and
a\in\mathbb{C}_{\text{alg}}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
.

(ii)

The
reduced cross-sectional
C^{*}
-algebra
of
\mathcal{A}
is the completion
C_{\text{red}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
of
\mathbb{C}_{\text{alg}}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
with respect to the
C^{*}
-norm

\|a\|_{r}\coloneqq\|\Lambda\left(a\right)\|_{\mathcal{L}\left(\ell^{2}\left(\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime}\right)\right)},\;\;\text{where}\;\,a\in\mathbb{C}_{\text{alg}}\left(\mathcal{A}\right).

Remark 3.4
.

By the universal property of
C_{\text{max}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
(see also
Proposition

2.36
) the left regular representation of
\mathcal{A}
gives a representation
C_{\text{max}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\to C_{\text{red}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\subseteq\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime}))
. By abuse of notation we will denote both by
\Lambda
.

The following proposition gives an explicit formula for the left regular representation
\Lambda\colon\mathbb{C}_{\text{alg}}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime}))
. For it, recall that the sections
A_{s}\cong A_{s}\delta_{s}
canonically embed into
\mathbb{C}_{\text{alg}}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
(see
Lemma

2.28
).

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(A_{s})_{s\in S}
be a Fell bundle over
S
. Then:

\Lambda_{s}\left(a_{s}\delta_{s}\right)\left(f+\mathcal{N}_{P}\right)=\sum_{t\in S}a_{s}f\left(t\right)\delta_{st}+\mathcal{N}_{P}

for every
a_{s}\in A_{s}
and
f\in C_{c}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime})
.

Proof.

We have
f=\sum_{r\in S}f(r)\delta_{r}+\mathcal{N}_{P}
, where the sum has, at most, finitely many non-zero terms. By definition

\Lambda\left(a_{s}\delta_{s}\right)\left(f\right)=\sum_{t\in S}a_{s}\delta_{s}f\left(t\right)\delta_{t}+\mathcal{N}_{P}=\sum_{t\in S}a_{s}f\left(t\right)\delta_{st}+\mathcal{N}_{P},

as claimed.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

Note that, by
Proposition

3.5
, we have a commuting diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C_{c}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C_{c}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{C}_{\text{alg}}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{C}_{\text{alg}}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{P}}

.

Given a representation

\displaystyle a_{s}\delta_{s}

Notice that

(or even on

.

3.2.
Fell’s absorption principle for groups

Let us now recall Fell’s absorption principle for the case of Fell bundles over (discrete) groups (see
[
22
]
*Chapter 18). Let
\mathcal{A}=(A_{g})_{g\in G}
be a Fell bundle over a group
G
and let
\pi=(\pi_{g})_{g\in G}
be a representation of
\mathcal{A}
on some Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. Then we obtain a new representation
\pi^{\lambda}=(\pi^{\lambda}_{g})_{g\in G}
of
\mathcal{A}
on
\ell^{2}(G,\mathcal{H})\cong\ell^{2}(G)\otimes\mathcal{H}
defined by
\pi^{\lambda}_{g}\coloneqq\lambda_{g}\otimes\pi_{g}
for all
g\in G
, where
\lambda\colon G\to\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2}(G))
denotes the left regular representation of
G
. Observe that

\pi^{\lambda}_{g}\left(f\right)\left(h\right)=\pi_{g}\left(f\left(g^{-1}h\right)\right),\;\;\text{for}\;\,g,h\in G\;\,\text{and}\;\,f\in\ell^{2}\left(G,\mathcal{H}\right).

Here
\ell^{2}(\mathcal{A})
denotes the Hilbert
A_{1}
-module obtained as the completion of
C_{c}(\mathcal{A})
with respect to the norm induced by the
A_{1}
-valued inner product

\langle\xi\!\mid\!\eta\rangle_{A_{1}}\coloneqq\sum_{g\in G}\xi\left(g\right)^{*}\eta\left(g\right).

The so-called left regular representation of
\mathcal{A}
is the representation

\Lambda\colon\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2}(\mathcal{A}))\quad\mbox{given by }(\Lambda(a_{g})\xi)(h)\coloneqq a_{g}\xi(g^{-1}h).

By definition, the reduced cross-sectional
C^{*}
-algebra
C_{\text{red}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
is

C_{\text{red}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\coloneqq\Lambda(C_{\text{max}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right))\subseteq\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2}(\mathcal{A})).

The following result is an analogue of Fell’s absorption principle in the context of Fell bundles over discrete groups (see
[
22
]
*Proposition 18.4). Before we state and prove the result, however, we need some notation.
Let
\ell^{2}_{\pi}(G,\mathcal{H})
be the (closed) subspace of
\ell^{2}(G,\mathcal{H})
consisting of the functions
\xi\in\ell^{2}(G,\mathcal{H})
satisfying
\xi(g)\in\mathcal{H}_{g}:=\cspn\pi_{g}(\mathcal{A}_{g})\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
. A simple computation shows that this is an invariant subspace under
\pi\otimes\lambda
, hence it restricts to a representation of
\mathcal{A}
on the Hilbert space
\ell^{2}_{\pi}(G,\mathcal{H})
, which we denote by
\pi^{\lambda}
.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(A_{g})_{g\in G}
be a Fell bundle over a discrete group
G
and let
\pi=(\pi_{g})_{g\in G}
be a representation of
\mathcal{A}
. Then the representation

\pi^{\lambda}\colon C_{\text{max}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\to\mathcal{L}\left(\ell^{2}_{\pi}\left(G,\mathcal{H}\right)\right)

is weakly contained in the regular representation
\Lambda\colon C_{\text{max}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\to C_{\text{red}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
, and therefore it factors through
C_{\text{red}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
.

Proof.

A simple computation with the inner products shows that the canonical map

C_{c}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\odot\mathcal{H}\to C_{c}\left(G,\mathcal{H}\right),\;\;\text{where}\;\,f\otimes v\mapsto\left[g\mapsto\pi_{g}\left(f\left(g\right)\right)v\right]

is an isometry and extends to an isomorphism

U_{\pi}\colon\ell^{2}(\mathcal{A})\otimes_{\pi_{1}}\mathcal{H}\xrightarrow{\sim}\ell^{2}_{\pi}(G,\mathcal{H})

intertwining the representations
\Ind\pi_{1}\coloneqq\Lambda\otimes_{\pi_{1}}\textup{id}
and the restriction of
\pi^{\lambda}
to
\ell^{2}_{\pi}(G,\mathcal{H})
. The representation
\Ind\pi_{1}
, being induced via
\ell^{2}(\mathcal{A})
, is always weakly contained in the regular representation of
\mathcal{A}
.
∎

Remark 3.11
.

We note in passing that
Proposition

3.10
is
not
actually Fell’s absorption principle, as employed in
[
22
]
*Proposition 18.4, unless the Fell bundle
\mathcal{A}
is assumed to be saturated (recall
Remark

2.6
) and the representation
\pi
is nondegenerate. Indeed, in such case the space
\ell^{2}_{\pi}(G,\mathcal{H})=\ell^{2}(G,\mathcal{H})
because
\mathcal{H}_{g}=\cspn\pi_{1}(A_{g}A_{g}^{*})\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}
for all
g\in G
, and hence we do recover
[
22
]
*Proposition 18.4. Otherwise,
Proposition

3.10
only proves that a sub-representation of
\pi\otimes\lambda
, namely
\pi^{\lambda}
, is weakly contained in
\Lambda
. Nevertheless, we shall stick to the naming of
Fell’s absorption principle
here and in the sequel because, in particular, the above version recovers the usual Fell’s absorption principle for ordinary group actions, in special for group representations, where the name comes from.

3.3.
Fell’s absorption principle for inverse semigroups

We are going to follow a similar strategy as the one outlined in
Section

3.2
and
Proposition

3.10
, but this time in the case of inverse semigroups, describing induced representations of a Fell bundle. The main complication in the more general case of inverse semigroups is the description of the regular representation
\Lambda
of a Fell bundle over an inverse semigroup
S
. By construction, this representation acts not just on a completion of
C_{c}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime})
, but of a certain quotient of it. Thus it is natural to expect that an induced representation will act not just on a completion of a space of sections
S\to\mathcal{H}
, but on a quotient of it; and this is what will be happening indeed (see
Propositions

3.14
and
3.18
and
Theorem

3.20
).

The main technical points of Fell’s absorption principle in the setting of inverse semigroups are the key
Propositions

3.14
and
3.18
, which allow to construct a unitary operator that will intertwine two representations of the Fell bundle
\mathcal{A}
.

Definition 3.12
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(A_{s})_{s\in S}
be a Fell bundle over
S
, and let
\pi=(\pi_{s})_{s\in S}
be a representation of
\mathcal{A}
.

(i)

We define
C_{c}^{\pi}(S,\mathcal{H})\coloneqq\{f\in C_{c}(S,\mathcal{H})\mid f(s)\in\mathcal{H}_{s}\;\,\text{for all}\;\,s\in S\},
where
\mathcal{H}_{s}\coloneqq\cspn\pi_{s}(A_{s})\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
(recall
Definition

2.34
).

(ii)

We denote by
U_{\pi,0}\colon C_{c}(\mathcal{A})\odot\mathcal{H}\rightarrow C_{c}^{\pi}(S,\mathcal{H})
the linear map defined by

U_{\pi,0}\left(f\otimes v\right)\left(s\right)\coloneqq\pi_{s}\left(f\left(s\right)\right)v

for
f\in C_{c}(\mathcal{A})
and
v\in\mathcal{H}
.

(iii)

We also denote by
U^{\prime\prime}_{\pi,0}\colon C_{c}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime})\odot\mathcal{H}\rightarrow C_{c}^{\pi}(S,\mathcal{H})
the linear map defined in the same way by

U_{\pi,0}^{\prime\prime}\left(f\otimes v\right)\left(s\right)\coloneqq\pi_{s}^{\prime\prime}\left(f\left(s\right)\right)v

f\in C_{c}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime})
and
v\in\mathcal{H}
. Recall that
\mathcal{H}_{s}=\cspn\pi_{s}^{\prime\prime}(A_{s}^{\prime\prime})\mathcal{H}
, so the above makes sense.

For notational convenience we shall henceforth denote
U_{\pi,0}\left(f\otimes v\right)
simply by
f_{v}
whenever
f\in C_{c}(\mathcal{A})
and
v\in\mathcal{H}
. We shall use the same notation if
f\in C_{c}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime})
.

Notice that
C_{c}^{\pi}(S,\mathcal{H})
is, as a vector space, just the algebraic direct sum
\oplus_{s\in S}^{\mathrm{alg}}\mathcal{H}_{s}\delta_{s}
. For technical reasons, we also need a certain special subspace of
C_{c}^{\pi}(S,\mathcal{H})
.
For this we define:

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{0}_{s}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\spn\pi_{s}\left(A_{s}\right)\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{s};\;\,\text{and}

\displaystyle C_{c}^{\pi}\left(S,\mathcal{H}^{0}\right)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{f\in C_{c}^{\pi}\left(S,\mathcal{H}\right)\mid f\left(s\right)\in\mathcal{H}_{s}^{0}\mbox{ for all }s\in S\right\}.

Observe that, following from the definitions,
\mathcal{H}^{0}_{s}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{s}
is dense for all
s
, and

C_{c}^{\pi}(S,\mathcal{H}^{0})
is exactly the image of
U_{\pi,0}
.
Hence we may see
U_{\pi,0}
as a surjective linear map
U_{\pi,0}\colon C_{c}(\mathcal{A})\odot\mathcal{H}\rightarrow C_{c}^{\pi}(S,\mathcal{H}^{0})
. Furthermore, we now wish to construct a unitary
U_{\pi}
from
U_{\pi,0}
between certain Hausdorff completions of its domain and target spaces (see
Proposition

3.18
). For this, however, we first need to define the adequate semi-inner product on
C_{c}^{\pi}(S,\mathcal{H})
(see
Proposition

3.14
).

Lemma 3.13
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(A_{s})_{s\in S}
be a Fell bundle, and let
\pi=(\pi_{s})_{s\in S}
be a representation of
\mathcal{A}
on
\mathcal{H}
. For any given
s,t\in S
, consider

\langle x\!\mid\!y\rangle_{s,t}\coloneqq\sum_{i,j}\langle v_{i}\!\mid\!\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}\left(P\left(a_{i}^{*}b_{j}\delta_{s^{*}t}\right)\right)w_{j}\rangle,

where
x=\sum_{i}\pi_{s}(a_{i})v_{i}\in\mathcal{H}_{s}^{0}
and
y=\sum_{j}\pi_{t}(b_{j})w_{j}\in\mathcal{H}_{t}^{0}
. Then
\langle\cdot\!\mid\!\cdot\rangle_{s,t}
is a well-defined sesquilinear form on
C_{c}^{\pi}(S,\mathcal{H}^{0})
.

Furthermore,
\langle x\!\mid\!y\rangle_{s,t}=\langle x\!\mid\!\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}\left(1_{st^{*}}\right)y\rangle
for every
x\in\mathcal{H}^{0}_{s}
and
y\in\mathcal{H}^{0}_{t}
.

Proof.

First, recall that
1_{s^{*}t}
is the unit of
I_{s^{*}t,1}^{\prime\prime}
(see
Definition

2.12
) and note that this is a central projection of
A_{1}^{\prime\prime}
. By
Proposition

2.24
we have

P\left(a_{s}^{*}b_{t}\delta_{s^{*}t}\right)=\theta_{s^{*}t,1}^{\prime\prime}\left(a_{s}^{*}b_{t}1_{s^{*}t}\right)\;\;\mbox{for all }a_{s}\in A_{s}\;\,\mbox{and }b_{t}\in A_{t},

where
\theta_{s^{*}t,1}^{\prime\prime}
denotes the canonical embedding
A_{s^{*}t}^{\prime\prime}\cdot I_{s^{*}t,1}^{\prime\prime}\hookrightarrow A_{1}^{\prime\prime}
. Since these embeddings are compatible with representations (see
[
10
]
*Proposition 2.9), we have

\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}\left(\theta_{s^{*}t,1}^{\prime\prime}\left(a_{s}^{*}b_{t}1_{s^{*}t}\right)\right)=\pi_{s^{*}t}^{\prime\prime}\left(a_{s}^{*}b_{t}1_{s^{*}t}\right)=\pi_{s}\left(a_{s}\right)^{*}\pi_{t}\left(b_{t}\right)\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}\left(1_{s^{*}t}\right).

Thus, by the commutation relations given in
Proposition

2.17

Item

(iii)
,

\displaystyle\langle x\!\mid\!y\rangle_{s,t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}\langle v_{i}\!\mid\!\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}\left(P\left(a_{i}^{*}b_{j}\delta_{s^{*}t}\right)\right)w_{j}\rangle=\sum_{i,j}\langle v_{i}\!\mid\!\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}\left(1_{s^{*}t}\right)\pi_{s}\left(a_{i}\right)^{*}\pi_{t}\left(b_{j}\right)w_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}\langle\pi_{s}\left(a_{i}\right)\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}\left(1_{s^{*}t}\right)v_{i}\!\mid\!\pi_{t}\left(b_{j}\right)w_{j}\rangle=\sum_{i}\langle\pi_{s}\left(a_{i}\right)\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}\left(1_{s^{*}t}\right)v_{i}\!\mid\!y\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\langle\pi_{s}\left(a_{i}\right)v_{i}\!\mid\!\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}\left(1_{st^{*}}\right)y\rangle=\langle x\!\mid\!\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}\left(1_{st^{*}}\right)y\rangle,

as desired. The fact that
\langle\cdot\!\mid\!\cdot\rangle_{s,t}
is a sesquilinear form is now immediate.
∎

We now assemble the sesquilinear forms from
Lemma

3.13
in order to get a semi-inner product
\langle\cdot\!\mid\!\cdot\rangle_{\pi}
on
C_{c}^{\pi}(S,\mathcal{H})
, as the following key proposition shows.

Proposition 3.14
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(A_{s})_{s\in S}
be a Fell bundle, and let
\pi=(\pi_{s})_{s\in S}
be a representation of
\mathcal{A}
on
\mathcal{H}
. Then

\langle x\!\mid\!y\rangle_{\pi}\coloneqq\sum_{s,t\in S}\langle x\left(s\right)\!\mid\!\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}\left(1_{st^{*}}\right)y\left(t\right)\rangle,

where
x,y\in C_{c}^{\pi}(S,\mathcal{H})
, defines a semi-inner product on
C_{c}^{\pi}(S,\mathcal{H})
. Furthermore,

\langle f_{v}\!\mid\!g_{w}\rangle_{\pi}=\langle v\!\mid\!\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}\left(P\left(f^{*}\cdot g\right)\right)w\rangle

for all
f,g\in C_{c}(\mathcal{A})
and
v,w\in\mathcal{H}
.

Proof.

By
Lemma

3.13
it follows that
\langle x\!\mid\!y\rangle_{\pi}=\sum_{s,t\in S}\langle x(s)\!\mid\!y(t)\rangle_{s,t}
. Observe that, by construction and
Lemma

3.13
again, for
f,g\in C_{c}(\mathcal{A})
and
v,w\in\mathcal{H}
, we have

\displaystyle\langle f_{v}\!\mid\!g_{w}\rangle_{\pi}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s,t\in S}\langle f_{v}\left(s\right)\!\mid\!g_{w}\left(t\right)\rangle_{s,t}=\sum_{s,t\in S}\langle\pi_{s}\left(f\left(s\right)\right)v\!\mid\!\pi_{t}\left(g\left(t\right)\right)w\rangle_{s,t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s,t\in S}\langle v\!\mid\!\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}\left(P\left(f\left(s\right)^{*}g\left(t\right)\delta_{s^{*}t}\right)\right)w\rangle=\langle v\!\mid\!\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}\left(P\left(f^{*}\cdot g\right)\right)w\rangle,

as stated in the claim. The form
\langle\cdot\!\mid\!\cdot\rangle_{\pi}
is clearly symmetric and linear, so in order to end the proof it is enough to show it is also positive semi-definite. For that, fix any
x\in C_{c}^{\pi}(S,\mathcal{H})
and observe that

\langle x\!\mid\!x\rangle_{\pi}=\sum_{s,t\in S}\langle x\left(s\right)\!\mid\!\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}\left(1_{st^{*}}\right)x\left(t\right)\rangle=\sum_{s,t\in S}\langle\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}\left(1_{ts^{*}}\right)x\left(s\right)\!\mid\!\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}\left(1_{st^{*}}\right)x\left(t\right)\rangle\geq 0

by
Proposition

2.17

Item

(i)
.
∎

In light of
Proposition

3.14
, the following definition is only natural (compare with
Definition

3.2
).

Definition 3.15
.

Given a Fell bundle
\mathcal{A}=(A_{s})_{s\in S}
we denote by
\ell^{2}_{\pi}(S,\mathcal{H})
the Hausdorff completion of
C_{c}^{\pi}(S,\mathcal{H})
with respect to the semi-inner product
\langle\cdot\!\mid\!\cdot\rangle_{\pi}
.

We briefly observe in passing that
\langle\cdot\!\mid\!\cdot\rangle_{\pi}
is a semi-inner product, that is, there may be non-zero elements
x\in C_{c}^{\pi}(S,\mathcal{H})
such that
\langle x\!\mid\!x\rangle_{\pi}=0
. Thus,
\ell^{2}_{\pi}(S,\mathcal{H})
is only the completion of a
quotient
of
C_{c}^{\pi}(S,\mathcal{H})
. However, the fibers
\mathcal{H}_{s}
embed isometrically into
\ell^{2}(S,\mathcal{H})
(compare the following with
Lemma

2.28
):

Lemma 3.16
.

For any Fell bundle
\mathcal{A}=(A_{s})_{s\in S}
and representation
\pi
, the canonical map
\mathcal{H}_{s}^{0}\to C_{c}^{\pi}(S,\mathcal{H}^{0})\to\ell^{2}_{\pi}(S,\mathcal{H})
is isometric. In particular, it extends to an isometry
\mathcal{H}_{s}\cong\mathcal{H}_{s}\delta_{s}\hookrightarrow\ell^{2}_{\pi}(S,\mathcal{H})
.

Proof.

The proof boils down to the fact that
s^{*}s
is an idempotent, and hence
P
acts as the identity on
A_{s^{*}s}\subseteq A_{1}
. In fact, we get

\langle v\delta_{s}\!\mid\!v\delta_{s}\rangle_{\pi}=\sum_{t,r\in S}\langle\left(v\delta_{s}\right)\left(t\right)\!\mid\!\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}\left(1_{tr^{*}}\right)\left(v\delta_{s}\right)\left(r\right)\rangle=\langle v\!\mid\!v\rangle

for every
v\in\mathcal{H}_{s}
and
s\in S
.
∎

The following is a technical point that needs to be resolved for later reference.

Lemma 3.17
.

The Hilbert space
\ell^{2}(S,\mathcal{H})
is the Hausdorff completion of
C_{c}^{\pi}(S,\mathcal{H}^{0})
with respect to the semi-inner product
\langle\cdot\!\mid\!\cdot\rangle_{\pi}
.

Proof.

By
Definition

3.15
, the Hilbert space
\ell^{2}_{\pi}(S,\mathcal{H})
is the Hausdorff completion of
C_{c}^{\pi}(S,\mathcal{H})
with respect to the semi-inner product
\langle\cdot\!\mid\!\cdot\rangle_{\pi}
. Thus, in order to prove the claim it is enough to show the canonical map
C_{c}^{\pi}(S,\mathcal{H}^{0})\subseteq C_{c}^{\pi}(S,\mathcal{H})\to\ell^{2}_{\pi}(S,\mathcal{H})
has dense image. But
\mathcal{H}^{0}_{s}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{s}
is dense for every
s\in S
, and by
Lemma

3.16

\mathcal{H}_{s}
embeds isometrically into
\ell^{2}_{\pi}(S,\mathcal{H})
, and hence so does
\mathcal{H}_{s}^{0}
. Therefore the image of
C_{c}^{\pi}(S,\mathcal{H}^{0})
in
\ell^{2}_{\pi}(S,\mathcal{H})
is dense in that of
C_{c}^{\pi}(S,\mathcal{H})
, as desired.
∎

Proposition 3.18
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(A_{s})_{s\in S}
be a Fell bundle, and let
\pi=(\pi_{s})_{s\in S}
be a representation of
\mathcal{A}
on
\mathcal{H}
. Then the map
U_{\pi,0}^{\prime\prime}
from
Definition

3.12
induces a unitary operator
U_{\pi}\colon\ell^{2}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime})\otimes_{\pi_{1}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}}\mathcal{H}\to\ell^{2}_{\pi}(S,\mathcal{H})
making the diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C_{c}\left(\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime}\right)\odot\mathcal{H}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C_{c}^{\pi}\left(S,\mathcal{H}\right)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ell^{2}\left(\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime}\right)\otimes_{\pi_{1}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}}\mathcal{H}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ell^{2}_{\pi}\left(S,\mathcal{H}\right),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U_{\pi,0}^{\prime\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U_{\pi}}

commute.

Proof.

By
Proposition

3.14
, notice that

\langle f_{v}\!\mid\!g_{w}\rangle_{\pi}=\langle v\!\mid\!\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}\left(P\left(f^{*}\cdot g\right)\right)w\rangle=\langle f\otimes_{\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}}v\!\mid\!g\otimes_{\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}}w\rangle,

that is, the semi-inner product
\langle\cdot\!\mid\!\cdot\rangle_{\pi}
is compatible with
U_{\pi,0}^{\prime\prime}\colon C_{c}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime})\odot\mathcal{H}\to C_{c}^{\pi}(S,\mathcal{H}^{0})
. By sesquilinearity the above equation extends to arbitrary elements of the tensor product, i.e., we get

\langle U_{\pi,0}^{\prime\prime}\left(x\right)\!\mid\!U_{\pi,0}^{\prime\prime}\left(y\right)\rangle_{\pi}=\langle x\!\mid\!y\rangle\;\;\mbox{for all }x,y\in C_{c}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\odot\mathcal{H}.

The above equation implies the statement. Indeed, recall (see
Definitions

3.8
and
3.17
) that
\ell^{2}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime})\otimes_{\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}}\mathcal{H}
and
\ell^{2}_{\pi}(S,\mathcal{H})
are, respectively, the Hausdorff completions of
C_{c}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime})\odot\mathcal{H}
w.r.t.
\langle\cdot\!\mid\!\cdot\rangle_{\ell^{2}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime})\otimes_{\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}}\mathcal{H}}
and
C_{c}^{\pi}(S,\mathcal{H})
w.r.t.
\langle\cdot\!\mid\!\cdot\rangle_{\pi}
.
∎

Remark 3.19
.

It follows from
Proposition

3.18
that the canonical map
C_{c}(\mathcal{A})\odot\mathcal{H}\to\ell^{2}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime})\otimes_{\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}}\mathcal{H}
has dense image because the latter is isomorphic to
\ell^{2}_{\pi}(S,\mathcal{H})
and the image of
U_{\pi,0}\colon C_{c}(\mathcal{A})\odot\mathcal{H}\to C_{c}^{\pi}(S,\mathcal{H})
is dense as a subspace of
\ell^{2}_{\pi}(S,\mathcal{H})
.

We are now ready to provide a version of Fell’s absorption principle for the context of inverse semigroups; this is the main result of the section. We start with a representation
\pi
of
\mathcal{A}
, restrict it to the representation
\pi_{1}
of
A_{1}=A
and then induce it back to
\mathcal{A}
via the regular representation
\Lambda
on
\ell^{2}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime})
. This results in a description of the induced representation
\Lambda\otimes_{\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}}\textup{id}
on
\ell^{2}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime})\otimes_{\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}}\mathcal{H}
in a more explicit way, identifying it with a representation on
\ell^{2}_{\pi}(S,\mathcal{H})
, which is a quotient of a certain space of sections
S\to\mathcal{H}
.

Theorem 3.20
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(A_{s})_{s\in S}
be a Fell bundle over an inverse semigroup
S
, and let
\pi=(\pi_{s})_{s\in S}
be a nondegenerate representation of
\mathcal{A}
. Consider the induced representation
\Lambda\otimes_{\pi_{1}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}}\textup{id}=((\Lambda\otimes_{\pi_{1}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}}\textup{id})_{s})_{s\in S}
given by

(3.21)

\displaystyle(\Lambda\otimes_{\pi_{1}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}}\textup{id})_{s}\colon A_{s}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathcal{L}\left(\ell^{2}\left(\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime}\right)\otimes_{\pi_{1}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}}\mathcal{H}\right),

\displaystyle(\Lambda\otimes_{\pi_{1}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}}\textup{id})_{s}

\displaystyle\left(a_{s}\right)\left(f\otimes v\right)\coloneqq\Lambda_{s}\left(a_{s}\right)\left(f\right)\otimes v,

where
a_{s}\in A_{s},f\in\ell^{2}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime})
and
v\in\mathcal{H}
. Then the unitary isomorphism
U_{\pi}
from
Proposition

3.18
conjugates
\Lambda\otimes_{\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}}\textup{id}
into the representation
\pi^{\Lambda}=(\pi^{\Lambda}_{s})_{s\in S}
given by

(3.22)

\displaystyle\pi^{\Lambda}_{s}\colon A_{s}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathcal{L}\left(\ell^{2}_{\pi}\left(S,\mathcal{H}\right)\right),

\displaystyle\pi^{\Lambda}_{s}

\displaystyle\left(a_{s}\delta_{s}\right)\left(v_{t}\delta_{t}\right)\coloneqq\pi_{s}\left(a_{s}\right)v_{t}\delta_{st}.

In other words, we have
\pi^{\Lambda}_{s}(a_{s}\delta_{s})U_{\pi}=U_{\pi}(\Lambda\otimes_{\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}}\id)(a_{s}\delta_{s})
for all
a_{s}\in A_{s}
and
s\in S
, that is,
U_{\pi}
intertwines
\pi^{\Lambda}
and
\Lambda\otimes_{\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}}\id
.

Proof.

Given the machinery of
Propositions

3.14
and
3.18
the proof of this result boils down to a computation. Indeed, note that for all basic tensors
a_{t}\delta_{t}\otimes v\in\ell^{2}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime})\otimes_{\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}}\mathcal{H}
(recall
Lemmas

2.28
and
3.16
), where
t\in S
,
a_{t}\in A_{t}
and
v\in\mathcal{H}
, we have

U_{\pi}\left(\Lambda_{s}\left(a_{s}\delta_{s}\right)\otimes_{\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}}\id\right)\left(a_{t}\delta_{t}\otimes v\right)=U_{\pi}\left(a_{s}a_{t}\delta_{st}\otimes v\right)=\pi_{st}\left(a_{s}a_{t}\right)v\delta_{st}.

Likewise,

\pi^{\Lambda}_{s}\left(a_{s}\delta_{s}\right)\left(U_{\pi}\left(a_{t}\delta_{t}\otimes v\right)\right)=\pi^{\Lambda}_{s}\left(a_{s}\delta_{s}\right)\left(\pi_{t}\left(a_{t}\right)v\delta_{t}\right)=\pi_{s}\left(a_{s}\right)\pi_{t}\left(a_{t}\right)v\delta_{st}.

Using
Remark

3.19
it follows that
\pi^{\Lambda}_{s}(a_{s}\delta_{s})=U_{\pi}(\Lambda_{s}(a_{s}\delta_{s})\otimes_{\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}}\id)U_{\pi}^{*}
, in particular this shows that
\pi^{\Lambda}
defined as in the statement gives a well-defined representation of
\mathcal{A}
since
\Lambda\otimes_{\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}}\id
is, itself, a representation of
\mathcal{A}
.
∎

Our main use of Fell’s absorption principle comes from the following consequence of
Theorem

3.20
.

Corollary 3.23
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(A_{s})_{s\in S}
be a Fell bundle over an inverse semigroup
S
, and let
\pi=(\pi_{s})_{s\in S}
be a representation of
\mathcal{A}
. Suppose that
\pi_{1}
extends to a faithful representation of
A^{\prime\prime}
. Then the reduced cross-sectional
C^{*}
-algebra
C_{\text{red}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
is isomorphic to the
C^{*}
-algebra

C^{*}\left(\left\{\pi^{\Lambda}_{s}\left(a_{s}\right)\;\mid\;a_{s}\in A_{s}\;\text{and}\;s\in S\right\}\right)\subseteq\mathcal{L}\left(\ell^{2}_{\pi}\left(S,\mathcal{H}\right)\right),

generated by the image of the representation
\pi^{\Lambda}=(\pi^{\Lambda}_{s})_{s\in S}
given by
Theorem

3.20
.

Proof.

By definition,
C_{\text{red}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
is the
C^{*}
-algebra generated by the image of the regular representation
\Lambda\colon\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime}))
, so that
\ell^{2}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime})
may be viewed as a faithful correspondence from
C_{\text{red}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
to
A_{1}^{\prime\prime}
, that is, it is a Hilbert module over
A_{1}^{\prime\prime}
with a faithful representation of
C_{\text{red}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
. Since
\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}
is assumed to be faithful, the induced representation
\Ind\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}=\Lambda\otimes_{\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}}\id
is also faithful as a representation of
C_{\text{red}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
. And therefore so is the equivalent representation
\pi^{\Lambda}
by
Theorem

3.20
, from which the result follows.
∎

We end the discussion about Fell’s absorption principle with the following remarks.

Remark 3.24
.

Notice that, in
Corollary

3.23
, with the same proof we may actually only assume that
\pi_{1}
extends to a faithful representation of the
C^{*}
-algebra generated by
P(C_{c}(\mathcal{A}))\subseteq A_{1}^{\prime\prime}
.

Remark 3.25
.

Observe that if
S
is a group then all the discussion above reduces to Fell’s absorption principle for discrete groups in the sense of
Section

3.2
. In particular, in such case
Theorem

3.20
reduces to
Proposition

3.10
.

4.
The approximation property

The present section introduces the central notion of the paper, namely the
approximation property
of a Fell bundle
\mathcal{A}=(A_{s})_{s\in S}
. The approach here taken generalizes the approximation property for Fell bundles over discrete groups, which was first defined by Exel in
[
18
]
as follows.

Definition 4.1
.

A Fell bundle
\mathcal{A}=(A_{s})_{s\in G}
over a discrete group
G
has the
approximation property
if there is a net
\{\xi_{i}\colon G\to A_{1}\}_{i\in I}
of finitely supported sections such that

(i)

\sup_{i\in I}\|\sum_{s\in G}\xi_{i}(s)^{*}\xi_{i}(s)\|<\infty
;

(ii)

and for every
s\in G
and
a_{s}\in A_{s}

\sum_{t\in G}\xi_{i}(st)^{*}a_{s}\xi_{i}(t)\xrightarrow{i}a_{s}

in norm.

Remark 4.2
.

Observe that assertion
Definition

4.1

Item

(i)
is actually stating that
\{\xi_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is a bounded net in the Hilbert
A_{1}
-module
\ell^{2}(G,A_{1})
, since
\|\xi_{i}\|_{2}^{2}=\|\sum_{s\in G}\xi_{i}(s)^{*}\xi_{i}(s)\|
.

We use the word
section
in
Definition

4.1
to refer to functions
\xi\colon G\to A_{1}
. We do this because, in fact, in the setting of inverse semigroups the functions
\xi\colon S\to A_{1}
will be honest sections of a certain bundle (see
Definition

4.4
). The following notation will, hence, be useful throughout the rest of the text.

Definition 4.3
.

Given a Fell bundle
\mathcal{A}=(A_{s})_{s\in S}
over
S
, we denote by
\Gamma_{c}(S,\mathcal{A})
the space of finitely supported sections of the bundle
(A_{ss^{*}})_{s\in S}
, that is, functions
\xi\colon S\to A_{1}
such that
\xi(s)\in A_{ss^{*}}
for every
s\in S
.

Working, as we are, in the setting of Fell bundles over inverse semigroups makes the definition of the approximation property a little more subtle. For it, recall that
I_{s,1}\mathrel{\triangleleft}A_{1}
is a two-sided closed ideal, and that
1_{s}
denotes the unit of the von Neumann algebra
I_{s,1}^{\prime\prime}
(recall
Definitions

2.12
and
2.17
).

Definition 4.4
.

A Fell bundle
\mathcal{A}=(A_{s})_{s\in S}
over an inverse semigroup with unit
1\in S
has the
approximation property
if there is a net
\{\xi_{i}\colon S\rightarrow A_{1}\}_{i\in I}\subseteq\Gamma_{c}(S,\mathcal{A})
of finitely supported sections such that

(i)

\sup_{i\in I}\|\sum_{p,t\in S}\xi_{i}(p)^{*}\xi_{i}(t)1_{pt^{*}}\|<\infty
;

(ii)

and for every
s\in S
and
a_{s}\in A_{s}

\sum_{p,t\in S}1_{p\left(st\right)^{*}}\xi_{i}\left(p\right)^{*}a_{s}\xi_{i}\left(t\right)\to a_{s}

in norm.

We note in passing that assertion
Item

(i)
in
Definition

4.4
states that
\{\xi_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is bounded in
some

\ell^{2}
-space of sections
\ell^{2}_{\Gamma}(S,\mathcal{A})
(as is the case for groups, recall
Remark

4.2
). Nevertheless, we will not introduce such a space as we do not need it in this paper. The following remarks are in order.

Remark 4.5
.

Note that, by
Proposition

2.17
, the element
1_{s}\in I_{s,1}^{\prime\prime}
is central in
A_{1}^{\prime\prime}
, and hence, as
\xi(s)\in A_{ss^{*}}\subseteq A_{1}^{\prime\prime}
, we have
1_{p(st)^{*}}\xi(p)=\xi(p)1_{p(st)^{*}}
. This fact will be used virtually without mention in the sequel.

Remark 4.6
.

Observe that
Definition

4.4
does, in fact, generalize
Definition

4.1
. Indeed, if
S=G
is a group then it is routine to show that
1_{s^{-1}t}=0
unless
s=t
, in which case
1_{s^{-1}t}=1\in A_{1}^{\prime\prime}
. Therefore the double sums in
Definition

4.4
simplify to simple sums, and we recover
Definition

4.1
(recall that we are considering non-saturated Fell bundles, see
Remark

2.6
).

Remark 4.7
.

Definition

4.4
indicates the expected behaviour of the inverse semigroup as
bisections
over a groupoid. Indeed, the
local
behaviour of the groupoid might be implemented by different bisections, and hence the nuclearity of
C_{\text{red}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
might be witnessed over possibly different choices of elements
s\in S
.
Definition

4.4
fixes these choices, by summing over all possible labels
p,t\in S
and then restricting to the appropriate ideals, namely
I_{p,t}^{\prime\prime}
and
I_{st,p}^{\prime\prime}
in
Item

(i)
and
Item

(ii)
respectively.

We end the section with the following simple examples.

Example 4.8
.

Let
\alpha\colon G\curvearrowright X
be an action of a discrete group
G
on a compact Hausdorff space
X
. Then we may construct a canonical Fell bundle
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}=(\mathcal{A}_{\alpha,g})_{g\in G}
by setting
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha,g}\coloneqq C(X)\times\{g\}
. The multiplication and involution maps of
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}
are given by

\left(f_{1},g_{1}\right)\cdot\left(f_{2},g_{2}\right)\coloneqq\left(f_{1}\alpha_{g_{1}}\left(f_{2}\right),g_{1}g_{2}\right),\quad\left(f_{1},g_{1}\right)^{*}\coloneqq\left(\alpha_{g_{1}^{-1}}(f_{1}^{*}),g_{1}^{-1}\right).

It is not hard to see that
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}
is then a Fell bundle over
G
, and that
C_{\text{red}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}\right)\cong C(X)\rtimes_{\text{red}}G
and
C_{\text{max}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}\right)\cong C(X)\rtimes_{\text{max}}G
. With this notation, it is also routine to show that
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}
has the approximation property, in the sense of
Definition

4.4
, if, and only if,
\alpha
is amenable (as an action, see
[
8
]
*Definition 4.3.5). In fact, if
m_{i}\colon X\to\text{Prob}\left(G\right)
witnesses the amenability of
\alpha
in the sense of
[
8
]
*Definition 4.3.5, then
\xi_{i}(g)\coloneqq[x\mapsto(m_{i}^{x}(g))^{1/2}]
witnesses the approximation property of
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}
in the sense of
Definition

4.4
. Likewise, given
\xi_{i}
witnessing the approximation property, the maps
m_{i}^{x}(g)\coloneqq\xi_{i}(g)(x)^{2}
witness the amenability of
\alpha
(see
Theorem

4.16
below for a similar argument).

Example 4.9
.

Let
G
be a discrete group, and let
S\coloneqq G\sqcup\{0\}
be the inverse semigroup obtained from
G
by adjoining a
0
element. Consider the Banach spaces
A_{g}\coloneqq C[0,1]
whenever
g\in G
and
A_{0}\coloneqq C(0,1]
. It is shown in
[
27
]
*Example 4.7 that
\mathcal{A}=(A_{s})_{s\in S}
forms a Fell bundle over
S
. Moreover, it is also mentioned that both
C_{\text{max}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
and
C_{\text{red}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
are
C[0,1]
-algebras with trivial fibers at
x\in(0,1]
. Nevertheless, their fiber at
0
may differ. Indeed, if
G
is not amenable then
C_{\text{max}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
has fiber
C_{\text{max}}^{*}\left(G\right)
at
0
, whereas
C_{\text{red}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
has fiber
\mathbb{C}\oplus C_{\text{red}}^{*}\left(G\right)
. On the contrary, if
G
is amenable then both have
C_{\text{red}}^{*}\left(G\right)
as fiber at
0
, and hence
C_{\text{max}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\cong C_{\text{red}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
via the left regular representation, that is,
\mathcal{A}
has the weak containment property. In fact, it is not hard to show that
\mathcal{A}
has the approximation property if, and only if,
G
is amenable (compare with
Theorems

6.2
and
6.12
below). Indeed, if
\{F_{n}\}_{n}
is a Følner net for
G
and
\{u_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subseteq C_{0}(0,1]
is any approximate unit, then we can define

\xi_{i,n}\left(0\right)\coloneqq u_{i},\;\;\text{and}\;\;\xi_{i,n}\left(g\right)\coloneqq\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\frac{1}{\left|F_{n}\right|^{1/2}}1_{C\left[0,1\right]}&\text{if}\;g\in F_{n},\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

It then follows from a computation that the net
\{\xi_{i,n}\}_{i,n}
witnesses the approximation property for
\mathcal{A}
. Likewise, by
[
27
]
*Example 4.7, if
G
is non-amenable then
C_{\text{max}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\not\cong C_{\text{red}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
. By
Theorem

6.2
below it follows that
\mathcal{A}
does not have the approximation property.

4.1.
The approximation property for groupoids and inverse semigroups

This section builds upon
Examples

4.8
and
4.9
above, and deals with the case of Fell bundles over inverse semigroups that arise from Fell bundles over groupoids. Indeed, it is well known, and discussed below, that Fell bundles
\mathcal{A}=(A_{s})_{s\in S}
over inverse semigroups generalize in a canonical way Fell bundles over (possibly non-Hausdorff) locally compact, étale groupoids. Recently, Kranz
[
25
]
defined the
approximation property
for Fell bundles over (second countable)
Hausdorff
étale groupoids as in
Definition

4.10
. This section has the goal of proving that our approach generalizes that of
[
25
]
in a canonical way (see
Theorem

4.16
). In fact, we will not need our groupoids to be Hausdorff or second countable, nor will we need the Fell bundle to be separable or saturated, as is assumed in the main results from
[
25
]
.

We first recall that, given a locally compact groupoid
G
, a set
u\subseteq G
is a
bisection
if the range and source maps
r,s\colon u\to G^{0}
are homeomorphisms. Likewise, recall that
G
is called
étale
if its topology admits a basis formed solely by open bisections. Moreover, the set
\Bis(G)
of open bisections is well known to be an inverse semigroup. For instance, given
u,v\in\Bis(G)
their product is defined to be

u\cdot v\coloneqq\left\{gh\;\mid\;g\in u,\,h\in v\;\text{and}\;s\left(g\right)=r\left(h\right)\right\}.

For the sequel, we refer the reader to
[
25
]
for the appropriate definition of a Fell bundle over a groupoid, along with several examples of Fell bundles over locally compact Hausdorff étale groupoids. Moreover, recall that in the non-Hausdorff case
C_{c}(\mathcal{A})
denotes the space spanned by sections
G\to\mathcal{A}
that are compactly supported and continuous in a bisection of
G
. Moreover, one may define
C_{c}(G,r^{*}(\mathcal{A}^{0}))
in a similar vein. If
G
is not Hausdorff, these sections are generally not globally continuous, but they are always Borel measurable functions. In any case, it makes sense to talk about pointwise or uniform convergence of these sections, as is done in the following.

Definition 4.10
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(A_{g})_{g\in G}
be a Fell bundle over a locally compact étale groupoid
G
with Hausdorff unit space
G^{0}
. We say that
\mathcal{A}
has the
approximation property
if there is a net
\{\xi_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subseteq C_{c}(G,r^{*}(\mathcal{A}^{0}))
such that

(i)

\sup_{i\in I}\sup_{x\in G^{0}}\|\sum_{g\in G^{x}}\xi_{i}(g)^{*}\xi_{i}(g)\|<\infty
;

(ii)

and for every
f\in C_{c}(\mathcal{A})
the net

f_{i}\colon G\to\mathcal{A},\;\;g\mapsto\sum_{h\in G^{s(g)}}\xi_{i}\left(gh\right)^{*}f\left(g\right)\xi_{i}\left(h\right)=\sum_{h\in G^{r(g)}}\xi_{i}\left(h\right)^{*}f\left(g\right)\xi_{i}\left(g^{-1}h\right)

converges to
f
uniformly over compact sets of
G
.

The following definition first appeared in
[
11
]
*Definition 2.12, but was based on the previous
[
20
]
*Proposition 5.4. It precisely captures those inverse subsemigroups
S\subseteq\Bis(G)
that retain all the topological information of
G
.

Definition 4.11
.

We say an inverse semigroup
S\subseteq\Bis(G)
is
wide
if

(i)

S
covers
G
, that is,
\cup_{u\in S}u=G
; and

(ii)

for any
g\in G
,
u,v\in S
, if
g\in u\cap v
then there is some
w\in S
such that
g\in w\subseteq u\cap v
.

Note that if
S\subseteq\Bis(G)
then
S
naturally acts on
G^{0}
. Indeed, given
u\in S
and
x\in G^{0}
, the action of
u
on
x
is only defined if
s^{-1}(x)\cap u\neq\emptyset
, i.e., there is some
g\in u
with
s(g)=x
. In such a case,
ux=r(g)
, as
g\in u
is then uniquely determined, since
u
is assumed to be a bisection. Therefore, if
S\subseteq\Bis(G)
we may consider the
groupoid of germs
G^{0}\rtimes S
of the natural action of
S
on
G^{0}
, that is, the set of equivalence classes
[s,x]
, where
s\in S
and
s
is defined at
x
, where
[s,x]=[t,y]
if, and only if,
x=y
and there is some idempotent
e\in E
such that
x\in e
and
se=te
. The groupoid structure of
X\rtimes S
comes from the multiplication and involution maps

\left[s,ty\right]\cdot\left[t,y\right]\coloneqq\left[st,y\right]\;\;\text{and}\;\;\left[s,x\right]^{-1}\coloneqq\left[s^{*},sx\right].

We then call
[s,x]\in X\rtimes S
the
germ of
s
at
x
. Note that
G^{0}\rtimes S
is an étale locally compact groupoid, and that
s
defines a bisection of
G^{0}\rtimes S
. It follows that
[s,x]
is the unique arrow in the usual groupoid of germs
G^{0}\rtimes S
contained in the bisection defined by
s
and with source
x
.

Proposition 4.12
.

Let
G
be a locally compact étale groupoid, and let
S\subseteq\Bis(G)
. The following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

G^{0}\rtimes S\cong G
as topological groupoids;

(ii)

S
is wide.

In particular,
G^{0}\rtimes\Bis(G)\cong G
.

Proof.

The proof this is mostly given in
[
20
]
*Proposition 5.4,
see
[
27
]
*Proposition 2.2 for a complete proof.
∎

We now turn our attention to
Theorem

4.16
, which shows that our approximation property for Fell bundles over inverse semigroups is, at least, as general as their counterparts over étale groupoids. In order to prove it, however, we first need to set some notation and prove some preliminary results. Let
G
be a locally compact étale groupoid, and fix any wide
S\subseteq\Bis(G)
. Then, given a Fell bundle
\mathcal{A}=(A_{g})_{g\in G}
over
G
we can construct a Fell bundle
\mathcal{B}=(B_{u})_{u\in S}
, where
B_{u}
is the Banach space of
C_{0}
-sections
u\to\mathcal{A}
, i.e., continuous functions
f\colon u\to\mathcal{A}
that vanish at infinity and such that
f(g)\in A_{g}
for every
g\in u
; see
[
11
]
*Example 2.11 for further details on this construction.

Lemma 4.13
.

Let
G
be a locally compact étale groupoid, and let
S\subseteq\Bis(G)
be wide. With notations as above, given any
\eta\in C_{c}(G,r^{*}(\mathcal{A}^{0}))
there are finitely many bisections
\{u_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}\subseteq S
and
\{\eta_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}\subseteq C_{c}(u_{j},r^{*}(\mathcal{A}^{0}))
such that
\eta=\eta_{1}+\dots+\eta_{n}
.

Proof.

Recall that
C_{c}(G,r^{*}(\mathcal{A}^{0}))
is, by definition, the set of sections
G\rightarrow\mathcal{A}^{0}
that can be written as linear combinations of functions that are compactly supported in a bisection. Therefore, there are finitely many bisections
\{v_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}\subseteq\Bis(G)
and
\{\eta_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}\subseteq C_{c}(v_{i},r^{*}(\mathcal{A}^{0}))
such that
\eta=\eta_{1}+\dots+\eta_{m}
. The statement will follow once we prove that the bisections
v_{i}
may be taken to be in
S
.

Since
S\subseteq\Bis(G)
is wide we have, for every
i
,

K_{i}\coloneqq\supp\left(\eta_{i}\right)\subseteq v_{i}\subseteq G=\cup_{v\in S}v.

By hypothesis
K_{i}
is compact, and hence we may choose a finite subcover, i.e., finitely many bisections
\{u_{i,\ell}\}^{n_{i}}_{\ell=1}\subseteq S
such that
K_{i}\subseteq\cup_{\ell=1}^{n_{i}}u_{i,\ell}
. Now take any partition of unity
\{\varphi_{i,\ell}\}^{n_{i}}_{\ell=1}
subject to the cover
\{u_{i,\ell}\}^{n_{i}}_{\ell=1}
, and let
\eta_{i,\ell}\coloneqq\eta_{i}\varphi_{i,\ell}
. Then
\{\eta_{i,\ell}\}_{i,\ell}
and
\{u_{i,\ell}\}_{i,\ell}
give the desired realization of
\eta
as a linear combination of functions compactly supported in elements of
S
.
∎

We highlight the following result, which details the (expected) relation between the projections
1_{s}
in
Definition

4.4
and the germ relation. For the following and in the sequel, we shall take an étale groupoid
G
, fix a wide inverse semigroup
S\subseteq\Bis(G)
and identify
G
with the groupoid of germs
G\cong G^{0}\rtimes S
as in
Proposition

4.12
. We shall also tacitly use the convention that whenever a germ
[s,x]
is written it is already implied that
s
is defined at
x
, so that
s\cdot x
makes sense.

Lemma 4.14
.

Let
G
be a locally compact étale groupoid and let
S\subseteq\Bis(G)
be wide. Given a Fell bundle
\mathcal{A}=(A_{g})_{g\in G}
let
\mathcal{B}=(B_{u})_{u\in S}
be constructed as above. For any
p,t\in S
and
x\in G^{0}
, the following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

1_{p^{*}t}(x)=1_{A_{x}^{\prime\prime}}\in\mathcal{M}\left(A_{x}\right)\subseteq A_{x}^{\prime\prime}
.

(ii)

There is an idempotent
e\in E
such that
x\in e\leq p^{*}pt^{*}t
and
pe=te
.

(iii)

[p,x]=[t,x]
.

Proof.

Before we start the proof, we need to explain why the point evaluations in
Item

(i)
make sense, that is, we need to explain why it makes sense to evaluate
1_{s}(x)
for
s\in S
. Recall that
1_{s}
is the unit of the multiplier
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{M}(I_{s,1})\subseteq I_{s,1}^{\prime\prime}
, and
I_{s,1}
is a certain ideal of
B_{1}
which, in turn, is the
C^{*}
-algebra of
C_{0}
-sections of the upper semi-continuous
C^{*}
-bundle
(A_{x})_{x\in G^{0}}
. Hence
I_{s,1}
also admits such a description as an upper semi-continuous bundle. In fact, one can see that
I_{s,1}\cong C_{0}(O_{s,1},(A_{x})_{x\in O_{s,1}})
for the open set
O_{s,1}=\cup_{e\leq s,1}e\subseteq G^{0}
. Note, however, we do not actually need such description in this proof. We may then describe the multiplier
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{M}(I_{1,s})
as the
C^{*}
-algebra of sections
f\colon O_{s,1}\to(\mathcal{M}(A_{x}))_{x\in O_{s,1}}
satisfying
f\cdot g\in C_{0}(O_{s,1},(A_{x})_{x\in O_{s,1}})
for all
g\in C_{0}(O_{s,1},(A_{x})_{x\in O_{s,1}})
.

We now proceed with the actual proof. First note that, by construction, either
1_{p^{*}t}(x)=1_{A_{x}^{\prime\prime}}
or
1_{p^{*}t}(x)=0
. Moreover, observe that if
1_{p^{*}t}(x)\neq 0
then, by construction there is some
e\in E
such that
x\in e
and
e\leq p^{*}t
. Thus,
p^{*}pe=p^{*}pp^{*}te=p^{*}te=e
. Likewise,
et^{*}t=p^{*}tt^{*}te=e
, and hence
x\in e\leq p^{*}pt^{*}t
. Moreover, recall that, in general,
a\leq b
if, and only if,
a^{*}\leq b^{*}
. Hence
e\leq(p^{*}t)^{*}=t^{*}p
, and

pe=pp^{*}tt^{*}te=t\left(t^{*}pp^{*}t\right)e=t\left(\left(p^{*}t\right)^{*}p^{*}t\right)e=te,

which proves that
Item

(i)
implies
Item

(ii)
. For the reciprocal, let
e\in E
witness
Item

(ii)
, and observe that then
p^{*}te=p^{*}pe=e
, and therefore
1_{p^{*}t}(x)=1_{e}(x)=1_{A_{x}^{\prime\prime}}
, as claimed. The equivalence between
Item

(ii)
and
Item

(iii)
is well known (and routine to check), so we omit its proof.
∎

Remark 4.15
.

It follows from
Lemma

4.14
that for
1_{p^{*}t}(x)\neq 0
it is
not
enough that
px=tx
(recall that
S
canonically acts on
G^{0}
).

We can now prove the approach taken in this paper generalizes that of
[
25
]
to étale groupoids that may be non-Hausdorff.

Theorem 4.16
.

Let
G
be a locally compact étale groupoid with Hausdorff unit space
G^{0}
, and let
S\subseteq\Bis(G)
be wide.
If
\mathcal{A}
is a Fell bundle over
G

-bimodule. Using these constructions we may define the tensor product of a Fell bundle and an arbitrary

-algebra in the following way.

Definition 5.1
.

Given a Fell bundle
\mathcal{A}=(A_{s})_{s\in S}
over an inverse semigroup
S
and a
C^{*}
-algebra
B

(i)

\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\text{min}}B
to be the bundle over
S
with fibres
(\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\text{min}}B)_{s}\coloneqq A_{s}\otimes_{\text{min}}B
;

(ii)

\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\text{max}}B
to be the bundle over
S
with fibres
(\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\text{max}}B)_{s}\coloneqq A_{s}\otimes_{\text{max}}B
.

The following lemma just checks that
\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\text{min}}B
and
\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\text{max}}B
do carry a natural structure of Fell bundles.

Lemma 5.2
.

With notation as above, for every
s,t\in S
the map

\displaystyle\left(A_{s}\odot B\right)\odot\left(A_{t}\odot B\right)\rightarrow A_{st}\odot B,\;\;\text{where}\;\,\left(a_{s}\otimes b_{1}\right)\odot\left(a_{t}\otimes b_{2}\right)\mapsto a_{s}a_{t}\otimes b_{1}b_{2}

extends to an embedding of Hilbert bimodules
(A_{s}\otimes_{\text{min}}B)\otimes_{\text{min}}(A_{t}\otimes_{\text{min}}B)\rightarrow A_{st}\otimes_{\text{min}}B

\displaystyle A_{s}\odot B\rightarrow A_{s^{*}}\odot B,\;\;\text{where}\;\,a_{s}\otimes b\mapsto a_{s}^{*}\otimes b^{*}

extends to an isomorphism of Hilbert bimodules
(A_{s}\otimes_{\text{min}}B)^{*}\xrightarrow{\sim}A_{s^{*}}\otimes_{\text{min}}B

\otimes_{\text{max}}
.

Proof.

The same proof given in
[
2
]
for groups extends to inverse semigroups.
∎

Using
Lemma

5.2
we may now see
\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\text{min}}B
and
\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\text{max}}B
as Fell bundles over
S
whose bundle structure comes as a continuous extension of that of the “algebraic” bundle
\mathcal{A}\odot B=(A_{s}\odot B)_{s\in S}
.

We end this section with two basic observations. First, notice that

(5.3)

\left\|x\right\|_{A_{s}\otimes_{\text{max}}B}=\left\|x^{*}x\right\|^{1/2}_{A_{s^{*}s}\otimes_{\text{max}}B}\;\;\text{and}\;\;\left\|y\right\|_{A_{s}\otimes_{\text{min}}B}=\left\|y^{*}y\right\|^{1/2}_{A_{s^{*}s}\otimes_{\text{min}}B}

for every
x\in A_{s}\otimes_{\text{max}}B
and
y\in A_{s}\otimes_{\text{min}}B
. The second observation states that maximal tensor products of Fell bundles enjoy the universal property one would expect.

Lemma 5.4
.

Given two representations
\pi=(\pi_{s})_{s\in S}
of
\mathcal{A}=(A_{s})_{s\in S}
and
\rho\colon B\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
of
B
on the some Hilbert module
\mathcal{H}
satisfying
\pi_{s}(a)\rho(b)=\rho(b)\pi_{s}(a)
for all
a\in A_{s}
,
s\in S
and
b\in B
, there is a unique representation
\pi\otimes_{\text{max}}\rho=(\pi_{s}\otimes_{\text{max}}\rho)_{s\in S}
of
\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\text{max}}B
on
\mathcal{H}
extending the map
\pi_{s}\otimes\rho\colon\mathcal{A}\odot B\to\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
,
a\otimes b\mapsto\pi_{s}(a)\rho(b)
.

Proof.

Observe that, whenever
e\in E
is an idempotent, the maps
\pi_{e}\otimes\rho
extend to
A_{e}\otimes_{\text{max}}B
by the universal property of
\otimes_{\text{max}}
, since
A_{e}
is then a
C^{*}
-algebra. The existence of
\pi_{s}\otimes_{\text{max}}\rho
for general
s\in S
then follows from
Equation

5.3
, since the norm on
A_{s}\otimes_{\text{max}}B
is induced from that of
A_{s^{*}s}\otimes_{\text{max}}B
. Moreover, it is routine to show that the resulting family
\pi\otimes_{\text{max}}\rho=(\pi_{s}\otimes_{\text{max}}\rho)_{s\in S}
is a representation of
\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\text{max}}B
.
∎

In particular the above shows that given representations
\pi\colon\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{1})
and
\rho\colon B\to\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{2})
on two Hilbert modules
\mathcal{H}_{1}
and
\mathcal{H}_{2}
(over possibly different
C^{*}
-algebras), we get a representation
\rho\colon\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\text{max}}B\to\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{1}\otimes_{\text{max}}\mathcal{H}_{2})
sending
a\otimes b\mapsto\pi_{s}(a)\otimes\rho(b)
. If we look at the minimal tensor product of Hilbert modules instead, this is a prototypical representation of the minimal tensor product. Indeed, using the same idea of the above proof we see that the above representation factors through a representation of the minimal tensor product Fell bundle:

\pi\otimes_{\text{min}}\rho\colon\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\text{min}}B\to\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{1}\otimes_{\text{min}}\mathcal{H}_{2}).

5.2.
Compatibility of tensor products and cross-sectional
C^{*}
-algebras

We may now prove the expected compatibility conditions between the maximal tensor product and the universal cross-sectional
C^{*}
-algebra (see
Proposition

5.5
); and likewise between their minimal/reduced companions (see

5.9
). We start with the approach to the universal objects, as it is, unsurprisingly, simpler.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(A_{s})_{s\in S}
be a Fell bundle over an inverse semigroup
S
, and let
B
be a
C^{*}
-algebra. Then the identity map on
C_{c}(\mathcal{A})\odot B
descends to a
∗
-isomorphism
C_{\text{max}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\otimes_{\text{max}}B\cong C_{\text{max}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\text{max}}B\right)

Proof.

To prove the claim we shall construct mutually inverse
∗
-homomorphisms

\Psi\colon C_{\text{max}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\otimes_{\text{max}}B\to C_{\text{max}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\text{max}}B\right)

and

\Phi\colon C_{\text{max}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\text{max}}B\right)\to C_{\text{max}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\otimes_{\text{max}}B,

C_{c}(\mathcal{A})\odot B

Observe that, in order to construct
\Phi\colon C_{\text{max}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\text{max}}B\right)\to C_{\text{max}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\otimes_{\text{max}}B
, by the universal property of the full cross-sectional
C^{*}
-algebra (see
Proposition

2.36
), it is enough to construct a representation
\phi=(\phi_{s})_{s\in S}
of
\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\text{max}}B
. For this, simply note that the identity map on
C_{c}(\mathcal{A})\odot B
induces the maps

\phi_{s}\colon A_{s}\odot B\to C_{\text{max}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\otimes_{\text{max}}B,\;\;\text{where}\;\;a_{s}\otimes b\mapsto a_{s}\otimes b

(recall here
Lemma

2.28
and the fact that
\mathbb{C}_{\text{alg}}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\subseteq C_{\text{max}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
is dense). Note, by the universal property of the maximal tensor product
\otimes_{\text{max}}
, and since
A_{e}
is a
C^{*}
-algebra, it follows that
\phi_{e}
is
\mathrm{max}
-continuous whenever
e\in E
is an idempotent. Likewise, whenever

5.3
, we have
\|x\|^{2}=\|x^{*}x\|
, and hence, using the

-identity, we get

\displaystyle\left\|\phi_{s}\left(x\right)\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|\phi_{s}\left(x\right)^{*}\phi_{s}\left(x\right)\right\|=\left\|\left(\phi_{e}\otimes\id\right)\left(\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}a_{k}^{*}a_{l}\otimes b_{k}^{*}b_{l}\right)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}a_{k}^{*}a_{l}\otimes b_{k}^{*}b_{l}\right\|=\left\|x^{*}x\right\|=\left\|x\right\|^{2}

for all

. Therefore, the maps
\phi_{s}
are
\mathrm{max}
-continuous for every

, and hence extend to maps
\phi_{s}\colon A_{s}\otimes_{\text{max}}B\to C_{\text{max}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\otimes_{\text{max}}B
that we denote with the same symbol by abuse of notation. It is now routine to see that
\phi=(\phi_{s})_{s\in S}
forms a representation of
\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\text{max}}B

\Phi\colon C_{\text{max}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\text{max}}B\right)\to C_{\text{max}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\otimes_{\text{max}}B
is a
∗
-homomorphism as well (see
Proposition

2.36
).

Consider the
unitization Fell bundle

\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
, that is, consider
\tilde{A}_{s}\coloneqq A_{s}
if

and
\tilde{A}_{1}
to be the unitization of
A_{1}
as a
C^{*}
-algebra. Notice that
\mathcal{A}
is a Fell bundle ideal of
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
as defined in
[
28
]

]
*Proposition 4.12 we get a canonical embedding

\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\text{max}}B\hookrightarrow\tilde{\mathcal{A}}\otimes_{\text{max}}\tilde{B}
.
Now we consider the maps

and

It is straightfoward to check that

\mathcal{A}
. By the universal property of the full cross-sectional

) we obtain that the integrated form

in

of

Lemma 5.12

(see

*Proposition 9.2.5)

A

-algebra

\iota\colon A^{\prime\prime}\otimes_{\text{min}}B\hookrightarrow(A\otimes_{\text{min}}B)^{\prime\prime}

Corollary 5.13
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(A_{s})_{s\in S}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C_{\text{red}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\otimes_{\text{min}}B}

Remark 5.14
.

By our discussions above, we see that

Let
\mathcal{A}=(A_{x})_{x\in X}

-bundle. In addition, note that
\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\text{min}}C
is u.s.c. for every

-algebra

precisely when

is exact (see
[

]
*Lemma 2.3 and recall that exact

-algebras are always locally reflexive

We end the discussion on tensor products of Fell bundles with the following proposition, which shows that they behave appropriately with respect to the approximation property.

Proposition 5.15
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(A_{s})_{s\in S}
be a Fell bundle over an inverse semigroup
S
, and let
B
be any fixed
C^{*}
-algebra. If
\mathcal{A}
has the approximation property, then
\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\text{min}}B
and
\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\text{max}}B
do as well.

Proof.

Fix a net
\{\xi_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subseteq\Gamma_{c}(S,\mathcal{A})
witnessing the approximation property of
\mathcal{A}
, and consider the net

\zeta_{i,j}\left(s\right)\coloneqq\xi_{i}\left(s\right)\otimes u_{j},

where
\{u_{j}\}_{j\in J}
is an approximate unit for

\{\zeta_{i,j}\}_{i\in I,j\in J}
witnesses the approximation property for both
\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\text{max}}B
and
\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\text{min}}B
. In fact, both proofs are identical, and hence we shall restrict ourselves to the proof for
\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\text{max}}B
. First, notice that
\zeta_{i,j}\in\Gamma_{c}(S,A_{1}\odot B)
, since
\supp(\zeta_{i,j})=\supp(\xi_{i})
. In order to avoid confusion, we will denote by
1^{\text{m}}_{s}
the unit of the ideal defined as in
Definition

2.12
for the bundle
\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\text{max}}B
(we try to omit naming it). In particular, observe that
1^{\text{m}}_{s}=1_{s}\otimes 1_{B}

denotes the unit of
\mathcal{M}(B)\subseteq B^{\prime\prime}

\{\zeta_{i,j}\}_{i\in I,j\in J}
does witness the approximation property for
\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\text{max}}B
. First,

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{p,t\in S}\zeta_{i,j}\left(p\right)^{*}\zeta_{i,j}\left(t\right)1^{\text{m}}_{pt^{*}}\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{p,t\in S}\left(\xi_{i}\left(p\right)^{*}\xi_{i}\left(t\right)1_{pt^{*}}\right)\otimes u_{j}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\sum_{p,t\in S}\xi_{i}\left(p\right)^{*}\xi_{i}\left(t\right)1_{pt^{*}}\right\|<\infty,

which proves assertion
Item

(i)
in
Definition

4.4
. Assertion
Item

(ii)
is proved similarly. First, given any
x_{s}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}\otimes b_{k}\in A_{s}\odot B
observe that

\displaystyle\sum_{p,t\in S}1^{\text{m}}_{p\left(st\right)^{*}}

\displaystyle\zeta_{i,j}\left(p\right)^{*}x_{s}\zeta_{i,j}\left(t\right)=\sum_{p,t\in S}\sum_{k=1}^{n}1_{p\left(st\right)^{*}}\xi_{i}\left(p\right)^{*}a_{k}\xi_{i}\left(t\right)\otimes u_{j}b_{k}u_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\sum_{p,t\in S}1_{p\left(st\right)^{*}}\xi_{i}\left(p\right)^{*}a_{k}\xi_{i}\left(t\right)\right)\otimes u_{j}b_{k}u_{j}\xrightarrow{i,j}\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}\otimes b_{k}=x_{s}.

Given any general
y_{s}\in(\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\text{max}}B)_{s}=A_{s}\otimes_{\text{max}}B
the same result follows since
A_{s}\odot B\subseteq A_{s}\otimes_{\text{max}}B
is dense and
\{\zeta_{i,j}\}_{i,j}
is uniformly bounded in the sense of
Definition

4.4

Item

(i)
.
∎

6.
Nuclearity of the full and reduced cross-sectional
C^{*}-
algebras

In this section we prove the two main results of the paper. Firstly,
Theorem

6.2
shows that if a Fell bundle has the approximation property then it also has the weak containment property. Secondly,
Theorem

6.7
shows, using the tensor product machinery of
Section

5
, that Fell bundles with the weak containment property and nuclear unit fiber give rise to nuclear (full or reduced) cross-sectional
C^{*}
-algebras.

We start the discussion with the proof of
Theorem

6.2
, which has two main ingredients. The first is Fell’s absorption principle in the context of inverse semigroups, particularly
Corollary

3.23
. The other main ingredient is the following lemma, which allows us to create completely positive maps
in the wrong direction
, namely from
C_{\text{red}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
to
C_{\text{max}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(A_{s})_{s\in S}
be a Fell bundle over the inverse semigroup
S
. Let
\xi\in\Gamma_{c}(S,\mathcal{A})
be such that
\|\sum_{p,t\in S}\xi(p)^{*}\xi(t)1_{pt^{*}}\|<\infty
. Then there are

(i)

a
C^{*}
-algebra
B
containing a copy of
C_{\text{max}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
;

(ii)

and a bounded completely positive map
\Psi_{\xi}\colon C_{\text{red}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\rightarrow B

such that

\Psi_{\xi}\left(a_{t}\delta_{t}\right)\coloneqq\sum_{p,t\in S}1_{p\left(st\right)^{*}}\xi\left(p\right)^{*}a_{s}\xi\left(t\right)

for every
a_{s}\in A_{s}
and
s\in S
. Furthermore,
\|\Psi_{\xi}\|\leq\|\sum_{p,t\in S}\xi(p)^{*}\xi(t)1_{pt^{*}}\|
.

Proof.

Fix a faithful representation
\pi\colon C_{\text{max}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\to\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
such that the normal extension
\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}\colon A_{1}^{\prime\prime}\to\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
is also faithful. Let
B\subseteq\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
be the
C^{*}
-algebra generated by the image of
\pi
along with
\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}(A_{1}^{\prime\prime})
in
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
. It is clear that
B
contains a copy of
C_{\text{max}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
. In addition, let
\ell^{2}_{\pi}(S,\mathcal{H})
be as in
Definition

3.15
, that is,
\ell^{2}_{\pi}(S,\mathcal{H})
is the Hausdorff completion of
C_{c}^{\pi}(S,\mathcal{H})
with respect to the semi-inner product
\langle\cdot\!\mid\!\cdot\rangle_{\pi}
. Moreover, let
\pi^{\Lambda}
be as in
Theorem

3.20
, that is,
\pi^{\Lambda}_{s}(a_{s}\delta_{s})(v_{t}\delta_{t})\coloneqq\pi_{st}(a_{s}a_{t})v_{t}\delta_{st}
. By
Corollary

3.23
, the
C^{*}
-algebra generated by the image of
\pi^{\Lambda}
is canonically isomorphic to
C_{\text{red}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
, since
\pi
is assumed to be faithful.

Consider the operator

T_{\xi}\colon\mathcal{H}\to\ell^{2}_{\pi}(S,\mathcal{H}),\;\;\text{where}\;\,v\mapsto\sum_{t\in S}\pi_{1}\left(\xi\left(t\right)\right)v\delta_{t}.

Observe that for every
v\in\mathcal{H}
and
w_{r}\delta_{r}\in\mathcal{H}_{r}\delta_{r}\subseteq\ell^{2}_{\pi}(S,\mathcal{H})
, we have

\displaystyle\langle T_{\xi}v\!\mid\!w_{r}\delta_{r}\rangle_{\pi}=\sum_{t\in S}\langle\pi_{1}\left(\xi\left(t\right)\right)v\delta_{t}\!\mid\!w_{r}\delta_{r}\rangle_{\pi}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t\in S}\langle\pi_{1}\left(\xi\left(t\right)\right)v\!\mid\!\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}\left(1_{tr^{*}}\right)w_{r}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle=\langle v\!\mid\!\sum_{t\in S}\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}\left(\xi\left(t\right)^{*}1_{tr^{*}}\right)w_{r}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}},

and hence
T_{\xi}^{*}(w_{r}\delta_{r})=\sum_{t\in S}\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}(\xi(t)^{*}1_{tr^{*}})w_{r}
. In addition, observe that
T_{\xi}
is bounded, as

\displaystyle\left\|T_{\xi}v\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\langle T_{\xi}v\!\mid\!T_{\xi}v\rangle_{\pi}=\sum_{t,p\in S}\langle\pi_{1}\left(\xi\left(t\right)\right)v\delta_{t}\!\mid\!\pi_{1}\left(\xi\left(p\right)\right)v\delta_{p}\rangle_{\pi}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t,p\in S}\langle\pi_{1}\left(\xi\left(t\right)\right)v\!\mid\!\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}\left(1_{tp^{*}}\xi\left(p\right)\right)v\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=\langle v\!\mid\!\sum_{t,p\in S}\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}\left(\xi\left(t\right)^{*}1_{tp^{*}}\xi\left(p\right)\right)v\rangle_{\mathcal{H}},

and hence
\|T_{\xi}\|^{2}\leq\|\sum_{p,t\in S}\xi(p)^{*}\xi(t)1_{pt^{*}}\|<\infty
by the assumption on
\xi
. Now consider the map
\Psi_{\xi}\colon\mathcal{L}(\ell^{2}_{\pi}(S,\mathcal{H}))\to\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
given by
\Psi_{\xi}(x)\coloneqq T_{\xi}^{*}xT_{\xi}
, and observe that
\Psi_{\xi}
is bounded, since
T_{\xi}
is bounded. Likewise, it is completely positive. Furthermore, observe that for every
v\in\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle\Psi_{\xi}\left(\pi^{\Lambda}_{s}\left(a_{s}\delta_{s}\right)\right)v

\displaystyle=\left(T_{\xi}^{*}\pi^{\Lambda}_{s}\left(a_{s}\delta_{s}\right)T_{\xi}\right)v=\sum_{t\in S}T_{\xi}^{*}\pi^{\Lambda}_{s}\left(a_{s}\delta_{s}\right)\pi_{1}\left(\xi\left(t\right)\right)v\delta_{t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t\in S}T_{\xi}^{*}\pi_{s}\left(a_{s}\xi\left(t\right)\right)v\delta_{st}=\sum_{p,t\in S}\pi_{1}^{\prime\prime}\left(\xi\left(p\right)^{*}1_{p\left(st\right)^{*}}\right)\pi_{s}\left(a_{s}\xi\left(t\right)\right)v

\displaystyle=\sum_{p,t\in S}\pi_{s}^{\prime\prime}\left(1_{p\left(st\right)^{*}}\xi\left(p\right)^{*}a_{s}\xi\left(t\right)\right)v,

as desired. In addition, note that
\|\Psi_{\xi}\|\leq\|T_{\xi}^{*}T_{\xi}\|=\|T_{\xi}\|^{2}<\infty
, as proved above. Lastly, observe that the image of
\Psi
is contained in
B
, as
1_{r}\in A_{1}^{\prime\prime},\xi(p)\in A_{1}
and
a_{s}\in A_{s}\delta_{s}\subseteq C_{\text{max}}^{*}\left(A\right)
, all of which are embedded into
B
via
\pi
.
∎

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(A_{s})_{s\in S}
be a Fell bundle over the inverse semigroup
S
. If
\mathcal{A}
has the approximation property then it has the weak containment property, i.e., the regular representation
\Lambda\colon C_{\text{max}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\rightarrow C_{\text{red}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
is a
∗
-isomorphism.

Proof.

Let
\{\xi_{i}\}_{i\in I}\subseteq\Gamma_{c}(S,\mathcal{A})
witness the approximation property of
\mathcal{A}
, and let
\Psi_{\xi_{i}}\colon C_{\text{red}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\to B
be as in
Lemma

6.1
. In fact, observe that it follows from the proof of
Lemma

6.1
that the
C^{*}
-algebra
B
does
not
depend on
\xi_{i}
, so we may take it to be generated by
C_{\text{max}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
and
A_{1}^{\prime\prime}
. Consider then maps
\Phi_{i}\colon C_{\text{max}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\to B
given by
\Phi_{i}\coloneqq\Psi_{\xi_{i}}\circ\Lambda
, and observe they are completely positive and bounded. Moreover,

(6.3)

\Phi_{i}\left(a_{s}\delta_{s}\right)=\Psi_{\xi_{i}}\left(\Lambda_{s}\left(a_{s}\delta_{s}\right)\right)=\Psi_{\xi_{i}}\left(a_{s}\delta_{s}\right)=\sum_{p,t\in S}1_{p\left(st\right)^{*}}\xi_{i}\left(p\right)^{*}a_{s}\xi_{i}\left(t\right)\xrightarrow{i}a_{s}\delta_{s}

since
\{\xi_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is assumed to witness the approximation property for
\mathcal{A}
. Therefore, since the subspaces
A_{t}\delta_{t}
span
\mathbb{C}_{\text{alg}}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
and this algebra is dense in
C_{\text{max}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
, we obtain that
\Phi_{i}(x)\to x
for every
x\in C_{\text{max}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
.

Now, for any
x\in C_{\text{max}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
in the kernel of
\Lambda
, we have

x=\lim_{i}\Phi_{i}\left(x\right)=\lim_{i}\Psi_{\xi_{i}}\left(\Lambda\left(x\right)\right)=0,

which shows that
\Lambda
is, indeed, injective, and hence a
∗
-isomorphism.
∎

Remark 6.4
.

It is apparent from the proof of
Theorem

6.2
that we could start with a net
\{\xi_{i}\}_{i}
in
\Gamma_{c}(S,\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime})
witnessing the approximation property of
\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime}
in order to get the weak containment of
\mathcal{A}
. Moreover, in this situation it is actually enough to have weak*-convergence in

(ii)
in order to get the conclusion in the proof above, as this would then yield weak convergence instead of norm convergence in
Equation

6.3
after taking a representation as in
Lemma

6.1
.

We now turn to the proof of
Theorem

6.7
. We start by recording the following known general result.

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
A\subseteq B
is an inclusion of
C^{*}
-algebras with a weak conditional expectation
P\colon B\to A^{\prime\prime}
. If
B
is nuclear, then so is
A
.

Proof.

Observe that
P
extends to a normal conditional expectation
P^{\prime\prime}\colon B^{\prime\prime}\to A^{\prime\prime}
. In such case,
B
is nuclear if, and only if,
B^{\prime\prime}
is an injective von Neumann algebra, and this property passes to von Neumann subalgebras which are the image of a conditional expectation.
∎

Lemma 6.6
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(A_{s})_{s\in S}
be a Fell bundle over
S
, and let
B
be any given
C^{*}
-algebra. If
A_{1}
is nuclear, then
\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\text{min}}B\cong\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\text{max}}B
.

Proof.

We have to show that
A_{s}\otimes_{\text{min}}B\cong A_{s}\otimes_{\text{max}}B
for all
s\in S
. By
Equation

5.3
this is immediate once we have
A_{e}\otimes_{\text{min}}B\cong A_{e}\otimes_{\text{max}}B
for all idempotents
e\in E
, which holds since, by assumption,
A_{1}
is nuclear, and nuclearity is well-known to pass to ideals.
∎

Theorem 6.7
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(A_{s})_{s\in S}
be a Fell bundle over an inverse semigroup
S
. Then the following assertions hold:

(i)

If either
C_{\text{red}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
or
C_{\text{max}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
is nuclear, then so is
A_{1}
.

(ii)

If
A_{1}
is nuclear and
\mathcal{A}
has the approximation property, then
C_{\text{max}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\cong C_{\text{red}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
is nuclear.

Proof.

Item

(i)
follows from
Lemma

6.5
. The proof of
Item

(ii)
uses the tensor product machinery of
Section

5
. Indeed, first observe that, by
Theorem

6.2
, we have
C_{\text{max}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\cong C_{\text{red}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
via the left regular representation, that is, the map induced from the identity map on
C_{c}(\mathcal{A})
. Then, for any unital
C^{*}
-algebra
B
the identity map on
C_{c}(\mathcal{A})\odot B
descends into
∗
-isomorphisms

\displaystyle C_{\text{max}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\otimes_{\text{max}}B

\displaystyle\cong C_{\text{max}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\text{max}}B\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\left(\text{Prop.}~\ref{prop:tensor-prod:full-comp}\right)

\displaystyle\cong C_{\text{max}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\text{min}}B\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\left(\text{Lem.}~\ref{lemma:tensor-prod-nuclear-fiber}\right)

\displaystyle\cong C_{\text{red}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\text{min}}B\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\left(\text{Prop.}~\ref{prop:tensor-prod:ap}\;\text{and}\;\text{Thm.}~\ref{thm:ap-implies-wk}\right)

\displaystyle\cong C_{\text{red}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\otimes_{\text{min}}B

\displaystyle\quad\quad\left(\text{Prop.}~\ref{prop:tensor-prod:red-comp}\right)

\displaystyle\cong C_{\text{max}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\otimes_{\text{min}}B,

\displaystyle\quad\quad\left(\text{Thm.}~\ref{thm:ap-implies-wk}\right)

as desired.
∎

An immediate consequence is the following.

Corollary 6.8
.

If
S
is a
C^{*}
-amenable inverse semigroup then
C_{\text{max}}^{*}\left(S\right)\cong C_{\text{red}}^{*}\left(S\right)
via the left regular representation, and both are nuclear
C^{*}
-algebras.

Another consequence of our main results (

4.12
,
4.16
and
6.7
) is the following corollary, which generalizes the main results of
[

]
in several ways. In particular, we do not require the groupoid to be Hausdorff or second-countable, nor do we need the Fell bundle be saturated or separable.

Corollary 6.9
.

Let
G
be an étale groupoid with Hausdorff unit space, and let
\mathcal{A}=(A_{g})_{g\in G}
be a Fell bundle over

. The following assertions then hold:

(i)

If
\mathcal{A}
has the approximation property then
C_{\text{red}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\cong C_{\text{max}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
via the left regular representation.

(ii)

If
\mathcal{A}
has the approximation property and
C_{0}(\mathcal{A}^{0})
is nuclear then
C_{\text{red}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\cong C_{\text{max}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
is nuclear.

This follows as a combination of
Theorems

6.2
and
6.7
and the fact that if
S
is a wide inverse semigroup of bisections of

[

,
10
]
.
∎

Theorem 6.10

(
[

*Theorem 4.11)
.

Given a Fell bundle
\mathcal{A}=(A_{s})_{s\in S}
over
S
, the identity map on
C_{c}(\mathcal{A})
induces canonical quotient maps

C_{\text{max}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\twoheadrightarrow C_{\text{red}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\twoheadrightarrow C_{\text{ess}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)

that restrict to the identity on
A_{1}
.

As an immediate consequence of the above theorem we get the following.

Corollary 6.11
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(A_{s})_{s\in S}
be a Fell bundle over a unital inverse semigroup
S
. If
A_{1}
is nuclear and
\mathcal{A}
has the approximation property, then
C_{\text{ess}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
is nuclear.

Proof.

The claim follows from
Theorems

6.7
and
6.10
and the very deep result of Kirchberg that states that quotients of nuclear
C^{*}
-algebras are nuclear as well (see, e.g.,
[
8
]
*Corollary 9.4.4).
∎

Observe that the reverse of
Corollary

6.11
does not hold, that is, that
C_{\text{ess}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
may be nuclear and
\mathcal{A}
not satisfy the approximation property, as the following example shows.

Example 6.12
.

Let
G
be a group, and let
S
and
\mathcal{A}=(A_{s})_{s\in S}
be as in
Example

4.9
(see also
[
27
]
*Example 4.7). By the discussion in
Example

4.9
, if
G
is non-amenable then
\mathcal{A}
does not have the approximation property, and the left regular representation
\Lambda\colon C_{\text{max}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\to C_{\text{red}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
is not injective. However, as stated in
[
27
]
*Example 4.7,
C_{\text{ess}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
is nuclear, regardless of the amenability of
G
. Indeed, in this case
C_{\text{ess}}^{*}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\cong C[0,1]
, since
(0,1]\subseteq[0,1]
is open and dense, and hence
C_{0}(0,1]\subseteq C[0,1]
is an essential (closed two-sided) ideal.

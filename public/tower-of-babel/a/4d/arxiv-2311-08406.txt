Abstract
. The aim of this paper is to transfer the restrictedness theory to Hom-Lie algebras. The concept of restricted Hom-Lie algebras which is introduced in [ 4 ] will be used in this paper. First, the existence of p p -structures on a Hom-Lie algebra is studied and the direct sum of restricted Hom-Lie algebras is analyzed. Then, the definition of a restrictable Hom-Lie algebra is given and the equivalence relation between restrictable Hom-Lie algebras and restricted Hom-Lie algebras is constructed. Finally, the p p -envelopes of a Hom-Lie algebra are defined and studied.



1.
Introduction and preliminaries

In physics,
q
-deformed Lie algebras naturally appeared when physicists
studied
q
-oscillators (see
[
6
]
). In
q
-deformed Lie algebras,
the classical Jacobi identity was replaced by
q
-deformed Jacobi
identity. Then the class of algebras was formally defined as Hom-Lie
algebras in
[
10
,
12
]
, where the Hom-Jacobi identity was called
as Jacobi-like identity with the form

\underset{x,y,z}{\leftturn}\left\langle(\varsigma+\mathrm{id})(x),\left\langle y,z\right\rangle_{\varsigma}\right\rangle_{\varsigma}=0.

In
[
12
]
, Hom-Lie algebras were further generalized by twisting
not only the Jacobi identity, but also the skew-symmetry and the structure
map. Recently, many people attach importance to the study of Hom-Lie
algebras for its close relation to discrete and deformed vector fields
and differential calculus
[
10
,
12
,
13
]
. Particularly, the enveloping
algebras of Hom-Lie algebras were constructed and studied in
[
22
]
;
In
[
14
]
, the authors extended the 1-parameter formal deformation
theory to Hom-Lie algebras and constructed a cohomology theory adapted
to this deformation theory; Quadratic Hom-Lie algebras were introduced
and studied in
[
3
]
; All rigid complex 3-dimensional multiplicative
Hom-Lie algebras were obtained in
[
1
]
by studying all deformations
of multiplicative Hom-Lie algebras; Hom-Lie structures on tensor products
were studied and the representations of current Hom-Lie algebras were
considered in
[
2
]
; In
[
8
]
, the authors investigated
the Nijenhuis operators on Hom-Lie algebras and defined the cohomology
associated to a Nijenhuis operator to study formal deformations on
a Hom-Lie algebra.

The concept of a restricted Lie algebra is attributable to Jacobson
(see
[
11
]
). It plays a predominant role in modular Lie algebra
theory, especially in the classification of the finite-dimensional
simple modular Lie algebras
[
18
,
19
]
. Because of its important
role in modular theory, restrictedness theory attracted great attention.
Bouarroudj, Lebedev, Leites and Shchepochkina studied the queerification
of restricted simple Lie algebras in characteristic 2
[
5
]
.
Petrogradski and Wang generalized the concept to modular Lie superalgebras
in
[
15
]
and
[
21
]
respectively. Then Sun studied the restricted
envelopes of Lie superalgebras in
[
20
]
. Moreover, Guan
[
9
]

and Bouarroudj
[
4
]
defined restricted Hom-Lie algebras respectively.

Due to the importance of Hom-Lie algebras and restrictedness theory,
this paper attempts to transfer the restrictedness theory to Hom-Lie
algebras. Although Guan has studied the restrictedness theory of Hom-Lie
algebras in
[
9
]
, definition of restricted Hom-Lie algebras
therein is too limited to be appropriate to the consideration of some
problems (see
[
4
]
). Therefore, the authors of
[
4
]

gave an alternative definition, which will be used in this paper.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 1, after recalling and
giving several basic definitions, we point out the difference between
two definitions of restricted Hom-Lie algebras (Remark 1.9) and give
several examples (Examples 1.10, 1.11 and 1.12). In Section 2, we
give a sufficient condition of the existence of
p
-structures on
Hom-Lie algebras (Theorem 2.3). In addition, the direct sum of restricted
Hom-Lie algebras is studied. In particular, it is proved that the
direct sum of two restricted Hom-Lie algebras is still a restricted
Hom-Lie algebra (Proposition 2.4). As by-products, some properties
of the morphism of restricted Hom-Lie algebras are obtained (Thoerems
2.8 and 2.9). In Section 3, the definition of restrictable Hom-Lie
algebras is introduced and examples of which are shown (Examples 3.2,
3.3 and 3.4). Moreover, the equivalence relation between restricted
and restrictable Hom-Lie algebras is built (Theorem 3.5). In Section
4, we study the restricted envelopes of Hom-Lie algebras. We generalize
the notion of
p
-envelopes and its relatives to Hom-Lie algebras
and construct an elementary theory. As an application, the Hom-version
of Iwasawa’s theorem is given (Theorem 4.8), which is a result on
the representation of a finite-dimensional Hom-Lie algebra.

(2) A Hom-Lie algebra is called a multiplicative Hom-Lie algebra if

(3) A Hom-Lie algebra is called a regular Hom-Lie algebra if

(4) A sub-vector space

Definition 1.4

(see

Proposition 1.8

Let

[p]_{\alpha}

For any

[\alpha(x),\alpha^{p-1}(z)]=\alpha([x,\alpha^{p-1}(\alpha^{-1}(z))])=0

\displaystyle[[x,y],\alpha^{p-1}(z)]

\displaystyle=[[y,\alpha^{p-2}(z)],\alpha(x)]+[[\alpha^{p-2}(z),x],\alpha(y)]

\displaystyle=[\alpha^{p}([\alpha^{-p}(y),\alpha^{-2}(z)]),\alpha(x)]+[[\alpha^{p-1}(\alpha^{-1}(z)),x],\alpha(y)]

To check that the triple

is a Hom-Lie algebra, we can refer to
[
9
]
. The map
[p]:L\oplus L_{1}\rightarrow L\oplus L_{1}

will be proved to be a
p
-structure on
L\oplus L_{1}
. Indeed,
for all
x_{1}+y_{1},x_{2}+y_{2}\in L\oplus L_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{ad}((x_{1}+y_{1})^{[p]})\text{\textopenbullet}(\alpha+\beta)^{p-1}(x_{2}+y_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[(x_{1}+y_{1})^{[p]},\alpha{}^{p-1}(x_{2})+\beta{}^{p-1}(y_{2})]_{L\oplus L_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[x_{1}^{[p]_{\alpha}}+y_{1}^{[p]_{\beta}},\alpha{}^{p-1}(x_{2})+\beta{}^{p-1}(y_{2})]_{L\oplus L_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[x_{1}^{[p]_{\alpha}},\alpha{}^{p-1}(x_{2})]_{L}+[y_{1}^{[p]_{\beta}},\beta{}^{p-1}(y_{2})]_{L_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{ad}x_{1}^{[p]_{\alpha}}\text{\textopenbullet}\alpha^{p-1}(x_{2})+\mathrm{ad}y_{1}^{[p]_{\beta}}\text{\textopenbullet}\beta^{p-1}(y_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{ad}(\alpha^{p-1}(x_{1}))\text{\textopenbullet}\mathrm{ad}(\alpha^{p-2}(x_{1}))\text{\textopenbullet}\cdot\cdot\cdot\text{\textopenbullet}\mathrm{ad}(x_{1})(x_{2})

\displaystyle+\mathrm{ad}(\beta^{p-1}(y_{1}))\text{\textopenbullet}\mathrm{ad}(\beta^{p-2}(y_{1}))\text{\textopenbullet}\cdot\cdot\cdot\text{\textopenbullet}\mathrm{ad}(y_{1})(y_{2})

and

\displaystyle\mathrm{ad}((\alpha+\beta)^{p-1}(x_{1}+y_{1}))\text{\textopenbullet}\mathrm{ad}((\alpha+\beta)^{p-2}(x_{1}+y_{1}))\text{\textopenbullet}\cdot\cdot\cdot\text{\textopenbullet}\mathrm{ad}(x_{1}+y_{1})(x_{2}+y_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{ad}(\alpha^{p-1}(x_{1})+\beta{}^{p-1}(y_{1}))\text{\textopenbullet}\mathrm{ad}(\alpha{}^{p-2}(x_{1}\text{)}+\beta{}^{p-2}(y_{1}))\text{\textopenbullet}\cdot\cdot\cdot\text{\textopenbullet}\mathrm{ad}(x_{1}+y_{1})(x_{2}+y_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{ad}(\alpha^{p-1}(x_{1}))\text{\textopenbullet}\mathrm{ad}(\alpha^{p-2}(x_{1}))\text{\textopenbullet}\cdot\cdot\cdot\text{\textopenbullet}\mathrm{ad}(x_{1})(x_{2})

\displaystyle+\mathrm{ad}(\beta^{p-1}(y_{1}))\text{\textopenbullet}\mathrm{ad}(\beta^{p-2}(y_{1}))\text{\textopenbullet}\cdot\cdot\cdot\text{\textopenbullet}\mathrm{ad}(y_{1})(y_{2}).

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{ad}((x_{1}+y_{1})^{[p]})\text{\textopenbullet}(\alpha+\beta)^{p-1}(x_{2}+y_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{ad}((\alpha+\beta)^{p-1}(x_{1}+y_{1}))\text{\textopenbullet}\mathrm{ad}((\alpha+\beta)^{p-2}(x_{1}+y_{1}))\text{\textopenbullet}\cdot\cdot\cdot\text{\textopenbullet}\mathrm{ad}(x_{1}+y_{1})(x_{2}+y_{2}).

Moreover, for all
k\in\mathbb{F},x+y\in L\oplus L_{1}
, one gets

\displaystyle(k(x+y))^{[p]}=(kx+ky)^{[p]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(kx)^{[p]_{\alpha}}+(ky)^{[p]_{\beta}}=k^{p}x{}^{[p]_{\alpha}}+k^{p}y{}^{[p]_{\beta}}=k^{p}(x+y){}^{[p]}.

Finally, for all
x_{1}+y_{1},x_{2}+y_{2}\in L\oplus L_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle((x_{1}+y_{1})+(x_{2}+y_{2}))^{[p]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle((x_{1}+x_{2})+(y_{1}+y_{2}))^{[p]}=(x_{1}+x_{2})^{[p]_{\alpha}}+(y_{1}+y_{2}){}^{[p]_{\beta}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x_{1}^{[p]_{\alpha}}+x_{2}^{[p]_{\alpha}}+\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}s_{i}(x_{1},x_{2})+y_{1}^{[p]_{\beta}}+y_{2}^{[p]_{\beta}}+\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}s_{i}(y_{1},y_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x_{1}^{[p]_{\alpha}}+y_{1}^{[p]_{\beta}})+(x_{2}^{[p]_{\alpha}}+y_{2}^{[p]_{\beta}})+\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}s_{i}(x_{1}+y_{1},x_{2}+y_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x_{1}+y_{1})^{[p]}+(x_{2}+y_{2})^{[p]}+\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}s_{i}(x_{1}+y_{1},x_{2}+y_{2}).

It follows that
[p]
is a
p
-structure on
L\oplus L_{1}
.

To sum up,
(L\oplus L_{1},[.,.]_{L\oplus L_{1}},\alpha+\beta,[p])

is a restricted Hom-Lie algebra.
∎

For further discussion, let’s recall the definitions of a (restricted)
morphism of a Hom-Lie algebra first.

Definition 2.5
.

(see
[
17
]
) Let
(L,[\cdot,\cdot]_{L},\alpha)

and
(L_{1},[\cdot,\cdot]_{L_{1}},\alpha_{1})
be two Hom-Lie algebras.
A linear map
f:L\rightarrow L_{1}
is said to be a morphism of Hom-Lie
algebras if

\displaystyle f[x,y]_{L}

\displaystyle=[f(x),f(y)]_{L_{1}},\forall x,y\in L,

\displaystyle f\text{\textopenbullet}\alpha

\displaystyle=\alpha_{1}\text{\textopenbullet}f.

Definition 2.6
.

(see
[
9
]
) A morphism of restricted Hom-Lie
algebras

f:(L,[\cdot,\cdot]_{L},\alpha,[p]_{\alpha})\rightarrow(L_{1},[\cdot,\cdot]_{L_{1}},\alpha_{1},[p]_{\alpha_{1}})

is said to be restricted if
f(x^{[p]_{\alpha}})=(f(x))^{[p]_{\alpha_{1}}}
,
for all
x\in L
.

For a linear map
\phi:L\rightarrow L^{\prime}
, let
\mathscr{\mathfrak{G}}_{\phi}=\{(x,\phi(x))|x\in L\}

denote the graph of
\phi
. We obtain the following proposition,
which proof is analogous to that of Proposition 3.4 in
[
9
]
:

Proposition 2.7
.

A linear map
\phi:(L,[\cdot,\cdot]_{L},\alpha,[p]_{\alpha})\rightarrow(L_{1},[\cdot,\cdot]_{L_{1}},\beta,[p]_{\beta})

is a restricted morphism of restricted Hom-Lie algebras if and only
if the graph
\mathfrak{G}_{\phi}\subseteq L\oplus L_{1}
is a restricted
Hom-Lie subalgebra of
(L\oplus L_{1},[\cdot,\cdot]_{L\oplus L_{1}},\alpha+\beta,[p])
,
which is a Hom-Lie algebra defined as in Proposition 2.4.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
(L,[\cdot,\cdot]_{L},\alpha,[p]_{\alpha})

be a restricted Hom-Lie algebra,
(L_{1},[\cdot,\cdot]_{L_{1}},\beta)

a multiplicative Hom-Lie algebra, and
f:L\rightarrow L_{1}
a morphism
of Hom-Lie algebras. Then
(f(L),[\cdot,\cdot]_{L_{1}},\beta|_{f(L)},[p]_{\beta})

is a restricted Hom-Lie algebra, where
[p]_{\beta}:f(L)\rightarrow f(L),f(x)\mapsto f(x^{[p]_{\alpha}})
.

Proof.

For
f(x),f(y)\in f(L)
, we have
[f(x),f(y)]_{L_{1}}=f([x,y]_{L})\in f(L)

and
\beta(f(x))=f(\alpha(x))\in f(L)
. Therefore,
(f(L),[\cdot,\cdot]_{L_{1}},\beta|_{f(L)})

is a Hom-Lie subalgebra of
L_{1}
. Next, the map
[p]_{\beta}:f(L)\rightarrow f(L)

will be proved to be a
p
-structure on
f(L)
and
(f(L),[\cdot,\cdot]_{L_{1}},

\beta|_{f(L)},[p]_{\beta})
is restricted.

The check of axioms (R2) and (R3) given in Definition
1.5
is a
routine. We will show the verification of axiom (R1) detailedly. For

f(x),f(y)\in f(L)
, one gets

\displaystyle\mathrm{ad}(f(x)^{[p]_{\beta}})\text{\textopenbullet}\beta^{p-1}(f(y))=[f(x)^{[p]_{\beta}},\beta^{p-1}(f(y))]_{L_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[f(x^{[p]_{\alpha}}),f(\alpha^{p-1}(y))]_{L_{1}}=f([x^{[p]_{\alpha}},\alpha^{p-1}(y)]_{L})=f(\mathrm{ad}(x^{[p]_{\alpha}})\circ\alpha^{p-1}(y))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(\mathrm{ad}(\alpha^{p-1}(x))\text{\textopenbullet}\mathrm{ad}(\alpha^{p-2}(x))\text{\textopenbullet}\cdot\cdot\cdot\text{\textopenbullet}\mathrm{ad}(x)(y))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f([\alpha^{p-1}(x),[\alpha^{p-2}(x),\cdot\cdot\cdot,[x,y]_{L}]_{L}]_{L})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[f(\alpha^{p-1}(x)),f([\alpha^{p-2}(x),\cdot\cdot\cdot,[x,y]_{L}]_{L})]_{L_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[f(\alpha^{p-1}(x)),[f(\alpha^{p-2}(x)),\cdot\cdot\cdot,[f(x),f(y)]_{L_{1}}]_{L_{1}}]_{L_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\beta^{p-1}(f(x)),[\beta^{p-2}(f(x)),\cdot\cdot\cdot,[f(x),f(y)]_{L_{1}}]_{L_{1}}]_{L_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{ad}(\beta^{p-1}(f(x)))\circ\mathrm{ad}(\beta^{p-2}(f(x)))\circ\cdot\cdot\cdot\circ\mathrm{ad}(f(x))(f(y)),

that is, axiom (R1) holds. The proof is complete.
∎

Theorem 2.9
.

Let
(L,[\cdot,\cdot]_{L},\alpha)
and
(L_{1},[\cdot,\cdot]_{L_{1}},\beta)

be multiplicative Hom-Lie algebras and
f:L\rightarrow L_{1}
a morphism
of Hom-Lie algebras. If
g
is a Hom-Lie subalgebra of
L_{1}
and

[p]_{\beta}:g\rightarrow g
is a
p
-structure, then
(f^{-1}(g),[\cdot,\cdot]_{L},\alpha|_{f^{-1}(g)})

is a Hom-Lie subalgebra of
L
and
[p]_{\alpha}:f^{-1}(g)\rightarrow f^{-1}(g),x\mapsto f^{-1}(f(x)^{[p]_{\beta}})

is a
p
-structure on
f^{-1}(g)
.

Proof.

Firstly,
f^{-1}(g)
will be shown to be a Hom-Lie subalgebra of

L
. For
x,y\in f^{-1}(g)
, one gets
f(x),f(y)\in g
. It follows
that
f([x,y]_{L})=[f(x),f(y)]_{L_{1}}\in g
and
f(\alpha(x))=\beta(f(x))\in g
.
Therefore,
[x,y]_{L}\in f^{-1}(g)
and
\alpha(x)\in f^{-1}(g)
.

Secondly, we will show that
[p]_{\alpha}:f^{-1}(g)\rightarrow f^{-1}(g)

is a
p
-structure. Indeed, for
x,y\in f^{-1}(g)
, there exist

u,v\in g
such that
f(x)=u,f(y)=v
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathrm{ad}(x^{[p]_{\alpha}})\text{\textopenbullet}\alpha^{p-1}(y)=[x^{[p]_{\alpha}},\alpha^{p-1}(y)]_{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[f^{-1}(f(x)^{[p]_{\beta}}),f^{-1}(f(\alpha^{p-1}(y)))]_{L}=f^{-1}([f(x)^{[p]_{\beta}},f(\alpha^{p-1}(y))]_{L_{1}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f^{-1}([f(x)^{[p]_{\beta}},\beta^{p-1}(f(y))]_{L_{1}})=f^{-1}([u^{[p]_{\beta}},\beta^{p-1}(v)]_{L_{1}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f^{-1}(\mathrm{ad}(u^{[p]_{\beta}})\circ\beta^{p-1}(v))=f^{-1}(\mathrm{ad}(\beta^{p-1}(u))\text{\textopenbullet}\mathrm{ad}(\beta^{p-2}(u))\text{\textopenbullet}\cdot\cdot\cdot\text{\textopenbullet}\mathrm{ad}(u)(v))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f^{-1}([\beta^{p-1}(u),[\beta^{p-2}(u),\cdot\cdot\cdot,[u,v]_{L_{1}}]_{L_{1}}]_{L_{1}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[f^{-1}(\beta^{p-1}(u)),f^{-1}([\beta^{p-2}(u),\cdot\cdot\cdot,[u,v]_{L_{1}}]_{L_{1}})]_{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[f^{-1}(\beta^{p-1}(u)),[f^{-1}(\beta^{p-2}(u)),\cdot\cdot\cdot,[f^{-1}(u),f^{-1}(v)]_{L}]_{L}]_{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\alpha^{p-1}(f^{-1}(u)),[\alpha^{p-2}(f^{-1}(u)),\cdot\cdot\cdot,[f^{-1}(u),f^{-1}(v)]_{L}]_{L}]_{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\alpha^{p-1}(x),[\alpha^{p-2}(x),\cdot\cdot\cdot,[x,y]_{L}]_{L}]_{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{ad}(\alpha^{p-1}(x))\text{\textopenbullet}\mathrm{ad}(\alpha^{p-2}(x))\text{\textopenbullet}\cdot\cdot\cdot\text{\textopenbullet}\mathrm{ad}(x)(y).

Until now, the check of axiom (R1) is complete. Moreover, the check
of axioms (R2) and (R3) is a routine, see
[
16
]
for more details.
The proof is complete.
∎

3.
Restrictable Hom-Lie Algebras

Definition 3.1
.

A multiplicative Hom-Lie algebra
(L,[.,.]_{L},\alpha)
is said to
be restrictable if

\mathrm{ad}(\alpha^{p-1}(x))\text{\textopenbullet}\mathrm{ad}(\alpha^{p-2}(x))\text{\textopenbullet}\cdot\cdot\cdot\text{\textopenbullet}\mathrm{ad}(x)\in\mathrm{ad}_{\alpha}(L),

for all
x\in L
, where
\mathrm{ad_{\alpha}}L=\{\mathrm{ad}_{\alpha}(x)|x\in L
}.

Several examples are shown here.

Example 3.2
.

Any abelian Hom-Lie algebra is restrictable.

Example 3.3
.

Let
(L,[.,.]_{L},\alpha)
be a nilpotent Hom-Lie
algebra such that
L^{p+1}=0
. Then
\mathrm{ad}(\alpha^{p-1}(x))\text{\textopenbullet}\mathrm{ad}(\alpha^{p-2}(x))\text{\textopenbullet}\cdot\cdot\cdot\text{\textopenbullet}\mathrm{ad}(x)=0
,

\forall x\in L
. If
\mathrm{dim}L<p+2
, then
(L,[.,.]_{L},\alpha)

is restrictable.

Example 3.4
.

Consider the two-dimensional nonabelian Lie algebra

L:=\mathbb{F}h\oplus\mathbb{F}x
with the nonzero bracket
[h,x]=x
.
Let
\alpha:L\rightarrow L
be a mapping defined by
\alpha(h)=h,\alpha(x)=0

and
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\alpha}=\alpha\circ[\cdot,\cdot]
. Then the triple

(L,[.,.]_{\alpha},\alpha)
is a Hom-Lie algebra. Furthermore,
\mathrm{ad}(\alpha^{p-1}(h))\text{\textopenbullet}\mathrm{ad}(\alpha^{p-2}(h))\text{\textopenbullet}\cdot\cdot\cdot\text{\textopenbullet}\mathrm{ad}(h)=\mathrm{ad}_{\alpha}(h)\in\mathrm{ad}_{\alpha}(L)

and
\mathrm{ad}(\alpha^{p-1}(x))\text{\textopenbullet}\mathrm{ad}(\alpha^{p-2}(x))\text{\textopenbullet}\cdot\cdot\cdot\text{\textopenbullet}\mathrm{ad}(x)=\mathrm{ad}_{\alpha}(x)\in\mathrm{ad}_{\alpha}(L)
.
It implies that
(L,[.,.]_{\alpha},\alpha)
is restrictable.

In fact, there exists a equivalence relation between restrictable
Hom-Lie algebras and restricted Hom-Lie algebras. The details are
as follows:

Theorem 3.5
.

A multiplicative Hom-Lie algebra
(L,[.,.]_{L},\alpha)

is restrictable if and only if there exists a
p
-structure
[p]_{\alpha}:L\rightarrow L

which makes
L
a restricted Hom-Lie algebra.

Proof.

(\Leftarrow)
Suppose that
L
is restricted. For any
x\in L
,
we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{ad}(\alpha^{p-1}(x))\text{\textopenbullet}\mathrm{ad}(\alpha^{p-2}(x))\text{\textopenbullet}\cdot\cdot\cdot\text{\textopenbullet}\mathrm{ad}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{ad}(x^{[p]_{\alpha}})\text{\textopenbullet}\alpha^{p-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{ad}_{\alpha}(x^{[p]_{\alpha}})\in\mathrm{ad_{\alpha}}L,

which proves that
L
is restrictable.

(\Rightarrow)
Let
L
be restrictable. Then for
x\in L
, we
obtain

\mathrm{ad}(\alpha^{p-1}(x))\text{\textopenbullet}\mathrm{ad}(\alpha^{p-2}(x))\text{\textopenbullet}\cdot\cdot\cdot\text{\textopenbullet}\mathrm{ad}(x)\in\mathrm{ad}_{\alpha}(L),

that is, there exists
y\in L
such that

\mathrm{ad}(\alpha^{p-1}(x))\text{\textopenbullet}\mathrm{ad}(\alpha^{p-2}(x))\text{\textopenbullet}\cdot\cdot\cdot\text{\textopenbullet}\mathrm{ad}(x)=\mathrm{ad}_{\alpha}(y).

Let
(e_{j})_{j\in J}
be a basis of
L
. Then there exist
y_{j}\in L

such that

\mathrm{ad}(\alpha^{p-1}(e_{j}))\text{\textopenbullet}\mathrm{ad}(\alpha^{p-2}(e_{j}))\text{\textopenbullet}\cdot\cdot\cdot\text{\textopenbullet}\mathrm{ad}(e_{j})=\mathrm{ad}_{\alpha}(y_{j}),\forall j\in J.

By virtue of Theorem 2.3, there exists exactly one
p
-structure

[p]_{\alpha}:L\rightarrow L
such that
e_{j}^{[p]_{\alpha}}=y_{j},\forall j\in J
,
which makes
L
a restricted Hom-Lie algebra.
∎

Clearly, the notion of restrictable Hom-Lie algebras is more tractable
than that of restricted Hom-Lie algebras. Moreover, it is required
only to know whether a
p
-structure can be defined on a given Hom-Lie
algebra in many cases. Therefore, we only need to determine whether
a given Hom-Lie algebra is restrictable instead of defining a
p
-structure
on it.

One advantage in considering restrictable Hom-Lie algebras instead
of restricted ones reflects in the following theorem:

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
f:(L,[\cdot,\cdot]_{L},\alpha)\rightarrow(L_{1},[\cdot,\cdot]_{L_{1}},\alpha_{1})

be a surjective morphism of Hom-Lie algebras. If
L
is restrictable,
so is
L_{1}
.

Proof.

Since
f
is surjective, we have
L_{1}=f(L)
, that is, for any

z\in L_{1}
, there exists
x\in L
such that
f(x)=z
. For
x\in L
,
it follows from
L
being restrictable that there is
y\in L
such
that

\mathrm{ad}(\alpha^{p-1}(x))\text{\textopenbullet}\mathrm{ad}(\alpha^{p-2}(x))\text{\textopenbullet}\cdot\cdot\cdot\text{\textopenbullet}\mathrm{ad}(x)=\mathrm{ad}_{\alpha}(y)\text{.}

For
w\in L
, one gets

\displaystyle\mathrm{ad}(\alpha_{1}^{p-1}(f(x)))\text{\textopenbullet}\mathrm{ad}(\alpha_{1}^{p-2}(f(x)))\text{\textopenbullet}\cdot\cdot\cdot\text{\textopenbullet}\mathrm{ad}(f(x))(f(w))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\alpha_{1}^{p-1}(f(x)),[\alpha_{1}^{p-2}(f(x)),\cdot\cdot\cdot,[f(x),f(w)]_{L_{1}}]_{L_{1}}]_{L_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[f(\alpha^{p-1}(x)),[f(\alpha^{p-2}(x)),\cdot\cdot\cdot,[f(x),f(w)]_{L_{1}}]_{L_{1}}]_{L_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f([\alpha^{p-1}(x),[\alpha^{p-2}(x),\cdot\cdot\cdot,[x,w]_{L}]_{L}]_{L}\text{)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(\mathrm{ad}(\alpha^{p-1}(x))\text{\textopenbullet}\mathrm{ad}(\alpha^{p-2}(x))\text{\textopenbullet}\cdot\cdot\cdot\text{\textopenbullet}\mathrm{ad}(x)(w\text{))}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(\mathrm{ad}_{\alpha}(y)(w))=f([y,\alpha^{p-1}(w)]_{L})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[f(y),f(\alpha^{p-1}(w))]_{L_{1}}=[f(y),\alpha_{1}^{p-1}(f(w))]_{L_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{ad}_{\alpha_{1}}(f(y))(f(w)),

which implies that
\mathrm{ad}(\alpha_{1}^{p-1}(f(x)))\text{\textopenbullet}\mathrm{ad}(\alpha_{1}^{p-2}(f(x)))\text{\textopenbullet}\cdot\cdot\cdot\text{\textopenbullet}\mathrm{ad}(f(x))=\mathrm{ad}_{\alpha_{1}}(f(y))\in\mathrm{ad}_{\alpha_{1}}L_{1}
.
Hence
L_{1}
is restrictable.
∎

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
be Hom-Lie ideals of Hom-Lie
algebra
(L,[\cdot,\cdot]_{L},\alpha)
such that
L=L_{1}\oplus L_{2}
.
Then
L
is restrictable if and only if
L_{1},L_{2}
are restrictable.

Proof.

(
\Leftarrow
) Assuming
L_{1},L_{2}
are restrictable. For
x\in L
,
we may suppose that
x=x_{1}+x_{2}
, where
x_{1}\in L_{1},x_{2}\in L_{2}
.
Since
L_{1},L_{2}
are Hom-Lie ideals, one gets
\alpha^{i}(x_{1})\in L_{1},\alpha^{j}(x_{2})\in L_{2}
,
for
i,j\in\{1,2,...,p-1\}
. As
L_{1},L_{2}
are restrictable,
there exist
y_{1}\in L_{1},y_{2}\in L_{2}
such that
\mathrm{ad}(\alpha^{p-1}(x_{1}))\text{\textopenbullet}\mathrm{ad}(\alpha^{p-2}(x_{1}))\text{\textopenbullet}\cdot\cdot\cdot\text{\textopenbullet}\mathrm{ad}(x_{1})=\mathrm{ad}_{\alpha}(y_{1})

and
\mathrm{ad}(\alpha^{p-1}(x_{2}))\text{\textopenbullet}\mathrm{ad}(\alpha^{p-2}(x_{2}))\text{\textopenbullet}\cdot\cdot\cdot\text{\textopenbullet}\mathrm{ad}(x_{2})=\mathrm{ad}_{\alpha}(y_{2})

respectively. Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathrm{ad}(\alpha^{p-1}(x))\text{\textopenbullet}\mathrm{ad}(\alpha^{p-2}(x))\text{\textopenbullet}\cdot\cdot\cdot\text{\textopenbullet}\mathrm{ad}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{ad}(\alpha^{p-1}(x_{1}+x_{2}))\text{\textopenbullet}\mathrm{ad}(\alpha^{p-2}(x_{1}+x_{2}))\text{\textopenbullet}\cdot\cdot\cdot\text{\textopenbullet}\mathrm{ad}(x_{1}+x_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{ad}(\alpha^{p-1}(x_{1}))\text{\textopenbullet}\mathrm{ad}(\alpha^{p-2}(x_{1}))\text{\textopenbullet}\cdot\cdot\cdot\text{\textopenbullet}\mathrm{ad}(x_{1})

\displaystyle\mathrm{+ad}(\alpha^{p-1}(x_{2}))\text{\textopenbullet}\mathrm{ad}(\alpha^{p-2}(x_{2}))\text{\textopenbullet}\cdot\cdot\cdot\text{\textopenbullet}\mathrm{ad}(x_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{ad}_{\alpha}(y_{1})+\mathrm{ad}_{\alpha}(y_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{ad}_{\alpha}(y_{1}+y_{2})\in\mathrm{ad}_{\alpha}L.

Hence
L
is restrictable.

(
\Rightarrow
) Suppose that
L
is restrictable. Theorem 3.6 applies
and
L_{1}\cong L/L_{2},L_{2}\cong L/L_{1}
are restrictable.
∎

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
L_{1},L_{2}
be restrictable Hom-Lie ideals
of Hom-Lie algebra
(L,[\cdot,\cdot]_{L},\alpha)
such that
L=L_{1}+L_{2}

and
[L_{1},L_{2}]=\{0\}
. Then
L
is restrictable.

Proof.

By virtue of Theorem 3.7, since
L_{1},L_{2}
are restrictable, one
gets
L_{1}\oplus L_{2}
is restrictable. We define a mapping
f:L_{1}\oplus L_{2}\rightarrow L,(x,y)\mapsto x+y
.
Obviously,
f
is surjective. Furthermore, be analogous to Corollary
3.7 in
[
9
]
, it can be proved that
f
is a morphism of Hom-Lie
algebras. Theorem 3.6 applies and
L
is restrictable.
∎

Corollary 3.9
.

Suppose that
(L,[.,.]_{L},\alpha)
is a regular
Hom-Lie algebra with
L=[L,L]_{L}+C(L)
. Let
I\subset C(L)
be
a Hom-Lie ideal of
L
. Then
L
is restrictable if and only if

L/I
is restrictable.

Proof.

(
\Rightarrow
) Assume that
L
is restrictable. Then Theorem 3.6
applies and
L/I
is restrictable.

(
\Leftarrow
) If
L/I
is restrictable, then for any
x\in[L,L]_{L}
,
there exists
y\in[L,L]_{L}
such that

\mathrm{(ad}(\alpha^{p-1}(x))\circ\mathrm{ad}(\alpha^{p-2}(x))\circ\cdot\cdot\cdot\circ\mathrm{ad}(x)-\mathrm{ad}_{\alpha}y)(L)\subset I\subset C(L).

Therefore,

\mathrm{(ad}(\alpha^{p-1}(x))\circ\mathrm{ad}(\alpha^{p-2}(x))\circ\cdot\cdot\cdot\circ\mathrm{ad}(x)-\mathrm{ad}_{\alpha}y)([L,L]_{L})\subset[I,L]_{L}\subset[C(L),L]_{L}=\{0\text{\lx@text@rbrace}.

It implies that

\mathrm{ad}(\alpha^{p-1}(x))\circ\mathrm{ad}(\alpha^{p-2}(x))\circ\cdot\cdot\cdot\circ\mathrm{ad}(x)=\mathrm{ad}_{\alpha}y\in\mathrm{ad}_{\alpha}([L,L]_{L}).

Hence

is restrictable. Moreover,

is clearly restrictable
by definition. Together with
[[L,L]_{L},C(L)]_{L}=\{0\}
, we conclude
by Corollary 3.8 that

is restrictable.
∎

At the end of this section, the influence of associative bilinear

Definition 3.11
.

Let

.

(see
[

) Let

.

.

.

Theorem 3.13
.

Let
(g,[.,.]_{g},\alpha,[p]_{\alpha})
be a restricted
Hom-Lie algebra and
L
a Hom-Lie subalgebra of

with

.
Assume

to be an associative

. Then

is restrictable.

Proof.

Since
\lambda
is nondegenerate on

, every linear form

f
on

is determined by a suitably chosen element

.
Then there exists

such that

which implies that

.
Thereby,

and

Hence

\displaystyle\mathrm{ad}(\alpha^{p-1}(x))\text{\textopenbullet}\mathrm{ad}(\alpha^{p-2}(x))\text{\textopenbullet}\cdot\cdot\cdot\text{\textopenbullet}\mathrm{ad}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{ad}_{\alpha}(x^{[p]_{\alpha}})=\mathrm{ad}_{\alpha}(y)\in\mathrm{ad}_{\alpha}L,

which proves that

∎

4.
Restricted Envelopes

In this section, the universal enveloping algebras and
p
-envelopes
of a restricted Hom-Lie algebra will be taken into consideration.
Like the case of restricted Lie algebras, the additional structure
of restrictedness should be considered.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
(L,[.,.]_{L},\alpha,[p]_{\alpha})
denote
a restricted Hom-Lie algebra. Let

be a Hom-associative
algebra with unity and

morphism. The pair

enveloping algebra if for every Hom-associative algebra
(A,\mu_{A},\alpha_{A})

and every restricted morphism

of Hom-Lie
algebras, there exists a unique morphism
h:u_{HLie}(L)\rightarrow A

of Hom-associative algebras such that
h\circ i=f
.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
(L,[.,.]_{L},\alpha_{L})
be any Hom-Lie
algebra.

(1) The 5-tuple
(G,[.,.]_{G},\alpha_{G},[p],i)
consisting of a
restricted Hom-Lie algebra
(G,[.,.]_{G},

\alpha_{G},[p])
and
a morphism of Hom-Lie algebras
i:L\rightarrow G
is called a
p
-envelope
of
L
if
i
is injective and
i(L)_{p}=G
.

(2) A
p
-envelope
(G,[.,.]_{G},\alpha_{G},[p],i)
is said to be
universal if it satisfies the following universal property: For every
restricted Hom-Lie algebra
(H,[.,.]_{H},\alpha_{H},[p]^{\prime})
and every
morphism
f:L\rightarrow H
, there exists exactly one restricted
morphism of Hom-Lie algebras
g:(G,[.,.]_{G},\alpha_{G},[p])\rightarrow(H,[.,.]_{H},\alpha_{H},[p]^{\prime})

such that
g\circ i=f
.

The existence of universal
p
-envelopes is ensured by the following
theorem:

Theorem 4.3
.

Every Hom-Lie algebra
(L,[.,.]_{L},\alpha_{L})

has a universal
p
-envelope
\hat{L}
. Moreover, if
(L,[.,.]_{L},\alpha_{L})

is involutive, then
(\hat{L},[.,.]_{\hat{L}},\alpha_{\hat{L}})

is also an involutive Hom-Lie algebra.

Proof.

Let
\hat{L}
be the
p
-subalgebra of
U_{HLie}(L)^{-}
generated
by
L
and
f:L\rightarrow H
a morphism. Be analogous to the case
of Lie algebra(see
[
18
]
),
\hat{f}:\hat{L}\rightarrow H
can
be proved to be the unique extension of
f
. Therefore,
\hat{L}

is a universal
p
-envelope of
L
. Moreover, if
\alpha_{L}:L\rightarrow L

is an involution, then
\alpha_{\hat{L}}:\hat{L}\rightarrow\hat{L}

is obviously also an involution. It follows that
(\hat{L},[.,.]_{\hat{L}},\alpha_{\hat{L}})

is an involutive Hom-Lie algebra.
∎

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
(L,[.,.]_{L},\alpha)
be an involutive

(1) Let
(G,[.,.]_{G},\alpha_{G},[p],i)

-envelope
of

.

(2) If

possesses a finite-dimensional

(3) Any morphism of Hom-Lie algebra

\hat{f}:\hat{L}\rightarrow\hat{G}
. If

is surjective, so is
\hat{f}
.

Proof.

(1) As

, we have
[G,G]\subset L
and
L
be a Hom-Lie
ideal of

. Let
L_{1}=\{x\in L|\alpha(x)=x\}
. Consider the morphism

\phi:G\rightarrow\mathrm{ad}(L)
,

If

, then
\mathrm{ad}(\alpha(x))(L_{1})=0
.
Since
\mathrm{ker}(\mathrm{ad}\alpha(x))
is a

-subalgebra of

G
, we have

. It follows that
\mathrm{ker}(\phi)=C(G)
.
Thereby,

\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{F}}(G/C(G))=\mathrm{dim}{}_{\mathbb{F}}(G/\mathrm{ker}(\phi))<\mathrm{dim}{}_{\mathbb{F}}\mathrm{ad}(L)<\infty.

(2) Choose a subspace

such that

Be analogous to the case of Lie algebra in
[

that the
p
-subalgebra generated by

in
\hat{L}/V
is the
desired
p
-envelope of

.

(3) Considering
L\rightarrow G\hookrightarrow\hat{G}
, the universal
property of
\hat{L}
ensures the existence of
\hat{f}

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ccc}&\hookrightarrow\end{array}\hat{L}

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}f&\downarrow\end{array}

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ccccc}&&&\downarrow&\hat{f}\end{array}

If

Along with

∎

Next, these results will be applied to representation theory. To this
end, several definitions and lemmas will be introduced first.

Definition 4.5
.

Let
(L,[\cdot,\cdot]_{L},\alpha)
be a multiplicative
Hom-Lie algebra.

(1) (see
[
17
]
) A representation of
(L,[\cdot,\cdot]_{L},\alpha)

on the vector space
V
with respect to
\beta\in\mathrm{gl}(V)

is a linear map
\rho_{\beta}:L\rightarrow\mathrm{gl}(V)
, such that
for any
x,y\in L
, the following equalities are satisfied:

\rho_{\beta}(\alpha(x))\text{\textopenbullet}\beta=\beta\text{\textopenbullet}\rho_{\beta}(x),\rho_{\beta}([x,y]_{L})\text{\textopenbullet}\beta=\rho_{\beta}(\alpha(x))\rho_{\beta}(y)-\rho_{\beta}(\alpha(y))\rho_{\beta}(x).

(2) The representation
\rho_{\beta}
is called as faithful if
\textrm{ker}(\rho_{\beta})=0
.

(3) For a restricted Hom-Lie algebra
(L,[\cdot,\cdot]_{L},\alpha,[p]_{\alpha})
,
the representation
\rho_{\beta}
is called as a
p
-representation
if
\rho_{\beta}(x^{[p]_{\alpha}})=\rho_{\beta}(x)^{p}
, for all

x\in L
.

(4) For
x\in L
,
\rho_{\beta}(x)
is said to be nilpotent if there
is
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\rho_{\beta}(x)^{k}=0
.

Be analogue to Proposition 3.1 in
[
4
]
, we have

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
(L,[\cdot,\cdot]_{L})
be a Lie algebra and
\rho:L\rightarrow\mathrm{gl}(V)

a representation. Let
\alpha:L\rightarrow L
be a Lie algebra morphism
and
\beta\in\mathrm{gl}(V)
be a linear map such that
\rho(\alpha(x))\text{\textopenbullet}\beta=\beta\text{\textopenbullet}\rho(x)
,

\forall x\in L
. Then
(L,[\cdot,\cdot]_{\alpha},\alpha)
with

[\cdot,\cdot]_{\alpha}=\alpha\text{\textopenbullet}[\cdot,\cdot]_{L}

is a Hom-Lie algebra and
\rho_{\beta}:L\rightarrow\mathrm{gl}(V)
,
where
\rho_{\beta}=\beta\circ\rho
, is a representation.

The following lemma is Iwasawa’s theorem in the Lie algebra case.

Lemma 4.7
.

(see
[
18
]
) Let
L
be a finite-dimensional Lie
algebra,
G
a finite-dimensional
p
-envelope of

and

a restricted universal enveloping algebra. Then the mapping
\mathscr{\rho}_{\mathfrak{L}}:L\rightarrow\mathrm{gl}(u(G))

given by the left multiplication in

\rho_{\mathfrak{L}}(x)\begin{array}[]{cc}nilpotent\end{array}\Longleftrightarrow\begin{array}[]{cc}&\mathrm{ad}x\end{array}nilpotent

holds for all

.

Thus, we obtain the following theorem, which can be seen as the Hom-version
of Iwasawa’s theorem.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
(L,[\cdot,\cdot]_{\alpha},\alpha)

Assume that

\rho_{\beta}=\beta\circ\rho_{\mathfrak{L}}:L\rightarrow\mathrm{gl}(u(G))

is a representation of the Hom-Lie algebra

.
It’s not difficult to prove that
\rho_{\beta}
is the representation
satisfying the requirement.

Proposition 4.11

Let

(1) Let

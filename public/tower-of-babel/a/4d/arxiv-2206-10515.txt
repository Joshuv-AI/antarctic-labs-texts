Root- 𝑻 ​ 𝑻 ¯ {{T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu}} Deformations in Two-Dimensional Quantum Field Theories
Christian Ferko, Alessandro Sfondrini, Liam Smith, Gabriele Tartaglino-Mazzucchelli

Abstract
In this letter we introduce a one-parameter deformation of two-dimensional quantum field theories generated by a non-analytic operator which we call Root- T ​ T ¯ T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu . For a conformal field theory, the operator coincides with the square-root of the T ​ T ¯ T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu operator. More generally, the operator is defined so that classically it is marginal and generates a flow which commutes with the T ​ T ¯ T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu -flow. Intriguingly, the Root- T ​ T ¯ T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu flow is closely related to the ModMax theory recently constructed by Bandos, Lechner, Sorokin and Townsend.



I
Introduction and Summary

The so-called
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
operator
is a universal composite operator defined from the stress-energy tensor
T^{\mu\nu}
of any two-dimensional quantum field theory (QFT) as
Zamolodchikov 2004

\displaystyle{\cal{O}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\text{d}^{2}z\,O(z,\bar{z})\,,

(1a)

\displaystyle O(z,\bar{z})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\text{Det}\left[{T^{\mu\nu}}\right]=\frac{1}{2}\left(T^{\mu\nu}T_{\mu\nu}-T_{\mu}{}^{\mu}T_{\nu}{}^{\nu}\right)\,.~~~~~~

(1b)

In a conformal field theory (CFT) the trace of the stress-energy tensor vanishes and we have, in light-cone coordinates,
O(z,\bar{z})=T(z)\overline{T}(\bar{z})
. This is however not the case for a generic QFT, making “
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
” somewhat of a misnomer.
Given a two-dimensional QFT defined by a “seed” action
{\cal S}_{0}
, the operator
{\cal O}
can be used to define a one-parameter family of theories
{\cal S}_{\lambda}

Smirnov and Zamolodchikov 2017
;
Cavaglià et al. 2016

\partial_{\lambda}{\cal S}_{\lambda}={\cal{O}}_{\lambda}\,,\qquad{\cal S}_{\lambda=0}={\cal S}_{0}\,.

(2)

Because
O
has scaling dimension four, the resulting flow is
irrelevant
in the sense of the renormalization group (RG). This is counter-intuitive in the usual RG picture, where one typically considers
relevant
or
marginal
(
i.e.
conformal) perturbations. Nonetheless, the
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
flow has many remarkable properties. Applied to a CFT, it breaks scale-invariance but preserves infinitely-many commuting charges, and it results in a family of integrable QFTs (IQFTs). If
{\cal S}_{0}
is not conformal, but integrable, the flow still preserves integrability. In fact, the flow is so well-behaved that it is possible to express the finite-volume spectrum of the deformed theory
{\cal S}_{\lambda}
in closed form starting from the spectrum of the seed theory
{\cal S}_{0}
.
These properties allowed for a detailed study of
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
-deformed theories which has revealed and is revealing many surprising connections to string theory
Cavaglià et al. 2016
;
Baggio and Sfondrini 2018
;
Frolov 2020
, holography
McGough et al. 2018
;
Giveon et al. 2017
;
Dubovsky et al. 2017
, two-dimensional gravity
Dubovsky et al. 2018
, geometry
Conti et al. 2019
, random geometry
Cardy 2018
, and more
— see
Jiang 2021
;
Ferko 2021
for reviews and references on
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
.

In this letter we propose a new “square-root”,
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
-like deformation. By this we mean a
marginal
deformation defined by
\partial_{\gamma}{\cal S}_{\gamma}={\cal{R}}_{\gamma}
which, when applied to a two-dimensional conformal field theory (CFT
2
)
{\cal S}_{0}
, amounts to deforming by a non-analytic operator

\displaystyle{\cal{R}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\text{d}^{2}z\,R(z,\bar{z})\,,

(3a)

\displaystyle R(z,\bar{z})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{T(z)\overline{T}(\bar{z})}\,,\qquad\text{(for a CFT).}

(3b)

Despite the square-root, this promises to be a well-behaved expression in a CFT
2
because the operator-product expansion between a chiral and an anti-chiral field (non-analytic as they may be) is regular.
This deformation was recently considered in
Rodríguez et al. 2021
;
Bagchi et al. 2022
to study the BMS
3
symmetry of ultra/non-relativistic limits of a CFT
2
.
It is however not obvious how such an expression should be generalised when
{\cal S}_{0}
is
not
conformal. We do this by requiring that the Root-
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
flow commutes with the ordinary
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
flow,
i.e.
that we can construct a
two-parameter
action
{\cal S}_{(\lambda,\gamma)}
so that the diagram in Figure
1
commutes. As we shall explain in the conclusions, this choice will make it easier to study a vaster class of theories.

Figure 1:
We require that first deforming
{\cal S}_{0}
by
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu

We will show that, classically, this can be obtained by setting

where

We will construct the explicit two-parameter action

II
Root-
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
Flow for
N
Scalars

We start by considering a rather general Lagrangian density for
N
real scalars
\phi^{i}
, of the form

L=\frac{1}{2}\Big(G_{ij}(\phi)g^{\mu\nu}+B_{ij}(\phi)\varepsilon^{\mu\nu}\Big)\partial_{\mu}\phi^{i}\partial_{\nu}\phi^{j}-V(\phi)\,,

(6)

where
g^{\mu\nu}
is the two-dimensional inverse metric (which we take to be Minkowski),

\varepsilon^{\mu\nu}
is the Levi-Civita tensor, and

i=1,\cdots,N
.
This ansatz encompasses many QFTs of interest including vector models, non-linear sigma models
(where
G_{ij}
and
B_{ij}
represent the target-space metric and Kalb-Ramond fields, respectively),
and Wess-Zumino-Witten models, but it is not closed under Root-
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
deformations. In fact, terms with a higher number of derivatives will appear along the flow, as it can be seen recalling that the (Hilbert) stress-energy tensor is

T_{\mu\nu}=-2\frac{\partial L}{\partial g^{\mu\nu}}+g_{\mu\nu}L\,.

(7)

To write the general ansatz for a flow driven by the operator
R
(
4
), we list the tensor structures that may arise from the stress-energy tensor associated to (
6
) and from its powers. As
g^{\mu\nu}
only appears in the first term of (
6
), we consider

(X_{1})_{\mu}{}^{\nu}=G_{ij}\partial_{\mu}\phi^{i}\partial^{\nu}\phi^{j},\quad(X_{2})_{\mu}{}^{\nu}=(X_{1})_{\mu}{}^{\rho}(X_{1})_{\rho}{}^{\nu}.

(8)

In principle, we could define
(X_{3})_{\mu}{}^{\nu}
,
(X_{4})_{\mu}{}^{\nu}
,
etc.
, in an obvious way. However, in the Lagrangian only the trace of these tensors may appear, and for any
2\times 2
matrix the only independent trace-invariants are
x_{1}=(X_{1})_{\mu}{}^{\mu}
and
x_{2}=(X_{2})_{\mu}{}^{\mu}
, due to identities such as

2x_{3}=3x_{1}x_{2}-x_{1}^{3},\quad 2x_{4}=2x_{1}^{2}x_{2}-x_{1}^{4}+x_{2}^{2}\,,

(9)

with
x_{n}=(X_{n})_{\mu}{}^{\mu}
.
Hence the most general Lagrangian will depend on
x_{1}
,
x_{2}
, and possibly on a term
x_{0}
which does not couple to
g^{\mu\nu}
,
L_{\gamma}=L_{\gamma}(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2})
. For such a Lagrangian

T_{\mu\nu}=-2(X_{1})_{\mu\nu}\frac{\partial L_{\gamma}}{\partial x_{1}}-4(X_{2})_{\mu\nu}\frac{\partial L_{\gamma}}{\partial x_{2}}+g_{\mu\nu}L_{\gamma}\,.

(10)

Computing the contractions
T_{\mu\nu}T^{\mu\nu}
and
(T_{\mu}{}^{\mu})^{2}
, and simplifying the resulting expression with the help of the trace identities (
9
), we can evaluate the square of the operator
R
of Eq. (
4
) as

R^{2}=\left(2x_{2}-x_{1}^{2}\right)\left(\frac{\partial L_{\gamma}}{\partial x_{1}}+2x_{1}\frac{\partial L_{\gamma}}{\partial x_{2}}\right)^{2}.

(11)

This expression is surprisingly nice in more than one way. Firstly, the partial derivatives appear in a perfect square so that, away from
\partial_{x_{1}}L_{\gamma}=-2x_{1}\partial_{x_{2}}L_{\gamma}
, the flow equation for the Root-
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
operator (
4
) will be
linear
,

\frac{\partial}{\partial\gamma}L_{\gamma}=R_{\gamma}=\pm\sqrt{2x_{2}-x_{1}^{2}}\left(\frac{\partial L_{\gamma}}{\partial x_{1}}+2x_{1}\frac{\partial L_{\gamma}}{\partial x_{2}}\right)\,.

(12)

Both choices of the branch can be recovered by flipping the sign of
\gamma
. As
2x_{2}=x_{1}^{2}
gives a fixed point of (
12
), the branch cannot change along the flow.
Additionally, the flow equation will not depend explicitly on
L
, so that the dependence on
x_{0}
will affect the flow
only through its initial conditions
. Finally, (
12
) can be integrated,

L_{\gamma}\left(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2}\right)=L_{\gamma=0}\left(x_{0},x_{1}^{(\gamma)},x_{2}^{(\gamma)}\right)\,,

(13)

where

\displaystyle x_{1}^{(\gamma)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{ch}(\gamma)\,x_{1}\pm\text{sh}(\gamma)\sqrt{2x_{2}-x_{1}^{2}}\,,

(14a)

\displaystyle x_{2}^{(\gamma)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{ch}(2\gamma)\,x_{2}\pm\text{sh}(2\gamma)\,x_{1}\sqrt{2x_{2}-x_{1}^{2}}\,,

(14b)

and
x_{0}
is not deformed. Note that the combination

\big(x_{1}^{(\gamma)}\big)^{2}-x_{2}^{(\gamma)}=x_{1}^{2}-x_{2}\,,

(15)

is constant along the flow. In fact, an action which depends on only this combination and through
x_{0}
is a fixed-point of this flow. We also note that (
14
) has the property

\displaystyle x_{1}\frac{\partial L_{\gamma}}{\partial x_{1}}+2x_{2}\frac{\partial L_{\gamma}}{\partial x_{2}}=x_{1}^{(\gamma)}\frac{\partial L_{\gamma}}{\partial x_{1}^{(\gamma)}}+2x_{2}^{(\gamma)}\frac{\partial L_{\gamma}}{\partial x_{2}^{(\gamma)}}\,,

(16)

and that the trace of the stress-energy tensor for
L_{\gamma}
(
10
) vanishes if and only if

\frac{1}{2}T_{\mu}{}^{\mu}=L_{\gamma}-\left(x_{1}\frac{\partial L_{\gamma}}{\partial x_{1}}+2x_{2}\frac{\partial L_{\gamma}}{\partial x_{2}}\right)=0\,.

(17)

Eq. (
16
) therefore implies that, if the stress-energy tensor
for a seed theory
L_{0}
is traceless, then the same is true for the deformed theory
L_{\gamma}
. Importantly, this confirms that the operator
R_{\gamma}
defines a classically marginal deformation.

Note that, in the special case of
N
free Bosons, it holds

L_{\gamma}=\frac{\text{ch}\gamma}{2}\Phi_{\mu}{}^{\mu}\pm\frac{\text{sh}\gamma}{2}\,\sqrt{2\Phi_{\mu}{}^{\nu}\Phi_{\nu}{}^{\mu}-(\Phi_{\mu}{}^{\mu})^{2}}\,,

(18)

with
\Phi_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}\phi^{i}\partial_{\nu}\phi^{i}
.
This is the result found in Ref.
Conti et al. 2022
from dimensional reduction of ModMax, equation (
5
).
Notice also that this flow is non-trivial only for
N\geq 2
, for which
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
are algebraically independent, otherwise, it is merely a rescaling of the Lagrangian,
L_{\gamma}=e^{\gamma}L_{0}
.

III
Two-Parameter Flow

Having determined the Root-
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
flow, we will now verify that it commutes with the ordinary
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
flow. The latter is sourced by the operator
O
of Eq. (
1
). We can express
O
in terms of the Lagrangian and its derivatives following what we did for
R
around Eq. (
11
), and we get the (rather cumbersome) formula

O=-L^{2}-2\left(\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial x_{1}}\right)^{2}+2\frac{\partial L}{\partial x_{1}}\frac{\partial L}{\partial x_{2}}x_{1}+2\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial x_{2}}\right)^{2}(x_{1}^{2}-x_{2})\right)(x_{1}^{2}-x_{2})+2L\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial x_{1}}x_{1}+2\frac{\partial L}{\partial x_{2}}x_{2}\right).

(19)

Now, consider
a
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
deformation of
L_{\gamma}
(
13
), where the dependence on
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
appears exclusively through
x_{1}^{(\gamma)}
and
x_{2}^{(\gamma)}
. A change of variables and some algebra, along with the observations (
15
–
16
), allows us to reformulate the operator
O
in terms of
L
and its partial derivatives
with respect to

x_{1}^{(\gamma)}
and
x_{2}^{(\gamma)}
. In fact, we find that
O
is given precisely by the same expression (
19
) up to promoting
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
to
x_{1}^{(\gamma)}
and
x_{2}^{(\gamma)}
, respectively.

Therefore, to take a theory defined by
L_{\gamma}
and
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
-deform (obtaining
L_{(\lambda,\gamma)}
), we may as well first
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
-deform
L_{0}
(thereby obtaining
L_{\lambda}
), and then replace everywhere
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
with
x_{1}^{(\gamma)}
and
x_{2}^{(\gamma)}
. This last step is precisely tantamount to a Root-
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
deformation, which goes to show that the diagram of Fig.
1
indeed commutes.

The two-parameter Lagrangian can then be found by taking any
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
-deformed Lagrangian, such as the ones in
Bonelli et al. 2018
, and replacing
x_{1}
,
x_{2}
with the expressions in Eq. (
14
).
For the special case of
N
free scalars we find

L_{(\lambda,\gamma)}=\frac{\sqrt{1+2\lambda x_{1}^{(\gamma)}+2\lambda^{2}\big[\big(x_{1}^{(\gamma)}\big)^{2}-x_{2}^{(\gamma)}\big]}-1}{2\lambda}\,,

(20)

which is precisely the result obtained in Ref.
Conti et al. 2022
from the dimensional reduction of the four-dimensional ModMax-Born-Infeld theory
Bandos et al. 2020
;
Bandos et al. 2021a
;
Babaei-Aghbolagh et al. 2022a
;
Ferko et al. 2022
.

IV
Fermions

It is natural to wonder whether our discussion may be extended to models with Fermions.
It is unclear how to treat a non-analytic function of Grassmann variables only, such as the one that would arise in a Root-
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
deformed model involving only Fermions. It is more natural to first look at a theory of Bosons and Fermions, which admits a regular expansion in Grassman variables (with non-analytic coefficients in the Boson fields).
Let us consider a free model of
N_{B}
Bosons and
N_{F}
Dirac Fermions,

L=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}\phi^{i}\partial^{\mu}\phi^{i}+\frac{{\rm i}}{2}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\psi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{I}\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\psi^{I}-\frac{{\rm i}}{2}\left(\partial_{\mu}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\psi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{I}\right)\gamma^{\mu}\psi^{I}\,,

(21)

where

i=1,\dots N_{B}
, and

I=1,\dots N_{F}
.

To build an ansatz for the
\gamma
-flowed action we need to consider the stress-energy tensor of this model. Here we will consider the Hilbert, rather than Noether, stress-energy tensor. Much like for
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
flows
Baggio et al. 2019
, we expect that the deformed Lagrangian differs in the two choices by terms which vanish on-shell.
It is possible to show that, for theories that depend on the Fermions only via the symmetric tensor
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\psi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\gamma_{(\mu}\partial_{\nu)}\psi-(\partial_{(\mu}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\psi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)\gamma_{\nu)}\psi
, the Hilbert tensor is the symmetrized Noether tensor. This ensures that the deformed Lagrangian will be a function of

(X_{1})_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}\phi^{i}\partial_{\nu}\phi^{i}+{\rm i}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\psi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{I}\gamma_{(\mu}\partial_{\nu)}\psi^{I}-{\rm i}\big(\partial_{(\mu}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\psi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu^{I}\big)\gamma_{\nu)}\psi^{I},

(22)

and of
(X_{2})_{\mu\nu}=(X_{1})_{\mu\rho}(X_{1})^{\rho}{}_{\nu}
— more precisely of their traces
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
.
Given such a dependence, the flow equation can be determined by truncating all expressions to the Bosons, which immediately reproduces Eq. (
12
).

Hence, given an action which depends on
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
as in Eq. (
22
), the Root-
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
flow is found by replacing the
\gamma
-dependent expressions (
14
).
The same argument of the previous section confirms this flow too commutes with the usual
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
flow.
Note that in (
21
) we started from a system of free Dirac Fermions. However, the same argument concerning the integration of the flows holds if chirality conditions are imposed.
Interestingly, in the presence of at least one real chiral Fermion the deformed Lagrangian is non-trivial even with
N_{B}=1
.

V
Conclusions and Outlook

We have discussed Root-
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
deformations, which commute with the
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
flow and are defined in terms of a classically marginal non-analytic operator. One might have expected the deformed theories to be pathological and unnatural. Instead, they are appealing, satisfying a simple flow equation, and encompass the models arising from dimensional reduction of ModMax
Bandos et al. 2020
;
Bandos et al. 2021a
.
ModMax is also a non-analytic classically conformal theory, with many intriguing properties: it admits various physical solutions such as plane waves
Bandos et al. 2020
and dyons
Lechner et al. 2022
, its Hamiltonian is bounded from below
Escobar et al. 2022
, it can be supersymmetrized
Bandos et al. 2021b
;
Kuzenko 2021
;
Kuzenko and Raptakis 2021
, and it can be related to Maxwell theory coupled to an axion-dilaton
Lechner et al. 2022
— see also the recent review
Sorokin 2021
.
While many questions about ModMax remain open, it is certainly a physically interesting theory and it is inspiring that the two-dimensional Root-
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
deformation falls in the same class of models.

The main open question is how to quantize such non-analytic
theories. This will be subtle, but may be possible for Root-
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
flows of CFTs, by demanding the deformed theory to be conformal and bootstrapping its properties. If successful, one could construct new marginal deformations of generic CFTs. In general, the Root-
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
operator will not be part of spectrum of local operators of the theory (with the intriguing exception of the single free Boson). Hence the deformation should result in one-parameter families of theories that are quite different from the familiar local unitary two-dimensional CFTs — and it would be very interesting to understand such differences. A first step is understanding how
N\geq 2
free Bosons get coupled by the deformation and extending the analysis to WZW models and minimal models where algebraic techniques are very well developed.
The quantization of Root-
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
-deformed QFTs (as opposed to CFTs) is likely to be harder. By imposing that
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
and Root-
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
deformations commute, one can first perform the Root-
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
deformation (of a CFT) and only later break conformal invariance by
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
. This will clarify the general quantum properties of Root-
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
.

Again with quantization in mind, it would also be useful to study Root-
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
in Hamiltonian terms
Rodríguez et al. 2021
;
Bagchi et al. 2022
, as was recently done
Escobar et al. 2022
for ModMax, and to investigate whether the Root-
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
deformation acts in a simple manner on the S-matrix of (integrable and non-integrable) theories, like
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
does
Mussardo and Simon 2000
;
Dubovsky et al. 2018
. (See
Borsato et al. 2022
for recent work on the integrability of Root-
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
.)
In turn, understanding the quantum properties of Root-
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
would also extend our understanding of ModMax, where quantization remains a major stumbling block. This is expected for a
D=4
model for which no perturbative quantization scheme is available. Approaching this issue from two dimensions, where CFTs are under especially good control, may shed new light on the issue.

Recalling that
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
is only one of many current-current deformations
Smirnov and Zamolodchikov 2017
, all obeying a flow equation
Hernández-Chifflet et al. 2020
, it would be interesting to see if the higher-spin analogues of
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
also commute with Root-
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
. Moreover, one could also consider higher roots of higher-spin
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
-like operators. They would also be naïvely marginal (non-analytic) operators. Their study would probably be difficult, as is the case for higher-spin
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
deformations
Hernández-Chifflet et al. 2020
, but might be instructive already in the case of simple theories, see
Camilo et al. 2021
for
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
.
By allowing for the breaking of Lorentz symmetry, more general scale-invariant roots of current-current deformations could also be considered — for example a Root-
J\overline{T}

deformation based on the one of
Guica 2018
.

In this letter we have merely initiated the study of models involving Fermions, considering a relatively small class of models. It would be especially interesting to perform a systematic study of the Root-
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
deformations of superconformal theories. It is well-established that a supersymmetric model remains supersymmetric under
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
flows
Baggio et al. 2019
;
Chang et al. 2019
;
Jiang et al. 2019
;
Chang et al. 2020
;
Coleman et al. 2019
. The fact that ModMax may be supersymmetrized is also encouraging. However, it is not clear that the deformation of a free superconformal action of the type (
21
) (for example the
{\cal N}=(1,1)
supersymmetric system when
N_{B}=N_{F}
) gives a superconformal model — this certainly requires the supersymmetry transformations to be modified. In the case of the standard
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
deformation it was shown that the operator
{\cal O}
of Eq. (
1
) is (on-shell) equivalent to a manifestly supersymmetric supercurrent-squared operator defined in superspace
Baggio et al. 2019
;
Chang et al. 2019
. It is straightforward to propose manifestly supersymmetric extensions of the Root-
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
operator, Eq. (
3
), for superconformal field theories. In the simplest case of
{\cal N}=(0,1)
supersymmetry the following superspace Lagrangian is an example

\int d\theta^{+}\frac{{\cal S}_{+++}{\cal T}_{----}}{\sqrt{{\cal T}_{++++}{\cal T}_{----}}}=\sqrt{T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu}+\text{Fermions\,}.

(23)

Here
{\cal S}_{+++}
and
{\cal T}_{----}
define the
{\cal N}=(0,1)
supercurrents, and

{\cal T}_{++++}=D_{+}{\cal S}_{+++}
, see
Refs.
Baggio et al. 2019
;
Chang et al. 2019
.
For a single

{\cal N}=(0,1)
real scalar multiplet, the superspace Lagrangian
{\cal A}_{-}(\gamma)=e^{\gamma}(D_{+}\Phi)(\partial_{--}\Phi)

satisfies a classical flow driven by the operator (
23
).
This is a direct analogue of
the bosonic Root-
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
deformation of a single real free scalar field discussed above.
Similar superspace Root-
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
operators can be defined for models with more supersymmetry.
Aside from supersymmetry, it would also be interesting to consider the bosonization and fermionization of simple two-dimensional CFTs and their Root-
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
flows.

Finally, a major application of
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
deformations has been to holography, mapping them to gravitational theories in three-dimensional anti-De Sitter space-time
McGough et al. 2018
;
Giveon et al. 2017
, see also
Apolo et al. 2020
;
Chakraborty et al. 2020
;
Coleman et al. 2021
.
The holographic interpretation of such a marginal but non-analytic deformation of the boundary CFT
2
should be understood more clearly. An immediate application of this letter is to recast the Root-
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
deformation in terms of the boundary conditions of the AdS
3
metric much like in
Bzowski and Guica 2019
, which will provide a holographic formulation of the deformation and yield a new class of holographic backgrounds
Ebert et al. 2022
.

Holography provided a way to define new irrelevant deformations in
D>2
dimensions
Taylor 2018
;
Hartman et al. 2019
, in particular in
D=4
for Maxwell
Conti et al. 2018
, its supersymmetrization
Ferko et al. 2020
and ModMax theories
Babaei-Aghbolagh et al. 2022a
;
Ferko et al. 2022
.
Both our
D=2
operator
R
(
4
), and the
D=4
operator generating the
\gamma
-flow of ModMax-Born-Infeld
Babaei-Aghbolagh et al. 2022a
can be written as

\sqrt{-t^{\mu\nu}t_{\mu\nu}}\,,\qquad t_{\mu\nu}=T_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{D}g_{\mu\nu}T_{\rho}{}^{\rho}\,,

(24)

where
t_{\mu\nu}
is the traceless part of
T_{\mu\nu}
. It is natural to wonder whether this operator plays a universal role in generating ModMax-like deformations in any dimension, and to investigate generic Root-
T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
-type deformations in
D>2
models, including holographic ones. One piece of evidence that non-analytic deformations may be relevant for holography in
D>2
is that operators involving square roots of field gradients have been studied in the context of the fluid-gravity correspondence
Compere et al. 2011
in general dimension
D
.

A different, but very interesting, direction is the “generalized holographic principle” of
Araujo-Regado et al. 2022
, which also involves stress-tensor deformations in
D>2
; one might wonder whether
\sqrt{TT}
-type operators may be needed to UV-complete the deformations used in that proposal. Furthermore, the interplay between ultra/non-relativistic limits of a CFT
2
and the BMS
3
symmetry found in
Rodríguez et al. 2021
;
Bagchi et al. 2022
also suggests possible relations between operators like (
24
) and deformations of space-time symmetries in dimensions higher than two. Finally, see also
García and Sánchez-Isidro 2022
for an analogue of the Root-
{T\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muT\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu}
deformation in
(0+1)
spacetime dimensions.

We believe that addressing all these questions may result in exciting new insights on the structure of conformal and non-conformal quantum field theories and of their holographic duals, and we plan to return to some of these in the near future.

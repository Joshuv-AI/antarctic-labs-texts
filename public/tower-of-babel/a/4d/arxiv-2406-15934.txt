Nondegenerate Turán problems under ( t , p ) (t,p) -norms
Wanfang Chen, Daniel Iľkovič, Jared León, Xizhi Liu, Oleg Pikhurko

Abstract
Given integers r > t ≥ 1 r>t\geq 1 and a real number p > 0 p>0 , the ( t , p ) (t,p) -norm ‖ ℋ ‖ t , p \left\lVert\mathcal{H}\right\rVert_{t,p} of an r r -graph ℋ \mathcal{H} is the sum of the p p -th power of the degrees d ℋ ​ ( T ) d_{\mathcal{H}}(T) over all t t -subsets T ⊂ V ⁡ ( ℋ ) T\subset V(\mathcal{H}) . We conduct a systematic study of the Turán-type problem of determining ex t , p ​ ( n , ℱ ) \mathrm{ex}_{t,p}(n,\mathcal{F}) , which is the maximum of ‖ ℋ ‖ t , p \left\lVert\mathcal{H}\right\rVert_{t,p} over all n n -vertex ℱ \mathcal{F} -free r r -graphs ℋ \mathcal{H} . We establish several basic properties for the ( t , p ) (t,p) -norm of r r -graphs, enabling us to derive general theorems from the recently established framework in [ CL24 ] that are useful for determining ex t , p ​ ( n , ℱ ) \mathrm{ex}_{t,p}(n,\mathcal{F}) and proving the corresponding stability. We determine the asymptotic value of ex t , p ​ ( n , H F r ) \mathrm{ex}_{t,p}(n,H_{F}^{r}) for all feasible combinations of ( r , t , p ) (r,t,p) and for every graph F F with chromatic number greater than r r , where H F r H_{F}^{r} represents the expansion of F F . In the case where F F is edge-critical and p ≥ 1 p\geq 1 , we establish strong stability and determine the exact value of ex t , p ​ ( n , H F r ) \mathrm{ex}_{t,p}(n,H_{F}^{r}) for all sufficiently large n n . These results extend the seminal theorems of Erdős–Stone–Simonovits, Andrásfai–Erdős–Sós, Erdős–Simonovits, and a classical theorem of Mubayi. For the 3 3 -uniform generalized triangle F 5 F_{5} , we determine the exact value of ex 2 , p ​ ( n , F 5 ) \mathrm{ex}_{2,p}(n,F_{5}) for all p ≥ 1 p\geq 1 and its asymptotic value for all p ∈ [ 1 / 2 , 1 ] ∪ { k − 1 : k ∈ 6 ​ ℕ + + { 0 , 2 } } p\in[1/2,1]\cup\{k^{-1}\colon k\in 6\mathbb{N}^{+}+\{0,2\}\} . This extends old theorems of Bollobás, Frankl–Füredi, and a recent result of Balogh–Clemen–Lidický. Our proofs utilize results on the graph inducibility problem, Steiner triple systems, and the feasible region problem introduced by Liu–Mubayi. Our results reveal two interesting phenomena: • The extremal structure for ex t , p ​ ( n , H F r ) \mathrm{ex}_{t,p}(n,H_{F}^{r}) remains consistent for all p > 0 p>0 , whereas the number of extremal structures for ex t , p ​ ( n , F 5 ) \mathrm{ex}_{t,p}(n,F_{5}) transitions from a single structure to infinitely many as p p approaches 0 0 . • Both problems exhibit degree-stability when p ≥ 1 p\geq 1 , but not when p < 1 p<1 . Keywords: hypergraphs, nondegenerate Turán problems, degree powers, stability, vertex-extendability.

Given an integer

We identify a hypergraph

The

The

For integers

Given a family

Studying the extremal properties of

In this work, we consider the following Turán-type problem, which is a common generalization of the classical Turán problem and the generalized Turán problem for counting stars (see Section

Given integers

Given a graph

Expansions are an important class of hypergraphs introduced by Mubayi

and

(i)

Every

-vertex

-free

-graph with

for some
n
-vertex complete
\ell
-partite
r
-graph
\mathcal{G}
.

We use

Recall that an

Proof of Proposition
2.1

Let
\mathcal{H}
be an
r
-graph and
u,v\in V(\mathcal{H})
be a pair of uncovered vertices.
For convenience, let

\mathcal{T}_{0}\coloneqq\binom{V\setminus\{u,v\}}{t}

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{u}\coloneqq\left\{T\in\binom{V\setminus\{v\}}{t}\colon u\in T\right\},\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{T}_{v}\coloneqq\left\{T\in\binom{V\setminus\{u\}}{t}\colon v\in T\right\}.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{0}\coloneqq\mathcal{H}
,
\mathcal{H}_{1}\coloneqq\mathcal{H}_{u\to v}
, and
\mathcal{H}_{2}\coloneqq\mathcal{H}_{v\to u}

For each

, and hence,

\displaystyle\Gamma(\mathcal{H}_{i})=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{u}}d^{p}_{\mathcal{H}_{i}}(T)+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{v}}d^{p}_{\mathcal{H}_{i}}(T)+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{0}}d^{p}_{\mathcal{H}_{i}}(T).

It is easy to see from the definition of symmetrization that

\displaystyle 2\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{u}}d^{p}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}(T)

\displaystyle=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{u}}d^{p}_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}(T)+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{v}}d^{p}_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}(T),\quad\text{and}\quad

\displaystyle 2\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{v}}d^{p}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}(T)

\displaystyle=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{u}}d^{p}_{\mathcal{H}_{1}}(T)+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{v}}d^{p}_{\mathcal{H}_{1}}(T).

So it suffices to show that

\displaystyle 2\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{0}}d^{p}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}(T)\leq\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{0}}d^{p}_{\mathcal{H}_{1}}(T)+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{0}}d^{p}_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}(T).

(2)

Fix
T\in\mathcal{T}_{0}
.
Let

,

, and

\displaystyle d^{p}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}(T)=\left(z_{u}+z_{v}+z_{0}\right)^{p},\quad d^{p}_{\mathcal{H}_{1}}(T)=\left(2z_{v}+z_{0}\right)^{p},\quad\text{and}\quad d^{p}_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}(T)=\left(2z_{u}+z_{0}\right)^{p}.

Since

\displaystyle\frac{\left(2z_{v}+z_{0}\right)^{p}+\left(2z_{u}+z_{0}\right)^{p}}{2}\geq\left(\frac{2z_{v}+z_{0}+2z_{u}+z_{0}}{2}\right)^{p}=\left(z_{u}+z_{v}+z_{0}\right)^{p}.

Therefore,
2d^{p}_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}(T)\leq d^{p}_{\mathcal{H}_{1}}(T)+d^{p}_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}(T)

Summing over all

Proposition 2.5

Fact 2.6

(Hölder’s inequality)
.

For every
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}),(b_{1},\ldots,b_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}
and positive real numbers

with

,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}b_{i}\leq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{s}\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}^{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{t}}.

Proof of Proposition
2.5
.

Fix
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\Delta^{n-1}
.
Let
s\coloneqq\frac{p_{2}-p_{1}}{p_{2}-p}
,
t\coloneqq\frac{p_{2}-p_{1}}{p-p_{1}}
,
a_{T}\coloneqq x_{T}^{\frac{1}{s}}\left(\sum_{I\in L_{\mathcal{H}}(T)}x_{I}\right)^{\frac{p_{1}}{s}}

Observe that

So, it follows from Hölder’s inequality that

\displaystyle L_{\mathcal{H},t,p}(\vec{x})

\displaystyle=\sum_{T\in\partial_{r-t}\mathcal{H}}a_{T}b_{T}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{T\in\partial_{r-t}\mathcal{H}}a_{T}^{s}\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}\left(\sum_{T\in\partial_{r-t}\mathcal{H}}b_{T}^{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{t}}=\left(L_{\mathcal{H},t,p_{1}}(\vec{x})\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}\left(L_{\mathcal{H},t,p_{2}}(\vec{x})\right)^{\frac{1}{t}},

For every
p\geq 1
,
1-px\leq(1-x)^{p}\leq 1-px+p^{2}x^{2}
.

In particular,

\displaystyle|(1-x)^{p}-(1-px)|\leq(p^{2}+1)x^{2}\quad\text{for every }p\geq 0,

(5)

and

\displaystyle x_{1}^{p}-(x_{1}-x_{2})^{p}\leq px_{1}^{p-1}x_{2}\quad\text{for all}\quad x_{1}\geq x_{2}\geq 0,\ p\geq 1.

(6)

Fact 2.9
.

Suppose that
x\in[0,1]
and
p\in(0,1)
are real numbers.
Then

(i)

(1-x)^{p}\geq 1-x^{p}
.

(ii)

x(1-x)^{p}\leq\frac{p^{p}}{(1+p)^{1+p}}
, and equality holds iff
x=\frac{1}{1+p}
.

3
General properties of
(t,p)
-norm Turán problems

In this section, we establish general properties of the
(t,p)
-norm of
r
-graphs that are necessary for applying the general theorems from
[
CL24
]
in the context of
(t,p)
-norm Turán problems.
Specifically, in addition to the symmetrization-increasing property and smoothness already established in Proposition
2.1
and Proposition
2.2
, we will show that the map
\Gamma\colon\mathfrak{G}^{t}\to\mathbb{R}
, defined by
\Gamma(\mathcal{H})\coloneqq\left\lVert\mathcal{H}\right\rVert_{t,p}
for all
\mathcal{H}\in\mathfrak{G}^{r}
, is
uniform
(Lemma
3.5
),
locally Lipschitz
(Lemma
3.8
),
continuous
(Lemma
3.7
), and
locally monotone
(Lemma
3.3
).
Instead of including the lengthy definitions of the properties mentioned above, we will introduce each property as we prove them later.
For detailed definitions, we refer the reader to
[
CL24
]
.
The focus of this section will be the case
p\geq 1
, although some results also apply for
p\in(0,1)
.

It should be noted that we will eventually apply the theorems from
[
CL24
]
to the map
\Gamma^{\prime}\colon\mathfrak{G}^{t}\to\mathbb{R}
(instead of
\Gamma
), defined by
\Gamma^{\prime}(\mathcal{H})\coloneqq\left\lVert\mathcal{H}\right\rVert_{t,p}\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathcal{H})
for all
\mathcal{H}\in\mathfrak{G}^{r}
.
However, deriving all the aforementioned properties (symmetrization-increasing, smooth, uniform, locally Lipschitz, continuous, and locally monotone) for
\Gamma^{\prime}
from those of
\Gamma
is a straightforward task when
\mathcal{F}
is nondegenerate and blowup-invariant (see Proposition
2.1
).

The first result of this section provides a handy expression for the
(t,p)
-degree of a vertex.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
r>t\geq 1
be integers and
p>0
be a real number.
For every
r
-graph
\mathcal{H}
and for every
v\in V(\mathcal{H})
,

\displaystyle d_{\mathcal{H},t,p}(v)

\displaystyle=\sum_{S\in\partial_{r-t}L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)}d_{\mathcal{H}}^{p}(S\cup\{v\})+\sum_{T\in\partial_{r-t-1}L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)}\left(d_{\mathcal{H}}^{p}(T)-\left(d_{\mathcal{H}}(T)-d_{\mathcal{H}}(T\cup\{v\})\right)^{p}\right).

In particular, if
p>1
, then

\displaystyle d_{\mathcal{H}}(v)\geq\frac{d_{\mathcal{H},t,p}(v)}{p\binom{r}{t}n^{(p-1)(r-t)}}.

(7)

Proof of Lemma
3.1
.

It follows from the definition of
(t,p)
-degree that

\displaystyle d_{\mathcal{H},t,p}(v)

\displaystyle=\sum_{T\in\partial_{r-t}\mathcal{H}}d_{\mathcal{H}}^{p}(T)-\sum_{T\in\partial_{r-t}(\mathcal{H}-v)}d_{\mathcal{H}-v}^{p}(T)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\in\partial_{r-t}\mathcal{H}\colon\\
v\in T\end{subarray}}d_{\mathcal{H}}^{p}(T)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\in\partial_{r-t}\mathcal{H}\colon\\
v\not\in T\end{subarray}}\left(d_{\mathcal{H}}^{p}(T)-d_{\mathcal{H}-v}^{p}(T)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\in\partial_{r-t}\mathcal{H}\colon\\
v\in T\end{subarray}}d_{\mathcal{H}}^{p}(T)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\in\partial_{r-t}\mathcal{H}\colon\\
v\not\in T\end{subarray}}\left(d_{\mathcal{H}}^{p}(T)-\left(d_{\mathcal{H}}(T)-d_{\mathcal{H}}(T\cup\{v\})\right)^{p}\right).

Notice that
d_{\mathcal{H}}^{p}(T)-\left(d_{\mathcal{H}}(T)-d_{\mathcal{H}}(T\cup\{v\})\right)^{p}\neq 0
iff
d_{\mathcal{H}}(T\cup\{v\})\geq 1
. Therefore, equations

\displaystyle\partial_{r-t}L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)

\displaystyle=\{T\setminus\{v\}\colon T\in\partial_{r-t}\mathcal{H}\text{ and }v\in T\}\quad\text{and}\quad

\displaystyle\partial_{r-t-1}L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)

\displaystyle=\left\{T\in\partial_{r-t}\mathcal{H}\colon v\not\in T\text{ and }d_{\mathcal{H}}(T\cup\{v\})\geq 1\right\}

imply the desired form of
d_{\mathcal{H},t,p}(v)
.

By (
6
), we obtain

\displaystyle d_{\mathcal{H},t,p}(v)

\displaystyle=\sum_{S\in\partial_{r-t}L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)}d_{\mathcal{H}}^{p}(S\cup\{v\})+\sum_{T\in\partial_{r-t-1}L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)}\left(d_{\mathcal{H}}^{p}(T)-\left(d_{\mathcal{H}}(T)-d_{\mathcal{H}}(T\cup\{v\})\right)^{p}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{S\in\partial_{r-t}L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)}d_{\mathcal{H}}^{p-1+1}(S\cup\{v\})+\sum_{T\in\partial_{r-t-1}L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)}p\cdot d_{\mathcal{H}}^{p-1}(T)\cdot d_{\mathcal{H}}(T\cup\{v\})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{S\in\partial_{r-t}L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)}d_{\mathcal{H}}(S\cup\{v\})\cdot n^{(p-1)(r-t)}+\sum_{T\in\partial_{r-t-1}L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)}pn^{(p-1)(r-t)}\cdot d_{\mathcal{H}}(T\cup\{v\})

\displaystyle\leq pn^{(p-1)(r-t)}\left(\sum_{S\in\partial_{r-t}L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)}d_{\mathcal{H}}(S\cup\{v\})+\sum_{T\in\partial_{r-t-1}L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)}d_{\mathcal{H}}(T\cup\{v\})\right)

\displaystyle=pn^{(p-1)(r-t)}\left(\binom{r-1}{t-1}d_{\mathcal{H}}(v)+\binom{r-1}{t}d_{\mathcal{H}}(v)\right)=pn^{(p-1)(r-t)}\binom{r}{t}d_{\mathcal{H}}(v),

which implies (
7
).

The following result is a straightforward corollary of Lemma
3.1
.
Its proof can be found in
Section
D
of the Appendix.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
r>t\geq 1
be integers and
p\geq 1
be a real number.
Suppose that
V_{1}\cup\cdots\cup V_{m}=[n]
is a partition and
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{G}(V_{1},\ldots,V_{m})
is a blowup of an
r
-graph
\mathcal{G}
on
[m]
. Then for every
i\in[m]
and
v\in V_{i}
,

\displaystyle D_{i}L_{\mathcal{G},t,p}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})-o(1)\leq\frac{d_{t,p,\mathcal{H}}(v)}{n^{t-1+p(r-t)}}\leq D_{i}L_{\mathcal{G},t,p}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}).

where
x_{i}\coloneqq|V_{i}|/n
for
i\in[m]
.

In the following lemma, we show that the
(t,p)
-norm is locally monotone, as defined in
[
CL24
]
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
r>t\geq 1
be integers and
p\geq 1
be a real number.
Suppose that
\mathcal{H}
is an
r
-graph and
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{H}
is a subgraph.
Then for every
v\in V(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})
,

\displaystyle d_{\mathcal{H},t,p}(v)-d_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime},t,p}(v)\geq\sum_{T}\left(d_{\mathcal{H}}^{p}(T)-\left(d_{\mathcal{H}}(T)-d_{\mathcal{H}}(T\cup\{v\})\right)^{p}\right),

(8)

where the summation is taken over
\partial_{r-t-1}L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)\setminus\partial_{r-t-1}L_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}(v)
.
In particular,

\displaystyle d_{\mathcal{H},t,p}(v)\geq d_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime},t,p}(v)\quad\text{for every }v\in V(\mathcal{H}^{\prime}).

Proof of Lemma
3.3
.

Fix
v\in V(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})
.
It is clear that
d_{\mathcal{H}}(S\cup\{v\})\geq d_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}(S\cup\{v\})
for every
S\in\partial_{r-t}L_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}(v)
.
So by Lemma
3.1
, to prove (
8
), it suffices to show that for every
T\in\partial_{r-t-1}L_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}(v)
,

\displaystyle d_{\mathcal{H}}^{p}(T)-\left(d_{\mathcal{H}}(T)-d_{\mathcal{H}}(T\cup\{v\})\right)^{p}\geq d_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}^{p}(T)-\left(d_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}(T)-d_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}(T\cup\{v\})\right)^{p}.

Let
\mathcal{G}\coloneqq\mathcal{H}\setminus\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
.
Fix
T\in\partial_{r-t-1}L_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}(v)
.
Notice that

\displaystyle\quad d_{\mathcal{H}}^{p}(T)-\left(d_{\mathcal{H}}(T)-d_{\mathcal{H}}(T\cup\{v\})\right)^{p}

\displaystyle=\left(d_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}(T)+d_{\mathcal{G}}(T)\right)^{p}-\left(\left(d_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}(T)+d_{\mathcal{G}}(T)\right)-\left(d_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}(T\cup\{v\})+d_{\mathcal{G}}(T\cup\{v\})\right)\right)^{p}

\displaystyle=\left(d_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}(T)+d_{\mathcal{G}}(T)\right)^{p}-\left(d_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}(T)-d_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}(T\cup\{v\})+d_{\mathcal{G}}(T)-d_{\mathcal{G}}(T\cup\{v\})\right)^{p}

\displaystyle\geq\left(d_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}(T)+d_{\mathcal{G}}(T)\right)^{p}-\left(d_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}(T)-d_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}(T\cup\{v\})+d_{\mathcal{G}}(T)\right)^{p}.

Consider the polynomial

\displaystyle g(X)\coloneqq\left(A+X\right)^{p}-(B+X)^{p},

where
A\coloneqq d_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}(T)
and
B\coloneqq d_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}(T)-d_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}(T\cup\{v\})
.
Since
A\geq B\geq 0
and
p\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle Dg(x)=p\left((A+x)^{p-1}-(B+x)^{p-1}\right)\geq 0\quad\text{for}\quad x\geq 0.

Therefore, by the monotonicity of
g(x)
,

\displaystyle\quad d_{\mathcal{H}}^{p}(T)-\left(d_{\mathcal{H}}(T)-d_{\mathcal{H}}(T\cup\{v\})\right)^{p}

\displaystyle\geq\left(d_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}(T)+d_{\mathcal{G}}(T)\right)^{p}-\left(\left(d_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}(T)-d_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}(T\cup\{v\})\right)+d_{\mathcal{G}}(T)\right)^{p}

\displaystyle\geq d_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}^{p}(T)-\left(\left(d_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}(T)-d_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}(T\cup\{v\})\right)\right)^{p},

proving Lemma
3.3
.

In the following lemma, we establish several combinatorial equalities that will be useful for subsequent estimations.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
r>t\geq 1
be integers and
p>0
be a real number.
For every
n
-vertex
r
-graph
\mathcal{H}
and for every
v\in V(\mathcal{H})
,

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in V(\mathcal{H})}\sum_{S\in\partial_{r-t}L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)}d_{\mathcal{H}}^{p}(S\cup\{v\})=t\cdot\left\lVert\mathcal{H}\right\rVert_{t,p},

(9)

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in V(\mathcal{H})}\sum_{T\in\partial_{r-t-1}L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)}d_{\mathcal{H}}(T\cup\{v\})\cdot d_{\mathcal{H}}^{p-1}(T)=(r-t)\cdot\left\lVert\mathcal{H}\right\rVert_{t,p},

(10)

and

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in V(\mathcal{H})}\sum_{T\in\partial_{r-t-1}L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)}\left(\frac{d_{\mathcal{H}}(T\cup\{v\})}{d_{\mathcal{H}}(T)}\right)^{2}d_{\mathcal{H}}^{p}(T)=O(n^{t+p(r-t)-\delta_{p}}),

(11)

where

\displaystyle\delta_{p}\coloneqq\begin{cases}\frac{p}{4},&\quad\text{if}\quad p\in(0,1],\\
\frac{p-1}{2p},&\quad\text{if}\quad p>1.\end{cases}

Proof of Lemma
3.4
.

Equation (
9
) follows from the following simple double counting
\colon

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in V(\mathcal{H})}\sum_{S\in\partial_{r-t}L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)}d_{\mathcal{H}}^{p}(S\cup\{v\})=\sum_{T\in\partial_{r-t}\mathcal{H}}|T|\cdot d_{\mathcal{H}}^{p}(T)=t\sum_{T\in\partial_{r-t}\mathcal{H}}d_{\mathcal{H}}^{p}(T).

Notice that for every
T\in\partial_{r-t}\mathcal{H}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in V(\mathcal{H})\setminus T}d_{\mathcal{H}}(T\cup\{v\})=(r-t)\cdot|L_{\mathcal{H}}(T)|=(r-t)\cdot d_{\mathcal{H}}(T).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in V(\mathcal{H})}\sum_{T\in\partial_{r-t-1}L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)}d_{\mathcal{H}}(T\cup\{v\})\cdot d_{\mathcal{H}}^{p-1}(T)

\displaystyle=\sum_{T\in\partial_{r-t}\mathcal{H}}\sum_{v\in V(\mathcal{H})\setminus T}d_{\mathcal{H}}(T\cup\{v\})\cdot d_{\mathcal{H}}^{p-1}(T)

\displaystyle=\sum_{T\in\partial_{r-t}\mathcal{H}}(r-t)\cdot d_{\mathcal{H}}(T)\cdot d_{\mathcal{H}}^{p-1}(T)

\displaystyle=(r-t)\cdot\left\lVert\mathcal{H}\right\rVert_{t,p},

proving (
10
).
So it suffices to prove (
11
).
Let

\displaystyle\delta\coloneqq\begin{cases}\frac{1}{4},&\quad\text{if}\quad p\in(0,1],\\
\frac{p-1}{2p},&\quad\text{if}\quad p>1.\end{cases}

Let us partition
\partial_{r-t-1}L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)
into two sets
\colon

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{1}\coloneqq\left\{T\in\partial_{r-t-1}L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)\colon d_{\mathcal{H}}(T)\geq n^{r-t-1+\delta}\right\}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{T}_{2}\coloneqq\partial_{r-t-1}L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)\setminus\mathcal{T}_{1}.

Observe that
\frac{d_{\mathcal{H}}(T\cup\{v\})}{d_{\mathcal{H}}(T)}\leq\frac{n^{r-t-1}}{n^{r-t-1+\delta}}=\frac{1}{n^{\delta}}
for every
T\in\mathcal{T}_{1}
. So it follows from (
10
) that

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{v\in V(\mathcal{H})}\sum_{T\in\partial_{r-t-1}L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)}\left(\frac{d_{\mathcal{H}}(T\cup\{v\})}{d_{\mathcal{H}}(T)}\right)^{2}d_{\mathcal{H}}^{p}(T)

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in V(\mathcal{H})}\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{1}}\left(\frac{d_{\mathcal{H}}(T\cup\{v\})}{d_{\mathcal{H}}(T)}\right)^{2}d_{\mathcal{H}}^{p}(T)+\sum_{v\in V(\mathcal{H})}\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{2}}\left(\frac{d_{\mathcal{H}}(T\cup\{v\})}{d_{\mathcal{H}}(T)}\right)^{2}d_{\mathcal{H}}^{p}(T)

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in V(\mathcal{H})}\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{1}}\frac{d_{\mathcal{H}}(T\cup\{v\})}{d_{\mathcal{H}}(T)}\cdot d_{\mathcal{H}}(T\cup\{v\})\cdot d_{\mathcal{H}}^{p-1}(T)+\sum_{v\in V(\mathcal{H})}\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{2}}\left(\frac{d_{\mathcal{H}}(T\cup\{v\})}{d_{\mathcal{H}}(T)}\right)^{2}d_{\mathcal{H}}^{p}(T)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n^{\delta}}\sum_{v\in V(\mathcal{H})}\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{1}}d_{\mathcal{H}}(T\cup\{v\})\cdot d_{\mathcal{H}}^{p-1}(T)+\sum_{v\in V(\mathcal{H})}\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{2}}\left(\frac{d_{\mathcal{H}}(T\cup\{v\})}{d_{\mathcal{H}}(T)}\right)^{2}d_{\mathcal{H}}^{p}(T)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(r-t)\left\lVert\mathcal{H}\right\rVert_{t,p}}{n^{\delta}}+\sum_{v\in V(\mathcal{H})}\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{2}}\left(\frac{d_{\mathcal{H}}(T\cup\{v\})}{d_{\mathcal{H}}(T)}\right)^{2}d_{\mathcal{H}}^{p}(T).

(12)

Case 1
\colon

p>1
.

Inequality (
12
) continues as

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{v\in V(\mathcal{H})}\sum_{T\in\partial_{r-t-1}L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)}\left(\frac{d_{\mathcal{H}}(T\cup\{v\})}{d_{\mathcal{H}}(T)}\right)^{2}d_{\mathcal{H}}^{p}(T)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(r-t)\left\lVert\mathcal{H}\right\rVert_{t,p}}{n^{\delta}}+\sum_{v\in V(\mathcal{H})}\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{2}}d_{\mathcal{H}}^{p}(T)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(r-t)\left\lVert\mathcal{H}\right\rVert_{t,p}}{n^{\delta}}+n^{t+1}\left(n^{r-t-1+\delta}\right)^{p}

\displaystyle\leq n^{t+p(r-t)-\delta}+n^{t+p(r-t)+p\delta-(p-1)}\leq 2n^{t+p(r-t)-\frac{p-1}{2p}}.

Case 2
\colon

p\in(0,1]
.

Let
\alpha\coloneqq 1-\frac{p}{2}\in(0,1)
. Inequality (
12
) continues as

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{v\in V(\mathcal{H})}\sum_{T\in\partial_{r-t-1}L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)}\left(\frac{d_{\mathcal{H}}(T\cup\{v\})}{d_{\mathcal{H}}(T)}\right)^{2}d_{\mathcal{H}}^{p}(T)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(r-t)\left\lVert\mathcal{H}\right\rVert_{t,p}}{n^{\delta}}+\sum_{v\in V(\mathcal{H})}\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{2}}\left(\frac{d_{\mathcal{H}}(T\cup\{v\})}{d_{\mathcal{H}}(T)}\right)^{2-\alpha}d_{\mathcal{H}}^{\alpha}(T\cup\{v\})\cdot d_{\mathcal{H}}^{p-\alpha}(T)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n^{t+p(r-t)}}{n^{\delta}}+\sum_{v\in V(\mathcal{H})}\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{2}}d_{\mathcal{H}}^{\alpha}(T\cup\{v\})\cdot d_{\mathcal{H}}^{p-\alpha}(T)

\displaystyle=n^{t+p(r-t)-\frac{1}{4}}+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{2}}d_{\mathcal{H}}^{p-\alpha}(T)\sum_{v\in V(\mathcal{H})\setminus T}d_{\mathcal{H}}^{\alpha}(T\cup\{v\}).

(13)

Fix
T\in\mathcal{T}_{2}
and let
\mathcal{G}\coloneqq L_{\mathcal{H}}(T)
. Notice that
d_{\mathcal{G}}(v)=d_{\mathcal{H}}(T\cup\{v\})
, and hence, by Jensen’s inequality,

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in V(\mathcal{H})\setminus T}d_{\mathcal{H}}^{\alpha}(T\cup\{v\})\leq(n-t)\left(\frac{\sum_{v\in V(\mathcal{H})\setminus T}d_{\mathcal{H}}(T\cup\{v\})}{n-t}\right)^{\alpha}\leq(r-t)^{\alpha}\cdot d_{\mathcal{H}}^{\alpha}(T)\cdot n^{1-\alpha}.

Therefore, (
13
) continues as

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{2}}d_{\mathcal{H}}^{p-\alpha}(T)\sum_{v\in V(\mathcal{H})\setminus T}d_{\mathcal{H}}^{\alpha}(T\cup\{v\})

\displaystyle\leq(r-t)^{\alpha}n^{1-\alpha}\cdot\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{2}}d_{\mathcal{H}}^{p}(T)

\displaystyle\leq rn^{1-\alpha}\cdot n^{p(r-t-1+\delta)}\cdot n^{t}

\displaystyle=rn^{t+p(r-t)+1-\alpha-p+p\delta}=rn^{t+p(r-t)-\frac{p}{4}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in V(\mathcal{H})}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\in\partial_{r-t}\mathcal{H}\colon\\
v\not\in T\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{d_{\mathcal{H}}(T\cup\{v\})}{d_{\mathcal{H}}(T)}\right)^{2}d_{\mathcal{H}}^{p}(T)\leq n^{t+p(r-t)-\frac{1}{4}}+rn^{t+p(r-t)-\frac{p}{4}}\leq 2rn^{t+p(r-t)-\frac{p}{4}}.

This completes the proof of (
11
).

In the following lemma, we show that the map
\Gamma
(defined at the beginning of this section) is
(t+p(r-t))
-uniform, as defined in
[
CL24
]
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
r>t\geq 1
be integers and
p>0
be a real number.
For every
n
-vertex
r
-graph
\mathcal{H}
,

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{v\in V(\mathcal{H})}d_{\mathcal{H},t,p}(v)-\left(t+p(r-t)\right)\cdot\left\lVert\mathcal{H}\right\rVert_{t,p}\right|=O(n^{t+p(r-t)-\delta_{p}}),

where
\delta_{p}>0
is the same constant as defined in Lemma
3.4
.

Proof of Lemma
3.5
.

Let
v\in V(\mathcal{H})
be a vertex and
T\in\partial_{r-t-1}L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)
be a
t
-set.
Since
\frac{d_{\mathcal{H}}(T\cup\{v\})}{d_{\mathcal{H}}(T)}\in(0,1]
, it follows from (
5
) that

\displaystyle\quad\left|d_{\mathcal{H}}^{p}(T)-\left(d_{\mathcal{H}}(T)-d_{\mathcal{H}}(T\cup\{v\})\right)^{p}-p\cdot d_{\mathcal{H}}(T\cup\{v\})\cdot d_{\mathcal{H}}^{p-1}(T)\right|

\displaystyle=d_{\mathcal{H}}^{p}(T)\cdot\left|1-\left(1-\frac{d_{\mathcal{H}}(T\cup\{v\})}{d_{\mathcal{H}}(T)}\right)^{p}-p\cdot\frac{d_{\mathcal{H}}(T\cup\{v\})}{d_{\mathcal{H}}(T)}\right|

\displaystyle\leq d_{\mathcal{H}}^{p}(T)\cdot(p^{2}+1)\cdot\left(\frac{d_{\mathcal{H}}(T\cup\{v\})}{d_{\mathcal{H}}(T)}\right)^{2}.

Combining with Lemma
3.1
, (
9
), (
10
), and (
11
), we obtain

\displaystyle\quad\left|\sum_{v\in V(\mathcal{H})}d_{\mathcal{H},t,p}(v)-(t+p(r-t))\cdot\left\lVert\mathcal{H}\right\rVert_{t,p}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{v\in V(\mathcal{H})}\sum_{T\in\partial_{r-t-1}L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)}\left|\left(d_{\mathcal{H}}^{p}(T)-\left(d_{\mathcal{H}}(T)-d_{\mathcal{H}}(T\cup\{v\})\right)^{p}\right)-p\cdot d_{\mathcal{H}}(T\cup\{v\})\cdot d_{\mathcal{H}}^{p-1}(T)\right|

\displaystyle\leq(p^{2}+1)\cdot\sum_{v\in V(\mathcal{H})}\sum_{T\in\partial_{r-t-1}L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)}\left(\frac{d_{\mathcal{H}}(T\cup\{v\})}{d_{\mathcal{H}}(T)}\right)^{2}\cdot d_{\mathcal{H}}^{p}(T)=O(n^{t+p(r-t)-\delta_{p}}),

completing the proof of Lemma
3.5
.

Next,
we show that the map
\Gamma
is continuous, as defined in
[
CL24
]
.
We will need the following simple inequality in the proof.

Proposition 3.6
.

Suppose that
r>t\geq 1
are integers and
p_{2}>p_{1}>0
are real numbers.
Then for every
r
-graph
\mathcal{H}
,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathcal{H}\right\rVert_{t,p_{1}}\leq\left\lVert\mathcal{H}\right\rVert_{t,p_{2}}^{\frac{p_{1}}{p_{2}}}\cdot|\partial_{r-t}\mathcal{H}|^{1-\frac{p_{1}}{p_{2}}}.

In particular, for every
p\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathcal{H}\right\rVert_{t,p}\leq\left(\binom{r}{t}\cdot|\mathcal{H}|\right)^{p}\cdot|\partial_{r-t}\mathcal{H}|^{1-p}\leq\left(\binom{r}{t}\cdot|\mathcal{H}|\right)^{p}\cdot n^{t(1-p)}.

(14)

Proof of Proposition
3.6
.

Since
p_{1}/p_{2}\in(0,1)
, it follows from Jensen’s inequality that

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathcal{H}\right\rVert_{t,p_{1}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{T\in\partial_{r-t}\mathcal{H}}\left(d_{\mathcal{H}}^{p_{2}}(T)\right)^{\frac{p_{1}}{p_{2}}}

Lemma 3.7

Notice from the definition that

(15)

Lemma 3.8

\displaystyle\left\lVert L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)\right\rVert_{t-1,p}-\left\lVert L_{\mathcal{G}}(v)\right\rVert_{t-1,p}

4.1
Excluding nontrivial Steiner triple systems

For every real number

The main task of this subsection is to establish the following result, which extends

Proposition 4.1
.

For every real number
p\geq 1
, there exists

Suppose that

is an

is
3

The key inequality for proving Proposition

To avoid being distracted too much from the proof of the main result (Theorem

.

Lemma 4.2
.

For every real number

\displaystyle\frac{p\cdot h_{p-1}^{\ast}+g_{p}^{\ast}}{(p+2)\cdot h_{p}^{\ast}}<6.88.

(17)

The following inequality will be useful for proving Lemma

The integral case corresponds to an old result of Brown–Sidorenko

The general case can be derived with a slight modification of their argument. For completeness, we include its proof in Section

Let

We prove this lemma by induction on

Additionally, since the number of vertices in every STS is in

Base case:

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\in[m]}D_{i}L_{\mathcal{S},2,p}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})

\displaystyle\leq\left(L_{\mathcal{S},2,0}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})\right)^{1-(p-1)}\left(L_{\mathcal{S},2,1}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})\right)^{p-1-0}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{\{i,j,k\}\in\mathcal{S}}\left(x_{i}x_{j}+x_{j}x_{k}+x_{k}x_{i}\right)\right)^{2-p}\left(\sum_{\{i,j,k\}\in\mathcal{S}}3x_{i}x_{j}x_{k}\right)^{p-1}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{\{i,j\}\in\binom{[m]}{2}}x_{i}x_{j}\right)^{2-p}\left(3\sum_{\{i,j,k\}\in\mathcal{S}}x_{i}x_{j}x_{k}\right)^{p-1}\leq\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2-p}\left(\frac{1}{9}\right)^{p-1},

\displaystyle m\leq\frac{1}{1-10^{-3}}\cdot\frac{p\cdot L_{\mathcal{S},2,p-1}(\vec{x})+L_{K_{m},1,p}(\vec{x})}{(p+2)\cdot h^{\ast}_{p}}\leq\frac{1}{1-10^{-3}}\cdot\frac{p\cdot\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2-p}\left(\frac{1}{9}\right)^{p-1}+\frac{1}{4^{p-1}}}{(p+2)\cdot\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)^{p+1}}<5,

Inductive step:
Now suppose that

\displaystyle m

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{1-10^{-3}}\cdot\frac{p\cdot L_{\mathcal{S},2,p-1}(\vec{x})+L_{K_{m},1,p}(\vec{x})}{(p+2)\cdot h^{\ast}_{p}}

(19)

-graph
\mathcal{H}_{1}\coloneqq\mathcal{H}-v_{\ast}
keeps
\mathcal{F}
-free, then

\mathrm{ex}_{t,p}(n-1,\mathcal{F})\geq\lVert\mathcal{H}_{1}\rVert_{t,p}=\lVert\mathcal{H}\rVert_{t,p}-d_{\mathcal{H},t,p}(v_{\ast})\geq\mathrm{ex}_{t,p}(n,\mathcal{F})-k\cdot\dfrac{\mathrm{ex}_{t,p}(n,\mathcal{F})}{n}-\varepsilon n^{k+\alpha}.

(30)

Claim B.5
.

\pi_{n-1}\geq\pi_{n}-{n^{\alpha}}.

Proof.

Note
k\geq 0
and
\frac{1}{n}\in[0,1]
.
It follows from (

) and Fact

(ii)
that

\displaystyle\dfrac{\mathrm{ex}_{t,p}(n-1,\mathcal{F})}{(n-1)^{k}}

\displaystyle\geq\dfrac{\mathrm{ex}_{t,p}(n,\mathcal{F})}{(n-1)^{k}}-k\cdot\dfrac{\mathrm{ex}_{t,p}(n,\mathcal{F})}{n(n-1)^{k}}-\dfrac{\varepsilon n^{k+\alpha}}{(n-1)^{k}}

\displaystyle\geq\dfrac{\mathrm{ex}_{t,p}(n,\mathcal{F})}{n^{k}}+\left(\dfrac{\mathrm{ex}_{t,p}(n,\mathcal{F})}{(n-1)^{k}}-\dfrac{\mathrm{ex}_{t,p}(n,\mathcal{F})}{n^{k}}-k\cdot\dfrac{\mathrm{ex}_{t,p}(n,\mathcal{F})}{n(n-1)^{k}}\right)-2\varepsilon n^{\alpha}

Appendix C
Estimations for
\alpha_{k}

Lemma C.1
.

Let
\ell(x)
denote the piecewise linear function that connects points
in

\left\{\left(\frac{k-1}{k},\frac{k-1}{k^{2}}\right)\colon k\in\mathbb{N}^{+}\right\}
. That is,

\displaystyle\ell(x)\coloneqq-\frac{k^{2}-k-1}{k(k+1)}x+\frac{k-1}{k+1}\quad\text{for}\quad x\in\left[\frac{k-1}{k},\frac{k}{k+1}\right].

For every real number
p\in[0,1)
, let also
g(p)\coloneqq\max\left\{\ell(x)^{p}\cdot x^{1-p}\colon x\in[0,1)\right\}
. Then for every
k\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 3}
, and
p\in\left[\frac{1}{k-1},\frac{k-1}{k(k-2)}\right)
,

g(p)=\ell\left(\frac{k-1}{k}\right)^{p}\cdot\left(\frac{k-1}{k}\right)^{1-p}=\frac{k-1}{k^{p+1}}.

(31)

Proof.

For every
i\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, let
\ell_{i}(x)\coloneqq-\frac{i^{2}-i-1}{i(i+1)}x+\frac{i-1}{i+1}
for every
x\in[0,1]
. Also, for a real number
p\in[0,1)
and
i
as above, let
t_{i,p}(x)\coloneqq\ell_{i}(x)^{p}x^{1-p}
, and let
g_{i}(p)\coloneqq\max\left\{t_{i,p}(x):x\in\left[\frac{i-1}{i},\frac{i}{i+1}\right]\right\}
. Clearly

\ell(x)=\ell_{i}(x)\quad\text{for }x\in\left[\frac{i-1}{i},\frac{i}{i+1}\right].

(32)

Thus, note that

g(p)=\max_{i\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}g_{i}(p).

(33)

Also, for
i\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2}
and
p\in[0,1)
, we have that

\displaystyle t_{i-1,p}\left(\frac{i-1}{i}\right)

\displaystyle=\ell_{i-1}\left(\frac{i-1}{i}\right)^{p}\cdot\left(\frac{i-1}{i}\right)^{1-p}

\displaystyle=\ell\left(\frac{i-1}{i}\right)^{p}\cdot\left(\frac{i-1}{i}\right)^{1-p}

by (
32
)

\displaystyle=\ell_{i}\left(\frac{i-1}{i}\right)^{p}\cdot\left(\frac{i-1}{i}\right)^{1-p}

by (
32
)

\displaystyle=t_{i,p}\left(\frac{i-1}{i}\right).

(34)

For
p\in(0,1)
, let

p^{(1)}\coloneqq\frac{\sqrt{4p^{2}+1}+1}{2p},\quad\text{ and }\quad p^{(2)}\coloneqq\frac{\sqrt{4p+1}+1}{2p},

and note that
1<p^{(1)}<p^{(2)}
.

Claim C.2
.

Fix
p\in(0,1)
, then for every
i\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
,

(a)

if
i<p^{(1)}
,
then
t_{i,p}
is increasing over
\left[\frac{i-1}{i},\frac{i}{i+1}\right]
, hence,
g_{i}(p)=t_{i,p}\left(\frac{i}{i+1}\right)
.

(b)

If
i>p^{(2)}
,
then
t_{i,p}
is decreasing over
\left[\frac{i-1}{i},\frac{i}{i+1}\right]
, hence,
g_{i}(p)=t_{i,p}\left(\frac{i-1}{i}\right)
.

Proof.

We analyze the first derivative of the function
t_{i,p}
to show that it is positive on the first case and negative on the
second. For any
i\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, let
a_{i}\coloneqq\frac{i^{2}-i-1}{i(i+1)}
and
b_{i}\coloneqq\frac{i-1}{i+1}
so that
\ell_{i}(x)=-a_{i}x+b
. Fix
p\in(0,1)
and
i\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
. We then have that

Dt_{i,p}(x)=\ell_{i}(x)^{p-1}\left(\frac{b_{i}(1-p)}{x^{p}}-a_{i}x^{1-p}\right).

(35)

Also, the following equality will be useful later

\frac{b_{i}(1-p)}{\left(\frac{b_{i}(1-p)}{a_{i}}\right)^{p}}-a_{i}\left(\frac{b_{i}(1-p)}{a_{i}}\right)^{1-p}=a_{i}^{p}b_{i}^{1-p}(1-p)^{1-p}-a_{i}^{p}b_{i}^{1-p}(1-p)^{1-p}=0.

(36)

To prove
(a)
, suppose that
i<p^{(1)}
. Assume first that
i=1
and observe that
a_{1}=-1/2
and
b_{1}=0
. Then
t_{1,p}(x)=(-a_{1}x+b_{1})^{p}x^{1-p}=\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^{p}x^{1-p}=\frac{x}{2^{p}}
, which is increasing in its entire domain. Assume now that
2\leq i<p^{(1)}
. Then
(2pi-1)^{2}<4p^{2}+1
, which implies that
-i<-p(i^{2}-1)
. By adding
i^{2}-1
to both sides of the inequality we have that
i^{2}-i-1<i^{2}-1-p(i^{2}-1)=(1-p)(i-1)(i+1)
. Multiplying by
\frac{i}{(i^{2}-i+1)(i+1)}
to both sides results in
\frac{i}{i+1}<\frac{i-1}{i+1}\cdot\frac{i(i+1)}{i^{2}-i+1}\cdot(1-p)=\frac{b_{i}(1-p)}{a_{i}}
. Suppose also that
x\in\left[\frac{i-1}{i},\frac{i}{i+1}\right]
. Then
x\leq\frac{i}{i+1}<\frac{b_{i}(1-p)}{a_{i}}
. By using this upper bound
on
x
in (
35
) we get

Dt_{i,p}(x)>\ell_{i}(x)^{p-1}\left(\frac{b_{i}(1-p)}{\left(\frac{b_{i}(1-p)}{a_{i}}\right)^{p}}-a_{i}\left(\frac{b_{i}(1-p)}{a_{i}}\right)^{1-p}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:helper_derivative_point}}}{{=}}0,

which proves
(a)
.

To prove
(b)
, suppose that
i>p^{(2)}
. This implies
that
i\geq 2
and that
(2pi-i)^{2}>4p+1
, which implies
that
-i-1>-pi^{2}
. By adding
i^{2}
to both sides of the inequality we have
that
i^{2}-i-1>i^{2}-pi^{2}=(1-p)i^{2}
. Multiplying
by
\frac{i-1}{i(i^{2}-i-1)}
to both sides results
in
\frac{i-1}{i}>\frac{i-1}{i+1}\cdot\frac{i(i+1)}{i^{2}-i-1}\cdot(1-p)=\frac{b_{i}(1-p)}{a_{i}}
. Suppose also
that
x\in\left[\frac{i-1}{i},\frac{i}{i+1}\right]
.
Then
x\geq\frac{i-1}{i}>\frac{b_{i}(1-p)}{a_{i}}
. By using this lower bound
on
x
in (
35
) we get

Dt_{i,p}(x)<\ell_{i}(x)^{p-1}\left(\frac{b_{i}(1-p)}{\left(\frac{b_{i}(1-p)}{a_{i}}\right)^{p}}-a_{i}\left(\frac{b_{i}(1-p)}{a_{i}}\right)^{1-p}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:helper_derivative_point}}}{{=}}0,

which proves
(b)
and completes the proof of the claim.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 3}
,
and
p\in\left[\frac{1}{k-1},\frac{k-1}{k(k-2)}\right)
be fixed. We will
first show that

k-1<p^{(1)}<p^{(2)}<k.

(37)

For the leftmost inequality, the fact that
p<\frac{k-1}{k(k-2)}
implies
that
pk^{2}-2pk-k+1<0
. Multiplying both sides of the inequality by
4p

results in
4p^{2}k^{2}-8p^{2}k-4pk+4p<0
. We now add
4p^{2}+1
to both sides to
get
4p^{2}k^{2}+4p^{2}+1-8p^{2}k-4pk+4p=\left(2pk-2p-1\right)^{2}<4p^{2}+1
, which after taking square roots from both sides implies
that
2pk-2p<\sqrt{4p^{2}+1}+1
. Dividing both sides by
2p
we
obtain
k-1<\frac{\sqrt{4p^{2}+1}+1}{2p}=p^{(1)}
, as required.

For the rightmost inequality, observe
that
p\geq\frac{1}{k-1}=\frac{k+1}{k^{2}-1}>\frac{k+1}{k^{2}}
. This
implies that
pk^{2}-k-1>0
. Multiplying both sides of the inequality by
4p

results in
4p^{2}k^{2}-4pk-4p>0
. We now add
4p+1
to both sides to
get
4p^{2}k^{2}-4pk+1=\left(2pk-1\right)^{2}>4p+1
, which after taking
square roots from both sides implies that
2pk>\sqrt{4p+1}+1
, and after
dividing both sides by
2p
we obtain
k>\frac{\sqrt{4p+1}+1}{2p}=p^{(2)}
,
which completes the proof of (
37
).

We will now determine
g(p)
by using (
33
), and considering the
cases
i\leq k-1
and
i\geq k
separately. In both cases, we analyze the
sequence
g_{i}(p)
, showing that it is non-decreasing in the former case, and
non-increasing in the latter.

For
2\leq i\leq k-1
, we have that

g_{i-1}(p)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:ps-between-ks}\ref{item:k-increasing}}}{{=}}t_{i-1,p}\left(\frac{i-1}{i}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:piece-intersection}}}{{=}}t_{i,p}\left(\frac{i-1}{i}\right)\leq g_{i}(p),

where the last inequality follows from the definition of
g_{i}(p)
. Therefore

\displaystyle\max_{i=1}^{k-1}g_{i}(p)=g_{k-1}(p)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:ps-between-ks}\ref{item:k-increasing}}}{{=}}t_{k-1,p}\left(\frac{k-1}{k}\right).

(38)

For
i\geq k+1
, by the definition of
g_{i-1}(p)
we have that

g_{i-1}(p)\geq t_{i-1,p}\left(\frac{i-1}{i}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:piece-intersection}}}{{=}}t_{i,p}\left(\frac{i-1}{i}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:ps-between-ks}\ref{item:i-decreasing}}}{{=}}g_{i}(p),

and therefore

\displaystyle\max_{i\geq k}g_{i}(p)=g_{k}(p)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:ps-between-ks}\ref{item:i-decreasing}}}{{=}}t_{k,p}\left(\frac{k-1}{k}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:piece-intersection}}}{{=}}t_{k-1,p}\left(\frac{k-1}{k}\right).

(39)

We now combine these results to obtain

\displaystyle g(p)

\displaystyle=\max\left\{\max_{i=1}^{k-1}g_{i}(p),\max_{i\geq k}g_{i}(p)\right\}

by (
33
)

\displaystyle=t_{k-1,p}\left(\frac{k-1}{k}\right)

by (
38
) and (
39
)

\displaystyle=\ell_{k-1}\left(\frac{k-1}{k}\right)^{p}\cdot\left(\frac{k-1}{k}\right)^{1-p}

by definition

\displaystyle=\ell\left(\frac{k-1}{k}\right)^{p}\cdot\left(\frac{k-1}{k}\right)^{1-p}

\displaystyle\quad\text{by~\eqref{eq:ell-i-domain}}.

This completes the proof.

Corollary D.1
.

Let
r>t\geq 1
be integers and

be a real number.
Suppose that

is a partition and
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{G}(V_{1},\ldots,V_{m})

r

\mathcal{G}
. Then for every

and

,

\displaystyle D_{i}L_{\mathcal{G},t,p}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})-o(1)\leq\frac{d_{t,p,\mathcal{H}}(v)}{n^{t-1+p(r-t)}}\leq D_{i}L_{\mathcal{G},t,p}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}).

where
x_{i}\coloneqq|V_{i}|/n
for

Proof of Corollary
D.1
.

For simplicity, let us assume that
V(\mathcal{G})=[m]
.
By relabelling the sets
V_{1},\ldots,V_{m}
we may assume that
v\in V_{1}
.
First, it follows from the definition that

\displaystyle\quad D_{1}L_{\mathcal{G},t,p}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})

\displaystyle=\sum_{S\in\partial_{r-t}L_{\mathcal{G}}(1)}x_{S}\left(\sum_{I\in L_{\mathcal{G}}(S\cup\{v\})}x_{I}\right)^{p}+\sum_{T\in\partial_{r-t-1}L_{\mathcal{G}}(1)}x_{T}\cdot p\left(\sum_{I\in L_{\mathcal{G}}(T)}x_{I}\right)^{p-1}\frac{\sum_{I\in L_{\mathcal{G}}(T),1\in I}x_{I}}{x_{1}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{S\in\partial_{r-t}L_{\mathcal{G}}(1)}x_{S}\left(\sum_{I\in L_{\mathcal{G}}(S\cup\{v\})}x_{I}\right)^{p}+\sum_{T\in\partial_{r-t-1}L_{\mathcal{G}}(1)}x_{T}\cdot p\left(\sum_{I\in L_{\mathcal{G}}(T)}x_{I}\right)^{p-1}\sum_{J\in L_{\mathcal{G}}(T\cup\{1\})}x_{J}.

Since
\mathcal{H}
is a blowup of
\mathcal{G}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{S\in\partial_{r-t}L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)}d_{\mathcal{H}}^{p}(S\cup\{v\})

\displaystyle=\sum_{S\in\partial_{r-t}L_{\mathcal{G}}(1)}x_{S}\cdot n^{t-1}\left(\sum_{I\in L_{\mathcal{G}}(S\cup\{v\})}x_{I}\cdot n^{r-t}\right)^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{S\in\partial_{r-t}L_{\mathcal{G}}(1)}x_{S}\left(\sum_{I\in L_{\mathcal{G}}(S\cup\{v\})}x_{I}\right)^{p}n^{t-1+p(r-t)},

and

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{T\in\partial_{r-t-1}L_{\mathcal{H}}(v)}\left(d_{\mathcal{H}}^{p}(T)-\left(d_{\mathcal{H}}(T)-d_{\mathcal{H}}(T\cup\{v\})\right)^{p}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{T\in\partial_{r-t-1}L_{\mathcal{G}}(1)}x_{T}\cdot n^{t}\left(\left(\sum_{I\in L_{\mathcal{G}}(T)}x_{I}\cdot n^{r-t}\right)^{p}-\left(\sum_{I\in L_{\mathcal{G}}(T)}x_{I}\cdot n^{r-t}-\sum_{J\in L_{\mathcal{G}}(T\cup\{1\})}x_{J}\cdot n^{r-t-1}\right)^{p}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{T\in\partial_{r-t-1}L_{\mathcal{G}}(1)}x_{T}\left(p\left(\sum_{I\in L_{\mathcal{G}}(T)}x_{I}\right)^{p-1}\sum_{J\in L_{\mathcal{G}}(T\cup\{1\})}x_{J}\right)n^{t-1+p(r-t)}-o(n^{t-1+p(r-t)}),

\displaystyle d_{t,p,\mathcal{H}}(v)

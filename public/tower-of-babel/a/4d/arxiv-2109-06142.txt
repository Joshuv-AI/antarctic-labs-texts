Slopes of Siegel cusp forms and geometry of compactified Kuga varieties
Flora Poon, Riccardo Salvati Manni and Gregory Sankaran

Abstract




Introduction

The universal family
X(\Gamma)
over a moduli space

\mathcal{A}(\Gamma)=\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}_{g}
of abelian varieties associated with a finite
index subgroup of
\mathop{\mathrm{Sp}}\nolimits(2g,\mathbb{Z})
is known as the Kuga variety. Such
families were first studied systematically by M. Kuga, whose 1964
Chicago lecture notes on the subject
[
12
]
have been recently
published. The construction is given in
[
12
]
for

\Gamma<\mathop{\mathrm{Sp}}\nolimits(2g,\mathbb{Z})
a torsion-free subgroup of finite index, but the
restriction to torsion-free can be removed. However, if
-1\in\Gamma

then the fibre of the family is no longer the abelian variety but
instead the corresponding Kummer variety. One could also allow

\Gamma
to be a subgroup of
\mathop{\mathrm{Sp}}\nolimits(2g,\mathbb{Q})
commensurable with

\mathop{\mathrm{Sp}}\nolimits(2g,\mathbb{Z})
, for example taking
\mathcal{A}(\Gamma)
to be the moduli space
of abelian varieties with some non-principal polarisation, but we
shall not pursue this here.

A natural generalisation is to consider the
n
-fold Kuga varieties

X^{n}(\Gamma)
, whose general fibre is the
n
-fold direct product of
the corresponding abelian variety
A
or, if
-1\in\Gamma
, of the
associated Kummer variety
A/\pm 1
.

Alternatively one may consider the universal family

\underline{X}^{n}(\Gamma)
over the stack

\underline{\mathcal{A}}(\Gamma):=[\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}_{g}]
. In this case the
fibre is the abelian variety in all cases, but if
-1\in\Gamma
then
the base has non-trivial stabilisers generically. This is the object
that is studied in a particular case in
[
9
]
.

We shall be concerned with compactifications of the
n
-fold Kuga
variety
X^{n}_{g}=X^{n}(\mathop{\mathrm{Sp}}\nolimits(2g,\mathbb{Z}))
associated with the coarse moduli
space
\mathcal{A}_{g}
of principally polarised abelian
g
-folds over

\mathbb{C}
. By convention,
X_{g}^{0}=\mathcal{A}_{g}
.

The starting point for our work is Ma’s study
[
13
]
of

X_{g}^{n}(\Gamma)
. We construct a special compactification, which we
call a Namikawa compactification, of
X^{n}_{g}
and this, together with
recent and less recent results about the slope of
\mathcal{A}_{g}
, allows
us to determine the Kodaira dimension
\kappa(X_{g}^{n})
of
X_{g}^{n}
whenever

g\geq 2
and
n\geq 1
.

Theorem 1

Suppose that
g\geq 2
and
n\geq 1
. Then the Kodaira dimension

\kappa(X^{n}_{g})
of

X^{n}_{g}
satisfies

•

\kappa(X^{n}_{g})={\frac{1}{2}}g(g+1)=\dim\mathcal{A}_{g}
if
g+n\geq 7
, except for

(g,n)=(4,3)
,
(3,5)
,
(3,4)
,
(2,7)
,
(2,6)
and
(2,5)
.

•

\kappa(X_{2}^{7})=\kappa(X^{5}_{3})=\kappa(X^{3}_{4})=0
, and

•

\kappa(X^{n}_{g})=-\infty
otherwise, in particular

\kappa(X_{2}^{6})=\kappa(X_{2}^{5})=\kappa(X^{4}_{3})=-\infty
.

In fact
X_{g}^{1}
is unirational for
g\leq 5
: see
[
22
]
for

g=3
and
g=4
and
[
9
]
for
g=5
. Note that
X_{g}^{1}
is the
boundary of the Mumford partial compactification
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{g+1}
. One can
therefore compactify
X_{g}^{1}
by taking its closure in any toroidal
compactification of
\mathcal{A}_{g+1}
, since these all contain
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{g+1}

as a dense open set, but it is not straightforward to control the
singularities that then arise.

Our main technical result concerns the existence of a sufficiently
good compactification of
X_{g}^{n}
. We will say that a compactification
of
X_{g}^{n}
is a Namikawa compactification if it dominates a toroidal
compactification of
\mathcal{A}_{g}
and boundary divisors are mapped to
boundary divisors: see Definition
1.1
for a full
explanation and precise details. We prove (see
Theorem
1.2
for the precise statement):

Theorem 2

Suppose
g\geq 2
and
n\geq 1
. There exists a Namikawa
compactification
\overline{X_{g}^{n}}
with canonical singularities as long as

g+n\geq 6
.

There is some overlap between our results and those of
[
13
]
. The
singularities of
X_{g}^{n}
are studied in
[
13
, Section 10]
, but the
singularities at the boundary
\overline{X_{g}^{n}}
are not considered
there. That is sufficient for computing the geometric genus, but not
other plurigenera: on the other hand, Ma’s approach gives precise
information about the geometric genus and therefore some information
about the Kodaira dimension.

Our original motivation came from the case
g=6
and
n=1
, and the
Kodaira dimension
\kappa(X_{6}^{1})
. As we have seen,
X_{g}^{1}
is
unirational for
g<6
. On the other hand, for
g\geq 7
we have

\kappa(X_{g})=\kappa(\mathcal{A}_{g})={\frac{1}{2}}g(g+1)
, since Iitaka’s conjecture
holds, cf.
[
11
]
, and
\mathcal{A}_{g}
is of general type.

Proposition 1.4

There exist a
\Gamma
-admissible collection
\Sigma^{\flat}
of fans

\Sigma^{\flat}(g^{\prime})
, for

-admissible relative

(iv)

\sigma\times\{0\}\in\widetilde{\Sigma}^{\flat}(g^{\prime})

Remark.
The uses of the word “admissible” in the
preamble to Proposition
1.4
and in condition (ii)
there are different.
\Gamma
-admissible refers to the property of a
collections of fans, one for each cusp, being compatible under
restriction (see
[
3
]
), whereas
\mathop{\mathrm{GL}}\nolimits(\mathbb{X})\ltimes\mathbb{X}^{n}
-admissible is a property of compatibility at each cusp
separately with the the projection map
X_{g}^{n}\to\mathcal{A}_{g}
, defined
in
[
7

.

Proof.
We take
\Sigma^{\flat}(g^{\prime})
to be defined by
the perfect cone decomposition of
C(\mathbb{X})
. This is the same as taking
the the cones of
\Sigma^{\flat}(g^{\prime})
to be the cones on the faces of
the convex hull of the rank-
1
forms in the closure of
C
with
rational radical, by
[
4

. It is known to give an admissible
decomposition and a polyhedral fundamental domain for the action of

\mathop{\mathrm{GL}}\nolimits(\mathbb{X})

[
17

and the references
there.

We can extend this to a decomposition of
\widetilde{C}(\mathbb{X})
by taking the

with
\mathop{\mathrm{rank}}\nolimits b=1
. From the description of the action of

\mathop{\mathrm{GL}}\nolimits(\mathbb{X})\ltimes\mathbb{X}^{n}
, given for example in
[
7
, VI.1.1]
, it
follows immediately that
\mathop{\mathrm{GL}}\nolimits(\mathbb{X})\ltimes\mathbb{X}^{n}
acts on
\widetilde{C}(\mathbb{X})
with a polyhedral fundamental domain.

If
q=(b;\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{n})=(b;\ell_{j})\in\widetilde{C}(\mathbb{X})
then

so we can write

, where

r_{i}=\xi_{i}{}^{t}\xi_{i}
is of rank
1
,
\xi_{i}\in\mathbb{X}
, and
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Since
\ell_{j}|_{\mathop{\mathrm{rad}}\nolimits b}
we may write

\ell_{j}=b(\mu_{j},\bullet)
with
\mu_{j}\in\mathop{\mathrm{rad}}\nolimits b
(notice that

are linearly

q=(b;\ell_{j})=(b;b(\mu_{j},\bullet))=(b;\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}r_{i}(\mu_{j},\bullet))=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}(r_{i};r_{i}(\mu_{j},\bullet)),

so that the cone over the convex hull of the integral
q
with

quadratic part

\widetilde{C}(\mathbb{X})
, and every

\widetilde{C}(\mathbb{X})
is in the convex hull.

The first of these conditions shows that the definition of

\widetilde{\Sigma}^{\flat}
does give an admissible collection of fans. That
is, it is
\widetilde{\Gamma}^{n}
-invariant, and chosen for different cusps

so as to be compatible with restriction (Siegel

\Phi
-operator) to adjacent cusps. This therefore yields a

\overline{X_{g}^{n}}^{\flat}
.

The second condition shows that the covering spaces

\mathop{\mathrm{Temb}}\nolimits(\widetilde{\Sigma}^{\flat}(g^{\prime}))
have canonical singularities.

Finally,
\overline{X_{g}^{n}}^{\flat}
is a Namikawa compactification because
there are no rays of
\widetilde{\Sigma}^{\flat}(g^{\prime})
in the interior of

\widetilde{C}(\mathbb{X})
, and the ray spanned by

projects onto
the ray spanned by

. ∎

Corollary 1.5

2

In this section we shall verify the conditions of Corollary

for the values of

Recall that any toroidal compactification
\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
of

with a surjective map to the Satake compactification

where
\mathcal{A}_{0}
is a point. Hence any compactification of

that dominates a Namikawa compactification has a map to

\mathcal{A}_{g}^{*}
and in particular there is a natural map
\pi\colon\overline{X_{g}^{n}}^{\sharp}\to\mathcal{A}_{g}^{*}
.

If

is such that
\pi(p)\in\mathcal{A}_{g^{\prime}}\subset\mathcal{A}_{g}^{*}
, then near
p
the toroidal compactification is a quotient of
an invariant open subset

\mathcal{D}^{\sharp}\subset\mathbb{H}_{g^{\prime}}\times\mathbb{C}^{g^{\prime}g^{\prime\prime}}\times\mathbb{C}^{g^{\prime}n}\times\mathop{\mathrm{Temb}}\nolimits(\widetilde{\Sigma}^{\sharp})

(4)

(where
g^{\prime}+g^{\prime\prime}=g
) by an action of
\widetilde{P}(g^{\prime})\cap\widetilde{\Gamma}^{n}

that preserves the product structure, extending the
decomposition (
3
). To determine the singularity at

p\in\overline{X_{g}^{n}}^{\sharp}
we therefore have to examine the action of
the stabiliser
G
of a preimage
\tilde{p}\in\mathcal{D}^{\sharp}
of
p
on
the tangent space
T_{\mathcal{D}^{\sharp},\tilde{p}}
.

We recall some basic facts from
[
21
]
and
[
18
]
. Suppose
that
\rho\colon G\to\mathop{\mathrm{GL}}\nolimits(m,\mathbb{C})
is a finite-dimensional
representation of a finite group
G
, and suppose that
h\in G

and that
\rho(h)
has order
k>1
. If the eigenvalues of

\rho(h)
are
\zeta^{a_{1}},\ldots,\zeta^{a_{m}}
(where

\zeta=e^{2\pi i/k}
is a primitive
k
-th root of unity and
0\leq a_{i}<k
) then the
Reid-Tai sum
of
h
, also called the

age
of
h
, is

\mathop{\mathrm{RT}}\nolimits(h)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{a_{i}}{k}.

The RST (Reid–Shepherd-Barron–Tai) criterion states that if

\rho(G)
has no quasireflections then the quotient
\mathbb{C}^{m}/\rho(G)

has canonical singularities if and only if
\mathop{\mathrm{RT}}\nolimits(h)\geq 1
for every

h\in G
.

If
\rho(h)
is a quasireflections then exactly one of the
a_{i}
is
non-zero, so
\mathop{\mathrm{RT}}\nolimits(h)<1
. It follows that in any case if
\mathop{\mathrm{RT}}\nolimits(h)\geq 1

for every
h\in G
then the quotient has canonical singularities.

We apply this to the action of
\tilde{\gamma}\in\widetilde{P}(g^{\prime})
. We use
the block decomposition given in (
1
)
and (
2
) and the notation for submatrices in the rest
of this section is taken from there.

To check that the singularity at
p
is canonical it is enough to
verify that
G
contains no quasireflection on the tangent space
(which we need to do anyway in order to apply
Lemma
1.3
) and that
\mathop{\mathrm{RT}}\nolimits(\widetilde{\gamma})\geq 1
for
any nontrivial
\widetilde{\gamma}\in G
.

Note that the decomposition (
4
) is
G
-invariant,
so that
\mathop{\mathrm{RT}}\nolimits(\widetilde{\gamma})
is the sum of the age of
\widetilde{\gamma}

restricted to each factor.

Proposition 2.1

If
g^{\prime\prime}=0

\overline{X_{g}^{n}}

and

Proof.
Recall that we are assuming

. But

, and there are no quasireflections by

and

. ∎

In view of the above, we may assume for the rest of this section that

In order to use the results of

Lemma 2.2

If
\tilde{\gamma}
fixes a cusp then it acts trivially on each cone of

\widetilde{\Sigma}^{\sharp}(g^{\prime})
.

Proof.
The eigenvectors in
\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}\nolimits^{2}V
are the
rank
1
forms
f_{i}f_{j}
, where

(if any) and thus there are no eigenvectors in the

. Now the result follows: for if

acts nontrivially on

of the rays

Proposition 2.3

Suppose that
p\in\overline{X^{n}_{g}}
and
\pi(p)\in\mathcal{A}_{g^{\prime}}
, and that

\widetilde{\gamma}\neq{\mathbf{1}}_{n+2g}

\widetilde{P}(g^{\prime})\cap\widetilde{\Gamma}^{n}

(g^{\prime\prime},n)\neq(1,1)

\widetilde{\gamma}
on

\mathop{\mathrm{RT}}\nolimits(\widetilde{\gamma})\geq 1

Proof.
For this, it is enough to look at the

\mathop{\mathrm{Temb}}\nolimits(\widetilde{\Sigma}^{\sharp})
factor. This is a toric variety with

whose lattice of 1-parameter subgroups (the dual of the

If the eigenvalues of
\tilde{\gamma}
on

\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{g^{\prime\prime}}
then the

on the toric boundary

and, as in

that

on the

unless

If

. ∎

Next, we examine the action of
\tilde{\gamma}
on the

For any

, we let

multiplication. Since

is identified with

by the choice of basis

\begin{bmatrix}Z\\
\tau\\
{\mathbf{1}}_{g}\end{bmatrix}\in M_{(n+2g)\times g}(\mathbb{C})/\mathop{\mathrm{GL}}\nolimits(g,\mathbb{C}).

of

in

in

{}^{t}\omega&\tau^{\prime\prime}\end{pmatrix}

\tau\\

Lemma 2.4

The eigenvalues of
\tilde{\gamma}
on

are exactly the

1

. Moreover,
\gamma^{\prime}

\tau^{\prime}
, i.e.
(A\tau^{\prime}+B)(C\tau^{\prime}+D)^{-1}=\tau^{\prime}

Immediate from the discussion
above. ∎

The next lemma and its corollaries apply to the usual action of

\mathop{\mathrm{Sp}}\nolimits(2g,\mathbb{Z})
on
\mathbb{H}_{g}
, for arbitrary

\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}A_{\gamma}&B_{\gamma}\\

Lemma 2.5

If
\gamma
fixes
\tau\in\mathbb{H}_{g}
, then

\gamma
is diagonalisable. Moreover
C_{\gamma}\tau+D_{\gamma}
is
diagonalisable and has
\bar{\Lambda}
as sequence of eigenvalues.

Proof.
For every
\beta\in\mathop{\mathrm{Sp}}\nolimits(2g,\mathbb{R})
and
\tau\in\mathbb{H}_{g}
we set

. Then

is a
cocycle, i.e.
J(\beta_{1}\beta_{2},\tau)=J(\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\tau)J(\beta_{2},\tau)
for every

\beta_{1},\,\beta_{2}\in\mathop{\mathrm{Sp}}\nolimits(2g,\mathbb{R})
and
\tau\in\mathbb{H}_{g}
.

It is well known, cf.
[
21
, Lemma 4.1]
, that there exists

\alpha\in\mathop{\mathrm{Sp}}\nolimits(2g,\mathbb{R})
such that

\alpha\tau=i{\mathbf{1}}_{g}\hskip 20.00003pt\text{and}\hskip 20.00003pt\alpha\gamma\alpha^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}\delta_{1}&\delta_{2}\\
-\delta_{2}&\delta_{1}\end{pmatrix}

with
\delta_{1}
,
\delta_{2}
real diagonal matrices and

\delta_{1}+i\delta_{2}\in\mathop{\hbox{}\mathrm{U}}\nolimits(g,\mathbb{C})
.

Obviously
\gamma
is diagonalisable, with eigenvalues
\Lambda=\delta_{1}+i\delta_{2}
and
\overline{\Lambda}=\delta_{1}-i\delta_{2}
. Now using the
cocycle property we have

\displaystyle\overline{\Lambda}

\displaystyle=J(\alpha\gamma\alpha^{-1},i{\mathbf{1}}_{g})

\displaystyle=J(\alpha,\gamma\alpha^{-1},i{\mathbf{1}}_{g})J(\gamma\alpha^{-1},i{\mathbf{1}}_{g})

\displaystyle=J(\alpha,\gamma\cdot\tau)J(\gamma,\tau)J(\alpha^{-1},i{\mathbf{1}}_{g})

Now, since
\gamma
fixes
\tau
and
J(\alpha^{-1},i{\mathbf{1}}_{g})=J(\alpha,\tau)^{-1}

\overline{\Lambda}
and

are conjugate. ∎

Corollary 2.6

.

\mathbb{C}^{g^{\prime}g^{\prime\prime}}
factor and, by Corollary
2.6
, a further

n
copies of
\epsilon\lambda_{i}^{\pm 1}
on the
\mathbb{C}^{ng}
factor. If
the order of
\tilde{\gamma}
on the tangent space at
\tilde{p}
is
d
,
then some
\lambda_{i}
is a nontrivial
d
-th root of unity and so this
gives a contribution of at least
(n+g^{\prime\prime})\frac{1}{d}
to
\mathop{\mathrm{RT}}\nolimits(\tilde{\gamma})
.

Moreover, according to
[
21
, Lemma 4.4]
we may assume
d\leq 6
,
and in each case the action of
\gamma^{\prime}
contributes at least

\frac{g^{\prime}}{d}
to
\mathop{\mathrm{RT}}\nolimits(\tilde{\gamma})
, so in any case we have

\mathop{\mathrm{RT}}\nolimits(\tilde{\gamma})\geq(g^{\prime}+n+g^{\prime\prime})\frac{1}{d}\geq\frac{n+g}{6}
. So if
n+g\geq 6
we are done. ∎

The condition
n+g\geq 6
cannot be strengthened: it is needed if

g^{\prime}=1
and
d=6
.

Theorem
1.2
now follows immediately.

3
Slope of
\mathcal{A}_{g}

We assume throughout that
g>1
, since if
g=1
the fibres of
X_{1}^{n}

are rational so
\kappa(X_{1}^{n})=-\infty
.

We shall construct differential forms by using Siegel modular forms, so
we begin with some elementary definitions concerning them.

Definition 3.1

A
modular form of weight
k
is a
holomorphic function
f\colon\mathbb{H}_{g}\to\mathbb{C}
on the Siegel upper
half-plane

\mathbb{H}_{g}=\{Z\in M_{g\times g}(\mathbb{C})\mid Z={}^{t}Z,\ \mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits Z>0\}

such that

f(\gamma\cdot\tau)=\det(C\tau+D)^{k}f(\tau)\text{ for any }\gamma\in\mathop{\mathrm{Sp}}\nolimits(2g,\mathbb{Z}).

Note that we need no extra condition at infinity when
g>1
.

A Siegel modular form has a Fourier expansion

f(\tau)=\sum_{T}a(T)\exp(\pi i\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits(T\tau))

where the sum runs over all even integral symmetric matrices
T
.

Definition 3.2

If
f
is a Siegel modular form, the
vanishing of
f
at the boundary
is

b:={\frac{1}{2}}\min\{x^{t}Tx\mid a(T)\neq 0,\ x\in\mathbb{Z}^{g}\smallsetminus\{0\}\}.

If
b>0
, i.e. if
a(T)\neq 0
implies
T>0
, we say that
f
is a
cusp form
.

We recall, mainly from
[
14
]
, some facts about

\mathcal{A}_{g}=\mathop{\mathrm{Sp}}\nolimits(2g,\mathbb{Z})\backslash\mathbb{H}_{g}
and its compactifications.

Modular forms of weight
1
determine a
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundle
L
, the
Hodge line bundle. The Satake compactification
\mathcal{A}_{g}^{*}
is Proj of
the ring of modular forms, and the Mumford partial compactification

\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{g}
is the blow-up of
\mathcal{A}_{g}\sqcup\mathcal{A}_{g-1}\subset\mathcal{A}_{g}^{*}
along

\mathcal{A}_{g-1}
. Every toroidal compactification of
\mathcal{A}_{g}
dominates

\mathcal{A}_{g}^{*}
and contains
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{g}
as a Zariski open subset: the toroidal
compactifications differ from one another only above the deeper strata

\mathcal{A}_{g^{\prime}}
for
g^{\prime}<g-1
.

If
g\geq 2
then
\mathop{\mathrm{Pic}}\nolimits(\mathcal{A}_{g}^{\prime})\otimes\mathbb{Q}=\mathbb{Q}\lambda\oplus\mathbb{Q}\delta
,
where
\lambda
is the class of
L
and
\delta
is the class of the
boundary divisor
\Delta_{\mathcal{A}^{\prime}}
, the proper transform of

\mathcal{A}_{g-1}\subset\mathcal{A}^{*}_{g}
. This is proved
in
[
14
, Corollary 1.6]
for
g\geq 4
, and for
g=2
and

g=3
it follows from the studies of the Chow ring in
[
15
]

and
[
6
]
respectively: see
[
10
]
.

Definition 3.3

The
slope
of an effective divisor
E=a\lambda-b\delta
on

\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{g}
with
a,\,b>0
is defined to be
s(E)=a/b
. In particular,
if
E
is the zero divisor of a cusp form
f
of weight
k
and
vanishing order
b
then
s(E)=k/b
.

The Kodaira dimension of the Kuga varieties
X_{g}^{n}
is related to
the transcendence degree of the field generated by cusp forms of slope
less or equal to
g+n+1
: see for example
[
13
, Theorem 1.3]
.

Definition 3.4

The
minimal slope

s_{\min}(g)
is the infimum of the slopes of
all effective divisors on
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{g}
.

An upper bound
s_{\min}(g)
is provided in small genera by the
Andreotti-Mayer divisor
N_{0}
, the locus of principally polarised
abelian varieties with singular theta divisor
[
1
]
. The divisor

N_{0}
has two components:
\Theta_{\mathop{\mathrm{null}}\nolimits}
, the locus where the theta
divisor has a singular point of order
2
, and
N^{\prime}_{0}
, the locus where
the theta divisor has a singular point not of order
2
. The classes
of
\Theta_{\mathop{\mathrm{null}}\nolimits}
, for
g\geq 1
, and
N^{\prime}_{0}
, for
g\geq 4
, are
computed in
[
14
]
:

\displaystyle[\Theta_{\mathop{\mathrm{null}}\nolimits}]

\displaystyle=2^{g-2}(2^{g}+1)\lambda-2^{2g-5}\delta.

\displaystyle[N^{\prime}_{0}]

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{(g+1)!}{4}+\frac{g!}{2}-2^{g-3}(2^{g}+1)\right)\lambda-\left(\frac{(g+1)!}{24}-2^{2g-6}\right)\delta.

For
g\leq 3
the minimal slope is achieved at
[\Theta_{\mathop{\mathrm{null}}\nolimits}]
,
giving the values
s_{\min}(1)=12
,
s_{\min}(2)=10
and
s_{\min}(3)=9
.

For
g=4
we have
s_{\min}(4)=8
, achieved by
s([N_{0}^{\prime}])
, and for

g=5
we have
s_{\min}(5)=54/7
, also achieved by
s([N^{\prime}_{0}])
:
see
[
19
]
and
[
8
]
, respectively.

In all these cases the divisors that minimise the slope are rigid.

For
g=6
we have
s_{\min}(6)\leq 7
: see
[
5
]
. However, the fact
that
s_{\min}(6)\leq s([N^{\prime}_{0}])=550/73<8
will suffice for our purposes.

Theorem 3.5

Suppose that
g\geq 2
and
\overline{X_{g}^{n}}
is a Namikawa compactification of
X_{g}^{n}

with canonical singularities. Then

1.

\kappa(X_{g}^{n})={\frac{1}{2}}g(g+1)
if
s_{\min}(g)<g+n+1
;

2.

\kappa(X_{g}^{n})=0
if
s_{\min}(g)=s(D)=g+n+1
and
D
is rigid;

3.

\kappa(X_{g}^{n})=-\infty
if
s_{\min}(g)>g+n+1
(even if the
singularities are not canonical).

Proof:
The first case (what one might call relatively
general type) follows easily from
[
13
, Proposition 9.2]
. Pulling
back via the morphism
f\colon\overline{X_{g}^{n}}^{\sharp}\to\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
, this
implies that for sufficiently divisible
k
one has
kK_{X}\geq f^{*}(k(g+n+1)L-k\Delta_{\mathcal{A}})
. So it is enough to show that the

\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
(g+n+1)L-\Delta_{\mathcal{A}}
is big: however, since it has
slope strictly greater than
s_{\min}(g)
it is in the interior of the
effective cone and can therefore be written as the sum of an effective
divisor and an ample divisor.

In the second case, the same argument shows that
K_{X}
is effective,
since it dominates the pullback of the effective divisor

(g+n+1)L-\Delta_{\mathcal{A}}
. Therefore
\kappa(X_{g}^{n})\geq 0
. On the other
hand, if some multiple of
K_{X}
moves, then so does some multiple of

(g+n+1)L-\Delta_{\mathcal{A}}
, which is to say that some multiple of
D

moves, but
D
is rigid.

In the third case, if
K_{X}\geq 0
then
f_{*}(K_{X})\geq 0
, but

s(f_{*}(K_{X}))=g+n+1<s_{\min}(g)
. So
K_{X}
is not effective, and

\kappa(X_{g}^{n})=-\infty
. ∎

Theorem
1
follows immediately from this.

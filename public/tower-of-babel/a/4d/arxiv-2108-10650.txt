Abstract
The irreducible representations ϕ n 1 \phi_{n}^{1} and ϕ n 2 \phi_{n}^{2} of the symplectic group G n = S ​ p 2 ​ n ​ ( P ) G_{n}=Sp_{2n}(P) over an algebraically closed field P P of characteristic p > 2 p>2 with highest weights ω n − 1 + p − 3 2 ​ ω n \omega_{n-1}+\frac{p-3}{2}\omega_{n} and p − 1 2 ​ ω n \frac{p-1}{2}\omega_{n} , respectively, are investigated. It is proved that the dimension of ϕ n i \phi_{n}^{i} ( i = 1 , 2 i=1,2 ) is equal to ( p n + ( − 1 ) i ) / 2 (p^{n}+(-1)^{i})/2 , all weight multiplicities of these representations are equal to 1, their restrictions to the group G k G_{k} naturally embedded into G n G_{n} are completely reducible with irreducible constituents ϕ k 1 \phi_{k}^{1} and ϕ k 2 \phi_{k}^{2} , and their restrictions to S ​ p 2 ​ n ​ ( p ) Sp_{2n}(p) can be obtained as the result of the reduction modulo p p of certain complex irreducible representations of the group S ​ p 2 ​ n ​ ( p ) Sp_{2n}(p) . These results allow us to obtain the exact list of rational irreducible representations of simple algebraic groups over fields of positive characteristics all whose weight subspaces have dimension 1. This generalizes a result of Seitz.



In this paper we consider the irreducible

(C)

by
[
9
, Theorem 6.1]
. For
p=2
,
we have
B_{n}(P)=C_{n}(P)

and
\phi(\omega_{n})\in I(B_{n}(P))
since
\omega_{n}
is a miniscule
weight of
B_{n}(P)
. Therefore,
\phi(\omega_{n})\in I^{\prime}_{0}(C_{n}(P))
.

Due to the remark prior to Corollary 2, it remains to
consider the case where
\phi=\phi(\omega_{1})\otimes\phi(\omega_{n})
.
Note that
\omega_{n}-\alpha_{n}\in X(\phi(\omega_{n}))
, and
\pm\frac{\alpha_{n}}{2}\in X(\phi(\omega_{1}))
. So

\omega_{n}-\frac{\alpha_{n}}{2}=(\omega_{n}-\alpha_{n})+\frac{\alpha_{n}}{2}\in X(\phi)

is of multiplicity
\geq 2
, that is,
\phi\notin I(G)
. This
completes the proof.

It is well known that
I(G)=
Irr
\,G
if
G=A_{1}(P)
.

Proposition 2

Let
G\neq A_{1}(P)
be a simply connected simple algebraic
group over an algebraically closed field
P
of characteristic

p>0
. Define the set of weights
\Omega=\Omega(G)
as follows:

\Omega(A_{n}(P))=\{\omega_{i},a\omega_{1},b\omega_{n},c\omega_{j}+(p-1-c)\omega_{j+1}~(1\leq i\leq n,~1\leq j<n,~0\leq a,b,c<p\};

\Omega(C_{n}(P))=\{\omega_{n}~{\rm for}~n=2,~~~~~\omega_{1},\omega_{n-1}+\frac{p-3}{2}\omega_{n},\frac{p-1}{2}\omega_{n}\}~{\rm for}~p>2

~{\rm and}~~~~\{\omega_{1},\omega_{n}\}~{\rm for}~p=2;

\Omega(B_{n}(P))=\{\omega_{1},\omega_{n}\}~~{\rm for}~~n>3,p>2;~~~~~\Omega(D_{n}(P))=\{\omega_{1},\omega_{n-1},\omega_{n}\};

\Omega(E_{6}(P))=\{\omega_{1},\omega_{6}\};~~~~~\Omega(E_{7}(P))=\{\omega_{7}\};

\Omega(F_{4}(P))=\{\omega_{4}\}~{\rm for}~p=3~{\rm and}~\emptyset~{\rm for}~p\neq 3;

\Omega(G_{2}(P))=\{\omega_{1}\}~{\rm for}~p\neq 3~{\rm and}~\{\omega_{1},\omega_{2}\}~{\rm for}~p=3.

Let
\phi
be an irreducible
rational representation of
G
with highest weight

\omega=\sum_{i=0}^{k}p^{i}\lambda_{i}
where
\lambda_{i}
are the highest
weights of representations from
Inf
(G)
. The multiplicities
of all weights of
\phi
are equal to
1
if
and only if
\lambda_{l}=0
or
\lambda_{l}\in\Omega(G)
for
0\leq l\leq k
, and
\lambda_{l+1}\neq\omega_{1}
provided
G=C_{n}(P)
,
p=2
,
\lambda_{l}=\omega_{n}
, or
G=G_{2}(P)
,
p=2
,
\lambda_{l}=\omega_{1}
, or

G=G_{2}(P)
,
p=3
,
\lambda_{l}=\omega_{2}
.

Proof
.
First, let
\phi\in{\rm Inf\,}G
and
\omega\neq 0
.
The case where
G=C_{n}(P)
has been considered in Corollary 2. Let
G\neq C_{n}(P)
and
G\neq G_{2}(P)
if
p=3
. Then
\omega\in\Omega

for
\phi\in I_{0}(G)
by
[
9
, Theorem 6.l]
and the observation
prior to Corollary 2, and it suffices to consider the cases where

\omega
is not a miniscule weight. It is well known that
\phi\in I_{0}(G)
if
G=B_{n}(P)
or
G_{2}(P)
and
\omega=\omega_{1}
(this is
also true for
p=3
.) For
p=3
and
G=F_{4}(P)
, Wong

[
18
, p.4]
has shown that
\phi(\omega_{n})\in I_{0}(G)
. It is
proved in
[
16
]
that
\phi\in I_{0}(G)
if
G=A_{n}(P)
and

\omega=c\omega_{i}+(p-1-c)\omega_{i+1}

(1\leq i\leq n)
(
[
16
, Theorem
3]
) or
\omega=a\omega_{1}
or
b\omega_{n}
(
[
16
, Remark 1]
). In the
latter case this is also mentioned in
[
9
, 1.14]
.

Now let
G=G_{2}(P)
for
p=3
. By
[
9
, Theorem 6.1]
,

\omega=\{\omega_{1},\omega_{2}\}
for
\phi\in I_{0}^{\prime}(G)
. For
p=3
, the representation
\phi(\omega_{2})
belongs to
I_{0}^{\prime}(G)
since it can be obtained from

\phi(\omega_{1})\in I_{0}^{\prime}(G)
by twisting it with an automorphism of

G
(see
[
14
, Corollary 11.2]
). Due to the remarks prior to
Corollary 2, it remains to consider the representation

\phi=\phi(\omega_{1})\otimes\phi(\omega_{2})
. Since
\omega_{1}-\alpha_{1},\omega_{1}-\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}\in X(\phi(\omega_{1}))
and
\omega_{2},\omega_{2}-\alpha_{2}\in X(\phi(\omega_{2}))
, the weight
\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}-\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2}\in X(\phi)

has multiplicity at least 2, i.e.
\phi\notin{\rm Irr\,}G
.

Now let
\phi\in
Irr
\,G
. By
[
13
, Theorem 41]
,

\phi=\otimes_{l=0}^{k}Fr^{l}\circ\phi(\lambda_{l})
where
Fr
is the
Frobenius morphism associated with raising elements of
P
to the
p
th power. If
\phi\in I(G)
, then it is obvious that

\phi(\lambda_{l})\in I_{0}(G)
; it follows from the above that every
weight
\lambda_{l}\in\Omega\cup\{0\}
.

Let
\rho\in I_{0}(G)
,
\psi\in I(G)
. It is clear that the
representation
\rho\otimes Fr\circ\psi\notin I(G)
if and only
if

\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}=p(\mu_{1}^{\prime}-\mu_{2}^{\prime})\neq 0

(2)

for some weights
\mu_{i}\in X(\rho)
,
\mu_{i}^{\prime}\in X(\psi),

i=l,2
. Set
\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}=\mu
and S
\omega^{\prime}=\omega(\rho)
.
Acting by the Weyl group, we make
\mu
a dominant weight. Observe
that
\mu
and
\mu_{1}^{\prime}-\mu_{2}^{\prime}
are radical weights. Let
\alpha
be
the maximal root of
G
,
a(\lambda)=\langle\lambda,\alpha\rangle
for

\lambda\in X(G)
, and
a=a(\rho)=a(\omega^{\prime})
. As it is noted in
[
17
, Lemma
11]
,
a(\lambda)\leq a
for
\lambda\in X(\rho)
. In order to find

a
, one may apply
formulae from
[
17
, Introduction]

or proceed by direct calculations
using the root tables from
[
2
]
. Note that
a(\lambda)
is
an integer valued linear function on
X(G)
(see
[
13
, §3]
);
a(\lambda)\neq 0
for
\lambda\in X^{+}(G)\setminus\{0\}
. Let

\rho^{*}
be the representation dual to
\rho
. By
[
13
, Lemma
73]
,
\omega(\rho^{*})=-w_{0}\omega^{\prime}
where
w_{0}
is the element of the
Weyl group sending all positive roots to the negative ones.
It is clear
that
-w_{0}\alpha=\alpha
. So
a(\rho^{*})=a
,
a(\lambda)\geq-a
for

\lambda\in X(\rho)
since
-\lambda\in X(\rho^{*})
, and
a(\mu)\leq 2a.

We shall consider all representations
\rho
with
\omega^{\prime}\in\Omega

and find
out when (2) holds. If
G=A_{n}(P)
or
C_{n}(P)
, then

a=\sum_{i=1}a_{i}
for
\omega^{\prime}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
.

If
G=A_{n}(P)
,
then
a(\rho)\leq p-1
for
\omega^{\prime}\in\Omega
. In this situation
(2) would imply that
a(\mu)=p
,
\mu^{\prime}_{1}-\mu_{2}^{\prime}=\omega_{i}
which is
false since
\omega_{i}
is not a radical weight.

Let
G=C_{n}(P)
and
n>2
. For
p>2
, (2) cannot hold since

a(\rho)\leq(p-1)/2
for
\omega^{\prime}\in\Omega
and
a(\mu)\leq p-1
. If

p=2
, then the weight
\mu
takes each value
2\omega_{i}
,
i\leq n

for
\omega^{\prime}=\omega_{n}
and
\mu=2\omega_{1}
or
\omega_{2}
for
\omega^{\prime}=\omega_{1}
. (This can be easily verified taking into account that

C_{n}(P)\cong B_{n}(P)
and that
\omega_{n}
a miniscule weight of
B_{n}(P)
.) Therefore, (2) is equivalent to the fact that
\omega^{\prime}=\omega_{n}
,
\psi=\phi(\omega_{1})\otimes Fr\circ\psi^{\prime}
where
\psi^{\prime}\in{\rm Irr\,}G
.

Let

G=G_{2}(P)
. Then one can directly verify that

\mu\in\{\omega_{1},\omega_{2},2\omega_{1}\}
for
\omega^{\prime}=\omega_{1}
and

\mu\in\{\omega_{2},2\omega_{2},3\omega_{1}\}
for
\omega^{\prime}=\omega_{2}
,
p=3
; all
these options are realized. It is clear that (2) holds in the following
cases only:
p=2
,
~\omega^{\prime}=\omega_{1}
,
~\psi=\rho\otimes Fr\circ\psi^{\prime}
and
p=3
,
~\omega^{\prime}=\omega_{2}
,
~\psi=\phi(\omega_{1})\otimes Fr\circ\psi^{\prime}
,
~\psi^{\prime}\in{\rm Irr\,}G
.

Let
G\neq C_{n}(P)
,
A_{n}(P)
, or
G_{2}(P)
. In this situation

a(\rho)=1
for
\omega^{\prime}\in\Omega
. So
a(\mu)\leq 2
. Since for

G=D_{n}(P)
,
E_{6}(P)
or
E_{7}(P)
, the weight
\lambda\in X^{+}(G)
is
not radical if
a(\lambda)=1
, and
p>2
for
G=B_{n}(P)
or

F_{4}(P)
, the condition (2) does not hold.

Taking into account that representations
\psi
and
Fr^{j}\circ\psi
from
{\rm Irr\,}G
simultaneously belong or do not belong to

I(G)
, we complete the proof by induction on
k
.

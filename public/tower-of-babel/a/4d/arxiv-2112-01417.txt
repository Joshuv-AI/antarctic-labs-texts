Shifted symplectic higher Lie groupoids and classifying spaces
Miquel Cueca, Chenchang Zhu

Abstract
. We introduce the concept of m m -shifted symplectic Lie n n -groupoids and symplectic Morita equivalences between them. We then build various models for the 2-shifted symplectic structure on the classifying stack in this setting and construct explicit symplectic Morita equivalences between them.

Remark 2.18

(Cotangent complex)

A way to reformulate the non-degeneracy is by introducing the

&\circ(Z_{\bullet},\beta_{\bullet})\xleftarrow{\id_{\bullet}}(Z_{\bullet},0)\xrightarrow{g_{\bullet}}(J_{\bullet},\beta_{\bullet}).\end{split}

Notice that in
[
102
]
, Xu has given a version of Morita equivalence between 1-shifted symplectic Lie 1-groupoids (see Example

) via Hamiltonian bimodules. The definition is as follows.

Definition 2.36

(See Def 3.1, 3.13 and 4.1 in
[
102
]
)
.

Two
1
-shifted symplectic Lie
1
-groupoids
(K_{\bullet},\alpha_{\bullet})
and
(J_{\bullet},\beta_{\bullet})
are symplectic Morita equivalent if there exists a principal bibundle
P
and
\phi\in\Omega^{2}(P)
that makes

into a Hamiltonian space for
K_{\bullet}
and
J_{\bullet}
.

To closed this section, we prove that our Morita equivalence coincides with the one defined by Xu in
[
102
]
when

.

Theorem 2.37
.

The two possible definitions of symplectic Morita equivalence for
1
-shifted symplectic Lie
1
-groupoids define the same equivalence relation.

Proof.

Suppose that
(K_{\bullet},\alpha_{\bullet})
and
(J_{\bullet},\beta_{\bullet})
are symplectic Morita equivalent according to Definition

. Then Proposition 3.14 in
[
102
]
shows that they are symplectic Morita equivalent according to Definition
2.31
.

For the converse statement, we use the decomposition (
2.13
). It is enough to show that the type of strict morphisms and that of gauge transformations both give rise to symplectic Morita equivalences defined in Definition
2.36
. The type of strict morphisms follows from
[
102
, Proposition 4.8]
. The type of gauge transformations follows from
[
102
, Proposition 4.6]
, because our gauge transformations for

coincide with gauge transformations of the second type therein.
∎

Remark 2.38
.

Symplectic Morita equivalence in Definition

provides a shifted Lagragian

). There is a zoo of natural examples of Hamiltonian principal bimodules in Poisson geometry. They all carry some important physical meaning (see

]
). The above theorem thus implies that each such Hamiltonian principal bimodule gives rise to a shifted Lagrangian stack (c.f. Remark

).

3.
Simplicial models of the classifying stack
\mathcal{B}G

Let
G
be a Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. We say that
(\mathfrak{g},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
is a
quadratic Lie algebra
if
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\to\mathbb{R}
is a symmetric and non-degenerate pairing satisfying

\langle[a,b],c\rangle+\langle b,[a,c]\rangle=0\qquad\forall a,b,c\in\mathfrak{g}.

When
\mathfrak{g}
is quadratic, it was proved in
[
77
]
that the classifying stack
\mathcal{B}G
carries a
2
-shifted symplectic structure.
In this section we present two different shifted symplectic Lie groupoids that encode
\mathcal{B}G
and its shifted symplectic structure. The first one, which we denote by
(NG_{\bullet},\Omega_{\bullet}),
is a
2
-shifted symplectic Lie
1
-group and it is a finite dimensional model. The second one, denoted here by
({\mathbb{G}}_{\bullet},\omega_{\bullet})
, is a
2
-shifted symplectic Lie
2
-group and it is infinite dimensional. In Theorem
3.15
we construct an explicit symplectic Morita equivalence between them.

3.1.
Finite dimensional model

Let
G
be a Lie group, by Example
2.5
we have that
NG_{\bullet}
is a Lie
1
-group. If
(\mathfrak{g},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
is a quadratic Lie algebra then we can define the following differential forms on
NG_{\bullet}
:

(3.1)

\Omega=\langle d_{2}^{*}\theta^{l},d_{0}^{*}\theta^{r}\rangle\in\Omega^{2}(NG_{2})\quad\text{and}\quad\Theta=\frac{1}{6}\langle\theta^{l},[\theta^{l},\theta^{l}]\rangle\in\Omega^{3}(NG_{1})

where
\theta^{l},\theta^{r}\in\Omega^{1}(G;\mathfrak{g})
are the left and right Maurer-Cartan
1
-forms on
G
.

The
2
-form
\Omega
appeared in the works of Brylinski
[
20
]
and Weinstein
[
101
]
and we therefore call
\Omega
the Brylinski-Weinstein
2
-form. More precisely, for
(v_{1},v_{2}),(w_{1},w_{2})\in T_{(g,h)}(G\times G)
we can write
\Omega
as

(3.2)

\Omega_{(g,h)}((v_{1},v_{2}),(w_{1},w_{2}))=\langle L_{g^{-1}}v_{1},R_{h^{-1}}w_{2}\rangle-\langle L_{g^{-1}}w_{1},R_{h^{-1}}v_{2}\rangle.

The
3
-form
\Theta
is known as the Cartan
3
-form (see
[
3
,
40
]
). For
u,v,w\in T_{g}G
we can express it as

\Theta_{g}(u,v,w)=\langle L_{g^{-1}}u,[L_{g^{-1}}v,L_{g^{-1}}w]\rangle.

Using the language of shifted symplectic groupoids, the results of
[
20
,
101
]
can be summarized in the following.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
G
be a Lie group with a quadratic Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
. The pair
(NG_{\bullet},\Omega_{\bullet})
is a
2
-shifted symplectic Lie
1
-group where
NG_{\bullet}=\cdots G\times G\begin{subarray}{c}\longrightarrow\\[-8.50006pt]
\longrightarrow\\[-8.50006pt]
\longrightarrow\end{subarray}G\rightrightarrows pt
is the nerve of the Lie group
G
and
\Omega_{\bullet}=\Omega-\Theta+0
is given by (
3.1
).

Proof.

We need to check that
\Omega_{\bullet}
is normalized, non-degenerate and closed. Clearly,
s^{*}_{0}\Theta=0
and

(s^{*}_{0}\Omega)_{g}(v,w)=\Omega_{(e,g)}((0_{e},v),(0_{e},w))=0,\quad\forall\ g\in G,\ v,w\in TG

where the zero was deduced by the explicit formula (
3.2
). The computation for
s_{1}
is analogous.

The tangent complex of
NG_{\bullet}
is concentrated in degree
1
and given by
\mathcal{T}_{1}NG=\mathfrak{g}
. Therefore the only non trivial homology group is
H_{1}(\mathcal{T}NG)=\mathfrak{g}
itself. By equation (
2.5
), we have

\lambda^{\Omega_{\bullet}}(v,w)=\Omega_{(e,e)}((v,0_{e}),(0_{e},w))-\Omega_{(e,e)}((0_{e},v),(w,0_{e}))=2\langle v,w\rangle,

which is non-degenerate due to the non-degeneracy of
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
.

In order to show that
\Omega_{\bullet}
is closed we recall that the Maurer-Cartan forms satisfy

\theta^{r}=\phi\theta^{l},\quad d\theta^{l}=-\frac{1}{2}[\theta^{l},\theta^{l}],\quad d\theta^{r}=\frac{1}{2}[\theta^{r},\theta^{r}]\quad\text{and}\quad d_{1}^{*}\theta^{l}=d_{0}^{*}\phi^{-1}d_{2}^{*}\theta^{l}+d_{0}^{*}\theta^{l},

where
\phi\in\Omega^{0}(G,\End(\mathfrak{g}))
is the adjoint representation. We then have that

d\Theta=\frac{1}{12}\langle[\theta^{l},\theta^{l}],[\theta^{l},\theta^{l}]\rangle=0

by
ad
-invariance of the pairing and the graded version of Jacobi identity, and

\delta\Theta=\frac{1}{6}\sum_{i=0}^{2}(-1)^{i}\langle d_{i}^{*}\theta^{l},[d_{i}^{*}\theta^{l},d_{i}^{*}\theta^{l}]\rangle=-\frac{1}{2}\langle d^{*}_{2}[\theta^{l},\theta^{l}],d_{0}^{*}\theta^{r}\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\langle d^{*}_{2}\theta^{l},d^{*}_{0}[\theta^{r},\theta^{r}]\rangle=d\Omega,

where we have used that the pairing is ad-invariant and finally

\delta\Omega=\sum_{i=0}^{3}(-1)^{i}\langle d^{*}_{i}d_{2}^{*}\theta^{l},d_{i}^{*}d_{0}^{*}\theta^{r}\rangle=0

by the simplicial identities.
∎

It is well known that the Morita equivalence class of the Lie 1-groupoid
NG_{\bullet}
gives rise to the differentiable stack
\mathcal{B}G
which plays the role of classifying space for a Lie group
G
. In fact, the geometric realisation of
NG_{\bullet}
is a topological classifying space
BG
of
G
(see e.g.
[
18
]
), and the stack
\mathcal{B}G
has analogous properties of
BG
in the world of stacks. Therefore the previous Theorem
3.1
gives a geometric model for a symplectic structure on the classifying stack
\mathcal{B}G
. We also point out that the de Rham simplicial double complex
(\Omega^{\bullet}(NG_{\bullet}),\delta,d)
calculates the singular cohomology of the topological classifying space
BG
(see e.g.
[
18
,
78
]
), that is

H^{k}(Tot(\Omega^{\bullet}(NG_{\bullet})),D)=H^{k}(BG,\mathbb{R}).

Proposition 3.2
.

If
G
is compact, connected, and simple, then
H^{4}(BG,\mathbb{R})=\mathbb{R}
, and the
4
-cocycle
\Omega_{\bullet}
represents a generator.

Proof.

It is a classical result that
H^{4}(BG,\mathbb{R})=H^{3}(G,\mathbb{R})=\mathbb{R}
if
G
is compact, connected, and simple (see e.g.
[
47
, Theorems 1.47, 1.81]
). The cocycle
\Omega_{\bullet}
is not exact because the Cartan
3
-form
\Theta
is not an exact 3-form on
G
. In fact,
\Theta
represents a generator of
H^{3}(G,\mathbb{R})=\mathbb{R}
in this case. Therefore
\Omega_{\bullet}
represents a generator.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

According to
[
51
]
, in his thesis
[
94
]
Shulman gives an approach to Chern-Weil
theory using the de Rham complex of
NG_{\bullet}
. These classes are explicitly calculated therein.

Another relevant property of the shifted symplectic structure
\Omega_{\bullet}
is that it provides an integration of the quadratic structure on the Lie algebra. More concretely, in
[
9
]
it was proved that the classical Van Est map relating Lie group cohomology and Lie algebra cohomology can be extended to a map of double complexes

(3.3)

VE:\big(\widehat{\Omega}^{q}(NG_{p}),\delta,d\big)\to\big(W^{p,q}\mathfrak{g},d^{h},d^{v}\big)

where
W^{p,q}\mathfrak{g}=\wedge^{q-p}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\otimes S^{q}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is a bi-graded version of the Weil algebra of
\mathfrak{g}
(see
[
9
]
for more details).

Remark 3.4
.

The Van Est map has a nice interpretation in terms of graded manifolds. Let us denote by
(\mathfrak{g}[1],Q)
the degree
1

Q
-manifold given by the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, where the function ring
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{g}[1]}^{i}=\wedge^{i}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
and
Q=d_{CE}
is the Chevalley-Eilenberg differential. Then the Weil algebra becomes the algebra of differential forms on
\mathfrak{g}[1]
. In fact, we have

W^{p,q}=\Omega^{q}(\mathfrak{g}[1])_{p},\quad d^{h}=\mathcal{L}_{Q},\quad d^{v}=d

where
\mathcal{L}_{Q}
is the Lie derivative with respect to the vector field
Q
and
d
is the de Rham differential on
\mathfrak{g}[1]
(see
[
58
,
73
]
for more details).

Proposition 3.5
.

We use the same notation in Theorem
3.1
. The pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is an element in
S^{2}\mathfrak{g}^{*}=W^{2,2}\mathfrak{g}
, and the Van Est map (
3.3
) satisfies

VE(\Omega_{\bullet})=-\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle.

Proof.

Since
\Theta\in\Omega^{3}(NG_{1})
we have that
VE(\Theta)=0
for dimensional reasons. Following
[
9
]
we see that if
v,w\in\mathfrak{g}
then
VE(\Omega)
is explicitly given by

VE(\Omega)(v,w)=-s^{*}_{0}i_{\widehat{v}}s_{0}^{*}i_{\widehat{w}}\Omega=-(s_{0}^{*}i_{\widehat{w}}\Omega)_{e}(v)=-\Omega_{(e,e)}((w,0),(0,v))=-\langle w,v\rangle,

where
\widehat{v}
and
\widehat{w}
denote the associted left invariant vector fields on
G
and
G^{\times 2}
respectively.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

As indicated in the introduction, the pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
on
\mathfrak{g}
can be interpreted as a degree
2
symplectic form in the graded manifold
\mathfrak{g}[1]
. Combining the previous Proposition
3.5
, and using the graded description of the Weil algebra (see Remark
3.4
), we obtain that
(\mathfrak{g}[1],Q=d_{CE},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
is a degree
2
symplectic
Q
-manifold. Therefore, we have proved that
(NG_{\bullet},\Omega_{\bullet})
is an integration for
(\mathfrak{g}[1],Q=d_{CE},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
.

We finish this section by answering the following question: Can we find a symplectic form
\Omega^{\prime}\in\Omega^{2}(NG_{2})
satisfying
VE(\Omega^{\prime})=\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and
\delta\Omega^{\prime}=0
?

If
\mathfrak{g}
is semisimple and
G
is a compact and connected integration, then it is impossible. Since
G
is compact then
G\times G
admits a symplectic structures if and only if
H^{2}(G\times G)\neq 0
. But by the compactness and connectedness, we have
H^{i}(G)=H^{i}_{CE}(\mathfrak{g})
. Moreover, since
\mathfrak{g}
is semisimple, Whitehead’s Lemma implies that
H^{1}_{CE}(\mathfrak{g})=H^{2}_{CE}(\mathfrak{g})=0
, therefore
H^{2}(G\times G)=0
. Thus there does not even exist a symplectic form
\Omega^{\prime}
on
G\times G=NG_{2}
. Nevertheless, on a natural infinite dimensional model of
\mathcal{B}G
, we do find a single 2-form to realise our desired 2-shifted symplectic 2-form.

3.2.
Infinite dimensional model

There is a natural infinite dimensional Lie
2
-group
{\mathbb{G}}_{\bullet}
for a given (finite dimensional) Lie group
G
, which is the 2-truncation of the universal integration of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. We refer to Appendices
A
,
B
for the technical aspects of infinite dimensional spaces and the details of the truncation procedure. Here we simply describe
{\mathbb{G}}_{\bullet}
in an explicit way. Recall from Example
2.6
that, in order to define a Lie
2
-group, it is enough to specify the first four levels and the faces and degeneracy between them. Let
G
be a Lie group. Then the spaces
{\mathbb{G}}_{k}
are given by

•

{\mathbb{G}}_{0}=pt,

•

{\mathbb{G}}_{1}=P_{e}G
, so
{\mathbb{G}}_{1}
consists of paths of a fixed Sobolev class
r
on
G
starting at
e
,

•

{\mathbb{G}}_{2}=\Omega G
, so elements of
{\mathbb{G}}_{2}
are based loops of Sobolev class
r
,

•

{\mathbb{G}}_{3}=\{(\tau_{0},\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\in{\mathbb{G}}^{\times 3}_{2}\ |\ \tau_{0}(t)=\tau_{1}(t),\ \tau_{1}(\frac{2}{3}+t)=\tau_{2}(\frac{2}{3}+t),\ \tau_{0}(1-t)=\tau_{2}(t)\quad t\in[0,\frac{1}{3}]\}
. Visually, elements of
{\mathbb{G}}_{3}
are empty tetrahedrons on
G
based at
e
.

A more compact notation for those spaces is

{\mathbb{G}}_{\bullet}=\cdots\ \Omega G\begin{subarray}{c}\longrightarrow\\[-8.50006pt]
\longrightarrow\\[-8.50006pt]
\longrightarrow\end{subarray}P_{e}G\rightrightarrows pt.

The face maps
d_{i}:\Omega G\to P_{e}G
for
i=0,1,2
are defined as

(3.4)

\displaystyle d_{0}(\tau)(t)=\tau(\frac{t}{3}),\quad d_{1}(\tau)(t)=\tau(1-\frac{t}{3}),\quad d_{2}(\tau)(t)=\tau(\frac{1}{3}+\frac{t}{3})\cdot\tau(\frac{1}{3})^{-1},

for
t\in[0,1]
and
d_{i}:{\mathbb{G}}_{3}\to\Omega G
for
i=0,1,2,3
are given by

(3.5)

d_{i}(\tau_{0},\tau_{1},\tau_{2})=\tau_{i}\quad\text{with}\quad\tau_{3}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\tau_{0}(t+\frac{1}{3})\cdot\tau_{0}(\frac{1}{3})^{-1}&\text{for }t\in[0,\frac{1}{3}],\\
\tau_{2}(t)\cdot\tau_{0}(\frac{1}{3})^{-1}&\text{for }t\in[\frac{1}{3},\frac{2}{3}],\\
\tau_{1}(\frac{4}{3}-t)\cdot\tau_{0}(\frac{1}{3})^{-1}&\text{for }t\in[\frac{2}{3},1].\end{array}\right.

The degeneracy maps
s_{i}:P_{e}G\to\Omega G
for
i=0,1
are defined as follows:

(3.6)

s_{0}(\gamma)(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\gamma(3t)&t\in[0,\frac{1}{3}]\\
\gamma(1)&t\in[\frac{1}{3},\frac{2}{3}]\\
\gamma(3-3t)&t\in[\frac{2}{3},1],\end{array}\right.\quad s_{1}(\gamma)(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}e&t\in[0,\frac{1}{3}]\\
\gamma(3t-1)&t\in[\frac{1}{3},\frac{2}{3}]\\
\gamma(3-3t)&t\in[\frac{2}{3},1].\end{array}\right.

Remark 3.7
.

Notice that even if we begin with smooth maps, our construction of face or degeneracy maps via concatenation does not end up always with a smooth map again, but rather a map in the Sobolev completion.

If
G
is a Lie group, then the tangent bundle
TG
is also a Lie group. The same is true for Lie
n
-groupoids: if
X_{\bullet}
is a Lie
n
-groupoid, then
TX_{\bullet}
equipped with tangent face and degeneracy maps is again a Lie
n
-groupoid because tangent functor keeps the property of being a surjective submersion and an isomorphism. Hence
T{\mathbb{G}}_{\bullet}
is a Lie
2
-group

T{\mathbb{G}}_{\bullet}=\cdots\Omega TG\ \begin{subarray}{c}\longrightarrow\\[-8.50006pt]
\longrightarrow\\[-8.50006pt]
\longrightarrow\end{subarray}P_{(e,0_{e})}TG\rightrightarrows pt

with faces and degeneracy maps
Td_{i}
and
Ts_{i}
. In Appendix
C
we give explicit formulas for the faces and degeneracies since they are important for computations. The tangent complex
(\mathcal{T}_{\bullet}{\mathbb{G}},\partial)
is given by

(3.7)

\mathcal{T}_{k}{\mathbb{G}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}P_{0}\mathfrak{g}=\{u:I\to\mathfrak{g}\ |\ u(0)=0\}&\text{for }k=1,\\
\Omega\mathfrak{g}=\{a:S^{1}\to\mathfrak{g}\ |\ a(0)=0\}&\text{for }k=2,\\
0&\text{otherwise},\end{array}\right.

and
\partial:\Omega\mathfrak{g}\to P_{0}\mathfrak{g}
the natural inclusion. To compute the homology groups
H_{*}(\mathcal{T}_{\bullet}{\mathbb{G}})
, we observe that
0\to\Omega\mathfrak{g}\xrightarrow{\partial}P_{0}\mathfrak{g}\xrightarrow{ev_{1}}\mathfrak{g}\to 0
is an exact sequence, where
ev_{1}
denotes the evaluation at time 1. Thus

(3.8)

H_{1}(\mathcal{T}_{\bullet}{\mathbb{G}})=\mathfrak{g}\qquad\text{and}\qquad H_{i}(\mathcal{T}_{\bullet}{\mathbb{G}})=0\ \text{for all $i\neq 1$.}

Remark 3.8
.

In this article we do not need the Lie
2
-algebra structure on the tangent complex but we included for completeness (see e.g.
[
14
]
). It is given by
\mathbb{g}=(\Omega\mathfrak{g}\xrightarrow{\partial}P_{0}\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot])
, where

[u,v](t)=[u(t),v(t)],\quad[u,a](t)=[u(t),a(t)],\quad\text{and}\quad[u,v,w](t)=0,

for
u,v,w\in P_{0}\mathfrak{g}
and
a\in\Omega\mathfrak{g}.

In the work of Segal (see e.g.
[
78
]
), it is shown that if in addition
(\mathfrak{g},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
is a quadratic Lie algebra then the based loop group
\Omega G
carries a
2
-form given by

(3.9)

\omega_{\tau}(a,b)=\int_{S^{1}}\langle\widehat{a}^{\prime}(t),\widehat{b}(t)\rangle\ dt\ \in\Omega^{2}(\Omega G),

where
\tau\in\Omega G,\ a,b\in T_{\tau}\Omega G,\ \widehat{a}(t)=L_{\tau(t)^{-1}}a(t),\widehat{b}(t)=L_{\tau(t)^{-1}}b(t)
and the prime denotes time derivative as usual. So we have all the necessary ingredients to show the following result.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
G
be a Lie group with quadratic Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
. The pair
({\mathbb{G}}_{\bullet},\omega_{\bullet})
is a
2
-shifted symplectic Lie
2
-group where
{\mathbb{G}}_{\bullet}=\cdots\Omega G\begin{subarray}{c}\longrightarrow\\[-8.50006pt]
\longrightarrow\\[-8.50006pt]
\longrightarrow\end{subarray}P_{e}G\rightrightarrows pt
is the
2
-group described by (
3.4
), (
3.5
) and (
3.6
) and
\omega_{\bullet}=\omega+0+0
with
\omega
given by (
3.9
).

Proof.

We start by showing that
\omega_{\bullet}
is normalized. Here we prove that
s_{0}^{*}\omega=0
; the proof for
s_{1}
is analogous. Using the equations (
C.5
), for
\gamma\in P_{e}G,\ u,v\in T_{\gamma}P_{e}G
, and
\widehat{u}(t)=L_{\gamma(t)^{-1}}u(t),\ \widehat{v}(t)=L_{\gamma(t)^{-1}}v(t)
we have

\begin{split}(s_{0}^{*}\omega)_{\gamma}(u,v)=&\ \omega_{s_{0}(\gamma)}(Ts_{0}(u),Ts_{0}(v))\\
=&\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{3}}\langle\widehat{u}^{\prime}(3t),\widehat{v}(3t)\rangle dt+\int^{\frac{2}{3}}_{\frac{1}{3}}\langle\widehat{u}^{\prime}(1),\widehat{v}(1)\rangle dt+\int_{\frac{2}{3}}^{1}\langle\widehat{u}^{\prime}(3-3t),\widehat{v}(3-3t)\rangle dt\\
=&\frac{1}{3}\int_{0}^{1}\langle\widehat{u}^{\prime}(s),\widehat{v}(s)\rangle ds-\frac{1}{3}\int_{0}^{1}\langle\widehat{u}^{\prime}(s),\widehat{v}(s)\rangle ds=0.\\
\end{split}

In order to see that
\omega_{\bullet}
is non-degenerate, we first observe that for
u,v\in P_{0}\mathfrak{g}=T_{\underline{e}}P_{e}G
, with
\underline{e}
the constant loop at the identity
e\in G
,

\begin{split}\omega_{\underline{e}}(Ts_{0}(u),Ts_{1}(v))=&\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{3}}\langle u^{\prime}(3t),v(0)\rangle dt+\int_{\frac{1}{3}}^{\frac{2}{3}}\langle u^{\prime}(1),v(3t-1)\rangle dt\\
&+\int_{\frac{2}{3}}^{1}\langle u^{\prime}(3-3t),v(3-3t)\rangle dt=\int_{\frac{2}{3}}^{1}\langle u^{\prime}(3-3t),v(3-3t)\rangle dt.\end{split}

Now we use the tangent complex (
3.7
) and its homology groups (
3.8
) to directly apply (
2.5
). Then for
u,v\in\mathcal{T}_{1}{\mathbb{G}}=P_{0}\mathfrak{g}
, the IM-form reads,

\begin{split}\lambda^{\omega_{\bullet}}(u,v)=&\omega_{\underline{e}}(Ts_{0}(u),Ts_{1}(v))-\omega_{\underline{e}}(Ts_{1}(u),Ts_{0}(v))\\
=&\int_{\frac{2}{3}}^{1}\langle u^{\prime}(3-3t),v(3-3t)\rangle dt+\int_{\frac{2}{3}}^{1}\langle v^{\prime}(3-3t),u(3-3t)\rangle dt=\langle u(1),v(1)\rangle.\end{split}

Since the projection
P_{0}\mathfrak{g}\xrightarrow{ev_{1}}H_{1}(\mathcal{T}{\mathbb{G}})=\mathfrak{g}
is evaluation at time 1, we conclude that
\lambda^{\omega_{\bullet}}(-,-)
is non-degenerate in the homology groups.

Finally the fact that
D\omega_{\bullet}=0
will follow from Theorem
3.15
which states

\omega_{\bullet}=\frac{1}{2}ev^{*}_{\bullet}\Omega_{\bullet}+D\omega^{P}_{\bullet},

and the fact that
D\Omega_{\bullet}=0
by Theorem
3.1
.
∎

Remark 3.10
.

One can directly compute
D\omega_{\bullet}=0
by showing that
d\omega=0
and
\delta\omega=0
. The equation
d\omega=0
it is well known and goes back to the works of Segal
[

]
. The second equation
\delta\omega=0
was never computed (as far as we know) and can be directly verified by using the face maps (
3.5
) and (
C.3
). One divides each loop into

Sobolev spaces

Here we recall some analytic facts about Sobolev spaces used in this article (see also
[
29
, Sect.4]
,
[
11
, Sect.14]
). For a finite-dimensional compact manifold

, possibly with boundary and corners, let

denote the order

L^{2}
-functions. The

, then the restriction of continuous functions from

to
Z
extends to a continuous linear map
H_{r}(M)\to H_{r-\frac{1}{2}\codim(Z)}(Z)
, with a continuous right inverse.

If
N

r-\frac{1}{2}\dim(M)>0
, one defines spaces
\Hom_{r}(M,N)
of maps from
M
to
N
of Sobolev class

by choosing local charts
for

. In particular, if

is a finite-dimensional Lie group and

, then

is a Banach (even Hilbert) Lie group under point-wise multiplication

29
, Sect.4]
.

We are particularly interested in the loop group

and the based loop group
\Omega G:=\{\tau\in LG|\tau(0)=e\}
for a fixed

. In addition to the advantage of having a Banach manifold, using the version of based loops with Sobolev completion is also helpful as sometimes our construction of face or degeneracy maps via concatenation does not result in a smooth map, but instead a map in the Sobolev completion.

Given a domain

\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the map sending

to the evaluation of its

Proposition C.1
.

The
2
-shifted
2
-form

on

is closed, that is,

Since

and similarly for

where
A_{i}
correspond to the first third of the loop,
B_{i}
to the second third and
C_{i}
to the last third. Since

maps are dense in Sobolev space, we only need to verify our result at smooth loops. In order to compare the terms
A_{i},B_{i}

C_{i}
for different values of

C.1
) and the face maps given by (

C.4
) implies

(C.6)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}a_{0}(t)=R_{\tau_{0}(\frac{1}{3})}a_{3}(t-\frac{1}{3})+L_{\tau_{3}(t-\frac{1}{3})}a_{0}(\frac{1}{3})&t\in[\frac{1}{3},\frac{2}{3}],\\

\end{array}\right.

and the same identities holds for

.

For

A_{0}=\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{3}}\langle\widehat{a}^{\prime}_{0}(t),\widehat{b}_{0}(t)\rangle dt=\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{3}}\langle\widehat{a}^{\prime}_{1}(t),\widehat{b}_{1}(t)\rangle dt=A_{1},

where the middle equality follows from the definition of

Appendix D
Transgression map

In this appendix we recollect some useful formulas for the transgression map going from
k
-forms on a manifold to
(k-1)
-forms on its path space, which should be well known to experts.

Let
M
be a manifold and denote by
PM
its path space, i.e.

PM=\{\gamma:[0,1]\to M\ |\ \gamma\text{ of class }H_{r}\}
,
and consider
PM
as a Banach manifold. The evaluation map
ev:[0,1]\times PM\to M,\ ev(t,\gamma)=\gamma(t)
at the level of forms produces the map

ev^{*}:\Omega^{k}(M)\to\Omega^{k}([0,1]\times PM),

and since the interval has dimension
1
we can give the more explicit description

(D.1)

\Omega^{k}([0,1]\times PM)=\Omega^{0}([0,1])\otimes\Omega^{k}(PM)\oplus\Omega^{1}([0,1])\otimes\Omega^{k-1}(PM).

The
transgression map
is defined as the composition of evaluation with integration:

(D.2)

\begin{array}[]{cccl}\tr:&\Omega^{k}(M)&\to&\Omega^{k-1}(PM)\\
&\omega&\mapsto&\tr(\omega)=\int_{0}^{1}ev^{*}\omega.\end{array}

Now we give short proofs for the properties of the transgression map that we use in the article.

Proposition D.1
.

The transgression map and de Rham differential satisfy the following relation:

\tr(d\omega)=ev_{1}^{*}\omega-ev_{0}^{*}\omega-d_{PM}\tr(\omega),

where
ev_{t}(\gamma)=\gamma(t)
for
t\in[0,1].

Proof.

\begin{split}\tr(d\omega)=&\int_{0}^{1}ev^{*}d\omega=\int_{0}^{1}d_{[0,1]\times PM}(ev^{*}\omega)=\int_{0}^{1}d_{[0,1]}ev^{*}\omega-\int_{0}^{1}d_{PM}(ev^{*}\omega)\\
=&ev^{*}_{1}\omega-ev^{*}_{0}\omega-d_{PM}\tr(\omega),\end{split}

where we use the decomposition (
D.1
) and Stokes’ theorem.
∎

Proposition D.2
.

In terms of vectors the transgression map has the following explicit formula:

\tr(\omega)_{\gamma}(v_{1},\cdots,v_{k-1})=\int_{0}^{1}\omega_{\gamma(t)}\big(\gamma^{\prime}(t),v_{1}(t),\cdots,v_{k-1}(t)\big)dt,

where
\omega\in\Omega^{k}(M)
and
v_{i}\in T_{\gamma}PM.

Proof.

With decomposition (
D.1
), we write

(D.3)

ev^{*}\omega=\omega_{0}\otimes\omega_{k}+\omega_{1}\otimes\omega_{k-1},

where
\omega_{k}\in\Omega^{k}(PM)
,
\omega_{k-1}\in\Omega^{k-1}(PM)
,
\omega_{1}\in\Omega^{1}([0,1])
and
\omega_{0}\in\Omega^{0}([0,1])
. Then contracting with
[0,1]
via
\int_{0}^{1}
, only the second term survives. Thus for tangent vectors
x_{i}\in T_{\gamma}PM
,
i=1,\dots,k-1
, at
\gamma\in PM
, we have

(D.4)

\int_{0}^{1}ev^{*}\omega_{\gamma}(v_{1},\dots,v_{k-1})=\int_{0}^{1}\omega_{1}\otimes\omega_{k-1}(v_{1},\dots,v_{k-1}).

At the same time, for a tangent vector
(w,v)\in T_{t}[0,1]\times T_{\gamma}PM
, we have
T_{\gamma}ev(w,v)=w\gamma^{\prime}+v
. This can be seen by taking a variation (a small path)
(t+w\epsilon,\gamma^{\epsilon})
representing
(w,v)
(thus
\gamma^{0}=\gamma
). Then

T_{\gamma}ev(w,v)=\frac{d}{d\epsilon}\bigg|_{\epsilon=0}(\gamma^{\epsilon}(t+w\epsilon))=\frac{d}{d\epsilon}\bigg|_{\epsilon=0}\gamma^{0}(t+w\epsilon)+\frac{d}{d\epsilon}\bigg|_{\epsilon=0}\gamma^{\epsilon}(t)=w\gamma^{\prime}(t)+v(t).

Thus

\begin{split}&ev^{*}\omega|_{(t,\gamma)}((w_{1},v_{1}),…,(w_{k},v_{k}))=\omega_{\gamma(t)}(T_{\gamma}ev(w_{1},v_{1}),…,T_{\gamma}ev(w_{k},v_{k}))\\
=&\omega_{\gamma(t)}(v_{1}(t),…,v_{k}(t))+\omega_{\gamma(t)}(w_{1}\gamma^{\prime}(t),v_{2}(t),…,v_{k}(t))+c.p..\end{split}

Thus comparing with (
D.3
), we have
\omega_{k}=\omega
,
\omega_{0}=1
,
\omega_{k-1}=\iota(\gamma^{\prime})\omega
, and
\omega_{1}=dt
. Combining with (
D.4
) obtains the desired formula.
∎

Suppose that we have a simplicial manifold
X_{\bullet}
. Then the path space
PX_{\bullet}
is again a simplicial manifold given by

(PX)_{n}=P(X_{n}),\quad(Pd)_{i}(\gamma)(t)=(d_{i}\circ\gamma)(t),\quad\text{and}\quad(Ps)_{i}(\gamma)(t)=(s_{i}\circ\gamma)(t).

Proposition D.3
.

Let
X_{\bullet}
be a simplicial manifold. Then the transgression commutes with the simplicial differentials, i.e.

\tr(\delta^{X}\omega)=\delta^{PX}\tr(\omega).

Proof.

In order to prove this, we first observe that if
X_{\bullet}
is a simplicial manifold then
TX_{\bullet}
is also a simplicial manifold with faces and degeneracies given by the corresponding tangent maps. It then follows that there is a canonical identification between
P(TX)_{\bullet}
and
T(PX)_{\bullet}
as simplicial manifolds. Using this canonical identification and the explicit formula of the transgression given by Proposition
D.2
, for
\omega\in\Omega^{k}(X_{n-1}),

\gamma\in PX_{n},
and
v_{j}\in T_{\gamma}PX_{n}
we have

\begin{split}&\big((Pd_{i})^{*}\tr(\omega)\big)_{\gamma}(v_{1},\cdots,v_{k-1})=\tr(\omega)_{Pd_{i}(\gamma)}\Big(TPd_{i}(v_{1}),\cdots,TPd_{i}(v_{k-1})\Big)dt\\
&=\int_{0}^{1}\omega_{Pd_{i}(\gamma)(t)}\Big(Pd_{i}(\gamma)^{\prime}(t),TPd_{i}(v_{1})(t),\cdots,TPd_{i}(v_{k-1})(t)\Big)dt\\
&=\int_{0}^{1}\omega_{d_{i}(\gamma(t))}\Big(Td_{i}(\gamma(t))^{\prime},PTd_{i}(v_{1})(t),\cdots,PTd_{i}(v_{k-1})(t)\Big)dt\\
&=\int_{0}^{1}\omega_{d_{i}(\gamma(t))}\Big(Td_{i}(\gamma^{\prime}(t)),Td_{i}(v_{1}(t)),\cdots,Td_{i}(v_{k-1}(t))\Big)\\
&=\int_{0}^{1}(d^{*}_{i}\omega)_{\gamma(t)}\Big(\gamma^{\prime}(t),v_{1}(t),\cdots,v_{k-1}(t)\Big)dt=\tr(d^{*}_{i}\omega)_{\gamma}(v_{1},\cdots,v_{k-1}).\end{split}

Since the simplicial differentials are alternating sums of face maps, this prove the statement.
∎

which can be illustrated as

\scriptsize{\begin{pmatrix}0&\dots&\mathbf{l}&\dots&p,&p+1&\dots&\mathbf{p+1+j}&\dots&p+q\\
\pi(0)<&\dots&<\mathbf{\pi(p+1+j)-1}<&\dots&<\pi(p),&\pi(p+1)<&\dots&<\mathbf{\pi(l)}<&\dots&<\pi(p+q)\end{pmatrix}}.

Then clearly
\text{sgn}(\pi)=-\text{sgn}(\tilde{\pi})
and

\displaystyle Td_{i}Ts_{\pi(p)}\dots Ts_{\pi(0)}w=Td_{i}Ts_{\tilde{\pi}(p)}\dots Ts_{\tilde{\pi}(0)}w,

\displaystyle Td_{i}Ts_{\pi(p+q)}\dots Ts_{\pi(p+1)}v=Td_{i}Ts_{\tilde{\pi}(p+q)}\dots Ts_{\tilde{\pi}(p+1)}v.

Notice that terms

\displaystyle\text{sgn}(\pi)\,\alpha_{m}(Td_{i}Ts_{\pi(p+q)}\dots Ts_{\pi(p+1)}v,Td_{i}Ts_{\pi(p)}\dots Ts_{\pi(0)}w),

\displaystyle\text{sgn}(\tilde{\pi})\,\alpha_{m}(Td_{i}Ts_{\tilde{\pi}(p+q)}\dots Ts_{\tilde{\pi}(p+1)}v,Td_{i}Ts_{\tilde{\pi}(p)}\dots Ts_{\tilde{\pi}(0)}w)

cancel with each other. The case
i\in\{\pi(p),\dots,\pi(0)\}
can be treated similarly. Thus

\displaystyle\sum_{\pi\in\mathsf{Shuff}(p+1,q)}\text{sgn}(\pi)\,\alpha_{m}(Td_{n+1}Ts_{\pi(p+q)}\dots Ts_{\pi(p+1)}v,Td_{n+1}Ts_{\pi(p)}\dots Ts_{\pi(0)}w)=0.

We now distinguish between two types of
(p+1,q)
-shuffles: shuffles satisfying
\pi(p+q)=m
and shuffles satisfying
\pi(p)=m
. There exists a 1-1 correspondence between
(p+1,q)
-shuffles
\pi
with
\pi(p+q)=m
and
(p+1,q-1)
-shuffles
\tau
via
\tau(k)=\pi(k)
for
k\in\{0,\dots,p+q-1\}
. Likewise there exists a 1-1 correspondence between
(p+1,q)
-shuffles
\pi
with
\pi(p)=m
and
(p,q)
-shuffles
\chi
via

\displaystyle\chi(k)=\begin{cases}\pi(k)\>\>\>\>\>\text{if}\>\>k\in\{0,\dots,p-1\},\\
\pi(k+1)\>\>\>\>\>\text{if}\>\>k\in\{p,\dots,p+q-1\}.\end{cases}

With these correspondences it follows that

(E.6)

\begin{split}0=&\sum_{\pi\in\mathsf{Shuff}(p+1,q)}\text{sgn}(\pi)\,\alpha_{m}(Td_{m+1}Ts_{\pi(p+q)}\dots Ts_{\pi(p+1)}v,Td_{m+1}Ts_{\pi(p)}\dots Ts_{\pi(0)}w)\\
=&\sum_{\pi\in\mathsf{Shuff}(p+1,q),\pi(p+q)=m}\text{sgn}(\pi)\,\alpha_{m}(Ts_{\pi(p+q-1)}\dots Ts_{\pi(p+1)}v,Ts_{\pi(p)}\dots Ts_{\pi(0)}Td_{q}w)\\
+&\sum_{\pi\in\mathsf{Shuff}(p+1,q),\pi(p)=m}\text{sgn}(\pi)\,\alpha_{m}(Ts_{\pi(p+q)}\dots Ts_{\pi(p+1)}Td_{p+1}v,Ts_{\pi(p-1)}\dots Ts_{\pi(0)}w)\\
=&(-1)^{q}\sum_{\tau\in\mathsf{Shuff}(p+1,q-1)}\text{sgn}(\tau)\,\alpha_{m}(Ts_{\tau(p+q-1)}\dots Ts_{\tau(p+1)}v,Ts_{\tau(p)}\dots Ts_{\tau(0)}\partial w)\\
+&(-1)^{p+1}\sum_{\chi\in\mathsf{Shuff}(p,q)}\text{sgn}(\chi)(-1)^{q}\,\alpha_{m}(Ts_{\chi(p+q-1)}\dots Ts_{\chi(p)}\partial v,Ts_{\chi(p-1)}\dots Ts_{\chi(0)}w)\\
=&(-1)^{q}\lambda^{\alpha_{\bullet}}(v,\partial w)+(-1)^{q}(-1)^{p+1}\lambda^{\alpha_{\bullet}}(\partial v,w).\end{split}

Thus (
E.1
) is proven.

(
3
) It is enough to show that
\lambda^{D\phi_{\bullet}}
vanishes for any
(m-1)
-shifted form
\phi_{\bullet}
. From (
E.6
) and the fact that
(D\phi)_{m}=\delta\phi_{m-1}
, it follows that

\displaystyle\lambda^{D\phi_{\bullet}}(v,w)=\lambda^{\phi_{\bullet}}(\partial v,w)+(-1)^{p}\lambda^{\phi_{\bullet}}(v,\partial w),

for tangent vectors
v\in T_{x_{0}}X_{p},\>w\in T_{x_{0}}X_{q},\>p+q=m,\>\,p,q\geq 1
.
The two summands on the right hand side turn out to be equal to zero: we note that

\displaystyle\underbrace{\lambda^{D\phi_{\bullet}}\big((-1)^{p}Ts_{p-1}\partial v,w\big)}_{=0}=\lambda^{\phi_{\bullet}}(\partial\,(-1)^{p}Ts_{p-1}\partial v,w)+(-1)^{p}\underbrace{\lambda^{\phi_{\bullet}}((-1)^{p}Ts_{p-1}\partial v,\partial w)}_{=0}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{\phi_{\bullet}}\big((-1)^{p}Td_{p}(-1)^{p}Ts_{p-1}\partial v,w\big)=\lambda^{\phi_{\bullet}}(\partial v,w).

The terms
\lambda^{D\phi_{\bullet}}\big((-1)^{p}Ts_{p-1}\partial v,w\big)
and
\lambda^{\phi_{\bullet}}\big((-1)^{p}Ts_{p-1}\partial v,\partial w\big)
are zero thanks to item (
1
). Thus
\lambda^{\phi_{\bullet}}(\partial v,w)=0
. Analogously
\lambda^{\phi_{\bullet}}(v,\partial w)=0
.

It remains to be shown that
\lambda^{D\phi_{\bullet}}(v,w)=0
if
v\in\mathcal{T}_{p}(X_{\bullet}),\>w\in\mathcal{T}_{q}(X_{\bullet})
,
p,q\in\{0,m\}
. We first consider the case
p=0,\>q=m
. Then

(E.7)

\begin{split}\lambda^{D\phi_{\bullet}}(v,w)=&\delta\phi_{m-1}(Ts_{m-1}\dots Ts_{0}v,w)\\
=&\sum_{i=0}^{m}(-1)^{i}\phi(Td_{i}Ts_{m-1}\dots Ts_{0}v,Td_{i}w)\\
=&(-1)^{m}\phi_{m-1}(Td_{m}Ts_{n-1}\dots Ts_{0}v,Td_{m}w)\\
=&\phi_{m-1}(Ts_{m-2}\dots Ts_{0}v,(-1)^{m}Td_{m}w)=\lambda^{\phi_{\bullet}}(v,\partial w),\end{split}

which equals zero since

\begin{split}0=\lambda^{D\phi_{\bullet}}\big(v,(-1)^{m}Ts_{m-1}\partial w\big)=&\lambda^{\phi_{\bullet}}(v,\partial\,(-1)^{m}Ts_{m-1}\partial w)\\
=&\lambda^{\phi_{\bullet}}(v,(-1)^{m}Td_{m}(-1)^{m}Ts_{m-1}\partial w)=\lambda^{\phi_{\bullet}}(v,\partial w),\end{split}

where we have used (
E.7
) for the first equal sign and the fact that
\lambda^{D\phi_{\bullet}}
vanishes on degeneracies.
Analogously it can be shown that
\lambda^{D\phi_{\bullet}}=0
if
p=m
and
q=0
.
∎

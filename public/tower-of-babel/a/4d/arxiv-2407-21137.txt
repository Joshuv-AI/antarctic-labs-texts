Feedback stabilization for entropy solutions of a 2 × 2 2\times 2 hyperbolic system of conservation laws at a junction
Giuseppe Maria Coclite, Nicola De Nitti, Mauro Garavello, Francesca Marcellini

Abstract
. We consider the p p -system in Eulerian coordinates on a star-shaped network. Under suitable transmission conditions at the junction and dissipative boundary conditions in the exterior vertices, we show that the entropy solutions of the system are exponentially stabilizable. Our proof extends the strategy by Coron et al. (2017) and is based on a front-tracking algorithm used to construct approximate piecewise constant solutions whose BV norms are controlled through a suitable exponentially-weighted Glimm-type Lyapunov functional.

35L65, 35L45, 35B25.

1.
Introduction

We aim to stabilize the entropy solutions to the
p
-system (or Euler equations)
in a star-shaped network around a given equilibrium by using boundary
feedback controls. The
p
-system models a fluid in a rectilinear 1D pipe
through the evolution of some macroscopic quantities, namely its density
and linear momentum; see
[
18
,
32
]
and the references therein for
a more complete description.

Let us consider
N\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0,1\}
rectilinear tubes,
modeled by the real interval
I\coloneqq(0,1)
, exiting a junction
J
,
which is located at the position
x=0
.
For

\ell\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
, the direction and section of the

\ell
-th tube are described, respectively, by the direction and the
norm of a vector
\nu_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\{0\}.

All tubes are filled with the same compressible, inviscid and
isentropic (or isothermal)
fluid, and we assume that friction along the walls is neglected.
Hence the fluid dynamics
can be modeled through
n
copies of the one-dimensional

p
-system in Eulerian coordinates:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\rho_{\ell}+\partial_{x}q_{\ell}=0,&t>0,\,x\in[0,1),\\
\partial_{t}q_{\ell}+\partial_{x}\left(\frac{q_{\ell}^{2}}{\rho_{\ell}}+p\left(\rho_{\ell}\right)\right)=0,\qquad\qquad&t>0,\ x\in{[0,1)},\end{cases}\qquad\ell\in\{1,\ldots,N\}.

Here,
t
is the time, and, along the
\ell
-th tube,

x
is the abscissa,
\rho_{\ell}=\rho_{\ell}(t,x)
is the fluid density,
and
q_{\ell}=q_{\ell}(t,x)
is its linear momentum density.
We assume that the pressure law
p=p(\rho)
is the same for all the tubes;
it plays the role of the equation of state of the fluid under
consideration and it is assumed to satisfy the following hypothesis:

(P)

\displaystyle p\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+};\mathbb{R}^{+}),\quad p^{\prime}>0,\quad p^{\prime\prime}>0.

We supplement
Eq.

1.1
with a set of initial conditions, of coupling
conditions at the junction
J
, and of boundary conditions at the position

x=1
of each pipe.
As initial conditions, we consider

(1.2)

\left(\rho_{\ell},q_{\ell}\right)(0,x)=\left({\rho}_{0,\ell},{q}_{0,\ell}\right)(x),\quad x\in I,\quad\text{$\ell\in\{1,\ldots,N\}$},

where
{\rho}_{0,\ell}
and
{q}_{0,\ell}
are given functions
in
L^{1}\left(I\right)
with finite total variation.

In this paper, at the junction
J
, we consider
the notion of
P-solutions
, introduced in
[
13
]
,
which prescribes that

(1)

mass is conserved;

(2)

the trace of the linear momentum flux
(sometimes called
dynamic pressure
) at
J

is the same for all tubes;

(3)

entropy may not decrease at the junction.

This concept of solution yields the well-posedness in

L^{1}
; see
[
14
,
15
]
for further details.
Finally, at the exterior boundary we impose a feedback control, described in terms of the Riemann invariants of the
p
-system.

The main result of the present paper is the existence of a feedback type
control, acting at the exterior boundary of the star-shaped network, which
asymptotically stabilizes the
p
-system
Eq.

1.1

around an equilibrium configuration.
Roughly speaking, with an equilibrium over the network we mean a vector

(1.3)

\left(\left(\bar{\rho}_{1},\bar{q}_{1}\right),\ldots,\left(\bar{\rho}_{N},\bar{q}_{N}\right)\right)\in\left(\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}\right)^{N}

of constant states, which provide an equilibrium solution for
Eq.

1.1
.
The stabilization’s proof is done through a Lyapunov-type functional, similar
to the one considered in
[
17
]
, which is decreasing in time,
on the wave-front tracking construction, and eventually vanishes.
We strongly use the fact that, locally around the equilibrium, one
eigenvalue of the linearized system is strictly negative and one
strictly positive.
It is important to notice that, in this paper, we do not work with strong solutions,
but with weak solutions, which
possibly have discontinuities.

Existence and well-posedness of entropy admissible solutions
for the Cauchy problem at junctions
was established in
[
15
]
, and, with a similar technique, can be also extended in the case of the initial-boundary value problem. The problem of the stabilization is, instead, more difficult. While our Lyapunov functional is similar
to the one considered in
[
17
]
, we have to face additional challenges. Indeed, in
[
17
]
, the authors considered a
2\times 2
hyperbolic system
of conservation laws with positive characteristic speeds on a bounded segment; as a consequence, the feedback boundary control acts on the left boundary according to the trace of the solution at the right boundary. On the other hand, in our work, the presence of a strictly positive eigenvalue and a strictly negative one complicates the analysis of the feedback mechanism.

Further recent results inspired by
[
17
]
are contained in
[
21
,
22
]
. Previous to
[
17
]
, the stabilization of a scalar
conservation law through a stationary feedback mechanism was achieved
in
[
33
]
; in the case of open-loop controls, results on
asymptotic stabilization and on controllability for a general hyperbolic system of
conservation laws with either genuinely nonlinear or linearly
degenerate characteristic fields (in the sense of Lax) and
characteristic speeds strictly separated from
0
, were obtained in

[
10
]
by suitably acting on both sides of the interval or
in
[
5
]
by acting on a single boundary point. In
[
7
]
, an entropy-based Lyapunov functional was used for the stability analysis of equilibria in networks of scalar conservation laws. For the problem of exact controllability of both classical and entropy solutions for systems of conservation laws on an interval, we refer to
[
3
,
4
,
28
,
29
,
30
,
31
]
.
Instead, few results about the stabilization of hyperbolic systems on networks in the context of entropy solutions are currently available in the literature (see
for example
[
19
,
34
]
for networked wave equations).

On the other hand, for the stabilization of classical solutions, many more works have appeared throughout the years (for both scalar equations and systems, on a single segment or on networks). The exponential stabilization of gas flow governed by the isothermal Euler equations in fan-shaped networks in the
L^{2}
-sense has been studied in
[
24
]
. For a single pipe, a strict
H^{1}
-Lyapunov functional and feedback stabilization for the quasilinear isothermal Euler equations with friction have been studied in
[
20
]
. Similar results have been obtained for
H^{2}
-Lyapunov functions in
[
25
]
and for
C^{1}
-Lyapunov functions in
[
16
]
. The finite-time stabilization of a network of strings is studied in
[
1
,
26
]
. For the problem of exact controllability of classical solutions on networks, we refer to
[
23
]
. Finally, we refer to the book
[
8
]
for further results.

The paper is organized as follows. In
Section

2
, we
collect several preliminary notions on the solutions of the
p
-system
at a junction. In
Section

3
, we present our main
stabilization result. The proof, based on a front-tracking approximation and on the
study of an exponentially weighted Glimm functional, is contained
in
Section

4
.

2.
Basic definitions

In this section, we recall the key features of the
p
-system
in Eulerian coordinates,
we introduce the concept of solution of
Eq.

1.1

with initial conditions
Eq.

1.2
and with coupling and feedback type
boundary conditions, and we state the main result of this paper.

2.1.
Quantities of interest for the
p
-system

Following
[
12
, Section 6.1]
, let us recall here some quantities
of interest for the
p
-system (
1.1
).
First, let us introduce the notation

(2.1)

u\coloneqq(\rho,q)^{\top},\qquad f(u)\coloneqq\left(q,\frac{q^{2}}{\rho}+p(\rho)\right)^{\top},

so that the
p
-system
Eq.

1.1
can be written in the compact form

\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}f(u)=0
.
The Jacobian matrix of flux function
f
is

\displaystyle\nabla f(u)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
-\frac{q^{2}}{\rho^{2}}+p^{\prime}(\rho)&\frac{2q}{\rho}\end{array}\right)

and so, by assumption
Eq.

P
, the system is strictly hyperbolic and
its eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}
,
\lambda_{2}

and the corresponding right eigenvectors
r_{1},r_{2}
are given by

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}\lambda_{1}(\rho,q)=\frac{q}{\rho}-c(\rho),&\lambda_{2}(\rho,q)=\frac{q}{\rho}+c(\rho),\\
r_{1}(\rho,q)=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}-\rho\\
\rho c(\rho)-q\end{array}\right],&r_{2}(\rho,q)=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\rho,\\
q+\rho c(\rho)\end{array}\right],\end{array}

where
c(\rho):=\sqrt{p^{\prime}(\rho)}
is the sound speed.
Moreover, we have that

(2.2)

\nabla\lambda_{1}(\rho,q)\cdot r_{1}(\rho,q)=c(\rho)+\rho c^{\prime}(\rho)>0,\qquad\nabla\lambda_{2}(\rho,q)\cdot r_{2}(\rho,q)=c(\rho)+\rho c^{\prime}(\rho)>0,

so that both characteristic fields are genuinely nonlinear.
The
Riemann invariants

v_{1}
and
v_{2}

of the
p
-system (
1.1
) are

(2.3)

v_{1}\left(\rho,q\right):=\frac{q}{\rho}+\int_{1}^{\rho}\frac{\sqrt{p^{\prime}(r)}}{r}\,\mathrm{d}r,\qquad v_{2}\left(\rho,q\right):=\frac{q}{\rho}-\int_{1}^{\rho}\frac{\sqrt{p^{\prime}(r)}}{r}\,\mathrm{d}r.

Note that

(2.4)

\begin{split}\nabla v_{1}(\rho,q)\cdot r_{1}(\rho,q)&=\nabla v_{2}(\rho,q)\cdot r_{2}(\rho,q)=0,\\
\nabla v_{1}(\rho,q)\cdot r_{2}(\rho,q)&=\nabla v_{2}(\rho,q)\cdot r_{1}(\rho,q)=2\sqrt{p^{\prime}(\rho)},\end{split}

for every
(\rho,q)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}
;
v_{1}
is constant along the rarefaction curves of the first family,
while
v_{2}
is constant along the rarefaction curves of the second family. Moreover,
v_{1}
is monotonically increasing along the
rarefaction curves of the second family and
v_{2}
is monotonically increasing along the
rarefaction curves of the first family.

The shock and the rarefaction curves through a state
u\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}
can be
parameterized as

(2.5)

\displaystyle\sigma\mapsto S_{k}(\sigma)(u),\quad\sigma\mapsto R_{k}(\sigma)(u),

where
k\in\left\{1,2\right\}
and the parametrization is chosen so that

\displaystyle\lambda_{k}\left(S_{k}(\sigma)(u)\right)=\lambda_{k}\left(R_{k}(\sigma)(u)\right)=\lambda_{k}(u)+\sigma;

see
Figure

1
(left). Consequently we use the following
parametrization for the Lax curve

\sigma\mapsto\mathcal{L}_{k}(\sigma)(u)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}R_{k}(\sigma)(u),&\qquad\textrm{ if }\sigma\geq 0,\\
S_{k}(\sigma)(u),&\qquad\textrm{ if }\sigma<0.\end{array}\right.

We introduce the regions

(2.6)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &A_{-}=\left\{\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}:\lambda_{2}(\rho,q)<0\right\},\\
&A_{0}^{-}=\left\{\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}:\lambda_{2}(\rho,q)\geq 0,q\leq 0\right\},\\
&A_{0}^{+}=\left\{\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}:\lambda_{1}(\rho,q)\leq 0,q\geq 0\right\},\\
&A_{+}=\left\{\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}:\lambda_{1}(\rho,q)>0\right\},\\
&A_{0}=A_{0}^{-}\cup A_{0}^{+}.\end{aligned}

Here
A_{0}
is known as
subsonic region

Figure

Finally, we introduce the functions

2.2.
Initial-Boundary Valued Problem

Assigning at time
t=0
an initial state

\left({\rho}_{o,\ell},{q}_{o,\ell}\right)\in L^{1}(I;\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R})
in each of the
N

and
N

B_{\ell}\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{+};\mathbb{R})
,
we consider with the following initial-boundary value problem:

(2.10)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\rho_{1}+\partial_{x}q_{1}=0,&t>0,\,x\in I,\\
\partial_{t}q_{1}+\partial_{x}P(\rho_{1},q_{1})=0,&t>0,\,x\in I,\\
\vdots\\
\partial_{t}\rho_{N}+\partial_{x}q_{N}=0,&t>0,\,x\in I,\\
\partial_{t}q_{N}+\partial_{x}P(\rho_{N},q_{N})=0,&t>0,\,x\in I,\\
(\rho_{1},q_{1})(0,x)=\left(\rho_{0,1},q_{0,1}\right)(x),&x\in I,\\
\vdots\\
(\rho_{N},q_{N})(0,x)=\left(\rho_{0,N},q_{0,N}\right)(x),&x\in I,\\
v_{2}(\rho_{1}(t,1),q_{1}(t,1))=B_{1}(t),&t>0,\\
\vdots\\
v_{2}(\rho_{N}(t,1),q_{N}(t,1))=B_{N}(t),&t>0.\end{cases}

Definition 2.1

(Entropy solution of
Eq.

2.10
)
.

The tuple
((\rho_{1},q_{1}),\ldots,(\rho_{N},q_{N}))
is an
entropy solution
to
the Cauchy problem
Eq.

2.10
if the following conditions
hold.

(1)

For every
\ell\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
,

(\rho_{\ell},q_{\ell})\in C^{0}([0,+\infty);L^{1}(I;\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}))
.

(2)

For every
\ell\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
and for a.e.
t>0
,
the map
x\mapsto(\rho_{\ell}(t,x),q_{\ell}(t,x))
has finite
total variation.

(3)

For every
\ell\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
and for every

\Phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}\left((0,+\infty)\times I;\mathbb{R}\right)
, it holds

\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\left[\partial_{t}\Phi(t,x)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\rho_{\ell}(t,x)\\
q_{\ell}(t,x)\end{array}\right)+\partial_{x}\Phi(t,x)f(\rho_{\ell}(t,x),q_{\ell}(t,x))\right]\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
0\end{array}\right).

(4)

For every
\ell\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
and for every

\Phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}\left((0,+\infty)\times I;\mathbb{R}^{+}\right)
, it holds

\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\left[\partial_{t}\Phi(t,x)E\left(\rho_{\ell}(t,x),q_{\ell}(t,x)\right)+\partial_{x}\Phi(t,x)F(\rho_{\ell}(t,x),q_{\ell}(t,x))\right]\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\geq 0.

(5)

For every
\ell\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
,

(\rho_{\ell}(0,x),q_{\ell}(0,x))=(\rho_{0,\ell}(x),q_{0,\ell}(x))

for a.e.
x\in I
.

(6)

For a.e.
t>0
, the following coupling conditions hold:

(a)

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\left\|\nu_{\ell}\right\|q_{\ell}(t,0^{+})=0
;

(b)

there exists
P_{\ast}=P_{\ast}(t)>0
(possibly depending on

t
) such that, for every
\ell\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
,

P(\rho_{\ell}(t,0^{+}),q_{\ell}(t,0^{+}))=P_{\ast}(t);

(c)

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\left\|\nu_{\ell}\right\|F(\rho_{\ell}(t,0^{+}),q_{\ell}(t,0^{+}))\leq 0
.

(7)

For every
\ell\in\left\{1,\ldots,N\right\}
and for a.e.
t>0
,

v_{2}\left(\rho_{\ell}(t,1^{-}),q_{\ell}(t,1^{-})\right)=B_{\ell}(t).

Given
N
constant states
\bar{u}_{1},\ldots,\bar{u}_{N}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}
and the vector

\vec{k}=\left(k_{1},\ldots,k_{N}\right)\in[0,1]^{N}
, we consider the
system with feedback boundary control:

(2.11)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\rho_{1}+\partial_{x}q_{1}=0,&t>0,\,x\in I,\\
\partial_{t}q_{1}+\partial_{x}P(\rho_{1},q_{1})=0,&t>0,\,x\in I,\\
\vdots\\
\partial_{t}\rho_{N}+\partial_{x}q_{N}=0,&t>0,\,x\in I,\\
\partial_{t}q_{N}+\partial_{x}P(\rho_{N},q_{N})=0,&t>0,\,x\in I,\\
(\rho_{1},q_{1})(0,x)=\left(\rho_{0,1},q_{0,1}\right)(x),&x\in I,\\
\vdots\\
(\rho_{N},q_{N})(0,x)=\left(\rho_{0,N},q_{0,N}\right)(x),&x\in I,\\

3.
Main results: well-posedness and feedback stabilization via a Lyapunov functional

The main result of this paper deals with the
well posedness result for the
Cauchy
problem
Eq.

2.11

and with a stabilization result
for the solutions of the same Cauchy
problem
Eq.

2.11

with feedback control acting at the external boundary, according
to
Definition

2.2
. More precisely,
following
[
15
, Theorem 3.3]
,
we prove existence of entropy-admissible
solutions; then we consider a suitable feedback mechanism to
achieve exponential stability in a similar way as in
[
17
]
.

Theorem 3.1

(Exponential stabilization for entropy solutions)
.

Fix an
N
-tuple of subsonic states

(\bar{\rho},\bar{q})=\left(\left(\bar{\rho}_{1},\bar{q}_{1}\right),\ldots,\left(\bar{\rho}_{N},\bar{q}_{N}\right)\right)\in(\mathring{A}_{0})^{N}
,
giving a
equilibrium solution to the Cauchy problem
Eq.

2.11

At first, we give the following definition of an

Definition 4.1

\varepsilon

Given

(1)

For every

For

(4)

For every
\ell\in\left\{1,\ldots,N\right\}

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\left\|\left(\rho_{\ell,\varepsilon}(0,\cdot),q_{\ell,\varepsilon}(0,\cdot)\right)-\left(\rho_{0,\ell},q_{0,\ell}\right)\right\|_{L^{1}(I_{\ell})}<\varepsilon,\\
\mathrm{TV}\left(\rho_{\ell,\varepsilon}(0,\cdot),q_{\ell,\varepsilon}(0,\cdot)\right)\leq\mathrm{TV}\left(\rho_{0,\ell},q_{0,\ell}\right).\end{array}\right.

(5)

For a.e.

(4.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\left\|\nu_{\ell}\right\|q_{\ell,\varepsilon}(t,0^{+})=0,\\

For every

We briefly review how to construct a wave-front tracking approximate solution

Definition

4.1

Define
\bar{u}_{\ell}=\left(\bar{\rho}_{\ell},\bar{q}_{\ell}\right)

Given

approximate the initial condition with piecewise constant

\left(\rho_{0,\ell,\varepsilon},q_{0,\ell,\varepsilon}\right)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\left\|\left(\rho_{0,\ell,\varepsilon},q_{0,\ell,\varepsilon}\right)-\left(\rho_{0,\ell},q_{0,\ell}\right)\right\|_{L^{1}(0,1)}<\varepsilon,\\

Then, at the junction, at the exterior boundary, and at each point of jump

(1)

At each discontinuity inside a pipe

(2)

At the boundary

We approximate each rarefaction wave by means of rarefaction fans.

This construction can be extended up to a first time

In this construction we impose that any rarefaction fan is not split

4.2.
Glimm functionals

First, let us introduce the definition of approaching waves in the case

Definition 4.3

The waves

V_{\gamma}(t)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{J}_{\ell}}\left(2K_{J}\left|\sigma_{\ell,1,\alpha}\right|e^{\gamma x_{\alpha}}+\left|\sigma_{\ell,2,\alpha}\right|e^{-\gamma x_{\alpha}}\right),

\sigma_{\ell,1,\alpha}=0

or
\sigma_{\ell,2,\alpha}=0

Moreover, we define the quadratic weighted functionals

Q_{\gamma}=Q_{\gamma}^{1,1}+Q_{\gamma}^{2,2}+Q_{\gamma}^{1,2},

where

\displaystyle Q_{\gamma}^{1,1}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\sum\left\{\left|\sigma_{\ell,1,\alpha}e^{\gamma x_{\alpha}}\sigma_{\ell,1,\beta}e^{\gamma x_{\beta}}\right|:\left(\sigma_{\ell,1,\alpha},\sigma_{\ell,1,\beta}\right)\in\mathcal{A}_{\ell}\right\},

\displaystyle Q_{\gamma}^{2,2}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\sum\left\{\left|\sigma_{\ell,2,\alpha}e^{-\gamma x_{\alpha}}\sigma_{\ell,2,\beta}e^{-\gamma x_{\beta}}\right|:\left(\sigma_{\ell,2,\alpha},\sigma_{\ell,2,\beta}\right)\in\mathcal{A}_{\ell}\right\},

\displaystyle Q_{\gamma}^{1,2}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\sum\left\{\left|\sigma_{\ell,1,\alpha}e^{\gamma x_{\alpha}}\sigma_{\ell,2,\beta}e^{-\gamma x_{\beta}}\right|:\left(\sigma_{\ell,1,\alpha},\sigma_{\ell,2,\beta}\right)\in\mathcal{A}_{\ell}\right\},

Remark 4.4
.

The functional

It is essentially composed by a

The term

The strengths of the waves of the first family are weighted

Moreover, the exponential terms produce an exponential decay

Note that

is equivalent to the total variation
of the approximate solution;

Finally,

The term

for all
t
, therefore,
since
\mathcal{I}_{4}
and
\mathcal{I}_{5}
are finite,
also
\mathcal{I}_{2}
is finite.

Note that all the points in
\mathcal{I}_{3}
do not belong to

\mathcal{I}
.

Starting from
\left(t_{1},x_{1}\right)
, we go forward in time with two continuous lines: the first one made of waves of
\mathcal{F}_{1}
(first family) and the second one using waves of
\mathcal{F}_{2}
(second family). When we reach an interaction point
(\tilde{t},\tilde{x})
belonging to
\mathcal{I}_{2}
,
\mathcal{I}_{4}
, or
\mathcal{I}_{5}
, we stop and start over from a point
\left(t_{j},x_{j}\right)
, with
t_{j}>\tilde{t}
.

Since
\mathcal{I}_{2}
,
\mathcal{I}_{4}
, and
\mathcal{I}_{5}
are finite sets, there exists a point
\left(t^{*},x^{*}\right)\in\mathcal{I}
from which we can construct two lines
\gamma_{1}(t),\gamma_{2}(t)
until the time
T
.
We conclude now exactly as in
Case 1
.

Case 3:
\bar{x}=0
.
We proceed as in Case 2, by noticing that there are at most a finite
number of wave interactions with
the junction at
x=0
, provided

\Lambda_{\max}T<1
.
∎

4.5.
Existence of a solution

Let us consider a
\varepsilon
-wave-front tracking solution
u_{\varepsilon}
.
By the interaction estimates, using the functional
J_{0}
on

u_{\varepsilon}
(i.e.,
J_{\gamma}
with
\gamma=0
), we deduce, owing to

Corollary

4.6
,

Corollary

4.8
, and
Corollary

4.11

(4.18)

J_{0}(t)\leq J_{0}(0^{+})

since
J_{0}
varies
only at times of interaction.
Therefore, we have

(4.19)

\mathrm{TV}(u_{\varepsilon}(t))\leq J_{0}(t)\leq J_{0}(0^{+}).

Since it is standard to prove that there exists
L>0
such that

\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\left\|(\rho_{\ell,\varepsilon}(t),q_{\ell,\varepsilon}(t))-(\rho_{\ell,\varepsilon}(s),q_{\ell,\varepsilon}(s))\right\|_{L^{1}(I_{\ell})}\leq L\left|t-s\right|

for every
t,s\geq 0
, then
we conclude,
by Helly’s compactness theorem (see
[
9
, Theorem 2.4]
), that
u_{\varepsilon}
converges strongly in
L^{1}
to a limit point
u
. Moreover, it is standard to deduce that
u
satisfies
3.1-(i)
as well as an entropy condition, i.e.,
3.1-(iv)
.

4.6.
Existence of a semigroup

Let us consider two
\varepsilon
-wave-front tracking solutions
u
and
\tilde{u}
, and define the functional

(4.20)

\Phi(u,\tilde{u})\coloneqq\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{I_{\ell}}\left|s_{\ell,i}(x)\right|W_{\ell,i}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x.

Here,
s_{\ell,i}(x)
measures the strengths of the
i
-th shock wave in the
\ell
pipe at the point
x
; the weights

W_{\ell,i}(x)
are defined by

W_{\ell,i}(x)\coloneqq 1+\kappa_{1}A_{\ell,i}(x)+\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}(J_{0}(u)+J_{0}(v))

for suitable
\kappa_{1}>0
and
\kappa_{2}>0
;

A_{\ell,i}(x)=A_{\ell,i}^{1}(x)+A^{2}_{\ell,i}(x)
, with

(4.21)

\displaystyle A_{\ell,i}^{1}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathcal{J}(u)\cup\mathcal{J}(\tilde{u})\\
x_{\alpha}<x\\
k_{\alpha}\neq i\\
k_{\alpha}=2\end{subarray}}\left|\sigma_{\ell,k_{\alpha},\alpha}\right|+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathcal{J}(u)\cup\mathcal{J}(\tilde{u})\\
x_{\alpha}>x\\
k_{\alpha}\neq i\\
k_{\alpha}=1\end{subarray}}\left|\sigma_{\ell,k_{\alpha},\alpha}\right|,

\displaystyle A_{\ell,i}^{2}(x)

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathcal{J}(u)\\
x_{\alpha}<x\\
k_{\alpha}=i\end{subarray}}\left|\sigma_{\ell,k_{\alpha},\alpha}\right|+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathcal{J}(\tilde{u})\\
x_{\alpha}>x\\
k_{\alpha}=i\end{subarray}}\left|\sigma_{\ell,k_{\alpha},\alpha}\right|,&\textrm{ if }s_{\ell,i}(x)<0,\\
\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathcal{J}(\tilde{u})\\
x_{\alpha}<x\\
k_{\alpha}=i\end{subarray}}\left|\sigma_{\ell,k_{\alpha},\alpha}\right|+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathcal{J}(u)\\
x_{\alpha}>x\\
k_{\alpha}=i\end{subarray}}\left|\sigma_{\ell,k_{\alpha},\alpha}\right|,&\textrm{ if }s_{\ell,i}(x)\geq 0,\end{array}\right.

(see
[
9
, Eq. 8.9]
and
[
15
]
).
We set
\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2}
to satisfy
1\leq W_{\ell,i}(x)\leq 2

for every
\ell\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
and
i=1,2
. In this way,
the functional
\Phi
is equivalent to the
L^{1}
-distance.

Exactly the same calculations as in
[
9
, Chapter 8]

imply that, for every
t>0
when no interaction happens,

(4.24)

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Phi(u(t),\tilde{u}(t))\leq C\varepsilon.

If
t>0
is an interaction time for
u
or
\tilde{u}
, then

Corollary

4.6
,
Corollary

4.8
, and

Corollary

4.11
imply that

(4.25)

\Delta[J_{0}(u(t))+J_{0}(\tilde{u}(t))]<0,

and so, choosing
\kappa_{2}
large enough, we obtain

(4.26)

\Delta\Phi(u(t),\tilde{u}(t))<0.

Thus, for every
0\leq s\leq t
, we obtain that

(4.27)

\Phi(u(t),\tilde{u}(t))-\Phi(u(s),\tilde{u}(s))\leq C\varepsilon(t-s),

proving
3.1-(iii)
.

In particular, from this Lipschitz dependence result, it is standard to deduce the semigroup property claimed in
3.1-(ii)
.

4.7.
Decay of the Glimm-type functional

In this part we prove that the Glimm-type functional
J_{\gamma}
, for
\gamma>0
,
has an exponential-in-time decay, similarly as in
[
17
, Lemma 3.2]
.
Let a time
\bar{t}
be fixed; we consider the variation of the functional
J_{\gamma}
locally around
\bar{t}
according to four cases:

(1)

there are no waves’ interactions at
t=\bar{t}
inside
the pipes nor an interaction of a wave with the junction or the
external boundary;

(2)

there is an interaction between two waves inside a pipe
at
t=\bar{t}
;

(3)

there is a wave hitting the external boundary at time

t=\bar{t}
;

(4)

there is a wave hitting the junction at time
t=\bar{t}
.

In the latter three cases, the estimates in
Section

4.3
imply that
\Delta J_{\gamma}(\bar{t})\leq 0
provided

V_{\gamma}
sufficiently small. Hence we focus only on the first case,
where no interaction happens.

Lemma 4.14
.

Assume that at the time
\bar{t}
no interactions of any type
happen. Then the functional
J_{\gamma}
, defined in (
4.10
),
is differentiable and it holds

(4.28)

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}J_{\gamma}(\bar{t})\leq-c\gamma J_{\gamma}(\bar{t}).

Proof.

Assume that there is no any interactions at time
t=\bar{t}
. Since there is a finite number of fronts
at time
\bar{t}
, then there exists a neighborhood
\mathcal{U}
of
\bar{t}
in which no interaction happens. Hence, in
\mathcal{U}
,
V_{\gamma}(t)
and
Q_{\gamma}
are smooth functional.

First compute

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}V_{\gamma}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{J}_{\ell}}\Big(2K_{J}|\sigma_{\ell,1,\alpha}|e^{\gamma x_{\alpha}(t)}\gamma\dot{x}_{\alpha}(t)-|\sigma_{\ell,2,\alpha}|e^{-\gamma x_{\alpha}(t)}\gamma\dot{x}_{\alpha}(t)\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{J}_{\ell}}\Big(-2c\gamma K_{J}|\sigma_{\ell,1,\alpha}|e^{\gamma x_{\alpha}(t)}-c\gamma|\sigma_{\ell,2,\alpha}|e^{-\gamma x_{\alpha}(t)}\Big)

\displaystyle=-c\gamma\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{J}_{\ell}}\Big(2K_{J}|\sigma_{\ell,1,\alpha}|e^{\gamma x_{\alpha}(t)}+|\sigma_{\ell,2,\alpha}|e^{-\gamma x_{\alpha}(t)}\Big)=-c\gamma V_{\gamma}(t).

Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}Q_{\gamma}^{1,1}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\sum_{(\sigma_{\ell,1,\alpha},\sigma_{\ell,1,\beta})\in\mathcal{A}_{\ell}}\left|\sigma_{\ell,1,\alpha}\sigma_{\ell,1,\beta}\right|e^{\gamma(x_{\alpha}(t)+x_{\beta}(t))}\gamma(\dot{x}_{\alpha}(t)+\dot{x}_{\beta}(t))

\displaystyle\leq-c\gamma\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\sum_{(\sigma_{\ell,1,\alpha},\sigma_{\ell,1,\beta})\in\mathcal{A}_{\ell}}\left|\sigma_{\ell,1,\alpha}\sigma_{\ell,1,\beta}\right|e^{\gamma(x_{\alpha}(t)+x_{\beta}(t))}=-c\gamma Q_{\gamma}^{1,1}(t),

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}Q_{\gamma}^{2,2}(t)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\sum_{(\sigma_{\ell,2,\alpha},\sigma_{\ell,2,\beta})\in\mathcal{A}_{\ell}}\left|\sigma_{\ell,2,\alpha}\sigma_{\ell,2,\beta}\right|e^{-\gamma(x_{\alpha}(t)+x_{\beta}(t))}\gamma(\dot{x}_{\alpha}(t)+\dot{x}_{\beta}(t))

\displaystyle\leq-c\gamma\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\sum_{(\sigma_{\ell,2,\alpha},\sigma_{\ell,2,\beta})\in\mathcal{A}_{\ell}}\left|\sigma_{\ell,2,\alpha}\sigma_{\ell,2,\beta}\right|e^{-\gamma(x_{\alpha}(t)+x_{\beta}(t))}=-c\gamma Q_{\gamma}^{2,2}(t),

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}Q_{\gamma}^{1,2}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\sum_{(\sigma_{\ell,1,\alpha},\sigma_{\ell,2,\beta})\in\mathcal{A}_{\ell}}\left|\sigma_{\ell,1,\alpha}\sigma_{\ell,2,\beta}\right|e^{\gamma(x_{\alpha}(t)-x_{\beta}(t))}\gamma(\dot{x}_{\alpha}(t)-\dot{x}_{\beta}(t))

\displaystyle\leq-c\gamma\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\sum_{(\sigma_{\ell,1,\alpha},\sigma_{\ell,2,\beta})\in\mathcal{A}_{\ell}}\left|\sigma_{\ell,1,\alpha}\sigma_{\ell,2,\beta}\right|e^{\gamma(x_{\alpha}(t)-x_{\beta}(t))}=-c\gamma Q_{\gamma}^{1,2}(t),

and so

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}Q_{\gamma}(t)\leq-c\gamma Q_{\gamma}(t).

Therefore, we deduce that

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}J_{\gamma}(t)\leq-c\gamma J_{\gamma}(t),

concluding the proof.
∎

Corollary 4.15
.

Fix
\gamma>0
.
Then the functional
J_{\gamma}
,
defined in (
4.10
),
satisfies the inequality

(4.29)

J_{\gamma}(t)\leq e^{-c\gamma}J_{\gamma}(0^{+}).

The proof is immediate and so we omit it. This completes the proof of
3.1-(v)
.

Putting together all the results obtained in the previous sections, we conclude the proof of
Theorem

3.1
.

Li-Yau gradient estimates on closed manifolds under Bakry-Émery Ricci curvature conditions
XingYu Song, Ling Wu

Abstract
. In this paper, motivated by the work of Qi S. Zhang in [ 28 ] , we derive Li-Yau gradient bounds for positive solutions of the f f -heat equation on closed manifolds with Bakry-Émery Ricci curvature bounded below.



1.
Introduction

Let
(M^{n},g,e^{-f}dv)
be a complete smooth metric measure space, where
(M^{n},g)
is an
n
-dimensional complete Riemannian manifold,
dv
is the volume element of
g
,
f
is a smooth function on
M
(called the potential function), and
e^{-f}dv
(for short,
d\mu
) is called the weighted volume element. The

m
-Bakry-Émery Ricci curvature (
[
1
]
,
[
13
]
) associated to
(M^{n},g,e^{-f}dv)
is defined by

Ric^{m,n}_{f}\ :=Ric+Hessf-\frac{1}{m-n}df\otimes df\ (m>n),

where
Ric
is the Ricci curvature of
(M^{n},g)
,
Hess
is the Hessian with respect to the metric
g
. The
m
-Bakry-Émery Ricci curvature is a natural generalization of Ricci curvature on Riemannian manifolds. When
m=\infty
, we denote

Ric_{f}=Ric^{\infty,n}_{f}=Ric+Hessf,

which is called the Bakry-Émery Ricci curvature (
[
1
]
). Manifolds with constant Bakry-Émery Ricci curvature are so called gradient Ricci solitons, which play a crucial role in the singularity analysis of the Ricci flow (
[
3
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
14
]
,
[
17
]
). With respect to the weighted volume element the natural self-adjoint Laplacian operator is the
f
-Laplacian

\Delta_{f}=\Delta-\left<\nabla f,\nabla\right>.

The
f
-heat equation is defined as

(\Delta_{f}-\partial_{t})u=0.

In
[
11
]
, Li and Yau showed that if
(M^{n},g)
is a complete Riemannian manifold with
Ric\ \geq-K
for some constant
K\geq 0
, then for any positive solution
u
of the heat equation
(\Delta-\partial_{t})u=0
, we have

\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u^{2}}-\alpha\frac{\partial_{t}u}{u}\leq\frac{n\alpha^{2}}{2t}+\frac{n\alpha^{2}K}{2(\alpha-1)},\ \forall\alpha>1,\ t>0.

(1.1)

In particular, when
Ric\ \geq 0
, one obtains the optimal Li-Yau bound

\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u^{2}}-\frac{\partial_{t}u}{u}\leq\frac{n}{2t}.

(1.2)

Many applications of (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) have been demonstrated, including the parabolic Harnack inequality, optimal Guassian estimates of the heat kernel, estimates of eigenvalues of the Laplace operator, and estimates of the Green’s function. Moreover, (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) can even imply the Laplacian Comparison Theorem (see e.g.
[
6
]
page 394).

The estimate (
1.2
) is sharp since the equality is achieved by the heat kernel of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. However, (
1.1
) is not sharp for
K>0
. An open question asks if we can find sharp Li-Yau-type gradient estimates for
K>0
. Many works were done to improve or generalize (
1.1
).

In
[
9
]
, Hamilton discovered Li-Yau-type bound for the heat equation

\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u^{2}}-e^{2Kt}\frac{\partial_{t}u}{u}\leq e^{4Kt}\frac{n}{2t}.

(1.3)

In
[
23
]
, Yau obtained the following estimate

\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u^{2}}-\frac{\partial_{t}u}{u}\leq\frac{n}{2t}+\sqrt{2nK}\sqrt{\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u^{2}}+\frac{n}{2t}+2nK}.

(1.4)

In
[
2
]
, Bakry and Qian obtained

\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u^{2}}-\left(1+\frac{2}{3}Kt\right)\frac{\partial_{t}u}{u}\leq\frac{n}{2t}+\frac{nK}{2}\left(1+\frac{K}{3}t\right).

(1.5)

In
[
15
]
, Bin Qian improved the estimate (
1.5
) in the following form

\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u^{2}}-\left(1+\frac{2K}{a(t)}\int_{0}^{t}a(s)ds\right)\frac{\partial_{t}u}{u}\leq\frac{nK}{2}+\frac{nK^{2}}{2a(t)}\int_{0}^{t}a(s)ds+\frac{n}{8a(t)}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{a^{\prime}(s)^{2}}{a(s)}ds,

(1.6)

where
a(t):(0,\infty)\rightarrow(0,\infty)
is any
C^{1}
positive function that satisfies the following two conditions:

(A1) For all
t>0
,
a(t)>0
,
a^{\prime}(t)>0
and
\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow 0}a(t)=\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow 0}\frac{a(t)}{a^{\prime}(t)}=0
.

(A2) For any
L>0
,
\frac{a^{\prime}(t)^{2}}{a(t)}
is continuous and integrable on the interval
[0,L]
.

In the above results, the constant
\alpha
in (
1.1
) is replaced by functions in the form of
\alpha(t,K)
which is strictly greater than
1
but converges to
1
as
t\rightarrow 0
. Recently, Qi S. Zhang
[
28
]
obtains that for all closed manifolds one can take
\alpha=1
for
K\ \geq 0
.
It shows that if
(M^{n},g)
is a closed Riemannian manifold with
Ric\ \geq-K
for
K\geq 0
, and
diam_{M}
is the diameter of
M
, then for any positive solution
u
of the heat equation
(\Delta-\partial_{t})u=0
,

t(\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u^{2}}-\frac{\partial_{t}u}{u})\leq\frac{n}{2}+\sqrt{2nK(1+Kt)(1+t)}diam_{M}+\sqrt{K(1+Kt)(C_{1}t+C_{2}Kt)},

(1.7)

where
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are positive constants only depending on
n
.
It is an improvement on Li-Yau gradient bound (
1.1
) on closed manifolds.

In (
[
29
]
,
[
30
]
), Qi S. Zhang and Meng Zhu obtained Li-Yau-type gradient estimates under integral curvature assumptions. Moreover, Li-Yau-type bounds were also got for weighted manifolds with Bakry-Émery Ricci curvature bounded below
[
12
]
. More information about Li-Yau-type bounds can be found in (
[
8
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
18
]
,
[
27
]
,
[
25
]
,
[
24
]
,
[
26
]
).

In this paper, we show the optimal Li-Yau gradient bounds for
f
-heat equation on closed manifolds with either Bakry-Émery Ricci curvature or
m
-Bakry-Émery Ricci curvature bounded below, which both generalize (
1.7
).

More precisely, we show that

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a closed Riemannian manifold with
Ric_{f}\geq-K
and
|\nabla f|\leq L
for some constants
K,L\geq 0
. Let
u
be a positive solution of the f-heat equation on
M\times(0,+\infty)
, i.e.,
(\Delta_{f}-\partial_{t})u=0
, and
diam_{M}
the diameter of
M
. Then there exists a contant
c
depending only on
n
, such that

\displaystyle t(\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u^{2}}-\frac{\partial_{t}u}{u})\leq

\displaystyle\frac{n}{2}+c(L+\sqrt{K})\sqrt{(1+Kt)(1+t)}diam_{M}

(1.8)

\displaystyle+c(L+\sqrt{K})\sqrt{(1+Kt)(t+Kt+L^{2}t+A^{2}t+A^{2}Kt)},

where
A=\sup\limits_{x\in M}|f(x)|
.

Notice that (
1.8
) still holds when we add
f
by any constant. In particular, for some point
o\in M
we may choose
f
such that
f(o)
equals zero. Then (
1.8
) becomes

\displaystyle t(\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u^{2}}-\frac{\partial_{t}u}{u})\leq

\displaystyle\frac{n}{2}+c(L+\sqrt{K})\sqrt{(1+Kt)(1+t)}diam_{M}

(1.9)

\displaystyle+c(L+\sqrt{K})\sqrt{(1+Kt)(t+Kt+L^{2}t+L^{2}diam_{M}^{2}t+L^{2}diam_{M}^{2}Kt)}.

If the potential function is constant, then from (
1.9
) we get a result similar to (
1.7
).

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a closed Rimannian manifold with
Ric\geq-K
where
K\geq 0
. Let
u
be a positive solution of the heat equation on
M\times(0,+\infty)
, i.e.,
(\Delta-\partial_{t})u=0
, and
diam_{M}
the diameter of
M
. Then there exists
a constant
c
depending only on
n
, such that

t(\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u^{2}}-\frac{\partial_{t}u}{u})\leq\frac{n}{2}+c\sqrt{K(1+Kt)(1+t)}diam_{M}+c\sqrt{K(1+Kt)(t+Kt)}.

(1.10)

For closed manifolds with
m
-Bakry-Émery Ricci curvature bounded below, we have a similar result. But there is no assumption on the potential function and the constants depend on
m
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a closed Riemannian manifold with
Ric^{m,n}_{f}\geq-K
for some constant
K\geq 0
. Let
u
be a positive solution of the f-heat equation on
M\times(0,+\infty)
, i.e.,
(\Delta_{f}-\partial_{t})u=0
, and
diam_{M}
the diameter of
M
. Then there exist contants
\widetilde{c_{1}}\ and\ \widetilde{c_{2}}
depending only on
m
such that

t(\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u^{2}}-\frac{\partial_{t}u}{u})\leq\frac{m}{2}+\sqrt{2mK(1+Kt)(\widetilde{c_{1}}+t)}diam_{M}+\widetilde{c_{2}}\sqrt{K(1+Kt)(t+Kt)}.

(1.11)

If the potential function is constant, then we can take
m=n
and get a result similar to Corollary
1.2
.

Remark 1.4
.

The constants
c
,
\widetilde{c_{1}}\ and\ \widetilde{c_{2}}
in Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.3
arise from the volume comparison theorem and upper and lower bounds for the
f
-heat kernel. Since the lower bound constants of the
f
-heat kernel cannot be written accurately, the constants
c

\widetilde{c_{1}}
cannot be written as accurately as Qi S. Zhang does.

We prove Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.3
separately in sections 2 and 3. The proofs follow a method of Qi S. Zhang
[
28
]
. It is sufficient to prove the optimal Li-Yau bound for the
f
-heat kernel, then the same bound holds for all positive solutions of the
f
-heat equation. The main tools in the proof of Theorem
1.1
are relative volume comparison
[
19
]
of Guofang Wei and Will Wylie, Yi Li’s Hamilton type estimates
[
12
]
, the upper and lower bounds for the
f
-heat kernel
[
22
]
and the Harnack inequality for the positive solutions of the
f
-heat equation
[
22
]
proved by Jiayong Wu and Peng Wu for the manifolds with Bakry-Émery Ricci curvature bounded below. Here we generally assume that the potential function is bounded or the gradient of the function is bounded. The proof of Theorem
1.3
is similar to Theorem
1.1
. However, in Theorem
1.3
for manifolds with
m
-Bakry-Émery Ricci curvature bounded below, there is no assumption on potential function and relative results can be found in (
[
4
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
19
]
).

\frac{\sup_{y\in M}V_{f}(B_{y}(\sqrt{2t_{0}}))}{\inf_{x\in M}V_{f}(B_{x}(\sqrt{t_{0}}))}=\frac{V_{f}(B_{q}(\sqrt{2t_{0}}))}{V_{f}(B_{p}(\sqrt{t_{0}}))}.

(2.12)

Here is the ratio of geodesic balls’s volumes at different points. Notice that the relationship between the geodesic ball
B_{q}(\sqrt{2t_{0}})
and the geodesic ball
B_{p}(\sqrt{2t_{0}}+d(p,q))
is
B_{q}(\sqrt{2t_{0}})\subset B_{p}(\sqrt{2t_{0}}+d(p,q))
. Then applying the volume comparison theorem (Lemma
2.2
), we get

\displaystyle\frac{V_{f}(B_{p}(\sqrt{2t_{0}}+d(p,q)))}{V_{f}(B_{p}(\sqrt{2t_{0}}))}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\int_{0}^{d(p,q)+\sqrt{2t_{0}}}\left(sinh\left(\sqrt{\frac{K}{n-1}}r\right)\right)^{n+4A-1}dr}{\int_{0}^{\sqrt{2t_{0}}}\left(sinh\left(\sqrt{\frac{K}{n-1}}r\right)\right)^{n+4A-1}dr}

(2.13)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{d(p,q)}{\sqrt{2t_{0}}}+1\right)^{n+4A}e^{d(p,q)\sqrt{\frac{K}{n-1}}(n+4A-1)}

\displaystyle=e^{(n+4A)\ln(\frac{d(p,q)}{\sqrt{2t_{0}}}+1)+d(p,q)\sqrt{\frac{K}{n-1}}(n+4A-1)}

\displaystyle\leq e^{(n+4A)\frac{d(p,q)}{\sqrt{2t_{0}}}+d(p,q)\sqrt{\frac{K}{n-1}}(n+4A-1)},

and

\displaystyle\frac{V_{f}(B_{p}(\sqrt{2t_{0}}))}{V_{f}(B_{p}(\sqrt{t_{0}}))}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\int_{0}^{\sqrt{2t_{0}}}\left(sinh\left(\sqrt{\frac{K}{n-1}}r\right)\right)^{n+4A-1}dr}{\int_{0}^{\sqrt{t_{0}}}\left(sinh\left(\sqrt{\frac{K}{n-1}}r\right)\right)^{n+4A-1}dr}

(2.14)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\left(\frac{n+4A}{2}\right)}e^{[(\sqrt{2}-1)\sqrt{t_{0}}\sqrt{\frac{K}{n-1}}](n+4A-1)}.

Substituting (
2.13
) and (
2.14
) into (
2.12
), we find

\displaystyle\frac{\sup_{y\in M}V_{f}(B_{y}(\sqrt{2t_{0}}))}{\inf_{x\in M}V_{f}(B_{x}(\sqrt{t_{0}}))}

\displaystyle=\frac{V_{f}(B_{q}(\sqrt{2t_{0}}))}{V_{f}(B_{p}(\sqrt{t_{0}}))}\leq\frac{V_{f}(B_{p}(\sqrt{2t_{0}}+d(p,q)))}{V_{f}(B_{p}(\sqrt{t_{0}}))}

\displaystyle=\frac{V_{f}(B_{p}(\sqrt{2t_{0}}+d(p,q)))}{V_{f}(B_{p}(\sqrt{2t_{0}}))}\frac{V_{f}(B_{p}(\sqrt{2t_{0}}))}{V_{f}(B_{p}(\sqrt{t_{0}}))}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\left(\frac{n+4A}{2}\right)}e^{[(\sqrt{2}-1)\sqrt{t_{0}}\sqrt{\frac{K}{n-1}}](n+4A-1)+{(n+4A)\frac{d(p,q)}{\sqrt{2t_{0}}}+d(p,q)\sqrt{\frac{K}{n-1}}(n+4A-1)}}.

This and (
2.11
) imply that

\displaystyle\ln\frac{B}{H(x,t+t_{0},y)}\leq\ln C_{7}+C_{2}A+C_{3}\sqrt{2Kt_{0}}+C_{3}A\sqrt{2Kt_{0}}+2C_{5}L^{2}t_{0}+2C_{5}Kt_{0}

\displaystyle+\frac{d^{2}(x,y)}{C_{6}t_{0}}+\frac{nd(p,q)}{\sqrt{2t_{0}}}+2\sqrt{2}A\frac{d(p,q)}{\sqrt{t_{0}}}+d(p,q)\sqrt{K(n-1)}+4d(p,q)A\sqrt{\frac{K}{n-1}}

\displaystyle+\sqrt{K(n-1)t_{0}}+4A\sqrt{\frac{Kt_{0}}{n-1}}+\frac{\ln 2}{2}(n+4A).

Using the following basic inequalities with

\sqrt{Kt_{0}}\leq\frac{1}{4}+Kt_{0},\ \frac{d(p,q)}{\sqrt{t_{0}}}\leq\frac{d^{2}(p,q)}{t_{0}}+\frac{1}{4},\ \frac{Ad(p,q)}{\sqrt{t_{0}}}\leq\frac{d^{2}(p,q)}{t_{0}}+\frac{1}{4}A^{2},

d(p,q)\sqrt{K}\leq\frac{d^{2}(p,q)}{t_{0}}+\frac{1}{4}Kt_{0},\ Ad(p,q)\sqrt{K}\leq\frac{d^{2}(p,q)}{t_{0}}+\frac{1}{4}A^{2}Kt_{0},\ \ A\sqrt{Kt_{0}}\leq\frac{1}{4}A^{2}+Kt_{0},

we get

\ln\frac{B}{H(x,t+t_{0},y)}\leq C_{8}\left(1+A+Kt_{0}+AKt_{0}+L^{2}t_{0}+\frac{diam_{M}^{2}}{t_{0}}+A^{2}+A^{2}Kt_{0}\right),\ \forall t\in(0,t_{0}].

Using
A\leq\frac{1}{4}+A^{2}
gives that

\ln\frac{B}{H(x,t+t_{0},y)}\leq C_{9}\left(1+Kt_{0}+L^{2}t_{0}+\frac{diam_{M}^{2}}{t_{0}}+A^{2}+A^{2}Kt_{0}\right).

Hamilton type estimate (Lemma
2.1
) implies that

t|\nabla_{x}\ln H(x,t+t_{0},y)|^{2}\leq C_{9}(1+2Kt_{0})\left(1+Kt_{0}+L^{2}t_{0}+\frac{diam_{M}^{2}}{t_{0}}+A^{2}+A^{2}Kt_{0}\right),\ t\in(0,t_{0}].

We take
t=t_{0}
and use the arbitrariness of
t_{0}
to conclude

\begin{aligned} t|\nabla_{x}\ln H(x,t,y)|^{2}\leq C_{10}(1+Kt)\left(1+Kt+L^{2}t+\frac{diam_{M}^{2}}{t}+A^{2}+A^{2}Kt\right),\ \forall t>0\end{aligned}.

(2.15)

The bound is adequate for us when the time is short, say
t\leq 4
. When
t
is large, the
f
-heat kernel converges to the positive constant
\frac{1}{V_{f}(M)}
, where
V_{f}(M)
is the weighted volume of
M
. In this case the above bound becomes inaccurate. Instead we will use a better bound based on the Harnack inequality (
2.2
).

Pick any time
t\geq 4
. Since
\int_{M}H(x,t+1,y)d\mu(x)=1
, there is a point
x_{1}\in M
such that
H(x_{1},t+1,y)=\frac{1}{V_{f}(M)}
. According to (
2.2
), there exists a dimensional constant
C_{0}>0
such that

H(x,t,y)\leq H(x_{1},t+1,y)e^{C_{0}(L^{2}+K+\frac{1}{t}+d^{2}(x,x_{1}))}.

Since
t\geq 4
, this implies

H(x,t,y)\leq\frac{1}{V_{f}(M)}e^{C_{0}(L^{2}+K+\frac{1}{4}+diam_{M}^{2})}:=B.

(2.16)

Similarly, there is a point
x_{2}
such that
H(x_{2},t-1,y)=\frac{1}{V_{f}(M)}
and that

H(x_{2},t-1,y)\leq H(x,t,y)e^{C_{0}(L^{2}+K+\frac{1}{t-1}+d^{2}(x,x_{2}))},

which infers

H(x,t,y)\geq\frac{1}{V_{f}(M)}e^{-C_{0}(L^{2}+K+\frac{1}{3}+diam_{M}^{2})}.

(2.17)

Using (
2.16
) and (
2.17
), we find, for
t_{0}\geq 4
, that

\ln\frac{B}{H(x,t+t_{0},y)}\leq 2C_{0}(L^{2}+K+1+diam^{2}_{M}),\ t\in(0,t_{0}].

This and Lemma
2.1
yield

t|\nabla_{x}\ln H(x,t+t_{0},y)|^{2}\leq 2C_{0}(1+2Kt_{0})(L^{2}+K+1+diam^{2}_{M}),\ t\in(0,t_{0}].

Therefore

\displaystyle t|\nabla_{x}\ln H(x,t,y)|^{2}\leq 4C_{0}(1+Kt)(L^{2}+K+1+diam^{2}_{M}),\ t\geq 4.

(2.18)

Next plugging (
2.15
) for
t<4
and (
2.18
) for
t\geq 4
into the term
T_{3}
and
T_{4}
in (
2.9
) with
u=H(x,t,y)
, we obtain

\displaystyle T_{3}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{2}{n}L^{2}+2K\right)\left(\int_{0}^{4}\int_{M}|\nabla\ln u|^{2}t^{2}((t-\epsilon)^{+}Y^{+})^{2j}d\mu dt\right)

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{2}{n}L^{2}+2K\right)\left(\int_{4}^{T}\int_{M}|\nabla\ln u|^{2}t^{2}((t-\epsilon)^{+}Y^{+})^{2j}d\mu dt\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{11}\left(L^{2}+K\right)(1+KT)(T+KT+L^{2}T+A^{2}T+A^{2}KT+diam^{2}_{M}

\displaystyle+Tdiam^{2}_{M})\int_{0}^{T}\int_{M}((t-\epsilon)^{+}Y^{+})^{2j}d\mu dt,

and

\displaystyle T_{4}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4}{n}L\int_{0}^{4}\int_{M}t|\nabla\ln u|((t-\epsilon)^{+}Y^{+})^{2j+1}d\mu dt+\frac{4}{n}L\int_{4}^{T}\int_{M}t|\nabla\ln u|((t-\epsilon)^{+}Y^{+})^{2j+1}d\mu dt

\displaystyle\leq C_{12}L\sqrt{(1+KT)(T+KT+L^{2}T+A^{2}T+A^{2}KT+diam^{2}_{M}+Tdiam^{2}_{M})}

\displaystyle\times\int_{0}^{T}\int_{M}((t-\epsilon)^{+}Y^{+})^{2j+1}d\mu dt.

Denote
\lambda=\sqrt{(1+KT)(T+KT+L^{2}T+A^{2}T+A^{2}KT+diam^{2}_{M}+Tdiam^{2}_{M})}
and (
2.9
) becomes

\displaystyle(

\displaystyle\frac{2}{n}-\frac{2}{2j+1})\int_{0}^{T}\int_{M}((t-\epsilon)^{+}Y^{+})^{2j+2}d\mu dt\leq\left(\frac{2j+2}{2j+1}+C_{12}L\lambda\right)\int_{0}^{T}\int_{M}((t-\epsilon)^{+}Y^{+})^{2j+1}d\mu dt

(2.19)

\displaystyle+C_{11}\left(L^{2}+K\right)\lambda^{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{M}((t-\epsilon)^{+}Y^{+})^{2j}d\mu dt.

Using the notation

A_{j,\epsilon}=\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{M}((t-\epsilon)^{+}Y^{+})^{j}d\mu dt\right)^{\frac{1}{j}},

we can write (
2.19
) as

\displaystyle(\frac{2}{n}-\frac{2}{2j+1})A_{2j+2,\epsilon}^{2j+2}\leq\left(\frac{2j+2}{2j+1}+C_{12}L\lambda\right)A_{2j+1,\epsilon}^{2j+1}+C_{11}\left(L^{2}+K\right)\lambda^{2}A_{2j,\epsilon}^{2j}.

(2.20)

By Hölder inequality,

A_{2j+1,\epsilon}^{2j+1}\leq A_{2j+2,\epsilon}^{2j+1}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{M}d\mu dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2j+2}},\ A_{2j,\epsilon}^{2j}\leq A_{2j+2,\epsilon}^{2j}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{M}d\mu dt\right)^{\frac{2}{2j+2}},

which imply, together with (
2.20
), that

\displaystyle(

\displaystyle\frac{2}{n}-\frac{2}{2j+1})A_{2j+2,\epsilon}^{2}\leq\left(\frac{2j+2}{2j+1}+C_{12}L\lambda\right)A_{2j+2,\epsilon}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{M}d\mu dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2j+2}}

(2.21)

\displaystyle+C_{11}\left(L^{2}+K\right)\lambda^{2}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{M}d\mu dt\right)^{\frac{2}{2j+2}}.

Letting
j\to\infty
, we arrive at

\displaystyle\frac{2}{n}A_{\infty,\epsilon}^{2}\leq\left(1+C_{12}L\lambda\right)A_{\infty,\epsilon}+C_{11}(L^{2}+K)\lambda^{2},

(2.22)

where
A_{\infty,\epsilon}=\sup\limits_{M\times[0,T]}(t-\epsilon)^{+}Y^{+}.

This shows

\displaystyle\sup_{M\times[0,T]}(t-\epsilon)^{+}Y^{+}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n}{2}+C_{13}(L+\sqrt{K})\sqrt{(1+KT)(1+T)}diam_{M}

(2.23)

\displaystyle+C_{13}(L+\sqrt{K})\sqrt{(1+KT)(T+KT+L^{2}T+A^{2}T+A^{2}KT)}.

Since
\epsilon>0
is arbitrary and
Y=-\Delta_{f}\ln H(x,t,y)
, we conclude that

\displaystyle t\left(\frac{|\nabla H|^{2}}{H^{2}}-\frac{\partial_{t}H}{H}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n}{2}+C_{13}(L+\sqrt{K})\sqrt{(1+Kt)(1+t)}diam_{M}

(2.24)

\displaystyle+C_{13}(L+\sqrt{K})\sqrt{(1+Kt)(t+Kt+L^{2}t+A^{2}t+A^{2}Kt)},\ \forall t>0.

From (
2.24
), a short argument from
[
28
]
(see also
[
26
]
) implies that the same bound actually holds if one replaces the
f
-heat kernel by any positive solution of the
f
-heat equation.

This completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

∎

3.
Li-Yau gradient bounds on closed manifolds under
m
-Bakry-Émery Ricci curvature conditions

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.3
. Since the proof of Theorem
1.3
is similar, so we only present the key steps.

Before starting the proof of Theorem
1.3
, let us present some results needed. First of all, Yi Li showed Li-Yau gradient estimate for manifolds with
m
-Bakry-Émery Ricci curvature bounded below.

Lemma 3.1
.

(
[
12
]
)(Li-Yau gradient estimate)
Let
(M^{n},g)
be a complete Riemannian manifold with
Ric^{m,n}_{f}\geq-K
for
K\geq 0
. Then any positive solution
u
of the f-heat equation
(\Delta_{f}-\partial_{t})u=0
on
M\times(0,T]

\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u^{2}}-\alpha\frac{u_{t}}{u}\leq\frac{m\alpha^{2}K}{\alpha-1}+\frac{m\alpha^{2}}{2t}

for any
\alpha>1
.

Based on the above Lemma, we can easily get the Harnack inequality.

Corollary 3.2
.

(Harnack inequality)
Under the same hypotheses as Lemma
3.1
, we have

u(x,t_{1})\leq u(y,t_{2})\left(\frac{t_{2}}{t_{1}}\right)^{\frac{m\alpha}{2}}e^{\left(\frac{\alpha d^{2}(x,y)}{4(t_{2}-t_{1})}+\frac{m\alpha K}{\alpha-1}(t_{2}-t_{1})\right)}

for all
x,y\in M
and
0<t_{1}<t_{2}\leq T
.

In
[
19
]
, Guofang Wei and Will Wylie proved the volume comparision for manifolds with
m
-Bakry-Émery Ricci curvature bounded below (see also
[
7
]
).

Lemma 3.3
.

(
[
19
]
)(volume comparison)
Let
(M^{n},g,e^{-f}dv)
be a complete Riemannian manifold with
Ric^{m,n}_{f}\geq-(m-1)H
for
H\geq 0
. Then
\frac{V_{f}(B_{p}(R))}{V^{m}_{H}(B(R))}
is nonincreasing in
R
.

In
[
4
]
, Nelia Charalambous, Zhiqin Lu and Julie Rowlett got the upper and lower bounds for the
f
-heat kernel.

Lemma 3.4
.

(
[
4
]
)
Let
(M^{n},g,e^{-f}dv)
be a complete Riemannian manifold with

on

where

M
and

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Let
u=u(x,t)
be a positive solution of the
f
-heat equation on
M\times(0,+\infty)
.
Write

Y=Y(x,t)=\left(\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u^{2}}-\frac{\partial_{t}u}{u}\right)=-\Delta_{f}\ln u.

The Bochner formula
[
12
]
for
Ric^{m,n}_{f}
is

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{f}|\nabla u|^{2}

\displaystyle=|Hess\ u|^{2}+\left<\nabla\Delta_{f}u,\nabla u\right>+Ric^{m,n}_{f}(\nabla u,\nabla u)+\frac{1}{m-n}|\left<\nabla f,\nabla u\right>|^{2}

(3.1)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(\Delta_{f}u)^{2}}{m}+\left<\nabla\Delta_{f}u,\nabla u\right>+Ric^{m,n}_{f}(\nabla u,\nabla u).

Using (
3.1
) and the
m
-Bakry-Émery Ricci curvature condition, we have

\displaystyle(\Delta_{f}-\partial_{t})Y+2\left<\nabla Y,\nabla\ln u\right>

\displaystyle=2|Hess\ln u|^{2}+2Ric_{f}^{m,n}(\nabla\ln u,\nabla\ln u)+\frac{2}{m-n}|\left<\nabla f,\nabla\ln u\right>|^{2}

(3.2)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2(\Delta_{f}\ln u)^{2}}{m}-2K|\nabla\ln u|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{m}Y^{2}-2K|\nabla\ln u|^{2}.

Let
Y^{+}(x,t)=\max\{Y(x,t),0\}
. Then inequality (
3.2
) implies that
Y^{+}
is a subsolution of the inequality in the weak sense: on
M\times(0,+\infty)
,

(\Delta_{f}-\partial_{t})Y^{+}+2\left<\nabla Y^{+},\nabla\ln u\right>\geq\frac{2}{m}(Y^{+})^{2}-2K|\nabla\ln u|^{2}.

(3.3)

For a positive integer

\epsilon
, multiplying both sides of (
3.3
) by

\displaystyle\frac{2}{m}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{M}((t-\epsilon)^{+}Y^{+})^{2j+2}d\mu dt\leq\int_{0}^{T}\int_{M}((t-\epsilon)^{+})^{2j+2}(Y^{+})^{2j}(\Delta_{f}-\partial_{t})Y^{+}d\mu dt

(3.4)

\displaystyle+2\int_{0}^{T}\int_{M}((t-\epsilon)^{+})^{2j+2}(Y^{+})^{2j}\left<\nabla Y^{+},\nabla\ln u\right>d\mu dt

\displaystyle+2K\int_{0}^{T}\int_{M}|\nabla\ln u|^{2}((t-\epsilon)^{+})^{2j+2}(Y^{+})^{2j}d\mu dt=T_{1}+T_{2}+T_{3}.

Using integration by parts as (
2.7
) and (
2.8

Using the upper and lower bounds (Lemma

2.1

For a time

, we consider the

with

According to Lemma

, say point

By lemma

\displaystyle\frac{V_{f}(B_{p}(\sqrt{2t_{0}}+d(p,q)))}{V_{f}(B_{p}(\sqrt{2t_{0}}))}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\int_{0}^{d(p,q)+\sqrt{2t_{0}}}\left(sinh(\sqrt{\frac{K}{m-1}}r)\right)^{m-1}dr}{\int_{0}^{\sqrt{2t_{0}}}\left(sinh(\sqrt{\frac{K}{m-1}}r)\right)^{m-1}dr}

\displaystyle=e^{m\ln\left(\frac{d(p,q)}{\sqrt{2t_{0}}}+1\right)+\sqrt{K(m-1)}d(p,q)}

\displaystyle\leq e^{m\frac{d(p,q)}{\sqrt{2t_{0}}}+\sqrt{K(m-1)}d(p,q)},

and

\frac{V_{f}(B_{p}(\sqrt{2t_{0}}))}{V_{f}(B_{p}(\sqrt{t_{0}}))}\leq\widetilde{C_{12}}e^{\widetilde{C_{11}}\sqrt{Kt_{0}}}.

This and (

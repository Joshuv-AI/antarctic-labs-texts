Northcott numbers for generalized weighted Weil heights
Masao Okazaki, Kaoru Sano

Abstract
. We give a generalization of weighted Weil heights. These heights generalize both Weil’s heights and Dobrowolski’s height. We study Northcott numbers for our heights. Our results generalize the authors’ former work on Vidaux and Videla’s question about the Northcott number. As an application, we evaluate Northcott numbers for Talamanca’s spectral height on matrices.

Primary 11G50, Secondary 11C20, 15B33

1.
Introduction

Let
S
be a set and
\mathfrak{h}\colon S\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be a function.
We set

B(S,\mathfrak{h},C)=\left\{s\in S\mid\mathfrak{h}(s)<C\right\}

for each
C\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
.
We also set

\Nor(S,\mathfrak{h})=\inf\left\{C\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}\mid\#B(S,\mathfrak{h},C)=\infty\right\}.

The non-negative real number
\Nor(S,\mathfrak{h})
is called the
\mathfrak{h}
-Northcott number of
S
,
introduced in
[
PTW22
]
and
[
VV16
]
.
Here note that we regard the value
\inf\emptyset
to be
\infty
.
It is said that
S
has the
\mathfrak{h}
-Northcott property
or
\mathfrak{h}
-(N) for short
(resp.
\mathfrak{h}
-Bogomolov property
or
\mathfrak{h}
-(B) for short)
if the equality
\Nor(S,\mathfrak{h})=\infty
(resp. the inequality
\Nor(S\setminus Z(S,\mathfrak{h}),\mathfrak{h})>0
) holds, where

Z(S,\mathfrak{h})=\{a\in S\mid\mathfrak{h}(a)=0\}

(see
[
BZ01
]
and
[
PTW22
]
).
Note that
\mathfrak{h}
-(B) follows from
\mathfrak{h}
-(N).

In this paper, we fix an algebraic closure
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
of
\mathbb{Q}
and consider any algebraic extension of
\mathbb{Q}
to be a subset of
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
.
Let
h\colon\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be the absolute (logarithmic) Weil height.
We will recall the definition of
h
in Section
2
.
In number theory, it is a basic problem to calculate the
h
-Northcott numbers.
Vidaux and Videla gave the following.

Question 1.1

(
[
VV16
, Question 6]
)
.

Which real numbers can be realized as
\Nor(L,h)
for some field
L\subset\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
?

On the basis of the former works
[
Wid11
]
and
[
PTW22
]
, the authors generalized Question
1.1
and answered to it in
[
OS22
]
.

Theorem 1.2

(
[
OS22
, Theorem 1.4]
)
.

Let
c
be a positive real number and
\gamma
a real number with
\gamma<1
.
Then we can construct a field
L\subset\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
satisfying the equality

\Nor(L,h_{\gamma})=c,

where
h_{\gamma}(a)=\deg(a)^{\gamma}h(a)

(
a\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
,
\deg(a)=[\mathbb{Q}(a):\mathbb{Q}]
)
is the
\gamma
-weighted Weil height, introduced in
[
PTW22
]
.

Remark 1.3
.

The case
\gamma=0
of Theorem
1.2
answers Question
1.1
.
In
[
Hul23
]
, Hultberg applied this result to the finiteness of cycles of small height on projective varieties.

In this paper, we introduce
w
-weighted heights
h^{w}
that further generalize the
\gamma
-weighted Weil heights.
The purpose of this paper is to generalize Theorem
1.2
to our heights
h^{w}
.
We say that a function
w\colon\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{>0}
ia a
weight
if it is eventually non-increasing or non-decreasing.
For a weight
w
and
a\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
, we set

h^{w}(a)=w(\deg(a))h(a).

We call
h^{w}

the
w
-weighted Weil height
.
We remark that the
\gamma
-weighted Weil height
h_{\gamma}
coincides with
h^{w_{\gamma}}
, where
w_{\gamma}(d)=d^{\gamma}
.
Another remarkable example is Dobrowolski’s height.
For the weight

w_{\text{D}}(d)=\left(\frac{\log^{+}(d)}{\log^{+}(\log(d))}\right)^{3}d,

Dobrowolski in
[
Dob79
]
proved that
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
has
h^{w_{\text{D}}}
-(B), where
\log^{+}(\cdot)=\max\{1,\log(\cdot)\}
.
A merit to introduce our generalized heights is the fact that for any infinite algebraic extension of
\mathbb{Q}
,
there exists a weight
w
such that
\Nor(L\setminus\mu,h^{w})
is a positive real number,
where
\mu
is the set of roots of unity in
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
(see Corollary
3.5
).

In addition, we address not only fields but also sets of algebraic numbers of bounded degree:
For a field
L\subset\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
and
N\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, we set

L^{(N)}=\left\{a\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\mid[L(a):L]\leq N\right\}.

Our motivations to focus on the sets
L^{(N)}
are a number of topics regarding the Northcott property and the Bogomolov property
(
e.g.
,
[
ADZ14
]
,
[
AZ00
]
,
[
Dob79
]
,
[
DZ08
, Theorem 2.1]
,
[
Sil07
, Conjecture 3.24]
).

Our main result is the following.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
c
be a positive real number,
N
a positive integer, and
w
a weight with the following two conditions.

(1)

lim

d
→
∞

w
(
d
)log(
d
)/
d
=0
.

(2)

w
(
d
)/
d is eventually non-increasing.

Then we can construct a field
L\subset\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
satisfying the equality

\Nor(L^{(N)},h^{w})=c.

Remark 1.5
.

Both conditions (
1
) and (
2
) in Theorem
1.4

demand that a weight
w
is smaller enough than the weight
w_{1}(d)=d
.
Here, we refer to Lehmer’s conjecture, which asserts that
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
has
h^{w_{1}}
-(B).

Remark 1.6
.

If
N=1
or the limit
\lim_{d\rightarrow\infty}w(d)
converges to a positive real number,
we can remove the condition (

) as shown in the proof of Theorem

.

As an application of Theorem
1.4
, we study Northcott numbers of matrices.
Let
h_{\rm s}
be the spectral height on
\M_{n}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
, introduced in
[
Tal00
]
.
The height
h_{\rm s}
is obtained from Tate’s limiting process
starting with the operator height
h_{\rm op}
(see Section

for the definitions of
h_{\rm s}
and
h_{\rm op}
).
Hence,
h_{\rm s}
is an analogue of the canonical heights on abelian varieties.
Now, we should refer to the following Talamanca’s work.

Theorem 1.7

(
[
Tal00
, Theorem 4.2]
)
.

For each
A\in{\rm M}_{n}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
, we denote by
(\lambda_{1}^{(A)},\ldots,\lambda_{n}^{(A)})
the
n
-tuple formed by the all eigenvalues of
A
.
Then, we have the equality

h_{\rm s}(A)=h(\lambda_{1}^{(A)},\ldots,\lambda_{n}^{(A)}),

where
h
is the Weil height on
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})\cup\{\bm{0}\}

(
for the definition, see Section
2
)
.

Combining the product formula in Section
2
, to study
h_{\rm s}
-Northcott numbers,
we should consider
h_{\rm s}
to be a function on the set
\Conj(\M_{n}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}))
.
Here, we set

\Conj(\M_{n}(L))=\M_{n}(L)/\mathord{\sim}_{\rm conj}

for each field
L\subset\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
, where
A\mathbin{\sim}_{\rm conj}B
(
A,B\in\M_{n}(L)
) means that
there exist
P\in\GL_{n}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
and
c\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times}
such that
B=c(P^{-1}AP)
.
We denote by
[A]_{\rm conj}
the class of
A\in\M_{n}(L)
in
\Conj(\M_{n}(L))
.
We also set

\Conj(\GL_{n}(L))=\{[A]_{\rm conj}\in\Conj(\M_{n}(L))\mid A\in\GL_{n}(L)\}.

We address
\Conj(\M_{n}(L))
motivated by thinking about
the
\PGL_{n}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
-conjugacy class of rational self-maps on the projective space
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
.
It is natural to ask about the existence of inequality relations among
\Nor(L,h)
,
\Nor(\Conj(\M_{n}(L)),h_{\rm s})
, and
\Nor(\Conj(\GL_{n}(L)),h_{\rm s})
.
More precisely, we want to find inequalities

\displaystyle\hphantom{=}c_{1}\Nor(L,h)\leq\Nor(\Conj(\M_{n}(L),h_{\rm s})

(1.1)

\displaystyle\leq\Nor(\Conj(\GL_{n}(L),h_{\rm s})\leq c_{2}\Nor(L,h)

(1.2)

for some
c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
.
However, the first author proved the following proposition.

Proposition 1.8

(
[
Oka19-1
, Theorem 1]
)
.

We have the inequalities

\Nor(\mathbb{Q}^{\rm tr},h)>\Nor(\Conj(\M_{n}(\mathbb{Q}^{\rm tr})),h_{\rm s})=0,

where
\mathbb{Q}^{\rm tr}
is the field of totally real numbers.

Remark 1.9
.

The fact that
\mathbb{Q}^{\rm tr}
has
h
-(B) was first proven in
[
Sch73
]
.

Proposition
1.8
reveals that it is impossible to seek for inequalities (
1.2
) for a general field
L\subset\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
.
Hence we will attempt to give an example of a field
L\subset\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
satisfying inequalities (
1.2
).
As we did for
h
, we address this problem not only for
h_{\rm s}
but also the
w
-weighted spectral height
h_{\rm s}^{w}

(for the definition, see Section
5
).
In Section
5
, for a weight
w
with several conditions, we will give a field
L\subset\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
and
c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
satisfying the inequalities

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle<c_{1}\Nor(L,h^{w})\leq\Nor(\Conj(\M_{n}(L),h_{\rm s}^{w})

(1.3)

\displaystyle\leq\Nor(\Conj(\GL_{n}(L),h_{\rm s}^{w})\leq c_{2}\Nor(L,h^{w})<\infty

(1.4)

by using results on Theorem

.

2.
A quick review on Weil heights

In this section, we summarize the definitions and basic properties of the Weil heights (see
[
HS00
]
for details).
We will frequently use them in the latter sections without mentioning them.

Let
K
be a number field.
For each
v\in\mathcal{M}_{K}
, we fix the absolute value
|\cdot|_{v}\colon K\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
such that the product formula

\prod_{v\in\mathcal{M}_{K}}|a|_{v}^{[K_{v}:\mathbb{Q}_{v}]}=1

holds for all
a\in K
, where
K_{v}
(resp.
\mathbb{Q}_{v}
) is the completion of
K
(resp.
\mathbb{Q}
) by
|\cdot|_{v}
.
For each
\bm{a}=(a_{0},\ldots,a_{n})\in K^{n+1}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}
, we set

h(\bm{a})=\sum_{v\in\mathcal{M}_{K}}\frac{[K_{v}:\mathbb{Q}_{v}]}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}\log\left(\max_{0\leq i\leq n}\{|a_{i}|_{v}\}\right)

and
h(\bm{0})=0
.
It is well-known that the value
h(\bm{a})
is independent of the choice of a number field
K
.
Combining the product formula, we may consider
h
to be a function on
\mathbb{P}^{n}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
.
The function
h
is called
the Weil height on
\mathbb{P}^{n}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
.

By abuse of notation, for each
a\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
, we set

h(a)=h(1,a).

The function
h
is called
the Weil height on
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
.
The following are basic formulae of the Weil height on
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

(1)

For each
a\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
and
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, we have

h(a^{1/d})=\frac{1}{d}h(a).

(2)

For each
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
with
\gcd(p,q)=1
, we have

h(p/q)=\log\left(\max\{|p|,|q|\}\right).

(3)

For each
a,b\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
, we have

h(ab)\leq h(a)+h(b).

Proof.

The assertions follow from the definition of
h
.
For proofs, see,
e.g.
,
[
BG06
, Section 1.5]
.
∎

The following fundamental theorem for the Weil heights is known as Northcott’s property.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
L\subset\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
be a field with
h
-(N)
.
Then, for any positive integer
N
, the set

\{[\bm{a}]\in\mathbb{P}^{n}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})\mid\deg_{L}(\bm{a})\leq N\}

has
h
-(N)
.
In particular, the set
L^{(N)}
has
h
-(N)
.

Proof.

See,
e.g.
,
[
DZ08
, Theorem 2.1]
.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

Lemma
2.1
(2) and Theorem
2.2
imply that any number field has
h^{w}
-(N) for any weight
w
.

We also give the following Kronecker’s theorem.

Theorem 2.4
.

The equality
Z(\overline{\mathbb{Q}},h)=\mu_{0}
holds.

Proof.

See,
e.g.
,
[
BG06
, Theorem 1.5.9]
.
∎

3.
Weights

In this section, we give two fundamental properties of the
w
-weighted Weil heights.
However, we will not use these properties in the latter sections.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
w
and
u
be weights with
\lim_{d\rightarrow\infty}u(d)/w(d)=\infty
.
If a set
S\subset\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
has
h^{w}
-(B)
, then the set
S\setminus\mu
has
h^{u}
-(N)
.

Proof.

Take any
C\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
.
We claim that there are only finitely many
a\in S\setminus\mu_{0}
satisfying the inequality
h^{u}(a)<C
.
Since
S
has
h^{w}
-(B), there exists
D\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
independent of
a
such that
h^{w}(a)\geq D
.
Hence, we have the inequality

\frac{u(\deg(a))}{w(\deg(a))}=\frac{h^{u}(a)}{h^{w}(a)}<\frac{C}{D}.

Combining the assumption that
\lim_{d\rightarrow\infty}u(d)/w(d)=\infty
,
we notice that there exists
M\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
independent of
a
such that

\displaystyle\deg(a)\leq M.

(3.1)

Thus we have the inequality

\displaystyle h(a)=\frac{h^{u}(a)}{u(\deg(a))}<\frac{C}{\min\left\{u(i)\mid 1\leq i\leq M\right\}}.

(3.2)

By Theorem
2.2
, the inequalities (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) imply that
there are only finitely many choices for
a\in S\setminus\mu_{0}
.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

Proposition
3.1
generalizes
[
OS22
, Proposition 2.3]
.

Remark 3.3
.

We did not use the fact that
w
and
u
are eventually non-decreasing or eventually non-increasing
in the above proof of Proposition
3.1
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
S\subset\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
be a set with
\#(S\cap\mu)<\infty
.
Then there exists a weight
w
such that
\Nor(S,h^{w})
is a positive finite real number if and only if
\{\deg(a)\mid a\in S\}
is an infinite set.

Proof.

We may assume that
S\cap\mu_{0}=\emptyset
.

First, assume that there exists a weight
w
such that
\Nor(S,h^{w})
is a positive real number.
This implies that the set
S
does not have
h^{w}
-(N).
Hence, there exists an infinite subset
S^{\prime}\subset S
such that
\sup\{h^{w}(a)\mid a\in S^{\prime}\}<\infty
.
If
\{\deg(a)\mid a\in S^{\prime}\}
is a finite set, we also have
\sup\{h(a)\mid a\in S^{\prime}\}<\infty
.
This contradicts Theorem
2.2
.

Next, assume that
\{\deg(a)\mid a\in S\}
is an infinite set.
We divide the proof into the following three cases.

(1)

\Nor(S,h)=\infty
.

Since
S
has
h
-(N), for each
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, we can take an element
a_{d}\in S
satisfying that
\deg(a_{d})\geq d
and

h(a_{d})=\min\{h(a)\mid a\in S,\,\deg(a)\geq d\}.

Note that
\{a\in S\mid\deg(a)\geq d\}\neq\emptyset
for all
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and in fact

\displaystyle\#\{a_{d}\mid d\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\}=\infty

(3.3)

by the assumption that
\{\deg(a)\mid a\in S\}
is an infinite set.
We also note that the sequence
(h(a_{d}))_{d\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}}
is non-decreasing.
Hence,

w(d)=\frac{1}{h(a_{d})}\quad(d\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0})

is a non-increasing weight.
We remark that

\displaystyle w(\deg(a_{d}))=\frac{1}{h(a_{d})}

(3.4)

holds for all
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.
This is because
h(a_{\deg(a_{d})})=h(a_{d})
holds:

\bullet

By
\deg(a_{d})\geq d
, we have
h(a_{\deg(a_{d})})\geq h(a_{d})
;

\bullet

Since
a_{d}
is an element of
\{a\in S\mid\deg(a)\geq\deg(a_{d})\}
, we have
h(a_{\deg(a_{d})})\leq h(a_{d})
.

Now, we claim that
\Nor(S,h^{w})=1
.

\bullet

By (
3.4
), for all
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, we have

h^{w}(a_{d})=w(\deg(a_{d}))h(a_{d})=\frac{h(a_{d})}{h(a_{d})}=1.

By (
3.3
), the above equalities imply that
\Nor(S,h^{w})\leq 1
holds.

\bullet

For each
a\in S
, we set
d_{1}=\deg(a)
and then observe that

h^{w}(a)=\frac{h(a)}{h(a_{d_{1}})}\geq 1\quad\text{by the definition of }a_{d_{1}}.

The above inequality implies that
\Nor(S,h^{w})\geq 1
also holds.

Thus, we have
\Nor(S,h^{w})=1
.
This means that
w
is a weight function that we want.

(2)

\Nor(S,h)\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
.

As this case is trivial, set
w(d)=1
.

(3)

\Nor(S,h)=0
.

Let
d_{0}=\min\{\deg(a)\mid a\in S\}
.
By Theorem
2.2
, for each
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq d_{0}}
, we can take an element
a_{d}\in S

satisfying that
\deg(a_{d})\leq d
and

h(a_{d})=\min\{h(a)\mid a\in S,\,\deg(a)\leq d\}.

Since
S
does not have
h
-(B), it is implied that
\lim_{d\rightarrow\infty}h(a_{d})=0

(this is a special case of Lemma
4.9
).
Hence we have

\#\left\{a_{d}\mid d\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\right\}=\infty.

(3.5)

We also note that the sequence
(h(a_{d}))_{d\geq d_{0}}
is non-increasing.
Hence,

w(d)=\begin{cases}1&(d\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0},\,d<d_{0})\\
1/h(a_{d})&(d\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq d_{0}})\end{cases}

(3.6)

is an eventually non-decreasing weight.
We remark that

\displaystyle w(\deg(a_{d}))=\frac{1}{h(a_{d})}

(3.7)

holds for all
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq d_{0}}
.
This is because
h(a_{\deg(a_{d})})=h(a_{d})
holds:

\bullet

By
\deg(a_{d})\leq d
, we have
h(a_{\deg(a_{d})})\geq h(a_{d})
;

\bullet

Since
a_{d}
is an element of
\{a\in S\mid\deg(a)\leq\deg(a_{d})\}
,
we have
h(a_{\deg(a_{d})})\leq h(a_{d})
.

Now, we claim that
\Nor(S,h^{w})=1
.

\bullet

By (
3.7
), for all
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq d_{0}}
, we have

h^{w}(a_{d})=w(\deg(a_{d}))h(a_{d})=\frac{h(a_{d})}{h(a_{d})}=1.

By (
3.5
), the above equalities imply that
\Nor(S,h^{w})\leq 1
holds.

\bullet

For each
a\in S
, we set
d_{2}=\deg(a)
and then observe that

h^{w}(a)=\frac{h(a)}{h(a_{d_{2}})}\geq 1\quad\text{by the definition of }a_{d_{2}}.

The above inequality implies that
\Nor(S,h^{w})\geq 1
also holds.

Thus, we have the equality
\Nor(S,h^{w})=1
.
This means that
w
is a weight function that we want.

∎

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
L\subset\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
be an infinite extension of
\mathbb{Q}
.
Then, there exists a weight
w
such that
\Nor(L\setminus\mu,h^{w})
is a positive real number.

4.
Northcott numbers for the generalized weighted heights

We will prove Theorem
1.4
in Subsection
4.3

A Rigidity Result for minimal Rotation hypersurfaces of 5d spaces of constant curvature
Aaron J. Tyrrell

Abstract
. In this paper we show that a particular extrinsic pointwise hypersurface invariant is always non-positive on minimal hypersurfaces of constant curvature spaces and vanishes identically if and only if the hypersurface is rotational. We show this for hypersurfaces of 5-dimensional spaces of constant curvature but we conjecture that this should generalize to a similar result in other dimensions.

Let
M^{5}(c)
be a simply connected, complete Riemannian manifold with constant curvature
c,

Do Carmo and Dajczer

2.
Statement and proof of result

Theorem 2.1

(
Rotational Rigidity
)
.

Let
N
be a minimal hypersurface of
M^{5}(c).
Let
A
be the second fundamental form of
N.
Then define

(2.1)

S(N):=|A^{2}|^{2}-\frac{7}{12}|A|^{4},

then

S(N)(p)\leq 0,\quad\forall\hskip 2.84526pt{p\in N}

with

S(N)\equiv 0

if and only if
N
is a rotational hypersurface.

Remark 2.2
.

This gives another way to view minimal rotation hypersurfaces of
M^{5}(c).
This is as minimizers amongst minimal hypersurfaces of the energy

(2.2)

E_{rot}[N]:=\int_{N}\frac{7}{12}|A|^{4}-|A^{2}|^{2}dA,

where
dA
is the area form of the induced metric on
N.

Proof.

Given
p\in N,
let
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3},\lambda_{4}
be the principal curvatures of
N
at
p.
Then letting

(2.3)

\displaystyle SN(p)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{1}^{4}+\lambda_{2}^{4}+\lambda_{3}^{4}+\lambda_{4}^{4}-\frac{7}{12}(\lambda_{1}^{2}+\lambda_{2}^{2}+\lambda_{3}^{2}+\lambda_{4}^{2})^{2}

\displaystyle=:s(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3},\lambda_{4}).

We can apply Lagrange multipliers to
s(x,y,z,w)
subject to the constraint
x+y+z+w=0
to get the system:

(2.4)

\displaystyle 4x^{3}-\frac{28}{12}||x||^{2}x

\displaystyle=\lambda

(2.5)

\displaystyle 4y\textsterling^{3}-\frac{28}{12}||x||^{2}y

\displaystyle=\lambda

(2.6)

\displaystyle 4z^{3}-\frac{28}{12}||x||^{2}z

\displaystyle=\lambda

(2.7)

\displaystyle 4w^{3}-\frac{28}{12}||x||^{2}w

\displaystyle=\lambda

(2.8)

\displaystyle x+y+z+w

\displaystyle=0,

where
||x||^{2}:=x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}+w^{2}.
We also define
x_{1}:=x,x_{2}:=y,x_{3}:=z,x_{4}:=w.

By the symmetry of these equations we see that the set of points satisfying these equations simultaneously is preserved under reflections through the hyperplanes
\{x_{i}=x_{j}:\hskip 2.84526pti\neq j\}
. A natural approach is to set
x=y=z
and try to solve.
This yields
w=-3x,z=y,x=y
and

-108x^{3}+84x^{3}=\lambda,

x^{3}=-\frac{\lambda}{24},

and so

(x,y,z,w)=-\sqrt[3]{\lambda}\big(\frac{1}{\sqrt[3]{24}},\frac{1}{\sqrt[3]{24}},\frac{1}{\sqrt[3]{24}},-\frac{\sqrt[3]{9}}{\sqrt[3]{8}}\big).

By the scale invariance of
(2.4)-(2.8)
we can let
\lambda=24\alpha^{3}
where
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and we get critical points

\alpha(-1,-1,-1,3)\hskip 5.69054pt\forall\hskip 2.84526pt\alpha\in\mathbb{R}.

Observe

s(\alpha(-1,-1,-1,3))=0.

Clearly we could have chosen any one of
x,y,z,w
to not be equal to the others, and therefore we get a critical point at
\alpha\sigma(-1,-1,-1,3)
for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
\sigma\in S_{4}
where
S_{4}
is the symmetric group on 4 letters.
Now to see that
0
is the largest critical value, first consider that at a critical point
(x,y,z,w)
subject to our constraint with
x,y,z
distinct,
(2.4)
and
(2.5)
are equal so we must have

(2.9)

4x^{3}-\frac{28}{12}||x||^{2}x=4y^{3}-\frac{28}{12}||x||^{2}y,

therefore

(2.10)

\displaystyle 4(x^{3}-y^{3})

\displaystyle=\frac{28}{12}||x||^{2}(x-y).

Factoring the left hand-side of
(2.10)
gives

\displaystyle 4(x-y)(x^{2}+xy+y^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{28}{12}||x||^{2}(x-y),

recall
x\neq y
so

(2.11)

(x^{2}+xy+y^{2})=\frac{7}{12}(x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}+w^{2}).

The right hand-side of
(2.11)
is independent of the choice of equations we chose to set equal in
(2.9)
and since
x\neq z
we get

x^{2}+xy+y^{2}=x^{2}+xz+z^{2}

this implies

x(y-z)+(y-z)(y+z)=0,

and therefore

(y-z)(x+y+z)=0

thus, since
y\neq z
we get

(2.12)

w=0.

This implies that
\lambda=0,

now if any two of
x,y,z
is non-zero then it follows that they differ by a sign. To see this take
(2.4)
and see that

(2.13)

4x^{2}=\frac{28}{12}||x||^{2},

since the right hand-side of
(2.13)
is independent of which of
(2.4)
,
(2.5),

(2.6)
and
(2.7)
we chose we get that
x_{i}^{2}=x_{j}^{2}
if
x_{i}\neq 0
and
x_{j}\neq 0.

The only possibility is that two of
x,y,z
are additive inverses and the other is
0.
WLOG assume
x=-y
then

(2.14)

\displaystyle s(x,-x,0,0)

\displaystyle=2x^{4}-\frac{7}{3}x^{4}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{3}x^{4}.

Therefore any constrained critical point with 3 distinct coordinates is not a global maximum (we only considered the case where
x,y
and
z
are distinct but by symmetry this covers every case where three of the coordinates are distinct).

We are left to consider points of the form

\sigma(x,y,x,y)
for
\sigma\in S_{4}
. Now by
(2.8)
we must have
x=-y
which implies the point has the form
(x,-x,x,-x).

Clearly then

\displaystyle s(x,-x,x,-x)

\displaystyle=4x^{4}-\frac{28}{3}x^{4}

\displaystyle=-\frac{26}{3}x^{4}

we see that these cannot be global maximums either.

Now to be sure that
0
is the global maximum value of
s(x,y,z,-x-y-z),
consider the following

(2.15)

s(t(x,y,z,w))=t^{4}s(x,y,z,w).

Therefore to prove that
0
is the global maximum value it is enough to prove that
0
is the maximum on a neighborhood of the origin. We will consider the closed unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
intersected with our constraint hyperplane:
x+y+z+w=0.
By homogeneity, if there is point
p\in\{(x,y,z,w):x+y+z+w=0\}
such that
s(p)>0
then
s
will have to achieve its maximum on the boundary of the ball. Assume for the sake of contradiction that this maximum is achieved at
\underline{x}\in\mathbb{S}^{4}\cap\{(x,y,z,w):x+y+z+w=0\}.

By homogeneity of the function we get

(2.16)

\displaystyle 4s(\underline{x})=\sum_{i}\partial_{i}s(\underline{x})x_{i}

and by Lagrange multipliers we get that there exists
\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb{R}
such that

(2.17)

\partial_{i}s(\underline{x})=\lambda+2\mu x_{i}\quad\forall\hskip 2.84526pti\in\{1,2,3,4\}.

Using
(2.16)
and
(2.17)
we can derive

(2.18)

4s(\underline{x})=2\mu.

Suppose that
\underline{x}
has one coordinate which is
0,
wlog say
x=0.
Then

\displaystyle\partial_{x}s(x,y,z,w)

\displaystyle=4x^{3}-\frac{28}{12}||x||^{2}x

\displaystyle=0.

Therefore by
(2.17)

\lambda=0.

This implies that
(2.17)
can be written as

(2.19)

\partial_{i}s(\underline{x})=2\mu x_{i}.

Now if
\underline{x}
has another coordinate equal to zero then the value of
s(\underline{x})
will be negative by
(2.14),
so suppose
y,z,w
are non-zero. Now exactly two of the non-zero coordinates must have the same sign. Assume
y\geq z>0.

Next consider

\displaystyle\partial_{z}s(x,y,z,w)

\displaystyle=4z^{3}-\frac{28}{12}||x||^{2}z

\displaystyle=4z^{3}-\frac{28}{12}(x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}+w^{2})z

\displaystyle\leq 4z^{3}-\frac{28}{12}z^{3}-\frac{28}{12}(y^{2}+z^{2}+x^{2})z

\displaystyle<0.

Therefore by
(2.19)

2\mu z<0,

this implies that

\mu<0

which is a contradiction to
(2.18).

We can make a similar argument in the case where two of
y,z,w
are negative as follows, suppose
0>z\geq w,
then

\displaystyle\partial_{z}s(x,y,z,w)

\displaystyle=4z^{3}-\frac{28}{12}||x||^{2}z

\displaystyle=4z^{3}-\frac{28}{12}(x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}+w^{2})z

\displaystyle\geq 4z^{3}-\frac{28}{12}z^{3}-\frac{28}{12}(y^{2}+z^{2}+x^{2})z

\displaystyle>0.

Therefore by
(2.19)

(2.20)

2\mu z>0,

this implies that

\mu<0

which is a contradiction to
(2.18).

Therefore
\underline{x}
can only have non-zero coordinates.

Suppose that
\underline{x}
has two positive coordinates and two negative coordinates, wlog assume that the positive coordinates are
x
and
y
and assume wlog
x\geq y
and that the negative coordinates are
z,w
with
z\geq w.

Then

\displaystyle\partial_{y}s(x,y,z,w)

\displaystyle=4y^{3}-\frac{28}{12}||x||^{2}y

\displaystyle=4y^{3}-\frac{28}{12}(x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}+w^{2})y

\displaystyle\leq 4y^{3}-\frac{28}{12}y^{3}-\frac{28}{12}(y^{2}+z^{2}+w^{2})y

\displaystyle<0.

Therefore

\lambda+2\mu y<0,

this implies that

\lambda<0.

Next consider

\displaystyle\partial_{z}s(x,y,z,w)

\displaystyle=4z^{3}-\frac{28}{12}||x||^{2}z

\displaystyle=4z^{3}-\frac{28}{12}(x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}+w^{2})z

\displaystyle\geq 4z^{3}-\frac{28}{12}z^{3}-\frac{28}{12}(y^{2}+z^{2}+x^{2})z

\displaystyle>0.

Therefore

\lambda+2\mu z>0,

this implies that

\lambda>0.

Therefore we have reached a contradiction and hence
\underline{x}
cannot have two positive coordinates and two negative coordinates.

Now we know that
\underline{x}
must have
3
coordinates of the same sign and the
4th
must be of the opposite.
Suppose
x\geq y\geq z>0
and
w=-x-y-z.
Then

(2.21)

\partial_{x}s(\underline{x})=4x^{3}-\frac{7}{3}x=\lambda+2\mu x

which implies

(2.22)

4x^{3}-\frac{7}{3}x-2\mu x=\lambda

the right hand-side of
(2.22)
is independent of which variable we chose in
(2.21)
so we get

(2.23)

4x_{i}^{3}-\frac{7}{3}x_{i}-2\mu x_{i}=4x_{j}^{3}-\frac{7}{3}x_{j}-2\mu x_{j}.

It follows that

(2.24)

4(x_{i}^{3}-x_{j}^{3})-(\frac{7}{3}+2\mu)(x_{i}-x_{j})=0

now if
x_{i}\neq x_{j}
then dividing by
x_{i}-x_{j}
gives

(2.25)

4(x_{i}^{2}+x_{i}x_{j}+x_{j}^{2})-(\frac{7}{3}+2\mu)=0

therefore

(2.26)

4(x_{i}^{2}+x_{i}x_{j}+x_{j}^{2})=(\frac{7}{3}+2\mu)

now since the right hand-side is independent of the choice of coordinates we get that if
x,y
and
z
are distinct then

(2.27)

\displaystyle 4(x^{2}+xy+y^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{7}{3}+2\mu

(2.28)

\displaystyle 4(x^{2}+xw+w^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{7}{3}+2\mu

writing
w=-x-y-z
and taking the average of
(2.27)
and
(2.28)
gives

2(x^{2}-xz+w^{2}+y^{2})=\frac{7}{3}+2\mu.

We could have swapped the roles of
x
and
z
and gotten

2(z^{2}-xz+w^{2}+y^{2})=\frac{7}{3}+2\mu.

Which implies

x=z.

Therefore
x,y
and
z
cannot be distinct.

Now suppose two of the coordinates are equal, wlog
x=z,
then if
y\neq x
we get the following equations

(2.29)

\displaystyle 4(y^{2}+yz+z^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{7}{3}+2\mu

(2.30)

\displaystyle 4(z^{2}+zw+w^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{7}{3}+2\mu

(2.31)

\displaystyle 4(y^{2}+yw+w^{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{7}{3}+2\mu

Writing

(2.29)

(2.30)

Taking the average of
(2.29)
and
(2.31)
gives

2(y^{2}-yx+z^{2}+w^{2})=\frac{7}{3}+2\mu.

This implies that
y=z.

Therefore
\underline{x}
is of the form
\alpha\sigma(1,1,1,-3)
for
\alpha\in\mathbb{R_{+}}
and
\sigma\in S_{4}
and we get

which is a contradiction.
If
x,y
and

would also be a local maximum on which

has the same value by symmetry but by what we’ve just argued we would get
s(\underline{x})=0.
Now by corollary
4.4
of

1.2.
Method of Proof

We now offer a heuristic overview on the genesis of our reciprocity formula in Theorem

. This is a back-of-the-envelope type sketch geared towards experts. For the sake of argument, we use approximate tools, while our proof is based on a circumspect analysis of
L
-functions in the region of absolute convergence. We also elide all polar terms, correction factors, and the averaging over
t
whose purpose is to ensure convergence.

The overall strategy is inspired by the work of Motohashi
[
Mot97b
, Sections 4.3–4.7]
. The off-diagonal contribution after Atkinson’s dissection looks like

\sum_{n,m}\tau(n,\overline{\psi})\tau(n+m,\psi),

where the twisted divisor function is defined by

\tau(m,\psi)=\frac{1+\psi(m)}{\tau(\psi)}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\ell}f\left(\frac{\ell}{\sqrt{m}}\right)\ \sideset{}{{}^{\ast}}{\sum}_{h{\@displayfalse\pmod{\ell q}}}\psi(h)e_{\ell q}(hm),

where

3.
Arithmetic Preliminaries

This section summarises fundamental properties of the twisted Estermann zeta function. The Ramanujan expansion for the twisted divisor function is also established.

3.1.
Estermann Zeta Function

For
h,\ell,q\in\mathbb{N}
with
(h,\ell q)=1
and
\mathrm{Re}(s)>1
, we define

D_{\psi}\left(s,\xi;\frac{h}{\ell q}\right)\coloneqq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{\xi}(n,\psi)e_{\ell q}(hn)n^{-s},

where
\psi
is an arbitrary primitive Dirichlet character modulo
q
, and

(3.1)

\sigma_{\xi}(n,\psi)\coloneqq\sum_{d\mid n}\psi(d)d^{\xi}.

Analytic properties of the twisted Estermann zeta function follow from the identity

(3.2)

D_{\psi}\left(s,\xi;\frac{h}{\ell q}\right)=(\ell q)^{\xi-2s}\sum_{a,b{\@displayfalse\pmod{\ell q}}}\psi(b)e_{\ell q}(abh)\zeta\left(s,\frac{a}{\ell q}\right)\zeta\left(s-\xi,\frac{b}{\ell q}\right),

where for
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
\mathrm{Re}(s)>1
,

\zeta(s,\alpha)\coloneqq\sum_{n+\alpha>0}(n+\alpha)^{-s}

is the Hurwitz zeta function. It has a meromorphic continuation to
\mathbb{C}
with a simple pole at
s=1
of residue 1, and satisfies the functional equation

(3.3)

\zeta(s,\alpha)=\sum_{\pm}G^{\mp}(1-s)\zeta^{(\pm\alpha)}(1-s),

where
G^{\pm}(s)\coloneqq(2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s)\exp(\pm\frac{i\pi s}{2})
and
\zeta^{(\alpha)}(s)\coloneqq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e(\alpha n)n^{-s}
. The formula (
3.3
) for
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}
can be viewed as a restatement of the Poisson summation formula in residue classes. As a function of a single variable
s
, the twisted Estermann zeta function has a meromorphic continuation to
\mathbb{C}
with a simple pole at
s=1+\xi
of residue

\frac{\overline{\psi}(h)\tau(\psi)L(1+\xi,\overline{\psi})}{\ell^{1+\xi}q},

provided
\xi\neq 0
. This follows from routine computations. In fact, one evaluates

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{res}}_{s=1+\xi}D_{\psi}\left(s,\xi;\frac{h}{\ell q}\right)

\displaystyle=(\ell q)^{-2-\xi}\sum_{a,b{\@displayfalse\pmod{\ell q}}}\psi(b)e_{\ell q}(abh)\zeta\left(1+\xi,\frac{a}{\ell q}\right)

\displaystyle=(\ell q)^{-2-\xi}\sum_{a{\@displayfalse\pmod{\ell q}}}\sum_{b_{1}=1}^{q}\psi(b_{1})\sum_{b_{2}=0}^{\ell-1}e_{\ell q}(ab_{1}h)e_{\ell}(ab_{2}h)\zeta\left(1+\xi,\frac{a}{\ell q}\right).

By orthogonality and the definition of the Gauß sum, the right-hand side is equal to

\ell^{-1-\xi}q^{-2-\xi}\sum_{a,b{\@displayfalse\pmod{q}}}\psi(b)e_{q}(abh)\zeta\left(1+\xi,\frac{a}{q}\right)=\frac{\overline{\psi}(h)\tau(\psi)L(1+\xi,\overline{\psi})}{\ell^{1+\xi}q}.

Furthermore, the functional equation for
D_{\psi}(s,\xi;\frac{h}{\ell q})
reads as follows.

Theorem 3.1
.

The twisted Estermann zeta function enjoys the functional equation

\displaystyle\begin{split}D_{\psi}\left(s,\xi;\frac{h}{\ell q}\right)&=\psi(-\overline{h})(2\pi)^{2s-\xi-2}(\ell q)^{\xi-2s+1}\Gamma(1-s)\Gamma(1+\xi-s)\\
&\quad\times\left[\left\{e\left(\frac{\xi}{4}\right)+\psi(-1)e\left(-\frac{\xi}{4}\right)\right\}D_{\psi}\left(1+\xi-s,\xi;\frac{\overline{h}}{\ell q}\right)\right.\\
&\quad-\left.\left\{e\left(\frac{2s-\xi}{4}\right)+\psi(-1)e\left(-\frac{2s-\xi}{4}\right)\right\}D_{\psi}\left(1+\xi-s;\xi;-\frac{\overline{h}}{\ell q}\right)\right],\end{split}

where
\overline{h}
denotes the multiplicative inverse of
h
modulo
\ell q
, namely
h\overline{h}\equiv 1{\@displayfalse\pmod{\ell q}}
.

This is essentially equivalent to the twisted
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
Voronoĭ summation formula.

Proof.

It follows from (
3.2
) that
D_{\psi}(s,\xi;\frac{h}{\ell q})
equals

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{2s-\xi-2}(\ell q)^{\xi-2s}\Gamma(1-s)\Gamma(1+\xi-s)

\displaystyle\times\left[\left\{\psi(-1)e\left(\frac{\xi}{4}\right)+e\left(-\frac{\xi}{4}\right)\right\}\sum_{a,b{\@displayfalse\pmod{\ell q}}}\psi(b)e_{\ell q}(-abh)\zeta^{(\frac{a}{\ell q})}(1-s)\zeta^{(\frac{b}{\ell q})}(1+\xi-s)\right.

\displaystyle-\left.\left\{e\left(\frac{2s-\xi}{4}\right)+\psi(-1)e\left(-\frac{2s-\xi}{4}\right)\right\}\sum_{a,b{\@displayfalse\pmod{\ell q}}}\psi(b)e_{\ell q}(abh)\zeta^{(\frac{a}{\ell q})}(1-s)\zeta^{(\frac{b}{\ell q})}(1+\xi-s)\right].

We then appeal to the identity

\zeta^{(\frac{a}{\ell q})}(1-s)\zeta^{(\frac{b}{\ell q})}(1+\xi-s)=(\ell q)^{2s-\xi-2}\sum_{c,d{\@displayfalse\pmod{\ell q}}}e_{\ell q}(ac+bd)\zeta\left(1-s,\frac{c}{\ell q}\right)\zeta\left(1+\xi-s,\frac{d}{\ell q}\right),

which follows from expanding
\zeta^{(\frac{a}{\ell q})}(1-s)
and
\zeta^{(\frac{b}{\ell q})}(1+\xi-s)
into sums over residue classes modulo
\ell q
. Then we observe

\sum_{a,b{\@displayfalse\pmod{\ell q}}}\psi(b)e_{\ell q}(ac+bd-abh)=\sum_{b{\@displayfalse\pmod{\ell q}}}\psi(b)e_{\ell q}(bd)\delta_{c\equiv bh{\@displayfalse\pmod{\ell q}}}=\psi(c\overline{h})e_{\ell q}(cd\overline{h}).

The proof of Theorem
3.1
is now complete.
∎

3.2.
Ramanujan Expansion

The main result of this subsection is the following.

Lemma 3.2

(Twisted Ramanujan expansion)
.

Let
\psi
be a Dirichlet character modulo
q
. Then we have for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mathrm{Re}(\xi)>0
that

\frac{\sigma_{\xi}(n,\psi)}{n^{\xi}}=\frac{L(1+\xi,\overline{\psi})}{\tau(\overline{\psi})}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\ell^{1+\xi}}\ \sideset{}{{}^{\ast}}{\sum}_{h{\@displayfalse\pmod{\ell q}}}\overline{\psi}(h)e_{\ell q}(hn).

Proof.

We express the coprimality condition
(h,\ell q)=1
via Möbius inversion, justifying that the coefficients of
\ell^{-\xi}
are equal on both sides of

(3.4)

\frac{\sigma_{\xi}(n,\psi)\tau(\overline{\psi})}{L(1+\xi,\overline{\psi})n^{\xi}}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\ell^{1+\xi}}\sum_{d\mid\ell}\mu(d)\overline{\psi}(d)\sum_{h{\@displayfalse\pmod{\frac{\ell q}{d}}}}\overline{\psi}(h)e_{\frac{\ell q}{d}}(hn).

It is convenient for the reader to describe a straightforward way to prove Lemma
3.2
. If we assume that
\psi
is primitive, then the right-hand side of (
3.4
) is equal to

\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\ell^{1+\xi}}\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}\mu(d)\frac{\overline{\psi}(d)}{d^{1+\xi}}\sum_{h{\@displayfalse\pmod{\ell q}}}\overline{\psi}(h)e_{\ell q}(hn)=\frac{1}{L(1+\xi,\overline{\psi})}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\ell^{-1-\xi}\sum_{h{\@displayfalse\pmod{\ell q}}}\overline{\psi}(h)e_{\ell q}(hn).

The identity
\tau(\psi,m)=\overline{\psi}(m)\tau(\psi)
implies

\frac{1}{L(1+\xi,\overline{\psi})}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\ell^{-1-\xi}\sum_{h_{1}=1}^{q}\overline{\psi}(h_{1})e_{\ell q}(h_{1}n)\sum_{h_{2}=0}^{\ell-1}e_{\ell}(h_{2}n)=\frac{\psi(n)\sigma_{-\xi}(n,\overline{\psi})\tau(\overline{\psi})}{L(1+\xi,\overline{\psi})},

Lemma
3.2
now follows from the relation
\sigma_{\xi}(n,\psi)n^{-\xi}=\psi(n)\sigma_{-\xi}(n,\overline{\psi})
.
∎

We state the following approximate functional equation since it is of independent interest.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
G(s)
be a fixed even entire holomorphic function of rapid decay in vertical strips, satisfying
G(0)=1
. Then we have for any
\xi\in\mathbb{C}
that

\sigma_{\xi}(n,\psi)=\frac{\psi(n)}{\tau(\psi)}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\ell^{1-\xi}}\ \sideset{}{{}^{\ast}}{\sum}_{h{\@displayfalse\pmod{\ell q}}}\psi(h)e_{\ell q}(hn)f_{\xi}\left(\frac{\ell}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\\
+\frac{n^{\xi}}{\tau(\psi)}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\ell^{1+\xi}}\ \sideset{}{{}^{\ast}}{\sum}_{h{\@displayfalse\pmod{\ell q}}}\psi(h)e_{\ell q}(hn)f_{-\xi}\left(\frac{\ell}{\sqrt{n}}\right),

where for
a>|\mathrm{Re}(\xi)|
,

f_{\xi}(x)\coloneqq\int_{(a)}x^{-w}L(1-\xi+w,\psi)\frac{G(w)}{w}\frac{dw}{2\pi i}.

Proof.

Shifting the contour, we derive

\int_{(a)}\sigma_{\xi-w}(n,\psi)n^{\frac{w}{2}}\frac{G(w)}{w}\frac{dw}{2\pi i}=\sigma_{\xi}(n,\psi)+\int_{(-a)}\sigma_{\xi-w}(n,\psi)n^{\frac{w}{2}}\frac{G(w)}{w}\frac{dw}{2\pi i}.

Making a change of variables
w\mapsto-w
gives

\sigma_{\xi}(n,\psi)=\int_{(a)}\sigma_{\xi-w}(n,\psi)n^{\frac{w}{2}}\frac{G(w)}{w}\frac{dw}{2\pi i}+\psi(n)n^{\xi}\int_{(a)}\sigma_{-\xi-w}(n,\overline{\psi})n^{\frac{w}{2}}\frac{G(w)}{w}\frac{dw}{2\pi i}.

By Lemma
3.2
, this matches the desired claim.
∎

3.3.
Hecke Relation

We record the Hecke relation for the twisted divisor function
\sigma_{\xi}(n,\psi)
.

Lemma 3.4
.

The twisted divisor function (
3.1
) enjoys the Hecke relation

\sigma_{\xi}(mn,\psi)=\sum_{c\mid(m,n)}\mu(c)\psi(c)c^{\xi}\sigma_{\xi}\left(\frac{m}{c},\psi\right)\sigma_{\xi}\left(\frac{n}{c},\psi\right).

Proof.

If
m=p_{1}^{k_{1}}p_{2}^{k_{2}}\cdots p_{r}^{k_{r}}
,
n=p_{1}^{\ell_{1}}p_{2}^{\ell_{2}}\cdots p_{r}^{\ell_{r}}
, and the claim holds for prime powers, then

(3.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sigma_{\xi}(mn,\psi)&=\sigma_{\xi}(p_{1}^{k_{1}+\ell_{1}},\psi)\sigma_{\xi}(p_{2}^{k_{2}+\ell_{2}},\psi)\cdots\sigma_{\xi}(p_{r}^{k_{r}+\ell_{r}},\psi)\\
&=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{j=0}^{\min(k_{i},\ell_{i})}\mu(p_{i}^{j})\psi(p_{i}^{j})p_{i}^{j\xi}\sigma_{\xi}(p_{i}^{k_{i}-j})\sigma_{\xi}(p_{i}^{\ell_{i}-j})\\
&=\sum_{c\mid(m,n)}\mu(c)\psi(c)c^{w}\sigma_{\xi}\left(\frac{m}{c}\right)\sigma_{\xi}\left(\frac{n}{c}\right).\end{split}

By multiplicativity, we assume that
m=p^{k}
and
n=p^{\ell}
. The claim follows if
\min(k,\ell)=0
, thus we focus on the case where both are at least
1
. If
X=\psi(p)p^{\xi}
, then the right-hand side of (
3.5
) is equal to

\displaystyle\sigma_{\xi}(p^{k},\psi)\sigma_{\xi}(p^{\ell},\psi)-\psi(p)p^{\xi}\sigma_{\xi}(p^{k-1},\psi)\sigma_{\xi}(p^{\ell-1},\psi)

\displaystyle\quad=(1+X+\cdots+X^{k})(1+X+\cdots+X^{\ell})-X(1+X+\cdots+X^{k-1})(1+X+\cdots+X^{\ell-1})

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{X^{k+\ell+1}-1}{X-1}=(1+\psi(p)p^{\xi}+\psi(p)^{2}p^{2\xi}+\cdots+\psi(p)^{k+\ell}p^{(k+\ell)\xi})=\sigma_{\xi}(p^{k+\ell},\psi),

as required. The proof of Lemma
3.4
is now complete.
∎

4.
The Kuznetsov Formula

We formulate an asymmetric trace formula relating sums of Kloosterman sums to Fourier coefficients of automorphic forms in the sense that the spectral and geometric sides possesses a quite different shape. We begin with an explanation of the Fourier expansions of Maaß and holomorphic forms as well as Eisenstein series in the same manner as in
[
BK19
, Section 3]
, and refer to this source for further details. Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
, and let

\nu(N)\coloneqq\prod_{p\mid N}\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right).

We equip
\Gamma_{0}(N)\backslash\mathbb{H}
with the Petersson inner product

\langle f,g\rangle\coloneqq\int_{\Gamma_{0}(N)\backslash\mathbb{H}}f(z)\overline{g(z)}\frac{dxdy}{y^{2}}.

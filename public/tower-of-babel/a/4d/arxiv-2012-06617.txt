Abstract
. This work is devoted to the study of the asymptotic behavior of nonautonomous reaction-diffusion equations in Dumbbell domains Ω ε ⊂ ℝ N \Omega_{\varepsilon}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N} . Each Ω ε \Omega_{\varepsilon} is the union of a fixed open set Ω \Omega and a channel R ε R_{\varepsilon} that collapses to a line segment R 0 R_{0} as ε → 0 + \varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+} . We first establish the global existence of solution for each problem by using two properties of the parabolic equation considered, which are the positivity of the solutions and comparison results for them. We prove the existence of pullback and uniform attractors and we obtain uniform bounds (in ε \varepsilon ) for them. Mathematics Subject Classification 2020 : 37B55, 35B41, 35B40, 35K58, 35B09, 35B51. Keywords: Dumbbell domains, almost sectorial operator, positive solutions, comparison results, pullback attractor, uniform attractor.

For each
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
, we consider the nonautonomous evolution equation

(1.1)

\begin{cases}u_{t}-a(t)\Delta u+u=f(u),\quad t>\tau,x\in\Omega_{\varepsilon},\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}=0,\qquad\qquad\qquad\mbox{ }\qquad\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon},\end{cases}

where
a:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[c_{0},c_{1}]
,
0<c_{0}<c_{1}
and
f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
are continuously differentiable. Furthermore,
a
is Hölder continuous with exponent
\delta\in(0,1]
and constant
C
, that is,

(1.2)

|a(t)-a(s)|\leq C|s-t|^{\delta},\quad\forall t,s\in\mathbb{R},

and the nonlinearity
f
satisfies a growth condition

(1.3)

|f^{\prime}(r)|\leq C(1+|r|^{\rho-1}),\quad\mbox{for some }\rho\geq 1.

As
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}
, the limit equation obtained is

(1.4)

\begin{cases}w_{t}-a(t)\Delta w+w=f(w),\quad\quad t>\tau,x\in\Omega,\\
\dfrac{\partial w}{\partial n}=0,\quad\hskip 108.12054ptx\in\partial\Omega,\\
v_{t}-a(t)\dfrac{1}{g}(gv^{\prime})^{\prime}+v=f(v),\hskip 11.38092ptt>\tau,s\in(0,1),\\
v(p_{0})=w(p_{0})\mbox{ and }v(p_{1})=w(p_{1}),\\
\end{cases}

where
p_{0}=(0,0,...,0)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
p_{1}=(1,0,...,0)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
g
is a function that appears as consequence of the geometry of the initial channel
R_{1}
. If
w\in W^{2,p}(\Omega)
,
then it is continuously extended to
\overline{\Omega}
and the boundary conditions for
v
make sense. Note that the dynamics in
\Omega
is independent of the dynamics in
R_{0}
, whereas the dynamics in
R_{0}
depends on the dynamics in
\Omega
through the continuity conditions at
p_{0}
and
p_{1}
. We express this feature by saying that this system is one-sided coupled. For further details about limit equations on thin domains, we recommend
[
14
,
22
,
25
]
.

Those Dumbbell domains emerged in the literature as a counterpart of convex domains in the following sense: if we consider an autonomous reaction-diffusion equation in a convex domain, the stable equilibria for the equation are constant in the domain (see, for instance,
[
11
,
19
]
). In order to obtain stable equilibria that are not spatially constant, we can not allow the domain to be convex. This is when the Dumbbell domains appeared as prototype of nonconvex domain. The earlier works dealing with Dumbbell domains considered the case in which the channel
R_{\varepsilon}
was a straight cylinder. In
[
15
,
16
,
17
]
, Jimbo made a detailed study of semilinear elliptic equations on those Dumbbell domains with straight cylinder channel and exhibited stable equilibrium that is not spatially constant. The same author proved in
[
18
]
a result on the existence of global attractor
\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}
for a parabolic semilinear equation in a Dumbbell domain
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
with straight channel, including the limiting case in
\Omega_{0}.

Allowing the channel
R_{\varepsilon}
to be more generic than the cylinders considered by Jimbo, the authors in
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
studied an autonomous version of the equations (
1.1
) and (
1.4
), when
a(t)=a>0
. To treat this equations in Dumbbell domains, they developed an appropriate functional setting. For
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
, the phase space considered was
U_{p}^{\varepsilon}:=L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
, with the norm

(1.5)

\left\|u\right\|_{U_{p}^{\varepsilon}}=\left\|u\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{\frac{N-1}{p}}}\left\|u\right\|_{L^{p}(R_{\varepsilon})}

and, for
\varepsilon=0
,
U_{p}^{0}:=L^{p}(\Omega)\oplus L^{p}_{g}(0,1)
, that is
(w,v)\in U_{p}^{0}
if
w\in L^{p}(\Omega)
,
v\in L^{p}(0,1)
and is equipped with norm

(1.6)

\left\|(w,v)\right\|_{U_{p}^{0}}=\left\|w\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\left[\int_{0}^{1}g(s)|v(s)|^{p}ds\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}.

This functional setting allowed them to prove the existence of stable equilibrium that was not constant in the domain
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
. The construction of such equilibrium involved a detailed study of the limiting problem in the domain
\Omega_{0}=\Omega\cup R_{0}
. They realized that the linear operator in this limiting problem possesses a deficiency in the resolvent estimate and belonged to a class of linear operators called
almost sectorial operators
(which we will define in Section
2
). Nevertheless, this setback on the estimate of the resolvent did not prevent them to investigate the asymptotic behavior of the equation, which was done through a study of the global attractor for the equations.

Using this framework, Carvalho et al. in
[
9
]
explored this deficiency in the resolvent now for the nonautonomous equation. They considered the equations (
1.1
) and (
1.4
) in Dumbbell domains and constructed the family of linear operators
A_{\varepsilon}(t):D(A_{\varepsilon}(t))\subset U_{p}^{\varepsilon}\rightarrow U_{p}^{\varepsilon}
given by
A_{\varepsilon}(t)u=-a(t)\Delta u+u
, for
0<\varepsilon\leq 1
, with
D(A_{\varepsilon}(t))=\{u\in W^{2,p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}):\mbox{ }{\partial u}/{\partial n}=0\mbox{ in }\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}\}
. For the case
\varepsilon=0
, the family of linear operators considered was
A_{0}(t):D(A_{0}(t))\subset U_{p}^{0}\rightarrow U_{p}^{0}
given by
A_{0}(t)(w,v)=(-a(t)\Delta w+w,-a(t)\frac{1}{g}(gv^{\prime})^{\prime}+v)
with
D(A_{0}(t))=\{(w,v)\in U_{p}^{0}:w\in D(\Delta_{N}^{\Omega});\mbox{ }(gv^{\prime})^{\prime}\in L^{p}_{g}(0,1),\mbox{ }v(p_{i})=w(p_{i}),i=0,1\}.
Note that the domain for both operators are fixed in time.

Regarding the nonlinearity, they considered
F_{\varepsilon}(u)(x)=f(u(x))
, for
x\in\Omega_{\varepsilon}
and
F_{0}(w,v)=(\overline{w},\overline{v})
where
\overline{w}(x)=f(w(x))
, if
x\in\Omega
, and
\overline{v}(x)=f(v(x))
, if
x\in R_{0}
.

For
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
, problem (
1.1
) was written as

(1.7)

\begin{cases}u_{t}+A_{\varepsilon}(t)u=F_{\varepsilon}(u),\quad t>\tau,\\
u(\tau)=u_{0}\in U_{p}^{\varepsilon},\end{cases}

and, for
\varepsilon=0
, problem (
1.4
) was given in its abstract form

(1.8)

\begin{cases}(w,v)_{t}+A_{0}(t)(w,v)=F_{0}(w,v),\quad t>\tau,\\
(w,v)(\tau)=(w_{0},v_{0})\in U_{p}^{0}.\end{cases}

They proved that each operator of the family
\{A_{0}(t);t\in\mathbb{R}\}
is closed, densely defined and satisfies the estimate

\left\|(\lambda+A_{0}(t))^{-1}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(U_{p}^{0})}\leq\dfrac{C}{|\lambda|^{\alpha}+1},

for
\lambda
in an appropriate sector in
\mathbb{C}
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
. The sector and constants being uniform in
t
.

A family of linear operators with those properties was called
uniform almost sectorial
and
it has a deficiency in the resolvent estimate, since
\alpha<1
. This deficiency comes from the continuity condition at the junction points of the fixed part
\Omega
with
R_{0}
and it prevented the authors to use the standard theory of generation of strongly continuous semigroups and continuous linear process (provided, for instance, in
[
23
,
26
]
).

Nevertheless, the authors in
[
9
]
proved that each operator
-A_{0}(t)
generates a semigroup of growth
1-\alpha
(a concept that will be presented later in Section
2
) and used those semigroups to solve the linear nonautonomous evolution equation

\begin{cases}(w,v)_{t}+A_{0}(t)(w,v)=0,\quad t>\tau,\\
(w,v)(\tau)=(w_{0},v_{0})\in U_{p}^{{0}}.\end{cases}

The solution of this problem generates a linear process of growth
1-\alpha
, denoted by
\{U_{0}(t,\tau):U_{p}^{0}\rightarrow U_{p}^{0};t\geq\tau\}
, and the function
[\tau,\infty)\ni t\rightarrow U_{0}(t,\tau)(w_{0},v_{0})
is not continuous at
t=\tau
for general
(w_{0},v_{0})\in U_{p}^{0}
. In Section
2
we will present further details on those semigroups and process with growth.

Furthermore, the authors managed to prove that the semilinear evolution equation (
1.8
) can be locally solved, even though the linear operators
A_{0}(t)
presents the mentioned deficiency in the resolvent. The solution, however, possesses the same type of discontinuity at
t=\tau
that the linear process
\{U_{0}(t,\tau);t\geq\tau\}
does.

We are interested in continuing this study of nonautonomous reaction-diffusion equation in which features a family of almost sectorial operators. We will compare the asymptotic dynamic of problems (
1.7
) and (
1.8
) and this shall be done through an analysis of attractors for both problems.

In this paper we focus on proving the existence of
pullback attractor
\{\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}(t)\subset U_{p}^{\varepsilon};t\in\mathbb{R}\}
and uniform attractor
\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{\varepsilon}\subset U_{p}^{\varepsilon}
for each
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
, with special attention to limiting case
\varepsilon=0
, where the singular behavior appears. We will also obtain uniform estimates (in
\varepsilon
) for the attractors.

The outline that we will follow is:

(1)

We will first show that, under appropriate dissipativeness condition on the nonlinearity
f
, the local solution obtained in
[
9
]
for problem (
1.8
) is globally defined in time. The classical variational approach of constructing an energy functional for the equation is not possible in this case, due to the nonautonomous coefficient that multiplies the diffusion term.
To overcome this problem, we will prove that the solutions of nonlinear equation are positive (that is, positive initial condition generates positive solution) and monotone (if two initial conditions satisfies an order relation, than the solutions they generate preserve this order). This is an interesting feature, considering that the solution can present discontinuity at the initial time.

(2)

With the global existence of the solution, we will prove that the nonlinear process associated to the equation possesses a compact set that pullback absorbs. The existence of the pullback attractor follows from the existence of such set.

(3)

We will prove that the compact pullback absorbing set found in (2) can be chosen uniform in
\varepsilon
and that it is also a forward attracting set. This guarantees the existence of uniform attractor for each problem and we can relate the pullback attractor and the uniform attractor obtained.

Steps (2) and (3) above illustrate an interesting feature of the problem being considered, that is the fact that information of the forward dynamics can be obtained from information of the pullback dynamics.
In general, those dynamics do not need to be related.

This paper is organized as follows: Section 2 is dedicated to the preliminaries results. We present the definitions of semigroups and process with growth
\alpha
, the definitions of almost sectorial operators and we also present the result of existence of local solution for an abstract semilinear evolution equation with almost sectorial operators. Additionally, we briefly introduce the concepts of pullback and uniform attractor, as well as results that ensure their existence. In Section 3 we begin the discussion of the equation in Dumbbell domain, and we establish the basic properties of the linear operators
A_{\varepsilon}(t)
and nonlinearity
F_{\varepsilon}
,
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
. In Section 4 we prove the positivity and a comparison result for the local solutions of the semilinear equation. This is done in Theorems
4.4
and
4.6
, respectively. In Section 5 we apply those results to obtain the existence of global solution (Theorem
5.3
) and the existence of pullback and uniform attractors (Theorems
5.4
and
5.6
).

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we present the concepts of
semigroups of growth
\alpha
,
process of growth
\alpha
and the result on the existence of local solution for a semilinear evolution equation with almost sectorial operator.

2.1.
Semigroups and process of growth
\alpha
: definition and properties

Definition 2.1
.

Let
X
be a Banach space and
\alpha>0
. A family of bounded linear operators in
X
,
\{T(t)\in\mathcal{L}(X);t>0\}
, is a
semigroup of growth
\alpha
if satisfies

(1)

T(0)=I
.

(2)

T(t)T(s)=T(t+s)
, for
t,s>0
.

(3)

If
T(t)x=0
for all
t>0
, then
x=0
.

(4)

There exists
\delta>0
such that
\left\|t^{\alpha}T(t)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\leq M
, for all
0<t\leq\delta
.

(5)

X_{0}=\bigcup_{t>0}T(t)X
is dense in
X
.

Unlike
C_{0}\mbox{-semigroups}
, the semigroups of growth
\alpha
might present discontinuity at the initial time
t=0
, that is,
T(t)x\nrightarrow x
when
t\rightarrow 0^{+}
for general
x\in X
. The following properties for semigroups of growth
\alpha
can be found in
[
13
,
21
]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\{T(t);t>0\}
be a semigroup of growth
\alpha
. There exist constants
M\geq 1
and
w\in\mathbb{R}
such that, for
t>0
,
\left\|T(t)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\leq Me^{wt}t^{-\alpha}

and the map
t\mapsto T(t)x
is continuous in
(0,\infty)
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{C}
and
\varphi\in(\frac{\pi}{2},\pi)
. We denote the sector centered in
a
and with an angle
\varphi
by

\Sigma_{\varphi,a}=\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C};|\arg(\lambda-a)|\leq\varphi\right\}.

If
a=0
, we simply denote the sector by
\Sigma_{\varphi}
.

A linear operator
A
is sectorial if
-A:D(A)\subset X\rightarrow X
is closed, densely defined linear operator,
\Sigma_{\varphi}\cup\{0\}
belongs to its resolvent and the following estimate holds

\left\|(\lambda+A)^{-1}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\leq\dfrac{M}{|\lambda|},\quad\lambda\in\Sigma_{\varphi}.

The operator
-A
generates a
C_{0}\mbox{-semigroup}
which is given by the following integral

T_{-A}(t)=\dfrac{1}{2\pi i}\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}e^{\lambda t}(\lambda+A)^{-1}d\lambda,

where
\Gamma
is the boundary of the sector
\Sigma_{\varphi}
. The above characterization of the semigroup allows one to prove that the semigroup is analytic and it regularizes as the time evolves (see
[
23
]
for more details).

However, in some applications, as the equation in Dumbbell domains that we consider here, the estimate on the resolvent of the operator
-A
possesses a deficiency. In this case,
A
will be an
almost sectorial operator
, which we define next.

Definition 2.3
.

A linear operator
A:D(A)\subset X\rightarrow X
is
almost sectorial
if

(1)

-A
is closed and densely defined.

(2)

There exist
\varphi\in\left(\frac{\pi}{2},\pi\right)
and
M>0
such that
\Sigma_{\varphi}\cup\{0\}\subset\rho(-A)

and, for all
\lambda\in\Sigma_{\varphi}
,

\left\|(\lambda+A)^{-1}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\leq\dfrac{M}{|\lambda|^{\alpha}},

for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
.

Despite the fact that
\alpha
is strictly less than
1
, we can still define for an almost sectorial operators the family

T_{-A}(t)=\dfrac{1}{2\pi i}\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}e^{\lambda t}(\lambda+A)^{-1}d\lambda.

In
[
2
]
the authors proved that the above integral converges and this family of linear operators defines a semigroup of growth
1-\alpha
. Such semigroup is continuous in
(0,\infty)
and, following the same ideas used in
[
23
]
, this semigroup is analytic in any interval
[\delta,\infty)
, for
\delta>0
. For further details and examples of semigroups of growth
\alpha
, we recommend
[
13
,
20
]
. In
[
21
]
a result on the generation of those type of semigroups is given and in
[
24
]
a functional calculus for almost sectorial operators is developed.

When dealing with nonautonomous evolution equations with almost sectorial operators, as we shall do in the sequence, we will also work with a 2-parameters family of linear operators with growth
\alpha
, which we define next.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
X
be a Banach space and
\alpha>0
. A family
\{U(t,s)\in\mathcal{L}(X);t>s\}
is a
process of growth
\alpha
if

(1)

U(t,t)=Id
.

(2)

U(t,\tau)U(\tau,s)=U(t,\tau)\circ U(\tau,s)=U(t,s)
, for all
s<\tau<t
.

(3)

There exists
M>0
such that
\left\|(t-s)^{\alpha}U(t,s)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\leq M
, for all
t>s
.

(4)

(t,s,x)\rightarrow U(t,s)x
is continuous for
t>s
and for all
x\in X
.

As it happens for the semigroups of growth
\alpha
, the process of growth
\alpha
might be discontinuous at the initial time
t=\tau
.

2.2.
Semilinear evolution equations with almost sectorial operators: Abstract theory

Consider the semilinear evolution equation

(2.1)

\begin{cases}u_{t}+A(t)u=F(u),\quad t>\tau,\\
u(\tau)=u_{0}\in X,\end{cases}

where
\{A(t):D(A(t))\subset X\rightarrow X,\mbox{ }t\in\mathbb{R}\}
is a family of
uniformly almost sectorial operators
, that is, for all
t\in\mathbb{R}

(1)

-A(t):D(A(t))\subset X\rightarrow X
is closed, densely defined and
D(A(t))=D=X^{1}
,
\forall t\in\mathbb{R}
.

(2)

There exists
\varphi\in\left(\frac{\pi}{2},\pi\right)
and constants
C>0
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
such that
\Sigma_{\varphi}\cup\{0\}\subset\rho(-A(t)),

and

(2.2)

\left\|(\lambda+A(t))^{-1}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\leq\dfrac{C}{|\lambda|^{\alpha}},\quad\forall\lambda\in\Sigma_{\varphi}.

(3)

The constants
\varphi
,
\alpha
and
C
are the same for the entire family
\{A(t);t\in\mathbb{R}\}
.

We also assume that
\{A(t);t\in\mathbb{R}\}
is
uniformly Hölder continuous
, that is, there exist
\delta\in(0,1]
and a constant
C>0
such that

\left\|[A(t)-A(\tau)]A(s)^{-1}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\leq C|t-\tau|^{\delta},\quad\forall t,\tau,s\in\mathbb{R}.

By taking
\tau=s
we conclude that

\left\|A(t)A(s)^{-1}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\leq 1+C(t-s)^{\delta}

and that
A(t)A(s)^{-1}
is a bounded linear operator in
X
. From the fact that
0\in\rho(-A(t))
and from the continuity of the function
\rho{(-A(t))}\ni\lambda\mapsto(\lambda+A(t))^{-1}
, the inequality (
2.2
) is equivalent to

\left\|(\lambda+A(t))^{-1}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\leq\dfrac{C}{1+|\lambda|^{\alpha}},\quad\forall\lambda\in\Sigma_{\varphi}\cup\{0\}.

Still from (
2.2
) and from the resolvent equality, we have

\left\|A(t)(\lambda+A(t))^{-1}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\leq 1+C|\lambda|^{1-\alpha},\quad\forall\lambda\in\Sigma_{\varphi}\cup\{0\}.

Under those conditions, for a fixed
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
, the operator
-A(\tau)
generates a semigroup of growth
1-\alpha
given by

(2.3)

T_{-A(\tau)}(t)x=\dfrac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma}e^{\lambda t}(\lambda+A(\tau))^{-1}xd\lambda,

where
\Gamma
is the boundary of the sector
\Sigma_{\varphi}
and
\left\|T_{-A(\tau)}(t)\right\|\leq Ct^{\alpha-1}
, for
t>0
.

Since
0\in\rho(-A(\cdot))
and the resolvent is an open set, there exists
\xi>0
such that the family
\{-\xi I+A(t);t\in\mathbb{R}\}
is uniformly almost sectorial. Therefore, as it is done in
[
23
]
for sectorial operators, we conclude that the semigroup has an exponential decay

\left\|T_{-A(\tau)}(t)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\leq Ct^{\alpha-1}e^{-\xi t},\quad\forall t>0.

In
[
9
]
, to obtain the existence of local solution for (
2.1
), the authors studied the problems in three stages: first the autonomous linear case, then the nonautonomous linear equation and, at last, the semilinear case. Next, we briefly mention this path followed by the authors.

2.3.
The autonomous linear problem

Consider the autonomous initial value problem

(2.4)

\begin{cases}u_{t}+A(\tau)u=0,\quad t>0,\\
u(0)=u_{0}\in X,\end{cases}

where
\tau
is fixed. The next result can be found in
[
2
]
and it claims that the semigroup generated by
-A(\tau)
gives a solution for the problem.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\{T_{-A(\tau)}(t);t>0\}
be the semigroup of growth
1-\alpha
defined above. Then
T_{-A(\tau)}(t):(0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(X)
is differentiable and
\frac{d}{dt}T_{-A(\tau)}(t)=-A(\tau)T_{-A(\tau)}(t),

that is, for all
u_{0}\in X
,

\dfrac{d}{dt}T_{-A(\tau)}(t)u_{0}+A(\tau)T_{-A(\tau)}(t)u_{0}=0,\quad\forall t>0,

and
u(t)=T_{-A(\tau)}(t)u_{0}
is a solution of (
2.4
).

We already mentioned that semigroups of growth
1-\alpha
are not necessarily continuous at
t=0
. However, it was proved in
[
2
]
that, for initial conditions in
D=D(A(\cdot))
, the continuity at
t=0
follows.

Lemma 2.6
.

If
u_{0}\in D=D(A(\cdot))
then

(1)

\left\|T_{-A(\tau)}(t)u_{0}-u_{0}\right\|_{X}\rightarrow 0
when
t\rightarrow 0^{+}
.

(2)

T_{-A(\tau)}(t)A(\tau)u_{0}=A(\tau)T_{-A(\tau)}(t)u_{0}
,
\forall t>0
. In this case, the solution
u(t)=T_{-A(\tau)}(t)u_{0}
is continuously differentiable in
(0,\infty)
.

2.4.
The nonautonomous linear problem

Next, we consider

(2.5)

\begin{cases}u_{t}+A(t)u=0,\quad t>\tau,\\
u(\tau)=u_{0}\in X,\quad\tau\in\mathbb{R}.\end{cases}

In
[
9
]
, assuming
\alpha+\delta>1
, where
\delta>0
comes from the Hölder continuity (
2.2
), the authors proved the existence of a family of solution operators
\{U(t,\tau)\in\mathcal{L}(X);\mbox{ }t\geq\tau\}
, given by

(2.6)

U(t,\tau)=T_{-A(\tau)}(t-\tau)+\int_{\tau}^{t}U(t,s)[A(\tau)-A(s)]T_{-A(\tau)}(s-\tau)ds.

Such family is a linear process of growth
1-\alpha
, that is,

\left\|U(t,\tau)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\leq C(t-\tau)^{\alpha-1},\quad\forall t>\tau.

The authors of
[
9
]
also exhibited another integral formulation for this process that makes it easier to deal with the analysis of some properties of the process. Let
\varphi_{1}
and
\Phi
be

\varphi_{1}(t,\tau)=[A(\tau)-A(t)]T_{-A(\tau)}(t-\tau)

\Phi(t,\tau)=\varphi_{1}(t,\tau)+\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{t}\Phi(t,s)\varphi_{1}(s,\tau)ds=\varphi_{1}(t,\tau)+\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{t}\varphi_{1}(t,s)\Phi(s,\tau)ds.

Then, if
\alpha+\frac{\delta}{2}>1
, the linear process (
2.6
) can also be given by

(2.7)

U(t,\tau)=T_{-A(\tau)}(t-\tau)+\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{t}T_{-A(s)}(t-s)\Phi(s,\tau)ds.

The operators
\varphi_{1}(t,\tau)
and
\Phi(t,\tau)
are continuous in
\{(t,\tau)\in\mathbb{R}^{2};\mbox{ }t>\tau\}
and satisfy

(2.8)

\left\|\varphi_{1}(t,\tau)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\leq C(t-\tau)^{\alpha+\delta-2},

(2.9)

\left\|\Phi(t,\tau)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}\leq C(t-\tau)^{\alpha+\delta-2}.

As it happens for the semigroup, if
x\in D(A(\cdot))
then the process is continuous at the initial time.

Proposition 2.7
.

If
x\in D(A(\cdot))
, then
U(t,\tau)x\xrightarrow{t\rightarrow\tau^{+}}x.

Proof.

Note that

\left\|U(t,\tau)x-x\right\|_{X}\leq\left\|T_{-A(\tau)}(t-\tau)x-x\right\|_{X}+\left\|\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{t}T_{-A(s)}(t-s)\Phi(s,\tau)xds\right\|_{X}.

The first term at the right side goes to zero as a consequence of Lemma
2.6
and, using (
2.8
) and (
2.9
) for the second term, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{t}T_{-A(s)}(t-s)\Phi(s,\tau)xds\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(X)}

\displaystyle\leq C\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}(s-\tau)^{\alpha+\delta-2}ds

\displaystyle\leq C\mathcal{B}(\alpha,\alpha+\delta-1)(t-\tau)^{2\alpha+\delta-2}\xrightarrow{t\rightarrow\tau^{+}}0,

where
\mathcal{B}(\cdot,\cdot):(0,\infty)\times(0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is the
Beta function

\mathcal{B}(a,b)=\int_{0}^{1}t^{a-1}(1-t)^{b-1}dt

and
2\alpha+\delta-2=2\left(\alpha+\frac{\delta}{2}-1\right)>0
. ∎

2.5.
Existence of local solution for the semilinear problem

Finally we consider the semilinear case

(2.10)

\begin{cases}u_{t}+A(t)u=F(u),\quad t>\tau,\\
u(\tau)=u_{0}\in X,\end{cases}

where the family
\{A(t);\mbox{ }t\in\mathbb{R}\}
is uniformly almost sectorial and uniformly Hölder continuous.

We also assume that there exists another Banach space
Y
and
0<\beta<1
such that
\beta+\delta>1
and the resolvent satisfies the following estimate

(2.11)

\left\|(\lambda+A(t))^{-1}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y,X)}\leq\dfrac{C}{|\lambda|^{\beta}+1},\qquad\forall\lambda\in\Sigma_{\varphi}\cup\{0\}.

Using (
2.3
), (
2.6
), (
2.11
) and the fact that
0\in\rho(-A(\tau))
, we have, for
t>0
,

\begin{matrix}\left\|T_{-A(\tau)}(t)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y,X)}\leq Ct^{\beta-1}&\mbox{ and }&\left\|T_{-A(\tau)}(t)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y,X)}\leq Ct^{\beta-1}e^{-\xi t}\end{matrix}

and, for
t>\tau
,

\left\|U(t,\tau)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y,X)}\leq C(t-\tau)^{\beta-1}.

We assume that the nonlinearity
F
satisfies:
F:X\rightarrow Y
and there exist constants
C>0\mbox{ and }\rho\geq 1

such that, for all
u,v\in X
,

(2.12)

\left\|F(u)-F(v)\right\|_{Y}\leq C\left\|u-v\right\|_{X}\left(1+\left\|u\right\|_{X}^{\rho-1}+\left\|v\right\|_{X}^{\rho-1}\right),

(2.13)

\left\|F(u)\right\|_{Y}\leq C(1+\left\|u\right\|_{X}^{\rho}).

The exponents
\alpha,\beta,\delta
and
\rho
must satisfy

(2.14)

\dfrac{1}{2}<\alpha,\beta<1,

(2.15)

\dfrac{\beta}{1-\alpha}>1\quad\mbox{ and }\quad 1\leq\rho<\dfrac{\beta}{1-\alpha},

(2.16)

\alpha+\delta>1\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\beta+\delta>1.

Definition 2.8
.

A function
u:(\tau,\tau+t_{0})\rightarrow X
is a
mild solution
for (
2.10
) if

(\tau,\tau+t_{0})\ni t\mapsto w(t):=u(t)-U(t,\tau)u_{0}\in X

is continuous,
\lim_{t\rightarrow\tau^{+}}\left\|w(t)\right\|_{X}=0
and

w(t)=u(t)-U(t,\tau)u_{0}=\int_{\tau}^{t}U(t,s)F(u(s))ds,\quad\forall t\in(\tau,\tau+t_{0}).

We point out here the main idea used in proving the existence of local solution, which is the definition of a contraction map in an appropriate space. This idea will be important in the sequence. For a complete proof, the reader may consult
[
9
, pages 34-36]
or
[
2
, pages 184-187]
(this second one deals with the autonomous case, but the general setting and the ideas applied there are essentially the same).

Since
\{U(t,\tau);t>\tau\}
, solution of (
2.5
), is discontinuous at
t=\tau
we expect that the same happens for solutions of the semilinear case (
2.10
). Hence, given
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
and
u_{0}\in X
, we will search for mild solutions in the following space

K(t_{0},u_{0})=\left\{v\in\mathcal{C}((\tau,\tau+t_{0}),X);\mbox{ }\sup_{t\in(\tau,\tau+t_{0})}\left\|v(t)-U(t,\tau)u_{0}\right\|_{X}\leq\mu\right\},

where
\mu>0
and
t_{0}>0
will be chosen later.
Such space is a Banach space with the norm

\left\|\phi\right\|_{K(t_{0},u_{0})}=\sup_{t\in(\tau,\tau+t_{0})}\left\|\phi(t)-U(t,\tau)u_{0}\right\|_{X}.

Theorem 2.9
.

[
9
, Theorem 3.1]

Suppose (
2.12
) up to (
2.16
) are satisfied. Then, for every
u_{0}\in X
, there exists
t_{0}>0
small enough so that the initial value problem

\begin{cases}u_{t}+A(t)u=F(u),\quad t>\tau,\\
u(\tau)=u_{0}\in X,\end{cases}

has a mild solution in
K(t_{0},u_{0})
. Furthermore, we can extend this mild solution to a maximal interval
(\tau,\tau+t_{\max}(u_{0}))
. Both
t_{0}
and
t_{\max}(u_{0})
depend on
u_{0}
, but can be chosen uniformly for
u_{0}
in bounded sets.

To prove this theorem, the authors considered the operator

T:K(t_{0},u_{0})\rightarrow\mathcal{C}((\tau,\tau+t_{0}),X)
defined by

(Tv)(t):=U(t,\tau)u_{0}+\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{t}U(t,s)F(u(s))ds.

For
t_{0}>0
small enough, they proved that this map is a contraction and its fixed point is a mild solution of (
2.10
). Also, for any
v\in K(t_{0},u_{0})
,

\begin{split}(t-\tau)^{1-\alpha}\left\|v(t)\right\|_{X}&\leq(t-\tau)^{1-\alpha}\left\|v(t)-U(t,\tau)u_{0}\right\|_{X}+(t-\tau)^{1-\alpha}\left\|U(t,\tau)v_{0}\right\|_{X}\\
&\leq(t-\tau)^{1-\alpha}\mu+C\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{X}\leq t_{0}^{1-\alpha}\mu+C\sup_{u_{0}\in B}\left\|u_{0}\right\|\leq k,\end{split}

where
B\subset X
is a bounded set that contains
u_{0}
. It follows that
t_{0}
can be taken uniformly for initial conditions in bounded set,
u_{0}\in B
.

Using the same ideas that appears in
[
2
]
for the autonomous case, we managed to prove the following proposition.

Proposition 2.10
.

In the conditions of Theorem
2.9
, for each
u_{0}\in X
there exists a unique mild solution of (
2.10
),
u:(\tau,\tau+t_{max}(u_{0}))\rightarrow X
, defined in a maximal interval, which satisfies

(1)

t_{max}(u_{0})=+\infty
or
\limsup_{t\rightarrow\tau+t_{max}(u_{0})}\left\|u(t)\right\|_{X}=+\infty.

(2)

The solution depends continuously on the initial data in the following sense: if
u_{0}\in X
and
t^{*}<t_{max}(u_{0})
, then there exists
\delta>0
small enough such that

\left\|u_{0}-v_{0}\right\|_{X}<\delta\mbox{ and }t\in(\tau,\tau+t^{*})\Rightarrow\left\|u(t,\tau,u_{0})-v(t,\tau,v_{0})\right\|_{X}\leq C(t-\tau)^{\alpha-1}\left\|u_{0}-v_{0}\right\|_{X},

that is, the solutions of (
2.10
) behave as the solutions of the associated linear problem.

2.6.
Pullback and uniform attractors

Let
X
be a Banach space and
\{S(t,\tau):X\rightarrow X;t\geq s\}
a family of operators satisfying:

(1)

S(t,t)=I_{X}
, for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

(2)

S(t,s)=S(t,\tau)S(\tau,s)
, for all
t\geq\tau\geq s
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
.

(3)

(s,\infty)\ni t\mapsto S(t,s)x
is continuous for all
x\in X
.

Such family is called an
evolution process in
X
and we also denote it by
S(\cdot,\cdot)
.

Given any
x\in X
,
there are two distinct manners of studying the asymptotic dynamics of such evolution process: One called the
pullback dynamics
that basically fixes the final time
t
and evaluate what happens to
S(t,s)x
when

s\rightarrow-\infty
and the other called
forward dynamics
, which consider
S(t,s)x
when
s
is fixed and
t\rightarrow\infty
.

The pullback dynamics can be described by an object in the phase space called
pullback attractor
. We recall in the sequence some basic concepts and results of the theory of pullback attractor. We refer to
[
10
]
and references therein for further details.

Furthermore, throughout the text, we shall use the Hausdorff semidistance to compare the distance between two sets in the phase space
X
, that is, given
A,B\subset X
, the
Hausdorff semidistance
between
A
and
B
is given by

dist(A,B)=\sup_{a\in A}\inf_{b\in B}d(a,b).

Definition 2.11
.

Let
S(\cdot,\cdot)
be a process. A family
A(\cdot)=\{A(t)\subset X;t\in\mathbb{R}\}

pullback attracts
B\subset X
if, for each
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

dist(S(t,s)B,A(t))\rightarrow 0\mbox{ when }s\rightarrow-\infty.

Definition 2.12
.

The
pullback attractor
of
S(\cdot,\cdot)
is a family
\mathcal{A}(\cdot)=\{\mathcal{A}(t)\subset X;\mbox{ }t\in\mathbb{R}\}
that satisfies:

(1)

\mathcal{A}(t)
is compact for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

(2)

\mathcal{A}(\cdot)
is
invariant
by
S(\cdot,\cdot)
, that is,
S(t,s)\mathcal{A}(s)=\mathcal{A}(t)
, for all
t\geq s
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
.

(3)

\mathcal{A}(\cdot)
pullback attracts bounded sets of
X
.

(4)

\mathcal{A}(\cdot)
is the minimal closed family that satisfies
(3)
.

Theorem 2.13
.

(See
[
10
]
)
Let
S(\cdot,\cdot)
be a process. The statements below are equivalent:

(1)

S(\cdot,\cdot)
has a pullback attractor
\mathcal{A}(\cdot)
.

(2)

There exists a family of compact sets
K(\cdot)
that pullback attracts bounded sets of
X
.

When it comes to the forward dynamics, the object that describes the asymptotic behavior is called
uniform attractor
. We present some concepts and results next, referring to
[
5
,
10
,
12
,
27
]
for more details.

Definition 2.14
.

Let
S(\cdot,\cdot)
be a process. A set
A\subset X
is
uniformly attracting
if, for any
B\subset X
bounded,

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\left(\sup_{s\in\mathbb{R}}dist\left(S(t+s,s)B,A\right)\right)=0.

Definition 2.15
.

The minimal compact attracting set for
S(\cdot,\cdot)
is called
uniform attractor
, denoted by
\overline{\mathcal{A}}
.

Theorem 2.16
.

(See
[
10
]
)
Let
S(\cdot,\cdot)
be a process. The statements below are equivalent:

(1)

S(\cdot,\cdot)
has uniform attractor
\overline{\mathcal{A}}
.

(2)

There exists a compact uniformly attracting set
K
for the process
S(\cdot,\cdot)
.

In those cases,
S(\cdot,\cdot)
also has a pullback attractor
\mathcal{A}(\cdot)
and

\cup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{A}(t)\subset\overline{\mathcal{A}}.

Remark 2.17
.

The results on existence of attractors provided in
[
10
]
are all obtained under the assumption that
[s,\infty)\ni t\mapsto S(t,s)x
is continuous for all
x\in X
, which includes left extreme of the interval,
t=s
. The processes that we will obtain in this work will not be continuous at the initial time. Nevertheless, the results on characterization and existence of attractors remain valid and no significant changes in the proofs of those results are necessary.

4.
Positivity and Monotonicity of solutions

We will prove in this section two features of the parabolic reaction-diffusion equation that allow us to conclude that the solution of the semilinear problem is bounded in bounded intervals. Consequently, this solution is globally defined.

The first feature is presented in Theorem
4.4
, which states that a solution that starts in a positive initial condition remains positive as long as it exists. The other is given in Theorem
4.6
and it corresponds to the order preserving of the semilinear evolution equation, that is, if
(w_{0},v_{0})\leq(w_{1},v_{1})
in
U_{p}^{0}
, then the solution obtained for the first initial condition is less than the solution obtained for the second, as long as they exist.

We will follow the ideas presented in
[
4
, Apendix A]
in order to obtained these two theorems mentioned above.

With those properties, we will be able to locate our mild solution

(w,v):(\tau,\tau+\tau_{max})\rightarrow U_{p}^{0}
of (
1.8
) between two bounded functions
\gamma^{+},\gamma^{-}:I\subset\mathbb{R}\rightarrow U_{p}^{0}
, that is

\gamma^{-}(t)\leq(w,v)(t)\leq\gamma^{+}(t),\quad\forall t\in(\tau,\tau+t_{\max}).

This implies that the solution
(w,v)(t)
is bounded.

Definition 4.1
.

Given two partially ordered Banach spaces
(X,\leq)
and
(Y,\leq)
, a map
T:X\rightarrow Y
is
positive
if
x\geq 0\Rightarrow Tx\geq 0
and is
increasing
if
x\leq\tilde{x}\Rightarrow Tx\leq T\tilde{x}
.

4.1.
The Semigroup of growth
1-\alpha
is positive

We already known that, for every
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
and every

, each real number

belongs to the resolvent of
-A_{\varepsilon}(t)
. Based in
[

, Theorem 6.43]
, we can prove that this family of operators has the following property: for any
\lambda>0
,
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
and

U_{p}^{\varepsilon}\ni x\geq 0\Rightarrow(\lambda+A_{\varepsilon}(t))^{-1}x\geq 0

(we point out that this include the limiting case and it is a consequence of the fact that the resolvent of
A_{\varepsilon}(t)
for
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
converges to the resolvent of
A_{0}(t)
, see
[
1
]
,
[

With the property above, added to the fact that every semigroup can be given by the exponential formula

T_{-A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)}(t)x=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(I+\dfrac{t}{n}A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)\right)^{-n}x,\quad\forall t>0,

it is straight forward to conclude that the semigroup is positive, that is,

U_{p}^{\varepsilon}\ni x\geq 0\Rightarrow T_{-A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)}(t)x\geq 0,\quad\forall t>0.

4.2.
The Linear process of growth
1-\alpha
is positive

We prove now that the linear process
\{U_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau):U_{p}^{\varepsilon}\rightarrow U_{p}^{\varepsilon};t>\tau\}

\begin{cases}u_{t}+A_{\varepsilon}(t)u=0,\quad t>\tau,\\

\varepsilon\in[0,1]

The proof for the sectorial case, where
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
is very similar to the proof for the almost sectorial case
\varepsilon=0

If we tried to prove that
\{U_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau);t>\tau\}

2.6

\beta>0
, the family

\begin{cases}u_{t}+(A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)+\beta I)u=0,\quad t>\tau,\\

Besides, the sectoriality can be chosen to remain the same (same sectors and same constants that bounds the resolvent), once
-\beta I
just shift the sector of
-A_{\varepsilon}(t)
to the left.

The family
\{U_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)-T_{-(A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)+\beta I)}(t-\tau);t>\tau\}

\begin{cases}u_{t}+A_{\varepsilon}(t)u=-[A_{\varepsilon}(t)-A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)]T_{-(A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)+\beta I)}(t-\tau)+\beta T_{-(A_{\varepsilon}(t)+\beta I)}(t-\tau),\quad t>\tau\\

\begin{split}U_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)=&\hskip 5.69046ptT_{-(A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)+\beta I)}(t-\tau)\\
&+\int_{\tau}^{t}U_{\varepsilon}(t,s)\left\{[A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)-A_{\varepsilon}(s)]T_{-(A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)+\beta I)}(s-\tau)+\beta T_{-(A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)+\beta I)}(s-\tau)\right\}ds.\end{split}

With this new characterization for the process, we can prove it is positive. Before doing so, we enunciate a lemma, consequence of

Clearly,
u(t,\tau,u_{0})
is also a solution for this problem, but now it can be obtained as a fixed point of

\mathcal{F}_{0}^{\beta}(u)(t)=U_{0}^{\beta}(t,\tau)u_{0}+\int_{\tau}^{t}U^{\beta}_{0}(t,s)[F_{0}(u(s))+\beta u(s)]ds.

From the previous discussion,
\{U_{0}^{\beta}(t,\tau);t>\tau\}
is also positive and
F(u(s))+\beta u(s)\geq 0
. Therefore, if we restrict the map
\mathcal{F}_{0}^{\beta}
to

K_{0}^{+}(t_{0},u_{0})=\{u\in K_{0}(t_{0},u_{0});\mbox{ }u\geq 0\},

we have
\mathcal{F}_{0}^{\beta}(K_{0}^{+}(t_{0},u_{0}))\subset K_{0}^{+}(t_{0},u_{0})
. Hence, the fixed point of
\mathcal{F}_{0}^{\beta}
belongs to
K_{0}^{+}(t_{0},u_{0})
, that is,
u(t,\tau,u_{0})\geq 0
, for all
t\in[\tau,t_{0})
.

For the general case where
u_{0}\in D(A_{0}(\cdot))
with
u_{0}\geq 0
, we consider the sequence
u_{n}=u_{0}+\frac{1}{n}I
and let
u_{n}(t)
be the solution in its maximal interval
[\tau,t_{n})
of the problem

\begin{cases}u_{t}+A_{0}(t)u=F_{0}(u),\quad t>\tau,\\
u(\tau)=u_{n}.\end{cases}

Once we have
u_{n}\rightarrow u_{0}
in
U_{p}^{0}
and in
U_{\infty}^{0}
, and from the fact that the maximal interval of existence can be taken uniformly in bounded set (see discussion after Theorem
2.9
), we have
t_{n}=t_{0}
. It follows from Proposition
2.10
, item (2), that
u_{n}(t)\rightarrow u_{0}(t)
in
U_{p}^{0}
, for
t\in[\tau,t_{0})
, which guarantees that
u_{0}(t)\geq 0
.

With continuation arguments, we conclude that the solution is positive as long as it exists.

For the nonlimiting case
(\varepsilon\in(0,1])
, we have the continuity of the solution for every initial condition
u_{0}\in U_{p}^{\varepsilon}
. Therefore we can ensure that
F(u(s))
is bounded for every
s\in[\tau,t_{0})
. Besides, we do not need the hypothesis
\alpha+\frac{\delta}{2}>1
to ensure the process
\{U_{\varepsilon}^{\beta}(t,\tau);t>\tau\}
is positive.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

Even though positive initial conditions
u_{0}\in U_{p}^{0}
imply that

T_{-A_{0}(\tau)}(t)u_{0}\geq 0\quad\mbox{and}\quad U_{0}(t,\tau)u_{0}\geq 0,

this, in general, will not be true for the solution
u(t,\tau,u_{0})
of the semilinear equation. This is a consequence of the discontinuity that might appear in the initial time, added to the presence of the nonlinearity
F_{0}
.

However, Theorem
4.4
assures the positivity of
u(t,\tau,u_{0})
when the initial condition is in the domain of
A_{0}(\tau)
. For this case, we remove the discontinuity on the initial time.

This is an important difference between the limiting case and the case
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
, where the positivity is assured for every initial condition
u_{0}\in U_{p}^{\varepsilon}
.

4.4.
Comparison results

We now prove the order preserving property of the semilinear evolution equation. This is done in the next theorem and we compare solutions under two different scenarios: One when we make changes in the initial condition and other when we make changes in the nonlinearity.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
F_{\varepsilon}(\cdot),G_{\varepsilon}(\cdot):U_{p}^{\varepsilon}\rightarrow U_{p}^{\varepsilon}
be maps satisfying the following hypothesis:
for every
r>0
, there exists
\beta=\beta(r)
such that
F_{\varepsilon}(\cdot)+\beta I
and
G_{\varepsilon}(\cdot)+\beta I
are increasing in
B_{U_{p}^{\varepsilon}}(0,r)
. For
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
, we have

(1)

If
u_{0},u_{1}\in U_{p}^{\varepsilon}
,
u_{0}\geq u_{1}
, then the solutions
u(t,\tau,u_{i})
,
i=0,1
, of the problems

u_{t}+A_{\varepsilon}(t)u=F_{\varepsilon}(u);\quad u(\tau)=u_{i},

will satisfy
u(t,\tau,u_{0})\geq u(t,\tau,u_{1})
as long as they exist.

(2)

If
F_{\varepsilon}(\cdot)\geq G_{\varepsilon}(\cdot)
for every
u_{0}\in U_{p}^{{\varepsilon}}
, and
u_{F}(t,\tau,u_{0})
,
u_{G}(t,\tau,u_{0})
are the solutions of the evolution equations

\displaystyle u_{t}+A_{\varepsilon}(t)u

\displaystyle=F_{\varepsilon}(u);\quad u(\tau)=u_{0},

\displaystyle u_{t}+A_{\varepsilon}(t)u

\displaystyle=G_{\varepsilon}(u);\quad u(\tau)=u_{0},

respectively, we have
u_{F}(t,\tau,u_{0})\geq u_{G}(t,\tau,u_{0})
as long as they exist.

For the limiting case
\varepsilon=0
, the two claims above remain valid if
u_{0},u_{1}\in D(A_{0}(\cdot))
and
\alpha+\frac{\delta}{2}>1
.

Proof.

We prove the theorem for the limiting case. Let
u_{0},u_{1}\in D(A_{0}(\cdot))
. We already know that
u_{i}(t)=u_{F}(t,\tau,u_{i})
is the unique fixed point of

\mathcal{F}_{0}^{\beta}(u)(t)=U_{0}^{\beta}(t,\tau)u_{i}+\int_{\tau}^{t}U_{0}^{\beta}(t,s)\left[F_{0}(u(s))+\beta Iu(s)\right]ds

in the space

V_{i}=\left\{u\in\mathcal{C}((\tau,t_{0}),U_{p}^{0});\quad u(\tau)=u_{i};\quad\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}((\tau,t_{0}),U_{p}^{0})}\leq(t-\tau)^{\alpha-1}(\mu+C\left\|u_{i}\right\|_{U_{p}^{0}})\right\},

for small
t_{0}-\tau>0
.

Consider
V_{0}^{+}=\{u\in V_{0};u(t)\geq u_{1}(t)\}.

This space is not empty for small
t_{0}-\tau
, once
u_{1}(t)+u_{0}-u_{1}
belongs to
V_{0}^{+}
(due to the continuity of the functions
u_{0}(t)
and
u_{1}(t)
at
[\tau,t_{0})
).

Besides,
F_{0}(u_{0})+\beta Iu_{0}\geq F_{0}(u_{1})+\beta Iu_{1}
for
\beta
large enough and this map is increasing. Hence,
\mathcal{F}_{0}^{\beta}(V_{0}^{+})\subset V_{0}^{+}.

Therefore, the fixed point of
\mathcal{F}_{0}^{\beta}
belongs to
V_{0}^{+}
and
u_{0}(t)\geq u_{1}(t),
for
t\in[\tau,t_{0})
.

A continuation argument implies that
u_{0}(t)\geq u_{1}(t),
as long the solutions exist.

The case where we change the nonlinearities follows the same ideas.

As for the case
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
, we proceed the same way, without the necessity of imposing that the initial conditions to belong in the domain of
A_{\varepsilon}(\cdot)
.∎

Remark 4.7
.

As a consequence of Theorems
4.4
,
4.6
and from what we have pointed out in Remark
4.5
, we will only have comparison results for the limiting case when the initial conditions belong to
D(A_{0}(\cdot))
.

5.
Global solutions and existence of attractors

The previous results about positivity and comparison of solutions are applied in this section to prove Theorem
5.3
below, which ensures that the solutions obtained for the problem are globally defined. To this end, we impose that the nonlinearity
f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfies the dissipativeness condition

(5.1)

\limsup_{|s|\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{f(s)}{s}<1.

This dissipativeness implies the next result.

Lemma 5.1
.

There exist
r>0
and
\xi\in(0,1)
such that, for every
s>r
,
f(s)<\xi s.

Proof.

Let
\xi:=\sup_{|s|>r}\frac{f(s)}{s}<1
.
There exists
r>0
such that, for all
|s|>r
,
\frac{f(s)}{s}\leq\xi
. Therefore, if
s>r
, than
f(s)\leq\xi s
.
∎

For
s\in[0,r]
, there exists
M>0
such that
f(s)\leq M
, since
f
is continuous. Then

f(s)\leq\begin{cases}\xi s,\mbox{ if }s>r,\\
M,\mbox{ if }s\in[0,r],\end{cases}

which implies that, if
v
is a positive solution in
U_{p}^{\varepsilon}
,

F_{\varepsilon}(v)(x)=f(v(x))\leq\begin{cases}\xi v(x),\mbox{ if }v(x)>r,\\
M,\mbox{ if }v(x)\in[0,r].\end{cases}

By considering in
U_{p}^{\varepsilon}
the constant function
K_{M}(x)=M
, we have

F_{\varepsilon}(\cdot)\leq\xi\cdot+K_{M}.

To prove the mild solutions are globally defined in time we focus on the case
\varepsilon=0
.
Consider, for
u_{0}\in D(A_{0}(\cdot))
, the three following evolution equations, for
t>\tau
,

\begin{matrix}\begin{cases}u_{t}+A_{0}(t)u=F_{0}(u),\\
u(\tau)=u_{0}\in D(A_{0}(\cdot)),\end{cases}&\begin{cases}u^{+}_{t}+A_{0}(t)u^{+}=F_{0}u^{+},\\
u^{+}(\tau)=|u_{0}|\in D(A_{0}(\cdot)),\end{cases}&\begin{cases}\gamma_{t}+A_{0}(t)\gamma=\xi\gamma+K_{M},\\
\gamma(\tau)=|u_{0}|\in D(A_{0}(\cdot)),\end{cases}\end{matrix}

with solutions, respectively, given by

u(\cdot,\tau,u_{0}),\mbox{ }u^{+}(\cdot,\tau,|u_{0}|)\mbox{ and }\gamma(\cdot,\tau,|u_{0}|),

defined each on its maximal interval. Then we use the comparison results proved previously to conclude that

u(t,\tau,u_{0})\leq u^{+}(t,\tau,|u_{0}|)\leq\gamma(t,\tau,|u_{0}|),

as long as they exist. In the same way, if we consider the problems, for
t>\tau
,

\begin{cases}u_{t}+A_{0}(t)u=F_{0}(u),\\
u(\tau)=u_{0}\in D(A_{0}(\cdot)),\end{cases}\begin{cases}u^{-}_{t}+A_{0}(t)u^{-}=F_{0}u^{-},\\
u^{-}(\tau)=-|u_{0}|\in D(A_{0}(\cdot)),\end{cases}\begin{cases}\gamma^{-}_{t}+A_{0}(t)\gamma^{-}=\xi\gamma^{-}-K_{M},\\
\gamma^{-}(\tau)=-|u_{0}|\in D(A_{0}(\cdot)),\end{cases}

we would have

u(t,\tau,u_{0})\geq u^{-}(t,\tau,-|u_{0}|)\geq\gamma^{-}(t,\tau,-|u_{0}|),

where these functions are the solutions of the above problems, respectively, and the inequality is valid as long as they exists. Due to the linearity and the uniqueness of solution, we can easily show that
\gamma^{-}(t,\tau,-|u_{0}|)=-\gamma(t,\tau,|u_{0}|)
. Therefore,

(5.2)

|u(t,\tau,u_{0})|\leq\gamma(t,\tau,|u_{0}|),

as long as the solutions exist. We have located the mild solution for the semilinear equation among two functions
\gamma
and
-\gamma
which are now solutions of linear equations and, therefore, is an easy task to obtain bounds for them. To do it, we need the following lemma, which is a consequence of Lemma
4.2
, the bounds (
2.8
), (
2.9
) and the formulation (
2.7
) that we have for the process.

Lemma 5.2
.

There exist
M,\tilde{M},\nu>0
such that, for
\varepsilon\in(0,1]

\left\|U_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)u_{0}\right\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq Me^{-\nu(t-\tau)}(t-\tau)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})},\quad t>\tau,u_{0}\in L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}),

\left\|U_{\varepsilon}(t,\tau)u_{0}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq\tilde{M}e^{-\nu(t-\tau)}(t-\tau)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})},\quad t>\tau,u_{0}\in L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}),

where
M
and
\tilde{M}
depend of
N
and
p
,
\nu
comes from the exponential decay that the semigroup generated by
-A_{\varepsilon}(\tau)
possesses. For the case
\varepsilon=0
,
t>\tau
and
u_{0}\in L^{p}(\Omega)\times L^{p}(0,1)
,

\left\|U_{0}(t,\tau)u_{0}\right\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)\times W^{1,p}(0,1)}\leq Me^{-\nu(t-\tau)}(t-\tau)^{-\frac{1}{2}-(1-\alpha)}\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)\times L^{p}(0,1)},

\left\|U_{0}(t,\tau)u_{0}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)\times L^{\infty}(0,1)}\leq\tilde{M}e^{-\nu(t-\tau)}(t-\tau)^{-\frac{1}{2}-(1-\alpha)}\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)\times L^{p}(0,1)}.

The constants can be chosen uniformly for
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
.

We can now prove:

Theorem 5.3
.

For any
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
, let
u_{0}\in U_{p}^{\varepsilon}.
The solution of the semilinear evolution problem

\begin{cases}u_{t}+A_{\varepsilon}(t)u=F_{\varepsilon}(u),\\
u(\tau)=u_{0}\in U_{p}^{\varepsilon},\end{cases}

is globally defined. Moreover, there exist
K_{\infty},K_{\frac{1}{2}}>0
, depending only on
N
and
p
(they can be chosen uniformly on
\varepsilon
), such that

(5.3)

\limsup_{s\rightarrow-\infty}\left\|u(t,s,u_{0})\right\|_{\infty}\leq K_{\infty},

(5.4)

\limsup_{s\rightarrow-\infty}\left\|u(t,s,u_{0})\right\|_{\frac{1}{2}}\leq K_{\frac{1}{2}}

and those limits are uniform for initial conditions in bounded subsets of
U_{p}^{\varepsilon}
. The norms above are the following

\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\infty}=\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\mbox{ and }\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\frac{1}{2}}=\left\|\cdot\right\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})},\mbox{ for }\varepsilon\in(0,1],

\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\infty}=\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)\times L^{\infty}(0,1)}\mbox{ and }\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\frac{1}{2}}=\left\|\cdot\right\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)\times W^{1,p}(0,1)},\mbox{ for }\varepsilon=0.

The only additional hypothesis we need for the limiting case is that
\alpha+\frac{\delta}{2}>1
.

Proof.

We give a proof for the case
\varepsilon=0
.
Let
B\subset D(A_{0}(\cdot))
be a bounded set in
U_{p}^{0}
, that is, for
u_{0}\in B
,
\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{U_{p}^{0}}\leq L
. We know from (
5.2
) and from the embedding
D(A_{0}(\cdot))\hookrightarrow W^{1,p}(\Omega)\oplus W^{1,p}(0,1)
that
\left\|u(t,s,u_{0})\right\|_{\infty}\leq\left\|\gamma(t,s,|u_{0}|)\right\|_{\infty}.

From the variation of parameters formula

\gamma(t,s,|u_{0}|)=U_{0}(t,s)|u_{0}|+\int_{s}^{t}U_{0}(t,\theta)\left[\xi\gamma(\theta,s,|u_{0}|)+K_{M}(\theta)\right]d\theta.

By Lemma
5.2
and Gronwall’s Lemma, denoting
\phi(t)=\left\|\gamma(t,s,|u_{0}|)\right\|_{\infty}
and
\beta=\frac{1}{2}+(1-\alpha)
, we have

\displaystyle\phi(t)

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{M}e^{-\nu(t-s)}(t-s)^{-\beta}\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{U_{p}^{\varepsilon}}+\int_{s}^{t}Ce^{-\nu(t-\theta)}(t-\theta)^{-\beta}\left[\phi(\theta)+M\right]d\theta

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{M}e^{-\nu(t-s)}(t-s)^{-\beta}\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{U_{p}^{\varepsilon}}+CM\int_{s}^{t}e^{-\nu(t-\theta)}(t-\theta)^{-\beta}d\theta+

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt+\int_{s}^{t}Ce^{-\nu(t-\theta)}(t-\theta)^{-\beta}\left[\phi(\theta)+M\right]d\theta

\displaystyle\leq\left\{CLe^{-\nu(t-s)}(t-s)^{-\beta}+C\int_{s}^{t}e^{-\nu(t-\theta)}(t-\theta)^{-\beta}d\theta\right\}exp\left(C{\int_{s}^{t}e^{-\nu(t-\theta)}(t-\theta)^{-\beta}d\theta}\right),

where
C
is a constant that can be chosen uniformly for
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
, as a consequence of Lemma
5.2
.

The integral appearing after the last inequality can be uniformly bounded in
s
and
t
, as a matter of fact,

\int_{s}^{t}e^{-\nu(t-\theta)}(t-\theta)^{-\beta}d\theta\leq\dfrac{e^{-\nu}}{\nu}+\dfrac{1}{1-\beta}=C_{\beta}.

Therefore, taking
K_{\infty}-1=CC_{\beta}e^{CC_{\beta}}
, we have

(5.5)

\phi(t)\leq e^{CC_{\beta}}CLe^{-\nu(t-s)}(t-s)^{-\beta}+K_{\infty}-1.

This is clearly bounded for
t
in bounded intervals (away from the initial time
s
). Besides, noting that

e^{-\nu(t-s)}(t-s)^{-\beta}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle s\rightarrow-\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}0,

we have

\limsup_{s\rightarrow-\infty}\left\|\gamma(t,s,\tau)\right\|_{\infty}=K_{\infty}-1\leq K_{\infty},

uniformly in the bounded set
B
.

For the general case where
B
is a subset of
U_{p}^{0}
, we note that the nonlinear process regularizes immediately, meaning that if we fix a small
0<\eta<t_{0}
,

u(s+\eta,s,u_{0})\in D(A_{0}(\cdot))

and, from Proposition
2.10
, if
B\subset B_{U_{p}^{0}}[0,L]
, then

\left\|u(s+\eta,s,u_{0})\right\|_{U_{p}^{0}}\leq\dfrac{CL}{\eta^{1-\alpha}}.

Therefore, for
t>s+\eta
,
u(t,s,u_{0})=u(t,s+\eta,\tilde{u}_{0})
, where
\tilde{u}_{0}=u(s+\eta,s,u_{0})
and
\tilde{u}_{0}
belongs to
B_{U_{p}^{0}}\left[0,\frac{CL}{\eta^{1-\alpha}}\right]\cap D(A_{0}(\cdot)).
Hence

\limsup_{s\rightarrow-\infty}\left\|u(t,s,u_{0})\right\|_{\infty}=\limsup_{s\rightarrow-\infty}\left\|u(t,s+\eta,\tilde{u}_{0})\right\|_{\infty}\leq K_{\infty}.

We know now that
\left\|u(t,s,u_{0})\right\|_{\infty}
can be uniformly bounded for
u_{0}\in B\subset B_{U_{p}^{0}}[0,L]
, at least for
t
not close to the initial time
s
. In that case,
F_{0}(u(\cdot))\in U_{p}^{0}
and we have a bound

\left\|F_{0}(u(\theta,s,u_{0}))\right\|_{U_{p}^{0}}\leq C,

where
C=C(\left\|u(\theta,s,u_{0})\right\|_{U_{p}^{0}})
.

Consider
t\geq\theta
, where
\theta=s+a
and
a
is large enough so that

\left\|u(t,s,u_{0})\right\|_{\infty}\leq K_{\infty}.

We also note that, from the fact that
u(t,s,u_{0})\in D(A_{0}(\cdot))\hookrightarrow\mathcal{C}(\Omega)\oplus\mathcal{C}(0,1)
,
we have

\left\|u(t,s,u_{0})\right\|_{U_{p}^{0}}\leq C\left\|u(t,s,u_{0})\right\|_{\infty}

and
C
can be taken uniformly in
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
.

So, writing
\beta=-\frac{1}{2}-(1-\alpha)
, we have

\left\|u(t,s,u_{0})\right\|_{\frac{1}{2}}=\left\|u(t,t_{0},u(t_{0},s,u_{0}))\right\|_{\frac{1}{2}}\leq Ce^{-\nu(t-t_{0})}(t-t_{0})^{-\beta}K_{\infty}+C\int_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{-\nu(t-\theta)}(t-\theta)^{-\beta}d\theta.

The first term goes to zero as
s\rightarrow-\infty
(
t_{0}\rightarrow-\infty
) and the second converges (independent of
t
or the bounded set
B
). This way we proved the existence of a constant
K_{\frac{1}{2}}
such that (
5.4
) is satisfied.
∎

5.1.
Existence of pullback attractor

As pointed out in the previous theorem, the constants
K_{\infty}
and
K_{\frac{1}{2}}
are independent of
t
and
B
, and can even be chosen uniformly in
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
. Thus

B_{U_{\infty}^{0}}\left[0,K_{\infty}\right]

is a set that pullback absorbs bounded sets of
U_{p}^{0}
in the
U_{\infty}^{0}
norm, and

B_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)\oplus W^{1,p}(0,1)}\left[0,K_{\frac{1}{2}}\right]

is a set that pullback absorbs bounded sets of
U_{p}^{0}
in the
W^{1,p}(\Omega)\oplus W^{1,p}(0,1)
norm.

The existence of those two closed balls that pullback absorb in stronger norms than the one defined for
U_{p}^{\varepsilon}
allows us to prove the main result on this section, Theorem
5.4
, which states the existence of pullback attractors. Besides, the uniformity of the constants
K_{\infty}
and
K_{\frac{1}{2}}
implies that we can obtain uniform bounds for the attractors.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let

N<p
,
X_{\varepsilon}=U_{p}^{\varepsilon}

and
f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfying both growth and dissipativeness conditions ((
1.3
) and (
5.1
)). If
K_{\infty}
and
K_{\frac{1}{2}}
are the constants obtained previously, then, for every
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
, the semilinear evolution problem

\begin{cases}u_{t}+A_{\varepsilon}(t)u=F_{\varepsilon}(u),\\
u(\tau)=u_{0}\in U_{p}^{\varepsilon},\end{cases}

has a pullback attractor
\{\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}(t);t\in\mathbb{R}\}
in
X_{\varepsilon}
such that

(5.6)

\bigcup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}(t)\subset B_{U_{\infty}^{\varepsilon}}\left[0,K_{\infty}\right].

Besides, for some
0<\nu<1
,

(5.7)

\bigcup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}(t)\subset{C}^{\nu}(\overline{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}),\quad\mbox{ when }\varepsilon\in(0,1],

\bigcup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{A}_{0}(t)\subset{C}^{\nu}(\overline{\Omega})\oplus{C}^{\nu}(0,1),\quad\mbox{ when }\varepsilon=0.

Moreover,
\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}(t)
pullback attracts bounded sets of
X_{\varepsilon}
in the topology of
{C}(\overline{\Omega_{\varepsilon}})
and
{C}(\overline{\Omega})\oplus{C}([0,1]),
for
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
and
\varepsilon=0
, respectively.

Proof.

Once
W^{1,p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
and
W^{1,p}(\Omega)\oplus W^{1,p}(0,1)
are compactly embedded in
U_{p}^{\varepsilon}
and
U_{p}^{0}
, respectively, we can conclude that the immersion of the two balls

B_{W^{1,p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\left[0,K_{\frac{1}{2}}\right]\quad\mbox{ and }\quad B_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)\oplus W^{1,p}(0,1)}\left[0,K_{\frac{1}{2}}\right]

are compact sets of
U_{p}^{\varepsilon}
and
U_{p}^{0}
, respectively, that pullback absorb bounded sets. Therefore, it follows from Theorem
2.13
that the associated process possesses pulback attractors
\{\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}(t);t\in\mathbb{R}\}
, for
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
.

From the fact that
B_{U_{\infty}^{\varepsilon}}\left[0,K_{\infty}\right]
is pullback absorbing, we then have (
5.6
). For the assertion (
5.7
) of the theorem, we consider the case
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
. The other needs exactly the same arguments.

We know that there exists a bounded subset in
W^{1,p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
that pullback absorbs. Moreover, this space is continuously embedded in
{C}^{\nu}(\overline{\Omega_{\varepsilon}})
for
0<\nu<1-\frac{N}{p}
(see
[
8
, Theorem 9.16]
) and

\bigcup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}(t)\subset W^{1,p}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow{C}^{\nu}(\overline{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}).

For the last assertion and
\varepsilon=0
, we consider the realization of the operator
A_{0}(t)
in
{C}(\overline{\Omega})\oplus{C}(0,1)
, which is sectorial (Proposition
3.1
).
The nonlinear process generated in this case
\tilde{S}_{0}(t,s):{C}(\overline{\Omega})\oplus{C}(0,1)\rightarrow{C}(\overline{\Omega})\oplus{C}(0,1)
will also have a pullback attractor
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(t)
. Clearly
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(t)\subset\mathcal{A}_{0}(t)
, since the second one attracts a bigger universe.

On the other hand, given any bounded set
B_{0}\subset U_{p}^{0}
,
S_{0}(t,s)B_{0}
is bounded in
W^{1,p}(\Omega)\oplus W^{1,p}(0,1)
, for any
t>s
. Hence,
S_{0}(t,s)B_{0}
is bounded in
{C}(\overline{\Omega})\oplus{C}(0,1)
and is attracted by
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(t)
in the topology of
{C}(\overline{\Omega})\oplus{C}(0,1)
and, consequently, in the topology of
U_{p}^{0}
.

Therefore,
\mathcal{A}_{0}(t)\subset\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(t)
, the equality of those sets follows and
\mathcal{A}(t)
attracts bounded sets of
U_{p}^{0}
also in the topology of
{C}(\overline{\Omega})\oplus C(0,1)

5.2.
Existence of uniform attractor

A closer look to the proof of Theorem
5.3
(inequality (
5.5
)) shows us that

\left\|u(t,s,u_{0})\right\|_{\infty}\leq\left\|\gamma(t,s,|u_{0}|)\right\|_{\infty}\leq e^{CC_{\beta}}CLe^{-\nu(t-s)}(t-s)^{-\beta}+K_{\infty}-1

and, if
t=s+\tau
,
\tau>0
,

\left\|u(\tau+s,s,u_{0})\right\|_{\infty}\leq e^{CC_{\beta}}CLe^{-\nu\tau}(\tau)^{-\beta}+K_{\infty}-1.

This implies that

\limsup_{\tau\rightarrow\infty}\left\|u(\tau+s,s,u_{0})\right\|_{\infty}\leq K_{\infty}

and this limsup is uniform for
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
u_{0}
in
B_{U_{p}^{\varepsilon}}[0,L]
. Therefore, the pullback absorbing set

B_{U_{\infty}^{\varepsilon}}\left[0,K_{\infty}\right],\quad\varepsilon\in[0,1],

is also forward attracting.

The same analysis can be done to
\left\|u(s+\tau,s,u_{0})\right\|_{\frac{1}{2}}
and we have

\limsup_{\tau\rightarrow\infty}\left\|u(\tau+s,s,u_{0})\right\|_{\frac{1}{2}}\leq K_{\frac{1}{2}},

uniform for

and

in a bounded set of

B_{W^{1,p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\left[0,K_{\frac{1}{2}}\right],\mbox{for }\varepsilon\in(0,1],\mbox{ and }B_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)\oplus W^{1,p}(0,1)}\left[0,K_{\frac{1}{2}}\right],\mbox{ for }\varepsilon=0

are also forward attracting.

This discussion proves the next result.

Remark 5.7
.

Note that the pullback attractor and the uniform attractor focus on different features of the problem. The first one deals with the pullback dynamics and is an invariant set. The invariance of the pullback attractor is replaced by the minimality of the uniform attractor, which is a fixed set in the phase space that attracts forward. Even though the pullback and the forward dynamics are, in general, not related, for the reaction-diffusion equations considered in this work, the forward dynamics was obtained from the pullback dynamics. As a matter of fact, we found, via the pullback approach, a set in the phase space
X_{\varepsilon}
and then showed that this set is also forward attracting. For further discussion about relations between the pullback and uniform attractor for a nonautonomous problem, we recommend
[

]
,

]
and
[

]

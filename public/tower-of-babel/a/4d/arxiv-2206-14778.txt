1.
Introduction

Homological mirror symmetry (HMS) for toric varieties is a well-studied subject, yet it can still offer new insights to classical problems. Our paper concerns a numerical conjecture that is a shadow of a full categorical conjecture
[
APW17
,
KS22
,
HK22
]
. We first sketch the categorical conjecture. On the B-side, we have a toric Calabi-Yau GIT problem and we study the derived equivalences and semi-orthogonal decompositions that arise from wall-crossing. On the A-side we have a fiberwise partially wrapped Fukaya category
[
AA21
]
associated to a fibration
\pi:Y\to B
and a superpotential
W:Y\to\mathbb{C}
, and we want to study the “pushforward” of
\Fuk(Y,W)

along
\pi
to get a Fukaya category on
B
with categorical coefficient. For
b\in B
, let
Y_{b}
be the fiber over
b
and
W_{b}
the restriction of
W
, then there is a discriminant loci
\nabla\subset B
where the fiberwise wrapped Fukaya category
\Fuk(Y_{b},W_{b})
become “degenerate”.
The full HMS predicts that, for each wall crossing
W
(corresponding to certain asymptotic region of
\nabla
), there is a B-side perverse schober coming from SOD of
\Coh(Z_{W})
where
Z_{W}
is certain toric stack associated to
W
(see Eq. (
1.1
)), and there is an A-side analog coming from a transversal curve (annuli) intersecting the discriminant loci
\nabla
in the asymptotic region of the wall. Our main theorem is a verification that the two schobers has the same number of singularities of each type.

The full conjecture is explained beautifully in the Kite-Segal paper
[
KS22
]
, which is based on the physicists conjecture
[
APW17
]
. In the remaining part of the introduction, we will focus on the numerical conjecture and state our main results.

1.1.
Main Result

Our input data is a collection of lattice points
q_{1},\cdots,q_{N}
in
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
, where
q_{i}=(q_{i1},\cdots,q_{ik})
, called weights. We assume that

generate the linear space
\mathbb{R}^{k}

\sum_{i}q_{i}=0
. Equivalently, the input data is a full rank linear map

Q:\mathbb{Z}^{N}\to\mathbb{Z}^{k},\quad e_{i}\mapsto q_{i},

such that
Q(1,\cdots,1)=0
.
From this input data, we can set up two problems (called A-side and B-side) as follows.

First, we consider the dual of
Q
,
Q^{\vee}:\mathbb{Z}^{k}\to\mathbb{Z}^{N}
, and let
A
denote the cokernel map

A:\mathbb{Z}^{N}\to\mathsf{N}

where
\mathsf{N}
may have torsion if
Q
is not surjective.

For the sake of the introduction, we assume that
Q
is surjective and thus
\mathsf{N}
is a lattice. We further assume that for any
i\in[N]=\{1,\cdots,N\}
,
a_{i}=A(e_{i})
are distinct. This is for easy quotation of results in
[
GKZ
]
. We also abuse notation and let
A
denote the set
\{a_{i}\}
. Both of the assumptions can be easily removed, as described in the appendix.

Let

1.1.2.
The B-side setup

Let
T=(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{k}
acts on
\mathbb{C}^{N}

q_{1},\cdots,q_{N}
. There is a GKZ fan in

The GIT quotient stacks

]

\lambda_{W}\perp\text{span}_{\mathbb{R}}(W)
. Let

denote the fixed loci of

, then

Z_{W}=[V^{\lambda_{W}}/\!/\!_{W}(T/\lambda_{W})]

(1.1)

See also definition

It is interesting to study the autoequivalence induced by window shift

1.1.3.
Statement of Results

Our first result is about any toric GIT problem, possibly non-CY.

Let
Q:\mathbb{Z}^{N}\to\mathbb{Z}^{k}
be a toric GIT problem,
C
a chamber in the GKZ fan and let
H\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
be a
relevant subspace

H=\cone(\{q_{i}:q_{i}\in H\})
(Definition

X_{C}
be the GIT quotient corresponding to the chamber
C
, and let
Z_{H}
be the SOD component for the relevant subspace
H
. We are interested in the SOD multiplicity of
\Coh(Z_{H})
in
\Coh(X_{C})
, denoted as
[X_{C}:Z_{H}]
. Let
[N]_{H}=\{i\in[N]:q_{i}\in H\}
be the index set of the weights lying on
H
. Then we have a
Coulomb GIT problem
(Section
2.4
)

Q_{/H}:\mathbb{Z}^{N}/\mathbb{Z}^{[N]_{H}}\to\mathbb{Z}^{k}/(\mathbb{Z}^{k}\cap H)

where the chamber

descends to a chamber

, with corresponding quotient
X_{C/H}
, the relevant subspace
H
quotient to a point
H/H
, and
Z_{H/H}=pt
.

Theorem 1.2

(Lemma
2.20
)
.

Let
Q
be any toric GIT problem,
C
a chamber,
H
a relevant subspace. Then the multiplicity is invariant under passing to the Coulomb problem
Q_{/H}

[X_{C}:Z_{H}]=[X_{C/H}:Z_{H/H}].

Combining the above result, and the result in Horja-Katzarkov
[

]

\sum_{F}n^{A}_{W,F}\rank(Z_{/F})=\sum_{F}n^{B}_{W,F}\rank(Z_{/F})

(1.2)

we can easily get the following main theorem

Theorem 1.3

(Theorem
3.4
)
.

Let
W
be a codimension one cone in the GKZ fan of a CY problem. For any minimal face
F
of the polytope
\Pi

n_{W,F}^{A}
denote the intersection multiplicity (A-side multiplicity) defined by the tropical complex, and

In addition, we obtain a recursive formula relating multiplicities to the ranks of K-theory, hence volumes of stacky fans (see Section

)

Let

1.2.
Related Work

Homological mirror symmetry for toric variety has been extensively studied, using Floer theoretic technique
[
Abo06
,
Han19
,
HH20
]
and using the microlocal sheaf method
[
FLTZ11
,
FLTZ12
,
Kuw20
,
Zho19
,
GPS18
]
, see a recent review using GIT quotient
[
She21
]
.

In the context of mirror symmetry to toric GIT problem, it is well-understood on the B-side
[
HHP08
,
BFK19
,
HL15
,
Seg11
]
how to use window subcategory to do wall-crossing between adjacent chambers, and sometimes in nice cases (e.g. quasi-symmetric case) how to do wall-crossing among all chambers simultaneously
[
HLS20
,
ŠVdB19
]
.

On the A-side mirror to toric GIT, if we use microlocal sheaf as the A-model, then thanks to the functoriality of coherent constructible correspondence
[
Bon06
,
FLTZ11
,
Kuw20
,
Zho19
]
, we can translate all the B-side VGIT result to the A-side
[
Zho20
,
HZ20
]
, window categories into window skeletons etc. If we use the more traditional Fukaya category A-model, then the question is more subtle and harder to solve
[
DKK16
,
BDF+15
,
Ker17
]
. The program of Ballard-Diemer-Favero-Katzarkov-Kerr is about matching the A-side and B-side SOD.

In general, the above result on B-model (and on microlocal A-model) only sees the codimension-1 wall crossing, and did not see the entire GKZ discriminant locus, the recent work of Kite-Segal
[
KS22
]
in some sense remedies the above deficiency.

The numerical version of the full conjecture, i.e.
n^{A}_{W,F}=n^{B}_{W,F}
, has been proven by Kite-Segal in the case when
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}T=1,2
. And Horja-Katzarkov
[
HK22
]
proved an integrated version of the desired equality (Eq.
1.2
), which does most of the heavy-lifting for us.

To get the full categorical conjecture
[
APW17
,
KS22
,
HK22
]
, one needs to identify the A-model SOD component mirror to the B-model counterpart
Z_{/F}
, and find a way to book-keep the relations between various SOD components. We leave these to future work.

Outline

2.
The B-side

In this section, we first setup the general toric GIT problem, then introduce the GKZ fan to relate various phases of GIT quotients (M-side). These data can be equally well encoded in certain triangulations problem (N-side). Next, we introduce the notion of a Coulomb-Higgs GIT problem, which represent a sub-quotient of the original GIT problem. See
[
KS22
,
HK22
,
GKZ
]
for more details. Then we prove our main lemma, namely the SOD multiplicity is invariant under passing to the Coulomb problem associated to a minimal face. Given the lemma, we then deduce a recursive formula for SOD multiplicities.

2.1.
Toric GIT setup

Our starting point is a torus
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{k}
acting on
V=\mathbb{C}^{N}
with weights
q_{1},\cdots,q_{N}\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}
. More invariantly, we have a rank
k
lattice
L\simeq\mathbb{Z}^{k}
, and
L_{\mathbb{C}^{*}}=L\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}^{*}\simeq(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{k}
acts on
\mathbb{C}^{N}
by factoring through
L_{\mathbb{C}^{*}}\to(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{N}
. This induces a map on the cocharacter lattices and character lattices

Q^{\vee}:L\to\mathbb{Z}^{N},\quad Q:(\mathbb{Z}^{N})^{\vee}\to L^{\vee}.

We assume the
\coker(Q)
is finite, or equivalently
Q^{\vee}
is injective.

We have a short exact sequence (SES), called ’N-sequence’

0\to L\xrightarrow{Q^{\vee}}\mathbb{Z}^{N}\xrightarrow{A}\mathsf{N}\to 0

where
\mathsf{N}
might have torsion

0\to\mathsf{N}_{tors}\to\mathsf{N}\to\mathsf{N}_{free}\to 0.

Let
\bar{A}:\mathbb{Z}^{N}\to\mathsf{N}_{free}
denote the obvious composition.
Apply the
\Hom(-,\mathbb{Z})
to the ’N-sequence’, and use injective resolution
\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Q}\to\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
, then we have a long exact sequece, the ’M-sequence’

0\to\mathsf{M}_{0}\xrightarrow{A^{\vee}}(\mathbb{Z}^{N})^{\vee}\xrightarrow{Q}L^{\vee}\to\mathsf{M}_{1}\to 0,

where
\mathsf{M}_{0}=\Hom(\mathsf{N},\mathbb{Z})=\Hom(\mathsf{N}_{free},\mathbb{Z})
is a lattice, and
\mathsf{M}_{1}=\Ext^{1}(\mathsf{N},\mathbb{Z})=\Hom(\mathsf{N}_{tors},\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
is the Pontryagin dual to the torsion subgroup
\mathsf{N}_{tors}
.

If
G
is an abelian group, we denote
(\cdots)_{G}:=(\cdots)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}G
, where
G
can be
\mathbb{R},\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{C},\mathbb{C}^{*}
. For example, if we apply
(\cdots)_{\mathbb{R}}
to
Q:(\mathbb{Z}^{N})^{\vee}\to L^{\vee}
, then we will get
Q_{\mathbb{R}}:(\mathbb{R}^{N})^{\vee}\to L^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}
.

Let the co-character lattice
X_{*}(({\mathbb{C}^{*}})^{N})\simeq\mathbb{Z}^{N}
be equipped with the standard basis
e_{1},\cdots,e_{N}
, and let the character lattice
X^{*}(({\mathbb{C}^{*}})^{N})\simeq(\mathbb{Z}^{N})^{\vee}
be equipped with the dual basis
e_{1}^{\vee},\cdots,e_{N}^{\vee}
. Define

\widetilde{a}_{i}=A(e_{i})\in\mathsf{N},\quad a_{i}=\bar{A}(e_{i})\in\mathsf{N}_{free},\quad q_{i}=Q(e_{i}^{\vee})\in L^{\vee}.

2.2.
M-side, chambers and walls

By the M-side, we mean the objects living on spaces in the M-sequence, for example about the map
Q_{\mathbb{R}}:(\mathbb{R}^{N})^{\vee}\to L^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}
. Here for the simplicity of notation, we write
(\mathbb{R}^{N})^{\vee}
as
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

We recall the definition of GKZ fan (rather GKZ stratification)
\Sigma_{GKZ}(Q)
in
L^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}
(aka GIT fan or secondary fan) for a toric GIT problem
Q
. We omit
(Q)
if there is no danger of confusion.

Let
\mathscr{P}(S)
denote the power set of a set
S
. By the GKZ stratification
\Sigma_{GKZ}(Q)
, we mean the stratification of
L^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}
given by the level sets of the following map:

\displaystyle L^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle\mathscr{P}(\{\tau|\tau\text{ is a closed face of }(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{\geq 0})^{\vee}\})

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle\{\tau|x\in Q_{\mathbb{R}}(\tau)\}.

A top dimensional stratum is called a chamber, and a codimension-1 stratum is called a wall.
The support of the GKZ fan is
Q_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{N})
. If the support is not the full
L^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}
, then its complement is still a GKZ strata, and we call it the empty chamber.

Here, the positive quadrant
P_{N}=(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{N})^{\vee}
can be identified as the image of the moment map of
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{N}
acting on
\mathbb{C}^{N}

\mu_{N}:\mathbb{C}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{N},\quad(z_{i})_{i}\mapsto(|z_{i}|^{2})_{i}.

And the moment map of

acting on
\mathbb{C}^{N}
is
\mu=Q_{\mathbb{R}}\circ\mu_{N}
.

For any

, we define the GIT quotient stack

X_{c}=[(\mathbb{C}^{N})^{ss}_{c}/L_{\mathbb{C}^{*}}],\quad(\mathbb{C}^{N})^{ss}_{c}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{N}\mid\overline{L_{\mathbb{C}^{*}}\cdot z}\cap\mu^{-1}(c)\neq\emptyset\}.

More concretely, the positive quadrant
P_{N}
is stratified by faces
\tau
,
P_{N}=\sqcup\tau
which induces a stratification of
\mathbb{C}^{N}
into strata
(\mathbb{C}^{N})_{\tau}=\mu_{N}^{-1}(\tau)
, and
(\mathbb{C}^{N})^{ss}_{c}=\sqcup\{(\mathbb{C}^{N})_{\tau}\mid Q(\overline{\tau})\cap c\neq\emptyset\}.
From the latter description, it is clear that

is constant when

varies within a GKZ stratum

, hence we also write

as

. For any GKZ stratum

Let
\det V=\sum_{i}q_{i}\in L^{\vee}
denote the weight of
L_{\mathbb{C}^{*}}
acting on
\det V
. The toric GIT is called Calabi-Yau (CY), if
\det V=0
.

Let
W
be a wall separating two chambers
C_{+},C_{-}
. We choose the
\pm

\det V
is pointing towards
C_{+}
. Let
\lambda_{W}\in L

.
If

, i.e,
\det V
is parallel to
W
, we say there is a balanced wall-crossing and we have (non-canonical) derived equivalence
\Coh(X_{C_{+}})\simeq\Coh(X_{C_{-}})
. If
d_{W}>0
, then we have semi-orthogonal decomposition

\Coh(X_{C_{+}})=\langle\Coh(X_{C_{-}}),\Coh(Z_{W}),\cdots,\Coh(Z_{W})\rangle,

where
Z_{W}
is defined in (
1.1
), and the factor
\Coh(Z_{W})
repeats
d_{W}
times.

Therefore, starting from any point
c\in L^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}
, we may form the ray in the direction of
-\det V
. This is called the “straight-line” run in
[
DKK16
]
. If
c
is generic, then the run will only encounter walls, and ends in a chamber
C
that contains
-\det V
in its closure. We call such a chamber
C
and the corresponding phase
X_{C}
minimal. It is possible that
X_{C}=\emptyset
or that there are several minimal chambers, but upto derived equivalence, the minimal phase is unique. Let
X_{\min}(Q)
denote the minimal phase (possibly empty) for toric GIT problem.

Remark 2.1
.

Here are some side remarks on the relations between CY and non-CY toric GIT problems, which will not be used in the rest of the paper.

For a non-CY problem, we can associate a CY problem to by adding a weight
q_{N+1}=-\det V
. The new problem is

0\to L\xrightarrow{\hat{Q}}\mathbb{Z}^{N+1}\xrightarrow{\hat{A}}\mathsf{N}\oplus\mathbb{Z}\to 0

We have
\hat{A}(e_{i})=(A(e_{i}),1)
for
i\in[N]
, and
\hat{A}(e_{N+1})=(0,1)
.

Going the other way around, suppose we have a toric CY GIT problem, then pick any
q_{i}
(which by assumption are all nonzero), say
q_{N+1}
by relabelling, we can delete it and get a toric non-CY GIT problem.

Assuming
Q
is a non-CY problem, then the toric CY GKZ stratification
\Sigma_{GKZ}(\hat{Q})
refines the non-CY GKZ stratification
\Sigma_{GKZ}(Q)
. The new walls in

\Sigma_{GKZ}(\hat{Q})
are all parallel to
\det V
.

2.3.
N-side, local triangulations and stacky fan

By the N-side, we mean the objects living on spaces in the N-sequence, for example the map
A:\mathbb{Z}^{N}\to\mathsf{N}
. Recall
a_{i}=\bar{A}(e_{i})\in\mathsf{N}_{free}\subset\mathsf{N}_{\mathbb{R}}
and
\widetilde{a}_{i}=A(e_{i})\in\mathsf{N}
.

2.3.1.
Localized Marked Polytope Subdivision

Let
\Delta_{N}=\conv(0,e_{1},\cdots,e_{N})
be the standard
N
-simplex in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Let
\Pi=A_{\mathbb{R}}(\Delta_{N})
be the image of
\Delta_{N}
in
\mathsf{N}_{\mathbb{R}}
, which is also the convex hull
\conv(0,a_{1},\cdots,a_{N})
.

We start with a pair of piecewise linear (PL) functions on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
(\mathbb{R}^{N})^{\vee}
related by Legendre transformation. Let

\varphi_{N}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{N}):=\min(0,x_{1},\cdots,x_{N}):(\mathbb{R}^{N})^{\vee}\to\mathbb{R}.

It is a concave function, with
\varphi_{N}^{-1}(0)=P_{N}=(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})^{N}
. By Legendre transformation, we have

\psi_{N}(y_{1},\cdots,y_{N})=\max_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}(\varphi(x)-(x,y)):\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}.

\psi_{N}
is a convex function, and explicitly

\psi_{N}(y)=\begin{cases}0&y\in\Delta_{N}\\
+\infty&y\notin\Delta_{N}\end{cases}

Given any point
c\in L^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}
, choose a lift
b\in Q^{-1}(c)
. Then, we can identify
\mathsf{M}_{\mathbb{R}}\simeq Q^{-1}(c)
by

f_{b}:\mathsf{M}_{\mathbb{R}}\to Q^{-1}(c),\quad\xi\mapsto b+A_{\mathbb{R}}^{\vee}(\xi)=(b_{1}+(a_{1},\xi),\cdots,b_{N}+(a_{N},\xi)).

The restriction of
\varphi_{N}
on
Q^{-1}(c)
pullback to
\mathsf{M}_{\mathbb{R}}
by
f_{b}
gives

\varphi_{b}(\xi):=\varphi_{N}(b+A_{\mathbb{R}}^{\vee}(\xi))=\min(0,b_{1}+a_{1}(\xi),b_{2}+a_{2}(\xi),\cdots,b_{N}+a_{N}(\xi)).

Its Legendre transformation is

\psi_{b}(\eta)=\max_{\xi\in\mathsf{M}_{\mathbb{R}}}(\varphi_{b}(\xi)-(\eta,\xi)).

Notation:
For uniform treatment later, it is useful to introduce another pair
(a_{N+1},b_{N+1})=0\in\mathsf{N}_{\mathbb{R}}\times\mathbb{R}
for the origin.

The overgraph of
\psi_{b}

\Gamma_{\geq\psi_{b}}:=\{(x,y)\in\Pi\times\mathbb{R}\mid y\geq\psi_{b}(x)\}

is the convex hull of the upward rays

\bigcup_{i=1}^{N+1}a_{i}\times\{y\geq b_{i}\}\subset\mathsf{N}_{\mathbb{R}}\times\mathbb{R}.

We record the subset
S\subset[N+1]
where the tip of the ray touches the graph, i.e.

S=\{i\in[N+1]\mid\psi_{b}(a_{i})=b_{i}\}.

For
x\in\Pi
, if
x=a_{i}
for some
i\in S
, we say
x
is marked by
i
. It is possible
x
is marked by more than one
i
.

The maximal linearity domain of
\psi_{b}
defines a polytope subdivision of
\Pi
, and the vertices of the polytopes and possibly some interior points are marked by some
i\in S
.

The polytope subdivision together with marking points
S
is called a coherent (multi-)marked polytope subdivision, and marked subdivision for short.

If we choose a different lift
b\in Q^{-1}(c)
, then
\psi_{b}
only change by a linear function and does not affect the coherent subdivision. For a generic choice of
c
, we have triangulations of
\Pi
with no interior marked points, and each vertex of the simplices is marked exactly once.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
x
be any point in
\Pi
, we say two marked subdivisions are
equivalent at
x
if they have the same collection of marked polytopes that contain
x
.

A
marked subdivision localized at
x
is an equivalence class of marked subdivisions modulo equivalence relations at
x
.

For any
c\in L^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}
, we may use the above procedure to obtain a marked subdivision
T(c)
of
\Pi
.

Proposition 2.3
.

(1)

For any
c\in L^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}
, the subdivision
T(c)
has a marked point at the origin if and only if
c
is not in the empty chamber of
\Sigma_{GKZ}(Q)
.

(2)

For any
c_{1},c_{2}\in L^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}
,
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
are in the same GKZ stratum of
\Sigma_{GKZ}(Q)
if and only if
T(c_{1})
is equivalent to
T(c_{2})
at the origin.

Proof.

For (1),
T(c)
has a marked point at the origin if and only if
\psi_{b}(0)=0
, which is equivalent to
\max_{\xi\in M_{\mathbb{R}}}\varphi_{b}(\xi)=0
, and is equivalent to
Q_{\mathbb{R}}^{-1}(c)\cap\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{N}\neq\emptyset
.

For (2),
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
are in the same GKZ stratum, if and only if the fibers
Q_{\mathbb{R}}^{-1}(c_{1})
and
Q_{\mathbb{R}}^{-1}(c_{2})
intersect each face
\tau
of
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{N}
in the same way. Each non-empty intersection of
\tau\cap Q_{\mathbb{R}}^{-1}(c)
corresponds to a polytope (possibly not of top dimension) in
T(c)
containing
0
.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

If the GIT problem
Q
is Calabi-Yau, then any marked subdivision of
\Pi
uses the origin and the equivalence relation is trivial. In this case, we have a bijection between GKZ chambers and marked triangulation of
\Pi
.

If the GIT problem
Q
is not CY, then we may consider the associated CY problem
\hat{Q}
. Then chambers in
\Sigma_{GKZ}(\hat{Q})
corresponds to marked triangulation of of
\Pi
; and chambers in
\Sigma_{GKZ}(Q)
corresponds to marked triangulations of
\Pi
localized at
0
.

Remark 2.5
.

Since the cones in the toric fan are precisely the cones on the (marked) polytopes that contain the origin, polytopes that do not contain the origin do not affect the toric data for that phase.

Remark 2.6
.

We remind the reader that, in comparison with
[
KS22
]
in the CY case, our polytope
\Pi
is not the same
\Pi
that appears in
[
KS22
]
, but is the cone on it from the origin. Furthermore, since all faces in our marked subdivision include the origin, these faces of our
\Pi
are in bijection with the faces of
\Pi
as in
[
KS22
]
.

2.3.2.
Stacky Fan and its Volume

Let
C
be a chamber of GKZ fan, and let
T(C)
be the corresponding marked triangulation localized at the base point. This defines a stacky fan in
\mathsf{N}
as follows.

Recall the definition of simplicial stacky fan following
[
BCS05
]
. Let
\mathsf{N}
be a finitely generated abelian group,
\Sigma
be a rational simplicial polyhedran fan in
\mathsf{N}_{\mathbb{R}}
, and
\{v_{i}\}_{i\in\Sigma^{1}}\subset\mathsf{N}
such that
(v_{i})_{\mathbb{R}}
generate the corresponding ray in
\Sigma
. The triple
{\bf\Sigma}=(\mathsf{N},\Sigma,\{v_{i}\})
is called a stacky fan.

Roughly speaking, modulo torsion, we can think of a stacky fan
{\bf\Sigma}
as a collection of simplices
{\hat{\sigma}}\subset\mathsf{N}_{\mathbb{R}}
with shared vertices
(v_{i})_{\mathbb{R}}
.

We write
|\Sigma|
for the union of the cones
\sigma
, and we write
|{\bf\Sigma}|
for the union of the simplices
{\hat{\sigma}}
.

We normalize volume
\vol_{\mathbb{R}}
on
\mathsf{N}_{\mathbb{R}}
such that a minimal simplex with vertices in
\mathsf{N}_{free}
has unit volume. Then we define the stacky volume by

\vol(\mathbf{\Sigma}):=|\mathsf{N}_{tors}|\cdot\vol_{\mathbb{R}}(|{\bf\Sigma}|).

By design of the volume, we have the following result

Proposition 2.7

(
[
HK22
]
)
.

The volume of the stacky fan equals the rank of the
K_{0}
group of

X_{\bf\Sigma}
,

\rank(K_{0}(X_{\bf\Sigma}))=\vol({\bf\Sigma}).

2.3.3.
Minimal phases and Minimal Fans

Given a toric GIT problem
A:\mathbb{Z}^{N}\twoheadrightarrow\mathsf{N}
or
Q:(\mathbb{Z}^{N})^{\vee}\to L^{\vee}
, on the
M
-side, we have some minimal chambers
C
, i.e. those containing
-\det(V)
. On the N-side, we have some minimal stacky fans corresponding to the minimal chambers. They have the same support
|{\bf\Sigma}^{min}|
which we describe now.

Let
S=A_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{N})
and
S_{+}=A_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{N}\backslash\{0\})
and define (slightly abusing notation)

|{\bf\Sigma}^{min}|=\conv(S)-\conv(S_{+}).

We see
|{\bf\Sigma}^{min}|=\emptyset
if and only if
S=S_{+}
, or
0\in\Pi=\conv(a_{i})
, or the support of the GKZ fan is not the whole
L^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}
.

Proposition 2.8

(
[
HK22
]
)
.

We have the rank-volume relation

\rank(K_{0}(X_{min}))=\vol(|{\bf\Sigma}^{min}|)=|\mathsf{N}_{tors}|\cdot\vol_{\mathbb{R}}(\conv(S)-\conv(S_{+})).

2.4.2.
Definition using subspaces and faces

Although the Coulomb-Higgs problems can be defined for general subsets
\Gamma_{1}\subset\Gamma_{2}\subset[N]
, they often arise from subspaces
H\subset L^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}
and faces
F\subset\Pi
.

Definition 2.10
.

A subspace
H\subset L^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}
is called
weight supported
if
H=\text{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{q_{i}\in H\}
.

A subset
F\subset\Pi
is called a
face
if there is a linear function
l:\mathsf{N}_{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathbb{R}
, such that
l|_{\Pi}\geq 0
and
F=l^{-1}(0)\cap\Pi
.

Recall
[N]_{H}=\{i:q_{i}\in H\}
and
[N]_{F}=\{i:a_{i}\in F\}
.

Definition 2.11
.

If
H_{1}\subset H_{2}\subset L^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}
are a pair of weight supported subspaces, we define the Coulomb-Higgs problem
Q_{H_{2}/H_{1}}

Q_{H_{2}/H_{1}}:\mathbb{Z}^{[N]_{H_{2}}-[N]_{H_{1}}}\to(L^{\vee}\cap H_{2})/H_{1}.

If
H_{1}=0
, we get a Higgs problem
Q_{H_{2}}
; if
H_{2}=L^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}
, we get a Coulomb problem
Q_{/H_{1}}
.

If
F_{1}\subset F_{2}\subset\Pi
is a pair of faces, we define the Coulomb-Higgs problem
A_{F_{2}/F_{1}}

A_{F_{2}/F_{1}}:\mathbb{Z}^{[N]_{F_{2}}-[N]_{F_{1}}}\to\mathsf{N}_{F_{2}}/\mathsf{N}_{F_{1}},\quad\mathsf{N}_{F}:=\mathsf{N}_{\Gamma=[N]_{F}}.

If
F_{1}=0
, we get a Coulomb problem
A_{F_{2}}
; if
F_{2}=\Pi
, we get a Higgs problem
A_{/F_{1}}
.

2.4.3.
Minimal faces and relevant subspaces

Here we again follow
[
KS22
,
APW17
]
and introduce certain important Coulomb Higgs problem, that describes the SOD components.

Definition 2.12
.

Let
S
be a subset of
[N]
. Recall that
a_{i}=A_{\mathbb{R}}(e_{i})
,
q_{i}=Q_{\mathbb{R}}(e_{i}^{\vee})
.

(1)

We say
S
is
A_{\mathbb{R}}
-redundant
, if there exists
c_{i}\neq 0
for each
i\in S
, such that
\sum_{i\in S}c_{i}a_{i}=0
.

(2)

We say
S
is
A_{\mathbb{R}}
-saturated
, if there is a linear function
l:\mathsf{N}_{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathbb{R}
, such that
S=\{i:l(a_{i})=0\}
.

(3)

We say
S
is
A_{\mathbb{R}}
-extremally-saturated
, if there is a linear function
l:\mathsf{N}_{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathbb{R}
, such that
S=\{i:l(a_{i})=0\}
, and
l(a_{i})>0
for all
i\in S^{c}
.

(4)

We say
S
is
Q_{\mathbb{R}}
-redundant
, if there exists
c_{i}\neq 0
for each
i\in S
, such that
\sum_{i\in S}c_{i}q_{i}=0
.

(5)

We say
S
is
Q_{\mathbb{R}}
-saturated
, if there is a linear function
l:L^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathbb{R}
, such that
S=\{i:l(q_{i})=0\}
.

(6)

We say
S
is
Q_{\mathbb{R}}
-positively-redundant
if there exists
c_{i}>0
for
i\in S
, such that
\sum_{i\in S}c_{i}q_{i}=0
.

Proposition 2.13
.

Let
S
be a subset of
[N]
.

(1)

S
is
A_{\mathbb{R}}
-redundant if and only if
S^{c}
is
Q_{\mathbb{R}}
-saturated.

(2)

S
is
Q_{\mathbb{R}}
-redundant if and only if
S^{c}
is
A_{\mathbb{R}}
-saturated.

(3)

S
is
Q_{\mathbb{R}}
-positively redundant if and only if
S^{c}
is
A_{\mathbb{R}}
-extremally-saturated.

Proof.

For (1), by definition,
S
is
A_{\mathbb{R}}
-redundant, if there exists
c_{i}\neq 0
for each
i\in S
, such that
\sum_{i\in S}c_{i}a_{i}=0
. By setting
c_{i}=0
for
i\notin S
, we get an element
\vec{c}=(c_{i})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, such that
A(\vec{c})=0
, i.e,
\vec{c}\in L
. Hence
\vec{c}
defines a linear function
l:L^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathbb{R}
, such that
l(q_{i})=0
if and only if
c_{i}=0
, i.e
i\in S^{c}
. Thus
S^{c}
is
Q_{\mathbb{R}}
-saturated. The argument can also be reversed, hence we get the equivalence.

For (2), we only need to change the above argument by swapping
A_{\mathbb{R}}
with
Q_{\mathbb{R}}
, and
a_{i}
with
q_{i}
.

For (3), we can check that the two positivity conditions match.
∎

Recall
\Pi=A_{\mathbb{R}}(\Delta^{N})\subset\mathsf{N}_{\mathbb{R}}
. A subset
F\subset\Pi
is called a face is there is a linear function
l:\mathsf{N}_{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathbb{R}
, such that
l(\Pi)\geq 0
and
F=l^{-1}(0)
. For example,
\Pi
is always a face of itself. And if
\Pi
contains a linear subspace
H
, then any face
F\supset H
.

Definition 2.14
.

Let
F
is a face of
\Pi
and define
[N]_{F}=\{i:a_{i}\in F\}
. By construction
[N]_{F}
is
A_{\mathbb{R}}
-extremally-saturated.
We say
F
is a
minimal face
of
\Pi
if
[N]_{F}
is
A_{\mathbb{R}}
-redundant.

Let
H\subset L^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}
be a weight supported subspace, and define
[N]_{H}=\{i:q_{i}\in H\}
. We say
H
is a
relevant subspace
of
L^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}
if
[N]_{H}
is
Q_{\mathbb{R}}
-positively-redundant and
Q_{\mathbb{R}}
-saturated.

Remark 2.15
.

Our definitions generalize the definitions of minimal faces and relevant subspaces in
[
KS22
]
in the toric CY setting.

We obtain the following slight generalization of Proposition 4.15 in
[
KS22
]
.

Proposition 2.16
.

There is a bijection between minimal faces of
\Pi
and relevant subspaces of
L^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}
, such that if a minimal face

corresponds to a relevant subspace

then
[N]_{F}=[N]_{H}^{c}
.

Proof.

Let
F
be a minimal face, then
[N]_{F}
is
A_{\mathbb{R}}
extremally-saturated and redundant, hence
[N]_{F}^{c}
is
Q_{\mathbb{R}}
-positively-redundant and saturated. Let

, we then get a relevant subspace. Going backward is similar.
∎

If

is a minimal face (of
\Pi
), we will abuse notation and also call
[N]_{F}
a minimal face. Similarly, if

is a relevant subspace, we will also call

is a relevant subspace.

Here we define some GIT quotients associated with the Higgs and Coulomb problems.

Definition 2.17

If

Lemma 2.20
.

Let

be a non-empty GKZ chamber and

[X_{C}:Z_{H}]=[X_{C/H}:Z_{H/H}].

Proof.

We induct on
r=\codim_{\mathbb{R}}H
. If
r=0
,
H=L^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}
. Since by assumption
H
is relevant,
Z_{H}=X_{min}\neq\emptyset
. We have

[X_{C}:Z_{H}]=[X_{C}:X_{min}]=1.
And we also have

X_{C/H}=pt
, hence
[X_{C/H}:Z_{H/H}]=1
.

Suppose the statement holds when
r<n
, and consider the case
r=n
. For any chamber
C
, consider “monotone decreasing run”
C=C_{0}\leadsto C_{1}\leadsto\cdots\leadsto C_{m}
, where
C_{m}
is a minimal phase, and let
W_{i}
denote the wall separating
C_{i-1}
and
C_{i}
. For any wall
W
, write
W\parallel H
if
\text{span}_{\mathbb{R}}(W)\supset H
.

For any wall
W
with
W\not\parallel H
, we have
[Z_{W}:Z_{H}]=0
. Thus

[X_{C}:Z_{H}]=\sum_{W}d_{W}[Z_{W}:Z_{H}]=\sum_{W\parallel H}d_{W}[Z_{W}:Z_{H}],

where summation of
W
runs over all the wall crossing in the run i.e.
W=W_{i}
for some
i
.

For
W
with
W\parallel H
,
[Z_{W}:Z_{H}]
is computed in the Higgs problem
Q_{\text{span}_{\mathbb{R}}W}
. By our induction hypothesis, we have
[Z_{W}:Z_{H}]=[Z_{W/H},Z_{H/H}]
. Hence

[X_{C}:Z_{H}]=\sum_{W\parallel H}d_{W}[Z_{W/H}:Z_{H/H}].

On the other hand, the sequence of “upstair chambers” in
L_{\mathbb{R}}^{\vee}
is sent to a sequence of “downstair chambers” in

(possibly with repetitions). In particular, if
W\not\parallel H
, then the two chambers separated by

are sent to the same chamber in

. Hence the downstairs wall crossing corresponds to those upstairs wall crossing with

. We get

[X_{C/H}:Z_{H/H}]=\sum_{W\parallel H}d_{W/H}[Z_{W/H}:Z_{H/H}].

Hence we only need to prove

, and this follows from
\pi_{H}^{*}(\lambda_{W/H})=\lambda_{W}
(up to sign ambiguity of

).
∎

Let

be the set of minimal faces indexing the SOD components. For a variety (or smooth DM stack)

For

where

and

are define in
[
GKZ
]
or
[
HK22
]
. (See also Section
2.3.3
).

Proposition 2.21
.

Let
C
be a GKZ chamber, and
{\bf\Sigma}
the corresponding stacky fan. For each minimal face
F\in\mathcal{F}
, let
n_{F}:=[X_{\bf\Sigma}:Z_{/F}]
denote the SOD multiplicity of
\Coh(Z_{/F})
in
\Coh(X_{\bf\Sigma})
. Then we have a system of linear equations labelled also by minimal faces in
\mathcal{F}
,

\rank(K_{0}(X_{{\bf\Sigma}\cap F}))=\sum_{F^{\prime}\leq F}n_{F^{\prime}}\rank(K_{0}(Z_{F/F^{\prime}})).

(2.1)

where equation the labelled by
F
only involves
n_{F^{\prime}}
with face
F^{\prime}\leq F
, and
\rank([Z_{F/F}])=1
.

Proof.

For each minimal face
F
, we have

K_{0}(X_{{\bf\Sigma}\cap F})=\oplus_{F^{\prime}\leq F}K_{0}(Z_{F/F^{\prime}})^{\oplus[X_{{\bf\Sigma}\cap F}:Z_{F/F^{\prime}}]}.

(2.2)

By our main Lemma (
2.20
), we have that

[X_{{\bf\Sigma}\cap F}:Z_{F/F^{\prime}}]=[X_{{\bf\Sigma}\cap F\cap F^{\prime}}:Z_{F^{\prime}/F^{\prime}}]=[X_{{\bf\Sigma}\cap F^{\prime}}:Z_{F^{\prime}/F^{\prime}}]=n_{F^{\prime}}.

Taking rank on both sides in Eq (

3.
Multiplicity Conjecture for toric CY GIT

Consider a toric GIT problem
Q:{({\mathbb{Z}^{\vee}})^{N}}\to L^{\vee}
that satisfies the Calabi-Yau condition, i.e.
\sum_{i=1}^{N}Q(e_{i}^{\vee})=0
.

In this case, GKZ defined a principal
A
-determinant
E_{A}
, an integer polynomial with
N
variables.
E_{A}
has many nice properties, its Newton polytope equals the secondary polytope, and its zero-loci descent to a hypersurface
\nabla_{GKZ}\subset L^{\vee}_{\mathbb{C}^{*}}
, the GKZ discriminant loci.

It is known that divisor
\nabla_{GKZ}
has decomposition into components
\nabla_{\Gamma}
,

\nabla_{GKZ}=\sum_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{F}}m_{\Gamma}\nabla_{\Gamma},

where
\mathcal{F}
denote the set of minimal faces, where
m_{\Gamma}=\rank(K_{0}(Z_{\Gamma}))
are certain multiplicities.

It is also known that the tropicalization of
\nabla_{GKZ}
is the union of walls in
\Sigma_{GKZ}
. We will now decorate the walls by multiplicities in free abelian group generated by minimal faces.

Let
\mathbb{Z}\mathcal{F}
denote the free abelian group generated by
\mathcal{F}
, with basis denoted by
[\Gamma]
. Then for each wall
W
, we define

[W]:=\sum_{\Gamma}[Z_{W}:Z_{\Gamma}]\cdot[\Gamma]=\sum_{\Gamma}n^{B}_{W,\Gamma}\cdot[\Gamma].

Let
\mathcal{W}
denote the set of walls in
\Sigma_{GKZ}(Q)
. Let
\mathbb{Z}\mathcal{W}
be the free abelian group generated by
\mathcal{W}
, with basis denoted as
\langle W\rangle
. Then any tropical complex supported on
\Sigma_{GKZ}
is equivalent to a non-negative linear combination of the walls. In particular, we have

\nabla_{\Gamma}^{trop}:=\sum_{W\in\mathcal{W}}n^{A}_{W,\Gamma}\langle W\rangle

Definition 3.1
.

Let
Q
be a toric CY GIT problem. A tropical complex is an element in
\mathbb{Z}\mathcal{W}
, and a decorated tropical complex is an element in
\mathbb{Z}(\mathcal{W}\times\mathcal{F})
.
We define the A-model tropical complexes as

\nabla^{A}_{GKZ}=\sum_{\Gamma}\nabla_{\Gamma}^{trop}\cdot[\Gamma]=\sum_{\Gamma,W}n^{A}_{W,\Gamma}\langle W\rangle[\Gamma],\quad\nabla_{\Gamma}^{A}=\sum_{W}n^{A}_{W,\Gamma}\langle W\rangle

We define the B-model tropical complexes as

\nabla^{B}_{GKZ}=\sum_{W}\langle W\rangle[W]=\sum_{W,\Gamma}n^{B}_{W,\Gamma}\langle W\rangle[\Gamma],\quad\nabla_{\Gamma}^{B}=\sum_{W}n^{B}_{W,\Gamma}\langle W\rangle

We write
\nabla^{A}_{GKZ}(Q),\nabla^{A}_{\Gamma}(Q),\nabla^{B}_{GKZ}(Q),\cdots
to emphasize the dependence on
Q
.

Our goal is to show that
\nabla^{A}(Q)=\nabla^{B}(Q)
, or more concretely for each
\Gamma
,
\nabla^{A}_{\Gamma}(Q)=\nabla^{B}_{\Gamma}(Q)
. We first reduce the task to showing just the top dimensional minimal face.

Let
Q_{\Gamma}:({\mathbb{Z}^{\vee}})^{\Gamma}\to L^{\vee}_{\Gamma}
denote the Coulomb problem for minimal face
\Gamma
. Note that
Q_{\Gamma}
still defines a toric CY GIT. Let
\pi_{\Gamma}:L^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}}\to(L^{\vee}_{\Gamma})_{\mathbb{R}}
. Define
\pi_{\Gamma}^{*}:\mathbb{Z}(\mathcal{W}_{\Gamma}\times\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma})\to\mathbb{Z}(\mathcal{W}\times\mathcal{F})
. For a wall
W_{\Gamma}\in\mathcal{W}_{\Gamma}
, we define
\pi_{\Gamma}^{*}(\langle W_{\Gamma}\rangle)=\langle\pi^{-1}W_{\Gamma}\rangle=\sum_{W\in\mathcal{W},\pi_{\Gamma}(W)=W_{\Gamma}}\langle W\rangle
. For any
\Gamma^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}
, since
\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}\subset\mathcal{F}
, we have
\pi_{\Gamma}^{*}([\Gamma^{\prime}])=[\Gamma^{\prime}]
.

Proposition 3.2
.

\nabla^{A}_{\Gamma}(Q)=\pi_{\Gamma}^{*}(\nabla^{A}_{\Gamma}(Q_{\Gamma}))
and

\nabla^{B}_{\Gamma}(Q)=\pi_{\Gamma}^{*}(\nabla^{B}_{\Gamma}(Q_{\Gamma}))
.

Proof.

On the A-side, this follows from relation from the complex hypersurfaces
\nabla_{\Gamma}(Q)=\pi_{\Gamma}^{-1}(\nabla_{\Gamma}(Q_{\Gamma}))
.

On the B-side, we know from Lemma
2.20
that
[Z_{W}:Z_{\Gamma}]=[Z_{W_{\Gamma}}:Z_{\Gamma/\Gamma}]
. Thus

\nabla_{\Gamma}^{B}(Q)=\sum_{W}[Z_{W}:Z_{\Gamma}]\langle W\rangle=\sum_{W:W\parallel\Gamma}[Z_{W_{\Gamma}}:Z_{\Gamma/\Gamma}]\langle W\rangle=\sum_{W_{\Gamma}\in\mathcal{W}_{\Gamma}}[Z_{W_{\Gamma}}:Z_{\Gamma/\Gamma}]\pi_{\Gamma}^{*}(\langle W_{\Gamma}\rangle)=\pi_{\Gamma}^{*}(\nabla^{B}_{\Gamma}(Q_{\Gamma})).

∎

We define a rank map,

\rank:\mathbb{Z}\mathcal{F}\to\mathbb{Z},\quad\rank([\Gamma]):=\rank(K_{0}(Z_{\Gamma})).

And we extend it by linearity to

\rank:\mathbb{Z}(\mathcal{W}\times\mathcal{F})\to\mathbb{Z}(\mathcal{W}).

Horja-Katzarkov proved a numerical version of the desired theorem.

Proposition 3.3

(
[
HK22
]
, Theorem 3.5)
.

\rank(\nabla^{A}_{GKZ})=\rank(\nabla^{B}_{GKZ})

Proof.

In loc.cit, we have

\rank([W])=\sum_{\Gamma}n^{A}_{W,\Gamma}\rank([\Gamma])
.
Hence

\rank(\nabla^{B}_{GKZ})=\sum_{W}\rank([W])\langle W\rangle=\sum_{W,\Gamma}n^{A}_{W,\Gamma}\rank([\Gamma])\langle W\rangle=\rank(\nabla^{A}_{GKZ}).

∎

Now we are ready to prove our main theorem.

Theorem 3.4
.

For any toric CY GIT problem
Q
, we have
\nabla^{A}_{GKZ}(Q)=\nabla^{B}_{GKZ}(Q)
, i.e.
n^{A}_{W,\Gamma}=n^{B}_{W,\Gamma}
for any wall
W
and minimal faces
\Gamma
.

Proof.

We induct on the rank of
L
. In the case
\rank L=0
, there is no wall, hence nothing to prove. Assume the case for
\rank L<n
is proven, and we have
\rank L=n
.

Let
\Gamma_{0}
be largest minimal face, concretely
\Gamma_{0}=\{i:q_{i}\neq 0\}
. For any minimal face
\Gamma\neq\Gamma_{0}
, we have
\rank L_{\Gamma}<n
, hence by induction
\nabla_{\Gamma}^{A}(Q_{\Gamma})=\nabla_{\Gamma}^{B}(Q_{\Gamma})
. By Proposition
3.2
, we have
\nabla_{\Gamma}^{A}(Q)=\nabla_{\Gamma}^{B}(Q)
for all minimal faces
\Gamma\neq\Gamma_{0}
.
Thus, we have

\nabla^{A}_{GKZ}(Q)-\nabla^{B}_{GKZ}(Q)=\sum_{\Gamma}(\nabla^{A}_{\Gamma}(Q)-\nabla^{B}_{\Gamma}(Q))[\Gamma]=(\nabla^{A}_{\Gamma_{0}}(Q)-\nabla^{B}_{\Gamma_{0}}(Q))[\Gamma_{0}]

Now, take
\rank
on both sides, using
\rank([\Gamma_{0}])=\rank(K_{0}\Coh(Z_{\Gamma_{0}}))=\rank(K_{0}\Coh(\mathbb{A}^{\Gamma_{0}^{c}}))=1\neq 0
, and Horja-Katzarkov result Proposition
3.3
, we get

0=\nabla^{A}_{\Gamma_{0}}(Q)-\nabla^{B}_{\Gamma_{0}}(Q).

Thus
\nabla^{A}_{GKZ}(Q)=\nabla^{B}_{GKZ}(Q)
.
∎

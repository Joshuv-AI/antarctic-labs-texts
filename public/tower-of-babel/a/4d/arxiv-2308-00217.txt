Existence of closed geodesics on certain non-compact Riemannian manifolds
Akashdeep Dey

Abstract
Let M M be a complete Riemannian manifold. Suppose M M contains a bounded, concave, connected open set U U with C 0 C^{0} boundary and M ∖ U M\setminus U is connected. We assume that either the relative homotopy set π 1 ​ ( M , M ∖ U ) = 0 \pi_{1}(M,M\setminus U)=0 or the union of all the conjugate subgroups of the image of the homomorphism π 1 ​ ( M ∖ U ) → π 1 ​ ( M ) \pi_{1}(M\setminus U)\rightarrow\pi_{1}(M) (induced by the inclusion M ∖ U ↪ M M\setminus U\hookrightarrow M ) is a proper subset of π 1 ​ ( M ) \pi_{1}(M) . (The first condition is equivalent to π 1 ​ ( M ∖ U ) → π 1 ​ ( M ) \pi_{1}(M\setminus U)\rightarrow\pi_{1}(M) is surjective; the second condition is satisfied if the relative homology group H 1 ​ ( M , M ∖ U ) ≠ 0 H_{1}(M,M\setminus U)\neq 0 .) Then there exists a non-trivial closed geodesic on M M . This partially proves a conjecture of Chambers, Liokumovich, Nabutovsky and Rotman [ 4 ] *Conjecture 1.1.

Introduction

In
[
16
]
, Poincaré asked the question whether every closed Riemannian surface
\Sigma
admits a closed geodesic. If the fundamental group of
\Sigma
(more generally, any closed Riemannian manifold
N
) is non-zero, then one can construct a non-trivial closed geodesic on
\Sigma
(resp. on
N
) by minimizing the length functional in a non-trivial (free) homotopy class of maps
S^{1}\rightarrow\Sigma
(resp.
S^{1}\rightarrow N
). In
[
3
]
, Birkhoff developed a min-max method and proved the existence of a closed geodesic on every Riemannian two-sphere. This theorem was generalized in higher dimensions by Fet and Lyusternik
[
11
]
; they proved that every closed Riemannian manifold admits a closed geodesic.

Minimal submanifolds are the higher dimensional generalizations of the geodesics. In
[
1
]
, Almgren developed a min-max theory to prove the existence of a closed (singular) minimal submanifold of dimension
l
in any closed Riemannian manifold
N^{n}
and for every
1\leq l\leq n-1
. The regularity theory in the co-dimension 1 case (i.e. when
l=n-1
) was further developed by Pitts
[
15
]
and Schoen–Simon
[
17
]
. When
l=1
, the Almgren–Pitts min–max theory produces stationary geodesic nets, which may not be smooth. (For a precise statement, see
[
10
]
*Proposition 3.2.) Using the phase-transition regularization of the length functional, Chodosh and Mantoulidis
[
5
]
proved that the geodesic nets, produced by the Almgren-Pitts min-max theory on surfaces, are closed geodesics. In
[
10
]
, Liokumovich and Staffa proved that for a generic Riemannian metric on a closed manifold, the union of all stationary geodesic nets is dense. In
[
9
]
, Li and Staffa proved the generic equidistribution of closed geodesics on closed surfaces and stationary geodesic nets on closed manifolds.

In general, the above mentioned Fet-Lyusternik theorem does not hold on non-compact manifolds. For example,
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, equipped with a Riemannian metric of non-positive curvature, does not contain a closed geodesic. By the works of Thorbergsson
[
18
]
and Bangert
[
2
]
, every complete Riemannian surface with locally convex ends admits a closed geodesic. Informally speaking, a complete Riemannian manifold
M
is said to have locally convex ends if
M
contains a bounded, concave (see Definition
1.1
), open set. In
[
4
]
, Chambers, Liokumovich, Nabutovsky and Rotman proved that every complete Riemannian manifold with locally convex ends admits a stationary geodesic net, which has at most one singular vertex. They also conjectured that such a manifold contains a closed geodesic. In this paper, we prove this conjecture (Theorems
1.2
,
1.3
, Remark
1.4
) under some additional topological assumptions.

Theorem 1.2

Let

Theorem 1.3
.

Let

be a complete Riemannian manifold. Suppose

contains a bounded, concave, connected, open set

(i)

The relative homotopy set
\pi_{1}(M,M\setminus U)=0
.

(ii)

The relative homology group

.

(iii)

(iv)

(v)

(vi)

Remark 1.4
.

Let
M
,
U
be as in the assumption of Theorem
1.2
. Then, there exists a closed geodesic on
M
if either
\pi_{1}(M,M\setminus U)=0
or the map
\pi_{0}(\Lambda(M\setminus U))\rightarrow\pi_{0}(\Lambda M)
is not surjective. Since

is connected, the homotopy exact sequence for the pair
(M,M\setminus U)
implies that
\pi_{1}(M,M\setminus U)=0
if and only if
\pi_{1}(M\setminus U)\rightarrow\pi_{1}(M)
is surjective. By the Seifert-van Kampen theorem, if

is connected and
\pi_{1}(\partial U)\rightarrow\pi_{1}(\overline{U})
is surjective, then
\pi_{1}(M\setminus U)\rightarrow\pi_{1}(M)
is also surjective. In the proof of Theorem

, we show that if
\partial U
is not connected, then
\pi_{0}(\Lambda(M\setminus U))\rightarrow\pi_{0}(\Lambda M)
is not surjective. Therefore, using Lemma

, we conclude that if

does not admit a closed geodesic, the following condition must be satisfied. The image of
\pi_{1}(M\setminus U)\rightarrow\pi_{1}(M)
is a proper subgroup of
\pi_{1}(M)
and the union of all its conjugate subgroups is
\pi_{1}(M)
. This condition implies the following condition on
\overline{U}
(which is a compact manifold with non-empty boundary).
\partial U
is connected, the image of
\pi_{1}(\partial U)\rightarrow\pi_{1}(\overline{U})
is a proper subgroup of
\pi_{1}(\overline{U})
and the union of all its conjugate subgroups is
\pi_{1}(\overline{U})
.

Let us briefly discuss the proof of Theorem
1.2
and elaborate on the condition
(*)
. When
\pi_{1}(M,M\setminus U)=0
, the proof for the existence of a closed geodesic on

is similar to the proof of the Fet-Lyusternik theorem
[
13
]
*Proof of Theorem 2.3.2. If
\pi_{1}(M,M\setminus U)=0
, we choose

such that
\pi_{i}(M,M\setminus U)=0
for

and
\pi_{k+1}(M,M\setminus U)\neq 0
. This implies
\pi_{k}(\Lambda M,\Lambda(M\setminus U),c_{p})\neq 0
(here

is the constant loop defined by

). We choose

such that

in
\pi_{k}(\Lambda M,\Lambda(M\setminus U),c_{p})
. We prove the existence of a closed geodesic on

by applying the Birkhoff curve shortening flow to the map

. The Birkhoff curve shortening flow is a discrete gradient flow for the length functional and (for the appropriately chosen parameters) the flow maps
\Lambda(M\setminus U)
to itself (since

is concave).

If the map
\pi_{0}(\Lambda(M\setminus U))\rightarrow\pi_{0}(\Lambda M)
is not injective or if
\pi_{m}(\Lambda M,\Lambda(M\setminus U),c^{*})\neq 0

).

On the other hand, when

, we aim to prove the existence of a closed geodesic on

using a minimization method. However, on a non-compact manifold, it may not be possible to obtain a closed geodesic by minimization since under the Birkhoff curve shortening flow, a loop might escape to infinity. So, for our proof method to work, we need to additionally assume that

is connected (so that a non-trivial element in
\pi_{1}(M,M\setminus U)
can be represented by a loop in

) and the map
\pi_{0}(\Lambda(M\setminus U))\rightarrow\pi_{0}(\Lambda M)
is not surjective. The map
\pi_{0}(\Lambda(M\setminus U))\rightarrow\pi_{0}(\Lambda M)
fails to be surjective if and only if there exists a loop
\gamma
in

which is not freely homotopic to a loop in

. As a consequence, under the Birkhoff curve shortening flow,
\gamma
can not escape to infinity and hence must converge to a non-trivial closed geodesic. We remark that for the above mentioned min-max argument, we do not need to assume that

is connected; however, if

is not connected,

is necessarily non-zero (cf. proof of Theorem

when either (C2) or (C3) holds in Section

The condition

has formal similarities with the topology of the free loop space

is a closed Riemannian manifold with negative sectional curvature.
(M,M\setminus U)
satisfies condition

\pi_{1}(M,M\setminus U)\neq 0
, for each
\tilde{\Pi}\in\pi_{0}(\Lambda M)
there exists a unique
\Pi\in\pi_{0}(\Lambda(M\setminus U))
such that
\Pi\subset\tilde{\Pi}
and for each non-standard path-component

(i.e.
\tilde{\Pi}
does not contain the constant loops),

for all

. (We note that, if

is connected, then

, i.e.

has more than one path-components.) On the other hand, if

is a closed Riemannian manifold with negative sectional curvature, then

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
G
be an arbitrary group and
H
be a proper finite index or normal subgroup of

. Then

G\neq\bigcup_{g\in G}gHg^{-1}.

(4.39)

Proof.

If
H
is a proper normal subgroup of

then
gHg^{-1}=H
for all
g\in G
; hence (
4.39
) holds.

If
H
is a proper finite index subgroup of
G
, we consider the set
S
of the left cosets of
H
in
G
. Let
\mathfrak{S}
denote the permutation group of
S
and
\pi:G\rightarrow\mathfrak{S}
be the homomorphism so that
\pi(x)\in\mathfrak{S}
maps
gH
to
xgH
. Let
G^{\prime}=\pi(G)
. By Burnside’s lemma,

1=\frac{1}{\left|G^{\prime}\right|}\sum_{\tau\in G^{\prime}}\left|\text{Fix}(\tau)\right|,

(4.40)

where for
\tau\in\mathfrak{S}
,
\text{Fix}(\tau)
is the set of the fixed points of
\tau
in
S
. Since the identity element of
G^{\prime}
has more than one fixed points, (
4.40
) implies there exists
\tau_{0}\in G^{\prime}
such that
\text{Fix}(\tau_{0})=\emptyset
.
x\in gHg^{-1}
(where

) if and only if

. Therefore, if
\tau_{0}=\pi(x_{0})
,

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
X
be a connected topological manifold. Suppose
A
is a connected open subset of
X
so that
\overline{A}
is a compact manifold with connected boundary
\partial A
. Let us consider the following three conditions.

(i)

The map
\pi_{0}(\Lambda(X\setminus A))\rightarrow\pi_{0}(\Lambda X)
(induced by the inclusion
X\setminus A\hookrightarrow X
) is surjective.

(ii)

The union of all the conjugate subgroups of the image of the homomorphism
\pi_{1}(X\setminus A)\rightarrow\pi_{1}(X)
(induced by the inclusion
X\setminus A\hookrightarrow X
) is
\pi_{1}(X)
.

(iii)

The union of all the conjugate subgroups of the image of the homomorphism
\pi_{1}(\partial A)\rightarrow\pi_{1}(\overline{A})
(induced by the inclusion
\partial A\hookrightarrow\overline{A}
) is
\pi_{1}(\overline{A})
.

Then (i) and (ii) are equivalent and they imply (iii).

Proof.

Given a group
G
, let
\text{conj}(G)
denote the set of conjugacy classes in
G
. A group homomorphism
\phi:H\rightarrow G
induces a map
\phi_{\#}:\text{conj}(H)\rightarrow\text{conj}(G)
which maps the conjugacy class of
h
to the conjugacy class of
\phi(h)
.
\phi_{\#}
is surjective if and only if

G=\bigcup_{g\in G}g\phi(H)g^{-1}.

Thus, the homomorphism
\pi_{1}(X\setminus A)\rightarrow\pi_{1}(X)
(induced by the inclusion
X\setminus A\hookrightarrow X
) induces a map
\text{conj}(\pi_{1}(X\setminus A))\rightarrow\text{conj}(\pi_{1}(X))
. For a topological space
Y
,
\pi_{0}(\Lambda Y)
can be canonically identified with
\text{conj}(\pi_{1}(Y))
. Under this identification, the map
\pi_{0}(\Lambda(X\setminus A))\rightarrow\pi_{0}(\Lambda X)
(induced by the inclusion
X\setminus A\hookrightarrow X
) corresponds to the map
\text{conj}(\pi_{1}(X\setminus A))\rightarrow\text{conj}(\pi_{1}(X))
, described above. Therefore, (i) and (ii) are equivalent.

By the Seifert-van Kampen theorem,
\pi_{1}(X)
is isomorphic to the amalgamated free product
\pi_{1}(\overline{A})\ast_{\pi_{1}(\partial A)}\pi_{1}(X\setminus A)
. From the properties of the amalgamated free product of groups
[
14
,
19
]
,
[
12
, Theorem 4.6(ii)]
it follows that (ii) implies (iii).
∎

Abstract
. This is the second in a series of papers on standard monomial theory and invariant theory of arc spaces. For any algebraically closed field K K , we construct a standard monomial basis for the arc space of the Pfaffian variety over K K . As an application, we prove the arc space analogue of the first and second fundamental theorems of invariant theory for the symplectic group.



1.
Introduction

Standard monomials and Pfaffian varieties

For a positive integer
p
, let

R=\mathbb{Z}[x^{(0)}_{uv}|\ 1\leq u,v\leq p]/(x^{(0)}_{uv}+x^{(0)}_{vu},x^{(0)}_{uu})

be the ring of polynomial functions with integer coefficients on the space of
p\times p
skew-symmetric matrices. Let the sequence
|u_{h},\dots,u_{2},u_{1}|
with
1\leq u_{i}<u_{i+1}\leq p
and
h\in 2\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
represent the Pfaffian
P(B)
of the skew-symmetric matrix

(1.1)

B=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}x^{(0)}_{u_{1}u_{1}}&x^{(0)}_{u_{1}u_{2}}&\cdots&x^{(0)}_{u_{1}u_{h}}\\
x^{(0)}_{u_{2}u_{1}}&x^{(0)}_{u_{2}u_{2}}&\cdots&x^{(0)}_{u_{2}u_{h}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
x^{(0)}_{u_{h}u_{1}}&x^{(0)}_{u_{h}u_{2}}&\cdots&x^{(0)}_{u_{h}u_{h}}\\
\end{array}\right).

There is a partial ordering on the set of these sequences given by

|u_{h},\dots,u_{2},u_{1}|\leq|u^{\prime}_{h^{\prime}},\dots,u^{\prime}_{2},u^{\prime}_{1}|,\quad\text{if }h^{\prime}\leq h,u_{i}\leq u^{\prime}_{i}.

R
has a standard monomial basis (cf.
[
6
]
) with
respect to this partially ordered set; the ordered products
A_{1}A_{2}\cdots A_{k}
of Pfaffians
A_{i}
with
A_{i}\leq A_{i+1}
, form a basis of
R
.

Similarly, let
R[h]
be the ideal of
R
generated by the Pfaffians of the diagonal
h\times h
-minors, and let
R_{h}=R/\penalty R[h]
. Then
R_{h}
has a basis consisting of ordered products
A_{1}A_{2}\cdots A_{k}
of the Pfaffians
A_{i}
with
(h-2,\dots,2,1)\leq A_{i}\leq A_{i+1}
.

For an arbitrary algebraically closed field
K
, let
SM_{p}(K)
be the affine space of
p\times p
skew-symmetric matrices with entries in
K
. The polynomial ring on
SM_{p}(K)
is
R^{K}=R\otimes K
. Let
R^{K}[h]
be the ideal generated by the Pfaffians of the diagonal
h\times h
minors. The Pfaffian variety
Pf_{h}(K)
is a closed subvariety of
SM_{p}(K)
with
R^{K}[h]
as the defining ideal. Then for an even integer
h
,
R^{K}/\penalty R^{K}[h]
, the affine coordinate ring of
Pf_{h}(K)
, has a standard monomial basis: the ordered products of
A_{1}A_{2}\cdots A_{k}
with
(h-2,\dots,2,1)\leq A_{i}\leq A_{i+1}
form a basis of
R^{K}/\penalty R^{K}[h]
.

Classical invariant theory for the symplectic group

For an even integer
h
, let
W=K^{\oplus h}
together with a non-degenerate, skew-symmetric bilinear form given by
w=\sum_{i=1}^{h/2}dz_{2i-1}\wedge dz_{2i}
. Let

Sp_{h}(K)=\{A\in SL_{h}(K)|\ A\ \text{ preserves}\ w\}

be the symplectic group over
K
. For
p\geq 1
, let
V=W^{\oplus p}
be the direct sum of
p
copies of
W
. The affine coordinate ring of
V
is

B^{K}=K[a^{(0)}_{il}|\ 1\leq i\leq p,\ 1\leq l\leq h].

Theorem 1.1
.

(1)

The ring of invariants
(B^{K})^{Sp_{h}(K)}
is generated by

X^{(0)}_{uv}=\sum_{i=1}^{h/2}(a^{(0)}_{u{2i-1}}a^{(0)}_{v2i}-a^{(0)}_{v{2i-1}}a^{(0)}_{u2i}).

(2)

The ideal of relations among the generators in (1) is generated by

P\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}X^{(0)}_{u_{1}v_{1}}&X^{(0)}_{u_{1}v_{2}}&\cdots&X^{(0)}_{u_{1}v_{h+2}}\\
X^{(0)}_{u_{2}v_{1}}&X^{(0)}_{u_{2}v_{2}}&\cdots&X^{(0)}_{u_{2}v_{h+2}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
X^{(0)}_{u_{h+2}v_{1}}&X^{(0)}_{u_{h+2}v_{2}}&\cdots&X^{(0)}_{u_{h+2}v_{h+2}}\\
\end{array}\right).

In particular,
V/\!\!/Sp_{k}(K)=\text{Spec}\ {B^{K}}^{Sp_{h}(K)}
is isomorphic to
Pf_{h+2}(K)
. Statements (1) and (2) are called the
first and second fundamental theorems of invariant theory
for
Sp_{h}(K)
. In the case
K=\mathbb{C}
, this theorem is classical and was proven by Weyl
[
12
]
. The general case is due to de Concini and Procesi
[
3
]
, and makes use of the standard monomial basis. A uniform treatment of the first and second fundamental theorems for all the classical groups using standard monomial theory can be found in the book of Lakshmibai and Raghavan
[
6
]
.

Arc spaces

Given an irreducible scheme
X
of finite type over
K
, the arc space
J_{\infty}(X)
is defined as the inverse limit of the finite jet schemes
J_{n}(X)

[
4
]
. By Corollary 1.2 of
[
2
]
, it is determined by its functor of points: for every
K
-algebra
A
, we have a bijection

\Hom(\Spec A,J_{\infty}(X))\cong\Hom(\Spec A[[t]],X).

If
G
is an algebraic group over
K
,
J_{\infty}(G)
is again an algebraic group over
K
. If
V
is a finite-dimensional
G
-module, there is an induced action of
J_{\infty}(G)
on
J_{\infty}(V)
. The quotient morphism
V\to V/\!\!/G
induces a morphism
J_{\infty}(V)\to J_{\infty}(V/\!\!/G)
, so we have a morphism

(1.2)

J_{\infty}(V)/\!\!/J_{\infty}(G)\to J_{\infty}(V/\!\!/G),

and the corresponding ring homomorphism

(1.3)

K[J_{\infty}(V/\!\!/G)]\rightarrow K[J_{\infty}(V)]^{J_{\infty}(G)}.

In the case
K=\mathbb{C}
, if
V/\!\!/G
is smooth or a complete intersection, it was shown in
[
7
]
that (
1.3
) is an isomorphism, although in general it is neither injective nor surjective.

Standard monomials for arc spaces

Let

\mathfrak{R}=\mathfrak{R}^{p}=\mathbb{Z}[x^{(k)}_{uv}|\ 1\leq u,v\leq p,\ k\geq 0]/(x^{(k)}_{uv}+x^{(k)}_{vu},x^{(k)}_{uu}),

with a derivation
\partial
characterized by
\partial x_{uv}^{(k)}=(k+1)x_{uv}^{(k+1)}
. It can be regarded as the ring of polynomial functions with integer coefficients on the arc space of
p\times p
skew-symmetric matrices.

Let
\mathfrak{R}[h]
be the ideal of
\mathfrak{R}
generated by the Pfaffians of the diagonal
h\times h
minors in the form of (
1.1
) and their normalized derivatives
\frac{1}{n!}\partial^{n}P(B)
. Let
\mathfrak{R}_{h}=\mathfrak{R}/\penalty\mathfrak{R}[h+2]
. Let
\mathcal{J}_{r}
be the set of Pfaffians of the matrices of the form of (
1.1
) with
h\leq r
and their normalized derivatives
\frac{1}{n!}\partial^{n}P(B)
. Note that
R
and
R_{h}
are naturally subrings of
\mathfrak{R}
and
\mathfrak{R}_{h}
, respectively. In Section
2
, we will define a notion of standard monomial on
\mathcal{J}_{h}
that extends the above notion on
R_{h}
, and we will prove the following result.

Theorem 1.2
.

For even integer
h
,
\mathfrak{R}_{h}
has a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis given by the standard monomials of
\mathcal{J}_{h}
.

Let
J_{\infty}(Pf_{h+2}(K))
be the arc space of the Pfaffian variety
Pf_{h+2}(K)
.
The affine coordinate ring of
J_{\infty}(Pf_{h+2}(K))
is
\mathfrak{R}_{h}\otimes K
, so we immediately have

Corollary 1.3
.

The affine coordinate ring of
J_{\infty}(Pf_{h+2}(K))
has a
K
-basis given by the standard monomials of
\mathcal{J}_{h}
.

Application in invariant theory

Our main application of Theorem
1.2
is to prove the arc space analogue of Theorem
1.1
. As above, for an even integer
h\geq 2
, let
Sp_{h}(K)
be the symplectic group over
K
,
W=K^{\oplus h}
its standard representation, and
V=W^{\oplus p}
the sum of
p
copies of
W
. The affine coordinate ring of
J_{\infty}(V)
is

\mathfrak{B}^{K}=K[a^{(k)}_{il}|\ 1\leq i\leq p,\ 1\leq l\leq h,\ k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}],

and the action of
Sp_{h}(K)
on
V
induces the action of
J_{\infty}(Sp_{h}(K))
on
\mathfrak{B}^{K}
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
X^{(k)}_{uv}={\bar{\partial}}^{k}\sum_{i=1}^{h/2}(a^{(0)}_{u{2i-1}}a^{(0)}_{v2i}-a^{(0)}_{v{2i-1}}a^{(0)}_{u2i})
.

(1)

The ring of invariants
{\mathfrak{B}^{K}}^{J_{\infty}(GL_{h}(K))}
is generated by
X^{(k)}_{uv}
.

(2)

The ideal of relations among the generators in (1) is generated by

(1.4)

{\bar{\partial}}^{k}P\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}X^{(0)}_{u_{1}v_{1}}&X^{(0)}_{u_{1}v_{2}}&\cdots&X^{(0)}_{u_{1}v_{h+2}}\\
X^{(0)}_{u_{2}v_{1}}&X^{(0)}_{u_{2}v_{2}}&\cdots&X^{(0)}_{u_{2}v_{h+2}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
X^{(0)}_{u_{h+2}v_{1}}&X^{(0)}_{u_{h+2}v_{2}}&\cdots&X^{(0)}_{u_{h+2}v_{h+2}}\\
\end{array}\right).

(3)

{\mathfrak{B}^{K}}^{J_{\infty}(Sp_{h}(K))}
has a
K
-basis given by standard monomials of
\mathcal{J}_{h}
.

Corollary 1.5
.

For all
h\geq 1
and
p\geq 1
, the map
K[J_{\infty}(V/\!\!/Sp_{h}(K))]\rightarrow K[J_{\infty}(V)]^{J_{\infty}(Sp_{h}(K))}
given by (
1.3
), is an isomorphism. In particular,

J_{\infty}(V)/\!\!/J_{\infty}(Sp_{h}(K))\cong J_{\infty}(V/\!\!/Sp_{h}(K)).

Corollary
1.5
generalizes Theorem 4.5 of
[
7
]
, which is the case
K=\mathbb{C}
and
p\leq h+2
. A similar result was proven in
[
8
]
for the general linear group
GL_{k}(K)
. The approach in this paper is similar to
[
8
]
but more involved since we need a result of Bardsley and Richardson
[
1
]
which provides a version of the Luna slice theorem in arbitrary characteristic.

Theorem
1.4
has significant applications to vertex algebras which we develop in
[
9
,
10
]
. First, it provides a complete description of certain cosets of affine vertex algebras inside free field algebras that are related to the classical Howe pairs. Next, for any smooth manifold
X
in either the algebraic, complex analytic or smooth settings, the
chiral de Rham complex

\Omega^{\text{ch}}_{X}
is a sheaf of vertex algebras on
X
that was introduced by Malikov, Schechtman and Vaintrob in
[
11
]
. Theorem
1.4
is essential in the description of the vertex algebra of global sections
\Gamma(X,\Omega^{\text{ch}}_{X})
for a
d
-dimensional compact Kähler manifold
X
with holonomy group
Sp(\frac{d}{2})
. This algebra is isomorphic to the simple small
\mathcal{N}=4
superconformal algebra with central charge
3d
, and is an important building block in the structure of
\Gamma(X,\Omega^{\text{ch}}_{X})
for an arbitrary compact Ricci-flat Kähler manifold
X
.

2.
Standard monomials

Derivation

Let
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
be the set of non-negative integers. For any
k,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, let

C_{n}^{k}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{n!}{k!(n-k)!},&0\leq k\leq n;\\
0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

As above, let
\mathfrak{R}=\mathbb{Z}[x^{(k)}_{uv}|\ 1\leq u,v\leq p,\ k\geq 0]/(x^{(k)}_{uv}+x^{(k)}_{vu},x^{(k)}_{uu})
be the ring with derivation
\partial
given on generators by
\partial x_{uv}^{(k)}=(k+1)x_{uv}^{(k+1)}
. Let
{\bar{\partial}}^{l}=\frac{1}{l!}\partial^{l}
be the
l^{\text{th}}
normalized derivation. We have

{\bar{\partial}}^{l}x_{ij}^{(k)}=C_{k+l}^{l}x_{ij}^{(k+l)}\in\mathfrak{R}.

The following statements are easy to verify.

Proposition 2.1
.

For any
a,b\in\mathfrak{R}
,

{\bar{\partial}}^{l}(ab)=\sum_{i=0}^{l}{\bar{\partial}}^{i}a\,{\bar{\partial}}^{l-i}b,

and
{\bar{\partial}}^{l}a\in\mathfrak{R}
.

Proposition 2.2
.

For a skew-symmetric matrix
B
of (
1.1
) with
h=2l
,

(2.1)

{\bar{\partial}}^{n}P(B)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1}+\cdots+n_{l}=n\\
n_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{l!2^{l}}\sum_{\sigma}\sign(\sigma)x^{(n_{1})}_{u_{\sigma(1)}u_{\sigma(2)}}x^{(n_{2})}_{u_{\sigma(3)}u_{\sigma(4)}}\cdots x^{(n_{l})}_{u_{\sigma(h-1)}u_{\sigma(h)}}.

Generators

Let the sequence

(2.2)

J={\bar{\partial}}^{k}|u_{h},\dots,u_{2},u_{1}|

with
1\leq u_{i}<u_{i+1}\leq p
and

represent
{\bar{\partial}}^{n}P(B)\in\mathfrak{R}
, the
n^{\text{th}}
normalized derivative of the Pfaffian of
B
in (
1.1
). We call
wt(J)=n
the weight of
J
and call
sz(J)=h
the size of
J
. Let
\mathcal{J}
be the set of these sequences
and

\mathcal{J}_{h}=\{J\in\mathcal{J}|sz(J)\leq h\}

be the set of elements in
\mathcal{J}
with size less than or equal to
h
.
Let
\mathcal{E}
be the set of sequences

(2.3)

E=|(u_{h},k_{h}),\dots,(u_{2},k_{2}),(u_{1},k_{1})|

with
1\leq u_{i}\leq p
,
u_{i}\neq u_{j}
if
i\neq j
and
k_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
Let

||E||={\bar{\partial}}^{n}|u_{\sigma(h)},\dots,u_{\sigma(2)},u_{\sigma(1)}|\in\mathcal{J}.

Here

n=\sum k_{i}
, and
\sigma
is the permutation of
1,2,\dots,h
such that
u_{\sigma(i)}<u_{\sigma(i+1)}
. Let

wt(E)=wt(||E||),\quad\quad sz(E)=sz(||E||).

Let

\mathcal{E}_{h}=\{E\in\mathcal{E}|\,\,sz(E)\leq h\}.

For
J\in\mathcal{J}
, let

\mathcal{E}(J)=\{E\in\mathcal{E}|\ ||E||=J\}.

\mathcal{J}
is a set of generators of
\mathfrak{R}
and we can use the elements in
\mathcal{E}(J)
to represent
J
.

Ordering

For any set
\mathcal{S}
, let
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{S})
be the set of ordered products of elements of
\mathcal{S}
.
If
\mathcal{S}
is an ordered set, we order the set
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{S})
lexicographically, that is

S_{1}S_{2}\cdots S_{m}\prec S^{\prime}_{1}S^{\prime}_{2}\cdots S^{\prime}_{n}\quad\text{ if }S_{i}=S^{\prime}_{i},i<i_{0},\text{ with }S_{i_{0}}\prec S^{\prime}_{i_{0}}\text{ or }i_{0}=m+1,n>m.

We order
\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{Z})
, the set of ordered products of integers, lexicographically.

There is an ordering on the set
\mathcal{J}
:

{\bar{\partial}}^{k}|u_{h},\dots,u_{2},u_{1}|\prec{\bar{\partial}}^{k^{\prime}}|u^{\prime}_{h^{\prime}},\dots,u^{\prime}_{2},u^{\prime}_{1}|

if

•

h^{\prime}<h
;

•

or
h^{\prime}=h
and
k<k^{\prime}
;

•

or
h^{\prime}=h
,
k=k^{\prime}
and
u_{h}\cdots u_{1}\prec u^{\prime}_{h}\cdots u^{\prime}_{1}
. Here we order the words of natural numbers lexicographically.

We order the pairs
(u,h)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\times\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
by

(u,h)\leq(u^{\prime},h^{\prime}),\text{ if }h<h^{\prime}\text{ or }h=h^{\prime}\text{ and }u\leq u^{\prime}.

There is a partial ordering on the set
\mathcal{E}
:

|(u_{h},k_{h}),\dots,(u_{1},k_{1})|\leq|(u^{\prime}_{h^{\prime}},k^{\prime}_{h^{\prime}}),\dots,(u^{\prime}_{1},k^{\prime}_{1})|

if
h^{\prime}\leq h
and
(u_{i},k_{i})\leq(u^{\prime}_{i},k^{\prime}_{i})
, for
1\leq i\leq h^{\prime}
.

Finally, there is an ordering on
\mathcal{E}
:

|(u_{h},k_{h}),\dots,(u_{1},k_{1})|\prec|(u^{\prime}_{h^{\prime}},k^{\prime}_{h^{\prime}}),\dots,(u^{\prime}_{1},k^{\prime}_{1})|

if

•

h>h^{\prime}
;

•

or
h=h^{\prime}
and
\sum k_{i}<\sum k^{\prime}_{i}
;

•

or
h=h^{\prime}
,
\sum k_{i}=\sum k^{\prime}_{i}
and

(u_{h},k_{h})\cdots(u_{1},k_{1})\prec(u^{\prime}_{h^{\prime}},k^{\prime}_{h^{\prime}})\cdots(u^{\prime}_{1},k^{\prime}_{1})

Here we order the words of
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\times\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
lexicographically.

Lemma 2.3
.

If
E\leq E^{\prime}
, then
||E||\prec||E^{\prime}||
.

Proof.

If
sz(E^{\prime})<sz(E)
or
sz(E)=sz(E^{\prime})
and

, then
||E||\prec||E^{\prime}||
.

If
sz(E)=sz(E^{\prime})
and
wt(E)=wt(E^{\prime})
, we must have

. So
u_{i}\leq u^{\prime}_{i}
, we have
||E||\prec||E^{\prime}||
.
∎

Relations

Let

{\bar{\partial}}^{k}|u_{h},\dots,u_{1}|=0

if there is
1\leq i<j\leq h

.

J\in\mathcal{J}

{\bar{\partial}}^{k}|u_{\sigma(h)},\dots,u_{\sigma(2)},u_{\sigma(1)}|=\sign(\sigma)J.

Lemma 2.4

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma}\frac{1}{h!s!}\sign(\sigma){\bar{\partial}}^{0}|u_{\sigma(h+s)},\dots,u_{\sigma(s+1)}|\,\,{\bar{\partial}}^{k}|u^{\prime}_{h^{\prime}},\dots,u^{\prime}_{s+1},u_{\sigma(s)},\dots,u_{\sigma(1)}|\in\mathfrak{R}[h+2].

Standard monomials

Now we give the definition of standard monomials of
\mathcal{J}
.

Definition 2.7
.

An ordered product
E_{1}E_{2}\cdots E_{m}
of elements of
\mathcal{E}
is said to be standard if

(1)

E_{a}\leq E_{a+1}
,
1\leq a<m
,

(2)

E_{1}
is the largest in
\mathcal{E}(||E_{1}||)
under the order
\prec
,

(3)

E_{a+1}
is the largest in
\mathcal{E}(||E_{a+1}||)
such that
E_{a}\leq E_{a+1}
.

An ordered product
J_{1}J_{2}\cdots J_{m}
of elements of
\mathcal{J}
is said to be standard
if there is a standard ordered product
E_{1}E_{2}\cdots E_{m}
such that
E_{i}\in\mathcal{E}(J_{i})
.

Let
\mathcal{S}\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{J})\subset\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{J})
be the set of standard monomials of
\mathcal{J}
.
Let
\mathcal{S}\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{E})\subset\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{E})
be the set of standard monomials of
\mathcal{E}
. Let
\mathcal{S}\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{J}_{h})=\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{J}_{h})\cap\mathcal{S}\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{J})
be the set of standard monomials of
\mathcal{J}_{h}
. Let
\mathcal{S}\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{E}_{h})=\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{E}_{h})\cap\mathcal{S}\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{E})
be the set of standard monomials of
\mathcal{E}_{h}
.

By Definition
2.7
, if
J_{1}J_{2}\cdots J_{m}
is standard, the standard monomial
E_{1}\cdots E_{m}\in\mathcal{S}\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{E})
corresponding to
J_{1}\cdots J_{m}
is unique and
E_{1}
has the form

|(u_{h},wt(E_{1})),(u_{h-1},0),\dots,(u_{1},0)|\in\mathcal{E}

with
u_{i}<u_{i+1}
. Therefore the map

\pi_{h}:\mathcal{S}\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{E}_{h})\to\mathcal{S}\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{J}_{h}),\quad E_{1}E_{2}\cdots E_{m}\mapsto||E_{1}||||E_{2}||\cdots||E_{m}||

is a bijection.

We order
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{J})
, the set of ordered products of elements of
\mathcal{J}
, lexicographically. The following lemma will be proved later in Section
6
.

Lemma 2.8
.

If
J=J_{1}\cdots J_{b}\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{J})
is not standard,
J
can be written as a linear combination of
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{J})
preceding
J_{1}\cdots J_{b-1}
with integer coefficients.

Recall that
\mathfrak{R}[h]
denotes the ideal generated by
J\in\mathcal{J}
with
sz(J)=h
, and
\mathfrak{R}_{h}=\mathfrak{R}/\penalty\mathfrak{R}[h+2]
. If
h\geq p
, then
\mathcal{J}_{h}=\mathcal{J}
and
\mathfrak{R}_{h}=\mathfrak{R}
.
By the above lemma, we immediately have

Lemma 2.9
.

Any element of
\mathfrak{R}_{h}
can be written as a linear combination of standard monomials of
\mathcal{J}_{h}
with integer coefficients.

Proof.

We only need to show that any element of
\mathfrak{R}
can be written as a linear combination of standard monomials of
\mathcal{J}
with integer coefficients. If the lemma is not true,
there must be a smallest element
J\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{J})
, which cannot be written as a linear combination of elements of
\mathcal{S}\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{J})
with integer coefficients. So
J
is not standard. By Lemma
2.8
,

J=\sum_{\alpha}\pm J_{\alpha}
with
J_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{J})
and
J_{\alpha}\prec J
. Since
J_{\alpha}
can be written as a linear combination of elements of
\mathcal{S}\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{J})
with integer coefficients,

J
can also be written as such a linear combination, which is a contradiction.
∎

4.
Application

In this section, we give the main application of our standard monomial basis, which is the arc space analogue of Theorem
1.1
.

Arc spaces

Suppose that
X
is a scheme of finite type over
K
. Its arc space (cf.
[
4
]
)
J_{\infty}(X)
is determined by its functor of points. For every
K
-algebra
A
, we have a bijection

\Hom(\Spec A,J_{\infty}(X))\cong\Hom(\Spec A[[t]],X).

If
i:X\to Y
is a morphism of schemes, we get a morphism of schemes
i_{\infty}:J_{\infty}(X)\to J_{\infty}(Y)
.
If
i
is a closed immersion, then
i_{\infty}
is a closed immersion.
If
i:X\to Y
is an étale morphism,
then we have a Cartesian diagram

\begin{array}[]{ccc}J_{\infty}(X)&\to&J_{\infty}(Y)\\
\downarrow&&\downarrow\\
X&\to&Y\end{array}

If
X=\Spec K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
, then
J_{\infty}(Y)=\Spec K[x^{(k)}_{i}|1\leq i\leq n,k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}]
. The identification is made as follows: for a
K
-algebra
A
, a morphism
\phi:K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to A[[t]]
determined by
\phi(x_{i})=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{i}^{(k)}t^{k}
corresponds to a morphism

K[x_{i}^{(k)}]\to A
determined by
x_{i}^{(k)}\to a_{i}^{(k)}
. Note that
K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
can be identified with the subalgebra
K[x^{(0)}_{1},\dots,x^{(0)}_{n}]\subset K[x_{i}^{(k)}]
, and from now on we use
x_{i}^{(0)}
instead of
x_{i}
.

The polynomial ring
K[x^{(k)}_{i}]
has a derivation
\partial
defined on generators by

(4.1)

\partial x_{i}^{(k)}=(k+1)x_{i}^{(k+1)}.

It is more convenient to work with the normalized
k
-derivation
{\bar{\partial}}=\frac{1}{k!}\partial^{k}
, but this is a priori not well-defined on
K[x_{i}^{(k)}]
if
\text{char}\ K
is positive. But
\partial
is well defined on
\mathbb{Z}[x_{i}^{(k)}]
, and
{\bar{\partial}}^{k}
maps
\mathbb{Z}[x_{i}^{(k)}]
to itself, so for any
K
, there is an induced
K
-linear map

(4.2)

{\bar{\partial}}^{k}:K[x_{i}^{(k)}]\to K[x_{i}^{(k)}],

obtained by tensoring with
K
.

If
X
is the affine scheme
\text{Spec}\ K[x^{(0)}_{1},\dots,x^{(0)}_{n}]/(f_{1},\dots,f_{r})
, then
J_{\infty}(X)
is the affine scheme

\text{Spec}\ K[x_{i}^{(k)}|\ i=1,\dots,\ k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}]/({\bar{\partial}}^{l}f_{j}|\ j=1,\dots,r,\ l\geq 0).

Indeed, for every
f\in K[x^{(0)}_{1},\dots,x^{(0)}_{n}]
, we have

\phi(f)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}({\bar{\partial}}^{k}f)(a_{1}^{(0)},\dots,a_{n}^{(k)})\,t^{k}.

It follows that
\phi
induces a morphism
K[x^{(0)}_{1},\dots,x^{(0)}_{n}]/(f_{1},\dots,f_{r})\to A[[t]]
if and only if

({\bar{\partial}}^{k}f_{i})(a_{1}^{(0)},\dots,a_{n}^{(k)})=0,\ \text{for all}\ i=1,\dots,r,\ k\geq 0.

If
Y
is the affine scheme
\text{Spec}\ K[y^{(0)}_{1},\dots,y^{(0)}_{m}]/(g_{1},\dots,g_{s})
, a morphism
P:X\to Y
gives a ring homomorphism
P^{*}:K[Y]\to K[X]
.
Then the induced homomorphism of arc spaces
P_{\infty}:J_{\infty}(X)\to J_{\infty}(Y)
is given by

P^{*}_{\infty}(y_{i}^{(k)})={\bar{\partial}}^{k}P^{*}(y_{i}^{(0)}).

In particular,
P^{*}_{\infty}
commutes with
{\bar{\partial}}^{k}
for all
k\geq 0
.

Arc space of the Pfaffian variety

R^{K}=K[x^{(0)}_{ij}|\ 1\leq i,\ j\leq p]/(x^{(0)}_{ij}-x^{(0)}_{ji},x^{(0)}_{ii})
is the affine coordinate ring of the space of
p\times p
skew-symmetric matrices
SM_{p}(K)
.
The Pfaffian variety
Pf_{h}(K)
is the subvariety of
SM_{p}(K)
determined by the ideal
R^{K}[h]
generated by the Pfaffians of all diagonal
h
-minors. So the affine coordinate ring of

Principle

Let

The following result is from

is trivial.

The group structure

induces the group structure on its arc space

J_{\infty}(G)\times J_{\infty}(G)\to J_{\infty}(G).

So

is an algebraic group. For a

J_{\infty}(G)\times J_{\infty}(X)\to J_{\infty}(X).

Proof.

For any étale morphism

\begin{array}[]{ccc}J_{\infty}(V)&\to&J_{\infty}(X/\!\!/G)\\
\downarrow&&\downarrow\\

\downarrow&&\downarrow\\

\displaystyle J_{\infty}(V)\times_{J_{\infty}(X/\!\!/G)}J_{\infty}(X)/\!\!/J_{\infty}(G)

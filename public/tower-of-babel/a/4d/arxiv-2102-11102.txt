Abstract
. A d d -dimensional random array on a nonempty set I I is a stochastic process 𝑿 = ⟨ X s : s ∈ ( I d ) ⟩ \bm{X}=\langle X_{s}:s\in\binom{I}{d}\rangle indexed by the set ( I d ) \binom{I}{d} of all d d -element subsets of I I . We obtain structural decompositions of finite, high-dimensional random arrays whose distribution is invariant under certain symmetries. Our first main result is a distributional decomposition of finite, (approximately) spreadable, high-dimensional random arrays whose entries take values in a finite set; the two-dimensional case of this result is the finite version of an infinitary decomposition due to Fremlin and Talagrand. Our second main result is a physical decomposition of finite, spreadable, high-dimensional random arrays with square-integrable entries that is the analogue of the Hoeffding/Efron–Stein decomposition. All proofs are effective. We also present applications of these decompositions in the study of concentration of functions of finite, high-dimensional random arrays.

1.3.

1.4.

2.
Approximation by a random array of lower complexity

The main result in this section—Proposition
2.1
below—asserts that any large subarray
of a finite-valued, approximately spreadable random array
\bm{X}
can be approximated, in distribution,
by a random array that is obtained by projecting the entries of
\bm{X}
on certain
\sigma
-algebras
of “lower complexity”.

2.1.
The
\sigma
-algebras
\Sigma(\mathcal{G}^{s}_{\ell},\bm{X})

Our first goal is to define the aforementioned
\sigma
-algebras.
To this end we need to introduce some notation that will be used throughout this section and Section

Let

be positive integers, and let

For every finite-valued,

(k\ell_{0})

Step 1

We start with the following lemma that is a consequence of spreadability.

Lemma 2.2

(Shift invariance of projections)
.

Let
n,d
be positive integers with
n\geqslant d
, let
s\in\binom{[n]}{d}
, and let
\mathcal{F}
be a nonempty
subset of
\binom{[n]}{d}
. Set
F\coloneqq s\cup(\cup\mathcal{F})
.
Also let
G
be a subset of
[n]
with
|F|=|G|
, and set

(2.9)

t\coloneqq\mathrm{I}_{F,G}(s)\ \ \ \text{ and }\ \ \ \mathcal{G}\coloneqq\big\{\mathrm{I}_{F,G}(s):s\in\mathcal{F}\big\}.

Finally, let
\mathcal{X}
be a finite set, let
\eta\geqslant 0
, and let
\bm{X}=\langle X_{s}:s\in\binom{[n]}{d}\rangle

be an
\mathcal{X}
-valued,
\eta
-spreadable,
d
-dimensional random array on
[n]
.
Then for every
a\in\mathcal{X}
we have

(2.10)

\Big|\big\|\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{1}_{[X_{s}=a]}\,|\,\Sigma(\mathcal{F},\bm{X})]\big\|_{L_{2}}^{2}-\big\|\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{1}_{[X_{t}=a]}\,|\,\Sigma(\mathcal{G},\bm{X})]\big\|_{L_{2}}^{2}\Big|\leqslant 5\eta|\mathcal{X}|^{|\mathcal{F}|}.

Proof.

Fix
a\in\mathcal{X}
. For every collection
\mathbf{a}=(a_{u})_{u\in\mathcal{F}}
of elements of
\mathcal{X}
we set

(2.11)

B_{\mathbf{a}}\coloneqq\bigcap_{u\in\mathcal{F}}[X_{u}=a_{u}]\ \ \ \text{ and }\ \ \ C_{\mathbf{a}}\coloneqq\bigcap_{u\in\mathcal{F}}[X_{\mathrm{I}_{F,G}(u)}=a_{u}].

Since the random array
\bm{X}
is
\eta
-spreadable, for every
\mathbf{a}\in\mathcal{X}^{\mathcal{F}}
we have

(2.12)

\big|\mathbb{P}(B_{\mathbf{a}})-\mathbb{P}(C_{\mathbf{a}})\big|\leqslant\eta\ \ \ \text{ and }\ \ \ \big|\mathbb{P}([X_{s}=a]\cap B_{\mathbf{a}})-\mathbb{P}([X_{t}=a]\cap C_{\mathbf{a}})\big|\leqslant\eta.

Set
\mathcal{B}\coloneqq\{\mathbf{a}\in\mathcal{X}^{\mathcal{F}}:\mathbb{P}(B_{\mathbf{a}})>0\text{ and }\mathbb{P}(C_{\mathbf{a}})>0\}
. By (
2.12
), for every
\mathbf{a}\in\mathcal{X}^{\mathcal{F}}\setminus\mathcal{B}

we have
\mathbb{P}(B_{\mathbf{a}})\leqslant\eta
and
\mathbb{P}(C_{\mathbf{a}})\leqslant\eta
and, consequently,

(2.13)

\big|\mathbb{P}\big([X_{s}=a]\,|\,B_{\mathbf{a}}\big)^{\!2}\,\mathbb{P}(B_{\mathbf{a}})-\mathbb{P}\big([X_{t}=a]\,|\,C_{\mathbf{a}}\big)^{\!2}\,\mathbb{P}(C_{\mathbf{a}})\big|\leqslant 2\eta.

Next, let
\mathbf{a}\in\mathcal{B}
be arbitrary, and observe that

(2.14)

\displaystyle\big|\mathbb{P}

\displaystyle\big([X_{s}=a]\,|\,B_{\mathbf{a}}\big)-\mathbb{P}\big([X_{t}=a]\,|\,C_{\mathbf{a}}\big)\big|=

\displaystyle\,\,\,=\bigg|\frac{\mathbb{P}\big([X_{s}=a]\cap B_{\mathbf{a}}\big)}{\mathbb{P}(B_{\mathbf{a}})}-\frac{\mathbb{P}\big([X_{t}=a]\cap C_{\mathbf{a}}\big)}{\mathbb{P}(C_{\mathbf{a}})}\bigg|

\displaystyle\,\,\,\leqslant\frac{1}{\mathbb{P}(B_{\mathbf{a}})}\,\big|\mathbb{P}\big([X_{s}=a]\cap B_{\mathbf{a}}\big)-\mathbb{P}\big([X_{t}=a]\cap C_{\mathbf{a}}\big)\big|+\mathbb{P}(C_{\mathbf{a}})\,\bigg|\frac{1}{\mathbb{P}(B_{\mathbf{a}})}-\frac{1}{\mathbb{P}(C_{\mathbf{a}})}\,\bigg|

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{e2.12}}}{{\leqslant}}\frac{\eta}{\mathbb{P}(B_{\mathbf{a}})}+\frac{1}{\mathbb{P}(B_{\mathbf{a}})}\,\big|\mathbb{P}(C_{\mathbf{a}})-\mathbb{P}(B_{\mathbf{a}})\big|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{e2.12}}}{{\leqslant}}\frac{2\eta}{\mathbb{P}(B_{\mathbf{a}})}.

On the other hand, we have
\mathbb{P}\big([X_{s}=a]\,|\,B_{\mathbf{a}}\big)+\mathbb{P}\big([X_{t}=a]\,|\,C_{\mathbf{a}}\big)\leqslant 2

and so, by (
2.14
),

(2.15)

\Big|\mathbb{P}\big([X_{s}=a]\,|\,B_{\mathbf{a}}\big)^{\!2}-\mathbb{P}\big([X_{t}=a]\,|\,C_{\mathbf{a}}\big)^{\!2}\Big|\leqslant\frac{4\eta}{\mathbb{P}(B_{\mathbf{a}})}.

Therefore, for every
\mathbf{a}\in\mathcal{B}
,

(2.16)

\displaystyle\Big|\mathbb{P}\big([X_{s}=a]\,|\,B_{\mathbf{a}}

\displaystyle\big)^{\!2}\,\mathbb{P}(B_{\mathbf{a}})-\mathbb{P}\big([X_{t}=a]\,|\,C_{\mathbf{a}}\big)^{\!2}\,\mathbb{P}(C_{\mathbf{a}})\Big|\leqslant

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{P}(B_{\mathbf{a}})\,\Big|\mathbb{P}\big([X_{s}=a]\,|\,B_{\mathbf{a}}\big)^{\!2}-\mathbb{P}\big([X_{t}=a]\,|\,C_{\mathbf{a}}\big)^{\!2}\Big|\,+

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ +\mathbb{P}\big([X_{t}=a]\,|\,C_{\mathbf{a}}\big)^{\!2}\,\big|\mathbb{P}(B_{\mathbf{a}})-\mathbb{P}(C_{\mathbf{a}})\big|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{e2.15},\eqref{e2.12}}}{{\leqslant}}5\eta.

By (
2.13
) and (
2.16
), we conclude that

(2.17)

\displaystyle\Big|\big\|

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{1}_{[X_{s}=a]}\,|\,\Sigma(\mathcal{F},\bm{X})]\big\|_{L_{2}}^{2}-\big\|\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{1}_{[X_{t}=a]}\,|\,\Sigma(\mathcal{G},\bm{X})]\big\|_{L_{2}}^{2}\Big|\,=

\displaystyle=\bigg|\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in\mathcal{X}^{\mathcal{F}}}\mathbb{P}\big([X_{s}=a]\,|\,B_{\mathbf{a}}\big)^{\!2}\,\mathbb{P}(B_{\mathbf{a}})-\mathbb{P}\big([X_{t}=a]\,|\,C_{\mathbf{a}}\big)^{\!2}\,\mathbb{P}(C_{\mathbf{a}})\bigg|\leqslant 5\eta|\mathcal{X}|^{|\mathcal{F}|}.\qed

and
m\geqslant 2
, and let

be a set with

let

, and let
\bm{X}=\langle X_{s}:s\in\binom{[n]}{d}\rangle
be an
\mathcal{X}
-valued,
\eta
-spreadable,

d
-dimensional random array on
[n]

(k\ell_{0})
-sparse
t\in\binom{[n]}{d}

there exists
\ell\in[\ell_{0}]
such that for every
a\in\mathcal{X}
,

(2.19)

\Big\|\mathbb{E}\big[\mathbf{1}_{[X_{t}=a]}\,|\,\Sigma(\mathcal{G}^{t}_{k\ell},\bm{X})\big]-\mathbb{E}\big[\mathbf{1}_{[X_{t}=a]}\,|\,\Sigma(\mathcal{G}^{t}_{\ell},\bm{X})\big]\Big\|_{L_{2}}\leqslant\sqrt{\theta}.

Proof.

Fix
t\in\binom{[n]}{d}
that is
(k\ell_{0})
-sparse.
For every
a\in\mathcal{X}
and
r\in[m\lfloor 1/\theta\rfloor+1]

(2.20)

D^{a}_{r}\coloneqq\mathbb{E}\big[\mathbf{1}_{[X_{t}=a]}\,|\,\Sigma(\mathcal{G}^{t}_{k^{r}},\bm{X})\big]-\mathbb{E}\big[\mathbf{1}_{[X_{t}=a]}\,|\,\Sigma(\mathcal{G}^{t}_{k^{r-1}},\bm{X})\big].

Clearly, it enough to show that there exists
r\in[m\lfloor 1/\theta\rfloor+1]
such that
\|D^{a}_{r}\|_{L_{2}}\leqslant\sqrt{\theta}

for every
a\in\mathcal{X}
. Assume, towards a contradiction, that for every
r\in[m\lfloor 1/\theta\rfloor+1]
there exists

a_{r}\in\mathcal{X}
such that

. Since

and a subset

of
[m\lfloor 1/\theta\rfloor+1]
with

such that

, which is equivalent to saying that

for every

.
Now, observe that the sequence

is increasing with respect to inclusion, which in turn implies, by (
2.1
), that the sequence

(2.21)

1\geqslant\|\mathbf{1}_{[X_{t}=b]}\|_{L_{2}}^{2}\geqslant\sum_{r=1}^{m\lfloor 1/\theta\rfloor+1}\|D^{b}_{r}\|_{L_{2}}^{2}\geqslant\sum_{r\in R}\|D^{b}_{r}\|_{L_{2}}^{2}>|R|\,\theta>1,

which is clearly a contradiction. The proof is completed.
∎

5.
Orbits

The following definition plays an important role in the proof of Theorem
1.7
.

Definition 5.1

(Orbits)
.

Let
\bm{X}=\langle X_{i}:i\in I\rangle
be a family of real-valued random variables defined on a common probability space,
indexed by a set
I
with
|I|\geqslant 2
, and such that
\|X_{i}\|_{L_{2}}=1
for every
i\in I
. Also let
\eta\geqslant 0
.
We say that
\bm{X}
is an
\eta\text{-orbit}

(
and simply an
orbit
if
\eta=0
)
if for every pair
\{i_{1},j_{1}\}

and
\{i_{2},j_{2}\}
of doubletons of
I
we have

(5.1)

\displaystyle\big|\mathbb{E}[X_{i_{1}}X_{j_{1}}]-\mathbb{E}[X_{i_{2}}X_{j_{2}}]\big|\leqslant\eta.

Arguably, the simplest example of an orbit is a (finite or infinite) sequence of independent random variables
with zero mean and unit variance. Note, however, that the notion of an orbit is significantly less restrictive
than independence—we will see several more refined examples of orbits in Sections
6
and
7
.

It is intuitively clear that an orbit is a stochastic process that “everywhere looks the same”. We formalize
this basic intuition in the following proposition.

Proposition 5.2

(Universality)
.

Let
\eta\geqslant 0
, and let
\bm{X}=\langle X_{i}:i\in I\rangle
be an
\eta
-orbit.
Also let
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}
be finite subsets of
I
with
|\mathcal{F}|,|\mathcal{G}|\geqslant 2
, and set

(5.2)

\displaystyle Z_{\mathcal{F}}\coloneqq\frac{1}{|\mathcal{F}|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{F}}X_{i}\ \ \ \text{ and }\ \ \ Z_{\mathcal{G}}\coloneqq\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{G}}X_{i}.

Then we have

(5.3)

\displaystyle\|Z_{\mathcal{F}}-Z_{\mathcal{G}}\|_{L_{2}}\leqslant 2\,\bigg(\frac{1}{\min\{|\mathcal{F}|,|\mathcal{G}|\}}+\eta\bigg)^{1/2}.

Proof.

Fix
\kappa,\ell\in I
with
\kappa\neq\ell
, and set
\delta\coloneqq\mathbb{E}[X_{\kappa}X_{\ell}]
.
Since
\bm{X}
is an
\eta
-orbit, we see that
\delta-\eta\leqslant\mathbb{E}[X_{i}X_{j}]\leqslant\delta+\eta
for every

i,j\in I
with
i\neq j
; also recall that
\mathbb{E}[X_{i}^{2}]=1
for every
i\in I
. Therefore,

(5.4)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[Z_{\mathcal{F}}^{2}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{F}|^{2}}\sum_{i,j\in\mathcal{F}}\mathbb{E}[X_{i}X_{j}]=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{F}|^{2}}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{F}}\mathbb{E}[X_{i}^{2}]+\frac{1}{|\mathcal{F}|^{2}}\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\in\mathcal{F}\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}[X_{i}X_{j}]

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{|\mathcal{F}|}+\Big(1-\frac{1}{|\mathcal{F}|}\Big)(\delta+\eta).

Similarly, we obtain that

(5.5)

\mathbb{E}[Z_{\mathcal{G}}^{2}]\leqslant\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|}+\Big(1-\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|}\Big)(\delta+\eta).

On the other hand, we have

(5.6)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[Z_{\mathcal{F}}Z_{\mathcal{G}}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{F}|\cdot|\mathcal{G}|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{F},j\in\mathcal{G}}\mathbb{E}[X_{i}X_{j}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{F}|\cdot|\mathcal{G}|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{G}}\mathbb{E}[X_{i}^{2}]+\frac{1}{|\mathcal{F}|\cdot|\mathcal{G}|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\mathcal{F},j\in\mathcal{G}\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}[X_{i}X_{j}]

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{|\mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{G}|}{|\mathcal{F}|\cdot|\mathcal{G}|}+\bigg(1-\frac{|\mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{G}|}{|\mathcal{F}|\cdot|\mathcal{G}|}\bigg)(\delta-\eta).

Finally, by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we have
|\delta|\leqslant 1
. Using this observation, the estimate (
5.3
)
follows from the identity

\|Z_{\mathcal{F}}-Z_{\mathcal{G}}\|_{L_{2}}^{2}=\mathbb{E}[Z_{\mathcal{F}}^{2}]+\mathbb{E}[Z_{\mathcal{G}}^{2}]-2\mathbb{E}[Z_{\mathcal{F}}Z_{\mathcal{G}}]

and inequalities (
5.4
)–(
5.6
).
∎

Proposition
5.2
will mostly be used in the following form; the proof follows immediately
from Proposition
5.2
and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality.

Corollary 5.3
.

Let

, let
\bm{X}=\langle X_{i}:i\in I\rangle
be an
\eta
-orbit, and let

be finite

(5.7)

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{1}{|\mathcal{F}|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{F}}\mathbb{E}[X_{i}Y]-\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{G}}\mathbb{E}[X_{i}Y]\bigg|\leqslant 2\,\bigg(\frac{1}{\min\{|\mathcal{F}|,|\mathcal{G}|\}}+\eta\bigg)^{1/2}.

In particular, if

6.
Comparing two-point correlations of spreadable random arrays

6.1.
Motivation

Let
n\geqslant 4
be an integer, and assume that
\bm{X}=\langle X_{s}:s\in\binom{[n]}{2}\rangle

is a real-valued, two-dimensional random array on
[n]
such that
\|X_{s}\|_{L_{2}}=1
for all
s\in\binom{[n]}{2}
.
We wish to compare the correlations

\alpha\coloneqq\mathbb{E}[X_{\{1,2\}}X_{\{3,4\}}]\ \ \ \text{ and }\ \ \ \beta\coloneqq\mathbb{E}[X_{\{1,3\}}X_{\{2,4\}}].

Of course, if
\bm{X}
is exchangeable, then
\alpha=\beta
. On the other hand, in
\bm{X}
is spreadable,
then Kallenberg’s representation theorem
[
Kal92
]
and an ultraproduct argument yield that
\alpha=\beta+o_{n\to\infty}(1)

but with an ineffective error term. We will see, however, that

(6.1)

|\alpha-\beta|\leqslant\frac{6}{\sqrt{n}}.

In other words, the symmetries of a finite, spreadable, high-dimensional random array with square-integrable entries,
impose explicit restrictions on its two-point correlations.

In order to see that (
6.1
) is satisfied, let
n\geqslant 10
be an integer (if
n\leqslant 9
, then
(
6.1
) is straightforward), let
\ell
be the largest positive integer such that
2\ell+3<n
,
and notice that
\ell\geqslant n/4
. Also observe that, by the spreadability of
\bm{X}
, for every
i\in\{2,\dots,\ell+1\}
we have

\beta=\mathbb{E}[X_{\{1,\ell+2\}}X_{\{i,n\}}]
, and on the other hand for every
j\in\{\ell+3,\dots,2\ell+3\}
we have

\alpha=\mathbb{E}[X_{\{1,\ell+2\}}X_{\{j,n\}}]
. Therefore, setting

Y\coloneqq(1/\ell)\,\sum_{i=2}^{\ell+1}X_{\{i,n\}}\ \ \ \text{ and }\ \ \ Z\coloneqq(1/\ell)\,\sum_{j=\ell+3}^{2\ell+3}X_{\{j,n\}},

we obtain that

(6.2)

\alpha=\mathbb{E}[X_{\{1,\ell+2\}}Z]\ \ \ \text{ and }\ \ \ \beta=\mathbb{E}[X_{\{1,\ell+2\}}Y].

The main observation, that follows readily from the spreadability of
\bm{X}
, is that the process

\langle X_{\{k,n\}}:k\in\{2,\dots,\ell+1\}\cup\{\ell+3,\dots,2\ell+3\}\rangle
is an orbit in the sense of
Definition
5.1
. This information together with (
6.2
) and Corollary
5.3

yield (
6.1
).

6.2.
​​

Our goal in this section is to study of the phenomenon outlined above and to characterize,
combinatorially, when two two-point correlations of a spreadable random array essentially coincide.
To this end, we need the following analogue of the notion of an aligned pair of partial maps
that was introduced in Paragraph
1.4.1
.

Definition 6.1

(Aligned pair of sets)
.

Let
d
be a positive integer, and let
s_{1},s_{2}\in\binom{\mathbb{N}}{d}
be distinct. We say that the
pair
\{s_{1},s_{2}\}
is
aligned
if there exists a proper
(
possibly empty
)
subset
G
of
[d]
such that:

(i)

\mathrm{I}_{s_{1}}\upharpoonright G=\mathrm{I}_{s_{2}}\upharpoonright G
, and

(ii)

\mathrm{I}_{s_{1}}\big([d]\setminus G\big)\cap\mathrm{I}_{s_{2}}\big([d]\setminus G\big)=\emptyset
.

(
Here,
\mathrm{I}_{s_{1}}
and
\mathrm{I}_{s_{2}}
denote the canonical isomorphisms associated with
the sets
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
; see Paragraph
1.4.2
.
)
We call the set
G

the
root of
\{s_{1},s_{2}\}
and we denote it by
r(s_{1},s_{2})
.

We have the following proposition.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
n,d
be positive integers with
n\geqslant 4d+2
, and let
s_{1},s_{2},t_{1},t_{2}\in\binom{[n]}{d}
with
s_{1}\neq s_{2}

and
t_{1}\neq t_{2}
. Assume that the pairs
\{s_{1},s_{2}\}
and
\{t_{1},t_{2}\}
are aligned and have the same root.
If
\bm{X}=\langle X_{s}:s\in\binom{[n]}{d}\rangle
is a real-valued, spreadable,
d
-dimensional random array on
[n]

such that
\|X_{s}\|_{L_{2}}=1
for all
s\in\binom{[n]}{d}
, then

(6.3)

\big|\mathbb{E}[X_{s_{1}}X_{s_{2}}]-\mathbb{E}[X_{t_{1}}X_{t_{2}}]\big|\leqslant\frac{8d^{2}}{\sqrt{n}}.

Remark 6.3
.

By considering spreadable, high-dimensional random arrays whose distribution
is of the form (
1.2
), it is not hard to see that the assumption in Proposition
6.2

(namely, the fact that the pairs
\{s_{1},s_{2}\}
and
\{t_{1},t_{2}\}
are aligned and have the same root)
is essentially optimal.

The proof of Proposition
6.2
is based on the following lemma.

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
n,d,s_{1},s_{2},t_{1},t_{2}
be as in Proposition
6.2
. Assume that the pairs
\{s_{1},s_{2}\}
and
\{t_{1},t_{2}\}

are aligned, and that there exists
i_{0}\in[d]
with the following properties.

(i)

We have
\mathrm{I}_{s_{1}}(i_{0})<\mathrm{I}_{s_{2}}(i_{0})
and

\mathrm{I}_{t_{2}}(i_{0})<\mathrm{I}_{t_{1}}(i_{0})
.

(ii)

We have

\mathrm{I}_{s_{1}}\upharpoonright([d]\setminus\{i_{0}\})=\mathrm{I}_{t_{1}}\upharpoonright([d]\setminus\{i_{0}\})

and
\mathrm{I}_{s_{2}}\upharpoonright([d]\setminus\{i_{0}\})=\mathrm{I}_{t_{2}}\upharpoonright([d]\setminus\{i_{0}\})
.

If
\bm{X}=\langle X_{s}:s\in\binom{[n]}{d}\rangle
is a real-valued, spreadable,

d
-dimensional random array on
[n]
such that
\|X_{s}\|_{L_{2}}=1
for all
s\in\binom{[n]}{d}
, then

(6.4)

\big|\mathbb{E}[X_{s_{1}}X_{s_{2}}]-\mathbb{E}[X_{t_{1}}X_{t_{2}}]\big|\leqslant\frac{4}{\sqrt{n}}.

Proof.

Since
\bm{X}
is spreadable, we may assume that there exist subintervals
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
of
[n]
with

|L_{1}|=|L_{2}|=\lfloor(n-2d+1)/2\rfloor
and satisfying the following properties.

(
\mathcal{P}
1)

We have
\max(L_{1})<\mathrm{I}_{s_{1}}(i_{0})<\min(L_{2})
.

(
\mathcal{P}
2)

If
i_{0}>1
, then
\mathrm{I}_{s_{1}}(i_{0}-1)<\min(L_{1})
and
\mathrm{I}_{s_{2}}(i_{0}-1)<\min(L_{1})
.

(
\mathcal{P}
3)

If
i_{0}<d
, then
\max(L_{2})<\mathrm{I}_{s_{1}}(i_{0}+1)
and
\max(L_{2})<\mathrm{I}_{s_{2}}(i_{0}+1)
.

Set
L\coloneqq L_{1}\cup L_{2}
and
\ell\coloneqq\lfloor(n-2d+1)/2\rfloor
; also set

g_{j}\coloneqq\big(s_{2}\setminus\{\mathrm{I}_{s_{2}}(i_{0})\}\big)\cup\{\mathrm{I}_{L}(j)\}\in\binom{[n]}{d}

for every
j\in[2\ell]
. Using the spreadability of
\bm{X}
again, we obtain that

(
\mathcal{P}
4)

\mathbb{E}[X_{s_{1}}X_{g_{j}}]=\mathbb{E}[X_{t_{1}}X_{t_{2}}]
for every
j\in[\ell]
, and

(
\mathcal{P}
5)

\mathbb{E}[X_{s_{1}}X_{g_{j}}]=\mathbb{E}[X_{s_{1}}X_{s_{2}}]
for every
j\in[2\ell]\setminus[\ell]
.

By the spreadability of
\bm{X}
once again, we see that the collection
\langle X_{g_{j}}:j\in[2\ell]\rangle
is an orbit
and, moreover,
\mathbb{E}[X_{s_{1}}X_{g_{i}}]=\mathbb{E}[X_{s_{1}}X_{g_{j}}]
if either
i,j\in[\ell]
, or
i,j\in[2\ell]\setminus[\ell]
.
The result follows using the previous remarks, Corollary
5.3
and the fact that
n\geqslant 4d+2
.
∎

We are ready to give the proof of Proposition
6.2
.

Proof of Proposition
6.2
.

Note that, by applying successively Lemma
6.4
at most
d^{2}
times, we obtain the following.

Let
\mathfrak{s}_{1},\mathfrak{s}_{2},\mathfrak{s}_{3},\mathfrak{s}_{4}\in\binom{[n]}{d}
with

\mathfrak{s}_{1}\neq\mathfrak{s}_{2}
and
\mathfrak{s}_{3}\neq\mathfrak{s}_{4}
. Assume that the pairs

\{\mathfrak{s}_{1},\mathfrak{s}_{2}\}
and
\{\mathfrak{s}_{3},\mathfrak{s}_{4}\}
are aligned and have the same root
G
.
Assume, moreover, that

(i)

\mathrm{I}_{\mathfrak{s}_{1}}\upharpoonright G=\mathrm{I}_{\mathfrak{s}_{3}}\upharpoonright G
,

(ii)

\mathrm{I}_{\mathfrak{s}_{2}}\upharpoonright G=\mathrm{I}_{\mathfrak{s}_{4}}\upharpoonright G
, and

(iii)

for every interval
I
of
[d]\setminus G
we have

\max\big(\mathrm{I}_{\mathfrak{s}_{3}}(I)\big)<\min\big(\mathrm{I}_{\mathfrak{s}_{4}}(I)\big)
.

Then we have
\big|\mathbb{E}[X_{\mathfrak{s}_{1}}X_{\mathfrak{s}_{2}}]-\mathbb{E}[X_{\mathfrak{s}_{3}}X_{\mathfrak{s}_{4}}]\big|\leqslant 4d^{2}/\sqrt{n}
.

The estimate (
6.3
) follows using this observation, the triangle inequality and the spreadability
of the random array
\bm{X}
.
∎

d\geqslant 2
;
we say that a subset of
\binom{[n]}{d}
is a
d
-dimensional box of
[n]
if it is of the form

(8.1)

\bigg\{s\in\binom{[n]}{d}:|s\cap H_{i}|=1\text{ for all }i\in[d]\bigg\},

where
H_{1},\dots,H_{d}
are
2
-element subsets of
[n]
that satisfy
\max(H_{i})<\min(H_{i+1})
for every
i\in[d-1]
.

Definition 8.2

(Box independence condition)
.

Let
n,d
be integers with
n\geqslant 2d
and
d\geqslant 2
, let
\mathcal{X}
be a finite set with
|\mathcal{X}|\geqslant 2
,
and let
\bm{X}=\langle X_{s}:s\in\binom{[n]}{d}\rangle
be a
d\text{-dimensional}
random array on
[n]

with
\mathcal{X}
-valued entries. Also let
\vartheta\geqslant 0
. We say that
\bm{X}
satisfies the

\vartheta
-box independence condition
if there exists a subset
\mathcal{S}
of
\mathcal{X}

with
|\mathcal{S}|=|\mathcal{X}|-1
such that for every
d
-dimensional box
B
of
[n]

and every
a\in\mathcal{S}
we have

(8.2)

\bigg|\mathbb{P}\Big(\bigcap_{s\in B}[X_{s}=a]\Big)-\prod_{s\in B}\mathbb{P}\big([X_{s}=a]\big)\bigg|\leqslant\vartheta.

Thus, for instance, if “
d=2
” and “
\mathcal{X}=\{0,1\}
”, then the
\vartheta
-box independence condition is equivalent
to saying that for every
i,j,k,\ell\in[n]
with
i<j<k<\ell
we have

(8.3)

\Big|\mathbb{E}[X_{\{i,k\}}X_{\{i,\ell\}}X_{\{j,k\}}X_{\{j,\ell\}}]-\mathbb{E}[X_{\{i,k\}}]\,\mathbb{E}[X_{\{i,\ell\}}]\,\mathbb{E}[X_{\{j,k\}}]\,\mathbb{E}[X_{\{j,\ell\}}]\Big|\leqslant\vartheta.

8.1.2.
​​

The main result in this section—Proposition
8.3
below—is a characterization of the box independence condition
in terms of the distributional decomposition obtained in Theorem
1.4
; as will become clear in the ensuing discussion,
the main advantage of this characterization lies in the fact that that it enables us to employ further analytical
and combinatorial tools in the broader context of Problem
8.1
. In a nutshell, it asserts that a random
array
\bm{X}
satisfies condition (
8.2
) if and only if its distribution is close to a distribution
of the form (
1.3
), where for “almost every”
j\in J
and every
a\in\mathcal{X}
the random variable
h^{a}_{j}
is

box uniform
and its average
\mathbb{E}[h^{a}_{j}]
is roughly equal to the expected value
\mathbb{P}\big([X_{[d]}=a]\big)
.

8.1.3.
Box uniformity

The aforementioned box uniformity is a well-known pseudorandomness property—see,
e.g.
,
[
Rő15
]
—that
is defined using the box norms. Specifically, let
d\geqslant 2
be an integer, let
(\Omega,\Sigma,\mu)
be a probability
space, and let
\Omega^{d}
be equipped with the product measure. Also let
\varrho>0
. We say that an integrable random
variable
h\colon\Omega^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is
\varrho
-box uniform
provided that

(8.4)

\big\|h-\mathbb{E}[h]\big\|_{\square}\leqslant\varrho,

where
\|\cdot\|_{\square}
denotes the corresponding box norm (see Subsection
3.1
).

8.2.
The characterization

We have the following proposition.

Proposition 8.3
.

Let
d,m\geqslant 2
be integers, and let
0<\varepsilon\leqslant 1
. Let
C=C(d,m,2d,\varepsilon)
be as in (
1.4
),
let
n,\mathcal{X},\bm{X}
be as in Theorem
1.4
, and set
\delta_{a}\coloneqq\mathbb{P}\big([X_{[d]}=a]\big)

for every
\alpha\in\mathcal{X}
. Finally, let
J,\Omega,\bm{\lambda}=\langle\lambda_{j}:j\in J\rangle
and

\bm{\mathcal{H}}=\langle h^{a}_{j}:j\in J,a\in\mathcal{X}\rangle
be as in Theorem
1.4

when applied to the random array
\bm{X}
for the parameters
d,m,\varepsilon
and
k=2d
. Then the following hold.

(i)

Let
\varrho>0
, and set

(8.5)

\vartheta=\vartheta(d,\varepsilon,\varrho)\coloneqq 2^{d}(2\varepsilon+4\varrho).

Assume that there is a subset
G
of
J
such that:
(a)

\sum_{j\in G}\lambda_{j}\geqslant 1-\varrho
,
and
(b)
for every
j\in G
and every
a\in\mathcal{X}
we have
|\mathbb{E}[h^{a}_{j}]-\delta_{a}|\leqslant\varrho

and
\big\|h^{a}_{j}-\mathbb{E}[h^{a}_{j}]\big\|_{\square}\leqslant\varrho
. Then,
\bm{X}
is
\vartheta
-box independent.

(ii)

Conversely, let
0<\vartheta\leqslant 1
, and set

(8.6)

\varrho=\varrho(d,m,\varepsilon,\vartheta)\coloneqq 4m\big(\varepsilon^{1/16^{d}}+\vartheta^{1/16^{d}}\big).

Assume that
\bm{X}
is
\vartheta
-box independent. Then there exists a subset
G
of
J
such
that:
(a)

\sum_{j\in G}\lambda_{j}\geqslant 1-\varrho
, and
(b)
for every
j\in G

and every
a\in\mathcal{X}
we have
|\mathbb{E}[h^{a}_{j}]-\delta_{a}|\leqslant\varrho

and
\big\|h^{a}_{j}-\mathbb{E}[h^{a}_{j}]\big\|_{\square}\leqslant\varrho
.

Proof.

First we argue for part (i). Let
B
be a
d
-dimensional box of
[n]
, and fix
a\in\mathcal{X}
.
By (
1.3
), (
1.5
) and part (a) of our assumptions, we have

(8.7)

\bigg|\mathbb{P}\Big(\bigcap_{s\in B}[X_{s}=a]\Big)-\sum_{j\in G}\lambda_{j}\int\prod_{s\in B}h^{a}_{j}(\bm{\omega}_{s})\,d\bm{\mu}_{j}(\bm{\omega})\bigg|\leqslant\varepsilon+\varrho.

Let
j\in G
be arbitrary, and observe that
\|h^{a}_{j}\|_{\square}\leqslant 1
. By the
\varrho
-box uniformity of
h^{a}_{j}
,
the Gowers–Cauchy–Schwarz inequality (
3.6
) and a telescopic argument, we see that

(8.8)

\bigg|\int\prod_{s\in B}h^{a}_{j}(\bm{\omega}_{s})\,d\bm{\mu}_{j}(\bm{\omega})-\prod_{s\in B}\mathbb{E}[h^{a}_{j}]\bigg|\leqslant 2^{d}\varrho,

and so, using the fact
|\mathbb{E}[h^{a}_{j}]-\delta_{a}|\leqslant\varrho
, we obtain that

(8.9)

\bigg|\int\prod_{s\in B}h^{a}_{j}(\bm{\omega}_{s})\,d\bm{\mu}_{j}(\bm{\omega})-\delta^{2^{d}}_{a}\bigg|\leqslant 2^{d+1}\varrho.

On the other hand, since
\bm{X}
is
(1/C)
-spreadable, we have

(8.10)

\bigg|\delta^{2^{d}}_{a}-\prod_{s\in B}\mathbb{P}\big([X_{s}=a]\big)\bigg|\leqslant\frac{2^{d}}{C}.

By (
8.7
)–(
8.10
), assumption (a), the fact that
1/C\leqslant\varepsilon

and the choice of
\vartheta
in (
8.5
), we conclude that

\bigg|\mathbb{P}\Big(\bigcap_{s\in B}[X_{s}=a]\Big)-\prod_{s\in B}\mathbb{P}\big([X_{s}=a]\big)\bigg|\leqslant\vartheta

that yields that the random array
\bm{X}
is
\vartheta
-box independent.

We proceed to the proof of part (ii). We will need the following fact that follows from

[
DTV23
, Theorem 3.2 and Lemma 3.6]
and the fact that
n\geqslant C\geqslant\varepsilon^{-1}
.
It shows that the box independence condition is inherited to subsets of
d
-dimensional boxes.

Fact 8.4
.

Let the notation and assumptions be as in part
(ii)
of Proposition
8.3
, and set

(8.11)

\Theta\coloneqq 36\cdot 2^{d}\cdot m^{2^{d}}\cdot\big(2\varepsilon^{1/4^{d}}+\vartheta^{1/4^{d}}\big).

Then for every
d
-dimensional box
B
of
[n]
, every nonempty subset
F
of
B
and every
a\in\mathcal{X}
we have

(8.12)

\bigg|\mathbb{P}\Big(\bigcap_{s\in F}[X_{s}=a]\Big)-\prod_{s\in F}\mathbb{P}\big([X_{s}=a]\big)\bigg|\leqslant\Theta.

Now, fix
a\in\mathcal{X}
, and set
s_{1}\coloneqq\{2i-1:i\in[d]\}\in\binom{[n]}{d}
and

s_{2}\coloneqq\{2i:i\in[d]\}\in\binom{[n]}{d}
. Since
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
are disjoint,
by (
1.3
) and (
1.5
), we have

(8.13)

\displaystyle\bigg|\delta_{a}-\sum_{j\in J}\lambda_{j}\mathbb{E}[h^{a}_{j}]\bigg|\leqslant\varepsilon,

(8.14)

\displaystyle\bigg|\mathbb{P}\big([X_{s_{1}}=

\displaystyle a]\cap[X_{s_{2}}=a]\big)-\sum_{j\in J}\lambda_{j}\mathbb{E}[h^{a}_{j}]^{2}\bigg|\leqslant\varepsilon.

By Fact
8.4
, the
(1/C)
-spreadability of
\bm{X}
and (
8.14
), we see that

(8.15)

\bigg|\delta_{a}^{2}-\sum_{j\in J}\lambda_{j}\mathbb{E}[h^{a}_{j}]^{2}\bigg|\leqslant\varepsilon+\Theta+\frac{2}{C}.

Thus, setting

(8.16)

\varrho_{1}\coloneqq\sqrt[3]{m(4\varepsilon+\Theta)},

by (
8.13
), (
8.15
), the fact that
2/C\leqslant\varepsilon
, Chebyshev’s inequality
and a union bound, we obtain a subset
G_{1}
of
J
such that
\sum_{j\in G_{1}}\lambda_{j}\geqslant 1-\varrho_{1}

and
|\mathbb{E}[h^{a}_{j}]-\delta_{a}|\leqslant\varrho_{1}
for every
j\in G_{1}
and every
a\in\mathcal{X}
.

Again, let
a\in\mathcal{X}
be arbitrary. We shall estimate the quantity

(8.17)

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in G_{1}}\lambda_{j}\big\|h^{a}_{j}-\mathbb{E}[h^{a}_{j}]\big\|_{\square}^{2^{d}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{e3.5}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\sum_{H\subseteq\{0,1\}^{d}}(-1)^{2^{d}-|H|}\,\times

\displaystyle\ \ \ \times\Big(\sum_{j\in G_{1}}\lambda_{j}\,\mathbb{E}[h^{a}_{j}]^{2^{d}-|H|}\int\prod_{\bm{\epsilon}\in H}h^{a}_{j}(\bm{\omega}_{\bm{\epsilon}})\,d\bm{\mu}(\bm{\omega})\Big).

(Here, as in Section
2
, we use the convention that the product of an empty family of functions is equal
to the constant function
1
.) To this end, let
H
be an arbitrary subset of
\{0,1\}^{d}
.
Notice first that, by the choice of
G_{1}
, we have

(8.18)

\displaystyle\bigg|\sum_{j\in G_{1}}\lambda_{j}\mathbb{E}[h^{a}_{j}]^{2^{d}-|H|}\int

\displaystyle\prod_{\bm{\epsilon}\in H}h^{a}_{j}(\bm{\omega}_{\bm{\epsilon}})\,d\bm{\mu}(\bm{\omega})-

\displaystyle-\delta_{a}^{2^{d}-|H|}\sum_{j\in J}\lambda_{j}\int\prod_{\bm{\epsilon}\in H}h^{a}_{j}(\bm{\omega}_{\bm{\epsilon}})\,d\bm{\mu}(\bm{\omega})\bigg|\leqslant 2\varrho_{1}.

Next note that if
H
is nonempty, then, by (
1.3
) and (
1.5
), we may select a
d\mathrm{-dimensional}

box
B
of
[n]
and a nonempty subset
F
of
B
with
|F|=|H|
, such that

(8.19)

\displaystyle\bigg|\mathbb{P}\bigg(\bigcap_{s\in F}[X_{s}=a]\Big)-\sum_{j\in J}\lambda_{j}\int\prod_{\bm{\epsilon}\in H}h^{a}_{j}(\bm{\omega}_{\bm{\epsilon}})\,d\bm{\mu}(\bm{\omega})\bigg|\leqslant\varepsilon.

Thus, by Fact
8.4
, the
(1/C)
-spreadability of
\bm{X}
and the fact that
|F|=|H|
, we have

(8.20)

\displaystyle\bigg|\delta_{a}^{|H|}-\sum_{j\in J}\lambda_{j}\int\prod_{\bm{\epsilon}\in H}h^{a}_{j}(\bm{\omega}_{\bm{\epsilon}})\,d\bm{\mu}(\bm{\omega})\bigg|\leqslant\varepsilon+\Theta+\frac{2^{d}}{C}.

By (
8.18
) and (
8.20
), we see that for every (possibly empty) subset
H
of
\{0,1\}^{d}
,

(8.21)

\displaystyle\bigg|\sum_{j\in G_{1}}\lambda_{j}\mathbb{E}[h^{a}_{j}]^{2^{d}-|H|}\int\prod_{\bm{\epsilon}\in H}h^{a}_{j}(\bm{\omega}_{\bm{\epsilon}})\,d\bm{\mu}(\bm{\omega})-\delta_{a}^{2^{d}}\bigg|\leqslant\varepsilon+\Theta+\frac{2^{d}}{C}+2\varrho_{1}.

By (
8.17
) and (
8.21
), we conclude that for every
a\in\mathcal{X}
we have

(8.22)

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in G_{1}}\lambda_{j}\big\|h^{a}_{j}-\mathbb{E}[h^{a}_{j}]\big\|_{\square}^{2^{d}}\leqslant 2^{d}\Big(\varepsilon+\Theta+\frac{2^{d}}{C}+2\varrho_{1}\Big).

Using the fact that
2^{d}/C\leqslant\varepsilon
, (
8.22
),
the choice of
\varrho
,
\Theta
and
\varrho_{1}
in (
8.6
), (
8.11
) and (
8.16
) respectively,
Markov’s inequality and a union bound, we may select a subset
G
of
G_{1}
with
\sum_{j\in G}\lambda_{j}\geqslant 1-\varrho

and such that
\big\|h^{a}_{j}-\mathbb{E}[h^{a}_{j}]\big\|_{\square}\leqslant\varrho
for every
j\in G
and every
a\in\mathcal{X}
.
Since
G\subseteq G_{1}
and
\varrho_{1}\leqslant\frac{\varrho}{2}<\varrho
, we see that
G
is as desired.
The proof of Proposition
8.3
is completed.

Appendix A
Proof of Lemma

Let
d,m,\varepsilon
be as in the statement of Lemma
3.4
, and let
n_{0}
be as in (
3.8
).
Fix coefficients
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{m}\geqslant 0
with
\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{m}=1
,
and let
V
be a finite set with
|V|\geqslant n_{0}
. Observe that, by the choice of
n_{0}
, we have

(A.1)

\bigg(\frac{1}{|V|^{d/3}}+\frac{(1+d!\,m)2d^{2}}{|V|}\bigg)^{1/2^{d}}\leqslant\varepsilon\ \ \ \text{ and }\ \ \ 1-2m\exp\bigg(-\frac{2}{d!\,4^{d}}\,|V|^{d/3}\bigg)>0.

We define an equivalence relation
\sim
on
V^{d}
by setting

(A.2)

\displaystyle(v_{1},\dots,v_{d})\sim(v^{\prime}_{1},\dots,v^{\prime}_{d})\Leftrightarrow

there exists a permutation

\pi
of
[d]

\displaystyle\text{ such that }v^{\prime}_{i}=v_{\pi(i)}\text{ for all }i\in[d],

and for every
e\in V^{d}
by
[e]\coloneqq\{e^{\prime}\in V^{d}:e^{\prime}\sim e\}
we denote the
\sim

e
.
We also set
\mathrm{Sym}(V^{d})\coloneqq V^{d}/\sim
.

Next, we fix a collection
\bm{X}=\langle X_{\mathbf{e}}:\mathbf{e}\in\mathrm{Sym}(V^{d})\rangle

of
[m]\text{-valued}
, independent random variables defined on some probability space
(\Omega,\Sigma,\mathbb{P})

that satisfy
\mathbb{P}\big([X_{\mathbf{e}}=j]\big)=\lambda_{j}
for every
\mathbf{e}\in\mathrm{Sym}(V^{d})

and every
j\in[m]
. Moreover, for every
j\in[m]
we define

by setting for every
\bm{x}=(x_{\mathbf{e}})_{\mathbf{e}\in\mathrm{Sym}(V^{d})}\in[m]^{\mathrm{Sym}(V^{d})}
,

(A.3)

\bm{v}=(v_{1}^{0},v^{1}_{1},\dots,v_{d}^{0},v^{1}_{d})\in V^{2d}
and every

\bm{v}_{\bm{\epsilon}}=(v_{1}^{\epsilon_{1}},\dots,v_{d}^{\epsilon_{d}})\in V^{d}
.
Let
\mathcal{I}
denote the subset of

consisting of all strings with distinct entries.
Observe that for every
\bm{x}=(x_{\mathbf{e}})_{\mathbf{e}\in\mathrm{Sym}(V^{d})}\in[m]^{\mathrm{Sym}(V^{d})}

and every

we have

(A.4)

\displaystyle f_{j}(\bm{x})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{e3.5}}}{{=}}\frac{1}{|V|^{2d}}\sum_{\bm{v}\in V^{2d}}\prod_{\bm{\epsilon}\in\{0,1\}^{d}}(\mathbf{1}_{\{j\}}(x_{[\bm{v}_{\bm{\epsilon}}]})-\lambda_{j})

\displaystyle\ \,=\frac{1}{|V|^{2d}}\sum_{\bm{v}\in\mathcal{I}}\ \sum_{H\subseteq\{0,1\}^{d}}(-\lambda_{j})^{2^{d}-|H|}\prod_{\bm{\epsilon}\in H}\mathbf{1}_{\{j\}}(x_{[\bm{v}_{\bm{\epsilon}}]})\ +

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ +\ \frac{1}{|V|^{2d}}\sum_{\bm{v}\in V^{2d}\setminus\mathcal{I}}\ \prod_{\bm{\epsilon}\in\{0,1\}^{d}}\big(\mathbf{1}_{\{j\}}(x_{[\bm{v}_{\bm{\epsilon}}]})-\lambda_{j}\big)

and

(A.5)

\bigg|\frac{1}{|V|^{2d}}\sum_{\bm{v}\in V^{2d}\setminus\mathcal{I}}\ \prod_{\bm{\epsilon}\in\{0,1\}^{d}}(\mathbf{1}_{\{j\}}(x_{[\bm{v}_{\bm{\epsilon}}]})-\lambda_{j})\bigg|\leqslant\frac{|V^{2d}\setminus\mathcal{I}|}{|V|^{2d}}\leqslant\frac{2d^{2}}{|V|}.

\mathcal{I}

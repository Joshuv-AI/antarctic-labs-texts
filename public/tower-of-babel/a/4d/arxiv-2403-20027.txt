Abstract
Yager [ 1 ] proposed a transformation for opposing(negating) the occurence of an event that is not certain using the idea that one can oppose the occurence of any uncertain event by allocating its probability among the other outcomes in the sample space without preference to any particular outcome i.e. the probability of every event in the sample space is redistributed equally among the other outcomes in the sample space. However this redistribution increases the uncertainty associated with the occurence of events. In the present work, we have established bounds on the uncertainty associated with negation of a probability distribution using well known Jensen inequality. The obtained results are validated with the help of various numerical examples. Finally a dissimilarity function between a probability distribution and its negation has been developed.

+\left(\frac{n-2}{n-1}\right)\left(\frac{n-3}{n-2}\right)f\left(\zeta_{n-3}\left({{p}_{1},{p}_{2},...,{p}_{i-1},{p}_{i+1},...,p_{n-2}}\right)\right)

=\left(\frac{1}{n-1}\right)f\left({p}_{n}\right)+\left(\frac{1}{n-1}\right)f\left({p}_{n-1}\right)

+\left(\frac{n-3}{n-1}\right)f\left(\frac{{{p}_{1}+{p}_{2}+...+{p}_{i-1}+{p}_{i+1}+...+p_{n-2}}}{n-3}\right)

...

\leq\left(\frac{1}{n-1}\right)f\left({p}_{n}\right)+\left(\frac{1}{n-1}\right)f\left({p}_{n-1}\right)+\left(\frac{1}{n-1}\right)f\left({p}_{n-2}\right)+...+\left(\frac{1}{n-1}\right)f\left({p}_{i-1}\right)

+\left(\frac{1}{n-1}\right)f\left({p}_{i+1}\right)...+\left(\frac{1}{n-1}\right)f\left({p}_{2}\right)+\left(\frac{1}{n-1}\right)f\left({p}_{1}\right)

which completes the proof. Using (2.8), for
i=1,2,\ldots,n

(2.9)

Taking
f(x)=-{\rm log\;}x=\Delta(x)
(say) in (2.9), we obtain

\left(-{\rm log\;}\overline{p}_{i}\right)\leq\left(\frac{1}{n-1}\right)\sum\limits_{j=1,i\neq j}^{n}\left(-{\rm log\;}{p}_{i}\right)

(2.10)

Equality holds in above when all the probabilties in

Quantifying the dissimilarity between a probability distribution and its negation

From (1.7), it is clear dissimilarity between a probability distribution and its negation signifies the dissimilarity between the probability distribution and the uniform distribution which is the maximum entropy probability distribution in the absence of any additional information regarding the occurence of events. So quantifying dissimilarity between the probability distribution is important from the information theoretic point of view
[

]
,
[

. We start with following theorem.

Theorem 3.1.
For all
P(n)=\{p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{n}\}
and
\overline{P}(n)=\{\overline{p}_{1},\overline{p}_{2},\ldots,\overline{p}_{n}\}
, the function defined by

I_{\alpha}(P(n);\overline{P}(n))=-log\left(\frac{1+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(min\left(p_{i},\frac{\left(2^{\alpha}-1\right)p_{i}+q_{i}}{2^{\alpha}}\right)+min\left(\frac{p_{i}+\left(2^{\alpha}-1\right)q_{i}}{2^{\alpha}},q_{i}\right)\right)}{2}\right)\,\,\,;\alpha=0,1,2,3,...

(3.1)

represents a series of dissimilarity functions between
P(n)
and
\overline{P}(n)
satisfying the following properties

M1.

0\leq I_{\alpha}(P(n);\overline{P}(n))\leq 1
. Further
{\ I}_{\alpha}\left(P(n);\overline{P}(n)\right)=0
if and only if
P(n)=\overline{P}(n)
.

M2.
I_{\alpha}(P(n);\overline{P}(n))=\,\,\,I_{\alpha}(\overline{P}(n);P(n))

M3.
I_{0}((P(n);\overline{P}(n))\leq I_{1}(P(n);\overline{P}(n))\leq I_{2}(P(n);\overline{P}(n))\leq...\leq I_{\alpha}(P(n);\overline{P}(n))\leq...\forall i

Proof.
For all
P(n)=\{p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{n}\}
and
\overline{P}(n)=\{\overline{p}_{1},\overline{p}_{2},\ldots,\overline{p}_{n}\}
, we have

min\left(p_{i},\overline{p}_{i}\right)\leq\left(\frac{p_{i}+\overline{p}_{i}}{2}\right)\forall i\Rightarrow 0\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}min\left(p_{i},\overline{p}_{i}\right)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{p_{i}+\overline{p}_{i}}{2}\right)=1

\Rightarrow\frac{1}{2}\leq\left(\frac{1+\sum_{i=1}^{n}min\left(p_{i},\overline{p}_{i}\right)}{2}\right)\leq 1\Rightarrow 0\leq-log\left(\frac{1+\sum_{i=1}^{n}min\left(p_{i},\overline{p}_{i}\right)}{2}\right)\leq 1

\Rightarrow 0\leq I_{0}(P(n);\overline{P}(n))\leq 1

Again for all
P(n)=\{p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{n}\}
and
\overline{P}(n)=\{\overline{p}_{1},\overline{p}_{2},\ldots,\overline{p}_{n}\}
, we have

min\left(p_{i},\left(\frac{p_{i}+\overline{p}_{i}}{2}\right)\right)\leq\frac{p_{i}+\left(\frac{p_{i}+\overline{p}_{i}}{2}\right)}{2}
and
min\left(\left(\frac{p_{i}+\overline{p}_{i}}{2}\right),\overline{p}_{i}\right)\leq\frac{\left(\frac{p_{i}+\overline{p}_{i}}{2}\right)+\overline{p}_{i}}{2}\forall i

\Rightarrow 0\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}min\left(p_{i},\left(\frac{p_{i}+\overline{p}_{i}}{2}\right)\right)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{p_{i}+\left(\frac{p_{i}+\overline{p}_{i}}{2}\right)}{2}\right)=1
and

0\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}min\left(\left(\frac{p_{i}+\overline{p}_{i}}{2}\right),\overline{p}_{i}\right)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{\left(\frac{p_{i}+\overline{p}_{i}}{2}\right)+\overline{p}_{i}}{2}\right)=1

\Rightarrow 0\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}min\left(p_{i},\left(\frac{p_{i}+\overline{p}_{i}}{2}\right)\right)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}min\left(\left(\frac{p_{i}+\overline{p}_{i}}{2}\right),\overline{p}_{i}\right)\right)\leq 1

\Rightarrow 0\leq-log\left(\frac{1+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(min\left(p_{i},\left(\frac{p_{i}+\overline{p}_{i}}{2}\right)\right)+min\left(\left(\frac{p_{i}+\overline{p}_{i}}{2}\right),\overline{p}_{i}\right)\right)}{2}\right)\leq 1

\Rightarrow 0\leq I_{1}(P(n);\overline{P}(n))\leq 1

Again, a choice of pairs
\left({p}_{i},\frac{3p_{i}+q_{i}}{4}\right)
and
\left({\frac{p_{i}+3\overline{p}_{i}}{4},\overline{p}_{i}}\right)
will give

\Rightarrow 0\leq-log\left(\frac{1+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(min\left(p_{i},\left(\frac{3p_{i}+\overline{p}_{i}}{4}\right)\right)+min\left(\left(\frac{p_{i}+3\overline{p}_{i}}{4}\right),\overline{p}_{i}\right)\right)}{2}\right)\leq 1

\Rightarrow 0\leq I_{2}(P(n);\overline{P}(n))\leq 1

Further a choice of pairs
\left(p_{i},\frac{{7p}_{i}+\overline{p}_{i}}{8}\right)
and
\left({\frac{p_{i}+7\overline{p}_{i}}{8},\overline{p}_{i}}\right)

\Rightarrow 0\leq-log\left(\frac{1+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(min\left(p_{i},\left(\frac{7p_{i}+\overline{p}_{i}}{8}\right)\right)+min\left(\left(\frac{p_{i}+7\overline{p}_{i}}{8}\right),\overline{p}_{i}\right)\right)}{2}\right)\leq 1

\Rightarrow 0\leq I_{3}(P(n);\overline{P}(n))\leq 1

Finally a choice of pairs

\Rightarrow 0\leq-log\left(\frac{1+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(min\left(p_{i},\left(\frac{\left(2^{\alpha}-1\right)p_{i}+\overline{p}_{i}}{2^{\alpha}}\right)\right)+min\left(\left(\frac{p_{i}+\left(2^{\alpha}-1\right)\overline{p}_{i}}{2^{\alpha}}\right),\overline{p}_{i}\right)\right)}{2}\right)\leq 1

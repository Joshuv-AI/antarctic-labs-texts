Abstract
. We study the stability of standing-waves solutions to a scalar non-linear Klein-Gordon equation in dimension one with a quadratic-cubic non-linearity. Orbits are obtained by applying the semigroup generated by the negative complex unit multiplication on a critical point of the energy constrained to the charge.

Primary 35Q55, 47J35

Introduction

This work aims to classify the stability and instability of standing-wave
solutions to the non-linear Klein-Gordon equation which can be written in the form

(1)

\frac{du}{dt}=JE^{\prime}(u(t)),

where
E
is functional defined on
X_{r}:=H^{1}_{r}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{C})\times L^{2}_{r}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{C})
, the space of complex-valued functions which are
radially symmetric with respect to the origin, and
J
is an unbounded linear
transformation
J\colon X_{r}^{*}\to X_{r}
. We will apply the abstract method devised
in
[
15
,
16
]
, and in
[
30
,
31
]
. We define the set

(2)

Orb(u_{\omega}):=\{T(s\omega)u_{\omega}\mid s\in\mathbb{R}\}

where
T(s)
is a continous semi-group of operators acting on
X_{r}
.
The equation to which we would like to apply this method is the
quadratic-cubic one-dimensional non-linear Klein-Gordon equation

(NLKG)

(\partial_{tt}^{2}-\partial_{xx}^{2}+m^{2})\phi-3a|\phi|\phi+4b|\phi|^{2}\phi=0,

where
m,a
and
b
are positive real numbers.
A standing-wave is a solution to (
NLKG
) which can be written as

\phi(t,x):=e^{-i\omega t}R(x),\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}

where
\omega
is a real number and
R
is a real-valued function of class

H^{1}_{r}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})
. Dispersive equations with competing powers non-linearities have been proposed for several applications. In
[
4
]
a one-dimensional cubic-quintic non-linear Schrödinger equation arises from boson gas interaction. The non-linear Klein-Gordon equation models the field equation for
spin-0 particles,
[
20
, §2]
; for a cubic-quintic non-linear Klein-Gordon
equation,
[
30
]
proved that there are stable and unstable standing-waves when
\omega
gets close to

13/16
and
1
, respectively, a result anticipated by the numerical inspection
in
[
3
]
. We also quote the work of G. Rosen,
[
29
]
, for a quintic non-linearity in
dimension three.

Standing-waves can be obtained as
minima of the energy functional

\begin{split}{E}(\phi,\psi):&=\frac{1}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}|\psi(x)|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}|\phi^{\prime}(x)|^{2}dx\\
&+\frac{m^{2}}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}|\phi(x)|^{2}dx+\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}G(|\phi(x)|)dx\end{split}

on the constraint

(3)

\begin{split}Q(\phi,\psi):=-\mathrm{Im}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\psi(x)\overline{\phi(x)}dx=(\phi,i\psi)_{2},\end{split}

which is the method followed in
[
7
]
. However, existence of standing-waves will be proved by solving an initial value problem, using the approach
devised in
[
17
,
25
,
11
]
and properties of one-dimensional elliptic equations proved in
[
9
]
. In this way, we will be able to discuss
the stability of all the standing-waves, not just the ones arising as minima
of
E
constrained on
Q
.
To prove the stability of the set
(
2
), we will check Assumptions 1, 2 and 3 presented in the introduction
of
[
15
]
, construct a smooth one-parameter family
u_{\omega}
of solutions to the differential equation

E^{\prime}(u_{\omega})=\omega Q^{\prime}(u_{\omega})

and study the convexity of the function

d(u_{\omega}):=E(u_{\omega})-\omega Q(u_{\omega}).

The conclusions of the first three chapters hold as long as
G
is
C^{3}
.
Considering a quadratic-cubic non-linearity, as we will do in Chapter
4
,
allows to find an exact form for
d^{\prime\prime}(\omega)
.
We introduce the following real-valued function defined on
(0,+\infty)^{3}

\tau(a,b,m):=\frac{2m^{2}b}{a^{2}}

which is convenient to classify the stability of all the standing-waves
for every

and non-linearity
-as^{2}+bs^{3}

Theorem 1

(Stability and instability)
.

There exist

(i).

(ii).

is stable for

\omega\in(\omega_{*},\omega_{1}(\tau))\cup(\omega_{2}(\tau),m)

and unstable for

(iii).

1.
Preliminary notations

In this section, we write the non-linear Klein-Gordon equation in the Hamiltonian
form (
1
) and check that the assumptions made in the introduction of

[
15
]
are verified. We follow a similar scheme to
[
15
, Example A, §6]
, where the authors proved that traveling waves are not stable.
We set

(4)

G(s):=-as^{3}+bs^{4},\quad F(s)=\frac{1}{2}m^{2}s^{2}+G(s)

for every
s\geq 0
.
Given
u_{1}=(\phi_{1},\psi_{1})
and

u_{2}=(\phi_{2},\psi_{2})
in
X_{r}
we define the
inner product

(u_{1},u_{2})_{X_{r}}:=m^{2}(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})_{2}+(\phi_{1}^{\prime},\phi_{2}^{\prime})_{2}+(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})_{2}

where

(f,g)_{2}:=\text{Re}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}f(x)\overline{g(x)}dx.

We use the notation

(5)

I\colon X_{r}\to X_{r}^{*}

for the Riesz isomorphism between real Hilbert spaces.
We use
\langle\xi,u\rangle
to denote
\xi(u)
for every
\xi\in X_{r}^{*}
and
u\in X_{r}
. We denote by

\mathscr{L}(X,Y)
and
\mathscr{L}_{c}(X,Y)
the space of bounded and compact operators between two Hilbert spaces
X
and
Y
, and
by
GL(X)
the group of invertible bounded operators on
X
. By
C^{k}_{0}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C})
we denote the space of continuously differentiable (to the order
k
) functions
with derivative vanishing at infinity up to the order
k
. We will also use the notation
\mathscr{R}(\zeta)
for the
resolvent of
-\Delta\colon H^{2}\subseteq L^{2}\to L^{2}
, for
every
\zeta
in the complement of the spectrum of
-\Delta
.

Let
I_{0}\colon L^{2}_{r}\times L^{2}_{r}\to X_{r}^{*}
be the bounded, injective linear transformation defined as

(6)

\langle I_{0}(u),(h,k)\rangle=(\phi,h)_{2}+(\psi,k)_{2}.

for every
u:=(\phi,\psi)\in X_{r}
and
(h,k)\in X_{r}
. In the rest of
this section, we introduce notations for operators and semigroups of operators
necessary to the abstract framework devised in
[
15
]
, and check that they satisfy assumptions required therein.

The operator
J

Let
J\colon D(J)\subseteq X_{r}^{*}\to X_{r}
be the unbounded operator with
dense domain
D(J):=I_{0}(L^{2}_{r}\times H^{1}_{r})

such that

JI_{0}(\psi,\phi)=(\phi,-\psi).

Proposition 1
.

J
is closed, onto and skew-adjoint. That is,
\langle\xi,J\eta\rangle=-\langle\eta,J\xi\rangle
for every
\xi,\eta\in D(J)\subseteq X_{r}^{*}
.

Proof.

Let
(\xi_{n},J\xi_{n})
be a sequence converging to
(\xi,u)\in X_{r}^{*}\times X_{r}
.
Set
u:=(\alpha,\beta)
. There exists a sequence
(\psi_{n},\phi_{n})\in L^{2}_{r}\times H^{1}_{r}
such that

I_{0}(\psi_{n},\phi_{n})=\xi_{n}
and

\lim_{n\to\infty}(\phi_{n},-\psi_{n})=(\alpha,\beta)
. For every
(h,k)\in X_{r}
we have
(\psi_{n},h)_{2}+(\phi_{n},k)_{2}=\langle\xi_{n},(h,k)\rangle
.
Taking the limit, we obtain
\xi=I_{0}(-\beta,\alpha)
,
proving that
\xi\in D(J)
. Also,
J\xi=u
. Therefore,
J
is closed.
Given
\xi=I_{0}(\psi_{1},\phi_{1})
and
\eta=I_{0}(\psi_{2},\phi_{2})
, there holds

\begin{split}\langle\xi,J\eta\rangle&=\langle\xi,JI_{0}(\psi_{2},\phi_{2})\rangle=\langle\xi,(\phi_{2},-\psi_{2})\rangle\\
&=\langle I_{0}(\psi_{1},\phi_{1}),(\phi_{2},-\psi_{2})\rangle=(\psi_{1},\phi_{2})_{2}-(\phi_{1},\psi_{2})_{2}\\
&=-\big[(\psi_{2},\phi_{1})_{2}-(\phi_{2},\psi_{1})_{2}\big]=-\langle I_{0}(\psi_{2},\phi_{2}),(\phi_{1},-\psi_{1})\rangle\\
&=-\langle I_{0}(\psi_{2},\phi_{2}),JI_{0}(\psi_{1},\phi_{1})\rangle=-\langle\eta,J\xi\rangle.\end{split}

Finally,
J
is onto. Given
u=(\phi,\psi)\in H^{1}_{r}\times L^{2}_{r}
,

I(-\psi,\phi)\in D(J)
and

JI(-\psi,\phi)=u
.
∎

The semigroup
T

We define
T(s)\colon X_{r}\to X_{r}
as

T(s)(\phi,\psi)=e^{-is}(\phi,\psi)

for every
s\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proposition 2
.

T(s)J=JT^{*}(-s)
for every
s\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

The two unbounded operators have the same domain
D(J)
. Given
\xi\in D(J)
, there exists

(\psi,\phi)\in L^{2}_{r}\times H^{1}_{r}
such that
\xi=I_{0}(\psi,\phi)
. Then

\begin{split}\langle T^{*}(-s)\xi,(h,k)\rangle&=\langle\xi,(e^{is}h,e^{is}k)\rangle\\
&=(\psi,e^{is}h)_{2}+(\phi,e^{is}k)_{2}=(e^{-is}\psi,h)_{2}+(e^{-is}\phi,k)_{2}.\end{split}

Therefore

\begin{split}JT^{*}(-s)I_{0}(\psi,\phi)&=JI_{0}(e^{-is}\psi,e^{-is}\phi)\\
&=(e^{-is}\phi,-e^{is}\psi)=e^{-is}(\phi,-\psi)=T(s)JI_{0}(\psi,\phi).\end{split}

∎

The operator
B

We define the bounded operator
B\colon X_{r}\to X_{r}^{*}
as
B(u):=I_{0}(i\psi,-i\phi)
.

Proposition 3
.

For every
u,v\in X_{r}
, there holds

(i).

Q(u)=\frac{1}{2}\langle Bu,u\rangle

(ii).

JB(u)=T^{\prime}(0)u

(iii).

\langle Bu,v\rangle=\langle Bv,u\rangle
.

Proof.

(i).

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\langle Bu,u\rangle&=\frac{1}{2}\langle B(\phi,\psi),(\phi,\psi)\rangle=\frac{1}{2}(i\psi,\phi)_{2}-\frac{1}{2}(i\phi,\psi)_{2}\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\text{Re}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}i\psi\overline{\phi}dx+\frac{1}{2}\text{Re}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}-i\phi\overline{\psi}dx\\
&=-\frac{1}{2}\text{Im}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\psi\overline{\phi}dx+\frac{1}{2}\text{Re}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\overline{-i\phi}\psi dx\\
&=-\frac{1}{2}\text{Im}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\psi\overline{\phi}dx+\frac{1}{2}\text{Re}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}i\overline{\phi}\psi dx\\
&=-\frac{1}{2}\text{Im}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\psi\overline{\phi}dx-\frac{1}{2}\text{Im}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\overline{\phi}\psi dx=Q(u).\end{split}

(ii).
T^{\prime}(0)\in\mathscr{L}(X_{r},X_{r})
is the infinitesimal generator of the semigroup
T
and
T^{\prime}(0)u=-iu
. We obtain the equality between the
two operators from

JB(u)=JI_{0}(i\psi,-i\phi)=(-i\phi,-i\psi)=T^{\prime}(0)u.

(iii). We set
u:=(\phi_{1},\psi_{1})
and
v:=(\phi_{2},\psi_{2})
. Then

\begin{split}\langle Bu,v\rangle&=(i\psi_{1},\phi_{2})_{2}-(i\phi_{1},\psi_{2})_{2}=-(\psi_{1},i\phi_{2})_{2}+(\phi_{1},i\psi_{2})_{2}\\
&=(i\psi_{2},\phi_{1})_{2}-(i\phi_{2},\psi_{1})_{2}=\langle Bv,u\rangle.\end{split}

∎

2.
Regularity assumptions

In this section, we check the regularity assumptions listed in the introduction of
[
15
]
, and
we construct a one-parameter family
u_{\omega}
of solutions
to
E^{\prime}(u_{\omega})=\omega Q^{\prime}(u_{\omega})

We summarize there results in the next proposition.

Proposition 4
.

(i).

Given
g
in
C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
such that
g(0)=0
,
the map
\phi\to g\circ\phi
is
C(H^{1}_{r},H^{1}_{r})

(ii).

if

C^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and
g(0)=g^{\prime}(0)=0
, then

\phi\to g\circ\phi
is
C^{1}(H^{1}_{r},H^{1}_{r})
and

\langle\mathscr{F}^{\prime}(\phi_{0}),\phi\rangle=g^{\prime}(\phi_{0})\phi

where
\mathscr{F}(\phi):=g\circ\phi
.

(iii).

Given
a\in C^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
, the
linear operator mapping
\phi
to
\mathscr{R}(\lambda)(a(x)\phi(x))
is
\mathscr{L}_{c}(H^{1}_{r},H^{1}_{r})

for every
\lambda<0

(iv).

E
and
Q
are
C^{2}(H^{1}_{r}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{R})
. Moreover, given
u_{0}:=(\phi_{0},\psi_{0})

\begin{array}[]{c}\langle E^{\prime}(u_{0}),(\phi,\psi)\rangle=(\phi_{0}^{\prime},\phi^{\prime})_{2}+(\psi_{0},\psi)_{2}+\left(G^{\prime}(|\phi_{0}|)\frac{\phi_{0}(x)}{|\phi_{0}(x)|},\phi\right)_{2}\\[6.99997pt]
\langle Q^{\prime}(u_{0}),(\phi,\psi)\rangle=(\phi_{0},i\psi)_{2}+(\phi,i\psi_{0})_{2}\end{array}

for every
(\phi,\psi)\in X_{r}
and

\begin{split}\langle E^{\prime\prime}(u_{0})(\phi,\psi),(h,k)\rangle=&\,(\phi^{\prime},h^{\prime})_{2}+(\psi,k)_{2}+m^{2}(\phi,h)_{2}\\
+&\,(G^{\prime\prime}(|\phi_{0}|)\text{Re}(\phi),h)_{2}+\big(|\phi_{0}|^{-1}G^{\prime}(|\phi_{0}|)\,i\text{Im}(\phi),h\big)_{2}\end{split}

and

\langle Q^{\prime\prime}(u_{0})(\phi,\psi),(h,k)\rangle=(\phi,ik)_{2}+(h,i\psi)_{2}.

In (i) and (ii) and (iv), the proof follows from the application of ideas illustrated in

[
2

regularity, with

.
However, their technique can be adapted to our setting, by taking advantage
of the bounded inclusion
H^{1}_{r}\subseteq L^{\infty}
.
In (iii), roughly speaking,

[

.

Assumption 1

According to

Proposition 5
.

Given

We define a function

\begin{split}&R_{\omega}^{\prime\prime}(x)-G^{\prime}(R_{\omega}(x))-(m^{2}-\omega^{2})R_{\omega}(x)=0,\\

From

Lemma 1
.

The function

u\colon(\omega_{*},m)\to X_{r}

C^{1}((\omega_{*},m),X_{r})

Proof.

We can rely on the argument of

The proof of the regularity

(i).
\mathscr{G}

(ii). It is differentiable at every point

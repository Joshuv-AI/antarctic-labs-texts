Uniform Calderón-Zygmund estimates in multiscale elliptic homogenization
Weisheng Niu, Jinping Zhuge

Abstract
. This paper is concerned with the elliptic equation − div ( A ε ∇ u ε ) = div f -\text{\rm div}(A_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon})=\text{\rm div}f in a bounded C 1 C^{1} domain, where A ε A_{\varepsilon} takes a form of A ε ​ ( x ) = A ⁡ ( x / ε 1 , x / ε 2 , ⋯ , x / ε n ) A_{\varepsilon}(x)=A(x/\varepsilon_{1},x/\varepsilon_{2},\cdots,x/\varepsilon_{n}) , with A ⁡ ( y 1 , y 2 , ⋯ , y n ) A(y_{1},y_{2},\cdots,y_{n}) being 1-periodic in each y i y_{i} . We prove the uniform Calderón-Zygmund estimate, namely, the uniform L p L^{p} boundedness of the linear map f ↦ ∇ u ε f\mapsto\nabla u_{\varepsilon} for any p ∈ ( 1 , ∞ ) p\in(1,\infty) with a constant independent of small parameters ( ε 1 , ε 2 , ⋯ , ε n ) ∈ ( 0 , 1 ] n (\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2},\cdots,\varepsilon_{n})\in(0,1]^{n} . Our result includes the uniform Calderón-Zygmund estimate in quasiperiodic elliptic homogenization (even without the Diophantine condition), which was previously unknown. The proof novelly combines the Dirichlet’s theorem on the simultaneous Diophantine approximation from number theory, a technique of reperiodization, reiterated periodic homogenization and a large-scale real-variable argument. Using the idea of reperiodization, we also obtain some large-scale or mesoscopic-scale Lipschitz estimates. Keywords: Multiscale homogenization, Diophantine approximation, Calderón-Zygmund estimate.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we study the uniform regularity for a family of linear elliptic equations or systems in divergence form in a bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

(1.1)

\displaystyle-\text{\rm div}(A_{\varepsilon}(x)\nabla u_{\varepsilon})=\text{\rm div}f,

where the coefficient matrix takes a form of
A_{\varepsilon}(x)=A(x/\varepsilon_{1},x/\varepsilon_{2},\cdots,x/\varepsilon_{n})
, and
(\varepsilon_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n}\in(0,1]^{n}
are small parameters describing
n
different oscillating scales of the coefficients. We assume that

A(y_{1},y_{2},\cdots,y_{n}):\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}\mapsto\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
is 1-periodic in each
y_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
1\leq i\leq n
. Due to the symmetry of
y_{i}
’s, it is harmless to assume
\varepsilon_{1}\geq\varepsilon_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\varepsilon_{n}>0
. The full assumptions on
A
will be given shortly.

The homogenization theorem (or quantitative convergence rates) and uniform regularity are the fundamental questions in homogenization theory. These two questions have been well answered for the equation (
1.1
) in the case of one oscillating scale (i.e.,
n=1
); see recent monographs
[
She18
,
AK22
]
and references therein. For the case of multiple oscillating scales, we refer to our recent work
[
NZ23
]
for a brief survey. It is important to point out that before the work
[
NZ23
]
, the homogenization theorem and uniform regularity were considered closely interconnected and always appear together, in the sense that the uniform regularity can be derived as a consequence of convergence rates either by a compactness method
[
AL87
,
KLS13
]
or a quantitative method (excess decay iteration
[
GNO20
,
AS16
,
She17
]
or a real-variable argument
[
CP98
]
). For the equation (
1.1
) with
n\geq 2
, we have the qualitative homogenization theorem or a quantitative convergence rate to a deterministic homogenized equation only if the scales
(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2},\cdots,\varepsilon_{n})
are separated or well-separated
[
AB96
]
, respectively. Recall that the scales
(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2},\cdots,\varepsilon_{n})\in(0,1]^{n}
are well-separated if
\varepsilon_{i+1}\lesssim\varepsilon_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}
for some
\alpha_{i}>1
.
Consequently, under this scale-separation condition,
the uniform regularity estimates for (
1.1
) were established in
[
NSX20
]
by a quantitative method. Yet, as pointed out by Avellaneda in
[
Ave96
]
(see also
[
GH03
]
) such an assumption is not adequate for treating the most general problems of transport and diffusion in self-similar (particularly random) media.
The main contribution of
[
NZ23
]
is that, without any separation condition on
(\varepsilon_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n}
, we obtained the uniform
C^{\alpha}
estimate for any
\alpha\in(0,1)
by the compactness method combined with a novel scale-reduction process. This result was unexpected since it shows for the first time that the uniform regularity holds stably even without homogenization, and in some sense the averaging effect always takes places across all the mesoscopic scales.

This paper is a continuation of our previous work
[
NZ23
]
. It was conjectured in
[
NZ23
]
that with
f=0
in (
1.1
) and without any scale-separation condition, the solution admits uniform Lipschitz estimate. We should mention that the gradient estimate (like Lipschitz estimate or Calderón-Zygmund estimate) cannot be established by the soft compactness method as in our previous paper without the help of correctors or convergence rates. In this paper, we partially solve the conjecture by proving a slightly weaker result, namely, the gradient
\nabla u_{\varepsilon}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{p}
spaces for any
p<\infty
. In particular, this recovers the uniform
C^{\alpha}
estimate in
[
NZ23
]
in view of the Sobolev embedding theorem. Moreover, our proof is quantitative (in contrast to the compactness method in
[
NZ23
]
) in the sense that the constant can be computed explicitly.

Before stating the main result, we list the assumptions on the coefficient matrix
A
:

•

Strong ellipticity condition: there exists
\Lambda\in(0,1]
so that

(1.2)

\Lambda|\xi|^{2}\leq\xi\cdot A(y_{1},\cdots,y_{n})\xi\quad\text{and}\quad|A(y_{1},\cdots,y_{n})\xi|\leq\Lambda^{-1}|\xi|,

for every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and for any
(y_{1},\cdots,y_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}.

•

Periodicity: for any
(y_{1},\cdots,y_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}
and
(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d\times n}
,

(1.3)

A(y_{1}+z_{1},\cdots,y_{n}+z_{n})=A(y_{1},\cdots,y_{n}).

•

Smoothness: there exists
\tau\in(0,1]
and
L>0
such that

(1.4)

|A(y_{1},\cdots,y_{n})-A(y_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,y_{n}^{\prime})|\leq L\big\{|y_{1}^{\prime}-y_{1}|+\cdots+|y_{n}^{\prime}-y_{n}|\big\}^{\tau}

for any
(y_{1},\cdots,y_{n}),(y_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,y_{n}^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}
.

We now state our main result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded
C^{1}
domain. Assume
A
satisfies (
1.2
), (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) and
(\varepsilon_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n}\in(0,1]^{n}
. Assume
1<p<\infty
. Let
u_{\varepsilon}
be the weak solution to the Dirichlet problem

(1.5)

\displaystyle-\text{\rm div}\big(A_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\big)=\text{\rm div}f\,\text{ in }\Omega,\quad u_{\varepsilon}=0\,\text{ on }\partial\Omega,

where
A_{\varepsilon}=A(x/\varepsilon_{1},x/\varepsilon_{2},\cdots,x/\varepsilon_{n})
and
f\in L^{p}(\Omega)^{d}
. Then
\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\in L^{p}(\Omega)^{d}
and

(1.6)

\displaystyle\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)},

where
C
depends only on
d,n,\Lambda,p
,
(\tau,L)
in (
1.4
), and
\Omega
.

The same uniform estimate holds if the Dirichlet boundary condition in (
1.5
) is replaced by the compatible Neumann boundary condition

(1.7)

\nu\cdot A_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}=-\nu\cdot f\,\text{ on }\partial\Omega,

where
\nu
is the unit outer normal vector of
\partial\Omega
.

The new ingredient in the proof of Theorem
1.1
is the Dirichlet’s theorem (see Theorem
3.1
) on simultaneous Diophantine approximation from number theory, which enters into a new technique of
reperiodization
and allow us to quantitatively separate one scale from the rest
n-1
scales, leading to a quantitative gradient approximation (this is not achievable by the rough scale separation argument in
[
NZ23
]
). Precisely, for a given sequence
(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2},\cdots,\varepsilon_{n})
in nonincreasing order and any number
Q>1
, we can find a new 1-periodic matrix
A^{\sharp}
, depending on
Q
and the ratios
\varepsilon_{n}/\varepsilon_{i}
, such that
A_{\varepsilon}(x)
can be rewritten as

(1.8)

A(x/\varepsilon_{1},x/\varepsilon_{2},\cdots,x/\varepsilon_{n})=A^{\sharp}(x/\varepsilon^{\prime}_{1},x/\varepsilon^{\prime}_{2},\cdots,x/\varepsilon^{\prime}_{n}),

with some
(\varepsilon_{1}^{\prime},\varepsilon_{2}^{\prime},\cdots,\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime})\in(0,\infty)^{n}
and the last scale
\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime}
is
Q
-separated
from the rest
\varepsilon_{i}^{\prime}
’s (i.e.,
\varepsilon^{\prime}_{i}\geq Q\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime}
for all
1\leq i\leq n-1
); see Section
3.1
for details. Theoretically,
Q
can be chosen as large as
(\varepsilon_{n}/r)^{-\sigma}
with some
\sigma>0
, yielding a well-separation condition and thus a good convergence rate to an approximate problem in
B_{r}
with at most
n-1
scales (see Remark
3.3
). However, as a payoff
the regularity of
A^{\sharp}(y_{1},y_{2},\cdots,y_{n})
in
y_{n}
will become worse as
Q
increases, which leads to a small-scale estimate depending on
Q
in a blow up argument.
Fortunately, a sufficiently large
Q
independent of
(\varepsilon_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n}
will be just enough for our proof. With the
Q
-separation condition, we can perform a quantitative argument of the reiterated homogenization and approximate the solution of the original equation with
n
oscillating scales by a solution of a new equation with at most
n-1
oscillating scales. Hence, an inductive argument on the number of scales combined with a careful real-variable argument (see Theorem
4.2
, which is a quantitative refined version of the one originating in
[
CP98
]
) involving a
double-averaging estimate
will complete the proof.

It is crucial to point out that the proofs for
C^{\alpha}
estimate (a type of Schauder estimates) and
L^{p}
gradient estimate (Calderón-Zygmund estimate related to singular integrals) are essentially different, for the latter is stronger and more informative. As in
[
NZ23
]
, the
C^{\alpha}
estimate can be derived with a qualitative
H
-convergence theorem and a compactness method (the correctors are not needed; also see
[
She15
, Theorem 3.1]
or
[
SZ18
, Theorem 6.1]
for similar situations). However, the estimate of gradient must involve a quantitative convergence rate or the fine properties of correctors. While our proof of Theorem
1.1
also uses a scale-reduction theorem and an inductive argument on the number of scales, it is exactly the Dirichlet’s theorem that helps us quantify the error in the scale-reduction process and provides much stronger implications and more information about the behavior of the solutions. In particular, it possibly explains the reason behind the occurrence of the mesoscopic averaging effect in arbitrary multiscale media.

As a direct consequence, Theorem
1.1
implies the uniform Calderón-Zygmund estimate for the operator
-\text{\rm div}(A(x/\varepsilon)\nabla)
with arbitrary quasiperiodic coefficient
A
(which was previously known only under a Diophantine condition). We recall that
A(y)
is said to be quasiperiodic if
A(y)=B(My)
, where
B(w)
is
1
-periodic in
w\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
1\leq N\in\mathbb{N}
and
M
is an
N\times d
constant real matrix. Note that we do not have any restriction on the entries of
M
and therefore
A
is allowed to be periodic with a very degenerate periodic cell.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded
C^{1}
domain. Assume that
A(y)=B(My)
is quasi-periodic as above and
B
satisfies (
1.2
), (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) (with
n=1
). Let
1<p<\infty
,
f\in L^{p}(\Omega)^{d}
, and
u_{\varepsilon}
be the weak solution to

\displaystyle-\text{\rm div}\big(A(x/\varepsilon)\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\big)=\text{\rm div}f\,\text{ in }\Omega,\quad u_{\varepsilon}=0\,\text{ on }\partial\Omega.

Then
\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\in L^{p}(\Omega)^{d}
and

\displaystyle\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)},

where
C
depends only on
d,\Lambda,p,\Omega,N
, and
(\tau,L)
in (
1.4
).

In the setting of quasiperiodic homogenization, the convergence rate was first obtained by Kozlov in
[
Koz78
]
under a Diophantine condition (a quantitative ergodicity condition): for each row
M_{i}
of
M
,
|M_{i}\cdot z|\geq c_{0}|z|^{-\beta}
for all
z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
and for some
c_{0},\beta>0
.
It was observed in
[
AL91
]
that the uniform regularity is valid under Kozlov’s Diophantine condition. This condition was also required in recent work
[
She15
,
AGK16
,
BG19
]
applied to the case of quasiperiodic coefficients.
In our Corollary
1.2
, we do not need the Diophantine condition and the constant in the estimate is independent of the entries of the matrix
M
(except for the dimension of
M
). This includes some typical degenerate cases, such as periodic coefficients with a degenerate periodic cell (e.g., a thin rectangle), quasiperiodic coefficients oscillating at two almost resonant frequencies (e.g., the ratio of the frequencies is a Liouville number), multiscale quasiperiodic coefficients, etc.

The proof of Corollary
1.2
based on Theorem
1.1
is simple and we include it here. In fact, it suffices to write
A(x/\varepsilon)
into a periodic coefficient matrix with multiple oscillating scales and apply Theorem
1.1
.
To this end, let
M=(M_{ij})_{1\leq i\leq N,1\leq j\leq d}
.
Let
y_{ij}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
1\leq i\leq N
and
1\leq j\leq d
be
Nd
independent variables.
Define a matrix with variables
y_{ij}

A^{+}(y_{11},\cdots,y_{ij},\cdots,y_{Nd})=B\big(\sum_{j=1}^{d}y_{1j}\cdot e_{j},\cdots,\sum_{j=1}^{d}y_{Nj}\cdot e_{j}\big).

Then
A^{+}
is 1-periodic in each
y_{ij}
since
B(w)
is 1-periodic. Moreover, it is straightforward to verify

(1.9)

A(x/\varepsilon)=B(Mx/\varepsilon)=A^{+}(M_{11}x/\varepsilon,\cdots,M_{ij}x/\varepsilon,\cdots,M_{Nd}x/\varepsilon).

This reduces the quasiperiodic coefficient matrix with one oscillating scale into a periodic coefficient matrix with
Nd
oscillating scales. Clearly, in this case, all the scales are possibly not well-separated. Moreover, if
B
is uniformly elliptic and Hölder continuous, so is
A^{+}
. As a result, Corollary
1.2
follows readily from Theorem
1.1
.

Finally, using a similar idea of scale separation, we can show the uniform large-scale or mesoscopic-scale Lipschitz estimates. In particular, in the case of two scales, i.e.,
n=2
, for any
\alpha\in(0,1)
, we have for all
\varepsilon_{2}^{1-\alpha}\leq r\leq 1
,

(1.10)

\bigg(\fint_{B_{r}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\leq C_{\alpha}\bigg\{\bigg(\fint_{B_{1}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+\bigg(\fint_{B_{1}}|F|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}\bigg\},

where
C_{\alpha}
depends only on
d,\Lambda,p,\tau,L
and
\alpha
; see Theorem
5.1
. For general cases
n\geq 3
, we have some mesoscopic-scale Lipschitz estimates near any given scale; see Theorem
5.2
.

Organization.
The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we recall some knowledge and known results from locally periodic homogenization and reiterated homogenization. In Section
3
, we use the Dirichlet’s theorem and reperiodization technique to separate scales and reduce one scale by a uniform approximation. The main theorem is proved in Section
4
by a real-variable argument. In Section
5
, we exploit the idea of scale separation to obtain the uniform large-scale or mesoscopic-scale Lipschitz estimates.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Local periodic homogenization

In this subsection, we recall some results concerning the homogenization of the locally periodic operator
-\text{\rm div}(A(x,x/\varepsilon)\nabla)
with a scalar
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, which will be used in our quantitative scale reduction process in Section 3.

Suppose
A(x,y)
satisfies the ellipticity condition (
1.2
) and is 1-periodic in
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
The effective (homogenized) matrix is given by

(2.1)

\displaystyle\widehat{A}(x)=\fint_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\Big(A(x,y)+A(x,y)\nabla_{y}\chi(x,y)\Big)dy,

where
\chi(x,y)=(\chi_{1}(x,y),\dots,\chi_{d}(x,y))
is the corrector given by the cell problem

(2.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\text{\rm div}_{y}\big(A(x,y)\nabla_{y}\chi_{j})=\text{\rm div}_{y}\big(A(x,y)\nabla_{y}y^{j}\big)\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{T}^{d}:=\mathbb{R}^{d}/\mathbb{Z}^{d},\\
&\chi_{j}=\chi_{j}(x,y)\text{ is 1-periodic in }y,\\
&\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\chi_{j}(x,y)\,dy=0,\end{aligned}\right.

for
1\leq j\leq d
. Here
y^{j}
denotes the
j
th component of
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Assume that

(2.3)

\|\nabla_{x}A\|_{\infty}=\|\nabla_{x}A\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{x}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}_{y})}<\infty.

The standard energy estimates for (
2.2
) imply that

(2.4)

\|\nabla_{y}\chi\|_{L^{2}(Y)}\leq C,\quad\|\nabla_{x}\nabla_{y}\chi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{x}^{d};L^{2}(Y))}\leq C\|\nabla_{x}A\|_{\infty}

with
C
depending only on
\Lambda,d.

This combined with the definition of
\widehat{A}
yields

\|\nabla_{x}\widehat{A}\|_{\infty}\leq C\|\nabla_{x}A\|_{\infty}.

Likewise, if

(2.5)

|A(x,y)-A(x^{\prime},y)|\leq L|x-x^{\prime}|^{\tau}

for some
0<\tau\leq 1
for any
x,x^{\prime},y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Then we have

(2.6)

\displaystyle|\widehat{A}(x)-\widehat{A}(x^{\prime})|\leq CL|x-x^{\prime}|^{\tau}.

Since we do not assume any smoothness of
A(x,y)
on
y
, a smoothing operator is needed to avoid the roughness of correctors at
\varepsilon
scale, which has been a standard technique even in the one-scale problem (see
[
She18
, Chapter 3.1]
).
Let
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be supported in
B_{1/2}(0)
such that
\varphi\geq 0
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi dx=1
.
For functions of the form
g^{\varepsilon}(x)=g(x,x/\varepsilon)
, we
define a partial-smoothing operator

(2.7)

\displaystyle S_{\varepsilon}(g^{\varepsilon})(x)=\varepsilon^{-d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(z,x/\varepsilon)\varphi((x-z)/\varepsilon)dz.

The following theorem provides the convergence rate in
H^{1}(\Omega)
for the locally periodic operator. The proof may be found in
[
NSX20
, Theorem 3.1 and Remark 3.1]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Let
u_{\varepsilon}
be the solution to
-\text{\rm div}(A(x,x/\varepsilon)\nabla u_{\varepsilon})=F
in
\Omega
with
u_{\varepsilon}=g
on
\partial\Omega,
and
u_{0}
the solution to
-\text{\rm div}(\widehat{A}(x)\nabla u_{0})=F
in
\Omega
with
u_{0}=g
on
\partial\Omega.
Then we have

(2.8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|u_{\varepsilon}-u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}-\nabla u_{0}-S_{\varepsilon}((\nabla_{y}\chi)^{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{0})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\\
&\leq C\varepsilon\big\{(1+\|\nabla_{x}A\|_{\infty})\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla^{2}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\setminus\Omega_{3\varepsilon})}\big\}+C\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{4\varepsilon})},\end{split}

where we have extended
u_{0}
to the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{d},

\Omega_{t}=\big\{x\in\Omega:\text{\rm dist}(x,\partial\Omega)<t\big\}
, and
C
depends only on
d
,
\Lambda
and
\Omega
.

We emphasize that in the above theorem,
A
has no smoothness assumption in
y
.

2.2.
Reiterated correctors and effective matrix

Consider the operator with
n
scales
-\text{\rm div}(A(x/\varepsilon_{1},x/\varepsilon_{2},\cdots,x/\varepsilon_{n})\nabla)
, where
A
satisfies the ellipticity and periodicity conditions (
1.2
) and (
1.3
). Assume that
\varepsilon_{n}
is separated from
\varepsilon_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq n-1.
By using the idea of reiterated homogenization, one can view
(\varepsilon_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n-1}
as parameters and homogenize the finest scale
\varepsilon_{n}
to obtain an operator of the same type but with only
n-1
scales.

Indeed rewrite

\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}(x,y):=A(x/\varepsilon_{1},x/\varepsilon_{2},\cdots,x/\varepsilon_{n-1},y).

It is obvious that
\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}(x,y)
satisfies (
1.2
), and is locally 1-periodic in
y
.
Let
\chi_{\varepsilon}(x,y)
be the corrector given by (
2.2
) with
A(x,y)
replaced by
\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}(x,y)
, and
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{\varepsilon}(x)
the effective matrix of
\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}
given by (
2.1
).
In view of the structure of
\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}(x,y)
, the corresponding corrector
\chi_{\varepsilon}(x,y)
takes a form of

(2.9)

\chi_{\varepsilon}(x,y)=\mathcal{X}(x/\varepsilon_{1},\cdots,x/\varepsilon_{n-1},y),

where the reiterated corrector
\mathcal{X}=(\mathcal{X}_{j})
is defined by the cell problem

(2.10)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\text{\rm div}_{y}\big(A(y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-1},y)\nabla_{y}\mathcal{X}_{j})=\text{\rm div}_{y}\big(A(y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-1},y)\nabla_{y}y^{j}\big)\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{T}^{d},\\
&\mathcal{X}_{j}=\mathcal{X}_{j}(y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-1},y)\text{ is 1-periodic in }y,\\
&\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mathcal{X}_{j}(y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-1},y)\,dy=0,\end{aligned}\right.

for
1\leq j\leq d
, which is also 1-periodic in
y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-1}
, due to the periodicity of
A
. Moreover,
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{\varepsilon}(x)

takes a form of

(2.11)

\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{\varepsilon}(x)=\widehat{\mathcal{A}}(x/\varepsilon_{1},\cdots,x/\varepsilon_{n-1})

with

(2.12)

\widehat{\mathcal{A}}(y_{1},y_{2},\cdots,y_{n-1})=\fint_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}A(y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-1},y)\Big(I+\nabla_{y}\mathcal{X}(y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-1},y)\Big)dy.

It is obvious that
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}(y_{1},y_{2},\cdots,y_{n-1})
is 1-periodic in each
y_{i}
with
1\leq i\leq n-1
, and it is defined independent of
(\varepsilon_{1},\cdots,\varepsilon_{n})
.
Similar to the case
n=1
,
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}
satisfies the ellipticity condition (
1.2
). Furthermore, similar to (
2.6
), if
A
is Hölder continuous in
y_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq n-1
, so is
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}
.

3.
Quantitative scale separation and scale reduction

3.1.
Scale separation

The key ingredient of this paper is the Dirichlet’s theorem on the simultaneous Diophantine approximation. The connection between the Dirichlet’s theorem and the regularity theory in homogenization is previously unknown. The only loosely related notion is the Diophantine condition imposed in the setting of quasiperiodic homogenization, as mentioned earlier. The Dirichlet’s theorem appears to be a powerful tool (instead of a condition) that eventually allows us to derive the uniform Calderón-Zygmund estimates in multiscale or quasiperiodic homogenization without any additional conditions.

Let us first recall the Dirichlet’s theorem; see
[
Sch91
]
.

Theorem 3.1

(Dirichlet (1842))
.

Suppose that
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\cdots,\alpha_{m}
are
m
real numbers and
Q>1
. There exist integers
q,p_{1},p_{2},\cdots,p_{m}
such that
1\leq q<Q^{m}
and

(3.1)

\sup_{1\leq i\leq m}\Big|\alpha_{i}-\frac{p_{i}}{q}\Big|<\frac{1}{qQ}.

Roughly speaking, the Dirichlet’s theorem states that any real numbers can be approximated by good rational numbers with a quantitative small error.
In the rest of this subsection, we apply the Dirichlet’s theorem to separate at least one scale in the coefficient matrix oscillating at multiple (possibly unseparated) scales.

Without loss of generality, hereafter we always assume

(3.2)

1\geq\varepsilon_{1}\geq\varepsilon_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\varepsilon_{n}>0.

Let
Q\gg 1
be a large number. We emphasize that the fixed number
Q
will be chosen later independent of
\{\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2},\cdots,\varepsilon_{n}\}
. We say
a
is
Q
-separated from
b
if
b\geq Qa
. This scale separation condition is crucial in quantitative reiterated homogenization.

Given
Q>1
as above, by the Dirichlet’s theorem with
\alpha_{i}=\varepsilon_{n}/\varepsilon_{i}\in(0,1]
for
i=1,2,\cdots,n-1
, we can find integers
q,p_{1},p_{2},\cdots,p_{n-1}
such that
1\leq q<Q^{n-1}
and

(3.3)

\sup_{1\leq i\leq n-1}\Big|\frac{\varepsilon_{n}}{\varepsilon_{i}}-\frac{p_{i}}{q}\Big|<\frac{1}{qQ}.

Moreover, because of (
3.2
), we have
0\leq p_{i}\leq p_{i+1}\leq q<Q^{n-1}
. Note that it is possible that
p_{i}=0
if
\varepsilon_{i}
is large enough that
\varepsilon_{n}/\varepsilon_{i}<1/Q
(i.e.,
\varepsilon_{n}
is already
Q
-separated from
\varepsilon_{i}
).

Define, for
1\leq i\leq n-1
,

(3.4)

\gamma_{i}:=\Big|\frac{\varepsilon_{n}}{\varepsilon_{i}}-\frac{p_{i}}{q}\Big|\quad\text{and}\quad s_{i}=\text{sgn}\Big(\frac{\varepsilon_{n}}{\varepsilon_{i}}-\frac{p_{i}}{q}\Big).

Then, we can write

(3.5)

\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{i}}=\frac{\gamma_{i}s_{i}}{\varepsilon_{n}}+\frac{p_{i}}{q}\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{n}}.

Now, we define

(3.6)

A^{\sharp}(y_{1},y_{2},\cdots,y_{n})=A(s_{1}y_{1}+p_{1}y_{n},s_{2}y_{2}+p_{2}y_{n},\cdots,s_{n-1}y_{n-1}+p_{n-1}y_{n},qy_{n}).

The key insight here is that,
since
s_{i}=\pm 1
and
p_{1},p_{2},\cdots,p_{n}
are all integers,
A^{\sharp}
is 1-periodic in each
y_{i}
. We thereby will call the transformation from
A
to
A^{\sharp}
a technique of reperiodization. As a result of (
3.5
) and (
3.6
), we have

(3.7)

A\big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{1}},\cdots,\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{n}}\big)=A^{\sharp}\big(\frac{\gamma_{1}x}{\varepsilon_{n}},\frac{\gamma_{2}x}{\varepsilon_{n}},\cdots,\frac{\gamma_{n-1}x}{\varepsilon_{n}},\frac{x}{q\varepsilon_{n}}\big).

In other words, the reperiodization allows us to rewrite the original periodic matrix
A
oscillating at scales
\{\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2},\cdots,\varepsilon_{n}\}
into a new periodic matrix
A^{\sharp}
oscillating at different scales
\{\varepsilon_{n}/\gamma_{1},\varepsilon_{n}/\gamma_{2},\cdots,\varepsilon_{n}/\gamma_{n-1},q\varepsilon_{n}\}
. Note that if
p_{i}=0
for some
i
, then the corresponding scale for that (slower) variable does not change, i.e.,
\varepsilon_{n}/\gamma_{i}=\varepsilon_{i}
. If
\gamma_{i}=0
for some
i
, then we just do not have the scale
\varepsilon_{n}/\gamma_{i}
and we have less oscillating scales after reperiodization (which is even better). Without loss of generality, we may assume this does not happen.

The new scales and the new 1-periodic matrix
A^{\sharp}
have the following crucial properties:

•

The smallest scale
q\varepsilon_{n}
is
Q
-separated from
\varepsilon_{n}/\gamma_{i}
for each
1\leq i\leq n-1
.
In fact, (
3.3
) implies

(3.8)

\frac{\varepsilon_{n}/\gamma_{i}}{q\varepsilon_{n}}=\frac{1}{q\gamma_{i}}\geq Q.

This is the key property for our application.

•

If
A
is Hölder continuous in
y_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq n-1
, i.e.,

(3.9)

\displaystyle|A(y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-1},y_{n})-A(y_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,y_{n-1}^{\prime},y_{n})|

\displaystyle\leq L\big\{|y_{1}^{\prime}-y_{1}|+\cdots+|y_{n-1}^{\prime}-y_{n-1}|\big\}^{\tau}

with
0<L,0<\tau\leq 1
for any
(y_{1},\cdots,y_{n}),(y_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,y_{n-1}^{\prime},y_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}
,
then
A^{\sharp}
is also Hölder continuous in
y_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq n-1
with the same constants
(\tau,L)
. However,
A^{\sharp}
may lose good regularity in
y_{n}
since many periods have been compressed into a single 1-periodic cell.

•

The construction of
A^{\sharp}
relies on the choice of
Q
as well as the ratios
\varepsilon_{n}/\varepsilon_{i}
with
1\leq i\leq n-1
. In other words,
A^{\sharp}
is scale-invariant. Also note that
Q
is a dimensionless parameter.

It is important to note that the above scale separation process can only separate the smallest scale from the rest
n-1
larger scales, while the relationships among the rest
n-1
larger scales cannot be determined. This allows us to perform a one-scale reduction by using the idea of reiterated homogenization.

To see more clearly how the above argument works, we provide a concrete example of two scales. Let
A_{\varepsilon}(x)=A(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\frac{x}{\varepsilon(1/3+\delta)})
, where
\varepsilon\ll 1,\delta\ll 1
(e.g.,
\delta=\varepsilon^{1/2}
). Clearly, in this case the two scales
\varepsilon_{1}=\varepsilon
and
\varepsilon_{2}=\varepsilon(1/3+\delta)
are not separated since their ratio is
\alpha=\varepsilon_{2}/\varepsilon_{1}=1/3+\delta
. Now, note that this ratio can be well-approximated by the rational number
1/3
in the sense of Dirichlet’s theorem for
p=1,q=3
and up to
Q=(3\delta)^{-1}\gg 1
. Using the previous argument, we can write

(3.10)

A\Big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\frac{x}{\varepsilon(1/3+\delta)}\Big)=A^{\sharp}\Big(\frac{\delta x}{\varepsilon(1/3+\delta)},\frac{x}{\varepsilon+3\varepsilon\delta}\Big),

where
A^{\sharp}(y_{1},y_{2})=A(y_{1}+y_{2},3y_{2})
is 1-periodic in both
y_{1}
and
y_{2}
. Moreover, the new scales
\varepsilon\delta^{-1}(1/3+\delta)
and
\varepsilon+3\varepsilon\delta
are obviously well-separated as their ratio is exactly
Q=(3\delta)^{-1}
.

3.2.
Scale reduction and uniform approximation

In this subsection we use the scale separation technique in Section
3.1
to reduce at least one scale. Precisely, we shall prove that the solution of (
1.1
) can be uniformly approximated by a solution to the equation of the same type but with at most
n-1
oscillating scales. The following is the main theorem.

Theorem 3.2
.

Suppose that
A
satisfies the assumptions (
1.2
), (
1.3
) and (
3.9
). Let
u_{\varepsilon}
be a weak solution to

(3.11)

\displaystyle-\text{\rm div}\big(A(x/\varepsilon_{1},x/\varepsilon_{2},\cdots,x/\varepsilon_{n})\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\big)=F\quad\text{in }B_{2r}:=B(0,2r).

Then for any
Q>1
, there exists a coefficient matrix
A^{\flat}=A^{\flat}(y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-1})
, scales
\varepsilon_{k}^{\prime}\in(0,\infty)
with
k=1,\cdots,n-1
, and a weak solution
u^{\flat}_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}
to

(3.12)

-\text{\rm div}\big(A^{\flat}(x/\varepsilon^{\prime}_{1},x/\varepsilon^{\prime}_{2},\cdots,x/\varepsilon^{\prime}_{n-1})\nabla u^{\flat}_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}\big)=F\quad\text{in }B_{r},

such that

(3.13)

\|\nabla u^{\flat}_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}\|_{L^{2}(B_{r})}\leq C\big\{\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(B_{2r})}+r\|F\|_{L^{2}(B_{2r})}\big\},

and

(3.14)

\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}-\nabla u^{\flat}_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}-U_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}\|_{L^{2}(B_{r})}\leq C\bigg\{\bigg(\frac{Q^{n-1}\varepsilon_{n}}{r}\bigg)^{\sigma}+Q^{-\tau}\bigg\}\big\{\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(B_{2r})}+r\|F\|_{L^{2}(B_{2r})}\big\}

for some
\sigma>0,

where
U_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}
is given by

(3.15)

U_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}(x)=(\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime})^{-d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi\bigg(\frac{x-z}{\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime}}\bigg)(\nabla_{y}\mathcal{X}^{\sharp})\bigg(\frac{z}{\varepsilon_{1}^{\prime}},\cdots,\frac{z}{\varepsilon^{\prime}_{n-1}},\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime}}\bigg)\nabla u^{\flat}_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}(z)dz,

\mathcal{X}^{\sharp}
is the corrector given by (
2.10
) with
A
replaced by
A^{\sharp}
, and
\varepsilon^{\prime}_{n}
is given by (
3.21
).
Moreover, the constant
C
depends only on
d,n,\Lambda
and
(\tau,L)
in (
3.9
), and the assumptions (
1.2
), (
1.3
) and (
3.9
) are preserved for
A^{\flat}
.

Remark 3.3
.

Note that in the above theorem, we only state the approximation error for the gradient
\nabla u_{\varepsilon}
. Actually, we also have the same error bound for
\|u_{\varepsilon}-u_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}^{\flat}\|_{L^{2}(B_{r})}
. In particular, if we pick
Q=(r/\varepsilon_{n})^{\theta/(n-1)}
for some
\theta\in(0,1)
, then we have for some
\gamma\in(0,1)
,

(3.16)

\|u_{\varepsilon}-u_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}^{\flat}\|_{L^{2}(B_{r})}\leq Cr\Big(\frac{\varepsilon_{n}}{r}\Big)^{\gamma}\big\{\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(B_{2r})}+r\|F\|_{L^{2}(B_{2r})}\big\}.

This error estimate shows that, at any scale
r\gg\varepsilon_{n}
, the solution
u_{\varepsilon}
of the problem (
3.11
) with
n
oscillating scales can be well approximated by the solution of (
3.12
) with (at most)
n-1
oscillating scales. Moreover, in general the coefficient matrix
A^{\flat}
(depending on
Q
) depends on
A
and
r/\varepsilon_{n}
. In other words, the approximate equation (
3.12
) may vary according to different radii
r
, which is quite different from the classical homogenization theory in which the homogenized equation is fixed (independent of
r
). This is essentially due to the absence of scale-separation condition between
\varepsilon_{n}
and the rest of the scales.

Remark 3.4
.

One may naturally ask if we can repeat Theorem
3.2
to get the next approximation with at most
n-2
oscillating scales for the equation (
3.12
). This is unfortunately impossible in general, because in the worst case all the scales
\varepsilon_{1}^{\prime},\varepsilon_{2}^{\prime},\cdots,\varepsilon_{n-1}^{\prime}
will not be small and we are not able to get a small error in the next step.

The proof of Theorem
3.2
relies on the scale separation technique in Section
3.1
and the following approximation theorem that pertains to the quantitative reiterated homogenization.

Theorem 3.5
.

Suppose
A
satisfies the assumption (
1.2
), (
1.3
), and (

).

such that

\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}

2.1
) (recall that
\chi_{\varepsilon}(x,y)
and
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{\varepsilon}(x)
take the forms of (
2.9
) and (
2.11
)).
Note that
\chi_{\varepsilon}-\chi_{\varepsilon}^{\rm app}=(\chi_{\varepsilon,j}-\chi_{\varepsilon,j}^{\rm app})
satisfies

\displaystyle-\text{\rm div}_{y}\big(\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}(x,y)(\nabla_{y}\chi_{\varepsilon,j}-\nabla_{y}\chi_{\varepsilon,j}^{\rm app})\big)

\displaystyle=\text{\rm div}_{y}\big((\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}(x,y)-\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}^{\rm app}(x,y))\nabla_{y}\chi^{app}_{\varepsilon,j}\big)+\text{\rm div}_{y}\big((\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}(x,y)-\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}^{\rm app}(x,y))e_{j}\big)\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{T}^{d}.

Thus, the standard energy estimate and the first inequality in (
3.29
) imply that

(3.41)

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\|\nabla_{y}\chi_{\varepsilon,j}(x,\cdot)-\nabla_{y}\chi^{\rm app}_{\varepsilon,j}(x,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\leq CL\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\varepsilon_{k}^{-\tau}\varepsilon_{n}^{\tau},

where
C
depends only on
d,\Lambda
and
\tau
.
This together with the definitions of
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{\varepsilon}^{\rm app}
and
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{\varepsilon}
gives

(3.42)

\|\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{\varepsilon}^{\rm app}-\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{\varepsilon}\|_{\infty}\leq CL\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\varepsilon_{k}^{-\tau}\varepsilon_{n}^{\tau}.

Let
\widehat{u}_{\varepsilon}
be the weak solution to

(3.43)

\displaystyle-\text{\rm div}\big(\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{\varepsilon}(x)\nabla\widehat{u}_{\varepsilon}\big)=F\quad\text{ in }B_{5/4},\quad\text{ and }\quad\widehat{u}_{\varepsilon}=\widehat{u}^{\rm app}_{\varepsilon}\quad\text{ on }\partial B_{5/4}.

Similar to (
3.33
), we apply
the standard energy estimate to the equation for
\widehat{u}_{\varepsilon}^{\rm app}-\widehat{u}_{\varepsilon}
,

(3.44)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\text{\rm div}\big(\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{\varepsilon}(x,x/\varepsilon_{n})\nabla(\widehat{u}_{\varepsilon}-\widehat{u}_{\varepsilon}^{\rm app})\big)=\text{\rm div}\big((\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{\varepsilon}(x,x/\varepsilon_{n})-\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{\rm app}_{\varepsilon}(x,x/\varepsilon_{n}))\nabla\widehat{u}_{\varepsilon}^{\rm app}\big)\quad\text{in }B_{5/4},\\
&\widehat{u}_{\varepsilon}-\widehat{u}_{\varepsilon}^{\rm app}=0\quad\text{on }\partial B_{5/4},\end{aligned}\right.

and use (
3.42
) to obtain

(3.45)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{B_{5/4}}|\nabla(\widehat{u}_{\varepsilon}-\widehat{u}_{\varepsilon}^{\rm app})|^{2}&\leq CL^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\varepsilon_{k}^{-2\tau}\varepsilon_{n}^{2\tau}\int_{B_{5/4}}|\nabla\widehat{u}_{\varepsilon}^{\rm app}|^{2}\\
&\leq CL^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\varepsilon_{k}^{-2\tau}\varepsilon_{n}^{2\tau}\bigg\{\int_{B_{2}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\int_{B_{2}}|F|^{2}\bigg\},\end{split}

where the energy estimate for
\widehat{u}_{\varepsilon}^{\rm app}
(or (
3.39
)) is also used in the last inequality.

Finally, combining (
3.33
), (
3.41
) and (
3.45
), we may replace the approximate solutions
u_{\varepsilon}^{\rm app},\widehat{u}_{\varepsilon}^{\rm app},\chi_{\varepsilon}^{\rm app}
on the left-hand side of (
3.40
) by
u_{\varepsilon},\widehat{u}_{\varepsilon},\chi_{\varepsilon}
, respectively. In particular,
by the property of the smoothing operator
S_{\varepsilon_{n}}
(see
[
NSX20
, Lemma 2.2]
), we have

(3.46)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{B_{1}}|S_{\varepsilon_{n}}\big((\nabla_{y}\chi_{\varepsilon})^{\varepsilon_{n}}\nabla\widehat{u}_{\varepsilon}\big)-S_{\varepsilon_{n}}\big((\nabla_{y}\chi_{\varepsilon}^{\rm app})^{\varepsilon_{n}}\nabla\widehat{u}_{\varepsilon}^{\rm app}\big)|^{2}\\
&\leq 2\int_{B_{1}}\big\{|S_{\varepsilon_{n}}\big((\nabla_{y}\chi_{\varepsilon})^{\varepsilon_{n}}(\nabla\widehat{u}_{\varepsilon}-\nabla\widehat{u}_{\varepsilon}^{\rm app})\big)|^{2}+|S_{\varepsilon_{n}}\big(((\nabla_{y}\chi_{\varepsilon}^{\rm app})^{\varepsilon_{n}}-(\nabla_{y}\chi_{\varepsilon})^{\varepsilon_{n}})\nabla\widehat{u}_{\varepsilon}^{\rm app}\big)|^{2}\big\}\\
&\leq C\int_{B_{1+\varepsilon_{n}}}|\nabla\widehat{u}_{\varepsilon}-\nabla\widehat{u}_{\varepsilon}^{\rm app}|^{2}+CL^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\varepsilon_{k}^{-2\tau}\varepsilon_{n}^{2\tau}\int_{B_{1+\varepsilon_{n}}}|\nabla\widehat{u}_{\varepsilon}^{\rm app}|^{2}\\
&\leq CL^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\varepsilon_{k}^{-2\tau}\varepsilon_{n}^{2\tau}\bigg\{\int_{B_{2}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\int_{B_{2}}|F|^{2}\bigg\}.\end{split}

It follows that

(3.47)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{5/4}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}-\nabla\widehat{u}_{\varepsilon}-S_{\varepsilon_{n}}((\nabla_{y}\chi)^{\varepsilon}\nabla\widehat{u}_{\varepsilon})|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\big\{\varepsilon_{n}^{1-\frac{2}{q}}+L^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\varepsilon_{k}^{-2\tau}\varepsilon_{n}^{2\tau}\big\}\bigg\{\int_{B_{2}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\int_{B_{2}}|F|^{2}\bigg\}.

Recall that
\varepsilon_{n}
is
Q
-separated from
\varepsilon_{k},k=1,\cdots,n-1
, i.e.,
\varepsilon_{k}/\varepsilon_{n}\geq Q
for
k=1,\cdots,n-1.

In view of (
2.9
) and (
2.11
),
the desired estimate (
3.19
) follows immediately from (
3.47
), while the estimate (
3.18
) follows directly from (
3.45
). The proof is complete.
∎

4.
The uniform Calderón-Zygmund estimates

In this section we prove the uniform Calderón-Zygmund estimate for the equation (
1.1
). Let us first consider the interior estimate.

Theorem 4.1
.

Assume
A
satisfies the assumptions (
1.2
), (
1.3
) and (
1.4
). Let
u_{\varepsilon}
be a weak solution to

(4.1)

\displaystyle-\text{\rm div}\big(A(x/\varepsilon_{1},x/\varepsilon_{2},\cdots,x/\varepsilon_{n})\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\big)=\text{\rm div}f\quad\text{in }5B_{0},

with
f\in L^{p}(5B_{0})^{d}
for some
2<p<\infty
, where
B_{0}=B(x_{0},r_{0})
for some
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},r_{0}>0
. Then
\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\in L^{p}(B_{0})^{d}
and

(4.2)

\displaystyle\bigg(\fint_{B_{0}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}\leq C\bigg\{\bigg(\fint_{5B_{0}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+\bigg(\fint_{5B_{0}}|f|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}\bigg\},

where
C
depends only on
d,\Lambda,p,n,
and
(\tau,L)
in (
1.4
).

The proof of Theorem
4.1
relies on a real-variable argument (a quantitative refined version of the one in
[
CP98
]
) involving a double-averaging estimate. We define the averaging operator as

(4.3)

M_{t}[f](x):=\bigg(\fint_{B_{t}(x)}|f|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}.

We need the averaging operator in the real-variable argument for two reasons. The first reason is that the approximation in (
3.14
) is meaningful only if
r\gg Q^{n-1}\varepsilon_{n}
for sufficiently large
Q\gg 1
. This forces us to take the large-scale averaging estimate above the scale
t=CQ^{n-1}\varepsilon_{n}
for sufficiently large
C
and ignore the possible irregularity below that scale. The second reason to introduce another averaging is due to the special structure of the corrector
\mathcal{X}^{\sharp}
in
U_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}
given by (
3.15
). Recall that
\mathcal{X}^{\sharp}=\mathcal{X}^{\sharp}(y_{1},y_{2},\cdots,y_{n})
is 1-periodic in each
y_{i}
. But its gradient in
y_{n}
does not have an
L^{p}
estimate uniformly in
Q
before we prove it in case of
n
oscillating scales (otherwise we will run into a circular reasoning), which causes a big problem in the real-variable argument. To resolve this issue, we take the averaging for another time at scale
t=\varepsilon^{\prime}_{n}=q\varepsilon_{n}
, a scale comparable to the size of the periodic cell (depending on
Q
as
1<q\leq Q^{n-1}
). This allows us to use only the periodic structure of
\mathcal{X}^{\sharp}
and the
L^{2}
energy estimate (independent of
Q
). Overall, the principle is that we have to make sure all the constants, except for
\eta
, in the real-variable argument (Theorem
4.2
below) are independent of
Q
.

Theorem 4.2
.

[
She23
, Theorem 2.1 and Remark 2.4]

Let
p_{0}>2
,
\mathcal{F}\in L^{2}(4B_{0})
and
G\in L^{p}(4B_{0})
for some
2<p<p_{0}
, where
B_{0}
is a ball in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Let
0\leq t<c_{0}\text{diam}(B_{0})
be a fixed
number. Suppose that for each ball

with the property that
t<\text{diam}(B)\leq c_{0}\text{diam}(B_{0})
, there exists two measurable functions
\mathcal{F}_{B}
and
\mathcal{R}_{B}
on
2B
such that
|\mathcal{F}|\leq|\mathcal{F}_{B}|+|\mathcal{R}_{B}|
on
2B
, and

(4.4)

\displaystyle\bigg(\fint_{2B}|\mathcal{R}_{B}|^{p_{0}}\bigg)^{1/p_{0}}\leq C_{1}\bigg\{\bigg(\fint_{4B}|\mathcal{F}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+\sup_{B\subset B^{\prime}\subset 4B_{0}}\bigg(\fint_{B^{\prime}}|G|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg\},

(4.5)

\displaystyle\bigg(\fint_{2B}|\mathcal{F}_{B}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\leq C_{2}\sup_{B\subset B^{\prime}\subset 4B_{0}}\bigg(\fint_{B^{\prime}}|G|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+\eta\bigg(\fint_{4B}|\mathcal{F}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2},

where
C_{1},C_{2}>0,0<c_{0}<1
, and
\eta\geq 0
. Then there exists
\eta_{0}>0
, depending only on
C_{1},C_{2},c_{0},p,p_{0},

with the property that if
0\leq\eta<\eta_{0}
, then

(4.6)

\displaystyle\bigg(\fint_{B_{0}}M_{t}[\mathcal{F}]^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}\leq C\bigg\{\bigg(\fint_{4B_{0}}|\mathcal{F}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+\bigg(\fint_{4B_{0}}|G|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}\bigg\},

where
C
depends only on
d,C_{1},C_{2},c_{0},p
and
p_{0}
. If
t=0
, then (

) is replaced by

(4.7)

\displaystyle\bigg(\fint_{B_{0}}|\mathcal{F}|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}\leq C\bigg\{\bigg(\fint_{4B_{0}}|\mathcal{F}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+\bigg(\fint_{4B_{0}}|G|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}\bigg\}.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Let us first consider the case
f=0
.
We use an inductive argument on the number of scales to prove the theorem. Note that the result is well-known for
n=1
(see e.g.,
[
She18
]
).
Assume the theorem is true if the number of scales is strictly less than
n
, which means that for any
\infty>\varepsilon_{1}\geq\varepsilon_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\varepsilon_{n-1}>0
, the solution
v_{\varepsilon}
to

-\text{\rm div}\big(A(x/\varepsilon_{1},\cdots,x/\varepsilon_{n-1})\nabla v_{\varepsilon}\big)=0\quad\text{in }B_{2r}

satisfies the uniform interior
W^{1,p}
estimate

(4.8)

\displaystyle\bigg(\fint_{B_{3r/2}}|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}\leq C_{p}\bigg(\fint_{B_{2r}}|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

for any
0<r<\infty
and any
2<p<\infty
.

We next prove the theorem for
n
scales by using Theorem
4.2
with
\mathcal{F}:=M_{\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime}}[\nabla u_{\varepsilon}]
with
\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime}=q\varepsilon_{n}
given in (
3.21
). Fix any
p\in(2,\infty)
and
p_{1}\in(2,p)
.

Let
Q>1
to be determined later, and
\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime}=q\varepsilon_{n}\leq Q^{n-1}\varepsilon_{n}<r<c_{0}r_{0}
for some fixed
c_{0}\in(0,1)
. For each ball
B_{r}=B_{r}(x)
such that
B_{r}\subset 2B_{0}=B(x_{0},2r_{0})
, we need to construct
\mathcal{F}_{B}
and
\mathcal{R}_{B}
, which satisfy (
4.4
) and (
4.5
), respectively.
Since
u_{\varepsilon}
is a solution to

-\text{\rm div}\big(A(x/\varepsilon_{1},\cdots,x/\varepsilon_{n})\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\big)=0
in
B_{4r}\subset 4B_{0}
,
by Theorem
3.2
, there exist a 1-periodic matrix
A^{\flat}=A^{\flat}(y_{1},y_{2},\cdots,y_{n-1})
, scales
\varepsilon^{\prime}=(\varepsilon_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,\varepsilon_{n-1}^{\prime})
, and a solution
u_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}^{\flat}(x)
to

(4.9)

\displaystyle-\text{\rm div}\big(A^{\flat}(x/\varepsilon^{\prime}_{1},\cdots,x/\varepsilon^{\prime}_{n-1})\nabla u_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}^{\flat}\big)=0\quad\text{in }B_{2r},

such that

(4.10)

\displaystyle\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}^{\flat}\|_{L^{2}(B_{2r})}\leq C\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(B_{4r})},

and

(4.11)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}-\nabla u_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}^{\flat}-U_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}\|_{L^{2}(B_{2r})}\leq C\bigg\{Q^{-\tau}+\bigg(\frac{Q^{n-1}\varepsilon_{n}}{r}\bigg)^{\sigma}\bigg\}\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(B_{4r})},\end{split}

where
U_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}
is given by (
3.15
).
Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{B}(x)=M_{\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime}}[\nabla u_{\varepsilon}-\nabla u_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}^{\flat}-U_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}](x)\,\,\text{ and }\,\,\mathcal{R}_{B}(x)=M_{\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime}}[\nabla u_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}^{\flat}+U_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}](x).

By the triangle inequality, we have
|\mathcal{F}|\leq|\mathcal{F}_{B}|+|\mathcal{R}_{B}|.

We recall a basic property of the averaging operator
M_{t}
. If
F\in L^{2}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then for any
r\geq t>0
, we have

(4.12)

\fint_{B_{r}}M_{t}[F]^{2}\leq C\fint_{B_{r+t}}|F|^{2}\leq C\fint_{B_{2r}}|F|^{2}\leq C\fint_{B_{2r}}M_{t}[F]^{2}.

This can be shown easily by the Fubini’s theorem.

Consequently, by the assumption
r>\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime}
, we have

(4.13)

\fint_{B_{r}}|\mathcal{F}_{B}|^{2}\leq C\fint_{B_{2r}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}-\nabla u_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}^{\flat}-U_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}|^{2},

and

(4.14)

\fint_{B_{4r}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\leq C\fint_{B_{4r}}|\mathcal{F}|^{2}.

Combining the last two inequalities with (
4.11
), we have

(4.15)

\displaystyle\fint_{B_{r}}|\mathcal{F}_{B}|^{2}\leq C\bigg\{Q^{-\tau}+\bigg(\frac{Q^{n-1}\varepsilon_{n}}{r}\bigg)^{\sigma}\bigg\}^{2}\fint_{B_{4r}}|\mathcal{F}|^{2}.

We now choose
Q=Q_{0}>1
large enough such that
CQ_{0}^{-\tau}<\eta_{0}/2
, and then take
r>k_{0}Q_{0}^{n-1}\varepsilon_{n}
for some
k_{0}
large enough such that
CQ_{0}^{(n-1)\sigma}(\varepsilon_{n}/r)^{\sigma}<\eta_{0}/2,
where
\eta_{0}
is given by Theorem
4.2
depending only on
C_{1},C_{2},c_{0},p,p_{1}.

It follows that

\mathcal{F}_{B}
satisfies condition (
4.5
) for
r>k_{0}Q_{0}^{n-1}\varepsilon_{n}
with
C_{2}=0
. In the following, we will verify that
\mathcal{R}_{B}
satisfies (
4.4
) and the constant
C_{1}
is independent of
Q
.

Note that

(4.16)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\fint_{B_{r}}|\mathcal{R}_{B}|^{p}&=\fint_{B_{r}}M_{\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime}}[\nabla u_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}^{\flat}+U_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}]^{p}\\
&\leq 2^{p-1}\fint_{B_{r}}|M_{\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime}}[\nabla u_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}^{\flat}](z)|^{p}dz+2^{p-1}\fint_{B_{r}}|M_{\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime}}[U_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}](z)|^{p}dz.\end{split}

Since
u_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}^{\flat}
satisfies (
4.9
), and the coefficient matrix
A^{\flat}
involves at most
n-1
scales and is periodic and Hölder continuous
y_{i}=x/\varepsilon_{i}^{\prime}
for
i=1,\cdots,n-1
. By the inductive assumption, we know that
u_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}^{\flat}
satisfies (
4.8
),
which combined with (
4.10
) implies

\displaystyle\bigg(\fint_{B_{3r/2}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}^{\flat}|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}\leq C\bigg(\fint_{B_{4r}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2},

for any
k_{0}\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime}\leq k_{0}Q_{0}^{n-1}\varepsilon_{n}\leq r\leq c_{0}r_{0}
.
By the Hölder’s inequality and Fubini’s theorem,

(4.17)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\fint_{B_{r}}|M_{\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime}}[\nabla u_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}^{\flat}](z)|^{p}dz&\leq\fint_{B_{r}}\fint_{B_{\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime}}(z)}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}^{\flat}(y)|^{p}dydz\\
&\leq C\fint_{B_{3r/2}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}^{\flat}(y)|^{p}dy\\
&\leq C\bigg(\fint_{B_{4r}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{p/2}\\
&\leq C\bigg(\fint_{B_{4r}}|\mathcal{F}|^{2}\bigg)^{p/2},\end{split}

where we have used (
4.12
) in the last step.

To bound the second term on the right hand side of (
4.16
), we have to take advantage of the periodic structure of
\mathcal{X}^{\sharp}
and it is right here that the averaging operator
M_{\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime}}
plays a key role.
We observe that, for each
y\in B_{r}
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}|M_{\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime}}[U_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}](y)|^{2}&=\fint_{B_{\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime}}(y)}\bigg|(\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime})^{-d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi\bigg(\frac{x-z}{\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime}}\bigg)(\nabla_{y}\mathcal{X}^{\sharp})\bigg(\frac{z}{\varepsilon_{1}^{\prime}},\cdots,\frac{z}{\varepsilon^{\prime}_{n-1}},\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime}}\bigg)\nabla u_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}^{\flat}(z)dz\bigg|^{2}dx\\
&\leq\fint_{B_{\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime}}(y)}(\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime})^{-d}\int_{B_{2\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime}}(y)}\varphi\bigg(\frac{x-z}{\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime}}\bigg)\bigg|(\nabla_{y}\mathcal{X}^{\sharp})\bigg(\frac{z}{\varepsilon_{1}^{\prime}},\cdots,\frac{z}{\varepsilon^{\prime}_{n-1}},\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime}}\bigg)\nabla u_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}^{\flat}(z)\bigg|^{2}dzdx\\
&\leq\fint_{B_{2\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime}}(y)}(\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime})^{-d}\int_{B_{\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime}}(y)}\varphi\bigg(\frac{x-z}{\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime}}\bigg)\bigg|(\nabla_{y}\mathcal{X}^{\sharp})\bigg(\frac{z}{\varepsilon_{1}^{\prime}},\cdots,\frac{z}{\varepsilon^{\prime}_{n-1}},\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime}}\bigg)\bigg|^{2}dx|\nabla u_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}^{\flat}(z)|^{2}dz\\
&\leq C\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\fint_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\bigg|(\nabla_{y}\mathcal{X}^{\sharp})\bigg(\frac{z}{\varepsilon_{1}^{\prime}},\cdots,\frac{z}{\varepsilon^{\prime}_{n-1}},y\bigg)\bigg|^{2}dy\fint_{B_{2\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime}}(y)}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}^{\flat}(z)|^{2}dz\\
&\leq C\fint_{B_{2\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime}}(y)}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}^{\flat}(z)|^{2}dz=C|M_{2\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime}}[\nabla u_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}^{\flat}](y)|^{2},\end{split}

where we have used Cauchy-Schwarz inequality in the first inequality (splitting a half of
(\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime})^{-d}\varphi(\frac{x-z}{\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime}})
whose
L^{2}
integral is 1), Fubini’s theorem in the second inequality, boundedness of
\varphi
and 1-periodicity of
\mathcal{X}^{\sharp}
in the third inequality (as well as Hölder’s inequality in

variable), and the
L^{2}
energy estimate for
\mathcal{X}^{\sharp}
in a periodic cell (see the equation (
2.10
)) in the last inequality. The constant
C
in the above inequality depends only on

and
\Lambda
.
This combined with (
4.17
) implies that

(4.18)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \fint_{B_{r}}|M_{\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime}}[U_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}](z)|^{p}&\leq C\fint_{B_{r}}|M_{2\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime}}[\nabla u_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}^{\flat}](y)|^{p}dy\\
&\leq C\fint_{B_{4r/3}}|M_{\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime}}[\nabla u_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}^{\flat}](y)|^{p}dy\leq C\bigg(\fint_{B_{4r}}|\mathcal{F}|^{2}\bigg)^{p/2},\end{aligned}

for any
k_{0}Q_{0}^{n-1}\varepsilon_{n}\leq r<c_{0}r_{0}
. We point out that we cannot simply take the

norm of

directly since

depends on

in

caused by reperiodization.

By taking (

) and (
4.18
) into (

), we find that
\mathcal{R}_{B}
satisfies (
4.4
) for
k_{0}Q_{0}^{n-1}\varepsilon_{n}<r<c_{0}r_{0}
(without the
G
part) with
C_{1}
independent of
Q
. Thus the conditions of Theorem
4.2
are all satisfied.
Thanks to (
4.6
), we obtain that

(4.19)

\displaystyle\bigg(\fint_{B_{0}}M_{\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime\prime}}[M_{\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime}}[\nabla u_{\varepsilon}]]^{p_{1}}\bigg)^{1/{p_{1}}}\leq C\bigg(\fint_{4B_{0}}|M_{\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime}}[\nabla u_{\varepsilon}](x)|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2},

where
\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime}=q\varepsilon_{n}
and
\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime\prime}=k_{0}Q_{0}^{n-1}\varepsilon_{n}
and
p_{1}\in(2,p)
. This is a double-averaging estimate since we have taken average twice in the
L^{p}
norm on the left-hand side. At this stage (after using Theorem
4.2
), it is safe to remove one averaging in (
4.19
) by (
4.12
). In fact, since
\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime\prime}>\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime}
, (
4.19
) implies

(4.20)

\bigg(\fint_{B_{0}}M_{2\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime\prime}}[\nabla u_{\varepsilon}]^{p_{1}}\bigg)^{1/{p_{1}}}\leq C\bigg(\fint_{5B_{0}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}.

Next, we claim that for each
y\in B_{0}
, we have

(4.21)

|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(y)|\leq CM_{2\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime\prime}}[\nabla u_{\varepsilon}](y)=C\bigg(\fint_{B_{2\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime\prime}}(y)}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}.

This pointwise Lipschitz estimate follows from a well-known blow-up argument at small scales and the fact
\varepsilon_{n}^{\prime\prime}=k_{0}Q_{0}^{n-1}\varepsilon_{n}
. Inserting this into (
4.20
), we arrive at

(4.22)

\bigg(\fint_{B_{0}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{p_{1}}\bigg)^{1/p_{1}}\leq C\bigg(\fint_{5B_{0}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}.

Since
p_{1}\in(2,p)
and
p\in(2,\infty)
are arbitrary with
C
depending on
p,p_{1}
, we actually have shown the uniform interior
W^{1,p}
estimate for any
p\in(2,\infty)
in the case of
f=0
.

Finally, we consider general case with
f\in L^{p}(5B_{0})^{d}
. Let
u_{\varepsilon}
be a solution to (
4.1
).
For each ball
B=B_{r}
such that
4B\subset 2B_{0}
, let
u_{\varepsilon,1}\in H^{1}_{0}(4B)
be the solution to

(4.23)

\displaystyle-\text{\rm div}\big(A(x/\varepsilon_{1},x/\varepsilon_{2},\cdots,x/\varepsilon_{n})\nabla u_{\varepsilon,1}\big)=\text{div }f\,\text{ in }4B,

and
u_{\varepsilon,2}
the solution to

(4.24)

\displaystyle-\text{\rm div}\big(A(x/\varepsilon_{1},x/\varepsilon_{2},\cdots,x/\varepsilon_{n})\nabla u_{\varepsilon,2}\big)=0\,\text{ in }4B,\,\text{ and }\,u_{\varepsilon,2}=u_{\varepsilon}\,\text{ on }\partial(4B).

Thus,
u_{\varepsilon}=u_{\varepsilon,1}+u_{\varepsilon,2}
in
4B
.
The energy estimate for (
4.23
) implies that

\bigg(\fint_{4B}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon,1}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\leq C\bigg(\fint_{4B}|f|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2},

while the uniform
W^{1,p}
estimates for
u_{\varepsilon,2}
proved above implies that

\displaystyle\bigg(\fint_{2B}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon,2}|^{p_{0}}\bigg)^{1/p_{0}}

\displaystyle\leq C\bigg(\fint_{4B}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon,2}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\bigg(\fint_{4B}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+C\bigg(\fint_{4B}|f|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

for some
p_{0}\in(p,\infty)
.
Let
\mathcal{F}=|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|
,
\mathcal{F}_{B}=|\nabla u_{\varepsilon,1}|
, and
\mathcal{R}_{B}=|\nabla u_{\varepsilon,2}|
. We obtain (
4.2
) from Theorem
4.2
immediately (with
t=0
).
∎

Theorem
4.1
provides the uniform interior
W^{1,p}
estimate for the equation (
1.1
).
Based on Theorem
3.6
and a boundary version of Theorem
4.2
(See Theorem 4.1 and Remark 4.2 in
[
She23
]
.), we can follow the same arguments to prove the following uniform boundary
W^{1,p}
estimate.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded
C^{1}
domain. There exists
r_{0}=r_{0}(\Omega)
such that the following statement is true. Let
B=B(x,r)
with

,

. Assume

), (

) and (

be a weak solution to

(4.25)

\displaystyle-\text{\rm div}\big(A(x/\varepsilon_{1},\cdots,x/\varepsilon_{n})\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\big)=\text{\rm div}f\,\text{ in }B_{2r}\cap\Omega,\quad u_{\varepsilon}=0\,\text{ on }B_{2r}\cap\partial\Omega,

with

5.
Large-scale Lipschitz estimates

In this section, we investigate the large-scale Lipschitz estimates for (
1.1
).
We distinguish between the cases of two scales and more scales. We recall that under the scale-separation condition: there exists a positive integer
N
such that

(5.1)

\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{i+1}}{\varepsilon_{i}}\right)^{N}\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{i}}{\varepsilon_{i-1}}\quad\text{ for }1\leq i\leq n-1,

the large-scale Lipschitz estimate for the multiscale elliptic operators has been derived in
[
NSX20
]
.
Indeed, let
u_{\varepsilon}
be the weak solution to

-\text{\rm div}(A(x,x/\varepsilon_{1},\cdots,x/\varepsilon_{n})\nabla u_{\varepsilon})=F\quad\text{ in }B_{1}

with
F\in L^{p}(B_{1})
for some
p>d
.
Suppose
A(x,y_{1},\cdots,y_{n})
is strongly elliptic, periodic in
y_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq n
, and
Hölder continuous in
x,y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-1}
(no smoothness is needed for
y_{n}
). Then, under the condition (
5.1
), for any
0<\varepsilon_{n}\leq r<1
,

(5.2)

\displaystyle\bigg(\fint_{B_{r}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\leq C\bigg\{\bigg(\fint_{B_{1}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+\bigg(\fint_{B_{1}}|F|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}\bigg\},

where
C
is independent of
\varepsilon
.

However, as we have stated before, without any scale separation condition the full-scale uniform Lipschitz estimate for (
1.1
) seems difficult and still remains open. The following theorems provide suboptimal results in this direction using the idea of scale separation similar to Section
3.1
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Assume
A(y_{1},y_{2})
satisfies (
1.2
), (
1.3
) and (
3.9
) with
n=2
. For
B_{1}=B_{1}(0)
, let
u_{\varepsilon}
be a weak solution of
-\text{\rm div}(A_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon})=F
in
B_{1}
with
F\in L^{p}(B_{1})
for some
p>d
. Then for any
\alpha>0
, and any
\varepsilon_{2}^{1-\alpha}\leq r\leq 1
, we have

(5.3)

\bigg(\fint_{B_{r}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\leq C_{\alpha}\bigg\{\bigg(\fint_{B_{1}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+\bigg(\fint_{B_{1}}|F|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}\bigg\},

where
C_{\alpha}
depends only on
d,\Lambda,p,\alpha,
and
(\tau,L)
in (
3.9
).

Proof.

Applying the argument in Section
3.1
and by (
3.7
), for any
Q>1
we can find a 1-periodic matrix
A^{\sharp}(y_{1},y_{2})
, Hölder continuous in
y_{1}
, and write

(5.4)

A\big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{1}},\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{2}}\big)=A^{\sharp}\big(\frac{\gamma_{1}x}{\varepsilon_{2}},\frac{x}{q\varepsilon_{2}}\big)=A^{\sharp}\big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{1}^{\prime}},\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{2}^{\prime}}\big),

where
1\leq q\leq Q,\varepsilon_{1}^{\prime}=\varepsilon_{2}/\gamma_{1}
and
\varepsilon_{2}^{\prime}=q\varepsilon_{2}
. Let
Q=\varepsilon_{2}^{-\alpha}
. Then
\varepsilon_{2}\leq\varepsilon_{2}^{\prime}=q\varepsilon_{2}\leq\varepsilon_{2}^{1-\alpha}
and
\varepsilon_{1}^{\prime}/\varepsilon_{2}^{\prime}\geq Q=\varepsilon_{2}^{-\alpha}
. Thus
\varepsilon_{2}^{\prime}
and
\varepsilon_{1}^{\prime}
are well-separated. Moreover,
u_{\varepsilon}
satisfies the equation

(5.5)

-\text{\rm div}(A^{\sharp}(x/\varepsilon_{1}^{\prime},x/\varepsilon_{2}^{\prime})\nabla u_{\varepsilon})=F\qquad\text{in }B_{1}.

Thanks to (
5.2
), we have for
\varepsilon_{2}^{\prime}\leq r\leq 1
,

(5.6)

\bigg(\fint_{B_{r}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\leq C_{\alpha}\bigg\{\bigg(\fint_{B_{1}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+\bigg(\fint_{B_{1}}|F|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}\bigg\}.

This implies the desired estimate since
\varepsilon_{2}^{\prime}\leq\varepsilon_{2}^{1-\alpha}
.
∎

Theorem 5.2
.

Assume
A(y_{1},\cdots,y_{n})
satisfies (
1.2
), (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) with

n\geq 3
. Let
u_{\varepsilon}
be a weak solution of
-\text{\rm div}(A_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon})=F
in
B_{1}
with
F\in L^{p}(B_{1})
for some
p>d
. Then for any
\alpha\in(0,1/n]
and
\delta\in[\varepsilon_{n},1)
, there exists
1\leq q\leq\delta^{-n\alpha}
, such that for any
q\delta\leq r\leq r_{1}:=\min\{q\delta^{1-\alpha},1\}
, we have

(5.7)

\bigg(\fint_{B_{r}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\leq C_{\alpha}\bigg\{\bigg(\fint_{B_{r_{1}}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+r_{1}^{1-d/p}\bigg(\fint_{B_{r_{1}}}|F|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}\bigg\},

where
C_{\alpha}
depends only on
d,n,\Lambda,p,\alpha
, and
(\tau,L)
in (
1.4
). Moreover, if
\delta=\varepsilon_{j}
for some
1\leq j\leq n
, then
\alpha
can be taken from
(0,1/(n-1)]
and
1\leq q\leq\delta^{-(n-1)\alpha}
.

Proof.

Fix
\delta\in[\varepsilon_{n},1)
. Let
Q=\delta^{-\alpha}>1
with
\alpha\in(0,1/n]
. By the Dirichlet’s theorem, there exist
q,p_{1},p_{2},\cdots,p_{n}
such that
1<q\leq Q^{n}=\delta^{-n\alpha}
and

(5.8)

\big|\frac{\delta}{\varepsilon_{i}}-\frac{p_{i}}{q}\big|\leq\frac{1}{qQ},

where
i=1,2,\cdots,n
. As in Section
3.1
, we set

\gamma_{i}:=\Big|\frac{\delta}{\varepsilon_{i}}-\frac{p_{i}}{q}\Big|\quad\text{and}\quad s_{i}=\text{sgn}\Big(\frac{\delta}{\varepsilon_{i}}-\frac{p_{i}}{q}\Big),

and write

\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{i}}=\frac{\gamma_{i}s_{i}}{\delta}+\frac{p_{i}}{q}\frac{1}{\delta}.

Define

(5.9)

A_{1}^{\sharp}(y_{1},y_{2},\cdots,y_{n},y_{n+1})=A(s_{1}y_{1}+p_{1}y_{n+1},\cdots,s_{n}y_{n}+p_{n}y_{n+1}).

As a consequence, we have

(5.10)

A\big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{1}},\cdots,\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{n}}\big)=A_{1}^{\sharp}\big(\frac{\gamma_{1}x}{\delta},\cdots,\frac{\gamma_{n}x}{\delta},\frac{x}{q\delta}\big).

The last identity shows that we can rewrite original coefficient matrix
A
with
n
scales as a new 1-periodic matrix
A_{1}^{\sharp}
with
n+1
scales. Moreover, the smallest scale
q\delta
is at least
Q
-separated from the remaining
n
scales, i.e.,

(5.11)

\frac{\delta/\gamma_{i}}{q\delta}\geq Q.

Hence, by a blow-up argument and (
5.2
) we have

(5.12)

\bigg(\fint_{B_{r}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\leq C_{\alpha}\bigg\{\bigg(\fint_{B_{r_{1}}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}+r_{1}^{1-d/p}\bigg(\fint_{B_{r_{1}}}|F|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}\bigg\}

for any
r
with
q\delta\leq r\leq r_{1}\leq\min\{Qq\delta,1\}
.
This is the desired estimate. To make sure
q\delta<1
, we require
q\delta\leq Q^{n}\delta=\delta^{1-n\alpha}<1
which gives
\alpha\leq 1/n
.

Finally, in the particular case
\delta=\varepsilon_{j}
for some
1\leq j\leq n
,
\delta/\varepsilon_{j}=1
leads to a trivial approximation in (
5.8
). Thus we will only need (
5.8
) for
i\neq j
and
1\leq q\leq Q^{n-1}
. The rest of the proof follows similarly.
∎

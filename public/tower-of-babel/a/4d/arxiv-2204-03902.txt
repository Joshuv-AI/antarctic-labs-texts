Minimal subsystems of given mean dimension in Bernstein spaces
Jianjie Zhao

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the shift on the space of uniformly bounded continuous functions band-limited in a given compact interval with the standard topology of tempered distributions. We give a constructive proof of the existence of minimal subsystems with any given mean dimension strictly less than twice its band-width. A version of real-valued function spaces is considered as well.

Introduction

Mean dimension is a topological invariant of dynamical systems introduced by Gromov
[
3
]
in 1999, which counts the number of parameters per second for describing a dynamical system just as topological entropy counts the number of bits per second for describing a dynamical system. Lindenstrauss and Weiss
[
9
]
firstly established a deep application of mean dimension by solving Auslander’s open problem in the negative: Whether every minimal dynamical system can be embedded

1

We say that a dynamical system can be
embedded
into another dynamical system if the first system can be regarded as a subsystem of the second one.
into the full shift on the Hilbert cube
([0,1]^{\mathbb{Z}}),\sigma)
, where

\sigma:[0,1]^{\mathbb{Z}}\to[0,1]^{\mathbb{Z}},\;\;\;(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mapsto(x_{n+1})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}.

Soon after, Lindenstrauss
[
7
]
proved that any minimal dynamical system of mean dimension less than
c
for
c={1}/{36}
can be embedded into the full shift on the Hilbert cube. This is a fairly amazing result. A natural question that was also posed by Lindenstrauss in
[
7
]
is what the optimal constant
c
is. In 2014, Lindenstauss and Tsukamoto
[
8
]
constructed a minimal dynamical system of mean dimension equal to
1/2
that cannot be embedded into the full shift on the Hilbert cube. This implies that the optimal constant
c
will not exceed
1/2
. In 2020, Gutman and Tsukamoto
[
2
]
confirmed that the value
c=1/2
is optimal, that is, any minimal dynamical system of mean dimension less than
1/2
can be embedded into the full shift on the Hilbert cube. The statement of this profound result lies purely in abstract topological dynamics but the approach in
[
2
]
involved signal analysis (or Fourier analysis) and complex analysis theory. More precisely, they encoded a given dynamical system into time-continuous signals whose frequencies are limited in a fixed interval (i.e. band-limited) by interpolation and then converted band-limited continuous signals into time-discrete ones by sampling. A similar approach was employed in
[
4
]
to solve the more complicated embedding problem of multi-parameter actions as well. This motivates us to study the spaces of band-limited continuous signals, i.e. Bernstein spaces. Let us first give the definitions.

A
rapidly decreasing function
is an infinitely differentiable function
f
on
\mathbb{R}

satisfying

\lim\limits_{|t|\to\infty}t^{n}f^{(j)}(t)=0,\ \ \ \ \forall n,j\in\mathbb{N}.

A
tempered distribution
on
\mathbb{R}
is a continuous linear functional on the space of all rapidly decreasing functions equipped with the topology given by a family of seminorms as follows:

\left\|{f}\right\|_{j,n}=\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}|t^{n}f^{(j)}(t)|\ \ \ \ (j,n\in\mathbb{N}).

For a rapidly decreasing function
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
the Fourier transform
of
f
is given by

\mathcal{F}(f)(\xi)=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}e^{-2\pi\sqrt{-1}t\xi}f(t)dt,\ \ \overline{\mathcal{F}}(f)(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}t\xi}f(\xi)d\xi.

The operators
\mathcal{F}
and
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
can be extended to tempered distributions in a standard way.
Let
I
be a compact subset of
\mathbb{R}
.
A bounded continuous function
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
is called
band-limited in
I
if
{\rm supp}(\mathcal{F}(f))\subset I
,
meaning that
\left\langle{{\mathcal{F}(f),g}}\right\rangle=0
for all rapidly decreasing functions

g:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
with
{\rm supp}(g)\cap I=\emptyset
. We remark here that
{\rm supp}(\mathcal{F}(\bar{f}))=-{\rm supp}(\mathcal{F}(f))
.
We denote by
\mathcal{B}^{\mathbb{C}}(I)
(resp.
\mathcal{B}(I)
) the set of continuous functions
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
(resp.
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
) band-limited in
I
with
\left\|{f}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leq 1
and call it a
Bernstein space
. For two functions
f,g\in\mathcal{B}^{\mathbb{C}}(I)
(resp.
\mathcal{B}(I)
), we define

D(f,g)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\left\|{f-g}\right\|_{L^{\infty}([-n,n])}}{2^{n}}.

An important and nontrivial fact
[
2
, Lemma 2.3]

[
10

is that both
\mathcal{B}^{\mathbb{C}}(I)
and
\mathcal{B}(I)
are compact metric spaces with respect to the distance
D
which coincides with the standard topology of tempered distributions. We define

\sigma:\mathcal{B}^{\mathbb{C}}(I)\to\mathcal{B}^{\mathbb{C}}(I),\ \ \ g(\cdot)\mapsto g(\cdot+1)

and

\sigma:\mathcal{B}(I)\to\mathcal{B}(I),\ \ \ g(\cdot)\mapsto g(\cdot+1).

Clearly, both
\mathcal{B}^{\mathbb{C}}(I)
and
\mathcal{B}(I)
are
\sigma

We consider the dynamical system
(\mathcal{B}^{\mathbb{C}}(I),\sigma)
(resp.
(\mathcal{B}(I),\sigma)
)
and call this system
the shift on

\mathcal{B}^{\mathbb{C}}(I)
(resp.
\mathcal{B}(I)
).

As the most important space of time-discrete signals, the system

Full shifts

By a

\sigma_{K}:K^{\mathbb{Z}}\to K^{\mathbb{Z}},\;\;\;(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mapsto(x_{n+1})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}.

We fix a compatible metric
D_{1}
on

as follows:

D_{1}(x,y)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{d(x_{n},y_{n})}{2^{|n|}},\;\;x=(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}},\;y=(y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in K^{\mathbb{Z}}.

For
x=(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in K^{\mathbb{Z}}
and two integers

,
we set

x|_{m}^{m^{\prime}}=x|_{[m,m^{\prime}]}=(x_{n})_{m\leq n\leq m^{\prime}}.

2.2.
Mean dimension

Let
X
and
P
be two compact metrizable spaces and
d
a compatible metric on
X
.
For
\varepsilon>0
,
a continuous mapping
f:X\to P
is called an
\varepsilon
-
embedding
with respect to
d
if it satisfies

f(x)=f(x^{\prime})\Rightarrow d(x,x^{\prime})<\varepsilon,\ \ \forall x,x^{\prime}\in X.

We define Widim
{}_{\varepsilon}(X,d)
as the minimum topological dimension dim
(P)
of a compact metrizable space
P
which admits an

\varepsilon
-embedding
f:X\to P
with respect to
d
. We state a very practical result in the calculation of mean dimension of subshifts.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
3
, Lemma 1.1.1]
)
.

For any
\varepsilon>0
and any positive integer
l

\emph{Widim}_{\varepsilon}([0,1]^{l},||\cdot||_{\infty})=l.

Here
||\cdot||_{\infty}
is the compatible metric on
[0,1]^{l}
defined by

||(a_{n})_{n=0}^{l-1}-(b_{n})_{n=0}^{l-1}||_{\infty}=\max_{0\leq n\leq l-1}|a_{n}-b_{n}|,\;\;\;(a_{n})_{n=0}^{l-1},(b_{n})_{n=0}^{l-1}\in[0,1]^{l}.

Let
(X,T)
be a topological dynamical system with a compatible metric
d
on
X
.
For every positive integer
n
, we define a compatible metric
d_{n}
on
X
as follows:

d_{n}(x,x^{\prime})=\max_{0\leq i\leq n-1}d(T^{i}x,T^{i}x^{\prime}),\ \ \forall x,x^{\prime}\in X.

The
mean dimension
of
(X,T)
is defined by

\text{mdim}(X,T)=\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{\text{Widim}_{\varepsilon}(X,d_{n})}{n}.

It is well known that the limits above definition always exist, and the value mdim
(X,T)
is independent of the choice of a
compatible metric
d
on
X
.

C=\max_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\max_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{j=0}^{q-1}\frac{C_{1}}{1+(x-\frac{p}{q}(n+j)+i)^{2}}<\infty.

Define a mapping
G
from
\mathcal{B}^{\mathbb{C}}([a+\varepsilon_{0},b])\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}
: for all
(g,i)\in\mathcal{B}^{\mathbb{C}}([a+\varepsilon_{0},b])\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}
,

G(g(x),i)=\frac{g(x)+H(i)(x)}{2}\ \ \text{for all}\ \ x\in\mathbb{R},

where
H:\mathbb{Z}_{p}\to\mathcal{B}^{\mathbb{C}}([a,a+\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2}])
is defined by

i\mapsto H(i)(x)=e^{-2\pi\sqrt{-1}c(x+i)}
.
Clearly,
H
is continuous and injective.

Set

X=G\circ F(Y\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}).

Part 2.
(X,\sigma)
is a subsystem of
(\mathcal{B}^{\mathbb{C}}([a,b]),\sigma)
which is topologically conjugate to
(Y\times Z_{p},S)
.

It suffices to show that
F
and
G
are embeddings from
(Y\times\mathbb{Z}_{p},S)
to
(\mathcal{B}^{\mathbb{C}}([a+\varepsilon_{0},b])\times\mathbb{Z}_{p},T)
and from
(\mathcal{B}^{\mathbb{C}}([a+\varepsilon_{0},b])\times\mathbb{Z}_{p},T)
to
(\mathcal{B}^{\mathbb{C}}([a,b]),\sigma)
, respectively.

We first verify the embeddability of
F
. To show the continuity of
F
, we consider a compatible metric
\rho
on
Y\times Z_{p}
: for any
x,y\in Y
and
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
,

\rho((x,i),(y,j))=\begin{cases}D_{1}(x,y)&i=j\\
2&i\neq j\end{cases},

and a compatible metric
{\bf d}
on
\mathcal{B}^{\mathbb{C}}([a+\varepsilon_{0},b])\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}
: for any
g_{1},g_{2}\in\mathcal{B}^{\mathbb{C}}([a+\varepsilon_{0},b])
and
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
,

{\bf d}((g_{1},i),(g_{2},j))=\begin{cases}D(g_{1},g_{2})&i=j\\
\ \ \ \ 2&i\neq j\end{cases}.

Fix
(a,i)=((a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}},i)\in Y\times Z_{p}
.
Take a sequence

\{(a^{(m)},i_{m})=((a^{(m)}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}},i_{m})\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\subset Y\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}

with
\rho((a^{(m)},i_{m}),(a,i))\to 0
as
m\to\infty
. Without loss of generality, we may assume
i_{m}=i
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Put

F((a^{(m)},i))=(g^{(m)},i)\ \ \text{and}\ \ F((a,i))=(g,i)

for some
g^{(m)},g\in\mathcal{B}^{\mathbb{C}}([a+\varepsilon_{0},b])
.

Fix
N\in\mathbb{N}
. For any
\delta>0
, there exists
L\in\mathbb{N}
sufficiently large such that

\frac{1}{C}\sum_{|n|>L}\sum_{j=0}^{q-1}\frac{C_{1}}{1+(x-\frac{p}{q}(n+j)+i)^{2}}<\frac{\delta}{2q}\;\;\;(\forall i\in\mathbb{Z}_{p},\;x\in[-N,N]).

For such
L\in\mathbb{N}
, we take
M\in\mathbb{N}
sufficiently large satisfying

||a^{(m)}_{n}-a_{n}||_{{\infty}}<\frac{\delta}{2}\ \ (\forall n\in[-L,L],\;m\geq M),

thus

|(a_{n}^{(m)})^{j}-(a_{n})^{j}|_{2}<\frac{\delta}{2},\ \ \forall j\in\{0,1,\cdots,q-1\},n\in[-L,L],m\geq M,

where
a_{n}^{(m)}=((a_{n}^{(m)})^{j})_{j=0}^{q-1},a_{n}=(a_{n}^{j})_{j=0}^{q-1}\in K
.

We remark here that the distance
|\cdot|
on
\mathbb{C}
is defined by

|a+b\sqrt{-1}|=\max\{|a|,|b|\}
for all
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
.

Therefore we have for all
m\geq M
and
x\in[-N,N]
,

\displaystyle|g^{(m)}(x)-g(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{C}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{j=0}^{q-1}|(a_{n}^{(m)})^{j}-(a_{n})^{j}|_{2}\cdot|f(x-\frac{p}{q}(nq+j)+i)|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{C}\sum_{|n|\leq L}\sum_{j=0}^{q-1}|(a_{n}^{(m)})^{j}-(a_{n})^{j}|_{2}\cdot|f(x-\frac{p}{q}(nq+j)+i)|

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{C}\sum_{|n|>L}\sum_{j=0}^{q-1}|(a_{n}^{(m)})^{j}-(a_{n})^{j}|_{2}\cdot|f(x-\frac{p}{q}(nq+j)+i)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\delta}{2C}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{j=0}^{q-1}\frac{C_{1}}{1+(x-\frac{p}{q}(n+j)+i)^{2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{q}{C}\sum_{|n|>L}\sum_{j=0}^{q-1}\frac{C_{1}}{1+(x-\frac{p}{q}(n+j)+i)^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\delta}{2}+\frac{\delta}{2}=\delta.

This implies

\lim_{m\to\infty}\left\|g^{(m)}-g\right\|_{L^{\infty}([-N,N])}=0.

Since
N\in\mathbb{N}
is arbitrary, we deduce that

\lim_{m\to\infty}D(g^{m},g)=0

and hence

\lim_{m\to\infty}{\bf d}(F((a^{(m)},i)),F((a,i)))=0.

So
F
is continuous.

To prove the injectivity of
F
, we assume
F((a,i_{1}))=F((b,i_{2}))
for
(a,i_{1}),(b,i_{2})\in Y\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.
We assume that

a=(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}},b=(b_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}},a_{n}=(a_{n}^{j})_{j=0}^{q-1},b_{n}=(b_{n}^{j})_{j=0}^{q-1}

and

a_{n}^{j}=(a_{n}^{j,1},a_{n}^{j,2}),b_{n}^{j}=(b_{n}^{j,1},b_{n}^{j,2}).

It implies that

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{j=0}^{q-1}(a_{n}^{j,1}+a_{n}^{j,2}\sqrt{-1})f(x-\frac{p}{q}(nq+j)+i_{1})=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{j=0}^{q-1}(b_{n}^{j,1}+b_{n}^{j,2}\sqrt{-1})f(x-\frac{p}{q}(nq+j)+i_{2})

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
i_{1}=i_{2}
. For every
m\in\mathbb{Z}
and
j_{0}\in\{0,\cdots,q-1\}
, by letting
x=\frac{p}{q}(mq+j_{0})-i_{1}
in the above equality we have
a_{m}^{j_{0}}=b_{m}^{j_{0}}
and thus
a=b
. It is clear that
F\circ S=T\circ F
.
So
F
is an embedding from
(Y\times\mathbb{Z}_{p},S)
to
(\mathcal{B}^{\mathbb{C}}([a+\varepsilon_{0},b])\times\mathbb{Z}_{p},T)
.

Now we check the embeddability of
G
. Obviously,
G
is continuous and
G\circ T=\sigma\circ G
. Since for any
(g,i)\in\mathcal{B}^{\mathbb{C}}([a+\varepsilon_{0},b])\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}
,

g(x)\in\mathcal{B}^{\mathbb{C}}([a+\varepsilon_{0},b])\ \ \text{and}\ \ e^{-2\pi\sqrt{-1}cx}\in\mathcal{B}^{\mathbb{C}}([a,a+\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2}]),

we have that
G
is injective. Therefore,
G
is an embedding from
(\mathcal{B}^{\mathbb{C}}([a+\varepsilon_{0},b])\times\mathbb{Z}_{p},T)
to
(\mathcal{B}^{\mathbb{C}}([a,b]),\sigma)
.

Part 3. Minimality of
(X,\sigma)
.

Since the dynamical system
(X,\sigma)
is topologically conjugate to
(Y\times\mathbb{Z}_{p},S)
as proved in Part 2,
it is suffices to show that the system
(Y\times\mathbb{Z}_{p},S)
is minimal.

Fix
\widetilde{x}=(x,i_{1}),\widetilde{y}=(y,i_{2})\in Y\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and
\varepsilon>0
. We choose
L\in\mathbb{N}
depending only on
\varepsilon>0
such that if two points
a=(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}},b=(b_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
coming from
K^{\mathbb{Z}}
satisfy
||a_{n}-b_{n}||_{\infty}<\varepsilon/2
(
-L\leq n\leq L
), then

\rho\big((a,i),(b,i)\big)<\varepsilon

for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. We take
k\in\mathbb{N}
large enough such that
N_{k-1}>2L+1
and
2/k<\varepsilon
.

Case 1.
i_{1}=i_{2}
.
Set
i_{1}=i_{2}=i_{0}
.
Since
y\in Y\subset Y_{k}
, by the construction of
Y_{k}
we know that there exists
n_{1}\in\{0,1,\dots,N_{k}-1\}
such that

(y_{n_{1}+i+jN_{k}})_{i=0}^{N_{k}-1}\in B_{k}\ \ \ (j\in\mathbb{Z}).

Assume that
[-L,L]\subset[n_{1}+(j_{0}-1)N_{k},n_{1}+(j_{0}+1)N_{k}-1]
for some
j_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Since
x\in Y\subset Y_{k+1}
, there exists
n_{2}\in\{0,1,\dots,N_{k+1}-1\}
such that

(x_{n_{2}+i+jN_{k+1}})_{i=0}^{N_{k+1}-1}\in B_{k+1}\ \ \ (j\in\mathbb{Z}).

By the construction of
B_{k+1}
and noting the relation between
B_{k}
and
B_{k+1}
, we may find
M_{0}\in N_{k}\mathbb{Z}
with

||(\sigma^{M_{0}}x)_{n}-y_{n}||_{\infty}\leq\frac{1}{k}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\ \ \ \big(n\in\big\{n_{1}+j_{0}N_{k},\dots,n_{1}+(j_{0}+1)N_{k}-1)\big\}\big)

and

(\sigma^{M_{0}}x)_{n}=y_{n}\ \ \ \big(n\in\big\{n_{1}+(j_{0}-1)N_{k},\dots,n_{1}+j_{0}N_{k}-1\big\}\big).

Thus,

\rho((\sigma^{M_{0}}x,i_{0}),(y,i_{0}))<\varepsilon.

Observe that
S^{M_{0}p}(\widetilde{x})=(\sigma^{M_{0}}x,i_{0})
.
So

\rho(S^{M_{0}p}(\widetilde{x}),\widetilde{y})<\varepsilon.

Case 2.
i_{1}\neq i_{2}
.
We may reduce this case to Case 1 by taking some
M_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}
with

S^{M_{1}}\widetilde{x}\in Y\times\{i_{2}\}.

By Case 1, there exists
M_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

\rho(S^{M_{2}}(S^{M_{1}}\widetilde{x})),\widetilde{y})<\varepsilon.

we conclude that
(Y\times\mathbb{Z}_{p},S)
is minimal.

Part 4. The calculation of mean dimension of
(X,\sigma)
.

We are going to prove
\text{mdim}(X,\sigma)=s
, which is equivalent to
\text{mdim}(Y\times\mathbb{Z}_{p},S)=s
. We first show
\text{mdim}(Y\times\mathbb{Z}_{p},S)\geq s
.

We fix a point
z=(z_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in K^{\mathbb{Z}}
satisfying that
z|_{jN_{k}}^{(j+1)N_{k}-1}\in B_{k}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and all
j\in\mathbb{Z}
. For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
we define a mapping
F_{k}
as follows:

F_{k}:B_{k}\to Y\times\mathbb{Z}_{p},\ \ a=(a_{n})_{n=0}^{N_{k}-1}\mapsto((c_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}},0),

where

c_{n}=\begin{cases}a_{n},&n\in\{0,\dots,N_{k}-1\}\\
z_{n},&n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0,\dots,N_{k}-1\}.\\
\end{cases}

Clearly,
F_{k}(a)
(where
a\in B_{k}
) is indeed in
Y\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and
F_{k}
is continuous. We have the following result about
F_{k}
.

Claim 1
.

The mapping
F_{k}:(B_{k},||\cdot||_{\infty})\to(Y\times\mathbb{Z}_{p},\rho_{N_{k}p})
is distance-increasing, i.e. for all
a,b\in B_{k}

||a-b||_{\infty}\leq\rho_{N_{k}p}(F_{k}(a),F_{k}(b)).

Proof of Claim
1
.

Recall that

\rho_{N_{k}p}(\widetilde{a},\widetilde{b})=\max_{0\leq n\leq N_{k}p-1}\rho(S^{n}\widetilde{a},S^{n}\widetilde{b}),\ \ \widetilde{a},\widetilde{b}\in Y\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}.

Let
a,b\in B_{k}
. Assume that
F_{k}(a)=(c_{1},0)
and
F_{k}(b)=(c_{2},0)
for some
c_{1},c_{2}\in Y
. Then for any
0\leq n\leq N_{k}-1
, we have

\displaystyle\rho(S^{np}F_{k}(a),S^{np}F_{k}(b))

\displaystyle=D_{1}(\sigma^{n}c_{1},\sigma^{n}c_{2})

\displaystyle\geq||(\sigma^{n}c_{1})_{0}-(\sigma^{n}c_{2})_{0}||_{\infty}

\displaystyle=||a_{n}-b_{n}||_{\infty}.

Thus for any
a,b\in B_{k}
, we have

||a-b||_{\infty}\leq\rho_{N_{k}p}(F_{k}(a),F_{k}(b)).

It follows from the definition that for every
\varepsilon>0
and every
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\text{Widim}_{\varepsilon}(B_{k},||\cdot||_{\infty}))\leq\text{Widim}_{\varepsilon}(Y\times\mathbb{Z}_{p},\rho_{N_{k}p})

and hence

\text{Widim}_{\varepsilon}(Y\times\mathbb{Z}_{p},\rho_{N_{k}p})\geq 2q\cdot|x^{(k)}(N_{k},\ast)|>2q\cdot(rN_{k}).

by Lemma
2.1
and (
1
).
∎

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\text{mdim}(Y\times\mathbb{Z}_{p},S)=

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\frac{\text{Widim}_{\varepsilon}(Y\times\mathbb{Z}_{p},\rho_{N_{k}p})}{N_{k}p}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\frac{2qrN_{k}}{N_{k}p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2r\frac{q}{p}=s.

Now we prove
\text{mdim}(Y\times\mathbb{Z}_{p},S)\leq s
. For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we consider a mapping from
Y_{k}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}
to itself defined similar to
S
(see (
2
)) and denote it still by
S
for the saving of symbols. Since
(Y\times\mathbb{Z}_{p},S)
is a subsystem of
(Y_{k}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p},S)
for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, it suffices to show that

\text{mdim}(Y_{k}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p},S)\leq s+\epsilon_{k}\;\;\;(k\in\mathbb{N})

for some positive sequence
\{\epsilon_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
tending to
0
.

Fix
k\in\mathbb{N}
for the moment. Given
\varepsilon>0
. Choose
L\in\mathbb{N}
large enough such that if two points
a,b\in K^{\mathbb{Z}}
satisfy that
a|_{[-L,L]}=b|_{[-L,L]}
, then
\rho\big((a,i),(b,i)\big)<\varepsilon
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.

For each
m\in\mathbb{N}
, define

H_{k,m}:(Y_{k}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p},\rho_{mp})\to([0,1]^{2})^{[-L,L+m+1]}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p},\;\;\;(a,i)\mapsto(a|_{[-L,L+m+1]},i).

Claim 2
.

The mapping
H_{k,m}
defined above is an
\varepsilon
-embedding with respect to
\rho_{mp}
.

Proof of Claim
2
.

Assume that for some
(a,i),(b,j)\in Y_{k}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}
,
H_{k,m}(a,i)=H_{k,m}(b,j)
.
It follows from the definition that

a|_{[-L,L+m+1]}=b|_{[-L,L+m+1]}\;\;\;\text{and}\;\;\;i=j.

For any
0\leq l\leq mp-1
, we observe that

S^{l}(a,i)=(\sigma^{l^{\prime}}(a),l^{\prime\prime})\;\;\;\text{and}\;\;\;S^{l}(b,i)=(\sigma^{l^{\prime}}(b),l^{\prime\prime})

where
l+i=l^{\prime}p+l^{\prime\prime}
for some
l^{\prime}\in\{0,1,\dots,m+1\}
and
l^{\prime\prime}\in\{0,1,\dots,p-1\}
. Since
a|_{[-L,L+m+1]}=b|_{[-L,L+m+1]}
, we get by the choice of
L
that

\rho(S^{l}(a,i),S^{l}(b,j))=D_{1}(\sigma^{l^{\prime}}(a),\sigma^{l^{\prime}}(b))<\epsilon.

So

\rho_{mp}((a,i),(b,j))=\max_{0\leq l\leq mp-1}\rho(S^{l}(a,i),S^{l}(b,j))<\epsilon.

We conclude that
H_{k,m}
is an
\varepsilon
-embedding with respect
\rho_{mp}
.
∎

From Claim
2
, we have that for all
\epsilon>0
and all
k,m\in\mathbb{N}
,

\text{Widim}_{\varepsilon}(Y_{k}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p},\rho_{mp})\leq\text{Widim}_{\varepsilon}(H_{k,m}(Y_{k}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}))\leq\text{dim}(H_{k,m}(Y_{k}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}))

and the constructions of the set
Y_{k}
and the mapping
H_{k,m}
make sure that

\displaystyle\text{dim}(H_{k,m}(Y_{k}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p}))

\displaystyle\leq\left(\Big\lceil\frac{2L+m+2}{N_{k}}\Big\rceil+1\right)\cdot\text{dim}(B_{k})

\displaystyle\leq\left(\Big\lceil\frac{2L+m+2}{N_{k}}\Big\rceil+1\right)\cdot\left(2N_{k}q\left(r+\frac{1}{N_{k}}\right)\right),

where
\Big\lceil\frac{2L+m+2}{N_{k}}\Big\rceil
is the integer part of
\frac{2L+m+2}{N_{k}}
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\text{mdim}(Y_{k}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p},S)

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\lim\limits_{m\to\infty}\frac{\text{Widim}_{\varepsilon}(Y_{k}\times\mathbb{Z}_{p},\rho_{mp})}{mp}

\displaystyle\leq\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\lim\limits_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{mp}\cdot\left(\Big\lceil\frac{2L+m+2}{N_{k}}\Big\rceil+1\right)\cdot\left(2N_{k}q\left(r+\frac{1}{N_{k}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2q}{p}\cdot\left(r+\frac{1}{N_{k}}\right).

Letting
k\to\infty
,
We get that

\text{mdim}(Y\times Z_{p},S)\leq\frac{2qr}{p}=s.

So far, we have proved Theorem
1.1
(
1
).

∎

1.1
(
2
) and Theorem
1.2
based on Theorem
1.1
(
1
).

Proof of Theorem
1.1
(
2
).

Fix
0\leq t<2c
and choose
0<\varepsilon_{0}<c
with
t<2(c-\varepsilon_{0})
.
By Theorem
1.1
(
1
), there exists a minimal subsystem
(X_{1},\sigma)
of
\mathcal{B}^{\mathbb{C}}([\varepsilon_{0},c])
with mean dimension
t
.
Define

H:(\mathcal{B}^{\mathbb{C}}([\varepsilon_{0},c]),\sigma)\to(\mathcal{B}([-c,c]),\sigma),\ \ \ \ f\mapsto\frac{f+\bar{f}}{2}.

Clearly, the mapping
H
is continuous and

. The injectivity of
H
follows from the fact that
f-g=\overline{g-f}

(f,g\in\mathcal{B}^{\mathbb{C}}([\varepsilon_{0},c])
implies

.

Put

. The system

is topologically conjugate to

and thus is minimal with mean dimension
t
.

∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let
0\leq a<1
and fix
0\leq r<a
. By Theorem
1.1
(
2
), there exists a minimal subsystem
(X_{1},\sigma)
of
(\mathcal{B}([-\frac{a}{2},\frac{a}{2}]),\sigma)
with mean dimension
r
. Define

H_{1}:(\mathcal{B}([-\frac{a}{2},\frac{a}{2}]),\sigma)\to([0,1]^{\mathbb{Z}},\sigma),\ \ \ \ f\mapsto f|_{\mathbb{Z}}=(f(n))_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}.

Obviously, the mapping
H_{1}
is continuous and
H_{1}\circ\sigma=\sigma\circ H_{1}
. To show the injectivity of
H_{1}
, we assume that

H_{1}(f)=H_{1}(g)\ \ \text{for}\ \ f,g\in\mathcal{B}([-\frac{a}{2},\frac{a}{2}]),

then

(f-g)(n)=0\ \ \text{for all}\ \ n\in\mathbb{Z}.

Since
f-g\in\mathcal{B}([-\frac{a}{2},\frac{a}{2}])
, we have
f=g
by Theorem
2.2
.

Put
X=H_{1}(X_{1})
. Then the system
(X,\sigma)
is a minimal subsystem of
(\mathcal{B}([-\frac{a}{2},\frac{a}{2}]),\sigma)
and
([0,1]^{\mathbb{Z}},\sigma)
with mean dimension
r
.

∎

Stability of pullbacks of foliations on weighted projective spaces
Javier Gargiulo Acea, Ariel Molinuevo, Federico Quallbrunn, Sebastián Velazquez

Abstract
We show a stability-type theorem for foliations on projective spaces which arise as pullbacks of foliations with a split tangent sheaf on weighted projective spaces. As a consequence, we will be able to construct many irreducible components of the moduli spaces of foliations of codimension 1 on ℙ n \mathbb{P}^{n} , most of them being previously unknown. This result also provides an alternative and unified proof for the stability of other families of foliations.



Keywords:
Singular projective foliations - Moduli spaces - Weighted projective spaces - Rational pullbacks.

: 14D20; 37F75; 14B10; 32S65; 14M25.

1
Introduction

In the present article we address the problem of classifying foliations on projective spaces by means of studying the geometry of the space of codimension
1
foliations of a given degree
d\in\mathbb{Z}
on
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\mathbb{C}}
. This is the quasi-projective scheme

\mathcal{F}^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{n},d)=\{[\omega]\in\mathbb{P}H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{n},{\Omega}^{1}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d)):\omega\wedge d\omega=0\,\mbox{ and }\,\mathrm{codim}(\mathrm{Sing}(\omega))\geq 2\},

where
\mathrm{Sing}(\omega)=\{x\in\mathbb{P}^{n}:\omega(x)=0\}
.
In
[
3
]
is proven that for
n\geq 3
and
d=2
there is only one component of this space while for
n\geq 3
and
d=3
there are two components. In
[
9
]
a complete classification is found for the case
n\geq 3
and
d=4
, which consists of six components. Since then, progress has been made through several articles in identifying new components of the space of foliations for different values of
n
and
d
.

Foliations of pullback-type have played a central role in the classification of codimension one foliations in projective spaces. For instance, Brunella’s conjecture states that every foliation on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
(
n\geq 3
) is either the pullback under a rational map of a foliation on a surface or admits invariant algebraic hypersurfaces. Taking this into account, it is only natural that the study of the geometry of the spaces
\mathcal{F}^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{n},d)
around foliations of pullback-type should lead to significant progress towards this issue.

This article started as an effort to understand and extend the main result of
[
10
]
, which is perhaps the most important work tackling the above matter. There, the authors show that the closure of the set of foliations
\omega
which can be written as
\omega=F^{*}\alpha
, where
\alpha
is a foliation of degree
e
in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
and
F:\mathbb{P}^{n}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{2}
is a generic rational map defined by polynomials
(F_{0},F_{1},F_{2})
each of degree
v
, is an irreducible component of the space
\mathcal{F}^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{n},v.e)
.
Since then, it remained an open problem in the area whether there exist other irreducible components whose generic points are pullbacks of foliations on other surfaces or more generally other higher dimensional varieties
X
. There has been very little progress towards this problem, even for the simplest case where
X
is a weighted projective plane. In this article we give a positive answer to this question when
X
is a weighted projective space of arbitrary dimension. As a consequence, we are able to construct many new families of irreducible components of the spaces of foliations (see Table 1.1 below), while at the same time giving a unified proof for constructions appearing in probably the most influential works addressing this topic.

Another important work regarding this matter is
[
12
]
, which deals with
split foliations
. We say a foliation
\omega
is of
split type
if its corresponding tangent sheaf,
\mathcal{T}_{\omega}(U):=\{\mathfrak{X}\in\Gamma(U,\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}})
such that
i_{\mathfrak{X}}\omega=0\}
splits as a sum of line bundles
\mathcal{T}_{\omega}\cong\bigoplus_{i}\mathcal{L}_{i}
. There it is shown that under some hypotheses on its singularities, every deformation of a split foliation is again split. As a consequence, the authors are able to deduce that for every irreducible component
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{F}^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{m},d)
whose generic element
\omega
defines a split foliation with
\mathrm{codim}(\mathrm{Sing}(d\omega))\geq 3
there exists an irreducible component
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{F}^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{n+m},d)
whose generic element is a pullback of an element in
\mathcal{C}
under a linear projection
\mathbb{P}^{n+m}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{m}
.

In this paper we find components whose general member is a foliation of the form
F^{*}\alpha
with
F:\mathbb{P}^{n}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{m}(\overline{e})
a dominant rational map from a projective space to a weighted projective space with weights
\overline{e}=(e_{0},\dots,e_{m})
and
\alpha
is a split foliation. See bellow for full statements. In this way we obtain in
Section

4
an infinite number of previously unknown components, and generalize the aforementioned result of
[
12
]
and the construction of the
exceptional components
made in
[
9
]
in the cases
n\geq m+2
and
n\geq 4
respectively.

Of course, our approach gives a new proof of the result of
[
10
]
in the case
n\geq 4
and shows further that the moduli spaces are generically reduced along these pullback components.
Also, regarding logarithmic foliations
\omega=(\prod_{i=0}^{m}F_{i})\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}\frac{dF_{j}}{F_{j}}
we obtain another proof - although only in the case where
m\leq n+2
and with some extra hypotheses on the degrees - of the fact proven in
[
7
]
,
i.e.
, that fixing the degrees
\deg(F_{i})=d_{i}
of the invariant divisors these logarithmic foliations define an irreducible component of
\mathcal{F}^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{n},\sum d_{i})
.

1.1
Statement of results

This article deals with codimension
1
foliations on projective spaces
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\mathbb{C}}
that are the pullback under rational maps of foliations defined on weighted projective spaces. We address the question of its stability under infinitesimal deformations and draw several consequences from it. To explain further the present work we introduce some definitions and notation.

It is
a priori
unclear what a foliation is when the ambient space is singular. At the very least, we should ask a foliation on a singular space
X
to restrict to a foliation on its maximal non-singular subscheme
X_{r}
. When
X
is a normal and complete variety, which is the case we will consider here,
X_{r}\xrightarrow{j}X
is an open subscheme whose complement
X\setminus X_{r}
is a closed subset of codimension at least
2
. So whatever a codimension
1
foliation on such an
X
is, it should determine (up to multiplication by elements in
H^{0}(X_{r},\mathcal{O}_{X_{r}}^{*})=\mathbb{C}
) a global section
\alpha\in H^{0}(X_{r},\Omega^{1}_{X_{r}}\otimes\mathcal{L})
for some line bundle
\mathcal{L}
on
X_{r}
verifying integrability. Being
X
normal, the restriction of divisors induces an isomorphism
Cl(X)\simeq Pic(X_{r})
. This motivates the following definitions:

Definition 1.1
.

Let
X
be a normal projective variety and
\delta\in Cl(X)
. A
codimension
1
foliation on
X
of degree
\delta
is an element
[\alpha]\in\mathbb{P}H^{0}(X_{r},j_{*}(\Omega^{1}_{X_{r}}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X_{r}}(\delta)))
satisfying

1.

\mathrm{Sing}(\alpha)=\{x\in X:\alpha(x)=0\}
has codimension greater or equal than
2
and

2.

\alpha
is
integrable
,
i.e.
,
\alpha\wedge d\alpha=0
.

Remark 1.2
.

In order to maintain a clearer notation, we will write
\widehat{\Omega}^{1}_{X}(\delta)
instead of
j_{*}(\Omega^{1}_{X_{r}}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X_{r}}(\delta))
. Observe that this is just a twisted version of the usual sheaf of Zariski differential
1
-forms
\widehat{\Omega}^{1}_{X}
. If
Y
is another
\mathbb{C}
-scheme,
\widehat{\Omega}^{1}_{X\times Y|Y}(\delta)
will stand for the sheaf
(j_{Y})_{*}(\Omega^{1}_{X_{r}\times Y|Y}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X_{r}}(\delta))
, where
j_{Y}:X_{r}\times Y\to X\times Y
is the natural inclusion.
With respect to twisted vector fields, we will also use the notation
\mathcal{T}_{X}(\delta)=j_{*}(\mathcal{T}_{X_{r}}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X_{r}}(\delta))
.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
X
be a complete normal variety and
\delta\in Cl(X)
.
The
space of codimension
1
singular foliations
of degree
\delta\in Cl(X)
on
X
is the quasi-projective variety

\mathcal{F}^{1}(X,\delta)=\{[\alpha]\in\mathbb{P}H^{0}(X,\widehat{\Omega}^{1}_{X}(\delta)):\alpha\wedge d\alpha=0\,\mbox{ and }\,\mathrm{codim}(\mathrm{Sing}(\alpha))\geq 2\}.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
X
be a complete normal variety,
\delta\in\mathrm{Cl}(X)
and
[\alpha]\in\mathcal{F}^{1}(X,\delta)
. Let
\Sigma=\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{C}[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon)^{2})
. A
first order deformation
of
\alpha
is an integrable element
\alpha_{\varepsilon}\in H^{0}(X\times\Sigma,\widehat{\Omega}^{1}_{X\times\Sigma|\Sigma}(\delta))
, modulo multiplication of invertible elements in
\mathbb{C}[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon)^{2}
, restricting to
\alpha
on the central fiber. Such an element must be of the form
\alpha_{\varepsilon}=\alpha+\varepsilon\beta
for some
\beta\in H^{0}(X,\widehat{\Omega}^{1}_{X}(\delta))/\langle\alpha\rangle
such that

(\alpha+\varepsilon\beta)\wedge d(\alpha+\varepsilon\beta)=0

as sections of
\widehat{\Omega}^{3}_{X\times\Sigma|\Sigma}(2\delta)
, or equivalently

\alpha\wedge d\beta+d\alpha\wedge\beta=0

as sections of
\widehat{\Omega}^{3}_{X}(2\delta)
. Since the vector space of first order deformations of
\alpha
identifies with the space
\mathcal{T}_{[\alpha]}\mathcal{F}^{1}(X,\delta)
, it seems only fair to refer to the elements
\beta
above as
Zariski tangent vectors
of
\alpha
.

Definition 1.5
.

A foliation defined by a differential
1
-form
\alpha
is said to have
split tangent sheaf
if the sheaf of vector fields tangent to the foliation
\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}(U)=\{v\in\mathcal{T}_{X}(U):\ i_{v}\alpha=0\}
is isomorphic to a direct sum

\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}\simeq\bigoplus_{i}\mathcal{O}_{X}(\delta_{i}).

for some
\delta_{i}\in Cl(X)
.

Now, let
X=\mathbb{P}^{m}(\overline{e})
be a weighted projective space with weight vector
\overline{e}=(e_{0},\dots,e_{m})\in\mathbb{N}^{m+1}
.

Definition 1.6
.

A foliation on
\mathbb{P}^{m}(\overline{e})
with split tangent sheaf is said to have a
non-positive splitting
if each Weil divisor class
\delta_{i}
corresponds to a non-positive integer under the usual isomorphism
Cl(\mathbb{P}^{m}(\overline{e}))\simeq\mathbb{Z}
.

By
[
16
, Theorem A]
if an integrable
1
-form
\alpha
in
X
has split tangent sheaf and the zeroes of
d\alpha
form a variety of codimension greater than
2
, then any deformation of
\alpha
has split tangent sheaf also. It is worth mentioning that although the referred result is stated for smooth toric varieties, its proof works just fine in the normal case. This in turn implies that, in the moduli space of foliations on
X
, the generic member of an irreducible component containing
\alpha
has split tangent sheaf (with the same splitting).

Recall that every rational map
F:\mathbb{P}^{n}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{m}(\overline{e})
lifts in homogeneous coordinates to a map
F:\mathbb{C}^{n+1}\setminus\{0\}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{m+1}\setminus\{0\}
defined by some unique (up to multiplication by scalars) homogeneous polynomials
F_{0},\dots,F_{m}
of degree
\deg(F_{i})=ke_{i}
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
and vanishing simultaneously in codimension greater than
1
. In this case, we say that
F
is of degree
k
. When no confusion arises, we will use the same notation for both the rational map and its polynomial lifting.
Now let
\delta\in\mathrm{Cl}(X)
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{P}H^{0}(X,\widehat{\Omega}^{1}_{X}(\delta))
such that
\alpha\wedge d\alpha=0
.
For a generic rational map
F
as above, the corresponding pullback
\omega=F^{*}(\alpha)
defines a codimension
1
foliation on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
of degree
k\delta
.

The main result of this article is the following.

Theorem 1

(Main Theorem)
.

Let
X=\mathbb{P}^{m}(\overline{e})
be a weighted projective space,
\delta\in\mathrm{Cl}(X)
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{P}H^{0}(X,\widehat{\Omega}^{1}_{X}(\delta))
defining a foliation with split tangent sheaf with non-positive splitting and such that
d\alpha
vanishes in codimension greater than
2
. Let
n\geq m+2
,
F:\mathbb{P}^{n}\dashrightarrow X
be a generic rational map of degree
k
and
\omega:=F^{*}\alpha
. If
\eta\in H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{n},\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(k\delta))
defines a Zariski tangent vector of
\omega
then there is a rational map
G:\mathbb{P}^{n}\dashrightarrow X
of degree
k
and a form
\beta\in H^{0}(X,\widehat{\Omega}^{1}_{X}(\delta))
defining a Zariski tangent vector of
\alpha
such that

\omega+\varepsilon\eta=(F+\varepsilon G)^{*}(\alpha+\varepsilon\beta).

as sections of
\Omega^{1}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\Sigma|\Sigma}(k\delta)
, where
\Sigma=\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{C}[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon)^{2})
.

In other words, the above Theorem is stating that any first order deformation of
F^{*}\alpha
is the pullback of a first order deformation of
\alpha
by a deformation of
F
. As an immediate consequence, we can construct many irreducible components of the spaces of foliations on
\mathbb{P}^{n}

Corollary 2
.

n\geq m+2

2
Preliminaries

Let us now briefly introduce some basic facts and conventions on weighted projective spaces. For complete proofs the reader is referred to
[
4
]
and
[
13
]
. Along the rest of the article,
X
will stand for the variety
\mathbb{P}^{m}(\overline{e})
with weight vector
\overline{e}=(e_{0},\dots,e_{m})
. This space can be presented as the quotient space of the action
\mathbb{C}^{*}\curvearrowright\mathbb{C}^{m+1}\setminus\{0\}
defined by

t\cdot(x_{0},\dots,x_{m})=(t^{e_{0}}x_{0},\dots,t^{e_{m}}x_{m}).

(1)

We will denote by
\pi_{\overline{e}}:\mathbb{C}^{m+1}\setminus\{0\}\to\mathbb{P}^{m}(\overline{e})
the quotient morphism.

Let us fix some notation that we will use in the rest of the article: we will denote by
S
the homogeneous coordinate ring of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
,
S=\mathbb{C}[z_{0},\ldots,z_{n}]
, and with a subscript
\overline{e}
the corresponding graded polynomial ring of
\mathbb{P}^{m}(\overline{e})
, that is
S_{\overline{e}}=\mathbb{C}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{m}]
. If no confusion arises, we will also denote with a subscript the homogeneous component of the given degree of
S_{\overline{e}}
or
S
.

The tangent space to the fibers of
\pi_{\overline{e}}
is pointwise spanned by the
radial vector field

R_{\overline{e}}=\sum_{i=0}^{m}e_{i}x_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}.

At the level of functions, the corresponding action of
Eq.

1
diagonalizes into a
\mathbb{Z}
-grading of
S_{\overline{e}}=\mathbb{C}[x_{0},\dots,x_{m}]
such that
\deg(x_{i})=e_{i}
. This naturally induces a
\mathbb{Z}
-grading for both
\Omega^{\bullet}_{S_{\overline{e}}}
and its dual
\bigwedge^{\bullet}Der(S_{\overline{e}},S_{\overline{e}})
where
dx_{i}
and
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}
are of degree
e_{i}
and
-e_{i}
respectively.

It is a standard fact that the class group
Cl(X)
is generated by the classes of the divisors
D_{i}=\pi_{\overline{e}}(\{x_{i}=0\})
. Moreover, the application
\deg:Cl(X)\to\mathbb{Z}
such that
[\sum a_{i}D_{i}]\mapsto\sum a_{i}e_{i}
defines an isomorphism
Cl(X)\simeq\mathbb{Z}
. Under this identification, the classes of Cartier divisors (
i.e.
, the Picard group of
X
) identify with the free subgroup
\langle lcm(e_{0},\dots,e_{m})\rangle\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
. One of the good features of this grading is that the pullback under
\pi_{\overline{e}}
induces an isomorphism

H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(\delta))\simeq(S_{\overline{e}})_{\delta}.

We will often make use of both the Euler sequence

0\longrightarrow\widehat{\Omega}_{X}^{1}\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{i=0}^{m}\mathcal{O}_{X}(-e_{i})\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}\longrightarrow 0

(2)

and its dual

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X}(e_{i})\longrightarrow\mathcal{T}_{X}\longrightarrow 0

(3)

(see for instance
[
6
]
) in order to deal with the sheaves
\widehat{\Omega}^{1}_{X}(\delta)
and
\mathcal{T}_{X}(\delta)
as follows. Recall that the second arrow in the first sequence corresponds to the dot product with the element
(e_{0}x_{0},\dots,e_{m}x_{m})
. For an element
\delta\in Cl(X)
, one can restrict
Eq.

2
to
X_{r}
and tensorize with
\mathcal{O}_{X_{r}}(\delta)
and get a short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\Omega_{X_{r}}^{1}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X_{r}}(\delta)\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{i=0}^{m}\mathcal{O}_{X_{r}}(\delta-e_{i})\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X_{r}}(\delta)\longrightarrow 0.

Just as in the case of the projective space, taking global sections we see that every
\omega\in H^{0}(X_{r},\Omega^{1}_{X_{r}}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X_{r}}(\delta))
(and therefore every global section of
\widehat{\Omega}^{1}_{X}(\delta)
) can be described in homogeneous coordinates as a polynomial differential form

\omega=\sum_{i=0}^{m}A_{i}(x)dx_{i}

(4)

where
A_{i}\in(S_{\overline{e}})_{\delta-e_{i}}
and
\imath_{R_{\overline{e}}}(\sum_{i=0}^{m}A_{i}(z)dz_{i})=0
. In fact, the differential form above is just the pullback
\pi_{\overline{e}}^{*}\omega
. Since the same reasoning applies to higher differential forms, it is only natural to define the following.

Definition 2.1
.

We will denote by

\widehat{\Omega}^{\bullet}_{S_{\overline{e}}}=\bigoplus_{\delta\in\mathbb{Z}}H^{0}(X,j_{*}(\Omega^{\bullet}_{X_{r}}\otimes O_{X_{r}}(\delta)))\subseteq\Omega^{\bullet}_{S_{\overline{e}}}

the
graded module of differential forms descending to
X
,
i.e.
, the elements
\omega
such that
{\imath}_{R_{\overline{e}}}(\omega)=0
.

Regarding
Eq.

3
, with a similar argument we can see that every element
\mathfrak{X}\in H^{0}(X,T_{X}(\delta))
can be written as

\mathfrak{X}=\sum_{i=0}^{m}B_{i}(x)\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}

where
B_{i}
is homogeneous of degree
\delta+e_{i}
. This description is unique up to addition of multiples of the radial vector field.

2.1
Idea of the proof

Since the proof of the Main Theorem is quite technical, we will now give a quick overview of the ideas therein.

Let us start by spelling out what
1
is saying in terms of homogeneous coordinates of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
. Let
F:\mathbb{P}^{n}\dashrightarrow X
be a generic rational map induced by some homogeneous polynomials
F_{0},\dots,F_{m}
of degree
\deg(F_{i})=ke_{i}
,
\alpha=\sum_{i=0}^{m}A_{i}(x)dx_{i}\in H^{0}(X,\widehat{\Omega}^{1}_{X}(\delta))
and
\omega=F^{*}\alpha
.
If
G=(G_{0},\dots,G_{m})
is another homogeneous tuple of the same degrees, writing down the expression for
(F+\varepsilon G)^{*}\alpha
in the ring
\mathbb{C}[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon)^{2}[z_{0},\dots,z_{n}]
we get

\displaystyle(F+\varepsilon G)^{*}\alpha=\sum_{i=0}^{n}A_{i}(F+\varepsilon G)d(F_{i}+\varepsilon G_{i})=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n}\left(A_{i}(F)+\varepsilon\left(\sum_{j=0}^{m}\frac{\partial A_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}(F)G_{j}\right)\right)(dF_{i}+\varepsilon dG_{i})=

\displaystyle\omega+\varepsilon\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}A_{i}(F)dG_{i}+\sum_{i=0}^{n}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{m}\frac{\partial A_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}(F)G_{j}\right)dF_{i}\right).

Since
(F+\varepsilon G)^{*}(\varepsilon\beta)=\varepsilon F^{*}\beta
, our main theorem is claiming that every Zariski tangent vector
\eta
of
\omega
can be decomposed as
\eta=\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}
, with
\eta_{1}=F^{*}\beta
and

\eta_{2}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}A_{i}(F)dG_{i}+\sum_{i=0}^{n}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{m}\frac{\partial A_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}(F)G_{j}\right)dF_{i}.

Observe that both
\eta_{1}
and

here induce deformations of

into a direct sum of two subspaces.

In
[
17

is proven that a Zariski tangent vector as

above actually give rise to

in the sense of
[

. As we will refer often to this particular type of deformations we make the following

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\omega=F^{*}\alpha=\sum_{i}A_{i}(F)dF_{i}
be a pullback form as in the main theorem.
We say a a deformation of
\omega
is a

if it is given by a Zariski tangent vector of the form

\eta_{2}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}A_{i}(F)dG_{i}+\sum_{i=0}^{n}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{m}\frac{\partial A_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}(F)G_{j}\right)dF_{i}.

If no confusion arises, we will refer as

to both the deformation of
\omega
and the Zariski tangent vector inducing such deformation.

In
[
17
, Theorem 4.14]
is proven that, under suitable hypotheses, every unfolding of
\omega
comes from a form like
\eta_{2}
above, so sometimes we can conclude that every unfolding is special in the above sense. However we will not need results of this type, as we will prove that every deformation of

is decomposed as a sum of the pullback by

of a deformation of
\alpha
and a deformation given by a special unfolding.

The first step in establishing the main theorem is the realization of the following fact.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
X
,
\omega
,
\alpha
and
F:\mathbb{P}^{n}\dashrightarrow X
be as in
1
. Let
\eta
be a Zariski tangent vector of
\omega
.
If
\eta

\eta=\sum_{j=0}^{m}B_{j}(z)dF_{j}

then there is a Zariski tangent vector
\beta
of
\alpha

.

In view of the above we need to prove that a Zariski tangent vector

is equivalent, as an element of

To do this we study the restriction of

to a certain subscheme of singularities of

.

Remark 2.4

Notice that, in general,

is well-defined only as a form in affine space

but does not descend to a form in

. Therefore, in view of

associated to

and generated by sections

.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\omega=\sum_{i=0}^{m}A_{i}(F)dF_{i}
be as in
1
. We define

to be the ideal generated by

of

Since under the hypotheses of

, a generic point

of

This immediately implies that

Stated in terms of the

\eta=\sum_{i=0}^{m}A_{i}(F)\gamma_{i}+\sum_{j=0}^{m}B_{j}dF_{j},

for some

Now the last step of the proof is to show that the elements

for some homogeneous polynomials

at the cost of adding some special unfoldings on the right side of

This, combined with

of the form

is equivalent modulo

. Finally, using
Proposition

∎

4
Applications

This section is devoted to construct new irreducible components of the spaces of foliations
\mathcal{F}^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{n},d)
by means of applying
2
. We will also show that the stability of some families already appearing in the literature can be deduced from this result as well.

4.1
Pullbacks of foliations on
\mathbb{P}^{2}(\overline{e})

In the case
m=2
, the integrability condition is trivial and therefore the spaces
\mathcal{F}^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{2}(\overline{e}),\delta)
are open in
\mathbb{P}H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{2}(\overline{e}),\widehat{\Omega}^{1}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}(\overline{e})}(\delta))
. In particular, they are irreducible and reduced. Since every foliation on a surface is defined by a single twisted vector field, the tangent sheaf of every foliation will have a non-positive splitting whenever
\delta\geq e_{0}+e_{1}+e_{2}
. This enables us to apply
2
to the case
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{F}^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{2}(\overline{e}),\delta)
provided that a generic element in
\mathcal{C}
admits only Kupka singularities:

Corollary 4.1
.

Let
\overline{e}=(e_{0},e_{1},e_{2})
,
n\geq 4
and
\delta\geq e_{0}+e_{1}+e_{2}
such that a generic foliation of degree
\delta
on
\mathbb{P}^{2}(\overline{e})
has only Kupka singularities. Then for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
there exists an irreducible component
PB(n,k,\overline{e},\delta))
of
\mathcal{F}^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{n},k\delta)
whose generic element is a pullback of a foliation of degree
\delta
on
\mathbb{P}^{2}(\overline{e})
under a rational map
F:\mathbb{P}^{n}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{2}(\overline{e})
of degree
k
. Moreover, the scheme
\mathcal{F}^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{n},k\delta)
is generically reduced along
PB(n,k,\overline{e},\delta))
.

Remark 4.2
.

Since the hypothesis on the singularities is clearly satisfied in the case of
\mathbb{P}^{2}(\overline{e})=\mathbb{P}^{2}
, this gives an alternative proof of the main Theorem in
[
10
]
for
n\geq 4
.

It could be the case, however, that for an arbitrary
\overline{e}
every foliation of some special degree
\delta
contains non-Kupka points among their singularities. Let us now construct for every
\overline{e}
an infinite family of degrees where this hypothesis is satisfied.

Depending on the arithmetic of
\overline{e}
and
\delta
, some of the points
[1:0:0]
,
[0:1:0]
and
[0:0:1]
may be forced to be a singularity of

foliation of degree
\delta
. Moreover, such points will be non-Kupka singularities. In

17
, Example 3.9]
it is shown that the degrees
\delta
avoiding this phenomenon are the ones such that for every

we have

e_{i}|\delta-(e_{0}+e_{1}+e_{2})+e_{j}

(9)

for some
j
. The solutions to the equations consist of a certain (positive) number of residues modulo
e_{0}e_{1}e_{2}
. Observe further that for such
\delta
the canonical map

H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{2}(\overline{e}),\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}(\overline{e})}(\delta-(e_{0}+e_{1}+e_{2})))\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}(\overline{e})}\to\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}(\overline{e})}(\delta-(e_{0}+e_{1}+e_{2}))

is non-zero at every fiber. In particular, applying
[
17
, Proposition 3.7]
we can deduce:

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\delta\geq e_{0}+e_{1}+e_{2}
satisfying conditions (
9
) above. Then for every
k\geq 1
a generic foliation of degree
\delta+ke_{0}e_{1}e_{2}
will have only Kupka singularities.

Until this point we have only dedicated ourselves to construct stable families which are pullbacks under
generic
rational maps. However, in some situations our methods also enable us to deal with the non-generic case as well.

In
[
15
, Section 4]
the authors construct subvarieties
TM_{s}(e_{0},e_{1},e_{2},l)
of the space
\mathcal{F}^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{3},s)
whose general member is conjugated to a foliation tangent to a multiplicative action of the form

\displaystyle\phi^{(e_{0},e_{1},e_{2})}:\mathbb{C}^{*}\times\mathbb{P}^{3}

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{P}^{3}

\displaystyle(\lambda,[x_{0},\dots,x_{3}])

\displaystyle\to[\lambda^{e_{0}}x_{0},\lambda^{e_{1}}x_{1},\lambda^{e_{2}}x_{2},x_{3}],

and satisfying

L_{v_{(e_{0},e_{1},e_{2})}}\omega_{\mathcal{F}}=l\,\omega_{\mathcal{F}},

where

is a vector field generating the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action.
The general leaf of these foliations happens to be a pullback of a foliation on
\mathbb{P}^{2}(\overline{e})
under the rational map

\displaystyle\pi_{\overline{e}}:\mathbb{P}^{3}

\displaystyle\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{2}_{(e_{0},e_{1},e_{2})}

\displaystyle[x_{0}:\dots:x_{3}]

\displaystyle\mapsto[x_{0}x_{3}^{e_{0}-1}:x_{1}x_{3}^{e_{1}-1}:x_{2}x_{3}^{e_{2}-1}].

Moreover,

(and its corresponding proof) states that if

.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let

be a generically reduced irreducible component of

whose general member has finitely many non-Kupka singularities. Then for every

there exists an irreducible component

4.2
Pullbacks of foliations induced by Lie group actions

Let us now move to a different type of objects, namely foliations on
\mathbb{P}^{m}
whose tangent sheaf is trivial. The corresponding integrable differential
1
-form can be constructed as follows. Let
\mathfrak{g}\subseteq H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{m},\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{P}^{m}})

\mathcal{T}_{\omega(\mathfrak{g})}\simeq\mathfrak{g}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{m}}.

Along the literature one can find many stable families of foliations of this kind. We will now apply

5
A word on pullbacks from toric varieties

One of the inquiries that motivated this work is wether there exist stable families that arise as pullbacks from varieties

different than (weighted) projective spaces. In order to address this problem, the authors first considered the case where

is a normal toric variety. In this short digression we will see that most of the stable families that could arise from such situation are included in the irreducible components presented in this paper.

Let

the simplicial toric variety of dimension

associated to the fan
\Sigma
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and let
\{v_{0}=(v_{1}^{0},\dots,v_{d}^{0}),\dots,v_{m}=(v_{1}^{m},\dots,v_{d}^{m})\}

generating its one-dimensional cones. The class group

is generated by the

establishes that

where

(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{m+1}\curvearrowright\mathbb{C}^{m+1}
after viewing

under the identification

see
[

. In particular, an element
(t^{a_{0}},\dots,t^{a_{m}})\in(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{m+1}

. Let us denote by

It follows from
[

that a general rational map onto a toric variety

admits a unique (up to multiplication of elements in

) complete polynomial lifting. This is, a map (which will be denoted in the same manner)

making the diagram

Let us denote by

the degree

\phi_{\overline{e}}:\mathbb{P}^{m}(\bar{e}/k)\dashrightarrow Y.

By construction, the map

factorizes through

,
i.e.
, the degree

Without loss of generality, we can assume that the one parameter subgroup generated by the first

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a sheaf on

\mathcal{G}|_{Y_{r}}\simeq\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\mathcal{O}_{Y}(\alpha_{i})

With respect to the pullbacks of foliations under

Lemma 5.2

Let

\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}

2.

\phi_{\overline{e}}^{*}\alpha

\mathbb{P}^{n}

.

Let
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{F}^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{n},\ell)
be an irreducible component whose generic element is a pullback under a rational map of degree
\overline{e}
of a split foliation on a toric variety
Y
such that
\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}
is non-positive with respect to
\overline{e}
and
d\pi_{Y}^{*}\alpha
vanishes in codimension greater than
2
.
If
n+2
is greater than the number of irreducible torus-invariant divisors on
Y
, then
\mathcal{C}
is one of the irreducible components described in
Corollary

4.8
.

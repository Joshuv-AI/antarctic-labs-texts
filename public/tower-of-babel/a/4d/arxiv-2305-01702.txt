Exciton and light induced ferromagnetism from doping a moiré Mott insulator
Hui Yang, Ya-Hui Zhang

Abstract
Significant efforts have been dedicated to achieving excitonic insulators. In this paper, we explore a new problem of doping excitons into a Mott insulator instead of a band insulator. Specifically, we start with a Mott insulator on a triangular moiré superlattice in a transition metal dichalcogenides (TMD) layer and inject excitons by either transferring particles to a different layer or optically pumping electrons from the valence to the conduction band. In both cases, the excitons move in the presence of local spin moments inherited from the Mott insulator. When the Heisenberg spin coupling J J is small, the kinetic energy of the excitons decides the magnetism, akin to Nagaoka ferromagnetism in hole-doped Mott insulators. Through density matrix renormalization group (DMRG) calculations, we demonstrate that the spin moments originating from the Mott insulator form 120 ∘ 120^{\circ} antiferromagnetic or ferromagnetic order for the two signs of the exciton hoppings over a broad range of exciton densities. Notably, the optical pump case may result in an antiferromagnetic to ferromagnetic transition with increasing exciton density, indicating a potential mechanism for light-induced ferromagnetism. A similar exciton-induced ferromagnetism could be achieved in a moiré-monolayer system where the monolayer is electron-doped while the moiré Mott insulator is hole-doped. Our works demonstrates a new possibility to engineering magnetism through doping neutral excitons.

Reduction to a bosonic t-J model
Let us assume that the spin of the top layer is polarized to the spin up. Then the four states at each site reduces to three states, we can label them

Conclusion
In summary, we study exciton doped Mott insulator in the TMD moiré systems. In these systems the spin-spin coupling
J
of the Mott insulator is usually very small because
U/t
is very large. As a result, the magnetism of the localized moments will be decided by the kinetic term of the exciton. We perform a DMRG simulation and find that the spin moments inherited from the Mott insulator form
120^{\circ}
AF order or ferromagnetic order depending on the sign of the effective hopping of excitons. Especially, if we dope a moiré+monolayer with electron and hole in the two layers respectively, there can be an antiferromagnetic to ferromagnetic transition when increasing the exciton density. The same physics can happen through optical pumping, providing a mechanism of light induced ferromagnetism. Our work demonstrates the possibility of engineering magnetism through doping neutral excitons.

\frac{\hbar^{2}}{ma_{M}^{2}}=2.90703meV

\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_{0}}\frac{e^{2}}{a_{M}}=182.274meV

The Coulomb interactions are

\displaystyle V_{tt}({\bf q})=

\displaystyle V_{bb}({\bf q})=\frac{e^{2}}{2\epsilon_{0}\epsilon|{\bf q}|}(1-e^{-D|{\bf q}|}),

(7)

\displaystyle V_{bt}({\bf q})=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{2}}{2\epsilon_{0}\epsilon|{\bf q}|}\left(e^{-d|{\bf q}|}-e^{-D|{\bf q}|}\right),

(8)

where

\displaystyle V_{tt}({\bf x})=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{2}}{4\pi\epsilon_{0}\epsilon}(\frac{1}{|{\bf x}|}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{{\bf x}^{2}+D^{2}}}),

(9)

\displaystyle V_{bt}({\bf x})=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{2}}{4\pi\epsilon_{0}\epsilon}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{{\bf x}^{2}+d^{2}}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{{\bf x}^{2}+D^{2}}}\right)

(10)

We first try to obtain the moiré bands in the bottom layer using the continuum model:

\displaystyle H_{b}=\sum_{{\bf k}}\frac{\hbar^{2}{\bf k}^{2}}{2m_{b}}h_{\bf k}^{\dagger}h_{\bf k}+\sum_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{G}}V({\bf k}+\mathbf{G}_{i})(h_{{\bf k}+\mathbf{G}_{i}}^{\dagger}h_{\bf k}+h.c),

(11)

here
\mathbf{G}_{1}=\frac{2\pi}{a_{M}}(0,\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}})

\mathbf{G}_{2}=\frac{2\pi}{a_{M}}(1,-\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}})
,
\mathbf{G}_{3}=\frac{2\pi}{a_{M}}(-1,\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}})
.

To simplify our analysis, following Ref.
Zhang and Senthil 2019
, we first construct Wannier orbitals from
H_{b}
to derive a low energy effective model. The Wannier state is

\displaystyle h_{i}^{\dagger}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{k}e^{\mathbbm{i}{\bf k}\cdot{\bf R}_{i}}e^{\mathbbm{i}\theta({\bf k})}h^{\dagger}({\bf k}),

(12)

\theta({\bf k})
can be calculate from the projection method
Marzari et al. 2012
, with
e^{\mathbbm{i}\theta({\bf k})}=\frac{\langle\mu({\bf k})|g({\bf k})\rangle}{|\langle\mu({\bf k})|g({\bf k})\rangle|}
. Here
\mu({\bf k})
is the Bloch wave function with energy
\xi({\bf k})
, and
g({\bf k})
is trail wave function (in our calculation, it is Gaussian).

In the Wannier orbital basis, the hopping term
t
is

\displaystyle t(m,n)=-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{{\bf k}}\xi({\bf k})e^{-\mathbbm{i}{\bf k}\cdot(m{\bf a}_{1}+n{\bf a}_{2})},

(13)

The intra-layer interaction in the bottom layer
\frac{1}{2}V_{bb}(x-x^{\prime})\rho_{b}(x)\rho_{b}(x^{\prime})
can be rewritten as:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x,R_{1},R_{2},R_{3}}U(R_{1},R_{2},R_{3})h^{\dagger}(x)h^{\dagger}(x+R_{1})h(x+R_{2})h(x+R_{3}),

(14)

with

\displaystyle U(R_{1},R_{2},R_{3})=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{{\bf k}_{1},{\bf q}_{1},{\bf k}_{2},{\bf q}_{2}}\sum_{x,x^{\prime}}V_{bt}(x-x^{\prime})\lambda({\bf k}_{1},{\bf q}_{1})\lambda({\bf k}_{2},{\bf q}_{2})

\displaystyle e

{}^{-\mathbbm{i}\theta({\bf k}_{1}+{\bf q}_{1})}e^{-\mathbbm{i}\theta({\bf k}_{1})}e^{-\mathbbm{i}\theta({\bf k}_{2}+{\bf q}_{2})}e^{-\mathbbm{i}\theta({\bf k}_{2})}e^{\mathbbm{i}({\bf q}_{1}+{\bf q}_{2})x^{\prime}}e^{\mathbbm{i}({\bf k}_{2}+{\bf q}_{2})R_{1}}e^{\mathbbm{i}k_{2}R_{2}}e^{-\mathbbm{i}{\bf k}_{1}R_{3}}e^{-\mathbbm{i}{\bf q}_{1}x}e^{-\mathbbm{i}{\bf q}_{2}x^{\prime}},

(15)

where
\lambda({\bf k},{\bf q})=\langle\mu({\bf k})|\mu({\bf k}+{\bf q})\rangle
is the form factor.

For the inter-layer Coulomb interaction
\int dxdx^{\prime}V_{bt}(x-x^{\prime})n_{b}(x)n_{t}(x^{\prime})
, we substitute
n_{b}(x)
with
\sum_{i}W_{i}^{*}(x-{\bf R}_{i})W_{i}(x-{\bf R}_{i})
. It becomes

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\int dxdx^{\prime}W_{i}^{*}(x^{\prime}-{\bf R}_{i})W_{i}(x^{\prime}-{\bf R}_{i})V_{bt}(x-x^{\prime})n_{t}(x)=\sum_{i}\int dxV_{i}(x-{\bf R}_{i})n_{t}(x),

(16)

with
V_{i}(x-{\bf R}_{i})=\int dx^{\prime}W_{i}^{*}(x^{\prime}-{\bf R}_{i})W_{i}(x^{\prime}-{\bf R}_{i})V_{bt}(x-x^{\prime})
, where
W_{i}(x-{\bf R}_{i})
is the Wannier function. The hole in the top layer at
x
feels a potential
\sum_{i}V_{i}(x-{\bf R}_{i})
. When we use
V_{bt}(x-x^{\prime})
, it means we consider the bottom layer as a continuous model with holes located at
x
, while when we use
V_{i}(x-{\bf R}_{i})
, it means we consider the bottom layer as a lattice model with holes located at lattice site
{\bf R}_{i}
.

The extended lattice Hubbard model describing the bottom layer is:

\displaystyle H_{b}=-t_{b}\sum_{<ij>}h^{\dagger}_{bi}h_{bj}+\frac{U}{2}\sum_{i}n_{bi}^{2}+V\sum_{<ij>}n_{bi}n_{bj},

(17)

where
h_{bi}^{\dagger}
creates a hole at site
i
, and
n_{bi}=h_{bi}^{\dagger}h_{bi}
is the hole density.
t_{b}=1.06meV
is the hooping in the bottom layer, and
U=660.634meV/\epsilon
and
V=92.689meV/\epsilon
correspond to the on site Hubbard interaction and nearest-neighbor Hubbard interaction in the bottom layer.

A.2
Exciton wave function and derivation of effective spin-exciton model

The total Hamiltonian now becomes

\displaystyle H=-

\displaystyle t_{b}\sum_{<ij>}h_{bi;\sigma}^{\dagger}h_{bj;\sigma}+\frac{U}{2}\sum_{i}n_{bi}^{2}+V\sum_{<ij>}n_{bi}n_{bj}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\int_{x}h_{t}^{\dagger}(x)\frac{\nabla^{2}}{2m}h_{t}(x)+\frac{1}{2}\int dxdx^{\prime}V_{tt}(x-x^{\prime})n_{t}(x)n_{t}(x^{\prime})+\sum_{i}\int dxV_{i}(x-{\bf R}_{i})n_{bi}n_{t}(x^{\prime}),

(18)

where
n_{bi}=\sum_{\sigma=\uparrow,\downarrow}h^{\dagger}_{bi;\sigma}h_{bi;\sigma}
.

A.2.1
One exciton problem

First we consider there only one hole in the bottom layer, and one electron in the top layer. We can calculate the wave function by solving the Schrödinger equation,

\displaystyle\left(-\frac{\hbar^{2}\nabla^{2}}{2m}+\sum_{i}V_{i}(x-R_{i})-V_{0}(x)\right)\phi_{in}(x)=E_{n}\phi_{in},

(19)

where
E_{n}
and
\phi_{in}
correspond to the
n
-th eigenenergy and
n
-th eigenstate, respectively. An excitonic state can be expressed as

\displaystyle b^{\dagger}_{i}|0\rangle=\int_{x}\phi_{in}(x)h_{t}^{\dagger}(x)h_{bi}\prod_{j}h_{bj}^{\dagger}|0\rangle,

(20)

here the spin indices are implicit. The exciton hopping term between a nearest neighbor pair
\langle ij\rangle
is
J_{p}b_{i}^{\dagger}b_{j}
, where
J_{p}
can be calculated from the first order perturbation theory:

\displaystyle J_{p}=\langle 0|b_{i}H_{b}b_{j}^{\dagger}|0\rangle=\langle 0|\int_{x}\phi_{in}^{*}(x)(\prod_{l}h_{bl})h_{bi}^{\dagger}h_{t}(x)(-t_{b}h_{bk}^{\dagger}h_{bl})\int_{x^{\prime}}\phi_{jn}(x^{\prime})h_{t}^{\dagger}(x^{\prime})h_{bj}\prod_{k}h_{bk}^{\dagger}|0\rangle=t_{b}\int_{x}\phi_{in}^{*}(x)\phi_{jn}(x),

(21)

where in the second equality we used the relation
h_{bi}^{\dagger}h_{bk}^{\dagger}h_{bl}h_{bj}=-h_{bi}^{\dagger}h_{bl}h_{bk}^{\dagger}h_{bj}=-\delta_{il}\delta_{kj}
. Note here we always have
i\neq j
. Thus the effective hopping of exciton is
J_{p}=t_{b}\int_{x}\phi_{in}^{*}(x)\phi_{jn}(x)
. For the case I it is always positive.

A.2.2
Two exciton problem

Next we calculate
J_{t}
, which is the energy difference of the triplet state and singlet state
E_{t}-E_{s}
of the two exciton problem. We need to consider two holes in the top layer locating in the nearest neighbor pair
\langle ij\rangle
and two vacancies of hole (two electrons) in the bottom layer. The Schrödinger equation is:

\displaystyle\left(-\frac{\hbar^{2}\nabla^{2}}{2m}+\sum_{i}V_{i}(x-R_{i})-V_{i}(x-R_{i})-V_{j}(x-R_{j})\right)\phi_{ij}^{n}(x)=E_{n}\phi_{ij}^{n},

(22)

With
E_{n}
the
n
-th eigenenergy, and
\phi_{ij}^{n}
the
n
-th eigenstate. With the existence of Coulomb interaction in the top layer, we can construct the symmetric and anti-symmetric wave function using the ground state and first excited state wave function
\phi_{ij}^{0}
and
\phi_{ij}^{1}
,

\displaystyle\psi_{+}=

\displaystyle\phi_{ij}^{0}(x_{1})\phi_{ij}^{0}(x_{2}),

(23)

\displaystyle\psi_{-}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\phi_{ij}^{0}(x_{1})\phi_{ij}^{1}(x_{2})-\phi_{ij}^{0}(x_{2})\phi_{ij}^{1}(x_{1})),

(24)

\displaystyle J_{t}=

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{-}|H|\psi_{-}\rangle-\langle\psi_{+}|H|\psi_{+}\rangle,

(25)

the spin indices is implicit hereinafter.

\displaystyle E_{t}=

\displaystyle\int\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\phi_{ij}^{0}(x_{1})\phi_{ij}^{1}(x_{2})-\phi_{ij}^{0}(x_{2})\phi_{ij}^{1}(x_{1}))^{*}(H_{1}+H_{2}+\frac{1}{2}V_{tt}(x_{1}-x_{2}))\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\phi_{ij}^{0}(x_{1})\phi_{ij}^{1}(x_{2})-\phi_{ij}^{0}(x_{2})\phi_{ij}^{1}(x_{1})),

(26)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{0}+E_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{2}\int{\phi_{ij}^{0}(x_{1})}^{*}{\phi_{ij}^{1}(x_{2})}^{*}\phi_{ij}^{0}(x_{1})\phi_{ij}^{1}(x_{2})V^{\prime}(x_{1}-x_{2})+{\phi_{ij}^{0}(x_{2})}^{*}{\phi_{ij}^{1}(x_{1})}^{*}\phi_{ij}^{0}(x_{2})\phi_{ij}^{1}(x_{1})V_{tt}(x_{1}-x_{2})

(27)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle{\phi_{ij}^{0}(x_{1})}^{*}{\phi_{ij}^{1}(x_{2})}^{*}\phi_{ij}^{0}(x_{2})\phi_{ij}^{1}(x_{1})V^{\prime}(x_{1}-x_{2})-{\phi_{ij}^{0}(x_{2})}^{*}{\phi_{ij}^{1}(x_{1})}^{*}\phi_{ij}^{0}(x_{1})\phi_{ij}^{1}(x_{2})V_{tt}(x_{1}-x_{2}),

(28)

\displaystyle E_{s}=

\displaystyle 2E_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\int V_{tt}(x_{1}-x_{2}){\phi_{ij}^{0}(x_{1})}^{*}{\phi_{ij}^{0}(x_{2})}^{*}\phi_{ij}^{0}(x_{1})\phi_{ij}^{0}(x_{2}),

(29)

\displaystyle J_{t}=

\displaystyle E_{t}-E_{s}.

(30)

We calculate
J_{t}
from the above equations numerically.

Next we move to estimate the exciton dipole-dipole repulsion

\displaystyle J_{pz}\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0&0\\

(31)

the basis in our model are expressed as

\displaystyle|t,t\rangle=

\displaystyle\int dx_{1}dx_{2}\phi_{ij}(x_{1})\phi_{ij}(x_{2})h^{\dagger}_{t}(x_{1})h^{\dagger}_{t}(x_{2})|0\rangle,

\displaystyle|t,b\rangle=

\displaystyle\int dx_{1}\phi_{i}(x_{1})h^{\dagger}_{t}(x_{1})h^{\dagger}_{bj}|0\rangle,

There energies can be calculated as:

\displaystyle E_{tt}=

Appendix B
reduction to bosonic

-

model

If the spin in the top layer is polarized, the four-flavor spin model will reduce to the bosonic
t
-
J

\displaystyle b^{\dagger}_{i\sigma}=\ket{\sigma}_{i}\bra{0}_{i}=h^{\dagger}_{bi\sigma}\int\phi(x-{\bf R}_{i})h_{it}(x),

this is just the exciton creation operator, so the bosonic hopping term is the exciton hopping

Appendix C
Magnetism in the bottom layer

To understand the
120^{\circ}
order in the bottom layer, we can introduce Schwinger bosons

2
as in Ref.

. The

and
{\bf K}^{\prime}

{\bf K}^{\prime}

{\bf Q}=0

Appendix F
Light induced FM at
n=-\frac{1}{3}

The experiment
Wang et al. 2018
observed light induced ferromagnetism at
n=-\frac{1}{3}
. When the nearest-neighbor interaction is strong enough, the system at
n=-\frac{1}{3}
is a Wigner crystal with one hole per-triangle in the initial triangular lattice. We can regard it as a new triangular lattice formed by the next-nearest neighbor sites. Now we should derive the new coupling parameters in our model Eq.
2
in the main text. Starting with the tight-binding model in the bottom layer

\displaystyle H_{b}=-t_{b}\sum_{<ij>}h^{\dagger}_{bi\sigma}h_{bj\sigma}+\frac{U}{2}\sum_{i}n_{i}^{2}+V\sum_{<ij>}n_{i}n_{j}+V_{2}\sum_{<<ij>>}n_{i}n_{j},

(58)

where
U=660.635meV/\epsilon
,
V=92.689meV/\epsilon
,
V_{2}=16.714meV/\epsilon
correspond to the onsite, nearest, next-nearest Hubbard interaction. We have the exciton state defined as

\displaystyle b^{\dagger}_{i}\ket{0}=\int dx\phi_{i}(x)c_{t}^{\dagger}(x)h_{bi}^{\dagger}\ket{0},

(59)

now the exciton hopping correspond to second order process. First the exciton (hole) hops to an intermediate site, the first order perturbed wave function is (step
1
to step
2
in Fig.
15
(b))

\displaystyle\ket{\psi}=\ket{\psi_{0}}+\sum_{i}\ket{\psi_{i}}\frac{\langle\psi_{i}|H_{1}|\psi_{0}\rangle}{E_{0}-E_{i}}=\ket{\psi_{0}}+\ket{\psi^{\prime}},

(60)

with
\ket{\psi_{0}}=b^{\dagger}_{i}\ket{0}
, and then we considet the exciton hops on the nearest-neighbor sites.

\displaystyle J_{p}=

\displaystyle\langle 0|b_{i}(-t_{b}h_{bm}^{\dagger}h_{bl})|\psi^{\prime}\rangle=\int dx^{\prime}\phi^{*}_{i}(x^{\prime})h_{bi}c_{t}(x^{\prime})(-t_{b}h_{bm}^{\dagger}h_{bl})\frac{1}{V_{2}-V}(-t_{b}h_{bl}^{\dagger}h_{bk})\int dx\phi_{j}(x)c_{t}^{\dagger}(x)h_{bj}^{\dagger}\ket{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\frac{t_{b}^{2}}{V-V_{2}}\int dx\phi^{*}_{i}(x)\phi_{j}(x),

(61)

where the factor of
2
comes from the summation over the site
l
on the initial triangular lattice.

J_{t}
and
J_{pz}
can be calculated from the same process as the case for
n=1
. To calculate

(b)),

\displaystyle\frac{-t_{b}}{V_{2}-V}h_{bi\gamma}^{\dagger}h_{bk\gamma}\frac{t_{b}^{2}}{V-U}(h^{\dagger}_{bk\sigma^{\prime}}h_{bj\sigma^{\prime}}h^{\dagger}_{bj\sigma}h_{bk\sigma}+h^{\dagger}_{bj\sigma^{\prime}}h_{bk\sigma^{\prime}}h^{\dagger}_{bk\sigma}h_{bj\sigma})\frac{-t_{b}}{V_{2}-V}h_{bk\beta}^{\dagger}h_{bi\beta}+({i\leftrightarrow j}),

(62)

which gives the term

i
and

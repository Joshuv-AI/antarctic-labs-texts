Kernels of paired operators and their adjoints
M. Cristina Câmara, Jonathan R. Partington

Abstract
We review the basic properties of paired operators and their adjoints, the transposed paired operators, with particular reference to commutation relations, and we study the properties of their kernels, bringing out their similarities and also, somewhat surprisingly, their stark differences. Various notions expressing different invariance properties are also reviewed and we extend to paired operators some known invariance results.



Keywords:

paired operator; transposed paired operator; Toeplitz operator; invariant subspace;
almost-invariant subspace;
nearly-invariant subspace; truncated Toeplitz operator;
kernel.

Introduction

The canonical example of a singular integral equation on
L^{p}(\mathbb{T})
,
1<p<\infty
, is

a(t)f(t)+b(t)(S_{\mathbb{T}}f)(t)=g(t)

(1.1)

where
a
and
b
are bounded functions in
\mathbb{T}
,
g
is a given function in
L^{p}(\mathbb{T})
and
S_{\mathbb{T}}
is the singular integral operator with Cauchy kernel defined by

(S_{\mathbb{T}}f)(t)=\frac{1}{\pi i}\hbox{PV}\int_{\mathbb{T}}{\frac{f(\tau)}{\tau-t}d\tau}.

(1.2)

The boundedness, Fredholmness and invertibility of the operator

T=aI+bS_{\mathbb{T}}

(1.3)

defined by the left hand side of (
1.1
), as well as methods to obtain explicit solutions to that equation and systems of equations of the same type, have been extensively studied, mainly in connection with factorisation of Wiener–Hopf type (see for example
[
30
,
14
,
21
]
and references therein). In general certain conditions are imposed on
a
and
b
, such as
a^{2}-b^{2}
being bounded away from zero
[
30
,
21
]
. For
p=2
, it was Shinbrot who first derived a constructive general method to find explicit solutions to (
1.1
), by placing it in an abstract Hilbert space setting, in
[
28
]
, using the fact that

I=P^{+}+P^{-}\qquad\hbox{and}\qquad S_{\mathbb{T}}=P^{+}-P^{-},

(1.4)

where
P^{\pm}
are the orthogonal projections from
L^{2}
onto
H_{+}^{2}:=H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
and
H_{-}^{2}=(H_{+}^{2})^{\perp}
, respectively. Indeed (
1.4
) implies that (
1.3
) can be written as a
paired operator

[
21
]

S_{A,B}=AP+BQ

(1.5)

where
A
and
B
are bounded operators and
P
,
Q
are complementary projections on a Banach space. In
[
28
]
Shinbrot assumed that
A^{-1}
existed and
A^{-1}B
was selfadjoint and of single sign, noting that the situation would be much more complicated in the general case.

Naturally, one can also consider singular integral equations of the form

af+bS_{\mathbb{T}}f=g

(1.6)

to which, by (
1.4
), one can associate in a similar way another paired operator of the form

\Sigma_{A,B}=P^{+}A+P^{-}B,

(1.7)

which we call a
transposed paired operator
.

If
A
and
B
are invertible operators, then (
1.5
) and (
1.7
) are said to be of
normal type
[
21
]
. This is by far the most studied case. Then we have that any operator of the form (
1.7
) is equivalent to an operator of the form (
1.5
), i.e., they are related by products with invertible operators:

PA+QB=[(I+PAB^{-1}Q)A](B^{-1}P+A^{-1}Q)[(I-QAB^{-1}P)B].

(1.8)

Moreover, if
AB=BA
, then
[
21
, III, Theorem 1.2]

PA+QB=[(I+PAB^{-1}Q)B^{-1}](AP+BQ)[(I-QAB^{-1}P)B].

(1.9)

This is true, in particular, if
A
and
B
are multiplication operators on
L^{2}
, as it happens in the case of singular integral operators.

Thus, for many purposes (such as the study of boundedness, Fredholmness, invertibility, solvability of equations) it suffices to consider only one type of paired operators. This may explain why transposed paired operators have hardly been considered separately in the literature on this topic, in spite of renewed interest in recent years
[
15
,
29
,
22
,
23
,
11
,
4
,
8
]
.

In this paper we shall work in the context
X=L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
, where
\mathbb{T}
is the unit circle, and
P=P^{+}
,
Q=P^{-}
. For functions
a,b\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})=:L^{\infty}
, we take
A=M_{a}
and
B=M_{b}
, the corresponding multiplication operators. Then we write
S_{a,b}
and
\Sigma_{a,b}
for the corresponding paired and transposed paired operators, i.e.,

S_{a,b}=aP^{+}+bP^{-}\qquad\hbox{and}\qquad\Sigma_{a,b}=P^{+}aI+P^{-}bI.

(1.10)

Writing
T_{a}
for the Toeplitz operator
P^{+}aP^{+}
on
H^{2}_{+}
, we see that

P^{+}S_{a,b}{}_{|H^{2}_{+}}=P^{+}\Sigma_{a,b}{}_{|H^{2}_{+}}=T_{a},

so that paired operators are dilations of Toeplitz operators. Moreover, if
b\in GL^{\infty}
(that is, invertible in
L^{\infty}
), then
S_{a,b}
and
\Sigma_{a,b}
are equivalent, and both are equivalent after extension to
T_{a/b}

[
3
]
(see also
[
1
]
for more on these concepts). Hence we have an isomorphism of kernels:

\hbox{ker}\Sigma_{a,b}\approx\hbox{ker}S_{a,b}\approx\hbox{ker}T_{a/b}.

(1.11)

The papers
[
4
]
and
[
8
]
were mainly concerned with paired operators
S_{a,b}
, but the theory of transposed paired operators is also very rich, and we shall also be discussing these, bringing out some of the similarities and differences.

We shall assume throughout the paper that
a,b\in L^{\infty}
and

a\neq 0,\quad b\neq 0\quad\hbox{a.e. on }\mathbb{T},\quad\hbox{and}\quad a=b\quad\hbox{or}\quad a-b\neq 0\quad\hbox{a.e. on }\mathbb{T}.

(1.12)

If
a\neq b
then we say that the pair
(a,b)
is
nondegenerate
.

After describing some of the basic properties of paired operators in Section
2
we shall present some of the properties of paired kernels in Section
3
, before moving on to transposed paired kernels in Section
4
, in which the similarities and differences between the two notions will be identified. Finally, Section
5
is devoted to reviewing various notions expressing different invariance properties, such as almost-invariant and nearly-invariant subspaces for a given operator, analysing their relations and extending some known invariance results to kernels of paired operators. Along the way we get new insights into Toeplitz and Hankel operators and new formulations of known results for Toeplitz kernels.

Some notation: we shall write
\tilde{T}_{a}
for the dual Toeplitz operator
P^{-}aP^{-}
on
H_{-}^{2}
and
\tilde{H}_{a}
for the Hankel operator
P^{+}aP^{-}:H_{-}^{2}\rightarrow H_{+}^{2}
. In addition, we shall say that
O_{-}\in H_{-}^{2}
(resp.
\overline{H^{\infty}}
) is
outer
if
\overline{z}\overline{O_{-}}
(resp.
\overline{O}_{-}
) is
outer
in
H_{+}^{2}
(resp.
H^{\infty}
).

2
Basic properties

We start by noting that the two types of paired operator,
S_{a,b}
and
\Sigma_{a,b}
,
are closely connected. Indeed, if
b\in\mathcal{G}L^{\infty}
, then
S_{a,b}
and
\Sigma_{a,b}
are equivalent, i.e., they
can be related by invertible operators

\Sigma_{a,b}=ES_{a,b}F

(2.1)

with
E,F
invertible
[
21
, Ch. III, Sec. 1]
.

Moreover, for any
a,b\in L^{\infty}
we have

S_{a,b}^{*}=\Sigma_{\overline{a},\overline{b}}

(2.2)

and

\overline{S_{a,b}f}=zS_{\overline{b},\overline{a}}\overline{z}\overline{f}=z\Sigma^{*}_{b,a}(\overline{z}\overline{f}),

(2.3)

so that some properties of

\Sigma_{a,b}
can be deduced easily from the corresponding properties
of
S_{a,b}
.
For example, we have the following optimal estimates from
[
4
]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

For
a,b\in L^{\infty}
, let
m=\max(\|a\|_{\infty},\|b\|_{\infty})

and
M=\|a\|_{\infty}+\|b\|_{\infty}
. Then

m\leq\|S_{a,b}\|=\|\Sigma_{\overline{a},\overline{b}}\|\leq\min(M,\sqrt{2}\,m).

Clearly, the same inequalities hold for
\Sigma_{a,b}
.
As an immediate consequence we have the following result, but we can also give
an easy direct proof.

Corollary 2.2
.

S_{a,b}=0\iff\Sigma_{a,b}=0\iff a=b=0,

(2.4)

and it follows that the symbol pair
(a,b)
is unique for any operator of the form
(
1.10
).

Proof.

If
S_{a,b}=0
, then, applying this to functions
\phi_{+}\in H^{2}_{+}
we see that
a\phi_{+}=0
for all
such functions and so
a=0
. Similarly, applying this to functions in
H^{2}_{-}
we see
that
b=0
. By (
2.2
) we obtain the same conclusion for
\Sigma_{a,b}
.
The converse is obvious.
∎

Since, for any
\eta\in L^{\infty}
,

M_{\eta}=S_{\eta,\eta}=\Sigma_{\eta,\eta},

we have that
S_{a,b}
(or
\Sigma_{a,b}
) is a multiplication operator on
L^{2}
if and only if
a=b
.

In the same way that nonzero Toeplitz operators cannot be compact (let alone finite rank),
a similar result holds for paired operators, and hence for transposed paired operators.

Proposition 2.3
.

The operators
S_{a,b}
and
\Sigma_{a,b}

can only be compact if
a=b=0
.

Proof.

Note that
\Sigma_{a,b}=P^{+}aI+P^{-}bI
, and this can
only be compact if the operators
P^{+}aI
and
P^{-}bI

are both compact, because a Hilbert space sequence
(x_{n}+y_{n})
with
\langle x_{n},y_{m}\rangle=0

for all
n
and
m
converges if and only if the two sequence
(x_{n})
and
(y_{n})
both
converge.

Now
P^{+}aI:L^{2}\to H^{2}_{+}
is an extension of the Toeplitz operator

T_{a}
on
H^{2}_{+}
and so is not compact unless
a=0
. Similarly for
P^{-}bI
.
Hence
\Sigma_{a,b}
is not compact unless
a=b=0
, and so neither is
S_{a,b}=\Sigma_{\overline{a},\overline{b}}^{*}
.
∎

We shall require certain commutation results from
[
4
, Prop. 3.1]
.
Since for two operators on a Hilbert space we have
XT=TX
if and only
if
T^{*}X^{*}=X^{*}T^{*}
, and indeed
the adjoint of the commutator
[X,T]=XT-TX
is the commutator

[T^{*},X^{*}]
, which has the same rank, we can immediately apply the results
we quote to the context of transposed paired kernels.
Similarly, questions on the commutant of two Toeplitz
operators, as analysed by Brown and Halmos in
[
2
]
,
have their generalizations to paired operators.
The first part of the following result is due to Gu
[
15
, Thm. 3.1]

and the second part follows by duality (this is a slight improvement on

[
4
, Lem. 3.1]
).

Proposition 2.4
.

For any nondegenerate
(a,b)
,
(\tilde{a},\tilde{b})
satisfying (
1.12
), we have:

(i)
S_{a,b}S_{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}}=S_{c,d}\iff\tilde{a}\in H^{\infty}\hbox{ and }\tilde{b}\in\overline{H^{\infty}}
, and in that case
c=a\tilde{a}
and
d=b\tilde{b}
;

(ii)
\Sigma_{a,b}\Sigma_{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}}=\Sigma_{c,d}\iff a\in\overline{H^{\infty}}\hbox{ and }b\in H^{\infty}
,
and in that case
c=a\tilde{a}
and
d=b\tilde{b}
.

As an immediate consequence of Proposition
2.4
we see the following.

Corollary 2.5
.

For nondegenerate
(a,b)
and
(\tilde{a},\tilde{b})
we have

S_{a,b}S_{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}}=0
if and only if
\tilde{a}\in H^{\infty}
,
\tilde{b}\in\overline{H^{\infty}}

and
a\tilde{a}=b\tilde{b}=0
.

More generally, we have by direct calculation (see also
[
4
, Prop. 3.2]
):

Proposition 2.6
.

(i)
S_{a,b}S_{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}}-S_{a\tilde{a},b\tilde{b}}=(a-b)(P^{+}\tilde{b}P^{-}-P^{-}\tilde{a}P^{+})
;

(ii)
\Sigma_{a,b}\Sigma_{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}}-\Sigma_{a\tilde{a},b\tilde{b}}=(P^{-}bP^{+}-P^{+}aP^{-})(\tilde{a}-\tilde{b})I
.

Hence, regarding the commutation relations for two paired operators or
transposed paired operators, we have the following.

Proposition 2.7
.

(i)
S_{a,b}S_{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}}-S_{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}}S_{a,b}=\\
\-\qquad\qquad(a-b)(P^{+}\tilde{b}P^{-}-P^{-}\tilde{a}P^{+})-(\tilde{a}-\tilde{b})(P^{+}bP^{-}-P^{-}aP^{+})
;

(ii)
\Sigma_{a,b}\Sigma_{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}}-\Sigma_{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}}\Sigma_{a,b}=\\
\-\qquad\qquad(P^{+}\tilde{a}P^{-}-P^{-}\tilde{b}P^{+})(a-b)I-(P^{+}aP^{-}-P^{-}bP^{+})(\tilde{a}-\tilde{b})I
.

The right hand sides of the equations in Propositions
2.6
and

2.7
are expressed in terms of operators of Hankel type, which allows us to
derive from these the following compactness results, where we
write
\mathcal{C}
for
C(\mathbb{T})
and
\mathcal{R}
for the space of all rational functions in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
.
(Standard results on Hankel operators can be found in
[
24
]
.)

Proposition 2.8
.

Suppose that
\tilde{a}\neq\tilde{b}
. Then:

(i)
S_{a,b}S_{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}}-S_{a\tilde{a},b\tilde{b}}
is a compact
(resp. finite rank) operator if
\tilde{a}\in H^{\infty}+\mathcal{C}
,
\tilde{b}\in\overline{H^{\infty}}+\mathcal{C}

(resp.
\tilde{a}\in H^{\infty}+\mathcal{R}
,
\tilde{b}\in\overline{H^{\infty}}+\mathcal{R}
).

(ii)
S_{a,b}S_{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}}-S_{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}}S_{a,b}
is a compact
(resp. finite rank operator) if
a,\tilde{a}\in H^{\infty}+\mathcal{C}
and
b,\tilde{b}\in\overline{H^{\infty}}+\mathcal{C}

(resp.
a,\tilde{a}\in H^{\infty}+\mathcal{R}
and
b,\tilde{b}\in\overline{H^{\infty}}+\mathcal{R}
).

(iii)
S_{a,b}\eta I-\eta S_{a,b}
is compact (resp. finite rank) if
\eta\in\mathcal{C}
(resp.
\eta\in\mathcal{R}
).

By duality, the analogous results for transposed paired operators
are the following.

Proposition 2.9
.

Suppose that
\tilde{a}\neq\tilde{b}
. Then:

(i)
\Sigma_{a,b}\Sigma_{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}}-\Sigma_{a\tilde{a},b\tilde{b}}
is a compact
(resp. finite rank) operator if
\tilde{a}\in\overline{H^{\infty}}+\mathcal{C}
,
\tilde{b}\in H^{\infty}+\mathcal{C}

(resp.
\tilde{a}\in\overline{H^{\infty}}+\mathcal{R}
,
\tilde{b}\in H^{\infty}+\mathcal{R}
).

(ii)
\Sigma_{a,b}\Sigma_{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}}-\Sigma_{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}}\Sigma_{a,b}
is a compact
(resp. finite rank operator) if
a,\tilde{a}\in\overline{H^{\infty}}+\mathcal{C}
and
b,\tilde{b}\in H^{\infty}+\mathcal{C}

(resp.
a,\tilde{a}\in\overline{H^{\infty}}+\mathcal{R}
and
b,\tilde{b}\in H^{\infty}+\mathcal{R}
).

(iii)
\Sigma_{a,b}\eta I-\eta\Sigma_{a,b}
is compact (resp. finite rank) if
\eta\in\mathcal{C}
(resp.
\eta\in\mathcal{R}
).

In particular, it is clear from (i) and (ii) in Proposition
2.7
that

[S_{a,b},S_{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}}]=0
, that is,
S_{a,b}
and
S_{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}}
commute,
if one of the following conditions hold:

\displaystyle a=b\quad

\displaystyle\hbox{and}\quad\tilde{a}=\tilde{b};

(2.5)

\displaystyle a,\tilde{a}\in H^{\infty}\quad

\displaystyle\hbox{and}\quad b,\tilde{b}\in\overline{H^{\infty}};

(2.6)

\displaystyle a=\lambda\tilde{a}+\mu\quad

\displaystyle\hbox{and}\quad b=\lambda\tilde{b}+\mu,\quad\hbox{with}\quad\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb{C}.

(2.7)

The converse was provided in
[
15
, Thm. 4.1]
, and so we have:

Proposition 2.10
.

[S_{a,b},S_{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}}]=0
if and only if one of the conditions
(
2.5
)–(
2.7
) holds.

A similar property holds for
[\Sigma_{a,b},\Sigma_{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}}]
by (
2.2
).

As an illustration of the results in Proposition
2.8
we have, for nondegenerate
(a,b)
, that
[S_{a,b},M_{z}]
and
[S_{a,b},M_{\overline{z}}]
are rank-one operators, such that

\begin{array}[]{lcl}[S_{a,b},M_{z}](f)=&S_{a,b}zf-zS_{a,b}f=&(a-b)(\overline{z}\overline{f_{-}})(0),\\
{[S_{a,b},M_{\overline{z}}](f)}=&S_{a,b}\overline{z}f-\overline{z}S_{a,b}f=&(b-a)\overline{z}f_{+}(0),\end{array}

(2.8)

where
f_{\pm}=P^{\pm}f
.

Analogously,
[\Sigma_{a,b},M_{z}]
and
[\Sigma_{a,b},M_{\overline{z}}]
are also rank-one operators,
such that

\begin{array}[]{lcl}[\Sigma_{a,b},M_{z}](f)=&\Sigma_{a,b}zf-z\Sigma_{a,b}f=&[zP^{-}(a-b)f](\infty),\\
{[\Sigma_{a,b},M_{\overline{z}}](f)}=&\Sigma_{a,b}\overline{z}f-\overline{z}\Sigma_{a,b}f=&-\overline{z}[P^{+}(a-b)f](0).\end{array}

(2.9)

Using Proposition
2.7
we also get the following, which is

[
4
, Cor. 3.3]
.

Corollary 2.11
.

If
(a,b)
is nondegenerate and
\eta\in L^{\infty}
then

S_{a,b}\eta I=\eta S_{a,b}\iff\eta\in\mathbb{C}\iff\Sigma_{a,b}\eta I=\eta\Sigma_{a,b}.

Proof.

Note that
\eta I=S_{\eta,\eta}
and it follows that

\eta S_{a,b}=S_{a,b}\eta I\iff P^{-}\eta P^{+}=P^{+}\eta P^{-},

which holds if and only if
\eta\in\mathbb{C}
. Once again, the result for
\Sigma_{a,b}

follows by duality.
∎

For
\eta\in L^{\infty}\setminus\mathbb{C}
one may then ask which functions
f\in L^{2}
satisfy the relation
S_{a,b}\eta f=\eta S_{a,b}f
. We have the following from

[
4
, Prop. 3.4]
.

Proposition 2.12
.

For
\eta\in L^{\infty}
,
(a,b)
nondegenerate, and
f\in L^{2}
,

\displaystyle\eta S_{a,b}f=S_{a,b}(\eta f)

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle f\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits H_{\eta}\oplus\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\tilde{H}_{\eta}

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\eta f_{+}\in H^{2}_{+},\eta f_{-}\in H^{2}_{-}

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\eta f_{+}=P^{+}(\eta f)

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\eta f_{-}=P^{-}(\eta f),

where the Hankel operators
H_{\eta}
and
\tilde{H}_{\eta}
are defined by

H_{\eta}=P^{-}\eta P^{+}{}_{|H^{2}_{+}}
and
\tilde{H}_{\eta}=P^{+}\eta P^{-}{}_{|H^{2}_{-}}
,
and we write
f_{\pm}=P^{\pm}f
.

So

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits[S_{a,b},\eta I]=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits H_{\eta}\oplus\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\tilde{H}_{\eta}.

Thus, in particular, for nondegenerate
(a,b)
, the space of all functions
f\in L^{2}
such that

S_{a,b}zf=zS_{a,b}f
is given by

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits[S_{a,b},M_{z}]=\overline{z}H^{2}_{-}\oplus H^{2}_{+}=\{f\in L^{2}:(\overline{z}\overline{f_{-}})(0)=0\}

(2.10)

and, analogously,

S_{a,b}\overline{z}f=\overline{z}S_{a,b}f\iff f\in H^{2}_{-}\oplus zH^{2}_{+}\iff f_{+}(0)=0.

(2.11)

We may prove a similar result for transposed paired kernels.

Proposition 2.13
.

For
\eta\in L^{\infty}
,
(a,b)
nondegenerate, and
f\in L^{2}
, we have

\Sigma_{a,b}\eta f=\eta\Sigma_{a,b}f\iff(a-b)f\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits[S_{a,b},\eta I]
,
and hence
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits[\Sigma_{a,b},\eta I]=\{f\in L^{2}:(a-b)f\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits H_{\eta}\oplus\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\tilde{H}_{\eta}\}
.

Proof.

P^{+}a\eta f+P^{-}b\eta f=\eta P^{+}af+\eta P^{-}bf\iff P^{+}a\eta f-P^{+}b\eta f=\eta P^{+}af-\eta P^{+}bf
.
This holds if and only if
P^{+}\eta(a-b)f=\eta P^{+}(a-b)f
,
and so by Proposition
2.12
if and only if

(a-b)f\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits[S_{a,b},\eta I]
.
∎

Finally, one may ask when a transposed paired operator
\Sigma_{a,b}=S_{\overline{a},\overline{b}}^{*}
is
also a paired operator. We have the following answer from
[
15
, Prop. 2.2, Cor. 2.3]
,
to which we provide an alternative proof.

Proposition 2.14
.

S^{*}_{a,b}=S_{c,d}
for some
c,d\in L^{\infty}
if and
only if
a-b\in\mathbb{C}
, and, in that case,
S^{*}_{a,b}=S_{\overline{a},\overline{b}}
. Thus
S_{a,b}

is self-adjoint if and only if
a
and
b
are real-valued functions with
a-b\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

Suppose that

P^{+}\overline{a}f+P^{-}\overline{b}f=cP^{+}f+dP^{-}f\qquad\hbox{for all}\quad f\in L^{2}.

(2.12)

Then, for
f=1
, we get that
P^{+}\overline{a}+P^{-}\overline{b}=c
, so
P^{+}\overline{a}=P^{+}c
and

P^{-}\overline{b}=P^{-}c
.
On the other hand, taking
f=\overline{z}
, we get
P^{+}(\overline{a}\overline{z})+P^{-}(\overline{b}\overline{z})=d\overline{z}
, i.e.,

\overline{z}(P^{+}\overline{a})-\overline{z}(P^{+}\overline{a})(0)+\overline{z}(P^{-}\overline{b})+\overline{z}(P^{+}\overline{b})(0)=d\overline{z},

that is,
\overline{z}c-\overline{z}k=\overline{z}d
with
k=(P^{+}(\overline{a}-\overline{b}))(0)\in\mathbb{C}
.

Therefore,
c=d+k
and it follows from (
2.12
) that

P^{+}(\overline{a}-k)I+P^{-}\overline{b}I=dI

so, by Corollary
2.2
,
\overline{a}-k=\overline{b}=d=c-k
; hence

a-b\in\mathbb{C}
and
S^{*}_{a,b}=S_{c,d}
. The converse is straightforward.
∎

Turning our attention to kernels, and given the close connection between paired operators of the form
S_{a,b}
and transposed paired operators
of the form
\Sigma_{a,b}
, which was mentioned in the introduction and the beginning
of this section, one might expect their kernels to have very similar properties. Moreover, we have the following simple relation, which generalizes
[
4
, Prop. 6.4]
.

Proposition 2.15
.

For nondegenerate
(a,b)

there is a well-defined injective linear mapping
\mathcal{J}:\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{a,b}\to\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{a,b}

given by

\mathcal{J}\psi=(a-b)\psi.

(2.13)

If there exist
a^{\prime},b^{\prime}\in L^{\infty}
such that

aa^{\prime}+bb^{\prime}=1
, then
\mathcal{J}
is an isomorphism with inverse

\mathcal{J}^{-1}=a^{\prime}P^{-}-b^{\prime}P^{+}:\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{a,b}\to\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{a,b},

and in that case

(a-b)\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{a,b}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{a,b}.

Proof.

For the last part, note that
a^{\prime}P^{-}(a-b)\psi-b^{\prime}P^{+}(a-b)\psi=a^{\prime}(a\psi)+b^{\prime}(b\psi)=\psi

when
P^{+}(a\psi)=P^{-}(b\psi)=0
, i.e., when
\psi\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{a,b}
.
∎

Somewhat surprisingly, in spite of some similarities, the two types of kernels present
significantly different and even contrasting properties, as shown in the next two sections,
where we study each type of kernel separately.

3
Paired kernels

We start by noting that the kernels of paired operators, called
paired kernels
, are determined
by the solutions of a Riemann–Hilbert problem for the unknowns
\phi_{\pm}=P^{\pm}\phi
:

\phi\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{a,b}\iff a\phi_{+}+b\phi_{-}=0.

(3.1)

It is clear from (
3.1
) that paired kernels are closely connected with Toeplitz kernels. Indeed, defining the
Toeplitz operator

T_{a/b}:\mathcal{D}_{a/b}:=\{f\in H^{2}_{+}:(a/b)f\in L^{2}\}\to H^{2}_{+},

(3.2)

f\mapsto P^{+}(a/b)f,

(3.3)

which is bounded on
H^{2}_{+}
if
a/b\in L^{\infty}
, we have from (
3.3
) that

P^{+}\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{a,b}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{a/b}.

(3.4)

Nevertheless, no nonzero paired kernel can be a Toeplitz kernel, or even be contained in
H^{2}_{+}
.
This is a consequence of the following
[
8
, Rem. 4.2]
.

Proposition 3.1
.

If
\phi\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{a,b}
then

\phi=0\iff\phi_{+}=0\iff\phi_{-}=0
.
Therefore,

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{a,b}\cap H^{2}_{+}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{a,b}\cap H^{2}_{-}=\{0\}.

(3.5)

Clearly, for any measurable complex-valued function
\eta
defined a.e. on
\mathbb{T}
,
such that
\eta\neq 0
a.e., we have that

a\phi_{+}+b\phi_{-}=0\iff a\eta\phi_{+}+b\eta\phi_{-}=0

so, for any
\eta\in L^{\infty}
with
\eta\neq 0
a.e.,

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{a,b}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{a\eta,b\eta}.

(3.6)

The next results show that this is the only case where two paired kernels are equal.

Proposition 3.2
.

[
4
, Prop. 4.3]

If
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{a,b}\neq\{0\}
then, for any nondegenerate
\tilde{a},\tilde{b}
satisfying the assumption (
1.12
) we have

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{a,b}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}}\iff a\tilde{b}=\tilde{a}b.

(3.7)

As a consequence,
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{a,b}
is uniquely determined by the quotient
a/b
, which is the same
as the quotient
-\phi_{-}/\phi_{+}
for any nonzero element of
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{a,b}
, by (
3.1
). Thus we get the following striking property of paired kernels
[
4
, Thm. 4.6]
.

Theorem 3.3
.

For each
\phi\in L^{2}\setminus\{0\}
there is one and only one paired kernel to which

\phi
belongs. If
\phi_{+}=I_{+}O_{+}
and
\phi_{-}=I_{-}O_{-}
, where
I_{+},\overline{I_{-}}
are inner functions
and
O_{\pm}
outer in
H^{2}_{\pm}
, then

\phi\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{a,b},\quad\hbox{with}\quad a=\overline{I_{+}}H_{1+}\overline{h_{2}+},\quad b=-z\overline{I_{-}}\,\overline{h_{1+}}H_{2+},

(3.8)

where
H_{1+},H_{2+},h_{1+},h_{2+}\in H^{\infty}
are such that

O^{-1}_{+}=\frac{H_{1+}}{H_{2+}},\quad z\overline{O^{-1}_{-}}=\frac{h_{1+}}{h_{2+}}.

(3.9)

Proof.

To derive the formulae for
a
and
b
we note that

\frac{\overline{I_{+}}}{O_{+}}\phi_{+}=\frac{\overline{I_{-}}}{O_{-}}\phi_{-}.

Now
O_{+}^{-1}
belongs to the Smirnoff class
\mathcal{N}_{+}
so there are
H_{1+}

and
H_{2+}\in H^{\infty}
such that
O_{+}^{-1}=H_{1+}/H_{2+}
; similarly

z\overline{O_{-}}^{-1}=h_{1+}/h_{2+}
with
h_{1+},h_{2+}\in H^{\infty}
.
The rest is clear.
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

If
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{a.b}\cap\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}}\neq\{0\}
, then

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{a,b}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}}
.

Corollary 3.5
.

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{a,b}\subseteq\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}}\iff\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{a,b}=\{0\}
or
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{a,b}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}}
.

When do we have
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{a,b}\neq\{0\}
? From the proof of
[
4
, Thm. 4.6]

we obtain a necessary and sufficient condition for a nonzero kernel.

Proposition 3.6
.

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{a,b}\neq\{0\}
if and only if

\frac{a}{b}=I_{-}O_{-}O_{+}^{-1},

(3.10)

where
I_{+}
is inner and
O_{\pm}
are outer in
H^{2}_{\pm}
.

Proof.

If (
3.10
) holds then
aO_{+}-bI_{-}O_{-}=0
, so that
O_{+}-I_{-}O_{-}\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{a,b}
.

Conversely, if
a\phi_{+}+b\phi_{-}=0
, then by taking inner–outer factorizations
we easily obtain (
3.10
).
∎

Note that this coincides with the necessary and sufficient
condition for
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{a/b}
to be nontrivial, when
a/b\in L^{\infty}
, obtained in

[
20
,
7
]
.

An analogue of Coburn’s lemma for Toeplitz kernels
[
10
]

also holds for paired kernels. To see this, however, it is useful to reformulate Coburn’s
lemma, usually expressed as:

\hbox{if}\quad g\neq 0\quad\hbox{then either}\quad\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g}=\{0\}\quad\hbox{or}\quad\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T^{*}_{g}=\{0\}.

Note that, while the adjoint of a Toeplitz operator is also a Toeplitz operator, the adjoint of a paired operator
is not, in general, a paired operator of the same type. Noting, however, that we
can always assume that the symbol in
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g}
satisfies
|g|=1

[
16
]

and, on the other hand,

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g}\cong\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{g,1},\quad\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T^{*}_{g}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\overline{g}}\cong\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{\overline{g},1}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{1,g}

by (
1.11
), we can also formulate Coburn’s lemma, equivalently,
as follows, in terms of paired operators.

Theorem 3.7
.

(Coburn’s lemma) If
|g|=1
then either
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{g,1}=\{0\}

or
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{1,g}=\{0\}
.

We can formulate the following analogues for paired kernels, which slightly generalize Theorem 6.8 in
[
4
]
.

Theorem 3.8
.

(i) Either
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{a,b}=\{0\}
or
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{b,a}=\{0\}
.

(ii) If there exist
a^{\prime},b^{\prime}\in L^{\infty}
such that
aa^{\prime}+bb^{\prime}=1
, then either

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{a,b}=\{0\}
or
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S^{*}_{a,b}=\{0\}
.

Proof.

(i) Suppose that there exist
\phi,\psi\neq 0
with
\phi\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{a,b}
and

\psi\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{b,a}
. Then we have

a\phi_{+}=-b\phi_{-}\qquad\hbox{and}\qquad b\psi_{+}=-a\psi_{-}.

Therefore
ab\phi_{+}\psi_{+}=ab\phi_{-}\psi_{-}
, and, since
a,b\neq 0
a.e. on
\mathbb{T}
,
it follows that

\underbrace{\phi_{+}\psi_{+}}_{\in H^{1}}=\underbrace{\phi_{-}\psi_{-}}_{\in\overline{H^{1}_{0}}},

and so both terms above are
0
.
This implies that either
\phi_{+}=0
or
\psi_{+}=0
and hence, by Proposition
3.1
,

\phi=0
or
\psi=0
, which is a contradiction.

(ii) This follows from Proposition
2.15
, since

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S^{*}_{a,b}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{\overline{a},\overline{b}}=\mathcal{J}^{-1}\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{\overline{a},\overline{b}}=\mathcal{J}^{-1}(J\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{b,a}),

where
\mathcal{J}
was defined in Proposition
2.15
and
J:\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{b,a}\to\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{\overline{a},\overline{b}}
, defined by
J\phi=\overline{z}\overline{\phi}
, is a bijective antilinear operator.
∎

4
Transposed paired kernels

It is not difficult to see that

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{a,b}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\phi\in L^{2}:P^{+}a\phi=P^{-}b\phi=0\}

(4.1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\phi\in L^{2}:a\phi=\psi_{-},\quad b\phi=\psi_{+}\quad\hbox{with}\quad\psi_{\pm}\in H^{2}_{\pm}\}

(4.2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle L^{2}\cap a^{-1}H^{2}_{-}\cap b^{-1}H^{2}_{+},\quad\hbox{for non-degenerate }(a,b).

(4.3)

Thus, in contrast with paired kernels, the elements of a transposed paired kernel are not, in general, determined by
the solution of a scalar Riemann–Hilbert problem as in (
3.1
).
As a consequence, transposed paired kernels possess properties that are different, and even
in contrast, with those of paired kernels and they have different relations with Toeplitz kernels.

Defining
T_{a/b}
as in (
3.2
) and (
3.3
), and taking into
account the fact that

a^{-1}H^{2}_{-}\cap b^{-1}H^{2}_{+}=b^{-1}\left(\frac{b}{a}H^{2}_{-}\cap H^{2}_{+}\right),

we have, from (
4.3
):

Proposition 4.1
.

(i)
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{a,b}=\left(\dfrac{1}{b}\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{a/b}\right)\cap L^{2}
;

(ii)

b\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{a,b}\subseteq\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{a/b}

(4.4)

and, if
b\in\mathcal{G}L^{\infty}
, then

b\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{a,b}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{a/b}.

(4.5)

In particular, we see from (
4.5
) that

b\in\mathcal{G}H^{\infty}\implies\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{a,b}=\frac{1}{b}\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{a/b}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{a},

(4.6)

so, in contrast with the paired kernels studied in Section
3
,
nontrivial transposed paired kernels can be Toeplitz kernels. Indeed, we have the following.

Proposition 4.2
.

Every Toeplitz kernel can be written as

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{a}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{a,b}\qquad\hbox{for any}\quad b\in H^{\infty},\quad\hbox{outer}.

(4.7)

Proof.

If
b\in H^{\infty}
is outer, then
b^{-1}H^{2}_{+}\cap L^{2}=H^{2}_{+}
and it
follows from (
4.3
) that
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{a,b}=a^{-1}H^{2}_{-}\cap H^{2}_{+}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{a}
.
∎

Corollary 4.3
.

If
a\in\overline{H^{\infty}}
and
b\in H^{\infty}
, with
b
outer, then
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{a,b}

is a model space
K_{\theta}
, where
\theta
is the inner factor of
\overline{a}
. In
particular,

K_{\theta}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{\overline{\theta},1}.

(4.8)

These results naturally raise the question of how to characterise
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{a,b}\cap H^{2}_{\pm}
, and, in particular, when
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{a,b}
is contained in
H^{2}_{\pm}

or vice-versa. The answer
reveals some interesting connections of transposed paired kernels with Toeplitz and Hankel operators.

Proposition 4.4
.

(i)

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{a,b}\cap H^{2}_{+}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{a}\cap\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits H_{b},

(4.9)

(ii)

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{a,b}\cap H^{2}_{-}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\widetilde{H}_{a}\cap\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\widetilde{T}_{b}.

Proof.

(i) and (ii) follow straightforwardly from (
4.1
).
∎

The following lemma will be used several times in what follows.

Lemma 4.5
.

If
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{a,b}\neq\{0\}
then there exist
\phi\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{a,b}
such that
a\phi=\psi_{-}
with
\psi_{-}\in H^{2}_{-}
, outer, and
\check{\phi}\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{a,b}
such that

b\check{\phi}=\psi_{+}
with
\psi_{+}\in H^{2}_{+}
, outer.

Proof.

Let
0\neq\tilde{\phi}\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{a,b}
; then
a\tilde{\phi}=\psi_{-}
and
b\tilde{\phi}=\tilde{\psi}_{+}

where
\tilde{\psi}_{\pm}\in H^{2}_{\pm}
. Let
\tilde{\psi}_{-}=I_{-}\psi_{-}
, where
\overline{I_{-}}
is inner and
\psi_{-}
is outer in
H^{2}_{-}
. Then we have

a(\overline{I_{-}}\tilde{\phi})=\psi_{-}
,
b(\overline{I_{-}}\tilde{\phi})=\overline{I_{-}}\tilde{\psi}_{+}\in H^{2}_{+}
, so

\phi:=\overline{I_{-}}\tilde{\phi}
satisfies
a\phi=\psi_{-}
with
\psi_{-}\in H^{2}_{-}
, outer.
We can prove similarly the second part of the proposition.
∎

Proposition 4.6
.

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{a,b}\subseteq H^{2}_{+}\iff\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{a,b}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{a}\cap\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits H_{b}
,
and this occurs if and only if there exists
\psi^{o}_{+}\in H^{2}_{+}
, outer, such that

\psi^{o}_{+}/b\in H^{2}_{+}
.

Proof.

The first equivalence is a consequence of Proposition
4.4
(i).
For the second equivalence, suppose that
\psi_{+}^{o}/b\in H^{2}_{+}
with
\psi^{o}_{+}\in H^{2}_{+}
,
outer. Then for any
\phi\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{a,b}
we have that
b\phi=\psi_{+}\in H^{2}_{+}
,
so

\phi=\frac{\psi_{+}}{b}=\frac{\psi_{+}(\psi_{+}^{o}/b)}{\psi_{+}^{o}}\in\mathcal{N}^{+}\cap L^{2}=H^{2}_{+}.

(Here
\mathcal{N}_{+}
is the Smirnoff class
[
13
, Thm. 2.11]
.)

Conversely, if
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{a,b}\subseteq H^{2}_{+}
, then by Lemma
4.5

there exists
\psi^{o}_{+}\in H^{2}_{+}
, outer, such that
b\check{\phi}=\psi^{o}_{+}
and

\check{\phi}\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{a,b}\subseteq H^{2}_{+}
,
so
\psi^{o}_{+}/b\in H^{2}_{+}
.
∎

Proposition 4.7
.

H^{2}_{+}\subseteq\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{a,b}\iff a=0\quad\hbox{and}\quad b\in H^{\infty}

(4.10)

and

H^{2}_{+}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{a,b}\iff a=0,\ b\in H^{\infty}\quad\hbox{with}\quad b\hbox{ outer}.

Proof.

If
H^{2}_{+}\subseteq\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{a,b}
then

H^{2}_{+}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{a,b}\cap H^{2}_{+}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{a}\cap\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits H_{b}
.
It follows that
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{a}=H^{2}_{+}
, so
a=0
and
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits H_{b}=H^{2}_{+}
, so

b\in H^{\infty}
. The converse is obvious. The conditions for equality follow from
(
4.10
) and Proposition
4.6
.
∎

Analogous results hold if we replace
H^{2}_{+}
with
H^{2}_{-}
, showing that
we can also have
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{a,b}\subseteq H^{2}_{-}
,

H^{2}_{-}\subseteq\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{a,b}
and
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{a,b}=H^{2}_{-}
.
In particular, if
h_{+}\in H^{\infty}
is outer, then

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{\overline{h_{+}},\theta}=\overline{z}\overline{K_{\theta}}=\overline{\theta}K_{\theta}\subseteq H^{2}_{-}.

(4.11)

Now, it is clear that, in general, for
\eta\in L^{\infty}
,

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{a,b}\neq\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{a\eta,b\eta},

(4.12)

in contrast with (
3.6
). The question then is when two transposed kernels are equal, or related by inclusion.
Here, again, the results are in contrast with those previously obtained
for paired kernels. First, however, we prove the following.

Proposition 4.8
.

If
\phi\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{a,b}
and
\phi\neq 0
, then
\phi
cannot vanish on a subset of
\mathbb{T}

with positive measure.

Proof.

If
\phi
vanishes on a set
E\subset\mathbb{T}
with poisitve measure, then

a\phi=\psi_{-}=0
(since it is in
H^{2}_{-}
and vanishes on
E
), and
b\phi=\psi_{+}=0

(similarly), so
\phi=0
.
∎

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{a,b}\neq\{0\}
. Then:

(i)
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{a,b}\subseteq\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}}
if and only if

\tilde{a}/a=\tilde{\psi}_{-}/\psi_{-}
with
\psi_{-},\tilde{\psi}_{-}\in H^{2}_{-}
and
\psi_{-}
outer, and

\tilde{b}/b=\tilde{\psi}_{+}/\psi_{+}
with
\psi_{+},\tilde{\psi}_{+}\in H^{2}_{+}
and
\psi_{+}
outer;

(ii)
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{a,b}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}}
if and only if
\tilde{a}/a
and
\tilde{b}/b
can be
written as in (i) with
\tilde{\psi}_{-}
and
\tilde{\psi}_{+}
outer in
H^{2}_{-}
and
H^{2}_{+}
, respectively;

(iii) if
h_{-}\in\overline{H^{\infty}}
and
h_{+}\in H^{\infty}
, then

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{a,b}\subseteq\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{ah_{-},bh_{+}},

where the inclusion is strict if
\overline{h_{-}}
or
h_{+}

has a nonconstant inner factor, and equality if
h_{-}
and
h_{+}
are outer functions.

Proof.

(i) Let
\{0\}\neq\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{a,b}\subseteq\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}}
and let

\phi\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{a,b}
with
a\phi=\psi_{-}\in H^{2}_{-}
,
\psi_{-}
outer
(see Lemma
4.5
). Then we have

a\phi=\psi_{-},\quad\tilde{a}\phi=\tilde{\psi}_{-}\in H^{2}_{-},

so
\tilde{a}/a=\tilde{\psi}_{-}/\psi_{-}
with
\psi_{-},\tilde{\psi}_{-}\in H^{2}_{-}
and
\psi_{-}
outer.
Analogously, using Lemma
4.5
, we can write
\tilde{b}/b=\tilde{\psi}_{+}/\psi_{+}
with

\psi_{+},\tilde{\psi}_{+}\in H^{2}_{+}
and
\psi_{+}
outer.

Conversely, if
\tilde{a}/a=\tilde{\psi}_{-}/\psi_{-}
and
\tilde{b}/b=\tilde{\psi}_{+}/\psi_{+}
, where

\tilde{\psi}_{\pm},\psi_{\pm}\in H^{2}_{\pm}
, with
\psi_{\pm}
, and
\phi\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{a,b}
, then

a\phi=\phi_{-}\in H^{2}_{-}
,
b\phi=\phi_{+}\in H^{2}_{+}
, and

\displaystyle\tilde{a}\phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{a}}{a}(a\phi)=\frac{\tilde{\psi}_{-}}{\psi_{i}}\in\overline{z}\overline{\mathcal{N}_{+}}\cap L^{2}=H^{2}_{-},

\displaystyle\tilde{b}\phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{b}}{b}(b\phi)=\frac{\tilde{\psi}_{+}}{\psi_{+}}\phi_{+}\in\mathcal{N}_{+}\cap L^{2}=H^{2}_{+}

where
\mathcal{N}_{+}
is the Smirnoff class
[
13
, Thm. 2.11]
. So
\phi\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}}
.

(ii) If
\psi_{-},\tilde{\psi}_{-}\in H^{2}_{-}
are outer and
\psi_{+},\tilde{\psi}_{+}\in H^{2}_{+}
are outer, then by (i) we have

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{a,b}\subseteq\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}}
and
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}}\subseteq\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{a,b}
.

Conversely, assume that
\tilde{a}/a=\tilde{\psi}_{-}/\psi_{-}
and
\tilde{b}/b=\tilde{\psi}_{+}/\psi_{+}
as in (i), where, for instance
\tilde{\psi}_{-}\in H^{2}_{-}
is not outer, so

\tilde{a}/a=\overline{\theta}\eta_{-}/\psi_{-}
with
\theta
inner and
\eta_{-},\psi_{-}\in H^{2}_{-}
outer.
Then by (i) we have that
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{a,b}\subseteq\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}}
, but we cannot have

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}}\subseteq\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{a,b}
.
Indeed, let
\psi\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}}
be such that (see Lemma
4.5
)

\tilde{a}\phi=\phi_{-}
with
\phi_{-}
outer in
H^{2}_{-}
.
If
\phi\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{a,b}
then we also have
a\phi=\chi_{-}\in H^{2}_{-}
; from
this it follows that

\tilde{a}\phi=a\overline{\theta}\frac{\eta_{-}}{\psi_{-}}\phi=\phi_{-}\quad\hbox{and}\quad a\phi=\chi_{-},

so that
\phi_{-}=\overline{\theta}\dfrac{\eta_{-}}{\psi_{-}}\chi_{-}
, which is impossible because
\phi_{-}
is outer in
H^{2}_{-}
.

(iii) This is a consequence of (i) and (ii).
∎

When is
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{a,b}\neq\{0\}
? We have the following necessary and sufficient condition.

Proposition 4.10
.

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{a,b}\neq\{0\}
if and only if there exist
I_{-},O_{-},O_{+}
such that
\overline{I_{-}}
is inner,
O_{\pm}\in H^{2}_{\pm}

are outer and

\frac{a}{b}=I_{-}O_{-}O^{-1}_{+},\quad\frac{O_{-}}{a},\frac{O_{+}}{b}\in L^{2}.

(4.13)

Proof.

Let
\phi\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{a,b}
,
\phi\neq 0
. Since
a\phi=\psi_{-}\in H^{2}_{-}
and
b\phi=\psi_{+}\in H^{2}_{+}
,
we have that
a/b=\psi_{-}/\psi_{+}
can be written as in (
4.13
), where
\psi_{-}=J_{-}O_{-}
and

\psi_{+}=J_{+}O_{+}
with
\overline{J_{-}},J_{+}
inner and
O_{\pm}\in H^{2}_{\pm}
outer, and then
I_{-}=J_{-}/J_{+}
.

We then have
O_{-}/a=\overline{J_{-}}\phi\in L^{2}
and
O_{+}/b=\overline{J_{+}}\phi\in L^{2}
.

Conversely, if (
4.13
) holds, then

\displaystyle a\frac{O_{+}}{b}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(I_{-}O_{-}O^{-1}_{+}b)\frac{O_{+}}{b}=I_{-}O_{-}\in H^{2}_{-},

\displaystyle b\frac{O_{+}}{b}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle O_{+}\in H^{2}_{+},

so
0\neq O_{+}/b\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{a,b}
.
∎

Remark 4.11
.

Comparing (
4.13
) with (
3.10
) in Proposition
3.6
,
and taking Proposition
2.15
into account, we see that if
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{a,b}\neq\{0\}
then
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{a,b}\neq\{0\}
. However, we may have

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{a,b}=\{0\}
and
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{a,b}\neq\{0\}
.
For example,
let
a(z)=1+1/z
and
b(z)=z+1
. Then with
f(z)=1-1/z
we have
f\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{a,b}
since

(1+1/z)P^{+}f+(z+1)P^{-}f=1+1/z-(z+1)/z=0.

But
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{a,b}=\{0\}
, since
if
P_{+}(af)=P_{-}(bf)=0
then
(1+1/z)f\in H^{2}_{-}
and
(z+1)f\in H^{2}_{+}
,
which implies that
(z+1)f\in zH^{2}_{-}\cap H^{2}_{+}=\mathbb{C}
. But this
is impossible unless
f=0
, since
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
.

Indeed, in this case
a/b=\overline{z}
(i.e.
I_{-}=\overline{z}
,
O_{-}=O_{+}=1
), but

1/a,1/b\not\in L^{2}
.

Another striking difference between paired kernels and transposed paired kernels is expressed by the following property.

Proposition 4.12
.

Not every
\phi\in L^{2}
belongs to a transposed paired kernel.
In particular, if
\phi
vanishes on a set of positive measure in
\mathbb{T}
,
then
\phi
does not belong to
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{a,b}
for any
a,b
, unless
\phi=0
.

Proof.

If
\phi\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{a,b}
then
P^{+}a\phi=P^{-}b\phi=0
. Thus

\phi=h_{+}/b
for some
h_{+}\in H^{2}_{+}
and, if
\phi

vanishes on a set of positive measure, then
h_{+}=0
.
∎

However we do get an analogue of Coburn’s lemma in the case of transposed paired kernels, as in the case of paired kernels.

Proposition 4.13
.

If
a,b\neq 0
a.e., then either

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{a,b}
or
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{b,a}
is
0
.

Proof.

This follows from Proposition
2.15
and Theorem
3.8
.
∎

5
Invariance properties of paired kernels

It is easy to see that if
X
and
T
are commuting operators on a Banach space
\mathcal{X}

then
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T
is invariant for
X
.
Thus from the commutation relations of Section
2

(namely Proposition
2.10
)
we have the
following.

Proposition 5.1
.

[
4
, Prop. 5.1]

If
a,\tilde{a}\in H^{\infty}
and
b,\tilde{b}\in\overline{H^{\infty}}
,
then
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{a,b}
is invariant for
S_{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}}
. If

a,\tilde{a}\in\overline{H^{\infty}}
and
b,\tilde{b}\in H^{\infty}
,
then
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{a,b}
is invariant for
\Sigma_{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}}
.

In particular, since for any inner function
\theta
, we have
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{\overline{\theta},1}=K_{\theta}
and, on the other hand,

\Sigma_{\overline{\theta},1}\Sigma_{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}}=\Sigma_{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}}\Sigma_{\overline{\theta},1}
if

\tilde{a}\in\overline{H^{\infty}}
and
\tilde{b}\in H^{\infty}
,
we have the following generalization of
the well-known
S^{*}
-invariance property of model spaces.

Corollary 5.2
.

Model spaces are invariant subspaces for the transposed
paired operators
\Sigma_{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}}
with

\tilde{a}\in\overline{H^{\infty}}
and
\tilde{b}\in H^{\infty}
.

Together with the notion of invariant subspaces for an operator
T
, one
can define also the concept of almost-invariant
subspaces for
T
.

Definition 5.3
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space,
X\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
, and let
\mathcal{M}
be a subspace of

\mathcal{H}
. We say that
\mathcal{M}
is almost invariant with defect
m

for
X
if and only if

X\mathcal{M}\subset\mathcal{M}\oplus\mathcal{F},

(5.1)

where
\mathcal{F}
is a finite-dimensional subspace of minimal dimension
m
.

It was shown by Popov and Tcaciuc
[
26
]

that every Hilbert space operator admits an almost-invariant half-space (that is, a subspace of infinite dimension
and infinite codimension) of defect 1.
The following result from
[
5
]

also holds.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
X,T\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
, where
\mathcal{H}
is a Hilbert space, such that
XT-TX
is a finite-rank operator with rank
r
. Then

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T
is almost invariant with defect
m\leq r
for
X
, i.e.,

f\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T\implies Xf\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T\oplus\mathcal{F},

(5.2)

where
\mathcal{F}
is a subspace of dimension at most
r
.

From this proposition and Propositions
2.7
–
2.9
we then have the following.

Proposition 5.5
.

If
a,\tilde{a}\in H^{\infty}+\mathcal{R}
and
b,\tilde{b}\in\overline{H^{\infty}}+\mathcal{R}
, then

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{a,b}
is almost invariant for
S_{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}}
with defect less than or equal to
\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits[S_{a,b},S_{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}}]
.

A similar property holds for transposed paired operators. From (
2.8
) and
(
2.9
) we have then:

Corollary 5.6
.

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{a,b}
and
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{a,b}
are almost invariant
for multiplication by
z
and
\overline{z}
with defect 1.

Besides invariant and almost-invariant subspaces for an operator
T
, one
can also define nearly-invariant subspaces. However, while the former
are defined in a general Banach space setting, the notion of
nearly
S^{*}
-invariant subspaces appeared in the particular context of

H^{2}_{+}
in the work of Hitt, Hayashi and Sarason

[
17
,
18
,
27
]
,
motivated by questions of invariant subspaces and kernels of
Toeplitz operators.

Definition 5.7
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a proper subspace of
H^{2}_{+}
. Then we say that
\mathcal{M}
is nearly
S^{*}
-invariant if and only if

f\in\mathcal{M},f(0)=0\implies S^{*}f\in\mathcal{M}.

(5.3)

It is well known that kernels of Toeplitz operators are nearly
S^{*}
- invariant.
However, kernels of Hankel operators
H_{g}:H^{2}_{+}\to H^{2}_{-}

with
H_{g}\phi=P^{-}g\phi
and
truncated Toeplitz operators
A_{g}^{\theta}:K_{\theta}\to K_{\theta}

with
A_{g}^{\theta}\phi=P_{\theta}g\phi
, where
P_{\theta}
is the orthogonal
projection onto
K_{\theta}
, are not in general
nearly
S^{*}
-invariant, and the following weaker notion is of interest in those cases.

Definition 5.8
.

[
9
]

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a proper subspace of
H^{2}_{+}
. Then we say that
\mathcal{M}
is nearly
S^{*}
-invariant
with defect
m
if and only if

f\in\mathcal{M},f(0)=0\implies S^{*}f\in\mathcal{M}\oplus\mathcal{F},

(5.4)

where
\mathcal{F}
is a finite-dimensional subspace of minimal dimension
m
.

A description of nearly
S^{*}
-invariant subspaces of defect
m
was given in
[
9
]
.
A nontrivial kernel of a Hankel operator, being of the form
\theta H^{2}_{+}
for
\theta
inner,
is nearly
S^{*}
-invariant with defect 1 if
\theta(0)=0
, since if
f\in\theta H^{2}_{+}
with

f(0)=0
then
\bar{z}f\in\theta H^{2}+\mathbb{C}\bar{z}\theta
; while if
\theta(0)\neq 0

it is already nearly
S^{*}
-invariant.
Likewise the kernel of an asymmetric truncated Toeplitz operator is nearly
S^{*}
-invariant
with defect 1, as shown in
[
25
]
; see also
[
5
]
.
Similar observations apply to kernels of certain finite-rank perturbations of Toeplitz operators

[
19
,
12
]
.

Near
S^{*}
-invariance, with or without positive
defect, can be seen and interpreted from various perspectives,
leading to different generalizations of the notion
of near invariance, allowing it to
apply beyond the context of
H^{2}_{+}
.
First, we see that (
5.3
) is equivalent to the condition

f\in\mathcal{M},\overline{z}f\in H^{2}_{+}\implies\overline{z}f\in\mathcal{M}.

(5.5)

So a natural generalization of Definition
5.8
is the following.

Definition 5.9
.

[
6
]
Let
\mathcal{M}
be a proper subspace of
H^{2}_{+}
and let
\eta
be a complex-valued function defined a.e. on
\mathbb{T}
. We
say that
\mathcal{M}
is nearly
\eta
-invariant (or nearly
M_{\eta}
-invariant) if and only if

f\in\mathcal{M},\ \eta f\in H^{2}_{+}\implies\eta f\in\mathcal{M},

(5.6)

and, if
\eta\in L^{\infty}
, we also say that
\mathcal{M}
is nearly
T_{\eta}
-invariant, since
(
5.6
) is equivalent to

f\in\mathcal{M},\ \eta f\in H^{2}_{+}\implies T_{\eta}f\in\mathcal{M}.

Further, if

f\in\mathcal{M},\ \eta f\in H^{2}_{+}\implies\eta f\in\mathcal{M}\oplus\mathcal{F},

(5.7)

where
\mathcal{F}
is a finite-dimensional subspace of minimal dimension
m
, then
we say that
\mathcal{M}
is
nearly
\eta
-invariant with defect
m
.

It was shown in
[
6
]
that Toeplitz kernels are not only nearly
\overline{z}
-invariant (that is, nearly
S^{*}
-invariant) subspaces of
H^{2}_{+}
, but also nearly
\eta
-invariant for a wide class of functions
\eta
, including all functions in
\overline{H^{\infty}}
and all rational functions
without poles in the exterior of the unit disc. This property of near
\eta
-invariance provides
important information characterizing Toeplitz kernels.
For instance, it implies that a nontrivial Toeplitz kernel cannot be of the form

\alpha K
with
\alpha
inner and nonconstant and
K\subset H^{2}_{+}
, since
Toeplitz kernels are nearly
\overline{\alpha}
-invariant. Nor can it be of the form
(z+a)K
with

a\in\mathbb{D}\cup\mathbb{T}
and
K\subset H^{2}_{+}
, since Toeplitz
kernels are also nearly
1/(z+a)
-invariant.

To extend this notion beyond the particular setting of
H^{2}_{+}
, the following was introduced recently in
[
5
]
.

Definition 5.10
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space and let
\mathcal{M}
,
\mathcal{N}
be closed subspaces
of
\mathcal{H}
, with
\mathcal{M}\subset\mathcal{N}
, such that for
X\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
we have

f\in\mathcal{M},\ Xf\in\mathcal{N}\implies Xf\in\mathcal{M}.

(5.8)

Then we say that
\mathcal{M}
is nearly
X
-invariant with respect to
\mathcal{N}
(for short, w.r.t.
\mathcal{N}
).
Also,
\mathcal{M}
is said to be nearly
X
-invariant w.r.t.
\mathcal{N}
with defect
m
if

f\in\mathcal{M},\ Xf\in\mathcal{N}\implies Xf\in\mathcal{M}\oplus\mathcal{F},

(5.9)

with
\mathcal{F}
as above.

Note that, if
\mathcal{N}
is a closed subspace and
P_{\mathcal{N}}
denotes the orthogonal projection onto
\mathcal{N}
, then (
5.8
) is equivalent to

f\in\mathcal{M},\ Xf\in\mathcal{N}\implies P_{\mathcal{N}}Xf\in\mathcal{M},

so we also say that
\mathcal{M}
is nearly
P_{\mathcal{N}}X_{|\mathcal{N}}
-invariant.

Taking, for instance
X=M_{\overline{z}}
,
\mathcal{N}=H^{2}_{+}
,
\mathcal{H}=L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
and
P_{\mathcal{N}}X=S^{*}
, we recover (
5.5
) from (
5.8
).

There are obvious relations between the notions of almost-invariant and nearly-invariant subspaces for an operator, especially in the context of
H^{2}_{+}
.
If
Xf\in\mathcal{N}
for all
f\in\mathcal{M}
, then the space
\mathcal{M}
is nearly
X
-invariant with defect
m
w.r.t.
\mathcal{N}

if and only if it is almost invariant with defect
m
for
X
.
This happens in particular if
\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{H}
. Moreover,
as pointed out in
[
9
]
, we also have that if
\mathcal{M}\subset H^{2}_{+}
is
almost invariant for
S^{*}
with defect at most

m
, then it is nearly
S^{*}
-invariant with defect at most
m
.
The converse is true if (i) all elements of
\mathcal{M}
vanish at
0
, or (ii)
there exists
f_{0}\in\mathcal{M}
with
f_{0}(0)\neq 0
such that

S^{*}f_{0}\in\mathcal{M}+\mathcal{F}
.

Nevertheless, even when the notion of nearly
X
-invariant subspace of
\mathcal{H}
, with or without defect, coincides with that of
almost-invariant subspace with a certain defect for the operator
X
, the two concepts
express different points of view and address different questions on how the operator acts on a certain space.

For instance, in the context of
H^{2}_{+}
, saying that a subspace
\mathcal{M}

is almost invariant with defect
m
for an operator
X
can be seen as answering the question
of where
X(\mathcal{M})
lies, if it is not
contained in
\mathcal{M}
. Saying that
\mathcal{M}
is nearly
X
-invariant, with or without defect, can be seen as
answering the question of which elements of
\mathcal{M}
are mapped into
\mathcal{M}

(or
\mathcal{M}\oplus\mathcal{F}
) by
X
.
Thus, considering the case of Toeplitz kernels, which are

S^{*}
-invariant if and only if the symbol is in
\overline{H^{\infty}}
, we may ask which
elements
f\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g}
are
mapped into
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g}
by multiplication by
\overline{z}
, or by
S^{*}
, when
g\not\in\overline{H^{\infty}}
.
The answer is given by
\overline{z}f\in H^{2}_{+}
, i.e.,

f\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g},\ \overline{z}f\in H^{2}_{+}\implies\overline{z}f\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g},

(5.10)

which is precisely the same as saying that
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g}
is nearly
S^{*}
-invariant.

If we now consider instead the problem of how “far”
S^{*}(\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g})
is from

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g}
when
g\not\in\overline{H^{\infty}}
, in the context of
H^{2}_{+}
, or the same
question for
\overline{z}\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g}
, in the context of
L^{2}
, the answer is given by saying that

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g}
is almost invariant with defect 1 for both

S^{*}
(in
H^{2}_{+})
and
M_{\overline{z}}
(in
L^{2}
). Thus in the latter
case one gets information on the image of
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g}
by
S^{*}
, while
in the case of near invariance one gets information describing
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g}

itself; in particular, there must be an element of
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g}

which does not vanish at
0
, and so
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g}\not\subseteq zH^{2}_{+}
.

In the more general context of
L^{2}
, to extend the
previous results to paired operators, one can look at near invariance in
the context of
\mathcal{N}
-stability, or stability in
\mathcal{N}
, introduced in
[
5
]
.

Definition 5.11
.

Let
\mathcal{A}\neq\{0\}
be a closed subspace of
\mathcal{N}\subset\mathcal{H}
and let
X\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
. We say that
\mathcal{A}
is
\mathcal{N}
-stable for
X
, or stable in
\mathcal{N}
for
X
, if
X(\mathcal{A})\subset\mathcal{N}
.

Thus, according to this definition, saying that
\mathcal{M}
is nearly
S^{*}
-invariant is equivalent to saying
that
\mathcal{M}\cap zH^{2}_{+}
is
\mathcal{M}
-stable for the operator
S^{*}
;
\mathcal{M}
is invariant
for
S^{*}
if and only if it is
\mathcal{M}
-stable for
S^{*}
.

When
\mathcal{M}
is the kernel of an operator
T
on
\mathcal{N}\subset\mathcal{H}
,
this notion can be related with the commutation
relations between two operators as follows.
It is clear that if
X
and
T
commute then all elements of
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T
are mapped back into
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T
by the operator
X
; i.e.,
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T
is invariant for
X
. But
if
X
and
T
do not commute on their domain, then
those elements of
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T
for which
X
and
T
do commute, i.e.,

\mathcal{M}=\{f\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T:XTf=TXf\}

are also mapped back into
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T
by
X
, and so
\mathcal{M}
is
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T
-stable for
X
.

Comparing this with (
5.3
), note that
for
g\not\in\overline{H^{\infty}}
, taking
T=T_{g}
and
X=S^{*}
,
the elements of
\mathcal{M}
, as defined above,
are precisely those with
f(0)=0
, so one must
indeed have that
S^{*}f\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g}
.
Noting moreover that
\overline{z}f\in H^{2}_{+}
means that
f\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits H_{\overline{z}}
, we see that
in this case

\mathcal{M}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g}\cap\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits H_{\overline{z}}

and near
S^{*}
-invariance can be expressed as saying that
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g}\cap\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits H_{\overline{z}}
is stable in
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g}

for the operator
S^{*}
. One could express near
\eta
-invariance analogously by saying that

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g}\cap\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits H_{\eta}
is stable in
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g}
for
M_{\eta}
.

Applying these ideas now to the setting of paired operators in
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
, we have from Propositions
2.12
and
2.13

that, for
\eta\in L^{\infty}
and
f\in L^{2}
,

\begin{array}[]{rclcl}\eta S_{a,b}f&=&S_{a,b}\eta f&\iff&f\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits H_{\eta}\oplus\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\widetilde{H}_{\eta}\\
\eta\Sigma_{a,b}f&=&\Sigma_{a,b}\eta f&\iff&(a-b)f\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits H_{\eta}\oplus\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\widetilde{H}_{\eta}.\end{array}

Thus we have the following analogue of near
\eta
-invariance.

Proposition 5.12
.

(i)
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{a,b}\cap(\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits H_{\eta}\oplus\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\widetilde{H}_{\eta})
is stable
in
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{a,b}
for multiplication by
\eta
; i.e.,

f\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{a,b},\ H_{\eta}f_{+}=0,\ \widetilde{H}_{\eta}f_{-}=0\implies\eta f\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{a,b}.

(ii) If
f\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{a,b}
and
(a-b)f\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits H_{\eta}\oplus\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\widetilde{H}_{\eta}
,
then
\eta f\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{a,b}
.

Corollary 5.13
.

(i)
f\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{a,b},\ f_{+}\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits H_{\overline{z}}\implies\overline{z}f\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{a,b}
,
so there exists in every non-zero paired kernel an element
f
with
f_{+}(0)\neq 0
.

(ii)
f\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{a,b},\ f_{-}\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\tilde{H}_{z}\implies zf\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{a,b}
,
so there exists in every non-zero paired kernel an element
f
with
zf_{-}(\infty)\neq 0
.

Note that (i) in the above corollary generalizes a well-known property of nonzero nearly
S^{*}
-invariant subspaces of
H^{2}_{+}
.

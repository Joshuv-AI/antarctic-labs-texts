Asymptotic synchronization of Kuramoto oscillators with time delay and non-universal interaction
Conor Carty, Young-Pil Choi, Chiara Cicolani, Cristina Pignotti

Abstract
. We study the emergence of synchronization in the Kuramoto model on a digraph in the presence of time delays. Assuming the digraph is strongly connected, we first establish a uniform bound on the phase diameter and subsequently prove the asymptotic frequency synchronization of the oscillators under suitable assumptions on the initial configurations. In the case of an all-to-all connection, we obtain an exponential synchronization estimate. Additionally, we present numerical simulations, providing further insights into the synchronization and oscillatory behaviors of the oscillator frequencies depending on the network structure and the magnitude of the time delay.



1.
Introduction

Synchronization phenomena appear in nature, ranging from the rhythmic flashing of fireflies to the coordinated beating of the heart muscle cells. Understanding the mechanisms underlying such spontaneous synchronization has been a subject of considerable interest across various scientific disciplines, including physics, biology, and engineering
[
5
,
25
,
22
,
26
]
. One of the seminal models that has greatly contributed to our understanding of synchronization is the Kuramoto model
[
20
]
; it provides a simple yet powerful framework for studying the emergence of synchronization in large ensembles of coupled oscillators. Over the years, the Kuramoto model has found applications in various fields, including neuroscience
[
4
,
28
]
and power grids
[
15
,
10
]
among others. Its mathematical tractability and versatility make it a valuable tool for theoretical investigations as well as for interpreting experimental observations of synchronization phenomena.

In the present work, we analyze the emergence of synchronization in the Kuramoto model on directed graphs, considering the effects of time delays in the coupling between oscillators. More precisely, we investigate the conditions under which synchronization emerges in such systems and explore the influence of network topology and time delays on the synchronization dynamics. To present our model, we consider a digraph
\mathcal{G}=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})
consisting of a finite set
\mathcal{V}=\{1,\dots,N\}
of vertices and a set
\mathcal{E}\subset\mathcal{V}\times\mathcal{V}
of arcs. We assume that Kuramoto oscillators are located at vertices and interact with each other via the underlying network topology. For each vertex
i
, let
\mathcal{N}_{i}
be the set of vertices that directly influence the vertex
i,
namely
\mathcal{N}_{i}=\left\{\,j\in\{1,\dots,N\}:(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}\,\right\}
, and we denote by
N_{i}
the number of oscillators in the set
\mathcal{N}_{i},
i.e.
N_{i}=\left|\mathcal{N}_{i}\right|
. Then, our main system reads

(1.1)

\frac{d}{dt}\theta_{i}(t)=\Omega_{i}+\frac{\kappa}{N-1}\sum_{j\neq i}\chi_{ij}\sin(\theta_{j}(t-\tau_{ij})-\theta_{i}(t)),\quad i=1,\dots,N,\quad t>0,

where
\theta_{i}=\theta_{i}(t)
represents the phase of
i
-th Kuramoto oscillator at vertex
i
and time
t>0
, and
\tau_{ij}>0
denotes communication time delay in the information flow from vertex
j
to vertex
i
. Here the network topology is given by its
(0,1)
-adjacency matrix
(\chi_{ij})
defined as

\chi_{ij}=\begin{cases}1\ \mbox{if $j$ transmits information to $i$},\\
0\ \mbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Notice that self-time delay is not allowed, i.e.
\tau_{ii}=0
and we exclude a self-loop, i.e.
i\notin\mathcal{N}_{i}
for all
i=1,\dots,N
.

The system (
1.1
) describes the dynamic interactions among the oscillators, accounting for both their natural frequencies
\Omega_{i}
, which is assumed to be a random variable extracted from a given distribution
g=g(\Omega)
, and the influence of neighboring oscillators weighted by the coupling strength
\kappa>0
and communication delays
\tau_{ij}
. The classical Kuramoto model, extensively studied in the literature (see, e.g.
[
1
,
3
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
11
,
14
,
18
,
21
,
24
]
), corresponds to the cases where
\chi_{ij}=1
and
\tau_{ij}=0
for all
i,j=1,\dots,N
. However, in our formulation (
1.1
), we introduce two additional structures, namely the time delay effect and network topology.

Incorporating time-delayed mathematical formulations is crucial for capturing the dynamics of real-world systems accurately. Time delays are prevalent in various physical and biological systems, stemming from finite signal propagation speeds, processing times, or communication latencies. Neglecting these delays can lead to inaccurate predictions and overlook essential aspects of system behavior. Additionally, taking into account the network topologies in the interaction is very natural. The connectivity structure dictates which oscillators directly influence each other’s phases. In (
1.1
), the network connectivity determines the phase relations between connected oscillators and influences the overall synchronization behavior. For recent results and more detailed background regarding these extensions, we refer to
[
10
,
9
,
15
,
13
,
17
,
27
,
29
,
30
]
and references therein.

We supply the system (
1.1
) with the initial data:

(1.2)

\theta_{i}(s)=\theta^{0}_{i}(s)\quad\mbox{for}\quad s\in[-\tau,0],

where
\theta_{i}^{0}\in\mathcal{C}^{1}_{b}(-\tau,0)\cap\mathcal{C}[-\tau,0],

i=1,\dots,N
, and
\tau:=\max_{i,j=1,\dots,N}\,\tau_{ij}
.

In the current work, we focus on the emergence of frequency synchronization in (
1.1
) with some interaction network and time delay, namely we identify the class of initial configurations leading to that all oscillators tend to have the same frequency as time goes to infinity. To begin with, we introduce some basic concepts related to the directed graphs. A
path
in a digraph
\mathcal{G}
from
i_{0}
to
i_{p}
is a finite sequence
i_{0},i_{1},\dots,i_{p}
of distinct vertices such that each successive pair of vertices is an arc of
\mathcal{G}.
The integer
p
is called
length
of the path. If there exists a path from
i
to
j
, then vertex
j
is said to be
reachable
from vertex
i
and we define the distance from
i
to
j
,
d(i,j)
, as the length of the shortest path from
i
to
j
. A digraph
\mathcal{G}
is said to be
strongly connected
if each vertex can be reachable from any vertex. We call the
depth

\gamma
of the digraph the quantity:

\gamma:=\max_{1\leq i,j\leq N}d(i,j).

Thus, any agent can be connected to one other via no more than
\gamma
intermediate agents. It is trivial that
\gamma\leq N-1.

We next introduce some notations to be used throughout the paper. For a solution
\theta(t):=(\theta_{1}(t),\cdots,\theta_{N}(t))
to (
1.1
), we denote the phase and velocity diameters by

\displaystyle d_{\theta}(t):=\max_{1\leq i,j\leq N}|\theta_{i}(t)-\theta_{j}(t)|\quad\mbox{and}\quad d_{\omega}(t):=\max_{1\leq i,j\leq N}|\omega_{i}(t)-\omega_{j}(t)|,

respectively, where
\omega_{i}(t):=\dot{\theta}_{i}(t):=\frac{d\theta_{i}(t)}{dt}
with
\omega_{i}(0):=\lim_{t\to 0-}\dot{\theta}_{i}(t).
Along with those notations, we also set

D(\Omega):=\max_{1\leq i,j\leq N}|\Omega_{i}-\Omega_{j}|,

(1.3)

D_{\theta}(0):=\max_{1\leq i,j\leq N,s,t\in[-\tau,0]}|\theta_{i}(s)-\theta_{j}(t)|,\quad\mbox{and}\quad D_{\omega}(0):=\max_{1\leq i,j\leq N,s,t\in[-\tau,0]}|\omega_{i}(s)-\omega_{j}(t)|.

We also provide a notion of our complete frequency synchronization in the definition below.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\theta(t):=(\theta_{1}(t),\dots,\theta_{N}(t))
be the global classical solution to the system (
1.1
)-(
1.2
). Then the system exhibits the complete frequency synchronization if and only if the velocity diameter tends to 0, as the time goes to infinity:

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}d_{\omega}(t)=0.

Let us now state our main asymptotic frequency synchronization results in a somewhat rough manner. The precise statements are given in Theorem
3.1
(strongly connected case) and Theorem
4.1
(all-to-all connected case).

Theorem A
.

Let
\{\theta_{i}(t)\}_{i=1}^{N}
be a solution to the Kuramoto model (
1.1
) with initial data (
1.2
). We have the following asymptotic frequency synchronization results.

(i)

(strongly connected case) Assume the digraph
\mathcal{G}
is strongly connected, i.e. each vertex can be reachable from any vertex. Suppose that
D_{\theta}(0)<\pi
,
\tau
is sufficiently small, and
\kappa
is large enough. Then the time-delayed Kuramoto oscillators (
1.1
) achieve the asymptotic complete frequency synchronization in the sense of Definition
1.1
.

(ii)

(all-to-all connected case) Assume that all oscillators are connected, i.e.,
\chi_{ij}=1
for all
i,j=1,\dots,N
. Suppose that
D_{\theta}(0)<\pi
,
\tau
is sufficiently small, and
\kappa
is large enough. Then the time-delayed Kuramoto oscillators (
1.1
) achieve the asymptotic complete frequency synchronization in the sense of Definition
1.1
exponentially fast.

The initial step of our analysis involves establishing a uniform-in-time bound estimate of the phase diameter. Specifically, we demonstrate that for sufficiently large times, the oscillators will be confined within a region of a quarter circle under suitable assumptions on the initial configurations. This bound estimate plays a critical role in unveiling the dissipation structure of (
1.1
), and thus it serves as a foundational element for deriving the asymptotic frequency synchronization result. Notably, our strategy improves the previous work
[
13
]
where the initial phase diameter
D_{\theta}(0)
is assumed to be less than
\pi/2
. In the case of all-to-all connection, i.e., the oscillators are all connected, we achieve a more robust result, namely an exponential asymptotic synchronization estimate. These results extend previous results in the literature. In particular, compared to
[
9
]
, we introduce considerations for the network structure and pair-dependent delays. Moreover, we significantly relax previous assumptions on the time delay size (see e.g.
[
9
,
30
]
).

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we consider the strongly connected case and provide that the diameter remains bounded by a constant less than
\pi/2
after some finite time. Sections
3
and
4
are devoted to presenting the detailed proofs of Theorem
3.1
(strongly connected case) and Theorem
4.1
(all-to-all connected case). Finally, in Section
5
, we present numerical simulations, illustrating further insights into the synchronization and oscillatory behaviors of the oscillator frequencies depending on the network structure and the magnitude of the time delay.

2.
Uniform-in-time bound of phase diameter

In this section, we provide the uniform-in-time bound estimate of the phase diameter. Specifically, our goal of this section is to obtain that the phase diameter
d_{\theta}(t)
is bounded by for some
d_{\infty}\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
for any
t
large enough under suitable assumptions on the initial configurations. For this, we first start with the estimate providing the bound on the difference between the time-delayed and non-time-delayed phases. Note that we can easily find

\displaystyle|\omega_{i}(t)|\leq|\Omega_{i}|+\kappa\leq\max_{1\leq i\leq N}|\Omega_{i}|+\kappa,

for all
i=1,\cdots,N.
Let us denote

(2.1)

R_{\omega}:=\max_{1\leq i\leq N}|\Omega_{i}|+\kappa,

then

(2.2)

|\omega_{i}(t)|\leq R_{\omega},\quad\forall\,t\geq 0.

From (
2.2
), we deduce that for all

i=1,\dots,N,

|\theta_{i}(t)-\theta_{i}(s)|=\left|\int_{s}^{t}\omega_{i}(r)dr\right|\leq R_{\omega}|t-s|,\quad\forall\,s,t\geq 0.

In particular, for all
i,j=1,\dots,N,
we have

\displaystyle|\theta_{i}(t-\tau_{ij})-\theta_{i}(t)|\leq R_{\omega}\tau,\quad t\geq 0.

For the uniform-in-time bounded estimate of phase diameter
d_{\theta}(t)
, we need to use the dissipative structure of the system (
1.1
). For this, motivated from
[
27
]
, we define an ensemble of oscillators as a convex combination denoted by

\mathcal{L}_{l}^{k}\left(C_{l,k}\right):=\sum_{i=l}^{k}c_{i}\theta_{i},\quad 1\leq l\leq k\leq N,

where all
c_{i}
are non-negative and
\sum_{i=l}^{k}c_{i}=1
. We also introduce notions of root and general root in the definition below.

Definition 2.1

(Root and General Root)
.

(i)

We say
\theta_{k}
is a root if it is not influenced by any other oscillators, i.e.
i\notin\mathcal{N}_{k}
for all
i\neq k
.

(ii)

An ensemble of oscillators
\mathcal{L}_{l}^{k}\left(C_{l,k}\right)
is a general root if it is not influenced by any oscillators excluded from the ensemble, i.e.
j\notin\mathcal{N}_{i}
for all
i\in\{l,\ldots,k\}
and
j\in\{1,\ldots,N\}\backslash\{l,\ldots,k\}
.

For the analysis, we use the following algorithm, denoted by
\mathcal{A}
, proposed in
[
16
]
for constructing convex combinations of oscillators.

Step I

For any time
t
, we reorder the oscillator indices such that the phases are increasing from minimum to maximum:

\displaystyle\theta_{1}(t)\leq\theta_{2}(t)\leq\cdots\leq\theta_{N}(t).

For the next steps, we introduce the following sub-algorithms:

\left(\mathcal{A}_{1}\right)
:

If
\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{k}^{N}\left(\bar{C}_{k,N}\right)
is not a general root, then we construct (from top to bottom)

\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{k-1}^{N}\left(\bar{C}_{k-1,N}\right)=\frac{\bar{a}_{k-1}\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{k}^{N}\left(\bar{C}_{k,N}\right)+\theta_{k-1}}{\bar{a}_{k-1}+1}.

\left(\mathcal{A}_{2}\right)
:

If
\underline{\mathcal{L}}_{1}^{l}\left(\underline{C}_{1,l}\right)
is not a general root, then we construct (from bottom to top)

\underline{\mathcal{L}}_{1}^{l+1}\left(\underline{C}_{1,l+1}\right)=\frac{\underline{a}_{l+1}\underline{\mathcal{L}}_{1}^{l}\left(\underline{C}_{1,l}\right)+\theta_{l+1}}{\underline{a}_{l+1}+1}.

Step II

Since
\mathcal{G}
is strongly connected, we have that
\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{1}^{N}\left(\bar{C}_{1,N}\right)
is a general root, and
\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{k}^{N}\left(\bar{C}_{k,N}\right)
is not a general root for
k>1
. Therefore, we may start from
\theta_{N}
and follow the process
\mathcal{A}_{1}
to construct
\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{k}^{N}\left(\bar{C}_{k,N}\right)
until
k=1
.

Step III

Similarly,
\underline{\mathcal{L}}_{1}^{N}\left(\underline{C}_{1,N}\right)
is a general root and
\underline{\mathcal{L}}_{1}^{l}\left(\underline{C}_{1,l}\right)
is not a general root for
l<N
. Therefore, we may analogously start from
\theta_{1}
and follow the process
\mathcal{A}_{2}
until
l=N
.

Remark 2.1
.

The coefficients in the above constructions are determined inductively according to

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{k-1}^{N}\left(\bar{C}_{k-1,N}\right)\text{ with }\bar{a}_{N}=0,\ \bar{a}_{k-1}=\eta\left(2N-k+2\right)\left(\bar{a}_{k}+1\right),\quad 2\leq k\leq N,\\
&\underline{\mathcal{L}}_{1}^{k+1}\left(\underline{C}_{1,k+1}\right)\text{ with }\underline{a}_{1}=0,\ \underline{a}_{k+1}=\eta\left(k+1+N\right)\left(\underline{a}_{k}+1\right),\quad 1\leq k\leq N-1\end{aligned}

or in summation form:

\displaystyle\bar{a}_{k-1}=\sum_{j=1}^{N-k+1}\eta^{j}P\left(2N-k+2,j\right),\quad 2\leq k\leq N,

\displaystyle\underline{a}_{k+1}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\eta^{j}P\left(k+1+N,j\right),\quad 1\leq k\leq N-1,

where
\eta
is a positive parameter and
P(m,k)
denotes the
k
-permutations of
m
, i.e. the number of permutations of
k
elements arranged in a specific order in a set of
m
elements:

P(m,k):=\frac{m!}{(m-k)!}=m\cdot(m-1)\cdots(m-k+1).

We now set

\bar{\theta}_{k}:=\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{k}^{N}\left(\bar{C}_{k,N}\right),\quad\underline{\theta}_{k}:=\underline{\mathcal{L}}_{1}^{k}\left(\underline{C}_{1,k}\right),\quad 1\leq k\leq N,

and define a non-negative
q_{\theta}(t)
quantity which will be used to control the phase diameter:

\displaystyle q_{\theta}:=\bar{\theta}_{1}-\underline{\theta}_{N}.

By using those newly defined notations, we state two lemmas on a monotone property of the interaction term and a relation between
q_{\theta}(t)
and
d_{\theta}(t)
whose proofs can be found in
[
27
, Lemma 4.1]
and
[
27
, Lemma 4.2]
, respectively. Here, we notice that these lemmas depend only on the graph structure.

Lemma 2.1
.

Consider the strongly connected network
\mathcal{G}
, with phases well-ordered according to
\mathcal{A}
. Moreover, we assume that the phase diameter and free parameter
\eta
satisfy

(2.3)

d_{\theta}(t)<\zeta<\xi<\pi\quad\mbox{and}\quad\eta>\max\left\{\frac{1}{\sin\xi},\frac{1}{\cos{(R_{\omega}\tau)}},\frac{2}{1-\frac{\zeta}{\xi}}\right\},

respectively. Then, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=n}^{N}\left(\eta^{i-n}\min\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathcal{N}_{i}\\
j\leq i\end{subarray}}\sin\left(\theta_{j}-\theta_{i}\right)\right)\leq\sin\left(\theta_{\bar{k}_{n}}-\theta_{N}\right)

and

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\eta^{n-i}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathcal{N}_{i}\\
j\geq i\end{subarray}}\sin\left(\theta_{j}^{0}-\theta_{i}^{0}\right)\right)\geq\sin\left(\theta_{\underline{k}_{n}}-\theta_{1}\right),

where

\displaystyle\bar{k}_{n}:=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\cup_{i=n}^{N}\mathcal{N}_{i}\end{subarray}}j\quad\mbox{and}\quad\underline{k}_{n}:=\max_{j\in\cup_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{N}_{i}}j\quad\mbox{for $1\leq n\leq N$}.

Lemma 2.2
.

Consider the strongly connected network
\mathcal{G}
, with the coefficients of the convex combinations satisfying the conditions according to algorithm
\mathcal{A}
. Then we have

\beta d_{\theta}(t)\leq q_{\theta}(t)\leq d_{\theta}(t)\mbox{ with }\beta=1-\frac{2}{\eta},

where
\eta
satisfies (
2.3
).

We next provide a result concerning the dynamics of
q_{\theta}(t)
which will serve as a cornerstone in deriving our phase bound.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\{\theta_{i}(t)\}_{i=1}^{N}
be a solution to the Kuramoto model (
1.1
) on a strongly connected digraph
\mathcal{G}
, with initial conditions satisfying

\displaystyle D_{\theta}(0)<\pi.

Let
\zeta
and
\xi
such that
D_{\theta}(0)<\zeta<\xi<\pi
and let
d_{\infty},\eta
be two parameters such that

d_{\infty}<\min\left\{\frac{\pi}{2},d_{\theta}(0)\right\},\quad\quad\eta>\max\left\{\frac{1}{\sin\xi},\frac{1}{\cos{(R_{\omega}\tau)}},\frac{2}{1-\frac{\zeta}{\xi}}\right\},

where
R_{\omega}
is the bound on the velocities defined in (
2.1
).
Assume the following conditions hold:

\displaystyle\tan(R_{\omega}\tau)<\frac{\beta d_{\infty}}{\left(1+\frac{\zeta}{\zeta-D_{\theta}(0)}\right)2(N-1)c},

\displaystyle d_{\infty}+R_{\omega}\tau<\frac{\pi}{2},\quad\kappa>\left(1+\frac{\zeta}{\zeta-D_{\theta}(0)}\right)\frac{\left(D(\Omega)+2\kappa\sin(R_{\omega}\tau)\right)(N-1)c}{2\cos(R_{\omega}\tau)}\frac{1}{\beta d_{\infty}},

where

\displaystyle c:=\frac{\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\eta^{j}P\left(2N,j\right)+1\right)\xi}{\sin\xi}.

Then we have

\displaystyle d_{\theta}(t)<\xi,\quad\forall\,t\in[0,\infty)

and

\displaystyle\dot{q}_{\theta}(t)\leq D(\Omega)+2\kappa\sin(R_{\omega}\tau)-\frac{2\kappa\cos(R_{\omega}\tau)}{(N-1)c}q_{\theta}(t),\quad a.e.\ t\in[0,+\infty).

Remark 2.2
.

From Lemma
2.3
, by applying the Grönwall’s lemma, we find

(2.4)

q_{\theta}(t)\leq q_{\theta}(0)\exp\left(-\frac{2\kappa\cos(R_{\omega}\tau)}{(N-1)c}t\right)+\frac{(D(\Omega)+2\kappa\sin(R_{\omega}\tau))(N-1)c}{2\kappa\cos(R_{\omega}\tau)}\left(1-\exp\left(-\frac{2\kappa\cos(R_{\omega}\tau)}{(N-1)c}t\right)\right).

In particular, we obtain

\displaystyle q_{\theta}(t)\leq\max\left\{d_{\theta}(0),\frac{(D(\Omega)+2\kappa\sin(R_{\omega}\tau))(N-1)c}{2\kappa\cos(R_{\omega}\tau)}\right\},\quad\forall\,t\in[0,\infty).

Proof of Lemma
2.3
.

Let us denote
\Omega_{M}
and
\Omega_{m}
the maximal and minimal natural frequency, respectively, i.e.

\displaystyle\Omega_{M}:=\max_{i=1,\dots,N}\,\Omega_{i}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\Omega_{m}:=\min_{i=1,\dots,N}\,\Omega_{i}.

We proceed with the proof in three steps.

Step I

We first define a set
\mathcal{S}:=\left\{T>0:d_{\theta}(t)<\xi,\ \forall\,t\in[0,T)\right\}
. Since
D_{\theta}(0)<\xi
and
d_{\theta}(t)
is continuous, the set
\mathcal{S}
is non-empty. Thus, we can set
T^{*}:=\sup\mathcal{S}
. We claim that
T^{*}=+\infty
.

Suppose not, i.e.
T^{*}<+\infty
, then by the continuity of
d_{\theta}(t)
we get

\displaystyle d_{\theta}(t)<\xi,\quad\forall t\in\left[0,T^{*}\right),\quad d_{\theta}(T^{*})=\xi.

Next, we divide the time interval into sub-intervals in which no two oscillators overlap,

\displaystyle\left[0,T^{*}\right)=\bigcup_{l}J_{l},\quad J_{l}=\left[t_{l-1},t_{l}\right),

where the end-points
t_{l}
are the times at which such an overlap occurs. We then apply the well-ordering of phases in each interval:

\displaystyle\theta_{1}(t)\leq\theta_{2}(t)\leq\ldots\leq\theta_{N}(t),\quad t\in J_{l}.

Step II

We claim that for
1\leq n\leq N

(2.5)

\dot{\bar{\theta}}_{n}(t)\leq\Omega_{M}+\kappa\sin(R_{\omega}\tau)+\frac{\kappa\cos(R_{\omega}\tau)}{(N-1)(\bar{a}_{n}+1)}\sum_{i=n}^{N}\eta^{i-n}\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathcal{N}_{i}\\
j\leq i\end{subarray}}\sin\left(\theta_{j}(t)-\theta_{i}(t)\right).

For this, we use the inductive argument. Note that
\bar{\theta}_{N}=\theta_{N}
, and thus by mean-value theorem, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \dot{\theta}_{N}(t)&=\Omega_{N}+\frac{\kappa}{N-1}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}_{N}}\sin\left(\theta_{j}(t-\tau_{jN})-\theta_{N}(t)\right)\\
&=\Omega_{N}+\frac{\kappa}{N-1}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}_{N}}\sin\left(\theta_{j}(t)-\theta_{N}(t)-\dot{\theta}_{j}(t_{jN}^{*})\tau_{jN}\right)\\
&\leq\Omega_{M}+\frac{\kappa}{N-1}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}_{N}}\left[\sin\left(\theta_{j}(t)-\theta_{N}(t)\right)\cos(\dot{\theta}_{j}(t_{jN}^{*})\tau_{jN})-\cos\left(\theta_{j}(t)-\theta_{N}(t)\right)\sin(\dot{\theta}_{j}(t_{jN}^{*})\tau_{jN})\right]\\
&\leq\Omega_{M}+\kappa\sin(R_{\omega}\tau)+\frac{\kappa}{N-1}\cos(R_{\omega}\tau)\min_{j\in\mathcal{N}_{N}}\sin\left(\theta_{j}(t)-\theta_{N}(t)\right)\end{aligned}

for some
t_{jN}^{*}\in(t-\tau_{jN},t)
and all
t\in[0,T^{*})
. This shows that (
2.5
) holds for
n=N
. Now we assume that (
2.5
) holds for
n\in[2,N-1]
. Note that

\displaystyle\frac{\kappa}{N-1}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}_{n-1}}\sin(\theta_{j}(t-\tau_{j(n-1)})-\theta_{n-1}(t))

\displaystyle\quad\leq\kappa\sin(R_{\omega}\tau)+\frac{\kappa}{N-1}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathcal{N}_{n-1}\\
j\leq n-1\end{subarray}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathcal{N}_{n-1}\\
j>n-1\end{subarray}}\right)\sin(\theta_{j}(t)-\theta_{n-1}(t))\cos(\dot{\theta}_{j}(t_{j(n-1)}^{*})\tau_{j(n-1)})

\displaystyle\quad\leq\kappa\sin(R_{\omega}\tau)+\frac{\kappa}{N-1}\cos(R_{\omega}\tau)\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathcal{N}_{n-1}\\
j\leq n-1\end{subarray}}\sin(\theta_{j}(t)-\theta_{n-1}(t))+\frac{\kappa}{N-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathcal{N}_{n-1}\\
j>n-1\end{subarray}}\sin(\theta_{j}(t)-\theta_{n-1}(t)).

By using the above together with

\displaystyle\frac{\bar{a}_{n-1}}{\bar{a}_{n}+1}=\eta(2N-n+2)=\eta N+\eta+\eta(N-n+1),

we estimate

\displaystyle\dot{\bar{\theta}}_{n-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\bar{a}_{n-1}\bar{\theta}_{n}+\theta_{n-1}}{\bar{a}_{n-1}+1}\right)=\frac{\bar{a}_{n-1}}{\bar{a}_{n-1}+1}\dot{\bar{\theta}}_{n}+\frac{1}{\bar{a}_{n-1}+1}\dot{\theta}_{n-1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\bar{a}_{n-1}}{\bar{a}_{n-1}+1}\left(\Omega_{M}+\kappa\sin(R_{\omega}\tau)+\frac{\kappa\cos(R_{\omega}\tau)}{(N-1)(\bar{a}_{n}+1)}\sum_{i=n}^{N}\eta^{i-n}\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathcal{N}_{i}\\
j\leq i\end{subarray}}\sin\left(\theta_{j}(t)-\theta_{i}(t)\right)\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{\bar{a}_{n-1}+1}\left(\Omega_{M}+\frac{\kappa}{N-1}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}_{n-1}}\sin(\theta_{j}(t-\tau_{j(n-1)})-\theta_{n-1}(t))\right)

\displaystyle\leq\Omega_{M}+\kappa\sin(R_{\omega}\tau)+\frac{\kappa\cos(R_{\omega}\tau)N}{(N-1)(\bar{a}_{n-1}+1)}\sum_{i=n}^{N}\eta^{i-n+1}\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathcal{N}_{i}\\
j\leq i\end{subarray}}\sin\left(\theta_{j}(t)-\theta_{i}(t)\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{\kappa\cos(R_{\omega}\tau)}{(N-1)(\bar{a}_{n-1}+1)}\left(\sum_{i=n}^{N}\eta^{i-n+1}\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathcal{N}_{i}\\
j\leq i\end{subarray}}\sin\left(\theta_{j}(t)-\theta_{i}(t)\right)+\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathcal{N}_{n-1}\\
j\leq n-1\end{subarray}}\sin(\theta_{j}(t)-\theta_{n-1}(t))\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{\kappa}{(N-1)(\bar{a}_{n-1}+1)}\left(\eta\left(N-n+1\right)\cos(R_{\omega}\tau)\sum_{i=n}^{N}\eta^{i-n}\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathcal{N}_{i}\\
j\leq i\end{subarray}}\sin\left(\theta_{j}-\theta_{i}\right)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathcal{N}_{n-1}\\
j>n-1\end{subarray}}\sin\left(\theta_{j}-\theta_{n-1}\right)\right).

Here the third term on the right-hand side is nonpositive, and the fourth term can be written as

\displaystyle\frac{\kappa\cos(R_{\omega}\tau)}{(N-1)(\bar{a}_{n-1}+1)}\left(\sum_{i=n-1}^{N}\eta^{i-(n-1)}\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathcal{N}_{i}\\
j\leq i\end{subarray}}\sin\left(\theta_{j}(t)-\theta_{i}(t)\right)\right).

Thus, to prove (
2.5
), it suffices to show that the last term is nonpositive.

We first observe from Lemma
2.1
that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=n}^{N}\eta^{i-n}\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathcal{N}_{i}\\
j\leq i\end{subarray}}\sin\left(\theta_{j}-\theta_{i}\right)\leq\sin\left(\theta_{\bar{k}_{n}}-\theta_{N}\right),\quad\bar{k}_{n}:=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\cup_{i=n}^{N}\mathcal{N}_{i}\end{subarray}}j.

We then consider the case
\xi>\frac{\pi}{2}
. If
\theta_{N}-\theta_{\bar{k}_{n}}\leq\frac{\pi}{2}
, then by noticing
\bar{k}_{n}\leq n-1
and
\eta\cos(R_{\omega}\tau)>1
, we find

\displaystyle\eta\left(N-n+1\right)\cos(R_{\omega}\tau)\sum_{i=n}^{N}\eta^{i-n}\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathcal{N}_{i}\\
j\leq i\end{subarray}}\sin\left(\theta_{j}-\theta_{i}\right)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathcal{N}_{n-1}\\
j>n-1\end{subarray}}\sin\left(\theta_{j}-\theta_{n-1}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\leq\eta\left(N-n+1\right)\cos(R_{\omega}\tau)\sin\left(\theta_{\bar{k}_{n}}-\theta_{N}\right)+\left(N-n+1\right)\sin(\theta_{N}-\theta_{n-1})

\displaystyle\quad\leq 0.

On the other hand, if
\frac{\pi}{2}<\theta_{N}-\theta_{\bar{k}_{N}}<\xi
, we use

\displaystyle\eta>\frac{1}{\sin\xi}\quad\text{and}\quad\sin\left(\theta_{N}-\theta_{\bar{k}_{n}}\right)>\sin\xi

to see
\eta\sin\left(\theta_{\bar{k}_{N}}-\theta_{N}\right)\leq-1
, and this gives the nonpositivity of the last term. The case for
\xi\leq\frac{\pi}{2}
follows similarly.

We now use (
2.5
) and Lemma
2.1
for
n=1
and find that

\displaystyle\dot{\bar{\theta}}_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\Omega_{M}+\kappa\sin(R_{\omega}\tau)+\frac{\kappa\cos(R_{\omega}\tau)}{(N-1)(\bar{a}_{1}+1)}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\eta^{i-1}\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathcal{N}_{i}\\
j\leq i\end{subarray}}\sin\left(\theta_{j}-\theta_{i}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\Omega_{M}+\kappa\sin(R_{\omega}\tau)+\frac{\kappa\cos(R_{\omega}\tau)}{(N-1)(\bar{a}_{1}+1)}\sin\left(\theta_{\bar{k}_{1}}-\theta_{N}\right)

\displaystyle=\Omega_{M}+\kappa\sin(R_{\omega}\tau)+\frac{\kappa\cos(R_{\omega}\tau)}{(N-1)(\bar{a}_{1}+1)}\sin\left(\theta_{1}-\theta_{N}\right),

where we used the strong connectivity of
\mathcal{G}
, and which completes the process for
\mathcal{A}_{1}
.

Step III

We can similarly build from bottom to top with
\mathcal{A}_{2}
to arrive at

\displaystyle\dot{\underline{\theta}}_{N}(t)

\displaystyle\geq\Omega_{m}-\kappa\sin(R_{\omega}\tau)+\frac{\kappa\cos(R_{\omega}\tau)}{(N-1)(\underline{a}_{N}+1)}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\eta^{i-1}\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathcal{N}_{i}\\
j\geq i\end{subarray}}\sin\left(\theta_{j}-\theta_{i}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\Omega_{m}-\kappa\sin(R_{\omega}\tau)+\frac{\kappa\cos(R_{\omega}\tau)}{(N-1)(\underline{a}_{N}+1)}\sin\left(\theta_{\underline{k}_{N}}-\theta_{1}\right)

\displaystyle=\Omega_{m}-\kappa\sin(R_{\omega}\tau)+\frac{\kappa\cos(R_{\omega}\tau)}{(N-1)(\bar{a}_{1}+1)}\sin\left(\theta_{N}-\theta_{1}\right),

from which we obtain

\displaystyle\dot{q}_{\theta}(t)

\displaystyle\leq D(\Omega)+2\kappa\sin(R_{\omega}\tau)-\frac{2\kappa\cos(R_{\omega}\tau)}{(N-1)(\bar{a}_{1}+1)}\sin\left(\theta_{N}-\theta_{1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq D(\Omega)+2\kappa\sin(R_{\omega}\tau)-\frac{2\kappa\cos(R_{\omega}\tau)}{N-1}\frac{1}{\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\eta^{j}P\left(2N,j\right)+1}\sin\left(\theta_{N}-\theta_{1}\right),

where we used

\bar{a}_{1}=\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\eta^{j}P\left(2N,j\right).

Since the function
\frac{\sin x}{x}
is monotonically decreasing in
(0,\pi]
, we obtain

\sin\left(\theta_{N}-\theta_{1}\right)\geq\frac{\sin\xi}{\xi}\left(\theta_{N}-\theta_{1}\right).

Moreover, since
q_{\theta}(t)\leq\theta_{N}(t)-\theta_{1}(t)
,

(2.6)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \dot{q}_{\theta}(t)&\leq D(\Omega)+2\kappa\sin(R_{\omega}\tau)-\frac{2\kappa\cos(R_{\omega}\tau)}{(N-1)c}q(t)\end{aligned}

for a.e.
t\in(0,T^{*})
. Then, by Lemma
2.2
and Remark
2.2
, we further have

\displaystyle\beta d_{\theta}(t)\leq q_{\theta}(t)\leq\max\left\{D_{\theta}(0),\frac{(D(\Omega)+2\kappa\sin(R_{\omega}\tau))(N-1)c}{2\kappa\cos(R_{\omega}\tau)}\right\}.

On the other hand, by the hypotheses, we get

\frac{D_{\theta}(0)}{\beta}<\frac{\zeta}{\beta}=\frac{\zeta}{1-\frac{2}{\eta}}<\xi\quad\mbox{and}\quad\frac{(D(\Omega)+2\kappa\sin(R_{\omega}\tau))(N-1)c}{2\beta\kappa\cos(R_{\omega}\tau)}<d_{\infty}<\zeta<\xi,

and hence,

\xi=d_{\theta}(T^{*})\leq\frac{1}{\beta}\max\left\{D_{\theta}(0),\frac{(D(\Omega)+2\kappa\sin(R_{\omega}\tau))(N-1)c}{2\kappa\cos(R_{\omega}\tau)}\right\}<\xi.

This is a contradiction, and thus
T^{*}=\infty
, i.e.
d_{\theta}(t)<\xi
for all
[0,\infty)
. Moreover, (
2.6
) holds for a.e.
t\in(0,\infty)
.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\{\theta_{i}(t)\}_{i=1}^{N}
be a solution to the Kuramoto model (
1.1
) on a strongly connected digraph
\mathcal{G}
and assume the hypothesis of Lemma
2.3
holds. Then, there exists a finite time
t_{*}
such that

(2.7)

d_{\theta}(t)\leq d_{\infty}\quad\text{ for }t\in\left[t_{*},+\infty\right).

Proof.

We first observe that

(2.8)

\frac{(D(\Omega)+2\kappa\sin(R_{\omega}\tau))(N-1)c}{2\kappa\cos(R_{\omega}\tau)}<\beta d_{\infty}<\beta d_{\theta}(0)\leq q_{\theta}(0).

Now, we set

\displaystyle f(t):=q_{\theta}(0)\exp\left(-\frac{2\kappa\cos(R_{\omega}\tau)}{(N-1)c}t\right)+\frac{(D(\Omega)+2\kappa\sin(R_{\omega}\tau))(N-1)c}{2\kappa\cos(R_{\omega}\tau)}\left(1-\exp\left(-\frac{2\kappa\cos(R_{\omega}\tau)}{(N-1)c}t\right)\right).

Then
f(t)
is continuous on
[0,\infty)
, monotonic, and

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}f(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{(D(\Omega)+2\kappa\sin(R_{\omega}\tau))(N-1)c}{2\kappa\cos(R_{\omega}\tau)}.

This together with (
2.8
) yields that there exists
t_{*}>0
such that

\displaystyle f(t)\leq\beta d_{\infty},\quad\forall\,t\in[t_{*},\infty).

Then, we now use Lemma
2.2
, Lemma
2.3
, and our Grönwall inequality (
2.4
) to conclude that

\displaystyle d_{\theta}(t)\leq\frac{1}{\beta}f(t)\leq d_{\infty},\quad\forall\,t\in[t_{*},\infty).

This completes the proof.
∎

3.
Asymptotic synchronization: strongly connected case

In this section, we discuss the asymptotic frequency synchronization result for solutions to system (
1.1
), i.e. the frequency-diameter decays to zero as time tends to infinity. For this, by differentiating the system (
1.1
) with respect to
t
, we obtain

(3.1)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \frac{d}{dt}\theta_{i}(t)&=\omega_{i}(t),\quad i=1,\dots,N,\quad t>0,\cr\frac{d}{dt}\omega_{i}(t)&=\frac{\kappa}{N-1}\sum_{k\neq i}\chi_{ij}\cos(\theta_{k}(t-\tau_{ik})-\theta_{i}(t))(\omega_{k}(t-\tau_{ik})-\omega_{i}(t)).\end{aligned}

In order to prove the diameter decay estimate for solutions to the above second-order system, we need some preliminary results.

In the rest of this section, we assume that the hypotheses of Lemma
2.3
are satisfied. Then, (
2.7
) holds for
t\geq t_{*}>0.
Without loss of generality, we may assume
t_{*}>\tau
, and we deduce the following estimates.

Remark 3.1
.

If
\{\theta_{i}(t)\}_{i=1}^{N}
is a global solution of (
4.1
), then

\begin{split}|\theta_{i}(t-\tau_{ik})-\theta_{j}(t)|&\leq|\theta_{i}(t-\tau_{ik})-\theta_{i}(t)|+|\theta_{i}(t)-\theta_{j}(t)|\\
&\leq R_{\omega}\tau+d_{\theta}(t)\\
&\leq R_{\omega}\tau+d_{\infty}<\frac{\pi}{2},\ \forall\,t\geq t_{*}.\end{split}

Thus, if we denote
\xi_{*}=\cos\Big(R_{\omega}\tau+d_{\infty}\Big),
we have that

(3.2)

\cos(\theta_{i}(t-\tau_{ik})-\theta_{j}(t))\geq\xi_{*}>0,\ \forall\,t\geq t_{*}.

Let us denote

\displaystyle\tau_{i}:=\max_{i\in\mathcal{N}_{j}}\{\tau_{ji}\}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\tau_{0}:=\min_{i=1,\dots,N}\{\tau_{i}\}.

Definition 3.1
.

We define two numbers,
M_{0}
and
m_{0},
as

(3.3)

\begin{split}M_{0}:=\max_{i=1,\dots,N}\,\max_{t\in I_{0}^{i}}\ \omega_{i}(t)\quad\mbox{and}\quad m_{0}:=\min_{i=1,\dots,N}\,\min_{t\in I_{0}^{i}}\ \omega_{i}(t),\end{split}

respectively, where
I_{0}^{i}:=[t_{*}-\tau_{i},t_{*}].

First, we show that the velocities remain bounded. Indeed, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\{\theta_{i}(t)\}_{i=1}^{N}
a global classical solution of (
1.1
). Then, for all
i=1,\dots,N,
we have

(3.4)

m_{0}\leq\omega_{i}(t)\leq M_{0},

for all
t\geq t_{*}-\tau_{i},
with
m_{0}
and
M_{0}
given by (
3.3
).

Proof.

Fix

\epsilon>0
and let us set

{\mathcal{T}}^{\epsilon}:=\Big\{t>t_{*}\ :\ \max_{i=1,...,N}\omega_{i}(s)<M_{0}+\epsilon\quad\forall s\in[t_{*},t)\Big\}.

Since the inequality (
3.4
) is trivial in
[t_{*}-\tau_{i},t_{*}],

i=1,\dots,N,
by continuity we deduce that
{\mathcal{T}}^{\epsilon}\neq\emptyset.
Let us denote
{\mathcal{S}}^{\epsilon}:=\sup{\mathcal{T}}^{\epsilon}
. By definition of
{\mathcal{T}}^{\epsilon}
, trivially
{\mathcal{S}}^{\epsilon}>0
. We claim that
{\mathcal{S}}^{\epsilon}=+\infty
. Assume by contradiction that
{\mathcal{S}}^{\epsilon}<+\infty
. By definition of
{\mathcal{S}}^{\epsilon}
we have that

\max_{i=1,...,N}\omega_{i}(t)<M_{0}+\epsilon,\quad\forall t\in[t_{*},{\mathcal{S}}^{\epsilon}),

and

\lim_{t\rightarrow{\mathcal{S}}^{\epsilon-}}\max_{i=1,...,N}\omega_{i}(t)=M_{0}+\epsilon\,.

For every
i=1,...,N
,
\forall t\in(t_{*},{\mathcal{S}}^{\epsilon}),
we obtain

\frac{d}{dt}\omega_{i}(t)=\frac{\kappa}{N-1}\sum_{k\neq i}\cos(\theta_{k}(t-\tau_{ik})-\theta_{i}(t))(\omega_{k}(t-\tau_{ik})-\omega_{i}(t)).

Note that, from (
3.2
)
\cos(\theta_{k}(t-\tau_{ik})-\theta_{i}(t))>0,
for all
i,k=1,\dots,N,
and for every
t\geq t_{*}.

Moreover, if
t\in(t_{*},{\mathcal{S}}^{\epsilon})
, then
t-\tau_{ik}\in(t_{*}-\tau,{\mathcal{S}}^{\epsilon})
and thus,

\omega_{k}(t-\tau_{ik})<M_{0}+\epsilon,\quad\forall k=1,...,N.

Then, it follows from (
3.1
) that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \frac{d}{dt}\omega_{i}(t)\leq\frac{\kappa}{N-1}\sum_{k\neq i}\cos(\theta_{k}(t-\tau_{ik})-\theta_{i}(t))(M_{0}+\epsilon-\omega_{i}(t))\leq\kappa(M_{0}+\epsilon-\omega_{i}(t)),\end{aligned}

where we used that also

\omega_{i}(t)<M_{0}+\epsilon
, for all
i=1,...,N,

and thus,
M_{0}+\epsilon-\omega_{i}(t)\geq 0
.

Using the Grönwall’s lemma, we find that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \omega_{i}(t)&\leq e^{-\kappa(t-t_{*})}\omega_{i}(t_{*})+\kappa(M_{0}+\epsilon)\int_{t_{*}}^{t}e^{-\kappa(t-s)}\,ds\cr&=e^{-\kappa(t-t_{*})}\omega_{i}(t_{*})+(M_{0}+\epsilon)(1-e^{-\kappa(t-t_{*})})\cr&\leq e^{-\kappa(t-t_{*})}M_{0}+M_{0}+\epsilon-e^{-\kappa(t-t_{*})}M_{0}-\epsilon e^{-\kappa(t-t_{*})}\cr&=M_{0}+\epsilon-\epsilon e^{-\kappa(t-t_{*})}\leq M_{0}+\epsilon-\epsilon e^{-\kappa({\mathcal{S}}^{\epsilon}-t_{*})}\,.\end{aligned}

This implies

\max_{i=1,...,N}\omega_{i}(t)\leq M_{0}+\epsilon-\epsilon e^{-\kappa(S^{\epsilon}-t_{*})},\quad\forall\,t\in(t_{*},{\mathcal{S}}^{\epsilon}).

Taking the limit for
t\rightarrow{\mathcal{S}}^{\epsilon-}
, we have that

\lim_{t\rightarrow{\mathcal{S}}^{\epsilon-}}\max_{i=1,...,N}\omega_{i}(t)\leq M_{0}+\epsilon-\epsilon e^{-\kappa({\mathcal{S}}^{\epsilon}-t_{*})}<M_{0}+\epsilon,

and this gives a contradiction. Then,
{\mathcal{S}}^{\epsilon}=+\infty
, and subsequently, we arrive at

\max_{i=1,...,N}\omega_{i}(t)<M_{0}+\epsilon,\quad\forall\,t\geq t_{*}.

Since
\epsilon>0
is arbitrary, we conclude that

\max_{i=1,...,N}\omega_{i}(t)\leq M_{0},

and then

\omega_{i}(t)\leq M_{0},\quad\forall\,t\geq t_{*}-\tau_{i},\ \forall\,i=1,...,N.

Applying the above argument to
-\omega_{i}(t),

t\geq t_{*},
we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} -\omega_{i}(t)&\leq\max_{j=1,...,N}\max_{s\in[t_{*}-\tau_{i},t_{*}]}\{-\omega_{j}(s)\}=-\min_{j=1,...,N}\min_{s\in[t_{*}-\tau_{i},t_{*}]}\omega_{j}(s)=-m_{0}.\end{aligned}

Hence we deduce

\omega_{i}(t)\geq m_{0},\quad\forall\,t\geq t_{*}-\tau_{i},\ \forall\,i=1,...,N.

This concludes the proof.
∎

Without loss of generality, being system (
1.1
) invariant by translation, we may assume

Definition 3.2

For all

Lemma 3.2

Let

m_{0}+\frac{\Gamma}{2}(M_{0}-m_{0})\leq\omega_{i}(t)\leq M_{0}-\frac{\Gamma}{2}(M_{0}-m_{0}),\quad t\in[t_{*}+(2\gamma-1)\tau,t_{*}+2\gamma\tau],

Let

m_{0}\leq\omega_{L}(t)\leq\frac{M_{0}+m_{0}}{2},\ t\in[\alpha_{L},\beta_{L}].

\chi_{i_{1}L}=1

\tau_{i_{1}L}=\tau_{L}
. Consider

From the equation (

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\omega_{i_{1}}(t)&=\frac{\kappa}{N-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\neq i_{1}\\

\begin{split}&\frac{d}{dt}\omega_{i_{1}}(t)\leq\frac{\kappa}{N-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\neq i_{1}\\

&\hskip 42.67912pt+\frac{\kappa}{N-1}\cos(\theta_{L}(t-\tau_{i_{1}L})-\theta_{i_{1}}(t))\Big(\frac{M_{0}+m_{0}}{2}-\omega_{i_{1}}(t)\Big)\\

&\hskip 42.67912pt+\frac{\kappa}{N-1}\cos(\theta_{L}(t-\tau_{i_{1}L})-\theta_{i_{1}}(t))\Big(\frac{M_{0}+m_{0}}{2}-\omega_{i_{1}}(t)\Big)\\

Integrating the above inequality over

Denoting

\omega_{i_{1}}(\beta_{L}+\tau_{L})\leq(1-\delta_{-})M_{0}.

Note that

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\omega_{i_{1}}(t)&=\frac{\kappa}{N-1}\sum_{j\neq i_{1}}\chi_{i_{1}j}\cos(\theta_{j}(t-\tau_{i_{1}j})-\theta_{i_{1}}(t))(\omega_{j}(t-\tau_{i_{1}j})-\omega_{i_{1}}(t))\\
&\leq\frac{\kappa}{N-1}N_{i_{1}}(M_{0}-\omega_{i_{1}}(t)).\end{split}

Integrating over
[\beta_{L}+\tau_{L},t]
with
t\in[\beta_{L}+\tau_{L},t_{*}+2\gamma\tau]
, we obtain

\begin{split}\omega_{i_{1}}(t)&\leq e^{-\frac{\kappa}{N-1}N_{i_{1}}(t-\beta_{L}-\tau_{L})}\omega_{i_{1}}(\beta_{L}+\tau_{L})+(1-e^{-\frac{\kappa}{N-1}N_{i_{1}}(t-\beta_{L}-\tau_{L})})M_{0}\\
&\leq e^{-\frac{\kappa}{N-1}N_{i_{1}}(t-\beta_{L}-\tau_{L})}(1-\delta_{-})M_{0}+(1-e^{-\frac{\kappa}{N-1}N_{i_{1}}(t-\beta_{L}-\tau_{L})})M_{0}\\
&\leq M_{0}(1-e^{-\frac{\kappa}{N-1}N_{i_{1}}2\gamma\tau}\delta_{-}),\end{split}

where we used (
3.9
).
Using
1\leq N_{i_{1}}\leq N-1
, we find

(3.10)

\omega_{i_{1}}(t)\leq M_{0}(1-e^{-\kappa 2\gamma\tau}\delta_{-}),\ t\in[\beta_{L}+\tau_{L},t_{*}+2\gamma\tau].

Consider now
i_{2}\in\{1,\dots,N\}\setminus\{i_{1}\}
such that
\chi_{i_{2}i_{1}}=1
and consider
t\in[t_{*}+2\tau,t_{*}+2\gamma\tau].
Again, from equation (
3.1
) we have

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\omega_{i_{2}}(t)&=\frac{\kappa}{N-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\neq i_{2}\\
j\neq i_{1}\end{subarray}}\chi_{i_{2}j}\cos(\theta_{j}(t-\tau_{i_{2}j})-\theta_{i_{2}}(t))(\omega_{j}(t-\tau_{i_{2}j})-\omega_{i_{2}}(t))\\
&\hskip 22.76228pt+\frac{\kappa}{N-1}\cos(\theta_{i_{1}}(t-\tau_{i_{2}i_{1}})-\theta_{i_{2}}(t))(\omega_{i_{1}}(t-\tau_{i_{2}i_{1}})-\omega_{i_{2}}(t))\\
&\leq\frac{\kappa}{N-1}(N_{i_{2}}-\cos(\theta_{i_{1}}(t-\tau_{i_{2}i_{1}})-\theta_{i_{2}}(t)))(M_{0}-\omega_{i_{2}}(t))\\
&\hskip 22.76228pt+\frac{\kappa}{N-1}\cos(\theta_{i_{1}}(t-\tau_{i_{2}i_{1}})-\theta_{i_{2}}(t))[M_{0}(1-e^{-\kappa 2\gamma\tau}\delta_{-})-\omega_{i_{2}}(t)]\\
&\leq\frac{\kappa}{N-1}M_{0}(N_{i_{2}}-e^{-\kappa 2\gamma\tau}\xi_{*}\delta_{-})-\frac{\kappa}{N-1}N_{i_{2}}\omega_{i_{2}}(t),\end{split}

where we used (
3.10
).
Integrating over
[t_{*}+2\tau,t]
with
t\in[t_{*}+2\tau,t_{*}+2\gamma\tau]
deduces

\omega_{i_{2}}(t)\leq M_{0}\left[1-e^{-\kappa 2\gamma\tau}\xi_{*}\delta_{-}\left(\frac{1-e^{-\frac{\kappa}{N-1}(t-t_{*}-2\tau)}}{N-1}\right)\right].

Then, for
t\in[t_{*}+3\tau,t_{*}+2\gamma\tau],
we have

\omega_{i_{2}}(t)\leq M_{0}\Big(1-e^{-\kappa 2\gamma\tau}\xi_{*}\delta_{-}\Big(\frac{1-e^{-\frac{\kappa\tau}{N-1}}}{N-1}\Big)\Big).

Iterating this process, along the path starting from
i_{1},
we obtain the following upper bound:

\omega_{i_{n}}(t)\leq M_{0}\left[1-e^{-\kappa 2\gamma\tau}\delta_{-}\xi_{*}^{n-1}\left(\frac{1-e^{-\frac{\kappa\tau}{N-1}}}{N-1}\right)^{n-1}\right],

with
n
such that
2\leq n\leq\gamma
and
t\in[t_{*}+(2n-1)\tau,t_{*}+2\gamma\tau].

Note that, being the digraph strongly connected, from
i_{1}
one can reach any other state along a path of length less or equal to the depth
\gamma.

Therefore, for all
i=1,\dots,N,
we have

(3.11)

\omega_{i}(t)\leq M_{0}\left[1-e^{-\kappa 2\gamma\tau}\delta_{-}\xi_{*}^{\gamma-1}\left(\frac{1-e^{-\frac{\kappa\tau}{N-1}}}{N-1}\right)^{\gamma-1}\right],\quad t\in[t_{*}+(2\gamma-1)\tau,t_{*}+2\gamma\tau].

Now, consider the state
R\in\{1,\dots,N\}
such that
\omega_{R}(s)=M_{0}
for some
s\in[t_{*}-\tau_{R},t_{*}]
. Again, we can find a closed interval
[\alpha_{R},\beta_{R}]\subset[t_{*}-\tau_{R},t_{*}]
such that

\frac{m_{0}+M_{0}}{2}\leq\omega_{R}(t)\leq M_{0},\ t\in[\alpha_{R},\beta_{R}].

Using arguments analogous to the previous ones, we find a lower bound for all
\omega_{i}(t),

i=1,\dots,N
:

(3.12)

\omega_{i}(t)\geq m_{0}\left[1+e^{-\kappa 2\gamma\tau}\delta_{+}\xi_{*}^{\gamma-1}\left(\frac{1-e^{-\frac{\kappa\tau}{N-1}}}{N-1}\right)^{\gamma-1}\right],\quad t\in[t_{*}+(2\gamma-1)\tau,t_{*}+2\gamma\tau],

with

\delta_{+}:=\frac{\xi_{*}}{2(N-1)}(1-e^{-\frac{\kappa}{N-1}\sigma})\Big(\frac{M_{0}}{m_{0}}-1\Big).

Finally, recalling definition (
3.7
), from (
3.11
) and (
3.12
),
we can find the final estimate

m_{0}+\frac{\Gamma}{2}(M_{0}-m_{0})\leq\omega_{i}(t)\leq M_{0}-\frac{\Gamma}{2}(M_{0}-m_{0}),

for
i=1,\dots,N
and
t\in[t_{*}+(2\gamma-1)\tau,t_{*}+2\gamma\tau]
.
∎

We are now able to prove the main result of this paper.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\{\theta_{i}(t)\}_{i=1}^{N}
be a solution to the Kuramoto model (
1.1
) on a strongly connected digraph
\mathcal{G}
, with initial data (
1.2
). Assume that the hypotheses of Lemma
2.3
are satisfied.
Then, the Kuramoto oscillators with delayed coupling achieve the asymptotic complete frequency synchronization in the sense of Definition
1.1
.

Proof.

We define the quantities
D_{n}:=M_{n}-m_{n}
and

\Gamma_{n}:=\Big(\frac{\xi_{*}}{N-1}\Big)^{\gamma}e^{-\kappa 2\gamma\tau}(1-e^{-\frac{\kappa\tau}{N-1}})^{\gamma-1}(1-e^{-\frac{\kappa\sigma_{n}}{N-1}}),

where
\sigma_{n}:=\min\{\tau_{0},\frac{M_{n}-m_{n}}{4\kappa R_{\omega}}\}.
Then,
\Gamma_{0}
is the constant
\Gamma
defined in (
3.7
) and
\Gamma_{n}\in(0,1)
for all
n\geq 0.
Now, we use Lemma
3.2
on the intervals
J_{n}:=[t_{*}+(2\gamma n-1)\tau,t_{*}+2\gamma n\tau],

n\in\mathbb{N}
.
For
t\in J_{1}
we get

\begin{split}D_{1}&=M_{1}-m_{1}\cr&=\max_{i=1,\dots,N}\max_{t\in I_{1}^{i}}\ \omega_{i}(t)-\min_{i=1,\dots,N}\min_{t\in I_{1}^{i}}\ \omega_{i}(t)\\
&\leq M_{0}-\frac{\Gamma_{0}}{2}(M_{0}-m_{0})-m_{0}-\frac{\Gamma_{0}}{2}(M_{0}-m_{0})\cr&=(M_{0}-m_{0})(1-\Gamma_{0})\cr&=D_{0}(1-\Gamma_{0}).\end{split}

Thus, we find that
D_{1}\leq(1-\Gamma_{0})D_{0}.
Iterating the argument gives

D_{n+1}\leq(1-\Gamma_{n})D_{n},\ \forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

Let us denote

\tilde{\sigma}(D):=\min\Big\{\tau_{0},\frac{D}{4\kappa R_{\omega}}\Big\},

so that
\sigma_{n}=\tilde{\sigma}(D_{n})
for
n\in\mathbb{N}.

Similarly, let us denote

\tilde{\Gamma}(D):=\Big(\frac{\xi_{*}}{N-1}\Big)^{\gamma}e^{-\kappa 2\gamma\tau}(1-e^{-\frac{\kappa\tau}{N-1}})^{\gamma-1}\left(1-e^{-\frac{\kappa\tilde{\sigma}(D)}{N-1}}\right)

so that
\Gamma_{n}=\tilde{\Gamma}(D_{n})
for
n\in\mathbb{N}.
Then we find

D_{n+1}\leq(1-\tilde{\Gamma}(D_{n}))D_{n},

and subsequently,
\{D_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a non-negative and decreasing sequence. Passing to the limit
n\to\infty
in the above estimate, and denoting
D
the limit of
\{D_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}},
we have

D\leq(1-\tilde{\Gamma}(D))D,

that is true only if
\tilde{\Gamma}(D)\leq 0.
This gives
D=0
and, noticing that, from Lemma
3.1
and Remark
3.2
, for further times to respect
J_{n},
we have

\omega_{i}(t)-\omega_{j}(t)\leq M_{n}-m_{n}=D_{n}
for all
i,j=1,\dots,N
, we conclude that

|\omega_{i}(t)-\omega_{j}(t)|\rightarrow 0,

as
t\rightarrow+\infty
and for all
i,j=1,\dots,N.

Then, the system achieves asymptotic synchronization. ∎

4.
Asymptotic synchronization: all-to-all connected case

In this section, we consider the Kuramoto oscillators with delayed coupling in the case of all-to-all connection, i.e.
\chi_{ij}=1,
for all
i,j=1,\dots,N
. In this case, the system (
1.1
) reduces to

(4.1)

\frac{d}{dt}\theta_{i}(t)=\Omega_{i}+\frac{\kappa}{N-1}\sum_{k\neq i}\sin(\theta_{k}(t-\tau_{ik})-\theta_{i}(t)),\quad i=1,\dots,N,\quad t>0,

subject to the initial data (
1.2
).

Differently from the strongly connected case, in the all-to-all connected case, we prove the exponential frequency synchronization of system (
4.1
), i.e. the frequency-diameter decays to zero exponentially fast as time goes to infinity.

To be more specific, our main result of this section is the following.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\{\theta_{i}(t)\}_{i=1}^{N}
be a global-in-time solution to the Kuramoto model (
4.1
) with initial data (
1.2
). Assume that the hypotheses of Lemma
2.3
are satisfied.
Then, the time-delayed Kuramoto oscillators achieve the asymptotic complete frequency synchronization in the sense of Definition
1.1
exponentially fast. More precisely, we have

\displaystyle d_{\omega}(t)\leq{\mathcal{C}}e^{-\tilde{\gamma}t}\quad\mbox{for all}\quad t\geq 0,

where
\gamma
and
{\mathcal{C}}
are positive constants depending on
R_{\omega}
,
\kappa
, and
\tau
.

To prove Theorem
4.1
we need some preliminary lemmas (cf.
[
23
,
12
]
).
In the whole section, we assume that the hypotheses of Lemma
2.3
are satisfied.

First, extending (
1.3
), we define, for
n\in\mathbb{N},

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{D_{\theta}^{*}(n):=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i,j\leq N,\\
s,t\in[t_{*}+(n-1)\tau,t_{*}+n\tau]\end{subarray}}|\theta_{i}(s)-\theta_{j}(t)|,}\\
\displaystyle{D_{\omega}^{*}(n):=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i,j\leq N,\\
s,t\in[t_{*}+(n-1)\tau,t_{*}+n\tau]\end{subarray}}|\omega_{i}(s)-\omega_{j}(t)|,}\end{array}

where
t_{*}
is the time in (
2.7
).

The following lemma generalizes Lemma
3.1
. We omit the proof since it is analogous to the previous one.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\{\theta_{i}(t)\}_{i=1}^{N}
be a solution to the Kuramoto model (
4.1
) with initial conditions (
1.2
).
Then, for any
T\geq t_{*}
, we have

\min_{j=1,...,N}\min_{s\in[T-\tau,T]}\omega_{j}(s)\leq\omega_{i}(t)\leq\max_{j=1,...,N}\max_{s\in[T-\tau,T]}\omega_{j}(s)

for all
t\geq T-{\tau}
and
i=1,...,N
.

From Lemma
4.1
, we deduce the properties below.

Lemma 4.2
.

For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
i,j=1,...,N,
we get

|\omega_{i}(s)-\omega_{j}(t)|\leq D_{\omega}^{*}(n),\quad\forall s,t\geq t_{*}+(n-1)\tau.

Proof.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
i,j=1,...,N.
Without loss of generality, taken
s,t\geq t_{*}+(n-1)\tau
, we can suppose
\omega_{i}(s)>\omega_{j}(t).

Then, using the Lemma
4.1
with
T=t_{*}+n\tau
and Cauchy–Schwartz inequality, we have

\displaystyle|\omega_{i}(s)-\omega_{j}(t)|

\displaystyle=\omega_{i}(s)-\omega_{j}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\max_{l=1,...,N}\max_{r\in[t_{*}+(n-1)\tau,t_{*}+n\tau]}\omega_{l}(r)-\min_{l=1,...,N}\min_{r\in[t_{*}+(n-1)\tau,t_{*}+n\tau]}\omega_{l}(r)

\displaystyle\leq\max_{l,k=1,...,N}\max_{r,\sigma\in[t_{*}+(n-1)\tau,t_{*}+n\tau]}|\omega_{l}(r)-\omega_{k}(\sigma)|=D_{\omega}^{*}(n).

This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.1
.

Note that, from Lemma
4.2
, for
n=0
, we find
|\omega_{i}(s)-\omega_{j}(t)|\leq D_{\omega}^{*}(0).
Moreover,

D_{\omega}^{*}(n+1)\leq D_{\omega}^{*}(n),\ \forall\,n\in\mathbb{N}.

Lemma 4.3
.

For all
i,j=1,...,N
and
n\in\mathbb{N},
we have that

(4.2)

\omega_{i}(t)-\omega_{j}(t)\leq e^{-\kappa(t-t_{0})}(\omega_{i}(t_{0})-\omega_{j}(t_{0}))+(1-e^{-\kappa(t-t_{0})})D_{\omega}^{*}(n),

for all
t\geq t_{0}\geq t_{*}+n\tau.
Moreover, for all
s_{0},t_{0}\in[t_{*}+n\tau,t_{*}+(n+2)\tau]
, we obtain

(4.3)

\omega_{i}(t_{*}+(n+2)\tau)-\omega_{j}(t_{*}+(n+2)\tau)\leq e^{-2\kappa\tau}(\omega_{i}(t_{0})-\omega_{j}(s_{0}))+(1-e^{-2\kappa\tau})D_{\omega}^{*}(n).

Proof.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
and let us denote

\displaystyle M_{n}^{*}:=\max_{i=1,...,N}\max_{t\in[t_{*}+(n-1)\tau,t_{*}+n\tau]}\omega_{i}(t)\quad\mbox{and}\quad m_{n}^{*}:=\min_{i=1,...,N}\min_{t\in[t_{*}+(n-1)\tau,t_{*}+n\tau]}\omega_{i}(t).

Note that
M_{n}^{*}-m_{n}^{*}\leq D_{\omega}^{*}(n)
.
For all
i=1,...,N
and
t\geq t_{0}\geq t_{*}+n\tau
, we write

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \frac{d}{dt}\omega_{i}(t)&=\frac{\kappa}{N-1}\sum_{k\neq i}\cos(\theta_{k}(t-\tau_{ik})-\theta_{i}(t))(\omega_{k}(t-\tau_{ik})-\omega_{i}(t))\cr&\leq\frac{\kappa}{N-1}\sum_{k\neq i}\cos(\theta_{k}(t-\tau_{ik})-\theta_{i}(t))(M_{n-1}^{*}-\omega_{i}(t)),\end{aligned}

where we used the fact that
t-\tau_{ik}\geq t_{*}+(n-1)\tau.

By Lemma 3.1, since
t\geq t_{*}+n\tau
, we get
\omega_{i}(t)\leq M_{n-1}^{*}
, that implies
M_{n-1}^{*}-\omega_{i}(t)\geq 0
. Then we write

\frac{d}{dt}\omega_{i}(t)\leq\kappa(M_{n-1}^{*}-\omega_{i}(t)).

Applying Grönwall’s lemma, we find

(4.4)

\omega_{i}(t)\leq e^{-\kappa(t-t_{0})}\omega_{i}(t_{0})+(1-e^{-\kappa(t-t_{0})})M_{n-1}^{*}.

Similarly, for all
i=1,...,N
and
t\geq t_{0}\geq t_{*}+n\tau
, we deduce

(4.5)

\omega_{i}(t)\geq e^{-\kappa(t-t_{0})}\omega_{i}(t_{0})+(1-e^{-\kappa(t-t_{0})})m_{n-1}^{*}.

Therefore, for
i,j=1,...,N
and
t\geq t_{0}\geq t_{*}+n\tau,
we have

\displaystyle\omega_{i}(t)-\omega_{j}(t)

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\kappa(t-t_{0})}\omega_{i}(t_{0})+(1-e^{-\kappa(t-t_{0})})M_{n-1}^{*}-e^{-\kappa(t-t_{0})}\omega_{j}(t_{0})-(1-e^{-\kappa(t-t_{0})})m_{n-1}^{*}

\displaystyle=e^{-\kappa(t-t_{0})}(\omega_{i}(t_{0})-\omega_{j}(t_{0}))+(1-e^{-\kappa(t-t_{0})})(M_{n-1}^{*}-m_{n-1}^{*})

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\kappa(t-t_{0})}(\omega_{i}(t_{0})-\omega_{j}(t_{0}))+(1-e^{-\kappa(t-t_{0})})D_{\omega}^{*}(n).

This gives the first assertion (
4.2
).

For (
4.3
), from (
4.4
) with
t_{0}\in[t_{*}+n\tau,t_{*}+(n+2)\tau]
and
t:=t_{*}+(n+2)\tau
we find

(4.6)

\begin{split}\omega_{i}(t_{*}+(n+2)\tau)&\leq e^{-\kappa(t_{*}+(n+2)\tau-t_{0})}\omega_{i}(t_{0})+(1-e^{-\kappa(t_{*}+(n+2)\tau-t_{0})})M_{n-1}^{*}\\
&=e^{-\kappa(t_{*}+(n+2)\tau-t_{0})}(\omega_{i}(t_{0})-M_{n-1}^{*})+M_{n-1}^{*}\\
&\leq e^{-2\kappa\tau}(\omega_{i}(t_{0})-M_{n-1}^{*})+M_{n-1}^{*}\\
&=e^{-2\kappa\tau}\omega_{i}(t_{0})+(1-e^{-2\kappa\tau})M_{n-1}^{*}.\end{split}

Similarly, from (
4.5
), for any
j=1,\dots,N
and
s_{0}\in[t_{*}+n\tau,t_{*}+(n+2)\tau]
we have

(4.7)

\omega_{i}(t_{*}+(n+2)\tau)\geq e^{-2\kappa\tau}\omega_{i}(s_{0})+(1-e^{-2\kappa\tau})m_{n-1}^{*}.

Combining (
4.6
) and (
4.7
) we arrive at (
4.3
), and this completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

For all
n\in\mathbb{N},
we have that

D_{\omega}^{*}(n+1)\leq e^{-\kappa\tau}d_{\omega}(t_{*}+n\tau)+(1-e^{-\kappa\tau})D_{\omega}^{*}(n).

Proof.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
and let
i,j=1,...,N,

s,t\in[t_{*}+n\tau,t_{*}+(n+1)\tau],
such that
D_{\omega}^{*}(n+1)=|\omega_{i}(s)-\omega_{j}(t)|
. Notice that if
|\omega_{i}(s)-\omega_{j}(t)|=0
then it is obvious that

\displaystyle 0=D_{\omega}^{*}(n+1)\leq e^{-\kappa\tau}d_{\omega}(t_{*}+n\tau)+(1-e^{-\kappa\tau})D_{\omega}^{*}(n).

Then, we now suppose
|\omega_{i}(s)-\omega_{j}(t)|>0
and, without loss of generality, we may assume
\omega_{i}(s)>\omega_{j}(t).

From (
4.4
) with
t_{0}=t_{*}+n\tau,
we get

\displaystyle\omega_{i}(s)

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\kappa(s-t_{*}-n\tau)}\omega_{i}(t_{*}+n\tau)+(1-e^{-\kappa(s-t_{*}-n\tau)})M_{n}^{*}

\displaystyle=e^{-\kappa(s-t_{*}-n\tau)}(\omega_{i}(t_{*}+n\tau)-M_{n}^{*})+M_{n}^{*}.

Thus, since
s\leq t_{*}+(n+1)\tau,
we obtain

\omega_{i}(s)\leq e^{-\kappa\tau}(\omega_{i}(t_{*}+n\tau)-M_{n}^{*})+M_{n}^{*}=e^{-\kappa\tau}\omega_{i}(t_{*}+n\tau)+(1-e^{-\kappa\tau})M_{n}^{*}.

Analogously, we have that

\omega_{j}(t)\geq e^{-\kappa\tau}\omega_{j}(t_{*}+n\tau)+(1-e^{-\kappa\tau})m_{n}^{*}.

Therefore, we conclude that

\displaystyle D_{\omega}^{*}(n+1)

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\kappa\tau}(\omega_{i}(t_{*}+n\tau)-\omega_{j}(t_{*}+n\tau))+(1-e^{-\kappa\tau})D_{\omega}^{*}(n)

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\kappa\tau}d_{\omega}(t_{*}+n\tau)+(1-e^{-\kappa\tau})D_{\omega}^{*}(n).

∎

Before proving our main result we need a further lemma.

Lemma 4.5
.

There exists a constant
C\in(0,1)
such that

d_{\omega}(t_{*}+n\tau)\leq CD_{\omega}^{*}(n-2),

for all
n\geq 2
.

Proof.

If
d_{\omega}(t_{*}+n\tau)=0,
then the assetion is obvious for all
C\in(0,1).
Then, let us suppose
d_{\omega}(t_{*}+n\tau)>0
. Fix
i,j=1,...,N
such that
d_{\omega}(t_{*}+n\tau)=|\omega_{i}(t_{*}+n\tau)-\omega_{j}(t_{*}+n\tau)|.
Without lost of generality, we may assume
\omega_{i}(t_{*}+n\tau)>\omega_{j}(t_{*}+n\tau).

As before, we consider the following quantities:

M_{n-1}^{*}=\max_{l=1,...,N}\max_{s\in[t_{*}+(n-2)\tau,t_{*}+(n-1)\tau]}\omega_{l}(s)

and

m_{n-1}^{*}=\min_{l=1,...,N}\min_{s\in[t_{*}+(n-2)\tau,t_{*}+(n-1)\tau]}\omega_{l}(s)\,.

Then we get
M_{n-1}^{*}-m_{n-1}^{*}\leq D_{\omega}^{*}(n-1)
.

Let us distinguish two cases.

Case I
: Suppose that there exist
t_{0},s_{0}\in[t_{*}+(n-2)\tau,t_{*}+n\tau]
such that
\omega_{i}(t_{0})-\omega_{j}(s_{0})<0
. Then, due to (
4.3
) we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} d_{\omega}(t_{*}+n\tau)&\leq e^{-2\kappa\tau}(\omega_{i}(t_{0})-\omega_{j}(s_{0}))+(1-e^{-2\kappa\tau})D_{\omega}^{*}(n-2)\cr&\leq(1-e^{-2\kappa\tau})D_{\omega}^{*}(n-2).\end{aligned}

Then, the assertion follows in this case.

Case II
: Suppose that
\omega_{i}(t)-\omega_{j}(s)\geq 0
for all
t,s\in[t_{*}+(n-2)\tau,t_{*}+n\tau]
. Then, for
t\in[t_{*}+(n-1)\tau,t_{*}+n\tau]
we obtain

(4.8)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \frac{d}{dt}(\omega_{i}(t)-&\omega_{j}(t))=\frac{\kappa}{N-1}\sum_{k\neq i}\cos(\theta_{k}(t-\tau_{ik})-\theta_{i}(t))(\omega_{k}(t-\tau_{ik})-\omega_{i}(t))\cr&-\frac{\kappa}{N-1}\sum_{k\neq j}\cos(\theta_{k}(t-\tau_{jk})-\theta_{j}(t))(\omega_{k}(t-\tau_{jk})-\omega_{j}(t))\cr&=\frac{\kappa}{N-1}\sum_{k\neq i}\cos(\theta_{k}(t-\tau_{ik})-\theta_{i}(t))(\omega_{k}(t-\tau_{ik})-M_{n-1}^{*}+M_{n-1}^{*}-\omega_{i}(t))\cr&+\frac{\kappa}{N-1}\sum_{k\neq j}\cos(\theta_{k}(t-\tau_{jk})-\theta_{j}(t))(\omega_{j}(t)-m_{n-1}^{*}+m_{n-1}^{*}-\omega_{k}(t-\tau_{jk}))\cr&:=S_{1}+S_{2}.\end{aligned}

Since
t\in[t_{*}+(n-1)\tau,t_{*}+n\tau]
, we get
t-\tau_{ij}\in[t_{*}+(n-2)\tau,t_{*}+(n-1)\tau]
for all
i,j=1,\dots,N
. Thus, it follows from Lemma
4.1
with
T=t_{*}+(n-2)\tau
that

\displaystyle m_{n-1}^{*}\leq\omega_{k}(t)\leq M_{n-1}^{*}\quad\mbox{and}\quad m_{n-1}^{*}\leq\omega_{k}(t-\tau_{ik})\leq M_{n-1}^{*}

for all
i,k=1,...,N.
Then, we estimate

(4.9)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} S_{1}&=\frac{\kappa}{N-1}\sum_{k\neq i}\cos(\theta_{k}(t-\tau_{ik})-\theta_{i}(t))(\omega_{k}(t-\tau_{ik})-M_{n-1}^{*})\cr&\quad+\frac{\kappa}{N-1}\sum_{k\neq i}\cos(\theta_{k}(t-\tau_{ik})-\theta_{i}(t))(M_{n-1}^{*}-\omega_{i}(t))\cr&\leq\frac{\kappa}{N-1}\xi_{*}\sum_{k\neq i}(\omega_{k}(t-\tau_{ik})-M_{n-1}^{*})+\kappa(M_{n-1}^{*}-\omega_{i}(t)),\end{aligned}

and

(4.10)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} S_{2}&:=\frac{\kappa}{N-1}\sum_{k\neq j}\cos(\theta_{k}(t-\tau_{jk})-\theta_{j}(t))(\omega_{j}(t)-m_{n-1}^{*})\cr&\quad+\frac{\kappa}{N-1}\sum_{k\neq j}\cos(\theta_{k}(t-\tau_{jk})-\theta_{j}(t))(m_{n-1}^{*}-\omega_{k}(t-\tau_{jk}))\cr&\leq\kappa(\omega_{j}(t)-m_{n-1}^{*})+\frac{\kappa}{N-1}\xi_{*}\sum_{k\neq j}(m_{n-1}^{*}-\omega_{k}(t-\tau_{jk})),\end{aligned}

where
\xi_{*}
is the positive constant given in (
3.2
).
Putting (
4.9
) and (
4.10
) in (
4.8
), we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \frac{d}{dt}(\omega_{i}(t)-&\omega_{j}(t))\leq\kappa(M_{n-1}^{*}-m_{n-1}^{*})-\kappa(\omega_{i}(t)-\omega_{j}(t))\cr&+\frac{\kappa}{N-1}\xi_{*}\sum_{k\neq i}(\omega_{k}(t-\tau_{ik})-M_{n-1}^{*})\cr&+\frac{\kappa}{N-1}\xi_{*}\sum_{k\neq j}(m_{n-1}^{*}-\omega_{k}(t-\tau_{jk})).\end{aligned}

Notice that, since
\omega_{k}(t-\tau_{ik})-M_{n-1}^{*}\leq 0
for all
i,k=1,\dots,N
and
t\in[t_{*}+(n-1)\tau,t_{*}+n\tau]
and
j\neq i,
we can write

\sum_{k\neq i}(\omega_{k}(t-\tau_{ik})-M_{n-1}^{*})\leq\omega_{j}(t-\tau_{ij})-M_{n-1}^{*}.

Analogously,

\sum_{k\neq j}(m_{n-1}^{*}-\omega_{k}(t-\tau_{jk}))\leq m_{n-1}^{*}-\omega_{i}(t-\tau_{ji}).

Therefore, we obtain

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}(\omega_{i}(t)-\omega_{j}(t))&\leq\kappa\Big(1-\frac{\xi_{*}}{N-1}\Big)(M_{n-1}^{*}-m_{n-1}^{*})-\kappa(\omega_{i}(t)-\omega_{j}(t))\\
&+\frac{\kappa}{N-1}\xi_{*}(\omega_{j}(t-\tau_{ij})-\omega_{i}(t-\tau_{ji}))\\
&\leq\kappa\Big(1-\frac{\xi_{*}}{N-1}\Big)(M_{n-1}^{*}-m_{n-1}^{*})-\kappa(\omega_{i}(t)-\omega_{j}(t)),\end{split}

where we used the assumption
\omega_{i}(t)-\omega_{j}(s)\geq 0
for all
t,s\in[t_{*}+(n-2)\tau,t_{*}+n\tau]
.

Applying now the Grönwall’s lemma over
[t,t_{*}+n\tau],
with
t\in[t_{*}+(n-1)\tau,t_{*}+n\tau],
we deduce

\begin{split}\omega_{i}(t)-\omega_{j}(t)&\leq e^{-\kappa(t-t_{*}-(n-1)\tau)}(\omega_{i}(t_{*}+(n-1)\tau)-\omega_{j}(t_{*}+(n-1)\tau))\\
&+\Big(1-\frac{\xi_{*}}{N-1}\Big)(M_{n-1}^{*}-m_{n-1}^{*})(1-e^{-\kappa(t-t_{*}-(n-1)\tau)}).\end{split}

For
t=t_{*}+n\tau,
we get

\begin{split}d_{\omega}(t_{*}+n\tau)&\leq e^{-\kappa\tau}(\omega_{i}(t_{*}+(n-1)\tau)-\omega_{j}(t_{*}+(n-1)\tau))\\
&\quad+\Big(1-\frac{\xi_{*}}{N-1}\Big)(M_{n-1}^{*}-m_{n-1}^{*})(1-e^{-\kappa\tau})\\
&\leq\Big(1-\frac{\xi_{*}}{N-1}(1-e^{-\kappa\tau})\Big)D_{\omega}^{*}(n-2),\end{split}

where we used that
M_{n-1}^{*}-m_{n-1}^{*}\leq D_{\omega}^{*}(n-1)
and the monotonicity property of
D_{\omega}^{*}(n).

Finally, we set

\displaystyle C:=\max\Big\{1-e^{-2\kappa\tau},1-\frac{\xi_{*}}{N-1}(1-e^{-\kappa\tau})\Big\}>0

to conclude the desired result.
∎

Now, we are ready to prove the exponential synchronization estimate for the system (
4.1
) with (
1.2
).

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Let
\{\theta_{i}(t)\}_{i=1}^{N}
a global classical solution of (
4.1
). We claim that

D_{\omega}^{*}(n+1)\leq\tilde{C}D_{\omega}^{*}(n-2),\quad\forall\,n\geq 2,

for a suitable constant
\tilde{C}\in(0,1).

Indeed, for
n\geq 2
, applying Lemmas
4.4
and
4.5
, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} D_{\omega}^{*}(n+1)&\leq e^{-\kappa\tau}d_{\omega}(t_{*}+n\tau)+(1-e^{-\kappa\tau})D_{\omega}^{*}(n)\cr&\leq e^{-\kappa\tau}CD_{\omega}^{*}(n-2)+(1-e^{-\kappa\tau})D_{\omega}^{*}(n)\cr&\leq e^{-\kappa\tau}CD_{\omega}^{*}(n-2)+(1-e^{-\kappa\tau})D_{\omega}^{*}(n-2)\cr&=D_{\omega}^{*}(n-2)[1-e^{-\kappa\tau}(1-C)].\end{aligned}

Denoting

\tilde{C}:=[1-e^{-\kappa\tau}(1-C)],

we have the claim. This implies that

D_{\omega}^{*}(3n)\leq\tilde{C}^{n}D_{\omega}^{*}(0),\quad\forall\,n\geq 1.

Then, we have that

D_{\omega}^{*}(3n)\leq e^{-3n\tau\gamma}D_{\omega}^{*}(0),\quad\forall\,n\in\mathbb{N},

with

\gamma:=\frac{1}{3\tau}\ln\left(\frac{1}{\tilde{C}}\right).

Now, fix

i,j=1,...,N.
For all
t\geq t_{*}-\tau,
we get
t\in[t_{*}+3n\tau-\tau,t_{*}+3n\tau+2\tau]
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then, by applying Lemma
4.2
, we can write

|\omega_{i}(t)-\omega_{j}(t)|\leq D_{\omega}^{*}(3n)\leq e^{-3n\tau\gamma}D_{\omega}^{*}(0).

Since
t\leq t_{*}+3n\tau+2\tau,
we have that

|\omega_{i}(t)-\omega_{j}(t)|\leq e^{-\gamma t}e^{\gamma(t_{*}+2\tau)}D_{\omega}^{*}(0).

Finally, passing to the maximum on
i,j=1,...,N,
we find that

d_{\omega}(t)\leq e^{-\gamma(t-t_{*}-2\tau)}D_{\omega}^{*}(0),

and this completes the proof.
∎

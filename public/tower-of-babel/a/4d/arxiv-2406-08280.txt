1.
Introduction

In 1999, M. Gromov in
[
6
]
introduced the notion of mean dimension for a topological dynamical system
(X,f)
, where
X
is a compact metric space and

a continuous self-map on

, which is, as well as the topological entropy, an invariant under conjugacy.
In 2000, E. Lindenstrauss and B. Weiss in
[
13
]
showed that a system
(X,f)
with finite dimension phase space has zero dimension, they also proved that the values of mean topological dimension of
\mathbb{Z}
-action can run over
[0,\infty]
. Due to this result, they also negatively answered the question asked by J. Auslander in
[
1
]
: Whether all minimal
\mathbb{Z}
-action systems can be embedded into
\left([0,1]^{\mathbb{Z}},\mathbb{Z}\right)
. In
[
12
]
, E. Lindenstrauss proved that a system
(X,f)
which is an extension of a minimal system, and
K
is a convex set with a non-empty interior such that

, then
(X,f)
can be embedded in the shift
\left(K^{\mathbb{Z}},\mathbb{Z}\right)
. Later, E. Lindenstrauss and M. Tsukamoto in
[
14
]
constructed a minimal system with mean dimension equal to

that can’t embed into
\left(([0,1]^{m})^{\mathbb{Z}},\mathbb{Z}\right)
. More recently, Y. Gutman and M. Tsukamoto in
[
7
]
showed

is a minimal system with

, then
(X,f)
can be embedded into
\big(([0,1]^{m})^{\mathbb{Z}},\mathbb{Z}\big)
. Therefore,
m/2
is the optimal number for minimal systems embedding into
\big(([0,1]^{m})^{\mathbb{Z}},\mathbb{Z}\big)

.

Given an infinite countable amenable group
G
, It is natural to discuss the value of the mean dimension of the system
(X,G)
. Let
P
be a polyhedron with topological dimension

and
(P^{G},G)
the

-shift on

. Given

[

. Soon after, let

be an infinite countable amenable group (without any additional assumption) and
\epsilon>0
. F. Krieger in
[
11
]
constructed a minimal subshift

of

such that its mean topological dimension is
\epsilon
-close to

. More recently, by applying the methods of construction of Toeplitz sequence in
[
17
]
and the newest results of the tiling theory of amenable groups in
[

]
, let

, D. Dou

]
constructed a minimal subshift

of

with

(

)
Let

In the case of

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Amenable groups

Let
G
be an infinite countable group and denote
\mathcal{F}(G)
the class of all finite non-empty subsets of

. Recall that a group

is

if it admits a sequence of non-empty finite subsets

such that for any

, we have

\underset{n\to\infty}{\lim}\,\displaystyle\frac{|B(F_{n},K)|}{|F_{n}|}=0
,

where
B(F_{n},K)=\{g\in G:Kg\cap F_{n}\neq\emptyset
and
Kg\cap(G\backslash F_{n})\neq\emptyset\}
and
|\cdot|
denotes the cardinality operator. Moreover, we call such sequence
\{F_{n}:F_{n}\in\mathcal{F}(G),n\in\mathbb{N}\}
a
F
\phi
lner sequence
of
G
. Throughout the paper, we assume that

is an infinite countable amenable group.

2.2.
Dynamical systems and shifts

We call

a

-space

. Let

.

A subset

. For any

, denote

and

.

The

. It is known that

(

.

Let

-

g^{\prime}(x_{g}:g\in G)=(x_{gg^{\prime}}:g\in G),

2.3.
Tiling of amenable group

The definition of tiling of a discrete countable group is originally from

]

[
5
]

Definition 2.1
.

(

.
Each

Definition 2.2

(
[

]

.
For each

.

For convenience, we assume that each element in

can not be the right translation of others and contains the identity

.

Definition 2.3
.

(
[

]
)
A finite tiling

.

2.4.
Topological dimension and Mean topological dimension

In this subsection, we recall the notations of the topological dimension and the mean topological dimension of
(X,G)
.

Let
(X,d)
be a compact metrizable space and
\alpha=\{U_{1},...,U_{n}\}
,
\beta=\{V_{1},...,V_{m}\}
finite open covers of
X
,
\beta
is said to be

than
\alpha
(written as

) if for any

such that
V_{i}\subset U_{j}
.

The

of

ord(\alpha)=\left(\max_{x\in X}\sum_{i=1}^{n}1_{U_{i}}(x)\right)-1,

where
1_{U_{i}}
is the character function of
U_{i}
, and we define a quantity

by

D(\alpha)=\min_{\alpha\preceq\beta}ord(\beta),

where
\beta
runs over all finite open covers of
X
that are finer than
\alpha
.

Definition 2.7
.

(
[
8
]
)
Let
X
be a compact topological space, the
topological dimension
of
X
is given

dim(X)=\sup_{\alpha}D(\alpha),

where
\alpha
runs over all finite open cover of
X
.

Let
(X,d)
be a compact metric space,

P
a compact metric space, and
\epsilon>0
a positive number. A continuous map
f:X\to P
is said to be
\epsilon
-injective
if
d(x_{1},x_{2})\leq\epsilon
for all
x_{1},x_{2}\in X
such that
f(x_{1})=f(x_{2})
.
Let
\epsilon>0
be a real number, we define

dim_{\epsilon}(X,d)=\inf_{P}dim(P),

where
P
runs overall compact metric spaces for which there exists an
\epsilon
-injective continuous map
f
from
X
to
P
.

.

(
[
2
, Proposition 4.6.2]
)
Let
(X,d)
be a compact metric space. It is known that

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}dim_{\epsilon}(X,d)=dim(X).

To prove the main theorem, We need some fundamental Lemmas:

Let

Proof..

The result follows that if

is an
\epsilon
-injective map, then
\varphi\circ f:X\to P
is
c\epsilon
-injective.
∎

Lemma 2.10
.

(
[
3

)
Let
(P,d)
be a polyhedron. For each

, put
d_{n}(x,y)=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}d(x_{i},y_{i})
,

Let
(X,G)
be a topological dynamical system with metric

on

,

and

a F
\phi

3.
Proof of Theorem 1.2

3.1.
Standing notations

Let
(P^{G},G)
be the
G
-shift on
P
. For each subset
F
of
G
denote
\pi_{F}:P^{G}\to P^{F}
the natural projection. Given
F\in\mathcal{F}(G)
, we say that
B_{F}
is a
block
with respect to
F
if
B_{F}\subset P^{F}
, and a block
B_{Fg}
is said to be equal to
B_{F}

up to translation
(or
B_{Fg}=B_{F}
up to translation) if
\pi_{F}\left(g^{-1}\pi^{-1}(B_{Fg})\right)=B_{F}
.

Since the proof when
P
is an
n
-dimensional cube is similar to the proof that
P=[0,1]
, for convenience, we assume
P=[0,1]
.

3.2.
Construction of
(X,G)

The construction will be performed by induction. In each step
k

Quantum walks on blow-up graphs
Bikash Bhattacharjya, ​​1 Hermie Monterde, ​​2 Hiranmoy Pal ​​3

Abstract
A blow-up of n n copies of a graph G G is the graph ⊎ 𝑛 ​ G \overset{n}{\uplus}~G obtained by replacing every vertex of G G by an independent set of size n n , where the copies of two vertices in G G are adjacent in the blow-up if and only if the two vertices are adjacent in G G . Our goal is to investigate quantum state transfer on a blow-up graph ⊎ 𝑛 ​ G \overset{n}{\uplus}~G relative to the adjacency matrix. We characterize strong cospectrality, periodicity, perfect state transfer (PST) and pretty good state transfer (PGST) in blow-up graphs. It turns out, if ⊎ 𝑛 ​ G \overset{n}{\uplus}~G admits PST or PGST, then one must have n = 2 . n=2. In particular, if G G has an invertible adjacency matrix, then each vertex in ⊎ 2 ​ G \overset{2}{\uplus}~G pairs up with a unique vertex to exhibit strong cospectrality. We then apply our results to determine infinite families of graphs whose blow-ups admit PST and PGST. Keywords: perfect state transfer, pretty good state transfer, graph spectra, blow-up, twin vertices, adjacency matrix. MSC: 05C50, 05C76, 15A16, 15A18, 81P45.

Introduction

A quantum spin network is modelled by an undirected graph
G
, where the vertices and edges of
G
represent the qubits and their interactions in the network. A
continuous-time quantum walk
on
G
is described by the unitary matrix

U(t)=\exp{\left(itA\right)}

where

,

, and

is the adjacency matrix of

represents the probability that the quantum state assigned at vertex

is found in vertex

at time

. We say

(PST) between

and
v
in

at time

if
|U\left(\tau\right)_{u,v}|=1
.
In this case, the quantum state initially at vertex
u
is transmitted to vertex
v
at time
\tau
with probability one. If
|U\left(\tau\right)_{u,u}|=1
, then
u
is said to be
periodic
in
G
at time
\tau
. A graph is
periodic
if it is periodic at all vertices at the same time.

PST is critical for short distance communication in a physical quantum computing scheme
[
21
]
. The study of PST was initiated by Bose
[
7
]
and Christandl et al.
[
11
]
. In
[

]
, we find that

and

are the only paths that admit PST. Following this negative result, several classes of graphs admitting PST have been studied, like cubelike graphs
[
6
,
9
]
, distance-regular graphs
[
13
]
, integral circulant graphs
[
5
,
36
]
, Hadamard diagonalizable graphs
[

,

]
, and quotient graphs
[

,
16
]

[

]
, Christandl et al. used Cartesian products to construct graphs with PST over long distances. PST was also studied under joins
[

[

,
30
]
and other graph products
[

Since PST in simple unweighted graphs is rare
[
20
]
, the notion of

(PGST) was introduced
[

,

]
. A graph

\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}U\left(\tau_{k}\right)=\gamma I,~\text{for some }\gamma\in{\mathbbm{C}},

where

[

,

,

]
, double stars
[

]
and circulant graphs are known to exhibit PGST
[

.

Our goal in this paper is to systematically study quantum state transfer on blow-up graphs. Here, we consider the more familiar type of blow-up where every vertex of a graph is replaced by an independent set. This variant of the blow-up operation is a special case of the lexicographic product. The
lexicographic product

G[H]
of
G
and
H
is the graph
G[H]
obtained by replacing every vertex of
G
by a copy of
H
, and adding all possible edges between the vertices in the copies of
H
corresponding to adjacent vertices in
G
. In
[
16
]
, Ge at al. provided sufficient conditions for PST to occur in lexicographic products. Their results about PST in

require that
H
exhibits PST. But in our case,
H
is an empty graph which cannot exhibit PST, and so their results are not applicable. This is our main motivation for studying quantum state transfer on blow-up graphs. Another motivation is to explore a graph operation that induces strong cospectrality between many pairs of vertices. Strong cospectrality is an important property because it is a requirement PGST. It turns out that if
0
is not in the eigenvalue support of vertex

in
G
, then the two copies of

are strongly cospectral in
\overset{2}{\uplus}~G
(see Theorem
2

\overset{2}{\uplus}~G
pair up to exhibit strong cospectrality whenever

has an invertible adjacency matrix (see Corollary
1
). Thus, blow-ups have the advantage of producing pairs of vertices that are promising for high probability state transfer. Lastly, the two copies of a vertex in
G
are twins in
\overset{2}{\uplus}~G
. This allows us to utilize some of the recent results on quantum state transfer between twins
[
22
,
25
,
26
,
35
]

-
5

-
10

only occurs between vertices at distance two, one may use the Cartesian product operation to obtain relatively sparse graphs that admit PST at large distances (Section

Transition matrix

Let
G
be a graph on
m
vertices with adjacency matrix
A.
We refer to the eigenvalues of
A
as the the eigenvalues of
G.
Suppose
J_{n}
is the square matrix of order
n\times n
with all entries equal to
1
. Considering the lexicographic ordering on
{\mathbbm{Z}}_{n}\times V(G),
the adjacency matrix of
\overset{n}{\uplus}~G
is evaluated as
A_{n}=J_{n}\otimes A,
which is the tensor product of the matrices
J_{n}
and
A
. Let
1
be the vector of length
n
with all entries equal to
1
, and let
\lambda
be an eigenvalue of
A
associated to the eigenvector
{\bf x}.
Then

\left(J_{n}\otimes A\right)\left(\textbf{1}\otimes{\bf x}\right)=J_{n}\textbf{1}\otimes A{\bf x}=n\lambda\left(\textbf{1}\otimes{\bf x}\right).

Let
\sigma(G)=\{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{d}\}
denote the distinct eigenvalues of
A
, and
E_{j}
be the idempotent associated with
\lambda_{j}
. The
eigenvalue support
of a vertex
u
in
G
is the set

\sigma_{u}(G)=\left\{\lambda\in\sigma(G)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}E_{\lambda}{\bf e}_{u}\neq\mathbf{0}\right\}.

In case
0\in\sigma\left(G\right),
we assume
\lambda_{d}=0
. Then the idempotents of
A_{n}
corresponding to the eigenvalues
n\lambda_{1},n\lambda_{2},\ldots,n\lambda_{d-1}
and
0
are respectively given by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}J_{n}\otimes E_{1},\ \ \frac{1}{n}J_{n}\otimes E_{2},\ \ \ldots,\ \ \frac{1}{n}J_{n}\otimes E_{d-1},\ \ I_{mn}-\frac{1}{n}J_{n}\otimes\left(I_{m}-E_{d}\right).

(1)

Consequently, the spectral decomposition of the transition matrix of
\overset{n}{\uplus}~G
becomes

\displaystyle U_{n}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp{\left(-itA_{n}\right)}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d-1}\exp{\left(-in\lambda_{j}t\right)}\cdot\frac{1}{n}J_{n}\otimes E_{j}+\left[I_{mn}-\frac{1}{n}J_{n}\otimes\left(I_{m}-E_{d}\right)\right].

(2)

For
(0,u),(1,u)\in{\mathbbm{Z}}_{n}\times V(G),
one can then check that

\displaystyle\hskip-36.135pt{\bf e}^{T}_{(0,u)}U_{n}(t){\bf e}_{(1,u)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}\left[\exp{\left(-in\lambda_{j}t\right)}-1\right](E_{j})_{u,u}.

(3)

If
0
not an eigenvalue of
G,
then we get the same set of idempotents for
A_{n}
in (
1
), except for the
d
th matrix which is now given by
I_{mn}-\frac{1}{n}J_{n}\otimes I_{m}
. Thus, the spectral decomposition of the transition matrix of
\overset{n}{\uplus}~G
can be evaluated as follows

U_{n}(t)=\exp{\left(-itA_{n}\right)}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}\exp{\left(-in\lambda_{j}t\right)}\cdot\frac{1}{n}J_{n}\otimes E_{j}+\left[I_{mn}-\frac{1}{n}J_{n}\otimes I_{m}\right].

Thus, we get an expression for
{\bf e}^{T}_{(0,u)}U_{n}(t){\bf e}_{(1,u)}
similar to (
3
). Next, we state an observation about the spectra and eigenvalue supports in blow-ups (see also
[
28
]
).

Proposition 2
.

Let
n\geq 2
. Then
\sigma\left(\overset{n}{\uplus}~G\right)=n\cdot\sigma\left(G\right)\cup\left\{0\right\}
and
\sigma_{(j,v)}\left(\overset{n}{\uplus}~G\right)=n\cdot\sigma_{v}(G)\cup\left\{0\right\}
for any vertex
u
of
G
and for all
j\in{\mathbbm{Z}}_{n}
.

In
[
12
, Lemma 3.3]
, the authors provided the form of transition matrices of graphs whose adjacency matrices look like
B\otimes M+C\otimes N
, where
(B,C)
and
(M,N)
are pairs of commuting matrices. This include blow-up graphs, since we may write

We also mention that Monterde investigated sedentariness, a type of low probability quantum transport, on several variants of the blow-up operation
[
27
]
. Apart from the work of Ge et al.
[
16
]
and Monterde, we are unaware of other results about quantum state transfer on blow-up graphs.

Strong cospectrality

Two vertices
u
and
v
in a graph
G
are said to be
strongly cospectral
if for every
\lambda\in\sigma_{u}(G)
,

E_{\lambda}\textbf{e}_{u}=\pm E_{\lambda}\textbf{e}_{v}.

Strong cospectrality is a necessary condition for PGST to occur between two vertices
[
19
, Lemma 13.1]
. This motivates us to characterize strong cospectrality in blow-up graphs.

Theorem 2
.

Let
G
be a graph with vertex
u
. The following hold.

1.

If
n\geq 3
, then
\overset{n}{\uplus}~G
does not exhibit strong cospectrality.

2.

Let
n=2
. Then
(0,u)
and
(1,u)
are strongly cospectral in
\overset{2}{\uplus}~G
if and only if
0\notin\sigma_{u}(G)
. Moreover,
(0,u)
can only be strongly cospectral with
(1,u)
in
\overset{2}{\uplus}~G
.

Proof.

We prove 1. If
n\geq 3
, then
|T_{u}|\geq 3
in
\overset{n}{\uplus}~G
for every vertex
u
of
G
. Invoking
[
25
, Corollary 3.10]
yields the desired conclusion. To prove 2, note that the
d-1
idempotents of
A_{2}
in (
1
) satisfy
\left(\frac{1}{2}J_{2}\otimes E_{j}\right)(\textbf{e}_{0}\otimes\textbf{e}_{u})=\frac{1}{2}\textbf{1}\otimes E_{j}\textbf{e}_{u}=\left(\frac{1}{n}J_{2}\otimes E_{j}\right)(\textbf{e}_{1}\otimes\textbf{e}_{u}).

Let
E
denoted the
d
th idempotent of
A_{2}
in (
1
). Note that
E(\textbf{e}_{0}\otimes\textbf{e}_{u})=\left(\textbf{e}_{0}-\frac{1}{2}\textbf{1}\right)\otimes\textbf{e}_{u}+\left(\frac{1}{2}\textbf{1}\otimes E_{d}\textbf{e}_{u}\right)
, and so we have
E(\textbf{e}_{0}\otimes\textbf{e}_{u})\neq E(\textbf{e}_{1}\otimes\textbf{e}_{u})
. However, one checks that
E(\textbf{e}_{0}\otimes\textbf{e}_{u})=-E(\textbf{e}_{1}\otimes\textbf{e}_{u})
if and only if
n=2
and
0\notin\sigma_{u}(G)
.
This proves the first statement in 2, while the second statement follows directly from
[
25

6
Periodicity

Before we move on to the next section, we include results on periodicity of vertices in blow-up graphs. Similar to strong cospectrality, periodicity is a requirement for two vertices to admit PST. The following is straightforward from
[
20
, Theorem 6.1]
.

Theorem 5
.

Let
n\in{\mathbbm{N}}.
Then
\overset{n}{\uplus}~G
is periodic at all vertices in
S_{u}=\left\{(j,u)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}j\in{\mathbbm{Z}}_{n}\right\}
if and only if the elements in
\sigma_{u}(G)
are either all integers or all integer multiples of
\sqrt{\Delta}
, where
\Delta>1
is a square-free integer. Moreover, periodicity of vertices in
S_{u}
in
\overset{n}{\uplus}~G
implies periodicity of
u
in
G
, and the time at which vertices in
S_{u}
are periodic in
\overset{n}{\uplus}~G
is equal to
\frac{\tau}{n}
, where
\tau
is the time at which
u
is periodic in
G
. Furthermore, if a vertex in
S_{u}
is periodic in
\overset{n}{\uplus}~G
for some
n,
then it is periodic in
\overset{n}{\uplus}~G
for all
n\in{\mathbbm{N}}.

From Theorem
5
, periodicity of vertices in
S_{u}
in
\overset{n}{\uplus}~G
implies periodicity of
u
in
G
. However, the converse of this does not hold whenever
u
is periodic in
G
and
\frac{1}{2}(a+b\sqrt{\Delta})\in\sigma_{u}(G)
for some integer
a\neq 0
and square-free integer
\Delta>1
. Theorem
5
also implies that the blow-up operation yields shorter times for periodicity than the original graph. We illustrate these two observations in the following example.

Example 3
.

Let
H
is a
k
-regular graph on
m
vertices. Let
G
be a graph resulting from
H
by adding a vertex
u
adjacent to all vertices of
H
. Then
\sigma_{u}(G)=\{\lambda_{\pm}\}
, where
\lambda_{\pm}=\frac{1}{2}(k\pm\sqrt{k^{2}+4m})
. Thus,
u
is periodic in
G
at
\tau=\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{k^{2}+4m}}
. Since
0\notin\sigma_{u}(G)
, Theorem
5
(2) implies that
\overset{n}{\uplus}~G
is periodic at all vertices in
S_{u}=\left\{(j,u)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}j\in{\mathbbm{Z}}_{n}\right\}
if and only if either

is a perfect square , or

(in which case

and

). In both cases, the vertices in
S_{u}
are periodic at

. Moreover, if
\overset{n}{\uplus}~G
is periodic at all vertices in

.

In Example
3

in

is not a perfect square, then each vertex in

is not periodic for all

. Thus, blow-ups need not preserve periodicity. The next result follows from

Proposition 3

If
G

then
\overset{n}{\uplus}~G

Complete graphs and cycles

We now start our investigation of PST and PGST on blow-ups of various classes of graphs.

Let

11
Cartesian products

PST is rare, and this fact motivates us to search for new graphs with PST. It is desirable for these graphs to be sparse, and for PST to occur between vertices that are far apart. For the case of blow-up graphs, PST in
\overset{2}{\uplus}~G
can only occur between vertices at distance two. Nonetheless, we may still utilize our results herein to illustrate the construction of relatively sparse graphs using blow-up graphs, with the property that PST occurs between vertices that are far apart. To do this, we make use of the Cartesian product operation.

Recall that the Cartesian product of graphs
G
and
H
, denoted
G\square H
, is the graph with adjacency matrix
A(G)\otimes I+I\otimes A(H)
. Thus,

U_{G\square H}(t)=U_{G}(t)\otimes U_{H}(t)

Consequently, PST occurs between
(u,x)
and
(v,y)
in
G\square H
whenever PST occurs between
u
and
v
in
G
and PST occurs between
x
and
y
in
H
at the same time. Combining this fact with
[
32
, Theorem 4.2]
yields the following observations.

Example 5
.

and

. Denote the hypercube of dimension

by
Q_{d}
, which is known to admit PST between vertices

that are at distance

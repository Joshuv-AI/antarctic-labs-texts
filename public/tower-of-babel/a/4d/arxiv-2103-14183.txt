Rapidly decaying Wigner functions are Schwartz functions
Felipe Hernández 1, C. Jess Riedel 2

Abstract
. We show that if the Wigner function of a (possibly mixed) quantum state decays toward infinity faster than any polynomial in the phase space variables x x and p p , then so do all of its derivatives, i.e., it is a Schwartz function on phase space. This is equivalent to the condition that the Husimi function is a Schwartz function, that the quantum state is a Schwartz operator in the sense of Keyl et al., and, in the case of a pure state, that the wavefunction is a Schwartz function on configuration space. We discuss the interpretation of this constraint on Wigner functions and provide explicit bounds on Schwartz seminorms.



1.
Introduction

In quantum mechanics, quantum states of
n
degrees of freedom can be represented by positive semidefinite trace-class operators on
L^{2}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.
Each quantum state
\rho
is associated with a kernel
{\mathcal{K}_{\rho}}
through
(\rho\phi)(x)=\int{\mathcal{K}_{\rho}}(x,y)\phi(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x
,
\phi\in L^{2}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
, and the corresponding
Wigner function

{\mathcal{W}_{\rho}}
is

{\mathcal{W}_{\rho}}(x,p):=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int e^{ip\cdot y}{\mathcal{K}_{\rho}}(x-y/2,x+y/2)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y.

We denote the set of all such Wigner function as
{\mathcal{V}}({\mathbf{R}}^{2n})
.
Our main result is a relationship between the decay of such Wigner functions and their
smoothness.

To quantify this we use the Schwartz-type seminorms

|F|_{a,b}

:=

\sup_{x,p}|x^{a_{\mathrm{x}}}p^{a_{\mathrm{p}}}\partial_{x}^{b_{\mathrm{x}}}\partial_{p}^{b_{\mathrm{x}}}F(x,p)|
of a function
F:{{\mathbf{R}}^{2n}}\to{\mathbf{C}}
on phase space, with multi-indices
a=(a_{\mathrm{x}},a_{\mathrm{p}}),b=(b_{\mathrm{x}},b_{\mathrm{p}})\in(\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\})^{\times 2n}.

With shorthand notation
|F|_{a}:=|F|_{a,0}
for the seminorms that only measure the decay of
F
, we say a function is
rapidly decaying
when
|F|_{a}<\infty
and is a
Schwartz function
when
|F|_{a,b}<\infty
for all
a,b
.
We denote the sets of rapidly decaying and Schwartz function by
{\mathcal{D}}({\mathbf{R}}^{2n})
and
{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbf{R}}^{2n})
, respectively. Our main result:

Theorem 1.1
.

If
\rho
is a positive semidefinite operator whose Wigner function
{\mathcal{W}_{\rho}}
exists and is rapidly decaying, then
{\mathcal{W}_{\rho}}
is a Schwartz function.

The assumed rapid decay of
{\mathcal{W}_{\rho}}
implies
\infty>\int{\mathcal{W}_{\rho}}(\alpha)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\alpha=\trace[\rho]
and hence that
\rho
is trace-class and so a quantum state. Thus the theorem can be rephrased as the set relation
{\mathcal{V}}({\mathbf{R}}^{2n})\cap{\mathcal{D}}({\mathbf{R}}^{2n})\subset{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbf{R}}^{2n})
.

In this paper we prove Theorem
1.1
in two different ways. The first proof is a bit more abstract, making use of the twisted convolution. The second proof is a bit more direct, using only basic objects, but requiring more computation. The second proof also results in an explicit bound on the Schwartz seminorms
|{\mathcal{W}_{\rho}}|_{a,b}
of a Wigner function in terms of only its decay seminorms
|{\mathcal{W}_{\rho}}|_{a}
(Theorem
3.9
).

In the rest of this introduction, we informally sketch the direct (second) proof of Theorem
1.1
in order to give the reader intuition, but we stop short of completing the computation. In the Sec.
2
, we recall some notation and basic properties around quantum mechanics in phase space, which can be skipped by experienced readers. In Sec.
3
we present our two proofs of our main result and exhibit explicit bounds on the Schwartz seminorms of a Wigner function in terms of its decay seminorms. In Section
4
we connect our results to the notion of Schwartz operators in the sense of Keyl et al.
[
8
]
, and in particular prove the equivalence of a large set of equivalent decay and regularity conditions for various representations of the quantum state. In Sec.
5
, we make some concluding remarks about the “overparameterization” of a quantum state by the Wigner function.

1.1.
Motivation

Why might one think the decay of a Wigner function constrains its derivatives? Consider a pure state
\rho=\mathinner{|{\psi}\rangle}\!\!\mathinner{\langle{\psi}|}
with
\mathinner{|{\psi}\rangle}\in L^{2}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
. We can see from the identity

|\widehat{\psi}(p)|^{2}=\int{\mathcal{W}_{\rho}}(x,p)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

(1.1)

that rapid decay (in both
x
and
p
) of the Wigner function implies decay (in
p
) of the Fourier transform
\widehat{\psi}
of the wavefunction. This implies that the wavefunction
\psi
is smooth:
|\psi|_{0,b}<\infty
for all
b\in(\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\})^{\times n}
. Unfortunately, a bit of trial and error suggests that it is not easy to generalize (
1.1
) and obtain a bound on the mixed Schwartz seminorms
|\psi|_{a,b}
(that is, to show that all the derivatives of
\psi
are not merely bounded but are also rapidly decaying).

A better way to approach Theorem
1.1
avoids privileging either the position or momentum variables by performing a wavepacket decomposition of the quantum state
\rho
. Using Gaussian wavepackets (coherent states), Zurek argued
[
12
]
that if the Wigner function
{\mathcal{W}_{\rho}}
of any quantum state is largely confined to a phase space region of volume
S\sim\ell_{\mathrm{x}}\times\ell_{\mathrm{p}}
, then the smallest structure it will develop is on scales of volume
\Delta s\sim(\hbar/\ell_{\mathrm{x}})\times(\hbar/\ell_{\mathrm{p}})\sim\hbar^{2}/S
.
This argument was further supported by numerical studies of “typical” states generated by chaotic quantum dynamics
[
12
]
.

1.2.
Sketch of direct proof

Consider a family of wavepackets
\chi_{\alpha}
of the form

\chi_{(\alpha_{\mathrm{x}},\alpha_{\mathrm{p}})}(x)=e^{i(x-\alpha_{x}/2)\cdot\alpha_{p}}\chi(x-\alpha_{x}),

(1.2)

for fixed smooth envelope function
\chi
concentrated near the origin. (For example,
\chi
can be chosen to be a Gaussian.) Given the spectral decomposition of a quantum state

\rho=\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}\mathinner{|{\psi_{j}}\rangle}\!\!\mathinner{\langle{\psi_{j}}|},

(1.3)

we can use the decomposition
\psi_{j}=(2\pi)^{-n}\int\mathinner{\langle{\chi_{\alpha}|\psi_{j}}\rangle}\chi_{\alpha}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\alpha
for each eigenfunction as an integral over phase space, which is a standard calculation proven in Lemma
2.9
. We can then express

\rho
as

\rho=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}\int\mathinner{|{\chi_{\alpha}}\rangle}\!\!\mathinner{\langle{\chi_{\beta}}|}\mathinner{\langle{\chi_{\alpha}|\psi_{j}}\rangle}\mathinner{\langle{\psi_{j}|\chi_{\beta}}\rangle}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\alpha\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\beta,

(1.4)

Applying the Wigner transform to both sides, this yields a decomposition

{\mathcal{W}_{\rho}}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int{\mathcal{W}_{\mathinner{|{\chi_{\alpha}}\rangle}\!\mathinner{\langle{\chi_{\beta}}|}}}\mathinner{\langle{\chi_{\alpha}|\rho|\chi_{\beta}}\rangle}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\alpha\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\beta.

(1.5)

in terms of the Wigner transform
{\mathcal{W}_{\mathinner{|{\chi_{\alpha}}\rangle}\!\mathinner{\langle{\chi_{\beta}}|}}}
of the “off-diagonal” operator
\mathinner{|{\chi_{\alpha}}\rangle}\!\!\mathinner{\langle{\chi_{\beta}}|}
. Although
{\mathcal{W}_{\mathinner{|{\chi_{\alpha}}\rangle}\!\mathinner{\langle{\chi_{\beta}}|}}}
is not a Wigner function (because
\mathinner{|{\chi_{\alpha}}\rangle}\!\!\mathinner{\langle{\chi_{\beta}}|}
is not positive semidefinite for
\alpha\neq\beta
), it is known
[
12
,
11
]
to be localized near the
phase space point
(\alpha+\beta)/2
and has an oscillation with frequency roughly
|\alpha-\beta|
.

Since
\mathinner{\langle{\chi_{\alpha}|\rho|\chi_{\alpha}}\rangle}
is just a convolution of the Wigner function
{\mathcal{W}_{\rho}}
, the rapid decay of
{\mathcal{W}_{\rho}}
implies the rapid decay of
\mathinner{\langle{\chi_{\alpha}|\rho|\chi_{\alpha}}\rangle}
, and then in turn one can show the rapid decay of
\mathinner{\langle{\chi_{\alpha}|\rho|\chi_{\beta}}\rangle}
using the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality:

\mathinner{\langle{\chi_{\alpha}|\rho|\chi_{\beta}}\rangle}^{2}\leq\mathinner{\langle{\chi_{\alpha}|\rho|\chi_{\alpha}}\rangle}\mathinner{\langle{\chi_{\beta}|\rho|\chi_{\beta}}\rangle},

(1.6)

which holds because
\rho
is positive semidefinite. The assumed decay and smoothness properties of
\chi
additionally give an estimate of the form

|{\mathcal{W}_{\mathinner{|{\chi_{\alpha}}\rangle}\!\mathinner{\langle{\chi_{\beta}}|}}}|_{a,b}\leq C(a,b)(1+|\alpha|+|\beta|)^{D(a,b)}.

(1.7)

When combined with decomposition (
1.5
) of
{\mathcal{W}_{\rho}}
, this is enough to show that all the Schwartz seminorms
|{\mathcal{W}_{\rho}}|_{a,b}
are finite.

Our other proof requires additional machinery but still rests heavily on wavepacket decompositions of the quantum state and on the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality (
1.6
).

2.
Preliminaries

This section establishes our notation and reviews standard features of phase-space representations of quantum mechanics. (To keep this paper self-contained, we provide proofs of the lemmas in this section in the Appendix.) Throughout this
paper, we take
\chi\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
to be a fixed Schwartz function that is normalized,
\|\chi\|_{L^{2}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})}=\int|\chi(y)|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y=1
, but otherwise arbitrary.
1
1

1

A standard choice is to specialize to a Gaussian coherent state
\chi(y)=\exp(-x^{2}/2)/\sqrt{(2\pi)^{n}}
(especially when used as in Subsection
2.2
as the reference wavefunction with respect to which Husimi function is defined). However, this specialization is not necessary and one could instead take
\chi
to be, e.g., a smooth and compactly supported wavefunction.

Experienced readers may prefer to skip directly to Sec.
3
for the proof of our main result and only refer back to this section as necessary.

2.1.
Notation

In what follows, a
wavefunction
of
n
continuous quantum degrees of freedom is represented by a member of
L^{2}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
and denoted by
\psi
,
\phi
, or
\chi
. A
quantum state
is the possibly mixed generalization, represented by a positive semidefinite (and hence self-adjoint) trace-class operator on
L^{2}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
and denoted by
\rho
or
\eta
.
Vectors on phase space are
\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\xi\in{{\mathbf{R}}^{2n}}
with position and momentum components denoted by (for example)
\alpha_{\mathrm{x}},\xi_{\mathrm{p}}\in{\mathbf{R}}^{n}
. Multi-indices are
a,b,c,d\in(\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\})^{\times 2n}
(or (
\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\})^{\times n}
in Sec.
4
) with

\alpha^{b}=\alpha_{\mathrm{x}}^{b_{\mathrm{x}}}\alpha_{\mathrm{p}}^{b_{\mathrm{p}}}=\prod_{i=1}^{2n}\alpha_{i}^{b_{i}}
,

|b|=|b_{\mathrm{x}}|+|b_{\mathrm{p}}|=\sum_{i=1}^{2n}b_{i}
,

b!=b_{\mathrm{x}}!b_{\mathrm{p}}!=\prod_{i=1}^{2n}b_{i}!
,
and
\binom{a}{b}=a!/((a-b)!b!)
. We use
b\leq c
to mean
b_{i}\leq c_{i}
for all
i=1,2,\ldots 2n
.

The symplectic form is
\alpha\wedge\beta=\alpha\cdot\Omega\cdot\beta=\alpha_{\mathrm{x}}\cdot\beta_{\mathrm{p}}-\alpha_{\mathrm{p}}\cdot\beta_{\mathrm{x}}
, with
\Omega=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&I\\
-I&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)
an antisymmetric matrix on
{\mathbf{R}}^{2n}
,
I
the identity matrix on
{\mathbf{R}}^{n}
, and “
\cdot
” the dot product on
{\mathbf{R}}^{n}
and
{\mathbf{R}}^{2n}
. The position and momentum operators are
X=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
and
P=(P_{1},\ldots,P_{n})
, which are combined into the phase-space operator
R=(X,P)
. For a given quantum state
\rho
and reference wavefunction
\chi\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
, some associated functions over phase space, doubled phase space, and doubled configuration space are
{\mathcal{W}_{\rho}}
,
{\mathcal{Q}^{\chi}_{\rho}}
,
{\mathcal{M}^{\chi}_{\rho}}
,
{\mathcal{F}_{\rho}}
, and
{\mathcal{K}_{\rho}}
(defined below). We use “
*
” to denote the convolution,
(f*g)(\alpha)=\int f(\alpha-\beta)g(\beta)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\beta
.
Given a matrix form
\Omega^{\prime}
we also define the twisted convolution

(f\circledast_{\Omega^{\prime}}g)(\alpha)=\int e^{i\alpha\cdot\Omega^{\prime}\cdot\beta/2}f(\alpha-\beta)g(\beta)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\beta.

(2.1)

For any wavefunction
\phi\in L^{2}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
, we denote the linear functional associated with it using bra notation,
\mathinner{\langle{\phi}|}=(\psi\mapsto\int\bar{\phi}(x)\psi(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x)\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
, and denote the scalar result with a bra-ket,
\mathinner{\langle{\phi|\psi}\rangle}=\mathinner{\langle{\phi}|}(\psi)=\int\bar{\phi}(x)\psi(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x
.
More generally, with an operator
E
we write
\mathinner{\langle{\phi|E|\psi}\rangle}=\mathinner{\langle{\phi}|}(E\psi)=\mathinner{\langle{E^{\dagger}\phi}|}(\psi)
. For any two wavefunction
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\in L^{2}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
, we use
\mathinner{|{\phi_{1}}\rangle}\!\!\mathinner{\langle{\phi_{2}}|}
for the rank-1 operator
\psi\mapsto\mathinner{\langle{\phi_{2}|\psi}\rangle}\phi_{1}
.

2.2.
The displacement operator and phase-space functions

In this subsection we recall standard results about quantum mechanics in phase space (see, e.g., Chapter 1 of Ref.
[
6
]
).

Definition 2.1
.

For
\xi\in{{\mathbf{R}}^{2n}}
, define the
(Weyl generator) displacement operator

D_{\xi}:=e^{i\xi\wedge R}=e^{i(\xi_{\mathrm{x}}\cdot P-\xi_{\mathrm{p}}\cdot X)}.

(2.2)

The following lemma describes the action of
D_{\xi}

on an arbitrary wavefunction.

Lemma 2.2
.

For any
\phi\in L^{2}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
,

D_{\xi}\phi(y)=e^{i(y-\xi_{\mathrm{x}}/2)\cdot\xi_{\mathrm{p}}}\phi(y-\xi_{\mathrm{x}}).

(2.3)

It’s easy to check these basic properties:
D_{\alpha}D_{\beta}=e^{i\beta\wedge\alpha/2}D_{\alpha+\beta}
and
D_{\alpha}^{\dagger}=D_{-\alpha}
.

Now we introduce the quasicharacteristic function, the Wigner function, and the Kernel.

Definition 2.3
.

For a given quantum state
\rho
, the
quasicharacteristic function
is

{\mathcal{F}_{\rho}}(\xi):=\trace[\rho D_{\xi}].

(2.4)

where the trace is well defined because
\rho
is trace-class and
D_{\xi}
is a bounded operator on
L^{2}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.
Because a quantum state
\rho
is necessarily compact, it has a spectral decomposition
[
7
]

\begin{split}(\rho\phi)(x)&=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\psi_{i}(x)\mathinner{\langle{\psi_{i}|\phi}\rangle}\end{split}

(2.5)

with unnormalized eigenvectors
\psi_{i}\in L^{2}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
and associated kernel
{\mathcal{K}_{\rho}}
satisfying
(\rho\phi)(x)=\int{\mathcal{K}_{\rho}}(x,y)\phi(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x
and

{\mathcal{K}_{\rho}}(x,y)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\psi_{i}(x)\bar{\psi}_{i}(y)

(2.6)

almost everywhere.
Finally, we define the
Wigner function
of
\rho
as

{\mathcal{W}_{\rho}}(\alpha):=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int e^{i\alpha_{\mathrm{p}}\cdot y}{\mathcal{K}_{\rho}}(\alpha_{\mathrm{x}}-y/2,\alpha_{\mathrm{x}}+y/2)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y,

(2.7)

where
{\mathcal{W}_{\rho}}\in L^{2}({\mathbf{R}}^{2n})
because it is a Fourier transform of
{\mathcal{K}_{\rho}}\in L^{2}({\mathbf{R}}^{2n})
in one variable.

More generally, we call
{\mathcal{W}_{E}}(\alpha):=(2\pi)^{-n}\int e^{i\alpha_{\mathrm{p}}\cdot y}{\mathcal{K}_{E}}(\alpha_{\mathrm{x}}-y/2,\alpha_{\mathrm{x}}+y/2)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y
and
{\mathcal{F}_{E}}(\xi):=\trace[ED_{\xi}]
the
Wigner transform
and
quasicharacteristic transform
of any kernel operator
E
, which in particular exists for any rank-1 operator
E=\mathinner{|{\phi}\rangle}\!\!\mathinner{\langle{\psi}|}
since
{\mathcal{K}_{\mathinner{|{\phi}\rangle}\!\mathinner{\langle{\psi}|}}}\in L^{2}({\mathbf{R}}^{2n})
.

Lemma 2.4
.

For any trace-class kernel operator
E
, the corresponding Wigner transform and quasicharacteristic transform are symplectic Fourier duals:

{\mathcal{W}_{E}}(\alpha)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2n}}\int e^{-i\alpha\wedge\xi}{\mathcal{F}_{E}}(\xi)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi.

(2.8)

The preceding expression is sometimes used as the definition of the Wigner transform, and it is notable for manifestly respecting the symplectic structure of phase space. The perhaps more traditional definition (
2.7
) relies on the kernel, and hence privileges position over momentum, but has the advantage of being more obviously well-defined.

Lemma 2.5
.

The Wigner function
{\mathcal{W}_{\rho}}
is a Schwartz function if and only if the kernel
{\mathcal{K}_{\rho}}
is a Schwartz function.

Roughly speaking, this is because

{\mathcal{W}_{\rho}}
and
{\mathcal{K}_{\rho}}
are Fourier transforms of each other in
one
of their two variables (after the linear change of variables
(x,y)\to(\bar{x}=(x+y)/2,\Delta x=x-y)
).

Lemma 2.6
.

The twisted convolution of a rapidly decaying function with a Schwartz function is itself a Schwartz function.

The proof is essentially the same as for the similar statement with the normal convolution.

Lemma 2.7
.

For any two quantum states
\rho
and
\eta
,

\displaystyle\trace[\rho\eta]=(2\pi)^{n}\int{\mathcal{W}_{\rho}}(\alpha){\mathcal{W}_{\eta}}(\alpha)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\alpha.

(2.9)

Now we introduce the Husimi function and the so-called matrix element; these are most often defined with respect to a preferred Gaussian reference wavefunction, but we will allow more generality (see, e.g., Ref.
[
9
]
).

Definition 2.8
.

Fixing a reference wavefunction
\chi\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
that is normalized (
\|\chi\|_{L^{2}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})}=\int|\chi(y)|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y=1
), and Schwartz-class but otherwise arbitrary, we define the
matrix element

{\mathcal{M}^{\chi}_{\rho}}(\alpha,\beta):=\mathinner{\langle{\chi_{\alpha}|\rho|\chi_{\beta}}\rangle},

(2.10)

and the
Husimi function

{\mathcal{Q}^{\chi}_{\rho}}(\alpha):=\mathinner{\langle{\chi_{\alpha}|\rho|\chi_{\alpha}}\rangle}={\mathcal{M}^{\chi}_{\rho}}(\alpha,\alpha).

(2.11)

using the shorthand
\mathinner{|{\chi_{\alpha}}\rangle}:=D_{\alpha}\mathinner{|{\chi}\rangle}
.

Lemma 2.9
.

For any trace-class operator
E
and any
\chi\in L^{2}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
satisfying
\|\chi\|_{L^{2}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})}=1
,

\displaystyle\trace[E]=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int\mathinner{\langle{\chi_{\alpha}|E|\chi_{\alpha}}\rangle}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\alpha

(2.12)

In particular, for any
\phi,\psi\in L^{2}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

\displaystyle\mathinner{\langle{\phi|\psi}\rangle}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int\mathinner{\langle{\phi|\chi_{\alpha}}\rangle}\mathinner{\langle{\chi_{\alpha}|\psi}\rangle}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\alpha

(2.13)

Lemma 2.10
.

For any quantum state
\rho
and reference wavefunction
\chi\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Q}^{\chi}_{\rho}}(\alpha)

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{n}({\mathcal{W}_{\rho}}\ast{\mathcal{W}_{\chi}^{-}})(\alpha)=(2\pi)^{n}\int{\mathcal{W}_{\rho}}(\beta){\mathcal{W}_{\chi}}(\beta-\alpha)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\beta

(2.14)

where
{{\mathcal{W}_{\chi}^{-}}}(\alpha):={\mathcal{W}_{\chi}}(-\alpha)
is a Schwartz function.

3.
Proof that rapidly decaying Wigner functions are Schwartz function

The first (more abstract) proof of our main result is given in subsection
3.1
below. The second (more direct) proof follows in subsection
3.2
. These two subsections are independent of each other and can be read in either order.

Both proofs will make crucial use of the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality in the following form:

Lemma 3.1
.

For any quantum state
\rho
, the Husimi function bounds the matrix element:

|{\mathcal{M}^{\chi}_{\rho}}(\alpha,\beta)|^{2}\leq{\mathcal{Q}^{\chi}_{\rho}}(\alpha){\mathcal{Q}^{\chi}_{\rho}}(\beta)

(3.1)

Proof.

We have

|{\mathcal{M}^{\chi}_{\rho}}(\alpha,\beta)|^{2}=|\mathinner{\langle{\chi_{\alpha}|\rho|\chi_{\beta}}\rangle}|^{2}\leq\mathinner{\langle{\chi_{\alpha}|\rho|\chi_{\alpha}}\rangle}\mathinner{\langle{\chi_{\beta}|\rho|\chi_{\beta}}\rangle}={\mathcal{Q}^{\chi}_{\rho}}(\alpha){\mathcal{Q}^{\chi}_{\rho}}(\beta)

(3.2)

where the inequality is just the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality
applied to the inner product
\langle\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\rangle_{\rho}:=\mathinner{\langle{\phi_{1}|\rho|\phi_{2}}\rangle}
.
∎

Corollary 3.2
.

If the Wigner function
{\mathcal{W}_{\rho}}
of a quantum state
\rho
is rapidly decaying, then the Husimi function
{\mathcal{Q}^{\chi}_{\rho}}
and the matrix element
{\mathcal{M}^{\chi}_{\rho}}
are also rapidly decaying.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.10
, the Husimi function
{\mathcal{Q}^{\chi}_{\rho}}
is a convolution of the rapidly decaying
{\mathcal{W}_{\rho}}
by the Schwartz function
{\mathcal{W}_{\chi}^{-}}(\alpha)={\mathcal{W}_{\chi}}(-\alpha)
, so
{\mathcal{Q}^{\chi}_{\rho}}
must also be rapidly decaying. We then get rapid decay of
{\mathcal{M}^{\chi}_{\rho}}
using Lemma
3.1
.
∎

We now turn to the first strategy.

3.1.
Abstract proof

Here is a sketch of our strategy: We obtain a reproducing formula expressing
{\mathcal{M}^{\chi}_{\rho}}
as a twisted convolution of itself with a Schwartz function constructed from
\chi
, showing that
{\mathcal{M}^{\chi}_{\rho}}
must itself be a Schwartz function. Then we find an integral expression for the Wigner function
{\mathcal{W}_{\rho}}
in terms of the matrix element
{\mathcal{M}^{\chi}_{\rho}}
, from which it follows that
{\mathcal{W}_{\rho}}
is a Schwartz function.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\Omega^{\prime}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\Omega&0\\
0&-\Omega\end{smallmatrix}\right)
be a symplectic form
on
{\mathbf{R}}^{4n}
. Then the matrix element
{\mathcal{M}^{\chi}_{\rho}}
satisfies the following reproducing formula

{\mathcal{M}^{\chi}_{\rho}}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2n}}({\mathcal{F}_{\chi}}\bar{\otimes}{\mathcal{F}_{\chi}})\circledast_{\Omega^{\prime}}{\mathcal{M}^{\chi}_{\rho}}

(3.3)

where
{\mathcal{F}_{\chi}}=\Tr[\mathinner{|{\chi}\rangle}\!\!\mathinner{\langle{\chi}|}D_{\xi}]=\mathinner{\langle{\chi|D_{\xi}|\chi}\rangle}=\mathinner{\langle{\chi_{-\xi/2}|\chi_{\xi/2}}\rangle}
is the quasicharacteristic function of the pure quantum state
\mathinner{|{\chi}\rangle}\!\!\mathinner{\langle{\chi}|}
and where
{\mathcal{F}_{\chi}}\bar{\otimes}{\mathcal{F}_{\chi}}:{\mathbf{R}}^{2n}\times{\mathbf{R}}^{2n}\to{\mathbf{C}}

is defined by

({\mathcal{F}_{\chi}}\bar{\otimes}{\mathcal{F}_{\chi}})(\xi,\omega):={\mathcal{F}_{\chi}}(\xi){\mathcal{F}_{\chi}}(-\omega).

(3.4)

Proof.

We have:

\begin{split}{\mathcal{M}^{\chi}_{\rho}}(\alpha,\beta)&=\mathinner{\langle{\chi_{\alpha}|\rho|\chi_{\beta}}\rangle}\\
&=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int\mathinner{\langle{\chi_{\alpha}|\chi_{\alpha^{\prime}}}\rangle}\mathinner{\langle{\chi_{\alpha^{\prime}}|\rho|\chi_{\beta}}\rangle}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\alpha^{\prime}\\
&=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2n}}\int\mathinner{\langle{\chi_{\alpha}|\chi_{\alpha^{\prime}}}\rangle}\mathinner{\langle{\chi_{\alpha^{\prime}}|\rho|\chi_{\beta^{\prime}}}\rangle}\mathinner{\langle{\chi_{\beta^{\prime}}|\chi_{\beta}}\rangle}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\alpha^{\prime}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\beta^{\prime}\\
&=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2n}}\int e^{i\alpha^{\prime}\wedge\alpha/2}{\mathcal{F}_{\chi}}(\alpha^{\prime}-\alpha){\mathcal{M}^{\chi}_{\rho}}(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}){\mathcal{F}_{\chi}}(\beta-\beta^{\prime})e^{i\beta\wedge\beta^{\prime}/2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\alpha^{\prime}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\beta^{\prime},\\
&=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2n}}(({\mathcal{F}_{\chi}}\bar{\otimes}{\mathcal{F}_{\chi}})\circledast_{\Omega^{\prime}}{\mathcal{M}^{\chi}_{\rho}})(\alpha,\beta)\end{split}

(3.5)

where to get the second line we use Eq. (
2.13
) of Lemma
2.9
for the inner product of
\mathinner{|{\chi_{\alpha}}\rangle},\rho\mathinner{|{\chi_{\beta}}\rangle}\in L^{2}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
and to get the third line we use the lemma again for the inner product of
\mathinner{|{\chi_{\beta}}\rangle}\in L^{2}({\mathbf{R}}^{n}),\rho\mathinner{|{\chi_{\alpha}^{\prime}}\rangle}\in L^{2}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.
The final line is just the definition of the twisted convolution with respect to the form
\Omega^{\prime}
.
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

If the matrix element
{\mathcal{M}^{\chi}_{\rho}}
is rapidly decaying, then it is a Schwartz function.

Proof.

First note that
\chi
and therefore
{\mathcal{K}_{\chi}}(x,y)=\chi(x)\bar{\chi}(y)
are Schwartz functions. By Lemma
2.5
this means
{\mathcal{W}_{\chi}}
is a Schwartz function, so therefore
{\mathcal{F}_{\chi}}
is a Schwartz function by Lemma
2.4
, meaning that
{\mathcal{F}_{\chi}}\bar{\otimes}{\mathcal{F}_{\chi}}
is a Schwartz function. Then by Lemma
3.3
,

{\mathcal{M}^{\chi}_{\rho}}
is the twisted convolution of a Schwartz function (
{\mathcal{F}_{\chi}}\bar{\otimes}{\mathcal{F}_{\chi}}
)
against a rapidly decaying function (
{\mathcal{M}^{\chi}_{\rho}}
, by assumption), and is therefore also Schwartz-class by Lemma
2.6
.
∎

We now deploy Lemma
2.4
to recover the Wigner function
{\mathcal{W}_{\rho}}
from the matrix element
{\mathcal{M}^{\chi}_{\rho}}
.

Lemma 3.5
.

For any quantum state
\rho
,

{\mathcal{W}_{\rho}}(\alpha)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3n}}\int e^{-i(\alpha-\beta/2)\wedge\xi}{\mathcal{M}^{\chi}_{\rho}}(\beta-\xi/2,\beta+\xi/2)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\beta.

(3.6)

Proof.

We have:

\begin{split}{\mathcal{W}_{\rho}}(\alpha)&=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2n}}\int e^{-i\alpha\wedge\xi}\trace[D_{\xi/2}\rho D_{\xi/2}]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi\\
&=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3n}}\int e^{-i\alpha\wedge\xi}\mathinner{\langle{\chi_{\beta}|D_{\xi/2}\rho D_{\xi/2}|\chi_{\beta}}\rangle}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\beta\\
&=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3n}}\int e^{-i\alpha\wedge\xi}\mathinner{\langle{\chi|D_{-\beta}D_{\xi/2}\rho D_{\xi/2}D_{\beta}|\chi}\rangle}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\beta\\
&=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3n}}\int e^{-i(\alpha-\beta/2)\wedge\xi}\mathinner{\langle{\chi|D_{\xi/2-\beta}\rho D_{\xi/2+\beta}|\chi}\rangle}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\beta\\
&=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3n}}\int e^{-i(\alpha-\beta/2)\wedge\xi}{\mathcal{M}^{\chi}_{\rho}}(\beta-\xi/2,\beta+\xi/2)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\beta,\end{split}

(3.7)

where for the first line we use Lemma
2.4
, for the second line we use the trace formula
from Lemma
2.9
, and for the fourth line we use the composition identity
D_{\alpha}D_{\beta}=e^{i\beta\wedge\alpha/2}D_{\alpha+\beta}
for the displacement operator.
∎

Corollary 3.6
.

For any quantum state
\rho
, if the matrix element
{\mathcal{M}^{\chi}_{\rho}}
is a Schwartz function, then
{\mathcal{W}_{\rho}}
is a Schwartz function.

Proof.

Lemma
3.5
shows that
{\mathcal{W}_{\rho}}
can be obtained from
{\mathcal{M}^{\chi}_{\rho}}
by (a) multiplying by the phase function
e^{i\beta\wedge\xi/2}
(quadratic in the variables
\beta
and
\xi
), (b) applying a symplectic Fourier transform (exchanging the variable
\xi
for the variable
\alpha
), and then (c) integrating over the variable
\beta
. All three of these operations preserve Schwartz-class functions, so
{\mathcal{W}_{\rho}}
is a Schwartz function.
∎

With all the hard work done, our main result follows easily.

Theorem
1.1
.

If
\rho
is a positive semidefinite operator whose Wigner function
{\mathcal{W}_{\rho}}
exists and is rapidly decaying, then
{\mathcal{W}_{\rho}}
is a Schwartz function.

Proof.

The rapid decay of
{\mathcal{W}_{\rho}}
means
\infty>\int{\mathcal{W}_{\rho}}(\alpha)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\alpha=\trace[\rho]
, so
\rho
is trace-class and hence a quantum state.
We then conclude that
{\mathcal{M}^{\chi}_{\rho}}
is rapidly decaying by Corollary
3.2
, so
{\mathcal{M}^{\chi}_{\rho}}
is a Schwartz function by Corollary
3.4
. Therefore,
{\mathcal{W}_{\rho}}
is a Schwartz function by Corollary
3.6
.
∎

This proof is constructive and so can in principle be used to derive effective bounds for the Schwartz seminorms for
{\mathcal{W}_{\rho}}
in terms of only the decay seminorms. However, computing the bounds through this method would be very laborious, so instead we use a more direct method in the next subsection.

3.2.
Direct proof

The strategy rests on showing that the Schwartz seminorms
of
{\mathcal{W}_{\mathinner{|{\chi_{\alpha}}\rangle}\!\mathinner{\langle{\chi_{\beta}}|}}}
depend
only polynomially on
\alpha
and
\beta
. In
this section, we will use for convenience the Schwartz
type norms

\|F\|_{a,b}:=\sum_{a^{\prime}\leq a}\sum_{b^{\prime}\leq b}|F|_{a^{\prime},b^{\prime}}

(3.8)

and the corresponding shorthand
\|F\|_{a}=\|F\|_{a,0}=\sum_{a^{\prime}\leq a}|F|_{a^{\prime},0}
.

Lemma 3.7
.

For any quantum state
\rho
and any reference wavefunction
\chi\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
,

{\mathcal{W}_{\rho}}(\gamma)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2n}}\int{\mathcal{W}_{\mathinner{|{\chi_{\alpha}}\rangle}\!\mathinner{\langle{\chi_{\beta}}|}}}(\gamma){\mathcal{M}^{\chi}_{\rho}}(\alpha,\beta)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\alpha\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\beta.

(3.9)

Proof.

Given the spectral decomposition
\rho=\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}\mathinner{|{\psi_{j}}\rangle}\!\!\mathinner{\langle{\psi_{j}}|}
we use Lemma
2.4
to get

\begin{split}{\mathcal{W}_{\rho}}(\gamma)&=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2n}}\int e^{-i\gamma\wedge\xi}\trace[\rho D_{\xi}]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi\\
&=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{4n}}\int e^{-i\gamma\wedge\xi}\mathinner{\langle{\chi_{\beta}|D_{\xi}|\chi_{\alpha}}\rangle}\mathinner{\langle{\chi_{\alpha}|\rho|\chi_{\beta}}\rangle}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\alpha\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\beta\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi\\
&=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{4n}}\int e^{-i\gamma\wedge\xi}{\mathcal{F}_{\mathinner{|{\chi_{\alpha}}\rangle}\!\mathinner{\langle{\chi_{\beta}}|}}}(\xi){\mathcal{M}^{\chi}_{\rho}}(\alpha,\beta)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\alpha\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\beta\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi\end{split}

(3.10)

where to get the second line we apply Lemma
2.9
to the trace-class operator
\rho D_{\xi}
twice. Using Lemma
2.4
yields (
3.9
).
∎

Lemma 3.8
.

The Schwartz seminorms of the Wigner transform of the off-diagonal operator
\mathinner{|{\chi_{\alpha}}\rangle}\!\!\mathinner{\langle{\chi_{\beta}}|}
obey

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{W}_{\mathinner{|{\chi_{\alpha}}\rangle}\!\mathinner{\langle{\chi_{\beta}}|}}}|_{a,b}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{c\leq b}\sum_{d\leq a}\sum_{e\leq c}\sum_{f\leq d}\binom{b}{c}\binom{a}{d}\binom{c}{e}\binom{d}{f}2^{-|d|}|{\mathcal{W}_{\chi}}|_{a-d,b-c}|\alpha^{\hat{e}+f}\beta^{\hat{c}-\hat{e}+d-f}|

(3.11)

\displaystyle\leq 4^{|a|+|b|}(1+|\alpha|+|\beta|)^{|a|+|b|}\|{\mathcal{W}_{\chi}}\|_{a,b},

(3.12)

where we use a hat to swap the position and momentum components of a multi-index:
\hat{a}=\widehat{(a_{\mathrm{x}},a_{\mathrm{p}})}:=(a_{\mathrm{p}},a_{\mathrm{x}})
.

Proof.

First note that

\begin{split}{\mathcal{F}_{\mathinner{|{\chi_{\alpha}}\rangle}\!\mathinner{\langle{\chi_{\beta}}|}}}(\xi)&=\trace[\mathinner{|{\chi_{\alpha}}\rangle}\!\!\mathinner{\langle{\chi_{\beta}}|}D_{\xi}]=\mathinner{\langle{\chi|D_{-\beta}D_{\xi}D_{\alpha}|\chi}\rangle}\\
&=e^{i(\alpha+\beta)\wedge\xi/2+i\beta\wedge\alpha/2}\mathinner{\langle{\chi|D_{\xi+\alpha-\beta}|\chi}\rangle}=e^{i\bar{\alpha}\wedge\xi+i\bar{\alpha}\wedge\Delta\alpha/2}{\mathcal{F}_{\chi}}(\xi+\Delta\alpha)\end{split}

(3.13)

where in the last line we introduced the shorthand
\bar{\alpha}=(\alpha+\beta)/2
and
\Delta\alpha=\alpha-\beta
. Then
{\mathcal{W}_{\mathinner{|{\chi_{\alpha}}\rangle}\!\mathinner{\langle{\chi_{\beta}}|}}}
is related to
{\mathcal{W}_{\chi}}
by

\begin{split}{\mathcal{W}_{\mathinner{|{\chi_{\alpha}}\rangle}\!\mathinner{\langle{\chi_{\beta}}|}}}(\gamma)&=\int e^{-i\gamma\wedge\xi}{\mathcal{F}_{\mathinner{|{\chi_{\alpha}}\rangle}\!\mathinner{\langle{\chi_{\beta}}|}}}(\xi)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi\\
&=\int e^{i(\bar{\alpha}-\gamma)\wedge\xi+i\bar{\alpha}\wedge\Delta\alpha/2}{\mathcal{F}_{\chi}}(\xi+\Delta\alpha)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi\\
&=\int e^{i(\bar{\alpha}-\gamma)\wedge(\xi-\Delta\alpha)+i\bar{\alpha}\wedge\Delta\alpha/2}{\mathcal{F}_{\chi}}(\xi)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi\\
&=e^{i(\gamma-\bar{\alpha}/2)\wedge\Delta\alpha}{\mathcal{W}_{\chi}}(\gamma-\bar{\alpha}),\end{split}

(3.14)

so

\begin{split}\gamma^{a}\partial_{\gamma}^{b}{\mathcal{W}_{\mathinner{|{\chi_{\alpha}}\rangle}\!\mathinner{\langle{\chi_{\beta}}|}}}(\gamma)&=\gamma^{a}\partial_{\gamma}^{b}e^{i(\gamma-\bar{\alpha}/2)\wedge\Delta\alpha}{\mathcal{W}_{\chi}}(\gamma-\bar{\alpha})\\
&=\sum_{c\leq b}\binom{b}{c}e^{i(\gamma-\bar{\alpha}/2)\wedge\Delta\alpha}(i\Omega\cdot\Delta\alpha)^{c}((\gamma-\bar{\alpha})+\bar{\alpha})^{a}\partial_{\gamma}^{b-c}{\mathcal{W}_{\chi}}(\gamma-\bar{\alpha})\end{split}

(3.15)

where in the second line we used

\begin{split}\partial_{\gamma}^{b}e^{i\gamma\wedge\xi}=\partial_{\gamma_{\mathrm{x}}}^{b_{\mathrm{x}}}\partial_{\gamma_{\mathrm{p}}}^{b_{\mathrm{p}}}e^{i\gamma_{\mathrm{x}}\cdot\xi_{\mathrm{p}}-i\gamma_{\mathrm{p}}\cdot\xi_{\mathrm{x}}}=(i\xi_{\mathrm{p}})^{b_{\mathrm{x}}}(-i\xi_{\mathrm{x}})^{b_{\mathrm{p}}}e^{i\gamma_{\mathrm{x}}\cdot\xi_{\mathrm{p}}-i\gamma_{\mathrm{p}}\cdot\xi_{\mathrm{x}}}=(i\Omega\cdot\xi)^{b}e^{i\gamma\wedge\xi}\end{split}

(3.16)

Then, using
|(i\Omega\cdot\xi)^{b}|=|\xi_{\mathrm{p}}^{b_{\mathrm{x}}}\xi_{\mathrm{x}}^{b_{\mathrm{p}}}|=|\xi^{\hat{b}}|
, it follows that

\begin{split}|{\mathcal{W}_{\mathinner{|{\chi_{\alpha}}\rangle}\!\mathinner{\langle{\chi_{\beta}}|}}}|_{a,b}&\leq\sum_{c\leq b}\sum_{d\leq a}\binom{b}{c}\binom{a}{d}|{\mathcal{W}_{\chi}}|_{a-d,b-c}|\Delta\alpha^{\hat{c}}\bar{\alpha}^{d}|\\
&\leq\sum_{c\leq b}\sum_{d\leq a}\sum_{e\leq c}\sum_{f\leq d}\binom{b}{c}\binom{a}{d}\binom{c}{e}\binom{d}{f}2^{-|d|}|{\mathcal{W}_{\chi}}|_{a-d,b-c}|\alpha^{\hat{e}+f}\beta^{\hat{c}-\hat{e}+d-f}|\\
&\leq 4^{|a|+|b|}(1+|\alpha|+|\beta|)^{|a|+|b|}\|{\mathcal{W}_{\chi}}\|_{a,b}.\end{split}

(3.17)

To get the third line, we bound the terms in the sum on the second line using
2^{-|d|}\leq 1
,
|{\mathcal{W}_{\chi}}|_{a-d,b-c}\leq\|{\mathcal{W}_{\chi}}\|_{a,b}
, and
|\alpha^{\hat{e}+f}\beta^{\hat{c}-\hat{e}+d-f}|\leq(|1+|\alpha|+|\beta|)^{|a|+|b|}
and then sum the binomial coefficients.
∎

Theorem 3.9
.

For any quantum state
\rho
and reference state
\chi\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
, the following inequality holds:

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{W}_{\rho}}|_{a,b}

\displaystyle\leq(2\pi)^{5n}2^{4(|a|+|b|+n)}\|{\mathcal{W}_{\chi}}\|_{a,b}\|{\mathcal{W}_{\chi}}\|_{2(a+\hat{b})+6}\|{\mathcal{W}_{\rho}}\|_{2(a+\hat{b})+4}.

(3.18)

Note that the right-hand side contains only the decay
norms of
{\mathcal{W}_{\rho}}
, and the left-hand side contains
an arbitrary Schwartz seminorm, so this implies Theorem
1.1
. We also observe that, on the right-hand side, the position and momentum indices are flipped in the derivative multi-index
\hat{b}=\widehat{(b_{\mathrm{x}},b_{\mathrm{p}})}=(b_{\mathrm{p}},b_{\mathrm{x}})
when it contributes to a coordinate power (rather than a derivative power).

Proof.

We start with Eq. (
3.9
) of Lemma
3.7
and apply Lemma
3.1
followed by Eq. (
3.11
) from Lemma
3.8
to get

\begin{split}|\gamma^{a}\partial_{\gamma}^{a}{\mathcal{W}_{\rho}}(\gamma)|&\leq\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2n}}\int|\gamma^{a}\partial_{\gamma}^{a}{\mathcal{W}_{\mathinner{|{\chi_{\alpha}}\rangle}\!\mathinner{\langle{\chi_{\beta}}|}}}(\gamma)||{\mathcal{Q}^{\chi}_{\rho}}(\alpha)|^{1/2}|{\mathcal{Q}^{\chi}_{\rho}}(\beta)|^{1/2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\alpha\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\beta\\
&\leq\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2n}}\sum_{c\leq b}\sum_{d\leq a}\sum_{e\leq c}\sum_{f\leq d}\binom{b}{c}\binom{a}{d}\binom{c}{e}\binom{d}{f}2^{-|d|}|{\mathcal{W}_{\chi}}|_{d-a,b-c}\\
&\qquad\times\int\frac{1}{\prod_{j=1}^{2n}(1+|\alpha_{j}|^{2})}|\alpha^{\hat{e}+f}|\prod_{j=1}^{2n}(1+|\alpha_{j}|^{2})|{\mathcal{Q}^{\chi}_{\rho}}(\alpha)|^{1/2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\alpha\\
&\qquad\times\int\frac{1}{\prod_{j=1}^{2n}(1+|\beta_{j}|^{2})}|\beta^{\hat{c}-\hat{e}+d-f}|\prod_{j=1}^{2n}(1+|\beta_{j}|^{2})|{\mathcal{Q}^{\chi}_{\rho}}(\beta)|^{1/2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\beta.\end{split}

(3.19)

To compute the integral over
\alpha
, we use

\begin{split}|\alpha^{\hat{e}+f}|\prod_{j=1}^{2n}(1+|\alpha_{j}^{2}|)|{\mathcal{Q}^{\chi}_{\rho}}(\alpha)|^{1/2}&\leq 2^{2n}\|{\mathcal{Q}^{\chi}_{\rho}}\|_{2\hat{e}+2f+4}^{1/2}\leq 2^{2n}\|{\mathcal{Q}^{\chi}_{\rho}}\|_{2(a+\hat{b})+4}^{1/2},\end{split}

(3.20)

and likewise for the integral over
\beta
. Integrating over
\alpha
with
\int(1+|\alpha_{j}|^{2})^{-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\alpha_{j}=\pi
and likewise for
\beta
, we obtain

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{W}_{\rho}}|_{a,b}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)^{2n}\|{\mathcal{Q}^{\chi}_{\rho}}\|_{2(a+\hat{b})+4}\sum_{c\leq b}\sum_{d\leq a}\sum_{e\leq c}\sum_{f\leq d}\binom{b}{c}\binom{a}{d}\binom{c}{e}\binom{d}{f}2^{-|d|}|{\mathcal{W}_{\chi}}|_{d-a,b-c}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)^{2n}2^{2(|a|+|b|)}\|{\mathcal{W}_{\chi}}\|_{a,b}\|{\mathcal{Q}^{\chi}_{\rho}}\|_{2(a+\hat{b})+4}

(3.21)

using
2^{-|d|}\leq 1
and
|{\mathcal{W}_{\chi}}|_{d-a,b-c}\leq\|{\mathcal{W}_{\chi}}\|_{a,b}
.
We then bound the decay seminorms of
{\mathcal{Q}^{\chi}_{\rho}}
using Lemma
2.10
:

\begin{split}\alpha^{a}{\mathcal{Q}^{\chi}_{\rho}}(\alpha)=(2\pi)^{n}\int{\mathcal{W}_{\rho}}(\alpha-\beta)((\alpha-\beta)+\beta)^{a}{\mathcal{W}_{\chi}^{-}}(\beta)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\beta\end{split}

(3.22)

so

\begin{split}|\alpha^{a}{\mathcal{Q}^{\chi}_{\rho}}(\alpha)|&\leq(2\pi)^{n}\sum_{b\leq a}\binom{a}{b}\int\frac{1}{\prod_{j=1}^{2n}(1+|\beta_{j}|^{2})}|(\alpha-\beta)^{b}{\mathcal{W}_{\rho}}(\alpha-\beta)|\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times|\beta^{a-b}|\prod_{j=1}^{2m}(1+|\beta_{j}|^{2})|{\mathcal{W}_{\chi}^{-}}(\beta)|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\beta\\
&\leq(2\pi)^{n}2^{|a|}\pi^{2n}\|{\mathcal{W}_{\rho}}\|_{a}\|{\mathcal{W}_{\chi}}\|_{a+2}\end{split}

(3.23)

using
\|{\mathcal{W}_{\chi}}\|_{a+2}=\|{\mathcal{W}_{\chi}^{-}}\|_{a+2}
.
Summing over the seminorms in the norm,

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{Q}^{\chi}_{\rho}}\|_{a}=\sum_{b\leq a}|{\mathcal{Q}^{\chi}_{\rho}}|_{b}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{n}\pi^{3n}\sum_{b\leq a}2^{|b|}\|{\mathcal{W}_{\rho}}\|_{b}\|{\mathcal{W}_{\chi}}\|_{b+2}

(3.24)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{3n+|a|}\pi^{3n}\|{\mathcal{W}_{\rho}}\|_{a}\|{\mathcal{W}_{\chi}}\|_{a+2},

(3.25)

and then inserting into (

) yields (

).
∎

4.
Schwartz states

In this section, we extend our main result by establishing an equivalence between the Schwartz-class and rapid-decay properties of many different representations of the quantum state. First, we will give a notion of Schwartz class to a set of orthogonal wavefunction
\{\psi_{i}\}
appearing in a spectral decomposition
{\mathcal{K}_{\rho}}(x,y)=\sum_{i}\psi_{i}(x)\bar{\psi}_{i}(y)
.
Then, we recall the definition of a Schwartz operator as identified by Keyl et al.
[
8
]
. Finally, we will prove our large equivalence theorem.

To guarantee that
{\mathcal{K}_{\rho}}
is a Schwartz function, it is, of course, not sufficient for each
\psi_{i}
to be a Schwartz function. For example, if each
\psi_{i}
is a Gaussian wavepacket with increasing variance
\sigma^{2}_{i}\propto i
centered on the origin, then the overall variance
\langle X^{2}\rangle=\Tr[X^{2}\rho]=\int x^{2}{\mathcal{K}_{\rho}}(x,x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=\sum_{i}\int x^{2}|\psi_{i}(x)|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=\sum_{i}p_{i}\sigma_{i}^{2}
can diverge if the norms
p_{i}=\mathinner{\langle{\psi_{i}|\psi_{i}}\rangle}
are decreasing slowly, so that
{\mathcal{K}_{\rho}}
is not a Schwartz function even though each
\psi_{i}
is. Instead, we consider the following definition.
2
2

2

Note that the multi-indices
a,b,c,d
in this section are
n
-dimensional rather than
2n
-dimensional because the wavefunction
\psi
and the kernel
{\mathcal{K}_{\rho}}
take arguments in position space rather than phase space.

Definition 4.1
.

A set
\{\psi_{i}\}
of unnormalized wavefunctions (
\psi_{i}\in L^{2}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
for all
i
) is
jointly Schwartz
when
|\{\psi_{i}\}|_{a,b}<\infty
for all
a,b\in({\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\})^{\times n}
, where the Schwartz seminorms of such a set are defined by

|\{\psi_{i}\}|_{a,b}^{2}:=\sup_{x}\sum_{i}\left|x^{a}\partial_{x}^{b}\psi_{i}(x)\right|^{2}.

(4.1)

This is denoted
\{\psi_{i}\}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathrm{j}}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.

Note that this seminorm (
4.1
) is not simply a function of the seminorm
|{\mathcal{K}_{\rho}}|_{(a,c),(b,d)}
of the kernel, nor is it simply a function of the individual seminorms
|\psi_{i}|_{a,b}:=\sup_{x}|x^{a}\partial_{x}^{b}\psi_{i}(x)|
of the wavefunctions
\psi_{i}
.
However, one can check that when the set
\{\psi_{i}\}
is finite, the jointly Schwartz property is equivalent to the condition that all the wavefunctions are Schwartz functions individually,
\{\psi_{i}\}\subset{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.

Lemma 4.2
.

For any quantum state
\rho
, the set of unnormalized wavefunctions
\{\psi_{i}\}
of the spectral decomposition is jointly Schwartz if and only if the kernel
{\mathcal{K}_{\rho}}
is a Schwartz function.

Proof.

First assume that
\{\psi_{i}\}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathrm{j}}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
. Then

\begin{split}|{\mathcal{K}_{\rho}}|_{(a,c),(b,d)}&=\sup_{x,y}\left|x^{a}y^{c}\partial_{x}^{b}\partial_{y}^{d}K(x,y)\right|\\
&=\sup_{x,y}\left|\sum_{i}\left(x^{a}\partial_{x}^{b}\psi_{i}(x)\right)\left(y^{c}\partial_{y}^{d}\bar{\psi}_{i}(y)\right)\right|\\
&\leq\sup_{x,y}\left(\sum_{i}\left|x^{a}\partial_{x}^{b}\psi_{i}(x)\right|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{i}\left|y^{c}\partial_{y}^{d}\psi_{i}(y)\right|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\\
&=\left(\sup_{x}\sum_{i}\left|x^{a}\partial_{x}^{b}\psi_{i}(x)\right|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sup_{y}\sum_{i}\left|y^{c}\partial_{y}^{d}\psi_{i}(y)\right|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\\
&=|\{\psi_{i}\}|_{a,b}|\{\psi_{i}\}|_{c,d},\end{split}

(4.2)

where the third line is the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality.
Therefore,
\{\psi_{i}\}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathrm{j}}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})\Rightarrow{\mathcal{K}_{\rho}}\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbf{R}}^{2n})
. To see the inverse, note that

\begin{split}|{\mathcal{K}_{\rho}}|_{(a,a),(b,b)}&=\sup_{x,y}\left|x^{a}y^{a}\partial_{x}^{b}\partial_{y}^{b}K(x,y)\right|\\
&\geq\sup_{x}\left|\left(x^{a}y^{a}\partial_{x}^{b}\partial_{y}^{b}K\right)(x,x)\right|\\
&=\sup_{x}\sum_{i}|x^{a}\partial_{x}^{b}\psi_{i}|^{2}\\
\end{split}

(4.3)

where the inequality holds because
{\mathcal{K}_{\rho}}
is a Schwartz function. This quantity diverges by definition if
\{\psi_{i}\}\notin{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathrm{j}}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
, implying
{\mathcal{K}_{\rho}}\notin{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbf{R}}^{2n})
.
∎

The natural way to characterize the Schwartz class of quantum states was suggested by Keyl et al.
[
8
]
as those quantum states
\rho
with bounded expectation value for all symmetric polynomials in
X
and
P
(i.e.,
\trace[X^{a}P^{b}\rho P^{b}X^{a}]<\infty
for all
a,b\in(\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\})^{\times n}
). More generally, for arbitrary operators (i.e., not necessarily positive semidefinite, self-adjoint, or trace-class), they define:

Definition 4.3
.

An operator
E
is a Schwartz operator, denoted
E\in{\mathcal{S}}(L^{2}({\mathbf{R}}^{n}))
, when
|E|_{a,b,c,d}<\infty
for all
a,b,c,d\in({\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\})^{\times n}
, where the Schwartz operator seminorms are

|E|_{a,b,c,d}:=\sup_{|\psi|,|\phi|=1}\left|\left\langle\psi\left|X^{a}P^{b}EP^{c}X^{d}\right|\phi\right\rangle\right|.

(4.4)

Here, the supremum is taken over all normalized wavefunctions
\psi,\phi\in L^{2}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
.

Equipped with Definitions
4.1
and
4.3
, we can state a theorem that subsumes the results of Sec.
3
.

Theorem 4.4
.

For any Schwartz-class reference wavefunction
\chi\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
and for any quantum state
(i.e., positive semidefinite trace-class operator on
L^{2}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
)

\rho
, with spectral decomposition
\{\psi_{i}\}
, quasicharacteristic function
{\mathcal{F}_{\rho}}
, Wigner function
{\mathcal{W}_{\rho}}
, kernel
{\mathcal{K}_{\rho}}
, Husimi function
{\mathcal{Q}^{\chi}_{\rho}}
, and matrix element
{\mathcal{M}^{\chi}_{\rho}}
, the following conditions are equivalent:

•

{\mathcal{W}_{\rho}}\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbf{R}}^{2n})

•

{\mathcal{W}_{\rho}}\in{\mathcal{D}}({\mathbf{R}}^{2n})

•

{\mathcal{Q}^{\chi}_{\rho}}\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbf{R}}^{2n})

•

{\mathcal{Q}^{\chi}_{\rho}}\in{\mathcal{D}}({\mathbf{R}}^{2n})

•

{\mathcal{M}^{\chi}_{\rho}}\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbf{R}}^{4d})

•

{\mathcal{M}^{\chi}_{\rho}}\in{\mathcal{D}}({\mathbf{R}}^{4d})

•

{\mathcal{F}_{\rho}}\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbf{R}}^{2n})

•

{\mathcal{K}_{\rho}}\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbf{R}}^{2n})

•

\{\psi_{i}\}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathrm{j}}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})

•

\rho\in{\mathcal{S}}(L^{2}({\mathbf{R}}^{n}))

Furthermore, if the set
\{\psi_{i}\}
is finite (e.g., if the state is pure,
\rho=|\psi\rangle\!\langle\psi|
), then the condition
\{\psi_{i}\}\subset{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
is also equivalent to the above.

Proof.

We have:

{\mathcal{W}_{\rho}}\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbf{R}}^{2n})\Rightarrow{\mathcal{W}_{\rho}}\in{\mathcal{D}}({\mathbf{R}}^{2n})
because
{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbf{R}}^{2n})\subset{\mathcal{D}}({\mathbf{R}}^{2n})
.

{\mathcal{W}_{\rho}}\in{\mathcal{D}}({\mathbf{R}}^{2n})\Rightarrow{\mathcal{Q}^{\chi}_{\rho}},{\mathcal{M}^{\chi}_{\rho}}\in{\mathcal{D}}({\mathbf{R}}^{2n})
by
Corollary
3.2
.

{\mathcal{M}^{\chi}_{\rho}}\in{\mathcal{D}}({\mathbf{R}}^{2n})\Rightarrow{\mathcal{M}^{\chi}_{\rho}}\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbf{R}}^{2n})
by Corollary
3.4
.

{\mathcal{M}^{\chi}_{\rho}}\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbf{R}}^{2n})\Rightarrow{\mathcal{W}_{\rho}}\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbf{R}}^{2n})
by Corollary
3.6
.

{\mathcal{Q}^{\chi}_{\rho}}\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbf{R}}^{2n})\Rightarrow{\mathcal{Q}^{\chi}_{\rho}}\in{\mathcal{D}}({\mathbf{R}}^{2n})

Discussion

Although the Wigner formalism provides a complete representation of quantum states and dynamics, it is often regarded as less fundamental. (It only really becomes uniquely preferred in the classical limit, and under, e.g., certain symmetry demands to distinguish it from other deformations of classical mechanics; see for instance the introduction of Ref.
[
5
]
.) One practical reason is that computations are often more difficult using the Moyal product
5
5

5

Of course, the peculiar features of the Moyal product are not just a matter of practicalities: Because the Moyal bracket has phase-space derivatives of arbitrarily high order, the dynamics of the Wigner function are non-local.
, the so-called “
\star
-genvalue equations”, and so on
[
3
]
. Another reason is that the state space is awkward to define.

Formally, the state space of valid Wigner functions can be delineated with the quantum generalization
[
10
,
2
]
of Bochner’s theorem
[
1
]
. (See also illuminating discussion and further generalizations to some discrete spaces in Ref.
[
4
]
.) This definition is sufficiently opaque that most physicists simply think of the allowed pure-state Wigner functions as the image of the Wigner transform of the space of allowed quantum states,
L^{2}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
, if they think of it at all. In particular, many simple (even positive-valued) functions on
L^{1}({\mathbf{R}}^{2n})
are not the Wigner functions of any quantum states.

In contrast, the
L^{2}({\mathbf{R}}^{n})
(pure) state space of the Schrödinger representation is relatively simple to understand, and the “parameterization” of that space is natural in the sense that all possible functions are allowed modulo only the single, easy-to-interpret constraint of normalization. This becomes even clearer in the case of a finite-dimensional quantum system, where there are no complications related to the continuum and where any complex-valued function over configuration space suffices as a (not necessarily normalized) state. Delineating the corresponding set of Wigner functions for finite-dimensional systems is much more subtle
[
4
]
.

In this sense, the Wigner representation is “overparameterized”. One can think of our Theorem
1.1
as better characterizing this overparameterization: the regularity of the
interior
of the Wigner function in terms of its derivatives of any order is tightly controlled by the Wigner function’s decay toward infinity, a feature that is obviously not shared by all normalized functions over
{\mathbf{R}}^{2n}
. With the seminorm bound of Theorem
3.9
, one can also recover a version of the uncertainty principle. For example, because the supremum of the gradient of
{\mathcal{W}_{\rho}}
is bounded by the decay seminorms of
{\mathcal{W}_{\rho}}
, it is impossible for
{\mathcal{W}_{\rho}}
to be supported in a ball of too small a radius.

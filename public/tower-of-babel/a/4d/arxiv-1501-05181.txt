Isomorphy up to complementation
Maurice Pouzet, Hamza Si Kaddour

Abstract
. Considering uniform hypergraphs, we prove that for every non-negative integer h h there exist two non-negative integers k k and t t with k ≤ t k\leq t such that two h h -uniform hypergraphs ℋ {\mathcal{H}} and ℋ ′ {\mathcal{H}}^{\prime} on the same set V V of vertices, with | V | ≥ t |V|\geq t , are equal up to complementation whenever ℋ {\mathcal{H}} and ℋ ′ {\mathcal{H}}^{\prime} are k k -hypomorphic up to complementation. We prove that s ⁡ ( h ) = h + 2 ⌊ log 2 ⁡ h ⌋ s(h)=h+2^{\lfloor\log_{2}h\rfloor} . The values s ⁡ ( 2 ) = 4 s(2)=4 and v ⁡ ( 2 ) = 6 v(2)=6 were obtained in [ 9 ] .

2.3.
Monomorphic decomposition of hypergraphs

We present in this section a notion of monomorphic decomposition of a uniform hypergraph. This notion was introduced in
[
24
]
. Due to the introduction of an equivalence relation previously considered in
[
21
]
and developed in
[
20
]
, our presentation is simpler. Let
{\mathcal{H}}:=(V,{\mathcal{E}})
be a
h
-uniform hypergraph. Let

, we say that
{\mathcal{H}}
is

-

or that

is a
constant block
if
\mathcal{H}(A)=\mathcal{H}(A^{\prime})
for every
A,A^{\prime}\in[V]^{h}
such that

.
We say that
\mathcal{H}
is

if it is
F
-constant for some finite subset

.

Example 1
.

A
2
-uniform hypergraph is simply a (undirected) graph. If
G:=(V,\mathcal{E})
is a graph then a subset

B
of

V
is a constant block if and only if it satisfies the two conditions:

(1)

B is either a clique or an independent of
G
;

(2)

B is an autonomous subset of
G
, that is for every
y\in{\mathcal{E}}\setminus B
and every
x,x^{\prime}\in B
,

\{y,x\}\in\mathcal{E}\Leftrightarrow\{y,x^{\prime}\}\in\mathcal{E}
.

A
monomorphic decomposition
of a
h
-uniform hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
is a partition of
V
into constant blocks.

Lemma 1
.

A partition
\mathcal{P}
of
V
is a monomorphic decomposition if
\mathcal{H}(A)=\mathcal{H}(A^{\prime})
for every
A,A^{\prime}\in[V]^{h}
such that
|A\cap B|=|A^{\prime}\cap B|
for every block
B\in\mathcal{P}
.

This is essentially Lemma 2.9 p.13 of
[
24
]
.

Let
x,y\in V
. We set
x\equiv_{\mathcal{H}}y
if

(1)

\mathcal{H}(K\cup\{x\})=\mathcal{H}(K\cup\{y\})

for every
K\in[V\setminus\{x,y\}]^{h-1}
.

Proposition 3
.

The relation
\equiv_{\mathcal{H}}
is an equivalence relation on
V
. The blocks of this equivalence are constant. They form a monomorphic decomposition of
\mathcal{H}
and every monomorphic decomposition is finer.

Except for the introduction of the equivalence relation, this is essentially Lemma 2.11 and Proposition 2.12 p. 14 of
[
24
]
. We give an outline of the proof below.

Proof.
First,

\equiv_{\mathcal{H}}
is an equivalence relation. For that it suffices
to check that it is transitive. Let
x,y,z\in V
with
x\equiv_{\mathcal{H}}y
and
y\equiv_{\mathcal{H}}z
.
We check that
x\equiv_{\mathcal{H}}z
. We may suppose these elements pairwise distinct.
Let
K\in[V\setminus\{x,z\}]^{h-1}
.

Case 1)
y\not\in K
. In this case

\mathcal{H}(K\cup\{x\})=\mathcal{H}(K\cup\{y\})
and
\mathcal{H}(K\cup\{y\})=\mathcal{H}(K\cup\{z\})
.
Hence
\mathcal{H}(K\cup\{x\}=\mathcal{H}(K\cup\{z\})
. Thus
x\equiv_{\mathcal{H}}z
.

Case 2)
y\in K
.
Set
K^{\prime}:=(K\setminus\{y\})\cup\{z\}
. Since
x\equiv_{\mathcal{H}}y
, we have
\mathcal{H}(K^{\prime}\cup\{x\})=\mathcal{H}(K^{\prime}\cup\{y\})=\mathcal{H}(K\cup\{z\})
. Similarly, setting
K^{\prime\prime}:=(K\setminus\{y\})\cup\{x\}
then, since

y\equiv_{\mathcal{H}}z
, we have
\mathcal{H}(K\cup\{x\})=\mathcal{H}(K^{\prime\prime}\cup\{y\})=\mathcal{H}(K^{\prime\prime}\cup\{z\})
. Since

K^{\prime}\cup\{x\}=K^{\prime\prime}\cup\{z\}
, we have
\mathcal{H}(K\cup\{x\})=\mathcal{H}(K\cup\{z\})
and thus
x\equiv_{\mathcal{H}}z
as claimed. Next, the blocks of this equivalence are constant. Let
C
be a block of
\equiv_{\mathcal{H}}
. We prove that
\mathcal{H}(A)=\mathcal{H}(A^{\prime})
for every
A,A^{\prime}\in[V]^{h}
such that
A\setminus C=A^{\prime}\setminus C
. Let
\ell:=|A\setminus A^{\prime}|
. If
\ell=0
,
A=A^{\prime}
, there is nothing to prove. If
\ell=1
, then
A=\{x\}\cup(A\cap A^{\prime})
and
A^{\prime}=\{y\}\cup(A\cap A^{\prime})
, with
x,y\in C

\mathcal{H}(A)=\mathcal{H}(A^{\prime})
since

and

. We may find a sequence of

-element subsets of

such that

,

and the symmetric difference of

and

is

or

. From the case

we have
\mathcal{H}(A_{i}\cup K)=\mathcal{H}(A_{i+1}\cup K)
for

. Hence
\mathcal{H}(A)=\mathcal{H}(A^{\prime})
. Since these blocks are constant, they form a monomorphic decomposition. To conclude that every other monomorphic decomposition is finer, note that the elements of a constant block are pairwise equivalent w.r.t.
\equiv_{\mathcal{H}}
hence contained into a block of this equivalence.

We call

the blocks of the equivalence relation
\equiv_{\mathcal{H}}
. Note that the equivalence relations
\equiv_{\mathcal{H}}
and

coincide and also that if
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
in an other

-uniform hypergraph, every isomorphism of

will transform

into

onto the components of
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
.

Various kind of isomorphy have been considered for hypergraphs e.g

be an integer. A
h
-uniform hypergraph valued by

of

5.1.

\mathfrak{G}

Take

,

where

for all other pairs and

and

for all other pairs.

.

Let

6.
Conclusions

The motivation of this line of research comes from several reconstruction results and conjectures about binary structures. The Ulam’s reconstruction conjecture, still unsolved, is well-known (see the surveys
[
3
,
4
]
). Fraïssé made a related conjecture about relational structures. The case of binary structures was handled by Lopez. A
binary structure
can be understood as a pair
R:=(V,c)
where
c
is a map from the set

V^{2}
of ordered pairs
(x,y)
of elements of
V
into a set
W
. The notion of isomorphism, and
k
-hypomorphism can be defined as for valued graphs. It was shown by Lopez
[
17
]
(see also
[
13
]
) that if two binary structures
R
and
R^{\prime}
on a finite set
V
are
k
-hypomorphic for all
k\leq 6
then they are
k
-hypomorphic for every integer
k
. Supposing
W=\{0,1\}
, Hagendorf and Lopez
[
15
]
say that two binary structures
R
and
R^{\prime}
are
hemimorphic
if either they are isomorphic or one is isomorphic to the dual of the other (the
dual
of
R:=(V,c)
is
R^{d}:=(V,c^{d})
where
c^{d}(x,y):=c(y,x)
). They prove that hemimorphy behaves as hypomorphy, with a treshold of
12
instead of
6
. Numerous publications are built on these results (e.g.
[
18
,
6
,
7
,
8
]
). But for
W:=\{0,1\}^{n}
and the corresponding notion of duality, the reconstruction problem is unsolved. Recently, Ben Amira, Chaari, Dammak and Si Kaddour
[
1
]
replace the dual of a binary structure
R:=(V,c)
(with value in
W=\{0,1\}
) by its
complement

R:=(V,\overline{c})
defined by setting
\overline{c}(x,y):=1+c(x,y)
(where the addition is defined modulo
2
), they consider the notion of isomorphy up to complementation, and obtain some encouraging results. Instead of
W=\{0,1\}
we may suppose that
W=\{0,1\}^{n}
, fix a group of permutations
\mathfrak{G}
on
W
and ask the same question as in Problem
3
. As it is easy to see, results mentionned above are special instances of this question. On an other hand, none of these results extend to ternary relations
[
23
]
. Since the relation associated to a
h
-uniform hypergraph has arity
2h
, the answer to Problem
3
does not seem to follow from general results.

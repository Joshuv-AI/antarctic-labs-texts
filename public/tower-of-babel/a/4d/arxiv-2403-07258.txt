Harmonic Metrics for Higgs Bundles of Rank 3 in the Hitchin Section
Hitoshi FUJIOKA

Abstract
Given a tuple of holomorphic differentials on a Riemann surface, one can define a Higgs bundle in the Hitchin section and a natural symmetric pairing of the Higgs bundle. We study whether a Higgs bundle of rank 3 in the Hitchin section has a compatible harmonic metric when the spectral curve is a 2-sheeted branched covering of the Riemann surface. In particular, we give a condition for Higgs bundles in the Hitchin section on ℂ \mathbb{C} or ℂ ∗ \mathbb{C}^{*} to have compatible harmonic metrics.

Introduction

1.1
Harmonic bundles

Suppose that
X
is a Riemann surface. Let
E
be a holomorphic vector bundle on
X
and
{\theta\in H^{0}(X,\operatorname{End}{E}\otimes K_{X})}
, where
K_{X}
is the canonical line bundle of
X
. The pair
(E,\theta)
is called a Higgs bundle and
\theta
is called a Higgs field.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
(E,\theta)
be a Higgs bundle over
X
. A Hermitian metric
h
of
(E,\theta)
is called harmonic if

F_{\nabla_{h}}+\bigl[\theta,\theta^{\ast h}\bigr]=0,

(1.1)

where
\nabla_{h}
is the Chern connection of
(E,h)
,
F_{\nabla_{h}}
is the curvature of
\nabla_{h}
and
\theta^{\ast h}
is the adjoint of
\theta
with respect to
h
. Such a tuple
(E,\theta,h)
is called a harmonic bundle.

Remark 1.2
.

When
X
is compact, for a
\mathbb{C}
-vector bundle
E
on
X
, the degree
\deg{E}
is defined by
\int_{X}c_{1}(E)
.
The equation (
1.1
) means that the connection
\nabla_{h}+\theta+\theta^{*h}
is flat. Therefore, we implicitly focus on holomorphic bundles of degree 0.

The trivial bundle
\mathcal{O}_{X}=X\times\mathbb{C}
has the trivial Hermitian metric
h_{X}
defined by
h_{X}(a,b)=a\bar{b}
. If a harmonic bundle
(E,\theta,h)
of rank
r
on
X
satisfies
\det(E)=\mathcal{O}_{X}
,
\operatorname{Tr}{\theta}=0
and
\det(h)=h_{X}
,
(E,\theta,h)
is called an
{\rm SL}(r,\mathbb{C})
-harmonic bundle.

The equation (
1.1
) was introduced by Hitchin in
[
2
]
as the dimensional reduction of the self-duality equations. It is an important question whether a harmonic metric exists for a given Higgs bundle. Because (
1.1
) is a nonlinear partial differential equation, it is difficult to solve it for general Higgs bundles. For Higgs bundles on compact Riemann surfaces, the following theorem, due to Hitchin and Simpson, establishes the existence and the uniqueness of harmonic metrics, and it is the most fundamental theorem about harmonic bundles.

Theorem 1.3

(Hitchin
[
2
]
and Simpson
[
8
]
)
.

Suppose that
X
is compact. A Higgs bundle
(E,\theta)
on
X
has a harmonic metric if and only if
(E,\theta)
is polystable and
\deg{E}=0
. Moreover, if
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
are harmonic metrics of
(E,\theta)
, then there exists a decomposition

(E,\theta)=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}(E_{i},\theta|_{E_{i}}),

such that
c_{i}h_{1}|_{E_{i}}=h_{2}|_{E_{i}}
for positive constants
c_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{n}
and this decomposition is orthogonal with respect to
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
.

For general Higgs bundles on noncompact Riemann surfaces, it is unknown when a harmonic metric exists.
Suppose that
X
is the complement of a finite subset in a compact Riemann surface. In
[
8
]
, Simpson gave a sufficient condition for the existence of a harmonic metric of a Higgs bundle
(E,\theta)
on
X
. Let
g_{X}
be a Kähler metric satisfying the condition in
[
8
, Proposition 2.4]
. Let
h_{0}
be a Hermitian metric of
E
such that
F_{\nabla_{h}}+\bigl[\theta,\theta^{\ast h}\bigr]
is bounded with respect to
h_{0}
and
g_{X}
. We can define the stability condition for
(E,\theta,h_{0})
as explained in
[
8
, Section 3]
. Suppose
\det(h_{0})
is flat for simplicity. If
(E,\theta,h_{0})
is stable, then there exists a harmonic metric
h
of
(E,\theta)
with
h_{0}
and
h
is mutually bounded
[
8
, Theorem 1]
. In addition, Theorem
1.3
is generalized also for filtered Higgs bundles. It was proved by Simpson
[
9
]
in the tame case and by Biquard–Boalch
[
1
]
and Mochizuki
[
7
]
in the wild case (see Section
2
for details).

1.2
Higgs bundles in the Hitchin section

1.2.1
Non-degenerate symmetric pairings

For a Higgs bundle
(E,\theta)
, a holomorphic symmetric pairing
C
of
E
is called a symmetric pairing of
(E,\theta)
if
C(\mathrm{id}\otimes\theta)=C(\theta\otimes\mathrm{id})
. A harmonic metric
h
of
(E,\theta)
is said to be compatible with a non-degenerate symmetric pairing
C
of
(E,\theta)
if the isomorphism
E\to E^{*}
,
v\mapsto(w\mapsto C(w,v))
is an isometry with respect to
h
and
h^{*}
, where
h^{*}
is the Hermitian metric on
E^{*}
induced by
h
.

1.2.2
Higgs bundles in the Hitchin section

Let
q_{j}
be a holomorphic
j
-differential on
X
for
j=2,\ldots,r
. The tuple
(q_{2},\dots,q_{r})
is denoted by
q
. We fix a line bundle
K_{X}^{1/2}
. The holomorphic differential
q_{j}
induces the morphisms

K_{X}^{(r-2i+1)/2}\longrightarrow K_{X}^{(r-2i+2j-1)/2}\otimes K_{X}

for
i=j,\ldots,r
. Then we can construct a Higgs bundle
(\mathbb{K}_{X,r},\theta(q))
of rank
r
as follows:

\mathbb{K}_{X,r}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}K_{X}^{(r-2i+1)/2},\qquad\theta(q)=\begin{pmatrix}0&q_{2}&q_{3}&\cdots&q_{r}\\
1&0&q_{2}&\cdots&q_{r-1}\\
0&1&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&q_{2}\\
0&\cdots&0&1&0\end{pmatrix}.

These Higgs bundles
(\mathbb{K}_{X,r},\theta(q))
were introduced by Hitchin in
[
3
]
and are called Higgs bundles in the Hitchin section. If
X
is compact, then a mapping, which is called the Hitchin fibration, from the moduli space
\mathcal{M}
of polystable
{\rm SL}(r,\mathbb{C})
-Higgs bundles on
X
to
\bigoplus_{i=2}^{r}H^{0}\bigl(X,K_{X}^{\otimes i}\bigr)
is constructed by assigning to
[(E,\theta)]\in\mathcal{M}
the coefficients of the characteristic polynomial of
\theta
, and these Higgs bundles
(\mathbb{K}_{X,r},\theta(q))
form a right-inverse of the Hitchin fibration.

1.2.3
Symmetric pairings of Higgs bundles in the Hitchin section

A Higgs bundle
(\mathbb{K}_{X,r},\theta(q))
in the Hitchin section has a natural symmetric pairing
C_{X,r}
defined by the morphism

K_{X}^{(r-2i+1)/2}\otimes K_{X}^{-(r-2i+1)/2}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}.

Suppose that
z
is a local holomorphic coordinate and
({\rm d}z)^{1/2}
is a local frame of
K_{X}^{1/2}
, the pairing
C_{X,2}
on
K_{X}^{1/2}\oplus K_{X}^{-1/2}
is given by

\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}

with respect to the frame
\bigl(({\rm d}z)^{1/2},({\rm d}z)^{-1/2}\bigr)
. The pairing
C_{X,3}
on
K_{X}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{X}\oplus K_{X}^{-1}
is given by

\begin{pmatrix}0&0&1\\
0&1&0\\
1&0&0\end{pmatrix}

with respect to the frame
\bigl({\rm d}z,1,({\rm d}z)^{-1}\bigr)
.

1.3
Main results

We will consider the following question.

Question 1.4
.

Suppose that
X
is a noncompact parabolic Riemann surface, i.e.,
X=\mathbb{C}
or
\mathbb{C}^{*}
.
Given a tuple of holomorphic polynomial differentials
q=(q_{2},\dots,q_{r})
on
X
, i.e., meromorphic differentials on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
which have a possible pole at
\infty
, does there exist a harmonic metric of
(\mathbb{K}_{X,r},\theta(q))
compatible with
C_{X,r}
?

Remark 1.5
.

In
[
5
]
, Li and Mochizuki proved that if
X
is hyperbolic, then there exists a harmonic metric of
(\mathbb{K}_{X,r},\theta(q))
compatible with
C_{X,r}
.

In general, for a Higgs bundle
(E,\theta)
on
X
, the spectral curve
\Sigma_{E,\theta}
of
(E,\theta)
is a curve defined by
\{t\in K_{X}\mid\det(t\,\mathrm{id}_{E}-\theta)=0\}
. We can define the natural projection from
\Sigma_{E,\theta}
to
X
by restricting
K_{X}\to X
to
\Sigma_{E,\theta}
. The spectral curve of
(\mathbb{K}_{X,r},\theta(q))
is denoted by
\Sigma_{X,q}
. We call the natural projection
\pi\colon\Sigma_{X,q}\to X
an
n
-sheeted branched covering if the fiber of
\pi\colon\Sigma_{X,q}\to X
with the largest cardinality consists of
n
elements. Let
z
be the holomorphic coordinate of
\mathbb{C}
. The following theorems are our main results, giving a partial answer to Question
1.4
.

Theorem 1.6
.

If the natural projection
\pi\colon\Sigma_{\mathbb{C},q}\to\mathbb{C}
is a one- or two-sheeted branched covering, then there exists a polynomial
f\in\mathbb{C}[z]
such that

q_{2}=3\cdot 2^{-5/3}f^{2}({\rm d}z)^{2},\qquad q_{3}=f^{3}({\rm d}z)^{3}.

If
\deg{f}\geq 2
, then there exists a harmonic metric of
(\mathbb{K}_{\mathbb{C},3},\theta(q))
compatible with
C_{\mathbb{C},3}
. Otherwise, there does not exist any harmonic metric compatible with
C_{\mathbb{C},3}
.

Theorem 1.7
.

If the natural projection
\pi\colon\Sigma_{\mathbb{C}^{*},q}\to\mathbb{C}^{*}
is a one- or two-sheeted branched covering, then there exists
f\in\mathbb{C}\bigl[z,z^{-1}\bigr]
such that

q_{2}=3\cdot 2^{-5/3}f^{2}({\rm d}z/z)^{2},\qquad q_{3}=f^{3}({\rm d}z/z)^{3}.

If
f
is not constant, then there exists a harmonic metric of
(\mathbb{K}_{\mathbb{C}^{*},3},\theta(q))
compatible with
C_{\mathbb{C}^{*},3}
. If
f
is constant, then there does not exist any harmonic metric compatible with
C_{\mathbb{C}^{*},3}
.

Remark 1.8
.

•

In
[
6
]
, Li and Mochizuki proved that a Higgs bundle
(E,\theta)
with a symmetric pairing admits a compatible harmonic metric if
(E,\theta)
is generically regular semisimple using an analytical approach. In particular, if
\pi
is a 3-sheeted branched covering, then
(\mathbb{K}_{X,3},\theta(q))
has a harmonic metric compatible with
C_{X,3}
. Therefore, we obtain the complete answer to the
r=3
case of Question
1.4
.

•

In the
r=2
case,
(\mathbb{K}_{X,2},\theta(q))
admits a compatible harmonic metric if
\pi
is a two-sheeted branched covering by Li–Mochizuki
[
6
]
. Otherwise, it has no such metric by
[
4
, Propositions 3.41 and 3.42]
.

•

As we will see in Remark
3.31
, compatible harmonic metrics of
(\mathbb{K}_{\mathbb{C},3},\theta(q))
correspond to filtered Higgs bundles satisfying some condition, which is parameterized by a real number in
(2,(\deg{f}+3)/2]
. Therefore, the uniqueness of compatible harmonic metrics is not true.

•

We need compatibility to prove the non-existence of harmonic metrics in Theorems
1.6
and
1.7
. The author does not know whether there exists a harmonic metric if
(\mathbb{K}_{X,3},\theta(q))
admits no compatible harmonic metric.

To prove Theorem
1.6
, we use the following theorem proved by Li and Mochizuki. See Section
2
for detailed definitions.

Theorem 1.9

(
[
6
]
)
.

Suppose that
X
is compact. The following two objects are equivalent:

•

Wild harmonic bundles on
(X,D)
compatible with a non-degenerate symmetric pairing.

•

Good polystable filtered Higgs bundles on
(X,D)
of degree 0 with a perfect symmetric pairing.

By Theorem
1.9
, it suffices to study when there exists a stable good filtered extension of
(\mathbb{K}_{X,3},\theta(q))
such that
C_{X,3}
extends to a perfect pairing. The proof of Theorem
1.6
is outlined as follows.

Step 1

We calculate the eigenvalues of the Higgs filed
\theta(q)
, and take sections
s_{1}
,
s_{2}
and
s_{3}
of
\mathbb{K}_{\mathbb{C},3}
, which form a frame around
\infty
such that the representation of
\theta(q)
is
a Jordan canonical form with respect to
(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})
.

Step 2

Using
(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})
, we construct a meromorphic extension of
\mathbb{K}_{\mathbb{C},3}
and a filtered bundle
\mathcal{P}^{d}_{*}\mathbb{K}_{\mathbb{C},3}
over
\mathbb{K}_{\mathbb{C},3}
depending
d=(d_{1},d_{2},d_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
such that

\mathcal{P}^{d}_{c}(\mathbb{K}_{\mathbb{C},3})_{\infty}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{3}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1},\infty}([c-d_{i}]\infty)\cdot s_{i},

where for
c\in\mathbb{R}
,
[c]
is an integer satisfying
c-1<[c]\leq c
.

Step 3

We find the condition for
d
that
\bigl(\mathcal{P}^{d}_{*}\mathbb{K}_{\mathbb{C},3},\theta(q)\bigr)
is good and stable, and that the pairing
C_{\mathbb{C},3}
extends to a perfect pairing. We can prove that if
\deg{f}\geq 2
, then there exists
d\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
satisfying the condition.

Step 4

We prove that if a filtered Higgs bundle is a good filtered extension of
(\mathbb{K}_{\mathbb{C},3},\theta(q))
and the pairing induced by
C_{\mathbb{C},3}
is perfect, then there exists
d\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
such that the filtered bundle is
\mathcal{P}^{d}_{*}(\mathbb{K}_{\mathbb{C},3})
. We can prove that if
\deg{f}\leq 1
, then there is no harmonic metric of
(\mathbb{K}_{\mathbb{C},3},\theta(q))
compatible with
C
from this.

In addition to Theorem
1.6
, we obtain the classification of filtered Higgs bundles corresponding to compatible harmonic metrics of
(\mathbb{K}_{\mathbb{C},3},\theta(q))
in the case when
\pi\colon\Sigma_{\mathbb{C},q}\to\mathbb{C}
is a two-sheeted branched covering.
Moreover, the proof of Theorem
1.6
can be partially generalized to the case of the Higgs bundle
(\mathbb{K}_{X,3},\theta(q))
whose Higgs filed is meromorphic, where
X
is any other quasi-projective curve.

The proof of Theorem
1.7
is outlined as follows.

Step 1
′

We see that there exists a harmonic metric compatible with
C_{\mathbb{C}^{*},3}
unless
f(z)=az^{b}
,
a\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
,
|b|\geq 3
, in the same way as Step 1, Step 2 and Step 3 in the outline of the proof of Theorem
1.6
.

Step 2
′

We see that there does not exist any harmonic metric compatible with
C_{\mathbb{C}^{*},3}
if
f
is constant in a manner analogous to Step 4 in the outline of the proof of Theorem
1.6
.

Step 3
′

In the case of
f(z)=az^{b}
,
a\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
,
|b|\geq 3
, we explicitly construct a stable good filtered extension of
(\mathbb{K}_{X,3},\theta(q))
such that the pairing induced by
C_{\mathbb{C}^{*},3}
become perfect.

In the general rank case, there are many cases about the multiplicities of the eigenvalues of the Higgs field. For example, the
r=4
case is divided into the case when the two eigenvalues with the multiplicity 2 appear, the case when the one eigenvalue with the multiplicity 1 appears and the ones of the others are 1, etc. Because of this difficulty, the author has not yet obtained similar results for the general rank.

2.1
Filtered Higgs bundles

2.1.1
Filtered bundles

We will review the notion of filtered bundles, following
[
7
,
8
,
9
]
. In this paper, the notations are based on
[
6
]
.
Let
X
be a Riemann surface, and let
D
be a discrete subset of
X
. We write
\mathcal{O}_{X}(*D)
for the sheaf of meromorphic functions on
X
whose poles are contained in
D
. For any sheaf
\mathcal{F}
, we write
\mathcal{F}_{p}
for the stalk of
\mathcal{F}
at
p\in X
.

Definition 2.1
.

A filtered bundle
\mathcal{P}_{*}E
on
(X,D)
is a locally free
\mathcal{O}_{X}(*D)
-module
E
with a tuple of filtrations
\{\mathcal{P}_{c}E_{p}\}_{c\in\mathbb{R}}
of free
\mathcal{O}_{X,p}
-submodules of
E_{p}
for
p\in D
such that for
c
,
c_{1}
and
c_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
,

1)

(\mathcal{P}_{c}E_{p})(*p)=E_{p}
,

2)

\mathcal{P}_{c_{1}}E_{p}\subset\mathcal{P}_{c_{2}}E_{p}
if
c_{1}\leq c_{2}
,

3)

\mathcal{P}_{c}E_{p}=\bigcap_{a>c}(\mathcal{P}_{a}E_{p})
,

4)

\mathcal{P}_{c+1}E_{p}=\mathcal{P}_{c}E_{p}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X,p}}\mathcal{O}_{X,p}(p)
.

The rank of
\mathcal{P}_{*}E
is defined to be the rank of
E
. We also say that
\mathcal{P}_{*}E
is a filtered bundle over
E
.
We define
\mathcal{P}_{<c}E_{p}
to be
\bigcup_{a<c}(\mathcal{P}_{a}E_{p})
and
\operatorname{\mathrm{Gr}}^{\mathcal{P}}_{c}(E_{p})
to be
\mathcal{P}_{c}E_{p}/\mathcal{P}_{<c}E_{p}
. Let
\operatorname{\mathcal{P}ar}(\mathcal{P}_{*}E_{p})
denote
\bigl\{c\in\mathbb{R}\mid\operatorname{\mathrm{Gr}}^{\mathcal{P}}_{c}(E_{p})\neq 0\bigr\}
.

For
c=\bigl(c^{(p)}\bigr)_{p\in D}\in\mathbb{R}^{D}
, let
\mathcal{P}_{c}E
denote the locally free
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-submodule of
E
such that if
p\in D
,
(\mathcal{P}_{c}E)_{p}=\mathcal{P}_{c^{(p)}}E_{p}
, otherwise
(\mathcal{P}_{c}E)_{p}=E_{p}
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{P}_{*}E_{1}
and
\mathcal{P}_{*}E_{2}
be filtered bundles on
(X,D)
. A morphism
\varphi
from
\mathcal{P}_{*}E_{1}
to
\mathcal{P}_{*}E_{2}
is a morphism of sheaves of
\mathcal{O}_{X}(*D)
-modules
\varphi\colon E_{1}\to E_{2}
such that
\varphi(\mathcal{P}_{c}E_{1})\subset\mathcal{P}_{c}E_{2}
for any
c\in\mathbb{R}^{D}
.

Let
\mathcal{P}_{*}^{(0)}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(*D))
denote
\mathcal{O}_{X}(*D)
with filtrations defined by

\mathcal{P}_{c}^{(0)}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(*D))=\mathcal{O}_{X}\biggl(\sum_{p\in D}\bigl[c^{(p)}\bigr]p\biggr).

Let
\mathcal{P}_{*}E_{1}
and
\mathcal{P}_{*}E_{2}
be filtered bundles. We can define some filtered bundles induced by
\mathcal{P}_{*}E_{1}
and
\mathcal{P}_{*}E_{2}
.

•

Direct sum

\mathcal{P}_{c}(E_{1}\oplus E_{2})_{p}=\mathcal{P}_{c}E_{1,p}\oplus\mathcal{P}_{c}E_{2,p}
.

•

Tensor product

\mathcal{P}_{c}(E_{1}\otimes E_{2})_{p}=\sum_{a+b\leq c}(\mathcal{P}_{a}E_{1,p}\otimes\mathcal{P}_{b}E_{2,p})
.

•

Hom

\mathcal{P}_{c}(\operatorname{\mathcal{H}{\rm om}}(E_{1},E_{2}))_{p}=\{f\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_{X,p}}(E_{1,p},E_{2,p})\mid f(\mathcal{P}_{a}E_{1})\subset\mathcal{P}_{a+c}E_{2}\}
.

We write
\mathcal{P}_{*}E_{1}\oplus\mathcal{P}_{*}E_{2}
,
\mathcal{P}_{*}E_{1}\otimes\mathcal{P}_{*}E_{2}
and
\operatorname{\mathcal{H}{\rm om}}(\mathcal{P}_{*}E_{1},\mathcal{P}_{*}E_{2})
for
\mathcal{P}_{*}(E_{1}\oplus E_{2})
,
\mathcal{P}_{*}(E_{1}\otimes E_{2})
, and
\mathcal{P}_{*}(\operatorname{\mathcal{H}{\rm om}}(E_{1},E_{2}))
. We define the dual bundle of
\mathcal{P}_{*}E_{1}
as
\operatorname{\mathcal{H}{\rm om}}\bigl(\mathcal{P}_{*}E_{1},\mathcal{P}_{*}^{(0)}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(*D))\bigr)
.

2.1.2
Filtered Higgs bundles

Definition 2.3
.

Suppose that
X
is compact.
The degree
\deg{\mathcal{P}_{*}E}
of a filtered bundle
\mathcal{P}_{*}E
on
(X,D)
is

\deg{\mathcal{P}_{*}E}=\deg{\mathcal{P}_{c}E}-\sum_{p\in D}\sum_{c^{(p)}-1<a\leq c^{(p)}}a\dim\operatorname{\mathrm{Gr}}_{a}^{\mathcal{P}}(E_{p})

for
c\in\mathbb{R}^{D}
. We write
\mu(\mathcal{P}_{*}E)
for
\deg{\mathcal{P}_{*}E}/\operatorname{rank}{E}
, which is called the slope of
\mathcal{P}_{*}E
.

Let
\mathcal{P}_{*}E
be a filtered bundle on
(X,D)
. Let
E^{\prime}
be a locally free
\mathcal{O}_{X}(*D)
-submodule of
E
such that
E/E^{\prime}
is also locally free. Then the filtered bundles
\mathcal{P}_{*}E^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{P}_{*}(E/E^{\prime})
are induced as follows: for
c\in\mathbb{R}^{D}
,

\mathcal{P}_{c}E^{\prime}=(\mathcal{P}_{c}E)\cap E^{\prime},\qquad\mathcal{P}_{c}(E/E^{\prime})=\operatorname{Im}\bigl(\mathcal{P}_{c}E\to E/E^{\prime}\bigr).

Lemma 2.4
.

Suppose that
X
is compact. Then the following holds:

\deg{\mathcal{P}_{*}E}=\deg{\mathcal{P}_{*}E^{\prime}}+\deg{\mathcal{P}_{*}\bigl(E/E^{\prime}\bigr)}.

Proof.

Though this lemma is well known, we provide proof for the convenience of the reader.
By the short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{P}_{c}E^{\prime}\longrightarrow\mathcal{P}_{c}E\longrightarrow\mathcal{P}_{c}\bigl(E/E^{\prime}\bigr)\longrightarrow 0
,
we obtain

\deg{\mathcal{P}_{c}E}=\deg{\mathcal{P}_{c}E^{\prime}}+\deg{\mathcal{P}_{c}\bigl(E/E^{\prime}\bigr)}.

In addition, for
a\in\mathbb{R}
, we obtain the following commutative diagram:

\CD@.0@.0@.0\\
@.@VVV@VVV@VVV\\
0@>>>\mathcal{P}_{<a}E^{\prime}_{p}@>>>\mathcal{P}_{<a}{E}_{p}@>>>\mathcal{P}_{<a}(E/E^{\prime})_{p}@>>>0\\
@.@VVV@VVV@VVV\\
0@>>>\mathcal{P}_{a}E^{\prime}_{p}@>>>\mathcal{P}_{a}E_{p}@>>>\mathcal{P}_{a}(E/E^{\prime})_{p}@>>>0\\
@.@VVV@VVV@VVV\\
0@>>>\operatorname{\mathrm{Gr}}^{\mathcal{P}}_{a}\bigl(E^{\prime}_{p}\bigr)@>>>\operatorname{\mathrm{Gr}}^{\mathcal{P}}_{a}(E_{p})@>>>\operatorname{\mathrm{Gr}}^{\mathcal{P}}_{a}(E/E^{\prime})_{p}@>>>0.\\
@.@VVV@VVV@VVV\\
@.0@.0@.0\\

Since all columns and the top two rows are exact, the bottom row is exact. Therefore, we obtain
\dim{\operatorname{\mathrm{Gr}}^{\mathcal{P}}_{a}(E_{p})}=\dim{\operatorname{\mathrm{Gr}}^{\mathcal{P}}_{a}\bigl(E^{\prime}_{p}\bigr)}+\dim{\operatorname{\mathrm{Gr}}^{\mathcal{P}}_{a}(E/E^{\prime})_{p}}

We review the notion of filtered Higgs bundles and define the notion of stability of filtered Higgs bundles.

Definition 2.5
.

A filtered Higgs bundle on

\mathcal{P}_{*}E
on
(X,D)
and a morphism

Definition 2.6

Suppose that

is compact.

is a direct sum of stable filtered Higgs bundles with the same slopes.

Definition 2.7
.

A filtered Higgs bundle

on
(X,D)

\theta
is logarithmic with respect to the filtrations, that is,

.

Let

be a ramified covering. We define a filtration of

to be

for

.

Definition 2.8
.

A filtered Higgs bundle
(\mathcal{P}_{*}E,\theta)
on
(X,D)
is called good if for any

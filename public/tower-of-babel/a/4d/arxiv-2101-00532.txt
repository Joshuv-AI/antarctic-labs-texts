Abstract
We propose an asynchronous block-iterative decomposition algorithm to solve Nash equilibrium problems involving a mix of nonsmooth and smooth functions acting on linear mixtures of strategies. The methodology relies heavily on monotone operator theory and in particular on warped resolvents.

Introduction

We consider a noncooperative game with
m
players indexed by

I=\{1,\ldots,m\}
, in which the strategy
x_{i}
of player

i\in I
lies in a real Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}_{i}
. A strategy
profile is a point
\boldsymbol{x}=(x_{i})_{i\in I}
in the Hilbert
direct sum
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{H}}=\bigoplus_{i\in I}{\mathcal{H}}_{i}
, and the associated
profile of the players other than
i\in I
is the vector

\boldsymbol{x}_{\smallsetminus i}=(x_{j})_{j\in I\smallsetminus\{i\}}
in

\boldsymbol{\mathcal{H}}_{\smallsetminus i}=\bigoplus_{j\in I\smallsetminus\{i\}}{\mathcal{H}}_{j}
. Given an index
i\in I
and a vector

(x_{i},\boldsymbol{y})\in{\mathcal{H}}_{i}\times\boldsymbol{\mathcal{H}}
, we set

(x_{i};\boldsymbol{y}_{\smallsetminus i})=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{i-1},x_{i},y_{i+1},\ldots,y_{m})
.

A fundamental equilibrium notion was introduced by Nash in

[
20
,
21
]
to describe a state in which the loss of each
player cannot be reduced by unilateral deviation. In our context, a
formulation of the Nash equilibrium problem is

\text{find}\;\>\overline{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{H}}\;\>\text{such that}\;\>(\forall i\in I)\;\;\overline{x}_{i}\in\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{i}\in{\mathcal{H}}_{i}\end{subarray}}{\text{\rm Argmin}}\;\;\theta_{i}(x_{i})+\boldsymbol{\ell}_{i}(x_{i};\overline{\boldsymbol{x}}_{\smallsetminus i}),

(1.1)

where the global loss function of player
i\in I
is the sum of an
individual loss
\theta_{i}\colon{\mathcal{H}}_{i}\to\left]{-}\infty,{+}\infty\right]
and a joint loss

\boldsymbol{\ell}_{i}\colon\boldsymbol{\mathcal{H}}\to\left]{-}\infty,{+}\infty\right]
that models the interactions
with the other players.
Under convexity assumptions, numerical methods to solve (
1.1
)
have been investigated since the early 1970s
[
4
]
and they
have since involved increasingly sophisticated tools from nonlinear
analysis; see
[

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

]
. In the present paper, we
consider the following highly modular Nash equilibrium problem
wherein the functions
(\theta_{i})_{i\in I}
and

(\boldsymbol{\ell}_{i})_{i\in I}
of (
1.1
) are decomposed into

Problem 1.1

Let
({\mathcal{H}}_{i})_{i\in I}
,
({\mathcal{K}}_{i})_{i\in I}
, and
({\mathcal{G}}_{k})_{k\in K}

be finite families of real Hilbert spaces, and set

\boldsymbol{\mathcal{H}}=\bigoplus_{i\in I}{\mathcal{H}}_{i}
,

\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}=\bigoplus_{i\in I}{\mathcal{K}}_{i}
, and

\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}=\bigoplus_{k\in K}{\mathcal{G}}_{k}
.
Suppose that the following are satisfied:

[a]

For every
i\in I
,
\varphi_{i}\colon{\mathcal{H}}_{i}\to\left]{-}\infty,{+}\infty\right]
is proper,
lower semicontinuous, and convex,

\alpha_{i}\in\left[0,{+}\infty\right[
, and
\psi_{i}\colon{\mathcal{H}}_{i}\to\mathbb{R}
is convex and
differentiable with an
\alpha_{i}
-Lipschitzian gradient.

[b]

For every
i\in I
,

\boldsymbol{f}_{\!i}\colon\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}\to\mathbb{R}
is such that, for every

\boldsymbol{y}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}
,
\boldsymbol{f}_{\!i}({\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu};\boldsymbol{y}_{\smallsetminus i})\colon{\mathcal{K}}_{i}\to\mathbb{R}

is convex and Gâteaux differentiable,
and we denote its gradient at
y_{i}\in{\mathcal{K}}_{i}
by

\nabla_{\!i}\boldsymbol{f}_{\!i}(\boldsymbol{y})
.
Further, the operator
\boldsymbol{Q}\colon\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}\to\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}\colon\boldsymbol{y}\mapsto(\nabla_{\!i}\boldsymbol{f}_{\!i}(\boldsymbol{y}))_{i\in I}

is monotone and Lipschitzian. Finally,
(\chi_{i})_{i\in I}

are positive numbers such that

(\forall\boldsymbol{y}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}})(\forall\boldsymbol{y}^{\prime}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}})\quad{\langle{{\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{y}^{\prime}}\mid{\boldsymbol{Q}\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{Q}\boldsymbol{y}^{\prime}}}\rangle}\leqslant\sum_{i\in I}\chi_{i}\|y_{i}-y_{i}^{\prime}\|^{2}.

(1.2)

[c]

For every
k\in K
,
g_{k}\colon{\mathcal{G}}_{k}\to\left]{-}\infty,{+}\infty\right]
is proper, lower
semicontinuous, and convex,
\beta_{k}\in\left[0,{+}\infty\right[
, and

h_{k}\colon{\mathcal{G}}_{k}\to\mathbb{R}
is convex and differentiable
with a
\beta_{k}
-Lipschitzian gradient.

[d]

For every
i\in I
and every
k\in K
,

M_{i}\colon{\mathcal{H}}_{i}\to{\mathcal{K}}_{i}
and
L_{k,i}\colon{\mathcal{H}}_{i}\to{\mathcal{G}}_{k}

are linear and bounded, and, for every
\boldsymbol{x}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{H}}
,
we write
\boldsymbol{L}_{k,\smallsetminus i}\boldsymbol{x}_{\smallsetminus i}=\sum_{j\in I\smallsetminus\{i\}}L_{k,j}x_{j}
and

\boldsymbol{M}\boldsymbol{x}=(M_{j}x_{j})_{j\in I}
.

The goal is to

\text{find}\;\>\overline{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{H}}\;\>\text{such that}\;\>(\forall i\in I)\\
\overline{x}_{i}\in\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{i}\in{\mathcal{H}}_{i}\end{subarray}}{\text{\rm Argmin}}\;\;\varphi_{i}(x_{i})+\psi_{i}(x_{i})+\boldsymbol{f}_{\!i}\big(M_{i}x_{i};(\boldsymbol{M}\overline{\boldsymbol{x}})_{\smallsetminus i}\big)+\sum_{k\in K}(g_{k}+h_{k})(L_{k,i}x_{i}+\boldsymbol{L}_{k,\smallsetminus i}\overline{\boldsymbol{x}}_{\smallsetminus i}).

(1.3)

In Problem
1.1
, the individual loss of player
i\in I

consists of a nonsmooth component
\varphi_{i}
and a smooth
component
\psi_{i}
, while his joint loss is decomposed into a
smooth function
\boldsymbol{f}_{\!i}
and a sum of nonsmooth
functions
(g_{k})_{k\in K}
and smooth functions
(h_{k})_{k\in K}

acting on linear mixtures of the strategies. We aim at solving
(
1.3
) with a numerical procedure that can be implemented
in a flexible fashion and that is able to cope with possibly very
large scale problems. This leads us to adopt the following design
principles:

•

Decomposition:

Each function and each linear operator in
Problem
1.1
is activated separately.

•

Block-iterative implementation:

Only a subgroup of functions needs to be
activated at any iteration. This makes it possible to best
modulate and adapt the computational load of each iteration in
large-scale problems.

•

Asynchronous implementation:

The computations are asynchronous in the sense that the result of
calculations initiated at earlier iterations can be incorporated at
the current one.

Our methodology is to first transform (
1.3
) into a system
of monotone set-valued inclusions and then approach it via monotone
operator splitting techniques. Since no splitting
technique tailored to (
1.3
) and compliant with the above
principles appears to be available, we adopt
a fresh perspective hinging on the theory of warped
resolvents
[
9
]
. In Section
2
we provide the
necessary notation and background on monotone operator theory.
Section
3
is devoted to the derivation of the proposed
asynchronous block-iterative algorithm to solve
Problem
1.1
. Application examples are provided in
Section
4
.

\text{\rm zer}\,A=\big\{{x\in{\mathcal{H}}}~|~{0\in Ax}\big\}

A

\big(\forall(x,x^{*})\in\text{\rm gra}\,A\big)\big(\forall(y,y^{*})\in\text{\rm gra}\,A\big)\quad{\langle{{x-y}\mid{x^{*}-y^{*}}}\rangle}\geqslant 0.

(2.1)

Then

\text{\rm gra}\,A\subset\text{\rm gra}\,\widetilde{A}

(\forall x\in\text{\rm dom}\,A)(\forall x^{*}\in\text{\rm ran}\,A)\quad\sup_{(y,y^{*})\in\text{\rm gra}\,A}{\langle{{x-y}\mid{y^{*}-x^{*}}}\rangle}<{{+}\infty}.

(2.2)

\Gamma_{0}({\mathcal{H}})

\lim_{\|x\|\to{{+}\infty}}\varphi(x)/\|x\|={{+}\infty}

\varphi\big(\alpha x+(1-\alpha)y\big)+\alpha(1-\alpha)\phi\big(\|x-y\|\big)\leqslant\alpha\varphi(x)+(1-\alpha)\varphi(y).

(2.3)

For every

,
\text{\rm prox}_{\varphi}x
denotes
the unique minimizer of
\varphi+(1/2)\|{\mkern 2.0mu\cdot\mkern 2.0mu}-x\|^{2}
.
The subdifferential of
\varphi

is the maximally monotone operator

\partial\varphi\colon{\mathcal{H}}\to 2^{{\mathcal{H}}}\colon x\mapsto\big\{{x^{*}\in{\mathcal{H}}}~|~{(\forall y\in{\mathcal{H}})\;{\langle{{y-x}\mid{x^{*}}}\rangle}+\varphi(x)\leqslant\varphi(y)}\big\}

{{+}\infty},&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

3
Algorithm

As mentioned in Section
1
, there exists no method
tailored to the format of Problem
1.1
that can solve it in
an asynchronous block-iterative fashion. Our methodology to design
such an algorithm can be broken down in the following steps:

1.

We rephrase (
1.3
) as a monotone inclusion problem in

\boldsymbol{\mathcal{H}}
, namely,

\text{find}\;\>\overline{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\boldsymbol{\mathcal{H}}\;\>\text{such that}\;\>\boldsymbol{0}\in\boldsymbol{A}\overline{\boldsymbol{x}}+\boldsymbol{M}^{*}\big(\boldsymbol{Q}(\boldsymbol{M}\overline{\boldsymbol{x}})\big)+\boldsymbol{L}^{*}\big(\boldsymbol{B}(\boldsymbol{L}\overline{\boldsymbol{x}})\big),

(3.1)

where
\boldsymbol{Q}
and
\boldsymbol{M}
are defined in
Problem
1.1
[b]
and
Problem
1.1
[d]
, respectively, and

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\boldsymbol{A}\colon\boldsymbol{\mathcal{H}}\to 2^{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{H}}}\colon\boldsymbol{x}\mapsto\bigtimes_{i\in I}\big(\partial\varphi_{i}(x_{i})+\nabla\psi_{i}(x_{i})\big)\\
\displaystyle\boldsymbol{B}\colon\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}\to 2^{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}}\colon\boldsymbol{z}\mapsto\bigtimes_{k\in K}\big(\partial g_{k}(z_{k})+\nabla h_{k}(z_{k})\big)\\
\boldsymbol{L}\colon\boldsymbol{\mathcal{H}}\to\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}\colon\boldsymbol{x}\mapsto\big(\sum_{i\in I}L_{k,i}x_{i}\big)_{k\in K}.\end{cases}

(3.2)

2.

The inclusion in (
3.1
) involves more than two operators,
namely
\boldsymbol{A}
,
\boldsymbol{B}
,
\boldsymbol{Q}
,

\boldsymbol{L}
, and
\boldsymbol{M}
. Hence, in the
spirit of the decomposition methodologies of

[
9
,
12
,
13
]
, a space bigger than
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{H}}
is
required to devise a splitting method to solve it. We set

\boldsymbol{\mathsf{X}}=\boldsymbol{\mathcal{H}}\oplus\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}\oplus\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}\oplus\boldsymbol{\mathcal{K}}\oplus\boldsymbol{\mathcal{G}}
and consider
the inclusion problem

\text{find}\;\>\overline{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}\in\boldsymbol{\mathsf{X}}\;\>\text{such that}\;\>\boldsymbol{\mathsf{0}}\in\boldsymbol{\mathsf{A}}\overline{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}},

(3.3)

where

\boldsymbol{\mathsf{A}}\colon\boldsymbol{\mathsf{X}}\to 2^{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{X}}}\colon(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y},\boldsymbol{z},\boldsymbol{u}^{*},\boldsymbol{v}^{*})\mapsto\\
(\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{x}+\boldsymbol{M}^{*}\boldsymbol{u}^{*}+\boldsymbol{L}^{*}\boldsymbol{v}^{*})\times\{\boldsymbol{Q}\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{u}^{*}\}\times(\boldsymbol{B}\boldsymbol{z}-\boldsymbol{v}^{*})\times\{\boldsymbol{y}-\boldsymbol{M}\boldsymbol{x}\}\times\{\boldsymbol{z}-\boldsymbol{L}\boldsymbol{x}\}.

(3.4)

3.

We show that, if

\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}=(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y},\boldsymbol{z},\boldsymbol{u}^{*},\boldsymbol{v}^{*})
solves
(
3.3
), then
\boldsymbol{x}
solves (
3.1
) and,
therefore, (
1.3
).

4.

To solve (
3.3
), we implement the warped resolvent algorithm
of Proposition
2.2
with a specific choice of the
auxiliary points

(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and the kernels

(\boldsymbol{\mathsf{K}}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
that will lead to an
asynchronous block-iterative splitting algorithm.

The methodology just described is put in motion in our main
theorem, which we now state and prove.

Iterate

1.
Introduction

The moduli spaces
M_{g,n}^{\mathrm{trop}}
of
n
-marked tropical curves of genus
g
and their extended counterparts
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muM\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{g,n}^{\mathrm{trop}}
have been extensively studied in recent years, with particular interest in the relationship between these spaces and the Deligne-Mumford-Knudsen compactifications
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}\subset\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathcal{M}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{g,n}
of the moduli stacks of algebraic curves. Abramovich, Caporaso, and Payne
[
ACP
]
identified the space
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muM\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{g,n}^{\mathrm{trop}}
with the skeleton of the toroidal embedding
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}\subset\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathcal{M}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{g,n}
. Recently, Chan, Galatius, and Payne
[
CGP1
,
CGP2
]
realized the subspace
\Delta_{g,n}\subset M_{g,n}^{\mathrm{trop}}
parameterizing tropical curves of volume one as the dual complex of the boundary divisor
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathcal{M}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{g,n}\smallsetminus\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
, and used this identification to study the top weight rational cohomology of
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
via the topology of
\Delta_{g,n}
. In this paper, we compute the automorphism groups of each of these related tropical moduli spaces for all
g,n\geq 0
such that
3g-3+n>0
. For
n\geq 1
, we set
I_{n}\vcentcolon=\{1,\ldots,n\}

and put
S_{n}\vcentcolon=\mathrm{Perm}(I_{n})
for the group of permutations of
I_{n}
. We also adopt the convention that
S_{0}
is the trivial group.

Theorem 1.1
.

Fix integers
g,n\geq 0
such that
3g-3+n>0
. If
2g-2+n\geq 3
, then

\operatorname{Aut}(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muM\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{g,n}^{\mathrm{trop}})\cong\operatorname{Aut}(M_{g,n}^{\mathrm{trop}})\cong\operatorname{Aut}(\Delta_{g,n})\cong S_{n}.

If
2g-2+n<3
, then
(g,n)\in\{(0,4),(1,1),(1,2)\}
. In these cases we have
\operatorname{Aut}(\Delta_{0,4})\cong S_{3}
while
\operatorname{Aut}(\Delta_{1,1})
and
\operatorname{Aut}(\Delta_{1,2})
are both trivial.

The automorphism groups of
\Delta_{g,n}
,
M_{g,n}^{\mathrm{trop}}
, and
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu{M}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{g,n}^{\mathrm{trop}}
are taken in the categories of
symmetric
\Delta
-complexes
,
generalized cone complexes
, and
extended generalized cone complexes
, respectively. The case
g=0
in Theorem
1.1
is due to Abreu and Pacini
[
AbreuPacini
]
, and our work specializes to give a new proof of their result. With respect to the algebraic moduli spaces, Massarenti
[
Mass
]
has shown that the automorphism group of
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathcal{M}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{g,n}
is isomorphic to
S_{n}
for all
g\geq 0
and
n\geq 1
such that
2g-2+n\geq 3
, while
\operatorname{Aut}(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathcal{M}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{g})
is trivial for
g\geq 2
. The automorphism group of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
was also computed independently by Bruno and Mella in
[
BM
]
. Thus Theorem
1.1
is yet another example of how the combinatorics of the skeleta
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muM\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{g,n}^{\mathrm{trop}}
reflect the geometry of the moduli stacks
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathcal{M}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{g,n}
in a meaningful way.

1.1.
Tropical moduli spaces

The space
M_{g,n}^{\mathrm{trop}}
parameterizes
n
-marked tropical curves of genus
g
. Such a curve is a pair
(\mathbf{G},\ell)
, where
\mathbf{G}
is a stable
n
-marked graph of genus
g
(see Section
2
for a formal definition) and
\ell:E(\mathbf{G})\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
is a function assigning positive real lengths to the edges of
\mathbf{G}
. Thus the building blocks of
M_{g,n}^{\mathrm{trop}}
are quotient spaces of cones by automorphism groups of graphs, i.e. cells of the form

C(\mathbf{G})=\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{E(\mathbf{G})}/\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbf{G}).

It is in this way that
M_{g,n}^{\mathrm{trop}}
is given the structure of a
generalized cone complex
. The tropical moduli space admits a compactification

M_{g,n}^{\mathrm{trop}}\subset\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muM\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{g,n}^{\mathrm{trop}}

by an
extended
generalized cone complex, which is obtained by allowing edge lengths of tropical curves to become infinite. See
[
BMV
,
Caporaso
]
for the construction of
M_{g,n}^{\mathrm{trop}}
,
[
ACP
]
for basics on (extended) generalized cone complexes, and
[
cavalieri2017moduli
]
for foundations on tropical moduli problems. The third combinatorial moduli space in which we are interested is the subspace
\Delta_{g,n}\subset M_{g,n}^{\mathrm{trop}}
parameterizing tropical curves of volume one, where the
volume
of a tropical curve
(\mathbf{G},\ell)
is defined to be

\mathrm{vol}(\mathbf{G},\ell)\vcentcolon=\sum_{e\in E(\mathbf{G})}\ell(e).

Besides its role as a moduli space,
\Delta_{g,n}
is also identified with the dual complex of the normal crossings boundary divisor
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathcal{M}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{g,n}\smallsetminus\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
. The dual complex of a simple normal crossings divisor
D
, first studied by Danilov
[
Danilov
]
, is a
\Delta
-complex which encodes the combinatorial data of the components of
D
and their intersections. The construction of dual complexes can been extended to the generality of normal crossings divisors on Deligne-Mumford stacks
[
ACP
,
CGP1
,
Harper
]
. The resulting combinatorial objects are generalizations of
\Delta
-complexes called
symmetric

\Delta
-complexes. The building blocks of a symmetric
\Delta
-complex are quotient spaces of simplices by subgroups of the symmetric group. In
[
CGP1
]
, it is proven that the category of symmetric
\Delta
-complexes is equivalent to the category of
smooth
generalized cone complexes, as defined in
[
ACP
]
. This fact leads us to the following lemma, which allows us to focus our attention on calculating
\operatorname{Aut}(\Delta_{g,n})
.

Lemma 1.2
.

For all
g,n\geq 0
with
3g-3+n>0
, there are canonical isomorphisms

\operatorname{Aut}(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muM\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{g,n}^{\mathrm{trop}})\cong\operatorname{Aut}(M_{g,n}^{\mathrm{trop}})\cong\operatorname{Aut}(\Delta_{g,n}).

Proof.

It is shown in
[
CGP1
,
CGP2
]
that
\Delta_{g,n}
is identified with the link of the cone point in
M_{g,n}^{\mathrm{trop}}
, and that taking the link gives rise to an equivalence of categories between smooth generalized cone complexes and symmetric
\Delta
-complexes. As a result, we get a canonical isomorphism
\operatorname{Aut}(\Delta_{g,n})\cong\operatorname{Aut}(M_{g,n}^{\mathrm{trop}})
. Any automorphism of
M_{g,n}^{\mathrm{trop}}
extends by linearity to one of
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muM\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{g,n}^{\mathrm{trop}}
, giving a map
\operatorname{Aut}(M_{g,n}^{\mathrm{trop}})\to\operatorname{Aut}(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muM\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{g,n}^{\mathrm{trop}})
. That this map is an isomorphism is worked out when
g=0
by Abreu and Pacini
[
AbreuPacini
]
, and their proof carries over verbatim to the general case.
∎

1.2.
Skeletons of toroidal embeddings

Following Thuillier’s work
[
Thuillier
]
on toroidal schemes, Abramovich, Caporaso, and Payne show in
[
ACP
]
how to associate an extended generalized cone complex
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Sigma\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu(\mathcal{X})
to a toroidal embedding
\mathcal{U}\subset\mathcal{X}
of Deligne-Mumford stacks. Moreover, they show that this construction is functorial with respect to toroidal morphisms of toroidal stacks, and study
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Sigma\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu(\mathcal{X})
when
\mathcal{X}=\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathcal{M}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{g,n}
and
\mathcal{U}=\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
. With respect to this toroidal structure, they show that

\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Sigma\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathcal{M}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{g,n})=\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muM\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{g,n}^{\mathrm{trop}}.

If we put
\operatorname{Aut}^{\mathrm{tor}}(\mathcal{X})
for the group of toroidal automorphisms of the toroidal stack
\mathcal{X}
, the functoriality of the construction of skeletons gives a group homomorphism
\operatorname{Aut}^{\mathrm{tor}}(\mathcal{X})\to\operatorname{Aut}(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Sigma\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu(\mathcal{X}))
. When
\mathcal{X}=\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathcal{M}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{g,n}
, Massarenti’s work gives
a posteriori
that

\operatorname{Aut}(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathcal{M}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{g,n})\cong\operatorname{Aut}^{\mathrm{tor}}(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathcal{M}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{g,n})\cong S_{n}

for
2g-2+n\geq 3
, since
S_{n}
preserves
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
and therefore acts toroidally. This yields a map of groups

(1.3)

S_{n}\to\operatorname{Aut}(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muM\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{g,n}^{\mathrm{trop}}),

and the content of Theorem
1.1
is that this map is an isomorphism.

1.3.
Connection with the complex of curves and Outer space

Suppose
g\geq 2
,
n\geq 0
, and let
\Sigma_{g,n}
be a smooth surface of genus
g
with
n
punctures. Then the
complex of curves

\mathcal{C}(\Sigma_{g,n})
, first considered by Harvey
[
harvey1981boundary
]
, is an abstract simplicial complex with vertices given by the set of free isotopy classes
[\gamma]
of essential simple closed curves on
\Sigma_{g,n}
. There is a
k
-simplex on set of
k+1
vertices in
\mathcal{C}(\Sigma_{g,n})
whenever the corresponding curve classes may be realized disjointly in
\Sigma_{g,n}
. The complex
\mathcal{C}(\Sigma_{g,n})
admits an action of the mapping class group
\mathrm{Mod}_{g,n}
of orientation-preserving self-diffeomorphisms of
\Sigma_{g,n}
modulo isotopy. The
pure
mapping class group
\mathrm{PMod}_{g,n}
is defined as the subgroup of
\mathrm{Mod}_{g,n}
which fixes each puncture individually. Upon choosing an ordering of the punctures, one has a homeomorphism
[
CGP2
]

\mathcal{C}(\Sigma_{g,n})/\mathrm{PMod}_{g,n}\cong\Delta_{g,n},

connecting the study of tropical moduli spaces with curve complexes. In this way our Theorem
1.1
can be compared with the work of Ivanov
[
Ivanov
]
and Luo
[
Luo
]
, who find that the automorphism group of
\mathcal{C}(\Sigma_{g,n})
in the simplicial category is given by the
extended
mapping class group
\mathrm{Mod}^{\pm}_{g,n}
of
all
self-diffeomorphisms modulo isotopy:

\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal{C}(\Sigma_{g,n}))\cong\mathrm{Mod}^{\pm}_{g,n}.

An analogous relationship exists between the moduli space of unmarked tropical curves
\Delta_{g}
and the simplicial completion
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{g}
of the Culler-Vogtmann Outer space
X_{g}

[
CV86
]
. Namely,
\Delta_{g}
is the topological quotient of
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muX\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{g}
by the action of the outer automorphism group
\mathrm{Out}(F_{g})
of the free group on
g
generators. Indeed, our proof of Theorem
1.1
is similar in some aspects to the work of Bridson and Vogtmann
[
bridson2001
]
, who have shown that

\operatorname{Aut}(\mathsf{K}_{g})\cong\mathrm{Out}(F_{g})

for
g\geq 3
, where
\mathsf{K}_{g}
, called the
spine
of Outer space, is an
\mathrm{Out}(F_{g})
-equivariant, simplicial deformation retract of
X_{g}
. See
[
CMV
]
for more on the connection between Outer space and tropical moduli spaces.

2.
Graph categories and the definition of
\Delta_{g,n}

In this section we describe the structure of
\Delta_{g,n}
as a symmetric
\Delta
-complex, via an interlude on graph categories. Our definitions of the categories
\Gamma_{g}
and
\Gamma_{g,n}
are equivalent to those in
[
CGP1
,
CGP2
]
; the few differences in presentation are purely superficial. Discussions of stable graphs of genus
g
and the morphisms between them have become standard in the study of moduli spaces of curves
[
Arbarello
,
Getzler
,
Kontsevich
]
. Similar categories have recently been studied in a representation-theoretic context by Proudfoot and Ramos
[
PR1
,
PR2
]
.

2.1.
Graph categories.

By a
graph

G
we mean a finite set
X(G)=V(G)\sqcup H(G)
of
vertices
and
half-edges
, together with a pair of maps
s_{G},r_{G}:X(G)\to X(G)
with
s_{G}^{2}=\mathrm{Id}
,
r_{G}^{2}=r_{G}
, and such that

\{x\in X(G)\mid r_{G}(x)=x\}=\{x\in X(G)\mid s_{G}(x)=x\}=V(G).

The function
r_{G}
sends half-edges to their incident vertex, while
s_{G}
couples half-edges. A graph
G
has a
geometric realization

|G|
which is a finite one-dimensional CW complex; we only work with
connected
graphs, which are those whose geometric realization is connected as a topological space. The
genus

b^{1}(G)
of a graph is the first Betti number of the space
|G|
. An
edge
of
G
is an element of the quotient set

E(G)\vcentcolon=H(G)/\{s_{G}(x)\sim x\}.

Then we have

b^{1}(G)=|E(G)|-|V(G)|+1,

and

2|E(G)|=|H(G)|.

Note that our definition allows for
loops
, which are those elements
e\in E(G)
such that
s_{G}
is constant on the half-edges
h_{1},h_{2}\in H(G)
, where
h_{1},h_{2}
are the two half edges of
e
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
weighted graph of genus
g
is a pair
(G,w)
where
G
is a connected graph, and
w:V(G)\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
is a function, such that

b^{1}(G)+\sum_{v\in V(G)}w(v)=g.

We will make a category
\mathsf{Graph}_{g}
whose objects are weighted graphs of genus
g
. The morphisms will consist of isomorphisms and edge-contractions.

Definition 2.2
.

An
isomorphism

\varphi:(G,w)\to(G^{\prime},w^{\prime})
of weighted graphs of genus
g
is given by a bijection
\varphi:X(G)\to X(G)
such that

•

r_{G}^{\prime}\circ\varphi=\varphi\circ r_{G}
,

•

s_{G}^{\prime}\circ\varphi=\varphi\circ s_{G}
, and

•

w^{\prime}(\varphi(v))=w(v)
for all
v\in V(G)
.

Definition 2.3
.

Given
(G,w)
a weighted graph of genus genus
g
and an edge
e\in E(G)
, the
edge-contraction

(G/e,w_{e})
is a weighted graph
(G/e,w_{e})
, together with a surjective map
c_{e}:X(G)\to X(G/e)
, which is determined as follows.

•

There is an identification

H(G/e)=H(G)\smallsetminus\{h_{1},h_{2}\},

where
h_{1},h_{2}
are the two half-edges uniquely determining
e
. Using this identification, set

{s_{G/e}}_{|_{H(G/e)}}={s_{G}}_{|_{H(G/e)}}.

•

If
e
is a loop, we set
V(G/e)=V(G)
,

{r_{G/e}}_{|_{H(G/e)}}={r_{G}}_{|_{H(G/e)}}.

We also put

w_{e}(v)=w(v)+1

if
v=r_{G}(h_{1})=r_{G}(h_{2})
,
and
w_{e}(v)=w(v)
otherwise.

•

If
e
is a non-loop edge, then we set

V(G/e)=(V(G)\smallsetminus\{r_{G}(h_{1}),r_{G}(h_{2})\})\cup\{\hat{v}\}.

Using this identification, we set

{r_{G/e}}(h)=\begin{cases}r_{G}(h)&\text{if }r_{G}(h)\notin\{r_{G}(h_{1}),r_{G}(h_{2})\}\\
\hat{v}&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

when
h\in H(G/e)=H(G)\smallsetminus\{h_{1},h_{2}\}
. We also put
w_{e}(v)=w(v)
if
v\in V(G)
, and we set

w_{e}(\hat{v})=w(r_{G}(h_{1}))+w(r_{G}(h_{2})).

The graph
G/e
is equipped with a surjective map

c_{e}:X(G)\to X(G/e),

called the
contraction map
, which satisfies

c_{e}(h_{1})=c_{e}(h_{2})=c_{e}(r_{G}(h_{1}))=c_{e}(r_{G}(h_{2}))=\hat{v},

and
c_{e}(x)=x
otherwise.

Informally, the pair
(G/e,w_{e})
is obtained from
(G,w)
by contracting the edge
e
to a point, and adjusting weight of the new vertex so that
(G/e,w_{e})
is still a weighted graph of genus
g
, i.e. adding weight one if
e
is a loop, and adding the two weights coming from the endpoints of
e
otherwise.

We are now ready to define the category
\mathsf{Graph}_{g}
.

Definition 2.4
.

The category
\mathsf{Graph}_{g}
has as its objects weighted graphs
(G,w)
of genus
g
, and morphisms
(G,w)\to(G^{\prime},w^{\prime})
given by maps of sets
X(G)\to X(G^{\prime})
which factor as compositions of isomorphisms and contraction maps.

We note that the processes of passing to sets of half-edges or edges are both contravariant with respect to morphisms in
\mathsf{Graph}_{g}
.

Definition 2.5
.

We define functors

H,E:\mathsf{Graph}_{g}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathsf{Set}

on objects by taking sets of half-edges and edges, respectively. Given a morphism
f:G\to G^{\prime}
and
h^{\prime}\in H(G^{\prime})
, there is a unique element
h\in H(G)
such that
f(h)=h^{\prime}
, and we set
H(f)(h^{\prime})=h
. Similarly, an edge
e^{\prime}\in E(G^{\prime})
has a unique preimage
e\in E(G)
, and we set
E(f)(e^{\prime})=e
.

Given a morphism
f:(G,w)\to(G^{\prime},w^{\prime})
of weighted graphs of genus
g
, we use the notation
f^{*}=E(f):E(G^{\prime})\to E(G)
. The category

has infinitely many isomorphism classes of objects. We pass to a category

which has only finitely many isomorphism classes via the algebro-geometric stability condition.

Definition 2.6
.

We say a weighted graph
(G,w)
of genus
g
is
stable
if for all
v\in V(G)
, we have

2w(v)-2+\operatorname{val}(v)>0,

where

\operatorname{val}(v)=|\{h\in H(G)\mid r_{G}(h)=v\}|.

Equivalently,
\operatorname{val}(v)\geq 3
whenever
w(v)=0
and
\operatorname{val}(v)\geq 1
whenever
w(v)=1
. We put
\Gamma_{g}
for the full subcategory of
\mathsf{Graph}_{g}
determined by the stable graphs; the objects of
\Gamma_{g}
are called
stable weighted graphs of genus
g
.

Note that
\Gamma_{g}
contains objects only when
g\geq 2
. We use the boldface notation
\mathbf{G}=(G,w)
for the objects of
\Gamma_{g}
, reserving the notation
(G,w)
for those instances where it is not required that the object we are working with is stable. We now define a marked analogue
\Gamma_{g,n}
of the category
\Gamma_{g}
. Recall that we have set
I_{n}=\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

.

Suppose that

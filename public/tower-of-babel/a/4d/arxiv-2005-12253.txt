Finiteness of log abundant log canonical pairs in log minimal model program with scaling
Kenta Hashizume

Abstract
. We study relations between the property of being log abundant for lc pairs and the termination of log MMP with scaling. We prove that any log MMP with scaling of an ample divisor starting with a projective dlt pair contains only finitely many log abundant dlt pairs. We also discuss log MMP for slc pairs.

1.
Introduction

Throughout this paper we will work over the complex number field
\mathbb{C}
(see Section
5
for generalization to varieties over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero).

In this paper, we study the minimal model theory for log abundant lc pairs, that is, lc pairs with the property of being log abundant (see Definition
2.16
).
In the minimal model theory, the properties of being abundant and being log abundant for lc pairs are closely related to the existence of good minimal models.
These two properties coincide when we deal with klt pairs.

It is well-known that the log MMP preserves the property of being abundant.
Hence, for any lc pair
(X,\Delta)
, it is well-known that the divisor
K_{X}+\Delta
, which we call the log canonical divisor, is abundant if the minimal model theory holds for
(X,\Delta)
.
In the klt case, the main result of Birkar–Cascini–Hacon–M
c
Kernan
[
3
]
and the ideas established by Lai
[
37
]
(see also
[
33
]
and
[
19
, Theorem 4.3]
) show that the converse also holds.

On the other hand, the log MMP does not necessarily preserve the property of being log abundant.
For example, the situations of the main results of
[
2
]
and
[
22
]
(cf.
[
26
]
) are the cases where the log MMP preserves the property of being log abundant after finitely many steps (see Remark
3.7
), whereas the situation of
[
5
, Example 5.2]
(see also
[
1
]
) is the case where the log MMP breaks the property of being log big, a special kind of the property of being log abundant. However, as an application of
[
14
, Theorem 4.11.5]
and the relation between the property of being abundant and the minimal model theory stated above, we know that the log canonical divisors of all lc pairs are log abundant if and only if the minimal model theory holds for all lc pairs.

Because induction on the dimension of varieties works well under the assumption of the property of being log abundant, the abundance conjecture for log abundant lc pairs was thoroughly studied (
[
42
]
,
[
17
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
23
]
) and proved by Hacon–Xu
[
23
]
(see also
[
16
]
by Fujino–Gongyo for projective case).
In contrast, we know relations between the log MMP and the property of being log abundant only in special cases (
[
2
]
,
[
22
]
,
[
26
]
,
[
32
]
).

In this paper, we study how the property of being log abundant affects the termination of the log MMP.
The following theorem is the main result of this paper.

Theorem 1.1

(=Theorem
3.6
)
.

Let
\pi\colon X\to Z
be a projective morphism of normal quasi-projective varieties and let
(X,\Delta)
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial dlt pair such that
K_{X}+\Delta
is pseudo-effective over
Z
.
Let
A
be a
\pi
-ample
\mathbb{R}
-divisor on
X
such that
(X,\Delta+A)
is lc and
K_{X}+\Delta+A
is
\pi
-nef.
Then there is an integer
l\geq 0
satisfying the following:
Let

(X_{0}:=X,\Delta_{0}:=\Delta)\dashrightarrow(X_{1},\Delta_{1})\dashrightarrow\cdots\dashrightarrow(X_{i},\Delta_{i})\dashrightarrow\cdots

be a sequence of steps of a
(K_{X}+\Delta)
-MMP over
Z
with scaling of
A
.
If
(X_{i},\Delta_{i})
is log abundant over
Z
for some
i\geq l
, then the
(K_{X}+\Delta)
-MMP over
Z
terminates with a good minimal model of
(X,\Delta)
over
Z
.

Corollary 1.2

(=Corollary
3.8
)
.

Any log MMP with scaling of an ample divisor starting with a projective dlt pair contains only finitely many log abundant dlt pairs.

Although the case of klt pairs of Theorem
1.1
is known by
[
3
]
and
[
37
]
as mentioned above, Theorem
1.1
can be applied to the situations of
[
2
]
,
[
22
]
,
[
26
]
, and
[
32
]
, and in addition
[
27
, Theorem 1.3]
(see Remark
3.7
).
Thus, Theorem
1.1
is the unification of those results as well as a relation between the log MMP and the property of being log abundant.
Moreover, Theorem
1.1
shows the existence of a good minimal model in the following new case.

Corollary 1.3

(=Corollary
3.10
)
.

Let
\pi\colon X\to Z
be a projective morphism of normal quasi-projective varieties and let
(X,\Delta)
be an lc pair.
Suppose that

•

(X,\Delta)
is log abundant over
Z
and
K_{X}+\Delta
is pseudo-effective over
Z
, and

•

the stable base locus of
K_{X}+\Delta
over
Z

(
[
3

)
does not contain the image of any prime divisor
P
over
X
whose discrepancy
a(P,X,\Delta)
is negative.

Then
(X,\Delta)
has a good minimal model over
Z
.

The second condition in Corollary
1.3
holds, for example, if
(X,{\rm Supp}\,\Delta)
is log smooth and there is an effective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
E\sim_{\mathbb{R},Z}K_{X}+\Delta
such that the support of
E
does not contain any lc center of
(X,{\rm Supp}\,\Delta)
.

Corollary

has a generalization to slc pairs (Theorem

For the generalization, the result by Ambro–Kollár
[

plays a crucial role.
In general, we cannot always run a log MMP for slc pairs.
Nevertheless,
[

enable us to run a log MMP for slc pairs terminating with a good minimal model in a similar situation to Corollary

By applying Theorem

Furthermore, by applying Theorem

We now explain the ideas of the proofs of Theorem
1.1
and Corollary
1.2
.
We will first prove Corollary
1.2
(in fact, we will prove Theorem
3.5
, the lc and relative setting of Corollary
1.2
).
Suppose by contradiction that there exist
a projective dlt pair
(X,\Delta)
and
an infinite sequence of steps of a
(K_{X}+\Delta)
-MMP with scaling of an ample divisor which contains infinitely many log abundant dlt pairs.
In particular, the log MMP does not terminate, hence
K_{X}+\Delta
is pseudo-effective by
[
3
]
.
We will prove the existence of a log minimal model of
(X,\Delta)
to get a contradiction with
[
2
, Theorem 4.1]
.
By the special termination (
[
10
]
), replacing
(X,\Delta)
, we may assume that
(X,\Delta)
is log abundant and the restrictions of
K_{X}+\Delta
to all lc centers of
(X,\Delta)
are nef.
Despite of the two properties, it is still difficult to prove the existence of a log minimal model of
(X,\Delta)
, as shown in the following example.

Example 1.4

(
[
5
, Example 5.2]
)
.

Fix a smooth projective variety
S
with
\kappa(S,K_{S})\geq 0
.
With notation as in
[
5
, Example 5.2]
, we can pick
\epsilon\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and an ample Cartier divisor
H
so that
K_{S}+\epsilon H
is ample.
Then the projective dlt pair
(X,B)
in
[
5
, Example 5.2]
is log abundant and the restrictions of
K_{X}+B
to all lc centers of
(X,B)
are nef.
Moreover, if
(X,B)
has a log minimal model, then so does
(S,0)
.
From this, the existence of log minimal models for projective dlt pairs with the above two properties is more difficult than that for smooth projective varieties with non-negative Kodaira dimension.

Hence, the two properties stated above are insufficient for our proof.
One more key property is that the non-nef locus of
K_{X}+\Delta
is disjoint from any lc center of
(X,\Delta)
.
The reduction with the special termination (
[
10
]
) allows us to assume these three properties, and therefore Corollary
1.2
can be reduced to the following statement, which derives a contradiction, as discussed above.

Theorem 1.5

(=Theorem
3.4
)
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a projective dlt pair.
Suppose that

•

K_{X}+\Delta
is pseudo-effective and abundant,

•

for any lc center
S
of
(X,\Delta)
, the restriction
(K_{X}+\Delta)|_{S}
is nef, and

•

\sigma_{P}(K_{X}+\Delta)=0
for every prime divisor
P
over
X
such that
a(P,X,\Delta)<0
and
c_{X}(P)
intersects an lc center of
(X,\Delta)
, where
\sigma_{P}(\,\cdot\,)
is the asymptotic vanishing order of
P
defined in Definition
2.1
.

Then
(X,\Delta)
has a log minimal model.

has not been mentioned in the papers related to Theorem

]

[

[

]

[

This condition enables us to unify the hypotheses of the results in

[

We will prove Theorem

]

[

]

[

]
,
[

]
, and
[

]
.
The third condition of Theorem

We will prove Theorem
1.1
by using Theorem

.
Fix a projective morphism
X\to Z
, a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial dlt pair

, and a relatively ample
\mathbb{R}
-divisor

By using the finiteness of models (

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we collect definitions and some results.

2.1.
Divisors, morphisms, and singularities of pairs

We collect notations and definitions on divisors, morphisms and singularities of pairs. We also show some lemmas.

Divisors and maps
.

Let
\pi\colon X\to Z
be a projective morphism from a normal variety to a variety.
We will use the standard definitions of
\pi
-nef
\mathbb{R}
-divisor,
\pi
-ample
\mathbb{R}
-divisor,
\pi
-semi-ample
\mathbb{R}
-divisor, and
\pi
-pseudo-effective
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier divisor.
All
\pi
-big
\mathbb{R}
-divisors in this paper are assumed to be
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier.

For an
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
D=\sum_{i}a_{i}D_{i}
on a variety
X
, where
D_{i}
are distinct prime divisors, an
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
\llcorner D\lrcorner
is defined by
\llcorner D\lrcorner=\sum_{i}\llcorner a_{i}\lrcorner D_{i}
, where
\llcorner a_{i}\lrcorner
is the largest integer not exceeding
a_{i}
.
For a morphism
f\colon X\to Y
of varieties and an
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier divisor
D^{\prime}
on
Y
, we sometimes denote
f^{*}D^{\prime}
by
D^{\prime}|_{X}
.
For any prime divisor
P
over
X
, the image of
P
on
X
is denoted by
c_{X}(P)
.

A projective morphism
f\colon X\to Y
of varieties is called a
contraction
if
f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{Y}
.
For a variety
X
and an
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
D
on
X
, a
log resolution of

(X,D)
is a projective birational morphism
f\colon Y\to X
from a smooth variety
Y
such that the exceptional locus
{\rm Ex}(f)
of
f
is pure codimension one and
{\rm Ex}(f)\cup{\rm Supp}\,f_{*}^{-1}D
is a simple normal crossing divisor.

A birational map
\phi\colon X\dashrightarrow X^{\prime}
of varieties is called a
birational contraction
if
\phi^{-1}
does not contract any divisor.
We say that
\phi
is
small
if
\phi
and
\phi^{-1}
are birational contractions.

Asymptotic vanishing order
.

We define asymptotic vanishing order for
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier divisors, and we collect some basic properties.

Definition 2.1

(Asymptotic vanishing order)
.

Let
X
be a normal projective variety, and let
D
be a pseudo-effective
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier divisor on
X
.
Let
P
be a prime divisor over
X
.
We define the
asymptotic vanishing order
of
P
with respect to
D
, denoted by
\sigma_{P}(D)
, as follows:
We take a projective birational morphism
f\colon Y\to X
so that
P
appears as a prime divisor on
Y
.
When
D
is big, we define
\sigma_{P}(D)
by

\sigma_{P}(D)={\rm inf}\!\set{{\rm coeff}_{P}(D^{\prime})}{f^{*}D\sim_{\mathbb{R}}D^{\prime}\geq 0}.

When
D
is not necessarily big,
\sigma_{P}(D)
is defined by

\sigma_{P}(D)=\underset{\epsilon\to 0+}{\rm lim}\sigma_{P}(D+\epsilon A)

for an ample
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
A
on
X
.

By definition,
\sigma_{P}(D)
is independent of
f\colon Y\to X
, and
\sigma_{P}(D)\geq 0
.
Moreover,
\sigma_{P}(D)
does not depend on
A
(
[
41
, III, 1.5 (2) Lemma]
) or the numerical equivalence class of
D
.
We can easily check
\sigma_{P}(D)={\rm sup}\!\set{\sigma_{P}(D+H)}{H\text{ is ample}}
.

Definition 2.2

(Nakayama–Zariski decomposition)
.

For
X
and
D
as in Definition
2.1
, the
negative part of the Nakayama–Zariski decomposition
of
D
, denoted by
N_{\sigma}(D)
, is defined by

N_{\sigma}(D)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P:{\rm\,prime\,divisor}\\
{\rm on\,}X\end{subarray}}\sigma_{P}(D)P.

We call the divisor
D-N_{\sigma}(D)
the
positive part of the Nakayama–Zariski decomposition
of
D
.
Note that
N_{\sigma}(D)
and
D-N_{\sigma}(D)
are not necessarily
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier in this paper.

When
X
is smooth, the definition of
N_{\sigma}(D)
coincides with
[
41
, III, 1.12 Definition]
.
When
X
is not necessarily smooth,
N_{\sigma}(D)
in the definition can be written as
g_{*}N_{\sigma}(g^{*}D)
where
g\colon W\to X
is a resolution and
N_{\sigma}(g^{*}D)
is defined in
[
41
, III, 1.12 Definition]
.
Hence
N_{\sigma}(D)
is well-defined as an
\mathbb{R}
-divisor.
For the Nakayama–Zariski decomposition in the case of singular varieties, see also
[
4
, Section 4]
.

We write down some properties of the asymptotic vanishing order and the negative part of the Nakayama–Zariski decomposition.

Remark 2.3
.

Let
X
be a normal projective variety,
D
a pseudo-effective
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier divisor on
X
, and let
P
be a prime divisor over
X
.

(1)

(Lower convexity, see
[
41
, III, 1.6 Definition]
).
For any pseudo-effective
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier divisor
D^{\prime}
on
X
, we have

\sigma_{P}(D+D^{\prime})\leq\sigma_{P}(D)+\sigma_{P}(D^{\prime}).

(2)

(
[
41
, III, 1.7 (2) Lemma]
).
For any pseudo-effective
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier divisor
G
on
X
, we have

\underset{\epsilon\to 0+}{\rm lim}\sigma_{P}(D+\epsilon G)=\sigma_{P}(D).

(3)

(
[
41
, III, 5.14 Lemma]
).
Let
f\colon Y\to X
be a projective birational morphism from a normal variety
Y
and let
E
be an effective
f
-exceptional
\mathbb{R}
-divisor on
Y
.
If
f^{*}D+E
is
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier (in this situation
E
is also
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier), then

\sigma_{P}(f^{*}D+E)=\sigma_{P}(f^{*}D)+{\rm ord}_{P}(E).

(4)

(cf.
[
41
, III, 5.16 Theorem]
).
Let
f\colon Y\to X
be a projective birational morphism from a normal variety
Y
and let
D^{\prime\prime}
be an
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier divisor on
X
.
If
D^{\prime\prime}\leq N_{\sigma}(D)
, then

N_{\sigma}(f^{*}D)=f^{*}D^{\prime\prime}+F

for an effective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
F
on
Y
such that
D^{\prime\prime}+f_{*}F=N_{\sigma}(D)
.

We outline the proof of (
4
).
Let
g\colon W\to Y
be a resolution of
Y
.
Since

N_{\sigma}(f^{*}D)-f^{*}D^{\prime\prime}=g_{*}N_{\sigma}(g^{*}f^{*}D)-f^{*}D^{\prime\prime}=g_{*}(N_{\sigma}(g^{*}f^{*}D)-g^{*}f^{*}D^{\prime\prime}),

we may replace
f
by
f\circ g
, and therefore we may assume that
Y
is smooth.
We can write
N_{\sigma}(f^{*}D)-f^{*}D^{\prime\prime}=G_{+}-G_{-}
, where
G_{+}\geq 0
and
G_{-}\geq 0
have no common components.
Then
G_{-}
is
f
-exceptional since
D^{\prime\prime}\leq N_{\sigma}(D)
.
On the other hand, we have

G_{+}-G_{-}\sim_{\mathbb{R},X}N_{\sigma}(f^{*}D)\sim_{\mathbb{R},X}N_{\sigma}(f^{*}D)-f^{*}D=-P_{\sigma}(f^{*}D).

Hence
-(G_{+}-G_{-})
is the limit of movable divisors over
X
.
This implies
G_{-}=0
(see, for example,
[
2
, Lemma 3.3]
).
So
F:=G_{+}
is the desired divisor.

In Remark
2.9
, we will discuss further properties of the asymptotic vanishing order.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
X
be a normal projective variety.
Let
D
and
D^{\prime}
be pseudo-effective
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier divisors on
X
.
Then there exists a real number
t_{0}>0
such that the support of
N_{\sigma}(D+tD^{\prime})
does not depend on
t
for any
t\in(0,t_{0}]
.

Proof.

By Remark
2.3
(
1
), the components of
N_{\sigma}(D+tD^{\prime})
are those of
N_{\sigma}(D)+N_{\sigma}(D^{\prime})
for any
t\in(0,1]
.
Let
D_{1},\cdots,D_{k}
be all components of
N_{\sigma}(D)+N_{\sigma}(D^{\prime})
, and we consider the set

J_{i}:=\{s\in(0,1]\,|\,\sigma_{D_{i}}(D+sD^{\prime})=0\}

for all
1\leq i\leq k
.

Fix
i
.
When
J_{i}
is not empty and
{\rm inf}\,J_{i}=0
, we pick an element
s_{i}
of
J_{i}
.
In this case, for every
s\in(0,s_{i}]
we can find real numbers
s^{\prime},s^{\prime\prime}\in(0,s_{i}]
such that
s\in[s^{\prime},s^{\prime\prime}]
and
\sigma_{D_{i}}(D+s^{\prime}D^{\prime})=\sigma_{D_{i}}(D+s^{\prime\prime}D^{\prime})=0
.
Hence we have
\sigma_{D_{i}}(D+sD^{\prime})=0
for all
s\in(0,s_{i}]
by Remark
2.3
(
1
).
We put
s_{i}=1
when
J_{i}
is empty, and we put
s_{i}=\frac{1}{2}{\rm inf}\,J_{i}
when
J_{i}
is not empty and
{\rm inf}\,J_{i}>0
, then
\sigma_{D_{i}}(D+sD^{\prime})>0
for all
s\in(0,s_{i}]
.
In this way, we can find
s_{i}\in(0,1]
such that
\sigma_{D_{i}}(D+sD^{\prime})=0
for all
s\in(0,s_{i}]
or
\sigma_{D_{i}}(D+sD^{\prime})>0
for all
s\in(0,s_{i}]
.

We set
t_{0}={\rm min}\{s_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq k}
.
By definition of
D_{1},\cdots,D_{k}

Lemma 2.24

Lemma 2.25

(cf.
[
21
, Lemma 5.3]
)
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a projective lc pair, and let
(Y,\Gamma)
be an lc pair with a projective birational morphism

Suppose that

has a log minimal model (resp. a good minimal model) if and only if

Proof.

Let
\Delta_{Y}
be an
\mathbb{R}
-divisor defined by

.
Then the hypothesis on discrepancies is equivalent to

Thus we have
\Delta_{Y}\geq 0
.
By Lemma

, we may replace
(X,\Delta)

Therefore we may assume

Pick any resolution

.
By Remark

is pseudo-effective.

\begin{split}N_{\sigma}(g^{*}(K_{X}+\Gamma))&=N_{\sigma}\bigl(g^{*}(K_{X}+\Delta)-g^{*}((K_{X}+\Delta)-(K_{X}+\Gamma))\bigr)\\

5.
Varieties over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero

In this section, we give a remark on generalizations of results of this paper to varieties over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero.

We go back to the definitions of the invariant Iitaka dimension (Definition
2.13
) and the numerical dimension (Definition
2.14
).
Let
k
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero.
For any normal projective variety
X
over
k
and
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier divisor
D
on
X
, we can define
\kappa_{\iota}(X,D)
and
\kappa_{\sigma}(X,D)
by the same way as in Definition
2.13
and Definition
2.14
, respectively.
A problem comes up when we define the relative invariant Iitaka dimension and the relative numerical dimension.
For a given projective morphism from a normal variety to a variety and an
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier divisor, the relative invariant Iitaka dimension and the relative numerical dimension are defined with a sufficiently general fiber of the Stein factorization of the morphism.
However, when
k
is countable, we cannot always find a sufficiently general closed point, so sufficiently general fibers do not always exist.
Therefore, when the base field is not necessarily the complex number field
\mathbb{C}
, we define the relative invariant Iitaka dimension and the relative numerical dimension as follows.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
X\to Z
be a projective morphism from a normal variety to a variety over an algebraically closed field
{{k}}
of characteristic zero, and let
X_{\bar{\eta}}
be the geometric generic fiber of the Stein factorization of
X\to Z
.
Let
D
be an
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier divisor on
X
.
We define the
relative invariant Iitaka dimension
of
D
over
Z
, denoted by
\hat{\kappa}_{\iota}(X/Z,D)
, by
\kappa_{\iota}(X_{\bar{\eta}},D|_{X_{\bar{\eta}}})
.
Similarly, we define the
relative numerical dimension
of
D
over
Z
, denoted by
\hat{\kappa}_{\sigma}(X/Z,D)
, by
\kappa_{\sigma}(X_{\bar{\eta}},D|_{X_{\bar{\eta}}})
.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
\pi\colon X\to Z
be a projective morphism from a normal variety to a variety over an algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic zero.
For any
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier divisor
D
on
X
, we say that
D
is
\pi
-
abundant
(or
abundant over

Z
) if the equality
\hat{\kappa}_{\iota}(X/Z,D)=\hat{\kappa}_{\sigma}(X/Z,D)
holds.

With Definition
5.2
, for any lc pair
(X,\Delta)\to Z
and any
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier divisor
D
on
X
, we can define the property of being log abundant over
Z
.

When
{{k}}=\mathbb{C}
and the base variety of a morphism is quasi-projective, the following lemma shows that the new definitions of the relative invariant Iitaka dimension and the relative numerical dimension coincide with those of Definition
2.13
and Definition
2.14
, respectively.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\pi\colon X\to Z
be a projective morphism from a normal variety to a variety over
\mathbb{C}
, and let
D
be an
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier divisor on
X
.
If
Z
is quasi-projective, then
\hat{\kappa}_{\iota}(X/Z,D)=\kappa_{\iota}(X/Z,D)
and
\hat{\kappa}_{\sigma}(X/Z,D)=\kappa_{\sigma}(X/Z,D)
.

Proof.

The equality
\hat{\kappa}_{\sigma}(X/Z,D)=\kappa_{\sigma}(X/Z,D)
follows from Definition
2.14
, the generic flatness, the semi-continuity of dimension of cohomology for flat coherent sheaves, and the flat base change theorem for cohomology.
By definition,
\kappa_{\iota}(X/Z,D)\geq 0
if and only if
D\sim_{\mathbb{R},Z}E
for some
E\geq 0
.

We show that the latter condition is equivalent to
\hat{\kappa}_{\iota}(X/Z,D)\geq 0
.
It is obvious that the existence of an effective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
E
such that
D\sim_{\mathbb{R},Z}E
implies
\hat{\kappa}_{\iota}(X/Z,D)\geq 0
.
Conversely, suppose that
\hat{\kappa}_{\iota}(X/Z,D)\geq 0
.
Then, by the standard argument of convex geometry, we can find
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisors
D_{1},\cdots,D_{l}
, positive real numbers
r_{1},\cdots,r_{l}
, and effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisors
\bar{E}_{1},\cdots,\bar{E}_{l}
on
X_{\bar{\eta}}
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{l}r_{i}=1
,
\sum_{i=1}^{l}r_{i}D_{i}=D
, and

D_{i}|_{X_{\bar{\eta}}}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}\bar{E}_{i}

for each
1\leq i\leq l
(see, for example,
[
32
, Proof of Lemma 2.10]
).
Since
D_{i}
are
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier, by the flat base change theorem for cohomology, the restriction of any
D_{i}
to the generic fiber of
\pi
is
\mathbb{Q}
-linearly equivalent to an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor.
By the quasi-projectivity of
Z
, for every
1\leq i\leq l
we can find
E_{i}\geq 0
such that
D_{i}\sim_{\mathbb{Q},Z}E_{i}
.
Then

D=\sum_{i=1}^{l}r_{i}D_{i}\sim_{\mathbb{R},Z}\sum_{i=1}^{l}r_{i}E_{i}.

Since all
r_{i}
are positive, the right hand side is effective.
In this way,
\hat{\kappa}_{\iota}(X/Z,D)\geq 0
implies the existence of an effective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor

such that
D\sim_{\mathbb{R},Z}E
.

The above discussion shows that
\kappa_{\iota}(X/Z,D)\geq 0
is equivalent to
\hat{\kappa}_{\iota}(X/Z,D)\geq 0
.
When
\kappa_{\iota}(X/Z,D)\geq 0
, since there is an effective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor

, the equality
\hat{\kappa}_{\iota}(X/Z,D)=\kappa_{\iota}(X/Z,D)
directly follows.
From these discussions, we have
\hat{\kappa}_{\sigma}(X/Z,D)=\kappa_{\sigma}(X/Z,D)
and
\hat{\kappa}_{\iota}(X/Z,D)=\kappa_{\iota}(X/Z,D)
.
We finish the proof.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

Lemma

\mathbb{Q}

On the other hand, when

Thanks to Lemma

Then, all the results of this paper hold for varieties over an algebraically closed field

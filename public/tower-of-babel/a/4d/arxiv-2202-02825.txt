On the behavior of Kodaira dimension under smooth morphisms
Mihnea Popa, Christian Schnell

Abstract
. We prove several results on the additivity of Kodaira dimension under smooth morphisms of smooth projective varieties.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
14D06, 14J10, 14D07

A.
Introduction

The purpose of this paper is to prove some of the conjectures in
[
Popa
]
about the
additivity of the Kodaira dimension under smooth morphisms between smooth projective
varieties. Our results are unconditional when varieties of general type are involved
in some way, or when the base space is either of dimension
\leq 3
or a good minimal
model of Kodaira dimension zero; for other varieties, we need a version of the
non-vanishing conjecture due to Campana and Peternell
[
CP
]
.
We use the convention that an
algebraic fiber space
is a surjective morphism

f\colon X\to Y
with connected fibers between two smooth projective varieties
X

and
Y
(over the field of complex numbers). We usually denote the general fiber by
the letter
F
. We say that an algebraic fiber space is
smooth
if
f
is a
smooth morphism.

A particular case of Conjectures 2.1 or 3.1 in
[
Popa
]
predicts that if
f\colon X\to Y
is a smooth algebraic fiber space, and
X
is of general type, then
Y
is of
general type as well. Our first result is the following more general statement:

Theorem A
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a smooth algebraic fiber space whose general fiber
F

satisfies
\kappa(F)\geq 0
. Then
Y
is of general type if and only if

\kappa(X)=\kappa(F)+\dim Y
.

By the Easy Addition formula
[
Mori
, Cor. 1.7]
, we always have the elementary
inequality
\kappa(X)\leq\kappa(F)+\dim Y
. Also recall that Iitaka’s
conjecture, to the effect that
\kappa(X)\geq\kappa(F)+\kappa(Y)
, is known when

Y
is of general type
[
Viehweg1
, Cor. IV]
. One half of the theorem is therefore
already known. Our contribution is to prove the other half: the identity

\kappa(X)=\kappa(F)+\dim Y
implies that
Y
is of general type.

Note
.

Using the same techniques, one can show that if
f\colon X\to Y
is an algebraic
fiber space with
Y
not uniruled,
\kappa(F)\geq 0
, and
\kappa(X)=\kappa(F)+\dim Y
, and if
V\subseteq Y
denotes the smooth locus of
f
, then
V
is of
log general type. This answers positively another question suggested by
[
Popa
]
, when
Y
is not
uniruled, but is weaker than
[
Popa
, Conj. 3.1]
. See also
Remark
1
.

Our next result deals with smooth algebraic fiber spaces with canonically polarized fibers.

Theorem B
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a smooth algebraic fiber space such that each fiber
F

is canonically polarized. If
\kappa(Y)\geq 0
, or if
Y
is uniruled, then we have

\kappa(X)=\kappa(F)+\kappa(Y)
.

For technical reasons, the case where
\kappa(Y)=-\infty
and
Y
is not uniruled
is missing; note however that a well-known conjecture in birational geometry, called
the
non-vanishing conjecture
, predicts that
\kappa(Y)=-\infty
is in fact equivalent to
Y
being uniruled.
Thus up to this prediction, our theorem implies the additivity of the Kodaira dimension for any smooth algebraic fiber space with fibers of this type. Unlike the other results in this paper,
besides hyperbolicity-type techniques,
Theorem
B
also relies on the existence and properties of moduli spaces of varieties of general type. We also observe that essentially the same proof works even when
X
and
Y
are only assumed to be quasi-projective, with the base of log Kodaira dimension
\kappa(Y)\geq 0
; see Theorem
2.2
.

We also prove a more general result that depends on
the following conjecture of Campana-Peternell
[
CP
, Conj. 2.4]
, which can be seen
as a generalization of the non-vanishing conjecture:

Conjecture

(Campana-Peternell)
.

Let
Y
be a smooth projective variety, and assume that
K_{Y}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}A+B
,
where
A
and
B
are
\mathbb{Q}
-divisors such that
A
is effective and
B
is
pseudo-effective. Then
\kappa(Y)\geq\kappa(A)
.

The non-vanishing conjecture is the case
A=0
. Concretely, it says
that if
K_{Y}
is pseudo-effective – which is equivalent to
Y
not being uniruled

[

]
– then
\kappa(Y)\geq 0
. It can be easily checked
[

, p. 12–13]

that the Campana-Peternell conjecture is implied by the existence of good minimal models;
it therefore holds if
\dim Y\leq 3
, since this conjecture in the Minimal Model Program (MMP)
is known to hold in this range. On the other hand, the Campana-Peternell
conjecture is definitely weaker than the full abundance conjecture; in fact, we
believe that it is basically equivalent to the non-vanishing conjecture

[

]
.

Theorem C
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a smooth algebraic fiber space, and assume that the
Campana-Peternell conjecture holds. Then
\kappa(F)+\kappa(Y)\geq\kappa(X)
.

Note
.

More precisely, we need the Campana-Peternell conjecture for the fibers of the Iitaka
fibration of
Y
if
\kappa(Y)\geq 0
; or the non-vanishing conjecture for
Y

itself if
\kappa(Y)=-\infty
.

Iitaka’s conjecture predicts of course the opposite inequality
\kappa(X)\geq\kappa(F)+\kappa(Y)
, for an arbitrary morphism; it is known to be implied by the
existence of a good minimal model for the geometric generic fiber of
f
by a result
of Kawamata
[
Kawamata
]
. Therefore we obtain the following loosely formulated consequence.

Corollary D
.

The conjectures of the MMP imply
[
Popa
, Conj. 2.5]
, stating that if
f\colon X\to Y
is a smooth algebraic fiber space, then
\kappa(X)=\kappa(F)+\kappa(Y)
.

In any event, we obtain unconditional results – and hence the solution to

[
Popa
, Conj. 2.1]
– for algebraic fiber spaces over surfaces and threefolds.

Corollary E
.

If
f\colon X\to S
is a smooth algebraic fiber space, with
S
a smooth projective
surface, then
\kappa(X)=\kappa(F)+\kappa(S)
.

Corollary F
.

If
f\colon X\to Y
is a smooth algebraic fiber space, with
Y
a smooth projective
threefold, then
\kappa(F)+\kappa(Y)\geq\kappa(X)
.

In
Corollary
E
we have equality due to the fact that the Iitaka conjecture is known over surfaces; see
[
Cao
]
, and the references therein. This is not yet known when
Y
is an arbitrary threefold.

Theorem
C
can be easily reduced to the two cases
\kappa(Y)=-\infty
and
\kappa(Y)=0
, and is therefore a consequence of the following two
results.

Proposition G
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a smooth morphism between smooth projective varieties, with

Y
uniruled. Then
\kappa(X)=-\infty
.

Thus the additivity of Kodaira dimension for smooth morphisms over varieties
Y
with

\kappa(Y)=-\infty
holds if one assumes the non-vanishing conjecture. The proof of

Proposition
G
is the first step in the proof of

Theorem
B
. It works by reduction to the case
Y=\mathbb{P}^{1}
, where a stronger
version is a deep result of Viehweg-Zuo
[
VZ1
]
. Therefore the main new input is
the following:

Theorem H
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a smooth algebraic fiber space with
\kappa(Y)=0
.

(i)

If the Campana-Peternell conjecture holds on
Y
, so for instance if
Y
has a good minimal model, then

\kappa(F)\geq\kappa(X)
.

(ii)

If
Y
is actually a good minimal model, in other words if
K_{Y}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}0
, then
\kappa(X)=\kappa(F)
.
Moreover, we have
P_{m}(F)\geq P_{m}(X)
for all
m\geq 1
, with equality if
Y
is simply connected (e.g.
Calabi-Yau).

Thus
[
Popa
, Conj. 2.5]
holds unconditionally when
Y
is a good minimal model
with
\kappa(Y)=0
. When
Y
is an abelian variety, this is a special case of

[
MP
, Thm. A(2)]
. Note for completeness that the conjecture also holds when

X
is a good minimal model with
\kappa(X)=0
, since then so is
Y
by
[
TZ
]
.

The proofs of the results in this paper rely on a Hodge module construction from

[
PS1
,
PS2
]
, which was in turn heavily inspired by techniques of Viehweg-Zuo in their work on hyperbolicity. Because of the smoothness assumption,
semistable reduction and the technical passage to logarithmic Higgs bundles are
however not needed. The proof of
Theorem
H
is somewhat more delicate
(than for instance that of
Theorem
A
), needing both an additional technical
ingredient and important analytic results from
[
PT
,
CaP
]
(see also
[
HPS18
]
).

Remark
.

The disadvantage of this method is the lack of symmetry between the
assumptions and the conclusion. Say
f\colon X\to Y
is a smooth algebraic fiber
space, and
D
a divisor on
Y
. Roughly speaking, we start from the assumption
that
mK_{X}-f^{\ast}D
is
effective
for some
m\geq 1
; and the
conclusion is that
m^{\prime}K_{Y}-D
is
pseudo-effective
for
m^{\prime}\gg m
. The
Campana-Peternell conjecture removes this asymmetry, and allows us to put
“pseudo-effective” or “effective” in both places; but it would of course be
desirable to have unconditional results.

We conclude by observing that the Campana-Peternell conjecture, together with the techniques above, also leads to the following characterization of
\kappa(Y)
that can be seen as a generalization of
Theorem
A
.

Corollary I
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a smooth algebraic fiber space, with general fiber
F
satisfying
\kappa(F)\geq 0
.
If the Campana-Peternell conjecture holds on
X
and
Y
, then

\kappa(Y)=\max\bigl\{\thinspace\kappa(L)\thinspace\thinspace\big|\thinspace\thinspace\text{$mK_{X}-f^{\ast}L$ is pseudo-effective for
some $m\geq 1$}\thinspace\bigr\},

where the maximum is taken over all line bundles on
Y
.

B.
Proofs

1.
Proof of Theorem
A

We start by considering a general construction.
Let
A
be an ample line bundle on
Y
. According to
[
Mori
, Prop. 1.14]
, the
condition that
\kappa(X)=\kappa(F)+\dim Y
is equivalent to having

H^{0}(X,\omega_{X}^{\otimes m}\otimes f^{*}A^{-1})\neq 0

for some
m\geq 1
. Using the projection formula, this is equivalent to
the existence of a nontrivial (hence injective) morphism

(1.1)

L:=A\otimes(\omega_{Y}^{-1})^{\otimes m}\hookrightarrow f_{*}\omega_{X/Y}^{\otimes m}.

Note that by the invariance of plurigenera, the sheaf
f_{*}\omega_{X/Y}^{\otimes m}

is locally free of rank
P_{m}(F)
on all of
Y
.

We now use Viehweg’s well-known fiber product trick, in the following form: let

X^{(m)}
denote the
m
-fold fiber
product
X\times_{Y}\cdots\times_{Y}X
, with its induced morphism

f^{(m)}:X^{(m)}\rightarrow Y
. Note that this is still a smooth algebraic fiber
space, with general fiber isomorphic to the
m
-fold product
F\times\cdots\times F
. Moreover,

(1.2)

f^{(m)}_{*}\omega_{X^{(m)}/Y}^{\otimes m}\simeq(f_{*}\omega_{X/Y}^{\otimes m})^{\otimes m}

for example by
[
Mori
, Cor. 4.11]
. This gives us an inclusion

L^{\otimes m}\hookrightarrow f^{(m)}_{*}\omega_{X^{(m)}/Y}^{\otimes m}.

By replacing
X
by
X^{(m)}
, and the fiber space
f
by
f^{(m)}
, we are therefore
allowed to assume from the beginning that we are working with a fiber space
f
such that

(1.3)

H^{0}(X,B^{\otimes m})\neq 0\quad\text{where \,\, $B=\omega_{X/Y}\otimes f^{*}L^{-1}$.}

Under this assumption, one can bring into play the machinery used in
[
PS1
,
PS2
]
, strongly inspired in turn by the celebrated method developed by Viehweg-Zuo

[
VZ1
,
VZ2
]
, based on the existence of so-called Viehweg-Zuo sheaves. Note however that, unlike in the
works above, the line bundle
L
we consider here is usually not positive any more.

First, using
[
PS2
, Thm. 2.2]
and denoting
\mathcal{A}_{Y}=\Sym T_{Y}
, (
1.3
) implies that there exists a graded
\mathcal{A}_{Y}
-module
\mathscr{G}_{\bullet}
that is coherent over
\mathcal{A}_{Y}
, and has the following among its special properties:

(i)

One has
\mathscr{G}_{0}\simeq L
.

(ii)

Each
\mathscr{G}_{k}
is torsion-free.

(iii)

There exists a regular holonomic
\mathscr{D}_{Y}
-module
\mathcal{M}
with good filtration

F_{\bullet}\mathcal{M}
, and an inclusion of graded
\mathcal{A}_{Y}
-modules
\mathscr{G}_{\bullet}\subseteq\gr_{\bullet}^{F}\!\mathcal{M}
.

(iv)

The filtered
\mathscr{D}
-module
(\mathcal{M},F_{\bullet}\mathcal{M})
underlies a polarizable Hodge module
M
on
Y
, with strict support
Y
, and
F_{k}\mathcal{M}=0
for
k<0
.

We next follow closely
[
PS2
, §3.2]
. The argument is based on considering the chain of

\mathscr{O}_{Y}
-module homomorphisms

0\longrightarrow\mathscr{G}_{0}\overset{\theta_{0}}{\longrightarrow}\mathscr{G}_{1}\otimes\Omega_{Y}^{1}\overset{\theta_{1}\circ\id}{\longrightarrow}\mathscr{G}_{2}\otimes(\Omega_{Y}^{1})^{\otimes 2}\longrightarrow\cdots

obtained from the graded
\mathcal{A}_{Y}
-module structure on
\mathscr{G}_{\bullet}
and the generalized
Kodaira-Spencer maps

\theta_{k}:\mathscr{G}_{k}\longrightarrow\mathscr{G}_{k+1}\otimes\Omega_{Y}^{1},

and originated in work of Kovács and Viehweg-Zuo. Using the identification
\mathscr{G}_{0}\simeq L
and
the fact that
{\rm Ker}(\theta_{k})^{\vee}
is a weakly positive sheaf for each
k
,
2

negativity of the kernels of generalized Kodaira-Spencer maps for pure Hodge modules

(i)

The line bundle

(ii)

There exists a weakly positive sheaf

In the first case, we immediately get that

Remark 1

(
The non-smooth case)
.

As mentioned in the introduction, when
Y
is not uniruled (which we recall implies that
\omega_{Y}
is pseudo-effective by
[
BDPP
]
) the proof can be extended without much effort to the general case, when
f
is only smooth over an open set
V\subseteq Y
, to deduce that
V
is of log general type. By blowing up it is immediate to reduce to the case
where
Y\smallsetminus V=D
, a simple normal crossing divisor. The only difference in the argument is that
(

) is now an isomorphism only away from
D
, which in practice means that in the proof above we have to
replace
L
by
L(-rD)
for some integer

. The same steps now lead to the bigness of a line bundle of the form

\omega_{Y}^{\otimes a}\otimes\mathscr{O}_{Y}(bD)

for some
a,b>0
, and the pseudo-effectivity of
\omega_{Y}
is then needed to deduce the bigness of
\omega_{Y}(D)

by multiplying by a suitable multiple of either
\omega_{Y}
or
\mathscr{O}_{Y}(D)
. (Compare
also with the proof of
[
PS2
, Thm. 4.1]
.)

Note also that if
\kappa(Y)\geq 0
and the complement of
V
in
Y
has codimension
at least
2
, it is not hard to check that
\kappa(V)=\kappa(Y)
; see e.g.

[
MP
, Lem. 2.6]
. Thus under this assumption the conclusion is still that
Y

is of general type.

Proof of Theorem
B

We divide the proof into a few steps.

Step 1.

In this step we deal with the case when
Y
is uniruled,

\kappa(Y)=-\infty

The hypothesis means that there exists a variety

Step 2.

In this step we assume that
\kappa(Y)\geq 0
, and show that we can reduce to the case

First, since the fibers of

We show the reduction for this inequality.

To this end, we consider the Iitaka fibration

, which after base change can be assumed to be a morphism, with

\kappa(H)+\dim Z\geq\kappa(X).

Putting the two inequalities together, we obtain (

Step 3.

Under the assumption that the fibers of

Step 4.

In this final step, we show the assertion in the theorem under the assumption that
\Var(f)=0
(with no assumptions on

). This is a consequence of the existence and properties of the moduli space of canonically polarized varieties, as suggested to us by Ziquan Zhuang.

Concretely, the family

to the appropriate coarse moduli space of canonically polarized varieties

it follows that

is the coarse moduli space of a Deligne-Mumford stack), it is therefore well-known that there is a finite étale base change

where

\kappa(X)=\kappa(\widetilde{X})=\kappa(F)+\kappa(\widetilde{Y})=\kappa(F)+\kappa(Y).

This concludes the proof.

Remark 2
.

Proposition
G
can be rephrased as saying that if

is smooth and
\kappa(X)\geq 0
, then

is pseudo-effective. Here is an

A
. We consider the construction in the proof of that theorem, only now we take

, which is possible because
\kappa(X)\geq 0
. The exact same proof shows then that either we have directly

is pseudo-effective (case (i)), or that there is an inclusion

where
\mathcal{W}
has pseudo-effective determinant (case (ii)). But
[
CPa
, Thm. 7.6]
says precisely that in the latter case
K_{Y}
is again pseudo-effective.

Remark 3
.

In fact, recent work in symplectic geometry can be used to prove the following
strengthening of
Proposition
G
:
Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a
smooth algebraic fiber space. If
K_{X}
is pseudo-effective, then
K_{Y}
is also
pseudo-effective.
Here is a sketch of the proof. If
K_{Y}
is not pseudo-effective,
then
Y
is covered by rational curves. After restricting to a smooth rational
curve whose normal bundle has nonnegative degree, we obtain a smooth algebraic
fiber space
g\colon Z\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
such that
K_{Z}
is pseudo-effective. But a
very recent theorem by Pieloch
[
Pieloch
, Thm. 1.1]
– answering a question by
Starr
[
Sta15
]
– says that
Z
is covered by
sections of

, and therefore uniruled. This is a contradiction.

Remark 4

(
The quasi-projective case)
.

A very similar proof to that of Theorem
B
leads in fact to a statement for smooth morphisms between quasi-projective varieties, addressing an analogous case of the more general
[
Popa
, Conj. 3.1]
. We thank Sung Gi Park for pointing this out.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
f\colon U\to V
be a smooth projective algebraic fiber space with canonically polarized fibers. If
\kappa(V)\geq 0
, then we have
\kappa(U)=\kappa(F)+\kappa(V)
.

Proof.

This is the analogue of Steps 2.-4. in the proof of Theorem
B
.

Since the fibers are of general type, the analogue of Iitaka’s conjecture, namely

Now again by

4.
Proof of Theorem

If
\kappa(F)=-\infty
, then we also have
\kappa(X)=-\infty
by Easy Addition. We may therefore assume throughout
that
\kappa(F)\geq 0
. The key players are again the vector bundles

\mathscr{F}_{m}:=f_{*}\omega_{X/Y}^{\otimes m},\,\,\,\,\,\,{\rm for}\,\,\,\,m\geq 1,

We also consider their twists

\mathscr{E}_{m}:=\mathscr{F}_{m}\otimes\omega_{Y}^{\otimes m}\simeq f_{*}\omega_{X}^{\otimes m}\,\,\,\,\,\,{\rm for}\,\,\,\,m\geq 1,

for which we have

r_{m}:=\rk(\mathscr{E}_{m})=\rk(\mathscr{F}_{m})=P_{m}(F)\,\,\,\,\,\,{\rm and}\,\,\,\,\,\,h^{0}(Y,\mathscr{E}_{m})=P_{m}(X).

We have for each
m
a (split) inclusion

\det\mathscr{F}_{m}\hookrightarrow\mathscr{F}_{m}^{\otimes r_{m}},

and therefore using the Viehweg fiber product trick just as in the proof of
Theorem
A
, we obtain an inclusion

(\det\mathscr{F}_{m})^{\otimes m}\hookrightarrow f^{(mr_{m})}_{*}\omega_{X^{(mr_{m})}/Y}^{\otimes m}.

Proceeding precisely as in the proof of that theorem (where the role of
L
there is now played by
\det\mathscr{F}_{m}
), the arguments from
[
PS2
]
lead then to one of the following two possibilities:

(1)
(\det\mathscr{F}_{m})^{-1}
is pseudo-effective.

(2) There exists a weakly positive sheaf
\mathcal{W}
on
Y
, and an inclusion

\mathcal{W}\otimes\det\mathscr{F}_{m}\hookrightarrow(\Omega_{Y}^{1})^{\otimes N}

for some positive integer
N>0
.

Recall on the other hand that
\mathscr{F}_{m}
is a weakly positive sheaf by
[
Viehweg1
, Thm. III]
,
and therefore
\det\mathscr{F}_{m}
is a pseudo-effective line bundle, e.g. by
[
Viehweg1
, Lem. 1.6 (6)]
.
If (1) holds, then both
\det\mathscr{F}_{m}
and
(\det\mathscr{F}_{m})^{-1}
are
pseudo-effective, and so
\det\mathscr{F}_{m}\equiv 0
(i.e. it is numerically trivial).

On the other hand, if (2) holds, by taking the saturation of the left hand side inside
(\Omega_{Y}^{1})^{\otimes N}
, we obtain an exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{W}^{\prime}\otimes\det\mathscr{F}_{m}\longrightarrow(\Omega_{Y}^{1})^{\otimes N}\longrightarrow\mathscr{Q}\longrightarrow 0

where
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}
is a weakly positive sheaf, and
\mathscr{Q}
is torsion-free. We also know that

\det\mathscr{Q}
is pseudo-effective; this follows from

[
CPa
, Thm. 1.3]
, which says that if
K_{Y}
is pseudo-effective, then any quotient
of any tensor power of
\Omega_{Y}^{1}
has pseudo-effective determinant.
Passing to determinants, we then obtain an isomorphism of the form

(4.1)

\omega_{Y}^{\otimes a}\simeq(\det\mathscr{F}_{m})^{\otimes b}\otimes P,

where
a,b>0
and
P
is a pseudo-effective line bundle.

Remark 6
.

Up to here we only used that
\kappa(F)\geq 0
and
K_{Y}
is pseudo-effective.

If we assume that
K_{Y}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}0
, from (
4.1
) we again deduce that

(\det\mathscr{F}_{m})^{-1}
is pseudo-effective, and therefore as above
\det\mathscr{F}_{m}\equiv 0
.
It follows from
[
CaP
, Thm. 5.2]
(see also
[
HPS18
, Cor. 27.2]
) that
\mathscr{F}_{m}
is a hermitian flat vector bundle,
and therefore comes from a unitary representation of the fundamental group. If we
assume in addition that
Y
is simply connected, we then obtain

\mathscr{F}_{m}\simeq\mathscr{O}_{Y}^{\oplus r_{m}}
, or equivalently

\mathscr{E}_{m}\simeq{(\omega_{Y}^{\otimes m})}^{\oplus r_{m}}.

Note that by hypothesis
\omega_{Y}^{\otimes m}\simeq\mathscr{O}_{X}
when
m
is sufficiently divisible, and otherwise vanishes.
Consequently
P_{m}(X)=P_{m}(F)
when
P_{m}(Y)=1
, and
P_{m}(X)=0
otherwise.
In general we only have
r_{m}\geq h^{0}(Y,\mathscr{E}_{m})
(because unitary representations are
semisimple), and therefore by the same argument we obtain

P_{m}(F)\geq P_{m}(X)\quad\text{for all $m\geq 1$},

hence also
\kappa(F)\geq\kappa(X)
. On the other hand, a result of Cao-Păun

[

, Thm. 5.6]
states, for any algebraic fiber space
f
, that if
c_{1}(\det\mathscr{F}_{m})=0\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{R})
for some
m\geq 2
, then Iitaka’s conjecture holds for

f
. Cao and Păun prove this result under the assumption that
c_{1}(\det\mathscr{F}_{m})=0\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
, but because of
[
HPS18
, Thm. 27.2]
, the weaker assumption is
sufficient to conclude that
\mathscr{F}_{m}
is a hermitian flat bundle; the rest of the
argument then proceeds as in
[
CaP
]
. In our case, this gives the opposite inequality

\kappa(X)\geq\kappa(F)
, and concludes the proof of (ii).

We are thus left with proving (i). We need the following lemma.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
E
be a nonzero vector bundle on a projective variety
X
. If
h^{0}(X,E)\geq\rk E+1
, then there is a line bundle
L
with
h^{0}(X,L)\geq 2
and an
inclusion
L\hookrightarrow E^{\otimes r}
for some
1\leq r\leq\rk E
.

Proof.

Let
\mathscr{F}\subseteq E
be the torsion-free subsheaf generated by the global sections of

E
, and let
1\leq r\leq\rk E
be the generic rank of
\mathscr{F}
. Choose
r

global sections
s_{1},\dotsc,s_{r}\in H^{0}(X,E)
that generate the stalk of
\mathscr{F}
at a
general point of
X
. The resulting morphism
\mathscr{O}_{X}^{\oplus r}\to\mathscr{F}
is injective; it cannot be surjective, for otherwise it would be an
isomorphism, and then
h^{0}(X,E)=h^{0}(X,\mathscr{F})=r\leq\rk E
, contradicting
the assumption that
h^{0}(X,E)\geq\rk E+1
. Let
x\in X
be one of the points
where the morphism fails to be surjective on stalks. Since
\mathscr{F}
is globally
generated, depending on the situation we can do one of the following two things:

\bullet
If
s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}
remain linearly independent at
x
,
we can find an additional global section
s\in H^{0}(X,E)
such that
such that
s(x)
does not lie in the linear span of
s_{1}(x),\dotsc,s_{r}(x)

inside the stalk
E_{x}
. It follows that, among the
r+1
sections

s_{1}\wedge\dotsb\wedge s_{r},\quad s_{1}\wedge\dotsb\wedge s_{i-1}\wedge s\wedge s_{i+1}\wedge\dotsb\wedge s_{r}\quad\text{for $i=1,\dotsc,r$}

of the bundle
\bigwedge^{r}E
, at least
2
are linearly indepedent.

\bullet
If
s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}
do not remain linearly independent at
x
, we can in any case find other global
sections
t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}\in H^{0}(X,E)
such that
t_{1}(x),\ldots,t_{r}(x)\in E_{x}
form part of a minimal system
of generators of
\mathscr{F}_{x}
. Then

s_{1}\wedge\dotsb\wedge s_{r}\quad{\rm and}\quad t_{1}\wedge\dotsb\wedge t_{r}

are linearly independent global sections of the bundle
\bigwedge^{r}E
.

Either way, by construction all these sections come from global sections of the sheaf
\bigwedge^{r}\mathscr{F}
. Let

L
be the reflexive hull of
\bigwedge^{r}\mathscr{F}
; then
L
is a line bundle with

h^{0}(X,L)\geq 2
. We also have inclusions

L\hookrightarrow\bigwedge^{r}E\hookrightarrow E^{\otimes r}

because
E
is locally free.
∎

Let us now suppose for the sake of contradiction that
P_{m}(X)\geq P_{m}(F)+1
. The
lemma, applied to the vector bundle
\mathscr{E}_{m}
, then gives us an inclusion

L\hookrightarrow\mathscr{E}_{m}^{\otimes r}\simeq\mathscr{F}_{m}^{\otimes r}\otimes\omega_{Y}^{\otimes mr}

for some integer
1\leq r\leq r_{m}
, where
L
is a line bundle
with
h^{0}(X,L)\geq 2
, and so
\kappa(L)\geq 1
. Arguing as above, using the fiber product trick, we obtain one of the
following two possibilities:

(1)
L^{-1}\otimes\omega_{Y}^{\otimes mr}
is pseudo-effective.

(2) There exists a weakly positive sheaf
\mathcal{W}
on
Y
, and a short exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{W}\otimes L\to(\Omega_{Y}^{1})^{\otimes N}\otimes\omega_{Y}^{\otimes mr}\to\mathscr{Q}\to 0

for some positive integer
N>0
.

In case (1), since
\kappa(L)\geq 1
, the Campana-Peternell conjecture implies that
\kappa(Y)\geq 1
, contradicting our assumption that
\kappa(Y)=0
.

In case (2), as above
\det\mathscr{Q}
must be a pseudo-effective line bundle by
[
CPa
, Thm. 1.3]
.
Passing to
determinants in the short exact sequence in (2), we now obtain an isomorphism

\omega_{Y}^{\otimes a}\simeq L^{\otimes b}\otimes P

where
a,b>0
and
P
is a pseudo-effective line bundle. We then conclude just as
in case (1).

5.
Proof of Corollary
I

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a smooth algebraic fiber space whose general fiber
F

satisfies
\kappa(F)\geq 0
. We use the notation

\kappa_{f}(Y):=\max\bigl\{\thinspace\kappa(Y,L)\thinspace\thinspace\big|\thinspace\thinspace\text{$mK_{X}-f^{\ast}L$ is pseudo-effective for some $m\geq 1$}\thinspace\bigr\},

and show that
\kappa(Y)=\kappa_{f}(Y)
provided that
\kappa(F)\geq 0
. One
inequality is easy. Our assumption that
\kappa(F)\geq 0
implies that the relative
canonical divisor
K_{X/Y}
is pseudo-effective; the reason is that

\omega_{X/Y}
has a canonical singular hermitian metric with semi-positive curvature

[
PT
, Thm. 4.2.2]
(see also
[
HPS18
, Thm. 27.1]
). Since
K_{X}-f^{\ast}K_{Y}\equiv K_{X/Y}
, we get
\kappa_{f}(Y)\geq\kappa(Y)
.

To prove the other inequality, let
L
be a line bundle on
Y
such that
mK_{X}-f^{\ast}L
is pseudo-effective, and such that
\kappa_{f}(Y)=\kappa(Y,L)
.
We may assume that
\kappa(Y,L)\geq 0
, and consider an integer
n\geq 1
such that the linear system
\lvert nL\rvert
gives the Iitaka
fibration of
L
. After
replacing
Y
by a log resolution of the base locus of
\lvert nL\rvert
, and
f\colon X\to Y
by the fiber product, we can assume that
nL\sim B+E
with
B
semi-ample
and
E
effective; consequently,
nmK_{X}-f^{*}B\sim n(mK_{X}-f^{*}L)+E
is still
pseudo-effective. Moreover, because of the assumption on
n
we have
that
\kappa(Y,B)=\kappa(Y,L)
, hence after replacing
L
by
B
we may assume from the
beginning that
L
is semi-ample. The Campana-Peternell conjecture (on
X
) implies
that
mK_{X}-f^{\ast}L
is effective
for
m\gg 0
; this is proved in
[
Schnell2
]
. We can now apply exactly
the same argument as in the proof of
Theorem
A
, up to the line containing
(
1.4
), and obtain a short exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{W}\otimes L\to(\Omega_{Y}^{1})^{\otimes N}\otimes\omega_{Y}^{\otimes m}\to\mathcal{Q}\to 0

for some
N\geq 1
, with
\mathcal{W}
weakly positive. (Concretely,
\mathcal{W}

is a torsion-free sheaf with a semi-positively curved singular hermitian metric.)
Since
m\geq 1
, both
K_{Y}
and
\det\mathcal{Q}
must therefore be pseudo-effective:
this follows by combining

[
CPa
, Thm. 7.6]
(to conclude that
K_{Y}
is pseudo-effective) and

[
CPa
, Thm. 1.3]
(to conclude that any quotient of any tensor power of

\Omega_{Y}^{1}
has pseudo-effective determinant). Passing to determinants in the short
exact sequence above, we see that there are two integers
a,b\geq 1
such that

aK_{Y}\sim bL+\det\mathcal{Q}+\det\mathcal{W}.

As both
\det\mathcal{Q}
and
\det\mathcal{W}
are pseudo-effective, the Campana-Peternell conjecture (on
Y
) now
gives us the desired inequality
\kappa(Y)\geq\kappa(Y,L)=\kappa_{f}(Y)
.

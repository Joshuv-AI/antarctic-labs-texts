Heisenberg uncertainty principle and its analogues in higher dimension: via Wigdersons’ method
Yiyu Tang

Abstract
The following question was proposed by Avi Wigderson and Yuval Wigderson in [ 6 ] : Is it possible to use the method in [ 6 ] to prove Heisenberg uncertainty principle in higher dimension ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} , and get the correct dependence of the constant on d d ? We answer this question affirmatively, and also prove some generalizations of Heisenberg uncertainty principle in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} via Wigdersons’ method.



1
Introduction

Recall the definition of Schwartz class:

\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):=\{f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{C}):\rho_{\alpha,\beta}(f)\text{ is finite for all }\alpha=(\alpha_{i})_{i=1}^{d},\beta=(\beta_{i})_{i=1}^{d}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}\},

where
\rho_{\alpha,\beta}(f)=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x^{\alpha}\partial^{\beta}f(x)|
. Here
x^{\alpha}=x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}x_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}\cdots x_{d}^{\alpha_{d}}
, and
\partial^{\beta}=\partial_{1}^{\beta_{1}}\partial_{2}^{\beta_{2}}\cdots\partial_{d}^{\beta_{d}}
.

For a Schwartz function
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, its Fourier transform
\hat{f}
is defined by
\hat{f}(\xi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}x\cdot\xi}\mathrm{d}x
, where
x\cdot\xi
is the inner product in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The Heisenberg uncertainty principle states that for any
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|^{2}|f(x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\bigg)\cdot\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\xi|^{2}|\hat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi\bigg)\geq\frac{d^{2}}{16\pi^{2}}||f||^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\cdot||\hat{f}||^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

(1.1)

and the equality holds if and only if
f
has the form
f(x)=C\mathrm{e}^{-\pi\delta|x|^{2}}
, where
\delta>0
.

The classical proof of (
1.1
) is to write

d||f||^{2}_{L^{2}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)\overline{f(x)}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}x_{j}\mathrm{d}x,\text{ where }x=(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{d}).

For
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we denote
|x|:=\sqrt{|x_{1}|^{2}+\cdots+|x_{d}|^{2}}
, and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
is the inner product in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Using integration by parts, Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, and Plancherel identity:

\displaystyle d||f||^{2}_{2}

\displaystyle=2\cdot\mathrm{Re}\bigg(-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\langle\nabla f,\overline{xf}\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathrm{d}x\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq 2\bigg|-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\langle\nabla f,\overline{xf}\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathrm{d}x\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla f|\cdot|\overline{xf}|\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq 2\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla f|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|^{2}|f|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=2\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sum_{j=1}^{d}|\widehat{\partial_{j}f}(\xi)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|^{2}|f|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

Finally, just recall a special property of Fourier transform
\widehat{\partial_{j}f}(\xi)=2\pi\mathrm{i}\cdot\xi_{j}\hat{f}(\xi)
.

1.1
An overview of Wigdersons’ method

In
[
6
]
, A.Wigderson and Y.Wigderson suggested a new method to give a unified version of various uncertainty principles. This method leads to a very different proof of (
1.1
) in dimension
1
. Comparing with the classical proof, their method did not use any special property of Fourier transform, so it can be generalized to prove analogues of uncertainty principle for other operators. In this paper, we only focus on the Fourier transform operator.

Now we give a quick review of Wigdersons’ proof of (
1.1
) in dimension
1
. Let’s begin with a basic inequality:
||\hat{f}||_{L^{\infty}}\leq||f||_{L^{1}}
. Replacing
f
by
\hat{f}
, we have
||f||_{L^{\infty}}\leq||\hat{f}||_{L^{1}}
. Multiplying these two inequalities gives us

\frac{||f||_{1}\cdot||\hat{f}||_{1}}{||f||_{\infty}\cdot||\hat{f}||_{\infty}}\geq 1,\text{ where }f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{1})\setminus\{0\}.

(1.2)

We call (
1.2
) the
primary uncertainty principle
.

Now for
1<p<\infty
, we have an obvious inequality:
||f||^{p}_{p}\leq||f||^{p-1}_{\infty}||f||_{1}
, this is just because
|f(x)|^{p}\leq||f||_{\infty}^{p-1}|f(x)|
. Multiplying both sides by
||f||_{1}^{p-1}
, we get

||f||_{1}^{p-1}||f||_{p}^{p}\leq||f||_{\infty}^{p-1}||f||_{1}^{p}.

(1.3)

The inequality (
1.3
) implies that

\frac{||f||_{1}}{||f||_{p}}\geq\bigg(\frac{||f||_{1}}{||f||_{\infty}}\bigg)^{\frac{p-1}{p}},\text{ and also }\frac{||\hat{f}||_{1}}{||\hat{f}||_{p}}\geq\bigg(\frac{||\hat{f}||_{1}}{||\hat{f}||_{\infty}}\bigg)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\text{ after replacing }f\text{ by }\hat{f}.

(1.4)

Define
V(f):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|x|^{2}|f|^{2}
, our goal is to prove that there exists a constant
C>0
independent of
f
, so that

V(f)V(\hat{f})\geq C||f||^{2}_{2}||\hat{f}||^{2}_{2}.

Now we choose
T:=\frac{1}{8}\big(\frac{||f||_{1}}{||f||_{2}}\big)^{2}
, by Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

\int_{-T}^{T}|f|=\int_{-T}^{T}1\cdot|f|\leq\sqrt{2T}\cdot\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f|^{2}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{1}{2}||f||_{1}.

The above inequality is equivalent to
\frac{1}{2}||f||_{1}\leq\int_{|x|>T}|f|
. For the part
|x|>T
, another application of Cauchy-Schwarz inequality yields that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}||f||_{1}\leq\int_{|x|>T}|f|\leq

\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{|x|>T}\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{|x|^{2}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg(\int_{|x|>T}|x|^{2}|f|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

(1.5)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{2}{T}}\sqrt{V(f)}.

After a simplification, we get

\frac{V(f)}{||f||_{2}^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{64}\bigg(\frac{||f||_{1}}{||f||_{2}}\bigg)^{4}.

(1.6)

Replacing
f
by
\hat{f}
and taking a multiplication, we get, by (
1.4
) and (
1.2
),

\frac{V(f)}{||f||_{2}^{2}}\cdot\frac{V(\hat{f})}{||\hat{f}||_{2}^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{64^{2}}\bigg(\frac{||f||_{1}||\hat{f}||_{1}}{||f||_{2}||\hat{f}||_{2}}\bigg)^{4}\geq\frac{1}{64^{2}}\bigg(\frac{||f||_{1}||\hat{f}||_{1}}{||f||_{\infty}||\hat{f}||_{\infty}}\bigg)^{2}\geq\frac{1}{64^{2}}.

(1.7)

Remark 1.1
.

Comparing with the original proof, this new proof seems longer. However, we should point out that, in the classical proof, we use the identity
\widehat{\partial_{j}f}(\xi)=2\pi\mathrm{i}\cdot\xi_{j}\hat{f}(\xi)
, which is a special property of Fourier transform, and not shared by other operators.

In the new proof, essentially, we only use the property that Fourier transform and its inverse are
L^{1}\rightarrow L^{\infty}
bounded, and this boundedness property is less restrictive. So the new proof can be generalized to many other operators, for example, if a linear operator
L:\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})\rightarrow\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
satisfies

||L||_{L^{1}\rightarrow L^{\infty}}\leq 1,\ ||L^{\star}Lf||_{\infty}\geq||f||_{\infty},

then we also have a Heisenberg-type uncertainty principle for
L
:

V(f)V(Lf)\geq C||f||_{2}^{2}||\hat{f}||_{2}^{2}.

For more details, see
[
6
]
, section 4.2 and 4.3.

Remark 1.2
.

Comparing with the optimal bound
\frac{1}{16\pi^{2}}
, in (
1.7
), the constant is
1/64^{2}
, which is much smaller than the optimal bound. It seems that this method can not be used to prove the sharp bound, since we do not use full properties of Fourier transform.

1.2
Questions proposed by A.Wigderson and Y.Wigderson

In
[
6
]
, the authors observed that this method can not be used to prove Heisenberg uncertainty principles in higher dimension
d\geq 2
directly. This is because the integral

\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|x|>T}\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{|x|^{2}}

is divergent. They asked if this convergence issues represented a real limitation of the new approach, or if there was a way around them. More precisely, they asked the following questions:

Question

(
[
6
]
, page 255, Open problem)
.

Is it possible to prove the Heisenberg uncertainty principle in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
via the new method? That’s to say, could one use this new approach to prove the existence of an absolute constant
C=C(d)>0
independent of
f
, so that

\frac{V(f)}{||f||_{2}^{2}}\frac{V(\hat{f})}{||\hat{f}||^{2}_{2}}\geq C,\text{ for all }f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\setminus\{0\}?

Here
V(f):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|^{2}|f(x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x
.

Moreover, we already knew that the best constant
C
is
\frac{d^{2}}{16\pi^{2}}
. Could one use this new approach to obtain the correct dependence on the dimension? That’s to say, is it possible to prove the existence of an absolute constant
C^{\prime}
, which is independent of
f
and
d
, so that

\frac{V(f)}{||f||_{2}^{2}}\frac{V(\hat{f})}{||\hat{f}||^{2}_{2}}\geq C^{\prime}d^{2},\text{ for all }f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\setminus\{0\}?

1.3
Main theorems

The following theorem answers the above two questions affirmatively.

Theorem 1
.

Via Wigdersons’s method, it’s possible to prove that for every
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\setminus\{0\}
, we have

\frac{V(f)}{||f||_{2}^{2}}\frac{V(\hat{f})}{||\hat{f}||^{2}_{2}}\geq Cd^{2},

where
C
is an absolute constant independent of
f
and
d
, for example, one may take
C=\frac{1}{10^{10}}
.

Now we consider a generalization of Heisenberg uncertainty principle. For
p\in(1,\infty)
, we define

V_{p}(f):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|^{p}|f(x)|^{p}\mathrm{d}x.

Then
V_{p}(f)=V(f)
when
p=2
, and we also have a Heisenberg-type inequality for
V_{p}
when
p
is “small”. This is the following theorem:

Theorem 2
.

Suppose that
d\geq 1
.

•

If
1<p<\frac{2d}{d-1}
, then via Wigdersons’s method, it’s possible to prove that there exists a constant
C=C(d,p)
, so that

\frac{V_{p}(f)}{||f||_{p}^{p}}\frac{V_{p}(\hat{f})}{||\hat{f}||^{p}_{p}}\geq C(d,p),\text{ for every }f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\setminus\{0\}.

Moreover, if
1<p\leq 2
is fixed, then as
d
goes to infinity, the growth of
C(d,p)
is
C_{1}(p)\cdot d^{p}
, where
C_{1}(p)
is a constant independent of the dimension
d
, and up to a dimension-free constant
C_{1}(p)
, the growth
d^{p}
is sharp.

•

If
\frac{2d}{d-1}<p<\infty
, then

\inf_{f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\setminus\{0\}}\frac{V_{p}(f)}{||f||_{p}^{p}}\frac{V_{p}(\hat{f})}{||\hat{f}||^{p}_{p}}=0.

So there is no uncertainty type theorem when
p>\frac{2d}{d-1}
.

Remark 1.3
.

It seems that Wigdersons’ method can not be dealt with the critical case
p=\frac{2d}{d-1}
. Also, this method does not work when
p=1
or
p=\infty
. The three cases
p=1
,
p=\frac{2d}{d-1}
and
p=\infty
are unknown.

Our final result deals with a theorem of Cowling-Price and its generalizations. Recall Cowling-Price uncertainty principle states that if
\theta,\phi\in(0,\infty)
and
p,q\in[1,\infty]
, then

\Big|\Big||x|^{\theta}f\Big|\Big|_{p}\cdot\Big|\Big||\xi|^{\phi}\hat{f}\Big|\Big|_{q}\geq C||f||^{2}_{2},\text{ for all }f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})

if and only if

\theta>\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p},\ \phi>\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}\quad\text{and}\quad\theta+\frac{1}{p}=\phi+\frac{1}{q}.

For more details on this theorem, see
[
2
]
, Theorem 5.1. Cowling-Price theorem was also discussed in
[
1
]
, Lemma 3.3 and Theorem 3.6.

By using Wigdersons’ method, we give another proof of Cowling-Price theorem (up to the endpoints) and its generalizations in higher dimension.

Theorem 3
.

Assume that
d\geq 1
. Let
1<p,q<\infty
and
0<\theta,\phi<\infty
, then there exists a constant
C
so that

\Big|\Big||x|^{\theta}f\Big|\Big|_{p}\cdot\Big|\Big||\xi|^{\phi}\hat{f}\Big|\Big|_{q}\geq C||f||^{2}_{2}

(1.8)

for all
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, if and only if

\frac{\theta}{d}>\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p},\quad\frac{\phi}{d}>\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{1}{q}+\frac{\phi}{d}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{\theta}{d}.

(1.9)

Take
d=1
, we get the Cowling-Price theorem except for two endpoints.

Proof of Theorem
1

In (
1.6
), we proved that when
d=1
:

\frac{V(f)}{||f||_{2}^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{64}\bigg(\frac{||f||_{1}}{||f||_{2}}\bigg)^{4}.

With this inequalities in mind, now we try to prove an inequality for general
d
of the following form:

\frac{V(f)}{||f||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}}\geq C(d)\bigg(\frac{||f||_{L^{a}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}{||f||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}\bigg)^{\kappa},

where
a
and
\kappa
are constants to be determined. Also, we should take the choice of
T
into consideration.

2.1
Choice of some parameters & Sketch of the proof

We choose
a,r,s\geq 1
as following:

a=\frac{2(d+1)}{d+3},\ r=\frac{d+3}{d+1},\ s=\frac{d+3}{2}.

(2.1)

Notice that
1\leq a<2
and
1/r+1/s=1
. Also, define

T:=c_{d}\cdot\bigg(\frac{||f||_{a}}{||f||_{2}}\bigg)^{\frac{d+1}{d}},

where
c_{d}
is a constant to be determined later, and it’s independent of
f
, see (
2.4
).

Our goal is to prove, for every
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\setminus\{0\}
,

\displaystyle\frac{V(f)}{||f||_{2}^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{4}\bigg(\frac{c_{d}^{2}}{\omega_{d-1}^{2}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{d+1}}\bigg(\frac{||f||_{a}}{||f||_{2}}\bigg)^{(2+\frac{2}{d})},

(2.2)

where
\omega_{d-1}
is the area of
(d-1)
-sphere
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. We will prove this inequality in the next subsection.

Assuming (
2.2
), then replacing
f
by
\hat{f}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{V(\hat{f})}{||\hat{f}||_{2}^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{4}\bigg(\frac{c_{d}^{2}}{\omega_{d-1}^{2}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{d+1}}\bigg(\frac{||\hat{f}||_{a}}{||\hat{f}||_{2}}\bigg)^{(2+\frac{2}{d})}.

Taking a multiplication we get

\displaystyle\frac{V(f)}{||f||_{2}^{2}}\cdot\frac{V(\hat{f})}{||\hat{f}||_{2}^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{16}\bigg(\frac{c_{d}^{2}}{\omega_{d-1}^{2}}\bigg)^{\frac{2}{d+1}}\bigg(\frac{||f||_{a}||\hat{f}||_{a}}{||f||_{2}||\hat{f}||_{2}}\bigg)^{(2+\frac{2}{d})}.

Let
a^{\prime}
be the conjugate exponent of
a
. Hölder inequality implies that

||\hat{f}||_{2}=||f||_{2}\leq||f||^{\frac{1}{2}}_{a}||f||^{\frac{1}{2}}_{a^{\prime}}.

Also, when
1\leq a<2
, Hausdorff-Young inequality yields that
||\hat{f}||_{a^{\prime}}\leq||f||_{a}
, replacing
f
by
\hat{f}
, we have
||f||_{a^{\prime}}\leq||\hat{f}||_{a}
, and

\frac{||f||_{a}||\hat{f}||_{a}}{||f||_{2}||\hat{f}||_{2}}\geq\frac{||f||_{a}||\hat{f}||_{a}}{||f||_{a}||f||_{a^{\prime}}}=\frac{||\hat{f}||_{a}}{||f||_{a^{\prime}}}\geq 1.

(2.3)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{V(f)}{||f||_{2}^{2}}\cdot\frac{V(\hat{f})}{||\hat{f}||_{2}^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{16}\bigg(\frac{c_{d}^{2}}{\omega_{d-1}^{2}}\bigg)^{\frac{2}{d+1}}.

Now only need to study the growth of the quotient
\big(\frac{c_{d}}{\omega_{d-1}}\big)^{\frac{2}{d+1}}
, when
d
goes to infinity. In subsection
2.3
, we will prove that

\bigg(\frac{c_{d}}{\omega_{d-1}}\bigg)^{\frac{2}{d+1}}>\frac{1}{C}d,

where
C
is a large constant independent of
d
, for example
C=100000
, and we eventually get

\displaystyle\frac{V(f)}{||f||_{2}^{2}}\cdot\frac{V(\hat{f})}{||\hat{f}||_{2}^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{C^{\prime}}d^{2}.

Here one may take
C^{\prime}=10^{10}
.

2.2
Proof of (
2.2
)

To prove (
2.2
), we use Hölder inequality for the pair
r
and
s
, notice that
ar=2
,
as=d+1
and
1/s=(2-a)/2
by our choice.

\begin{aligned} \int_{|x|\leq T}|f|^{a}\leq&\bigg(\int_{|x|\leq T}1^{s}\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{s}}\cdot\bigg(\int_{|x|\leq T}|f|^{ar}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{r}}\\
=&(T^{d}v_{d})^{\frac{1}{s}}\bigg(\int_{|x|\leq T}|f|^{2}\bigg)^{\frac{a}{2}}\\
\leq&(T^{d}v_{d})^{\frac{2-a}{2}}||f||_{2}^{a}\end{aligned},

where
v_{d}
is the volume of the unite ball in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

The term
(T^{d}v_{d})^{\frac{2-a}{2}}
is

(T^{d}v_{d})^{\frac{2-a}{2}}=(c_{d}^{d}v_{d})^{\frac{2-a}{2}}\bigg(\frac{||f||^{d+1}_{a}}{||f||^{d+1}_{2}}\bigg)^{\frac{2-a}{2}}

Notice that
\frac{(d+1)(2-a)}{2}=a
, so

\bigg(\frac{||f||^{d+1}_{a}}{||f||^{d+1}_{2}}\bigg)^{\frac{2-a}{2}}=\bigg(\frac{||f||_{a}}{||f||_{2}}\bigg)^{a}

Now we choose
c_{d}
so that

(c_{d}^{d}v_{d})^{\frac{2-a}{2}}=1/2.

(2.4)

Particularly, the constant
c_{d}
depends only on dimension
d
.
For this
c_{d}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{|x|\leq T}|f|^{a}\leq\frac{1}{2}||f||_{a}^{a},

which is equivalent to
\int_{|x|\geq T}|f|^{a}\geq\frac{1}{2}||f||_{a}^{a}
.

Another application of Hölder inequality gives

\displaystyle\int_{|x|\geq T}|f|^{a}=

\displaystyle\int_{|x|\geq T}\frac{1}{|x|^{a}}|x|^{a}|f(x)|^{a}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{|x|\geq T}\frac{1}{|x|^{as}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{s}}\cdot\bigg(\int_{|x|\geq T}|x|^{ar}|f(x)|^{ar}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{|x|\geq T}\frac{1}{|x|^{d+1}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{s}}\cdot\bigg(\int_{|x|\geq T}|x|^{2}|f(x)|^{2}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{1}{T}\int_{|x|\geq 1}\frac{1}{|x|^{d+1}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{s}}\cdot V(f)^{\frac{1}{r}}

An easy calculation shows that
\int_{|x|\geq 1}\frac{1}{|x|^{d+1}}=\omega_{d-1}
. Therefore

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}||f||_{a}^{a}\leq\int_{|x|\geq T}|f|^{a}\leq

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{\omega_{d-1}}{T}\bigg)^{\frac{2}{d+3}}\cdot V(f)^{\frac{d+1}{d+3}}.

After a simplification, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{d+3}}\frac{||f||_{a}^{a(d+3)+\frac{2(d+1)}{d}}}{||f||_{2}^{\frac{2(d+1)}{d}+2(d+1)}}\leq\frac{\omega_{d-1}^{2}}{c_{d}^{2}}\bigg(\frac{V(f)}{||f||_{2}^{2}}\bigg)^{d+1}

Notice that
a(d+3)=2(d+1)
. So

\displaystyle\frac{\omega_{d-1}^{2}}{c_{d}^{2}}\bigg(\frac{V(f)}{||f||_{2}^{2}}\bigg)^{d+1}\geq\frac{1}{2^{d+3}}\frac{||f||_{a}^{a(d+3)+\frac{2(d+1)}{d}}}{||f||_{2}^{\frac{2(d+1)}{d}+2(d+1)}}=\frac{1}{2^{d+3}}\bigg(\frac{||f||_{a}}{||f||_{2}}\bigg)^{(d+1)(2+\frac{2}{d})},

taking a
\frac{1}{d+1}
-power we get

\displaystyle\frac{V(f)}{||f||_{2}^{2}}\geq\bigg(\frac{c_{d}^{2}}{\omega_{d-1}^{2}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{d+1}}\frac{1}{2^{\frac{d+3}{d+1}}}\bigg(\frac{||f||_{a}}{||f||_{2}}\bigg)^{(2+\frac{2}{d})}\geq\frac{1}{4}\bigg(\frac{c_{d}^{2}}{\omega_{d-1}^{2}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{d+1}}\bigg(\frac{||f||_{a}}{||f||_{2}}\bigg)^{(2+\frac{2}{d})}.

2.3
Asymptotic behavior of the quotient

In this subsection, we study the growth of
\big(\frac{c_{d}^{2}}{\omega_{d-1}^{2}}\big)^{\frac{1}{d+1}}
when
d
tends to infinity. Recall the choice of
c_{d}
, after a calculation we have

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{c_{d}}{\omega_{d-1}}\bigg)^{\frac{2}{d+1}}=\frac{1}{(v_{d})^{\frac{2}{d(d+1)}}2^{\frac{d+3}{d(d+1)}}\omega_{d-1}^{\frac{2}{d+1}}}

The first factor

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(v_{d})^{\frac{2}{d(d+1)}}}=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{d}{2}+1)^{\frac{2}{d(d+1)}}}{\pi^{\frac{1}{d+1}}}\in\bigg[\frac{\Gamma(\frac{d}{2}+1)^{\frac{2}{d(d+1)}}}{\sqrt{\pi}},\Gamma(\frac{d}{2}+1)^{\frac{2}{d(d+1)}}\bigg]

Recall the approximation of Gamma function when
x>0
is large:
\Gamma(x)\sim\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{x}}\big(\frac{x}{e}\big)^{x}
.

\displaystyle\Gamma(\frac{d}{2}+1)^{\frac{2}{d(d+1)}}\sim

\displaystyle\Bigg(\sqrt{\frac{4\pi}{d+2}}\bigg(\frac{2+d}{2\mathrm{e}}\bigg)^{\frac{d}{2}+1}\Bigg)^{\frac{2}{d(d+1)}}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\Bigg(2\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{d+2}}\bigg(\frac{2+d}{\pi}\bigg)^{\frac{d}{2}+1}\Bigg)^{\frac{2}{d(d+1)}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\bigg(\frac{2+d}{\pi}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{d}}.

Notice that if
d\geq 1
, then the following two inequalities hold:
\frac{2+d}{\pi}\leq d
, and
d^{\frac{1}{d}}\leq\mathrm{e}
. So we have

1\leq\Gamma(\frac{d}{2}+1)^{\frac{2}{d(d+1)}}\leq 2\mathrm{e}.

(2.5)

The lower bound is trivial because
\Gamma(x)\geq 1
when
x\geq 1
. Actually, we cheated a little bit, because the asymptotic formula
\Gamma(x)\sim\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{x}}\big(\frac{x}{e}\big)^{x}
holds only when
x
is large. The inequality (
2.5
) is true only when
d
is large. However, this is really a minor problem, since we’re only interested in the behavior of the constant when
d
is large, and we may assume
d
is already large at the very beginning.

These bounds implies that the first factor
\frac{1}{(v_{d})^{\frac{2}{d(d+1)}}}
lies in the interval
[\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}},2\mathrm{e}]
, which does not give a decay or growth on dimension
d
. Like the first factor, the 2nd factor also makes no contribution on the growth because

\frac{1}{4}\leq\frac{1}{2^{\frac{d+3}{d(d+1)}}}\leq 1.

Recall the surface area
\omega_{d-1}=\frac{2\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}}{\Gamma(\frac{d}{2})}
. So for the 3rd factor, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\omega_{d-1}^{\frac{2}{d+1}}}=

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(\frac{d}{2})^{\frac{2}{d+1}}}{(2\pi^{\frac{d}{2}})^{\frac{2}{d+1}}}>\frac{\Gamma(\frac{d}{2})^{\frac{2}{d+1}}}{10}.

Combining all three factors yields

\frac{1}{(v_{d})^{\frac{2}{d(d+1)}}2^{\frac{d+3}{d(d+1)}}\omega_{d-1}^{\frac{2}{d+1}}}>\frac{1}{100}\Gamma(\frac{d}{2})^{\frac{2}{d+1}}.

Finally

\displaystyle\Gamma(\frac{d}{2})^{\frac{2}{d+1}}\sim\Bigg(\sqrt{\frac{4\pi}{d}}\bigg(\frac{d}{2e}\bigg)^{\frac{d}{2}}\Bigg)^{\frac{2}{d+1}}>\bigg(\frac{d}{2e}\bigg)^{1-\frac{2}{d+1}}=\frac{d}{2e}\bigg(\frac{d}{2e}\bigg)^{-\frac{2}{d+1}}>\frac{d}{2\mathrm{e}^{3}}.

So

\bigg(\frac{c_{d}}{\omega_{d-1}}\bigg)^{\frac{2}{d+1}}>\frac{1}{10^{5}}d,

which implies that

\displaystyle\frac{V(f)}{||f||_{2}^{2}}\cdot\frac{V(\hat{f})}{||\hat{f}||_{2}^{2}}\geq\bigg(\frac{c^{2}_{d}}{\omega^{2}_{d-1}}\bigg)^{\frac{2}{d+1}}\geq\frac{1}{10^{10}}d^{2}.

(3.1)

) holds, replacing

This theorem was proved in

3.1
Choice of some parameters

The following proposition and (
3.2
) are substitutes of (
2.1
) in
L^{p}
case.

Proposition 3.1
.

For every
p\in(1,\frac{2d}{d-1})
, one can always find an
\epsilon>0
so that

\frac{(d+\epsilon)p}{d+\epsilon+p}>1,\text{ and }p\leq\frac{2(d+\epsilon)}{d-1+\epsilon}.

Proof.

These two inequalities are equivalent to

\epsilon>\frac{d+p-dp}{p-1},\text{ and }(p-2)\epsilon\leq 2d-p(d-1).

Notice that
2d-p(d-1)>0
always holds. There are two cases.

•

When
p\in(1,2]
: The second inequality
(p-2)\epsilon\leq 2d-p(d-1)
is trivial, because
(p-2)\epsilon
is negative while
2d-p(d-1)>0
. We only need to choose an
\epsilon>0
satisfies the first inequality, for example
\epsilon=p/(p-1)
.

•

When
p\in(2,\frac{2d}{d-1})
: Now we need to prove the existence of
\delta>0
so that

\frac{d+p-dp}{p-1}<\delta\leq\frac{2d-p(d-1)}{p-2}.

It suffices to prove that the interval
(\frac{d+p-dp}{p-1},\frac{2d-p(d-1)}{p-2}]
is non-empty, and the right endpoint
\frac{2d-p(d-1)}{p-2}>0
. These are just direct calculations.

∎

Now we choose, for
\epsilon
in Proposition
3.1
,

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle:=\frac{p(d+\epsilon)}{d+\epsilon+p},

(3.2)

\displaystyle r

\displaystyle:=\frac{p}{a}=\frac{d+\epsilon+p}{d+\epsilon},\ s=\frac{d+\epsilon+p}{p},

\displaystyle T

\displaystyle:=c_{d}\bigg(\frac{||f||_{a}}{||f||_{p}}\bigg)^{\frac{as}{d}}=c_{d}\bigg(\frac{||f||_{a}}{||f||_{p}}\bigg)^{\frac{d+\epsilon}{d}},

where
c_{d}
is a constant independent of function
f
to be determined later, see (
3.3
). Notice that
1/r+1/s=1
, and
1<r,s<\infty
. By Proposition
3.1
, it’s easy to verify

1<a<p,\text{ and }\frac{1}{a}+\frac{1}{p}\geq 1.

So the pair
(a,p)
verifies the hypothesis in Theorem
4
. In fact, the inequality
a<p
is trivial, and
a>1
is equivalent to
(d+\epsilon)p/(d+\epsilon+p)>1
. Also, the second inequality
1/a+1/p\geq 1
is equivalent to
p\leq 2(d+\epsilon)/(d-1+\epsilon)
.

Similar to the case
L^{2}
, we use Hölder inequality for
p
and
q
:

\displaystyle\int_{|x|\leq T}|f|^{a}\leq

\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{|x|\leq T}1\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{s}}\cdot\bigg(\int_{|x|\leq T}|f|^{ar}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(T^{d}v_{d})^{\frac{1}{s}}||f||_{p}^{\frac{p}{r}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(v_{d}c_{d}^{d})^{\frac{1}{s}}\bigg(\frac{||f||_{a}}{||f||_{p}}\bigg)^{a}||f||_{p}^{\frac{p}{r}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(v_{d}c_{d}^{d})^{\frac{1}{s}}||f||_{a}^{a}.

Choose
c_{d}
so that

(v_{d}c^{d}_{d})^{\frac{1}{s}}=1/2,

(3.3)

and we have

\int_{|x|>T}|f|^{a}\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f|^{a}.

For the integral
\int_{|x|>T}|f|^{a}
, again, we use Hölder inequality. In
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
case, we used
\int_{|x|>T}1/|x|^{d+1}
. Now we replace it by
\int_{|x|>T}1/|x|^{d+\epsilon}
.

\displaystyle\int_{|x|\geq T}|f|^{a}=

\displaystyle\int_{|x|\geq T}\frac{1}{|x|^{a}}|x|^{a}|f(x)|^{a}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{|x|\geq T}\frac{1}{|x|^{as}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{s}}\cdot\bigg(\int_{|x|\geq T}|x|^{ar}|f(x)|^{ar}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{|x|\geq T}\frac{1}{|x|^{d+\epsilon}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{s}}\cdot\bigg(\int_{|x|\geq T}|x|^{p}|f(x)|^{p}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{\omega_{d-1}}{\epsilon}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{s}}\bigg(\frac{1}{T}\bigg)^{\frac{\epsilon}{s}}V_{p}(f)^{\frac{1}{r}}.

We have

\bigg(\frac{1}{T}\bigg)^{\frac{\epsilon}{s}}=\bigg(\frac{1}{c_{d}^{\epsilon}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{s}}\bigg(\frac{||f||_{p}}{||f||_{a}}\bigg)^{\frac{a\epsilon}{d}}

So

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}||f||_{a}^{a}\leq\int_{|x|\geq T}|f|^{a}\leq

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{\omega_{d-1}}{\epsilon c_{d}^{\epsilon}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{s}}\bigg(\frac{||f||_{p}}{||f||_{a}}\bigg)^{\frac{a\epsilon}{d}}V_{p}(f)^{\frac{1}{r}}.

This is equivalent to

\displaystyle\frac{||f||_{a}^{a+\frac{a\epsilon}{d}}}{||f||_{p}^{\frac{a\epsilon}{d}+\frac{p}{r}}}\leq 2\bigg(\frac{\omega_{d-1}}{\epsilon c_{d}^{\epsilon}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{s}}\bigg(\frac{V_{p}(f)}{||f||_{p}^{p}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{r}}.

By the choice of
p
and
a
, we have
p/r=a
. Therefore

\displaystyle\frac{V_{p}(f)}{||f||_{p}^{p}}\geq\frac{1}{2}\bigg(\frac{\epsilon c_{d}^{\epsilon}}{2\omega_{d-1}}\bigg)^{r-1}\bigg(\frac{||f||_{a}}{||f||_{p}}\bigg)^{p(1+\frac{\epsilon}{d})}.

Replace
f
by
\hat{f}
,

\displaystyle\frac{V_{p}(\hat{f})}{||\hat{f}||_{p}^{p}}\geq\frac{1}{2}\bigg(\frac{\epsilon c_{d}^{\epsilon}}{2\omega_{d-1}}\bigg)^{r-1}\bigg(\frac{||\hat{f}||_{a}}{||\hat{f}||_{p}}\bigg)^{p(1+\frac{\epsilon}{d})}.

Taking a product and use Theorem
4
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{V_{p}(\hat{f})\cdot V_{p}(f)}{||\hat{f}||_{p}^{p}\cdot||f||_{p}^{p}}\geq\frac{1}{4}\bigg(\frac{\epsilon c_{d}^{\epsilon}}{2\omega_{d-1}}\bigg)^{2(r-1)}\bigg(\frac{||f||_{a}\cdot||\hat{f}||_{a}}{||f||_{p}\cdot||\hat{f}||_{p}}\bigg)^{p(1+\frac{\epsilon}{d})}\geq\frac{1}{4}\bigg(\frac{\epsilon c_{d}^{\epsilon}}{2\omega_{d-1}}\bigg)^{2(r-1)}.

3.2
Asymptotic behavior of the quotient

In this subsection, we study the growth of the quotient
\big(\frac{\epsilon c_{d}^{\epsilon}}{2\omega_{d-1}}\big)^{r-1}
when
d
tends to infinity and
p
fixed. Notice that if
p
is fixed and
1<p<2d/(d-1)
holds for all
d\geq 1
, then we must have
1<p\leq 2
. The case
p=2
has been proved. Therefore, in this subsection, we consider a fixed number
p\in(1,2)
. Moreover, once
p\in(1,2)
is fixed, the choice of
\epsilon
in Proposition
3.1
can be made independent of
d
, for example, we may take
\epsilon=p/(p-1)>1
. By (
3.3
),

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{\epsilon c_{d}^{\epsilon}}{2\omega_{d-1}}\bigg)^{r-1}=\bigg(\frac{\epsilon}{2^{\frac{s\epsilon}{d}+1}}\bigg)^{r-1}\bigg(\frac{1}{\omega_{d-1}v_{d}^{\frac{\epsilon}{d}}}\bigg)^{r-1}

Recall
r=1+\frac{p}{d+\epsilon}
, for the second factor, we have

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{1}{\omega_{d-1}v_{d}^{\frac{\epsilon}{d}}}\bigg)^{r-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\omega_{d-1}^{\frac{p}{d+\epsilon}}v_{d}^{\frac{\epsilon p}{d(d+\epsilon)}}}

Asymptotic behavior of
\big(\epsilon/2^{\frac{s\epsilon}{d}+1}\big)^{r-1}
:

Recall that
\epsilon
is independent of
d
, we have

\bigg(\frac{\epsilon}{2^{\frac{s\epsilon}{d}+1}}\bigg)^{r-1}=\frac{\epsilon^{\frac{p}{d+\epsilon}}}{2^{\frac{\epsilon r}{d}+(r-1)}}\sim\frac{1}{2^{(\frac{\epsilon}{d}+1)r}}=\frac{1}{2^{\frac{d+\epsilon+p}{d}}}\sim 1.

Asymptotic behavior of
v_{d}^{\frac{\epsilon p}{d(d+\epsilon)}}
:

We have

\frac{1}{v_{d}^{\frac{\epsilon p}{d(d+\epsilon)}}}=\frac{\Gamma\Big(\frac{d}{2}+1\Big)^{\frac{\epsilon p}{d(d+\epsilon)}}}{\pi^{\frac{p}{2(d+\epsilon)}}}\sim\Gamma\Big(\frac{d}{2}+1\Big)^{\frac{\epsilon p}{d(d+\epsilon)}}\sim\bigg(\sqrt{\frac{4\pi}{d+2}}\Big(\frac{d+2}{2\mathrm{e}}\Big)^{\frac{d}{2}+1}\bigg)^{\frac{\epsilon p}{d(d+\epsilon)}}\sim 1.

Asymptotic behavior of
\omega_{d-1}^{\frac{p}{d+\epsilon}}
:

We have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\omega_{d-1}^{\frac{p}{d+\epsilon}}}=\frac{\Gamma(d/2)^{\frac{p}{d+\epsilon}}}{(2\pi^{d/2})^{\frac{p}{d+\epsilon}}}\sim\Gamma\Big(\frac{d}{2}\Big)^{\frac{p}{d+\epsilon}}\sim\bigg(\sqrt{\frac{4\pi}{d}}\big(\frac{d}{2\mathrm{e}}\big)^{\frac{d}{2}}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{d+\epsilon}}.

The following inequalities holds for all
d\geq 1
:

\sqrt{\frac{2\mathrm{e}}{d}}\big(\frac{d}{2\mathrm{e}}\big)^{\frac{d}{2}}<\sqrt{\frac{4\pi}{d}}\big(\frac{d}{2\mathrm{e}}\big)^{\frac{d}{2}}<2\sqrt{\frac{2\mathrm{e}}{d}}\big(\frac{d}{2\mathrm{e}}\big)^{\frac{d}{2}}.

Therefore

\bigg(\sqrt{\frac{4\pi}{d}}\big(\frac{d}{2\mathrm{e}}\big)^{\frac{d}{2}}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{d+\epsilon}}\sim\bigg(\sqrt{\frac{2\mathrm{e}}{d}}\big(\frac{d}{2\mathrm{e}}\big)^{\frac{d}{2}}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{d+\epsilon}}=\bigg(\frac{d}{2\mathrm{e}}\bigg)^{\frac{p(d-1)}{2(d+\epsilon)}}\sim d^{\frac{p}{2}}.

Sharpness of the growth
d^{p}
:

Taking Gaussian
g(x)=e^{-\pi|x|^{2}}
, a direct calculation shows that

||g||_{p}^{p}=\frac{1}{p^{\frac{d}{2}}},\text{ and }V_{p}(g)=\frac{\omega_{d-1}}{2(\pi p)^{\frac{p+d}{2}}}\Gamma\Big(\frac{p+d}{2}\Big).

Therefore,

\frac{V_{p}(g)}{||g||_{p}^{p}}\frac{V_{p}(\hat{g})}{||\hat{g}||^{p}_{p}}=\frac{1}{(\pi p)^{p}}\frac{\Gamma\Big(\frac{p+d}{2}\Big)^{2}}{\Gamma\Big(\frac{d}{2}\Big)^{2}}\sim d^{p},

which shows that up to a dimension-free constant, the growth
d^{p}
is optimal.

3.3
Sharpness of the range
p

To prove the sharpness of the range, we need to show that when
p\in(\frac{2d}{d-1},\infty)
,

\inf_{f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\setminus\{0\}}\frac{V_{p}(f)}{||f||_{p}^{p}}\frac{V_{p}(\hat{f})}{||\hat{f}||^{p}_{p}}=0.

We use the functions

g_{c}=c^{-\frac{d}{2}}\mathrm{e}^{-\pi\frac{|x|^{2}}{c^{2}}}+c^{\frac{d}{2}}\mathrm{e}^{-\pi c^{2}|x|^{2}},\ c>0.

Notice that
g_{c}=\widehat{g_{c}}
, and these functions already appeared in
[
6
]
when
d=1
. We estimate the
L^{p}
norm of
g_{c}
and
|x|\cdot|g_{c}|

\displaystyle\int|g_{c}|^{p}\geq

\displaystyle c^{-\frac{dp}{2}}\int\mathrm{e}^{-\pi\frac{p|x|^{2}}{c^{2}}}\mathrm{d}x+c^{\frac{dp}{2}}\int\mathrm{e}^{-\pi pc^{2}|x|^{2}}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c^{-\frac{dp}{2}}\cdot\frac{c^{d}}{p^{\frac{d}{2}}}+c^{\frac{dp}{2}}\cdot\frac{1}{p^{\frac{d}{2}}c^{d}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{c^{d-\frac{dp}{2}}+c^{\frac{dp}{2}-d}}{p^{\frac{d}{2}}}.

For
|x|g_{c}
, we have

\displaystyle\int|x|^{p}|g_{c}|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p-1}\int|x|^{p}c^{-\frac{dp}{2}}\mathrm{e}^{-\pi\frac{p|x|^{2}}{c^{2}}}+|x|^{p}c^{\frac{dp}{2}}\mathrm{e}^{-\pi pc^{2}|x|^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{p-1}\omega_{d-1}\Gamma(\frac{d+p}{2})}{2(\pi p)^{\frac{d+p}{2}}}\bigg(c^{d+p-\frac{dp}{2}}+c^{-(d+p)+\frac{dp}{2}}\bigg).

So

\frac{\int|x|^{p}|g_{c}|^{p}}{\int|g_{c}|^{p}}\lesssim\frac{c^{d+p-\frac{dp}{2}}+c^{-(d+p)+\frac{dp}{2}}}{c^{d-\frac{dp}{2}}+c^{\frac{dp}{2}-d}}=:h(c),

where the implicit constant in
\lesssim
is independent of
c
. Let
t:=c^{\frac{dp}{2}-d}
, then

\frac{c^{d+p-\frac{dp}{2}}+c^{-(d+p)+\frac{dp}{2}}}{c^{d-\frac{dp}{2}}+c^{\frac{dp}{2}-d}}=\frac{t^{\frac{2}{(p-2)d}(d+p-\frac{dp}{2})}+t^{-\frac{2}{(p-2)d}(d+p-\frac{dp}{2})}}{t+\frac{1}{t}}

There are two cases

•

The case
d+p-\frac{dp}{2}\geq 0
. Notice that

, so we have
\frac{2}{(p-2)d}(d+p-\frac{dp}{2})\geq 0
. In this case, it’s easy to check that

•

The case

, now we have

. It is easy to check that

So after a change of variable

, the function

has the form

\frac{t^{\alpha}+\frac{1}{t^{\alpha}}}{t+\frac{1}{t}},\ 0\leq\alpha<1.

Let

.

4.1
Proof of Theorem

Similar to the proof of Theorem

Proposition 4.1
.

There exists

We consider two cases:

•

∎

Once

(4.1)

\frac{d}{\epsilon}=\frac{d+\phi q-\delta\phi q}{\delta\phi q}.

By Proposition

, the numerator

, so
\epsilon>0
. By the same reason, we have
\tilde{\epsilon}>0
.

Now we choose

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle:=\frac{p}{1+\frac{p\theta}{d+\epsilon}},

\displaystyle r

\displaystyle:=\frac{2}{a}\text{ and }s:=\frac{r}{r-1},

\displaystyle T

\displaystyle:=c_{d}\bigg(\frac{||f||_{a}}{||f||_{2}}\bigg)^{\frac{as}{d}},

where

.

By Hölder inequality for the pair

as before, we have

\frac{1}{2}||f||_{a}^{a}\leq\int_{|x|>T}|f|^{a}.

Now we choose
b>0
and

4.2.1
The case

Our goal is to construct an

For this, we need a theorem of Wainger. The following version is a special case of Wainger’s theorem, where we choose

in slowly varying class(
[

Theorem 5

(
[
4
]

.

Let

. Let

•

•

Assume that

Define

(1)

-differentiable when

(2)

For any integer

When

The error term satisfies

Also, if

To apply this theorem, we define our

However, the function

The function

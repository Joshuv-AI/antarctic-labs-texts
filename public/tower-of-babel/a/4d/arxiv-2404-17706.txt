Energy decay for semilinear evolution equations with memory and time-dependent time delay feedback
Elisa Continelli, Cristina Pignotti

Abstract
In this paper, we study well-posedness and exponential stability for semilinear second order evolution equations with memory and time-varying delay feedback. The time delay function is assumed to be continuous and bounded. Under a suitable assumption on the delay feedback, we are able to prove that solutions corresponding to small initial data are globally defined and satisfy an exponential decay estimate.



1
Introduction

Let
H
be a Hilbert space and let
A
be a positive self-adjoint operator with dense domain
D(A)
in
H.
Let us consider the system:

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{u_{tt}(t)+Au(t)-\int_{0}^{+\infty}\beta(s)Au(t-s)ds+k(t)BB^{*}u_{t}(t-\tau(t))=\nabla\psi(u(t)),}\\
\hskip 341.43306pt\ t\in(0,+\infty),\\
\displaystyle{u(t)=u_{0}(t),\quad t\in(-\infty,0],}\\
\displaystyle{u_{t}(t)=g(t),\quad t\in[-\bar{\tau},0],}\end{array}

(1.1)

where
\bar{\tau}
is a fixed positive constant and the function
\tau:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
represents the time dependent time delay. We assume that the time delay is a continuous function satisfying

\tau(t)\leqslant\bar{\tau},\quad\forall\ t\geqslant 0.

(1.2)

In (
1.1
),
B
is a bounded linear operator of
H
into itself,
B^{*}
denotes its adjoint. Also,
(u_{0}(\cdot),g(\cdot))
are the initial data taken in suitable spaces and we denote with
u_{1}:=g(0)
.

Moreover, on the delay damping coefficient
k:[-\bar{\tau},+\infty)\rightarrow{{\rm I}\kern-1.49994pt{\rm R}}
we assume that
k(\cdot)\in L^{1}_{loc}([-\bar{\tau},+\infty))
and the integral on time intervals of length
\bar{\tau}
is uniformly bounded, i.e. there exists a positive constant
K
such that

\int_{t-\bar{\tau}}^{t}|k(s)|ds<K,\quad\forall t\geqslant 0.

(1.3)

The memory kernel
\beta:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
satisfies the following classical assumptions:

(i)

\beta\in C^{1}({{\rm I}\kern-1.49994pt{\rm R}}^{+})\cap L^{1}({{\rm I}\kern-1.49994pt{\rm R}}^{+})
;

(ii)

\beta(0)=\beta_{0}>0
;

(iii)

\int_{0}^{+\infty}\beta(t)dt=\tilde{\beta}<1
;

(iv)

\beta^{\prime}(t)\leqslant-\delta\beta(t)
, for some
\delta>0
.

The nonlinear term satisfies some local Lipschitz continuity property; we will state precisely the assumptions later.

Time delays are often present in applications and physical models and it is by now well-known that a time lag, even arbitrarily small, may induce instability effects also in systems that are uniformly exponentially stable in the absence of time delays (see e.g.
[
16
,
28
,
39
]
). Nevertheless, suitable choices of the time delay value can return stability (cf.
[
21
,
22
]
) as well as suitable
feedback laws (cf.
[
28
,
39
]
).

Here, we are interested in studying well–posedness and exponential stability, for small initial data, for the above model (
1.1
).
Our results extend the ones in
[
34
]
where the time delay is assumed to be constant (see also
[
11
]
for the constant delay case). The extension is not trivial since the classical step by step argument, often used to deal with time delay models, does not work in this case. Moreover, our exponential decay proof, valid also in the constant case, significantly simplifies the scheme of
[
34
]
.

Time-dependent time delays are also considered in the paper
[
25
]
dealing with abstract evolution equations with delay but, in the case of semilinear wave equations with memory damping, an extra frictional not delayed damping was needed (see also
[
29
,
30
]
). In this paper, we focus then on wave-type equations with viscoelastic damping, delay feedback, and source term, obtaining well-posedness and stability results for small initial data without adding any extra frictional not delayed dampings. Moreover, concerning the time delay function, here we work in a very general setting. Indeed, in
[
25
]
, in addition to (
1.2
), it is assumed that the time delay function belongs to

W^{1,\infty}(0,+\infty)
with

\tau^{\prime}(t)\leqslant c<1.

These are the standard assumptions used to deal with wave-type equations with a time dependent time delay (see e.g.
[
31
,
13
,
17
]
).

Here, instead, we only assume that the time delay is a continuous function bounded from above.
Therefore, our results improve previous related literature in a significant way.

Other models with memory damping and time delay effects have been studied in the recent literature. The first result is due to
[
23
]
, in the linear setting. In that paper, a standard frictional damping, not delayed, is included in the model to compensate for the destabilizing effect of the delay feedback. As later understood, the viscoelastic damping alone can counter the destabilizing delay effect, under suitable assumptions, without the need of any artificial extra dampings. This has been shown, e.g., in
[
5
,
15
,
20
,
40
]
. The case of intermittent delay feedback has been studied in
[
36
]
while the paper
[
27
]

analyzes a plate equation with memory, source term, delay feedback and, in the same spirit of
[
23
]
, an extra not delayed frictional damping. Models for wave-type equations with memory damping have been previously studied by several authors in the undelayed case (see e.g.
[
3
,
4
,
10
]
). See also
[
2
]
for results on the Timoshenko model, also in the undelayed case, and extensions to the time delay framework (see e.g.
[
38
,
7
]
).

More rich is the literature in the case of frictional/structural damping, instead of a memory term, which compensates for the destabilizing effect of time delays and, for specific models, various stability results have been quite recently obtained under suitable assumptions (see e.g.
[
1
,
6
,
7
,
12
,
8
,
28
,
32
,
24
,
9
,
37
,
39
]
).

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we detail our functional setting and prove some preliminary results; in particular, we rewrite system (
1.1
) in an abstract form. In Section 3, we prove the exponential decay of the energy associated to (
1.1
). Finally, in Section 4 some examples are illustrated.

2
Preliminaries

Here, we precise the functional setting for our analysis and prove some preliminary results.

On the nonlinear term, as in
[
4
,
34
]
, we assume that
\psi:D(A^{\frac{1}{2}})\rightarrow{{\rm I}\kern-1.49994pt{\rm R}}
is a functional having Gâteaux derivative
D\psi(u)
at every
u\in D(A^{\frac{1}{2}}).
Moreover, we assume the following hypotheses:

(H1)

For every
u\in D(A^{\frac{1}{2}})
, there exists a constant
c(u)>0
such that

|D\psi(u)(v)|\leqslant c(u)||v||_{{H}}\qquad\forall v\in{D}(A^{\frac{1}{2}}).

Then,
\psi
can be extended to the whole

and we denote by

the unique vector representing

in the Riesz isomorphism, i.e.

\langle\nabla\psi(u),v\rangle_{H}=D\psi(u)(v),\qquad\forall v\in H;

(H2)

for all
r>0
there exists a constant
L(r)>0
such that

||\nabla\psi(u)-\nabla\psi(v)||_{H}\leqslant L(r)||A^{\frac{1}{2}}(u-v)||_{H},

for all
u,v\in{D}(A^{\frac{1}{2}})
satisfying
||A^{\frac{1}{2}}u||_{H}\leqslant r
and
||A^{\frac{1}{2}}v||_{H}\leqslant r
.

(H3)

\psi(0)=0,

\nabla\psi(0)=0
and
there exists a strictly increasing continuous function
h
such that

||\nabla\psi(u)||_{H}\leqslant h(||A^{\frac{1}{2}}u||_{H})||A^{\frac{1}{2}}u||_{H},

(2.1)

for all
u\in{D}(A^{\frac{1}{2}})
.

As in Dafermos
[
14
]
, we define the function

\eta^{t}(s):=u(t)-u(t-s),\quad s,t\in(0,+\infty),

(2.2)

so that we can rewrite (
1.1
) in the following way:

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{u_{tt}(t)+(1-\tilde{\beta})Au(t)+\int_{0}^{+\infty}\beta(s)A\eta^{t}(s)ds+k(t)BB^{*}u_{t}(t-\tau(t))}\\
\displaystyle{\hskip 227.62204pt=\nabla\psi(u(t)),\ t\in(0,+\infty),}\\
\displaystyle{\eta^{t}_{t}(s)=-\eta^{t}_{s}(s)+u_{t}(t),\ \quad t,s\in(0,+\infty),}\\
\displaystyle{u(0)=u_{0}(0),}\\
\displaystyle{u_{t}(t)=g(t),\quad t\in[-\bar{\tau},0],}\\
\displaystyle{\eta^{0}(s)=\eta_{0}(s)=u_{0}(0)-u_{0}(-s)\quad s\in(0,+\infty).}\end{array}

(2.3)

Let us define the energy of the model (
1.1
) (equivalently (
2.3
)) as

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{E(t):=E(u(t))=\frac{1}{2}||u_{t}(t)||_{H}^{2}+\frac{1-\tilde{\beta}}{2}||A^{\frac{1}{2}}u(t)||^{2}_{H}-\psi(u)}\\
\hskip 56.9055pt\displaystyle{+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\beta(s)||A^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta^{t}(s)||^{2}_{H}ds+\frac{1}{2}\int_{t-\bar{\tau}}^{t}|k(s)|\cdot||B^{*}u_{t}(s)||_{H}^{2}ds.}\end{array}

(2.4)

Note that, apart from the last term, this is the natural energy for nonlinear wave-type equations with memory (cf. e.g.
[
4
]
). The additional term

\frac{1}{2}\int_{t-\bar{\tau}}^{t}|k(s)|\cdot\|B^{*}u_{t}(s)\|_{H}^{2}\,ds

is crucial in order to deal with the delay feedback in the case of time varying time delay (cf.
[
25
,
34
]
for similar terms).

Moreover, let us define the functional

\mathcal{E}(t):=\max\left\{\frac{1}{2}\max_{s\in[-\bar{\tau},0]}\|g(s)\|_{H}^{2},\,\,\max_{s\in[0,t]}E(s)\right\}.

In particular, for
t=0
,

\mathcal{E}(0):=\max\left\{\frac{1}{2}\max_{s\in[-\bar{\tau},0]}\|g(s)\|_{H}^{2},\,\,E(0)\right\}.

We will show that, thanks to the assumption (
2.1
), for solutions to system (
1.1
) corresponding to sufficiently
small
initial data, the energy is positive for any
t\geqslant 0.
Moreover, we will prove that an exponential decay estimate holds.

First of all, we will reformulate (
2.3
) (see (
2.8
)) as an abstract first order equation.
Let us denote

\|B\|_{\mathcal{L}(H)}=\|B^{*}\|_{\mathcal{L}(H)}=b.

(2.5)

Our result will be obtained under an assumption on the coefficient
k(t)
of the delay feedback.
More precisely, we assume (cf.
[
25
]
) that
there exist two constants
\omega^{\prime}\in[0,\omega)
and
\gamma\in{{\rm I}\kern-1.49994pt{\rm R}}
such that

b^{2}Me^{\omega\bar{\tau}}\int_{0}^{t}|k(s)|ds\leqslant\gamma+\omega^{\prime}t,\quad\mbox{\rm for all}\ t\geqslant 0.

(2.6)

Note that (
2.6
) includes, as particular cases,
k
integrable or
k
in
L^{\infty}
with
\|k\|_{\infty}
sufficiently small.

Let
L^{2}_{\beta}((0,+\infty);D(A^{\frac{1}{2}}))
be the Hilbert space of the
D(A^{\frac{1}{2}})-
valued functions in
(0,+\infty)
endowed with the scalar product

\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle_{L^{2}_{\beta}((0,+\infty);D(A^{\frac{1}{2}}))}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\beta(s)\langle A^{\frac{1}{2}}\varphi,A^{\frac{1}{2}}\psi\rangle_{H}ds

and denote by
{\mathcal{H}}
the Hilbert space

\mathcal{H}=D(A^{\frac{1}{2}})\times H\times L^{2}_{\beta}((0,+\infty);D(A^{\frac{1}{2}})),

equipped with the inner product

\left\langle\left(\begin{array}[]{l}u\\
v\\
w\end{array}\right),\left(\begin{array}[]{l}\tilde{u}\\
\tilde{v}\\
\tilde{w}\end{array}\right)\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}:=(1-\tilde{\beta})\langle A^{\frac{1}{2}}u,A^{\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{u}\rangle_{H}+\langle v,\tilde{v}\rangle_{H}+\int_{0}^{\infty}\beta(s)\langle A^{\frac{1}{2}}w,A^{\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{w}\rangle_{H}ds.

(2.7)

Setting
U=(u,u_{t},\eta^{t})
, we can restate (
1.1
) in the abstract form

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{U^{\prime}(t)=\mathcal{A}U(t)-k(t)\mathcal{B}U(t-\tau(t))+F(U(t)),}\\
\displaystyle{U(s)=\tilde{g}(s),\quad s\in[-\bar{\tau},0],}\end{array}

(2.8)

where the operator
{\mathcal{A}}
is defined by

\mathcal{A}\begin{pmatrix}u\\
v\\
w\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}v\\
-(1-\tilde{\beta})Au-\int_{0}^{+\infty}\beta(s)Aw(s)ds\\
-w_{s}+v\end{pmatrix}

with domain

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle{{D}(\mathcal{A})=\{(u,v,w)\in D(A^{\frac{1}{2}})\times D(A^{\frac{1}{2}})\times L_{\beta}^{2}((0,+\infty);D(A^{\frac{1}{2}})):}\\
\hskip 42.67912pt\displaystyle{(1-\tilde{\beta})u+\int_{0}^{+\infty}\beta(s)w(s)ds\in D(A),\quad w_{s}\in L^{2}_{\beta}((0,+\infty);D(A^{\frac{1}{2}}))\},}\end{array}

(2.9)

in the Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}},
and the operator
{\mathcal{B}}:{\mathcal{H}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{H}}
is defined by

{\mathcal{B}}\left(\begin{array}[]{l}u\\
v\\
w\end{array}\right):=\left(\begin{array}[]{l}0\\
BB^{*}v\\
0\end{array}\right).

Note that, by (
2.5
), it turns out that
\|{\mathcal{B}}\|_{\mathcal{L(H)}}=b^{2}.

Moreover,
\tilde{g}(s)=(u_{0}(0),g(s),\eta_{0})
for
s\in[-\bar{\tau},0]
, and we denote
U_{0}:=\tilde{g}(0)=(u_{0}(0),u_{1},\eta_{0})
. Also,
F(U):=(0,\nabla\psi(u),0)^{T}.
From (H2) and (H3) we deduce that
the function
F
satisfies:

(F1)

F(0)=0
;

(F2)

for each
r>0
there exists a constant
L(r)>0
such that

||F(U)-F(V)||_{\mathcal{H}}\leqslant L(r)||U-V||_{\mathcal{H}}

(2.10)

whenever
||U||_{\mathcal{H}}\leqslant r
and
||V||_{\mathcal{H}}\leqslant r

It is well-known (see e.g.
[
18
]
) that the operator
{\mathcal{A}}

in the problem’s formulation (
2.8
), corresponding to the linear undelayed part of the model, generates an exponentially stable semigroup

\{S(t)\}_{t\geqslant 0},
namely there exist two constants

M,\omega>0
such that

||S(t)||_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})}\leqslant Me^{-\omega t}.

(2.11)

First of all, we can prove a local well-posedness result.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let us consider the system (
2.8
) with initial datum
\tilde{g}\in C([-\bar{\tau},0];\mathcal{H}).
Then, there exists a unique local solution
U(\cdot)
defined on a time interval
[0,\delta)
.

Proof.

Let
\tilde{g}\in C([-\bar{\tau},0];\mathcal{H})
. We set

C:=\max\left\{2M\max_{s\in[-\bar{\tau},0]}\lVert\tilde{g}(s)\rVert_{\mathcal{H}},\max_{s\in[-\bar{\tau},0]}\lVert\tilde{g}(s)\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}\right\}.

Let
\xi>0
be a sufficiently small time such that

\xi L(C)+b^{2}\lVert k\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{1}([0,\xi];{{\rm I}\kern-1.22911pt{\rm R}})}<\frac{1}{4M},

(2.12)

with
L(C)
as in (
2.10
).
Let us denote

C_{\tilde{g}}([-\bar{\tau},\xi];{\mathcal{H}}):=\{U\in C([-\bar{\tau},\xi];{\mathcal{H}}):U(s)=\tilde{g}(s),\,\forall s\in[-\bar{\tau},0]\}.

Note that
C_{\tilde{g}}([-\bar{\tau},\xi];{\mathcal{H}})
is a nonempty and closed subset of
C([-\bar{\tau},\xi];{\mathcal{H}})
.
As a consequence,
(C_{\tilde{g}}([-\bar{\tau},\xi];{\mathcal{H}}),\lVert\cdot\rVert_{C([-\bar{\tau},\xi];{\mathcal{H}})})
is a Banach space.
Moreover, let us denote

C^{C}_{\tilde{g}}([-\bar{\tau},\xi];{\mathcal{H}}):=\{U\in C_{\tilde{g}}([-\bar{\tau},\xi];{\mathcal{H}}):\lVert U(t)\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}\leqslant C,\,\forall t\in[-\bar{\tau},\xi]\}.

Let us note that
C^{C}_{\tilde{g}}([-\bar{\tau},\xi];{\mathcal{H}})
is nonempty since it suffices to take

U(s)=\begin{cases}U_{0},\quad&s\in[0,\xi],\\
\tilde{g}(s),\quad&s\in[-\bar{\tau},0),\end{cases}

to have that
U\in C_{\tilde{g}}([-\bar{\tau},\xi];{\mathcal{H}})
and
\lVert U(t)\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}\leqslant\lVert\tilde{g}\rVert_{C([-\bar{\tau},\xi];{\mathcal{H}})}\leqslant C
, for all
t\in[-\bar{\tau},\xi]
. So,
U
belongs to
C^{C}_{\tilde{g}}([-\bar{\tau},\xi];{\mathcal{H}})
. Also, it is easy to see that
C^{C}_{\tilde{g}}([-\bar{\tau},\xi];{\mathcal{H}})
is closed in
C([-\bar{\tau},\xi];{\mathcal{H}})
. Hence,
(C^{C}_{\tilde{g}}([-\bar{\tau},\xi];{\mathcal{H}}),\lVert\cdot\rVert_{C([-\bar{\tau},\xi];{\mathcal{H}})})
is a Banach space too.

Next, we define the map

\Gamma:C^{C}_{\tilde{g}}([-\bar{\tau},\xi];{\mathcal{H}})\rightarrow C^{C}_{\tilde{g}}([-\bar{\tau},\xi];{\mathcal{H}})
given by

\Gamma U(t)=\begin{cases}S(t)U_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)[F(U(s))+k(s)\mathcal{B}U(s-\tau(s))]\,ds,\quad&t\in(0,\xi],\\
\tilde{g}(t),\quad&t\in[-\bar{\tau},0].\end{cases}

We claim that
\Gamma
is well-defined. Indeed, let
U\in C^{C}_{\tilde{g}}([-\bar{\tau},\xi];H)
. Then, from the semigroup theory,
t\mapsto S(t)U_{0}
is continuous. Also, since
U(\cdot)
is continuous in
[-\bar{\tau},\xi]
,
\tau(\cdot)
is a continuous function and
{\mathcal{B}}
is a bounded linear operator from
H
into itself,
[0,\xi]\ni t\mapsto{\mathcal{B}}U(t-\tau(t))
is continuous. Moreover,
k\in{\mathcal{L}}^{1}([0,\xi];{{\rm I}\kern-1.49994pt{\rm R}})
. So
k(\cdot){\mathcal{B}}U(\cdot-\tau(\cdot))\in{\mathcal{L}}^{1}([0,\xi];\mathcal{H})
. Also, since
U(\cdot)
is continuous in
[-\bar{\tau},\xi]
and
F(\cdot)
is locally Lipschitz continuous in
\mathcal{H}
from
(F_{2}),
the map
t\to F(U(t))
is continuous in
[-\bar{\tau},\xi]
. So,
F((U(\cdot)))\in{\mathcal{L}}^{1}([0,\xi];\mathcal{H})
. As a consequence, the map
t\mapsto\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)[F(U(s))+k(s){\mathcal{B}}U(s-\tau(s))]ds
is continuous in
[0,\xi]
. Thus,
\Gamma U\in C([0,\xi];H)
. Furthermore,
\Gamma U=\tilde{g}
in
[\bar{\tau},0]
. Finally, for all
t\in[-\bar{\tau},0]
,

\lVert\Gamma U(t)\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}=\lVert\tilde{g}(t)\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}\leqslant C.

On the other hand, for all
t\in(0,\xi]
, from
(F_{2})
with
F(0)=0
and
\lVert U\rVert_{C([-\bar{\tau},\xi];{\mathcal{H}})}\leqslant C
, we can write

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{\lVert\Gamma U(t)\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}\leqslant Me^{-\omega t}\lVert U_{0}\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}+M\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\omega(t-s)}(\lVert F(U(s))\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}+b^{2}\lvert k(s)\rvert\lVert U(s-\tau(s))\rVert_{\mathcal{H}})ds}\\
\displaystyle{\hskip 51.21504pt\leqslant M\lVert U_{0}\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}+M\int_{0}^{t}(L(C)+b^{2}\lvert k(s)\rvert)(\lVert U(s)\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}+\lVert U(s-\tau(s))\rVert_{\mathcal{H}})ds}\\
\displaystyle{\hskip 51.21504pt\leqslant M\lVert U_{0}\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}+2MC(\xi L(C)+b^{2}\lVert k\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{1}([0,\xi];{{\rm I}\kern-1.22911pt{\rm R}})}).}\end{array}

Thus, using (
2.12
), by definition of

we get

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{\lVert\Gamma U(t)\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}\leqslant\frac{C}{2}+2MC(\xi L(C)+b^{2}\lVert k\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{1}([0,\xi];{{\rm I}\kern-1.22911pt{\rm R}})})\leqslant\frac{C}{2}+2MC\frac{1}{4M}=C.}\end{array}

\lVert\Gamma U(t)\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}\leqslant C,\quad\forall t\in[-\bar{\tau},\xi].

So, we can conclude that
\Gamma
is well defined.

Next, we claim that
\Gamma
is a contraction. Indeed, let
U,V\in C^{C}_{\tilde{g}}([-\bar{\tau},\xi];H)
. Then, for all
t\in[-\bar{\tau},0]
,

\lVert\Gamma U(t)-\Gamma V(t)\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}=0.

On the other hand, for all
t\in(0,\xi]
, since
\lVert U\rVert_{C([-\bar{\tau},\xi];{\mathcal{H}})},\lVert V\rVert_{C([-\bar{\tau},\xi];{\mathcal{H}})}\leqslant C
, from

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\lVert\Gamma U(t)-\Gamma V(t)\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}\leqslant\int_{0}^{t}\lVert S(t-s)\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})}\lVert F(U(s))-F(V(s))\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}ds}\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\hskip 56.9055pt+\int_{0}^{t}\lVert S(t-s)\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})}\lvert k(s)\rvert\lVert\mathcal{B}U(s-\tau(s))-\mathcal{B}V(s-\tau(s))\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}ds}\\
\displaystyle{\hskip 42.67912pt\leqslant M(L(C)\xi+b^{2}\lVert k\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{1}([0,\xi];{{\rm I}\kern-1.22911pt{\rm R}})})\lVert U-V\rVert_{C([-\bar{\tau},\xi];{\mathcal{H}})}}\end{array}

Thus,

\lVert\Gamma U-\Gamma V\rVert_{C([-\bar{\tau},\xi];{\mathcal{H}})}\leqslant M(L(C)\xi+b^{2}\lVert k\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{1}([0,\xi];{{\rm I}\kern-1.22911pt{\rm R}})})\lVert U-V\rVert_{C([-\bar{\tau},\xi];{\mathcal{H}})}.

As a consequence, since from (

M(L(C)\xi+b^{2}\lVert k\rVert_{\mathcal{L}^{1}([0,T];{{\rm I}\kern-1.22911pt{\rm R}})})<\frac{1}{4}<1,
the map
\Gamma
is a contraction. Thus, from the Banach’s Theorem,

has a unique fixed point
U\in C^{C}_{\tilde{g}}([-\bar{\tau},\xi];{\mathcal{H}})
. So, the fixed point
U\in C^{C}_{\tilde{g}}([-\bar{\tau},\xi];{\mathcal{H}})
is a local solution to (

) that can be extended to some maximal interval

since

.

Now, we prove that the fixed point
U
is the unique local mild solution to (
2.8
). Indeed, assume that (
2.8
) has another local mild solution

defined in a time interval
[0,\delta^{\prime})

. Then, for every

, we have that

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{\lVert U(t)-V(t)\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}\leqslant M\int_{0}^{t}b^{2}\lvert k(s)\rvert\lVert U(s-\tau(s))-V(s-\tau(s))\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}ds}\\
\displaystyle{\hskip 85.35826pt+ML(c)\int_{0}^{t}\lVert U(s)-V(s)\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}ds}\\

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{\bar{\tau}}e^{\omega(s-\tau(s))}|k(s)|\cdot||\mathcal{B}U(s-\tau(s))||_{\mathcal{H}}ds}\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\hskip 28.45274pt\leqslant b^{2}\int_{0}^{\bar{\tau}}|k(s)|\left(\max_{r\in[-\bar{\tau},0]}\{e^{\omega r}||\tilde{g}(r)||_{\mathcal{H}}\}+\max_{r\in[0,s]}\{e^{\omega r}||U(r)||_{\mathcal{H}}\}\right)ds}\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\hskip 28.45274pt\leqslant Kb^{2}\max_{r\in[-\bar{\tau},0]}\{e^{\omega r}||\tilde{g}(r)||_{\mathcal{H}}\}+b^{2}\int_{0}^{\bar{\tau}}|k(s)|\max_{r\in[0,s]}\{e^{\omega r}||U(r)||_{\mathcal{H}}\}ds.}\\
\displaystyle{\hskip 28.45274pt=Kb^{2}\max_{r\in[-\bar{\tau},0]}\{e^{\omega r}||\tilde{g}(r)||_{\mathcal{H}}\}+b^{2}\int_{0}^{\bar{\tau}}|k(s)|\max_{r\in[s-\bar{\tau},s]\cap[0,s]}\{e^{\omega r}||U(r)||_{\mathcal{H}}\}ds.}\end{array}

Also,

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\int_{\bar{\tau}}^{t}e^{\omega(s-\tau(s))}|k(s)|\cdot||\mathcal{B}U(s-\tau(s))||_{\mathcal{H}}ds\leqslant b^{2}\int_{\bar{\tau}}^{t}e^{\omega(s-\tau(s))}|k(s)|\cdot||U(s-\tau(s))||_{\mathcal{H}}ds}\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\hskip 85.35826pt\leqslant b^{2}\int_{\bar{\tau}}^{t}|k(s)|\max_{r\in[s-\bar{\tau},s]}\{e^{\omega r}||U(r)||_{\mathcal{H}}\}ds}\\
\displaystyle{\hskip 85.35826pt=b^{2}\int_{\bar{\tau}}^{t}|k(s)|\max_{r\in[s-\bar{\tau},s]\cap[0,s]}\{e^{\omega r}||U(r)||_{\mathcal{H}}\}ds.}\end{array}

Therefore, (
3.4
) becomes

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{t}e^{\omega(s-\tau(s))}|k(s)|\cdot||\mathcal{B}U(s-\tau(s))||_{\mathcal{H}}ds\leqslant Kb^{2}\max_{r\in[-\bar{\tau},0]}\{e^{\omega r}||\tilde{g}(r)||_{\mathcal{H}}\}}\\
\displaystyle{\hskip 85.35826pt+\int_{0}^{t}b^{2}|k(s)|\max_{r\in[s-\bar{\tau},s]\cap[0,s]}\{e^{\omega r}||U(r)||_{\mathcal{H}}\}ds,}\end{array}

(3.5)

for all
t\geqslant\bar{\tau}
.

On the other hand, if
t<\bar{\tau}
, using (
1.3
) it rather holds

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{t}e^{\omega(s-\tau(s))}|k(s)|\cdot||\mathcal{B}U(s-\tau(s))||_{\mathcal{H}}ds}\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\hskip 56.9055pt\leqslant b^{2}\int_{0}^{t}|k(s)|\max_{r\in[-\bar{\tau},0]}\{e^{\omega r}||\tilde{g}(r)||_{\mathcal{H}}\}ds+b^{2}\int_{0}^{t}|k(s)|\max_{r\in[0,s]}\{e^{\omega r}||U(r)||_{\mathcal{H}}\}ds}\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\hskip 56.9055pt=b^{2}\int_{0}^{t}|k(s)|\max_{r\in[-\bar{\tau},0]}\{e^{\omega r}||\tilde{g}(r)||_{\mathcal{H}}\}ds+b^{2}\int_{0}^{t}|k(s)|\max_{r\in[s-\bar{\tau},s]\cap[0,s]}\{e^{\omega r}||U(r)||_{\mathcal{H}}\}ds}\\
\displaystyle{\hskip 56.9055pt\leqslant b^{2}\int_{0}^{\bar{\tau}}|k(s)|\max_{r\in[-\bar{\tau},0]}\{e^{\omega r}||\tilde{g}(r)||_{\mathcal{H}}\}ds+b^{2}\int_{0}^{t}|k(s)|\max_{r\in[s-\bar{\tau},s]\cap[0,s]}\{e^{\omega r}||U(r)||_{\mathcal{H}}\}ds}\\
\displaystyle{\hskip 56.9055pt\leqslant Kb^{2}\max_{r\in[-\bar{\tau},0]}\{e^{\omega r}||\tilde{g}(r)||_{\mathcal{H}}\}+b^{2}\int_{0}^{t}|k(s)|\max_{r\in[s-\bar{\tau},s]\cap[0,s]}\{e^{\omega r}||U(r)||_{\mathcal{H}}\}ds.}\end{array}

So, (
3.5
) holds for every
t\in[0,T]
. Putting (
3.5
) in (
3.3
), we can write

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{||U(t)||_{\mathcal{H}}\leqslant Me^{-\omega t}\left(||U_{0}||_{\mathcal{H}}+e^{\omega\bar{\tau}}Kb^{2}\max_{r\in[-\bar{\tau},0]}\{e^{\omega r}||\tilde{g}(r)||_{\mathcal{H}}\}\right)}\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\hskip 28.45274pt+Me^{-\omega t}e^{\omega\bar{\tau}}b^{2}\int_{0}^{t}|k(s)|\max_{r\in[s-\bar{\tau},s]\cap[0,s]}\{e^{\omega r}||U(r)||_{\mathcal{H}}\}ds+ML(C)e^{-\omega t}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\omega s}||U(s)||_{\mathcal{H}}ds,}\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\hskip 14.22636pt\leqslant Me^{-\omega t}\left(||U_{0}||_{\mathcal{H}}+e^{\omega\bar{\tau}}Kb^{2}\max_{r\in[-\bar{\tau},0]}\{e^{\omega r}||\tilde{g}(r)||_{\mathcal{H}}\}\right)}\\
\displaystyle{\hskip 28.45274pt+e^{-\omega t}\int_{0}^{t}(Me^{\omega\bar{\tau}}b^{2}|k(s)|+ML(C))\max_{r\in[s-\bar{\tau},s]\cap[0,s]}\{e^{\omega r}||U(r)||_{\mathcal{H}}\}ds,}\end{array}

from which

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{e^{\omega t}||U(t)||_{\mathcal{H}}\leqslant M\left(||U_{0}||_{\mathcal{H}}+e^{\omega\bar{\tau}}Kb^{2}\max_{r\in[-\bar{\tau},0]}\{e^{\omega r}||\tilde{g}(r)||_{\mathcal{H}}\}\right)}\\
\hskip 56.9055pt\displaystyle{\hskip 28.45274pt+\int_{0}^{t}(Me^{\omega\bar{\tau}}b^{2}|k(s)|+ML(C))\max_{r\in[s-\bar{\tau},s]\cap[0,s]}\{e^{\omega r}||U(r)||_{\mathcal{H}}\}ds,}\end{array}

for all
t\in[0,T]
. Thus,

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{\max_{r\in[t-\bar{\tau},t]\cap[0,t]}\{e^{\omega r}||U(r)||_{\mathcal{H}}\}\leqslant M\left(||U_{0}||_{\mathcal{H}}+e^{\omega\bar{\tau}}Kb^{2}\max_{r\in[-\bar{\tau},0]}\{e^{\omega r}||\tilde{g}(r)||_{\mathcal{H}}\}\right)}\\
\hskip 56.9055pt\displaystyle{\hskip 42.67912pt+\int_{0}^{t}(Me^{\omega\bar{\tau}}b^{2}|k(s)|+ML(C))\max_{r\in[s-\bar{\tau},s]\cap[0,s]}\{e^{\omega r}||U(r)||_{\mathcal{H}}\}ds.}\end{array}

Hence, denoted with

\tilde{U}(t):=\max_{r\in[t-\bar{\tau},t]\cap[0,t]}\{e^{\omega r}||U(r)||_{\mathcal{H}}\},

using Gronwall’s inequality we get

\|\tilde{U}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leqslant M\left(||U_{0}||_{\mathcal{H}}+e^{\omega\bar{\tau}}Kb^{2}\max_{r\in[-\bar{\tau},0]}\{e^{\omega r}||\tilde{g}(r)||_{\mathcal{H}}\}\right)e^{Mb^{2}e^{\omega\bar{\tau}}\int_{0}^{t}|k(s)|ds+ML(C)t}.

Finally,

e^{\omega t}\|U(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leqslant M\left(||U_{0}||_{\mathcal{H}}+e^{\omega\bar{\tau}}Kb^{2}\max_{r\in[-\bar{\tau},0]}\{e^{\omega r}||\tilde{g}(r)||_{\mathcal{H}}\}\right)e^{Mb^{2}e^{\omega\bar{\tau}}\int_{0}^{t}|k(s)|ds+ML(C)t},

which implies that

\|U(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leqslant M\left(||U_{0}||_{\mathcal{H}}+e^{\omega\bar{\tau}}Kb^{2}\max_{r\in[-\bar{\tau},0]}\{e^{\omega r}||\tilde{g}(r)||_{\mathcal{H}}\}\right)e^{Mb^{2}e^{\omega\bar{\tau}}\int_{0}^{t}|k(s)|ds+ML(C)t}e^{-\omega t}.

From (
2.6
), we can conclude that

\|U(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leqslant Me^{\gamma}\left(||U_{0}||_{\mathcal{H}}+e^{\omega\bar{\tau}}Kb^{2}\max_{r\in[-\bar{\tau},0]}\{e^{\omega r}||\tilde{g}(r)||_{\mathcal{H}}\}\right)e^{-(\omega-\omega^{\prime}-ML(C))t},

for all
t\in[0,T]
, which proves the exponential decay estimate (
3.2
).

Now, we prove that, for sufficiently small initial data, the system (
1.1
) has a unique global solution that satisfies (
3.1
), whenever the coefficient of the delay feedback

satisfies (

).

Theorem 3.2
.

Assume (
2.6
). There exist
\rho>0
and
C_{\rho}>0
, with
L(C_{\rho})<\frac{\omega-\omega^{\prime}}{2M}
, for which, if
\tilde{g}=(u_{0}(0),g,\eta_{0})
is such that

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{||u_{1}||^{2}_{H}+(1-\tilde{\beta})||A^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{0}(0)||^{2}_{H}+\int_{-\bar{\tau}}^{0}|k(s)|\cdot||B^{*}g(s)||^{2}_{H}}\\
\displaystyle{\hskip 142.26378pt+\int_{0}^{+\infty}\beta(s)||A^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta_{0}(s)||^{2}_{H}ds<\rho^{2}}\end{array}

(3.6)

and

\displaystyle{\max_{s\in[-\bar{\tau},0]}||g(s)||_{H}<\rho,}

(3.7)

then the problem (
2.3
) with initial datum
\tilde{g}
has a unique global solution
U\in C([0,+\infty)),\mathcal{H})
that satisfies

\lVert U(t)\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}\leqslant C_{\rho},\quad\forall t\geqslant 0.

(3.8)

Proof.

Let us fix a time
T
sufficiently large,
T\geqslant\bar{\tau},
such that

C_{T}:=4M^{2}e^{2\gamma}\max\left\{(1+Kb^{2}e^{\omega\bar{\tau}}),\,e^{\omega\bar{\tau}}\right\}\left(1+e^{2\omega\bar{\tau}}K^{2}b^{4}\right)e^{-(\omega-\omega^{\prime})T}<1.

(3.9)

Also, we set

C^{*}_{T}:=\sup\left\{e^{3b^{2}\int_{nT}^{(n+1)T}\lvert k(s)\rvert ds}:n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}.

(3.10)

The assumption (
1.3
) ensures that
C^{*}_{T}<+\infty.

Note that
C^{*}_{T}\geqslant\bar{C}(T),
where
\bar{C}(T)
is defined in (
2.16
).
Now, we pick
\rho>0
in such a way that

\rho\leqslant\frac{(1-\tilde{\beta})^{\frac{1}{2}}}{2(C^{*}_{T})^{\frac{1}{2}}}h^{-1}\left(\frac{1-\tilde{\beta}}{2}\right).

(3.11)

Let
(u_{0}(0),u_{1},\eta_{0})
and
g
be such that (
3.6
) and (
3.7
) holds true. Then, the initial datum
\tilde{g}
satisfies the following smallness condition:

\max_{s\in[-\bar{\tau},0]}||\tilde{g}(s)||_{\mathcal{H}}\leqslant\sqrt{2}\rho.

(3.12)

Now, from Theorem
2.1
there exists a unique local solution
U(\cdot)
to (
2.8
) with the initial datum
\tilde{g}(s)
,
s\in[-\bar{\tau},0]
which is defined on a time interval
[0,\delta)
and that satisfies the Duhamel’s formula

U(t)=S(t)U_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)[-k(s)\mathcal{B}U(s-\tau(s))+F(U(s))]\,ds,

(3.13)

for all
t\in[0,\delta)
. Without loss of generality, we can suppose that
\delta\leqslant T
. Also, we can assume that
U
is a non-zero solution to (
2.8
), since, otherwise, (
3.8
) is trivially satisfied. Thus, using (
3.11
), the assumption (
3.6
) on the initial data,
and recalling that
h
is a strictly increasing function, we get

h(||A^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{0}(0)||_{H})<h\left(\frac{\rho}{(1-\tilde{\beta})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\leqslant h\left(\frac{1}{2(C_{T}^{*})^{\frac{1}{2}}}h^{-1}\left(\frac{1-\tilde{\beta}}{2}\right)\right)\leqslant h\left(h^{-1}\left(\frac{1-\tilde{\beta}}{2}\right)\right)=\frac{1-\tilde{\beta}}{2},

were in the above inequality we used the fact that
2(C_{T}^{*})^{\frac{1}{2}}\geqslant 1
. Hence, being
h(||A^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{0}(0)||_{H})<\frac{1-\tilde{\beta}}{2}
, from Lemma
2.4
it follows that
E(0)>0
.

Also, using (
2.22
) and the fact that
h(||A^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{0}(0)||_{H})<\frac{1-\tilde{\beta}}{2}
, we can write

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{E(0)=\frac{1}{2}||u_{1}||_{H}^{2}+\frac{1-\tilde{\beta}}{2}||A^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{0}(0)||_{H}^{2}-\psi(u_{0}(0))+\frac{1}{2}\int_{-\bar{\tau}}^{0}|k(s)|\cdot||B^{*}u_{t}(s)||^{2}_{H}ds}\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\hskip 56.9055pt+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\beta(s)||A^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta_{0}(s)||_{H}^{2}ds}\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\hskip 28.45274pt\leqslant\frac{1}{2}||u_{1}||_{H}^{2}+\frac{1-\tilde{\beta}}{2}||A^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{0}(0)||_{H}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}h(||A^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{0}(0)||_{H})||A^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{0}(0)||^{2}_{H}}\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\hskip 56.9055pt+\frac{1}{2}\int_{-\bar{\tau}}^{0}|k(s)|\cdot||B^{*}g(s)||^{2}_{H}ds+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\beta(s)||A^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta_{0}(s)||_{H}^{2}ds}\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\hskip 28.45274pt\leqslant\frac{1}{2}||u_{1}||_{H}^{2}+\frac{1-\tilde{\beta}}{2}||A^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{0}(0)||_{H}^{2}+\frac{1-\tilde{\beta}}{4}||A^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{0}(0)||^{2}_{H}}\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\hskip 56.9055pt+\frac{1}{2}\int_{-\bar{\tau}}^{0}|k(s)|\cdot||B^{*}g(s)||^{2}_{H}ds+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\beta(s)||A^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta_{0}(s)||_{H}^{2}ds}\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\hskip 28.45274pt=\frac{1}{2}||u_{1}||_{H}^{2}+\frac{3}{4}(1-\tilde{\beta})||A^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{0}(0)||_{H}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{-\bar{\tau}}^{0}|k(s)|\cdot||B^{*}g(s)||^{2}_{H}ds}\\

The above inequality, together with (
3.11
), implies that

h\left(\frac{2}{(1-\tilde{\beta})^{\frac{1}{2}}}(C_{T}^{*})^{\frac{1}{2}}E^{\frac{1}{2}}(0)\right)<h\left(\frac{2}{(1-\tilde{\beta})^{\frac{1}{2}}}(C_{T}^{*})^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho\right)\leqslant h\left(h^{-1}\left(\frac{1-\tilde{\beta}}{2}\right)\right)=\frac{1-\tilde{\beta}}{2}.

(3.14)

Furthermore, from (

),

(3.15)

Then, from (
3.14
), (
3.15
) and by definition of

, we have that

(3.16)

Since

, the above inequality (

2.4
, that ensures that (

Combining (

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\frac{1}{4}||u_{t}(t)||_{H}^{2}+\frac{1-\tilde{\beta}}{4}||A^{\frac{1}{2}}u(t)||_{H}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\int_{t-\bar{\tau}}^{t}|k(s)|\cdot||B^{*}u_{t}(s)||_{H}^{2}ds+\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\beta(s)||A^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta^{t}(s)||_{H}^{2}ds}\\
\displaystyle{\hskip 170.71652pt<E(t)\leqslant C_{T}^{*}\mathcal{E}(0),}\end{array}

(3.17)

for all

. Thus, since the solution
U
is bounded from (
3.17
), we can extend it in

and in the whole interval
[0,T]
. Moreover, (
3.17
) holds for all

.

Now, using (
3.16
) and (
3.17
) with

, we can write

h(||A^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{0}(T)||_{H})\leqslant h\left(\frac{2}{(1-\tilde{\beta})^{\frac{1}{2}}}(C_{T}^{*})^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{E}^{\frac{1}{2}}(0)\right)<\frac{1-\tilde{\beta}}{2}.

(3.18)

Furthermore, from the smallness assumption (
3.6
) on the initial data and from (

\frac{1}{4}||U(t)||^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}\leqslant E(t)\leqslant C_{T}^{*}\rho^{2},

where here we have used the fact that

. Thus,

||U(t)||_{\mathcal{H}}\leqslant C_{\rho}:=2(C_{T}^{*})^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho.

(3.19)

Next, eventually choosing a smaller value of
\rho
, we can suppose that
L(C_{\rho})<\frac{\omega-\omega^{\prime}}{2M}
. We have so proved that there exist
\rho>0
,
C_{\rho}>0
such that, whenever the initial data
(u_{0}(0),g,\eta_{0})
satisfy the smallness condition (
3.6
) and (
3.7
), then the system (
2.8
) with the initial data
U_{0}
,
\tilde{g}
, has a unique solution
U
defined in the time interval
[0,T]
such that

.

As a consequence, from Theorem
3.1
, being
L(C_{\rho})<\frac{\omega-\omega^{\prime}}{2M}
, the following estimate holds

\|U(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leqslant Me^{\gamma}\left(||U_{0}||_{\mathcal{H}}+e^{\omega\bar{\tau}}Kb^{2}\max_{r\in[-\bar{\tau},0]}\{e^{\omega r}||\tilde{g}(r)||_{\mathcal{H}}\}\right)e^{-\frac{\omega-\omega^{\prime}}{2}t},

for all
t\in[0,T]
. Thus, using (
3.12
), we can write

\|U(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leqslant Me^{\gamma}\left(||U_{0}||_{\mathcal{H}}+e^{\omega\bar{\tau}}Kb^{2}\sqrt{2}\rho\right)e^{-\frac{\omega-\omega^{\prime}}{2}t},

for any

. Then,

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\|U(t)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}\leqslant M^{2}e^{2\gamma}\left(||U_{0}||_{\mathcal{H}}+e^{\omega\bar{\tau}}Kb^{2}\sqrt{2}\rho\right)^{2}e^{-(\omega-\omega^{\prime})t}}\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\hskip 42.67912pt\leqslant 2M^{2}e^{2\gamma}\left(||U_{0}||^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}+(e^{\omega\bar{\tau}}Kb^{2}\sqrt{2}\rho)^{2}\right)e^{-(\omega-\omega^{\prime})t}}\\
\displaystyle{\hskip 42.67912pt=2M^{2}e^{2\gamma}\left(||U_{0}||^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}+e^{2\omega\bar{\tau}}K^{2}b^{4}2\rho^{2}\right)e^{-(\omega-\omega^{\prime})t},}\end{array}

from which, taking into account (
3.12
) with

,

\|U(t)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}\leqslant 4M^{2}e^{2\gamma}\rho^{2}\left(1+e^{2\omega\bar{\tau}}K^{2}b^{4}\right)e^{-(\omega-\omega^{\prime})t},

(3.20)

for all
t\in[0,T]
.

Also, for all
s\in[T-\bar{\tau},T]\subseteq[0,T]

3.20
) yields

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{||u_{t}(s)||_{H}^{2}\leqslant\|U(s)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}}\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\hskip 59.75095pt\leqslant 4M^{2}e^{2\gamma}\rho^{2}\left(1+e^{2\omega\bar{\tau}}K^{2}b^{4}\right)e^{-(\omega-\omega^{\prime})s}}\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\hskip 59.75095pt\leqslant 4M^{2}e^{2\gamma}\rho^{2}\left(1+e^{2\omega\bar{\tau}}K^{2}b^{4}\right)e^{-(\omega-\omega^{\prime})(T-\bar{\tau})}}\\
\displaystyle{\hskip 59.75095pt\leqslant 4M^{2}e^{2\gamma}e^{\omega\bar{\tau}}\rho^{2}\left(1+e^{2\omega\bar{\tau}}K^{2}b^{4}\right)e^{-(\omega-\omega^{\prime})T}.}\end{array}

As a consequence, from (
1.3
)

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\int_{T-\bar{\tau}}^{T}|k(s)|\cdot||B^{*}u_{t}(s)||_{H}^{2}ds\leqslant 4M^{2}e^{2\gamma}e^{\omega\bar{\tau}}b^{2}\rho^{2}\left(1+e^{2\omega\bar{\tau}}K^{2}b^{4}\right)e^{-(\omega-\omega^{\prime})T}\int_{T-\bar{\tau}}^{T}|k(s)|ds}\\
\displaystyle{\hskip 85.35826pt\leqslant 4KM^{2}e^{2\gamma}e^{\omega\bar{\tau}}b^{2}\rho^{2}\left(1+e^{2\omega\bar{\tau}}K^{2}b^{4}\right)e^{-(\omega-\omega^{\prime})T}.}\end{array}

This last fact together with (
3.20
) implies that

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\|U(T)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}+\int_{T-\bar{\tau}}^{T}|k(s)|\cdot||B^{*}u_{t}(s)||_{H}^{2}ds}\\
\displaystyle{\hskip 28.45274pt\leqslant 4M^{2}e^{2\gamma}\rho^{2}\left(1+e^{2\omega\bar{\tau}}K^{2}b^{4}\right)\left(1+Kb^{2}e^{\omega\bar{\tau}}\right)e^{-(\omega-\omega^{\prime})T}\leqslant C_{T}\rho^{2}<\rho^{2}.}\end{array}

(3.21)

Moreover, (
3.20
) implies that

\max_{s\in[T-\bar{\tau},T]}||u_{t}(s)||_{H}^{2}\leqslant 4M^{2}e^{2\gamma}e^{\omega\bar{\tau}}\rho^{2}\left(1+e^{2\omega\bar{\tau}}K^{2}b^{4}\right)e^{-(\omega-\omega^{\prime})T}\leqslant C_{T}\rho^{2}<\rho^{2},

from which

\max_{s\in[T-\bar{\tau},T]}||u_{t}(s)||_{H}<\rho.

(3.22)

The conditions (
3.21
) and (
3.22
) allow us to apply the same arguments employed before on the interval
[T,2T]
. Namely, we consider the initial value problem

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{V^{\prime}(t)=\mathcal{A}V(t)-k(t)\mathcal{B}V(t-\tau(t))+F(V(t)),\quad t\in[T,2T],}\\
\displaystyle{V(s)=U(s),\quad s\in[T-\bar{\tau},T],}\end{array}

(3.23)

where

is the solution to (
2.8
) in the interval
[0,T]
.

We define now the energy of the solution

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\tilde{E}(t):=\tilde{E}(v(t))=\frac{1}{2}||v_{t}(t)||_{H}^{2}+\frac{1-\tilde{\beta}}{2}||A^{\frac{1}{2}}v(t)||^{2}_{H}-\psi(v)}\\
\hskip 56.9055pt\displaystyle{+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\beta(s)||A^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta^{t}(s)||^{2}_{H}ds+\frac{1}{2}\int_{t-\bar{\tau}}^{t}|k(s)|\cdot||B^{*}v_{t}(s)||_{H}^{2}ds,}\end{array}

(3.24)

where
\eta^{t}(s)=v(t)-v(t-s)
, and the functional

\tilde{\mathcal{E}}(t):=\max\left\{\frac{1}{2}\max_{s\in[T-\bar{\tau},T]}\|u_{t}(s)\|_{H}^{2},\,\,\max_{s\in[T,t]}\tilde{E}(s)\right\}.

(3.25)

Let us note that
\tilde{E}(T)=E(T)
.

Now, from Theorem
2.1
, the problem (
3.23
) with the initial datum
U(s)
,
s\in[T-\bar{\tau},T]
, has a unique local solution
V(\cdot)
defined on a time interval
[T,T+\delta)
given by the Duhamel’s formula

V(t)=S(t-T)U(T)+\int_{0}^{t}S(t-T-s)[-k(s)\mathcal{B}V(s-\tau(s))+F(V(s))]\,ds,

(3.26)

for all
t\in[T,T+\delta)
. We can assume that
\delta\leqslant\bar{\tau}
and that
V
is a non-zero solution, since, otherwise, (
3.8
) is obviously satisfied for all
t\geqslant T
.

First of all, the inequality (
3.18
) yields
\tilde{E}(T)>0
.

Let us note that, if
\tilde{E}(t)\geqslant\frac{1}{4}\lVert v_{t}(t)\rVert^{2}_{H}
, for all
t\in[T,T+\delta)
, arguing as in Lemma
2.3
,

\tilde{E}(t)\leqslant e^{3b^{2}\int_{T}^{t}\lvert k(s)\rvert ds}\,\tilde{\mathcal{E}}(T),\quad\forall t\in[T,T+\delta).

(3.27)

Also, using the same arguments employed in Lemma
2.4
and observing that

C^{*}_{T}\geqslant e^{3b^{2}\int_{T}^{2T}|k(s)|ds},

if

h\left(\frac{2}{(1-\tilde{\beta})^{\frac{1}{2}}}(C^{*}_{T})^{\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(T)\right)<\frac{1-\tilde{\beta}}{2},

(3.28)

then

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\tilde{E}(t)>\frac{1}{4}||v_{t}(t)||_{H}^{2}+\frac{1-\tilde{\beta}}{4}||A^{\frac{1}{2}}v(t)||_{H}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\int_{t-\bar{\tau}}^{t}|k(s)|\cdot||B^{*}v_{t}(s)||_{H}^{2}ds}\\
\hskip 42.67912pt\displaystyle{+\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\beta(s)||A^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta^{t}(s)||_{H}^{2}ds,}\end{array}

(3.29)

for all
t\in[T,T+\delta)
. In particular,

\tilde{E}(t)>\frac{1}{4}\|V(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2},\quad\forall t\in[T,T+\delta).

(3.30)

Now, from (
3.21
), we have
\tilde{E}(T)<\rho^{2}
. This together with (
3.22
) implies that

\tilde{\mathcal{E}}(T)<\rho^{2}.

(3.31)

Hence, using (
3.11
) we get

h\left(\frac{2}{(1-\tilde{\beta})^{\frac{1}{2}}}(C^{*}_{T})^{\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{\mathcal{E}}(T)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)<h\left(\frac{2}{(1-\tilde{\beta})^{\frac{1}{2}}}(C^{*}_{T})^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho\right)<\frac{1-\tilde{\beta}}{2}.

(3.32)

So, (
3.28
) is satisfied. As a consequence, inequalities (
3.29
) and (
3.30
) hold for all
t\in[T,T+\delta)
. In particular, from (
3.30
) we get

\tilde{E}(t)\geqslant\frac{1}{4}\lVert v_{t}(t)\rVert_{H}^{2},\quad\forall t\in[T,T+\delta).

Therefore, also inequality (
3.27
) is fulfilled. Combining (
3.27
) and (
3.29
), we finally get

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69046pt\displaystyle{\frac{1}{4}||v_{t}(t)||_{H}^{2}+\frac{1-\tilde{\beta}}{4}||A^{\frac{1}{2}}v(t)||_{H}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\int_{t-\bar{\tau}}^{t}|k(s)|\cdot||B^{*}v_{t}(s)||_{H}^{2}ds+\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\beta(s)||A^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta^{t}(s)||_{H}^{2}ds}\\
\displaystyle{\hskip 85.35826pt<\tilde{E}(t)\leqslant e^{3b^{2}\int_{T}^{2T}\lvert k(s)\rvert ds}\tilde{\mathcal{E}}(T)\leqslant C^{*}_{T}\tilde{\mathcal{E}}(T).}\end{array}

(3.33)

Thus, the solution
V
is bounded and we can extend it in
t=T+\delta
and in the whole interval
[T,2T]
. Moreover, (
3.33
) and (
3.30
) hold true for all
t\in[T,2T]
. So, taking into account of (
3.31
), it holds

\lVert V(t)\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}\leqslant 2(C_{T}^{*})^{\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\frac{1}{2}}(T)\leqslant 2(C_{T}^{*})^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho=C_{\rho}.

(3.34)

Putting together the two partial solutions (
3.13
) and (
3.26
) to (
2.8
) obtained in the time intervals
[0,T]
and
[T,2T]
, respectively, we get the existence of a unique solution
U\in C([0,2T];\mathcal{H})
to (
2.8
) that is defined in the time interval
[0,2T]
and that satisfies the Duhamel’s formula (
3.13
), for all
t\in[0,2T]
. Moreover, from (
3.19
) and (
3.34
), the solution
U
satisfies (
3.1
) with
C=C_{\rho}
. Thus, since
L(C_{\rho})<\frac{\omega-\omega^{\prime}}{2M}
, the exponential decay estimate (
3.2
) is satisfied by the solution
U
, i.e.

\|U(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leqslant Me^{\gamma}\left(||U_{0}||_{\mathcal{H}}+e^{\omega\bar{\tau}}Kb^{2}\max_{r\in[-\bar{\tau},0]}\{e^{\omega r}||\tilde{g}(r)||_{\mathcal{H}}\}\right)e^{-\frac{\omega-\omega^{\prime}}{2}t},\quad\forall t\in[0,2T].

Again, we deduce that (
3.20
) holds for all
t\in[0,2T]
. Furthermore, for all
s\in[2T-\bar{\tau},2T],

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{||u_{t}(s)||_{H}^{2}\leqslant||U(s)||_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\leqslant 4M^{2}e^{2\gamma}e^{\omega\bar{\tau}}\rho^{2}\left(1+e^{2\omega\bar{\tau}}K^{2}b^{4}\right)e^{-(\omega-\omega^{\prime})2T}.}\end{array}

As a consequence,

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\|U(2T)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}+\int_{2T-\bar{\tau}}^{2T}|k(s)|\cdot||B^{*}u_{t}(s)||_{H}^{2}ds}\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\hskip 28.45274pt\leqslant 4M^{2}e^{2\gamma}\rho^{2}(1+e^{2\omega\bar{\tau}}K^{2}b^{4})e^{-(\omega-\omega^{\prime})2T}}\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\hskip 56.9055pt+4M^{2}e^{2\gamma}e^{\omega\bar{\tau}}Kb^{2}\rho^{2}(1+e^{2\omega\bar{\tau}}K^{2}b^{4})e^{-(\omega-\omega^{\prime})2T}}\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\hskip 28.45274pt\leqslant 4M^{2}e^{2\gamma}\rho^{2}(1+e^{2\omega\bar{\tau}}K^{2}b^{4})(1+Kb^{2}e^{\omega\bar{\tau}})e^{-(\omega-\omega^{\prime})2T}}\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\hskip 28.45274pt\leqslant 4M^{2}e^{2\gamma}\rho^{2}(1+e^{2\omega\bar{\tau}}K^{2}b^{4})(1+Kb^{2}e^{\omega\bar{\tau}})e^{-(\omega-\omega^{\prime})T}}\\
\displaystyle{\hskip 28.45274pt\leqslant C_{T}\rho^{2}<\rho^{2}.}\end{array}

(3.35)

Also,

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\max_{s\in[2T-\bar{\tau},2T]}||u_{t}(s)||_{H}^{2}\leqslant 4M^{2}e^{2\gamma}e^{\omega\bar{\tau}}\rho^{2}\left(1+e^{2\omega\bar{\tau}}K^{2}b^{4}\right)e^{-(\omega-\omega^{\prime})2T}}\\
\displaystyle{\hskip 56.9055pt\leqslant 4M^{2}e^{2\gamma}e^{\omega\bar{\tau}}\rho^{2}\left(1+e^{2\omega\bar{\tau}}K^{2}b^{4}\right)e^{-(\omega-\omega^{\prime})T}\leqslant C_{T}\rho^{2}<\rho^{2},}\end{array}

from which

\max_{s\in[2T-\bar{\tau},2T]}||u_{t}(s)||_{H}<\rho.

(3.36)

The conditions (
3.35
) and (
3.36
) allows us to apply the same arguments employed before on the interval
[2T,3T]
, obtaining the existence of a unique solution to (
2.8
) defined in the time interval
[0,3T]
and that satisfies (
3.8
) for all
t\in[0,3T]
. Iterating this procedure, we finally get the existence of a unique global solution
U\in C([0,+\infty);\mathcal{H})
to (
2.8
) with the initial datum
\tilde{g}(s)
,
s\in[-\bar{\tau},0]
, that satisfies (
3.8
).

We have then proved that, for suitably small initial data, solutions to problem (
2.8
) are globally defined and bounded by a positive constant
C_{\rho}
that satisfies
L_{C_{\rho}}<\frac{\omega-\omega^{\prime}}{M}
(see Theorem
3.2
). Thus, solutions to problem (
2.8
) satisfy the exponential decay estimate (
3.2
). Therefore, we are ready to prove the energy decay
for model (
2.3
).

Theorem 3.3
.

Let us consider model (
2.3
) and assume (
2.6
). There exists
\rho>0
such that,
if the following smallness conditions on the initial data are satisfied:

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{||u_{1}||^{2}_{H}+(1-\tilde{\beta})||A^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{0}(0)||^{2}_{H}+\int_{-\bar{\tau}}^{0}|k(s)|\cdot||B^{*}g(s)||^{2}_{H}}\\
\displaystyle{\hskip 113.81102pt+\int_{0}^{+\infty}\beta(s)||A^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta_{0}(s)||^{2}_{H}ds<\rho^{2},}\end{array}

(3.37)

and

\displaystyle{\max_{s\in[-\bar{\tau},0]}||g(s)||_{H}<\rho,}

(3.38)

then (
2.3
) has a unique solution globally defined. Moreover, the energy satisfies the exponential decay estimate

E(t)\leqslant\tilde{C}e^{-\mu t},

(3.39)

where
\mu:=\omega-\omega^{\prime}
and
\tilde{C}
is a constant depending on the initial data.

Proof.

Let
\rho>0
be the positive constant in Theorem
3.2
. Let us consider initial data for which the smallness conditions (
3.37
) and (
3.38
) hold true. Then, from Theorem
3.2
, the problem (
2.8
) has a unique global
U(\cdot)
solution that satisfies
||U(t)||_{\mathcal{H}}<C_{\rho}
, with
L(C_{\rho})<\frac{\omega-\omega^{\prime}}{2M},
and the exponential decay estimate (
3.2
). From (
2.22
) and (
3.17
), we have that

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{E(t)=\frac{1}{2}||u_{t}(t)||_{H}^{2}+\frac{1-\tilde{\beta}}{2}||A^{\frac{1}{2}}u(t)||^{2}_{H}-\psi(u(t))+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\beta(s)||A^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta^{t}(s)||^{2}_{H}ds}\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\hskip 42.67912pt+\frac{1}{2}\int_{t-\bar{\tau}}^{t}|k(s)|\cdot||B^{*}u_{t}(s)||_{H}^{2}ds}\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\hskip 28.45274pt\leqslant\frac{1}{2}||u_{t}(t)||_{H}^{2}+\frac{1-\tilde{\beta}}{2}||A^{\frac{1}{2}}u(t)||^{2}_{H}+\frac{1-\tilde{\beta}}{4}||A^{\frac{1}{2}}u(t)||^{2}_{H}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\beta(s)||A^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta^{t}(s)||^{2}_{H}ds}\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\hskip 42.67912pt+\frac{b^{2}}{2}\int_{t-\bar{\tau}}^{t}|k(s)|\cdot||u_{t}(s)||_{H}^{2}ds}\\
\vskip 8.5359pt\displaystyle{\hskip 28.45274pt\leqslant||U(t)||_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}+\frac{b^{2}}{2}\int_{t-\bar{\tau}}^{t}|k(s)|\cdot||U(s)||_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}ds}\end{array}

for any
t\geqslant\bar{\tau}
. Now, applying Theorem
3.1
, there exists a positive constant
\hat{C}
such that

||U(t)||_{\mathcal{H}}\leqslant\hat{C}e^{-\frac{\omega-\omega^{\prime}}{2}t},\quad\forall t\geqslant 0,

(3.40)

where here we have used the fact that
L(C_{\rho})<\frac{\omega-\omega^{\prime}}{2M}
.
Thus,

E(t)\leqslant\hat{C}^{2}e^{-(\omega-\omega^{\prime})t}+\frac{\hat{C}^{2}b^{2}}{2}\int_{t-\bar{\tau}}^{t}|k(s)|e^{-(\omega-\omega^{\prime})s}ds\leqslant\hat{C}^{2}e^{-(\omega-\omega^{\prime})t}+\frac{\hat{C}^{2}b^{2}}{2}Ke^{\omega\bar{\tau}}e^{-(\omega-\omega^{\prime})t},

for all
t\geqslant\bar{\tau}
. Setting

\tilde{C}:=\max\left\{\hat{C}^{2},\frac{\hat{C}^{2}b^{2}}{2}Ke^{\omega\bar{\tau}}\right\},

we can write

E(t)\leqslant\tilde{C}e^{-(\omega-\omega^{\prime})t},

from which,

E(t)\leqslant\tilde{C}e^{-\mu t},\quad\forall t\geqslant\bar{\tau},

where
\mu=\omega-\omega^{\prime}
. Hence, (
3.39
) holds true for all
t\geqslant\bar{\tau}
.

4
Examples

4.1
The wave equation with memory and source term

Let
\Omega
be a non-empty bounded set in
{{\rm I}\kern-1.49994pt{\rm R}}^{n}
, with boundary
\Gamma
of class
C^{2},
and let
\mathcal{O}\subset\Omega
be a nonempty open subset of
\Omega
. We assume
n\geqslant 3.
The lower dimension cases could be studied analogously. We consider the following wave equation:

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{u_{tt}(x,t)-\Delta u(x,t)+\int_{0}^{+\infty}\beta(s)\Delta u(x,t-s)ds+k(t)\chi_{\mathcal{O}}u_{t}(x,t-\tau(t))}\\
\hskip 199.16928pt\displaystyle{=|u(x,t)|^{\sigma}u(x,t),\qquad\text{in}\ \Omega\times(0,+\infty),}\\
\displaystyle{u(x,t)=0,\qquad\text{in}\ \Gamma\times(0,+\infty),}\\
\displaystyle{u(x,t)=u^{0}(x,t)\qquad\text{in}\ \Omega\times(-\infty,0],}\\
\displaystyle{u_{t}(x,0)=u_{1}(x),\qquad\text{in}\ \Omega,}\\
\displaystyle{u_{t}(x,t)=g(x,t),\qquad\text{in}\ \Omega\times[-\bar{\tau},0],}\end{array}

(4.1)

where the time delay function
\tau(\cdot)
satisfies (
1.2
),
\beta:(0,+\infty)\rightarrow(0,+\infty)
is a locally absolutely continuous memory kernel satisfying the assumptions (i)-(iv),
\sigma>0
and the damping coefficient
k(\cdot)
is a function in
L^{1}_{loc}([-\bar{\tau},+\infty))
for which (
1.3
) holds.
Then, system (
4.1
) falls in the form (
1.1
) with
A=-\Delta
and
D(A)=H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).
Moreover,
D(A^{\frac{1}{2}})=H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

Let us define
\eta_{s}^{t}
as in (
2.2
). Then, system (
4.1
) can be rewritten as follows:

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{u_{tt}(x,t)-(1-\tilde{\beta})\Delta u(x,t)-\int_{0}^{+\infty}\beta(s)\Delta\eta^{t}(x,s)ds+k(t)\chi_{\mathcal{O}}u_{t}(x,t-\tau)}\\
\hskip 199.16928pt\displaystyle{=|u(x,t)|^{\sigma}u(x,t),\quad\text{in}\ \Omega\times(0,+\infty),}\\
\displaystyle{\eta_{t}^{t}(x,s)=-\eta_{s}^{t}(x,s)+u_{t}(x,t),\qquad\text{in}\ \Omega\times(0,+\infty)\times(0,+\infty),}\\
\displaystyle{u(x,t)=0,\qquad\text{in}\ \Gamma\times(0,+\infty),}\\
\displaystyle{\eta^{t}(x,s)=0,\qquad\text{in}\ \Gamma\times(0,+\infty),\quad\text{for}\ t\geqslant 0,}\\
\displaystyle{u(x,0)=u_{0}(x):=u^{0}(x,0),\qquad\text{in}\ \Omega,}\\
\displaystyle{u_{t}(x,0)=u_{1}(x):=\frac{\partial u^{0}}{\partial t}(x,t)\Bigr|_{t=0},\qquad\text{in}\ \Omega,}\\
\displaystyle{\eta^{0}(x,s)=\eta_{0}(x,s):=u^{0}(x,0)-u^{0}(x,-s),\qquad\text{in}\ \Omega\times(0,+\infty),}\\
\displaystyle{u_{t}(x,t)=g(x,t),\qquad\text{in}\ \Omega\times[-\bar{\tau},0].}\par\end{array}

(4.2)

In order to reformulate (
4.2
) as an abstract first order equation, we introduce the Hilbert space
L^{2}_{\beta}((0,+\infty);H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))
endowed with the inner product

\langle\phi,\varphi\rangle_{L^{2}_{\beta}((0,+\infty);H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))}:=\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{0}^{+\infty}\beta(s)\nabla\phi(x,s)\nabla\varphi(x,s)ds\right)dx,

and the Hilbert space

\mathcal{H}=H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)\times L^{2}_{\beta}((0,+\infty);H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)),

equipped with the inner product

\left\langle\left(\begin{array}[]{l}u\\
v\\
w\end{array}\right),\left(\begin{array}[]{l}\tilde{u}\\
\tilde{v}\\
\tilde{w}\end{array}\right)\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}:=(1-\tilde{\beta})\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\nabla\tilde{u}dx+\int_{\Omega}v\tilde{v}dx+\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\beta(s)\nabla w\nabla\tilde{w}dsdx.

We set
U=(u,u_{t},\eta^{t})
. Then, (
4.2
) can be rewritten in the form (
2.8
), where

\mathcal{A}\begin{pmatrix}u\\
v\\
w\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}v\\
(1-\tilde{\beta})\Delta u+\int_{0}^{+\infty}\beta(s)\Delta w(s)ds\\
-w_{s}+v\end{pmatrix},

with domain

\displaystyle{D(\mathcal{A})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{(u,v,w)\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times L^{2}_{\beta}((0,+\infty);H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)):

\displaystyle(1-\tilde{\beta})u+\int_{0}^{+\infty}\beta(s)w(s)ds\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),\ w_{s}\in L^{2}_{\beta}((0,+\infty);H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))\},

\mathcal{B}(u,v,\eta^{t})^{T}:=(0,\chi_{\mathcal{O}}v,0)^{T}
,
\tilde{g}=(u_{0},g,\eta^{0})^{T}
, in
[-\bar{\tau},0]
, and
F(U(t))=(0,|u(t)|^{\sigma}u(t),0)^{T}
.

Now, we consider the functional

\psi(u):=\frac{1}{\sigma+2}\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{\sigma+2}dx,\quad\forall u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

By Sobolev’s embedding theorem,
\psi
is well-defined for
0<\sigma\leqslant\frac{4}{n-2}
. Also, the Gâteaux derivative of
\psi
at any point
u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
is

D\psi(u)(v)=\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{\sigma}u(x)v(x)dx,

for all
v\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
. Moreover, as in
[
4
]
, if
0<\sigma\leqslant\frac{2}{n-2}
, then
\psi
satisfies the assumptions (H1), (H2), (H3).

Let us define the energy in this way:

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{E(t):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|u_{t}(x,t)|^{2}dx+\frac{1-\tilde{\beta}}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x,t)|^{2}dx-\psi(u(x,t))}\\
\hskip 56.9055pt\displaystyle{+\frac{1}{2}\int_{t-\bar{\tau}}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{O}}|k(s)|\cdot|u_{t}(x,s)|^{2}dxds+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\beta(s)\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\eta^{t}(x,s)|^{2}dxds.}\end{array}

Then, from Theorem
3.3
we have that (
4.1
) is well-posed and that, for solutions corresponding to suitably small initial data, an exponential decay estimate holds provided that the condition (
2.6
) is satisfied.

4.2
The wave equation with memory and integral source term

Let
\Omega
be a non-empty bounded set in
{{\rm I}\kern-1.49994pt{\rm R}}^{n}
, with boundary
\Gamma
of class
C^{2}
. Moreover, let
\mathcal{O}\subset\Omega
be a nonempty open subset of
\Omega
. We consider the following wave equation:

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{u_{tt}(x,t)-\Delta u(x,t)+\int_{0}^{+\infty}\beta(s)\Delta u(x,t-s)ds+k(t)\chi_{\mathcal{O}}u_{t}(x,t-\tau(t))}\\
\hskip 199.16928pt\displaystyle{=\left(\int_{\Omega}|u(x,t)|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}u(x,t),\qquad\text{in}\ \Omega\times(0,+\infty),}\\
\displaystyle{u(x,t)=0,\qquad\text{in}\ \Gamma\times(0,+\infty),}\\
\displaystyle{u(x,t)=u_{0}(x,t)\qquad\text{in}\ \Omega\times(-\infty,0],}\\
\displaystyle{u_{t}(x,0)=u_{1}(x),\qquad\text{in}\ \Omega,}\\
\displaystyle{u_{t}(x,t)=g(x,t),\qquad\text{in}\ \Omega\times[-\bar{\tau},0],}\end{array}

(4.3)

where the time delay function
\tau(\cdot)
satisfies (
1.2
),
\beta:(0,+\infty)\rightarrow(0,+\infty)
is a locally absolutely continuous memory kernel such that the assumptions (i)-(iv) are fulfilled,
p\geqslant 1,
and the damping coefficient
k(\cdot)
is a function in
L^{1}_{loc}([-\bar{\tau},+\infty))
for which (
1.3
) holds true.
Then, system (
4.3
) falls in the form (
1.1
) with
A=-\Delta
,
D(A)=H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and
D(A^{\frac{1}{2}})=H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

Let us note that system (
4.3
) is analogous to system (
4.1
), with the only difference given by the nonlinearity. So, arguing as in Example
4.1
, (
4.3
) can be rewritten as (
4.2
) and then as an abstract first order equation.

Now, consider the functional

\psi(u):=\frac{1}{p+2}\left(\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{p+2}{2}}=\frac{1}{p+2}\lVert u\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{p+2},\quad\forall u\in L^{2}(\Omega).

Note that
\psi
is well defined. Also, the Gâteaux derivative of
\psi
at any point
u\in L^{2}(\Omega)
is given by

D\psi(u)(v)=\left(\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\int_{\Omega}u(x)v(x)dx,

for any
v\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. Then,
\psi
is defined in the whole
L^{2}(\Omega)
and

\nabla\psi(u)=\left(\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}u(x),\quad\forall u\in L^{2}(\Omega),

is the unique vector representing
D\psi(u)
in the Riesz isomorphism. So
(H_{1})
is trivially satisfied.
Arguing as in
[
26
]
, we can find a positive constant
C>0
such that

\lVert\nabla\psi(u)-\nabla\psi(v)\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leqslant C(\lVert u\rVert^{2p}_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}+\lVert v\rVert^{2p}_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)})\lVert u-v\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)},

(4.4)

for all
u,v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).
Thus, for any
r>0
and for all
u,v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
with
\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)},\lVert\nabla v\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leqslant r
, since from Poincaré inequality
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}
and
\lVert\nabla(\cdot)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
are equivalent norms on
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, from (
4.4
) we get

\lVert\nabla\psi(u)-\nabla\psi(v)\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leqslant 2r^{2p}C\lVert\nabla u-\nabla v\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

from which

\lVert\nabla\psi(u)-\nabla\psi(v)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leqslant\sqrt{2C}r^{p}\lVert\nabla u-\nabla v\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

Hence,
(H_{2})
is satisfied.

Finally, we prove that
(H_{3})
holds true. Note that
\psi(0),\nabla\psi(0)=0
. Also, using (
4.4
) with
v=0
and Poincaré inequality, for all
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
we can write

\lVert\nabla\psi(u)\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leqslant C\lVert\nabla u\rVert^{2p}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lVert\nabla u\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

which implies

\lVert\nabla\psi(u)\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leqslant\sqrt{C}\lVert\nabla u\rVert^{p}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lVert\nabla u\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

Thus,
(H_{3})
is fulfilled with
h(z)=\sqrt{C}z^{p}
, for all
z\geqslant 0
, which is a continuous and strictly increasing function.

Let us define the energy as follows:

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle{E(t):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|u_{t}(x,t)|^{2}dx+\frac{1-\tilde{\beta}}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x,t)|^{2}dx-\psi(u(x,t))}\\
\hskip 56.9055pt\displaystyle{+\frac{1}{2}\int_{t-\tau}^{t}\int_{\mathcal{O}}|k(s)|\cdot|u_{t}(x,s)|^{2}dxds+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\beta(s)\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\eta^{t}(x,s)|^{2}dxds.}\end{array}

Then, applying Theorem
3.3
to this model, we get well-posedness and exponential decay of the energy for solutions corresponding to suitably small initial data provided that the condition (
2.6
) is satisfied.

TIT/HEP-702 June 2024 Giant graviton expansion for general Wilson line operator indices
Yosuke Imamura, Akihiro Sei, Daisuke Yokoyama

Abstract
We propose a giant graviton expansion for Wilson line operator indices in general representations. The inserted line operators are specified by power sum symmetric polynomials p λ p_{\lambda} labeled by partitions λ \lambda . We interpret the partitions as the structure of fundamental string worldsheets wrapping around the temporal circle. The strings may or may not end on giant gravitons, and by summing the contributions from all brane configurations consistent with the specified partitions, we obtain the finite N N line operator index. The proposed formula is consistent with known results and passes highly non-trivial numerical tests.



1
Introduction

The superconformal index
[
1
,
2
]
is a useful tool
to study supersymmetric field theories.
We can exactly calculate the index for a large class of field theories
and it enables us to analyze non-perturbative aspects such as dualities
and the AdS/CFT correspondence
[
3
,
4
,
5
]
.

It was found in the seminal work
[
2
]
that the large
N
index of the

{\cal N}=4
SYM
is reproduced by the index of the supergravity multiplet
in
AdS_{5}\times S^{5}
.
Furthermore, we can calculate finite
N
corrections to the index on the gravity side
as the contributions
from giant gravitons
[
6
,
7
]
with different wrapping numbers.
Namely, the finite
N
index can be expressed as an expansion over wrapping numbers
of giants.
Such expansions are called giant graviton expansions

[
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
]
.

Recently, giant graviton expansions were extended to the index with Wilson line operator insertions.
In
[
12
]
the insertion of Wilson line operators
in the fundamental and the anti-fundamental
representations was studied,
and in
[
13
]
a giant graviton expansion for
the anti-symmetric representations was proposed.
See
[
14
,
15
]
for approaches from the gauge theory side.
See also
[
11
,
16
]
for giant graviton expansions of the index with surface operator insertions,
and
[
17
]
for a mutual relation between half indices and line operator indices.
The purpose of this work is to generalize the expansion in
[
12
]

for the fundamental representation to lines in general representations.

We consider
{\cal N}=4

U(N)
SYM and define
the six Cartan generators
of the superconformal algebra
psu(2,2|4)
:

\displaystyle H,\quad J_{1},\quad J_{2},\quad R_{x},\quad R_{y},\quad R_{z},

(1)

where
H
is the Hamiltonian,

J_{1}
and
J_{2}
are spin operators, and
R_{x}
,
R_{y}
, and
R_{z}
are Cartan generators
of the
SU(4)_{R}
symmetry.
Wilson line operator indices
[
18
,
19
,
20
,
21
,
22
,
23
]

are defined by operator insertion
from the Schur index
[
24
]
, which
is a specialization of the superconformal index.
We define the Schur index by

\displaystyle I=\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits((-1)^{F}q^{J_{1}}x^{R_{x}}y^{R_{y}}),\quad q=xy.

(2)

See
[
25
,
26
,
27
]
for
analytic formulas of the Schur index without line insertions.

We can calculate the Schur index as the partition function of the theory
in
S^{3}\times S^{1}
,
and line operator indices as
correlators of line operators.
We insert two line operators at two antipodal points in
S^{3}

so that the insertion does not break symmetries used in the definition of the Schur index (
2
).
The path integral reduces to the following holonomy integral

\displaystyle\langle{\cal O}(U){\cal O}^{\prime}(U^{-1})\rangle^{U(N)}=\int_{U(N)}\hskip-13.00005ptdU\mathop{\rm Pexp}\nolimits(f^{({\rm vec})}\chi_{\rm adj}(U)){\cal O}(U){\cal O}^{\prime}(U^{-1}),

(3)

where
\int_{U(N)}dU
is the integral over the gauge group
U(N)
with the normalized Haar measure,
and
\mathop{\rm Pexp}\nolimits
is the plethystic exponential.
The letter index is the product of the
letter index of the
{\cal N}=4
vector multiplet

\displaystyle f^{({\rm vec})}=1-\frac{(1-x)(1-y)}{1-q},

(4)

and
the character of the
U(N)
adjoint representation
\chi_{\rm adj}(U)
.

{\cal O}(U)
and
{\cal O}^{\prime}(U^{-1})
are
gauge invariant functions of the holonomy
U
associated with
the two lines.
We usually diagonalize
U
in practical calculations, and treat
{\cal O}(U)
and
{\cal O}^{\prime}(U^{-1})
as
symmetric functions of
N
diagonal components of
U
.

Large
N
index

Let us review the
line operator indices in the large
N
limit

[
19
,
23
,
28
]
.

A natural basis of Wilson line operators is the set of ones associated with the
U(N)
irreducible representations,
which are labeled by partitions
\mu
.
In the localization formula (
3
), they appear as the characters of the representations.
The characters of
U(N)
irreducible representations
\mu
are called the Schur polynomials,
and denoted by
s_{\mu}(U)
.
For the following analysis on the AdS side, however,
as was pointed out in
[
23
]
,
it is more convenient to use the power sum symmetric polynomials
p_{\lambda}(U)
labeled by partitions
\lambda
:

\displaystyle p_{\lambda}(U)=\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits[P_{\lambda}U^{\otimes|\lambda|}]=\prod_{i=1}^{\ell(\lambda)}(\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits U^{\lambda_{i}}),

(5)

where
\ell(\lambda)
is the number of parts in
\lambda
and
|\lambda|
is the total of
\lambda
.

p_{\lambda}(U)
are related to the Schur polynomials
by Frobenius formula

\displaystyle p_{\lambda}(U)=\sum_{\mu,\ell(\mu)\leq N}\chi_{\mu}(\lambda)s_{\mu}(U),

(6)

where
\chi_{\mu}(\lambda)
are the characters of a representation
of the symmetry group
S_{k}

labeled by a partition
\mu
evaluated at a conjugacy class specified by
\lambda
.

U^{\otimes k}
in (
5
) acts on the tensor product of
k
copies of
N
-dimensional vector space
V
,
and
P_{\lambda}
is the permutation of the vector spaces specified by
\lambda
.

It is convenient to introduce the notation

\displaystyle[f(x)]_{\lambda}=\prod_{i=1}^{\ell(\lambda)}f(x^{\lambda_{i}}).

(7)

By definition, the following relation holds

\displaystyle[f(x)]_{\lambda}[f(x)]_{\lambda^{\prime}}=[f(x)]_{\lambda+\lambda^{\prime}},

(8)

where
\lambda+\lambda^{\prime}
is the union of partitions
\lambda
and
\lambda^{\prime}
,
which is a partition of
|\lambda|+|\lambda^{\prime}|
.

p_{\lambda}
can be expressed as
p_{\lambda}(U)=[\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits U]_{\lambda}
.

With the notation (
7
) we can rewrite the plethystic exponential as

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm Pexp}\nolimits(f(x))=\sum_{\lambda}\frac{1}{z_{\lambda}}[f(x)]_{\lambda}.

(9)

z_{\lambda}
is defined by
z_{\lambda}=\prod_{m=1}^{\infty}m^{r_{m}}r_{m}!
,
where
r_{m}
is the number of occurrences of
m
in
\lambda
.
Let us regard
f(x)
in (
9
) as a letter index of a particle,
and
\mathop{\rm Pexp}\nolimits(f(x))
as the corresponding full index, which is
the sum of contributions
from different particle numbers
n=0,1,2,\ldots
.
For each
n
we can depict particles as a bundle of
n
worldlines,
and when they go around the temporal circle,
they can connect in different ways.
The structure of worldlines, which we refer to as the Chan-Paton structure, can be specified with
partitions
\lambda
with
|\lambda|=n
.
Let
B_{\lambda}
be the bundle of worldlines specified by

\lambda=\{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell(\lambda)}\}
.
It consists of
\ell(\lambda)
components, and each
component is a loop wrapping
\lambda_{i}
times around
the temporal circle.
(Figure
1
(a).)

Figure 1:
(a) World lines
B_{\lambda}
with
\lambda=\{4,2,1,1\}
are shown.
(b) An example of a worldsheet with the Chan-Paton structures on two sides are different.

Each loop gives the factor
f(x^{\lambda_{i}})
,
and together with the statistical factor
1/z_{\lambda}
,
the contribution from a bundle
B_{\lambda}
is given by
the summand in (
9
).
By summing up contributions from all
\lambda
,
we obtain the full index (
9
).

A nice property of the line operators associated with the functions
p_{\lambda}

is that they form an orthogonal basis in the large
N
limit in the sense that

\langle p_{\lambda}(U)p_{\lambda^{\prime}}(U^{-1})\rangle^{U(\infty)}\propto\delta_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}
.
This can be easily shown by using
character expansion method
[
29
,
30
]
;

\displaystyle I_{N,\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\equiv\langle p_{\lambda}(U)p_{\lambda^{\prime}}(U^{-1})\rangle^{U(N)}

\displaystyle=\int_{U(N)}\hskip-13.00005ptdU\sum_{\lambda^{\prime\prime}}\frac{1}{z_{\lambda^{\prime\prime}}}f^{({\rm vec})}_{\lambda^{\prime\prime}}(x)p_{\lambda^{\prime\prime}+\lambda}(U)p_{\lambda^{\prime\prime}+\lambda^{\prime}}(U^{-1})

\displaystyle=\int_{U(N)}\hskip-13.00005ptdU\sum_{\lambda^{\prime\prime}}\frac{1}{z_{\lambda^{\prime\prime}}}f^{({\rm vec})}_{\lambda^{\prime\prime}}(x)\sum_{\mu,\ell(\mu)\leq N}\chi_{\mu}(\lambda^{\prime\prime}+\lambda)s_{\mu}(U)\sum_{\nu,\ell(\nu)\leq N}\chi_{\nu}(\lambda^{\prime\prime}+\lambda^{\prime})s_{\nu}(U^{-1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda^{\prime\prime}}\frac{1}{z_{\lambda^{\prime\prime}}}f^{({\rm vec})}_{\lambda^{\prime\prime}}(x)\sum_{\mu,\ell(\mu)\leq N}\chi_{\mu}(\lambda^{\prime\prime}+\lambda)\chi_{\mu}(\lambda^{\prime\prime}+\lambda^{\prime}).

(10)

We used (
3
), (
8
), and (
9
) at the second equality
and (
6
) at the third equality.
At the last equality we used the orthonormality

\displaystyle\int_{U(N)}\hskip-13.00005ptdUs_{\mu}(U)s_{\nu}(U^{-1})=\delta_{\mu,\nu}.

(11)

In the large
N
limit, the bound
\ell(\mu)\leq N
in the summation over
\mu
is removed,
and we can use another orthogonality

\displaystyle\sum_{\mu}\chi_{\mu}(\lambda)\chi_{\mu}(\lambda^{\prime})=z_{\lambda}\delta_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}.

(12)

Then, we obtain

\displaystyle I_{\infty,\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\delta_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}\sum_{\lambda^{\prime\prime}}\frac{z_{\lambda^{\prime\prime}+\lambda}}{z_{\lambda^{\prime\prime}}}f^{({\rm vec})}_{\lambda^{\prime\prime}}(x)

\displaystyle=z_{\lambda}\delta_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}\prod_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(1-f^{({\rm vec})}(x^{m}))^{r_{m}+1}}

\displaystyle=z_{\lambda}\delta_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}[I_{\rm F1}]_{\lambda}I_{\rm sugra},

(13)

where
I_{\rm sugra}
and
I_{\rm F1}
are
the index of the gravity multiplet in the AdS bulk
[
2
]

and that of fluctuation modes on a single string worldsheet
extended along
AdS_{2}\subset AdS_{5}
obtained in
[
19
]

based on the mode analysis in
[
31
,
32
]
:

\displaystyle I_{\rm sugra}

\displaystyle=\mathop{\rm Pexp}\nolimits\left(\frac{x}{1-x}+\frac{y}{1-y}-\frac{q}{1-q}\right),

\displaystyle I_{\rm F1}

\displaystyle=\mathop{\rm Pexp}\nolimits(x+y-q)=\frac{1-q}{(1-x)(1-y)}=1+\frac{x}{1-x}+\frac{y}{1-y}.

(14)

The large
N
result
(
13
)
can be interpreted on the AdS side as follows
[
21
]
.
The objects corresponding to the inserted line operators
p_{\lambda}(U)
and
p_{\lambda^{\prime}}(U^{-1})

are strings extending along
AdS_{2}\subset AdS_{5}
.
The bundles of worldlines
B_{\lambda}
and
B_{\lambda^{\prime}}
are identified with boundaries of the worldsheets
[
33
,
34
]
.
Let us consider a stack of
|\lambda|
semi-infinite strings
ending on
B_{\lambda}
and another
stack of
|\lambda^{\prime}|
semi-infinite strings ending on
B_{\lambda^{\prime}}
.
The two stacks must be connected in the bulk,
and it is possible only when
|\lambda|=|\lambda^{\prime}|
.
In addition, even if this condition is satisfied,
if
\lambda
and
\lambda^{\prime}
are not the same,
we need to introduce branch points on the worldsheets.
(Figure
1
(b).)
Such worldsheets have higher genus and the amplitude depends
on the string coupling constant
g_{\rm str}
.
We expect such configurations cannot contribute to the index,
and hence we impose the constraint
\lambda=\lambda^{\prime}
.

\delta_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}
in (
13
) expresses this constraint.
The combinatorial factor
z_{\lambda}
is the number of ways of matching between
two stacks of strings.
The structure of the string worldsheets around the temporal circle
is again specified by
\lambda
(
=\lambda^{\prime}
), and the
index of the fluctuation modes on the strings is given by
[I_{\rm F1}]_{\lambda}
.

We remark that for finite
N
the orthogonality of the line operators breaks,
and
I_{N,\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}
are non-vanishing
even when
\lambda\neq\lambda^{\prime}
as long as
|\lambda|=|\lambda^{\prime}|
.
In the following we see that the finite
N
indices
I_{N,\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}
, including the non-diagonal ones,
are reproduced by taking account of contributions of giant gravitons.

2
Double-sum expansion

The giant graviton expansion for the Schur index without operator insertion
is
[
9
]
:

\displaystyle\frac{I_{N}}{I_{\rm sugra}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m_{x},m_{y}=0}^{\infty}x^{m_{x}N}y^{m_{y}N}(xy)^{m_{x}m_{y}}F^{(x)}_{m_{x}}F^{(y)}_{m_{y}}.

(15)

m_{x}
and
m_{y}
are the wrapping numbers of giants
wrapped around
C_{x}
and
C_{y}
, respectively,
where
C_{x}
and
C_{y}
are the three spheres defined as the
R_{x}
-fixed locus and the
R_{y}
-fixed locus
in the internal space
S^{5}
.
The factors
x^{m_{x}N}
and

y^{m_{y}N}
are the classical contributions
from giants.

F^{(x)}_{m_{x}}
is the index of the
U(m_{x})
gauge theory realized on
m_{x}
giants
wrapped on
C_{x}
.

F^{(y)}_{m_{y}}
is defined similarly.
They are related to the Schur index
I_{m}
of the
{\cal N}=4

U(m)
SYM
by
[
9
]

\displaystyle F^{(x)}_{m}=\sigma_{x}I_{m},\quad F^{(y)}_{m}=\sigma_{y}I_{m},

(16)

where
\sigma_{x}
and
\sigma_{y}
are the variable changes

\displaystyle\sigma_{x}:(q,x,y)\rightarrow(y,x^{-1},q),\quad\sigma_{y}:(q,x,y)\rightarrow(x,q,y^{-1}).

(17)

In AdS/CFT correspondence,
a fundamental Wilson line operator is realized
as the boundary of a fundamental string worldsheet
[
33
,
34
]
.
Corresponding to the line operators inserted at antipodal points in
S^{3}
,
we introduce two semi-infinite strings.
By connecting them, we obtain a single worldsheet extended on
AdS_{2}\subset AdS_{5}
.

If
N
is finite and giant gravitons are present,
the semi-infinite strings can end on the giants.

\displaystyle\frac{I_{N,\hskip 0.0pt\vbox{\vbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.3pt\hbox{\vrule height=2.72pt,width=0.3pt,depth=0.67998pt\hbox to3.4pt{\hfil}\vrule height=2.72pt,width=0.3pt,depth=0.67998pt}\hrule height=0.3pt}\vskip-0.3pt}}\hskip 0.0pt,\hskip 0.0pt\vbox{\vbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.3pt\hbox{\vrule height=2.72pt,width=0.3pt,depth=0.67998pt\hbox to3.4pt{\hfil}\vrule height=2.72pt,width=0.3pt,depth=0.67998pt}\hrule height=0.3pt}\vskip-0.3pt}}\hskip 0.0pt}}{I_{\rm sugra}}

In (

the contribution from specific wrapping numbers
(m_{x},m_{y})
consists of
three terms
corresponding to three ways of connection of the two
semi-infinite fundamental strings:
the direct connection,
the connection via giants on
C_{x}
,

.

I_{\rm F1}^{(x/y)}

\displaystyle I_{\rm F1}^{(x)}=\mathop{\rm Pexp}\nolimits(y-xy),\quad I_{\rm F1}^{(y)}=\mathop{\rm Pexp}\nolimits(x-xy).

From the viewpoint of theories realized on giants,

F^{(x/y)}_{m,\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}
are line-operator

They are given by

\displaystyle F^{(x)}_{m,\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}=\sigma_{x}I_{m,\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}},\quad F^{(y)}_{m,\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}=\sigma_{y}I_{m,\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}.

For

The factors

A lesson from
(

is that we obtain finite

line-operator indices
by summing up contributions of all ways of connection
of semi-infinite strings.
With this lesson in mind,
let us generalize (
18
) to
more general line operator indices
I_{N,\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}
.

We want an expansion in the form

\displaystyle\frac{I_{N,\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}}{I_{\rm sugra}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m_{x},m_{y}=0}^{\infty}S_{N,\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}^{(m_{x},m_{y})},

(21)

where each function
S_{N,\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}^{(m_{x},m_{y})}
is
the contribution from the system consisting of
giants with wrapping numbers
(m_{x},m_{y})

and two stacks of semi-infinite strings with
the Chan-Paton structures specified by
\lambda
and
\lambda^{\prime}
.
There are different ways of connection of two stacks of strings,
and as the sum of their contributions
we propose the formula

\displaystyle S_{N,\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}^{(m_{x},m_{y})}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\ell(\lambda)+\ell(\lambda^{\prime})}\sum_{\lambda=\lambda_{0}+\lambda_{x}+\lambda_{y}}\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}=\lambda^{\prime}_{0}+\lambda^{\prime}_{x}+\lambda^{\prime}_{y}}z_{\lambda_{0}}\delta_{\lambda_{0},\lambda^{\prime}_{0}}[I_{\rm F1}]_{\lambda_{0}}

\displaystyle\times x^{m_{x}N}\frac{1}{x^{|\lambda_{x}|}}F^{(x)}_{m_{x},\lambda_{x},\lambda^{\prime}_{x}}[I_{\rm F1}^{(x)}]_{\lambda_{x}+\lambda^{\prime}_{x}}

\displaystyle\times y^{m_{y}N}\frac{1}{y^{|\lambda_{y}|}}F^{(y)}_{m_{y},\lambda_{y},\lambda^{\prime}_{y}}[I_{\rm F1}^{(y)}]_{\lambda_{y}+\lambda^{\prime}_{y}}

\displaystyle\times(xy)^{m_{x}m_{y}}.

,
\lambda_{x}
, and
\lambda_{y}
.

\lambda

,
\lambda_{x}
, and
\lambda_{y}

\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}=\lambda^{\prime}_{0}+\lambda^{\prime}_{x}+\lambda^{\prime}_{y}}

4
Conclusions and Discussion

As a natural generalization of large
N
line-operator indices
in arbitrary representations (
13
)
and the giant graviton expansion for the fundamental line-operator index (
18
),
we proposed a double-sum giant graviton expansion of Wilson
line-operator indices in arbitrary representations (
21
) and (
22
).
We adopted the holographic realization of line operators by fundamental strings, and
a weight
k
representation is realized by
k
coincident fundamental strings.
In this picture, it is natural to use the symmetric power sum polynomials
p_{\lambda}

rather than the Schur polynomials
as the basis of symmetric polynomials, and a partition
\lambda
specifies not an irreducible
U(N)
representation
but the Chan-Paton structure of the worldsheet around the temporal circle.
Coincident worldsheets consist of
\ell(\lambda)
disconnected components,
and each component may or may not end on giant gravitons.
There are different ways of connecting semi-infinite strings,
and by summing up all contributions
from them, we obtain the finite
N
line operator index.
The formula (
21
) with (
22
) passes highly non-trivial numerical tests.
We also numerically confirmed the simple-sum version
of the expansion (
38
) and (
39
)
reproduces correct indices.

There are many ways of extending our results.
One is to consider composite systems of F1, D5, and D3-branes extending along
AdS_{2}
.
The F1 description of line operators in this paper is appropriate
for representations with weights of order one.
For the symmetric and anti-symmetric representations with weight of order
N
,
it is more natural to realize the line operators by D3-branes

[
38
]
and D5-branes
[
39
]
, respectively.
In particular, the line-operator index for anti-symmetric representations
is reproduced on the AdS side by D5-brane for large
N

[
19
]

and for finite
N

[
13
]
.
We can combine them to realize more complicated line operators corresponding
to a Young diagram consisting of a few long columns, a few long rows, and a few boxes.
Naive expectation is that we can realize such a line operator
as a composite system of F1, D3, and D5-branes.
It is interesting to investigate giant graviton expansions for such line operators.

For representation with weight of order
N^{2}
,
the line operators are holographically realized by bubbling geometries

[
40
]
, and indices for such line operators
are studied in
[
23
]
.
It is interesting to consider the finite
N
correction in such cases.

It is also interesting to consider other theories.
In this work, we investigated
{\cal N}=4

U(N)
SYM exclusively.
We can also consider
{\cal N}=4
SYM with other gauge groups and
more general field theories with fewer supersymmetries.
We hope we will return to these problems near future.

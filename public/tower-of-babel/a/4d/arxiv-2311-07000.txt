(3)

Proof.

(1) Let

\displaystyle T^{-}_{t}\circ T^{+}_{t}\phi(x)=

\displaystyle\,\inf_{y\in M}\{T^{+}_{t}\phi(y)+A_{t}(y,x)\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\inf_{y\in M}\{\sup_{z\in M}\{\phi(z)-A_{t}(y,z)\}+A_{t}(y,x)\}

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\,\phi(x)

by taking

. Thus, if

, then

\displaystyle T^{-}_{t_{2}}\circ T^{+}_{t_{2}}\phi=T^{-}_{t_{1}}\circ T^{-}_{t_{2}-t_{1}}\circ T^{+}_{t_{2}-t_{1}}\circ T^{+}_{t_{1}}\phi\geqslant T^{-}_{t_{1}}\circ T^{+}_{t_{1}}\phi.

Now, let

. Then there exists a

(3) Suppose

\displaystyle T^{+}_{t}\circ T^{-}_{t}\circ T^{+}_{t}\phi=T^{+}_{t}\circ(T^{-}_{t}\circ T^{+}_{t})\phi\geqslant T^{+}_{t}\phi.

u^{+}(x)\leqslant u(t,x)\leqslant u^{-}(x)
. If
u(t,x)=u^{-}(x)
, then we can choose
\gamma:[t,0]\to M
,
\gamma(t)=x
to be a calibrated curve for

. Thus,

\displaystyle u^{-}(x)=u(t,x)=u^{-}(\gamma(0))-\int_{t}^{0}L(\gamma,\dot{\gamma})\ ds.

So,
\gamma
is in fact a calibrated curve for
u^{-}
, which implies

. As a result,
(t,x)\in\mathcal{A}^{*}(u^{-})
. This leads to a contradiction. So we have
u(t,x)<u^{-}(x)
. If
u^{+}(x)=u(t,x)
, we know that
(0,D^{-}u^{+}(x))\in D^{-}u(t,x)=\mbox{\rm co}\,D^{*}u(t,x)
. Since

for all calibrated curves of

\displaystyle q=-H(x,p)>0,\qquad\forall(q,p)\in D^{*}u(t,x).

This leads to a contradiction with

.
∎

.

Corollary 3.15
.

Under the same assumptions of Proposition
3.14
, the following statements are equivalent.

(1)

\phi=T^{-}_{t_{0}}\psi
.

(2)

u_{\psi}:[-t_{0},0]\times M\to\mathbb{R}
is a viscosity solution of the Hamilton-Jacobi equation

\displaystyle\begin{cases}D_{t}u+H(x,D_{x}u)=0,&(t,x)\in[-t_{0},0)\times M,\\

(3)

\psi(x)=T^{+}_{t_{0}}\phi(x)
for all

, and
T^{+}_{t_{0}}\phi(x)\leqslant\psi(x)
otherwise.

(1)
\Leftrightarrow
(2) is trivial. (1)
\Rightarrow
(3) follows directly from Proposition
3.14
. We only need to prove that (3)
\Rightarrow
(1).
\psi\geqslant T^{+}_{t_{0}}\phi
implies
T^{-}_{t_{0}}\psi\geqslant T^{-}_{t_{0}}T^{+}_{t_{0}}\phi=\phi
. On the other hand, for any

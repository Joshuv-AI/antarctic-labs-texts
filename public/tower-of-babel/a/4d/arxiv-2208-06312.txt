We denote by

of

The purpose of this paper is twofold. The first one is to find some numerical sufficient and necessary conditions in terms of the degrees of irreducible representations of

K

To explain the second purpose of the paper, we need to recall the following concept that was introduced by the first author in

]

Definition 1.1

Let

A Mathieu subspace is also called a Mathieu-Zhao space or Mathieu-Zhao subspace

One of the most fundamental theorems in the theory of Mathieu subspaces is the Duistermaat and van der Kallen theorem
[

which was proved in

can be essentially restated as follows.

Theorem 1.2

Let

(the free abelian group of rank

V_{G}
is a MS of

\mathbb{C}G
. In terms of
K

in

V
of all Laurent polynomials in

with no constant term is a MS of
{\mathbb{C}}[x^{-1},x]
.

In order to understand the Duistermaat and van der Kallen theorem in a more general setting, the following problem was proposed
in
[

, Problem

]

Open Problem 1.3

Decide commutative rings

such that

is a Mathieu subspace of the group algebra

of

.

The following proposition and theorem among some other results

is a MS of

does not contain any nonzero idempotent.

.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.6

Throughout this section,
G
stands for a finite group and
K
a splitting field of
G
of characteristic
p>0
. We denote by
\phi_{i}

(1\leq i\leq s)
all the (non-isomorphic) irreducible representations of
G
over
K
and
n_{i}
the degree of the representation
\phi_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq s
. Here we fix the irreducible representation
\phi_{i}

(1\leq i\leq s)
as a
K
-algebra homomorphism from
KG
to the
n_{i}\times n_{i}
matrix algebra
M_{n_{i}}(K)
. Note that by Burnside’s Theorem (e.g., see
[
PD
, Corollary, p.
11
]
) each
\phi_{i}

(1\leq i\leq s)
is surjective.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}={\bigoplus}_{i=1}^{s}M_{n_{i}}(K)
and

{\mathcal{A}}_{i}=M_{n_{i}}(K)

(1\leq i\leq s)
. For each
1\leq i\leq s

we define
\operatorname{tr}_{i}:{\mathcal{A}}\to K
to the
K
-linear functional whose restriction on the
i
-th component

of
{\mathcal{A}}
is the usual trace function of the
n_{i}\times n_{i}
matrices,
and on
j
-th component of
{\mathcal{A}}
is equal to
0
for all
j\neq i
.

With the notations and settings above we recall the classical Wedderburn Structure Theorem
(see
[
PD
, Theorem
1.3A
]
) for
KG
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Define
\Phi:KG\to{\mathcal{A}}
be setting
\Phi(\sigma)=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\phi_{i}(\sigma)
for all
\sigma\in KG
. Then

\Phi
is a surjective homomorphism of
K
-algebras and
\operatorname{Ker\,}\Phi=J(KG)
, where
J(KG)
stands for the Jacobson radical
of
KG
.

Next, we recall the following theorem which clarifies all co-dimensional one MS’ of
{\mathcal{A}}
.

Theorem 2.2
.

(
[
Ko
, Corollary
4.5
]
)

A codimensional one
K
-subspace
V
of
{\mathcal{A}}
is a MS of
{\mathcal{A}}
,
if and only if
V=\operatorname{Ker\,}L
for some
K
-linear functional
L
of
{\mathcal{A}}
such that
L=\sum_{i=1}^{s}c_{i}\operatorname{tr}_{i}
, where
the coefficients
c_{i}\in K

(1\leq i\leq s)

satisfy the property:
for all
\emptyset\neq J\subseteq\{1,2,...,s\}
with
not all
c_{i}

(i\in J)
being zero, we have

\sum_{i\in J}c_{i}\neq 0
.

The following proposition classifies all the co-dimensional one MS’
V

of
KG
with
V\supseteq J(KG)
(the Jacobson radical of
KG
).

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
V
be a co-dimensional one
K
-subspace of
KG
with
V\supseteq J(KG)
, and
\chi_{i}
the character of
G
obtained from the irreducible representation
\phi_{i}

(1\leq i\leq s)
.
Then
V
is a MS of
KG
,
if and only if
V=\operatorname{Ker\,}\ell
, where
\ell=\sum_{i=1}^{s}c_{i}\chi_{i}
for some
c_{i}\in K

(1\leq i\leq s)
which satisfy the property:
for all
\emptyset\neq J\subseteq\{1,2,...,s\}
with
not all
c_{i}

(i\in J)
being zero, we have

\sum_{i\in J}c_{i}\neq 0
.

Proof:
Since
\Phi
by Theorem
2.1
is surjective and
V\supseteq J(KG)
, there exists a co-dimensional one
K
-subspace
W
of
{\mathcal{A}}
such that
V=\Phi^{-1}(W)
(Actually,
W=\Phi(V)
).
By Theorem
2.2
(or
[
EKC
, Theorem
5.8.1
]
),

W
is a MS of
{\mathcal{A}}
iff
W=\operatorname{Ker\,}L
for some
K
-linear functional
L
of
{\mathcal{A}}
such that
L=\sum_{i=1}^{s}c_{i}\operatorname{tr}_{i}
for some
c_{i}\in K

(1\leq i\leq s)
which satisfy the property in the proposition.

Let
\Phi^{*}
be the dual map of
\Phi
, i.e.,
\Phi^{*}(\psi)=\psi\circ\Phi
for all
K
-linear functional
\psi
of
{\mathcal{A}}
.
Then by definition of characters we have
\Phi^{*}(\operatorname{tr}_{i})=\chi_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq s
. Since
V=\Phi^{-1}(W)
,
W=\Phi(V)
and
W=\operatorname{Ker\,}L
, we have
V=\operatorname{Ker\,}\ell
with
\ell=\phi^{*}(L)=\sum_{i=1}^{s}c_{i}\chi_{i}
.
Since by Theorem

and
[

, Proposition
2.7
]
,

is a MS of

, if and only if

is a MS of
{\mathcal{A}}
, the proposition follows.

Now, we can prove Theorem

for the semisimple case.

Lemma 2.4
.

Assume that
p\nmid|G|
. Then

i)

\Phi(V_{G})=\operatorname{Ker\,}F
, where
F=\sum_{i=1}^{s}n_{i}\operatorname{tr}_{i}
.

ii)

V_{G}=\operatorname{Ker\,}\ell
, where
\ell=\Phi^{*}(F)=\sum_{i=1}^{s}n_{i}\chi_{i}
.

iii)

V_{G}
is a MS of
KG
, if and only if
\sum_{i=1}^{s}c_{i}n_{i}\neq 0
for all

\vec{0}\neq(c_{1},...,c_{s})\in\{0,1,...,n_{1}\}\times\cdots\times\{0,1,...,n_{s}\}
. In other words,
Theorem
1.6
holds in this case.

Proof:
Note first that, since
p\nmid|G|
, by Maschke’s Theorem (e.g., see
[
PD
, Theorem
1.3B
]
)
the group algebra
KG
is semisimple, i.e.,
J(KG)=0
. Then
\Phi
in Theorem
2.1
is a
K
-algebra isomorphism.

i)
Let
1_{G}\neq g\in G
and
u=\Phi(g)
. Write
u=\sum_{i=1}^{s}B_{i}
with
B_{i}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{i}\!:=M_{n_{i}}(K)
for all
1\leq i\leq s
. To show
u\in\operatorname{Ker\,}F
, we consider the trace of
the regular action of
g
on
KG
and the trace of the regular action of
u
on
{\mathcal{A}}
.
Since
\Phi
in this case is an isomorphism of
K
-algebras, we know that these two traces are equal to one another.

First, by choosing elements of
G
as a basis of
KG
, it is easy to see that the trace of the regular action of
g

on
KG
is equal to
0
.

Second, for each
1\leq i\leq s
, since the matrix
B_{i}
annihilates all elements of
{\mathcal{A}}_{j}
for all

j\neq i
, the trace of the regular action of
B_{i}
on
{\mathcal{A}}
is equal to the trace of the regular action of
B_{i}
on

{\mathcal{A}}_{i}=M_{n_{i}}(K)
, which can be readily checked to be

n_{i}\operatorname{tr}_{i}(B_{i})
by choosing the matrices

’s
(with

as the
(k,\ell)
-entry and zero elsewhere) as a basis of
\mathcal{A}_{i}=M_{n_{i}}(K)
. Hence the trace of
the regular action of

on
{\mathcal{A}}
is equal to

\sum_{i=1}^{s}n_{i}\operatorname{tr}_{i}(B_{i})
.
Therefore
\sum_{i=1}^{s}n_{i}\operatorname{tr}_{i}(B_{i})=0
, i.e.,

, whence
\Phi(V_{G})\subseteq\operatorname{Ker\,}F
for

is spanned over

by all non-identity elements of

. Since
\Phi(V_{G})
and
\operatorname{Ker\,}F
are both co-dimensional

{\mathcal{A}}
, we have
\Phi(V_{G})=\operatorname{Ker\,}F
, and statement
i)
follows.

ii)
follows immediately from
i)
, and the fact that
\Phi^{*}(\operatorname{tr}_{i})=\chi_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq s
.

iii)
follows immediately from
ii)
, Proposition
2.3
above and the fact that
n_{i}\neq 0
for all
1\leq i\leq s
.
\Box

Finally, from Theorem

Lemma 4.1

i)\Rightarrow ii)

. Since

and hence algebraic over

[

ii)\Leftrightarrow iii)

Scaling Symmetry Reductions of Coupled KdV Systems
Allan P. Fordy, School of Mathematics

Abstract
In this paper we discuss the Painlevé reductions of coupled KdV systems. We start by comparing the procedure with that of stationary reductions . Indeed, we see that exactly the same construction can be used at each step and parallel results obtained. For simplicity, we restrict attention to the t 2 t_{2} flow of the KdV and DWW hierarchies and derive respectively 2 and 3 compatible Poisson brackets, which have identical structure to those of their stationary counterparts. In the KdV case, we derive a discrete version, which is a non-autonomous generalisation of the well known Darboux transformation of the stationary case.



Keywords
: Coupled KdV, bi-Hamiltonian, stationary flow, Hamiltonian system, Poisson matrix.

MSC: 35Q53, 37J37, 37K05, 70H06

Introduction

In 1976 Bogoyavlenskii and Novikov
[
8
]
considered stationary flows of the KdV hierarchy and showed that each stationary flow defines a completely integrable, finite dimensional Hamiltonian system. Importantly the Lagrangian and canonical coordinates of the stationary flow, as well as the first integrals, were
deduced
from those of the KdV hierarchy.
This idea was generalised to
coupled KdV (cKdV)
hierarchies, as well as to higher order Lax operators, in several papers (see, for example,
[
4
,
10
,
11
,
5
,
9
]
). After some years of dormancy, the subject has recently been revisited
[
12
,
6
,
7
,
13
]
. The emphasis in
[
6
,
7
]
has been on separability, whilst that of
[
12
,
13
]
has been to show how the important structures of the stationary systems are
directly constructed
from those of the original PDE system.

In this paper we consider (some specific) nonlinear evolution equations of the cKdV hierarchy. Recall that these are isospectral to the “energy-dependent Schrödinger operator”
[
2
,
3
]

L=\partial_{x}^{2}+u,\quad\mbox{with}\quad u=\sum_{i=0}^{N}u_{i}\lambda^{i},\;\;\mbox{where}\;\;u_{N}=-1,

(1)

and that the resulting
N
component system for
(u_{0},\dots,u_{N-1})
has
N+1
compatible Hamiltonian operators, with each flow being written

{\bf u}_{t_{n}}=B_{N}\delta_{u}H_{n}=B_{N-1}\delta_{u}H_{n+1}=\cdots=B_{0}\delta_{u}H_{n+N}.

(2)

In
[
3
]
it was shown that the
N
component system possessed
N
modifications, generalising the MKdV hierarchy. These play an important role in this paper.

These equations also have some additional Lie point symmetries: time translation and scaling. For each of these we can consider invariant solutions, which are therefore constrained to satisfy an additional first order, linear PDE, with the result that (
2
) reduces to an ODE. It is not always the case that a Hamiltonian formulation can be reduced to this ODE.

The simplest case is the
stationary flow
,
{\bf u}_{t}=0
, related to the time-translation symmetry. If

{\bf u}_{t}=B\delta_{u}H,

(3)

and
\mbox{Ker}B=\delta_{u}{\cal C}
, for some Casimir
\cal C
, then

B\delta_{u}H=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad\delta_{u}\left(H-{\cal C}\right)=0,

(4)

giving a Lagrangian form of the reduced equation for the stationary flow, from which a canonical Hamiltonian formulation can be found. This is the case discussed in the papers listed above.

In this paper we consider the reduction related to the scaling symmetry, which gives rise to equations of “Painlevé” type (see
[
1
,
15
,
16
]
). For this reduction, most of the Hamiltonian formulations cannot be reduced. For example, the KdV operator
B_{0}
and the dispersive water wave (DWW) operators
B_{0}
and
B_{2}
cannot be reduced. However, we still have a Miura map to a modified system, so, when (
3
) can be reduced, we can still construct multi-Hamiltonian formulations for the finite dimensional, reduced system. In fact the formulae can be taken directly from the stationary case.

It was shown in
[
12
,
13
]
that the second nontrivial flow (the “
t_{2}
flow”) has (for each
N
) a particularly interesting stationary reduction, of the form

h^{(Q)}=\frac{1}{2}(P_{1}^{2}+P_{2}^{2})+U(Q_{1},Q_{2}),

(5)

where
U(Q_{1},Q_{2})
is one of the family of potentials, separable in
parabolic coordinates
(see Equation 2.2.41 in
[
17
]
):

u=\sqrt{Q_{1}^{2}+Q_{2}^{2}}+Q_{1},\quad v=\sqrt{Q_{1}^{2}+Q_{2}^{2}}-Q_{1}.

(6)

We consider non-autonomous extensions of these, arising from the scaling symmetry reductions. Some of these equations are well known (see
[
15
,
16
]
), but here we also introduce generalisations of the Poisson brackets derived in
[
12
,
13
]
. In fact, it was shown in
[
13
]
that the entire class of
h^{(Q)}
(separable in parabolic coordinates) have Poisson brackets which are written in terms of gradients of
h^{(Q)}
and the commuting integral
f^{(Q)}
. These formulae do not change in the non-autonomous case, but the functions
h^{(Q)},\,f^{(Q)}
now possess non-autonomous terms.

This brief paper presents these ideas in two main sections.

Section
2
is concerned with the simplest case of (
1
) with
N=1
, which is the standard KdV hierarchy. For the
t_{2}
flow, this gives a non-autonomous Hénon-Heiles system (see also
[
15
]
) and its modification. The Miura map is used to construct a bi-Hamiltonian formulation of both.
In
[
14
]
the Darboux transformation is used to construct a discrete version of the stationary flows of the MKdV hierarchy. This is generalised to the non-autonomous case in Section
2.4
.

Section
3
is concerned with the case of (
1
) with
N=2
, which is the DWW hierarchy. For the
t_{2}
flow, this gives a non-autonomous Hamiltonian with quartic potential (see also
[
16
]
). The Miura map is now used to construct a tri-Hamiltonian formulation of this and its modification.

2
The KdV and MKdV Hierarchies (N=1)

This is a well known case, with
u=u_{0}-\lambda
, so some details will be sparse.

2.1
The KdV Hierarchy

The KdV hierarchy has the bi-Hamiltonian representation

u_{0t_{n}}=B_{1}^{u}\delta_{u_{0}}H_{n}^{u}=B_{0}^{u}\delta_{u_{0}}H_{n+1}^{u},\;\;n\geq 0,

(7a)

with

B_{1}^{u}=\partial_{x}^{3}+4u_{0}\partial_{x}+2u_{0x},\quad B_{0}^{u}=4\partial_{x},

(7b)

where
\delta_{u_{0}}
denotes the variational derivative with respect to
u_{0}
. The first 4 Hamiltonian densities are

H_{0}^{u}=u_{0},\;\;\;H_{1}^{u}=\frac{1}{4}\,u_{0}^{2},\;\;\;H_{2}^{u}=\frac{1}{8}\left(u_{0}^{3}-\frac{1}{2}u_{0x}^{2}\right),\;\;\;H_{3}^{u}=\frac{1}{64}\left(5u_{0}^{4}-10u_{0}u_{0x}^{2}+u_{0xx}^{2}\right).

(7c)

2.1.1
Reduction Through Scaling Symmetry

The scaling symmetry for the
t_{n}
flow is

S^{u}=x\partial_{x}+(2n+1)t_{n}\partial_{t_{n}}-2u_{0}\partial_{u_{0}},\;\;\;\mbox{with invariants}\;\;\;z=x\,t_{n}^{-\frac{1}{2n+1}},\;\;w=t_{n}^{\frac{2}{2n+1}}u_{0}.

(8a)

We substitute
u_{0}(x,t_{n})=w(z)\,t_{n}^{-\frac{2}{2n+1}}
and find

\partial_{x}=t_{n}^{-\frac{1}{2n+1}}\,\partial_{z},\;\;B_{1}^{u}=t_{n}^{-\frac{3}{2n+1}}B_{1}^{w},\;\;H_{\ell}^{u}=t_{n}^{-\frac{2(\ell+1)}{2n+1}}H_{\ell}^{w},\;\;\delta_{u_{0}}=t_{n}^{\frac{2}{2n+1}}\,\delta_{w},

(8b)

where, for
B_{1}^{w}
and
H_{n}^{w}
, we just replace
u_{0}
by
w
(and
\partial_{x}
by
\partial_{z}
). Since this is a symmetry of the flow, the result of substitution in (
7a
) has a
homogeneous
factor of
t_{n}^{-\frac{2n+3}{2n+1}}
, which can be cancelled, so the result can be written

-\frac{1}{2n+1}\,\left(zw_{z}+2w\right)=B_{1}^{w}\delta_{w}H_{n}^{w}=B_{0}^{w}\delta_{w}H_{n+1}^{w}.

(8c)

Since
zw_{z}+2w
is not in the range of
B_{0}^{w}=4\partial_{z}
, we cannot write a formula analogous to (
4
). However

B_{1}^{w}z=4w+2zw_{z}\quad\Rightarrow\quad B_{1}\delta_{w}\left(H_{n}^{w}+\frac{zw}{2(2n+1)}\right)=0.

(8d)

Following the approach outlined in
[
5
]
and
[
12
]
(after Eq (3.2a)), we have

\delta_{w}\left(H_{n}^{w}+\frac{zw}{2(2n+1)}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\varphi^{2}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\varphi_{zz}+w\varphi=2\beta\varphi^{-3},

(8e)

leading to the Lagrangian

{\cal L}_{n}^{w}=H_{n}^{w}+\frac{zw}{2(2n+1)}-\frac{1}{2}w\varphi^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\varphi_{z}^{2}-\frac{\beta}{\varphi^{2}}.

(8f)

2.2
The Miura Map and MKdV Hierarchy

The modified KdV hierarchy is related to the KdV hierarchy through the Miura map

u_{0}=-v_{0x}-v_{0}^{2},

(9a)

and the second Hamiltonian operator,
B_{1}
, of the KdV hierarchy factorises

\partial_{x}^{3}+4u_{0}\partial_{x}+2u_{0x}=(-\partial_{x}-2v_{0})(-\partial_{x})(\partial_{x}-2v_{0})\quad\Rightarrow\quad v_{0t_{n}}=-\partial_{x}\,\delta_{v_{0}}H_{n}^{v},

(9b)

where
H_{n}^{v}[v_{0}]=H_{n}^{u}\left[-v_{0x}-v_{0}^{2}\right]
. There is an additional Hamiltonian density
H_{-1}^{v}=v_{0}
, which is the Casimir of the Hamiltonian operator. Also, by direct substitution, the formula
H_{n}^{u}\left[-v_{0x}-v_{0}^{2}\right]
includes
exact
x-
derivatives
, which can be removed, to simplify the formula for
H_{n}^{v}[v_{0}]
.

2.2.1
Reduction through Scaling Symmetry

Here we use
z=x\,t_{n}^{-\frac{1}{2n+1}}
and
v_{0}(x,t_{n})=\theta(z)\,t_{n}^{-\frac{1}{2n+1}}
(which follows from the Miura map), leading to

v_{0t_{n}}=\frac{-1}{(2n+1)t_{n}^{\frac{2(n+1)}{2n+1}}}\left(z\theta\right)_{z},\quad H_{n}^{v}=t_{n}^{-\frac{2(n+1)}{2n+1}}H_{n}^{\theta},\quad\delta_{v_{0}}=t_{n}^{\frac{1}{2n+1}}\,\delta_{\theta},\quad\partial_{x}=t_{n}^{-\frac{1}{2n+1}}\partial_{z}.

(10a)

Piecing these together, we obtain

\frac{-1}{(2n+1)}\,\partial_{z}\left(z\theta\right)=-\partial_{z}\delta_{\theta}H_{n}^{\theta}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\delta_{\theta}{\cal L}_{n}^{\theta}=0,\;\;\mbox{where}\;\;{\cal L}_{n}^{\theta}=H_{n}^{\theta}-\frac{1}{2(2n+1)}z\theta^{2}-\alpha\theta.

(10b)

2.4
The Discrete Case

In
[
14
]
(see Section 4.2) we used a Darboux transformation to construct a discrete version of the stationary flows of the MKdV hierarchy.
It is straightforward to generalise these maps to the non-autonomous case. Recall that we have two choices of Miura map (
9a
), giving
Q_{1}=-\tilde{q}_{2}-\tilde{q}_{1}^{2}=q_{2}-q_{1}^{2}
, where
q_{1}
and
\tilde{q}_{1}
correspond to two different solutions of (

\tilde{p}_{2}+p_{2}+2q_{1}q_{2}-2\tilde{q}_{1}\tilde{q}_{2}=0
, etc, to construct the equations, which can be solved explicitly, to give (with

\displaystyle\tilde{q}_{1}=-q_{1}-\frac{\alpha}{A^{+}},\qquad\tilde{q}_{2}=-q_{2}-\frac{2\alpha q_{1}}{A^{+}}-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{(A^{+})^{2}},

\displaystyle\tilde{p}_{1}=-p_{1}+\frac{2\alpha(p_{2}-6q_{1}^{3}-4q_{1}q_{2})}{A^{+}}-\frac{2\alpha^{2}(11q_{1}^{2}+q_{2})}{(A^{+})^{2}}-\frac{16\alpha^{3}q_{1}}{(A^{+})^{3}}-\frac{4\alpha^{4}}{(A^{+})^{4}},

(16)

\displaystyle\tilde{p}_{2}=-p_{2}-\frac{2\alpha(2q_{1}^{2}+q_{2})}{A^{+}}-\frac{6\alpha^{2}q_{1}}{(A^{+})^{2}}-\frac{2\alpha^{3}}{(A^{+})^{3}},

where
A^{+}
is defined above (
14a
). This map preserves both Poisson brackets and the functions
h^{(q)}
and
f^{(q)}
and satisfies
A^{+}({\bf q})=-A^{-}({\bf\tilde{q}})
.

Remark 2.1

This construction relies on the discrete symmetry
v\mapsto-v
of the MKdV hierarchy.

3
The DWW and MDWW Hierarchies (N=2)

This is the simplest example of a (truly)
coupled
KdV system.

3.1
The DWW Hierarchy

The DWW hierarchy is tri-Hamiltonian, with the
n^{th}
flow being written

{\bf u}_{t_{n}}=B_{2}\delta_{u}H_{n}=B_{1}\delta_{u}H_{n+1}=B_{0}\delta_{u}H_{n+2},

(17a)

where

B_{2}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&J_{0}^{u}\\
J_{0}^{u}&J_{1}^{u}\end{array}\right),\;\;B_{1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}J_{0}^{u}&0\\
0&-J_{2}^{u}\end{array}\right),\;\;B_{0}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-J_{1}^{u}&-J_{2}^{u}\\
-J_{2}^{u}&0\end{array}\right),

(17b)

with
{\bf u}=(u_{0},u_{1})^{T}
and

J_{0}^{u}=\partial_{x}^{3}+4u_{0}\partial_{x}+2u_{0x},\quad J_{1}^{u}=4u_{1}\partial_{x}+2u_{1x},\quad J_{2}^{u}=-4\partial_{x}.

(17c)

The first 4 Hamiltonians are

H_{0}^{u}=u_{1},\;\;\;H_{1}^{u}=u_{0}+\frac{1}{4}u_{1}^{2},\;\;\;H_{2}^{u}=\frac{1}{2}u_{1}\left(u_{0}+\frac{1}{4}u_{1}^{2}\right),\;\;\;H_{3}^{u}=\frac{1}{4}\left(u_{0}^{2}+\frac{3}{2}u_{0}u_{1}^{2}+\frac{5}{16}u_{1}^{4}-\frac{1}{4}u_{1x}^{2}\right).

(17d)

3.1.1
Reduction Through Scaling Symmetry

The DWW hierarchy corresponds to the Lax operator (
1
) with
N=2
, with scaling symmetry

S^{u}=x\partial_{x}+mt_{n}\partial_{t_{n}}-2u_{0}\partial_{u_{0}}-u_{1}\partial_{u_{1}},\quad\mbox{and invariants}\;\;z=\frac{x}{t_{n}^{\frac{1}{m}}},\;\;w_{0}=u_{0}t_{n}^{\frac{2}{m}},\;\;w_{1}=u_{1}t_{n}^{\frac{1}{m}},

(18a)

where
m=n+1
(as shown below).

Defining
u_{0}(x,t_{n})=w_{0}(z)t_{n}^{-\frac{2}{m}},\;u_{1}(x,t_{n})=w_{1}(z)t_{n}^{-\frac{1}{m}}
, we have

J_{0}^{u}=\frac{1}{t_{n}^{\frac{3}{m}}}J_{0}^{w},\quad J_{1}^{u}=\frac{1}{t_{n}^{\frac{2}{m}}}J_{1}^{w},\quad J_{2}^{u}=\frac{1}{t_{n}^{\frac{1}{m}}}J_{2}^{w},

where

J_{0}^{w}=\partial_{z}^{3}+4w_{0}\partial_{z}+2w_{0z},\quad J_{1}^{w}=4w_{1}\partial_{z}+2w_{1z},\quad J_{2}^{w}=-4\partial_{z}.

(18b)

The Hamiltonians have scaling
H_{\ell}^{u}(u_{0},u_{1})=\frac{1}{t_{n}^{\frac{\ell+1}{m}}}H_{\ell}^{w}(w_{0},w_{1})
. To fix
m
we consider the first component of (
17a
):

u_{0t_{n}}=J_{0}^{u}\delta_{u_{1}}H_{n}^{u}\quad\Rightarrow\quad-\,\frac{1}{mt_{n}^{\frac{2}{m}+1}}\,(zw_{0z}+2w_{0})=\frac{1}{t_{n}^{\frac{n+3}{m}}}\,J_{0}^{w}\delta_{w_{1}}H_{n}^{w}\quad\Rightarrow\quad m=n+1.

(18c)

Writing (
17a
) in terms of the scale invariants, we have

-\frac{1}{n+1}\,\left(\begin{array}[]{c}zw_{0z}+2w_{0}\\
(zw_{1})_{z}\end{array}\right)=B_{2}^{w}\delta_{w}H_{n}^{w}=B_{1}^{w}\delta_{w}H_{n+1}^{w}=B_{0}^{w}\delta_{w}H_{n+2}^{w}.

(19a)

Looking at the structure of the operators in (
17b
), we see that the
second
row of
B_{2}^{w}
cannot give the expression
(zw_{1})_{z}
, whilst the

row of
B_{0}^{w}
cannot give the expression

, so

of these Hamiltonian representations can be reduced to this space. However, the

representation

3.2
The Miura map and MDWW Hierarchy

Whilst there are two modifications of the DWW hierarchy, the operator

is only associated with the first of these, given by

(20b)

Again, there is an additional integral,

3.2.1
Reduction through Scaling Symmetry

The modification has scaling symmetry

S^{v}=x\partial_{x}+(n+1)t_{n}\partial_{t_{n}}-v_{0}\partial_{v_{0}}-v_{1}\partial_{v_{1}},\;\;\mbox{with invariants}\;\;z=\frac{x}{t_{n}^{\frac{1}{n+1}}},\;\;\theta_{0}=v_{0}t_{n}^{\frac{1}{n+1}},\;\;\theta_{1}=v_{1}t_{n}^{\frac{1}{n+1}}.

(21a)

Writing
v_{0}(x,t_{n})=\theta_{0}(z)t_{n}^{-\frac{1}{n+1}},\;v_{1}(x,t_{n})=\theta_{1}(z)t_{n}^{-\frac{1}{n+1}}

\partial_{z}\delta_{\theta_{0}}\left(H_{n+1}^{\theta}-\frac{z}{2(n+1)}\,\theta_{0}^{2}\right)=0,\quad\partial_{z}\delta_{\theta_{1}}\left(H_{n+1}^{\theta}+\frac{z}{8(n+1)}\,\theta_{1}^{2}\right)=0,

(21b)

giving the Lagrangian

(21c)

4
Conclusions

The purpose of this paper has been to show that the Painlevé reductions of the cKdV hierarchy fit neatly into the framework developed in
[
12
,
13
]
, with very little additional calculation.

In the two examples explained in this paper, just one of the Hamiltonian operators (of the PDE hierarchy) could be reduced to give a Lagrangian formulation for the Painlevé reduction. However,
all
the Poisson brackets of the stationary reduction are directly applicable the Painlevé case,
unchanged in structure
. In detail, there are differences, but these are hidden in the form of the Poisson commuting functions.

These calculations could easily be generalised to the cases of higher
N
(in (
1
)), as in
[
13
]
.

Klein-Gordon oscillator subject to a Coulomb-type potential in Bonnor-Melvin universe with a cosmological constant
L. G. Barbosa, C. C. Barros Jr

Abstract
In this work we study spin-0 particles described by the Klein-Gordon oscillator formalism in a spacetime which structure is determined by a homogeneous magnetic field and a cosmological constant. For this purpose we take into account a framework based on the Bonnor-Melvin solution with the inclusion of the cosmological constant. We write the Klein-Gordon equation and the effect of a scalar potential is also considered.

2
Klein-Gordon oscillator in Bonnor-Melvin-
\Lambda
universe

In this section we will study the Klein-Gordon oscillator in the background of the
Bonnor-Melvin with cosmological constant
spacetime. This metric is a static solution with cylindrical symmetry of Einstein’s equations, generated by a homogeneous magnetic field which,
in a cylindrical coordinate system, is given by
[
28
]

ds^{2}=-dt^{2}+d\rho^{2}+\sigma^{2}\sin^{2}\left(\sqrt{2\Lambda}\rho\right)d\varphi^{2}+dz^{2}

(1)

where
\Lambda
is the cosmological constant and
\sigma
is a constant of integration which
is related with the magnetic field by the relation

H(\rho)=\sqrt{\Lambda}\sigma\sin\left(\sqrt{2\Lambda}\rho\right)\ .

(2)

In the Minkowski spacetime, the scalar bosons are described by the Klein-Gordon equation which may be generalized to curved spaces by
[
30
]

\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\partial_{\mu}\left[g^{\mu\nu}\sqrt{-g}\partial_{\nu}\Psi\right]-m^{2}\Psi=0.

(3)

We can obtain the Klein-Gordon oscillator following a procedure similar to the minimal coupling performed in electromagnetism proposed by Bruce and Mining
[
31
]
and used in other works
[
2
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
18
]
by
changing the momentum operator

p_{\mu}\rightarrow\left(p_{\mu}+im\Omega X_{\mu}\right),

(4)

where
\Omega
is the oscillation frequency and
X_{\mu}=\left(0,\rho,0,0\right)
. We can also introduce a interaction described by a scalar potential
V(\rho)
in the equation by
a similar procedure and the final effect is the same one that would be obtained simply by modifying the mass terms
m\rightarrow m+V
. So that the equation (
3
) becomes

\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\left(\partial_{\mu}+m\Omega X_{\mu}\right)\left(g^{\mu\nu}\sqrt{-g}\left(\partial_{\mu}-m\Omega X_{\nu}\right)\Psi\right)-\left(m+V\right)^{2}\Psi.

(5)

Substituting the coefficients and the determinant of the metric, the equation describing a Klein-Gordon oscillator is given by

\begin{split}&\left[-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}+\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\rho^{2}}+\left(\frac{\sqrt{2\Lambda}}{\tan\left(\sqrt{2\Lambda}\rho\right)}+m\Omega\rho\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}+\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z^{2}}\right.\\
&\qquad\left.+\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}\sin^{2}\left(\sqrt{2\Lambda}\rho\right)}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\varphi^{2}}-\left(m\Omega+\left(m+V\right)^{2}+m^{2}\Omega^{2}\rho^{2}+\frac{\sqrt{2\Lambda}m\Omega\rho}{\tan\left(\sqrt{2\Lambda}\rho\right)}\right)\right]\Psi=0.\end{split}

(6)

It should be noted that equation (
6
) does not depend on
t
,
z
and
\varphi
, so that we can consider plane wave solutions for these components and it remains to determine the dynamics of the radial component of the
solution
R(\rho)
, that is given by

\Psi\left(t,\rho,\varphi,z\right)=e^{i\epsilon t}e^{il\varphi}e^{ip_{z}z}R\left(\rho\right),

(7)

where
\epsilon
,
l=0,\pm 1,\pm 2,\cdots
and
p_{z}
are respectively the quantum numbers associated with energy, angular momentum around
\varphi
and momentum in the
z
direction. Substituting (
7
) into (
6
) we obtain the radial equation for the Klein -Gordon oscillator in the space-time in question

\begin{split}&\frac{d^{2}R\left(\rho\right)}{d\rho^{2}}+\left(\frac{\sqrt{2\Lambda}}{\tan\left(\sqrt{2\Lambda}\rho\right)}+m\Omega\rho\right)\frac{dR\left(\rho\right)}{d\rho}\\
&\qquad+\left(\epsilon^{2}-p_{z}^{2}-\left(m+V\right)^{2}-m\Omega-\frac{l^{2}}{\sigma^{2}\sin^{2}\left(\sqrt{2\Lambda}\rho\right)}-m^{2}\Omega^{2}\rho^{2}-\frac{\sqrt{2\Lambda}m\Omega\rho}{\tan\left(\sqrt{2\Lambda}\rho\right)}\right)R\left(\rho\right)=0.\end{split}

(8)

Observing the fact that
the cosmological constant is very small, we can consider a first-order expansion in
\Lambda\rho
in the components of the radial equation obtaining a new equation that will provide solutions for eq. (
8
) with a high degree of accuracy. As an example we may study
the case in which the given potential is of Couloumb type
V(\rho)=\frac{\eta}{\rho}
, where
\eta
is the coupling parameter. Now the radial equation can be written as

\begin{split}&\frac{d^{2}R\left(\rho\right)}{d\rho^{2}}+\left(\frac{1}{\rho}+m\Omega\rho\right)\frac{dR\left(\rho\right)}{d\rho}\\
&\qquad+\left(\left(\zeta^{2}-2m\Omega\right)-m^{2}\Omega^{2}\rho^{2}-\left[\frac{l^{2}}{2\Lambda\sigma^{2}}+\eta^{2}\right]\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}-\frac{2m\eta}{\rho}\right)R\left(\rho\right)=0,\end{split}

(9)

where we define
\zeta^{2}=\epsilon^{2}-p_{z}^{2}-m^{2}
. To obtain the solution of eq. (
9
), we first take a transformation in the radial coordinate
s=\rho^{2}
, which results in

\begin{split}&\frac{d^{2}R\left(s\right)}{ds^{2}}+\left(\frac{1}{s}+\frac{m\Omega}{2}\right)\frac{dR\left(s\right)}{ds}\\
&\qquad+\left(\frac{\left(\zeta^{2}-2m\Omega\right)}{4s}-\frac{m^{2}\Omega^{2}}{4}+\frac{m\eta}{2s^{\frac{3}{2}}}-\left(\frac{l^{2}}{2\Lambda\sigma^{2}}+\eta^{2}\right)\frac{1}{4s^{2}}\right)R\left(s\right)=0.\end{split}

(10)

The normalizable eigenfunctions can be obtained using the following solution proposal

R\left(s\right)=s^{\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\eta^{2}+\frac{l^{2}}{2\Lambda\sigma^{2}}}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}m\Omega s}G\left(s\right)

(11)

which results in

\frac{d^{2}G\left(s\right)}{ds^{2}}+\left(-a+\frac{b}{s}\right)\frac{dG\left(s\right)}{ds}+\left(\frac{c}{s}+\frac{d}{s^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)G\left(s\right)=0,

(12)

in which we define

a=\frac{\Omega m}{2},\qquad b=\sqrt{\eta^{2}+\frac{l^{2}}{2\Lambda\sigma^{2}}}+1,\qquad c=\left[-\frac{\Omega m}{4}\sqrt{\eta^{2}+\frac{l^{2}}{2\Lambda\sigma^{2}}}+\frac{\zeta^{2}}{4}\right],\qquad d=\frac{\eta m}{2}.

(13)

Returning the variable change
s=\rho^{2}
and then taking a new transformation for the radial variable
\overline{\rho}=-\sqrt{a}\rho
we obtain the Biconfluent Heun equation

\frac{d^{2}G}{d\overline{\rho}^{2}}+\left(\frac{1+\alpha}{\overline{\rho}}-\beta-2\overline{\rho}\right)\frac{dG}{d\overline{\rho}}+\left\{\left(\gamma-\alpha-2\right)-\frac{1}{2}\left[\delta+\left(1+\alpha\right)\beta\right]\frac{1}{\overline{\rho}}\right\}G=0,

(14)

whose parameters are given by

\alpha=2b-2,\qquad\beta=0,\qquad\gamma=\frac{2ab+4c}{a},\qquad\delta=\frac{8d}{\sqrt{a}}\ ,

(15)

which in turn has the following general solution

G\left(\rho\right)=c_{1}\text{HeunB}\left(\alpha,0,\gamma,\delta,-\sqrt{a}\rho\right)+c_{2}\rho^{-2b+2}\text{HeunB}\left(-\alpha,0,\gamma,\delta,-\sqrt{a}\rho\right)

(16)

where
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
are arbitrary constants. Given the divergence presented at the origin of the second term, we take
c_{2}=0
. In order to determine the energy spectrum, we take a polynomial asymptotic form for small values of
\rho
of
the biconfluent Heun function and then we have

\gamma-\alpha-2=2n,

(17)

with
n
being a positive integer. Replacing the values of (
13
) and (
15
) in (
17
) and carrying out some manipulations we can write the energy spectrum

\epsilon=\sqrt{\Omega m\left(n+\sqrt{\eta^{2}+\frac{l^{2}}{2\Lambda\sigma^{2}}}\right)+m^{2}+p_{z}^{2}}.

(18)

that, as we can see, is influenced by the oscillation frequency
\Omega
, by the
scalar potential parameter
\eta
and by the intensity of the magnetic field

H_{0}=\sqrt{\Lambda}\sigma
. More energy levels may be found analysing the truncation of the power series for each value of
n
, in the same way it has been done in
[
32
]
,
[
33
]
. In Figures 1 and 2 we show typical values for these energy levels in terms of the parameters of the theory. As an example we used the
\pi^{0}
mass and a strong magnetic field,
H_{0}=10^{19}
G.

3
Conclusions

In this work we studied spin-0 oscillators in a background determined by a homogeneous magnetic field and the cosmological constant
\Lambda
. For this purpose the Klein-Gordon equation has been written in the Bonnor-Melvin with cosmological constant spacetime. We also introduced a scalar potential, then the equation has been solved and the energy levels
\epsilon
has been calculated. As it can be noticed in eq. (
18
) these levels depend on the proposed parameters, the oscillation frequency

\Omega
, the intensity of the magnetic field
H_{0}
as well as on the potential parameter
\eta
. As it would be expected, the wave functions are also determined by these parameters.

So, in accordance with many works that have been referred in the previous sections, the calculations presented in this work show that if we study quantum systems in arbitrary spacetimes their dynamics are determined by the structure of the background. Consequently it provides more insights about the relations between the general relativity and quantum mechanics.

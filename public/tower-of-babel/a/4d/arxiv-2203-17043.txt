Symmetric cohomology and symmetric Hochschild cohomology of cocommutative Hopf algebras
Yuta Shiba, Katsunori Sanada, Ayako Itaba

Abstract
. Staic defined symmetric cohomology of groups and studied that the secondary symmetric cohomology group is corresponding to group extensions and the injectivity of the canonical map from symmetric cohomology to classical cohomology. In this paper, we define symmetric cohomology and symmetric Hochschild cohomology for cocommutative Hopf algebras. The first one is a generalization of symmetric cohomology of groups. We give an isomorphism between symmetric cohomology and symmetric Hochschild cohomology, which is a symmetric version of the classical result about cohomology of groups by Eilenberg-MacLane and cohomology of Hopf algebras by Ginzburg-Kumar. Moreover, to consider the condition that symmetric cohomology coincides with classical cohomology, we investigate the projectivity of a resolution which gives symmetric cohomology.

2.2.
Symmetric cohomology of groups

In this subsection, we recall the definition
of the symmetric cohomology of groups

7

Let

G^{n}

For a

{\delta_{\mathrm{C}}^{n}}\colon{\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{}}^{n}({G},{X})}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow{\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{}}^{n+1}({G},{X})}

\displaystyle=-f(g_{1},\dots,g_{i-1}g_{i},g_{i}^{-1},g_{i}g_{i+1},\dots,g_{n}),\text{ for }2\leq i\leq n-1,

On the other hand,

There is a projective resolution of

\cdots\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}[G^{n+1}]\overset{d_{n}^{\mathrm{}}}{\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow}\mathbb{Z}[G^{n}]\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\cdots\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}[G^{2}]\overset{d_{1}^{\mathrm{}}}{\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow}\mathbb{Z}[G^{1}]\overset{d_{0}^{\mathrm{}}}{\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow}\mathbb{Z}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow 0,

where we set

Then

In this section, we recall the definition

3.1.
Definition of symmetric cohomology

Let us start with the definition of the Hopf algebra cohomology.

Definition 3.1

(cf.
[
11

)
.

Let

be a Hopf algebra and

-module.
The

\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}({A},{M})

of

We construct a standard non-homogeneous complex

Let

be a non-negative integer.
Suppose that, for any

\mathrm{\mathrm{T}}_{n}^{\mathrm{}}({A})=A^{\otimes n+1}
is a left

-module via

Then there is a projective resolution of

\cdots\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathrm{\mathrm{T}}_{n}^{\mathrm{}}({A})\overset{d_{n}^{\mathrm{T}}}{\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow}\mathrm{\mathrm{T}}_{n-1}^{\mathrm{}}({A})\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\cdots\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathrm{\mathrm{T}}_{1}^{\mathrm{}}({A})\overset{d_{1}^{\mathrm{T}}}{\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow}\mathrm{\mathrm{T}}_{0}^{\mathrm{}}({A})\overset{d_{0}^{\mathrm{T}}}{\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow}k\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow 0,

\displaystyle d_{0}^{\mathrm{T}}=\varepsilon,

\displaystyle d_{n}^{\mathrm{T}}(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n})=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{i}a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i}a_{i+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n}

\displaystyle\hskip 99.58464pt+(-1)^{n}a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n-1}\varepsilon(a_{n}),\text{ for }n\geq 1.

\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{}}^{n}({A},{M})=\mathrm{Hom}_{A}({\mathrm{\mathrm{T}}_{n}^{\mathrm{}}({A})},{M})

Let
A

Then we define the action of

by

(3.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}(\sigma_{i}f)(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n})=-f(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i-1}a_{i}^{(1)}\otimes S(a_{i}^{(2)})\otimes a_{i}^{(3)}a_{i+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n}),\\
\hskip 300.17657pt\text{ for }1\leq i\leq n-1,\\
(\sigma_{n}f)(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n})=-f(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n-1}a_{n}^{(1)}\otimes S(a_{n}^{(2)}))\end{cases}

) are well-defined

Proposition 3.2
.

The above formulas (

) define an action
of the symmetric group

on
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{}}^{n}({A},{M})

which is compatible with the differential
\delta_{\mathrm{C}}^{n}

Proof.

First, we check that, for

,
the action of
\sigma_{i}
satisfies relations of the Coxeter presentation
of
S_{n+1}
which is

\displaystyle S_{n+1}=\langle\sigma_{i},i\in\{1,2,\cdots,n\}\mid\,

\displaystyle\sigma_{i}^{2}=e\ \text{for}\ i\in\{1,\cdots,n\},

\displaystyle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_{i}=\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i+1}\ \text{for}\ i\in\{1,\cdots,n-1\},

\displaystyle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}=\sigma_{j}\sigma_{i}\ \text{for}\ |i-j|\geq 2\rangle.

Let
f\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{}}^{n}({A},{M})
,
a_{0},\dots,a_{n}\in A

and
i
an integer such that
1\leq i\leq n-1
.
For the left hand side of the second relation

\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_{i}=\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i+1}
,
we have

\displaystyle(\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_{i}f)(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n})

\displaystyle\quad=-(\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_{i}f)(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i-1}a_{i}^{(1)}\otimes S(a_{i}^{(2)})\otimes a_{i}^{(3)}a_{i+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n})

\displaystyle\quad=(\sigma_{i}f)(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i-1}a_{i}^{(1)}\otimes S(a_{i}^{(2)})a_{i}^{(3)}a_{i+1}^{(1)}\otimes S(a_{i}^{(4)}a_{i+1}^{(2)})\otimes a_{i}^{(5)}a_{i+1}^{(3)}a_{i+2}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n})

\displaystyle\quad=(\sigma_{i}f)(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i-1}a_{i}^{(1)}\otimes\varepsilon(a_{i}^{(2)})a_{i+1}^{(1)}\otimes S(a_{i+1}^{(2)})S(a_{i}^{(3)})\otimes a_{i}^{(4)}a_{i+1}^{(3)}a_{i+2}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n})

\displaystyle\quad=(\sigma_{i}f)(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i-1}a_{i}^{(1)}\otimes a_{i+1}^{(1)}\otimes S(a_{i+1}^{(2)})S(a_{i}^{(2)})\otimes a_{i}^{(3)}a_{i+1}^{(3)}a_{i+2}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n})

\displaystyle\quad=-f(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i-1}a_{i}^{(1)}a_{i+1}^{(1)}\otimes S(a_{i+1}^{(2)})\otimes a_{i+1}^{(3)}S(a_{i+1}^{(4)})S(a_{i}^{(2)})\otimes a_{i}^{(3)}a_{i+1}^{(5)}a_{i+2}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n})

\displaystyle\quad=-f(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i-1}a_{i}^{(1)}a_{i+1}^{(1)}\otimes S(a_{i+1}^{(2)})\otimes\varepsilon(a_{i+1}^{(3)})S(a_{i}^{(2)})\otimes a_{i}^{(3)}a_{i+1}^{(4)}a_{i+2}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n})

\displaystyle\quad=-f(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i-1}a_{i}^{(1)}a_{i+1}^{(1)}\otimes S(a_{i+1}^{(2)})\otimes S(a_{i}^{(2)})\otimes a_{i}^{(3)}\varepsilon(a_{i+1}^{(3)})a_{i+1}^{(4)}a_{i+2}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n})

\displaystyle\quad=-f(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i-1}a_{i}^{(1)}a_{i+1}^{(1)}\otimes S(a_{i+1}^{(2)})\otimes S(a_{i}^{(2)})\otimes a_{i}^{(3)}a_{i+1}^{(3)}a_{i+2}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n}).

On the other hand, for the right hand side, we have

\displaystyle(\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i+1}f)(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n})

\displaystyle\quad=-(\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i+1}f)(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i}a_{i+1}^{(1)}\otimes S(a_{i+1}^{(2)})\otimes a_{i+1}^{(3)}a_{i+2}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n})

\displaystyle\quad=(\sigma_{i+1}f)(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i-1}a_{i}^{(1)}a_{i+1}^{(1)}\otimes S(a_{i}^{(2)}a_{i+1}^{(2)})\otimes a_{i}^{(3)}a_{i+1}^{(3)}S(a_{i+1}^{(4)})\otimes a_{i+1}^{(5)}a_{i+2}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n})

\displaystyle\quad=(\sigma_{i+1}f)(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i-1}a_{i}^{(1)}a_{i+1}^{(1)}\otimes S(a_{i+1}^{(2)})S(a_{i}^{(2)})\otimes a_{i}^{(3)}\varepsilon(a_{i+1}^{(3)})\otimes a_{i+1}^{(4)}a_{i+2}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n})

\displaystyle\quad=(\sigma_{i+1}f)(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i-1}a_{i}^{(1)}a_{i+1}^{(1)}\otimes S(a_{i+1}^{(2)})S(a_{i}^{(2)})\otimes a_{i}^{(3)}\otimes a_{i+1}^{(3)}a_{i+2}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n})

\displaystyle\quad=-f(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i-1}a_{i}^{(1)}a_{i+1}^{(1)}\otimes S(a_{i+1}^{(2)})S(a_{i}^{(2)})a_{i}^{(3)}\otimes S(a_{i}^{(4)})\otimes a_{i}^{(5)}a_{i+1}^{(3)}a_{i+2}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n})

\displaystyle\quad=-f(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i-1}a_{i}^{(1)}a_{i+1}^{(1)}\otimes S(a_{i+1}^{(2)})\varepsilon(a_{i}^{(2)})\otimes S(a_{i}^{(3)})\otimes a_{i}^{(4)}a_{i+1}^{(3)}a_{i+2}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n})

\displaystyle\quad=-f(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i-1}a_{i}^{(1)}a_{i+1}^{(1)}\otimes S(a_{i+1}^{(2)})\otimes S(a_{i}^{(2)})\otimes a_{i}^{(3)}a_{i+1}^{(3)}a_{i+2}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n}).

Hence,
\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_{i}f=\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i+1}f
holds.
Similarly, the other relations can be checked by calculations.

Next, we show that this action is compatible
with the differential
\delta_{\mathrm{C}}^{n}
.

be invariant under the action of
S_{n+1}
and

\displaystyle\quad=-\delta_{\mathrm{C}}^{n}(f)(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i-1}a_{i}^{(1)}\otimes S(a_{i}^{(2)})\otimes a_{i}^{(3)}a_{i+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n+1})

\displaystyle\quad=-\Bigg\{\sum_{j=0}^{i-3}(-1)^{j}f(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{j}a_{j+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i-1}a_{i}^{(1)}\otimes S(a_{i}^{(2)})\otimes a_{i}^{(3)}a_{i+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n+1})

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\,+(-1)^{i-2}f(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i-2}a_{i-1}a_{i}^{(1)}\otimes S(a_{i}^{(2)})\otimes a_{i}^{(3)}a_{i+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n+1})

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\,+(-1)^{i-1}f(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i-1}a_{i}^{(1)}S(a_{i}^{(2)})\otimes a_{i}^{(3)}a_{i+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n+1})

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\,+(-1)^{i}f(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i-1}a_{i}^{(1)}\otimes S(a_{i}^{(2)})a_{i}^{(3)}a_{i+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n+1})

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\,+(-1)^{i+1}f(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i-1}a_{i}^{(1)}\otimes S(a_{i}^{(2)})\otimes a_{i}^{(3)}a_{i+1}a_{i+2}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n+1})

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\,+\sum_{j=i+2}^{n}(-1)^{j}f(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i-1}a_{i}^{(1)}\otimes S(a_{i}^{(2)})\otimes a_{i}^{(3)}a_{i+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{j}a_{j+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n+1})

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\,+(-1)^{n+1}f(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i-1}a_{i}^{(1)}\otimes S(a_{i}^{(2)})\otimes a_{i}^{(3)}a_{i+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n}\varepsilon(a_{n+1}))\Bigg\}.

\displaystyle(-1)^{i-1}f(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i-1}a_{i}^{(1)}S(a_{i}^{(2)})\otimes a_{i}^{(3)}a_{i+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n+1})

\displaystyle\quad=(-1)^{i-1}f(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i-1}\varepsilon(a_{i}^{(1)})\otimes a_{i}^{(2)}a_{i+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n+1})

\displaystyle\quad=(-1)^{i-1}f(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i-1}\otimes a_{i}a_{i+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n+1}),

\displaystyle(-1)^{i}f(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i-1}a_{i}^{(1)}\otimes S(a_{i}^{(2)})a_{i}^{(3)}a_{i+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n+1})

\displaystyle\quad=(-1)^{i}f(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i-1}a_{i}^{(1)}\otimes\varepsilon(a_{i}^{(2)})a_{i+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n+1})

\displaystyle\quad=(-1)^{i}f(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i-1}a_{i}\otimes a_{i+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n+1}).

Moreover, using that
f
is invariant under the action of
S_{n+1}
,
the first term is deformed by

\displaystyle f(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{j}a_{j+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i-1}a_{i}^{(1)}\otimes S(a_{i}^{(2)})\otimes a_{i}^{(3)}a_{i+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n+1})

\displaystyle\quad=-(\sigma_{i}f)(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{j}a_{j+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i-1}\otimes a_{i}\otimes a_{i+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n+1})

\displaystyle\quad=-f(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{j}a_{j+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i-1}\otimes a_{i}\otimes a_{i+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n+1}).

Hence, for

holds.
By same as the above calculations,
the same equation holds for other cases.
∎

Definition 3.3

-module.

symmetric cohomology

Remark 3.4

3.2.
Symmetric cohomology constructed by the homogeneous complex

Similar to group cohomology,
we can construct a homogeneous complex
which gives the Hopf algebra cohomology.
We describe its construction and an action of symmetric groups
on the homogeneous complex.
Let
A
be a Hopf algebra and
n
a non-negative integer.
Then
\mathrm{\mathrm{\widetilde{T}}}_{n}^{\mathrm{}}({A})=A^{\otimes n+1}

is a left
A
-module via

b\cdot(a_{0}\otimes a_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n})=b^{(1)}a_{0}\otimes b^{(2)}a_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes b^{(n+1)}a_{n}

for any
b,a_{0},\dots,a_{n}\in A
,
and there is a projective resolution of
k
as left
A
-modules:

\displaystyle\cdots\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathrm{\mathrm{\widetilde{T}}}_{n}^{\mathrm{}}({A})\overset{d_{n}^{\mathrm{\widetilde{T}}}}{\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow}\mathrm{\mathrm{\widetilde{T}}}_{n-1}^{\mathrm{}}({A})\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\cdots\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathrm{\mathrm{\widetilde{T}}}_{1}^{\mathrm{}}({A})\overset{d_{1}^{\mathrm{\widetilde{T}}}}{\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow}\mathrm{\mathrm{\widetilde{T}}}_{0}^{\mathrm{}}({A})\overset{d_{0}^{\mathrm{\widetilde{T}}}}{\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow}k\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow 0,

where we set

\displaystyle d_{0}^{\mathrm{\widetilde{T}}}=\varepsilon,

\displaystyle d_{n}^{\mathrm{\widetilde{T}}}(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n})=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}\varepsilon(a_{i})a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i-1}\otimes a_{i+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n},\text{ for }n\geq 1.

Moreover, we denote

\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{}}^{n}({A},{M})=\mathrm{Hom}_{A}({\mathrm{\mathrm{\widetilde{T}}}_{n}^{\mathrm{}}({A})},{M})

and
\delta_{\mathrm{K}}^{n}=\mathrm{Hom}_{A}({d_{n+1}^{\mathrm{\widetilde{T}}}},{M})
.
Next, we define an action of
S_{n+1}
on
\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{}}^{n}({A},{M})

for each
n\geq 0
.
Let
A
be a cocommutative Hopf algebra.
Then, for

,

and
1\leq i\leq n

we define the action of
\sigma_{i}
on
\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{}}^{n}({A},{M})
by

(3.2)

\displaystyle(\sigma_{i}\cdot f)(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n})=-f(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i}\otimes a_{i-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n}).

)

Proposition 3.5
.

The above formulas (
3.2
) define an action
of the symmetric group
S_{n+1}
on
\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{}}^{n}({A},{M})

which is compatible with the differential
\delta_{\mathrm{K}}^{n}
.

Proof.

First, we check that, for
1\leq i\leq n
,
the action of
\sigma_{i}
satisfies relations of the Coxeter presentation
of
S_{n+1}
.
Let
f\in\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{}}^{n}({A},{M})
,
a_{0},\dots,a_{n}\in A

and
i
an integer such that
1\leq i\leq n-1
.
For the second relation

\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_{i}=\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i+1}
,
we have

\displaystyle(\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_{i}f)(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n})

\displaystyle=-(\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_{i}f)(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i}\otimes a_{i-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n})

\displaystyle=(\sigma_{i}f)(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i}\otimes a_{i+1}\otimes a_{i-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n})

\displaystyle=-f(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i+1}\otimes a_{i}\otimes a_{i-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n})

\displaystyle=(\sigma_{i+1}f)(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i+1}\otimes a_{i-1}\otimes a_{i}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n})

\displaystyle=-(\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i+1}f)(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i-1}\otimes a_{i+1}\otimes a_{i}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n})

\displaystyle=(\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i+1}f)(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i-1}\otimes a_{i}\otimes a_{i+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n})

Hence,
\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_{i}=\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i+1}
holds.
Similarly, the other relations can be checked by calculations.

Next, we show that this action is compatible
with the differential
\delta_{\mathrm{K}}^{n}
.
Let
f\in\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{}}^{n}({A},{M})
be invariant under the action of
S_{n+1}
and
i
an integer
such that
3\leq i\leq n
.
Then we have

\displaystyle(\sigma_{i}\delta_{\mathrm{K}}^{n}(f))(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n+1})

\displaystyle\quad\,=-\delta_{\mathrm{K}}^{n}(f)(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i}\otimes a_{i-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n+1})

\displaystyle\quad\,=-\Bigg\{\sum_{j=0}^{i-3}(-1)^{j}f(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes\varepsilon(a_{j})a_{j+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i}\otimes a_{i-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n+1})

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\,+(-1)^{i-2}f(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes\varepsilon(a_{i-2})a_{i}\otimes a_{i-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n+1})

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\,+(-1)^{i-1}f(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes\varepsilon(a_{i})a_{i-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n+1})

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\,+(-1)^{i}f(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i}\otimes\varepsilon(a_{i-1})a_{i+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n+1})

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\,+\sum_{j=i+1}^{n+1}(-1)^{j}f(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i}\otimes a_{i-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\varepsilon(a_{j})a_{j+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n+1})\Bigg\}

\displaystyle\quad\,=-\sum_{j=0}^{i-3}(-1)^{j}f(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes\varepsilon(a_{j})a_{j+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i}\otimes a_{i-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n+1})

\displaystyle\quad\quad\,-(-1)^{i-2}f(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i}\otimes\varepsilon(a_{i-2})a_{i-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n+1})

\displaystyle\quad\quad\,-(-1)^{i}f(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes\varepsilon(a_{i-1})a_{i}\otimes a_{i+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n+1})

Here, using that

is invariant under the action of

By same as the above calculations,
the same equation holds for other cases.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
A
be a Hopf algebra and
M
a left
A
-module.
Then there is an isomorphism

\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{}}^{\bullet}({A},{M})\cong\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{}}^{\bullet}({A},{M})

as complexes.
Moreover, if
A
is cocommutative,
then this isomorphism induces an isomorphism

\mathrm{CS}_{\mathrm{}}^{\bullet}({A},{M})\cong\mathrm{KS}_{\mathrm{}}^{\bullet}({A},{M})

as complexes.
Therefore,
\mathrm{HS}^{n}({A},{M})=\mathrm{H}^{n}({\mathrm{KS}_{\mathrm{}}^{\bullet}({A},{M})})
holds.

Proof.

First, we will construct an isomorphism

\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{}}^{\bullet}({A},{M})\cong\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{}}^{\bullet}({A},{M})

as complexes.
For any
f\in\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{}}^{n}({A},{M})
and
a_{0},\dots,a_{n}\in A
, define the morphism

{\varphi^{\bullet}}\colon{\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{}}^{\bullet}({A},{M})}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow{\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{}}^{\bullet}({A},{M})}

by

\varphi^{n}(f)(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n})=f(a_{0}^{(1)}\otimes a_{0}^{(2)}a_{1}^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{0}^{(n+1)}a_{1}^{(n)}\cdots a_{n-1}^{(2)}a_{n}).

Then we show that
\varphi^{\bullet}
is compatible with the differential.
Namely, we show

\varphi^{n+1}\circ\delta_{\mathrm{k}}^{n}=\delta_{\mathrm{C}}^{n}\circ\varphi^{n}
.
For the left hand side, we have

\displaystyle(

\displaystyle\varphi^{n+1}\circ\delta_{\mathrm{K}}^{n})(f)(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n+1})

\displaystyle=\delta_{\mathrm{K}}^{n}(f)(a_{0}^{(1)}\otimes a_{0}^{(2)}a_{1}^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{0}^{(n+2)}a_{1}^{(n+1)}\cdots a_{n}^{(2)}a_{n+1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n+1}(-1)^{i}f(a_{0}^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes\varepsilon(a_{0}^{(i+1)}\cdots a_{i}^{(1)})a_{0}^{(i+2)}\cdots a_{i+1}^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{0}^{(n+2)}a_{1}^{(n+1)}\cdots a_{n}^{(2)}a_{n+1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}f(a_{0}^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes\varepsilon(a_{0}^{(i+1)})a_{0}^{(i+2)}\varepsilon(a_{1}^{(i)})a_{1}^{(i+1)}\cdots\varepsilon(a_{i}^{(1)})a_{i}^{(2)}a_{i+1}^{(1)}\otimes\cdots

\displaystyle\quad\,\otimes a_{0}^{(n+2)}a_{1}^{(n+1)}\cdots a_{n}^{(2)}a_{n+1})+(-1)^{n+1}f(a_{0}^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{0}^{(n+1)}a_{1}^{(n)}\cdots a_{n-1}^{(2)}a_{n}\varepsilon(a_{n+1}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}f(a_{0}^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{0}^{(i+1)}a_{1}^{(i)}\cdots a_{i-1}^{(2)}a_{i}^{(1)}a_{i+1}^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{0}^{(n+1)}a_{1}^{(n)}\cdots

\displaystyle\quad\,\times a_{i}^{(n-i+1)}a_{i+1}^{(n-i+1)}\cdots a_{n}^{(2)}a_{n+1})+(-1)^{n+1}f(a_{0}^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{0}^{(n+1)}a_{1}^{(n)}\cdots a_{n-1}^{(2)}a_{n}\varepsilon(a_{n+1})).

On the other hand, for the right hand side, we have

\displaystyle(

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathrm{C}}^{n}\circ\varphi^{n})(f)(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n+1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}\varphi^{n}(f)(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i}a_{i+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n+1})+(-1)^{n+1}\varphi^{n}(f)(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n}\varepsilon(a_{n+1}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}f(a_{0}^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{0}^{(i+1)}a_{1}^{(i)}\cdots a_{i-1}^{(2)}a_{i}^{(1)}a_{i+1}^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{0}^{(n+1)}a_{1}^{(n)}\cdots

\displaystyle\quad\,\times a_{i}^{(n-i+1)}a_{i+1}^{(n-i+1)}\cdots a_{n}^{(2)}a_{n+1})+(-1)^{n+1}f(a_{0}^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{0}^{(n+1)}a_{1}^{(n)}\cdots a_{n-1}^{(2)}a_{n}\varepsilon(a_{n+1})).

Hence,
\varphi^{n+1}\circ\delta_{\mathrm{K}}^{n}=\delta_{\mathrm{C}}^{n}\circ\varphi^{n}
holds.
While, for any
g\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{}}^{n}({A},{M})
and
a_{0},\dots,a_{n}\in A
,
we define the morphism

{\psi^{\bullet}}\colon{\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{}}^{\bullet}({A},{M})}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow{\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{}}^{\bullet}({A},{M})}
by

\psi^{n}(g)(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n})=g(a_{0}^{(1)}\otimes S(a_{0}^{(2)})a_{1}^{(1)}\otimes S(a_{1}^{(2)})a_{2}^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes S(a_{n-1}^{(2)})a_{n}).

Then we show that
\psi^{\bullet}
is the inverse of
\varphi^{\bullet}
.
For all
g\in\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{}}^{n}({A},{M})
, we have

\displaystyle(

\displaystyle\varphi^{n}\circ\psi^{n})(g)(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n})

holds.
On the other hand, for all

\displaystyle\quad=f(a_{0}^{(1)}\otimes\varepsilon(a_{0}^{(2)})a_{1}^{(1)}\otimes\varepsilon(a_{0}^{(3)})\varepsilon(a_{1}^{(2)})a_{2}^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes\varepsilon(a_{0}^{(n+1)})\cdots\varepsilon(a_{n-1}^{(2)})a_{n})

,

\displaystyle\quad\quad\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{0}^{(n+1)}\cdots a_{n-1}^{(2)}a_{n})

Definition 3.7

\mathrm{KS}_{\mathrm{e}}^{n}({A},{M})\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{A}({\mathrm{\mathrm{\widetilde{T}}}_{n}^{\mathrm{e}}({A})\otimes_{kS_{n+1}}k},{M})

as
k
-vector spaces, with the trivial left
kS_{n+1}
-module
k
.

Next, we define a resolution of
A
.
We take the
A
-bimodule sequence:

\cdots\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathrm{\widetilde{S}}_{n}^{\mathrm{e}}({A})\overset{d_{n}^{\mathrm{\widetilde{S}^{\mathrm{e}}}}}{\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow}\mathrm{\widetilde{S}}_{n-1}^{\mathrm{e}}({A})\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\cdots\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathrm{\widetilde{S}}_{1}^{\mathrm{e}}({A})\overset{d_{1}^{\mathrm{\widetilde{S}^{\mathrm{e}}}}}{\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow}\mathrm{\widetilde{S}}_{0}^{\mathrm{e}}({A})\overset{d_{0}^{\mathrm{\widetilde{S}^{\mathrm{e}}}}}{\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow}A\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow 0,

.
Then this sequence is a resolution of

as

-modules.

\mathrm{\widetilde{S}}_{n}^{\mathrm{e}}({A})\cong\mathrm{\widetilde{S}}_{n}^{\mathrm{}}({A})\otimes A

-bimodules where the right

-module structure
of

is trivial.

4.2.
The relationships between symmetric cohomology and symmetric Hochschild cohomology

In this subsection,
we show that there is an isomorphism
between symmetric cohomology and symmetric Hochschild cohomology
as
k
-vector spaces.
First, we describe the fact about group cohomology
by Eilenberg and MacLane.
Here, we recall that
{}^{\mathrm{ad}}X
is a left module
whose structure given by the left adjoint action.
According to the structure of the coproduct and the antipode
of group algebras,
{}^{\mathrm{ad}}X
is a left
G
-module
by
g\cdot x=gxg^{-1}
for
g\in G
and
x\in X
.

Theorem 4.3

(Eilenberg-MacLane
[
3
, Section 5]
)
.

Let
G
be a group and X a
G
-bimodule.
Then, for each
n\geq 0
, there is an isomorphism

\mathrm{HH}^{n}({\mathbb{Z}G},{X})\cong\mathrm{H}^{n}({G},{{}^{\mathrm{ad}}X})

as
\mathbb{Z}
-modules.

Remark 4.4
.

Theorem
4.3
is generalized
to the case of Hopf algebras
by Ginzburg-Kumar
[
4
, Subsection 5.6]
.

In this paper, we have the following result
which is a symmetric version of the above isomorphism.

Theorem 4.5
.

If
A
be a cocommutative Hopf algebra and
M
an
A
-bimodule.
Then, for each
n\geq 0
, there is an isomorphism

\mathrm{HHS}^{n}({A},{M})\cong\mathrm{HS}^{n}({A},{{}^{\mathrm{ad}}M})

as
k
-vector spaces.

Proof.

According to Lemma
2.4
,
we have the following isomorphisms

\displaystyle\mathrm{KS}_{\mathrm{e}}^{n}({A},{M})

\displaystyle\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{A^{e}}({\mathrm{\widetilde{S}}_{n}^{\mathrm{e}}({A})},{M})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Hom}_{A^{e}}({\mathrm{\widetilde{S}}_{n}^{\mathrm{}}({A})\otimes A},{M})\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{A^{e}}({\mathrm{\widetilde{S}}_{n}^{\mathrm{}}({A})},{\mathrm{Hom}_{k}({A},{M})}).

So, since
\mathrm{\widetilde{S}}_{n}^{\mathrm{}}({A})
is a trivial right
A
-module,
there is an isomorphism

\mathrm{Hom}_{A^{e}}({\mathrm{\widetilde{S}}_{n}^{\mathrm{}}({A})},{\mathrm{Hom}_{k}({A},{M})})\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{l\operatorname{\mathsf{\hskip-1.0pt-\hskip-1.0pt}}A}({\mathrm{\widetilde{S}}_{n}^{\mathrm{}}({A})},{\mathrm{Hom}_{k}({A},{M})^{A}}),

where
\mathrm{Hom}_{l\operatorname{\mathsf{\hskip-1.0pt-\hskip-1.0pt}}A}
(or
\mathrm{Hom}_{r\operatorname{\mathsf{\hskip-1.0pt-\hskip-1.0pt}}A}
) denotes a set of homomorphisms as a left (or right)
A
-module. While, there are isomorphisms of left
A
-modules

\mathrm{Hom}_{k}({A},{M})^{A}\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{r\operatorname{\mathsf{\hskip-1.0pt-\hskip-1.0pt}}A}({A},{M})\cong{}^{\mathrm{ad}}{M}.

Hence, we have

\mathrm{Hom}_{l\operatorname{\mathsf{\hskip-1.0pt-\hskip-1.0pt}}A}({\mathrm{\widetilde{S}}_{n}^{\mathrm{}}({A})},{\mathrm{Hom}_{k}({A},{M})^{A}})\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{l\operatorname{\mathsf{\hskip-1.0pt-\hskip-1.0pt}}A}({\mathrm{\widetilde{S}}_{n}^{\mathrm{}}({A})},{{}^{\mathrm{ad}}M})\cong\mathrm{KS}_{\mathrm{}}^{n}({A},{{}^{\mathrm{ad}}M})
.
∎

Moreover, we have the following assertion
from Theorem
4.5
.

Corollary 4.6
.

If
A
be a finite dimensional, commutative
and cocommutative Hopf algebra.
Then, for each
n\geq 0
, there is an isomorphism

\mathrm{HHS}^{n}({A},{A})\cong A\otimes\mathrm{HS}^{n}({A},{k})

as
k
-vector spaces.

Proof.

According to Theorem
4.5
,
there is an isomorphism

\mathrm{KS}_{\mathrm{e}}^{n}({A},{A})\cong\mathrm{KS}_{\mathrm{}}^{n}({A},{{}^{\mathrm{ad}}{A}})
.
Furthermore, by Lemma
2.3

and
2.5
, there are isomorphisms

\displaystyle\mathrm{KS}_{\mathrm{}}^{n}({A},{{}^{\mathrm{ad}}A})

\displaystyle\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{A}({\mathrm{\widetilde{S}}_{n}^{\mathrm{}}({A})},{{}^{\mathrm{ad}}A})

\displaystyle\cong{}^{A}\mathrm{Hom}_{k}({\mathrm{\widetilde{S}}_{n}^{\mathrm{}}({A})},{{}^{\mathrm{ad}}A})\cong{}^{A}({}^{\mathrm{ad}}A\otimes\mathrm{Hom}_{k}({\mathrm{\widetilde{S}}_{n}^{\mathrm{}}({A})},{k})).

Since
A
is commutative, we have

\displaystyle{}^{A}({}^{\mathrm{ad}}A\otimes\mathrm{Hom}_{k}({\mathrm{\widetilde{S}}_{n}^{\mathrm{}}({A})},{k}))

\displaystyle\cong A\otimes{}^{A}\mathrm{Hom}_{k}({\mathrm{\widetilde{S}}_{n}^{\mathrm{}}({A})},{k})

\displaystyle\cong A\otimes\mathrm{Hom}_{A}({\mathrm{\widetilde{S}}_{n}^{\mathrm{}}({A})},{k})\cong A\otimes\mathrm{KS}_{\mathrm{}}^{n}({A},{k}).

∎

Theorem 4.9
.

Let

be a cocommutative Hopf algebra.

\mathrm{\widetilde{S}}_{n}^{\mathrm{}}({A})

Proof.

First, we show that

{\phi}\colon{\mathrm{\widetilde{S}}_{n}^{\mathrm{}}({A})}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow{A\otimes\mathrm{\widetilde{S}}_{n-1}^{\mathrm{}}({A})}

\phi((a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n})\otimes_{kS_{n+1}}x)=\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}a_{i}\otimes((a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i-1}\otimes a_{i+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n})\otimes_{kS_{n}}x)

Then this morphism is well-defined.
Indeed, we define the morphism

We show that

The left hand side is

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime}((a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n})\cdot\sigma_{j},x)=\phi^{\prime}((-a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{j}\otimes a_{j-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n}),x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n+1}\left\{\sum_{i=0}^{j-2}(-1)^{i}a_{i}\otimes((-a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i-1}\otimes a_{i+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{j}\otimes a_{j-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n})\otimes_{kS_{n}}x)\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 36.98866pt+(-1)^{j-1}a_{j}\otimes((-a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{j-2}\otimes a_{j-1}\otimes a_{j+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n})\otimes_{kS_{n}}x)

\displaystyle\hskip 36.98866pt+(-1)^{j}a_{j-1}\otimes((-a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{j-2}\otimes a_{j}\otimes a_{j+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n})\otimes_{kS_{n}}x)

\displaystyle\hskip 36.98866pt+\left.\sum_{i=j+1}^{n}(-1)^{i}a_{i}\otimes((-a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{j}\otimes a_{j-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i-1}\otimes a_{i+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n})\otimes_{kS_{n}}x)\right\}.

is a trivial left

\displaystyle(-a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{j-1}\otimes a_{j+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{j}\otimes a_{j-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n})\otimes_{kS_{n}}x

\displaystyle\quad=(-a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{j-1}\otimes a_{j+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{j}\otimes a_{j-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n})\otimes_{kS_{n}}\sigma_{j-1}\cdot x

\displaystyle\quad=(-a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{j-1}\otimes a_{j+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{j}\otimes a_{j-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n})\cdot\sigma_{j-1}\otimes_{kS_{n}}x

\displaystyle\quad=(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{j-1}\otimes a_{j+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{j-1}\otimes a_{j}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n})\otimes_{kS_{n}}x.

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle(-a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{j}\otimes a_{j-1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i-1}\otimes a_{i+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n})\otimes_{kS_{n}}x

\phi
is well-defined.
Next, we show that

is an
A
-module homomorphism.
A direct calculation shows that, for all

and

\displaystyle\phi(a\cdot((a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n})\otimes_{kS_{n+1}}x))

\displaystyle\quad=\phi(a\cdot(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n})\otimes_{kS_{n+1}}x)

\displaystyle\quad=\phi((a^{(1)}a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a^{(n+1)}a_{n})\otimes_{kS_{n+1}}x)

is an
A
-module homomorphism.
Finally, we construct a retraction of

.
We define the morphism

{\psi}\colon{A\otimes\mathrm{\widetilde{S}}_{n-1}^{\mathrm{}}({A})}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow{\mathrm{\widetilde{S}}_{n}^{\mathrm{}}({A})}

4.4.
Example

In the last subsection, we describe an example
of the resolution which gives symmetric cohomology.
Let
p
be a odd prime number,

k
a field with characteristic
p
and
C_{p}
a cyclic group of order
p
.
Then we calculate the symmetric cohomology of
A=kC_{p}
.

Proposition 4.11
.

Let
p
be a odd prime number,
\mathrm{ch}({k})=p
and
A=kC_{p}
.
Then
\mathrm{\widetilde{S}}_{n}^{\mathrm{}}({A})
is a free
A
-module
with rank
\dfrac{{}_{p}C_{n+1}}{p}
for each
1\leq n\leq p-2
.

Proof.

Let any generator

(g_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes g_{n})\otimes_{kS_{n+1}}1

of
\mathrm{\widetilde{S}}_{n}^{\mathrm{}}({A})
as a left
A
-module.
There exists
0\leq a_{i}\leq p-1
such that
g_{i}
is represented
by
g^{a_{i}}
where
C_{p}=\langle g\mid g^{p}=e\rangle
.
We can change the order of tensor products
by an action of symmetric groups,
and hence we can assume
a_{0}<a_{1}<\cdots<a_{n}

without loss of generality.
First, we show

h\cdot((g_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes g_{n})\otimes_{kS_{n+1}}1)\neq\pm(g_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes g_{n})\otimes_{kS_{n+1}}1

for
(e\neq)h=g^{a}\in C_{p}
.
Assume that there exists

such that
h\cdot((g_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes g_{n})\otimes_{kS_{n+1}}1)=\pm(g_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes g_{n})\otimes_{kS_{n+1}}1
. Then there exists
i
such that

\displaystyle a_{j}\equiv\begin{cases}a_{i+j}+a&(i+j\leq n),\\
a_{i+j-(n+1)}+a&(i+j\geq n+1)\end{cases}

modulo
p
for
0\leq j\leq n
.
In particular,
a_{0}\equiv a_{i}+a\pmod{p}
and

\displaystyle a_{i}\equiv\begin{cases}a_{2i}+a&(2i\leq n),\\
a_{2i-(n+1)}+a&(2i\geq n+1)\end{cases}

hold. Hence, we have

\displaystyle a_{0}\equiv\begin{cases}a_{2i}+2a&(2i\leq n),\\
a_{2i-(n+1)}+2a&(2i\geq n+1).\end{cases}

Repeating this operation,
there exists
1\leq m\leq n+1
and
0\leq l\leq m

such that
mi-l(n+1)=0
, and hence

holds.
Moreover, since
a_{mi-l(n+1)}=a_{0}
, we have
ma\equiv 0\pmod{p}
.
This result contradicts
1\leq a\leq p-1

and
1\leq m\leq n+1\leq p-1
.
Therefore,

h\cdot((g_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes g_{n})\otimes_{kS_{n+1}}1)\neq\pm(g_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes g_{n})\otimes_{kS_{n+1}}1
for
(e\neq)h=g^{a}\in C_{p}
is obtained.

Next, we show that generators of
\mathrm{\widetilde{S}}_{n}^{\mathrm{}}({A})

as a left
A
-module are not torsion. Assume that

\displaystyle a\cdot((g_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes g_{n})\otimes_{kS_{n+1}}1)=0

for any
a\in A
. If
a=\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}x_{i}g^{i}
, then

\displaystyle a\cdot((g_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes g_{n})\otimes_{kS_{n+1}}1)=\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}x_{i}(g^{i}\cdot((g_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes g_{n})\otimes_{kS_{n+1}}1)).

By the above discussion,
each
g^{i}\cdot((g_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes g_{n})\otimes_{kS_{n+1}}1)

is linear independent.
So, this implies
a=0
, and hence generators are not torsion.

Finally, we construct a basis as an
A
-module from a basis
as a
k
-vector space.
Let
\mathcal{B}=\{b_{1},\dots,b_{{}_{p}C_{n+1}}\}

be a basis of
\mathrm{\widetilde{S}}_{n}^{\mathrm{}}({A})
as a
k
-vector space.
We put
\mathcal{B}_{(i)}
by remove
b_{i}
from
\mathcal{B}
,
if there exists
j
such that
b_{i}\in\mathrm{Orb}_{C_{p}}(b_{j})\cup\mathrm{Orb}_{C_{p}}(-b_{j})
,
where
\mathrm{Orb}_{C_{p}}(b)
is an orbit of
b
under the action of
C_{p}
.
Moreover, continue this operation to
\mathcal{B}_{(i)}

Abstract
. For the wavelet type orthonormal systems ϕ n \phi_{n} , we establish a new bound (0.1) ‖ max 1 ≤ m ≤ n ⁡ | ∑ j ∈ G m ⟨ f , ϕ j ⟩ ​ ϕ j | ‖ p ≲ log ⁡ ( n + 1 ) ⋅ ‖ f ‖ p , 1 < p < ∞ , \left\|\max_{1\leq m\leq n}\left|\sum_{j\in G_{m}}\langle f,\phi_{j}\rangle\phi_{j}\right|\right\|_{p}\lesssim\sqrt{\log(n+1)}\cdot\|f\|_{p},\quad 1<p<\infty, where G m ⊂ ℕ G_{m}\subset\mathbb{N} are arbitrary sets of indexes. Using this estimate, we prove that log ⁡ n \log n is an almost everywhere convergence Weyl multiplier for any orthonormal system of non-overlapping wavelet polynomials. It will also be remarked that log ⁡ n \log n is the optimal sequence in this context.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
An historical overview

Recall some definitions well-known in the theory of orthogonal series (see
[
11
]
).

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\Phi=\{\phi_{n}:\,n=1,2,\ldots\}\subset L^{2}(0,1)
be an orthonormal system. A sequence of positive numbers
\omega(n)\nearrow\infty
is said to be an a.e. convergence Weyl multiplier (C-multiplier) if every series

(1.1)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}\phi_{n}(x),

with coefficients satisfying the condition
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}^{2}w(n)<\infty
is a.e. convergent. If such series converges a.e. after any rearrangement of the terms, then we say
w(n)
is an a.e unconditional convergence Weyl multiplier (UC-multiplier) for
\Phi
.

The Menshov-Rademacher classical theorem (
[
12
]
,
[
18
]
, see also
[
11
]
or
[
6
]
) states that the sequence
\log^{2}n
is a C-multiplier for any orthonormal system. The sharpness of
\log^{2}n
in this theorem was proved by Menshov in the same paper
[
12
]
. That is any sequence
w(n)=o(\log^{2}n)
fails to be C-multiplier for some orthonormal system. The following inequality is the key ingredient in the proof of the Menshov-Rademacher theorem.

Theorem A

(Menshov-Rademacher,
[
12
]
,
[
18
]
)
.

If
\{\phi_{k}:\,\,k=1,2,\ldots,n\}\subset L^{2}(0,1)
is an orthogonal system, then

(1.2)

\left\|\max_{1\leq m\leq n}\left|\sum_{k=1}^{m}\phi_{k}\right|\,\right\|_{2}\leq c\cdot\log(n+1)\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\phi_{k}\right\|_{2},

where
c>0
is an absolute constant.

Similarly, the counterexample of Menshov is based on the following results.

Theorem B

(Menshov,
[
12
]
)
.

For any natural number
n\in\mathbb{N}
there exists an orthonormal system
\{\phi_{k}:\,k=1,2,\ldots,n\}\subset L^{2}(0,1)
, such that

(1.3)

\left|\left\{x\in(0,1):\,\max_{1\leq m\leq n}\left|\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\phi_{k}(x)\right|\geq c\log(n+1)\right\}\right|\gtrsim 1,

for an absolute constant
c>0
.

Theorem
B
shows that the factor
\log(n+1)
is optimal in inequality (
1.2
) when the class of all orthogonal systems is considered. However, if we narrow the class of
orthogonal systems, it is of interest to find the best constant in inequality (
1.2
) for the class under consideration.

For the further discussion, we need to introduce some notation.
The relation
a\lesssim b
(
a\gtrsim b
) will stand for the inequality
a\leq c\cdot b
(
a\geq c\cdot b
), where
c>0
is an absolute constant. Given two sequences of positive numbers
a_{n},b_{n}>0
, we write
a_{n}\sim b_{n}
if we have
c_{1}\cdot a_{n}\leq b_{n}\leq c_{2}\cdot a_{n}
,
n=1,2,\ldots
for some constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
. Throughout the paper, the base of
\log
is equal
2
.

Let
\Phi=\{\phi_{k}(x),\,k=1,2,\ldots\}\subset L^{\infty}(0,1)
be an infinite orthonormal system. Given
g\in L^{1}(0,1)
, consider the Fourier coefficients

a_{n}=\langle g,\phi_{n}\rangle=\int_{0}^{1}g\phi_{n}.

Let
f
be a
\Phi
-polynomial (i.e. a finite linear combination of functions of
\Phi
). Then we say
f
satisfies the relation
f\prec g
(with respect to the system
\Phi
), if
f=\sum_{n=1}^{m}\lambda_{n}a_{n}\phi_{n}
, where
|\lambda_{n}|\leq 1
.
Given orthonormal system
\Phi
and
1<p<\infty
we consider the numerical sequence

(1.4)

\mathcal{A}_{n}^{p}(\Phi)=\sup_{\|g\|_{p}\leq 1}\,\sup_{g_{k}\prec g,\,k=1,\ldots,n}\left\|\max_{1\leq k\leq n}|g_{k}(x)|\right\|_{p},

where the second
\sup
is taken over all sequences of polynomials
g_{k}
,
k=1,2,\ldots,n,
satisfying
g_{k}\prec g
.
One can consider in (
1.4
) only the monotonic sequences of polynomials

g_{k}=\sum_{j\in G_{k}}\langle g,\phi_{k}\rangle\phi_{j}\prec g,\quad k=1,2,\ldots,n,

where
G_{1}\subset G_{2}\subset\ldots\subset G_{n}\subset\mathbb{N}
,
\#G_{n}<\infty
. Then we will get another sequence
\mathcal{A}^{p}_{n,\text{mon}}(\Phi)
. If we additionally suppose that
each
G_{k+1}\setminus G_{k}
consists of a single integer, then we will have the sequence
\mathcal{A}^{p}_{n,\text{sng}}(\Phi)
. Clearly we have

(1.5)

\mathcal{A}^{p}_{n,\text{sng}}(\Phi)\leq\mathcal{A}^{p}_{n,\text{mon}}(\Phi)\leq\mathcal{A}^{p}_{n}(\Phi).

The case of
p=2
is special for (
1.4
). Theorem
A
implies
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{n,\text{mon}}(\Phi)\lesssim\log(n+1)
for every orthonormal system
\Phi
.
On the other hand, applying Theorem
B
, one can also construct an infinite orthonormal system with the lower bound
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{n,\text{sng}}(\Phi)\gtrsim\log(n+1)
,
n=1,2,\ldots
. Thus we conclude that for the general orthonormal systems the logarithmic upper bound of
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{n,\text{mon}}(\Phi)
is optimal. As it was remarked in
[
9
]
from some results of Nikishin-Ulyanov
[
15
]
and Olevskii
[
16
]
it follows that
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{n,\text{mon}}(\Phi)\gtrsim\sqrt{\log(n+1)}
for any complete orthonormal system
\Phi
.

The recent papers of one of the authors
[
9
,
10
,
8
]
highlight the relation of sequences (
1.5
) in the study of almost everywhere convergence of special orthogonal series. It was proved in
[
9
]
that

Theorem C
.

If
\Phi
is a martingale difference, then
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{n,\text{mon}}(\Phi)\lesssim\sqrt{\log(n+1)}
.

Theorem D
.

For any generalized Haar system
\mathcal{H}
we have the relation

(1.6)

\mathcal{A}^{2}_{n,\text{sng}}(\mathcal{H})\sim\mathcal{A}^{2}_{n,\text{mon}}(\mathcal{H})\sim\sqrt{\log(n+1)}.

The paper
[
9
]
also provides corollaries of these results like those that will be considered below.
In the case of trigonometric system
[
10
,
8
]
it was proved the following.

Theorem E
.

If
\mathcal{T}
is the trigonometric system, then we have

(1.7)

\mathcal{A}^{2}_{n,\text{sng}}(\mathcal{T})\sim\mathcal{A}^{2}_{n,\text{mon}}(\mathcal{T})\sim\log(n+1).

Note that the upper bound
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{n,\text{mon}}(\mathcal{T})\lesssim\log(n+1)
in (
1.7
) follows from the Menshov-Rademacher theorem. So the novelity here is the estimate
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{n,\text{sng}}(\mathcal{T})\gtrsim\log(n+1)
, which shows that the trigonometric system has no better estimate of the sequences
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{n}
than the general orthonormal systems have.

1.2.
The statement of the new results

In this paper we will consider orthonormal systems of wavelet type
\Phi=\{\phi_{k}(x):\,k=1,2,\ldots\}
defined on the interval
[0,1]
. For the precise definition of such systems we use the function

(1.8)

\xi(x)=\frac{1}{(1+|x|)^{1+\delta}},\quad 0<\delta<1,

as well as the notations

(1.9)

\displaystyle t_{1}=\frac{1}{2},\quad t_{k}=\frac{2j-1}{2^{n+1}},\quad k\geq 2,

(1.10)

\displaystyle\text{where }k=2^{n}+j,\,1\leq j\leq 2^{n},\,n=0,1,2,\ldots.

Hence we suppose

(1.11)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\phi_{k}(t)dt=0,\quad k\geq 1,

(1.12)

\displaystyle|\phi_{k}(x)|\leq c2^{n/2}\cdot\xi\big(2^{n}(x-t_{k})\big),

(1.13)

\displaystyle|\phi_{k}(t)-\phi_{k}(t^{\prime})|\leq c2^{n/2}(2^{n}|t-t^{\prime}|)^{\alpha}\cdot\xi\big(2^{n}(t-t_{k})\big)\text{ if }|t-t^{\prime}|\leq 2^{-n},

where
0<\alpha\leq 1
. Recall that such wavelet type systems in an other context were considered in
[
14
]
. Note that the Fourier series of any function
f\in L^{p}
,
1<p<\infty
, in a wavelet type system
converges in
L^{p}
unconditionally (see Remark
1.5
). This fact will be used in the statements of some results below. Besides, in the definition of
\mathcal{A}_{n}^{p}(\Phi)
instead of a
\Phi
-polynomial
f
we can equivalently consider
f\in L^{p}
.
Examples of wavelet type systems are considered in Remark
1.4
below.

From this moment the constants occurring in the relations
\lesssim
and
\sim
may depend on the constants form (
1.12
), (
1.13
) and the parameter
1<p<\infty
. The main result of the paper is the following sharp estimate.

Theorem 1.1
.

If an orthonormal system
\Phi
satisfies (
1.11
)-(
1.13
), then for any
1<p<\infty
it holds the relation

(1.14)

\mathcal{A}^{p}_{n}(\Phi)\lesssim\sqrt{\log(n+1)},\quad n=1,2,\ldots.

Note that in view of (
1.5
), a likewise bound holds also for the sequences
\mathcal{A}^{p}_{n,\text{mon}}(\Phi)
and
\mathcal{A}^{p}_{n,\text{sgn}}(\Phi)
. The following two corollaries show that for a wide class of wavelet type systems bound (
1.14
) is sharp. Namely,

Corollary 1.1
.

If an orthonormal system
\Phi
satisfies (
1.11
)-(
1.13
) and is a basis in
L^{2}
, then for any
1<p<\infty
we have

(1.15)

\mathcal{A}^{p}_{n,\text{mon}}(\Phi)\sim\mathcal{A}^{p}_{n}(\Phi)\sim\sqrt{\log(n+1)}.

Corollary 1.2
.

If
\mathcal{F}
is either Haar or Franklin system, then for any
1<p<\infty
we have

(1.16)

\mathcal{A}^{p}_{n,\text{sng}}(\mathcal{F})\sim\mathcal{A}^{p}_{n,\text{mon}}(\mathcal{F})\sim\mathcal{A}^{p}_{n}(\mathcal{F})\sim\sqrt{\log(n+1)}.

The following bound is an interesting phenomenon of the wavelet type systems and immediately follows from (
1.14
).

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
\Phi=\{\phi_{k}\}
be a wavelet type system and
G_{k}\subset\mathbb{N}
,
k=1,2,\ldots,n
be a arbitrary sets of indexes. Then for any function
f\in L^{p}
,
1<p<\infty
, we have

(1.17)

\left\|\max_{1\leq m\leq n}\left|\sum_{j\in G_{m}}\langle f,\phi_{j}\rangle\phi_{j}\right|\right\|_{p}\lesssim\sqrt{\log(n+1)}\cdot\|f\|_{p}.

In case of
p=2
Theorem
1.1
implies the following results concerning the C or UC multipliers for non-overlapping
\Phi
-polynomials.

Corollary 1.4
.

If
\Phi=\{\phi_{k}\}
is a wavelet type system, then the sequence
\log n
is a C-multiplier for any system of
L^{2}
-normalized non-overlapping
\Phi
-polynomials

(1.18)

p_{n}(x)=\sum_{j\in G_{n}}c_{j}\phi_{j}(x),\quad n=1,2,\ldots,

where
G_{n}\subset\mathbb{N}
are finite and pairwise disjoint.

The following particular case of Corollary
1.4
is also new and interesting even for the classical Franklin system.

Corollary 1.5
.

The sequence
\log n
is a C-multiplier for any rearrangement of a wavelet type system.

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
\Phi=\{\phi_{k}\}
be a wavelet type system and
\{p_{n}\}
be a sequence of
L^{2}
-normalized non-overlapping
\Phi
-polynomials. If
w(n)/\log(n+1)
is increasing and

(1.19)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{nw(n)}<\infty,

then
w(n)
is an UC-multiplier for
\{p_{n}\}
.

The only prior result in the context of Wail multipliers, concerning to non Haar wavelet type systems, is due to Gevorkyan
[
2
]
, who proved

Theorem F

(Gevorkyan,
[
2
]
)
.

The sequence
w(n)\nearrow
is an UC-multiplier for the Franklin system if and only if it satisfies (
1.19
).

Similarly, as in the proofs of Theorem

Remark 1.4
.

Examples of wavelet type systems are the classical wavelets, orthonormal spline systems on

, where an interesting case is the Franklin system. All the results of the paper can be applied also to the periodic wavelet systems, since those can be split into a union of two wavelet type systems. With a slight change in the proofs one can also establish the results for the biorthogonal systems

satisfy the conditions (

Finally, observe that the Haar system doesn’t satisfy (

. Indeed, let
f=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}b_{j}h_{j}\in L^{p}
and

,

. Instead of (

) one needs to consider the function

\mathcal{P}(x)=\sup_{1\leq k\leq n}S(p_{k})(x)\leq S\left(f\right)(x),

where

Remark 1.5
.

Observe that the wavelet type systems share the following property of unconditional bases of
L^{p}
: for any sequence
\lambda=\{\lambda_{k}\}
,
|\lambda_{k}|\leq 1
, and a function
f\in L^{p}
,
1<p<\infty
, the series
T_{\lambda}(f)=\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}\langle f,\phi_{k}\rangle\phi_{k}
converges in

and
\left\|T_{\lambda}(f)\right\|_{p}\lesssim\|f\|_{p}
.
This property can be established by a well-known argument (see for example
[

), proving that

is a Calderón-Zygmund kernel, namely

(1.20)

\displaystyle\left|K_{\lambda}(x,t)\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{|x-t|},

(1.21)

\displaystyle|K_{\lambda}(x,t)-K_{\lambda}(x,t^{\prime})|\lesssim\frac{|t-t^{\prime}|^{\beta}}{|x-t|^{1+\beta}},\quad|x-t|>2|t-t^{\prime}|,

where
0<\beta<\min\{\alpha,\delta\}
. Concerning to unconditional wavelet bases in
L^{p}
,
1<p<\infty
, we can also refer the readers to the papers
[
13
,
21
,
4
,
22
]
.

Remark 1.6
.

Suppose
T_{k}
,

, is a collection of Calderón-Zygmund operators with kernels, satisfying the standard conditions (

) and (

2.
Notations and auxiliary estimates

Recall the definition of
L^{2}
-normalized Haar system
h_{k}(x)
,
k=1,2,\ldots
, on
[0,1]
. That is
h_{1}(x)=1
and for
k\geq 2
of the form (
1.10
) we have

\displaystyle h_{k}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rrll}&2^{n/2}&\hbox{ if }&x\in\left[{\frac{j-1}{2^{n}}},\frac{2j-1}{2^{n+1}}\right),\\
&-2^{n/2}&\hbox{ if }&x\in\left[\frac{2j-1}{2^{n+1}},\frac{j}{2^{n}}\right),\\
&0&\hbox{ otherwise. }&\end{array}\right.

Given
1<q<\infty
define the maximal function

\mathcal{M}_{q}(f)(x)=\sup_{I:\,I\supset x}\left(\frac{1}{|I|}\int_{I}|f|^{q}\right)^{1/q},\quad f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T}),

where
\sup
is taken over all the open intervals
I\subset[0,1)
containing the point
x
. The case of
q=1
, which is the classical Hardy-Littlewood maximal function, will be simply denoted by
\mathcal{M}
.
Recall the Fefferman-Stein vector valued maximal inequality (see
[
19
]
, chap. 2)

(2.4)

\left\|\left(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}|\mathcal{M}_{q}\left(g_{k}\right)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{p}\lesssim\left\|\left(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}|g_{k}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{p},

where
1<p<\infty
,
1\leq q<\min\{2,p\}
. In this section we will establish standard estimates to be used in the proof of the main lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

If
\{\phi_{n}\}
is a wavelet type system, then for any coefficients
a_{k}
there hold the bounds

(2.5)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=2^{m-1}+1}^{2^{m}}|a_{k}\phi_{k}(x)|\lesssim\mathcal{M}\left(\sum_{k=2^{m-1}+1}^{2^{m}}a_{k}h_{k}\right)(x),

(2.6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}\left(\sum_{k=2^{m-1}+1}^{2^{m}}a_{k}h_{k}\right)(x)\lesssim\mathcal{M}\left(\sum_{k=2^{m-1}+1}^{2^{m}}|a_{k}\phi_{k}|\right)(x).

Proof.

Using (
1.12
) and a standard argument, we have

(2.7)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=2^{m-1}+1}^{2^{m}}|a_{k}\phi_{k}(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{m/2}\sum_{k=2^{m-1}+1}^{2^{m}}|a_{k}|\xi\big(2^{m}(x-t_{k})\big)

(2.8)

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{m}\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{k=2^{m-1}+1}^{2^{m}}|a_{k}||h_{k}(t)|\xi\big(2^{m}|x-t|\big)dt

(2.9)

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathcal{M}\left(\sum_{k=2^{m-1}+1}^{2^{m}}a_{k}h_{k}\right)(x)

that gives (
2.5
). To prove (
2.6
) we use (
1.13
) and the normality condition
\|\phi_{k}\|_{2}=1
. So one can fix a number
a>0
, depending on the constant in (
1.12
), such that

(2.10)

\int_{t_{k}-a/2^{m}}^{t_{k}+a/2^{m}}|\phi_{k}(t)|^{2}dx\geq\frac{1}{2},\quad 2^{m-1}<k<2^{m}.

Given
\varepsilon>0
consider the set

E_{k}=\{x\in(t_{k}-a/2^{m},t_{k}+a/2^{m}):\,|\phi_{k}(x)|>\varepsilon 2^{m/2}\}.

From (
2.10
) and (
1.12
) we obtain

\frac{1}{2}\leq c^{2}2^{m}|E_{k}|+\varepsilon^{2}2^{m}(2a/2^{m}-|E_{k}|),

that implies
|E_{k}|\gtrsim 1/n
for a small enough
\varepsilon
independent on
k
. Thus one can easily get

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}\left(\sum_{k=2^{m-1}+1}^{2^{m}}a_{k}h_{k}\right)(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathcal{M}\left(\sum_{k=2^{m-1}+1}^{2^{m}}2^{m/2}|a_{k}|\textbf{1}_{E_{k}}\right)(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathcal{M}\left(\sum_{k=2^{m-1}+1}^{2^{m}}|a_{k}||\phi_{k}|\right)(x)

and so (
2.6
).
∎

The following lemma is a version of Littlewood-Paley inequality.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\{\phi_{n}\}
be a wavelet type system and
1<p<\infty
. Then for any function
f\in L^{p}
it holds the inequality

(2.11)

\left\|\left(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{k=2^{m-1}+1}^{2^{m}}|\langle f,\phi_{k}\rangle\phi_{k}|\right)^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{p}\lesssim\|f\|_{p}.

Proof.

Denote
a_{k}=\langle f,\phi_{k}\rangle
and let
r_{k}(x)
be the Rademacher system. According to Remark
1.5
and Khintchin’s inequality we have

(2.12)

\left\|\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{k}^{2}\phi_{k}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{p}^{p}\sim\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\left|\sum_{k}r_{k}(t)a_{k}\phi_{k}(x)\right|^{p}dxdt\lesssim\|f\|_{p}^{p}.

which is the classical version of the Littlewood-Paley inequality.
Then, by (
2.6
) in particular we have
|h_{k}(t)|\lesssim\mathcal{M}(\phi_{k})(t)
. Thus, from (
2.5
), (
2.4
) with
q=1
and (
2.12
), we obtain

(2.13)

\displaystyle\left\|\left[\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{k=2^{m-1}+1}^{2^{m}}|a_{k}\phi_{k}|\right)^{2}\right]^{1/2}\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\left[\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{M}\left(\sum_{k=2^{m-1}+1}^{2^{m}}a_{k}h_{k}\right)^{2}\right]^{1/2}\right\|_{p}

(2.14)

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\left(\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}a_{k}^{2}h_{k}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{p}\lesssim\left\|\left[\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}(\mathcal{M}(a_{k}\phi_{k}))^{2}\right]^{1/2}\right\|_{p}

(2.15)

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\left(\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}a_{k}^{2}\phi_{k}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{p}\lesssim\|f\|_{p}.

∎

3.
The main lemma

Let
\Phi=\{\phi_{k}(x):\,k=1,2,\ldots\}
be our wavelet type system, i.e. it satisfies (
1.11
), (
1.12
) and (
1.13
). For
f\in L^{1}(0,1)
, we consider the Fourier partial sums

(3.1)

\displaystyle\Phi_{n}(f)(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{2^{n}}\langle f,\phi_{j}\rangle\phi_{j}(x),\quad n=0,1,2,\ldots,

(3.2)

\displaystyle\Delta\Phi_{n}(f)(x)=\sum_{j=2^{n-1}+1}^{2^{n}}\langle f,\phi_{j}\rangle\phi_{j}(x),\quad n\geq 1.

The Haar system analogues of such sums will be denoted by

(3.3)

H_{n}(f)(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{2^{n}}\langle f,h_{j}\rangle h_{j}(x),\quad\Delta H_{n}(f)(x)=\sum_{j=2^{n-1}+1}^{2^{n}}\langle f,h_{j}\rangle h_{j}(x).

We denote by
I_{n}(x)
the dyadic interval of the form
[(j-1)/2^{n},j/2^{n})
,
containing a point
x\in[0,1)
. For the Haar partial sum (
3.3
) we have

(3.4)

H_{n}(f)(x)=\frac{1}{|I_{n}(x)|}\int_{I_{n}(x)}f,\quad x\in[0,1)

(see
[
11
]
, chap. 3). The following standard bound

(3.5)

\displaystyle\sum_{2^{n-1}<k\leq 2^{n}}\left|\phi_{k}(x)\phi_{k}(t)\right|\lesssim 2^{n}\xi\big(2^{n}(x-t)\big)

one can find for example in
[
5
]
(see chap. 5, Lemma 3.12).

Lemma 3.1
.

For any interval
I=[a,b)\subset[0,1)
we have

(3.6)

\displaystyle|\Delta\Phi_{m}(\textbf{1}_{I})(x)|\lesssim\frac{1}{(1+2^{m}|x-a|)^{\delta}}+\frac{1}{(1+2^{m}|x-b|)^{\delta}},\quad x\in[0,1),

(3.7)

\displaystyle|\Delta\Phi_{m}(\textbf{1}_{I})(x)|\lesssim 2^{m}|I|\xi(2^{m}(x-a)),\quad x\in[0,1]\setminus 2I.

Proof.

To prove (
3.6
) first consider the case
x\in[0,1)\setminus I
and suppose that
0\leq x<a
. Using (
3.5
), we obtain

(3.8)

\displaystyle|\Delta\Phi_{m}(\textbf{1}_{I})(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{a}^{b}\sum_{2^{m-1}<k\leq 2^{m}}|\phi_{k}(x)\phi_{k}(t)|dt\lesssim\int_{a}^{b}\frac{2^{m}}{(1+2^{m}|x-t|)^{1+\delta}}dt

(3.9)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k\geq 2^{m}|x-a|}\frac{1}{k^{1+\delta}}\lesssim\frac{1}{(1+2^{m}|x-a|)^{\delta}}.

In the case
x\geq b
we will have the bound
\lesssim\frac{1}{(1+2^{m}|x-b|)^{\delta}}
. If
x\in I
, then by (
1.11
) we can write

\Delta\Phi_{m}(\textbf{1}_{I})(x)=\Delta\Phi_{m}(\textbf{1}_{[0,1)\setminus I})(x),

and (
3.6
) can be obtained similarly. Then, under the condition
x\in[0,1]\setminus 2I
we have

\displaystyle|\Delta\Phi_{m}(\textbf{1}_{I})(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{I}\frac{2^{m}}{(1+2^{m}|x-t|)^{1+\delta}}dt

\displaystyle\leq|I|\cdot\max_{t\in I}\frac{2^{m}}{(1+2^{m}|x-t|)^{1+\delta}}\lesssim 2^{m}|I|\xi(2^{m}(x-a))

that gives us (
3.7
).
∎

For a function
f(t)
defined on a set
E
we denote

{\rm OSC}_{E}(f)=\sup_{x,t\in E}|f(x)-f(t)|.

Lemma 3.2
.

If
g\in L^{1}(0,1)
, then for any integers
n=m\geq 1
it holds the inequality

(3.10)

|\Delta H_{n}(\Delta\Phi_{m}(g))(x)|\lesssim 2^{\alpha(m-n)}\cdot\mathcal{M}\left(\sum_{k=2^{m-1}+1}^{2^{m}}|\langle g,\phi_{k}\rangle||\phi_{k}|\right)(x),\quad x\in[0,1).

Proof.

The case of
n=m
is trivial. So we suppose
n>m
and denote
a_{k}=\langle g,\phi_{k}\rangle
. First using (
1.13
) then (
2.6
), we obtain

(3.11)

\displaystyle|\Delta H_{n}(\Delta\Phi_{m}(g))(x)|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{1}{|I_{n-1}(x)|}\int_{I_{n-1}(x)}\Delta\Phi_{m}(g)-\frac{1}{|I_{n}(x)|}\int_{I_{n}(x)}\Delta\Phi_{m}(g)\right|

(3.12)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=2^{m-1}+1}^{2^{m}}|a_{k}|{\rm OSC}_{I_{n-1}(x)}(\phi_{k})

(3.13)

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{m/2}\cdot 2^{\alpha(m-n)}\sum_{k=2^{m-1}+1}^{2^{m}}|a_{k}|\xi(2^{m}(x-t_{k}))

(3.14)

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{\alpha(m-n)}\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{k=2^{m-1}+1}^{2^{m}}|a_{k}||h_{k}(t)|\cdot 2^{m}\xi(2^{m}(x-t))dt

(3.15)

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{\alpha(m-n)}\cdot\mathcal{M}\left(\sum_{k=2^{m-1}+1}^{2^{m}}a_{k}h_{k}\right)(x)

(3.16)

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{\alpha(m-n)}\cdot\mathcal{M}\left(\sum_{k=2^{m-1}+1}^{2^{m}}|a_{k}||\phi_{k}|\right)(x),

and lemma is proved.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

If
f\in L^{1}(0,1)
and
m\geq n\geq 1
, then for any
q>1
with
q^{\prime}=q/(q-1)>\delta^{-1}
we have

(3.17)

|H_{n}(\Delta\Phi_{m}(f))(x)|\lesssim 2^{(n-m)/q^{\prime}}\mathcal{M}_{q}(\Delta\Phi_{m}(f))(x),\quad x\in[0,1).

Proof.

Let
x\in[0,1)
. By the orthogonality of
\{\phi_{n}\}
we have
\Delta\Phi_{m}(f)=\Delta\Phi_{m}(\Delta\Phi_{m}(f))
. Let
J_{0}=I_{n}(x)
,
J_{k}=2^{k}I_{n}(x)\cap[0,1]
,
k=1,2,\ldots
.
Thus, using a duality argument, we can write

(3.18)

\displaystyle\left|H_{n}(\Delta\Phi_{m}(f))(x)\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|I_{n}(x)|}\left|\int_{I_{n}(x)}\Delta\Phi_{m}(f)(u)du\right|

(3.19)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|I_{n}(x)|}\left|\int_{I_{n}(x)}\Delta\Phi_{m}(\Delta\Phi_{m}(f))(u)du\right|

(3.20)

\displaystyle=2^{n}\left|\int_{0}^{1}\Delta\Phi_{m}(\textbf{1}_{I_{n}(x)})(u)\Delta\Phi_{m}(f)(u)du\right|

(3.21)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{n}\left|\int_{J_{0}}\Delta\Phi_{m}(\textbf{1}_{I_{n}(x)})(u)\Delta\Phi_{m}(f)(u)du\right|

(3.22)

\displaystyle\quad+2^{n}\sum_{k\geq 1}\left|\int_{J_{k}\setminus J_{k-1}}\Delta\Phi_{m}(\textbf{1}_{I_{n}(x)})(u)\Delta\Phi_{m}(f)(u)du\right|.

Thus using (
3.6
) for the first integral, we obtain

(3.23)

\displaystyle 2^{n}

\displaystyle\left|\int_{J_{0}}\Delta\Phi_{m}(\textbf{1}_{I_{n}(x)})(u)\Delta\Phi_{m}(f)(u)du\right|

(3.24)

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim{2^{n}}\left(\int_{J_{0}}|\Delta\Phi_{m}(f)(u)|^{q}du\right)^{1/q}\cdot\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{dt}{(1+2^{m}|t|)^{q^{\prime}\delta}}\right)^{1/q^{\prime}}

(3.25)

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim 2^{(n-m)/q^{\prime}}\mathcal{M}_{q}(\Delta\Phi_{m}(f))(x).

From (
3.7
) it follows that

|\Delta\Phi_{m}(\textbf{1}_{I_{n}(x)})(u)|\lesssim 2^{m-n}\left(\frac{2^{n-m}}{2^{k}}\right)^{1+\delta}=\frac{2^{\delta(n-m)}}{2^{k(1+\delta)}},\quad u\in J_{k}\setminus J_{k-1},

and so

(3.26)

\displaystyle 2^{n}

\displaystyle\left|\int_{J_{k}\setminus J_{k-1}}\Delta\Phi_{m}(\textbf{1}_{I_{n}(x)})(u)\Delta\Phi_{m}(f)(u)du\right|

(3.27)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\lesssim 2^{n}\frac{2^{\delta(n-m)}}{2^{k(1+\delta)}}\left|\int_{J_{k}}|\Delta\Phi_{m}(f)(u)|du\right|

(3.28)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\lesssim 2^{n}\frac{2^{\delta(n-m)}}{2^{k(1+\delta)}}|J_{k}|\mathcal{M}(\Delta\Phi_{m}(f))(x)

(3.29)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq\frac{2^{\delta(n-m)}}{2^{k\delta}}\mathcal{M}(\Delta\Phi_{m}(f))(x).

Hence, combining (
3.22
), (
3.25
) and (
3.29
), we get (
3.17
).
∎

Next, we will need a well-known discrete convolution inequality

(3.30)

\left(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|a_{k}b_{n-k}|\right)^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq\left(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{k}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\cdot\left(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|b_{k}|\right).

Also, recall the definition of the Haar square function

S(f)(x)=\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|\langle f,h_{k}\rangle|^{2}h_{k}^{2}(x)\right)^{1/2}.

Lemma 3.4

(main)
.

If
f\in L^{p}
,
1<p<\infty
, then

(3.31)

\left\|\sup_{|\lambda_{k}|\leq 1}S\left(\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}a_{k}\phi_{k}\right)\right\|_{p}\lesssim\|f\|_{p},

where the
\sup
is taken over all the sequences
\lambda=\{\lambda_{k}\}
with
|\lambda_{k}|\leq 1
.

Proof.

Clearly, we can suppose that
a_{1}=0
and
0<\delta<1
. For a given sequence
\lambda=\{\lambda_{k}:\,|\lambda_{k}|\leq 1\}
, we denote

(3.32)

\displaystyle f_{\lambda}=\sum_{k\geq 1}\lambda_{k}a_{k}\phi_{k}=\sum_{k\geq 2}\lambda_{k}a_{k}\phi_{k}.

Chose a number
q=\min\{(p+1)/2,(1-\delta/2)^{-1},3/2\}
, which is depended only on
p
and
\delta
. Combining Lemma
3.2
, Lemma
3.3
, and inequality (
3.30
), we can write

(3.33)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left|\Delta H_{n}\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}\lambda_{k}a_{k}\phi_{k}\right)(x)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left[\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}|\Delta H_{n}(\Delta\Phi_{m}(f_{\lambda}))(x)|\right]^{2}

(3.34)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left[\sum_{m=n+1}^{\infty}2^{(n-m)/q^{\prime}}\mathcal{M}_{q}(\Delta\Phi_{m}(f_{\lambda}))(x)\right]^{2}

(3.35)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left[\sum_{m=1}^{n}2^{\alpha(m-n)}\big(\mathcal{M}(\Delta\Phi_{m}(f_{\lambda}))(x)\big)\right]^{2}

(3.36)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left[\sum_{m=n+1}^{\infty}2^{(n-m)/q^{\prime}}\mathcal{M}_{q}\left(\sum_{k=2^{m-1}+1}^{2^{m}}|a_{k}\phi_{k}|\right)(x)\right]^{2}

(3.37)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left[\sum_{m=1}^{n}2^{\alpha(m-n)}\mathcal{M}\left(\sum_{k=2^{m-1}+1}^{2^{m}}|a_{k}\phi_{k}|\right)(x)\right]^{2}

(3.38)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\left[\mathcal{M}_{q}\left(\sum_{k=2^{m-1}+1}^{2^{m}}|a_{k}\phi_{k}|\right)(x)\right]^{2}.

Thus we get the pointwise estimate

\sup_{|\lambda_{k}|\leq 1}S\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}\lambda_{k}a_{k}\phi_{k}\right)(x)\lesssim\left[\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\left(\mathcal{M}_{q}\left(\sum_{k=2^{m-1}+1}^{2^{m}}|a_{k}\phi_{k}|\right)(x)\right)^{2}\right]^{1/2}.

Then, using (
2.4
) and (
2.11
), we obtain

\left\|\sup_{|\lambda_{k}|\leq 1}S\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}\lambda_{k}a_{k}\phi_{k}\right)\right\|_{p}\lesssim\left\|\left[\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{k=2^{m-1}+1}^{2^{m}}|a_{k}\phi_{k}|\right)^{2}\right]^{1/2}\right\|_{p}\lesssim\|f\|_{p}.

Lemma is proved.
∎

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.1
and Corollaries
1.1
and
1.2

A key argument in the proof of Theorem
1.1
is the following good-
\lambda
inequality due to Chang-Wilson-Wolff (see
[
1
]
, Corollary 3.1):

(4.1)

|\{x\in[0,1):\,\mathcal{M}^{d}(f)(x)>\lambda,\,Sf(x)<\varepsilon\lambda\}|\\
\lesssim\exp\left(-\frac{c}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right)|\{\mathcal{M}^{d}(f)(x)>\lambda/2\}|,\,\lambda>0,\,0<\varepsilon<1,

where
\mathcal{M}^{d}
denotes the dyadic maximal function

\mathcal{M}^{d}(f)(x)=\sup_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{|I_{n}(x)|}\int_{I_{n}(x)}|f|.

Let
f\in L^{p}(0,1)
,
1<p<\infty
and
a_{k}=\langle f,\phi_{k}\rangle
. Suppose the functions
p_{k}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{T})
,
k=1,2,\ldots,n
satisfy
p_{k}\prec f
with respect to our wavelet type system
\Phi
. It is clear that

(4.2)

\mathcal{P}(x)=\sup_{1\leq k\leq n}S(p_{k})(x)\leq\sup_{|\lambda_{k}|\leq 1}S\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}a_{k}\phi_{k}\right)(x).

Thus, according to Lemma
3.4
, we have

(4.3)

\|\mathcal{P}\|_{p}\lesssim\|f\|_{p}.

On the other hand,
|g(x)|\leq\mathcal{M}^{d}g(x)
a.e. for any function
g\in L^{1}
, as well as
S(p_{k})(x)\leq\mathcal{P}(x)
,
k=1,2,\ldots,n
. Thus, applying inequality (
4.1
) with
\varepsilon_{n}=(c/\ln n)^{1/2}
, we obtain

(4.4)

\displaystyle|\{|p_{k}(x)|>

\displaystyle\lambda,\,\mathcal{P}(x)\leq\varepsilon_{n}\lambda\}|

(4.5)

\displaystyle\lesssim\exp\left(-\frac{c}{\varepsilon_{n}^{2}}\right)|\{\mathcal{M}^{d}p_{k}(x)>\lambda/2\}|.

For
p^{*}(x)=\max_{1\leq k\leq n}|p_{k}(x)|
we obviously have

(4.6)

\displaystyle\{p^{*}(x)>\lambda\}

\displaystyle\subset\{p^{*}(x)>\lambda,\,\mathcal{P}(x)\leq\varepsilon_{n}\lambda\}

(4.7)

\displaystyle\cup\{\mathcal{P}(x)>\varepsilon_{n}\lambda\}=A(\lambda)\cup B(\lambda),

and thus

(4.8)

\|p^{*}\|_{p}^{p}\leq p\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{p-1}|A(\lambda)|d\lambda+p\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{p-1}|B(\lambda)|d\lambda.

From (
4.4
) it follows that

(4.9)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{p-1}|A(\lambda)|d\lambda

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{p-1}|\{|p_{k}|>\lambda,\,\mathcal{P}\leq\varepsilon_{n}\lambda\}|d\lambda

(4.10)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(-\frac{c}{\varepsilon_{n}^{2}}\right)\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{p-1}|\{\mathcal{M}^{d}p_{k}>\lambda/2\}|d\lambda

(4.11)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|\mathcal{M}^{d}p_{k}\|_{p}^{p}

(4.12)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|p_{k}\|_{p}^{p}

(4.13)

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{p}^{p}.

Combining this and

\displaystyle p\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{p-1}|B(\lambda)|d\lambda

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{n}^{-p}\|\mathcal{P}\|_{p}^{p}\lesssim(\log(n+1))^{p/2}\cdot\|f\|_{p}^{p},

we get

(4.14)

\|p^{*}\|_{p}=\left\|\max_{1\leq m\leq n}|p_{m}(x)|\right\|_{p}\lesssim\sqrt{\log(n+1)}\cdot\|f\|_{p}

and so (
1.14
).

Proof of Corollary
1.1
.

In view of (
1.5
) it remains to show

(4.15)

\mathcal{A}^{p}_{n,\text{mon}}(\Phi)\gtrsim\sqrt{\log(n+1)}.

We remarked in
[
9
]
that from some results of Nikishin-Ulyanov
[
15
]
and Olevskii
[
16
]
it follows that
\mathcal{A}^{2}_{n,\text{mon}}(\Phi)\gtrsim\sqrt{\log(n+1)}
for any complete orthonormal system
\Phi
. Thus (
4.15
) holds in the case
p=2
. So there is a function
f\in L^{2}
and a sequence of integer sets
G_{1}\subset G_{2}\subset\ldots\subset G_{n}
such that

\left\|\max_{1\leq m\leq n}\left|\sum_{j\in G_{m}}\langle f,\phi_{j}\rangle\phi_{j}\right|\right\|_{2}\geq c\sqrt{\log(n+1)}\cdot\|f\|_{2},

Thus for the operator

U(f)=\max_{1\leq m\leq n}\left|\sum_{j\in G_{m}}\langle f,\phi_{j}\rangle\phi_{j}\right|

we have
\|U\|_{2\to 2}\geq c\sqrt{\log(n+1)}
. On the other, according to Theorem
1.1
, we have
\|U\|_{p\to p}\leq c_{p}\sqrt{\log(n+1)}
for all
1<p<\infty
. Combining these two estimates with the ”full version” of Marcinkiewicz interpolation theorem (see
[
23
]
, Theorem 4.6) one can easily obtain
\|U\|_{p\to p}\gtrsim\sqrt{\log(n+1)}
that completes the proof of Corollary
1.1
.
∎

Proof of Corollary
1.2
.

The upper bounds for both systems follow from Theorem
1.1
. The lower bound
\mathcal{A}^{p}_{n,\text{sgn}}(\Phi)\gtrsim\sqrt{\log(n+1)}
for the Franklin system if
p=2
follows from the remark (1) given in the introduction. For the Haar system the case of
p=2
of the same bound is known from
[
15
]
. To show the general case
1<p<\infty
we again use the argument of the Marcinkiewicz interpolation theorem.
∎

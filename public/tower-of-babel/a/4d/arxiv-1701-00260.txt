Loop conditions
Miroslav Olšák

Abstract
We prove that the existence of a term s s satisfying s ⁡ ( x , y , y , z , z , x ) = s ⁡ ( y , x , z , y , x , z ) s(x,y,y,z,z,x)=s(y,x,z,y,x,z) is the weakest non-trivial strong Maltsev condition given by a single identity.



1
Introduction

One of the most interesting by-products of the research on the fixed-template constraint satisfaction problems is the result of Mark Siggers
[
11
]
, which says that there exists a weakest non-trivial strong Maltsev condition for idempotent locally finite varieties.

Let us recall that a
strong Maltsev condition
(see
[
5
,
9
,
4
]
) is a condition for a variety (or an algebra) postulating the existence of finitely many terms satisfying a given finite set of identities. Such conditions can be compared by their strength: a condition
C
is
weaker
than
D
if each variety satisfying
D
also satisfies
C
. The weakest conditions are those satisfied in every variety, we call them
trivial
. The concept of strength can be naturally relativized to special types of varieties, such as idempotent locally finite varieties in the above mentioned result of Siggers.

His weakest non-trivial condition has an especially simple form: it is given by a single linear identity in one operation symbol appearing on both sides, namely

s(x,y,y,z,z,x)=s(y,x,z,y,x,z).

A well known fact, stated here as Proposition
1
, is that
strong Maltsev conditions of this form can be characterized by the existence of a loop
in certain binary relations compatible with algebras in the variety.
This structural property of compatible relations proved useful (see
[
1
,
2
]
) and inspired the following terminology.

Definition 1
.

A
loop condition
is a strong Maltsev condition given by a single identity of the form

t(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=t(y_{1},\dots,y_{n}),

where
x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y_{1},\dots,y_{n}
are variables.

In idempotent locally finite varieties, the 6-ary loop condition by Siggers is equivalent to many other loop conditions, such as the existence of a 4-ary term
t
satisfying
t(r,a,r,e)=t(a,r,e,a)

[
7
]
.

What happens when we drop the local finiteness assumption?
While there is no weakest non-trivial strong Maltsev condition for general varieties
[
12
,
8
]
, it turned out that there is one for idempotent varieties
[
10
]
; for instance, the existence of a
6
-ary
t
satisfying
t(x,y,y,y,x,x)=t(y,x,y,x,y,x)=t(y,y,x,x,x,y)
.
Such a condition cannot be a loop condition: A. Kazda
[
6
]
proved that the free idempotent algebra over
\{x,y\}
in the signature consisting of one ternary operation symbol
w
modulo the weak near unanimity identities
w(x,y,y)=w(y,x,y)=w(y,y,x)
does not satisfy any non-trivial loop condition.

On the other hand, a consequence of the main result of this paper, Theorem
1
, is that the existence of a 6-ary Siggers term is a weakest non-trivial loop condition
in general
, that is, for varieties that are not necessarily idempotent or locally finite. To describe the main result, it will be convenient to assign a directed graph (digraph) to each loop condition in a natural way:

Definition 2
.

Let
C
be a loop condition given by an identity

t(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=t(y_{1},\dots,y_{n})
, where

x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y_{1},\dots,y_{n}
are variables from a set
V
.
The
digraph of
C
, denoted
\mathbb{G}_{C}
, is the
digraph
(V,E)
with vertex set
V
equals to the variable set and edge
set
E=\{(x_{i},y_{i}):i=1,\dots,n\}
.

For example, the digraph of the loop condition
s(x,y)=s(y,x)
is
\mathbb{K}_{2}
and
the digraph of the
6
-ary loop condition by Siggers is
\mathbb{K}_{3}
, where
\mathbb{K}_{i}
denotes the complete loopless digraph on
i
vertices.

It is easy to see that two loop conditions, whose associated digraphs are isomorphic, are equivalent. Therefore, we may talk about “loop condition
\mathbb{G}
” (or sometimes “
\mathbb{G}
loop condition”) instead of “a loop condition whose associated digraph is
\mathbb{G}
”. Also note that
\mathbb{G}_{C}
contains a loop if and only if
C
is trivial, i.e., satisfied in every algebra.

Our main result, Theorem
1
, fully classifies the stregth of undirected loop conditions: Each such nontrivial condition is either equivalent to the existence of a commutative term (condition
\mathbb{K}_{2}
) or the existence of a Siggers
6
-ary term (condition
\mathbb{K}_{3}
).

Preliminaries

An
n
-ary operation
f
on a set
A
is
compatible
with an
m
-ary relation
R\subseteq A^{m}
, or
R
is
compatible
with
f
, if
f(\mathbf{r}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{r}_{n})\in R
for any
\mathbf{r}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{r}_{n}\in R
. Here (and later as well) we abuse the notation and use
f
also for the
n
-ary operation on
A^{m}
defined from
f
coordinate-wise.

An algebra
\mathbf{A}=(A,f_{1},f_{2},\ldots)
is said to be compatible with a relational structure
\mathbb{A}=(A,R_{1},R_{2},\ldots)
if all the operations
f_{1},f_{2},\ldots
are compatible with all the relations
R_{1},R_{2},\ldots
.

Digraph
is a relational structure
\mathbb{G}=(G,E)
with one binary relation
E
. If
E
is symmetric, then the digraph
\mathbb{G}
is called
undirected
. A
loop
in
\mathbb{G}
is a pair of the form
(x,x)\in E
.

We will extensively use a standard method for building compatible
relations from existing ones – primitive positive (
pp
, for short) definitions.
A relation
R
is
pp-definable
from relations

R_{1},\ldots,R_{n}
if it can be defined by a first order formula using variables,
existential quantifiers, conjunctions, the equality relations, and predicates
R_{1},\ldots,R_{n}
.
Clauses in pp-definitions are also referred to as
constraints
.
Recall that
R_{1},\ldots,R_{n}
are compatible with an algebra, then so is
R
.

It is sometimes helpful to visualize a pp-definition of
k
-ary relation
R
from a digraph
\mathbb{G}
as a digraph
\mathbb{H}
with
k
distinguished vertices
u_{1},\ldots,u_{k}
:
the vertices of
\mathbb{H}
are the variables, its edges correspond to the constraints, and the distinguished vertices are the free variables.
Observe that
(v_{1},\ldots,v_{k})
is in
R
if and only if there is a digraph homomorphism
\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{G}
which maps
u_{i}
to
v_{i}
for each
i=1,\ldots,k
. In a similar way, we can visualize pp-definitions from a relational structure consisting of more than one binary relation.

A
pp-power
of a relational structure
\mathbb{A}
on
A
is a relational structure
\mathbb{B}
on
A^{l}
whose relations are pp-definable from
\mathbb{A}
in the sense that a
k
-ary relation from
\mathbb{B}
, regarded as a
(k\cdot l)
-ary relation on
A
, is pp-definable from
\mathbb{A}
. Observe that if
\mathbb{A}
is compatible with an algebra
\mathbf{A}
, then the pp-power
\mathbb{B}
is compatible with the algebraic power
\mathbf{A}^{l}
.

For more background on pp-definitions and its relevance for constraint satisfaction problems we refer the reader to
[
3
]
.

Finally, we state the promised correspondence between loop conditions and loops in digraphs.

Proposition 1
.

Let
\mathcal{V}
be a variety and
C
a loop condition.
The following conditions are equivalent.

(i)

\mathcal{V}
satisfies
C
.

(ii)

For every
\mathbf{A}\in\mathcal{V}
and for every digraph

\mathbb{G}=(A,G)
compatible
with
\mathbf{A}
, the following holds: If there is a digraph homomorphism

\mathbb{G}_{C}\to\mathbb{G}
then
\mathbb{G}
contain a loop.

(iii)

For every
\mathbf{A}\in\mathcal{V}
and for every digraph

\mathbb{G}=(A,G)
compatible
with
\mathbf{A}
, the following holds: If a subdigraph of
\mathbb{G}
is
isomorphic to
G_{C}
then
G
contains a loop.

Proof.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii)
Let
C
be of the form

t(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=t(y_{1},\dots,y_{n})
and

f\colon\mathbb{G}_{C}\to\mathbb{G}
be a digraph
homomorphism. Then
(f(x_{i}),f(y_{i}))\in G
for all

i=1,\ldots,n
. By compatibility of
\mathbb{G}
with
\mathbf{A}
also

(t(f(x_{1}),\ldots,f(x_{n})),t(f(y_{1}),\dots,f(y_{n})))\in G,

Since equality

t(f(x_{1}),\ldots,f(x_{n}))=t(f(y_{1}),\dots,f(y_{n}))

is ensured by
C
, we get the desired loop in
\mathbb{G}
.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii) Trivial.

(iii)
\Rightarrow
(i) Let
\mathbf{F}
be the free algebra in
\mathcal{V}

generated by the vertices of
\mathbb{G}_{C}
and let
E
be the subuniverse of

\mathbf{F}^{2}
generated by the edges of
\mathbb{G}_{C}
.
The set
E
is the edge–set a
digraph compatible with
\mathbf{F}
containing
\mathbb{G}_{C}
as a subgraph. Therefore, by
(iii), there is loop
(a,a)
in
E
. The pair
(a,a)
is generated from edges of
\mathbb{G}_{C}
, so there is a term operation
t
of
\mathbf{F}^{2}
taking edges of

\mathbb{G}_{C}
as arguments and returning
(a,a)
. Thus, in
\mathbf{F}
, the
term
t
satisfies the loop condition
C
, at least when we plug in the generators. By the universality of free algebra, the term
t

satisfies
C
in general.
∎

Corollary 1
.

Let
C
,
D
be loop conditions. If there is a digraph
homomorphism
\mathbb{G}_{C}\to\mathbb{G}_{D}
, then
C
implies

D
. In particular,
C
implies
D
whenever
\mathbb{G}_{C}
is a subdigraph of

\mathbb{G}_{D}
.

Corollary 2
.

Loop conditions with homomorphically equivalent digraphs are
equivalent. In particular, loop conditions with isomorphic digraphs
are equivalent.

3
Undirected case

In this section, we will focus on loop conditions with
undirected digraphs, briefly graphs.

We start with several simple consequences of
Corollary
1
.

Proposition 2
.

1.

Any loop condition of a bipartite graph is equivalent to the edge loop condition (commutativity).

2.

The
(l+2)
-cycle loop condition implies the
l
-cycle loop condition for any odd length
l\geq 3
.

3.

The
n
-clique loop condition implies the
(n+1)
-clique loop condition for any size
n\geq 3
.

4.

For any non-bipartite graph
\mathbb{G}
there is an odd
length
l\geq 3
such that the
l
-cycle loop condition implies
the loop condition given by
\mathbb{G}
.

5.

For any loopless digraph
\mathbb{G}
there is a size

n\geq 3
such that the loop condition given by
\mathbb{G}

implies the
n
-clique loop condition.

Our aim in the rest of the chapter is to reverse the second and the third implication, that is,

Proposition 3
.

The

-cycle loop condition implies the
(l+2)
-cycle loop

Proof.

There is a graph homomorphism from the

-cycle because both cycles are odd and

.
It is thus sufficient to show that the
l
-cycle loop condition

-cycle one.

We use Proposition
1
. Let
\mathbf{A}
be an
algebra on a set
A
such that every digraph compatible with
\mathbf{A}
containing a
homomorphic image of the

-cycle has a loop. We need to prove that every

\mathbb{G}=(A,G)
containing a homomorphic image of the

-cycle has a loop. Take such a digraph
\mathbb{G}
where the cycle is

v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{l^{2}-1}
. We may assume that

is symmetric, since the set of symmetric edges of

is clearly pp-definable from

.

We construct a binary relation

The

-clique loop condition implies the

Proof.

As in the previous proposition, we use Proposition
1
. Let
\mathbf{A}
on a set

be an algebra satisfying the

Let
\mathbb{G}=(A,G)

It suffices to prove that

Theorem 1
.

There are exactly three equivalence classes of loop conditions given by undirected digraphs
\mathbb{G}
:

(1)

loop conditions
\mathbb{G}
, where
\mathbb{G}
is bipartite,

(2)

loop conditions
\mathbb{G}
, where
\mathbb{G}
is non-bipartite and loopless,

(3)

loop conditions
\mathbb{G}
, where
\mathbb{G}
contains a loop (trivial).

Conditions (1) imply (2) imply (3).
Conditions (2) are the weakest non-trivial loop conditions.

Proof.

If a graph
\mathbb{G}
is bipartite (contains a loop, respectively), then the
\mathbb{G}
loop condition is equivalent to the edge loop condition by item 1 of Proposition
2
(is trivial, respectively). If
\mathbb{G}
is non-bipartite and loopless, then the
\mathbb{G}
loop condition implies a clique loop condition (item 5 of Proposition
2
), which implies the triangle loop condition (by Proposition
4
), which implies the
l
-cycle loop condition for any odd
l
(by Proposition
3
), which, finally, implies the
\mathbb{G}
loop condition (by item 4 of Proposition
2
).

Clearly, conditions (1) imply (2) imply (3), and (3) do not imply (2). The implication from (2) to (1) cannot be reversed even for idempotent finitely generated varieties: an example of an algebra satisfying (2) but not (1) is the algebra
(\{0,1,2\},m)
where
m(x,y,z)=x+y-z
modulo 3.
∎

4
Directed case

A digraph
\mathbb{G}
is said to be
smooth
if every vertex has an incoming
and an outgoing edge;
\mathbb{G}
is said to have

algebraic length 1
if there is no graph homomorphism from

\mathbb{G}
to a directed cycle of length greater than one.
The following theorem holds for finite algebras
[
7
]
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
\mathbf{A}
be a finite algebra. Let
\mathbb{G},\mathbb{H}
be weakly connected smooth
digraphs of algebraic length one. Then
\mathbf{A}
satisfies loop
condition
\mathbb{G}
if and only if it satisfies loop condition
\mathbb{H}
.

Less formally, all connected smooth loop conditions with algebraic length
1 are equivalent for finite algebras. This gives us a simpler weakest loop
condition than the triangle for finite algebras:

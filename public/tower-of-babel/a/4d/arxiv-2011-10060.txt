Quantum-limit Hall effect with large carrier density in topological semimetals
Guang Yang, Yi Zhang

Abstract
The quantum-limit Hall effect at ν = n ​ h / e ​ B ∼ O ⁡ ( 1 ) \nu=nh/eB\sim O(1) that hosts a variety of exotic quantum phenomena requires demanding strong magnetic field B B and low carrier density n n . We propose to realize quantum-limit Hall effect even in the presence of large carrier density residues n e n_{e} and n h n_{h} relative to the magnetic field B B in topological semimetals, where a single Fermi surface contour allow both electron-type and hole-type carriers and approaches charge neutrality as n e ∼ n h n_{e}\sim n_{h} . The underlying filling factor ν = | n e − n h | ​ h / e ​ B \nu=|n_{e}-n_{h}|h/eB explicitly violates the Onsager’s relation for quantum oscillations.



A two-dimensional electron system may display the integer quantum Hall effect with exactly quantized Hall conductance
\sigma_{xy}=\nu e^{2}/h

;

;

Semi-classically, the charge carriers in a magnetic field move along constant-energy field-normal cyclotron orbits, whose overall phase’s Bohr-Sommerfeld quantization

;
Baym and Pethick 1991
. More recently, the cyclotron orbit extended its concept to the Weyl orbit

;
Zhang et al. 2016b
;
Moll et al. 2016
;

;

—For concreteness, let’s consider the following three-dimensional Weyl semimetal tight-binding model with slab geometry of thickness

Such cancellation effect between the electron carriers and hole carriers is also in effect for the quantum Hall effects in general, such as the conditions for the quantum limit
\nu=\left|n_{e}-n_{h}\right|h/eB
at relatively larger magnetic fields. The Landau fan diagrams in Fig.
3
demonstrate the entrance of the quantum limit. Importantly, as the filling factor is dominated by the absolute difference between the electron density
n_{e}
and the hole density
n_{h}

B
to reach a target
\nu
can be suppressed by orders of magnitude, especially when the electron and hole densities are close
n_{e}\sim n_{h}

B(\Phi_{z}/\Phi_{0})=0.0048

H=H_{0}+H_{1}

\epsilon_{0}^{\prime}=3.02

\mu=0.0

B(\Phi_{z}/\Phi_{0})=0.199
for a fully hole-type system
H=H_{0}
with similar settings to reach the quantum limit - the cancellation effect leads to small effective carrier density despite large carrier density residues and reduced the required magnetic field by over 97%. Therefore, cyclotron orbits with both electron-type and hole-type carriers is indeed physically possible and can potentially allow quantum-limit Hall effect despite large carrier density residues, as we have showcased with the Weyl orbit in topological semimetal.

To understand the discrepancies, we revisit the Weyl orbit components for Fermi energy away from the Weyl nodes. The above proposal relies upon the proper tuning of the Fermi energy and the magnetic field angle and is generally more applicable for experiments and realizable with existing Weyl semimetals. The separation between the Weyl nodes should be appropriate: a large separation may lead to Fermi arcs and surface carrier density too large for a proper chemical potential in the bulk to compensate; a small separate may lead to the pair-wise annihilation of Weyl nodes in a magnetic field
Chan and Lee 2017
;
Zhang et al. 2017b
;
Ramshaw et al. 2018

Meng et al. 2020

n_{e}{\sim}n_{h}
thus the Hall number
n_{H}=B/e\rho_{xy}

n_{e}+n_{h}

\sigma=(n_{e}+n_{h})e^{2}\tau/m

Potter et al. 2014

Conclusion.
—We discover that electron-type and hole-type carriers may coexist in the same Fermi surface contour and contribute oppositely to the geometric phase and quantum Hall phenomena, which can approach the quantum limit
\nu=|n_{e}-n_{h}|h/eB
with a much smaller magnetic field than commonly needed with properly-tuned despite large carrier density residues
n_{e}{\sim}n_{h}
. We illustrate our conclusions with the Weyl orbit in topological semimetals and note that the physics also holds for quantum systems where a single cyclotron orbit traverses different segments with opposite carrier types, such as surface states and bulk states.

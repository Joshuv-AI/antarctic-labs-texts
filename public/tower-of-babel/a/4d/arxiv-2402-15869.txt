Phase Diagram and Spectroscopic Signatures of Supersolids in Quantum Ising Magnet K 2 Co(SeO 3 ) 2
Tong Chen, Alireza Ghasemi, Junyi Zhang, Liyu Shi, Zhenisbek Tagay, Youzhe Chen, Lei Chen, Eun-Sang Choi, Marcelo Jaime, Minseong Lee, Yiqing Hao, Huibo Cao, Barry Winn, Andrey A. Podlesnyak, Daniel M. Pajerowski, Ruidan Zhong, Xianghan Xu, N. P. Armitage, Robert Cava, Collin Broholm

Abstract
A supersolid is a quantum-entangled state of matter that exhibits the dual characteristics of superfluidity and solidity. While theoretical studies have predicted that hard-core bosons with repulsive interactions on a triangular lattice can host a supersolid phase, experimental validation has remained elusive. Leveraging an exact mapping between bosons and spins, we investigate the supersolid phase in a spin- 1 2 \frac{1}{2} triangular-lattice antiferromagnet K 2 Co(SeO 3 ) 2 . Here, we present the magnetic phase diagram and neutron scattering results for K 2 Co(SeO 3 ) 2 , which features nearest-neighbor Ising-like interactions with J z = 2.96 ​ ( 2 ) J_{z}=2.96(2) meV and J ⟂ = 0.21 ​ ( 3 ) J_{\perp}=0.21(3) meV. In zero field, neutron spectroscopy reveals the gradual development of a quasi-two-dimensional 3 × 3 \sqrt{3}\times\sqrt{3} magnetic order with Z 3 translational symmetry breaking (solidity) below 15 K. At temperatures below 0.3 K, the fully developed supersolid phase is evidenced by the coexistence of a gapless Goldstone mode arising from broken U ( 1 ) (1) spin rotational symmetry (superfluidity), and a gapped pseudo-Goldstone mode associated with lifted accidental XY degeneracy (solidity). In 𝐜 \bf c -axis-oriented magnetic fields 1.1 T < < B B < < 21 T, a prominent 1 / 3 \nicefrac{{1}}{{3}} magnetization plateau phase emerges, accompanied by a plausible high-field supersolid phase (17 T < < B B < < 21 T). Our results establish K 2 Co(SeO 3 ) 2 as an exceptional realization of a spin- 1 2 \frac{1}{2} triangular-lattice quantum Ising magnet, document its magnetic phase diagram featuring two supersolid phases, and uncover spectroscopic signatures of zero-field supersolidity in a triangular lattice antiferromagnet.

Coherent Spinwaves in the UUD Phase

Figure
4

(M

U spins in the UUD phase. Spin-wave propagation, driven by the transverse exchange, can reverse an anti-parallel spin pair. However, for a D spin surrounded by U spins, such propagation is inhibited, resulting in a dispersionless mode. The energy cost of the spin-flip is approximately
2zJ_{z}S^{2}-2g_{z}S\mu_{\rm B}B\approx 5.8
meV, in good agreement with the observed data (Fig.
4

meV and
g_{z}=7.8
were inferred from the

(
M_{\rm sat}=g_{z}S\mu_{\rm B}
)

The two lower-energy dispersive modes are each associated with flipping one of the two U spins in the unit cell. Since U spins are surrounded by an equal number of U and D spins, the
\mathbf{Q}
-averaged energy for these modes is simply
2g_{z}S\mu_{\rm B}B_{z}=3.16
meV. Dispersion arises because the newly created D spin can move to adjacent U sites through the transverse exchange term. The bandwidth provides an estimate for
3J_{\perp}\approx 0.78

To quantitatively test the spin-wave theory against the experimental data, we performed a pixel-to-pixel least squares fit of the data in Figure

standard deviation of 0.15
meV. Given the weak dispersion, finite

meV and

Summary and Conclusions

K
2
Co(SeO
3
)
2
has proven to be an excellent platform for exploring the quantum collective properties of a foundational model in frustrated magnetism: the spin-
\frac{1}{2}
triangular lattice Ising model with weak transverse exchange interactions. The
\nicefrac{{1}}{{3}}
plateau phase, characterized by long-range UUD three sublattice order, is accessible to inelastic neutron scattering. In this phase our neutron data reveal coherent spin-wave modes that are well-described by the spin-Hamiltonian in Equation
1
with
J_{z}=2.96(1)
meV and
J_{\perp}=0.21(3)
meV. In particular, the lack of dispersion of the minority spin-flip excitation provides direct experimental evidence that nearest neighbor interactions dominate (
J_{\perp,z}^{(2)}\leq 0.02
meV). The ratio
J_{z}/J_{\perp}=14(1)
places KCSO deeply in the Ising limit, though its quantum dynamics are readily exposed with modern neutron scattering instrumentation.

Elastic neutron scattering in zero field reveals the gradual onset of solidity below 15 K, indicated by the quasi-2D
\sqrt{3}\times\sqrt{3}
magnetic order associated with Z
3
translational symmetry breaking. While superfluidity is absent down to 0.3 K, the lack of scattering at
\Gamma
points (Fig.
1
c) and the reduced moment size (Fig.
1
f) at 0.1 K reflects significant quantum fluctuations.

Inelastic neutron scattering further reveals the quantum fluctuations through flat bands of excitations at quantized energies
\hslash\omega=nJ_{z}
. The fully developed supersolid phase at 0.25 K is evidenced by the coexistence of a Goldstone mode (gapless) and a pseudo-Goldstone mode (gapped). These modes arise from the breaking of U
(1)
spin rotational symmetry (superfluidity) and the lifting of accidental XY degeneracy (solidity), respectively, providing spectroscopic confirmation of the supersolid phase.

We have comprehensively mapped the full
B-T
phase diagram of KCSO, which includes a prominent UUD phase, a plausible high-field supersolid phase, and a second-order transition boundary in the 2D three-state Potts universality class.

Hard-Core Boson Representation

To understand the quantum magnetic order emerging in spin-
\frac{1}{2}
systems on triangular lattices, as described by the spin-Hamiltonian in Equation(
1
), it is convenient to represent the spin operators with hard-core bosons

\begin{split}S_{i}^{z}=&n_{i}-\frac{1}{2},\quad S_{i}^{+}=&b_{i}^{\dagger},\quad S_{i}^{-}=b_{i},\end{split}

(3)

where
n_{i}=b_{i}^{\dagger}b_{i}
is restricted to 0 or 1. Rewriting the Hamiltonian (Eq.(
1
)) in terms of the hard-core bosons yields:

\begin{split}{\cal H}=\sum_{<i,j>}\left[V\left(n_{i}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\left(n_{j}-\frac{1}{2}\right)-t(b_{i}^{\dagger}b_{j}+b_{j}^{\dagger}b_{i})\right]-\mu\sum_{i}\left(n_{i}-\frac{1}{2}\right),\end{split}

(4)

where the model parameters
V=J_{z}
,
-t=\frac{1}{2}J_{\perp}
and
\mu=g\mu_{\rm B}B_{z}
. In this bosonic representation,
V>0
signifies a repulsive interaction between nearest neighbor bosons,
-t
represents the hopping amplitude, and
\mu
corresponds to the chemical potential. The rotational symmetry of the
x
- and
y
-components of the spins become U
(1)
symmetry of the bosons.

We focus on two distinct types of orders: diagonal charge order, and off-diagonal phase order. Diagonal charge order corresponds to charge density waves (or equivalently, spatial modulations in
S^{z}
), often referred to as solidity.
Off-diagonal order involves the spontaneous breaking of the U
(1)
symmetry, characteristic of superfluidity.
According to the Mermin-Wagner theorem, true long-range orders associated with continuous symmetry cannot develop at finite temperatures.
In the superfluid phase, only quasi-long-range order is present at low temperatures, with vortex proliferation (via the BKT mechanism) destroying coherence at high temperatures.

The antiferromagnetic Heisenberg model on triangular lattices with easy-axis anisotropy has been suggested to host phases exhibiting both diagonal and off-diagonal long-range orders, referred to as supersolids. Since
m_{\perp}=g_{\perp}\langle S_{\perp}\rangle
is linked to the creation and annihilation of bosons (
b^{\dagger}
and
b
), the nonzero
m_{\perp}
reflects a macroscopic quantum coherence of bosons, indicative of superfluidity. Similarly, nonzero
m_{z}=g_{z}\langle S_{z}\rangle
reflects the spatial modulation of boson density, analogous to a classical charge density wave in a solid.

Materials Synthesis

\rm K_{2}Co(SeO_{3})_{2}
single crystals were synthesized using a previously reported solid-state reaction method
[
28
]
at Johns Hopkins University and Princeton University.
X-ray diffraction analysis of polycrystalline samples confirmed the absence of stacking faults or secondary phases.
The crystals were found to crystallize in space group
R\bar{3}m
(No. 166) with lattice constants
a=b=5.5049(7)
Å, and
c=18.411(3)
Å at 100 K.

Specific Heat

The specific heat capacity of KCSO was measured using a thermal-relaxation method in a Quantum Design Physical Properties Measurement System (PPMS) at Johns Hopkins University. A 2.1 mg plate-like sample was mounted horizontally to align the
\bf c
-axis along the vertical field direction of the 14 T magnet.

Magnetization

The high-field magnetization data,
M(T)
, were obtained using a conventional vibrating sample magnetometer (VSM) and a water-cooled resistive magnet in Cell 8 of the DC Field Facility of the National High Magnetic Field Laboratory (NHMFL) in Tallahassee, FL. The VSM was calibrated using a standard Ni sphere and the sample was glued to a polycarbonate sample holder with GE7031 varnish. These magnetization data were normalized by comparison to a DC-magnetization measurement with a VSM in a 14 T PPMS (Quantum Design) at Johns Hopkins University.

The magnetization,
M(B)
, in millisecond pulsed magnets reaching up to 60 T was measured at the NHMFL pulsed field facility at Los Alamos National Laboratory. The experiments were conducted in three configurations with magnetic fields along the
\bf a
-,
\bf b
-, and
\bf c
-axis directions. To reduce the background from the spatially uniform pulsed magnetic field, the samples were placed in a radially counter-wound copper coil. An additional one turn of the coil further compensated for any residual signals. For each magnetization curve, two configurations, with the sample inside and outside the coil, were measured at the same temperature, and the sample-out curve was subtracted from the sample-in curve. The temperature was stabilized using a
3
He and
4
He dilution refrigerator. A Lakeshore Cernox thermometer recorded the temperatures before each field pulse.

An 8.22 mg single crystal was mounted on

The experiment used a four-circle mode and a beam of neutrons with wavelength

Global existence of strong solutions to the Inhomogeneous Kinetic Wave Equation
Ioakeim Ampatzoglou, Tristan Léger

Abstract
. In this paper we construct global dispersive solutions to the space inhomogeneous kinetic wave equation (KWE) which propagate L x ​ v 1 L^{1}_{xv} – moments and conserve mass, momentum and energy. We prove that they scatter, and that the wave operators mapping the initial data to the scattering states are 1-1, onto and continuous in a suitable topology. This is the first global existence result for strong solutions for KWE. This contrasts with prior global existence results for mild solutions, which satisfy a transported version of the equation but do not solve the equation itself. Our proof is carried out entirely in physical space and combines dispersive estimates for the free transport with new trilinear bounds for the gain and loss operators of the KWE on weighted Lebesgue spaces. The main difficulty is the fast growth of the hard-sphere kernel. Our fundamental tool to handle it is a novel collisional averaging estimate. We also show that the nonlinear evolution preserves positivity forward in time. For this, we use the Kaniel-Shinbrot iteration scheme [ 40 ] , properly initialized to ensure successive approximations are dispersive.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background

In this paper we consider the 3D space inhomogeneous kinetic wave equation

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}f+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f=\mathcal{C}[f]\\
f(t=0)=f_{0}\end{cases}

where
f:(-T,T)\times\mathbb{R}_{x}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}_{v}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
T>0
and
f_{0}:{\mathbb{R}}^{3}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3}_{v}\to{\mathbb{R}}
.

The collision operator
\mathcal{C}
is defined as

(1.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{C}[f]&:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{9}}\delta(\Sigma)\,\delta(\Omega)\,ff_{1}f_{2}f_{3}\,\big(\frac{1}{f}+\frac{1}{f_{1}}-\frac{1}{f_{2}}-\frac{1}{f_{3}}\big)\,dv_{1}dv_{2}dv_{3}\\
\Sigma&:=v+v_{1}-v_{2}-v_{3}\\
\Omega&:=|v|^{2}+|v_{1}|^{2}-|v_{2}|^{2}-|v_{3}|^{2}.\end{split}

This equation is well-known in the field of wave turbulence, which has seen a surge of interest in recent years. Although some basic concepts and principles had already been laid out in 1929 by R. Peierls
[
45
]
and independently in 1962 by K. Hasselman
[
36
,
37
]
, a connection to hydrodynamic turbulence uncovered by V. Zakharov, V. L’vov and G. Falkovich
[
49
]
contributed to a dramatic uptick in popularity of the subject.

This theory aims to describe the out-of-equilibrium dynamics of physical system composed of random waves. More precisely the objective is to track and predict statistical properties of solutions to these equations, such as transfers of energy between scales. This is typically achieved by deriving effective equations (of wave kinetic type) for the quantities of interest. We refer to the lecture notes of S. Nazarenko
[
44
]
for a comprehensive list of examples where this theory was successfully implemented.

At the mathematical level the theory has matured: there are now rigorous derivations of wave kinetic equations for several models. The most studied is the homogeneous kinetic wave equation with Laplacian dispersion relation (it corresponds to solutions to (
1.1
) that do not depend on
x
). It was shown to capture the statistical properties of a system of random waves modelled by the cubic nonlinear Schrödinger equation on large time scales. The validity of the derivation was proved on increasingly large time intervals by various groups until the optimal time (so called kinetic time) was reached by Y. Deng and Z. Hani. We refer the reader to the works

[

]
. Later, inhomogeneous turbulence was also derived from the Wick (NLS) by Z. Hani, J. Shatah and H. Zhu in
[

. Other models have also been considered, such as KdV-type equations by G. Staffilani and M.-B. Tran

With the derivation of these kinetic wave equations now on firm mathematical ground, it is legitimate to start analyzing them to gain further insight into the out-of-equilibrium behavior of systems of interacting waves. There are still relatively few results in this direction. In the case of the homogeneous kinetic wave equation for example, only local well-posedness is known for strong solutions by work of P. Germain, A. Ionescu and M.-B. Tran

Of course the kinetic community has already studied equations similar to (

1.2.
Results obtained

We now focus more specifically on equation (
1.1
). We first mention that global existence of
mild
solutions has been proved by two different methods in
[
5
]
and
[
9
]
. Mild solutions satisfy a transported version of (
1.1
), but not the equation itself.

In the present article, we prove the first global existence result for
strong
solutions to (
1.1
). Note that our solutions are also mild solutions, while the converse is not true. At the technical level, this is due to the fact that mild solutions are constructed in spaces that are weighted in
x.
Since free transport is not an isometry on such spaces, mild solutions do not correspond to solutions of (
1.1
).

To avoid the pitfall of
x
weighted spaces, and the subsequent inevitable restriction to mild solutions, our proof relies on dispersive estimates for free transport instead of closeness to traveling maxwellians. The use of dispersive methods is not without precedent in the kinetic community. For the Boltzmann equation they were used to study local well-posedness at low regularity. In that direction, see works of T. Chen, R. Denlinger and N. Pavlović
[
16
,
18
,
17
]
, as well as X. Chen and J. Holmer
[
19
]
, X. Chen, S. Shen and Z. Zhang
[
20
,
21
]
. All these papers actually focused on the Fourier transform of the solution rather than work in physical space. We also mention the work of D. Arsenio
[
10
]
that relies on Strichartz estimates for the free transport to construct weak solutions to the Boltzmann equation for soft potentials. The problems mentioned above are subcritical with respect to scattering. A currently very active related line of research consists in proving global existence results near vacuum for equations that are critical with respect to scattering. Most closely related to our work, we mention
[
15
]
which treats the Boltzmann equation with moderately soft potentials and no angular cutoff. The main difficulty we address in this article is different, and comes from the hard-sphere nature of the potential in (
1.1
). Kinetic equations with such cross-sections typically have a much worse behavior than equations with soft potentials. For example it is easy to see that the equation with right-hand side equal to the loss term of the Boltzmann equation

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f=f(v)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v-v_{1}|^{\gamma}f(v_{1})\,dv_{1}

is locally ill-posed in any space of the form
\langle v\rangle^{-M}L^{\infty}_{xv}
for large enough
M>0
and any
\gamma>0.
For comparison, recall that the hard-sphere potential present in (
1.1
) corresponds to
\gamma=1.

Therefore the main contribution of the present article is the discovery and use of a new collisional averaging effect (see Lemma
2.1
). It overcomes the fast growth of the hard-sphere kernel in the context of the kinetic wave equation while not requiring localization in the spatial variable, i.e. weights in
x.

To the best of our knowledge, the closest result to ours is the very recent article
[
38
]
, where global solutions to the Boltzmann equation with hard spheres are constructed by means of Strichartz estimates. Of course here we deal with a different equation. We also emphasize that our method to prove Lebesgue boundedness of the collision operator is also very different, relying on purely kinetic tools instead of Fourier analysis. Moreover we are able to prove a number of desirable physical properties for our solutions, namely that they propagate moments, conserve mass, energy and momentum and preserve positivity forward in time. Informally, our results can be stated as

Theorem 1.1
.

The equation (
1.1
) with small and sufficiently decaying initial data
f_{0}
has a unique global dispersive solution. Moreover as
t\to\pm\infty
this solution scatters, that is there exist
f_{+\infty},f_{-\infty}
such that

\displaystyle f(t,x,v)-f_{+\infty}(x-vt,v)\longrightarrow_{t\to+\infty}0,\quad f(t,x,v)-f_{-\infty}(x-vt,v)\longrightarrow_{t\to-\infty}0,

in a suitable sense.

Moreover these solutions propagate
L^{1}_{xv}
– moments: if
\|{\langle}v{\rangle}^{N}f(0)\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}
is small enough, then
\|{\langle}v{\rangle}^{N}f(t)\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}
remains uniformly bounded in time, and they conserve total mass, momentum and energy i.e.

\partial_{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{6}}\phi f(t)\,dv\,dx=0,\quad\phi\in\{1,v,|v|^{2}\}.

Finally the nonlinear flow preserves positivity forward in time: if
f(0)\geq 0,
then for all
t\geq 0
we have
f(t)\geq 0.

To refine the analysis, we describe the scattering states using concepts from scattering theory, namely wave operators. It is indeed natural to seek further information on the scattering states
f_{\pm\infty}
, about their general shape for example. By analogy with the Boltzmann equation, it could be expected that they have to be thermodynamic equilibria, called Rayleigh-Jeans distributions (the counterparts of maxwellians in kinetic wave theory). Indeed L. Desvillettes and C. Villani proved in
[
31
]
that the scattering states have to be maxwellians for the inhomogeneous Boltzmann equation set on a torus. We answer in the negative and prove that the scattering states can be any reasonably fast decaying function near vacuum.

A scattering result of this kind has been obtained for
mild
solutions to the Boltzmann equation by C. Bardos, I.M. Gamba, F. Golse and C.D. Levermore in
[
11
]
. However as stressed previously, this is really a statement about solutions to a transported version of the Boltzmann equation, not the equation itself. Indeed the authors work in spaces exponentially weighted in
x
and
v
, whereas we avoid weights in
x
and work in spaces that are polynomially weighted in
v
only. We emphasize that this is not a mere technicality and leads to conceptual differences with existing works in the literature: it allows us to construct strong solution (the standard notion of solutions in PDE theory) for the equation, rather than mild solutions as in
[
11
,
5
,
9
]
.

Moreover in the setting of wave turbulence polynomially weighted spaces are more physically relevant than exponentially weighted ones, especially when it comes to long-time dynamics. Indeed, thermodynamic equilibrium is reached for distributions that exhibit polynomial decay, unlike for the Boltzmann equation where such states are maxwellians.

Besides allowing us to describe the asymptotic behavior of solutions (namely scattering), another advantage of our method is that it straightforwardly yields global propagation of
L^{1}_{xv}
– moments for our solutions. This is far from evident a priori; in fact, to the best of our knowledge, for hard cutoff potentials, propagation of
L^{1}_{xv}
– moments is not known for the inhomogeneous Boltzmann equation
[
48
]
, although moment production estimates are well studied in the spatially homogeneous case, see e.g.
[
30
,
13
,
43
,
1
]
.

Our method of proof is versatile, and we expect that it can be implemented for many other kinetic models. In this direction, a similar result for the Boltzmann equation will be the subject of an upcoming work
[
8
]
.

1.3.
Ideas of the proof

Our proof is perturbative in nature: we show that the nonlinearity is dominated by the free transport in the small data regime. Since this operator has well-known dispersive properties, this allows us to control the evolution globally in time.

To use a bootstrap-based perturbative approach modelled on similar arguments for dispersive equations (e.g. the nonlinear Schrödinger equation), it is necessary to know mapping properties of the collision operator on (possibly weighted) Lebesgue spaces. However nonlinearities in kinetic equations are notoriously hard to work with due to their complicated expression as integrals on resonant manifolds. Dealing with this difficulty is a key step in our paper. In fact this specific problem has a long history for the Boltzmann equation, whose theory is much more advanced than that of the kinetic wave equation. A large literature is devoted solely to establishing boundedness of the collision operator on weighted Lebesgue spaces (see the papers of R. Alonso, E. Carneiro
[
2
]
, and the same authors with I.M. Gamba
[
3
]
, as well as references therein). Using these estimates, distributional and classical solutions for the Boltzmann equation for soft potentials were constructed by R. Alonso and I.M. Gamba in
[
4
]
.

The fact that we work in polynomially weighted spaces adds a layer a complexity to the problem. Indeed the presence of linear growth in the kernel of the collision operator could lead to believe that estimates cannot be closed (naively the right-hand side of the equation always has one more
v
weight than the linear part). To deal with this problem, we leverage the decay of a singular integral (see Lemma
2.1
) to offset the linear growth of the kernel. This can be seen as a kind of regularization by angular averaging. To the best of our knowledge this observation was not made in the kinetic literature previously. The same difficulty is present for the Boltzmann equation with hard potentials. Despite intense efforts to obtain sharp weighted Lebesgue estimates of the collision operator, this case was only very recently settled in
[
38
]
, where the authors build on the extensive literature mentioned above. The regularization mechanism we uncover for (
1.1
) is different (and simpler) than the one used in
[
38
]
and its precursors.

Of course the bounds we obtain are much less favorable than those typically available in more standard dispersive problems, such as the nonlinear Schrödinger equation. Therefore the bootstrap assumptions have to be carefully chosen to accommodate the rough nonlinearity. As a result multiple estimates quantifying the decay, amplitude and integrability of the solution have to be propagated to close the argument.

As mentioned above, an upside of our method of proof is that a number of properties of the solution can be directly deduced from the analysis. For example we are able to show that the solutions propagate
L^{1}_{xv}
– moments. This in turn allows us to prove that the natural conservation laws (mass, momentum, energy) are satisfied. We also stress that our proof is entirely perturbative. This differs from techniques used for the Boltzmann equation, which consist in solving the gain only equation and using the Kaniel-Shinbrot scheme to control the full evolution. In particular, we can prove lower bounds for the moments, which cannot be done with the aforementioned Boltzmann methods.

Finally we prove that the nonlinear flow preserves positivity forward in time. This is a non-perturbative argument which uses the gain-loss structure of the collision operator. Therefore one expects the equation to display time irreversibility, unlike the transport which is time reversible. This is reflected in the fact that our argument only applies to positive times, although the free transport propagates positivity both forward and backward in time. To show positivity of the solution for positive times, we use the Kaniel-Shinbrot
[
40
]
iteration scheme which is a classical tool in collisional kinetic theory, e.g.
[
40
,
39
,
12
,
47
,
4
,
7
]
; however it was not until recently exploited in the context of wave turbulence by the first author in
[
5
]
, and later by the first author and collaborators in
[
9
]
. In our dispersive setting though, the use of this method is more delicate. Indeed it is harder to guarantee dispersion of the iterates. To remedy this, we carefully initialize the scheme by solving the gain only equation (
1.1
) and using its dispersive bounds. We note that similar ideas of initializing the scheme by solving the gain equation were first used in the works
[
39
,
17
,
21
]
.

1.4.
The kinetic model

The collision operator can be split in gain-loss form

(1.3)

\mathcal{C}[f]=\mathcal{G}[f]-\mathcal{L}[f],

where the gain and loss operators are respectively given by

(1.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}[f]

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{9}}\delta(\Sigma)\delta(\Omega)f_{2}f_{3}(f+f_{1})\,dv_{1}\,dv_{2}\,dv_{3},

(1.5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}[f]

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{9}}\delta(\Sigma)\delta(\Omega)ff_{1}(f_{2}+f_{3})\,dv_{1}\,dv_{2}\,dv_{3},

the resonant manifolds by

(1.6)

\displaystyle\Sigma

\displaystyle=v+v_{1}-v_{2}-v_{3},

(1.7)

\displaystyle\Omega

\displaystyle=|v|^{2}+|v_{1}|^{2}-|v_{2}|^{2}-|v_{3}|^{2},

and we use the standard notation
f:=f(x,v)
,
f_{i}=f(x,v_{i})
,
i=1,2,3
. Notice that both
\mathcal{G},\mathcal{L}
are positive operators and monotone for non-negative inputs.

Relabeling the variables, one can see that the collisional operator
\mathcal{C}[f]
satisfies the following weak formulation

(1.8)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\mathcal{C}[f]\phi\,dv=\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{12}}\delta(\Sigma)\delta(\Omega)f_{1}f_{2}f_{3}\left(\phi+\phi_{1}-\phi_{2}-\phi_{3}\right)\,dv_{1}\,dv_{2}\,dv_{3}\,dv,

where
\phi
is a test function appropriate for all the above integrations to make sense. Choosing
\phi\in\{1,v,|v|^{2}\}
and using the resonant conditions, one can formally see that a solution
f
to (
1.1
) conserves the total mass, momentum and energy i.e.

(1.9)

\partial_{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{6}}f\phi\,dv\,dx=0,\quad\phi\in\{1,v,|v|^{2}\}.

Now we focus on two ways of equivalently expressing the collisional operator that will be useful throughout the paper.

1.4.1.
Multilinear decomposition

Instead of further decomposing the gain and loss operators into multilinear terms in the obvious way, we will decompose them taking advantage of the symmetries of the equation. In our context, that simple observation is of crucial importance in controlling the nonlinear effects caused by the resonances.

More specifically, let us introduce the sharp cutoff
b=\mathds{1}_{(0,+\infty)}
. Then, we can write

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}[f]

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{9}}\delta(\Sigma)\delta(\Omega)f_{2}f_{3}(f+f_{1})b\left(\left(v-v_{1}\right)\cdot\left(v_{2}-v_{3}\right)\right)\,dv_{1}\,dv_{2}\,dv_{3}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{9}}\delta(\Sigma)\delta(\Omega)f_{2}f_{3}(f+f_{1})b\left(\left(v_{1}-v\right)\cdot\left(v_{2}-v_{3}\right)\right)\,dv_{1}\,dv_{2}\,dv_{3}

\displaystyle=2\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{9}}\delta(\Sigma)\delta(\Omega)f_{2}f_{3}(f+f_{1})b\left(\left(v-v_{1}\right)\cdot\left(v_{2}-v_{3}\right)\right)\,dv_{1}\,dv_{2}\,dv_{3},

where to obtain the last line we exchanged
v_{2}
with
v_{3}
in the second integrand and used symmetry of the product
f_{2}f_{3}
.

Hence, we can write

(1.10)

\mathcal{G}[f]=\mathcal{G}_{1}[f,f,f]+\mathcal{G}_{2}[f,f,f],

where
we use the multilinear notations

(1.11)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{1}[f,g,h]

\displaystyle:=2\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{9}}\delta(\Sigma)\delta(\Omega)f_{1}g_{2}h_{3}\,b\left(\left(v-v_{1}\right)\cdot\left(v_{2}-v_{3}\right)\right)\,dv_{1}\,dv_{2}\,dv_{3},

(1.12)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{2}[f,g,h]

\displaystyle:=2\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{9}}\delta(\Sigma)\delta(\Omega)fg_{2}h_{3}\,b\left(\left(v-v_{1}\right)\cdot\left(v_{2}-v_{3}\right)\right)\,dv_{1}\,dv_{2}\,dv_{3}.

With an identical argument, using the symmetry of the sum
f_{2}+f_{3}
instead, we can write

(1.13)

\mathcal{L}[f]=\mathcal{L}_{1}[f,f,f]+\mathcal{L}_{2}[f,f,f],

where

(1.14)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{1}[f,g,h]

\displaystyle:=2\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{9}}\delta(\Sigma)\delta(\Omega)fg_{1}h_{3}\,b\left(\left(v-v_{1}\right)\cdot\left(v_{2}-v_{3}\right)\right)\,dv_{1}\,dv_{2}\,dv_{3},

(1.15)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{2}[f,g,h]

\displaystyle:=2\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{9}}\delta(\Sigma)\delta(\Omega)fg_{1}h_{2}\,b\left(\left(v-v_{1}\right)\cdot\left(v_{2}-v_{3}\right)\right)\,dv_{1}\,dv_{2}\,dv_{3}.

It is evident that the operators
\mathcal{G}_{1},\mathcal{G}_{2},\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathcal{L}_{2}
are multilinear, positive, and increasing for non-negative inputs.

Under this notation, the collisional operator can be equivalently written as

(1.16)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}[f]

\displaystyle=\mathcal{G}_{1}[f,f,f]+\mathcal{G}_{2}[f,f,f]-\mathcal{L}_{1}[f,f,f]-\mathcal{L}_{2}[f,f,f].

Expression (
1.16
) will be useful for estimating the impact of the resonances in the equation.

1.4.2.
Collision frequency

Another important aspect for our context is that the loss operator is local in
v
, i.e. we have

(1.17)

\mathcal{L}[f]=f\mathcal{R}[f,f],

where

(1.18)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}[g,h]:=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{9}}\delta(\Sigma)\delta(\Omega)g_{1}(h_{2}+h_{3})\,dv_{1}\,dv_{2}\,dv_{3},

can be interpreted as the collision frequency. We note that
\mathcal{R}
is also a positive operator and increasing for non-negative inputs, however it is not linear in the second argument.

With this notation, the collisional operator
\mathcal{C}[f]
can be equivalently written as

(1.19)

\mathcal{C}[f]=\mathcal{G}[f]-f\mathcal{R}[f,f]

Expression (
1.18
) will be useful for exploiting the monotonicity properties of the equation.

Finally we record one last identity, proved exactly like (
1.10
)

(1.20)

\displaystyle f\mathcal{R}[g,g]=\mathcal{L}_{1}[f,g,g]+\mathcal{L}_{2}[f,g,g].

1.5.
Parametrization of the resonant manifolds

In this subsection we parametrize the resonant manifolds, and convert the operators to quantum Boltzmann type operators. This is possible due to the Laplacian dispersion relation. In other words, we can view the interaction of
4
waves with Laplacian dispersion relation in
d=3
as the collision between two incoming hard spheres. With this parametrization in hand, we will be able to address the weighted
L^{p}
boundness of the collisional operators.

Using Lemma A.7. from
[
9
]
, we may write

(1.21)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{1}[f,g,h]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}|v-v_{1}|f(v_{1})g(v^{*})h(v^{*}_{1})b\left(\left(v-v_{1}\right)\cdot\sigma\right)\,d\sigma\,dv_{1}

(1.22)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{2}[f,g,h]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}f(v)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}|v-v_{1}|g(v^{*})h(v^{*}_{1})b\left(\left(v-v_{1}\right)\cdot\sigma\right)\,d\sigma\,dv_{1}

(1.23)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{1}[f,g,h]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}f(v)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}|v-v_{1}|g(v_{1})h(v^{*}_{1})b\left(\left(v-v_{1}\right)\cdot\sigma\right)\,d\sigma\,dv_{1}

(1.24)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{2}[f,g,h]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}f(v)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}|v-v_{1}|g(v_{1})h(v^{*})b\left(\left(v-v_{1}\right)\cdot\sigma\right)\,d\sigma\,dv_{1},

where given
\sigma\in{\mathbb{S}}^{2}
we denote

(1.25)

\displaystyle v^{*}

\displaystyle=\frac{v+v_{1}}{2}+\frac{|v-v_{1}|}{2}\sigma,

(1.26)

\displaystyle v_{1}^{*}

\displaystyle=\frac{v+v_{1}}{2}-\frac{|v-v_{1}|}{2}\sigma.

One can easily verify that in accordance with the resonant conditions, momentum and energy is conserved i.e. given
\sigma\in{\mathbb{S}}^{2}
, we have

(1.27)

\displaystyle v^{*}+v_{1}^{*}

\displaystyle=v+v_{1},

(1.28)

\displaystyle|v^{*}|^{2}+|v_{1}^{*}|^{2}

\displaystyle=|v|^{2}+|v_{1}|^{2}.

Moreover, there hold the relations

(1.29)

\displaystyle|v^{*}-v_{1}^{*}|

\displaystyle=|v-v_{1}|,

(1.30)

\displaystyle(v^{*}-v_{1}^{*})\cdot\sigma

\displaystyle=-(v-v_{1})\cdot\sigma.

Finally, given
\sigma\in{\mathbb{S}}^{2}
the transformation
T_{\sigma}:(v,v_{1})\mapsto(v^{*},v_{1}^{*})
is a linear involution of
{\mathbb{R}}^{6}
.

1.6.
Organization of the paper

We start by proving trilinear bounds for the collision operator in Section
2
. This enables us to run a bootstrap-based argument to prove global well-posedness of (
1.1
) in Section
3
. As a byproduct of the proof we obtain that the solution satisfies dispersive estimates. It also shows propagation of
L^{1}_{xv}
– moments, from which we also deduce that solutions satisfy the natural conservation laws of mass, momentum and energy. We then study the asymptotic behavior of solutions, and show that they scatter in Section
4
. Moreover we establish various properties of the corresponding wave operators to describe which states can be reached by the solution. Finally we prove that the nonlinear evolution preserves positivity in Section
5
. We stress that this is a non-perturbative result which requires to exploit monotonicity properties of the collision operator.

2.
Weighted
L^{p}
boundedness of the collision operator

In this section we prove trilinear estimates for the operators that make up the nonlinearity.

We start by proving technical lemmas, chief among which a crucial angular averaging estimate, see Lemma
2.1
in the next section. These facts are then used to prove boundedness of the nonlinear terms on weighted Lebesgue spaces in the next sections.

2.1.
Preliminary results

We begin with the angular averaging lemma. This is key in controlling the linear growth of the hard-sphere kernel.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\gamma\in[0;2]
and

\displaystyle F(v,v_{1}):=\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}\frac{|v-v_{1}|^{\gamma}}{{\langle}v^{*}{\rangle}^{3}}\,d\sigma,\quad F_{1}(v,v_{1}):=\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}\frac{|v-v_{1}|^{\gamma}}{{\langle}v_{1}^{*}{\rangle}^{3}}\,d\sigma,

where

\displaystyle v^{*}

\displaystyle=\frac{v+v_{1}}{2}+\frac{|v-v_{1}|}{2}\sigma,

\displaystyle v_{1}^{*}

\displaystyle=\frac{v+v_{1}}{2}-\frac{|v-v_{1}|}{2}\sigma.

Then, we have

(2.1)

\displaystyle\|F(v,v_{1})\|_{L^{\infty}_{v,v_{1}}},\,\|F_{1}(v,v_{1})\|_{L^{\infty}_{v,v_{1}}}\lesssim 1.

Proof.

Fix
v,v_{1}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
.
Substituting
\sigma\mapsto-\sigma
we see that
F_{1}(v,v_{1})=F(v,v_{1})
, so it suffices to prove the claim for
F(v,v_{1})
.

Let us write

v^{*}=V+\frac{|u|}{2}\sigma,

where
V=\frac{v+v_{1}}{2}
and
u=v-v_{1}
. Let us also denote
E=|v|^{2}+|v_{1}|^{2}
.
We have

{\langle}v^{*}{\rangle}^{2}=1+|V|^{2}+\frac{|u|^{2}}{4}+|u||V|(\hat{V}\cdot\sigma).

Moreover,

|V|^{2}+\frac{|u|^{2}}{4}=\frac{1}{4}(|v+v_{1}|^{2}+|v-v_{1}|^{2})=\frac{|v|^{2}+|v_{1}|^{2}}{2}=\frac{E}{2},

thus

{\langle}v^{*}{\rangle}^{2}=1+\frac{E}{2}+|u||V|(\hat{V}\cdot\sigma).

Let us compute

I(v,v_{1})=\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}\frac{1}{{\langle}v^{*}{\rangle}^{3}}\,d\sigma.

Applying the spherical coordinates integration formula

\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}b(\hat{V}\cdot\sigma)\,d\sigma=2\pi\int_{0}^{\pi}b(\cos\theta)\sin\theta\,d\theta=2\pi\int_{-1}^{1}b(z)\,dz

for

b(z)=\left(1+\frac{E}{2}+|u||V|z\right)^{-3/2},

which is integrable in
[-1,1]
since

|u||V|=2\frac{|u|}{2}|V|\leq\frac{|u|^{2}}{4}+|V|^{2}=\frac{E}{2},

we obtain

\displaystyle I(v,v_{1})

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{1}{|u||V|}\left(\left(1+\frac{E}{2}-|u||V|\right)^{-1/2}-\left(1+\frac{E}{2}+|u||V|\right)^{-1/2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|u||V|}\frac{\left(1+\frac{E}{2}+|u||V|\right)^{1/2}-\left(1+\frac{E}{2}-|u||V|\right)^{1/2}}{\left(1+\frac{E}{2}-|u||V|\right)^{1/2}\left(1+\frac{E}{2}+|u||V|\right)^{1/2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\left(1+\frac{E}{2}-|u||V|\right)^{1/2}\left(1+\frac{E}{2}+|u||V|\right)^{1/2}\left(\left(1+\frac{E}{2}-|u||V|\right)^{1/2}+\left(1+\frac{E}{2}+|u||V|\right)^{1/2}\right)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{1+E},

using again
|u||V|\leq E/2
.

Now the above inequality, triangle inequality, and the fact that
\gamma\in[0,2]
yield

F(v,v_{1})=|v-v_{1}|^{\gamma}I(v,v_{1})\lesssim\frac{|v|^{\gamma}+|v_{1}|^{\gamma}}{1+|v|^{2}+|v_{1}|^{2}}\leq{\langle}v{\rangle}^{\gamma-2}+{\langle}v_{1}{\rangle}^{\gamma-2}\lesssim 1,

and (
2.1
) follows.
The proof is complete.
∎

We also record some better known results, starting with the following which was used in

]
. For convenience of the reader we provide a proof.

Lemma 2.2
.

For
\sigma\in{\mathbb{S}}^{2}
, we define the map

(2.2)

\displaystyle\nu=R_{\sigma}(u):=\frac{u}{2}+\frac{|u|}{2}\sigma.

Then
R_{\sigma}
is a diffeomorphism from
\{u\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}:u\cdot\sigma\neq-|u|\}
onto
\{\nu\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}:\nu\cdot\sigma>0\}
with inverse

(2.3)

\displaystyle u=\big(R_{\sigma}\big)^{-1}(\nu)=2\nu-\frac{|\nu|^{2}}{\sigma\cdot\nu}\sigma,

and Jacobian

(2.4)

\text{Jac}\,(R_{\sigma})^{-1}(\nu)=\frac{4|\nu|^{2}}{(\sigma\cdot\nu)^{2}}.

Also note that

(2.5)

\displaystyle|u|

\displaystyle=\frac{|R_{\sigma}(u)|^{2}}{(R_{\sigma}(u)\cdot\sigma)},

(2.6)

\displaystyle\hat{u}\cdot\sigma

\displaystyle=\bigg(\frac{2\big(R_{\sigma}(u)\cdot\sigma\big)^{2}}{|R_{\sigma}(u)|^{2}}-1\bigg).

Proof.

The fact that
R_{\sigma}
is a diffeomorphism from
\{u\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}:u\cdot\sigma\neq-|u|\}
onto
\{\nu\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}:\nu\cdot\sigma>0\}
with inverse given by (
2.3
) follows immediately by substitution.

In order to compute the Jacobian, we differentiate (
2.3
) in
\nu
, to obtain

D(R_{\sigma})^{-1}(\nu)=2I_{3}-\sigma\nabla^{T}f(\nu),

where
f(\nu)=\frac{|\nu|^{2}}{(\nu\cdot\sigma)}
. Thus, we obtain
1
1

1

we use the Linear Algebra identity
\det(\lambda I_{n}+vw^{T})=\lambda^{n}(1+\lambda^{-1}v\cdot w)
, where
v,w\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\lambda\neq 0
.

(2.7)

\text{Jac}(R_{\sigma})^{-1}(\nu)=\det\left(2I_{3}-\sigma\nabla^{T}f(\nu)\right)=8\left(1-\frac{1}{2}\nabla f(\nu)\cdot\sigma\right).

We readily compute

\nabla f(\nu)\cdot\sigma=\frac{2(\nu\cdot\sigma)^{2}-|\nu|^{2}}{(\nu\cdot\sigma)^{2}}=2-\frac{|\nu|^{2}}{(\nu\cdot\sigma)^{2}}

which, combined with (
2.7
), gives (
2.4
).

Finally, expression (
2.5
) follows directly by (
2.2
), and expression (
2.6
) follows by combining (
2.5
) and (
2.2
).
∎

We now deduce integrability estimates for post-collision quantities from this result. We stress that the presence of the cut-off

Lemma 2.3
.

The following estimates hold

(2.8)

\displaystyle\sup_{v\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}\,|\psi(v_{1}^{*})|b\left(\frac{v-v_{1}}{|v-v_{1}|}\cdot\sigma\right)\,dv_{1}\,d\sigma

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\psi\|_{L^{1}},

(2.9)

\displaystyle\sup_{v_{1}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}\,|\psi(v^{*})|b\left(\frac{v-v_{1}}{|v-v_{1}|}\cdot\sigma\right)\,dv\,d\sigma

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\psi\|_{L^{1}},

where we denote
v^{*}=\frac{v+v_{1}}{2}+\frac{|v-v_{1}|}{2}\sigma
,
v_{1}^{*}=\frac{v+v_{1}}{2}-\frac{|v-v_{1}|}{2}\sigma
.

Proof.

We first prove (
2.8
). Let

I(v)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}\,|\psi(v_{1}^{*})|b\left(\frac{v-v_{1}}{|v-v_{1}|}\cdot\sigma\right)\,dv_{1}\,d\sigma.

Notice that we can write
v_{1}^{*}=v-\frac{v-v_{1}}{2}-\frac{|v-v_{1}|}{2}\sigma
.
Setting
u=v-v_{1}
, and using Lemma
2.2
, we obtain

\displaystyle I(v)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}\left|\psi\left(v-\frac{u}{2}-\frac{|u|}{2}\sigma\right)\right|\,b(\hat{u}\cdot\sigma)\,du\,d\sigma

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|\psi(v-R_{\sigma}(u))\,|b\left(\frac{2\big(R_{\sigma}(u)\cdot\sigma\big)^{2}}{|R_{\sigma}(u)|^{2}}-1\right)\,du\,d\sigma

\displaystyle\simeq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|\psi(v-\nu)|\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}\frac{b\left(2(\hat{\nu}\cdot\sigma)^{2}-1\right)}{(\hat{\nu}\cdot\sigma)^{2}}\,d\sigma\,d\nu

\displaystyle\simeq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|\psi(v-\nu)|\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{b(\cos 2\theta)}{\cos^{2}\theta}\sin\theta\,d\theta\,d\nu

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|\psi(v-\nu)|\int_{[0,\pi/4]\cup[3\pi/4,\pi]}\frac{\sin\theta}{\cos^{2}\theta}\,d\theta\,d\nu

\displaystyle\simeq\|\psi\|_{L^{1}}.

The proof for (
2.9
) follows similarly by writing
v^{*}=v_{1}+\frac{v-v_{1}}{2}+\frac{|v-v_{1}|}{2}\sigma
.
∎

2.2.
The loss operators

We are now in a position to prove boundedness results for the collision operator on weighted Lebesgue spaces. We start with loss operators.

Lemma 2.4
.

We have, for
\mathcal{T}\in\{\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathcal{L}_{2}\}
and
p\geq 1
and
l\geq 0

(2.10)

\displaystyle\|\langle v\rangle^{l}\mathcal{T}[f,g,h]\|_{L^{p}_{v}}\lesssim\|\langle v\rangle^{l}f\|_{L^{p}_{v}}\|g\|_{L^{1}_{v}}\|\langle v\rangle^{3}h\|_{L^{\infty}_{v}}.

Moreover

(2.11)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{L}_{1}[f,g,h]\|_{L^{1}_{v}}\lesssim\|\langle v\rangle f\|_{L^{1}_{v}}\|\langle v\rangle g\|_{L^{\infty}_{v}}\|h\|_{L^{1}_{v}},

and

(2.12)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{L}_{2}[f,g,h]\|_{L^{1}_{v}}\lesssim\|\langle v\rangle f\|_{L^{\infty}_{v}}\|\langle v\rangle g\|_{L^{1}_{v}}\|h\|_{L^{1}_{v}}.

Proof.

For
p\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\|\langle v\rangle^{l}\mathcal{L}_{1}[f,g,h]\|_{L^{p}_{v}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\langle v\rangle^{l}f\|_{L^{p}_{v}}\Bigg\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}g(v_{1})|v-v_{1}|h(v_{1}^{*})\,d\sigma dv_{1}\Bigg\|_{L^{\infty}_{v}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\langle v\rangle^{l}f\|_{L^{p}_{v}}\|g\|_{L^{1}_{v}}\Bigg\|\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}\langle v_{1}^{*}\rangle^{3}h(v_{1}^{*})\frac{|v-v_{1}|}{\langle v_{1}^{*}\rangle^{3}}d\sigma\Bigg\|_{L^{\infty}_{v,v_{1}}}.

The result follows by Lemma
2.1
. The proof for
\mathcal{L}_{2}
is identical, substituting
v_{1}^{*}
by
v^{*}.

We now focus on proving (
2.11
). Let us denote
\widetilde{f}(v)={\langle}v{\rangle}f(v)
,
\widetilde{g}(v)={\langle}v{\rangle}g(v)
. We have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{L}_{1}[f,g,h](v)\|_{L^{1}_{v}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\widetilde{g}\|_{L^{\infty}_{v}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}\frac{|v-v_{1}|}{\langle v_{1}\rangle\langle v\rangle}|\widetilde{f}(v)|\,|h(v_{1}^{*})|\,b\big(\frac{v-v_{1}}{|v-v_{1}|}\cdot\sigma\big)\,dv_{1}d\sigma\,dv

\displaystyle\leq\|\widetilde{g}\|_{L^{\infty}_{v}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}|\widetilde{f}(v)|\,|h(v_{1}^{*})|\,b\big(\frac{v-v_{1}}{|v-v_{1}|}\cdot\sigma\big)\,dv_{1}d\sigma\,dv

\displaystyle\leq\|\widetilde{g}\|_{L^{\infty}_{v}}\|\widetilde{f}\|_{L^{1}}\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}\,|h(v_{1}^{*})|\,b\big(\frac{v-v_{1}}{|v-v_{1}|}\cdot\sigma\big)\,dv_{1}d\sigma\,dv\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{v}},

\displaystyle\leq\|\widetilde{f}\|_{L^{1}_{v}}\|\widetilde{g}\|_{L^{\infty}_{v}}\|h\|_{L^{1}_{v}},

where to obtain the second line we used the elementary fact

\displaystyle\frac{|v-v_{1}|}{{\langle}v{\rangle}{\langle}v_{1}{\rangle}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|v|+|v_{1}|}{{\langle}v{\rangle}{\langle}v_{1}{\rangle}}\leq\frac{1}{\langle v_{1}\rangle}+\frac{1}{\langle v\rangle}\leq 2,

and to obtain the last line we used (
2.8
) from Lemma
2.3
.

The proof of (
2.12
) is similar, pulling
\|\widetilde{f}\|_{L^{\infty}_{v}}
out first and then using (
2.9
).

∎

2.3.
The gain operators

In this subsection we prove boundedness results on Lebesgue spaces for the gain operators. The proofs are different from that of the previous subsection. In particular for the loss operators our proofs hold for super-hard potentials
|v-v_{1}|^{\gamma},\gamma\in(1;2]
. However this is not the case for the gain operators, see the proof of (
2.21
) below for example.

Lemma 2.5
.

For
p\geq 1
and
l\geq 0
, there holds the estimate

(2.13)

\|{\langle}v{\rangle}^{l}\mathcal{G}_{2}[f,g,h]\|_{L^{p}_{v}}\leq\|{\langle}v{\rangle}^{l}f\|_{L^{p}_{v}}\|{\langle}v{\rangle}g\|_{L^{\infty}_{v}}\|{\langle}v{\rangle}h\|_{L^{1}_{v}}

Proof.

Let us denote
\widetilde{g}(v)={\langle}v{\rangle}g(v)
,
\widetilde{h}(v)={\langle}v{\rangle}h(v)
. We take

\displaystyle\|{\langle}v{\rangle}^{l}\mathcal{G}_{2}[f,g,h]\|_{L^{p}_{v}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|{\langle}v{\rangle}^{l}f\|_{L^{p}_{v}}\left\|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}\frac{|v-v_{1}|}{{\langle}v^{*}{\rangle}{\langle}v_{1}^{*}{\rangle}}|\widetilde{g}(v^{*})||\widetilde{h}(v_{1}^{*})|b\left(\frac{v-v_{1}}{|v-v_{1}|}\cdot\sigma\right)\,dv_{1}\,d\sigma\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{v}}

\displaystyle\leq\|{\langle}v{\rangle}^{l}f\|_{L^{p}_{v}}\|\widetilde{g}\|_{L^{\infty}_{v}}\left\|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}\frac{|v-v_{1}|}{{\langle}v^{*}{\rangle}{\langle}v_{1}^{*}{\rangle}}|\widetilde{h}(v_{1}^{*})|b\left(\frac{v-v_{1}}{|v-v_{1}|}\cdot\sigma\right)\,dv_{1}\,d\sigma\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{v}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|{\langle}v{\rangle}^{l}f\|_{L^{p}_{v}}\|\widetilde{g}\|_{L^{\infty}_{v}}\left\|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}|\widetilde{h}(v_{1}^{*})|b\left(\frac{v-v_{1}}{|v-v_{1}|}\cdot\sigma\right)\,dv_{1}\,d\sigma\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{v}}

\displaystyle\leq\|{\langle}v{\rangle}^{l}f\|_{L^{p}_{v}}\|\widetilde{g}\|_{L^{\infty}_{v}}\|\widetilde{h}\|_{L^{1}_{v}},

where in the last two lines we used (
1.29
) to obtain

\frac{|v-v_{1}|}{{\langle}v^{*}{\rangle}{\langle}v_{1}^{*}{\rangle}}=\frac{|v^{*}-v_{1}^{*}|}{{\langle}v^{*}{\rangle}{\langle}v_{1}^{*}{\rangle}}\leq 2,

and (
2.8
) from Lemma
2.3
.
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

For
l\geq 0
, there holds the estimate

(2.14)

\displaystyle\|\langle v\rangle^{l}\mathcal{G}_{1}[f,g,h]\|_{L^{\infty}_{v}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{1}_{v}}\|\langle v\rangle^{l}g\|_{L^{\infty}_{v}}\|\langle v\rangle^{3}h\|_{L^{\infty}_{v}}+\|{\langle}v{\rangle}^{l}f\|_{L^{\infty}_{v}}\|{\langle}v{\rangle}g\|_{L^{\infty}_{v}}\|{\langle}v{\rangle}h\|_{L^{1}_{v}}

Proof.

We split the estimate into two different cases based on the magnitude of
|v|
and
|v_{1}|
.

Fix
\delta>0
,
and assume
|v_{1}|\leq\delta|v|
and
(v-v_{1})\cdot\sigma>0
.
Writing
V=v+v_{1}
,
u=v-v_{1}
, triangle inequality implies

|u|\geq(1-\delta)|v|
and
|V|\geq(1-\delta)|v|
. Moreover
V\cdot\sigma=(u+2v_{1})\cdot\sigma>2v_{1}\cdot\sigma\geq-2|v_{1}|\geq-2\delta|v|

\displaystyle 4|v^{*}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\Big|V+|u|\sigma\Big|^{2}=|V|^{2}+|u|^{2}+2|u|V\cdot\sigma

\displaystyle\geq 2(1-\delta)^{2}|v|^{2}-2\delta(1-\delta)|v|^{2}

\displaystyle=2(1-\delta)(1-2\delta)|v|^{2}

Choosing
\delta=1/4
we obtain
|v^{*}|\geq\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}|v|
.
Hence, we have

(2.15)

\mathds{1}_{|v_{1}|\leq|v|/4}b\left(\frac{v-v_{1}}{|v-v_{1}|}\cdot\sigma\right)\leq\mathds{1}_{|v^{*}|\geq\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}|v|}b\bigg(\frac{v-v_{1}}{|v-v_{1}|}\cdot\sigma\bigg).

We will estimate separately for
|v_{1}|>|v|/4
and for
|v_{1}|\leq|v|/4
. First, denoting
\widetilde{g}(v)={\langle}v{\rangle}g(v)
,
\widetilde{h}(v)={\langle}v{\rangle}h(v)

\displaystyle{\langle}v{\rangle}^{l}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}

\displaystyle\mathds{1}_{|v_{1}|>|v|/4}|v-v_{1}||f(v_{1})||g(v^{*})||h(v_{1}^{*})|b\big(\frac{v-v_{1}}{|v-v_{1}|}\cdot\sigma\big)dv_{1}d\sigma

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}|v-v_{1}||{\langle}v_{1}{\rangle}^{l}f(v_{1})||g(v^{*})||h(v_{1}^{*})|b\big(\frac{v-v_{1}}{|v-v_{1}|}\cdot\sigma\big)dv_{1}d\sigma

\displaystyle\leq\|{\langle}v{\rangle}^{l}f\|_{L^{\infty}_{v}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}\frac{|v-v_{1}|}{{\langle}v^{*}{\rangle}{\langle}v_{1}^{*}{\rangle}}|\widetilde{g}(v^{*})||\widetilde{h}(v_{1}^{*})|b\big(\frac{v-v_{1}}{|v-v_{1}|}\cdot\sigma\big)\,dv_{1}\,d\sigma

(2.16)

\displaystyle\leq\|{\langle}v{\rangle}^{l}f\|_{L^{\infty}_{v}}\|\widetilde{g}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\widetilde{h}\|_{L^{1}_{v}},

where the last line is obtained in the same way as estimate (
2.13

Now using (
2.15

\displaystyle{\langle}v{\rangle}^{l}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}

\displaystyle\mathds{1}_{|v_{1}|\leq|v|/4}|v-v_{1}||f(v_{1})||g(v^{*})||h(v_{1}^{*})|b\big(\frac{v-v_{1}}{|v-v_{1}|}\cdot\sigma\big)dv_{1}d\sigma

Combining (

Lemma 2.8
.

We have for

\displaystyle\|\langle v\rangle^{l}\mathcal{G}_{1}[f,g,h]\|_{L^{2}_{v}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{1}_{v}}\|\langle v\rangle^{l}g\|_{L^{2}_{v}}\|\langle v\rangle^{3/2}h\|_{L^{\infty}_{v}}+\|\langle v\rangle^{l}f\|_{L^{1}_{v}}\|g\|_{L^{2}_{v}}\|\langle v\rangle^{3/2}h\|_{L^{\infty}_{v}}.

\displaystyle I:=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\phi(v)|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}|f(v_{1})||g(v^{*})||h(v_{1}^{*})||v-v_{1}|b\big(\frac{v-v_{1}}{|v-v_{1}|}\cdot\sigma\big)d\sigma dv_{1}dv

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{1}_{v}}\Bigg\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\phi(v)|\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}|g(v^{*})||h(v_{1}^{*})||v-v_{1}|b\big(\frac{v-v_{1}}{|v-v_{1}|}\cdot\sigma\big)d\sigma dv\Bigg\|_{L^{\infty}_{v_{1}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{1}_{v}}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}_{v}}\Bigg\|\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}|g(v^{*})||h(v_{1}^{*})||v-v_{1}|b\big(\frac{v-v_{1}}{|v-v_{1}|}\cdot\sigma\big)d\sigma\Bigg\|_{L^{\infty}_{v_{1}}L^{2}_{v}},

where for the last line we used the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality in
v.

Next we use the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality in
\sigma
to write

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}|g(v^{*})||h(v_{1}^{*})||v-v_{1}|b\big(\frac{v-v_{1}}{|v-v_{1}|}\cdot\sigma\big)d\sigma

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\langle v\rangle^{3/2}h\|_{L^{\infty}_{v}}\bigg(\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}g^{2}(v^{*})b\bigg(\frac{v-v_{1}}{|v-v_{1}|}\cdot\sigma\bigg)d\sigma\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg(\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}\frac{|v-v_{1}|^{2}}{\langle v_{1}^{*}\rangle^{3}}b\bigg(\frac{v-v_{1}}{|v-v_{1}|}\cdot\sigma\bigg)d\sigma\bigg)^{1/2}.

Squaring this estimate, integrating in
v
and using (

) applied to

we find

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{1}_{v}}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}_{v}}\|\langle v\rangle^{3/2}h\|_{L^{\infty}_{v}}\|g\|_{L^{2}_{v}}\Bigg\|\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}\frac{|v-v_{1}|^{2}}{\langle v_{1}^{*}\rangle^{3}}b\bigg(\frac{v-v_{1}}{|v-v_{1}|}\cdot\sigma\bigg)d\sigma\Bigg\|_{L^{\infty}_{v,v_{1}}},

which yields the desired result with Lemma

Now for
l>0

b\bigg(\frac{v-v_{1}}{|v-v_{1}|}\cdot\sigma\bigg)\leq\left(\mathds{1}_{|v_{1}|>|v|/4}+\mathds{1}_{|v^{*}|\geq\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}|v|}\right)b\bigg(\frac{v-v_{1}}{|v-v_{1}|}\cdot\sigma\bigg).

Using the triangle inequality as well, we obtain

and the result follows from the case

.
∎

3.
Global dispersive solutions

In this section we construct global dispersive solutions to (
1.1
). Since the argument is perturbative, the free transport plays a fundamental role in studying the full nonlinear problem. We start by recalling some of its basic properties. We also define the functional analytic spaces used in our proof. Then we show local existence in a weighted
L^{\infty}
space. To continue our solutions for all times, we rely on a bootstrap argument, which boils down to proving dispersive, amplitude and integrability bounds. We conclude by showing that our analysis also yields propagation of
L^{1}_{xv}
– moments (even though these are not needed in the global existence proof), and conservation in the special cases of mass, momentum and energy.

3.1.
Free transport

The free transport semi-group is defined as follows:

Definition 3.1

(Free transport semi-group)
.

Let
f:{\mathbb{R}}_{x}^{3}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{v}^{3}\to{\mathbb{R}}
.
We denote

(3.1)

\displaystyle\big(\mathcal{S}(t)f\big)(x,v):=f(x-vt,v).

Remark 3.2
.

It is evident that the linear flow is measure preserving. In particular, for any
f\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}_{x}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3}_{v})
, there holds

(3.2)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\big(\mathcal{S}(t)f\big)(x,v)\,dx\,dv=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}f(x,y)\,dx\,dv,\quad\forall t\in{\mathbb{R}}.

Remark 3.3
.

An obvious, yet important, property of the linear semigroup is that it commutes with velocity weights:

(3.3)

{\langle}v{\rangle}^{l}(\mathcal{S}(t)f(x,v))=\mathcal{S}(t)({\langle}v{\rangle}^{l}f(x,v)),\quad\forall\,t\in{\mathbb{R}},\quad l\geq 0.

We now recall several well known properties of this semi-group, see e.g.
[
10
]
.
They consist in dispersive estimates and conservation of weighted
L^{p}
norms, which we prove for convenience of the reader.

Proposition 3.4
.

We have for
p\geq r\geq 1

(3.4)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{S}(t)f\|_{L^{p}_{x}L^{r}_{v}}\leq|t|^{-3\big(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{p}\big)}\|f\|_{L^{r}_{x}L^{p}_{v}}.

Moreover for
1\leq p\leq\infty
,
l\geq 0
, and all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

(3.5)

\displaystyle\|\langle v\rangle^{l}\mathcal{S}(t)f\|_{L^{p}_{xv}}

\displaystyle=\|\mathcal{S}(t)\langle v\rangle^{l}f\|_{L^{p}_{xv}}=\|\langle v\rangle^{l}f\|_{L^{p}_{xv}}.

Proof.

We first prove the dispersive bound (
3.4
). Consider
p\geq r\geq 1
and
t\neq 0
. Then, given
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
, we change variables
x^{\prime}=x-vt
to obtain

\|\mathcal{S}(t)f(x,v)\|_{L^{r}_{v}}=|t|^{-\frac{3}{r}}\left\|f\left(x^{\prime},\frac{x-x^{\prime}}{t}\right)\right\|_{L^{r}_{x^{\prime}}}.

Hence, changing variables
v^{\prime}=\frac{x-x^{\prime}}{t}
, and using Minkowski’s integral inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{S}(t)f(x,v)\|_{L^{p}_{x}L^{r}_{v}}=|t|^{-3\left(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{p}\right)}\|f(x^{\prime},v^{\prime})\|_{L^{p}_{v^{\prime}}L^{r}_{x^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq|t|^{-3\left(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{p}\right)}\|f(x^{\prime},v^{\prime})\|_{L^{r}_{x^{\prime}}L^{p}_{v^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=|t|^{-3\left(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{p}\right)}\|f(x,v)\|_{L^{r}_{x}L^{p}_{v}}.

Now (
3.5
) follows from (
3.3
) and (
3.2
).
∎

3.2.
Functional spaces and definition of a solution

For the rest of the paper let us fix
M>8
and
\frac{5}{2}<\alpha<M-\frac{3}{2}
.

Motivated by the free transport estimates of the previous section, we define the space of our initial data

(3.6)

X_{M,\alpha}=\{f\in{\langle}v{\rangle}^{-M}L^{\infty}_{xv}\cap L^{1}_{xv}:\|f\|_{X_{M,\alpha}}<\infty\},

where we denote

(3.7)

\|f\|_{X_{M,\alpha}}:=\|{\langle}v{\rangle}^{M}f\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}+\|f\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}+\sup_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}\langle t\rangle^{3/2}\big(\|{\langle}v{\rangle}^{\alpha}\mathcal{S}(t)f\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}+\|{\langle}v{\rangle}\mathcal{S}(t)f\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{1}_{v}}\big).

We note that
(X_{M,\alpha},\|\cdot\|_{X_{M,\alpha}})
is a Banach space.

Remark 3.5
.

X_{M,\alpha}
contains
\langle v\rangle^{-1}L^{1}_{x}L^{2}_{v}\big(\mathbb{R}_{x}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}_{v}^{3}\big)\cap\langle v\rangle^{-\alpha}L^{2}_{x}L^{\infty}_{v}\big(\mathbb{R}_{x}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}_{v}^{3}\big)\cap\langle v\rangle^{-M}L^{\infty}_{xv}\big(\mathbb{R}_{x}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}_{v}^{3}\big)\cap L^{1}_{xv}\big(\mathbb{R}_{x}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}_{v}^{3}\big)
which (taking
M=8^{+}
) itself contains
\langle x\rangle^{-3^{-}}\langle v\rangle^{-8^{-}}L^{\infty}_{xv}.

With the definition of
X_{M,\alpha}
in hand now give the definition of a solution to (
1.1
)

Definition 3.6
.

Let
I\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}
be an interval with
0\in I
, and
f_{0}\in X_{M,\alpha}
. We say that
f\in\mathcal{C}(I,{\langle}v{\rangle}^{-M}L^{\infty}_{xv})
is a solution of (
1.1
) in
I
with initial data

, if

(3.8)

f(t)=\mathcal{S}(t)f_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{S}(t-s)\mathcal{C}[f](s)\,ds,\quad\forall t\in I.

If
I={\mathbb{R}}
, we say that the solution

is global.

Remark 3.7
.

For
f\in\mathcal{C}(I,{\langle}v{\rangle}^{-M}L^{\infty}_{xv})
, we have

\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{S}(t-s)\mathcal{C}[f](s)\,ds\in{\langle}v{\rangle}^{-M}L^{\infty}_{xv},\quad\forall t\in I,

thus every term in (

) is a well-defined element of
{\langle}v{\rangle}^{-M}L^{\infty}_{xv}
.
Indeed, for
t\in I
, (
3.5
) and (
2.29
) imply

\displaystyle\left\|{\langle}v{\rangle}^{M}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{S}(t-s)\mathcal{C}[f](s)\,ds\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|t|\sup_{s\in I}\|{\langle}v{\rangle}^{M}f(s)\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}^{3}<+\infty

Remark 3.8
.

As stressed in the introduction, we work with strong solutions in this paper. This differs from other works in the literature
[
11
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
9
]
where, due to the functional analytic framework used, the less familiar concept of mild solution has to be introduced.

3.3.
Main result

We now state the main result of this section. Given
\varepsilon_{0}>0
, let us denote

(3.9)

B_{X_{M,\alpha}}(\varepsilon_{0}):=\{f\in X_{M,\alpha}\,:\,\|f\|_{X_{M,\alpha}}\leq\varepsilon_{0}\},

the closed ball of radius
\varepsilon_{0}
around the origin.

Theorem 3.9
.

There exists
\overline{\varepsilon}>0
and
A>6
such that if
0<\varepsilon_{0}<\overline{\varepsilon}
and
f_{0}\in B_{X_{M,\alpha}}(\varepsilon_{0}),
then (
1.1
) has a unique global solution
f\in\mathcal{C}({\mathbb{R}},{\langle}v{\rangle}^{-M}L^{\infty}_{xv})
which is dispersive, meaning it satisfies the bound

(3.10)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\big\{\langle t\rangle^{3/2}\|\langle v\rangle f(t)\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{1}_{v}}+\langle t\rangle^{3/2}\|\langle v\rangle^{\alpha}f(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}+\|f(t)\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}+\|\langle v\rangle^{M}f(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}\big\}<A\varepsilon_{0}.

Moreover the solution is continuous with respect to initial data in the
L^{1}_{xv}
topology i.e. if
f,g\in\mathcal{C}({\mathbb{R}},{\langle}v{\rangle}^{-M}L^{\infty}_{x,v})
are the solutions corresponding to
f_{0},g_{0}\in B_{X_{M,\alpha}}(\varepsilon_{0})
, there holds the estimate

(3.11)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}\|f(t)-g(t)\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|f_{0}-g_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}.

Remark 3.10
.

We elected to only prove continuity with respect to initial data for the
L^{1}_{xv}
norm for simplicity. Indeed the contraction estimate (
2.26
) is the most straightforward.

As a corollary of our analysis we show that these global solutions propagate
L^{1}_{xv}
– moments and conserve the total mass, momentum, and energy:

Corollary 3.11
.

Let
\bar{\varepsilon}
be as in the statement of Theorem
3.9
and consider
N>0
. There exists
\overline{\varepsilon}_{2}<\overline{\varepsilon}
independent of
N
such that if
0<\varepsilon_{0}<\overline{\varepsilon}_{2}
and
f_{0}\in B_{X_{M,\alpha}}(\varepsilon_{0})
is such that
\|\langle v\rangle^{N}f_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}<\varepsilon_{0},
then the global solution
f
given by Theorem
3.9
satisfies

(3.12)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\|\langle v\rangle^{N}f(t)\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}<2\varepsilon_{0}.

Moreover this control can be upgraded to conservation in the case where the weights are
1,v
or
|v|^{2}.
That is, for
\phi\in\{1,v,|v|^{2}\}
we have the conservation laws

(3.13)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\phi(v)f(t,x,v)\,dvdx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{6}}\phi(v)f_{0}(x,v)\,dvdx

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}.

Remark 3.12
.

We treat the full nonlinearity perturbatively, thus it is also possible to prove a lower bound of order
\varepsilon_{0}
for the moments. This cannot be done with methods traditionally used for the Boltzmann equation (as in
[
38
]
for example), which consist in combining control for the gain only equation with a Kaniel-Shinbrot iteration scheme.

3.4.
Local existence

We start by proving a local existence result for (
1.1
). Recall the notation introduced in (
3.7
), (
3.9
).

Theorem 3.13
.

There exists
\overline{\varepsilon}_{0}>0
such that for all
0<\varepsilon_{0}<\overline{\varepsilon}_{0},
and
f_{0}\in B_{X_{M,\alpha}}(\varepsilon_{0})
, equation (
1.1
) with initial data
f_{0}
has a unique solution
f\in\mathcal{C}([0;3],{\langle}v{\rangle}^{-M}L^{\infty}_{x,v})
. Moreover,
f
satisfies the dispersive estimate:

(3.14)

\sup_{t\in[0;3]}\big\{\langle t\rangle^{3/2}\|\langle v\rangle f(t)\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{1}_{v}}+\langle t\rangle^{3/2}\|\langle v\rangle^{\alpha}f(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}+\|f(t)\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}+\|\langle v\rangle^{M}f(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}\big\}\leq A_{0}\varepsilon_{0},

for some numerical constant
A_{0}>6
independent of
\varepsilon_{0}
.

Proof.

Consider
\bar{\varepsilon}_{0}>0
.
Given
0<\varepsilon_{0}<\bar{\varepsilon}_{0}
and
f_{0}\in B_{X_{M,\alpha}}(\varepsilon_{0})
, we define the mapping

\Phi:f(t)\longmapsto\mathcal{S}(t)f_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{S}(t-s)\mathcal{C}[f]\,ds,\quad t\in[0,3].

We show that
\Phi
is a contraction on

\bm{B}:=\left\{f\in\mathcal{C}([0;3],{\langle}v{\rangle}^{-M}L^{\infty}_{xv})\,:\,\sup_{t\in[0;3]}\|{\langle}v{\rangle}^{M}f(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}\leq 2\varepsilon_{0}\right\},

as long as
\bar{\varepsilon}_{0}
is small enough.

Stability:

Let
f\in\bm{B}
. Given
t\in[0,3]
, the triangle inequality, followed by (
3.5
) and the fact that
f_{0}\in B_{X_{M,\alpha}}(\varepsilon_{0})
yield

(3.15)

\displaystyle\|{\langle}v{\rangle}^{M}\Phi f(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon_{0}+\sum_{\mathcal{T}\in\{\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathcal{L}_{2},\mathcal{G}_{1},\mathcal{G}_{2}\}}\int_{0}^{t}\|{\langle}v{\rangle}^{M}\mathcal{T}[f,f,f](s)\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}\,ds.

Now, using (
2.29
), for
\mathcal{T}\in\{\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathcal{L}_{2},\mathcal{G}_{1},\mathcal{G}_{2}\}
we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\|{\langle}v{\rangle}^{M}\mathcal{T}[f,f,f](s)\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}\,ds\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\|{\langle}v{\rangle}^{M}f(s)\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}^{3}\,ds\leq 3\sup_{s\in[0;3]}\|{\langle}v{\rangle}^{M}f(s)\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}^{3}\lesssim\varepsilon_{0}^{3}<\varepsilon_{0},

since
\varepsilon_{0}<\bar{\varepsilon}_{0}
and
\bar{\varepsilon}_{0}
is sufficiently small.
Therefore by (
3.15
), we obtain

\sup_{t\in[0;3]}\|{\langle}v{\rangle}^{M}\Phi f(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}<2\varepsilon_{0}.

Moreover,
\Phi
is clearly continuous on
[0;3]
, hence
\Phi:\bm{B}\to\bm{B}
.

Contraction:

Let
f,g\in\bm{B}
. Given
t\in[0;3]
, trilinearity of
\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathcal{L}_{2},\mathcal{G}_{1},\mathcal{G}_{2}
implies

\displaystyle\big|\Phi f(t)

\displaystyle-\Phi g(t)\big|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\mathcal{T}\in\{\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathcal{L}_{2},\mathcal{G}_{1},\mathcal{G}_{2}\}}\bigg|\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{S}(t-s)\big(\mathcal{T}[f-g,f,f]+\mathcal{T}[g,f-g,f]+\mathcal{T}[g,g,f-g]\big)(s)\,ds\bigg|.

Hence, (
3.5
) followed by estimate (
2.29
) imply

\displaystyle\|{\langle}v{\rangle}^{M}\left(\Phi f(t)-\Phi g(t)\right)\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\|{\langle}v{\rangle}^{M}\left(f(s)-g(s)\right)\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\left(\|{\langle}v{\rangle}^{M}f(s)\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}^{2}+\|{\langle}v{\rangle}^{M}f(s)\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}\|{\langle}v{\rangle}^{M}g(s)\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}+\|{\langle}v{\rangle}^{M}g(s)\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}^{2}\right)\,ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon_{0}^{2}\|f-g\|_{\mathcal{C}([0;3],{\langle}v{\rangle}^{-M}L_{x,v}^{\infty})}<\frac{1}{2}\|f-g\|_{\mathcal{C}([0;3],{\langle}v{\rangle}^{-M}L_{x,v}^{\infty})},

since
\varepsilon_{0}<\bar{\varepsilon}_{0}
and
\bar{\varepsilon}_{0}
is sufficiently small.

We conclude that
\Phi
is a contraction of
\bm{B}
, hence the contraction mapping principle implies that (
1.1
) has a unique solution
f
in
[0;3]
with initial data
f_{0}
.

It remains to prove the dispersive bound (
3.14
).
By construction
f\in\bm{B}
, hence

(3.16)

\sup_{t\in[0;3]}\|{\langle}v{\rangle}^{M}f(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}\leq 2\varepsilon_{0}.

We now estimate
\|f(t)\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}
. For
t\in[0,3]
, Duhamel’s formula, the triangle inequality, (
3.5
), and the fact that
f_{0}\in B_{X_{M,\alpha}}(\varepsilon_{0})
imply

(3.17)

\displaystyle\|f(t)\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon_{0}+\sum_{\mathcal{T}\in\{\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathcal{L}_{2},\mathcal{G}_{1},\mathcal{G}_{2}\}}\int_{0}^{t}\big\|\mathcal{T}[f,f,f](s)\big\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}\,ds

Hence, for
\mathcal{T}\in\{\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathcal{L}_{2},\mathcal{G}_{1},\mathcal{G}_{2}\}
, estimates (
2.25
), (
3.16
) imply

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\big\|\mathcal{T}[f,f,f](s)\big\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}\,ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\|f(s)\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}\|{\langle}v{\rangle}f(s)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{1}_{v}}\|{\langle}v{\rangle}f(s)\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq 3\sup_{s\in[0;3]}\|f(s)\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}\|{\langle}v{\rangle}f(s)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{1}_{v}}\|{\langle}v{\rangle}f(s)\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon_{0}^{2}\sup_{s\in[0;3]}\|f(s)\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}<\frac{1}{2}\sup_{s\in[0;3]}\|f(s)\|_{L^{1}_{xv}},

since
\varepsilon_{0}<\bar{\varepsilon}_{0}
and
\bar{\varepsilon}_{0}
is sufficiently small. To obtain the second to last line, we used the inequality

\|{\langle}v{\rangle}u\|_{L^{1}_{v}}\leq\|{\langle}v{\rangle}^{-3^{-}}\|_{L^{1}_{v}}\|{\langle}v{\rangle}^{4^{+}}u\|_{L^{\infty}_{v}}\lesssim\|{\langle}v{\rangle}^{4^{+}}u\|_{L^{\infty}_{v}},

as well as (
3.16
).
This estimate combined with (
3.17
) implies

(3.18)

\sup_{t\in[0;3]}\|f(t)\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}<2\varepsilon_{0}.

In order to treat the term
{\langle}t{\rangle}^{-3/2}\|{\langle}v{\rangle}f(t)\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{1}_{v}}
, we prove a slightly more general fact that will be useful later on. Let
\beta
be such that
0\leq\beta<\frac{M-3}{2}
. For an arbitrary measurable function
u:{\mathbb{R}}_{x}^{3}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{v}^{3}\to{\mathbb{R}}
, we use Cauchy-Schwarz in
v
to write

\displaystyle\|\langle v\rangle^{\beta}u\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{1}_{v}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\langle v\rangle^{-3/2^{-}}\|_{L^{2}_{v}}\|\langle v\rangle^{\beta+3/2^{+}}u\|_{L^{2}_{xv}}

and

(3.19)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\langle v\rangle^{\beta+3/2^{+}}u\|_{L^{2}_{xv}}&\lesssim\|\langle v\rangle^{2\beta+3^{+}}u\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}^{1/2}\|u\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}^{1/2}\leq\|\langle v\rangle^{M}u\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}^{1/2}\|u\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}^{1/2},\end{split}

where for the last line we used the fact that
0\leq\beta<\frac{M-3}{2}
.
Now using (
3.16
), (
3.18
), we obtain

(3.20)

\displaystyle\|\langle v\rangle^{\beta}f(t)\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{1}_{v}}\lesssim\varepsilon_{0}.

Finally, we have

(3.21)

\displaystyle\|{\langle}v{\rangle}^{\alpha}f(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}\leq\|{\langle}v{\rangle}^{-3/2^{-}}\|_{L^{2}_{v}}\|{\langle}v{\rangle}^{\alpha+3/2^{+}}f(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}\lesssim\|{\langle}v{\rangle}^{M}f(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}\lesssim\varepsilon_{0},

where we used the fact that
\alpha<M-\frac{3}{2}
and (
3.16
).

Taking
A_{0}>6
large enough, the result follows.

∎

3.5.
Global existence

The extension of the solution to arbitrary large times relies on a bootstrap argument. The main bootstrap proposition is the following

Proposition 3.14
.

Let
\bar{\varepsilon}_{0}
and
A_{0}>6
be as in the statement of Theorem
3.13
. Then there is
0<\bar{\varepsilon}_{1}<\bar{\varepsilon}_{0}
with the following property:

If
f
is a solution of (
1.1
) in
[0,T]
with initial data
f_{0}\in B_{X_{M,\alpha}}(\varepsilon_{0})
, where
0<\varepsilon_{0}<\bar{\varepsilon}_{1}
and
T>3
, satisfying the bounds

(3.22)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0;T]}\langle t\rangle^{3/2}\|\langle v\rangle f(t)\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{1}_{v}}<A_{0}^{2}\varepsilon_{0},

(3.23)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0;T]}\langle t\rangle^{3/2}\|\langle v\rangle^{\alpha}f(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}<A_{0}^{2}\varepsilon_{0},

(3.24)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0;T]}\|f(t)\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}<A_{0}^{2}\varepsilon_{0},

(3.25)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0;T]}\|\langle v\rangle^{M}f(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}<A_{0}^{2}\varepsilon_{0},

then the improved estimates

(3.26)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0;T]}\langle t\rangle^{3/2}\|\langle v\rangle f(t)\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{1}_{v}}<A_{0}\varepsilon_{0},

(3.27)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0;T]}\langle t\rangle^{3/2}\|\langle v\rangle^{\alpha}f(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}<A_{0}\varepsilon_{0},

(3.28)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0;T]}\|f(t)\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}<A_{0}\varepsilon_{0},

(3.29)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0;T]}\|\langle v\rangle^{M}f(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}<A_{0}\varepsilon_{0},

hold.

Proof.

Consider
0<\bar{\varepsilon}_{1}<\bar{\varepsilon}_{0}
sufficiently small.
Let
0<\varepsilon_{0}<\bar{\varepsilon}_{1}
, and consider
f_{0}\in B_{X_{M,\alpha}}(\varepsilon_{0})
. Due to the uniqueness part of Theorem
3.13
, for
0\leq t\leq 3
the solution
f
coincides with the one obtained in Theorem
3.13
, thus it satisfies (
3.14
). Consequently (
3.26
)-(
3.29
) are automatically satisfied.

Hence, it suffices to prove (
3.26
)-(
3.29
) for
3<t\leq T
under the assumptions (
3.22
)-(
3.25
).
The next four subsections are dedicated to the proof of each of these improved estimates.

In the following, given
\mathcal{T}\in\{\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathcal{L}_{2},\mathcal{G}_{1},\mathcal{G}_{2}\}
, we denote

\displaystyle I_{\mathcal{T}}(t):=\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{S}(t-s)\mathcal{T}[f,f,f](s)\,ds,\quad 3<t\leq T.

Let us also denote
\varepsilon:=A_{0}^{2}\varepsilon_{0}
.

3.5.1.
Proof of (
3.26
)

By Duhamel’s formula, we obtain

(3.30)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\big\|\langle v\rangle f(t)\big\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{1}_{v}}\leq\|\langle v\rangle\mathcal{S}(t)f_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{1}_{v}}&+\sum_{\mathcal{T}\in\{\mathcal{G}_{1},\mathcal{G}_{2},\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathcal{L}_{2}\}}\|\langle v\rangle I_{\mathcal{T}}(t-1)\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{1}_{v}}\\
&+\sum_{\mathcal{T}\in\{\mathcal{G}_{1},\mathcal{G}_{2},\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathcal{L}_{2}\}}\|\langle v\rangle\big(I_{\mathcal{T}}(t)-I_{\mathcal{T}}(t-1)\big)\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{1}_{v}}.\end{split}

Fix
\mathcal{T}\in\{\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathcal{L}_{2},\mathcal{G}_{1},\mathcal{G}_{2}\}
.
Using the commutation identity (
3.3
) as well as the dispersive estimate (
3.4
) we find

\displaystyle\|\langle v\rangle I_{\mathcal{T}}(t-1)\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{1}_{v}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t-1}\frac{1}{(t-s)^{3/2}}\big\|\langle v\rangle\mathcal{T}[f,f,f](s)\big\|_{L^{1}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}ds.

Now using (
2.23
) and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality in
x
, we obtain

\displaystyle\big\|\langle v\rangle\mathcal{T}[f,f,f](s)\big\|_{L^{1}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\langle v\rangle f(s)\|_{L^{2}_{xv}}\|\langle v\rangle f(s)\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{1}_{v}}\|\langle v\rangle^{3}f(s)\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}\lesssim\langle s\rangle^{-3/2}\varepsilon^{3},\quad s\in[0,t-1].

where we used (
3.19
) and (
3.22
), (
3.25
).
Therefore,

\displaystyle\|\langle v\rangle I_{\mathcal{T}}(t-1)\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{1}_{v}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\varepsilon^{3}}{(t-s)^{3/2}}ds+\int_{1}^{t-1}\frac{\varepsilon^{3}}{(t-s)^{3/2}s^{3/2}}ds

(3.31)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{3}\left((t-1)^{-3/2}+t^{-3/2}\right)\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{-3/2}\varepsilon^{3}<\frac{A_{0}\varepsilon_{0}}{3}{\langle}t{\rangle}^{-3/2},

since
t>3
,
\varepsilon_{0}<\bar{\varepsilon}_{1}
, and
\bar{\varepsilon}_{1}
is sufficiently small.

For the term
I_{\mathcal{T}}(t)-I_{\mathcal{T}}(t-1)
, consider
\frac{3}{2}<q\leq\min\{\alpha-1,\frac{M-5}{2}\}
. We note that the interval for
q
is non-trivial because
\alpha>5/2
and
M>8
. Using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality in
v
, the commutation relation (
3.3
) and (
3.5
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|\langle v\rangle\big(I_{\mathcal{T}}(t)-I_{\mathcal{T}}(t-1)\big)\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{1}_{v}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t-1}^{t}\|{\langle}v{\rangle}^{-q}\|_{L^{2}_{v}}\|\langle v\rangle^{1+q}\mathcal{S}(t-s)\mathcal{T}[f,f,f](s)\|_{L^{2}_{xv}}\,ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{t-1}^{t}\|\langle v\rangle^{1+q}\mathcal{T}[f,f,f](s)\|_{L^{2}_{xv}}\,ds.

Then using (
2.23
), for
s\in[t-1,t]
, we have

\displaystyle\|

\displaystyle\langle v\rangle^{1+q}\mathcal{T}[f,f,f](s)\|_{L^{2}_{xv}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\langle v\rangle^{1+q}f(s)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}\|\langle v\rangle f(s)\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{1}_{v}}\|\langle v\rangle^{3}f(s)\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}+\|f(s)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}\|\langle v\rangle^{1+q}f(s)\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{1}_{v}}\|\langle v\rangle^{3}f(s)\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}.

Using (
3.23
) (since
1+q\leq\alpha
), (
3.22
) and (
3.25
), we have

\|\langle v\rangle^{1+q}f(s)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}\|\langle v\rangle f(s)\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{1}_{v}}\|\langle v\rangle^{3}f(s)\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}\lesssim{\langle}s{\rangle}^{-3}\varepsilon^{3}.

Moreover, we have

\|f\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}\|\langle v\rangle^{1+q}f(s)\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{1}_{v}}\|\langle v\rangle^{2}f(s)\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}\lesssim{\langle}s{\rangle}^{-3/2}\varepsilon^{3},

where we used (
3.23
) and (
3.25
) for the first and last term respectively, while for the second
second term, we used (
3.20
) (since
1+q<\frac{M-3}{2}
).

Hence, we obtain

(3.32)

\displaystyle\big\|\langle v\rangle\big(I_{\mathcal{T}}(t)-I_{\mathcal{T}}(t-1)\big)\big\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{1}_{v}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{3}\int_{t-1}^{t}{\langle}s{\rangle}^{-3/2}\,ds\leq{\langle}t-1{\rangle}^{-3/2}\varepsilon^{3}\lesssim{\langle}t{\rangle}^{-3/2}\varepsilon^{3}<\frac{A_{0}\varepsilon_{0}}{3}{\langle}t{\rangle}^{-3/2},

since
t>3
,
\varepsilon_{0}<\bar{\varepsilon}_{1}
, and
\bar{\varepsilon}_{1}
is sufficiently small.

Finally, by (
3.3
)-(
3.4
) we have

(3.33)

\displaystyle\|\langle v\rangle\mathcal{S}(t)f_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{1}_{v}}\leq t^{-3/2}\|\langle v\rangle f_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}<{\langle}t{\rangle}^{-3/2}\varepsilon_{0}<\frac{A_{0}\varepsilon_{0}}{3}{\langle}t{\rangle}^{-3/2},

since
t>3
and
A_{0}>6
.

Now putting together (
3.30
)-(
3.33
), we obtain

\big\|\langle v\rangle f(t)\big\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{1}_{v}}<{\langle}t{\rangle}^{-3/2}A_{0}\varepsilon_{0},

and (
3.26
) follows.

3.5.2.
Proof of (
3.27
)

Again, by Duhamel’s formula, we obtain

(3.34)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\big\|\langle v\rangle^{\alpha}f(t)\big\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}&\leq\|\langle v\rangle^{\alpha}\mathcal{S}(t)f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}+\sum_{\mathcal{T}\in\{\mathcal{G}_{1},\mathcal{G}_{2},\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathcal{L}_{2}\}}\|\langle v\rangle^{\alpha}I_{\mathcal{T}}(t-1)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}\\
&+\sum_{\mathcal{T}\in\{\mathcal{G}_{1},\mathcal{G}_{2},\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathcal{L}_{2}\}}\|\langle v\rangle^{\alpha}\big(I_{\mathcal{T}}(t)-I_{\mathcal{T}}(t-1)\big)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}.\end{split}

Fix
\mathcal{T}\in\{\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathcal{L}_{2},\mathcal{G}_{1},\mathcal{G}_{2}\}
.
First, we write

\displaystyle\|\langle v\rangle^{\alpha}I_{\mathcal{T}}(t-1)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t-1}\|\langle v\rangle^{\alpha}\mathcal{S}(t-s)\mathcal{T}[f,f,f]\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{2}_{x}}\,ds.

Using again (
3.3
)-(
3.4
) we find

\displaystyle\big\|\langle v\rangle^{\alpha}\mathcal{S}(t-s)\mathcal{T}[f,f,f](s)\big\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}

\displaystyle=\big\|\mathcal{S}(t-s)\langle v\rangle^{\alpha}\mathcal{T}[f,f,f](s)\big\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{(t-s)^{3/2}}\|\langle v\rangle^{\alpha}\mathcal{T}[f,f,f](s)\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{\infty}_{v}}.

Now, (
2.22
) followed by (
3.22
), (
3.25
) implies

\displaystyle\|\langle v\rangle^{\alpha}\mathcal{T}[f,f,f](s)\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{\infty}_{v}}\lesssim\|\langle v\rangle^{\alpha}f(s)\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}\|\langle v\rangle f(s)\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{1}_{v}}\|\langle v\rangle^{3}f(s)\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}\lesssim\langle s\rangle^{-3/2}\varepsilon^{3},

which after estimating as in (
3.31
) gives

(3.35)

\displaystyle\|\langle v\rangle^{\alpha}I_{\mathcal{T}}(t-1)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}\lesssim{\langle}t{\rangle}^{-3/2}\varepsilon^{3}<\frac{A_{0}\varepsilon_{0}}{3}{\langle}t{\rangle}^{-3/2},

since
\varepsilon_{0}<\bar{\varepsilon}_{1}
, and
\bar{\varepsilon}_{1}
is sufficiently small.

To deal with the term
I_{\mathcal{T}}(t)-I_{\mathcal{T}}(t-1)
, we bound as follows

\displaystyle\|\langle v\rangle^{\alpha}\big(I_{\mathcal{T}}(t)-I_{\mathcal{T}}(t-1)\big)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{t-1}^{t}\big\|\langle v\rangle^{\alpha}\mathcal{S}(t-s)\mathcal{T}[f,f,f](s)\big\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}\,ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{t-1}^{t}\big\|\mathcal{S}(t-s)\langle v\rangle^{\alpha+3/2^{+}}\mathcal{T}[f,f,f](s)\big\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}\,ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{t-1}^{t}\big\|\langle v\rangle^{\alpha+3/2^{+}}\mathcal{T}[f,f,f](s)\big\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}\,ds,

where for the last line we used (
3.5
).
Next, by (
2.22
) we obtain

\displaystyle\big\|\langle v\rangle^{\alpha+3/2^{+}}\mathcal{T}[f,f,f](s)\big\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\langle v\rangle^{\alpha+3/2^{+}}f(s)\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}\|\langle v\rangle f(s)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{1}_{v}}\|\langle v\rangle^{3}f(s)\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{2}\|{\langle}v{\rangle}^{5/2^{+}}f(s)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}

\displaystyle\lesssim{\langle}s{\rangle}^{-3/2}\varepsilon^{3},

where for the second line we used Cauchy-Scharz in
v
and (
3.25
) (since
\alpha<M-\frac{3}{2})
, and for the last line we used (
3.23
) (since
\alpha>5/2)
.
Hence

(3.36)

\displaystyle\|\langle v\rangle^{\alpha}\big(I_{\mathcal{T}}(t)-I_{\mathcal{T}}(t-1)\big)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}\lesssim\varepsilon^{3}\int_{t-1}^{t}{\langle}s{\rangle}^{-3/2}\,ds\leq{\langle}t-1{\rangle}^{-3/2}\varepsilon^{3}\lesssim{\langle}t{\rangle}^{-3/2}\varepsilon^{3}<\frac{A_{0}\varepsilon_{0}}{3}{\langle}t{\rangle}^{-3/2},

since
t>3
,
\varepsilon_{0}<\bar{\varepsilon}_{1}
, and
\bar{\varepsilon}_{1}
is chosen sufficiently small.

Finally, similarly as in (
3.33
) we have

(3.37)

\displaystyle\|\langle v\rangle^{\alpha}\mathcal{S}(t)f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}<\frac{A_{0}\varepsilon_{0}}{3}{\langle}t{\rangle}^{-3/2},

since
t>3
and
A_{0}>6
.
Now, (
3.34
)-(
3.36
) imply

\|\langle v\rangle^{\alpha}f(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}<\langle t\rangle^{-3/2}A_{0}\varepsilon_{0},

and (
3.27
) follows.

3.5.3.
Proof of (
3.28
)

By Duhamel’s formula, (
3.5
), and the fact that
f_{0}\in B_{X_{M,\alpha}}(\varepsilon_{0})
we find that

(3.38)

\displaystyle\|f(t)\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon_{0}+\sum_{\mathcal{T}\in\{\mathcal{G}_{1},\mathcal{G}_{2},\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathcal{L}_{2}\}}\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathcal{T}[f,f,f](s)\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}\,ds.

Next using (
2.25
) and we find for
\mathcal{T}\in\{\mathcal{G}_{1},\mathcal{G}_{2},\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathcal{L}_{2}\}

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{T}[f,f,f](s)\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f(s)\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}\|\langle v\rangle f(s)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{1}_{v}}\|\langle v\rangle^{3}f(s)\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f(s)\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}\|\langle v\rangle^{5/2^{+}}f(s)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}\|\langle v\rangle^{3}f(s)\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}

(3.39)

\displaystyle\lesssim\langle s\rangle^{-3/2}\varepsilon^{3},

where for the second to last line we used the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality in
v
, and for the last line we used (
3.24
), (
3.23
) (since
\alpha>5/2)
and (
3.25
).

Integrating (
3.39
), we obtain

(3.40)

\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathcal{T}[f,f,f](s)\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}\,ds\lesssim\varepsilon^{3}<\frac{A_{0}\varepsilon_{0}}{2},

since
\varepsilon_{0}<\bar{\varepsilon}_{1}
, and
\bar{\varepsilon}_{1}
is sufficiently small.
Combining (
3.38
), (
3.40
) we find

(3.41)

\displaystyle\|f(t)\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}<\varepsilon_{0}+\frac{A_{0}\varepsilon_{0}}{2}<A_{0}\varepsilon_{0},

since
A_{0}>6
. Estimate (
3.28
) follows.

3.5.4.
Proof of (
3.29
)

Finally to improve the last bootstrap assumption, we use again Duhamel’s formula, (
3.5
), and the fact that
f_{0}\in B_{X_{M,\alpha}}(\varepsilon_{0})
to write

\displaystyle\|\langle v\rangle^{M}f(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}\leq\|\langle v\rangle^{M}f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}+\sum_{\mathcal{T}\in\{\mathcal{G}_{1},\mathcal{G}_{2},\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathcal{L}_{2}\}}\int_{0}^{t}\|\langle v\rangle^{M}\mathcal{T}[f,f,f]\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}\,ds.

For fixed
\mathcal{T}\in\{\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathcal{L}_{2},\mathcal{G}_{1},\mathcal{G}_{2}\}
, we use (
2.22
) and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality in
v
to obtain,

(3.42)

\displaystyle\|\langle v\rangle^{M}\mathcal{T}[f,f,f](s)\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\langle v\rangle^{M}f\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}\|\langle v\rangle f(s)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{1}_{v}}\|\langle v\rangle^{3}f\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\langle v\rangle^{M}f\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}\|\langle v\rangle^{5/2^{+}}f(s)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}\|\langle v\rangle^{3}f\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\langle s\rangle^{-3/2}\varepsilon^{3},

and we can conclude as in (
3.41
) that
\|\langle v\rangle^{M}f(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}<A_{0}\varepsilon_{0},

since
\varepsilon_{0}<\bar{\varepsilon}_{1}
and
\bar{\varepsilon}_{1}
is sufficiently small. Estimate (
3.29
) follows.

The proof is complete.

∎

3.5.5.
Conclusion of the proof

Now with Proposition
3.14
in hand, we move to the proof of Theorem
3.9
. The argument is fairly standard, nonetheless we provide some details for the convenience of the reader.

Proof of Theorem
3.9
.

Let
\bar{\varepsilon}_{1},A_{0}
be as in the statement of Proposition
3.14
.
Setting
\bar{\varepsilon}=\frac{\bar{\varepsilon}_{1}}{A_{0}}
, consider
0<\varepsilon_{0}<\bar{\varepsilon}
and
f_{0}\in B_{X_{M,\alpha}}(\varepsilon_{0})
. We define

\displaystyle T^{*}

\displaystyle:=\sup\Big\{T>0:\text{ \eqref{KWE} has a solution $f$ with initial data $f_{0}$ in $[0;T]$, which satisfies \eqref{boot1imp}-\eqref{boot4imp}}\Big\}.

Since
f_{0}\in B_{X_{M,\alpha}}(\varepsilon_{0})
and
\varepsilon_{0}<\frac{\bar{\varepsilon}_{1}}{A_{0}}<\bar{\varepsilon}_{0}
, Theorem
3.13
implies that
T^{*}\geq 3
. Arguing by contradiction, we will show that
T^{*}=+\infty
. Indeed, assume that
T^{*}<+\infty
, and consider the solution
f
of (
1.1
) with initial data
f_{0}
satisfying (
3.26
)-(
3.29
), restricted to
[0,T^{*}-1]
. Then by (
3.29
) we have
\|{\langle}v{\rangle}^{M}f(T^{*}-1)\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}<A_{0}\varepsilon_{0}<\bar{\varepsilon}_{1}<\bar{\varepsilon}_{0}
. Applying Theorem
3.13
to (
1.1
) with initial data
f(T^{*}-1),

we may extend
f
up to
T^{*}+2
. Moreover, by (
3.14
) combined with the fact that (
3.29
) holds up to time
T^{*}-1
, we obtain

(3.43)

\sup_{t\in[0;T^{*}+2]}\|{\langle}v{\rangle}^{M}f(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}<A_{0}^{2}\varepsilon_{0}.

By Proposition
3.14
, bound (
3.43
) implies
(
3.29
) up to time
T^{*}+2
. Similarly we can show that (
3.26
)-(
3.28
) hold up to time
T^{*}+2
as well. However, this contradicts the maximality of
T^{*}
, thus
T^{*}=+\infty
.

Hence, (
1.1
) with initial data
f_{0}
has a solution
f
in
[0,+\infty)
, which satisfies the dispersive bound

(3.44)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\geq 0}\big\{\langle t\rangle^{3/2}\|\langle v\rangle f(t)\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{1}_{v}}+\langle t\rangle^{3/2}\|\langle v\rangle^{\alpha}f(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}+\|f(t)\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}+\|\langle v\rangle^{M}f(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}\big\}<A\varepsilon_{0},

where
A=4A_{0}
.
This solution is also unique by Theorem
3.13
.

Let us now briefly explain how to extend the solution to negative times. Consider the initial value problem

(3.45)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}g-v\cdot\nabla_{x}g=-\mathcal{C}[g],\quad t>0\\
g(t=0)=f_{0}\end{cases}

Since our argument for solving (
1.1
) was perturbative, we may apply an identical reasoning for (
3.45
) instead to obtain a unique solution
g\in\mathcal{C}([0;+\infty),{\langle}v{\rangle}^{-M}L^{\infty}_{xv})
which satisfies the dispersive bound

\sup_{t\geq 0}\big\{\langle t\rangle^{3/2}\|\langle v\rangle g(t)\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{1}_{v}}+\langle t\rangle^{3/2}\|\langle v\rangle^{\alpha}g(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}+\|g(t)\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}+\|\langle v\rangle^{M}g(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}\big\}<A\varepsilon_{0}.

We can then define

f(-t):=g(t)
for
t>0
. Then, one can easily see that
f\in\mathcal{C}({\mathbb{R}},{\langle}v{\rangle}^{-M}L^{\infty}_{xv})
is the unique global solution of (
1.1
) with initial data
f_{0}
, and that it satisfies (
3.10
).

Finally, we show continuity with respect to initial data in the
L^{1}_{xv}
topology. Assume
f,g
are the global solution of (
1.1
) with initial data
f_{0},g_{0}\in B_{X_{M,\alpha}}(\varepsilon_{0})
respectively.

By Duhamel’s formula and (
3.5
), for any
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
, we have

(3.46)

\displaystyle\|f(t)-g(t)\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}\leq\|f_{0}-g_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\mathcal{T}\in\{\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathcal{L}_{2},\mathcal{G}_{1},\mathcal{G}_{2}\}}\int_{-|t|}^{|t|}\big(\|\mathcal{T}[f-g,f,f](s)\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}+\|\mathcal{T}[g,f-g,f](s)\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}+\|\mathcal{T}[g,g,f-g](s)\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}\big)\,ds.

Now using (
2.26
) for the nonlinear part followed by (
3.10
), we find that for some numerical constant
C>0

(3.47)

\displaystyle\|f(t)-g(t)\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}

\displaystyle\leq\|f_{0}-g_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}+C\sup_{s\in{\mathbb{R}}}\|f(s)-g(s)\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{h_{1},h_{2}\in\{f,g\}}\sup_{s\in{\mathbb{R}}}\|\langle v\rangle^{3}h_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}\cdot\sup_{s\in{\mathbb{R}}}\langle s\rangle^{3/2}\|\langle v\rangle h_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{1}_{v}}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}{\langle}s{\rangle}^{-3/2}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\|f_{0}-g_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}+12A^{2}C\varepsilon_{0}^{2}\sup_{s\in{\mathbb{R}}}\|f(s)-g(s)\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}

\displaystyle\leq\|f_{0}-g_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}+\frac{1}{2}\sup_{s\in{\mathbb{R}}}\|f(s)-g(s)\|_{L^{1}_{xv}},

where to obtain the last line we used the fact that
\varepsilon_{0}<\bar{\varepsilon}
and the fact that
\bar{\varepsilon}
is sufficiently small. Estimate (
3.11
) clearly follows.

∎

3.6.
Propagation of
L^{1}_{xv}
– moments and conservation laws

In this section we prove Corollary
3.11
which shows that the global solution to (
1.1
) propagates
L^{1}_{xv}
– moments as long as they are initially small enough. Moreover, using the propagation of
L^{1}_{xv}
– moments, we show that the solution conserves the total mass, momentum, and energy.

Proof of Corollary
3.11
.

We first prove the propagation of
L^{1}_{xv}
– moments (
3.12
). Consider
0<\bar{\varepsilon}_{2}<\bar{\varepsilon}
sufficiently small. Let
0<\varepsilon_{0}<\bar{\varepsilon}_{2}
,
f_{0}\in B_{X_{M,\alpha}}(\varepsilon_{0})
and let
f
denote the global solution of (
1.1
) obtained in Theorem
3.9
.

Fix
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
. By Duhamel’s formula and (
3.5
) we find

(3.48)

\displaystyle\|\langle v\rangle^{N}f(t)\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}\leq\|\langle v\rangle^{N}f_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}+\sum_{\mathcal{T}\in\{\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathcal{L}_{2},\mathcal{G}_{1},\mathcal{G}_{2}\}}\int_{-|t|}^{|t|}\|\langle v\rangle^{N}\mathcal{T}[f,f,f](s)\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}\,ds.

Using (
2.24
), for
-|t|\leq s\leq|t|
we have

(3.49)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\langle v\rangle^{N}\mathcal{T}[f,f,f](s)\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}&\lesssim\|\langle v\rangle^{N}f\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}\|\langle v\rangle f(s)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{1}_{v}}\|\langle v\rangle^{3}f\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}\\
&\lesssim A^{2}\varepsilon_{0}^{2}\langle s\rangle^{-3/2}\sup_{s\in{\mathbb{R}}}\|\langle v\rangle^{N}f(s)\|_{L^{1}_{xv}},\end{split}

where for the last line we used the global bounds (
3.10
). Plugging that estimate into (
3.48
), we obtain for some constant
C>0

(3.50)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\langle v\rangle^{N}f(t)\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}&\leq\varepsilon_{0}+CA^{2}\varepsilon_{0}^{2}\cdot\sup_{s\in{\mathbb{R}}}\|\langle v\rangle^{N}f(s)\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}\,\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}{\langle}s{\rangle}^{-3/2}\,ds\\
&<\varepsilon_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\sup_{s\in{\mathbb{R}}}\|\langle v\rangle^{N}f(s)\|_{L^{1}_{xv}},\end{split}

since
0<\varepsilon_{0}<\bar{\varepsilon}_{2}
and
\bar{\varepsilon}_{2}
is sufficiently small. Since
t
was arbitrary, the above estimate implies (
3.12
).

Now we prove the conservation laws (
3.13
).

First, we note that for any
\mathcal{T}\in\{\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathcal{L}_{2},\mathcal{G}_{1},\mathcal{G}_{2}\}
and
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
, (
2.24
), (
3.11
) and (
3.10
) imply

\displaystyle\|{\langle}v{\rangle}^{2}\mathcal{T}[f,f,f](s)\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|{\langle}v{\rangle}f(s)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{1}_{v}}\|{\langle}v{\rangle}^{3}f\|_{L_{xv}^{\infty}}\|{\langle}v{\rangle}^{2}f\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}

\displaystyle\leq\|{\langle}v{\rangle}^{-3^{-}}\|_{L^{1}_{v}}\|{\langle}v{\rangle}^{4^{+}}f\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}\|{\langle}v{\rangle}^{3}f\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}\|{\langle}v{\rangle}^{2}f\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon_{0}^{3}.

Therefore
\mathcal{L}[f],\mathcal{G}[f]\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}},{\langle}v{\rangle}^{-2}L^{1}_{xv})
, hence
\mathcal{C}[f]=\mathcal{G}[f]-\mathcal{L}[f]\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}},{\langle}v{\rangle}^{-2}L^{1}_{xv})
as well.

Now fix
\phi\in\{1,v,|v|^{2}\}
and
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Relabeling variables, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\phi\,\mathcal{G}[f]\,dv\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{15}}\delta(\Sigma)\delta(\Omega)(\phi+\phi_{1})f_{1}f_{2}f_{3}\,dv\,dv_{1}\,dv_{2}\,dv_{3}\,dx,

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\phi\,\mathcal{L}[f]\,dv\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{15}}\delta(\Sigma)\delta(\Omega)(\phi_{2}+\phi_{3})f_{1}f_{2}f_{3}\,dv\,dv_{1}\,dv_{2}\,dv_{3}\,dx.

Hence

(3.51)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\phi\,\mathcal{C}[f]\,dv\,dx=\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{15}}\delta(\Sigma)\delta(\Omega)(\phi+\phi_{1}-\phi_{2}-\phi_{3})f_{1}f_{2}f_{3}\,dv\,dv_{1}\,dv_{2}\,dv_{3}\,dx=0,

since the integrand vanishes due to the resonant conditions.

Now, by Duhamel’s formula, Fubini’s theorem and (
3.51
) we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{6}}\phi(v)f(t,x,v)\,dv\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{6}}\phi(v)f_{0}(x-tv,v)\,dv\,dx+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\phi(v)\mathcal{C}[f](s,x+(s-t)v,v)\,dv\,dx\,ds

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{6}}\phi(v)f_{0}(x,v)\,dv\,dx+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\phi(v)\mathcal{C}[f](s,x,v)\,dv\,dx\,ds

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{6}}\phi(v)f_{0}(x,v)\,dv\,dx,

and the result is proved.

∎

4.
Scattering

In this section we study the asymptotic behavior of solutions constructed in Section
3
. We prove that they scatter, meaning that they behave like free transport as time reaches infinity. We then refine our understanding of the scattering states by studying properties of wave operators, thereby proving an asymptotic completeness result for (
1.1
). The upshot of our analysis is that the scattering state characterizes the evolution fully, and moreover that any reasonably fast decaying distribution near vacuum can be achieved as a scattering state of (
1.1
).

The results are mostly obtained as a byproduct of the dispersive analysis carried out in Section
3
. As a result many of the arguments are similar, therefore we will omit some details in the proofs.

4.1.
Scattering states

We start by constructing the scattering states.
Recall the definition of the set
B_{X_{M,\alpha}}(\varepsilon_{0})
given in (
3.9
).

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
f_{0}\in B_{X_{M,\alpha}}(\varepsilon_{0}),
where
0<\varepsilon_{0}<\overline{\varepsilon}
(given in Theorem
3.9
).

Then there exist two unique functions
f_{-\infty},f_{+\infty}\in L^{1}_{xv}\cap\langle v\rangle^{-M}L^{\infty}_{xv}
such that if
f(t)
denotes the global solution to (
1.1
) with initial data
f_{0}
, then

(4.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|\langle v\rangle^{M}\big(f(t)-\mathcal{S}(t)f_{+\infty}\big)\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}+\|f(t)-\mathcal{S}(t)f_{+\infty}\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}\longrightarrow_{t\to\infty}0\\
\end{split}

and

(4.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\|\langle v\rangle^{M}\big(f(t)-\mathcal{S}(t)f_{-\infty}\big)\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}+\|f(t)-\mathcal{S}(t)f_{-\infty}\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}\longrightarrow_{t\to-\infty}0.\\
\end{split}

Remark 4.2
.

As a byproduct of the proof we obtain the decay rate of
t^{-1/2}
for the convergence in (
4.1
) and (
4.2
).

Proof.

Notice that as a direct consequence of (
3.39
) and (
3.10
),
for any
s>0
, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{S}(-s)\mathcal{C}[f](s)\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}

\displaystyle\lesssim{\langle}s{\rangle}^{-3/2}\sup_{s\in\mathbb{R}}\left\{\|f\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}\cdot\|{\langle}v{\rangle}^{M}f\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}\cdot\langle s\rangle^{3/2}\|{\langle}v{\rangle}^{\alpha}f(s)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}\right\}

(4.3)

\displaystyle\leq A^{3}\varepsilon_{0}^{3}{\langle}s{\rangle}^{-3/2},

hence, for any
t>0
, we have

(4.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathcal{S}(-s)\mathcal{C}[f](s)\|_{L^{1}_{x,v}}\,ds\lesssim A^{3}\varepsilon_{0}^{3}\int_{0}^{\infty}{\langle}s{\rangle}^{-3/2}\,ds<\infty,\end{split}

thus
\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{S}(-s)\mathcal{C}[f](s)\,ds
is well-defined in
L^{1}_{xv}
for all
t>0
.

Now, considering
1<t_{1}<t_{2}
, and integrating (
4.3
), we have

(4.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\|\mathcal{S}(-s)\mathcal{C}[f](s)\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}\,ds\lesssim A^{3}\varepsilon_{0}^{3}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\frac{ds}{{\langle}s{\rangle}^{3/2}}\leq A^{3}\varepsilon_{0}^{3}\,t_{1}^{-1/2}\longrightarrow_{t_{1},t_{2}\to+\infty}0.\end{split}

Therefore
\big(\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{S}(-s)\mathcal{C}[f]\,ds\big)_{t}
is Cauchy in
L^{1}_{xv}
, hence its limit as
t\to+\infty
belongs to
L^{1}_{xv}
. Let us define

(4.6)

\displaystyle f_{+\infty}:=f_{0}+\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{S}(-s)\mathcal{C}[f](s)\,ds\in L^{1}_{xv}.

Then using Duhamel’s formula, (
3.5
), and (
4.3
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|f(t)-\mathcal{S}(t)f_{+\infty}\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t}^{\infty}\|\mathcal{S}(t-s)\mathcal{C}[f](s)\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}\,ds\lesssim A^{3}\varepsilon_{0}^{3}\,t^{-1/2}\longrightarrow_{t\to+\infty}0.

Similarly, using (
3.42
) instead of (
3.39
), we obtain that
f_{+\infty}\in\langle v\rangle^{-M}L^{\infty}_{xv}
, and that
\|\langle v\rangle^{M}\big(f(t)-\mathcal{S}(t)f_{+\infty}\big)\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}\longrightarrow_{t\to+\infty}0
.

Uniqueness of the scattering state
f_{+\infty}
follows by uniqueness of the limit in either
L^{1}_{xv}
or
{\langle}v{\rangle}^{-M}L^{\infty}_{xv}
.

In the exact same way we can show that there exists a unique scattering state as
t\to-\infty
given by

(4.7)

\displaystyle f_{-\infty}:=f_{0}-\int_{-\infty}^{0}\mathcal{S}(-s)\mathcal{C}[f]\,ds

that satisfies (
4.2
).
∎

As a consequence of Proposition
4.1
(in particular the uniqueness of the scattering states), for
0<\bar{\varepsilon}_{0}<\bar{\varepsilon}
, we can define the mappings

(4.8)

\displaystyle U_{+}:\begin{cases}B_{X_{M,\alpha}}(\varepsilon_{0})&\longrightarrow U_{+}\big(B_{X_{M,\alpha}}(\varepsilon_{0})\big)\\
f_{0}&\longmapsto f_{+\infty}\end{cases}

and

(4.9)

\displaystyle U_{-}:\begin{cases}B_{X_{M,\alpha}}(\varepsilon_{0})&\longrightarrow U_{-}\big(B_{X_{M,\alpha}}(\varepsilon_{0})\big)\\
f_{0}&\longmapsto f_{-\infty}\end{cases}.

4.2.
Continuity of
U_{+},U_{-}

Here, we prove that the scattering maps are continuous.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
0<\varepsilon_{0}<\bar{\varepsilon}
, where
\bar{\varepsilon}
is given in the statement of Theorem
3.9
. Then the operators

\displaystyle U_{\pm}:\begin{cases}\big(B_{X_{M,\alpha}}(\varepsilon_{0}),\|\cdot\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}\big)&\longrightarrow\big(U_{\pm}\big(B_{X_{M,\alpha}}(\varepsilon_{0})\big),\|\cdot\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}\big)\\
f_{0}&\longmapsto f_{\pm\infty}\end{cases}

are Lipschitz continuous. More precisely,

\displaystyle\|U_{\pm}f_{0}-U_{\pm}g_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}\leq 2\|f_{0}-g_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{xv}},\quad\forall\,f_{0},g_{0}\in B_{X_{M,\alpha}}(\varepsilon_{0}).

Proof.

We carry out the proof for
U_{+},
the other case being similar.

Let
0<\varepsilon_{0}<\bar{\varepsilon}
,
f_{0},g_{0}\in B_{X_{M,\alpha}}(\varepsilon_{0})
and
f,g
be the solutions constructed using Theorem
3.9
with initial data
f_{0}
and
g_{0}
respectively.

This implies that for any
t>0

\displaystyle\|U_{+}f_{0}-U_{+}g_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}

\displaystyle=\|\mathcal{S}(t)U_{+}f_{0}-\mathcal{S}(t)U_{+}g_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}

\displaystyle\leq\|f(t)-g(t)\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}+\|f(t)-\mathcal{S}(t)U_{+}f_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}+\|g(t)-\mathcal{S}(t)U_{+}g_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|f_{0}-g_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}+\|f(t)-\mathcal{S}(t)U_{+}f_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}+\|g(t)-\mathcal{S}(t)U_{+}g_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{xv}},

where to obtain the last line we used (
3.11
). Letting
t\to+\infty
and using (
4.1
), the result follows.

∎

4.3.
Injectivity of
U_{+},U_{-}

Now, we prove that the scattering maps are injective, i.e. different initial data scatter to different states.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\bar{\varepsilon},A
be as in the statement of Theorem
3.9
. Then, there exists
0<\bar{\varepsilon}_{3}<\bar{\varepsilon}
such that for
0<\varepsilon_{0}<\bar{\varepsilon}_{3}
, the operators
U_{\pm}:B_{X_{M,\alpha}}(\varepsilon_{0})\rightarrow U_{\pm}\big(B_{X_{M,\alpha}}(\varepsilon_{0})\big)
are injective.

Proof.

Consider
0<\bar{\varepsilon}_{3}<\bar{\varepsilon}
sufficiently small, and
0<\varepsilon_{0}<\bar{\varepsilon}_{3}
.
Let
f_{0},g_{0}\in B_{X_{M}}(\varepsilon_{0})
be such that
U_{+}f_{0}=U_{+}g_{0}.
Then by definition of
U_{+}
(
4.6
), we have

|f_{0}-g_{0}|=\bigg|\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{S}(-s)(\mathcal{C}[g](s)-\mathcal{C}[f](s))\,ds\bigg|.

Hence, using (
3.5
) followed by (
2.26
), (
3.10
) and (
3.11
) we obtain

\displaystyle\|f_{0}-g_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}\leq\int_{0}^{+\infty}\|\mathcal{C}[f](s)-\mathcal{C}[g](s)\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\mathcal{T}\in\{\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathcal{L}_{2},\mathcal{G}_{1},\mathcal{G}_{2}\}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\big(\|\mathcal{T}[f-g,f,f](s)\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}+\|\mathcal{T}[g,f-g,f](s)\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}+\|\mathcal{T}[g,g,f-g](s)\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}\big)\,ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{s\in[0;t]}\|f(s)-g(s)\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}\sum_{h_{1},h_{2}\in\{f,g\}}\sup_{s\in{\mathbb{R}}}\|\langle v\rangle^{3}h_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}\cdot\sup_{s\in{\mathbb{R}}}\langle s\rangle^{3/2}\|\langle v\rangle h_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{1}_{v}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}{\langle}s{\rangle}^{-3/2}\,ds

\displaystyle\lesssim 2A^{2}\varepsilon_{0}^{2}\|f_{0}-g_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}<\frac{1}{2}\|f_{0}-g_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{xv}},

since
\varepsilon_{0}<\bar{\varepsilon}_{3}
, and
\bar{\varepsilon}_{3}
is sufficiently small. We conclude that
f_{0}=g_{0}
, thus
U_{+}
is injective.

The reasoning for the
U_{-}
is similar, thus we omit the details.

∎

4.4.
Surjectivity of
U_{+},U_{-}

We finally prove that the scattering maps are surjective. In other words any appropriately decaying function can be viewed as a scattering state.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
\bar{\varepsilon}
be as in Theorem
3.9
. Then, there exists
0<\overline{\varepsilon}_{4}<\frac{\bar{\varepsilon}}{2}
such that for any
0<\varepsilon_{0}<\bar{\varepsilon}_{4}
and any
f_{+\infty}\in B_{X_{M,\alpha}}(\varepsilon_{0})
, there exists unique
f_{0}\in B_{X_{M,\alpha}}(2\varepsilon_{0})
such that the global solution
f
to (
1.1
) with initial data
f_{0}
satisfies

(4.10)

\displaystyle\|f(t)-\mathcal{S}(t)f_{+\infty}\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}+\big\|{\langle}v{\rangle}^{M}\big(f(t)-\mathcal{S}(t)f_{+\infty}\big)\big\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}\longrightarrow_{t\to+\infty}0.

Remark 4.6
.

A similar statement holds for
t\to-\infty.

Proof.

Consider
0<\bar{\varepsilon}_{4}<\frac{\bar{\varepsilon}}{2}
sufficiently small and let
0<\varepsilon_{0}<\bar{\varepsilon}_{4}
. Given
f_{+\infty}\in B_{X_{M,\alpha}}(\varepsilon_{0})

we define the map

(4.11)

\displaystyle\Phi:f\mapsto\mathcal{S}(t)f_{+\infty}-\int_{t}^{+\infty}\mathcal{S}(t-s)\mathcal{C}[f]\,ds.

To prove the result, it suffices to show that
\Phi
is a contraction on the ball

\bm{\widetilde{B}}:=\Big\{f\in\mathcal{C}([0;+\infty),X_{M,\alpha})\,:\,\sup_{t\geq 0}\|f(t)\|_{X_{M,\alpha}}\leq 2\varepsilon_{0}\Big\}.

Indeed, if that is the case, the contraction mapping principle implies that
\Phi
has a unique fixed point
f\in\bm{\widetilde{B}}
i.e.

(4.12)

f(t)=\mathcal{S}(t)f_{+\infty}-\int_{t}^{+\infty}\mathcal{S}(t-s)\mathcal{C}[f](s)\,ds,\quad\forall t\geq 0.

Defining
f_{0}=f(0)
, we take that
f_{0}\in B_{X_{M}}(2\varepsilon_{0})
. Then filtering (
4.12
) by
\mathcal{S}(-t)
, we take that
f_{+\infty}=U_{+}f_{0}
which is equivalent to (
4.10
).

Hence, we now focus on proving that
\Phi
is a contraction of
\bm{\widetilde{B}}
.

Stability:

Let
f\in\bm{\widetilde{B}}
. Given
\mathcal{T}\in\{\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathcal{L}_{2},\mathcal{G}_{1},\mathcal{G}_{2}\}
, let us denote

\displaystyle J_{\mathcal{T}}(t):=\int_{t}^{+\infty}\mathcal{S}(t-s)\mathcal{T}[f,f,f]\,ds.

Since
f_{+\infty}\in B_{X_{M,\alpha}}(\varepsilon_{0})
, triangle inequality implies

(4.13)

\|\Phi f(t)\|_{X_{M,\alpha}}\leq\varepsilon_{0}+\sum_{\mathcal{T}\in\{\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathcal{L}_{2},\mathcal{G}_{1},\mathcal{G}_{2}\}}\|J_{\mathcal{T}}(t)\|_{X_{M,\alpha}}.

Fix
\mathcal{T}\in\{\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathcal{L}_{2},\mathcal{G}_{1},\mathcal{G}_{2}\}
and
t\geq 0
.
Using (
2.25
) and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality in
v
we obtain

\displaystyle\|J_{\mathcal{T}}(t)\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{t}^{+\infty}\|\mathcal{T}[f,f,f]\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}\,ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{s\geq t}\langle s\rangle^{3/2}\|\langle v\rangle^{\alpha}f(s)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}\cdot\sup_{s\in[t;+\infty)}\|f(s)\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\times\sup_{s\geq t}\|\langle v\rangle f(s)\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}\int_{t}^{+\infty}\langle s\rangle^{-3/2}\,ds

(4.14)

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon_{0}^{3}<\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{4},

since
\varepsilon_{0}<\bar{\varepsilon}_{4}
, and
\bar{\varepsilon}_{4}
is sufficiently small.

Similarly, using (
2.22
) instead, we obtain

(4.15)

\|\langle v\rangle^{M}J_{\mathcal{T}}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}\lesssim\varepsilon_{0}^{3}<\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{4},

since
\varepsilon_{0}<\bar{\varepsilon}_{4}
, and
\bar{\varepsilon}_{4}
is sufficiently small.

For
\|\langle v\rangle^{\alpha}J_{\mathcal{T}}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}
, we decompose

(4.16)

\displaystyle\|\langle v\rangle^{\alpha}J_{\mathcal{T}}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}\leq\|\langle v\rangle^{\alpha}J_{\mathcal{T}}(t+1)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}+\big\|\langle v\rangle^{\alpha}\big(J_{\mathcal{T}}(t)-J_{\mathcal{T}}(t+1)\big)\big\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}.

To estimate the bulk term, we use (
3.3
)-(
3.4
), as well as (
2.22
) to obtain

\displaystyle\|\langle v\rangle^{\alpha}J_{\mathcal{T}}(t+1)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t+1}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{(s-t)^{3/2}}\|\langle v\rangle^{\alpha}\mathcal{T}[f,f,f]\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{\infty}_{v}}\,ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{s\geq t+1}\langle s\rangle^{3/2}\|\langle v\rangle f(s)\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{1}_{v}}\cdot\big(\sup_{s\geq t+1}\|\langle v\rangle^{3}f(s)\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}\big)^{2}

\displaystyle\times\int_{t+1}^{+\infty}\frac{ds}{(s-t)^{3/2}\langle s\rangle^{3/2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim{\langle}t{\rangle}^{-3/2}\varepsilon_{0}^{3}.

We may also bound in the same way

\displaystyle\big\|\langle v\rangle^{\alpha}\big(J_{\mathcal{T}}(t)-J_{\mathcal{T}}(t+1)\big)\big\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon_{0}^{3}\int_{t}^{t+1}\frac{ds}{\langle s\rangle^{3/2}}\lesssim{\langle}t{\rangle}^{-3/2}\varepsilon_{0}^{3}.

By (
4.16
) we obtain

(4.17)

\|\langle v\rangle^{\alpha}J_{\mathcal{T}}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}\lesssim{\langle}t{\rangle}^{-3/2}\varepsilon_{0}^{3}<{\langle}t{\rangle}^{-3/2}\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{4},

since
\varepsilon_{0}<\bar{\varepsilon}_{4}
and
\bar{\varepsilon}_{4}
is sufficiently small.

Finally, by a similar argument using (
2.23
) instead, obtain

(4.18)

\|\langle v\rangle J_{\mathcal{T}}(t)\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{1}_{v}}\lesssim{\langle}t{\rangle}^{-3/2}\varepsilon_{0}^{3}<{\langle}t{\rangle}^{-3/2}\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{4},

since
\varepsilon_{0}<\bar{\varepsilon}_{4}
and
\bar{\varepsilon}_{4}
is sufficiently small.

Combining (
4.13
), (
4.14
), (
4.15
), (
4.17
), (
4.18
), we obtain

\|\Phi f\|_{\mathcal{C}([0;+\infty),X_{M,\alpha})}<2\varepsilon_{0}.

Moreover
\Phi f
is clearly continuous in time, hence
\Phi:\bm{B}\to\bm{B}
.

Contraction:

The proof is almost identical to stability, relying this time on Lemma
2.10
instead, therefore we omit the details of the proof. For
f,g\in\bm{\widetilde{B}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\Phi f-\Phi g\|_{\mathcal{C}([0;+\infty),X_{M,\alpha})}\lesssim\varepsilon_{0}^{2}\|f-g\|_{\mathcal{C}([0;+\infty),X_{M,\alpha})}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|f-g\|_{\mathcal{C}([0;+\infty),X_{M,\alpha})},

since
\varepsilon_{0}<\bar{\varepsilon}_{4}
, and
\bar{\varepsilon}_{4}
is sufficiently small.

The result is proved.
∎

5.
Positivity of the solution

In this section we show that (
1.1
) preserves positivity forward in time i.e. for positive initial data, the solution obtained in Theorem
3.9
remains positive for
t\geq 0
. This comes in agreement with the fact that the solution represents an energy spectrum. In order to show positivity, we will rely on the monotonicity properties of the equation. Namely, we will employ a classical tool from the kinetic theory of particles, the so-called Kaniel-Shinbrot
[
40
]
iteration scheme. The idea is to approximate the solution of the equation with positive subsolutions and supersolutions, and pass to the limit to retrieve the global solution of the nonlinear equation (
1.1
). Unlike classical applications of the Kaniel-Shinbrot
[
40
]
iteration, we will need to show that the iterates satify dispersive bounds as well. This can be achieved by initializing the scheme properly, namely by solving the gain only equation (
1.1
).

The main result of this section is the following:

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\overline{\varepsilon},A
be as in the statement of Theorem
3.9
. Then, there exists
0<\bar{\varepsilon}_{5}<\bar{\varepsilon}
such that if
0<\varepsilon_{0}<\overline{\varepsilon}_{5}
and
f_{0}\in B_{X_{M,\alpha}}(\varepsilon_{0})
with
f_{0}\geq 0
, the global solution in Theorem
3.9
is non-negative for all
t\geq 0
.

5.1.
The gain only equation

We start by studying the gain only equation, since that will allow us to properly initialize our iteration scheme. We will show that the gain only equation has a unique solution forward in time which is dispersive, and that for positive initial data, the corresponding solution remains positive. First we give a definition of a solution to the gain only equation (
1.1
):

Definition 5.2
.

Let
I\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}
be an interval with
0\in I
, and
f_{0}\in X_{M,\alpha}
. We say that
f\in\mathcal{C}(I,{\langle}v{\rangle}^{-M}L^{\infty}_{x,v})
is a solution of the gain only (
1.1
) in
I
with initial data
f_{0}
, if

(5.1)

f(t)=\mathcal{S}(t)f_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{S}(t-s)\mathcal{G}[f](s)\,ds,\quad\forall t\in I.

Remark 5.3
.

Analogous conclusions to Remark
3.7
hold for
\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{S}(t-s)\mathcal{G}[f](s)\,ds
.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
\overline{\varepsilon},A
be as in the statement of Theorem
3.9
. Then, for
0<\varepsilon_{0}<\overline{\varepsilon}
and
f_{0}\in B_{X_{M,\alpha}}(\varepsilon_{0})
with
f_{0}\geq 0
, the gain equation (
1.1
) has a unique non-negative solution
\widetilde{f}\in\mathcal{C}([0;+\infty),{\langle}v{\rangle}^{-M}L^{\infty}_{xv})
which is dispersive, meaning it satisfies the bound

(5.2)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\geq 0}\big\{\langle t\rangle^{3/2}\|\langle v\rangle\widetilde{f}(t)\|_{L^{2}_{x}L^{1}_{v}}+\langle t\rangle^{3/2}\|\langle v\rangle^{\alpha}\widetilde{f}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{2}_{v}}+\|\widetilde{f}(t)\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}+\|\langle v\rangle^{M}\widetilde{f}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}\big\}<A\varepsilon_{0}.

Proof.

Simply set
\mathcal{L}=0
and all the estimates written in the proof of Theorem
3.9
remain true. Omitting the details, this yields existence and uniqueness of a solution
\widetilde{f}\in\mathcal{C}([0;+\infty),{\langle}v{\rangle}^{-M}L^{\infty}_{xv})
to the gain only (
1.1
), as well as the dispersive bound (
5.2
).

For positivity, we consider the following iteration scheme for
t\geq 0
:

(5.3)

\begin{cases}\widetilde{f}_{n+1}=\mathcal{S}(t)f_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{S}(t-s)\mathcal{G}[\widetilde{f}_{n}](s)\,ds,\quad n\in\mathbb{N},\\
\widetilde{f}_{0}=\mathcal{S}(t)f_{0}.\end{cases}

Since
f_{0}\geq 0
and
\mathcal{G}
is positive, by induction we obtain that
(\widetilde{f}_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
is increasing and non-negative, hence
\widetilde{f}_{n}\nearrow\widetilde{g}
. Clearly
\widetilde{g}\geq 0
.

Now, we can use the monotone convergence theorem to pass to the limit in (
5.3
), and obtain that
\widetilde{g}
satisfies the same initial value problem as
\widetilde{f}
. By uniqueness, we obtain
\widetilde{f}=\widetilde{g},
hence
\widetilde{f}\geq 0.

∎

5.2.
The Kaniel-Shinbrot iteration scheme

We formally first outline the Kaniel-Shinbrot iteration scheme which we will use in order to construct subsolutions and supersolutions to (
5.4
).

Using (
1.19
), we write the equation as follows:

(5.4)

(\partial_{t}+v\cdot\nabla_{x})f+f\mathcal{R}[f,f]=\mathcal{G}[f],

where
\mathcal{R},\mathcal{G}
are given by (
1.18
) and (
1.4
) respectively.
Let
u_{0},l_{0}:[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}_{x}^{3}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{v}^{3}\to{\mathbb{R}}
with
0\leq l_{0}\leq u_{0}
.
For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, consider the coupled linear initial value problems

(5.5)

\begin{cases}(\partial_{t}+v\cdot\nabla_{x})l_{n}+l_{n}\mathcal{R}[u_{n-1},u_{n-1}]&=\mathcal{G}[l_{n-1}],\\
l_{n}(0)=f_{0},\end{cases}

(5.6)

\begin{cases}(\partial_{t}+v\cdot\nabla_{x})u_{n}+u_{n}\mathcal{R}[l_{n-1},l_{n-1}]&=\mathcal{G}[u_{n-1}],\\
u_{n}(0)=f_{0},\end{cases}

Solutions to (
5.5
)-(
5.6
) are given inductively by

(5.7)

\begin{split}l_{n}(t)&=(\mathcal{S}(t)f_{0})\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{S}(t-s)\mathcal{R}[u_{n-1},u_{n-1}](s)\,ds\right)\\
&\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{S}(t-s)\mathcal{G}[l_{n-1}](s)\exp\left(-\int_{s}^{t}\mathcal{S}(t-\tau)\mathcal{R}[u_{n-1},u_{n-1}](\tau)\,d\tau\right)\,ds,\end{split}

and

(5.8)

\begin{split}u_{n}(t)&=(\mathcal{S}(t)f_{0})\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{S}(t-s)\mathcal{R}[l_{n-1},l_{n-1}](s)\,ds\right)\\
&\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{S}(t-s)\mathcal{G}[u_{n-1}](s)\exp\left(-\int_{s}^{t}\mathcal{S}(t-\tau)\mathcal{R}[l_{n-1},l_{n-1}](\tau)\,d\tau\right)\,ds.\end{split}

Initialization

We first initiate the scheme by choosing
l_{0},u_{0}
so dispersion is preserved and the following beginning condition

0\leq l_{0}\leq l_{1}\leq u_{1}\leq u_{0}

is satisfied.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
\overline{\varepsilon},A
be as in the statement of Theorem
3.9
. Then, for
0<\varepsilon_{0}<\overline{\varepsilon}
and
f_{0}\in B_{X_{M,\alpha}}(\varepsilon_{0})
with
f_{0}\geq 0
,
there exist
l_{0},u_{0}
with
0\leq l_{0}\leq u_{0}
for the coupled initial value problems (
5.5
)-(
5.6
) such that the first iterates
l_{1},u_{1}
satisfy

(5.9)

0\leq l_{0}\leq l_{1}\leq u_{1}\leq u_{0}.

Moreover
u_{0}
satisfies the dispersive bound (
5.2
).

Proof.

Let
\widetilde{f}
be the solution given by Proposition
5.4
for initial data
f_{0}\in B_{X_{M,\alpha}}(\varepsilon_{0}).

We define
l_{0}=0
,
u_{0}=\widetilde{f}
.

Then (
5.7
)-(
5.8
) give the following expressions for the first iterates:

\displaystyle l_{1}(t)

\displaystyle=(\mathcal{S}(t)f_{0})\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{S}(t-s)\mathcal{R}[u_{0},u_{0}](s)\,ds\right),

\displaystyle u_{1}(t)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{S}(t)f_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{S}(t-s)\mathcal{G}[u_{0}](s)\,ds.

Since the operator
\mathcal{G},\mathcal{R}
are positive, and
\widetilde{f}\geq 0
by Proposition
5.4
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{S}(t-s)\mathcal{R}[u_{0},u_{0}](s)\,ds,\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{S}(t-s)\mathcal{G}[u_{0}](s)\,ds\geq 0.

As a result
0=l_{0}\leq l_{1}\leq u_{1}
.

Note that the initial value problem for
u_{1}
is exactly that of the gain-only KWE, therefore by uniqueness in Proposition
5.4
we have
u_{1}=\widetilde{f}=u_{0},
and we can conclude that the initialization condition is fulfilled.
∎

Properties of iterates

Now, we prove monotonicity of the scheme.

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
l_{n},u_{n}
be the solutions to (
5.7
)-(
5.8
) for
l_{0},u_{0}
chosen as in Lemma
5.5
.

Then for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
t\geq 0
, we have

(5.10)

\begin{split}0&\leq l_{0}\leq l_{1}\leq\dots\leq l_{n-1}\leq l_{n}\leq u_{n}\leq u_{n-1}\leq\dots\leq u_{1}\leq u_{0}\end{split}

Moreover
(l_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
,
(u_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
satisfy the dispersive estimate (
5.2
)

Proof.

We argue by induction. For
n=1,
(
5.10
) is given by (
5.9
), while Proposition
5.4
gives (
3.10
) at the first step.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}.
Assume that (
5.10
) holds for
n
, and that
l_{n}
and
u_{n}
satisfy (
3.10
).

Since
l_{n-1}\leq l_{n}
and
u_{n}\leq u_{n-1}
, we have

\mathcal{R}[u_{n},u_{n}]\leq\mathcal{R}[u_{n-1},u_{n-1}],\quad\mathcal{G}[l_{n-1}]\leq\mathcal{G}[l_{n}],

which, together with (
5.7
), implies
l_{n}\leq l_{n+1}
.

Similarly,

u_{n+1}\leq u_{n}
.

Finally

\mathcal{R}[l_{n},l_{n}]\leq\mathcal{R}[u_{n},u_{n}],\quad\mathcal{G}[l_{n}]\leq\mathcal{G}[u_{n}],

and (
5.7
)-(
5.8
) imply
l_{n+1}\leq u_{n+1}
. Hence (
5.10
) holds for all
n
by induction.

Finally, the dispersive estimate (
5.2
) holds for
(l_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
,
(u_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
due to (
5.10
), and the fact that
u_{0}=\widetilde{f}
.

∎

Conclusion of the proof

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

Consider
0<\bar{\varepsilon}_{6}<\bar{\varepsilon}
sufficiently small.
Let
0<\varepsilon_{0}<\bar{\varepsilon}_{5}
,
f_{0}\in B_{X_{M,\alpha}}(\varepsilon_{0})
, and consider the sequences
(l_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
,
(u_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
constructed in Lemma
5.6
.

For fixed
t\geq 0,
the sequence
(l_{n}(t))_{n=0}^{\infty}
is increasing and upper bounded by (
5.10
), therefore it converges
l_{n}(t)\nearrow l(t)
. Similarly the sequence
(u_{n}(t))_{n=0}^{\infty}
is decreasing and lower bounded so
u_{n}(t)\searrow u(t)
. Moreover, by (
5.10
) we have

0\leq l(t)\leq u(t)\leq u_{0}.

Since
u_{0}=\widetilde{f}
satisfies (
5.2
), so do
l
and
u.

Integrating (
5.5
)-(
5.6
) in time and using the dominated convergence theorem to let
n\to\infty
, we obtain

(5.11)

\displaystyle l(t)+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{S}(t-s)[l\mathcal{R}[u,u](s)]\,ds

\displaystyle=\mathcal{S}(t)f_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{S}(t-s)\mathcal{G}[l](s)\,ds,

(5.12)

\displaystyle u(t)+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{S}(t-s)[u\mathcal{R}[l,l](s)]\,ds

\displaystyle=\mathcal{S}(t)f_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{S}(t-s)\mathcal{G}[u](s)\,ds.

Given
t\geq 0
, (
5.11
)-(
5.12
), (
1.10
), (
1.20
) and (
3.5
) imply

\begin{split}\|&u(t)-l(t)\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\mathcal{S}(t-s)\big(\mathcal{G}[u]-\mathcal{G}[l]\big)\right\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\mathcal{S}(t-s)\big(l\mathcal{R}[u,u]-u\mathcal{R}[l,l]\big)\right\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}\,ds\\
&\leq\sum_{i=1,2}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\mathcal{G}_{i}[u,u,u](s)-\mathcal{G}_{i}[l,l,l](s)\right\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\mathcal{L}_{i}[l,u,u](s)-\mathcal{L}_{i}[u,l,l](s)\right\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}\,ds\\
&\lesssim\sum_{\mathcal{T}\in\{\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathcal{L}_{2},\mathcal{G}_{1},\mathcal{G}_{2}\}}\sum_{h_{1},h_{2}\in\{u,l,l-u,u-l\}}\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\|\mathcal{T}[u-l,h_{1},h_{2}](s)\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}\\
&\hskip 142.26378pt+\|\mathcal{T}[h_{1},u-l,h_{2}](s)\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}+\|\mathcal{T}[h_{1},h_{2},u-l](s)\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}\Big)\,ds.\end{split}

Now using (
2.26
) for
f=u-l
and (
3.10
) we find that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{T}[u-l,h_{1},h_{2}](s)\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}+\|\mathcal{T}[h_{1},u-l,h_{2}](s)\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}+\|\mathcal{T}[h_{1},h_{2},u-l](s)\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|u(s)-l(s)\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}\sum_{h_{1},h_{2}\in\{u,l,l-u,u-l\}}\|{\langle}v{\rangle}h_{1}(s)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}L^{1}_{v}}\|{\langle}v{\rangle}^{3}h_{2}(s)\|_{L^{\infty}_{xv}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\langle s\rangle^{-3/2}A^{2}\varepsilon_{0}^{2}\|u-l\|_{L^{\infty}([0;\infty),L^{1}_{xv})},

Hence

\displaystyle\|u(t)-l(t)\|_{L^{1}_{xv}}\lesssim A^{2}\varepsilon_{0}^{2}\|u-l\|_{L^{\infty}([0;\infty),L^{1}_{xv})}\int_{0}^{t}{\langle}s{\rangle}^{-3/2}\,ds\leq\frac{1}{2}\|u-l\|_{L^{\infty}([0;\infty),L^{1}_{xv})},

since
0<\varepsilon_{0}<\bar{\varepsilon}_{5}
and
\bar{\varepsilon}_{5}
is sufficiently small. Since
t
was chosen arbitrarily we conclude that
u=l
. Plugging this information into (
5.11
), (
5.12
), we find that
u,l
satisfy the same initial value problem as
f,
thus
u=l=f
by the uniqueness part of Theorem
3.9
. We conclude that
f\geq 0
.

∎

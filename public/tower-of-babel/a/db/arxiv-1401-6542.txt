Bergman Kernels for a sequence of almost Kähler-Ricci solitons Thanks: * Partially supported by the NSFC Grants 11271022 and 11331001
Wenshuai Jiang, Feng Wang, Xiaohua Zhu ∗ \text{Zhu}^{*}

Abstract
. In this paper, we give a lower bound of Bergman kernels for a sequence of almost Kähler-Einstein Fano manifolds, or more general, a sequence of Fano manifolds with almost Kähler-Ricci solitons. This generalizes a result by Donaldson-Sun, Tian for Kähler-Einstein manifolds sequence with positive scalar curvature. As an application of our result, we prove that the Gromov-Hausdorff limit of sequence is homomorphic to a log terminal Q Q -Fano variety which admits a Kähler-Ricci soliton on its smooth part.

Suppose that the Ricci potential

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{g}\leq 1.

Then for

(3.2)

\displaystyle\|s\|_{h}+l^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla s\|_{h}\leq C(C_{s},n)l^{\frac{n}{2}}(\int_{M}|s|^{2}{\rm dv}_{g})^{\frac{1}{2}},

\displaystyle\Delta|s|_{h}^{2}=|\nabla s|_{h}^{2}-nl|s|_{h}^{2}.

It follows

(3.3)

\displaystyle-\Delta|s|_{h}^{2}\leq nl|s|_{h}^{2}.

Thus applying the standard Moser iteration method to (
3.3
), we get

(3.4)

\displaystyle\|s\|_{h}\leq C(C_{s},n)l^{\frac{n}{2}}(\int_{M}|s|^{2}{\rm dv}_{g})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

On the other hand, we have the following Bochner formula,

\Delta|\nabla s|_{h}^{2}=|\nabla\nabla s|^{2}+|\bar{\nabla}\nabla s|^{2}-(n+2)l|\nabla s|^{2}+\langle\text{Ric}(\nabla s,.),\nabla s\rangle.

Then we can also apply the Moser iteration to obtain a
L^{\infty}
-estimate for
|\nabla s|_{h}^{2}
as done for
|s|_{h}^{2}
.
In fact, it suffices to deal with the extra integral terms like
\langle\text{Ric}(\nabla s,.),\nabla s\rangle|\nabla s|^{2p}
.
But those terms can be controlled by the integral of
(|\nabla\nabla s|^{2}+|\bar{\nabla}\nabla s|^{2})|\nabla s|_{h}^{2p}

by taking integral by parts with the help of the condition (
3.1
) (cf.
[
32
]
,
[
27
]
).
As a consequence, we obtain

(3.5)

\displaystyle\|\nabla s\|_{h}\leq C(C_{s},n)l^{\frac{n}{2}}(\int_{M}|\nabla s|^{2}{\rm dv}_{g})^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C(C_{s},n)l^{\frac{n+1}{2}}(\int_{M}|s|^{2}{\rm dv}_{g})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Therefore, combining (
3.4
) and (
3.5
), we derive (
3.6
).

∎

Remark 3.2

Using the same argument in Lemma

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
(M,g)
be a Fano manifold which satisfies (
3.1
) as in Lemma
3.1
.
Let
\bar{\partial}
-operator be defined for smooth sections on
(M,L^{l})
(

) with the induced metric
h
.
Then for any
\sigma\in C^{\infty}(\Gamma(M,L^{l}))
, there exists a solution

with property:

(3.7)

\displaystyle\int_{M}|v|^{2}\leq 4l^{-1}\int_{M}|\bar{\partial}\sigma|^{2}.

Proof.

The existence part comes from the Hömander
L^{2}
-theory. We suffice to verify (
3.7
), which is equal to prove
that the first eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}(\bar{\partial},L^{l})
of

Note that the following two identities hold for any

\Delta_{\bar{\partial}}\theta=\bar{\nabla}^{*}\bar{\nabla}\theta+\text{Ric}(\theta,.)+l\theta

It follows

(3.8)

\displaystyle\Delta_{\bar{\partial}}\theta=(1-\frac{1}{2n})\bar{\nabla}^{*}\bar{\nabla}+(1-\frac{1}{2n})\text{Ric}(\theta,.)+\frac{1}{2n}\nabla^{*}\nabla\theta+\frac{l}{2}\theta.

Then with the help of condition (
3.1

\displaystyle\int_{M}\langle\Delta_{\bar{\partial}}\theta,\theta\rangle

\displaystyle=(1-\frac{1}{2n})\int_{M}|\bar{\nabla}\theta|^{2}+\frac{1}{2n}\int_{M}|\nabla\theta|^{2}+\frac{l}{2}\int_{M}|\theta|^{2}

\displaystyle+(1-\frac{1}{2n})\int_{M}(|\theta|^{2}+\langle\nabla\bar{\nabla}u(\theta,.),\theta\rangle)

\displaystyle\geq(1-\frac{1}{2n})\int_{M}|\bar{\nabla}\theta|^{2}+\frac{1}{2n}\int_{M}|\nabla\theta|^{2}+\frac{l}{2}\int_{M}|\theta|^{2}

\displaystyle+(1-\frac{1}{2n})\int_{M}|\theta|^{2}-(1-\frac{1}{2n})\int_{M}\langle\bar{\nabla}u,(\langle\nabla\theta,\theta\rangle+\langle\theta,\bar{\nabla}\theta\rangle)\rangle

\displaystyle\geq(1-\frac{1}{2n})\int_{M}|\bar{\nabla}\theta|^{2}+\frac{1}{2n}\int_{M}|\nabla\theta|^{2}+\frac{l}{2}\int_{M}|\theta|^{2}

\displaystyle+(1-\frac{1}{2n})\int_{M}|\theta|^{2}-(1-\frac{1}{2n})\int_{M}[\frac{1}{2n}(|\bar{\nabla}\theta|^{2}+|\nabla\theta|^{2})+n|\theta|^{2}]

(3.9)

\displaystyle\geq(\frac{l}{2}-n)\int_{M}|\theta|^{2}.

Now we can choose

∎

4.
Construction of locally approximate holomorphic sections

Let
\{(M_{i},g^{i})\}
be a sequence of almost Kähler-Einstein manifolds as in Section 3 and
(M_{\infty},g_{\infty})
its Gromov-Hausdorff limit.
It was proved by Tian and B. Wang that the regular part
\mathcal{R}
of
M_{\infty}
is an open Kähler manifold
and the codimension of singularities of
M_{\infty}
is at least 4
[
26
]
. Moreover, according to Proposition 5.1 in that paper, we have

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
x\in M_{\infty}
.
Then there exist constants
\epsilon=\epsilon(n)
and
r_{0}=r_{0}(n,C)
such that if
{\rm vol}(B_{x}(r))\geq(1-\epsilon)\omega_{2n}r^{2n}
for some
r\leq r_{0}
, then

B_{x}(\frac{r}{2})\subseteq\mathcal{R}
, and

\displaystyle{\rm Ric}(g_{\infty})=g_{\infty},\,\|\nabla^{l}{\rm Rm}\|_{C^{0}(B_{x}(\frac{r}{2}))}\leq\frac{C}{r^{l+2}},

where the constant
C
depends only on
l
, and the constants
\Lambda
and
D
in (
3
).

Recall that a tangent cone
C_{x}
at
x\in M_{\infty}
is a Gromov-Hausdorff limit defined by

(4.1)

\displaystyle(C_{x},g_{x},x)=\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}(M_{\infty},\frac{g_{\infty}}{r_{j}^{2}},x),

where
\{r_{j}\}
is some sequence which goes to
0
. Without the loss of generality, we may assume that
l_{j}=\frac{1}{r_{j}^{2}}
are integers. Since
(C_{x},g_{x},x)
is a metric cone,
g_{x}={\rm hess}\frac{\rho_{x}^{2}}{2}
, where
\rho_{x}={\rm dist}(x,\cdot)

in
C_{x}
.

Denote the regular part of

by

, which consists of points in
C_{x}
with flat cones.
By Lemma
4.1
, we prove

Lemma 4.2
.

\mathcal{CR}
is an open Kähler-Ricci flat manifold. Moreover, for any compact set

, there exist a sequence of
(K_{j}\subset\mathcal{R},\frac{1}{r_{j}^{2}}g_{\infty})

in
C^{\infty}
-topology.

Proof.

Let
\epsilon
be a small number chosen as in Lemma
4.1
. Then for any
y\in\mathcal{CR}
,
there exists some small

such that
\hat{B}_{y}(r)\subset C_{x}
and

\displaystyle\text{vol }(\hat{B}_{y}(r))\geq(1-\frac{\epsilon}{2})\omega_{2n}r^{2n}.

Thus there exists a sequence of
y_{\alpha}\in C_{x}
such that

\displaystyle\text{vol }(B_{y_{\alpha}}(rr_{\alpha}))\geq(1-\epsilon)\omega_{2n}(rr_{\alpha})^{2n},

where the sequence
\{r_{\alpha}\}
is chosen as in (
4.1
).
By Lemma
4.1
, it follows

\displaystyle\|{\rm Rm}(\tilde{g}_{\infty})\|_{C^{l}(\tilde{B}_{y_{\alpha}}(\frac{r}{2}))}\leq\frac{C_{l}}{r^{l+2}},

where
\tilde{g}_{\infty}=\frac{g_{\infty}}{r_{\alpha}^{2}}
and
\tilde{B}_{y_{\alpha}}(\frac{r}{2})\subset M_{\infty}
is a
\frac{r}{2}
-geodesic ball with respect to the metric
\tilde{g}_{\infty}
.
Hence, by the Cheeger-Gromov compactness theorem [GW],
(\tilde{B}_{y_{\alpha}}(\frac{r}{2}),\tilde{g}_{\infty})
converge to
(\hat{B}_{y}(\frac{r}{2}),g_{x})
in
C^{\infty}
-topology.
In particular,
B_{y_{\alpha}}(\frac{r_{\alpha}r}{2}

)\subset\mathcal{R}
and
\hat{B}_{y}(\frac{r}{2})\subset\mathcal{CR}
. This implies that
\mathcal{CR}
is an open manifolds.
Moreover,
\mathcal{CR}
is a Kähler-Ricci flat manifold since each
(B_{y_{\alpha}}(\frac{r_{\alpha}r}{2}),{g}_{\infty})
is an open Kähler-Einstein manifold.
If
K
is a compact set of
\mathcal{CR}
, then
by taking finite small geodesic covering balls, one can find a sequence

in

-topology.
∎

Define an open set

of
\mathcal{CR}
by

(4.2)

\displaystyle V(x;\delta)=\{y\in C_{x}|~{\rm dist}(y,S_{x})\geq\delta,d(y,x)\leq\frac{1}{\delta}\},

where

. The following lemma shows that there exists a “nice” cut-off function on

For any
\eta,\delta>0

\beta
on

which supported in

\beta=1,\text{ in }V(x;\delta);

\displaystyle\int_{C_{x}}|\nabla\beta|^{2}e^{-\frac{\rho_{x}^{2}}{2}}d{\rm v}_{g_{x}}\leq\eta.

Lemma
4.3
is in fact a corollary of following fundamental lemma.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
(X^{m},d,\mu)
be a measured metric space such that

(4.3)

\displaystyle\mu(B_{y}(r)\leq C_{0}r^{m},~\forall~r\leq 1,~y\in X.

Let
Z
be a closed subset of
X
with
\mathcal{H}^{m-2}(Z)=0

on
X
such that

\int_{X}fd\mu\leq 1.

Then
for any
x\in X
,
\eta>0
and
\delta>0
, there exist a positive
\delta_{1}\leq\delta
and a cut-off function
\beta\geq 0
, which supported in
B_{x}(\frac{1}{\delta_{1}})\setminus Z_{\delta_{1}}
with property:
{\beta}=1,\text{ in }B_{x}(\frac{1}{\delta})\setminus Z_{\delta};

(4.4)

\displaystyle\int_{X}f|{\rm Lif}(\beta)|^{2}d\mu\leq\eta.

Here
Z_{\delta_{1}}=\{x^{\prime}\in X|~{\rm dist}(x^{\prime},Z)\leq\delta_{1}\}
and
{\rm Lip}(\beta)(z)=\sup_{w\rightarrow z}|\frac{f(w)-f(z)}{d(w,z)}|
.

Proof.

Let
R\geq\sqrt{\frac{8}{\eta}}+\frac{2}{\delta}
. Since
\mathcal{H}^{m-2}(Z)=0
, then for any
\kappa>0
,
we can take finite geodesic balls
B_{x_{i}}(r_{i})

(r_{i}\leq\delta)
with

to cover

such that

\displaystyle\Sigma_{i}r_{i}^{m-2}\leq\kappa.

Let
\zeta:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a cut-off function which satisfies:

\displaystyle\zeta(t)=1,\text{ for }t\leq\frac{1}{2};\zeta(t)=0,\text{ for }t\geq 1;|\zeta^{\prime}(t)|\leq 2.

Set

\chi(y)=\min_{i}\{1-\zeta(\frac{d(y,x_{i})}{r_{i}})\}

and

\beta(y)=\zeta(\frac{\epsilon}{d(y,x)})\zeta(\frac{d(y,x)}{R})\chi(y),

where
\epsilon\leq\frac{\delta}{2}
. Then it is easy to see that
\beta
is supported in
B_{x}(R)\setminus\cup B_{x_{i}}(\frac{r_{i}}{2})
with
\beta\equiv 1
in

. Moreover,

\displaystyle\int_{X}f|Lif\beta|^{2}d\mu

\displaystyle\leq 4C_{0}\Sigma_{i}r_{i}^{-2}r_{i}^{m}+4C_{0}\epsilon^{m-2}+\frac{4}{R^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 4C_{0}\kappa+4C_{0}\epsilon^{2n-2}+\frac{\eta}{2}.

Thus, if we choose
\epsilon
and
\kappa
such that

4C_{0}\kappa+4C_{0}\epsilon^{2n-2}\leq\frac{\eta}{2}
,
then we get (
4.4
).
By choosing
\delta_{1}\leq\min\{\frac{\epsilon}{2},\frac{1}{2R}\}
such that

we can also get

Hence

satisfies all conditions required in the lemma.

∎

Proof of Lemma
4.3
.

Applying Lemma
4.4
to
X=C_{x},Z=S_{x},f=e^{-\frac{\rho_{x}^{2}}{2}}
, we get the lemma.
∎

By Lemma
4.2
, we see that for any
\delta>0
there exists a sequence of
K_{j}\subset(M_{\infty},r_{j}^{-2}g_{\infty})
which converge
to
V(x;\delta)
. Let
L_{0}=(C_{x},\mathbb{C})
be the trivial holomorphic bundle over

with a hermitian metric
h_{0}=e^{-\frac{\rho_{x}^{2}}{2}}
.
Then

induces the Chern connection
\nabla_{0}
with its curvature

In the following we show that a sufficiently large multiple line bundles of

will approximate to

over

. This is in fact an application of the following fundamental lemma.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
(V,g)
be a
C^{2}
open Riemannian manifold and

are two pre-compact open subsets of

with
U\subset\subset U^{\prime}
.
Then for any positive number
\epsilon

\delta=\delta(U^{\prime},g,\epsilon)
and a positive integer

, which depends on the fundamental group of

(L,h)

\displaystyle|{\rm Ric}^{\nabla}|_{g}\leq\delta,~{\rm in}~U^{\prime},

\psi
of

over

\displaystyle|D^{\nabla\otimes l}\psi|_{h,g}\leq\epsilon,~{\rm in}~U.

The proof seems standard.
First we show that
(L,U)
is a flat bundle with respect to some connection.
Let
B_{x_{i}}(r_{i})
(

\bar{U}\subset\cup B_{x_{i}}(r_{i})\subset U^{\prime}

which connects

and

In particular,

\displaystyle D_{T}[D_{X}e_{i}]=D_{X}[D_{T}e_{i}]+{\rm Ric}^{\nabla}(T,X)e_{i}={\rm Ric}^{\nabla}(T,X)e_{i}.

\displaystyle|De_{i}|_{h,g}\leq C({U^{\prime}},g)||\text{Ric}^{\nabla}||_{({U^{\prime}},g)}\leq C({U^{\prime}},g)\delta,~{\rm in}~B_{x_{i}}(r_{i}).

\displaystyle\|\nabla-\nabla^{\prime}\|_{(U,g)}=\|\alpha\|_{(U,g)}\leq C({U^{\prime}},g)||\text{Ric}^{\nabla}||_{({U^{\prime}},g)}\leq C({U^{\prime}},g)\delta.

Hence, there exist

||\psi|_{h}-1|,\|\psi^{{\nabla^{\prime}}^{\otimes l}}\|_{h,g}\leq C(U^{\prime},g)\gamma(\delta),{\rm in}~U.

Moreover, By (

is a

-Fano variety.

Let
\mathcal{S}={\rm Sing}(M_{\infty})
,
\mathcal{\hat{S}}=T_{k_{0}l,\infty}(\mathcal{S})
, and let

\mathcal{\hat{S}}

since
(W^{k_{0}l},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}P^{N}}(1))=

\newline
K^{-k_{0}l}_{W^{k_{0}l}\setminus\mathcal{\hat{S}}}
. In the following, we give
a proof for the general limit Kähler-Ricci soliton
(M_{\infty},g_{\infty})
in Section 7 by using PDE method as in
[
7
]
. Namely,
g_{\infty}
satisfies
an equation,

(8.7)

\displaystyle{\rm Ric}(g_{\infty})-g_{\infty}-L_{X_{\infty}}g_{\infty}=0,~{\rm in}~M_{\infty}\setminus\mathcal{S},

where
X_{\infty}
is the limit holomorphic vector field of
(M_{i},X_{i})
on
M_{\infty}\setminus\mathcal{S}

[
32
]
.

On contrary, we suppose that
W_{s}\neq\mathcal{\hat{S}}
. Then there exists some
x\in\mathcal{S}
such that
p=T_{k_{0}l,\infty}(x)\in W^{k_{0}l}\setminus W_{s}
, a smooth point in
W^{k_{0}l}
. Thus there exists a small ball
B
around
p
in
W^{k_{0}l}
with the standard holomorphic coordinates such that the induced Kähler form
\omega_{0}=\frac{1}{k_{0}l}\omega_{g_{FS}}
by the Fubini-Study metric

of the projective space is smooth on
B
.
We may assume that
\omega_{0}=\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}v
for some Kähler potential
v
on
B
.

Let
\rho_{\infty}
be the limit of
\rho_{k_{0}l}(M_{i},g^{i})
(perhaps replaced by a subsequence of
\rho_{k_{0}l}(M_{i},g^{i})
) on

(M_{\infty}\setminus\mathcal{S},g_{\infty})

\rho_{\infty}
and

Let

. Then by (

), we see that

It follows

\displaystyle\log{\rm det}(u_{i\bar{j}})+X_{\infty}(u)+u=const.,~{\rm in}~B\setminus\mathcal{\hat{S}}.

We claim that there exists a uniform

\displaystyle C^{-1}\delta_{i\overline{j}}\leq u_{i\overline{j}}\leq C\delta_{i\overline{j}},~{\rm in}B\setminus\mathcal{\hat{S}}.

Since the basis in

-bounded, we have

On the other hand, by (

|X_{\infty}(\rho_{\infty})|\leq|X_{\infty}|_{g_{\infty}}|\nabla\rho_{\infty}|_{g_{\infty}}\leq C,~{\rm in}~M_{\infty}.

Then

is uniformly bounded.
Thus by (
8.8

By the above claim, we can apply the following lemma to show that

-Fano variety.

∎

Lemma 8.3
.

Let

be a smooth solution of (

) in

, where

is a ball in the euclidean space in

and

is a closed subset in

. Suppose that

satisfies (
8.9

Then

can be extended to a smooth function on
\frac{1}{4}B

Proof.

By the Schaulder estimate for the equation (

), we suffices to get a

-regularity of

in
\frac{1}{4}B
. We first do the

-estimate.

For any
0<\epsilon<\frac{1}{8}
and any unit vector
v

w=w_{\epsilon}=\frac{u(x+\epsilon v)+u(x-\epsilon v)-2u(x)}{\epsilon^{2}}.

Then by the convexity of

, we get from (

),

(8.10)

\displaystyle u^{i\bar{j}}w_{i\bar{j}}\geq e^{g}\frac{g(x+\epsilon v)+g(x-\epsilon v)-2g(x)}{\epsilon^{2}},

where

(a_{\alpha\beta})
to be the

g_{\infty}

(a^{\alpha\beta})={\rm det}(a_{\delta\gamma})(a_{\alpha\beta})^{-1}.
It is clear that (

\displaystyle(a_{\alpha\beta}w_{\beta})_{\alpha}\geq l(x)+\frac{h(x+\epsilon v)-h(x)}{\epsilon},~{\rm in}~\frac{3}{4}B\setminus\mathcal{\hat{S}},

where

Note that

can be extended to a holomorphic vector field on

can be regarded as a weak sub-solution in (

\displaystyle\sup_{\frac{1}{2}B}(w_{\epsilon})\leq C(|w_{\epsilon}|_{L^{p}(\frac{3}{4}B)}+|l|_{L^{\frac{q}{2}}(B)}+|h|_{L^{q}(B)}),

In fact, by Theorem 8.17 in

Lemma 10.1
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a compact complex manifold,
G
a holomorphic line bundle,
E
a holomorphic line bundle with a hermitian metric
e^{-\psi}

whose Ricci curvature is positive. Let
\{s_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq p}
be a basis of
H^{0}(M,G)
and
|s|^{2}=\Sigma_{i=1}^{p}|s_{i}|^{2}
. Then for any

\int_{M}\frac{|f|^{2}e^{-\psi}}{|s|^{2(n+k+1)}}d{\rm v}_{g}<+\infty,

Proposition 10.2
.

Let
(M,g)
be a Kähler manifold
with

{\rm Ric}(g)+{\rm Hess}\,u\geq-Cg,

where

X=\nabla u
is a holomorphic vector field and
|u|\leq A
. Assume that

(10.1)

\displaystyle c^{\prime}\geq\rho_{l}(M,g)\geq c>0

for some
l\in\mathbb{N}
. Then for any
s\in H^{0}(M,K_{M}^{-m})
with
m\geq(n+2)l+C+1
, there are
u_{i}\in H^{0}(M,K_{M}^{-(m-l)})

such that
s=\Sigma_{i=0}^{N}u_{i}\otimes s_{i}
, where
\{s_{i}\}
is an orthonormal basis of
H^{0}(M,K_{M}^{-l})
. Moreover, each
u_{i}
satisfies

(10.2)

\displaystyle\int_{M}|u_{i}|^{2}_{h^{\otimes m-l}}{\rm dv}_{g}\leq(n+1)e^{2A}(\frac{c^{\prime}}{c})^{\frac{m}{l}}\int_{M}|s|^{2}_{h^{\otimes m}}{\rm dv}_{g}.

Proof.

Putting
L=K_{M}^{-1}
and
m-C-1=(n+k+1)l+r

(0\leq r<l)
, we decompose
mL
as

mL=(n+k+1)(lL)+((m-(n+k+1)l)L-K_{M})+K_{M}.

Let
h
and
\omega_{g}^{n}
be two hermitian metrics on
L
such that

{\rm Ric}(L,h)=g,\,{\rm Ric}(L,\omega_{g}^{n})={\rm Ric}(g).

Denote the line bundle
(m-(n+k+1)l)L-K_{M}
by
E
. Then
h_{1}=h^{\otimes m-(n+k+1)l}\otimes e^{-u}\otimes\omega_{g}^{n}
is a hermitian metric on

It is easy to see

{\rm Ric}(E,h_{1})=(m-(n+k+1)l)\omega_{g}+{\rm Ric}(g)+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}u\geq\omega_{g}.

Now applying the above lemma to

,
s_{i}
,

and

, we see that there are

such that

\int_{M}\frac{|u_{i}|^{2}_{h^{\otimes(n+k)l}\otimes h_{1}}}{(\Sigma_{i=0}^{N}|s_{i}|^{2}_{h^{\otimes l}})^{n+k}}{\rm dv}_{g}\\
\leq\frac{n+k}{k}\int_{M}\frac{|s|^{2}_{h^{\otimes(n+k+1)l}\otimes h_{1}}}{(\Sigma_{i=0}^{N}|s_{i}|^{2}_{h^{\otimes l}})^{n+k+1}}{\rm dv}_{g}.

The above is equivalent to

\displaystyle\int_{M}\frac{|u_{i}|^{2}_{h^{\otimes m-l}}}{(\Sigma_{i=0}^{N}|s_{i}|^{2}_{h^{\otimes l}})^{n+k}}e^{-u}{\rm dv}_{g}\leq\frac{n+k}{k}\int_{M}\frac{|s|^{2}_{h^{\otimes m}}}{(\Sigma_{i=0}^{N}|s_{i}|_{h^{\otimes l}}^{2})^{n+k+1}}e^{-u}{\rm dv}_{g}.

By (

\displaystyle\frac{1}{e^{2A}c^{\prime n+k}}\int_{M}|u_{i}|^{2}_{h^{\otimes m-l}}{\rm dv}_{g}\leq\frac{n+k}{kc^{n+k+1}}\int_{M}|s|^{2}_{h^{\otimes m}}{\rm dv}_{g},

which implies (

∎

Relativistic BGK model for massless particles in the FLRW spacetime
Byung-Hoon Hwang, Ho Lee, Seok-Bae Yun

Abstract
. In this paper, we address the Cauchy problem for the relativistic BGK model proposed by Anderson and Witting for massless particles in the Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker (FLRW) spacetime.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Relativistic BGK model

We consider the Anderson and Witting type relativistic BGK model
[
1
]
:

(1.1)

p^{\alpha}\frac{\partial F}{\partial x^{\alpha}}-\Gamma^{\alpha}_{\beta\gamma}p^{\beta}p^{\gamma}\frac{\partial F}{\partial p^{\alpha}}=-\frac{U^{\mu}p_{\mu}}{c^{2}\tau}\left(J(F)-F\right).

Here
F\equiv F(x^{\alpha},p^{\alpha})
is the distribution function representing a number density of particles in the phase space spanned by the spacetime coordinates
x^{\alpha}
and four-momentum
p^{\alpha}
.
The Greek indices run from 0 to 3 and the repeated Greek indices are assumed to be summed over its whole range. In (
1.1
),
\Gamma^{\alpha}_{\beta\gamma}
denote the Christoffel symbols,
U^{\mu}
is the Landau-Lifshitz four-velocity,
c
is the speed of light,
\tau
is the characteristic time of order of the mean free time, and
J(F)
is the Jüttner distribution
[
16
,
17
]
which takes the form of

J(F)=\begin{cases}\frac{g_{s}}{h^{3}}\exp\left\{\frac{\mu_{E}}{kT_{E}}+\frac{U_{E}^{\alpha}p_{\alpha}}{kT_{E}}\right\}&\text{for the relativistic Maxwell-Boltzmann statistics},\cr\frac{g_{s}/h^{3}}{\exp\left\{-\frac{\mu_{E}}{kT_{E}}-\frac{U_{E}^{\alpha}p_{\alpha}}{kT_{E}}\right\}-1}&\text{for the relativistic Bose-Einstein statistics},\end{cases}

where
h
is the Planck constant,
k
is the Boltzmann constant,
g_{s}
is the degeneracy factor,
\mu_{E}
is the chemical potential,
U_{E}^{\alpha}=(\sqrt{c^{2}+|U_{E}|^{2}},U_{E})
is the four-velocity, and
T_{E}
is the equilibrium temperature.
Note that
\mu_{E}
,
U_{E}
and
T_{E}
are functions of
t
and
x
determined by the relations

\displaystyle U^{\mu}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}p_{\mu}\left(J(F)-F\right)\sqrt{-|\eta|}\,\frac{dp}{p^{0}}=0,\qquad U^{\mu}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}p_{\mu}p^{\nu}\left(J(F)-F\right)\sqrt{-|\eta|}\,\frac{dp}{p^{0}}=0,

where
|\eta|
denotes the determinant of the metric tensor
\eta_{\alpha\beta}
and
dp=dp^{1}dp^{2}dp^{3}
, so that the conservation laws of particle number, momentum and energy for (
1.1
) hold true

(1.2)

\displaystyle{N^{\alpha}}_{;\alpha}=\partial_{\alpha}N^{\alpha}+\Gamma^{\alpha}_{\alpha\mu}N^{\mu}=0,\qquad{T^{\alpha\beta}}_{;\alpha}=\partial_{\alpha}T^{\alpha\beta}+\Gamma^{\alpha}_{\alpha\mu}T^{\mu\beta}+\Gamma^{\beta}_{\alpha\mu}T^{\alpha\mu}=0.

The BGK model
[
7
,
28
]
is the most well-known model equation of the classical Boltzmann equation. Three relativistic generalizations have been proposed respectively by Marle
[
23
,
22
]
, Anderson and Witting
[
1
]
, and recently by Pennisi and Ruggeri
[
26
]
, which have been widely applied to various physical problems
[
9
,
10
,
15
,
24
,
25
,
21
]
. The first mathematical analysis for relativistic BGK models was carried out in
[
5
]
where the unique determination of equilibrium variables, the scaling limits, and the linearization problem were studied for the Marle model. For the existence theory of the nonlinear Marle model, we refer to
[
6
]
for near-equilibrium solutions,
[
14
]
for stationary solutions, and
[
8
]
for weak solutions. In the case of the Anderson-Witting model, the unique determination of equilibrium variables, and the global existence and asymptotic behavior of near-equilibrium solutions were studied in
[
12
]
, and the stationary problem was covered in
[
13
]
. Recently, the Pennisi-Ruggeri model for polyatomic gases was studied in
[
11
]
where the unique determination of equilibrium variables, and the global existence and asymptotic behavior of near-equilibrium solutions were addressed. To the best knowledge of authors, the BGK model has not been much studied in the context of cosmology, which is the main motivation of the current work.

1.2.
Reduction in the case of the FLRW spacetime

In this paper, we are concerned with the Cauchy problem of the Anderson-Witting model (
1.1
) for massless particles in an isotropic and spatially homogeneous spacetime, namely the Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker (FLRW) spacetime. As such, the distribution function is also assumed to be isotropic and spatially homogeneous. Throughout the paper, we set all physical constants to be unity except for particle mass
(m=0)
for brevity. In the FLRW case, the metric tensor
\eta_{\mu\nu}
is given by

\eta_{00}=\eta^{00}=-1,\qquad\eta_{ij}=R^{2}\delta_{ij},\qquad\eta^{ij}=R^{-2}\delta^{ij}

for
1\leq i,j\leq 3
, where
\delta_{ij}
and
\delta^{ij}
are the Kronecker delta, and
\eta^{\mu\nu}
is the inverse of
\eta_{\mu\nu}
. Hence the four momentum
p^{\alpha}
and its covariant components
v_{\alpha}:=\eta_{\alpha\beta}p^{\beta}(=p_{\alpha})
are defined by

(1.3)

p^{\alpha}=\left(R|p|,p\right),\qquad v_{\alpha}=\left(-p^{0},R^{2}p\right)=\left(-R^{-1}|v|,v\right),

due to the mass shell condition

\eta_{\alpha\beta}p^{\alpha}p^{\beta}=-m^{2}=\eta^{\alpha\beta}v_{\alpha}v_{\beta}

with
m=0
. Here
R=R(t)
denotes the cosmic scale factor that is determined by the following two equations (called the Friedmann and the acceleration equations respectively)

\left(\frac{\dot{R}}{R}\right)^{2}=\frac{8\pi}{3}en,\qquad\frac{\ddot{R}}{R}=-\frac{4\pi}{3}\left\{en+3P\right\}

where the dot denotes the derivative with respect to
t
. In the massless case, the scale factor is easily solved from the above equations since

\left(\frac{\dot{R}}{R}\right)^{2}+\frac{\ddot{R}}{R}=0

(see (
1.4
) below) so that it depends only on
R(0)
and
\dot{R}(0)
. Hence, we may assume that the scale factor is given by

R=C(t+t_{0})^{1/2}

for some positive constants
C
and
t_{0}
.
To define the macroscopic quantities
e,n
and
P
, we consider the particle four-flow
N^{\alpha}
and energy-momentum tensor
T^{\alpha\beta}
:

N^{\alpha}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}p^{\alpha}F\,\sqrt{-|\eta|}\frac{dp}{p^{0}},\qquad T^{\alpha\beta}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}p^{\alpha}p^{\beta}F\,\sqrt{-|\eta|}\frac{dp}{p^{0}}.

In the Landau-Lifshitz frame
[
18
]
with the isotropy assumption, both
N^{\alpha}
and
T^{\alpha\beta}
are decomposed as

N^{\alpha}=nU^{\alpha},\qquad T^{\alpha\beta}=(en+p)U^{\alpha}U^{\beta}+p\eta^{\alpha\beta}.

Here the particle number density
n
, the Landau-Lifshitz four-velocity
U^{\alpha}
, the internal energy per particle
e
, and the pressure
P
are defined as follows

(1.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}n&=R^{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}F\,dp,\cr U^{\alpha}&=\frac{R^{3}}{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}p^{\alpha}F\,\frac{dp}{p^{0}}=(1,0,0,0),\cr e&=\frac{R^{4}}{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|p|F\,dp=R\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|p|F\,dp}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}F\,dp},\cr P&=\frac{R^{5}}{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|p|^{2}F\,\frac{dp}{p^{0}},\end{split}

where we used
\sqrt{-|\eta|}=R^{3}
and (
1.3
). In the FLRW case, the nonzero Christoffel symbols are

\Gamma^{0}_{ij}=R\dot{R}\delta_{ij},\qquad\Gamma^{i}_{j0}=\Gamma^{i}_{0j}=\frac{\dot{R}}{R}\delta^{i}_{j}

for
1\leq i,j\leq 3
. In conclusion, the Cauchy problem of the Anderson-Witting model (
1.1
) for massless particles in the FLRW spacetime is reduced into

(1.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{t}F-2\frac{\dot{R}}{R}p\cdot\nabla_{p}F&=J(F)-F,\cr F(0,p)&=F_{0}(p)\end{split}

where the scale factor is

R=C(t+t_{0})^{1/2}.

We then consider the characteristic curve of (
1.5
):

(1.6)

\frac{dp}{dt}=-2\frac{\dot{R}}{R}p,\qquad p(0)=y

which can be solved explicitly:

p(t)=R^{-2}(t)y.

Therefore, in terms of the covariant variable
v=R^{2}(t)p
, (
1.5
) can be simplified further into

(1.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{t}F(t,v)&=J(F)(t,v)-F(t,v),\cr F(0,v)&=F_{0}(v)\end{split}

where the Jüttner distribution
J(F)
is written as

(1.8)

J(F)=\exp\left\{\frac{\mu_{E}}{T_{E}}+\frac{U_{E}^{\alpha}v_{\alpha}}{T_{E}}\right\}

for the relativistic Maxwell-Boltzmann statistics, and

(1.9)

J(F)=\frac{1}{\exp\left\{-\frac{\mu_{E}}{T_{E}}-\frac{U_{E}^{\alpha}v_{\alpha}}{T_{E}}\right\}-1}

for the relativistic Bose-Einstein statistics.
The Cauchy problem (
1.7
) is not as simple as it looks: Due to the specific structure of
J(F)
, the Cauchy problem (
1.7
) must be understood to be coupled to the nonlinear relation (
2.5
) in the case of the relativistic Bose-Einstein statistics:

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\partial_{t}F=\frac{1}{\exp\{c+\gamma|v|\}-1}-F,

\displaystyle\hskip 42.67912pt\beta(c)=\frac{\rho(F)}{\left(3T(F)\right)^{3}},

\displaystyle\gamma=\left\{\rho(F)\right\}^{-\frac{1}{3}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{\exp\left\{c+|v|\right\}-1}\,dv\right)^{\frac{1}{3}},

which makes the problem (
1.7
) a highly nontrivial one (See Lemma
2.2
and (
3.3
)).

To state our main result, we define two types of global equilibriums

(1.10)

J^{0}=\begin{cases}\exp\left\{-|v|\right\}&\text{for the relativistic Maxwell-Boltzmann statistics},\cr\frac{1}{\exp\left\{1+|v|\right\}-1}&\text{for the relativistic Bose-Einstein statistics. }\end{cases}

Our main result is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume
F_{0}=F_{0}(v)
is nonnegative, and
F_{0}
and
J^{0}
share the total particle number and energy in the following sense:

(1.11)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}F_{0}\,dv=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}J^{0}\,dv,\qquad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v|F_{0}\,dv=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v|J^{0}\,dv.

Then the Cauchy problem (
1.7
) is explicitly solved as follows

\displaystyle F(t,v)

\displaystyle=\exp(-t)F_{0}(v)+\left\{1-\exp(-t)\right\}J^{0}.

As is mentioned above, mathematical analysis for BGK models in cosmological framework has never been made in the literature. We refer to
[
21
]
for the study of exact solutions and to
[
3
,
4
]
in the Boltzmann case. For an introduction to the Boltzmann or Vlasov equation for massless particles we refer to
[
2
,
19
,
20
,
27
]
.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we find an explicit form of Jüttner distribution of (
1.8
) and (
1.9
) for massless particles in the FLRW spacetime. In Section 3, we investigate the iteration scheme for (
1.7
) to prove Theorem
1.1
.

2.
Determination of
J(F)

Recall from (
1.8
) and (
1.9
) that the Jüttner distribution
J(F)
has unknown variables
\mu_{E},U_{E}
and
T_{E}
, and using the covariant variable
v
of (
1.3
), unknown variables are determined through the relation

(2.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}U^{\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}v_{\alpha}\left(J(F)-F\right)\frac{1}{\sqrt{-|\eta|}}\,\frac{dv}{v_{0}}&=0,\cr U^{\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}v_{\alpha}\eta^{\mu\beta}v_{\mu}\left(J(F)-F\right)\frac{1}{\sqrt{-|\eta|}}\,\frac{dv}{v_{0}}&=0\end{split}

so that the conservation laws (
1.2
) hold true. In the following lemma, we investigate the explicit form of
J(F)
using the relation (
2.1
) for the case of massless particles in the FLRW spacetime.

Lemma 2.1
.

The explicit form of (
1.8
) satisfying (
2.1
) is given by

J(F)=\frac{\rho}{8\pi T^{3}}\exp\left\{-\frac{|v|}{T}\right\}

where
\rho
and
T
denote

\displaystyle\rho=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}F\,dv,\qquad 3T=\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v|F\,dv}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}F\,dv}.

Proof.

Taking
U_{E}^{\alpha}=U^{\alpha}
(see (
1.4
)), then (
1.8
) becomes

(2.2)

J(F)=\exp\left\{\frac{\mu_{E}}{T_{E}}-\frac{|v|}{RT_{E}}\right\}

and (
2.1
) reduces to

(2.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}J(F)\,dv&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}F\,dv,\cr\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v|J(F)\,dv,0,0,0\right)&=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v|F\,dv,0,0,0\right)\end{split}

due to the isotropic property of
F
. Inserting (
2.2
) into (
2.3
), we have

\displaystyle 8\pi R^{3}T_{E}^{3}\exp\left\{\frac{\mu_{E}}{T_{E}}\right\}

\displaystyle=\rho,

\displaystyle 24\pi R^{4}T_{E}^{4}\exp\left\{\frac{\mu_{E}}{T_{E}}\right\}

\displaystyle=3\rho T,

which leads to

(2.4)

T_{E}=R^{-1}T,\qquad\exp\left\{\frac{\mu_{E}}{T_{E}}\right\}=\frac{\rho}{8\pi T^{3}}.

Putting (
2.4
) into (
2.2
) gives the desired result.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

The explicit form of (
1.9
) satisfying (
2.1
) is given by

J(F)=\frac{1}{\exp\left\{c+\gamma|v|\right\}-1}

where
c
and
\gamma
are determined by the relations

(2.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\beta(c)\equiv\frac{\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{\exp\left\{c+|v|\right\}-1}\,dv\right\}^{4}}{\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{|v|}{\exp\left\{c+|v|\right\}-1}\,dv\right\}^{3}}=\frac{\rho}{(3T)^{3}},\cr&\gamma=\rho^{-\frac{1}{3}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{\exp\left\{c+|v|\right\}-1}\,dv\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}.\end{split}

If
c
is positive, and
F
satisfies

(2.6)

0<\frac{\rho}{(3T)^{3}}<\frac{8\pi}{27}\frac{\left\{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{3}}\right\}^{4}}{\left\{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{4}}\right\}^{3}},

then (
2.5
) determines unique
c
and
\gamma
.

Proof.

\bullet
The explicit form of (
1.9
): As in (
2.2
) of Lemma
2.1
, we can see that
J(F)
satisfying (
2.1
) is given by

(2.7)

J(F)=\frac{1}{\exp\left\{c+\gamma|v|\right\}-1},

where
c
and
\gamma
denote

c=-\frac{\mu_{E}}{T_{E}},\qquad\gamma=\frac{1}{RT_{E}}.

Inserting (
2.7
) into (
2.3
) gives

(2.8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\gamma^{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{\exp\left\{c+|v|\right\}-1}\,dv=\rho,\cr&\gamma^{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{|v|}{\exp\left\{c+|v|\right\}-1}\,dv=3\rho T\end{split}

from which we can derive (
2.5
).

\bullet
Unique determination of
c
and
\gamma
: The integrals of the Bose-Einstein distribution can be represented by

(2.9)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{r^{n}}{\exp\left\{r\right\}/z-1}\,dr=n!\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{k}}{k^{n+1}}

which converges for
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
z<1
. For
c>0
, we put
z=e^{-c}
and apply (
2.9
) to
\beta(c)
of (
2.5
) to see

(2.10)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\beta(c)&=4\pi\frac{\left\{\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{r^{2}}{\exp\left\{c+r\right\}-1}\,dr\right\}^{4}}{\left\{\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{r^{3}}{\exp\left\{c+r\right\}-1}\,dr\right\}^{3}}=4\pi\frac{\left\{2!\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\exp\{-ck\}}{k^{3}}\right\}^{4}}{\left\{3!\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\exp\{-ck\}}{k^{4}}\right\}^{3}}\cr&=\frac{8\pi}{27}\frac{\left\{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\exp\{-ck\}}{k^{3}}\right\}^{4}}{\left\{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\exp\{-ck\}}{k^{4}}\right\}^{3}}.\end{split}

Now we differentiate
\beta(c)
with respect to
c
and use the following relation

\frac{\partial}{\partial c}\left\{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\exp\{-ck\}}{k^{n}}\right\}=-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\exp\{-ck\}}{k^{n-1}}

to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dc}\beta(c)

\displaystyle=\frac{8\pi}{27}\left[-4\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\exp\{-ck\}}{k^{2}}\frac{\left\{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\exp\{-ck\}}{k^{3}}\right\}^{3}}{\left\{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\exp\{-ck\}}{k^{4}}\right\}^{3}}+3\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\exp\{-ck\}}{k^{3}}\frac{\left\{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\exp\{-ck\}}{k^{3}}\right\}^{4}}{\left\{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\exp\{-ck\}}{k^{4}}\right\}^{4}}\right]

\displaystyle=\beta(c)\left[-4\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\exp\{-ck\}}{k^{2}}}{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\exp\{-ck\}}{k^{3}}}+3\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\exp\{-ck\}}{k^{3}}}{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\exp\{-ck\}}{k^{4}}}\right]

\displaystyle=\beta(c)\left\{\frac{3\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\exp\{-ck\}}{k^{3}}\right)^{2}-4\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\exp\{-ck\}}{k^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\exp\{-ck\}}{k^{4}}}{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\exp\{-ck\}}{k^{3}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\exp\{-ck\}}{k^{4}}}\right\}

which is strictly negative since

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\exp\{-ck\}}{k^{3}}\right)^{2}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\exp\{-ck\}}{k^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\exp\{-ck\}}{k^{4}}.

Thus
\beta(c)
is stricitly decreasing on
c\in(0,\infty)
. On the other hand, the limiting behavior of
\beta(c)
is

\displaystyle\lim_{c\rightarrow 0}\beta(c)

\displaystyle=\frac{8\pi}{27}\lim_{c\rightarrow 0}\frac{\left\{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\exp\{-ck\}}{k^{3}}\right\}^{4}}{\left\{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\exp\{-ck\}}{k^{4}}\right\}^{3}}=\frac{8\pi}{27}\frac{\left\{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{3}}\right\}^{4}}{\left\{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{4}}\right\}^{3}},

and

\displaystyle\lim_{c\rightarrow\infty}\beta(c)

\displaystyle=\frac{8\pi}{27}\lim_{c\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\left\{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\exp\{-ck\}}{k^{3}}\right\}^{4}}{\left\{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\exp\{-ck\}}{k^{4}}\right\}^{3}}=0.

So we can see that the range of
\beta(c)
is
\left(0,\lim_{c\rightarrow 0}\beta(c)\right)
on
c\in(0,\infty)
. Since we have assumed (
2.6
), one finds

0<\frac{\rho}{(3T)^{3}}<\lim_{c\rightarrow 0}\beta(c).

Therefore, there is a one-to-one correspondence between
c
and
\rho/(3T)^{3}
of (
2.5
), which guarantees the unique determination of
c
and
\gamma
.
∎

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

The proof of Theorem
1.1
for the relativistic Maxwell-Boltzmann statistics is straightforward. Recall from Lemma
2.1
that
J(F)
for the relativistic Maxwell-Boltzmann statistics is defined by

J(F)=\frac{\rho}{8\pi T^{3}}\exp\left\{-\frac{|v|}{T}\right\}

with

\rho=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}F\,dv,\qquad 3T=\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v|F\,dv}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}F\,dv}

and it satisfies (
2.3
):

(3.1)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}J(F)-F\,\,dv=0,\qquad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v|(J(F)-F)\,\,dv=0.

In terms of (
1.7
), this leads to the following conservation laws

(3.2)

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}F\,dv=0,\qquad\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v|F\,dv=0.

Since we assumed

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}F_{0}\,dv

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}J^{0}\,dv(=8\pi),

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v|F_{0}\,dv

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v|J^{0}\,dv(=24\pi),

combining (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) gives

\rho=8\pi,\qquad T=1,

and hence

J(F)=J^{0}.

Therefore (
1.7
) is explicitly solved as

\displaystyle F(t,v)

\displaystyle=\exp(-t)F_{0}(v)+\big(1-\exp(-t)\big)J^{0}.

In the case of relativistic Bose-Einstein statistics, it is not obvious because, by Lemma
2.2

J(F)
is well-defined in a way to satisfiy (
3.1
) only when the solution
F
satisfies (
2.6
).
For this, we consider the following iteration scheme that for
n\geq 0
,

(3.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\hskip 14.22636pt\partial_{t}F^{n+1}=\frac{1}{\exp\{c^{n}+\gamma^{n}|v|\}-1}-F^{n+1},\cr&\hskip 42.67912pt\beta(c^{n})=\frac{\rho(F^{n})}{\left(3T(F^{n})\right)^{3}},\cr&\gamma^{n}=\left\{\rho(F^{n})\right\}^{-\frac{1}{3}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{\exp\left\{c^{n}+|v|\right\}-1}\,dv\right)^{\frac{1}{3}},\end{split}

where we set
F^{n}(0,v)=F_{0}(v)
and
c^{n}>0
.
Then it follows from (
1.11
) that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}F^{0}\,dv=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}J^{0}\,dv,\qquad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v|F^{0}\,dv=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v|J^{0}\,dv.

Inserting
F^{0}
into the definition of
\rho/(3T)^{3}
, this gives

0<\frac{\rho(F^{0})}{\left\{3T(F^{0})\right\}^{3}}=\frac{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}F^{0}\,dv\right)^{4}}{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v|F^{0}\,dv\right)^{3}}=\frac{\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}J^{0}\,dv\right\}^{4}}{\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v|J^{0}\,dv\right\}^{3}}=\beta(1).

Since
\beta(c)
is strictly decreasing on
c\in(0,\infty)
(see the proof of Lemma
2.2
), we have

0<\frac{\rho(F^{0})}{\left\{3T(F^{0})\right\}^{3}}=\beta(1)<\lim_{c\rightarrow 0}\beta(c)=\frac{8\pi}{27}\frac{\left\{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{3}}\right\}^{4}}{\left\{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{4}}\right\}^{3}},

which says that
F^{0}
satisfies (
2.6
). So we conclude by Lemma
2.2
that
c^{0}
is equal to
1
and

\gamma^{0}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}J^{0}\,dv\right)^{-\frac{1}{3}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{\exp\left\{1+|v|\right\}-1}\,dv\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}=1,

and hence

\frac{1}{\exp\{c^{0}+\gamma^{0}|v|\}-1}=J^{0}.

Thus
F^{1}
of (
3.3
) satisfies

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(\begin{matrix}1\cr|v|\end{matrix}\right)F^{1}(t,v)\,dv

\displaystyle=\exp(-t)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(\begin{matrix}1\cr|v|\end{matrix}\right)F_{0}(v)\,dv+\big(1-\exp(-t)\big)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(\begin{matrix}1\cr|v|\end{matrix}\right)J^{0}\,dv

\displaystyle=\exp(-t)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(\begin{matrix}1\cr|v|\end{matrix}\right)J^{0}\,dv+\big(1-\exp(-t)\big)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(\begin{matrix}1\cr|v|\end{matrix}\right)J^{0}\,dv

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(\begin{matrix}1\cr|v|\end{matrix}\right)J^{0}\,dv.

Applying the same argument, we get

\frac{1}{\exp\{c^{1}+\gamma^{1}|v|\}-1}=J^{0}.

Proceeding in the same manner leads to

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(\begin{matrix}1\cr|v|\end{matrix}\right)F^{n}(t,v)\,dv=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(\begin{matrix}1\cr|v|\end{matrix}\right)F_{0}\,dv,

and hence

\frac{1}{\exp\{c^{n}+\gamma^{n}|v|\}-1}=J^{0}

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Therefore, the iteration (
3.3
) can be explicitly solved as

\displaystyle F^{n+1}(t,v)

\displaystyle=\exp(-t)F_{0}(v)+\big(1-\exp(-t)\big)J^{0}

which gives the desired result.

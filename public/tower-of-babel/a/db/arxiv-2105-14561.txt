Rotationally Symmetric Extremal Kähler Metrics on ℂ n \mathbb{C}^{n} and ℂ 2 ∖ { 0 } \mathbb{C}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
Selin Taşkent

Abstract
In this paper, we study rotationally symmetric extremal Kähler metrics on ℂ n \mathbb{C}^{n} ( n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 ) and ℂ 2 \ { 0 } \mathbb{C}^{2}\backslash\{0\} . We present a classification of such metrics based on the zeros of the polynomial appearing in Calabi’s Extremal Equation. As applications, we prove that there are no U ⁡ ( n ) U(n) invariant complete extremal Kähler metrics on ℂ n \mathbb{C}^{n} with positive bisectional curvature, and we give a smooth extension lemma for U ⁡ ( n ) U(n) invariant extremal Kähler metrics on ℂ n \ { 0 } \mathbb{C}^{n}\backslash\{0\} . We retrieve known examples of smooth or singular extremal Kähler metrics on Hirzebruch surfaces, bundles over ℂ ​ ℙ 1 \mathbb{CP}^{1} , and weighted complex projective spaces. We also show that certain solutions on ℂ 2 \ { 0 } \mathbb{C}^{2}\backslash\{0\} correspond to new complete families of constant-scalar-curvature Kähler and strictly extremal Kähler metrics on complex line bundles over ℂ ​ ℙ 1 \mathbb{CP}^{1} and on ℂ 2 \ { 0 } \mathbb{C}^{2}\backslash\{0\} .

Background

In Riemannian geometry, the curvature tensor can be decomposed into its irreducible components under the action of the orthogonal group. The three components are the scalar curvature, the traceless Ricci curvature and the Weyl curvature. In Kähler geometry, there is a similar decomposition under the action of the unitary group. The Kähler decomposition has the scalar curvature, the traceless Ricci curvature and the Bochner curvature as its components. For a Kähler manifold of real dimension four (
n=2
), the anti-self-dual part of the Weyl tensor is the same as the Bochner tensor. Hence, in real dimension four, Bochner-Kähler metrics and self-dual Kähler metrics are the same. Bochner-Kähler metrics are known to be extremal in the sense of Calabi. It is easy to see that
U(n)
invariant extremal Kähler metrics on
\mathbb{C}^{n}\backslash\{0\}
extend smoothly to the origin if and only if they are Bochner-Kähler. (we include this as an equivalent condition in our smooth extension lemma (Lemma
3.5
)). The first examples of
U(n)
invariant Bochner-Kähler metrics with non-constant scalar curvature were found by Tachibana and Liu
[
TL70
]
, using a Kähler potential defined on an interval
s\in I\subset\mathbb{R}
, (
s=|z|^{2}
).

In
[
TL70
]
, Tachibana-Liu give the ansatz for
U(n)
invariant Bochner-Kähler metrics on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. In
[
Bry01
]
, Bryant gives a classification of these metrics, and determines that
U(n)
invariant complete Bochner-Kähler metrics with non-constant scalar curvature are unique (up to scaling). Furthermore, he gives a complete classification of Bochner-Kähler metrics in dimension
n
. He shows that when singularities are allowed, every weighted projective space supports a Bochner-Kähler metric.

In
[
AG02
]
, Apostolov-Gauduchon obtain a local classification of self-dual Hermitian-Einstein metrics, and this implies a local classification of self-dual Kähler metrics. They prove that such metrics have local cohomogeneity at most
2

n=2

Kähler Potentials

We begin with a well-known decomposition of

Let

\omega=i\partial\overline{\partial}u=i\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}(\delta_{jk}u^{\prime}(s)+u^{\prime\prime}(s)\overline{z}_{j}z_{k})dz^{j}\wedge d\overline{z}^{k}

(3)

gives a positive definite Kähler metric on

if and only if

u^{\prime}(s)>0,\qquad u^{\prime}(s)+su^{\prime\prime}(s)>0.

(4)

We introduce the function
g(s)=su^{\prime}(s)
and reformulate (

g(s)>0,\qquad g^{\prime}(s)>0.

(5)

We note that the function

\displaystyle\lim_{s\rightarrow 0^{+}}g(s)=A
and
\displaystyle\lim_{s\rightarrow\infty}g(s)=B

Let us write the metric (
3

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{s}g(s)\delta_{jk}+\frac{1}{s^{2}}(sg^{\prime}(s)-g(s))\overline{z}_{j}z_{k}\right)dz^{j}\otimes d\overline{z}^{k}

\displaystyle=\left(g(s)\left(\frac{1}{s}\delta_{jk}-\frac{1}{s^{2}}\overline{z}_{j}z_{k}\right)+sg^{\prime}(s)\left(\frac{1}{s^{2}}\overline{z}_{j}z_{k}\right)\right)dz^{j}\otimes d\overline{z}^{k}

(6)

We will view
\mathbb{CP}^{n-1}
as the quotient

as well as the quotient
S^{2n-1}(1)/\penalty S^{1}

The real part of the standard Hermitian product on

\mathfrak{g}_{FS}

The metric (

]
by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}

\displaystyle=g(s)(\mathfrak{g}_{S^{2n-1}}-\eta\otimes\eta)+sg^{\prime}(s)\left(\frac{1}{4s^{2}}ds\otimes ds+\eta\otimes\eta\right)

(7)

\displaystyle=g(s)\pi_{1}^{*}\mathfrak{g}_{FS}+sg^{\prime}(s)\mathfrak{g}_{cyl}.

Here

The nonzero terms are denoted by

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=R_{1\overline{1}1\overline{1}}=-\frac{1}{g^{\prime}}\left(\frac{sg^{\prime\prime}}{g^{\prime}}\right)^{\prime}

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=R_{i\overline{i}i\overline{i}}=2R_{i\overline{i}j\overline{j}}=-\frac{2u^{\prime\prime}}{(u^{\prime})^{2}}

41
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle(a^{2}+2a(\alpha+\beta)+b^{2}+\alpha\beta)g^{\prime}\left\{c_{1}\frac{c_{1}}{g(s)-\alpha}+\frac{c_{2}}{g(s)-\beta}+\right.

\displaystyle\left.\frac{-(c_{1}+c_{2})g(s)+2(c_{1}+c_{2})a-c_{1}\alpha-c_{2}\beta}{g^{2}(s)-2ag(s)+a^{2}+b^{2}}\right\}=\frac{1}{s},

where
c_{1}=\frac{\alpha}{(\alpha-\beta)(\alpha^{2}-2a\alpha+a^{2}+b^{2})}
and
c_{2}=\frac{\beta}{(\beta-\alpha)(\beta^{2}-2a\beta+a^{2}+b^{2})}
.

On the other hand, Lemma
5.4
implies that there exists a unique smooth, strictly increasing function
g(s):(0,\infty)\rightarrow(\alpha,\beta)
determined by

\displaystyle(a^{2}+2a(\alpha+\beta)+b^{2}+\alpha\beta)\left\{c_{1}\log(g(s)-\alpha)+c_{2}\log(\beta-g(s))+\right.

(
35
)

\displaystyle\left.\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}\frac{-(c_{1}+c_{2})g(s)+2(c_{1}+c_{2})a-c_{1}\alpha-c_{2}\beta}{g^{2}(s)-2ag(s)+a^{2}+b^{2}}g^{\prime}(s)ds\right\}=\log s.

Here we note that the integral in equation (
35
) is a proper integral, since the denominator is never zero.

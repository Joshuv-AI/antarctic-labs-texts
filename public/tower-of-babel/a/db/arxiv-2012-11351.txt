Existence results and iterative method for solving a fourth order nonlinear integro-differential equation
Dang Quang A, Dang Quang Long

Abstract
In this paper we consider a class of fourth order nonlinear integro-differential equations with Navier boundary conditions. By the reduction of the problem to operator equation we establish the existence and uniqueness of solution and construct a numerical method for solving it. We prove that the method is of second order accuracy and obtain an estimate for total error. Some examples demonstrate the validity of the obtained theoretical results and the efficiency of the numerical method.



Keywords:
Fourth order boundary value problem; Integro-differential equation; Existence and uniqueness of solution; Iterative method; Total error.

Lemma 2.1

If the function

Due to the above lemma we shall study the original BVP (

u(x)=\int_{0}^{1}G_{0}(x,s)\varphi(s)ds,\quad 0<x<1,

(10)

Set

\displaystyle\begin{split}M_{0}&=\max_{0\leq x\leq 1}\int_{0}^{1}|G_{0}(x,s)|ds,\\

M_{2}&=\max_{0\leq x\leq 1}\int_{0}^{1}|k(x,s)|ds\end{split}

(12)

&|v|\leq M_{1}M,\;|z|\leq M_{0}M_{2}M\}.\end{split}

Theorem 2.2
(Existence and uniqueness)

Suppose that the function
k(x,t)
is continuous in the square
[0,1]\times[0,1]
and there exist numbers
M>0
,
L_{0},L_{1},L_{2}\geq 0
such that:

(i)

The function
f(x,u,v,z)
is continuous in the domain
\mathcal{D}_{M}
and
|f(x,u,v,z)|\leq M
,
\forall(x,u,v,z)\in\mathcal{D}_{M}
.

(ii)

|f(x_{2},u_{2},v_{2},z_{2})-f(x_{1},u_{1},v_{1},z_{1})|\leq L_{0}|u_{2}-u_{1}|+L_{1}|v_{2}-v_{1}|+L_{2}|z_{2}-z_{1}|,\;\forall(x_{i},u_{i},v_{i},z_{i})\in\mathcal{D}_{M},\;i=1,2.

(iii)

q=L_{0}M_{0}+L_{1}M_{1}+L_{2}M_{0}M_{2}<1

u\in C^{4}[0,1]

|u(x)|\leq M_{0}M,

|u^{\prime}(x)|\leq M_{1}M
for any
0\leq x\leq 1.

Proof.

Under the assumptions of the theorem we shall prove that the operator
A
is a contraction mapping in the closed ball
B[O,M]
. Then the operator equation (
6

u\in C^{(4)}[0,1]

Indeed, take
\varphi\in B[O,M]
. Then the problem (
5
) has a unique solution of the form (
7

|u(x)|\leq M_{0}\|\varphi\|

. Analogously, we have
\|u^{\prime}(x)\|\leq M_{1}\|\varphi\|
for all
x\in[0.1]
. Denote by

Then from the last equation in (

. Thus, if

Therefore,
(x,u(x),u^{\prime}(x),(Ku)(x))\in\mathcal{D}_{M}
. By the assumption
(i)

|f(x,u(x),u^{\prime}(x),(Ku)(x))|\leq M\quad\forall x\in[0,1].

Hence,
|(A\varphi)(x)|\leq M\forall x\in[0,1]

Next, take
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\in B[O,M]

(ii)

(iii)

\displaystyle\|A\varphi_{2}-A\varphi_{1}\|\leq(L_{0}M_{0}+L_{1}M_{1}+L_{2}M_{0}M_{2})\|\varphi_{2}-\varphi_{1}\|=q\|\varphi_{2}-\varphi_{1}\|.

3
Iterative method

Consider the following iterative method

1.

Given

\varphi_{0}(x)=f(x,0,0,0),

(15)

2.

Knowing
\varphi_{m}(x)

(m=0,1,...)
compute

\begin{split}u_{m}(x)&=\int_{0}^{1}G_{0}(x,t)\varphi_{m}(t)dt,\\
v_{m}(x)&=\int_{0}^{1}G_{1}(x,t)\varphi_{m}(t)dt,\\
z_{m}(x)&=\int_{0}^{1}k(x,t)u_{m}(t)dt\end{split}

(16)

3.

Update

\begin{split}\varphi_{m+1}(x)&=f(x,u_{m}(x),v_{m}(x),z_{m}(x)).\end{split}

(17)

. Therefore, it converges with the rate of geometric progression and there holds the estimate

\|\varphi_{m}-\varphi\|\leq\frac{q^{m}}{1-q}\|\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{0}\|=p_{m}d,

where
\varphi
is the fixed point of the operator
A

p_{m}=\tfrac{q^{m}}{1-q},\;d=\|\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{0}\|.

(18)

These estimates imply the following result of the convergence of the iterative method (

Theorem 3.1

The iterative method (

)-(
17

u_{k}(t)

\displaystyle\|u_{m}-u\|

\displaystyle\leq M_{0}p_{m}d,\;\|u^{\prime}_{m}-u^{\prime}\|\leq M_{1}p_{m}d,

where

Given

\Phi_{0}(x_{i})=f(x_{i},0,0,0),\ i=0,...,N;

Proposition 3.2

Assume that the function
f(t,u,v,z)
has all continuous partial derivatives up to second order in the domain
\mathcal{D}_{M}
and the kernel function

has all continuous partial derivatives up to second order in the square

\varphi_{m}(x),u_{m}(x),v_{m}(x),z_{m}(x),m=0,1,...

\varphi_{m}(x)\in C^{2}[0,1]

,

Proof.

We prove the proposition by induction. For

by the assumption on the function

we have
\varphi_{0}(t)\in C^{2}[0,1]
since
\varphi_{0}(x)=f(x,0,0,0)
. Taking into account that

\displaystyle u_{0}(x)=\int_{0}^{1}G_{0}(x,t)\varphi_{0}(t)dt

\displaystyle u_{0}^{(4)}(x)

\displaystyle=\varphi_{0}(x),\quad x\in(0,1),

\displaystyle u_{0}(0)

\displaystyle=u_{0}(1)=u_{0}^{\prime\prime}(0)=u_{0}^{\prime\prime}(1)=0.

Therefore,
u_{0}(x)\in C^{6}[0,1]
. It implies that

. Since by assumptions

Now suppose

For any function
\varphi(x)\in C^{2}[0,1]

\int_{0}^{1}G_{n}(x_{i},t)\varphi(t)dt=\sum_{j=0}^{N}h\rho_{j}G_{n}(x_{i},t_{j})\varphi(t_{j})+O(h^{2}),\quad(n=0,1),\\

(22)

Proposition 3.4

Under the assumption of Proposition
3.2
for any

there hold the estimates

\|\Phi_{m}-\varphi_{m}\|=O(h^{2}),\quad\|U_{m}-u_{m}\|=O(h^{2}),

(23)

\begin{split}\|V_{m}-v_{m}\|=O(h^{2}),\;\|Z_{m}-z_{m}\|=O(h^{2}).\end{split}

(24)

where
\|.\|=\|.\|_{C(\bar{\omega}_{h})}
is the max-norm of function on the grid

Proof.
We prove the proposition by induction. For

\|\Phi_{0}-\varphi_{0}\|=0

5
Conclusion

In this paper we have established the existence and uniqueness of solution for a fourth order nonlinear integro-differential equation with the Navier boundary conditions and proposed an iterative method at both continuous and discrete levels for finding the solution. The second order of accuracy of the discrete method has been proved. Some examples, where the exact solution is known and is not known, demonstrate the validity of the obtained theoretical results and the efficiency of the iterative method. It should be emphasized that for the example of Wang in
[
17
]
we have established the existence and uniqueness of solution and found it numerically but Wang could prove only the convergence of the iterative sequences constructed by the monotone method to extremal solutions.

The method used in this paper with appropriate modifications can be applied to nonlinear integro-differential equations of any order with other boundary conditions and more complicated nonlinear terms. This is the direction of our research in the future.

Integral of scalar curvature on manifolds with a pole Thanks: The author was partially supported by Beijing Natural Science Foundation Z190003, NSFC 11771230 and NSFC 12141103.
Guoyi Xu

Abstract
. On any complete three dimensional Riemannian manifold with a pole and non-negative Ricci curvature, we show that the asymptotic scaling invariant integral of scalar curvature, is equal to a term determined by the asymptotic volume ratio of this Riemannian manifold. Mathematics Subject Classification: 53C20, 53C21.



1.
Introduction

Since the well-known Gauss-Bonnet Theorem, there are a lot of studies around the integral of scalar curvature. Especially, Cohn-Vossen
[
3
]
proved:

Theorem 1.1
.

If
(M^{2},g)
is a finitely connected, complete, oriented Riemannian manifold, and assume
\int_{M^{2}}R
exists as an extended real number, then

\displaystyle\int_{M^{2}}R\leq 4\pi\cdot\chi(M^{2}),

where
R
is the scalar curvature and
\chi(M^{2})
is the Euler characteristic of
(M^{2},g)
respectively.

Motivated to get a generalization of the Cohn-Vossen’s inequality, Yau
[
15
, Problem
9
]
posed the following question:

Question 1.2
.

Given a
n
-dimensional complete manifold
(M^{n},g)
with
Rc\geq 0
, let
B_{p}(r)
be the geodesic ball around
p\in M^{n}
and
\sigma_{k}
be the
k
-th elementary symmetric function of the Ricci tensor, is it true that
\displaystyle\lim\limits_{r\rightarrow\infty}r^{-n+2k}\int_{B_{p}(r)}\sigma_{k}<\infty
?

Related to the above question, Shi and Yau
[
12
]
gave a scaling invariant upper bound estimate for the average integral of the scalar curvature (note that the scalar curvature is
\sigma_{1}
in Question
1.2
), on Kähler manifolds with bounded, pinched, nonnegative holomorphic bisectional curvature. Also see
[
11
]
for discussion with respect to manifolds with lower bound of sectional curvature.

Later Yang
[
14
]
constructed examples, which answered Question
1.2
for
k>1
negatively. But the case
k=1
is left open, where
\sigma_{1}
is the scalar curvature
R
. We reformulate the rest case of Question
1.2
as follows.

Question 1.3

(Yau)
.

For any complete Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},g)
with
Rc\geq 0
, any
p\in M^{n}
, is it true that

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{r\rightarrow\infty}r^{2-n}\int_{B_{p}(r)}R<\infty?

Let
\omega_{n}
be the volume of the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we recall the definition of the
asymptotic volume ratio
of the manifold
M^{n}
with
Rc\geq 0
:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{V}_{M^{n}}\vcentcolon=\lim\limits_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathrm{Vol}(B_{p}(r))}{\omega_{n}r^{n}}\in[0,1].

Using the monotonicity formulas of Colding and Minicozzi
[
4
]
, we
[
13
]
obtained: for a complete non-compact, non-parabolic Riemannian manifold
M^{3}
with
Rc\geq 0
, there is

\displaystyle\varlimsup_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\int_{b\leq r}R\cdot|\nabla b|}{r}\leq 8\pi\big[1-\mathrm{V}_{M^{3}}\big],

where
b=G^{-1}
and
G
is the positive Green’s function of
(M^{3},g)
.

If there is
p\in(M^{n},g)
such that
\exp_{p}:T_{p}M^{n}\rightarrow M^{n}
is a diffeomorphism, we call
(M^{n},g)
is a Riemannian manifold with a
pole
(also see
[
6
]
). Among other things, for
3
-dim Riemannian manifolds with a pole and
Rc\geq 0
, Zhu
[
16
]
,
[
17
]
proved

\displaystyle\varlimsup_{r\rightarrow\infty}r^{-1}\int_{B_{p}(r)}R\leq 20\pi.

In this note, we prove the following theorem, which answers Question
1.3
for
3
-dim manifolds with a pole and
Rc\geq 0
.

Theorem 1.4
.

If
(M^{3},g)
is a Riemannian manifold with a pole and
Rc\geq 0
, then

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\int_{B_{q}(r)}R}{r}=8\pi(1-\mathrm{V}_{M^{3}}),\quad\quad\quad\forall q\in M^{3}.

Recall that
(M^{n},g)
is called
Ricci-pinched
if there is
\epsilon>0
such that

\displaystyle Rc\geq\epsilon\cdot R\cdot g\geq 0.

Hamilton has proposed the following conjecture:

Conjecture 1.5
.

If
(M^{3},g)
is a complete, non-compact Riemannian manifold and Ricci pinched, then
(M^{3},g)
is flat.

The conjecture has been verified with additional assumption by
[
2
]
and
[
10
]
. For recent progress on Conjecture
1.5
, see
[
5
]
and
[
8
]
for discussion using Ricci flow (also see
[
7
]
for argument by
the inverse mean curvature flow).

In this note, we prove the following result as a byproduct of the proof of Theorem
1.4
, which is a special case of Conjecture
1.5
.

Theorem 1.6
.

If
(M^{3},g)
is a Riemannian manifold with a pole and Ricci pinched, then
(M^{3},g)
is flat.

We conclude this section with the sketch of the proof of Theorem
1.4
and the structure of the paper. By the Gauss-Codazzi equation, we firstly decompose the scalar curvature into three parts.

The first part is the intrinsic scalar curvature of geodesic sphere, whose integral can be calculated by Gauss-Bonnet Theorem; because geodesic sphere is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{S}^{2}
in our case.

The second part and third part involve the Ricci curvature along the radial direction and the principal curvatures of the geodesic spheres. By Calabi’s classical computation, we reduce these two parts as the Ricci curvature along the radial direction and the radial derivative of geodesic spheres’ area.

One difficulty we need to overcome is estimating the average integral of the Ricci curvature along the radial direction. We firstly observe the derivative of volume elements of geodesic sphere along the radial direction is non-negative on manifolds with a pole. This observation and Gromov-Bishop’s Volume Comparison Theorem, reduce the estimate of radial Ricci curvature to the estimate of the radial derivative of geodesic spheres’ area.

Then we expand (or shrink) the geodesic ball suitably, such that the radial derivative of suitable geodesic sphere’ area can be represented as the difference of two geodesic spheres’ area, by the help of the Mean Value Theorem. This trick is partially motivated by the argument in
[
11
]
. Again combining the Gromov-Bishop’s Volume Comparison Theorem, we get the estimate of the radial derivative of geodesic spheres’ area. Furthermore, the average integral of the Ricci curvature along the radial direction vanishes.

The structure of the paper is as follows. The estimate of the radial derivative of suitable geodesic sphere’ area is discussed in Section
2
. Then we present the estimate of Ricci curvature along the radial direction in Section
3
. Finally, the proof of Theorem
1.4
and Theorem
1.6
is provided in Section
4
.

2.
The volume of geodesic spheres

Without loss of generality, we assume
p
is one pole of
(M^{3},g)
in the rest argument. And we always use
\rho(x)=d(p,x)
unless otherwise mentioned.

For any
q\in M^{n}
, we can write
q=(r,\theta)
in terms of polar normal coordinates at
p
, where
r=d(p,q)
. And it is well known that we can write the volume element of
(M^{n},g)
as

(2.1)

\displaystyle d\mu_{g}=J(t,\theta)dtd\theta,

where
d\theta
is the area element of the unit
(n-1)
-sphere. The area element of geodesic sphere
\partial B_{p}(t)
is given by
J(t,\theta)d\theta
.

Now we recall the Bishop-Gromov Volume Comparison Theorem for manifolds with a pole (see
[
9
]
etc.) as follows:

Theorem 2.1

(Bishop-Gromov)
.

If
(M^{n},g)
is a Riemannian manifold with a pole and
Rc\geq 0
, assume
s\geq t>0
, then

\displaystyle\frac{J(s,\theta)}{J(t,\theta)}\leq\frac{s^{n-1}}{t^{n-1}},\quad\quad\frac{J^{\prime}}{J}(t,\theta)\leq\frac{n-1}{t},\quad\quad J(t,\theta)\leq t^{n-1},

\displaystyle\frac{V(B_{p}(s))}{V(B_{p}(t))}\leq\frac{s^{n}}{t^{n}},\quad\quad\quad V(B_{p}(t))\leq\omega_{n}t^{n}.

∎

Define
\mathscr{A}(t)=V(\partial B_{p}(t))
, where
t>0
. From (
1.1
) and Theorem
2.1
, we get

(2.2)

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathscr{A}(r)}{4\pi r^{2}}=\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}J(r,\theta)d\theta}{4\pi r^{2}}=\mathrm{V}_{M^{3}}.

Fixing
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, for any
s>0
, there are
b_{s}\in((1-\epsilon)s,s),c_{s}\in(s,(1+\epsilon)s)
such that

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}^{\prime}(b_{s})=\frac{\mathscr{A}(s)-\mathscr{A}((1-\epsilon)s)}{\epsilon s},\quad\quad\mathscr{A}^{\prime}(c_{s})=\frac{\mathscr{A}((1+\epsilon)s)-\mathscr{A}(s)}{\epsilon s}.

For any
s>a
, we define
A_{a,s}=B_{p}(s)-B_{p}(a)
in the rest argument.

Lemma 2.2
.

If
(M^{3},g)
is a Riemannian manifold with a pole
p
and
Rc\geq 0
, then for any
a>0
, we have

\displaystyle\varlimsup_{s\rightarrow\infty}\int_{A_{a,c_{s}}}\frac{-J^{\prime\prime}}{s\cdot J}=4\pi\mathrm{V}_{M^{3}}(-2-\epsilon),\quad\quad\varliminf_{s\rightarrow\infty}\int_{A_{a,b_{s}}}\frac{-J^{\prime\prime}}{s\cdot J}=4\pi\mathrm{V}_{M^{3}}\cdot(-2+\epsilon).

Proof:
Now using (
2.2
), we get

\displaystyle\varlimsup_{s\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{s}\int_{A_{a,c_{s}}}(-\frac{J^{\prime\prime}}{J})=\varlimsup_{s\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathscr{A}^{\prime}(a)-\mathscr{A}^{\prime}(c_{s})}{s}=\varlimsup_{s\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathscr{A}(s)-\mathscr{A}((1+\epsilon)s)}{\epsilon s^{2}}

\displaystyle=4\pi\mathrm{V}_{M^{3}}(-2-\epsilon).

\displaystyle\varliminf_{s\rightarrow\infty}\int_{A_{a,b_{s}}}\frac{-J^{\prime\prime}}{s\cdot J}=\varliminf_{s\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathscr{A}^{\prime}(a)-\mathscr{A}^{\prime}(b_{s})}{s}=\varliminf_{s\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathscr{A}((1-\epsilon)s)-\mathscr{A}(s)}{\epsilon s^{2}}.

\displaystyle=4\pi\mathrm{V}_{M^{3}}\cdot(\epsilon-2).

∎

3.
The Ricci curvature along the radial direction

Lemma 3.1
.

If
(M^{3},g)
is a Riemannian manifold with a pole and
Rc\geq 0
,

\displaystyle 0\leq\frac{J^{\prime}}{J}(t,\theta)\leq\frac{2}{t},\quad\quad\quad\quad\forall\ t\in\mathbb{R}^{+},\theta\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}.

Proof:
From
[
1
]
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{J^{\prime}}{J}=\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2},\quad\quad\quad(\frac{J^{\prime}}{J})^{\prime}=-(\lambda_{1}^{2}+\lambda_{2}^{2})-Rc(\nabla\rho,\nabla\rho),

where
\lambda_{i}
are principal curvature of
\rho^{-1}(t)
where
i=1,2
. Therefore we get

(3.1)

\displaystyle Rc(\nabla\rho)=2\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}-\frac{J^{\prime\prime}}{J}.

By (
3.1
), we have

(3.2)

\displaystyle Rc(\nabla\rho)\leq\frac{1}{2}(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})^{2}-\frac{J^{\prime\prime}}{J}=\frac{1}{2}(\frac{J^{\prime}}{J})^{2}-\frac{J^{\prime\prime}}{J}.

From (
3.2
), we obtain

(3.3)

\displaystyle(-\frac{1}{2}\frac{J^{\prime}}{J})^{\prime}\geq\frac{1}{2}Rc(\nabla\rho)+(\frac{1}{2}\frac{J^{\prime}}{J})^{2}\geq(\frac{1}{2}\frac{J^{\prime}}{J})^{2}.

Note
J(t)>0
and
J^{\prime}(t)
is well-defined for any
t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, because
M^{3}
is a Riemannian manifold with a pole. Hence from (
3.3
) we know that

\displaystyle J^{\prime}(t)\geq 0,\quad\quad\quad\quad\forall t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}.

Otherwise
\frac{-J^{\prime}}{J}
will be positive infinity for some finite
t_{0}>0
because of (
3.3
).

Combining Theorem
2.1
, we get the conclusion.

∎

Lemma 3.2
.

If
(M^{3},g)
is a Riemannian manifold with a pole
p
and
Rc\geq 0
, then

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\int_{B_{p}(r)}Rc(\nabla\rho)}{r}=0.

Proof:

Step (1)
. By (
2.2
), for
\epsilon>0
, we can find
a>0
such that
\displaystyle\frac{\mathscr{A}(a)}{a^{2}}\leq 4\pi\mathrm{V}_{M^{3}}(1+\epsilon)
. By Theorem
2.1
, we know that
\frac{J(t,\theta)}{t^{2}}\leq\frac{J(a,\theta)}{a^{2}}
for any
t\geq a
. Therefore, integration by parts yields

\displaystyle\varlimsup_{s\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{s}\int_{A_{a,c_{s}}}\frac{J^{\prime}(t,\theta)}{t\cdot J(t,\theta)}=\varlimsup_{s\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{s}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}d\theta\int_{a}^{c_{s}}\frac{J^{\prime}(t,\theta)}{t}dt

\displaystyle=\varlimsup_{s\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{s}\cdot\Big\{\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\frac{J(c_{s},\theta)}{c_{s}}d\theta+\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}d\theta\int_{a}^{c_{s}}\frac{J}{t^{2}}dt\Big\}

\displaystyle=\varlimsup_{s\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathscr{A}(c_{s})}{s\cdot c_{s}}+\frac{c_{s}-a}{s}\frac{\mathscr{A}(a)}{a^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\epsilon)\cdot\varlimsup_{s\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathscr{A}(c_{s})}{c_{s}^{2}}+(1+\epsilon)\cdot 4\pi\mathrm{V}_{M^{3}}(1+\epsilon)

(3.4)

\displaystyle\leq 8\pi\mathrm{V}_{M^{3}}(1+\epsilon)^{2}.

Step (2)
. Now from (
3.2
) and Lemma
3.1
, we get

(3.5)

\displaystyle Rc(\nabla\rho)(t,\theta)\leq\frac{J^{\prime}(t,\theta)}{t\cdot J(t,\theta)}-\frac{J^{\prime\prime}}{J}(t,\theta).

Using (
3.5
), (
3.4
) and Lemma
2.2
, we get

\displaystyle\varlimsup_{s\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{s}\int_{B_{p}(s)}Rc(\nabla\rho)=\varlimsup_{s\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\int_{A_{a,s}}Rc(\nabla\rho)}{s}\leq\varlimsup_{s\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\int_{A_{a,c_{s}}}Rc(\nabla\rho)}{s}

\displaystyle\leq\varlimsup_{s\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\int_{A_{a,c_{s}}}\frac{J^{\prime}(t,\theta)}{t\cdot J(t,\theta)}}{s}+\varlimsup_{s\rightarrow\infty}\int_{A_{a,c_{s}}}\frac{-J^{\prime\prime}}{s\cdot J}\leq 4\pi\mathrm{V}_{M^{3}}(3\epsilon+2\epsilon^{2}).

Let
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
above, we have

\displaystyle\varlimsup_{s\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{s}\int_{B_{p}(s)}Rc(\nabla\rho)\leq 0.

On the other hand we know that
Rc(\nabla\rho)\geq 0
, therefore the conclusion follows.

∎

4.
The integral of the scalar curvature

Theorem 4.1
.

If
(M^{3},g)
is a Riemannian manifold with a pole and
Rc\geq 0
, then

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\int_{B_{q}(r)}R}{r}=8\pi(1-\mathrm{V}_{M^{3}}),\quad\quad\quad\forall q\in M^{3}.

Proof:

Step (1)
. Recall that
\lambda_{i}
are principal curvature of
\rho^{-1}(t)
where
i=1,2
. From the Gauss-Codazzi equation we have

(4.1)

\displaystyle R(M^{3})(t,\theta)=R(\partial B_{p}(t))(t,\theta)+2Rc(M^{3})\Big(\nabla\rho,\nabla\rho\Big)-2\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2},

where
R(\partial B_{p}(t))(t,\theta)
is the intrinsic scalar curvature of
\partial B_{p}(t)
at
\exp_{p}(t\theta)
and
\exp_{p}
is the exponential map of
M^{3}
.

Note
\rho^{-1}(t)
is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{S}^{2}
. By the co-area formula and the Gauss-Bonnet Theorem for the compact surface
\rho^{-1}(t)
, we get

(4.2)

\displaystyle\int_{\rho^{-1}[a,s]}R(\partial B_{p}(t))dx

\displaystyle=\int_{a}^{s}dt\int_{\rho^{-1}(t)}R(\partial B_{p}(t))=8\pi(s-a).

From (
4.1
) and (
3.1
), we get

\displaystyle\int_{\rho^{-1}[a,s]}R(M^{3})

\displaystyle=\int_{\rho^{-1}[a,s]}\Big\{R(\partial B_{p}(t))+2Rc(M^{3})\Big(\nabla\rho,\nabla\rho\Big)-2\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\Big\}

(4.3)

\displaystyle=8\pi(s-a)+\int_{\rho^{-1}[a,s]}\Big\{Rc(\nabla\rho)-\frac{J^{\prime\prime}}{J}\Big\}.

Step (2)
. From (
4.3
) and
Rc\geq 0
, using Lemma
2.2
and
\frac{b_{s}}{s}\in[1-\epsilon,1]
, we have

\displaystyle\varliminf_{s\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{s}\int_{A_{a,s}}R\geq\varliminf_{s\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{s}\int_{A_{a,b_{s}}}R\geq 8\pi(1-\epsilon)+\varliminf_{s\rightarrow\infty}\int_{A_{a,b_{s}}}\frac{-J^{\prime\prime}}{s\cdot J}

\displaystyle=8\pi(1-\epsilon)+4\pi\mathrm{V}_{M^{3}}\cdot(\epsilon-2).

Let
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
in the above, we get

(4.4)

\displaystyle\varliminf_{s\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{s}\int_{B_{p}(s)}R=\varliminf_{s\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{s}\int_{A_{a,s}}R\geq 8\pi(1-\mathrm{V}_{M^{3}}).

Step (3)
. Using (
4.3
), Lemma
3.2
and Lemma
2.2
, also note
\frac{c_{s}}{s}\in[1,1+\epsilon]
, we gave

\displaystyle\varlimsup_{s\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{s}\int_{B_{p}(s)}R=\varlimsup_{s\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{s}\int_{A_{a,s}}R\leq\varlimsup_{s\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{s}\int_{A_{a,c_{s}}}R

\displaystyle\leq 8\pi(1+\epsilon)+\varlimsup_{s\rightarrow\infty}\frac{c_{s}}{s}\cdot\frac{1}{c_{s}}\int_{B_{p}(c_{s})}Rc(\nabla\rho)+\varlimsup_{s\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{s}\int_{A_{a,c_{s}}}(-\frac{J^{\prime\prime}}{J})

\displaystyle\leq 8\pi(1+\epsilon)+4\pi\mathrm{V}_{M^{3}}(-2-\epsilon).

Let
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
in the above, we obtain

(4.5)

\displaystyle\varlimsup_{s\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{s}\int_{B_{p}(s)}R\leq 8\pi(1-\mathrm{V}_{M^{3}}).

From (
4.4
) and (
4.5
), we get

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\int_{B_{p}(r)}R}{r}=8\pi(1-\mathrm{V}_{M^{3}}).

Because
\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\int_{B_{p}(r)}R}{r}
exists and is finite, it is easy to see that

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\int_{B_{q}(r)}R}{r}=\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\int_{B_{p}(r)}R}{r}=8\pi(1-\mathrm{V}_{M^{3}}),\quad\quad\quad\forall q\in M^{3}.

∎

Corollary 4.2
.

If
(M^{3},g)
is a Riemannian manifold with a pole
p
and
Rc\geq 0
, then

\displaystyle\lim_{s\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathscr{A}^{\prime}(s)}{s}=8\pi\cdot\mathrm{V}_{M^{3}}.

Proof:
From (
4.3
), using Lemma
3.2
and Theorem
1.4
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{s\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathscr{A}^{\prime}(s)}{s}

\displaystyle=\lim_{s\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\rho^{-1}[a,s]}\frac{J^{\prime\prime}}{s\cdot J}=8\pi+\lim_{s\rightarrow\infty}s^{-1}\Big\{\int_{\rho^{-1}[a,s]}Rc(\nabla\rho)-R(M^{3})\Big\}

\displaystyle=8\pi+\lim_{s\rightarrow\infty}s^{-1}\Big\{\int_{B_{p}(s)}Rc(\nabla\rho)-R(M^{3})\Big\}=8\pi\cdot\mathrm{V}_{M^{3}}.

∎

Theorem 4.3
.

If
(M^{3},g)
is a Riemannian manifold with a pole and Ricci pinched, then
(M^{3},g)
is flat.

Proof:
From Ricci pinched assumption, there is
\epsilon>0
such that
Rc\geq\epsilon\cdot R\cdot g
. Now by Lemma
3.2
and Theorem
1.4
, we get

\displaystyle 0=\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\int_{B_{p}(r)}Rc(\nabla\rho)}{r}\geq\epsilon\cdot\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\int_{B_{p}(r)}R}{r}=\epsilon\cdot 8\pi(1-\mathrm{V}_{M^{3}}).

Therefore, we obtain that
\mathrm{V}_{M^{3}}=1
, which implies that
M^{3}
is isometric to
\mathbb{R}^{3}
by the rigidity part of Bishop-Gromov’s Volume Comparison Theorem.

∎

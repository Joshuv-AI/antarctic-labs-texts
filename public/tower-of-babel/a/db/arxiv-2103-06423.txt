Particle Creation in some LRS Bianchi I models
Luis O. Pimentel, Flavio Pineda

Abstract
In this work we consider particle creation by the expansion of the universe, using two Bianchi type I anisotropic models. The particles studied are of spin 0 and 1/2. The cosmological models have rotational symmetry, which allows us to solve exactly the equations of motion. The number density of the created particles is calculated with the method of Bogolubov transformations.



I
Introduction

Quantum effects of the gravitational field are one of the greatest mysteries of nature; having a quantum theory of gravity would be one of the greatest achievements of modern science. However, we do not currently have a satisfactory theory of the quantum nature of the gravitational field that can describe and explaining the various scenarios of the very early universe.
Although we do not have a satisfactory quantum gravity theory, there is no impediment to develop quantum field theory in non-flat space-time; quantum field theory can be developed including gravitational fields without a quantum theory of gravity,using a classical gravitational field, i.e. one given as a Lorentzian metric that is a solution of Einstein’s equations.

The scheme is a theory describing the dynamics of quantum fields propagating in a curved space-time background, described by a Lorentzian manifold with a general classical metric
g_{\mu\nu}
. In this way it is possible to go quite far in generalizing quantum field theory without considering the difficulties and problems involved in any quantum field theory of gravity. The Unruh effect, Hawking radiation, the production of particles in the early universe, the generation of primordial gravitational waves or even the explanation of the isotropy of the universe are some consequences of the quantum field theory in curved space-time. Particle production from vacuum is one of the most amazing predictions of quantum theory; in curved space-time, production takes place because of very intense or changing gravitational fields such as the expansion of the universe, an effect Schrödinger expected to occur
(

II
Asymptotic Solution to the Hamilton Jacobi Equation

We must solve the Hamilton-Jacobi equation for the LRS Bianchi I model given by the metric (
I.1
). To define particles with the exact solution of the KG equation a generally covariant approach should be used, which is based on solutions of the Hamilton-Jacobi equation in the respective space-time. The way to proceed consists of the following steps
(

1.

Solve the Hamilton-Jacobi equation for the respective cosmological model.

2.

Solve the KG and Dirac equations for the respective cosmological model.

3.

Compare the asymptotic behaviour of the solutions of the field equations and the Hamilton-Jacobi equation.

4.

Positive and negative frequency states are obtained
according to the following correspondence

\displaystyle\Phi(x)\sim\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}e^{-iS}&\mbox{Positive frequency states}\\[8.5359pt]
e^{iS}&\mbox{Negative frequency states}\,,\end{array}\right.

(II.7)

\displaystyle-\left(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}f}{\mathrm{d}t}\right)^{2}+t^{-2q}\,k_{\perp}^{2}+t^{2\,(2q-1)}\,k_{z}^{2}+m^{2}=0\,,

where

V
Dirac equation

We proceed to solve the Dirac equation in the Bianchi I model. The Dirac equation in curved space-time can be written as

\displaystyle\left[\tilde{\gamma}^{\mu}(x)\,(\partial_{\mu}-\Gamma_{\mu})+m\right]\Psi(x)=0\,,

(V.55)

where
\Gamma_{\mu}
the spin connections that are calculated through the expression
(
collas
)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\mu}=\dfrac{1}{4}\,\omega_{\mu\,\alpha\,\beta}\,\gamma^{\alpha}\,\gamma^{\beta}\,,

(V.56)

where
\omega_{\mu\alpha\beta}
are Ricci’s rotation coefficients and
e^{\mu}{}_{\alpha}(x)
is a tetrad that satisfies the relation

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}(x)=e_{\mu}{}^{\alpha}\,e_{\nu}{}^{\beta}\,\eta_{\alpha\beta}\,.

(V.57)

Dirac matrices in curved space-time are
\tilde{\gamma}^{\mu}(x)
that are related to gamma matrices
\gamma^{\mu}
of flat spacetime by

\displaystyle\tilde{\gamma}^{\mu}(x)=e^{\mu}{}_{\alpha}(x)\,\gamma^{\alpha}\,,

(V.58)

and comply with the anticommutation rule

\displaystyle\{\tilde{\gamma}^{\mu}\,,\tilde{\gamma}^{\nu}\}=2\,g^{\mu\nu}\,.

(V.59)

The tetrad for the metric is chosen diagonally

\displaystyle e^{0}=\mathrm{d}t\,,\quad e^{1}=t^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\,,\quad e^{2}=t^{q}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\quad e^{3}=t^{1-2q}\,\mathrm{d}z\,,

(V.60)

while the spin connections are given by

\displaystyle\Gamma_{0}=0\,,\quad\Gamma_{1}=\dfrac{1}{2}\,q\,t^{q-1}\,\gamma^{1}\,\gamma^{0}\,,\quad\Gamma_{2}=\dfrac{1}{2}\,q\,t^{q-1}\,\gamma^{2}\,\gamma^{0}\,,\quad\Gamma_{3}=\dfrac{1}{2}\,(1-2q)\,t^{-2q}\,\gamma^{3}\,\gamma^{0}\,,

(V.61)

and Dirac matrices in curved space-time are

\displaystyle\tilde{\gamma}^{0}(x)=\gamma^{0}\,,\quad\tilde{\gamma}^{1}(x)=t^{-q}(x)\gamma^{1}\,,\quad\tilde{\gamma}^{2}(x)=t^{-q}(x)\gamma^{2}\,,\quad\tilde{\gamma}^{3}(x)=t^{2q-1}(x)\gamma^{3}\,.

(V.62)

If
\Psi(x)=t^{-1/2}\,\Psi_{0}(x)
, the equation to be solved is

\displaystyle\left[\gamma^{0}\,t^{q}\,\partial_{t}+\gamma^{1}\,\partial_{x}+\gamma^{2}\,\partial_{y}+t^{3q-1}\,\gamma^{3}\,\partial_{z}+t^{q}\,m\right]\Psi_{0}(x)=0

(V.63)

It is a system of partial differential equations, however the fact that you have two equal and one different direction of propagation makes it difficult to separate variables. There is a general method for separating variables in the Dirac equation in curved space-time developed by Shishkin and Villalba
(
shishkin1
)
. The method consists of writing the Dirac equation in terms of the sum of two first-order differential operators

\displaystyle\{H\}\Psi_{0}(t)=\{H\}\Gamma\,\Gamma^{-1}\,\Psi_{0}(t)=0\Rightarrow(K_{i}+K_{j})\Theta(x)=0\,,\quad[K_{i}\,,K_{j}]=0\,,

(V.64)

where
\Theta(x)=\Gamma\Psi(x)
is an auxiliary spinor and
\Gamma
is a non-singular separation matrix.

We separated variables
(t\,,z)
from
(x\,,y)
; the operators
K_{i}
are

\displaystyle K_{1}=t^{q}[\gamma^{0}\,\partial_{t}+t^{2q-1}\,\gamma^{3}\,\partial_{z}+m]\gamma^{3}\,\gamma^{0}\,,\quad K_{2}=(\gamma^{1}\,\partial_{x}+\gamma^{2}\,\partial_{y})\gamma^{3}\,\gamma^{0}\,,

(V.65)

where we choose
\Gamma=\gamma^{3}\,\gamma^{0}
. We can rewrite the Dirac equation as

\displaystyle(K_{1}+K_{2})\Theta(x)=0\,.

(V.66)

As the Bianchi I model is homogeneous, it can be proposed a solution of the following form

\displaystyle\Theta(x)=e^{i\,\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}}\,\Theta_{0}(t)\,,

(V.67)

where

\displaystyle\Theta_{0}(t)=\begin{pmatrix}\Theta_{1}\\
\Theta_{2}\end{pmatrix}\,,\quad\Theta_{1}(t)=\begin{pmatrix}\xi_{1}\\
\xi_{2}\end{pmatrix}\,,\quad\Theta_{2}(t)=\begin{pmatrix}\xi_{3}\\
\xi_{4}\end{pmatrix}\,.

(V.68)

By separating variables we have

\displaystyle K_{1}\Theta_{0}=-K_{2}\Theta_{0}=-k\Theta_{0}\,,

(V.69)

where
k
is a separation constant which is obtained from the equation

\displaystyle K_{2}\Theta_{0}=i(\gamma^{1}\,k_{x}+\gamma^{2}\,k_{y})\gamma^{3}\,\gamma^{0}\,\Theta_{0}=k\,\Theta_{0}\,.

(V.70)

We use the following representation of Dirac’s matrices
(
shishkin2
)

\displaystyle\gamma^{0}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-\sigma^{1}\\
\sigma^{1}&0\end{pmatrix}\,,\quad\gamma^{1}=\begin{pmatrix}\sigma^{1}&0\\
0&-\sigma^{1}\end{pmatrix}\,,\quad\gamma^{2}=-\begin{pmatrix}\sigma^{2}&0\\
0&\sigma^{2}\end{pmatrix}\,,\quad\gamma^{3}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\sigma^{1}\\
\sigma^{1}&0\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle\,.

(V.71)

The equation (
V.70
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle(k_{x}+i\,k_{y})\Theta_{1}=k\,\sigma^{2}\,\Theta_{2}

(V.72)

\displaystyle(k_{x}-i\,k_{y})\sigma^{2}\,\Theta_{2}=k\Theta_{1}\,.

The solution for
\Theta_{2}
in terms of
\Theta_{1}
is

\displaystyle\Theta_{2}=\dfrac{k_{x}+\,ik_{y}}{k_{\perp}}\,\sigma^{2}\,\Theta_{1}\,,

(V.73)

where

and
\sigma^{2}
is the second Pauli’s matrix. Therefore the spinor
\Theta_{0}
has the following structure

\displaystyle\Theta_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}\Theta_{1}\\[5.69046pt]
\dfrac{k_{x}+i\,k_{y}}{k_{\perp}}\,\sigma^{2}\,\Theta_{1}\end{pmatrix}\,.

(V.74)

To determine
\Theta_{0}
you must solve the system of equations

\displaystyle\left(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}+i\,k_{z}\,t^{2q-1}\right)\Theta_{1}=\sigma^{1}\,(m-i\,k_{\perp}\,t^{-q})\,\Theta_{2}

(V.75)

\displaystyle\left(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}-i\,k_{z}\,t^{2q-1}\right)\Theta_{2}=-\sigma^{1}\,(m+i\,k_{\perp}\,t^{-q})\,\Theta_{1}\,.

By decoupling the system we have an equation for the components of
\Theta_{1}(t)

\displaystyle\dfrac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\xi_{1\,,2}}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}

VII
Final Remarks

In this article we discuss creation of scalar and spin 1/2 particles in some LRS Bianchi I models using the semi-classical method to identify and define the positive and negative frequency states of the fields at the asymptotic limits of the
t\to 0
,
t\to\infty
expansion. For scalar particles, the same results were obtained as those by Duru with the path integral method, while for massless spin
1/2
particles the result obtained is the Fermi-Dirac distribution. This calculation corroborates that the semi-classical method coincides with the more sophisticated of path integrals to define the vacuum state in general relativity, which makes it efficient and accessible in many cases of interest.

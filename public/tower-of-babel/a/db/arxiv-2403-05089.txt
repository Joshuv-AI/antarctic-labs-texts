1.
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a locally finite metric tree with the path metric
d
. The geometric boundary
\partial\mathcal{T}
of the tree
\mathcal{T}
is the collection of the equivalence classes of geodesic rays in
\mathcal{T}
up to bounded Hausdorff distance. Suppose that the degree of every vertex is larger than 2 and the geometric boundary
\partial\mathcal{T}
consists of infinitely many points. Suppose that a discrete group
\Gamma
acts isometrically and geometrically (i.e. properly and cocompactly) on
\mathcal{T}
.

To define Brownian motion on
\mathcal{T}
, we choose a Dirichlet form on graphs in
[
BSSW
]
(see Section
2.3
). The Dirichlet form allows us to define the Laplacian
\Delta
and the heat kernel
p(t,x,y)
on graphs. The heat kernel is the fundamental solution of the heat equation, i.e.

\Delta p(t,x,y)=\frac{d}{dt}p(t,x,y)\text{ and }\underset{t\rightarrow 0+}{\lim}p(t,x,y)=\delta_{x}(y),

where
\delta_{x}(y)
is the Dirac delta mass at
x
. The heat kernel
p(t,x,y)
is the probability density function of our Brownian motion.

Our main result is the local limit theorem of Brownian motion on
\mathcal{T}
which describes the asymptotic behavior of the heat kernel as
t\rightarrow\infty
under some assumptions related to the length spectrum of
\Gamma
. The
translation length

l_{\gamma}
of an element
\gamma\in\Gamma
is defined by

l_{\gamma}:=\inf\{d(x,\gamma x):x\in\mathcal{T}\}.

The
length spectrum

\Lambda_{\Gamma}
of
\Gamma
is the additive subgroup of
\mathbb{R}
generated by
\{l_{\gamma}:\gamma\in\Gamma\}
.

Definition 1.1
.

The length spectrum
\Lambda_{\Gamma}
is
Diophantine
if there exist two elements
\gamma
and
\gamma^{\prime}
such that the following property holds. There exist positive numbers
C
and
\beta
such that for all
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}
with
q>0
,

\left|\frac{l_{\gamma}}{l_{\gamma^{\prime}}}-\frac{p}{q}\right|>Cq^{-\beta}.

The following theorem is the main theorem of this article.

Theorem 1.2
.

(Local Limit Theorem)
Let
\mathcal{T}
be a topologically complete locally finite metric tree. Every degree of a vertex is at least 3. Suppose that the cardinality of
\partial T
is infinite and a discrete group
\Gamma
acts isometrically and geometrically on
\mathcal{T}
. Suppose that the length spectrum of
\Gamma
is a dense subgroup of
\mathbb{R}
and is Diophantine. There exists a function
C:\mathcal{T}\times\mathcal{T}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that for any
x,y\in\mathcal{T}
,

(1.1)

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}t^{3/2}e^{\lambda_{0}t}p(t,x,y)=C(x,y).

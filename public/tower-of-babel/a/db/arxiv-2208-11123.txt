An explicit version of Bombieri’s log-free density estimate and Sárközy’s theorem for shifted primes
Jesse Thorner, Asif Zaman

Abstract
. We make explicit Bombieri’s refinement of Gallagher’s log-free “large sieve density estimate near σ = 1 \sigma=1 ” for Dirichlet L L -functions. We use this estimate and recent work of Green to prove that if N ≥ 2 N\geq 2 is an integer, A ⊆ { 1 , … , N } A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N\} , and for all primes p p no two elements in A A differ by p − 1 p-1 , then | A | ≪ N 1 − 1 / 10 18 |A|\ll N^{1-1/10^{18}} . This strengthens a theorem of Sárközy.



1.
Introduction and statement of results

Let
N\geq 2
be an integer, and let
\delta(N)
be the relative density of a largest set
A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N\}
such that for all primes
p
, no two elements in
A
differ by
p-1
. Erdős conjectured that
\delta(N)\to 0
as
N\to\infty
, and Sárközy
[
32
]
proved the conjecture in the form
\delta(N)\ll(\log\log N)^{-2+o(1)}
. Several improvements followed (e.g., Lucier
[
20
]
and Ruzsa and Sanders
[
31
]
). Wang
[
35
]
proved that there exists an absolute and effectively computable constant
>0
such that
\delta(N)\ll\exp(-\Cr{Wang}(\log N)^{1/3})
. The proofs in
[
31
,
35
]
use the distribution of zeros of Dirichlet
L
-functions. Assuming the generalized Riemann hypothesis for Dirichlet
L
-functions (GRH), Ruzsa and Sanders
[
31
]
proved that
\delta(N)\ll\exp(-\Cr{Wang}(\log N)^{1/2})
and asked whether GRH implies the existence of a power-saving bound on
\delta(N)
.

In a recent tour de force, Green
[
10
]
unconditionally proved that such a power-saving bound exists—that is, there exists a constant
>0
such that
\delta(N)\ll N^{-\Cr{Green}}
. Assuming GRH, Green’s arguments simplify to show that any fixed
0<\Cr{Green}<\frac{1}{12}
is admissible
[
9
]
, but an unconditional admissible value of
\Cr{Green}
was not produced. In this paper, we produce such an admissible value.

Theorem 1.1
.

If
A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N\}
and no prime
p
satisfies
p-1\in A-A
, then
|A|\ll N^{1-1/10^{18}}
.

The new ingredient that we supply in order to deduce
Theorem

1.1
from the work in
[
10
]
is a certain log-free zero density estimate for Dirichlet
L
-functions. Such a result was first proved by Linnik as part of his proof that there exists an absolute and effectively computable constant
b>0
such the least prime in an arithmetic progression modulo
q
is at most
q^{b}

[
19
]
. For an integer
q\geq 1
, let
\chi\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\,q)
be a Dirichlet character, and let
L(s,\chi)
be its
L
-function. For
Q\geq 3
and
\sigma\geq 0
, define

(1.1)

\mathscr{L}(s,Q):=\prod_{1\leq q\leq Q}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\,q)\\
\textup{$\chi$ primitive}\end{subarray}}L(s,\chi),\qquad\beta_{1}(Q):=\max\{\beta\in\mathbb{R}\colon\mathscr{L}(\beta,Q)=0\}

and

(1.2)

\displaystyle N(\sigma,Q)

\displaystyle:=\#\{\rho=\beta+i\gamma\colon\beta>\sigma,~|\gamma|\leq Q,~\mathscr{L}(\rho,Q)=0\},

\displaystyle N^{*}(\sigma,Q)

\displaystyle:=\#\{\rho=\beta+i\gamma\neq\beta_{1}(Q)\colon\beta>\sigma,~|\gamma|\leq Q,~\mathscr{L}(\rho,Q)=0\}.

(All sums and counts over
\rho
weigh the contribution for each
\rho
with its multiplicity.) Gallagher
[
8
]
used Turán’s lower bound for power sums with a “pre-sifted” large sieve inequality (
Lemma

5.1
) to prove that there exist absolute and effectively computable constants
>0
and
>0
such that

(1.3)

N(\sigma,Q)\leq\Cr{Gallagher1}Q^{\Cr{Gallagher2}(1-\sigma)},\qquad\sigma\geq 0,\quad Q\geq 3.

Jutila
[
16
]
used Selberg’s theory of pseudocharacters to sharpen (
1.3
), proving that for all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists an effectively computable constant
c(\varepsilon)>0
such that

(1.4)

N(\sigma,Q)\leq c(\varepsilon)Q^{(6+\varepsilon)(1-\sigma)},\qquad\sigma\geq 4/5,\quad Q\geq 3.

The exponent in (
1.4
) is as strong as the generalized Lindelöf hypothesis implies. Another refinement comes from Bombieri
[
3
]
, who proved that there exist absolute and effectively computable constants
>0
and
>0
such that

(1.5)

N^{*}(\sigma,Q)\leq\Cr{Bombieri1}(1-\beta_{1}(Q))(\log Q)Q^{\Cr{Bombieri2}(1-\sigma)},\qquad\sigma\geq 0,\quad Q\geq 3.

If
\beta_{1}(Q)
and
\sigma
are suitably close to
1
as a function of
Q
, then (
1.5
) improves upon (
1.3
) and (
1.4
).

We prove explicit versions of (
1.3
) and (
1.5
) that hold for all
Q\geq 3
and all
\sigma\geq\frac{39}{40}
(see
Corollary

6.1
below for versions that permit
\sigma\geq 0
). These appear to be the first explicit log-free zero density estimates for Dirichlet
L
-functions that do not require
Q
to be “sufficiently large” or
\sigma
to be “sufficiently close” to
1
, where sufficiency is not explicitly quantified. Such estimates are useful in contexts beyond our proof of
Theorem

1.1
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
Q\geq 3
and
\sigma\geq\frac{39}{40}
. If
N(\sigma,Q)
and
N^{*}(\sigma,Q)
are as in (
1.2
), then

N(\sigma,Q)\leq 10^{88}(10^{421}Q^{99})^{1-\sigma},\qquad N^{*}(\sigma,Q)\leq 10^{93}\min\{1,(1-\beta_{1}(Q))\log Q\}(10^{466}Q^{170})^{1-\sigma}.

Remark
.

If
Q\geq 5
, then
\mathscr{L}(0,Q)=0
and
\mathscr{L}(\sigma,Q)\neq 0
for
\sigma\geq 1
. Thus, we have that
\beta_{1}(Q)\in[0,1)
.

The proof of
Theorem

1.1
is sensitive to the size of the implied constants, so we cannot use (
1.4
) without making

explicit first. We did not try to estimate

because this requires us to make certain intricate results of Huxley
[
15
]
on large values of Dirichlet polynomials completely explicit in all ranges. While we avoided this potentially onerous task for the sake of brevity, we encourage the interested reader to make (
1.4
) explicit for all
Q\geq 3
and all
\sigma\geq\frac{4}{5}
. Our proof of
Theorem

1.1
shows that further improvements to
Theorem

1.2
do not substantially improve the power savings in
Theorem

1.1
without a numerical optimization of the arguments in
[
10
]
or a substantial numerical improvement in the standard zero-free region for

.

2.
Auxiliary results on Dirichlet
L
-functions

Let
\chi\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\,q_{\chi})
be a primitive Dirichlet character, and let

L(s,\chi)=\prod_{p}(1-\chi(p)p^{-s})^{-1}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\chi(n)}{n^{s}},\qquad\textup{Re}(s)>1

be its
L
-function. We let
\delta_{\chi}
be the indicator function for the trivial character modulo 1, whose
L
-function is the Riemann zeta function
\zeta(s)
.

2.1.
The Hadamard factorization

All of the content in this subsection can be found in
[
6
]
. Let

\mathfrak{a}_{\chi}=\frac{1-\chi(-1)}{2},\qquad\tau(\chi)=\sum_{j=1}^{q_{\chi}}\chi(n)e^{2\pi ij/q_{\chi}}.

Since
\chi
is primitive, we have that
|\tau(\chi)|=\sqrt{q_{\chi}}
. The completed
L
-function

\Lambda(s,\chi)=(s(s-1))^{\delta_{\chi}}\Big(\frac{q_{\chi}}{\pi}\Big)^{\frac{s+\mathfrak{a}_{\chi}}{2}}\Gamma\Big(\frac{s+\mathfrak{a}_{\chi}}{2}\Big)L(s,\chi)

is entire of order one and satisfies the functional equation

(2.1)

\Lambda(s,\chi)=\frac{\tau(\chi)}{i^{\mathfrak{a}_{\chi}}\sqrt{q_{\chi}}}\Lambda(1-s,\overline{\chi}).

Since
\Lambda(s,\chi)
is entire of order one, it exhibits a Hadamard factorization

\Lambda(s,\chi)=e^{A_{\chi}+B_{\chi}s}\prod_{\Lambda(\rho,\chi)=0}\Big(1-\frac{s}{\rho}\Big)e^{\frac{s}{\rho}}.

The zeros
\rho=\beta+i\gamma
are the nontrivial zeros of
L(s,\chi)
, and they satisfy
0\leq\beta\leq 1
and
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
. The poles of
s^{\delta_{\chi}}\Gamma(\frac{s+\mathfrak{a}_{\chi}}{2})
are the trivial zeros; these form the sequence
(-2(j+\delta_{\chi})-\mathfrak{a}_{\chi})_{j=0}^{\infty}
. Upon taking logarithmic derivatives, we find that

(2.2)

\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,\chi)=B_{\chi}+\sum_{\rho}\Big(\frac{1}{s-\rho}+\frac{1}{\rho}\Big)-\delta_{\chi}\Big(\frac{1}{s-1}+\frac{1}{s}\Big)-\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{q_{\chi}}{\pi}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}}{\Gamma}\Big(\frac{s+\mathfrak{a}_{\chi}}{2}\Big).

Since

\textup{Re}(B_{\chi})=-\sum_{\rho}\textup{Re}\Big(\frac{1}{\rho}\Big),

it follows that

(2.3)

\textup{Re}\Big(\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,\chi)\Big)=\sum_{\rho}\textup{Re}\Big(\frac{1}{s-\rho}\Big)-\delta_{\chi}\textup{Re}\Big(\frac{1}{s-1}+\frac{1}{s}\Big)-\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{q_{\chi}}{\pi}-\frac{1}{2}\textup{Re}\Big(\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}}{\Gamma}\Big(\frac{s+\mathfrak{a}_{\chi}}{2}\Big)\Big).

2.3.
Counting zeros

We use the following estimate for the
\Gamma
-function.

Lemma 2.7
.

If
\textup{Re}(z)\geq\frac{1}{2}
, then

\textup{Re}\Big(\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}}{\Gamma}\Big(\frac{z}{2}\Big)\Big)\leq\log|z|-\gamma_{\mathbb{Q}},\qquad\gamma_{\mathbb{Q}}:=\lim_{N\to\infty}\Big(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{n}-\log N\Big)=0.5772156649\ldots

Proof.

In
[
14
, Lemma 4.1]
, it is proved that if
\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(z)=\pi^{-z/2}\Gamma(\frac{z}{2})
and
\textup{Re}(z)\geq\frac{1}{2}

\textup{Re}\Big(\frac{\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}^{\prime}}{\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}}(z)\Big)\leq-\frac{\log\pi+\gamma_{\mathbb{Q}}}{2}+\frac{\log|z|}{2}.

Since
\frac{\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}^{\prime}}{\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}}(z)=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}}{\Gamma}(\frac{z}{2})-\frac{\log\pi}{2}
, the lemma follows.

Our proof of

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
\sigma\geq 1
,
0<r\leq 1
,
q\geq 1
,
T\geq 1
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
|t|\leq T
, and
\chi\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\,q)
be a primitive Dirichlet character. Define

(2.6)

n_{\chi}(r,\sigma+it):=\#\{\rho=\beta+i\gamma\colon 0<\beta<1,~L(\rho,\chi)=0,~|\sigma+it-\rho|\leq r\}.

The bound
n_{\chi}(r,\sigma+it)\leq n_{\chi}(r,1+it)\leq n_{\chi}(2r,1+r+it)\leq r(2\log(qT)-1)+4
holds.

Proof.

If
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
\sigma>1
, and
0<r\leq 1
, then it follows from the geometry of complex numbers that

\displaystyle n_{\chi}(r,\sigma+it)\leq n_{\chi}(r,1+it)\leq n_{\chi}(2r,1+r+it)

\displaystyle\leq 4r\sum_{|1+r+it-\rho|\leq 2r}\textup{Re}\Big(\frac{1}{1+r+it-\rho}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq 4r\sum_{\rho}\textup{Re}\Big(\frac{1}{1+r+it-\rho}\Big).

By (
2.3
), the last line equals

4r\Big(\textup{Re}\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(1+r+it,\chi)+\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{q}{\pi}+\frac{1}{2}\textup{Re}\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}}{\Gamma}\Big(\frac{1+r+\mathfrak{a}_{\chi}+it}{2}\Big)+\delta_{\chi}\textup{Re}\Big(\frac{1}{1+r+it}+\frac{1}{r+it}\Big)\Big).

Note that

\textup{Re}\Big(\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(1+r+it,\chi)\Big)\leq-\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(1+r)\leq\frac{1}{r},

and
Lemma

2.7
implies that (since
|t|\leq T
and
T\geq 1
)

\frac{1}{2}\textup{Re}\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}}{\Gamma}\Big(\frac{1+r+\mathfrak{a}_{\chi}+it}{2}\Big)\leq\frac{\frac{1}{2}\log((1+r+\mathfrak{a}_{\chi})^{2}+t^{2})-\gamma_{\mathbb{Q}}}{2}\leq\frac{\log T+\frac{1}{2}\log 10-\gamma_{\mathbb{Q}}}{2}.

Finally, if
\chi
is trivial, then
L(s,\chi)=\zeta(s)
does not vanish when
0\leq\textup{Re}(s)\leq 1
and
|\textup{Im}(s)|\leq 14.13
. Therefore, it suffices to assume that
|t|\geq 13.1
, in which case we have that

\delta_{\chi}\textup{Re}\Big(\frac{1}{1+r+it}+\frac{1}{r+it}\Big)\leq 0.017\,182\,3\delta_{\chi}.

The lemma follows once we collect the estimates above.
∎

Since the growth of

v
, the above integral is at most

\lim_{\xi\to 0^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\log|L(1+\xi+i(t+v),\chi)|\frac{dv}{\cosh(\pi v)}+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\log|1+i(t+v)|\frac{dv}{\cosh(\pi v)}+\frac{1}{4}\log\frac{q_{\chi}}{2\pi}.

By dominated convergence again, the limit equals

\lim_{\xi\to 0^{+}}\textup{Re}\Big(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\Lambda(n)\chi(n)}{n^{1+\xi+it}\log n}\int_{\mathbb{R}}n^{-iv}\frac{dv}{\cosh(\pi v)}\Big)\\
=2\textup{Re}\Big(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\Lambda(n)\chi(n)}{(n^{\frac{3}{2}+it}+n^{\frac{1}{2}+it})\log n}\Big)\leq 2\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\log n}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{j}}{n^{\frac{3}{2}+j}}=2\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{j}\log\zeta(j+\tfrac{3}{2})\leq 1.4988.

If
t\in\mathbb{R}
, then

\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\log|1+i(t+v)|\frac{dv}{\cosh(\pi v)}=\frac{1}{4}\log|1+it|+\frac{1}{8}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\log\Big|1+\frac{v^{2}+2|t|v}{t^{2}+1}\Big|\frac{dv}{\cosh(\pi v)}.

If
w\geq-1
, we see via Taylor expansions that

\lim_{w_{0}\to w^{+}}\log|1+w_{0}|\leq w-\frac{w^{2}}{2}+\frac{w^{3}}{3}.

For all
t,v\in\mathbb{R}
, we have that
v^{2}+2|t|v=(v+|t|)^{2}-t^{2}\geq-t^{2}>-(t^{2}+1)
, so

\frac{1}{8}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\log\Big|1+\frac{v^{2}+2|t|v}{t^{2}+1}\Big|\frac{dv}{\cosh(\pi v)}\leq\frac{1}{8}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sum_{j=1}^{3}\frac{(-1)^{j+1}}{j}\Big(\frac{v^{2}+2|t|v}{t^{2}+1}\Big)^{j}\frac{dv}{\cosh(\pi v)}=\frac{79+210t^{2}-48t^{4}}{1536(t^{2}+1)^{3}}.

Since

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\exp\Big(\frac{1}{4}\log\frac{1}{2\pi}+1.4988+\frac{79+210t^{2}-48t^{4}}{1536(t^{2}+1)^{3}}\Big)\leq 2.97655,

the result follows.

If
\chi=1
, then
L(s,\chi)=\zeta(s)
. In this case, the identity
\zeta(\overline{s})=\overline{\zeta(s)}
and
[
13
, Theorem 1.1]
yield

|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)|\leq\begin{cases}1.461&\mbox{if $0\leq|t|\leq 3$,}\\
0.63|t|^{1/6}\log|t|&\mbox{if $|t|\geq 3$.}\end{cases}

This finishes our proof in all cases.
∎

2.4.
The Deuring–Heilbronn phenomenon

Here, we quantify the idea that if

is close to

in

for low-lying zeros. Our proof relies on the following lower bound for power sums.

Lemma 2.13

(Theorem 2.2 of
[
17
]
)
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. Let
(z_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
(resp.
(b_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
) be a sequence of complex (resp. nonnegative real) numbers with
z_{1}\neq 0
(resp.

1\leq j\leq(8+\varepsilon)\frac{1}{b_{1}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{b_{n}|z_{n}|}{|z_{1}|+|z_{n}|}

such that

\textup{Re}\Big(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}z_{n}^{j}\Big)\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{32+4\varepsilon}b_{1}|z_{1}|^{j}.

Corollary 2.14
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. Let
(z_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of complex numbers with
z_{1}\neq 0
. Define
\mathcal{M}:=\frac{1}{|z_{1}|}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|z_{n}|
. There exists an integer

such that

\textup{Re}\Big(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}z_{n}^{j}\Big)\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{32+4\varepsilon}|z_{1}|^{j}.

Proof.

Since
\frac{|z_{n}|}{|z_{1}|+|z_{n}|}\leq\frac{|z_{n}|}{|z_{1}|}
when

, this follows from
Lemma

2.13
with
b_{n}=1
for all

.
∎

We use

to prove what appears to be the first completely explicit version of the Deuring–Heilbronn zero repulsion phenomenon.

Theorem 2.15
.

Let

\beta\leq 1-\frac{\log(\frac{1}{(1-\beta_{1}(Q))(670\,564.676+347\,029.502\log Q+107\,906.278\log T)})}{104.645+54.156\log Q+16.84\log T}.

Remark
.

Our proof produces a stronger result than we have stated. For numerical convenience, we assign specific values to the parameters
\varepsilon
and
\alpha
occurring in our proof below.

Remark
.

Jutila
[
16

proved a numerically stronger result under the assumption that
QT
is sufficiently large (in terms of a parameter
\varepsilon
occurring in Jutila’s proof but not ours). To our knowledge,

2.15
is the only explicit result of its type with no such hypotheses.

Proof.

The result is trivial when

\beta_{1}(Q)\leq 1-\frac{1}{670\,564.676+347\,029.502\log Q+107\,906.278\log T},

so we assume otherwise. Let
1\leq q\leq Q
be an integer, and let
\chi\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\,q)
be a primitive Dirichlet character. Let
\beta+i\gamma\neq\beta_{1}(Q)
be a nontrivial zero of
L(s,\chi)
with
\beta>\frac{1}{2}
and
|\gamma|\leq T
, and let
\psi\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\,q_{\psi})
be the primitive character that induces
\chi_{1}\chi
. Note that
q_{\psi}
divides the least common multiple
[q,q_{1}]
. Let
\omega
denote a zero of the function

D(s):=\zeta(s)L(s,\chi_{1})L(s+i\gamma,\chi)L(s+i\gamma,\chi_{1}\chi)=\zeta(s)L(s,\chi_{1})L(s+i\gamma,\chi)L(s+i\gamma,\psi)\prod_{p|[q,q_{1}]}\Big(1-\frac{\psi(p)}{p^{s+i\gamma}}\Big).

There exists numbers
a_{D},b_{D}\in\mathbb{C}
such that

(s-1)(s-1+i\gamma)^{\delta_{\chi}+\delta_{\psi}}D(s)=s^{\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}_{s=0}D(s)}e^{a_{D}+b_{D}s}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\omega\neq 0\\
D(\omega)=0\end{subarray}}\Big(1-\frac{s}{w}\Big)e^{\frac{s}{\omega}}.

Let
\Lambda(n)
be the von Mangoldt function, and define
a(n):=(1+\chi_{1}(n))(1+\chi(n)n^{-i\gamma})\Lambda(n)
. By construction, we have the uniform lower bound
\textup{Re}(a(n))\geq 0
. Also note that if
\textup{Re}(s)>1
, then

-\frac{D^{\prime}}{D}(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a(n)\Lambda(n)}{n^{s}}.

Let
\alpha\geq 1
. For an integer
j\geq 1
, the
(2j-1)
-th derivative at
s=\alpha+1
is

\frac{1}{(2j-1)!}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a(n)\Lambda(n)(\log n)^{2j-1}}{n^{\alpha+1}}=\frac{1}{\alpha^{2j}}+\frac{\delta_{\chi}+\mathbf{1}_{\psi=1}}{(\alpha+i\gamma)^{2j}}-\sum_{\omega}\frac{1}{(\alpha+1-\omega)^{2j}}.

We extract the contribution from
\beta_{1}(Q)
, arriving at

\frac{1}{(2j-1)!}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a(n)\Lambda(n)(\log n)^{2j-1}}{n^{1+\alpha}}\\
=\frac{1}{\alpha^{2j}}+\frac{\delta_{\chi}+\delta_{\psi}}{(\alpha+i\gamma)^{2j}}-\frac{1}{(\alpha+1-\beta_{1}(Q))^{2j}}-\frac{\delta_{\chi}+\delta_{\psi}}{(\alpha+1-\beta_{1}(Q)+i\gamma)^{2j}}-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}z_{n}^{j},

where
z_{n}=z_{n}(\gamma)
runs through the (multi)sets

\displaystyle\{(1+\alpha-\omega)^{-2}\colon\zeta(\omega)=0\},

\displaystyle\{(1+\alpha-\omega)^{-2}\colon\omega\neq\beta_{1}(Q)\textup{ and }L(\omega,\chi_{1})=0\},

\displaystyle\{(1+\alpha+i\gamma-\omega)^{-2}\colon\omega\neq\beta_{1}(Q)\textup{ and }L(\omega,\chi)=0\},

\displaystyle\{(1+\alpha+i\gamma-\omega)^{-2}\colon\omega\neq\beta_{1}(Q)\textup{ and }L(\omega,\psi)=0\},

\displaystyle\{(1+\alpha+i\gamma+(h+2k)\pi i/\log p)^{-2}\colon h\in\{0,1\},~k\in\mathbb{Z},~p\nmid q_{\psi}\},

arranged so that
z_{1}=(\alpha+1+i\gamma-(\beta+i\gamma))^{-2}=(\alpha+1-\beta)^{-2}
. Since
\textup{Re}(a(n))\geq 0
, it follows that

\textup{Re}\Big(\frac{1}{(2j-1)!}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a(n)\Lambda(n)(\log n)^{2j-1}}{n^{1+\alpha}}\Big)\geq 0.

Since
\delta_{\chi}+\mathbf{1}_{\psi=1}\in\{0,1\}
, it follows that

\displaystyle\textup{Re}\Big(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}z_{n}^{j}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\alpha^{2j}}-\frac{1}{(\alpha+1-\beta_{1}(Q))^{2j}}+(\delta_{\chi}+\mathbf{1}_{\psi=1})\textup{Re}\Big(\frac{1}{(\alpha+i\gamma)^{2j}}-\frac{1}{(\alpha+1-\beta_{1}(Q)+i\gamma)^{2j}}\Big)

\Big|1-\frac{1}{(1+\frac{1-\beta_{1}(Q)}{\alpha+i\gamma})^{2j}}\Big|=\frac{2j}{|\alpha+i\gamma|}\Big|\int_{\beta_{1}(Q)}^{1}\frac{1}{(1+\frac{1-u}{\alpha+i\gamma})^{2j+1}}du\Big|\leq\frac{2j(1-\beta_{1}(Q))}{|\alpha+i\gamma|}\leq\frac{2j(1-\beta_{1}(Q))}{\alpha},

regardless of whether or not
\gamma=0
, we find that

(2.7)

\textup{Re}\Big(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}z_{n}^{j}\Big)\leq\frac{4j(1-\beta_{1}(Q))}{\alpha^{2j+1}}.

Corollary

2.14
to bound the left hand side of (

) from below. Since
\beta>\frac{1}{2}
, we have that
|z_{1}|\geq(\alpha+\frac{1}{2})^{-2}
, so
\mathcal{M}:=|z_{1}|^{-1}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|z_{n}|
is at most

\Big(\alpha+\frac{1}{2}\Big)^{2}\Big(\sum_{\zeta(\omega)=0}\frac{1}{|\alpha+1-\omega|^{2}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\omega\neq\beta_{1}(Q)\\

p\nmid q_{\psi}\end{subarray}}\sum_{h=0}^{1}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1}{|\alpha+1+i\gamma+\frac{(h+2k)\pi i}{\log p}|^{2}}\Big).

[

and its proof (with

) to bound the contribution not arising from the nontrivial zeros; this contribution is at most

[

, Equation (7–2)]
(with

, which ensures that
\psi
is quadratic) to bound the contribution arising from the nontrivial zeros. In their proof (with

so that

and

. These simplifying estimates are straightforward to reverse. We conclude (using the bound
\delta_{\chi}+\mathbf{1}_{\chi_{1}\chi=1}\leq 1

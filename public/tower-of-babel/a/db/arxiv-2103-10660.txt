The reference distributions of Maurer’s universal statistical test and its improved tests
Yasunari Hikima, Atsushi Iwasaki, Ken Umeno

Abstract
Maurer’s universal statistical test can widely detect non-randomness of given sequences. Coron proposed an improved test, and further Yamamoto and Liu proposed a new test based on Coron’s test. These tests use normal distributions as their reference distributions, but the soundness has not been theoretically discussed so far. Additionally, Yamamoto and Liu’s test uses an experimental value as the variance of its reference distribution. In this paper, we theoretically derive the variance of the reference distribution of Yamamoto and Liu’s test and prove that the true reference distribution of Coron’s test converges to a normal distribution in some sense. We can apply the proof to the other tests with small changes.



1
Introduction

Random number sequences are used in many fields such as the Monte Carlo method and information security, including cryptography.
In particular, for cryptography, high randomness is required.
Therefore, we have to evaluate the randomness of a given sequence or property of its generator.
Randomness test is one of such evaluating method.
It is a hypothesis test, and the null is that the given sequence is truly random.
It is just an experimental method and cannot give proof of randomness.
On the other hand, the randomness test has a merit that we can adapt the test for any sequences regardless of their generators.
For instance, Tamura and Shikano used randomness tests to inspect the properties of a quantum computer developed by IBM
[
1
]
.

Since we can consider numberless alternative hypotheses against the null, many randomness tests have been proposed.
We do not use a single randomness test for evaluation in practice, but a set of randomness tests called a test suite.
There are many test suites such as TestU01, Diehard and Dieharder
[
2
,
3
,
4
]
, and NIST SP800-22
[
5
]
is one of the most widely used test suites.
SP800-22 consists of 15 types of randomness tests.
Some of the tests were pointed out that are problematic, and improvements have been proposed
[
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
]
.

This paper focuses on Maurer’s universal statistical test
[
12
]
, which is included in SP800-22.
Almost all proposed randomness tests aim to detect non-randomness which the developers explicitly specify, but universal tests that aim to detect a wide range of non-randomness are exceptions.
Maurer’s universal statistical test was also developed to have such property.
Coron proposed an improved test based on Maurer’s test
[
14
]
, but the improved test has not been adopted for SP800-22 so far.
Further, Yamamoto and Liu added an optional improvement to Coron’s test and state that their test can detect non-randomness more sensitively
[
15
]
.
These three tests use normal distributions as their reference distributions.
As a common problem of the three tests, it has not been proven that their true reference distributions are normal distributions at least approximately.
In other words, these tests use normal distributions as their reference distributions without theoretical evidence, although there is no report that using normal distribution is improper as far as the authors know.
This is one of the problems we address in this paper.
Additionally, the expected values and variances of the normal distributions are needed to perform these tests.
Deriving the theoretical values of the variances is harder than deriving the expected values.
As the variance of the Maurer’s test, an experimental value was at first used, and later, Coron and Naccache derived the theoretical value
[
13
]
.
The method in Ref.
[
13
]
can be applied to Coron’s test and derived the theoretical value of the variance of Coron’s test.
However, the theoretical value of the variance of Yamamoto and Liu’s test has not been derived, and an experimental value is used.
The lack of the theoretical value is the other problem we address in this paper.

In section 2, we introduce Maurer’s test, Coron’s test, and Yamamoto and Liu’s test.
In section 3, we theoretically derive the variance of the reference distribution of Yamamoto and Liu’s test.
Section 3 is a reconstruction of Ref.

In section 4, we prove that the distribution of the test statistic of Coron’s test converges to a normal distribution in some sense.

Finally, we offer a conclusion.

2
Maurer’s test, Coron’s test, and Yamamoto and Liu’s test

This section briefly introduces Maurer’s test, Coron’s test, and Yamamoto and Liu’s test.

2.1
Maurer’s universal statistical test

Let us consider a case that we test a given
N
-bit sequence
X
.
First, we divide
X
into
L
-bit blocks.
We use the first
Q
blocks for initialization, and the remained
K
blocks for testing.
Here,
Q
and
K
are required to be sufficiently large comparing with
2^{L}
.
In practical,
Q\geq 10\cdot 2^{L}
and
K\geq 1000\cdot 2^{L}
are suggested.
For simplicity, we assume that
N=L\times(Q+K)
.
Let
b_{n}(X)
be the
n
-th block and define
A_{n}(X)
as

\displaystyle A_{n}(X)=\begin{cases}n\ &(\text{$b_{n-m}(X)\neq b_{n}(X)$ for $m=1,2,\dots,n-1$})\\
\min\{m\in\mathbb{N}\mid b_{n-m}(X)=b_{n}(X)\}&(\text{otherwise})\end{cases}.

(1)

In other words,
A_{n}(X)
implies the distance between the
n
-th block and the nearest former block which has the same value of the
n
-th block if exists.
Using
A_{n}(X)
, the test statistic is described as

\displaystyle f_{M}(X):=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{n=Q+1}^{Q+K}\log_{2}A_{n}(X).

(2)

Finally, using a normal distribution as the reference distribution (i.e., assuming that
f_{M}\left(R^{(0.5)}\right)
follows a normal distribution where
R^{(p)}
is a random variable sequence and each element independently takes one with probability
p
and zero with probability
1-p
), we compute the corresponding p-value.
In practical use of Maurer’s test, we need the expected value and variance of the reference distribution.
For sufficiently large
Q
, we can regard that the distributions of
A_{n}\left(R^{(0.5)}\right)

(n\geq Q+1)
do not depend on
n
and their support is
\mathbb{N}
.
Then, the expected value is given as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[f_{M}\left(R^{(0.5)}\right)\right]=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{K}\sum_{n=Q+1}^{Q+K}\mathbb{E}\left[\log A_{n}\left(R^{(0.5)}\right)\right]

(3)

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle 2^{-L}\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}(1-2^{-L})^{l-1}\log l.

(4)

As an experimentally derived approximation of the variance, Maurer proposed that

\displaystyle\text{Var}\left[f_{M}\left(R^{(0.5)}\right)\right]\simeq\left\{0.7-\frac{0.8}{L}+\left(1.6+\frac{12.8}{L}\right)K^{-\frac{4}{L}}\right\}\frac{\text{Var}\left[\log A_{n}\left(R^{(0.5)}\right)\right]}{K},

(5)

where by the same reason of deriving (
4
),

\displaystyle\text{Var}\left[\log A_{n}\left(R^{(0.5)}\right)\right]\simeq 2^{-L}\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}(1-2^{-L})^{l-1}\left(\log l\right)^{2}-\left\{2^{-L}\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}(1-2^{-L})^{l-1}\log l\right\}^{2}.

(6)

Later, Coron and Naccache derived the theoretical formula of the variance of the reference distribution for
Q\to\infty

[
13
]
.

The test statistic of Maurer’s test relates to entropy of the tested sequence and its universality is stated based on the fact.
Maurer proved that

\displaystyle\lim_{L\to\infty}\left\{\mathbb{E}\left[f_{M}\left(R^{(p)}\right)\right]-LH(p)\right\}=C

(7)

where
C
is a constant and
H(p):=-p\ln p-(1-p)\ln(1-p)
is per bit entropy of
R^{(p)}
.
It was also proven that

\displaystyle\lim_{L\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[f_{M}\left(U_{\mathcal{S}}\right)\right]}{L}=H_{\mathcal{S}},

(8)

where
U_{\mathcal{S}}
is a binary sequence generated by an ergodic stationary source
\mathcal{S}
which has finite memory and
H_{\mathcal{S}}
is per bit entropy
\mathcal{S}
generates.
Maurer gave a conjecture that

\displaystyle\lim_{L\to\infty}\left\{\mathbb{E}\left[f_{M}\left(U_{\mathcal{S}}\right)\right]-LH_{\mathcal{S}}\right\}=C,

(9)

but it was shown that the conjecture is false
[
13
]
.

2.2
Coron’s universal statistical test

Coron proposed a test which replaces the test statistic
f_{M}
by

\displaystyle f_{C}(X):=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{n=Q+1}^{Q+K}g\left(A_{n}(X)\right),

(10)

where

\displaystyle g(i)=\frac{1}{\ln 2}\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\frac{1}{j}.

(11)

This test uses a normal distribution as its reference distribution, too.
For sufficiently large
Q
, the expected value of
f_{C}\left(R^{(p)}\right)
is approximated as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[f_{C}\left(R^{(p)}\right)\right]=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{K}\sum_{n=Q+1}^{Q+K}\mathbb{E}\left[g\left(A_{n}\left(R^{(p)}\right)\right)\right]

(12)

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle LH(p)

(13)

for any
p\in(0,1)
.
Then, the expected value of the reference distribution is given as
LH(0.5)
.
For
p=\frac{1}{2}
, by the same way in Ref.
[
13
]
, we can derive the theoretical formula of
\text{Var}\left[f_{C}\left(R^{(0.5)}\right)\right]
as
Q\to\infty
.
Based on the theoretical formula, Coron proposed an approximation

\displaystyle\text{Var}\left[f_{C}\left(R^{(0.5)}\right)\right]\simeq\left\{d(L)+\frac{e(L)\times 2^{L}}{K}\right\}\frac{\text{Var}\left[g\left(A_{n}\left(R^{(0.5)}\right)\right)\right]}{K}

(14)

for sufficient large
Q
, and
d(L)
and
e(L)
are given in Ref.
[
14
]
.

As seen in (
13
), the test statistic of Coron’s test relates to entropy of the tested sequence, too.
Unlike the case of Maurer’s test, (
13
) holds for finite
L
and this is an advantage of Coron’s test.

2.3
Yamamoto and Liu’s test

Yamamoto and Liu proposed to flip a part of bits in a given sequence before performing Coron’s test
[
15
]
.
The flipping is stochastic, and each bit
x_{k}
is independently converted to
\hat{x}_{k}
following the rule as

\displaystyle\text{Pr}\left[\hat{x}_{k}=0|x_{k}=0\right]=

\displaystyle 1,

(15)

\displaystyle\text{Pr}\left[\hat{x}_{k}=1|x_{k}=1\right]=

\displaystyle\alpha.

(16)

They stated that the flipping made Coron’s test more sensitive, and showed some experimental results to detect non-randomness sensitively.
It is obvious that the exact reference distribution is the distribution of
f_{C}\left(R^{(p)}\right)
for
p=\frac{\alpha}{2}
.
Approximately, this test also uses a normal distribution as its reference distribution, but the expected value and variance are not equal to those of Coron’s test.
As we mentioned in the former subsection, (
13
) holds for any
p\in(0,1)
and we can use (
13
) with
p=\frac{\alpha}{2}
as the expected value for sufficiently large
Q
.
On the other hand, an experimentally derived value is used as the variance.
Yamamoto and Liu pointed out that
\left\{A_{n}\left(R^{(p)}\right)\right\}
are not i.i.d, and thus to derive the theoretical variance is difficult.
However, the derivation process for the variances of the reference distributions of Maurer’s test and Coron’s test do not need independent but just identity.
Even in Yamamoto and Liu’s test, identity is preserved.
Thus, we expect that we can theoretically derive the variance of the reference distribution of Yamamoto and Liu’s test by a similar way of the derivation process.

3
Derivation of the variance of the reference distribution of Yamamoto and Liu’s test

In this section, we derive the theoretical formula of the variance of the reference distribution that Yamamoto and Liu’s test uses and numerically evaluate the formula.
To get the formula, we need the distributions of
A_{n}\left(R^{(p)}\right)
and the joint distributions of
\left(A_{n}\left(R^{(p)}\right),A_{n+k}\left(R^{(p)}\right)\right)
.
For simplicity, we omit to write argument
R^{(p)}
for every variable.
In the following, we replace the index of
A_{n}
as illustrated in Figure
1
and consider the case that
Q\to\infty
.
Then, the sequence of
\{A_{n}\}_{n=1}^{K}
exactly follows a stationary ergodic process, that is, the joint distribution of
\{A_{n}\}_{n=k}^{k+m}
depends only on
m
, and its support fully covers
\mathbb{N}^{m}
.

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}=\left<b_{n-i}=b_{n},b_{n-i+1}\neq b_{n},\dots,b_{n-1}\neq b_{n}\right>.

Let

be the number of “

\displaystyle=w_{r}\times(1-w_{r})^{i-1},

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}[\ell(b_{n})=r]

\displaystyle=\binom{L}{r}w_{r},

(20)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{L}\binom{L}{r}w_{r}^{2}(1-w_{r})^{i-1}.

(21)

The joint distribution of
A_{n}
and

\displaystyle e_{2}(b)\land e_{2}(b^{\prime})=\emptyset.

Then, since

\displaystyle=\sum_{b\in B^{L}}\mathrm{Pr}[e_{2}(b)]

\displaystyle=\sum_{b\in B_{0}^{L}\cup\dots\cup B_{L}^{L}}\mathrm{Pr}[e_{2}(b)]

3.3
Derivation of the variance

Finally, we derive
\mathrm{Var}[f_{C}]
using
\mathrm{Pr}[A_{n}=i]
and
\mathrm{Pr}[A_{n}=i,\,A_{n+k}=j]
.
Since
\{A_{n}\}_{n=1}^{K}
is ergodic stationary under the assumption that
Q\to\infty
,
\mathrm{Var}[g(A_{n})]
does not depend on
n
and
\mathrm{Cov}[g(A_{i}),g(A_{j})]
depends only on
j-i
.
Then, we get

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}[f_{C}]=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}\left[\frac{1}{K}\sum_{n=1}^{K}g(A_{n})\right]

(45)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{K^{2}}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{K}\mathrm{Var}[g(A_{n})]+2\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq K}\mathrm{Cov}[g(A_{i}),g(A_{j})]\right)

(46)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{K}\mathrm{Var}[g(A_{n})]+\frac{2}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K-1}\left(1-\frac{k}{K}\right)\mathrm{Cov}[g(A_{n}),g(A_{n+k})].

(47)

Here, by (
13
), we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}[g(A_{n})]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\{g(i)\}^{2}\mathrm{Pr}[A_{n}=i]-\left\{LH(p)\right\}^{2},

(48)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cov}[g(A_{n}),g(A_{n+k})]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}g(i)g(j)\mathrm{Pr}[A_{n}=i,\,A_{n+k}=j]-\left\{LH(p)\right\}^{2}.

(49)

Substituting (

4
Form of the reference distribution

In this section, we prove that the distribution of the test statistic of Coron’s test converges to a normal distribution.
More exactly, the test statistic converges to zero as
K\to\infty
and we prove that the distribution of
\sqrt{K}\times f_{C}\left(R^{(0.5)}\right)
converges to a normal distribution in some sense.
This proof can also be applied for Maurer’s test and Yamamoto and Liu’s test, with minor changes.
In the following, we omit to write argument
R^{(0.5)}
for every variables.
Note that the omitted argument is different from that in the previous section.

4.1
Stationary case

First, we consider the case that
Q\to\infty
, i.e., the random variable sequence
\{A_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies ergodic stationary condition.
We use the following lemmas repeatedly:

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
M
be a real number satisfying
0<M<1
.
Then, there exist real numbers
C>0
and
0<\delta<1
such that

\displaystyle\forall p\in\mathbb{N},\ \forall a\in\mathbb{N},\ \forall b\in\mathbb{N},\ 0\leq\sum_{j=a}^{b}\left\{g(j)\right\}^{p}M^{j}<C\delta^{a-1}\left(1-\delta^{b}\right).

(52)

Proof.

Since
g(i)=\mathcal{O}(\log i)
, the lemma is obvious.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

There exist positive real numbers
C
and
\delta<1
such that

\displaystyle\left|\text{Pr}\left[A_{n}=i,\ A_{n+k}=j\right]-\text{Pr}\left[A_{n}=i\right]\text{Pr}\left[A_{n+k}=j\right]\right|<C\delta^{i+j}.

(53)

Proof.

In Ref.
[
13
]
, it is shown that

\displaystyle\text{Pr}[A_{n}=i,A_{n+k}=j]

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}2^{-2L}(1-2^{-L})^{i+j-2}&(1\leq j\leq k-1)\\
2^{-2L}(1-2^{-L})^{i+j-2}&(j=k)\\
2^{-2L}(1-2^{-L})^{i-j+2k-1}\left(1-2^{-L+1}\right)^{j-k-1}&(k+1\leq j\leq k+i-1)\\
0&(j=k+i)\\
2^{-2L}(1-2^{-L})^{-i+j-1}\left(1-2^{-L+1}\right)^{i-1}&(j\geq k+i+1)\end{cases},

(54)

\displaystyle\text{Pr}\left[A_{n}=i\right]

\displaystyle=\text{Pr}\left[A_{n+k}=i\right]=2^{-L}(1-2^{-L})^{i-1}.

(55)

(We can get the same results by substituting
p=0.5
into the results of the former section.)
Then, the lemma holds.
∎

Let
X_{n}
be
g(A_{n})
.

Lemma 4.3
.

The average
\mathbb{E}\left[X_{n}\right]
exists and does not depend on
n
.

Proof.

Since
\left\{X_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is ergodic stationary, it is obvious that
\mathbb{E}\left[X_{n}\right]
does not depend on
n
if it exists.
The existence is directly proven by lemma
4.1
and (
55
).
∎

We use the notation
\mu_{X}
as
\mathbb{E}\left[X_{n}\right]
.

Lemma 4.4
.

The variance
\text{Var}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{K}}\sum_{n=1}^{K}(X_{n}-\mu_{X})\right]
exists for all
K\in\mathbb{N}
and the limit
\lim_{K\to\infty}\text{Var}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{K}}\sum_{n=1}^{K}(X_{n}-\mu_{X})\right]
also exists.

Proof.

By lemmas
4.1
and
4.2
, there exist positive real numbers
C_{1}
,
C_{2}
and
\delta<1
such that

\displaystyle\text{Var}\left[X_{n}-\mu_{X}\right]<

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left\{g(j)\right\}^{2}2^{-L}(1-2^{-L})^{j-1}

(56)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle C_{1},

(57)

\displaystyle\left|\text{Cov}\left[X_{1}-\mu_{X},X_{n}-\mu_{X}\right]\right|=

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}g(i)g(j)\left(\text{Pr}\left[A_{1}=i,\ A_{n}=j\right]-\text{Pr}\left[A_{1}=i\right]\text{Pr}\left[A_{n}=j\right]\right)\right|

(58)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}g(i)g(j)\left|\text{Pr}\left[A_{1}=i,\ A_{n}=j\right]-\text{Pr}\left[A_{1}=i\right]\text{Pr}\left[A_{n}=j\right]\right|

(59)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=n}^{\infty}g(i)g(j)\left|\text{Pr}\left[A_{1}=i,\ A_{n}=j\right]-\text{Pr}\left[A_{1}=i\right]\text{Pr}\left[A_{n}=j\right]\right|

(60)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle C_{2}\delta^{n}.

(61)

Since
\{X_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is ergodic stationary,
\text{Var}\left[X_{n}-\mu_{X}\right]
does not depend on
n
and

\text{Cov}\left[X_{n}-\mu_{X},X_{n+k}-\mu_{X}\right]
depends only on
k
.
Then, we obtain

\displaystyle\text{Var}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{K}}\sum_{n=1}^{K}(X_{n}-\mu_{X})\right]=

\displaystyle\text{Var}\left[X_{1}-\mu_{X}\right]+2\sum_{n=2}^{K}\left(1-\frac{n-1}{K}\right)\text{Cov}\left[X_{1}-\mu_{X},X_{n}-\mu_{X}\right].

(62)

We have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{K}\left|\left(1-\frac{n-1}{K}\right)\text{Cov}\left[X_{1}-\mu_{X},X_{n}-\mu_{X}\right]\right|\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{K}\left|\text{Cov}\left[X_{1}-\mu_{X},X_{n}-\mu_{X}\right]\right|

(63)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\frac{C_{2}\delta^{2}}{1-\delta}\left(1-\delta^{K-1}\right)\ \underset{K\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\ \frac{C_{2}\delta^{2}}{1-\delta}.

(64)

From the above, lemma
4.4
holds.
∎

We use the notation
\tau_{X}
as
\lim_{K\to\infty}\text{Var}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{K}}\sum_{n=1}^{K}(X_{n}-\mu_{X})\right]
.
For arbitrary
J\in\mathbb{R}
, we describe
Y_{n}^{(J)}
,
Z_{n}^{(J)}
and
M_{J}
as

\displaystyle Y_{n}^{(J)}:=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&X_{n}\ \ \ \ (X_{n}\leq J)\\
&\ 0\ \ \ \ \ (X_{n}>J)\end{cases},

(65)

\displaystyle Z_{n}^{(J)}:=

\displaystyle X_{n}-Y_{n}^{(J)},

(66)

\displaystyle M_{J}:=

\displaystyle\min\left\{j\in\mathbb{N}\ |\ g(j)>J\right\}.

(67)

Clearly, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{J\to\infty}M_{J}=\infty.

(68)

Lemma 4.5
.

For arbitrary
J\in\mathbb{R}
,
\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{n}^{(J)}\right]
and
\mathbb{E}\left[Z_{n}^{(J)}\right]
exist and do not depend on
n
.

Proof.

Since
\left\{Y_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is ergodic stationary,
\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{n}^{(J)}\right]
does not depend on
n
if it exists.
For arbitrary
J
and
n
, the support of

is a finite set.
Therefore, its existence is also apparent.
By lemma
4.3
and (

), it is clear that

exists and does not depend on
n
.
∎

We use the notation
\mu_{Y}^{(J)}
and
\mu_{Z}^{(J)}
as
\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{n}^{(J)}\right]
and
\mathbb{E}\left[Z_{n}^{(J)}\right]
, respectively.
Clearly, it is satisfied that

\displaystyle\mu_{X}=\mu_{Y}^{(J)}+\mu_{Z}^{(J)}.

(69)

Lemma 4.6
.

For arbitrary
J\in\mathbb{R}
and
K\in\mathbb{N}
,
\text{Var}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{K}}\sum_{n=1}^{K}\left(Y_{n}^{(J)}-\mu_{Y}^{(J)}\right)\right]

and
\text{Var}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{K}}\sum_{n=1}^{K}\left(Z_{n}^{(J)}-\mu_{Z}^{(J)}\right)\right]
exist.

Proof.

For all
J
, the support of
Y_{n}^{(J)}-\mu_{Y}^{(J)}
is a finite set.
Then, for all
J
and
K
, the support of
\sum_{n=1}^{K}\left(Y_{n}^{(J)}-\mu_{Y}^{(J)}\right)
is also a finite set and
\text{Var}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{K}}\sum_{n=1}^{K}\left(Y_{n}^{(J)}-\mu_{Y}^{(J)}\right)\right]
exists.
By (
66
) and (
69
), we have

\displaystyle\text{Var}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{K}}\sum_{n=1}^{K}\left(Z_{n}^{(J)}-\mu_{Z}^{(J)}\right)\right]

(70)

\displaystyle\begin{split}=&\text{Var}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{K}}\sum_{n=1}^{K}\left(X_{k}-\mu_{X}\right)\right]+\text{Var}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{K}}\sum_{n=1}^{K}\left(Y_{n}^{(J)}-\mu_{Y}^{(J)}\right)\right]\\
&-2\text{Cov}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{K}}\sum_{n=1}^{K}\left(X_{n}-\mu_{X}\right),\frac{1}{\sqrt{K}}\sum_{n=1}^{K}\left(Y_{n}^{(J)}-\mu_{Y}^{(J)}\right)\right]\end{split}

(71)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\leq&\text{Var}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{K}}\sum_{n=1}^{K}\left(X_{n}-\mu_{X}\right)\right]+\text{Var}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{K}}\sum_{n=1}^{K}\left(Y_{n}^{(J)}-\mu_{Y}^{(J)}\right)\right]\\
&+2\sqrt{\text{Var}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{K}}\sum_{n=1}^{K}\left(X_{n}-\mu_{X}\right)\right]\text{Var}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{K}}\sum_{n=1}^{K}\left(Y_{n}^{(J)}-\mu_{Y}^{(J)}\right)\right]}.\end{split}

(72)

Then, by lemma
4.4
,
\text{Var}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{K}}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left(Z_{k}^{(J)}-\mu_{Z}^{(J)}\right)\right]
exists for all
J
and
K
.
∎

Lemma 4.7
.

As
J\to\infty
,
\mu_{Z}^{(J)}\to 0
.

Proof.

By lemma
4.1
and (
55
), there exist positive real numbers
C
and
\delta<1
such that

\displaystyle\left|\mu_{Z}^{(J)}\right|=

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{j=M_{J}}^{\infty}g(j)2^{-L}(1-2^{-L})^{j-1}\right|

(73)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle C\delta^{M_{J}}\ \underset{J\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\ 0.

(74)

∎

Lemma 4.8
.

For all
J\in\mathbb{R}
, there exists a positive real number
\tau_{Y}^{(J)}
such that
\frac{1}{\sqrt{K}}\sum_{n=1}^{K}(Y_{n}^{(J)}-\mu_{Y}^{(J)})
follows a normal distribution
\mathcal{N}(0,\tau_{Y}^{(J)})
as
K\to\infty
.

Proof.

It is obvious that
\left\{Y_{n}^{(J)}-\mu_{Y}^{(J)}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is ergodic stationary.
By the definitions of
\{Y_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, for arbitrary positive integer
l
,
\left\{Y_{n}^{(J)}-\mu_{Y}^{(J)}\right\}_{n=1,2,\dots,l}
and
\left\{Y_{n}^{(J)}-\mu_{Y}^{(J)}\right\}_{n=l+M_{J},l+M_{J}+1,\dots}
are independent each other.
For fixed

,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[|Y_{1}^{(J)}-\mu_{Y}^{(J)}|^{3}\right]<\infty

(75)

because the support of

is a finite set.
Then, by the theorem 2 in Ref.
[
19
]
, the lemma holds.
∎

Lemma 4.9
.

The following equation is true:

\displaystyle\lim_{J\to\infty}\lim_{K\to\infty}\text{Var}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{K}}\sum_{n=1}^{K}(Z_{n}^{(J)}-\mu_{Z}^{(J)})\right]=0.

(76)

Proof.

Since
\left\{Z_{n}^{(J)}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is ergodic stationary, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\text{Var}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{K}}\sum_{n=1}^{K}(Z_{n}^{(J)}-\mu_{Z}^{(J)})\right]\\
=&\text{Var}\left[Z_{1}^{(J)}-\mu_{Z}^{(J)}\right]+2\sum_{n=2}^{K}\left(1-\frac{n-1}{K}\right)\text{Cov}\left[Z_{1}^{(J)}-\mu_{Z}^{(J)},Z_{n}^{(J)}-\mu_{Z}^{(J)}\right].\end{split}

(77)

By lemma
4.1
, there exist positive real numbers
C_{1}
and
\delta_{1}<1
such that

\displaystyle\text{Var}\left[Z_{1}^{(J)}-\mu_{Z}^{(J)}\right]=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=M_{J}}^{\infty}\left\{g(j)\right\}^{2}2^{-L}\left(1-2^{{}^{L}}\right)^{j-1}-\left(\mu_{Z}^{(J)}\right)^{2}

(78)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j=M_{J}}^{\infty}C_{1}\delta_{1}^{j-1}-\left(\mu_{Z}^{(J)}\right)^{2}

(79)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{C_{1}\delta_{1}^{M_{J}}}{1-\delta_{1}}-\left(\mu_{Z}^{(J)}\right)^{2}.

(80)

Then, by lemma
4.7
,

\displaystyle\lim_{J\to\infty}\lim_{K\to\infty}\text{Var}\left[Z_{1}^{(J)}-\mu_{Z}^{(J)}\right]=0.

(81)

By (

), we have

\displaystyle\text{Pr}\left[A_{1}=i,\ A_{n}=j\right]-\text{Pr}\left[A_{1}=i\right]\text{Pr}\left[A_{n}=j\right]=0

(82)

for

.
Then, by lemmas
4.1
and
4.2
, for
n\geq M_{J}+1
, there exist positive real numbers
C_{2}
and
\delta_{2}<1
such that

\displaystyle\left|\text{Cov}\left[Z_{1}^{(J)}-\mu_{Z}^{(J)},Z_{n}^{(J)}-\mu_{Z}^{(J)}\right]\right|

(83)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{i=M_{J}}^{\infty}\sum_{j=n-1}^{\infty}g(i)g(j)\left(\text{Pr}\left[A_{1}=i,\ A_{n}=j\right]-\text{Pr}\left[A_{1}=i\right]\text{Pr}\left[A_{n}=j\right]\right)\right|

(84)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle C_{2}\delta_{2}^{M_{J}+n},

(85)

and for
n\leq M_{J}
, there exist positive real numbers
C_{3}
and
\delta_{3}<1
such that

\displaystyle\left|\text{Cov}\left[Z_{k}^{(J)}-\mu_{Z}^{(J)},Z_{l}^{(J)}-\mu_{Z}^{(J)}\right]\right|

(86)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{i=M_{J}}^{\infty}\sum_{j=M_{J}}^{\infty}g(i)g(j)\left(\text{Pr}\left[A_{1}=i,\ A_{n}=j\right]-\text{Pr}\left[A_{1}=i\right]\text{Pr}\left[A_{n}=j\right]\right)\right|

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle C_{3}\delta_{3}^{2M_{J}}.

(88)

Then, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{n=2}^{K}\left(1-\frac{n-1}{K}\right)\text{Cov}\left[Z_{1}^{(J)}-\mu_{Z}^{(J)},Z_{n}^{(J)}-\mu_{Z}^{(J)}\right]\right|

(89)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{K}\left|\text{Cov}\left[Z_{1}^{(J)}-\mu_{Z}^{(J)},Z_{n}^{(J)}-\mu_{Z}^{(J)}\right]\right|

(90)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{M_{J}}C_{3}\delta_{3}^{2M_{J}}+\sum_{n=M_{J}+1}^{K}C_{2}\delta_{2}^{M_{J}+n}

\displaystyle\underset{K\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\ C_{3}\delta_{3}^{2M_{J}}(M_{J}-1)+\frac{C_{2}\delta_{2}^{2M_{J}+1}}{1-\delta_{2}}

\displaystyle\underset{J\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\ 0.

(94)

is holds.

4.2
Nonstationary case

In real situation, we set
Q
sufficiently large but finite, and we cannot assume that
\{A_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is ergodic stationary.
However, even in such situation,
\{A_{n}\}_{n\geq T}
approximately satisfies ergodic stationary condition as
T\to\infty
and we can show that using a normal distribution is proper.
Let
X_{n}^{(Q)}
be a random variable described as

\displaystyle X_{n}^{(Q)}=\begin{cases}X_{n}\ &(X_{n}\leq Q+n-1)\\
g(Q+n-1)\ &(\text{otherwise})\end{cases}.

(95)

We show that theorem
4.1
holds even if we replace
\{X_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
with
\left\{X^{(Q)}_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.
Concretely, we prove the following.

Theorem 4.2
.

For all
Q
and
K
,
\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{K}}\sum_{n=1}^{K}(X_{n}-X_{n}^{(Q)})\right]
and
\text{Var}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{K}}\sum_{n=1}^{K}(X_{n}-X_{n}^{(Q)})\right]
exist, and

\displaystyle\lim_{K\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{K}}\sum_{n=1}^{K}(X_{n}-X_{n}^{(Q)})\right]

\displaystyle=0,

(96)

\displaystyle\lim_{K\to\infty}\text{Var}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{K}}\sum_{n=1}^{K}(X_{n}-X_{n}^{(Q)})\right]

\displaystyle=0.

(97)

Proof.

By lemma
4.1
and (
55
), there exist positive real number
C_{1}
and
\delta_{1}<1
such that

\displaystyle 0\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{K}}\sum_{n=1}^{K}(X_{n}-X_{n}^{(Q)})\right]

(98)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{K}}\sum_{n=1}^{K}\sum_{i=Q+n}^{\infty}\left(g(i)-g(Q+n-1)\right)\text{Pr}\left[A_{n}=i\right]

(99)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{K}}\sum_{n=1}^{K}C_{1}\delta_{1}^{Q+n}

(100)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{K}}\frac{C_{1}\delta_{1}^{Q+1}}{1-\delta_{1}}\left(1-\delta_{1}^{K}\right)\ \underset{K\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\ 0.

(101)

By lemmas
4.1
and
4.2
, there exist positive real number
C_{2}
and
\delta_{2}<1
such that

\displaystyle\text{Var}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{K}}\sum_{n=1}^{K}(X_{n}-X_{n}^{(Q)})\right]

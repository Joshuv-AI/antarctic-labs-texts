Marginal Fermi liquid behavior at the onset of 𝟐 ​ 𝒌 𝑭 \boldsymbol{2k_{F}} density wave order in two-dimensional metals with flat hot spots
Lukas Debbeler, Walter Metzner

Abstract
We analyze quantum fluctuation effects at the onset of incommensurate 2 ​ k F 2k_{F} charge- or spin-density wave order in two-dimensional metals, for a model where the ordering wave vector 𝐐 {\bf Q} connects a single pair of hot spots on the Fermi surface with a vanishing Fermi surface curvature. The tangential momentum dependence of the bare dispersion near the hot spots is proportional to | k t | α |k_{t}|^{\alpha} with α > 2 \alpha>2 . We first compute the order parameter susceptibility and the fermion self-energy in random phase approximation (RPA). Logarithmic divergences are subsequently treated by a renormalization group analysis. The coupling between the order parameter fluctuations and the fermions vanishes logarithmically in the low-energy limit. As a consequence, the logarithmic divergences found in RPA do not sum up to anomalous power laws. Instead, only logarithmic corrections to Fermi liquid behavior are obtained. In particular, the quasiparticle weight and the Fermi velocity vanish logarithmically at the hot spots.



I
Introduction

Quantum fluctuations at and near quantum critical points in metallic electron systems can trigger non-Fermi liquid behavior with unconventional temperature, momentum, and frequency dependencies of thermodynamic, spectroscopic, and transport properties
[
1
]
. In view of non-Fermi liquid or “strange metal” behavior observed in several layered compounds such as the high-
T_{c}
cuprates, quantum criticality in two-dimensional systems has attracted particular interest.

Metals at the onset of charge or spin-density wave order can be grouped in several distinct universality classes of quantum critical non-Fermi liquids. The most thoroughly studied case of Néel order is just one example
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
]
.
A particularly intriguing situation arises when the wave vector
{\bf Q}
of the density wave is a
nesting vector
(also known as “
2k_{F}
” vector
[
6
]
) of the Fermi surface, that is, when it connects Fermi points with collinear Fermi velocities
[
7
]
. Charge and spin susceptibilities exhibit a singularity at such wave vectors due to an enhanced phase space for low-energy particle-hole excitations.
The wave vector of a Néel state is a nesting vector only for special electron densities
[
8
,
9
]
.
While fluctuations are naturally stronger in two dimensions, quantum fluctuation effects at the onset of
2k_{F}
density wave order are interesting also in three dimensions
[
10
]
.

Non-Fermi liquid behavior at the onset of charge- or spin-density wave order with
incommensurate

[
11
]
nesting wave vectors
{\bf Q}
in two-dimensional metals has already been analyzed, too.
In a perturbative one-loop calculation of the fermion self-energy, a breakdown of Fermi liquid behavior was found at the
hot spots
on the Fermi surface connected by the ordering wave vector
[
12
]
. If the ordering wave vector
{\bf Q}
connects only a single pair of hot spots, in axial or diagonal direction, the frequency dependence of the one-loop self-energy at the hot spots obeys a power-law with exponent
\frac{2}{3}
. If
{\bf Q}
connects two pairs of hot spots, the imaginary part of the real frequency one-loop self-energy exhibits a linear frequency dependence.
In none of these two cases the perturbative solution is self-consistent, and the feedback of the non-Fermi liquid self-energy seems to shift the ordering wave vector away from the nesting point
[
13
,
14
]
. For the case of a single hot spot pair, it was argued already long ago that quantum fluctuations replace the QCP by a first order transition
[
6
]
.
However, a fluctuation induced flattening of the Fermi surface at the hot spots might save the QCP
[
13
]
, and this scenario was supported by a systematic
\epsilon
-expansion around the critical dimension
d_{c}=\frac{5}{2}

[
15
]
.
For the two hot-spot pair case, a self-consistent solution with a stable QCP was found numerically
[
14
]
.

Recently, we have analyzed non-Fermi liquid behavior at the onset of density wave order for a case where the nesting vector connects a single pair of
flat
hot spots, where the Fermi surface curvature vanishes already in the non-interacting reference system, that is, before fluctuations are taken into account
[
16
]
. Such a situation can arise at special electron filling factors, for example, in a tight-binding model with nearest and next-nearest neighbor hopping on a square lattice.

Figure 1:
Hot spots with vanishing Fermi surface curvature connected by the ordering wave vector
{\bf Q}
, and the relative momentum coordinates
k_{r}
and
k_{t}
.

Expanded in terms of relative momentum coordinates
k_{r}
and
k_{t}
in normal and tangential directions with respect to the Fermi surface at a hot spot (see Fig.
1
), the dispersion relation assumed in Ref.
[
16
]
has the form
\xi_{\bf k}=\epsilon_{\bf k}-\mu=vk_{r}+bk_{t}^{4}
, where
v
is the Fermi velocity at the hot spot, and
b
is a real constant.
Unlike the conventional case of hot spots with finite curvature, the order parameter susceptibility of our model with flat hot spots has a robust peak at the nesting vector. The imaginary part of the perturbative one-loop self-energy was found to depend linearly on (real) frequency, while the momentum and frequency dependences of its real part exhibit logarithmic divergences indicating non-Fermi liquid behavior with a vanishing quasiparticle weight and a vanishing Fermi velocity. The logarithmic divergences were tentatively interpreted as perturbative signatures of power-laws with anomalous exponents
[
16
]
.

In this paper we extend our previous analysis of non-Fermi liquid behavior at
2k_{F}
density wave quantum criticality with flat hot spots in two directions. First, we treat the logarithmic divergences obtained in perturbation theory in a controlled and systematic way by using a field theoretic renormalization group. Second, we generalize the tangential momentum dependence of the bare dispersion from quartic to arbitrary powers, that is,

\xi_{\bf k}=\epsilon_{\bf k}-\mu=vk_{r}+b|k_{t}|^{\alpha}\,,

(1)

with an arbitrary exponent
\alpha>2
. Our motivation for this generalization is to disentangle general features of models with flat hot spots from features specific for the case
\alpha=4
. Moreover, we would like to explore whether one can shed light on the most important but also most tricky case
\alpha=2
by considering the limit
\alpha\to 2
.

The renormalization group analysis reveals that the logarithms found in perturbation theory do not sum up to anomalous power-laws, but rather yield only a logarithmic breakdown of Fermi liquid theory. Hence, the flat hot spot model turns out to be a
marginal
Fermi liquid
[
18
]
.
For
\alpha>2
the renormalization group analysis is controlled by the inverse number of fermion flavors
N
. However, in the limit
\alpha\to 2
, the fluctuation corrections exhibit divergences which cannot be tamed by the renormalization group, indicating a qualitatively different behavior for
\alpha=2
.

Recently, Song
[
17
]
et al. have computed the quasiparticle decay rate near Fermi points with a dispersion of the form Eq. (
1
) in a stable two-dimensional Fermi liquid (away from instabilities), and found an energy dependence of the form
\epsilon^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}}
for
\alpha>2
. While this decay rate is enhanced compared to the conventional quadratic behavior, it is still parametrically smaller than the quasiparticle energy in the low energy limit, for any finite
\alpha
, so that the quasiparticles remain asymptotically stable.

The remainder of the paper is structured as follows. In Sec. II we compute the order parameter susceptibility and the effective interaction at the QCP in one-loop approximation. A perturbative one-loop calculation of the momentum and frequency dependence of the fermion self-energy is performed in Sec. III, and the corresponding renormalization group analysis in Sec. IV. In Sec. V we close the presentation with a summary and discussion of the main results.

II
Susceptibility and effective interaction

We consider a one-band system of interacting fermions with a bare single-particle energy-momentum relation
\epsilon_{\bf k}
. We are dealing exclusively with ground state properties (temperature
T=0
). The bare fermion propagator has the form

G_{0}({\bf k},ik_{0})=\frac{1}{ik_{0}-\xi_{\bf k}}\,,

(2)

where
k_{0}
denotes the imaginary frequency, and
\xi_{\bf k}=\epsilon_{\bf k}-\mu
.
We assume that, in mean-field theory, the system undergoes a charge or spin-density wave transition with an incommensurate and nested wave vector
{\bf Q}
, which connects a pair of hot spots on the Fermi surface, where the dispersion relation in the vicinity of the hot spots has a momentum dependence of the from Eq. (
1
).

In random phase approximation (RPA) the order parameter susceptibility has the form

\chi({\bf q},iq_{0})=\frac{\chi_{0}({\bf q},iq_{0})}{1+g\chi_{0}({\bf q},iq_{0})}\,,

(3)

where
g<0
is the coupling constant parametrizing the interaction in the instability channel. The bare charge or spin susceptibility
\chi_{0}
is related to the particle-hole bubble
\Pi_{0}
by
\chi_{0}({\bf q},iq_{0})=-N\Pi_{0}({\bf q},iq_{0})
, where
N
is the spin multiplicity, and
[
19
]

\Pi_{0}({\bf q},iq_{0})=\int_{\bf k}\int_{k_{0}}G_{0}({\bf k},ik_{0})\,G_{0}({\bf k}-{\bf q},ik_{0}-iq_{0})\,.

(4)

\int_{\bf k}
is a shorthand notation for
\int\frac{d^{2}{\bf k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}
, and
\int_{k_{0}}
for
\int\frac{dk_{0}}{2\pi}
.
While keeping
N
as a general parameter in our equations, we choose
N=2
, corresponding to spin-
\frac{1}{2}
fermions, in all numerical results.
Continuing
\Pi_{0}({\bf q},iq_{0})
analytically to the real frequency axis from the upper complex frequency half-plane yields the retarded polarization function
\Pi_{0}({\bf q},\omega)
.

The RPA susceptibility diverges when
g\chi_{0}({\bf Q},0)=-1
, signalling an instability at the critical coupling
g_{c}=-1/\chi_{0}({\bf Q},0)
toward charge or spin density wave order with one of the nesting wave vectors
{\bf Q}
at which
\chi_{0}({\bf q},0)
has a (finite) peak.

To analyze the behavior of the susceptibility near the singularity, we expand

\delta\Pi_{0}({\bf q},\omega)=\Pi_{0}({\bf q},\omega)-\Pi_{0}({\bf Q},0)\,.

(5)

for
{\bf q}
near
{\bf Q}
and small
\omega
. Momenta near
{\bf Q}
are parametrized by relative momentum coordinates
q_{r}
and
q_{t}
, parallel and perpendicular to
{\bf Q}
, respectively.
The leading contributions to
\delta\Pi_{0}({\bf q},\omega)
come from fermionic momenta near the hot spots connected by
{\bf Q}
, where the dispersion relations in Eq. (
4
) can be expanded as in Eq. (
1
), that is,
\xi_{\bf k}=vk_{r}+b|k_{t}|^{\alpha}
and

\xi_{{\bf k}-{\bf q}}=-v(k_{r}-q_{r})+b|k_{t}-q_{t}|^{\alpha}
.
In the following we assume that
b
is positive. Our derivations and results can be easily adapted to negative
b
.

For
q_{t}=0
all integrations are elementary, and we obtain

\delta\Pi_{0}(q_{r},0,\omega)=\delta\Pi_{0}^{+}(q_{r},0,\omega)+\delta\Pi_{0}^{-}(q_{r},0,\omega)
,
where

\delta\Pi_{0}^{+}(q_{r},0,\omega)=\frac{|\omega-vq_{r}|^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}}{4\pi v(2b)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}}\times\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\cot\frac{\pi}{\alpha}-i&\mbox{for}\;\,\omega>vq_{r}\,,\\[8.53581pt]
(\sin\frac{\pi}{\alpha})^{-1}&\mbox{for}\;\,\omega<vq_{r}\,,\end{array}\right.

(6)

and
\delta\Pi_{0}^{-}(q_{r},0,\omega)=[\delta\Pi_{0}^{+}(q_{r},0,-\omega)]^{*}
.
In the static limit
\omega\to 0
, this yields

\delta\Pi_{0}(q_{r},0,0)=\frac{|vq_{r}|^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}}{2\pi v(2b)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}}\times\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\cot\frac{\pi}{\alpha}&\mbox{for}\;\,q_{r}<0\,,\\[8.53581pt]
(\sin\frac{\pi}{\alpha})^{-1}&\mbox{for}\;\,q_{r}>0\,.\end{array}\right.

(7)

\delta\Pi_{0}(q_{r},0,0)
has a cusp with diverging slope for
q_{r}\to 0
for any
\alpha>2
. In the special case
\alpha=2
, the slope vanishes for
q_{r}<0
.

For
q_{t}\neq 0
, the particle-hole bubble can be expressed in a scaling form as (see Appendix
A
)

\delta\Pi_{0}(q_{r},q_{t},\omega)=\frac{|q_{t}|}{4v}\,\Big[I_{\alpha}\Big(\frac{\omega-vq_{r}}{b|q_{t}|^{\alpha}}\Big)+I_{\alpha}^{*}\Big(\frac{-\omega-vq_{r}}{b|q_{t}|^{\alpha}}\Big)\Big]\,,

(8)

with the dimensionless scaling function

I_{\alpha}(x)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{d\tilde{k}_{t}}{2\pi}\ln\frac{|\tilde{k}_{t}+\frac{1}{2}|^{\alpha}+|\tilde{k}_{t}-\frac{1}{2}|^{\alpha}-x-i0^{+}}{2|\tilde{k}_{t}|^{\alpha}}\,.

(9)

In Fig.
2
we show
I_{\alpha}(x)
for various choices of
\alpha
.

for

.

The integrand in Eq. (
9
) is real for

x<|\tilde{k}_{t}+\frac{1}{2}|^{\alpha}+|\tilde{k}_{t}-\frac{1}{2}|^{\alpha}
.
For
x>|\tilde{k}_{t}+\frac{1}{2}|^{\alpha}+|\tilde{k}_{t}-\frac{1}{2}|^{\alpha}\geq 2^{1-\alpha}
,
the logarithm has a constant imaginary part
-\pi
.
Hence, the imaginary part of
I_{\alpha}(x)
vanishes for
x<2^{1-\alpha}
, while

{\rm Im}I_{\alpha}(x)=-\frac{\tilde{k}_{t}^{0}(x)}{\pi}\quad\mbox{for}\quad x>2^{1-\alpha}\,,

(10)

where
\tilde{k}_{t}^{0}(x)
is the unique positive solution of the equation

|\tilde{k}_{t}+\frac{1}{2}|^{\alpha}+|\tilde{k}_{t}-\frac{1}{2}|^{\alpha}=x
.
For
x=1
, one has
\tilde{k}_{t}^{0}=\frac{1}{2}
for any
\alpha
. This is why all the curves in the right panel of Fig.

go through the same crossing point at

For large
|x|
, the scaling function behaves asymptotically as

I_{\alpha}(x)\sim\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{\cot\frac{\pi}{\alpha}-i}{2^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\pi}|x|^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}&\mbox{for}\;\;x\to\infty\,,\\[5.69054pt]
\frac{(\sin\frac{\pi}{\alpha})^{-1}}{2^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\pi}|x|^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}&\mbox{for}\;\;x\to-\infty\,.\end{array}\right.

(11)

The next-to-leading correction for large
|x|
is of order
|x|^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}
.

For
\alpha=2
and
\alpha=4
, the scaling functions
I_{\alpha}(x)
can be expressed in terms of square roots,

\displaystyle I_{2}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}\pi}\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}-(x+i0^{+})}\,,

(12)

\displaystyle I_{4}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}+\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}-2(x+i0^{+})}}\,.

(13)

Inserting
I_{2}(x)
into Eq. (
8
) one recovers the well-known expression for the particle-hole bubble for a quadratic dispersion relation.
I_{4}(x)
has been computed numerically in Ref.
[
16
]
, but analytic results were found there only for
x=0
and
x=\frac{1}{8}
. We derive Eq. (
13
) in Appendix
A
.

The RPA effective interaction is given by

D({\bf q},iq_{0})=\frac{g}{1+g\chi_{0}({\bf q},iq_{0})}

(14)

on the imaginary frequency axis, and by the same expression with
iq_{0}\to\omega
on the real frequency axis. At the QCP,
g\chi_{0}({\bf Q},0)
is equal to minus one, so that

D({\bf q},\omega)=-\frac{1}{N\delta\Pi_{0}({\bf q},\omega)}\,.

(15)

III
Fermion self-energy

To leading order in the effective interaction
D
, the fermion self-energy is given by the one-loop integral

\Sigma({\bf k},ik_{0})=-M\int_{\bf q}\int_{q_{0}}D({\bf q},iq_{0})\,G_{0}({\bf k}-{\bf q},ik_{0}-iq_{0})\,,

(16)

with
M=1
for a charge-density and
M=3
for a spin-density wave instability
[
13
]
. This approximation for the self-energy is also known as random-phase approximation (RPA).
Analytic continuation of Eq. (
16
) to the real frequency axis yields
[
20
]

\displaystyle\Sigma({\bf k},\omega+i0^{+})=-\frac{M}{\pi}\int d\nu\int_{\bf q}

\displaystyle\Big[b(\nu)\,{\rm Im}D({\bf q},\nu+i0^{+})\,G_{0}({\bf k}-{\bf q},\nu+\omega+i0^{+})

(17)

\displaystyle-\,f(\nu)\,D({\bf q},\nu-\omega-i0^{+})\,{\rm Im}G_{0}({\bf k}-{\bf q},\nu+i0^{+})\Big]\,,

where
b(\nu)=[e^{\beta\nu}-1]^{-1}
and
f(\nu)=[e^{\beta\nu}+1]^{-1}
are the Bose and Fermi functions, respectively. At zero temperature (
\beta=\infty
) these functions become step functions
b(\nu)=-\Theta(-\nu)
and
f(\nu)=\Theta(-\nu)
.
In the following we denote
\Sigma({\bf k},\omega+i0^{+})
,
G({\bf k},\omega+i0^{+})
, and
D({\bf q},\nu+i0^{+})
by
\Sigma({\bf k},\omega)
,
G({\bf k},\omega)
, and
D({\bf q},\nu)
, respectively.

We analyze
\Sigma({\bf k},\omega)
at the QCP for low frequencies
\omega
and momenta
{\bf k}
near one of the hot spots on the Fermi surface, which we denote as
{\bf k}_{H}
. The effective interaction
D({\bf q},\omega)
at the QCP is given by Eq. (
15
) with
\delta\Pi_{0}({\bf q},\omega)
from Eq. (
8
).
The dominant contributions come from momentum transfers
{\bf q}
near
{\bf Q}
, so that
{\bf k}-{\bf q}
is situated near the antipodal hot spot
-{\bf k}_{H}
. Using relative momentum variables as above, the dispersion relation in the fermion propagator can be expanded as
\xi_{{\bf k}-{\bf q}}=-v(k_{r}-q_{r})+b|k_{t}-q_{t}|^{\alpha}
.

To evaluate the self-energy, it is convenient to first consider its imaginary part, and then compute the real part from a Kramers-Kronig relation. The imaginary part of Eq. (
17
) reads

\displaystyle{\rm Im}\Sigma({\bf k},\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{M}{\pi}\int d\nu\int_{\bf q}\left[b(\nu)+f(\nu+\omega)\right]\,{\rm Im}D({\bf q},\nu)

(18)

\displaystyle\times\,{\rm Im}G_{0}({\bf k}-{\bf q},\omega+\nu)\,.

Note that
{\rm Im}D({\bf q},\nu-i0^{+})=-{\rm Im}D({\bf q},\nu+i0^{+})
.
Using the Dirac identity
{\rm Im}G_{0}({\bf k},\omega)=-\pi\delta(\omega-\xi_{{\bf k}})
, the frequency integral in Eq. (
18
) can be easily carried out, yielding

\displaystyle{\rm Im}\Sigma({\bf k},\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle M\int_{\bf q}\left[b(\xi_{{\bf k}-{\bf q}}-\omega)+f(\xi_{{\bf k}-{\bf q}})\right]

(19)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\,{\rm Im}D({\bf q},\xi_{{\bf k}-{\bf q}}-\omega)\,.

At zero temperature, the sum of Bose and Fermi functions in Eq. (
19
) is given by

b(\xi_{{\bf k}-{\bf q}}-\omega)+f(\xi_{{\bf k}-{\bf q}})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}-1&\mbox{for}\;0<\xi_{{\bf k}-{\bf q}}<\omega\,,\\
1&\mbox{for}\;\omega<\xi_{{\bf k}-{\bf q}}<0\,,\\
0&\mbox{else}\,,\end{array}\right.

(20)

restricting thus the contributing momentum region.
The integral in Eq. (
19
) is convergent even if the momentum integration over
q_{r}
and
q_{t}
is extended to infinity.

The real part of the self-energy can be obtained from the Kramers-Kronig-type relation

\Sigma({\bf k},\omega)=-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\omega^{\prime}\,\frac{{\rm Im}\Sigma({\bf k},\omega^{\prime})}{\omega-\omega^{\prime}+i0^{+}}+\mbox{const}\,.

(21)

The last term in this relation is a real constant, which can be absorbed by a shift of the chemical potential. The real part of the self-energy is dominated by contributions from large frequencies in Eq. (
21
), where the low-frequency expansion of
{\rm Im}\Sigma
is not valid. Since we are not interested in a constant offset but rather in the frequency and momentum dependence of the self-energy near the hot spots, we will analyze the difference

\delta\Sigma({\bf k},\omega)=\Sigma({\bf k},\omega)-\Sigma({\bf k}_{H},0)
, where the leading ultraviolet contributions cancel each other.

III.1
Frequency dependence at hot spot

The frequency dependence at the hot spot (for
{\bf k}={\bf k}_{H}
) can be derived by a simple rescaling of the integration variables in Eq. (
19
). Substituting
q_{r}=|\omega/v|\tilde{q}_{r}
and
q_{t}=|\omega/b|^{1/\alpha}\tilde{q}_{t}
, one obtains

{\rm Im}\Sigma({\bf k}_{H},\omega)=-\frac{M}{N}\,A_{s(\omega)}|\omega|\,,

(22)

where
A_{+}
and
A_{-}
are two positive dimensionless numbers depending on
\alpha
and on the sign of
\omega
. These numbers are determined by the integral

A_{s}=-\int^{\prime}_{\tilde{\bf q}}\!{\rm Im}\frac{4s}{|\tilde{q}_{t}|\left[I_{\alpha}\left(\frac{|\tilde{q}_{t}|^{\alpha}-s}{|\tilde{q}_{t}|^{\alpha}}\right)+I_{\alpha}^{*}\left(\frac{-2\tilde{q}_{r}-|\tilde{q}_{t}|^{\alpha}+s}{|\tilde{q}_{t}|^{\alpha}}\right)\right]}\,,

(23)

where
s=s(\omega)=\pm 1
, and the prime at the integral sign indicates a restriction of the integration region to
0<\tilde{q}_{r}+|\tilde{q}_{t}|^{\alpha}<1
for
\omega>0
, and to
-1<\tilde{q}_{r}+|\tilde{q}_{t}|^{\alpha}<0
for
\omega<0
.
Note that the frequency dependence of the self-energy at the hot spot depends neither on
v
nor on
b
.
In Appendix B we show a plot of the coefficients
A_{\pm}
as a function of
\alpha
.
They are positive and finite for all
\alpha>2
and diverge for
\alpha\to 2
. The divergence for a quadratic dispersion is due to the vanishing slope of
\delta\Pi_{0}(q_{r},0,0)
for
q_{r}<0
, see Eq. (
7
).

The real part of the self-energy can be obtained from the Kramers-Kronig relation Eq. (
21
). With
{\rm Im}\Sigma({\bf k}_{H},\omega)
as in Eq. (
22
), the integral in Eq. (
21
) is logarithmically divergent at large frequencies
\omega^{\prime}
. This is due to the fact that the linear frequency dependence has been obtained from an expansion that captures only the asymptotic low frequency behavior, which cannot be extended to all frequencies. The imaginary part of the exact self-energy of any physical system has to vanish in the high-frequency limit. To compute the low-frequency behavior of
{\rm Re}\Sigma
, we mimic the high-frequency decay of
{\rm Im}\Sigma
by imposing an ultraviolet (UV) frequency cutoff
\Lambda
, so that the frequency integration in Eq. (
21
) is restricted to
|\omega^{\prime}|<\Lambda
. Defining
\delta\Sigma({\bf k},\omega)=\Sigma({\bf k},\omega)-\Sigma({\bf k}_{H},0)
, we then obtain

{\rm Re}\,\delta\Sigma({\bf k}_{H},\omega)=-\frac{M}{N}\,\frac{A_{+}+A_{-}}{\pi}\,\omega\ln\frac{\Lambda}{|\omega|}\,,

(24)

for
|\omega|\ll\Lambda
. The imaginary frequency self-energy
\delta\Sigma({\bf k}_{H},ik_{0})
is given by the same expression with
\omega\mapsto ik_{0}
.

The logarithm in Eq. (
24
) implies a logarithmic divergence of the inverse quasiparticle weight
[
19
]
,

1-\partial\Sigma({\bf k}_{H},\omega)/\partial\omega\sim\ln(\Lambda/|\omega|)
.
Hence, Landau quasiparticles do not exist at the hot spots, and Fermi liquid theory breaks down.

Logarithmic divergences are frequently a perturbative manifestation of power-law behavior, especially in (quantum) critical systems. Assuming that the one-loop result in Eq. (
24
) reflects the leading order of an expansion of a power-law, one obtains

\omega-\delta\Sigma({\bf k}_{H},\omega)\propto\big(|\omega|/\Lambda\big)^{-\eta}\omega

(25)

at low frequencies, with the anomalous dimension

\eta=\frac{M}{N}\,\frac{A_{+}+A_{-}}{\pi}\,.

(26)

Hence, the quasiparticle weight vanishes as
|\omega|^{\eta}
in the low-energy limit.
A plot of
\eta
as a function of
\alpha
is shown in Fig.
3
.

Figure 3:
Anomalous dimension
\eta
for
M=1
and
N=2
as a function of
\alpha
.

The power-law in Eq. (
25
) is only an educated guess.
In Sec.
IV
we will see that
\eta
is actually scale dependent, so that the quasiparticle weight ultimately vanishes only logarithmically.

Frequency and momentum dependencies near hot spot

For
k_{t}=0
, we can express
{\rm Im}\Sigma({\bf k},\omega)
from Eq. (

{\rm Im}\Sigma({\bf k},\omega)=-\frac{M}{N}\,A^{(r)}_{s(\omega)}(\tilde{k}_{r})\,|\omega|\,,

(27)

with the dimensionless scaling functions

\displaystyle A^{(r)}_{s}(\tilde{k}_{r})=

(28)

\displaystyle-\int^{\prime}_{\tilde{\bf q}}{\rm Im}\frac{4s}{|\tilde{q}_{t}|\left[I_{\alpha}\left(\frac{-\tilde{k}_{r}+|\tilde{q}_{t}|^{\alpha}-s}{|\tilde{q}_{t}|^{\alpha}}\right)+I_{\alpha}^{*}\left(\frac{\tilde{k}_{r}-2\tilde{q}_{r}-|\tilde{q}_{t}|^{\alpha}+s}{|\tilde{q}_{t}|^{\alpha}}\right)\right]}\,,

where the integration region is restricted to

\omega>0
, and to

.
The rescaled variables are defined by

,

, and
k_{r}=|\omega/v|\tilde{k}_{r}

The scaling functions

are shown graphically for various choices of

IV
Renormalization group analysis

The logarithmic divergences encountered in the one-loop self-energy imply a breakdown of perturbation theory and indicate possible power law behavior with anomalous exponents. For a controlled treatment of these divergences, we now use the field theoretic renormalization group
[
22
,
23
]
.

For a renormalization group analysis it is convenient to describe our system by a quantum field theory with fermion and boson fields, where the latter represent fluctuations of the order parameter. The corresponding action has the form

\displaystyle{\cal S}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{k}\sum_{\sigma}G_{0}^{-1}(k)\psi_{\sigma}^{*}(k)\psi_{\sigma}(k)-\frac{1}{2}\int_{q}\!D^{-1}(q)\phi^{*}(q)\phi(q)

(45)

\displaystyle+\,u\int_{k,q}\sum_{\sigma}\phi(q)\psi_{\sigma}^{*}(k+q)\psi_{\sigma}(k)\,,

where
\psi_{\sigma}^{*}(k)
and
\psi_{\sigma}(k)
with
\sigma\in\{1,\dots,N\}
are fermionic fields corresponding to fermionic creation and annihilation operators, respectively, while
\phi(q)
is a bosonic field describing the order parameter fluctuations. The variables
k=({\bf k},ik_{0})
and
q=({\bf q},iq_{0})
contain frequencies and momenta;
\int_{k}
and
\int_{q}
are shorthand notations for
\int_{\bf k}\int_{k_{0}}
and
\int_{\bf q}\int_{q_{0}}
, respectively.
Note that the interaction term in Eq. (
45
) is hermitian since
\phi(-q)=\phi^{*}(q)
.

An action of the form Eq. (
45
) can be derived from the original purely fermionic action by decoupling the two-fermion interaction via a Hubbard-Stratonovich transformation
[
19
]
. The boson propagator is thereby obtained as
D(q)=D_{0}(q)=g
, and the bare Yukawa coupling is
u=1
.
In one-loop approximation the bosonic self-energy is given by
u^{2}N\Pi_{0}(q)
, so that the boson propagator becomes
D(q)=\left[g^{-1}-N\Pi_{0}(q)\right]^{-1}
, which is equal to the RPA effective interaction in Eq. (
14
).
At the quantum critical point, where
N\Pi_{0}({\bf Q},0)=g^{-1}
, we thus obtain
D^{-1}(q)=-N\delta\Pi_{0}(q)
.
Our scaling and renormalization group analysis is based on the action Eq. (
45
) with

D^{-1}(q)=-N\delta\Pi_{0}(q)
. The one-loop boson self-energy has thus already been taken into account. An analogous procedure has been used previously for a renormalization group analysis of the Ising nematic quantum critical point
[
24
,
25
]
.

Since singular contributions are due to fermions with momenta near the two hot spots
\pm{\bf k}_{H}
, we introduce relative momentum coordinates and expand around the hot spots as in the perturbative analysis in the preceding section. We then need to label fermion fields with an additional index
l=\pm
to distinguish fields with momenta near
{\bf k}_{H}
from those near
-{\bf k}_{H}
, and the bosonic fields are labeled by their momentum transfer being close to
{\bf Q}
or
-{\bf Q}
as
\phi(\pm{\bf Q}+{\bf q},q_{0})=\phi_{\pm}({\bf q},q_{0})=\phi_{\pm}(q)
.
The action Eq. (
45
) can then be written as

\displaystyle{\cal S}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{k}\sum_{l,\sigma}\left(-ik_{0}+vk_{r}+b|k_{t}|^{\alpha}\right)\psi_{l,\sigma}^{*}(k)\psi_{l,\sigma}(k)^{\phantom{*}}

(46)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle N\int_{q}\delta\Pi_{0}(q)\phi_{+}(q)\phi_{-}(-q)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle u\int_{k,q}\sum_{l,\sigma}\phi_{l}(q)\psi_{l,\sigma}^{*}(k+q)\psi_{-l,\sigma}^{\phantom{*}}(k)\,.

We start with a simple analysis of canonical scaling dimensions. Rescaling fermionic momenta and frequencies as

k_{0}\to sk_{0}\,,\;k_{r}\to sk_{r}\,,\;k_{t}\to s^{1/\alpha}k_{t}\,,

(47)

and anagously for bosonic momenta and frequencies
q_{0}
,
q_{r}
, and
q_{t}
, implies

G_{0}(k)\to s^{-1}G_{0}(k)\,,\;D(q)\to s^{-1/\alpha}D(q)\,.

(48)

The latter relation follows from Eq. (
8
). Scaling frequencies by a linear factor
s
, the scaling of the momentum variables is determined by requiring a homogeneous scaling relation for
G_{0}(k)
, that is, all terms contributing to
G_{0}^{-1}(k)
should scale linearly with
s
.
Requiring that the quadratic parts of the action be scale invariant, and taking into account that the integration measures in
\int_{k}
and
\int_{q}
scale as
s^{2+1/\alpha}
, the fields need to be rescaled as

\displaystyle\psi_{l,\sigma}\to s^{-\frac{3}{2}-\frac{1}{2\alpha}}\psi_{l,\sigma}\,,\;\psi_{l,\sigma}^{*}\to s^{-\frac{3}{2}-\frac{1}{2\alpha}}\psi_{l,\sigma}^{*}\,,

(49)

\displaystyle\phi_{l}\to s^{-1-\frac{1}{\alpha}}\phi_{l}\,.

(50)

Inserting this scaling behavior of the fields into the interaction term in Eq. (
46
), one finds that this term is scale invariant, so that the Yukawa coupling constant
u
is marginal.

We now turn to the renormalization group analysis with fluctuations on one-loop level, which corresponds to the leading order in a
1/N
expansion. While the bosonic one-loop self-energy is finite, the fermionic self-energy exhibits logarithmic divergences which were derived in the preceding section. Expressing the self-energy as a function of the three variables
k_{r}
,
k_{t}
, and
k_{0}
, and subtracting
\Sigma({\bf k}_{H},0)
, we found

\displaystyle\delta\Sigma(0,0,ik_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\eta u^{2}\,ik_{0}\ln\frac{\Lambda}{|k_{0}|}\,,

(51)

\displaystyle\delta\Sigma(k_{r},0,0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta_{r}u^{2}\,vk_{r}\ln\frac{\Lambda}{v|k_{r}|}\,,

(52)

for any
\alpha>2
, where
\eta
and
\eta_{r}
are the (tentative) anomalous dimensions defined in Eqs. (
26
) and (
34
), respectively. Note that we have explicitly included the coupling constant
u=1
, for reasons that will become clear below.
For
\alpha=4
there is also a logarithmic divergence in the tangential momentum dependence,

\delta\Sigma(0,k_{t},0)=\eta_{t}u^{2}\,bk_{t}^{4}\ln\frac{\Lambda}{bk_{t}^{4}}\,,

(53)

with
\eta_{t}
from Eq. (
43
), while for
\alpha<4
only a finite correction of the order
|k_{t}|^{\alpha}
is obtained. However, the prefactor
\delta b
of that correction diverges for
\alpha\to 4
, see Eq. (
44
). For
\alpha>4
, the coefficient of the quartic contribution to the self-energy diverges as a power of the ultraviolet cutoff. In the following we focus on the cases
2<\alpha\leq 4
.

A complete one-loop calculation for an action of the form Eq. (
45
) involves also a one-loop correction of the Yukawa vertex. However, for ordering wave vectors distinct from half a reciprocal lattice vector, there is no choice of momenta in the vertex correction at which the singularities of the propagators coalesce
[
6
]
, so that the vertex correction is finite. Hence, the only divergences come from the fermion self-energy.

Since the leading tangential momentum dependence of the self-energy diverges for
\alpha=4
, while it is finite for
\alpha<4
, we need to distinguish these two cases.

IV.1
\boldsymbol{\alpha=4}

Following the field theoretic renormalization procedure
[
22
,
23
]
, we define a renormalized fermionic two-point vertex function

\bar{\Gamma}^{(2)}(k;\bar{v},\bar{b},\bar{u};\lambda)=Z(u;\lambda/\Lambda)\,\Gamma^{(2)}(k;v,b,u;\Lambda)\,,

(54)

where
\Gamma^{(2)}=G_{0}^{-1}-\delta\Sigma
is the unrenormalized vertex function,

Z=1-\eta u^{2}\ln\frac{\Lambda}{\lambda}\,,

(55)

and the renormalized parameters
\bar{u}
,
\bar{v}
,
\bar{b}
are defined as

\displaystyle\bar{u}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Zu\,,

(56)

\displaystyle\bar{v}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Z\left(1+\eta_{r}u^{2}\ln\frac{\Lambda}{\lambda}\right)v\,,

(57)

\displaystyle\bar{b}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Z\left(1+\eta_{t}u^{2}\ln\frac{\Lambda}{\lambda}\right)b\,,\quad\mbox{for}\quad\alpha=4\,.

(58)

The energy scale
\lambda
can be chosen arbitrarily, or it may be used to satisfy normalization conditions of the renormalized theory at specific frequencies and momenta
[
22
,
23
]
.
To second order in the coupling constant, the renormalized two-point vertex, expressed as a function of the renormalized parameters
\bar{v}
,
\bar{b}
, and
\bar{u}
, is finite for
\Lambda\to\infty
:

\displaystyle\bar{\Gamma}^{(2)}(k;\bar{v},\bar{b},\bar{u};\lambda)=ik_{0}\left(1+\eta\bar{u}^{2}\ln\frac{\lambda}{|k_{0}|}\right)

\displaystyle-\bar{v}k_{r}\left(1+\eta_{r}\bar{u}^{2}\ln\frac{\lambda}{\bar{v}|k_{r}|}\right)-\bar{b}k_{t}^{4}\left(1+\eta_{t}\bar{u}^{2}\ln\frac{\lambda}{\bar{b}k_{t}^{4}}\right).\qquad

(59)

The renormalization of the Yukawa coupling, Eq. (
56
), is determined by the renormalization of the Yukawa vertex

\bar{\Gamma}^{(2,1)}(k,q;\bar{u};\lambda)=Z(u;\lambda/\Lambda)\,\Gamma^{(2,1)}(k,q;u;\Lambda)\,.

(60)

Since
\Gamma^{(2,1)}
is finite at the one-loop level, the renormalization of
u
is determined exclusively by
Z
. Discarding non-universal finite one-loop vertex corrections, we approximate
\Gamma^{(2,1)}(0,0;u;\Lambda)
by its bare value
u
, so that
\bar{u}=\bar{\Gamma}^{(2,1)}(0,0;\bar{u};\lambda)=Zu
.

The renormalization group flow is obtained from the evolution of
\bar{\Gamma}^{(2)}(k;\bar{v},\bar{b},\bar{u};\lambda)
upon varying the energy scale
\lambda

[
22
,
23
]
. The bare vertex
\Gamma^{(2)}(k;v,b,u;\Lambda)
does not depend on
\lambda
, so that

\left.\lambda\frac{d}{d\lambda}Z^{-1}\bar{\Gamma}^{(2)}(k;\bar{v},\bar{b},\bar{u};\lambda)\right|_{v,b,u,\Lambda}=0\,,

(61)

and thus

\big[\lambda\partial_{\lambda}+\beta_{\bar{u}}\partial_{\bar{u}}+\beta_{\bar{v}}\partial_{\bar{v}}+\beta_{\bar{b}}\partial_{\bar{b}}-\gamma\big]\bar{\Gamma}^{(2)}(k;\bar{v},\bar{b},\bar{u};\lambda)=0\,,\;

(62)

where
\partial_{\lambda}
is a partial derivative with respect to
\lambda
,

\beta_{\bar{x}}=\left.\lambda\frac{\partial\bar{x}}{\partial\lambda}\right|_{v,b,u,\Lambda}

(63)

for
\bar{x}=\bar{v}
,
\bar{b}
,
\bar{u}
, and

\gamma=\left.\lambda\frac{\partial\ln Z}{\partial\lambda}\right|_{v,b,u,\Lambda}\,.

(64)

Inserting Eq. (
55
) into Eq. (
56
), we obtain

\beta_{\bar{u}}=\eta{\bar{u}}^{3}\,.

(65)

On the right hand side we have replaced
u
by
\bar{u}
, which is justified since
\bar{u}-u
is of order
(\bar{u})^{3}
. The flow equation for
\bar{u}
does not depend on the other variables and can be easily integrated. With the initial condition
\bar{u}=\bar{u}_{0}=1
for
\lambda=\lambda_{0}
we find

\bar{u}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+2\eta\ln(\lambda_{0}/\lambda)}}\,.

(66)

Hence,
\bar{u}
tends to
\bar{u}^{*}=0
for
\lambda\to 0
, albeit very slowly.
Inserting Eq. (
55
) into Eq. (
64
) yields, to order
\bar{u}^{2}
,

\gamma=\eta{\bar{u}}^{2}=\frac{\eta}{1+2\eta\ln(\lambda_{0}/\lambda)}\,.

(67)

Eqs. (
57
) and (
58
) yield, respectively,

\displaystyle\beta_{\bar{v}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\eta-\eta_{r})\,{\bar{u}}^{2}{\bar{v}}\,,

(68)

\displaystyle\beta_{\bar{b}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\eta-\eta_{t})\,{\bar{u}}^{2}{\bar{b}}\,.

(69)

Inserting the solution for
\bar{u}
, Eq. (
66
), the flow equations for
\bar{v}
and
\bar{b}
can be easily integrated to

\displaystyle\bar{v}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{v}_{0}\big[1+2\eta\ln(\lambda_{0}/\lambda)\big]^{\frac{\eta_{r}-\eta}{2\eta}}\,,

(70)

\displaystyle\bar{b}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{b}_{0}\big[1+2\eta\ln(\lambda_{0}/\lambda)\big]^{\frac{\eta_{t}-\eta}{2\eta}}\,,

(71)

where
\bar{v}_{0}
and
\bar{b}_{0}
are the initial values of
\bar{v}
and
\bar{b}
, repectively, at
\lambda=\lambda_{0}
.
Since
\eta_{r}
and
\eta_{t}
are both smaller than
\eta
, the renormalized quantities
\bar{v}
and
\bar{b}
decrease upon decreasing
\lambda
. Initially this decrease is very slow, while ultimately (for
\lambda\ll\lambda_{0}e^{-1/(2\eta)}
) they vanish as some power of a logarithm.

From the flow of the renormalized quantities and
\gamma
as a function of
\lambda
we can obtain the momentum and frequency dependences of the fermionic two-point vertex
\Gamma^{(2)}
, following the standard procedure
[
22
,
23
]
.
We begin with the frequency dependence at the hot spot. For
k_{r}=k_{t}=0
, the flow equation (
62
) for the renormalized vertex
\bar{\Gamma}^{(2)}
can be integrated to

\bar{\Gamma}^{(2)}(0,0,ik_{0})=ik_{0}\big[1+2\eta\ln(\lambda/|k_{0}|)\big]^{\frac{1}{2}}\,,

(72)

with the normalization condition
\bar{\Gamma}^{(2)}(0,0,ik_{0})=ik_{0}
for
|k_{0}|=\lambda
. A simple dimensional argument then yields

\Gamma^{(2)}(0,0,ik_{0})=ik_{0}\big[1+2\eta\ln(\Lambda/|k_{0}|)\big]^{\frac{1}{2}}\,.

(73)

This result agrees with the perturbative expression to linear order in
\eta
. However, the naive expectation of a power law with an anomalous exponent
\eta
was not confirmed by the renormalization group analysis. Instead, only a weaker logarithmic correction to the bare frequency dependence is obtained.
This behavior is similar to the momentum dependence of the two-point vertex in the
\phi^{4}
-theory at the critical dimension
d_{c}=4
, where the interaction is also marginally irrelevant and leads to logarithmic corrections of the momentum dependence
[
23
]
. In that case, however, the beta function is quadratic in the coupling to leading order, while in our model it is cubic, so that the renormalized coupling vanishes more slowly.

From the flow of the renormalized quantities
\bar{v}
and
\bar{b}
in Eqs. (
70
) and (
71
), respectively, we obtain momentum dependent coefficients of the fluctuation-corrected dispersion as

\displaystyle v(k_{r})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v\left(1+2\eta\ln\frac{\Lambda}{v|k_{r}|}\right)^{\frac{\eta_{r}-\eta}{2\eta}}\,,

(74)

\displaystyle b(k_{t})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b\left(1+2\eta\ln\frac{\Lambda}{bk_{t}^{4}}\right)^{\frac{\eta_{t}-\eta}{2\eta}}\,,

(75)

via dimensional arguments. These results agree with the perturbative expressions to leading order in the anomalous dimensions
\eta
,
\eta_{r}
, and
\eta_{t}
. Since
\eta
is larger than
\eta_{r}
and
\eta_{t}
, both
v(k_{r})
and
b(k_{t})
vanish upon approaching the hot spot in momentum space, albeit very slowly.

IV.2
\boldsymbol{\alpha<4}

For
\alpha<4
the tangential momentum dependence of the fermion propagator acquires only a finite correction, as given by
\delta b
in Eq. (
41
). The divergences in the self-energy corrections to the frequency and radial momentum dependence are qualitatively the same as for
\alpha=4
. These divergences can therefore be treated by the same renormalization group procedure as for
\alpha=4
. The defining equations of
Z
,
\bar{u}
, and
\bar{v}
, as well as their flow equations, remain the same, and one obtains the same results for the frequency and radial momentum dependence as for
\alpha=4
. The dependence on
\alpha
enters only via the parameters
\eta
and
\eta_{r}
. As to the tangential momentum dependence, a renormalized coefficient
\bar{b}
could be defined as

\bar{b}=Z\left(b+u^{2}\delta b\right)\,.

(76)

The flow of
\bar{b}
is then driven only by the divergence of the
Z
-factor.

The above procedure is satisfactory as long as
\delta b/b
is small, which is the case for
\alpha
staying sufficiently far away from two and four. For
\alpha\to 2
, not only
\delta b/b
becomes large, but also
\eta
and
\eta_{r}
, so that the one-loop theory breaks down completely. For
\alpha\to 4
, however, the frequency and radial momentum dependence seem to be well captured by the one-loop approximation, and we can deal with the tangential momentum dependence by resorting to an
\epsilon
-expansion in
\epsilon=4-\alpha
.

To this end, we define our renormalization group analysis for a scaling of the tangential momentum variable as
k_{t}\to s^{1/4}k_{t}
instead of
k_{t}\to s^{1/\alpha}k_{t}
. In this way, the coefficient
b
becomes a relevant variable for
\alpha<4
already at tree level (without loop corrections), while the Yukawa coupling
u
remains marginal.
The self-energy from Eq. (
40
) is written accordingly in the form

\delta\Sigma(0,k_{t},0)=\frac{4}{\epsilon}\eta_{t}u^{2}b|k_{t}|^{-\epsilon}k_{t}^{4}\,,

(77)

where
\eta_{t}
can be evaluated for
\alpha=4
to leading order in
\epsilon
.
The renormalized vertex
\bar{\Gamma}^{(2)}=Z\Gamma^{(2)}
is finite for
\Lambda\to\infty
and
\epsilon\to 0
if
\bar{u}
and
\bar{v}
are chosen as before, and

\bar{b}=Z\left(1+\frac{4}{\epsilon}\eta_{t}u^{2}\right)\lambda^{-\epsilon/4}\,b\,.

(78)

Applying
\lambda\partial_{\lambda}
to
\bar{b}
, and expanding in
\bar{u}
and
\epsilon
, we obtain the
\beta
-function for the flow of
\bar{b}
to order
\bar{u}^{2}
and order
\epsilon
,

\beta_{\bar{b}}=-\frac{\epsilon}{4}\,\bar{b}+(\eta-\eta_{t})\bar{u}^{2}\,\bar{b}\,.

(79)

For
\epsilon\to 0
this
\beta
-function reduces continuously to the
\beta
-function for
\alpha=4
in Eq. (
69
).
The flow equation for
\bar{b}
can be integrated to

\bar{b}=\bar{b}_{0}(\lambda_{0}/\lambda)^{\epsilon/4}\big[1+2\eta\ln(\lambda_{0}/\lambda)\big]^{\frac{\eta_{t}-\eta}{2\eta}}\,,

(80)

where
\bar{b}_{0}
is the initial value of
\bar{b}
at
\lambda=\lambda_{0}
.
Using once again the common dimensional arguments
[
23
]
, we thus obtain the tangential momentum dependence of the fluctuation corrected dispersion in the form
b(k_{t})|k_{t}|^{\alpha}
, with

b(k_{t})=b\left(1+2\eta\ln\frac{\Lambda}{b|k_{t}|^{\alpha}}\right)^{\frac{\eta_{t}-\eta}{2\eta}}\,.

(81)

The first scale dependent factor in Eq. (
80
) simply shifts the exponent from
k_{t}^{4}
to
|k_{t}|^{\alpha}
, while the second factor leads to a logarithmic correction. For
\alpha\to 4
we recover the behavior of
b(k_{t})
in Eq. (
75
).

Since
\eta_{r}>\eta_{t}
for all
\alpha
, the renormalized Fermi velocity
v(k_{r})
vanishes slightly faster than
b(k_{t})
. Hence, the renormalized Fermi surface is slightly (logarithmically) flatter than the bare one.

V
Conclusion

We have analyzed quantum fluctuation effects at the onset of incommensurate
2k_{F}
charge- or spin-density wave order in two-dimensional metals, for a model where the ordering wave vector
{\bf Q}
connects a single pair of hot spots on the Fermi surface with vanishing Fermi surface curvature. The tangential momentum dependence of the bare dispersion near the hot spots is proportional to
|k_{t}|^{\alpha}
with
\alpha>2
.
Varying
\alpha
yields a smooth interpolation between the conventional parabolic case with a finite Fermi surface curvature, for
\alpha=2
, and the quartic case
\alpha=4
, which we have analyzed previously within RPA
[
16
]
.

We have first computed the order parameter susceptibility and the fermion self-energy within RPA for generic values of
\alpha
. The static susceptibility forms a sharp peak in momentum space at
{\bf Q}
for any
\alpha>2
, while for
\alpha=2
the susceptibility becomes flat in a half-plane near
{\bf Q}

[
26
]
. The susceptibility (as a function of momentum and frequency) can be written in a scaling form with a scaling function that depends only on one variable. For
\alpha=2
and
\alpha=4
exact analytic expressions for the scaling functions can be obtained.
At the hot spots the (real) frequency dependence of the imaginary part of the fermion self-energy is linear and slightly asymmetric for any
\alpha>2
, while the real part exhibits a logarithmic divergence, which indicates a vanishing quasiparticle weight. The momentum dependence perpendicular to the Fermi surface also develops a logarithmic divergence for any
\alpha>2
. For
\alpha=4
also the coefficient of the quartic momentum dependence diverges logarithmically, while for
\alpha<4
only a finite correction to the prefactor of the leading
|k_{t}|^{\alpha}
term is obtained. For
\alpha=4
, these RPA results have already been derived in Ref.
[
16
]
, except for the exact analytic formula for the susceptibility.

A field-theoretic renormalization group analysis reveals that the logarithmic divergences in RPA are
not
perturbative signatures of non-Fermi liquid behavior with anomalous power laws. Instead, only logarithmic corrections to Fermi liquid behavior are obtained. In particular, the quasiparticle weight and the Fermi velocity vanish logarithmically at the hot spots. Fermi liquids with a logarithmically vanishing quasiparticle weight are known as
marginal
Fermi liquids
[
18
]
.
The reason for this relatively mild breakdown of Fermi liquid behavior is the vanishing renormalized coupling between the electrons and the critical order parameter fluctuations, which is due to the suppression of spectral weight at the Fermi level.

The one-loop renormalization group analysis is controlled by a
1/N
expansion, where
N
is the number of fermion species. Higher loop orders correspond to higher orders in
1/N
. The one-loop fluctuation corrections to the fermion self-energy are numerically quite small for
N=2
(that is, for electrons), as long as
\alpha
is not close to two. Feedback of the marginal Fermi liquid behavior of the self-energy on the susceptibility occurs only at the two-loop level. Except for
\alpha\approx 2
, it is unlikely that this feedback will significantly affect the robust peak at
{\bf q}={\bf Q}
in the susceptibility. Hence, the
2k_{F}
quantum critical point is not destroyed by fluctuations. This conclusion holds, in particular, for the physically realizable case
\alpha=4
.

For
\alpha\to 2
, instead, the one-loop fluctuation corrections become large for any fixed finite
N
. Not only the RPA, but also the one-loop renormalization group becomes uncontrolled in this limit. Hence, for the parabolic case
\alpha=2
, we have not gained much further insight beyond the results of Refs.
[
13
]
and
[
15
]
.
The fluctuation induced flattening of the Fermi surface found for
\alpha=2
in Refs.
[
13
,
15
]
has been confirmed for
\alpha>2
, and may thus be a rather generic effect.

Abstract
. We study a nonlocal capillarity problem with interaction kernels that are possibly anisotropic and not necessarily invariant under scaling. In particular, the lack of scale invariance will be modeled via two different fractional exponents s 1 , s 2 ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) s_{1},s_{2}\in(0,1) which take into account the possibility that the container and the environment present different features with respect to particle interactions. We determine a nonlocal Young’s law for the contact angle and discuss the unique solvability of the corresponding equation in terms of the interaction kernels and of the relative adhesion coeﬃcient.

1.
Introduction and main results

In the classical capillarity theory (see e.g.
[
5
,
6
]
)
the contact angle is defined as the angle
\vartheta
at which a liquid interface meets a solid surface.
At the equilibrium, this angle is expressed by the Young’s law
in terms of the relative adhesion coeﬃcient
\sigma
via the classical formula

\cos(\pi-\vartheta)=\sigma.

The contact angle plays also an important role in the
fluid spreading on a solid surface, determining also the velocity of the moving contact lines
(see e.g.
[
4
]
and the references therein).

The contact angle is certainly the “macroscopic”
outcome of several complex “microscopic” phenomena, involving physical chemistry,
statistical physics and fluid dynamics,
and ultimately relying on the effect of long-range and
distance-dependent interactions between atoms or molecules
(such as van der Waals forces). It is therefore
of great interest to understand how the interplay between different microscopic
effects generates an effective contact angle at a macroscopic scale,
and to detect the proximal regions of the interfaces (likely, at a very small distance
from the contact line)
in which the effect of the singular long-range potentials may produce a significant effect,
see e.g.
[
15
,
12
]
.

To further understand the role of long-range particle interactions in models
related to capillarity theory,
a modification of the classical Gauß free energy functional
has been introduced in
[
17
]
that took into account
surface tension energies of nonlocal type and modeled on the fractional perimeter
presented in
[
1
]
.
These new variational principles lead to suitable equilibrium conditions that determine
a specific contact angle depending on the relative adhesion coeﬃcient
and on the properties of the interaction kernel.
The classical limit angle was then obtained
from this long-range prescription via a limit procedure, and precise asymptotics
have been provided in
[
9
]
.
Local minimizers in the fractional capillarity model have been studied in
[
8
]
,
where their blow-up limits at boundary points have been considered, showing, by means of
a new monotonicity formula, that these blow-up limits are cones, and giving a complete
characterization of such cones in the planar case.

The main goal of this paper is to present a capillarity theory of nonlocal
type in which the long-range particle interactions are
possibly anisotropic
and not necessarily invariant under scaling.
This setting is specifically motivated by the case
in which
the potential interactions of the droplet with
the container and those with the environment
are subject to different van der Waals forces.
These two different interactions will be modeled here by
two different fractional exponents.
In this setting, we determine a nonlocal Young’s law for the contact angle,
which extends the known one in the nonlocal isotropic setting and
recovers the classical one as a limit case.

We now discuss in further detail the type of particle interactions that we take into account
and the variational structure of the corresponding anisotropic nonlocal capillarity theory.

1.1.
Interaction kernels

Owing to
[
1
]
, the most widely studied interaction kernel of singular type
in problems related to nonlocal surface tension is

(1.1)

K_{s}(\zeta):=\frac{1}{|\zeta|^{n+s}}\quad\text{for all $\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}$},

with
s\in(0,1)
.
Here, we aim at considering more general kernels than the one in (
1.1

with a twofold objective: on the one hand, we wish to initiate and consolidate
a nonlocal capillarity theory in an
anisotropic
scenario; on the other hand,
we want to also model the case in which the particle interaction
of the container has a
different structure
with respect to the one
of the external environment.

The first of these two goals will be pursued by considering interaction kernels that are
not
necessarily invariant under rotation

interactions
with different homogeneity
inside the container and in the external environment.

More specifically, the mathematical setting in which we work is the following.

1.3.
Main results: nonlocal Young’s law

One of the pivotal steps of any capillarity theory is the determination
of the contact angle between the droplet and the container
(in jargon, the Young’s law). In our setting,
this Young’s law is very sensitive to the relative homogeneity of the
interacting kernels.

Loosely speaking, when
s_{1}<s_{2}
, at small scales (which are the ones
which we believe are more significant in the local determination of the contact angle),
the interaction between the droplet and the exterior of the container
prevails
2
2

2

For instance, when
s_{1}<s_{2}
,
\Omega:=\{x_{n}>0\}
,

E:=\{0<x_{n}<\beta\,|x^{\prime}|\}

and
r\in(0,\varrho)
,
one sees from (
1.2
) and the change of variables
(X,Y):=\left(\frac{x}{r},\frac{y}{r}\right)
that

\displaystyle\frac{I_{1}(E\cap B_{r},E^{c}\cap\Omega\cap B_{r})}{I_{2}(E\cap B_{r},\Omega^{c}\cap B_{r})}\leqslant\lambda^{2}\;\frac{\displaystyle\iint_{(E\cap B_{r})\times(E^{c}\cap\Omega\cap B_{r})}\frac{dx\,dy}{|x-y|^{n+s_{1}}}}{\displaystyle\iint_{(E\cap B_{r})\times(\Omega^{c}\cap B_{r})}\frac{dx\,dy}{|x-y|^{n+s_{2}}}}

\displaystyle\qquad=\lambda^{2}\;r^{s_{2}-s_{1}}\;\frac{\displaystyle\iint_{(E\cap B_{1})\times(E^{c}\cap\Omega\cap B_{1})}\frac{dX\,dY}{|X-Y|^{n+s_{1}}}}{\displaystyle\iint_{(E\cap B_{1})\times(\Omega^{c}\cap B_{1})}\frac{dX\,dY}{|X-Y|^{n+s_{2}}}},

which is infinitesimal when
r\searrow 0
. This suggests that in the small vicinity of contact points,
when
s_{1}<s_{2}
, the effect of the kernel
K_{2}
in the determination of the energy minimizers
and of their geometric properties plays a dominant role with respect to that played by
K_{1}
.
with respect to the one between the droplet and the interior of the container.
Thus, in this situation, the
sign of the relative adhesion coeﬃcient
\sigma
becomes determinant:
in the hydrophilic regime
\sigma<0
the droplet is “absorbed”
by the boundary of the container, thus producing a zero contact angle;
instead, in the hydrorepellent regime
\sigma>0

the droplet is “held off” the boundary of the container,
thus producing a contact angle equal to
\pi
; finally,
in the neutral case
\sigma=0
the behavior of the second interaction kernel
becomes irrelevant. When
\sigma=0
and additionally the problem is isotropic, the contact angle becomes
\pi/2
.

Conversely, when
s_{1}>s_{2}
,
the interaction between the droplet and the interior of the container
is, at small scales, significantly stronger
than that between the droplet and the exterior of the container.
In this situation, the relative adhesion coeﬃcient
\sigma

does not play any role and the contact angle is determined
by an integral cancellation condition (that will be explicitly provided in (
1.20
)).
When the first kernel is isotropic, this condition simplifies and the contact
angle is proved to be
\pi/2
.

More precisely, the determination of the contact angle relies on a delicate cancellation
of the singular kernel contributions, which requires the determination
of an auxiliary angle which is “symmetric” (in a suitable sense of “measuring singular
interactions”) with respect to the contact angle itself: this “dual contact angle”
will be denoted by
\widehat{\vartheta}
and the cancellation property will be described
in detail in the forthcoming formula (
1.20
).

The detailed analysis of the contact angle when
s_{1}\neq s_{2}
is given in the forthcoming
Theorem
1.4

When instead
s_{1}=s_{2}
, the internal and external interactions
equally contribute at small scales. This situation will be analyzed in Theorem

and will lead to a contact angle described by an integral condition (given explicitly in (
1.25

and reformulated in (

Besides, we think that the detection of a contact angle in a nonlocal capillarity setting
is an interesting feature in itself, especially when we compare this situation
with the stickiness phenomenon for the nonlocal minimal surfaces, as discovered in
[
10

.
More specifically, for nonlocal minimal surfaces, the long-range interactions
make it possible for the surface to stick to a domain (even if the domain is smooth and convex),
thus changing dramatically the boundary analysis (moreover, this phenomenon is essentially “generic”, see
[
11

). The possible detection of the contact
angle for the nonlocal capillarity theory instead highlights the fact that the boundary analysis of this theory is somewhat “sufficiently robust” with respect to the classical case.
Roughly speaking, we believe that this important difference between nonlocal minimal surfaces
and nonlocal capillarity theory is due to the fact that in the latter the mass is always placed in a bounded
region, whence the energy contributions coming from infinity have a different nature than the ones occurring
for nonlocal perimeter functionals.

We also stress that conditions (

to speak about a contact angle some quantitative condition is in order (roughly speaking,
otherwise the droplet does not meet the boundary of the container
with a nontrivial angle, rather preferring to either detach from the container
and float, or to completely stick at the boundary
by surrounding it).

Proof of Proposition

\mathcal{D}_{\vartheta}(\bar{\vartheta})=\lim_{\rho\searrow 0}\left(\int_{J_{\vartheta,\vartheta+\bar{\vartheta}}\setminus B_{\rho}(e(\vartheta))}\frac{a_{1}(\overrightarrow{x-e(\vartheta)})}{|x-e(\vartheta)|^{n+s_{1}}}\,dx-\int_{J_{0,\vartheta}\setminus B_{\rho}(e(\vartheta))}\frac{a_{1}(\overrightarrow{x-e(\vartheta)})}{|x-e(\vartheta)|^{n+s_{1}}}\,dx\right).

The intuition behind this set decomposition
is that, on the one hand, the set

accounts for the cancellations due to the symmetry of

(corresponding to the reflection through

, namely

on the other hand, the remaining integral contributions

,
but they may provide additional terms when

is not constant.
To overcome this difficulty, our idea is to exploit the continuity
of

together with the reflection through the horizontal hyperplane
in order to “approximately cancel” as many contributions as possible.

This idea by itself however does not exhaust the complexity of the problem,
because two adjacent points can end up being projected far away from each other
on the sphere (for instance, if a point is close to

and the other to

complication, we exploit the set

: roughly
speaking, points outside

remain sufficiently
close after they get projected
on the sphere (and here we can take advantage of the continuity of
a_{1,\vartheta}
),
while the points in
{\mathcal{B}}_{\varepsilon}

provide an additional, but small, correction, in view of
the location of
{\mathcal{B}}_{\varepsilon}
and of its measure.

The details of the quantitative computation needed to
implement this combination of ideas go as follows.

We stress that

Thus, using (

B_{2}
,

\begin{split}\int_{J_{-\varepsilon,0}\cap B_{2}\cap R_{\varepsilon}}\frac{a_{1,\vartheta}(\overrightarrow{x-e_{1}})}{|x-e_{1}|^{n+s_{1}}}\,dx&=\int_{R_{\varepsilon}\cap\{x_{n}<0\}\cap B_{2}}\frac{a_{1,\vartheta}(\overrightarrow{x-e_{1}})}{|x-e_{1}|^{n+s_{1}}}\,dx\\
&\leqslant\int_{R_{\varepsilon}\cap\{x_{n}>0\}\cap B_{2}}\frac{a_{1,\vartheta}(\overrightarrow{e_{1}-x})}{|e_{1}-x|^{n+s_{1}}}\,dx+C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{2}}\frac{dx}{|x-e_{1}|^{n+s_{1}}}\\
&\leqslant\int_{J_{0,\varepsilon}\cap B_{2}\cap R_{\varepsilon}}\frac{a_{1,\vartheta}(\overrightarrow{x-e_{1}})}{|x-e_{1}|^{n+s_{1}}}\,dx+C.\end{split}

Plugging this cancellation into (
2.7
), we conclude that

\Xi\leqslant\lim_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\left(\int_{(J_{-\varepsilon,0}\cap B_{2})\setminus R_{\varepsilon}}\frac{a_{1,\vartheta}(\overrightarrow{x-e_{1}})}{|x-e_{1}|^{n+s_{1}}}\,dx-\int_{(J_{0,\varepsilon}\cap B_{2})\setminus R_{\varepsilon}}\frac{a_{1,\vartheta}(\overrightarrow{x-e_{1}})}{|x-e_{1}|^{n+s_{1}}}\,dx-\frac{1}{C\varepsilon^{s_{1}}}+C\right).

Using the change of variable
x\mapsto(x^{\prime},-x_{n})
and noticing that
|(x^{\prime},-x_{n})-e_{1}|=|x-e_{1}|
, we thus find that

(2.9)

\begin{split}\Xi\,&\leqslant\,\lim_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\left(\int_{(J_{0,\varepsilon}\cap B_{2})\setminus R_{\varepsilon}}\frac{a_{1,\vartheta}(\overrightarrow{(x^{\prime},-x_{n})-e_{1}})-a_{1,\vartheta}(\overrightarrow{x-e_{1}})}{|x-e_{1}|^{n+s_{1}}}\,dx-\frac{1}{C\varepsilon^{s_{1}}}+C\right)\\
&=\,\lim_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\left(\int_{J^{\star}_{\varepsilon}\cap B_{2}}\frac{a_{1,\vartheta}(\overrightarrow{(x^{\prime},-x_{n})-e_{1}})-a_{1,\vartheta}(\overrightarrow{x-e_{1}})}{|x-e_{1}|^{n+s_{1}}}\,dx-\frac{1}{C\varepsilon^{s_{1}}}+C\right).\end{split}

We point out that

(2.10)

J_{\varepsilon}^{\star}\subseteq\{x_{1}\geqslant 1\}.

Indeed, if
x\in J_{\varepsilon}^{\star}
, then
x\in J_{0,\varepsilon}
, whence

(2.11)

x_{n}\in\left(0,\tan\varepsilon x_{1}\right).

Also, we have that
x\not\in R_{\varepsilon}
and therefore
either
x\not\in J_{-\varepsilon,\varepsilon}
or
x\not\in J^{\sharp}_{\varepsilon}
.
In fact, since
J_{0,\varepsilon}\subseteq J_{-\varepsilon,\varepsilon}
,
we have that necessarily
x\not\in J^{\sharp}_{\varepsilon}
,
and, as a result,
2e_{1}-x\not\in J_{-\varepsilon,\varepsilon}
.
This gives that
|x_{n}|\geqslant\tan\varepsilon\,(2-x_{1})
. Therefore, by (
2.11
),

(2.12)

2-x_{1}\leqslant\frac{|x_{n}|}{\tan\varepsilon}=\frac{x_{n}}{\tan\varepsilon}\leqslant x_{1},

and this entails (
2.10
).

Now we claim that

(2.13)

{\mathcal{B}}_{\varepsilon}\subseteq\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,|x_{1}-1|\leqslant 2\varepsilon\,|\log\varepsilon|,\,|x_{n}-\tan\varepsilon|\leqslant 2\varepsilon^{2}\,|\log\varepsilon|\right\}.

To check this, let
x\in{\mathcal{B}}_{\varepsilon}
. Then,

(2.14)

\frac{x_{1}-1}{|\log\varepsilon|}\leqslant x_{n}\leqslant\tan\varepsilon\,x_{1}=\tan\varepsilon\,(x_{1}-1)+\tan\varepsilon.

Recalling (
2.10
), we thus find that

\left(\frac{1}{|\log\varepsilon|}-\tan\varepsilon\right)|x_{1}-1|=\left(\frac{1}{|\log\varepsilon|}-\tan\varepsilon\right)(x_{1}-1)\leqslant\tan\varepsilon.

Consequently, if
\varepsilon
is conveniently small,

(2.15)

\frac{9}{10}\,|x_{1}-1|\leqslant\left(1-\tan\varepsilon\,|\log\varepsilon|\right)|x_{1}-1|\leqslant\tan\varepsilon\,|\log\varepsilon|\leqslant\frac{11}{10}\,\varepsilon\,|\log\varepsilon|.

Furthermore, by the second inequality in (
2.14
)
and (
2.15
),

(2.16)

x_{n}-\tan\varepsilon\leqslant\tan\varepsilon\,(x_{1}-1)\leqslant\tan\varepsilon\,|x_{1}-1|\leqslant\frac{11}{9}\,\varepsilon\,\tan\varepsilon\,|\log\varepsilon|\leqslant 2\varepsilon^{2}\,|\log\varepsilon|.

Moreover, from (
2.12
),

x_{n}\geqslant\tan\varepsilon\,(2-x_{1}),

whence, utilizing again (
2.15
),

\tan\varepsilon-x_{n}\leqslant\tan\varepsilon+\tan\varepsilon\,(x_{1}-2)\leqslant\tan\varepsilon\,|x_{1}-1|\leqslant\frac{11}{9}\,\varepsilon\,\tan\varepsilon\,|\log\varepsilon|\leqslant 2\varepsilon^{2}\,|\log\varepsilon|.

From this, (
2.15
) and (
2.16
)
we obtain (
2.13
), as desired.

Now, using (
2.13
) and the changes of variable
y:=\frac{x-e_{1}}{\tan\varepsilon}

and
z:=\left(\frac{y^{\prime}}{|y_{n}|},y_{n}\right)
,
we see that

(2.17)

\begin{split}\int_{{\mathcal{B}}_{\varepsilon}}\frac{dx}{|x-e_{1}|^{n+s_{1}}}\,&\leqslant\,\int_{{\{|x_{1}-1|\leqslant 2\varepsilon\,|\log\varepsilon|\}}\atop{\{|x_{n}-\tan\varepsilon|\leqslant 2\varepsilon^{2}\,|\log\varepsilon|\}}}\frac{dx}{|x-e_{1}|^{n+s_{1}}}\\
&=\,\frac{1}{(\tan\varepsilon)^{s_{1}}}\int_{{\{|y_{1}|\leqslant 2\varepsilon\,|\log\varepsilon|/\tan\varepsilon\}}\atop{\{|y_{n}-1|\leqslant 2\varepsilon^{2}\,|\log\varepsilon|/\tan\varepsilon\}}}\frac{dy}{|y|^{n+s_{1}}}\\
&\leqslant\,\frac{2}{\varepsilon^{s_{1}}}\int_{\{|y_{n}-1|\leqslant 4\varepsilon\,|\log\varepsilon|\}}\frac{dy}{|y|^{n+s_{1}}}\\
&=\,\frac{2}{\varepsilon^{s_{1}}}\int_{\{|z_{n}-1|\leqslant 4\varepsilon\,|\log\varepsilon|\}}\frac{dz}{|z_{n}|^{1+s_{1}}\big(|z^{\prime}|^{2}+1\big)^{\frac{n+s_{1}}{2}}}\\
&\leqslant\,\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{s_{1}}}\int_{1-4\varepsilon\,|\log\varepsilon|}^{1+4\varepsilon\,|\log\varepsilon|}\frac{dz_{n}}{z_{n}^{1+s_{1}}}\\
&\leqslant\,C\varepsilon^{1-s_{1}}|\log\varepsilon|,\end{split}

up to renaming the positive constant
C

line after line.

We also recall that
|(x^{\prime},-x_{n})-e_{1}|=|x-e_{1}|

and accordingly

(2.18)

\left|\overrightarrow{(x^{\prime},-x_{n})-e_{1}}-\overrightarrow{x-e_{1}}\right|=\frac{\left|((x^{\prime},-x_{n})-e_{1})-(x-e_{1})\right|}{|x-e_{1}|}=\frac{2|x_{n}|}{|x-e_{1}|}.

As a result, recalling (
2.10
) and (
2.11
),
if
x\in J^{\star}_{\varepsilon}\setminus{\mathcal{B}}_{\varepsilon}
then

|x_{n}|=x_{n}\leqslant\frac{x_{1}-1}{|\log\varepsilon|}=\frac{|x_{1}-1|}{|\log\varepsilon|}.

This and (
2.18
) give that

\left|\overrightarrow{(x^{\prime},-x_{n})-e_{1}}-\overrightarrow{x-e_{1}}\right|\leqslant\frac{2}{|\log\varepsilon|}.

Consequently, if we consider the modulus of continuity of
a_{1,\vartheta}
, namely

\sigma(t):=\sup_{{v,w\in\partial B_{1}}\atop{|v-w|\leqslant t}}|a_{1,\vartheta}(v)-a_{1,\vartheta}(w)|,

we deduce that
if
x\in J^{\star}_{\varepsilon}\setminus{\mathcal{B}}_{\varepsilon}
then

\big|a_{1,\vartheta}(\overrightarrow{(x^{\prime},-x_{n})-e_{1}})-a_{1,\vartheta}(\overrightarrow{x-e_{1}})\big|\leqslant\sigma\left(\frac{2}{|\log\varepsilon|}\right)

and thus

(2.19)

\int_{J^{\star}_{\varepsilon}\setminus{\mathcal{B}}_{\varepsilon}}\frac{a_{1,\vartheta}(\overrightarrow{(x^{\prime},-x_{n})-e_{1}})-a_{1,\vartheta}(\overrightarrow{x-e_{1}})}{|x-e_{1}|^{n+s_{1}}}\,dx\leqslant\sigma\left(\frac{2}{|\log\varepsilon|}\right)\int_{J^{\star}_{\varepsilon}\setminus{\mathcal{B}}_{\varepsilon}}\frac{dx}{|x-e_{1}|^{n+s_{1}}}.

Notice also that

(2.20)

J^{\star}_{\varepsilon}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{\varepsilon/100}(e_{1}).

Indeed, if
x\in J^{\star}_{\varepsilon}
, from (
2.12
) we have that

(2.21)

|x_{n}|=x_{n}\geqslant\tan\varepsilon(2-x_{1}).

Now, if
x_{1}\geqslant\frac{19}{10}
, then
|x-e_{1}|\geqslant|x_{1}-1|\geqslant\frac{9}{10}

and (
2.20
) plainly follows; if instead
x_{1}<\frac{19}{10}
we deduce from (
2.21
) that

|x-e_{1}|\geqslant|x_{n}|\geqslant\frac{\tan\varepsilon}{10},

from which (
2.20
) follows in this case too.

By combining (
2.19
)
and (
2.20
) we deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{J^{\star}_{\varepsilon}\setminus{\mathcal{B}}_{\varepsilon}}\frac{a_{1,\vartheta}(\overrightarrow{(x^{\prime},-x_{n})-e_{1}})-a_{1,\vartheta}(\overrightarrow{x-e_{1}})}{|x-e_{1}|^{n+s_{1}}}\,dx

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\sigma\left(\frac{2}{|\log\varepsilon|}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{\varepsilon/100}}\frac{dy}{|y|^{n+s_{1}}}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{s_{1}}}\,\sigma\left(\frac{2}{|\log\varepsilon|}\right),

which together with (
2.17
) leads to

\int_{J^{\star}_{\varepsilon}}\frac{a_{1,\vartheta}(\overrightarrow{(x^{\prime},-x_{n})-e_{1}})-a_{1,\vartheta}(\overrightarrow{x-e_{1}})}{|x-e_{1}|^{n+s_{1}}}\,dx\leqslant\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{s_{1}}}\,\sigma\left(\frac{2}{|\log\varepsilon|}\right)+C\varepsilon^{1-s_{1}}|\log\varepsilon|.

Joining this information with (
2.9
) we find that

\displaystyle\Xi

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\left[\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{s_{1}}}\,\sigma\left(\frac{2}{|\log\varepsilon|}\right)+C\varepsilon^{1-s_{1}}|\log\varepsilon|-\frac{1}{C\varepsilon^{s_{1}}}+C\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{s_{1}}}\,\left[C\sigma\left(\frac{2}{|\log\varepsilon|}\right)+C\varepsilon\,|\log\varepsilon|-\frac{1}{C}+C\varepsilon^{s_{1}}\right]

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\left(-\frac{1}{2C\varepsilon^{s_{1}}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\infty

This completes the proof of (
2.3
).

Now, using (
2.3
),

\displaystyle\lim_{\bar{\vartheta}\searrow 0}\mathcal{D}_{\vartheta}(\bar{\vartheta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{\bar{\vartheta}\searrow 0}\left(\int_{J_{\vartheta,\vartheta+\bar{\vartheta}}}\frac{a_{1}(\overrightarrow{x-e(\vartheta)})}{|x-e(\vartheta)|^{n+s_{1}}}\,dx-\int_{J_{\vartheta-2\bar{\vartheta},\vartheta}}\frac{a_{1}(\overrightarrow{x-e(\vartheta)})}{|x-e(\vartheta)|^{n+s_{1}}}\,dx-\int_{J_{0,\vartheta-2\bar{\vartheta}}}\frac{a_{1}(\overrightarrow{x-e(\vartheta)})}{|x-e(\vartheta)|^{n+s_{1}}}\,dx\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\lim_{\bar{\vartheta}\searrow 0}\left(\int_{J_{\vartheta,\vartheta+\bar{\vartheta}}}\frac{a_{1}(\overrightarrow{x-e(\vartheta)})}{|x-e(\vartheta)|^{n+s_{1}}}\,dx-\int_{J_{\vartheta-2\bar{\vartheta},\vartheta}}\frac{a_{1}(\overrightarrow{x-e(\vartheta)})}{|x-e(\vartheta)|^{n+s_{1}}}\,dx\right)=-\infty,

which proves (
1.18
), and

\displaystyle\lim_{\bar{\vartheta}\nearrow 2\pi}\mathcal{D}_{\vartheta}(\bar{\vartheta})=\lim_{\alpha\searrow 0}\mathcal{D}_{\vartheta}(2\pi-\alpha)=\lim_{\alpha\searrow 0}\left(\int_{J_{\vartheta,\vartheta+2\pi-\alpha}}\frac{a_{1}(\overrightarrow{x-e(\vartheta)})}{|x-e(\vartheta)|^{n+s_{1}}}\,dx-\int_{J_{0,\vartheta}}\frac{a_{1}(\overrightarrow{x-e(\vartheta)})}{|x-e(\vartheta)|^{n+s_{1}}}\,dx\right)

\displaystyle\qquad=\lim_{\alpha\searrow 0}\left(\int_{J_{\vartheta,2\pi}}\frac{a_{1}(\overrightarrow{x-e(\vartheta)})}{|x-e(\vartheta)|^{n+s_{1}}}\,dx-\int_{J_{\vartheta-\alpha,\vartheta}}\frac{a_{1}(\overrightarrow{x-e(\vartheta)})}{|x-e(\vartheta)|^{n+s_{1}}}\,dx\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\geqslant\lim_{\alpha\searrow 0}\left(\int_{J_{\vartheta,\vartheta+2\alpha}}\frac{a_{1}(\overrightarrow{x-e(\vartheta)})}{|x-e(\vartheta)|^{n+s_{1}}}\,dx-\int_{J_{\vartheta-\alpha,\vartheta}}\frac{a_{1}(\overrightarrow{x-e(\vartheta)})}{|x-e(\vartheta)|^{n+s_{1}}}\,dx\right)=+\infty,

which proves (
1.19
).
∎

3.
Nonlocal Young’s law and proofs of Theorems
1.4
and
1.6
, of Corollary
1.5
and of Proposition
1.9
,

In order to prove Theorems
1.4

and
1.6
,
Corollary
1.5
and Proposition
1.9
, we first
recall an ancillary result on the continuity of the nonlocal
K
-mean curvature defined
in (
1.7
)
(for the usual fractional mean curvature, that is when the kernel
K
is as in (
1.1
),
similar continuity results were presented in
[
13
,
3
]
).

From now on,
we denote points
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
as
x=(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}
and we set

\begin{split}\mathbf{C}&:=\{x=(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x^{\prime}|<1,|x_{n}|<1\}\\
{\mbox{and }}\quad\mathbf{D}&:=\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}:|z|<1\}.\end{split}

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\lambda\geqslant 1
,
s\in(0,1)
and
\alpha\in(s,1)
.
Let
\{F_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of Borel sets in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
0\in\partial F_{k}
and

\text{$F_{k}\rightarrow F$ in $L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})$ for some $F\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}$}.

and
u_{k}
,
u\in C^{1,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})
be such that

\mathbf{C}\cap F_{k}=\{x\in\mathbf{C}:x_{n}\leqslant u_{k}(x^{\prime})\}

and

\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\|u_{k}-u\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(\mathbf{D})}=0.

Let
K_{k}
,
K\in\mathbf{K}(n,s,\lambda,0)
be
such that
K_{k}\rightarrow K
pointwise in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
as
k\rightarrow+\infty
.

Then

\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\mathbf{H}^{K_{k}}_{\partial F_{k}}(0)=\mathbf{H}^{K}_{\partial F}(0).

For the proof of Lemma
3.1
here, see Lemma 4.1 in
[
17
]
.

We will also need a technical lemma to distinguish between the nondegenerate case
\vartheta\in(0,\pi)

and the
particular cases in which
\vartheta\in\{0,\pi\}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
K_{1}\in\mathbf{K}^{2}(n,s_{1},\lambda,\varrho)
be such that
it admits a
blow-up limit
K_{1}^{\ast}

(according to (
1.4
)).
Let
\Omega
be an open bounded set with
C^{1}
-boundary and
E
be a volume-constrained critical set of
\mathcal{C}
.

Let
x_{0}\in\mathrm{Reg}_{E}\cap\partial\Omega
,
x_{k}\in\mathrm{Reg}_{E}\cap\Omega

such that
x_{k}\to x_{0}
as
k\to+\infty

and
r_{k}>0
such that
r_{k}\to 0
as
k\to+\infty
.

Suppose that
H
and
V
are open
half-spaces such that

(3.1)

\Omega^{x_{0},r_{k}}\rightarrow H\qquad{\mbox{and}}\qquad E^{x_{0},r_{k}}\rightarrow H\cap V\quad{\mbox{in }}L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\;{\mbox{as }}k\to+\infty.

Set
v_{k}:=\frac{x_{k}-x_{0}}{r_{k}}
and suppose that there exists
v\in H\cap\partial V
such
that
v_{k}\to v
as
k\to+\infty
.

Let
\vartheta\in[0,\pi]
be the angle between
the half-spaces
H
and
V
, that is
H\cap V=J_{0,\vartheta}
in the notation of (
1.11
).

Then,

i)

if
\vartheta=0
then

\lim_{k\to+\infty}r_{k}^{s_{1}}\left[\mathbf{H}^{K_{1}}_{\partial E}(x_{k})-\int_{\Omega^{c}}K_{1}(x_{k}-y)\,dy\right]=+\infty;

ii)

if
\vartheta=\pi
then

\lim_{k\to+\infty}r_{k}^{s_{1}}\left[\mathbf{H}^{K_{1}}_{\partial E}(x_{k})-\int_{\Omega^{c}}K_{1}(x_{k}-y)\,dy\right]=-\infty;

iii)

if
\vartheta\in(0,\pi)
then

\lim_{k\to+\infty}r_{k}^{s_{1}}\left[\mathbf{H}^{K_{1}}_{\partial E}(x_{k})-\int_{\Omega^{c}}K_{1}(x_{k}-y)\,dy\right]=\mathbf{H}^{K_{1}^{\ast}}_{\partial(H\cap V)}(v)-\int_{H^{c}}K_{1}^{\ast}(v-y)\,dy\in\mathbb{R}.

Proof.

We start by proving i). For this, we notice that

\displaystyle\Xi_{k}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle r_{k}^{s_{1}}\left[\mathbf{H}^{K_{1}}_{\partial E}(x_{k})-\int_{\Omega^{c}}K_{1}(x_{k}-y)\,dy\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r_{k}^{s_{1}}\left[\int_{E^{c}\cap\Omega}K_{1}(x_{k}-y)\,dy-\int_{E}K_{1}(x_{k}-y)\,dy\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r_{k}^{n+s_{1}}\left[\int_{(E^{x_{0},r_{k}})^{c}\cap\Omega^{x_{0},r_{k}}}K_{1}(x_{k}-x_{0}-r_{k}z)\,dz-\int_{E^{x_{0},r_{k}}}K_{1}(x_{k}-x_{0}-r_{k}z)\,dz\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r_{k}^{n+s_{1}}\left[\int_{(E^{x_{0},r_{k}})^{c}\cap\Omega^{x_{0},r_{k}}}K_{1}\big(r_{k}(v_{k}-z)\big)\,dz-\int_{E^{x_{0},r_{k}}}K_{1}\big(r_{k}(v_{k}-z)\big)\,dz\right],

where the change of variable
z=\frac{y-x_{0}}{r_{k}}
has been used.

Now we point out that

r_{k}^{n+s_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{1/2}(v_{k})}K_{1}\big(r_{k}(v_{k}-z)\big)\,dz\leqslant\lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{1/2}(v_{k})}\frac{dz}{|v_{k}-z|^{n+s_{1}}}\leqslant C,

thanks to (
1.2
), for some positive constant
C
, depending only on
n
,
s_{1}
and
\lambda
.

From these observations we conclude that

(3.2)

\begin{split}\Xi_{k}\geqslant\,&r_{k}^{n+s_{1}}\left[\int_{(E^{x_{0},r_{k}})^{c}\cap\Omega^{x_{0},r_{k}}\cap B_{1/2}(v_{k})}K_{1}\big(r_{k}(v_{k}-z)\big)\,dz\right.\\
&\qquad\qquad\left.-\int_{E^{x_{0},r_{k}}\cap B_{1/2}(v_{k})}K_{1}\big(r_{k}(v_{k}-z)\big)\,dz\right]-C.\end{split}

Now we notice that
E^{x_{0},r_{k}}\cap B_{1/2}(v_{k})
can be written as a portion of space
included between the graphs of the functions describing
\partial\Omega^{x_{0},r_{k}}

and
\partial E^{x_{0},r_{k}}
, that we denote respectively by
\psi_{k}
and
u_{k}
. More precisely,
recalling that
x_{0}\in\mathrm{Reg}_{E}\cap\partial\Omega
, in the
vicinity of
x_{0}
we can describe
\partial\Omega

and
\partial E
by the graphs of two functions
\psi
and
u
, respectively, with
\psi

of class
C^{1}
and
u
of class
C^{1,\alpha}
with
\alpha\in(s_{1},1)
, and
\psi(x_{0}^{\prime})=u(x_{0}^{\prime})=x_{0,n}
.
Up to a rotation, we also assume that
\nabla\psi(x_{0}^{\prime})=0
.
In this way,

(3.3)

\psi_{k}(x^{\prime})=\frac{\psi(x_{0}^{\prime}+r_{k}x^{\prime})-x_{0,n}}{r_{k}}\qquad{\mbox{ and }}\qquad u_{k}(x^{\prime})=\frac{u(x_{0}^{\prime}+r_{k}x^{\prime})-x_{0,n}}{r_{k}}.

Moreover,

\displaystyle E^{x_{0},r_{k}}\cap B_{1/2}(v_{k})=\Big\{x\in B_{1/2}(v_{k})\,:\,x_{n}\in\big(\psi_{k}(x^{\prime}),u_{k}(x^{\prime})\big)\Big\}

and notice that, since
E\subseteq\Omega
, it follows that
\psi\leqslant u
and so
\psi_{k}\leqslant u_{k}
. As a result,

\big\{x\in B_{1/2}(v_{k})\,:\,x_{n}>u_{k}(x^{\prime})\big\}\subseteq(E^{x_{0},r_{k}})^{c}\cap\Omega^{x_{0},r_{k}}\cap B_{1/2}(v_{k}).

Hence, from (
3.2
) we obtain that

(3.4)

\begin{split}\Xi_{k}\geqslant\,&r_{k}^{n+s_{1}}\left[\int_{B_{1/2}(v_{k})\cap\{x_{n}>u_{k}(x^{\prime})\}}K_{1}\big(r_{k}(v_{k}-z)\big)\,dz\right.\\
&\qquad\qquad\left.-\int_{B_{1/2}(v_{k})\cap\{x_{n}\in(\psi_{k}(x^{\prime}),u_{k}(x^{\prime}))\}}K_{1}\big(r_{k}(v_{k}-z)\big)\,dz\right]-C.\end{split}

We now define

\widetilde{u}_{k}(x^{\prime}):=u_{k}(v^{\prime}_{k})+\nabla u_{k}(v_{k}^{\prime})\cdot(x^{\prime}-v_{k}^{\prime})

and we point out that, if
|x^{\prime}-v_{k}^{\prime}|\leqslant 3
,

\displaystyle|u_{k}(x^{\prime})-\widetilde{u}_{k}(x^{\prime})|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\frac{u(x_{0}^{\prime}+r_{k}x^{\prime})-u(x_{0}^{\prime}+r_{k}v_{k}^{\prime})}{r_{k}}-\nabla u(x_{0}^{\prime}+r_{k}v_{k}^{\prime})\cdot(x^{\prime}-v_{k}^{\prime})\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\frac{u\big(x_{k}^{\prime}+r_{k}(x^{\prime}-v_{k}^{\prime})\big)-u(x_{k}^{\prime})}{r_{k}}-\nabla u(x_{k}^{\prime})\cdot(x^{\prime}-v_{k}^{\prime})\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{1}\nabla u\big(x_{k}^{\prime}+tr_{k}(x^{\prime}-v_{k}^{\prime})\big)\cdot(x^{\prime}-v_{k}^{\prime})\,dt-\nabla u(x_{k}^{\prime})\cdot(x^{\prime}-v_{k}^{\prime})\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\|u\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(B^{\prime}_{\rho}(x^{\prime}_{0}))}\,r_{k}^{\alpha}\,|x^{\prime}-v_{k}^{\prime}|^{1+\alpha},

for a suitable
\rho>0
. As a consequence,

\displaystyle r_{k}^{n+s_{1}}\int_{(\{x_{n}>u_{k}(x^{\prime})\}\Delta\{x_{n}>\widetilde{u}_{k}(x^{\prime})\})\cap B_{1/2}(v_{k})}K_{1}\big(r_{k}(v_{k}-z)\big)\,dz

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leqslant\lambda\int_{(\{x_{n}>u_{k}(x^{\prime})\}\Delta\{x_{n}>\widetilde{u}_{k}(x^{\prime})\})\cap B_{1/2}(v_{k})}\frac{dz}{|v_{k}-z|^{n+s_{1}}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leqslant\lambda\|u\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(B^{\prime}_{\rho}(x^{\prime}_{0}))}\,r_{k}^{\alpha}\int_{B^{\prime}_{1/2}(v^{\prime}_{k})}\frac{|v_{k}^{\prime}-z^{\prime}|^{1+\alpha}}{|v_{k}^{\prime}-z^{\prime}|^{n+s_{1}}}\,dz^{\prime}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leqslant C\,r_{k}^{\alpha},

up to renaming
C
, possibly in dependence of
u
as well.

Plugging this information into (
3.4
), and possibly renaming
C
again, we obtain that

(3.5)

\begin{split}\Xi_{k}\geqslant\,&r_{k}^{n+s_{1}}\left[\int_{B_{1/2}(v_{k})\cap\{x_{n}>\widetilde{u}_{k}(x^{\prime})\}}K_{1}\big(r_{k}(v_{k}-z)\big)\,dz\right.\\
&\qquad\qquad\left.-\int_{B_{1/2}(v_{k})\cap\{x_{n}\in(\psi_{k}(x^{\prime}),\widetilde{u}_{k}(x^{\prime}))\}}K_{1}\big(r_{k}(v_{k}-z)\big)\,dz\right]-C.\end{split}

Now, from (
3.3
) we see that
\psi_{k}(x^{\prime})\to\nabla\psi(x^{\prime}_{0})\cdot x^{\prime}

and
u_{k}(x^{\prime})\to\nabla u(x^{\prime}_{0})\cdot x^{\prime}
as
k\to+\infty
.
Hence, if
\vartheta=0
it follows that
\nabla\psi(x^{\prime}_{0})=\nabla u(x^{\prime}_{0})
.
Consequently, if
x^{\prime}\in B^{\prime}_{1/2}(v^{\prime}_{k})
then

(3.6)

\begin{split}&\,|\widetilde{u}_{k}(x^{\prime})-\psi_{k}(x^{\prime})|\\
=\,&\left|u_{k}(v^{\prime}_{k})+\nabla u_{k}(v_{k}^{\prime})\cdot(x^{\prime}-v_{k}^{\prime})-\frac{\psi(x_{0}^{\prime}+r_{k}x^{\prime})-\psi(x_{0}^{\prime})}{r_{k}}\right|\\
=\,&\left|\frac{u(x_{0}^{\prime}+r_{k}v_{k}^{\prime})-u(x_{0}^{\prime})}{r_{k}}+\nabla u(x_{0}^{\prime}+r_{k}v_{k}^{\prime})\cdot(x^{\prime}-v_{k}^{\prime})-\int_{0}^{1}\nabla\psi(x_{0}^{\prime}+tr_{k}x^{\prime})\cdot x^{\prime}\,dt\right|\\
=\,&\left|\int_{0}^{1}\nabla u(x_{0}^{\prime}+tr_{k}v_{k}^{\prime})\cdot v_{k}^{\prime}\,dt+\nabla u(x_{0}^{\prime}+r_{k}v_{k}^{\prime})\cdot(x^{\prime}-v_{k}^{\prime})-\int_{0}^{1}\nabla\psi(x_{0}^{\prime}+tr_{k}x^{\prime})\cdot x^{\prime}\,dt\right|\\
\leqslant\,&\left|\int_{0}^{1}\nabla u(x_{0}^{\prime})\cdot v_{k}^{\prime}\,dt+\nabla u(x_{0}^{\prime})\cdot(x^{\prime}-v_{k}^{\prime})-\int_{0}^{1}\nabla\psi(x_{0}^{\prime})\cdot x^{\prime}\,dt\right|+\delta_{k}\\
=\,&\delta_{k},\end{split}

for a suitable
\delta_{k}
such that
\delta_{k}\to 0
as
k\to+\infty
.

This and (
3.5
) give that

(3.7)

\begin{split}\Xi_{k}\geqslant\,&r_{k}^{n+s_{1}}\left[\int_{B_{1/2}(v_{k})\cap\{x_{n}>\widetilde{u}_{k}(x^{\prime})\}}K_{1}\big(r_{k}(v_{k}-z)\big)\,dz\right.\\
&\qquad\qquad\left.-\int_{B_{1/2}(v_{k})\cap\{x_{n}\in(\widetilde{u}_{k}(x^{\prime})-\delta_{k},\widetilde{u}_{k}(x^{\prime}))\}}K_{1}\big(r_{k}(v_{k}-z)\big)\,dz\right]-C.\end{split}

Now we define the map
Y(z):=2v_{k}-z
and we show that

(3.8)

Y\Big(B_{1/2}(v_{k})\cap\{x_{n}\in(\widetilde{u}_{k}(x^{\prime})-\delta_{k},\widetilde{u}_{k}(x^{\prime}))\}\Big)\subseteq B_{1/2}(v_{k})\cap\{x_{n}\in(\widetilde{u}_{k}(x^{\prime}),\widetilde{u}_{k}(x^{\prime})+\delta_{k})\}.

Indeed, let
z\in B_{1/2}(v_{k})\cap\{x_{n}\in(\widetilde{u}_{k}(x^{\prime})-\delta_{k},\widetilde{u}_{k}(x^{\prime}))\}

and call
y:=Y(z)
. We have that
|y-v_{k}|=|v_{k}-z|<1/2
. Moreover,

\displaystyle y_{n}-\widetilde{u}_{k}(y^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2v_{k,n}-z_{n}-\widetilde{u}_{k}\big(2v_{k}^{\prime}-z^{\prime}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2u_{k}(v_{k}^{\prime})-z_{n}-\widetilde{u}_{k}\big(2v_{k}^{\prime}-z^{\prime}\big)

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle\Big(2u_{k}(v_{k}^{\prime})-\widetilde{u}_{k}(z^{\prime})-\widetilde{u}_{k}\big(2v_{k}^{\prime}-z^{\prime}\big),\,2u_{k}(v_{k}^{\prime})-\widetilde{u}_{k}(z^{\prime})-\widetilde{u}_{k}\big(2v_{k}^{\prime}-z^{\prime}\big)+\delta_{k}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(2u_{k}(v_{k}^{\prime})-2\widetilde{u}_{k}(v_{k}^{\prime}),\,2u_{k}(v_{k}^{\prime})-2\widetilde{u}_{k}(v_{k}^{\prime})+\delta_{k}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(0,\,\delta_{k}\big)

and the proof of (
3.8
) is thus complete.

Using (
3.8
) and changing variable
y=Y(z)
we see that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1/2}(v_{k})\cap\{x_{n}\in(\widetilde{u}_{k}(x^{\prime})-\delta_{k},\widetilde{u}_{k}(x^{\prime}))\}}K_{1}\big(r_{k}(v_{k}-z)\big)\,dz

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1/2}(v_{k})\cap\{x_{n}\in(\widetilde{u}_{k}(x^{\prime}),\widetilde{u}_{k}(x^{\prime})+\delta_{k})\}}K_{1}\big(r_{k}(y-v_{k})\big)\,dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1/2}(v_{k})\cap\{x_{n}\in(\widetilde{u}_{k}(x^{\prime}),\widetilde{u}_{k}(x^{\prime})+\delta_{k})\}}K_{1}\big(r_{k}(v_{k}-y)\big)\,dy.

Combining this and (
3.7
), and recalling (
1.2
),
we arrive at

(3.9)

\begin{split}\Xi_{k}\geqslant\,&r_{k}^{n+s_{1}}\int_{B_{1/2}(v_{k})\cap\{x_{n}>\widetilde{u}_{k}(x^{\prime})+\delta_{k}\}}K_{1}\big(r_{k}(v_{k}-z)\big)\,dz-C\\
\geqslant\,&\frac{1}{\lambda}\int_{B_{1/2}(v_{k})\cap\{x_{n}>\widetilde{u}_{k}(x^{\prime})+\delta_{k}\}}\frac{dz}{|v_{k}-z|^{n+s_{1}}}\,dz-C.\end{split}

Now we define

(3.10)

\nu_{k}:=\frac{(-\nabla u_{k}(v^{\prime}_{k}),1)}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla u_{k}(v^{\prime}_{k})|^{2}}}\qquad{\mbox{and}}\qquad\zeta_{k}:=v_{k}+3\delta_{k}\nu_{k}

and we
claim that, if
k
is sufficiently large,

(3.11)

\begin{split}B_{\delta_{k}}(\zeta_{k})\subseteq B_{1/2}(v_{k})\cap\{x_{n}>\widetilde{u}_{k}(x^{\prime})+\delta_{k}\}.\end{split}

To check this, we observe that

\lim_{k\to+\infty}|\nabla u_{k}(v_{k}^{\prime})|=\lim_{k\to+\infty}|\nabla u(x_{k}^{\prime})|=|\nabla u(x_{0}^{\prime})|=|\nabla\psi(x_{0}^{\prime})|=0

and consequently

(3.12)

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\frac{3}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla u_{k}(v^{\prime}_{k})|^{2}}}-4|\nabla u_{k}(v_{k}^{\prime})|-2=1.

Now, pick
w\in B_{\delta_{k}}(\zeta_{k})
. We have that

|w-v_{k}|\leqslant|w-\zeta_{k}|+|\zeta_{k}-v_{k}|<\delta_{k}+3\delta_{k}=4\delta_{k}

and thus
w\in B_{1/2}(v_{k})
as long as
k
is large enough.

Moreover,

\displaystyle w_{n}-\widetilde{u}_{k}(w^{\prime})-\delta_{k}

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle(\zeta_{k,n}-\delta_{k})-u_{k}(v_{k}^{\prime})-\nabla u_{k}(v_{k}^{\prime})(w^{\prime}-v_{k}^{\prime})-\delta_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(v_{k,n}+\frac{3\delta_{k}}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla u_{k}(v^{\prime}_{k})|^{2}}}-\delta_{k}\right)-v_{k,n}-\nabla u_{k}(v_{k}^{\prime})(w^{\prime}-v_{k}^{\prime})-\delta_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{3\delta_{k}}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla u_{k}(v^{\prime}_{k})|^{2}}}-\nabla u_{k}(v_{k}^{\prime})(w^{\prime}-v_{k}^{\prime})-2\delta_{k}

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\frac{3\delta_{k}}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla u_{k}(v^{\prime}_{k})|^{2}}}-|\nabla u_{k}(v_{k}^{\prime})|\,|w^{\prime}-v_{k}^{\prime}|-2\delta_{k}

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\left(\frac{3}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla u_{k}(v^{\prime}_{k})|^{2}}}-4|\nabla u_{k}(v_{k}^{\prime})|-2\right)\delta_{k}

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle 0,

thanks to (
3.12
).

The proof of (
3.11
) is thereby complete.

Thus, exploiting (
3.9
) and (
3.11
), we find that

\begin{split}\Xi_{k}\geqslant\int_{B_{\delta_{k}}(\zeta_{k})}\frac{dz}{|v_{k}-z|^{n+s_{1}}}\,dz-C.\end{split}

Notice also that if
z\in B_{\delta_{k}}(\zeta_{k})
then
|v_{k}-z|\leqslant|v_{k}-\zeta_{k}|+|\zeta_{k}-z|\leqslant 3\delta_{k}+\delta_{k}=4\delta_{k}
and accordingly

\begin{split}\Xi_{k}\geqslant\int_{B_{\delta_{k}}(\zeta_{k})}\frac{dz}{(4\delta_{k})^{n+s_{1}}}\,dz-C=\frac{c}{\delta_{k}^{s_{1}}}-C,\end{split}

for some
c>0
. This establishes the claim in i), as desired.

The claim in ii) can be proved similarly.

As for the claim in iii), we suppose that
\vartheta\in(0,\pi)

and, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote by
F_{k}
the set obtained
by a suitable rigid motion of the set
E^{x_{0},r_{k}}-v_{k}
so as to have that
0\in\partial F_{k}
and

(3.13)

\mathbf{C}\cap F_{k}=\left\{x\in\mathbf{C}:x_{n}\leqslant u_{k}(x^{\prime})\right\},

for some
u_{k}\in C^{1,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})
.
Let also
u
be the linear function such that
u_{k}\rightarrow u
in
C^{1,\alpha}(\mathbf{D})

as
k\to+\infty
.
We notice that, by (
3.1
), up to a rigid motion,

(3.14)

F_{k}\rightarrow F:=H\cap V-v\quad\text{in $L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})$ as~$k\to+\infty$}.

Furthermore, recalling the definition of
mean curvature in (
1.7
) and exploiting the change of variable
y=x_{0}+r_{k}z
,
we see that

(3.15)

\begin{split}\mathbf{H}^{K_{1}}_{\partial E}(x_{k})=\,&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}K_{1}(x_{k}-y)\bigl(\chi_{E^{c}}(y)-\chi_{E}(y)\bigr)\,dy\\
=\,&r_{k}^{-s_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}r_{k}^{n+s_{1}}K_{1}(x_{k}-x_{0}-r_{k}z)\left(\chi_{(E^{x_{0},r_{k}})^{c}}(z)-\chi_{E^{x_{0},r_{k}}}(z)\right)\,dz.\end{split}

We also introduce, for every
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
, the kernel

K_{1,k}(\zeta):=r_{k}^{n+s_{1}}K_{1}(r_{k}\zeta),

and we observe that, in light of (
3.15
),

(3.16)

\mathbf{H}^{K_{1}}_{\partial E}(x_{k})=r_{k}^{-s_{1}}\mathbf{H}^{K_{1,k}}_{\partial F_{k}}(0).

Furthermore, we recall that
K_{1,k}\rightarrow K^{\ast}_{1}
pointwise in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
, hence one can infer from (
3.13
), (
3.14
), (
3.16
)
and Lemma
3.1
that

(3.17)

\lim_{k\to+\infty}r_{k}^{s_{1}}\mathbf{H}^{K_{1}}_{\partial E}(x_{k})=\mathbf{H}^{K_{1}^{\ast}}_{\partial(H\cap V)}(v).

Moreover, since
\vartheta\in(0,\pi)
,
one can use the Lebesgue’s Dominated Convergence Theorem and find that

\begin{split}\lim_{k\to+\infty}r_{k}^{s_{1}}\int_{\Omega^{c}}K_{1}(x_{k}-y)\,dy=\,&\lim_{k\to+\infty}\int_{(\Omega^{x_{0},r_{k}})^{c}}r_{k}^{n+s_{1}}K_{1}(r_{k}(v_{k}-y))\,dy\\
=\,&\int_{H^{c}}K_{1}^{\ast}(v-y)\,dy.\end{split}

From this and (
3.17
) we obtain the desired result in iii).
∎

Now we showcase a refinement of Lemma
3.2

which will be needed to exclude the degenerate blow-up limits
\vartheta\in\{0,\pi\}

in the case
s_{1}>s_{2}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
s_{1}>s_{2}
,
K_{1}\in\mathbf{K}^{2}(n,s_{1},\lambda,\varrho)
and
K_{2}\in\mathbf{K}^{2}(n,s_{2},\lambda,\varrho)
.
Let
\Omega
be an open bounded set with
C^{1}
-boundary and
E
be a volume-constrained critical set of
\mathcal{C}
.

Let
x_{0}\in\mathrm{Reg}_{E}\cap\partial\Omega
,

x_{k}\in\mathrm{Reg}_{E}\cap\Omega

such that
x_{k}\to x_{0}
as
k\to+\infty

and
r_{k}>0
such that
r_{k}\to 0
as
k\to+\infty
.

Suppose that
H
and
V
are open
half-spaces such that

\Omega^{x_{0},r_{k}}\rightarrow H\qquad{\mbox{and}}\qquad E^{x_{0},r_{k}}\rightarrow H\cap V\quad{\mbox{in }}L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\;{\mbox{as }}k\to+\infty.

Let
\vartheta\in[0,\pi]
be the angle between
the half-spaces
H
and
V
, that is
H\cap V=J_{0,\vartheta}
in the notation of (
1.11
).

Then,

i)

if
\vartheta=0
then

\lim_{k\to+\infty}r_{k}^{s_{1}}\left[\mathbf{H}^{K_{1}}_{\partial E}(x_{k})-\int_{\Omega^{c}}K_{1}(x_{k}-y)\,dy\right]+\sigma\,r_{k}^{s_{1}-s_{2}}\,r_{k}^{s_{2}}\int_{\Omega^{c}}K_{2}(x_{k}-y)\,dy=+\infty;

ii)

if
\vartheta=\pi
then

\lim_{k\to+\infty}r_{k}^{s_{1}}\left[\mathbf{H}^{K_{1}}_{\partial E}(x_{k})-\int_{\Omega^{c}}K_{1}(x_{k}-y)\,dy\right]+\sigma\,r_{k}^{s_{1}-s_{2}}\,r_{k}^{s_{2}}\int_{\Omega^{c}}K_{2}(x_{k}-y)\,dy=-\infty.

Proof.

We focus on the proof of i), since the proof of ii) is similar, up to sign changes.
To this end, we exploit
the notation introduced in Lemma
3.2
, and specifically (
3.5
),
and we set
v_{k}:=\frac{x_{k}-x_{0}}{r_{k}}
,
to see that

(3.18)

\begin{split}\Upsilon_{k}:=\,&r_{k}^{s_{1}}\left[\mathbf{H}^{K_{1}}_{\partial E}(x_{k})-\int_{\Omega^{c}}K_{1}(x_{k}-y)\,dy\right]+\sigma\,r_{k}^{s_{1}-s_{2}}\,r_{k}^{s_{2}}\int_{\Omega^{c}}K_{2}(x_{k}-y)\,dy\\
\geqslant\,&\Xi_{k}-|\sigma|\,r_{k}^{s_{1}-s_{2}}\,r_{k}^{s_{2}}\int_{\Omega^{c}}K_{2}(x_{k}-y)\,dy\\
\geqslant\,&r_{k}^{n+s_{1}}\left[\int_{B_{1/2}(v_{k})\cap\{x_{n}>\widetilde{u}_{k}(x^{\prime})\}}K_{1}\big(r_{k}(v_{k}-z)\big)\,dz\right.\\
&\qquad\qquad\left.-\int_{B_{1/2}(v_{k})\cap\{x_{n}\in(\psi_{k}(x^{\prime}),\widetilde{u}_{k}(x^{\prime}))\}}K_{1}\big(r_{k}(v_{k}-z)\big)\,dz\right]\\
&\qquad\qquad-|\sigma|\,r_{k}^{s_{1}-s_{2}}\,r_{k}^{n+s_{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega^{x_{0},r_{k}}}K_{2}\big(r_{k}(v_{k}-z)\big)\,dz-C\\
\geqslant\,&r_{k}^{n+s_{1}}\left[\int_{B_{1/2}(v_{k})\cap\{x_{n}>\widetilde{u}_{k}(x^{\prime})\}}K_{1}\big(r_{k}(v_{k}-z)\big)\,dz\right.\\
&\qquad\qquad\left.-\int_{B_{1/2}(v_{k})\cap\{x_{n}\in(\psi_{k}(x^{\prime}),\widetilde{u}_{k}(x^{\prime}))\}}K_{1}\big(r_{k}(v_{k}-z)\big)\,dz\right]\\
&\qquad\qquad-|\sigma|\,r_{k}^{s_{1}-s_{2}}\,r_{k}^{n+s_{2}}\int_{B_{1/2}(v_{k})\cap\{x_{n}<\psi_{k}(x^{\prime})\}}K_{2}\big(r_{k}(v_{k}-z)\big)\,dz-C,\end{split}

up to changing
C>0
from line to line.

Also, by (
3.6
),

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1/2}(v_{k})\cap\{x_{n}<\psi_{k}(x^{\prime})\}}K_{2}\big(r_{k}(v_{k}-z)\big)\,dz

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1/2}(v_{k})\cap\{x_{n}\in(\widetilde{u}_{k}(x^{\prime})-\delta_{k},\psi_{k}(x^{\prime}))\}}K_{2}\big(r_{k}(v_{k}-z)\big)\,dz+\int_{B_{1/2}(v_{k})\cap\{x_{n}<\widetilde{u}_{k}(x^{\prime})-\delta_{k}\}}K_{2}\big(r_{k}(v_{k}-z)\big)\,dz.

Therefore, we can write (
3.18
) as

(3.19)

\begin{split}\Upsilon_{k}\geqslant\,&r_{k}^{n+s_{1}}\left[\int_{B_{1/2}(v_{k})\cap\{x_{n}>\widetilde{u}_{k}(x^{\prime})\}}K_{1}\big(r_{k}(v_{k}-z)\big)\,dz\right.\\
&\qquad\qquad\left.-\int_{B_{1/2}(v_{k})\cap\{x_{n}\in(\psi_{k}(x^{\prime}),\widetilde{u}_{k}(x^{\prime}))\}}K_{1}\big(r_{k}(v_{k}-z)\big)\,dz\right]\\
&\qquad\qquad-|\sigma|\,r_{k}^{s_{1}-s_{2}}\,r_{k}^{n+s_{2}}\int_{B_{1/2}(v_{k})\cap\{x_{n}\in(\widetilde{u}_{k}(x^{\prime})-\delta_{k},\psi_{k}(x^{\prime}))\}}K_{2}\big(r_{k}(v_{k}-z)\big)\,dz\\
&\qquad\qquad-|\sigma|\,r_{k}^{s_{1}-s_{2}}\,r_{k}^{n+s_{2}}\int_{B_{1/2}(v_{k})\cap\{x_{n}<\widetilde{u}_{k}(x^{\prime})-\delta_{k}\}}K_{2}\big(r_{k}(v_{k}-z)\big)\,dz-C.\end{split}

Now we set

(3.20)

{\mathcal{Z}}_{k}(x):=\max\Big\{r_{k}^{n+s_{1}}\,K_{1}(x),\;\,|\sigma|\,r_{k}^{s_{1}-s_{2}}\,r_{k}^{n+s_{2}}\,K_{2}(x)\Big\}.

In this way, we deduce from (
3.19
) that

(3.21)

\begin{split}\Upsilon_{k}\geqslant\,&r_{k}^{n+s_{1}}\int_{B_{1/2}(v_{k})\cap\{x_{n}>\widetilde{u}_{k}(x^{\prime})\}}K_{1}\big(r_{k}(v_{k}-z)\big)\,dz\\
&\qquad\qquad-\int_{B_{1/2}(v_{k})\cap\{x_{n}\in(\widetilde{u}_{k}(x^{\prime})-\delta_{k},\widetilde{u}_{k}(x^{\prime}))\}}{\mathcal{Z}}_{k}\big(r_{k}(v_{k}-z)\big)\,dz\\
&\qquad\qquad-|\sigma|\,r_{k}^{s_{1}-s_{2}}\,r_{k}^{n+s_{2}}\int_{B_{1/2}(v_{k})\cap\{x_{n}<\widetilde{u}_{k}(x^{\prime})-\delta_{k}\}}K_{2}\big(r_{k}(v_{k}-z)\big)\,dz-C.\end{split}

Let
Y(z):=2v_{k}-z
. We also use the short notation

\displaystyle{\mathcal{P}}_{k}:=B_{1/2}(v_{k})\cap\{x_{n}>\widetilde{u}_{k}(x^{\prime})\},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Q}}_{k}:=B_{1/2}(v_{k})\cap\{x_{n}\in(\widetilde{u}_{k}(x^{\prime})-\delta_{k},\widetilde{u}_{k}(x^{\prime}))\}

and

\displaystyle{\mathcal{R}}_{k}:=B_{1/2}(v_{k})\cap\{x_{n}<\widetilde{u}_{k}(x^{\prime})-\delta_{k}\}.

We know from (
3.8
) that

(3.22)

Y({\mathcal{Q}}_{k})\subseteq B_{1/2}(v_{k})\cap\{x_{n}\in(\widetilde{u}_{k}(x^{\prime}),\widetilde{u}_{k}(x^{\prime})+\delta_{k})\}\subseteq{\mathcal{P}}_{k}.

We also claim that

(3.23)

Y({\mathcal{R}}_{k})\subseteq{\mathcal{P}}_{k}\setminus Y({\mathcal{Q}}_{k}).

Indeed, if there were a point
y\in Y({\mathcal{Q}}_{k})\cap Y({\mathcal{R}}_{k})

we would have that
y=2v_{k}-Q=2v_{k}-R
for some
Q\in{\mathcal{Q}}_{k}
and
R\in{\mathcal{R}}_{k}
, but this would entail that
Q=R\in{\mathcal{Q}}_{k}\cap{\mathcal{R}}_{k}=\varnothing
, which is a contradiction. This shows that
Y({\mathcal{R}}_{k})
lies in the complement of
Y({\mathcal{Q}}_{k})
, thus, to complete the proof of (
3.23
), it only remains to show that
Y({\mathcal{R}}_{k})\subseteq{\mathcal{P}}_{k}
. To this end, we observe that if
z_{n}<\widetilde{u}_{k}(z^{\prime})-\delta_{k}

and
y=Y(z)
, then

\displaystyle y_{n}-\widetilde{u}_{k}(y^{\prime})=2v_{k,n}-z_{n}-\widetilde{u}_{k}(y^{\prime})=2\widetilde{u}_{k}(v^{\prime}_{k})-z_{n}-\widetilde{u}_{k}(2v^{\prime}_{k}-z^{\prime})

\displaystyle\qquad>2\widetilde{u}_{k}(v^{\prime}_{k})-\widetilde{u}_{k}(z^{\prime})+\delta_{k}-\widetilde{u}_{k}(2v^{\prime}_{k}-z^{\prime})=\delta_{k}>0.

This completes the proof of (
3.23
).

Hence, by (
3.21
), (
3.22
) and (
3.23
),

(3.24)

\begin{split}\Upsilon_{k}\geqslant\,&r_{k}^{n+s_{1}}\int_{{\mathcal{P}}_{k}}K_{1}\big(r_{k}(v_{k}-z)\big)\,dz-\int_{{\mathcal{Q}}_{k}}{\mathcal{Z}}_{k}\big(r_{k}(v_{k}-z)\big)\,dz\\
&\qquad\qquad-|\sigma|\,r_{k}^{s_{1}-s_{2}}\,r_{k}^{n+s_{2}}\int_{{\mathcal{R}}_{k}}K_{2}\big(r_{k}(v_{k}-z)\big)\,dz-C\\
=\,&r_{k}^{n+s_{1}}\int_{{\mathcal{P}}_{k}}K_{1}\big(r_{k}(v_{k}-z)\big)\,dz-\int_{Y({\mathcal{Q}}_{k})}{\mathcal{Z}}_{k}\big(r_{k}(v_{k}-y)\big)\,dy\\
&\qquad\qquad-|\sigma|\,r_{k}^{s_{1}-s_{2}}\,r_{k}^{n+s_{2}}\int_{Y({\mathcal{R}}_{k})}K_{2}\big(r_{k}(v_{k}-y)\big)\,dy-C\\
=\,&r_{k}^{n+s_{1}}\int_{{\mathcal{P}}_{k}\setminus(Y({\mathcal{Q}}_{k})\cup Y({\mathcal{R}}_{k}))}K_{1}\big(r_{k}(v_{k}-z)\big)\,dz+\int_{Y({\mathcal{Q}}_{k})}\alpha_{k}(z)\,dz+\int_{Y({\mathcal{R}}_{k})}\beta_{k}(z)\,dz-C,\end{split}

where

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}(z):=r_{k}^{n+s_{1}}\,K_{1}\big(r_{k}(v_{k}-z)\big)-{\mathcal{Z}}_{k}\big(r_{k}(v_{k}-z)\big)

and

\displaystyle\beta_{k}(z):=r_{k}^{n+s_{1}}\,K_{1}\big(r_{k}(v_{k}-z)\big)-|\sigma|\,r_{k}^{s_{1}-s_{2}}\,r_{k}^{n+s_{2}}\,K_{2}\big(r_{k}(v_{k}-z)\big).

We stress that up to now the condition
s_{1}>s_{2}
has not been used.
We are going to exploit it now to bound
\alpha_{k}
and
\beta_{k}
.
For this, we note that, if
z\in B_{1/2}(v_{k})
and
k
is large enough, then

\displaystyle|\sigma|\,r_{k}^{s_{1}-s_{2}}\,r_{k}^{n+s_{2}}\,K_{2}\big(r_{k}(v_{k}-z)\big)\leqslant\frac{\lambda\,|\sigma|\,r_{k}^{s_{1}-s_{2}}}{|v_{k}-z|^{n+s_{2}}}\leqslant\frac{\lambda\,|\sigma|\,r_{k}^{s_{1}-s_{2}}}{|v_{k}-z|^{n+s_{1}}}=\frac{\lambda\,|\sigma|\,r_{k}^{s_{1}-s_{2}}\,r_{k}^{n+s_{1}}}{\big|r_{k}(v_{k}-z)\big|^{n+s_{1}}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leqslant\lambda^{2}\,|\sigma|\,r_{k}^{s_{1}-s_{2}}\,r_{k}^{n+s_{1}}\,K_{1}\big(r_{k}(v_{k}-z)\big)\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\,r_{k}^{n+s_{1}}\,K_{1}\big(r_{k}(v_{k}-z)\big).

This and (
3.20
)
entail that if
z\in B_{1/2}(v_{k})
and
k
is large enough, then
{\mathcal{Z}}_{k}\big(r_{k}(v_{k}-z)\big)=r_{k}^{n+s_{1}}\,K_{1}\big(r_{k}(v_{k}-z)\big)
, and therefore
\alpha_{k}(z)=0
.
In addition,

\beta_{k}(z)\geqslant\frac{1}{2}\,r_{k}^{n+s_{1}}\,K_{1}\big(r_{k}(v_{k}-z)\big).

From these observations and (
3.24
) we arrive at

(3.25)

\begin{split}\Upsilon_{k}&\,\geqslant\,r_{k}^{n+s_{1}}\int_{{\mathcal{P}}_{k}\setminus(Y({\mathcal{Q}}_{k})\cup Y({\mathcal{R}}_{k}))}K_{1}\big(r_{k}(v_{k}-z)\big)\,dz+\frac{1}{2}\,r_{k}^{n+s_{1}}\,\int_{Y({\mathcal{R}}_{k})}K_{1}\big(r_{k}(v_{k}-z)\big)\,dz-C\\
&\geqslant\,\frac{1}{2}\,r_{k}^{n+s_{1}}\int_{{\mathcal{P}}_{k}\setminus Y({\mathcal{Q}}_{k})}K_{1}\big(r_{k}(v_{k}-z)\big)\,dz-C.\end{split}

Now we utilize the notation in (
3.10
),
the inclusion in (
3.11
) and the first inclusion in (
3.22
) to see that

(3.26)

\begin{split}{\mathcal{P}}_{k}\setminus Y({\mathcal{Q}}_{k})\supseteq\,&{\mathcal{P}}_{k}\setminus\Big(B_{1/2}(v_{k})\cap\{x_{n}\in(\widetilde{u}_{k}(x^{\prime}),\widetilde{u}_{k}(x^{\prime})+\delta_{k})\}\Big)\\

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
r
,
t>0
,
s\in(0,1)
and

D:=\big\{x=(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,|x^{\prime}|<r{\mbox{ and }}x_{n}\in(0,t)\big\}.

Then,

\iint_{D\times\{y_{n}<0\}}\frac{dx\,dy}{|x-y|^{n+s}}=c_{\star}\,r^{n-1}\,t^{1-s},

for a suitable
c_{\star}>0
, depending only on
n
and
s
.

Proof.

We recall that the surface area of the
(n-1)
-dimensional unit sphere is equal to
{\frac{2\pi^{\frac{n}{2}}}{\Gamma\left({\frac{n}{2}}\right)}}
,
where
\Gamma
is the Gamma Function. Furthermore,

\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{\ell^{n-2}\,d\ell}{\big(\ell^{2}+1\big)^{\frac{n+s}{2}}}=\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{n-1}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{1+s}{2}\right)}{2\Gamma\left(\frac{n+s}{2}\right)}.

Hence,
we use the substitution
\xi:=\frac{y^{\prime}-x^{\prime}}{x_{n}-y_{n}}
to see that

\displaystyle\iint_{D\times\{y_{n}<0\}}\frac{dx\,dy}{|x-y|^{n+s}}

\displaystyle\qquad=\int_{0}^{t}\left[\int_{\{|x^{\prime}|<r\}}\left[\int_{-\infty}^{0}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\frac{d\xi}{(x_{n}-y_{n})^{1+s}\big(|\xi|^{2}+1\big)^{\frac{n+s}{2}}}\right]\,dy_{n}\right]\,dx^{\prime}\right]\,dx_{n}

\displaystyle\qquad={\frac{2\pi^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}{\Gamma\left({\frac{n-1}{2}}\right)}}\int_{0}^{t}\left[\int_{\{|x^{\prime}|<r\}}\left[\int_{-\infty}^{0}\left[\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{\ell^{n-2}\,d\ell}{(x_{n}-y_{n})^{1+s}\big(\ell^{2}+1\big)^{\frac{n+s}{2}}}\right]\,dy_{n}\right]\,dx^{\prime}\right]\,dx_{n}

\displaystyle\qquad={\frac{\pi^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{1+s}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n+s}{2}\right)}}\int_{0}^{t}\left[\int_{\{|x^{\prime}|<r\}}\left[\int_{-\infty}^{0}\frac{dy_{n}}{(x_{n}-y_{n})^{1+s}}\right]\,dx^{\prime}\right]\,dx_{n}

\displaystyle\qquad={\frac{2\pi^{\frac{2n-1}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{1+s}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left({\frac{n}{2}}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{n+s}{2}\right)}}\,r^{n-1}\,\int_{0}^{t}\left[\int_{-\infty}^{0}\frac{dy_{n}}{(x_{n}-y_{n})^{1+s}}\right]\,dx_{n}

\displaystyle\qquad={\frac{2\pi^{\frac{2n-1}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{1+s}{2}\right)}{s\,\Gamma\left({\frac{n}{2}}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{n+s}{2}\right)}}\,r^{n-1}\,\int_{0}^{t}\frac{dx_{n}}{x_{n}^{s}}

\displaystyle\qquad={\frac{2\pi^{\frac{2n-1}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{1+s}{2}\right)}{s\,(1-s)\,\Gamma\left({\frac{n}{2}}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{n+s}{2}\right)}}\,r^{n-1}\,t^{1-s},

as desired.∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
r
,
t>0
,
s\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle D:=\big\{x=(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,|x^{\prime}|<r{\mbox{ and }}x_{n}\in(0,t)\big\}

and

\displaystyle F:=\big\{x=(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,|x^{\prime}|>r{\mbox{ and }}x_{n}\in(0,t)\big\}.

Then,

\iint_{D\times F}\frac{dx\,dy}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\leqslant Ct\,r^{n-1-s},

for some
C>0
depending only on
n
and
s
.

Proof.

Differently from the proof of Lemma
4.1
,
here it is convenient to exploit the substitutions
\alpha:=\frac{x_{n}}{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|}
and
\beta:=\frac{y_{n}}{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|}
.
In this way we see that

\displaystyle\iint_{D\times F}\frac{dx\,dy}{|x-y|^{n+s}}

\displaystyle\qquad=\int_{\{|x^{\prime}|<r\}}\left[\int_{\{|y^{\prime}|>r\}}\left[\int_{0}^{t/|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|}\left[\int_{0}^{t/|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|}\frac{d\beta}{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{n+s-2}(1+(\alpha-\beta)^{2})^{\frac{n+s}{2}}}\right]\,d\alpha\right]\,dy^{\prime}\right]\,dx^{\prime}

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant\int_{\{|x^{\prime}|<r\}}\left[\int_{\{|y^{\prime}|>r\}}\left[\int_{0}^{t/|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|}\left[\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{d\gamma}{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{n+s-2}(1+\gamma^{2})^{\frac{n+s}{2}}}\right]\,d\alpha\right]\,dy^{\prime}\right]\,dx^{\prime}

\displaystyle\qquad=C\int_{\{|x^{\prime}|<r\}}\left[\int_{\{|y^{\prime}|>r\}}\left[\int_{0}^{t/|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|}\frac{d\alpha}{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{n+s-2}}\right]\,dy^{\prime}\right]\,dx^{\prime}

\displaystyle\qquad=Ct\int_{\{|x^{\prime}|<r\}}\left[\int_{\{|y^{\prime}|>r\}}\frac{dy^{\prime}}{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{n+s-1}}\right]\,dx^{\prime}

\displaystyle\qquad=Ct\,r^{n-1-s}\int_{\{|X^{\prime}|<1\}}\left[\int_{\{|Y^{\prime}|>1\}}\frac{dY^{\prime}}{|X^{\prime}-Y^{\prime}|^{n+s-1}}\right]\,dX^{\prime}

\displaystyle\qquad=Ct\,r^{n-1-s},

where, as customary, we took the freedom of renaming
C
line after line.
∎

Now, in the forthcoming Lemma
4.3
we present a further technical result that detects suitable cancellations
involving “thin sets”. This is a pivotal result to account for the nonlocal scenario.
Indeed, in the classical capillarity theory, to look for a competitor for a given set,
one can dig out a (small deformation of a)
cylinder with base radius equal to
\varepsilon
and height
\delta\varepsilon

and then add a ball with the same volume. A very convenient fact in this scenario is that
the surface error produced by the cylinder is of order
\varepsilon^{n-1}
, while the one produced by the balls
are of order
(\delta\varepsilon^{n})^{\frac{n-1}{n}}=\delta^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\varepsilon^{n-1}
.
That is, for
\delta
suitably small, the surface tension produced by the new ball is negligible with respect
to the surface tension of the cylinder, thus allowing us to construct competitors in a nice and simple way.

Instead, in the nonlocal setting, for a given value of the fractional parameter, the corresponding nonlocal
surface tension produced by cylinders and balls of the same volume are comparable. This makes
the idea of “adding a ball to compensate the loss of volume caused by removing a cylinder” not suitable for the nonlocal framework.
Instead, as we will see in the proof
of Theorem
1.7
,
the volume compensation should occur through the addition of a suitably thin set placed at a regular point of
the droplet. The fact that the corresponding nonlocal surface energy produces a negligible contribution
will rely on the following result:

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
,
0<\varepsilon<\delta<1
and
\eta\in(0,1)
. Let
f\in C^{1,\alpha}_{0}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n-1},\,\left(-\frac{\delta}{2},\frac{\delta}{2}\right)\right)
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)

and assume that
f(0)=0
and
\partial_{i}f(0)=0
for all
i\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
.

Let
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1},[0,+\infty))
be such that
\varphi(x^{\prime})=0
whenever
|x^{\prime}|\geqslant 1

and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\varphi(x^{\prime})\,dx^{\prime}=1
.

Let

\displaystyle\psi(x^{\prime}):=\frac{\eta}{\varepsilon^{n-1}}\varphi\left(\frac{x^{\prime}}{\varepsilon}\right),

\displaystyle{\mathcal{P}}:=\big\{x=(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,|x^{\prime}|<\delta{\mbox{ and }}x_{n}>f(x^{\prime})+\psi(x^{\prime})\big\},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Q}}:=\big\{x=(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,|x^{\prime}|<\delta{\mbox{ and }}x_{n}\in\big(f(x^{\prime}),\,f(x^{\prime})+\psi(x^{\prime})\big)\big\}

and

\displaystyle{\mathcal{R}}:=\big\{x=(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,|x^{\prime}|<\delta{\mbox{ and }}x_{n}<f(x^{\prime})\big\}.

Then, there exist
\delta_{0}\in(0,1)

and
C>0
, depending only on
n
,
s
,
\alpha
,
f
and
\varphi
, such that if
\delta<\delta_{0}
and
\eta<\delta_{0}\varepsilon^{n}
then

\left|\iint_{{\mathcal{P}}\times{\mathcal{Q}}}\frac{dx\,dy}{|x-y|^{n+s}}-\iint_{{\mathcal{R}}\times{\mathcal{Q}}}\frac{dx\,dy}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\right|\leqslant C\left(\delta^{\alpha}+\frac{\eta}{\varepsilon^{n}}\right)\,\varepsilon^{(n-1)s}\,\eta^{1-s}.

Proof.

The gist of this proof is to use a suitable reflection to simplify most of the integral contributions.
For this, we consider the map

T(x):=\left(-x^{\prime},\,2f(x^{\prime})+\psi(x^{\prime})-x_{n}\right).

We observe that when
|x^{\prime}|<\delta
the distance between the Jacobian of
T
and minus the identity matrix is bounded from above by

C\sup_{|x^{\prime}|<\delta}\big(|\nabla f(x^{\prime})|+|\nabla\psi(x^{\prime})|\big)\leqslant C\sup_{|x^{\prime}|<\delta}\left(|\nabla f(x^{\prime})-\nabla f(0)|+\frac{\eta}{\varepsilon^{n}}\right)\leqslant C\left(\delta^{\alpha}+\frac{\eta}{\varepsilon^{n}}\right),

and the latter is a small quantity, as long as
\delta_{0}
is chosen sufficiently small.

Moreover, the condition
T(x)\in{\mathcal{Q}}
is equivalent to
|x^{\prime}|<\delta

and
2f(x^{\prime})+\psi(x^{\prime})-x_{n}\in\big(f(x^{\prime}),\,f(x^{\prime})+\psi(x^{\prime})\big)
, which is in turn equivalent to
x\in{\mathcal{Q}}
.

Similarly, the condition
T(x)\in{\mathcal{P}}
is equivalent to
x\in{\mathcal{R}}
, as well as
the condition
T(x)\in{\mathcal{R}}
is equivalent to
x\in{\mathcal{P}}
.

From these observations and the change of variable
(X,Y):=(T(x),T(y))
we arrive at

\iint_{{\mathcal{P}}\times{\mathcal{Q}}}\frac{dx\,dy}{|x-y|^{n+s}}=\left(1+O\left(\delta^{\alpha}+\frac{\eta}{\varepsilon^{n}}\right)\right)\iint_{{\mathcal{R}}\times{\mathcal{Q}}}\frac{dX\,dY}{|X-Y|^{n+s}}.

As a result,

(4.1)

\left|\iint_{{\mathcal{P}}\times{\mathcal{Q}}}\frac{dx\,dy}{|x-y|^{n+s}}-\iint_{{\mathcal{R}}\times{\mathcal{Q}}}\frac{dx\,dy}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\right|\leqslant C\left(\delta^{\alpha}+\frac{\eta}{\varepsilon^{n}}\right)\,\iint_{{\mathcal{R}}\times{\mathcal{Q}}}\frac{dx\,dy}{|x-y|^{n+s}}.

Now we consider the transformation
S(x):=(x^{\prime},x_{n}-f(x^{\prime}))
.
When
|x^{\prime}|<\delta
the distance between the Jacobian of
S
and the identity matrix is bounded from above by

C\sup_{|x^{\prime}|<\delta}|\nabla f(x^{\prime})|\leqslant C\delta^{\alpha}.

Besides, if
x\in{\mathcal{R}}
then
S(x)\in\big\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,|x^{\prime}|<\delta{\mbox{ and }}x_{n}<0\big\}
.
Also, if
x\in{\mathcal{Q}}
then

S(x)\in\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,|x^{\prime}|<\delta{\mbox{ and }}x_{n}\in(0,\psi(x^{\prime}))\}\subseteq\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,|x^{\prime}|<\varepsilon{\mbox{ and }}x_{n}\in\left(0,\frac{C\eta}{\varepsilon^{n-1}}\right)\right\}.

We stress that we are using here the fact that
\psi(x^{\prime})=0
when
|x^{\prime}|\geqslant\varepsilon
.

From these remarks and (
4.1
),
using now the change of variable
(X,Y):=(S(x),S(y))
, it follows that

\displaystyle\left|\iint_{{\mathcal{P}}\times{\mathcal{Q}}}\frac{dx\,dy}{|x-y|^{n+s}}-\iint_{{\mathcal{R}}\times{\mathcal{Q}}}\frac{dx\,dy}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant C\left(\delta^{\alpha}+\frac{\eta}{\varepsilon^{n}}\right)\,\iint_{\{X_{n}<0\}\times\left\{|Y^{\prime}|<\varepsilon,\;Y_{n}\in\left(0,\frac{C\eta}{\varepsilon^{n-1}}\right)\right\}}\frac{dX\,dY}{|X-Y|^{n+s}}.

We can thus employ Lemma
4.1
with
r:=\varepsilon
and
t:=\frac{C\eta}{\varepsilon^{n-1}}

and conclude that

\displaystyle\left|\iint_{{\mathcal{P}}\times{\mathcal{Q}}}\frac{dx\,dy}{|x-y|^{n+s}}-\iint_{{\mathcal{R}}\times{\mathcal{Q}}}\frac{dx\,dy}{|x-y|^{n+s}}\right|\leqslant C\left(\delta^{\alpha}+\frac{\eta}{\varepsilon^{n}}\right)\,\varepsilon^{n-1}\,\left(\frac{\eta}{\varepsilon^{n-1}}\right)^{1-s},

from which the desired result follows.∎

Also, by Lemmata

(5.6)

Appendix A
Existence of minimizers and proof of Proposition
1.1

The proof of the existence result in Proposition
1.1

is based on a semicontinuity argument and on a direct minimization procedure.
We first provide the following lower semicontinuity lemma.

Lemma A.1

(Semicontinuity of the energy)
.

If
I_{2}(\Omega,\Omega^{c})<+\infty
,

E_{j}\subseteq\Omega
and
E_{j}\rightarrow E
in
L^{1}(\Omega)
, then

\liminf_{j\rightarrow+\infty}\mathcal{E}(E_{j})\geqslant\mathcal{E}(E).

Proof.

If
\sigma\geqslant 0
, the proof follows by Fatou’s Lemma.
If instead
\sigma<0
, then we observe that

I_{2}(\Omega,\Omega^{c})=I_{2}(E,\Omega^{c})+I_{2}(E^{c}\cap\Omega,\Omega^{c}),

and therefore, using that
\sigma=-|\sigma|
,
we can write

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}(E)&=I_{1}(E,E^{c}\cap\Omega)-|\sigma|I_{2}(E,\Omega^{c})+(|\sigma|+1)I_{2}(\Omega,\Omega^{c})-(|\sigma|+1)I_{2}(\Omega,\Omega^{c})\\
&=I_{1}(E,E^{c}\cap\Omega)+I_{2}(E,\Omega^{c})+(|\sigma|+1)I_{2}(E^{c}\cap\Omega,\Omega^{c})-(|\sigma|+1)I_{2}(\Omega,\Omega^{c}).\end{split}

As a consequence, we can exploit Fatou’s Lemma and obtain the desired result.
∎

With this we are able to prove Proposition
1.1
:

Proof of Proposition
1.1
.

We observe that, if
K_{1}\in\mathbf{K}(n,s_{1},\lambda,\varrho)
, then, for any
p\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

(A.1)

I_{1}(F,F^{c})\geqslant\frac{1}{\lambda}I_{s_{1}}(F\cap B_{\varrho/2}(p),F^{c}\cap B_{\varrho/2}(p)),\qquad{\mbox{for every }}F\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}.

To prove it, we notice that if
x,y\in B_{\varrho/2}(p)
, then
|x-y|\leqslant|x-p|+|p-y|<\varrho
,
and therefore, recalling (
1.2
),

\displaystyle I_{1}(F,F^{c})

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\int_{F\cap B_{\varrho/2}(p)}\int_{F^{c}\cap B_{\varrho/2}(p)}K_{1}(x-y)\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda}\int_{F\cap B_{\varrho/2}(p)}\int_{F^{c}\cap B_{\varrho/2}(p)}\frac{dx\,dy}{|x-y|^{n+s_{1}}},

which establishes (
A.1
).

Now, if
H
is a half-space such that
|H\cap\Omega|=m
and

is such that
\Omega\subseteq B_{R}

), we see that

for some

,

\displaystyle I_{1}(H\cap\Omega,(H\cap\Omega)^{c}\cap\Omega)+\sigma I_{2}(H\cap\Omega,\Omega^{c})

As a consequence, we find that

Continuous time Markov chain based approximation of stationary and weak KAM Hamilton-Jacobi equations
Yurii Averboukh

Abstract
Main objects of the paper are stationary and weak KAM Hamilton-Jacobi equations on the finite-dimensional torus. The key idea of the paper is to replace the underlying calculus of variations problems with continuous time Markov decision problems. This directly leads to an approximation of the stationary Hamilton-Jacobi equation by the Bellman equation for a discounting Markov decision problem. Developing elements of the weak KAM theory for the Markov decision problem, we obtain an approximation of the effective Hamiltonian. Additionally, convergences of the functional parts of the discrete weak KAM equations and Mather measures are shown. It turns out that the approximating equations are systems of algebraic equations. Thus, the paper’s result can be seen as numerical schemes for stationary and weak KAM Hamilton-Jacobi equations. MSC Classification (2020): 49L25, 37J51, 60J28, 93E20, 70H09. Keywords: Hamilton-Jacobi equation, discrete weak KAM theory, Markov chain approximation, discrete Mather measure.

Introduction

Overview of the main results.

The paper is concerned with the discrete approximations of solutions of stationary Hamilton-Jacobi equation

\lambda u_{\lambda}+H(x,-\nabla u_{\lambda})=0

(1)

and the weak KAM equation

H(x,-\nabla u)=\bar{H}.

(2)

The Hamiltonian
H
is assumed to be periodical in
x
, i.e., we consider them on the
d
-dimensional torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}
. Notice that in the weak KAM equation, the unknowns are a function
u:\mathbb{T}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and a constant
\bar{H}
.

The stationary Hamilton-Jacobi equation describes the value of the discounting infinite horizon problem of calculus of variations:

\text{minimize }\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\lambda s}L(x(s),\dot{x}(s))ds

(3)

subject to

x(\cdot)\in\operatorname{AC}([0,+\infty);\mathbb{T}^{d}),\ \ x(t)=x_{*}.

(4)

In this case (see
[
15
,
3
]
), the value function of problem (
3
), (
4
) is equal to
u_{\lambda}(x_{*})
, where
u_{\lambda}
is a viscosity solution of (
1
).

Similarly, weak KAM equation (
2
) provides
[
14
,
5
]
the fixed points of the backward Lax-Oleinik operator. This means that a pair
(u,\bar{H})
is a viscosity solution of (
2
) if and only if, for each
T>0
,

\begin{split}u(x_{*})=\min\Bigg\{\int_{0}^{T}L(x(t),&\dot{x}(t))dt+u(x(T)):\\
&x(\cdot)\in\operatorname{AC}([0,T];\mathbb{T}^{d}),\,x(0)=x_{*}\Bigg\}+\bar{H}T.\end{split}

The number
\bar{H}
is called an effective Hamiltonian or a Mañé critical value.

Our approach relies on the approximation of an absolutely continuous curve
x(\cdot)
by a continuous-time Markov chain on the regular lattice
\Lambda_{N}\triangleq\mathbb{Z}^{d}/(N^{-1}\mathbb{Z}^{d})
with the generator
Q^{N}(v)=(Q_{x,y}^{N}(v))_{x,y\in\Lambda_{N}}
that depends on a vector
v=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{d})^{T}
and is defined by the rule:

Q_{x,y}^{N}(v)\triangleq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}N|v_{i}|,&y=x+h\operatorname{sgn}(v_{i})e_{i},\\
-N\sum_{j=1}^{N}|v_{j}|,&y=x,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Hereinafter,
h=N^{-1}
,
\operatorname{sgn}(a)
denotes the sign of the number
a
, whilst
e_{i}
stands for the
i
-th coordinate vector.

For this Markov chain, we consider the criterion

\text{minimize }\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\lambda t}L(X_{t},V_{t})dt,

where
X_{t}
and
V_{t}
are a state and a stochastic control respectively. The corresponding Bellman equation turns out to be a system of algebraic equations.

The first main result of the paper is the rate of approximation of a solution to stationary Hamilton-Jacobi equation (
1
) by the Bellman equation for the aforementioned discounting Markov decision problem. We show that it is of order
N^{-1/2}
(see Theorem
5.1
).

The study of the limit behavior of the discounting Markov decision problem in the case where
\lambda\rightarrow 0
makes it possible to develop some elements of the weak KAM theory for continuous time Markov chains. In particular, we derive the weak KAM equation on the lattice that is also a system of algebraic equations. As for the continuous phase space case, we are seeking for a function and an effective Hamiltonian.

The second main result is the fact that the effective Hamiltonian for the lattice
\Lambda_{N}
approximates the number
\bar{H}
with an error of the order
{N^{-1/2}}
(see Theorem
7.1
). Additionally, the functional parts of the discrete weak KAM equations converge up to subsequence to the functional part of the solution of weak KAM equation (
2
) (see Theorem
7.6
).

Recall that, in the continuous phase space case, the effective Hamiltonian can be characterized via Mather measures those minimize the action
\nu\mapsto\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}L(x,v)\nu(d(x,v))
, where
L
stands for the Lagrangian. We also introduce the concept of Mather measures for the weak KAM theory on the lattices and show their convergence up to subsequence to a Mather measure for the classical weak KAM theory (see Theorems
8.3
,
8.4
).

Literature overview.

The modern theory of first order Hamilton-Jacobi equations relies on the notion of viscosity solutions proposed by Crandall and Lions
[
7
]
. This concept, in particular, provides a characterization of the value of optimal control problems through Bellman equations. We refer to
[
3
,
15
]
for the exposition of the viscosity equation and its applications to the optimal control theory.

The passing to the limit in the stationary Hamilton-Jacobi equation as the discounting factor tends to the zero leads to the weak KAM equation
[
22
]
. This result is primary used within the study of homogenization of the evolutionary Hamilton-Jacobi equation
[
22
,
11
]
. Another source of weak KAM equation is the weak KAM theory itself
[
13
,
28
]
. Recall that this theory studies the long time behavior of the calculus of variation problems and the Euler-Lagrange flows. In particular, the infinitesimal form of the definition of calibrated curve immediately yields the weak KAM equation
[
13
,
14
,
10
,
9
,
5
]
. Notice that the effective Hamiltonian also known as the Mañé critical value provides the averaged optimal outcome for the long time calculus of variations problem. The effective Hamiltonian can be calculated using a Mather measure
[
24
]
(see also
[
5
,
28
,
12
,
13
]
) that is a measure on the tangent bundle minimizing the action of the Lagrangian over the set of measure invariant w.r.t. the Euler-Lagrange flow (the equivalent form of this condition is obtained in
[
4
,
23
]
).

There are several extensions and analogs of the weak KAM theory (see, in particular,
[
1
,
8
,
18
,
16
]
). We especially mention papers
[
17
,
27
,
20
,
25
]
where stochastic analogs of the weak KAM theory are developed. Papers
[
17
,
20
,
25
]
deal with a problem coming from perturbation of the dynamics by the Brownian motion and give an insights into the selection problem for Mather measures, whilst in
[
27
]
the weak KAM theory for the discrete time random walk on the regular lattice is constructed. There, in fact, it is shown that the corresponding weak KAM equations converge to a solution of the continuous phase space weak KAM equation if the time and space steps vanish. More precisely, effective Hamiltonians converge to the continuous phase space effective Hamiltonian, while the function parts converge up to the choice of a subsequence. Thus, one can regard the results of
[
27
]
as a numerical method for weak KAM equation (
2
). This concept is close to one of the paper. However, in the contrast to
[
27
]
, we start with the continuous time Markov chain. This gives half less number of algebraic equation needed to assure the same order of approximation. Moreover, we impose milder conditions and do not use the completeness of the Euler-Lagrange flow.

Finally, we refer to
[
2
]
, where an approximation of an optimal control problem by a continuous time Markov decision problem was developed for the case of compact control space. Additionally, the approximation technique for the Hamilton-Jacobi equation based on discrete-time Markov chains was examined in
[
26
]
.

Organization of the paper.

In Section
2
, we give the general notation and assumptions. Additionally, here we remind the definitions of viscosity solutions for equations (

), (
2
).
The next section (Section

Preliminaries

2.1
General notation and assumptions

If
(X,\rho_{X})
is a metric space,
x\in X
,
r>0
, then
\mathbb{B}_{r}(x)
stands for the open ball in
X
of radius
r
centered at
x
, i.e.,

\mathbb{B}_{r}(x)\triangleq\big\{y\in X:\,\rho_{X}(x,y)<r\big\}.

Let
\mathbb{T}^{d}\triangleq\mathbb{R}^{d}/\mathbb{Z}^{d}
denote the
d
-dimensional flat torus. Recall that an element of
\mathbb{T}^{d}
is a set given by the rule:

x=\{\tilde{x}+n:n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\}

for some
\tilde{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We denote the standard Euclidean norm on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
by
|\cdot|
. Further, with some abuse of notation, for
x,y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
, we put

|x-y|\triangleq\min\{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|:\,x^{\prime}\in x,\,y^{\prime}\in y\}.

The quantity
|x-y|
is a distance on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
.

Elements of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
are considered as column vectors, while
\mathbb{R}^{d,*}
consists of row vectors. Below we regard
\mathbb{R}^{d}
as the tangent space to
\mathbb{T}^{d}
, and
\mathbb{R}^{d,*}
as the cotangent space.

Further, if
x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
,
\varphi
is a function defined in a neighborhood of
x
that is differentiable at
x
, then
\nabla\phi(x)
denotes the derivative of
\phi
at
x
. We assume that
\nabla\phi(x)
is a row-vector.

Assume that we are given with a function
L:\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
that is called a
Lagrangian
. We impose the following condition on the function
L
:

(L1)

L
and
L_{x}
are continuous;

(L2)

L
is convex;

(L3)

L
satisfies the superlinear growth condition, i.e., for each
a\geq 0
there exists a constant
g(a)
such that

L(x,v)\geq a|v|+g(a).

In the following, we will widely use the notation

K(c)\triangleq\sup_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d},|v|\leq c}|L_{x}(x,v)|.

(5)

The Hamiltonian
H:\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d,*}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is defined by the Legendre transform:

H(x,p)\triangleq\max_{v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big[pv-L(x,v)\Big].

2.2
Stationary Hamilton-Jacobi and weak KAM equations

The first main object of the paper is stationary Hamilton-Jacobi equation (
1
).

We consider the concept of viscosity solutions
[
3
,
7
]
. A function
u_{\lambda}
is a viscosity solution of (
1
) if, for every point
x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
and each smooth function
\psi
defined in some neighborhood of
x
such that the mapping
y\mapsto u_{\lambda}(y)-\psi(y)
attains
the minimum (respectively, maximum) at the point
x
, one has that
\lambda u_{\lambda}(x)+H(x,-\nabla\psi(x))\geq 0
(respectively,
\lambda u_{\lambda}(x)+H(x,-\nabla\psi(x))\leq 0
).

The second object of the paper is weak KAM equation (
2
).
As above, its solution is considered in the viscosity sense: a pair
(u,\bar{H})
is a viscosity solution of (
2
) provided that, for every point
x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
and each function
\psi\in C^{1}(\mathbb{B}_{r}(x))
for some
r>0
such that the mapping
y\mapsto u(y)-\psi(y)
attains
the minimum (respectively, maximum) at the point
x
, one has that
H(x,-\nabla\psi(x))\geq\bar{H}
(respectively,
H(x,-\nabla\psi(x))\leq\bar{H}
).

In
[
22
]
, it is shown that, if, for each sufficiently small
\lambda>0
,
u_{\lambda}
solves (
1
), then up subsequence
(u_{\lambda}(x)-u_{\lambda}(z),-\lambda u_{\lambda}(x))
converge uniformly on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
to a pair
(u(x),\bar{H})
that is a viscosity solution of weak KAM equation (
2
) (here
z
is a fixed point on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
).

The constant
\bar{H}
can be characterized through a so called Mather measure
\mu
. To introduce this concept, we follow
[
5
]
and put

{w}(v)\triangleq\inf_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}}L(x,v)\vee 1.

(6)

Further, we denote by
\mathcal{M}
the set of probability measures on
\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
with finite integral of
{w}
, i.e.,

\mathcal{M}\triangleq\Bigg\{\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}):\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}{w}(v)\nu(d(x,v))<\infty.\Bigg\}

A Mather measure
[
5
]
for the torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}
is a probability on
\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
that minimizes the functional

\nu\mapsto\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}L(x,v)\nu(dxdv)

over the set of measures
\nu\in\mathcal{M}
satisfying the holonomic constraints:

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla\phi(x)v\nu(d(x,v))=0

for each
\phi\in C^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
. It is shown (see, in particular,
[
5
]
) that, if
\mu
is a Mather measure for
\mathbb{T}^{d}
, then

-\bar{H}=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}L(x,v)\mu(d(x,v)).

3
Controlled continuous time Markov chain

3.1
Construction of approximating Markov chain

The approximation results derived in the paper rely on a construction of continuous time Markov chains on infinite or finite time interval. To unify the presentation, we will denote a finite or infinite time by interval
\mathcal{I}
, i.e., either
\mathcal{I}=[0,T]
or
\mathcal{I}=[0,+\infty)
.

To explain the main idea of construction of the approximation continuous time Markov chain, we notice that each function
x(\cdot)\in\operatorname{AC}(\mathcal{I},\mathbb{R}^{d})
is entirely determined by
x(0)
and a velocity
v(\cdot)\in L^{1}(\mathcal{I},\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We say that a pair
(x(\cdot),v(\cdot))
is a control process on
\mathcal{I}
provided that
x(\cdot)\in C(\mathcal{I},\mathbb{T}^{d})
,
v(\cdot)\in L^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathcal{I},\mathbb{R}^{d})
and

\frac{d}{dt}x(t)=v(t).

In the Introduction, we already briefly described the main concept of approximating Markov chain. We fix

1.

a natural number
N
;

2.

a regular lattice
\Lambda_{N}\triangleq(h\mathbb{Z}^{d})/\mathbb{Z}^{d}\subset\mathbb{T}^{d}
.

Hereinafter, we denote

h=N^{-1}.

Additionally, as we mentioned above,
e_{i}
stands for the
i
-th coordinate vector on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

The Kolmogorov matrix of the approximating Markov chain is constructed as follows. If
v=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{d})^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, then we define the matrix

Q^{N}(v)=(Q^{N}_{x,y}(v))_{x,y\in\Lambda_{N}}
by the following rule:

Q^{N}_{x,y}(v)\triangleq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}h^{-1}|v_{i}|,&y=x+\operatorname{sgn}(v_{i})he_{i},\\
-h^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{d}|v_{j}|,&y=x,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Now let us define stochastic control processes those are determined by this Kolmogorov matrix. First, we start with open-loop controls.

Definition 3.1
.

We say that a 6-tuple
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathcal{I}},\mathbb{P},X,V)
is a controlled Markov chain process for the lattice
\Lambda_{N}
provided that

•

(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathcal{I}},\mathbb{P})
is a filtered probability space;

•

X
is a
\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathcal{I}}
-adopted process with values in
\Lambda_{N}
;

•

V
is a
\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathcal{I}}
-progressively measurable process with values in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}|V_{s}|dt<\infty
for each
t\in\mathcal{I}
;

•

for each
\phi:\Lambda_{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
the process

\phi(X_{t})-\int_{0}^{t}Q^{N}(V_{s})\phi(X_{s})ds

is a
\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathcal{I}}
-martingale.

Hereinafter,
\mathbb{E}
stands for the expectation corresponding to the probability
\mathbb{P}
.

Below, we denote the set of all controlled Markov chain process for the lattice
\Lambda_{N}
on the time interval
\mathcal{I}
satisfying the initial condition
X(0)=x_{*}

\mathbb{P}
-a.s. by
\operatorname{MCP}_{N}(\mathcal{I},x_{*})
.

The most convenient tool in the theory of controlled Markov chain is feedback strategies (policies).

Definition 3.2
.

A feedback strategy on
\Lambda_{N}
is a measurable mapping
\pi:\mathcal{I}\times\Lambda_{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
. A stationary feedback strategy is a mapping
\pi:\Lambda_{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

A feedback strategy
\pi
defines a Kolmogorov matrix

\mathcal{Q}^{N}[\pi,t]=(\mathcal{Q}_{x,y}^{N}[\pi,t])_{x,y\in\Lambda_{N}}
with entries

\mathcal{Q}_{x,y}^{N}[\pi,t]=Q_{x,y}^{N}[\pi(t,x)].

If
\pi
is a stationary feedback strategy, we will write simply
\mathcal{Q}^{N}[\pi]
instead of
\mathcal{Q}^{N}[\pi,t]
.

Definition 3.3
.

Given a feedback strategy
\pi
, we say that
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathcal{I}},\mathbb{P},X)
is a motion produced by
\pi
provided that

•

(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathcal{I}},\mathbb{P})
is a filtered probability space;

•

X
is
\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathcal{I}}
-adopted process with values in
\Lambda_{N}
;

•

for every
t\in\mathcal{I}
,

\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}|\pi(s,X_{s})|ds<\infty;

•

for each
\phi:\Lambda_{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, the process

\phi(X_{t})-\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{Q}^{N}[\pi,s]\phi(X_{s})ds

(7)

is a
\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathcal{I}}
-martingale.

If
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathcal{I}},\mathbb{P},X)
is a motion produced by
\pi
, then, letting

V_{t}\triangleq\pi(t,X_{t}),

we obtain a controlled Markov chain process
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathcal{I}},\mathbb{P},X,V)
.

Further, a distribution on
\Lambda_{N}
is a sequence
m=(m_{x})_{x\in\Lambda_{N}}
with nonnegative entries such that

\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{N}}m_{x}=1.

We say that a motion
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\in[0,+\infty)},\mathbb{P},X)
produced by a feedback strategy
\pi
has the initial distribution
m_{0}=\{m_{0,x}\}_{x\in\Lambda_{N}}
if

\mathbb{P}(X_{0}=x)=m_{0,x},\ \ x\in\Lambda_{N}.

Additionally, we say that a motion
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\in[0,+\infty)},\mathbb{P},X)
produced by
\pi
has the initial state
z
provided that

X(0)=z,\ \ \mathbb{P}-\text{a.s.}

This corresponds to the initial distribution
\mathbbm{1}_{z}=(\mathbbm{1}_{z,x})_{x\in\Lambda_{N}}
with entries

\mathbbm{1}_{z,x}\triangleq\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}1,&x=z,\\
0,&x\neq z.\end{array}\right.

There exists (see
[
21
, Theorem 5.4.1]
) at least one motion produced by the stationary feedback strategy
\pi
with the initial distribution equal to
m_{0}
.

Notice that an evolution of distributions produced by the feedback strategy
\pi
and the initial distribution
m_{0}
is described by a mapping
\mathcal{I}\ni t\mapsto m(t)=(m_{x}(t))_{x\in\Lambda}
such that

m_{x}(t)=\mathbb{P}(X_{t}=x).

(8)

The function
m(\cdot)
satisfies by the Kolmogorov equation

\frac{d}{dt}m(t)=m(t)\mathcal{Q}^{N}[\pi,t],\ \ m(t)=m_{0}.

(9)

Here, we regard
m(t)
as a row-vector.

3.2
Finite differences

Let
\phi:\Lambda_{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
x\in\Lambda_{N}
and
i\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
. Define, for
x\in\Lambda_{N}
,

\begin{split}&\Delta_{N,i}^{+}\phi(x)\triangleq\frac{\phi(x+he_{i})-\phi(x)}{h},\\
&\Delta_{N,i}^{-}\phi(x)\triangleq\frac{\phi(x-he_{i})-\phi(x)}{h}.\end{split}

(10)

If there exists a smooth function
u
such that
\phi(x)=u(x)
on
\Lambda_{N}
, then the quantity
\Delta_{N,i}^{+}\phi(x)
is the standard right approximation of the partial derivative of
u
at
x
w.r.t.
x_{i}
. Analogously,
\Delta_{N,i}^{-}\phi(x)
is a left approximation of the
-\partial_{x_{i}}u(x)
.

Notice that

\begin{split}&\Delta_{N,i}^{+}\phi(x)=-\Delta_{N,i}^{-}\phi(x+he_{i}),\\
&\Delta_{N,i}^{-}\phi(x)=-\Delta_{N,i}^{+}\phi(x-he_{i}).\end{split}

(11)

Further, we set

\Delta_{N}\phi(x)=((\Delta_{N,1}^{+}\phi(x),\Delta_{N,1}^{-}\phi(x)),\ldots,(\Delta_{N,d}^{+}\phi(x),\Delta_{N,d}^{-}\phi(x))).

Thus, it is convenient to work with sequences of pairs.
If
\xi=((\xi_{1}^{+},\xi_{1}^{-}),\ldots,(\xi_{d}^{+},\xi_{d}^{-}))
is a
d
-tuple of pairs, where
\xi_{i}^{+},\xi_{i}^{-}\in\mathbb{R}
,
v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, then, with some abuse of notation, we denote

\xi\cdot v\triangleq\sum_{i=1}^{d}\big[\xi_{i}^{+}v_{i}^{+}+\xi_{i}^{i}v_{i}^{-}\big].

Notice that, for each
\phi:\Lambda_{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
x\in\Lambda_{N}
, one has that

\sum_{y\in\Lambda_{N}}Q_{x,y}^{N}[v]\phi(y)=\Delta_{N}\phi(x)\cdot v.

(12)

Thus, if
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathcal{I}},\mathbb{P},X,V)
is an controlled Markov chain process for the lattice
\Lambda_{N}
, then, for each function
\phi:\Lambda_{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, and
s,r\in\mathcal{I}
,
s<r
,

\mathbb{E}\phi(X_{r})-\mathbb{E}\phi(X_{s})=\mathbb{E}\int_{s}^{r}\big(\Delta_{N}\phi(X_{t})\cdot V_{t}\big)dt.

In particular, given a feedback strategy
\pi
and a corresponding motion
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathcal{I}},\mathbb{P},X)
, we have that

\mathbb{E}\phi(X_{r})-\mathbb{E}\phi(X_{s})=\mathbb{E}\int_{s}^{r}\big(\Delta_{N}\phi(X_{t})\cdot\pi(t,X_{t})\big)dt.

Now, for
x\in\Lambda_{N}
,
\xi=((\xi_{i}^{+},\xi_{i}^{-}))_{i=1}^{d}
, where
\xi_{i}^{+},\xi_{i}^{-}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we set

\mathcal{H}_{N}(x,\xi)\triangleq\sup_{v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big[\xi\cdot v-L(x,v)\Big].

(13)

This and (
12
) give that

\begin{split}\mathcal{H}_{N}(x,(-\Delta_{N})\phi(x))&=\max_{v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big[(-\Delta_{N})\phi(x)\cdot v-L(x,v)\Big]\\
&=-\min_{v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Bigg[\sum_{y\in\Lambda_{N}}Q^{N}_{x,y}[v]\phi(y)+L(x,v)\Bigg].\end{split}

(14)

Here,

(-\Delta_{N})\phi(x)=(((-\Delta_{N,1}^{+})\phi(x),(-\Delta_{N,1}^{-})\phi(x)),\ldots,((-\Delta_{N,d}^{+})\phi(x),(-\Delta_{N,d}^{-})\phi(x))).

3.3
Distance between the deterministic evolution and the Markov chain

We recall that
\mathbb{T}^{d}=\mathbb{R}^{d}/\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, while
\Lambda_{N}=(h\mathbb{Z}^{d})/\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. It is convenient to denote by
[\tilde{x}]
the equivalence class of
\tilde{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, i.e.,

[\tilde{x}]\triangleq\{\tilde{x}+n:n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\}.

Let
\pi
be a feedback strategy on
\Lambda_{N}
. We assume that its entries are uniformly bounded. Denote by
\hat{\pi}
a feedback strategy on
h\mathbb{Z}^{d}
such that, for
\tilde{x}\in h\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
\hat{\pi}(t,\tilde{x})=\pi(t,[\tilde{x}])
.

Now we define the infinite matrix
\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}^{N}[\pi,t]=\{\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}^{N}_{\tilde{x},\tilde{y}}[\pi,t]\}_{\tilde{x},\tilde{y}\in h\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
indexed with elements of
h\mathbb{Z}^{d}
by the rule:

\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}^{N}_{\tilde{x},\tilde{y}}[\pi,t]\triangleq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}h^{-1}|\hat{\pi}_{i}(t,\tilde{x})|,&\tilde{y}=\tilde{x}+h\operatorname{sgn}(\hat{\pi}_{i}(t,\tilde{x}))e_{i},\\
-h^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{d}|\hat{\pi}_{j}(t,\tilde{x})|,&\tilde{y}=\tilde{x},\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Notice that, if
\tilde{y}=\tilde{x}\pm he_{i}
, then
\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}^{N}_{\tilde{x},\tilde{y}}[\pi,t]=\mathcal{Q}^{N}_{x,y}[\pi,t]
, where
x=[\tilde{x}]
,
y=[\tilde{y}]
.

Let us introduce the generator
\mathcal{L}^{\pi}_{t}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(h\mathbb{Z}^{d})
by the following rule: for
\phi\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(h\mathbb{Z}^{d}))
with at most quadratic growth,

\mathcal{L}_{t}^{\pi}\phi(\tilde{x}^{1},\tilde{x}^{2})\triangleq\nabla_{\tilde{x}^{1}}\phi(\tilde{x}^{1},\tilde{x}^{2})\pi(t,\tilde{x}^{2})+\sum_{\tilde{y}\in h\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}^{N}_{\tilde{x}^{2},\tilde{y}}[\pi,t]\phi(\tilde{x}^{1},\tilde{y}).

Hereinafter, we denote by
C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(h\mathbb{Z}^{d}))
the set of continuous functions from
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(h\mathbb{Z}^{d})
to
\mathbb{R}
those are continuously differentiable w.r.t. the first variable.

It follows from
[
21
, Theorem 5.4.1]
that, given
(\tilde{x}^{1}_{*},\tilde{x}^{2}_{*})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(h\mathbb{Z}^{d})
, there exist a filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathcal{I}},\mathbb{P})
and
\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathcal{I}}
adopted process
(\widetilde{X}^{1},\widetilde{X}^{2})
such that

(\widetilde{X}^{1}_{0},\widetilde{X}^{2}_{0})=(\tilde{x}^{1}_{*},\tilde{x}^{2}_{*})

(15)

and, for each
\phi\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(h\mathbb{Z}^{d}))
with at most quadratic growth, one has that
the process

\phi(\widetilde{X}^{1}_{t},\widetilde{X}^{2}_{t})-\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{L}_{s}^{\pi}\phi(\widetilde{X}^{1}_{s},\widetilde{X}^{2}_{s})ds

(16)

is a
\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathcal{I}}
-martingale. Notice that, in this case,
\widetilde{X}^{1}
satisfies
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. the system of ODEs

\frac{d}{dt}\widetilde{X}^{1}_{t}=\hat{\pi}(t,\widetilde{X}^{2}_{t}),

while
\widetilde{X}^{2}
is a continuous time Markov chain with Kolmogorov matrix
\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}^{N}[\pi,\cdot]
.
Furthermore, if we let

X^{1}_{t}\triangleq[\widetilde{X}^{1}_{t}],\ \ X^{2}_{t}\triangleq[\widetilde{X}^{2}_{t}],

(17)

then

\frac{d}{dt}{X}^{1}_{t}=\pi(t,{X}^{2}_{t}),

and
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathcal{I}},\mathbb{P},X^{2})
is a motion produced by the feedback strategy
\pi
and the initial state
x^{2}_{*}=[\tilde{x}^{2}_{*}]
.

The key result of this section is the following.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let

•

\pi
be a feedback strategy such that, for some constant
c>0
,
|\pi(t,x)|\leq c
;

•

x^{1}_{*}\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
,
x^{2}_{*}\in\Lambda_{N}
,

•

\tilde{x}_{*}^{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\tilde{x}^{2}_{*}\in h\mathbb{Z}^{d}
be such
x^{1}_{*}=[\tilde{x}^{1}_{*}]
,
x^{2}_{*}=[\tilde{x}^{2}_{*}]
and
|x_{*}^{1}-x^{2}_{*}|=|\tilde{x}_{*}^{1}-\tilde{x}^{2}_{*}|
;

•

(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathcal{I}},\mathbb{P})
be a filtered probability space;

•

(\widetilde{X}^{1},\widetilde{X}^{2})
be a stochastic process defined on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathcal{I}},\mathbb{P})
with values in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(h\mathbb{Z}^{d})
satisfying conditions (
15
) and (
16
);

•

(X^{1},X^{2})
be defined by (
17
).

Then, for each
t\in\mathcal{I}
,

\mathbb{E}|{X}^{1}_{t}-{X}^{2}_{t}|^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}|\widetilde{X}^{1}_{t}-\widetilde{X}^{2}_{t}|^{2}\leq|x^{1}_{*}-x^{2}_{*}|+\sqrt{d}cN^{-1}t.

(18)

Proof.

The first inequality in (
18
) directly follows from the construction of
({X}^{1},{X}^{2})
.

To prove the second inequality, we first notice that, due to the assumption that
\pi
has uniformly bounded entries,
\mathbb{E}|\widetilde{X}^{1}_{t}-\widetilde{X}^{2}_{t}|^{2}
is bounded. Further, let
{q}(\tilde{x}_{1},\tilde{x}_{2})\triangleq|\tilde{x}_{1}-\tilde{x}_{2}|^{2}
.
Plugging in (
16
) the function
{q}
, we obtain that

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}|\widetilde{X}^{1}_{t}-\widetilde{X}^{2}_{t}|^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}|\widetilde{X}^{1}_{0}-\widetilde{X}^{2}_{0}|^{2}+\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{L}^{\pi}_{s}{q}(\widetilde{X}^{1}_{s},\widetilde{X}^{2}_{s})ds.\end{split}

Direct computation gives that

\mathcal{L}^{\pi}_{t}{q}(x_{1},x_{2})=h\sum_{i=1}^{d}|\pi_{i}(t,x_{2})|\leq hc\sqrt{d}.

Therefore, taking into account that
h=N^{-1}
, we obtain the second inequality in (
18
).
∎

4
Discounting Markov decision problem

In this section, we work with the infinite time interval, i.e., we put
\mathcal{I}=[0,+\infty)
.

If
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\in[0,+\infty)},\mathbb{P},X,V)
is a controlled Markov chain process for the lattice
\Lambda_{N}
, then its quality is evaluated by the quantity

\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\lambda t}L(X_{t},V_{t}).

Furthermore, the outcome of the feedback strategy
\pi
and the initial distribution
m_{0}
is equal to

\mathcal{J}_{N,\lambda}[\pi,m_{0}]\triangleq\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\lambda t}L(X_{t},\pi(t,X_{t})dt,

where
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\in[0,+\infty)},\mathbb{P},X)
is a motion produced by
\pi
and
m_{0}
. The quantity
\mathcal{J}_{N,\lambda}[\pi,m_{0}]
does not depend on the concrete choice of the process
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\in[0,+\infty)},\mathbb{P},X)
produced by
\pi
and
m_{0}
. Indeed, (
8
) implies that

\mathcal{J}_{N,\lambda}[\pi,m_{0}]=\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\lambda t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}L(x,\pi(t,x))m(t)dt.

Here
m(\cdot)
satisfies (
9
). If
m_{0}
is equal to
1
at
z
and zero elsewhere, then we write
\mathcal{J}_{N,\lambda}[\pi,z]
instead of
\mathcal{J}_{N,\lambda}[\pi,m_{0}]
.

The Bellman equation for the examined discounting Markov decision problem takes the form:

\lambda\varphi_{N,\lambda}(x)+\mathcal{H}_{N}(x,(-\Delta_{N})\varphi_{N,\lambda}(x))=0,\ \ x\in\Lambda_{N}.

(19)

This fact is formalized in the following statement.

Proposition 4.1
.

Equation (
19
) has a unique solution. Moreover, the stationary feedback strategy
\pi_{\lambda,N}^{*}
defined by the rule:

\pi_{\lambda,N}^{*}(x)\in\underset{v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\operatorname{Argmax}}\Big[(-\Delta_{N})\varphi_{N,\lambda}(x)\cdot v-L(x,v)\Big]

(20)

is optimal, i.e.,

\varphi_{N,\lambda}(z)=\mathcal{J}_{N,\lambda}[\pi_{\lambda,N}^{*},z]

(21)

and, for each
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\in[0,+\infty)},\mathbb{P},X,V)\in\operatorname{MCP}_{N}([0,+\infty),z)
, one has

\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\lambda t}L(X_{t},V_{t})dt\geq\varphi_{N,\lambda}(z).

(22)

Proof.

The proof relies on truncations arguments. Let
A>0
. We consider the Markov decision problem

\text{minimize }\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\lambda t}L(X_{t},V_{t})dt\Bigg]

over the set of 6-tuples
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\in[0,+\infty)},\mathbb{P},X,V)\in\operatorname{MCP}_{N}([0,+\infty),z)
satisfying the additional constraint
|V_{t}|\leq A
. Due to
[
19
, Lemma 4.4, Theorems 4.6, 4.10]
, this problem has a value denoted by
\varphi_{N,\lambda}^{A}
. The latter satisfies the following Bellman equation on
\Lambda_{N}
:

\lambda\varphi_{N,\lambda}^{A}(x)+\max_{|v|\leq A}\Big[(-\Delta_{N})\varphi_{N,\lambda}^{A}(x)\cdot v-L(x,v)\Big]=0.

Now recall that due to condition
(L3)

L(x,v)\geq g(0).

Thus,
\varphi_{N,\lambda}^{A}(x)\geq\lambda^{-1}g(0)
for each
x\in\Lambda_{N}
. On the other hand, letting
V_{t}\equiv 0
, we obtain that
\varphi_{N,\lambda}^{A}(x)\leq\lambda^{-1}L(z,0)
. Therefore, denoting

C_{0}^{\prime}\triangleq|g(0)|\vee\Big[\sup_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}}L(x,0)\Big],

we conclude that

|\varphi_{N,\lambda}^{A}(x)|\leq\lambda^{-1}C_{0}^{\prime},\ \ x\in\Lambda_{N}.

Hence, for each
x\in\Lambda_{N}
and
i\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
,

\Big|\Delta_{N,i}^{+}\varphi_{N,\lambda}^{A}(x)\Big|,\,\Big|\Delta_{N,i}^{-}\varphi_{N,\lambda}^{A}(x)\Big|\leq 2N\lambda^{-1}C_{0}^{\prime}.

Choosing in condition
(L3)

a=2N\lambda^{-1}\sqrt{d}C_{0}^{\prime}+1
, we have that, if
x\in\Lambda_{N}
,
v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\begin{split}(-\Delta_{N})\varphi_{N,\lambda}^{A}(&x)\cdot v-L(x,v)\\
&\leq 2N\lambda^{-1}\sqrt{d}C_{0}^{\prime}|v|-(2N\lambda^{-1}\sqrt{d}C_{0}^{\prime}|v|+1)|v|-g(2N\lambda^{-1}\sqrt{d}C_{0}^{\prime}+1)\\
&=-|v|-g(2N\lambda^{-1}\sqrt{d}C_{0}^{\prime}+1).\end{split}

Hence,

(-\Delta_{N})\varphi_{N,\lambda}^{A}(x)\cdot v-L(x,v)\rightarrow-\infty\text{ as }|v|\rightarrow\infty

uniformly w.r.t.
|v|
.
Thus, there exists a constant
A_{N,\lambda}
such that, for every
A>A_{N,\lambda}
,

\max_{|v|\leq A}\Big[(-\Delta_{N})\varphi_{N,\lambda}^{A}(x)\cdot v-L(x,v)\Big]=\sup_{v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big[(-\Delta_{N})\varphi_{N,\lambda}^{A}(x)\cdot v-L(x,v)\Big].

Therefore, if
A>A_{N,\lambda}
, the function
\varphi_{N,\lambda}^{A}
satisfies

\lambda\varphi_{N,\lambda}^{A}(x)+\sup_{v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big[(-\Delta_{N})\varphi_{N,\lambda}^{A}(x)\cdot v-L(x,v)\Big]=0.

This gives the existence of solution to (
19
). The fact that
\pi_{\lambda,N}^{*}
is optimal, i.e., the fact that equality (
21
) and inequality (
22
) are valid, can be proved using the standard verification arguments. They also give the uniqueness of the solution of (
19
).
∎

5
Approximation of a solution of the stationary Hamilton-Jacobi equation

The aim of this section is to prove the following approximation result.

Theorem 5.1
.

There exists a constant
C_{1}
depending only on the Lagrangian
L
such that if
u_{\lambda}
,
\varphi_{N,\lambda}
are solutions of (
1
) and (
19
) respectively, while
x\in\Lambda_{N}
, one has that

|u_{\lambda}(x)-\varphi_{N,\lambda}(x)|\leq C_{1}\lambda^{-3/2}N^{-1/2}.

The proof of this statement relies on several auxiliary statements.

Lemma 5.2
.

There exist constants
c_{0}
and
c_{1}
depending only on
L
such that, if

\lambda>0
and
u_{\lambda}
is a viscosity solution of (
1
), then, for every
x,y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
,

1.

\lambda|u_{\lambda}(x)|\leq c_{0}
;

2.

|u_{\lambda}(x)-u_{\lambda}(y)|\leq c_{1}|x-y|
.

Proof.

To prove the first statement, we use condition
(L3)
. It gives that

L(x,v)\geq g(0).

Since
u_{\lambda}
is a value function for the discounting control problem (
3
), (
4
), we have that

\lambda u_{\lambda}(z)\geq\lambda\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\lambda t}g(0)dt=g(0).

Simultaneously,

\lambda u_{\lambda}(z)\leq\lambda\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\lambda t}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}}L(x,0)dt=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}}L(x,0).

Letting

c_{0}\triangleq g(0)\vee\Big[\sup_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}}L(x,0)\Big],

we derive the first statement.

To prove the second statement, we argue as in the proof of
[
3
, Proposition 4.1]
. We set

c_{1}\triangleq c_{0}+\sup_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d},|v|=1}L(x,v)+1,

fix
x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
and consider a test function
\phi(y)\triangleq c_{1}|y-x|
. We wish to prove that the maximum of
u_{\lambda}(y)-\phi(y)
attains at
x
. Indeed, in the converse case, let
\hat{y}
be a point where the maximum of
u_{\lambda}(y)-\phi(y)
is achieved. Since we assume that
\hat{y}\neq x
, the function
\phi
is differentiable at
\hat{y}
with the derivative equal to
c_{1}\frac{\hat{y}-x}{|\hat{y}-x|}
. Using the fact that
u_{\lambda}
is a solution of (
1
), we have that

\lambda u_{\lambda}(\hat{y})+H\Bigg(\hat{y},-c_{1}\frac{\hat{y}-x}{|\hat{y}-x|}\Bigg)\leq 0.

Plugging in the right-hand side of the Hamiltonian (see (
13
))
v\triangleq-\frac{\hat{y}-x}{|\hat{y}-x|}
, we have that

c_{1}-\sup_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d},|v|=1}L(x,v)\leq c_{0}.

This contradicts with the choice of
c_{1}
. Therefore, for each
y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
,

u_{\lambda}(y)-c_{1}|y-x|\leq u_{\lambda}(x).

Interchanging the variable, we conclude that the function
u_{\lambda}
is Lipschitz continuous with the constant
c_{1}
that does not depend on
\lambda
.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\lambda>0
,
x_{*}\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
and let
(x(\cdot),v(\cdot))
be such that
v(\cdot)\in L^{1}([0,+\infty);\mathbb{T}^{d})
,
x(0)=x_{*}
,
\frac{d}{dt}x(t)=v(t)
and

u_{\lambda}(x_{*})=\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\lambda t}L(x(t),v(t))dt.

Then,

|v(t)|\leq c_{2}\text{ for a.e. }t\in[0,+\infty).

Here
c_{2}
is a constant determined only by the Lagrangian
L
.

Proof.

Let
\tau
be a Lebesgue point of the function
v(\cdot)
. For
r>0
, from the dynamic programming arguments we have that

e^{-\lambda\tau}u_{\lambda}(x(\tau))=\int_{0}^{r}e^{-\lambda(\tau+t)}L(x(\tau+t),v(\tau+t))dt+e^{-\lambda(\tau+r)}u_{\lambda}(x(\tau+r)).

Therefore,

\int_{0}^{r}e^{-\lambda t}L(x(\tau+t),v(\tau+t))dt=u_{\lambda}(x(\tau))-u_{\lambda}(x(\tau+r))+u_{\lambda}(x(\tau+r))\big[1-e^{-\lambda r}\big].

Using Lemma
5.2
, we have that

\int_{0}^{r}e^{-\lambda t}L(x(\tau+t),v(\tau+t))dt\leq c_{1}\int_{0}^{r}|v(\tau+t)|dt+c_{0}r.

(23)

Now assume that
r\leq\lambda^{-1}\ln 2
. Using this, inequality (
23
) and choosing in superlinear growth condition
(L3)

a=2c_{1}+2
, we obtain the following inequality

\begin{split}(c_{1}+&1)\int_{0}^{r}|v(\tau+t)|dt+2^{-1}g(2c_{1}+2)r\\
&\leq(2c_{1}+1)\int_{0}^{r}e^{-\lambda t}|v(\tau+t)|dt+g(2c_{1}+2)\int_{0}^{r}e^{-\lambda t}dt\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{r}e^{-\lambda t}L(x(\tau+t),v(\tau+t))dt\leq c_{1}\int_{0}^{r}|v(\tau+t)|dt+c_{0}r.\end{split}

This gives,

\int_{0}^{r}|v(\tau+t)|dt\leq\big[c_{0}-g(2c_{1}+2)/2\big]r.

Since
\tau
is a Lebesgue point of the function
v(\cdot)
, we conclude that

|v(\tau)|\leq c_{2}\triangleq\big[c_{0}-g(2c_{1}+2)/2\big].

This and the fact that almost every points of
[0,+\infty)
are Lebesgue points of the function
v(\cdot)
completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\varphi_{N,\lambda}
solve stationary Hamilton-Jacobi equation (
19
). Then, for each
x,y\in\Lambda_{N}
,
i\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
,

•

\lambda|\varphi_{N,\lambda}(x)|\leq c_{0}
for each

;

•

|\Delta_{N,i}^{+}\varphi_{N,\lambda}(x)|,\,|\Delta_{N,i}^{+}\varphi_{N,\lambda}(x)|\leq c_{3}
;

•

|\varphi_{N,\lambda}(x)-\varphi_{N,\lambda}(y)|\leq c_{4}|x-y|
.

Here,
c_{0}
is the same constant as in Lemma
5.2
,
c_{3}
and
c_{4}
are determined only by the Lagrangian
L
.

Proof.

The proof of the first statement is the same as in Lemma
5.2
.

Now let us proves the second statement. From the first statement, we have that, given
x\in\Lambda_{N}
,

\sup_{v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big[(-\Delta_{N})\varphi_{N,\lambda}(x)-L(x,v)\Big]\leq c_{0}.

If

\Delta_{N,i}^{-}\varphi_{N,\lambda}(x)\leq c_{0}+L(x,-e_{i}).

From this, using equalities (

), we arrive at the second statement of the lemma.

The third statement obviously follows from this, the definition of right and left differences (see (

∎

Lemma 5.5
.

There exists a constant

), then

From the definition of the feedback strategy

-th coordinate of the vector
\pi_{\lambda,N}^{*}(x)
. Using condition
(L3)
with

\varphi_{N,\lambda}(z)-u_{\lambda}(z)\leq C_{2}^{\prime}N^{-1/2}\int_{0}^{+\infty}t^{1/2}e^{-\lambda t}dt=C_{2}^{\prime}N^{-1/2}\lambda^{-3/2}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\alpha^{1/2}e^{-\alpha}d\alpha.

, there exists an optimal feedback strategy

that is stationary. By Lemma

,

|\pi_{\lambda,N}^{*}(x)|\leq c_{5}.

Furthermore,
we construct a filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\in[0,+\infty)},\mathbb{P})
and a process
(X^{1},X^{2})
as in Lemma
3.4
for

,
\mathcal{I}=[0,+\infty)
and
x_{*}^{1}=x^{2}_{*}=z
. Denoting

we conclude that

\frac{d}{dt}X_{t}^{1}=V_{t},\ \ \mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

Therefore,

u_{\lambda}(z)\leq\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\lambda t}L(X^{1}_{t},V_{t})dt=\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\lambda t}L(X^{1}_{t},\pi_{\lambda,N}^{*}(X^{2}_{t}))dt.

(27)

Additionally, the choice of
\pi^{*}_{\lambda,N}
gives that

\varphi_{N,\lambda}(z)=\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\lambda t}L(X^{2}_{t},\pi_{\lambda,N}^{*}(X^{2}_{t}))dt.

(28)

Taking into account the boundness of
\pi_{\lambda,N}^{*}
, Lemma
3.4
and assumption
(L1)
, we have that

\mathbb{E}|L(X^{1}_{t},\pi_{\lambda,N}^{*}(X^{2}_{t}))-L(X^{2}_{t},\pi_{\lambda,N}^{*}(X^{2}_{t}))|\leq C_{1}^{\prime}\mathbb{E}|X^{1}_{t}-X^{2}_{t}|\leq C_{3}^{\prime}t^{1/2}N^{1/2}.

Here,

is as above, while
C_{3}^{\prime}\triangleq C_{1}^{\prime}(c_{5})^{1/2}d^{1/4}
.
This, (
27
) and (
28
) give that

u_{\lambda}(z)-\varphi_{N,\lambda}(z)\leq C_{3}^{\prime}N^{-1/2}\int_{0}^{+\infty}t^{1/2}e^{-\lambda t}dt=C_{3}^{\prime}N^{-1/2}\lambda^{-3/2}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\alpha^{1/2}e^{-\alpha}d\alpha.

The conclusion of the theorem with

C_{1}\triangleq(C_{2}^{\prime}\vee C_{3}^{\prime})\cdot\int_{0}^{+\infty}\alpha^{1/2}e^{-\alpha}d\alpha

follows from this and (
26
).

6
Weak KAM theory on the lattice

In this section, we consider the weak KAM theory problem for the controlled Markov chain:
find a function
\varphi_{N}:\Lambda_{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and a constant
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{N}
such that, for each
T>0
and
z\in\Lambda_{N}
,

\begin{split}\varphi_{N}(z)=\min\Bigg\{\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{T}L(X_{t}&,V_{t})dt+\varphi_{N}(X_{T})\bigg]:\\
(\Omega&,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T]},\mathbb{P},X,V)\in\operatorname{MCP}_{N}([0,T],z)\Bigg\}+\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{N}T.\end{split}

The dynamic programming arguments give that a pair
(\varphi_{N},\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{N})
is a solution of this problem if and only if they satisfies the following weak KAM equation on the lattice
\Lambda_{N}
:

\mathcal{H}_{N}(x,(-\Delta_{N})\varphi_{N}(x))=\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{N},\ \ x\in\Lambda_{N}.

(29)

Theorem 6.1
.

For each
N
, there exists a solution of (
29
). Moreover, the constant
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{N}
is unique.

Proof.

We use a method borrowed from
[
22
]
. Due to Lemma
5.4
, one has that on
\Lambda_{N}

|\lambda\varphi_{N,\lambda}(x)|\leq c_{0},

(30)

while, for each
x,y\in\Lambda_{N}
,

|\varphi_{N,\lambda}(x)-\varphi_{N,\lambda}(y)|\leq c_{4}|x-y|.

Hereinafter,
\varphi_{N,\lambda}
is the unique solution of (
19
).

Furthermore, we fix,
z\in\Lambda_{N}
and set

\psi_{N,\lambda}(x)\triangleq\varphi_{N,\lambda}(x)-\varphi_{N,\lambda}(z).

By construction, we have that, for each
x\in\Lambda_{N}
,

|\psi_{N,\lambda}(x)|\leq C_{4}^{\prime}\triangleq c_{4}\sqrt{d}/2.

(31)

Since
\varphi_{N,\lambda}
solves (
19
), we have that

\lambda\varphi_{N,\lambda}+\mathcal{H}_{N}(x,(-\Delta_{N})\psi_{N,\lambda}(x))=0,\ \ x\in\Lambda_{N}.

(32)

From (
30
) and (
31
), it follows that there exist a sequence
\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
, a number
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{N}
and a function
\varphi_{N}:\Lambda_{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that

•

\lambda_{k}\rightarrow 0
as
k\rightarrow\infty
;

•

\lambda_{k}\varphi_{N,\lambda_{k}}\rightarrow-\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{N}
as
k\rightarrow\infty
uniformly on
\Lambda_{N}
;

•

for each
x\in\Lambda_{N}
,
\psi_{N,\lambda_{k}}(x)\rightarrow\varphi_{N}(x)
as
k\rightarrow\infty
.

Passing to the limit in (
32
), we obtain the existence of a solution to the weak KAM equation on the lattice
\Lambda_{N}
(
29
).

Now let us prove the uniqueness of the number
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{N}
. Assume that there exist two solutions of (
29
), namely,
(\varphi_{N}^{\prime}(\cdot),\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{N}^{\prime})
,
(\varphi_{N}^{\prime\prime}(\cdot),\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{N}^{\prime\prime})
.

We choose a feedback strategy
\pi^{\prime}_{N}
such that

\pi_{N}^{\prime}(x)\in\underset{v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\operatorname{Argmax}}\Big[(-\Delta_{N})\varphi^{\prime}_{N}(x)\cdot v-L(x,v)\Big].

(33)

Let
z\in\Lambda_{N}
and let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\in[0,+\infty)},\mathbb{P},X^{\prime})
be a motion produced by the strategy
\pi^{\prime}_{N}
and the initial state
z
. For each
T>0
, we have that

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}[\varphi^{\prime}(&X^{\prime}(T))-\varphi^{\prime}(z)]=\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\Delta_{N}\varphi_{N}^{\prime}(X_{t}^{\prime})dt\\
&=\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\Big[\Delta_{N}\varphi_{N}^{\prime}(X_{t}^{\prime})+L(X_{t}^{\prime},\pi^{\prime}_{N}(X_{t}^{\prime}))\Big]dt-\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}L(X_{t}^{\prime},\pi^{\prime}_{N}(X_{t}^{\prime}))dt.\end{split}

Due to the choice of the strategy
\pi^{\prime}_{N}
(see (
33
)) and the fact that
(\varphi_{N}^{\prime},\overline{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}_{N})
is a solution of the weak KAM solution, we have that

\mathbb{E}[\varphi^{\prime}(z)-\varphi^{\prime}(X^{\prime}(T))]=T\overline{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}_{N}+\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}L(X_{t}^{\prime},\pi^{\prime}_{N}(X_{t}^{\prime}))dt.

(34)

Simultaneously,

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}[\varphi^{\prime\prime}(z&)-\varphi^{\prime\prime}(X^{\prime}(T))]\\
&=-\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\Big[\Delta_{N}\varphi_{N}^{\prime\prime}(X_{t}^{\prime})+L(X_{t}^{\prime},\pi^{\prime}_{N}(X_{t}^{\prime}))\Big]dt+\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}L(X_{t}^{\prime},\pi^{\prime}_{N}(X_{t}^{\prime}))dt.\end{split}

Since, for each
x\in\Lambda_{N}
,

\begin{split}\Big[(-\Delta_{N})\varphi_{N}^{\prime\prime}(x)-L(x,\pi^{\prime}_{N}(x))\Big]&\leq\max_{v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big[(-\Delta_{N})\varphi_{N}^{\prime\prime}(x)-L(x,v)\Big]\\
&=\mathcal{H}_{N}(x,(-\Delta_{N})\varphi_{N}^{\prime\prime}(x))=\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{N}^{\prime\prime},\end{split}

we have that

\mathbb{E}[\varphi^{\prime\prime}(z)-\varphi^{\prime\prime}(X^{\prime}(T))]\leq T\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{N}^{\prime\prime}+\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}L(X_{t}^{\prime},\pi^{\prime}_{N}(X_{t}^{\prime}))dt.

This and (
34
) yield that

T\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{N}^{\prime\prime}-\mathbb{E}[\varphi^{\prime\prime}(z)-\varphi^{\prime\prime}(X^{\prime}(T))]\geq T\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{N}^{\prime}-\mathbb{E}[\varphi^{\prime}(x_{0})-\varphi^{\prime}(X^{\prime}(T))].

Since that functions
\varphi_{N}^{\prime}
and
\varphi_{N}^{\prime\prime}
are bounded on
\Lambda_{N}
, dividing both parts of this inequality by
T
and passing to the limit when
T\rightarrow\infty
, we conclude that

\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{N}^{\prime\prime}\geq\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{N}^{\prime}.

The opposite inequality in proved in the same way. Thus, we conclude that
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{N}^{\prime}=\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{N}^{\prime\prime}
.
∎

T
be a positive number. We have that

\varphi_{N_{l}}(z_{l})-\mathbb{E}^{l}\varphi_{N_{l}}(X^{l,2}_{T})\leq\mathbb{E}^{l}\int_{0}^{T}L(X^{l,2}_{t},v)dt+\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{N_{l}}T.

From (
41
), Lemma
7.4
and the definition of the function
K
(see (
5
)), we conclude that

\begin{split}\varphi_{N_{l}}(&x)-\varphi_{N_{l}}(x+vT)-L(z_{l},v)T\\
&\leq\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{N_{l}}T+c_{4}(d^{1/4}|v|^{1/2}T^{1/2}N_{l}^{-1/2}+2|z_{l}-x|)\\
&{}\hskip 70.0pt+K(|v|)d^{1/4}|v|^{1/2}T^{3/2}N_{l}^{-1/2}+K(|v|)|v|T^{3/2}.\end{split}

Passing to the limit when
l\rightarrow\infty
, we obtain that

u(x)-u(x+vT)-L(x,v)T\leq\bar{H}T+K(|v|)|v|T^{3/2}.

Now let
\psi
be a smooth function such that, for some
r>0
, the mapping
\mathbb{B}_{r}(x)\ni y\mapsto u(y)-\psi(y)
attains the maximum at
x
. If
|v|T<r
, we have that

\psi(x)-\psi(x+vT)-L(x,v)T\leq\bar{H}T+K(|v|)|v|T^{3/2}.

Dividing both parts by
T
and passing to the limit when
T\rightarrow 0
, we obtain that

-\nabla\psi(x)v-L(x,v)\leq\bar{H}.

Since the choice of
v
is arbitrarily, we conclude that

H(x,-\nabla\psi(x))\leq\bar{H}.

(42)

This provides the first part of the definition of the viscosity solution.
Let us prove the second part.

As above, given
x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
, we consider a sequence
\{z_{l}\}_{l=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathbb{T}^{d}
converging to
x
such that
z_{l}\in\Lambda_{N_{l}}
. For each natural
l
, let
\pi_{N_{l}}^{*}
be such that

\pi_{N_{l}}^{*}\in\underset{v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\operatorname{Argmax}}\Big[(-\Delta_{N_{l}})\varphi_{N_{l}}(x)\cdot v-L(x,v)\Big].

Lemma
7.5
says that

|\pi_{N_{l}}^{*}|\leq c_{5}.

There exists a 6-tuple
(\Omega^{l},\mathcal{F}^{l},\{\mathcal{F}^{l}\}_{t\in[0,+\infty},\mathbb{P}^{l},X^{l,1},X^{l,2})
satisfying conditions of Lemma
3.4
for
x_{*}^{1}=x_{2}^{*}=z_{l}
and the stationary strategy
\pi_{N_{l}}^{*}
. Arguing as in the proof of the first viscosity inequality, we conclude that

\mathbb{E}^{l}|X^{1}_{t}-X^{2}_{t}|^{2}\leq\sqrt{d}c_{5}tN_{l}.

Here, as above,
\mathbb{E}^{l}
stands for the expectation corresponding to the probability
\mathbb{P}^{l}
. Due to the choice of the strategy
\pi_{N_{l}}^{*}
, we have that

\varphi_{N_{l}}(z_{l})-\mathbb{E}^{l}\varphi_{N_{l}}(X^{l,2}_{T})=\mathbb{E}^{l}\int_{0}^{T}L(X^{l,2}_{T},\pi_{N_{l}}^{*}(X^{l,2}_{t}))dt+\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{N_{l}}T.

(43)

Put

V^{l}(t)\triangleq\pi_{N_{l}}^{*}(X^{2,l}_{t})
. The construction of the processes
X^{1,l}
,
X^{2,l}
gives that

X_{t}^{1,l}=z_{l}+\int_{0}^{t}V^{l}(s)ds,\ \ \mathbb{P}\text{-a.s.}

Thanks to Lemma
7.2
, the Lipschitz continuity of the function
\hat{\varphi}_{N_{l}}
, the definition of the function
K
(see (
5
)) and equality (
43
), we have that

\begin{split}u(x)-\mathbb{E}^{l}u\Bigg(x+\int_{0}^{T}V^{l}(t)dt\Bigg)\geq&\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}L(x,V^{l}(t))dt+\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{N_{l}}T\\
-2\|u-\varphi_{N_{l}}\|&-c_{4}(2|z_{l}-x|+d^{1/4}c_{5}T^{1/2}N_{l}^{-1/2})\\
&-K(c_{5})(|z_{l}-x|+c_{5}d^{1/4}T^{3/2}N^{-1/2}_{l}+c_{5}T^{3/2}).\end{split}

Now let
\psi
be a smooth function defined in
\mathbb{B}_{r}(x)
for some
r>0
such that the mapping
\mathbb{B}_{r}(x)\ni y\mapsto u(y)-\psi(y)
attains the minimum at
x
. For
T
such that
c_{5}T<r
, we have that

\psi(x)-\mathbb{E}^{l}\psi\Bigg(x+\int_{0}^{T}V^{l}(t)dt\Bigg)\geq u(x)-\mathbb{E}^{l}u\Bigg(x+\int_{0}^{T}V^{l}(t)dt\Bigg).

Additionally, since
|V^{l}(t)|\leq c_{5}
,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s., the following inequality holds true:

H(x,-\nabla\psi(x))T\geq\psi(x)-\mathbb{E}^{l}\psi\Bigg(x+\int_{0}^{T}V^{l}(t)dt\Bigg)-\mathbb{E}^{l}\int_{0}^{T}L(x,V^{l}(t))dt+o(T),

where
o(T)/T\rightarrow 0
as
T\rightarrow 0
uniformly w.r.t.
l
. Here we used the definition of the Hamiltonian
H
. Thus,

\begin{split}H(x,-\nabla\psi(x))T\geq\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{N_{l}}T\\
-2\|u-\varphi_{N_{l}}\|&-c_{1}(2|z_{l}-x|+d^{1/4}c_{5}T^{1/2}N_{l}^{-1/2})\\
&-K(c_{5})(|z_{l}-x|+c_{5}d^{1/4}T^{3/2}N^{-1/2}_{l}+c_{5}T^{3/2})+o(T).\end{split}

Passing to the limit when
l\rightarrow\infty
, we have that

H(x,-\nabla\psi(x))T\geq\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{N_{l}}T+K(c_{5})c_{5}T^{3/2}+o(T).

Dividing this inequality by
T
and passing to the limit as
T\rightarrow 0
, we have that

H(x,-\nabla\psi(x))\geq\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{N_{l}}

for each smooth function
\psi
such that the mapping
\mathbb{B}_{r}(x)\ni y\mapsto u(y)-\psi(y)
attains the minimum at
x
. This together with (
42
) gives the fact that
u
is a viscosity solution of (
2
).
∎

8
Mather measures on
\Lambda_{N}
and their limit behavior

As in the continuous space case, the constant
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{N}
can be characterized using a Mather measure. To introduce this concept for the lattice system, we first denote by
\mathcal{M}_{N}
the set of probabilities on
\Lambda_{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
with finite integral of the function
{w}
defined by (
6
), i.e,

\mathcal{M}_{N}\triangleq\Bigg\{\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\Lambda_{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}):\int_{\Lambda_{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}{w}(v)\nu(d(x,v))<\infty\Bigg\}.

Here the function
{w}
is defined by (
6
).
Obviously,
\mathcal{M}_{N}\subset\mathcal{M}
.

Definition 8.1
.

A measure
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\Lambda_{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
is called a holonomic for the lattice
\Lambda_{N}
if, for each
\phi:\Lambda_{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, the following equality holds true:

\int_{\Lambda_{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big(\Delta_{N}\phi(x)\cdot v\big)\mu(d(x,v))=0.

Definition 8.2
.

A measure
\mu_{N}\in\mathcal{M}_{N}
is called a Mather measure for the lattice
\Lambda_{N}
provided that

•

\mu_{N}
is holonomic for the lattice
\Lambda_{N}
;

•

\begin{split}\int_{\Lambda_{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}L(x&,v)\mu_{N}(d(x,v))\\
&=\min\Bigg\{\int_{\Lambda_{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}L(x,v)\nu_{N}(d(x,v)):\ \ \nu\in\mathcal{M}_{N},\,\nu\text{ is holonomic}\Bigg\}.\end{split}

Below, we denote by
\overline{\mathbb{B}}_{c}
the closed ball of the radius
c
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
centered in the origin.

Theorem 8.3
.

There exists a constant
C_{3}
such that, for each
N
, one can find a Mather measure for the lattice
\Lambda_{N}
concentrated on
\Lambda_{N}\times\overline{\mathbb{B}}_{C_{3}}
.

Proof.

Let
(\varphi_{N},\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{N})
be a solution of the weak KAM equation on the lattice
\Lambda_{N}
. Let
\pi_{N}^{*}
be defined by (
35
). By Lemma
7.5
,
|\pi_{N}^{*}(x)|\leq c_{5}
on
\Lambda_{N}
. The Kolmogorov matrix
\mathcal{Q}^{N}[\pi_{N}^{*}]
determines a Markov chain on the lattice
\Lambda_{N}
. It is well known (see, for instance,
[
29
, Theorem 5.4.6]
) that this Markov chain has at least one stationary distribution, i.e., a sequence
\hat{m}_{N}=\{\hat{m}_{N,x}\}_{x\in\Lambda_{N}}
such that, for each
y\in\Lambda_{N}
,

\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{N}}\hat{m}_{N,x}\mathcal{Q}_{x,y}^{N}[\pi_{N}]=0.

(44)

Now let
\phi:\Lambda_{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. Taking into account the definition of the matrix
\mathcal{Q}^{N}
, from (
12
) and (
44
), we obtain that

\begin{split}0&=\sum_{y\in\Lambda_{N}}\Bigg[\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{N}}\hat{m}_{N,x}\mathcal{Q}_{x,y}^{N}[\pi_{N}^{*}]\Bigg]\phi(y)=\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{N}}\hat{m}_{N,x}\Bigg[\sum_{y\in\Lambda_{N}}\mathcal{Q}_{x,y}^{N}[\pi_{N}^{*}]\phi(y)\Bigg]\\
&=\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{N}}\hat{m}_{N,x}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\Big(\Delta_{N,i}^{+}\phi(x)(\pi_{N}^{*}(x))_{i}^{+}+\Delta_{N,i}^{-}\phi(x)(\pi_{N}^{*}(x))_{i}^{-}\Big)\\
&=\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{N}}\hat{m}_{N,x}\Big(\Delta_{N}\phi(x)\cdot(\pi_{N}^{*}(x))\Big).\end{split}

(45)

Set

\hat{\mu}_{N}\triangleq\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{N}}\hat{m}_{N,x}\delta_{(x,\pi_{N}^{*}(x))}.

(46)

Here,
\delta_{w}
stands for the Dirac measure concentrated at
w
. Due to (
45
), we have that

\int_{\Lambda_{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big(\Delta_{N}\phi(x)\cdot v\Big)\hat{\mu}_{N}(d(x,v))=0,

i.e.,
\hat{\mu}_{N}
is holonomic. Furthermore,
\hat{\mu}_{N}
is concentrated on
\Lambda_{N}\times\overline{\mathbb{B}}_{C_{3}}
for the constant
C_{3}\triangleq c_{5}
that does not depend on a number
N
.

Now let us prove that

\int_{\Lambda_{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}L(x,v)\hat{\mu}_{N}(d(x,v))=-\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{N}.

(47)

Recall that, due to the choice of the strategy
\pi_{N}^{*}
, we have that, for each
x\in\Lambda_{N}
,

\Delta_{N}\varphi_{N}(x)\cdot\pi_{N}^{*}(x)+L(x,\pi_{N}^{*}(x))=-\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{N}.

Multiplying each equality on
\hat{m}_{x}
and summing up, we obtain that

\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{N}}\Bigg[\hat{m}_{x}\big(\Delta_{N}\varphi_{N}(x)\cdot\pi_{N}^{*}(x)+L(x,\pi_{N}^{*}(x))\big)\Bigg]=-\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{N}.

Equality (
45
) and the definition of the measure
\hat{\mu}_{N}
(see (
46
)) yield that (
47
) holds true.

To complete the proof it suffices to show that, for each probability
\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{N}
that is holonomic, one has that

\int_{\Lambda_{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}L(x,v)\nu(d(x,v))\geq-\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{N}.

(48)

Indeed, given a holonomic measure
\nu
, let

m_{x}\triangleq\nu(\{x\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\})
. Further, denote by
\nu_{x}
a probability on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that, for each Borel set
\Upsilon\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}

\nu_{x}(\Upsilon)=\nu(\{x\}\times\Upsilon).

Additionally,
we define a feedback strategy
\bar{\nu}
by the rule: if
x\in\Lambda_{N}
,

\bar{\nu}(x)\triangleq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}v\nu_{x}(dv).

(49)

It is convenient to set, for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
,

\tilde{\nu}_{i}^{+}(x)\triangleq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}v^{+}_{i}\nu_{x}(dv),\ \ \tilde{\nu}_{i}^{-}(x)\triangleq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}v^{-}_{i}\nu_{x}(dv).

Obviously,

\bar{\nu}(x)\triangleq\sum_{i=1}^{d}\Big(\tilde{\nu}_{i}^{+}(x)-\tilde{\nu}_{i}^{-}(x)\Big)e_{i}.

Notice that the numbers
\tilde{\nu}_{i}^{+}(x)
and
\tilde{\nu}_{i}^{-}(x)
are nonnegative. For each
\phi:\Lambda_{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
x\in\Lambda_{N}
, the following equalities hold true

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big(\Delta_{N}\phi(x)\cdot v\Big)\nu_{x}(dv)&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\Big(\Delta_{N,i}^{+}\phi(x)\cdot v_{i}^{+}+\Delta_{N,i}^{-}\phi(x)\cdot v_{i}^{-}\Big)\nu_{x}(dv)\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\Big[\Delta_{N,i}^{+}\phi(x)\tilde{\nu}_{i}^{+}(x)+\Delta_{N,i}^{-}\phi(x)\tilde{\nu}_{i}^{-}(x)\Big]=\Delta_{N}\phi(x)\cdot\bar{\nu}(x).\end{split}

This and the definition of the sequence
m=\{m_{x}\}_{x\in\Lambda_{N}}
imply the following property for each function
\phi:\Lambda_{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
:

0=\int_{\Lambda_{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big(\Delta_{N}\phi(x)\cdot v\Big)\nu(d(x,v))=\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{N}}m_{x}\Big(\Delta_{N}\phi(x)\cdot\bar{\nu}(x)\Big).

(50)

Now recall that
(\varphi_{N},\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{N})
is a solution of (
29
). Therefore, using the definition of the Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}_{N}
, we have that

(-\Delta_{N})\varphi_{N}(x)\cdot\bar{\nu}(x)-L(x,\bar{\nu}(x))\leq\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{N}.

Taking into account equality (
50
), equality (
49
) and the convexity of the function
L
w.r.t. the second variable, we deduce the following estimates:

\begin{split}-\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{N}\leq\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{N}}m_{x}&\Big[L(x,\bar{\nu}(x))+\Delta_{N}\varphi_{N}(x)\cdot\bar{\nu}(x)\Big]\\
&=\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{N}}m_{x}L\Bigg(x,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}v\nu_{x}(dv)\Bigg)\leq\sum_{x\in\Lambda_{N}}m_{x}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}L(x,v)\nu_{x}(dv)\\
&=\int_{\Lambda_{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}L(x,v)\nu(d(x,v))\end{split}

This, in fact, is inequality (
48
). Thus,
\hat{\mu}_{N}
is a Mather measure, that is concentrated on
\Lambda_{N}\times\overline{\mathbb{B}}_{C_{3}}
.
∎

We complete this section with the statement providing the limit behavior of Mather measures. Following
[
5
,
28
]
, we put

C_{L}^{0}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})\triangleq\Bigg\{\phi\in C(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}):\ \ \sup_{(x,v)\in\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Bigg|\frac{\phi(x,v)}{{w}^{+}(v)}\Bigg|<\infty,\,\lim_{|v|\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\phi(x,v)}{{w}(v)}=0\Bigg\},

where

{w}^{+}(v)\triangleq\sup_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}}L(x,v)\vee 1.

We say that a sequence
\{\mu_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal{M}
converges
(C_{L}^{0})^{*}
-weakly to
\mu
iff, for each
\phi\in C_{L}^{0}
,

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi(x,v)\mu_{n}(d(x,v))\rightarrow\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi(x,v)\mu(d(x,v)).

Notice that if
\{\mu_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
converges to
\mu

(C_{L}^{0})^{*}
-weakly, then it converges to the same measure in the narrow sense.

The limiting properties of Mather measures are described as follows.

Theorem 8.4
.

Let

•

\{N_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
be an increasing sequence of natural numbers;

•

for each
k
,
\mu_{N_{k}}
be a Mather measure for
\Lambda_{N_{k}}
;

•

\{\mu_{N_{k}}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}

(C_{L}^{0})^{*}
-weakly converges to a measure
\mu
.

Then,
\mu
is a Mather measure on the torus.

Before the proof of this theorem, we introduce the following result.

Corollary 8.5
.

The sequence of Mather measures
\{\hat{\mu}_{N}\}_{N=1}^{\infty}
defined in Theorem
8.3
is precompact. Each its accumulation point is a Mather measure for the continuous phase space. In particular, there exists a Mather measure for the flat torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}
supported on the compact set
\Lambda_{N}\times\overline{\mathbb{B}}_{C_{3}}
.

Proof of Theorem
8.4
.

Let
\phi\in C^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
, we have that, if
x\in\Lambda_{N}
,

|\Delta_{N,i}^{+}\phi(x)-\nabla\phi(x)|,|\Delta_{N,i}^{-}\phi(x)-\nabla\phi(x)|\leq\varsigma_{N,i},

where the number
\varsigma_{N,i}
is equal to

\varsigma_{N,i}[\phi]\triangleq\sup_{\varepsilon\in[-h,h]}|\partial_{x_{i}}\phi(x+\varepsilon e_{i})-\partial_{x_{i}}\phi(x)|.

Denote

\varsigma_{N}[\phi]\triangleq\Bigg[\sum_{i=1}^{d}(\varsigma_{N,i}[\phi])^{2}\Bigg]^{1/2}.

Notice that
\varsigma_{N}[\phi]\rightarrow 0
as
N\rightarrow\infty
. Therefore, for each
\phi\in C^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
,

\Big|\Delta_{N}\phi(x)\cdot v-\nabla\phi(x)v\Big|\leq\varsigma_{N}[\phi]\cdot|v|.

We have that

\begin{split}\int_{\Lambda_{N_{k}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla\phi(x)v\mu_{N_{k}}&(d(x,v))\\
=\int_{\Lambda_{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}&\Delta_{N_{k}}\phi(x)\cdot v\mu_{N_{k}}(d(x,v))\\
&-\int_{\Lambda_{N_{k}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big[\Delta_{N_{k}}\phi(x)\cdot v-\nabla\phi(x)v\Big]\mu_{N_{k}}(d(x,v)).\end{split}

(51)

The estimate of
\Delta_{N_{k}}\phi(x)\cdot v-\nabla\phi(x)v
gives that

\int_{\Lambda_{N_{k}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big|\Delta_{N_{k}}\phi(x)\cdot v-\nabla\phi(x)v\Big|\mu_{N_{k}}(d(x,v))\leq\varsigma_{N_{k}}[\phi]\int_{\Lambda_{N_{k}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}|v|\mu_{N_{k}}(d(x,v)).

Since
L(x,v)\geq|v|+g(1)
, we have that the functions
(x,v)\mapsto|v_{i}|
and
(x,v)\mapsto|v|
lies in
C_{L}^{0}
. Thus, the integrals

\int_{\Lambda_{N_{k}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla\phi(x)v\mu_{N_{k}}(d(x,v))
,
\int_{\Lambda_{N_{k}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Delta_{N_{k}}\phi(x)v\mu_{N_{k}}(d(x,v))
are well-defined.
Using
(C_{L}^{0})^{*}
-weak convergence of the sequence
\{\mu_{N_{k}}\}
, we conclude that the quantities
\int_{\Lambda_{N_{k}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}|v|\mu_{N_{k}}(d(x,v))
are uniformly bounded. Using this, the fact that each measure
\{\mu_{N_{k}}\}
is holonomic for the lattice
\Lambda_{N_{k}}
, the convergence
\varsigma_{N_{k}}[\phi]\rightarrow 0
as
k\rightarrow\infty
, we pass to the limit in (
51
) and obtain that

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla\phi(x)v\mu(d(x,v))=0.

Thus,
\mu
is holonomic.

Since

\int_{\Lambda_{N_{k}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}L(x,v)\mu_{N_{k}}(d(x,v))=-\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{N_{k}},

using the
(C_{L}^{0})^{*}
-weak convergence of the sequence
\{\mu_{N_{k}}\}
to
\mu
and Theorem
7.1
, we arrive at the equality

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}L(x,v)\mu(d(x,v))=-\bar{H}.

This gives that
\mu
is a Mather measure for the flat torus.
∎

Proof of Corollary
8.5
.

Recall that each measure
\hat{\mu}_{N}
is concentrated on
\Lambda_{N}\times\overline{\mathbb{B}}_{C_{3}}\subset\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\overline{\mathbb{B}}_{C_{3}}
. Furthermore, the set
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\overline{\mathbb{B}}_{C_{3}})
is compact in the topology of narrow convergence. Now, to obtain the conclusion of the corollary, it suffices to notice that on
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\overline{\mathbb{B}}_{C_{3}})
the narrow convergence coincides with the
(C_{L}^{0})^{*}
-weak convergence and use Theorem
8.4
.
∎

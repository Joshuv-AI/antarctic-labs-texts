Infinitesimal reference frames suffice to determine the asymmetry properties of a quantum system
Rhea Alexander, Si Gvirtz-Chen, David Jennings

Abstract
Symmetry principles are fundamental in physics, and while they are well understood within Lagrangian mechanics, their impact on quantum channels has a range of open questions. The theory of asymmetry grew out of information-theoretic work on entanglement and quantum reference frames, and allows us to quantify the degree to which a quantum system encodes coordinates of a symmetry group. Recently, a complete set of entropic conditions was found for asymmetry in terms of correlations relative to infinitely many quantum reference frames. However, these conditions are difficult to use in practice and their physical implications unclear. In the present theoretical work, we show that this set of conditions has extensive redundancy, and one can restrict to reference frames forming any closed surface in the state space that has the maximally mixed state in its interior. This in turn implies that asymmetry can be reduced to just a single entropic condition evaluated at the maximally mixed state. Contrary to intuition, this shows that we do not need macroscopic, classical reference frames to determine the asymmetry properties of a quantum system, but instead infinitesimally small frames suffice. Building on this analysis, we provide simple, closed conditions to estimate the minimal depolarization needed to make a given quantum state accessible under channels covariant with any given symmetry group.

Introduction

Symmetry principles have been extensively studied both in classical and quantum theory, and in particular for Lagrangian dynamics of a quantum system

This more general setting not only includes unitary dynamics, open system dynamics, and the ability to vary the system dimension as it transforms, but it also interpolates between deterministic unitary dynamics and measurements that sharply collapse a quantum system. How symmetry principles constrain quantum channels is therefore a crucial question.

The study of quantum entanglement

The central question considered in this paper concerns transforming from a quantum state
\rho
to another quantum state
\sigma
under a symmetry constraint. More precisely, we address the following fundamental question:

Core Question:

When is it possible to transform
\rho_{A}
to
\sigma_{B}
under a quantum channel between systems
A
and
B
that is covariant with respect to a symmetry group
G
?

This question addresses the fundamental way in which symmetries constrain quantum theory, and turns out to be surprisingly non-trivial. One might initially conjecture that if we consider the generators
\{J_{k}\}
of the group representation
U(g)=\exp[i\sum_{k}g_{k}J_{k}]
and compute their moments
\langle J^{n}_{k}\rangle:=\mathrm{tr}[J_{k}^{n}\rho]
for a given state that this provides an answer to the above question. However, while this intuition is correct in the case of unitary dynamics on pure states, it is false more generally
Marvian and Spekkens 2014a
. In the case of the rotation group, for example, it is possible for
\langle J_{k}\rangle
to both increase and decrease under rotationally symmetric operations
Marvian and Spekkens 2014a
;
Cîrstoiu et al. 2020
.

Given that a disconnect occurs between the symmetry principle and the generators of the group as observables for mixed quantum states, one might therefore conjecture that the problem requires an additional entropic accounting, and we must supplement our analysis with the von Neumann entropy
S(\rho)
of the quantum state (or any general entropies that are a function of the spectrum of the state, such as the Rényi entropies) to determine the solution. Again, this turns out to still be insufficient, and it has been shown that even if we consider all moments of the generators and the entire spectrum of the quantum state
\rho
, this still does not answer the above question
Marvian and Spekkens 2014a
. The missing asymmetry ingredient is instead a non-trivial combination of quantum information aspects and physics specific to the symmetry group.

Recent work
Gour et al. 2018
has provided a complete set of necessary and sufficient conditions for asymmetry which fully determine the state interconversion structure with respect to a symmetry group
G
. However, this set of conditions turns out to not be particularly intuitive and moreover forms an infinite set of conditions that must be checked. The present work unpacks these conditions, determines the minimal set of conditions needed and obtains conditions that could be used in practical situations.

I.1
Main results of the paper

The complete set of asymmetry conditions in
Gour et al. 2018
are framed in terms of correlations between the given quantum system
A
and quantum frame systems
R
that is locally in a state
\eta_{R}
that transforms non-trivially under the group action. These correlations are measured via the single-shot conditional entropy measure
H_{\rm min}(R|A)
, which is a central quantity in quantum encryption
Renner 2005
. However, the problem is that the complete set of conditions requires this entropy to be computed for
all
possible reference frame states
\eta_{R}
, and so the question is whether one can reduce to a much simpler set of conditions for asymmetry theory.

The main results of this work are as follows:

1.

We prove that it suffices to consider any closed surface
\partial{\cal D}
of reference frame states
\eta_{R}
that contains the maximally mixed state in its interior.

2.

We prove that under
\varepsilon
–smoothing a finite number of reference states suffice to determine those states accessible under
G
–covariant channels.

3.

We prove that infinitesimal reference frames suffice to specify asymmetry, and interconversion under
G
–covariant channels is equivalent to a single entropic minimality condition at the maximally mixed state.

4.

We derive
O(d^{2})
closed conditions to estimate the minimal depolarization noise needed to make any given output state
\sigma
accessible from a state
\rho
under
G
–covariant channels. These essentially take the form

\displaystyle\log||\sigma^{\lambda}_{j}||_{1}-D_{2}(\rho^{\lambda}_{j}||{\cal G}(\rho))\leq f(d,p),

(1)

where
\sigma^{\lambda}_{j},\rho^{\lambda}_{j}
are asymmetry modes with respect to the group
Marvian and Spekkens 2014b
, and
f(d,p)
is a function depending on the output system dimension, its irrep structure, and the level of depolarization. The function
D_{2}(X||Y)
is a generalization of the Sandwiched
\alpha=2
Rényi divergence
Müller-Lennert et al. 2013
;
Wilde et al. 2014
.

Results (1–3) show that the structure of reference frame states that determine the asymmetry properties of a system has a range of freedoms. In particular, result (3) is surprising because it is contrary to what is expected from previous work on this topic. Previously, it was natural to conjecture that in order to specify the asymmetry of a state one should make use of reference frame states that encode a group element as distinguishably as possible.
Finally, result (4) exploits the general structure analysed to provide conditions, and could find application in describing symmetry-constrained quantum information in concrete settings.

II
Symmetry constraints and relational physics

Quantum entanglement
Horodecki et al. 2009
is usually understood as associated with a pre-order
\succ_{e}
on quantum states, defined by a class of quantum channels called Local Operations and Classical Communications (LOCC). The set of states that can be generated under LOCC are called separable states, and any other state is then said to have non-trivial entanglement. The pre-order is defined as
\rho\succ_{e}\sigma
if and only if we can transform from
\rho
into
\sigma
via an LOCC channel. This provides the resource-theoretic formulation of entanglement.

This general perspective on properties of quantum systems can be used in the above problem on transforming between quantum states under a symmetry constraint. Specifically, we can identify a symmetry ordering
\succ
on quantum states, defined now by
\rho\succ\sigma
whenever it is possible to transform from
\rho
into
\sigma
via a quantum channel that respects a given symmetry group
G
. The symmetry pre-order then
defines
what it means for one quantum state to be more asymmetric than another with respect to the group
G
.

We can make this precise in the following way. Given a quantum system
A
, with associated Hilbert space
{\cal H}_{A}
, we denote by
{\cal B}({\cal H}_{A}
) the space of bounded linear operators on
{\cal H}_{A}
. A symmetry group
G
acts on the system via a unitary representation
U(g)
on
{\cal H}_{A}
. States of
A
are positive, trace-one operators
\rho\in{\cal B}({\cal H}_{A})
, and at the level of the density operator
\rho
the symmetry group acts as
{\cal U}_{g}(\rho):=U(g)\rho U(g)^{\dagger}
. A quantum channel
{\cal E}:{\cal B}({\cal H}_{A})\rightarrow{\cal B}({\cal H}_{B})
is a completely-positive, trace-preserving map
Watrous 2018
that sends states of an input system
A
to states of some output system
B
. A quantum channel
{\cal E}
is then said to be symmetric, or
G
–covariant, with respect to a group action if
{\cal E}(U(g)\rho U(g)^{\dagger})=U(g){\cal E}(\rho)U(g)^{\dagger}
for all
g\in G
and all states
\rho
of the input system. Expressed purely in terms of composition of channels this amounts to

[{\cal U}_{g},{\cal E}]=0\mbox{ for all }g\in G.

(2)

We then have that
\rho\succ\sigma
when there is a
G
–covariant channel
{\cal E}
such that
\sigma={\cal E}(\rho)
. Moreover, a
measure
of the system’s asymmetry is any real-valued function
{\cal M}
on quantum states such that if
\rho\succ\sigma
then it must be the case that
{\cal M}(\rho)\geq{\cal M}(\sigma)
.

A number of measures of asymmetry have been developed, such as relative entropy measures
Vaccaro et al. 2008
;
Gour et al. 2009
, the skew-Fisher information
Marvian Mashhad 2012
;
Marvian and Spekkens 2014a
;
Takagi 2019
, and the purity of coherence
Marvian 2020
. Any such monotone
{\cal M}(\rho)
provides a
necessary
condition for a transformation to be possible. However, what is a harder question is whether one can determine a
sufficient
set of monotones. Any such set of measures would encode all the features of the quantum system that relate to the symmetry constraint.

Very recently
Gour et al. 2018
just such a complete set of measures has been found, in terms of single-shot entropies. The monotones appearing in these relations are the quantum conditional min-entropies
Renner 2005
, which are defined, for some state
\Omega_{RA}
on a bipartite system
RA
, as:

\displaystyle H_{\mathrm{min}}(R|A)_{\Omega}:=-\log\inf_{X_{A}\geq 0}\{\mathrm{tr}[X_{A}]:\mathbbm{1}_{R}\otimes X_{A}\geq\Omega_{RA}\},

where the infimum ranges over all positive semidefinite operators
X_{A}
on Hilbert space
{\cal H}_{A}
. For any state
\rho_{A}
a complete set of measures is then given by

\displaystyle H_{\eta}(\rho)\coloneqq H_{\mathrm{min}}(R|A)_{{\cal G}(\eta\otimes\rho)}.

(3)

where
\eta_{R}
is an arbitrary quantum state on an
external
reference frame system
R
, and the single-shot entropy is evaluated on the bipartite state

{\cal G}(\eta_{R}\otimes\rho_{A}):=\int\!\!dg\,\,{\cal U}_{g}(\eta_{R})\otimes{\cal U}_{g}(\rho_{A}).

(4)

In terms of transformations between quantum states under a
G
–covariant channel, we now have the following result.

Theorem 1
.

Gour et al. 2018

Let
A
,
B
, and
R
be three quantum systems with respective Hamiltonians
H_{A}
,
H_{B}
and
H_{R}
and dimensions
d_{A}
,
d_{B}
and
d_{R}
. Furthermore, let the reference system
R
be such that
d_{R}=d_{B}
and
H_{R}=-(H_{B})^{T}
. The state transformation
\rho_{A}\rightarrow\sigma_{B}
is possible under a
G
–covariant operation if and only if

\Delta H_{\eta}\geq 0,

(5)

for all states
\eta_{R}
on
{\cal H}_{R}
, where we have defined

\displaystyle\Delta H_{\eta}=H_{\eta}(\sigma)-H_{\eta}(\rho),

(6)

as the difference in entropy between input and output systems.

As shown in the original paper, the infinite set of entropic conditions outlined in Theorem
1
can be reformulated as a semi-definite program that can be solved efficiently for sufficiently low-dimensional quantum systems. However, for larger system sizes it quickly becomes computationally intensive. Moreover, without simplification, working with these expressions analytically is not an option, since we have an infinite set of conditions and the physics involved remains hidden.

II.1
Appearance of relativistic features in the quantum-information framework

In quantum gravity, one has the Wheeler-de Witt equation
DeWitt 1967
that provides a Hamiltonian constraint
{H|\Psi\rangle=0}
for a global wavefunction
|\Psi\rangle
. This in particular implies global time-translation covariance, and raised the question of how observed dynamics are consistent with this condition. One answer to this question was presented by Page and Wootters
Page and Wootters 1983
, who argued that time-evolution of subsystems should be properly viewed in terms of relational correlations between subsystems.

The above complete set of entropic conditions for general covariant transformations has links with this formalism. In particular, the following features appear from the quantum information-theoretic treatment when specialised to
G
being the time-translation group
Gour et al. 2018
:

•

External reference frame systems automatically appear in the information-theoretic analysis.

•

The Hamiltonians on
R
and
B
obey
H_{R}+H_{B}^{T}=0
as matrices.

•

The properties of any system
A
that transform non-trivially under the symmetry group are fully described by correlations between
R
and
A
.

•

A
single-shot
Page–Wootters condition emerges in the classical reference frame limit in terms of optimal guessing-probabilities of the time parameter.

•

Local gauge symmetries can be formulated with a causal structure on asymmetry resources
Cirstoiu and Jennings 2017
.

The appearance of a Page–Wootters condition is surprising. The classical limit here is when reference frame
\eta
acts as a good clock, in the sense that one can encode the classical information
t
into it in such a way that one can discriminate between two different values
t_{1}
and
t_{2}
with high probability.

For this regime, the state
\Omega_{RA}={\cal G}(\eta_{R}\otimes\rho_{A})
tends to a classical-quantum state, with
R
behaving as a classical ‘register’ for
t
. However, it can be shown that for a classical quantum state
\Omega_{RA}
, where
R
is classical, the single-shot entropy corresponds exactly to an optimal guessing probability
Konig et al. 2009
. More precisely, it can be proved that

-\log H_{\rm min}(R|A)_{\Omega}=p_{\rm opt}(t),

(7)

where
p_{\rm opt}(t)
is the optimal guessing probability for the value of
t
, over all generalised POVM measurements, given the state
U(t)\rho U(t)^{\dagger}
. This provides a refinement of the Page–Wootters formalism.

This interpretation of the
H_{\rm min}(R|A)_{\Omega}
terms extends to arbitrary states on
R
. In the fully general case it quantifies the optimal singlet fraction
Konig et al. 2009
, the degree to which the state
\Omega_{RA}
can be transformed to a maximally entangled (perfectly correlated) state through action on
A
alone. It is also possible to include thermodynamics into this setting without much complication. For this extension, if we consider varying the state
\eta_{R}
we also smoothly interpolate between free energy–like conditions and clock conditions
Gour et al. 2018
.

The above features come solely from the single-shot quantum-information formalism of the problem, and show that these aspects are fundamental. It therefore motivates a deeper analysis of the complete set of entropic conditions with the aim of unpacking the physical content and determining the minimal information-theoretic conditions that describe fully general symmetric transformations of quantum systems.

II.2
Warm-up example: a curious dependence on reference frame states

It is useful to first illustrate special cases of the
H_{\rm min}(R|A)
conditions for the elementary case of channels sending a single qubit to a single qubit under a
U(1)
covariant symmetry constraint. For concreteness we take this to be time-translation
U(t)=e^{itH_{A}}
under a qubit Hamiltonian
H_{A}=\sigma_{z}
, where
(\sigma_{x},\sigma_{y},\sigma_{z})
are the Pauli matrices for the qubit system.

We must therefore consider an auxiliary qubit reference frame
R
in a state
\eta_{R}
with
H_{R}=-\sigma_{z}
, and compute the conditional min-entropy on the joint state

{\cal G}(\eta_{R}\otimes\rho_{A})=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\!\!\frac{dt}{2\pi}\,{\cal U}_{t}(\eta_{R})\otimes{\cal U}_{t}(\rho_{A}).

(8)

We also choose
\rho_{A}=\frac{1}{2}(\mathbbm{1}+\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{x}+\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{z})
and look at how each choice of reference frame state
\eta_{R}
constrains the region of quantum states accessible under time-covariant quantum channels. For fixed input state
\rho
, we define the set

{\cal T}_{\eta}:=\{\sigma_{A}:H_{\eta}(\sigma)\geq H_{\eta}(\rho)\}.

(9)

In other words,
{\cal T}_{\eta}
is the region of quantum states the reference frame state
\eta_{R}
classes as admissible for time-covariant transformations. As such,
\sigma\in{\cal T}_{\eta}
constitutes a necessary, but not sufficient, condition on the pre-order
\rho\succ\sigma
.

The natural first choice for a reference frame state is
\eta_{R}=|+\rangle\langle+|
; with a uniform superposition over energy eigenstates, this is in a sense the ‘best’ clock state one can find for the qubit in that it can encode a single bit of data about the parameter
t
, which is the maximum allowed by the Holevo bound
Watrous 2018
. The region
S_{\eta}
in this case is plotted in Fig. (
1
)(c).

We now consider other choices of reference frame states, and find a surprising result. If we take
\eta_{R}=|\varphi\rangle\langle\varphi|
with
|\varphi\rangle

|0\rangle
or

We also find the following striking result: the accessible region is exactly recovered if we consider just
two

\eta_{0}=|\varphi_{0}\rangle\langle\varphi_{0}|
and
\eta_{1}=|\varphi_{1}\rangle\langle\varphi_{1}|
, where
\eta_{0}
is infinitesimally close to
|0\rangle\langle 0|
and
\eta_{1}
infinitesimally close to

(a proof may be found in Appendix

). However, if we took the reference frame states to be exactly equal to these pure symmetric states, then
{\cal T}_{\eta}
is the entire Bloch sphere, and the constraints determined by the reference frame state disappear completely!

This suggests that the constraints coming from the reference frame, via the correlations in the state
\Omega(\rho_{A})
, have a non-trivial and counter-intuitive dependence on the state
\eta_{R}
. However, this simple example also illustrates that there are significant redundancies in the entropic set of conditions – we have reduced from having to compute infinitely many conditions to just two conditions. The question then becomes whether such features carry over to more general situations, and to what degree can we reduce the set of reference frames so as to determine the minimal relational data needed to specify the asymmetry of a quantum system with respect to a general group
G
.

III
Sufficient surfaces of reference frame states for a general group
G

We shall begin our analysis by reducing the set of reference frames needed substantially and establishing high-level results. These shed light on the structure of the problem and lead to our tractable set of conditions in Section
V
.

III.1
Basic reference frame redundancies

The entropic conditions in Theorem
1
are over-complete, and contain a large number of redundancies.
Firstly, given two reference states
\eta_{0}
and
\eta_{1}
on
R
such that

\displaystyle\eta_{1}={\cal V}(\eta_{0}),

(10)

for some unitary channel
{\cal V}_{R}
such that

[{\cal V}_{R}\otimes\text{id}_{A},{\cal G}]=0,

(11)

then it can be shown (see Appendix
B.1
) that
{\Delta H_{\eta_{0}}=\Delta H_{\eta_{1}}}
for all possible input and output states. This invariance is a special case of the following lemma, which we prove in Appendix

Lemma 2
.

Let
{\cal V}_{R}:{\cal B}({\cal H}_{R})\rightarrow{\cal B}({\cal H}_{R^{\prime}})

{\cal W}_{A}:{\cal B}({\cal H}_{A})\rightarrow{\cal B}(H_{A^{\prime}})
be local isometries that jointly commute with the

\displaystyle[{\cal V}_{R}\otimes{\cal W}_{A},{\cal G}]=0.

(12)

We then have:

\displaystyle H_{{\cal V}(\eta)}({\cal W}(\rho))=H_{\eta}(\rho)

(13)

for any pair of quantum states
\eta
and
\rho
on
{\cal H}_{R}
and
{\cal H}_{A}
respectively.

A second set of redundancies comes from considering the asymmetric modes of the input state. Let the asymmetric modes of a state

\displaystyle\text{ modes}(\sigma)\subseteq\text{modes}(\rho),

III.2
Necessary and sufficient surfaces of reference frame states

It turns out that the entropic set of conditions have a more non-obvious kind of redundancy. Any reference state

\displaystyle\eta(\bm{x})\coloneqq\frac{\mathbbm{1}}{d}+\sum_{k=1}^{d^{2}-1}x_{k}X_{k},

where

and

.

(Sufficient surfaces of states).

be any closed

that has

is possible under a

Combined with the redundancies of Sec.

III.3
A finite set of reference states under
\varepsilon
–smoothing

It is natural to consider a ‘smoothed’ version of the asymmetry conditions in which we are limited to some
\varepsilon
-ball resolution around states
Renner 2005
;
Tomamichel 2012
, where an
\varepsilon
-ball
around a state
\tau
is defined by

{\cal B}_{\varepsilon}(\tau)\coloneqq\{\tilde{\tau}\in{\cal B}({\cal H}):D(\tau,\tilde{\tau})\leq\varepsilon\},

(17)

and
D
is the generalized trace distance
Tomamichel et al. 2010
. This is physically motivated by the fact that in any practical experimental scenario, two states that are in a sense close cannot be distinguished up to some finite precision in the measurement apparatus. Here we prove that, if we allow for some
\varepsilon

To perform the
\varepsilon
-smoothing over the reference system, we need the following lemma, which states that the entropic relations are continuous functions of the reference frame state.

Lemma 4
.

For any
\tilde{\eta}\in{\cal B}_{\varepsilon}(\eta)
, we have

\displaystyle|\Delta H_{\tilde{\eta}}-\Delta H_{\eta}|\leq\frac{2d_{R}^{2}\varepsilon}{\ln 2(1-2\varepsilon)}.

(18)

Note that if we further restrict
\tilde{\eta}
to normalised states and define
r(\varepsilon)\coloneqq\frac{2d_{R}^{2}\varepsilon}{\ln 2}
this simplifies to

\displaystyle|\Delta H_{\tilde{\eta}}-\Delta H_{\eta}|\leq r(\varepsilon).

(19)

Proof.

A proof is given in Appendix
E.1
.
∎

Therefore, an
\varepsilon
-variation in the choice of the reference state
\eta
corresponds to an
\varepsilon
-small variation in the entropy difference
\Delta H_{\eta}
.

A corollary of Theorem
3
is that it is sufficient to consider only reference frame states of the form Eq. (
15
) that live on the surface of a sphere about the maximally mixed state. It can be shown
Tkocz 2019
;
Ledoux and Talagrand 1991
that there exists a finite
\varepsilon
-net
\{\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\dots,\eta_{N}\}
covering this set of reference states. This gives rise to the following result:

Theorem 5
.

Given any
\varepsilon>0
resolving scale, there is a finite set of reference frame states
{\cal N}\coloneqq\{\eta_{k}\}_{k=1}^{N}
with
N=O\left((1+\frac{1}{\varepsilon})^{d^{2}-1}\right)
that leads to the following cases:

•

if
\Delta H_{\eta_{k}}<0
for any
\eta_{k}\in{\cal N}
then
\rho\rightarrow\sigma
is forbidden under all
G
–covariant quantum channels.

•

if
\Delta H_{\eta_{k}}\geq r(\varepsilon)
for all
\eta_{k}\in{\cal N}
then
\rho\rightarrow\sigma
under a
G
–covariant quantum channel.

•

For each
\eta_{k}\in{\cal N}
that has
0\leq\Delta H_{\eta_{k}}<r(\varepsilon)
we can obtain
O(\varepsilon)
upper bound estimates of the minimal asymmetry resources needed to realise the transformation.

A proof is provided in Appendix
E.2
.

This implies that the entropic relation can be checked on a finite number of reference frames states, and furthermore each individual reference frame state can give us some information.

The above result is potentially of interest in numerical studies of low-dimensional systems. However, it does not shed much additional light on the structure of covariant state transformations. Therefore, instead of developing this line further here, we look at a limiting regime of reference frame states that make things clearer. This in turn lead to a more user-friendly set of conditions for ‘smoothed’ interconversions in Section
V
.

IV
Infinitesimal reference frames and a single minimality condition for asymmetry

While we have a finite number of conditions for smoothed asymmetry, these are, by construction, of only approximate validity, and are not very physically informative. The surface condition of Theorem
3
reduces the problem significantly, but still leaves us with an infinite set of reference frames to check.

However we can always take the region
{\cal D}
in Theorem
3
to be an arbitrarily small region around the maximally mixed state, and so restrict to reference frame states that are arbitrarily close to being trivial, and this does not affect the completeness of the set of reference frames. This then shows that the counter-intuitive features we highlighted in the qubit case are in fact generic and appear for any dimension and any group action.

A statement of this is as follows.

Theorem 6
.

Given systems
A
and
B
, it is possible to transform a quantum state
\rho_{A}
of
A
into a state
\sigma_{B}
of
B
under a
G
–covariant quantum channel if and only if
\Delta H_{\eta}
has a local minimum at
\eta_{R}=\frac{\mathbbm{1}}{d}
.

Proof.

We first note that
\Delta H_{\eta}=0
whenever
\eta_{R}
is symmetric (see Lemma
11
in the appendices), and therefore
\Delta H_{\eta}=0
when
\eta_{R}=\frac{\mathbbm{1}}{d}
. If we assume
\rho_{A}\xrightarrow{G}\sigma_{B}
, namely
\rho_{A}
can be transformed into
\sigma_{B}
via a
G
–covariant channel, then Theorem
1
implies that
\Delta H_{\eta}
has a global minimum at
\eta_{R}=\frac{\mathbbm{1}}{d}
, which must therefore be a local minimum as well. Conversely, if we assume
\Delta H_{\eta}
has a local minimum at
\eta_{R}=\frac{\mathbbm{1}}{d}
, then there exists a neighbourhood
{\cal D}
around
\eta_{R}=\frac{\mathbbm{1}}{d}
in which
\Delta H_{\eta}\geq\Delta H_{\mathbbm{1}/d}=0
. The conditional entropies are continuous in
\eta_{R}
, so we have
\Delta H_{\eta}\geq 0
on
\partial{\cal D}
as well. We conclude by Theorem
3
that
\rho_{A}\xrightarrow{G}\sigma_{B}
. Therefore,
\rho_{A}\xrightarrow{G}\sigma_{B}
if and only if
\eta_{R}=\frac{\mathbbm{1}}{d}
is a local minimum of
\Delta H_{\eta}
.
∎

This result is surprising, since we would expect that ‘optimal’ information would be obtained by evaluating the entropic relations on reference states that are closest to being a “classical” reference frame
Bartlett et al. 2007
, namely a state
|\phi\rangle
whose orbit
|\phi(g)\rangle:=U(g)|\psi\rangle
under
G
encodes all group elements completely distinguishably in the sense that

\displaystyle\innerproduct{\phi(g)}{\phi(g')}=\delta(g^{-1}g^{\prime}),

(20)

for all
g,g^{\prime}\in G
.

The use of such a reference frame
\ket{\phi}
allows us to ‘relativise’ all symmetries and construct
covariant
versions of every aspect of quantum theory
Bartlett et al. 2007
;
Bartlett et al. 2009
;
Marvian Mashhad 2012
;
Loveridge et al. 2017
;
Loveridge et al. 2018
;
Loveridge 2020
This is done via a relativising map

\displaystyle A\rightarrow\tilde{A}\coloneqq\int dg\ {\cal U}_{g}(A)\otimes\outerproduct{\phi(g)}{\phi(g)},

(21)

which can be viewed as a quantum to classical-quantum channel. In the limit of a classical reference frame with
\bra{\phi(g)}\ket{\phi(g')}=\delta(g^{-1}g^{\prime})
, the mapping becomes reversible via a readout from the classical register. However, for reference frame states that are not classical, the encoding is fundamentally noisy and so it is expected that the asymmetry features of a quantum state are not properly described within the encoding. Theorem
6
, however, tells us this is not the case.

This ability to restrict relational data to the case of infinitesimally small reference frames suggests that asymmetry theory admits a differential geometry description in terms of tangent space of operators at the maximally mixed state. Given that the interconversion of states under
G
–covariant channels corresponds to a local minimum condition we might also expect that asymmetry is described by information geometry
Hayashi 2006
;
Bengtsson and Życzkowski 2017
, and a single curvature computed from the
H_{\rm min}(R|A)
entropy. This would imply that the asymmetry properties of a system are fully described by a form of quantum Fisher information
Marvian Mashhad 2012
.

We find that, for our warm-up example of
G=U(1)
on a qubit, something like this does indeed occur. We show in Appendix
G.1.1
that

\rho\xrightarrow{U(1)}\sigma\mbox{ if and only if }\partial_{\theta}^{2}(\Delta H_{\eta})\geq 0,\mbox{ at }\theta=0,\pi,

(22)

where
\theta
is the angle the Bloch vector of
\eta
makes with the
Z
–axis. Therefore we reduce the problem down to checking just two conditions, framed as a curvature term in the angular direction. However, in the radial direction one does not have a smooth variation. Instead, we conjecture that under
\varepsilon
–smoothing a complete curvature condition exists in all directions with the angular directions providing the non-trivial constraint. In the next section we give explicit details on this troublesome radial behaviour.

IV.1
Conical behaviour at the maximally mixed state

We now consider the behaviour of the
H_{\eta}
entropies in the neighbourhood of the maximally mixed state. Once again, we characterise reference frame states using the co-ordinate system in Equation
15
as
\eta(\bm{x})
, where the maximally mixed state is located at
\bm{x}=\mathbf{0}
. We further define

\displaystyle\Phi_{\tau}(\bm{x})\coloneqq 2^{-H_{\eta(\bm{x})}(\tau)}.

(23)

and
\tilde{\Phi}_{\tau}(\bm{x})\coloneqq\Phi_{\tau}(\bm{x})-\Phi_{\tau}(\mathbf{0})
, which gives the difference in
\Phi_{\tau}
between the maximally mixed state to the reference state at
\bm{x}
.

As a result of Lemma
19
,
\tilde{\Phi}_{\tau}(\bm{x})
has the following properties:

Lemma 7
.

Let
\lambda\geq 0
. Then for all
\bm{x},\lambda\bm{x}\in{\cal S}
, where
{\cal S}
is the set of all co-ordinates corresponding to reference states:

\displaystyle\tilde{\Phi}_{\tau}(\lambda\bm{x})=\lambda\tilde{\Phi}_{\tau}(\bm{x})

(24)

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\tilde{\Phi}_{\tau}(\bm{x})\geq 0.

(25)

Proof.

A proof can be found at Appendix
F
.
∎

We conclude from the above lemma that
\tilde{\Phi}_{\tau}(\bm{x})
, and consequently
\Phi_{\tau}(\bm{x})
, is linearly non-decreasing in every direction out of the maximally mixed state. This means
\Phi_{\tau}(\bm{x})
will, in general, have a conical form at the maximally mixed state; as a result, unless
\Delta\Phi(\bm{x})
is completely linear, it too will have a conical form at the maximally mixed state. Using the defining relationship between
\Phi_{\tau}(\bm{x})
and the min–entropy, we further derive from Lemma
7
that, for sufficiently small
\varepsilon\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\Delta H(\varepsilon\bm{x})=d\varepsilon\Delta\Phi(\bm{x})+O(\varepsilon^{2}),

(26)

where
\Delta\Phi(\bm{x})\coloneqq\Phi_{\rho}(\bm{x})-\Phi_{\sigma}(\bm{x})
.
In the neighbourhood of the maximally mixed state, the behaviour of
\Delta H_{\eta}
is thus given by that of
\Delta\Phi
, and so in this single-shot regime we do not in general have smooth behaviour.

IV.2
Structure of
\Phi_{\tau}(x)
for simple cases

To illustrate this in practice, we now provide two examples on a qubit system for
G=U(1)
and
G=SU(2)
.

IV.2.1
The case of time–covariant
U(1)

We first present
\Phi_{\tau}
for time-covariant transformations in a non-degenerate qubit. Consider a qubit with the Hamiltonian
\sigma_{z}
. The states of this qubit are restricted to transforming among each other exclusively via channels that commute with all time translations
\{{\cal U}_{t}:t\in[0,2\pi]\}
, where
{\cal U}_{t}(\cdot)=e^{-i\sigma_{z}t}(\cdot)e^{i\sigma_{z}t}
. This set of time-translations form a unitary representation of the group
U(1)
.

We parameterise an arbitrary state
\tau
of this qubit in its energy eigenbasis as:

\displaystyle\tau\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}p_{\tau}&c_{\tau}\\
c^{*}_{\tau}&1-p_{\tau}\end{pmatrix}

(27)

We further use the (scaled) Pauli operators
\{\frac{\mathbbm{1}}{2},\frac{\sigma_{x}}{2},\frac{\sigma_{y}}{2},\frac{\sigma_{z}}{2}\}
as our basis for characterising reference frame states according to Equation
15
. The Bloch vector of a state,
(x,y,z)
, gives its co-ordinates in this basis. A direct computation (see Appendix
G.1
) gives

\Phi_{\tau}(x,y,z)=\frac{\absolutevalue{c_\tau}^{2}}{1-p_{\tau}}\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}}{4z}+\frac{z}{2}+\frac{1}{2}

(28a)

for the region
0\leq\frac{\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}}{2z}\leq\frac{1-p_{\tau}}{\absolutevalue{c_\tau}}
, and

\Phi_{\tau}(x,y,z)=\left(p_{\tau}-\frac{1}{2}\right)z+\absolutevalue{c_\tau}\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}

(28b)

for the region
\frac{\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}}{2z}\geq\frac{1-p_{\tau}}{\absolutevalue{c_\tau}}
and
\frac{\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}}{2z}\leq-\frac{p_{\tau}}{\absolutevalue{c_\tau}}
, and finally

\Phi_{\tau}(x,y,z)=-\frac{\absolutevalue{c_\tau}^{2}}{p_{\tau}}\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}}{4z}-\frac{z}{2}+\frac{1}{2}

(28c)

for the region
0\geq\frac{\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}}{2z}\geq-\frac{p_{\tau}}{\absolutevalue{c_\tau}}
. When neither
\rho
nor
\sigma
is symmetric, one can find neighbourhoods around the poles of the Bloch sphere in which
\Delta\Phi(\bm{x})
is not completely linear if and only if
\frac{\absolutevalue{c_\rho}^{2}}{1-p_{\rho}}=\frac{\absolutevalue{c_\sigma}^{2}}{1-p_{\sigma}}
and
\frac{\absolutevalue{c_\rho}^{2}}{p_{\rho}}=\frac{\absolutevalue{c_\sigma}^{2}}{p_{\sigma}}
. Since these conditions are equivalent to
\rho={\cal U}_{t}(\sigma)
for some
t
, for arbitrary choices of
\rho
and
\sigma
we almost always expect a conical singularity in
\Delta H_{\eta}
at
\eta=\frac{\mathbbm{1}}{d}
.

Figure 3:

This analysis illustrates how the original complete set of entropic conditions has many redundancies. We explicitly see the conical behaviour as
(x,y,z)\rightarrow(0,0,0)
in that

\displaystyle\Phi_{\tau}(\lambda(x,y,z))=\lambda\left(\Phi_{\tau}(x,y,z)-\frac{1}{2}\right)+\frac{1}{2},

(29)

where
\lambda
is a positive scaling factor. Furthermore, because
U(1)
is Abelian, we have that
[{\cal U}_{t}\otimes\mathbbm{1},{\cal G}]=0
for all

, so Lemma
2
implies, for any
r\geq 0
, that

\displaystyle\Phi_{\tau}(r\cos(t),r\sin(t),z)=\Phi_{\tau}(r,0,z),

(30)

and so
\Phi_{\tau}
has cylindrical symmetry around the
z
-axis.
More non-trivially, Lemma
2
may also be applied to
{{\cal X}\otimes{\cal X}}
, where
{\cal X}(\cdot)\coloneqq\sigma_{x}(\cdot)\sigma_{x}
, since
[{\cal X}\otimes{\cal X},{\cal G}]=0
. This means

\displaystyle\Phi_{{\cal X}(\tau)}(x,y,z)=\Phi_{\tau}(x,-y,-z)

(31)

According to the parameterisation of
\tau
we have chosen,
{\cal X}(\tau)
means
c_{\tau}\rightarrow c^{*}_{\tau}
and
p_{\tau}\rightarrow 1-p_{\tau}
. In this way,
\Phi_{\tau}
for reference states in the bottom half of the Bloch sphere (i.e.

) can be calculated from
\Phi_{{\cal X}(\tau)}
for reference states in the top half (
z\geq 0
).

Given any
\rho
and
\sigma
, we can look at the minimality condition at
(0,0,0)
and obtain reference frame independent conditions that recover known results
Korzekwa et al. 2016
on necessary and sufficient conditions for time-covariant transitions in a non-degenerate qubit:

\displaystyle\frac{\absolutevalue{c_\rho}^{2}}{1-p_{\rho}}\geq\frac{\absolutevalue{c_\sigma}^{2}}{1-p_{\sigma}}

\displaystyle\text{ for }p_{\sigma}\geq p_{\rho},

(32)

and

\displaystyle\frac{\absolutevalue{c_\rho}^{2}}{p_{\rho}}\geq\frac{\absolutevalue{c_\sigma}^{2}}{p_{\sigma}}

\displaystyle\text{ for }p_{\sigma}\leq p_{\rho}.

(33)

Comparing with Eq. (
28
), this means when
p_{\sigma}\geq p_{\rho}
, checking
\Delta\Phi_{\eta}\geq 0
for a
single
reference state with co-ordinates in the range

0\leq\frac{\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}}{2z}\leq\min\left\{\frac{1-p_{\rho}}{\absolutevalue{c_\rho}},\frac{1-p_{\tau}}{\absolutevalue{c_\tau}}\right\}

(34)

is sufficient to determine whether a covariant transition can occur. Similarly, when
p_{\sigma}\leq p_{\rho}
, checking
\Delta\Phi_{\eta}
for a
single
reference state with co-ordinates in the range

0\geq\frac{\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}}{2z}\geq\max\left\{-\frac{p_{\rho}}{\absolutevalue{c_\rho}},-\frac{p_{\sigma}}{\absolutevalue{c_\sigma}}\right\}

(35)

is sufficient.

IV.2.2
The case of
SU(2)
–covariant transformations.

We now consider the case of
G=SU(2)
on a qubit. In this case,
G
–covariant channels partially depolarise and may additionally invert the input state about the maximally mixed state
Cîrstoiu et al. 2020
. We will continue to write
\eta
in its Bloch basis as in the
U(1)
example, and will parameterise
\tau
as before. Using the simplifying abbreviations
\bm{\sigma}\coloneqq(\sigma_{x},\sigma_{y},\sigma_{z})^{T}
and
\bm{x}=(x,y,z)^{T}
, we have

\displaystyle\eta(\bm{x})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}(\mathbbm{1}+\bm{x}\cdot\bm{\sigma}),

(36)

\displaystyle\rho(\bm{r})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}(\mathbbm{1}+\bm{r}\cdot\bm{\sigma}),

(37)

Given
\bm{x}=(x,y,z)

\overline{\bm{x}}=(x,-y,z)
.

The form of
\Phi_{\tau}(\bm{x})
is then given by (see Appendix
G.2

\displaystyle\Phi_{\tau}(\bm{x})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}(1+\overline{\bm{x}}\cdot\bm{r})&\text{if }\overline{\bm{x}}\cdot\bm{r}\geq 0,\\
\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{1}{3}\overline{\bm{x}})\cdot\bm{r}&\mbox{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

(38)

We see that
\Phi_{\tau}(\bm{x})
is piecewise linear with the plane
{\overline{\bm{x}}\cdot\bm{r}=0}
distinguishing the two regions.

Consider an input state
\rho
and an output state
\sigma
that are not maximally mixed. Letting
\bm{r}
and
\bm{s}
be the Bloch vectors of
\rho
and
\sigma
respectively, this means
\bm{r},\bm{s}\neq\bm{0}
. Let us further restrict ourselves to the case where
\rho
and
\sigma
are not located along the same diameter in the Bloch sphere. This implies both
\bm{r}-\bm{s}\neq\bm{0}
and that
\bm{r}-\bm{s}
cannot be anti-parallel to
\bm{s}
. Therefore, it is always possible to find
\overline{\bm{x}^{\prime}}\neq\bm{0}
such that
\overline{\bm{x}^{\prime}}
lies strictly above both the plane
\bm{x}\cdot\bm{s}=0
and the plane
\bm{x}\cdot(\bm{r}-\bm{s})=0

\displaystyle\overline{\bm{x}^{\prime}}\cdot\bm{r}>\overline{\bm{x}^{\prime}}\cdot\bm{s}>0,

so
\Delta\Phi(\overline{\bm{x}^{\prime}})
must be calculated from the top solution in Eq. (
38

\displaystyle\Delta\Phi(\overline{\bm{x}^{\prime}})=\frac{1}{2}\bm{x}^{\prime}\cdot(\bm{r}-\bm{s})

(40)

Conversely,

must lie strictly below both the plane
\bm{x}\cdot\bm{s}=0
and the plane
\bm{x}\cdot(\bm{r}-\bm{s})=0

\displaystyle\Delta\Phi(\bm{0})=0

\displaystyle\neq\frac{2}{3}\bm{x}^{\prime}\cdot(\bm{r}-\bm{s})

V
Robust symmetric transformations of general states with minimal depolarization

Figure 4:
Constructing state interconversion conditions.
If, for a complete set of reference frame states
\{\eta\}
, we construct some family of covariant protocols that transform from a state
\rho
to
{\cal E}^{\eta}(\rho)
that has a higher overlap with
\eta
than
\sigma
has with
\eta
then it is possible to transform from
\rho
to
\sigma
under a
G
–covariant channel.

In principle the condition given in Theorem
6
gives a complete description of the asymmetry properties of quantum states. However, as the preceding examples have shown, standard tests for local minima are typically not applicable for the functional
\Delta H_{\eta}
, and thus computing this necessary and sufficient condition presents a technical challenge which we must leave for future study. Instead, we can adopt a more physical perspective on the problem and look for a complete set of conditions where we weaken the assumptions for the interconversion.
For example instead of
\rho\xrightarrow{G}\sigma
we could ask the question:

What is the minimal amount of depolarization noise we need to add to
\sigma
so as to make it accessible from the initial state
\rho
via a
G
–covariant channel?

Since the maximally mixed state is invariant for any group action this form applies to all symmetry groups
G
. It also incorporates robustness. Suppose, for example, that
\sigma
was essentially identical to
\rho
except it has a very small, but non-zero,
O(\varepsilon)
mode that does not appear in
\rho
. The strict conditions would say that it is impossible to transform from
\rho
to
\sigma
, yet it is clear that we only require
O(\varepsilon)
amount of depolarising noise in order to make the transformation possible. Therefore the above question is more physically relevant than the simple ‘yes/no’ question of exact interconversion.

The formulation of the problem therefore involves smoothing our output state with the maximally mixed state:

\displaystyle\sigma\rightarrow\sigma_{p}:=(1-p)\sigma+p\frac{\mathbbm{1}}{d},

(44)

where
p
is an error probability, and we wish to estimate how small
p
can be so as to make
\rho\xrightarrow{G}\sigma_{p}
possible via a covariant quantum channel. As we will see, this set of sufficient conditions has the benefit of being straightforward to compute.

We make use of two core ingredients for our results. First, note that any state
\rho
can be decomposed into independent modes of asymmetry
Marvian and Spekkens 2014b
labelled by
(\lambda,j)
:

\displaystyle\rho=\sum_{\lambda,j}\rho^{\lambda}_{j}:\,\rho^{\lambda}_{j}=\sum_{\alpha}\mathrm{tr}\left[X^{(\lambda,\alpha)\dagger}_{j}\rho\right]X^{(\lambda,\alpha)}_{j},

(45)

where
\lambda
labels an irreducible representation (irrep) of
G
,
j
labels the basis vector of the given irrep
\lambda
,
\alpha
labels any multiplicity degrees of freedom, and the set
\left\{X^{(\lambda,\alpha)}_{j}\right\}
form an orthonormal irreducible tensor operator (ITO) basis for
{\cal B}({\cal H})
(see Appendix
A
for details). We denote the trivial irrep of the group by
\lambda=0
. It was shown Ref.
Marvian and Spekkens 2014b
that every
G
–covariant operation
{\cal E}:{\cal E}(\rho)=\sigma
acts independently on the different modes of the input state such that

\displaystyle{\cal E}(\rho^{\lambda}_{j})=\sigma^{\lambda}_{j},

(46)

for any
(\lambda,j)
. In other words, a
G
–covariant quantum channel always maps any given mode of the input state to the very same mode of the output state, with no “mixing” between the different modes.

Secondly, we have the Sandwiched
\alpha
–Rényi divergence
D_{\alpha}(\rho||\sigma)
for two states
\rho,\sigma
of a quantum system, which is defined as
Müller-Lennert et al. 2013
;
Wilde et al. 2014

D_{\alpha}(\rho||\sigma):=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log\mathrm{tr}\left[\sigma^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2\alpha}}\rho\sigma^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2\alpha}}\right]^{\alpha},

(47)

whenever the support of
\rho
lies in the support of
\sigma
, and is infinite otherwise. Our results turn out to be most compactly expressed in terms of the following generalization of the
\alpha=2
Sandwiched Rényi divergence, which extends the domain of the first argument to all linear operators in the support of
\sigma
, and reproduces the standard definition when that first argument is Hermitian:

D_{2}(X||\sigma):=\log\mathrm{tr}\left(\left[\sigma^{-\frac{1}{4}}X\sigma^{-\frac{1}{4}}\right]^{\dagger}\left[\sigma^{-\frac{1}{4}}X\sigma^{-\frac{1}{4}}\right]\right).

(48)

We now have the following theorem, which gives an estimate of the minimal amount of depolarization needed in order to make a transformation possible under covariant channels.

Theorem 8
.

Let
0\leq p\leq 1
be a probability. There exists a
G
–covariant channel transforming
\rho
into
\sigma_{p}\coloneqq(1-p)\sigma+p\frac{\mathbbm{1}}{d}
if

\displaystyle D_{2}(\rho^{\lambda}_{j}||{\cal G}(\rho))\geq\log g^{\lambda}_{j}(\sigma)-\log n^{-1}\left(\lambda_{\rm min}+\frac{p}{d(1-p)}\right),

(49)

for all
\lambda\neq 0,j
, where
\lambda_{\mathrm{min}}
is the smallest non-zero eigenvalue of
{\cal G}(\sigma)
, and
g^{\lambda}_{j}(\sigma)\coloneqq\sum_{\alpha}\absolutevalue{\tr[X^{(\lambda,\alpha)\dagger}_j \sigma] }
. The operators
X^{(\lambda,\alpha)}_{j}
form an ITO basis for
{\cal B}({\cal H}_{B})
, where
{\cal H}_{B}
is the Hilbert space of the output system, and
n
is the sum of the dimensions of all distinct non-trivial irreps appearing in the representation of
G
on
{\cal B}({\cal H}_{B})
.

A proof is given in Appendix
H
, and exploits the SDP duality structure for covariant interconversion to determine an admissible range of values for
p
. This analysis is done by using a family of Pretty Good Measurement schemes
Hausladen and Wootters 1994

VI
Outlook

In this work we have shown that the recent complete set of entropic conditions for asymmetry can be greatly simplified, and more importantly, can be converted into useful forms. The fact that the reference frames that are needed to describe asymmetry can be taken to have arbitrarily small modes of asymmetry suggest that a deeper analysis should be possible in terms of differential geometry, as opposed to quantities such as the degree to which a quantum state encodes group data. We expect that this should take the form of a Fisher-like information
Marvian Mashhad 2012
;
Marvian and Spekkens 2014a
;
Takagi 2019
, and in particular it is of interest to see if it is possible to replace the
H_{\rm min}(R|A)
entropy with the conditional von-Neumann entropy
H(R|A)
, which would allow explicit analytic computations.

Beyond this, a range of other interesting questions exist. For example we have not exploited the duality relations
Konig et al. 2009
;
Tomamichel et al. 2010
between
H_{\rm min}(R|A)
and
H_{\rm max}(R|S)
where
S
is a purifying system for the state
\Omega_{RA}
. For example, for the case of time-translation symmetry the joint purified state admits two notable forms. The first is an energetic form:

\Omega_{RAS}=\sum_{E}\sqrt{p(E)}|\varphi(E)\rangle_{RA}\otimes|E\rangle_{S}

(52)

obtained from considering
\Omega_{RA}={\cal G}(\eta_{R}\otimes\rho_{A})=\sum_{E}\Pi(E)(\eta_{R}\otimes\rho_{A})\Pi(E)
as an ensemble of states over energy sectors,
\Pi(E)
being the projector onto the energy
E
subspace of
RA
. While the second is a temporal form, given by

\Omega_{RAS}=\int\!\!dt\,\,|\phi(t)\rangle_{RAS},

(53)

with
|\phi(t)\rangle_{RAS}
being a purification of
{\cal U}_{t}(\eta_{R})\otimes{\cal U}_{t}(\rho_{A})
. It would be of interest to explore these two forms and also their connection to entropic uncertainty relations.

Finally, it would also be valuable to see how the explicit conditions given by Theorem
8
and Theorem
9
could be used in concrete settings, such as for covariant quantum error-correcting codes
Faist et al. 2020
;
Woods and Alhambra 2020
;
Yang et al. 2020
, thermodynamics
Gour et al. 2018
or metrology
Hall and Wiseman 2012
. Moreover, the method of constructing these conditions can certainly be improved upon by using more detailed covariant protocols.

Appendix A
Notation and background details

To any quantum system we have an associated Hilbert space
{\cal H}
, and the set of bounded linear operators on this space denoted by
{\cal B}({\cal H})
. Given a group
G
we denote its representation on
{\cal H}
by
U(g)
and on
{\cal B}({\cal H})
as
{\cal U}_{g}
where
{\cal U}_{g}(X)=U(g)XU(g)^{\dagger}
for any
X\in{\cal B}({\cal H})
and any
g\in G
.

We define
D(\rho,\sigma)\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\norm{\rho- \sigma}_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\absolutevalue{\tr\rho- \tr\sigma}
to be the generalized trace distance between any two
\rho,\sigma\in{\cal B}({\cal H})
.
We also define
{\cal S}_{\leq}({\cal H})
to be the set of all normalized and sub-normalized quantum states on
{\cal H}
.

A quantum channel
{\cal E}:{\cal B}({\cal H}_{A})\rightarrow{\cal B}({\cal H}_{B})
, from a quantum system
A
to a quantum system
B
, is a superoperator that is both trace-preserving and completely positive
Watrous 2018
. A quantum channel is
covariant
with respect to the group action if we have that

{\cal E}({\cal U}_{g}(\rho))={\cal U}_{g}({\cal E}(\rho)),

(54)

for all quantum states
\rho\in{\cal B}({\cal H}_{A})
and all
g\in G
. Note here that the group representation of the input and output systems are generally different, and we should strictly write
{\cal U}_{g}^{A}
and
{\cal U}_{g}^{B}
for each action. However, to simplify notation, we shall use
{\cal U}_{g}
throughout, as it does not cause ambiguity in practice. The above condition can also be compactly written as
[{\cal U}_{g},{\cal E}]=0
for all
g\in G
.

The reference system
R
for
G
–covariant transformations from
A
to
B
is chosen such that the representation of
G
on
{\cal H}_{R}
, the Hilbert space of system
R
, is
dual
to its representation on
{\cal H}_{B}
.

We also make use of an irreducible tensor operator (ITO) basis. An ITO consists of a basis of operators
{X^{(\lambda,\alpha)}_{j}\in{\cal B}({\cal H})}
that have the property that

{\cal U}_{g}\left(X^{(\lambda,\alpha)}_{j}\right)=\sum_{i}v^{\lambda}_{ij}(g)X^{(\lambda,\alpha)}_{i},

(55)

where
(v^{\lambda}_{ji}(g))
are the matrix components of the
\lambda
–irrep of the group
G
on
{\cal H}
. The irrep
\lambda
may occur with multiplicities, and so we also denote this as
(\lambda,\alpha)
where
\alpha
is a multiplicity label for the irrep. Since
\{X^{(\lambda,\alpha)}_{j}\}
form a basis for
{\cal B}({\cal H})
we may decompose any operator, and in particular any quantum state as

\rho=\sum_{\lambda,j}\left(\sum_{\alpha}\mathrm{tr}\left(X^{(\lambda,\alpha)\dagger}_{j}\rho\right)X^{(\lambda,\alpha)}_{j}\right)\coloneqq\sum_{\lambda,j}\rho^{\lambda}_{j}

(56)

Since each
\rho^{\lambda}_{j}
transforms irreducibly under the group action
{\cal U}_{g}(\cdot)
, this defines a decomposition of
\rho
into modes of asymmetry. If
{\cal E}
is a covariant channel, then it does not mix modes, and so

{\cal E}(\rho^{\lambda}_{j})={\cal E}(\rho)^{\lambda}_{j},

(57)

for all
\lambda,j
. See
Marvian Mashhad 2012
;
Marvian and Spekkens 2014b
for more details.

Appendix B
Properties of min-entropies

In this section we review some useful properties of the conditional min-entropies
H_{\rm min}(R|A)_{\Omega}
and properties that relate to the case of
\Omega_{RA}
being a
G
-twirled bipartite quantum state.

It proves useful to define the functional
\Phi(M_{RA}):M_{RA}\mapsto 2^{-H_{\mathrm{min}}(R|A)_{M}}
defined on bipartite Hermitian operators
M_{RA}
:

Definition 10
.

Let
M_{RA}
be a Hermitian operator on
{\cal H}_{RA}
. Then we define the argument of the conditional min-entropy of
M_{RA}
,
\Phi(M_{RA})
, via

\displaystyle\Phi(M_{RA})\coloneqq\inf_{X_{A}\geq 0}\{\mathrm{tr}[X_{A}]\ :\ \mathbbm{1}_{R}\otimes X_{A}-M_{RA}\geq 0\}.

(58)

We note the following known properties of the functions
\Phi(\cdot)
, proofs of which can be found in
Tomamichel 2012
or are obvious from the definition.

(P1)

(Scalar multiplication).

\Phi(\lambda M_{RA})=\lambda\Phi(M_{RA})
for any
\lambda\geq 0
.

(P2)

(Convexity).

\Phi(pM_{RA}+(1-p)N_{RA})\leq p\Phi(M_{RA})+(1-p)\Phi(N_{RA})
for any
p\in[0,1]
.

(P3)

(Invariance under local isometries).
Let
\mathcal{U}_{R}\coloneqq U_{R}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{A}(\cdot)U_{R}^{\dagger}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{A}
and
\mathcal{V}_{A}\coloneqq\mathbbm{1}_{R}\otimes V_{A}(\cdot)\mathbbm{1}_{R}\otimes V_{A}^{\dagger}
be isometries on subsystems
R
and
A
respectively. Then
\Phi({\cal U}_{R}\circ{\cal V}_{A}(M_{RA}))=\Phi(M_{RA})
.

(P4)

(Local data processing inequality).
Let
{\cal E}_{R}:{\cal B}({\cal H}_{R})\rightarrow{\cal B}({\cal H}_{R^{\prime}})
be a unital CPTP map,
{\cal N}_{A}:{\cal B}({\cal H}_{A})\rightarrow{\cal B}({\cal H}_{B})
be a CPTP map, and
\mathcal{I}_{X}(\cdot)\coloneqq\mathbbm{1}_{X}(\cdot)\mathbbm{1}_{X}
denote the identity channel on system
X
. Then
\Phi\left(({\cal E}_{R}\otimes\mathcal{I}_{A})(M_{RA})\right)\leq\Phi(M_{RA})
and
\Phi((\mathcal{I}_{R}\otimes{\cal N}_{A})(M_{RA}))\leq\Phi(M_{RA})
.

We also introduce the following simplifying notation for the bipartite
{\cal G}
–twirled states:

\displaystyle\Phi_{\eta}(\tau)\coloneqq\Phi({\cal G}(\eta_{R}\otimes\tau_{A})),

(59)

and also
H_{\eta}(\tau):=-\log\Phi_{\eta}(\tau)
.

B.1
Invariance under local isometries that commute with
{\cal G}

Here we prove the following lemma from the main text, which specializes property
(P4)
to the particular form of the conditional min-entropies appearing in Theorem
1
, which are instead equivalent up to local isometries that jointly commute with the
G
-twirl on the global system.

See
2

Proof.

By a straightforward appeal to Property
(P3)
, we have

\displaystyle\Phi({\cal G}[{\cal U}_{R}(\eta)\otimes{\cal V}_{A}(\tau)])=\Phi({\cal U}_{R}\otimes{\cal V}_{A}\circ{\cal G}[\eta\otimes\tau])=\Phi({\cal G}[\eta\otimes\tau]).

(60)

Since

\displaystyle H_{{\cal U}(\eta)}({\cal V}(\rho))=H_{\eta}(\rho),

(61)

∎

B.2
Symmetric input states

Lemma 11
.

For any input state
\rho
on system
A
and reference state
\eta
on system
R
, the following identity holds

\displaystyle H_{{\cal G}(\eta)}(\rho)

\displaystyle=H_{\eta}({\cal G}(\rho))=H_{{\cal G}(\eta)}({\cal G}(\rho))

(62)

\displaystyle=-\log\norm{\G(\eta)}_{\infty}.

(63)

Proof.

The first two equalities in Eq. (
62
) straightforwardly follow from the fact that
{\cal G}({\cal G}(\eta)\otimes\rho)={\cal G}(\eta)\otimes{\cal G}(\rho)={\cal G}(\eta\otimes{\cal G}(\rho))={\cal G}({\cal G}(\eta)\otimes{\cal G}(\rho))
. To show the final equality, we first examine

\displaystyle\Phi({\cal G}(\eta)\otimes{\cal G}(\rho))

\displaystyle=\inf_{X\geq 0}\{\mathrm{tr}[X]\ :\ \mathbbm{1}\otimes X-{\cal G}(\eta)\otimes{\cal G}(\rho)\geq 0\}

(64)

Since
{\cal G}(\eta)
is Hermitian, it can be diagonalised as
{\cal G}(\eta)\coloneqq\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}\outerproduct{i}{i}
for some basis
\{\ket{i}\}
of the reference system. Working in this basis, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbbm{1}\otimes X-{\cal G}(\eta)\otimes{\cal G}(\rho)=\left(\sum_{i}\outerproduct{i}{i}\otimes X\right)-\left(\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}\outerproduct{i}{i}\otimes{\cal G}(\rho)\right)=\sum_{i}\outerproduct{i}{i}\otimes(X-\lambda_{i}{\cal G}(\rho))

(65)

Therefore,
\mathbbm{1}\otimes X-{\cal G}(\eta)\otimes{\cal G}(\rho)\geq 0
if and only if
X-\lambda_{i}{\cal G}(\rho)\geq 0
for all
i
, which in turn is true if and only if
X-\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}{\cal G}(\rho)\geq 0
, where
\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}
denotes the largest eigenvalue of
{\cal G}(\rho)
. We can lower-bound the
\mathrm{tr}[X]
needed to achieve this by

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}[X]\geq\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}\mathrm{tr}[{\cal G}(\rho)]=\lambda_{\mathrm{max}},

(66)

and this minimum can be attained simply by choosing
X\coloneqq\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}{\cal G}(\rho)
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\Phi({\cal G}(\eta)\otimes{\cal G}(\rho))=\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}=\norm{\G(\rho_A)}_{\infty}\Rightarrow H_{{\cal G}(\eta)}({\cal G}(\rho))=-\log\norm{\G(\rho)}_{\infty},

(67)

which completes the proof.∎

B.3
Dual Formulation

From Eq. (
59
), we see that
\Phi_{\eta}(\tau)
is defined via a semidefinite programme (SDP). In this subsection, we prove a lemma stating what the dual form of this SDP is, which is convenient for proving several results of this paper, including the depolarization conditions for
G
–covariant channels.

Lemma 12
.

Let
\mathcal{O}_{\rm cov}
be the set of
G
–covariant channels from input system
A
to output system
B
. Let
\eta
be state of the reference system
R
for this transformation, and
\tau
be a state of input system
A
. Then the dual formulation of
\Phi_{\eta}(\tau)
is

\displaystyle\Phi_{\eta}(\tau)=\max_{\mathcal{E}\in\mathcal{O}_{\text{\rm cov}}}\mathrm{tr}(\eta^{T}{\cal E}(\tau)),

(68)

Proof.

From Lemma 3 in
Gour et al. 2018
, we see that

\displaystyle\Phi_{\eta}(\tau)=\max_{{\cal E}\in\mathcal{O}_{\rm cov}}\bra{\phi_+}\mathbbm{1}\otimes{\cal E}[{\cal G}(\eta\otimes\tau)]\ket{\phi_+},

(69)

where
\ket{\phi_+}\coloneqq\sum_{i}\ket{ii}
for local computational bases
\{\ket{i}\}
for the reference
R
and output system
B
. We first note that
\ket{\phi_+}=\ket{vec(\id)}
, which means
Watrous 2018
that

\displaystyle U^{*}(g)\otimes U(g)\ket{\phi_+}=U^{*}(g)\otimes U(g)\ket{vec(\id)}=\ket{vec\left(U^*(g)\id U^T(g)\right)}=\ket{vec(\id)}=\ket{\phi_+},

(70)

where
U(g)
is the representation of the group element
g
on
{\cal H}_{B}
, the Hilbert space of
B
. We therefore see that
{{\cal G}(\outerproduct{\phi_+}{\phi_+})=\outerproduct{\phi_+}{\phi_+}}
, which allows us to derive:

\displaystyle\Phi_{\eta}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\max_{{\cal E}\in\mathcal{O}_{\rm cov}}\mathrm{tr}(\outerproduct{\phi_+}{\phi_+}\mathbbm{1}\otimes{\cal E}[{\cal G}(\eta\otimes\tau)])

(71)

\displaystyle=\max_{{\cal E}\in\mathcal{O}_{\rm cov}}\mathrm{tr}(\outerproduct{\phi_+}{\phi_+}{\cal G}(\eta\otimes{\cal E}[\tau]))

(72)

\displaystyle=\max_{{\cal E}\in\mathcal{O}_{\rm cov}}\mathrm{tr}\left(\outerproduct{\phi_+}{\phi_+}\left[\int dgU^{*}(g)\otimes U(g)(\eta\otimes{\cal E}[\tau])U^{T}(g)\otimes U^{\dagger}(g)\right]\right)

(73)

\displaystyle=\max_{{\cal E}\in\mathcal{O}_{\rm cov}}\mathrm{tr}\left(\left[\int dgU^{T}(g)\otimes U^{\dagger}(g)\outerproduct{\phi_+}{\phi_+}U^{*}(g)\otimes U(g)\right]\eta\otimes{\cal E}[\tau]\right)

(74)

\displaystyle=\max_{{\cal E}\in\mathcal{O}_{\rm cov}}\mathrm{tr}\left(\left[\int dg\ {\cal U}^{*}_{g^{-1}}\otimes{\cal U}_{g^{-1}}(\outerproduct{\phi_+}{\phi_+})\right]\eta\otimes{\cal E}[\tau]\right)

(75)

\displaystyle=\max_{{\cal E}\in\mathcal{O}_{\rm cov}}\mathrm{tr}({\cal G}(\outerproduct{\phi_+}{\phi_+})\eta\otimes{\cal E}[\tau])

(76)

\displaystyle=\max_{{\cal E}\in\mathcal{O}_{\rm cov}}\mathrm{tr}(\outerproduct{\phi_+}{\phi_+}\eta\otimes{\cal E}[\tau])

(77)

\displaystyle=\max_{{\cal E}\in\mathcal{O}_{\rm cov}}\sum_{ij}\bra{j}\eta\ket{i}\bra{j}{\cal E}[\tau]\ket{i}

(78)

\displaystyle=\max_{{\cal E}\in\mathcal{O}_{\rm cov}}\sum_{ij}\bra{i}\eta^{T}\ket{j}\bra{j}{\cal E}[\tau]\ket{i}

(79)

\displaystyle=\max_{{\cal E}\in\mathcal{O}_{\rm cov}}\mathrm{tr}(\eta^{T}{\cal E}[\tau]),

(80)

as claimed.
∎

B.4
Truncation of Output System

The following two Lemmas detail when one can truncate the Hilbert space of the output system without affecting the possibility of interconversion to a particular output state
\sigma
. This is of use in our analysis of state interconversion with partial depolarization.

Lemma 13
.

Let
\rho
be a state of the input system
A
, associated to the Hilbert space
{\cal H}_{A}
. Let
\sigma
be a state of the output system
B
, associated to the Hilbert space
{\cal H}_{B}
.

Let
{\cal H}_{S}
be any subspace of
{\cal H}_{B}
with the following two properties:

1.

{\cal H}_{S}
carries its own representation of
G
, i.e.
{\cal H}_{B}
can be decomposed into
{\cal H}_{B}={\cal H}_{S}\oplus{\cal H}_{A}
such that
U_{B}(g)=U_{S}(g)\oplus U_{A}(g)
.

2.

The support of
\sigma
is contained entirely within
{\cal H}_{S}
, i.e. letting
\Pi_{S}
be the projector onto
{\cal H}_{S}

\Pi_{S}\sigma\Pi_{S}=\sigma
.

Let
{\cal H}_{S}
be the Hilbert space appropriate to a new output system

truncated from

. Then there exists a
G
–covariant operation from
A
to
B
that takes

to
\sigma
if and only if there exists a
G
–covariant operation from
A
to

that takes
\rho
to
\sigma
.

Proof.

Let us first assume that there exists a
G
–covariant operation
{\cal E}_{\rm cov}:{\cal B}({\cal H}_{A})\rightarrow{\cal B}({\cal H}_{B})
such that
{{\cal E}_{\rm cov}(\rho)=\sigma}
. We then observe that

\displaystyle\Pi_{S}U_{B}(g)=\left(\mathbbm{1}_{S}\oplus 0_{A}\right)\left(U_{S}(g)\oplus U_{A}(g)\right)=U_{S}(g)\oplus 0_{A}=(U_{S}(g)\mathbbm{1}_{S})\oplus 0_{A}=U_{S}(g)\Pi_{S}

(81)

Therefore,
\Pi_{S}(\cdot)\Pi_{S}
is a covariant map from
{\cal B}({\cal H}_{B})
to
{\cal B}({\cal H}_{S})
. As a result,
\Pi_{S}[{\cal E}_{\rm cov}(\cdot)]\Pi_{S}
is a covariant operation from
{\cal B}({\cal H}_{A})

\displaystyle\Pi_{S}{\cal E}_{\rm cov}(\rho)\Pi_{S}=\Pi_{S}\sigma\Pi_{S}=\sigma.

(82)

Conversely, let us now assume that that there exists a covariant transformation
{{\cal F}_{\rm cov}:{\cal B}({\cal H}_{A})\rightarrow{\cal B}({\cal H}_{S})}
such that

. We then extend
{\cal H}_{S}
into the bigger Hilbert space

such that
{\cal H}_{S}
still forms its own representation of

, i.e.
U_{B}(g)=U_{S}(g)\oplus U_{A}(g)

can be reinterpreted as a covariant channel from

to

.

We therefore conclude that

to

as a state of

or as a state of

.
∎

Given any particular output state
\sigma

{\cal H}_{B}

without affecting the possibility of

The representation of

{\cal H}_{B}

\displaystyle{\cal H}_{B}=\bigoplus_{q}{\cal H}_{q}.

(83)

The
{\cal H}_{q}
are known as the

of
{\cal H}_{B}
, and they each carry an

{\cal H}_{q}

\displaystyle{\cal H}_{q}={\cal M}_{q}\otimes{\cal N}_{q}.

The
{\cal M}_{q}

{\cal H}_{q}

\displaystyle\left[\Pi_{q},\mathbbm{1}_{{\cal M}_{q}}\otimes\outerproduct{\psi_q}{\psi_q}\right]=0

For the same reason,
\Pi_{q}

\displaystyle\mathbbm{1}_{M_{q}}\otimes\outerproduct{\psi_q}{\psi_q}=\Pi_{q}\left(\mathbbm{1}_{{\cal M}_{q}}\otimes\outerproduct{\psi_q}{\psi_q}\right).

A subspace

{\cal H}_{B}

{\cal H}

\displaystyle{\cal G}(\eta\otimes\rho)=\sum_{i,\mu}\eta^{\mu^{*}}_{i}\otimes\rho^{\mu}_{i}.

\langle\eta^{\lambda}_{j},{\cal E}_{\mathrm{pgm}}(\rho_{j}^{\lambda})\rangle=f^{\lambda}_{j}(\rho)\langle\eta_{j}^{\lambda},\tau_{j}^{\lambda}\rangle,

(196)

where we have introduced the functions
f^{\lambda}_{j}(\rho)\coloneqq\langle\rho^{\lambda^{*}}_{j},\overline{\rho}_{j}^{\lambda^{*}}\rangle
.

Proof.

We have:

\displaystyle\langle\eta^{\lambda}_{j},{\cal E}_{\mathrm{pgm}}(\rho_{j}^{\lambda})\rangle

\displaystyle=\int dg\,\langle\eta_{j}^{\lambda},\tau(g)\rangle\langle\rho^{\lambda^{*}}_{j},\overline{\rho}(g)\rangle

(197)

\displaystyle=\int dg\,\langle\eta^{\lambda}_{j}\otimes\rho^{\lambda^{*}}_{j},\tau(g)\otimes\overline{\rho}(g)\rangle

(198)

\displaystyle=\langle\eta^{\lambda}_{j}\otimes\rho^{\lambda^{*}}_{j},\int dg\,{\cal U}_{g}(\tau\otimes\overline{\rho})\rangle

(199)

\displaystyle=\langle\eta^{\lambda}_{j}\otimes\rho^{\lambda^{*}}_{j},{\cal G}(\tau\otimes\overline{\rho})\rangle

(200)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,\mu}\langle\eta^{\lambda}_{j}\otimes\rho^{\lambda^{*}}_{j},\tau_{i}^{\mu}\otimes\overline{\rho}_{i}^{\mu^{*}}\rangle

(201)

\displaystyle=\langle\eta^{\lambda}_{j}\otimes\rho^{\lambda^{*}}_{j},\tau_{j}^{\lambda}\otimes\overline{\rho}_{j}^{\lambda^{*}}\rangle

(202)

\displaystyle=\langle\eta^{\lambda}_{j},{\tau}_{j}^{\lambda}\rangle\langle\rho^{\lambda^{*}}_{j},\overline{\rho}_{j}^{\lambda^{*}}\rangle

(203)

\displaystyle=\langle\eta^{\lambda}_{j},f^{\lambda}_{j}(\rho){\tau}_{j}^{\lambda}\rangle,

(204)

where in going from line
200
to line

we have made use of lemma

. This completes the proof.
∎

We now note the following properties of the functions

, which hold for any input state
\rho
and for all modes labelled by

(F1)

(Real-valued).

Proof of

We note that

\displaystyle f_{j}^{\lambda}(\rho)=\mathrm{tr}[\rho^{\lambda}_{j}{\cal G}(\rho)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho^{\lambda^{*}}_{j}{\cal G}(\rho)^{-\frac{1}{2}}]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}[\rho^{\lambda^{*}}_{j}{\cal G}(\rho)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho^{\lambda}_{j}{\cal G}(\rho)^{-\frac{1}{2}}]=f_{j}^{\lambda^{*}}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}[(\rho^{\lambda}_{j}{\cal G}(\rho)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho^{\lambda^{*}}_{j}{\cal G}(\rho)^{-\frac{1}{2}})^{\dagger}]=\mathrm{tr}[\rho^{\lambda}_{j}{\cal G}(\rho)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho^{\lambda^{*}}_{j}{\cal G}(\rho)^{-\frac{1}{2}}]^{*}=[f^{\lambda}_{j}(\rho)]^{*}

(206)

∎

Proof of

We begin by noting that

\displaystyle\rho^{\lambda}_{j}\mathcal{G}(\rho)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho^{\lambda^{*}}_{j}=\left(\rho^{\lambda}_{j}{\cal G}(\rho)^{-\frac{1}{4}}\right)\left(\rho^{\lambda}_{j}{\cal G}(\rho)^{-\frac{1}{4}}\right)^{\dagger}\eqqcolon AA^{\dagger},

where we have defined

∎

We first note

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda,j}\int dg\ \mathcal{U}_{g}\left(\sum_{\alpha}\mathrm{tr}({X_{j}^{(\lambda,\alpha)}}^{\dagger}\rho)X_{j}^{(\lambda,\alpha)}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda,j,j^{\prime}}\int dgv^{(\lambda)}_{j^{\prime}j}(g)\sum_{\alpha}\mathrm{tr}({X_{j}^{(\lambda,\alpha)}}^{\dagger}\rho)X_{j^{\prime}}^{(\lambda,\alpha)}

(209)

(212)

where in going from line

to line

\displaystyle f_{0}^{0}(\rho)=\mathrm{tr}[\mathcal{G}(\rho)\mathcal{G}(\rho)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{G}(\rho)\mathcal{G}(\rho)^{-\frac{1}{2}}]=\mathrm{tr}[\mathcal{G}(\rho)]=1,

H.3
Derivation of general conditions

Lemma 30
.

Let us define components
t^{(\lambda,\alpha)}_{j}\coloneqq\langle X^{(\lambda,\alpha)}_{j},\tau\rangle
and
s^{(\lambda,\alpha)}_{j}\coloneqq\langle X^{(\lambda,\alpha)}_{j},\sigma\rangle
. The state
\rho
can be transformed into
\sigma
covariantly with respect to a symmetry group
G
(i.e.
\rho\xrightarrow{G}\sigma
) if there exists a family of valid quantum states

\displaystyle\left\{\forall\theta^{(\lambda,\alpha)}_{j}\in[0,2\pi):\tau_{\Theta}=\sum_{\lambda,\alpha,j}t^{(\lambda,\alpha)}_{j}X^{(\lambda,\alpha)}_{j}\right\},

(214)

where each
(\lambda,\alpha,j)
-component of

satisfies either:

\displaystyle f^{\lambda}_{j}(\rho)t^{(\lambda,\alpha)}_{j}

\displaystyle=s^{(\lambda,\alpha)}_{j},\text{ or,}

(215)

\displaystyle f^{\lambda}_{j}(\rho)\absolutevalue{t^{(\lambda,\alpha)}_j}-\absolutevalue{s^{(\lambda,\alpha)}_j}

\displaystyle\geq 0,\quad t^{(\lambda,\alpha)}_{j}=e^{i\theta^{(\lambda,\alpha)}_{j}}\absolutevalue{t^{(\lambda,\alpha)}_j}.

(216)

Proof.

We begin with Corollary
28
and make the explicit choice for each member in our family of covariant channels
\{{\cal E}^{\eta}\}
to be a PGM-and-prepare channel
{\cal E}^{\eta}\coloneqq{\cal E}^{\eta}_{\mathrm{pgm}}
, as defined in Eq. (
192
). Combined with Lemma
29
this gives rise to the following sufficient condition on the transition
\rho\xrightarrow{G}\sigma
:

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda,j}\left\{\langle\eta^{\lambda}_{j},f_{j}^{\lambda}(\rho)\tau_{j}^{\lambda}-\sigma_{j}^{\lambda}\rangle+\langle\eta^{\lambda^{*}}_{j},f_{j}^{\lambda}(\rho)\tau_{j}^{\lambda^{*}}-\sigma_{j}^{\lambda^{*}}\rangle\right\}\geq 0,\quad\forall\eta.

(217)

We emphasise that the choice of preparation state
\tau

can
vary with
\eta
, though this dependence has been suppressed in our notation for clarity. We now introduce the simplifying notation
\bm{\mu}\coloneqq\{\lambda,\alpha,j\}
and define the following coefficients of the states
\eta,\tau,
and
\sigma
in the ITO basis
\{X^{\bm{\mu}}\}
:

\displaystyle n^{\bm{\mu}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\langle X^{\bm{\mu}},\eta\rangle=\absolutevalue{n^{\bm{\mu}}}e^{i\varphi_{\eta}^{\bm{\mu}}},

(218)

\displaystyle t^{\bm{\mu}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\langle X^{\bm{\mu}},\tau\rangle=\absolutevalue{t^{\bm{\mu}}}e^{i\varphi_{\tau}^{\bm{\mu}}},

(219)

\displaystyle s^{\bm{\mu}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\langle X^{\bm{\mu}},\sigma\rangle=\absolutevalue{s^{\bm{\mu}}}e^{i\varphi_{\sigma}^{\bm{\mu}}},

(220)

such that
\eta_{j}^{\lambda}=\sum_{\alpha}n^{\bm{\mu}}X^{\bm{\mu}}
etc.
By the hermiticity of
\eta
, we have
n^{\bm{\mu}^{*}}\coloneqq n^{(\lambda^{*},\alpha)}_{j}=\left(n^{(\lambda,\alpha)}_{j}\right)^{*}=(n^{\bm{\mu}})^{*}
etc. We further introduce the notation

\displaystyle f^{\bm{\mu}}(\rho)\coloneqq f^{\lambda}_{j}(\rho),\forall\alpha.

(221)

Substituting these definitions into Eq. (
217
) and using the orthonormality of the ITO basis,
\langle X^{\bm{\mu}},X^{\bm{\nu}}\rangle=\delta_{\bm{\mu},\bm{\nu}}
, gives the following sufficient condition:

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{\mu}}\left[n^{\bm{\mu}^{*}}\left(f^{\bm{\mu}}(\rho)t^{\bm{\mu}}-s^{\bm{\mu}}\right)+n^{\bm{\mu}}\left(f^{\bm{\mu}}(\rho)t^{\bm{\mu}^{*}}-s^{\bm{\mu}^{*}}\right)\right]\geq 0,\quad\forall\eta.

(222)

This can instead be written as

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{\mu}}\real\{n^{\bm{\mu^{*}}}\left(f^{\bm{\mu}}(\rho)t^{\bm{\mu}}-s^{\bm{\mu}}\right)\}=\sum_{\bm{\mu}}\absolutevalue{n^{\bm{\mu^*}}}\real\left\{e^{-i\varphi_{\eta}^{\bm{\mu}}}\left[f^{\bm{\mu}}(\rho)t^{\bm{\mu}}-s^{\bm{\mu}}\right]\right\}\geq 0,\quad\forall\eta.

(223)

We can always choose
X^{\bm{0}}\coloneqq\frac{\mathbbm{1}}{\sqrt{d}}
, which means all other ITO basis elements must be traceless. Since we must choose
\tau
to be a valid quantum state, this means we must assign

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}(\tau)=\sum_{\bm{\mu}}t^{\bm{\mu}}\mathrm{tr}(X^{\bm{\mu}})=\sqrt{d}t^{\bm{0}}=1.

(224)

As
\sigma
is a valid quantum state, by similar logic we conclude that
\sqrt{d}s^{\bm{0}}=1
. Due to
(F3)
, this means Eq. (
223
) reduces to

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{\mu}\neq\bm{0}}\absolutevalue{n^{\bm{\mu^*}}}\real\left\{e^{-i\varphi_{\eta}^{\bm{\mu}}}\left[f^{\bm{\mu}}(\rho)t^{\bm{\mu}}-s^{\bm{\mu}}\right]\right\}\geq 0,\quad\forall\eta.

(225)

Given any
\bm{\nu}\neq\bm{0}
, there exists a valid reference state
\eta=\sum_{\bm{\mu}}n^{\bm{\mu}}X^{\bm{\mu}}
where

\displaystyle n^{\bm{\mu}}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}&\text{ for }\bm{\mu}=\bm{0}\\
n\text{ such that }\frac{1}{2d}\geq n>0&\text{ for }\bm{\mu}=\bm{\nu},\bm{\nu^{*}}\\
0&\text{ otherwise,}\end{cases}

(226)

since these assignments evidently lead to
\eta
being Hermitian and trace 1, and we can further verify, for any pure state
\ket{\psi}
of the reference system, that

\displaystyle\bra{\psi}\eta\ket{\psi}=\sum_{\bm{\mu}}n^{\bm{\mu}}\bra{\psi}X^{\bm{\mu}}\ket{\psi}=\frac{1}{d}-n(\bra{\psi}X^{\bm{\nu}}\ket{\psi}+\bra{\psi}X^{\bm{\nu^{*}}}\ket{\psi})\geq\frac{1}{d}-2n\geq\frac{1}{d}-\frac{2}{2d}=0\Rightarrow\eta\geq 0.

(227)

The only way that Eq. (

) is if for each
\bm{\mu}
-component, we have

\displaystyle\real\left\{e^{-i\varphi_{\eta}^{\bm{\mu}}}\left[f^{\bm{\mu}}(\rho)t^{\bm{\mu}}-s^{\bm{\mu}}\right]\right\}\geq 0,\quad\forall\eta.

(228)

Recall that we are free to choose
\tau
as we like for
every

\eta
, and our aim here is to derive a set of conditions that are independent of
\eta
. For a given component
\bm{\mu}
, one possible way of satisfying Eq. (

is if we can choose a valid quantum state
\tau=\sum_{\bm{\mu}}t^{\bm{\mu}}X^{\bm{\mu}}
such that

\displaystyle f^{\bm{\mu}}(\rho)t^{\bm{\mu}}=s^{\bm{\mu}}.

(229)

Alternatively, note that we can rewrite Eq. (

\displaystyle f^{\bm{\mu}}(\rho)\absolutevalue{t^{\bm{\mu}} }\cos{( \varphi_\tau^{\bm{\mu}}-\varphi_\eta^{\bm{\mu}})}-\absolutevalue{s^{\bm{\mu}}}\cos{( \varphi_\sigma^{\bm{\mu}}-\varphi_\eta^{\bm{\mu}})}\geq 0,\quad\forall\eta.

(230)

Therefore, an alternative way of satisfying the
\bm{\mu}
-component of the full set of sufficient conditions, is to set the phases of
\tau
such that they cancel those of
\eta

\displaystyle\varphi_{\tau}^{\bm{\mu}}=\varphi_{\eta}^{\bm{\mu}},

(231)

for all
\eta
.
Substituting this phase choice on
\tau
into the sufficient condition in Eq. (

) gives

\displaystyle f^{\bm{\mu}}(\rho)\absolutevalue{t^{\bm{\mu}} }-\absolutevalue{s^{\bm{\mu}}}\cos{( \varphi_\sigma^{\bm{\mu}}-\varphi_\eta^{\bm{\mu}})}\geq 0,\quad\forall\eta.

(232)

Noting that
\max_{x}\cos{x}=1
, we see that the above equation holds for all
\eta
if

\displaystyle f^{\bm{\mu}}(\rho)\absolutevalue{t^{\bm{\mu}}}\geq\absolutevalue{s^{\bm{\mu}} },

(233)

which together with Eq. (

) gives us a second
\eta
-independent way of satisfying the
\bm{\mu}
-component of Eq. (

).
Putting back in our explicit labels
\bm{\mu}=\{\lambda,\alpha,j\}

we chose at every

\displaystyle f^{\lambda}_{j}(\rho)t^{(\lambda,\alpha)}_{j}=s^{(\lambda,\alpha)}_{j}\,\text{ or }\,f^{\lambda}_{j}(\rho)\absolutevalue{t^{(\lambda,\alpha)}_j}\geq\absolutevalue{s^{(\lambda,\alpha)}_j},\ \quad\forall\lambda,j,\alpha,

then
\rho\xrightarrow{G}\sigma
, and thus Eq. (

, as claimed. ∎

We now present the following theorem from which Theorem

in the main text follows as a corollary.

Theorem 31
.

Assume without loss of generality (Lemma
14
) that
{\cal G}(\sigma)
is full-rank. Then there exists a
G
–covariant operation transforming
\rho
into
\sigma
if

\displaystyle\lambda_{\mathrm{min}}n^{-1}f^{\lambda}_{j}(\rho)\geq g^{\lambda}_{j}(\sigma),\quad\forall\lambda\neq 0,j,

(235)

where we have defined

f^{\lambda}_{j}(\rho)\coloneqq\mathrm{tr}[\rho^{\lambda}_{j}{\cal G}(\rho)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho^{\lambda^{*}}_{j}{\cal G}(\rho)^{-\frac{1}{2}}]
,
g^{\lambda}_{j}(\sigma)\coloneqq\sum_{\alpha}\absolutevalue{\tr[X^{(\lambda,\alpha)\dagger}_j \sigma] }
,
\lambda_{\mathrm{min}}
as the smallest eigenvalue of
{\cal G}(\sigma)
, and
n
as the sum of the dimensions of all distinct non-trivial irreps appearing in the representation of
G
on
{\cal B}({\cal H}_{B})
, with
{\cal H}_{B}
being the Hilbert space of the output system.

Proof.

We begin by making an explicit choice for
\{\tau_{\Theta}\}
in Lemma
30
and proceed by showing that each
\tau_{\Theta}
corresponds to a valid quantum state.

For the
\lambda=0
irrep we always choose

\displaystyle t^{(0,\alpha)}_{0}\coloneqq s^{(0,\alpha)}_{0},\ \forall\alpha,

(236)

which corresponds to setting
{\cal G}(\tau_{\Theta})={\cal G}(\sigma)
. Due to
(F3)
, this choice guarantees that all zero mode conditions from Lemma
30
are satisfied for any given
\sigma
.

For
\lambda\neq 0
, let us choose

\displaystyle t^{(\lambda,\alpha)}_{j}\coloneqq e^{i\theta^{(\lambda,\alpha)}_{j}}c^{\lambda}_{j}\absolutevalue{s^{(\lambda,\alpha)}_j},\quad c^{\lambda}_{j}\coloneqq\frac{\lambda_{\min}}{n\left(\sum_{\alpha}\absolutevalue{s^{(\lambda,\alpha)}_j}\right)},\quad\theta^{(\lambda^{*},\alpha)}_{j}=-\theta^{(\lambda,\alpha)}_{j}

(237)

where
n
is the sum of the dimensions of all distinct non-trivial irreps appearing in the representation of
G
on
{\cal B}({\cal H}_{B})
.

If
\tau_{\Theta}
corresponds to a valid quantum state for all values of
\theta^{(\lambda,\alpha)}_{j}
, from Lemma
30
we immediately have the sufficient condition

\displaystyle f^{\lambda}_{j}(\rho)c^{\lambda}_{j}\geq 1,\forall\lambda\neq 0,j,

(238)

which, with the identification
g^{\lambda}_{j}(\sigma)\coloneqq\sum_{\alpha}\absolutevalue{s^{(\lambda,\alpha)}_j}=\sum_{\alpha}\absolutevalue{\tr[X^{(\lambda,\alpha)\dagger}_j \sigma] }
, gives the statement of the lemma.

All that is left to do then is demonstrate that these choices of coefficients
t^{(\lambda,\alpha)}_{j}
indeed correspond to a valid quantum state
\tau_{\Theta}
. We first note that the choice
t^{(0,0)}_{0}=s^{(0,0)}_{0}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}
ensures that
\tau_{\Theta}
has trace 1. Furthermore, since
\tau_{\Theta}={\cal G}(\sigma)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda,\alpha,j:\\
\lambda\neq 0\end{subarray}}t^{(\lambda,\alpha)}_{j}X^{(\lambda,\alpha)}_{j}
, where the
t^{(\lambda,\alpha)}_{j}
in the sum have been so chosen that
t^{(\lambda^{*},\alpha)}_{j}=\left(t^{(\lambda,\alpha)}_{j}\right)^{*}
,
\tau_{\Theta}
is the sum of two Hermitian operators and so is also Hermitian. All that remains is to verify that
\tau_{\theta}
is positive semidefinite, i.e.,

\displaystyle\bra{\psi}\tau_{\Theta}\ket{\psi}\geq 0,

(239)

for any pure state
\ket{\psi}
of the output system.
The left hand side of this expression can be lower bounded in the following way for any
\theta^{(\lambda,\alpha)}_{j}\in[0,2\pi)
:

\displaystyle\bra{\psi}\tau_{\Theta}\ket{\psi}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda,\alpha,j}t^{(\lambda,\alpha)}_{j}\bra{\psi}X_{j}^{(\lambda,\alpha)}\ket{\psi}

(240)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha:\lambda=0}s^{0,\alpha}_{0}\bra{\psi}X_{0}^{(0,\alpha)}\ket{\psi}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda,\alpha,j:\\
\lambda\neq 0\end{subarray}}e^{i\theta^{(\lambda,\alpha)}_{j}}c^{\lambda}_{j}\absolutevalue{s^{(\lambda,\alpha)}_j}\bra{\psi}X_{j}^{(\lambda,\alpha)}\ket{\psi}

(241)

\displaystyle\geq\bra{\psi}{\cal G}(\sigma)\ket{\psi}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda,\alpha,j:\\
\lambda\neq 0\end{subarray}}c^{\lambda}_{j}\absolutevalue{s^{(\lambda,\alpha)}_j}\absolutevalue{\bra{\psi} X_j^{(\lambda,\alpha)} \ket{\psi} }

(242)

\displaystyle\geq\lambda_{\min}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda,\alpha,j:\\
\lambda\neq 0\end{subarray}}c^{\lambda}_{j}\absolutevalue{s^{(\lambda,\alpha)}_j}

(243)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{\min}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda,\alpha,j:\\
\lambda\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{\lambda_{\min}}{n\left(\sum_{\alpha^{\prime}}\absolutevalue{s^{(\lambda,\alpha')}_j}\right)}\absolutevalue{s^{(\lambda,{\alpha})}_j}

(244)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{\min}-\sum_{\lambda\neq 0,j}\frac{\lambda_{\min}}{n}

(245)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{\min}-\lambda_{\min}=0,

(246)

where in the second inequality we have used the fact that the operators
\left\{X^{(\lambda,\alpha)}_{j}\right\}
are normalized. This confirms that
\tau_{\Theta}\geq 0
for all
\theta^{(\lambda,\alpha)}_{j}\in[0,2\pi)
, which completes the proof. ∎

Speed of Random Walks in Dirichlet Environment on a Galton-Watson Tree
Dongjian Qian, Yang Xiao

Abstract
This paper deals with a transient random walk in Dirichlet environment, or equivalently a linearly edge reinforced random walk, on a Galton-Watson tree. We compute the stationary distribution of the environment seen from the particle of an edge reinforced random walk. We obtain a formula for the speed and give a necessary and sufficient condition for the walk to have a positive speed under some moment conditions on the offspring distribution of the tree.

Introduction

An edge reinforced random walk (ERRW) is a non-Markov process that tends to favor previously visited edges, first introduced by Coppersmith and Diaconis
[
7
]
. Let
G=(V,E)
be an oriented graph and
(\alpha_{e})_{e\in E}
a set of positive deterministic weights. For two adjacent points
x,y\in V
, we denote by
(x,y)
the edge from
x
to
y

is thus written as

, where
\underline{e}
and
\overline{e}
are the tail and head of

respectively. We define the ERRW
(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
on
G
with transition probabilities

\mathbf{P}(X_{n+1}=y|X_{1},\cdots,X_{n})=\frac{\alpha_{(X_{n},y)}+N^{X}_{(X_{n},y)}(n)}{\sum_{\underline{e}=X_{n}}\alpha_{e}+N^{X}_{e}(n)}\textbf{1}_{\{(X_{n},y)\in E\}},

(1)

where
N_{e}^{X}(n):=\#\{1\leq k\leq n:(X_{k-1},X_{k})=e\}
. In words, if the process is at a vertex
x
at time
n
, it will choose for its next step some neighbour
y
with probability proportional to
\alpha_{(x,y)}+N^{X}_{(x,y)}(n)
.

By means of Polya’s urns, it is well-known that this process can be represented as a mixture of Markov chains called Random Walk in Dirichlet Environment (RWDE), a special case of random walk in random environment. Specifically, independently at each vertex

, pick a random vector with positive entries

. The joint law of

is taken to be the Dirichlet distribution with parameters
(\alpha_{e})_{\underline{e}=x}
, i.e. it has density

\frac{\Gamma(\sum_{\underline{e}=x}\alpha_{e})}{\prod_{\underline{e}=x}\Gamma(\alpha_{e})}\prod_{\underline{e}=x}(y_{e}^{\alpha_{e}-1}\textbf{1}_{\{0<y_{e}<1\}})\textbf{1}_{\{\sum_{\underline{e}=x}y_{e}=1\}}.

We call

a Dirichlet environment, and denote its distribution by
{\rm DE}(\cdot)
. Given the environment
(\eta_{e})_{e\in E}
, we define the RWDE

as the Markov chain on

{{\rm P}}^{\eta}(X_{n+1}=y|X_{n}=x)=\eta(x,y),\qquad(x,y)\in E.

We write

for the quenched measure
{\rm P}^{\eta}(\cdot|X_{0}=x)
. The connection between ERRW and RWDE is as follows: the RWDE

under the annealed measure
\mathbf{P}(\cdot):=\int{{\rm P}}^{\eta}(\cdot){\rm DE}(\mathrm{d}\eta)
is an ERRW.

In the article, we focus on the case where

Preliminaries

2.1
Facts about Regeneration Time

For a random walk
(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
on a Galton-Watson tree starting from
\rho
, we call
\theta
a fresh epoch if
X_{\theta}\not=X_{n}
for all

and a regeneration epoch if additionally,
X_{\theta-1}\not=X_{n}
for all
n\geq\theta
. More specifically, let
\theta_{0}:=0,\Theta_{0}:=0
. For
k\geq 1
, let

\theta_{k}:=\inf\{n>\theta_{k-1}:X_{n}\not=X_{j},\forall 0\leq j<n\}

be the
k
-th fresh epoch, and

\Theta_{k}:=\inf\{n>\Theta_{k-1}:n\in\{\theta_{i},i\geq 1\},X_{j}\not=(X_{n})_{*},\forall j\geq n\}

(13)

be the
k
-th regeneration epoch. In
[
14
, Section 3]
, properties of these random times for biased random walk are given and these facts can be enhanced to the proposition below.

Proposition 2.1
.

For any transient random walk in random environment on a Galton-Watson tree given non-extinction, there are infinitely many regeneration epochs a.s. Moreover
\{\Theta_{k+1}-\Theta_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
are i.i.d. as are the increments
\{|X_{\Theta_{k+1}}|-|X_{\Theta_{k}}|\}_{k\geq 1}
.

We omit the proof of the proposition since it is similar to the one in
[
14
]
.

Let
\mathbb{E}_{\rho}[\cdot]=\mathbb{E}[\cdot|X_{0}=\rho]
. As proved in
[
9
]
, the speed of random walk in random environment is a.s. the deterministic constant

\displaystyle\dfrac{\mathbb{E}_{\rho}[|X_{\Theta_{2}}|-|X_{\Theta_{1}}||\mathcal{S}]}{\mathbb{E}_{\rho}[\Theta_{2}-\Theta_{1}|\mathcal{S}]},

(14)

where the numerator
\mathbb{E}_{\rho}[|X_{\Theta_{2}}|-|X_{\Theta_{1}}||\mathcal{S}]
is always finite. Therefore, the speed is positive if and only if
\mathbb{E}_{\rho}[\Theta_{2}-\Theta_{1}|\mathcal{S}]<\infty
. We sum up these results as a fact.

Fact 2.2
.

For a transient RWRE
(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
on a Galton-Watson tree, the following statements are equivalent:

1.

\lim_{n\to\infty}|X_{n}|/n
is deterministic and positive;

2.

\mathbb{E}_{\rho}[\Theta_{2}-\Theta_{1}|\mathcal{S}]<\infty
.

In particular, the speed is deterministic for RWDE, and we deduce that the speed of ERRW is identical to that of RWDE.

This section covers some basic properties of Dirichlet environment. See

Let

be a fixed positive integer and
(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{p})
be positive real numbers. We say that the random vector
D(\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{p}):=(D_{1},\cdots,D_{p})

(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{p})
if it has density

\frac{\Gamma(\sum_{j=1}^{p}\alpha_{j})}{\prod_{j=1}^{p}\Gamma(\alpha_{j})}\prod_{j=1}^{p}(x_{j}^{\alpha_{j}-1}\textbf{1}_{\{0<x_{j}<1\}})\textbf{1}_{\{\sum x_{j}=1\}}.

(15)

We denote by

the gamma distribution with parameters
\alpha
and

whose density is

\displaystyle\frac{r^{\alpha}}{\Gamma(\alpha)}x^{\alpha-1}e^{-rx}\textbf{1}_{\{x\geq 0\}}.

By computation, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 2.3
.

If
\Gamma_{i}\sim Gamma(\alpha_{i},1),1\leq i\leq p
are mutually independent random variables, then
(\Gamma_{i}/\sum_{j=1}^{p}\Gamma_{j})_{1\leq i\leq p}\sim D(\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{p})
. Besides, for
\alpha_{j}+s_{j}>0,1\leq j\leq p
,

{{\mathbf{E}}}\left[\prod_{j=1}^{p}(D_{j})^{s_{j}}\right]=\frac{\Gamma(\sum_{j=1}^{p}\alpha_{j})}{\prod_{j=1}^{p}\Gamma(\alpha_{j})}\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{p}\Gamma(\alpha_{j}+s_{j})}{\Gamma(\sum_{j=1}^{p}\alpha_{j}+\sum_{j=1}^{p}s_{j})}.

As in the beginning of the introduction, let
G=(V,E)
be an oriented graph and
(\alpha_{e})_{e\in E}
a set of positive deterministic weights. Recall that (
1
) defines an ERRW on

, a self-interacting model without the Markov property, under the measure

. For a path
\gamma

the local time of

in

Lemma 2.4

For a path

with length

(i.e. with

\mathbf{P}((X_{k})_{0\leq k\leq n}=\gamma)=\prod_{y\in V}\frac{\Gamma(\sum_{\underline{e}=y}\alpha_{e})}{\Gamma(\sum_{\underline{e}=y}\alpha_{e}+N_{e}(\gamma))}\prod_{e\in E}\frac{\Gamma(\alpha_{e}+N_{e}(\gamma))}{\Gamma(\alpha_{e})}.

The right-hand side of (

e\notin\gamma
,
N_{e}(\gamma)=0
.

Next, we consider two paths on
G
. Let
\alpha+N(\gamma)
denote the weights
(\alpha_{e}+N_{e}(\gamma))_{e\in E}
. When the graph
G
and weights
(\alpha_{e})_{e\in E}
are fixed, we define
{\rm DE}^{G}(\cdot|\alpha)
(resp.
\mathbf{E}_{DE}^{G}(\cdot|\alpha)
) as the probability measure (resp. expectation) corresponding to the Dirichlet environment on
G
with weights
(\alpha_{e})_{e\in E}
. Sometimes we write
\mathbf{E}_{DE}(\cdot|\alpha)
(resp.
\mathbf{E}_{DE}^{G}(\cdot)
) when the graph (resp. weights) is clear from the context.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be an arbitrary graph. For two paths
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}
on
G
with weight
(\alpha_{e})_{e\in E}
, we have

\mathbf{E}_{DE}[{{\rm P}}^{\eta}(\gamma_{1}){{\rm P}}^{\eta}(\gamma_{2})|\alpha]=\mathbf{P}(\gamma_{2}|\alpha+N(\gamma_{1}))\mathbf{P}(\gamma_{1}|\alpha)=\mathbf{P}(\gamma_{1}|\alpha+N(\gamma_{2}))\mathbf{P}(\gamma_{2}|\alpha).

Proof.

We only need to prove the first equality.

\begin{split}&\mathbf{E}_{DE}[{{\rm P}}^{\eta}(\gamma_{1}){{\rm P}}^{\eta}(\gamma_{2})|\alpha]=\mathbf{E}_{DE}\left[\prod_{e\in E}\eta_{e}^{N_{e}(\gamma_{1})+N_{e}(\gamma_{2})}\right]\\
&=\prod_{y\in V}\frac{\Gamma(\sum_{\underline{e}=y}\alpha_{e})}{\Gamma(\sum_{\underline{e}=y}\alpha_{e}+N_{e}(\gamma_{1})+N_{e}(\gamma_{2}))}\prod_{e\in E}\frac{\Gamma(\alpha_{e}+N_{e}(\gamma_{1})+N_{e}(\gamma_{2}))}{\Gamma(\alpha_{e})}\\
&={\mathbf{P}}(\gamma_{1}|\alpha)\prod_{y\in V}\frac{\Gamma(\sum_{\underline{e}=y}\alpha_{e}+N_{e}(\gamma_{1}))}{\Gamma(\sum_{\underline{e}=y}\alpha_{e}+N_{e}(\gamma_{1})+N_{e}(\gamma_{2}))}\prod_{e\in E}\frac{\Gamma(\alpha_{e}+N_{e}(\gamma_{1})+N_{e}(\gamma_{2}))}{\Gamma(\alpha_{e}+N_{e}(\gamma_{1}))}\\
&={\mathbf{P}}(\gamma_{2}|\alpha+N(\gamma_{1})){\mathbf{P}}(\gamma_{1}|\alpha).\end{split}

∎

Lemma
2.5
shows that, in the same environment, the information one walk gives to the other is visualized as edge local times added to the weights after averaging the environment.

For completeness, we give a specific account of conditions on transience of RWDE on a Galton-Watson tree. Recall that
A_{i},1\leq i\leq\nu
are, according to (
2
), ratios of Dirichlet random variables.
Lyons and Pemantle
[
12
]
show that a random walk in random environment is transient if and only if
\inf_{t\in[0,1]}\mathbb{E}[\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}A_{i}^{t}]>1
, that is in our case

\inf_{t\in[0,1\wedge\alpha_{p}]}\frac{\Gamma(\alpha_{p}-t)\Gamma(\alpha_{c}+t)}{\Gamma(\alpha_{c})\Gamma(\alpha_{p})}>\frac{1}{m}.

(17)

By taking the logarithm derivative with respect to
t
on the left-hand side, we get
-\psi(\alpha_{p}-t)+\psi(\alpha_{c}+t)
, where
\psi:=\Gamma^{\prime}/\Gamma
is a strictly increasing function named digamma function. Hence the minimum is attained at
t=0\vee\frac{\alpha_{p}-\alpha_{c}}{2}\wedge 1
. We consider three cases:
\alpha_{p}\leq\alpha_{c}
,
\alpha_{p}\geq\alpha_{c}+2
and
\alpha_{p}\in(\alpha_{c},\alpha_{c}+2)
separately.

1.

When
\alpha_{p}\leq\alpha_{c}
, the minimum is reached at
0
and RWDE is always transient.

2.

When
\alpha_{p}\geq\alpha_{c}+2
, the minimum is reached at
1
, so RWDE is transient if and only if
\alpha_{p}<m\alpha_{c}+1
. In this case,
\alpha_{c}\leq\frac{1}{m-1}
always implies recurrence.

3.

Finally let us focus on the last case
\alpha_{p}\in(\alpha_{c},\alpha_{c}+2)
, where the minimum is reached at
\frac{\alpha_{p}-\alpha_{c}}{2}
. We plug
t=\frac{\alpha_{p}-\alpha_{c}}{2}
into (
17
) with
\alpha_{c}
fixed, obtaining a function
g(\alpha_{p})
. By taking the logarithm derivative of
g(\alpha_{p})
with respect to
\alpha_{p}
, we have

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\alpha_{p}}\log g(\alpha_{p})=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\alpha_{p}}\log\left(\frac{\Gamma(\frac{\alpha_{p}+\alpha_{c}}{2})^{2}}{\Gamma(\alpha_{p})\Gamma(\alpha_{c})}\right)=\psi\left(\frac{\alpha_{p}+\alpha_{c}}{2}\right)-\psi(\alpha_{p})\leq 0.

At once we see that the minimum of
g(\alpha_{p})
for all
\alpha_{p}\in[\alpha_{c},\alpha_{c}+2]
is reached at
\alpha_{p}=\alpha_{c}+2
. We take
\alpha_{p}=\alpha_{c}+2
and then
g(\alpha_{p})=g(\alpha_{c}+2)>\frac{1}{m}
becomes
\alpha_{c}>\frac{1}{m-1}
. Therefore, when
\frac{1}{m-1}<\alpha_{c}<\alpha_{p}<\alpha_{c}+2
, the walk is always transient. When
\alpha_{c}\leq\frac{1}{m-1}
there will be a critical point
\phi_{0}(\alpha_{c})
such that
g(\phi_{0}(\alpha_{c}))=\frac{1}{m}
. The function
g(\alpha_{p})
is decreasing with respect to
\alpha_{p}
, so the walk is transient only when
\alpha_{p}<\phi_{0}(\alpha_{c})
.

In summary, we have the following proposition.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be a random walk in an
(\alpha_{p},\alpha_{c})
-Dirichlet environment on a Galton-Watson tree conditioned on non-extinction.

(a)

When
\alpha_{c}>\frac{1}{m-1}
, if
\alpha_{p}<m\alpha_{c}+1
then RWDE is transient and otherwise recurrent;

(b)

when
\alpha_{c}\leq\frac{1}{m-1}
, if
\alpha_{p}<\phi_{0}(\alpha_{c})
then RWDE is transient and otherwise recurrent, where
\phi_{0}(\alpha_{c})\in(\alpha_{c},\alpha_{c}+2]
satisfies

\frac{\Gamma(\frac{\phi_{0}(\alpha_{c})+\alpha_{c}}{2})^{2}}{\Gamma(\phi_{0}(\alpha_{c}))\Gamma(\alpha_{c})}=\frac{1}{m}.

Following Neveu

, let

if

as a subset of

s.t.

1.

\rho\in T
,

2.

if

, then
x_{*}\in T
,

3.

if
x=\rho i_{1}\cdots i_{n}\in T\backslash\{\rho\}
, then any word
\rho i_{1}\cdots i_{n-1}j
with

belongs to
T
.

Given

, we write

if
x=y
or
y=xj_{1}j_{2}\cdots j_{n}
i.e.
x
is an ancestor of
y
. Set

if
x\preceq y
and
x\not=y
. We create a new tree called

by adding a vertex
\rho_{*}
to

as the parent of
\rho
. For all

\rho_{*}\prec\rho\preceq x
.

Then we define a double tree

introduced in
[
2
, Section 2.2]
. Intuitively, we first pick two trees
T^{-}
and
T^{+}
with root
\rho^{-}
and
\rho^{+}
, and then artificially connect
\rho^{-}
and
\rho^{+}
. See Figure

. We use the double tree to study the environment seen from the particle. Suppose a transient random walk starts from the root
\rho
and moves on the tree
T
. After a fairly long time, the walk would be far away from the root, and the tree seen from the current location looks like a double tree
T^{-}\rightleftharpoons T^{+}
. The starting point of the walk is somewhere high on
T^{-}
. We call the random double tree a Galton-Watson double tree if
\mathbb{T}^{-}
and
\mathbb{T}^{+}
are i.i.d Galton-Watson trees.

Now we represent vertices on a double tree by two sets of words
\mathcal{U}^{+}
and
\mathcal{U}^{-}
. Specifically, we denote by
\rho^{+}i_{1}\cdots i_{n}\in\mathcal{U}^{+}
(resp.
\rho^{-}i_{1}\cdots i_{n}\in\mathcal{U}^{-}
) the vertex on the double tree
T^{-}\rightleftharpoons T^{+}\subset\mathcal{U}^{-}\cup\mathcal{U}^{+}
corresponding to
\rho i_{1}\cdots i_{n}
on
T^{+}
(resp.
T^{-}
). If
x\in T^{+}\backslash\{\rho^{+}\}
(resp.
T^{-}\backslash\{\rho^{-}\}
), we set the parent of
x
, also denoted by
x_{*}
, as its parent on
T^{+}
(resp.
T^{-}
). We also assume that
(\rho^{+})_{*}=\rho^{-}
and
(\rho^{-})_{*}=\rho^{+}
. At last, we stress that afterward we always refer to a part of the corresponding double tree when we say
x\in T^{+}
, i.e.
x
is in the
T^{+}
part of
T^{-}\rightleftharpoons T^{+}
.

Both RWDE and ERRW require specific parameter settings called edge weights. Parameters
(\alpha_{e})_{e}
on a double tree

should satisfy

\alpha_{e}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\alpha_{p}&e=(x,x_{*})\text{ for some }x\in T^{-}\rightleftharpoons T^{+},\\
\alpha_{c}&\text{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

(18)

We say a double tree has
(\alpha_{p},\alpha_{c})
environment if the weights of edges satisfy (

For each vertex
x\in T^{-}\rightleftharpoons T^{+}
, we assign a group of Dirichlet random variables
(\eta_{e})_{\underline{e}=x}
with parameters
(\alpha_{e})_{\underline{e}=x}
to edges starting from

. Note that for an arbitrary vertex

, there is only one edge starting from

having weight
\alpha_{p}
.

The transition law of ERRW is determined by the trajectory of the walk. We call a sequence of words
\gamma=(y_{i})_{a<i<b},y_{i}\in\mathcal{U}^{-}\cup\mathcal{U}^{+}
a path if
y_{i}
and
y_{i-1}
are adjacent for
a<i-1<i<b
, where
a,b\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty,\infty\}
. We say
\gamma
is left-finite (resp. right-finite) if
a>-\infty
(resp.
b<\infty
). Here we do not care about specific indices of the path, i.e.
(y_{i})_{i\leq 0}
and
(y_{i-1})_{i\leq 1}
are considered the same. We define

\Omega_{\gamma}:=\{\gamma=(y_{i})_{i\leq 0},y_{0}=\rho^{+};\exists M,s.t.\text{ for }i<M,y_{i}=\rho^{-}y_{i}^{(1)}\dots y_{i}^{(n_{i})}\text{ and }n_{i}\rightarrow\infty\text{ as }i\rightarrow-\infty\}.

The set
\Omega_{\gamma}
contains paths coming from one infinite end of
T^{-}
.

For a right-finite
\gamma^{(a)}
and a left-finite
\gamma^{(b)}
, such that the last word of
\gamma^{(a)}
is adjacent to the first word of
\gamma^{(b)}
, we can define their concatenation, denoted by
\gamma^{(a)}\ast\gamma^{(b)}
. More precisely, if
\gamma^{(a)}=(y^{(a)}_{i})_{i\leq 0}
and
\gamma^{(b)}=(y^{(b)}_{i})_{i\geq 0}

\gamma^{(a)}\ast\gamma^{(b)}:=(\dots,y^{(a)}_{-1},y^{(a)}_{0},y^{(b)}_{0},y^{(b)}_{1},\dots)
. We also define

as the reverse of a path

: if

, then

.

\displaystyle=\prod_{e\in R_{x}\backslash\{(\rho,\rho_{*}),(x_{*},x)\}}\frac{\Gamma(\alpha_{\check{e}}+N_{\check{e}}(\gamma))}{\Gamma(\alpha_{\check{e}})}

\displaystyle=\prod_{e\in R_{x}\backslash\{(\rho,\rho_{*}),(x_{*},x)\}}\frac{\Gamma(\alpha_{e}+N_{e}(\gamma))}{\Gamma(\alpha_{e})}.

The path

except for the start and end, which implies

. Till now, by canceling the corresponding terms, we only have

left on the left-hand side of (
19

\begin{split}\frac{\alpha_{(x_{*},x)}}{\alpha_{(\rho_{*},\rho)}}&\frac{\Gamma(\alpha^{\prime}_{\Psi_{x}((\rho,\rho_{*}))}+N_{(\rho_{*},\rho)}(\gamma))}{\Gamma(\alpha^{\prime}_{\Psi_{x}((\rho,\rho_{*}))})}=\frac{\alpha_{(x_{*},x)}}{\alpha_{(\rho_{*},\rho)}}\frac{\Gamma(\alpha_{(\rho_{*},\rho)}+1)}{\Gamma(\alpha_{(\rho_{*},\rho)})}=\alpha_{(x_{*},x)}.\end{split}

The proof is complete
∎

3.3
Asymptotic distribution of the environment seen from a particle

The main result of this section is Theorem

which implies that, under the condition (

defined in the introduction, renormalized to be a probability measure. We follow the strategy in

We first state a lemma similar to
[
2
, Lemma 4.4]
.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
Y
be a RWDE on Galton-Watson double tree with
(\alpha_{p},\alpha_{c})
-environment, then

\begin{split}&{\rm E}_{\rho^{+}}^{\eta}\left[\sum_{l\geq 0}\Phi(N_{(\rho^{-},\rho^{+})}^{Y}(l))\textbf{1}_{\{Y_{l}=\rho^{+}\}}\right]\\
=&\frac{(1-\beta(\rho^{+}))}{\eta(\rho^{+},\rho^{-})}\sum_{k\geq 0}\Phi(k)(1-\beta(\rho^{+}))^{k}(1-\beta(\rho^{-}))^{k}.\end{split}

Proof.

Recall the definition of
\beta(x)
in (
5
) and observe that

\displaystyle{\rm E}_{\rho^{+}}^{\eta}\left[\sum_{l\geq 0}\Phi(N^{Y}_{(\rho^{-},\rho^{+})}(l))\textbf{1}_{\{Y_{l}=\rho^{+}\}}\right]=\sum_{k\geq 0}\Phi(k)\sum_{l\geq 0}{\rm P}_{\rho^{+}}^{\eta}(Y_{l}=\rho^{+},N_{(\rho^{+},\rho^{-})}^{Y}(l)={k}).

We can define the stopping times

\displaystyle s_{k}:=\inf\{l\geq 0:N_{(\rho^{+},\rho^{-})}^{Y}(l)={k}\}

and
t_{k}:=\inf\{l\geq s_{k}:Y_{l}=\rho^{+}\}
. We have a formula for conductance obtained by Markov property that

\frac{1}{\beta(x)}=1+\frac{\eta(x,x_{*})}{\sum_{i=1}^{\nu(x)}\eta(x,xi)\beta(xi)}.

(27)

Then by the Markov property at
t_{k}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{l\geq 0}{\rm P}_{\rho^{+}}^{\eta}(Y_{l}=\rho^{+},N_{(\rho^{-},\rho^{+})}^{Y}(l)={k})={\rm E}_{\rho^{+}}^{\eta}\left[\textbf{1}_{\{t_{k}<\infty\}}\sum_{l=t_{k}}^{s_{{k}+1}}\textbf{1}_{\{Y_{l}=\rho^{+}\}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm P}_{\rho^{+}}^{\eta}(t_{k}<\infty){\rm E}_{\rho^{+}}^{\eta}\left[\sum_{l=0}^{s_{1}}\textbf{1}_{\{Y_{l}=\rho^{+}\}}\right]=(1-\beta(\rho^{-}))^{k}(1-\beta(\rho^{+}))^{k}\frac{1}{1-{\rm P}_{\rho^{+}}^{\eta}(\tau_{\rho^{+}}<s_{1})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-\beta(\rho^{-}))^{k}(1-\beta(\rho^{+}))^{k}\frac{1}{\eta(\rho^{+},\rho^{-})+\sum_{i=1}^{\nu(\rho^{+})}\eta(\rho^{+},\rho^{+}i)\beta(\rho^{+}i)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-\beta(\rho^{-}))^{k}(1-\beta(\rho^{+}))^{k}\frac{1-\beta(\rho^{+})}{\eta(\rho^{+},\rho^{-})},

where the last equality comes from the formula (
27
). The proof is complete.
∎

In order to describe the limit distribution, we construct the law for two dependent ERRW on a double Galton-Watson tree. We first sample a double Galton-Watson tree
\mathbb{T}^{-}\rightleftharpoons\mathbb{T}^{+}
with weight
\alpha
defined in (
18

(resp

) starting from
\rho^{-}
(resp.
\rho^{+}
). In other words, we first run an ERRW
(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
, add the local time of
(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
to the initial weight

and run another ERRW

. We call the probability measure constructed above

and denote by

Recall that the law of an ERRW can be viewed as the annealed law of a RWDE. From Lemma
2.5
, we can view

as the annealed law of two independent RWDE on a double Galton-Watson tree. Recall the definition of

). By independence of

and

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{(\rho^{-},\rho^{+})}\left[\frac{\beta(\rho^{-})(1-\beta(\rho^{+}))}{\eta(\rho^{+},\rho^{-})}\sum_{k\geq 0}\Phi(k)(1-\beta(\rho^{+}))^{k}(1-\beta(\rho^{-}))^{k}\right].

\begin{split}\mathscr{C}=&\mathbb{E}_{(\rho^{-},\rho^{+})}\left[\sum_{l\geq 0}\Phi(N_{(\rho^{-},\rho^{+})}^{Y}(l))\textbf{1}_{\{\tau^{X}_{\rho^{+}}=\infty\}}\textbf{1}_{\{Y_{l}=\rho^{+}\}}\right].\end{split}

(28)

\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\textbf{1}_{\mathcal{S}}}{(\beta^{(n)})^{k}}\right]\leq 1+N^{k}+C(N)\lambda_{\epsilon}\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\textbf{1}_{\mathcal{S}}}{(\beta^{(n-1)})^{k}}\right]+C(N)\epsilon^{-k},

where
\lambda_{\epsilon}:={\mathbb{E}}\left[A^{-k}\right]f^{\prime}(q)+\delta<1/C(N)
as long as
\epsilon
is small enough and
N
large enough. Finally,

\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\textbf{1}_{\mathcal{S}}}{(\beta^{(n)})^{k}}\right]\leq(1+C(N)\epsilon^{-k}+N^{k})\frac{1}{(1-C(N)\lambda_{\epsilon})}+(C(N)\lambda_{\epsilon})^{n}(1-q).

We let
n
go to infinity and apply Fatou’s lemma to complete the proof.

We continue to prove the ‘only if’ part. Assume that
{\mathbb{E}}\left[A^{-k}\right]f^{\prime}(q)\geq 1
. Recall that
\mu
is the number of children of
\rho
where an infinite subtree is rooted. By (
45
), we have that conditioned on survival,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta}\geq 1+\textbf{1}_{\{\mu=1\}}\frac{1}{A_{1}\beta_{1}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta^{k}}>\textbf{1}_{\{\mu=1\}}\frac{1}{A_{1}^{k}\beta^{k}_{1}}.

By taking expectations on both sides, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\beta^{-k}|\mathcal{S}\right]>f^{\prime}(q){\mathbb{E}}\left[A_{1}^{-k}\right]\mathbb{E}\left[\beta_{1}^{-k}|\mathcal{S}\right],

where
f^{\prime}(q)
comes from the fact that
\mathbb{P}(\mu=1|\mathcal{S})=f^{\prime}(q)
. Since
\beta
and
\beta_{1}
have the same distribution, and
{\mathbb{E}}[A^{-k}]f^{\prime}(q)\geq 1
by assumption, we have
\mathbb{E}\left[\beta^{-k}|\mathcal{S}\right]=\infty
.

∎

Now assume
{\mathbb{E}}[A^{-k}]f^{\prime}(q)=1
. We can also deduce by induction that

\displaystyle\frac{\textbf{1}_{\mathcal{S}}}{\beta^{k}}\geq\textbf{1}_{\mathcal{S}}(1+\textbf{1}_{\{\mu(0)=1\}}\frac{1}{A(0)}+\textbf{1}_{\{\mu(0)=1,\mu(1)=1\}}\frac{1}{A(0)A(1)}\dots)^{k},

where
\mu(n)
,
n\geq 0
are i.i.d with the law of
\mu
and
A(n)
are i.i.d with the law of
A
.
Set
\textbf{1}_{\{\mu(n)=1\}}A(n)
as
\Xi_{n}
for
n\geq 0
, which are i.i.d, with

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[(\Xi_{n})^{k}|\mathcal{S}\right]=f^{\prime}(q){\mathbb{E}}\left[\frac{1}{A^{k}}\right]=1.

Then
\psi:=1+\Xi_{0}+\Xi_{0}\Xi_{1}\dots
satisfies that

\displaystyle\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{{\mathbb{P}}(\psi>x|\mathcal{S})}{x^{-k}}=C,

for a positive constant
C
by the Kesten–Grincevicius–Goldie theorem. (See, for example,
[
10
]
for a statement of the theorem.) Hence

\displaystyle\liminf_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{{\mathbb{P}}(\beta^{-1}>x|\mathcal{S})}{x^{-k}}\geq C.

(46)

It indicates the ‘heavy tail’ property of
1/\beta
.

For an ERRW, there is analogous result when
p_{0}+p_{1}=0
. Recall that
d:=\min\{n\geq 1,p_{n}>0\}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

For a transient
(\alpha_{p},\alpha_{c})
-ERRW on a Galton-Watson tree such that
d\geq 2
and a real number
k\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{\beta^{k}}\right]<\infty

if and only if
k<d\alpha_{c}
.

Proof.

Mimic the ‘if’ part of Lemma
4.1
, and we have for
k<d\alpha_{c}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(\beta^{(n)}(\rho))^{k}}\leq 1+C(N)\frac{1}{(\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}A(\rho i)\beta^{(n)}{(\rho i)})^{k}}+N^{k}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 1+C(N)\frac{1}{(\sum_{i=1}^{d}A(\rho i)\beta^{(n)}{(\rho i)})^{k}}+N^{k},

where
C(N)
goes to
1
as
N
goes to infinity. Then we can prove by induction. In fact,

{\mathbb{E}}\left[\max_{1\leq i\leq d}A(\rho i)^{-k}\right]\leq d^{k}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}A(\rho i)\right)^{-k}\right]<\infty

by Lemma
2.3
. By taking
1\leq I\leq d
that maximizes
\{A_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq d}
, it holds that

\displaystyle 1+C(N)\frac{1}{(\sum_{i=1}^{d}A(\rho i)\beta^{(n)}{(\rho i)})^{k}}+N^{k}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 1+C(N)\frac{1}{(A(\rho I)\beta^{(n)}(\rho I))^{k}}\textbf{1}_{\{A(\rho i)\beta^{(n)}(\rho i)<\epsilon,\,\forall i\neq I\}}+C(N)\frac{1}{\epsilon^{k}}+N^{k}

When
\epsilon
is small enough, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{(\beta^{(n)}(\rho))^{k}}\right]\leq 1+C(N)\delta(\epsilon)\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{(\beta^{(n)}(\rho I))^{k}}\right]+C(N)\frac{1}{\epsilon^{k}}+N^{k}

for
\delta(\epsilon):=\mathbb{E}\left[A(\rho I)^{-k}\textbf{1}_{\{A(\rho i)\beta^{(n-1)}(\rho i)<\epsilon,\,\forall i\neq I\}}\right]
goes to
0
as
\epsilon
goes to
0
by dominated convergence. Fix
\epsilon
and
N
such that
C(N)\delta(\epsilon)<1
, by the induction argument, we have that
(1/\beta^{(n)}(\rho))^{k}
is integrable and so is
(1/\beta(\rho))^{k}
by Fatou’s lemma.

On the other hand, if
k\geq d\alpha_{c}
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(\beta(\rho))^{k}}=\left(1+\frac{1}{\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}A(\rho i)\beta{(\rho i)}}\right)^{k}\geq\textbf{1}_{\{\nu=d\}}\frac{1}{(\sum_{i=1}^{d}A(\rho i)\beta{(\rho i)})^{k}}\geq\textbf{1}_{\{\nu=d\}}\frac{1}{(\sum_{i=1}^{d}A(\rho i))^{k}}.

Thus, we have
\mathbb{E}\left[\beta^{-k}\right]=\infty
from
{\mathbb{E}}\left[(\sum_{i=1}^{d}A(\rho i))^{-k}\right]=\infty
.
∎

Now we are ready to investigate when
\mathscr{C}
is finite. We need a technical condition that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{GW}\left[\nu^{K}\right]<\infty,

(47)

where
K:=d\alpha_{c}+3+\alpha_{p}
. The condition is not necessary for the finiteness of
\mathscr{C}
. As is shown in Figure
1
, we deal with three different situations separately:

1.

p_{0}=0,p_{1}>0
;

2.

p_{0}=0,p_{1}=0
;

3.

p_{0}>0
.

First assume that
p_{0}=0
and
0<p_{1}\leq 1
. For simplicity of notation, let
s:=\alpha_{c}-\alpha_{p}+2
and

\displaystyle r:=\sup\{t:{\mathbb{E}}\left[A^{-t}\right]p_{1}<1\}.

(48)

The function
{\mathbb{E}}\left[A^{-t}\right]
is decreasing for
t\in(-\alpha_{p},(\alpha_{c}-\alpha_{p})/2)
and increasing for
t\in((\alpha_{c}-\alpha_{p})/2,\alpha_{c})
. It implies that
(\alpha_{c}-\alpha_{p})\vee 0\leq r<\alpha_{c}
, a fact we often need in the proof below. Now we claim the proposition in this case.

Proposition 4.4
.

For a transient
(\alpha_{p},\alpha_{c})
-ERRW, when
p_{0}=0
,
p_{1}>0
, and (
47
) holds,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\beta_{0}(1-\beta)}{\eta_{0}}\sum_{k\geq 0}\dfrac{\Gamma(\alpha_{p})\Gamma(\alpha_{c}+k+1)}{\Gamma(\alpha_{c}+1)\Gamma(\alpha_{p}+k)}(1-\beta)^{k}(1-\beta_{0})^{k}\right]<\infty

(49)

if and only if
2r-\alpha_{c}+\alpha_{p}-1>0
.

Remark 4.5
.

In the special case
p_{1}=1
, where the tree degenerates to
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, we need
\alpha_{c}>\alpha_{p}
to make the walk transient. Then
r=\alpha_{c}-\alpha_{p}
and the criterion becomes
\alpha_{c}-\alpha_{p}-1>0
, which coincides with the condition

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\frac{1}{A}\right]^{-1}=\frac{\alpha_{c}-1}{\alpha_{p}}>1

given in
[
21
, Theorem 1.16]
.

Proof.

For two quantities
A
and
B
, we write
A\lesssim B
(resp.
A\gtrsim B
) when there exists a positive constant
c
, such that
A\leq cB
(resp.
A\geq cB
). Also, write
A\approx B
if
A\lesssim B
and
A\gtrsim B
. The condition
2r-\alpha_{c}+\alpha_{p}-1>0
is equivalent to
2r-s+1>0
.

Since as
k\rightarrow\infty
,

\displaystyle\dfrac{\Gamma(\alpha_{p})\Gamma(\alpha_{c}+k+1)}{\Gamma(\alpha_{c}+1)\Gamma(\alpha_{p}+k)}\sim k^{\alpha_{c}-\alpha_{p}+1},

we have for
s>0
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 0}\dfrac{\Gamma(\alpha_{p})\Gamma(\alpha_{c}+k+1)}{\Gamma(\alpha_{c}+1)\Gamma(\alpha_{p}+k)}(1-\beta)^{k}(1-\beta_{0})^{k}\approx\frac{1}{(1-(1-\beta)(1-\beta_{0}))^{s}},

for
s=0
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 0}\dfrac{\Gamma(\alpha+p)\Gamma(\alpha_{c}+k+1)}{\Gamma(\alpha_{c}+1)\Gamma(\alpha_{p}+k)}(1-\beta)^{k}(1-\beta_{0})^{k}\approx\ln(1-(1-\beta)(1-\beta_{0})),

and for
s<0
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 0}\dfrac{\Gamma(\alpha+p)\Gamma(\alpha_{c}+k+1)}{\Gamma(\alpha_{c}+1)\Gamma(\alpha_{p}+k)}(1-\beta)^{k}(1-\beta_{0})^{k}\approx 1.

We first deal with the last case. When
s=\alpha_{c}-\alpha_{p}+2<0
, we see
\alpha_{p}>2
, and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\beta_{0}(1-\beta)}{\eta_{0}}\sum_{k\geq 0}\dfrac{\Gamma(\alpha_{p})\Gamma(\alpha_{c}+k+1)}{\Gamma(\alpha_{c}+1)\Gamma(\alpha_{p}+k)}(1-\beta)^{k}(1-\beta_{0})^{k}\right]

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\beta_{0}(1-\beta)}{\eta_{0}}\right]\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{\eta_{0}}\right]\lesssim\mathbb{E}_{GW}\left[\nu\right]<\infty.

In the next step, we assume
s\geq 0
. Let
s^{\prime}:=s+\epsilon 1_{\{s=0\}}
. First notice that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\beta_{0}(1-\beta)}{\eta_{0}(1-(1-\beta)(1-\beta_{0}))^{s^{\prime}}}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\dfrac{\beta_{0}(\eta_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\eta_{i}\beta_{i})^{s^{\prime}-1}}{(\eta_{0}\beta_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\eta_{i}\beta_{i})^{s^{\prime}}}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\dfrac{\beta_{0}\eta_{0}^{s^{\prime}-1}}{(\eta_{0}\beta_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\eta_{i}\beta_{i})^{s^{\prime}}}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\dfrac{\beta_{0}(\eta_{0}\beta_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\eta_{i}\beta_{i})^{s^{\prime}-1}}{(\eta_{0}\beta_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\eta_{i}\beta_{i})^{s^{\prime}}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\dfrac{\beta_{0}\eta_{0}^{s^{\prime}-1}}{(\eta_{0}\beta_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\eta_{i}\beta_{i})^{s^{\prime}}}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\dfrac{\beta_{0}}{\eta_{0}\beta_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\eta_{i}\beta_{i}}\right]=:I_{1}+I_{2},

where we use
1-\beta=\eta_{0}/(\eta_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\eta_{i}\beta_{i})
for the first equality;
(\eta_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\eta_{i}\beta_{i})^{s^{\prime}-1}\lesssim\eta_{0}^{s^{\prime}-1}+(\sum_{i=0}^{\nu}\eta_{i}\beta_{i})^{s^{\prime}-1}
for the second inequality.

Under the condition
2r-s+1>0
, we intend to prove that (taking
\epsilon>0
small enough when
s=0
)
I_{1}+I_{2}<\infty
, which implies (
49
). By discussing whether
\eta_{0}
is small, we have

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathbb{E}\left[\nu^{s^{\prime}}\dfrac{\beta_{0}\eta_{0}^{s^{\prime}-1}}{(\eta_{0}\beta_{0}+\beta_{1})^{s^{\prime}}}1_{\{\eta_{0}\leq 1/2\}}\bigg|\eta_{1}=\max_{1\leq i\leq\nu}\{\eta_{i}\}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\dfrac{\beta_{0}}{(\beta_{0}+\eta_{1}\beta_{1})^{s^{\prime}}}1_{\{\eta_{0}\geq 1/2\}}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\nu^{s^{\prime}}\dfrac{\beta_{0}\eta_{0}^{s^{\prime}-1}}{(\eta_{0}\beta_{0}+\beta_{1})^{s^{\prime}}}1_{\{\eta_{0}\leq 1/2\}}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\dfrac{\beta_{0}}{(\beta_{0}+\eta_{1}\beta_{1})^{s^{\prime}}}1_{\{\eta_{0}\geq 1/2\}}\right]=:I_{3}+I_{4},

where we use the observation that
\{\eta_{0}\leq 1/2\}
implies that
\{\max_{1\leq i\leq\nu}\eta_{i}>1/2\nu\}
.

For
I_{3}
, it holds that

\displaystyle I_{3}\lesssim\mathbb{E}\left[\nu^{s^{\prime}}\dfrac{\beta_{0}\eta_{0}^{s^{\prime}-1}}{(\eta_{0}\beta_{0}+\beta_{1})^{s^{\prime}}}\right]\lesssim\mathbb{E}\left[\nu^{s^{\prime}}\frac{1}{\beta_{0}^{s^{\prime}-1}\eta_{0}}\textbf{1}_{\{\beta_{1}\leq\eta_{0}\beta_{0}\}}+\nu^{s^{\prime}}\frac{\beta_{0}\eta_{0}^{s^{\prime}-1}}{\beta_{1}^{s^{\prime}}}\textbf{1}_{\{\beta_{1}>\eta_{0}\beta_{0}\}}\right].

Since
\mathbb{E}[(\beta_{1})^{-r+\delta}]<\infty
for any positive
\delta
by Lemma
4.1
,
\mathbb{P}(\beta_{1}\leq x)\lesssim x^{r-\delta}
by Markov inequality. The density of
\eta_{0}\in(0,1)
is given by

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}(\eta_{0}\leq w|\nu)}{\mathrm{d}w}=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha_{p}+\nu\alpha_{c})}{\Gamma(\nu\alpha_{c})\Gamma(\alpha_{p})}w^{\alpha_{p}-1}(1-w)^{\nu\alpha_{c}-1}\lesssim\frac{\Gamma(\alpha_{p}+\nu\alpha_{c})}{\Gamma(\nu\alpha_{c})\Gamma(\alpha_{p})}w^{\alpha_{p}-1}\approx\nu^{\alpha_{p}}w^{\alpha_{p}-1}

(50)

by (
15

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\nu^{s^{\prime}}\frac{1}{\beta_{0}^{s^{\prime}-1}\eta_{0}}\textbf{1}_{\{\beta_{1}\leq\eta_{0}\beta_{0}\}}\right]=\mathbb{E}_{GW}\left[\nu^{s^{\prime}}\int_{x\leq zw}\frac{1}{z^{s^{\prime}-1}w}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}(\beta_{0}\leq z)\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}(\eta_{0}\leq w|\nu)\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}(\beta_{1}\leq x)\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{GW}\left[\nu^{s^{\prime}+\alpha_{p}}\int z^{r-s^{\prime}+1-\delta}w^{r+\alpha_{p}-\delta-2}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}(\beta_{0}\leq z)\mathrm{d}w\right]\lesssim\mathbb{E}_{GW}\left[\nu^{s^{\prime}+\alpha_{p}}\right]<\infty,

where the last approximation follows from

(and thus
r+\alpha_{p}-1>\alpha_{c}-r>0
) by taking
\epsilon
and
\delta
small enough. Similarly, together with integration by parts, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\nu^{s^{\prime}}\frac{\beta_{0}\eta_{0}^{s^{\prime}-1}}{\beta_{1}^{s^{\prime}}}\textbf{1}_{\{\beta_{1}>\eta_{0}\beta_{0}\}}\right]=\mathbb{E}_{GW}\left[\nu^{s^{\prime}}\int_{x>zw}\frac{zw^{s^{\prime}-1}}{x^{s^{\prime}}}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}(\beta_{0}\leq z)\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}(\eta_{0}\leq w|\nu)\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}(\beta_{1}\leq x)\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\nu^{s^{\prime}}\beta_{0}\eta_{0}^{s^{\prime}-1}\right]+\mathbb{E}_{GW}\left[\nu^{s^{\prime}}\int_{x>zw}\frac{zw^{s^{\prime}-1}}{x^{s^{\prime}+1}}\mathbb{P}(\beta_{1}\leq x)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}(\beta_{0}\leq z)\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}(\eta_{0}\leq w|\nu)\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{GW}\left[\nu^{s^{\prime}}\int zw^{s^{\prime}-1}(z^{r-s^{\prime}-\delta}w^{r-s^{\prime}-\delta}\vee 1)\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}(\beta_{0}\leq z)\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}(\eta_{0}\leq w|\nu)\right]\lesssim\mathbb{E}_{GW}\left[\nu^{s^{\prime}+\alpha_{p}}\right]<\infty,

where we use the fact that
\mathbb{E}\left[\nu^{s^{\prime}}\beta_{0}\eta_{0}^{s^{\prime}-1}\right]
is finite since
\alpha_{p}+s^{\prime}-1\geq\alpha_{c}+1>0
and
\mathbb{E}[\nu]\leq\mathbb{E}[\nu^{\alpha_{c}+3+\alpha_{p}}]<\infty
.

For
I_{4}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\beta_{0}}{(\beta_{0}+\eta_{1}\beta_{1})^{s^{\prime}}}\right]\lesssim\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\beta_{0}}{\beta_{1}^{s^{\prime}}\eta_{1}^{s^{\prime}}}\textbf{1}_{\{\beta_{0}\leq\eta_{1}\beta_{1}\}}+\frac{1}{\beta_{0}^{s^{\prime}-1}}\textbf{1}_{\{\beta_{0}>\eta_{1}\beta_{1}\}}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{GW}\left[\int w^{r-s^{\prime}+1-\delta}z^{r-s^{\prime}+1-\delta}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}(\beta_{1}\leq z)\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}(\eta_{1}\leq w|\nu)\right]\lesssim\mathbb{E}_{GW}\left[\nu^{\alpha_{c}}\right]<\infty,

since
\mathbb{E}[(\beta_{1})^{-r+\delta}]<\infty
,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}(\eta_{1}\leq w|\nu)}{\mathrm{d}w}\lesssim\frac{\Gamma(\alpha_{p}+\nu\alpha_{c})}{\Gamma(\alpha_{c})\Gamma((\nu-1)\alpha_{c}+\alpha_{p})}w^{\alpha_{c}-1}\approx\nu^{\alpha_{c}}w^{\alpha_{c}-1}

(51)

and
2r-s^{\prime}+1>0
(which implies

\alpha_{c}>r
).

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\dfrac{\beta_{0}(\eta_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\eta_{i}\beta_{i})^{s-1}}{(\eta_{0}\beta_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\eta_{i}\beta_{i})^{s}}\right]\gtrsim\mathbb{E}\left[\dfrac{\beta_{0}\eta_{0}^{s-1}}{(\eta_{0}\beta_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\eta_{i}\beta_{i})^{s}}\right]=I_{1}

Proposition 4.6
.

For a transient
(\alpha_{p},\alpha_{c})
-ERRW, when
p_{0}=0
,

, and (

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\beta_{0}(1-\beta)}{\eta_{0}}\sum_{k\geq 0}\dfrac{\Gamma(\alpha_{p})\Gamma(\alpha_{c}+k+1)}{\Gamma(\alpha_{c}+1)\Gamma(\alpha_{p}+k)}(1-\beta)^{k}(1-\beta_{0})^{k}\right]<\infty,

(2d-1)\alpha_{c}+\alpha_{p}-1>0
.

Proof.

Follow the notation in Proposition
4.4
. Recall that
1-\beta=\eta_{0}/(\eta_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\eta_{i}\beta_{i})

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}(1-\eta_{1}\leq x|\nu)}{\mathrm{d}x}=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha_{p}+\nu\alpha_{c})}{\Gamma(\alpha_{c})\Gamma((\nu-1)\alpha_{c}+\alpha_{p})}x^{(\nu-1)\alpha_{c}+\alpha_{p}-1}(1-x)^{\alpha_{c}-1}.

To prove the necessity, suppose
(2d-1)\alpha_{c}+\alpha_{p}-1\leq 0
, we see

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\beta_{0}(1-\beta)}{\eta_{0}}\sum_{k\geq 0}\dfrac{\Gamma(\alpha_{p})\Gamma(\alpha_{c}+k+1)}{\Gamma(\alpha_{c}+1)\Gamma(\alpha_{p}+k)}(1-\beta)^{k}(1-\beta_{0})^{k}\right]

\displaystyle\gtrsim

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1-\beta}{\eta_{0}}\right]\gtrsim\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\textbf{1}_{\{\nu=d\}}}{\eta_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\eta_{i}\beta_{i}}\right]

\displaystyle\gtrsim

\displaystyle\int\frac{1}{x+y}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}(\eta_{0}+\sum_{2}^{d}\eta_{i}\leq x|\nu=d)\mathbb{\mathrm{d}}\mathbb{P}(\beta_{1}\leq y)

\displaystyle\gtrsim

\displaystyle\int_{x\leq y\leq 1/2}\frac{1}{y}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}(1-\eta_{1}\leq x|\nu=d)\mathbb{\mathrm{d}}\mathbb{P}(\beta_{1}\leq y)

\displaystyle\gtrsim

\displaystyle\int_{y\leq 1/2}y^{(d-1)\alpha_{c}+\alpha_{p}-1}\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}(\beta_{1}\leq y)=\infty

since
(d-1)\alpha_{c}+\alpha_{p}-1\leq-d\alpha_{c}
.

Following the proof in Proposition
4.4
, we only need to prove for sufficiency that when
(2d-1)\alpha_{c}+\alpha_{p}-1>0
and
\mathbb{E}\left[\nu^{K}\right]<\infty
(recall that
K=d\alpha_{c}+3+\alpha_{p}
),

\displaystyle I_{1}=\mathbb{E}\left[\dfrac{\beta_{0}\eta_{0}^{s^{\prime}-1}}{(\eta_{0}\beta_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{\nu}\eta_{i}\beta_{i})^{s^{\prime}}}\right]<\infty

where
s^{\prime}=s+\textbf{1}_{\{s=0\}}\epsilon
. (Note that
I_{2}
is finite automatically by taking

in
I_{1}
.) By separating the case
\eta_{0}>1/2
and
\eta_{0}\leq 1/2

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle[(\frac{1}{2\nu})^{(\nu-d+1)\alpha_{c}-1}\vee 1]w^{\alpha_{p}-1}\mathrm{d}w\prod_{i=2}^{d}(x_{i}^{\alpha_{c}-1}\mathrm{d}x_{i})\Bigg]

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\nu^{s^{\prime}+1+(d-1)\alpha_{c}+\alpha_{p}}\dfrac{\beta_{0}\bar{\eta}_{0}^{s^{\prime}-1}}{(\beta_{0}\bar{\eta}_{0}+\beta_{1}+\sum_{i=2}^{d}\bar{\eta}_{i}\beta_{i})^{s^{\prime}}}\right],

Proposition 4.7
.

For a transient

-ERRW, when

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\beta_{0}(1-\beta)}{\eta_{0}}\sum_{k\geq 0}\dfrac{\Gamma(\alpha_{p})\Gamma(\alpha_{c}+k+1)}{\Gamma(\alpha_{c}+1)\Gamma(\alpha_{p}+k)}(1-\beta)^{k}(1-\beta_{0})^{k}\Bigg|\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{T}^{-}}\right]<\infty,

if and only if
r-\alpha_{c}+\alpha_{p}-1>0
.

Proof.

Let us first deal with the ‘if’ part. The inequality (
52
) holds if

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{\beta_{0}}{\eta_{0}}\sum_{k\geq 0}\dfrac{\Gamma(\alpha_{p})\Gamma(\alpha_{c}+k+1)}{\Gamma(\alpha_{c}+1)\Gamma(\alpha_{p}+k)}(1-\beta_{0})^{k}\right]<\infty.

If
s=\alpha_{c}-\alpha_{p}+2<0

\mathbb{E}[1/\eta_{0}]<\infty
. It is naturally satisfied since

(which implies
\alpha_{p}>1
by

is finite when

(which implies
\alpha_{p}>1
and
1-s^{\prime}>-r
) and

.

For the ‘only if ’ part, when

\mathbb{P}(\beta=0)>0

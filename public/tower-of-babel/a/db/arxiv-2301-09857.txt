Large amplitude problem of BGK model: Relaxation to quadratic nonlinearity
Gi-Chan Bae, Gyounghun Ko, Donghyun Lee, Seok-Bae Yun

Abstract
. Bhatnagar–Gross–Krook (BGK) equation is a relaxation model of the Boltzmann equation which is widely used in place of the Boltzmann equation for the simulation of various kinetic flow problems. In this work, we study the asymptotic stability of the BGK model when the initial data is not necessarily close to the global equilibrium pointwisely. Due to the highly nonlinear structure of the relaxation operator, the argument developed to derive the bootstrap estimate for the Boltzmann equation leads to a weaker estimate in the case of the BGK model, which does not exclude the possible blow-up of the perturbation. To overcome this issue, we carry out a refined analysis of the macroscopic fields to guarantee that the system transits from a highly nonlinear regime into a quadratic nonlinear regime after a long but finite time, in which the highly nonlinear perturbative term relaxes to essentially quadratic nonlinearity.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
BGK model

The Boltzmann equation is the fundamental equation bridging the particle description and the fluid description of gases
[
10
,
11
,
42
,
43
]
. However, the high dimensionality of the equation and the complicated structure of the collision operator have been major obstacles in applying the Boltzmann equation to various flow problems in kinetic theory. In this regard, a relaxational model equation, which now goes by the name BGK model, was introduced in pursuit of a numerically amenable model of the Boltzmann equation
[
5
,
45
]
:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{t}F+v\cdot\nabla_{x}F&=\nu(\mathcal{M}(F)-F),\quad(t,x,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3},\cr F(0,x,v)&=F_{0}(x,v).\end{split}

Instead of tracking the complicated collision process using the collision operator of the Boltzmann equation, the BGK model captures the relaxation process by measuring the distance between the velocity distribution to its local equilibrium state:

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(F)(t,x,v):=\frac{\rho(t,x)}{\sqrt{(2\pi T(t,x))^{3}}}e^{-\frac{|v-U(t,x)|^{2}}{2T(t,x)}}.

which is called the local Maxwellian. The macroscopic density
\rho
, bulk velocity
U
, and temperature
T
are defined by:

(1.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\rho(t,x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}F(t,x,v)dv,\\
&\rho(t,x)U(t,x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}F(t,x,v)vdv,\\
&3\rho(t,x)T(t,x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}F(t,x,v)|v-U(t,x)|^{2}dv.\end{split}

Various forms are available for the collision frequency
\nu
. In this work, we consider the collision frequency of the following form:

(1.3)

\displaystyle\nu(t,x)=(\rho^{a}T^{b})(t,x),\quad a\geq b\geq 0,

which covers most of the relevant models in the literature.
The relaxation operator satisfies the following cancellation property because
\mathcal{M}
shares the first three moments with the distribution function:

(1.4)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\big\{\mathcal{M}(F)-F\big\}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
v\\
|v|^{2}\end{pmatrix}dvdx=0.

This leads to the conservation laws of mass, momentum, and energy:

(1.5)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}F(t,x,v)\begin{pmatrix}1\\
v\\
|v|^{2}\end{pmatrix}dvdx=0,

and the celebrated H-theorem:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}F(t)\ln F(t)dvdx=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\{\mathcal{M}(F)-F\}\{\ln\mathcal{M}(F)-\ln F\}dvdx\leq 0.

[
35
]
, in which Perthame obtained the global existence of weak solutions when the mass, energy, and entropy of the initial data are bounded. Perthame and Pulvirenti then found in
[

. The existence and asymptotic behavior of solutions to the BGK model near equilibrium was considered in
[

,

,

,

]
. A stationary solution for the BGK model was found using the Schauder fixed point theorem in
[

,

]
. The existence and uniqueness of stationary solutions to the BGK model in a slab were investigated in
[
3
,

]
. The argument was extended to a relativistic BGK model

and the quantum BGK model
[

[

and references therein.

In this paper, we consider
L^{\infty}_{x,v}
solution of the BGK equation. Low regularity

L^{2}
-

1.2.
Main theorem and scheme of proof

Let us write
F=\mu+f
where
\mu=\mu(v)
is the global equilibrium:

\displaystyle\mu(v)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{(2\pi)^{3}}}e^{-\frac{|v|^{2}}{2}},

and
f
denotes the perturbation around the equilibrium. In terms of
f
, BGK equation (
1.1
) can be rewritten as

(1.6)

\begin{split}\partial_{t}f+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f&=Lf+\Gamma(f),\end{split}

where
L
denotes the linearized relaxation operator and
\Gamma
is nonlinear perturbation.
For the derivation (
1.6
) and explicit form of
L
and
\Gamma
, see Lemma
2.1
.

To state our main theorem, we need to define relative entropy:

(1.7)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(F)(t)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}(F\ln F-\mu\ln\mu)dvdx=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}F\ln\frac{F}{\mu}dvdx,

where the last equality comes from (
1.5
).
We also introduce some necessary notations:

•

We define
q
-th order velocity weight as
\langle v\rangle^{q}:=1+|v|^{q}
.

•

We define the standard
L^{\infty}
norm

\displaystyle\|F(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}:=\esssup_{(x,v)\in\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}|F(t,x,v)|,

and weighted
L^{\infty}
norm as

\displaystyle\|F(t)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}:=\esssup_{(x,v)\in\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\langle v\rangle^{q}|F(t,x,v)|,\quad\|F(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}(m)}:=\esssup_{(x,v)\in\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}m(v)|F(t,x,v)|.

•

We denote standard
L^{2}
norm

\displaystyle\|F\|_{L^{2}_{v}}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|F(v)|^{2}dv\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad\|F\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}|F(x,v)|^{2}dvdx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

and weighted
L^{2}
norm as

\displaystyle\|F\|_{L^{2}_{v}(m)}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|m(v)F(v)|^{2}dv\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad\|F\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}(m)}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}|m(v)F(x,v)|^{2}dvdx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

•

We define the pairing
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{v}
as

(1.8)

\displaystyle\langle g,h\rangle_{v}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}g(v)h(v)dv,\quad\text{ if }~gh\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{v}).

We are now ready to state our main theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
F_{0}
is non-negative:
F_{0}(x,v)=\mu(v)+f_{0}(x,v)\geq 0
, and satisfies

(1.9)

\displaystyle\inf_{(t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}F_{0}(x-vt,v)dv\geq C_{0},

for some positive constant
C_{0}>0
. We also assume
F_{0}
shares the same mass, momentum, and energy with
\mu
:

(1.10)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{0}(1,v,|v|^{2})dvdx=0.

Then, for any
M_{0}>0
, there exists
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(M_{0},C_{0})
such that if initial data
f_{0}
satisfies
(q>10)

\displaystyle\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}\leq M_{0},\quad\mathcal{E}(F_{0})\leq\varepsilon,

then there exists a unique global-in-time solution
F(t,x,v)=\mu(v)+f(t,x,v)
to the BGK model (
1.6
) with collision frequency (
1.3
). Moreover
f
satisfies

\displaystyle\|f(t)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}\leq C_{M_{0}}e^{-kt},

where
C_{M_{0}}
depending on
M_{0}
, and
k
are positive constants.

In previous results on the BGK model near the global equilibrium
[
4
,
46
,
49
,
48
]
, the fact that the macroscopic fields
(\rho,U,T)
remains close to those of global equilibrium
(1,0,1)
was crucially used to close energy estimates and derive asymptotic stability. Even for the large amplitude setting, developed in
[
17
]
, conditions that corresponds more or less to the statement that the initial macroscopic fields lie close to the global equilibrium has to be imposed to control nonlinear terms and derive bootstrap estimates.

Figure 1
.
Three different regimes

To overcome this restriction, the relaxation of macroscopic fields into the equilibrium macroscopic fields
(1,0,1)
has to be carefully investigated. In this regard, we divide the evolution of the solution into three different phases, namely, highly nonlinear regime, quadratic nonlinear regime, and small amplitude regime (See Figure
1
). In the highly nonlinear regime, the amplitude of the perturbation can be arbitrarily large and its macroscopic fields are not necessarily close to
(1,0,1)
. We show that macroscopic fields relax to
(1,0,1)
uniformly after a time
t_{eq}
which is the onset of the quadratic nonlinear regime. This enables us to carry out the crucial bootstrap argument. After a sufficiently large time
t_{*}
, the solution enters the near-equilibrium regime (small amplitude regime) for which various existence theories are available.

The major difficulties arise in the first two regimes in Figure
1
in which the amplitude of solutions is not necessarily small. In the study of the large-amplitude solution to the Boltzmann equation
[
14
,
16
,
15
,
29
]
, the fact that the perturbation term is only quadratically nonlinear was crucially used in the derivation of the following key bootstrap estimate:

\displaystyle\|f(t)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}\lesssim C(\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}})\left(1+\int_{0}^{t}\|f(s)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}ds\right)e^{-\lambda t}+D,\quad D\ll 1.

In the case of the BGK model, the typical term in the nonlinear perturbation
\Gamma(f)
looks like

(1.11)

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq 5}\mathcal{A}_{ij}(\rho,(v-U),U,T)\mathcal{M}(F)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}fe_{i}dv\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}fe_{j}dv,

where
\mathcal{A}_{ij}
(
1\leq i,j\leq 5
) denote generic rational functions and
e_{i}~(i=1,\dots,5)
are the orthogonal basis of the null space of
L
, and suffers from much stronger nonlinearity than the quadratic nonlinearity of the Boltzmann equation.
In the presence of such strong nonlinearity, a naive computation would lead to the following estimate:

\displaystyle\|f(t)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}\lesssim C(\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}})\left(1+\int_{0}^{t}\left[\|f(s)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}\right]^{n}ds\right)e^{-\lambda t}+D,

where exponent
n
is determined by the order of nonlinearity of
\Gamma
. Unfortunately, this does not exclude the possibility of a blow-up of
\|f(t)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}
and, therefore, cannot be applied to bootstrap arguments.
To overcome this difficulty, we first note that the strong nonlinearity of (
1.11
) comes from the nonlinearity of
\mathcal{A}_{ij}
. We will perform careful asymptotic analysis of
\rho,U,T
to show that after some finite time
t_{eq}
the nonlinearity of
\mathcal{A}_{ij}
essentially vanishes so that (
1.11
) become essential quadratically nonlinear.
This enables one to derive the desired bootstrap inequality with
n=1
in the quadratic nonlinear regime.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we consider the linearization of the BGK model and derive basic estimates for the macroscopic fields. In Section 3, under a priori assumption, we prove the key estimate to control the macroscopic fields. Especially, we obtain the transition time
t_{eq}
after which the solution enters the quadratic nonlinear regime. In Section 4, we prove the local-in-time existence and uniqueness of the BGK solutions. In Section 5, we show that the solution satisfies the desired bootstrap inequality in the quadratic nonlinear regime. In Section 6, we prove the well-posedness and exponential decay of the solution to the BGK equation in the small amplitude regime. In Appendix
A
, we present the explicit form of the nonlinear perturbation
\Gamma
.

Lemma 2.1
.

[

Let

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f+f

\displaystyle=\mathbf{P}f+\Gamma(f).

The linear term
\mathbf{P}f

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}f

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}fdv\mu+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}fvdv\cdot(v\mu)+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f\frac{|v|^{2}-3}{\sqrt{6}}dv\left(\frac{|v|^{2}-3}{\sqrt{6}}\mu\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{5}\langle f,e_{i}\rangle_{v}(e_{i}\mu),

where we used the pairing notation
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{v}
in (
1.8
) and
(e_{1},\cdots,e_{5})=(1,v,(|v|^{2}-3)/\sqrt{6})
. The nonlinear term
\Gamma(f)
is written as

(2.3)

\displaystyle\Gamma(f)

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{1}(f)+\Gamma_{2}(f).

Here,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{1}(f)

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{P}f-f)\sum_{1\leq i\leq 5}\int_{0}^{1}A_{i}(\theta)d\theta\langle f,e_{i}\rangle_{v},

(precise definition of

is given in (
2.7

(2.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Gamma_{2}(f)&=\rho^{a}T^{b}\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq 5}\int_{0}^{1}\left[\nabla_{(\rho_{\theta},\rho_{\theta}U_{\theta},G_{\theta})}^{2}\mathcal{M}(\theta)\right]_{ij}(1-\theta)d\theta\langle f,e_{i}\rangle_{v}\langle f,e_{j}\rangle_{v}\cr&=\rho^{a}T^{b}\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq 5}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\mathcal{P}_{ij}((v-U_{\theta}),U_{\theta},T_{\theta})}{\rho_{\theta}^{\alpha_{ij}}T_{\theta}^{\beta_{ij}}}\mathcal{M}(\theta)(1-\theta)d\theta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}fe_{i}dv\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}fe_{j}dv,\end{split}

where the transition of the macroscopic fields are defined as

(2.5)

\displaystyle\rho_{\theta}=\theta\rho+(1-\theta),\qquad\rho_{\theta}U_{\theta}=\theta\rho U,\qquad\rho_{\theta}|U_{\theta}|^{2}+3\rho_{\theta}T_{\theta}-3\rho_{\theta}=\theta(\rho|U|^{2}+3\rho T-3\rho).

Here,
\mathcal{P}_{ij}
denotes a generic polynomial such that
\mathcal{P}_{ij}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})=\sum_{m}a^{ij}_{m}x_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{m_{n}}
and
\alpha_{ij},\beta_{ij}\geq 0
.
Precise definitions of
\alpha_{ij},\beta_{ij}
, and

are given in Appendix
A
.

Proof.

The linearization of the local Maxwellian
\mathcal{M}(F)
and

can be found in
[
46
]
and
[
2
]
, respectively. But for the reader’s convenience, we briefly sketch the proof here. The main idea of linearization is constructing a convex combination of the following macroscopic fields:

(2.6)

\displaystyle\rho=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mu+fdv,\quad\rho U=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}vfdv,\quad G=\frac{\rho|U|^{2}+3\rho T-3\rho}{\sqrt{6}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{|v|^{2}-3}{\sqrt{6}}fdv.

We note that the mapping of the macroscopic fields
(\rho,U,T)\leftrightarrow(\rho,\rho U,G)
is one to one if
\rho>0
because of the following reverse relation:

\displaystyle U=\frac{\rho U}{\rho},\qquad T=\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\frac{G}{\rho}-\frac{|\rho U|^{2}}{3\rho^{2}}+1.

Using the transition of the macroscopic fields (
2.5
), we write the local Maxwellian depending on
(\rho_{\theta},U_{\theta},T_{\theta})
as
\mathcal{M}(\theta)
and we apply Taylor’s theorem at
\theta=0
.

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(1)=\mathcal{M}(0)+\frac{d\mathcal{M}(\theta)}{d\theta}\bigg|_{\theta=0}+\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d^{2}\mathcal{M}(\theta)}{d\theta^{2}}(1-\theta)d\theta.

Since
(\rho_{\theta},U_{\theta},T_{\theta})|_{\theta=1}=(\rho,U,T)
and
(\rho_{\theta},U_{\theta},T_{\theta})|_{\theta=0}=(1,0,1)
, we have
\mathcal{M}(1)=\mathcal{M}(F)
and
\mathcal{M}(0)=\mu
, respectively. Then we consider the first derivative of
\mathcal{M}(\theta)
:

\displaystyle\frac{d\mathcal{M}(\theta)}{d\theta}\bigg|_{\theta=0}

\displaystyle=\frac{d\rho_{\theta}}{d\theta}\frac{\partial\mathcal{M}(\theta)}{\partial\rho_{\theta}}+\frac{d(\rho_{\theta}U_{\theta})}{d\theta}\frac{\partial\mathcal{M}(\theta)}{\partial(\rho_{\theta}U_{\theta})}+\frac{dG_{\theta}}{d\theta}\frac{\partial\mathcal{M}(\theta)}{\partial G_{\theta}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{d(\rho_{\theta},\rho_{\theta}U_{\theta},G_{\theta})}{d\theta}\right)^{T}\left(\frac{\partial(\rho_{\theta},\rho_{\theta}U_{\theta},G_{\theta})}{\partial(\rho_{\theta},U_{\theta},T_{\theta})}\right)^{-1}\nabla_{(\rho_{\theta},U_{\theta},T_{\theta})}\mathcal{M}(\theta)\bigg|_{\theta=0},

where we used that the last definition of (

. Then substituting the computation of the Jacobian and
\nabla_{(\rho_{\theta},U_{\theta},T_{\theta})}\mathcal{M}(\theta)
in Lemma

at

\displaystyle\left(\frac{d(\rho_{\theta},\rho_{\theta}U_{\theta},G_{\theta})}{d\theta}\right)=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}fdv,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}vfdv,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{|v|^{2}-3}{\sqrt{6}}fdv\right),

we obtain that

\displaystyle\frac{d\mathcal{M}(\theta)}{d\theta}\bigg|_{\theta=0}=\mathbf{P}f.

For the nonlinear term, applying the chain rule twice yields

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}\mathcal{M}(\theta)}{d\theta^{2}}

\displaystyle=(\rho-1,\rho U,G)^{T}\left\{D^{2}_{(\rho_{\theta},\rho_{\theta}U_{\theta},G_{\theta})}\mathcal{M}(\theta)\right\}(\rho-1,\rho U,G)

\displaystyle=\left[\nabla_{(\rho_{\theta},\rho_{\theta}U_{\theta},G_{\theta})}^{2}\mathcal{M}(\theta)\right]_{ij}\langle f,e_{i}\rangle_{v}\langle f,e_{j}\rangle_{v}.

The explicit form of
\nabla_{(\rho_{\theta},\rho_{\theta}U_{\theta},G_{\theta})}^{2}\mathcal{M}(\theta)
will be given in Appendix
A
. Now we consider the collision frequency

\displaystyle\nu(1)=\nu(0)+\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d}{d\theta}\nu(\theta)d\theta.

By an explicit computation, we have

\displaystyle\nu(t,x)

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{1\leq i\leq 5}\int_{0}^{1}A_{i}(\theta)d\theta\langle f,e_{i}\rangle_{v},

where

(2.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}A_{1}(\theta)&=a\rho^{a-1}_{\theta}T^{b}_{\theta},\qquad A_{i+1}(\theta)=-a\rho^{a-2}_{\theta}U_{\theta i}T^{b}_{\theta},\quad i=1,2,3,\cr A_{5}(\theta)&=\frac{|U_{\theta}|^{2}-3T_{\theta}+3}{3\rho_{\theta}}a\rho^{a-1}_{\theta}T^{b}_{\theta}+\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}b\rho^{a-1}_{\theta}T^{b-1}_{\theta}.\end{split}

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\nu(\mathcal{M}(F)-F)=(\mathbf{P}f-f)+\Gamma_{1}(f)+\Gamma_{2}(f).

Lemma 2.2
.

Recall the macroscopic fields defined in (

(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{T}^{3}

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\rho(t,x)\\

3.
Transition to quadratic nonlinear regime

As mentioned before, the highly nonlinear behavior of the BGK operator combined with large amplitude

Throughout this section, we impose a priori assumption

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq s\leq t_{*}}\|f(s)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}\leq\mkern 1.3mu\overline{\mkern-1.3muM\mkern-1.3mu}\mkern 1.3mu,\quad\mbox{for}\quad q>10,

where
t_{*}
is arbitrary large as much as needed. Both
\mkern 1.3mu\overline{\mkern-1.3muM\mkern-1.3mu}\mkern 1.3mu
and

will be chosen depending on initial data in the proof of Theorem

Proposition 3.1
.

Let us assume (
1.9
) and the a priori assumption (
3.1
).
For any given positive constant
\delta\in(0,1)
, there exists sufficiently small
\mathcal{E}(\mkern 1.3mu\overline{\mkern-1.3muM\mkern-1.3mu}\mkern 1.3mu,t_{*},\delta)
such that if
\mathcal{E}(F_{0})\leq\mathcal{E}(\mkern 1.3mu\overline{\mkern-1.3muM\mkern-1.3mu}\mkern 1.3mu,t_{*},\delta)
, then the following estimate holds on
t\in[0,t_{*}]
:

(3.2)

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\langle v\rangle^{2}f(t,x,v)dv\right|\leq C_{q}M_{0}e^{-t}+\frac{3}{4}\delta.

The above proposition implies the following two important properties. First, the macroscopic fields
(\rho,U,T)
become uniformly close to
(1,0,1)
after time
t\geq t_{eq}(M_{0},\delta)
, i.e.,

\displaystyle|\rho-1|,~|\rho U|,~|3\rho T+\rho|U|^{2}-3|\leq\delta,\quad\mbox{for}\quad t\geq t_{eq}.

This will be proved in Lemma
3.7
. And after
t_{eq}
, the high order nonlinearity of
\Gamma
on the BGK model is transformed into quadratic nonlinear form for which we are able to prove the asymptotic behavior of the solution.
To prove Proposition
3.1
, we first decompose the L.H.S of (
3.2
) into several pieces.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let us assume the a priori assumption (
3.1
). For an arbitrary real number
N>1
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\langle v\rangle^{2}f(t,x,v)dv\right|

\displaystyle\leq C_{q}M_{0}e^{-t}+\frac{C_{q}}{N^{q-5}}\mkern 1.3mu\overline{\mkern-1.3muM\mkern-1.3mu}\mkern 1.3mu

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)}\int_{|v|\leq N}\langle v\rangle^{2}|\mathbf{P}f(s,x-v(t-s),v)|dvds

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)}\int_{|v|\leq N}\langle v\rangle^{2}|\Gamma(f)(s,x-v(t-s),v)|dvds.

Proof.

We split the velocity integration into
\{|v|\leq N\}
and
\{|v|\geq N\}
for arbitrary real number
N>1
:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\langle v\rangle^{2}f(t,x,v)dv=\int_{|v|\geq N}\langle v\rangle^{2}f(t,x,v)dv+\int_{|v|\leq N}\langle v\rangle^{2}f(t,x,v)dv.

For the large velocity region
\{|v|\geq N\}
, applying the a priori bound
\sup_{0\leq s\leq t_{*}}\|f(s)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}\leq\mkern 1.3mu\overline{\mkern-1.3muM\mkern-1.3mu}\mkern 1.3mu
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{|v|\geq N}\langle v\rangle^{2}f(t,x,v)dv\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{|v|\geq N}\langle v\rangle^{-q+2}\langle v\rangle^{q}|f(t,x,v)|dv\leq\frac{C_{q}}{N^{q-5}}\mkern 1.3mu\overline{\mkern-1.3muM\mkern-1.3mu}\mkern 1.3mu,

for
t\in[0,t_{*}]
. For the bounded velocity region
\{|v|\leq N\}
, we use the mild formulation of the reformulated BGK equation (
2.1
) to get

(3.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left|\int_{|v|\leq N}\langle v\rangle^{2}f(t,x,v)dv\right|&\leq e^{-t}\int_{|v|\leq N}\langle v\rangle^{2}|f_{0}(x-vt,v)|dv\cr&\quad+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)}\int_{|v|\leq N}\langle v\rangle^{2}|\mathbf{P}f(s,x-v(t-s),v)|dvds\\
&\quad+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)}\int_{|v|\leq N}\langle v\rangle^{2}|\Gamma(f)(s,x-v(t-s),v)|dvds.\end{split}

The first term on the R.H.S of (
3.3
) is bounded as follows:

\displaystyle e^{-t}\int_{|v|\leq N}\langle v\rangle^{2}|f_{0}(x-vt,v)|dv=e^{-t}\int_{|v|\leq N}\langle v\rangle^{-q+2}\langle v\rangle^{q}|f_{0}(x-vt,v)|dv\leq C_{q}e^{-t}M_{0},

which gives the desired result.
∎

The estimates for the second and third terms on the R.H.S of (
3.3
) will be given in Lemma
3.3
and Lemma
3.6
, respectively.

Before we present the estimate of the second term of (

Lemma 3.2
.

[
19
,
17
,
29
]

Assume that
F(t,x,v)=\mu(v)+f(t,x,v)
is a solution of the BGK model (
1.1
). For any
t\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{4\mu(v)}|f(t,x,v)|^{2}\mathbf{1}_{|f(t,x,v)|\leq\mu(v)}dvdx+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{4}|f(t,x,v)|\mathbf{1}_{|f(t,x,v)|>\mu(v)}dvdx\leq\mathcal{E}(F_{0}),

where initial relative entropy
\mathcal{E}(F_{0})
was defined in (
1.7
).

Proof.

Notice that the mean value theorem gives

\displaystyle F\ln F-\mu\ln\mu=(1+\ln\mu)(F-\mu)+\frac{1}{2\tilde{F}}|F-\mu|^{2},

where
\tilde{F}
is between
F
and
\mu
. If we define the function
\psi(x):=x\ln x-x+1
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\tilde{F}}|F-\mu|^{2}

\displaystyle=F\ln F-\mu\ln\mu-(1+\ln\mu)(F-\mu)=\psi\left(\frac{F}{\mu}\right)\mu.

Hence, one obtains that

(3.4)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{2\tilde{F}}|F-\mu|^{2}dvdx=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\psi\left(\frac{F}{\mu}\right)\mu dvdx.

For the L.H.S in (
3.4
), we divide

\displaystyle 1=\textbf{1}_{|F-\mu|\leq\mu}+\textbf{1}_{|F-\mu|>\mu}.

On
\{|F-\mu|>\mu\}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{|F-\mu|}{\tilde{F}}=\frac{F-\mu}{\tilde{F}}>\frac{F-\frac{1}{2}F}{F}=\frac{1}{2},

where we used the fact
F>2\mu
. On the other hand, over
\{|F-\mu|\leq\mu\}
, we obtain
0\leq F\leq 2\mu
. This implies that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tilde{F}}\geq\frac{1}{2\mu}.

Thus, it follows from (
3.4
) that

(3.5)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{4\mu}|F-\mu|^{2}\textbf{1}_{|F-\mu|\leq\mu}dvdx+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{4}|F-\mu|\textbf{1}_{|F-\mu|>\mu}dvdx\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\psi\left(\frac{F}{\mu}\right)\mu dvdx.

By
\psi^{\prime}(x)=\ln x
, we can deduce from (
1.1
) that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left[\mu\psi\left(\frac{F}{\mu}\right)\right]+\nabla_{x}\cdot\left[\mu\psi\left(\frac{F}{\mu}\right)v\right]=\nu(\mathcal{M}(F)-F)\ln\frac{F}{\mu}.

By taking integration over
(x,v)\in\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\psi\left(\frac{F}{\mu}\right)\mu dvdx=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nu(\mathcal{M}(F)-F)\ln Fdvdx.

Because of the following inequality

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nu(\mathcal{M}(F)-F)\ln Fdvdx\leq 0,

we get

(3.6)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\psi\left(\frac{F}{\mu}\right)\mu dvdx\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\psi\left(\frac{F_{0}}{\mu}\right)\mu dvdx=\mathcal{E}(F_{0}).

Combining (
3.5
) and (
3.6
) yields that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{4\mu}|F-\mu|^{2}\textbf{1}_{|F-\mu|\leq\mu}dvdx+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{4}|F-\mu|\textbf{1}_{|F-\mu|>\mu}dvdx\leq\mathcal{E}(F_{0}).

We complete the proof of Lemma
3.2
.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

For
q>5
, if a priori assumption (
3.1
) holds, then we have the following estimate

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{|v|\leq N}e^{-(t-s)}\langle v\rangle^{2}|\mathbf{P}f(s,x-v(t-s),v)|dvds

\displaystyle\leq C_{q}N^{5}(1-e^{-\lambda})\mkern 1.3mu\overline{\mkern-1.3muM\mkern-1.3mu}\mkern 1.3mu+\frac{C_{q}}{N^{q-10}}\mkern 1.3mu\overline{\mkern-1.3muM\mkern-1.3mu}\mkern 1.3mu

\displaystyle\quad+CN^{6}(\lambda^{-2}+N^{3})\left(\mathcal{E}(F_{0})+N^{\frac{3}{2}}\sqrt{\mathcal{E}(F_{0})}\right),

for some arbitrary constants
N>1
and
\lambda\in(0,t)
.

Proof.

We split the integration region into
I
,
II_{1}
, and
II_{2}
as follows:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)}\int_{|v|\leq N}\langle v\rangle^{2}|\mathbf{P}f(s,x-v(t-s),v)|dvds\leq I+II_{1}+II_{2},

where

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=\int_{t-\lambda}^{t}e^{-(t-s)}\int_{|v|\leq N}\langle v\rangle^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|f(s,x-v(t-s),u)|(1+|u|+|u|^{2})dudvds,

\displaystyle II_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t-\lambda}e^{-(t-s)}\int_{|v|\leq N}\langle v\rangle^{2}\int_{|u|\geq 2N}|f(s,x-v(t-s),u)|(1+|u|+|u|^{2})dudvds,

\displaystyle II_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t-\lambda}e^{-(t-s)}\int_{|v|\leq N}\langle v\rangle^{2}\int_{|u|\leq 2N}|f(s,x-v(t-s),u)|(1+|u|+|u|^{2})dudvds,

for a positive constant
\lambda\in(0,t)
.

(Estimate of
I
) Multiplying and dividing
\langle u\rangle^{q}
, we have

(3.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}I&\leq\int_{t-\lambda}^{t}e^{-(t-s)}\int_{|v|\leq N}\langle v\rangle^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\langle u\rangle^{-q+2}\langle u\rangle^{q}|f(s,x-v(t-s),u)|dudvds\\
&\leq C_{q}N^{5}(1-e^{-\lambda})\sup_{0\leq s\leq t_{*}}\|f(s)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}\cr&\leq C_{q}N^{5}(1-e^{-\lambda})\mkern 1.3mu\overline{\mkern-1.3muM\mkern-1.3mu}\mkern 1.3mu,\end{split}

where we used
\int_{|v|\leq N}\langle v\rangle^{2}dv\leq CN^{5}
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\langle u\rangle^{-q+2}du\leq C_{q}
for
q>5
.

(Estimate of
II_{1}
) Similarly, we multiply and divide
\langle u\rangle^{q}
on
II_{1}
:

(3.8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}II_{1}&=\int_{0}^{t-\lambda}e^{-(t-s)}\int_{|v|\leq N}\int_{|u|\geq 2N}\langle v\rangle^{2}|f(s,x-v(t-s),u)|(1+|u|+|u|^{2})dudvds\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{t-\lambda}e^{-(t-s)}ds\int_{|v|\leq N}\langle v\rangle^{2}dv\int_{|u|\geq 2N}\langle u\rangle^{-q+2}du\sup_{0\leq s\leq t_{*}}\|f(s)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}\\
&\leq\frac{C_{q}}{N^{q-10}}\mkern 1.3mu\overline{\mkern-1.3muM\mkern-1.3mu}\mkern 1.3mu,\end{split}

where we used
\int_{|v|\leq N}\langle v\rangle^{2}dv\leq CN^{5}
and
\int_{|u|\geq 2N}\langle u\rangle^{-q+2}du\leq CN^{-q+5}
for
q>5
.

(Estimate of
II_{2}
) Using the upper bound
\langle v\rangle^{2}\leq N^{2}
and
(1+|u|+|u|^{2})\leq 4N^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle II_{2}

\displaystyle\leq 4N^{6}\int_{0}^{t-\lambda}e^{-(t-s)}\int_{|v|\leq N}\int_{|u|\leq 2N}|f(s,x-v(t-s),u)|dudvds.

Then we apply a change of variable
y=x-v(t-s)
with
dy=-(t-s)^{3}dv
to make a change
dv
integral to space integral
dy
. Such a change of variable transforms the integral region
\{|v|\leq N\}
to a sphere with a center
x
and radius
v(t-s)
. We note that the maximum radius is
N(t-s)
. Since the space variable is in the torus, if
N(t-s)\geq 1
, then the maximum number of cubic reached by
y
is
(N(t-s))^{3}
.
Conversely, if
N(t-s)\leq 1
, then the minimum number of cubic reached by
y
is
1
.
Thus we have

\displaystyle II_{2}

\displaystyle\leq 4N^{6}\int_{0}^{t-\lambda}e^{-(t-s)}\frac{1+(N(t-s))^{3}}{(t-s)^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{|u|\leq 2N}|f(s,y,u)|dudyds.

In order to apply Lemma
3.2
, we split the integral region into
\{|f|>\mu\}
and
\{|f|\leq\mu\}
, and we multiply
1/\sqrt{\mu(u)}\geq 1
on the region
\{|f|\leq\mu\}
:

\displaystyle II_{2}

\displaystyle\leq 4N^{6}\int_{0}^{t-\lambda}e^{-(t-s)}\frac{1+(N(t-s))^{3}}{(t-s)^{3}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{|u|\leq 2N}|f(s,y,u)|\mathbf{1}_{|f(s,y,u)|>\mu(u)}dudy\right.

\displaystyle\quad\left.+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{|u|\leq 2N}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu(u)}}|f(s,y,u)|\mathbf{1}_{|f(s,y,u)|\leq\mu(u)}dudy\right)ds.

By the Hölder inequality on the second term, and applying Lemma
3.2
, we have

(3.9)

\displaystyle\begin{split}II_{2}&\leq CN^{6}\int_{0}^{t-\lambda}e^{-(t-s)}\frac{1+(N(t-s))^{3}}{(t-s)^{3}}\left[\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{|u|\leq 2N}|f(s,y,u)|\mathbf{1}_{|f(s,y,u)|>\mu(u)}dudy\right.\cr&\quad\left.+\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{|u|\leq 2N}\frac{1}{\mu(u)}|f(s,y,u)|^{2}\mathbf{1}_{|f(s,y,u)|\leq\mu(u)}dudy\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{|u|\leq 2N}1dudy\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right]ds\cr&\leq CN^{6}(\lambda^{-2}+N^{3})\left(\mathcal{E}(F_{0})+N^{\frac{3}{2}}\sqrt{\mathcal{E}(F_{0})}\right),\end{split}

where we used

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t-\lambda}e^{-(t-s)}\frac{1+(N(t-s))^{3}}{(t-s)^{3}}ds=\int_{\lambda}^{t}e^{-\tau}\frac{1+(N\tau)^{3}}{\tau^{3}}d\tau\leq C(\lambda^{-2}+N^{3}).

Combining (
3.7
), (
3.8
) and (
3.9
), we finish the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.4

and
IV_{2}\leq C_{M}|\rho^{f}T^{f}-\rho^{g}T^{g}|
, respectively. To make
\int|v|^{2}fdv
and
\int|v|^{2}gdv
terms, we consider

\displaystyle\rho^{f}T^{f}-\rho^{g}T^{g}

\displaystyle=\left(\rho^{f}T^{f}+\frac{1}{3}\rho^{f}|U^{f}|^{2}-\rho^{g}T^{g}-\frac{1}{3}\rho^{g}|U^{g}|^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{3}\left(\rho^{g}|U^{g}|^{2}-\rho^{f}|U^{f}|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(f-g)|v|^{2}+\frac{1}{3}\left(\rho^{g}|U^{g}|^{2}-\rho^{f}|U^{f}|^{2}\right).

For the last quantity, we apply

\displaystyle\rho^{g}|U^{g}|^{2}-\rho^{f}|U^{f}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{|\rho^{g}U^{g}|^{2}}{\rho^{g}}-\frac{|\rho^{f}U^{f}|^{2}}{\rho^{f}}

\displaystyle=\frac{|\rho^{g}U^{g}|^{2}}{\rho^{g}}-\frac{|\rho^{f}U^{f}|^{2}}{\rho^{g}}+\frac{|\rho^{f}U^{f}|^{2}}{\rho^{g}}-\frac{|\rho^{f}U^{f}|^{2}}{\rho^{f}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{M}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v||f-g|dv+C_{M}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|f-g|dv

\displaystyle\leq C_{M}\|f-g\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}},

to have

\displaystyle|\rho^{f}T^{f}-\rho^{g}T^{g}|\leq C_{M}\|f-g\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}.

Thus we obtain
IV_{2}\leq C_{M}\|f-g\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}
. Finally, for
III
term, we have

\displaystyle III

\displaystyle\leq C_{M}\Bigg|\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq 5}\int_{0}^{1}\langle v\rangle^{q}\left(\frac{\mathcal{P}_{ij}((v-U_{\theta}^{f}),U_{\theta}^{f},T_{\theta}^{f})}{(\rho_{\theta}^{f})^{\alpha_{ij}}(T_{\theta}^{f})^{\beta_{ij}}}\mathcal{M}^{f}(\theta)-\frac{\mathcal{P}_{ij}((v-U_{\theta}^{g}),U_{\theta}^{g},T_{\theta}^{g})}{(\rho_{\theta}^{g})^{\alpha_{ij}}(T_{\theta}^{g})^{\beta_{ij}}}\mathcal{M}^{g}(\theta)\right)(1-\theta)d\theta\Bigg|.

We split the terms inside
III
as follows:

\displaystyle III_{1}^{ij}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\mathcal{P}_{ij}((v-U_{\theta}^{f}),U_{\theta}^{f},T_{\theta}^{f})}{(\rho_{\theta}^{f})^{\alpha_{ij}}(T_{\theta}^{f})^{\beta_{ij}}}-\frac{\mathcal{P}_{ij}((v-U_{\theta}^{g}),U_{\theta}^{g},T_{\theta}^{g})}{(\rho_{\theta}^{g})^{\alpha_{ij}}(T_{\theta}^{g})^{\beta_{ij}}}\right)\langle v\rangle^{q}\mathcal{M}^{f}(\theta),

\displaystyle III_{2}^{ij}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{P}_{ij}((v-U_{\theta}^{g}),U_{\theta}^{g},T_{\theta}^{g})}{(\rho_{\theta}^{g})^{\alpha_{ij}}(T_{\theta}^{g})^{\beta_{ij}}}\langle v\rangle^{q}(\mathcal{M}^{f}(\theta)-\mathcal{M}^{g}(\theta)).

For the
III_{1}^{ij}
term, we apply triangle inequality several times and use the estimate (
4.1
) and (
4.2
) to have
III_{1}^{ij}\leq C_{M}\|f-g\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}
. For the term
\langle v\rangle^{q}(\mathcal{M}^{f}(\theta)-\mathcal{M}^{g}(\theta))
inside
III_{2}^{ij}
, we can have the Lipschitz continuity of the local Maxwellian
III_{2}^{ij}\leq C_{M}\|f-g\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}
as in
[
35
,
47
]
.
This completes the proof.
∎

We prove the local wellposedness theory of the BGK solutions.

Proposition 4.1
.

Consider the BGK equation (
2.1
) with initial data
f_{0}
which satisfies
\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}<\infty
and (
1.9
). Then, there exists a time
t_{0}=t_{0}(\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}})
depending only on
\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}
such that there exists a unique local-in-time solution for
t\in[0,t_{0}]
which safisfies

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq s\leq t_{0}}\|f(s)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}\leq 2\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}.

Proof.

From (
2.1
) in Lemma
2.1
, we obtain the following mild solution:

\displaystyle f(t,x,v)=e^{-t}f_{0}(x-vt,v)+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)}[\mathbf{P}f+\Gamma(f)](s,x-v(t-s),v)ds.

Multiplying the above by
\langle v\rangle^{q}
, we have

\displaystyle\langle v\rangle^{q}|f(t,x,v)|\leq e^{-t}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)}\langle v\rangle^{q}|[\mathbf{P}f+\Gamma(f)](s,x-v(t-s),v)|ds.

By definition (
2.2
) of
\mathbf{P}f
in Lemma
2.1
, we directly deduce that

(4.3)

\displaystyle\langle v\rangle^{q}\mathbf{P}f(s)\leq C_{q}\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\|f(s)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}},\quad 0\leq s\leq t.

To obtain estimate for
\Gamma(f)
, we note that the macroscopic fields
(\rho,U,T)
and
(\rho_{\theta},U_{\theta},T_{\theta})
are bounded depending on
M_{0}
by the assumption (
1.9
) with Lemma
3.4
and Lemma
3.5
. Then using a similar argument in the proof of (
3.16
), we have

\displaystyle\langle v\rangle^{q}|\Gamma(f)(s)|\leq C_{q}\left(\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\|f(s)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}\right)^{n}e^{C_{q}(\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\|f(s)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}})^{a}t},\quad 0\leq s\leq t,

where
C_{q},n
and
a
are the same constant as in (
3.16
). Note that (
3.16
) holds for
\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\|f(s)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}
instead of
\mkern 1.3mu\overline{\mkern-1.3muM\mkern-1.3mu}\mkern 1.3mu
. In sum, we obtain

\displaystyle\|f(t)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}

\displaystyle\leq e^{-t}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}+C_{q}(1-e^{-t})\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\|f(s)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}

\displaystyle\quad+C_{q}\left(\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\|f(s)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}\right)^{n}te^{C_{q}(\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\|f(s)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}})^{a}t}.

Hence, there exists
t_{0}=t_{0}(M_{0})
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq t_{0}}\|f(s)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}\leq 2\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}.

For the uniqueness of solutions, we assume that
g
satisfies the reformulated BGK equation (
2.1
) with the same initial data
f_{0}
and

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq s\leq t_{0}}\|g(s)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}\leq 2\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}.

Using the mild formulation, we have

\displaystyle(f-g)(t,x,v)=\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)}[\mathbf{P}(f-g)+\Gamma(f)-\Gamma(g)](s,x-v(t-s),v)ds.

Moreover, one obtains that

\displaystyle\|(f-g)(t)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}\leq\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)}\left(\|\mathbf{P}(f-g)(s)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}+\|(\Gamma(f)-\Gamma(g))(s)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}\right)ds.

For the
\mathbf{P}(f-g)
part above, it follows from (
4.3
) that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)}\|\mathbf{P}(f-g)(s)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}ds\leq C_{q}\int_{0}^{t}\|(f-g)(s)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}ds.

To treat the nonlinear part
\Gamma(f)-\Gamma(g)
, using Lemma
4.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)}\langle v\rangle^{q}\|(\Gamma(f)-\Gamma(g))(s)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}ds\leq C_{f_{0}}\int_{0}^{t}\|(f-g)(s)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}ds,

where
C_{f_{0}}
is a constant depending on
\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}
.
Combining the estimates for
\mathbf{P}(f-g)
and
\Gamma(f)-\Gamma(g)
, one obtains that

\displaystyle\|(f-g)(t)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}\leq C_{q,f_{0}}\int_{0}^{t}\|(f-g)(s)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}ds,

which implies the uniqueness due to Grönwall’s inequality.
∎

6.
Asymptotic stability for small amplitude regime

In this section, we prove that if the initial
L^{\infty}
norm is sufficiently small, then there exists a unique non-negative global solution.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
f_{0}
satisfy the conservation laws (
1.10
). There exists
\delta>0
such that if
\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}\leq\delta
, then there exists a unique global solution of the BGK model (
1.6
). Moreover, the following holds:

(1)

The solution
f(t,x,v)
satisfies the conservation laws

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}f(t,x,v)(1,v,|v|^{2})dvdx=0.

(2)

The solution is non-negative:
F(t,x,v)=\mu(v)+f(t,x,v)\geq 0
.

(3)

The perturbation decays exponentially:

\displaystyle\|f(t)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{q}\delta e^{-kt},

for positive constants
k
and
C_{q}
.

(4)

Let
f
and
\tilde{f}
be solutions corresponding to the initial data
f_{0}
and
\tilde{f}_{0}
, respectively. Then

\displaystyle\sup_{s\in[0,t]}e^{ks}\|(f-\tilde{f})(s)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{q}\|f_{0}-\tilde{f}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}},

for positive constants
k
and
C_{q}
.

To prove the above proposition, we follow the argument of
[
6
]
where the asymptotic stability of the Boltzmann equation (with some boundary condition) is proved in
L^{\infty}
with small initial data.
We decompose (
1.6
) into the following two equations:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f_{1}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{1}+f_{1}=\Gamma(f_{1}+f_{2}),\qquad f_{1}(0,x,v)=f_{0}(x,v),

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f_{2}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{2}=(\mathbf{P}f_{2}-f_{2})+\mathbf{P}f_{1},\qquad f_{2}(0,x,v)=0,

where
f=f_{1}+f_{2}
. In the following, we study the above two equations to derive the existence and asymptotic behavior of
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
.

Lemma 6.1
.

There exists
\delta>0
such that if
\|f(t)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}\leq\delta
and
\|g(t)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}\leq\delta
, then we have

\displaystyle(1)~

\displaystyle\|\Gamma(f)(t)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}\leq C\|f(t)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}^{2},

\displaystyle(2)~

\displaystyle\|(\Gamma(f)-\Gamma(g))(t)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}\leq C_{\delta}\|(f-g)(t)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}},

for positive constants
C
and
0<C_{\delta}<1
.

Proof.

(1) By Lemma
3.7
, the assumption
\|f\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}\leq\delta
implies

\displaystyle|\rho-1|,~|U|,~|T-1|\leq 2\delta.

So that, for a sufficiently small
\delta
, the nonlinear term
\Gamma_{1}
can be reduced to

\displaystyle\big|\langle v\rangle^{q}\Gamma_{1}(f)\big|

\displaystyle\leq C\big|\langle v\rangle^{q}(\mathbf{P}f-f)\big|\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(1+|v|^{2})fdv\Big|\leq C\|f\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}^{2}.

Applying a similar process to Lemma
5.3
, we also have

\displaystyle|\langle v\rangle^{q}\Gamma_{2}(f)|

\displaystyle=\rho^{a}T^{b}\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq 5}\int_{0}^{1}\langle v\rangle^{q}\frac{\mathcal{P}_{ij}((v-U_{\theta}),U_{\theta},T_{\theta})}{\rho_{\theta}^{\alpha_{ij}}T_{\theta}^{\beta_{ij}}}\mathcal{M}(\theta)(1-\theta)d\theta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}fe_{i}dv\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}fe_{j}dv

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}^{2}.

(2) Since we have
|\rho-1|,~|U|,~|T-1|\leq 2\delta
for
f
and
g
, applying the same argument as in Lemma
4.1
with

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}fe_{i}dv\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(1+|v|+|v|^{2})|f|dv\leq C_{q}\|f\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}\leq C_{q}\delta,\quad\mbox{for}\quad i=1,\cdots,5,

we can obtain
C_{\delta}<1
satisfying

\displaystyle\|\Gamma(f)-\Gamma(g)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}\leq C_{\delta}\|f-g\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}},

for sufficiently small
\delta
.
∎

Lemma 6.2
.

There exists
\delta>0
such that if
\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}\leq\delta
and
\|g(t)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}\leq C_{q}\delta e^{-(1-\epsilon)t}
for any
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, then there exists a solution
f_{1}
to the following equation

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f_{1}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{1}+f_{1}=\Gamma(f_{1}+g),\qquad f_{1}(0,x,v)=f_{0}(x,v),

satisfying

\displaystyle\|f_{1}(t)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}\leq e^{-(1-\epsilon)t}(\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}+\delta).

Proof.

We define the following iteration for
f_{1}^{n}
starting with
f_{1}^{0}(t,x,v)=0

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f_{1}^{n+1}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{1}^{n+1}+f_{1}^{n+1}=\Gamma(f_{1}^{n}+g),\qquad f_{1}(0,x,v)=f_{0}(x,v).

Then we prove that
\{f_{1}^{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
is uniformly bounded and Cauchy. We write the equation in the mild form:

(6.1)

\displaystyle f_{1}^{n+1}(t,x,v)=e^{-t}f_{0}(x-vt,v)+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)}\Gamma(f_{1}^{n}+g)(s,x-v(t-s),v)ds.

Taking
L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}
on (
6.1
) yields

\displaystyle\|f_{1}^{n+1}(t)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}

\displaystyle\leq e^{-t}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)}\|\Gamma(f_{1}^{n}+g)(s)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}ds.

We multiply
e^{(1-\epsilon)t}
on both sides and use Lemma
6.1
to get

\displaystyle e^{(1-\epsilon)t}\|f_{1}^{n+1}(t)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\epsilon t}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{(1-\epsilon)s}e^{-\epsilon(t-s)}\|\Gamma(f_{1}^{n}+g)(s)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}ds

\displaystyle\leq\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}+C\sup_{s\in[0,t]}e^{(1-\epsilon)s}\|(f_{1}^{n}+g)(s)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}^{2}.

Taking supremum on each side, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{s\in[0,t]}e^{(1-\epsilon)s}\|f_{1}^{n+1}(s)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}

\displaystyle\leq\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}+C\sup_{s\in[0,t]}e^{(1-\epsilon)s}\left(\|f_{1}^{n}(s)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}^{2}\right)+CC_{q}^{2}\delta^{2}.

Thus, if the
n
-th step has the following bound,

\displaystyle\sup_{s\in[0,t]}e^{(1-\epsilon)s}\|f_{1}^{n}(s)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}\leq\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}+\delta,

then the
(n+1)
-th step satisfies

\displaystyle\sup_{s\in[0,t]}e^{(1-\epsilon)s}\|f_{1}^{n+1}(s)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}

\displaystyle=\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}+C(2\delta)^{2}+CC_{q}^{2}\delta^{2}\leq\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}+\delta,

for sufficiently small
\delta
satisfying
C(4+C_{q}^{2})\delta^{2}\leq\delta
. This gives the desired uniform boundedness:

\displaystyle\sup_{s\in[0,t]}e^{(1-\epsilon)s}\|f_{1}^{n}(s)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}\leq\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}+\delta.

To prove that
\{f_{1}^{n}\}
is a Cauchy sequence, we consider the difference between
f^{n+1}
and
f^{n}
:

\displaystyle e^{t}(f_{1}^{n+1}-f_{1}^{n})(t,x,v)=\int_{0}^{t}e^{s}\left[\Gamma(f_{1}^{n}+g)-\Gamma(f_{1}^{n-1}+g)\right](s,x-v(t-s),v)ds.

Since, for sufficiently small
\delta
, we have for all
n\geq 0

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(1,v,|v|^{2})(f_{1}^{n}+g)dv\leq\|f_{1}^{n}\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}+\|g\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}\leq(C_{q}+1)\delta,

we can employ Lemma
6.1
(2) to get

\displaystyle\|\Gamma(f^{n}+g)-\Gamma(f^{n-1}+g)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}\leq C_{\delta}\|f^{n}-f^{n-1}\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}},

for
n\geq 1
. Therefore we have

\displaystyle\sup_{s\in[0,t]}e^{(1-\epsilon)s}\|(f_{1}^{n+1}-f_{1}^{n})(s)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\delta}\sup_{s\in[0,t]}e^{(1-\epsilon)s}\|(f_{1}^{n}-f_{1}^{n-1})(s)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}},

for
0<C_{\delta}<1
. This completes the proof.
∎

Before we proceed to the next lemma, we define

\displaystyle\Pi(f)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\mathbf{P}fdx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}fdvdx\mu+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}fvdvdx\cdot(v\mu)+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}f\frac{|v|^{2}-3}{\sqrt{6}}dvdx\left(\frac{|v|^{2}-3}{\sqrt{6}}\mu\right).

Note that, unlike projection operator
\mathbf{P}
,
\Pi
does commutes with transport
v\cdot\nabla_{x}
.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
\|g\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}<\infty
. Then there exists a unique solution
f_{2}\in L_{t}^{\infty}L^{\infty}_{x,v}(\mu^{-\zeta})
to

(6.2)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f_{2}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{2}=(\mathbf{P}f_{2}-f_{2})+\mathbf{P}g,\qquad f_{2}(0,x,v)=0,

where
\zeta\in[0,1)
. Moreover, if
\Pi(f_{2}+g)=0
and
\|g(t)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}\leq e^{-(1-\epsilon)t}(\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}+\delta)
for
\epsilon\in(1-\eta_{s},1)
(
\eta_{s}
will be determined in the proof), then we have

\displaystyle\|f_{2}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}(\mu^{-\zeta})}\leq C_{q}\delta e^{-(1-\epsilon)t}.

Proof.

Let
S(t)
be the semi-group so that
S(t)f_{0}
solves the following equation:

(6.3)

(\partial_{t}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}+1)f=\mathbf{P}f,\qquad f(0)=f_{0},\qquad\Pi f_{0}=0.

We first consider the
L^{\infty}
decay of
S(t)
. We write (
6.3
) in the mild form

\displaystyle f(t,x,v)=e^{-t}f_{0}(x-vt,v)+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)}\mathbf{P}f(s,x-v(t-s),v)ds.

Multiplying
\mu^{-\zeta}
and applying double iteration on
\mathbf{P}f
, we have

\displaystyle\|f(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}(\mu^{-\zeta})}\lesssim e^{-\frac{1}{2}t}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}(\mu^{-\zeta})}+C_{T_{0}}\int_{0}^{t}\|f(s)\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}(\mu^{-1/2})}ds,\quad\mbox{for}\quad t\in[0,T_{0}],

where we used
\|\mathbf{P}f\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}(\mu^{-\zeta})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}(\mu^{-1/2})}
. Recalling that there is
\eta
such that
\|f(t)\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}(\mu^{-1/2})}\leq Ce^{-\eta t}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}(\mu^{-1/2})}
(See
[
46
,
48
]
), we have from Proposition 5.4 in
[
6
]
that

(6.4)

\displaystyle\|S(t)f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}(\mu^{-\zeta})}\leq e^{-\eta_{s}t}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}(\mu^{-\zeta})},

for
0<\eta_{s}<1
. Now, we consider the estimate of
f_{2}
in (
6.2
). From
\Pi(f_{2}+g)=0
, we can estimate
\Pi f_{2}
as follows:

(6.5)

\displaystyle\|\Pi f_{2}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}(\mu^{-\zeta})}

\displaystyle=\|\Pi g(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}(\mu^{-\zeta})}\leq C_{q}\|g(t)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}\leq C_{q}e^{-(1-\epsilon)t}(\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}+\delta).

To estimate
(I-\Pi)f_{2}
, we rewrite
f_{2}
by using the definition of the semi-group
S(t)
:

\displaystyle f_{2}=\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)\mathbf{P}gds.

We claim that
I-\Pi
commutes with the semi-group
S(t)
:

(6.6)

\displaystyle(I-\Pi)f_{2}=\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)(I-\Pi)\mathbf{P}gds.

With this claim assumed to be true, we apply (
6.4
) and (
6.5
) to obtain

(6.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|(I-\Pi)f_{2}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}(\mu^{-\zeta})}&\leq\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\eta_{s}(t-s)}\|\mathbf{P}g(s)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}(\mu^{-\zeta})}ds\cr&\leq C_{q}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\eta_{s}(t-s)}e^{-(1-\epsilon)s}(\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}+\delta)ds\cr&\leq\frac{C_{q}}{\eta_{s}-(1-\epsilon)}e^{-(1-\epsilon)t}(\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}+\delta),\end{split}

where we used
0<1-\epsilon<\eta_{s}
. Combining (
6.5
) and (
6.7
) gives the desired result:

\displaystyle\|f_{2}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}(\mu^{-\zeta})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\Pi f_{2}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}(\mu^{-\zeta})}+\|(I-\Pi)f_{2}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}(\mu^{-\zeta})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{q}e^{-(1-\epsilon)t}(\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}+\delta).

Now we go back to the proof of the claim (
6.6
). We first prove that
\Pi
commutes with
\mathbf{P}
:

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\Pi f

\displaystyle=\mathbf{P}\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}fdvdx\mu+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}fvdvdx\cdot(v\mu)+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}f\frac{|v|^{2}-3}{\sqrt{6}}dvdx\left(\frac{|v|^{2}-3}{\sqrt{6}}\mu\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{P}(\mu)\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}fdvdx+\mathbf{P}(v\mu)\cdot\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}fvdvdx+\mathbf{P}\left(\frac{|v|^{2}-3}{\sqrt{6}}\mu\right)\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}f\frac{|v|^{2}-3}{\sqrt{6}}dvdx

\displaystyle=\Pi\mathbf{P}f.

So that
(I-\Pi)(I-\mathbf{P})=(I-\mathbf{P})(I-\Pi)
. We can easily check that
\Pi
also commutes with
v\cdot\nabla_{x}
as in
[
6
]
. Now, we apply
(I-\Pi)
on (
6.2
) and use these commutation relations, we get

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(I-\Pi)f_{2}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}(I-\Pi)f_{2}=(P-I)(I-\Pi)f_{2}+(I-\Pi)\mathbf{P}g.

This completes the proof of the claim (
6.6
).
∎

Proof of Proposition
6.1
.

We define the following iteration:

(6.8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\partial_{t}f_{1}^{n+1}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{1}^{n+1}+f_{1}^{n+1}=\Gamma(f_{1}^{n+1}+f_{2}^{n}),\qquad f_{1}^{n+1}(0,x,v)=f_{0}(x,v),\cr&\partial_{t}f_{2}^{n+1}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{2}^{n+1}=(\mathbf{P}f_{2}^{n+1}-f_{2}^{n+1})+\mathbf{P}f_{1}^{n+1},\qquad f_{2}^{n+1}(0,x,v)=0,\end{split}

for
n\geq 0
, start with
f_{1}^{0}(t,x,v)=0
, and
f_{2}^{0}(t,x,v)=0
. The existence of a solution is guaranteed by Lemma
6.2
and
6.3
in the following manner: For the
g=f_{2}^{n}
case, Lemma
6.2
implies that if
\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}\leq\delta
and
\|f_{2}^{n}(t)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}\leq C_{q}\delta e^{-(1-\epsilon)t}
, then there exists solution
f_{1}^{n+1}
of equation
\eqref{f1f2}_{1}
, and
f_{1}^{n+1}
satisfies

\displaystyle\|f_{1}^{n+1}(t)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}\leq e^{-(1-\epsilon)t}(\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}+\delta).

Similarly, for case
g=f_{1}^{n+1}
, Lemma
6.3
implies that if
\|f_{1}^{n+1}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}\leq\infty
, then there exists a unique solution
f_{2}^{n+1}
of
\eqref{f1f2}_{2}
in
L_{t}^{\infty}L^{\infty}_{x,v}(\mu^{-\zeta})
.
Moreover, if
\Pi(f_{2}^{n+1}+f_{1}^{n+1})=0
and
\|f_{1}^{n+1}(t)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}\leq e^{-(1-\epsilon)t}(\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}+\delta)
, then we have

\displaystyle\|f_{2}^{n+1}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}(\mu^{-\zeta})}\leq C_{q}\delta e^{-(1-\epsilon)t}.

Since we started the iteration with
f_{1}^{0}(t,x,v)=0
, and
f_{2}^{0}(t,x,v)=0
, by induction, we obtain

(6.9)

\displaystyle\|f_{1}^{n}(t)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}

\displaystyle\leq(\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}+\delta)e^{-(1-\epsilon)t},\qquad\|f_{2}^{n}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}(\mu^{-\zeta})}\leq C_{q}\delta e^{-(1-\epsilon)t},

for all
n\geq 0
. We note that as we proved in Lemma
6.2
, sequence
\{f_{1}^{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
is the Cauchy sequence. Thus there exists
f_{1}\in L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}
such that

\displaystyle f_{1}^{n}\rightarrow f_{1},\qquad\mbox{with}\qquad\|f_{1}(t)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}

\displaystyle\leq(\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}+\delta)e^{-(1-\epsilon)t}.

For sequence
\{f_{2}^{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
, we obtained uniform boundedness. Thus there exists a weak star converging subsequence
f_{2}
such that

\displaystyle f_{2}^{n}\overset{\ast}{\rightharpoonup}f_{2},\qquad\mbox{with}\qquad\|f_{2}(t)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{q}\delta e^{-(1-\epsilon)t}.

Thanks to (
6.9
),
f_{1}^{n}
and
f_{2}^{n}
are weakly compact in
L^{1}
, and for any finite time
T
, there exists a positive constant
C_{T}
such that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v|^{3}(\mu+f_{1}^{n+1}+f_{2}^{n})dvdxdt<C_{T}.

This third-moment estimate combined with velocity averaging lemma in
[
35
]
, we have the following strong compactness for the macroscopic fields

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\mu+f_{1}^{n+1}+f_{2}^{n})dv:=\rho^{n}\rightarrow\rho,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}v(\mu+f_{1}^{n+1}+f_{2}^{n})dv:=\rho^{n}U^{n}\rightarrow\rho U,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v|^{2}(\mu+f_{1}^{n+1}+f_{2}^{n})dv:=3\rho^{n}T^{n}+\rho^{n}|U^{n}|^{2}\rightarrow 3\rho T+\rho|U|^{2}.

On the other hand, the weak compactness of
f_{1}^{n}
and
f_{2}^{n}
gives rise to the weak compactness of the local Maxwellian. The weak compactness of
\mathcal{M}(\mu+f_{1}^{n+1}+f_{2}^{n})
together with the strong compactness of
(\rho^{n},U^{n},T^{n})
yields the following desired weak convergence:

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(\mu+f_{1}^{n+1}+f_{2}^{n})\rightharpoonup\mathcal{M}(\mu+f_{1}+f_{2}),

in
L^{1}([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^{3})
. See
[
35
,
48
]
for detailed arguments. This guarantees that
f_{1}+f_{2}
is a solution of the system (
6.8
).
Moreover, the solution
f_{1}+f_{2}
satisfies

\displaystyle\|(f_{1}+f_{2})(t)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{q}\delta e^{-(1-\epsilon)t}.

To prove the conservation laws, we add the two equations in (
6.8
):

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(f_{1}^{n+1}+f_{2}^{n+1})+v\cdot\nabla_{x}(f_{1}^{n+1}+f_{2}^{n+1})+(f_{1}^{n+1}+f_{2}^{n+1})=\mathbf{P}(f_{1}^{n+1}+f_{2}^{n+1})+\Gamma(f_{1}^{n+1}+f_{2}^{n}).

We take
\Pi
on both sides to have

(6.10)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\Pi(f_{1}^{n+1}+f_{2}^{n+1})=\Pi\left(\Gamma(f_{1}^{n+1}+f_{2}^{n})\right).

Now we show that
\Pi(\Gamma(f))=0
for any function
f\in L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}
.
For any function
f\in L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}
with
F=\mu+f
, the cancellation property of the BGK operator (
1.4
) implies

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}(\nu(\mathcal{M}(F)-F))=0.

Applying the linearization of the BGK operator in Lemma
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle 0=\mathbf{P}(\nu(\mathcal{M}(F)-F))=\mathbf{P}((\mathbf{P}f-f)+\Gamma(f))=\mathbf{P}(\Gamma(f)),

which guarantees
\Pi\left(\Gamma(f_{1}^{n+1}+f_{2}^{n})\right)=0
. This combining with (
6.10
), we have

\displaystyle\Pi(f_{1}^{n+1}(t)+f_{2}^{n+1}(t))=\Pi(f_{1}^{n+1}(0)+f_{2}^{n+1}(0))=\Pi f_{0}=0.

Therefore
f_{1}^{n}+f_{2}^{n}
satisfies the conservation laws for all
n\geq 0
.

For stability and uniqueness, let
f
and
\tilde{f}
be solutions corresponding to the initial data
f_{0}
and
\tilde{f}_{0}
, respectively. Subtracting the following two equations

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f+f

\displaystyle=\mathbf{P}f+\Gamma(f),

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\tilde{f}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}\tilde{f}+\tilde{f}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{P}\tilde{f}+\Gamma(\tilde{f}),

yields

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(f-\tilde{f})+v\cdot\nabla_{x}(f-\tilde{f})+f-\tilde{f}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{P}(f-\tilde{f})+\Gamma(f)-\Gamma(\tilde{f}).

Using the semi-group operator, we write

\displaystyle(f-\tilde{f})(t,x,v)=S(t)(f_{0}-\tilde{f}_{0})(x-vt,v)+\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)(\Gamma(f)-\Gamma(\tilde{f}))(s,x-v(t-s),v)ds.

Multiplying
e^{(1-\epsilon)t}\langle v\rangle^{q}
on both sides and applying Lemma
6.1
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{s\in[0,t]}e^{(1-\epsilon)s}\|(f-\tilde{f})(s)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\epsilon t}\|f_{0}-\tilde{f}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\epsilon(t-s)}e^{(1-\epsilon)s}\|(\Gamma(f)-\Gamma(\tilde{f}))(s)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}ds

\displaystyle\leq\|f_{0}-\tilde{f}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}C_{\delta}\sup_{s\in[0,t]}e^{(1-\epsilon)s}\|(f-\tilde{f})(s)\|_{L^{\infty,q}_{x,v}}.

For sufficiently small
C_{\delta}<\epsilon
, we obtain stabtility and uniqueness of the solution. For the non-negativity of the solution, we recover the equation for
F=\mu+f
:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}F+v\cdot\nabla_{x}F+\nu F=\nu\mathcal{M}(F).

Then the mild form of
F
with non-negativity of
F_{0}
and
\mathcal{M}(F)
gives the non-negativty of the solution

\displaystyle F(t,x,v)=e^{-\int_{0}^{t}\nu(s,x)ds}F_{0}(x-vt,v)+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\int_{s}^{t}\nu(\tau,x)d\tau}\mathcal{M}(F)(s,x-v(t-s),v)ds\geq 0.

This completes the proof of Proposition
6.1
.
∎

\nabla_{(\rho,U,T)}R
is equal to
\nabla_{(\rho,U,T)}\left(\left(\frac{|U|^{2}-3T+3}{3\rho^{2}}-\frac{2}{3}\frac{U\cdot(v-U)}{\rho T}+\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\frac{1}{\rho}\left(-\frac{3}{2}\frac{1}{T}+\frac{|v-U|^{2}}{2T^{2}}\right)\right)\mathcal{M}(F)\right)
, and which become

\displaystyle\left[{\begin{array}[]{c}-\frac{|U|^{2}-3T+3}{3\rho^{3}}\cr\frac{2U_{1}}{3\rho^{2}}-\frac{2}{3}\frac{v_{1}-2U_{1}}{\rho T}-\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\frac{1}{\rho}\left(\frac{v_{1}-U_{1}}{T^{2}}\right)+\frac{v_{1}-U_{1}}{T}\cr\frac{2U_{2}}{3\rho^{2}}-\frac{2}{3}\frac{v_{2}-2U_{2}}{\rho T}-\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\frac{1}{\rho}\left(\frac{v_{2}-U_{2}}{T^{2}}\right)+\frac{v_{2}-U_{2}}{T}\cr\frac{2U_{3}}{3\rho^{2}}-\frac{2}{3}\frac{v_{3}-2U_{3}}{\rho T}-\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\frac{1}{\rho}\left(\frac{v_{3}-U_{3}}{T^{2}}\right)+\frac{v_{3}-U_{3}}{T}\cr-\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}+\frac{2}{3}\frac{U\cdot(v-U)}{\rho T^{2}}+\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\frac{1}{\rho}\left(\frac{3}{2}\frac{1}{T^{2}}-\frac{|v-U|^{2}}{T^{3}}\right)+\left(-\frac{3}{2}\frac{1}{T}+\frac{|v-U|^{2}}{2T^{2}}\right)\end{array}}\right]\mathcal{M}(F).

Note that we computed
\nabla_{(\rho,U,T)}R
in (
A.3
). Now we should multiply the matrix
\nabla_{(\rho,U,T)}R
by
\left(\frac{\partial(\rho,\rho U,G)}{\partial(\rho,U,T)}\right)^{-1}
.
It is a product of two
5\times 5
matrices. We present the calculations for each row.

\bullet
The first row of
\nabla_{(\rho,\rho U,G)}^{2}\mathcal{M}(F)
:

\displaystyle\left[0~\frac{U_{1}}{\rho^{3}}~\frac{U_{2}}{\rho^{3}}~\frac{U_{3}}{\rho^{3}}~-\frac{|U|^{2}-3T+3}{3\rho^{3}}\right]\mathcal{M}(F).

\bullet
The second row of
\nabla_{(\rho,\rho U,G)}^{2}\mathcal{M}(F)
:

\displaystyle\left[{\begin{array}[]{c}\frac{v_{1}-U_{1}}{\rho^{2}T}\cr-\frac{U_{1}^{2}}{\rho^{4}}-\left(\frac{1}{\rho^{3}}+\frac{1}{\rho^{2}T}\right)+\left(-\frac{U_{1}}{\rho^{3}}+\frac{v_{1}-U_{1}}{\rho^{2}T}\right)\frac{v_{1}-U_{1}}{T}\cr-\frac{U_{1}U_{2}}{\rho^{4}}+\left(-\frac{U_{2}}{\rho^{3}}+\frac{v_{2}-U_{2}}{\rho^{2}T}\right)\frac{v_{1}-U_{1}}{T}\cr-\frac{U_{1}U_{3}}{\rho^{4}}+\left(-\frac{U_{3}}{\rho^{3}}+\frac{v_{3}-U_{3}}{\rho^{2}T}\right)\frac{v_{1}-U_{1}}{T}\cr\frac{U_{1}}{\rho}\frac{|U|^{2}-3T+3}{3\rho^{3}}+\frac{1}{\rho}\left(\frac{2U_{1}}{3\rho^{2}}-\frac{2}{3}\frac{v_{1}-2U_{1}}{\rho T}-\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\frac{1}{\rho}\left(\frac{v_{1}-U_{1}}{T^{2}}\right)+\frac{v_{1}-U_{1}}{T}\right)\end{array}}\right]^{T}\mathcal{M}(F).

\bullet
The third row of
\nabla_{(\rho,\rho U,G)}^{2}\mathcal{M}(F)
:

\displaystyle\left[{\begin{array}[]{c}\frac{v_{2}-U_{2}}{\rho^{2}T}\cr-\frac{U_{1}U_{2}}{\rho^{4}}+\left(-\frac{U_{1}}{\rho^{3}}+\frac{v_{1}-U_{1}}{\rho^{2}T}\right)\frac{v_{2}-U_{2}}{T}\cr-\frac{U_{2}^{2}}{\rho^{4}}-\left(\frac{1}{\rho^{3}}+\frac{1}{\rho^{2}T}\right)+\left(-\frac{U_{2}}{\rho^{3}}+\frac{v_{2}-U_{2}}{\rho^{2}T}\right)\frac{v_{2}-U_{2}}{T}\cr-\frac{U_{2}U_{3}}{\rho^{4}}+\left(-\frac{U_{3}}{\rho^{3}}+\frac{v_{3}-U_{3}}{\rho^{2}T}\right)\frac{v_{2}-U_{2}}{T}\cr\frac{U_{1}}{\rho}\frac{|U|^{2}-3T+3}{3\rho^{3}}+\frac{1}{\rho}\left(\frac{2U_{2}}{3\rho^{2}}-\frac{2}{3}\frac{v_{2}-2U_{2}}{\rho T}-\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\frac{1}{\rho}\left(\frac{v_{2}-U_{2}}{T^{2}}\right)+\frac{v_{2}-U_{2}}{T}\right)\end{array}}\right]^{T}\mathcal{M}(F).

\bullet
The fourth row of
\nabla_{(\rho,\rho U,G)}^{2}\mathcal{M}(F)
:

\displaystyle\left[{\begin{array}[]{c}\frac{v_{3}-U_{3}}{\rho^{2}T}\cr-\frac{U_{1}U_{3}}{\rho^{4}}+\left(-\frac{U_{1}}{\rho^{3}}+\frac{v_{1}-U_{1}}{\rho^{2}T}\right)\frac{v_{3}-U_{3}}{T}\cr-\frac{U_{2}U_{3}}{\rho^{4}}+\left(-\frac{U_{2}}{\rho^{3}}+\frac{v_{2}-U_{2}}{\rho^{2}T}\right)\frac{v_{3}-U_{3}}{T}\cr-\frac{U_{3}^{2}}{\rho^{4}}-\left(\frac{1}{\rho^{3}}+\frac{1}{\rho^{2}T}\right)+\left(-\frac{U_{3}}{\rho^{3}}+\frac{v_{3}-U_{3}}{\rho^{2}T}\right)\frac{v_{3}-U_{3}}{T}\cr\frac{U_{3}}{\rho}\frac{|U|^{2}-3T+3}{3\rho^{3}}+\frac{1}{\rho}\left(\frac{2U_{3}}{3\rho^{2}}-\frac{2}{3}\frac{v_{3}-2U_{3}}{\rho T}-\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\frac{1}{\rho}\left(\frac{v_{3}-U_{3}}{T^{2}}\right)+\frac{v_{3}-U_{3}}{T}\right)\end{array}}\right]^{T}\mathcal{M}(F).

\bullet
The fifth row of
\nabla_{(\rho,\rho U,G)}^{2}\mathcal{M}(F)
:
Since the fifth row is too complicated, we present each component separately.
The first component of the fifth row, i.e.
[\nabla_{(\rho,\rho U,G)}^{2}\mathcal{M}(F)]_{51}
:

\displaystyle\left(-\frac{2}{3}\frac{U}{\rho}\cdot\frac{v-U}{\rho T}+\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\frac{1}{\rho}\left(-\frac{3}{2}\frac{1}{\rho T}+\frac{|v-U|^{2}}{2\rho T^{2}}\right)\right)\mathcal{M}(F).

The second component of the fifth row, i.e.
[\nabla_{(\rho,\rho U,G)}^{2}\mathcal{M}(F)]_{52}
:

\displaystyle\bigg[\frac{|U|^{2}-3T+3}{3\rho}\frac{U_{1}}{\rho^{3}}-\frac{2}{3}\frac{U}{\rho}\cdot\frac{v-U}{T}\left(\left(-\frac{U_{1}}{\rho^{2}}+\frac{v_{1}-U_{1}}{\rho T}\right)\right)-\frac{2}{3}\frac{U_{1}}{\rho}\left(-\left(\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}+\frac{1}{\rho T}\right)\right)

\displaystyle+\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\frac{1}{\rho}\left(-\frac{v_{1}-U_{1}}{\rho T^{2}}+\left(-\frac{U_{1}}{\rho^{2}}+\frac{v_{1}-U_{1}}{\rho T}\right)\left(-\frac{3}{2}\frac{1}{T}+\frac{|v-U|^{2}}{2T^{2}}\right)\right)\bigg]\mathcal{M}(F).

The third component of the fifth row, i.e.
[\nabla_{(\rho,\rho U,G)}^{2}\mathcal{M}(F)]_{53}
:

\displaystyle\bigg[\frac{|U|^{2}-3T+3}{3\rho}\frac{U_{2}}{\rho^{3}}-\frac{2}{3}\frac{U}{\rho}\cdot\frac{v-U}{T}\left(-\frac{U_{2}}{\rho^{2}}+\frac{v_{2}-U_{2}}{\rho T}\right)-\frac{2}{3}\frac{U_{2}}{\rho}\left(-\left(\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}+\frac{1}{\rho T}\right)\right)

\displaystyle+\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\frac{1}{\rho}\left(-\frac{v_{2}-U_{2}}{\rho T^{2}}+\left(-\frac{U_{2}}{\rho^{2}}+\frac{v_{2}-U_{2}}{\rho T}\right)\left(-\frac{3}{2}\frac{1}{T}+\frac{|v-U|^{2}}{2T^{2}}\right)\right)\bigg]\mathcal{M}(F).

The fourth component of the fifth row, i.e.
[\nabla_{(\rho,\rho U,G)}^{2}\mathcal{M}(F)]_{54}
:

\displaystyle\bigg[\frac{|U|^{2}-3T+3}{3\rho}\frac{U_{3}}{\rho^{3}}-\frac{2}{3}\frac{U}{\rho}\cdot\frac{v-U}{T}\left(-\frac{U_{3}}{\rho^{2}}+\frac{v_{3}-U_{3}}{\rho T}\right)-\frac{2}{3}\frac{U_{3}}{\rho}\left(-\left(\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}+\frac{1}{\rho T}\right)\right)

\displaystyle+\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\frac{1}{\rho}\left(-\frac{v_{3}-U_{3}}{\rho T^{2}}+\left(-\frac{U_{3}}{\rho^{2}}+\frac{v_{3}-U_{3}}{\rho T}\right)\left(-\frac{3}{2}\frac{1}{T}+\frac{|v-U|^{2}}{2T^{2}}\right)\right)\bigg]\mathcal{M}(F).

The fifth component of the fifth row, i.e.
[\nabla_{(\rho,\rho U,G)}^{2}\mathcal{M}(F)]_{55}
:

\displaystyle\bigg[\frac{|U|^{2}-3T+3}{3\rho}\left(-\frac{|U|^{2}-3T+3}{3\rho^{3}}\right)-\frac{2}{3}\frac{U}{\rho}\cdot\left(\frac{2U}{3\rho^{2}}-\frac{2}{3}\frac{v-2U}{\rho T}-\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\frac{1}{\rho}\left(\frac{v-U}{T^{2}}\right)+\frac{v-U}{T}\right)

\displaystyle+\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\frac{1}{\rho}\left(-\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}+\frac{2}{3}\frac{U\cdot(v-U)}{\rho T^{2}}+\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\frac{1}{\rho}\left(\frac{3}{2}\frac{1}{T^{2}}-\frac{|v-U|^{2}}{T^{3}}\right)+\left(-\frac{3}{2}\frac{1}{T}+\frac{|v-U|^{2}}{2T^{2}}\right)\right)\bigg]\mathcal{M}(F).

We completed the calculation of
5\times 5
matrix
\nabla_{(\rho,\rho U,G)}^{2}\mathcal{M}(F)
.

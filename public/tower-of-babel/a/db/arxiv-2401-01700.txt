Tulczyjew triple on the Atiyah algebroid with connection
Katarzyna Grabowska, Paweł Korzeb, Kuba Krawczyk

Abstract
The Tulczyjew triple on a principal bundle with connection is constructed in a convenient trivialisation. A reduction by the structure group is performed leading to the triple on the trivialised Atiyah algebroid and a presentation of this algebroid via a double vector bundle morphism. The dynamics of physical systems with configuration manifolds having the structure of a principal bundle with connection or the Atiyah algebroid is discussed and applied to the example of an axially symmetric body confined to a sphere. Keywords: Hamiltonian mechanics, Lagrangian mechanics, Tulczyjew triple, Ehresmann connection Lie Algebroid MSC: 22E70, 53D05, 53Z05, 70E17, 70H33.



1
Introduction

Lie algebroid is a geometric object very extensively described in the literature. First, it appeared in the 1967 works of Pradines
[
12
]
as an infinitesimal object for a Lie groupoid. In 1996, Weinstein
[
20
]
and Liebermann
[
10
]
used both structures – Lie groupoids and Lie algebroids – in analytical mechanics. The structure of Lie algebroid in Lagrangian mechanics plays a similar role as symplectic or Poisson structure in Hamiltonian mechanics, i.e. it provides tools for generating relevant equations from a Lagrangian or, more generally, from a Lagrangian generating object.

The Tulczyjew triple is a structure used in geometric analytical mechanics introduced by Tulczyjew in his various works (ex.
[
15
,
16
,
13
]
). It is a commutative diagram consisting of maps essential in both Lagrangian and Hamiltonian descriptions of physical systems.

In
[
7
]
, Grabowski and Urbański showed that a standard Lie algebroid structure, or its generalised version (
general algebroid
), is equivalently encoded in a certain double vector bundle morphism. Together with Grabowska, they showed
[
5
]
that mechanics on Lie algebroids may be described by the Tulczyjew triple, and in that case its defining morphisms encode the base algebroid.

In this paper, we closely examine the case where the base configuration manifold is the well-known
Atiyah Lie algebroid
, which additionally enjoys the presence of a connection on its principal bundle.

The paper is organised as follows. In Sections 2.1 and 2.2, we recall some necessary elements of the general theory of the Tulczyjew triple and Lie algebroids. In Section 2.3, we briefly present the construction and reduction of the Tulczyjew triple on a Lie group (see
[
6
]
), which is instructive for the rest of this work. In Section 3, we introduce some basic notions related to principal bundles and connection theory. Then, Section 4 contains the first part of the results of this paper. There, we construct the Tulczyjew triple on a principal bundle and offer its convenient trivialisation when a connection is present. We end the section by calculating the trivialised dynamics for a configuration manifold being a principal bundle with connection, and we apply it to a physical example – an axially symmetric body confined to a sphere. In Section 5, we reduce all the previous constructions by the action of the tangent group to obtain the, conveniently trivialised, Tulczyjew triple on the Atiyah algebroid, including the morphism encoding its Lie structure and the dynamics of systems modelled by it.

Part of the results presented in Section 4 have already been shown by Esen, Kudeyt and Sütlü
[
4
]
, but they used different methods and then discussed a completely separate reduction by the action of the structure group (not the tangent of the group) and hence made no connection to the Atiyah algebroid.

Tulczyjew triple and algebroids

The original Tulczyjew triple is a diagram containing all the information necessary for deriving dynamics both from Lagrangian or Hamiltonian generating objects and understanding the nature of the Legendre transformation. For

(1)

The map
\beta_{M}
is associated with the canonical symplectic form
\omega_{M}
on
\mathsf{\mathsf{T}}^{\ast}M
– it is just a contraction of a vector from
\mathsf{\mathsf{T}}\mathsf{\mathsf{T}}^{\ast}M
with this form. The map
\alpha_{M}
is the Tulczyjew isomorphism introduced in
[
16
]
. This simple diagram works for autonomous mechanics, i.e. when the Lagrangian or Hamiltonian does not depend explicitly on time. It can be used also in relativistic mechanics where
M
is the Minkowski space-time, and there is no distinguished time.

The three top bundles in the Tulczyjew triple have rich geometric structures. Each
of them is a double vector bundle, i.e. it has two compatible vector bundle structures (see

[
8
,
9
]
). We also note that having one of the maps, we may canonically obtain the other one via Dufour’s canonical isomorphism
[
2
]
as
\beta_{M}=\gamma_{\mathsf{T}M}^{-1}\circ\alpha_{M}
.

In the following two sections, we shall present shortly the mechanical aspects of the triple and its mathematical significance in the context of algebroids. We shall finish this section with an instructive example – the Tulczyjew triple in the case where the base manifold is a Lie group, and we use the left translation to trivialise tangent and cotangent iterated bundles.

2.1
Tulczyjew triple in mechanics

Tulczyjew’s approach to mechanics, based on a profound analysis of the variational approach to physical theories
[
13
]
, has proven very fruitful. The Tulczyjew triple is only one of the results of this analysis. Originally it was designed to make sense of the Legendre transformation for Lagrangian systems with singular Lagrangians. However, it turned out that the triple can be easily modified to different physical situations. There exists the Tulczyjew triple for time-dependent mechanics in non-relativistic cases, i.e. when Lagrangian or Hamiltonian depend explicitly on time. There exists also the Tulczyjew triple formulated in the language of the geometry of affine values, which can be used, for example, to describe mechanical systems in Newtonian space-time in a way which is independent of the choice of a particular observer. Tulczyjew’s approach serves well also in the case of constrained systems and systems with symmetry.

In its infinitesimal version, a mechanical system in Tulczyjew’s vision is described by phase equations called
the dynamics
. The dynamics is a subset of vectors tangent to the phase space. In many cases, this subset is an image of a vector field, but there are other situations, e.g. free relativistic particle where implicit equations are needed (other examples can be found in
[
19
]
).

To illustrate the use of the Tulczyjew triple, we shall use the classical diagram (
1
). Let
M
be the manifold of positions of the mechanical system we describe. In autonomous mechanics, a Lagrangian is a function depending on positions and velocities, i.e. it is a function on the tangent bundle
L:\mathsf{\mathsf{T}}M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. The image of the differential
\mathrm{d}L(\mathsf{\mathsf{T}}M)
is a Lagrangian submanifold in
\mathsf{\mathsf{T}}^{\ast}\mathsf{\mathsf{T}}M
, and the dynamics
\mathcal{D}
is given by the formula

\mathcal{D}=\alpha_{M}^{-1}(\mathrm{d}L(\mathsf{\mathsf{T}}M)).

In local coordinates
(q^{i})
on
M
,
(q^{i},p_{j})
on the phase space
\mathsf{\mathsf{T}}^{\ast}M
and
(q^{i},p_{j},\dot{q}^{k},\dot{p}_{l})
on
\mathsf{\mathsf{T}}\mathsf{\mathsf{T}}^{\ast}M
, the equations for
\mathcal{D}
read

p_{i}=\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot{q}^{i}},\qquad\dot{p}_{j}=\frac{\partial L}{\partial q_{j}}.

It is easy to see that the Euler-Lagrange equation with external forces equal to zero is a differential consequence of the dynamics. The dynamics contains then the definition of momenta, as well as, the equations of motion. In more complicated physical situations, the Lagrangian submanifold
\mathrm{d}L(\mathsf{\mathsf{T}}M)
can be replaced by a Lagrangian submanifold generated by a more complicated generating object, for example, a function on a submanifold (e.g. for vaconomic mechanics) or a generating family (e.g. for massless particles in relativistic setting). The full diagram of the Lagrangian side of the Tulczyjew triple, that includes the double vector bundle structure of iterated tangent and cotangent bundles as well as the Legendre map
\lambda_{L}
, reads

\scriptstyle{\alpha_{M}}
\textstyle{\mathsf{\mathsf{T}}^{\ast}\mathsf{\mathsf{T}}M\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

.

The Hamiltonian side of the Tulczyjew triple is associated with the symplectic form
\omega_{M}
and the map
\beta_{M}\mathsf{\mathsf{T}}\mathsf{\mathsf{T}}^{\ast}M\rightarrow\mathsf{\mathsf{T}}^{\ast}\mathsf{\mathsf{T}}^{\ast}M
, which is defined as
\beta_{M}(v)=\omega_{M}(v,\cdot)
. The formula for generating the dynamics from a Hamiltonian reads

\mathcal{D}=\beta_{M}^{-1}(\mathrm{d}H(\mathsf{\mathsf{T}}^{\ast}M)).

This formula is just another way of writing the definition of the Hamiltonian vector field for a function
H:\mathsf{\mathsf{T}}^{\ast}M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. This is evident when we write the equations defining
\mathcal{D}
in coordinates:

\dot{q}^{i}=\frac{\partial H}{\partial p_{i}},\qquad\dot{p}_{j}=-\frac{\partial H}{\partial q^{j}}.

The Lagrangian submanifold
\mathrm{d}H(\mathsf{\mathsf{T}}^{\ast}M)
can, however, be replaced by a Lagrangian submanifold generated by another Hamiltonian generating object, as it is, for example, in the case of a free relativistic particle. Using Lagrangian submanifolds instead of vector fields and generating families instead of just functions allows for both Lagrangian and Hamiltonian description of most mechanical systems. It works even for those with singular Lagrangians or no Lagrangians at all, as a massless particles. The full diagram of the Hamiltonian side of the Tulczyjew triple, that includes the double vector bundle structure of iterated tangent and cotangent bundles, reads

2.2
Algebroids via Tulczyjew triple morphisms

According to the most widely-used definition, a
Lie algebroid
is a vector bundle
\tau:E\rightarrow M
together with a Lie bracket on sections of the bundle and a vector bundle morphism
\rho:E\rightarrow\mathsf{\mathsf{T}}M
, called
the anchor
, satisfying the condition

[X,fY]=f[X,Y]+\rho(X)(f)Y

for all sections
X,Y
of the bundle
\tau
and all smooth functions
f
on
M
. The first canonical example of a Lie algebroid is the tangent bundle
\mathsf{\mathsf{T}}M
with the Lie bracket of vector fields and identity as the anchor. Another example is a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
over a one-point manifold with the Lie algebra bracket and the anchor that maps every element of
\mathfrak{g}
to the zero vector. The Atiyah algebroid of a principal bundle that will be discussed in detail later is another canonical example of the structure.

It is well known that the presence of a Lie algebroid structure on
\tau:E\rightarrow M
is equivalent to the existence of a linear Poisson structure on the dual bundle
\pi:E\rightarrow M
. Then, it turns out that every linear Poisson structure
\Lambda
on
E
may be written as a unique double vector bundle morphism

3
Principal bundles and connections

In this section, we briefly recall the geometric notions of a principal bundle and a connection, which will serve us as the base manifold of the Tulczyjew triple.

A
principal bundle
is a differential manifold
P
with a Lie group
G
,
n:=\dim G
, acting on it freely and properly from the right
P\times G\ni(p,g)\mapsto\wp_{g}(p)\equiv pg\in P
. Properties of the action ensure that the space of its orbits is also a differential manifold, which we often denote with
M
,
m:=\mathrm{dim}M
. Therefore, the principal bundle is a fibre bundle
(P,M,G,\pi)
.

The
vertical bundle

(\mathsf{V}P,P,\mathbb{R}^{n},\pi_{\mathsf{V}P})
of
P
is a bundle, in which the fibre over any point
p\in P
consists of the
vertical vectors
, i.e. elements of
\mathsf{T}_{p}P
belonging to the kernel of
\mathsf{T}\pi
.

We will also use the
adjoint bundle

({\mathrm{ad}P},M,\mathfrak{g},\pi_{\mathrm{ad}P})
of a principal bundle
P
, i.e. the bundle associated with
P
via the adjoint representation
\mathrm{Ad}
of
G
on its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. One may obtain the adjoint bundle by dividing the vertical bundle
\mathsf{V}P
by
G
. Additionally, we introduce a bracket on
{\mathrm{ad}P}
inherited from the Lie bracket of
\mathfrak{g}
:
\big[[(p,X)],[(p,Y)]\big]_{\mathrm{ad}P}:=[(p,[X,Y]_{\mathfrak{g}})]
.

An
Ehresmann connection

(\mathsf{H}P,P,\mathbb{R}^{m},\pi_{\mathsf{H}P})
is a vector subbundle of the tangent bundle of
P
complementary to the vertical subbundle
\mathsf{V}P
over every point of the base, i.e.
\mathsf{T}P\simeq\mathsf{H}P\oplus_{P}\mathsf{V}P
. Additionally, the connection on a principal bundle is
principal
if the following condition is satisfied:

\forall\,p\in P,\,g\in G\qquad\mathsf{T}_{p}\wp_{g}(\mathsf{H}_{p}P)=\mathsf{H}_{pg}P.

In the next sections, we will assume all connections are principal.

Any connection gives rise to a projection onto
\mathsf{H}P
along
\mathsf{V}P
, which we denote with
\mathrm{pr}_{\mathsf{H}}:\mathsf{T}P\longrightarrow\mathsf{H}P
, and a collection of maps
\mathsf{T}_{x}M\rightarrow\mathsf{\mathsf{T}}_{p}P
for all pairs
(x,p)
such that
x=\pi(p)
, called the
horizontal lift

\mathsf{T}_{\pi(p)}M\ni v\mapsto v_{p}^{H}\in\mathsf{H}_{p}P\subset\mathsf{T}_{p}P
providing an isomorphism
\mathsf{H}_{p}P\simeq\mathsf{T}_{\pi(p)}M
. Therefore, there is an identification

\mathsf{H}P\simeq P\times_{M}\mathsf{T}M.

(2)

If the bundle is principal, the fibres are isomorphic to the structure Lie group. The
G
-equivariant
fundamental vector fields

\sigma(X)_{p}:=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big|_{t=0}\,(pe^{tX})
induce, via the mapping
P\times\mathfrak{g}\ni(p,X)\mapsto\sigma(X)_{p}\in\mathsf{V}P
, a trivialisation

\mathsf{V}P\simeq P\times\mathfrak{g}.

(3)

This observation inspires an equivalent way of defining a connection for principal bundles. A
connection one-form
on a principal bundle
P
is a
\mathfrak{g}
-valued one-form
\omega\in\Omega(P,\mathfrak{g})
, which satisfies the condition

\omega(\sigma(X))=X,

and is
G
-equivariant:

{\wp_{g}}^{*}\omega=\mathrm{Ad}_{g^{-1}}\circ\omega.

A principal Ehresmann connection defines the connection one-form
\omega:=(\sigma_{p})^{-1}\circ(\mathrm{id}_{\mathsf{T}P}-\mathrm{pr}_{\mathsf{H}})
, and a connection one-form defines the Ehresmann connection
\mathsf{H}P:=\ker\omega
.

Any principal connection has the associated
curvature two-form

\Omega
given by

\Omega:=\mathrm{pr}_{\mathsf{H}}^{*}\mathrm{d}\omega\equiv\mathrm{d}\omega\circ(\mathrm{pr}_{\mathsf{H}}\times\mathrm{pr}_{\mathsf{H}})\in\Omega^{2}(P,\mathfrak{g}).

This two-form may be also expressed via:

•

when
V,W\in\mathfrak{X}(P)

\Omega(V,W)=-\omega([\mathrm{pr}_{\mathsf{H}}V,\mathrm{pr}_{\mathsf{H}}W]),

(4)

•

(
the structure equation
)

\Omega(V,W)=\mathrm{d}\omega(V,W)+[\omega(V),\omega(W)].

Remark 3.1
.

Because the value of
\Omega(V,W)
depends only on the horizontal parts of
V,W\in\mathfrak{X}(P)
, we often abuse the notation by writing
\Omega(v,w)
for
v,w\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
when it does not lead to confusion. Then,

\Omega(v,w)\equiv\Omega(v^{\mathsf{H}},w^{\mathsf{H}})=[v^{\mathsf{H}},w^{\mathsf{H}}]-[v,w]^{\mathsf{H}}.

(5)

4
Tulczyjew triple on a principal bundle with connection

Hereafter, we consider a principal bundle
P
with a principal connection
\mathsf{H}P
. In this section, we start by recalling how the presence of the connection allows to trivialise
\mathsf{T}P
. Then, we will use it to further trivialise iterated tangent and cotangent bundles and the canonical double vector bundle morphisms between them.

4.1
Trivialisation of the tangent and cotangent of a principal bundle

The connection on the bundle provides us with a decomposition
\mathsf{T}P\simeq\mathsf{H}P\oplus_{P}\mathsf{V}P
. Using the isomorphisms (
3
) and (
2
), we further rewrite it to
\mathsf{T}P\simeq(P\times_{M}\mathsf{T}M)\oplus_{P}(P\times\mathfrak{g})
. Omitting the redundant information, we obtain (slightly abusing the notation)

\imath_{\mathsf{T}P}:\mathsf{T}P\simeq P\times_{M}\mathsf{T}M\times\mathfrak{g}.

(6)

Remark 4.1
.

The formula above is explicitly given by

\imath_{\mathsf{T}P}(V)=\left(\tau_{P}(V),\mathsf{T}\pi(V),\omega(V)\right)\in P\times_{M}\mathsf{T}M\times\mathfrak{g},

where
\tau_{P}:\mathsf{T}P\rightarrow P
,
\pi:P\rightarrow M
and
\omega
is the connection one-form of
\mathsf{H}P
. We also note that the inverse mapping is given by

\imath_{\mathsf{T}P}^{-1}(p,\mathcal{V},X)=\mathcal{V}^{H}_{p}+\sigma(X)_{p}\in\mathsf{T}P.

Making use of (
6
), as well as the isomorphism
(\mathsf{V}_{p}P\times\mathsf{H}_{p}P)^{*}\simeq(\mathsf{V}_{p}P)^{*}\times(\mathsf{H}_{p}P)^{*}
over every
p\in P
, we have

\mathsf{T}^{*}_{p}P=(\mathsf{T}_{p}P)^{*}\simeq(\mathsf{V}_{p}P\times\mathsf{H}_{p}P)^{*}\simeq(\mathsf{V}_{p}P)^{*}\times(\mathsf{H}_{p}P)^{*}\simeq\mathfrak{g}^{*}\times(\mathsf{T}_{\pi(p)}M)^{*}=\mathfrak{g}^{*}\times\mathsf{T}^{*}_{\pi(p)}M,

which leads us to a trivialisation of the cotangent bundle in the form of

\imath_{\mathsf{T}^{*}P}:\mathsf{T}^{*}P\simeq P\times_{M}\mathsf{T}^{*}M\times\mathfrak{g}^{*}.

(7)

The pairing of
(p,\varphi,A)\in P\times_{M}\mathsf{T}^{*}M\times\mathfrak{g}^{*}
and
(p,\mathcal{V},X)\in P\times_{M}\mathsf{T}M\times\mathfrak{g}
takes the form

\displaystyle\langle(p,\varphi,A),(p,\mathcal{V},X)\rangle=\langle\varphi,\mathcal{V}\rangle+\langle A,X\rangle.

(8)

The trivialisations of
\mathsf{T}P
and
\mathsf{T}^{*}P
give rise to further trivialisations of the iterated tangent and cotangent bundles presented in the next subsections.

Now, we again apply (

4.3
Trivialisation of the canonical flip

As an intermediate step in the construction of the Tulczyjew triple on the discussed geometric structure, we develop a trivialisation of the automorphism
\kappa_{P}
of
\mathsf{T}\mathsf{T}P
. Later on, we will use it for building up the Tulczyjew isomorphism.

Let
\tau:E\rightarrow M
be a vector bundle and
e,f\in E
be elements of the same fibre of
E
, i.e.
\tau(e)=\tau(f)
. The vector tangent to the curve
t\mapsto e+tf
will be denoted by
f^{\mathsf{V}}_{e}
and called the
vertical lift of
f
to
e
. It is indeed a vertical vector at point the
e
.

Proposition 4.3.

Let
X
and
Y
be two vector fields on a manifold
M
. The following formula holds:

\mathsf{T}X(Y(x))-\kappa_{M}(\mathsf{T}Y(X(x)))=[Y,X]^{\mathsf{V}}_{X(x)}.

Proof.

We will do the calculation in local, adapted coordinates. Starting from coordinates
(q^{i})
defined on some open subset
\mathcal{O}\subset M
, we construct, in the usual way, the coordinates
(q^{i},\dot{q}^{j})
on
\tau_{M}^{-1}(\mathcal{O})
and
(q^{i},\dot{q}^{j},\delta q^{k},\delta\dot{q}^{l})
on
\tau_{\mathsf{T}M}^{-1}(\tau_{M}^{-1}(\mathcal{O}))
. If the vector fields
X
and
Y
are given in coordinates by functions
X^{i}(q)
and
Y^{i}(q)
respectively (
q
denotes the whole set of coordinates
q^{i}
), then the map
\mathsf{T}X
reads

\mathsf{T}X(q^{i},\dot{q}^{j})=\left(\,q^{i},\,X^{j}(q),\,\dot{q}^{k},\,\frac{\partial X^{l}}{\partial q^{m}}\dot{q}^{m}\,\right).

Similarly,
\mathsf{T}Y
reads

\mathsf{T}Y(q^{i},\dot{q}^{j})=\left(\,q^{i},\,Y^{j}(q),\,\dot{q}^{k},\,\frac{\partial Y^{l}}{\partial q^{m}}\dot{q}^{m}\,\right).

Since
\kappa_{M}(q^{i},\dot{q}^{j},\delta q^{k},\delta\dot{q}^{l})=(q^{i},\delta q^{j},\dot{q}^{k},\delta\dot{q}^{l}),
the difference
\mathsf{T}X(Y)(x)-\kappa_{M}(\mathsf{T}Y(X(x)))
reads

\mathsf{T}X(Y(x))-\kappa_{M}(\mathsf{T}Y(X(x)))=\left(\,q^{i},\,X^{j}(q),\,0,\,\frac{\partial X^{m}}{\partial q^{l}}Y^{l}-\frac{\partial Y^{m}}{\partial q^{l}}X^{l}\,\right),

which is indeed the coordinate expression for
[X,Y]^{\mathsf{V}}_{X(x)}
.
∎

Using the above proposition, we may find the formula for
\tilde{\kappa}_{P}
, which is
\kappa_{P}
composed with the trivialising
\imath_{\mathsf{T}\mathsf{T}P}
, i.e. the map

\tilde{\kappa}_{P}:P\times_{M}\mathsf{T}\mathsf{T}M\times\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow P\times_{M}\mathsf{T}\mathsf{T}M\times\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}.

First we will use Proposition
4.3
and two special vector fields on
P
. Let
\vec{X}
be the sum of the horizontal lift
\chi^{\mathsf{H}}
of a vector field
\chi
on
M
with the fundamental vector field
\partial_{X}
of an element
X\in\mathfrak{g}
. Such a field, in trivialisation, has the form

\imath_{\mathsf{T}P}\left(\vec{X}(p)\right)=(p,\chi(x),X)\in P\times_{M}\mathsf{T}M\times\mathfrak{g},

where
x=\pi(p)
. The value of
\mathsf{T}\vec{X}
on
(p,v,Y)
reads in trivialisation

\mathsf{T}\vec{X}(p,v,Y)=(p,\mathsf{T}\chi(v),X,Y,0).

Therefore, we have

\mathsf{T}\vec{X}(\vec{Y}(p))=(p,\mathsf{T}\chi(\eta(x)),X,Y,0),\qquad\mathsf{T}\vec{Y}(\vec{X}(p))=(p,\mathsf{T}\eta(\chi(x)),Y,X,0),

where
\vec{Y}=(p,\eta(q),Y)
for a vector field
\eta
on
M
and
Y\in\mathfrak{g}
. The Lie bracket
[\vec{X},\vec{Y}]
can be easily calculated. Indeed, we have

\vec{X}=\chi^{\mathsf{H}}+\partial_{X},\qquad\vec{X}=\eta^{\mathsf{H}}+\partial_{Y},

so also

\displaystyle[\vec{X},\vec{Y}]

\displaystyle=[\chi^{\mathsf{H}}+\partial_{X},\eta^{\mathsf{H}}+\partial_{Y}]=[\chi^{\mathsf{H}},\eta^{\mathsf{H}}]+[\chi^{\mathsf{H}},\partial_{Y}]+[\partial_{X},\eta^{\mathsf{H}}]+[\partial_{X},\partial_{Y}]

\displaystyle=[\chi,\eta]^{\mathsf{H}}+\partial_{\Omega(\chi,\eta)}+\partial_{[X,Y]},

where we used (
5
) and the fact that horizontal lifts are invariant vector fields, therefore
[\chi^{\mathsf{H}},\partial_{Y}]=0
and
[\partial_{X},\eta^{\mathsf{H}}]=0
.
In trivialisation, the bracket reads

\imath_{\mathsf{T}P}\left([\vec{X},\vec{Y}](p)\right)=(\,p,\,[\chi,\eta](p),\,\Omega_{p}(\chi,\eta)+[X,Y]_{\mathfrak{g}}\,)\in P\times_{M}\mathsf{T}M\times\mathfrak{g}.

Using Proposition
4.3
for the vector fields
\vec{X}
and
\vec{Y}
, we get in trivialisation

\displaystyle\tilde{\kappa}_{P}(p,\mathsf{T}\eta(\chi(q)),Y,X,0)

\displaystyle=(\,p,\,\mathsf{T}\chi(\eta(q)),\,X,\,Y,\,0\,)+(\,p,\,[\chi,\eta]^{\mathsf{V}}_{\chi(q)},\,X,\,0,\,\Omega_{p}(\chi,\eta)+[X,Y]_{\mathfrak{g}}\,)

\displaystyle=(\,p,\,\mathsf{T}\chi(\eta(q))+[\chi,\eta]^{\mathsf{V}}_{\chi(q)},\,X,\,Y,\,\Omega_{p}(\chi,\eta)+[X,Y]_{\mathfrak{g}}\,)

\displaystyle=(\,p,\,\kappa_{M}(\mathsf{T}\eta(\chi(q)),\,X,\,Y,\,\Omega_{p}(\chi,\eta)+[X,Y]_{\mathfrak{g}}\,).

Varying
\vec{X}
and
\vec{Y}
, we conclude that

\tilde{\kappa}_{P}(\,p,\,\mathcal{V},\,Y,X,0)=(\,p,\,\kappa_{M}(\mathcal{V}),\,X,\,Y,\,\Omega_{p}(\tau_{\mathsf{\mathsf{T}}M}(\mathcal{V}),\mathsf{T}\tau_{M}(\mathcal{V}))+[X,Y]_{\mathfrak{g}}\,).

To determine the value of
\tilde{\kappa}_{P}
on the most general element of the trivialised
\mathsf{T}\mathsf{T}P

\kappa_{P}
is an automorphism of the double vector bundle
\mathsf{T}\mathsf{T}P
over identities on both vector bundles, and it is an identity when restricted to the core. If
v
is an element of the core of
\mathsf{T}\mathsf{T}P
and
w
is an element of
\mathsf{\mathsf{T}}\mathsf{\mathsf{T}}P
over the same point in
M

\kappa_{P}(w+v)=\kappa_{P}(w)+v.

(10)

In trivialisation, the core of
P\times_{M}\mathsf{T}\mathsf{T}P\times\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}
consists of elements of the form
(p,u,0,0,Z)
, where

is in the core of
\mathsf{T}\mathsf{T}M
. Using (
10
), we get the final formula for
\tilde{\kappa}_{P}

\tilde{\kappa}_{P}(\,p,\,\mathcal{V},\,Y,X,Z)=(\,p,\,\kappa_{M}(\mathcal{V}),\,X,\,Y,\,Z+\Omega_{p}(\tau_{\mathsf{\mathsf{T}}M}(\mathcal{V}),\mathsf{T}\tau_{M}(\mathcal{V}))+[X,Y]_{\mathfrak{g}}\,).

(11)

\displaystyle\mathsf{T}^{*}(P\times_{M}\mathsf{T}M)

(m^{i})

\mathcal{O}\subset M

(m^{i},g^{j})
on

where
(g^{j})
are adopted to some open subset of the fibre

(m^{i},\delta m^{i})
are the induced coordinates on

,

\displaystyle\tilde{\psi}=\left((m^{i},g^{j}),(m_{i},p_{i}),(m^{i},\dot{m}^{i},\varphi_{i},\psi_{i}),A\right)\in P\times_{M}\mathsf{T}^{*}M\times_{M}\mathsf{T}^{*}\mathsf{T}M\times\mathfrak{g}^{*}

for every vector

\displaystyle\tilde{v}=\left((m^{i},g^{j}),(m^{i},\delta m^{i}),(m^{i},\dot{m}^{i},\delta m^{i},\delta\dot{m}^{i}),X\right)\in P\times_{M}\mathsf{T}M\times_{\mathsf{T}M}\mathsf{T}\mathsf{T}M\times\mathfrak{g}.

and
\mathrm{pr}_{3}(\widetilde{\psi})=\mathrm{pr}_{3}(\widetilde{v})
. Using the findings of the previous subsection, the evaluation is given by

\displaystyle\langle\widetilde{\psi},\widetilde{v}\rangle=\langle(p,\rho,X,A,B),(p,\mathcal{V},X,Y,Z)\rangle=\langle\rho,\mathcal{V}\rangle+\langle A,Y\rangle+\langle B,Z\rangle.

(14)

4.8
Trivialisation of the Tulczyjew isomorphism

Now, we are ready to construct the trivialised Tulczyjew isomorphism
\widetilde{\alpha}_{P}
. We do it directly trivialising the definition
\langle\!\langle\phi,\kappa_{P}(v)\rangle\!\rangle=\langle\alpha_{P}(\phi),v\rangle
to

\displaystyle\langle\!\langle\widetilde{\phi},\widetilde{\kappa}_{P}(\widetilde{v})\rangle\!\rangle=\langle\widetilde{\alpha}_{P}(\widetilde{\phi}),\widetilde{v}\rangle

(15)

for any
v\in\mathsf{T}\mathsf{T}P
and
\phi\in\mathsf{T}\mathsf{T}^{*}P
having the trivialisations

\displaystyle\widetilde{v}

\displaystyle:=\imath_{\mathsf{T}\mathsf{T}P}(v)=(p,\mathcal{V},Y,X,Z)\in P\times_{M}\mathsf{T}\mathsf{T}M\times\mathfrak{g}\times{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathfrak{g}}\times{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathfrak{g}},

\displaystyle\widetilde{\phi}

\displaystyle:=\imath_{\mathsf{T}\mathsf{T}^{*}P}(\psi)=(p,\varphi,A,Y,B)\in P\times_{M}\mathsf{T}\mathsf{T}^{*}M\times{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathfrak{g}^{*}}\times{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathfrak{g}}\times{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathfrak{g}^{*}}

and satisfying the evaluation conditions
\mathsf{T}\pi_{M}(\varphi)=\mathsf{T}\tau_{M}(\kappa_{M}(\mathcal{V}))=\tau_{\mathsf{T}M}(\mathcal{V})
.

We start with the right-hand side of the defining equation (
15
). By (
14
), we have

\displaystyle\langle\widetilde{\alpha}_{P}(\widetilde{\phi}),\widetilde{v}\rangle=\langle\mathrm{pr}_{2}(\widetilde{\alpha}_{P}(\widetilde{\phi})),\mathcal{V}\rangle+\langle\mathrm{pr}_{4}(\widetilde{\alpha}_{P}(\widetilde{\phi})),X\rangle+\langle\mathrm{pr}_{5}(\widetilde{\alpha}_{P}(\widetilde{\phi})),Z\rangle,

(16)

where in order for this evaluation to be well-defined
\mathrm{pr}_{1}(\widetilde{\alpha}_{P}(\widetilde{\phi}))=p
,
\pi_{\mathsf{T}M}\circ\mathrm{pr}_{2}(\widetilde{\alpha}_{P}(\widetilde{\phi}))=\tau_{\mathsf{T}M}(\mathcal{V})
and
\mathrm{pr}_{3}(\widetilde{\alpha}_{P}(\widetilde{\phi}))=Y
. To find the rest of the components, we will compare the above formula with the left-hand side of (
15
).

Using (
11
), (
12
) and
\mathsf{T}\pi_{M}(\varphi)=\tau_{\mathsf{T}M}(\mathcal{V})
, it reads

\displaystyle\langle\!\langle\widetilde{\phi},\widetilde{\kappa}_{P}(\widetilde{v})\rangle\!\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\!\langle(p,\varphi,A,Y,B),(p,\kappa_{M}(\mathcal{V}),X,Y,Z+\Omega_{p}(\tau_{\mathsf{T}M}\mathcal{V},\mathsf{T}\tau_{M}\mathcal{V})-[Y,X])\rangle\!\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\alpha_{M}(\varphi),\mathcal{V}\rangle+\langle A,Z\rangle-\langle A,\mathrm{ad}_{Y}(X)\rangle+\langle A,\Omega_{p}(\mathsf{T}\pi_{M}(\varphi),\mathsf{T}\tau_{M}(\cdot))(\mathcal{V})\rangle+\langle B,X\rangle.

In order to further simplify the left-hand side, we use the dual of the linear mapping
\mathrm{ad}_{Y}:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
, i.e.
\mathrm{ad}_{Y}^{*}:\mathfrak{g}^{*}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{*}
and the dual of the linear mapping
\Omega_{p}(\mathsf{T}\pi_{M}(\varphi),\mathsf{T}_{u}\tau_{M}(\cdot)):\mathsf{T}_{u}\mathsf{T}M\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
, i.e.
\Omega_{p}(\mathsf{T}\pi_{M}(\varphi),\mathsf{T}_{u}\tau_{M}(\cdot))^{*}:\mathfrak{g}^{*}\rightarrow\mathsf{T}^{*}_{u}\mathsf{T}M
where
u:=\tau_{\mathsf{T}M}(\mathcal{V})=\mathsf{T}\pi_{M}(\varphi)
. Because the latter is uniquely specified by just
(p,u)\in P\times_{M}\mathsf{T}M
, to ease the notation we will write
\Omega^{*}_{(p,\varphi)}\equiv\Omega^{*}_{\varphi}:=\Omega_{p}(\mathsf{T}\pi_{M}(\varphi),\mathsf{T}_{u}\tau_{M}(\cdot))^{*}
, whenever there is only one projection onto
\mathsf{T}M
. Then,

\displaystyle\langle\!\langle\widetilde{\phi},\widetilde{\kappa}_{P}(\widetilde{v})\rangle\!\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\alpha_{M}(\varphi),\mathcal{V}\rangle+\big\langle\Omega^{*}_{\varphi}(A),\mathcal{V}\big\rangle+\langle A,Z\rangle+\langle B-\mathrm{ad}_{Y}^{*}(A),X\rangle.

We notice that
\Omega^{*}_{\varphi}(A)
is an element of the core of the double vector bundle
\mathsf{T}^{*}\mathsf{T}M
(the image of
(\mathsf{T}_{u}\tau_{M})^{*}
belongs to the core). Denoting addition in the core with
\dot{+}
, the left-hand side reads

\langle\!\langle\widetilde{\phi},\widetilde{\kappa}_{P}(\widetilde{v})\rangle\!\rangle=\left\langle\alpha_{M}(\varphi)\dot{+}\Omega^{*}_{\varphi}(A),\mathcal{V}\right\rangle+\langle B-\mathrm{ad}_{Y}^{*}(A),X\rangle+\langle A,Z\rangle.

(17)

Comparing directly (
16
) and (
17
), we obtain the rest of the components of
\widetilde{\alpha}_{P}
:

\displaystyle\widetilde{\alpha}_{P}

\displaystyle:P\times_{M}\mathsf{T}\mathsf{T}^{*}M\times{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathfrak{g}^{*}}\times{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathfrak{g}}\times{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathfrak{g}^{*}}\longrightarrow P\times_{M}\mathsf{T}^{*}\mathsf{T}M\times\mathfrak{g}\times{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathfrak{g}^{*}}\times{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathfrak{g}^{*}}

(18)

\displaystyle:(p,\varphi,A,Y,B)\longmapsto(p,\alpha_{M}(\varphi)\dot{+}\Omega^{*}_{\varphi}(A),Y,B-\mathrm{ad}_{Y}^{*}(A),A).

and the pairing with

Trivialisation of
\mathsf{T}^{*}\mathsf{T}^{*}P
is analogous to that of
\mathsf{T}^{*}\mathsf{T}P
. The only difference is that we start with the trivialisation of
\mathsf{T}^{*}P
(
7
), and then we repeat the reasoning which led to (

\imath_{\mathsf{T}^{*}\mathsf{T}^{*}P}:\mathsf{T}^{*}\mathsf{T}^{*}P\simeq P\times_{M}\mathsf{T}^{*}\mathsf{T}^{*}M\times{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathfrak{g}^{*}}\times{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathfrak{g}^{*}}\times{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathfrak{g}}.

(19)

Similarly as for the bundles

\mathsf{T}\mathsf{T}P

and

as long as

4.10

In

, Dufour introduced the canonical isomorphism
\gamma_{E}:\mathsf{T}^{*}E^{*}\rightarrow\mathsf{T}^{*}E
for a vector bundle

. The graph of this morphism is precisely the Lagrangian submanifold
\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}
generated by the pairing function
\epsilon
between

and

, defined on the submanifold

We will now repeat this construction but taking the trivialised version of
\mathsf{T}P
as the vector bundle and (

{\widetilde{\epsilon}}
.
\mathcal{L}_{\widetilde{\epsilon}}
is now a submanifold of a trivialisation of

, projecting onto the trivialised

\displaystyle\langle\Theta,\mathcal{U}\rangle=\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\epsilon}(\mathcal{U}),

(21)

where

is any element of a trivialisation of

4.11
Trivialisation of the symplectic-based isomorphism

As mentioned earlier, the symplectic-based isomorphism may be written as
\beta_{M}=\gamma_{\mathsf{T}M}^{-1}\circ\alpha_{M}:\mathsf{T}\mathsf{T}^{*}M\rightarrow\mathsf{T}^{*}\mathsf{T}^{*}M
. Therefore, in the case of a trivialised principal bundle with connection, the formula reads

\displaystyle\widetilde{\beta}_{P}(p,\varphi,A,Y,B)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\gamma}_{\mathsf{T}P}^{-1}\circ\widetilde{\alpha}_{P}(p,\varphi,A,Y,B)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\gamma}_{\mathsf{T}P}^{-1}(p,\alpha_{M}(\varphi)\dot{+}\Omega^{*}_{\varphi}(A),Y,B-\mathrm{ad}_{Y}^{*}(A),A)

\displaystyle=\left(p,\gamma_{\mathsf{T}M}^{-1}\big(\alpha_{M}(\phi)\dot{+}\Omega^{*}_{\varphi}(A)\big),A,\mathrm{ad}_{Y}^{*}(A)-B,Y\right).

We may, once again, rewrite it using the fact that both double vector bundles
\mathsf{T}^{*}\mathsf{T}^{*}M
and
\mathsf{T}^{*}\mathsf{T}M
have the same core, and
\gamma_{\mathsf{T}M}
inverts the sign of vectors in the core. Thus, denoting by
\dot{-}
subtraction in the core of
\mathsf{T}^{*}\mathsf{T}^{*}M
, we arrive at the final form of the trivialised symplectic-based isomorphism:

\displaystyle\widetilde{\beta}_{P}

\displaystyle:P\times_{M}\mathsf{T}\mathsf{T}M\times{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathfrak{g}^{*}}\times{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathfrak{g}}\times{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathfrak{g}^{*}}\longrightarrow P\times_{M}\mathsf{T}^{*}\mathsf{T}^{*}M\times\mathfrak{g}^{*}\times{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\mathfrak{g}^{*}}\times{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\mathfrak{g}}

(23)

\displaystyle:(p,\varphi,A,Y,B)\longmapsto\left(p,\beta_{M}(\varphi)\dot{-}\Omega^{*}_{\varphi}(A),A,\mathrm{ad}_{Y}^{*}(A)-B,Y\right).

Example: Tulczyjew triple on the frame bundle of a sphere

In this chapter, we present the machinery derived in the previous subsections in action. We start by considering a
2

{P_{S^{2}}}
denote the principal bundle of oriented orthogonal frames. We will now describe a more convenient form of presenting

First, we embed

into

Furthermore, every point of

In this presentation, some other constructions also take convenient forms.

•

There is a (left)

-action on

\displaystyle SO(3)\times{P_{S^{2}}}\ni\big(\chi,p\big)\longmapsto\chi\cdot p=\left(\chi\vec{n},\big(\chi\vec{f}_{1},\chi\vec{f}_{2}\big)\right)\in{P_{S^{2}}},

(identified with
3

\mathbb{R}^{3}

\displaystyle{P_{S^{2}}}\times so(3)\ni(p,X)\longmapsto\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big|_{t=0}\,\exp(tX)\cdot p=Xp

\mathsf{T}{P_{S^{2}}}\simeq{P_{S^{2}}}\times so(3)

•

The algebra

is isomorphic to a Lie algebra built out of the vector space
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with a bracket given by the standard cross product. The isomorphism reads

\displaystyle so(3)\;:\;X:=\begin{bmatrix}0&-z&y\\
z&0&-x\\
-y&x&0\end{bmatrix}\longmapsto\begin{bmatrix}x\\
y\\
z\end{bmatrix}=:\vec{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

What is more, the isomorphism turns the action of the standard representation of

. Additionally,
\vec{x}

•

The structure (right)

-action takes the form

\displaystyle{P_{S^{2}}}\times SO(2)\ni(p,\lambda)\longrightarrow p\triangleleft\lambda:=\lambda^{-1}_{\vec{n}}\cdot p=(\vec{n},\lambda^{-1}_{\vec{n}}(f))\in{P_{S^{2}}},

where

denotes the embedding of

into

as a subgroup of rotations around the axis
\vec{n}
. We obviously identify the trivial algebra

with
\mathbb{R}

{P_{S^{2}}}\times\mathbb{R}\ni(p,x)\longmapsto\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big|_{t=0}\,p\triangleleft\exp(txN)=-xNp.

•

We now also have a convenient way of thinking about curves representing a vector

(where
\vec{n}
and

are orthogonal) as
\exp(tX)\vec{n}
such that

. Furthermore, any element of

(\exp(tY)\vec{n},(1+yt)\exp(tY)\exp(txN)\vec{v})\in\mathsf{T}S^{2}

where

,

and

\displaystyle\kappa_{S^{2}}(\vec{n},\vec{v},\vec{w},\vec{u})=(\vec{n},\vec{w},\vec{v},\vec{u}).

•

It is worth noting that the actions of

and

commute, in the sense that

\displaystyle\chi\circ\lambda_{\vec{z}}=\lambda_{\chi(\vec{z})}\circ\chi.

(31)

•

Using (

on

\displaystyle(p,X)\triangleleft\lambda

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big|_{t=0}\,\lambda_{\exp(tX)p}^{-1}\exp(tX)p=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big|_{t=0}\,\exp(tX)\lambda_{p}^{-1}p\equiv(\lambda_{p}^{-1}p,X).

(32)

The above constructions provide a way to transport a frame between fibres with the help of an element of
SO(3)
, but the transport is not unique. Now, we will specialise it to a proper connection.

We recall that the vertical subbundle is defined as the kernel of
\mathsf{T}\pi_{P_{S^{2}}}
. Hence, we first calculate the projection onto
\mathsf{T}S^{2}
of the trivialised tangent vector

\displaystyle\mathsf{T}\pi_{P_{S^{2}}}((\vec{n},f),X)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big|_{t=0}\,\pi_{P_{S^{2}}}\big(\exp(tX)\vec{n},\exp(tX)f\big)=X\vec{n}\equiv\vec{x}\times\vec{n}.

Therefore, the vertical bundle over every

we may express this condition in an alternative form

, which yields

\displaystyle\mathsf{V}{P_{S^{2}}}=\left\{\big((\vec{n},f),\vec{x}\big)\in{P_{S^{2}}}\times so(3)\;\mid\;\langle\vec{n}\mid\vec{x}\rangle=\|\vec{x}\|\right\}.

This inspires a connection on
{P_{S^{2}}}
in the form of the orthogonal complement
\mathsf{H}_{p}{P_{S^{2}}}:=(\mathsf{V}_{p}{P_{S^{2}}})^{\bot}

\displaystyle\mathrm{pr}_{\mathsf{V}}

\displaystyle:\mathsf{T}{P_{S^{2}}}\longrightarrow\mathsf{V}{P_{S^{2}}}:((\vec{n},f),\vec{x})\longmapsto((\vec{n},f),\langle\vec{n}\mid\vec{x}\rangle\vec{n}),

\displaystyle\mathrm{pr}_{\mathsf{H}}

\displaystyle:\mathsf{T}{P_{S^{2}}}\longrightarrow\mathsf{H}{P_{S^{2}}}:((\vec{n},f),\vec{x})\longmapsto((\vec{n},f),\vec{x}-\langle\vec{n}\mid\vec{x}\rangle\vec{n}).

The induced connection one-form takes the form

\displaystyle\omega_{P_{S^{2}}}

\displaystyle:\mathsf{T}{P_{S^{2}}}\longrightarrow so(2)\simeq\mathbb{R}:(p,X)\equiv((\vec{n},f),\vec{x})\longmapsto\langle\vec{n}\mid\vec{x}\rangle,

and the principality of the connection is ensured by the calculation

\displaystyle(\wp_{\lambda}^{*}\omega_{P_{S^{2}}})((\vec{n},f),\vec{x})

\displaystyle=\omega_{P_{S^{2}}}\big(\mathsf{T}\wp_{\lambda}((\vec{n},f),\vec{x})\big)\equiv\omega_{P_{S^{2}}}\big(\lambda^{-1}_{\vec{n}}(\vec{n},f),\vec{x}\big)

\displaystyle=\omega_{P_{S^{2}}}\big((\vec{n},\lambda^{-1}_{\vec{n}}f),\vec{x}\big)=\langle\vec{n}\mid\vec{x}\rangle=\mathrm{Ad}_{\lambda^{-1}}(\langle\vec{n}\mid\vec{x}\rangle).

We consider
\vec{v}\in\mathsf{T}_{q}S^{2}
. The horizontal lift takes the form
\vec{v}_{p}^{\mathsf{H}}=(p,\vec{n}\times\vec{v})
. Indeed, it is horizontal as
\omega(p,\vec{n}\times\vec{v})=\bra{\vec{n}}\ket{\vec{n}\times\vec{v}}=0
and
\mathsf{T}\pi_{P_{S^{2}}}(p,\vec{n}\times\vec{v})=(\vec{n}\times\vec{v})\times\vec{n}=\vec{v}
.

In order to formulate the trivialised Tulczyjew triple on the frame bundle, we have to find the curvature two-form.

We will use the identity (
4
). We consider two fundamental vector fields – generated by
X
and
Y
. First, we want to calculate the Lie bracket of their horizontal parts (we treat
\vec{n}
as the tautological vertical vector field on
{P_{S^{2}}}
, which assigns to a point on the sphere its unit vector:
{P_{S^{2}}}\ni p\longmapsto\pi_{P_{S^{2}}}(p)\simeq\vec{n}\simeq N\in so(3)

[X-\bra{\vec{n}}\ket{\vec{x}}N,Y-\bra{\vec{n}}\ket{\vec{y}}N]=[X,Y]-[\bra{\vec{n}}\ket{\vec{x}}N,Y]-[X,\bra{\vec{n}}\ket{\vec{y}}N]+[\bra{\vec{n}}\ket{\vec{x}}N,\bra{\vec{n}}\ket{\vec{y}}N].

The components above become:

•

[X,Y]=[Y,X]_{so(3)}=\vec{y}\times\vec{x}
,

•

\vec{n}
is a vector field conserved by any rotation of
S^{2}
. Therefore,
\vec{n}
commutes with the generators of the rotations, i.e.
[N,X]=[N,Y]=0
. This enables the calculation

\displaystyle[\bra{\vec{n}}\ket{\vec{x}}N,Y]

\displaystyle=\bra{\vec{n}}\ket{\vec{x}}[N,Y]-Y(\bra{\vec{n}}\ket{\vec{x}})N=-Y(\bra{\vec{n}}\ket{\vec{x}})N=-\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big|_{t=0}\,\bra{\exp(tY)\vec{n}}\ket{\vec{x}}N

\displaystyle=-\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big|_{t=0}\,\bra{\vec{n}}\ket{\exp(-tY)\vec{x}}N=\bra{\vec{n}}\ket{\vec{y}\times\vec{x}}N,

•

we get the same result for

•

for the last component, we use the product rule twice:

Using the calculated horizontal lift, we can express the curvature two-form in the way present in the Tulczyjew triple. We take

. Then,

\Omega_{p}(\vec{v},\vec{w})\equiv\Omega(\vec{v}_{p}^{\mathsf{H}},\vec{w}_{p}^{\mathsf{H}})=\bra{\vec{n}}\ket{(\vec{n}\times\vec{w})\times(\vec{n}\times\vec{v})}=\bra{\vec{n}}\ket{\bra{\vec{n}}\ket{\vec{w}\times\vec{v}}\vec{n}}=\bra{\vec{n}}\ket{\vec{w}\times\vec{v}}.

Having prepared all the necessary components, we can trivialise the iterated tangent bundle and, in consequence, the canonical flip. The choice of the connection provides the explicit form of the trivialisation map

in the case of the frame bundle identified with three vectors in

\displaystyle\imath_{\mathsf{T}{P_{S^{2}}}}

\displaystyle:\mathsf{T}{P_{S^{2}}}\simeq{P_{S^{2}}}\times_{S^{2}}\mathsf{T}S^{2}\times so(2)

\displaystyle:\big((\vec{n},f),\vec{x}\big)\longmapsto((\vec{n},f),\vec{x}\times\vec{n},\langle\vec{n}\mid\vec{x}\rangle).

By (

\displaystyle\imath_{\mathsf{T}\mathsf{T}{P_{S^{2}}}}:\mathsf{T}\mathsf{T}{P_{S^{2}}}\simeq{P_{S^{2}}}\times_{S^{2}}\mathsf{T}\mathsf{T}S^{2}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}.

(33)

To formulate the first canonical isomorphism, we consider

\displaystyle p=(\vec{n},f)\in{P_{S^{2}}},\quad\mathcal{V}=(\vec{n},\vec{v},\vec{w},\vec{u})\in\mathsf{T}\mathsf{T}S^{2},\quad x,y,z\in\mathbb{R}\simeq so(2).

We find the form of

using duality:

4.14
Example: Dynamics of a system modelled by the frame bundle on the sphere

A natural physical example of a system with configuration space modelled by the frame bundle is an axially symmetric rigid body confined to move on a sphere in a way that the symmetry axis is always normal to the sphere. The Lagrangian of such a system, explicitly exercising the connection on the frame bundle, is

\displaystyle\imath_{\mathsf{T}{P_{S^{2}}}}({P_{S^{2}}})\ni(p,(\vec{n},\vec{v}),r)\longmapsto L(p,(\vec{n},\vec{v}),r):=\frac{1}{2}I_{\perp}\|\vec{n}\times\vec{v}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}I_{ax}r^{2}\in\mathbb{R},

where
I_{\perp}
is the moment of inertia for rotations of the body around the centre of the sphere and
I_{ax}
is the moment of inertia for rotations around the symmetry axis of the body.

We find the explicit form of
\mathrm{d}L(p,(\vec{n},\vec{v}),r)=(p,(\vec{n},\vec{v},\vec{\alpha},\vec{\beta}),r,a,b)\in\imath_{\mathsf{T}^{*}\mathsf{T}{P_{S^{2}}}}(\mathsf{T}^{*}\mathsf{T}{P_{S^{2}}})
evaluating it on a vector
(p,(\vec{n},\vec{v},\vec{u},\vec{w}),r,k,s)\in\imath_{\mathsf{T}\mathsf{T}{P_{S^{2}}}}(\mathsf{T}\mathsf{T}{P_{S^{2}}})
represented by the curve
(\exp(tY)p,(\exp(tY)\vec{n},(1+yt)\exp(tY)\exp(txN)\vec{v}),r+ts)\in\imath_{\mathsf{T}{P_{S^{2}}}}(\mathsf{T}{P_{S^{2}}})
, where
Yp\simeq(p,Y\vec{n},k)
,
\vec{u}=Y\vec{n}
,
\vec{w}=(Y+xN+y)\vec{v}
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}L(p,(\vec{n},\vec{v},\vec{u},\vec{w}),r,k,s)

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big|_{t=0}\,L(\exp(tY)p,(\exp(tY)\vec{n},(1+yt)\exp(tY)\exp(txN)\vec{v}),r+ts)

\displaystyle=I_{\perp}\bra{\vec{v}}\ket{y\vec{v}}+I_{ax}rs.

This leads us to
\mathrm{d}L(p,(\vec{n},\vec{v}),r)=(p,(\vec{n},\vec{v},c\vec{n},I_{\perp}\vec{v}),r,0,I_{ax}r)
, where
c\in\mathbb{R}
and
c\vec{n}
is a representative of the annihilator of
\{Y\vec{n}\}_{Y\in so(3)}
. Therefore, the dynamics takes the form

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}=\left\{\left(p,(\vec{n},I_{\perp}\vec{v},\vec{v},c\vec{n}-I_{ax}r\vec{v}\times\vec{n}),I_{ax}r,r,0\right)\right\},

and the Euler-Lagrange equations for this system are

\displaystyle I_{\perp}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big|_{t=0}\,\vec{v}

\displaystyle=c\vec{n}-I_{ax}r\vec{v}\times\vec{n},

(34)

\displaystyle I_{ax}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big|_{t=0}\,r

\displaystyle=0.

In the first equation, as expected,
c\vec{n}
corresponds to the centrifugal force, and
I_{ax}r\vec{v}\times\vec{n}
describes the gyroscopic precession enforced by the symmetry axis revolving around the sphere.

5
Atiyah algebroid with connection and reduced Tulczyjew triple

In Section
2.2
, we recalled how the framework of the Tulczyjew triple allows us to encode a Lie algebroid structure in one double vector bundle morphism.

In this section, we describe the reduction of the Tulczyjew triple on a principal bundle by the action of the tangent group
\mathsf{T}G
. From such a reduction emerges a particular Lie algebroid – the Atiyah algebroid. We will show that in the presence of a connection on the principal bundle, the Atiyah algebroid is also trivialisable, and we will present the trivialised double vector bundle morphism encoding it.

The
Atiyah algebroid

(A(P),M,[\cdot,\cdot]_{A(P)},\rho_{A(P)})
of a principal bundle
P
is a Lie algebroid built on the vector bundle
\pi_{A(P)}:A(P):=\mathsf{T}P/G\longrightarrow M
obtained by dividing
P
by the tangent action of
G
. Its sections may be identified with the
G
-invariant vector fields on
P
, and the Lie bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]_{A(P)}
is, therefore, induced from the vector field bracket on
P
. The anchor
\rho_{A(P)}
is induced by
\mathsf{T}\pi_{P}
. We denote the projection
\mathsf{T}P\rightarrow A(P)
with
\delta_{A(P)}
.

It is a well-known fact that a principal connection induces a splitting of the anchor
\rho_{A(P)}
leading to a trivialisation of the Lie algebroid
\imath_{A(P)}:A(P)\simeq\mathsf{T}M\oplus_{M}\mathrm{ad}P
(see
[
1
]
). However, this direct sum is in the category of Lie algebroids over
M
only if the connection is flat – otherwise, it is just a sum of vector bundles. We note that the above decomposition agrees with the trivialisation (
6
) divided by
G
. We denote the trivialised projection onto the quotient with
{\tilde{\delta}_{A(P)}}
.

Starting with the cotangent of a principal bundle and dividing it by the dual of the tangent action of
G
, we get the
dual of the Atiyah algebroid
, which, if there is a connection, in a similar manner may be decomposed as
\imath_{A^{*}(P)}:A^{*}(P)\simeq\mathrm{ad}^{*}P\oplus_{M}\mathsf{T}^{*}M
, where
\mathrm{ad}^{*}P
denotes the dual of the adjoint bundle (the defining action on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is
\mathrm{Ad}^{*}
). The projections are denoted with
\delta_{A^{*}(P)}
and
{\tilde{\delta}_{A^{*}(P)}}
.

5.1
Reduction of trivialised
TT
P

To reduce the canonical flip, we first find the explicit form of a trivialisation

Remark 5.1
.

The group structure of the tangent group
\mathsf{T}G
carries to the trivialisation
G\times\mathfrak{g}
as a semidirect product.

We start by considering a curve
\mathbb{R}\ni t\longmapsto\gamma_{g}(t)=g\exp(tW)\in G
representing a generic vector
(g,W)\in\imath_{\mathsf{T}G}(\mathsf{T}G)=G\times\mathfrak{g}
and a curve

representing a vector

, i.e.

\displaystyle\mathsf{T}P\ni\dot{\gamma}_{p}(0)

\displaystyle\simeq(p,\mathsf{T}\tau_{M}(\mathcal{V}),Y)\in P\times_{M}\mathsf{T}M\times\mathfrak{g},

\displaystyle\mathsf{T}\mathsf{T}M\ni\dot{\gamma}_{v}(0)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{V},

\displaystyle\mathsf{T}\mathfrak{g}\ni\dot{\gamma}_{X}(0)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big|_{t=0}\,X+tZ\simeq(X,Z)\in\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}.

We now calculate the right action of
(g,W)
on
(p,\mathcal{V},X,Y,Z)
, that is

\displaystyle(p,\mathcal{V},X,Y,Z)\cdot(g,W)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big|_{t=0}\,(\gamma_{p}(t)\cdot\gamma_{g}(t),\gamma_{v}(t),\mathrm{Ad}_{\gamma_{g}(t)^{-1}}(\gamma_{X}(t))).

•

The tangent to the

-curve:

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big|_{t=0}\,\gamma_{p}(t)\cdot\gamma_{g}(t)=\mathsf{T}_{p}\wp_{g}(\dot{\gamma}_{p}(0))+\sigma(W)_{pg}\simeq\\
(pg,\mathsf{T}\tau_{M}(\mathcal{V}),\mathrm{Ad}_{g^{-1}}Y+W)\in P\times_{M}\mathsf{T}M\times\mathfrak{g},

•

and the tangent to the
\mathfrak{g}

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big|_{t=0}\,\mathrm{Ad}_{\gamma_{g}(t)^{-1}}(\gamma_{X}(t))

\displaystyle\simeq(\mathrm{Ad}_{g^{-1}}X,[\mathrm{Ad}_{g^{-1}}X,W]_{\mathfrak{g}}+\mathrm{Ad}_{g^{-1}}Z)\in\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}.

(g,W)

and notice that, at any

\mathsf{T}A(P)

\{e\}\times\mathfrak{g}\subset G\times\mathfrak{g}

(\ker\mathsf{T}\delta_{A(P)})^{\circ}
at every point of
\mathsf{T}P
. Therefore, we should find the explicit form of the annihilator in trivialisation, starting with the kernel of
\widetilde{\mathsf{T}\delta}_{A(P)}

\displaystyle\langle\omega,0\rangle+\langle B,Y\rangle+\langle C,-[Y,X]_{\mathfrak{g}}\rangle=0.

\displaystyle\tilde{K}=\big\{(p,\omega,X,B,C)\in P\times_{M}\mathsf{T}^{*}\mathsf{T}M\times\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}^{*}\times\mathfrak{g}^{*}\;\mid\;B=-\mathrm{ad}_{X}^{*}(C)\big\}.

A^{*}(P)
are constant also on orbits of the subgroup
G\times\{0\}\subset G\times\mathfrak{g}
. It yields a relation on
\tilde{K}
, which equivalence classes we find using an evaluation on two vectors from the same orbit:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \langle(\omega,B,C),(\mathcal{V},Y,Z)\rangle&=\langle(\omega^{\prime},B^{\prime},C^{\prime}),(\mathcal{V},\mathrm{Ad}_{g^{-1}}(Y),\mathrm{Ad}_{g^{-1}}(Z))\rangle,\end{aligned}

where the left evaluation is at the point
(p,v,X)
and the right at
(pg,v,\mathrm{Ad}_{g^{-1}}(X))
. Comparing both sides, we get

\displaystyle(p,\omega,X,-\mathrm{ad}_{X}^{*}(C),C)\sim_{G}\big(pg,\omega,\mathrm{Ad}_{g^{-1}}(X),-(\mathrm{ad}_{X}\circ\mathrm{Ad}_{g})^{*}(C),\mathrm{Ad}_{g}^{*}(C)\big).

We denote the submanifold
\tilde{K}
divided by the relation by
\tilde{K}/G

5.10
Example: Reduction of the triple on the frame bundle of a sphere

In this section, we showcase the reduction algorithm on the example of the Tulczyjew triple on the frame bundle
{P_{S^{2}}}
calculated in Section
4.13
.

First, by (
37
), we state a trivialisation of the Atiyah algebroid of the frame bundle

\displaystyle\mathsf{T}A({P_{S^{2}}})\simeq\mathsf{T}\mathsf{T}S^{2}\times_{S^{2}}\mathrm{ad}{P_{S^{2}}}\times_{S^{2}}\mathrm{ad}{P_{S^{2}}}.

Next, we notice that, as the algebra
so(2)
is commutative, the adjoint bundle is trivialisable –
\mathrm{ad}{P_{S^{2}}}\simeq S^{2}\times so(2)
. Hence, we may further simplify
\mathsf{T}A({P_{S^{2}}})
as

\displaystyle\mathsf{T}A({P_{S^{2}}})\simeq\mathsf{T}\mathsf{T}S^{2}\times_{S^{2}}(S^{2}\times so(2))\times_{S^{2}}(S^{2}\times so(2))\simeq\mathsf{T}\mathsf{T}S^{2}\times so(2)\times so(2).

The projection from
\imath_{\mathsf{T}\mathsf{T}{P_{S^{2}}}}(\mathsf{T}\mathsf{T}{P_{S^{2}}})
onto trivialised orbits of the
(SO(2)\times so(2))
-action takes the simple form

\displaystyle\mu_{\mathsf{T}A({P_{S^{2}}})}((\vec{n},f),\mathcal{V},x,y,z)=\left(\mathcal{V},x,z\right)\in\mathsf{T}\mathsf{T}S^{2}\times so(2)\times so(2).

Similarly, using additionally the fact that
\mathrm{ad}^{*}{P_{S^{2}}}\simeq S^{2}\times so(2)^{*}
, we can trivialise also other tangents and cotangents of the Atiyah algebroid.

Having all final trivialisations, we reduce the triple on the frame bundle. The canonical flip (relation) is given by

\displaystyle\mathsf{T}\mathsf{T}S^{2}\times so(2)\times so(2)\ni\left(\mathcal{V},x,y\right)\sim_{\tilde{\kappa}_{A({P_{S^{2}}})}}\left(\mathcal{U},u,z\right)\in\mathsf{T}\mathsf{T}S^{2}\times so(2)\times so(2)

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}=\kappa_{S^{2}}(\mathcal{V})\quad\text{and}\quad z=y+\langle\vec{w}\times\vec{v}\mid\vec{n}\rangle,\;\tau_{\mathsf{T}S^{2}}(\mathcal{V})=(\vec{n},\vec{v}),\;\mathsf{T}\tau_{S^{2}}(\mathcal{V})=(\vec{n},\vec{w}),

the Tulczyjew map by

\displaystyle\tilde{\varepsilon}_{A({P_{S^{2}}})}

\displaystyle:\mathsf{T}^{*}\mathsf{T}S^{2}\times so(2)\times so(2)^{*}\longrightarrow\mathsf{T}\mathsf{T}^{*}S^{2}\times so(2)^{*}\times so(2)^{*}

\displaystyle:(\omega,x,c)\longmapsto(\alpha_{S^{2}}^{-1}(\omega)\dot{-}c\langle\vec{v}\times\vec{n}\mid\mathrm{pr}_{3}(\cdot)\,\rangle,c,0),\qquad\pi_{\mathsf{T}S^{2}}(\omega)=(\vec{n},\vec{v}),

and, finally, the symplectic-based map by

\displaystyle\tilde{\eta}_{A({P_{S^{2}}})}

\displaystyle:\mathsf{T}^{*}\mathsf{T}^{*}S^{2}\times so(2)^{*}\times so(2)\longrightarrow\mathsf{T}\mathsf{T}^{*}S^{2}\times so(2)^{*}\times so(2)^{*}

\displaystyle:(\Theta,a,z)\longmapsto(\beta_{S^{2}}^{-1}(\Theta)\dot{+}a\langle\vec{v}\times\vec{n}\mid\mathrm{pr}_{3}(\cdot)\,\rangle,a,0),\qquad\xi_{\mathsf{T}S^{2}}(\Theta)=(\vec{n},\vec{v}).

The zeros in the last component are due to commutativity of
so(2)
, which yields
\forall x\in so(2):\;\;\mathrm{ad}_{x}^{*}=0
.

5.11
Example: Reduction of the dynamics

We observe that the Lagrangian
L(p,(\vec{n},\vec{v}),r):=\frac{1}{2}I_{\perp}\|\vec{n}\times\vec{v}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}I_{ax}r^{2}
is invariant under the tangent action (
32
). Therefore, we may introduce its reduced version (remembering that
\mathrm{ad}{P_{S^{2}}}\simeq S^{2}\times so(2)
)

\displaystyle l(\vec{n},\vec{v},r)\equiv l((\vec{n},\vec{v}),[(p,r)]_{\mathrm{ad}{P_{S^{2}}}}):=L(p,(\vec{n},\vec{v}),r),

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}l(\vec{v},\vec{n},r)=((\vec{n},\vec{v},c\vec{n},I_{\perp}\vec{v}),r,I_{ax}r).

The dynamics reads

\displaystyle\widetilde{d}=\tilde{\varepsilon}_{A({P_{S^{2}}})}\circ\mathrm{d}l(\mathsf{T}S^{2}\times so(2))=\left\{\left((\vec{n},I_{\perp}\vec{v},\vec{v},c\vec{n}-I_{ax}r\vec{v}\times\vec{n}),I_{ax}r,0\right)\right\}

and, obviously, leads to the same Euler-Lagrange equations as the unreduced version (
34
).

On diminished multiplier ideal and the termination of flips
Donghyeon Kim

Abstract
. In this paper, we develop a theory of diminished multiplier ideals on singular varieties which was introduced by Hacon, and developed by Lehmann. We prove a result regarding the termination of certain type of flips with scaling of an ample divisor if the Cartier index is bounded, and if κ σ ​ ( K X + Δ ) ≥ dim X − 1 \kappa_{\sigma}(K_{X}+\Delta)\geq\dim X-1 holds. The proof uses a theory of diminished multiplier ideal.

1.
Introduction

The goal of this paper is twofold: first, to extend the notion of the diminished multiplier ideal to the singular case, and second, to prove a result on the termination of flips. The former development underpins our proof of the latter.

Hacon introduced the notion of diminished multiplier ideal
\mathcal{J}_{\sigma}(\|D\|)
and later, Lehmann developed a theory of
\mathcal{J}_{\sigma}(\|D\|)
on smooth varieties (cf.
[
Hac04
]
,
[
Leh14
]
). Asymptotic multiplier ideals
\mathcal{J}(\|D\|)
are defined for big or effective divisors, and can be considered as a “continuous” extension of
\mathcal{J}(\|D\|)
for big divisors to pseudo-effective divisor. The first goal of this paper is to further develop a theory of diminished multiplier ideal
\mathcal{J}_{\sigma}(X,\Delta;\|D\|)
associated to any klt pair
(X,\Delta)
and any pseudo-effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
D
on
X
. For the definition of asymptotic multiplier ideals
\mathcal{J}(X,\Delta;\|D\|)
, see Section
4
, and for the definition of diminished multiplier ideals
\mathcal{J}_{\sigma}(X,\Delta;\|D\|)
, see ?THM?
. The three main theorems in this direction are presented below:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a projective klt pair, and
D
a big
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor. Then we have

\mathcal{J}_{\sigma}(X,\Delta;\|D\|)=\mathcal{J}(X,\Delta;\|D\|).

?THM?
is a generalization of Corollary 6.12 in
[
Leh14
]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a projective klt pair, and
D
a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on
X
. Then we have that

\mathbf{B}_{-}(D)=\bigcup_{m}Z(\mathcal{J}_{\sigma}(X,\Delta;\|mD\|))_{\mathrm{red}}.

For the definition of
\mathbf{B}_{-}
, see ?THM?
.

?THM?
is a generalization of Lemma 6.4 in
[
Leh14
]
.

The following can be considered as a generalization of Nadel vanishing theorem.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a projective klt pair such that
\Delta
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier, and suppose that

is a pseudo-effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on

H^{i}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(L)\otimes\mathcal{J}_{\sigma}(X,\Delta;\|D\|))=0\text{ for }i>\dim X-\kappa_{\sigma}(D),

where

is a Cartier divisor on

holds. For the definition of

We now apply the preceding results to the problem of flip termination, a longstanding topic in birational geometry. A common strategy in studying termination is to introduce an appropriate invariant that remains well-behaved along the Minimal Model Program (MMP). For instance, Shokurov introduced the
difficulty function
in
[
Sho85
]
and used it to prove the termination of
3
-fold terminal flips. His idea gave rise to the notion of
special termination
(cf.
[
Fuj07
]
), and in
[
Sho04
]
, he further showed that the
ACC condition
and the lower semi-continuity of
minimal log discrepancy
(mld) together imply the termination of flips.

In our setting, under the assumptions that
\kappa_{\sigma}(K_{X}+\Delta)\geq\dim X-1
and that the index of
K_{X}+\Delta
is bounded along the MMP, we propose another invariant, namely

h^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(m(K_{X}+\Delta)))

for some positive integer
m
. Building on Theorems
and
, we use this cohomological invariant to prove the following result on the termination of flips.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial projective klt pair, and suppose that

(X_{0},\Delta_{0}):=(X,\Delta)\overset{f_{1}}{\dashrightarrow}(X_{1},\Delta_{1})\overset{f_{2}}{\dashrightarrow}\cdots

is a
(K_{X}+\Delta)
-MMP with scaling of an ample divisor. If

(a)

all of
f_{i}
are flips of type
(1,\dim X-2)
,

(b)

there is a positive integer
m
such that
m(K_{X_{i}}+\Delta_{i})
are Cartier divisors for all
i
, and

(c)

\kappa_{\sigma}(K_{X}+\Delta)\geq\dim X-1
holds,

then the MMP terminates.

Note that the
\mathbb{Q}
-factoriality of
X
is required only to use ?THM?
. Moreover,
[
Sho96
, Lemma 4.4.1]
induces a new proof of the termination of
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial
3
-fold klt flips with scaling of an ample divisor if
\kappa_{\sigma}(K_{X}+\Delta)\geq 2
. Furthermore, if
\kappa_{\sigma}(K_{X}+\Delta)=\dim X
(i.e.,
K_{X}+\Delta
is big), then the termination of flips with scaling of an ample divisor automatically follows from
[
BCHM10
, Corollary 1.4.2]
.

The result may be useful for investigating another related problems on termination of flips. For instance, it is known by
[
LX23
]
that a certain termination of flips is enough to guarantee the termination of a larger class of flips. Moreover, we also expect that ?THM?
provide some hint of the proof of the termination of flips (cf.
[
KMM87
, Theorem 5-1-15]
).

A sketch of the proof is given below. Firstly, for a fixed positive integer
m
such that all
m(K_{X_{i}}+\Delta_{i})
are Cartier (such an
m
exists by
(b)
), we show that

d_{i}:=h^{1}(X_{i},\mathcal{O}_{X_{i}}(2m(K_{X_{i}}+\Delta_{i})))

is non-increasing. Then since
d_{i}\geq 0
for any
i
, we can find
i
such that
d_{i-1}=d_{i}
holds. Moreover, if
d_{i-1}=d_{i}
holds, then by
(a)
and
(c)
, we obtain a vanishing of higher direct image of the flipping contraction. We can translate the vanishing into the vanishing of cohomology of fibre of flipping contraction, and it leads to a contradiction if the MMP does not terminate. Note that to use the condition
(c)
, we develop a theory of diminished multiplier ideals for singular varieties.

The remaining of the paper is organized as follows. We begin Section 2 by introducing notions and proving lemmas which are used in the paper. In Section 3, we prove a version of
[
JM12

. Section 4 is devoted to developing a theory of diminished multiplier ideal for singular varieties, and proving three main theorems on the notion. In Section 5, we prove ?THM?

3.1.
Log canonical threshold and asymptotic multiplier ideals

Let
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}:=\{\mathfrak{a}_{m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}}
be a set of ideals in
\mathcal{O}_{X}
. We say that
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
is a
graded sequence of ideals
in
\mathcal{O}_{X}
if there exists a subsemigroup
\Phi\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
such that

\mathfrak{a}_{m}\cdot\mathfrak{a}_{m^{\prime}}\subseteq\mathfrak{a}_{m+m^{\prime}}

for all
m,m^{\prime}\in\Phi
. Let us define
\Phi(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})
for a graded sequence of ideals
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
in
\mathcal{O}_{X}
by

\Phi(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}):=\{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\mid\mathfrak{a}_{m}\neq(0)\}.

Let
\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{q}
be ideals in
\mathcal{O}_{X}
, and let
\lambda>0
. Suppose
f:X^{\prime}\to X
is a log resolution of both
(X,\Delta)
and
\mathfrak{a}\cdot\mathfrak{q}
. Let
F
be an effective divisor on
X^{\prime}
such that

\mathfrak{a}\cdot\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}=\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}(-F).

We define the
multiplier ideal sheaf

\mathcal{J}\left(X,\Delta;\mathfrak{a}^{\lambda}\right)
by

\mathcal{J}\left(X,\Delta;\mathfrak{a}^{\lambda}\right):=f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}\left(K_{X^{\prime}}-\left\lfloor f^{*}(K_{X}+\Delta)+\lambda F\right\rfloor\right).

Note that this definition does not depend on the choice of
f
.

Let
\lambda>0
. We define the
asymptotic multiplier ideal sheaf

\mathcal{J}(X,\Delta;\mathfrak{a}^{\lambda}_{\bullet})
as the largest (with respect to inclusion) among the sheaves

\left\{\mathcal{J}\left(X,\Delta;\mathfrak{a}_{m}^{\frac{\lambda}{m}}\right)\Bigm|m\in\Phi(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})\right\}.

By the Noetherian property of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
, this maximum indeed exists.

For a graded sequence of ideals
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
in
\mathcal{O}_{X}
and ideals
\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{q}
in
\mathcal{O}_{X}
, we define the
log canonical threshold
as follows:

\lct^{\mathfrak{q}}(X,\Delta;\mathfrak{a}):=\inf\left\{\lambda\Bigm|\mathfrak{q}\subseteq\mathcal{J}\left(X,\Delta;\mathfrak{a}^{\lambda}_{\bullet}\right)\right\},

\lct^{\mathfrak{q}}(X,\Delta;\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}):=\sup_{m\geq 1}\left(m\cdot\lct^{\mathfrak{q}}\left(X,\Delta;\mathfrak{a}_{m}\right)\right).

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a pair,
\mathfrak{q}
an ideal in
\mathcal{O}_{X}
, and
\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}
a graded sequence of ideals in
\mathcal{O}_{X}
. Then

\lct^{\mathfrak{q}}(X,\Delta;\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})=\inf\left\{\lambda\bigm|\mathfrak{q}\nsubseteq\mathcal{J}\left(X,\Delta;\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}^{\lambda}\right)\right\}.

Proof.

First, we show

\lct^{\mathfrak{q}}(X,\Delta;\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})\leq\inf\left\{\lambda\Bigm|\mathfrak{q}\nsubseteq\mathcal{J}\left(X,\Delta;\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}^{\lambda}\right)\right\}.

Let
\lambda
be a positive number such that

\lct^{\mathfrak{q}}(X,\Delta;\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})>\lambda
.
Then, for some sufficiently large
m_{0}
, we have

\lct^{\mathfrak{q}}(X,\Delta;\mathfrak{a}_{m_{0}})>\frac{\lambda}{m_{0}}
.
By the definition of the log canonical threshold, for any
\lambda^{\prime}\leq\lambda
, it follows that

\mathfrak{q}\subseteq\mathcal{J}\left(X,\Delta;\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}^{\lambda^{\prime}}\right)=\mathcal{J}\left(X,\Delta;\mathfrak{a}_{m_{0}}^{\tfrac{\lambda^{\prime}}{m_{0}}}\right).

This proves the desired inequality.

For the converse, we note that for any positive integer
m
,

\lct^{\mathfrak{q}}(X,\Delta;\mathfrak{a}_{m})\leq\frac{\lct^{\mathfrak{q}}(X,\Delta;\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})}{m}.

Hence, again by the definition of the log canonical threshold,

\mathfrak{q}\nsubseteq\mathcal{J}\left(X,\Delta;\mathfrak{a}_{m}^{\tfrac{\lct^{\mathfrak{q}}(X,\Delta;\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})}{m}}\right).

Therefore, by the definition of the asymptotic multiplier ideal, we also have

\mathfrak{q}\nsubseteq\mathcal{J}\left(X,\Delta;\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}^{\lct^{\mathfrak{q}}(X,\Delta;\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})}\right),

which completes the proof of the reverse inequality.
∎

Recalling the definition of the log canonical threshold, one obtains the following characterization (cf.
[
JM12
, Lemma 1.7]
):

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a pair, and let
\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{q}
be ideals in
\mathcal{O}_{X}
. Suppose
f:X^{\prime}\to X
is a log resolution of both
(X,\Delta)
and
\mathfrak{a}\cdot\mathfrak{q}
. Then

\lct^{\mathfrak{q}}(X,\Delta;\mathfrak{a})=\inf_{E}\frac{A_{X,\Delta}(E)+\ord_{E}(\mathfrak{q})}{\ord_{E}(\mathfrak{a})},

where
E
runs through all prime divisors in

\mathrm{Supp}\left(f^{-1}_{*}\Delta\right)\cup\mathrm{Supp}\left((\mathfrak{a}\cdot\mathfrak{q})\cdot\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}\right).

Proof.

Let
\lambda>0
. Take an effective divisor
F
on
X^{\prime}
such that
\mathfrak{a}\cdot\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}=\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}(-F)
. Let
E_{1},\cdots,E_{r}
be the prime divisors in

\mathrm{Supp}\left(f^{-1}_{*}\Delta\right)\cup\mathrm{Supp}\left((\mathfrak{a}\cdot\mathfrak{q})\cdot\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}\right).

Observe that

\mathfrak{q}\subseteq\mathcal{J}(X,\Delta;\mathfrak{a}^{\lambda}):=f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}(K_{X^{\prime}}-\left\lfloor f^{*}(K_{X}+\Delta)+\lambda F\right\rfloor)

holds if and only if

\ord_{E_{i}}(K_{X^{\prime}}-\left\lfloor f^{*}(K_{X}+\Delta)+\lambda F\right\rfloor)\geq-\ord_{E_{i}}(\mathfrak{q})\text{ for all }i.

This condition is equivalent to

A_{X,\Delta}(E_{i})-\lambda\cdot\ord_{E_{i}}(\mathfrak{a})>-\ord_{E_{i}}(\mathfrak{q})\text{ for all }i.

which can be rewritten as

\lambda<\frac{A_{X,\Delta}(E_{i})+\ord_{E_{i}}(\mathfrak{q})}{\ord_{E_{i}}(\mathfrak{a})}\text{ for all }i.

Hence, by the definition of the log canonical threshold,
\mathrm{lct}^{\mathfrak{q}}(X,\Delta;\mathfrak{a})
is the infimum of

\frac{A_{X,\Delta}(E_{i})+\ord_{E_{i}}(\mathfrak{q})}{\ord_{E_{i}}(\mathfrak{a})}
, completing the proof.
∎

3.2.
Valuation and log canonical threshold

The goal of this subsection is to prove ?THM?
. We begin by recalling some basic notions on valuations; for further details, see
[
JM12
]
and
[

, Chapter 1]

A
valuation

\nu
over
X
is a function

\nu:K(X)\to\mathbb{R}

satisfying the following properties:

(1)

\nu(a)=0
for all constants
a\in\mathbb{C}
.

(2)

\nu(fg)=\nu(f)+\nu(g)
for all
f,g\in K(X)
.

(3)

\nu(f+g)\geq\min\{\nu(f),\nu(g)\}
for all
f,g\in K(X)
.

We denote by
\mathrm{Val}_{X}
the set of all valuations over
X
, and denote by
\mathrm{Val}^{*}_{X}
the set of non-zero valuations over
X
. For any ideal
\mathfrak{a}
in
\mathcal{O}_{X}
, define

\nu(\mathfrak{a}):=\min\{\nu(f)\mid f\in\mathfrak{a}\}.

We endow
\mathrm{Val}_{X}
with the weakest topology such that, for every ideal
\mathfrak{a}
in
\mathcal{O}_{X}
, the map

\nu\mapsto\nu(\mathfrak{a})

is continuous.

Next, let
f:X^{\prime}\to X
be a resolution of
X
, and let
E\subseteq X^{\prime}
be a simple normal crossing (snc) divisor on
X^{\prime}
. We say that
(X^{\prime},E)
is a
log smooth model
over
X
. Write
E=E_{1}+\cdots+E_{r}
as the sum of prime divisors on
X^{\prime}
. For a subset
\{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}\}\subseteq\{1,\dots,r\}
with
1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq r
, let
\eta:=\eta_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}

be the generic point of
E_{i_{1}}\cap\cdots\cap E_{i_{k}}
(if it exists). Locally around
\eta
, let
z_{j}
be a local defining equation of
E_{i_{j}}
.

By the Cohen structure theorem (cf.
[
Stacks
, Lemma 032D]
), there is an isomorphism

\widehat{\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime},\eta}}\cong K(\eta)\left[\left[z_{1},\dots,z_{k}\right]\right],

and thus any
f\in\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime},\eta}
can be expressed as

f=\sum_{\beta=(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{k})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{k}}a_{\beta}z_{1}^{\beta_{1}}\cdots z_{k}^{\beta_{k}},

where
a_{\beta}\in K(\eta)
. For any
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k})\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{k}
, we define

\nu_{(X^{\prime},E),\alpha}(f):=\min\!\left\{\sum^{k}_{j=1}\alpha_{j}\beta_{j}\Bigm|a_{\beta}\neq 0\right\}.

It is straightforward to verify that
\nu_{(X^{\prime},E),\alpha}
is indeed a valuation over
X
. We denote by
\mathrm{QM}_{\eta}(X^{\prime},E)
the set of all valuations of the form
\nu_{(X^{\prime},E),\alpha}
. Observe that we can naturally identify
\mathrm{QM}_{\eta}(X^{\prime},E)
with the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{k}
. Define

\mathrm{QM}(X^{\prime},E):=\bigcup_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq r}\mathrm{QM}_{\eta_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}}(X^{\prime},E).

If
E_{i_{1}}\cap\cdots\cap E_{i_{k}}=\varnothing
, we set
\mathrm{QM}_{\eta}(X^{\prime},E)=\varnothing
. For a valuation
\nu\in\mathrm{Val}_{X}
, we say that
\nu
is
quasi-monomial
if there is a log smooth model
(X^{\prime},E)
over
X
such that
\nu\in\mathrm{QM}(X^{\prime},E)
holds.

We now introduce the notions of
retraction map
and
log discrepancy
for valuations. For any log smooth model
(X^{\prime},E)
over
X
, define the
retraction map

r_{(X^{\prime},E)}:\mathrm{Val}_{X}\to\mathrm{QM}_{\eta}(X^{\prime},E)\;\;\text{ by }\;\;\nu\mapsto\nu_{(X^{\prime},E),(\nu(E_{1}),\dots,\nu(E_{k}))}.

The above map also defines

r_{(X^{\prime},E)}:\mathrm{Val}_{X}\to\mathrm{QM}(X^{\prime},E).

Next, we define

A_{X,\Delta}(\nu_{(X^{\prime},E),\alpha}):=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\alpha_{j}A_{X,\Delta}(E_{j}),

and for any
\nu\in\mathrm{Val}_{X}
,

A_{X,\Delta}(\nu):=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}(X^{\prime},E)\\
\text{log smooth over }X\end{subarray}}A_{X,\Delta}\left(r_{(X^{\prime},E)}(\nu)\right).

By construction, the map
A_{X,\Delta}:\mathrm{Val}_{X}\to\mathbb{R}
is lower semi-continuous. For more details on the log discrepancy of valuations, see
[
JM12
, Chapter 5]
and
[
Blu18
, Chapter 7]
.

Let us prove the following lemma.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a pair,
\mathfrak{a}
an ideal in
\mathcal{O}_{X}
, and

a log resolution of
\mathfrak{a}
. Let

be a log smooth model over
X
, and let
\eta
be the generic point of

\nu\mapsto\nu(\mathfrak{a})

is a linear function on

Proof.

Let

be a local defining equation of
E_{i}
around
\eta
. Since the divisor
\mathfrak{a}\cdot\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}
is simple normal crossing on

This clearly shows that

Theorem 3.7

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a klt pair,
\mathfrak{q}

We argue similarly to the proof of
[

First, observe that if

By ?THM?

Then, by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n!}\ord_{E}\left(\mathfrak{b}_{n!}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n}{(n+1)!}\ord_{E}(I)+\frac{1}{(n+1)!}\ord_{E}\left(\mathfrak{b}_{(n+1)!}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n^{\prime}\geq n}\frac{n^{\prime}}{(n^{\prime}+1)!}\ord_{E}(I)+\ord_{E}\left(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n!}\ord_{E}(I)+\ord_{E}\left(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\right).

Fix a constant

\ord_{E}(I)\leq C\cdot A_{X,\Delta}(E)
for every prime divisor

over

, for any

Let

be a prime divisor over

such that

\lct^{\mathfrak{q}}(X,\Delta;\mathfrak{b}_{n!})=\frac{A_{X,\Delta}(E)+\ord_{E}(\mathfrak{q})}{\ord_{E}\left(\mathfrak{b}_{n!}\right)}.

Then

\frac{1}{n!\lct^{\mathfrak{q}}(X,\Delta;\mathfrak{b}_{n!})}=\frac{\ord_{E}\left(\mathfrak{b}_{n}\right)}{n!A_{X,\Delta}(E)+n!\ord_{E}(\mathfrak{q})}\leq\frac{\ord_{E}\left(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\right)}{A_{X,\Delta}(E)+\ord_{E}(\mathfrak{q})}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

Hence we obtain

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\lct^{\mathfrak{q}}(X,\Delta;\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})}-\frac{\ord_{E}\left(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\right)}{A_{X,\Delta}(E)+\ord_{E}(\mathfrak{q})}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{\lct^{\mathfrak{q}}(X,\Delta;\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet})}-\frac{1}{n!\lct^{\mathfrak{q}}(X,\Delta;\mathfrak{b}_{n!})}\right)+\left(\frac{1}{n!\lct^{\mathfrak{q}}(X,\Delta;\mathfrak{b}_{n!})}-\frac{\ord_{E}\left(\mathfrak{a}_{\bullet}\right)}{A_{X,\Delta}(E)+\ord_{E}(\mathfrak{q})}\right)

The goal of this section is to define the notion of the

introduced by Hacon and Lehmann (cf.

]

]

We begin by stating the definition of the
asymptotic order
of a divisor.

Definition 4.1

(cf.
[
ELMNP06
]
,
[
Nak04
, Defiition 2.2, and Chapter III, 1.6 Definition]
)
.

Let
X
be a normal projective variety,
\nu\in\mathrm{Val}_{X}
a valuation,
A
an ample divisor on
X
, and
D
a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor on
X
such that
\kappa(D)\geq 0
. For a positive integer
n
such that
|nD|
is non-empty, we define

\nu(\|D\|):=\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{\nu(\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{Bs}(|mnD|)})}{nm}.

We then set

\sigma_{\nu}(D):=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varepsilon\to 0\\
\varepsilon\text{ is rational}\end{subarray}}\nu(\|D+\varepsilon A\|).

Note that
\sigma_{\nu}(D)
is independent to the choice of ample divisor (cf.
[
Nak04
, 1.5 in Chapter III]
).

Let us prove the following lemma.

Lemma 4.2

(cf.
[
Leh14
, Lemma 2.10]
)
.

Let
X
be a normal projective variety,
A
an ample divisor on
X
, and
D
a pseudo-effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on
X
. Let
\nu\in\mathrm{Val}_{X}
be a valuation.

(a)

The function

f(t):=\nu(\|D+tA\|)

is strictly decreasing for any rational
t>0
.

(b)

If
D
is big, then
\nu(\|D\|)=\sigma_{\nu}(D)
.

Proof.

Let us prove (a). Suppose
0<t<t^{\prime}
. Choose
0<\varepsilon\ll t^{\prime}-t
. Since
A
is ample, the divisor
(t^{\prime}-t)A+\varepsilon(D+tA)
is also an ample divisor. We obtain

\displaystyle\nu(\|D+t^{\prime}A\|)

\displaystyle=\nu(\|(1-\varepsilon)(D+tA)+(t^{\prime}-t)A+\varepsilon(D+tA)\|)

\displaystyle\leq(1-\varepsilon)\nu(\|D+tA\|)<\nu(\|D+tA\|).

Thus
\nu(\|D+tA\|)
is strictly larger than
\nu(\|D+t^{\prime}A\|)
, proving that
f(t)
is strictly decreasing in
t
.

Let us prove (b). Assume

\displaystyle\to H^{1}(X^{\prime}_{i},\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}_{i}}(g^{*}_{i1}D_{i-1}))=H^{1}(X_{i-1},\mathcal{O}_{X_{i-1}}(D_{i-1})),

Hence we conclude that
d_{i}\leq d_{i-1}
for all
i
.

Step 3
. Assume that the MMP does not terminate. Since
\{d_{i}\}
is a non-increasing sequence of positive integers, for sufficiently large
i_{0}
we must have
d_{i_{0}-1}=d_{i_{0}}
.

Next, consider the following Leray spectral sequences:

\displaystyle{}^{{}^{\prime}}E^{st}_{2}

\displaystyle=H^{s}(Z_{i_{0}},R^{t}\varphi_{i_{0}1*}\mathcal{O}_{X_{i_{0}-1}}(D_{i_{0}-1}))\implies H^{s+t}(X_{i_{0}-1},\mathcal{O}_{X_{i_{0}-1}}(D_{i_{0}-1})),\text{ and }

\displaystyle{}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}E^{st}_{2}

\displaystyle=H^{s}(Z_{i_{0}},R^{t}(\varphi_{i_{0}1}\circ g_{i_{0}1})_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}_{i_{0}-1}}(D_{i_{0}-1}))\implies H^{s+t}(X^{\prime}_{i_{0}},\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}_{i_{0}}}(g^{*}_{i_{0}1}D_{i_{0}-1})).

Because
X
has rational singularities (cf.
[
KM98
, Theorem 5.22]
), the natural map

\mathcal{O}_{X_{i_{0}-1}}(D_{i-1})\cong\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}_{i_{0}}}(g^{*}_{i_{0}1}D_{i_{0}-1})

induces isomorphisms
{}^{{}^{\prime}}E^{st}_{2}\cong{}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}E^{st}_{2}
for all
s,t
. Thus, these two spectral sequences are isomorphic. From the resulting edge map

\alpha_{1}:H^{1}(Z_{i_{0}},\varphi_{i_{0}1*}\mathcal{O}_{X_{i_{0}-1}}(D_{i_{0}-1}))\to H^{1}(X_{i_{0}-1},\mathcal{O}_{X_{i_{0}-1}}(D_{i_{0}-1}))

we can construct an injection

\alpha_{2}:H^{1}(Z_{i_{0}},(\varphi_{i_{0}2}\circ g_{i_{0}2})_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}_{i_{0}}}(g^{*}_{i_{0}1}D_{i_{0}-1}))\to H^{1}(X_{i_{0}-1},\mathcal{O}_{X_{i_{0}-1}}(D_{i_{0}-1})),

using the commutative diagram from earlier steps. By (
5.1
), this in turn yields

\alpha_{3}:H^{1}(Z_{i_{0}},\varphi_{i_{0}2*}\mathcal{O}_{X_{i_{0}}}(D_{i_{0}}))\to H^{1}(X_{i_{0}-1},\mathcal{O}_{X_{i_{0}-1}}(D_{i_{0}-1})).

Observe that

D_{i_{0}}=(K_{X_{i_{0}}}+\Delta_{i_{0}})+(D_{i_{0}}-(K_{X_{i_{0}}}+\Delta_{i_{0}}))

and

D_{i_{0}}-(K_{X_{i_{0}}}+\Delta_{i_{0}})=(2m-1)(K_{X_{i_{0}}}+\Delta_{i_{0}})

which is
\varphi_{i_{0}2}
-ample. By the relative Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing theorem (cf.
[
KMM87
, Theorem 1-2-3]
) and the Leray spectral sequence, we obtain an injection

\alpha_{4}:H^{1}(X_{i_{0}},\mathcal{O}_{X_{i_{0}}}(D_{i_{0}}))\to H^{1}(X_{i_{0}-1},\mathcal{O}_{X_{i_{0}-1}}(D_{i_{0}-1})).

Moreover,
\alpha_{1}
is an isomorphism if and only if
\alpha_{4}
is an isomorphism. Since
d_{i_{0}-1}=d_{i_{0}}
, we conclude that
\alpha_{4}
, and hence
\alpha_{1}
is indeed an isomorphism.

Step 4
. The first four terms of the five-term exact sequence of
{}^{{}^{\prime}}E^{st}_{2}
are

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\to H^{1}(Z_{i_{0}},\varphi_{i_{0}1*}\mathcal{O}_{X_{i_{0}-1}}(D_{i_{0}-1}))\overset{\alpha_{1}}{\to}H^{1}(X_{i_{0}-1},\mathcal{O}_{X_{i_{0}-1}}(D_{i_{0}-1}))

\displaystyle\to H^{0}(Z_{i_{0}},R^{1}\varphi_{i_{0}1*}\mathcal{O}_{X_{i_{0}-1}}(D_{i_{0}-1}))\to H^{2}(Z_{i_{0}},\varphi_{i_{0}1*}\mathcal{O}_{X_{i_{0}-1}}(D_{i_{0}-1})).

Arguing as in Step 3, we compute:

\displaystyle H^{2}(Z_{i_{0}},\varphi_{i_{0}1*}\mathcal{O}_{X_{i_{0}-1}}(D_{i_{0}-1}))

\displaystyle=H^{2}(Z_{i_{0}},(\varphi_{i_{0}1}\circ g_{i_{0}1})_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}_{i_{0}}}(g^{*}_{i_{0}1}D_{i_{0}-1}))

\displaystyle\overset{(1)}{=}H^{2}(Z_{i_{0}},(\varphi_{i_{0}2}\circ g_{i_{0}2})_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}_{i_{0}}}(g^{*}_{i2}D_{i_{0}}+E_{i_{0}}))

\displaystyle=H^{2}(Z_{i_{0}},\varphi_{i_{0}2*}\mathcal{O}_{X_{i_{0}}}(D_{i_{0}}))

\displaystyle\overset{(2)}{=}H^{2}(X_{i_{0}},\mathcal{O}_{X_{i_{0}}}(D_{i_{0}}))\overset{(3)}{=}0,

where
(1)
follows from the commutative diagram and (
5.1
),
(2)
follows from the relative Kawamata–Viehweg vanishing theorem combined with the Leray spectral sequence, and
(3)
holds by the argument in the last sentence of Step 1,
(c)
, together with ?THM?
.

Since
\alpha_{1}
is an isomorphism, it follows that

H^{0}(Z_{i_{0}},R^{1}\varphi_{i_{0}1*}\mathcal{O}_{X_{i_{0}-1}}(D_{i_{0}-1}))=0.

By (a), the map
f_{i_{0}}
is a flip of type
(1,\dim X-2)
. Hence the support of

R^{1}\varphi_{i_{0}1*}\mathcal{O}_{X_{i_{0}-1}}(D_{i_{0}-1})

is zero-dimensional, which forces

R^{1}\varphi_{i_{0}1*}\mathcal{O}_{X_{i_{0}-1}}(D_{i_{0}-1})=0.

Step 5
. In this step, we show that there exists a rational curve
C\subseteq X_{i_{0}-1}
such that

H^{1}(C,\mathcal{O}_{X_{i_{0}-1}}(D_{i_{0}-1})|_{C})=0.

By the Cone Theorem (cf.
[
Kaw91
, Theorem 1]
), there is a rational curve
C\subseteq X_{i_{0}-1}
that is contracted by
\varphi_{i_{0}1}
. Let
x:=\varphi_{i_{0}1}(C)
, and let
E:=\varphi^{-1}_{i_{0}1}(x)
be the scheme-theoretic fiber of
\varphi_{i_{0}1}
over
x
. Since
C\subseteq E\subseteq X_{i_{0}-1}
, we can construct a coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on
X_{i_{0}-1}
fitting into an exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{O}_{X_{i_{0}-1}}(D_{i_{0}-1})\to\mathcal{O}_{X_{i_{0}-1}}(D_{i_{0}-1})|_{C}\to 0.

Because every fiber of
\varphi_{i_{0}1}
is
1
-dimensional, we have
R^{2}\varphi_{i_{0}1*}\mathcal{F}=0
. Consequently, thee is a surjection

0=R^{1}\varphi_{i_{0}1*}\mathcal{O}_{X_{i_{0}-1}}(D_{i_{0}-1})\twoheadrightarrow R^{1}\varphi_{i_{0}1*}\mathcal{O}_{X_{i_{0}-1}}(D_{i_{0}-1})|_{C},

whoch implies

H^{1}(C,\mathcal{O}_{X_{i_{0}-1}}(D_{i_{0}-1})|_{C})=R^{1}\varphi_{i_{0}1*}\mathcal{O}_{X_{i_{0}-1}}(D_{i_{0}-1})|_{C}=0.

Step 6
. Since
-(K_{X_{{i_{0}}-1}}+\Delta_{i_{0}-1})
is
\varphi_{i_{0}1}
-ample, it follows that

\mathcal{L}:=\mathcal{O}_{X_{i_{0}-1}}(m(K_{X_{i_{0}-1}}+\Delta_{i_{0}-1}))|_{C}

is an anti-ample line bundle on
C
. Let
\nu:\mathbb{P}^{1}\to C
be the normalization. As
\nu
is finite and pullback of an ample line bundle along a finite morphism remains ample (cf.
[
Stacks
, Lemma 0B5V]
), we see that
\nu^{*}\mathcal{L}
is anti-ample on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Hence
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-\ell)=\nu^{*}\mathcal{L}
for some positive integer
\ell
. Recalling that

D_{i_{0}-1}=2m(K_{X_{i_{0}-1}}+\Delta_{i_{0}-1}),

we obtain
\mathcal{O}_{X_{i_{0}-1}}(D_{i_{0}-1})|_{C}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-2\ell)
. Moreover, since any finite morphism has vanishing higher direct images (cf.
[
Stacks
, Lemma 02OE]
), the Leray spectral sequence gives

H^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-2\ell))=H^{1}(C,\mathcal{O}_{X_{i_{0}-1}}(D_{i_{0}-1})|_{C})=0,

which contradicts the well-known fact that

H^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-N))\neq 0\text{ for all }N\geq 2.

This contradiction forces the MMP to terminate, thereby completing the proof of the assertion.

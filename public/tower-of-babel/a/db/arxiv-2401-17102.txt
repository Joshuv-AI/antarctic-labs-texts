Abstract




Abstract
This paper is concerned with the linear stability analysis for the Couette flow of the Euler-Poisson system for both ionic fluid and electronic fluid in the domain
\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}
. We establish the upper and lower bounds of the linearized solutions of the Euler-Poisson system near Couette flow. In particular, the inviscid damping for the solenoidal component of the velocity is obtained.

Keywords
: Euler-Poisson equations; Couette flow; inviscid damping

Mathematics Subject Classification
: 35M30; 76E05

1. Introduction

Consider the following two fluid Euler-Poisson system in a two dimensional domain
\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}
,

\begin{split}&\partial_{t}{\widetilde{\eta}_{\pm}}+\nabla\cdot(\widetilde{\eta}_{\pm}\widetilde{v}_{\pm})=0,\\
&\widetilde{\eta}_{\pm}m_{\pm}[\partial_{t}\widetilde{v}_{\pm}+\widetilde{v}_{\pm}\cdot\nabla\widetilde{v}_{\pm}]+T_{\pm}\nabla\widetilde{p}_{\pm}=\mp e\widetilde{\eta}_{\pm}\nabla\widetilde{\phi},\\
&-\Delta\widetilde{\phi}=4\pi e[\widetilde{\eta}_{+}-\widetilde{\eta}_{-}],\end{split}

(1.1)

where
{\widetilde{\eta}_{\pm}}(x,y,t)
and
{\widetilde{v}_{\pm}}(x,y,t)
denote the density and the velocity of the ion fluid
(+)
and the electron fluid
(-)
in a plasma,
\nabla\widetilde{\phi}
represents the self-consistent electric field,
\nabla{\widetilde{p}}_{\pm}
denote the pressure of ions and electrons,
m_{\pm}
denote the mass density of the ions and the electrons,
e
is the charge for an electron and
T_{\pm}
denote the effective temperatures of the ions and the electrons. Such a system is the simplified model from the two fluid Euler-Maxwell system as the light speed tends to infinity and is very fundamental in plasma physics. This Euler-Poisson system is also an important origin of many famous dispersive equations such as KdV, KP, NLS equations and is widely studied in the literature in recent years. See a series of papers of the first author and his collaborators
[
44
,
40
,
21
,
41
,
39
]
for the rigorous mathematical justifications of these dispersive equations from the Euler-Poisson system. For more physics background and the mathematical development of such a system, one may see the concise review paper
[
17
]
and the references cited therein.

The two-fluid Euler-Poisson system has rich and complex dynamics with distinct physical parameters. In particular, since the mass ratio of electrons and ions is of
10^{-3}\ll 1
, the system (
1.1
) can be simplified to the following one-fluid Euler-Poisson system for the electron fluid dynamics of
({\widetilde{\eta}}_{-},{\widetilde{v}}_{-},\widetilde{\phi})
in an ion background (Langmuir waves,
[
18
,
19
]
)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\widetilde{\eta}_{-}+\nabla\cdot(\widetilde{\eta}_{-}\widetilde{v}_{-})=0,

(1.2)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\eta}_{-}\left[\partial_{t}\widetilde{v}_{-}+(\widetilde{v}_{-}\cdot\nabla)\widetilde{v}_{-}\right]+\frac{1}{m_{-}}\nabla{\widetilde{p}}_{-}(\widetilde{\eta}_{-})=\frac{e}{m_{-}}\widetilde{\eta}_{-}\nabla\widetilde{\phi},

(1.3)

\displaystyle\Delta\widetilde{\phi}=4\pi e\widetilde{\eta}_{-}-4\pi e.

(1.4)

On the other hand, assume
\widetilde{p}_{-}(\widetilde{\eta}_{-})=T_{-}\widetilde{\eta}_{-}
in (
1.1
) with a constant temperature
T_{-}
and take formally
m_{-}/m_{+}\to 0
(such a limit is rigorously justified by Grenier
et al.
recently in
[
16
]
), we deduce
\nabla\widetilde{p}_{-}=T_{-}\nabla\widetilde{\eta}_{-}=e\widetilde{\eta}_{-}\nabla\tilde{\phi}
, from which the celebrated Boltzmann relation for the electron density can be derived:

\widetilde{\eta}_{-}=\eta_{0}\exp{\{e\widetilde{\phi}/T_{-}\}},

where
\eta_{0}
is a constant, set to be one for simplicity. Then we obtain the reduced Euler-Poisson system for ion dynamics for
(\widetilde{\eta}_{+},\widetilde{v}_{+},\widetilde{\phi})

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\widetilde{\eta}_{+}+\nabla\cdot(\widetilde{\eta}_{+}\widetilde{v}_{+})=0,

(1.5)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\eta}_{+}\left[\partial_{t}\widetilde{v}_{+}+(\widetilde{v}_{+}\cdot\nabla)\widetilde{v}_{+}\right]+\frac{T_{+}}{m_{+}}\nabla\widetilde{\eta}_{+}=-\frac{e}{m_{+}}\widetilde{\eta}_{+}\nabla\widetilde{\phi},

(1.6)

\displaystyle\Delta\widetilde{\phi}=4\pi e\left[\exp\left(\frac{e}{T_{-}}\widetilde{\phi}\right)-\widetilde{\eta}_{+}\right].

(1.7)

For global well-posedness of such ion dynamics system in
\mathbb{R}^{3+1}
, one may refer to
[
20
]
.

In this paper, we are interested in another topic of this system, i.e., stability of the Couette flow of the above Euler-Poisson system. To be precise, we will consider the linear stability of the Couette flow for the two Euler-Poisson systems (
1.2
)-(
1.4
) and (
1.5
)-(
1.7
) in a periodic strip
\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}
with
\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
. It is clear that there is a stationary solution

(\widetilde{\eta}_{s},\widetilde{v}_{s},\widetilde{\phi}_{s})=({1,(y,0)^{\top},0})

for both system (
1.5
)-(
1.7
) and (
1.2
)-(
1.4
). We also call this solution Couette flow, following the name in Navier-Stokes equation in fluid dynamics. Formal stability analysis of plasma equilibria to the related Poisson-Vlasov and Maxwell-Vlasov systems in one, two and three dimensions is referred to the paper of Holm
et al

[
22
]
. To the best of our knowledge, no rigorous mathematical stability analysis of the Couette flow for the Euler-Poisson system either for the one-fluid Euler-Poisson system or the two-fluid Euler-Poisson system can be found in the literature and we believe that this topic is new and interesting for the Euler-Poisson system. The purpose of the present paper is to study the linear stability of the Couette flow for both the ion and electron one-fluid Euler-Poisson dynamics.

Let us deviate slightly to briefly introduce some related results on the stability analysis of the Couette flow in fluid dynamics, in particular for the Navier-Stokes or the Euler system. Indeed, from the mathematical point of view, the stability of the Couette flow and related topics have been extensively studied and many fruitful results were obtained in the past decades. For an incompressible fluid, the classical results on the linear stability analysis of the Couette flow were obtained by Rayleigh
[
45
]
and Kelvin
[
34
]
. Inviscid damping for the Euler equations on the domain
\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}
was studied by Bedrossian and Masmoudi
[
7

, which is a significant result on nonlinear stability of the planar Couette flow. For nonlinear inviscid damping for a class of monotone shear flows for the 2D Euler equation in finite channel for initial perturbation in Gevrey class with compact support, one may refer to
[
42
]
. See also
[
24
]
for the inviscid damping near the Couette flow in a channel for the 2D Euler equation, where the authors showed that the velocity field converges to a nearby shear flow when the initial perturbation is small in a suitable Gevrey sapce. One may also refer to other nice work
[
11
,
50
,
25
,
29
,
30
,
38
,
43
,
47
]
for the stability analysis of the Couette flow or general shear flows for the Euler equation in various situations. In the presence of viscosity, some interesting results have been obtained in the study of transition threshold problem related to enhanced dissipation (see, e.g.,
[
9
,
3
,
5
,
6
,
8
,
46
,
10
,
43
]
for various situations). More results on the stability of the two-dimensional and three-dimensional Couette flow in the Navier-Stokes equations at high Reynolds number can be found in the review paper
[
4
]
.

In the case of compressible fluid, the linear stability of plane Couette flow has been studied in the past decades in physics literature in the past nearly a century starting from the ’40s
[
35
,
22
,
12
]
. Glatzel
[

]
considered the inviscid stability of this problem based on a normal mode analysis, and the effects of viscosity were then taken into account by the same author
[

]
. Duck
et al.
[

]
investigated the compressible stability of the plane Couette flow for realistic compressible flow models, where they show that the details of the mean flow profile have a profound effect on the stability of the flow. Hu and Zhong
[
23
]
studied the linear stability of viscous supersonic plane Couette flow for a perfect gas governed by Sutherland viscosity law by using a fourth-order finite-difference method and a spectral collocation method. For rigorous mathematical analysis, when the initial value is sufficiently close to the plane Couette flow, Kagei
[
31
]
proved that the plane Couette flow is asymptotically stable provided that the Reynolds and Mach numbers are sufficiently small, see also Kagei
[
32
,
33
]
for more general parallel flows. Subsequently, the constraint on the Reynolds number in
[
31
]
, which is used to ensure the stability of the plane Couette flow, was relaxed by Li and Zhang
[

]
. The inviscid damping and enhanced dissipation phenomena were detected by Antonelli
et al.
[

,
2
]
for the homogeneous Couette flow in a 2D isentropic compressible fluid. In addition, we refer to
[
49
]
for a inviscid damping result on the non-isentropic compressible Euler equations on the domain
\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}
. For the 3D case, Zeng
et al.
[
48

established the linear stability result of the Couette flow in the isentropic compressible Navier-Stokes equations on the domain
\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}
. However, the rigorous mathematical study is less developed compared to the incompressible case, and a lot of work is to be established in both the linear and the nonlinear level in the future. In particular, there is no stability analysis for the Couette flow for the Euler-Poisson system studied in this paper.

In the following, we will discuss the linear stability of the Couette flow for the ion Euler-Poisson system and the electron Euler-Poisson system separately. Compared to the classical Euler system and the Navier-Stokes system, the analysis includes some new insights, since the system is nonlocal due to the presence of the electric field.

1.1. Ion dynamics case

To study the linear stability for the Couette flow in system (
1.5
)-(
1.7
), we consider a perturbation around the above stationary solution, i.e., let

\widetilde{\eta}_{+}=\eta_{+}+\widetilde{\eta}_{s},\hskip 10.00002pt\widetilde{v}_{+}=u_{+}+\widetilde{v}_{s},\hskip 10.00002pt\widetilde{\phi}=\phi+\widetilde{\phi}_{s}.

We first consider the ion dynamics system (
1.5
)-(
1.7
). Then the linearized system around the stationary solution
(\widetilde{\eta}_{s},\widetilde{v}_{s},\widetilde{\phi}_{s})
for system (
1.5
)-(
1.7
) is written as

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\eta_{+}+y\partial_{x}\eta_{+}+(\nabla\cdot u_{+})=0,

(1.8)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u_{+}+y\partial_{x}u_{+}+\begin{pmatrix}u^{y}_{+}\\
0\end{pmatrix}+\frac{T_{+}}{m_{+}}\nabla\eta_{+}=-\frac{e}{m_{+}}\nabla\phi,

(1.9)

\displaystyle\Delta\phi=4\pi e\left(\frac{e}{T_{-}}\phi-\eta_{+}\right).

(1.10)

Let
\psi_{+}=\nabla\cdot u_{+}
and
\omega_{+}=\nabla^{\perp}\cdot u_{+}
with
\nabla^{\perp}=(-\partial_{y},\partial_{x})^{\top}
. Then the Helmholtz projection operators are defined as

u_{+}=(u^{x}_{+},u^{y}_{+})^{\top}=\nabla\Delta^{-1}\psi_{+}+\nabla^{\perp}\Delta^{-1}\omega_{+}:=\mathbb{Q}[u_{+}]+\mathbb{P}[u_{+}].

(1.11)

Then the linearized system (
1.8
)-(
1.10
) is transformed into the form with respect to
(\eta_{+},\psi_{+},\omega_{+})

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\eta_{+}+y\partial_{x}\eta_{+}+\psi_{+}=0,

(1.12)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\psi_{+}+y\partial_{x}\psi_{+}+2\partial_{x}u^{y}_{+}+\frac{T_{+}}{m_{+}}\Delta\eta_{+}=-\frac{4\pi e^{2}}{m_{+}}\Delta\left(-\Delta+\frac{4\pi e^{2}}{T_{-}}\right)^{-1}\eta_{+},

(1.13)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\omega_{+}+y\partial_{x}\omega_{+}-\psi_{+}=0.

(1.14)

Denote the average of a function in the
x
direction by

f_{a}(y)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}f(x,y)dx.

We take the
x
-average for equations (
1.12
), (
1.13
) and (
1.14
) to obtain the following system

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\eta_{+,a}+\psi_{+,a}=0,

(1.15)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\psi_{+,a}+\frac{T_{+}}{m_{+}}\partial_{yy}\eta_{+,a}=-\frac{4\pi e^{2}}{m_{+}}\Delta\left(-\Delta+\frac{4\pi e^{2}}{T_{-}}\right)^{-1}\eta_{+,a},

(1.16)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\omega_{+,a}-\psi_{+,a}=0.

(1.17)

Combining (
1.15
) with (
1.17
) yields
\partial_{t}\left(\eta_{+,a}+\omega_{+,a}\right)=0
.
It is easy to see that

\eta_{+,a}+\omega_{+,a}=\eta^{in}_{+,a}+\omega^{in}_{+,a},

(1.18)

where
(\eta^{in}_{+,a},\omega^{in}_{+,a})
represents the initial data. In the rest of the paper, we still use similar notation to represent the initial data.

According to (
1.15
) and (
1.16
), we get

\partial_{tt}\eta_{+,a}-\frac{T_{+}}{m_{+}}\partial_{yy}\eta_{+,a}-\frac{4\pi e^{2}}{m_{+}}\Delta\left(-\Delta+\frac{4\pi e^{2}}{T_{-}}\right)^{-1}\eta_{+,a}=0~in~\mathbb{R}.

(1.19)

Next we supply system (
1.15
)-(
1.17
) with the initial condition

\left(\eta_{+,a},\psi_{+,a},\omega_{+,a}\right)|_{t=0}=\left(\eta^{in}_{+,a},\psi^{in}_{+,a},\omega^{in}_{+,a}\right)=(0,0,0).

(1.20)

Then
\left(\eta_{+,a},\psi_{+,a},\omega_{+,a}\right)=(0,0,0)
is the unique solution to system (
1.15
)-(
1.17
) with initial condition (
1.20
). In fact, taking the
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
inner product of (
1.19
) with
\partial_{t}\eta_{+,a}
, then it follows from integration by parts that

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\left\lVert\partial_{t}\eta_{+,a}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}+\frac{T_{+}}{m_{+}}\left\lVert\partial_{y}\eta_{+,a}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}+\frac{4\pi e^{2}}{m_{+}}\left\lVert\nabla\left(-\Delta+\frac{4\pi e^{2}}{T_{-}}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\eta_{+,a}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}\right)=0,

which implies that
\eta_{+,a}=0
is the unique solution to the cauchy problem, equation (
1.19
) with
\eta^{in}_{+,a}=0
. By virtue of (
1.18
) and (
1.15
), we deduce that
\left(\psi_{+,a},\omega_{+,a}\right)=(0,0)
. Based on this fact, for simplicity of notation, we study the dynamics of
(\eta_{+},\psi_{+},\omega_{+})
with
\left(\eta^{in}_{+,a},\psi^{in}_{+,a},\omega^{in}_{+,a}\right)=(0,0,0)
, instead of studying the dynamics for
(\eta_{+}-\eta_{+,a},\psi_{+}-\psi_{+,a},\omega_{+}-\omega_{+,a})
.

The main results for the ion dynamics are as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
(\eta^{in}_{+},\omega^{in}_{+})\in H^{1}_{x}H^{2}_{y}
and that
\psi^{in}_{+}\in H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{x}L^{2}_{y}
with
\left(\eta^{in}_{+,a},\psi^{in}_{+,a},\omega^{in}_{+,a}\right)=(0,0,0)
. Let
(\eta_{+},u_{+},\phi)
be a smooth solution for the system (
1.8
)-(
1.10
). Then the following estimates hold

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathbb{P}[u_{+}]^{x}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert\phi(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{\frac{m_{+}}{T_{+}}}\frac{1}{\left\langle t\right\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}}\left(\left\lVert\sqrt{\frac{T_{+}}{m_{+}}}\eta^{in}_{+}\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{x}L^{2}_{y}}+\left\lVert\eta^{in}_{+}+\omega^{in}_{+}\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{x}H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{y}}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\frac{1}{\left\langle t\right\rangle}\left\lVert\eta^{in}_{+}+\omega^{in}_{+}\right\rVert_{H^{-1}_{x}H^{1}_{y}}+\sqrt{\frac{m_{+}}{T_{+}}}\frac{1}{\left\langle t\right\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}}\left\lVert\psi^{in}_{+}\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{x}H^{-1}_{y}},

(1.21)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathbb{P}[u_{+}]^{y}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{\frac{m_{+}}{T_{+}}}\frac{1}{\left\langle t\right\rangle^{\frac{3}{2}}}\left(\left\lVert\sqrt{\frac{T_{+}}{m_{+}}}\eta^{in}_{+}\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{x}H^{1}_{y}}+\left\lVert\eta^{in}_{+}+\omega^{in}_{+}\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{x}H^{\frac{3}{2}}_{y}}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\frac{1}{\left\langle t\right\rangle^{2}}\left\lVert\eta^{in}_{+}+\omega^{in}_{+}\right\rVert_{H^{-1}_{x}H^{2}_{y}}+\sqrt{\frac{m_{+}}{T_{+}}}\frac{1}{\left\langle t\right\rangle^{\frac{3}{2}}}\left\lVert\psi^{in}_{+}\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{x}L^{2}_{y}},

(1.22)

and

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathbb{Q}[u_{+}](t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\sqrt{\frac{T_{+}}{m_{+}}}\left\lVert\eta_{+}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\lesssim\left\langle t\right\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\left\lVert\sqrt{\frac{T_{+}}{m_{+}}}\eta^{in}_{+}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert\psi^{in}_{+}\right\rVert_{H^{-1}}+\left\lVert\eta^{in}_{+}+\omega^{in}_{+}\right\rVert_{H^{1}}\right).

(1.23)

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume that
\left(\eta^{in}_{+},\omega^{in}_{+}\right)\in L^{2}_{x}H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{y}
and that
\psi^{in}_{+}\in H^{-\frac{3}{2}}_{x}H^{-2}_{y}
with
\left(\eta^{in}_{+,a},\psi^{in}_{+,a},\omega^{in}_{+,a}\right)=(0,0,0)
. Let
(\eta_{+},u_{+},\phi)
be a smooth solution for the system (
1.8
)-(
1.10
). Then it holds that

\left\lVert\mathbb{Q}[u_{+}](t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\sqrt{\frac{T_{+}}{m_{+}}}\left\lVert\eta_{+}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\gtrsim\mathcal{C}^{in}_{\delta}\left(\eta^{in}_{+},\psi^{in}_{+},\omega^{in}_{+}\right)\left\langle t\right\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}},

where the constant
\mathcal{C}^{in}_{\delta}\left(\eta^{in}_{+},\psi^{in}_{+},\omega^{in}_{+}\right)
is a suitable combination of
\left\lVert\eta^{in}_{+}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{y}}
,
\left\lVert\psi^{in}_{+}\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{3}{2}}_{x}H^{-2}_{y}}
and
\left\lVert\omega^{in}_{+}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{y}}
.

The above two theorems give upper and lower bounds of the linearized solutions of the Euler-Poisson system near Couette flow. In particular, the inviscid damping for the solenoidal component of the velocity is established, while the
L^{2}
-norm of the velocity grows as
t^{1/2}
. We also remark that it may happen that the constant
\mathcal{C}^{in}_{\delta}
vanishes at some positive time
t>0
, however we can prove that the set of initial data for which the RHS of the lower bound in Theorem
1.2
vanishes at some time has empty interior in any Sobolev space in which the initial data are taken. The details are similar to that for the Euler system in
[
1
]
and are omitted here.

1.2. Electron dynamics case

To study the linear stability for the Couette flow in system (
1.2
)-(
1.4
), we also carry out a perturbation around the above stationary solution, i.e.,

\widetilde{\eta}_{-}=\eta_{-}+\widetilde{\eta}_{s},\hskip 10.00002pt\widetilde{v}_{-}=u_{-}+\widetilde{v}_{s},\hskip 10.00002pt\widetilde{\phi}=\phi+\widetilde{\phi}_{s}.

Then the linearized system around the stationary solution
(\widetilde{\eta}_{s},\widetilde{v}_{s},\widetilde{\phi}_{s})
for system (
1.2
)-(
1.4
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\eta_{-}+y\partial_{x}\eta_{-}+(\nabla\cdot u_{-})=0,

(1.24)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u_{-}+y\partial_{x}u_{-}+\begin{pmatrix}u^{y}_{-}\\
0\end{pmatrix}+\frac{1}{m_{-}}\nabla\eta_{-}=\frac{e}{m_{-}}\nabla\phi,

(1.25)

\displaystyle\Delta\phi=4\pi e\eta_{-}.

(1.26)

Let
\psi_{-}=\nabla\cdot u_{-}
and
\omega_{-}=\nabla^{\perp}\cdot u_{-}
with
\nabla^{\perp}=(-\partial_{y},\partial_{x})^{\top}
. Then the Helmholtz projection operators are defined as

u_{-}=(u^{x}_{-},u^{y}_{-})^{\top}=\nabla\Delta^{-1}\psi_{-}+\nabla^{\perp}\Delta^{-1}\omega_{-}:=\mathbb{Q}[u_{-}]+\mathbb{P}[u_{-}].

(1.27)

The linearized system (
1.24
)-(
1.26
) is rewritten as (in the variables
(\eta_{-},\psi_{-},\omega_{-})
)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\eta_{-}+y\partial_{x}\eta_{-}+\psi_{-}=0,

(1.28)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\psi_{-}+y\partial_{x}\psi_{-}+2\partial_{x}u^{y}_{-}+\frac{1}{m_{-}}\Delta\eta_{-}=\frac{4\pi e^{2}}{m_{-}}\eta_{-},

(1.29)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\omega_{-}+y\partial_{x}\omega_{-}-\psi_{-}=0.

(1.30)

Just as in the ion dynamics case, we take the
x
-average for equations (
1.28
), (
1.29
) and (
1.30
) to obtain the following system

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\eta_{-,a}+\psi_{-,a}=0,

(1.31)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\psi_{-,a}+\frac{1}{m_{-}}\partial_{yy}\eta_{-,a}=\frac{4\pi e^{2}}{m_{-}}\eta_{-,a},

(1.32)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\omega_{-,a}-\psi_{-,a}=0.

(1.33)

Again, combining (
1.31
) with (
1.33
) yields
\partial_{t}\left(\eta_{-,a}+\omega_{-,a}\right)=0
and hence

\eta_{-,a}+\omega_{-,a}=\eta^{in}_{-,a}+\omega^{in}_{-,a},

(1.34)

where
(\eta^{in}_{-,a},\omega^{in}_{-,a})
represents the initial data. According to (

) and (
1.32
), we get

\partial_{tt}\eta_{-,a}-\frac{1}{m_{-}}\partial_{yy}\eta_{-,a}+\frac{4\pi e^{2}}{m_{-}}\eta_{-,a}=0~in~\mathbb{R},

(1.35)

which is the famous Klein-Gordon equation. The fact that at the linearized level (near the constant solution), the electron Euler-Poisson system satisfies the Klein-Gordon equation was found by Guo
[
18
]
and the global irrotational flows in the large to the electron Euler-Poisson system was hence obtained in
\mathbb{R}^{3+1}
. For global well-posedness of the lower dimensional Euler-Poisson system, one may refer to
[
28
,
27
,
26
,
36
]
.

Similar to the ion dynamics case, we supply system (
1.31
)-(
1.33
) with the initial condition

\left(\eta_{-,a},\psi_{-,a},\omega_{-,a}\right)|_{t=0}=\left(\eta^{in}_{-,a},\psi^{in}_{-,a},\omega^{in}_{-,a}\right)=(0,0,0).

(1.36)

From (
1.34
) and the explicit representation formula for the Klein-Gordon equation, we deduce that
\left(\eta_{-,a},\psi_{-,a},\omega_{-,a}\right)=(0,0,0)
is the unique solution to system (
1.31
)-(
1.33
) with initial condition (
1.36
). Based on this fact, for simplicity of notation, we study the dynamics of
(\eta_{-},\psi_{-},\omega_{-})
with
\left(\eta^{in}_{-,a},\psi^{in}_{-,a},\omega^{in}_{-,a}\right)=(0,0,0)
, instead of studying the dynamics for
(\eta_{-}-\eta_{-,a},\psi_{-}-\psi_{-,a},\omega_{-}-\omega_{-,a})
.

The main results for the linear stability of the electron Euler-Poisson system are stated as follows.

Theorem 1.3
.

Suppose that
(\eta^{in}_{-},\omega^{in}_{-})\in H^{1}_{x}H^{2}_{y}
and that
\psi^{in}_{-}\in H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{x}L^{2}_{y}
with
\left(\eta^{in}_{-,a},\psi^{in}_{-,a},\omega^{in}_{-,a}\right)=(0,0,0)
. Let
(\eta_{-},u_{-},\phi)
be a smooth solution for the system (
1.24
)-(
1.26
). Then the following estimates hold

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathbb{P}[u_{-}]^{x}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert\phi(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\sqrt{m_{-}}}{\left\langle t\right\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}}\left(\left\lVert\sqrt{\frac{1}{m_{-}}}\eta^{in}_{-}\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{x}L^{2}_{y}}+\left\lVert\eta^{in}_{-}+\omega^{in}_{-}\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{x}H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{y}}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\frac{1}{\left\langle t\right\rangle}\left\lVert\eta^{in}_{-}+\omega^{in}_{-}\right\rVert_{H^{-1}_{x}H^{1}_{y}}+\frac{\sqrt{m_{-}}}{\left\langle t\right\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}}\left\lVert\psi^{in}_{-}\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{x}H^{-1}_{y}},

(1.37)

2. Notations

The Fourier transform for a function
f(x,y)
is denoted by

\widehat{f}(k,\xi)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\iint_{\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}}e^{-i\left(kx+\xi y\right)}f(x,y)dxdy.

Then

f(x,y)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{i\left(kx+\xi y\right)}\widehat{f}(k,\xi)d\xi.

The anisotropic Sobolev space is defined by

H^{r}_{x}H^{s}_{y}({\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}})=\left\{f:\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{r}_{x}H^{s}_{y}({\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}})}=\sum_{k}\int\left\langle k\right\rangle^{2r}\left\langle\xi\right\rangle^{2s}\left|\widehat{f}\right|^{2}(k,\xi)d\xi<+\infty\right\},

where
\left\langle a\right\rangle=(1+a^{2})^{1/2}
for
a\in\mathbb{R}
. In the same way, the usual Sobolev space is defined as

H^{s}({\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}})=\left\{f:\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{s}({\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}})}=\sum_{k}\int\left\langle k,\xi\right\rangle^{2s}\left|\widehat{f}\right|^{2}(k,\xi)d\xi<+\infty\right\}.

Here
\left\langle a,b\right\rangle=(1+a^{2}+b^{2})^{1/2}
for
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
.

In order to deal with the transport term in system (
1.12
)-(
1.14
) or (
1.28
)-(
1.30
), we introduce a set of coordinate transformations

X=x-yt,~Y=y.

(2.1)

So the differential operators can be represented as

\partial_{x}=\partial_{X},~\partial_{y}=\partial_{Y}-t\partial_{X},~\Delta=:\Delta_{L}=\partial_{XX}+\left(\partial_{Y}-t\partial_{X}\right)^{2}.

(2.2)

We denote the symbol for
-\Delta_{L}
by

\alpha(t,k,\xi)=k^{2}+(\xi-kt)^{2}.

(2.3)

Then the symbol of the operator
2\partial_{X}(\partial_{Y}-t\partial_{X})
is

\partial_{t}\alpha(t,k,\xi)=-2k(\xi-kt).

(2.4)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k}\int\left(\alpha^{-1}\left|\widehat{F^{in}_{+}}\right|^{2}+\frac{m_{+}}{T_{+}\alpha^{1/2}}\left|A^{in}\right|^{2}+\frac{m_{+}}{T_{+}\alpha^{1/2}}\left|\widehat{F^{in}_{+}}\right|^{2}\right)d\xi

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k}\int\left(\frac{\left\langle\xi\right\rangle^{2}}{\left\langle t\right\rangle^{2}\left\langle k\right\rangle^{2}}\left|\widehat{\eta^{in}_{+}}+\widehat{\omega^{in}_{+}}\right|^{2}+\frac{m_{+}\left\langle\xi\right\rangle}{T_{+}\left\langle t\right\rangle\left\langle k\right\rangle}\left|\widehat{\eta^{in}_{+}}+\widehat{\omega^{in}_{+}}\right|^{2}\right)d\xi

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\sum_{k}\int\frac{m_{+}\left\langle\xi\right\rangle}{T_{+}\left\langle t\right\rangle\left\langle k\right\rangle}\left(\frac{T_{+}}{m_{+}}\frac{\left|\widehat{\eta^{in}_{+}}\right|^{2}}{\left(1+\xi^{2}\right)^{1/2}}+\frac{\left|\widehat{\psi^{in}_{+}}\right|^{2}}{\left(1+\xi^{2}\right)^{3/2}}\right)d\xi,

(3.34)

where we have used the fact that
\left\langle t\right\rangle\lesssim\left\langle\xi/k-t\right\rangle\left\langle\xi/k\right\rangle
. Thanks to the definition of the anisotropic Sobolev space, from the above inequality we deduce (
1.21
). Moreover, a similar argument as that of (
1.21
) yields (
1.22
).

Using the Helmholtz decomposition and the Plancherel’s theorem, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathbb{Q}[u_{+}](t)\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}+\frac{T_{+}}{m_{+}}\left\lVert\eta_{+}(t)\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\left(\nabla\Delta^{-1}\psi_{+}\right)(t)\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}+\frac{T_{+}}{m_{+}}\left\lVert\eta_{+}(t)\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\left(\partial_{x}\Delta^{-1}\psi_{+}\right)(t)\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert\left(\partial_{y}\Delta^{-1}\psi_{+}\right)(t)\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}+\frac{T_{+}}{m_{+}}\left\lVert\eta_{+}(t)\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\int\left(\frac{1}{k^{2}+(\xi-kt)^{2}}\left|\widehat{\Psi_{+}}(t)\right|^{2}+\frac{T_{+}}{m_{+}}\left|\widehat{\Pi_{+}}(t)\right|^{2}\right)d\xi.

(3.35)

Note that the fact that
k^{2}+(\xi-kt)^{2}\lesssim\left\langle t\right\rangle^{2}\left\langle k,\xi\right\rangle^{2}
. From (
3.33
) and (
3.35
), it follows that

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathbb{Q}[u_{+}](t)\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}+\frac{T_{+}}{m_{+}}\left\lVert\eta_{+}(t)\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\int\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}\left|\widehat{\Psi_{+}}(t)\right|^{2}+\frac{T_{+}}{m_{+}}\left|\widehat{\Pi_{+}}(t)\right|^{2}\right)d\xi

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\int\alpha^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\left|\alpha^{-\frac{3}{4}}\widehat{\Psi_{+}}(t)\right|^{2}+\left|\sqrt{\frac{T_{+}}{m_{+}}}\alpha^{-\frac{1}{4}}\widehat{\Pi_{+}}(t)\right|^{2}\right)d\xi

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\int\alpha^{\frac{1}{2}}\left|A(t)\right|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\langle t\right\rangle\left(\left\lVert\sqrt{\frac{T_{+}}{m_{+}}}\eta^{in}_{+}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert\psi^{in}_{+}\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{-1}}+\left\lVert\eta^{in}_{+}+\omega^{in}_{+}\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{1}}\right),

(3.36)

which gives the estimate (
1.23
).
∎

Now we prove Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Set

\mathcal{R}\left(t,A^{in},F^{in}_{+}\right)=A^{in}+\int^{t}_{0}S_{L}(0,s)M_{+}(s)\widehat{F^{in}_{+}}ds.

According to (
3.12
), we have

A(t)=S_{L}(t,0)A^{in}+\int^{t}_{0}S_{L}(t,s)M_{+}(s)\widehat{F^{in}_{+}}ds=S_{L}(t,0)\mathcal{R}\left(t,A^{in},F^{in}_{+}\right).

By virtue of Lemma
3.1
, we obtain

|A(t)|\geq C|\mathcal{R}\left(t,A^{in},F^{in}_{+}\right)|.

Using the identity in (
3.36
), we get from the above inequality that

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathbb{Q}[u_{+}](t)\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}+\frac{T_{+}}{m_{+}}\left\lVert\eta_{+}(t)\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\int\alpha^{\frac{1}{2}}\left|A(t)\right|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\gtrsim\sum_{k}\int\alpha^{\frac{1}{2}}|\mathcal{R}\left(t,A^{in},F^{in}_{+}\right)|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\gtrsim\sum_{k}\int\left\langle\xi-kt\right\rangle|\mathcal{R}\left(t,A^{in},F^{in}_{+}\right)|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\gtrsim\sum_{k}\int\frac{\left\langle kt\right\rangle}{\left\langle\xi\right\rangle}|\mathcal{R}\left(t,A^{in},F^{in}_{+}\right)|^{2}d\xi,

where we have used the facts that
\alpha^{\frac{1}{2}}\geq\left\langle\xi-kt\right\rangle
and
\left\langle\xi-kt\right\rangle\left\langle\xi\right\rangle\gtrsim\left\langle kt\right\rangle
. The definition of the anisotropic Sobolev space gives

\left\lVert\mathbb{Q}[u_{+}](t)\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}+\frac{T_{+}}{m_{+}}\left\lVert\eta_{+}(t)\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}\gtrsim\left\langle t\right\rangle\left\lVert\mathcal{R}\left(t,A^{in},F^{in}_{+}\right)\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{y}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

4. Analysis for the electron dynamics

In this section, we consider the dynamics of the electron dynamics system (
1.28
)-(
1.30
). Also using coordinate transformations (
2.1
), we define the functions

\displaystyle\Pi_{-}(t,X,Y)=\eta_{-}(t,X+tY,Y),

(4.1)

\displaystyle\Psi_{-}(t,X,Y)=\psi_{-}(t,X+tY,Y),

(4.2)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{-}(t,X,Y)=\omega_{-}(t,X+tY,Y).

(4.3)

Set
F_{-}(t,X,Y):=\Pi_{-}(t,X,Y)+\Gamma_{-}(t,X,Y)
. Combining (
1.28
) with (
1.30
), we have
\partial_{t}F_{-}=0
, which yields

\Gamma_{-}(t,X,Y)=F^{in}_{-}(X,Y)-\Pi_{-}(t,X,Y),

where
F^{in}_{-}=\eta^{in}_{-}+\omega^{in}_{-}
. According to (
1.27
), we obtain

\displaystyle U^{y}_{-}

\displaystyle=\left(\partial_{Y}-t\partial_{X}\right)\Delta^{-1}_{L}\Psi_{-}+\partial_{X}\Delta^{-1}_{L}\Gamma_{-}

\displaystyle=\left(\partial_{Y}-t\partial_{X}\right)\Delta^{-1}_{L}\Psi_{-}+\partial_{X}\Delta^{-1}_{L}F^{in}_{-}-\partial_{X}\Delta^{-1}_{L}\Pi_{-}.

In consequence, we can rewrite system (
1.28
)-(
1.30
) as

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\Pi_{-}+\Psi_{-}=0,

(4.4)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\Psi_{-}+2\partial_{XX}\Delta^{-1}_{L}F^{in}_{-}+2\partial_{X}\left(\partial_{Y}-t\partial_{X}\right)\Delta^{-1}_{L}\Psi_{-}

\displaystyle-\left(2\partial_{XX}\Delta^{-1}_{L}-\frac{1}{m_{-}}\Delta_{L}+\frac{4\pi e^{2}}{m_{-}}\right)\Pi_{-}=0.

(4.5)

Taking the Fourier transform on system (
4.4
)-(
4.5
), we get the following system

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\widehat{\Pi_{-}}+\widehat{\Psi_{-}}=0,

(4.6)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\widehat{\Psi_{-}}+\frac{2k^{2}}{\alpha}\widehat{F^{in}_{-}}-\frac{\partial_{t}\alpha}{\alpha}\widehat{\Psi_{-}}-\left(\frac{2k^{2}}{\alpha}+\frac{\alpha}{m_{-}}+\frac{4\pi e^{2}}{m_{-}}\right)\widehat{\Pi_{-}}=0.

(4.7)

In order to study system (
4.6
)-(
4.7
), we need to find a suitable symmetrization for this system. Define

B(t)=\left(B_{1}(t),B_{2}(t)\right)^{\top}=\left(\frac{\widehat{\Pi_{-}}(t)}{\sqrt{m_{-}}\alpha^{1/4}},\frac{\widehat{\Psi_{-}}(t)}{\alpha^{3/4}}\right)^{\top}.

(4.8)

By a delicate calculation,
B(t)
satisfy a non-autonomous 2D dynamical system

\begin{cases}\frac{d}{dt}B(t)=L_{-}(t)B(t)+M_{-}(t)\widehat{F^{in}_{-}},\\
B(0)=B^{in},\end{cases}

(4.9)

where

\displaystyle L_{-}(t)=\begin{bmatrix}-\frac{1}{4}\alpha^{-1}\partial_{t}\alpha&-\sqrt{\frac{1}{m_{-}}}\alpha^{1/2}\\
\frac{4\pi e^{2}}{\sqrt{m_{-}}\alpha^{1/2}}+\frac{2k^{2}\sqrt{m_{-}}}{\alpha^{3/2}}+\sqrt{\frac{1}{m_{-}}}\alpha^{1/2}&\frac{1}{4}\alpha^{-1}\partial_{t}\alpha\end{bmatrix},

(4.10)

\displaystyle M_{-}(t)=\left(0,-\frac{2k^{2}}{\alpha^{7/4}}\right)^{\top},~B^{in}=\left(\frac{\widehat{\Pi^{in}_{-}}}{\sqrt{m_{-}}\left(k^{2}+\xi^{2}\right)^{1/4}},\frac{\widehat{\Psi^{in}_{-}}}{\left(k^{2}+\xi^{2}\right)^{3/4}}\right)^{\top}.

(4.11)

By virtue of Duhamel’s formula, the solution
B(t)
to system (
4.9
) is given by

B(t)=S_{L}(t,0)B^{in}+\int^{t}_{0}S_{L}(t,s)M_{-}(s)\widehat{F^{in}_{-}}ds.

(4.12)

Here,
S_{L}
denotes the solution operator, satisfying the group property

S_{L}(t,0)S_{L}(0,s)=S_{L}(t,s)

for any
t,s>0
. In consequence, it suffices to study the homogeneous problem of system (
4.9
).

Lemma 4.1
.

Denote by
B(t)
a solution to system (
4.9
) with
\widehat{F^{in}_{-}}=0
. Let

\mathcal{E}_{-}(t)=\left(\sqrt{\frac{p_{2}}{m_{2}}}|B_{1}|^{2}\right)(t)+2\left(\frac{h_{2}}{\sqrt{m_{2}p_{2}}}\operatorname{Re}\left(B_{1}\bar{B_{2}}\right)\right)(t)+\left(\sqrt{\frac{m_{2}}{p_{2}}}|B_{2}|^{2}\right)(t),

(4.13)

where

\displaystyle h_{2}(t)=\frac{1}{4}\alpha^{-1}\partial_{t}\alpha,~m_{2}(t)=\sqrt{\frac{1}{m_{-}}}\alpha^{1/2},

(4.14)

\displaystyle p_{2}(t)=\frac{4\pi e^{2}}{\sqrt{m_{-}}\alpha^{1/2}}+\frac{2k^{2}\sqrt{m_{-}}}{\alpha^{3/2}}+\sqrt{\frac{1}{m_{-}}}\alpha^{1/2}.

(4.15)

Then there exists positive constants
C_{1}
,
C^{\sigma}_{1}
,
C_{2}
,
C^{\sigma}_{2}
that do not depend on
k
and
\xi
such that

C_{1}\mathcal{E}_{-}(0)\leq\mathcal{E}_{-}(t)\leq C^{\sigma}_{1}\mathcal{E}_{-}(0),

(4.16)

and

C_{2}\left|B^{in}\right|\leq\left|B(t)\right|\leq C^{\sigma}_{2}\left|B^{in}\right|.

(4.17)

Proof.

Define

\displaystyle\lambda_{-}=\sqrt{\frac{p_{2}}{m_{2}}}=\left(1+\frac{4\pi e^{2}}{\alpha}+\frac{2m_{-}k^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}\right)^{1/2},

(4.18)

\displaystyle\gamma_{-}=\sqrt{m_{2}p_{2}}=\left(\frac{4\pi e^{2}}{m_{-}}+\frac{2k^{2}}{\alpha}+\frac{\alpha}{m_{-}}\right)^{1/2}.

(4.19)

By the definition of
\lambda_{-}
, it holds that

1\leq(\lambda_{-})^{2}\leq 1+4\pi e^{2}+2m_{-}.

(4.20)

Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\frac{|h_{2}|}{\gamma_{-}}

\displaystyle=\frac{|\partial_{t}\alpha|}{4\alpha}\left(\frac{4\pi e^{2}}{m_{-}}+\frac{2k^{2}}{\alpha}+\frac{\alpha}{m_{-}}\right)^{-1/2}

\displaystyle=\frac{|\partial_{t}\alpha|}{4\alpha}\left(\frac{m_{-}}{\alpha}\right)^{1/2}\left(1+\frac{4\pi e^{2}}{\alpha}+\frac{2m_{-}k^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}\right)^{-1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|k|}{2\sqrt{\alpha}}\left(\frac{m_{-}}{\alpha}\right)^{1/2}\left(\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2m_{-}k^{2}}\right)^{1/2}=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{4},

(4.21)

where we have used the fact that
|\partial_{t}\alpha|\leq 2|k|\sqrt{\alpha}
.

Setting

\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{-}(t)=\left(\lambda_{-}|B_{1}|^{2}\right)(t)+\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_{-}}|B_{2}|^{2}\right)(t).

Then from (
4.21
) it follows that

\left(1-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{4}\right)\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{-}(t)\leq\mathcal{E}_{-}(t)\leq\left(1+\frac{\sqrt{2}}{4}\right)\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{-}(t).

(4.22)

Obviously, the coerciveness of
\mathcal{E}_{-}(t)
is ensured by the above inequality and (
4.20
).

According to (
4.14
), (
4.15
), (
4.18
) and (
4.19
), system (
4.9
) becomes

\displaystyle\lambda_{-}\frac{d}{dt}B_{1}=-h_{2}\lambda_{-}B_{1}-\gamma_{-}B_{2},

(4.23)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda_{-}}\frac{d}{dt}B_{2}=\gamma_{-}B_{1}+\frac{h_{2}}{\lambda_{-}}B_{2}.

(4.24)

Multiplying equations (
4.23
) and (
4.24
) by
\bar{B_{1}}
and
\bar{B_{2}}
, respectively, we obtain

\frac{\lambda_{-}}{2}\frac{d}{dt}|B_{1}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2\lambda_{-}}\frac{d}{dt}|B_{2}|^{2}=-h_{2}\lambda_{-}|B_{1}|^{2}+\frac{h_{2}}{\lambda_{-}}|B_{2}|^{2}.

(4.25)

Note that

\frac{h_{2}}{\gamma_{-}}\frac{d}{dt}\operatorname{Re}(B_{1}\bar{B_{2}})=h_{2}\lambda_{-}|B_{1}|^{2}-\frac{h_{2}}{\lambda_{-}}|B_{2}|^{2}.

(4.26)

Adding (
4.25
) to (
4.26
), we get

\lambda_{-}\frac{d}{dt}|B_{1}|^{2}+\frac{2h_{2}}{\gamma_{-}}\frac{d}{dt}\operatorname{Re}(B_{1}\bar{B_{2}})+\frac{1}{\lambda_{-}}\frac{d}{dt}|B_{2}|^{2}=0,

which yields

\frac{d\mathcal{E}_{-}}{dt}=\lambda_{-}|B_{1}|^{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\log\lambda_{-}\right)+2\operatorname{Re}(B_{1}\bar{B_{2}})\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{h_{2}}{\gamma_{-}}\right)-\frac{1}{\lambda_{-}}|B_{2}|^{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\log\lambda_{-}\right).

Using Young’s inequality, from (
4.22
) we deduce that

\displaystyle\frac{d\mathcal{E}_{-}}{dt}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\left|\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{h_{2}}{\gamma_{-}}\right)\right|+\left|\frac{d}{dt}\left(\log\lambda_{-}\right)\right|\right)\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{-}

\displaystyle\leq\left(2+\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\right)\left(\left|\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{h_{2}}{\gamma_{-}}\right)\right|+\left|\frac{d}{dt}\left(\log\lambda_{-}\right)\right|\right)\mathcal{E}_{-}.

(4.27)

An argument similar to that of (
4.27
) gives

\frac{d\mathcal{E}_{-}}{dt}\geq-\left(2+\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\right)\left(\left|\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{h_{2}}{\gamma_{-}}\right)\right|+\left|\frac{d}{dt}\left(\log\lambda_{-}\right)\right|\right)\mathcal{E}_{-}.

(4.28)

For the purpose of applying Grönwall’s inequality, we need to estimate the integral terms
\int^{+\infty}_{0}\left|\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{h_{2}}{\gamma_{-}}\right)\right|d\vartheta
and
\int^{+\infty}_{0}\left|\frac{d}{dt}\left(\log\lambda_{-}\right)\right|d\vartheta
. First of all, we deal with the first one. By a direct calculation, we have

\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{h_{2}}{\gamma_{-}}\right)=\frac{\mathcal{M}}{8m_{-}(\gamma_{-})^{3}\alpha^{3}},

where

\mathcal{M}=4m_{-}k^{2}(\gamma_{-})^{2}\alpha^{2}-2m_{-}(\gamma_{-})^{2}\alpha(\partial_{t}\alpha)^{2}-\alpha^{2}(\partial_{t}\alpha)^{2}+2m_{-}k^{2}(\partial_{t}\alpha)^{2}

is a polynomial in time of order 6. Denote by
t_{i}(i=1,2,...,6)
the positive root for
\mathcal{M}
. We set
i_{0}=0
provided that
\mathcal{M}(0)\leq 0
and take
i_{0}=1
for the other cases. Assume that
t_{0}=0
and that
t_{7}=+\infty
. By virtue of (
4.21
), we conclude that

\int^{+\infty}_{0}\left|\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{h_{2}}{\gamma_{-}}\right)\right|d\vartheta=\sum^{7}_{i=1}(-1)^{i+i_{0}}\left(\frac{h_{2}}{\gamma_{-}}(t_{i})-\frac{h_{2}}{\gamma_{-}}(t_{i-1})\right)\leq 2\sum^{7}_{i=0}\frac{|h_{2}|}{\gamma_{-}}(t_{i})\leq 4\sqrt{2}.

(4.29)

For the second integral term, we get from (
4.20
) that

\displaystyle\int^{+\infty}_{0}\left|\frac{d}{dt}\left(\log\lambda_{-}\right)\right|d\vartheta=\frac{1}{2}\int^{\frac{\xi}{k}}_{0}\left|\frac{d}{dt}\left(\log(\lambda_{-})^{2}\right)\right|d\vartheta+\frac{1}{2}\int^{+\infty}_{\frac{\xi}{k}}\left|\frac{d}{dt}\left(\log(\lambda_{-})^{2}\right)\right|d\vartheta

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\leq\frac{1}{2}\left[\left(\log(\lambda_{-})^{2}\right)\left(\frac{\xi}{k}\right)-\left(\log(\lambda_{-})^{2}\right)\left(0\right)\right]+\frac{1}{2}\left[\left(\log(\lambda_{-})^{2}\right)\left(\frac{\xi}{k}\right)-\left(\log(\lambda_{-})^{2}\right)\left(+\infty\right)\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\leq\log\left(1+4\pi e^{2}+2m_{-}\right).

(4.30)

Here, we have used the fact that
\frac{1}{(\lambda_{-})^{2}}\frac{d(\lambda_{-})^{2}}{dt}=(\lambda_{-})^{-2}\alpha^{-3}\left(-4m_{-}k^{2}-4\pi e^{2}\alpha\right)\partial_{t}\alpha
changes sign only in
t=\xi/k
. Applying Grönwall’s inequality to (
4.27
) and (
4.28
), respectively, from (
4.29
) and (
4.30
) we obtain (
4.16
).

Next, it remains to prove (
4.17
). Owing to (
4.16
) and (
4.22
), it can be deduced that

C^{\prime}_{1}\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{-}(0)\leq\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{-}(t)\leq C^{\sigma^{\prime}}_{1}\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{-}(0).

(4.31)

By virtue of (
4.20

\left(1+4\pi e^{2}+2m_{-}\right)^{-1}\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{-}(t)\leq|B(t)|^{2}\leq\left(1+4\pi e^{2}+2m_{-}\right)\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{-}(t).

(4.32)

Combining (
4.31
) with (
4.32
) yields (

∎

\displaystyle|B(t)|

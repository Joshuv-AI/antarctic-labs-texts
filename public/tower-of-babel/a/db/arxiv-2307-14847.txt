Thermal one-point functions: CFT’s with fermions, large d d and large spin
Justin R. David, Srijan Kumar

Abstract
We apply the OPE inversion formula on thermal two-point functions of fermions to obtain thermal one-point function of fermion bi-linears appearing in the corresponding OPE. We primarily focus on the OPE channel which contains the stress tensor of the theory. We apply our formalism to the mean field theory of fermions and verify that the inversion formula reproduces the spectrum as well as their corresponding thermal one-point functions. We then examine the large N N critical Gross-Neveu model in d = 2 ​ k + 1 d=2k+1 dimensions with k k even and at finite temperature. We show that stress tensor evaluated from the inversion formula agrees with that evaluated from the partition function at the critical point. We demonstrate the expectation values of 3 different classes of higher spin currents are all related to each other by numerical constants, spin and the thermal mass. We evaluate the ratio of the thermal expectation values of higher spin currents at the critical point to the Gaussian fixed point or the Stefan-Boltzmann result, both for the large N N critical O ⁡ ( N ) O(N) model and the Gross-Neveu model in odd dimensions. This ratio is always less than one and it approaches unity on increasing the spin with the dimension d d held fixed. The ratio however approaches zero when the dimension d d is increased with the spin held fixed.

Introduction

Given a quantum field theory, it is usually important to understand its behaviour at finite temperature.
That is when one of the directions of the Euclidean theory is taken to be a circle of radius
\beta

This question is particularly relevant when the quantum field theory is a conformal field theory.
Usually critical points of quantum field theories occur at finite temperature.
Furthermore, studying conformal field theories which arise in
AdS/CFT
context implies that one is studying
properties of
AdS

It is possible to use the symmetries of the conformal field theory to constraint conformal field theories on

Under some reasonable assumptions of

Iliesiu:2018fao

This inversion formula
allowed one to obtain the thermal one-point functions for all operators which appear in the
OPE of the given 2 point function.
The inversion formula was applied to fermionic 2-point functions in
Petkou:2018ynm
, however the OPE channel studied was
the scalar channel, that is the spinor indices of the fermionic operators in the 2-point function were contracted.
This channel for instance does not contain the stress tensor of the theory.

In this paper we apply the OPE inversion formula on fermionic 2-point functions and focus on the OPE
channel which contains the stress tensor.
We will see that there are
2
classes of operators that exist in this channel, these operators are schematically of the
form

\displaystyle{\cal O}_{+}[n,l]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{\psi}\gamma_{\mu_{1}}\partial_{\mu_{2}}\cdots\partial_{\mu_{l}}\partial^{2n}\psi,

(1)

\displaystyle{\cal O}_{-}[n,l]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\mu_{1}}\partial_{\mu_{2}}\cdots\partial_{\mu_{l}}\partial^{2n}\psi,

\psi

To isolate the one-point functions of operators belonging to each of these classes we need
to apply the OPE inversion formula to 2 related thermal 2-point functions.
There is a third class of symmetric traceless tensors schematically of the form

{\cal O}_{0}[n,l]=\bar{\psi}\partial_{\mu_{1}}\partial_{\mu_{2}}\cdots\partial_{\mu_{l}}\partial^{2n}\psi.

(2)

These occur in the scalar channel of the OPE expansion of 2 fermionic operators, to isolate the one-point function of
these operators we apply the OPE inversion formula to thermal 2-point functions in which the spinor indices are contracted

Petkou:2018ynm
.

After presenting the general formalism for evaluating thermal one-point functions belonging to the classes

We show that

The gap equation has a real solution for the thermal mass for

We show that the one-point function of the operator

The form of expectation value of

Finally we show that for the large

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{\psi}\partial^{\mu_{1}}\cdots\partial^{\mu_{J}}\partial^{2n}\psi-{\rm Traces},\qquad l=J,

4.1
OPE inversion on
g_{2}(x)

The two-point function of fermions with a thermal mass
m_{\rm th}
and at finite temperature is given by

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{\alpha}(x)\psi_{\beta}^{\dagger}(0)\rangle=\frac{i}{2^{\frac{d-1}{2}}}\sum_{n,\,k_{0}=2\pi(n+\frac{1}{2})}\int\frac{d^{d-1}k}{(2\pi)^{d-1}}\frac{\gamma^{\mu}_{\alpha\beta}k_{\mu}-im_{th}\delta_{\alpha\beta}}{k^{2}+m_{th}^{2}}e^{ikx}.

(110)

Note that this correlator is anti-periodic under the shift
\tau\rightarrow\tau+1
, again we have divided
by the dimension of the Dirac spinor so that traces over
\gamma
matrices gives unity.
It is useful for us to write down the correlator with
\bar{\psi}
and
\psi
inter-changed for the constructions
of
g_{2}(x)
and
g_{3}(x)
. From (
110
) we obtain

\langle\psi^{\dagger}_{\beta}(x)\psi_{\alpha}(0)\rangle=\frac{i}{2^{\frac{d-1}{2}}}\sum_{n,\,k_{0}=2\pi(n+\frac{1}{2})}\int\frac{d^{d-1}k}{(2\pi)^{d-1}}\frac{\gamma^{\mu}_{\alpha\beta}k_{\mu}+im_{th}\delta_{\alpha\beta}}{k^{2}+m_{th}^{2}}e^{ikx}.

(111)

To derive (
111
) from (
110
), we inter-change the fermions, change the dummy variables of integrations and
summations and also use translation invariance.
Using this we can write down the correlator
g_{2}(x)
using the definition in (
7
)

\displaystyle g_{2}(\tau,\vec{x})=\frac{i}{|x|}

\displaystyle\sum_{n,\,k_{0}=2\pi(n+\frac{1}{2})}\int\frac{d^{d-1}k}{(2\pi)^{d-1}}\frac{k_{\mu}x^{\mu}}{k^{2}+m_{th}^{2}}e^{ikx},

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{x^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}}{|x|}\sum_{n,\,k_{0}=2\pi(n+\frac{1}{2})}\int\frac{d^{d-1}k}{(2\pi)^{d-1}}\frac{e^{ikx}}{k^{2}+m_{th}^{2}}.

(112)

To perform the integral, we first use the Poisson re-summation formula to convert the sum over Matsubara frequencies to
sum over images in
\tau
.

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}f\big[(2n+1)\pi\big]=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{n}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}f(\omega)e^{in\omega}.

(113)

Applying the re-summation on the integral in (
4.1
) and performing the resultant integral, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{n,\,k_{0}=2\pi(n+\frac{1}{2})}\int\frac{d^{d-1}k}{(2\pi)^{d-1}}\frac{e^{i\vec{k}.\vec{x}}e^{ik_{0}\tau}}{k_{0}^{2}+\vec{k}^{2}+m_{\rm th}^{2}}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{n}\int\frac{d^{d-1}kd\omega}{(2\pi)^{d}}\frac{e^{i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{x}}e^{i\omega(\tau+n)}}{\omega^{2}+\vec{k}^{2}+m_{\rm th}^{2}},

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{n}(2\pi)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\bigg(\frac{|x^{(n)}|}{m_{\rm th}}\bigg)^{1-\frac{d}{2}}K_{\frac{d}{2}-1}(m_{\rm th}|x^{(n)}|).

(114)

Here
x^{(n)}=(\tau+n,\vec{x})
, choose the configuration given in (
28
) we can express the correlator as
a function of
(z,\bar{z})

\displaystyle g_{2}(z,\bar{z})=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{m+1}\big(\frac{m_{\rm th}}{2\pi}\big)^{\frac{d}{2}}\bigg[-\frac{m}{2}\sqrt{\frac{z}{\bar{z}}}-\frac{m}{2}\sqrt{\frac{\bar{z}}{z}}+\sqrt{z\bar{z}}\bigg]\frac{K_{\frac{d}{2}}\left(m_{\rm th}\sqrt{(m-z)(m-\bar{z})}\right)}{\big(\sqrt{(m-z)(m-\bar{z})}\,\big)^{d/2}}.

One consistency check of this correlator is the following, on taking the
m_{\rm th}\rightarrow 0
limit it is proportional
to the MFT correlator in (
43
) or (
65
) with
\Delta_{\psi}=k

g_{2}(z,\bar{z})|_{{\rm Gross-Neveu},\;m_{\rm th}\rightarrow 0}=-\frac{\Gamma(k+\frac{1}{2})}{2\pi^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}g_{2}(z,\bar{z})|_{{\rm MFT},\;\Delta_{\psi}=k}.

(116)

On comparing the two-point function in (
4.1
),
with the corresponding one for the bosonic
O(N)
model studied in

Iliesiu:2018fao
, we have an insertion of
(-1)^{m}
since we are dealing with fermions.
We also have the factor in the square brackets in addition to the Bessel function.
Examining this factor in the
w
-plane where
w
is defined as (
28

affect the branch cut structure present in the Bessel function together with the

\big(\sqrt{(m-z)(m-\bar{z})}\,\big)^{-d/2}
.
Therefore, the branch cuts in the
w

2.2
and we can proceed to apply the
inversion formula.
Let us first write down the contribution from the discontinuities

\displaystyle\hat{a}_{\rm disc}(\Delta,l)=2K_{l}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d\bar{z}}{\bar{z}}\int_{1}^{\frac{1}{\bar{z}}}\frac{dz}{z}(z-\bar{z})^{2\nu}(z\bar{z})^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\Delta}{2}}F_{l}\Big(\sqrt{\frac{\bar{z}}{z}}\Big){\rm Disc}[g_{2}(z,\bar{z})].

(117)

Here we have substituted
\Delta_{\psi}=\frac{d-1}{2}
and

. At this point we note that we have taken
some input from the perturbative results of
Moshe:2003xn
;

, that the fundamental field does not acquire anomalous dimensions
at large

for the Gross-Neveu model.
It should also be noted that the branch cut for each term in (

) depends on

and the integration range in

depends on

As we have mentioned earlier, the branch cut in the

-plane can be obtained from the relation

{\rm Disc}\left[\frac{K_{k+\frac{1}{2}}(\sqrt{-x})}{(-x)^{\frac{k}{2}+\frac{1}{4}}}\right]=\pi\frac{J_{-\frac{1}{2}-k}(\sqrt{x})}{x^{\frac{k}{2}+\frac{1}{4}}}\theta(x).

(118)

Substituting this relation in (
117
) we obtain

\displaystyle\hat{a}_{\rm disc}(\Delta,l)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\pi K_{l}\bigg(\frac{m_{\rm th}}{2\pi}\bigg)^{\frac{d}{2}}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d\bar{z}}{\bar{z}}\int_{1}^{{\rm max}(m,\frac{1}{\bar{z}})}\frac{dz}{z}(z-\bar{z})^{2\nu}(z\bar{z})^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\Delta}{2}}F_{l}\Big(\sqrt{\frac{\bar{z}}{z}}\Big)

(119)

\displaystyle\times\frac{(-1)^{m+1}J_{-\frac{1}{2}-k}(m_{\rm th}\sqrt{(z-m)(m-\bar{z})})}{\big[(z-m)(m-\bar{z})\big]^{\frac{k}{2}+\frac{1}{4}}}\bigg[-\frac{m}{2}\sqrt{\frac{z}{\bar{z}}}-\frac{m}{2}\sqrt{\frac{\bar{z}}{z}}+\sqrt{z\bar{z}}\bigg].

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}.

Just as before the last line defines the 3 integrals required to obtain

Consider the

\displaystyle I_{1}(m)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi m(-1)^{m}K_{l}\bigg(\frac{m_{\rm th}}{2\pi}\bigg)^{\frac{d}{2}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d\bar{z}}{\bar{z}}\int_{1}^{{\rm max}(m,\frac{1}{\bar{z}})}\frac{dz}{z}\left[(z-\bar{z})^{2\nu}(z\bar{z})^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\Delta}{2}}F_{l}\Big(\sqrt{\frac{\bar{z}}{z}}\Big)\sqrt{\frac{z}{\bar{z}}}\right.

(120)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\left.\times\frac{J_{-\frac{1}{2}-k}(m_{\rm th}\sqrt{(z-m)(m-\bar{z})})}{\big[(z-m)(m-\bar{z})\big]^{\frac{k}{2}+\frac{1}{4}}}\right].

We follow the same procedure as in the application of the inversion formula for the MFT to find the leading
poles.
We first change variables as in (
90
), this leads to

\displaystyle I_{1}(m)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi m^{k+\frac{1}{2}-\Delta}(-1)^{m}\bigg(\frac{m_{\rm th}}{2\pi}\bigg)^{\frac{d}{2}}K_{l}\int_{0}^{1}d\bar{z}\int_{1}^{{\rm max}(\frac{1}{m\sqrt{\bar{z}}},1)}dz\;\left[\bar{z}^{-1-\frac{\Delta}{2}}z^{2k-1-\Delta}(z-1)^{-\frac{k}{2}-\frac{1}{4}}\right.

(121)

\displaystyle\qquad\;\;\left.\times(1-\bar{z})^{2k-1}(1-z\bar{z})^{-\frac{k}{2}-\frac{1}{4}}F_{l}(\sqrt{\bar{z}})J_{-\frac{1}{2}-k}\big(m_{\rm th}m\sqrt{(z-1)(1-z\bar{z})}\big)\right].

Now we can expand in small
\bar{z}
, this decouples the integrals and then we perform the integral term by term in
\bar{z}
.
The leading pole is given by

\displaystyle I_{1}(m)^{(0)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\pi m^{k+\frac{1}{2}-\Delta}(-1)^{m}\bigg(\frac{m_{\rm th}}{2\pi}\bigg)^{k+\frac{1}{2}}K_{l}\frac{1}{-\Delta+l+2k-1}

(122)

\displaystyle\times\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{-k+\frac{1}{2}}(1+y^{2})^{2k-1-\Delta}J_{-\frac{1}{2}-k}\big(m_{\rm th}m\;y\big).

To obtain the above equation, we have also made a change of variables to
y=\sqrt{z-1}
. The superscript denotes the fact
that we are focussing on the leading term in the small
\bar{z}
expansion.
The integral over
y
is known and we obtain

\displaystyle I_{1}^{(0)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{2(-1)^{m}\pi^{\frac{1}{2}-k}2^{-\Delta+k+\frac{1}{2}}m^{\frac{1}{2}-k}m_{\rm th}^{\Delta-k+\frac{1}{2}}K_{l}}{\Gamma(1+\Delta-2k)(-\Delta+l+2k-1)}\;K_{\Delta-k+\frac{1}{2}}(m_{\rm th}m).

(123)

We also need the first sub-leading term in the small
\bar{z}
expansion.
After performing the
\bar{z}
integral, the first sub-leading contribution is given by

\displaystyle I_{1}^{(1)}(m)=4\pi m^{k+\frac{1}{2}-\Delta}(-1)^{m}\bigg(\frac{m_{\rm th}}{2\pi}\bigg)^{k+\frac{1}{2}}K_{l}\frac{1}{-\Delta+l+2k+1}\times{\cal I},

(124)

\displaystyle{\cal I}=\int_{0}^{\infty}dyy^{-k+\frac{1}{2}}(1+y^{2})^{2k-1-\Delta}\left[\Big(-\frac{(2k-1)(2+l)}{1+2k+2l}+(k+\frac{1}{2})(1+y^{2})\Big)J_{-\frac{1}{2}-k}(y)\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\left.+\frac{mm_{\rm th}}{2}y(1+y^{2})J_{\frac{1}{2}-k}(y)\right].

Integrating over
z
we obtain

\displaystyle I_{1}^{(1)}(m)=\frac{2(-1)^{m}\pi^{\frac{1}{2}-k}2^{-\Delta+k+\frac{1}{2}}m^{\frac{1}{2}-k}m_{\rm th}^{\Delta-k+\frac{1}{2}}K_{l}}{(-\Delta+l+2k+1)}\left[-\frac{(2k-1)(2+l)}{1+2k+2l}\frac{K_{\frac{1}{2}+\Delta-k}(mm_{\rm th})}{\Gamma(1+\Delta-2k)}\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\left.+(2k+1)(mm_{\rm th})^{-1}\frac{K_{-\frac{1}{2}+\Delta-k}(mm_{\rm th})}{\Gamma(\Delta-2k)}+\frac{K_{-\frac{3}{2}+\Delta-k}(mm_{\rm th})}{\Gamma(\Delta-2k)}\right].

(125)

Let us evaluate the residue at the pole
\Delta=2k+l-1

3
) we can

\displaystyle\left.a_{{\cal O}_{+}}[n=0,l]\right|_{\rm disc}=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{2(-1)^{m}m^{\frac{1}{2}-k}\pi^{\frac{1}{2}-k}K_{l}m_{\rm th}^{l+k-\frac{1}{2}}}{2^{l+k-\frac{3}{2}}\Gamma(l)}K_{l+k-\frac{1}{2}}(mm_{\rm th}).

(127)

where

,

in (

(129)

and

The

\frac{2\nu}{x}K_{\nu}(x)=-K_{\nu-1}+K_{\nu+1},

(132)

we can simply this to

\displaystyle-I(m)|_{{\hbox{ Res at}}\;\Delta=2k+l+1}

Contribution from the arcs

To complete the evaluation of the one-point function we need to evaluate the contribution from the arc at
infinity.
This is given by the expression in (
86
). Taking the limit
w\rightarrow\infty
in
g_{2}(x)
, it can be seen only the
mode
m=0
contributes, this is due to fact the argument of the Bessel function has a square root, which ensures that
it vanishes on the arc exponentially for
m>0
.
Furthermore due to the integration over the full circle in the
w
plane only
l=0
contributes.
Due to these reasons the contribution from the arcs is given by

\displaystyle\hat{a}_{\rm arc}(\Delta,0)=-4\pi K_{0}\left(\frac{m_{\rm th}}{2\pi}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}}\int_{0}^{1}dr\frac{K_{k+\frac{1}{2}}(m_{\rm th}r)}{r^{\Delta-k+\frac{1}{2}}}.

(136)

To obtain the location of the pole in the
\Delta
plane we can push the upper limit of the integral to
\infty
. This does not change either the location of the poles nor their residues.
This results in the following

\hat{a}_{\rm arc}(\Delta,0)=-\frac{1}{4\pi^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\big(\frac{m_{\rm th}}{2}\big)^{\Delta}\Gamma\Big(-\frac{\Delta}{2}\Big)\Gamma\Big(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\Delta}{2}+k\Big).

(137)

6
Conclusions

In this paper we have used the OPE inversion formula on thermal two-point functions of fermions in the
channel which contains the stress tensor. The OPE inversion formula was applied to the
MFT of fermions and the large
N
critical Gross-Neveu model.
We studied the properties of the resulting thermal one-point functions.
The inversion formula made it easy to study the behaviour of the one-point functions at large
spin as well as arbitrary dimensions.

There are other theories where the OPE inversion formula could be applied.
One such class of theories are the large
N
Chern-Simons matter theories
Giombi:2011kc
;
Aharony:2011jz
.
These models are connected to the Gross-Neveu or the
O(N)
vector model.
It would be interesting to obtain thermal one-point functions in these models and study their
spin dependence and also see how the boson-fermion duality exhibited by these models are
reflected in the one-point functions.
Thermal correlators in these models are known in momentum space

Giombi:2011kc
;
Jain:2012qi
;
Aharony:2012ns
;
Gur-Ari:2016xff
;
Ghosh:2019sqf
;
Mishra:2020wos
, so perhaps one way
of proceeding in these models is to derive a OPE inversion formula directly in momentum space.

One of our motivations to study thermal one-point functions in detail was that
one-point functions of conformal primaries evaluated in AdS black holes
can be used to probe the interior geometry of black holes
Grinberg:2020fdj
;
Rodriguez-Gomez:2021pfh
;
McInnes:2022pig
;
Georgiou:2022ekc
;
Berenstein:2022nlj
;
David:2022nfn
.
The time to the singularity in the interior could be obtained as a phase factor by suitable
analytic continuation of the conformal dimension.
However the one-point functions, even that of the fermionic MFT studied here
or the bosonic one in
Iliesiu:2018fao
, do not exhibit this feature.
It will be interesting to see if the application of the OPE inversion formula to the
holographic two-point functions
evaluated in
Dodelson:2022yvn
;
Bhatta:2022wga
;
Dodelson:2023vrw

can be used to show that holographic one-point functions contain
information about the interior geometry of the black hole.

In this paper we studied one-point functions in the geometry
S^{1}\times R^{d-1}
,
other geometries which are relevant to holography and evaluation of entanglement entropy
are hyperbolic cylinders or
the
S^{1}\times AdS_{d-1}
geometry.
Black holes with hyperbolic horizons are dual to conformal field theories on this background.
It should be possible to obtain an OPE inversion formula for field theories on such backgrounds.
Recently a proposal to write two-point functions on such curved backgrounds have been given in

Parisini:2022wkb
. It would be interesting to use the OPE inversion formula to these two-point functions and
study the properties of the resulting one-points functions in these geometries.

Appendix A
Gross-Neveu model: partition function, gap equation

In this appendix we evaluate the partition function of the critical Gross-Neveu model at large
N
.
We obtain the gap equation as the saddle point equation at large
N
and then
evaluate its stress tensor.
We begin with the Lagrangian of
N
massless Dirac fermions
\psi^{a},a=1,2,\cdots N
transforming in the
fundamental of
U(N)
along with the 4-fermi interaction.

S=\int d^{2k+1}x\left[\bar{\psi}(i\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu})\psi+\frac{\lambda}{N}(\bar{\psi}\psi)^{2}\right].

(170)

Since we are in Euclidean space
\bar{\psi}=\psi^{\dagger}
and the
\gamma
matrices obey

\{\gamma^{\mu},\gamma^{\nu}\}=2\delta^{\mu\nu}.

(171)

The partition function of the theory
S^{1}\times R^{2k}
is given by

\tilde{Z}=\int{\cal D}\bar{\psi}{\cal D}\psi e^{-S(\psi,\bar{\psi})}.

(172)

We first linearise the theory using the Hubbard-Stratonovich transformation

\displaystyle\tilde{Z}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int{\cal D}\bar{\psi}{\cal D}\psi\exp\left[{-\int_{0}^{\beta}d\tau d^{2k}x\Big(i\bar{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\psi+\frac{\lambda}{N}(\bar{\psi}\psi)^{2}\Big)}\right],

(173)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int{\cal D}\bar{\psi}{\cal D}\psi{\cal D}\zeta\left[-\int_{0}^{\beta}d\tau d^{2k}x\Big(i\bar{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\psi+\frac{\zeta^{2}N}{\lambda}+i\zeta(\bar{\psi}\psi)\Big)\right].

In the second line we have absorbed the normalization of the Gaussian integral over
\zeta
into the measure.
We can separate the zero mode of
\zeta
and the non-zero modes and write the partition function

\displaystyle\zeta=\tilde{\zeta}+\zeta_{0}.

(174)

Here
\zeta_{0}
is the zero mode, substituting for
\zeta
we can write the partition function as

\displaystyle\tilde{Z}=\int d\zeta_{0}{\cal D}\bar{\psi}{\cal D}\psi\left[\exp\Big(-\frac{\zeta_{0}^{2}N\beta V_{2k}}{4\lambda}\Big)\exp(-S_{0}-S_{I})\right],

(175)

\displaystyle S_{0}=\int d\tau d^{2k}x\Big[i\bar{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\psi+i\zeta_{0}\bar{\psi}\psi+\frac{\tilde{\zeta}^{2}N}{4\lambda}\Big],\qquad\qquad S_{I}=\int d\tau d^{2k}x\;i\tilde{\zeta}\bar{\psi}\psi.

We can neglect
S_{I}
in the leading large
N
limit, observe that on canonically
normalising the quadratic term in
\tilde{\zeta}
, the interaction
S_{I}
acquires a factor of
\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}
.
After performing the Gaussian integration in
\tilde{\zeta}
, we are left with

\displaystyle\tilde{Z}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d\zeta_{0}\exp\left[-\beta V_{2k}N\Big(\frac{\zeta_{0}^{2}}{4\lambda}-\frac{1}{\beta}\log Z(\zeta_{0})\Big)\right],

(176)

where

\displaystyle\log Z(\zeta_{0})=2^{k-1}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\int\frac{d^{2k}p}{(2\pi)^{2k}}\log\left[\frac{4\pi^{2}(n+\frac{1}{2})^{2}}{\beta^{2}}+\vec{p}^{2}+\zeta_{0}^{2}\right].

(177)

The
2^{k-1}
factor arises from the fact that the Dirac operator is a
2^{k}\times 2^{k}
dimensional matrix.
After evaluating the Matsubara sum, we obtain

\displaystyle\log Z(\zeta_{0})=\frac{2^{k-1}}{\beta^{2k}}\int\frac{d^{2k}p}{(2\pi)^{2k}}\left[\sqrt{\vec{p}^{2}+\zeta_{0}^{2}\beta}+2\log\Big(1+e^{-\sqrt{\vec{p}^{2}+\zeta_{0}^{2}\beta^{2}}}\big)\right].

(178)

To integrate the first term in the square bracket we resort to the analytical continuation of the
integral

\int_{0}^{\infty}dx\;\frac{x^{2k-1}}{(x^{2}+1)^{a}}=\frac{\Gamma(a-k)\Gamma(k)}{2\Gamma(a)}.

(179)

The integral involving the second term in the square bracket of (
178
) is convergent and after some
straightforward manipulations can be written in terms of Polylogarithms.
This leads us to

\displaystyle\log Z(\zeta_{0})=-\frac{m^{2k+1}\beta}{\pi^{k}2^{k+2}}\left[\sum_{n=0}^{k}\frac{(k-n+1)_{2n}(\zeta_{0}\beta)^{-k-n-1}{\rm Li}_{k+n+1}\left(-e^{-\zeta_{0}\beta}\right)}{2^{n-k-2}n!}+\frac{\Gamma\left(-k-\frac{1}{2}\right)}{\sqrt{\pi}}\right].

We can obtain the partition function
\tilde{Z}
in (
176
) by using the saddle point approximation to perform the integral over

\zeta_{0}
. The saddle point
\zeta_{0}^{*}=m_{\rm th}
is determined by the equation

m_{\rm th}=2\lambda\frac{1}{\beta}\frac{\partial}{\partial m_{\rm th}}Z(m_{\rm th}).

(181)

For the critical Gross-Neveu model we take the large
\lambda
limit. To the leading order in the large
\lambda

expansion, the
saddle point equation reduces to

\frac{\partial}{\partial m_{\rm th}}Z(m_{\rm th})=0.

(182)

This results in the following gap equation for the critical value
m_{\rm th}
.

\displaystyle 2(2m_{\rm th}\beta)^{k}\sum_{n=0}^{k-1}\frac{(k-n)_{2n}}{(2m_{\rm th}\beta)^{n}n!}\text{Li}_{k+n}(-e^{-m_{\rm th}\beta})+\frac{(m_{\rm th}\beta)^{2k}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\Gamma\big(\frac{1}{2}-k\big)=0.

(183)

We can evaluate the stress tensor at the critical point from the partition function by

T_{00}=-\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}\log Z(m_{\rm th}).

(184)

The above expression yields the energy density divided by the number of fermions
N
.
Performing this differentiation on the partition function given in (
A
), we obtain

\displaystyle T_{00}

\displaystyle=-\frac{m_{\rm th}^{2k+1}\Gamma\left(-k-\frac{1}{2}\right)}{2^{k+2}\pi^{k+\frac{1}{2}}}

(185)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\frac{m_{\rm th}^{k+1}}{\pi^{k}\beta^{k}}\sum_{n=0}^{k+1}\frac{\left[(k+n)^{2}+(k-n)\right](k-n+2)_{2n-2}}{(2m_{\rm th}\beta)^{n}n!}\text{Li}_{k+n}\big(-e^{-m_{\rm th}\beta}\big).

Note that as expected for the critical theory, this energy density can be written as

T_{00}=T^{d}\,H(m_{\rm th}\beta).

(186)

where
H
is a function of

m_{\rm th}\beta
, the dimensionless number which is the root of the gap equation (
183
).

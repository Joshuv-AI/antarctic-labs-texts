Abstract
. In this paper we generalize and study a notion of (unipotent) nearby cycles over a higher dimensional base based on Beĭlinson’s description of unipotent nearby cycles, following an idea of Gaitsgory. This generalization, in the setting of affine Grassmannians, is required in recent work of Bezrukavnikov–Braverman–Finkelberg–Kazhdan [ 7 ] .



1.
Introduction

1.1.

Nearby cycles form a crucial ingredient in the local geometric Langlands program, in particular in Gaitsgory’s construction of central (perverse) sheaves on the affine flag variety of a reductive algebraic group
[
8
]
. In order to prove a certain technical property of this construction required in work of Bezrukavnikov
[
6
]
, Gaitsgory has introduced in
[
9
]
a somewhat ad-hoc construction of “nearby cycles along a 2-dimensional base.”
In this paper we elaborate on this idea and explain how to define much more general “nearby cycles” functors over any finite-dimensional affine space. This generalization is used in recent work of Bezrukavnikov–Braverman–Finkelberg–Kazhdan on local L-factors, see
[
7
]
.

1.2.
Beĭlinson’s unipotent nearby cycles functor

The starting point of this construction is Beĭlinson’s description of the unipotent nearby cycles functor using local systems on
\mathbb{A}^{1}\smallsetminus\{0\}
associated with unipotent Jordan blocks. More specifically, fix a base field
\mathbb{F}
, and consider a scheme
X
of finite type over
\mathbb{A}^{1}=\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\mathbb{F}}
, with structure morphism
f:X\to\mathbb{A}^{1}
. Fix also a field
\Bbbk
of coefficients (of characteristic invertible in
\mathbb{F}
), and let
\mathscr{F}
be a perverse sheaf on
f^{-1}(\mathbb{A}^{1}\smallsetminus\{0\})
. Then Beĭlinson’s construction provides, for any integer
a\geq 1
, a complex
\Psi_{f,a}(\mathscr{F})
on
f^{-1}(0)
concentrated in perverse degrees
-1
and
0
, and morphisms
\Psi_{f,a}(\mathscr{F})\to\Psi_{f,b}(\mathscr{F})
if
a\leq b
. The main observation is that these complexes “stabilize” to the unipotent nearby cycles
\Psi^{\mathrm{un}}_{f}(\mathscr{F})
in the sense that:

•

if
a\gg 0
, for any
b\geq a
the morphism
{}^{p}\!\mathscr{H}^{-1}(\Psi_{f,a}(\mathscr{F}))\to{}^{p}\!\mathscr{H}^{-1}(\Psi_{f,b}(\mathscr{F}))
is an isomorphism, and both objects identify with
\Psi^{\mathrm{un}}_{f}(\mathscr{F})
;

•

for any
a
,
if
b\gg a
the morphism
{}^{p}\!\mathscr{H}^{0}(\Psi_{f,a}(\mathscr{F}))\to{}^{p}\!\mathscr{H}^{0}(\Psi_{f,b}(\mathscr{F}))
vanishes.

1.3.
Higher dimensional version

Gaitsgory’s construction in
[
9
]
is based on the same idea, but now for a scheme
X
(of finite type) over
\mathbb{A}^{2}
, and produces complexes indexed by a pair of nonnegative integers. In this generality, one cannot expect the same stabilization phenomenon as above for all perverse sheaves; but Gaitsgory simply defines his 2-dimensional nearby cycles functor using a homotopy colimit of these complexes. In general, this construction forces one to leave the constructible derived category; to avoid this problem, in our account of Gaitsgory’s construction in
[
2
]
we proposed instead to restrict to complexes such that a stabilization property as above occurs. So, for us the
2
-dimensional nearby cycles functor is only “partially defined.”
The starting point of this paper is the observation that this definition can be phrased so that it makes sense for a scheme
X
(of finite type) over any affine space
\mathbb{A}^{d}
.

1.4.
Composition of higher nearby cycles functors

The application of nearby cycles over a 2-dimensional base in
[
9
]
uses a comparison with two related operations: (a) compute the nearby cycles of the given perverse sheaf successively along the 2 factors in
\mathbb{A}^{2}
; (b) restrict the perverse sheaf to the preimage of the diagonal copy of
\mathbb{A}^{1}
, and then compute the nearby cycles of (a shift of) this complex. Gaitsgory shows that his
2
-dimensional nearby cycles complex maps to each of the complexes obtained in (a) or (b), and that these maps are isomorphisms in the specific setting required in
[
6
]
. In
[
7
]
the authors consider (in a related setting) a variant of (a) where one considers a complex over
\mathbb{A}^{d}
and computes nearby cycles along each
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-factor successively, and they assert that the result does not depend on the choice of order on the coordinates. To justify this assertion, here we introduce the (partially defined)
d
-dimensional nearby cycles functor, and show that, in the setting of
[
7
]
, each of the complexes identifies canonically with the image of the inital complex under our functor.

To justify this fact, we interpret nearby cycles along each factor as the case
d=1
of our construction, and show roughly that a composition of
d
- and
e
-dimensional nearby cycles functors receives a canonical map from a
(d+e)
-dimensional nearby cycles functor, which in the setting of
[
7
]
is an isomorphism. In fact, in the body of the paper we consider a more general construction of higher nearby cycles and their compositions, which also covers the construction in (b) above, and thereby (in our opinion) clarifies the general picture. See Definition
3.1
for the main definition, and (
3.9
) for the construction involving the composition of such functors.

1.5.
Compatibilities

In full generality, it is not reasonable to expect that the morphism in (
3.9
) is always an isomorphism. As in
[
9
]
, what we show here is that the constructions of higher nearby cycles and of the morphisms considered above are compatible with smooth pullback and proper pushforward in an appropriate sense (see Lemmas
3.13
,
3.14
,
3.17
and
3.18
for precise statements), and then study a product-type situation (see §
3.9
). This is sufficient to show that the
d
-dimensional nearby cycles are well defined in the setting of
[
7
]
, and that their formation is compatible with composition, which amounts to the statement these authors require. See Theorem
4.2
and §
4.7
for precise statements.

1.6.
Further comments

There exists a general theory of nearby and vanishing cycles over general bases, which is much more elaborate than the version we consider here; see
[
11
]
for a brief account. In the companion paper
[
16
]
, A. Salmon explains the relationship between the latter theory and ours.

As explained above, the construction considered here is an extension of our study in
[
2
, §9.4]
. There is one important difference in our treatment though: in
[
2
]
we introduced a general condition of “iterated cleanness” implying that the
2
-dimensional nearby cycles complex is well defined, and showed that this condition is satisfied in the setting at hand. It is not obvious (to us) how to extend this condition over a higher-dimensional base; here we bypass this question by proving a compatibility statement with proper pushforward which is stronger than its counterpart in
[
2
]
(compare Lemma
3.14
and
[
2
, Proposition 9.4.2]
) and allows us to reduce the question to the product-type setting.

In this paper we work in the setting of étale sheaves on schemes over fields, in order to meet the setting of
[
7
]
. However all our statements have obvious variants for “usual” sheaves on complex algebraic varieties endowed with their analytic topology (and coefficients in a field), and all our proofs apply in both settings.

After we completed a preliminary version of this paper, it was pointed out to us that a special case of our construction had already been introduced by A. Salmon, with applications to the cohomology of shtukas; see
[
15
]
and Example
3.11
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Pointed maps and associated linear morphisms

We fix a base field
\mathbb{F}
. Below, by a “scheme” we will mean an
\mathbb{F}
-scheme of finite type.
If
P
is a finite set, we may consider the affine space

\mathbb{A}^{P}=\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{F}[X_{p}:p\in P])

with coordinates indexed by
P
. The
generic part
of this affine space, denoted by
\mathbb{A}^{P}_{\eta}
, is the open subscheme where no coordinates vanish:

\mathbb{A}^{P}_{\eta}=\{(x_{p})_{p\in P}\mid\text{$x_{p}\neq 0$ for all $p$}\}.

(In case
P=\varnothing
, we interpret these definitions as
\mathbb{A}^{\varnothing}=\mathbb{A}^{\varnothing}_{\eta}=\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{F})
.)

For any finite set
P
we
let
P_{*}
denote the disjoint union
P\amalg\{*\}
, where
*
denotes a new element. For finite sets
P
and
Q
, a
pointed map

\alpha:P_{*}\to Q_{*}

is a function that satisfies
\alpha(*)=*
. If
\alpha:P_{*}\to Q_{*}
is a pointed map, there is an induced linear map of affine spaces

\bar{\alpha}:\mathbb{A}^{Q}\to\mathbb{A}^{P}

given by

\bar{\alpha}((x_{q})_{q\in Q})=(y_{p})_{p\in P}\qquad\text{where}\qquad y_{p}=\begin{cases}x_{\alpha(p)}&\text{if $\alpha(p)\in Q$,}\\
0&\text{if $\alpha(p)=*$.}\end{cases}

We also set

\mathbb{A}^{P}_{\eta,\alpha}=\{(x_{p})_{p\in P}\in\mathbb{A}^{P}\mid x_{p}\neq 0\text{ if $\alpha(p)\neq*$}\}
; then the restriction of
\bar{\alpha}
to
\mathbb{A}^{Q}_{\eta}
factors through a morphism
\bar{\alpha}_{\eta}:\mathbb{A}^{Q}_{\eta}\to\mathbb{A}^{P}_{\eta,\alpha}
.

Remark 2.1
.

From the definition we see that
\bar{\alpha}
is injective if
\alpha
is surjective, and surjective if
\alpha
is injective. For arbitrary
\alpha
, one can decompose the situation into a combination of these two settings as follows: set
R=\alpha(P)\cap Q
. Then
\alpha
decomposes in the obvious way as a composition of pointed maps

P_{*}\xrightarrow{\alpha_{1}}R_{*}\xrightarrow{\alpha_{2}}Q_{*},

and we have
\bar{\alpha}=\bar{\alpha}_{1}\circ\bar{\alpha}_{2}
where
\bar{\alpha}_{1}
is injective and
\bar{\alpha}_{2}
is surjective.

2.2.
Scheme morphisms associated with pointed maps

Now suppose we have a scheme
X
equipped with a map

f:X\to\mathbb{A}^{P}
.
The
generic part
of
X
is defined by

X_{\eta}=X\times_{\mathbb{A}^{P}}\mathbb{A}^{P}_{\eta}
;
the natural morphism
X_{\eta}\to\mathbb{A}^{P}_{\eta}
will be denoted
f_{\eta}
, and the embedding
X_{\eta}\to X
will be denoted
\mathbf{j}_{X}
(or
\mathbf{j}
when no confusion is likely).

If
\alpha:P_{*}\to Q_{*}
is a pointed map, then one can consider the schemes

X^{\alpha}:=X\times_{\mathbb{A}^{P}}\mathbb{A}^{Q}\qquad\text{and}\qquad X^{\alpha}_{\eta}:=X\times_{\mathbb{A}^{P}}\mathbb{A}^{Q}_{\eta}

where the fiber products are taken with respect to
\bar{\alpha}:\mathbb{A}^{Q}\to\mathbb{A}^{P}
and its restriction to
\mathbb{A}^{Q}_{\eta}
. The natural morphism
X^{\alpha}\to X
will be denoted
\mathbf{i}^{\prime}_{X,\alpha}
(or
\mathbf{i}^{\prime}_{\alpha}
), its restriction to
X^{\alpha}_{\eta}
will be denoted
\mathbf{i}_{X,\alpha}
(or
\mathbf{i}_{\alpha}
) and we will denote by

f^{\alpha}:X^{\alpha}\to\mathbb{A}^{Q}

the natural projection morphism. By Remark
2.1
,
\mathbf{i}^{\prime}_{\alpha}
is a closed immersion if
\alpha
is surjective, and is smooth and surjective if
\alpha
is injective; moreover we have
\mathbf{i}_{\alpha}=\mathbf{i}^{\prime}_{\alpha}\circ\mathbf{j}_{X^{\alpha}}
. Note also that if
\alpha^{-1}(*)=\{*\}
then
\mathbf{i}_{\alpha}
factors through a morphism

\mathbf{i}_{\alpha}^{\prime\prime}:X^{\alpha}_{\eta}\to X_{\eta}.

2.3.
Jordan block local systems

Let
\ell
be a prime different from the characteristic of
\mathbb{F}
, and let
\Bbbk
be a topological field of one of the following two forms:

•

an algebraic extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
, equipped with the
\ell
-adic topology;

•

an algebraic extension of
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
, equipped with the discrete topology.

Our goal in the rest of this section is to describe the construction of a family of indecomposable
\Bbbk
-local systems on
\mathbb{A}^{1}\smallsetminus\{0\}
on which a generator of the geometric fundamental group acts by a unipotent Jordan block.

This construction is certainly well known when
\Bbbk
has characteristic
0
(see, for instance,
[
4
,
13
]
), and it is relatively easy when
\mathbb{F}
is separably closed (so that there is no Galois action to consider). It is probably known in the generality we consider here, but as we could not find a suitable reference, we include the details. The construction uses identities involving some polynomials with rational coefficients, which are discussed in the next subsection.

2.4.
\mathbb{Z}
-closed polynomials and binomial identities

Let us say that a polynomial
f(x)\in\mathbb{Q}[x]
is
\mathbb{Z}
-closed
if it has the property that for all nonnegative integers
n\geq 0
, we have
f(n)\in\mathbb{Z}
. Of course, any polynomial
f\in\mathbb{Q}[x]
determines a continuous (for the
\ell
-adic topology) function
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}\to\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
. Because the nonnegative integers
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
are dense in
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
, if
f
is
\mathbb{Z}
-closed, then it restricts to a function
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
. Furthermore, with
\Bbbk
as above, any
\mathbb{Z}
-closed polynomial
f\in\mathbb{Q}[x]
determines a continuous function
f:\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}\to\Bbbk
.

Any polynomial with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}
is obviously
\mathbb{Z}
-closed, but there are others: for instance, the polynomials
\delta_{0},\delta_{1},\ldots
given by

\delta_{r}(x)=\binom{x}{r}=\frac{x(x-1)\cdots(x-r+1)}{r!}.

(When
r=0
, this expression is interpreted as
1
.)
For integers
r,s\geq 0
, these polynomials satisfy

(2.1)

\delta_{r}(x)\delta_{s}(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{\min\{r,s\}}\frac{(r+s-i)!}{(r-i)!(s-i)!i!}\delta_{r+s-i}(x).

To prove this, observe that if we replace
x
by a nonnegative integer
n
, the left-hand side is the coefficient of
u^{r}v^{s}
in
(1+u)^{n}(1+v)^{n}
, which can also be written as

(1+u+v+uv)^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(u+v+uv)^{k}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g,h,i\geq 0\\
g+h+i=k\end{subarray}}\frac{k!}{g!h!i!}u^{g+i}v^{h+i}.

The coefficient of
u^{r}v^{s}
is found by taking those terms where
g=r-i
,
h=s-i
, and
k=r+s-i
. This yields the right-hand side of (
2.1
).

Next, consider the two-variable polynomial
\delta_{r}(tx)=\binom{tx}{r}\in\mathbb{Q}[x,t]
. Since
\delta_{0},\delta_{1},\ldots
form a
\mathbb{Q}
-basis for
\mathbb{Q}[x]
, there is a unique expression of the form

(2.2)

\delta_{r}(tx)=\sum_{j=0}^{r}c_{r}^{j}(t)\delta_{j}(x),

for some polynomials
c_{r}^{j}(t)\in\mathbb{Q}[t]
. Comparing the coefficients of
x^{r}
and evaluating at
x=0
, we see that

(2.3)

c_{r}^{r}(t)=t^{r}\in\mathbb{Z}[t]\qquad\text{and}\qquad c_{r}^{0}(t)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $r=0$,}\\
0&\text{if $r>0$.}\end{cases}

In particular, these polynomials are
\mathbb{Z}
-closed.

We claim that the
c_{r}^{j}
are in fact
\mathbb{Z}
-closed for all
j
, i.e., that
c_{r}^{j}(m)\in\mathbb{Z}
for integers
m\geq 0
. We prove this by induction on
m
: if
r>0
we obviously have
c_{r}^{j}(0)=0
, and for
m>0
, by Vandermonde’s identity, we have

\binom{mx}{r}=\sum_{g=0}^{r}\binom{(m-1)x}{g}\binom{x}{r-g}=\sum_{g=0}^{r}\sum_{h=0}^{g}c_{g}^{h}(m-1)\delta_{h}(x)\delta_{r-g}(x).

Expanding the right-hand side using (
2.1
) and comparing coefficients of
\delta_{j}
, we find that

c_{r}^{j}(m)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq h\leq g\leq r\\
0\leq r-g+h-j\leq\min\{h,r-g\}\end{subarray}}c_{g}^{h}(m-1)\frac{j!}{(j+g-r)!(j-h)!(r-g+h-j)!}\in\mathbb{Z}.

2.5.
Functions on the Tate module

Let
\overline{\mathbb{F}}
be a separable closure of
\mathbb{F}
, and let
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1)
be the Tate module of
\overline{\mathbb{F}}{}^{\times}
, i.e., the inverse limit of the groups of
\ell^{n}
-th roots of unity in
\overline{\mathbb{F}}
. This is naturally a free
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
-module of rank
1
. Let

\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1),\Bbbk)=\text{set of continuous functions $\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1)\to\Bbbk$}.

Here, “continuous” should be understood with respect to the profinite topology on
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1)
and the topology on
\Bbbk
indicated in §
2.3
. This set is a ring under pointwise multiplication.

The group
\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}|\mathbb{F})
acts on
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1)
by the cyclotomic character
\chi_{\ell}:\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}|\mathbb{F})\to\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}^{\times}
. There is an induced action of the semidirect product
\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}|\mathbb{F})\ltimes\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1)
on
\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1),\Bbbk)
, given by

((\gamma,g)\cdot f)(h)=f(\chi_{\ell}(\gamma)^{-1}(h-g))

for
\gamma\in\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}|\mathbb{F})
and
g,h\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1)
. This is an action by ring automorphisms.

To write down some explicit elements in
\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1),\Bbbk)
, let us choose a generator
g
for
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1)
as a
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
-module. Thus every element in
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1)
can be written uniquely as
ng
for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
. Then for
r\geq 0
there is a continuous function

\varphi^{g}_{r}:\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1)\to\Bbbk\qquad\text{given by}\qquad\varphi^{g}_{r}(ng)=(-1)^{r}\delta_{r}(n).

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
g\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1)
be an element that generates
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1)
as a
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
-module, and for
a\geq 0
let

L_{a}=\mathrm{span}_{\Bbbk}\{\varphi^{g}_{0},\varphi^{g}_{1},\ldots,\varphi^{g}_{a-1}\}\subset\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1),\Bbbk).

(1)

The subspace
L_{a}\subset\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1),\Bbbk)
is independent of the choice of
g
.

(2)

The subspace
L_{a}
is stable under the action of
\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}|\mathbb{F})\ltimes\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1)
.

(3)

We have
\dim L_{a}=a
.

(4)

Any generator of
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1)
acts on
L_{a}
by a unipotent Jordan block.

(5)

For any integer
a\geq 1
, there is a canonical short exact of
\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}|\mathbb{F})\ltimes\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1)
-modules

0\to L_{a-1}\to L_{a}\to\Bbbk(-a+1)\to 0.

(6)

For any two integers
a,b\geq 1
, there is a canonical map of
\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}|\mathbb{F})\ltimes\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1)
-modules

L_{a}\otimes_{\Bbbk}L_{b}\to L_{a+b-1}

whose image contains
L_{\max\{a,b\}}
.

Proof.

(
1
) If
g^{\prime}
is another generator for
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1)
as a
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
-module, then we have
g^{\prime}=ug
for some
u\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}^{\times}
. It follows from (
2.2
) that

\varphi_{r}^{g^{\prime}}=\sum_{j=0}^{r}(-1)^{j+r}c_{r}^{j}(u)\varphi_{j}^{g}.

This shows that the span of
\varphi_{0}^{g^{\prime}},\ldots,\varphi_{a-1}^{g^{\prime}}
is contained in the span of
\varphi_{0}^{g},\ldots,\varphi_{a-1}^{g}
. By symmetry the opposite containment also holds, so
L_{a}
is independent of the choice of
g
.

(
2
)
We have

((-g)\cdot\varphi_{r}^{g})(ng)=\varphi_{r}^{g}((n+1)g)=(-1)^{r}\binom{n+1}{r}=(-1)^{r}\binom{n}{r-1}+(-1)^{r}\binom{n}{r}

or, in other words,

(2.4)

(-g)\cdot\varphi_{r}^{g}=\begin{cases}\varphi_{0}^{g}&\text{if $r=0$,}\\
\varphi_{r}^{g}-\varphi_{r-1}^{g}&\text{if $r>0$.}\end{cases}

This formula shows that
(-g)
preserves
L_{a}
and acts by a unipotent operator. It follows that the cyclic subgroup
\mathbb{Z}g\subset\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1)
preserves
L_{a}
as well. Since
\mathbb{Z}g
is dense in
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1)
, continuity considerations show that
L_{a}
is preserved by all of
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1)
.

Similarly, for
\gamma\in\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}|\mathbb{F})
, we have

(2.5)

\gamma\cdot\varphi_{r}^{g}=\varphi_{r}^{\chi_{\ell}(\gamma)^{-1}g}=\sum_{j=0}^{r}(-1)^{j+r}c_{r}^{j}(\chi_{\ell}(\gamma)^{-1})\varphi_{j}^{g}.

Thus,
L_{a}
is stable under
\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}|\mathbb{F})
, and hence under
\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}|\mathbb{F})\ltimes\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1)
.

(
3
)
We wish to show that the functions
\varphi_{0}^{g},\varphi_{1}^{g},\ldots
are linearly independent. If not, find some nontrivial linear dependence relation

b_{0}\varphi_{0}^{g}+\cdots+b_{m}\varphi_{m}^{g}=0

with
b_{0},\ldots,b_{m}\in\Bbbk
. We may assume that this relation is chosen so that
m
as small as possible; then
b_{m}\neq 0
and
m>0
. Apply the operator
f\mapsto f-(-g)\cdot f
to this equation. By (
2.4
), we obtain

b_{1}\varphi_{0}^{g}+b_{2}\varphi_{1}^{g}+\cdots+b_{m}\varphi_{m-1}^{g}=0,

contradicting the minimality of
m
. We conclude that
\dim L_{a}=a
.

(
4
)
This follows from (
2.4
) and the independence on
g
(see (
1
)).

(
5
)
By construction we have an embedding of representations
L_{a-1}\subset L_{a}
.
If
g
is a generator of
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1)
as a
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
-module, then it is clear from (
2.4
) that
(-g)
acts trivially on the quotient
L_{a}/L_{a-1}
. Therefore, the group
\mathbb{Z}g\subset\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1)
acts trivially, and by continuity, all of
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1)
acts trivially.

On the other hand, by (
2.5
), an element
\gamma\in\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}|\mathbb{F})
acts on
L_{a}/L_{a-1}
by the scalar
c_{a-1}^{a-1}(\chi_{\ell}(\gamma)^{-1})=\chi_{\ell}(\gamma)^{-a+1}
(see (
2.3
)). We conclude that
L_{a}/L_{a-1}\cong\Bbbk(-a+1)
.

(
6
)
Consider the multiplication map
\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1),\Bbbk)\otimes_{\Bbbk}\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1),\Bbbk)\to\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1),\Bbbk)
. This map is compatible with the action of
\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}|\mathbb{F})\ltimes\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1)
. It follows from (
2.1
) that the multiplication map restricts to the desired map

L_{a}\otimes_{\Bbbk}L_{b}\to L_{a+b-1}.

The image of this map contains
\varphi_{a}=\varphi_{a}\varphi_{0}
and
\varphi_{b}=\varphi_{0}\varphi_{b}
. By (
2.4
), it contains all of
L_{a}
and
L_{b}
, and thus it contains
L_{\max\{a,b\}}
.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Assume that
\Bbbk
has characteristic
0
.

(1)

For
r\geq 0
, there is an isomorphism of
\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}|\mathbb{F})
-modules

L_{a}\cong\Bbbk\oplus\Bbbk(-1)\oplus\cdots\oplus\Bbbk(-a+1).

In terms of the right-hand side, the action of
g\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1)
is given by

(2.6)

g\mapsto\exp\left[\begin{smallmatrix}0&\langle-g,-\rangle\\
&0&\langle-g,-\rangle\\
&&\ddots&\ddots\\
&&&0&\langle-g,-\rangle\\
&&&&0\end{smallmatrix}\right]

where
\langle{-},{-}\rangle:\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}}\Bbbk(-r)\to\Bbbk(-r+1)
is the natural pairing map.

(2)

For
a\leq b
, there is a short exact sequence of
\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}|\mathbb{F})\ltimes\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1)
-modules

0\to L_{a}\to L_{b}\to L_{b-a}(-a)\to 0.

Proof.

By definition we have

\Bbbk(-r)=\Bbbk\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}}\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}}(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(r),\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})=\Bbbk\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}}\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}}(\underbrace{\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1)\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1)}_{\text{$r$ copies}},\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}).

Define a
\Bbbk
-linear map
\theta_{r}:\Bbbk(-r)\to\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1),\Bbbk)
as follows: for
u\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}}(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(r),\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})
, we let
\theta_{r}(1\otimes u):\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1)\to\Bbbk
be the function given by

\theta_{r}(1\otimes u)(g)=\textstyle\frac{1}{r!}u(g\otimes\cdots\otimes g).

Then
\theta_{r}
is
\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}|\mathbb{F})
-equivariant and nonzero. We claim that
\theta_{r}
induces an isomorphism

(2.7)

\Bbbk(-r)\cong\left\{f\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1),\Bbbk)\,\Big|\,\begin{array}[]{@{}c@{}}\text{$f(ng)=n^{r}f(g)$ for all}\\
\text{$n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}$ and all $g\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1)$}\end{array}\right\}.

Indeed, it is clear that the image of
\theta_{r}
is contained in the right-hand side of (
2.7
). Moreover, the latter set is
1
-dimensional, because any element satisfying this condition is determined by its value on some generator of
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1)
as a
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
-module.

Let us describe the pairing
\langle{-},{-}\rangle:\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}}\Bbbk(-r)\to\Bbbk(-r+1)
in terms of
\theta_{r}
and
\theta_{r-1}
. For
g\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1)
and
f
belonging to the right-hand side of (
2.7
), we have

(2.8)

\langle g,f\rangle(g)=rf(g).

For the remainder of this proof, we assume that
g
is a generator of
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1)
as a
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
-module. Consider the continuous function

\nu_{r}^{g}:\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1)\to\Bbbk\qquad\text{given by}\qquad\nu_{r}^{g}(ng)=n^{r}/r!.

It is clear that
\nu_{r}^{g}
spans the right-hand side of (
2.7
). On the other hand,
\nu_{0}^{g},\ldots,\nu_{a-1}^{g}
is a basis for
L_{a}
, so
L_{a}\cong\Bbbk\oplus\cdots\oplus\Bbbk(-a+1)
as a
\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}|\mathbb{F})
-module. By (
2.8
),
\langle g,\nu_{r}^{g}\rangle(g)=1/(r-1)!
, so

\langle g,\nu_{r}^{g}\rangle=\nu_{r-1}^{g}.

In the usual
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1)
-action on
L_{a}
, the action of
g
on
\nu_{r}^{g}
is given by

(g\cdot\nu^{g}_{r})(ng)=\frac{(n-1)^{r}}{r!}=\sum_{j=0}^{r}\frac{(-1)^{j}}{j!}\nu^{g}_{r-j}(ng)=\sum_{j=0}^{r}\frac{1}{j!}\underbrace{\langle-g,\langle\cdots\langle-g,}_{\text{$j$ times}}\nu^{g}_{r}\rangle\cdots\rangle(ng).

This shows that (
2.6
) holds when
g
is a generator of
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1)
as a
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
-module. It then holds in general by continuity.

(
2
)
We continue to let
g
be a fixed generator of
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1)
as a
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
-module. Let
\partial^{a}:L_{b}\to L_{b-a}
be the map given by

\partial^{a}(\nu_{r}^{g})=\begin{cases}\nu_{r-a}^{g}&\text{if $a\leq r\leq b-1$,}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

If we identify
L_{b}
with the space of polynomial functions
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}\to\Bbbk
of degree
{}\leq b-1
, then
\partial^{a}
is the
a
-fold differentiation operator. It is easy to check that
\partial^{a}
is
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1)
-equivariant, but not
\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}|\mathbb{F})
-equivariant. Specifically, for
\gamma\in\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}|\mathbb{F})
and
f\in L_{b}
, we have

\partial^{a}(\gamma\cdot f)=\chi_{\ell}(\gamma)^{-a}\gamma\cdot\partial^{a}(f).

We conclude that
\partial^{a}
induces an isomorphism
L_{b}/L_{a}\cong L_{b-a}(-a)
.
∎

2.6.
Definition of the local systems

Consider the affine line
\mathbb{A}^{1}
over
\mathbb{F}
. Recall that there is a canonical exact sequence

\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{geom}}(\mathbb{A}^{1}\smallsetminus\{0\},1)\hookrightarrow\pi_{1}(\mathbb{A}^{1}\smallsetminus\{0\},1)\twoheadrightarrow\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}|\mathbb{F}).

This sequence is split by the choice of the unit
\mathbb{F}
-point in
\mathbb{A}^{1}\smallsetminus\{0\}
, and we identify
\pi_{1}(\mathbb{A}^{1}\smallsetminus\{0\},1)=\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}|\mathbb{F})\ltimes\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{geom}}(\mathbb{A}^{1}\smallsetminus\{0\},1)
. We also have a canonical quotient map
\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{geom}}(\mathbb{A}^{1}\smallsetminus\{0\},1)\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1)
, which is compatible with the action of
\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}|\mathbb{F})
on both groups. Combining these observations, we obtain a canonical surjective map

\pi_{1}(\mathbb{A}^{1}\smallsetminus\{0\},1)\twoheadrightarrow\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}|\mathbb{F})\ltimes\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1).

Thus, any continuous
\Bbbk
-representation of
\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}|\mathbb{F})\ltimes\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1)
gives rise to an étale
\Bbbk
-local system on
\mathbb{A}^{1}
. For
a\geq 0
, we let
\mathscr{L}_{a}
be the local system on
\mathbb{A}^{1}\smallsetminus\{0\}
corresponding to the representation
L_{a}
as in Lemma
2.2
.

The pullback of
\mathscr{L}_{a}
to
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}}\smallsetminus\{0\}
is the local system corresponding to the continuous representation of
\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{geom}}(\mathbb{A}^{1}\smallsetminus\{0\},1)
given by the restriction of
L_{a}
to this subgroup. By construction this action factors through an action of the commutative group
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1)
. It therefore admits a canonical action of
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1)
by automorphisms of local systems.

By Lemma
2.2
, for
a\geq 1
there exists a canonical exact sequence

\mathscr{L}_{a-1}\hookrightarrow\mathscr{L}_{a}\twoheadrightarrow\mathscr{L}_{1}(-a+1)

where
\mathscr{L}_{1}
is the trivial local system. Iterating these embeddings we obtain, for any
a\leq b
, a canonical embedding

(2.10)

\mathscr{L}_{a}\hookrightarrow\mathscr{L}_{b}.

For any
a,b\geq 1
, there is a canonical morphism

\mathscr{L}_{a}\otimes\mathscr{L}_{b}\to\mathscr{L}_{a+b-1}

whose image contains

, and such that the following diagram commutes, where the vertical maps are the surjections appearing in (

):

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{L}_{a}\otimes\mathscr{L}_{b}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{L}_{a+b-1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{L}_{1}(-a-b+2)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{L}_{1}(-a-b+2).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{id}}

Moreover, these morphisms are compatible with the embeddings (
2.10
)
in the obvious way.
Iterating this construction one obtains, for any
k\geq 1
and any integers

, a morphism of local systems on

\mathscr{L}_{a_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathscr{L}_{a_{k}}\to\mathscr{L}_{a_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}-k+1}.

3.1.
Setting

Let

,

The goal of this section is to explain the construction, for

[

3.2.
Definition

Let
\mathbf{a}:P\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}

For any

\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1)
on the pullback of
\mathscr{L}_{\mathbf{a}}
to
\overline{\mathbb{F}}
induced by the action on the factor labelled by

.
If
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}:P\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
are two functions, we say that

if
\mathbf{a}(p)\leq\mathbf{b}(p)

. If

\mathscr{L}_{\mathbf{a}}\hookrightarrow\mathscr{L}_{\mathbf{b}}

on

for all but one element

of

, and moreover

\mathscr{L}_{\mathbf{a}}\hookrightarrow\mathscr{L}_{\mathbf{b}}\twoheadrightarrow\mathscr{L}_{\mathbf{c}}(\mathbf{a}(p_{0}))\quad\text{where}\quad\begin{cases}\mathbf{c}(p)=\mathbf{a}(p)=\mathbf{b}(p)&\text{for all $p\neq p_{0}$,}\\
\mathbf{c}(p_{0})=1.\end{cases}

Let us say that

-special

Let

Remark 3.2
.

(1)

It should be clear that, although we omit the adjective “unipotent” from our terminology for simplicity, what we consider in Definition

is an extension of the construction of the

of the nearby cycles functor.

(2)

In the definition above one can allow more generally

to be an ind-scheme over

which is of ind-finite type. By definition a complex on such an ind-scheme is supported on a closed subscheme, and the functor of Definition

instead of

are well defined and if

which intertwines the monodromy actions (after pullback to

).

3.7.
Compositions of higher nearby cycles: construction

Let
\alpha:P_{*}\to Q_{*}
and
\beta:Q_{*}\to R_{*}
be pointed maps, and let

be an object which satisfies the following properties:

•

the
\alpha
-nearby cycles and the
\beta\alpha
-nearby cycles of

are well defined;

•

the
\beta
-nearby cycles of

In the rest of this subsection we explain how, in this setting, one can define a canonical morphism

\Upsilon^{\beta\alpha}_{f}(\mathscr{F})\to\Upsilon^{\beta}_{f^{\alpha}}(\Upsilon_{f}^{\alpha}(\mathscr{F})).

Let
\mathbf{c}:P\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
be a
\beta\alpha
-special function. Recall that this means that
\mathbf{c}(p)\neq 1

\mathbf{a}(p)=\begin{cases}\mathbf{c}(p)&\text{if $\alpha(p)=*$,}\\
1&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}\qquad\mathbf{b}(p)=\begin{cases}\mathbf{c}(p)&\text{if $\beta(\alpha(p))=*$ but $\alpha(p)\neq*$,}\\
1&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

We clearly have that
\mathbf{a}
is
\alpha
-special, and that

\mathscr{L}_{\mathbf{c}}\cong\mathscr{L}_{\mathbf{a}}\otimes\mathscr{L}_{\mathbf{b}}

Next, define

\mathbf{b}^{\prime}(q)=-|\alpha^{-1}(q)|+1+\sum_{p\in\alpha^{-1}(q)}\mathbf{b}(p).

We claim that
\mathbf{b}^{\prime}

\beta
-special. Indeed, if
\beta(q)\neq*
, then the summation involves only elements
p
satisfying
\beta(\alpha(p))\neq*
, and the claim follows from the fact that
\mathbf{c}
is
\beta\alpha
-special. Note also that if
\beta(q)=*
we have

(3.10)

\mathbf{b}^{\prime}(q)=-|\alpha^{-1}(q)|+1+\sum_{p\in\alpha^{-1}(q)}\mathbf{c}(p).

Recall the open subscheme
\mathbb{A}^{P}_{\eta,\alpha}
from §
2.1
and the morphism
\bar{\alpha}_{\eta}:\mathbb{A}^{Q}_{\eta}\to\mathbb{A}^{P}_{\eta,\alpha}

\mathscr{L}_{\mathbf{b}}
on

\mathscr{L}_{\mathbf{b},\alpha}
on
\mathbb{A}^{P}_{\eta,\alpha}

We claim that there exists a natural morphism

(3.11)

\bar{\alpha}_{\eta}^{*}\mathscr{L}_{\mathbf{b},\alpha}\to\mathscr{L}_{\mathbf{b}^{\prime}}

of local systems on
\mathbb{A}^{Q}_{\eta}
. Indeed, for
q\in Q

\mathbb{A}^{1}
in

is mapped under
\bar{\alpha}
to the diagonal copy of
\mathbb{A}^{1}

\mathbb{A}^{\alpha^{-1}(q)}

It follows that

\bar{\alpha}_{\eta}^{*}\mathscr{L}_{\mathbf{b},\alpha}\cong\bigboxtimes_{q\in Q}\Big(\bigotimes_{p\in\alpha^{-1}(q)}\mathscr{L}_{\mathbf{b}(p)}\Big).

The morphisms (
2.11
) provide a map

for each

; taking the external tensor product over

).

Note now that the restriction of

to

3.9.
Product-type situations

Let
P
be a finite set, and suppose we have a collection of maps
(f_{p}:X_{p}\to\mathbb{A}^{1})_{p\in P}
. For each
p
, let

X_{p,\eta}=f_{p}^{-1}(\mathbb{A}^{1}\smallsetminus\{0\})\qquad\text{and}\qquad X_{p,0}=f_{p}^{-1}(\{0\}).

Denote the inclusion maps by

j_{p}:X_{p,\eta}\to X_{p}
and

i_{p}:X_{p,0}\to X_{p}
.
Set

X=\prod_{p\in P}X_{p}\qquad\text{and}\qquad f=\prod_{p\in P}f_{p}:X\to\mathbb{A}^{P}.

We obviously have
X_{\eta}=\prod_{p\in P}X_{p,\eta}
. More generally, for any pointed map
\alpha:P_{*}\to Q_{*}
, we can describe
X^{\alpha}_{\eta}
as follows:

(3.13)

X^{\alpha}_{\eta}\cong\prod_{q\in Q}X^{\alpha}_{q,\eta}\times\prod_{p\in\alpha^{-1}(*)\cap P}X_{p,0}\quad\text{where}\quad X^{\alpha}_{q,\eta}=\mathop{\prod\nolimits_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}}\limits_{p\in\alpha^{-1}(q)}X_{p,\eta}.

Here, the right-hand side is a fiber product over
\mathbb{A}^{1}
. If
\alpha^{-1}(q)=\varnothing
, the right-hand side should be understood to be
\mathbb{A}^{1}
.

The following lemma is immediate from the definitions.

Lemma 3.19
.

Let
(f_{p}:X_{p}\to\mathbb{A}^{1})_{p\in P}
be as above. Suppose we have a collection of objects
\mathscr{F}_{p}\in D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathrm{c}}(X_{p,\eta},\Bbbk)
, and set

\mathscr{F}=\bigboxtimes_{p\in P}\mathscr{F}_{p}\in D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathrm{c}}(X,\Bbbk).

Then the object
\mathbf{i}_{\alpha}^{*}\mathbf{j}_{*}\mathscr{F}\in D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathrm{c}}(X^{\alpha}_{\eta},\Bbbk)
is given by

\mathbf{i}_{\alpha}^{*}\mathbf{j}_{*}\mathscr{F}\cong\bigboxtimes_{q\in Q}\left(\mathop{\bigboxtimes\nolimits_{\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\eta}}}\limits_{p\in\alpha^{-1}(q)}\mathscr{F}_{p}\right)\boxtimes\bigboxtimes_{p\in\alpha^{-1}(*)\cap P}i_{p}^{*}j_{p*}\mathscr{F}_{p}.

Here, the notation “
\boxtimes_{\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\eta}}
” is a relative external tensor product: it is the pullback of the usual external tensor product
\bigboxtimes\limits_{p\in\alpha^{-1}(q)}\mathscr{F}_{p}
along the map

\mathop{\prod\nolimits_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}}\limits_{p\in\alpha^{-1}(q)}X_{p,\eta}\hookrightarrow\prod_{p\in\alpha^{-1}(q)}X_{p,\eta}.

(When
\alpha^{-1}(q)=\varnothing
, this is the map
\mathbb{A}^{1}\to\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{F})
, and
\bigboxtimes\limits_{p\in\alpha^{-1}(q)}\mathscr{F}_{p}
should be understood to be the constant sheaf
\underline{\Bbbk}
on
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{F})
.)

Lemma 3.20
.

Let
(f_{p}:X_{p}\to\mathbb{A}^{1})_{p\in P}
and
\alpha:P_{*}\to Q_{*}
be as above. Suppose we have a collection of perverse sheaves
\mathscr{F}_{p}\in\mathsf{Perv}(X_{p,\eta},\Bbbk)
that satisfy the following condition: for each
q\in Q
, the object

\left(\mathop{\bigboxtimes\nolimits_{\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\eta}}}\limits_{p\in\alpha^{-1}(q)}\mathscr{F}_{p}\right)[1-|\alpha^{-1}(q)|]\in D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathrm{c}}(X^{\alpha}_{q,\eta},\Bbbk)

is perverse. Then the
\alpha
-nearby cycles of
\mathscr{F}
are well defined, and we have

\Upsilon^{\alpha}_{f}(\mathscr{F})\cong\bigboxtimes_{q\in Q}\left(\mathop{\bigboxtimes\nolimits_{\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\eta}}}\limits_{p\in\alpha^{-1}(q)}\mathscr{F}_{p}\right)[1-|\alpha^{-1}(q)|]\boxtimes\bigboxtimes_{p\in\alpha^{-1}(*)\cap P}\Psi^{\mathrm{un}}_{f_{p}}(\mathscr{F}_{p}).

Proof.

Consider two
\alpha
-special functions
\mathbf{a}\leq\mathbf{b}
. By Lemma
3.19
, the map

\mathbf{i}_{\alpha}^{*}\mathbf{j}_{*}(\mathscr{F}\otimes f_{\eta}^{*}\mathscr{L}_{\mathbf{a}})\to\mathbf{i}_{\alpha}^{*}\mathbf{j}_{*}(\mathscr{F}\otimes f_{\eta}^{*}\mathscr{L}_{\mathbf{b}})

is the external tensor product of the following two kinds of maps:

(3.14)

\displaystyle\text{for $q\in Q$}:

\displaystyle\mathop{\bigboxtimes\nolimits_{\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\eta}}}\limits_{p\in\alpha^{-1}(q)}(\mathscr{F}_{p}\otimes f_{p,\eta}^{*}\mathscr{L}_{\mathbf{a}(p)}\to\mathscr{F}_{p}\otimes f_{p,\eta}^{*}\mathscr{L}_{\mathbf{b}(p)});

(3.15)

\displaystyle\text{for $p\in\alpha^{-1}(*)\cap P$}:

\displaystyle i_{p}^{*}j_{p*}(\mathscr{F}_{p}\otimes f_{p,\eta}^{*}\mathscr{L}_{\mathbf{a}(p)}\to\mathscr{F}_{p}\otimes f_{p,\eta}^{*}\mathscr{L}_{\mathbf{b}(p)}).

In (
3.14
), because
\mathbf{a}
and
\mathbf{b}
are
\alpha
-special, we have
\mathbf{a}(p)=\mathbf{b}(p)=1
for each
p
that appears. That is, (
3.14
) is just the identity map of the object
\mathop{\bigboxtimes\nolimits_{\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\eta}}}\limits_{p\in\alpha^{-1}(q)}\mathscr{F}_{p}
, which is a shifted perverse sheaf by assumption.

The perverse cohomology of (
3.15
) is precisely Beĭlinson’s description of the unipotent nearby cycles of
\mathscr{F}_{p}
. More precisely, for
\mathbf{a}
and
\mathbf{b}
large enough, the
i
-th perverse cohomology of the map in (
3.15
) is an isomorphism if
i=-1
, and is
0
otherwise.

We conclude that, for
\mathbf{a}
and
\mathbf{b}
large enough, the perverse cohomology
{}^{p}\!\mathscr{H}^{i}
of the external tensor product of all the maps (

) and (

) is an isomorphism when

and zero otherwise.
∎

Lemma 3.21
.

Let
(f_{p}:X_{p}\to\mathbb{A}^{1})_{p\in P}
be as above, and let
\alpha:P_{*}\to Q_{*}
and

be pointed maps. Suppose we have a collection of perverse sheaves

3.20
we have

\mathscr{G}\cong\left(\bigboxtimes_{q\in Q}\mathscr{G}_{q}\right)\boxtimes\mathscr{G}_{Z}.

The diagram

X^{\alpha}_{\eta}\xrightarrow{\mathbf{j}_{X^{\alpha}}}X^{\alpha}\xleftarrow{\mathbf{i}_{X^{\alpha},\beta}}X^{\beta\alpha}_{\eta}

can be redrawn as

\prod_{q\in Q}X^{\alpha}_{q,\eta}\times Z\xrightarrow{\mathbf{j}_{X^{\alpha}}}\prod_{q\in Q}X^{\alpha}_{q}\times Z\xleftarrow{\mathbf{i}_{X^{\alpha},\beta}}\prod_{r\in R}\left(\mathop{\prod\nolimits_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}}\limits_{r\in\beta^{-1}(q)}X^{\alpha}_{q,\eta}\right)\times\prod_{q\in\beta^{-1}(*)\cap Q}X^{\alpha}_{q,0}\times Z.

This almost matches the general set-up at the beginning of this subsection, except for the extra factor of
Z
. A minor variant of Lemma
3.20
says that a sufficient condition for the
\beta
-nearby cycles of
\mathscr{G}
to be well defined is that for each
r\in R
the object

(3.16)

\left(\mathop{\bigboxtimes\nolimits_{\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\eta}}}\limits_{q\in\beta^{-1}(r)}\mathscr{G}_{q}\right)[1-|\beta^{-1}(r)|]

be perverse. If this holds, then we have

(3.17)

\Upsilon^{\beta}_{f^{\alpha}}(\mathscr{G})\cong\bigboxtimes_{r\in R}\left(\mathop{\bigboxtimes\nolimits_{\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\eta}}}\limits_{q\in\beta^{-1}(r)}\mathscr{G}_{q}\right)[1-|\beta^{-1}(r)|]\boxtimes\bigboxtimes_{q\in\beta^{-1}(*)\cap Q}\Psi^{\mathrm{un}}_{f^{\alpha}_{q}}(\mathscr{G}_{q})\boxtimes\mathscr{G}_{Z}.

Using the definition of
\mathscr{G}_{q}
, we rewrite the object in (
3.16
) as

\left(\mathop{\bigboxtimes\nolimits_{\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\eta}}}\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\in\beta^{-1}(r)\\
p\in\alpha^{-1}(q)\end{subarray}}\mathscr{F}_{p}\right)\left[1-|\beta^{-1}(r)|+\sum_{q\in\beta^{-1}(r)}(1-|\alpha^{-1}(q)|)\right]\\
\cong\left(\mathop{\bigboxtimes\nolimits_{\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\eta}}}\limits_{p\in\alpha^{-1}(\beta^{-1}(r))}\mathscr{F}_{p}\right)[1-|\alpha^{-1}(\beta^{-1}(r))|].

This object is perverse by the second assumption in the lemma. We conclude that the
\beta
-nearby cycles of
\mathscr{G}
are well defined. Moreover, using the definition of
\mathscr{G}_{q}
, we can rewrite (
3.17
) as

\bigboxtimes_{r\in R}\left(\mathop{\bigboxtimes\nolimits_{\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\eta}}}\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\in\beta^{-1}(r)\\
p\in\alpha^{-1}(q)\end{subarray}}\mathscr{F}_{p}\right)[1-|\alpha^{-1}(\beta^{-1}(r))|]\boxtimes\bigboxtimes_{q\in\beta^{-1}(*)\cap Q}\Psi^{\mathrm{un}}_{f^{\alpha}_{q}}(\mathscr{G}_{q})\boxtimes\bigboxtimes_{p\in\alpha^{-1}(*)\cap P}\Psi^{\mathrm{un}}_{f_{p}}(\mathscr{F}_{p}).

To finish the comparison
with
\Upsilon^{\beta\alpha}_{f}(\mathscr{F})
, we must show that if
\beta(q)=*
, then

\Psi^{\mathrm{un}}_{f_{q}^{\alpha}}(\mathscr{G}_{q})\cong\bigboxtimes_{p\in\alpha^{-1}(q)}\Psi^{\mathrm{un}}_{f_{p}}(\mathscr{F}_{p}).

This claim follows from the definition of
\mathscr{G}_{q}
, our third assumption, and the compatibility of nearby cycles with external tensor products, see
[
10
, Théorème 4.7]
.
∎

4.
Application to central sheaves

In this section we assume (for simplicity) that
\mathbb{F}
is algebraically closed, and denote by
\mathsf{Alg}_{\mathbb{F}}
the category of
\mathbb{F}
-algebras.

4.1.
Graphs of points

We consider the curve
C=\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\mathbb{F}}
and the closed point
0\in C(\mathbb{F})
.
Given
R\in\mathsf{Alg}_{\mathbb{F}}
and
y\in C(R)
, we denote by
\Gamma_{y}\subset C_{R}:=C\times_{\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{F})}\mathrm{Spec}(R)
the graph of
y
. The constant
R
-point defined by
0\in C(\mathbb{F})
will also be denoted
0
. If
P
is a finite set,
for
R\in\mathsf{Alg}_{\mathbb{F}}
and
(y_{p})_{p\in P}\in C^{P}(R)
we set

\Gamma_{\{y_{p}:p\in P\}}=\bigcup_{p\in P}\Gamma_{y_{p}}\quad\subset C_{R}.

Of course, for any subset
Q\subset P
we have a closed immersion

\Gamma_{\{y_{p}:p\in Q\}}\to\Gamma_{\{y_{p}:p\in P\}}
.
We will also denote by
\widehat{\Gamma}_{\{y_{p}:p\in P\}}
the completion of
C_{R}
along
\Gamma_{\{y_{p}:p\in P\}}
(i.e. the spectrum of the completion of
\mathscr{O}(C_{R})
with respect to the ideal of definition of the closed subscheme
\Gamma_{\{y_{p}:p\in P\}}
). We have a natural morphism

\widehat{\Gamma}_{\{y_{p}:p\in P\}}\to C_{R}
,
and the closed immersion
\Gamma_{\{y_{p}:p\in P\}}\to C_{R}
factors through a closed immersion

\Gamma_{\{y_{p}:p\in P\}}\to\widehat{\Gamma}_{\{y_{p}:p\in P\}}
.
We set

\widehat{\Gamma}_{\{y_{p}:p\in P\}}^{\circ}:=\widehat{\Gamma}_{\{y_{p}:p\in P\}}\smallsetminus\Gamma_{\{y_{p}:p\in P\}}.

4.2.
Satake category and central sheaves

Let
G
be a connected reductive algebraic group over
\mathbb{F}
.
To
G
and a choice of Borel subgroup
B\subset G
we can associate in the usual way the loop group
LG
, the positive loop group
L^{+}G
, the Iwahori subgroup
I\subset L^{+}G
, the affine Grassmannian
\mathrm{Gr}_{G}=LG/L^{+}G
and the affine flag variety
\mathrm{Fl}_{G}=LG/I
. Here the quotients are the fppf quotients, and they are represented by ind-projective ind-schemes over
\mathbb{F}
; for all of this, see
[
2
]
for details. Recall that the
L^{+}G
-equivariant derived category
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{L^{+}G}(\mathrm{Gr}_{G},\Bbbk)
of
\Bbbk
-sheaves on
\mathrm{Gr}_{G}
, resp. the
I
-equivariant derived category
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{I}(\mathrm{Fl}_{G},\Bbbk)
of
\Bbbk
-sheaves on
\mathrm{Fl}_{G}
, is endowed with a canonical unital and associative convolution product
\star^{L^{+}G}
, resp.
\star^{I}
.

The Satake category is the category
\mathsf{Perv}_{L^{+}G}(\mathrm{Gr}_{G},\Bbbk)
of
L^{+}G
-equivariant
\Bbbk
-perverse sheaves on
\mathrm{Gr}_{G}
.
It is a fundamental standard fact (see
[
12
,
3
]
) that the product
\star^{L^{+}G}
is t-exact on both sides, hence restricts to a bifunctor on the Satake category, and moreover that this restriction admits a canonical commutativity constraint. For a finite collection
(\mathscr{A}_{p})_{p\in P}
of objects in
\mathsf{Perv}_{L^{+}G}(\mathrm{Gr}_{G},\Bbbk)
, it therefore makes sense to consider the convolution product
\bigstar_{p\in P}^{L^{+}G}\mathscr{A}_{p}
.

We will denote by
\mathcal{G}
the smooth affine group scheme over
C
constructed (following X. Zhu) in
[
2
, §2.2.3.1]
: its restriction to
C\smallsetminus\{0\}
, resp. to the formal neighborhood of
0
, identifies with
G\times(C\smallsetminus\{0\})
, resp. with the Iwahori group scheme of
LG
attached to
B
. For any scheme
X
over
C
, we will denote by
\mathcal{E}^{0}_{X}=X\times_{C}\mathcal{G}
the trivial principal
\mathcal{G}
-bundle over
X
.
Recall the ind-scheme
\mathbf{Gr}^{\mathrm{Cen}}_{\mathcal{G}}
over
C
defined in
[
2
, §2.2.3.2]
; it represents the functor sending
R\in\mathsf{Alg}_{\mathbb{F}}
to the set of equivalence classes of triples
(y,\mathcal{E},\beta)
where:

•

y\in C(R)
;

•

\mathcal{E}
is a principal
\mathcal{G}
-bundle over
\widehat{\Gamma}_{y}
;

•

\beta:\mathcal{E}_{|\widehat{\Gamma}_{y}^{\circ}}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{E}^{0}_{\widehat{\Gamma}_{y}^{\circ}}
is an isomorphism.

We have canonical identifications

(4.1)

\mathbf{Gr}^{\mathrm{Cen}}_{\mathcal{G}}{}_{|\{0\}}\cong\mathrm{Fl}_{G},\quad\mathbf{Gr}^{\mathrm{Cen}}_{\mathcal{G}}{}_{|C\smallsetminus\{0\}}\cong\mathrm{Gr}_{G}\times(C\smallsetminus\{0\}).

Following Gaitsgory
[
8
]
, we consider the functor

\mathsf{Z}:\mathsf{Perv}_{L^{+}G}(\mathrm{Gr}_{G},\Bbbk)\to\mathsf{Perv}_{I}(\mathrm{Fl}_{G},\Bbbk)

defined by

\mathsf{Z}(\mathscr{A})=\Upsilon_{\mathbf{Gr}^{\mathrm{Cen}}_{\mathcal{G}}}(\mathscr{A}\boxtimes\underline{\Bbbk}_{C\smallsetminus\{0\}}[1])
.
In fact, in this setting it is known that the nearby cycles of
\mathscr{A}\boxtimes\underline{\Bbbk}_{C\smallsetminus\{0\}}[1]
are unipotent (see
[
2
, §2.4.5]
), so that
\mathsf{Z}(\mathscr{A})
coincides with the full nearby cycles, see Example
3.11
. It is known that this functor is monoidal when seen as a functor with values in
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{I}(\mathrm{Fl}_{G},\Bbbk)
(see
[
2
, Theorem 3.4.1]
), that for any
\mathscr{F}
in
\mathsf{Perv}_{I}(\mathrm{Fl}_{G},\Bbbk)
and
\mathscr{A}
in
\mathsf{Perv}_{L^{+}G}(\mathrm{Gr}_{G},\Bbbk)
the convolution
\mathscr{F}\star^{I}\mathsf{Z}(\mathscr{A})
is perverse (see
[
2
, Corollary 3.2.5]
), and that
\mathsf{Z}
is a central functor; in particular, for
\mathscr{F}
,
\mathscr{A}
as above there exists a canonical isomorphism
\mathscr{F}\star^{I}\mathsf{Z}(\mathscr{A})\cong\mathsf{Z}(\mathscr{A})\star^{I}\mathscr{F}
, see
[
2
, Theorem 3.2.3 and §3.5.1]
. In particular, for a finite collection
(\mathscr{A}_{p})_{p\in P}
of objects in
\mathsf{Perv}_{L^{+}G}(\mathrm{Gr}_{G},\Bbbk)
, it makes sense to consider the convolution product
\bigstar_{p\in P}^{I}\mathsf{Z}(\mathscr{A}_{p})
.

4.3.
Iterated affine Grassmannians

Let
P
be a finite set. Define a functor
\mathbf{Gr}_{P}
on
\mathsf{Alg}_{\mathbb{F}}
as follows: for
R\in\mathsf{Alg}_{\mathbb{F}}
,
\mathbf{Gr}_{P}(R)
is the set of equivalence classes of the following data:

•

a point
(y_{p})_{p\in P}
in
C^{P}(R)
;

•

a principal
\mathcal{G}
-bundle
\mathcal{E}
over
\widehat{\Gamma}_{\{0\}\cup\{y_{p}:p\in P\}}
;

•

an isomorphism
\beta:\mathcal{E}_{|\widehat{\Gamma}^{\circ}_{\{0\}\cup\{y_{p}:p\in P\}}}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{E}^{0}_{\widehat{\Gamma}_{\{0\}\cup\{y_{p}:p\in P\}}^{\circ}}
.

This functor is represented by an ind-proper ind-scheme over
C^{P}
. It is also easily seen that if
Q
is another finite set and
\alpha:P_{*}\to Q_{*}
is a surjective pointed map, there is a canonical identification
\mathbb{A}^{Q}\times_{\mathbb{A}^{P}}\mathbf{Gr}_{P}=\mathbf{Gr}_{Q}
.

Example 4.1
.

For
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
and
P=\{1,\ldots,n\}
, the ind-scheme
\mathbf{Gr}_{\{1,\ldots,n\}}
coincides with the ind-scheme
\mathbf{Gr}_{n}
of
[
7
, §5.1]
. If
P=\varnothing
we have
\mathbf{Gr}_{\varnothing}=\mathrm{Fl}_{G}
.

Denote by

C^{P,{\dagger}}\subset C^{P}

the open subscheme consisting of the points
(y_{p})_{p\in P}
such that
y_{p}\neq 0
for any
p
and
y_{p}\neq y_{p^{\prime}}
for any
p\neq p^{\prime}
. By standard arguments we have a canonical identification

(4.2)

(\mathbf{Gr}_{P})_{|C^{P,{\dagger}}}\cong\mathrm{Fl}_{G}\times(\mathrm{Gr}_{G})^{P}\times C^{P,{\dagger}}.

Denote by
\jmath_{P}
the open embedding

(\mathbf{Gr}_{P})_{|C^{P,{\dagger}}}\to(\mathbf{Gr}_{P})_{|(C\smallsetminus\{0\})^{P}}=(\mathbf{Gr}_{P})_{\eta}.

Below we will consider collections of perverse sheaves
\mathscr{A}_{*}\in\mathsf{Perv}_{I}(\mathrm{Fl}_{G},\Bbbk)
and
\mathscr{A}_{p}\in\mathsf{Perv}_{L^{+}G}(\mathrm{Gr}_{G},\Bbbk)
for each
p\in P
. For brevity, we denote this collection by

(\mathscr{A}_{i})_{i\in P_{*}}
.
We consider the functor

\mathsf{C}_{P}:\mathsf{Perv}_{I}(\mathrm{Fl}_{G},\Bbbk)\times\prod_{p\in P}\mathsf{Perv}_{L^{+}G}(\mathrm{Gr}_{G},\Bbbk)\to\mathsf{Perv}((\mathbf{Gr}_{P})_{\eta})

defined by

\mathsf{C}_{P}((\mathscr{A}_{i})_{i\in P_{*}})=(\jmath_{P})_{!*}\left(\mathscr{A}_{*}\boxtimes\left(\bigboxtimes_{p\in P}\mathscr{A}_{p}\right)\boxtimes\underline{\Bbbk}_{C^{P,{\dagger}}}[|P|]\right),

where we use the identification (
4.2
).

The main result of this section is the following statement, proved in §
4.6
. The statement involves
the extension of the constructions of Section
3
to ind-schemes of ind-finite type, see Remark
3.2
(
2
).

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\alpha:P_{*}\to Q_{*}
be a surjective pointed map. For any
\mathscr{A}_{*}
in
\mathsf{Perv}_{I}(\mathrm{Fl}_{G},\Bbbk)
and
(\mathscr{A}_{p})_{p\in P}
in
\mathsf{Perv}_{L^{+}G}(\mathrm{Gr}_{G},\Bbbk)
, the
\alpha
-nearby cycles of
\mathsf{C}_{P}((\mathscr{A}_{i})_{i\in P_{*}})
are well defined, and moreover we have a canonical identification

\Upsilon^{\alpha}_{\mathbf{Gr}_{P}}\bigl(\mathsf{C}_{P}((\mathscr{A}_{i})_{i\in P_{*}})\bigr)\cong\mathsf{C}_{Q}\bigl((\mathscr{B}_{j})_{j\in Q_{*}}\bigr)

where

\mathscr{B}_{*}=\mathscr{A}_{*}\star^{I}\left(\bigstar_{p\in\alpha^{-1}(*)\cap P}^{I}\mathsf{Z}(\mathscr{A}_{p})\right)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathscr{B}_{q}=\bigstar_{p\in\alpha^{-1}(q)}^{L^{+}G}\mathscr{A}_{p}\quad\text{for $q\in Q$.}

If
\beta:Q_{*}\to R_{*}
is another surjective pointed map, then the natural map

\Upsilon^{\beta\alpha}_{\mathbf{Gr}_{P}}\bigl(\mathsf{C}_{P}((\mathscr{A}_{i})_{i\in P_{*}})\bigr)\to\Upsilon^{\beta}_{\mathbf{Gr}_{Q}}\Upsilon^{\alpha}_{\mathbf{Gr}_{P}}\bigl(\mathsf{C}_{P}((\mathscr{A}_{i})_{i\in P_{*}})\bigr)

given by (
3.9
)
is an isomorphism.

Remark 4.3
.

As was explained to us by A. Salmon, Theorem
4.2
can be restated as the construction of a category cofibered over the category of finite pointed sets (and pointed maps).

Remark 4.4
.

In this remark we assume that
P=\{1,\ldots,n\}
for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
and
Q=\varnothing
. In this case there is a unique choice for
\alpha
, we have
\mathbf{Gr}_{Q}=\mathrm{Fl}_{G}
, and Theorem
4.2
says that

(4.3)

\Upsilon^{\alpha}_{\mathbf{Gr}_{P}}(\mathscr{A}_{*},\mathscr{A}_{1},\ldots,\mathscr{A}_{n})\cong\mathscr{A}_{*}\star^{I}\mathsf{Z}(\mathscr{A}_{1})\star^{I}\cdots\star^{I}\mathsf{Z}(\mathscr{A}_{n}).

(1)

If
n=1
, by Example
3.11
the fact that the nearby cycles are well defined is automatic; the isomorphism (
4.3
) is the content of
[
2
, Proposition 3.2.1]
. In case

, this statement is closely related to the results of
[
2
, §3.5]
.

(2)

There exists a natural action of the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
on
\mathbf{Gr}_{P}
by permutation of the points

. This action preserves the preimage of

and, under the identification (
4.2
), its restriction to this open subset identifies with the diagonal action by permutation of the factors in

and

. It also preserves the preimage of
(0,\ldots,0)
and restricts to the identity on this preimage. For any

we deduce a canonical isomorphism between
\Upsilon_{\mathbf{Gr}_{P}}(\mathsf{C}_{P}(\mathscr{A}_{*},\mathscr{A}_{1},\ldots,\mathscr{A}_{n}))
and the similar object obtained by permutation of the
\mathscr{A}_{i}
’s. Using the same techniques as in
[
2
, §3.5.8]
one can check that, under (

), this isomorphism is induced by the “centrality” isomorphism for the functor
\mathsf{Z}
(see
[
2
, Theorem 3.2.3]
) or, equivalently (by
[
9
]
, see
[

, Theorem 3.5.1]
), by the commutativity constraint on the Satake category.

4.4.
Convolution–torsor affine Grassmannians

We now introduce some auxiliary ind-schemes needed for the proof of Theorem
4.2
. In this section, we
assume that
P_{*}
and
Q_{*}
are equipped with total orders such that

is the smallest element, and
that

\alpha:P_{*}\to Q_{*}

is a surjective, order-preserving pointed map.
We set

\min(\alpha^{-1}):=\{i\in P\mid i=\min(\alpha^{-1}(\alpha(i)))\},\quad\overline{\text{m\char 16\relax n}}(\alpha^{-1}):=P_{*}\smallsetminus\min(\alpha^{-1}).

For

in
P
or

, we will denote by

the predecessor of

.

Let

.
We will call

the “convolution locus,” and

\widehat{\mathbf{Gr}}{}_{\alpha}^{\mathbf{c},\mathbf{t}}

,
\widehat{\mathbf{Gr}}{}_{\alpha}^{\mathbf{c},\mathbf{t}}(R)
is the set of equivalence classes of the following data:

•

a point
(y_{q})_{q\in Q}
in

;

•

-bundle

In this definition,
if

In the special case where

Example 4.5
.

For
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
and
P=\{1,\ldots,n\}
, the ind-scheme
\widehat{\mathbf{Gr}}{}_{\{1,\ldots,n\}}^{\{1,\ldots,n\},\varnothing}
coincides with the ind-scheme

of

.

If
\mathbf{t}^{\prime}\subset\mathbf{t}
, there is an obvious map

(4.4)

given by forgetting the

’s with

(4.5)

\widehat{\mathbf{Gr}}{}_{\alpha}^{\varnothing,\varnothing}\cong\underbrace{\mathrm{Fl}_{G}\times\cdots\times\mathrm{Fl}_{G}}_{\text{$|\alpha^{-1}(*)|$ copies}}\times\prod_{j\in Q}(\underbrace{\mathbf{Gr}^{\mathrm{Cen}}_{\mathcal{G}}\times_{C}\cdots\times_{C}\mathbf{Gr}^{\mathrm{Cen}}_{\mathcal{G}})}_{\text{$|\alpha^{-1}(j)|$ copies}}.

We have

\prod_{j\in Q}\underbrace{LG\times^{L^{+}G}LG\times^{L^{+}G}\cdots\times^{L^{+}G}\mathrm{Gr}_{G}}_{\text{$|\alpha^{-1}(j)|$ factors}}\times(C\smallsetminus\{0\})^{Q}.

More generally, the previous description remains valid over

for any

(4.9)

(\widehat{\mathbf{Gr}}{}_{\alpha}^{\mathbf{c},\varnothing})_{|C^{Q,{\dagger}}}\cong(\widehat{\mathbf{Gr}}{}_{\alpha}^{\overline{\text{m\char 16\relax n}}(\alpha^{-1}),\varnothing})_{|C^{Q,{\dagger}}}\qquad\text{if $\mathbf{c}\supset\overline{\text{m\char 16\relax n}}(\alpha^{-1})$.}

, the fiber of

may differ from (
4.8
) in the following way: some instances of “

” are replaced by “

,” depending on

’s.

We now explain why
\mathbf{t}
is called the “torsor locus.” Define the pro-smooth group scheme
\mathcal{L}_{Q}^{+}\mathcal{G}
over
C^{Q}
which represents the functor on
\mathsf{Alg}_{\mathbb{F}}
such that
(\mathcal{L}_{Q}^{+}\mathcal{G})(R)

with
(y_{q})_{q\in Q}\in C^{Q}(R)
and

. (The representability of this group scheme can be proved as in
[
2
, §3.5.2]
.) The following lemma follows from standard arguments (see e.g.
[
2
, Lemma 2.3.9]

Lemma 4.6
.

The maps (
4.4
) and (

4.7.
Groupoid perspective

Let
P
be a finite set. Let
K=K_{P}
be the set whose elements are sequences of surjective pointed maps

(4.18)

\gamma=(P_{*}\xrightarrow{\alpha_{1}}P_{1*}\xrightarrow{\alpha_{2}}\cdots\xrightarrow{\alpha_{k-1}}P_{k-1,*}\xrightarrow{\alpha_{k}}\varnothing_{*}).

Given such a sequence
\gamma
, an
elementary refinement
of
\gamma
is a new sequence
\gamma^{\prime}
obtained by decomposing some
\alpha_{i}
into a composition of two surjective maps: say

\gamma^{\prime}=(P_{*}\xrightarrow{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\xrightarrow{\alpha_{i-1}}P_{i-1,*}\xrightarrow{\alpha^{\prime}_{i}}Q_{*}\xrightarrow{\alpha^{\prime\prime}_{i}}P_{i,*}\xrightarrow{\alpha_{i+1}}\cdots\xrightarrow{\alpha_{k}}\varnothing_{*})

where
\alpha_{i}=\alpha^{\prime\prime}_{i}\circ\alpha_{i}^{\prime}
. Make
K
into a poset by declaring that
\gamma\preceq\gamma^{\prime}
if
\gamma^{\prime}
can be obtained from
\gamma
by a (possibly empty) sequence of elementary refinements. Of course, this poset can be regarded as a category in the usual way: there is a morphism
\gamma\to\gamma^{\prime}
if
\gamma\preceq\gamma^{\prime}
. This poset (resp. category) has a unique minimal element (resp. initial object): namely, the unique pointed map
P_{*}\to\varnothing_{*}
.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
K^{\cong}
be the groupoid obtained from
K
by formally inverting all morphisms. Then
K^{\cong}
is a contractible groupoid.

Recall that a groupoid is said to be
contractible
if for any two objects
x
and
y
, there is a unique morphism
x\to y
. (This is equivalent to requiring that the nerve of the groupoid be a contractible Kan complex.) The following standard argument applies to any poset with a unique minimal (or maximal) element.

Proof.

The initial object
e
of
K
remains an initial object in
K^{\cong}
, so there is a unique morphism from
e
to every other object. This implies that every object of
K^{\cong}
is initial, and then that
K^{\cong}
is contractible.
∎

Let
\gamma
be as in (
4.18
). For brevity, we introduce the notation

\underline{\Upsilon}^{\gamma}(\mathsf{C}_{P}((\mathscr{A}_{i})_{i\in P_{*}})):=\Upsilon_{\mathbf{Gr}_{P_{k-1}}}^{\alpha_{k}}\circ\cdots\circ\Upsilon_{\mathbf{Gr}_{P_{1}}}^{\alpha_{2}}\circ\Upsilon_{\mathbf{Gr}_{P}}^{\alpha_{1}}(\mathsf{C}_{P}((\mathscr{A}_{i})_{i\in P_{*}})).

(Here, all the functors are well defined thanks to Theorem
4.2
.)

Proposition 4.10
.

For any object
\mathscr{A}_{*}
in
\mathsf{Perv}_{I}(\mathrm{Fl}_{G},\Bbbk)
, and any collection of objects
(\mathscr{A}_{p})_{p\in P}
in
\mathsf{Perv}_{L^{+}G}(\mathrm{Gr}_{G},\Bbbk)
, there is a contractible groupoid whose objects are of the form
\underline{\Upsilon}^{\gamma}(\mathsf{C}_{P}((\mathscr{A}_{i})_{i\in P_{*}}))
.

Proof.

Define a functor
F:K\to\mathsf{Perv}_{I}(\mathrm{Fl}_{G},\Bbbk)
as follows: on objects, we set

F(\gamma)=\underline{\Upsilon}^{\gamma}(\mathsf{C}_{P}((\mathscr{A}_{i})_{i\in P_{*}})).

If
\gamma\to\gamma^{\prime}
is an elementary refinement, then Theorem
4.2
gives us an isomorphism

F(\gamma\to\gamma^{\prime}):F(\gamma)\xrightarrow{\sim}F(\gamma^{\prime}).

By Lemma
3.16
, this rule extends to arbitrary morphisms in
K
, so
F
is a well-defined functor. Since
F
sends every morphism in
K
to an isomorphism, it extends uniquely to a faithful functor
F^{\cong}:K^{\cong}\to\mathsf{Perv}_{I}(\mathrm{Fl}_{G},\Bbbk)
. Its image is a (non-full) subcategory of
\mathsf{Perv}_{I}(\mathrm{Fl}_{G},\Bbbk)
that is a contractible groupoid by Lemma
4.9
.
∎

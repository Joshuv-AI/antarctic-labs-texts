1.
Introduction

The mean topological dimension of a dynamical system
(\mathbb{M},f)
, denoted by
\text{mdim}(\mathbb{M},f)
, where
\mathbb{M}
is a compact topological space and
f
is a continuous map, is an invariant under topological conjugacy. This concept was introduced by Gromov in 1999 (
[
11
]
). It serves as an essential tool for understanding systems with infinite topological entropy. In 2000, Lindenstrauss and Weiss (
[
16
]
) demonstrated that the left-shift map defined on
([0,1]^{n})^{\mathbb{Z}}
has a mean topological dimension equal to
n
, where
n
is a positive integer. We define the mean topological dimension in Section
2
.

The concept of mean topological dimension is closely related to problems involving the embedding of minimal dynamical systems. The works
[
16
]
,
[
19
]
,
[
13
]
, and
[
12
]
demonstrate that any minimal system with a mean topological dimension less than
\frac{n}{2}
can be embedded into the shift map on
([0,1]^{n})^{\mathbb{Z}}
. It is worth noting that the value
\frac{n}{2}
is optimal in this context. In
[
8
]
, the author constructed minimal subshifts on a countable infinite amenable group with arbitrarily mean topological dimension. It is also worth mentioning that calculating the mean topological dimension is a challenging task. Consequently, it becomes crucial to obtain upper bounds for the mean topological dimension of a dynamical system.

The metric mean dimension for dynamical systems defined on compact metric spaces, introduced by Lindenstrauss and Weiss in 2000 (
[
16
]
), offers upper bounds for the mean topological dimension. Since its introduction, the notion of metric mean dimension has been extensively studied, as we can see in the works
[
4
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
15
]
,
[
20
]
,
[
25
]
, among other works.

In 2019, Lindenstrauss and Tsukamoto (
[
18
]
) introduced a new tool that provides a better upper bound for the mean topological dimension: the mean Hausdorff dimension. However, it is important to note that both the metric mean dimension and mean Hausdorff dimension are not invariant under topological conjugacy; they depend on the chosen metric for the space.

In summary, the metric mean dimension and mean Hausdorff dimension depend on three variables: the dynamics represented by
f
, the space denoted as
\mathbb{M}
, and the metric
d
employed on
\mathbb{M}
. We denote by
\text{mdim}_{\text{M}}(\mathbb{M},d,f)
and
\text{mdim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d,f)
the metric mean dimension and the mean Hausdorff dimension of
f
, respectively.

Several works explore the metric mean dimension concerning the dynamics and the invariant space in which these dynamics operate. For instance, in
[
6
]
, the authors establish that, for
C^{0}
-generic homeomorphisms acting on a compact, smooth, boundaryless manifold
\mathbb{M}
with dimension greater than one, the upper metric mean dimension concerning the smooth metric coincides with the dimension of the manifold. Furthermore, in
[
3
]
it is proved the set of all homeomorphisms on
\mathbb{M}
with metric mean dimension equal to a fixed
\alpha\in[0,\text{dim}(\mathbb{M})]
is dense in
\text{Hom}(\mathbb{M})
, where
\text{dim}(\mathbb{M})
is the topological dimension of
\mathbb{M}
. These results are similarly demonstrated in
[
2
]
for the case of the mean Hausdorff dimension.
Moreover, in
[
1
]
it is proved that if
\text{dim}(\mathbb{M})\geq 2
, the mapping

\displaystyle\text{mdim}_{\text{M}}(\mathbb{M},d,\cdot)\colon\text{Hom}(\mathbb{M})

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle\mapsto\text{mdim}_{\text{M}}(\mathbb{M},d,f)

is not continuous anywhere.

The dependence of the metric mean dimension on the metric has been explored in various works. For instance, in
[
16
]
it is proven that for any metric
d
on
\mathbb{M}
, we have

\text{mdim}(\mathbb{M},f)\leq\overline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{M}}(\mathbb{M},d,f).

Furthermore, it is conjectured that for any dynamical system
(\mathbb{M},f)
, there exists a metric
d
on
\mathbb{M}
such that

\text{mdim}(\mathbb{M},f)=\overline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{M}}(\mathbb{M},d,f).

This conjecture has been verified for specific cases of dynamical systems (see
[
18
]
, Theorem 3.12).
In
[
18
]
, the authors present an example of a left shift
(A^{\mathbb{Z}},\sigma)
and two metrics
d
and
d^{\prime}
on
A^{\mathbb{Z}}
such that

{\text{mdim}}_{\text{M}}(A^{\mathbb{Z}},d,\sigma)=\frac{1}{2}=\text{dim}_{\text{B}}(A)\quad\text{and}\quad{\text{mdim}}_{\text{M}}(A^{\mathbb{Z}},d^{\prime},\sigma)=0,

where
\text{dim}_{\text{B}}(A)
denotes the box dimension of A (for the definition of box dimension, see
[
9
]
, Section 3.1). In Example
4.1
, we will provide an example of a fixed dynamical system
f:[0,1]\rightarrow[0,1]
such that for any fixed
a\in[0,1]
there exists an explicit metric
d_{a}
on
[0,1]
such that
\text{mdim}_{\text{M}}([0,1],d_{a},f)=\text{mdim}_{\text{H}}([0,1],d_{a},f)=a
(see Remark
4.2
).

In
[
6
]
, Corollary D states that there exist a dense subset of metrics
\mathcal{D}
on
[0,1]
and a generic subset

of

\text{mdim}_{\text{M}}([0,1],\rho,f)=1\text{ for all }f\in\mathcal{G},\text{ for all }\rho\in\mathcal{D}.

Next, in
[

, Theorem 1.1 states that if
A
is a finite set, then

\text{mdim}_{\text{M}}(\mathcal{X},d_{\alpha},\sigma_{1})=\frac{2h_{\text{top}}(\mathcal{X},\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2})}{\log\alpha},

where
\sigma_{1}((x_{m,n})_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}})=((x_{m+1,n})_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}})
and
\sigma_{2}((x_{m,n})_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}})=((x_{m,n+1})_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}})

,
\mathcal{X}
is a closed subset of
A^{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
invariant under both

and
\sigma_{2}
and

d_{\alpha}(x,y)=\alpha^{-\min\{|u|_{\infty}:x_{u}\neq y_{u}\}},

where
|u|_{\infty}=\max(|m|,|n|)
for

and
\alpha>1
. In Examples
4.4
and
4.5
, we will consider a similar metric
\textbf{d}_{\alpha}
on the Cantor set
C
and calculate the metric mean dimension of some particular maps on
(\textit{{C}},\textbf{d}_{\alpha})
.

From Examples
7.1
and
7.3
, we can conclude that, for any
b\in[n,\infty)
, there exists a metric
d_{b}
on
([0,1]^{n})^{\mathbb{Z}}
such that

\text{mdim}_{\text{M}}(([0,1]^{n})^{\mathbb{Z}},d_{b},\sigma)=\text{mdim}_{\text{H}}(([0,1]^{n})^{\mathbb{Z}},d_{b},\sigma)=b

(see (
7.2
) and (
7.3
)).

The purpose of this work is to explore the continuity of the metric mean dimension on the metric
d
on
\mathbb{M}
. We will prove that, in general, the functions
d\mapsto\text{mdim}_{\text{M}}(\mathbb{M},d,f)
and
d\mapsto\text{mdim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d,f)
are not continuous anywhere. On the other hand, we will present examples of certain classes of metrics for which

and
d\mapsto\text{mdim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d,f)
are continuous functions.

The paper is organized as follows: in the next section, we will introduce the concepts of mean topological dimension, metric mean dimension and mean Hausdorff dimension. Furthermore, we will present some alternative formulas to calculate the Hausdorff dimension of any compact metric space, which are more aligned with the definition of mean Hausdorff dimension for dynamical systems (see Lemmas
2.2
and
2.3
).

In Section
3
, we will establish several properties of the mean Hausdorff dimension, inspired by properties already known for the metric mean dimension and based on the foundational concepts of the Hausdorff dimension. For instance, it is well known that, given two metric spaces
(\mathbb{M},d)
and
(\mathbb{E},d^{\prime})
, we have that

\text{dim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M}\times\mathbb{E})\geq\text{dim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M})+\text{dim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{E})

(see
[
9
]
, Chapter 7).
In Proposition

, we show that

\underline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M}\times\mathbb{E},d\times d^{\prime},f\times g)\geq\underline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d,f)+\underline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{E},d^{\prime},g),

for any two maps

and
g:(\mathbb{E},d^{\prime})\rightarrow(\mathbb{E},d^{\prime})
. Furthermore, in Theorem

, we prove that, for
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{Z}
or
\mathbb{N}

\text{dim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d)\leq\underline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M}^{\mathbb{K}},\text{{d}},\sigma),

where

is the left shift map and

is a specific metric on

on

(see (

In Section

changing the metric on
\mathbb{M}
, when
\mathbb{M}
is the interval
[0,1]
or the Cantor set.

In Section

In Section

We conclude this work by presenting some illustrative examples in Section

2.
Mean dimension, metric mean dimension and mean Hausdorff dimension

Throughout this work, we will fix a metrizable compact space
\mathbb{M}
and we will fix a metric
d
on
\mathbb{M}
, compatible with the topology on
\mathbb{M}
. In this section we will present the notions of mean topological dimension, metric mean dimension and mean Hausdorff dimension, introduced in
[
16
]
and
[
18
]
, respectively.

We briefly present the definition of mean topological dimension. Let
\alpha=\{A_{i}\}_{i}
be an open cover of
\mathbb{M}
and define
\text{ord}(\alpha)=\displaystyle\sup_{x\in X}\displaystyle\sum_{A_{i}\in\alpha}1_{A_{i}}(x)-1
. A
refinement
of
\alpha
is an open cover
\beta=\{B_{j}\}_{j}
such that for any
B_{j}\in\beta
, there exists
A_{i}\in\alpha
, such that
B_{j}\subset A_{i}
. When
\beta
is a refinement of
\alpha
, we write
\beta\succ\alpha
. Set
D(\alpha)=\displaystyle\min_{\beta\succ\alpha}\text{ord}(\beta)
, where
\alpha
runs over all finite open covers of
\mathbb{M}
refining
\alpha
. The
topological dimension
of
\mathbb{M}
is

\dim(\mathbb{M})=\sup\{D(\alpha):\alpha\text{ is a cover of }\mathbb{M}\}.

Consider any continuous function
f:\mathbb{M}\to\mathbb{M}
, the
mean topological dimension
is defined as follow

\text{mdim}(\mathbb{M},f)=\displaystyle\sup_{\alpha}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{D(\alpha\vee f^{-1}(\alpha)\vee\cdots\vee f^{-n+1}(\alpha))}{n},

where
\alpha
runs over all finite open covers of
\mathbb{M}
.
The sequence
\alpha\vee f^{-1}(\alpha)\vee\cdots\vee f^{-n+1}(\alpha)
is subadditive for
n\geq 1
, and the above limit exists.

For a continuous map
f:\mathbb{M}\to\mathbb{M}
and any non-negative integer

n
, set

d_{n}(x,y)=\max\left\{d(x,y),d(f(x),f(y)),\dots,d(f^{n-1}(x),f^{n-1}(y))\right\}\quad\text{for any }x,y\in\mathbb{M}.

We say that
A\subset\mathbb{M}
is an
(n,f,\varepsilon)
-
separated subset

if
d_{n}(x,y)>\varepsilon
, for any two distinct points
x,y\in A
. We denote by
\text{sep}(n,f,\varepsilon)
the maximal cardinality of any
(n,f,\varepsilon)
-separated
subset of
\mathbb{M}
. We say that
E\subset\mathbb{M}
is an
(n,f,\varepsilon)
-
spanning
set for
\mathbb{M}
if
for any
x\in\mathbb{M}
there exists
y\in E
such that
d_{n}(x,y)<\varepsilon
. Let
\text{span}(n,f,\varepsilon)
be the minimum cardinality
of any
(n,f,\varepsilon)
-spanning subset of
\mathbb{M}
. Given an open cover
\alpha
of
\mathbb{M}
, we say that
\alpha
is an

(n,f,\varepsilon)
-
cover
of
\mathbb{M}
if the
d_{n}
-diameter of any element of
\alpha
is less than

\varepsilon
. Let
\text{cov}(n,f,\varepsilon)
be the minimum number of elements in any
(n,f,\varepsilon)
-cover of
\mathbb{M}
. Set

•

\text{sep}(f,\varepsilon)=\underset{n\to\infty}{\limsup}\frac{1}{n}\log\text{sep}(n,f,\varepsilon)
;

•

\text{span}(f,\varepsilon)=\underset{n\to\infty}{\limsup}\frac{1}{n}\log\text{span}(n,f,\varepsilon)
;

•

\text{cov}(f,\varepsilon)=\underset{n\to\infty}{\limsup}\frac{1}{n}\log\text{cov}(n,f,\varepsilon)
.

Definition 2.1
.

We define the
lower metric mean dimension
of
(\mathbb{M},d,f)
and the
upper metric mean dimension
of
(\mathbb{M},d,f)
by

\underline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{M}}(\mathbb{M},d,f)=\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\text{sep}(f,\varepsilon)}{|\log\varepsilon|}=\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\text{span}(f,\varepsilon)}{|\log\varepsilon|}=\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\text{cov}(f,\varepsilon)}{|\log\varepsilon|}

and

\overline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{M}}(\mathbb{M},d,f)=\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\text{sep}(f,\varepsilon)}{|\log\varepsilon|}=\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\text{span}(f,\varepsilon)}{|\log\varepsilon|}=\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\text{cov}(f,\varepsilon)}{|\log\varepsilon|},

respectively (see
[
16
]
, Section 4).

Now, we present the definition of the Hausdorff dimension given in
[
18
]
:
for
s\geq 0
and
\varepsilon>0
, set

\text{H}_{\varepsilon}^{s}(\mathbb{M},d)=\inf\left\{\Sigma_{n=1}^{\infty}(\text{diam}E_{n})^{s}:\mathbb{M}=\cup_{n=1}^{\infty}E_{n}\text{ with }\text{diam}E_{n}<\varepsilon\text{ for all }n\geq 1\right\}.

(2.1)

By convention we consider
0^{0}=1
and
\text{diam}(\emptyset)^{s}=0
. Let
\Theta>0
. Take

\text{dim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d,\varepsilon,\Theta)=\sup\{s\geq 0:\text{H}_{\varepsilon}^{s}(\mathbb{M},d)\geq\Theta\}.

The
Hausdorff dimension
of
(\mathbb{M},d)
, presented in
[
18
]
,
is given by

\text{dim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d):=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\text{dim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d,\varepsilon,1).

By simplicity in the notation, if
\Theta=1
, we will set

\text{dim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d,\varepsilon):=\text{dim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d,\varepsilon,1).

The usual definition of the Hausdorff dimension in the literature it is as follows: let

\text{H}^{s}(\mathbb{M},d)=\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\text{H}^{s}_{\varepsilon}(\mathbb{M},d).

The
Hausdorff dimension
of
(\mathbb{M},d)
, denoted by
\dim_{\text{H}}^{\ast}(\mathbb{M},d)
, is given by

\dim_{\text{H}}^{\ast}(\mathbb{M},d)=\sup\{s\geq 0:\text{H}^{s}(\mathbb{M},d)>0\}=\sup\{s\geq 0:\text{H}^{s}(\mathbb{M},d)=\infty\}.

Lemma 2.2
.

Fix any
\Theta>0
.
We have that
\dim_{\emph{H}}(\mathbb{M},d)=\dim_{\emph{H}}^{\ast}(\mathbb{M},d)
and furthermore

\dim_{\emph{H}}^{\Theta}(\mathbb{M},d):=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\emph{dim}_{\emph{H}}(\mathbb{M},d,\varepsilon,\Theta)=\dim_{\emph{H}}(\mathbb{M},d).

Proof.

First, notice that if
\varepsilon>0
in (
2.1
) decreases, the class of permissible covers of
\mathbb{M}
, with diameter less than
\varepsilon
, decreases. Therefore, for any
s\geq 0
,
\text{H}^{s}_{\varepsilon}(\mathbb{M},d)
increases as
\varepsilon
decreases. Hence,

\text{H}^{s}_{\varepsilon}(\mathbb{M},d)\leq\text{H}^{s}(\mathbb{M},d)\quad\text{ for any }\varepsilon>0.

Thus, if
s\geq 0
is such that
\text{H}^{s}_{\varepsilon}(\mathbb{M},d)\geq\Theta
, we have that
\text{H}^{s}(\mathbb{M},d)>0
. Consequently,

\displaystyle\dim_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d,\varepsilon,\Theta)=\sup\{s\geq 0:\text{H}^{s}_{\varepsilon}(\mathbb{M},d)\geq\Theta\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup\{s\geq 0:\text{H}^{s}(\mathbb{M},d)>0\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dim_{\text{H}}^{\ast}(\mathbb{M},d).

Taking the limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
, we obtain that

\dim_{\text{H}}^{\Theta}(\mathbb{M},d)\leq\dim_{\text{H}}^{\ast}(\mathbb{M},d).

(2.2)

Next, notice that, if
\dim_{\text{H}}^{\ast}(\mathbb{M},d)=0
, then
\dim_{\text{H}}^{\Theta}(\mathbb{M},d)=0
. Suppose that
\dim_{\text{H}}^{\ast}(\mathbb{M},d)>0
. From the definition, for each
\delta>0
there exists
s_{\delta}>0
such that

\dim_{\text{H}}^{\ast}(\mathbb{M},d)-\delta<s_{\delta}\leq\dim_{\text{H}}^{\ast}(\mathbb{M},d)\quad\text{and}\quad\text{H}^{s_{\delta}}(\mathbb{M},d)=\infty.

Thus, there exists
\varepsilon_{0}
such that
\text{H}_{\varepsilon}^{s_{\delta}}(\mathbb{M},d)\geq\Theta
, for every
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
. Hence,

\dim_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d,\varepsilon,\Theta)\geq s_{\delta}>\dim_{\text{H}}^{\ast}(\mathbb{M},d)-\delta.

Taking the limits as
\varepsilon\to 0
and
\delta\to 0
, we conclude that

\dim_{\text{H}}^{\Theta}(\mathbb{M},d)\geq\dim_{\text{H}}^{\ast}(\mathbb{M},d).

(2.3)

From (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) we have that
\dim_{\text{H}}^{\Theta}(\mathbb{M},d)
is independent of
\Theta>0
and furthermore

\dim_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d)=\dim_{\text{H}}^{\Theta}(\mathbb{M},d)=\dim_{\text{H}}^{\ast}(\mathbb{M},d),

as we want to prove.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose that
(\mathbb{M},d)
is a compact space.
For
s\geq 0
and
\varepsilon>0
, set

\emph{B}_{\varepsilon}^{s}(\mathbb{M},d)=\inf\left\{\Sigma_{n=1}^{m}(\emph{diam}(B_{n}))^{s}:\{B_{n}\}_{n=1}^{m}\text{ is a cover of }\mathbb{M}\text{ by open balls with }\emph{diam}(B_{n})\leq\varepsilon\right\}.

Setting

{\emph{dim}}^{\star}_{\emph{H}}(\mathbb{M},d,\varepsilon)=\sup\{s\geq 0:\emph{B}_{\varepsilon}^{s}(\mathbb{M},d)\geq 1\},

we have that

\dim_{\emph{H}}(\mathbb{M},d)=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\emph{dim}^{\star}_{\emph{H}}(\mathbb{M},d,\varepsilon).

Proof.

We can prove that

\text{H}_{\varepsilon}^{s}(\mathbb{M},d)\leq\text{B}_{\varepsilon}^{s}(\mathbb{M},d)\leq 2^{s}\text{H}_{{\varepsilon}/{2}}^{s}(\mathbb{M},d)

(2.4)

(see
[
9
]
, Section 2.4). It follows from the first inequality in (
2.4
) that

{\text{dim}}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d,\varepsilon)\leq{\text{dim}}^{\star}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d,\varepsilon).

(2.5)

Next, if
t
is such that
1\leq\text{B}_{\varepsilon}^{t}(\mathbb{M},d)
, then by (
2.4
) we have
\frac{1}{2^{t}}\leq\text{H}_{\varepsilon/2}^{t}(\mathbb{M},d)
. Therefore,

{\text{dim}}^{\star}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d,\varepsilon)\leq\dim_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d,\varepsilon/2,{1}/{2^{t}}).

(2.6)

From (
2.5
), (
2.6
) and Lemma
2.2
, we have that

\text{dim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d)=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}{\text{dim}}^{\star}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d,\varepsilon),

as we want to prove.
∎

Definition 2.4
.

The
upper mean Hausdorff dimension
and
lower mean Hausdorff dimension
of
(\mathbb{M},d,f)
are defined respectively as

\overline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d,f)=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\left(\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\text{dim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d_{n},\varepsilon)\right)=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\left(\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\text{dim}^{\star}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d_{n},\varepsilon)\right),

\underline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d,f)=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\left(\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\text{dim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d_{n},\varepsilon)\right)=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\left(\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\text{dim}^{\star}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d_{n},\varepsilon)\right)

(see
[
18
]
, Section 3).

Remark 2.5
.

Denote by
\text{mdim}(\mathbb{M},f)
the mean dimension of a continuous map
f:\mathbb{M}\rightarrow\mathbb{M}
(see
[
16
]
). The inequalities

\displaystyle\text{mdim}(\mathbb{M},f)

\displaystyle\leq\underline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d,f)\leq\overline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d,f)\leq\underline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{M}}(\mathbb{M},d,f)\leq\overline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{M}}(\mathbb{M},d,f)

always hold (see
[
18
]
).

Recently, in
[
17
]
, the authors introduce the concepts of mean packing dimension and mean pseudo-packing dimension for dynamical systems. They proved that the mean Hausdorff dimension of a dynamical system is lower than its mean packing dimension and its mean pseudo-packing dimension. Hence, the mean Hausdorff dimension remains a more accurate approximation of the mean topological dimension.

3.
Some fundamental properties of the mean Hausdorff dimension

Let
f:\mathbb{M}\rightarrow\mathbb{M}
be a continuous map, and let
A\subset\mathbb{M}
be a non-empty closed subset that is invariant under
f
. It is straightforward to observe that:

\overline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{H}}(A,d,f|_{A})\leq\overline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d,f)\quad\text{and}\quad\underline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{H}}(A,d,f|_{A})\leq\underline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d,f).

Next, it is well-known that for any
p\in\mathbb{N}
,
we have

{\text{mdim}_{\text{M}}}(\mathbb{M},d,f^{p})\leq p\,{\text{mdim}_{\text{M}}}(\mathbb{M},d,f).

In
[
1
]
, Corollary 3.4 provides a formula for
{\text{mdim}_{\text{M}}}(\mathbb{M},d,f^{p})
for a certain class of continuous maps on the interval (see Remark
4.3
). For the mean Hausdorff dimension, similar relationships apply.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
f:\mathbb{M}\rightarrow\mathbb{M}
be a continuous map.
For any
p\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\underline{\emph{mdim}}_{\emph{H}}(\mathbb{M},d,f^{p})\leq p\,\underline{\emph{mdim}}_{\emph{H}}(\mathbb{M},d,f)\quad\text{and}\quad\overline{\emph{mdim}}_{\emph{H}}(\mathbb{M},d,f^{p})\leq p\,\overline{\emph{mdim}}_{\emph{H}}(\mathbb{M},d,f).

Proof.

For any positive integer
m
, we know that

\max_{0\leq j<m}d(f^{jp}(x),f^{jp}(y))\leq\max_{0\leq j<mp}d(f^{j}(x),f^{j}(y)).

Hence, for each
s\geq 0
and
\varepsilon>0
, we have

\displaystyle\text{H}_{\varepsilon}^{s}(\mathbb{M},d_{m},f^{p})

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{\Sigma_{n=1}^{\infty}(\underset{d_{m,f^{p}}}{\text{diam}}E_{n})^{s}:\mathbb{M}=\cup_{n=1}^{\infty}E_{n}\text{ with }\underset{d_{m,f^{p}}}{\text{diam}}E_{n}<\varepsilon\text{ for all }n\geq 1\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\inf\left\{\Sigma_{n=1}^{\infty}(\underset{d_{mp,f}}{\text{diam}}E_{n})^{s}:\mathbb{M}=\cup_{n=1}^{\infty}E_{n}\text{ with }\underset{d_{mp,f}}{\text{diam}}E_{n}<\varepsilon\text{ for all }n\geq 1\right\}

\displaystyle=\text{H}_{\varepsilon}^{s}(\mathbb{M},d_{mp},f),

where
\underset{d_{m,f}}{\text{diam}}
represents the diameter with respect to the dynamic metric
d_{m}
associated to
f
. Therefore,

\text{dim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d_{m},\varepsilon,f^{p})\leq\text{dim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d_{mp},\varepsilon,f)

and hence

\limsup_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{m}\text{dim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d_{m},\varepsilon,f^{p})\leq p\limsup_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{mp}\text{dim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d_{mp},\varepsilon,f).

This fact proves the proposition.
∎

Next, consider two continuous maps
f\colon\mathbb{M}\to\mathbb{M}
and
g\colon\mathbb{E}\to\mathbb{E}
, where
(\mathbb{M},d)
and
(\mathbb{E},d^{\prime})
are compact metric spaces. We will endow the product space
\mathbb{M}\times\mathbb{E}
with the metric

(d\times d^{\prime})((x_{1},y_{1}),(x_{2},y_{2}))=\max\{d(x_{1},x_{2}),d^{\prime}(y_{1},y_{2})\},\text{ for }x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{M}\text{ and }y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathbb{E}.

(3.1)

This metric is uniformly equivalent to (see Remark
5.1
) the both metrics

(d\times d^{\prime})^{\ast}((x_{1},y_{1}),(x_{2},y_{2}))=d(x_{1},x_{2})+d^{\prime}(y_{1},y_{2}),\text{ for }x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{M}\text{ and }y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathbb{E}.

\overline{(d\times d^{\prime})}((x_{1},y_{1}),(x_{2},y_{2}))=\sqrt{d(x_{1},x_{2})^{2}+d^{\prime}(y_{1},y_{2})^{2}},\text{ for }x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{M}\text{ and }y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathbb{E}.

It is well known that

\text{dim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M}\times\mathbb{E})\geq\text{dim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M})+\text{dim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{E})

(see
[
9
]
, Chapter 7). In Proposition
3.4
we will prove the analog result for mean Hausdorff dimension. We will use the next lemmas.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
(\mathbb{M},d)
be a compact metric space and
\varepsilon>0
. Suppose there is a Borel measure
\mu
on
(\mathbb{M},d)
such that
\mu(\mathbb{M})\geq 1
and for any open ball
E_{i}
with
\emph{diam}_{d}E_{i}\leq\varepsilon
, we have that

\mu(E_{i})\leq(\emph{diam}_{d}(E_{i}))^{{s}}\quad\text{for any }i\geq 1.

Then,

\emph{dim}^{\star}_{\emph{H}}(\mathbb{M},d,\varepsilon)\geq{s}.

Proof.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
and take a finite cover
\{E_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
of
\mathbb{M}
, by balls
E_{i}
with
\text{diam}_{d}(E_{i})\leq\varepsilon
. We have that

\Sigma_{i=1}^{m}(\text{diam}_{d}(E_{i}))^{s}\geq\Sigma_{i=1}^{m}\mu(E_{i})\geq\mu(\cup_{k=1}^{m}E_{i})=\mu(\mathbb{M})=1.

(3.2)

Hence,

\text{B}^{{s}}_{\varepsilon}(\mathbb{M},d)\geq 1
and therefore
\text{dim}^{\star}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d,\varepsilon)\geq{s}
(see Lemma
2.3
).
∎

The Lemma
3.2
is an adaption of the
Mass Distribution Principle
(see
[
9
]
, Chapter 4), which states that if there is a mass distribution
\mu
on
(\mathbb{M},d)

and for some
s
there are numbers
c>0
and
\varepsilon>0
such that
\mu(E_{i})\leq c(\text{diam}_{d}(E_{i}))^{s}
for any set
E_{i}
with
\text{diam}_{d}(E_{i})\leq\varepsilon
, we have that

\text{dim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d)\geq{s}.

We choose the version in Lemma
3.2
, because it is more compatible with the definition of mean Hausdorff dimension used in this work.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
c\in(0,1)
. There exists
\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(c)\in(0,1)
depending only on
c
and such that: for any compact metric space
(\mathbb{M},d)
and
0<\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}
there exists a Borel probability measure
\mu
on
(\mathbb{M},d)
such that

\mu(E)\leq(\emph{diam}_{d}(E))^{c\emph{dim}_{\emph{H}}(\mathbb{M},d,\varepsilon)}

for all
E\subset\mathbb{M}
with
\emph{diam}_{d}(E)<\frac{\varepsilon}{6}.

Proof.

See
[
18
]
, Lemma 4.5.
∎

Proposition 3.4
.

Take two continuous maps
f:\mathbb{M}\rightarrow\mathbb{M}
and
g:\mathbb{E}\rightarrow\mathbb{E}
. On
\mathbb{M}\times\mathbb{E}
consider the metric given in (
3.1
). We have:

\underline{\emph{mdim}}_{\emph{H}}(\mathbb{M}\times\mathbb{E},d\times d^{\prime},f\times g)\geq\underline{\emph{mdim}}_{\emph{H}}(\mathbb{M},d,f)+\underline{\emph{mdim}}_{\emph{H}}(\mathbb{E},d^{\prime},g).

Proof.

First, we will prove for any
0<c<1
there is
\delta_{0}=\delta_{0}(c)\in(0,1)
such that, for all
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0}]
, we have

\text{dim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M}\times\mathbb{E},d\times d^{\prime},\delta/6)\geq c(\text{dim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d,\delta)+\text{dim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{E},d^{\prime},\delta)).

Fix
0<c<1
. It follows from Lemma
3.3
that there is
\delta_{0}=\delta_{0}(c)\in(0,1)
such that for all
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0}]
there are Borel probabilities measures
\mu
and
\nu
in
(\mathbb{M},d)
and
(\mathbb{E},d^{\prime})
, respectively, satisfying

\mu(M)\leq(\text{diam}_{d}(M))^{c\text{dim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d,\delta)}\quad\text{
and }\quad\nu(E)\leq(\text{diam}_{d^{\prime}}(E))^{c\text{dim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{E},d^{\prime},\delta)}

for all
M\subset\mathbb{M}
and
E\subset\mathbb{E}
with
\text{diam}_{d}(M)<\frac{\delta}{6}
and
\text{diam}_{d^{\prime}}(E)<\frac{\delta}{6}
. Observe that

\text{diam}_{d\times d^{\prime}}(M\times E)\geq\max(\text{diam}_{d}(M),\text{diam}_{d^{\prime}}(E)).

If
B
is a ball in
\mathbb{M}\times\mathbb{E}
with the metric (
3.1
), then
B=M\times E
, where
M\subseteq\mathbb{M}
and
E\subseteq\mathbb{E}
. Next, for all
M\times E\subseteq\mathbb{M}\times\mathbb{E}
such that
\text{diam}_{d\times d^{\prime}}(M\times E)<\frac{\delta}{6}
, we have

\displaystyle(\mu\times\nu)(M\times E)

\displaystyle=\mu(M)\nu(E)\leq(\text{diam}_{d}(M))^{c\text{dim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d,\delta)}(\text{diam}_{d^{\prime}}(E))^{c\text{dim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{E},d^{\prime},\delta)}

\displaystyle\leq(\text{diam}_{d\times d^{\prime}}(M\times E))^{c\text{dim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d,\delta)}(\text{diam}_{d\times d^{\prime}}(M\times E))^{c\text{dim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{E},d^{\prime},\delta)}

\displaystyle=(\text{diam}_{d\times d^{\prime}}(M\times E))^{c(\text{dim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d,\delta)+\text{dim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{E},d^{\prime},\delta))}.

By Lemma
3.2
, we get

\text{dim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M}\times\mathbb{E},d\times d^{\prime},\delta/6)\geq c(\text{dim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d,\delta)+\text{dim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{E},d^{\prime},\delta)).

Next, for each
k\geq 1
, take
c_{k}\in(0,1)
such that
c_{k}\rightarrow 1
as
k\rightarrow\infty
. It follows from the above fact there is a
\delta_{k}(c_{k})=\delta_{k}\in(0,1)
such that
\delta_{k}\rightarrow 0
as
k\rightarrow\infty
and

\text{dim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M}\times\mathbb{E},(d\times d^{\prime})_{n},\delta_{k}/6)\geq c_{k}(\text{dim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d_{n},\delta_{k})+\text{dim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{E},d^{\prime}_{n},\delta_{k})),

for all
n,k\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence, for each
k,n
, we have

\frac{1}{n}\text{dim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M}\times\mathbb{E},(d\times d^{\prime})_{n},\delta_{k}/6)\geq\frac{c_{k}}{n}(\text{dim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d_{n},\delta_{k})+\text{dim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{E},d^{\prime}_{n},\delta_{k})).

Therefore, taking the limit infimum as
n\to\infty
and the limit as
k\to\infty
, we have

\underline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M}\times\mathbb{E},d\times d^{\prime},f\times g)\geq\underline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d,f)+\underline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{E},d^{\prime},g),

which proves the result.
∎

Let
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{N}
or
\mathbb{Z}
. For
\bar{x}=(x_{k}),\bar{y}=(y_{k})\in\mathbb{M}^{\mathbb{K}}
, set

{\textbf{d}}(\bar{x},\bar{y})=\underset{j\in\mathbb{K}}{\sum}\frac{1}{2^{|j|}}d(x_{k},y_{k}).

(3.3)

Let
\sigma:\mathbb{M}^{\mathbb{K}}\rightarrow\mathbb{M}^{\mathbb{K}}
be the left shift map. In
[
16
]
, it is proved that

{\text{mdim}}(\mathbb{M}^{\mathbb{K}},\sigma)\leq{\text{dim}}(\mathbb{M}).

In
[
24
]
, it is proved that

\underline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{M}}(\mathbb{M}^{\mathbb{K}},\text{{d}},\sigma)=\underline{\text{dim}}_{\text{B}}(\mathbb{M},d)\quad\text{and}\quad\overline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{M}}(\mathbb{M}^{\mathbb{K}},\text{{d}},\sigma)=\overline{\text{dim}}_{\text{B}}(\mathbb{M},d).

We address these facts for the case of the mean Hausdorff dimension. We will need the following lemma:

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\sigma:\mathbb{M}^{\mathbb{K}}\rightarrow\mathbb{M}^{\mathbb{K}}
be the left shift map, with
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{N}
or
\mathbb{Z}
. Let
\mathcal{T}
be the set consisting of all finite open cover
\{C_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}

of
\mathbb{M}^{\mathbb{K}}
, such that each
C_{i}
has the form
C_{i}=A_{i,1}\times A_{i,2}\times\dots\times A_{i,\beta}\times\mathbb{M}\times\mathbb{M}\times\cdots
and
A_{i,j}
is an open subset of
\mathbb{M}
, for
i=1,\dots,m
,
j=1,\dots,\beta
. For every
s\geq 0
and
\varepsilon>0
, set

\emph{P}_{\varepsilon}^{s}(\mathbb{M}^{\mathbb{N}},\emph{\textbf{d}}_{n})=\underset{\{C_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}\in\mathcal{T}}{\inf}\left\{\Sigma_{i=1}^{m}(\emph{diam}_{\emph{\textbf{d}}_{n}}(C_{n}))^{s}:\mathbb{M}^{\mathbb{K}}=\cup_{i=1}^{m}C_{i}\text{ with }\emph{diam}_{\emph{\textbf{d}}_{n}}(C_{i})<\varepsilon\right\}.

Let
\Theta>0
and set

\emph{dim}^{\bullet}_{\emph{H}}(\mathbb{M},\emph{\textbf{d}}_{n},\varepsilon,\Theta)=\sup\{s\geq 0:\emph{P}_{\varepsilon}^{s}(\mathbb{M}^{\mathbb{N}},\emph{\textbf{d}}_{n})\geq\Theta\}.

We have that

\underline{\emph{mdim}}_{\emph{H}}(\mathbb{M}^{\mathbb{K}},\emph{\textbf{d}},\sigma)=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\left(\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\emph{dim}^{\bullet}_{\emph{H}}(\mathbb{M},\emph{\textbf{d}}_{n},\varepsilon,\Theta)\right)

(3.4)

and

\overline{\emph{mdim}}_{\emph{H}}(\mathbb{M}^{\mathbb{K}},\emph{\textbf{d}},\sigma)=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\left(\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\emph{dim}^{\bullet}_{\emph{H}}(\mathbb{M},\emph{\textbf{d}}_{n},\varepsilon,\Theta)\right).

(3.5)

Proof.

Clearly we have that

\underline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M}^{\mathbb{K}},\text{{d}},\sigma)\geq\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\left(\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\text{dim}^{\bullet}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},\text{{d}}_{n},\varepsilon,\Theta)\right)

and

\overline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M}^{\mathbb{K}},\text{{d}},\sigma)\geq\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\left(\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\text{dim}^{\bullet}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},\text{{d}}_{n},\varepsilon,\Theta)\right).

Next, we can prove that

\text{B}_{\varepsilon}^{s}(\mathbb{M}^{\mathbb{K}},\text{{d}}_{n})\leq 2^{s}\text{P}_{{\varepsilon}/{2}}^{s}(\mathbb{M}^{\mathbb{K}},\text{{d}}_{n})

(see
[
9
]
, Section 2.4). From this fact (see (
2.6
)), we can show that there exists
\Theta>0
such that

{\text{dim}}^{\star}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M}^{\mathbb{K}},\text{{d}}_{n},\varepsilon)\leq\dim_{\text{H}}^{\bullet}(\mathbb{M}^{\mathbb{K}},\text{{d}}_{n},\varepsilon/2,\Theta).

From the above results, we have that (
3.4
) and (
3.5
) are valid for any
\Theta>0
.
∎

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\sigma:\mathbb{M}^{\mathbb{K}}\rightarrow\mathbb{M}^{\mathbb{K}}
be the left shift map, with
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{N}
or
\mathbb{Z}
. For any metric
d
on
\mathbb{M}
, we have that

\emph{dim}_{\emph{H}}(\mathbb{M},d)\leq\underline{\emph{mdim}}_{\emph{H}}(\mathbb{M}^{\mathbb{K}},\emph{\textbf{d}},\sigma)\leq\overline{\emph{mdim}}_{\emph{H}}(\mathbb{M}^{\mathbb{K}},\emph{\textbf{d}},\sigma)\leq\underline{\emph{dim}}_{\emph{B}}(\mathbb{M},d).

Proof.

The second inequality is immediate from the definition. Next, in
[
24
]
it is proved that
\underline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{M}}(\mathbb{M}^{\mathbb{K}},\text{{d}},\sigma)=\underline{\text{dim}}_{\text{B}}(\mathbb{M},d)
. Hence, the third inequality from the theorem follows from the fact that
\overline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M}^{\mathbb{K}},{\textbf{d}},\sigma)\leq\underline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{M}}(\mathbb{M}^{\mathbb{K}},\text{{d}},\sigma)
(see Remark
2.5
).

We will prove the first inequality for
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{N}
(the case
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{Z}
can be proved analogously). For each
k\geq 1
, take
c_{k}\in(0,1)
such that
c_{k}\rightarrow 1
as
k\rightarrow\infty
. It follows from Lemma
3.3
that, for each
k\geq 1
, there exists a
\delta_{k}=\delta_{k}(c_{k})\in(0,1)
, such that
\delta_{k}\rightarrow 0
as
k\rightarrow\infty
, for which there is a Borel probability measure
\mu
on
(\mathbb{M},d)
such that

\mu(E)\leq(\text{diam}_{d}(E))^{c_{k}\text{dim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d,\delta_{k})}

for all
E\subset\mathbb{M}
with
\text{diam}_{d}(E)<\frac{\delta_{k}}{6}.

Next, we will consider the Borel probability measure
\tilde{\mu}={\mu}^{\mathbb{N}}
on
\mathbb{M}^{\mathbb{N}}
. Let
\{C_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
be a finite open cover of
\mathbb{M}^{\mathbb{N}}
with the form
C_{i}=A_{i,1}\times A_{i,2}\times\dots\times A_{i,\beta}\times\mathbb{M}\times\mathbb{M}\times\cdots,
where
A_{i,j}
is an open subset of
\mathbb{M}
, for all
1\leq j\leq\beta.
We will suppose that
\text{diam}_{\textbf{d}_{n}}(C_{i})<\frac{\delta_{k}}{6(2^{\beta})}
, for all

. In this case, we must have that
\text{diam}_{{d}}(A_{i,j})<\frac{\delta_{k}}{6}
, for
i=1,\dots,m
,
j=1,\dots,\beta
and
\beta\gg n
.
Therefore, for all
C_{i}
, we have that

\displaystyle\tilde{\mu}(C_{i})

\displaystyle=\mu(A_{i,1})\mu(A_{i,2})\cdots\mu(A_{i,\beta})\leq(\text{diam}_{d}(A_{i,1}))^{c_{k}\,\text{dim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d,\delta_{k})}\cdots(\text{diam}_{d}(A_{i,\beta}))^{c_{k}\,\text{dim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d,\delta_{k})}

\displaystyle\leq(\text{diam}_{d}(A_{i,1}))^{c_{k}\,\text{dim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d,\delta_{k})}\cdots(\text{diam}_{d}(A_{i,n}))^{c_{k}\,\text{dim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d,\delta_{k})}

\displaystyle\leq(\text{diam}_{\textbf{d}_{n}}(C_{i}))^{c_{k}n\,\text{dim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d,\delta_{k})}.

From this fact, we can to prove that

(see (

)),
where
c_{k}\to 1
and
\delta_{k}\to 0
as

. The theorem follows from Lemma
3.5
. ∎

4.
Some examples changing the metric

In this section, we will calculate the metric mean dimension of several continuous maps changing the metric on
\mathbb{M}
. For any homeomorphism
h\colon\mathbb{M}\to\mathbb{M}
, take the metric
d_{h}\in\mathbb{M}(\tau)
defined by

d_{h}(x,y)=d(h(x),h(y))\quad\text{for all }x,y\in\mathbb{M}.

(4.1)

Next, take
g:\mathbb{M}\rightarrow\mathbb{M}
given by
g(x)=h\circ f\circ h^{-1}(x)
, for all
x\in\mathbb{M}
, where
f:\mathbb{M}\rightarrow\mathbb{M}
is a fixed continuous map. We have that the map
h\colon(\mathbb{M},d_{h})\to(\mathbb{M},d)
is an isometry. Therefore, for any homeomorphism
h:\mathbb{M}\rightarrow\mathbb{M}
we have

\text{mdim}_{\text{M}}(\mathbb{M},d_{h},f)=\text{mdim}_{\text{M}}(\mathbb{M},d,h\circ f\circ h^{-1})=\text{mdim}_{\text{M}}(\mathbb{M},d,g)

and

\text{mdim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d_{h},f)=\text{mdim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d,h\circ f\circ h^{-1})=\text{mdim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d,g).

Consequently,

\text{mdim}_{\text{M}}(\mathbb{M},d_{h},f)\in[0,\text{dim}_{\text{B}}(\mathbb{M},d)]\quad\text{ and }\quad\text{mdim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d_{h},f)\in[0,\text{dim}_{\text{B}}(\mathbb{M},d)].

Since the metric mean dimension depends on the metric, we can have two topologically conjugate dynamical systems with different metric mean dimension, as we will see in the next example (see
[
1
]
,
[
14
]
and
[
24
]
).

Example 4.1
.

For any closed interval
J
, let
T_{J}:J\rightarrow[0,1]
be the unique increasing affine map from
J
onto
[0,1]
. Set
g(x)=|1-|3x-1||
for any
x\in[0,1]
. Fix
r\in(0,\infty)
and
s\in\mathbb{N}
.

For any
n\geq 1
, set
a_{0}=0
,
a_{n}=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\frac{A}{3^{ir}}
and take
I_{n}=[a_{n-1},a_{n}]
, where
A=\frac{1}{\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{3^{ir}}}=\frac{3^{r}-1}{3^{r}}
. Next, take
\phi_{s,r}\in C^{0}([0,1])
, given by
\phi_{s,r}|_{I_{n}}=T_{I_{n}}^{-1}\circ g^{sn}\circ T_{I_{n}}
for any
n\geq 1
. We have (see
[
2
, Example 2.5]
,
[
1
, Example 3.1]
and
[
24
, Lemma 6]
)

{{\text{mdim}}_{\text{H}}}([0,1],|\cdot|,\phi_{s,r})={{\text{mdim}}_{\text{M}}}([0,1],|\cdot|,\phi_{s,r})=\frac{s}{r+s}.

For a fixed
s
and any
r_{1},r_{2}\in(0,\infty)
, we have
\phi_{s,r_{1}}
and
\phi_{s,r_{2}}
are topologically conjugate by a conjugacy
h_{1,2}:[0,1]\rightarrow[0,1]
(see
[
1
]
, Remark 3.2), such that

\phi_{s,r_{1}}=h_{1,2}\circ\phi_{s,r_{2}}\circ h_{1,2}^{-1}.

Hence,

\text{mdim}_{\text{M}}([0,1],d_{h_{1,2}},\phi_{s,r_{2}})=\frac{s}{r_{1}+s}\neq\frac{s}{r_{2}+s}=\text{mdim}_{\text{M}}([0,1],|\cdot|,\phi_{s,r_{2}}),

where
d_{h_{1,2}}
is defined in
4.1
. The same fact holds for the mean Hausdorff dimension.

Next, for
n\geq 1
, set
J_{n}=[2^{-n^{n}},2^{-{n}^{n}+1}]
.
Take
\varphi_{s}\in C^{0}([0,1])
, given by
\varphi_{s}|_{J_{n}}=T^{-1}_{J_{n}}\circ g^{sn}\circ T_{J_{n}}
for any
n\geq 1
. We can prove that

\text{mdim}_{\text{H}}([0,1],|\cdot|,\varphi_{s})=\text{mdim}_{\text{M}}([0,1],|\cdot|,\varphi_{s})=0

(see
[
1
, Theorem 3.3]
).
Note that, for any
s\in\mathbb{N}
and
r\in(0,\infty)
,
\varphi_{s}
and
\phi_{r,s}
are topologically conjugate by a topological conjugacy
h:[0,1]\rightarrow[0,1]
such that
\varphi_{s}=h\circ\phi_{s,r}\circ h^{-1}
. Hence,

\text{mdim}_{\text{H}}([0,1],d_{h},\phi_{s,r})=\text{mdim}_{\text{M}}([0,1],d_{h},\phi_{s,r})=\text{mdim}_{\text{M}}([0,1],|\cdot|,\varphi_{s})=0.

Finally, let
b_{0}=0
and
b_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{6}{\pi^{2}i^{2}}
for any
n\geq 1.
Take
K_{n}=[b_{n-1},b_{n}]
. Let
\psi_{s}\in C^{0}([0,1])
be defined by
\psi_{s}|_{K_{n}}=T_{K_{n}}^{-1}\circ g^{sn}\circ T_{K_{n}}
for any
n\geq 1
.
We have that (see
[
1
, Example 3.5]
and
[
2
, Example 2.6]
)

\text{mdim}_{\text{H}}([0,1],|\cdot|,\psi_{s})=\text{mdim}_{\text{M}}([0,1],|\cdot|,\psi_{s})=1.

Note that, for any
s\in\mathbb{N}
and
r\in(0,\infty)
,
\psi_{s}
and
\phi_{r,s}
are topologically conjugate by a topological conjugacy
j:[0,1]\rightarrow[0,1]
such that
\psi_{s}=j\circ\phi_{s,r}\circ j^{-1}
. Hence,

\text{mdim}_{\text{H}}([0,1],d_{j},\phi_{s,r})=\text{mdim}_{\text{M}}([0,1],d_{j},\phi_{s,r})=\text{mdim}_{\text{M}}([0,1],|\cdot|,\psi_{s})=1.

Remark 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be the subset of
C^{0}([0,1])
consisting of each map
f
such that for some closed subinterval
K\subseteq[0,1]
,
f|_{K}:K\rightarrow K
is such that
f=T_{K}^{-1}\circ\psi\circ T_{K}
, where
\psi
is one of the maps defined in Example
4.1
(that is,
\phi_{s,r}
, or
\varphi_{s}
, or
\psi_{s}
), and
f|_{K^{c}}:K^{c}\rightarrow K^{c}
is a piecewise
C^{1}
-map.
\mathcal{M}
is dense in
C^{0}([0,1])
(see
[
6
]
and
[
1
]
). Note that for each
f\in\mathcal{M}
and
a\in[0,1]
, based on Example
4.1
, it is possible to construct an explicit metric
d_{a}
on
[0,1]
such that

\text{mdim}_{\text{H}}([0,1],d_{a},f)=\text{mdim}_{\text{M}}([0,1],d_{a},f)=a.

Remark 4.3
.

In Example
4.1
, note that
\phi_{s,r}=\phi_{1,r}^{s}
for any
s\in\mathbb{N}
and
r\in(0,\infty)
. Hence,

\text{mdim}_{\text{M}}([0,1],|\cdot|,\phi^{s}_{1,r})=\frac{s\,\text{mdim}_{\text{M}}([0,1],|\cdot|,\phi_{1,r})}{1+(s-1)\text{mdim}_{\text{M}}([0,1],|\cdot|,\phi_{1,r})}.

The same fact holds for the mean Hausdorff dimension.

Let

\textit{{C}}=\{(x_{1},x_{2},\dots):x_{n}=0,2\text{ for }n\in\mathbb{N}\}=\{0,2\}^{\mathbb{N}}

be the Cantor set. For a fixed
\alpha\in(1,\infty)
, consider the metric

\textbf{d}_{\alpha}(\bar{x},\bar{y})=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\alpha^{-n}|x_{n}-y_{n}|,\quad\text{for any }\bar{x}=(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}},\bar{y}=(y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in\textit{{C}}.

We have that
\text{dim}_{\text{B}}(\textit{{C}},\textbf{d}_{\alpha})=\frac{\log 2}{\log\alpha}
(see
[
10
]
, Proposition III.1 or
[
9
]
, page 31). Therefore, for any
\varphi\in C^{0}(\textit{{C}}),
we have from Remark
2.5
that

\underline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{M}}({\textit{{C}}},\textbf{d}_{\alpha},\varphi)\leq\overline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{M}}({\textit{{C}}},\textbf{d}_{\alpha},\varphi)\leq\text{dim}_{\text{B}}(\textit{{C}},\textbf{d}_{\alpha})=\frac{\log 2}{\log\alpha}.

(4.2)

For any
k\geq 1
, set

\displaystyle\textit{{C}}_{k}=\{(x_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}:x_{i}=0\text{ for }i\leq k-1,x_{k}=2\text{ and }x_{n}\in\{0,2\}\text{ for }n\geq k+1\}.

Note that if
k\neq s
, then
\textit{{C}}_{k}\cap\textit{{C}}_{s}=\emptyset
and
{\textit{{C}}\setminus\cup_{k=1}^{\infty}\textit{{C}}_{k}}=\{(0,0,\dots)\}
. Furthermore, each
\textit{{C}}_{k}
is a clopen subset homeomorphic to
C
via the homeomorphism

T_{k}:\textit{{C}}_{k}\rightarrow\textit{{C}},\quad(\underset{(k-1)\text{-times}}{\underbrace{{0},\dots,{0}}},2,x_{1},x_{2},\dots)\mapsto(x_{1},x_{2},\dots),

which is Lipschitz.

Example 4.4
.

For
j\in\mathbb{N}
, consider
\psi_{j}:(\textit{{C}},\textbf{d}_{\alpha})\rightarrow(\textit{{C}},\textbf{d}_{\alpha})
defined as
\psi_{{j}}(0,0,\dots)=(0,0,\dots)
and
\psi_{j}|_{\textit{{C}}_{k}}=T_{k}^{-1}\sigma^{jk}T_{k}
for
k\geq 1
, where
\sigma:\textit{{C}}\rightarrow\textit{{C}}
is the left shift map. In
[
1
]
, Proposition 5.1, it is proven that if
\alpha=3
, then

{\text{mdim}}_{\text{M}}({\textit{{C}}},\textbf{d}_{3},\psi_{j})=\frac{j\log 2}{(j+1)\log 3}.

Following the same steps, we will prove that

{\text{mdim}}_{\text{M}}({\textit{{C}}},\textbf{d}_{\alpha},\psi_{j})=\frac{j\log 2}{(j+1)\log\alpha}\quad\text{for any }\alpha>1.

Take
\varepsilon>0
. For any
k\geq 1,
set
\varepsilon_{k}=\alpha^{-k(j+1)}
. There exists
k\geq 1
such that
\varepsilon\in[\varepsilon_{k+1},\varepsilon_{k}]
.
For
n\geq 1
and
k\geq 1
, take
\bar{z}_{1}=(z_{1}^{1},\dots,z_{jk}^{1}),\dots,\bar{z}_{n}=(z_{1}^{n},\dots,z_{jk}^{n})
, with
z_{i}^{s}\in\{0,2\},
and set

{A}^{k}_{\bar{z}_{1},\dots,\bar{z}_{n}}=\{(\underset{(k-1)\text{-times}}{\underbrace{{0},\dots,{0}}},2,z_{1}^{1},\dots,z_{jk}^{1},\dots,z_{1}^{n},\dots,z_{jk}^{n},x_{1},\dots,x_{s},.\,.\,.):x_{i}\in\{0,2\}\}\subseteq\textit{{C}}_{k}.

Note that if
{A}^{k}_{\bar{z}_{1},\dots,\bar{z}_{n}}\neq{A}^{k}_{\bar{w}_{1},\dots,\bar{w}_{n}}
and
\bar{x}\in{A}^{k}_{\bar{z}_{1},\dots,\bar{z}_{n}}
,
\bar{y}\in{A}^{k}_{\bar{w}_{1},\dots,\bar{w}_{n}}
, then
(\textbf{d}_{\alpha})_{n+1}(\bar{x},\bar{y})>\frac{1}{\alpha^{k(j+1)}}
. Therefore,

\text{sep}(n+1,\psi_{j},\varepsilon_{k})\geq 2^{jnk}
and hence

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log\text{sep}(n+1,\psi_{j},\varepsilon)}{n+1}\geq\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log\text{sep}(n+1,\psi_{j},\varepsilon_{k})}{n+1}

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n\log(2^{jk})}{n+1}=\log 2^{jk}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\underline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{M}}({\textit{{C}}},\textbf{d}_{\alpha},\psi_{j})

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log\text{sep}(\psi_{j},\varepsilon_{k})}{-\log\varepsilon_{k+1}}\geq\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log(2^{jk})}{\log(\alpha^{(k+1)(j+1)})}=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{{k}j\log 2}{(k+1)(j+1)\log\alpha}

\displaystyle=\frac{j\log 2}{(j+1)\log\alpha}.

Therefore,

\overline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{M}}({\textit{{C}}},\textbf{d}_{\alpha},\psi_{j})\geq\underline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{M}}({\textit{{C}}},\textbf{d}_{\alpha},\psi_{j})\geq\frac{j\log 2}{(j+1)\log\alpha}.

(4.3)

On the other hand, note that for each
l\in\{1,\dots,k\}
, the sets
{A}^{l}_{\bar{z}_{1},\dots,\bar{z}_{n}}
have
(\textbf{d}_{\alpha})_{n}
-diameter less than

\varepsilon_{k}
. Furthermore, the sets
\{(0,0,\dots)\}
and
\overset{\infty}{\underset{s=k+1}{\bigcup}}\textbf{{C}}_{s}
has
(\textbf{d}_{\alpha})_{n}
-diameter less than

\varepsilon_{k}
. Hence

\text{cov}(n,\psi_{j},\varepsilon_{k})\leq k2^{njk}+2\leq 2k2^{njk}

and therefore

\text{cov}(\psi_{j},\varepsilon_{k})\leq\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log(2k2^{njk})}{n}=\log 2^{jk}.

Hence

\overline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{M}}({\textit{{C}}},\textbf{d}_{\alpha},\psi_{j})=\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{\text{cov}(\psi_{j},\varepsilon)}{-\log\varepsilon}\leq\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\text{cov}(\psi_{j},\varepsilon_{k+1})}{-\log\varepsilon_{k}}\leq\frac{j\log 2}{(j+1)\log\alpha}.

(4.4)

It follows from (
4.3
) and (
4.4
) that

{\text{mdim}}_{\text{M}}({\textit{{C}}},\textbf{d}_{\alpha},\psi_{j})=\frac{j\log 2}{(j+1)\log\alpha}.

Example 4.5
.

Take
\varphi:(\textit{{C}},\textbf{d}_{\alpha})\rightarrow(\textit{{C}},\textbf{d}_{\alpha})
the map defined as
\varphi(0,0,\dots)=(0,0,\dots)
and
\varphi|_{\textit{{C}}_{k}}=T_{k}^{-1}\sigma^{{k}^{2}}T_{k}
for
k\geq 1
, where
\sigma:\textit{{C}}\rightarrow\textit{{C}}
is the left shift map. Note that
\varphi
is a continuous map. We prove that

{\text{mdim}}_{\text{M}}({\textit{{C}}},\textbf{d}_{\alpha},\varphi)=\text{dim}_{\text{B}}(\textit{{C}},\textbf{d}_{\alpha})=\frac{\log 2}{\log\alpha}.

Take
\varepsilon>0
. For any
k\geq 1,
set
\varepsilon_{k}=\frac{1}{\alpha^{{k}^{2}+k}}
. There exists
k\geq 1
such that
\varepsilon\in[\varepsilon_{k+1},\varepsilon_{k}]
.
For
n\geq 1
and
k\geq 1
, take
\bar{z}_{1}=(z_{1}^{1},\dots,z_{{k}^{2}}^{1}),\dots,\bar{z}_{n}=(z_{1}^{n},\dots,z_{{k}^{2}}^{n})
, with
z_{i}^{s}\in\{0,2\},
and set

{A}^{k}_{\bar{z}_{1},\dots,\bar{z}_{n}}=\{(\underset{(k-1)\text{-times}}{\underbrace{{0},\dots,{0}}},2,z_{1}^{1},\dots,z_{{k}^{2}}^{1},\dots,z_{1}^{n},\dots,z_{{k}^{2}}^{n},x_{1},\dots,x_{s},.\,.\,.):x_{i}\in\{0,2\}\}\subseteq\textit{{C}}_{k}.

Note that if
{A}^{k}_{\bar{z}_{1},\dots,\bar{z}_{n}}\neq{A}^{k}_{\bar{w}_{1},\dots,\bar{w}_{n}}
and
\bar{x}\in{A}^{k}_{\bar{z}_{1},\dots,\bar{z}_{n}}
,
\bar{y}\in{A}^{k}_{\bar{w}_{1},\dots,\bar{w}_{n}}
, then
(\textbf{d}_{\alpha})_{n+1}(\bar{x},\bar{y})>\frac{1}{\alpha^{k^{2}+k}}
. Therefore

\text{sep}(n+1,\varphi,\varepsilon_{k})\geq\left(2^{k^{2}}\right)^{n}
and hence

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log\text{sep}(n+1,\varphi,\varepsilon)}{n+1}\geq\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log\text{sep}(n+1,\varphi,\varepsilon_{k})}{n+1}

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n\log(2^{{k}^{2}})}{n+1}=\log 2^{{k}^{2}}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\underline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{M}}({\textit{{C}}},\textbf{d}_{\alpha},\varphi)

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log\text{sep}(\varphi,\varepsilon_{k})}{-\log\varepsilon_{k+1}}\geq\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log(2^{k^{2}})}{\log(\alpha^{(k+1)^{2}+k+1})}

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{k^{2}\log 2}{((k+1)^{2}+k+1)\log\alpha}=\frac{\log 2}{\log\alpha}.

Therefore, by (
4.2
), we have that

\overline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{M}}({\textit{{C}}},\textbf{d}_{\alpha},\varphi)=\underline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{M}}({\textit{{C}}},\textbf{d}_{\alpha},\varphi)=\frac{\log 2}{\log\alpha}.

5.
On the continuity of metric and Hausdorff mean dimension maps

Throughout this section, we will work with a fixed metrizable compact topological space
(\mathbb{M},\tau)
. We use
\mathbb{M}(\tau)
to denote the set of all metrics that induce the same topology
\tau
on
\mathbb{M}
. Formally, this set is defined as:

\mathbb{M}(\tau)=\{d\colon d\text{ is a metric for }\mathbb{M}\text{ and }\tau_{d}=\tau\},

where
\tau_{d}
is the topology induced by
d
on
\mathbb{M}
. We remember that two metrics on a space
\mathbb{M}
are equivalent if they induce the same topology on
\mathbb{M}
. Therefore, if
d
is a fixed metric on
\mathbb{M}
which induces the topology
\tau
, then
\mathbb{M}(\tau)
consists on all the metrics on
\mathbb{M}
which are equivalent to
d
.

From now on, we will fix a continuous map
f:\mathbb{M}\rightarrow\mathbb{M}
. Consider the functions

\begin{aligned} \text{mdim}_{\text{M}}(\mathbb{M},f)\colon\mathbb{M}(\tau)&\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}\\
d&\mapsto\text{mdim}_{\text{M}}(\mathbb{M},d,f)\end{aligned}\quad\text{and}\quad\begin{aligned} \text{mdim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},f)\colon\mathbb{M}(\tau)&\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}\\
d&\mapsto\text{mdim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d,f),\end{aligned}

where
\mathbb{M}(\tau)
is endowed with the metric

D(d_{1},d_{2})=\max_{x,y\in\mathbb{M}}\left\{|d_{1}(x,y)-d_{2}(x,y)|:\text{ for }d_{1},d_{2}\in\mathbb{M}(\tau)\right\}

(see
[
22
]
). We will prove there exist continuous maps
f:\mathbb{M}\rightarrow\mathbb{M}
such that
\text{mdim}_{\text{M}}(\mathbb{M},f)
is not a continuous map.

Remark 5.1
.

Remember that two metrics
d_{1}
and
d_{2}
on
\mathbb{M}
are called
uniformly equivalent
if there are real constants
0<a\leq b
such that

ad_{1}(x,y)\leq d_{2}(x,y)\leq bd_{1}(x,y),

for all
x,y\in\mathbb{M}
. It is not difficult to see that, if
d_{1}
and
d_{2}\in\mathbb{M}(\tau)
are two uniformly equivalent metrics on
\mathbb{M}
, then

\text{mdim}_{\text{M}}(\mathbb{M},d_{1},f)=\text{mdim}_{\text{M}}(\mathbb{M},d_{2},f)\quad\text{
and
}\quad\text{mdim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d_{1},f)=\text{mdim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d_{2},f).

Remark 5.2
.

Note if
h_{\text{top}}(\mathbb{M},f)<\infty
, then
\text{mdim}_{\text{M}}(\mathbb{M},d,f)=0
. Therefore, as the topological entropy does not depend on
the metric, we have that
\text{mdim}_{\text{M}}(\mathbb{M},\tilde{d},f)=0
for any
\tilde{d}\in\mathbb{M}(\tau
). Analogously, we can prove that
\text{mdim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},\tilde{d},f)=0
for any
\tilde{d}\in\mathbb{M}(\tau
). Hence, if
h_{\text{top}}(\mathbb{M},f)<\infty
, then

\text{mdim}_{\text{M}}(\mathbb{M},f)\colon\mathbb{M}(\tau)\to\mathbb{R}\quad\text{ and }\quad\text{mdim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},f)\colon\mathbb{M}(\tau)\to\mathbb{R}

are the zero maps.

In the next example, we will exhibit a class of dynamical systems such that the metric and Hausdorff mean dimension maps are not continuous, with respect to the metric.

Example 5.3
.

Take
\mathbb{M}=[0,1]
endowed with the metric
|\cdot|
induced by the absolute value. For fixed
s\in\mathbb{N}
and
r\in(0,\infty)
, set
f=\phi_{s,r}:[0,1]\rightarrow[0,1]
and
I_{n}=[a_{n-1},a_{n}]
defined in Example
4.1
. Hence,

{\text{mdim}}_{\text{H}}([0,1],|\cdot|,f)={\text{mdim}}_{\text{M}}([0,1],|\cdot|,f)=\frac{s}{r+s}.

Fix any metric
d
on
\mathbb{M}
equivalent to
|\cdot|
. We will find two metrics
{d}_{1}
and
d_{2}
on
[0,1]
, arbitrarily close to
d
, such that

{\text{mdim}}_{\text{M}}([0,1],{d}_{1},f)=1\quad\text{and}\quad{\text{mdim}}_{\text{M}}([0,1],{d}_{2},f)=\frac{1}{2}.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. There exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\max\{\text{diam}_{d}(\cup_{n=N}^{\infty}I_{n})\}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

Set
b_{N}=a_{N}
and
b_{n}=a_{N}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{6\varepsilon}{2\pi^{2}j^{2}}
for
n\geq N+1
and consider
J_{n}=[b_{n-1},b_{n}]
for any
n\geq N+1
. Take the homeomorphism
h:[0,1]\rightarrow[0,a_{N}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}]
defined by

h(x)=\begin{cases}x\quad&\text{if }x\in[0,a_{N}]\\
a_{N}+\varepsilon/2\quad&\text{if }x=1\\
\left[\frac{b_{n+1}-b_{n}}{a_{n+1}-a_{n}}\right](x-a_{n})+b_{n}\quad&\text{if }x\in I_{n},\text{ for some }n\geq N+1.\end{cases}

Consider the metric
d_{1}
on
[0,1]
given by

d_{1}(x,y)=\begin{cases}d(x,y)\quad&\text{if }x,y\in[0,a_{N}]\\
|h(x)-h(y)|\quad&\text{if }x,y\in[a_{N},1]=\overline{\bigcup_{n=N+1}^{\infty}I_{n}}\\
|h(x)-a_{N}|+d(y,a_{N})\quad&\text{if }y\in[0,a_{N}],x\in[a_{N},1]\\
|h(y)-a_{N}|+d(x,a_{N})\quad&\text{if }x\in[0,a_{N}],y\in[a_{N},1].\end{cases}

As
d_{1}
depends of the metric
d
and of the homeomorphism
h
, we have that
d_{1}
belongs to
\mathbb{M}(\tau)
. Furthermore,

\text{diam}_{d_{1}}\left(\bigcup_{j=N+1}^{\infty}I_{j}\right)=\text{diam}_{|\cdot|}\left(\bigcup_{j=N+1}^{\infty}J_{j}\right)=\sum_{j=N+1}^{\infty}|J_{j}|=\sum_{j=N+1}^{\infty}\frac{6\varepsilon}{2\pi^{2}j^{2}}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

We prove that
D(d_{1},d)<\varepsilon
. If
x,y\in[0,a_{N}]
or if
x,y\in[a_{N},1]
, then
|d(x,y)-d_{1}(x,y)|=0
. Suppose that
x\in[0,a_{N}]
and
y\in[a_{N},1]
. From definition of
d_{1}
, we have that

d_{1}(x,y)=|h(y)-a_{N}|+d(x,a_{N}).

Since
d(x,y)\leq d(x,a_{N})+d(a_{N},y)
, it follows that

\displaystyle d(x,y)-d_{1}(x,y)

\displaystyle\leq d(x,a_{N})+d(a_{N},y)-d(x,a_{N})-|a_{N}-h(y)|

\displaystyle=d(a_{N},y)-|a_{N}-h(y)|<\varepsilon

and

\displaystyle d_{1}(x,y)-d(x,y)

\displaystyle=d(x,a_{N})+|a_{N}-h(y)|-d(x,y)

\displaystyle\leq d(x,y)+d(y,a_{N})+|a_{N}-h(y)|-d(x,y)

\displaystyle=d(y,a_{N})+|a_{N}-h(y)|<\varepsilon.

Hence,
D(d_{1},d)<\varepsilon
.

Next, given that
h_{\text{top}}(f|_{[0,a_{N}]})<\infty,
we have

{{\text{mdim}}_{\text{M}}}([0,1],d_{1},f)={{\text{mdim}}_{\text{M}}}([0,1],d_{1},f|_{[a_{N},a_{N}+\varepsilon/2]}).

By
[
1
, Example 3.1]
and
[
2
, Example 2.6]
, it is possible to obtain that

{{\text{mdim}}_{\text{H}}}([0,1],d_{1},f)={{\text{mdim}}_{\text{M}}}([0,1],d_{1},f)=1.

The existence of
d_{2}
can be shown analogously
taking
r=s
,
c_{N}=a_{N}
and
c_{n}=a_{N}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{A\varepsilon}{3^{is}}
for
n\geq N+1
, where
A=\frac{1}{\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}{3^{-is}}}
, and considering
K_{n}=[c_{n-1},c_{n}]
for any
n\geq N+1
. In consequence,
\text{mdim}_{\text{M}}(\mathbb{M},f)
and
\text{mdim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},f)
are not continuous on
d
.

In Example
5.3
, we proved that there exists a dynamical system with metric mean dimension and mean Hausdorff dimension maps not continuous with respect to the metrics. In the following theorem, we will prove that this result is more general.

Theorem 5.4
.

Set
\emph{Q}=\emph{M}
or
\emph{H}
. If there exists a continuous map
f:\mathbb{M}\rightarrow\mathbb{M}
such that
\emph{mdim}_{\emph{Q}}(\mathbb{M},d,f)>0
, for some
d\in\mathbb{M}(\tau)
, then

\displaystyle\emph{mdim}_{\emph{Q}}(\mathbb{M},f)\colon\mathbb{M}(\tau)

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}

\displaystyle d

\displaystyle\mapsto\emph{mdim}_{\emph{Q}}(\mathbb{M},d,f)

is not continuous anywhere.

Proof.

Let
(\mathbb{M},d)
be a compact metric space and
f:\mathbb{M}\to\mathbb{M}
be a continuous map such that
\text{mdim}_{\text{M}}(\mathbb{M},d,f)>0
. Given any
\alpha,\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, we define the metric

d_{\alpha,\varepsilon}(x,y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cccc}d(x,y),\text{ if }d(x,y)\geq\varepsilon,\\
\varepsilon^{1-\alpha}d(x,y)^{\alpha},\text{ if }d(x,y)<\varepsilon.\end{array}\right.

Note that
d_{\alpha,\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{M}(\tau)
. Moreover, taking
x,y\in\mathbb{M}
such that
d(x,y)\geq\varepsilon
, we have that
|d(x,y)-d_{\alpha,\varepsilon}(x,y)|=0<\varepsilon
. On the other hand, if we consider
x,y\in\mathbb{M}
such that
d(x,y)<\varepsilon
, we have that

\displaystyle|d(x,y)-d_{\alpha,\varepsilon}(x,y)|=|d(x,y)-\varepsilon^{1-\alpha}d(x,y)^{\alpha}|\leq d(x,y)+\varepsilon^{1-\alpha}d(x,y)^{\alpha}<2\varepsilon.

Hence,
D(d,d_{\alpha,\varepsilon})<2\varepsilon
. However, for
\text{Q}=\text{M}
or
H
we prove

\text{mdim}_{\text{Q}}(\mathbb{M},d_{\alpha,\varepsilon},f)=\frac{\text{mdim}_{\text{Q}}(\mathbb{M},d,f)}{\alpha}.

Firstly, we prove to claim for metric mean dimension. Consider any
\eta\in(0,\varepsilon)
. Let
A
an
(n,f,\eta)
-spanning set of
(\mathbb{M},d)
. Then, for any
y\in\mathbb{M}
, there exists
x\in A
such that

. Hence,

(d_{\alpha,\varepsilon})_{n}(x,y)=\varepsilon^{1-\alpha}d_{n}(x,y)^{\alpha}<\varepsilon^{1-\alpha}\eta^{\alpha}.

Thus,
A
is an
(n,f,\varepsilon^{1-\alpha}\eta^{\alpha})
-spanning set of
(\mathbb{M},d_{\alpha,\varepsilon})

\text{span}_{d_{\alpha,\varepsilon}}(f,\varepsilon^{1-\alpha}\eta^{\alpha})\leq\text{span}_{d}(f,\eta),

and consequently, we obtain that

\displaystyle\text{mdim}_{\text{M}}(\mathbb{M},d_{\alpha,\varepsilon},f)=\displaystyle\lim_{\eta\to 0}\frac{\text{span}_{d_{\alpha,\varepsilon}}(f,\varepsilon^{1-\alpha}\eta^{\alpha})}{|\log(\varepsilon^{1-\alpha}\eta^{\alpha})|}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lim_{\eta\to 0}\frac{\text{span}_{d}(f,\eta)}{\alpha|\log\eta|}\frac{|\log(\eta^{\alpha})|}{|\log(\varepsilon^{1-\alpha}\eta^{\alpha})|}

(5.1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\text{mdim}_{\text{M}}(\mathbb{M},d,f)}{\alpha}.

On the other hand, notice that, for any
x,y\in\mathbb{M}
such that
(d_{\alpha,\varepsilon})_{n}(x,y)<\varepsilon
, we have that
d_{n}(x,y)<\varepsilon
, because otherwise
(d_{\alpha,\varepsilon})_{n}(x,y)=d_{n}(x,y)\geq\varepsilon
. Let
E
be an
(n,f,\eta)
-spanning set of
(\mathbb{M},d_{\alpha,\varepsilon})
, where
\eta\in(0,\varepsilon)

y\in\mathbb{M}
, there exists

(d_{\alpha,\varepsilon})_{n}(x,y)=\varepsilon^{1-\alpha}d_{n}(x,y)^{\alpha}<\eta<\varepsilon\Rightarrow d_{n}(x,y)<\varepsilon^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}\eta^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}.

Thus,
E
is an
(n,f,\varepsilon^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}\eta^{\frac{1}{\alpha}})

(\mathbb{M},d)
and therefore

\text{span}_{d_{\alpha,\varepsilon}}(f,\eta)\geq\text{span}_{d}(f,\varepsilon^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}\eta^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}).

Hence,

\displaystyle\text{mdim}_{\text{M}}(\mathbb{M},f,d_{\alpha,\varepsilon})=\displaystyle\lim_{\eta\to 0}\frac{\text{span}_{d_{\alpha,\varepsilon}}(f,\eta)}{|\log(\eta)|}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lim_{\eta\to 0}\frac{\text{span}_{d}(f,\varepsilon^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}\eta^{\frac{1}{\alpha}})}{|\log(\varepsilon^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}\eta^{\frac{1}{\alpha}})|}\frac{|\log(\varepsilon^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}\eta^{\frac{1}{\alpha}})|}{|\log\eta|}

(5.2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lim_{\eta\to 0}\frac{\text{span}_{d}(f,\varepsilon^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}\eta^{\frac{1}{\alpha}})}{|\log(\varepsilon^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}\eta^{\frac{1}{\alpha}})|}\frac{|\log(\eta^{\frac{1}{\alpha}})|}{|\log\eta|}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\text{mdim}_{\text{M}}(\mathbb{M},f,d)}{\alpha}.

It follows from (

) and (

\text{mdim}_{\text{M}}(\mathbb{M},f,d_{\alpha,\varepsilon})=\frac{\text{mdim}_{\text{M}}(\mathbb{M},f,d)}{\alpha}.

Next, we prove the theorem for mean Hausdorff dimension. We will need the relation

\text{mdim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},f,d^{\alpha})=\frac{\text{mdim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},f,d)}{\alpha},\quad\text{ for any }\alpha\in(0,1),

which will be shown in Example
7.1
. Fix
\eta\in(0,\varepsilon)
. For every
x,y\in\mathbb{M}
with

we have that
(d_{\alpha,\epsilon})_{n}(x,y)=\varepsilon^{1-\alpha}d_{n}(x,y)^{\alpha}
. Thus, for all

such that
\text{diam}_{d_{n}^{\alpha}}(E)<\eta
, we have that
\text{diam}_{(d_{\alpha,\varepsilon})_{n}}(E)<\varepsilon^{1-\alpha}\eta

H_{\varepsilon^{1-\alpha}\eta}^{s}(\mathbb{M},(d_{\alpha,\varepsilon})_{n})\leq H_{\eta}^{s}(\mathbb{M},d_{n}^{\alpha}),\quad\text{for every }0<\eta<\epsilon.

\text{mdim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d_{\alpha,\varepsilon},f)\leq\text{mdim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d^{\alpha},f)=\frac{\text{mdim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d,f)}{\alpha}.

(5.3)

On the other hand, given
\eta\in(0,\varepsilon)
, we have for every

, with

, that

(d_{\alpha,\epsilon})_{n}(x,y)=\varepsilon^{1-\alpha}d_{n}(x,y)^{\alpha}>\eta^{1-\alpha}d_{n}(x,y)^{\alpha}.

Thus, for all

with
\text{diam}_{(d_{\alpha,\varepsilon})_{n}}(E)<\eta

\text{diam}_{d_{n}^{\alpha}}(E)<\eta^{\alpha}
. Therefore, we obtain that

H_{\eta}^{s}(\mathbb{M},(d_{\alpha,\varepsilon})_{n})\geq H_{\eta^{\alpha}}^{s}(\mathbb{M},d_{n}^{\alpha}).

Consequently,

\text{mdim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d_{\alpha,\varepsilon},f)\geq\text{mdim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d^{\alpha},f)=\frac{\text{mdim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d,f)}{\alpha}.

(5.4)

It follows from (

\text{mdim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d_{\alpha,\varepsilon},f)=\frac{\text{mdim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d,f)}{\alpha}.

Next, given that

and

6.
Composing metrics with subadditive continuous maps

In this section, we will consider metrics in the set

\mathcal{A}_{d}(\mathbb{M})=\{g_{d}\colon g_{d}(x,y)=g(d(x,y))\text{ for all }x,y\in\mathbb{M},\text{ and }g\in\mathcal{A}[0,\rho]\},

where
\rho
is the diameter of
\mathbb{M}
and

\mathcal{A}[0,\rho]=\left\{g:[0,\rho]\rightarrow[0,\infty):g\text{ is continuous, increasing, subadditive and }g^{-1}(0)=\{0\}\right\}.

Remember that
g:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
is called
subadditive
if
g(x+y)\leq g(x)+g(y)
for all
x,y.
For instance, if
g
is
concave
(that is, if
g(tx+(1-t)y)\geq tg(x)+(1-t)g(y)
, for any
t\in[0,1]
and
x,y\in[0,\rho]
) and
g(0)\geq 0
, then
g
is subadditive. In fact, if
g:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
is concave and
g(0)=0
, then
tg(x)\leq g(tx)
for any
t\in[0,1]
and
x\in[0,\infty)
. Hence, for any
x,y\in[0,\infty)
, taking
t=\frac{x}{x+y}\in[0,1]
, we have

g(x)=g(t(x+y))\geq tg(x+y)\quad\text{and}\quad g(y)=g((1-t)(x+y))\geq(1-t)g(x+y).

Therefore,
g(x)+g(y)\geq g(x+y).

Lemma 6.1
.

For any
g\in\mathcal{A}[0,\rho]
, we have that:

i)

g_{d}
is a metric on
\mathbb{M}
.

ii)

g_{d}\in\mathbb{M}(\tau)
. Consequently,
\mathcal{A}_{d}(\mathbb{M})\subseteq\mathbb{M}(\tau)
.

iii)

If
f:\mathbb{M}\rightarrow\mathbb{M}
is a continuous map, then, for any

and
x,y\in\mathbb{M}
, we have
({g_{d}})_{n}(x,y)=g(d_{n}(x,y))
.

Proof.

i) Clearly
g_{d}(x,y)\geq 0
and
g_{d}(x,y)=g_{d}(y,x)
hold. Furthermore, since
g^{-1}\{0\}=\{0\}
, we have

g_{d}(x,y)=0\Leftrightarrow g(d(x,y))=0\Leftrightarrow d(x,y)=0\Leftrightarrow x=y.

Next, since
g
is increasing, then, for
x,y,z\in\mathbb{M}
, it follows that

\displaystyle g_{d}(x,z)

\displaystyle=g(d(x,z))\leq g(d(x,y)+d(y,z))\leq g(d(x,y))+g(d(y,z))=g_{d}(x,z)+g_{d}(z,y).

Hence,
g_{d}
is a metric on
\mathbb{M}
.

ii) We prove that, given any
x\in\mathbb{M}
, then for any
\varepsilon>0
there is
\delta>0
such that

B_{d}(x,\delta)\subset B_{g_{d}}(x,\varepsilon),
where
B_{d^{\prime}}(x,\epsilon)
denotes the open ball with center
x
and radius
\epsilon>0
with respect a metric
d^{\prime}
.
Indeed, since
g
is continuous at 0 and
g^{-1}\{0\}=\{0\}
, for all
\varepsilon>0
, there is
\delta>0
such that if
0\leq a<\delta
, then
0\leq g(a)<\varepsilon.
Thus, for any
y\in\mathbb{M}
such that
d(x,y)<\delta
, we have
g(d(x,y))<\varepsilon
, that is,
g_{d}(x,y)<\varepsilon.
Therefore,
B_{d}(x,\delta)\subset B_{g_{d}}(x,\varepsilon).

Next, we prove for all
x\in\mathbb{M}
and each
\varepsilon>0
, there is

such that

B_{g_{d}}(x,\delta)\subset B_{d}(x,\varepsilon).
We show that if

, then

Indeed, if

, since

From this fact, setting

g(d(x,y))<\frac{g(\varepsilon)}{2}\quad\Rightarrow\quad d(x,y)<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}<\varepsilon.

Therefore
B_{g_{d}}(x,\delta)\subset B_{d}(x,\varepsilon).
If follows from the above facts that
g_{d}\in\mathbb{M}(\tau)
.

iii) Fix a continuous map
f:\mathbb{M}\rightarrow\mathbb{M}
. Since
g
is increasing, we have that

g(d(f^{m}(x),f^{m}(y)))=\max\{g(d(x,y)),g(d(f(x),f(y)))\dots,g(d(f^{n-1}(x),f^{n-1}(y)))\}

if and only if

Hence, given

that

\displaystyle(g_{d})_{n}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\max\{g_{d}(x,y),g_{d}(f(x),f(y))\dots,g_{d}(f^{n-1}(x),f^{n-1}(y))\}

\displaystyle=\max\{g(d(x,y)),g(d(f(x),f(y)))\dots,g(d(f^{n-1}(x),f^{n-1}(y)))\}

\displaystyle=g\left(\max\{d(x,y),d(f(x),f(y))\dots,d(f^{n-1}(x),f^{n-1}(y))\}\right)=g({d}_{n}(x,y)),

which proves iii).

Lemma 6.2
.

For any

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we can assume that
\rho\in(0,1)
. We prove that there exists
m\in(0,\infty)
such that

for any

Since

is subadditive, we have that

g(\rho)\leq 2g\left(\frac{\rho}{2}\right)\leq\cdots\leq 2^{n}g\left(\frac{\rho}{2^{n}}\right)\Rightarrow\frac{g(\rho)}{\rho}\leq\frac{g\left(\frac{\rho}{2}\right)}{\frac{\rho}{2}}\leq\cdots\leq\frac{g\left(\frac{\rho}{2^{n}}\right)}{\frac{\rho}{2^{n}}},

for any
n\in\mathbb{N}.

If

n\geq 0
such that

, and hence
\frac{2^{n}}{\rho}\leq\frac{1}{y}\leq\frac{2^{n+1}}{\rho}

\frac{g(\rho)}{\rho}\leq\frac{g\left(\frac{\rho}{2^{n+1}}\right)}{\frac{\rho}{2^{n+1}}}\leq\frac{g(y)}{\frac{\rho}{2^{n+1}}}=2\frac{g(y)}{\frac{\rho}{2^{n}}}\leq 2\frac{g(y)}{y}.

Therefore, taking
m=\frac{g(\rho)}{2\rho}
, we have that
my\leq g(y)
for any
y\in[0,\rho].
Thus, for any
x\in(0,\rho]
, we have that

\log mx\leq\log g(x)\Rightarrow-\log g(x)\leq-\log mx\Rightarrow\frac{\log g(x)}{\log x}\leq\frac{\log mx}{\log x}.

Given that
\frac{\log mx}{\log x}\rightarrow 1
, as
x\rightarrow 0
, we have that
k_{m}(g)\leq k_{M}(g)\leq 1
.
∎

\tilde{g}(x)=\begin{cases}\frac{\log g(x)}{\log x},\text{ if }x\in(0,\rho]\\
k(g),\text{ if }x=0.\end{cases}

Note that
\tilde{g}:[0,\rho]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a continuous map. Specifically,
\tilde{g}
is continuous at 0, because

\tilde{g}(0)=k(g)=\lim_{x\rightarrow 0}\tilde{g}(x).

Next, fix

h\in\tilde{B}(g,\varepsilon)
. Given that
\rho<1
, then for any
x\in(0,\rho]
we have that

\displaystyle x^{\varepsilon}g(x)<h(x)<\frac{g(x)}{x^{\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle\iff x^{\varepsilon}<\frac{h(x)}{g(x)}<\frac{1}{x^{\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle\iff\varepsilon\log x<\log h(x)-\log g(x)<-\varepsilon\log x.

Therefore,
-\varepsilon<\tilde{g}(x)-\tilde{h}(x)<\varepsilon
for any
x\in(0,\rho]
. Thus,
|k(g)-k(h)|=|\tilde{g}(0)-\tilde{h}(0)|\leq\varepsilon
, by the continuity of both
\tilde{g}
and
\tilde{h}
. This fact proves that
g\mapsto k(g)
is a continuous map.
∎

For the next results, we will consider the set

{\mathcal{A}}^{+}_{d}(\mathbb{M})=\{g\circ d\in\mathcal{A}_{d}(\mathbb{M}):g\in{\mathcal{A}}^{+}[0,\rho]\}.

Notice that
{\mathcal{A}}^{+}_{d}(\mathbb{M})\neq\emptyset
, because the function
g(x)=x^{a}
, for a fixed
a\in(0,1]
, belongs to
\mathcal{A}^{+}[0,\rho]
(see Example
7.1
). In particular,
d\in{\mathcal{A}}^{+}_{d}(\mathbb{M})
.

Lemma 6.6
.

Let
\mathbb{M}
be a compact space such that the metric map
d:\mathbb{M}\times\mathbb{M}\rightarrow[0,\rho]
is surjective. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}:{\mathcal{A}}^{+}[0,\rho]

\displaystyle\to{\mathcal{A}}^{+}_{d}(\mathbb{M})

\displaystyle g

\displaystyle\mapsto g\circ d

is a bijective map.

Proof.

Clearly
\mathcal{Z}
is surjective. Next, we prove that for any
\tilde{d}\in{\mathcal{A}}^{+}_{d}(\mathbb{M})
, there exists a unique
g_{\tilde{d}}\in{\mathcal{A}}^{+}[0,\rho]
such that
\tilde{d}=g\circ d
. Suppose that
g_{1},g_{2}\in{\mathcal{A}}^{+}[0,\rho]
and
\tilde{d}=g_{1}\circ d=g_{2}\circ{d}
. Since
d
is surjective, for any
t\in[0,\rho]
, there exist
x,y\in\mathbb{M}
such that
t=d(x,y)
. Therefore,
g_{1}(t)=g_{2}(t)
, as we want to prove.
∎

Suppose that
d:\mathbb{M}\times\mathbb{M}\rightarrow[0,\rho]
is surjective. We will equip
{\mathcal{A}}^{+}_{d}(\mathbb{M})
with the topology
\mathcal{W}
which becomes the map

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}:({\mathcal{A}}^{+}[0,\rho],\mathcal{T})

\displaystyle\to({\mathcal{A}}^{+}_{d}(\mathbb{M}),\mathcal{W})

\displaystyle g

\displaystyle\mapsto d

a homeomorphism.

Theorem 6.7
.

Let
\mathbb{M}
be a compact space such that the metric map
d:\mathbb{M}\times\mathbb{M}\rightarrow[0,\rho]
is surjective.
Suppose that
\emph{mdim}_{\emph{M}}(\mathbb{M},f,d)<\infty
. The maps

\displaystyle\overline{\emph{mdim}}_{\emph{M}}(\mathbb{M},f)\colon({\mathcal{A}}^{+}_{d}(\mathbb{M}),\mathcal{W})

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle g_{d}

\displaystyle\mapsto\overline{\emph{mdim}}_{\emph{M}}(\mathbb{M},g_{d},f)

and

\displaystyle\underline{\emph{mdim}}_{\emph{M}}(\mathbb{M},f)\colon({\mathcal{A}}^{+}_{d}(\mathbb{M}),\mathcal{W})

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle g_{d}

\displaystyle\mapsto\underline{\emph{mdim}}_{\emph{M}}(\mathbb{M},g_{d},f)

are continuous.

Proof.

We prove the case
\overline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{M}}(\mathbb{M},f)\colon{\mathcal{A}}^{+}_{d}(\mathbb{M})\to\mathbb{R}
, since the proof of the theorem is analogous for the case
\underline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{M}}(\mathbb{M},f)\colon{\mathcal{A}}^{+}_{d}(\mathbb{M})\to\mathbb{R}
. If
\overline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{M}}(\mathbb{M},f,d)=0
, it follows from Lemma
6.4
that
\overline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{M}}(\mathbb{M},f)\colon{\mathcal{A}}^{+}_{d}(\mathbb{M})\to\mathbb{R}
is the zero map.

We will suppose that
0<\overline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{M}}(\mathbb{M},f,d)<\infty
. Take
\tilde{d}
in
{\mathcal{A}}^{+}_{d}(\mathbb{M})
and let
g_{\tilde{d}}
be the unique map in
{\mathcal{A}}^{+}[0,\rho]
such that
\tilde{d}=g_{\tilde{d}}\circ d
. From Lemma
6.4
, we have that

\overline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{M}}(\mathbb{M},f)(\tilde{d})=\overline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{M}}(\mathbb{M},f)(g_{\tilde{d}}\circ d)=\frac{\overline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{M}}(\mathbb{M},d,f)}{k(g_{\tilde{d}})}.

Hence, the continuity of
\overline{\text{mdim}}_{\text{M}}(\mathbb{M},f)\colon{\mathcal{A}}^{+}_{d}(\mathbb{M})\to\mathbb{R}
follows from Lemma
6.5
and given that
k(g)>0
for any
g\in{\mathcal{A}}^{+}[0,\rho]
.
∎

7.
Additional examples

In this section we will present some examples of maps
g\in{\mathcal{A}}^{+}[0,\rho]
and the respective expressions for
\text{mdim}_{\text{M}}(\mathbb{M},g_{d},f)
.

Example 7.1
.

Fix any
a\in(0,1]
. Consider the function
g(x)=x^{a}
defined for all
x\in[0,\infty)
. Notice that
g(x+y)\leq g(x)+g(y)
for any
x,y\geq 0
. Next, by defining
g_{d}(x,y)=d(x,y)^{a}
, we find that
k(g)=a
, and therefore

\text{mdim}_{\text{M}}(\mathbb{M},g_{d},f)=\frac{\text{mdim}_{\text{M}}(\mathbb{M},d,f)}{a}.

(7.1)

For instance, we have that

\text{mdim}_{\text{M}}(([0,1]^{n})^{\mathbb{Z}},h_{\textbf{d}},\sigma)=\frac{n}{a},

(7.2)

where is the metric defined in Theorem
3.6
and
\sigma:([0,1]^{n})^{\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow([0,1]^{n})^{\mathbb{Z}}
is the left shift.

Example 7.2
.

Fix any
a\in(0,1]
. Consider the function
g(x)=x^{a}
defined for all
x\in[0,\infty)
. We will prove that

\text{mdim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},g_{d},f)=\frac{1}{a}\text{mdim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d,f).

(7.3)

In fact, consider a fixed
a\in(0,1]
. In fact, consider any
a\in(0,1]
fixed. Given any
\eta>0
, we have that
d(x,y)\leq\eta
if and only if
d(x,y)^{a}\leq\eta^{a}
. Hence, it follows that

\displaystyle\text{H}_{\eta^{a}}^{s}(\mathbb{M},(g_{d})_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf\left\{\Sigma_{k=1}^{\infty}(\text{diam}_{d_{n}^{a}}(E_{k}))^{s}:\mathbb{M}=\cup_{k=1}^{\infty}E_{k}\text{ with }\text{diam}_{d_{n}^{a}}(E_{k})<\eta^{a}\text{ for all }k\geq 1\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf\left\{\Sigma_{k=1}^{\infty}(\text{diam}_{d_{n}^{a}}(E_{k}))^{s}:\mathbb{M}=\cup_{k=1}^{\infty}E_{k}\text{ with }\text{diam}_{d_{n}}(E_{k})<\eta\text{ for all }k\geq 1\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf\left\{\Sigma_{k=1}^{\infty}(\text{diam}_{d_{n}}(E_{k}))^{as}:\mathbb{M}=\cup_{k=1}^{\infty}E_{k}\text{ with }\text{diam}_{d_{n}}(E_{k})<\eta\text{ for all }k\geq 1\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{H}_{\eta}^{as}(\mathbb{M},d_{n}).

Hence,

\displaystyle\dim_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},(g_{d})_{n},\eta^{a})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\{s\geq 0:\text{H}_{\eta^{a}}^{s}(\mathbb{M},(g_{d})_{n})\geq 1\}=\sup\{s\geq 0:\text{H}_{\eta}^{as}(\mathbb{M},d_{n})\geq 1\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{a}\sup\{as\geq 0:\text{H}_{\eta}^{as}(\mathbb{M},d_{n})\geq 1\}=\frac{1}{a}\dim_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d_{n},\eta),

This fact proves (
7.3
).

Let
f:\mathbb{M}\rightarrow\mathbb{M}
be a continuous map such that
\text{mdim}_{\text{M}}(\mathbb{M},d,f)>0
. It follows from Example
7.1
that the image of the map

\text{mdim}_{\text{M}}(\mathbb{M},f)\colon{\mathcal{A}}^{+}_{d}(\mathbb{M})\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}

contains the interval
[\text{mdim}_{\text{M}}(\mathbb{M},d,f),\infty)
. Hence,

\sup_{d^{\prime}\in\mathbb{M}(\tau)}\text{mdim}_{\text{M}}(\mathbb{M},d^{\prime},f)=\infty.

Similar fact holds for the mean Hausdorff dimension.

Example 7.3
.

Consider
g(x)=\log(1+x)
. Since
1+x+y\leq 1+x+y+xy,
we have

\displaystyle g(x+y)

\displaystyle=\log(1+x+y)\leq\log((1+x)(1+y))=\log(1+x)+\log(1+y)=g(x)+g(y).

Hence,
g
is subadditive.
Note that if
g_{1}
and
g_{2}\in{\mathcal{A}}^{+}[0,\infty)
, then
g_{1}\circ g_{2}\in{\mathcal{A}}^{+}[0,\infty)
. Consider
g_{1}(x)=x^{a}
, for
a\in(0,1)
, and
g_{2}(x)=\log(1+x)
. The composition
h(x)=g_{2}\circ g_{1}(x)=\log(1+x^{a})
belongs to
{\mathcal{A}}^{+}[0,\infty)
. We can prove that
k(h)=a
. Hence

\text{mdim}_{\text{M}}(\mathbb{M},h_{d},f)=\frac{\text{mdim}_{\text{M}}(\mathbb{M},d,f)}{a}.

Example 7.4
.

Suppose that
h:\mathbb{M}\rightarrow\mathbb{M}
is
\alpha
-Hölder for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
, that is, there exists
K>0
such that

d(h(x),h(y))\leq Kd(x,y)^{\alpha}\quad\text{for all }x,y\in\mathbb{M}.

Setting
d_{h}(x,y)=d(h(x),h(y))
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{M}
, we have respectively from Examples
7.1
and
7.2
that

\text{mdim}_{\text{M}}(\mathbb{M},d_{h},f)\leq\text{mdim}_{\text{M}}(\mathbb{M},d^{\alpha},f)=\frac{\text{mdim}_{\text{M}}(\mathbb{M},d,f)}{\alpha}

and

\text{mdim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d_{h},f)\leq\text{mdim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d^{\alpha},f)=\frac{\text{mdim}_{\text{H}}(\mathbb{M},d,f)}{\alpha}.

If
\mathbb{M}
is a compact Riemannian manifold with
\text{dim}(\mathbb{M})\geq 2
, then the set
\mathcal{G}
consisting of homeomorphisms with positive metric mean dimension is residual in
\text{Hom}(\mathbb{M})
(see
[
6
]
). Therefore, for any
f\in\mathcal{G}
, we have

0=\text{mdim}(\mathbb{M},f)<\sup_{d^{\prime}\in\mathbb{M}(\tau)}\text{mdim}_{\text{M}}(\mathbb{M},d^{\prime},f)=\sup_{d^{\prime}\in\mathbb{M}(\tau)}\text{dim}_{\text{B}}(\mathbb{M},d^{\prime})=\infty,

where the first equality is because
\mathbb{M}
is finite dimensional (see
[
16
]
, page 6). Similar result holds for the case of mean Hausdorff dimension, following the facts proved in
[
2
]
.

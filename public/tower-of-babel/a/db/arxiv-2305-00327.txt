Existence and instability of standing waves for the biharmonic nonlinear Schrödinger equation with combined nonlinearities
Xiaojun Chang, Hichem Hajaiej, Zhouji Ma, Linjie Song

Abstract
We consider the following biharmonic nonlinear Schrödinger equation with combined nonlinearities i ∂ t ψ − Δ 2 ψ + μ | ψ | q − 2 ψ + | ψ | p − 2 ψ = 0 , ( t , x ) ∈ ℝ × ℝ N , \displaystyle i\partial_{t}\psi-\Delta^{2}\psi+\mu|\psi|^{q-2}\psi+|\psi|^{p-2}\psi=0,\qquad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}, where N ≥ 5 , ψ ∈ ℝ × ℝ N → ℂ , μ > 0 , 2 + 8 N ≤ q < p ≤ 4 ∗ N\geq 5,\ \psi\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{C},\ \mu>0,\ 2+\frac{8}{N}\leq q<p\leq 4^{*} and 4 ∗ = 2 ​ N N − 4 4^{*}=\frac{2N}{N-4} . We prove the existence of normalized ground state solutions for the biharmonic Schrödinger equation with combined nonlinearities and show that all ground states correspond to the local minima of the associated energy functional restricted to the Pohozaev set. Moreover, we prove that the standing waves are strongly unstable by blowup. In particular, our results cover the Sobolev case p = 4 ∗ = 2 ​ N N − 4 p=4^{*}=\frac{2N}{N-4} . Our method is novel and innovative as previous ideas cannot be used in many cases under this study.

Introduction and main results

In the last decades, there has been a growing interest in the study of the biharmonic Schrödinger equation

i\partial_{t}\psi+\gamma\Delta^{2}\psi+\epsilon\Delta\psi+f(|\psi|)\psi=0,

(1.1)

where
N\geq 5,\gamma\not=0,\ \epsilon=\pm 1

\epsilon=0

\psi:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}

[

28

[
5

obtained necessary Strichartz estimates for the biharmonic operator
\Delta^{2}
. Here, we are particularly interested in the case
\epsilon=0

[
22

43

to study the stability of solitons in magnetic materials once the effective quasi particle mass becomes infinite. Fibich, Ilan and Papanicolaou
[
17

described some properties of the biharmonic Schrödinger equation in the subcritical case. Pausader
[
38

proved the global well-posedness and scattering theory under some conditions for the mass critical biharmonic Schrödinger equation. The mass supercritical and Sobolev subcritical regime were considered in
[
20
]
. For the global well-posedness and scattering of the focusing Sobolev critical biharmonic Schrödinger equations, one can refer to
[
35
]
and
[
37
]
. For more related studies, the reader can see
[
19
,

for the biharmonic Schrödinger equation, and
[

for the biharmonic Schrödinger equation with mixed-dispersion term.

In this paper, we are concerned with the existence and orbital instability of ground state solutions having prescribed mass, also called normalized ground state solutions for the following biharmonic nonlinear Schrödinger equation with combined nonlinearities

i\partial_{t}\psi-\Delta^{2}\psi+\mu|\psi|^{q-2}\psi+|\psi|^{p-2}\psi=0,\quad\psi(0,x)=\psi_{0}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N},

(1.2)

where
N\geq 5,\ \psi:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{C},\ \mu>0,\ 2+\frac{8}{N}\leq q<p\leq 4^{*}
and
4^{*}=\frac{2N}{N-4}
.

Firstly, we recall that the standing waves of (
1.2
), are solutions of the form
\psi(t,x)=e^{i\omega t}u(x),\ \omega\in\mathbb{R}
. Then the ansatz yields the elliptic equation

\Delta^{2}u+\omega u-\mu|u|^{q-2}u-|u|^{p-2}u=0,\quad\mathrm{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N}.

(1.3)

While looking for solutions to (
1.3
), one reasonable approach is to fix
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
and search for the critical points of the following action functional

A_{p,q}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{\omega}{2}\|u\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{\mu}{q}\|u\|_{q}^{q}-\frac{1}{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p}.

Alternatively, we can search for the solutions to (
1.3
) with prescribed mass, which is a meaningful problem from both mathematical and physical perspectives. Indeed, the mass of a quantum system is a fundamental property that is related to the probability density of the system. In quantum mechanics, the squared modulus of the wave function represents the probability density of finding the system in a particular state. For normalized solutions, the total probability of finding the system in any state is conserved over time, as the mass is conserved.
By considering solutions to the biharmonic Schrödinger equation with prescribed mass, we can gain insights into the properties of the system in a consistent and meaningful way. Furthermore, studying the solutions to the biharmonic Schrödinger equation with prescribed mass
can lead to interesting mathematical challenges and the discovery of new tools and insights.

We define on
H^{2}=H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{C})
the energy functional

E_{p,q}(u):=\frac{1}{2}\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{\mu}{q}\|u\|_{q}^{q}-\frac{1}{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p}.

It is standard to check that
E_{p,q}
is of class
C^{1}
and that a critical point of
E_{p,q}
restricted to the mass constraint

S(c)=\{u\in H^{2}:\|u\|_{2}^{2}=c\}

corresponds to a solution of (
1.3
) with prescribed mass
\|u\|_{2}^{2}=c
. Here, the parameter
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
is unknown and it appears as a Lagrange multiplier. In this paper, we are interested in the solutions that minimize
E_{p,q}(u)
among all non-trivial solutions, namely ground state solutions. We define it in the following way:

Definition 1.1
.

We say that a solution
u_{c}\in S(c)
to (
1.3
) is a ground state solution to (
1.3
) if it is a solution with the least energy among all the solutions that belong to
S(c)
. Namely, it satisfies

E_{p,q}(u_{c})=\inf\{E_{p,q}(u),u\in S(c),(E_{p,q}|_{S(c)})^{{}^{\prime}}(u)=0\}.

In recent years, the study of the normalized ground state solutions of nonlinear Schrödinger type equations has been a hot topic. Particularly, there have been several results for the mixed-dispersion fourth-order Schrödinger equations. However, there are few references to the corresponding study of biharmonic Schrödinger equation with mixed nonlinearities in the literature. Recently, in
[
34
]
, the authors proved the existence of the normalized ground state solutions to (
1.2
) for
2<q<2+\frac{8}{N}<p=4^{*}
. In this paper, we will study the normalized ground state solutions to (
1.2
) in the remaining regimes.

Under the assumption
2+\frac{8}{N}\leq q<p\leq 4^{*}
,
E_{p,q}
restricted to
S(c)
is unbounded from below. To overcome this difficulty, we construct a subset of
S(c)
on which
E_{p,q}
is bounded from below and coercive. We exploit a natural constraint called Pohozaev set of
E_{p,q}
restricted to
S(c)
, in which all the critical points of
E_{p,q}|_{S(c)}
belong to this Pohozaev set. The set is defined as follows

\mathcal{Q}_{p,q}(c)=\{u\in S(c):Q_{p,q}(u)=0\},

where

Q_{p,q}(u)=\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{2}-\mu\gamma_{q}\|u\|_{q}^{q}-\gamma_{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p}.

with

\gamma_{r}=\frac{N(r-2)}{4r}=\frac{N}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{r}),\forall r\in(2,4^{*}].

Hence it is natural to consider the minimization problem on this Pohozaev set

m_{p,q}(c)=\inf\limits_{u\in\mathcal{Q}_{p,q}(c)}E_{p,q}(u).

Finally in this section, we will discuss the strong instability of standing waves to (
1.2
). First we recall the definition of instability below.

Definition 1.5
.

We say that a solution
u\in H^{2}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})
to (
1.3
) is unstable by blowup in finite time, if for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists a
v\in H^{2}({\mathbb{R}^{N}})
such that
\|v-u\|_{H^{2}}<\epsilon
and the solution
\psi(t)
to (
1.2
) with initial data
\psi_{0}=v
blows up in finite time.

The instability of standing waves to nonlinear Schrödinger equations has been extensively studied in the literature, we refer the reader to
[
21
,
29
,
40
,
41
]
and the references therein. The strategy for proving instability is often based on the variational characterization of the ground states on the Pohozaev or Nehari manifold, see
[
12
]
for example. The key point is to show the following inequality

Q_{p,q}(\psi)\leq E_{p,q}(\psi_{0})-E_{p,q}(u),

where
u
is the corresponding ground state. Then by using the virial identity, we can obtain a differential inequality as follows

\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x\psi(t)|^{2}dx=8Q_{p,q}(\psi(t))\leq-(E_{p,q}(u)-E_{p,q}(\psi_{0}))<0.

This implies that the solution
\psi(t)
blows up in finite time.

While for the biharmonic nonlinear Schrödinger equation with mixed dispersion

i\partial\psi-\gamma\Delta^{2}\psi+\epsilon\Delta\psi+|\psi|^{2\sigma}\psi=0,\qquad\gamma>0,\ \epsilon\in\mathbb{R},\ 4\leq\sigma\leq\frac{4}{N-4},

(1.4)

the virial identity is not available due to the appearance of the operator
\Delta^{2}
.
Boulenger and Lenzmann
[
12
]
used a localized version of the virial identity from
[
35
,
37
]
to prove blowup results for radial solutions in
H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
of (
1.4
) with
\gamma=1
under various criteria in the mass-critical, mass-supercritical and Sobolev critical cases. In particular when
\epsilon=0
, they assumed that the energy of the initial data was either negative or non-negative with some additional conditions on the initial data. Bonheure et al.
[
9
]
proved the instability by blowup of the radial ground state solutions for (
1.4
) when
\epsilon=0
in both the mass-critical and mass-supercritical regimes. Later, in
[
8
]
, they showed that the instability of the radial normalized ground state solutions can be proved by blowup with
\epsilon=1
when
4\leq\sigma<\frac{4}{N-4}
.

In the present paper, we aim to study the instability by blowup for ground states of (
1.2
). To the best of our knowledge, so far, there has been no existing instability results for the biharmonic nonlinear Schrödinger equations with combined nonlinearities. We will borrow an idea from Boulenger and Lenzmann
[
12
]
to study this issue. However, we cannot directly use the arguments from Boulenger and Lenzmann’s work
due to the fact that the energy of the initial data satisfies
E_{p,q}(\psi_{0})>0
without any additional assumptions on
\psi_{0}
. Therefore, we need to come up with a different approach to establish instability results for (
1.2
).

More precisely, in order to study the instability of (
1.2
), one of the main challenges is to obtain the following estimate

\frac{d}{dt}M_{\varphi_{R}}[\psi(t)]\leq-\delta\|\Delta\psi(t)\|^{2}_{2},\qquad\delta>0,

(1.5)

where the modified energy
M_{\varphi_{R}}[\psi(t)]
is defined in (
6.2
). This estimate is crucial for establishing the instability result. However, there are several terms in the inequality that make it difficult to estimate (
1.5

\mu|u|_{q}^{q}
or
|u|_{p}^{p}
that remains in the inequality after applying variational arguments.
Another challenge is that the term
|\Delta\psi(t)|^{2}_{2}
in (
1.5
) may be unbounded, which further complicates the estimation process. To overcome these challenges, a key point is to carefully choose a suitable bound for
|\Delta\psi(t)|^{2}_{2}
, which can be used to
obtain the desired estimate for
\frac{d}{dt}M_{\varphi_{R}}[u(t)]
.

In what follows, we state our main instability results in Sobolev subcritical case and Sobolev critical case respectively.

Theorem 1.6

Theorem 1.7

Preliminaries

In this section, we present some useful lemmas to be often used in this paper.

Take

\|u\|_{H^{2}}=(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\Delta u|^{2}+|u|^{2}dx)^{\frac{1}{2}}

to be the norm of
H^{2}
, which is equivalent to the standard one

\|u\|_{H^{2}}=(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|D^{2}u|^{2}+|\nabla u|^{2}+|u|^{2}dx)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We first recall the well-known Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality for any
u\in H^{2}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
N\geq 5
and
2<r<4^{*}
. Then the following sharp Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality

\|u\|_{r}^{r}\leq C^{r}_{N,r}\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{r\gamma_{r}}\|u\|_{2}^{r(1-\gamma_{r})},

(2.1)

holds for any
u\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, where
C_{N,r}
denotes the sharp constant.

Then, by similar arguments as in
[
46
]
, we have the following Lions’ type lemma in
H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
R>0
. If
\{u_{n}\}
is bounded in
H^{2}
and if

\sup\limits_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{B(y,R)}|u_{n}|^{2}dx\rightarrow 0,\quad\mathrm{as}\quad n\rightarrow\infty,

then
u_{n}\rightarrow 0
in
L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for
2<p<4^{*}
.

For any
u\in S(c)
, we define

u_{t}(x)=t^{\frac{N}{4}}u(\sqrt{t}x).

(2.2)

Clearly,
u_{t}\in S(c)
for any
t>0
. Furthermore,

E_{p,q}(u_{t})=\frac{1}{2}t^{2}\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{\mu}{q}t^{\frac{N(q-2)}{4}}\|u\|_{q}^{q}-\frac{1}{p}t^{\frac{N(p-2)}{4}}\|u\|_{p}^{p}

(2.3)

and

Q_{p,q}(u_{t})=t^{2}\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{2}-\mu\gamma_{q}t^{\frac{N(q-2)}{4}}\|u\|_{q}^{q}-\gamma_{p}t^{\frac{N(p-2)}{4}}\|u\|_{p}^{p}.

Then one has the following properties for
E_{p,q}(u_{t})
and
Q_{p,q}(u_{t})
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
N\geq 5
,
c>0
,
\mu>0
and
2+\frac{8}{N}\leq q<p\leq 4^{*}
. If
q=2+\frac{8}{N}
, we further assume that
\mu c^{\frac{4}{N}}<\frac{N+4}{NC^{q}_{N,q}}
. Then for any

, there exists a unique

, then

.

Proof.

First we let

Q_{p,q}(u_{t})=t^{2}(\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{2}-\mu\gamma_{q}t^{\frac{N(q-2)}{4}-2}\|u\|_{q}^{q}-\gamma_{p}t^{\frac{N(p-2)}{4}-2}\|u\|_{p}^{p})=t^{2}h(t).

\frac{N(p-2)}{4}-2>\frac{N(q-2)}{4}-2>0

for

has a unique zero
t_{u}

By direct calculations, we have
\frac{d}{dt}E_{p,q}(u_{t})=t^{-1}Q_{p,q}(u_{t})

E_{p,q}(u_{t_{u}})=\max_{t\in(0,+\infty)}E_{p,q}(u_{t})

Now writing

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{d^{2}t}E_{p,q}(u_{t})

\displaystyle=\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{2}-\mu\gamma_{q}(\frac{N(q-2)}{4}-1)t^{\frac{N(q-2)}{4}-2}\|u\|_{q}^{q}-\gamma_{p}(\frac{N(p-2)}{4}-1)t^{\frac{N(p-2)}{4}-2}\|u\|_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle=\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{2}-\mu\gamma_{q}(\frac{N(q-2)}{4}-1)s^{\frac{N(q-2)}{4}-2}t_{u}^{\frac{N(q-2)}{4}-2}\|u\|_{q}^{q}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{t_{u}^{2}}\big[t_{u}^{2}\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{2}-\mu\gamma_{q}(\frac{N(q-2)}{4}-1)s^{\frac{N(q-2)}{4}-2}t_{u}^{\frac{N(q-2)}{4}}\|u\|_{q}^{q}

\displaystyle-\gamma_{p}(\frac{N(p-2)}{4}-1)s^{\frac{N(p-2)}{4}-2}t_{u}^{\frac{N(p-2)}{4}}\|u\|_{p}^{p}\big].

we infer that

, it follows that

. This completes the proof.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
2+\frac{8}{N}\leq q<p\leq 4^{*}
. For any
u\in S(c)
, the map
u\mapsto t_{u}
is of class
C^{1}
, where

Proof.

For
2+\frac{8}{N}\leq q<p\leq 4^{*}
and
u\in S(c)
, we consider the
C^{1}
function
\Psi:\mathbb{R}\times S(c)\mapsto\mathbb{R}
defined by
\Psi(t,u)=\psi^{\prime}_{u}(t)
, where
\psi_{u}(t)=E_{p,q}(u_{t})
. By direct computation and the definition of
t_{u}
, we have

\Psi(t_{u},u)=\frac{1}{t_{u}}Q_{p,q}(u_{t_{u}})=0\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\partial_{t}\Psi(t_{u},u)=\psi^{\prime\prime}_{u}(t_{u})<0.

By the Implicit Function Theorem, we can get that the map
u\mapsto t_{u}
is of class
C^{1}
.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
N\geq 5
,
c>0
,
\mu>0
and
2+\frac{8}{N}\leq q<p\leq 4^{*}
. If
q=2+\frac{8}{N}
, we further assume that
\mu c^{\frac{4}{N}}<\frac{N+4}{NC^{q}_{N,q}}
. Then,

(i)\ \mathcal{Q}_{p,q}(c)\not=\emptyset;

(ii)\ \inf\limits_{u\in\mathcal{Q}_{p,q}(c)}\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{2}>0;

(iii)\ m_{p,q}(c)>0;

(iv)\ E_{p,q}
is coercive on
\mathcal{Q}_{p,q}(c)
, that is
E_{p,q}(u_{n})\rightarrow+\infty
for any
\{u_{n}\}\subset\mathcal{Q}_{p,q}(c)
with
\|u_{n}\|_{H^{2}}\rightarrow\infty
.

Proof.

(i)
By Lemma
2.3
, for any
u\in S(c)
, there always exists
t_{u}>0
such that
u_{t_{u}}\in\mathcal{Q}_{p,q}(c)
, it follows that
\mathcal{Q}_{p,q}(c)\neq\emptyset
.

(ii)
We divide the proof into two cases.

Case
1:

(p<4^{*})\
For any
u\in\mathcal{Q}_{p,q}(c)
, by the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\mu\gamma_{q}\|u\|_{q}^{q}+\gamma_{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\mu\gamma_{q}C^{q}_{N,q}\|u\|_{2}^{q(1-\gamma_{q})}\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{q\gamma_{q}}+\gamma_{p}C^{p}_{N,p}\|u\|_{2}^{p(1-\gamma_{p})}\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{p\gamma_{p}}

\displaystyle=\mu\gamma_{q}C^{q}_{N,q}(\sqrt{c})^{q(1-\gamma_{q})}\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{q\gamma_{q}}+\gamma_{p}C^{p}_{N,p}(\sqrt{c})^{p(1-\gamma_{p})}\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{p\gamma_{p}}.

If
2+\frac{8}{N}<q<p
, then
p\gamma_{p}>q\gamma_{q}>2
. It follows that there exists a constant
C>0
such that
\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{2}\geq C
.
If
2+\frac{8}{N}=q<p
and
\mu c^{\frac{4}{N}}<\frac{N+4}{NC^{q}_{N,q}}
, then
p\gamma_{p}>q\gamma_{q}=2
and
\frac{\mu NC^{q}_{N,q}}{N+4}c^{\frac{4}{N}}<1
. This also implies that there exists a constant
C>0
such that
\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{2}\geq C
.

Case
2:

(p=4^{*})\
The proof here is similar to Case 1. The only difference is that we estimate the term
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{4^{*}}dx
by using

\mathcal{S}\|u\|^{2}_{4^{*}}\leq\|\Delta u\|^{2}_{2}.

(iii)
If
2+\frac{8}{N}<q<p
, we can easily get
\mu\gamma_{q}\|u\|_{q}^{q}+\gamma_{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p}=\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{2}\geq C
. If
2+\frac{8}{N}=q<p
and
\mu c^{\frac{4}{N}}<\frac{N+4}{NC^{q}_{N,q}}
, it follows that

\gamma_{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p}\geq(1-\frac{\mu NC^{q}_{N,q}}{N+4}c^{\frac{4}{N}})\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{2}\geq C(1-\frac{\mu NC^{q}_{N,q}}{N+4}c^{\frac{4}{N}}).

(2.4)

Then, for any
u\in\mathcal{Q}_{p,q}(c)
, we have

\displaystyle E_{p,q}(u)

\displaystyle=(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q\gamma_{q}})\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{2}+(\frac{\gamma_{p}}{q\gamma_{q}}-\frac{1}{p})\|u\|_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle=(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p\gamma_{p}})\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{2}+(\frac{\gamma_{q}}{p\gamma_{p}}-\frac{1}{q})\mu\|u\|_{q}^{q}

\displaystyle=(\frac{\gamma_{p}}{2}-\frac{1}{p})\|u\|_{p}^{p}+(\frac{\gamma_{q}}{2}-\frac{1}{q})\mu\|u\|_{q}^{q}\geq C_{1}

(2.5)

for some
C_{1}>0
, which implies that
m_{p,q}(c)>0
.

(iv)
By (
2
) it is easily seen that the conclusion holds.
∎

Since for given
c>0
, one can see from Lemma
2.5
that the infimum

m_{p,q}(c)=\inf\limits_{u\in\mathcal{Q}_{p,q}(c)}E_{p,q}(u)

is well-defined and strictly positive. We will characterize further the behavior of
m_{p,q}(c)
when
c>0
varies.

Lemma 2.6
.

The function
c\mapsto m_{p,q}(c)
is non-increasing on
(0,+\infty)
.

Proof.

First we set
\gamma(c)=\inf\limits_{u\in S(c)}\max\limits_{t>0}E_{p,q}(u_{t})
. We claim that
\gamma(c)=m_{p,q}(c)
.
Indeed, for any
u\in S(c)
, by Lemma
2.3
, there exists a
t_{u}>0
such that
u_{t_{u}}\in\mathcal{Q}_{p,q}(c)
and
\max\limits_{t>0}E_{p,q}(u_{t})=E_{p,q}(u_{t_{u}})\geq m_{p,q}(c)
, thus we get
\gamma(c)\geq m_{p,q}(c)
. On the other hand, for any
u\in\mathcal{Q}_{p,q}(c),\ \max\limits_{t>0}E_{p,q}(u_{t})=E_{p,q}(u)
, this immediately implies that
\gamma(c)\leq m_{p,q}(c)
. Then we conclude that
\gamma(c)=m_{p,q}(c)
.

Next we assume that
0<c_{1}<c_{2}<+\infty
. For any
u\in S(c_{1})
, define
v(x)=(\frac{c_{1}}{c_{2}})^{\frac{N-4}{8}}u((\frac{c_{1}}{c_{2}})^{\frac{1}{4}}x),\ \forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. then we have the followings:

\|v\|_{2}^{2}=c_{2},\quad\|\Delta v\|_{2}^{2}=\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{2},

\|v\|_{q}^{q}=(\frac{c_{1}}{c_{2}})^{\frac{q(N-4)-2N}{8}}\|u\|_{q}^{q},\quad\|v\|_{p}^{p}=(\frac{c_{1}}{c_{2}})^{\frac{p(N-4)-2N}{8}}\|u\|_{p}^{p}.

By the definition of
m_{p,q}(c)
and the fact
\gamma(c)=m_{p,q}(c)
, we have

\displaystyle m_{p,q}(c_{2})

\displaystyle\leq\max_{t\in(0,+\infty)}E_{p,q}(v_{t})

\displaystyle=E_{p,q}(v_{t_{v}})=E_{p,q}(t_{v}^{\frac{N}{4}}v(\sqrt{t_{v}}x))

\displaystyle=E_{p,q}(t_{v}^{\frac{N}{4}}(\frac{c_{1}}{c_{2}})^{\frac{N-4}{8}}u((\frac{c_{1}}{c_{2}})^{\frac{1}{4}}\sqrt{t_{v}}x))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\Delta u_{t_{v}}|^{2}dx-(\frac{c_{1}}{c_{2}})^{\frac{q(N-4)-2N}{8}}\frac{\mu}{q}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{t_{v}}|^{q}dx-(\frac{c_{1}}{c_{2}})^{\frac{p(N-4)-2N}{8}}\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{t_{v}}|^{p}dx

\displaystyle<\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\Delta u_{t_{v}}|^{2}dx-\frac{\mu}{q}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{t_{v}}|^{q}dx-\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{t_{v}}|^{p}dx

\displaystyle=E_{p,q}(u_{t_{v}})\leq\max_{t\in(0,+\infty)}E_{p,q}(u_{t}),

where
2+\frac{8}{N}\leq q<p\leq 4^{*}
and
N\geq 5
. Since
u\in S(c_{1})
is arbitrary, we have

m_{p,q}(c_{2})\leq\inf_{u\in S(c_{1})}\max_{t\in(0,+\infty)}E_{p,q}(u_{t})=m_{p,q}(c_{1}).

(2.6)

∎

Next we will prove that
m_{p,q}(c)
is continuous. Before that, we will show some lemmas which will be used in the proof for the continuity of
m_{p,q}(c)
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
2+\frac{8}{N}\leq q<p\leq 4^{*}
. Let
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}
be a bounded sequence in
H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
a_{n}
be a sequence satisfying
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}a_{n}=1
. Then

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\big|E_{p,q}(a_{n}u_{n})-E_{p,q}(u_{n})\big|=0.

Proof.

It follows from direct calculations that

\big|E_{p,q}(a_{n}u_{n})-E_{p,q}(u_{n})\big|\leq\big|\frac{(a_{n}^{2}-1)}{2}\|\Delta u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\big|+\big|\frac{\mu}{q}(a_{n}^{q}-1)\|u_{n}\|_{q}^{q}\big|+\big|\frac{1}{p}(a_{n}^{p}-1)\|u_{n}\|_{p}^{p}\big|

Since
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{R}}
is bounded in
H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we can directly get the conclusion.
∎

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
2+\frac{8}{N}\leq q<p\leq 4^{*}
. If
q=2+\frac{8}{N}
, we further assume that
\mu c^{\frac{4}{N}}<\frac{N+4}{NC^{q}_{N,q}}
. For any
u\in S(c)
, let
t_{u}
be be given as in Lemma
2.3
. Then
t_{u}
satisfies

t_{u}^{\frac{N(p-2)}{4}-2}<C\frac{\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{2}}{\|u\|_{p}^{p}}.

where
C
is a constant independent of
u
.

Proof.

For convenience, in this part, we set
f(t)=\mu|t|^{q-2}t+|t|^{p-2}t
, then
F(t)=\mu\frac{1}{q}|t|^{q}+\frac{1}{p}|t|^{p}
. We claim that

f(t)t>(2+\frac{8}{N})F(t)>0,\quad\mathrm{for}\ \mathrm{all}\ t\not=0.

Indeed, when
2+\frac{8}{N}<q<p\leq 4^{*}
, we obviously have

f(t)t-(2+\frac{8}{N})F(t)=\mu(1-\frac{2+8/N}{q})|t|^{q}+(1-\frac{2+8/N}{p})|t|^{p}>0,\quad\mathrm{for}\ \mathrm{all}\ t\not=0.

When
2+\frac{8}{N}=q<p\leq 4^{*}
, we still find that

f(t)t-(2+\frac{8}{N})F(t)=(1-\frac{2+8/N}{p})|t|^{p}>0,\quad\mathrm{for}\ \mathrm{all}\ t\not=0.

Therefore, we have the following inequality,

f(t)t-2F(t)>\frac{8}{N}F(t),\quad\mathrm{for}\ \mathrm{all}\ t\not=0.

(2.7)

According to Lemma
2.3
, we have
u_{t_{u}}\in\mathcal{Q}_{p,q}(c)
. Hence
Q_{p,q}(u_{t_{u}})=0,\ i.e.
,

\|\Delta u_{t_{u}}\|_{2}^{2}=\mu\gamma_{q}\|u_{t_{u}}\|_{q}^{q}+\gamma_{p}\|u_{t_{u}}\|_{p}^{p}.

Then by the inequality in (
2.7
), we have

\displaystyle t_{u}^{2}\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\mu\gamma_{q}\|u_{t_{u}}\|_{q}^{q}+\gamma_{p}\|u_{t_{u}}\|_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle=\frac{N}{4}(\mu\frac{q-2}{q}\|u_{t_{u}}\|_{q}^{q}+\frac{p-2}{p}\|u_{t_{u}}\|_{p}^{p})

\displaystyle=\frac{N}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(f(u_{t_{u}})u_{t_{u}}-2F(u_{t_{u}}))dx

\displaystyle>\frac{N}{4}\cdot\frac{8}{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(u_{t_{u}})dx=2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(u_{t_{u}})dx

\displaystyle=2(\mu\frac{1}{q}\|u_{t_{u}}\|_{q}^{q}+\frac{1}{p}\|u_{t_{u}}\|_{p}^{p})

\displaystyle=2(t_{u}^{\frac{N(q-2)}{4}}\mu\frac{1}{q}\|u\|_{q}^{q}+t_{u}^{\frac{N(p-2)}{4}}\frac{1}{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p})

\displaystyle>2t_{u}^{\frac{N(p-2)}{4}}\frac{1}{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p}.

Hence, we deduce that

t_{u}^{\frac{N(p-2)}{4}-2}<\frac{p}{2}\frac{\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{2}}{\|u\|_{p}^{p}}.

∎

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
N\geq 5
,
\mu>0
and
2+\frac{8}{N}\leq q<p\leq 4^{*}
. If
q=2+\frac{8}{N}
, we further assume that
\mu c^{\frac{4}{N}}<\frac{N+4}{NC^{q}_{N,q}}
. It holds that

(i)

c\mapsto m_{p,q}(c)
is a continuous mapping.

(ii)

Let
c\in(0,+\infty)
. We have
m_{p,q}(c)\leq m_{p,q}(\alpha)+m_{p,q}(c-\alpha)
for all
\alpha\in(0,c)
. The inequality is strict if
m_{p,q}(\alpha)
or
m_{p,q}(c-\alpha)
is reached.

Proof.

(i)\
Based on the above lemma, for any fixed
c>0
,
m_{p,q}(c-h)
and
m_{p,q}(c+h)
are monotonic and bounded as
h\rightarrow 0^{+}
, so they have limits. Moreover,
m_{p,q}(c-h)\geq m_{p,q}(c)\geq m_{p,q}(c+h)
, thus we obtain

\lim\limits_{h\rightarrow 0^{+}}m_{p,q}(c-h)\geq m_{p,q}(c)\geq\lim\limits_{h\rightarrow 0^{+}}m_{p,q}(c+h).

It remains to show the other direction. We finish it by proving two claims.

Claim
1
:
\lim\limits_{h\rightarrow 0^{+}}m_{p,q}(c-h)\leq m_{p,q}(c)
.

Take
u\in\mathcal{Q}_{p,q}(c)
, for any
h>0
, we set
u^{h}(x)=\sqrt{1-\frac{h}{c}}u(x)
. Noticing that
\|u^{h}\|_{2}^{2}=c-h
, by Lemma
2.3
, there exists
t_{h}>0
such that
u^{h}_{t_{h}}\in\mathcal{Q}_{p,q}(c-h)
. By Lemma
2.8
, we have

t_{h}^{\frac{N(p-2)}{4}-2}<\frac{p}{2}\frac{\|\Delta u^{h}\|_{2}^{2}}{\|u^{h}\|_{p}^{p}}=\frac{p}{2}\frac{(1-\frac{h}{c})}{(1-\frac{h}{c})^{\frac{p}{2}}}\frac{\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{2}}{\|u\|_{p}^{p}},

which implies that
t_{h}
is bounded as
h\rightarrow 0^{+}
. Hence
u_{t_{h}}
is bounded in
H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. So according to Lemma
2.7
, we have

m_{p,q}(c-h)\leq E_{p,q}(u^{h}_{t_{h}})=E_{p,q}(\sqrt{1-\frac{h}{c}}u_{t_{h}})\leq E_{p,q}(u_{t_{h}})+o(h)\leq E_{p,q}(u)+o(h).

Then we prove the claim.

Claim
2
:
\lim\limits_{h\rightarrow 0^{+}}m_{p,q}(c+h)\geq m_{p,q}(c)
.

Choose
u\in\mathcal{Q}_{p,q}(c+h)
for
h>0
and let
v^{h}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\frac{h}{c}}}u(x)
. Since
\|v^{h}\|_{2}^{2}=c
, similar to Claim
1
, there exists
t_{h}>0
such that
v^{h}_{t_{h}}\in\mathcal{Q}_{p,q}(c)
and

t_{h}^{\frac{N(p-2)}{4}-2}<\frac{p}{2}\frac{\|\Delta v^{h}\|_{2}^{2}}{\|v^{h}\|_{p}^{p}}=\frac{p}{2}\frac{(1+\frac{h}{c})^{\frac{p}{2}}}{(1+\frac{h}{c})}\frac{\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{2}}{\|u\|_{p}^{p}}.

So
u_{t_{h}}
is bounded in
H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Using Lemma
2.7
,
it follows that

m_{p,q}(c)\leq E_{p,q}(v^{h}_{t_{h}})=E_{p,q}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\frac{h}{c}}}u_{t_{h}})\leq E_{p,q}(u_{t_{h}})+o(h)\leq E_{p,q}(u)+o(h).

Hence, we prove that
c\mapsto m_{p,q}(c)
is continuous.

(ii)\
We observe that for fixed
\alpha\in(0,c)
, it is sufficient to prove that the following holds

\forall\theta\in(1,\frac{c}{\alpha}]:m_{p,q}(\theta\alpha)\leq\theta m_{p,q}(\alpha)

(2.8)

and that, if
m_{p,q}(\alpha)
is reached, the inequality is strict. In fact, if (
2.8
) holds then we have

\displaystyle m_{p,q}(c)

\displaystyle=\frac{c-\alpha}{c}m_{p,q}(c)+\frac{\alpha}{c}_{p,q}m_{p,q}(c)

\displaystyle=\frac{c-\alpha}{c}m_{p,q}(\frac{c}{c-\alpha}(c-\alpha))+\frac{\alpha}{c}m_{p,q}(\frac{c}{\alpha}\alpha)

\displaystyle\leq m_{p,q}(c-\alpha)+m_{p,q}(\alpha),

with a strict inequality if
m_{p,q}(\alpha)
is reached. By the definition of
m_{p,q}(c)
, we notice that, for any
\epsilon>0
sufficiently small, there exists a
u\in\mathcal{Q}_{p,q}(\alpha)
such that

E_{p,q}(u)\leq m_{p,q}(\alpha)+\epsilon.

(2.9)

Since
Q_{p,q}(u)=0
, we define
\widetilde{u}(x)=(\frac{1}{\theta})^{\frac{1}{p-2}}u((\frac{1}{\theta})^{\frac{p}{N(p-2)}}x)
. Then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\widetilde{u}|^{2}dx=\theta\alpha,\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\widetilde{u}|^{p}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{p}dx,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\widetilde{u}|^{q}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\widetilde{u}|^{q}dx=(\frac{1}{\theta})^{\frac{q-p}{p-2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{q}dx\geq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{q}dx,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\Delta\widetilde{u}|^{2}dx=(\frac{1}{\theta})^{\frac{2N+p(4-N)}{N(p-2)}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\Delta u|^{2}dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\Delta u|^{2}dx{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}.}

Hence
Q_{p,q}(\widetilde{u})\leq 0
. So there exists
t_{0}\in(0,1]
such that
\widetilde{u}_{t_{0}}\in\mathcal{Q}_{p,q}(\theta\alpha)
and for any
\theta>1
,

\displaystyle m_{p,q}(\theta\alpha)

\displaystyle\leq E_{p,q}(\widetilde{u}_{t_{0}})=(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q\gamma_{q}})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\Delta\widetilde{u}_{t_{0}}|^{2}dx+(\frac{\gamma_{p}}{q\gamma_{q}}-\frac{1}{p})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\widetilde{u}_{t_{0}}|^{p}dx

\displaystyle=(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q\gamma_{q}})t_{0}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\Delta\widetilde{u}|^{2}dx+(\frac{\gamma_{p}}{q\gamma_{q}}-\frac{1}{p})t_{0}^{\frac{Np}{4}-\frac{N}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\widetilde{u}|^{p}dx

\displaystyle=(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q\gamma_{q}})t_{0}^{2}(\frac{1}{\theta})^{\frac{2N+p(4-N)}{N(p-2)}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\Delta u|^{2}dx

\displaystyle+(\frac{\gamma_{p}}{q\gamma_{q}}-\frac{1}{p})t_{0}^{\frac{Np}{4}-\frac{N}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{p}dx

\displaystyle\leq\theta(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q\gamma_{q}})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\Delta u|^{2}dx+\theta(\frac{\gamma_{p}}{q\gamma_{q}}-\frac{1}{p})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{p}dx

\displaystyle\leq\theta E_{p,q}(u)<\theta(m_{p,q}(\alpha)+\epsilon).

Since
\epsilon
is arbitrary, we have that
m_{p,q}(\theta\alpha)\leq\theta m_{p,q}(\alpha)
. If
m_{p,q}(\alpha)
is reached, we can choose
\epsilon=0
. Then the strict inequality follows.
∎

For Lemma
2.9

(ii)
, it is not used in the rest of the paper but is useful for future application. Although the key point for us to show the strong convergence is Lemma
2.10
, it is possible to prove the strong convergence by this property.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
2+\frac{8}{N}\leq q<p\leq 4^{*}
,
\mu>0
and
c>0
. If
q=2+\frac{8}{N}
, we further assume that
\mu c^{\frac{4}{N}}<\frac{N+4}{NC^{q}_{N,q}}
. If there exists
u\in S(c)
such that
E_{p,q}(u)=m_{p,q}(c)
and

\Delta^{2}u+\omega u=\mu|u|^{q-2}u+u|^{p-2}u,

(2.10)

then
Q_{p,q}(u)=0
and
\omega>0
. Moreover, the function
c\mapsto m_{p,q}(c)
is strictly decreasing in a right neighborhood of
c
.

Proof.

The fact that any solution to (
2.10
) satisfies
Q_{p,q}(u)=0
is a direct consequence of the Pohozaev identity. Now we deduce from (
2.10
)

\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{2}+\omega\|u\|_{2}^{2}-\mu\|u\|_{q}^{q}-\|u\|_{p}^{p}=0.

(2.11)

Combining (
2.11
) with
Q_{p,q}(u)=0
, we have

\omega\|u\|_{2}^{2}=\mu(1-\gamma_{q})\|u\|_{q}^{q}+(1-\gamma_{p})\|u\|_{p}^{p}.

Since
0<\gamma_{q}<\gamma_{p}\leq 1
, it follows that
\omega>0
. Then we let
u_{\lambda,t}(x)=t^{\frac{N}{4}}\sqrt{\lambda}u(\sqrt{t}x)
for
\lambda,\ t>0
. We define

\mathcal{K}(\lambda,t)=E_{p,q}(u_{\lambda,t})=\frac{1}{2}t^{2}\lambda\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{\mu}{q}t^{\frac{N(q-2)}{4}}\lambda^{\frac{q}{2}}\|u\|_{q}^{q}-\frac{1}{p}t^{\frac{N(p-2)}{4}}\lambda^{\frac{p}{2}}\|u\|_{p}^{p}

and

\mathcal{M}(\lambda,t)=Q_{p,q}(u_{\lambda,t})=t^{2}\lambda\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{2}-\mu\gamma_{q}t^{\frac{N(q-2)}{4}}\lambda^{\frac{q}{2}}\|u\|_{q}^{q}-\gamma_{p}t^{\frac{N(p-2)}{4}}\lambda^{\frac{p}{2}}\|u\|_{p}^{p}.

By suitable calculations, we have

\frac{\partial\mathcal{K}}{\partial\lambda}(1,1)=\frac{1}{2}\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{\mu}{2}\|u\|_{q}^{q}-\frac{1}{2}\|u\|_{p}^{p}=-\frac{1}{2}\omega c,

\frac{\partial\mathcal{K}}{\partial t}(1,1)=\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{2}-\mu\gamma_{q}\|u\|_{q}^{q}-\gamma_{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p}=0,

and

\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{K}}{\partial t^{2}}(1,1)=\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{2}-\mu\gamma_{q}(\frac{N(q-2)}{4}-1)\|u\|_{q}^{q}-\gamma_{p}(\frac{N(p-2)}{4}-1)\|u\|_{p}^{p}<0,

which yields for
\delta_{t}
small enough and
\delta_{\lambda}>0
,

\mathcal{K}(1+\delta_{\lambda},1+\delta_{t})<\mathcal{K}(1,1)\quad\mathrm{for}\ \omega>0.

(2.12)

In addition, we can also observe that

\mathcal{M}(1,1)=Q_{p,q}(u)=\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{2}-\mu\gamma_{q}\|u\|_{q}^{q}-\gamma_{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p}=0

and

\frac{\partial\mathcal{M}}{\partial t}(1,1)=2\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{2}-\mu\gamma_{q}\frac{N(q-2)}{4}\|u\|_{q}^{q}-\gamma_{p}\frac{N(p-2)}{4}\|u\|_{p}^{p}\not=0.

From the Implicit Function Theorem, we deduce that there exists
\epsilon>0
and a continuous function
g:[1-\epsilon,\ 1+\epsilon]\mapsto\mathbb{R}
satisfying
g(1)=1
such that
\mathcal{M}(\lambda,g(\lambda))=0
for
\lambda\in[1-\epsilon,\ 1+\epsilon]
. Therefore, we inter from (
2.12
) that

m_{p,q}((1+\epsilon)c)=\inf\limits_{u\in\mathcal{Q}_{p,q}((1+\epsilon)c)}E_{p,q}(u)\leq E_{p,q}(u_{1+\epsilon,g(1+\epsilon)})<E(u)=m_{p,q}(c).

This proves the last assertion of the lemma.
∎

Ground States In Sobolev Subcritical Case

In this section we will establish the existence of normalized ground states to (

First, let us introduce the functional
\Psi(u):H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\backslash\{0\}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
defined by

\Psi(u):=E_{p,q}(u_{t_{u}})=\frac{1}{2}t_{u}^{2}\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{\mu}{q}t_{u}^{\frac{N(q-2)}{4}}\|u\|_{q}^{q}-\frac{1}{p}t_{u}^{\frac{N(p-2)}{4}}\|u\|_{p}^{p},

where
t_{u}
is the unique number determined in Lemma
2.3
. Then we get

Lemma 3.1
.

The functional
\Psi(u):H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\backslash\{0\}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is of class
C^{1}
and

\displaystyle d\Psi(u)[\varphi]

\displaystyle=t_{u}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta u\cdot\Delta\varphi dx-t_{u}^{\frac{N(q-2)}{4}}\mu\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{q-2}u\cdot\varphi dx-t_{u}^{\frac{N(p-2)}{4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{p-2}u\cdot\varphi dx

\displaystyle=dE_{p,q}(u_{s_{u}})[\varphi_{s_{u}}]

for any
u\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\backslash\{0\}
and
\varphi\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Proof.

Let
u\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\backslash\{0\}
and
\varphi\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. By the definition of
\Psi
, we have

\Psi(u+s\varphi)-\Psi(u)=E_{p,q}((u+s\varphi)_{t_{s}})-E_{p,q}(u_{t_{0}}),

where
|s|
is small enough,
t_{s}=t_{u+s\varphi}
and
t_{0}=t_{u}
is the unique maximum point of the functional
E_{p,q}(u)
. By the mean value theorem we get

\displaystyle E_{p,q}((u+s\varphi)_{t_{s}})-E_{p,q}(u_{t_{0}})

\displaystyle\leq E_{p,q}((u+s\varphi)_{t_{s}})-E_{p,q}(u_{t_{s}})

\displaystyle=\frac{t_{s}^{2}}{2}(\|\Delta(u+s\varphi)\|_{2}^{2}-\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{2})-\frac{t_{s}^{\frac{N(p-2)}{4}}}{p}(\|u+s\varphi\|_{p}^{p}-\|u\|_{p}^{p})

\displaystyle-\mu\frac{t_{s}^{\frac{N(q-2)}{4}}}{q}(\|u+s\varphi\|_{q}^{q}-\|u\|_{q}^{q})

\displaystyle=\frac{t_{s}^{2}}{2}(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}2s\Delta u\cdot\Delta\varphi+s^{2}|\Delta\varphi|^{2}dx)

\displaystyle-\mu t_{s}^{\frac{N(q-2)}{4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(\int_{0}^{1}|u+s\eta_{s}\varphi|^{q-2}(u+s\eta_{s}\varphi)s\varphi d\eta_{s})dx

\displaystyle-t_{s}^{\frac{N(p-2)}{4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(\int_{0}^{1}|u+s\eta_{s}\varphi|^{p-2}(u+s\eta_{s}\varphi)s\varphi d\eta_{s})dx,

where
\eta_{s}\in(0,1)
. Similarly,

\displaystyle E_{p,q}((u+s\varphi)_{t_{s}})-E_{p,q}(u_{t_{0}})

\displaystyle\geq E_{p,q}((u+s\varphi)_{t_{0}})-E_{p,q}(u_{t_{0}})

\displaystyle=\frac{t_{0}^{2}}{2}(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}2s\Delta u\cdot\Delta\varphi+s^{2}|\Delta\varphi|^{2}dx)

\displaystyle-\mu t_{0}^{\frac{N(q-2)}{4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(\int_{0}^{1}|u+s\tau_{s}\varphi|^{q-2}(u+s\tau_{s}\varphi)s\varphi d\tau_{s})dx

\displaystyle-t_{0}^{\frac{N(p-2)}{4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(\int_{0}^{1}|u+s\tau_{s}\varphi|^{p-2}(u+s\tau_{s}\varphi)s\varphi d\tau_{s})dx,

where
\tau_{s}\in(0,1)
. Since the map
u\mapsto t_{u}
is of class
C^{1}
, it follows from the two inequalities above that

\lim\limits_{s\rightarrow 0}\frac{\Psi(u+s\varphi)-\Psi(u)}{s}=t_{u}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta u\cdot\Delta\varphi dx-t_{u}^{\frac{N(q-2)}{4}}\mu\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{q-2}u\cdot\varphi dx-t_{u}^{\frac{N(p-2)}{4}}\mu\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{p-2}u\cdot\varphi dx.

Hence, we see that the G
\hat{a}
teaux derivative of
\Psi
is bounded linear in
\varphi
and continuous in
u
. Therefore,
\Psi
is of class
C^{1}
. In particular, by changing variables in the integrals, we have

\displaystyle d\Psi(u)[\varphi]

\displaystyle=t_{u}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta u\cdot\Delta\varphi dx-t_{u}^{\frac{N(q-2)}{4}}\mu\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{q-2}u\cdot\varphi dx-t_{u}^{\frac{N(p-2)}{4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{p-2}u\cdot\varphi dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta u_{t_{u}}\cdot\Delta\varphi_{t_{u}}dx-\mu\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{t_{u}}|^{q-2}u_{t_{u}}\cdot\varphi_{t_{u}}dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{t_{u}}|^{p-2}u_{t_{u}}\cdot\varphi_{t_{u}}dx.

This completes the proof.
∎

Definition 3.3
.

Let

\mathcal{G}
of compact subsets of

(ii)

\eta\in C([0,1]\times Y,Y)

\eta(t,x)=x

(t,x)\in(\{0\}\times Y)\bigcup([0,1],B)

\eta(\{1\}\times A)\in\mathcal{G}
.

We remark that

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
2+\frac{8}{N}\leq q<p<4^{*}
. If
q=2+\frac{8}{N}
, we further assume that
\mu c^{\frac{4}{N}}<\frac{N+4}{NC^{q}_{N,q}}

If

Proof.

Let

be an arbitrary minimizing sequence of

. We define the mapping

\eta:[0,1]\times S(c)\rightarrow S(c),\qquad\eta(s,u)=u_{1-s+st_{u}},

Since

\mathcal{G}
, one has

A_{n}:=\eta(1,D_{n})=\{u_{t_{u}}:u\in D_{n}\}\in\mathcal{G}.

(3.1)

In particular,
A_{n}\subset\mathcal{G}
for all

. Let

,

\max_{A_{n}}I=\max_{D_{n}}I\rightarrow c_{\mathcal{G}}
. Therefore
\{A_{n}\}\subset\mathcal{Q}_{p,q}(c)
is another minimizing sequence of
c_{\mathcal{G}}
. Now, using the equivalent minimax principle
[
18

, we obtain a Palais-Smale sequence
\{v_{n}\}\subset S(c)
for

c_{\mathcal{G}}
such that

as
n\rightarrow\infty

t_{n}=t_{v_{n}}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad u_{n}=(v_{n})_{t_{n}}=(v_{n})_{t_{v_{n}}}.

Then we claim that there exists a

such that,

t_{n}^{-2}\leq C

(3.2)

for
n\in\mathbb{N}
large enough. First, we notice that

t_{n}^{-2}=\frac{\int_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}|\Delta v_{n}|^{2}dx}{\int_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}|\Delta u_{n}|^{2}dx}.

Since
E_{p,q}(u_{n})=I(v_{n})\rightarrow c_{\mathcal{G}}
, by Lemma
2.3

2.5
, we can deduce that

is bounded from below by a positive constant. On the other hand, since

is coercive on

, it follows that

. Clearly, this proves the claim.

Now, for
\{u_{n}\}\subset\mathcal{Q}_{p,q}(c)
, it follows that

E_{p,q}(u_{n})=I(u_{n})=I(v_{n})\rightarrow c_{\mathcal{G}}.

We then only need to show that

is a Palais-Smale sequence for
E_{p,q}
on
S(c)
at level
c_{\mathcal{G}}
. For any

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}v_{n}\phi_{t_{n}^{-1}}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(v_{n})_{t_{n}}\phi dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u_{n}\phi dx,

which means

Denoting by

, we deduce that

\displaystyle\|dE_{p,q}(u_{n})\|_{\ast}

\displaystyle=\sup\limits_{\phi\in T_{u_{n}}S(c),\|\phi\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq 1}|dE_{p,q}(u_{n})[\phi]|

\displaystyle=\sup\limits_{\phi\in T_{u_{n}}S(c),\|\phi\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq 1}|dE_{p,q}((v_{n})_{t_{n}})[(\phi_{t_{n}^{-1}})_{t_{n}}]|

\displaystyle=\sup\limits_{\phi\in T_{u_{n}}S(c),\|\phi\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq 1}|dI(v_{n})[\phi_{t_{n}^{-1}}]|

\displaystyle\leq\|dI(v_{n})\|_{\ast}\cdot\sup\limits_{\phi\in T_{u_{n}}S(c),\|\phi\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq 1}\|\phi_{t_{n}^{-1}}\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}

Since

is a Palais-Smale sequence of

E_{p,q}
on
S(c)
at the level

.

Lemma 4.1

Proof.

6
Instability

In this section, we consider the instability of standing waves associated with the ground states. We will consider our problem in
H^{2}_{rad}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. In fact, we need to use the local virial identity to prove the instability by blowup and it is not clear how to control errors term without radial symmetry.

For now, we haven’t removed the radial symmetry assumption. The strategy here was introduced in
[
36
]
, where Ogawa and Tsutsumi showed blowup for radial solutions for mass supercritical NLS with radial
u_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with infinite variance (
i.e.
we may not have
xu_{0}\not\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
). We requires a careful analysis of the time evolution for the localized virial identity
M_{\varphi_{R}}[u(t)]
, which is essential in the proof of blowup result.
Firstly, from
[
37
]
we recall the local well-posedness of the Cauchy problem (
1.2
) holds for
2+\frac{8}{N}\leq q<p\leq 4^{*}
.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
2+\frac{8}{N}\leq q<p\leq 4^{*}
. For any

(6.1)

Next we recall the localized virial to (

Lemma 6.2

For all

(i)\ 1-\varphi^{\prime\prime}_{R}(r)\geq 0,\quad 1-\frac{\varphi^{\prime}_{R}(r)}{r}\geq 0,

Lemma 6.4

with

A_{R}^{1}[u]=:\big<u(t),[\Delta^{2}u+i\Gamma_{\varphi_{R}}]u(t)\big>,

B_{R}^{2}[u]=:\big<u(t),[-\mu|u|^{q-2}u+i\Gamma_{\varphi_{R}}]u(t)\big>\quad\mathrm{and}\quad B_{R}^{3}[u]=:\big<u(t),[-|u|^{p-2}u+i\Gamma_{\varphi_{R}}]u(t)\big>.

For convenience, we state some identities to be used in the next. By using the fact that
\Delta A+A\Delta=2\partial_{k}A\partial_{k}+[\partial_{k},[\partial_{k},A]]
, we observe that

[\Delta^{2},i\Gamma_{\varphi_{R}}]=\Delta[\Delta,i\Gamma_{\varphi_{R}}]+[\Delta,i\Gamma_{\varphi_{R}}]\Delta=2\partial_{k}[\Delta,i\Gamma_{\varphi_{R}}]\partial_{k}+[\partial_{k},[\partial_{k},[\Delta,i\Gamma_{\varphi_{R}}]]].

(6.4)

Then by simple calculations, it follows that

[\Delta,i\Gamma_{\varphi_{R}}]=[\Delta,\nabla\varphi_{R}\cdot\nabla+\nabla\cdot\nabla\varphi_{R}]=4\partial_{k}(\partial^{2}_{kl}\varphi_{R})\partial_{l}+\Delta^{2}\varphi_{R}.

(6.5)

Combining (
6.4
) and (
6.5
), we obtain the identity

[\Delta^{2},i\Gamma_{\varphi_{R}}]=8\partial^{2}_{kl}(\partial^{2}_{lm}\varphi_{R})\partial^{2}_{mk}+4\partial_{k}(\partial^{2}_{kl}\Delta\varphi_{R})\partial_{l}+2\partial_{k}(\Delta^{2}\varphi_{R})\partial_{k}+\Delta^{3}\varphi_{R}.

(6.6)

We recall that the Hessian of sufficiently regular and radial function
f:\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is given by

\partial^{2}_{kl}f=(\delta_{kl}-\frac{x_{k}x_{l}}{r^{2}})\frac{\partial_{r}f}{r}+\frac{x_{k}x_{l}}{r^{2}}\partial^{2}_{r}f.

(6.7)

Applying (
6.7
) to
\varphi_{R}(r)
and
u(t,r)
, a calculation combined with integration by parts yields that

\displaystyle 8\big<u,8\partial^{2}_{kl}(\partial^{2}_{lm}\varphi_{R})\partial^{2}_{mk}u\big>

\displaystyle=8\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(\partial^{2}_{kl}\overline{u})(\partial^{2}_{lm}\varphi_{R})(\partial^{2}_{mk}u)

\displaystyle=8\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(\partial_{r}^{2}\varphi_{R}|\partial_{r}^{2}u|^{2}+\frac{d-1}{r^{2}}\frac{\partial_{r}\varphi_{R}}{r}|\partial_{r}u|^{2})

\displaystyle=8\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\Delta u|^{2}-(1-\partial_{r}^{2}\varphi_{R})|\partial_{r}^{2}u|^{2}-(1-\frac{\partial_{r}\varphi_{R}}{r})\frac{d-1}{r^{2}}|\partial_{r}u|^{2}.

Noticing that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\Delta u|^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\partial_{r}^{2}u|^{2}+\frac{d-1}{r^{2}}|\partial_{r}u|^{2}
for radial
u\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, according to the inequalities in Lemma
6.2
, we have

8\big<u,8\partial^{2}_{kl}(\partial^{2}_{lm}\varphi_{R})\partial^{2}_{mk}u\big>\leq 8\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\Delta u|^{2}.

(6.8)

For the rest terms in (
6.6
), direct arguments yield that

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cc}&|\big<u,\partial_{k}(\partial^{2}_{kl}\Delta\varphi_{R})\partial_{l}u\big>|\lesssim\|\partial^{2}_{kl}\Delta\varphi_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{2}\lesssim R^{-2}\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{2}\lesssim R^{-2}\|u\|_{2}\|\Delta u\|_{2},\\
&|\big<u,\partial_{k}(\Delta^{2}\varphi_{R})\partial_{k}u\big>|\lesssim\|\Delta^{2}\varphi_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{2}\lesssim R^{-2}\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{2}\lesssim R^{-2}\|u\|_{2}\|\Delta u\|_{2},\\
&|\big<u,\Delta^{3}\varphi_{R}u\big>||\lesssim\|\Delta^{3}\varphi_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|^{2}_{2}\lesssim R^{-4}\|u\|^{2}_{2}.\\
\end{array}

By (
6.8
) and (
6
), we conclude that

A_{R}^{1}[u(t)]\leq 8\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\Delta u(t)|^{2}+O(R^{-4}+R^{-2}\|\Delta u(t)\|_{2}).

(6.13)

Next, we turn to the combined nonlinearity. By using the identity
\nabla(|u|^{q})=\frac{q}{q-2}\nabla(|u|^{q-2})|u|^{2}
, we note that integration by parts yields that

\displaystyle B_{R}^{2}[u]

\displaystyle=:-\big<u,[\mu|u|^{q-2}u,\nabla\varphi_{R}\cdot\nabla+\nabla\cdot\nabla\varphi_{R}]u\big>=2\mu\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2}\nabla\varphi_{R}\nabla(|u|^{q-2})

\displaystyle=-\mu\frac{2(q-2)}{q}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Delta\varphi_{R}|u|^{q}.

By the definition of
\varphi_{R}(r)
, we get that
\Delta\varphi_{R}(r)-N\equiv 0
for
r\leq R
, it follows that

\displaystyle B_{R}^{2}[u]

\displaystyle=\mu\frac{-2N(q-2)}{q}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{q}-\mu\frac{2(q-2)}{q}\int_{|x|\geq R}(\Delta\varphi_{R}-N)|u|^{q}

\displaystyle=\mu\frac{-2N(q-2)}{q}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{q}+O(R^{-\frac{(q-2)(N-1)}{2}}\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{\frac{q-2}{4}}),

where the last term follows from the fact
\|\Delta\varphi_{R}(r)-N\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim 1
and applying the Strauss inequality, which gives us

\int_{|x|\geq R}|u|^{q}\lesssim\|u\|_{2}^{2}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}^{q-2}\lesssim R^{-\frac{(q-2)(N-1)}{2}}\|u\|_{2}^{\frac{q+2}{2}}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{\frac{q-2}{2}}\lesssim R^{-\frac{(q-2)(N-1)}{2}}\|u\|_{2}^{\frac{3q+2}{4}}\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{\frac{q-2}{4}}.

Similarly, we can also obtain that

\displaystyle B_{R}^{3}[u]

\displaystyle=\frac{-2N(p-2)}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{p}-\frac{2(p-2)}{p}\int_{|x|\geq R}(\Delta\varphi_{R}-N)|u|^{p}

\displaystyle=\frac{-2N(p-2)}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{p}+O(R^{-\frac{(p-2)(N-1)}{2}}\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{\frac{p-2}{4}}).

Finally, from the above, we conclude that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}M_{\varphi_{R}}[u(t)]

\displaystyle\leq 2N(q-2)E_{p,q}(u)-(N(q-2)-8)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\Delta u(t)|^{2}-\frac{2N(p-q)}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u(t)|^{p}

\displaystyle+O(R^{-4}+R^{-2}\|\Delta u(t)\|_{2}+R^{-\frac{(q-2)(N-1)}{2}}\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{\frac{q-2}{4}}+R^{-\frac{(p-2)(N-1)}{2}}\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{\frac{p-2}{4}})

\displaystyle=8Q_{p,q}(u(t))+O(R^{-4}+R^{-2}\|\Delta u(t)\|_{2}+R^{-\frac{(q-2)(N-1)}{2}}\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{\frac{q-2}{4}}+R^{-\frac{(p-2)(N-1)}{2}}\|\Delta u\|_{2}^{\frac{p-2}{4}}).

This completes the proof.
∎

passing radial coordinates, we get

\|u_{\epsilon}\|_{q}^{q}=R^{q}\omega\epsilon^{\frac{(N-4)q}{2}}\int_{0}^{2}\frac{\varphi^{q}(r)r^{N-1}}{(\epsilon^{2}+r^{2})^{\frac{(N-4)q}{2}}}dr

that can be decomposed as

\displaystyle\|u_{\epsilon}\|_{q}^{q}

\displaystyle=R^{q}\omega\epsilon^{\frac{(N-4)q}{2}}\int_{0}^{2}\frac{(\varphi^{q}(r)-1)r^{N-1}}{(\epsilon^{2}+r^{2})^{\frac{(N-4)q}{2}}}dr+R^{q}\omega\epsilon^{\frac{(N-4)q}{2}}\int_{0}^{2}\frac{r^{N-1}}{(\epsilon^{2}+r^{2})^{\frac{(N-4)q}{2}}}dr

\displaystyle=I_{1}(\epsilon)+I_{2}(\epsilon).

Since
\varphi(r)\equiv 1
on
[0,1]
, the integral in
I_{1}(\epsilon)
is converging and thus
I_{1}(\epsilon)=O(\epsilon^{\frac{(N-4)q}{2}})
. Now by making a change of variable, we rewrite
I_{2}(\epsilon)
as

I_{2}(\epsilon)=R^{q}\omega\epsilon^{N-\frac{(N-4)q}{2}}\int_{0}^{\frac{2}{\epsilon}}\frac{r^{N-1}}{(1+r^{2})^{\frac{(N-4)q}{2}}}dr.

The integral is converging, as
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
, to a finite value if and only if
q>\frac{N}{N-4}
. Hence we have that, for some constant
K>0
,

I_{2}(\epsilon)=K\epsilon^{N-\frac{(N-4)q}{2}}+o(\epsilon^{N-\frac{(N-4)q}{2}}).

And recording that
I_{1}(\epsilon)=O(\epsilon^{\frac{(N-4)q}{2}})
, we have

\|u_{\epsilon}\|_{q}^{q}=K\epsilon^{N-\frac{(N-4)q}{2}}+o(\epsilon^{N-\frac{(N-4)q}{2}}).

Now assuming that
q=\frac{N}{N-4}
, and proceeding as before, we get that

\displaystyle\|u_{\epsilon}\|_{q}^{q}

\displaystyle=R^{q}\omega\epsilon^{\frac{N}{2}}\int_{0}^{2}\frac{(\varphi^{q}(r)-1)r^{N-1}}{(\epsilon^{2}+r^{2})^{\frac{N}{2}}}dr+R^{q}\omega\epsilon^{\frac{N}{2}}\int_{0}^{2}\frac{r^{N-1}}{(\epsilon^{2}+r^{2})^{\frac{N}{2}}}dr

\displaystyle=I_{1}(\epsilon)+I_{2}(\epsilon).

with
I_{1}(\epsilon)=O(\epsilon^{\frac{N}{2}})
. Also,

I_{2}(\epsilon)=R^{q}\omega\epsilon^{\frac{N}{2}}\int_{0}^{\frac{2}{\epsilon}}\frac{r^{N-1}}{(1+r^{2})^{\frac{N}{2}}}dr=R^{q}\omega\epsilon^{\frac{N}{2}}(|\log\epsilon|+O(1)).

Summarizing, for
q=\frac{N}{N-4}
we obtain that

\|u_{\epsilon}\|_{q}^{q}=R^{q}\omega\epsilon^{\frac{N}{2}}|\log\epsilon|+O(\epsilon^{\frac{N}{2}}).

It remains to study the case
2\leq q<\frac{N}{N-4}
. We can observe that for all
r>0
,

\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\frac{\varphi^{q}(r)r^{N-1}}{(\epsilon^{2}+r^{2})^{\frac{N-4}{2}q}}=\frac{\varphi^{q}(r)}{r^{(N-4)q-(N-1)}}

and also that, for all
\epsilon>0
, for some constant
C

\big|\frac{\varphi^{q}(r)r^{N-1}}{(\epsilon^{2}+r^{2})^{\frac{N-4}{2}q}}\big|\leq\frac{C}{r^{(N-4)q-(N-1)}}\in L^{2}([0,2]).

Then from lebesgue’s theorem, we can get that

\displaystyle\|u_{\epsilon}\|_{q}^{q}

\displaystyle=R^{q}\omega\epsilon^{\frac{(N-4)q}{2}}\int_{0}^{2}\frac{\varphi^{q}(r)r^{N-1}}{(\epsilon^{2}+r^{2})^{\frac{(N-4)q}{2}}}dr

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{\frac{(N-4)q}{2}}R^{q}\omega\int_{0}^{2}\frac{\varphi^{q}(r)}{r^{(N-4)q-(N-1)}}dr+o(\epsilon^{\frac{(N-4)q}{2}}).

This completes the proof.
∎

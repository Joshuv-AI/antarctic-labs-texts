On the Classification of Finite Quasi-Quantum Groups over Abelian Groups Thanks: *Corresponding author
Hua-Lin Huang, Gongxiang Liu, Yuping Yang*, and Yu Ye

Abstract
. Using a variety of methods developed in the theory of finite-dimensional quasi-Hopf algebras, we classify all finite-dimensional coradically graded pointed coquasi-Hopf algebras over abelian groups. As a consequence, we partially confirm the generation conjecture of pointed finite tensor categories due to Etingof, Gelaki, Nikshych and Ostrik.

1.
Introduction

As a continuation to a series of previous works
[
17
,
18
,
20
]
, this paper completes the classification problem of finite-dimensional coradically graded pointed coquasi-Hopf algebras over abelian groups. Throughout, we work over an algebraically closed field
\mathbbm{k}
of characteristic zero. Unless stated otherwise, in this paper all spaces, maps, (co)algebras, (co)modules, and categories, etc., are over
\mathbbm{k}.

The classification of finite-dimensional pointed Hopf algebras over finite abelian groups was completed over the last two decades, and a systematic approach (in particular Weyl groupoids and arithmetic root systems) was established, see
[
3
,
5
,
6
,
14
,
15
]
. Meanwhile, Etingof and Gelaki proposed to classify pointed finite tensor categories. By the Tannakian formalism
[
11
]
, this amounts to a classification of certain finite quasi-quantum groups, namely finite-dimensional elementary quasi-Hopf algebras, or dually finite-dimensional pointed coquasi-Hopf algebras. In the pioneering works
[
7
,
8
,
9
,
13
,
4
]
, a few examples and classification results of such algebras, and consequently the associated pointed finite tensor categories, are thus obtained.
In
[
17
,
18
,
20
]
, the authors of the present paper continue the study of the classification problem of finite quasi-quantum groups and several interesting classification results are also obtained. To explain this and our main result of this paper, we need some concrete notations.

Once and for all, let
G
be a finite abelian group and
\Phi
be a
3
-cocycle on
G.
Based on an analog of the lifting method in the theory of finite-dimensional pointed Hopf algebras, a complete understanding of the Nichols algebras in the twisted Yetter-Drinfeld module category
{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{D}^{\Phi}
is the crux for the classification of finite-dimensional pointed coquasi-Hopf algebras. A twisted Yetter-Drinfeld module
V\in{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{D}^{\Phi}}
is said to be of diagonal type, if it is a direct sum of
1
-dimensional twisted Yetter-Drinfeld modules. The associated Nichols algebra
B(V)
is called diagonal if
V
is so. Let
H
be a pointed coquasi-Hopf algebra over
G
, and
\operatorname{gr}(H)
the coradically graded coquasi-Hopf algebra associated to
H
. The coinvariant subalgebra
R
of
\operatorname{gr}(H)
will be a twisted Yetter-Drinfeld module in
{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{D}^{\Phi}
for certain
\Phi
, and
H
is called diagonal if
R
is diagonal as a twisted Yetter-Drinfeld module.
In
[
17
,
18
]
, we classified all finite-dimensional Nichols algebras of diagonal type in
{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{D}^{\Phi}}
and proved that every finite-dimensional pointed coquasi-Hopf algebra of diagonal type must be the form of
B(V)\#\mathbbm{k}G
.

The aim of this paper is to study Nichols algebras of nondiagonal type and the main result is the following (see Theorem
3.1
for an equivalent form) .

Theorem 0.1
.
Let
B(V)\in\ _{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}
be a Nichols algebra of nondiagonal type with
G_{V}=G
. Then
B(V)
is infinite dimensional.

Here
G_{V}
is the support group of
V
(see the paragraph after Definition
2.3
). Under assumption that
G_{V}=G
, which is natural for us since the braided Hopf algebra structure of
B(V)
is determined by
G_{V}
rather than
G
(see the paragraph after Proposition
2.6
), our result tells us that every finite-dimensional Nichols algebra
B(V)\in\ _{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}
must be of diagonal type finally, which already was classified in our previous works. As consequences, we can get the structure of general finite-dimensional pointed coquasi-Hopf algebras now (see Theorems
5.1
and
5.2
).

Theorem 0.2
. If
M
is a finite-dimensional pointed coquasi-Hopf algebra over finite abelian group
G
, then
\operatorname{gr}(M)\cong B(V)\#\mathbbm{k}G
for a Yetter-Drinfeld module of finite type
V\in\ {{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
.

In
[
11
]
, Etingof, Gelaki, Nikshych and Ostrik conjecture that every pointed finite tensor category over a field of characteristic zero is tensor generated by objects of length
2
. Let
G(\mathcal{C})
be the set of isomorphism classes of simple objects in a pointed tensor category
\mathcal{C}
. Then
G(\mathcal{C})
is naturally a group under tensor product. Using our classification result and some useful result in
[
18
]
, we can partially prove the conjecture (see Theorem
5.5
).

Theorem 0.3
. Let
\mathcal{C}
be a pointed finite tensor category over a field of characteristic zero. If the group
G(\mathcal{C})
is abelian, then
\mathcal{C}
is tensor generated by objects of length
2
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we recall some necessary notions and particularly introduce a method to study Nichols algebras in
{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
called change of based groups. Sections 3 and 4 are designed to give a proof of the above Theorem 0.1. The last section is devoted to the classification of finite-dimensional pointed coquasi-Hopf algebras and the generation problem of pointed finite tensor categories.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall some necessary notions and basic facts about pointed coquasi-Hopf algebras, twisted Yetter-Drinfeld modules and Nichols algebras. The reader is referred to
[

,
17
,

2.1.
Pointed coquasi-Hopf algebras

A coquasi-Hopf algebra is a coalgebra
(H,\Delta,\varepsilon)
equipped with a
compatible quasi-algebra structure and a quasi-antipode. Namely,
there exist two coalgebra homomorphisms

m\colon H\otimes H\longrightarrow H,\ a\otimes b\mapsto ab\quad\text{and}\quad\mu\colon\mathbbm{k}\longrightarrow H,\ \lambda\mapsto\lambda 1_{H},

a convolution-invertible map
\Phi\colon H^{\otimes 3}\longrightarrow\mathbbm{k}

called the
associator
, a coalgebra antimorphism
S\colon H\longrightarrow H
and two
functions
\alpha,\beta\colon H\longrightarrow\mathbbm{k}
such that for all
a,b,c,d\in H
the following equalities hold:

\displaystyle a_{1}(b_{1}c_{1})\Phi(a_{2},b_{2},c_{2})=\Phi(a_{1},b_{1},c_{1})(a_{2}b_{2})c_{2},

\displaystyle 1_{H}a=a=a1_{H},

\displaystyle\Phi(a_{1},b_{1},c_{1}d_{1})\Phi(a_{2}b_{2},c_{2},d_{2})=\Phi(b_{1},c_{1},d_{1})\Phi(a_{1},b_{2}c_{2},d_{2})\Phi(a_{2},b_{3},c_{3}),

\displaystyle\Phi(a,1_{H},b)=\varepsilon(a)\varepsilon(b).

\displaystyle S(a_{1})\alpha(a_{2})a_{3}=\alpha(a)1_{H},\quad a_{1}\beta(a_{2})S(a_{3})=\beta(a)1_{H},

\displaystyle\Phi(a_{1},S(a_{3}),a_{5})\beta(a_{2})\alpha(a_{4})=\Phi^{-1}(S(a_{1}),a_{3},S(a_{5}))\alpha(a_{2})\beta(a_{4})=\varepsilon(a).

The triple
(S,\alpha,\beta)
is called a quasi-antipode.
H
is called a
pointed coquasi-Hopf algebra
if
(H,\Delta,\varepsilon)
is a pointed coalgebra, i.e., every simple comodule of
H
is
1
-dimensional.

Let
C
be a coalgebra, the coradical
C_{0}
of
C
is the sum of all simple subcoalgebras of
C
. Fix a coalgebra
C
with coradical
C_{0}
, define
C_{n}
inductively as follows: for each
n\geq 1
, define

C_{n}=\Delta^{-1}(C\otimes C_{n-1}+C_{0}\otimes C).

Then we get a filtration
C_{0}\subset C_{1}\subset\cdots C_{n}\subset\cdots,
which is called the
coradical filtration
of
C
. A coquasi-Hopf algebra has a coradical filtration since it is a coalgebra.

Given a coquasi-Hopf algebra
(H,\Delta,\varepsilon,m,\mu,\Phi,S,\alpha,\beta),
let
\{H_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
be its coradical filtration, and let

\operatorname{gr}H=H_{0}\oplus H_{1}/H_{0}\oplus H_{2}/H_{1}\oplus\cdots,

the corresponding coradically graded coalgebra. Then naturally

\operatorname{gr}H
inherits from
H
a graded coquasi-Hopf algebra structure. The
corresponding graded associator
\operatorname{gr}\Phi
satisfies

\operatorname{gr}\Phi(\bar{a},\bar{b},\bar{c})=0
for all homogeneous elements

\bar{a},\bar{b},\bar{c}\in\operatorname{gr}H
unless they all lie in
H_{0}.

Similar conditions hold for
\operatorname{gr}\alpha
and
\operatorname{gr}\beta.
A coquasi-Hopf algebra
H
is called
coradically graded
if
H\cong\operatorname{gr}(H)
as coquasi-Hopf algebras.

If
H
is a pointed coquasi-Hopf algebra, then
H_{0}
is a pointed cosemisimple coquasi-Hopf algebra, which is determined by a group
G
together with a
3
-cocycle on
G
as follows.

Example 2.1
.

Let
G
be a group. Clearly the group algebra
\mathbbm{k}G
is a Hopf algebra with
\Delta(g)=g\otimes g,\;S(g)=g^{-1}
and

\varepsilon(g)=1
for any
g\in G
. Let
\omega
be a normalized
3
-cocycle on
G
, i.e.

(2.1)

\displaystyle\omega(ef,g,h)\omega(e,f,gh)=\omega(e,f,g)\omega(e,fg,h)\omega(f,g,h),

(2.2)

\displaystyle\omega(f,1,g)=1

for all
e,f,g,h\in G
. By linearly extending,
\omega\colon(\mathbbm{k}G)^{\otimes 3}\to\mathbbm{k}
becomes a
convolution-invertible map. Define two linear functions

\alpha,\beta\colon\mathbbm{k}G\to\mathbbm{k}
by

\alpha(g):=\varepsilon(g)\quad\text{and}\quad\beta(g):=\frac{1}{\omega(g,g^{-1},g)}

for any
g\in G
. Then
kG
together with these
\omega,\ \alpha
and
\beta

makes a coquasi-Hopf algebra, which will be written as
(\mathbbm{k}G,\omega)
in the following. The comodule category of
(\mathbbm{k}G,\omega)
forms a tensor category, which is called a Gr-category and denoted by
\operatorname{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}
.

Let us now consider the construction of Gr-categories which will be especially important in this paper. The crux to determine all the Gr-categories is to give a complete list of the representatives of the
3
-cohomology classes in
\operatorname{H}^{3}(G,\mathbbm{k}^{*})
for all groups
G.
However, when
G
is a finite abelian group, the problem was solved in
[
18
]
, and a list of the representatives of
\operatorname{H}^{3}(G,\mathbbm{k}^{*})
can be given as follows.

Let
\mathbb{N}
denote the set of nonnegative integers,
\mathbb{Z}
the ring of integers, and
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
the cyclic group of order
m.
Any finite abelian group
G
is of the form
\mathbb{Z}_{m_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{Z}_{m_{n}}
with
m_{j}\in\mathbb{N}

for
1\leq j\leq n.
Denote by
\mathcal{A}
the set of all
\mathbb{N}
-sequences

(2.3)

(c_{1},\ldots,c_{l},\ldots,c_{n},c_{12},\ldots,c_{ij},\ldots,c_{n-1,n},c_{123},\ldots,c_{rst},\ldots,c_{n-2,n-1,n})

such that
0\leq c_{l}<m_{l},\ 0\leq c_{ij}<(m_{i},m_{j}),\ 0\leq c_{rst}<(m_{r},m_{s},m_{t})
for
1\leq l\leq n,\ 1\leq i<j\leq n,\ 1\leq r<s<t\leq n
, where
c_{ij}
and
c_{rst}
are ordered in the lexicographic order of their indices. We denote by
\underline{\mathbf{c}}
the sequence (
2.3
) in the following. Let
g_{i}
be a generator of
\mathbb{Z}_{m_{i}},1\leq i\leq n
. For any
\underline{\mathbf{c}}\in\mathcal{A}
, define

\displaystyle\omega_{\underline{\mathbf{c}}}\colon G\times G\times G\longrightarrow\mathbbm{k}^{\ast}

(2.4)

\displaystyle[g_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots g_{n}^{i_{n}},g_{1}^{j_{1}}\cdots g_{n}^{j_{n}},g_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots g_{n}^{k_{n}}]\mapsto

\displaystyle\prod_{l=1}^{n}\zeta_{m_{l}}^{c_{l}i_{l}[\frac{j_{l}+k_{l}}{m_{l}}]}\prod_{1\leq s<t\leq n}\zeta_{m_{t}}^{c_{st}i_{t}[\frac{j_{s}+k_{s}}{m_{s}}]}\prod_{1\leq r<s<t\leq n}\zeta_{(m_{r},m_{s},m_{t})}^{c_{rst}i_{r}j_{s}k_{t}}.

Here and below
\zeta_{m}
stands for an
m
-th primitive root of unity.
According to
[
18
, Proposition 3.8]
,
\{\omega_{\underline{\mathbf{c}}}\mid\underline{\mathbf{c}}\in\mathcal{A}\}
forms a complete set of representatives of the normalized
3
-cocycles on
G
up to
3
-cohomology.

2.2.
Nichols algebras of Yetter-Drinfeld modules

Nichols algebras are very important for the construction of pointed coquasi-Hopf algebras. For our purpose, we are mainly concerned with the Nichols algebras in the Yetter-Drinfeld module category of the coquasi-Hopf algebra
(\mathbbm{k}G,\Phi)
, where
G
is a finite abelian group and
\Phi
is a normalized 3-cocycle on
G
. To emphasize
\Phi,
we denote the Yetter-Drinfeld category of
(\mathbbm{k}G,\Phi)
as
{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{D}^{\Phi}
. For convenience, we call an object in
{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{D}^{\Phi}
a twisted Yetter-Drinfeld module. Define

(2.5)

\widetilde{\Phi}_{g}(x,y)=\frac{\Phi(g,x,y)\Phi(x,y,g)}{\Phi(x,g,y)}

for all
g,x,y\in G
. By direct computation one can show that
\widetilde{\Phi}_{g}
is a 2-cocycle on
G
. The construction of category of twisted Yetter-Drinfeld modules can be summarized as follows, the detailed computations can be found in
[
18
,
19
]
.

Definition 2.2
.

An object in
{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{D}^{\Phi}
is a
G
-graded vector space
V=\oplus_{g\in G}V_{g}

(
V_{g}=\{v\in V|\rho(v)=g\otimes v\}
as a
kG
-comodule
)
with each
V_{g}
a projective

G
-representation with respect to the 2-cocycle
\widetilde{\Phi}_{g},
namely for any
e,f\in G,v\in V_{g}
we have

(2.6)

e\triangleright(f\triangleright v)=\widetilde{\Phi}_{g}(e,f)(ef)\triangleright v.

The module structure on the tensor product
V_{g}\otimes V_{h}
is determined by

(2.7)

e\triangleright(X\otimes Y)=\widetilde{\Phi}_{e}(g,h)(e\triangleright X)\otimes(e\triangleright Y),\ X\in V_{g},\ Y\in V_{h}.

The associativity and the braiding constraints of
{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{D}^{\Phi}
are given respectively by

(2.8)

\displaystyle\ a_{V_{e},V_{f},V_{g}}((X\otimes Y)\otimes Z)=\Phi(e,f,g)^{-1}X\otimes(Y\otimes Z)

(2.9)

\displaystyle R(X\otimes Y)=e\triangleright Y\otimes X

for all
X\in V_{e},\ Y\in V_{f},\ Z\in V_{g}.

Let
\Phi
be a
3
-cocycle on
G
as given in (
2.1
).
One can verify directly that

(2.10)

\widetilde{\Phi}_{g}\widetilde{\Phi}_{h}=\widetilde{\Phi}_{gh},\ \forall g,h\in G.

Suppose
V_{g}
is
(G,\widetilde{\Phi}_{g})
-representation,
V_{h}
is a
(G,\widetilde{\Phi}_{h})
-representation, then
V_{g}\otimes V_{h}
is a
(G,\widetilde{\Phi}_{gh})
-representation.
In particular, the dual object
V_{g}^{*}
of
V_{g}
is a
(G,\widetilde{\Phi}_{g^{-1}})
-representation and
(V_{g}^{*})^{*}=V_{g}
, see
[
20
, Proposition 2.5]
for details.

A Yetter-Drinfeld module
V\in{{}^{\mathbbm{k}G}_{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{D}^{\Phi}}
is called diagonal if
V
is direct sum of
1
-dimensional twisted Yetter-Drinfeld modules. For a simple Yetter-Drinfeld module
V
in
{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{D}^{\Phi}}
, there exists some
g\in G
such that
V=V_{g}
and we define
g_{V}:=g
in this case. Recall that a
2
-cocycle
\varphi
on
G
is called symmetric if
\varphi(g,h)=\varphi(h,g)
for all
h,g\in G
. By (
2.6
), it is not hard to show that a simple Yetter-Drinfeld module
V
with
g_{V}=g
is
1
-dimensional if and only if
\widetilde{\Phi}_{g}
is symmetric.

Let
V
be a nonzero object in
{}^{\mathbbm{k}G}_{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{D}^{\Phi}.
By
T(V)
we denote the tensor algebra in
{}^{\mathbbm{k}G}_{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{D}^{\Phi}
generated freely by

It is clear that
T(V)
is isomorphic to
\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}V^{\otimes\overrightarrow{n}}
as a linear space, where
V^{\otimes\overrightarrow{n}}
means

\underbrace{(\cdots((}_{n-1}V\otimes V)\otimes V)\cdots\otimes V).
This induces a natural
\mathbb{N}
-graded structure on
T(V).
Define a comultiplication on
T(V)
by
\Delta(X)=X\otimes 1+1\otimes X,\ \forall X\in V,
a counit by
\varepsilon(X)=0,
and an antipode by
S(X)=-X.
These provide a graded Hopf algebra structure on
T(V)
in the braided tensor category
{}^{\mathbbm{k}G}_{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{D}^{\Phi}.

Definition 2.3
.

The Nichols algebra
B(V)
of
V
is the quotient Hopf algebra
T(V)/I
in
{}^{\mathbbm{k}G}_{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{D}^{\Phi},
where

is the unique maximal graded Hopf ideal contained in
\bigoplus_{n\geq 2}V^{\otimes\overrightarrow{n}}
.

A Nichols algebra
B(V)
is called
of diagonal type
if
V
is diagonal. Suppose
V=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}\in{{}^{\mathbbm{k}G}_{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{D}^{\Phi}}
is direct sum of simple objects, then we will say that the rank of

is

. According to
[
20
, Proposition 3.1]
,

is a
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-graded algebra with
\operatorname{deg}V_{i}=e_{i}
, where
\{e_{i}:1\leq i\leq n\}
is a set of free generators of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.
For
V\in{{}^{\mathbbm{k}G}_{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{D}^{\Phi}}
, we will call
G
the
based group
of
V
and
B(V)
. Let
V=V_{1}\oplus V_{2}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{n}
be direct sum of simple Yetter-Drinfeld modules in
{{}^{\mathbbm{k}G}_{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{D}^{\Phi}}
,
g_{i}
the degree of
V_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq n
, the subgroup
G_{V}:=\langle g_{1},g_{2},\cdots g_{n}\rangle
will be called the

of

.

Next we will recall the definition of the twisting of a Nichols algebra through a 2-cochain of
G
. Let
(V,\triangleright,\delta_{L})\in{{}^{\mathbbm{k}G}_{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
, and let

be a

-cochain of

of

on

determined by

g\triangleright_{J}X=\frac{J(g,x)}{J(x,g)}g\triangleright X

for homogenous element

and

Here

is the

. We denote

, and one can verify that

Moreover there is a tensor equivalence

which takes

and

Let

be a Nichols algebra in

Then

is a Hopf algebra in

with multiplication

for all homogenous elements

is a twisting of

.

Lemma 2.4
.

[

The twisting

of

is a Nichols algebra in

.

2.3.
Reduction

The study of Nichols algebras in
{}^{\mathbbm{k}G}_{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{D}^{\Phi}
is deeply related on the
3
-cocycle
\Phi
on
G
. Recall that a
3
-cocycle
\Phi
on
G
is called an
abelian
3
-cocycle
if
{}^{\mathbbm{k}G}_{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{D}^{\Phi}
is pointed, i.e. each simple object of

A key observation in

is twist equivalent to a Nichols algebra in a normal Yetter-Drinfeld category when

is an abelian
3
-cocycle.
Suppose
G=\mathbb{Z}_{m_{1}}\times\mathbb{Z}_{m_{2}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{Z}_{m_{n}}
,

is a generator of
\mathbb{Z}_{m_{i}}
for all

, and

is an abelian

-cocycle on

Then up to cohomology

must be of the form

(2.13)

\Phi(e_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots e_{n}^{i_{n}},e_{1}^{j_{1}}\cdots e_{n}^{j_{n}},e_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots e_{N}^{k_{n}})=\prod_{l=1}^{n}\zeta_{l}^{c_{l}i_{l}[\frac{j_{l}+k_{l}}{m_{l}}]}\prod_{1\leq s<t\leq n}\zeta_{m_{t}}^{c_{st}i_{t}[\frac{j_{s}+k_{s}}{m_{s}}]}.

The folowing lemma follows immediately.

Lemma 2.5
.

(1).

Each
3
-cocycle of a finite cyclic group or direct sum of two finite cyclic groups is abelian;

(2).

Let
B(V)\in{{}^{\mathbbm{k}G}_{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{D}^{\Phi}}
be a Nichols algebra of rank
1
or rank

, then
\Phi_{G_{V}}
is an abelian

-cocycle on

.

Suppose
G=\mathbb{Z}_{m_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{Z}_{m_{n}}=\langle g_{1}\rangle\times\cdots\langle g_{n}\rangle
. Associated to
G
there is a finite group
\widehat{G}
defined by

(2.14)

\widehat{G}=\mathbb{Z}_{m_{1}^{2}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{Z}_{m_{n}^{2}}=\langle h_{1}\rangle\times\cdots\langle h_{n}\rangle

Let

(2.15)

\pi\colon\widehat{G}\to G,\;\;\;\;h_{i}\mapsto g_{i},\;\;\;\;\;1\leq i\leq n

be the canonical epimorphism. The following proposition is very important for the study of Nichol algebras of diagonal type.

Proposition 2.6
.

[
18
, Proposition 3.15]

Suppose that
\Phi
is an abelian
3
-cocycle on
G.
Then
\pi^{*}\Phi
is a
3
-coboundary on
\widehat{G}
, namely, there is a
2
-cochain
J
of
\widehat{G}
such that
\partial J=\pi^{*}\Phi
.

Since the Yetter-Drinfeld module structure of
B(V)
is determined by the based group
G
, while the braided
Hopf algebra structure of
B(V)
and the braiding are determined by the support group
G_{V}
. So if the braided Hopf algebra structure of
B(V)
is the only concern, we can omit some Yetter-Drinfeld module information of
B(V)
and realize it in a new Yetter-Drinfeld category. Let
B(V)\in\ _{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}
and
B(U)\in\ _{\mathbbm{k}H}^{\mathbbm{k}H}\mathcal{YD}^{\Psi}
, we will say that
B(V)
is isomorphic to
B(U)
if there is a linear isomorphism
F\colon B(V)\longrightarrow B(U)
which preserves the multiplication and comultiplication.

For a Yetter-Drinfeld module
V\in\ _{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}
, we use
\delta_{V}\colon V\longrightarrow\mathbbm{k}G\otimes V
to denote the comodule structure map of
V
.

Lemma 2.7

(
[
18
]
, Lemma 4.4)
.

Suppose
V\in\ _{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}
and
U\in\ _{\mathbbm{k}H}^{\mathbbm{k}H}\mathcal{YD}^{\Psi}
, where
H
is a finite abelian group. Let
G_{V}
and
H_{U}
be the support groups of
V
and
U
respectively. If there is a linear isomorphism
F\colon V\longrightarrow U
and
a group epimorphism
\pi\colon G_{V}\longrightarrow H_{U}
such that:

\displaystyle\delta_{U}\circ F=(\pi\times F)\circ\delta_{V},

\displaystyle F(g\triangleright v)=\pi(g)\triangleright F(v),

\displaystyle\Phi|_{G_{V}}=(\pi^{*}\Psi)|_{H_{U}}

for any
g\in G_{V},\ v\in V
. Then
B(V)
is isomorphic to
B(U)
.

Let
G
and
\mathbb{G}
be two finite groups and
\pi\colon\mathbb{G}\to G
a group epimorphism,
\iota\colon G\to\mathbb{G}
be a section of
\pi
, that is
\pi\circ\iota=\operatorname{id}_{G}
. With these notations, we have following lemma.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
V\in{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
. Then there is an object
\widetilde{V}\in{{}_{\mathbbm{k}\mathbb{G}}^{\mathbbm{k}\mathbb{G}}\mathcal{YD}^{\pi^{*}{\Phi}}}
such that
\widetilde{V}=V
as linear spaces and the Yetter-Drinfeld module structure is determined by

(2.16)

\displaystyle\delta_{\widetilde{V}}=(\iota\otimes\operatorname{id})\circ\delta_{V},

(2.17)

\displaystyle g\triangleright v=\pi(g)\triangleright v

for any
g\in\mathbb{G},\ v\in V
. Moreover, we have
B(V)\cong B(\widetilde{V})
.

Proof.

We need to show that the space
\widetilde{V}
with action and coaction of
\mathbb{G}
defined by (
2.16
) and (
2.17
) is a Yetter-Drinfeld module in
{{}_{\mathbbm{k}\mathbb{G}}^{\mathbbm{k}\mathbb{G}}\mathcal{YD}^{\pi^{*}{\Phi}}}
. Let
V=\bigoplus_{g\in G}V_{g}
. Then it is obvious that we have
\widetilde{V}=\bigoplus_{g\in G}\widetilde{V}_{\iota(g)}
such that
\widetilde{V}_{\iota(g)}=V_{g}
as vector spaces for all
g\in G
. We only need to prove that
\widetilde{V}_{\iota(g)}
is a projective
\mathbb{G}
-representation associated to

2
-cocycle
{\pi^{*}{\Phi}}_{\iota(g)}
. Let
e,f\in\mathbb{G}
and
v\in V
, we have

\displaystyle e\triangleright(f\triangleright v)=\pi(e)\triangleright(\pi(f)\triangleright v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Phi}_{g}(\pi(e),\pi(f))\pi(ef)\triangleright v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Phi(g,\pi(e),\pi(f))\Phi(\pi(e),\pi(f),g)}{\Phi(\pi(e),g,\pi(f))}ef\triangleright v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Phi(\pi\circ\iota(g),\pi(e),\pi(f))\Phi(\pi(e),\pi(f),\pi\circ\iota(g))}{\Phi(\pi(e),\pi\circ\iota(g),\pi(f))}ef\triangleright v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\pi^{*}\Phi(\iota(g),e,f)\pi^{*}\Phi(e,f,\iota(g))}{\pi^{*}\Phi(e,\iota(g),f)}ef\triangleright v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widetilde{\pi^{*}\Phi}_{\iota(g)}(e,f)ef\triangleright v.

So we have proved that
\widetilde{V}_{\iota(g)}
is a projective
\mathbb{G}
-representation associated to the

2
-cocycle
{\pi^{*}{\Phi}}_{\iota(g)}
, and hence
\widetilde{V}\in{{}_{\mathbbm{k}\mathbb{G}}^{\mathbbm{k}\mathbb{G}}\mathcal{YD}^{\pi^{*}{\Phi}}}
.
The isomorphism
B(V)\cong B(\widetilde{V})
follows from Lemma
2.7
immediately.
∎

Let
B(V)
be a Nichols algebra in
{}^{\mathbbm{k}G}_{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{D}^{\Phi}
. By lemma
2.8
,
B(V)
is isomorphic to a Nichols algebra
B(\widetilde{V})
in
{}^{\mathbbm{k}\widehat{G}}_{\mathbbm{k}\widehat{G}}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{D}^{\pi^{*}\Phi}
. If
\Phi
is an abelian
3
-cocycle, then there is a
2
-cochain
J
of
\widehat{G}
such that
\partial J=\pi^{*}\Phi
. According to Lemma
2.4
,

is a Nichols algebra in
{}^{\mathbbm{k}\widehat{G}}_{\mathbbm{k}\widehat{G}}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{D}
, which is twist equivalent to

. So we obtain the following proposition.

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
\Phi
be an abelian
3
-cocycle and
B(V)
be a Nichols algebra in
{}^{\mathbbm{k}G}_{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{D}^{\Phi}
. Then

is twist equivalent to a Nichols algebra in
{}^{\mathbbm{k}\widehat{G}}_{\mathbbm{k}\widehat{G}}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{D}
.

Let
V\in{{}^{\mathbbm{k}G}_{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{D}^{\Phi}}
, if the action of the support group
G_{V}
on
V
is diagonal, then
V
has a basis
\{X_{1},\cdots,X_{n}\}
such that

(2.18)

\begin{split}\delta(X_{i})=g_{i}\otimes X_{i},\ \ g_{i}\triangleright X_{j}=q_{ij}X_{j},\end{split}

where
q_{ij}\in\mathbbm{k}
for
1\leq i,j\leq n
. Such a basis

is called a

of

.
The following lemma follows from
[
18
, Lemma 4.1]
immediately.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
V\in{{}^{\mathbbm{k}G}_{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{D}^{\Phi}}
, the following three conditions are equivalent:

(1).

V
has a standard basis.

(2).

The action of support group
G_{V}
on
V
is diagonal.

(3).

\Phi_{G_{V}}
is an abelian
3
-cocycle on
G_{V}
.

Now suppose
V
has a standard basis
\{X_{1},\cdots,X_{n}\}
, then we can define a nondirected graph
\mathcal{D}(V)
associated to

as follows:

1)

There is a bijecton
\phi
from
I=\{1,2,\dots,n\}
to the set of vertices of
\mathcal{D}(V)
.

2)

For all
1\leq i\leq n,
the vertex

is labelled by

3)

For all
1\leq i,j\leq n,
the number

of edges between

and

is either
0
or

If

or

otherwise

and the edge is labelled by
\widetilde{q_{ij}}=q_{ij}q_{ji}
for all

The diagram
\mathcal{D}(V)
is called the

of

. Note that a Nichols algebra of diagonal type always has a generalized Dynkin diagram. It is also helpful to point out that if the generalized Dynkin diagram

exists, it does not depend on the choice of the standard basis of

. It is not hard to see that if
B(V)
has a generalized Dynkin diagram, then
B(V^{J})
also have the same generalized Dynkin diagram with

for any
2
-cochain
J
of

. So combining this with Proposition

, we have the following important proposition.

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
B(V)\in{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
be a Nichols algebra with a standard basis. Then

is twist equivalent to a Nichols algebra

in
{{}_{\mathbbm{k}\widehat{G_{V}}}^{\mathbbm{k}\widehat{G_{V}}}\mathcal{YD}}
, and the two Nichols algebras have the same generalized Dynkin diagrams.

According to this proposition, all finite-dimensional Nichols algebras with a standard basis can be determined by Heckenberger’s classification result of arithmetic root systems
[
15
]
. Note that if

is rank
1
or rank

, then

must be a finite cyclic group or direct product of two finite cyclic groups. According to (

), all the

-cocycles on finite cyclic group or direct product of two finite cyclic groups must be abelian. So a Nichols algebra of rank

or rank

always has a standard basis. One of the main result in
[
20
]
is as follows.

Proposition 2.12
.

[
20
, Proposition 3.18]

Suppose

is a simple Yetter-Drinfeld module of nondiagonal type with

. Then

is finite dimensional if and only if

is one of the following two types:

(I).

g\triangleright v=-v
for all

(II).

\operatorname{dim}(V)=2

for all

-rd primitive root of unity.

3.
Nichols algebras

In this section, we will study Nichols algebras without a standard basis in
{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
, where
G
is a finite abelian group and
\Phi
is a
3
-cocycle on

. The main result is as follows.

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose that
B(V)\in{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
has no standard basis, then

is infinite dimensional.

See Remark
3.14
for the proof. Since a Nichols algebra of rank
1
or
2
always has a standard basis, so our start point will be Nichols algebras of rank

.

3.1.
Nichols algebras of rank
3

Suppose
B(V)\in{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
is a Nichols algebra of rank
3
. If
\Phi_{G_{V}}
is an abelian
3
-cocycle on
G_{V}
, then the dimension of
B(V)
can be determined by Proposition
2.11
. So in this subsection, we will mainly consider the case that
\Phi_{G_{V}}
is nonabelian.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\alpha
be a
2
-cocycle on
G
. An element
g\in G
is called an
\alpha
-element if
\alpha(g,h)=\alpha(h,g)
for all
h\in G
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Suppose
\Phi
is a
3
-cocycle on
G
,
g\in G
, then
g
is a
\widetilde{\Phi}_{g}
-element.

Proof.

For each element
h\in G
, we have

\widetilde{\Phi}_{g}(g,h)=\frac{\Phi(g,g,h)\Phi(g,h,g)}{\Phi(g,g,h)}=\frac{\Phi(h,g,g)\Phi(g,h,g)}{\Phi(h,g,g)}=\widetilde{\Phi}_{g}(h,g).

∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
G
be a finite abelian group and
\Phi
a
3
-cocycle on
G
. Then for any
g_{1},g_{2},g_{3}\in G
, we have

(3.1)

\frac{\widetilde{\Phi}_{g_{1}}(g_{2},g_{3})}{\widetilde{\Phi}_{g_{1}}(g_{3},g_{2})}=\frac{\widetilde{\Phi}_{g_{2}}(g_{3},g_{1})}{\widetilde{\Phi}_{g_{2}}(g_{1},g_{3})}=\frac{\widetilde{\Phi}_{g_{3}}(g_{1},g_{2})}{\widetilde{\Phi}_{g_{3}}(g_{2},g_{1})}.

Proof.

By definition of
\widetilde{\Phi}_{g}
(see (
2.5
)), we have

\frac{\widetilde{\Phi}_{g_{1}}(g_{2},g_{3})}{\widetilde{\Phi}_{g_{1}}(g_{3},g_{2})}=\frac{\Phi(g_{1},g_{2},g_{3})\Phi(g_{2},g_{3},g_{1})\Phi(g_{3},g_{1},g_{2})}{\Phi(g_{2},g_{1},g_{3})\Phi(g_{1},g_{3},g_{2})\Phi(g_{3},g_{2},g_{1})}.

Similarly,

\displaystyle\frac{\widetilde{\Phi}_{g_{2}}(g_{3},g_{1})}{\widetilde{\Phi}_{g_{2}}(g_{1},g_{3})}=\frac{\widetilde{\Phi}_{g_{3}}(g_{1},g_{2})}{\widetilde{\Phi}_{g_{3}}(g_{2},g_{1})}=\frac{\Phi(g_{1},g_{2},g_{3})\Phi(g_{2},g_{3},g_{1})\Phi(g_{3},g_{1},g_{2})}{\Phi(g_{2},g_{1},g_{3})\Phi(g_{1},g_{3},g_{2})\Phi(g_{3},g_{2},g_{1})}.

Thus we obtain (
3.1
).
∎

Next, we consider the structure of simple twisted Yetter-Drinfeld modules of nondiagonal type.

Lemma 3.5
.

Assume
G=\langle g_{1},g_{2},g_{3}\rangle
and
V\in{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
is a simple Yetter-Drinfeld module with
\operatorname{deg}V=g_{1}
. Then

\operatorname{dim}(V)=n
, where
n
is the order of
\frac{\widetilde{\Phi}_{g_{1}}(g_{2},g_{3})}{\widetilde{\Phi}_{g_{1}}(g_{3},g_{2})}
. Moreover, there exists a basis
\{X_{1},X_{2},\cdots,X_{n}\}
of
V
such that

(3.2)

\displaystyle g_{1}\triangleright X_{i}=\alpha X_{i},\ \ 1\leq i\leq n;

(3.3)

\displaystyle g_{2}\triangleright X_{i}=\beta(\frac{\widetilde{\Phi}_{g_{1}}(g_{2},g_{3})}{\widetilde{\Phi}_{g_{1}}(g_{3},g_{2})})^{i-1}X_{i},\ \ 1\leq i\leq n;

(3.4)

\displaystyle g_{3}\triangleright X_{i}=X_{i+1},\ g_{3}\triangleright X_{n}=\gamma X_{1},\ \ 1\leq i\leq n-1.

Here
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathbbm{k}^{*}
satisfy

(3.5)

\displaystyle\alpha^{m_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{m_{1}-1}\Phi_{g_{1}}(g_{1},g_{1}^{i}),

(3.6)

\displaystyle\beta^{m_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{m_{2}-1}\Phi_{g_{1}}(g_{2},g_{2}^{i}),

(3.7)

\displaystyle\gamma^{\frac{m_{3}}{n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{m_{3}-1}\Phi_{g_{1}}(g_{3},g_{3}^{i}),

where
m_{i}=|g_{i}|
is the order of
g_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq 3
. In particular,
V
is of diagonal type if and only if
\frac{\widetilde{\Phi}_{g_{1}}(g_{2},g_{3})}{\widetilde{\Phi}_{g_{1}}(g_{3},g_{2})}=1
.

Proof.

Let
g\in G
and
v\in V
,
m=|g|
, by (
2.5
) we have

\underbrace{g\triangleright(g\triangleright(\cdots(g}_{m}\triangleright v)\cdots))=\prod_{i=1}^{m-1}\widetilde{\Phi}_{g_{1}}(g,g^{i})v.

So it is obvious that the action of each element of
G
on
V
is diagonal. Moreover, by Lemma
3.3
, for any
g\in G
and
v\in V
we have

(3.8)

g_{1}\triangleright(g\triangleright v)=\widetilde{\Phi}_{g_{1}}(g_{1},g)(g_{1}g)\triangleright v=\widetilde{\Phi}_{g_{1}}(g,g_{1})(gg_{1})\triangleright v=g\triangleright(g_{1}\triangleright v).

The identity implies that the map

g_{1}\colon V\longrightarrow V,\ v\mapsto g_{1}\triangleright v

is an isomorphism of projective
G
-representations associated to
\Phi_{g_{1}}
. Since
V
is irreducible, by Schur’s Lemma we have

g_{1}\triangleright v=\alpha v,\ \ \forall v\in V

for some scalar
\alpha\in\mathbbm{k}^{*}
. Since

\underbrace{g_{1}\triangleright(g_{1}\triangleright(\cdots(g_{1}}_{m_{1}}\triangleright v)\cdots))=\prod_{i=1}^{m_{1}-1}\Phi_{g_{1}}(g_{1},g_{1}^{i})v,

we get (
3.2
).

Take
0\neq v\in V
such that
g_{2}\triangleright v=\beta v
for some
\beta\in\mathbbm{k}^{*}
. Let
s
be the minimal positive integer such that

(3.9)

\underbrace{g_{3}\triangleright(g_{3}\triangleright(\cdots(g_{3}}_{s}\triangleright v)\cdots))=\gamma v

for some
\gamma\in\mathbbm{k}^{*}
. Note that such integer
s
exits and
s|m_{3}
since

(3.10)

\underbrace{g_{3}\triangleright(g_{3}\triangleright(\cdots(g_{3}}_{m_{3}}\triangleright v)\cdots))=\prod_{i=1}^{m_{3}-1}\widetilde{\Phi}_{g_{1}}(g_{3},g_{3}^{i})\ v.

Since
g_{1}\triangleright v=\alpha v
and
V
is an irreducible projective
G
-representation with respect to
\Phi_{g_{1}}
,
V
must be spanned by

\{v,g_{3}\triangleright v,\ g_{3}\triangleright(g_{3}\triangleright v),\cdots,\underbrace{g_{3}\triangleright(g_{3}\triangleright(\cdots(g_{3}}_{s-1}\triangleright v)\cdots))\}.

In fact, let
X_{i}=\underbrace{g_{3}\triangleright(g_{3}\triangleright(\cdots(g_{3}}_{i-1}\triangleright v)\cdots)),1\leq i\leq s
. Then we have
g_{2}\triangleright X_{1}=\beta X_{1}
, and for all
1\leq i\leq s
we have

(3.11)

\begin{split}g_{2}\triangleright X_{i}=&g_{2}\triangleright(g_{3}\triangleright X_{i-1})=\Phi_{g_{1}}(g_{2},g_{3})(g_{2}g_{3})\triangleright X_{i-1}\\
=&\frac{\Phi_{g_{1}}(g_{2},g_{3})}{\Phi_{g_{1}}(g_{3},g_{2})}g_{3}\triangleright(g_{2}\triangleright X_{i-1}).\end{split}

So we get

(3.12)

g_{2}\triangleright X_{i}=(\frac{\Phi_{g_{1}}(g_{2},g_{3})}{\Phi_{g_{1}}(g_{3},g_{2})})^{i-1}\beta X_{i},\ 1\leq i\leq s.

We claim that
\operatorname{dim}V=s
. Let
\zeta_{s}
be a primitive
s
-th roots of unit and
\epsilon_{1}
be an
s
-th root of
\gamma
. Then
\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2}=\epsilon_{1}\zeta_{s},\cdots,\epsilon_{s}=\epsilon_{1}\zeta_{s}^{s-1}
are all
s
-th roots of
\gamma
. For all
1\leq i\leq s
, we set

Y_{i}=X_{1}+\epsilon_{i}^{-1}X_{2}+\cdots+\epsilon_{i}^{1-l}X_{l}+\cdots+\epsilon_{i}^{1-s}X_{s}.

Then for all
1\leq i\leq s
we have

(3.13)

\begin{split}g_{3}\triangleright Y_{i}=&g_{3}\triangleright(X_{1}+\epsilon_{i}^{-1}X_{2}+\cdots+\epsilon_{i}^{1-l}X_{l}+\cdots+\epsilon_{i}^{1-s}X_{s})\\
=&X_{2}+\epsilon_{i}^{-1}X_{3}+\cdots+\epsilon_{i}^{1-l}X_{l+1}\cdots+\epsilon_{i}^{2-s}X_{s}+\epsilon_{i}^{1-s}\gamma X_{1})\\
=&\epsilon_{i}X_{1}+X_{2}+\epsilon_{i}^{-1}X_{3}+\cdots+\epsilon_{i}^{1-l}X_{l+1}\cdots+\epsilon_{i}^{s-2}X_{s}\\
=&\epsilon_{i}Y_{i}.\end{split}

Then
Y_{i}
’s are clearly linearly independent since they correspond to different eigenvalues. Then
\{Y_{1},\cdots,Y_{s}\}
forms a basis of
V
, and
\{\epsilon_{i}|1\leq i\leq s\}
are all eigenvalues of
g_{3}
when viewed as a linear transformation on
V
.

Next we prove that
s=n
.
Notice that

(3.14)

\begin{split}g_{3}\triangleright(g_{2}\triangleright Y_{1})=&\Phi_{g_{1}}(g_{3},g_{2})(g_{3}g_{2})\triangleright Y_{1}\\
=&\frac{\Phi_{g_{1}}(g_{3},g_{2})}{\Phi_{g_{1}}(g_{2},g_{3})}g_{2}\triangleright(g_{3}\triangleright Y_{1})\\
=&\frac{\Phi_{g_{1}}(g_{3},g_{2})}{\Phi_{g_{1}}(g_{2},g_{3})}\epsilon_{1}g_{2}\triangleright Y_{1}.\end{split}

Inductively we have

(3.15)

\begin{split}&g_{3}\triangleright(\underbrace{g_{2}\triangleright(g_{2}\triangleright(\cdots(g_{2}}_{i}\triangleright Y_{1})\cdots)))\\
&=\Big(\frac{\Phi_{g_{1}}(g_{3},g_{2})}{\Phi_{g_{1}}(g_{2},g_{3})}\Big)^{i}\epsilon_{1}\underbrace{g_{2}\triangleright(g_{2}\triangleright(\cdots(g_{2}}_{i}\triangleright Y_{1})\cdots)).\end{split}

So we have
g_{3}\triangleright(\underbrace{g_{2}\triangleright(g_{2}\triangleright(\cdots(g_{2}}_{n}\triangleright Y_{1})\cdots)))=\epsilon_{1}\underbrace{g_{2}\triangleright(g_{2}\triangleright(\cdots(g_{2}}_{n}\triangleright Y_{1})\cdots))
, and hence

\underbrace{g_{2}\triangleright(g_{2}\triangleright(\cdots(g_{2}}_{n}\triangleright Y_{1})\cdots)))=kY_{1}

for some scaler
k\in\mathbbm{k}^{*}
. This implies that
Y_{1},g_{2}\triangleright Y_{1},\cdots,\underbrace{g_{2}\triangleright(g_{2}\triangleright(\cdots(g_{2}}_{n-1}\triangleright Y_{1})\cdots))
span a sub-Yetter-Drinfeld module of
V
, and hence
V
since
V
is simple. So we obtain that
\operatorname{dim}(V)=n
and hence
s=n
.

The equations
(
3.3
) and (
3.4
) follow from

\displaystyle\underbrace{g_{2}\triangleright(g_{2}\triangleright(\cdots(g_{2}}_{m_{2}}\triangleright X_{1})\cdots))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{m_{2}-1}\Phi_{g_{1}}(g_{2},g_{2}^{i})X_{1},

\displaystyle\underbrace{g_{3}\triangleright(g_{3}\triangleright(\cdots(g_{3}}_{m_{3}}\triangleright X_{1})\cdots))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{m_{3}-1}\Phi_{g_{1}}(g_{3},g_{3}^{i})X_{1}.

The last statement is obvious and the proof is completed.
∎

By this lemma, we have the following important proposition.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
V=V_{1}\oplus V_{2}\oplus V_{3}\in{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
be a direct sum of simple twisted Yetter-Drinfeld modules with
G=G_{V}
. Then we have

(3.16)

\operatorname{dim}(V_{1})=\operatorname{dim}(V_{2})=\operatorname{dim}(V_{3}).

Proof.

Let
\operatorname{deg}V_{i}=g_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq 3
. Then
G=\langle g_{1},g_{2},g_{3}\rangle
since
G_{V}=G
. By Lemma
3.5
, we have

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
V_{1},V_{2},V_{3}\in{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
be simple Yetter-Drinfeld modules of type (I) such that
\operatorname{dim}(V_{i})=2

B(V_{1}\oplus V_{2}\oplus V_{3})

The proof of the theorem is quite technical and lengthy. To avoid digressing from the present main theme, we postpone the proof to the next section. With the help of this theorem, we can prove the following proposition.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
V=V_{1}\oplus V_{2}\oplus V_{3}\in{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
be a direct sum of simple Yetter-Drinfeld modules and
G=G_{V}
. If
\operatorname{dim}(V_{1})=\operatorname{dim}(V_{2})=\operatorname{dim}(V_{3})=2
and
V_{1},V_{2},V_{3}
are all simple Yetter-Drinfeld modules of

B(V)
is infinite dimensional.

Let
g_{i}=\operatorname{deg}(V_{i})
and
m_{i}=|g_{i}|
for
i=1,2,3
. Let
\mathbbm{G}=\langle\mathbbm{g}_{1}\rangle\times\langle\mathbbm{g}_{2}\rangle\times\langle\mathbbm{g}_{3}\rangle
be the abelian group with free generators
\mathbbm{g}_{1},\mathbbm{g}_{2},\mathbbm{g}_{3}
such that
|\mathbbm{g}_{i}|=m_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq 3
. Then it is obvious that there is a group epimorphism
\pi\colon\mathbbm{G}\longrightarrow G
such that
\pi(\mathbbm{g}_{i})=g_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq 3
. Let
\iota\colon G\longrightarrow\mathbbm{G}
be a section of
\pi
( that is
\pi\circ\iota=\operatorname{id}_{G}
) such that
\iota(\mathbbm{g}_{i})=g_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq 3
. For each
i\in\{1,2,3\}
, let
\widetilde{V_{i}}\in{{}_{\mathbbm{k}\mathbb{G}}^{\mathbbm{k}\mathbb{G}}\mathcal{YD}^{\pi^{*}{\Phi}}}
be the Yetter-Drinfeld module associated to
V_{i}
define by (
2.16
)-(
2.17
). Let
\widetilde{V}=\widetilde{V_{1}}\oplus\widetilde{V_{2}}\oplus\widetilde{V_{3}}
. By Lemma
2.8
, we have
B(V)\cong B(\widetilde{V})
. On the other hand, by Theorem
3.9
,
B(\widetilde{V})
is infinite dimensional, thus
B(V)
is also infinite dimensional.
∎

3.7
,
3.8
and

, the following theorem is clear.

Theorem 3.11
.

Let
B(V)\in{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
be a nondiagonal Nichols algebra of rank
3
with
G_{V}=G
. Then
B(V)
is infinite dimensional.

Corollary 3.12
.

Let
B(V)\in{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
be a Nichols algebra of rank
3
, and
\Phi_{G_{V}}
a nonabelian
3
-cocycle on
G_{V}
. Then
B(V)
is infinite dimensional.

Proof.

Let
H=G_{V}
and
\Psi=\Phi_{H}
. Then the Nichols algebra
B(V)
can be realized in
{{}_{\mathbbm{k}H}^{\mathbbm{k}H}\mathcal{YD}^{\Psi}}
, and the rank of
B(V)
must be greater than or equal to
3
.
For each
i\in\{1,2,3\}
, let
U_{i}
be a nonzero simple Yetter-Drinfeld submodule of
V_{i}
and
U=U_{1}\oplus U_{2}\oplus U_{3}
. It is clear
H=G_{U}
. By Lemma
2.10
,
U
is nondiagonal since
\Psi
is nonabelian on
H=G_{U}
, thus
U_{1},U_{2},U_{3}
are all nondiagonal in
{{}_{\mathbbm{k}H}^{\mathbbm{k}H}\mathcal{YD}^{\Psi}}
. Now
B(U)
is infinite dimensional by Theorem
3.11
, and so is
B(V)
since
B(U)\subset B(V)
.
∎

3.2.
The general case

In this subsection, we will give a classification of finite-dimensional Nichols algebras in
{}^{\mathbbm{k}G}_{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}
, where
G
is a finite abelian group and
\Phi
is a
3
-cocycle on
G
. Firstly, we have the following theorem.

Theorem 3.13
.

Let
B(V)\in{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
such that
\Phi_{G_{V}}
is nonabelian. Then
B(V)
is infinite dimensional.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.7
,
B(V)
can be realized in
{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G_{V}}^{\mathbbm{k}G_{V}}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi|_{G_{V}}}}
. Since each
3
-cocycle of cyclic group or direct sum of two cyclic groups is abelian, so the rank of
B(V)
must be greater than or equal to
3
because
{\Phi|_{G_{V}}}
is nonabelian. Thus
B(V)
is infinite dimensional by Corollary
3.12
.
∎

Remark 3.14
.

Since the conditions “
\Phi|_{G_{V}}
is abelian” and “
B(V)
has a standard basis” are equivalent by Lemma
2.10
, Theorem
3.13
is equivalent to Theorem
3.1
.

We draw the following immediate consequences.

Corollary 3.15
.

Suppose
B(V)\in{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
is a finite-dimensional Nichols algebra and
G=G_{V}
. Then
B(V)
must be of diagonal type.

Corollary 3.16
.

Suppose
B(V)\in{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
is a finite-dimensional Nichols algebra. Then we have

(1).

\Phi|_{G_{V}}
is abelian and
V
has a standard basis;

(2).

B(V)
is isomorphic to a Nichols algebra of diagonal type in
{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G_{V}}^{\mathbbm{k}G_{V}}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi_{G_{V}}}}
.

Proof.

(1) follows from Theorem
3.13
, and (2) follows from Proposition
3.10
.
∎

\operatorname{ht}(\alpha)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}|q_{\alpha}|,&\hbox{if $q_{\alpha}\neq 1$ is a root of unity;}\\
\infty,&\hbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Let

be a Yetter-Drinfeld module with a standard basis

\operatorname{deg}(Y_{i})=g_{i},1\leq i\leq n
. Then there is a pair

associated to
V
, where
E=\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{n}\}
is a set of free generator of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and
\chi
is a bicharacter of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}

Definition 3.18

A Yetter-Drinfeld module

(2)

Theorem 3.19
.

Let
V\in{{}_{\mathbbm{k}G}^{\mathbbm{k}G}\mathcal{YD}^{\Phi}}
be a Yetter-Drinfeld module. Then
B(V)
is finite dimensional if and only if
V
is of finite type.

Proof.

Firstly, suppose that
B(V)
is finite dimensional. By Corollary
3.16
,
\Phi|_{G_{V}}
is an abelian
3
-cocycle on
G_{V}
, thus
V
has a standard basis. Let
H=G_{V}
,
\Psi=\Phi|_{G_{V}}
, and
(\chi,E)
a pair associated to
V
. By Proposition
2.11
,
B(V)
is isomorphic to a Nichols algebra
B(U)
in
{}_{\mathbbm{k}\widehat{H}}^{\mathbbm{k}\widehat{H}}\mathcal{YD}
. Note that
U
can be obtained from
V
by change of based groups and twisting, which do not change the pair
(\chi,E)
associted to
V
, thus
(\chi,E)
is also a pair associated to
U
. On the other hand, since
B(U)\in{{}_{\mathbbm{k}H}^{\mathbbm{k}H}\mathcal{YD}}
is finite dimensional,
\Delta_{\chi,E}
is an arithmetic root system. Moreover,
B(U)
is finite dimensional implies that the nilpotent index of a root vector of each root
\alpha\in\Delta_{\chi,E}
is finite, i.e.,
\operatorname{ht}(\alpha)<\infty
for all
\alpha\in\Delta_{\chi,E}
.

Conversely, Suppose that
V
is finite type. By Proposition
2.11
,
B(V)
is twist equivalent to a Nichols algebra
B(U)
in
{{}_{\mathbbm{k}\widehat{H}}^{\mathbbm{k}\widehat{H}}\mathcal{YD}}
, and
B(V)
have the same generalized Dynkin diagram with
B(V)
. So
\Delta_{\chi,E}
is the arithmetic root system of
B(U)
. Furthermore,
B(U)
is finite dimensional since
\operatorname{ht}(\alpha)<\infty
for all
\alpha\in\Delta_{\chi,E}
. This implies
B(V)
is infinite dimensional since
B(V)
is twist equivalent to

.
∎

5.

In this section, we will give a classification of finite-dimensional coradically graded pointed coquasi-Hopf algebras over finite abelian groups.

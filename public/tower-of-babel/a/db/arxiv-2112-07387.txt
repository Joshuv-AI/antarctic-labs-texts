Decomposition of higher-order Wright convex functions revisited
Zsolt Páles, Mahmood Kamil Shihab

Abstract
. In 2009, Maksa and Páles established an extension of the decomposition theorem of Ng in the context of higher-order convexity notions. They proved that a real function is Wright convex of order n n if and only if it can be decomposed as the sum of a convex function of order n n and a polynomial function of order at most n n . Their proof was based on transfinite tools in the background. The main purpose of this paper is to adopt the methods of a paper of Páles published in 2020 and establish a new and elementary proof for the theorem of Maksa and Páles.

Primary 26A51, 39B62

1.
Introduction

Throughout this paper, let
I
denote a proper open subinterval of the real line.
For a function
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
, one can define three notions of convexity in the following way:

–
f
is called
Jensen convex
on
I
, if

\displaystyle f\bigg(\frac{x+y}{2}\bigg)\leq\frac{f(x)+f(y)}{2}\qquad(x,y\in I).

–
f
is called
Wright convex
on
I
, if

\displaystyle f(tx+(1-t)y)+f((1-t)x+ty)\leq f(x)+f(y)\qquad(x,y\in I,\,t\in[0,1]).

–
f
is called
convex
on
I
, if

\displaystyle f(tx+(1-t)y)\leq tf(x)+(1-t)f(y)\qquad(x,y\in I,\,t\in[0,1]).

One can see that convexity implies Wright convexity and Wright convexity implies Jensen convexity and non of these implications can be reversed.
For an overview about the generalizations, stability and regularity properties of Wright convex functions, we refer to the list of references, which may give the impression that this subfield of functional equations and inequalities is still in the focus of research.

A celebrated result of C. T. Ng
[
18
]
established a deeper connection between convexity and Wright convexity. It characterizes Wright convex functions as those functions that are of the form
f=g+a
, where
g
is convex and
a
is additive. The original proof of the paper
[
18
]
applied de Bruijn’s theorem
[
1
]
which is related to functions which have continuous differences. Several subsequent proofs of the result of Ng (c.f., Nikodem
[
19
]
and Kominek
[
6
]
) used another approach, which was based on Rode’s theorem
[
37
]
. Basically, all the previously known proofs used transfinite induction for the construction of the additive part
a
of the decomposition. In a recent paper
[
32
]
, Páles obtained a new proof in which the convex summand
g
was first constructed in an elementary way. Therefore, there was no transfinite induction involved.

In the paper
[
14
]
, Maksa and Páles extended the decomposition theorem of Ng to the context of higher-order convexity notions. They proved that a real function is Wright convex of order
n
if and only if it can be decomposed as the sum of a convex function of order
n
and a polynomial function of order at most
n
. Their proof was again using transfinite tools in the background. The main purpose of this paper is to adopt the methods of the paper
[
32
]
and establish a new and elementary proof for the theorem of Maksa and Páles.

2.
Higher-order convex and Wright convex functions

In what follows, we are going to define several higher-order convexity concepts in terms of
difference operators
and
divided differences
.

We recall that, for a fixed real number
h
, the operator
\Delta_{h}
, acting on a real function
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
, is defined by

\displaystyle\Delta_{h}f(x):=f(x+h)-f(x)\qquad(x\in I\cap(I-h)).

Obviously, if
|h|
is small enough, then
I\cap(I-h)

is a non-void open interval again. The product of these operators
can also be defined in the usual way (see e.g. Kuczma
[
8
]
).

Given a fixed
n\in\mathbb{N}
, a map
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
is said to be
Jensen convex of order
n
(briefly
n
-Jensen convex) if

(1)

\displaystyle\Delta^{n+1}_{h}f(x)\geq 0\qquad\big(h>0,\,x\in I\cap(I-(n+1)h)\big).

A map
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
is said to be
Wright convex of order
n
(briefly
n
-Wright convex) if it satisfies the functional inequality

(2)

\displaystyle\Delta_{h_{1}}\cdots\Delta_{h_{n+1}}f(x)\geq 0\qquad\big(h_{1},\dots,h_{n+1}>0,\,x\in I\cap(I-(h_{1}+\dots+h_{n+1}))\big).

In the investigation of functional inequalities (
1
) and (
2
), those maps that fulfil these inequalities with equality play a fundamental role in the theory of linear functional equations. Therefore, for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we consider the equation

\displaystyle\Delta^{n+1}_{h}f(x)=0\qquad(h>0,\,x\in I\cap(I-(n+1)h)),

which is termed the
Fréchet functional equation
in this theory.
It is well-known (see
[
8
]
,
[
38
]
) that
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies this equation if and only if it is a
polynomial function of degree at most
n
, i.e.,
it has the representation

\displaystyle f(x)=a_{0}+a_{1}(x)+\dots+a_{n}(x)\qquad(x\in I),

where
a_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
and
a_{k}
is the
diagonalization
of some

k
-additive and symmetric function
A_{k}:\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}
, that is,

a_{k}(x)=A_{k}(x,\dots,x),\,\,(x\in\mathbb{R},\,\,k=1,\dots,n)
.
Standard polynomials are exactly the continuous polynomial functions.
On the other hand, using Hamel bases, it is not difficult to construct non-continuous polynomial functions (see
[
8
]
).

The
divided difference
of the function
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
with respect to the
pairwise distinct points
x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}\in I
is defined by

\displaystyle[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n};f]=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\frac{f(x_{i})}{\prod_{\begin{smallmatrix}j=0\\
j\neq i\end{smallmatrix}}^{n}(x_{i}-x_{j})}.

Obviously, divided differences are symmetric functions of their variables,
furthermore, it is easy to show that they enjoy the following recursive property

\displaystyle[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n};f]=\frac{[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n};f]-[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n-1};f]}{x_{n}-x_{0}}

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and pairwise distinct elements
x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}\in I
.

Based on the works of T. Popoviciu
[
33
,
34
]
, given
n\in\mathbb{N}
, a map
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
is said to be
convex of order
n
on
I
(shortly
n
-convex on
I
) if the inequality

(3)

\displaystyle[x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},x_{n+1};f]\geq 0

holds for all pairwise distinct elements
x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},x_{n+1}\in I
.
Due to the symmetry of divided differences, without loss of generality, we may assume
x_{0}<x_{1}<\cdots<x_{n}<x_{n+1}
here.

The following result was obtained in the book
[
8
]
and in a more general form in the paper
[
5
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then every
n
-convex function is
n
-​Wright convex, and every
n
-​Wright convex function is
n
-Jensen convex.

One of the main results of the paper
[
14
]
established the following generalization of Ng’s decomposition theorem
[
18
]
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
be an
n
-​Wright convex function. Then, there exist an
n
-convex function
g:I\to\mathbb{R}
and a polynomial function
P:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
of degree at most
n
such that

\displaystyle f(x)=g(x)+P(x)\qquad(x\in I).

Our aim is to obtain a new proof for this result.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
be an
n
-Jensen convex function. Then there exists a continuous
n
-convex function
g:I\to\mathbb{R}
such that
g|_{I\cap\mathbb{Q}}=f|_{I\cap\mathbb{Q}}
.

Proof.

Using
[
5
, Lemma 5.1]
, we have the following identity

\displaystyle[x,x+h,\dots,x+nh,x+(n+1)h;f]=\frac{\Delta_{h}^{n+1}f(x)}{(n+1)!h^{n+1}}\qquad(h>0,\,x\in I\cap(I-(n+1)h)).

Therefore, the
n
-Jensen convexity of
f
implies that

\displaystyle[x,x+h,x+2h\dots,x+(n+1)h;f]\geq 0\qquad(h>0,\,x\in I\cap(I-(n+1)h)).

In the terminology of the paper
[
5
]
, this property says that
f
is
(t_{0},\dots,t_{n})
-convex with
t_{0}=\dots=t_{n}=1
. According to
[
5
, Theorem 3.2]
, it follows that
f
is
(r_{0},\dots,r_{n})
-convex for all positive rational numbers
(r_{0},\dots,r_{n})
-convex, that is

(4)

\displaystyle[x,x+r_{0}h,x+(r_{0}+r_{1})h,\dots,x+(r_{0}+\dots+r_{n})h;f]\geq 0

\displaystyle(h>0,\,x\in I\cap(I-(r_{0}+\dots+r_{n})h)).

We now deduce that
f
satisfies the
n
-convexity property with rational arguments, that is,

(5)

\displaystyle[x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{n},x_{n+1};f]\geq 0\qquad(x_{0},\dots,x_{n+1}\in I\cap\mathbb{Q}\mbox{ such that $x_{i}\neq x_{j}$ if $i\neq j$}).

Indeed, let
x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{n},x_{n+1}\in I\cap\mathbb{Q}
be arbitrary. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
x_{0}<x_{1}<\dots<x_{n}<x_{n+1}
. Applying now the inequality (
4
) with
x:=x_{0}
,
h=1
and
r_{i}:=x_{i+1}-x_{i}
for
i\in\{0,\dots,n\}
, we can see that (
5
) holds.

Claim: For any compact subinterval
[a,b]\subseteq I
, there exists
L\geq 0
such that

\displaystyle|f(x)-f(y)|\leq L|x-y|\qquad(x,y\in[a,b]\cap\mathbb{Q}).

To show this claim, let
[a,b]\subseteq I
. Without loss of generality, we can assume that
a,b\in\mathbb{Q}
. Let
x,y\in[a,b]\cap\mathbb{Q}
with
x<y
be arbitrary. Then, for all pairwise distinct elements
u_{1},\dots,u_{n}\in(I\setminus[a,b])\cap\mathbb{Q}
, we get

(6)

\displaystyle[y,x,u_{1},\dots,u_{n};f]\geq 0,

that is,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{y-x}\bigg(\frac{f(y)}{\prod_{j=1}^{n}(y-u_{j})}-\frac{f(x)}{\prod_{j=1}^{n}(x-u_{j})}\bigg)\geq-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{f(u_{i})}{(u_{i}-x)(u_{i}-y)\prod_{j\in\{1,\dots,n\}\setminus\{i\}}(u_{i}-u_{j})}.

The mapping

\displaystyle[a,b]^{2}\ni(x,y)\mapsto-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{f(u_{i})}{(u_{i}-x)(u_{i}-y)\prod_{j\in\{1,\dots,n\}\setminus\{i\}}(u_{i}-u_{j})}

is continuous on
[a,b]^{2}
and therefore it is bounded from below by a
constant
C(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})
, therefore, for all
x,y\in[a,b]\cap\mathbb{Q}
, the inequality (
6
) implies

(7)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{y-x}\bigg(\frac{f(y)}{\prod_{j=1}^{n}(y-u_{j})}-\frac{f(x)}{\prod_{j=1}^{n}(x-u_{j})}\bigg)\geq C(u_{1},\dots,u_{n}).

Let first
u_{1}<\dots<u_{n}<a
be fixed elements of
I\cap\mathbb{Q}
and define
U(t):=\prod_{j=1}^{n}(t-u_{j})
for
t\in[a,b]
. Then
U
is an increasing and positive polynomial on
[a,b]
. Therefore
U\leq U(b)
and there exists a positive number
M>0
such that
|U^{\prime}|\leq M
on
[a,b]
. Hence, by the Lagrange mean value theorem,
U
is Lipschitzian over
[a,b]
with a Lipschitz modulus
M
. From (
7
), for all
x,y\in[a,b]\cap\mathbb{Q}
, it follows that

(8)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{y-x}\bigg(\frac{f(y)}{U(y)}-\frac{f(x)}{U(x)}\bigg)\geq C(u_{1},\dots,u_{n}).

By putting
x=a
, this inequality yields

\displaystyle f(y)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{U(y)}{U(a)}f(a)+U(y)C(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})(y-a)

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{U(y)}{U(a)}|f(a)|-U(y)|C(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})|(y-a)

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{U(b)}{U(a)}|f(a)|-U(b)|C(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})|(b-a),

which shows that
f
is bounded from below on
[a,b]\cap\mathbb{Q}
. On the other hand, putting
y=b
in (
8
), we can obtain that

\displaystyle f(x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{U(x)}{U(b)}f(b)+U(x)C(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})(x-b)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{U(x)}{U(b)}|f(b)|+U(x)|C(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})|(b-x)

\displaystyle\leq|f(b)|+U(b)|C(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})|(b-a),

which shows that
f
is also bounded from above on
[a,b]\cap\mathbb{Q}
. Thus, there exists a nonnegative number
K
such that
|f(x)|\leq K
for
x\in[a,b]\cap\mathbb{Q}
.
The inequality (
8
) now yields

\displaystyle f(y)-f(x)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{U(y)-U(x)}{U(x)}f(x)+U(y)C(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})(y-x)

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{U(y)-U(x)}{U(x)}|f(x)|-U(y)|C(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})|(y-x)

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{M(y-x)}{U(a)}K-U(b)|C(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})|(y-x)

\displaystyle=-\bigg(\frac{MK}{U(a)}+U(b)|C(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})|\bigg)(y-x).

Let, additionally
b<u_{n}^{\prime}
. Then
V(t):=(t-u_{n}^{\prime})\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}(t-u_{j})
for
t\in[a,b]
. Then
V
is negative polynomial on
[a,b]
. Therefore there exist positive numbers
M_{0},M_{1}
and
M_{2}
such that
M_{0}\leq|V|\leq M_{1}
and
|V^{\prime}|\leq M_{2}
on
[a,b]
. Hence again by the Lagrange mean value theorem
V
is Lipschitzian over
[a,b]
with a Lipschitz modulus
M_{2}
. From (
7
) for all
x,y\in[a,b]\cap\mathbb{Q}
we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{y-x}\bigg(\frac{f(y)}{V(y)}-\frac{f(x)}{V(x)}\bigg)\geq C(u_{1},\dots,u_{n-1},u_{n}^{\prime}),

which is equivalent to

\displaystyle f(y)-f(x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{V(y)-V(x)}{V(x)}f(x)+V(y)C(u_{1},\dots,u_{n-1},u^{\prime}_{n})(y-x)

\displaystyle\leq\bigg|\frac{V(y)-V(x)}{V(x)}f(x)\bigg|+|V(y)C(u_{1},\dots,u_{n-1},u^{\prime}_{n})|(y-x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{M_{2}|y-x|}{M_{0}}K+M_{1}|C(u_{1},\dots,u_{n-1},u^{\prime}_{n})|(y-x)

\displaystyle=\bigg(\frac{M_{2}K}{M_{0}}+M_{1}|C(u_{1}.\dots,u_{n-1},u^{\prime}_{n})|\bigg)(y-x)

Define

\displaystyle L:=\max\bigg\{\bigg(\frac{MK}{U(a)}+U(b)|C(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})|\bigg),\bigg(\frac{M_{2}K}{M_{0}}+M_{1}|C(u_{1},\dots,u_{n-1},u^{\prime}_{n})|\bigg)\bigg\}.

Therefore
f
is Lipschitz with modulus
L
on the dense set
[a,b]\cap\mathbb{Q}
.
By applying
[
32
, Lemma 1]
with
D=[a,b]\cap\mathbb{Q}
, we get that there exists a continuous function
g:I\to\mathbb{R}
such that
g|_{I\cap\mathbb{Q}}=f|_{I\cap\mathbb{Q}}
.

To complete the proof, we have to show that
g
is
n
-convex. Let
y_{0},\dots,y_{n+1}
be arbitrary pairwise distinct elements of
I
. Then, for all
j\in\{0,\dots,n+1\}
, there exist rational sequences
(x_{k,j})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
converging to
y_{j}
as
k\to\infty
with the property that the elements
x_{k,0},x_{k,1},\dots,x_{k,n},x_{k,n+1}
are pairwise distinct for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then, by applying the
n
-convexity property of

with rational arguments (i.e., inequality (
5
)), for all

, we get

\displaystyle[x_{k,0},x_{k,1},\dots,x_{k,n},x_{k,n+1};g]=[x_{k,0},x_{k,1},\dots,x_{k,n},x_{k,n+1};f]\geq 0

By the continuity of
g
the
(n+1)
st-order divided difference of
g
is a continuous function of its arguments. Thus, upon taking the limit as
k\to\infty
, the above inequality yields that

\displaystyle[y_{0},y_{1},\dots,y_{n},y_{n+1};g]\geq 0.

Therefore,
g
is
n
-convex, indeed.
∎

Now we give a new proof for Theorem
2.2
.

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

Since
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
is
n
-Wright convex, therefore by Lemma
2.1
,
f
is
n
-Jensen convex, i.e,
f
satisfies (
1
). In particular,
f|_{I\cap\mathbb{Q}}
is
n
-Jensen convex on
I\cap\mathbb{Q}
. Thus, in view of Lemma
2.3
, there exists a continuous
n
-convex function
g:I\to\mathbb{R}
such that
f|_{I\cap\mathbb{Q}}=g|_{I\cap\mathbb{Q}}
.

To complete the proof, we show that
f-g
is a polynomial function of degree at most
n
. For this we prove that
\Delta_{h}^{n+1}(f-g)(x)=0
for
h>0
and
x\in I\cap(I-(n+1)h)
. This equation is equivalent to

(9)

\displaystyle\Delta_{h}^{n+1}f(x)=\Delta_{h}^{n+1}g(x)\qquad(h>0,x\in I\cap(I-(n+1)h)).

More generally, we will show that

(10)

\displaystyle\Delta_{h_{0}}\cdots\Delta_{h_{n}}f(x)=\Delta_{h_{0}}\cdots\Delta_{h_{n}}g(x)

holds for all
h_{0},\dots,h_{n}>0
and
x\in I\cap(I-(h_{0}+\dots+h_{n}))
.

By the
n
-Wright convexity of
f
, for all
h_{0},\dots,h_{n}>0
and
x\in I\cap(I-(h_{0}+\dots+h_{n}))
, we have the inequality

(11)

\displaystyle\Delta_{h_{0}}\cdots\Delta_{h_{n}}f(x)\geq 0.

This implies that
\Delta_{h_{1}}\cdots\Delta_{h_{n}}f:I\cap(I-(h_{1}+\dots+h_{n}))\to\mathbb{R}
is nondecreasing for all
h_{1},\dots,h_{n}>0
. On the other hand,
\Delta_{h_{1}}\cdots\Delta_{h_{n}}g:I\cap(I-(h_{1}+\dots+h_{n}))\to\mathbb{R}
is continuous and the equality
f|_{I\cap\mathbb{Q}}=g|_{I\cap\mathbb{Q}}
gives us
\Delta_{h_{1}}\cdots\Delta_{h_{n}}f(x)=\Delta_{h_{1}}\cdots\Delta_{h_{n}}g(x)
for
x\in I\cap(I-(h_{1}+\dots+h_{n}))\cap\mathbb{Q}
whenever
h_{1},\dots,h_{n}\in\mathbb{Q}_{+}
. Applying
[
32
, Lemma 3]
, it follows that these two functions are equal to each other also at irrational points of
I\cap(I-(h_{1}+\dots+h_{n}))
, that is,

\displaystyle\Delta_{h_{1}}\cdots\Delta_{h_{n}}f(x)=\Delta_{h_{1}}\cdots\Delta_{h_{n}}g(x)\qquad(h_{1},\dots,h_{n}\in\mathbb{Q}_{+},x\in I\cap(I-(h_{1}+\dots+h_{n})).

Applying this equality at
x+h_{0}
and at
x
, and then subtracting the two equalities side by side, we can see that (
10
) is valid if
h_{0}>0
,
h_{1},\dots,h_{n}\in\mathbb{Q}_{+}
and
x\in I\cap(I-(h_{0}+\dots+h_{n}))
.

Let
k\in\{0,\dots,n\}
and consider the statement
S_{k}
which says that (
10
) holds if
h_{0},\dots,h_{k}>0
,
h_{k+1},\dots,h_{n}\in\mathbb{Q}_{+}
and

. According to the previous argument, we have that

is true. Now assume that, for some

holds. We show that

is also valid. To prove this let

,

\displaystyle\bigg(\prod_{i\in\{0,\dots,n\}\setminus\{k+1\}}\Delta_{h_{i}}\bigg)\Delta_{h_{k+1}-h^{\prime}_{k+1}}f(x+h^{\prime}_{k+1})\geq 0.

Therefore, using the statement

\displaystyle\Delta_{h_{0}}\cdots\Delta_{h_{n}}f(x)

\displaystyle=\bigg(\prod_{i\in\{0,\dots,n\}\setminus\{k+1\}}\Delta_{h_{i}}\bigg)\cdot\Delta_{h_{k+1}}f(x)

\displaystyle=\bigg(\prod_{i\in\{0,\dots,n\}\setminus\{k+1\}}\Delta_{h_{i}}\bigg)\big(f(x+h_{k+1})-f(x)\big)

\displaystyle=\bigg(\prod_{i\in\{0,\dots,n\}\setminus\{k+1\}}\Delta_{h_{i}}\bigg)\big(f(x+h_{k+1})-f(x+h^{\prime}_{k+1})+f(x+h^{\prime}_{k+1})-f(x)\big)

\displaystyle=\bigg(\prod_{i\in\{0,\dots,n\}\setminus\{k+1\}}\Delta_{h_{i}}\bigg)\big(\Delta_{h_{k+1}-h^{\prime}_{k+1}}f(x+h^{\prime}_{k+1})+\Delta_{h^{\prime}_{k+1}}f(x)\big)

\displaystyle\geq\bigg(\prod_{i\in\{0,\dots,n\}\setminus\{k+1\}}\Delta_{h_{i}}\bigg)\Delta_{h^{\prime}_{k+1}}f(x)

\displaystyle=\bigg(\prod_{i\in\{0,\dots,n\}\setminus\{k+1\}}\Delta_{h_{i}}\bigg)\Delta_{h^{\prime}_{k+1}}g(x).

Using that
g
is continuous, after taking the limit
h_{k+1}^{\prime}\to h_{k+1}
, we get that

\displaystyle\Delta_{h_{0}}\cdots\Delta_{h_{n}}f(x)\geq\bigg(\prod_{i\in\{0,\dots,n\}\setminus\{k+1\}}\Delta_{h_{i}}\bigg)\Delta_{h_{k+1}}g(x)=\Delta_{h_{0}}\cdots\Delta_{h_{n}}g(x).

To prove the other direction let
h^{\prime\prime}_{k+1}>h_{k+1}
be an arbitrary rational number, again by the
n
-Wright convexity of
f
and statement
S_{k}
, we get

\displaystyle\Delta_{h_{0}}\cdots\Delta_{h_{n}}f(x)

\displaystyle=\bigg(\prod_{i\in\{0,\dots,n\}\setminus\{k+1\}}\Delta_{h_{i}}\bigg)\cdot\Delta_{h_{k+1}}f(x)

\displaystyle=\bigg(\prod_{i\in\{0,\dots,n\}\setminus\{k+1\}}\Delta_{h_{i}}\bigg)\big(f(x+h_{k+1})-f(x)\big)

\displaystyle=\bigg(\prod_{i\in\{0,\dots,n\}\setminus\{k+1\}}\Delta_{h_{i}}\bigg)\big(f(x+h_{k+1})-f(x+h^{\prime\prime}_{k+1})+f(x+h^{\prime\prime}_{k+1})-f(x)\big)

\displaystyle=\bigg(\prod_{i\in\{0,\dots,n\}\setminus\{k+1\}}\Delta_{h_{i}}\bigg)\big(-\Delta_{h^{\prime\prime}_{k+1}-h_{k+1}}f(x+h_{k+1})+\Delta_{h^{\prime\prime}_{k+1}}f(x)\big)

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\prod_{i\in\{0,\dots,n\}\setminus\{k+1\}}\Delta_{h_{i}}\bigg)\Delta_{h^{\prime\prime}_{k+1}}f(x)

\displaystyle=\bigg(\prod_{i\in\{0,\dots,n\}\setminus\{k+1\}}\Delta_{h_{i}}\bigg)\Delta_{h^{\prime\prime}_{k+1}}g(x).

Upon taking the limit
h_{k+1}^{\prime\prime}\to h_{k+1}
and using that
g
is continuous, we get that

\displaystyle\Delta_{h_{0}}\cdots\Delta_{h_{n}}f(x)\leq\bigg(\prod_{i\in\{0,\dots,n\}\setminus\{k+1\}}\Delta_{h_{i}}\bigg)\Delta_{h_{k+1}}g(x)=\Delta_{h_{0}}\cdots\Delta_{h_{n}}g(x).

Combining the above two inequalities, we can see that
S_{k+1}
is valid and hence, we have proved that
S_{k}
is true for all
k\in\{0,\dots,n\}
, in particular, for
k=n
. This means that (
10
) is satisfied for any
h_{0},\cdots,h_{n}>0
. Consequently, it holds with
h_{0}=\cdots=h_{n}=h>0
, proving that (
9
) is satisfied. This implies that
f-g
is a polynomial function of degree at most
n
.

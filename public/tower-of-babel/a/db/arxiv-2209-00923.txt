Convergence of the empirical measure in expected Wasserstein distance: non asymptotic explicit bounds in ℝ d {\mathbb{R}}^{d} Thanks: I thank J.L. Verger-Gaugry and T. Le Gouic for fruitful discussions and the anonymous referee for his comments
Nicolas Fournier

Abstract
. We provide some non asymptotic bounds, with explicit constants, that measure the rate of convergence, in expected Wasserstein distance, of the empirical measure associated to an i.i.d. N N -sample of a given probability distribution on ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} .



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
60F25, 65C05

1.
Introduction

Let
d\geq 1
.
We consider
\mu\in{\mathcal{P}}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})
, the set of probability measures on
{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}
,
and an i.i.d. sequence
(X_{k})_{k\geq 1}

of
\mu
-distributed random variables. For
N\geq 1
, we introduce the empirical measure

(1)

\mu_{N}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\delta_{X_{k}}.

Estimating the rate of convergence of
\mu_{N}
to
\mu
is of course a fundamental problem,
and it seems that measuring this convergence in Wasserstein distance
is nowadays a widely adopted choice.
Some seminal works on the subject are those by Dudley
[
7
]
,
Ajtai-Komlós-Tusnády
[
1
]
and Dobrić-Yukich
[
6
]
. More recently, some results
have been established by Bolley-Guillin-Villani
[
4
]
, Boissard-Le Gouic
[
3
]
,
Le Gouic
[
9
]
, Dereich-Scheutzow-Schottstedt
[
5
]
and Fournier-Guillin
[
8
]
.
In particular, we can find in
[
8
]
the following result.

Fix some norm
|\cdot|
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and consider, for
p>0
and
\mu,\nu\in{\mathcal{P}}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})
, the transport cost

{\mathcal{T}}_{p}(\mu,\nu)=\inf\Big\{\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\times{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}}|x-y|^{p}\xi({\rm d}x,{\rm d}y):\xi\in{\mathcal{H}}(\mu,\nu)\Big\},

where

{\mathcal{H}}(\mu,\nu)
stands for the set of probability measures on
{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\times{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}
with marginals

\mu
and
\nu
. It holds that
{\mathcal{T}}_{p}={\mathcal{W}}_{p}^{p}
, with the usual notation, if
p\geq 1
.
For
q>0
, we also define

{\mathcal{M}}_{q}(\mu)={\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}}}|x|^{q}\mu({\rm d}x).

There exists a constant
C_{d,p,q}
such that for all
\mu\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, for all
N\geq 1
,
with
\mu_{N}
defined in (
1
),

\mathbb{E}[{\mathcal{T}}_{p}(\mu_{N},\mu)]\leq C_{d,p,q}[{\mathcal{M}}_{q}(\mu)]^{p/q}\times\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}N^{-1/2}&\hbox{if $p>d/2$ and $q>2p$},\\[3.0pt]
N^{-1/2}\log(1+N)&\hbox{if $p=d/2$ and $q>2p$},\\[3.0pt]
N^{-p/d}&\hbox{if $p\in(0,d/2)$ and $q>dp/(d-p)$}.\end{array}\right.

This bound is sharp, as well as the number of required moments,
but the constant
C_{d,p,q}
is not explicit.
Some explicit constants are provided in
[
5
]
when
p>d/2
, but they are quite large.
One can also get some explicit bounds using
[
9
]
, see a few lines below.

It seems that applied scientists really need some explicit values for the constant

C_{d,p,q}
. If following the proofs in
[
5
,
8
]
, one finds
some rather large constants.
But revisiting these proofs and optimizing as often as possible the computations,
which is the purpose of the present paper,
we obtain some rather reasonable constants, when using the maximum norm
|\cdot|_{\infty}
on
{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}
.
In particular, they remain finite
as the dimension tends to infinity (but of course, the rate of convergence in
N^{-p/d}
is worse and worse).

The reason why the maximum norm
|\cdot|_{\infty}
is used in
[
5
,
8
]
it that the proofs rely
on a partitioning of the unit ball, and that a cube is very easy to cut into
smaller cubes. We of course deduce some bounds for the more natural Euclidean norm
|\cdot|_{2}
,
multiplying the constant by
d^{p/2}
. This leads to a constant (for the Euclidean norm)
that explodes as
d\to\infty
.

Using similar arguments, together with some ideas found in Boissard-Le Gouic
[
3
]
and Weed-Bach
[
14
]
, Lei
[
10
, Theorem 3.1]

proves that one can also find, in the case of the Euclidean norm,
some constants (that he does not make explicit) that remain finite as
d\to\infty
.
We also produce, in the present paper, some explicit constants in this context.

Finally, let us mention that Dudley
[
7
]
and more recently
Boissard-Le Gouic
[
3
]
, Le Gouic
[
9
]
and
Weed-Bach
[
14
]
study the very interesting problem
of obtaining some rates of convergence depending on the
true
dimension of the problem:
if e.g.
\mu
is a measure on
{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}
but is actually carried by a manifold of lower dimension, what about
the rate of convergence ?
They introduce some notion of dimension of the measure
\mu
and get some bounds
of
\mathbb{E}[{\mathcal{T}}_{p}(\mu_{N},\mu)]
in terms of this dimension.
Let us mention the following formula, that can be found in the work of Le Gouic
[
9
, Theorem 3.2]

(after correcting two small mistakes, with the agreement of the author):
for any metric space
(E,d)
with finite diameter
D
, for
\mu
a probability measure on
E
and
for
\mu_{N}
the associated empirical measure, it holds that for all
N\geq 1
, all
p\geq 1
, all
k\geq 0
,

\mathbb{E}[{\mathcal{T}}_{p}(\mu_{N},\mu)]\leq 2^{2p-1}\Big[D^{p}2^{1-kp}+\frac{2^{p}}{\sqrt{N}}\int_{D^{p}2^{-(k+1)p}}^{D^{p}2^{-p}}\sqrt{{\mathcal{N}}(E,u^{1/p})-1}\;{\rm d}u\Big],

where
{\mathcal{N}}(E,{\varepsilon})
is the minimal number of balls of radius
{\varepsilon}
required to cover
E
.
This is a very deep and elegant formula. Let us mention that when applied to the case where

E=B(0,1)
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, after optimizing in
k
,
this produces some good bounds, with some constants that are a little greater than what
we will find below. Le Gouic
[
9
]
also studies the non compact case
E={\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, without really tracking
the constants.

We refer to the introductions of
[
3
,
5
,
8
,
9
,
14
]
for some much more detailed presentations
of the subject and its numerous applications. Let us also mention the closely related topic of

optimal matching
, see Barthe-Bordenave
[
2
]
and the references therein.

Let us emphasize that the present paper contains no new idea: all the deep arguments
have been previously introduced in the above mentioned papers.
We only try to handle some slightly more precise computations.

2.
Main results

2.1.
Basic notation

For
m\in[1,\infty)
and
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, we set

|x|_{m}=\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{d}|x_{i}|^{m}\Big)^{1/m}\qquad\hbox{and}\qquad|x|_{\infty}=\max\{|x_{1}|,\dots,|x_{d}|\}.

For
p>0
, for
\mu,\nu
in
{\mathcal{P}}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})
and for
m\in[1,\infty)\cup\{\infty\}
, we set

{\mathcal{T}}_{p}^{(m)}(\mu,\nu)=\inf\Big\{\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\times{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}}|x-y|_{m}^{p}\xi({\rm d}x,{\rm d}y):\xi\in{\mathcal{H}}(\mu,\nu)\Big\},

where
{\mathcal{H}}(\mu,\nu)
is the set of probability measures on
{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\times{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}
with marginals

\mu
and
\nu
.
For
q>0
, for
\mu\in{\mathcal{P}}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})
and for
m\in[1,\infty)\cup\{\infty\}
, we define

{\mathcal{M}}_{q}^{(m)}(\mu)={\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}}}|x|_{m}^{q}\mu({\rm d}x).

We of course have, since
|\cdot|_{\infty}\leq|\cdot|_{m}\leq d^{1/m}|\cdot|_{\infty}
,

(2)

{\mathcal{T}}_{p}^{(\infty)}(\mu,\nu)\leq{\mathcal{T}}_{p}^{(m)}(\mu,\nu)\leq d^{p/m}{\mathcal{T}}_{p}^{(\infty)}(\mu,\nu)\quad\hbox{and}\quad{\mathcal{M}}_{q}^{(\infty)}(\mu)\leq{\mathcal{M}}_{q}^{(m)}(\mu)\leq d^{q/m}{\mathcal{M}}_{q}^{(\infty)}(\mu).

2.2.
Covering number

Our proofs are based on a suitable partitioning of the unit ball. The case
of the maximum norm is not hard, because it is easy to cut a cube into smaller cubes.
The other cases are more intricate. For
{\varepsilon}\in(0,1]
and
m\in[1,\infty)
, we define

(3)

N_{\varepsilon}^{(m)}=\min\Big\{k\in{\mathbb{N}}:\exists\;x_{1},\dots,x_{k}\in B_{m}(0,1)\hbox{ such that }B_{m}(0,1)\subset\cup_{i=1}^{k}B_{m}(x_{i},{\varepsilon})\Big\},

where
B_{m}(x,{\varepsilon})=\{y\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}:|x-y|_{m}<{\varepsilon}\}
, as well as

(4)

K_{d}^{(m)}=\sup_{{\varepsilon}\in(0,1]}{\varepsilon}^{d}N_{\varepsilon}^{(m)},\quad\hbox{so that for all }{\varepsilon}\in(0,1],\quad N_{\varepsilon}^{(m)}\leq K_{d}^{(m)}\,{\varepsilon}^{-d}.

See (
7
) and (
8
) below for some estimates of these covering numbers.

2.3.
Main result

For
x>0
and
q>s>0
, we set

(5)

H(x,s,q)=\Big(x\frac{q-s}{s}+(1+x)\Big(\frac{q}{s}\Big)^{q/(q-s)}\Big)^{s/q}\frac{q}{q-s}.

Observe that for each
x>0
, each
s>0
,
\lim_{q\to\infty}H(x,s,q)=1
.
The following formulas, that constitute the main results of the paper, are a little complicated,
but rather easy to calculate explicitly with a computer.

Theorem 1
.

We fix
p>0
and
\mu\in{\mathcal{P}}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})
, we set

{\varepsilon}_{p}=\max\{2^{-1},2^{-p}\}
and we recall (
1
).

(i) If
p>d/2
and
q>2p
, then for
m\in[1,\infty)\cup\{\infty\}
, for all
N\geq 1
,

\mathbb{E}[{\mathcal{T}}_{p}^{(m)}(\mu_{N},\mu)]\leq 2^{p}\frac{\kappa_{d,p}^{(m)}}{\sqrt{N}}[{\mathcal{M}}_{q}^{(m)}(\mu)]^{p/q}\theta_{d,p,q}^{(m)},\quad\hbox{where}\quad\theta_{d,p,q}^{(m)}=H\Big(\frac{{\varepsilon}_{p}}{\kappa_{d,p}^{(m)}},2p,q\Big),

with

\kappa_{d,p}^{(\infty)}=\frac{2^{d/2-1}}{1-2^{d/2-p}},

and with, if
m\in[1,\infty)
,

\kappa_{d,p}^{(m)}=\min\{\kappa_{d,p,r}^{(m)}:r\geq 2\},\qquad\hbox{where}\qquad\kappa_{d,p,r}^{(m)}=\sqrt{K_{d}^{(m)}}\frac{2^{p-1-d/2}r^{p+d/2}}{(r-1)^{p}(1-r^{d/2-p})}.

(ii) If
p=d/2
and
q>2p
, then for
m\in[1,\infty)\cup\{\infty\}
, for all
N\geq 1
,

\mathbb{E}[{\mathcal{T}}_{p}^{(m)}(\mu_{N},\mu)]\leq 2^{p}\frac{\kappa_{d,p,N}^{(m)}}{\sqrt{N}}[{\mathcal{M}}_{q}^{(m)}(\mu)]^{p/q}\theta_{d,p,q,N}^{(m)},\quad\hbox{where}\quad\theta_{d,p,q,N}^{(m)}=H\Big(\frac{{\varepsilon}_{p}}{\kappa_{d,p,N}^{(m)}},2p,q\Big),

with (here
\log_{+}(x)=\max\{\log x,0\}
)

\kappa_{d,p,N}^{(\infty)}=\frac{2^{p-1}}{p\log 2}\log_{+}\Big((2^{1-p}-2^{1-2p})\sqrt{N}\Big)+\frac{2^{p-1}}{1-2^{-p}},

and with, if
m\in[1,\infty)
,

\kappa_{d,p,N}^{(m)}=\min\{\kappa_{d,p,N,r}^{(m)}:r\geq 2\},

where

\kappa_{d,p,N,r}^{(m)}=\frac{\sqrt{K_{d}^{(m)}}}{2}\frac{r^{2p}}{(r-1)^{p}p\log r}\log_{+}\Big(2^{p+1}(r^{-p}-r^{-2p})\sqrt{\frac{N}{K_{d}^{(m)}}}\Big)+\frac{\sqrt{K_{d}^{(m)}}}{2}\frac{r^{3p}}{(r-1)^{p}(r^{p}-1)}.

(iii) If
p\in(0,d/2)
and
q>dp/(d-p)
, then for
m\in[1,\infty)\cup\{\infty\}
, for all
N\geq 1
,

\mathbb{E}[{\mathcal{T}}_{p}^{(m)}(\mu_{N},\mu)]\leq 2^{p}\frac{\kappa_{d,p}^{(m)}}{N^{p/d}}[{\mathcal{M}}_{q}^{(m)}(\mu)]^{p/q}\theta_{d,p,q}^{(m)},\quad\hbox{where}\quad\theta_{d,p,q}^{(m)}=H\Big(\frac{2^{1-2p/d}{\varepsilon}_{p}}{\kappa_{d,p}^{(m)}},\frac{dp}{d-p},q\Big),

with

\kappa_{d,p}^{(\infty)}=\frac{2^{p-2p/d}(1-2^{-d/2})^{1-2p/d}}{1-2^{p-d/2}},

and with, if
m\in[1,\infty)
,

\kappa_{d,p}^{(m)}=\min\{\kappa_{d,p,r}^{(m)}:r\geq 2\},\qquad\hbox{where}\qquad\kappa_{d,p,r}^{(m)}=\Big(\frac{K_{d}^{(m)}}{4}\Big)^{p/d}\frac{r^{2p}(1-r^{-d/2})^{1-2p/d}}{(r-1)^{p}(1-r^{p-d/2})}.

2.4.
Comments

By invariance by translation, we can replace
{\mathcal{M}}_{q}^{(m)}(\mu)
, in all the formulas, by

\inf\Big\{{\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}}}\color[rgb]{0,0,0}|x-x_{0}|_{m}^{q}\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mu({\rm d}x):x_{0}\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\Big\}.

Let us next observe, and we will see that this is often advantageous, that
concerning the bound of
\mathbb{E}[{\mathcal{T}}_{p}^{(m)}(\mu_{N},\mu)]
when
m\in[1,\infty)
, we can replace, by (
2
),

\bullet

\kappa^{(m)}_{d,p}
by
d^{p/m}\kappa_{d,p}^{(\infty)}
and
\theta^{(m)}_{d,p,q}
by
\theta^{(\infty)}_{d,p,q}
in items (i) and (iii);

\bullet

\kappa^{(m)}_{d,p,N}
by
d^{p/m}\kappa_{d,p,N}^{(\infty)}
and
\theta^{(m)}_{d,p,q,N}
by

\theta^{(\infty)}_{d,p,q,N}

in item (ii).

In each case, we present the bound under the form

(
diameter of
B_{m}(0,1)\Big)^{p}
\!\!\times
(
bound in the compact case
)
\times[{\mathcal{M}}_{q}^{(m)}(\mu)]^{p/q}\times
(
\theta_{d,p,q}^{(m)}
or
\theta_{d,p,q,N}^{(m)}
)
,

where
diameter of
B_{m}(0,1)
equals
2
and where by
compact case

we mean the case where
\mu
is supported by the ball
B_{m}(0,1/2)
with diameter
1
.

One can tediously check that in each case,
q\mapsto\theta_{d,p,q}^{(m)}

(or
q\mapsto\theta_{d,p,q,N}^{(m)}
) is decreasing
and tends to
1
as
q\to\infty
. Hence if we are in the compact case, i.e. if
\mu
is supported in

B_{m}(0,1/2)
, then

{\mathcal{M}}_{q}^{(m)}(\mu)\leq 2^{-q}
for all
q>0
, and we find that

\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\lim_{q\to\infty}\hbox{{\tt\Big(diameter of $B_{m}(0,1)\Big)^{p}$}$\times[{\mathcal{M}}_{q}^{(m)}(\mu)]^{p/q}\times${\tt\Big($\theta_{d,p,q}^{(m)}$ or $\theta_{d,p,q,N}^{(m)}$\Big)}}=1,\color[rgb]{0,0,0}

which justifies the denomination
bound in the compact case
.

One can tediously check
from (iii) that
p\mapsto(\kappa_{d,p}^{(m)})^{1/p}
is increasing for
p\in(0,d/2)
, which is natural
by monotony in
p
of
[{\mathcal{T}}_{p}(\mu,\nu)]^{1/p}
. Actually, when
m\in[1,\infty)
, it holds that

p\mapsto\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(\kappa_{d,p,r}^{(m)})^{1/p}
is increasing for
p\in(0,d/2)
for each
r\geq 2
.

However, in the non compact case, we did not manage to guarantee such a property: it does not hold true that
in item (iii),
p\mapsto[\kappa_{d,p}^{(m)}\theta_{d,p,q}^{(m)}]^{1/p}
is increasing
for
p\in(0,d/2)
, as it should. Hence it may be sometimes be preferable to use the bound:
for
p\in(0,d/2)
and
q>dp/(d-p)
,

(6)

\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbb{E}[{\mathcal{T}}_{p}^{(m)}(\mu_{N},\mu)]\!\leq\!\inf_{p^{\prime}\in[p,d/2)}\mathbb{E}[{\mathcal{T}}_{p^{\prime}}^{(m)}(\mu_{N},\mu)]^{p/p^{\prime}}\!\leq\!\inf_{p^{\prime}\in[p,d/2)}2^{p}\frac{[\kappa_{d,p^{\prime}}^{(m)}]^{p/p^{\prime}}}{N^{p/d}}[{\mathcal{M}}_{q}^{(m)}(\mu)]^{p/q}[\theta_{d,p^{\prime},q}^{(m)}]^{p/p^{\prime}},\color[rgb]{0,0,0}

with the convention that
\theta_{d,p^{\prime},q}^{(m)}=\infty
if
q\leq dp^{\prime}/(d-p^{\prime})
.
This is the major default of this work. We identified some computations that
might be done more carefully, but this led to awful complications, without producing some marked improvements.

Finally, one can easily check from (iii)
that for any
p>0
,

\lim_{d\to\infty}\kappa_{d,p}^{(\infty)}=2^{p}\qquad\hbox{ and }\qquad\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\lim_{d\to\infty}\kappa_{d,p}^{(2)}=4^{p}.\color[rgb]{0,0,0}

Concerning the Euclidean case, we use (
8
) below which implies that
\lim_{d\to\infty}(K_{d}^{(2)})^{1/d}=1
,
we find that
\lim_{d\to\infty}\kappa_{d,p,r}^{(2)}=(r-1)^{-p}r^{2p}
for each
r\geq 2
, and this (optimally)
equals
4^{p}
with
r=2
. When
m\in[1,\infty)\setminus\{2\}
, we deduce from (
7
) that

\limsup_{d\to\infty}\kappa_{d,p}^{(m)}\leq 12^{p}.

2.7.
On a possible lowerbound

As mentioned to us by Pagès, we have the following lowerbound,
holding for any
m\in[1,\infty)\cup\{\infty\}
. Consider
X_{1},\dots,X_{N}
independent and
\mu
-distributed.
It holds that
{\mathcal{T}}_{p}^{(m)}(\mu_{N},\mu)\geq{\mathcal{S}}_{p}^{(m)}(\mu;X_{1},\dots,X_{N})
, where

{\mathcal{S}}_{p}^{(m)}(\mu;x_{1},\dots,x_{d})=\inf\Big\{{\mathcal{T}}_{p}^{(m)}\Big(\mu,\sum_{i=1}^{N}\alpha_{i}\delta_{x_{i}}\Big):(\alpha_{i})_{i=1,\dots,N}\in[0,1]^{N},\;\sum_{i=1}^{N}\alpha_{i}=1\Big\},

and Luschgy-Pagès show in
[
11
]
that, under some technical conditions on
\mu
,

\lim_{N\to\infty}N^{-p/d}\mathbb{E}[{\mathcal{S}}_{p}^{(m)}(\mu;X_{1},\dots,X_{n})]=\frac{\Gamma(1+p/d)}{[\lambda_{d}(B_{(m)}(0,1))]^{p/d}}\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}}[f(x)]^{1-p/d}{\rm d}x,

where
f
is the density of
\mu
, where
\lambda_{d}
is the Lebesgue measure on
{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}

and where
\Gamma
is the classical
\Gamma
function.
Choosing for
\mu
the uniform law on
B_{m}(0,1/2)
, we find that

\liminf_{N\to\infty}N^{-p/d}\mathbb{E}[{\mathcal{T}}_{p}^{(m)}(\mu_{N},\mu)]\geq\lim_{N\to\infty}N^{-p/d}\mathbb{E}[{\mathcal{S}}_{p}^{(m)}(\mu;X_{1},\dots,X_{n})]=\frac{\Gamma(1+p/d)}{2^{p}}=:\gamma_{d,p},

to be compared with
\kappa_{d,p}^{(m)}
when
p\in(0,d/2)
.
This may be a rough lowerbound, because this is an asymptotic bound as
N\to\infty
, and because

{\mathcal{S}}_{p}^{(m)}(\mu;X_{1},\dots,X_{N})
is likely to be really smaller
than
{\mathcal{T}}_{p}^{(m)}(\mu_{N},\mu)
(in particular it decreases in
N^{-p/d}
when
p>d/2
while
{\mathcal{T}}_{p}^{(m)}(\mu_{N},\mu)

decreases in
N^{-1/2}
in general when
p>d/2
).

We have
\gamma_{d,1}\in(0.44,0.5)
for all
d\geq 3
, to be compared with
the numerators in Tables 1 and 3. Hence when
p=1
and say
d\geq 4
, see Table 1,

\kappa_{d,1}^{(\infty)}
is at worst
(2.45/0.44)\simeq 5.6
times too large.
When
p=1
and
d\geq 8
, see Table 3,
\min\{\kappa_{d,1}^{(2)},\sqrt{d}\kappa_{d,1}^{(\infty)}\}

is at worst
(6.74/0.44)\simeq 15.4
times too large.
We hope this is pessimistic.

It holds that
\sqrt{\gamma_{d,2}}\in(0.47,0.5)
for all
d\geq 5
,
to be compared with the numerators in Tables 2 and 4. When
p=2
and
d\geq 5
, see Table 2,

(\kappa_{d,2}^{(\infty)})^{1/2}
is at worst
(2.75/0.47)\simeq 5.9
times too large.
When
p=2
and
d\geq 8
, see Table 4,
\min\{(\kappa_{d,2}^{(2)})^{1/2},\sqrt{d}(\kappa_{d,2}^{(\infty)})^{1/2}\}

is at worst
(6.76/0.47)\simeq 14.4
times too large.
Again, we hope this is pessimistic.

We do not discuss the non compact case, but the numerical results do not seem
quite favorable.

2.8.
The case with a low order finite moment

Since this last result is likely to be much
less useful for applications, we only treat the case of the maximum norm.

Theorem 2
.

Let
q>p>0
such that
q<\min\{2p,dp/(d-p)\}
, i.e.
q\in(p,2p)
if

p\geq d/2
and
q\in(p,dp/(d-p))
if
p\in(0,d/2]
.
Fix

.
For all

,

\mathbb{E}[{\mathcal{T}}_{p}^{(\infty)}(\mu_{N},\mu)]\leq 2^{p}[{\mathcal{M}}_{q}^{(\infty)}(\mu)]^{p/q}\frac{\zeta_{d,p,q}^{(\infty)}}{N^{(q-p)/q}},

where, setting
{\varepsilon}_{p}=\max\{2^{-1},2^{-p}\}
,

\zeta_{d,p,q}^{(\infty)}=\Big[{\varepsilon}_{p}2^{2p/q-1}+\Big(\frac{2^{d-1}}{2^{d/2}-1}\Big)^{2(q-p)/q}\frac{1-2^{-p}}{1-2^{d-p-dp/q}}\Big]\min_{a\in(1,\infty)}\Big(\frac{a^{p}}{a^{p-q/2}-1}+\frac{a^{p}}{1-a^{p-q}}\Big).

Plan of the paper

In Section

In Section

.

3.
Upperbound of the transport cost between two measures

The result we prove in this section, Proposition
4
,
is more or less classical, see Boissard-Le Gouic
[
3
, Proposition 1.1]
,
Dereich-Scheutzow-Schottstedt
[
5
, Lemma 2 and Theorem 3]
,
Fournier-Guillin
[
8
, Lemma 5]
and Weed-Bach
[
14
, Proposition 1]
.
As noted in
[
14
]
, similar ideas can already be found in Ajtai-Komlós-Tusnády
[
1
]
.
However, we provide a slightly more
precise version, that allows us to get some smaller constants.
We consider fixed the following objects.

Setting 3
.

(a) We fix a norm
|\cdot|
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. We denote by
B(x,r)=\{y\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}:|x-y|<r\}
the corresponding balls,
by
{\mathcal{T}}_{p}(\mu,\nu)=\inf_{\xi\in{\mathcal{H}}(\mu,\nu)}\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\times{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}}|x-y|^{p}\xi({\rm d}x,{\rm d}y)

the corresponding transport cost, and by
{\mathcal{M}}_{q}(\mu)=\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}}|x|^{q}\mu({\rm d}x)
the corresponding moments.

(b) Let
G_{0}=B(0,1)
. For
a>1
, we set
G_{0}^{a}=G_{0}
and, for all
n\geq 1
,

G_{n}^{a}=B(0,a^{n})\setminus B(0,a^{n-1})
.

(c) We consider a family
({\mathcal{Q}}_{\ell})_{\ell=0,\dots,k}
of nested partitions of
G_{0}
such that
{\mathcal{Q}}_{0}=\{G_{0}\}
.
For each
\ell=1,\dots,k
, each
C\in{\mathcal{Q}}_{\ell}
,
there exists a unique
F\in{\mathcal{Q}}_{\ell-1}
such that
C\subset F
; we then say that
C
is a child of
F
.
For
\ell=0,\dots,k
, we denote by
|{\mathcal{Q}}_{\ell}|
the cardinal of

{\mathcal{Q}}_{\ell}
and we set

\delta_{\ell}=\max_{C\in{\mathcal{Q}}_{\ell}}\sup_{x,y\in C}|x-y|.

For
C\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
r>0
, we put
rC=\{rx:x\in C\}
. Recall that
{\varepsilon}_{p}=2^{-1}\lor 2^{-p}
.

Proposition 4
.

We adopt Setting
3
and consider

\mu,\nu\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.
For all
a>1
,
there are some nonnegative numbers

(r_{a,n,\ell}(\mu,\nu))_{n\geq 0,\ell=0,\dots,k-1}
satisfying

(9)

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-1}r_{a,n,\ell}(\mu,\nu)\leq 1

for all
n\geq 0
and, with the convention that
0/0=0
,

(10)

\displaystyle r_{a,n,\ell}(\mu,\nu)\!\leq\!\frac{1}{2}\sum_{F\in{\mathcal{Q}}_{\ell}}\Big(\frac{\mu(a^{n}F\cap G_{n}^{a})}{\mu(G_{n}^{a})}\land\frac{\nu(a^{n}F\cap G_{n}^{a})}{\nu(G_{n}^{a})}\Big)\hskip-5.0pt\sum_{C\!\hbox{\tiny{ child of }}\!F}\Big|\frac{\mu(a^{n}C\cap G_{n}^{a})}{\mu(a^{n}F\cap G_{n}^{a})}-\frac{\nu(a^{n}C\cap G_{n}^{a})}{\nu(a^{n}F\cap G_{n}^{a})}\Big|

for all
n\geq 0
, all
\ell=0,\dots,k-1
,
and such that for all
p>0
,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{T}}_{p}(\mu,\nu)\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}a^{pn}\Big(2^{p}{\varepsilon}_{p}|\mu(G_{n}^{a})-\nu(G_{n}^{a})|+(\mu(G_{n}^{a})\land\nu(G_{n}^{a}))\Big[\delta_{k}^{p}+\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-1}\delta_{\ell}^{p}r_{a,n,\ell}(\mu,\nu)\Big]\Big).

The coefficients
r_{a,n,\ell}(\mu,\nu)
are actually explicit, but it seems difficult
to use more than the properties (
9
)-(
10
).
We start with the compact case.

Lemma 5
.

Let
\mu,\nu\in{\mathcal{P}}(B(0,1))
. There is

(u_{\ell}(\mu,\nu))_{\ell=0,\dots,k-1}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{k}
satisfying

(11)

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-1}u_{\ell}(\mu,\nu)\leq 1

and

(12)

\displaystyle u_{\ell}(\mu,\nu)\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{F\in{\mathcal{Q}}_{\ell}}(\mu(F)\land\nu(F))\hskip-5.0pt\sum_{C\!\hbox{\tiny{ child of }}\!F}\Big|\frac{\mu(C)}{\mu(F)}-\frac{\nu(C)}{\nu(F)}\Big|,\qquad\ell=0,\dots,k-1,

and such that for all
p>0
,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{T}}_{p}(\mu,\nu)\leq

\displaystyle\delta_{k}^{p}+\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-1}\delta_{\ell}^{p}u_{\ell}(\mu,\nu).

Proof.

For all
0\leq i\leq\ell\leq k
and
C\in{\mathcal{Q}}_{\ell}
, let

f_{i}(C)
be the unique element of
{\mathcal{Q}}_{i}
containing
C
.

Step 1: construction of the coupling.
For all
F\in{\mathcal{Q}}_{k}
, we set

(13)

\xi_{F}({\rm d}x,{\rm d}y)=\frac{\mu|_{F}({\rm d}x)}{\mu(F)}\frac{\nu|_{F}({\rm d}y)}{\nu(F)}.

Then by reverse induction, for
\ell\in\{0,\dots,k-1\}
and
F\in{\mathcal{Q}}_{\ell}
, we build

(14)

\displaystyle\xi_{F}({\rm d}x,{\rm d}y)=\sum_{C\!\hbox{\tiny{ child of }}\!F}\rho_{C}\xi_{C}({\rm d}x,{\rm d}y)+q_{F}\alpha_{F}({\rm d}x)\beta_{F}({\rm d}y),

where

\rho_{C}=\frac{\mu(C)}{\mu(F)}\land\frac{\nu(C)}{\nu(F)},

which depends only on
C\in{\mathcal{Q}}_{\ell+1}
since
F=f_{\ell}(C)
, where

(15)

q_{F}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{C\!\hbox{\tiny{ child of }}\!F}\Big|\frac{\mu(C)}{\mu(F)}-\frac{\nu(C)}{\nu(F)}\Big|,

and where

\displaystyle\alpha_{F}({\rm d}x)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q_{F}}\sum_{C\!\hbox{\tiny{ child of }}\!F}\Big(\frac{\mu(C)}{\mu(F)}-\frac{\nu(C)}{\nu(F)}\Big)_{+}\frac{\mu|_{C}({\rm d}x)}{\mu(C)},

\displaystyle\beta_{F}({\rm d}x)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q_{F}}\sum_{C\!\hbox{\tiny{ child of }}\!F}\Big(\frac{\nu(C)}{\nu(F)}-\frac{\mu(C)}{\mu(F)}\Big)_{+}\frac{\nu|_{C}({\rm d}x)}{\nu(C)}.

It holds that
\alpha_{F}
and
\beta_{F}
are two probability measures on
F
, because

(16)

q_{F}=\sum_{C\!\hbox{\tiny{ child of }}\!F}\Big(\frac{\mu(C)}{\mu(F)}-\frac{\nu(C)}{\nu(F)}\Big)_{+}=\sum_{C\!\hbox{\tiny{ child of }}\!F}\Big(\frac{\nu(C)}{\nu(F)}-\frac{\mu(C)}{\mu(F)}\Big)_{+}.

Step 2.
Here we show that
\xi_{G_{0}}\in{\mathcal{H}}(\mu,\nu)
,
so that
{\mathcal{T}}_{p}(\mu,\nu)\leq\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\times{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}}|x-y|^{p}\xi_{G_{0}}({\rm d}x,{\rm d}y)
.
We recall that
G_{0}=B(0,1)
is the unique element of
{\mathcal{Q}}_{0}
.

We actually prove by
reverse induction that for all
\ell\in\{0,\dots,k\}
, all
F\in{\mathcal{Q}}_{\ell}
, it holds that

\xi_{F}\in{\mathcal{H}}(\frac{\mu|_{F}}{\mu(F)},\frac{\nu|_{F}}{\nu(F)})
.
The result will then follow by choosing
\ell=0
and
F=G_{0}
.

This is obvious if
\ell=k
, see (
13
).
Next, we assume that this holds true for some
\ell+1\in\{1,\dots,k\}
,
and we consider
F\in{\mathcal{Q}}_{\ell}
.
For
A\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, we use (
14
) to write

\xi_{F}(A\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})=\sum_{C\!\hbox{\tiny{ child of }}\!F}\rho_{C}\xi_{C}(A\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})+q_{F}\alpha_{F}(A)=\sum_{C\!\hbox{\tiny{ child of }}\!F}\rho_{C}\frac{\mu(A\cap C)}{\mu(C)}+q_{F}\alpha_{F}(A)

by induction assumption. Thus

\xi_{F}(A\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})=\hskip-5.0pt\sum_{C\!\hbox{\tiny{ child of }}\!F}\Big[\frac{\mu(C)}{\mu(F)}\land\frac{\nu(C)}{\nu(F)}+\Big(\frac{\mu(C)}{\mu(F)}-\frac{\nu(C)}{\nu(F)}\Big)_{+}\Big]\frac{\mu(A\cap C)}{\mu(C)}=\hskip-5.0pt\sum_{C\!\hbox{\tiny{ child of }}\!F}\frac{\mu(C)}{\mu(F)}\frac{\mu(A\cap C)}{\mu(C)},

whence
\xi_{F}(A\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})=\mu(A\cap F)/\mu(F)
. One shows
similarly that
\xi_{F}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times A)=\nu(A\cap F)/\nu(F)
.

Step 3.
For
i\in\{0,\dots,k\}
and
F\in{\mathcal{Q}}_{i}
, we put
m_{F}=\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\times{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}}|x-y|^{p}\xi_{F}({\rm d}x,{\rm d}y)
.
In this step, we show by induction that for all
i\in\{0,\dots,k-1\}
,

(17)

m_{G_{0}}\leq\delta_{0}^{p}q_{G_{0}}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{i}\delta_{\ell}^{p}\sum_{C_{\ell}\in{\mathcal{Q}}_{\ell}}\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}\rho_{f_{j}(C_{\ell})}\Big)q_{C_{\ell}}+\sum_{C_{i+1}\in{\mathcal{Q}}_{i+1}}\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{i+1}\rho_{f_{j}(C_{i+1})}\Big)m_{C_{i+1}}.

Recalling (
14
), since the set of all the children of
G_{0}
is
{\mathcal{Q}}_{1}
, and since

|x-y|\leq\delta_{0}
for all
x,y\in G_{0}
, so that
\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\times{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}}|x-y|^{p}\alpha_{G_{0}}({\rm d}x)\beta_{G_{0}}({\rm d}y)\leq\delta_{0}^{p}
, we see that

m_{G_{0}}\leq\delta_{0}^{p}q_{G_{0}}+\sum_{C_{1}\in{\mathcal{Q}}_{1}}\rho_{C_{1}}m_{C_{1}}=\delta_{0}^{p}q_{G_{0}}+\sum_{C_{1}\in{\mathcal{Q}}_{1}}\rho_{f_{1}(C_{1})}m_{C_{1}},

which is (
17
) with
i=0
. Assume now that (
17
) holds true for some

i\in\{0,\dots,k-2\}
. For all
C_{i+1}\in{\mathcal{Q}}_{i+1}
, we use (
14
) and
that
\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\times{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}}|x-y|^{p}\alpha_{C_{i+1}}({\rm d}x)\beta_{C_{i+1}}({\rm d}y)\leq\delta_{i+1}^{p}

to write

m_{C_{i+1}}\leq\delta_{i+1}^{p}q_{C_{i+1}}+\sum_{C_{i+2}\!\hbox{\tiny{ child of }}\!C_{i+1}}\rho_{C_{i+2}}m_{C_{i+2}}.

Hence, since
f_{j}(C_{i+2})=f_{j}(C_{i+1})

for all
j=1,\dots,i+1
if
C_{i+2}
is a child of
C_{i+1}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{C_{i+1}\in{\mathcal{Q}}_{i+1}}\hskip-5.0pt\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{i+1}\rho_{f_{j}(C_{i+1})}\Big)m_{C_{i+1}}\leq

\displaystyle\delta_{i+1}^{p}\hskip-5.0pt\sum_{C_{i+1}\in{\mathcal{Q}}_{i+1}}\hskip-5.0pt\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{i+1}\rho_{f_{j}(C_{i+1})}\Big)q_{C_{i+1}}+\hskip-5.0pt\sum_{C_{i+2}\in{\mathcal{Q}}_{i+2}}\hskip-5.0pt\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{i+2}\rho_{f_{j}(C_{i+2})}\Big)m_{C_{i+2}}.

This last formula, inserted in (
17
), gives (
17
) with
i+1
instead of
i
.

Step 4.
For all
C_{k}\in{\mathcal{Q}}_{k}
, we have
m_{C_{k}}\leq\delta_{k}^{p}
by (

) and since

implies
that
|x-y|\leq\delta_{k}
. Hence, by definition of
\rho_{F}
,

\sum_{C_{k}\in{\mathcal{Q}}_{k}}\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{k}\rho_{f_{j}(C_{k})}\Big)m_{C_{k}}\leq\delta_{k}^{p}\sum_{C_{k}\in{\mathcal{Q}}_{k}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{\mu(f_{j}(C_{k}))}{\mu(f_{j-1}(C_{k}))}=\delta_{k}^{p}\sum_{C_{k}\in{\mathcal{Q}}_{k}}\mu(C_{k})=\delta_{k}^{p}.

This, inserted in (

A general estimate concerning the empirical measure

Lemma 9

x=r^{-\alpha t_{x}}
,

v=x(\ell_{x}+1-t_{x})+\frac{r^{-\alpha(\ell_{x}+1)}-x}{1-r^{-\alpha}}=x\Big[1-s_{x}+\frac{r^{-\alpha(1-s_{x})}-1}{1-r^{-\alpha}}\Big].

To show that
v\leq 0
, which will complete the proof of (
33
),
it suffices to prove that
g(u)=u+\frac{r^{-\alpha u}-1}{1-r^{-\alpha}}

is nonpositive for all
u\in[0,1]
. But

g^{\prime\prime}(u)=\frac{(\alpha\log r)^{2}}{1-r^{-\alpha}}r^{-\alpha u}\geq 0
, so that

g
is convex, and
g(0)=g(1)=0
. The conclusion follows.

We fix
\beta>\alpha>0
and prove (
34
).
If
x>1
, we write

\Psi_{r,\alpha,\beta}(x)\leq\sum_{\ell\geq 0}r^{-\alpha\ell}=\frac{1}{1-r^{-\alpha}}\leq\frac{x^{\alpha/\beta}}{1-r^{-\alpha}}.

If
x\in[0,1]
, we set
t_{x}=\log(1/x)/(\beta\log r)\geq 0
,
\ell_{x}=\lfloor t_{x}\rfloor\in{\mathbb{N}}
and

s_{x}=t_{x}-\ell_{x}\in[0,1)
and write

\Psi_{r,\alpha,\beta}(x)\leq\sum_{\ell=0}^{\ell_{x}}xr^{(\beta-\alpha)\ell}+\sum_{\ell=\ell_{x}+1}^{\infty}r^{-\alpha\ell}\leq x\frac{r^{(\beta-\alpha)(\ell_{x}+1)}}{r^{\beta-\alpha}-1}+\frac{r^{-\alpha(\ell_{x}+1)}}{1-r^{-\alpha}}=u+v,

where
u=x^{\alpha/\beta}/(r^{\beta-\alpha}-1)+x^{\alpha/\beta}/(1-r^{-\alpha})
is the desired bound and where,
since
x=r^{-\beta t_{x}}
,

v=\frac{xr^{(\beta-\alpha)(\ell_{x}+1)}-x^{\alpha/\beta}}{r^{\beta-\alpha}-1}+\frac{r^{-\alpha(\ell_{x}+1)}-x^{\alpha/\beta}}{1-r^{-\alpha}}=x^{\alpha/\beta}\Big[\frac{r^{(\beta-\alpha)(1-s_{x})}-1}{r^{\beta-\alpha}-1}+\frac{r^{-\alpha(1-s_{x})}-1}{1-r^{-\alpha}}\Big].

To show that
v\leq 0
, which will complete the proof of (
34
),
it suffices to show that
g(u)=\frac{r^{(\beta-\alpha)u}-1}{r^{\beta-\alpha}-1}+\frac{r^{-\alpha u}-1}{1-r^{-\alpha}}

is nonpositive for all
u\in[0,1]
. But
g^{\prime\prime}(u)=\frac{((\beta-\alpha)\log r)^{2}}{r^{\beta-\alpha}-1}r^{(\beta-\alpha)u}+\frac{(\alpha\log r)^{2}}{1-r^{-\alpha}}r^{-\alpha u}\geq 0
, so that

g
is convex, and it holds that
g(0)=g(1)=0
.
∎

6.
Theoretical result for a general norm

Recall that
{\varepsilon}_{p}=2^{-1}\lor 2^{-p}
and that
H
was defined in (
5
).
Here we prove the following general result,
to be applied to some specific norms later.

Proposition 10
.

We adopt Setting
7
, we fix
\mu\in{\mathcal{P}}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})
and consider the associated empirical
measure
\mu_{N}
, see (
1
). We fix
q>p>0
and assume that
{\mathcal{M}}_{q}(\mu)<\infty
.

(i) If
p>d/2
and
q>2p
, then for all
N\geq 1
,

\mathbb{E}[{\mathcal{T}}_{p}(\mu_{N},\mu)]\leq 2^{p}\frac{\kappa_{d,p}}{\sqrt{N}}[{\mathcal{M}}_{q}(\mu)]^{p/q}H\Big(\frac{{\varepsilon}_{p}}{\kappa_{d,p}},2p,q\Big),

where

\kappa_{d,p}=\frac{D^{p}\sqrt{Ar^{d}}}{2^{p+1}(1-r^{d/2-p})}.

(ii) If
p=d/2
and
q>2p
, then for all
N\geq 1
,

\mathbb{E}[{\mathcal{T}}_{p}(\mu_{N},\mu)]\leq 2^{p}\frac{\kappa_{d,p,N}}{\sqrt{N}}[{\mathcal{M}}_{q}(\mu)]^{p/q}H\Big(\frac{{\varepsilon}_{p}}{\kappa_{d,p,N}},2p,q\Big),

where

\kappa_{d,p,N}=\frac{D^{p}\sqrt{A}r^{p}}{2^{p+1}p\log r}\log_{+}\Big(2(r^{-p}-r^{-2p})\sqrt{\frac{N}{A}}\Big)+\frac{D^{p}\sqrt{A}r^{2p}}{2^{p+1}(r^{p}-1)}.

(iii) If
p\in(0,d/2)
and
q>dp/(d-p)
, then for all
N\geq 1
,

\mathbb{E}[{\mathcal{T}}_{p}(\mu_{N},\mu)]\leq 2^{p}\frac{\kappa_{d,p}}{N^{p/d}}[{\mathcal{M}}_{q}(\mu)]^{p/q}H\Big(\frac{2^{1-2p/d}{\varepsilon}_{p}}{\kappa_{d,p}},\frac{dp}{d-p},q\Big),

where

\kappa_{d,p}=\frac{D^{p}A^{p/d}r^{p}(r^{d/2}-1)^{1-2p/d}}{2^{p+2p/d}(r^{d/2-p}-1)}.

Proof.

We fix
q>p>0
. We have
(G_{0}^{a})^{c}\subset\{x\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}:|x|\geq 1\}
and

G_{n}^{a}\subset\{x\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}:|x|\geq a^{n-1}\}
for each
n\geq 1
, whence

(35)

1-\mu(G_{0}^{a})\leq{\mathcal{M}}_{q}(\mu)\qquad\hbox{and}\qquad\mu(G_{n}^{a})\leq{\mathcal{M}}_{q}(\mu)a^{-q(n-1)}\quad\hbox{if $n\geq 1$}.

We know from Proposition
8
that

\mathbb{E}[{\mathcal{T}}_{p}(\mu_{N},\mu)]\leq K_{N}+\min\{L_{N},M_{N}\}
.

Case (i):
p>d/2
and
q>2p
.
First, by (
35
), we have

K_{N}\leq 2^{p}{\varepsilon}_{p}\sqrt{\frac{{\mathcal{M}}_{q}(\mu)}{N}}+2^{p}{\varepsilon}_{p}\sum_{n\geq 1}a^{pn}\sqrt{\frac{{\mathcal{M}}_{q}(\mu)}{Na^{q(n-1)}}}=2^{p}{\varepsilon}_{p}\sqrt{\frac{{\mathcal{M}}_{q}(\mu)}{N}}\Big[1+\frac{a^{p}}{1-a^{p-q/2}}\Big].

Next,

\displaystyle L_{N}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{D^{p}\sqrt{Ar^{d}}}{2\sqrt{N}}\sum_{\ell\geq 0}r^{(d/2-p)\ell}\Big(1+\sum_{n\geq 1}a^{pn}\sqrt{\frac{{\mathcal{M}}_{q}(\mu)}{a^{q(n-1)}}}\Big)=2^{p}\frac{\kappa_{d,p}}{\sqrt{N}}\Big(1+\sqrt{{\mathcal{M}}_{q}(\mu)}\frac{a^{p}}{1-a^{p-q/2}}\Big)

recall that
\kappa_{d,p}=(D/2)^{p}\sqrt{Ar^{d}}/[2(1-r^{d/2-p})]
.
All in all, we have proved that

(36)

\mathbb{E}[{\mathcal{T}}_{p}(\mu_{N},\mu)]\leq\frac{2^{p}}{\sqrt{N}}\Big(\kappa_{d,p}+\sqrt{{\mathcal{M}}_{q}(\mu)}\Big[{\varepsilon}_{p}+({\varepsilon}_{p}+\kappa_{d,p})\frac{a^{p}}{1-a^{p-q/2}}\Big]\Big).

This holds true for any value of
a>1
and we optimally choose

a=[q/(2p)]^{2/(q-2p)}
and set

v_{p,q}=\frac{a^{p}}{1-a^{p-q/2}}=\frac{q}{q-2p}\Big(\frac{q}{2p}\Big)^{2p/(q-2p)}.

We thus have

\mathbb{E}[{\mathcal{T}}_{p}(\mu_{N},\mu)]\leq\frac{2^{p}}{\sqrt{N}}\Big(\kappa_{d,p}+\sqrt{{\mathcal{M}}_{q}(\mu)}[{\varepsilon}_{p}+({\varepsilon}_{p}+\kappa_{d,p})v_{p,q}]\Big)=\frac{2^{p}\kappa_{d,p}}{\sqrt{N}}\Big(1+\sqrt{{\mathcal{M}}_{q}(\mu)}\rho_{d,p,q}\Big),

where
\rho_{d,p,q}={\varepsilon}_{p}/\kappa_{d,p}+({\varepsilon}_{p}+\kappa_{d,p})v_{p,q}/\kappa_{d,p}
.

For any
\alpha>0
, we may apply this formula to
\mu^{\alpha}
, the image measure of
\mu

by the map
x\mapsto\alpha x
, which satisfies
\mathbb{E}[{\mathcal{T}}_{p}(\mu_{N}^{\alpha},\mu^{\alpha})]=\alpha^{p}\mathbb{E}[{\mathcal{T}}_{p}(\mu_{N},\mu)]

and
{\mathcal{M}}_{q}(\mu^{\alpha})=\alpha^{q}{\mathcal{M}}_{q}(\mu)
. We thus get

\mathbb{E}[{\mathcal{T}}_{p}(\mu_{N},\mu)]\leq\frac{2^{p}\kappa_{d,p}}{\sqrt{N}}\frac{1}{\alpha^{p}}\Big(1+\sqrt{\alpha^{q}{\mathcal{M}}_{q}(\mu)}\rho_{d,p,q}\Big).

We optimally choose
\alpha=[(q-2p)\rho_{d,p,q}\sqrt{{\mathcal{M}}_{q}(\mu)}/(2p)]^{-2/q}
and find

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[{\mathcal{T}}_{p}(\mu_{N},\mu)]\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2^{p}\kappa_{d,p}}{\sqrt{N}}[{\mathcal{M}}_{q}(\mu)]^{p/q}\Big(\rho_{d,p,q}\frac{q-2p}{2p}\Big)^{2p/q}\frac{q}{q-2p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2^{p}\kappa_{d,p}}{\sqrt{N}}[{\mathcal{M}}_{q}(\mu)]^{p/q}\Big(\frac{{\varepsilon}_{p}}{\kappa_{d,p}}\frac{q-2p}{2p}+\frac{{\varepsilon}_{p}+\kappa_{d,p}}{\kappa_{d,p}}v_{p,q}\frac{q-2p}{2p}\Big)^{2p/q}\frac{q}{q-2p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2^{p}\kappa_{d,p}}{\sqrt{N}}[{\mathcal{M}}_{q}(\mu)]^{p/q}\Big(\frac{{\varepsilon}_{p}}{\kappa_{d,p}}\frac{q-2p}{2p}+\frac{{\varepsilon}_{p}+\kappa_{d,p}}{\kappa_{d,p}}\Big(\frac{q}{2p}\Big)^{q/(q-2p)}\Big)^{2p/q}\frac{q}{q-2p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2^{p}\kappa_{d,p}}{\sqrt{N}}[{\mathcal{M}}_{q}(\mu)]^{p/q}H\Big(\frac{{\varepsilon}_{p}}{\kappa_{d,p}},2p,q\Big).

Case (ii):
p=d/2
and
q>2p
.
Exactly as in Case (i),

K_{N}\leq 2^{p}{\varepsilon}_{p}\sqrt{\frac{{\mathcal{M}}_{q}(\mu)}{N}}\Big[1+\frac{a^{p}}{1-a^{p-q/2}}\Big].

We next write

\displaystyle M_{N}\leq

\displaystyle D^{p}(1-r^{-p})\sum_{n\geq 0}a^{pn}\mu(G^{a}_{n})\sum_{\ell\geq 0}r^{-p\ell}\Big(1\land\Big[\frac{\sqrt{A}r^{d\ell/2+d}}{2(r^{d/2}-1)}\frac{1}{\sqrt{N\mu(G_{n}^{a})}}\Big]\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D^{p}(1-r^{-p})\sum_{n\geq 0}a^{pn}\mu(G^{a}_{n})\Psi_{r,p,d/2}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{A}r^{d}}{2(r^{d/2}-1)\sqrt{N\mu(G_{n}^{a})}}\Big).

By (
33
) with
\alpha=\beta=p=d/2
, we can
bound
M_{N}
by

\displaystyle D^{p}(1-r^{-p})\sum_{n\geq 0}a^{pn}\mu(G^{a}_{n})\Big[\frac{\log_{+}(2(r^{-d/2}-r^{-d})\sqrt{N\mu(G_{n}^{a})/A})}{p\log r}+\frac{1}{1-r^{-p}}\Big]\frac{\sqrt{A}r^{d}}{2(r^{d/2}-1)\sqrt{N\mu(G_{n}^{a})}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{D^{p}\sqrt{A}r^{2p}}{2(r^{p}-1)\sqrt{N}}\Big[\frac{(1-r^{-p})\log_{+}(2(r^{-p}-r^{-2p})\sqrt{N/A})}{p\log r}+1\Big]\sum_{n\geq 0}a^{pn}\sqrt{\mu(G^{a}_{n})}

since
\mu(G_{n}^{a})\leq 1
. Observing that, by (
35
),

\sum_{n\geq 0}a^{pn}\sqrt{\mu(G^{a}_{n})}\leq 1+\sum_{n\geq 1}a^{pn}\sqrt{\frac{{\mathcal{M}}_{q}(\mu)}{a^{q(n-1)}}}=1+\sqrt{{\mathcal{M}}_{q}(\mu)}\frac{a^{p}}{1-a^{p-q/2}},

and recalling that

\kappa_{d,p,N}=\frac{(D/2)^{p}\sqrt{A}r^{p}\log_{+}(2(r^{-p}-r^{-2p})\sqrt{N/A})}{2p\log r}+\frac{(D/2)^{p}\sqrt{A}r^{2p}}{2(r^{p}-1)},

we conclude that

M_{N}\leq 2^{p}\frac{\kappa_{d,p,N}}{\sqrt{N}}\Big(1+\sqrt{{\mathcal{M}}_{q}(\mu)}\frac{a^{p}}{1-a^{p-q/2}}\Big).

All in all, we have proved that

\mathbb{E}[{\mathcal{T}}_{p}(\mu_{N},\mu)]\leq\frac{2^{p}}{\sqrt{N}}\Big(\kappa_{d,p,N}+\sqrt{{\mathcal{M}}_{q}(\mu)}\Big[{\varepsilon}_{p}+({\varepsilon}_{p}+\kappa_{d,p,N})\frac{a^{p}}{1-a^{p-q/2}}\Big]\Big).

From there we conclude exactly as in Case (i) (compare the above formula to (
36
)) that

\mathbb{E}[{\mathcal{T}}_{p}(\mu_{N},\mu)]\leq 2^{p}\frac{\kappa_{d,p,N}}{\sqrt{N}}[{\mathcal{M}}_{q}(\mu)]^{p/q}H\Big(\frac{{\varepsilon}_{p}}{\kappa_{d,p,N}},2p,q\Big).

Case (iii):
p\in(0,d/2)
and
q>dp/(d-p)
.
We write, using that
2p/d\in(0,1)
and then (
35
),

\displaystyle K_{N}\leq

\displaystyle 2^{p}{\varepsilon}_{p}\Big[2(1-\mu(G_{0}^{a}))\Big]^{1-2p/d}\Big[\sqrt{\frac{1-\mu(G_{0}^{a})}{N}}\Big]^{2p/d}+2^{p}{\varepsilon}_{p}\sum_{n\geq 1}a^{pn}\Big[2\mu(G_{n}^{a})\Big]^{1-2p/d}\Big[\sqrt{\frac{\mu(G_{n}^{a})}{N}}\Big]^{2p/d}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{p}{\varepsilon}_{p}\Big[2{\mathcal{M}}_{q}(\mu)\Big]^{1-2p/d}\Big[\sqrt{\frac{{\mathcal{M}}_{q}(\mu)}{N}}\Big]^{2p/d}+2^{p}{\varepsilon}_{p}\sum_{n\geq 1}a^{pn}\Big[\frac{2{\mathcal{M}}_{q}(\mu)}{a^{q(n-1)}}\Big]^{1-2p/d}\Big[\sqrt{\frac{{\mathcal{M}}_{q}(\mu)}{Na^{q(n-1)}}}\Big]^{2p/d}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{p}{\varepsilon}_{p}\frac{2^{1-2p/d}[{\mathcal{M}}_{q}(\mu)]^{1-p/d}}{N^{p/d}}+2^{p}{\varepsilon}_{p}\frac{2^{1-2p/d}[{\mathcal{M}}_{q}(\mu)]^{1-p/d}}{N^{p/d}}\sum_{n\geq 1}a^{pn-q(1-p/d)(n-1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{p}{\varepsilon}_{p}\frac{2^{1-2p/d}[{\mathcal{M}}_{q}(\mu)]^{1-p/d}}{N^{p/d}}\Big(1+\frac{a^{p}}{1-a^{p-q+pq/d}}\Big).

We used that
p-q+pq/d<0
because
q>dp/(d-p)
. Next,

\displaystyle M_{N}\leq D^{p}(1-r^{-p})\sum_{n\geq 0}a^{pn}\mu(G^{a}_{n})\Psi_{r,p,d/2}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{A}r^{d}}{2(r^{d/2}-1)\sqrt{N\mu(G_{n}^{a})}}\Big)

as in Case (ii). Thus, using (
34
) with
\alpha=p
and
\beta=d/2
,

\displaystyle M_{N}\leq

\displaystyle D^{p}(1-r^{-p})\Big[\frac{1}{r^{d/2-p}-1}+\frac{1}{1-r^{-p}}\Big]\sum_{n\geq 0}a^{pn}\mu(G^{a}_{n})\Big(\frac{\sqrt{A}r^{d}}{2(r^{d/2}-1)\sqrt{N\mu(G_{n}^{a})}}\Big)^{2p/d}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{p}\frac{\kappa_{d,p}}{N^{p/d}}\sum_{n\geq 0}a^{pn}[\mu(G^{a}_{n})]^{1-p/d},

because

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{D}{2}\Big)^{p}(1-r^{-p})\Big[\frac{1}{r^{d/2-p}-1}+\frac{1}{1-r^{-p}}\Big]\Big(\frac{\sqrt{A}r^{d}}{2(r^{d/2}-1)}\Big)^{2p/d}=\frac{D^{p}A^{p/d}r^{p}(r^{d/2}-1)^{1-2p/d}}{2^{p+2p/d}(r^{d/2-p}-1)}=\kappa_{d,p}.

But, using (
35
),

\sum_{n\geq 0}a^{pn}[\mu(G^{a}_{n})]^{1-p/d}\leq 1+\sum_{n\geq 1}a^{pn}\Big[\frac{{\mathcal{M}}_{q}(\mu)}{a^{q(n-1)}}\Big]^{1-p/d}=1+[{\mathcal{M}}_{q}(\mu)]^{1-p/d}\frac{a^{p}}{1-a^{p-q+pq/d}},

whence

M_{N}\leq 2^{p}\frac{\kappa_{d,p}}{N^{p/d}}\Big(1+[{\mathcal{M}}_{q}(\mu)]^{1-p/d}\frac{a^{p}}{1-a^{p-q+pq/d}}\Big).

All in all, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[{\mathcal{T}}_{p}(\mu_{N},\mu)]\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2^{p}}{N^{p/d}}\Big(\kappa_{d,p}+[{\mathcal{M}}_{q}(\mu)]^{1-p/d}\Big[2^{1-2p/d}{\varepsilon}_{p}+(2^{1-2p/d}{\varepsilon}_{p}+\kappa_{d,p})\frac{a^{p}}{1-a^{p-q+pq/d}}\Big]\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2^{p}}{N^{p/d}}\Big(\kappa_{d,p}+[{\mathcal{M}}_{q}(\mu)]^{p/\tau}\Big[2^{1-2p/d}{\varepsilon}_{p}+(2^{1-2p/d}{\varepsilon}_{p}+\kappa_{d,p})\frac{a^{p}}{1-a^{p-pq/\tau}}\Big]\Big),

where we have set
\tau=dp/(d-p)
.
We choose
a=[q/\tau]^{\tau/(p(q-\tau))}>1
, for which

v_{d,p,q}=\frac{a^{p}}{1-a^{p-pq/\tau}}=\frac{q}{q-\tau}\Big(\frac{q}{\tau}\Big)^{\tau/(q-\tau)}.

Thus

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[{\mathcal{T}}_{p}(\mu_{N},\mu)]\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2^{p}\kappa_{d,p}}{N^{p/d}}\Big(1+[{\mathcal{M}}_{q}(\mu)]^{p/\tau}\Big[\frac{2^{1-2p/d}{\varepsilon}_{p}}{\kappa_{d,p}}+(2^{1-2p/d}{\varepsilon}_{p}+\kappa_{d,p})\frac{v_{d,p,q}}{\kappa_{d,p}}\Big]\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2^{p}\kappa_{d,p}}{N^{p/d}}\Big(1+[{\mathcal{M}}_{q}(\mu)]^{p/\tau}\rho_{d,p,q}\Big),

where
\rho_{d,p,q}\!=\!2^{1-2p/d}{\varepsilon}_{p}/\kappa_{d,p}+(2^{1-2p/d}{\varepsilon}_{p}+\kappa_{d,p})v_{d,p,q}/\kappa_{d,p}
.
As in Case (i), we deduce that

\mathbb{E}[{\mathcal{T}}_{p}(\mu_{N},\mu)]\leq\frac{2^{p}\kappa_{d,p}}{N^{p/d}}\frac{1}{\alpha^{p}}\Big(1+[\alpha^{q}{\mathcal{M}}_{q}(\mu)]^{p/\tau}\rho_{d,p,q}\Big)

for all
\alpha>0
.
With
\alpha=[\rho_{d,p,q}{\mathcal{M}}_{q}^{p/\tau}(q-\tau)/\tau]^{-\tau/(pq)}
, which is optimal,
we find

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[{\mathcal{T}}_{p}(\mu_{N},\mu)]\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2^{p}\kappa_{d,p}}{N^{p/d}}[{\mathcal{M}}_{q}(\mu)]^{p/q}\Big(\rho_{d,p,q}\frac{q-\tau}{\tau}\Big)^{\tau/q}\frac{q}{q-\tau}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2^{p}\kappa_{d,p}}{N^{p/d}}[{\mathcal{M}}_{q}(\mu)]^{p/q}\Big(\frac{2^{1-2p/d}{\varepsilon}_{p}}{\kappa_{d,p}}\frac{q-\tau}{\tau}+\frac{2^{1-2p/d}{\varepsilon}_{p}+\kappa_{d,p}}{\kappa_{d,p}}v_{d,p,q}\frac{q-\tau}{\tau}\Big)^{\tau/q}\frac{q}{q-\tau}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2^{p}\kappa_{d,p}}{N^{p/d}}[{\mathcal{M}}_{q}(\mu)]^{p/q}\Big(\frac{2^{1-2p/d}{\varepsilon}_{p}}{\kappa_{d,p}}\frac{q-\tau}{\tau}+\frac{2^{1-2p/d}{\varepsilon}_{p}+\kappa_{d,p}}{\kappa_{d,p}}\Big(\frac{q}{\tau}\Big)^{q/(q-\tau)}\Big)^{\tau/q}\frac{q}{q-\tau}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2^{p}\kappa_{d,p}}{N^{p/d}}[{\mathcal{M}}_{q}(\mu)]^{p/q}H\Big(\frac{2^{1-2p/d}{\varepsilon}_{p}}{\kappa_{d,p}},\tau,q\Big)

as desired.
∎

Conclusion for the other norms

If next

\kappa_{d,p,N}^{(m)}=\min\{\kappa_{d,p,N,r}^{(m)}:r\geq 2\}
, where

\displaystyle\kappa_{d,p,N,r}^{(m)}=

\displaystyle\frac{D^{p}\sqrt{A}r^{p}}{2^{p+1}p\log r}\log_{+}\Big(2(r^{-p}-r^{-2p})\sqrt{\frac{N}{A}}\Big)+\frac{D^{p}\sqrt{A}r^{2p}}{2^{p+1}(r^{p}-1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{K_{d}^{(m)}}}{2}\frac{r^{2p}}{(r-1)^{p}p\log r}\log_{+}\Big(2^{p+1}(r^{-p}-r^{-2p})\sqrt{\frac{N}{K_{d}^{(m)}}}\Big)+\frac{\sqrt{K_{d}^{(m)}}}{2}\frac{r^{3p}}{(r-1)^{p}(r^{p}-1)}.

If finally
p\in(0,d/2)
, we have
\kappa_{d,p}^{(m)}=\min\{\kappa_{d,p,r}^{(m)}:r\geq 2\}
, where

\kappa_{d,p,r}^{(m)}=\frac{D^{p}A^{p/d}r^{p}(r^{d/2}-1)^{1-2p/d}}{2^{p+2p/d}(r^{d/2-p}-1)}=\Big(\frac{K_{d}^{(m)}}{4}\Big)^{p/d}\frac{r^{2p}(1-r^{-d/2})^{1-2p/d}}{(r-1)^{p}(1-r^{p-d/2})}.

9.
The case of a low order finite moment

We finally handle the case where
\mu
has a low order moment.
We only treat the case of the maximum norm for simplicity.
We thus may apply Proposition
8
with

A=1
,
D=2
and
r=2
, see the beginning of Section
7
.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

We consider
p>0
,
q\in(p,\min\{2p,dp/(d-p)\})
,
\mu\in{\mathcal{P}}({{\mathbb{R}}^{d}})
and the associated empirical
measure
\mu_{N}
.
We know that
\mathbb{E}[{\mathcal{T}}_{p}^{(\infty)}(\mu_{N},\mu)]\leq K_{N}+\min\{L_{N},M_{N}\}

by Proposition
8
, and we have

(37)

\mu(G_{n}^{a})\leq{\mathcal{M}}_{q}^{(\infty)}(\mu)a^{-q(n-1)}\quad\hbox{if $n\geq 1$}.

as usual. First,

\displaystyle K_{N}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2^{p}{\varepsilon}_{p}}{\sqrt{N}}+2^{p}{\varepsilon}_{p}\sum_{n\geq 1}a^{pn}\Big[\frac{2{\mathcal{M}}_{q}^{(\infty)}(\mu)}{a^{q(n-1)}}\land\sqrt{\frac{{\mathcal{M}}_{q}^{(\infty)}(\mu)}{Na^{q(n-1)}}}\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2^{p}{\varepsilon}_{p}}{\sqrt{N}}+2^{p}{\varepsilon}_{p}a^{p}\sum_{n\geq 0}a^{(p-q)n}\Big[\Big(2{\mathcal{M}}_{q}^{(\infty)}(\mu)\Big)\land\Big(\sqrt{\frac{{\mathcal{M}}_{q}^{(\infty)}(\mu)}{N}}a^{qn/2}\Big)\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2^{p}{\varepsilon}_{p}}{\sqrt{N}}+2^{p+1}{\varepsilon}_{p}{\mathcal{M}}_{q}^{(\infty)}(\mu)a^{p}\Psi_{a,q-p,q/2}\Big(\frac{1}{2\sqrt{N{\mathcal{M}}_{q}^{(\infty)}(\mu)}}\Big).

Since
q/2>q-p
because
q<2p
, we may apply (
34
) with
r=a
, with
\alpha=q-p
and
\beta=q/2
:

\displaystyle K_{N}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2^{p}{\varepsilon}_{p}}{\sqrt{N}}+2^{p+1}{\varepsilon}_{p}{\mathcal{M}}_{q}^{(\infty)}(\mu)a^{p}\Big[\frac{1}{a^{p-q/2}-1}+\frac{1}{1-a^{p-q}}\Big]\Big(\frac{1}{2\sqrt{N{\mathcal{M}}_{q}^{(\infty)}(\mu)}}\Big)^{2(q-p)/q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2^{p}{\varepsilon}_{p}}{\sqrt{N}}+2^{p}{\varepsilon}_{p}\rho_{a}[{\mathcal{M}}_{q}^{(\infty)}(\mu)]^{p/q}\frac{2^{2p/q-1}}{N^{(q-p)/q}},

where
\rho_{a}=a^{p}[1/(a^{p-q/2}-1)+1/(1-a^{p-q})]
. Next, recalling that
A=1
,
D=2
and
r=2
,

\displaystyle M_{N}\leq

\displaystyle 2^{p}(1-2^{-p})\sum_{n\geq 0}a^{pn}\sum_{\ell\geq 0}2^{-p\ell}\Big[\mu(G_{n}^{a})\land\Big(\frac{2^{d-1}2^{d\ell/2}}{2^{d/2}-1}\sqrt{\frac{\mu(G_{n}^{a})}{N}}\Big)\Big]\leq M_{N,1}+M_{N,2},

where we separate the cases
n=0
and
n\geq 1
, i.e.

M_{N,1}=2^{p}(1-2^{-p})\sum_{\ell\geq 0}2^{-p\ell}\Big[1\land\Big(\frac{2^{d-1}2^{d\ell/2}}{2^{d/2}-1}\sqrt{\frac{1}{N}}\Big)\Big]\leq 2^{p}(1-2^{-p})\sum_{\ell\geq 0}2^{-p\ell}=2^{p},

and

\displaystyle M_{N,2}=

\displaystyle 2^{p}(1-2^{-p})\sum_{n\geq 1}a^{pn}\sum_{\ell\geq 0}2^{-p\ell}\Big[\frac{{\mathcal{M}}_{q}^{(\infty)}(\mu)}{a^{q(n-1)}}\land\Big(\frac{2^{d-1}2^{d\ell/2}}{2^{d/2}-1}\sqrt{\frac{{\mathcal{M}}_{q}^{(\infty)}(\mu)}{Na^{q(n-1)}}}\Big)\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{p}(1-2^{-p})a^{p}\sum_{\ell\geq 0}2^{-p\ell}\sum_{n\geq 0}a^{pn}\Big[\frac{{\mathcal{M}}_{q}^{(\infty)}(\mu)}{a^{qn}}\land\Big(\frac{2^{d-1}2^{d\ell/2}}{2^{d/2}-1}\sqrt{\frac{{\mathcal{M}}_{q}^{(\infty)}(\mu)}{Na^{qn}}}\Big)\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{p}(1-2^{-p})a^{p}\sum_{\ell\geq 0}2^{-p\ell}\sum_{n\geq 0}a^{(p-q)n}\Big[{\mathcal{M}}_{q}^{(\infty)}(\mu)\land\Big(\frac{2^{d-1}2^{d\ell/2}}{2^{d/2}-1}\sqrt{\frac{{\mathcal{M}}_{q}^{(\infty)}(\mu)}{N}}a^{qn/2}\Big)\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{p}(1-2^{-p})a^{p}{\mathcal{M}}_{q}^{(\infty)}(\mu)\sum_{\ell\geq 0}2^{-p\ell}\Psi_{a,q-p,q/2}\Big(\frac{2^{d-1}2^{d\ell/2}}{(2^{d/2}-1)\sqrt{N{\mathcal{M}}_{q}^{(\infty)}(\mu)}}\Big).

By (
34
) with
r=a
,
\alpha=q-p
and
\beta=q/2
, recalling that

\rho_{a}=a^{p}[1/(a^{p-q/2}-1)+1/(1-a^{p-q})]
,

\displaystyle M_{N,2}\leq

\displaystyle 2^{p}(1-2^{-p}){\mathcal{M}}_{q}^{(\infty)}(\mu)\rho_{a}\sum_{\ell\geq 0}2^{-p\ell}\Big(\frac{2^{d-1}2^{d\ell/2}}{(2^{d/2}-1)\sqrt{N{\mathcal{M}}_{q}^{(\infty)}(\mu)}}\Big)^{2(q-p)/q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{p}\rho_{a}\frac{[{\mathcal{M}}_{q}^{(\infty)}(\mu)]^{p/q}}{N^{(q-p)/q}}\Big(\frac{2^{d-1}}{2^{d/2}-1}\Big)^{2(q-p)/q}\frac{1-2^{-p}}{1-2^{d-p-dp/q}},

observe that
d-p-dp/q<0
because
q<dp/(d-p)
. All in all, we conclude that

\mathbb{E}[{\mathcal{T}}_{p}^{(\infty)}(\mu_{N},\mu)]\leq 2^{p}\Big(\frac{{\varepsilon}_{p}}{{\sqrt{N}}}+1+\rho_{a}\frac{[{\mathcal{M}}_{q}^{(\infty)}(\mu)]^{p/q}}{N^{(q-p)/q}}\Big[{\varepsilon}_{p}2^{2p/q-1}+\Big(\frac{2^{d-1}}{2^{d/2}-1}\Big)^{2(q-p)/q}\frac{1-2^{-p}}{1-2^{d-p-dp/q}}\Big]\Big).

For any
\alpha>0
, we may apply this above formula to
\mu^{\alpha}
, the image measure of
\mu

by the map
x\mapsto\alpha x
, for which

\mathbb{E}[{\mathcal{T}}_{p}^{(\infty)}(\mu_{N}^{\alpha},\mu^{\alpha})]=\alpha^{p}\mathbb{E}[{\mathcal{T}}_{p}^{(\infty)}(\mu_{N},\mu)]

and
{\mathcal{M}}_{q}^{(\infty)}(\mu^{\alpha})=\alpha^{q}{\mathcal{M}}_{q}^{(\infty)}(\mu)
. We get

\mathbb{E}[{\mathcal{T}}_{p}^{(\infty)}(\mu_{N},\mu)]\!\leq\frac{2^{p}}{\alpha^{p}}\Big(\frac{{\varepsilon}_{p}}{{\sqrt{N}}}+1+\rho_{a}\frac{[\alpha^{q}{\mathcal{M}}_{q}^{(\infty)}(\mu)]^{p/q}}{N^{(q-p)/q}}\Big[{\varepsilon}_{p}2^{2p/q-1}\!+\Big(\frac{2^{d-1}}{2^{d/2}-1}\Big)^{2(q-p)/q}\hskip-4.0pt\frac{1-2^{-p}}{1-2^{d-p-dp/q}}\Big]\Big).

Letting
\alpha\to\infty
, we find

\mathbb{E}[{\mathcal{T}}_{p}^{(\infty)}(\mu_{N},\mu)]\leq 2^{p}\frac{[{\mathcal{M}}_{q}^{(\infty)}(\mu)]^{p/q}}{N^{(q-p)/q}}\rho_{a}\Big[{\varepsilon}_{p}2^{2p/q-1}+\Big(\frac{2^{d-1}}{2^{d/2}-1}\Big)^{2(q-p)/q}\frac{1-2^{-p}}{1-2^{d-p-dp/q}}\Big]\Big).

Since
\rho_{a}=a^{p}/(a^{p-q/2}-1)+a^{p}/(1-a^{p-q})

and since this result holds for any
a\in(1,\infty)
, the proof is complete.
∎

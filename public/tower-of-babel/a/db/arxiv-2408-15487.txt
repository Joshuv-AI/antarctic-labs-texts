Introduction

For a graph
G
, let
V(G)
and
E(G)
denote the vertex set and the edge set of
G
respectively. Write
e(G)=|E(G)|
for the number of edges of a graph
G
. For
T\subseteq V(G)
, let
G-T
denote the graph obtained from
G
by deleting all vertices from
T
and all edges incident to
T
.
Given graphs
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
, the union
G=G_{1}\cup G_{2}
is the graph
with
V(G)=V(G_{1})\cup V(G_{2})
and
E(G)=E(G_{1})\cup E(G_{2})
.

We say that a graph
G
is
F
-free if it does not contain
an isomorphic copy of
F
as a subgraph.
For a positive integer
n
and a graph
F
,
the
Turán number

\mathrm{ex}(n,F)
,
is the maximum number of edges
in an
n
-vertex
F
-free graph.
An
F
-free graph on
n
vertices with
\mathrm{ex}(n,F)
edges is called an
extremal graph
for
F
.

Let
K_{r+1}
be the complete graph on
r+1
vertices.
Let
T_{r}(n)
denote the complete
r
-partite graph on
n
vertices where
its part sizes differ by at most
1
.
Turán
[
18
]
obtained that if
G
is a
K_{r+1}
-free graph on
n
vertices,
then
e(G)\leq e(T_{r}(n))
, equality holds if and only if
G=T_{r}(n)
.
There are many extensions and generalizations on Turán’s result.
A celebrated extension of Turán’s theorem attributes to a result of
Erdős, Stone and Simonovits
[
8
,
7
]
. They showed that if
F
is a graph with chromatic number

\chi(F)=r+1
, then
\mathrm{ex}(n,F)=e(T_{r}(n))+o(n^{2})=\left(1-\frac{1}{r}+o(1)\right)\frac{n^{2}}{2}
.
Furthermore, Erdős
[
4
,
5
]
and Simonovits
[
16
]
proved
a stronger structural stability theorem and
discovered that this extremal problem exhibits a certain stability phenomenon. They showed
the following: If
F
is a graph with
\chi(F)=r+1\geq 3
, then for any
\varepsilon>0
,
there exist
\delta>0
and
n_{0}
such that
if
G
is a graph on
n\geq n_{0}
vertices,
and
G
is
F
-free such that

e(G)\geq e(T_{r}(n))-\delta n^{2}
, then
T_{r}(n)
can be obtained from
G
by adding or deleting in total at most
\varepsilon n^{2}
edges.

Stability phenomenon has attracted lots of attentions and has
played an important role in the
development of extremal graph theory. An alternative form of stability is to study the distance of an
F
-free graph from being
r
-partite, where
\chi(F)=r+1
. This question has been well-studied for
K_{r+1}
. Let
f_{r}(n,t)
be the smallest number such that any
K_{r+1}
-free graph
G
with at least
e(T_{r}(n))-t
edges can be made
r
-partite by deleting
f_{r}(n,t)
edges. The Stability Theorem of Erdős and Simonovits implies that
f_{r}(n,t)=o(n^{2})
if
t=o(n^{2})
. When
t
is small, Brouwer
[
2
]
showed that
f_{r}(n,t)=0
for

t\leq n/r+O(1)
. For general
t
, Füredi
[
10
]
proved that
f_{r}(n,t)\leq t
. Later, Roberts and Scott
[
15
]
showed that
f_{r}(n,t)=O(t^{3/2}/n)
when
t=o(n^{2})
, and that this bound is tight up to a constant factor (in fact, they proved much more general results for
H
-free graphs, where
H
is edge-critical). Balogh, Clemen, Lavrov, Lidicky and
Pfender
[
1
]
determined
f_{r}(n,t)
asymptotically for
t=o(n^{2})
, and conjectured that
f_{r}(n,t)
is witnessed by a pentagonal Turán graph if
t=o(n^{2})
. Recently, Korándi, Roberts and Scott
[
13
]
confirmed this conjecture. In this paper, we focus on cycles.

Let
C_{l}
denote a cycle with
l
vertices.
Füredi-Gunderson
[
11
]
determined
ex(n,C_{2k+1})
and gave a characterization of the extremal graph.

Theorem 1.1

(Füredi-Gunderson
[
11
]
)
.

For
n\geq 4k-2
,

ex(n,C_{2k+1})=\lfloor\frac{n^{2}}{4}\rfloor.

Furthermore, the only extremal graph is the complete bipartite graph
K_{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor,\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil}
.

It is natural to ask how far a
C_{2k+1}
-free graph is from being bipartite. In this paper, we show that if
G
is an
n
-vertex
C_{2k+1}
-free graph satisfying
ex(n,C_{2k+1})-e(G)\leq{(r-1)(2n-r+1)\over 4}-{r\choose 2}
, then
G
can be obtained by adding suspensions to a bipartite graph one by one and the total number of vertices not in this bipartite graph is no more than
r-1
if
2k\geq r+4
and
n\geq 20(r+2)^{2}k
, moreover, the total number of vertices not in this bipartite graph equals
r-1
if and only if
G
is the graph obtained by adding a suspension of
K_{r}
to
K_{\lfloor\frac{n-r+1}{2}\rfloor,\lceil\frac{n-r+1}{2}\rceil}
. Our result strengthen results in
[
14
]
and gives a strong structural information for
C_{2k+1}
-free graph
G
rather than the distance from being bipartite when
ex(n,C_{2k+1})-e(G)=O(nk)
if
k=O(n^{1\over 3})
. Let us give details below.

Define

d_{2}(G)=\min\{|T|:T\subseteq V(G),G-T\ \text{is bipartite}\},

\gamma_{2}(G)=\min\{|E|:E\subseteq E(G),G-E\ \text{is bipartite}\}.

T^{*}(r,n)

Define
T^{*}(r,n)
to be the graph consisting of a
K_{\lfloor\frac{n-r+1}{2}\rfloor,\lceil\frac{n-r+1}{2}\rceil}
and a
K_{r}
sharing exactly one vertex.
Note that

d_{2}(T^{*}(r,n))=r-1,\ \gamma_{2}(T^{*}(r,n))={\lceil\frac{r}{2}\rceil\choose 2}+{\lfloor\frac{r}{2}\rfloor\choose 2},\ e(T^{*}(r,n))=\big\lfloor{\frac{(n-r+1)^{2}}{4}}\big\rfloor+{r\choose 2}.

Recently, Ren-Wang-Wang-Yang
[
14
]
showed that if
G
is
C_{2k+1}
-free and
e(G)\geq e(T^{*}(r,n))
, then
d_{2}(G)\leq r-1
and
\gamma_{2}(G)\leq{\lceil\frac{r}{2}\rceil\choose 2}+{\lfloor\frac{r}{2}\rfloor\choose 2}
, and equalities hold if and only if
G=T^{*}(r,n)
.

Theorem 1.2

(Ren-Wang-Wang-Yang
[
14
]
)
.

Let
3\leq r\leq 2k
and
n\geq 318(r-2)^{2}k
. If
G
is an
n
-vertex
C_{2k+1}
-free graph with
e(G)\geq\big\lfloor{\frac{(n-r+1)^{2}}{4}}\big\rfloor+{r\choose 2}
. Then

d_{2}(G)\leq r-1
and
\gamma_{2}(G)\leq{\lceil\frac{r}{2}\rceil\choose 2}+{\lfloor\frac{r}{2}\rfloor\choose 2}
, and equalities hold if and only if
G=T^{*}(r,n)
.

In
[
14
]
, they proved
d_{2}(G)\leq r-1
and
\gamma_{2}(G)\leq{\lceil\frac{r}{2}\rceil\choose 2}+{\lfloor\frac{r}{2}\rfloor\choose 2}
in two separate theorems by different proofs. In this paper, we give a new and simpler method to obtain a structural result implying both results if
2k\geq r+4
.

If a graph
G
and a graph
B
have at most one vertex in common and there is no edge connecting
V(G)-V(G)\cap V(B)
and
V(B)-V(G)\cap V(B)
, then we call graph
G
a
suspension
to graph
B
with
|V(G)\cap V(B)|
suspension point, and call
V(G)\cap V(B)
the
suspension point
. For example, in Figure 2,
G_{1}
is a suspension of graph
B
,
G_{2}
is a suspension to graph
B\cup G_{1}
and
G_{3}
is a suspension to graph
B\cup G_{1}\cup G_{2}
.

Suspension Example

The main result in this paper is the following.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
r\geq 1
,
2k\geq r+4
and
n\geq 20(r+2)^{2}k
be integers. Let
G
be an
n
-vertex
C_{2k+1}
-free graph. If
e(G)\geq\big\lfloor{\frac{(n-r+1)^{2}}{4}}\big\rfloor+{r\choose 2}=e(T^{*}(r,n))
, then
G
is obtained by adding suspensions to a bipartite graph
B=G[V_{1},V_{2}]
one by one (in other words,
G=B\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{p}G_{i}
for some
p
,
G_{1}
is a suspension to
B
,
G_{j}
is a suspension to
B\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{j-1}G_{i}
for
2\leq j\leq p
) such that the total number of vertices not in
B
is no more than
r-1
,
\frac{n-\sqrt{(r-1)(n-r+1)}}{2}\leq|V_{1}|,|V_{2}|\leq\frac{n+\sqrt{(r-1)(n-r+1)}}{2}
and
e(B)\geq\big\lfloor{\frac{(n-r+1)^{2}}{4}}\big\rfloor
. Furthermore, the total number of vertices not in
B
equals
r-1
if and only if
G=T^{*}(r,n)
.

Theorem
1.3
guarantees that the total number of vertices not in the bipartite graph
B
is no more than
r-1
. Deleting the vertices not in
B
yields a bipartite graph, thus
d_{2}(G)\leq r-1
. Meanwhile, we can delete half of the edges in all suspensions (at most
{\lceil\frac{r}{2}\rceil\choose 2}+{\lfloor\frac{r}{2}\rfloor\choose 2}
edges) to obtain a bipartite graph. So
\gamma_{2}(G)\leq{\lceil\frac{r}{2}\rceil\choose 2}+{\lfloor\frac{r}{2}\rfloor\choose 2}
. Therefore, Theorem
1.3
implies Theorem
1.2
if
2k\geq r+4
. The condition on
e(G)
in Theorem
1.3
means that
ex(n,C_{2k+1})-e(G)\leq{(r-1)(2n-r+1)\over 4}-{r\choose 2}
. Combining the conditions that
2k\geq r+4
and
n\geq 20(r+2)^{2}k
, we have
ex(n,C_{2k+1})-e(G)=O(nk)
if
k=O(n^{1\over 3})
. In this case, we give a strong structural information for
C_{2k+1}
-free graph
G
rather than the distance from being bipartite.

Proof of Theorem
1.3

The following well-known result will be applied.

Lemma 2.1
.

If
G
has
m
edges and
n
vertices, then
G
contains a subgraph
H
with
\delta(H)\geq\frac{m}{n}
.

We first show the following preliminary result.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
r,k
and
n
be positive integers with
2k\geq r+4
and
n\geq 20(r+2)^{2}k
. Let
G
be a
C_{2k+1}
-free graph on
n
vertices. If
e(G)\geq\big\lfloor{\frac{(n-r+1)^{2}}{4}}\big\rfloor+{r\choose 2}=e(T^{*}(r,n))
, then for any even path
P_{xy}
of order at most
2k
, we have
|(N(x)\cap N(y))\setminus V(P_{xy})|\leq 8k.

Proof of Lemma
2.2
.
Assume that there exist an even path
P_{xy}
of order
2l
(
\leq 2k
) such that
|(N(x)\cap N(y))\setminus V(P_{xy})|>8k
. Let
U\subset(N(x)\cap N(y))\setminus V(P_{xy})
with
|U|=8k
. Let
W=V(G)\setminus(U\cup V(P_{xy}))
. We claim that
e(G[U,W])\leq kn
. If
e(G[U,W])>kn
, then by Lemma
2.1
there exists
U_{0}\subseteq U
and
W_{0}\subseteq W
such that
\delta(G[U_{0},V_{0}])\geq k
. Then there exists an odd path
P
of order

in the bipartite graph
G[U_{0},V_{0}]
with endpoints in
U_{0}
, combining with
P_{xy}
forms a

, a contradiction. Hence
e(G[U,W])\leq kn
. Theorem
1.1
implies that
e(G[V(P_{xy})\cup W])\leq\frac{(n-8k)^{2}}{4}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle e(G)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e(G[U,V(P_{xy})\cup W])+e(G[U])+e(G[V(P_{xy})\cup W])

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle kn+8k|V(P_{xy})|+{8k\choose 2}+\frac{(n-8k)^{2}}{4}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle kn+48k^{2}+\frac{(n-8k)^{2}}{4}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\frac{(n-r+1)^{2}}{4}-kn+64k^{2}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle{r\choose 2}+\bigg\lfloor{\frac{(n-r+1)^{2}}{4}}\bigg\rfloor=e(T^{*}(r,n))

since

,
a contradiction.

\hfill\square

Let

be a strong-

-core of

with

such that

and

) such that
xu\in E(G)
and

for some

with

, then

.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
r,k
and
n
be positive integers with
2k\geq r+4
and
n\geq 20(r+2)^{2}k
. Let
G
be a

-free graph on

.

Proof of Lemma
2.4
.
We will show that
|V(H)|\leq r+1
first. Suppose on the contrary that
|V(H)|\geq r+2
. By Lemma
2.2
, we have
|(N(x)\cap N(y))\setminus V(P_{xy})|\leq 8k
for

, where

is an even path of order at most

. Then
|N(x)\cap N(y)|\leq 10k
for

. Take

Note that

-free, by Theorem

we have

. Hence

\displaystyle e(G)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg(e(G[U,V(G)\setminus U])+e(G[U])\bigg)+e(G[V(G)\setminus U])

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\bigg({|U|\choose 2}\cdot 10k\cdot 2+n\bigg)+\frac{(n-|U|)^{2}}{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{r+2\choose 2}\cdot 10k\cdot 2+n+\frac{(n-r-2)^{2}}{4}

\displaystyle\lfloor{\frac{(n-r+1)^{2}}{4}}\rfloor+{r\choose 2}=e(T^{*}(r,n))

.
A contradiction.

Next we will show that

. Otherwise, there exists a strong-

with

. By Fact

\displaystyle e(G)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg(e(G[H_{0},V(G)\setminus H_{0}])+e(G[H_{0}])\bigg)+e(G[V(G)\setminus H_{0}])

\displaystyle{r+1\choose 2}+|A|+\frac{(n-|H_{0}|)^{2}}{4}

Concluding Remarks

We have shown that if
G
is an
n
-vertex
C_{2k+1}
-free graph satisfying
ex(n,C_{2k+1})-e(G)\leq{(r-1)(2n-r+1)\over 4}-{r\choose 2}
, then
G
can be obtained by adding suspensions to a bipartite graph
B=G[V_{1},V_{2}]
one by one such that the total number of vertices not in
B
is no more than
r-1
,
\frac{n-\sqrt{(r-1)(n-r+1)}}{2}\leq|V_{1}|,|V_{2}|\leq\frac{n+\sqrt{(r-1)(n-r+1)}}{2}
and
e(B)\geq\big\lfloor{\frac{(n-r+1)^{2}}{4}}\big\rfloor
. Furthermore, the total number of vertices not in
B
equals
r-1
if and only if
G=T^{*}(r,n)
.
In this case,
ex(n,C_{2k+1})-e(G)=O(kn^{4\over 3})
if
k=O(n^{1\over 3})
. It would be interesting to extend the range of
ex(n,C_{2k+1})-e(G)
. Indeed, Korándi, Roberts and Scott
[
13
]
made the following interesting conjecture.

Conjecture 2.5
.

Fix
k\geq 2
and let
\delta
be small enough. Then for any
\delta>\delta_{0}>0
and large enough
n
, the
following holds. For every
C_{2k-1}
-free graph
G
on
n
vertices with
(1/4-\delta_{0})n^{2}\geq e(G)\geq(1/4-\delta)n^{2}
edges,
there is a
C_{2k+1}
-blowup
G^{*}
satisfying
e(G^{*})\geq e(G)
and
D_{2}(G^{*})\geq D_{2}(G)
.

A closely related problem is the old conjecture of Erdős
[
3
]
claiming
D_{2}(G)\leq n^{2}/25
for every
K_{3}
-free graph
G
on
n
vertices. Since every graph contains a bipartite graph with at least half edges, it holds when
e(G)\leq 2n^{2}/25
. Erdős, Faudree, Pach
and Spencer
[
6
]
confirmed it for
e(G)\geq n^{2}/5
. If the conjecture is true, it is tight for a balanced blowup of
C_{5}
. This problem led to further study on how far
K_{r+1}
-free graphs can be from being bipartite.
Sudakov
[
17
]
showed that
D_{2}(G)
is maximized by
G=T_{3}(n)
among all
K_{4}
-free graphs, and he conjectured that this generalizes to larger cliques (i.e., among

K_{r+1}
-free graphs,
D_{2}(G)
is maximum when
G=T_{r}(n)
). Hu, Lidický, Martins, Norin and Volec
[
12
]
has given a proof for
K_{6}
. All other cases remain wide open.

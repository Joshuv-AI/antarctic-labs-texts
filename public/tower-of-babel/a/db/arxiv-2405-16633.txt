Random walks on edge colored random graphs
Colin Cooper, Alan Frieze

Abstract
We consider random walks on edge coloured random graphs, where the colour of an edge reflects the cost of using it. In the simplest instance, the edges are coloured red or blue. Blue edges are free to use, whereas red edges incur a unit cost every time they are traversed.

Introduction

The cover time of a connected graph is the maximum over the start vertex of the expected time for a simple random walk to visit every vertex of the graph. There is a large literature on this subject see for example
[
1
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
14
]
, and including
[
4
]
–
[

which give precise estimates of cover time for various models of random graphs.

We consider the following scenario: There is a connected graph
G
in which the edges are colored red and blue. We study the cover time of such a graph when there is a bound on the number red edges that can be used. We can think of blue edges as free to use, whereas red edges represent toll roads, the bound being the maximum budget.
The decision to use, or not to use, a toll road can be made in various ways, which we characterize as congestion charging, flip walks, oblivious walks and smooth walks.
In the simplest case, the
oblivious model
, the random walk uses edges of
G
regardless of colour until the budget is used up, after which it will be restricted to the blue sub-graph.

Models of red-blue regular graphs.

Let
G
be a connected

be a simple random walk on

for the constrained cover time of

Let

\sigma_{RB}=\frac{r+b-1}{r+b-2},\qquad\sigma_{B}=\frac{b-1}{b-2}.

(1)

Another simple model is to take

This model, based on short cutting a Hamilton cycle,

For

Subsequently, the fact that 3-regular graphs are Hamiltonian w.h.p. was established by Robinson and Wormald

Thus almost all 3-regular graphs have a decomposition into a blue Hamilton cycle and a random red 1-factor.

Smooth walks.

The walk alternates in phases between using edges of either colour (a red/blue phase) and edges of colour blue only (a blue phase). In a red/blue phase the walk is on
G
, and in a blue phase the walk is on
G_{B}
, the subgraph induced by the blue edges. The length of the phases can vary, but to analyse the model, we require that the length of each phase is at least
\omega T_{G}\log n
where
T_{G}
is a mixing time given by (
5

\omega\to\infty
with

smooth
.
We say that such a random walk is
\alpha
-constrained
if in total, it is only allowed to use a red edge at most
\gamma(\alpha)n\log n
times, where
\gamma(\alpha)
is given in Theorem
1
(a) below.

The
congestion pricing model
fits naturally as a special case of smooth walks. Peak and off-peak periods alternate as in the smoothed model. The length
C,F
(charged, free) of the peak and off peak periods are fixed and in constant proportion. The walk uses the red (charged) edges only in off-peak periods when they are free to use. In peak periods the walk uses only blue (free) edges. We assume there are at most
n^{\theta}
distinct phases of equal length

(Smooth walks)

Let

Flip walks.

In a flip walk there is no hard bound on the number of times we use a red edge, only a stochastic one. At any step, let
\rho_{R}
be the probability of transition down a given red edge adjacent to the current vertex and
\rho_{B}
the probability of transition down a given blue edge. Thus the probability the next transition is red is
r\rho_{R}
etc. Examples include the following. Let
\rho_{R}=\alpha/r

\rho_{B}=(1-\alpha)/b
in which case the probability of transition along a red edge at any step is
\alpha

1-\alpha

(Flip walks)

Proof outline.

The main tool in our proofs will be the
First Time Visit Lemma
,
a technique which will allow us to make very precise estimates of cover time in certain classes of graphs. The technique was introduced in
[
4
]
, and subsequently refined in
[
7
]
, and in
[
8
]
where various
technical conditions were removed.
In outline, the approach is to bound the cover time above and below using
(
7
) of Lemma
4
. Much of the work is in constructing the lower bound on the cover time.
On regular graphs the walks we consider have uniform stationary distribution, and are rapidly mixing.
The unvisit probability (
7
) has a parameter
p_{v}\sim\pi_{v}/R_{v}
(see (
6
)) where
R_{v}
is the expected number of returns
to vertex
v
during the mixing time. On regular graphs a simple random walk has a uniform stationary distribution, and is rapidly mixing
on random
r
-regular graphs provided the vertex degree
r
is at least three.

2.1
First Time Visit Lemma

Let
G
denote a fixed connected graph, and let
u
be some arbitrary vertex from which a walk
{\mathcal{W}}_{u}
is started. Let
{\mathcal{W}}_{u}(t)
be the vertex reached at step
t
, let
P
be the matrix of transition probabilities of the walk, and let
P_{u}^{(t)}(v)=\mathbb{P}({\mathcal{W}}_{u}(t)=v)
. Let
\pi
be the steady state distribution of the random walk
{\mathcal{W}}_{u}
. For an unbiased ergodic random walk on a graph
G
with
m=m(G)
edges,
\pi_{v}=\frac{d(v)}{2m}
, where
d(v)
denotes the degree of
v
in
G
.

We denote the steady state probability of a simple random walk on
G=(V,E)
by
\pi_{G}(v)
for
v\in V
. We let
\mathbb{P}_{u,G}^{(t)}(v)
denote the probability that a simple random walk on
G
started at
u
is at
v
after
t
steps. We then define a mixing time
T_{G}
by

T_{G}=\min\left\{\tau:\max_{u,v}\left\{\frac{|\mathbb{P}_{u,G}^{(t)}(v)-\pi(v)|}{\pi(v)}\right\}=o(1)\text{ for }t\geq\tau\right\}.

(5)

For
t\geq 0
, let
{\mathcal{A}}_{t}(v)
be the event that
{\mathcal{W}}_{u}
does not visit
v
in steps
T_{G},T_{G}+1,\ldots,t
. The vertex
u
will have to be implicit in this definition. We will use the following version, it can be found in
[

]
:

Lemma 4
.

Let
R_{v}\geq 1
is the expected number of visits by
{\mathcal{W}}_{v}
to
v
in the time interval
[0,T_{G}]
. Suppose that
T_{G}\pi_{v}=o(1)
and
R_{v}=O(1)
. Let

p_{v}=\frac{\pi_{v}}{R_{v}(1+O(T_{G}\pi_{v}))}.

(6)

Then for all
t\geq T_{G}
,

\mathbb{P}({\mathcal{A}}_{t}(v))=\frac{(1+O(T_{G}\pi_{v}))}{(1+p_{v})^{t}}+O(T_{G}^{2}\pi_{v}e^{-\mu t/2}),

(7)

where
\mu=\Omega(1/T_{G})
.

3.1
Proof of Theorem

(a)

In this section

-regular graph with blue edges, where

and

. We prove that if the budget is
\gamma(\alpha)n\log n
, where
\gamma(\alpha)=\alpha\sigma_{RB}r/(r+b)

then w.h.p. the cover time of an

It is shown in

that w.h.p. the unconstrained cover time of

\widehat{C}_{G}\sim\sigma_{RB}\,n\log n

If the phases are less than a mixing time in length (

), so that the walk is not smooth, there are some technical difficulties that we have not yet managed to overcome.

Remark 1
.

There does not seem to be a compelling reason for us to only consider smooth walks. If we allow the walk to switch arbitrarily between allowing red edges and not allowing them, then because

there are no problems with the walk mixing. This is because the blue subgraph is an expander and the steady state probabilities are

regardless. The problem arises because the

Upper bound

Suppose now that we start a walk at vertex

Lemma 6

W.h.p. a random 2-factor contains at least 2 cycles of length at least

Proof.

We begin by arguing that the number of cycles in a random 2-factor is

, among which we choose the longest. We then argue that w.h.p. the maximum length of this cycle is at most

Number of cycles.

Let

, where

. Now the proof of Proposition 2(a) of Karp
[

\mathbb{P}(\ell(\pi)\geq a\log n)\leq\mathbb{P}(Bin(a\log n,1/2)\leq\log_{2}n)\leq n^{-a/3}\qquad\text{for }a\geq 2.

And

\mathbb{P}(\ell(F)\geq 3\log_{2}n)=\sum_{m\geq 3\log_{2}n}\frac{|\{\pi\in S^{\prime}:\ell(\pi)=m\}|}{2^{m}M}=O(n^{-1}).

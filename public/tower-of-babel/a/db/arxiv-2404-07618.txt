Optimal State Equation for the Control of a Diffusion with Two Distinct Dynamics
Zengjing Chen, Panyu Wu, Xiaowen Zhou

Abstract




Abstract:
We consider a class of stochastic control problems which has been widely used in optimal
foraging theory. The state processes have two distinct dynamics, characterized by two pairs of drift and diffusion coefficients, depending on whether it takes values bigger or smaller than a threshold value. Adopting a perturbation type approach, we find an expression for potential measure of the optimal state process.
We then obtain an expression for the transition density of the optimal state process by inverting the associated Laplace transform. Properties including the stationary distribution of the optimal state process are discussed. Finally, the expression of the value function is given for this class stochastic control problems.

MSC (2020):
93E20, 60J60, 60G17

Keywords:
stochastic optimal control, value function, diffusion process, Laplace transform, potential measure, density

Introduction

In this paper, we revisit a stochastic control problem introduced in McNamara
[
17
]
in which a one-dimensional diffusion process is controlled by
switching between two given diffusions over time interval
[0,T]
with drift and diffusion coefficients
(\mu_{1},\sigma_{1})
and
(\mu_{2},\sigma_{2})
, respectively. The objective is to maximize the probability that the process lies in
[0,\infty)
at the final time
T
.
It is known that the optimal state equation can be transformed into the following stochastic differential equation (SDE)

\mathrm{d}X_{t}=(\mu_{1}\mathbf{1}_{\{X_{t}\leq 0\}}+\mu_{2}\mathbf{1}_{\{X_{t}>0\}})\mathrm{d}t+(\sigma_{1}\mathbf{1}_{\{X_{t}\leq 0\}}+\sigma_{2}\mathbf{1}_{\{X_{t}>0\}})\mathrm{d}B_{t}.

This control model was widely used to investigate the optimal diet of a forager faced with two types of preys, where

(\mu_{1},\sigma_{1})
and
(\mu_{2},\sigma_{2})
denote the mean and standard deviation of the energy gain per minute under the two option, respectively. The animal can switch freely between the two options.
Here
X_{t}
denotes the energy gained by capturing prey at time
t
and the objective is to maximize
the probability of
X_{T}\geq 0
; see, for example, Houston and McNamara
[
9
,
10
]
, Kacelnik and Bateson
[
11
]
and references therein.
By identifying an autonomous vehicle or software module as a forager
searching for tasks, Andrews et al.

[
1
]
expanded this foraging model to fit an autonomous vehicle control problem and provided how to make high-level control
decisions through this control model.

But the maximal survival probability can not be evaluated.
To evaluate the value function of this control problem, one needs to know the transition probability density of
X:=(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
.
Thus, it is an interesting problem and also the aim of this paper to characteristic the probability law of solution
X
to

\mathrm{d}X_{t}=(\mu_{1}\mathbf{1}_{\{X_{t}\leq a\}}+\mu_{2}\mathbf{1}_{\{X_{t}>a\}})\mathrm{d}t+(\sigma_{1}\mathbf{1}_{X_{t}\leq a}+\sigma_{2}\mathbf{1}_{\{X_{t}>a\}})\mathrm{d}B_{t},

(1.1)

where
a
is any fixed real value.

In order to obtain the transition probability densities of SDE (
1.1
), many scholars work on the following two special cases SDEs (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) for
\mu_{1}=\mu_{2}=\mu
or
\sigma_{1}=\sigma_{2}=\sigma
respectively,

\mathrm{d}X_{t}=\mu\mathrm{d}t+(\sigma_{1}\mathbf{1}_{\{X_{t}\leq a\}}+\sigma_{2}\mathbf{1}_{\{X_{t}>a\}})\mathrm{d}B_{t},

(1.2)

\mathrm{d}X_{t}=(\mu_{1}\mathbf{1}_{\{X_{t}\leq a\}}+\mu_{2}\mathbf{1}_{\{X_{t}>a\}})\mathrm{d}t+\sigma\mathrm{d}B_{t}.

(1.3)

These two special cases SDEs (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) are the optimal state equations for bang-bang controls of diffusion or drift, respectively.

For example, the transition probability density of
X
satisfying (
1.2
) with bang-bang diffusion,
known as an oscillating Brownian motion, was obtained by Keilson and Wellner
[
15
]
using the properties of occupation time and Laplace transform. McNamara
[
18
]
established a regularity condition on the distribution of
X
for (
1.2
).
The transition probability density of
X
satisfying (
1.3
) can be recovered from the trivariate density of the Brownian motion, its local and occupation times obtained by Karatzas and Shreve
[
12
]
. However, the expression for transition density of the diffusion process satisfying SDE (
1.1
) with different coefficients for both drift and diffusion remains unknown.

Bene
\check{s}

et al.
[
2
]
first found an expression for transition density of a Brownian motion with a two-valued drift to characterize the
optimal state trajectory in a control problem. For the bang-bang control of drift, Steven
[
22
]
obtained the explicit representation of
the optimal value function through the joint distribution of Brownian motion and its local time for square cost function.
Zhang
[
24
]
derived a probabilistic representation of the transition density of
Brownian motion with a general bounded piecewise-continuous drift function and applied it to a bang-bang control problem.
Chen et al.
[
5
]
gave an explicit representation of the optimal control and the optimal value
function for the bang-bang control problem of drift by the explicit solution of backward SDEs for symmetric cost function.
Ocone and Weerasinghe
[
19
]
obtained some explicit characterizations of the optimal control and optimal value functions with possibly degenerate variance control.
Decamps et al.
[
7
]
considered scalar diffusions with scale and speed functions
discontinuous at certain level and called that family of processes self exciting threshold diffusions and obtained semi-analytical expressions for the transition densities of these diffusions. They also proposed several applications to interest rates modeling.
More recently, an optimal dividend problem is studied in Wang et al.
[
23
]
for a risk process with endogenous regime switching whose dynamics follows SDE (
1.1
).

In this paper we aim at finding an expression of the value function for the control problem in McNamara
[
17
]
by finding an expression for transition density of the optimal state process
X
satisfying (
1.1
). To this end, we adopt an approach that is different from those for the previous results along this line. More precisely, we first find an expression for potential measure
\mathbb{P}(X_{e_{q}}\in\mathrm{d}z)
for which we apply a perturbation argument and take use of solution to the exit problem for
X
. Here
e_{q}
is an independent exponential random variable with rate
q
. The desired expression of transition density then follows from Laplace inversion, which generalizes those results in Karatzas and Shreve
[
12
]
, as well as in Keilson and Wellner
[
15
]
.
Properties of the obtained transition density are also discussed. Specifically, we find that the optimal state equation is time reversible if and only if the diffusion and drift controls are both single control. We also establish the continuity of the transition density function with respect to the initial state, and observe that the continuity with respect to the final state depends solely on whether the diffusion control is single control, and is not unaffected by the drift controls. Moreover, we determine the stationary distribution of the optimal state process when
\mu_{1}>0
and
\mu_{2}<0
. Lastly, we give the expression of the value function for the control problem.

The rest of the paper is structured as follows.
Section 2 contains preparations for the main results.
In Section 3, we derive an expression for the density of
X_{e_{q}}
.
In Section 4, we obtain an expression for the transition density of
X
and investigate properties of the density function.
Examples and graphs of the transition density function are provided in Section 5. Finally, in Section 6, we apply the findings to a control problem involving two given drift-diffusion pairs.

2
Preliminaries

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{F},\mathbb{P})
be a complete filtered probability space, where
\mathbb{F}=(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is the corresponding filtration satisfies the usual condition.
Fix any
a\in\mathbb{R}
and
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in\mathbb{R},\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}>0
. Consider the following stochastic differential equation

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}X_{t}=(\mu_{1}\mathbf{1}_{\{X_{t}\leq a\}}+\mu_{2}\mathbf{1}_{\{X_{t}>a\}})\mathrm{d}t+(\sigma_{1}\mathbf{1}_{\{X_{t}\leq a\}}+\sigma_{2}\mathbf{1}_{\{X_{t}>a\}})\mathrm{d}B_{t}

(2.1)

with two-valued drift and diffusion coefficients. It follows from Étoré and Martinez
[
8
]
or Le Gall
[
16
]
that (
2.1
) admits a unique strong solution.

The following solutions to the exit problems for process
X
were first obtained in Wang et al.
[
23
]
using a martingale approach. We outline their derivations for convenience of the readers.

Write

\displaystyle\delta_{1}^{+}:=\frac{\sqrt{2q\sigma_{1}^{2}+\mu_{1}^{2}}+\mu_{1}}{\sigma_{1}^{2}},\quad\delta_{2}^{+}:=\frac{\sqrt{2q\sigma_{2}^{2}+\mu_{2}^{2}}+\mu_{2}}{\sigma_{2}^{2}},

\displaystyle\delta_{1}^{-}:=\frac{\sqrt{2q\sigma_{1}^{2}+\mu_{1}^{2}}-\mu_{1}}{\sigma_{1}^{2}},\quad\delta_{2}^{-}:=\frac{\sqrt{2q\sigma_{2}^{2}+\mu_{2}^{2}}-\mu_{2}}{\sigma_{2}^{2}}.

Then two solutions
g^{\pm}_{q}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})\cap C^{2}(\mathbb{R}\backslash\{a\})
to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\sigma_{1}^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\{x\leq a\}}+\sigma_{2}^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\{x>a\}})g^{\prime\prime}(x)+(\mu_{1}\mathbf{1}_{\{x\leq a\}}+\mu_{2}\mathbf{1}_{\{x>a\}})g^{\prime}(x)=qg(x)

are

\displaystyle\begin{cases}g_{q}^{-}(x)=\left(c_{-}e^{\delta_{1}^{-}{(x-a)}}+(1-c_{-})e^{-\delta_{1}^{+}{(x-a)}}\right)\mathbf{1}_{\{x\leq a\}}+e^{-\delta_{2}^{+}{(x-a)}}\mathbf{1}_{\{x>a\}},\\
g_{q}^{+}(x)=e^{\delta_{1}^{-}{(x-a)}}\mathbf{1}_{\{x\leq a\}}+\left((1-c_{+})e^{\delta_{2}^{-}{(x-a)}}+c_{+}e^{-\delta_{2}^{+}{(x-a)}}\right)\mathbf{1}_{\{x>a\}},\end{cases}

(2.2)

where the two constants
c_{\pm}
, determined by the smooth pasting conditions
g_{q}^{\pm\prime}(a-)=g_{q}^{\pm\prime}(a+)
, are given as

\displaystyle c_{-}=\frac{\delta_{1}^{+}-\delta_{2}^{+}}{\delta_{1}^{-}+\delta_{1}^{+}},\quad 1-c_{-}=\frac{\delta_{1}^{-}+\delta_{2}^{+}}{\delta_{1}^{-}+\delta_{1}^{+}}>0,

and

\displaystyle c_{+}=\frac{\delta_{2}^{-}-\delta_{1}^{-}}{\delta_{2}^{-}+\delta_{2}^{+}},\quad 1-c_{+}=\frac{\delta_{2}^{+}+\delta_{1}^{-}}{\delta_{2}^{-}+\delta_{2}^{+}}>0.

We introduce the following first hitting time of level
y\in\mathbb{R}
for the process
X
as

T_{y}:=\inf\{t\geq 0;\,X_{t}=y\}

with the convention
\inf\emptyset=\infty
.
For
y\leq x\leq z
, applying the Meyer-Itô formula (see Theorem 70 of
Chapter IV in Protter
[
20
]
) we know that the two processes

(e^{-qt}g_{q}^{\pm}(X_{t}))_{t\geq 0}
are local martingales. Then, it holds that

g_{q}^{-}(x)=\mathbb{E}_{x}[e^{-q(T_{y}\wedge T_{z})}g_{q}^{-}(X_{T_{y}\wedge T_{z}})]=g_{q}^{-}(y)\mathbb{E}_{x}[e^{-qT_{y}}\mathbf{1}_{\{T_{y}<T_{z}\}}]+g_{q}^{-}(z)\mathbb{E}_{x}[e^{-qT_{z}}\mathbf{1}_{\{T_{z}<T_{y}\}}],

and

g_{q}^{+}(x)=\mathbb{E}_{x}[e^{-q(T_{y}\wedge T_{z})}g_{q}^{+}(X_{T_{y}\wedge T_{z}})]=g_{q}^{+}(y)\mathbb{E}_{x}[e^{-qT_{y}}\mathbf{1}_{\{T_{y}<T_{z}\}}]+g_{q}^{+}(z)\mathbb{E}_{x}[e^{-qT_{z}}\mathbf{1}_{\{T_{z}<T_{y}\}}].

Solving the above two equations, we get the following solutions to the exit problems

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x}[e^{-qT_{y}}\mathbf{1}_{\{T_{y}<T_{z}\}}]=\frac{g_{q}^{+}(z)g_{q}^{-}(x)-g_{q}^{-}(z)g_{q}^{+}(x)}{g_{q}^{-}(y)g_{q}^{+}(z)-g_{q}^{-}(z)g_{q}^{+}(y)},

(2.3)

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x}[e^{-qT_{z}}\mathbf{1}_{\{T_{z}<T_{y}\}}]=\frac{g_{q}^{+}(y)g_{q}^{-}(x)-g_{q}^{-}(y)g_{q}^{+}(x)}{g_{q}^{-}(z)g_{q}^{+}(y)-g_{q}^{-}(y)g_{q}^{+}(z)}.

(2.4)

Combining (
2.3
) and (
2.2
), we have for
y\leq x
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[e^{-qT_{y}}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[e^{-qT_{y}}\mathbf{1}_{\{T_{y}<\infty\}}\right]=\lim_{z\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[e^{-qT_{y}}\mathbf{1}_{\{T_{y}<T_{z}\}}\right]=\frac{g_{q}^{-}(x)}{g_{q}^{-}(y)}.

(2.5)

Similarly, combining (
2.4
) and (
2.2
), we have for
x\leq z
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[e^{-qT_{z}}\right]=\frac{g_{q}^{+}(x)}{g_{q}^{+}(z)}.

(2.6)

Let
e_{q}
be an exponential random variable with rate
q
which is independent of
X
. We proceed to find an expression for

\mathbb{P}_{x}(X_{e_{q}}\in\mathrm{d}z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}qe^{-qt}\mathbb{P}_{x}(X_{t}\in\mathrm{d}z)\mathrm{d}t

(2.7)

using a perturbation approach and solution to the exit problem.
Then an expression for the transition density function of
X
can be obtained by inverting the above Laplace transform.

We recall some Laplace transforms for linear diffusion process. The results in the following lemma can be found in 1.0.5 on p250 and in 2.0.1 on p295 of Borodin and Salminen
[
3
]
.

Lemma 2.1

For
X_{t}^{*}=X_{0}^{*}+\mu t+\sigma B_{t}
, write
T_{z}^{*}:=\inf\{t\geq 0;\ X_{t}^{*}=z\}
. For any
x,z\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\mathbb{P}_{x}(X_{e_{q}}^{*}\in\mathrm{d}z)=\frac{q}{\sqrt{2q\sigma^{2}+\mu^{2}}}\exp{\left(\frac{\mu(z-x)-|z-x|\sqrt{2q\sigma^{2}+\mu^{2}}}{\sigma^{2}}\right)}\mathrm{d}z,

(2.8)

\mathbb{E}_{x}e^{-qT^{*}_{z}}=\exp{\left(\frac{\mu(z-x)-|z-x|\sqrt{2q\sigma^{2}+\mu^{2}}}{\sigma^{2}}\right)},

(2.9)

\frac{1}{\sqrt{2q\sigma^{2}+\mu^{2}}}\exp{\left(\frac{\mu(z-x)-|z-x|\sqrt{2q\sigma^{2}+\mu^{2}}}{\sigma^{2}}\right)}=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-qt}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi t\sigma^{2}}}\exp{\left(-\frac{(z-x-\mu t)^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}t}\right)}\mathrm{d}t.

(2.10)

Denote

X_{t}^{(1)}=X_{0}^{(1)}+\mu_{1}t+\sigma_{1}B_{t},\quad T_{z}^{(1)}:=\inf\{t\geq 0;\ X_{t}^{(1)}=z\},

and

X_{t}^{(2)}=X_{0}^{(2)}+\mu_{2}t+\sigma_{2}B_{t},\quad T_{z}^{(2)}:=\inf\{t\geq 0;\ X_{t}^{(2)}=z\}.

We will use the following function
h
to represent the transition density of
X
in Section 4, where

h(t;x,\mu):=\frac{|x|}{\sqrt{2\pi t^{3}}}\exp\left[-\frac{(x+\mu t)^{2}}{2t}\right],\quad x,\mu\in\mathbb{R},t>0,

(2.11)

is introduced by Karlin and Taylor
[
14
]
in Theorem 5.3 of Chapter 7 (see also p824 of Karatzas and Shreve
[
12
]
).
It is easy to check that

h(t;x,\mu)=h(t;-x,-\mu),\quad h(t;-x,\mu)=h(t;x,-\mu)=h(t;x,\mu)e^{2\mu x}.

(2.12)

Let
T_{x}^{\mu}
be the passage time of a Brownian motion defined by

T_{x}^{\mu}:=\inf\{t\geq 0;\mu t+B(t)=x\}.

Then from the result on p197 of Karatzas and Shreve
[
13
]
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(T_{0}^{\mu}\in dt\right)=h(t;x,\mu)dt,\quad x,\mu\in\mathbb{R},t>0,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{0}\left(T_{x}^{\mu}\in dt\right)=h(t;x,-\mu)dt,\quad x,\mu\in\mathbb{R},t>0.

In addition,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-qt}h(t;x,\mu)\mathrm{d}t=\begin{cases}\exp{\left[-\left(\mu+\mathrm{sgn}(x)\sqrt{\mu^{2}+2q}\right)x\right]},&x\neq 0,\\
0,&x=0,\end{cases}

(2.13)

and then

h(t;x_{1}+x_{2},\mu)=\int_{0}^{t}h(t-\tau;x_{1},\mu)h(\tau;x_{2},\mu)\mathrm{d}\tau\,\,\text{ for}\,\,x_{1}x_{2}>0.

(2.14)

From now on we always write
X
for the unique solution to SDE (
2.1
) and write
p(t;x,z)
for its transition density from state
x
to
z
after time
t
.

3
Probability distribution of
X_{e_{q}}

Recall that
e_{q}
is an exponential random variable with rate
q
that is independent of
X
. In this section, we find expressions of
\mathbb{P}_{x}(X_{e_{q}}\in\mathrm{d}z)
for different choices of
x
and
z
.

Theorem 3.1

For any
x\geq a
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}(X_{e_{q}}\in\mathrm{d}z)=\begin{cases}\frac{qe^{-\delta_{2}^{-}(z-a)}}{\sqrt{2q\sigma_{2}^{2}+\mu_{2}^{2}}}\left[e^{\delta_{2}^{-}(x-a)}+\frac{(\delta_{2}^{-}-\delta_{1}^{-})e^{-\delta_{2}^{+}(x-a)}}{\delta_{2}^{+}+\delta_{1}^{-}}\right]\mathrm{d}z,\quad z\geq x\geq a\\
\frac{qe^{-\delta_{2}^{+}(x-a)}}{\sqrt{2q\sigma_{2}^{2}+\mu_{2}^{2}}}\left[e^{\delta_{2}^{+}(z-a)}+\frac{(\delta_{2}^{-}-\delta_{1}^{-})e^{-\delta_{2}^{-}(z-a)}}{\delta_{2}^{+}+\delta_{1}^{-}}\right]\mathrm{d}z,\quad x\geq z\geq a.\end{cases}

(3.1)

Proof.

By the strong Markov property for
X
and the
memoryless property for exponential random variable we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(X_{e_{q}}\in\mathrm{d}z\right)=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(T_{a}<e_{q},X_{e_{q}}\in\mathrm{d}z\right)+\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(T_{a}\geq e_{q},X_{e_{q}}\in\mathrm{d}z\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(T_{a}<e_{q}\right)\mathbb{P}_{a}\left(X_{e_{q}}\in\mathrm{d}z\right)+\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(T^{(2)}_{a}\geq e_{q},X^{(2)}_{e_{q}}\in\mathrm{d}z\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x}e^{-qT_{a}}\mathbb{P}_{a}\left(X_{e_{q}}\in\mathrm{d}z\right)+\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(T^{(2)}_{a}\geq e_{q},X^{(2)}_{e_{q}}\in\mathrm{d}z\right).

(3.2)

Since
x\geq a
, by (
2.5
), we have

\mathbb{E}_{x}e^{-qT_{a}}=\frac{g_{q}^{-}(x)}{g_{q}^{-}(a)}=e^{-\delta_{2}^{+}(x-a)}.

(3.3)

For any
z\in\mathbb{R}
, let
r
be any numbers between
a
and
z
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{a}\left(X_{e_{q}}\in\mathrm{d}z\right)=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{a}\left(T_{r}<e_{q},X_{e_{q}}\in\mathrm{d}z\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{a}\left(T_{r}<e_{q}\right)\mathbb{P}_{r}\left(X_{e_{q}}\in\mathrm{d}z\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{a}e^{-qT_{r}}\left[\mathbb{P}_{r}\left(e_{q}<T_{a},X_{e_{q}}\in\mathrm{d}z\right)+\mathbb{P}_{r}\left(e_{q}>T_{a},X_{e_{q}}\in\mathrm{d}z\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{a}e^{-qT_{r}}\left[\mathbb{P}_{r}\left(e_{q}<T_{a},X_{e_{q}}\in\mathrm{d}z\right)+\mathbb{P}_{r}\left(e_{q}>T_{a}\right)\mathbb{P}_{a}\left(X_{e_{q}}\in\mathrm{d}z\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{a}e^{-qT_{r}}\left[\mathbb{P}_{r}\left(e_{q}<T_{a},X_{e_{q}}\in\mathrm{d}z\right)+\mathbb{E}_{r}e^{-qT_{a}}\mathbb{P}_{a}\left(X_{e_{q}}\in\mathrm{d}z\right)\right].

Thus,

\mathbb{P}_{a}\left(X_{e_{q}}\in\mathrm{d}z\right)=\frac{\mathbb{E}_{a}e^{-qT_{r}}\mathbb{P}_{r}\left(e_{q}<T_{a},X_{e_{q}}\in\mathrm{d}z\right)}{1-\mathbb{E}_{a}e^{-qT_{r}}\mathbb{E}_{r}e^{-qT_{a}}},\quad z\in\mathbb{R}.

(3.4)

In this theorem
z\geq a
, thus,
a\leq r\leq z
. Combining (
2.5
) and (
2.6
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{a}e^{-qT_{r}}=\frac{g_{q}^{+}(a)}{g_{q}^{+}(r)}=\frac{\delta_{2}^{+}+\delta_{2}^{-}}{(\delta_{2}^{+}+\delta_{1}^{-})e^{\delta_{2}^{-}(r-a)}+(\delta_{2}^{-}-\delta_{1}^{-})e^{-\delta_{2}^{+}(r-a)}},

(3.5)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{r}e^{-qT_{a}}=\frac{g_{q}^{-}(r)}{g_{q}^{-}(a)}=e^{-\delta_{2}^{+}(r-a)}.

(3.6)

Using the strong Markov property for
X^{(2)}
and the
memoryless property for exponential random variable, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{r}\left(e_{q}<T_{a},X_{e_{q}}\in\mathrm{d}z\right)=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{r}\left(e_{q}<T^{(2)}_{a},X^{(2)}_{e_{q}}\in\mathrm{d}z\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{r}\left(X^{(2)}_{e_{q}}\in\mathrm{d}z\right)-\mathbb{P}_{r}\left(e_{q}\geq T^{(2)}_{a},X^{(2)}_{e_{q}}\in\mathrm{d}z\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{r}\left(X^{(2)}_{e_{q}}\in\mathrm{d}z\right)-\mathbb{P}_{r}\left(e_{q}\geq T^{(2)}_{a}\right)\mathbb{P}_{a}\left(X^{(2)}_{e_{q}}\in\mathrm{d}z\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{r}\left(X^{(2)}_{e_{q}}\in\mathrm{d}z\right)-\mathbb{E}_{r}e^{-qT^{(2)}_{a}}\mathbb{P}_{a}\left(X^{(2)}_{e_{q}}\in\mathrm{d}z\right)

(3.7)

By Lemma
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{r}\left(e_{q}<T_{a},X_{e_{q}}\in\mathrm{d}z\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{q}{\sqrt{2q\sigma_{2}^{2}+\mu_{2}^{2}}}\left[\exp\left(\frac{(z-r)}{\sigma_{2}^{2}}\left(\mu_{2}-\sqrt{2q\sigma_{2}^{2}+\mu_{2}^{2}}\right)\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\exp\left(\frac{-(r-a)}{\sigma_{2}^{2}}\left(\mu_{2}+\sqrt{2q\sigma_{2}^{2}+\mu_{2}^{2}}\right)\right)\exp\left(\frac{(z-a)}{\sigma_{2}^{2}}\left(\mu_{2}-\sqrt{2q\sigma_{2}^{2}+\mu_{2}^{2}}\right)\right)\right]\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{q}{\sqrt{2q\sigma_{2}^{2}+\mu_{2}^{2}}}\left(e^{\delta_{2}^{-}(r-a)}-e^{-\delta_{2}^{+}(r-a)}\right)e^{-\delta_{2}^{-}(z-a)}\mathrm{d}z.

(3.8)

Combing (
3.4
), (
3.5
), (
3.6
) and (
3.8
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{a}\left(X_{e_{q}}\in\mathrm{d}z\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{q}{\sqrt{2q\sigma_{2}^{2}+\mu_{2}^{2}}}e^{-\delta_{2}^{-}(z-a)}\frac{(\delta_{2}^{+}+\delta_{2}^{-})\left(e^{\delta_{2}^{-}(r-a)}-e^{-\delta_{2}^{+}(r-a)}\right)\mathrm{d}z}{(\delta_{2}^{+}+\delta_{1}^{-})e^{\delta_{2}^{-}(r-a)}+(\delta_{2}^{-}-\delta_{1}^{-})e^{-\delta_{2}^{+}(r-a)}-(\delta_{2}^{+}+\delta_{2}^{-})e^{-\delta_{2}^{+}(r-a)}}

\displaystyle=\frac{q}{\sqrt{2q\sigma_{2}^{2}+\mu_{2}^{2}}}\frac{\delta_{2}^{+}+\delta_{2}^{-}}{\delta_{2}^{+}+\delta_{1}^{-}}e^{-\delta_{2}^{-}(z-a)}\mathrm{d}z.

(3.9)

Similarly to (
3.7
) and by Lemma
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(e_{q}<T^{(2)}_{a},X^{(2)}_{e_{q}}\in\mathrm{d}z\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(X^{(2)}_{e_{q}}\in\mathrm{d}z\right)-\mathbb{E}_{x}e^{-qT^{(2)}_{a}}\mathbb{P}_{a}\left(X^{(2)}_{e_{q}}\in\mathrm{d}z\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{q}{\sqrt{2q\sigma_{2}^{2}+\mu_{2}^{2}}}\left[\exp\left(\frac{\mu_{2}(z-x)-|z-x|\sqrt{2q\sigma_{2}^{2}+\mu_{2}^{2}}}{\sigma_{2}^{2}}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\exp\left(\frac{\mu_{2}(a-x)-(x-a)\sqrt{2q\sigma_{2}^{2}+\mu_{2}^{2}}}{\sigma_{2}^{2}}\right)\exp\left(\frac{\mu_{2}(z-a)-(z-a)\sqrt{2q\sigma_{2}^{2}+\mu_{2}^{2}}}{\sigma_{2}^{2}}\right)\right]\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=

Substituting (
4.8
) into (

), we have

\displaystyle p(t;x,z;a,\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\left\{\frac{2}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\frac{2\mu_{1}(z-a)}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}}e^{\frac{-2\mu_{2}b}{\sigma_{2}^{2}}}h\left(t-\tau;\frac{b}{\sigma_{2}},\frac{-\mu_{2}}{\sigma_{2}}\right)h\left(\tau;\frac{-x-z+2a+b}{\sigma_{1}},\frac{-\mu_{1}}{\sigma_{1}}\right)\mathrm{d}\tau\mathrm{d}b\right.\\
\left.\ \ +\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi t\sigma_{1}^{2}}}\left[\exp\left(-\frac{\left(z-x-\mu_{1}t\right)^{2}}{2t\sigma_{1}^{2}}\right)-\exp\left(-\frac{\left(z+x-2a-\mu_{1}t\right)^{2}}{2t\sigma_{1}^{2}}-\frac{2\mu_{1}(x-a)}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}\right)\right]\right\},\quad-z\geq-a,\\
\frac{2}{\sigma_{2}^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\frac{-2\mu_{2}b}{\sigma_{2}^{2}}}h\left(t-\tau;\frac{b+z-a}{\sigma_{2}},\frac{-\mu_{2}}{\sigma_{2}}\right)h\left(\tau;\frac{-x+a+b}{\sigma_{1}},\frac{-\mu_{1}}{\sigma_{1}}\right)\mathrm{d}\tau\mathrm{d}b,\quad-z<-a.\end{cases}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\left\{\frac{2}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{0}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\frac{2\mu_{1}(z-a)}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}}e^{\frac{2\mu_{2}b}{\sigma_{2}^{2}}}h\left(t-\tau;\frac{-b}{\sigma_{2}},\frac{-\mu_{2}}{\sigma_{2}}\right)h\left(\tau;\frac{-x-z+2a-b}{\sigma_{1}},\frac{-\mu_{1}}{\sigma_{1}}\right)\mathrm{d}\tau\mathrm{d}b\right.\\
\left.\ \ +\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi t\sigma_{1}^{2}}}\left[\exp\left(-\frac{\left(z-x-\mu_{1}t\right)^{2}}{2t\sigma_{1}^{2}}\right)-\exp\left(-\frac{\left(z+x-2a-\mu_{1}t\right)^{2}}{2t\sigma_{1}^{2}}-\frac{2\mu_{1}(x-a)}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}\right)\right]\right\},\quad z\leq a,\\
\frac{2}{\sigma_{2}^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{0}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\frac{2\mu_{2}b}{\sigma_{2}^{2}}}h\left(t-\tau;\frac{-b+z-a}{\sigma_{2}},\frac{-\mu_{2}}{\sigma_{2}}\right)h\left(\tau;\frac{-x+a-b}{\sigma_{1}},\frac{-\mu_{1}}{\sigma_{1}}\right)\mathrm{d}\tau\mathrm{d}b,\quad z>a.\end{cases}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\left\{\frac{2}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{0}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\frac{2\mu_{1}(z-a)}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}}e^{\frac{2\mu_{2}b}{\sigma_{2}^{2}}}h\left(t-\tau;\frac{b}{\sigma_{2}},\frac{\mu_{2}}{\sigma_{2}}\right)h\left(\tau;\frac{z+x-2a+b}{\sigma_{1}},\frac{\mu_{1}}{\sigma_{1}}\right)\mathrm{d}\tau\mathrm{d}b\right.\\
\left.\ \ +\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi t\sigma_{1}^{2}}}\left[\exp\left(-\frac{\left(z-x-\mu_{1}t\right)^{2}}{2t\sigma_{1}^{2}}\right)-\exp\left(-\frac{\left(z+x-2a-\mu_{1}t\right)^{2}}{2t\sigma_{1}^{2}}-\frac{2\mu_{1}(x-a)}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}\right)\right]\right\},\quad z\leq a,\\
\frac{2}{\sigma_{2}^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{0}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\frac{2\mu_{2}b}{\sigma_{2}^{2}}}h\left(t-\tau;\frac{b-z+a}{\sigma_{2}},\frac{\mu_{2}}{\sigma_{2}}\right)h\left(\tau;\frac{x-a+b}{\sigma_{1}},\frac{\mu_{1}}{\sigma_{1}}\right)\mathrm{d}\tau\mathrm{d}b,\quad z>a,\end{cases}

where the second equation is due to variable substitution and the last equation is due to (
2.12
). The proof of (
4.9
) is completed.

\square

The diffusion process
X
exhibits a stationary distribution, as demonstrated by the following proposition, when the parameters
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
in equation (

) satisfy

and
\mu_{2}<0
.

Proposition 4.1

If
\mu_{1}>0
and
\mu_{2}<0
in (
2.1
), then

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}p(t;x,z)=\begin{cases}\frac{-\mu_{1}\mu_{2}}{\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}}\frac{2}{\sigma_{2}^{2}}e^{\frac{2\mu_{2}(z-a)}{\sigma_{2}^{2}}},\quad z\geq a,\\
\frac{-\mu_{1}\mu_{2}}{\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}}\frac{2}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}e^{\frac{2\mu_{1}(z-a)}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}},\quad z<a.\end{cases}

(4.14)

Proof.

Based on the Terminal-Value Theorem (Theorem 2.36 in Schiff
[
21
]
) and equation (
2.7
), we have

\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{x}(X_{t}\in\mathrm{d}z))=\lim_{q\to 0}\mathbb{P}_{x}(X_{e_{q}}\in\mathrm{d}z).

Therefore, in order to prove equation (
4.14
), it is sufficient to establish the following equation (
4.15
):

\displaystyle\lim_{q\to 0}\mathbb{P}_{x}(X_{e_{q}}\in\mathrm{d}z)=\begin{cases}\frac{-\mu_{1}\mu_{2}}{\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}}\frac{2}{\sigma_{2}^{2}}e^{\frac{2\mu_{2}(z-a)}{\sigma_{2}^{2}}}\mathrm{d}z,\quad z\geq a,\\
\frac{-\mu_{1}\mu_{2}}{\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}}\frac{2}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}e^{\frac{2\mu_{1}(z-a)}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}}\mathrm{d}z,\quad z<a.\end{cases}

(4.15)

Since
\mu_{1}>0
and
\mu_{2}<0
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{q\to 0}\frac{q}{\delta_{2}^{+}+\delta_{1}^{-}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{q\to 0}\frac{q}{\frac{\sqrt{2q\sigma_{2}^{2}+\mu_{2}^{2}}+\mu_{2}}{\sigma_{2}^{2}}+\frac{\sqrt{2q\sigma_{1}^{2}+\mu_{1}^{2}}-\mu_{1}}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}}

(4.16)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{q\to 0}\frac{1}{\frac{2\sigma_{2}^{2}}{2\sigma_{2}^{2}\sqrt{2q\sigma_{2}^{2}+\mu_{2}^{2}}}+\frac{2\sigma_{1}^{2}}{2\sigma_{1}^{2}\sqrt{2q\sigma_{1}^{2}+\mu_{1}^{2}}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-\mu_{1}\mu_{2}}{\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}}.

For
z\geq x\geq a
, it can be deduced from Theorem
3.1
and (
4.16
) that

\displaystyle\lim_{q\to 0}\mathbb{P}_{x}(X_{e_{q}}\in\mathrm{d}z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{q\to 0}\frac{qe^{-\delta_{2}^{-}(z-a)}}{\sqrt{2q\sigma_{2}^{2}+\mu_{2}^{2}}}\left[e^{\delta_{2}^{-}(x-a)}+\frac{(\delta_{2}^{-}-\delta_{1}^{-})e^{-\delta_{2}^{+}(x-a)}}{\delta_{2}^{+}+\delta_{1}^{-}}\right]\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{q\to 0}\frac{qe^{\frac{2\mu_{2}(z-a)}{\sigma_{2}^{2}}}}{-\mu_{2}}\left[\frac{\frac{-2\mu_{2}}{\sigma_{2}^{2}}}{\delta_{2}^{+}+\delta_{1}^{-}}\right]\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-\mu_{1}\mu_{2}}{\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}}\frac{2}{\sigma_{2}^{2}}e^{\frac{2\mu_{2}(z-a)}{\sigma_{2}^{2}}}\mathrm{d}z.

Similarly, for
x\geq z\geq a
, it can be deduced from Theorem
3.1
and (
4.16
) that

\displaystyle\lim_{q\to 0}\mathbb{P}_{x}(X_{e_{q}}\in\mathrm{d}z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{q\to 0}\frac{qe^{-\delta_{2}^{+}(x-a)}}{\sqrt{2q\sigma_{2}^{2}+\mu_{2}^{2}}}\left[e^{\delta_{2}^{+}(z-a)}+\frac{(\delta_{2}^{-}-\delta_{1}^{-})e^{-\delta_{2}^{-}(z-a)}}{\delta_{2}^{+}+\delta_{1}^{-}}\right]\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{q\to 0}\frac{q}{-\mu_{2}}\left[\frac{\frac{-2\mu_{2}}{\sigma_{2}^{2}}e^{\frac{2\mu_{2}}{\sigma_{2}^{2}}(z-a)}}{\delta_{2}^{+}+\delta_{1}^{-}}\right]\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-\mu_{1}\mu_{2}}{\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}}\frac{2}{\sigma_{2}^{2}}e^{\frac{2\mu_{2}(z-a)}{\sigma_{2}^{2}}}\mathrm{d}z.

For
x\geq a,z<a
, it can be deduced from Theorem
3.2
and (
4.16
) that

\displaystyle\lim_{q\to 0}\mathbb{P}_{x}(X_{e_{q}}\in\mathrm{d}z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{q\to 0}\frac{q}{\sqrt{2q\sigma_{1}^{2}+\mu_{1}}}\frac{\delta_{1}^{+}+\delta_{1}^{-}}{\delta_{2}^{+}+\delta_{1}^{-}}e^{-\delta_{2}^{+}(x-a)}e^{\delta_{1}^{+}(z-a)}\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{q\to 0}\frac{q}{\mu_{1}}\frac{\frac{2\mu_{1}}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}}{\delta_{2}^{+}+\delta_{1}^{-}}e^{\frac{2\mu_{1}}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}(z-a)}\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-\mu_{1}\mu_{2}}{\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}}\frac{2}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}e^{\frac{2\mu_{1}(z-a)}{\sigma_{1}^{2}}}\mathrm{d}z.

For

, (

Therefore, we complete the proof of this proposition.

\square

6
The control problem

In this section, we consider the control problem of McNamara
[
17
]
on
diffusion with two dynamics over a finite time interval
[0,T]
. Two pairs of real numbers
\left(\underline{\mu},\underline{\sigma}\right)
and
\left(\overline{\mu},\overline{\sigma}\right)
are given with
0<\underline{\sigma}<\overline{\sigma}
.

An admissible control is defined as a process
\sigma(\cdot):=(\sigma(t))_{0\leq t\leq T}
adapted to the filtration
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{0\leq t\leq T}
and taking values in the two element set
\left\{\underline{\sigma},\overline{\sigma}\right\}
. We denote the set of all admissible controls by
\mathcal{U}
.
For
\sigma(\cdot)\in\mathcal{U}
, consider the following stochastic control
system

\begin{cases}\mathrm{d}X^{\sigma}_{t}=\mu(\sigma(t))\mathrm{d}t+\sigma(t)\mathrm{d}B_{t},\quad t\in[0,T],\\
X^{\sigma}_{0}=x,\end{cases}

(6.1)

where
\mu(\cdot)
denotes the function which maps
\underline{\sigma}
to
\underline{\mu}
and
\overline{\sigma}
to
\overline{\mu}
, respectively.
Thus, we may either choose
\underline{\mu}
as the drift coefficient and
\underline{\sigma}
as the diffusion coefficient or choose
\overline{\mu}
as the drift and
\overline{\sigma}
as the diffusion coefficient.

The objective is to maximize the following profit functional over
\mathcal{U}

J(x;\sigma(\cdot)):=P(X_{T}^{\sigma}\geq a).

The value function is defined by
V(x):=\max_{\sigma(\cdot)\in\mathcal{U}}J(x;\sigma(\cdot))
.
An admissible control
\sigma(\cdot)
is called optimal if
P(X_{T}^{\sigma}\geq a)=V(x)
, and the corresponding equation (
6.1
) is the optimal state equation.

Theorem 6.1

In the above control problem,
the optimal state equation is

\begin{cases}\mathrm{d}X^{\sigma^{*}}_{t}=\left(\overline{\mu}\mathbf{1}_{\{X^{\sigma^{*}}_{t}\leq\alpha(T-t)+a\}}+\underline{\mu}\mathbf{1}_{\{X^{\sigma^{*}}_{t}>\alpha(T-t)+a\}}\right)\mathrm{d}t\\
\quad\quad\quad\quad+\left(\overline{\sigma}\mathbf{1}_{\{X^{\sigma^{*}}_{t}\leq\alpha(T-t)+a\}}+\underline{\sigma}\mathbf{1}_{\{X^{\sigma^{*}}_{t}>\alpha(T-t)+a\}}\right)\mathrm{d}B_{t},\\
X^{\sigma^{*}}_{0}=x,\quad t\in[0,T],\end{cases}

(6.2)

the optimal control is

\sigma^{*}(t)=\overline{\sigma}\mathbf{1}_{\{X_{t}^{\sigma^{*}}\leq\alpha(T-t)+a\}}+\underline{\sigma}\mathbf{1}_{\{X_{t}^{\sigma^{*}}>\alpha(T-t)+a\}},

and the value function is

V(x)=\int_{a}^{\infty}p(T;x-\alpha T,z)\mathrm{d}z,

where
\alpha=\frac{\overline{\mu}\underline{\sigma}-\underline{\mu}\overline{\sigma}}{\overline{\sigma}-\underline{\sigma}}
, and
p(T;x-\alpha T,z)
is the transition density function in Theorem
4.3
with
\mu_{1}=\overline{\mu}+\alpha
,
\mu_{2}=\underline{\mu}+\alpha
,
\sigma_{1}=\overline{\sigma}
,
\sigma_{2}=\underline{\sigma}
.

In particular,
if
\underline{\mu}/\underline{\sigma}=\overline{\mu}/\overline{\sigma}
,
then
\alpha=0
, and
the optimal state equation (
6.2
) becomes

\begin{cases}\mathrm{d}X^{\sigma^{*}}_{t}=\left(\overline{\mu}\mathbf{1}_{\{X^{\sigma^{*}}_{t}\leq a\}}+\underline{\mu}\mathbf{1}_{\{X^{\sigma^{*}}_{t}>a\}}\right)\mathrm{d}t+\left(\overline{\sigma}\mathbf{1}_{\{X^{\sigma^{*}}_{t}\leq a\}}+\underline{\sigma}\mathbf{1}_{\{X^{\sigma^{*}}_{t}>a\}}\right)\mathrm{d}B_{t},\quad t\in[0,T],\\
X^{\sigma^{*}}_{0}=x,\end{cases}

the optimal control becomes

\sigma^{*}(t)=\overline{\sigma}\mathbf{1}_{\{X_{t}^{\sigma^{*}}\leq a\}}+\underline{\sigma}\mathbf{1}_{\{X_{t}^{\sigma^{*}}>a\}},

and the value function is

V(x)=\int_{a}^{\infty}p(T;x,z)\mathrm{d}z,

where
p(T;x,z)
is the transition density function in Theorem
4.3
with
\mu_{1}=\overline{\mu}
,
\mu_{2}=\underline{\mu}
,
\sigma_{1}=\overline{\sigma}
,
\sigma_{2}=\underline{\sigma}
.

Proof.

We introduce an auxiliary process
Y_{t}^{\sigma}:=X_{t}^{\sigma}-\alpha(T-t)-a
.
Then
Y_{T}^{\sigma}=X_{T}^{\sigma}-a
and

\begin{cases}\mathrm{d}Y^{\sigma}_{t}=[\mu(\sigma(t))+\alpha]\mathrm{d}t+\sigma(t)\mathrm{d}B_{t},\quad t\in[0,T],\\
Y^{\sigma}_{0}=x-\alpha T-a,\end{cases}

where

\alpha=\frac{\overline{\mu}\underline{\sigma}-\underline{\mu}\overline{\sigma}}{\overline{\sigma}-\underline{\sigma}}.

Thus, we just need to find
\sigma^{*}_{t}\in\mathcal{U}
such that

J(x;\sigma^{*}(\cdot))=\max_{\sigma(\cdot)\in\mathcal{U}}P(Y_{T}^{\sigma}\geq 0).

Since

\frac{\underline{\mu}+\alpha}{\underline{\sigma}}=\frac{\overline{\mu}+\alpha}{\overline{\sigma}}=\frac{\overline{\mu}-\underline{\mu}}{\overline{\sigma}-\underline{\sigma}},

by the result in McNamara
[
17
]
, we can show that the optimal control
\sigma^{*}(t)
is given by
\sigma^{*}(t)=\overline{\sigma}\mathbf{1}_{\{Y_{t}^{\sigma^{*}}\leq 0\}}+\underline{\sigma}\mathbf{1}_{\{Y_{t}^{\sigma^{*}}>0\}}
, where

\begin{cases}\mathrm{d}Y^{\sigma^{*}}_{t}=\left(\overline{\mu}\mathbf{1}_{\{Y^{\sigma^{*}}_{t}\leq 0\}}+\underline{\mu}\mathbf{1}_{\{Y^{\sigma^{*}}_{t}>0\}}+\alpha\right)\mathrm{d}t+\left(\overline{\sigma}\mathbf{1}_{\{Y^{\sigma^{*}}_{t}\leq 0\}}+\underline{\sigma}\mathbf{1}_{\{Y^{\sigma^{*}}_{t}>0\}}\right)\mathrm{d}B_{t},\quad t\in[0,T],\\
Y^{\sigma^{*}}_{0}=x-\alpha T-a.\end{cases}

Thus, for
t\in[0,T]
, the optimal control is

\sigma^{*}(t)=\overline{\sigma}\mathbf{1}_{\{X_{t}^{\sigma^{*}}\leq\alpha(T-t)+a\}}+\underline{\sigma}\mathbf{1}_{\{X_{t}^{\sigma^{*}}>\alpha(T-t)+a\}},

and the optimal state equation is (
6.2
).

For compute the value function, let us introduce another auxiliary process
Z^{\sigma}_{t}:=Y^{\sigma}_{t}+a
. Then

\begin{cases}\mathrm{d}Z^{\sigma^{*}}_{t}=\left(\overline{\mu}\mathbf{1}_{\{Z^{\sigma^{*}}_{t}\leq a\}}+\underline{\mu}\mathbf{1}_{\{Z^{\sigma^{*}}_{t}>a\}}+\alpha\right)\mathrm{d}t+\left(\overline{\sigma}\mathbf{1}_{\{Z^{\sigma^{*}}_{t}\leq a\}}+\underline{\sigma}\mathbf{1}_{\{Z^{\sigma^{*}}_{t}>a\}}\right)\mathrm{d}B_{t},\quad t\in[0,T],\\
Z^{\sigma^{*}}_{0}=x-\alpha T,\end{cases}

and the optimal control can be written as
\sigma^{*}(t)=\overline{\sigma}\mathbf{1}_{\{Z_{t}^{\sigma^{*}}\leq a\}}+\underline{\sigma}\mathbf{1}_{\{Z_{t}^{\sigma^{*}}>a\}}
.
The value function becomes

V(x)=J(x;\sigma^{*}(\cdot))=P(X_{T}^{\sigma^{*}}\geq a)=P(Z_{T}^{\sigma^{*}}\geq a)=\int_{a}^{\infty}p(T;x-\alpha T,z)\mathrm{d}z,

where
p(T;x-\alpha T,z)
is the transition density function in Theorem
4.3
with
\mu_{1}=\overline{\mu}+\alpha
,
\mu_{2}=\underline{\mu}+\alpha
,
\sigma_{1}=\overline{\sigma}
,
\sigma_{2}=\underline{\sigma}
.

\square

Remark 6.1

In the foraging model,
\left(\underline{\mu},\underline{\sigma}\right)
and
\left(\overline{\mu},\overline{\sigma}\right)
represent
the mean and standard deviation of the energy gain per minute under two prey
types. The forager can survive at terminal time
T
if the energy reserved
X_{T}
exceeding some critical level
a
.
The optimal diet of a forager maximizes its probability of
survival.
By Theorem
6.1
, we not only get the optimal foraging strategy but also the explicit expression of maximum survival distribution
\max_{\sigma(\cdot)\in\mathcal{U}}P(X_{T}^{\sigma}\geq a)
.

Association schemes obtained from the action of the general unitary group on isotropic vectors
Nathaniel Benjamin, Sung Y. Song

Abstract
An infinite family of association schemes obtained from the general unitary groups acting transitively on the sets of isotropic vectors in the finite unitary spaces are investigated. We compute the parameters and determine the character tables for all nontrivial commutative association schemes belonging to this family. 1 1 1 This work contains a part of the first author’s Ph.D. dissertation [ 8 ] .



Keywords: Schurian association scheme, character table.

MSC-2020 Classification: 05E30 (primary); 20G15, 11E39 (secondary).

Introduction and preliminaries

An association scheme of class
d
is a pair
\mathcal{X}=\left(X,\{R_{i}\}_{i\in[d]}\right)
of a finite set
X
and a set of non-empty
d+1
relations
\{R_{0},R_{1},\dots,R_{d}\}
of
X
such that

(1)

R_{0}=\{(x,x):x\in X\}
is the identity relation;

(2)

R_{0}\cup R_{1}\cup\cdots\cup R_{d}=X\times X
and
R_{i}\cap R_{j}=\emptyset
for
i\neq j
in
[d]:=\{0,1,2,\dots,d\}
;

(3)

for each
i\in[d]
,
R_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}=R_{i^{\prime}}
for some
i^{\prime}\in[d]
where
R_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}:=\{(x,y):(y,x)\in R_{i}\}
;

(4)

for each triple
h,i,j\in[d]
, there exists a non-negative integer
p_{ij}^{h}
such that for all
(x,y)\in R_{h}
, the number

|\{z\in X:(x,z)\in R_{i},(z,y)\in R_{j}\}|
is equal to
p^{h}_{ij}
.

The constants
p_{ij}^{h}
are called the
intersection numbers
(parameters) of the scheme
\mathcal{X}
. If an association scheme
\mathcal{X}=\left(X,\{R_{i}\}_{i\in[d]}\right)
satisfies
that, for all
h,i,j\in[d]
,
p_{ij}^{h}=p_{ji}^{h}
,
then it is said to be
commutative
. If for all
i\in[d]
,
R_{i}^{\prime}=R_{i}
, then it is called
symmetric
.

A major source of association schemes is the set of transitive permutation groups (cf.
[
1
,
3
,
4
,
BHSWZie
]
). If a finite group
G
acts on a finite set
\Phi
transitively, the set of the orbitals (also called 2-orbits) of
G
; that is, the set of the orbits of the action of
G
on
\Phi\times\Phi
, forms an association scheme. Such an association scheme
\mathcal{X}=\left(\Phi,\{R_{i}\}_{i\in[d]}\right)
where
R_{0},R_{1},\dots,R_{d}
are the orbitals of the permutation group
G
on
\Phi
(of rank
d+1
) is called a
Schurian association scheme
(of class
d
) and denoted by
\mathcal{X}(G,\Phi)
.

In this paper, we study the Schurian association schemes coming from the transitive action of the finite general unitary groups on the sets of isotropic vectors of the
n
-dimensional unitary space equipped with a non-degenerate Hermitian inner product over the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
of order
q^{2}
for all
n\geq 2
and prime powers
q
. Our aim is to provide a complete description of these Schurian association schemes in terms of their character tables. These association schemes help fill the void in the existing collection of the Schurian association schemes coming from the classical groups acting on various sets of vectors or subspaces of the corresponding geometries.

For known interesting examples of Schurian association schemes, we refer the readers to Brouwer-Cohen-Neumaier
[
9
]
for the
P
-polynomial association schemes (distance-regular graphs) corresponding to the dual polar spaces, Brouwer-van Maldeghem
[
10
]
for known schemes of class 2 (strongly regular graphs), and Hanaki
[
13
]
for all Schurian schemes of small orders (orders up to 40 or so). For the list of infinite families of Schurian schemes whose character tables are known, see
[
19
]
and the references there.

Given a
d
-class commutative association scheme
\mathcal{X}=(X,\{R_{i}\}_{i\in[d]})
of order
n
(i.e.,
|X|=n
),
let
A_{i}
denote the
i
th adjacency matrix representing
R_{i}
; that is,
\{0,1\}
-matrix whose
(x,y)
-entry is defined by

\left(A_{i}\right)_{xy}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\mbox{if }(x,y)\in R_{i}\\
0&\mbox{otherwise.}\\
\end{array}\right.

By the definition of commutative association scheme, these matrices satisfy that

(1)

A_{0}=I
, the identity matrix;

(2)

A_{0}+A_{1}+\cdots+A_{d}=J
, where
J
is the all-ones matrix;

(3)

for each
i\in[d]
,
A_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}=A_{i^{\prime}}
for some
i^{\prime}\in[d]
where
A_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}
denotes the transpose of
A_{i}
;

(4)

for any
h,i,j\in[d]
, there exists a constant
p_{ij}^{h}
such that

A_{i}A_{j}=\sum\limits_{h=0}^{d}p_{ij}^{h}A_{h};

(5)

A_{i}A_{j}=A_{j}A_{i}
for every
i,j\in[d]
.

In the full matrix algebra consisting of all
n\times n
matrices over the field of complex numbers, these adjacency matrices of
\mathcal{X}
generate the
(d+1)
-dimensional commutative algebra
\mathcal{A}:=\left<A_{0},A_{1},\dots,A_{d}\right>
known as the
Bose-Mesner algebra
of
\mathcal{X}
.
The algebra
\mathcal{A}
being a semi-simple algebra admits central primitive idempotents.
Let
E_{0}=\frac{1}{n}J,E_{1},\dots,E_{d}
denote the primitive idempotents in
\mathcal{A}
. Then there exist complex numbers
p_{j}(i)
and
q_{i}(j)
for
i,j\in[d]
such that

A_{j}=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{d}p_{j}(i)E_{i},\quad E_{i}=\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{h}q_{i}(j)A_{j}.

The
(d+1)\times(d+1)
base-change matrices
P
and
Q
, whose
(i,j)
-entries are
p_{j}(i)
and
q_{j}(i)
, respectively, are called the
1st
and
2nd eigenmatrix
of
\mathcal{X}
, respectively. The
d+1
entries
p_{j}(0),p_{j}(1),\dots,p_{j}(d)
, the entries of the column indexed with
j
of
P
, are the roots of the minimal polynomial of
A_{j}
for each
j\in[d]
. The first eigenmatrix
P
is also called the
character table
of the association scheme. We denote the multiplicities of
\mathcal{X}
by
m_{0},m_{1},\dots,m_{d}
, which are the ranks of idempotents
E_{0},E_{1},\dots,E_{d}
, respectively. Note that
m_{i}
is the trace of
E_{i}
as the eigenvalues of
E_{i}
are 1 and 0. The
i^{\textnormal{th}}

valency
, i.e., the number of elements of
X
that are in the
i^{\textnormal{th}}
associates with
x
for any fixed
x\in X
, is denoted by
k_{i}
. Note that
k_{i}=p_{ii^{\prime}}^{0}
. Then we have the following formulae for the parameters and eigenvalues of
\mathcal{X}
(cf.
[
3
, Ch 2]
):

Proposition 1.1

\begin{array}[]{llll}&m_{i}=n\big(\sum\limits_{j=0}^{d}\frac{|p_{j}(i)|^{2}}{k_{j}}\big)^{-1}&&\text{for}\ i\in[d]\\
&p_{ij}^{h}=\frac{1}{n\cdot k_{h}}\sum\limits_{l=0}^{d}p_{i}(l)p_{j}(l)\overline{p_{h}(l)}m_{l}&&\text{for}\ h,i,j\in[d].\end{array}

where
\overline{a}
denotes the complex
conjugate of
a
.

Proposition 1.2

The character table
P=\big[p_{j}(i)\big]
of
\mathcal{X}=(X,\{R_{i}\}_{i\in[d]})

satisfies the orthogonality relations:

\begin{array}[]{llll}&\sum\limits_{j=0}^{d}\frac{1}{k_{j}}p_{j}(i_{1})\cdot\overline{p_{j}(i_{2})}=\delta_{i_{1}i_{2}}\frac{n}{m_{i_{1}}},&&i_{1},i_{2}\in[d]\\
&\sum\limits_{i=0}^{d}m_{i}p_{j_{1}}(i)\overline{p_{j_{2}}(i)}=\delta_{j_{1}j_{2}}\cdot n\cdot k_{j_{1}},&&j_{1},j_{2}\in[d]\end{array}

where
\delta_{ij}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\mbox{if }i=j\\
0&\mbox{if }i\neq j\end{array}\right.
.

There is another matrix algebra associated with
\mathcal{X}
. Let
B_{i}
,
i\in[d]
, be the
i
th
intersection matrix
defined by

\left(B_{i}\right)_{jh}=p_{ij}^{h}.
Then

B_{i}B_{j}=\sum\limits_{h=0}^{d}p_{ij}^{h}B_{h}.

It follows that the algebra
\mathcal{B}:=\left<B_{0},B_{1},\dots,B_{d}\right>
over
\mathbb{C}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{A}:=\left<A_{0},A_{1},\dots,A_{d}\right>
. In particular, the minimal polynomial of
B_{i}
coincides with that of
A_{i}
for each
i\in[d]
, and thus,
the eigenvalues of
B_{i}
s are the entries of the character table of the scheme
\mathcal{X}
.
Therefore, it is possible that the character table can be determined from the eigenvalues of
B_{i}
with or without additional information. We will refer to this approach of constructing the character table of
\mathcal{X}
as an elementary and ‘direct approach’.

We note that there is an ‘indirect approach’ that has been used as well. When
G
acts transitively on
\Phi
, there is a natural one-to-one correspondence between
\Phi
and the set
G_{x}\backslash G
of the cosets of a point stabilizer
G_{x}
for
x\in\Phi
. Thus, the permutation representation of the action
G
on
\Phi
is identical to that of the action
G
on the set
H\backslash G
of cosets of
H
, where
H=G_{x}
for
x\in\Phi
. The permutation character of the representation is the same as the induced character
1^{G}_{H}
of
H
in
G
. We also note that there is a one-to-one correspondence between any two of the following three:

(i) the set of orbitals of the permutation group
(G,\Phi)
,

(ii) the set of suborbits of
(G,\Phi)
and

(iii) the set of double cosets
Hg_{i}H
of
H
in
G
,
g_{i}\in G
.

It follows that there are links between the following three in terms of associated algebra:

(i) the Bose-Mesner algebra of the Schurian scheme
\mathcal{X}(G,\Phi)
;

(ii) the centralizer algebra (Hecke algebra) of
(G,\Phi)
;

(iii) the double coset algebra, the subalgebra spanned by the set of simple quantities
\frac{1}{|H|}\sum\limits_{g\in Hg_{i}H}g
in group algebra.

Although we take the direct approach to construct the character table of a Schurian association scheme
\mathcal{X}(G,\Phi)
with
G=GU(n,q)
and
\Phi=\Phi(n,q)
for suitable
(n,q)
, there is an alternative way to find the entries of the character table for some cases. Namely, its character table may be determined by using the relationship between Bose-Mesner algebra and the associated algebra of the permutation group
(G,\Phi)
as mentioned above. For instance, suppose all orbitals are self-paired (thus, the scheme is symmetric) and the induced character
1^{G}_{H}
is multiplicity-free, so that

1^{G}_{H}=\chi_{0}+\chi_{1}+\cdots+\chi_{d},

where
\chi_{0}=1_{G}
and
\chi_{1},\chi_{2},\dots,\chi_{d}
are distinct irreducible characters of
G
. Then the entries
p_{j}(i)
of the character table
P=\left[p_{j}(i)\right]
of the scheme
\mathcal{X}(G,H\backslash G)
can be explicitly expressed as:

p_{j}(i)\ \ =\ \ \frac{1}{|H|}\sum\limits_{a\in HGH}\chi_{i}(a)\ \ =\ \ \frac{1}{|H|}\sum\limits_{l}|Hg_{j}H\cap C_{l}|\cdot\chi_{i}(c_{l})

where
C_{0},C_{1},\dots,C_{l},\dots,C_{d}
is the conjugacy classes of
G
and
c_{l}
is the class representative of
C_{l}
. However, finding the entries
p_{j}(i)
of the character table using the above equation is sometimes involved if it is not impossible (cf.
[
19
]
and references in it).

(for arbitrary

) is constructed.

2
The orbitals of permutation group
GU(n,q)
on
\Phi(n,q)

In this section, we shall see that for each prime power
q
and integer
n\geq 2
, the permutation action of finite unitary group
GU(n,q)
on the set
\Phi(n,q)
of isotropic vectors in the finite unitary space
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{n}
is transitive, and thus, its orbitals form an association scheme. Our aim is to give a complete description of orbitals that form the relation set of the association scheme.

For more information on the facts on finite fields and finite classical geometries that are used in this discussion, we refer the readers to the books by Wan
[
21
]
, Grove
[
12
]
and Taylor
[
20
]
.

Given a prime power
q
, let
V=\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{n}
be the
n
-dimensional unitary space over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, that is,
V
is the vector space equipped with a non-degenerate Hermitian inner product on which
GU(n,q)
is acting. Recall that the field
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
has an involution
a\mapsto\bar{a}=a^{q}
whose fixed field is
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. We will repeatedly use the fact that:
(i) For any
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
, the equation
x\bar{x}=\lambda
has exactly
q+1
solutions in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{*}
.
(ii) For any
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, the equation
x+\bar{x}=\lambda
has exactly
q
solutions in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
(cf.
[
21
]
).

Given row vectors
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n})
and
y=(y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{n})
in
V
, denote the Hermitian inner product of
x
and
y
by

\langle x,y\rangle=xy^{*}=x_{1}y_{1}^{q}+x_{2}y_{2}^{q}+\cdots+x_{n}y_{n}^{q}.

Recall that the set of isotropic vectors in
V
is

\Phi(n,q):=\{x\in V-\{0\}:\langle x,x\rangle=0\}

and the number of isotropic vectors in
V
is:

|\Phi(n,q)|=(q^{n}-(-1)^{n})(q^{n-1}-(-1)^{n-1})

In what follows,
GU(n,q)
and
\Phi(n,q)
will be often denoted by
G
and
\Phi
, respectively, for short, once
n
and
q
are fixed.
The group action here is described for
x\in\Phi
and
U\in G
by
xU
using typical matrix multiplication.
We extend the group action of
G
on
\Phi
to
G
acting on
\Phi\times\Phi
in the obvious way described by
(x,y)U=(xU,yU)
.

In order to verify that the action of
G
on
\Phi
is indeed transitive, we need to recall the following well-known results. Note that a
hyperbolic pair
in
V
is a pair of vectors
(u,v)
such that
u
and
v
are isotropic, and the Hermitian inner product
\langle u,v\rangle=1
. The subspace spanned by
\{u,v\}
is called a
hyperbolic line
.

Proposition 2.1

Suppose that
L
is a two-dimensional subspace of
V
which contains an isotropic vector
u
with respect to a non-degenerate Hermitian inner product. Then there exists a vector
v
, such that
L={Span}\{u,v\}
, and
(u,v)
is a hyperbolic pair.

It is known that
V
can be decomposed into

V=L_{1}\bot L_{2}\bot\cdots\bot L_{m}\bot W

where each
L_{i}
is a hyperbolic line, with
m
as the Witt index, and such that
W
does not contain any isotropic vectors. For our case, we can ignore
W
, since we are concerned with isotropic vectors only, though in general for finite fields, dim
(W)
= 0 or 1 because of the previous proposition,
which ensures that we can find more isotropic vectors while the dimension of remaining subspace is at least 2. (cf.
[
20
, pp.116-117]
).

Proposition 2.2

For a vector space
V
that is decomposed as

V=L_{1}\bot L_{2}\bot\cdots\bot L_{m}\bot W,

let
(u_{i},v_{i})
be hyperbolic pairs that form a basis for each corresponding
L_{i}
, and let
w
be a basis for
W
with
\langle w,w\rangle=1
. Then
G
acts regularly on the basis vectors
u_{1},\dots u_{m},v_{1},\dots,v_{m},w
. That is, the action of
G
on
\Phi
is transitive and semiregular.

From these propositions we can deduce the following.

Theorem 2.3

GU(n,q)
acts transitively on
\Phi(n,q)
, for all
n\geq 2
and all prime powers
q
.

Proof:
Let
u,v\in\Phi(n,q)
, and let
(u,u^{\prime})
be a hyperbolic pair of
V=\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{n}
, which exists by Proposition
2.1
. Define
L_{1}
as the hyperbolic line spanned by
(u,u^{\prime})
, and decompose
V
into hyperbolic lines

V=L_{1}\bot L_{1}^{\bot}=L_{1}\bot L_{2}\bot\cdots\bot L_{m}.

Now it is shown that there exists a unitary matrix that takes
u
to
v
in case by case.

(i)

Suppose
v\in L_{1}^{\bot}
.
Then again by the above proposition there is a hyperbolic pair
(v,v^{\prime})
that spans some
L_{i}
with
i\neq 1
. We can choose hyperbolic pairs arbitrarily to form a basis for each of the remaining hyperbolic lines. Then we have a basis for
V
including
u
and
v
as basis elements. By Proposition
2.2
,
GU(n,q)
acts regularly on these vectors, and thus transitively. Hence, there is a unitary matrix that takes
u
to
v
.

(ii)

Suppose
v\in L_{1}
and
(u,v)
forms a basis for
L_{1}
.
Then again we can arbitrarily choose hyperbolic pairs to extend to a basis for
V
and apply Proposition
2.2
to obtain a unitary matrix that takes
u
to
v
.

(iii)

Suppose
v\in L_{1}
and
(u,v)
does not form a basis for
L_{1}
.
Then
u
and
v
are linearly dependent, i.e.
v=\beta u
. Recall that
(u,u^{\prime})
does form a basis for
L_{1}
, so
(v,u^{\prime})
also forms a basis for
L_{1}
. By (ii), there is a unitary matrix
U\in GU(n,q)
such that
vU=u^{\prime}
. Similarly, there is a unitary matrix
U^{\prime}\in GU(n,q)
such that
uU^{\prime}=u^{\prime}
. Hence, there is a unitary matrix
U^{\prime}U^{-1}\in GU(n,q)
that takes
u
to
v
, since

uU^{\prime}U^{-1}=u^{\prime}U^{-1}=v.

Therefore,
GU(n,q)
indeed acts transitively on the set of isotropic vectors.

In order to classify the orbitals of the transitive permutation group
(GU(n,q),\Phi(n,q))
,
we will first define the desired partition of
\Phi(n,q)\times\Phi(n,q)
, and then proceed to show that these are indeed the orbitals of the action of
GU(n,q)
on
\Phi(n,q)
; and so the desired association relations.

Let
\alpha
be an arbitrary but fixed primitive element of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. Consider the following sets where
i,j\in[q^{2}-2]
and
\Phi=\Phi(n,q)
.

\begin{array}[]{c}S_{\alpha^{i}}(n,q)=\{(x,y)\in\Phi\times\Phi:y=\alpha^{i}x\}\\
R_{\alpha^{j}}(n,q)=\{(x,y)\in\Phi\times\Phi:\langle x,y\rangle=\alpha^{j}\}\\
T(n,q)=\{(x,y)\in\Phi\times\Phi:\langle x,y\rangle=0,y\notin{Span}\{x\}\}.\end{array}\qquad\qquad(*)

Lemma 2.4

For any
n
and
q
and for each
i,j\in[q^{2}-2]
, the sets
S_{\alpha^{i}}
,
R_{\alpha^{j}}
’s, and
T
defined above partition
\Phi\times\Phi
, i.e.

\Phi\times\Phi\ \ =\ \ T\ \cup\ \bigcup_{i=0}^{q^{2}-2}S_{\alpha^{i}}\ \cup\ \bigcup_{j=0}^{q^{2}-2}R_{\alpha^{j}}.

Proof:
This follows from the fact that if
\langle x,y\rangle\neq 0
, then
(x,y)
lies in exactly one of the
R_{\alpha^{i}}
’s. If
\langle x,y\rangle=0
then either
(x,y)\in T
or
y\in{Span}\{x\}
. In the latter case,
y=\alpha^{i}x
for some
i
, implying that
(x,y)\in S_{\alpha^{i}}
. Hence, each element of
\Phi\times\Phi
lies in exactly one of the sets above
(*)
.

Notice that the three types of orbitals:
S_{\alpha}
’s,
R_{\alpha}
’s, and
T
, correspond directly with Cases (iii), (ii), and (i) respectively, addressed in the proof of Theorem
2.3
. The next task is to show that these sets indeed form the orbitals of the group action, that is, showing that
\Phi(n,q)\times\Phi(n,q)
is not partitioned any finer or courser by the action. This is displayed by showing each set closed under the group action so that none of them need to be combined to form the relations, and that
GU(n,q)
does not refine any further.
We will use the following result, known as Witt’s Extension Theorem, repeatedly.

Proposition 2.5
(Witt’s Extension Theorem
[
22
]
)

Suppose
V_{1},V_{2}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{n}
are subspaces and that there is a linear isomorphism
U^{\prime}:V_{1}\rightarrow V_{2}
such that
\langle xU^{\prime},yU^{\prime}\rangle=\langle x,y\rangle
for any
x,y\in V_{1}
. Then there exists
U\in GU(n,q)
such that
U|_{V_{1}}=U^{\prime}
.

Lemma 2.6

GU(n,q)
acts transitively on
S_{\alpha^{i}}
, for each
i\in[q^{2}-2]
.

Proof:
Take
(x,y)\in S_{\alpha^{i}}
, then
y=\alpha^{i}x
, so for any
U\in GU(n,q)
, we have
yU=\alpha^{i}xU
. Thus,
(xU,yU)=(x,y)U\in S_{\alpha^{i}}
.

To show transitivity, take
(x,y),(w,z)\in S_{\alpha^{i}}
we want to show that some
U\in GU(n,q)
takes
(x,y)
to
(w,z)
. Since
GU(n,q)
acts transitively on
\Phi(n,q)
by Theorem
2.3
, there exists a
U\in GU(n,q)
such that
w=xU
. Then
yU=\alpha^{i}xU=\alpha^{i}w=z
. Hence,
(x,y)U=(w,z)
, as desired.

Lemma 2.7

GU(n,q)
acts transitively on
R_{\alpha^{j}}
, for each
j\in[q^{2}-2]
.

Proof:
Take
(x,y)\in R_{\alpha^{j}}
, then
\langle x,y\rangle=xy^{*}=\alpha^{j}
, so for any
U\in GU(n,q)
,

\langle xU,yU\rangle=xU(yU)^{*}=xUU^{*}y^{*}=xy^{*}=\alpha^{j}.

Thus,
(xU,yU)=(x,y)U\in R_{\alpha^{j}}
.

To show transitivity, take
(x,y),(w,z)\in R_{\alpha^{j}}
. There exists a linear isomorphism

U^{\prime}:{Span}\{x,y\}\to{Span}\{w,z\}

such that
xU^{\prime}=w
and
yU^{\prime}=z
, as they are both 2-dimensional subspaces. Since

5.3

\mathcal{X}(GU(n,2),\Phi(n,2))

In the classification of association schemes, often knowledge of the character table of its fusion or fission scheme is useful. A fusion scheme of a Schurian association scheme is not necessarily Schurian; however, we observe that
\mathcal{X}(GU(n,2),\Phi(n,2))

Definition 5.3

Proposition 5.4
